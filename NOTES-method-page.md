# Note — the `/method` page, measured against Bonaventure's

**Written 2026-07-28,** after the Bonaventure About page was rewritten with a full
methodology section. Wilson asked whether Migne's is as deep. Mostly it is. One thing is
missing, and it is the thing that answers the actual objection.

## What `site/method/index.html` already does well

Ten sections; the disclosure is squarer than Bonaventure's own. Do **not** rewrite these:

- It names what Migne's text actually is — a reprint of the Maurists and la Bigne, reset
  at speed, no fresh collation — and says outright that CSEL/GCS/CCSL supersede it for
  citing the text. Very few digital corpora admit their own source's standing.
- It discloses drafting by language model **and gives the reason for disclosing**: *"a
  method is easier to defend when it is described than when it is discovered."* That
  sentence is better than anything on the Bonaventure page and could be borrowed back.
- It states the objection in its strongest form before answering it — that a model
  produces fluent English about the right subject whether or not it understood the
  sentence, and that the fluency is what hides the failure.
- It has the refusals section (*What this method refuses to do*) and *How to check us*,
  which is the right closing move.

## What is missing

**There is no "why this way at all" argument.** The page defends the *procedure*
thoroughly and never defends the *project*. A skeptic granting every safeguard can still
ask why it should be done by machine, and the page has no answer on the shelf.

Bonaventure's page now closes with that argument, in Wilson's own framing (2026-07-28):

> Beneath the question of human against machine lies a prior question that settles it:
> nobody was doing this. There is no complete English Bonaventure, no English *Acta
> Sanctorum*, nothing approaching an English Migne. These are not gaps waiting on a
> translator who is nearly finished. The cost is measured in human lifetimes and has not
> moved since the nineteenth century, so they stood no closer to being filled this year
> than they would a thousand years from now. A corpus of this size is not translated
> slowly. It is not translated at all.
>
> The aim is therefore not a perfect translation. It is to unlock the language gate to the
> tradition.
>
> There will be errors, including serious ones — as there are in Migne, and in Quaracchi,
> and in every edition of this kind ever assembled. The model carries a fault forward, a
> line or a spacing or an artifact of the transcription, much as a clerk copying his
> exemplar carried forward the mistake set in front of him. Working this way has made me
> feel nearer to the medieval copyists than to a modern translator.

The clerk sentence lands with particular force here, since the page has already explained
in detail that Migne's own text is a lineage of copied errors. The argument and the source
description reinforce each other on this site in a way they cannot on the other two.

**Second gap: no cross-links.** Bonaventure's About now links **actasanctorum.org** and
**migne.app** by name at the point where it says these corpora have never existed in
English. Link the other two back from here. Three projects, one method, each vouching for
the others.

## Suggested edit

Add one section — *Why translate this way* — between *What this method refuses to do* and
*How to check us*: refusals, then the reason the work is attempted at all, then the
invitation to check it. Everything before it stays as written.

Acta is the weak one of the three; its note is at `~/acta-sanctorum/NOTES-methodology-page.md`.
