## Findings

### F1. 0011, col. 1040A — class 2 (who suffers what) — Latin itself inverted; English faithful but unmarked
- **Latin:** `Quae est autem ista nigredo? persecutio plane, quae infertur filiis ancillae; quae ideo a perversis ... putatur nigra`
- **English as it stands:** `Plainly persecution, which is inflicted on the sons of the bondwoman;`
- **Proposed English:** `Plainly persecution, which is inflicted on the sons of the bondwoman [cj: *ancillae*; read *liberae*, "of the free woman"];`
- **Why:** the whole passage (1040A–C) has the sons of the BONDWOMAN persecuting the sons of the FREE woman (*filios ... civitatis illius quae per Saram ... signatur, persequuntur filii alterius civitatis, quae per Agar ancillam*; *filios liberae persequuntur filii ancillae*); the blackness is the Bride's, i.e. Sara's line. As printed, the persecution is suffered by the persecutors. The English renders what Migne prints (correct under Pattern 7), but a reader meets a sentence that inverts the allegory with no signal. Real word, faithful English misleads → Pattern 18 shape. If the census closure (18a) bars a new `[cj:]`, at minimum a crux.
- **Confidence:** probable (that the sense requires *liberae*; the plate reading itself is not in doubt from our file).
- **Already in the cruces?** (see reconciliation below)

### F2. 0011, col. 1039B — Pattern 8 (punctuation), in a passage ABOUT punctuation
- **Latin:** `ut vel hoc modo proferatur: *Exsultabimus et laetabimur in te; memores uberum tuorum super vinum.* Vel hoc modo: *Exsultabimus et laetabimur in te:* et hic subdistinctione facta, subinferatur, *memores uberum tuorum super vinnm; recti diligunt te.*`
- **English as it stands:** (a) `*We will exult and we will rejoice in thee, mindful of thy breasts, better than wine.*` and (b) `*mindful of thy breasts, better than wine [sic: vinnm], the right love thee.*`
- **Proposed English:** (a) `*We will exult and we will rejoice in thee; mindful of thy breasts, better than wine.*` (b) `*mindful of thy breasts, better than wine [sic: *vinnm*]; the right love thee.*`
- **Why:** both printed semicolons are turned into commas. Wolbero is explicitly discussing where the *subdistinctio* falls, and the plate's marks are the evidence of how Migne's editors set the two readings; Pattern 8 forbids replacing them. (Also: the `[sic:]` content lacks the italic asterisks the pattern shows; cosmetic.)
- **Confidence:** certain (as to the change of marks).
- **Already in the cruces?** (see below)

### F3. 0011, col. 1040A — class 2/3 (subject shifted), minor
- **Latin:** `quam se dicit Ecclesia, sive unaquaeque sancta habere anima, ... cum hanc ipsam recta consideratione non nigredinem sed formositatem potius decernat.`
- **English as it stands:** `whereas right consideration judges this very thing to be not blackness but rather beauty.`
- **Proposed English:** `whereas she, by right consideration, judges this very thing to be not blackness but rather beauty.`
- **Why:** *recta consideratione* is ablative; the subject of *decernat* is the Church/soul who "says she has" the blackness. The English makes an abstraction the judge and loses that it is the Bride herself who reverses her detractors' verdict (which is what *Nigra sum sed formosa* says).
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** (see below)

### F5. 0013, col. 1046B — Pattern 7a (word rendered as something it does not mean), class 3
- **Latin:** `humanam creaturam ad eam gloriam de qua gesta fuerat per Dei gratiam assurgere videns et invidens`
- **English as it stands:** `seeing and envying the human creature rise by the grace of God to that glory from which it had been cast down,`
- **Proposed English:** `seeing and envying the human creature rise by the grace of God to that glory from which it [cj: *gesta*; read *dejecta*, "cast down"] had been carried,` — or, if no `[cj:]` is to be added, `from which it had been carried` with the conjecture in a crux.
- **Why:** *gesta fuerat* means "had been borne/carried", not "had been cast down"; "cast down" is the sense the passage wants (the fallen angels envy man rising to the glory THEY lost), silently supplied. It also leaves "it" binding naturally to "the human creature" in English, so the page now says MAN had been cast down from the glory he is rising to; in the Latin, *gesta* (fem.) can take *pars illa angelorum* as subject, which is the whole point of the envy.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** (see below)
