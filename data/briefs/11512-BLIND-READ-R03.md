## Findings

### F1. 0018, col. 1065D (and 0020, cols. 1071B–C): *proportior* marked `[sic:]` once, rendered unmarked twice
- **Class:** 5 (marker / consistency within the work; bears on a public claim against the plate)
- **Latin:** 0018 *tantumque proportior est veritas figura*; 0020 *una altera proportior est juxta excellentiorem*; 0020 *Castitas namque virginalis proportior est castitate viduali*; also 0021 *in eo proportior esse*.
- **English as it stands:** 0018 `and the truth is so much greater [sic: *proportior*] than the figure`; 0020 `one is greater in proportion than another` and `For virginal chastity is greater in proportion than widowly and conjugal chastity`.
- **Proposed:** make the work speak with one voice. The form recurs four times in three chunks (cols. 1065D, 1071B, 1071C, and 0021), always as a comparative "more proportionate / greater in proportion", which is Wolbero's own coinage, not a broken sort. A compositor does not repeat the same misprint four times over six columns. Recommend withdrawing the 0018 `[sic:]` and rendering `and the truth is so much greater in proportion than the figure`, matching 0020. If the plate-read at 0018 stands, then 0020's two sites (and 0021's) need the same marker. Either way the current split is incoherent.
- **Why:** a `[sic:]` accuses Migne's type. Frequency across columns is evidence for the author's word here, and the 0020 translator already treated it as a real word.
- **Confidence:** probable (the inconsistency itself is certain).

### F2. 0018, col. 1069A (lemma VERSE 9) and 0019, col. 1069D: *monilia* rendered "jewels", against the fixed ruling "necklaces"
- **Class:** 5 (lemma wording; brief's fixed rendering)
- **Latin:** 0018 *collum tuum sicut monilia*; 0019 *turtur scilicet et monilia* … *materialis in monilibus*.
- **English as it stands:** 0018 `*Thy cheeks are beautiful as the turtle-dove's, thy neck as jewels.*`; 0019 `namely the turtle-dove and the jewels, so that` and `for the beauty in the turtle-dove is natural, in the jewels material.`
- **Proposed:** 0018 `*Thy cheeks are beautiful as the turtle-dove's, thy neck as necklaces.*`; 0019 `namely the turtle-dove and the necklaces, so that`; `for the beauty in the turtle-dove is natural, in the necklaces material.`
- **Why:** the brief fixes *monilia* "necklaces". The very next chunk (0021) quotes the same lemma as "thy neck as necklaces" and expounds "the necklace". The lemma is rendered two ways within two columns: within-work drift.
- **Confidence:** certain.

### F3. 0016, cols. 1057A–1058C: Bride/Bridegroom capitalized where Migne prints lower case
- **Class:** 2-adjacent (fixed convention: capitals exactly where Migne capitalizes *Sponsus/Sponsa*)
- **Latin:** *Sponsam igitur suam sponsus, hoc est Christus*; *inter sodales sponsi qui sint veri*; *Sponsus ergo sponsam severius alloquitur*; *Dicit itaque Sponsus sponsae*.
- **English as it stands:** (a) `The Bridegroom, then, that is, Christ, chiding his Bride,`; (b) `to discern among the companions of the Bridegroom who are true`; (c) `The Bridegroom, then, addresses the Bride more severely`; (d) `The Bridegroom, therefore, says to the Bride, that is,`
- **Proposed:** (a) `The bridegroom, then, that is, Christ, chiding his bride,` (*sponsus* lower case; *Sponsam* has only a sentence-initial capital); (b) `to discern among the companions of the bridegroom who are true`; (c) `The Bridegroom, then, addresses the bride more severely`; (d) `The Bridegroom, therefore, says to the bride, that is,`
- **Why:** the other five sites in my range follow the plate exactly (0015 *sodales sponsi* "companions of the bridegroom", 0016 *vos sponsus vester* "your bridegroom", 0019 *sponsus sponsam* "the bridegroom … the bride"). These four do not.
- **Confidence:** certain for (b)–(d); probable for (a) (sentence-initial *Sponsam* is ambiguous).

### F4. 0016, col. 1056D: *diluerit* "washed", where the sentence requires *dilexerit* "loved"
- **Class:** 1 (a real word rendered faithfully, but the English asserts something else; Pattern 18)
- **Latin:** *quod tantum ejus naturam Deus diluerit, ut pro ea redimenda sanguinem suum fundere dignatus sit*
- **English as it stands:** `that God so washed his nature that he deigned to shed his blood to redeem it`
- **Proposed:** `that God so washed [cj: *diluerit*; read *dilexerit*, "loved"] his nature that he deigned to shed his blood to redeem it`
- **Why:** *tantum … ut* + blood-shedding is John 3:16's frame (*sic Deus dilexit … ut*). "So washed … that he shed his blood" inverts cause and effect, since the washing follows from the blood. It reads plausibly, so it is not caught.
- **Confidence:** probable.

### F5. 0018, col. 1067A: *causa divina* "the cause divine", where the argument requires *diversa*
- **Class:** 1 (Pattern 18: the English asserts the opposite of the argument)
- **Latin:** *sunt ergo sibi contraria sicut equitatus, sic ipsius equitatus et affectio et causa divina: affectio enim in una parte est dilectionis, in altera invidiae; causa pro qua pugnatur in una parte vita aeterna, in altera transitoria.*
- **English as it stands:** `so both the affection of that company of horsemen and the cause divine:`
- **Proposed:** `so both the affection of that company of horsemen and the cause divine [cj: *divina*; read *diversa*, "diverse"]:`
- **Why:** the colon clause explains that each side's cause *differs* (eternal vs transitory life). Calling the devil's side's cause "divine" says the reverse of the point being made.
- **Confidence:** probable.

### F6. 0016, col. 1059C: *judicem se continent* "hold themselves a judge"
- **Class:** 3 (a garbled clause rendered literally, but the sense is lost with no marker)
- **Latin:** *Unum namque sunt et judicem se continent Vetus et Novum Testamentum*
- **English as it stands:** `For the Old and New Testament are one, and hold themselves a judge,`
- **Proposed:** `For the Old and New Testament are one, and hold themselves a judge [cj: *judicem*; read *invicem*, "contain each other"],`
- **Why:** the wheel-within-a-wheel exposition just made the point that each Testament contains the other (*rota in medio rotae*). As it stands, "a judge" is an unexplained non-sequitur.
- **Confidence:** probable.

### F7. 0017, col. 1063C: *de hoc nomine extollente se* "of this name exalting itself" (read *homine*)
- **Class:** 2 (who: the subject is Antichrist the man, 2 Thess 2:3–4 *homo peccati … qui extollitur*)
- **Latin:** *Nam de hoc nomine extollente se supra omne quod dicitur Deus*
- **English as it stands:** `For of this name exalting itself *above all that is called God,`
- **Proposed:** `For of this name [cj: *nomine*; read *homine*, "man"] exalting itself *above all that is called God,`
- **Why:** 2 Thess 2 speaks of the *homo peccati*, and the next chunk (1063D) calls him *ille … homo damnatus*. "Name" misleads a reader about who exalts himself.
- **Confidence:** probable.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **0015 1054C** *nec … potuit vel poterit* rendered "neither could nor can": the future *poterit* is flattened to the present. The negation and the sense stand, and the parallel *habuit et habebit* ("has had and will have") is kept. Tense nuance only, not reported. (If touched: "neither could nor will be able to be had".)
- **0015 1056C** *divina se deferente gratia* "divine grace betaking itself away": *se deferre* can bear "betake oneself (off)", and *deserente* is a conjecture. Logged as crux only, which is right.
- **0015 1055D** *ut sit ei necesse vagabundam circuire*: `[cj:]` with *ne* is already present, and the English carries it. OK.
- **0016 1058C** *ignorantia suae injustitiae malum incidens* "by ignorance of herself": *suae* could agree with *injustitiae* ("her own injustice"). Either way the sense is unchanged. Rejected.
- **0016 1059D** *attendere … attendere* twice: two different constructions, not a doubling under Pattern 11. Rejected.
- **0016 1060A** *Domine virtutum* "O Lord of hosts": the standard Douay rendering, not a conformation of a word Migne prints differently. Rejected.
- **0017 1061B** *intenta mente cogitur* "is gathered": literal for a probable *cogitatur*, and the English does not mislead into a false claim. Rejected.
- **0017 1062C** *sed ipse et certitudine* "but it itself is also separated by the certainty": probably *spe et certitudine*, rendered literally and harmless. Rejected.
- **0018 1065C** *antiqui Dei circumcisio* "circumcision of the ancient God": this does mislead, but the cure is a missing word (*populi*), not a one-word swap, so `[cj:]` fits poorly. It is logged as a crux. Worth an adjudicator's glance.
- **0018 1065A** *Triumphato … equitatu … esset intratura*: an anacoluthon after *sic ipsa … liberanda, post …*. The English keeps the break ("so she … after the observance of the divine law. Triumphed over at last …"). A faithful rendering of a broken sentence. Rejected.
- **0020 1071C** *dum eis solius professionis sit meritum* "since to them belongs the merit of this profession alone": *eis* could be read as widows and wives, which would invert Apoc 14. The Latin is just as ambiguous, and "this profession" points back to the virgins. Rejected.
- **0020 1074B** *quasi immeritus repellerentur* (plural verb with a singular adjective) "as though an undeserving man, they were being repelled": the English mirrors the discord. Rejected.
- **0014** the page number *37* moved to the end of its sentence in the English, and *45* likewise in 0016: page anchors fall under the ruling. Not reported.
