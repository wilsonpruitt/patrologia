### F1 — 0022 @1079B — class 1 (polarity, English ambiguity) — probable
- **Latin:** `Haec ergo septem in radice nardi naturalia possunt non absurde accipi haec quae posuimus ex septiformi gratia procedentis charitatis praeconia`
- **English as it stands:** `can not absurdly be taken as these praises`
- **Proposed:** `can, not absurdly, be taken as these praises`
- **Why:** *non* negates *absurde*, not *possunt*. "can not absurdly" reads naturally as "cannot absurdly", i.e. as a negated modal, inverting the claim that the seven properties CAN be so taken. Commas remove the ambiguity without changing a word.
- **Confidence:** probable (the words are right; the risk is the parse).

### F2 — 0022 @1078B — class 5 (the gloss rests on a word; Pattern 18 candidate) — probable
- **Latin:** `*Charitas enim gratia* est, quia omnibus praeponderat virtutibus`
- **English as it stands:** `*For charity is grace*, because it outweighs all virtues;`
- **Proposed:** `*For charity is grace* [cj: *gratia*; read *gravis*, "heavy", the root's first property (*radice gravi*, 0021 @1078A)], because it outweighs all virtues;`
- **Why:** The paragraph walks the seven properties of the nard root listed at 1078A in order (*gravi, crassa, brevi, nigra, fragili, folio parvo densoque, aristas*); every other property is taken up by name (*Crassa… Brevis… Nigra… Fragilis… folio parvo… In aristas*), and "outweighs" (*praeponderat*) is the gloss of *gravis*. The printed *gratia* is a real word, so literal "grace" stands, but the reader cannot see that the gloss explains "heavy"; without the first property the "seven" at 1079B counts to six. Only if the cruces have not already carried this.
- **Confidence:** probable (conjecture itself near-certain; whether it warrants `[cj:]` is the editor's call).

- **Latin:** `Magnum plane hujus intellectus donum, quo quidem talem sibi vivendi constituit modum, ut et Deo et hominibus placeat et hoc sit [1088C] verae innocentiae signum Deum diligere et proximum, quod est universae sanctitatis perfectivum.`
