### F1 — 0031, col. 1105C–D — class 1 (word rendered as a different real word; faithful English misleads)
- **Latin:** *Quid autem dilecti est adjuvare, nisi praeceptorum [1105D] suorum decretali conscripto obligare?*
- **English as it stands:** `And what is it of the beloved to help, but to bind by the decretal writ of his [1105D] precepts?`
- **Proposed English:** `And what is it of the beloved to help [cj: *adjuvare*; read *adjurare*, "to adjure"], but to bind by the decretal writ of his [1105D] precepts?`
- **Why:** The sentence glosses the lemma *Adjuro vos* (the gloss "to bind by decree" is a definition of adjuring, and the next sentence expounds *per capreas cervosque*, the terms of the oath). Wolbero's own parallel at 0055 prints *Sed quid est meum adjurare? profecto…*. As rendered, the page says the beloved "helps", a verb the lemma never has. Real word, so `[cj:]`, not `[sic:]`.
- **Confidence:** probable (plate not read by me; could be our file's *v/r*).
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes: cruces-0030.md, 1105D. Plate p. 559 confirms *adjuvare*; logged "Crux only". So the defect is Migne's, and my point is the disposition: the same range promoted 1109C *obedientiae* to `[cj:]` on the same test (the faithful English asserts something false). Here the English has the beloved "help" in a gloss of *Adjuro*. Recommend `[cj:]` for consistency within the work.

### F2 — 0031, cols. 1106D, 1106D–1107A, 1107A — class 1 (same shape: *admiratio* for *adjuratio*, three times)
- **Latin:** (a) *Denique hujus admirationis talis est vis, qua constringuntur filii obedientiae*; (b) *Ipsi quoque subjecti hujus admirationis verbum inter se invicem [1107A] suscipere debent*; (c) *Vox, inquit, praedictae admirationis non est vox alicujus prophetae*
- **English as it stands:** (a) `Finally, the force of this admiration is such,` (b) `The subjects themselves also ought to receive the word of this admiration among one another` (c) `*The voice,* she says, of the aforesaid admiration is not the voice`
- **Proposed English:** (a) `Finally, the force of this admiration [cj: *admirationis*; read *adjurationis*, "adjuration"] is such,` — (b) and (c) unchanged if one `[cj:]` is judged enough at the first occurrence; otherwise the same gloss at each.
- **Why:** Every one of the three sits in the exposition of *Adjuro vos* (vs 7): the "force … by which the sons of obedience are constrained" is the binding force of an oath, and at (c) the "voice of the aforesaid [adjuration]" is at once called *vocis adjurantis et quasi juridicialis* (1107B). Surrounding text prints *adjuratio* (1104D), *adjurantur* (1105C), *adjuramento* (1107A). "Admiration" gives the reader a concept the passage never discusses. Three identical occurrences may point to the 1630 edition's abbreviation rather than Migne's type, which is why this wants `[cj:]` (real word, sound type) and a plate/witness look before any `[sic:]`.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes: cruces-0030.md, 1106D/1106D–1107A, with 1107B noted. Plate confirms *admirationis*; "Crux only". Same recommendation as F1: the reader meets "admiration" three times with no signal, and 1109C sets the precedent for `[cj:]`.

### F3 — 0031, col. 1104D — class 5 (word added to a quoted lemma; minor, within-work drift)

### F4 — 0034, col. 1119A — class 1 (real word for another real word; faithful English misleads)
- **Latin:** *Debet enim unusquisque cultro spiritualis exercitii sacramenta vitiorum praecidere*
- **English as it stands:** `For each one ought with the knife of spiritual exercise to cut back the sacraments of the vices,`
- **Proposed English:** `For each one ought with the knife of spiritual exercise to cut back the sacraments [cj: *sacramenta*; read *sarmenta*, "the shoots"] of the vices,`
- **Why:** The whole sentence is vine-dresser's work (knife, hoe, root, watering, binding to the tree). *sarmenta* (vine-shoots) is what a pruning knife cuts; "sacraments of the vices" is nonsense that reads as a theological phrase. Real word, so `[cj:]`.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes: cruces-0030.md, 1119A. Plate p. 566 confirms *sacramenta*; "Crux only". Same recommendation as F1/F2 (`[cj:]`, per the 1109C precedent).

### F5 — 0034, col. 1117D — class 2 (subject shifted; minor)
- **Latin:** *Sed haec vox honestatis in exterioris hominis vultu et habitu constituta, tunc in Dei auribus sonat, quando hanc pro sola aeternae vitae gloria promerenda immutabiliter servat.*
- **English as it stands:** `sounds in the ears of God then, when it keeps it unchangeably for meriting the glory of eternal life alone.`
- **Proposed English:** `sounds in the ears of God then, when she keeps it unchangeably for meriting the glory of eternal life alone.`
- **Why:** *hanc* is the voice, so the voice cannot be the subject of *servat*; the unexpressed subject is the bride/soul being addressed throughout. "it keeps it" makes the voice keep itself. ("one keeps it" would also do.)
- **Confidence:** probable; low stakes.
- **Already in the cruces?** No.
