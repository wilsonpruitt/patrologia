### F1 — 0035, col. 1121D (class 2)
- **Latin:** *Quia ergo in Divinitatis contemplatione dilecta deficit, quasi jam avolantem dilectum diu consequi non potest, revocat*
- **English as it stands:** "Because, therefore, in the contemplation of the Divinity the beloved fails, and cannot long follow the beloved as though already flying away"
- **Proposed:** "Because, therefore, in the contemplation of the Divinity she who is beloved fails, and cannot long follow the beloved as though already flying away"
- **Why:** *dilecta* (the Bride) and *dilectum* (the Bridegroom) both come out "the beloved" in one clause, so the English says the beloved cannot follow the beloved. The translator's own solution eight sentences later (1122D, "him who is beloved and her who is beloved") fixes it. Who-does-what; the "she calls him back" that follows only half-rescues it.
- **Confidence:** probable (clarity defect, not a mistranslation).
- **Already in the cruces?** (see check below)

  - *Addendum after 0036:* the same *dilecta … dilectum* → "the beloved … the beloved" collision recurs at 0036 @1123B ("the beloved seeks the beloved") and @1125C ("The beloved, therefore, brings the beloved into the house"). There the following pronouns (she/him) disambiguate; @1121D is the worst of the three because the verb "fails" has no gendered pronoun before it. If adopted, treat as a work-level consistency decision rather than a one-site fix.

### F2 — 0036, col. 1126B (class 3)
- **Latin:** *id est Ecclesiae, quae eum generat, nutrit et informat ad honestatem monasticae vitae, quam ipse postmodum tanta virtutum excellentia praecellit, ut ejus exemplo et doctrina magis ipsa Ecclesia proficiat, quam ipse antea ejus eruditione profecerit*
- **English as it stands:** "which begets, nourishes and forms him to the honesty of the monastic life, which he himself afterwards so surpasses by so great an excellence of virtues, that by his example and doctrine the Church herself advances more than he had before advanced by her instruction"
- **Proposed:** "which begets, nourishes and forms him to the honesty of the monastic life; and her he himself afterwards so surpasses by so great an excellence of virtues, that by his example and doctrine the Church herself advances more than he had before advanced by her instruction"
- **Why:** the *ut*-clause compares him with the Church (*magis ipsa Ecclesia proficiat quam ipse … ejus eruditione profecerit*), so *quam … praecellit* most naturally takes *Ecclesiae* as antecedent: he comes to excel the Church that formed him. The English "which" binds to "the monastic life", the nearest noun, so he surpasses monastic life. (Latin is grammatically ambiguous; the result clause decides it.)
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** (see check below)

### F3 — 0038, col. 1130A (class 1/3, Pattern 18 candidate)
- **Latin:** *quia et deserit continentem et deseritur a continente: vero gratia mundus deseret se continentem, quia nullo modo potest accidere sui affectione ad animam sanctam*
- **English as it stands:** "but by grace the world will desert the one who contains himself"
- **Proposed:** "but by grace [cj: *vero gratia*; read *verbi gratia*, "for example"] the world will desert the one who contains himself"
- **Why:** the sentence is the worked example of *deserit … et deseritur* just stated (world deserts the continent man; the continent man deserts the world); *verbi gratia* is the formula that introduces it. Rendered as printed, "by grace" makes the author assert that divine grace is the cause of the world's deserting the soul, a theological claim he does not make (the stated cause is the *quia* clause, the soul's contempt for the world). Real words, faithful English, false assertion: Pattern 18's class. Plate should be checked first (could be our file's misreading of a contracted *v. gratia*).
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** (see check below)

### F4 — 0039, col. 1133A (class 3, Pattern 18 candidate)
- **Latin:** *quatenus per has determinatas partes insinuet quod quaecunque ultima, et de toto corpore boni munitissima fuerit virtus, haec sit charitatis atomus.*
- **English as it stands:** "that whatever virtue shall have been the last, and the most fortified of the whole body of good, this is the atom of charity."
- **Proposed:** "that whatever virtue shall have been the last, and the most fortified [cj: *munitissima*; read *minutissima*, "the smallest"] of the whole body of good, this is the atom of charity."
- **Why:** the whole passage is the powder image (*ad quantam subtilitatem quasi in pulverem … redegit*; *in minutissimum pulverem cribratur*, *Minutissimo … pulvere* at 1133C–D): the "last and smallest" part is what makes an *atomus*. "Most fortified" makes the smallest particle the strongest, which contradicts the image the sentence completes. A one-letter transposition (*muni-/minu-*). Plate check first.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** (see check below)

### F5 — 0039, cols. 1135A–B (class 3, mis-parse with inverted sense)
- **Latin:** *quia cum haec sit virtus, et res sacramenti ut invicem se diligendo, de unitate corporis Ecclesiae sint, quicunque [1135B] de eo participantur, quod nulli ad exitum, sed omni credenti proficit ad salutem, quicunque tantae societatis conformante et uniente Christo, vere participes non fuerint, Christum veraciter non sumunt*
- **English as it stands:** "because, since this is the virtue and the reality of the sacrament, that by loving one another they be of the unity of the body of the Church, whoever [1135B] partake of that which profits no one to his end, but profits every believer to salvation, whoever, Christ conforming and uniting them, shall not have been true partakers of so great a fellowship, do not truly take Christ,"
- **Proposed:** "because, since this is the virtue and the reality of the sacrament, that by loving one another all who partake [1135B] of that which profits no one to his end, but profits every believer to salvation, be of the unity of the body of the Church, whoever, Christ conforming and uniting them, shall not have been true partakers of so great a fellowship, do not truly take Christ,"
- **Why:** *quicunque de eo participantur* is the subject of *sint* inside the *ut*-clause (the *res sacramenti* is that all who partake be one body by mutual love). The English closes the *ut*-clause at "Church" and sets "whoever partake of that which … profits every believer to salvation" in apposition to the second *quicunque*, so the sentence now says that those who partake of the saving reality "do not truly take Christ": the reverse of the argument (the second *quicunque … non fuerint* is the negative class).
- **Confidence:** probable (the Latin is loose, but the English as it stands asserts the opposite of the passage's point).
- **Already in the cruces?** (see check below)

### F6 — 0040, col. 1136B (class 1, polarity: printed *non* that inverts the argument)
- **Latin:** *Nam quibus omnia cum illo donavit, quando omnipotentem non habent, nihil est quod in ipso et per ipsum non possint.*
- **English as it stands:** "For to those to whom he has given all things with him, when they do not have the Almighty, there is nothing which in him and through him they cannot do."
- **Proposed:** "For to those to whom he has given all things with him, when they do not [cj: *non habent*; read *nunc habent*, "now have"] have the Almighty, there is nothing which in him and through him they cannot do."
- **Why:** the English says what the plate prints, but the plate's *non* inverts the argument: the whole paragraph (1136A) says the soul joined to the Almighty shares his omnipotence *because* it has him (*cum omnipotens sit cui … copulatur*; *omnipotentiae … participes*). "When they do not have the Almighty, there is nothing … they cannot do" is the reverse, and *in ipso et per ipsum* in the same clause presupposes that they have him. *non*/*nunc* is the classic abbreviation confusion. Pattern 18 extension: a negation that asserts the opposite takes a gloss. Plate check first (our file vs Migne).
- **Confidence:** probable (that the *non* is wrong: near-certain; the exact repair *nunc* vs deletion: probable).
- **Already in the cruces?** (see check below)

### F7 — 0040, col. 1139B (class 1, a word pair rendered as printed but inverted)
- **Latin:** *aliud sobrietatis contra luxuriam, aliud avaritiae contra liberalitatem, vel si cujus virtutis, quae sibi opposito reluctatur vitio*
- **English as it stands:** "another of avarice against liberality,"
- **Proposed:** "another of avarice against liberality [cj: *avaritiae contra liberalitatem*; read *liberalitatis contra avaritiam*, "of liberality against avarice"],"
- **Why:** every other member of the list is virtue (genitive) against vice (accusative): humility/pride, chastity/lust, patience/anger, obedience/contumacy, sobriety/luxury; and the list closes *vel si cujus virtutis, quae … reluctatur vitio*. Only this pair is inverted, so the English makes avarice the virtue that wages the war. The next paragraph (1139B) lists *avaritia* among the vices to be repressed. The English faithfully carries a printed inversion that asserts something false; a transposition takes `[cj:]` (cf. E13). May be Wolbero's own slip rather than the compositor's: the gloss is still honest either way.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** (see check below)
