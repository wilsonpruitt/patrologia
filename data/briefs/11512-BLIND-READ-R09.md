### F1. 0056 col. 1195A and 0057 col. 1195D/1196A — lemma conformed: "as" added to *Labia ejus lilia*
- **Class:** 5 (Scripture/lemma; Pattern 7a″).
- **Latin (both sites):** *Labia ejus lilia distillantia myrrham primam.* (0056: in the VERS. 13 head; 0057: the re-quoted lemma opening the chunk.)
- **English as it stands:** 0056 `His lips are as lilies dropping choice myrrh.*` (in the VERSE 13 head); 0057 `*His lips are as lilies dropping choice myrrh.*`
- **Proposed English:** 0056 `His lips are lilies dropping choice myrrh.*`; 0057 `*His lips are lilies dropping choice myrrh.*`
- **Why:** Migne prints no *sicut/quasi* here (the Douay "are as lilies" has arrived in place of the printed text). The same verse head prints *sicut* for the cheeks and the English keeps "as" there; the work elsewhere drops "as" where none is printed (0049 "Thy lips are a dropping honeycomb"; 0057 VERSE 15 "His legs are pillars of marble"). Wolbero himself then adds the simile in the gloss (*Qui sunt quasi lilia* → "They are as *lilies*"), so the added "as" in the lemma also takes away the gloss's own step.
- **Confidence:** probable (small, but a real departure from the printed lemma).
- **Already in the cruces?** (checked after, see end)

### F2. 0058 col. 1200B — *tantum* bound to the wrong word ("God alone known")
- **Class:** 3 (mis-parsed grammar).
- **Latin:** *In illo antiquo Dei populo, in quo tunc tantum notus erat Deus, duas praecipue dignitates posuit Deus*
- **English as it stands:** `In that ancient people of God, in which God was then alone known, God set chiefly two dignities`
- **Proposed English:** `In that ancient people of God, in which alone God was then known, God set chiefly two dignities`
- **Why:** *tantum* restricts *in quo* (Israel was the only people that then knew God; cf. *Notus in Judaea Deus*). "God was then alone known" reads as "God alone was known", which is not what the Latin says.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** (checked after)

### F3. 0058 col. 1203B — accusative *conventum* turned into a subject with nothing to govern it
- **Class:** 3 (mis-parsed grammar).
- **Latin:** *Adhuc nunc conventum quasi ad quemdam montem Dei, currit quotidie fidelis populus prospicere in spe terram promissionis illius*
- **English as it stands:** `Still now the assembly, as it were to a certain mountain of God, the faithful people runs daily, to look forth in hope`
- **Proposed English:** `Still now, to the assembly as it were to a certain mountain of God, the faithful people runs daily, to look forth in hope`
- **Why:** *conventum* is accusative, the goal of *currit* (parallel with *quasi ad … montem*). The English leaves "the assembly" as a hanging nominative, so the sentence has two subjects and does not construe. (*Adhuc nunc* looks like a misprint for *Ad hunc*, "to this assembly". If the editor wants that, it is a crux or `[cj:]` question, but even the literal reading makes *conventum* the goal.)
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** (checked after)

### F4. 0059 col. 1206A — "toward" supplied: *quempiam moveri* made the beloved's motion toward someone
- **Class:** 2 (who does what to whom), with a silent-repair element (Pattern 7a).
- **Latin:** *Sed in hoc loco dilecti hujus abire, est misericordia bonitatis suae quempiam moveri, declinare quoque ejus est gratiae suae munere menti cujuslibet illabi.*
- **English as it stands:** `But in this place the going away of this beloved is to be moved toward someone by the mercy of his goodness, and his turning aside also is to glide into the mind of anyone by the gift of his grace.`
- **Proposed English:** `But in this place the going away of this beloved is for someone to be moved by the mercy of his goodness, and his turning aside also is to glide into the mind of anyone by the gift of his grace.`
- **Why:** the plate prints no *ad*. As printed, *quempiam* is the accusative subject of *moveri*: the someone is moved, not the beloved. The English supplies a preposition and so makes the beloved the one who moves. The later *ad quos moveatur … dilectus tuus* (1206C) does suggest that sense was intended (a dropped *ad*?). If the editor prefers it, the fix is to render literally and log the crux, or use `[cj:]`. It should not be supplied silently in the running text.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** (checked after)

### F5. 0060 col. 1206D — *descendere* where the argument needs "ascend": the English states the reverse of the argument, with no marker
- **Class:** 1 (a word printed as its opposite, rendered smoothly). This is a `[cj:]` candidate. The English faithfully renders the Latin as printed.
- **Latin:** *Quid nempe fuit dilectum quaerere, nisi ad eum per contemplationis virtutem ascendere? quia enim in sanctorum cordibus fides per dilectionem operatur, dilectio vero mentem semper ad superna trahit, bonum ergo operari est ad Deum mente descendere.*
- **English as it stands:** `to work good is therefore to descend to God in mind.`
- **Proposed English:** `to work good is therefore to descend [cj: *descendere*; read *ascendere*, "to ascend"] to God in mind.`
- **Why:** The whole paragraph pairs the soul ascending with God descending: *quaerere* = *ascendere*; love draws the mind *ad superna*; Moses *ascendit*, the Lord *descendit*; *quantum quisque ad Deum ascendit … tantum ad illum Deus descendit*. The *ergo* sentence concludes from "love draws the mind upward", so the soul must ascend. As it stands, the page says good work is the soul going down to God, and in the next sentence descending is God's move toward the soul. Under Pattern 18 as restated in 18a, this is an English that asserts something false. Plate check first: the error could be the TEI's.
- **Confidence:** probable (that the printed word is an error; the English is faithful to our Latin).
- **Already in the cruces?** (checked after)

### F6. 0060 col. 1208A — the rejoicing moved from the Bride to the Bridegroom
- **Class:** 2 (who does what).
- **Latin:** *nimirum vicem rependit obedientiae meae gratulabundae mihi decantans laudem dignationis suae*
- **English as it stands:** `truly he repays the return of my obedience, chanting to me, rejoicing, the praise of his condescension`
- **Proposed English:** `truly he repays the return of my joyful obedience, chanting to me the praise of his condescension`
- **Why:** *gratulabundae* is feminine (with *obedientiae meae*, or dative with *mihi*: "to me, rejoicing"), so the joy is the Bride's. The English puts "rejoicing" between "chanting to me" and its object, where it reads as the Bridegroom's. If the dative reading is preferred: `chanting to me in my rejoicing the praise of his condescension`.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** (checked after)

### F7. 0061 col. 1214A (VERSE 8 head) and 0062 col. 1214C (re-quoted lemma) — *una est matri suae* conformed to the Douay "the only one"
- **Class:** 5 (lemma; Pattern 7a″). Low weight.
- **Latin:** 0061 *Una est columba mea, perfecta mea: una est matri suae, electa genitrici suae.* (twice: verse head and *sicut sequitur* re-quote); 0062 *Una est matri suae, id est Jerusalem supernae, quia quae hic in terris una est in fide …, ibi in coelis una erit*
- **English as it stands:** 0061 `she is the only one to her mother` (occurs twice: head, and the re-quote at 1214A); 0062 `*She is the only one to her mother,* that is, to the Jerusalem above`
- **Proposed English:** 0061 `she is one to her mother` (both); 0062 `*She is one to her mother,* that is, to the Jerusalem above`
- **Why:** Migne prints *una*, not *unica/sola*. Wolbero's gloss turns on unity, not uniqueness: *Unde una?* … one faith and sacraments; "one in faith … will be one in the beatitude". Douay's "the only one" shifts the word the gloss explains. The same verse opens *Una est columba mea*, "One is my dove", and the English keeps that literal.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** (checked after)

### F8. 0062 col. 1217A — *plerumque* dropped
- **Class:** 4 (missing text).
- **Latin:** *cumque a divina contemplatione causa fraternae dilectionis, vel objectu propriae fragilitatis, plerumque recedens a lumine subtilis intelligentiae paululum defecerunt*
- **English as it stands:** `and when, withdrawing from divine contemplation for the sake of fraternal love, or by the obstacle of their own frailty, they have waned a little`
- **Proposed English:** `and when, often withdrawing from divine contemplation for the sake of fraternal love, or by the obstacle of their own frailty, they have waned a little`
- **Why:** The adverb has no English. It matters to the moon allegory: the saints' waning is a frequent, recurring thing, like the moon's phases.
- **Confidence:** certain (that the word is missing); the fix is minor.
- **Already in the cruces?** (checked after)

## Candidates considered and rejected

- **0060 *areola* rendered "little bed"** (the lead from my launch note). The orchestrator's correction is confirmed on reading: the word is *areola* (Ct 6:1), and Wolbero's gloss at 1207C turns on the diminutive (*Cur vero non aream, sed per diminutionem, dicit areolam*), so "little bed" is required there. 0056 renders the plural *areolae* (Ct 5:13) as "beds". That is a within-work difference, but it is justified, because only 0060 has a gloss that depends on the diminutive. Not reported.
- 0056 1193B *sine quo vel qua nihil est factum* → "without which nothing is made": *quo vel qua* is collapsed into a single "which". Harmless, since "which" covers both antecedents (Word, wisdom).
- 0056 1195A *superius praedicanti*: probably meant for *praedicati*. The English "proclaimed above" gives the evident sense and the form is a real word. Too slight to argue as `[cj:]`.
- 0057 1197B *secundo loco incorporea creatura rerum superiorum*: *incorporea* is printed twice, and the second is probably meant for *corporea* (the upper bodies, "nourishments of the inferior"). The English renders the print literally and asserts nothing clearly false. Worth an editor's glance, but not a finding.
- 0057 1196C Ps 103:24 *Quam magnificata sunt* → "How great are": the standard rendering of the Vulgate text Migne prints, not a divergence.
- 0059 1204B *attento adjutori* ("to the attentive helper"): probably meant for *auditori*. Rendered literally. Real word, and the English asserts nothing false, so no finding.
- 0059 1205C *Auribus quippe …*: probably meant for *Animus*, since the sentence needs a subject for *Deus est*. Rendered literally ("For by the ears … it is God"). Literal rendering of garbled type is fixed by ruling.
- 0059 1206C *quae pura gloria* etc.: fine.
- 0060 1207A *dilecta … respondit* → "the beloved fittingly answered": the same English noun serves for *dilecta* and *dilectus* in one sentence ("the beloved answered those desiring to seek the beloved"). The work's convention appears to be "the beloved" for both, and the feminine is recoverable from "as if she said". Not reported.
- 0060 1209D *Pax Christi* has a `[var:]`, and *dilecta* for *amica* is rendered "beloved": both handled.
- 0061 1213A *qui numerus … demonstrant* → "which numbers show": the plural verb follows the Latin *demonstrant*. Migne's own *Deest aliquid* note sits at this lacuna (`[cn: 3]`), and the English correctly carries no twin.

- 0062 1218A *in summorum spirituum … transeuntes vero* → "in of the highest spirits": garbled Latin rendered literally (fixed by ruling).
- 0059 1204B/0061 1212B: the same 1 Cor 2:9 quotation is rendered "them that love him" in one place and "those who love him" in the other. That is within-work drift, but each renders its own Latin faithfully. Trivial.
- 0058 1202A 4 Kgs 3:13–15 (*Deus* for *Dominus*; *te* lacking): the English follows Migne. 1202A John 1:33 *in Spiritu* (no *sancto*): the English follows.
