## Sacred markers — violating any of these fails the work

- **Column anchors** `[0181A]` (also bare `[1137]`): reproduce VERBATIM, in

- **Notes** `[n: ...]`: reproduce verbatim, contents untranslated — they are
  Migne's citations. Same count, same order. **Exception, Pattern 15:** a note
  whose content is editorial PROSE rather than a citation is translated and

- **`## ` heads**: translate the head text, keep the `## ` line structure 1:1.
- **Italics `*...*` and list `- ` lines**: preserve structure.
- **Defect markers, English-only** (patterns 11–13, read them before using any):

  that is the plate's own defect · `[ed: ...]` our own voice, sparingly, only

## §5 · PHILIP'S RULED VOCABULARY — settled on 11638, do not re-decide

This is the merge cost of the previous work paid in advance. Sixteen places had
to be swept on 11638 because five of six agents collapsed a distinction; you are
being handed the answer so that it does not happen twice.

- **`tomus` = "Tome" · `liber` = "book".** The work's divisions are *tomi*; the
  Song itself is the *liber*. The decisive text on 11638 was *Ad quartum tomum
  exponendum hujus libri accedentes* — collapse the two and it reads "the fourth
  book of this book". Migne glosses it himself: *Explicit primus tomus, id est
  primum volumen*. Capitalized as a locator, Pattern 2.
- **`INCIPIT` = "HERE BEGINS" · `EXPLICIT` = "Here ends".**
- **Pattern 3, the standing name is "Holy Scripture"** — both words capitalized,
  for *divina*, *sacra* and *divinae Scriptura(e)* alike. ⚑ On 11638 the sweep's
  pattern list matched *divina* and *holy* and **missed *sacra* entirely**;
  check for all three forms in your own range.
- **The colour triple, kept strictly distinct:** *nigra* = **black** · *fusca* =
  **swarthy** · *formosa* = **comely**. *fusca* is never "black".
- **`charitas` = charity**, never "love" (the doublet *charitas, sive dilectio*
  forces the split) · **`clementia` = clemency** kept distinct from
  **`misericordia` = mercy** · *moralitas* = "the morality" · *cellaria* =
  **storerooms** (vs *cella vinaria* = wine cellar) · *recti* = "the upright" ·
  *adolescentulae* = **maidens** · *torcular* = winepress · *anaglypta* and
  *scalio* stay italic Latin.
- ⭐ **`amica mea` (Cant. 1:8) = "my friend", NOT "my love".** Ruled from *Caput*
  V, where Philip glosses the word and proves it from John 15:14's *amici mei*.
  **The lemma recurs at Cant. 2:10, 2:13, 4:1, 4:7, 5:2 and 6:3** — all in later
  ranges, so almost every remaining agent will meet it. Match it.
- ⭐ **`muraenula` (Cant. 1:10) STAYS LATIN, italic.** *Caput* VIII's whole
  argument is that the word names a **fish** — *Muraenula piscis est… cum eumdem
  piscem aureum vel argenteum esse dicit* — and the chapter runs tail-to-mouth →
  ring → net-mesh → chain → Moses' head-and-tail offering. "Chains of gold"
  makes seventeen paragraphs explain nothing. This follows 10804's precedent,
  which `translation-style.md` names as correct for this very word. 17 instances
  in chunks 0030–0035; **the lemma recurs later and every range must match it.**
- ⭐ **`vermiculatas argento` IS translated ("vermiculated")** — the companion
  call, decided the other way on purpose, because English carries the whole gloss
  chain: vermiculate → vermilion → little worm → *Ego autem sum vermis*. Italic
  Latin is reserved for words with no English exponent; this one has one.
- ⭐ **`emissio` = "emission"**, not Douay's "plants" — set on eight occurrences
  of the root at Cant. 4:13; ranges from 0090 on inherit it.
- ⭐ **`vicus` = "lane", `platea` = "street"** — kept distinct, against Douay.
  Set in chunk 0051 and matched in 0060–0065; *platea* is load-bearing at 0318D.
- ⭐ **`lectulus` = "little bed", `lectus` = "bed"** — kept strictly distinct.
  0279B's whole humility argument turns on the diminutive, and the lemma recurs.
- ⭐ **`cubile` = "couch"** — ruled here to close a live drift: cruces-0060 set
  "couch", cruces-0066 rendered it "bed" eleven columns later. **"Bed" is not
  available**, because *lectus* already holds it and *lectulus* holds "little
  bed"; a third word collapsing into the pair would void the diminutive argument
  above. Remaining sites: chunks 0082, 0083, 0115.
- **`ferculum` stays italic Latin** (17 sites), on the *muraenula* precedent.
- ⭐ **`cancelli` = "lattice"**, for both the lemma and the architectural sense.
  0312D identifies the two (*in rotundum aedificans **cancellum** voluit
  nominare*), and "chancel" would sever the argument. Expensive to reverse late.
- ⚑ **Pattern 3 fires on a BARE *in Scripturis* too** (0260A — no *divina*, no
  *sacra*). That is the exact form 11638's sweep missed. Check for it.
- ⭐ **Cant. 1:5 prints *nigra sum* where the Vulgate has *fusca sim*** — the
  colour word AND the mood both changed, at 0230D. Philip builds four columns of
  *nigredo* exposition on it and the whole *nigra sed formosa* architecture. Mark
  `[var:]`, never conform. **If you meet *fusca* at this verse, that is itself a
  reportable inconsistency in Migne** — say so.
- **`candidatio` is TRANSLATED, not carried** ("a whitening"). Italic Latin is
  reserved for technical words with no clean English equivalent; the arguments
  run whiteness → purity → virginity and the Latin left standing makes the
  inference unavailable to an English reader.
- **Pattern 17: `thou` = singular, `you` = plural.** It is a NUMBER marker, not
  a reverence marker.
- **Migne's lower-case after a misplaced full stop STANDS.** Ruled on 11638 and
  explicitly not to be churned: Pattern 9 normalizes only where English has no
  exponent for the Latin defect, and lowercase-after-a-stop has one.

## §7 · THE TYPOGRAPHY — 11632 divides its marks BY FUNCTION, not by book

⭐ **Settled by wave 1 (chunks 0000–0017), three agents converging independently.
Do not re-derive it and do not go looking for a seam.**

11638 changes how it marks scripture partway through, and there the change is a
**book boundary**: italics in Books I–II, guillemets « » from Book III. **11632
is not built that way.** It runs both marks in parallel from chunk 0000 onward,
divided by FUNCTION:

- **Guillemets « » carry A QUOTATION ADDUCED AS AN AUTHORITY.** ⚠ **Waves 1–2
  first stated this as "« » = scripture", and that narrower form is WRONG** —
  three range files still carry it. From col. 0251D Migne guillemets the Delphic
  oracle twice and a line of Virgil (*« Dii, si qua est pietas coelo »*), where
  the prologue's Horace was italic; at 0254B he guillemets two bare pronouns
  displayed as words (« Meos », « tuos »). Scripture is the commonest case, not
  the rule.
- **Italics `*…*` carry everything else** — the running Song lemma under
  exposition, classical verse quoted as verse rather than adduced as proof
  (Horace in the prologue), book titles, words discussed as words, printed
  locators (one sits *inside* a guillemet run at 0203A), and Migne's marginal
  source-edition page numbers (*101* onward).

⚠ **The widened rule is PERMISSIVE, not predictive.** It tells you what a « »
may contain; it does NOT predict that an adduced authority will be guillemeted.
At 0314A Philip adduces **Sallust** explicitly as an authority — *verax
sententia… ethnicorum* — and Migne prints it in neither guillemets nor italics,
where the Delphic oracle and Virgil ARE guillemeted at 0251D–0253A, and an Ovid
case at 0259 runs the same way. **Reproduce what is printed. Never add a mark
because the function seems to call for one.**

⚑ **Where the two functional rules COLLIDE, the verse rule wins — and the plate
says so.** Classical **verse** adduced as **proof** is the one case where §7's
two halves pull opposite ways. At 0399B Horace (*Epist.* I.xvi.52) is adduced as
an explicit authority and Migne prints it in **italics**, with the verse rule
governing. Note this is not a fourth counter-instance: the three below are
printed *bare*, which is a different thing from being italicised.

⚑ **And it is not that Migne declines to guillemet pagans.** At 0337B Philip
adduces **Ambrose by name** — *Excursus ejus a patre, ait Ambrosius, recursus
ejus ad patrem* — unmarked. Three counter-instances now, one of them a Father.
There is no predictive rule to find here; there is only the plate.

⚑ **The book boundaries are CROSSED and carry no seam — confirmed twice.**
`LIBER SECUNDUS.` at col. 0246C and `LIBER TERTIUS.` at col. 0303A; both marks
run identically on either side of each. Those are the places 11638's model
predicted a switch, and it does not happen at either. **The question is closed:
translate later book boundaries as ordinary text.**

⚑ **Migne's `[n:]` is USUALLY inside the closing » but not always** — it prints
outside at 0235B and 0239B, and at 0232D and 0236D he prints a bare locator
inside a run with no `[n:]` wrapper at all. Reproduce what is printed.

**The proof is that the Song ITSELF takes guillemets when quoted as a proof-text
rather than as the lemma** — Cant. 4:11 @0220A, Cant. 5:11 @0226A, Cant. 2:14
@0226C, each guillemeted with the italic lemma a few lines away.

⚠ **So an italic run in this work is NOT evidence of a pre-seam regime — it is
probably just a lemma.** If you nonetheless meet a genuine change of practice at
an *Explicit/Incipit*, report it as an exception with the column named. **Do not
tidy anything in either direction.** Under Pattern 6 a « » is a mark Migne PRINTS
and is reproduced 1:1; a `" "` is ours.

⚠ **This work has no *tomi*.** Its divisions are *liber* = book, *caput* =
chapter, *volumen* = volume. So the *tomus*/*liber* collapse that cost 11638
sixteen sweep sites cannot arise here in that shape — but §5's rule still governs
if a *tomus* turns up in a later range.

⭐⭐ **AND ONE MEMBER OF THE CLASS IS A DELETION, WHICH IS WORSE.** John 19:39 at
0399D prints *ferens misturam myrrhae et aloes **libras centum*** for the
Vulgate's ***quasi** libras centum* — no guillemets, no italics, no note. Twelve

numero** consummatum*: his whole four-sentence argument needs the hundred to be
**exact**, and what has gone is the Vulgate's hedge.

Every other member of this class **substitutes** one reading for another, and a
substitution at least leaves a strange word on the page for you to notice. **A
deletion leaves nothing at all.** Nothing on the plate can signal it, no
extraction channel can reach it, and the sentence reads perfectly. When you run
the prose pass, ask not only "is this word wrong?" but **"is a word missing?"**

**So the class is "an expounded quotation Migne leaves unmarked," full stop.**
Some members carry a note and appear in your list; some carry nothing and cannot
appear in it by any mechanical means whatever. That is the strongest available
argument for §8's separate prose pass, and it is why that pass is mandatory
rather than advisory.

Logging it costs nothing and is reversible; marking it is neither. The merge

## §9 · Negation fidelity — read `translation-style.md` 7a before you start

Every `non`, `nec`, `neque`, `nisi`, `haud`, `nunquam`, `nullus`, `nihil` the
Latin prints appears in your English, and none appears that the Latin does not
print — **including when carrying it makes the sentence contradict itself.** That
contradiction is usually a defect in Migne's plate and is exactly what this
edition exists to show.

**Never translate a word as its opposite, or as anything other than what it
means, to rescue a sentence that will not construe.** If it will not construe,
render it as closely as the printed words allow and log the crux. A repair hidden
inside the translation leaves no trace, passes every automated check, and is the
one error class nobody downstream can find.

Scripture policy generally: translate what Migne prints, never substitute a
conventional version. Garbled type: render literally, log it (column + reading +
your note).

## §10 · Known false positives — do NOT report these

Each has been investigated and settled; re-raising one costs a session and, when
acted on, has twice nearly broken working data. If you believe you have a genuine
exception, say so **as an exception** and give the evidence named below — never
report it as a fresh discovery.

1. **Column-band arithmetic.** Migne's A–D marks are positional quarter-guides
   transcribed where they appear on the plate — **not four per column**. So
   `0350A → 0351A` "missing B–D", a D-band mark followed by an A-band mark, or a
   recurring column number, are all normal; two works can interleave by band
   within the same columns. Arithmetic over band letters manufactures phantom
   gaps — 49 were examined corpus-wide and none was real. **The only real signal
   is text broken mid-word or mid-construction across an anchor.** Report that
   and nothing else in this class. No `[ed:]`, no patch, no plate check.
2. **An `Ibid.` resolving to the "wrong" antecedent.** Anaphora resolves against
   the printed sequence at index time and corrections apply upstream of it, so
   "fixing" the antecedent makes the *Ibid.* inherit the corrected key and
   resolve **wrong**. Never propose a correction to a reference a following
   *Ibid.* depends on.
3. **`noteCount` off by one against the body.** The frontmatter `heads[]` can
   hold a duplicate; corpus-wide there are 0 real mismatches. Count `[n:]` in the
