## What to hunt, in order of yield
1. **Polarity.** A `non / nec / neque / nisi / haud / nunquam / nullus / nihil / absque / sine` the Latin
   prints and the English drops, or one the English adds; a word rendered as its opposite to rescue a
   sentence. ⚠ **In a penitential this is the costliest class:** a tariff or an exception clause turned
   round still reads like law. Read *si … si non*, *nisi*, *exceptis*, *absque* with care.
2. **Numbers and quantities.** Every tariff: *dies / hebdomadas / menses / annos / hiemes /
   quadragesimas*, and every numeral. Check each against its Latin. A dropped "or ten", a *septem* read as
   *septuaginta*, days turned into years: none of these is visible to any check.
3. **Mis-parsed grammar.** Wrong subject or object (who fasts: the man, the woman, the priest?), a
   relative on the wrong antecedent, active vs passive, a conditional's protasis and apodosis swapped,
   a subjunctive of command read as a statement.
4. **Missing or added text.** A clause, canon or list item in one column and not the other. Count
   sentences per paragraph on both sides.
5. **Received scripture wording arriving OVER the Latin.** The English giving the familiar verse where
   Migne prints something else.

**Not defects, do not report:** em-dashes; column-band arithmetic or anchors that step backwards;
`(cont.)` heads; literal renderings the Latin forces; the fixed vocabulary above; plain rendering of
sexual matter.
