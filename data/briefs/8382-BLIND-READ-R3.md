## Findings

### F1 — 0008 · 0429B · class 3 (mis-parse: *ipsi* dative, not nominative)
- **Latin:** `et amici ipsius, cum in vivis esset, eum redimere possunt, et ipsi remissionem apud Deum obtinere per pia servitia, et per possessiones suas mundanas.`
- **English as it stands:** `and themselves obtain remission with God through pious services, and through their worldly possessions.`
- **Proposed:** `and obtain remission for him with God through pious services, and through their worldly possessions.`
- **Why:** the eighth remission is the dead man's (*cum quis ex hac vita decedit*); the friends redeem *him* and obtain remission *for him*. Read as nominative, the friends win remission for themselves, which changes whose sin is remitted. This Latin uses *ipsi* as a dative singular twice in the same passage (*prouti ipsi praescripserit* 0429B; *cum Salvator ipsi diceret* 0429C), and the English renders both as "for him / to him". The form is ambiguous, so this is a parse call, not a certainty.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in cruces:** see "Cruces check" below.

### F2 — 0010 · 0434D · class 3 (*pro his* attached to the wrong verb; *assistere* as complement of *pertimescat*)
- **Latin:** `sed potius pro his ante tribunal districti judicis assistere rationem redditurus pertimescat.`
- **English as it stands:** `but rather let him greatly fear for these, as one who is to stand before the tribunal of the strict judge to render an account.`
- **Proposed:** `but rather let him greatly fear to stand before the tribunal of the strict judge, to render an account for these.`
- **Why:** *pertimescat* governs the infinitive *assistere* ("dread to stand"); *pro his* goes with *rationem redditurus* ("about to render account for these [sins]"). "Fear for these" reads in English as anxiety on the sins' behalf, and "as one who is to stand" turns the infinitive into a participle the Latin does not have.
- **Confidence:** probable.

### F3 — 0010 · 0434B · class 3/4 (subject dropped in the English apodosis)
- **Latin:** `Si quis excitatur et contristatur, et irascitur adversus eum qui ad orationem illum excitavit, [cn: c | …] excommunicari debet, et non manducare …`
- **English as it stands:** `as above, chap. 25.] ought to be excommunicated, and not to eat until`
- **Proposed:** `as above, chap. 25.] he ought to be excommunicated, and not to eat until`
- **Why:** the Latin construes (the subject of *debet* is the *si quis*); the English main clause has no subject and does not construe. Not a Latin-forced literalism. Compare XXX in 0009, which does supply the subject.
- **Confidence:** certain (minor).

## Candidates considered and rejected
- 0008 *reliquos dies jejunet contra* → "let him fast in turn": obscure Latin, carried literally; no better reading that isn't conjecture.
- 0008 *quas quam optime noris* → "which thou knowest as best thou mayest": slightly loose ("as best thou knowest them"), but no change of sense.
- 0008 *ipsi … praescripserit* → "just as he shall have prescribed for him": fine; the confessor is the implied subject.
- 0008 *diebus Solis* → "on Sundays": standard, not a defect.
- 0009 *vel suspendatur* → "suspended": fixed vocabulary by ruling.
- 0009 *si hoc frustra fecerit* → "if he did this to no purpose": the Latin probably renders OE "by accident" (Theodore has *casu*), but *frustra* is printed and literal rendering is correct under Pattern 7.
- 0009 *ad vesperam* → "until evening": matches the Theodoran source (*usque ad vesperum*); acceptable.
- 0009 *quoniam mater illorum contigit ea, quae haec opponit*: does not construe; carried literally by rule, and Wilkins's note is rendered.
- 0009 *nisi certa et maximi officii sui necessitate* → "except by a certain necessity, and that of his greatest duty": wordy, but the sense is kept.
- 0010 `[n: (Matth. I)]` for Matt 12:36 is Migne's mis-citation; the English keeps the printed locator, which is correct.
