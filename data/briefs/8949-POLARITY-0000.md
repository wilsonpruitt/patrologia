## Findings

### F1 — chunk 0007, col. 0199D — likely dropped `non` in Migne (Migne's defect)

Latin as printed:

> Flagellari jubet scribas Hebraeorum, **paleas non dari**, opus exigi; … servis autem Dei etiam
> victus parvus et humilis non succedit. **Hae sunt enim paleae quae dabantur a Pharaone**

English (literal, faithful to the plate):

> He commands the scribes of the Hebrews to be scourged, **straw not to be given**, work to be
> exacted; … but to the servants of God even a small and lowly sustenance does not come. **For these
> are the straws which were given by Pharao**

**Why the neighbour contradicts it.** Two sentences earlier the same gloss states that Pharaoh's
order is *paleas non dari* — straw withheld. The immediately preceding clause is likewise negative:
the servants of God do not even get a small and lowly sustenance. The sentence that follows then
identifies that withheld sustenance with the straw — but as printed it asserts the straw *was*
given, reversing the very point the neighbour makes. Origen's sense requires a negation:
*Hae sunt enim paleae quae **non** dabantur a Pharaone.* Direction is Migne's, not ours: the English
renders the plate literally and adds nothing. (Residual doubt: the imperfect *dabantur* could be
stretched to "used to be given [and now are not]", but that reading fights both neighbours, and the
gloss's whole point is deprivation.) Adjudication + crux note recommended; no English repair needed
beyond whatever convention the edition uses for a supplied negative.

## Low-confidence candidates (reported, not asserted)

- **0010, col. 0207D — `quamvis de Filio non tacuerunt`** ("although they were not silent concerning
  the Son"), the tail of an *usque ad* excerpt from Aug. *Quaest.* 25. The surrounding glosses
  (Isid., Strab.) make the magicians' *Digitus Dei est hic* a confession of the **third** person, the
  Spirit, with the Father named as *Deus*; on that logic the Son is precisely the one **not** named,
  which would want *quamvis de Filio tacuerunt*. Unverifiable from the text in range because the
  neighbour clause is inside the elided excerpt. Migne-direction if real; the English is literal.
- **0007, col. 0198B — `Gens Israel valet super nos, utinam et nos sentiat valere super se`**
  (chunk 0007's neighbour reads `ut scilicet … non suscipiam sed jacula ignita scuto fidei repellam`).
  The devil's wish and the explanatory *ut*-clause pull in opposite directions. Reads as compressed
  Origen rather than a reversed particle; English is literal. Flagging only for the record.

## Rejected candidates — and why

- **0000, 0183A `corporalis noster exitus de Aegypto spirituali`** — a genuine sense-reversal
  (*corporalis* for *spiritualis*), but it is **already marked in the English with an inline `[cj:]`**
  conjecture. Known, not new.
- **0009, 0202B `qui est Deus benedictus in saecula`** — the plate's quotation drops *super omnia*,
  which the very next sentence (*non tantum Deum, sed Deum super omnia esse memoravit*) requires. A
  real self-contradicting neighbour, but the English **already carries a `[var:]` note** supplying
  the Vulgate *qui est super omnia Deus*. Known, not new.
- **0006, 0197D `non est ostium apertum a Domino`** ("there is no door opened to thee by the Lord") —
  the whole passage is about mouths being opened (*os tuum aperuit Deus* in the next clause), so
  *ostium* is almost certainly a misread of *os tuum*. Lexical/OCR, not a polarity reversal; out of
  scope for this sweep but worth a separate note.
- **0010, 0209A `Aegyptii abominantur oves et eas adorant`** — reads as a contradiction, but the
  sense is intelligible as printed (they abhor slaughtering/eating sheep *because* they revere them
  as Jupiter Ammon); the strain is in the conjunction *et* for *quia*, not in a negation or a
  reversed word. English renders it literally.
- **0010, 0207C `dicentes: Digitus Dei est hic, ne intelligant unde decipiantur`** — the final clause
  sits oddly on a confession, echoing the sciniphs gloss at 0207A (*ut deceptus nec videat nec
  intelligat unde decipiatur*). Looks like compression/borrowing rather than a flipped particle;
  no candidate word to reverse.
- **0009, 0204B `post haec nec intrat, sed accersitus accedit`** → "he neither enters in, but being
  summoned he approaches". *nec* = *non* here; the English "neither … but" is clumsy but the sense
  (he does **not** enter) is preserved. Style, not polarity.
- **0005, 0194B `ubi per vitam morte consumpta`** → "where, death being swallowed up by life". Checked
  because the agent/patient could be inverted; the ablative absolute is right as translated.
- **0004, 0193B `Non exibitis`** — a truncated lemma of Ex 3:21 *non exibitis vacui*, not a stray
  negation.
- **0004, 0192B `quia prava itinera semel captos tanto delectabiliter tenent, quanto quod libuerit,
  non licet`** — strained as printed, but the English is literal and the comparative logic can be
  read (the forbidden pleases the more). No decisive neighbour.
- **0008, 0201C `in illis septuaginta quinque`** ("seventy-five") against *septuaginta animae* at
  0183C — LXX/Acts 7:14 figure vs Vulgate figure, a source variance, not a translation defect.
- Negations deliberately spot-checked and found **intact** in both directions (these are where a
  silent drop would show): 0002 `Ego autem confidenter dico, non vivificant feminas`; 0002 `non enim
  propterea domus fit, quia timetur Deus`; 0003 `nisi projecti fuissent pueri, non fuisset servatus
  Moses`; 0004 `Sed et flamma rubum non comburit`; 0005 `Quis autem nisi insciens crediderit …
  quod Deus nolit`; 0010 Ps 18 `Non sunt loquelae … quorum non audiantur voces eorum` (double
  negative preserved); 0010 `Certe nunc non dictum est, Ingravatum est cor Pharaonis`.

## Note on yield

One live finding in eleven chunks is below the rate a lemma-and-gloss commentary usually gives, so
the claim needs justifying. This range is unusually **excerpt-heavy**: a large share of it is
`etc., usque ad …` abbreviations of Augustine, Gregory and Rabanus, where Migne prints only the head
and tail of a sentence and the contradicting neighbour is physically absent from the page — the very
structure this check depends on is missing. The continuous stretches that do survive (Origen hom.
1–4, the Isidore allegories) were read in full and are, on the evidence, sound; the negation
spot-checks above show the English is carrying Latin negatives rather than smoothing them. Chunks
richer in continuous exposition later in the book should be expected to yield more.
