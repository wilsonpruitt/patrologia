## Finding 1 — chunk 0025, col. 0251B — OURS (sense-reversing mistranslation of `quia`)

**Latin:** *De idolis vero, «quia nil est idolum in mundo»; de similitudinibus autem non addidit,
quia nil sunt.*

**English as published:** "But of idols, *that an idol is nothing in the world;* of likenesses,
however, he did not add it, **because they are nothing**."

**Why the neighbour contradicts it.** The whole gloss that immediately follows exists to distinguish
idol from likeness on exactly this point: *«Qui vero facit quod non est … non similitudinem, sed
idolum facit: facit enim quod non est»* — the idol is of a thing that does not exist; *«Similitudo
vero est cum aliquid ex his quae sunt vel in coelo, vel in terra, vel in aquis formatur»* — a
likeness is of a thing that DOES exist. So likenesses are precisely *not* nothing; that is the
argument. The Latin says the Apostle did **not** add of likenesses **that** they are nothing
(late-Latin `quia` + indirect statement, exactly as in the parallel first member which the
translator rendered correctly two words earlier: "of idols, **that** an idol is nothing in the
world"). Rendering the second `quia` as causal "because" asserts the opposite of the paragraph.

That the translator knows this idiom is shown one column earlier at 0250C, where
*«non negavit quia sint»* is correctly rendered "he did not deny **that** they are." This is a
local lapse, not a convention.

**Suggested English:** "…of likenesses, however, he did not add **that** they are nothing."

## Finding 2 — chunk 0026, col. 0253C — MIGNE'S (sense-reversing letter: *injustus* for *justus*), unflagged

**Latin:** *Aiunt haeretici quod non boni Dei sermo sit, qui pro peccatis alterius alterum dicat
plecti; Deum tamen legis justum dicunt, **nec possunt ostendere quomodo injustus sit**, si alium
alio peccante punit.*

**English as published (literal, correct policy):** "…yet they say that the God of the law is just,
and they cannot show **how he is unjust**, if he punishes one when another sins."

**Why the neighbour contradicts it.** Migne prints this Origen passage twice; the doublet stands
three paragraphs later at col. 0254B and settles the sense: *«Sed qui Deum legis mandantem haec
dicent non bonum, justum tamen dicunt: nec ipsi quidem possunt ostendere **quomodo justitiae
videatur convenire** si alio peccante alius puniatur»* — "nor can they show how it should seem
agreeable to **justice** if, when one sins, another is punished." The argument is that the heretics
concede the God of the law is *just* and then cannot make that concession good; showing him
*unjust* is what they *want*, not what they fail at. The first member's *injustus* should be
*justus*.

Direction: Migne's defect, correctly rendered literally in the English. But it carries no `var`/`cj`
note and, so far as this blind read can tell, is not marked as a crux — the reader meets a
self-contradicting pair of paragraphs with no signal. **Recommend a note, not an emendation.**

## Secondary observations (not polarity; flagged for the orchestrator)

**A. Chunk 0029, col. 0261A — silent repair.** Migne prints *«quinque boves pro uno bove, et
quatuor oves **pro uno bove** redduntur»* — "pro uno bove" twice. The English reads "five oxen for
one ox, and four sheep **for one sheep**." The second member is silently corrected (Ex 22:1 and the
gloss's own arithmetic — ox five uses, sheep four — require *pro una ove*). Correct in substance,
but repaired without a note.

**B. Chunk 0024, col. 0249A — Migne polarity defect, already handled.** *«ut videntes non videant,
et audientes **nos** intelligant»* (Isa VI) — *nos* for *non*, a sense-reversing letter that undoes
the second limb. Rendered literally and carried with an in-text `var` giving the Vulgate
*non intelligant*. No action needed; recorded here to show it was seen.

**C. Chunk 0025, col. 0249D — *liberalitatis* for *libertatis*.** *«Judaea et Hierusalem, domus
liberalitatis»*, rendered "the house of liberality," where every neighbouring sentence (four
occurrences of *domus libertatis* / *mater libertatis* within eight lines) requires *libertatis*.
Not a polarity reversal, so left as a Migne oddity rendered literally; worth a note if the volume
is annotating this class.

## Rejected candidates (each looked at, each cleared)

1. **0022, 0243C** — *«alius vero, id est, Judaicus populus, **non** elevat manus Moysi a terra, nec
   altum aliquid nec sublime in eo intelligit»*: three negations in a row, all present in the
   English, and the neighbour (the Gentile people who *does* lift them) confirms polarity. Clean.
2. **0022, 0242C** — *«Antequam manducaret panem de coelo … **non** dicitur pugnasse populus»*
   against *«exspecta pugnam, praepara te ad bellum»* four lines later. The "not" is genuinely
   Origen's (fighting begins only after the manna), not a dropped particle. Clean.
3. **0023, 0245D** — *«Sciebat quoque Moyses quia hunc populum lex audiret, et faceret quaecunque
   diceret»*: reads backwards at first (the law hearing the people), but *populum* is accusative,
   *lex* nominative, and the parallel at 0246D — *«audiret eos lex faciens omnia quae dicerent»* —
   confirms the law is the hearer. English correct. Rejected.
4. **0023, 0246D** — *«Non enim potest facere lex sicut Judaei dicunt»* + *«nihil potest secundum
   litteram»* + *«Nihil enim ad perfectum adduxit lex»*: a dense negation cluster, all three
   preserved and mutually consistent. Rejected.
5. **0025, 0252B** — *«nil ultra in te lascivum videre, nihil dissolutum vel prodigum potest»*
   ("he can see nothing further in thee that is wanton"). Reads oddly after "he marks all thy
   motions," and a dropped *non*/*nec* would be easy here. But the English tracks Migne exactly;
   whatever is wrong is Migne's or Rufinus's, and it is not a divergence. Rejected as ours; too
   weak to log as Migne's — the jealous-husband sense ("he can bear to see nothing wanton") is
   recoverable.
6. **0026, 0255A** — the Isidore adultery question: *«aliquando moechetur qui **non** concupiscit
   uxorem proximi … Aliquando vero concupiscat, **nec** ei misceatur poenam timens»*. Both halves
   turn entirely on their negations and both survive intact in the English, with the payoff
   *«quod utraque peccata sunt»* consistent. This was the single highest-risk sentence in the
   range and it is correct. Rejected.
7. **0026, 0255B** — *«Non quod omnis qui concupiscit rem proximi, furatur; sed omnis qui furatur,
   rem proximi concupiscit»*: an asymmetric pair where dropping the first *non* would still read
   fluently. Both members correct in the English. Rejected.
8. **0026, 0255A** — *«non putandum est fieri contra hoc praeceptum quod lex occidit»* on *Non
   occides*: negation-on-negation, correct. Rejected.
9. **0026, 0256A** — *«non ut Deo noti fierent … quem non latebat, sed ut inter se et
   sibimetipsis»*: correct, and the *sed* clause confirms the polarity. Rejected.
10. **0028, 0260D** — *«Sed hoc de carnibus occisi pecoris fieri non potest, quo non vescuntur»*:
    two negations, both rendered; the sense (they do not eat such flesh) is coherent with Augustine's
    part-for-whole point. Rejected.
11. **0028, 0259B** — *«quaedam peccata ad damnum pertinent, non tamen ad interitum … quaedam
    peccata esse ad mortem, quaedam non»*: intact and mutually confirming. Rejected.
12. **0030, 0262D** — *«Per nomen suum jurare permittit, **non** quod bonum sit jurare … **non**
    vult ut jurent per nomina exteriorum deorum»*, closing *«Sic et hostias **non** approbat Deus,
    mavult tamen sacrificari sibi quam idolis»*: three negations carrying a permit/forbid argument
    that would invert on any one of them. All present. Rejected.
13. **0030, 0264D** — *«quae nec simul, nec uno tempore in sanctis exstinguuntur»* and
    *«relinquunt minora, et permittuntur dominari, ne per elationem gravius corruant»*: correct;
    the Pauline sting-of-the-flesh neighbour confirms. Rejected.
14. **0031, 0265D** — *«sicut de his praeteriri quidquam non oportet: sed omnia quae mandavit …
    committere necesse est»*: the English inverts the surface ("just as nothing ought to be passed
    over, so it is needful to commit all things") but preserves both polarity and the *sicut…sed*
    correlation. Rejected.
15. **0032, 0267C** — *«quae minus quam inter duos haberi non potest»* → "cannot be had among fewer
    than two": double-negative construction, rendered correctly. Rejected.
16. **0032, 0269B** — *«quae non cognoscitur exterius nisi, soluto carnis domicilio»*: the
    `non … nisi` pair intact, and the nut-and-shell neighbour confirms. Rejected.
