## Findings

### 1. 0033, col. 0272C — *altera* for *latera* (Migne's defect, rendered literally, unflagged)

Latin (STRAB., on VERS. 14): *Pelles vero rubricatae vel ianthinae, tectum tantum operiebant,
**nec ad altera declinabant**.*
English: "But the reddened or violet skins covered the roof only, **nor did they slope down to
the other parts**."

The neighbour settles it, and it is a doublet inside the same gloss: two sentences earlier
*ut ad **latera** descenderent*, one sentence earlier *saga autem tectum et **latera** operiebant*.
The whole gloss is built on the roof/sides contrast, so *altera* is *latera* with one letter
changed. "The other parts" is a faithful rendering of what Migne prints, so the translation is
not at fault — but with no var note a reader cannot see that the sentence has lost its contrast.
**Direction: Migne's. Wanted: a var note (or `[sic: altera]`).**

### 2. 0038, col. 0283D — *aere* for *auro* (Migne's defect, rendered literally, unflagged)

Latin (VERS. 3, the lemma being Ex 30:3 *Vestiesque illud auro purissimo*):
*Apte vero illud **aere**, etc.*
English: "Fitly moreover it **with brass**, etc."

Two contradictions, both immediate. (i) The lemma the gloss is attached to prescribes *auro
purissimo*. (ii) The gloss directly above it, on the same verse, is: *Apte etiam altare thymiamatis
**quantum metalli fulgore praecellebat**, tanto quantitate minus erat* — the incense altar's whole
point in this passage is that its metal outshines the brazen altar of holocaust of ch. XXVII
(*operiesque aere*, 27:2). An incense altar overlaid with brass erases the comparison the
neighbouring sentence is making. **Direction: Migne's. Wanted: a var note.**

### 3. 0038, col. 0285C — *videant* for *videantur* (Migne's defect; English preserves the nonsense unflagged)

Latin (ISID., on VERS. 35): *…orationes fidelium significat, quamvis quatuor elementa haec quatuor
**videant** significare: ut thus, quod lucidum est, aeri comparetur…*
English: "…signifies the prayers of the faithful; although these four **may see** the four elements
to signify: so that frankincense, which is bright, may be compared to air…"

The English sentence is not English, because the Latin is not Latin: an active *videant* has no
possible subject here. The continuation settles the sense — *ut thus … aeri comparetur, stactes
aquis, galbanus et onyx terrae et igni* — the four ingredients are what *seem to signify* the four
elements, i.e. *videantur*. This is the active-for-passive form of the single-letter class.
The literal rendering is defensible policy, but it is silently unmarked, so the defect reads as our
incompetence rather than Migne's. **Direction: Migne's. Wanted: a var note or `[sic: videant]`.**

### 4. 0043, col. 0295A — *annuns* silently repaired to "rings" (ours)

Latin: *In tabernaculo autem Domini erant tabulae, quarum **annuns** erant infixi vectes de lignis
sethim…*
English: "…there were boards, in whose **rings** were fixed bars of sethim wood…"

*annuns* is Migne's garble of *annulis*. The English is certainly right about the sense — but it
repairs the text without a mark, and the English in this very range otherwise never repairs a
Migne garble silently: four are marked with the house convention — `[sic: Sicuttibi]` (0035),
`[sic: *uque ad*]` (0038), `[sic: iu]` (0041), `[sic: Isael]` (0041) — and a fifth, *snpra* for
*supra* at 0038 col. 0283C, is carried through verbatim, unrepaired. *annuns* is the one place the
English quietly corrects instead of preserving, which a reader cannot detect. Not a sense reversal, but it is the silent-
repair class. **Direction: ours. Wanted: `[sic: annuns]`.**

## Rejected candidates — named, with the reason

- **0038, col. 0283D–0284A, *sed spe percipiendae mercedis*.** *…nec palam protestari audent …
  sibi repositam esse mercedem: cuncta tamen agunt intentione bene placendi Deo, **sed** spe
  percipiendae mercedis.* The adversative *sed* with nothing negated before it looks like a dropped
  *non* ("not with hope of the reward"). **Rejected:** the crown round the altar *is* the reward in
  this gloss, so the point is that the reward-hope is present in the lesser sort even though they
  dare not boast of it; *sed* reads as "but nonetheless". Adding a negation would delete the
  crown from the very people the gloss says have it.
- **0038, col. 0285D, *erat intra sancta sanctorum*.** Factually the incense altar stood outside the
  veil. **Rejected:** Heb. 9:4 places the *thuribulum aureum* in the Holy of Holies, and Augustine
  is repeatedly read that way; this is very likely Augustine's own text, not a Migne slip.
- **0036, col. 0279A, *usque ad custodia mentis valeant prolabi*.** Reads as a positive where sense
  wants *ne a custodia mentis valeant prolabi*. **Rejected:** this is an *usque ad* tail, and the
  governing *ne a* falls outside the words the editor quotes. Genre artifact, not a dropped negation.
- **0040, col. 0288C, VERS. 2 *Mittam praecursorem*.** *Cum his non possum esse propter te, ut
  vindicem et puniam; nec propter eos, ut visitem et confortem* looked like the two halves might be
  swapped. **Rejected:** it is coherent as printed and as translated — no punishing, for Moses's
  sake; no comforting, for their own sakes — and the English preserves the order exactly.
- **0038, col. 0285C, *videant*** is reported above; its sibling *cujus odore fugantur serpentes*
  and *minus lignosum* were checked and are sound.
- **0036, col. 0280B, AUG. *demonstratio et veritas*** rendered "the doctrine and the truth"
  (Urim and Thummim = δήλωσις καὶ ἀλήθεια, "manifestation"). A loose word choice, **not** a polarity
  or sense reversal; noted only so the orchestrator knows it was seen.
- **0041, col. 0292D, *intra ejusdem fidei firmitatem, id est unitatem*.** Watched for the
  *veritatem/unitatem* pattern. **Rejected:** *unitatem* is what the gloss argues for (four Gospels,
  one crown), and the immediately following gloss on the bars says *Ecclesiae **unitatem**
  denuntient*. The doublet confirms *unitatem*.
- **0043, col. 0294C, BEDA on the soul's names.** *dum vult, animus est; dum scit, mens est*
  rendered "mind" / "understanding". Unusual pairing but internally consistent and not reversing.
- **0033/0034/0035/0039/0040/0041 negation sweep.** A token count of Latin negatives against English
  negatives was run per chunk as a backstop to the sense reading; the two chunks where English ran
  short (0036, 0038) were re-read negation by negation. Every *non / nec / nisi / nunquam / nullo /
  ne* in those chunks is carried into the English. The counts differ only because English needs more
  negative words to render one Latin one.

## Bottom line

Four sites, three of them Migne's own defects rendered faithfully but without the var note that
would let a reader see them, one a silent repair of ours. **No case was found in this range where
the English drops a negation the Latin prints, or translates a word as its opposite to smooth a
broken sentence** — the golden-calf and Moses-veil glosses (0039, 0041, 0043), which were the
flagged risk (b), carry their negations across intact, including the run of *nec … nec …* at
0288A–B and *nunquam sacramenta Scripturae valuit penetrare* at 0294D.
