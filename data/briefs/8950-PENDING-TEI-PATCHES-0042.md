## Part F — CONFIRMED NEGATIVES

- **0182C `Nos quosque moraliter instruit`** — archive p. 96 shows `quosque`, and **our twin
  carries it verbatim**. One of the very few defective-looking forms in this book the twin did not
  tidy. A real word (acc. pl. of *quisque*) in a slot that will just bear it, so Pattern 7's main
  rule governs, no marker fires, and the English reads "He instructs us each morally also."
  Migne's evident intention is *quoque*.
- **0176A `Aegyptii autem violenter … Hebraei vero violenter …`** — archive p. 93: both adverbs are
  there, well formed and normally spaced, so the sentence's collapsed contrast is Migne's. Rendered
  literally; no `[cj:]`, because two conjectures are open and nothing on the page chooses.
- **0182B `Si dicit aliquis quia bella timebant, sicut …`** — a protasis with no apodosis, archive
  p. 96. Rendered as printed. Nothing is missing from our text that Migne prints.
- **cols 0179–0180** — read line by line on the archive with no word divergence at all.
