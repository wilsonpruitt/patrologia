## How the ✓ / ⚠ marks were made, and what they do NOT mean

Mechanical phrase search of the normalized span against
`sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt` (all 73 books, one verse per line).

- **✓ followed by an ADDRESS** = this exact word sequence occurs, as whole words, in the
  verse(s) named. ⛔ Still **not clearance** — but now you can see WHERE it matched, which is
  the thing a bare tick used to hide. **Read the address.** If you are glossing Luke 2 and the
  mark says `✓ Lc 19:38`, the mark has just told you the lemma matches a different verse.
- **⚑ MATCHES ONLY ELSEWHERE** = the span carries a `VERS.` address, so it is the verse lemma
  proper, and it occurs in the Clementine — but **not in this book and chapter.** In a gospel
  commentary that is usually the harmony answering for the wrong evangelist. Measured on 9000:
  six spans scored a bare ✓ and every one was a real divergence found by a stint walking the
  line — `Quid cogitatis mala…` off Mt 9:4 inside a commentary on Lc 5:22, and `Gloria in
  excelsis Deo` at Lc 2:14 off Lc 19:38, the verse the gloss expounds two lines later.
- **⚠ NOT in Clementine verbatim** = the sequence was not found. Most of these will be
  innocent: a fragment cut mid-phrase, an `usque ad` abridgment span, a span carrying an
  editorial `etc.`, a Migne spelling. **A ⚠ is a place to look, never a divergence.**
- Single words are not searchable this way and are marked for checking in place.

## ⚠ THE COMPARAND ITSELF HAS DEFECTS — CHECK BEFORE YOU ACCUSE MIGNE

`clementine-flat.txt` is Tweedale's 2005 "Quasimodo" release and **the upstream
correction diffs published after it are deliberately NOT applied** (see
`sources/vulgate/README.md`). So a divergence can live on OUR side of the collation, and
a `[var:]` fired against it is a false public claim about Migne's text.

**Confirmed defects in this copy** (found by stints, both forms of the file affected):

    Is 22:18   `Coronas cornonabit te`   — read `Coronans coronabit te`

⛔ **The test is cheap and it is yours to run: if the brief's Clementine reading is not
itself sound Latin, the defect is in the comparand.** `cornonabit` is not a word. Say so,
collate against the sense, and do NOT fire a marker on it. Add any new instance to this
list. ⚑ The same rule applies at one remove to a form that IS sound but looks wrong: a
peer declined a `[sic:]` on Migne's `requiscere` because this file prints the syncopated
stem at the verse quoted — the form lives outside Migne's compositor.

⛔ **You still walk every line.** The marks order your work; they do not do it. A `[var:]`
is a public claim about another text and must rest on the file, cited by book chapter:verse —
never on this pre-scan and never on recollection.

## ⛔ THIS BOOK'S GUILLEMETS ARE UNBALANCED AND THE PLATE SAYS THEY ARE MIGNE'S

The inventory reports **9 unbalanced-guillemet line(s)** and about twenty more turn up on a
raw scan of the Latin: a `VERS. n.--` lemma that closes with `»` but never opened, or opens
with `«` and never closes. **Three of them were read at the plate on 2026-09-07 and all three
are Migne's own setting, faithfully carried by our text** (Gallica `bpt6k5505319w`, left-column
native crops, no gutter crossed):

    0767B  f384  `VERS. 20.-- De mane usque ad vesperam. »`        no opening « on the plate
    0781A  f391  `VERS. 4.-- « Quis restitit ei et pacem habuit.`   no closing » on the plate
    0819C  f410  `VERS. 5.-- Ut sciam verba quae, » etc.`           no opening « on the plate

In each case the lemmata immediately above and below on the SAME page are normally delimited
(`VERS. 4.-- « Os meum replebo. »`, `VERS. 6.-- « Nolo multa fortitudine contendat. »`), so this
is compositor's inconsistency, not a damaged page and not a Corpus Corporum corruption.

⛔ **Consequences, and they are the point of this section:**

1. **Do not fire `[sic:]`, `[cj:]` or a TEI patch on a missing guillemet.** The class is
   plate-confirmed at three sites in three chunks and three quires. If you meet a fourth, it
   is the same class until a plate read says otherwise — and a plate read is the only thing
   that may say otherwise.
2. **Do not take the marks as the lemma boundary.** Where the opening `«` is absent the lemma
   still runs from the `VERS. n.--` address to the closing `»`; where the closing is absent,
   read the sense and Migne's own italics. 7a″ applies to the lemma, not to the punctuation
   around it, and this inventory harvests by the marks — so a span here can be short or long
   at one end. **Count your own Latin against your brief's total anyway** (see below): the
   count is unaffected by which end is ragged.
3. This is a **guillemet book** (the lemmata are in `« »`, not italic). Do not carry Genesis's
   or Luke's convention over.
