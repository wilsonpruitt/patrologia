## ⭐ THE LAST THREE ARE THE POINT, AND THEY ARE THE SHAPE THE ORCHESTRATOR NAMED

`putenm` @0417A, `positus es` @0418A and `ut de eorum.` @0419A are sites where **Gallica — the
printing our TEI follows — carries the defect and the archive copy is clean.** Add the unclosed
`(VERS. 16.` @0425D, which Gallica prints and the archive does not, and this range has **four**
instances of the class.

Under the pre-correction heuristic (`putenm` is a non-word, therefore ours) the first of these
would have been filed here as a patch and applied. **It would have been wrong**, and it would have
moved our Latin off its own exemplar and onto the other printing, permanently and silently. All
four take no patch, no `[sic:]` and no `[cj:]`: they are rendered by sense or preserved as printed
and disclosed in `src/english/8963/cruces-0014.md` §3.2.

## What a later patch pass should NOT do with this range

- Do not "repair" `putenm` @0417A, `positus es` @0418A, the stop after `eorum` @0419A, the missing
  stop after `orituram` @0422B, the missing stop after `ignis` @0416C, or the unclosed paren at
  @0425D. Every one of them is Migne's own type or punctuation in the Gallica printing, verified
  against the corner numbers on both copies.
- Do not read the archive copy's readings in the table above as evidence about our file. They are
  that copy's ink and its press run, nothing more.
