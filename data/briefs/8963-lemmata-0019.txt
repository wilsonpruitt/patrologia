## How the ✓ / ⚠ marks were made, and what they do NOT mean

Mechanical phrase search of the normalized span against
`sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt` (all 73 books, one verse per line).

- **✓ followed by an ADDRESS** = this exact word sequence occurs, as whole words, in the
  verse(s) named. ⛔ Still **not clearance** — but now you can see WHERE it matched, which is
  the thing a bare tick used to hide. **Read the address.** If you are glossing Luke 2 and the
  mark says `✓ Lc 19:38`, the mark has just told you the lemma matches a different verse.
- **⚑ MATCHES ONLY ELSEWHERE** = the span carries a `VERS.` address, so it is the verse lemma
  proper, and it occurs in the Clementine — but **not in this book and chapter.** In a gospel
  commentary that is usually the harmony answering for the wrong evangelist. Measured on 9000:
  six spans scored a bare ✓ and every one was a real divergence found by a stint walking the
  line — `Quid cogitatis mala…` off Mt 9:4 inside a commentary on Lc 5:22, and `Gloria in
  excelsis Deo` at Lc 2:14 off Lc 19:38, the verse the gloss expounds two lines later.
- **⚠ NOT in Clementine verbatim** = the sequence was not found. Most of these will be
  innocent: a fragment cut mid-phrase, an `usque ad` abridgment span, a span carrying an
  editorial `etc.`, a Migne spelling. **A ⚠ is a place to look, never a divergence.**
- Single words are not searchable this way and are marked for checking in place.

⛔ **You still walk every line.** The marks order your work; they do not do it. A `[var:]`
is a public claim about another text and must rest on the file, cited by book chapter:verse —
never on this pre-scan and never on recollection.
