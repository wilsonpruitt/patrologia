## Site 1 — chunk 0008, col. 0085A. **Direction: OURS. Decisive.**

**Latin as printed:**

> VERS. 5.— *Tunc assumpsit.* Prima et ultima tentatio in deserto, media quoque historialiter,
> ultima fuit postquam regressus est Jerusalem. Quia Matthaeus non secundum ordinem historiae, sed
> secundum tentationem Adae prosequitur: Lucas vero ut res gesta est.

**English as it stands:**

> VERS. 5.— *Then he took him.* The first and the last temptation were in the desert, **the middle one
> also as history has it, the last was after he had returned to Jerusalem.** Because Matthew follows
> not the order of the history, but the order of Adam's temptation: whereas Luke follows the order in
> which the thing was done.

**The contradiction, in its own immediate neighbour.** The sentence says the last temptation was in
the desert and then, six words later, that the last was after he returned to Jerusalem. Both cannot
stand. This is exactly the shape test 3 is for: the English *contains* every word and every negative,
and still asserts what the next clause denies.

**What went wrong.** The construal, not the vocabulary. The English broke the clause at Migne's comma
after *historialiter* and made *ultima fuit* a new main clause. The Latin's own predicate is
**`media … historialiter ultima fuit`** — "the middle one was **historically the last**, after he had
returned to Jerusalem." Nothing needs emending; the words are all there and in order.

**Proof from the sentence's own structure, not from what reads better:**
1. The very next sentence states the principle: *Matthaeus non secundum ordinem historiae … prosequitur*
   — Matthew's sequence is **not** the historical one. A gloss that has just distinguished Matthew's
   order from the historical order is telling you which member moved.
2. The members fit: Matthew's first (stones → bread) and last (the mountain) are in the desert;
   Matthew's **middle** one is on the pinnacle of the temple **in Jerusalem** — which is why *postquam
   regressus est Jerusalem* attaches to it and to nothing else.
3. Confirmed three columns later at 0085D, on Matthew's third: *Haec tentatio ordine praecessit, sed
   narratur praepostero* — the mountain temptation came **earlier**. Historical order is therefore
   1st, 3rd, 2nd, i.e. Matthew's middle one is historically last. The gloss is internally consistent;
   only our English is not.

**Proposed English:**

> The first and the last temptation were in the desert; the middle one also was, historically, the
> last, after he had returned to Jerusalem.

(or: "…the middle one, however, was historically the last, being after he had returned to Jerusalem.")

Migne's comma after *historialiter* is typographic pointing, not a word of the text; the words as
printed do not need it to be construed correctly, and following it is what produced the contradiction.

## Site 2 — chunk 0005, col. 0077D. **Direction: MIGNE'S.** English is right; the defect is unmarked.

**Latin as printed:**

> Vel plorat: quia dum parit, **justitiam** habet unde dicitur: *Vos plorabitis,* etc., sed additur
> consolatio: *Tristitia vestra vertetur in gaudium* [n: (Joan. XVI)].

**English as it stands:**

> Or she weeps: because while she brings forth, **she has justice**, whence it is said: *You shall
> weep,* etc., but a consolation is added: *Your sorrow shall be turned into joy.*

**The finding.** *justitiam* is a plate defect for **`tristitiam`** — Jn 16:21, *mulier cum parit
tristitiam habet quia venit hora ejus*, which is the verse the clause is quoting and which the whole
sentence is built on. The plate hands us its own contradicting neighbour: the consolation quoted five
words later is *Tristitia vestra vertetur in gaudium*. "She has justice while she brings forth" does
not license "but a consolation is added"; sorrow does.

**The English is correct as it stands and must not be repaired.** It renders the printed word
literally, which is 7a's requirement — the edition exists to show this. What is missing is the record:
there is no `[sic:]` and no crux visible in the chunk. Recommended: keep "she has justice" and add a
crux (or `[sic: justitiam]` with the literal rendering retained per 7a⁗ — the rendering stays, the
marker is additional), noting the neighbouring *Tristitia vestra* as the decisive evidence and Jn 16:21
as the source. Frequency reasoning is worthless here (*justitiam* is a real word in a work saturated
with *justitia*); the adjacent citation is what decides it.

## Site 3 — chunk 0010, col. 0089A. **Direction: OURS. Lower confidence, but the English asserts a word that is not on the page.**

**Latin as printed:**

> *Videns autem Jesus. [n: (AUG.)]* Huic rei consentit Lucas … Sed Lucas dicit, etc., *usque ad* non
> enim omnes omnia possunt: quod verius putatur quamvis **idem** utriusque simul possit videri
> **factus**.

**English as it stands:**

> …which is thought the truer, although **the same day** may be seen to have been made by both at once.

**The finding.** *idem … factus* is masculine and has no expressed noun; the English supplies **"day"**,
unbracketed. Nothing in the Latin supports it, and the question Augustine is being cited on — Matthew's
sermon on the mountain against Luke's on the plain — is whether it is **the same discourse**, not the
same day. *sermo* is the natural masculine antecedent. The surrounding English uses square brackets
for its supplies elsewhere in this very range (`[Egypt]` at 0004 @0076A, `[came]` at 0002 @0069C,
`[was]` at 0001 @0067A), so an unbracketed noun reads as though it were printed.

**Proposed English:** "…although the same [sermon] may be seen to have been delivered by both at once,"
with the supply bracketed. If the elided *usque ad* material is thought to settle the referent
differently, the minimum repair is to drop "day" for a bracketed or neutral supply — the current text
asserts a fact about chronology that the printed words do not.

Flagged as lower confidence only because the clause sits inside an `usque ad` elision and the
antecedent is not on our page; the *absence of any warrant for "day"* is not in doubt.

## Candidates examined and REJECTED

These were formed as suspicions and then dismissed; naming them is part of the result.

1. **0000 @0064A** — *nolebant praefinitum numerum cessari* ("they were unwilling that the
   predetermined number should be brought to an end"). *nolebant* has no expressed subject and the
   clause is odd. **Rejected:** literal, no polarity movement, and the sense (the four are fixed by the
   power of the sacrament) is coherent with the preceding *nec recepta sunt*.
2. **0000 @0064B** — Migne gives *Marcus in leone, quia agit de resurrectione* and *Lucas in vitulo …
   de sacerdotio*, and later *leo in resurrectione*. Unusual assignment. **Rejected:** internally
   consistent across both lists and rendered literally; this is Migne's recension, not our conformation.
3. **0001 @0067A** — *ex capris et ovibus id est ex justis et peccatoribus* is crossed (goats↔just).
   **Rejected:** the English preserves Migne's own order rather than tidying it. Correct behaviour.
4. **0001 @0068B** — *dubitabat ne promissio Dei impleretur* → "was in doubt whether the promise of God
   would be fulfilled." With a verb of fearing, `ne` would give "feared that it would **not** be
   fulfilled." **Rejected as a defect:** *dubitare* is not *timere*, the English neither adds nor drops
   a negative, and the neutral rendering does not contradict the neighbour (*E contra credebat*).
   Noted only because it is the one place in the range where a `ne` was rendered non-negatively.
5. **0001 @0067C** — *Non quod solus in hac genealogia rex, sed quia primus rex* → "Not that he alone
   is king in this genealogy, but because he is the first king." A textbook 2a trigger (`non` + `solus`
   + `sed`). **Rejected:** the negative is correctly on *solus*, proved by the *sed* clause. Handled
   right.
6. **0004 @0073B** — *non ideo non plures quam tres fuisse probantur* (double negative) →
   "are not on that account proved not to have been more than three." **Rejected:** both negatives
   carried, and the *sed* clause that follows confirms the resulting sense.
7. **0006 @0079B** — *quod qui non facit, non modo virtutes non comprehendit, sed nec etiam supplicia
   fugit.* Four negatives in one sentence. **Rejected:** all four present and each on its own host
   (*facit*, *non modo … comprehendit*, *nec etiam … fugit*).
8. **0006 @0082B** — *Quia non exstinguetur neque exstinguet cruciatos, sed aeternaliter puniet.*
   **Rejected:** both negatives on the right verbs; the "neither shall it quench those it torments"
   reading (annihilation denied) is what the *sed aeternaliter puniet* requires.
9. **0008 @0086B** — *Nota diabolum in his vinci in quibus Adam vicit* → "the devil is conquered in
   those things in which he conquered Adam." Subject/object of *vicit* is genuinely ambiguous
   (*Adam* indeclinable). **Rejected:** the next sentence, *Quem de gula tentavit*, makes the devil the
   subject throughout. The English has it right.
10. **0010 @0089D** — *Mites sunt, non qui prorsus non irascuntur* → "The meek are not those who are
    not angry at all." **Rejected:** both negatives present and correctly nested.
11. **0010 @0090A** — *consolationem meretur et non alius* → "earns consolation, and no other does."
    **Rejected:** correct; the negative attaches to *alius*, not to *meretur*.
12. **0010 @0091C** — *nec terrae utilis est … nec sterquilinio quod non fecundare sinit.* Three
    negatives, easy to lose one. **Rejected:** all three present and attached.
13. **0002 @0071C, 0006 @0081D, 0010 @0092A** — the marriage/shoe-loosing/pinnacle passages, each of
    which reads awkwardly in English. **Rejected:** awkwardness tracks the Latin's own awkwardness in
    each case; no smoothing was done.
14. **Scripture lemmata across the range.** Spot-checked word by word for 7a″ conformation: *Ecce virgo
    in utero habebit* (0002 @0072A, **not** conformed to "shall conceive" — and the gloss's own
    *Pro concipiet, ponit: In utero habebit* depends on it), *Dabo ipsi Roboam tribum Judam*
    (0001 @0067D), *Fidelis Dominus in omnibus viis suis … ut gallina pullos suos* (0001 @0068C),
    *Rex injustus omnes ministros impios habet* (0004 @0074B), *Puer natus est nobis* (0004 @0075B),
    *fructus dignos* (0006 @0080B), *Mundans eos lavacro aquae in verbo vitae sanctificatae*
    (0006 @0082A), *Non veni solvere legem, sed adimplere* (0010 @0092C). **All follow Migne against
    the Vulgate, and each material divergence already carries a `[var: …]`.** This is the failure mode
    the genre predicts, and in this range it did not occur.

edition's citation apparatus is built on. Flagging for whoever owns marker policy on this work; a
uniform decision is better than four independent ones.
