## A. DECISIVE SITES

### A1. 0037 @0164C — *aliorum virtutes, aliorum vitia sublevare*: subject and object collapsed (OURS)

**Latin (VERS. 9):** `*Ne forte* unusquisque pro suis operibus mercedem suscipiet, nec possunt in die judicii aliorum virtutes, aliorum vitia sublevare.`

**English as it stands:** "nor can the virtues of some, **or the vices of some, lift up others** in the day of judgment."

The English makes *aliorum vitia* a second **subject** coordinate with *virtutes*, and
invents an object ("others") that the Latin does not print. *aliorum vitia* is
accusative and is the **object** of *sublevare*: the virtues of some cannot relieve the
vices of others. The whole paragraph is arguing exactly this — *quisque pro se rationem
reddet*, *nec alieno testimonio juvatur quis apud Deum*. As it stands the English says
that vices might lift people up, which is the opposite of the sentence's own argument,
and it says nothing about the mechanism the gloss exists to deny.

**Read instead:** "nor in the day of judgment can the virtues of some relieve the vices of others."

### A2. 0037 @0166A — *sine qua non sunt virtutes*: the relative bound to the wrong noun (OURS)

**Latin (VERS. 29):** `Sed qui in fide non habet etiam virtutes quas naturaliter habet, perdit sine qua non sunt virtutes.`

**English as it stands:** "But he who has it not in faith loses even the virtues which he has naturally, **without which there are no virtues**."

*qua* is **feminine singular** — it is *fides*, not *virtutes*. The English produces a
sentence that contradicts itself in six words ("the virtues … without which there are no
virtues") and quietly deletes the point, which is that faith is what makes the natural
virtues virtues at all. This is the contradicting-neighbour tell inside a single clause.

**Read instead:** "…loses even the virtues which he has by nature, without which [faith] there are no virtues."

### A3. 0033 @0151D — *Has Dominus pergens Jerusalem*: the object of *sublimat* taken from the wrong word (OURS)

**Latin:** `Bethphage, domus buccae. Bethania, domus obedientiae. Has Dominus pergens, Jerusalem sua praesentia sublimat, quia multos ante passionem docendo donis piae confessionis et obedientiae implevit.`

**English as it stands:** "**Making his way past these**, the Lord **exalts Jerusalem** by his presence, because before his passion he filled many by his teaching with the gifts of devout confession and of obedience."

The sentence's own *quia*-clause proves the host: what he fills men with is **confession
and obedience** — precisely the two etymologies just given for **Bethphage** and
**Bethania**. So *has* (acc. pl. fem., the two towns) is the object of *sublimat*, and
*Jerusalem* is the goal of *pergens*. The English has swapped them, and additionally
renders *pergens* as "making his way **past**", which drops the towns out of the honour
the sentence is conferring on them. Nothing is added or dropped; only the construal
moves — the shape the earlier readers flagged.

**Read instead:** "Journeying to Jerusalem, the Lord exalts these by his presence, because…"

### A4. 0040 @0173D — *Nihil tibi* expanded into the Vulgate verse (OURS, 7a″)

**Latin lemma (VERS. 19):** `*Nihil tibi.*`

**English as it stands:** "*Have thou nothing to do with that just man.*"

Migne prints two words. The English prints the whole of Mt 27:19's *Nihil tibi et justo
illi* from the remembered English Bible — words that are not on the page. Every other
lemma in this chunk is rendered at the length Migne prints it (*Cum felle mistum*, *Si
Filius Dei es*, *Sine videamus*, *Eli, Eli, lamma*), which is what makes this one visible.

**Read instead:** "*Nothing to thee.*"

### A5. 0033 @0153C — *Infantium et lac*: lemma conformed, and *Christi* re-bound (OURS, 7a″ ×2)

**Latin (VERS. 16):** `*Infantium et lac.* Testes miraculorum. Christi infantes dicuntur, non aetate, sed simplicitate.`

**English as it stands:** "*Of infants and **sucklings**.* Witnesses of the miracles **of Christ, they are called infants**, not by age, but by simplicity."

Two defects in one line.
1. Migne prints ***lac*** — "milk", not *lactentium*. The English supplies the familiar
   Ps 8:3 / Mt 21:16 wording. (The gloss then does speak of *Lactentes*, but that is the
   gloss's own word, not the lemma's.) Render "*Of infants and milk.*"
2. Migne's punctuation closes at *miraculorum*. ***Christi*** belongs to the next
   sentence — *Christi infantes dicuntur* — "they are called **Christ's** infants, not by
   age but by simplicity." The English pulls *Christi* back onto *miraculorum* and the
   phrase "Christ's infants", which is what the sentence is defining, disappears.

**Read instead:** "*Of infants and milk.* Witnesses of the miracles. They are called Christ's infants, not by age, but by simplicity."

### A6. 0033 @0154A — *debuit … signare* rendered as an unfulfilled obligation (OURS)

**Latin (VERS. 20):** `Passurus Dominus et scandalum crucis portaturus, discipulorum animos debuit signi anticipatione signare, unde et mirabantur, quod ficus aruit.`

**English as it stands:** "he **ought to have marked** the minds of the disciples by the anticipation of a sign, whence they wondered too that the fig tree had withered".

In English "he ought to have marked" asserts that he did **not**. The next clause —
*unde et mirabantur* — says he did. *debuit* here is "it behoved him / he had to", and
the withered fig is the sign he in fact gave. The English contradicts its own neighbour.

**Read instead:** "it behoved the Lord … to mark the minds of the disciples by the anticipation of a sign, whence they also wondered that the fig tree had withered."

### A7. 0034 @0155B — *sicut de praesenti Ecclesia*: comparison attached to the wrong verb (OURS)

**Latin (VERS. 2):** `Aliud simile ex parte Lucas dicit de coena, id est, aeterno convivio, de quo nemo ingressus repellitur, sicut de praesenti Ecclesia.`

**English as it stands:** "…of the eternal banquet, from which no one who has entered is driven out, **as he does of the present Church**."

The English makes *sicut* govern *Lucas dicit*. But *sicut de praesenti Ecclesia* is
parallel to *de quo … repellitur*: from the eternal banquet no one who has entered is
expelled, **as one is from the present Church** — which is the whole point of the
sentence, and the reason the parable of the wedding garment is being read as being about
the Church on earth, where a man *is* cast out. As it stands the contrast is gone and the
clause asserts something about Luke instead.

**Read instead:** "…from which no one who has entered is driven out, as [he is] from the present Church."

### A8. 0041 @0176C — *tanto intervallo* rendered "so great", inverting the argument (OURS)

**Latin (VERS. 55):** `Matthaeus et Lucas dicunt Mariam Magdalenam a longe stetisse. Joannes vero juxta crucem, quia tanto intervallo aberat, ut juxta diceretur: quia praesto erat in conspectu ejus, et longe comparatione turbae propioris.`

**English as it stands:** "because she was distant by **so great** an interval that she could be said to be **beside** it".

*tanto* is "such", and the clause is explaining why *juxta* is true, not why *a longe* is.
"So great … that she could be said to be beside it" says the opposite of what it is
adduced to prove; the *quia*-clause that follows (*praesto erat in conspectu ejus*)
confirms the interval in view is the small one.

**Read instead:** "because she was away by such an interval that she could be called 'beside' it".

## B. LESSER SITES (material, lower confidence)

- **0033 @0152A, *Duo propter scientiam veritatis…*** — `Duo propter scientiam veritatis et munditiam operis, et sacramentum geminae dilectionis praedicaturi.` English: "Two, as being about to **preach the knowledge of truth** and cleanness of work…". *propter* is printed and unrendered; it governs all three accusatives (*Two, on account of the knowledge of truth…, who are to preach*). The English silently converts a causal phrase into the object of *praedicaturi*.
- **0037 @0164D, *Christi carnem nostram… duxit*** — English "he led the flesh of Christ, our own, unto foreign parts" supplies a subject the Latin does not print and re-binds *nostram* as an appositive. The printed *Christi* is very likely Migne for *Christus* ("therefore Christ led our flesh into foreign parts when he ascended"). Either way the English's unanchored "he" should not stand; render literally + crux.
- **0038 @0168D, *quasi dicat, non ego dixi*** — English "for it can be understood **as though he were saying**, I did not say it". The nearest English "he" is Judas; the speaker of *dicat* is the Lord (*Tu dixisti*). Name him: "as though the Lord should say".
- **0038 @0170C, *a poenitentibus malis*** — English "protection from **the evils we repent of**". The sentence's own structure is a past/present/future triad (*a praeteritis peccatis … a X malis … a futuris periculis*), which fixes the middle term as *praesentibus*. Migne's *poenitentibus* is almost certainly defective; the English's smooth reading hides that. Render literally and log the crux — do not silently supply either reading.
- **0041 @0178C, *Qui jam in lucem venit*** — English "**He who** has now come into the light, which until now had for certain causes been doubtful to men". The following *quod … dubium fuerat* is neuter; the subject wanted is the thing now brought to light (the mission to all nations), not a person. Printed *Qui* is likely for *Quod*. The English's supplied masculine subject asserts something the Latin does not.
- **0035 @0159B, *et patet eos scientes peccare*** — English "and it is plain that **they** sin knowingly". An English reader binds "they" to *your fathers* two clauses back; *eos* is the Pharisees being addressed. Name them.

## F. Note on the supplied-subject hazard in this range

Every English "he/she/it/they" not printed in the Latin was named. The two that failed
are B3 (*quasi dicat* → the Lord, not Judas) and B2 (*Christi carnem nostram*); B5
(*Qui jam in lucem venit*) is a supplied subject where the Latin probably wants none at
all. Elsewhere the supplied subjects check out against the lemma above — notably
0032 @0150D *Audita resurrectione putavit* → "she" (the mother of Zebedee's sons, fixed
by *aviditate feminea* and by VERS. 20's lemma) and 0036 @0162A *credunt non venire in
publicum* → "he" (Christ, fixed by *Ecce in deserto*). The 11613 profile (12 of 25 sites
in this class) does not repeat here; in this range the dominant class is **mis-construal
of a case or a relative that leaves the count of words untouched** — five of the eight
decisive sites.
