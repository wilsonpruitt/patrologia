# Dorotheus *Doctrinae*: blind read R1 (2026-10-27)

Reader R1. Chunks 0001–0005 of `dorotheus-gazaeus-doctrinae` (cols 1612–1648). Brief:
`DOROTHEUS-DOCTRINAE-BLIND-READ-BRIEF.md`. Every sentence of Greek read against the English; every
negative checked. Latin twin consulted at each candidate. Work in progress, written progressively.

## Findings

### F1. Chunk 0004, col 1637 D (§16): the elder's "thee" turned into "me"
- **Class:** 2 (who does what: speaker / who is protected).
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 849 left col., band D):** λέγει αὐτῷ ὁ γέρων· « Ἐγείρου, ὕπαγε· » καὶ οὐκ εἶπεν αὐτῷ· Ἐλπίζω εἰς τὸν Θεόν μου ὅτι σκεπάζῃ **σε**· ἀλλὰ λέγει· « Ἐλπίζω εἰς τὰς εὐχὰς τοῦ Πατρός μου ὅτι σκεπάζουσί **με**. » Ὁμοίως καὶ ὁ ἀδελφὸς, ὅτε ἐπειράσθη, …
- **Latin (col. 1638–39):** *Exsurge, ait, et actutum perge. Iis cedens frater dixit: Non spero in Deum quia me proteget, sed in orationibus Patris mei, quia me tuebuntur.* The Latin gives the line to the BROTHER, with *me* twice. That is Allatius's resolution, not the Greek's.
- **English as it stands:** `And he did not say to him, "I hope in my God that he will protect me," but he says, "I hope in the prayers of my father that they will protect me."`
- **Proposed English:** `And he did not say to him, "I hope in my God that he will protect thee," but he says, "I hope in the prayers of my father that they will protect thee [sic: *με*]."`
- **Why:** the plate prints σε: the elder, who has just said "Get up, go", is the subject of οὐκ εἶπεν αὐτῷ, and the paragraph's frame is "the humility of both" (elder first, then Ὁμοίως καὶ ὁ ἀδελφός). The English silently turned σε into "me", which (with the second "me") hands the speech to the brother and leaves "Likewise the brother" with no elder-half to be "likewise" to. The second clause's printed με is then the odd form out (the received text reads σε there too). At minimum the first "me" must be "thee". The [sic:] on the second is my proposal; if the house ruling is that a real-word misprint is rendered without a marker, render "thee" and crux it.
- **Confidence:** certain for the first clause (plate σε, English "me"); probable for the second.
- **Already in the cruces?** No (checked after: nothing at 1637–1640 or 1633 touches it).

### F2. Chunk 0004, col 1640 A (§16): ὥσπερ κἀκείνῳ rendered from the Latin
- **Class:** 3 (mis-parsed grammar).
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 850 right col., band A):** ἐν μηδενὶ διστάζων, ἀλλὰ πάντα ποιῶν, ὥσπερ κἀκείνῳ, μετὰ πληροφορίας ὡς ὅτι τῷ Θεῷ ὑπακούει;
- **Latin:** *omnia ipsius arbitrio facere, ea fide et certitudine, ac si Deus in cunctis non homini pareret, ut illi quos praediximus factitarunt*. The Latin reads a nominative plural (κἀκεῖνοι), and the English follows it.
- **English as it stands:** `but doing all things, as those men did, with full assurance, as one who is obeying God?`
- **Proposed English:** `but doing all things as unto him, with full assurance, as one who is obeying God?`
- **Why:** the plate prints the dative singular κἀκείνῳ ("as to him too", i.e. as to the father/God being obeyed), not "as those men did". "Those men" is Allatius's reading (*ut illi … factitarunt*) leaking into the English (the Register rule: the Latin is a verifier, never a source). If the plural is kept as a conjecture it needs a crux, not silence.
- **Confidence:** probable (the reading of κἀκείνῳ is certain; the exact referent, father or God, is interpretation).
- **Already in the cruces?** No (checked after: nothing at 1637–1640 or 1633 touches it).

### F3. Chunk 0003, col 1633 A (§12): "upon the Lord" added to 1 Cor 7
- **Class:** 4 (added text), low weight.
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 847):** καὶ Θεῷ μόνῳ σχολάσαι, ὡς λέγει, ἵνα ᾖ παρθένος εὐπάρεδρος, καὶ ἀπερίσπαστος. The plate has no τῷ Κυρίῳ.
- **English as it stands:** `"that there may be a virgin waiting well upon the Lord, and undistracted."`
- **Proposed English:** `"that there may be a virgin waiting well, and undistracted."`
- **Why:** "the Lord" comes from the received text of 1 Cor 7:35 (εὐπάρεδρον τῷ Κυρίῳ), not from Dorotheus's quotation. The brief says Scripture is rendered as Dorotheus quotes it (Pattern 7a″: the quietly conformed lemma). If "waiting well" reads too bare, "assiduous" renders εὐπάρεδρος alone.
- **Confidence:** certain that the words are absent; low consequence.
- **Already in the cruces?** No (checked after: nothing at 1637–1640 or 1633 touches it).

## Candidates considered and rejected

1. **0003 col 1625 §7, ἀληθινὴν ταπείνωσιν vs Latin *veram requiem*.** Plate-read (p. 843): the Greek prints
   ταπείνωσιν, and the English follows it. Allatius's free rendering (he also adds *unde incidimus in miseriam
   tantam?*); not a material divergence.
2. **0003 col 1628 §9, Οὖν διὰ τῆς συντριβῆς** (suspected Οὐ, which would make it a question). Plate-read (p. 844)
   as Οὖν. The English "So [?]" with its crux (logged) is right; the Latin *Igitur si* supports a conditional, not a
   negative question.
3. **0003 col 1632 Zosimas, ἐκείνῳ ποιῆσαι ἐν ἡμῖν.** Plate-read (p. 846): printed exactly so, with the object
   missing. The English "do in us [?]" with its crux citing *loco centenarii* is already handled (logged).
4. **0003 §7 ἐπειράσθητε τοῦ κακοῦ, καὶ ἀνυποταξίας ὑμῶν → "tasted the evil of your insubordination".** This merges
   two genitives, but the Latin (*experti estis malum contumaciae vestrae*) reads it the same way and the sense is not
   changed. Not reported.
5. **0003 §8 ἠξιώθη μέμψασθαι ἑαυτόν → "thought fit to blame himself".** "Deigned" is supported by the Latin
   *Neuter sese dignatus est accusare*. Not reversed.
6. **0003 §7 ἀνθ᾿ ὧν ἐθανατώθητε → "you who were put to death".** Loose ("in place of having been put to death"),
   but the sense is kept. Not reported.
7. **0003 §8 Ἐκέλευσε· Μὴ ποιήσῃς → "He was commanded".** Active rendered as passive, but the agent (God) and the
   addressee (Adam) are unchanged. Not reported.
8. **0004 col 1640 Τὶς ἄξιος … τὶς ἄξιος → "[?] Such a one is worthy…".** Plate-read (p. 850): grave accents, so
   the print is not an interrogative. Following the Latin *hic … hic* is logged in the cruces. Not reported.
9. **0004 §14 καὶ πᾶσαν περιπατεῖ τις → "And how? A man walks…".** Silently reads πῶς; for πᾶσαν. A sound sense,
   but I found no crux for it at 1636. It might be worth a crux line. It is not a meaning error, so it is not
   reported as a finding.
10. **0004 §15 ἵνα μόρον (sic) εἴπῃ → "he had only to say".** Migne's own "(sic)" is not carried into the English.
    The sense (μόνον) is right. That is a marker question, not my class.
11. **0005 §3 Ἀλλὰ καὶ ἀπὸ παντὸς πάθους → "It shelters the soul from every passion".** The English drops ἀλλὰ
    καί ("nay, even"), which loses some force but does not reverse anything. Not reported.
12. **0005 §5 ἐπιεικής → "capable".** A debatable lexical choice ("modest/reasonable" is likelier), but it does not
    change the claim. Not reported.
13. **Band letters** missing from the English in 0002 and 0004 (e.g. [b: C]/[b: D] in 0002 §§1, 2, 4, 6; [b: A] at
    1625, 1636, 1640). These are excluded by the brief. I mention them only so that a later marker pass can
    find them.
