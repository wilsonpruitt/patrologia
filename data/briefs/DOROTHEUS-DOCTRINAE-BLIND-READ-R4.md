### F1. 0016, col. 1729A — class 1 (polarity: printed negative dropped without a marker)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0911, Greek col. 1729 lines 7–8):** `Εἰ δέ τινες καὶ πλεῖον οὐ κατώρθωσαν ἀναλύουσιν·` The plate prints εἰ and οὐ exactly as our file has them.
- **Latin (col. 1730):** *Sunt et alii qui plus dissolvunt, quam moliti sint.*
- **English as it stands:** `And there are some who undo even more than they achieved.`
- **Proposed English:** `And there are some who undo even more than they achieved [sic: *Εἰ … οὐ κατώρθωσαν*].`
- **Why:** The printed Greek has a negative (οὐ, almost certainly a misprint for ὧν, with εἰ for οἵ). The English gives the sense (rightly; the Latin agrees) but drops the οὐ with no `[sic:]`, so a reader can't tell the plate prints a negative here. Under the brief's convention the sense stays and the printed form gets a marker. The fix is the marker, not the wording.
- **Confidence:** certain that the plate prints οὐ and the English carries no marker; probable that a `[sic:]` (rather than a crux entry alone) is the house treatment.
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes, the conjecture is logged (cruces.md, 1729A, and the plate table row `p0911.07`, class `print`, attribution `migne`). But every other `print`/`migne` row in this range carries a `[sic:]` in the English (μύλην, ἔβλεπον, οἱ, ἄλλην ἄλλως, ἐπαινοῦντες, κάκην, παρὰ τῶν, Λέγει, διαφόρου, μετὰ Θεόν). This is the one that doesn't. So the logged crux stands, and the missing marker is the finding.

### F2. 0018, col. 1740B–C — class 1 (interrogative μή rendered as a negation; the self-examination now asks the reverse)
- **Greek (file only; the finding is about the English, not the reading):** `ἆρα μὴ εἶδον αὐτὸν ποιοῦντα πράγματα, καὶ κατέκρινα αὐτὸν, ἢ ἐξουδένωσα αὐτὸν, ἢ κατελάλησα κατ᾿ αὐτοῦ;`
- **Latin (col. 1741):** *an fratrem aliquid facientem judicavi? an eum contempsi? num oblocutus de ipso sum?* (a positive question)
- **English as it stands:** `Did I not see him doing things, and condemn him, or despise him, or speak ill of him?`
- **Proposed English:** `Did I perhaps see him doing things, and condemn him, or despise him, or speak ill of him?`
- **Why:** ἆρα μή is a hesitant question particle (like the ἆρα μήπως in the line just before), not a negative on εἶδον. "Did I not see him…" makes the monk ask whether he *failed* to see the brother's faults, which is the opposite of the examination of conscience. The same chunk renders the identical construction correctly at 1740C (`ἢ μὴ ὀλιγώρησε` → "or whether one made light") and 1744B (`ἢ μὴ ἀφῆκε` → "or whether he left").
- **Confidence:** certain.
- **Already in the cruces?** No. Not logged (cruces 0018 has no entry at 1740B–C; the plate row `p0916.31` is about μὴ, τὸ ἔψη only).

### F3. 0018, col. 1745A — class 3 (μή-question turned into a negative question)
- **Greek (file only):** `Λέγω αὐτῷ· « Μὴ ἐμὲ αἰσχύνῃ; » Λέγει μοι· « Οὔ. »`
- **Latin (col. 1746):** *ab eo quaerere coepi an de me erubesceret; quod cum negasset* (a plain question; he denies being ashamed)
- **English as it stands:** `I say to him, "Art thou not ashamed before me?" He says to me, "No."`
- **Proposed English:** `I say to him, "Thou art not ashamed before me, art thou?" He says to me, "No."`
- **Why:** μή expects the answer "no" ("surely thou art not ashamed before me?"). The English negative question expects "yes", which suggests Dorotheus thinks the brother *is* ashamed of him. The brother's "No" then reads ambiguously, when the story needs it to mean plainly "I am not", because that is why Dorotheus offers to supply him himself. (A plain "Art thou ashamed before me?" would also do.)
- **Confidence:** probable. The facts of the story survive, but the polarity of the question is inverted.
- **Already in the cruces?** No. Not logged.

### F4. 0019, col. 1749D — class 3 (statement rendered as a question)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0921, Greek col. 1749 band D):** `« Ἐπειδὴ τὴν θλίψιν φεύγῃ, καὶ οὐκ οἶδας ὅτι ἡ μέλλουσα θλίψις πολὺ χεῖρων ἐστὶ τῆς ἐνταῦθα. »` The plate sets a full stop before the closing guillemet, not a ;.
- **English as it stands:** `"That is because thou fleest affliction; and dost thou not know that the affliction to come is far worse than the one here?"`
- **Proposed English:** `"That is because thou fleest affliction, and thou dost not know that the affliction to come is far worse than the one here."`
- **Why:** The plate makes the elder state a diagnosis: the brother flees affliction *and does not know* what is coming. The English turns the second half into a rhetorical question, which Pattern 8 forbids (punctuation follows the plate). The sense is close, but the elder's two-part explanation becomes a reproach.
- **Confidence:** probable (certain on the plate punctuation; low impact on the sense).
- **Already in the cruces?** No. Not logged.

### F5. 0020, col. 1756B — class 1 (an "οὐ μόνον" reversed by a ⟨…⟩ supplement where the plate has lost nothing)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0924, Greek col. 1756 band B; the line is clean, nothing is lost or damaged):** `Λέγει οὖν τις ἀγωνίσασθαι, ὡς εἶπον, πρὸς τὰς κακὰς συνηθείας, καὶ πρὸς τὰ πάθη, καὶ οὐ μόνον πρὸς τὰς αἰτίας αὐτῶν, αἵτινές εἰσι ῥίζαι.`
- **Latin (col. 1755):** *Certamen igitur primum sit adversus malos mores et vitia causasque eorum, quae radices sunt.* (no "not only" in either direction)
- **English as it stands:** `So one must struggle, [sic: *Λέγει*] as I said, against the bad customs and against the passions, and not only ⟨against these, but also⟩ against their causes, which are roots.`
- **Proposed English:** `So one must struggle, [sic: *Λέγει*] as I said, against the bad customs and against the passions, and not only against their causes [cj: *καὶ οὐ μόνον*; read *καὶ οὐ μόνον πρὸς ταῦτα ἀλλὰ καὶ*, "and not only against these but also"], which are roots.` (Or keep the English sense and give the crux the evidence. Either way, the ⟨⟩ has to go.)
- **Why:** As printed, the sentence construes and says the reverse of the English. Struggle against customs and passions, "and not *only* against their causes", which makes the causes the lesser target. The English adds words that make the causes the further target. The context (the roots image, and 1757A `οὐ μόνον ἐκκόψαι τὰ πάθη, ἀλλὰ καὶ τὰς αἰτίας αὐτῶν`) supports the English's sense. But ⟨…⟩ is reserved for words the plate has *lost*, and the plate has lost none here. So the brackets present an emendation of an intact sentence, one that flips where the οὐ μόνον lands, as a recovery of damaged type.
- **Confidence:** certain that the plate is intact and that the supplement reverses the printed οὐ μόνον; probable on the treatment (`[cj:]` vs crux).
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes, logged as plate row `p0924.24`, class `lacuna`, attribution `migne`, with the supplement as the reading. So the translator judged it a lacuna in Migne's text, the same house treatment as `νήψωμεν ⟨…⟩ καιρόν` (1736C) and `ὁ θέλων ⟨σωθῆναι⟩` (1761A). The difference is that those two printed texts do not construe, and this one does, with the opposite emphasis. That makes it an adjudication call rather than a clear error, and I am reporting it for the polarity flip. Downgrade it to "logged, no action" if the lacuna ruling stands.
