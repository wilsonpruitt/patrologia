## Findings

### F1. 0021, col 1764 (before [b: A]): class 1 (scope of negative)
- **Greek** (file only): ἀλλ᾿ οὐ πάντως καὶ ταύτην ἔχει σύνεσιν, ὥστε διοικῆσαι τὰ κατ᾿ ἐμὲ, καὶ διὰ τοῦτο, ὡς εἰκὸς, καὶ μὴ θέλων βλάπτει με
- **Latin**: *cum tamen non esset in eo omnis providentia et sapientia … fortasse nolens obfuit mihi* (hedged: *fortasse*).
- **English as it stands**: "but he surely does not also have the understanding to manage my affairs"
- **Proposed**: "but he does not necessarily also have the understanding to manage my affairs"
- **Why**: οὐ πάντως is "not necessarily / not in every case". The friend's lack of understanding is a possibility, and ὡς εἰκός ("as is likely") in the same sentence keeps it one. "Surely does not" turns a hedge into an assertion that every friend lacks it. The parallel clause at s4 (οὐ μὴν καὶ δύναμιν ἔχει) is the flat one, and the English should not level the two.
- **Confidence**: probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** No. No 1764 entry.

### F2. 0021, col 1769C: class 2 (who does what)
- **Greek** (file only): Ὅτε δὲ ἀπέστειλεν ὁ Θεὸς τὸν Μωϋσῆν ἐξαγαγεῖν αὐτοὺς ἐξ Αἰγύπτου καὶ ἐκ τῆς δουλείας τοῦ Φαραὼ, καταβαρύνει κατ᾿ αὐτῶν τὰ ἔργα, καὶ λέγει αὐτοῖς· Σχολασταί ἐστε…
- **Latin**: *aggravati sunt magis operibus **a rege**, qui ait eis: Otiosi estis…*
- **English as it stands**: "he made their works heavier, and said to them"
- **Proposed**: "Pharaoh made their works heavier, and said to them"
- **Why**: The Greek verb has no expressed subject, and the sense (Exod 5, and the devil-analogy that follows: "likewise the devil … he too weighs the passions more heavily") makes it Pharaoh. In the English the only antecedent for "he" is God, the subject of the preceding clause, so the English has God oppressing Israel and calling them idlers. The Latin names the king.
- **Confidence**: probable (the English is ambiguous, not grammatically wrong; but the nearest reading is the wrong one).
- **Already in the cruces?** No. The 1769C entry gives only the Exod 1:11 source for the cities; nothing on the subject of καταβαρύνει.

### F3. 0022, col 1776D–1777A: class 5 (fixed convention: thou = singular)
- **Greek** (file only): οἷόν τι λέγω· Ἰδοὺ ἔρχεται ὁ ἀδελφός σου, καὶ λέγει σοι ῥῆμα, θλίβων σε, ἢ πλήσσων, καὶ σιωπᾷς, καὶ βάλλεις μετάνοιαν· ἰδοὺ ἔβαλες ἕνα λίθον. Εἶτα ὑπάγεις καὶ λέγεις ἄλλῳ ἀδελφῷ … Ἰδοὺ ἔβαλες; ἕνα λίθον, καὶ δύο ἐπῆρες.
- **English as it stands**: "What I mean is this: see, your [1777] [b: A] brother comes and says a word to you that afflicts you or wounds you, and you keep silent and make a prostration: see, you have laid one stone." / "Then you go off and say to another brother:" / "See, you laid one stone and lifted off two."
- **Proposed**: "What I mean is this: see, thy [1777] [b: A] brother comes and says a word to thee that afflicts thee or wounds thee, and thou keepest silent and makest a prostration: see, thou hast laid one stone." / "Then thou goest off and sayest to another brother:" / "See, thou didst lay one stone and lift off two."
- **Why**: Every second person here is singular (σου, σοι, σε, σιωπᾷς, βάλλεις, ἔβαλες, ὑπάγεις, λέγεις, ἐπῆρες). Pattern 17 makes thou/you a number marker "without exception", and an indefinite singular is still singular. The same translator uses thou for the identical construction elsewhere in my range (0023 p1, the two ladders; 0023 p3, the almsgiving catechism). This is the only you-for-singular passage I found in five chunks.
- **Confidence**: certain.
- **Already in the cruces?** No.

### F4. 0024, col 1788C–D: Greek file (Migne's note keyed to the wrong word). Plate-read.
- **Greek file**: s4 ταύτας ἑπτὰ **[n: ὀκτώ.]** ἑβδομάδας τῶν νηστειῶν … s6 Αἱ γὰρ ἑπτὰ ἑβδομάδες ὑφαιρουμένων τῶν Σαββάτων…
- **Plate** (p-0940, right column, band C–D): the note mark (a small superscript, read here as 33; the cruces table gives 35) stands at **Αἱ γὰρ ἑπτὰ³³ ἑβδομάδες** (s6), **not** at ταύτας ἑπτὰ (s4). The foot reads "³³ ὀκτώ."
- **Latin**: *Octo enim hebdomadas, subtractis Sabbatis et Dominicis, quadraginta dies perficiunt*.
- **English**: already correct: s4 "these seven weeks", s6 "For eight [sic: *ἑπτὰ*] weeks". The arithmetic agrees: the apostles' seven, the Fathers' added one makes eight, 8 × 5 = 40, and 7 × 5 = 35 at s7.
- **Proposed fix (Greek file, not English)**: move `[n: ὀκτώ.]` from after s4's ἑπτὰ to after s6's ἑπτὰ (`Αἱ γὰρ ἑπτὰ [n: ὀκτώ.] ἑβδομάδες`).
- **Why**: As it stands the Greek file has Migne correcting the apostles' seven to eight, which contradicts the next sentence. Whoever builds a variant layer or an index from `[n:]` in the Greek will key it to the wrong word.
- **Confidence**: certain (plate-read).
- **Already in the cruces?** Half of it. The `[sic:]` table row `p0940.37 | ἑπτὰ | ὀκτώ | … γὰρ ἑπτὰ ³⁵ ἑβδομάδες` records the reading at the right word (s6), and that is why the English is right. The Greek file's `[n: ὀκτώ.]` standing at s4 is not logged.

## Candidates considered and rejected

- **0021 1765B–C, s12–13** (καταπατήσαντος κρίσιν, Ephraim): Hos 5:11 loosely run on. The English follows it. Not a finding.
- **0021 1768B, s6** (κἂν συνεῖδεν … κἂν πρὸς τὸ παρὸν, κἂν μὴ συνεῖδε): the English attaches "for the present" to "does not" rather than to "knows". Either reading carries the point (cause known or not, God's judgment stands). Too fine to be a sense change.
- **0021 1772B, s22** (τὴν νοητὴν θάλασσαν → "the sea of the mind"): "the spiritual / intelligible sea" would be closer, but the meaning is not reversed. Not reported.
- **0021, every negative**: οὐ πάντως (F1); οὐ δυνάμεθα, οὐ μὴν, οὐδὲν ἀδυνατεῖ, οὐ παραβλέπει … οὐδέ, οὐ συγχωρεῖ, μὴ ἔχοντες / μηδὲν / μηδέ, οὐ προκόπτομεν, μὴ ἔχειν, οὐ δυνατὸν μὴ, μὴ θέλων ×4, Μὴ κατέλθητε, Μὴ φοβηθῆτε, οὐ μὴ ×3, τὸ μὴ ὀλιγωρεῖν / μηδὲ, οὐκ ἔστιν ἄξιος οὐδέ, οὐδὲν ἄκριτον οὐδὲ ἄδικον, οὐκ εἰμὶ ἄξιος, μὴ πειρασθῇ … οὔτε, μὴ ἔχων, οὔτε … οὐδέ, μηδὲν φρονεῖν, οὐκ ἀφιοῦσιν, μὴ μικροψυχήσωσιν, Μὴ ὁ πίπτων οὐκ, οὐκ ἐπιστρέφει, Οὐχὶ, οὐχ ὑπολειψόμεθα οὐδέ, μὴ εὑρίσκων, οὐ χρὴ … οὐδέ. All are carried.
- **0022 1776D, s11–12** (Ἰδοὺ ἔβαλες; ἕνα λίθον, καὶ δύο ἐπῆρες): the Greek question mark is flattened to a statement. It is rhetorical and the sense holds. Not reported (the you/thou in the same sentence is F3).
- **0022 1777B, s19** (Τὸ δὲ [μὴ] μετρεῖν): the English drops the bracketed μή. This is right (the Latin has *metiri*), and it is logged at 1777B. Not plate-checked by me.
- **0023 1781C, s9 and s10** (Οὐ θέλει οὖν ἡμᾶς … ἵνα βάλωμεν … ; / οὐχ ἐπειδὴ θέλημα αὐτοῦ ἐστιν … θέλει ἡμᾶς ἵνα θλίβωμεν αὐτόν … ;): the English renders both as rhetorical questions with no surface negative ("Does he then want us…?", "is it, then, … that he wants us to afflict him"). The οὐ / οὐχ heads a rhetorical question expecting "no", and the Latin (*nunquid etiam vult Deus…*) does the same. The polarity of the claim (God does not want it) is preserved. Not reported.
- **0023 1784A, s17** (δόξαντος αὐτοῦ, ὡς ὀρφανὴν πτωχὴν εὔμορφον → "so that he seems to do it for her as a poor orphan"): the clause is obscure, the English is defensible, the Latin's *exterius* supports "seems", and it is logged at 1784A.
- **0023 1784C, s13** (ἀπέχωσι τὸν μισθόν → "have their reward"): a Matt 6:2 echo, and it is logged. Fine.
- **0024 1792A–B, s18** (μὴ προσέχειν … ἵνα μὴ … ζητῇ … ἢ νομίζειν): the English folds νομίζειν under "so that … not". The negative still governs it. Not reported.
- **0024 1793D, p9 s5** (Καὶ εἰ δύναται ἀβλαβῶς, ἀνακάμψει εἰς τὸ κελλίον αὐτοῦ → "and whether he can go back to his cell unharmed; [sic: *ἀνακάμψει*]"): I first suspected an εἰ-conditional turned into an indirect question. The Latin reads it as an indirect question too (*et an idem redeat … sine damno*). Withdrawn.
- **0025 1797A, s3** (βλάπτεται, οὐκ ὠφελεῖται → "harmed or helped [sic: *οὐκ ὠφελεῖται*]"): this `[sic:]` removes a printed negative, so I checked it at the plate. **Plate-read p-0951: Migne prints βλάπτεται, οὐκ ὠφελεῖται·**, so the `[sic:]` is a true report of the type. The emendation is supported by the Latin *vel laeditur, vel juvatur* and by s5 ("we can be both helped and harmed by every thing"). It is logged (cruces sic table p0951.06). Stands.
- **0025 1800C, p6 s2** (ἐν στρυφνότητι καρδίας → "with sternness of heart"): odd beside "bowels of mercy", but the Latin has *cordis severitate*. As printed. Not reported.
- **0025, the second person**: ὑμεῖς passages take "you" (p1 s2, p2 s5–6, s13), σύ passages take "thou" (p6–p7), and Ὑπόθου (μοι) is impersonal. Conforms.
- **0021/0023/0024/0025, the second person generally**: conforming throughout (Ἔχεις → "Thou hast", Θέτε → "Set your minds", the two-ladders and almsgiving passages in thou, Οἶδας … βλέπεις → "Dost thou know … thou seest"). The one exception is F3.
