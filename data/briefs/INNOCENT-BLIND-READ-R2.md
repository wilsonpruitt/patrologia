## A. Findings in the English

### A1. 7090 / 0007 / col 0497A, note a (key after *divina*). Class 3
- **Latin:** `Sequentia autem cum Gratianus dist. 31. c. 6, sic laudat, Eos ad sacrificia fas sit admitti, qui vel cum uxore non exercent carnale officium, adulteratae Innocentii sententiae merito postuletur.`
- **English as it stands:** `And when Gratian, dist. 31, c. 6, cites what follows thus, *that it be lawful for those to be admitted to the sacrifices who do not exercise the carnal office with a wife*, he is rightly asked for the adulterated sentence of Innocent.`
- **Proposed:** `And when Gratian, dist. 31, c. 6, cites what follows thus, *that it be lawful for those to be admitted to the sacrifices who do not exercise the carnal office even with a wife*, he is rightly arraigned for adulterating Innocent's sentence.`
- **Why:** *postulare* + genitive of the charge is "to accuse of" (the genitive *adulteratae sententiae* is the crime). "Asked for the adulterated sentence" gets the construction wrong and makes the sentence meaningless. The *vel* ("even") in Gratian's wording is also dropped. That *vel* is the whole point of the comparison with Innocent's *qui exercent vel cum uxore*.
- **Confidence:** certain (construction). The *vel* is certain too.
- **In cruces?** No.

### A2. 7090 / 0009 / Ep. VIII summary (head, before col 0507B). Class 3
- **Latin:** `Templi augusti et senatus incendium primum esse divinae iracundiae indicium.`
- **English as it stands:** `That the burning of the temple of the augusta and of the senate is the first sign of the divine wrath.`
- **Proposed:** `That the burning of the august temple and of the senate is the first sign of the divine wrath.`
- **Why:** *augusti* is the genitive of the adjective and agrees with *templi*. "Of the augusta" would need *augustae*. The body (0010, §2) is about the church called *augustissimam* (St Sophia) and the senate house. The English points the reader at a temple belonging to Eudoxia, which the Latin does not say. That sense is Baronius's reading, and Coustant's note c at 0507B rejects it.
- **Confidence:** probable, close to certain.
- **In cruces?** No.

### A3. 7090 / 0010 / col 0509A–B, §2. Class 3
- **Latin:** `aedificia quoque [cn: b | …] divina non minore splendore nobilia, ex concitatione saevientis incendii, … consumpta`
- **English as it stands:** `that the buildings also [nt: …], noble with a splendour no less divine, were consumed`
- **Proposed:** `that the sacred buildings also [nt: …], noble with no less splendour, were consumed`
- **Why:** *divina* (neut. pl.) agrees with *aedificia*. It cannot modify the ablative *splendore*. The English moves the adjective onto the wrong noun. ("Sacred" or "imperial" is the late-Latin sense of *divina* for public buildings. Note b says these are the senate house.)
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In cruces?** No.

### A4. 7090 / 0009 / col 0508A, note e (key after *principali*). Class 2 (number) and 4 (missing word)
- **Latin:** `exceptis his quos quinque immanitas criminum minime patitur relaxare`
- **English as it stands:** `*for the Paschal festival, except those whom the enormity of their crimes by no means allows to be released*`
- **Proposed:** `*for the Paschal festival, except those whom the enormity of the five crimes by no means allows to be released*`
- **Why:** *quinque* is dropped. The Theodosian Easter amnesty excepts the five named capital crimes, so the number is the content of the exception. The plate (p. 259) prints *quinque*.
- **Confidence:** certain.
- **In cruces?** No.

### A5. 7090 / 0009 / col 0508B, §1. Class 4 (added text) and 3
- **Latin:** `aut quo pacto extra summum Romani orbis omniumque mortalium [ *Supple,* quis] putaret esse discrimen?`
- **English as it stands:** `or how [ *Supply,* quis] would think the danger to lie outside the highest peril of the Roman world and of all mortals?`
- **Proposed:** `or how [ *Supply,* quis] would think it to lie outside the highest peril of the Roman world and of all mortals?`
- **Why:** *discrimen* occurs once, as the head noun of *summum … discrimen*. The English renders it twice ("the danger" … "the highest peril") and so invents a subject the Latin does not have. The implied subject is "it", meaning the crime just described.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In cruces?** Partly. The cruces log the need for *quis* (0508B) and not this doubling.

### A6. 7090 / 0011 / col 0515A, Ep. XIII §1. Class 3 (Pattern 7a silent repair)
- **Latin:** `ipso maxime vel etiam divinis [cn: b | …] consolationibus vacaturo.`
- **English as it stands:** `he himself being free for the greatest or even divine [nt: …] consolations.`
- **Proposed:** `he himself being free chiefly, or even, for divine [nt: …] consolations.`
- **Why:** The plate (p. 263) prints the adverb *maxime*. "The greatest" renders *maximis*, which is Coustant's conjecture in note b (*ipso maximis vel etiam divinis consultationibus vacaturo*). The English silently adopts half of the conjecture (*maximis*) while keeping the printed *consolationibus*. The cruces record only the *consolationibus* half.
- **Confidence:** certain.
- **In cruces?** Partly. `0515A consolationibus` is logged; *maxime* is not.

### A7. 7090 / 0007 / col 0495A, Dionysian title V. Class 3
- **Latin:** `--V. *Quod qui preces vel criminales dictant, habeantur immunes.*`
- **English as it stands:** `--V. *That those who dictate petitions, even capital ones, be held immune.*`
- **Proposed:** `--V. *That those who dictate petitions or criminal ones be held immune.*`
- **Why:** *criminales* means "criminal" (i.e. accusatory) and not "capital". Reading *vel* as "even" turns a disjunction into an intensifier. §11's body is indeed about capital petitions, so the English imports the body's sense into the title.
- **Confidence:** probable (low stakes: the body carries the matter).
- **In cruces?** No.

### A8. 7090 / 0006 / col 0488A, note e (key before *Num*). Class 5 (siglum)
- **Latin:** `Sirm. cum mss. Corc. et Colb., Numquidnam aliquid.`
- **English as it stands:** `Sirmond, with the Corb. and Colb. manuscripts: *Numquidnam aliquid*.`
- **Proposed:** `Sirmond, with the Corc. and Colb. manuscripts: *Numquidnam aliquid*.`
- **Why:** The plate (p. 249) prints *Corc.*. P0 says a manuscript siglum stays as printed. The silent normalization to *Corb.* asserts an identification the page does not make. It is probably right, but the place for it is a crux.
- **Confidence:** certain (as a departure from the printed form).
- **In cruces?** No. `cruces-0000` quotes the note with *Corc.* and says nothing about it.

## D. Candidates considered and rejected
- **0007 note a, Bigot's "cogitur" and the two Greek participles.** The English follows the plate (γενουμένη). This is a Latin-side issue, moved to B1.
- **0006 note b (0491A), `Granatium`.** Printed as such by Migne (plate p. 251). The English "Gratian" normalizes a printer's error in Coustant's note. That is under P0's no-marker rule for Coustant, and the identity is certain. Not reported. A crux line would do no harm.
- **0006 heading of §10, *Uxorum baptismum non aboleri* → "That the baptism of wives is not abolished".** Literal to the printed accusative. The sense ("the wives are not abolished by baptism") is odd, but the English renders what is printed (Pattern 7).
- **0007 Ep. VI inline "EPISTOLA IV. … *a num. 8 usque ad finem, nisi quod num. 9 contenta, etiam hic praetermittuntur*".** The numbers look inconsistent with the curtailed text (§9's content does appear in its titles). The English renders them exactly. Rejected as a translation issue.
- **0008 note c, *Cod. Th. lib. … XIX, tit. 40*.** The Theodosian Code has 16 books. The English is faithful to the print, so this is not a translation defect.
- **0008 note b, *Nicaeno canone 9*; note c (0501), Toledo IV *can. 13*; 0011 note k, Arles I *anno 514*; 0009 note b, Pagi *anni 310*.** All are Coustant's or Migne's figures and are rendered as printed. The 514 is already logged. The 310 is a 3/5/1 digit case on a worn plate (p. 259 reads 3?0) and could be looked at by someone with the second witness.
- **0010 Greek head `ΑΡΚΑΔΙΟΝ ΒΑΣΙΛΕΑ` → "ARCADIUS THE KING".** βασιλεύς is the regular Greek for the emperor, so "emperor" would read better, but "king" is not wrong as a literal rendering. Style only.
- **0011 §3, `et inter ipsos primates primus` → "and first among the primates themselves".** The English could be misread as the adverb "firstly". "And, as first among the primates themselves" would be clearer. This is ambiguity, not an error, so it is not raised as a finding.
- **0011 note a (Juliana), Pelagius *nullus sibi praeter te conferre poterit* → "no one besides thyself will be able to confer on thee".** The English reads *tibi* (Pelagius's actual text). I did not check the plate (col 519, p. 265). If the plate prints *sibi*, this is a small Pattern 7 repair. It is worth one look by whoever reads 0012.
- **0009 Greek §1, τῆς ἡμετέρας ἀγάπης "our charity"; §4 τοῦ Χριστοῦ αὐτῶν; 0010 Greek αὐτῶν κοινωνίας, ἐπισκοπῶν.** These are rendered as printed and already logged.
- **0007 note g (Ambrose, *Quae enim proprie in Dominum peccasti*) → "For since thou hast sinned".** The relative *quae* is rendered causally. That is acceptable.
