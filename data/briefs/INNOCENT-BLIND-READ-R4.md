### 2. 7090 / 0018 / 0550A: class 3. *quam … potius, quam fidem dixerim* mis-parsed
- **Latin.** *quam per impietatis suae perfidiam potius, quam [cn: f] fidem dixerim, perdiderunt.*
- **English as it stands.** "which they lost through the perfidy of their impiety rather than, as I
  should say, [nt: …] through faith."
- **Proposed.** "which they lost through what I should call the perfidy of their impiety rather than
  their [nt: …] faith."
- **Why.** *dixerim* governs the correction "perfidy rather than faith": Innocent says that what the
  heretics call faith he would call perfidy, and note *f* says exactly that ("the faith of heretics is
  rightly called perfidy"). The current English attaches "as I should say" to "through faith". It
  reads as if Innocent would name faith as the means of loss, which inverts the point.
- **Confidence.** Probable, strong.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### 3. 7090 / 0018 / 0546A–B: class 2/3. Who gathered John's clergy
- **Latin.** *ut et Evagrianos … susciperet: et Joannis sanctae memoriae vel clericos vel laicos in
  unum colligeret atque congregaret, promittens ipsius civitatis episcopus frater meus Alexander …*
- **English as it stands.** "and gathered and assembled into one both the clerics and the laymen of
  John of holy memory, the bishop of that city, my brother [0546B] Alexander [n: …], promising that"
- **Proposed.** "and that the bishop of that city himself, my brother [0546B] Alexander [n: …],
  gathered and assembled into one both the clerics and the laymen of John of holy memory, promising
  that"
- **Why.** In the English, "the bishop of that city, my brother Alexander" reads as an apposition to
  "John of holy memory", which makes John and Alexander one person, or John the bishop of Antioch.
  Coustant's note *h* says that *colligeret* refers to Alexander and *susciperent* to the church.
  *episcopus … Alexander* is the subject of *colligeret*/*promittens*.
- **Confidence.** Probable.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### 4. 7090 / 0019 / 0559A: class 2. *quinta feria ante Pascha*
- **Latin.** *quinta feria ante Pascha eis remittendum Romanae Ecclesiae consuetudo demonstrat*
- **English as it stands.** "remission is to be given them on the fifth day before Easter"
- **Proposed.** "remission is to be given them on the fifth day of the week before Easter"
- **Why.** *feria quinta* is Thursday, that is, Maundy Thursday, the Roman day for reconciling
  penitents. "The fifth day before Easter" counts back five days and lands on Tuesday or Wednesday.
  Elsewhere the chunk renders *sexta feria* as "the sixth day" (Friday), so a "…of the week" phrasing
  keeps the house convention and fixes the count.
- **Confidence.** Probable, strong.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### 5. 7090 / 0023 / 0573C–0574A: class 3. Relative clause attached to the wrong antecedent
- **Latin.** *ad concupiscentiam tentationesque vincendas, quem ditissime effudit super nos qui
  ascendit in coelum, et captivans captivitatem dedit dona hominibus*
- **English as it stands.** "to conquering concupiscence and temptations, [0574A] the Spirit whom he
  poured out most richly upon us who ascended into heaven, and, leading captivity captive, gave gifts
  to men"
- **Proposed.** "to conquering concupiscence and temptations, [0574A] the Spirit whom he who ascended
  into heaven, and, leading captivity captive, gave gifts to men, poured out most richly upon us"
- **Why.** *qui ascendit* is the subject of *effudit*, which is Christ (Eph. 4:8). The English "upon us
  who ascended into heaven" makes *us* the ones who ascended.
- **Confidence.** Certain.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### 6. 7090 / 0022 / 0570B: class 1. *quae fidelia corda sustineant*
- **Latin.** *haec interim duo, quibus omnino totum quod Christiani sumus conantur evertere, quae
  fidelia corda [cn: j | Garner., non sustinent, adjecta particula negante praeter veterum codicum
  fidem ac citra necessitatem.] sustineant, non esse rogandum Deum …*
- **English as it stands.** "and which faithful hearts [nt: …] must endure:"
- **Proposed.** "and what faithful hearts [nt: …] could endure them:" (no question mark supplied, in
  line with Pattern 8)
- **Why.** The potential subjunctive is rhetorical: which faithful hearts *could* bear these? The answer
  expected is none. Coustant's note *j* says Garnier added *non* "without necessity", which means the
  sentence already carries a negative force. "Must endure" states the opposite: that the faithful are
  obliged to put up with the two Pelagian tenets. The lack of a question mark on the plate doesn't
  license an affirmative-obligation reading, because *sustineant* is not a gerundive.
- **Confidence.** Probable.
- **In the cruces?** Yes, logged at 0570B. The stint rendered it as a statement for want of a question
  mark. I contest that rendering: the sense is reversed even without the mark.

### 9. 7090 / 0018 / 0548B–0549A: class 1 (minor). Negation scope over the second infinitive

  is a `[cj:]` candidate, not a translation error.

  It construes, and the polarity is intact.

  Justin, where the kiss comes *before*, which makes the logic odd. But *excipit* = "follows upon" is a
  defensible literal rendering, and I couldn't prove a reversal.
- **0547A note a, "more often" in italics.** The Latin has no italics. This is cosmetic only.
- **0561A note a, italic "penitents in good health…".** Also cosmetic.
- **0545B note d, *Jam vero* → "For now".** Loose but harmless.
- **0564 list, note n, *retinent* (plural) rendered "it keeps".** The Latin is itself irregular, and
