## Findings

### F1. 7090 / 0025 / 0579C. Class 3 (relative attached to the wrong antecedent; direction of sending reversed)
- **Latin:** `Unde et ad ipsum scriptam ab uno nostrum epistolam, ad quem per quemdam Orientalem diaconum, civem autem Hipponensem, tamquam purgationis suae quaedam scripta transmisit, tuae beattiudini potius credidimus dirigendam`
- **English as it stands:** `Wherefore we have thought that the letter written by one of us to him himself, to whom he sent certain writings, as it were of his`
- **Proposed:** `Wherefore we have thought that the letter written to him himself by one of us, to whom he sent certain writings, as it were of his`
- **Why:** *ad quem* refers to *uno nostrum* (Augustine). Pelagius sent the writings to Augustine by Charus, as Coustant's note e shows with *quam mihi … misit*. In the current English, "to whom" follows "him himself" (Pelagius), so it reads as though one of the bishops sent writings to Pelagius.
- **Confidence:** probable (certain as to the sense; the fix is a word order)
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F2. 7090 / 0025 / 0582A (§17). Class 2 (who did what: subject of *perviderit*)
- **Latin:** `Caetera quae illi objecta sunt, sicut gestis defensa perviderit, ita de his procul dubio tua beatitudo judicabit.`
- **English as it stands:** `The other things which were objected to him, just as he shall have seen them defended in the proceedings, so thy blessedness will without doubt judge of them.`
- **Proposed:** `The other things which were objected to him, thy blessedness will without doubt judge of them just as thou shalt have seen them defended in the proceedings.`
- **Why:** *perviderit* and *judicabit* share one subject, *tua beatitudo*: the pope will judge the remaining charges as he finds them defended in the Diospolis acts. "He" points to Pelagius, which makes Pelagius the one who reviews the proceedings.
- **Confidence:** probable
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F3. 7090 / 0025 / 0579C–0580A (§15). Class 1 (polarity legibility: "can not undeservedly")
- **Latin:** `tamen quoniam non immerito movere potest, ubi et quando …`
- **English as it stands:** `yet, since it can not undeservedly [nt:`
- **Proposed:** `yet, since it can, not undeservedly, [nt:`
- **Why:** With *can not* printed as two words straight after "it", the phrase reads as "cannot", which reverses the sense ("it cannot deservedly…"). The Latin says the question *can*, with good reason, be raised. The commas take *non* with *immerito*, where it belongs. This is the note-g site, and there the Merlin/Crabbe variant *quoniam merito quaeri potest* confirms the positive sense.
- **Confidence:** probable
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F4. 7090 / 0026 / 0586B (§6). Class 4 (word dropped: adverb *post*)
- **Latin:** `nisi eum post Christi [cn: k | …] pro sua gratia relevasset adventus`
- **English as it stands:** `had not the coming of Christ [nt:`
- **Proposed:** `had not the coming of Christ afterwards [nt:`
- **Why:** *adventus* is nominative, the subject of *relevasset*, so *post* cannot govern it. *post* is the adverb "afterwards", and the English leaves it out. It carries the time sequence: first the fall, then Christ's coming.
- **Confidence:** probable
- **In the cruces?** No. The crux at 0586B covers only *oppressu*.

### F5. 7090 / 0026 / 0588A–B (§8). Class 3 (jussive subjunctives read as indicatives inside the protasis)
- **Latin:** `Qui si tamen aliquod in se Dei adjutorium … provocarint, et opus sibi ejus auxilio esse cognoverint, … damnent haec quae huc usque senserunt; et aliquando animum rectis disputationibus commodantes, … veris se sanandos consiliis tribuant atque submittant. Quod si fecerint, erit in potestate pontificum`
- **English as it stands:** `they condemn these things which up to now they have held; and at length, applying their mind to right disputations, corrected in some small measure from this stain, they give and submit themselves to be healed`
- **Proposed:** `let them condemn these things which up to now they have held; and at length, applying their mind to right disputations, corrected in some small measure from this stain, let them give and submit themselves to be healed`
- **Why:** *damnent / tribuant atque submittant* are jussive and form the apodosis of *si … provocarint … cognoverint*: if they come to recognize their need of grace, let them condemn and submit. The English folds them into the condition, so the ruling's demand ("they must condemn") becomes one more hypothesis. Readmission then rests on a stated requirement, *Quod si fecerint*, and the English loses that. It is a canon-law "may, on condition" clause.
- **Confidence:** probable
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F6. 7090 / 0027 / 0590A–B (Ep. XXX §2). Class 3 (accusative subject read as nominative + reflexive)
- **Latin:** `Fiant enim necesse est cautiores, cum inventores malorum, ad duplicis relationem synodi, sententiae nostrae statutis viderint [cn: k | Solus Quesn., se ab ecclesiastica communione sejungi. …] ab ecclesiastica communione sejunctos.`
- **English as it stands:** `For they must needs become more cautious, when the inventors of evils see themselves, on the report of a double synod, by the statutes of our judgment [nt:`
- **Proposed:** `For men must needs become more cautious, when they see the inventors of evils, on the report of a double synod, by the statutes of our judgment [nt:`
- **Why:** The text has no *se*. Only Quesnel's variant supplies one, as note k says, and the English has in effect adopted his reading. *inventores … sejunctos* is the accusative object of *viderint*. Those who "become more cautious" are the others, who see the heresiarchs cut off. The English makes the heresiarchs both the ones who take warning and the ones who see themselves excluded.
- **Confidence:** probable, near certain, since the contrast with note k's *se* is itself the evidence.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F7. 7090 / 0027 / 0588B (Ep. XXIX §9). Class 3 (ablative of means read as dative; who answered whom)
- **Latin:** `Sed satis vestris monitis, sic abundantibus nostrae legis exemplis probatur esse responsum.`
- **English as it stands:** `But it is proved that sufficient answer has been made to your admonitions, so abounding in examples of our law.`
- **Proposed:** `But it is proved that sufficient answer has been made by your admonitions, so abounding in examples of our law.`
- **Why:** The next sentences say that the bishops left nothing out (*nihil praetermissum a vobis*), so nothing is left for Innocent to add, and he sets down no testimonies himself. So the sense is that the bishops' own admonitions have already answered the heretics. "Answer … made to your admonitions" turns this into Innocent answering the bishops, and that contradicts what follows.
- **Confidence:** probable
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F8. 7090 / 0027 / 0591B (Ep. XXX §3). Class 3 (*tantum* read as "only")
- **Latin:** `Quae in cassum dixerit, si [cn: d | Garner., si totum.] tantum in ejus erat positum voluntate, quod a Domino flebili sermone poscebat.`
- **English as it stands:** `Which he would have said in vain, if [nt: Garnier: *si totum*.] only so much was placed in his will as what he asked of the Lord with tearful speech.`
- **Proposed:** `Which he would have said in vain, if [nt: Garnier: *si totum*.] what he asked of the Lord with tearful speech had lain so fully in his own will.`
- **Why:** *tantum* means "so much, so fully", and Garnier's *totum* glosses it as "wholly". The argument is that David's prayer would be pointless if what he asked for were already in his own power. "Only so much … as" brings in a limiting sense, and the conditional stops making the anti-Pelagian point.
- **Confidence:** probable
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F9. 7090 / 0027 / 0589B (Ep. XXX §1). Class 3 (subject of *videantur ingestae* left ambiguous)
- **Latin:** `ne vel suscipiendo tales, simili caeterae ducantur exemplo; vel spernendo redeuntes, luporum morsibus videantur ingestae.`
- **English as it stands:** `or by spurning those who return, they seem to be thrown to the bites of wolves.`
- **Proposed:** `or, by spurning those who return, these should seem to be thrown to the bites of wolves.`
- **Why:** *ingestae* is feminine: the returning sheep (*oves*) are what gets thrown to the wolves. In English, "they" after "by spurning" naturally takes the spurners (the shepherds) as its subject.
- **Confidence:** probable (a minor point)
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F10. 7090 / 0025 / 0583B (Ep. XXIX §1). Class 1 (negative prefix lost: *inemundabili*)
- **Latin:** `quos veluti coeno inemundabili sordidatos, mundis digna corporibus unda vitaret.`
- **English as it stands:** `whom, soiled as with unclean mire, the water worthy of clean bodies should avoid.`
- **Proposed:** `whom, soiled as with mire that cannot be cleansed, the water worthy of clean bodies should avoid.`
- **Why:** *in-emundabilis* means "that cannot be cleansed", not "unclean". The negative is what separates this third class (the incorrigible, who are shunned) from the second (the soiled but penitent, who are washed). Coustant's note b sets out that three-class reading explicitly.
- **Confidence:** probable
- **In the cruces?** No.
