## Findings

### 1. 7090 / 0028 / 0595A: polarity (class 1)
- **Latin:** `et in tanta populi multitudine deprehendi aliquis facile, [cn: c | …] nec alicubi possit agnosci`
- **English as it stands:** `and in so great a multitude of people no one could easily be detected,`
- **Proposed:** `and in so great a multitude of people someone could easily be detected,` (and log a crux
  that the sentence wants a negative, citing Coustant's note c: lower-grade manuscripts read
  *nec deprehendi aliquis facile*, and Garnier reads *nec ubi lateat possit*)
- **Why:** there's no negative before *deprehendi* in the TEI, and none on the plate either (p. 303, left
  column: "deprehendi aliquis fa-|cile, ᶜ nec alicubi possit agnosci"). The English supplies one. The
  sense wants it, and the manuscripts that print *nec deprehendi* have it. But Coustant's note shows
  that he chose the text without it, so the English is quietly following the variant that he
  rejected. Pattern 7 says to render the text as printed and put the conjecture in the crux.
- **Confidence:** certain that the negative is missing from the Latin and the plate. It's only probable
  that the literal rendering is right, because a reader might defend *nec* as governing both verbs. If
  so, that reading still needs a crux.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### 2. 7090 / 0028 / 0597B: who does what to whom (class 3)
- **Latin:** `Etenim jus firmius charitatis officia melius seorsum mercantur.`
- **English as it stands:** `For the more firmly founded right of charity buys its offices better apart.`
- **Proposed:** `For the offices of charity buy a firmer right better apart.`
- **Why:** *mercantur* is plural, so its subject has to be *officia* (the offices of charity), with
  *jus firmius* as the object. The English makes *jus* the subject and *officia* the object, which
  turns the maxim around. Note f (*melius se orsa mercantur*) also has the plural verb.
- **Confidence:** certain.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### 3. 7090 / 0028 / 0594A: relative clause on the wrong antecedent (class 3)
- **Latin:** `et idoneum, dato quovis nostrae legis exemplo, quem sequi debeant, dignum possint praebere doctorem.`
- **English as it stands:** `and, given any example whatever of our law which they ought to follow, may offer them a worthy and fit teacher.`
- **Proposed:** `and, given any example whatever of our law, may offer them a worthy and fit teacher whom they ought to follow.`
- **Why:** *quem* is masculine and can't refer to the neuter *exemplo*. Its antecedent is *doctorem*,
  so what they ought to follow is the teacher, not the example.
- **Confidence:** certain.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### 4. 7090 / 0028 / 0595A: a demonstrative read as an instrumental (class 3)
- **Latin:** `seu hac illi in urbe sint, quod nescientes nec manifestare possumus nec negare`
- **English as it stands:** `whether those are in the City by this, which, not knowing it,`
- **Proposed:** `whether those are in this city, which, not knowing it,`
- **Why:** *hac … in urbe* is a hyperbaton: *hac* agrees with *urbe* ("in this city", meaning Rome).
  "By this" treats it as a separate ablative that has no job in the sentence. The plate (p. 303)
  confirms *hac*. Note a's variant *seu hic illi* ("here") agrees with the local sense.
- **Confidence:** certain.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### 5. 7090 / 0028 / 0595B: whose letters (class 3)
- **Latin:** `ut illos cogeret epistolis suis quid judicaverant indicare`
- **English as it stands:** `namely to compel them to indicate by their letters`
- **Proposed:** `namely to compel them by his letters to indicate`
- **Why:** in a *ut* clause, *suis* refers back to the clause's subject, Pelagius (*cogeret*). The
  sense is that Pelagius should have used his own letters to make the synod report its verdict. "By
  their letters" moves the letters to the bishops.
- **Confidence:** probable (an indirect reflexive toward *illos* is grammatically possible but less natural).
- **In the cruces?** No.

### 6. 7090 / 0031 / 0612A: active infinitive made passive (class 3)
- **Latin:** `eo ipso tempore quo noster e coelis Servator advenit, atque in Virginis Mariae uterum descendit, divinitatem cum humanitate conjunxisse`
- **English as it stands:** `the divinity was joined with the humanity,`
- **Proposed:** `he joined the divinity with the humanity,`
- **Why:** *conjunxisse* is an active perfect infinitive with *divinitatem* as its object. The subject
  is the Saviour of the *quo* clause. The passive leaves out the agent, and the agent is the point of
  the fragment's argument ("he himself formed his body… he alone made it").
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### 7. 7090 / 0031 / 0612B: a neuter subject turned into "he" (class 3)
- **Latin:** `verum ipso temporis articulo, quo in uterum Virginis descendit, unum cum corpore et divinitate individuum effectum est;`
- **English as it stands:** `he was made one individual with the body and the divinity;`
- **Proposed:** `one indivisible thing was made with the body and the divinity;`
- **Why:** *unum … individuum effectum est* is neuter, so the subject is "one indivisible [thing]",
  not the masculine Word. "He was made one individual *with the body and the divinity*" turns the
  Word into a third party alongside the two natures, which the Latin doesn't say.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### 8. 7090 / 0030 / 0605B: gerundive obligation lost (class 3, minor)
- **Latin:** `maxime cum a laicis religiosis viris et sciatur, et custodienda esse ducatur.`
- **English as it stands:** `especially since they are both known by religious lay men and considered to be kept.`
- **Proposed:** `especially since they are both known by religious lay men and held to be binding.` (or "held to be such as must be kept")
- **Why:** *custodienda esse* is a gerundive of obligation. "Considered to be kept" reads as a claim of
  fact, that people think the canons are being observed.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In the cruces?** No.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **R-1. 0606A *epicopis Apulis*.** The plate (p. 308) prints *epicopis*, and the English silently
  renders "bishops" with no `[sic:]`, while *fratrer* (0598A) and *Cathaginensi* (0599A) in the same
  range do carry one. It's **logged in the cruces** as a deliberate Pattern 9 call ("the head's
  facing Latin shows it"), so I'm not reporting it. I'd still flag the inconsistency for whoever
  adjudicates: *Cathaginensi* is also in a line with facing Latin, and it got a marker.
- **0600B *ut ratio non tractandi consulendique sit*.** The English renders it literally, and it
  contradicts the sense. That's correct under Pattern 7, the crux is logged, and note f carries *ut
  nulla ratio*.
- **0602B *Cum enim in captivitate praedicta Ursa mulier teneretur*.** "The aforesaid woman Ursa"
  vs "in the aforesaid captivity": *praedicta* can be nominative with *Ursa* or ablative with
  *captivitate*, and both were mentioned before. It's ambiguous, so I left it.
- **0601A head Ep. XXXV, *ne … opprimerentur*.** The English makes Paula and Eustochium the
  subject. That's the implied subject, so it's acceptable. *Joannem Hieronymum* is logged.
- **0600B *multis saepe narrastis*** ("you have often related in many words"). *multis* could be
  dative ("to many"), and Coustant's gloss *seu exposuistis* doesn't settle it. Either reading is
  defensible, and the cruces log the plural *you*.
- **0600B *percelletur*** (future) rendered "is struck". Coustant's note g rephrases it the same way
  and the sense isn't affected.
- **0610B note g *Videte latebras ambiguitatis falsitati praeparare refugia*** → "See him preparing
  hiding-places of ambiguity as refuges". *latebras* could be the subject of the infinitive, but the
  gerund *offundendo* implies a personal agent, so the English is defensible.
- **0601B *gregi illius*** → "his flock". It's literal, and Coustant's note d explains the Greek
  idiom. Correct.
- **0598A `safe, [sic: *fratrer*] dearest brother`.** The marker sits before the rendering of the
  word rather than after it. That's placement only, and it's covered by ruling.
- **0598A Monitum, *Hieronymus unus appellatur*** → "Jerome alone is named". *unus* could mean "as
  one", but "alone" suits the argument (only Jerome is named among those contending).
- **0607A note a, *a Romanis vel Orientalium partium episcopis*** → "by the bishops of the Romans or
  of the Eastern parts". It could be read as "by the Romans or by the bishops of the East". The
  difference doesn't matter to Quesnel's claim.
- **0594A note j** (Merlin/Crabbe/Garnier/Augustine and the Vatican manuscripts, *deesse*), note g at
  0595B (the whole *judicaverint/judicaverunt/judicarerunt/dijudicaverant* stemma), and note d at
  0597B (the consular dating: *p. c.*, *VI kal.*, *Theodosii VII aug.*, Vatican form). I checked
  each witness-to-reading pairing and each *non/nec* in the notes, and every one matches.
- **0597B, 0598A dates.** "The sixth day before the Kalends of February", with [n: Jan. 27, 417],
  consistent with p.c. Theodosius VII and Palladius. Correct.
