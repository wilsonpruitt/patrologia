## Findings

### F1. 7091/0001, col 0633A, Ep. II summary head. Class 3 (who does what to whom)
- **Latin:** `*Ut se et Eudoxiam ab excommunicatione liberet.*`
- **English as it stands:** `*That he free himself and Eudoxia from excommunication.*`
- **Proposed:** `*That he free him and Eudoxia from excommunication.*`
- **Why:** *liberet* has Innocent as subject (the letter asks the pope to lift the sentence: *obsecro ut
  nos a segregatione liberes*). *se* is the indirect reflexive, pointing back to Arcadius, the one asking.
  "Free himself" makes Arcadius (or the pope) the one who frees himself. The Ep. III head, *relaxet
  sententiam, qua se cum conjuge … arcet*, is rendered correctly in the same chunk ("by which he keeps him").
- **Confidence:** certain.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F2. 7091/0001, col 0633B, Ep. III opening. Class 1 (polarity), logged but I dispute the handling
- **Latin:** `Omnino, o pontifex Dei, nequidquam [ *Forte* nil quidquam] eorum, que in legatos … audacter perpetrata sunt, sciente me factum est`
- **English as it stands:** `Altogether, O pontiff of God, in vain [ *Perhaps* nil quidquam] of the things which were boldly perpetrated against the legates`
- **Proposed:** `Altogether, O pontiff of God, not anything [ *Perhaps* nil quidquam] of the things which were boldly perpetrated against the legates`
- **Why:** the printed *nequidquam* can be read as it is spelled, *ne quidquam* ("not anything"). That
  reading construes, keeps the negative the sentence rests on, and is what Migne's bracket supports.
  "In vain" drops the only negation in the clause, so the English reader gets "… was done with my
  knowledge", the opposite of Arcadius's plea (the letter's head is *Se insontem esse*). If "in vain" stays,
  it needs a `[cj:]`, because the faithful-looking English asserts the reverse (Pattern 18).
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In the cruces?** Yes (7091 cruces, 0633B). The stint rendered it literally and declined a `[cj:]`
  because the edition already glosses it. I think Pattern 18 still applies: Migne's bracket shows only the
  Latin word *nil quidquam*, and the English around it reads as an affirmative.

### F3. 7091/0001, col 0632C, Greek Ep. I §2. Class 1 (polarity in the English), logged but I dispute the handling
- **Greek:** `οὔτε οὐ δυνήσεται ἀξίωμα βιωτικόν τινα ὠφελῆσαι`
- **English as it stands:** `nor will worldly rank not be able to help anyone`
- **Proposed:** `nor will worldly rank be able to help anyone` (with the pleonastic οὐ kept in the crux),
  or keep the English as it stands and add `[cj:]` to it.
- **Why:** in Greek, οὔτε οὐ is an emphatic negative. In English a double negative cancels, so the line
  now says rank *will* help, the opposite of the Greek sense and of the Latin (*nec quemquam … adjuvari
  posse*). A literal rendering that reverses polarity in the target language doesn't preserve the evidence.
  It misstates it.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In the cruces?** Yes (7091 cruces, §2 Greek). It was carried deliberately under 7a. Submitted for
  adjudication.

### F4. 7091/0001, Greek heads at 0630C, 0634A, 0636A. Class 5 (wrong title), logged as a standing decision
- **Greek:** `ΙΝΝΟΚΕΝΤΙΟΥ ΡΩΜΗΣ ΑΡΧΙΕΡΕΩΣ` / `ΙΝΝΟΚΕΝΤΙΟΝ ΡΩΜΗΣ ΑΡΧΙΕΡΕΑ`
- **English as it stands:** `EPISTLE OF INNOCENT, ARCHPRIEST OF ROME, TO ARCADIUS THE EMPEROR.` (twice) and
  `EPISTLE OF ARCADIUS THE EMPEROR TO INNOCENT, ARCHPRIEST OF ROME.`
- **Proposed:** `HIGH PRIEST OF ROME` in all three.
- **Why:** "archpriest" is a real and different, subordinate office (Greek πρωτοπρεσβύτερος, Latin
  *archipresbyter*). Calling the pope an archpriest demotes him. Byzantine ἀρχιερεύς means a hierarch,
  i.e. a bishop. "High priest" is the literal sense and keeps the Greek apart from the Latin's
  *archiepiscopus*, which is the reason the stint gave.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In the cruces?** Yes, as a register decision (7091 cruces, Standing decisions). The wrong-office
  problem is not discussed there.

### F5. 7089/0000, col 0625C, Dissertatio ¶1. Class 3 / 4 (one witness made into several)
- **Latin:** `quod ab uno nostro manuscripto suppeditatur, nec in editis legitur`
- **English as it stands:** `which is supplied by one of our manuscripts and is not read in the printed editions`
- **Proposed:** `which is supplied by our manuscript alone and is not read in the printed editions`
- **Why:** *uno* is "alone/only", set against *in editis*. The writer has one codex, the Lucca breviary
  (*in ms. codice Lucensi*, *codex noster*). "One of our manuscripts" invents a plurality of witnesses.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F6. 7089/0000, col 0625D, Dissertatio ¶3. Class 3 (lexical sense misleads)
- **Latin:** `cum a Nicolao I in gemina epistola … tamquam ex Innocentio laudentur`
- **English as it stands:** `since they are praised as from Innocent by Nicholas I in a twin letter`
- **Proposed:** `since they are cited as from Innocent by Nicholas I in a twin letter`
- **Why:** *laudare* in scholarly Latin means "to cite, adduce". The argument is about attribution (Nicholas
  quotes the canon as Innocent's), not about approval.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F7. 7091/0000, col 0630C, Monitum §3. Class 3 / 7a (silent repair of the printed word)
- **Latin:** `cum istud tam insigne Arsacii *etiam post mortem* ordine suo moti non fugisset`
- **English as it stands:** `so notable a case of Arsacius, removed from his rank *even after death*, would not have escaped him`
- **Proposed:** keep the sense, and log a crux (and a plate/TEI check) for printed *cum*, which the
  apodosis needs to be *eum* ("him"). If the plate prints *cum*, the English is a silent repair of it.
- **Why:** as printed, the second *cum* leaves the main clause of *si publicae tum fuissent* without an
  object, and the English supplies "him" without saying so. It's the same kind of letter as the 0630B *eum*
  the stint did check at 600 dpi, three lines earlier.
- **Confidence:** probable (that a crux is owed; I can't say whose the *c* is without the plate).
- **In the cruces?** No. The 0630B entry covers *ut eum ne salutare quidem*, a different site.

### F9. 7089/0001, col 0628B, canon VII. Class 7a (non-word rendered for sense, no marker)
- **Latin:** `vel alii manum ponant inergumeno.`

## Considered and rejected
- 7089 canon II *non tacito* → "tacitly": a real word, logged in the cruces, and the literal rendering is
  right.
- 7089 canon VI (0628B) *presbyter missae teneat* has no *non*, and the English has none: literal, correct.
  The sense surely wants a negative, but the plate is read and the cruces logs it.
- 7089 canon IX (0628B) *et ungere chrisma, poenitentem … non liceat*: polarity carried correctly.
- 7089 Aurelius *Facile non imponatur manus Episcopo* → "on a Bishop": dative as printed. The Pauline sense
  ("let no hand be laid hastily; a bishop is not to be ordained from a layman") suggests *Episcopus*, but the
  English is a fair literal rendering of the dative. Not pressed.
- 7089 Tolosa (0627A) *Hispanos vituperant ii, qui …*: *ii* is the subject, and the English ("Those blame the
  Spaniards who …") parses it right, though the order is awkward.
- 7089 Dissertatio *nisi quod Pontifex ea in re nihil judicat decernendum*: polarity kept.
- 7089 Dissertatio *neque enim communione privantur uxorati … sed innocens ab adultero segregatur*: kept.
- 7089 *epitome litterarum Innocentii ad Aurelium* → "the letters": *litterae* is plausibly one letter (the
  next sentence says *epistola*). Idiomatic, not an error. Not pressed.
- 7089 *gemina epistola* → "a twin letter": could be "two letters", but it's ambiguous. Not pressed.
- 7089 Observatio *cum ab eis nec decretorum, nec scriptionis forma abhorreat* → "is foreign to them": the
  antecedent (Innocent's letters) is recoverable.
- 7089 Observatio *ut nemo non videt* → "as everyone sees": correct resolution of the double negative.
- 7091 Monitum §2 *factorumque* → "Greek acts": logged in the cruces (sense *fastorum*). A real word, so
  literal is right.
- 7091 Monitum §2 *in secunda et quarta epistola fingitur* (the English keeps "second and fourth"): printed
  as given. The Monitum's own numbering makes Ep. III, not IV, an Arcadius letter, but this is
  Coustant's text and the English reproduces it.
- 7091 Monitum §2 *nec privato cuique … nisi monitionibus praemissis*: correct.
- 7091 Monitum §3 *nusquam tum … exstitisse* → "did not yet exist anywhere at that time": "yet" is mild
  supply, but the sense is right.
- 7091 Greek Ep. I §1 βασιλεῦ → "O king": a logged register decision (the heads use "emperor").
  Acceptable.
- 7091 Ep. I note e *praeter sententiam Arcadii* → "contrary to the mind of Arcadius": fair ("without
  Arcadius's decision" would be tighter). Not pressed.
- 7091 Ep. IV note a *nec … nec … nihil curat* → "cares nothing, neither … nor": correct. This emphatic
  Latin double negative is handled as emphasis, while the Greek οὔτε οὐ in F3 is not, which is an
  inconsistency across the chunk.
- 7091 Ep. III *et si accusavimus* → "and if": could be *etsi*, but "and if" is as printed.
