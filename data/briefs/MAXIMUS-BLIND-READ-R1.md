# Maximus, *Epistulae*: blind read R1, chunks 0001–0005 (PG 91, cols 364–408)

Reader R1, 2026-11-06. Range: Letter 1 to George (whole letter plus scholia, chunks 0001–0003) and Letter 2 to John the
Cubicularius (chunks 0004–0005, through its scholia). I read every sentence of the Greek against the English, and the
Latin twin where a point needed it. I checked every negative in the Greek against the English, counted the anaphora
lists, and checked who does what in each nested speech (the damned speaking in Letter 1, the Baruch/Jeremiah speech in
Letter 2). I opened `cruces.md` only after the findings below were formed.

## Findings

### F1. Chunk 0001, col 0364 band B, s5: class 5 (an ascetic term turned)
- **Greek** (plate-read, p-0200): τῇ πρὸς τὸ ἓν ἀσχέτῳ νεύσει γενόμενος, εἷς τε καὶ μόνος, καὶ ἄτρεπτος.
- **Latin**: *qui nimirum libera in unum illud propensione* ("free inclining").
- **English as it stands**: `having become, by his unrestrained inclining toward the One, one and alone and unchangeable.`
- **Proposed English**: `having become, by his inclining toward the One, free of relation, one and alone and unchangeable.`
  (A shorter alternative: `by his unattached inclining toward the One`.)
- **Why**: ἄσχετος is the privative of σχέσις, which the glossary fixes as *relation*. In Maximus it means the inclining
  that is held by no tie to anything else. "Unrestrained" is the classical sense, and in an ascetic text it reads as a
  vice, the lack of self-control. The same translators give ἀσχέτως as "without relation" at col 0393 (chunk 0004, s3:
  "removed, without relation, from all the things comprised in the form of vice") and again at col 0508 (cruces line
  285). So this one place breaks a usage the work otherwise keeps.
- **Confidence**: probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** No. The 0508 crux sets out the rendering "without relation". Nothing is logged for 0364.

### F2. Chunk 0001, col 0369 band B, s22: class 3 (participle mis-parsed, so the sense shifts)
- **Greek** (plate-read, p-0203): ἵνα μὴ τὴν κατὰ φύσιν τοῦ θέλειν τῇ παραχρήσει διαφθείρωμεν δύναμιν, βουλομένην
  ἐπιλαβέσθαι τῶν ἀμηχάνων, καὶ ὑπ᾿ αὐτῆς κατορθοῦσθαι μὴ δυναμένων.
- **Latin**: *quo scilicet naturalem voluntatis vim per abusum minime vitiemus, dum quae illi impossibilia sunt aggredi
  nititur* ("while it strives to attempt what is impossible for it").
- **English as it stands**: `lest by misuse we corrupt the natural power of willing, which wishes to lay hold of things impracticable, and that cannot be achieved by it.`
- **Proposed English**: `lest by misuse we corrupt the natural power of willing, when it wishes to lay hold of things impracticable, and that cannot be achieved by it.`
- **Why**: βουλομένην is circumstantial. It names the misuse: the power is corrupted when it reaches for what it cannot
  achieve. As a relative clause ("which wishes"), it states that the *natural* power of willing by nature wants
  impossible things. That says the opposite of the argument, which is that this wanting is the παράχρησις and not the
  nature. Combefis's *dum … nititur* reads it the same way.
- **Confidence**: probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** No.

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **0005, col 0404 A, s11, "a partaker of divine fatness"**. The plate prints **ποιότητος** ("quality"; I read it on
   p-0220). The English follows Combefis's *pinguedinis* (= πιότητος). I flagged it as a possible place where the English
   follows the Latin over the Greek. ποιότητος parses, and θείας ποιότητος μέτοχος is a phrase Maximus could write.
   **Checked afterwards: logged.** It is in the misprint table (`p0220.12 | ποιότητος | πιότητος | plate`) and in the
   0404 crux, which argues from the vine-and-rooting image and Rom 11:17. That is a documented editorial judgment, so I
   leave it. If the editor wants the Greek kept, the replacement would be `a partaker of divine quality [lat: the Latin gives *pinguedinis*, fatness]`.
2. **0005, col 0404 A, s10, εἰς τὰ Ἅγια γίνεται τῶν ἁγίων → "comes to be within the Holy of Holies"**. One could also
   read it as "becomes one of the saints". The hyperbaton reading is defensible, and Combefis has *ipsa Sancta sanctorum
   penetrat*. Plate checked: the file is correct. Rejected.
3. **0004, col 0400 C, s26, τελείῳ τῷ κατὰ φύσιν διὰ φιλανθρωπίας λόγῳ συνένδημον → "to receive him, dwelling together
   with him, through love for man, by the perfect principle according to nature"**. συνένδημον agrees with τὸν Θεόν (God
   as Abraham's guest and fellow-dweller; Latin *hospitem ac contubernalem*). The English is loose about which of them
   dwells, but nothing is reversed. A tighter version would be "to receive him as a fellow-dweller". Style only; rejected.
4. **0002, col 0381 C, s38, ταχύ → "thickness"**. The English follows παχύ. Logged as a Migne misprint
   (`p0209.30 ταχύ | παχύ`). Rejected.
5. **0001, col 0365 B, s8, τῆς ψυχῆς χαυνώσῃ τὸ εὔτονον → "sweetening the soul's hearing … slacken its good tension"**.
   τῆς ψυχῆς could go with τὸ εὔτονον (M1 itself cites "the soul's good tension"). Either way the referent is the soul,
   and no sense is lost. Rejected.
6. **0001, col 0368 B, s15, μὴ συμβᾶσαν αὐτῷ τὴν σοφίαν κτησάμενος → "has acquired wisdom not as something that has
   befallen Him"**. The negative sits on the participle of accident, and the English carries it. Polarity is intact.
   Rejected.
7. **0005, col 0405 B, s18, ἵνα καὶ ὑμᾶς ἀποδεξάμενος εἴπῃ δι᾿ Ἱερεμίου → "that, receiving you too, he may say through
   Jeremiah"**. The bare "he" follows "the man who lives according to it". The speaker is God, and "through Jeremiah"
   settles that for any reader. Not a who-did-what flip. Rejected. (The quotation is from Baruch, and the Greek
   attributes it to Jeremiah. The English follows the Greek.)
8. **0003, col 0388 A, s36, Τί … μὴ κατασβέσαι … καὶ μὴ … τηγανίζεσθαι → "Why did we … not quench … and not be fried
   now"**. The English is slightly elliptical (the sense is "and so not be fried"), but it is not inverted. Rejected.
9. **0004, col 0396 A, s8, δύνανται [δυνάμεθα] → "we are able"**. The English takes the bracketed alternative printed in
   the text. Not a finding.
10. **0002, col 0377 C, s19, δεχώμεθα [ἐκδεχώμ.] τὴν θείαν ἀψευδῶς ἀπόφασιν → "await the divine sentence as
    unfailing"**. This follows the printed alternative ἐκδεχώμεθα. ἀψευδῶς, an adverb, is rendered as a predicate. A
    small shift with no change of sense. Rejected.
