# Maximus the Confessor, *Epistulae*: blind read R2 (chunks 0006–0010)

Reader R2, 2026-11-06. Brief: `MAXIMUS-BLIND-READ-BRIEF.md`. Range: chunks 0006–0010, Greek vs English sentence by
sentence, every negative checked; Latin consulted at points of doubt. Cruces checked only after findings were formed.

## Findings

### F1. Chunk 0006, col 0412 A (Letter 3, s11): the verb of the Isaiah quotation
- **Class:** 5 (wrong term: English follows the Latin/LXX, not Migne's Greek)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0224):** `Ἀνέχετε σύνδεσμον καὶ χειροτονίαν καὶ ῥῆμα γογγυσμοῦ` (italic on the plate, keyed to Combefis's note (f)). Plate confirms Ἀνέχετε; the file reads correctly.
- **Latin:** *Auferte colligationem, manusque protensionem [delectum] et verbum murmurationis* (Isa. lviii, 6 [sic, = 9]; LXX ἐὰν ἀφέλῃς ἀπὸ σοῦ σύνδεσμον…).
- **English as it stands:** `«Take away the bond, and the stretching-out of the hand, and the word of murmuring»`
- **Proposed English:** `«Hold back the bond, and the stretching-out of the hand, and the word of murmuring»` (or keep "Take away" and add a note that Migne's Greek prints Ἀνέχετε, "restrain / hold back", against the LXX ἀφέλῃς and Combefis's *Auferte*).
- **Why:** ἀνέχω does not mean "take away"; the English has silently taken the Latin's/LXX's verb. "Hold back / check" gives nearly the same sense, so this is a fidelity point, not a reversal. If the translator judged Ἀνέχετε a misprint for Ἀφέλετε, a note should say so; none stands in the English.
- **Confidence:** probable (that the English departs from the printed Greek: certain; that it needs changing rather than a note: probable).
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes. Cruces 0412 (line 109) records that the English reads Ἀνέλετε, "take away", for the printed Ἀνέχετε, and the misprint table lists `p0224.10 | Ἀνέχετε | Ἀνέλετε | plate`. So this was a deliberate, logged emendation. **Downgraded:** no change to the English is needed. At most, put the misprint note where a reader of the English will see it, since the English text itself gives no sign that it departs from what Migne prints.

### F2. Chunk 0009, col 0445 C (Letter 9, s4): who deifies, nature or God
- **Class:** 2 (who does what), with a Christological/ascetic weight: the English makes *nature* the agent of deification.
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0241):** `πλὴν μέντοι τῆς φύσεως. Ἐκείνη γάρ, τοῦθ᾿ ὅπερ ἐστὶ διαφυλάττει τὸν ἄνθρωπον, θεὸν ποιεῖν θέσει πέφυκε, θέωσιν ὡς ἀγαθὸς ὑπὲρ φύσιν τῷ ἀγομένῳ δωρούμενος· καὶ τῶν δύο καθαρῶς ἀποτέμνει, κόσμου λέγω καὶ φύσεως.` The plate reads exactly as the file: Migne's Greek has no subject for θεὸν ποιεῖν… (the God-clause has fallen out between διαφυλάττει τὸν ἄνθρωπον and θεὸν ποιεῖν; the next two sentences are its pair, Εἰ δὲ φύσις… / Εἰ δὲ κόσμος…).
- **Latin:** *excepta tamen natura, quippe quae, id quod est, incolumem reipsa servet hominem. Verbi gratia, siquidem Deus est qui agit trahitque hominem, deum adoptione facere novit … tanquam bonus largiens: a duobusque reliquis (mundo scilicet ac natura) liquido abscindit.* Combefis supplies the subject, God.
- **English as it stands:** `For that one — it preserves man as that which he is — is of a nature to make him god by adoption, bestowing deification, as good, above nature on the one led; and it cuts him off cleanly from the two, I mean the world and nature.`
- **Proposed English:** `For she preserves man as that which he is. ⟨But if it is God who leads him, he⟩ is of a nature to make him god by adoption, bestowing deification, as good, above nature on the one led; and he cuts him off cleanly from the two, I mean the world and nature.` with a note: "Migne's Greek has no subject here; the masculine δωρούμενος, the cutting-off from 'nature', and the paired Εἰ δὲ φύσις / Εἰ δὲ κόσμος clauses that follow require God, whom Combefis's Latin supplies (*siquidem Deus est qui agit*)."
- **Why:** As it stands, the one sentence makes "that one" (Ἐκείνη, nature, the exception just named) both the thing that preserves man unchanged and the thing that deifies him and cuts him off from "the world and nature". Nature cannot cut man off from nature, and the participle δωρούμενος is masculine, so it is not nature's. The English hands deification to nature, which reverses Maximus's point that only God deifies and nature alone keeps man as he is.
- **Confidence:** certain that the English is wrong as it reads; probable on the exact supplement wording.
- **Already in the cruces?** Partly. Cruces 0445 (line 174) says that "that one" means God, judged from the masculine ἀγαθός … δωρούμενος, and calls the Latin *siquidem Deus est* "a gloss and not a witness to a lost clause". So the translator understood the sentence correctly. **The English does not carry that understanding.** "For that one" comes straight after "except, however, nature", so it reads as nature, and the parenthesis "— it preserves man as that which he is —" (which is said of nature) makes the misreading worse. The finding stands as a defect in the English. If the crux's no-lacuna view is kept, a minimal fix that keeps the printed Ἐκείνη for nature and names the implied agent: `For she preserves man as that which he is; but ⟨God⟩ is of a nature to make him god by adoption, bestowing deification, as good, above nature on the one led; and he cuts him off cleanly from the two, I mean the world and nature.` Or keep "that one" and gloss it: `For that one (God)`, which is what the crux says.

### F3. Chunk 0010, col 0460 A (Letter 12, s3): the negative in the opening clause is muddled
- **Class:** 1 (polarity: the English can be read as saying the reverse)
- **Greek (file only; the sense is clear from the file and the Latin, so no plate needed):** `Καλῶς ἔχειν λογισάμενος, μηδὲν ὑμῖν τῶν εἰς ἐμὲ ἐρχομένων περὶ ὑμῶν αὐτῶν, θεοφύλακτοι, ἀναγγείλαι ὑποστείλασθαι`. Here μηδέν is the object of ὑποστείλασθαι: "to hold back nothing", and ἀναγγεῖλαι depends on it ("from announcing").
- **Latin:** *Operae pretium ratus … quidquid ad me rerum vestrarum venerit, nihil subtrahere, quominus vobis annuntiem*
- **English as it stands:** `Having reckoned it well to hold back from announcing to you nothing of what comes to me concerning yourselves, God-guarded ones`
- **Proposed English:** `Having reckoned it well not to hold back from announcing to you anything of what comes to me concerning yourselves, God-guarded ones`
- **Why:** "To hold back from announcing … nothing" reads naturally as "to refrain from telling you nothing", a double negative that leaves a reader unsure whether Maximus tells all or tells nothing. The Greek and Latin both say he withholds nothing. The proposed wording puts the negative on the verb, where it belongs.
- **Confidence:** probable (it is a matter of phrasing, but the phrasing as it stands can be read backwards).
- **Already in the cruces?** No. The 0460 cruces (lines 202–208) cover the addressee, αὐτοαγάπη, the indiction, the cancellarius, Patricia, the prefect, and Severus, but not this clause.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **0006, 0409 (L3 s3)** ἀλλήλοις ὑμᾶς ἑνῶσαι: the English has "us" for ὑμᾶς. Context (ἡμῖν in the same clause; God's general law for all men) requires "us"; ὑμᾶς/ἡμᾶς is a stock itacism. Not worth a finding.
- **0006, 0412 (L3 s11–12)** ᾧ τὸ μεταμελεῖσθαι παρέπεται: the referent (the deliberating) is ambiguous in English but not wrong.
- **0006, 0416 (L4 s22)** πρὸς τὰ πνευματικὰ τῆς πονηρίας διάγνωσιν: "before the spiritual hosts" for πρός ("in face of / against"). It is within range and the sense stands.
- **0007, 0428 B (L6 syll. 5 s8)** τὸ ἀκούσιον: plate-read. Migne prints ἀκούσιον, and the English "without essence" follows the sense ἀνούσιον. Logged in the misprint table (p0232.20). Correct handling.
- **0007, 0429 (L6 s29)** "seems to me": "to me" is supplied (οὐ μὴ δοκεῖ). The sense is unaffected.
- **0007 (L6, syllogism heading)**: the Greek [n: Συλλογισμοὶ φυσικοί…] is not rendered in the English. It is Migne's apparatus [n:], so it is excluded by the brief.
- **0007, 0428 D (L6 s13)** ἢ Θεὸς ἐξ ἡμῶν … ἢ παρ᾿ ἡμῶν προσηγορεύθη Θεός: the English is clumsy ("or rather, was called God from us") but does not reverse anything.
- **0009, 0448 A (L9 s12)** πάσχειν καλῶς: plate-read. Migne prints καλῶς, and the English "suffer evil" follows the sense (the s15 parallel πάσχειν κακῶς … ὑπὲρ ἀρετῆς; Latin *mala ferre*). Logged (p0242.13). Correct handling.
- **0009 (L11 s2)** "or rather, the first and only mark of the infinite goodness", applied to the Word: it could also be taken as said of repentance (neuter ὅπερ … θειότερον). Combefis takes it of the Word (*Verbum (quinimo, primum illud solumque infinitae bonitatis insigne)*). The English follows a defensible parse.
- **0009 (L11 s20)** διὰ τὸν φόβον: attached in the English to "buy salvation" rather than to συνεπάγεται. Either parse is possible.
- **0009 (L11 s27)** εἰ μή τι ἄλλο … μιμησάμενοι: the English rendering "if we have not imitated it at least toward our own kind" keeps the sense.
- **0010, 0460 B (L12 s3)** καινῆς δεσποίνης: the English "common" follows the sense κοινῆς (cf. s10 τὴν κοινὴν ἡμῶν δέσποιναν). Logged (p0248.15).
- **0010, 0461 A (L12 s6)** Thomas "happens to be one of those who come to her": the Greek and Latin are equally ambiguous between this being part of the heretics' lie and being a fact. The English follows the construction.
- **0010, 0461 C–D (L12 s12)** ὅπερ θαυμάζω, οὐκ ἀποδέχομαι· οὐκ ἔξω βάρους εἶναι ὑμᾶς τεκμαιρόμενος: the English moves "do not accept" into the relative clause and makes "I judge" the main verb. Both negatives are kept and the sense stands.
- **0010, 0461 D (L12 s13)** πολλοῖς ῥερυπωμένος: the nominative agrees with Jehoshaphat, but the sense (idolatry) requires Ahab, and the English gives it to Ahab. This is probably a misprint (-μένῳ); not plate-read, since the English follows the only possible sense.
- **0010, 0468 (Exposition s3)** τὴν τῆς σαρκὸς αὐτοῦ φύσιν καὶ ὑμετέραν: "ours" for ὑμετέραν. This is an itacism and the sense requires "ours".
