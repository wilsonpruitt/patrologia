## Findings

(written progressively; see each entry)

### F1. 0011, col 473A — class 1 (polarity: the negative moved from the infinitive to "follows")
- **Greek** (file only; the sentence parses cleanly): καὶ ἀναιρουμένου πάλιν ἑνὸς, μηδὲ τὸ ἕτερον εἶναι λέγειν ἀκόλουθον.
- **Latin:** *rursus sublato uno, consequens fit ut nec dicamus … alterum esse.*
- **English as it stands:** "and when one, again, is taken away, it does not follow to say that the other is either."
- **Proposed:** "and when one, again, is taken away, it follows to say that the other is not either."
- **Why:** ἀκόλουθον is positive and μηδέ negates εἶναι. The step in the syllogism is "remove one, and the other is
  removed too." The English says only that the other's existence cannot be inferred, which is a weaker claim, and
  the co-introduction argument of s9–s11 then fails.
- **Confidence:** certain.

### F2. 0012, col 480C — class 2 (who does what: the condescension belongs to the invisible Word)
- **Greek** (file only): καὶ τὸν ἀόρατον αἰσθήσει ὑποπεσεῖν ἡμετέρᾳ, διὰ τὴν φυσικὴν ἰδιότητα τῆς ἐξ ἡμῶν ἁγίας αὐτοῦ
  σαρκὸς ἀνασχέσθαι ὁμολογεῖν αἰσχυνομένους.
- **Latin:** *eumdemque, qui invisibilis sit, … sub sensum nostrum cadere non abnuisse, fateri vereantur.*
- **English as it stands:** "and are ashamed to bring themselves to confess that the invisible fell under our sense
  because of the natural property of his holy flesh from us."
- **Proposed:** "and are ashamed to confess that the invisible endured to fall under our sense because of the natural
  property of his holy flesh from us."
- **Why:** τὸν ἀόρατον is the subject of ἀνασχέσθαι, and ὑποπεσεῖν depends on it. The sentence mirrors the Nestorius
  member a line above (κρατεῖσθαι σαρκὶ τὸν ἀπερίγραφον εὐδοκῆσαι, "the uncircumscribed was pleased … to be held by
  flesh"). The English gives the Word's "enduring" to the heretics ("bring themselves") and so loses the parallel
  kenosis.
- **Confidence:** probable (high).

### F3. 0012, cols 481B–484C — class 5 (a fixed Chalcedonian adverb varied)
- **Greek** (file only): ἀδιαιρέτως, five times in 0012, in the formula δύο φύσεις ἡνωμένας ἀδιαιρέτως and in Cyril's
  τὸ δὲ ἀδιαιρέτως προστεθέν.
- **Glossary rule:** Sophronius R1 (inherited by Maximus) fixes Chalcedon's four adverbs as *without confusion,
  without change, without division, without separation*. The same formula is "without division" in 0013 col 496C
  ("to say two natures united without division is significant of right belief") and in 0014 col 500B.
- **English as it stands → proposed** (each string unique in 0012):
  1. "who say two natures united undividedly after the union conflicts" → "who say two natures united without division after the union conflicts"
  2. «The addition "undividedly" seems somehow» → «The addition "without division" seems somehow»
  3. "he sets down saying two undividedly as orthodox" → "he sets down saying two without division as orthodox"
  4. "united undividedly — by the union, that is, according to hypostasis" → "united without division — by the union, that is, according to hypostasis"
  5. "saying two natures united undividedly after the union, but the sense" → "saying two natures united without division after the union, but the sense"
- **Why:** this is the formula the whole Succensus section argues over, and 0013 s22 quotes it back. As the English
  stands, a reader sees two different wordings and cannot tell they are one. (The noun τὸ ἀδιαίρετον, "the undivided",
  is fine. 0011 col 476B "possess undividedly the quantity of the colours" is the stone analogy, not the formula: optional.)
- **Confidence:** probable. It depends on whether the glossary binds the adverb outside the four-adverb list. R1's
  wording says it does.

### F4. 0012, col 485A — class 4/3 (an added phrase; the μηδέ attached to the wrong member)
- **Greek** (file only): Ἔδει γὰρ αὐτοὺς, εἴπερ ἀληθῶς τοῦτο μὴ ἦν, μηδὲ τὴν διαφορὰν ψιλῇ καὶ μόνῃ τῇ προφορᾷ, ὡς κατὰ
  μηδενὸς κειμένην, πρὸς ἐξαπάτην τῶν ἀφελεστέρων εἰρωνευτικῶς προφέρουσι, μὴ παραιτεῖσθαι λέγειν, τίνα καὶ πόσα …
- **Latin:** *Decebat enim, nisi vere sic erat, nec nuda solaque voce, velut de nullo dictam, nec reipsa afficientem,
  irrisione ad simpliciores decipiendos, distinctionem … proferunt, nulla tergiversatione dicere …* (Combefis
  expands with *nec reipsa afficientem*.)
- **English as it stands:** "they who put forward the difference not even as of things, but by bare and sole utterance, as though it were predicated of nothing, ironically, to deceive the simpler."
- **Proposed** (whole sentence): "For they ought — if this were truly not so, and they were not putting forward the difference by bare and sole utterance, as though it were predicated of nothing, ironically, to deceive the simpler — not to refuse to say what and how many the different things are of which they confess the difference to be."
- **Why:** "not even as of things, but" has nothing under it in the Greek. It looks like μηδέ given a supplied object,
  partly echoing Combefis's *nec reipsa*. The μηδέ belongs to the same hypothetical as εἴπερ … μὴ ἦν. If the full
  recast is too bold, the minimum fix is to delete "not even as of things, but".
- **Confidence:** probable. The sentence is knotted (προφέρουσι is dative or finite against accusative αὐτούς).

### F5. 0014, col 504B — class 5 (the Scripture conformed to the received verb)
- **Greek** (file only): Σκοποῦντας τί τὸ θέλημα τοῦ Θεοῦ τὸ ἀγαθὸν καὶ εὐάρεστον καὶ τέλειον.
- **Latin:** *Considerantes …*
- **English as it stands:** "Proving what is the will of God, the good and acceptable and perfect"
- **Proposed:** "Considering what is the will of God, the good and acceptable and perfect"
- **Why:** Rom 12:2 has δοκιμάζειν ("prove"), and Maximus prints σκοπεῖν. The PG rule is Scripture as printed, so the
  conventional verb should not be restored.
- **Confidence:** probable (high).

### F6. 0014, col 508C — class 3/5 (a word that reads as its opposite)
- **Greek** (file only): ἀνοχὴν διωγμοῖς ἐξισοῦν· βλασφημίαις παράκλησιν·
- **Latin:** *maledictis obsecrationem.*
- **English as it stands:** "exhortation to blasphemies"
- **Proposed:** "entreaty to blasphemies"
- **Why:** the phrase is gapped under ἐξισοῦν: match entreaty to blasphemies, as in 1 Cor 4:13 βλασφημούμενοι
  παρακαλοῦμεν. In English, "exhortation to blasphemies" reads as urging men to blaspheme, the reverse of the sense.
- **Confidence:** probable (high).

### F7. 0011 col 472B and 0014 col 505D — class 5 (addressee's number: singular rendered "you")
- **(a) Greek** (file only): « Ἀμοιρήσεις δ᾿ ἂν καὶ μώμου παντὸς, τὸ εἰδέναι τυχὸν … » (2 sg.)
  - **English as it stands:** "« You would be free of all blame in knowing, perhaps"
  - **Proposed:** "« Thou wouldst be free of all blame in knowing, perhaps"
- **(b) Greek** (file only): Ἀγαπήσεις Κύριον τὸν Θεόν σου ἐξ ὅλης τῆς καρδίας σου, καὶ ἐξ [0508] ὅλης τῆς ψυχῆς σου
  - **English as it stands:** "You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart, and with [0508] all your soul"
  - **Proposed:** "Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with all thy heart, and with [0508] all thy soul"
- **Why:** Pattern 17 and the brief: thou is singular, you is plural. Both are singular in the Greek. (Matt 7:16
  ἐπιγνώσεσθε, Rom 16:17 ὑμᾶς, and the plural ὑμῶν to the "master" in 0014 s30 are correctly "you".)
- **Confidence:** certain on the Greek number. It is a finding only if the rule reaches quotations, and Pattern 17
  says it does.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **0011 col 469D** ὡς οὐχ εὔλογον ὁμολογεῖν τὰ σῳζόμενα, "that it is not reasonable to confess": correct.
- **0011 col 472B** εὐσεβῶς … νοούμενον κατ᾿ αὐτῶν καὶ λεγόμενον. αὐτῶν could be "against them", but the English
  "according to him" agrees with the Latin *ex ejus mente*; probably αὐτόν. Not reported.
- **0011 col 472C** Cyril's τῷ πρὸς μίαν συντελεῖν ὑπόστασιν, "by concurring": the dative is rendered as printed. Fine.
- **0011 col 476B** τὸ μοναδικόν, "singleness": the glossary fixes μοναδικός = singular. "Singleness" is a noun for the
  noun (M1) on the same stem. Noted, not reported; the glossary was not opened.
- **0011 col 477A** the parallel in s3 (difference indicative of quantity, introducing the how; number indicative of the
  quantity of differing things, introductive of difference): the government is correct.
- **0012 col 480B** πῶς τὴν διαίρεσιν τροπώσασθαι οὐ γινώσκομεν; → "how are we not ignorant of how to put division to
  flight?" The English has two negatives where the Greek has one. A literal "how do we not know how …?" would read as
  "surely we know", the opposite of the sense, so the translator's version carries the right sense. Logged in the 0480
  crux. Rejected.
- **0012 col 481A** "as Saint Cyril supposes" (ὑποτίθεται): it could suggest that Cyril grants the conflict, but the
  Greek is equally open and the 0481 crux explains it. Rejected.
- **0012 col 484D** οὐ μᾶλλον δρῶντες, ἢ πάσχοντες, "not doing more than suffering": awkward but not reversed.
- **0013 col 496B** ὥσπερ οὖν καὶ τοῦ ἓν εἶναι … ἀδύνατον: the English "for these not to be one … is also impossible"
  supplies the μή carried over from τοῦ μὴ δύο εἶναι. This is right; without it the clause would deny the union.
- **0013 col 492C** καὶ στέργομεν εὐπειθῶς τῆς μιᾶς τὴν στέρησιν, "Then we readily acquiesce": "Then" is not in the
  Greek, but it gives the apodosis the Greek implies. Rejected.
- **0013 cols 496B–C** the nine οὐκ ἄν counterfactuals, including οὐκ ἂν … μὴ ἀνῃρῆσθαι and οὐκ ἂν μὴ μεταπεφοιτηκέναι …
  μὴ μεταβληθῆναι: every negative checked; all correct.
- **0014 col 505A** σπουδάσωμεν; with a Greek question mark: hortatory, and followed at once by ἵνα. This is punctuation,
  not a question. Rejected.
- **0014 col 505C** ἀλλὰ μὴ ἐναπομένειν αὐτοῖς, "not to remain among them": "them" sits next to "the things that stand",
  but the Greek αὐτοῖς is equally ambiguous and the sense (the visible things) is recoverable. Rejected.
- **0014 col 505B** καθ᾿ ἣν ἑαυτοῖς προσλαβεῖν ἀρχόμενοι, "beginning to converse with ourselves": this suggests
  προσλαλεῖν. The Latin for this line was not located in the twin. Flagged for a translator's look, not reported.
- **0014 col 500B** ἀδιαιρέτως = "without division": glossary-correct (it is the baseline for F3).
- **0014 s30** Δέσποτά μου εὐλογημένε with plural ὑμῶν → "you": correct per the brief, and logged.
