## Findings

### F1. Chunk 0015, col. 513D (two sites, 513D and 516C). Class 5, wrong term: πάντως rendered "wholly" in the syllogism

**(a) col. 513D, ⟦p3⟧ s5.**
- **Greek** (file only): Εἰ δὲ μὴ κατὰ πάντα καὶ λόγον καὶ τρόπον ἕν ἐστιν ὁ Χριστὸς μετὰ τὴν ἕνωσιν· δηλονότι κατά τινα λόγον τε καὶ τρόπον, **δύο πάντως** ἐστὶν ὁ Χριστὸς μετὰ τὴν ἕνωσιν.
- **Latin**: *secundum aliquam et rationem et modum omnino Christus duo est post unionem* (*omnino* is just as ambiguous, so it does not decide the point).
- **English as it stands**: `Christ is wholly two after the union`
- **Proposed**: `Christ is certainly two after the union`
- **Why**: here πάντως is the logical "necessarily, certainly" of the conclusion, not "in every way". "Wholly two" says Christ is two in every respect. That is the Nestorian thesis Maximus rejects six lines later, in s9: κατὰ πάντα λόγον τε καὶ τρόπον οὐκ ἔστιν ὁ Χριστὸς δύο. It also contradicts the "according to some principle and mode" in the same clause. The English makes the step say the opposite of its restriction.
- **Confidence**: probable (certain that "wholly" misleads; πάντως = "certainly" is the standard sense in this syllogistic register).

**(b) col. 516C, ⟦p3⟧ s10.**
- **Greek** (file only): Εἰ δὲ μὴ κατὰ πάντα λόγον τε καὶ τρόπον ἐστὶν ὁ Χριστὸς δύο μετὰ τὴν ἕνωσιν· **πάντως** κατά τινὰ λόγον τε καὶ τρόπον … ἓν ἐστιν ὁ Χριστός.
- **English as it stands**: `then wholly according to some principle and mode`
- **Proposed**: `then certainly according to some principle and mode`
- **Why**: this is the mirror step of (a). "Wholly according to some principle" contradicts itself, and the parallel needs the same word as (a).
- **Confidence**: probable.
- **Related, not proposed**: chunk 0019, col. 549B: `wholly of other hypostases` (πάντως ἀλλήλοις ἑτεροϋπόστατα), `wholly other in essence`, and col. 552B `wholly consubstantial`. These are the same πάντως, but there "wholly" does not turn the claim, so I note them for consistency only. If (a) and (b) are adopted, a sweep of πάντως → "wholly" in the dogmatic letters (12–15, 19) is the class check.
- **Already in the cruces?** Not logged. (The cruces table has a misprint entry for πάντως at p0206, which is a different chunk and a different issue.)

## Candidates considered and rejected

1. **0015, col. 512C, `as they think, wronging the heralds of piety in no way`** (μηδὲν ἀδικοῦντες, ὡς οἴονται, … τοὺς κήρυκας). Where ὡς οἴονται belongs is ambiguous ("not harming them, as they suppose they do" or "harming none, so they think"). The English keeps the Greek order, and Combefis does the same (*nihilque laedentes, ut autumant*). There is no reversal I can prove.
2. **0015, col. 512A, `grateful to God`** (εὐχάριστός τε τῷ Θεῷ). "Acceptable/pleasing to God" would match εὐάρεστος and εὐπρόσιτος better. But "grateful" is a real sense of the word, Latin *gratus* is equally two-sided, and the sense is not turned. This is a style point, not a finding.
3. **0016, col. 520B, διαστρέψαι σπέρμα** ("to pervert seed") **for LXX διαθρέψαι** in Gen 7:3. The English follows the sense ("keep seed alive"). Already in the cruces misprint table (p0284.28, plate).
4. **0016, col. 521D, τὴν οἰκον· μίαν·**. Damaged text. The English "economy" follows the sense, and the Latin has *dispensationem … ac nullam … naturam*. Already in the misprint table (p0285.51).
5. **0016, col. 520B, Υἷς for Οἷς** (`Among which`). The English follows the sense. Not a finding.
6. **0017, col. 528A, the definitions of hypostasis** (οὐσία τις τῶν καθ᾿ ἕκαστα περιληπτικὴ …), English `an essence comprehensive of the particulars, of all the idioms`. I suspected τῶν καθ᾿ ἕκαστα belongs with οὐσία τις ("a particular essence"), not with περιληπτική. Combefis takes it the English way (*quae singulatim omnes proprietates … complectatur*). The parse is defensible, so rejected.
7. **0017, col. 528C, διὰ τὸ σαφὲς εἶναι**, rendered as purpose (`so that it may be clear`) where the Greek is causal. The sense of the step is unchanged (definitions start from the generic because that is what makes the "what-it-is" clear). Rejected.
8. **0017, col. 528D, s11**. The English starts an apodosis ("then clearly every hypostasis too …") at a καί-member that could still belong to the protasis, with the apodosis at Δείξωσιν οὖν. δηλονότι supports the English. Not a defect.
9. **0018, col. 537C, `having come to know his simple disposition … I preferred him to my own soul`**. The object of προὐτίμησα is more probably τὴν ἁπλῆν αὐτοῦ … διάθεσιν ("I valued his simple disposition above my own soul"). The English moves the object to Cosmas himself. It is close in sense and does not change who did what, so I note it and do not report it.
10. **0018, col. 537C, Ὠφελεῖτε**, rendered as an indicative (`For you benefit him`). It could be an imperative. γάρ after the request reads naturally as an indicative of the reason, so I rejected it.
11. **0018, col. 541D, καὶ οὐ ξενιζόμεθα παντελῶς, καὶ σάλον κατὰ ψυχὴν ὑπομένομεν**. The second member carries no negative of its own. The English carries the οὐ through (`nor do we undergo a tossing`), which is what the sense and the following ἀλλά require. This is not an added negative. A plate check could show whether an οὐδέ was lost, but the English is right either way.
12. **0019, col. 544D, ἁψάμενος** (`thou, touching upon the very subtle doctrines`). The participle could agree with λόγος ("a discussion which touched on …") and not with the second-person subject. Both readings are grammatical, and the Latin *auspicatus* is no clearer. Rejected.
13. **0019, Gregory quotation (col. 548B), ὡς μήτε … συναιρεθέντα …**: the quotation stops after one μήτε member. That is how Maximus prints it, and the English follows. Not a missing clause.
14. **Duplicate sentence markers** in the English (two ⟦s3⟧ in 0015, merged ⟦s4+5+6⟧ and similar groups throughout) are structural, not findings. I counted sentences across every merged group and found no clause missing.
15. **Second person**: Letter 13 moves from the third-person "my blessed master" to `you blessed ones` (τοὺς εὐλογημένους ὑμᾶς). Letter 14 addresses `you, God-guarded ones` in the plural throughout. Letter 15 addresses Cosmas as `thou` (ἀπήτησας, εὑρήσεις). Gregory's οἶδας is `thou knowest` and περιαστράφθητε is `be ye`. All follow the Greek.
