## Findings

### F1. 0024, col 0597 C (Letter 20 to Marinus, opening period), class 2 (what a pronoun refers to)
- **Greek** (file only; the sense question needs no plate): ὡς οὐ μόνον αὐτὸς ἐν ἐμαυτῷ διὰ τῶν πονηρῶν μου τρόπων τοὺς ζωοποιοὺς τοῦ Θεοῦ καταφονεύσας **λόγους**, ἀλλὰ καὶ τοῖς ἀκούουσι τῆς πρὸς **αὐτοὺς** καταφρονήσεως εἰκόνα ζῶσαν … παρεχόμενος.
- **Latin**: *verum etiam qui eorum contemptus expressam vivamque imaginem, auditoribus … ingeram*. Here *eorum* means the *sermones*.
- **English as it stands**: `presenting to the hearers the whole evil conduct of my life as a living image, plain to all, of contempt for them.`
- **Proposed**: `presenting to the hearers the whole evil conduct of my life as a living image, plain to all, of contempt for those words.`
- **Why**: αὐτούς means the life-giving λόγους he has just "murdered". In the English the nearest antecedent of "them" is "the hearers", so it reads as Maximus showing contempt for his audience. That is a different sin from the one he confesses.
- **Confidence**: certain. **In cruces?** No (checked after).

### F2. 0024, col 0597 D–0600 (Letter 20), class 1 (a negative in the wrong clause)
- **Greek** (file only): πᾶσι γίνεται τοῖς θείοις κατάκομος ἀγαθοῖς, **μηδενὶ τρόπῳ** τὴν ἐν αὐτῷ γνῶσιν **ἀργεῖν** δειχθῆναι παρασκευάζων.
- **Latin**: *hoc praestans, ne qua fultus scientia est, ullo modo iners illi ac otiosa sit.* The Latin puts the negative inside the purpose clause.
- **English as it stands**: `contriving in no way that the knowledge in him be shown idle.`
- **Proposed**: `contriving that the knowledge in him be shown in no way idle.`
- **Why**: μηδενὶ τρόπῳ goes with ἀργεῖν (the knowledge is never idle). It does not go with παρασκευάζων. The English says the man makes no effort to have his knowledge idle. The Greek says he works to make sure it is never idle. The scope of the negative has shifted and the sentence's point is weakened.
- **Confidence**: probable (tending to certain, given the Latin). **In cruces?** No.

### F3. 0024, col 0604 D–0605 A (Letter 21 to the bishop of Cydonia), class 2/3 (wrong subject)
- **Greek** (file only): ὡς τὴν ἱερωσύνην ὁ Θεὸς ἐπὶ γῆς ἀνθ᾿ ἑαυτοῦ χειροτονήσας προὐβάλετο. Ἐφ᾿ ᾧτε καὶ σωματικῶς **ὁρώμενος**, καὶ τὰ **αὐτοῦ** μυστήρια τοῖς ὁρᾶν δυναμένοις μὴ **διαλίπῃ** φαινόμενα.
- **Latin**: *Deum sui vice sacerdotium in terra ordinasse ac constituisse, in quo corporaliter ipse semper cerneretur, ejusque mysteria valentibus intueri nunquam conspicua esse desinerent.* Here *ipse* is God.
- **English as it stands**: `so that, even while it is seen bodily, it may not fail to make his mysteries apparent to those able to see.`
- **Proposed**: `so that he, being seen even bodily, may not fail to make his mysteries apparent to those able to see.`
- **Why**: ὁρώμενος is masculine and cannot agree with ἱερωσύνη (feminine). The subject of διαλίπῃ is God. In his priesthood God himself is seen bodily, and his mysteries do not cease to appear. The English makes the priesthood the thing seen and the agent. That loses the claim the letter goes on to praise, that the bishop is God's image (θεοειδῶς).
- **Confidence**: probable. **In cruces?** No. The cruces line for 0604 is about the bishop's third person only.

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **0020 p1.s4** συνόδῳ τῇ καθ᾿ ἕνωσιν φυσικὴν …: I first suspected that φυσικὴν belongs with ὑπόστασιν. It does not. καθ᾿ ἕνωσιν φυσικήν is κατά with the accusative, so "the concourse by natural union, that is true and real" is right. The same formula recurs at 0021 p4.s10/15.
2. **0020 p1.s5** "lest the coming-to-be of a difference … become a total passing-away": the printed ἡ (article) where s2 has ἢ. The English construes the printed ἡ grammatically, and the sense is not reversed.
3. **0020 p5.s17**: my aligner seemed to show the first half missing, but that was a duplicated ⟦s17⟧ marker in the English. The text is all there. The duplicate marker is a mechanical point, not a reading finding.
4. **0021 p1.s2 / 0022 p2.s6** "how … not just … according to some" (κατά τινας): the English keeps the Greek's ambiguity, and the next clause makes clear that "some" are the opponents. Left alone.
5. **0021 p3.s8** ἔσται πρὸς τὴν ἑτεροούσιον: a misprint (listed in cruces as πρὸς αὐτὸν ἑτεροούσιος). The English renders the sense, "of other essence toward him".
6. **0021 p4.s3** "believing Christ to be": a bare εἶναι, rendered literally. It is odd but it is the Greek.
7. **0022 p1.s2** George's position in "what had befallen, … the lord George": "befallen" takes George as its object, which is the Greek dative of the person affected. Correct, if dense.
8. **0022 p1.s7** "at the extreme of his own hypostatic monad" for τῷ κατ᾿ ἄκρον … ἑνικῷ: loose ("by the supreme singleness …"), but nothing is reversed.
9. **0022 p1.s9** "achieved by those who love the Lord only by hardship of the flesh": "only" could be misread as going with "love the Lord". Minor. Possible fix: "achieved only by hardship of the flesh … by those who love the Lord". Not filed.
10. **0022 p2.s20** ἡλικία where Ps 86:7 LXX has κατοικία: the English renders "dwelling" by the sense. The Scripture locator is Combefis's.
11. **0023 p1.s4** "he, as good" for printed ὁ ἡμῶν ἀγαθός, and "after the incarnation" for μετὰ τοσαύτην σάρκωσιν: I formed these as a candidate and then found them logged with the second read and the Latin (cruces 0585). Withdrawn.
12. **0023 p1.s15** "to gratify … deceiving men who have been taught only to bait toward death" (ἀπατεῶσιν ἀνθρώποις = to deceitful men): "deceiving" could be taken as a participle with the nuns as subject. Read as an adjective it is right, and the dative object is kept. Not filed. A clearer version would be "deceitful men".
13. **0023 p2.s11** Letter 19: through divine operations "showing what he became unaltered", through sufferings "what he was not changed". This is the reverse of the Letter 16 pattern, but the Greek is chiastic and the English follows it.
14. **0023 p2.s17/32** "your letters", "your zeal" (ὑμετέροις, ὑμετέρας) inside a thou-letter to Pyrrhus: the English follows the Greek pronoun, as the brief says. Not a finding.
15. **0024 p1.s18** "not allowing the law of the flesh, through the praised mortification of the members, to rise up": the parenthetical goes with "allowing". Acceptable.
16. **0024 Letter 22**, ἐξ οὗ τε καὶ εἰς δι᾿ …: a damaged phrase, rendered by the sense ("from whom and into whom"). Acceptable.
