## Findings

### F1. 0026, col 0632 A (Letter 36): Greek FILE misreads the plate (English is right)
- **Class:** 5 (wrong word, in our Greek file, not in the English)
- **Greek, file:** `ὥσπερ οἴνῳ τὴν χρείαν λόγῳ διαθερμαίνων πρὸς ἔκστασιν θεοποιὸν τῶν σῶν ὀπαδῶν`
- **Greek, plate-read (p-0350, right column, first line):** `ὥσπερ οἴνῳ τὴν καρδίαν λόγῳ διαθερμαίνων` — καρδίαν is clear.
- **Latin:** *tanquam vino, ad deificam exstasim discipulorum tuorum corda concalefacias* (agrees with the plate).
- **English as it stands:** "warming the heart of thy followers to a god-making ecstasy" — correct; it renders the plate.
- **Proposed:** no change to the English. Correct the Greek file `χρείαν` → `καρδίαν` (and/or log it), since the
  English and the Greek file now disagree and a later checker reading the file would flag the English.
- **Why:** the file's χρείαν ("need") is a read error; the translator silently followed the plate/Latin.
- **Confidence:** certain (plate-read).
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes, at the 0632 entry ("…unforgetting knowledge, as with wine, warming the heart"). **But that crux is wrong about the plate.** It says "The Greek prints τὴν χρείαν … the English reads καρδίαν." The plate prints καρδίαν. χρείαν is our file's misreading, not Migne's. The crux should be corrected, because as it stands it charges Migne's plate with a defect it does not have (the CLAUDE.md attribution rule). Proposed crux text: "T. Our Greek file read τὴν χρείαν; the plate prints τὴν καρδίαν (re-read 2026-11-06, p-0350), with the Latin *corda*." The file's Greek should be corrected too.

### F2. 0027, col 0641 B/C (Letter 43, s23): "not yet" turned into "thus" — polarity, silently conformed to Letter 24
- **Class:** 1 (polarity)
- **Greek, plate-read (p-0355, left column, line ending band B):** `Οὔπω γὰρ τιμᾶται τὰ θεῖα δωρήματα, καὶ ἡ τῆς θείας εἰρήνης φιλοφρονεῖται παρουσία.` The file has `Οὕπω` (breathing aside, the same word); the plate clearly sets -πω, not -τω.
- **Latin:** *Nondum enim divina munera honorantur, divinæque pacis præsentia magnifice præstatur.* Combefis read "not yet" too.
- **English as it stands:** "For thus are the divine gifts honoured, and the presence of the divine peace welcomed with friendliness." (the second of the two identical sentences in the range; the one in 0027)
- **Proposed English:** "For not yet are the divine gifts honoured, nor the presence of the divine peace welcomed with friendliness."
- **Why:** Letter 24's twin sentence (0025 s23) prints Οὕτω, and the 43 English was copied from it. Letter 43 prints Οὔπω and the Latin agrees. If the translator thought Οὔπω was Migne's misprint for Οὕτω, that is a conjecture against both columns, and it belongs in a crux, not silently in the text (Pattern 7a). (In my proposal the καί clause is turned to "nor" to carry the negative over; "and … not yet welcomed" would also do.)
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes. The 0641 entry gives Οὕπω and *Nondum*, and says the English follows Letter 24's Οὕτω "which the sense requires". The misprint table has `p0355.29 | Οὕπω | Οὕτω | plate`. So this is a logged, deliberate emendation, not a silent one. **I am keeping it as a finding for adjudication, at lower force.** Combefis translated *Nondum*, so he did not take it for a misprint. And "For not yet are the divine gifts honoured" construes as a reproach: we have not yet honoured them so. The emendation reverses a negative that both columns of the page print. If it stands, the misprint-table entry is what licenses it, and an editor should confirm it, not a translator.
- **Confidence:** certain that the plate and Latin read "not yet" and the English reverses them. Whether Οὔπω is the text or a misprint is an editor's call. Either way, a reader of this English cannot see that the plate differs.

### F3. 0027, col 0640 D (Letter 43, s16): φθειρομένην rendered as Letter 24's φερομένην
- **Class:** 5 (a word changed, silently conformed to the parallel letter)
- **Greek, plate-read (p-0354, right column, band C/D):** `Ἐγκρατείας τόνοις καὶ ἀγρυπνίας κατ᾿ ὄρεξιν εἰς πολλὰ φθειρομένην ἀτάκτως τὴν ἐπιθυμίαν, σωφρόνως καταστείλωμεν.` φθειρομένην is clear on the plate. Letter 24 (0025 s16) prints φερομένην.
- **Latin:** *cupiditatem inordinate in multa desideria diffluentem … constringamus* (neither word exactly).
- **English as it stands (0027):** "With the tensions of self-control and vigil let us temperately restrain desire, carried in disorder by appetite toward many things."
- **Proposed English:** "With the tensions of self-control and vigil let us temperately restrain desire, corrupted in disorder by appetite toward many things." Alternatively, keep "carried" and log in the cruces that the plate prints φθειρομένην here and this is a conjectured misprint for φερομένην, on the evidence of Letter 24.
- **Why:** the English renders a word that Letter 43 does not print. φθειρομένην is less smooth with εἰς πολλά, but it construes. This is the same kind of silent harmonizing as F2, in the same letter.
- **Confidence:** probable (that some trace is owed). Certain on the plate reading.
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes, in the misprint table: `p0354.42 | φθειρομένην | φερομένην | plate`. Under the brief (misprints are kept, and the English follows the sense) **this is withdrawn as a finding**. It stays here only because it is the same move as F2, in the same letter.

### F4. 0027, col 0632 A/B (Letter 37, s2): "not believing that … at random" weakens "believing that … not at random"
- **Class:** 1 (a negative moved to the wrong clause)
- **Greek, file only:** `οὐκ εἰκῆ καὶ μάτην, καὶ ὡς ἔτυχε ταῦτα παρὰ σοῦ πεπέμφθαι πιστεύσαντες`
- **Latin:** *persuasum habentes, hæc a te non temere ac frustra nullaque ratione missa esse*. Combefis puts the negative inside the belief.
- **English as it stands:** "not believing that these had been sent by thee at random and in vain and as it chanced"
- **Proposed English:** "believing that these had not been sent by thee at random and in vain and as it chanced"
- **Why:** the Greek states a positive conviction (that the gifts were purposeful). "Not believing that …" only denies a belief. The negative governs the adverbs inside the infinitive clause, not the main participle.
- **Confidence:** probable (minor, but it is the polarity class).
- **Already in the cruces?** No.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **0025 L24 s7** (μὴ πρότερον … πολεμωθέντας, "unless we have first become wholly at war"): the negative is rendered correctly as a condition.
- **0025 L25 s2** (`ἀδήλων;`): a Greek question mark mid-sentence. It is clearly a misprinted stop (a ';' set where a comma or raised point belongs), and the English is rightly a statement.
- **0025 L25 s4** (παρακαλῶ δεήσεις … ποιεῖσθαι, "I beg that supplications be made"): the impersonal passive is acceptable. The addressee is implied.
- **0025 L27 s3** (διίστησι κόρος): satiety is the subject. The English follows the grammar.
- **0026 L36 s2** (Εὐλογημένος ἐστὶν … τῷ Κυρίῳ, "Blessed be"): LXX formula (cf. Ruth 2:20). "Blessed be … to the Lord" is the conventional rendering even with ἐστίν. Not reported.
- **0026 L32 s4** (μή πως οὐχ ἰσχύσωμεν μετανοῆσαι … ἢ πικρῶς κλαῦσαι): both infinitives depend on οὐχ ἰσχύσωμεν, as the English has it.
- **0026 L30 heading**: the English has a stray marker line `⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5⟧` after the title. This is structural only, and no Greek text is missing.
- **0027 L37 s5** (λάβῃς μισθὸν παρὰ τοῦ διδόντος Θεοῦ τὴν τελείαν … κάθαρσιν): it is ambiguous whether κάθαρσιν is the object of διδόντος or of λάβῃς with μισθόν as predicate. The English choice is defensible.
- **0027 L41 s6** (εἰ γινώσκετε δύνασθαι ἀνέξεσθαι αὐτῆς → "that he can bear with it"): the Greek has no subject for the infinitive, so "you" would be the default. But Combefis has *quod eam ille sustinere possit*, and the crux (0636) discusses the feminine. So "he" is a defensible reading. I would prefer "her/it" to keep αὐτῆς's ambiguity, but this is not a reversal. Not reported.
- **0027 L42 s7** (βλάβος λαμβάνουσαν, "receiving harm"): the sense is odd, but it sits in an eaten passage and the English follows the print.
- **0028 L44 s5** (κύριον θεοχάριστον as the name Theocharistus?): the plate prints it in lower case and the Latin has *hominem Deo gratum*. The crux already covers it. Rejected.
- **0028 L44 s2** (ἑκὼν ὡς ὑπεύθυνος, "as one liable"): ὡς = "as though". The English "as one liable" carries it adequately.
