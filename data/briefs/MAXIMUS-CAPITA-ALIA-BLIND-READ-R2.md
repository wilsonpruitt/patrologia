## Findings

### F1. Ch. 123, col. 1429A (band A): a question mark the plate does not print (class 3, Pattern 8)
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 728):** `πῶς οὖν συναναστῇ αὐτῷ ὁ τοιοῦτος, ἐν καινότητι ζωῆς πολιτεύσασθαι (u).` The plate closes with a full stop (the file agrees).
- **Latin:** `Qui ergo fiat, ut hic talis cum eo resurgat, ut in novitate vitae ambulet` (the OCR loses the end mark).
- **English as it stands:** `to conduct his life in newness of life? [n: Rom. vi, 4.]`
- **Proposed English:** `to conduct his life in newness of life. [n: Rom. vi, 4.]`, *or* keep the `?` and add a crux line saying the plate prints a full stop.
- **Why:** πῶς οὖν is a real rhetorical question and the English sense is right. But Pattern 8 says never supply a mark, and Migne prints a period here. This is a punctuation-fidelity point only. No meaning is reversed. Whether 8b's "English grammar refuses it" exemption covers a direct question ending in a period is a ruling for the editor. Flagged so it is decided, not because the sense is wrong.
- **Confidence:** certain that the plate has a period. Whether to change is the editor's call.

- **Same class, same verdict, two more sites:** **ch. 171** (col. 1441A, plate p. 734): `ἢ τίς ὁδηγήσει με, ἕως τοῦ μὴ βλέπειν εἰς ματαιότητας καὶ μανίας ψευδεῖς ἐν τῷ εὔχεσθαι.` The plate prints a period. The English is `… *upon vanities and lying frenzies* [n: Psal. xxxix, 5.] in praying?`. **Ch. 199** (col. 1448A, plate p. 737): `πῶς μολυνῶ αὐτούς.` The plate prints a period (the Greek file reads a comma). The English is `how shall I defile them?* [n: Cant. v, 3.]`. In all three the Greek is a real question and the English sense is right. Only the mark is supplied.

### F2. Ch. 193, col. 1445B (band B): Migne's supplied word [ὄφεως] is dropped from the English (class 4)
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 736):** `Ἔλαφος μὲν ἐπὶ σωματικῶν ὑδάτων τρέχει πηγὰς, ὑπὸ [ὄφεως] θηρὸς ὡς ὁ δηχθείς (f)`. Migne's note (f), plate-read: *Ὑπὸ θηρὸς ὡς ὁ δηχθείς. Quin scriptum sit Maximo, ὑπὸ θηρὸς ὄφεως, nullus dubito. Sic enim θῆρες et θηρία … dicuntur … quaecunque animalia noxia, … cujusmodi sunt serpentes.*
- **English as it stands:** `A deer runs to springs of bodily waters, as one bitten by a beast;`
- **Proposed English:** `A deer runs to springs of bodily waters, as one bitten by a [serpent] beast;`
- **Why:** this bracket is **not a variant**. The editor's own note says he has no doubt Maximus wrote ὄφεως, and he supplies it. That is the same kind of bracket as ch. 125 [μή] and ch. 204 [ἴσ. ἔμπλεως], and the English shows both of those in [ ]. The brief files it among the variants («ch. 177 [ὄφεως]: English follows the main reading»), but the plate's note puts it with the supplied words. ⚑ Also, the brief's chapter number is wrong: [ὄφεως] is in ch. **193** (ρϟγʹ), not 177. Ch. 177's plate mark is an asterisk keyed to «Vide Bochart. *Hierozoicon*» (the plate prints *Dea nimal. sacrae Scripturae*). The sense is not reversed, so the stakes are low. But the English hides a word the editor supplies and calls certain, and it does so inconsistently with how the work handles his other supplied words.
- **Confidence:** probable. That the bracket is a supplement is certain from note (f). Whether to show it is a convention call.

### F3. Ch. 197, col. 1445C–D: guillemets the plate does not print (Pattern 6; not class 1–5)

**Ch. 125 «[not] fittingly»: KEEP.** Plate p. 728, col. 1429A: `εἰ μὲν εὐχερῶς καὶ [μὴ] προσηκόντως (x) κατέρχεται`. The bracket and note key are printed as the brief says. The English «if it goes down lightly and [not] fittingly» gives the editor's supplied word in square brackets, exactly as the bracket requires. The Latin has *et non pro eo ac decet*, so it supports the negative. One more point I checked: the plate's comma after `ἐπανέρχεται` leaves `περὶ τὸν καιρὸν καὶ τὴν χρείαν` free to go either with the return ("comes back … about the time and the need", the Latin's *cum occasio ac necessitas tulerit*) or with ἀπερίσκεπτος (the English). The English construal is grammatical and does not reverse anything, so no change. s4 `Πλὴν οὐ πάντες ἐκείνου ἐκτὸς εὑρεθήσονται, ὅσοι … οὐκ ἴσχυσαν` (plate-read, same page): both negatives are rendered («not all … were not strong enough»). «clear of that» for ἐκείνου ἐκτὸς matches the Latin *ab hac immunes noxa*.

**Ch. 143 γνωστικῆς «the soul of knowledge»: KEEP the construal (optional wording tweak).** Plate p. 730, col. 1433C: `τέλος δὲ γνωστικῆς, ἡ θεωρία τῶν ἀρετῶν.` The plate has γνωστικῆς, feminine singular, clearly. (The Latin *gnosticorum*, plural, either read γνωστικῶν or is making it match *activorum*. Under the brief that is not a finding, because the English follows the Greek.) A feminine noun has to be understood. In this work an elliptical feminine πρακτική / θεωρητική regularly means the *soul*: ch. 159 `ἐν οἴκῳ τῆς πρακτικῆς ψυχῆς … τῆς θεωρητικῆς`, ch. 199 `ψυχὴ πρακτική`, ch. 201 `ἡ πρακτική` = «The practical soul». So supplying «soul» is defensible. The other candidate is ἀρετή (ch. 138 `θεωρητικῆς δὲ ἀρετῆς`, ch. 145 `τὴν πρακτικὴν ἀρετήν`). No reversal either way. The wording «the soul of knowledge» does read like "the soul that belongs to knowledge". If it is touched, I suggest «the end of the knowing soul» (or «of the soul of the man of knowledge»). Not a finding.

**Ch. 159: KEEP.** Plate p. 732, col. 1437C: `φωνὴ δὲ ἀγαλλιάσεως καὶ ἐξομολογήσεως, διὰ τὴν γνῶσιν διὰ τὸν τῆς θεωρητικῆς ἐξακούεται.` The file is exact. The second διὰ τὸν does not construe; the sense must be «in [the house] of the contemplative», parallel to `ἐν οἴκῳ τῆς πρακτικῆς ψυχῆς`, and the Latin has *in ejus qui studet contemplationi*. The English «is heard, because of knowledge, in that of the contemplative» gives that sense without adding or reversing anything. It is already noted, per the brief.

**Ch. 165 οὔτε … οὐ δύναται: KEEP (one negative).** Plate p. 733, col. 1440B–C: `οὔτε νεοτελὴς νοῦς (e) τὸ ἀπεστενωμένον ἐπὶ πολὺ καρτερεῖν τῆς εὐχῆς οὐ δύναται· ἡδέως μᾶλλον …`. The οὐ is printed. Read strictly, a simple οὐ after a compound negative would cancel. Here, though, οὔτε is the correlative conjunction answering `Οὔτε πῶλος … ἀνέχεται`, and the οὐ is a pleonastic repetition inside the second member. The next clause (`ἡδέως μᾶλλον … ἐξέρχεσθαι`, «it is rather glad … to go out») only works if the mind **cannot** endure. The Latin agrees: *neque mens … potest; quin … gaudet magis*. So «nor can a newly initiated mind endure» is right.

**Ch. 175 κακῶν [καλῶν]: KEEP the main reading. The English is literal to it, but I recommend disclosing the bracket.** Plate p. 734, col. 1441B: `Καταπλήττει δὲ τούτους, οὐχὶ πλῆθος τῶν κακῶν [καλῶν], ὡς τοὺς ἔξωθεν πολεμίους τὸ στράτευμα`. The bracket is printed with **no** «al.» and no note key. Compare ch. 154 on p. 732, `σκώλωπα [alii, σκόλοπα.]`, which is labelled as a variant. Does «not a multitude of evils» say what the Greek says? It says what κακῶν says. But it is a denial that does no work: nobody would expect demons to be terrified by evils, and the comparison («as an army strikes the enemies outside») needs the thing denied to be a *force on our side*, which is καλῶν «good things» (Latin *non bonorum turba*). With κακῶν the chapter is not reversed. It is pointless. Under the brief's ruling the English follows the main reading. I suggest, at least, a crux line, or showing the bracket as the English does for the other unlabelled brackets: `not a multitude of evils [goods]`. Probable, not certain, because the plate does not say whether this unlabelled bracket is a variant or a correction.

**Ch. 207 οἱ θεωροῦντες μηδέν: KEEP.** Plate p. 738, col. 1449B–C: `ὁρῶσι δὲ τὸν Θεὸν, οἱ θεωροῦντες μηδὲν ἐν τῷ εὔχεσθαι. Καὶ ὅσοι μὲν ὁρῶσι τὸν Θεὸν, καὶ εἰσακούονται παρ᾿ αὐτοῦ· ὅσοι δὲ οὐκ εἰσακούονται, οὐδὲ ὁρῶσιν αὐτόν.` «those who contemplate nothing in praying see God» is right: this is the Evagrian imageless prayer, and the Latin *qui inter orandum nihil vident* agrees. s3's οὐκ … οὐδέ is rendered («are not heard do not see him either»), and s4's οὐ σαλευθήσεται is rendered («will not be shaken»).

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **113** λιπαρῶν τὸν μέτοχον ἀπολυθῆναι, «begging the one who shares with it to be released». Who is released? The English is as ambiguous as the Greek (accusative + infinitive). The Latin *rogans qui sit particeps, ad ea dimitti* is the same. Nothing is flipped. Rejected.
- **122** the s1+s2+s3 and the stray `⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1+s2⟧` *after* ch. 123's text in the English: a structural-marker oddity, not sense. The mechanical checks pass it. I only mention it.
- **125 s3**, where περὶ τὸν καιρὸν καὶ τὴν χρείαν attaches (see the R2 case): both attachments are grammatical. Rejected.
- **129** ἄπαυστον ἕλκει τὸν τρώσαντα ἔρωτα, «draws the unceasing yearning that wounded it». The Latin construes *amorem adversus vulnerantem* («love toward the one who wounded it»). The Greek allows the attributive reading the English takes. The image (love that wounds) is traditional, cf. ch. 197 Τετρωμένη ἀγάπης. Not a reversal. Rejected, but a reviewer might prefer the Latin's construal.
- **130** «from the much-divided soul thoughts are driven away»: this is what the plate's text says (Latin *animo qui multis divisus est, abscedunt quidem cogitationes*). Rejected.
- **143** γνωστικῆς: see the R2 case. The plate is confirmed and the Latin *gnosticorum* is a Latin-side difference.
- **156** τὰ δι᾿ ἐπιθυμίας τῶν ἐπηγγελμένων καλά, where «through desire» is attached to «shuts off». It is a loose attachment but the sense is not reversed. Rejected.
- **170** οὗτος/ἐκεῖνος (which diver strips what): the English «this one … that one» keeps the Greek's order. The sense needs the sensible diver to strip clothing, and the English does not force the opposite. Rejected.
- **172** τῷ νῷ μετερχομένη εὐχή, «prayer … reckoned by the mind it passes to». The participle more naturally means «pursued/practised by the mind» (cf. ch. 153 ὁ τὴν πρακτικὴν μετερχόμενος «pursues»; Latin *menti eam colenti*). This is a wording weakness, not a reversal. Possible improvement: `will prayer without compunction be reckoned by the mind that pursues it`. Rejected as a finding, offered as a polish.
- **181** τὴν πονηθεῖσαν, «laboured for her long ago»: the participle agrees with κιθάραν, and the English attachment is loose but correct. Rejected.
- **182** the plate ends without a stop (the file has no stop) and the English supplies a final period. This is trivial and at a chapter end. Not reported.
- **210** straddles the chunks (0004 ends `αὔλα-`, 0005 opens `κας·`). The English splits it the same way and loses nothing.
