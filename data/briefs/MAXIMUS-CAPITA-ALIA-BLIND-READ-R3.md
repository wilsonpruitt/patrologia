### F1. Ch. 243, col. 1460 D — class 3 (genitive attached to the wrong word)

- **Greek (plate-read, p. 743, right column, band D):** Οἱ ταῖς ἡδοναῖς συνέρχεσθαι παρακαλοῦντες τοῦ φάρυγγος ἐντελεῖς ὄντας (p)
- **Migne's own note (p), same plate:** «Ἐντελεῖς ὄντας. Erat foedo errore in ἀπογράφῳ, ἀτελεῖς … De illis plane sermo est, qui cum sibi videantur jam virtutis arcem arripuisse, ac perfecti esse, genio sibi indulgendum putant.» ἐντελεῖς is his emendation and he glosses it as *perfect* without qualification.
- **Latin (col. 1461):** *Qui nos gulae deliciis indulgere hortantur, idcirco quod perfecti sumus*: τοῦ φάρυγγος taken with ἡδοναῖς («the pleasures of the gullet»).
- **English as it stands:** Those who exhort men who are perfect as regards the gullet to come together with pleasures
- **Proposed English:** Those who exhort men who are perfect to come together with the pleasures of the gullet
- **Why:** ἐντελής with a genitive of respect is strained; ἡδοναὶ τοῦ φάρυγγος is the ordinary phrase, held apart by hyperbaton. The editor who made the word glosses it as plain «perfect» (those who think they have reached the citadel of virtue), and the Latin agrees. The English makes the men's perfection a perfection *of the gullet* (i.e. already mastered gluttony), which is not what the chapter or its editor says. Not a polarity reversal; the exhortation stays an exhortation to indulge.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** Checked after: NO. cruces.md has note (p) itself with its working translation («'Being perfect.' … those who … seem to themselves … to be perfect»), which supports the proposal, but no T-line logs the English construal of τοῦ φάρυγγος.

### F2. Chs. 237, 225, 234 — punctuation (Pattern 8; outside classes 1–5, low severity, no sense change)

- **Ch. 237, col. 1460 A (plate-read, p. 743):** the plate prints a full stop: «ἀλλ᾿ οὐχ ἐκτός. Ὡς οἱ τῆς ἰδίας …». The English merges the two sentences (marker `⟦s3+4⟧`) with a colon.
  English as it stands: `but not without: as those who are not moved` → proposed: `but not without. As those who are not moved`
  Why: Pattern 8, «never delete a mark the plate prints»; the Ὡς-sentence is a verbless fragment on the plate and may stay one. Confidence: certain (as to the plate); trivial as to sense.
- **Ch. 225, col. 1456 A (plate-read, p. 741):** the chapter ends «ὡς οὐ τοιαῦτα τυγχάνουσιν» with **no** terminal stop on the plate. English supplies one: `as though they were not such.` Pattern 8 says never supply a terminal stop. Same at **ch. 234** (col. 1457 C, p. 742, seen at the overview scale only): «καταλύσαντες» unpunctuated, English `the love of honour.` Proposed: drop the final full stop in each, or log both as the project's chosen exception for chapter ends. Confidence: certain as to the plate; trivial.

## The R3 cases

- **Ch. 223 (col. 1453 C), «a go-between is found for them» — KEEP.** Plate-read p. 740: «μεσίτιδος εὑρισκομένης αὐτοῖς, ἐλλείψεως καὶ ὑπερβολῆς» — no negative anywhere in the clause; our file is exact. The Latin's *quippe cum … juxta mediocritatem non invenerint* reads μεσῖτις as «the mean» and has to supply *non* to make it fit. The Greek construes without a negative if ἐλλείψεως καὶ ὑπερβολῆς is apposed to μεσίτιδος (both genitive in the absolute): deficiency (for the negligent man) and excess (for the conceited man) is what stands *between* each of them and the goods of justice. That is the construal the English takes, and it makes the γάρ-sentence prove the first one. Adding «not» would import the Latin. Two optional refinements, not findings: (a) «go-between» in English suggests a helper who brings parties together; «something that stands between» or «an intermediary» is less misleading; (b) a negation divergence is a material divergence under Pattern 16, so a `[lat: the Latin has *non invenerint*, «did not find the mean»]` at this point would be in order if the project wants Pattern 16 applied here.
- **Ch. 225 (cols. 1453 D–1456 A), the double negatives — KEEP.** Plate-read p. 740: «Οὔτε πάντες οἱ μὴ ἀγαπῶντες τὸν πλησίον μισεῖν δύνανται· οὔτε οἱ μὴ μισοῦντες αὐτὸν ἀγαπᾷν· … ἕτερον δὲ τὸ μὴ ἐμποδὼν αὐτῷ εὑρίσκεσθαι πρὸς προκοπήν». Every negative is carried (Not … do not / nor … do not / not to be found an obstacle; and on p. 741 «τὸ μὴ μόνον … ἀλλὰ καί» and «ὡς οὐ τοιαῦτα» both carried). πάντες stands in the first member only, on the plate and in the Latin (*nec qui non oderunt*); the English mirrors that exactly. One might argue πάντες is understood in the second member too («nor are all who do not hate him able to love»); the plate does not print it, so the English is right not to add it.
- **Ch. 228 (col. 1456 B), the editor's deletion — KEEP.** Plate-read p. 741: «φαντασίαις [οὐ] σαλεύονται (l)», and note (l) on the same page: «Φαντασίαις οὐ σαλεύονται. Negatio omnino incommoda ac expungenda.» English «are shaken by imaginations» follows the note, as the brief rules; the Latin (*cogitationibus commoventur*) also has no negative; the sense (the ladder of ch. 227: imagination → desire → … → touch, with those nearest passionlessness troubled only at the first rung) requires it. **Side observation on our Greek file, not the English:** the file reads «[ο]σαλεύονται»; the plate prints «[οὐ] σαλεύονται» (with the accented υ and a space). The OCR lost the υ. If the Greek column is ever displayed, it should read «[οὐ] σαλεύονται».
- **Ch. 238 (col. 1460 B), «[not] having received» — KEEP.** Plate-read p. 743: «ὡς τῶν γεγηρακότων πύρωσιν ἐκ πυρὸς [μὴ] δεξαμένων» — the bracketed μή is printed; English «as with the aged who have [not] received a burning from fire» carries it, bracketed, as the brief rules. The Latin has the negative unbracketed (*qui incendium ex igne non sentiant*). The other negatives of the chapter: «ἀδύνατον μὴ … συνέπεσθαι» → «impossible that all the senses should not follow along» ✓; «ἀλλ᾿ οὐ … κριθήσεται» → «will not be judged temperate» ✓; «τὸν μὴ πορνεύοντα … μηδὲ ὁράσει κατακηλούμενον» → «who does not commit fornication, nor is bewitched by sight» ✓.

## Candidates considered and rejected

- **Ch. 213 εἴσω [ἔξω]**: English «within the things seen» follows the main reading as the brief rules; the Latin prints *intra res [extra res]*, the same pair. Not a finding.
- **Ch. 212 «this … that»**: τοῦτο (reining the mind) κατὰ φύσιν, ἐκεῖνο (damming the river) παρὰ φύσιν; the English keeps the Greek's near/far demonstratives, and the Latin (*hoc … illud*) agrees. Not flipped.
- **Ch. 216 duplicate `⟦s2⟧` marker** in the English (second sentence also labelled s2) and «·» rendered as a full stop: bookkeeping/punctuation, no sense change. Worth a mechanical fix of the label only.
- **Ch. 219 who is who**: τοῖς μέν = those to whom the world is crucified (nails = fasting, vigil); τοῖς δέ = those crucified to the world (non-possession, contempt). English matches. `⟦s2+3⟧` merges markers only; the full stop is kept.
- **Ch. 221 ἡ μέν / ἡ δέ**: self-control against the body's enmity, love toward the neighbour: matches.
- **Ch. 222 «the deprivation that belongs to endurance»** (διὰ τοῦ ἀποστερήματος τῆς … ὑπομονῆς): the Latin is equally literal (*patientiae fraudationem*), and Migne's note (k) on p. 740 discusses the phrase; the English does not reverse it (it avoids «deprivation of endurance» = losing endurance). Rejected.
- **Ch. 224 «greater than his choice»**: κρεῖττον τῆς προαιρέσεως ἡ σπουδή, literal; matches Latin.
- **Ch. 227 ἧς μὴ παραδεχομένης rendered passive** («if this is not admitted»): middle participle could have the motion as subject, but the Latin (*quo non admisso*) reads it the same way and the polarity is kept. Rejected.
- **Ch. 232 restructuring** of «οἱ δὲ συνδυάζονται μέν, … συγχωροῦσιν» into «those who pair with them allow»: Migne's note (n), p. 742, records that he himself supplied δέ and altered what follows; the English sense (they pair, let the thoughts in, forbid them to touch) matches the printed text and the Latin. Rejected.
- **Ch. 233 βασιλέως … φοβεροῦ**: the vine is untouched «as of a king» — matches Greek and Latin.
- **Ch. 240 «when it follows or leads»**: genitive absolute with αὐτοῦ = fear; literal and matches Latin (*illo comite aut praeeunte*).
- **Ch. 242 ὁ μέν / ὁ δέ**: soldier keeps his house barely; officer lacks honour. Verbless on the plate as in the English.
- **Ch. 243 ἀποφράττειν «fence in»** paired with «allow … to come in»: odd-looking, but the Latin has *obstruere* too; the English is literal. «ὡς σταφυλὰς διαβόσκεσθαι» «so as to graze on grapes»: Latin *ad uvas depascendas* agrees. Rejected.
- **Paired terms**: χρώμενον/παραχρώμενον (239), παραχρώμενοι (228), ἐνέργεια/ἐνέργημα (237), ἡδονή/λύπη (227), φαντασία/ἐπιθυμία/σχέσις/αἴσθησις/ἁφή (227–228): none swapped.
