## Findings

### F1. md-001, col. 296C: "the wills of the Father" (class 3; Trinitarian term turned)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0166, col. 296C):** Προσεπιτούτοις δὲ καὶ πάλιν αὐτὸν θελήμασι τοῦ Πατρὸς χωρίζετε [al. χωρήσετε], συνθέτῳ θελήματι σύνθετον καὶ μόνην χαρακτηρίσαντες φύσιν.
- **Latin:** "Praeterea vero rursus a Patre voluntatis jure ipsum separatis".
- **English as it stands:** "And besides this you again separate him from the wills of the Father,"
- **Proposed:** "And besides this you again separate him from the Father in respect of the wills,"
- **Why:** χωρίζω takes the thing separated from in the genitive (τοῦ Πατρός); the dative θελήμασι is respect/means. The English hangs τοῦ Πατρός on θελήμασι and so gives the Father plural "wills", which is the opposite of the dyothelite position (one will of the Trinity). Latin agrees: *a Patre voluntatis jure*.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** Partly. cruces.md l.24 (0296) notes the Greek plural θελήμασι τοῦ Πατρός and the Latin *a Patre voluntatis jure*, but keeps "from the wills of the Father". It does not take up the parse (genitive of separation with χωρίζετε), which is the point of this finding.

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **md-001 col. 293C, ὁρῶμεν "we see" vs English "have impulse".** Plate-read: the plate prints καὶ ὁρῶ|μεν. The parallel list in the next sentence has ὁρμᾷν, and Combefis has *aggredimur*. So it is Migne's misprint for ὁρμῶμεν, and the English follows the sense, as the brief directs. Not a finding. Checked after writing: it is logged (cruces l.21, and the OCR table p0165.30, plate pass 2).
2. **md-001 col. 296D, p30, the clause supplied in brackets.** "[natures, so also he was, without division, the union of what belonged to them by nature]". The plate reads ὑπόστασις ἦν ὁ αὐτὸς ἀσυγχύτως τῶν αὐτῶν φυσικῶν, so the eye-skip runs φύσεων → φυσικῶν. The supplement matches Combefis ("sic et unio indivise eorum quae ipsis natura competebant"). This is the one licensed bracket; it does not distort. Not a finding.
3. **md-002 col. 301D, p26 (Pyrrhus's concession).** Our Greek file is badly garbled here (σκὰ, λότου, ἓν φύσιν, Δήλοις, ἠργμέν): this archive copy clips the line-initial letters of the left column. Read on the plate, the English carries every member: "approving as God, obeying as man", "without beginning / begun", "it is not possible … ever to fall together … into one will", and the full list of six pairs. The speaker is Pyrrhus throughout, and "those in Byzantium" stays with him; the plate shows no label until ΜΑΞ. at 304A. Not a finding.
   - *Side observation, not class 1–5:* the plate sets Combefis's foot-note 39 after ἀπόδειξιν, "Quasi divinis humanisque rationibus evidenter probasti; sed obscura hic [phra]sis Maximi." Our Greek file has no `[n:]` at that point. It is a prose note, not a variant, so the omission may be by design. I report it in case it is not. Checked after writing: the note is recorded in cruces.md l.199, keyed to ἀπόδειξιν.
4. **md-001 col. 292B, φύσαντες** (s5 of p22). This is a non-word for φήσαντες; the English reads "having said". It follows the sense, so it is a misprint or OCR slip and not a finding. I did not plate-read it because the English is unaffected.
5. **md-001 p27, μετὰ δὲ πολλῆς εὐκρινείας ἔδειξεν.** The English joins it as "and with great clarity the argument has shown … : yet it is possible". δυνατὸν δέ is rendered "yet", and the sense is kept.
6. **md-002 p35, "he had by bare appropriation".** "he had" is supplied for an elliptical λέγειν construction. It is faithful to the sense (they must *say* the other natural things too are by bare appropriation).
7. **md-002 p9 s6, παραδεδώκασι (plural) → "has also handed down".** This follows the bracketed singular variant [παρέδωκε] with the fifth synod as subject. Not a finding.
8. **Sentence-marker duplicates** (e.g. two ⟦s11⟧ in md-001 p26, two ⟦s3⟧ in md-002 p5). These are structural only, and the text is complete. Outside the read's classes.
9. **md-001 p28 s8, "a nature too that is composite and his alone"** (σύνθετον καὶ μόνην … φύσιν). It agrees with Combefis's *naturam compositam eamque solam*. It stands.
