## Findings

### F1. md-003, col. 312 A (p21 s5): class 4 (added text), and the crux's plate claim
- **Greek** (plate-read, p. 174 right column, line 7, crop magnified 3×): ὥσπερ καὶ τῇ τοῦ ἰοῦ ἀποβολῇ, **ᾑ** τοῦ σιδήρου κατὰ φύσιν αὐγὴ καὶ λαμπρότης. The plate shows an eta with a rough breathing and a dot below. There is **no tau**, so this is not τῇ. The file's ᾑ is what the plate prints: ἡ with a stray subscript, or a damaged sort.
- **Latin:** sicut abstracta rubigine ac extrita, ferri splendor ac fulgor, qui natura inerat, patescit (nominative: "the iron's gleam … appears").
- **English as it stands:** `just as by the casting off of rust, the iron's casting off, its gleam and brightness according to nature.`
- **Proposed:** `just as by the casting off of rust, the iron's gleam and brightness according to nature.`
- **Why:** Read as ἡ, the article of αὐγή, the sentence construes simply, and the Latin agrees: "the iron's natural gleam and brightness [shine through]". The English instead resumes ἀποβολῇ ("the iron's casting off"), which makes the iron the thing cast off and adds a phrase the Greek does not need.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Cruces (checked after):** logged at 0312 as a deliberate reading: "Migne prints τῇ τοῦ σιδήρου (confirmed on the plate; not ἡ, not ᾑ)". **My plate read contradicts that claim:** the letter before τοῦ σιδήρου is a single eta with a subscript dot and no τ (compare τῇ τοῦ ἰοῦ on the same line, where the tau is plain). Re-read the plate before ruling. If the crux's τῇ cannot be confirmed, the English rests on a reading the plate does not carry.

### F2. md-004, col. 329 A (p17 s2): class 1 (sense reversed by idiom)
- **Greek** (file only): ἢ οἱ ἐν Κωνσταντινουπόλει τὰ ἀπὸ καρδίας λαλοῦντες;
- **Latin:** an ii, qui Constantinopoli [loquuntur] quod in mentem venit? ("whatever comes into their head")
- **English as it stands:** `or those in Constantinople who speak what is from their heart?`
- **Proposed:** `or those in Constantinople who speak things out of their own heart?`
- **Why:** τὰ ἀπὸ καρδίας λαλεῖν is the false prophets' phrase (Jer. 23:16 LXX, ἀπὸ καρδίας αὐτῶν λαλοῦσι), meaning "speak their own inventions". In modern English "speak from the heart" means "speak sincerely", so a reader takes it as praise of the Constantinopolitans. That reverses Maximus's point: they are the untrustworthy interpreters, set against the trustworthy author. "Out of their own heart" keeps the biblical echo, and the Latin confirms the pejorative sense.
- **Confidence:** probable (the Greek is unambiguous; the problem is how the English idiom reads).

### F3. md-004, col. 325 A (p11 s39): class 3 (mis-parsed genitive absolute)
- **Greek** (plate-read, p. 181 left column, lines 4–7): ἐπὶ δὲ ἀνθρώπων, ὡς χρονικῶς τῆς ἕξεως ἐπινοουμένης τῆς προχειρήσεως. The plate matches the file.
- **Latin:** in hominibus autem, ut in quibus habitus tempore aggressioni praeintelligatur.
- **English as it stands:** `of men, as the readiness of the habit being conceived in time.`
- **Proposed:** `of men, as the habit being conceived as prior in time to the readiness.`
- **Why:** The subject of the genitive absolute is τῆς ἕξεως, with ἐπινοουμένης agreeing. τῆς προχειρήσεως is a second genitive, the readiness that the habit precedes. This answers the angels' clause just before it: there the readiness *runs together with* the habit and admits *no interval of time*. In men the habit comes first in time and the readiness follows. The English makes "readiness" the subject and "of the habit" a possessive, so the contrast that is the point of the sentence is lost. The Latin (*praeintelligatur*) confirms the priority.
- **Confidence:** probable.

### F4. md-004, col. 325 D – 328 A (p11 s47–51 / p12): class 2 (turn boundary; paragraph marker on the wrong speaker)
- **Greek** (file only): ⟦p11⟧ is all Maximus's, through s51 (…καὶ καθ᾿ ὃ Θεός ἐστι, καὶ καθ᾿ ὃ ἄνθρωπος;). ⟦p12⟧ opens `ΠΥΡ. Οὐδὲν τούτων…`.
- **English as it stands:** `⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ And again he says: As the Father raises the dead` … then, as an unmarked paragraph, `⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ PYRRHUS. Nothing is clearer`.
- **Proposed:** remove `⟦p12⟧ ` from before `⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ And again he says:` so that Maximus's argument from John 5:21 stays in his ⟦p11⟧ turn, and set `⟦p12⟧ ` before `⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ PYRRHUS. Nothing is clearer`. The s-marker numbering inside both paragraphs is also collapsed (`s41+42+…+51`, `s1+s2+s3` used for both speakers) and should be re-split to match the Greek.
- **Why:** The words are right and the PYRRHUS label is in place, so a continuous reader is not misled. But the paragraph anchor that pairs the English with the Greek puts Maximus's conclusion ("These are the doctrines of the evangelists… What greater demonstration…?") against the Greek turn that begins ΠΥΡ. In a facing display that is a turn shown opposite the wrong speaker.
- **Confidence:** certain (as to the misalignment); the severity depends on the renderer.

Fallback if the editor wants nothing beyond the printed word: `of men, as the habit being conceived in time with respect to the readiness.`

## Candidates considered and rejected
- md-003 p33 s6 τὴν ἐν πᾶσιν … τοῦ Πατρὸς ὑπόληψιν, "revered in all things the repute of the Father". The attributive position favours "the Father's repute among all". But the Latin (*Patris opinionem in omnibus reveriti*) reads it as the English does, and the sense barely moves. Rejected.
- md-004 p11 s38 οὐχ ὁμονύμως. The Latin (*non uno modo dicuntur*) reads it as homonymous. The plate prints οὐχ (p. 180), and the English follows the Greek. Already in the cruces (0324). Rejected as a Greek/Latin disagreement.
- md-003 p14 s6 ἐπεὶ καὶ ἀπειράκις μετέβαλεν ἡ φύσις: the English supplies "otherwise … would have been converted". That is a fair rendering of the implied unreal condition. Rejected.
- md-003 p27 s4 Θεὸν καὶ μόνον φύσει ὄντα, "God alone by nature". Ambiguous in English but not reversed. Rejected.
- md-003 p31 s14 οὐκ ἔστι κοινὸν ἀμφοτέρων … τὸ θέλημα: checked against the dilemma's logic and the Latin (*non est communis voluntas ambarum*). Correct.
- md-003 p4 Οὐκοῦν, ἢ γνωμικὸν: ἢ is evidently for εἰ. The English "Then if of inclination" follows the sense. Not a finding.
- md-004 p9 s2 τοῦτο … καὶ ἡμᾶς ἐδίδαξαν, "taught us this too" (καί goes with ἡμᾶς: "us too"). Trivial. Rejected.
- md-004 p7 s4 (did not innovate the principle … but the mode): the ἀλλά pairing is kept across the ἐπεί clause. Correct.
- md-004 p12 s3 ἐμφανισθέντος αὐτοῦ ἐν τῷ βασιλικῷ σεκρέτῳ τοῦ … βασιλεύοντος, "in the imperial secretum of him who was then reigning". Acceptable (or "before him"). Rejected.
- md-003 p17: the bracketed ΜΑΞ. line (Combefis's supplement) is set as its own unmarked paragraph. Harmless, and logged at 0309.
- md-003 / md-004: collapsed or duplicated s-markers (`⟦s5⟧` twice in p2, `s11+12+13+14` repeated, etc.) are mechanical. They are raised only where they cross a speaker boundary (F4).
- Every negative in both chunks was checked: οὐ/οὐκ/οὐχ/μή/οὐδέ/μηδέ/οὐδείς/οὔτε/ἄνευ/χωρίς/εἰ μή/οὐ μόνον… ἀλλὰ καί. No polarity loss found. The privatives (ἀθελήτως, ἀβουλεύτως, ἀπρόσληπτον, ἀθεράπευτον, ἀκατάλληλον, ἀναμφιβόλου) are all rendered negatively.
