## Findings

### F1. I.2, col. 1177B — class 1 (scope of the negative)
- **Greek** (file only; no plate needed, the point is the English's construal): ⟦s4⟧ «Τὸ δὲ κυρίως οὐκ ὂν, κἂν εἶναι λέγηται, κατὰ μέθεξιν βουλήσει τοῦ κυρίως ὄντος, καὶ ἔστι καὶ λέγεται.»
- **Latin** (col. 1178, before the [1179] anchor): «Quod autem non vere ac proprie est, quanquam esse dicatur, per participationem, et est et dicitur esse» — the negative governs *proprie est*, not *est*.
- **English as it stands:** «And what properly is not, even if it is said to be,»
- **Proposed:** «And what is not properly being, even if it is said to be,»
- **Why:** in English "properly is not" puts the adverb outside the negative and says the thing is, strictly speaking, non-existent — the very claim the clause then denies ("both is and is said to be"). The Greek negates κυρίως ὄν as a unit: it is what does not *properly* be, the same thing ⟦s3⟧ just called «not properly being either». The English already renders ⟦s3⟧ that way; ⟦s4⟧ should match it.
- **Confidence:** probable (the reversal is one of scope, and an attentive reader can recover it from ⟦s3⟧).
- **Already in the cruces?** see the check at the end.

That is the only finding. Every negative and privative in I.1–II.15 was checked against the English. Apart from F1 each one stands, in the clause where the Greek has it.

## R1 cases

- **I.4–I.13 (col. 1181, supplied line openings): KEEP.** I read each chapter against the Latin twin (cols 1179–1183). The English construes throughout and says nothing the Latin contradicts. I.4 «alone essentially Begetter of the alone Word» = *sola solius … Verbi Parens*. I.7's double «willing» = *volens/voluntate*. I.8's «[?] he shows himself» (the Greek file prints a stray «ς» where the verb was lost) matches *seipsum manifestat*, so the supply is right and the [?] is honest. I.9 «undid no token of her virginity» = *nullum virginitatis solvit signaculum*. I.10's not-only / nor-only / but-also frame is intact. In I.11 the negatives «passing over nothing of nature save sin, since that was not of nature at all» = *nihil … uno duntaxat dempto peccato (quando nec ipsum ex censu naturae erat)*. One small point in I.9 ⟦s4⟧: «making her who bore him, a mother, a virgin» is ambiguous. The Greek is μητέρα (predicate) τὴν τεκοῦσαν παρθένον (object), "making the virgin who bore him a mother", and the Latin has *puerperam matrem virginem praestans*. It reverses nothing because the paradox holds either way, so I am not raising it as a finding. A clearer form would be «making the virgin who bore him a mother so far as to make…».
- **I.77 ἀνύπαρκτον: KEEP.** «that contrary to nature is without existence, because it is also inert». The privatives ἀόριστον / ἀνύπαρκτον / ἀδρανές are all rendered as privatives («unbounded», «without existence», «inert»), and the pairing of above nature (unbounded because efficacious) with contrary to nature (non-existent because inert) is not inverted. I did not open the plate; the brief records it as read.
- **I.89 (the editor's conjecture kept out of the running text): KEEP.** **Plate-read, p-0621** (right column, band C). ## Candidates considered and rejected
- **I.15 ⟦s4⟧** ζημιωθησώμεθα rendered «shall we be harmed» (future consequence) inside a chain of hortatives. The form is anomalous (future-passive stem with a subjunctive ending). The Latin also reads it as a consequence (*nihilque prorsus naturalis circumscriptio oberit*), and the negative is kept. Rejected.
- **I.26 ⟦s4⟧** ἀμφοῖν rendered «by both». It is better as "to both" (hope shows faith and love their objects; Latin *utrique rei … ostendat*). This is a nuance of case and does not change who does what or reverse anything. Rejected (cosmetic).
- **I.20** The Greek file has a period after ψυχῶν with lowercase ἰατρεύων following, and the English merges ⟦s2+3⟧. That period is almost certainly our OCR artifact and not a printed full stop, so this is not a Pattern 8 deletion. Rejected (not opened at the plate).
- **I.68 ⟦s3⟧** «even though he has not yet acquired the habit … and become a partaker of the wisdom». The scope of οὔπω over the second verb is as ambiguous in English as it is in the Greek. Not reversed. Rejected.
- **I.69** The file prints ἀγνός for ἁγνός (twice). The English «pure» is the sense. Misprint or OCR; not a finding.
- **I.72 ⟦s3⟧** «and him who has his choice pliant…» turns into two objects what the Greek may intend as one person (τὸν αἰσθανόμενον … καὶ … ἔχοντα). No reversal. Rejected.
- **I.85** «the soul's voluntary alienation toward the body» (πρὸς σῶμα): the preposition is literal and the sense (estrangement in respect of the body) survives. Rejected.
- **I.95 ⟦s2⟧** «according to one and the same ever-moving around God» lacks its noun (ἀεικινησίαν, "perpetual motion"). It is a grammatical gap, not a change of meaning. Not a hunt class, but worth a touch: «according to one and the same perpetual motion around God».
- **II.13 ⟦s3⟧** I checked whether «like an immovable mountain» attaches to the right noun (the law of nature, the mountain of II.9). It does. Rejected.
- **I.31 ⟦s4⟧** μὴ οὐχί after οὐδεὶς ἀντερεῖ is the redundant negative after a verb of denying. The English correctly makes it affirmative («no argument will gainsay that the evil one made these subsist»). Checked, correct.
