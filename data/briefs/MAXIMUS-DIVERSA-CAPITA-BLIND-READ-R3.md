## Findings

### F1. III.77, col. 1292 C (chunk 0010 ⟦p26⟧), class 2 (who/what: instrument and target swapped)
- **Greek** (file only; the words are clear in the file and the Latin agrees, so no plate read): καὶ διὰ τῶν δεξιῶν [b: C] τοῖς ἀριστεροῖς ἐπικεκρυμμένως ἐμπείροντες.
- **Latin:** *per ea quae secunda sunt, sinistris occulte inserunt* ("by means of the things on the right they secretly fix [us] into the things on the left").
- **English as it stands:** `and secretly pierce through the things on the right with the things on the left.`
- **Proposed:** `and secretly, by means of the things on the right, impale upon the things on the left.`
- **Why:** διά + gen. is the means (the right-hand things, i.e. the virtues pushed to excess); the dative τοῖς ἀριστεροῖς is what one is impaled ON (ἐμπείρω τινί). The English makes the right the thing pierced and the left the instrument, which reverses the demons' method; III.78 (περιπαρῆναι, lifted up by the excesses and transfixed) confirms the direction.
- **Confidence:** probable (high).

## R3 cases

- **III.55 «is absent»: KEEP.** The file reads ἐν ᾗ πᾶσα παντελῶς ἔπεστι πρὸς τὸ Θεῖον ἀπέμφασις. I read the plate (p-0655, right column, col. 1284 C): the verb is a blot. A smooth-breathed initial vowel shows, then ink fill, and I cannot tell ἔ- from ἄ-. I do not claim either reading. The Latin has *in qua nulla prorsus a Deo dissimilitudo occurrit*. The chapter (the image of the kingdom without beginning) requires that unlikeness to God be ABSENT; ἔπεστι would make the image contain every unlikeness to God. The English is right, and it needs its note saying the plate is illegible.
- **III.88 (no word lost): KEEP.** All four members of the Greek are in the English: soul-grief is the end of sense-pleasure; it arises from it; flesh-grief is the end of soul-pleasure; the soul's gladness becomes the flesh's punishment. The Latin carries the same four (*Animi enim laetitia, carnis poena existit*). The printed κόλασις is rendered ("punishment"), and the apparatus «(qu. illa 58, λύπη)» is rightly not followed. Only nits: Migne prints no space in «πηʹ.Τὴν», and the English doubles the marker «⟦s1⟧ 88. ⟦s1⟧». Plate not opened.
- **IV.22 (brackets removed): KEEP.** I read the plate (p-0670, left column, col. 1313 A–B): no square brackets print in IV.22, so nothing bracketed is lost. The English has every clause. Two side notes: the plate prints «;» after ἀλλήλαις and the English drops it (F2), and the English repeats the marker «⟦s1+2+3⟧» three times (mechanical).
- **IV.26 (brackets removed): KEEP.** I read the plate (p-0670, col. 1313 D): Migne prints a stray CLOSING bracket «ἀγνώ-|στως ]πρὸς τὴν τῶν ὄντων αἰτίαν». - **IV.29 αἴσθησιν [male edita, ἔφεσιν]: KEEP the English («the sense of what is known»).** I read the plate (p-0671, right column, col. 1316 B). It prints «τὴν αἴσθησιν [*male edita*, ἔφεσιν, *cupiditatem*], ἀφαιρετικὴν». So αἴσθησιν is Migne's text and ἔφεσιν is the earlier edition's bad reading. ## Candidates considered and rejected
- **III.80** ὡς μὴ μόνον ἐν ψιλῷ λόγῳ … ἀργὴν κεῖσθαι … ἀλλ᾿ ἐνεργόν: the English drops «only/merely» ("does not lie idle … in bare word, but active"). Here μὴ μόνον … ἀλλά (no καί) is the contrastive "not merely … but", and the claim is unchanged. Not a reversal. An editor may add «merely».
- **III.87** πρὸς τὸ μὴ πεφυκὸς κατὰ φύσιν καὶ ὑφιστάμενον: the English carries the negative onto both members ("not according to nature and not subsisting"). The Latin agrees (*minime comparatum est, neque vere est*). Kept.
- **III.79 end** ὡς τοῦ κατόπιν ὑπερφεροῦς παρ᾿ ἑαυτῷ γινομένου, οὐκ ἐπιγινώσκει: the English "does not recognize that with him what is superior is coming to stand behind" is awkward but keeps the sense (he has put the superior behind himself) and the negative.
- **III.71** τίς ἂν … καταλάβοι νοῦς: a rhetorical question that the plate's file closes with a full stop. The English also uses a full stop. Consistent, no finding.
- **III.36** ἀνέραστος … κακίας ἀπάγεται; μὴ συγχωρούμενος: polarity correct ("drawn away from vice", "not being permitted").
- **III.57** οὐ νοητόν / μὴ νοεῖν, οὐ ποιούμενον / μὴ γίνεσθαι: all correct. The Greek has no ἄρρητον member, and the English adds none.
- **III.70** list of eleven negated items: all eleven are present.
- **IV.30** κατ᾿ ἐνέργειαν μόνην: "only according to activity" fits μόνην with ἐνέργειαν. Kept.
- **IV.33** περιορίζων τῆς τοῦ νοῦ μανίας … τὴν ἔφεσιν: the English "limiting the aspiration of the mind's madness" is one possible construal. Not a reversal.
- **III.31/32** «[duo Reg. φύσιν]» at III.50: the English follows the printed βάσιν ("base"). Correct.
