### Finding 1. IV.71, col. 1336 C. Class 3 (construal). Probable; low weight.
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0681 right column):** «ἀλλ᾿ ἕκαστον τῶν λεγομένων πρὸς τὴν ὑποκειμένην δηλονότι τῷ τρόπῳ τῆς ἁγίας Γραφῆς δύναμιν νοητέον». The file matches the plate (τρόπῳ, not τόπῳ).
- **Latin:** «pro subjecta loco sacrae Scripturae vi» (reads τόπῳ; a witness only, and the English rightly follows τρόπῳ).
- **English as it stands:** «according to the power underlying, namely, the mode of holy Scripture»
- **Proposed:** «according to the power that clearly underlies the mode of holy Scripture»
- **Why:** τῷ τρόπῳ is the dative governed by ὑποκειμένην («underlying the mode»), and δηλονότι is the adverb «clearly». The English reads it as an apposition, so the power and the mode come out as the same thing. The sense is distorted but not reversed.
- **Cruces:** see the end of the report.

- IV.70: the elliptical «[is]» completes the predicate from the first clause, which is correct.

### Considered and rejected in 0016
- V.22 s3 «there is also the pleasure-loving meditation». The Greek is elliptical: ἐν ᾧ… ἤγουν ἡ τὸν κόσμον φιλοῦσα διάθεσις, καὶ ἡ… μελέτη has no main verb. The English supplies «there is also», which is exactly the Latin's construal («in hoc etiam… inest»). It is a defensible filling of an ellipse, not a reversal. One could argue that ἤγουν glosses ὁ κόσμος with *both* nouns («that is, the world-loving disposition and the pleasure-loving meditation»), but the sense survives either way.
- V.30 «having become the typical servitude's in the letter» (γενομένη τῆς τυπικῆς… δουλείας, a genitive of belonging). The sense is right, but the English possessive is hard to read. A smoother option, if wanted: «having come to belong to the typical servitude in the letter». It is a style matter, not a sense finding.
- V.9 πᾶσιν [male edita πᾶν]: the English follows the printed πᾶσιν («to wish for all»), which is correct.
- V.16 ἤπουγε [unus Reg. ὅπου γε]: «much more than» renders the printed ἤπουγε, which is correct.
- V.13 καθ᾿ ἣν («according to which there is the division»): the antecedent is ἀλογίας, and the English keeps the relative close enough to it. Not reported.
