### F1. V.53 (col. 1372 A–B): class 3 (scope of the negative over the participle)
- **Greek** (file only): ἐν καινότητι πνεύματος πρακτικῶς διὰ τῶν ὑψηλῶν θεωρημάτων τῷ Θεῷ λατρεύων, καὶ οὐ παλαιότητι γράμματος, [b: B] διὰ τῆς κατ᾿ αἴσθησιν πρὸς σῶμα ταπεινοτέρας τοῦ νόμου παραδοχῆς, τροφεὺς παθῶν κατὰ τοὺς Ἰουδαίους γινόμενος, καὶ ἁμαρτίας θεραπευτής.
- **Latin**: «ut in novitate spiritus … Deo serviat, non autem in vetustate litterae per humiliorem … legis acceptionem, affectuum pastor ac peccati cultor Judaeorum more efficiatur»: the becoming-a-nurturer falls under the negative.
- **English as it stands**: "and not in oldness of letter, [n: Rom. vii, 6.] through the lowlier reception of the Law by sense toward the body, becoming, like the Jews, a nurturer of passions and a servant of sin."
- **Proposed**: "and not in oldness of letter, [n: Rom. vii, 6.] through the lowlier reception of the Law by sense toward the body, as one who becomes, like the Jews, a nurturer of passions and a servant of sin."
- **Why**: γινόμενος sits under καὶ οὐ: it describes the worship that is excluded. In the English, "becoming…" hangs off the main subject after a comma, and the natural reading is that the man who has taken up the philosophy of the virtues *becomes* a nurturer of passions. That reverses the chapter's point.
- **Confidence**: probable. This is a matter of how the English reads, not a mistranslation of a word.
- **Already in the cruces?** No (the col. 1372 entries are Scripture and name notes only).

### F2. V.42 (col. 1365 B): class 3 (relative clause misread)
- **Greek** (file only): τὸ διὰ τοῦ σωματικοῦ μορίου τυπικῶς δηλούμενον, ὅπερ γνῶσις διὰ τῆς κατὰ πρᾶξιν ἀνδρίας, τῆς γνώμης ἀποτίθεσθαι πέφυκεν.
- **Latin**: «quam utique scientia, fortis actionis vi, animo [deponere solet]»: *quam* is the object and *scientia* the subject.
- **English as it stands**: "which knowledge, through courage in practice, is of a nature to put away from the inclination."
- **Proposed**: "and this, knowledge, through courage in practice, is of a nature to put away from the inclination."
- **Why**: ὅπερ is the object (the passible part), and γνῶσις is the subject. "which knowledge" reads as "the knowledge which…", so the verb is left with no object and the sense is lost. The priest-signifies-knowledge sentence that follows depends on knowledge being the agent.
- **Confidence**: probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** No.

### F3. V.95 (col. 1389 B): class 3 (a nominative read as an object)
- **Greek** (file only): ἕξις ὑπάρχουσα κατ᾿ εἶδος γεννητικὴ πάντων τῶν ἐν τοῖς οὖσι λόγων, καὶ τῆς ἐν πνεύματι κατὰ τῷ ἴσον ἑκάστῳ ζωτικῆς διαδόσεως ἀπονεμητική· καὶ τῆς οἰκείας ἐν τῷ καλῷ κατὰ τὴν θέσιν βάσεως ἀμετάθετος [male edita, μετάθετος] ἵδρυσις.
- **Latin**: «singulis vitalem distributionem impartiens, nec non sui in eo quod honestum est fundati situs firmitas ac immobilis sedes exsistens»: justice *is* the immovable seat.
- **English as it stands**: "and apportioning to each, according to equality, the life-giving distribution in spirit, and the unchangeable establishment of its own base in the good, according to position."
- **Proposed**: "and apportioning to each, according to equality, the life-giving distribution in spirit; and it is the unchangeable establishment of its own base in the good, according to position."
- **Why**: ἵδρυσις is nominative, parallel to ἕξις (a second predicate of justice, matching earth as the fixed element). Migne's own note (q) (p-0708, as transcribed in the cruces plate-note list; I did not open that page) glosses the phrase as a predicate of justice: «Immobilis firmitas et sedes fundati in honesto situs sui, quod maxime justitiae convenit». It is not a second object of ἀπονεμητική, which governs the genitive. As it stands, the English has justice handing out an "establishment" rather than being one. The privative ἀμετάθετος is rightly kept; see case V.95 below.
- **Confidence**: probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** The bracket and the privative are logged (col. 1389, «ἀμετάθετος [male edita, μετάθετος]»), but the construal is not.

## The R5 cases

- **V.51 «motion» (editor conjectures κένωσιν): KEEP «motion» in the running text, with a note.** Plate p-0698 (foot of col. 1369/1370), Migne's note (i), lemma «Τὴν κατὰ πρόθεσιν κίνησιν»: the printed text is κίνησιν, and the note says it stands so in Qu. 65 as well. The editor argues it is plain one should read κένωσιν, «seu magis χύσιν», since the metaphor is the pouring out of the sacrifice's blood. He rejects Picus's *commotionem sanguinis*. The facing Latin translates the conjecture: «vitamque omnem carnalem, instar sanguinis, animi proposito fundimus ac libamus». «Motion» is a real word that construes and reverses nothing, so Pattern 7 keeps it. The note should give κένωσιν/χύσιν, the Latin's *fundimus ac libamus*, and Migne's note (i). **Cruces (checked after):** already logged at col. 1369, with κένωσιν/χύσιν and the Latin. Keep as is.
- **V.65–67 οὐδεμία ἐμφέρεια: KEEP the negative («no likeness») as printed; a note is owed.** Plate p-0701, col. 1376 A: «οὐδεμία δὲ ἐμφέρεια τούτων πρὸς ἄλληλα (l)». The plate prints the negative plainly and keys Migne's note (l) to it. V.67 reads the same in the file; I did not plate-read V.67 separately. V.66 «Οὐκ ἔστιν ἀκριβὴς ἐμφέρεια» is also on that plate. Note (l) (same page, foot) says ἐμφέρεια means an *exact* likeness, such that one could stand for the other, not ὁμοιότης. It says the next chapter («Οὐκ ἔστιν ἀκριβὴς ἐμφέρεια») softens the claim for that reason, and it faults Picus («Nulla horum cognatio est»). The facing Latin builds the gloss into the text («exacta omnino et accurata similitudo» in 65, «nullam per omnia accuratam similitudinem» in 67). That is the editor's harmonization, not a different Greek: no `[lat:]` and no change to the English. The English «no likeness» sits beside «effects have such images of the causes as are possible» in the same chapter, so a reader meets an apparent contradiction. A T note on V.65 citing Migne's note (l) is the remedy, not a softened rendering. **Cruces (checked after):** col. 1376 logs the bare negative and the Latin's softening «as a doubt», but does not cite Migne's note (l). Note (l) is transcribed in the plate-note list (p. 0701). Recommend the T note cite (l): ἐμφέρεια = exact likeness, as distinct from ὁμοιότης, which is not denied. That turns the «doubt» into the editor's settled reading.
- **V.95 ἀμετάθετος [male edita, μετάθετος]: KEEP.** The English «unchangeable establishment» follows the printed privative, not the earlier edition's error. The Latin «immobilis» agrees. (See F3 for the construal of the clause around it.)
- **V.99 ἀπροσδεής [male edita προσδεής]: KEEP.** «being in need of nothing» follows the printed privative. It matches the chapter's logic (passionless → no deceit; needing nothing → free of the necessity of the arts; wise → above natural contemplation) and V.98's «in need … of one thing alone».

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **V.62** «applies himself entirely either to practice or to contemplation» (πάντως). «certainly» would be closer, and «entirely» could suggest exclusiveness, which the next sentence (virtue *and* knowledge) denies. The Latin «prorsus» carries the same word, though, and the English is a defensible rendering. Not a reversal.
- **V.80** «moved by which, they were taught, God gave being». κινηθέντα agrees with τὸν Θεόν. With the commas, the English does make God the one moved, and the next clause («if … lawful to speak of motion in God») confirms it. Rejected.
- **V.48 / V.50** «the word knows» (οἶδεν ὁ λόγος). Migne's note (h) on p-0698 says ὁ λόγος in V.48 is Dionysius's discourse, not Scripture. The lower-case «word» does not commit to either. It is interpretive, not a polarity issue, though a note citing (h) would help.
- **V.54** «has God angered» (ἔχει τὸν Θεὸν ὀργιζόμενον). The idiom is awkward («has God angry with him»), but the sense is not reversed.
- **V.79** κατὰ περίστασιν «by distress». «by circumstance» is closer, but the contrast with κατὰ πρόθεσιν survives.
- **V.98**: the Greek chapter ends without a stop and the English supplies one (Pattern 8, trivial). ἀσχέτου «without relation» keeps the privative.
- **V.97**: the Greek's one sentence (with a raised point) is split into two in the English. Nothing is lost.
- **Mechanical**: ⟦s⟧ labels are duplicated in the English at V.35, V.37, V.41, V.71, V.89 and V.97 (two ⟦s2⟧ or ⟦s4⟧). They are marker bookkeeping, not meaning, but worth a sweep. V.39's Scripture note reads «II Cor. iii, 7» for 2 Cor 3:6; that is Migne's note as printed and was not checked at the plate.
- **V.41** «[qu. 65, τυπικ.]»: the English follows the printed τοπικῆς («local position»), as the convention requires.
- **V.70** «δεχόμενον [male edita δεδόμενον]»: the English «receiving» follows the printed word.
