## my-003 (cols 680–688)

### F1. my-003, col. 685/688 (685 D → 688 A), class 2 (who/what: the participle's referent)
- **Greek** (plate-read, p-0377, Greek col. left, foot): σαρκὸς καταφρονείτω τῆς ὑποκειμένης φθορᾷ καὶ θανάτῳ, καὶ τὸ τῆς ψυχῆς ἀμελούμενον ῥυπῶσαι … δυναμένης ἀξίωμα.
- **Latin**: *carnem autem spernat, quae sit interitioni mortique obnoxia, atque neglectam animae dignitatem maculare valeat.*
- **English as it stands**: `and is able, when it is neglected, [0688] to soil the dignity of the soul.`
- **Proposed**: `and is able [0688] to soil the dignity of the soul when that dignity is neglected.`
- **Why**: ἀμελούμενον is neuter and agrees with ἀξίωμα: it is the soul's dignity that is neglected. In the English, "it" reads as the flesh (the subject of "is able"), which turns the sense around: the flesh does its damage when the soul is neglected, not when the flesh is. The Latin *neglectam … dignitatem* agrees.
- **Confidence**: probable (the English pronoun is ambiguous, but its natural antecedent is the flesh).

### Case p-0374 (col. 680 C/D): ἆρα τὰ θεῖα οὐ πάσχει: KEEP
- **Greek** (plate-read, p-0374, right col., band C→D): `ἆρα τὰ θεῖα οὐ πάσχει ²⁷ καὶ | D πᾶσα ψυχή, ἡ τὴν θείαν καταξιωθεῖσα κομίσασθαι εἰρήνην;` The οὐ and the question mark are both on the plate. Var. 27 (Fr.) drops οὐ.
- **Latin**: *num et cuivis animae divina usu veniunt, quae divinam eam pacem nancisci meruerit?*
- **English**: `does not every soul too that has been counted worthy to obtain the divine peace undergo the divine things?`
- **Judgment**: the English follows Migne's printed οὐ and keeps the question. A rhetorical "does not … ?" expects yes, so it gives the same affirmation as Fr.'s reading without οὐ. Polarity is correct. No change.

## my-004 (cols 689–697)

### F2. my-004, col. 692 A, class 5 (Christological term: one subject read as two)
- **Greek** (file only): στρατηγοῦντος αὐτοῖς τοῦ Θεοῦ καὶ Λόγου, καὶ τὰ πικρὰ τοῦ διαβόλου … μηχανήματα διασκεδάζοντος.
- **Latin**: *Deo illis ac Verbo duce, … dissipante diaboli machinas* (equally ambiguous).
- **English as it stands**: `God and the Word being their general, and scattering`
- **Proposed**: `the God and Word being their general, and scattering`
- **Why**: one article governs both nouns, and the participles are singular, so this is one person, the Word who is God (as at 0696 "the Word and God" and 0693 "says God and Word"). "God and the Word" can be read as two generals, Father and Son. Also, the singular "scattering" needs a single subject.
- **Confidence**: probable (the error lies in the wording, not the doctrine; the singular participles hold the sense for a careful reader).

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **col. 680 B**, ὡς τοῖς αἰτίοις τὰ αἰτιατὰ … συνημμένας, Θεῷ προσκομίσασα, rendered "as having brought to God, as effects joined to their causes, the activities…". The English turns the first pair into a simile. But the effects joined to causes and the activities joined to powers are the same objects named twice, so nothing is lost. Latin *velut quae causis, effecta* does the same.
2. **col. 680 C**, [γνώμην] rendered "[knowledge]". Migne's misprint (on the plate); the English follows the sense. Logged (cruces 0680).
3. **col. 685 C**, ὁ κόσμος, ὡς ἄνθρωπος, τῶν φαινομένων τεθνήξεται, rendered "the world of the things that appear … will die". The genitive could also go with the verb ("will die in respect of its phenomena"; Fr. τῷ φαινομένῳ). The English matches the Latin *mundus hic rerum aspectabilium*, and the sense (the visible world dies) is not reversed.
4. **col. 688 C**, παθοῦσαν rendered "sold". A deliberate variant; the cruces note (0688) states the evidence correctly (var. 37, c. A et Fr., Latin *venditum*). No inline note marks it in the English, but the brief allows that.
5. **col. 692 B**, λόγων τε καὶ πραγμάτων, rendered as "the intelligibles, both principles and things". Could go with μύσαντας ("their senses shut to words and things"). Latin *intelligibilium rationum pariter rerumque conspectum* agrees with the English. Keep.
6. **col. 689 D**, ὁρίζει τοὺς ἀγῶνας, rendered "sets an end to the contests". Latin *certamina finit*; the context (dissolving, giving peace) supports it.
7. **col. 697 A**, θέσει … θεούς, rendered "by adoption". Not the θέσις/ἀφαίρεσις of the divine names; logged (cruces 0697, glossary § V).
8. **col. 697 A**, ἐν μηδενὶ αὐτοῦ λειπομένους, rendered "lacking it in nothing". "Falling short of it in nothing" would be closer, but the polarity and sense are intact.
9. **col. 692 D–693 B**, the genitive-absolute chain παραγινομένου … ποιοῦντος … ἀφορίζοντος … ἀποδιδόντος: all four are correctly assigned to Christ.
