## Findings

### F1. my-006, col. 716 B (p-0396), class 3 (mis-parsed grammar: genitive of comparison read as partitive; the sense is denied)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0396):** Οὐδὲν γὰρ τούτων τῶν ὀργάνων πρὸς ἁμαρτίαν ἐστὶν εὐολισθότερον, μὴ λόγῳ παιδαγωγουμένων· καὶ οὐδὲν πάλιν πρὸς σωτηρίαν αὐτῶν ἑτοιμότερον, τάσσοντος αὐτὰ τοῦ λόγου …
- **Latin:** *Nihil enim hisce organis ad peccatum lubricum magis … nihilque rursus ad salutem iis expeditius* (ablative of comparison: Combefis read it the same way).
- **English as it stands:** "For none of these organs is more slippery toward sin, when they are not schooled by reason; and again none is more ready for salvation, when reason orders them,"
- **Proposed:** "For nothing is more slippery toward sin than these organs, when they are not schooled by reason; and again nothing is more ready for salvation than they, when reason orders them,"
- **Why:** τούτων τῶν ὀργάνων and αὐτῶν are genitives of comparison (the second, αὐτῶν beside ἑτοιμότερον, admits no partitive reading). The English reads as a *denial* that the eye, ear and tongue are slippery, the reverse of the point that they are the most slippery of all and so need reason's reins (which is why the preceding sentence restricts them).
- **Confidence:** certain.

### F2. my-006, col. 713 D (p-0395), class 1 (polarity strengthened: οὐ πάντως "not altogether" rendered "by no means")
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0395):** ἀμυδρῶς εἰς τὸ φαινόμενον βλέποντες· περὶ ὃ οὐ πάντως ἐστὶν ἡ ἀλήθεια, οὐδὲ τῶν γινομένων ὁ λόγος.
- **Latin:** *in quo non omnino sita veritas, aut eorum quae fiunt ratio posita est.*
- **English as it stands:** "about which the truth by no means lies, nor the principle of the things that come to pass."
- **Proposed:** "about which the truth does not altogether lie, nor the principle of the things that come to pass."
- **Why:** οὐ πάντως (negative before the adverb) = "not wholly / not necessarily", not πάντως οὐ "in no way". Maximus says the appearance does not wholly contain the truth, so men's judgment of it is unreliable; "by no means" makes the appearance wholly void of truth, a stronger claim than the text. Later Greek does sometimes use οὐ πάντως as a flat negative, hence probable, not certain.
- **Confidence:** probable.

### F3. my-006, col. 717 B (p-0397), class 3 (the comparative "more" of the widow's mites lost)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0397; the bracket is Migne's):** ἀλλ᾿ οὖν τοῖς ἐκ χρυσοῦ νομίσμασι … κατὰ τὸ ἴσον τὸν βασιλικὸν χαρακτῆρα φέροντα, κατὰ [καὶ τὸ] πλέον ἴσως ἔχοντα τῆς προσαγούσης τὴν ἐξ ὅλης διαθέσεως πρόθεσιν.
- **Latin:** *cum obsignatam regiam figuram habent, tum forte etiam offerentis ex toto affectu proposito praestant* ("and perhaps even surpass them by the offerer's intent").
- **English as it stands:** "and in respect of the more [also], perhaps, they have the intent of the woman who offers from her whole disposition."
- **Proposed:** "and perhaps they have over and above [also] the intent of the woman who offers from her whole disposition."
- **Why:** κατὰ πλέον / καὶ τὸ πλέον answers κατὰ τὸ ἴσον: the mites bear the royal stamp *equally* with the gold coins, and *beyond* them perhaps carry the widow's whole intent. "in respect of the more" does not let the reader see that the mites surpass the gold; the climax of the comparison is lost (not reversed).
- **Confidence:** probable (the sense is certain; the wording is a judgment).

### F4. my-006, col. 709/712 (English ⟦s3+4⟧), class 4/5 (Scripture locator at the wrong sentence)
- **Greek (file only):** the Luke 15:31 quotation is ⟦s6⟧ Τέκνον, σὺ πάντοτε μετ᾿ ἐμοῦ εἶ …
- **English as it stands:** "the three orders of those who are saved. [n: Luc. xv, 31.] For slaves,"
- **Proposed:** remove it there ("the three orders of those who are saved. For slaves,") and set it after the quotation: "*Child, thou art always with me, and all that is mine is thine*; [n: Luc. xv, 31.] being,"
- **Why:** the locator is attached to the sentence on the three orders, which quotes nothing; the verse it names is quoted three sentences later and carries no locator.
- **Confidence:** certain that it is misplaced (a mechanical-apparatus point, low stakes).

  Plate (p-0393, both columns): no Scripture locator is keyed anywhere near ἐκάλει / *vocabat*; the only mark there is variant 59 (Fr.). So Migne did not put the Luke locator at the three-orders sentence; the misplacement is ours.

## Brief's case to judge: p-0396 (my-006, col. 716 A), συμπαθῶς μᾶλλον, ἀλλ᾿ οὐ μὴ ἐμπαθῶς: KEEP
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0396):** εἰ δὲ μήγε, συμπαθῶς μᾶλλον, ἀλλ᾿ οὐ μὴ ἐμπαθῶς ⁶⁶ τούτοις ἐνεργεῖν … ἐπιτρέποντες. The plate prints ἐμπαθῶς with the variant mark 66 (codex A ἀσυμπαθῶς).
- **English:** "but if not, permitting them to act on these sympathetically rather, and not passionately," Polarity correct against the printed text: positive (sympathetically) / negated (passionately). The Latin (*miserantis potius affectu quam libidine*) agrees. Codex A's ἀσυμπαθῶς would give "and not unsympathetically", a flatter tautology; following Migne is right. No change.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **my-005, col. 700 C, πρὸς μὲν ἑαυτῆς … πρὸς δὲ τὸν Λόγον, ἑαυτὴν καταντᾷ → "on its own side, it brings itself to the Word".** The Latin takes ἑαυτήν with συναγαγοῦσαν ("gathering its powers to itself and itself to the Word … arrives"). The English's transitive καταντᾷ is an unusual construal, but the sense (the soul's powers gathered, the soul brought to the Word) is the same either way; nothing reversed. Logged in cruces (0700).
