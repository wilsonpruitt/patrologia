### F1. 0002, col. 25 C–D — class 1 (polarity / scope of "which is impossible")
- **Greek** (plate-read, pdf 19, left column, two crops): `εἰσί· | .[damaged sort] τε κατὰ φύσιν ἀγαθῷ θελήματι, τὸ κατὰ μίμησιν πρὸς αὐτὸ κινηθὲν ἀνθρώπινον, ποιότητί τε φυσικῇ καὶ ποσότητι εἴη ταυτόν, ὅπερ ἀμήχανον (πῶς γὰρ ἂν τῷ φύσει μεθεκτικῷ, τὸ φύσει μεθεκτὸν εἴη ταυτόν;)`. Our file has `εἰσί.εὶ τε`. At the head of the line the plate has a single broken sort under an overstroke, which I cannot read at 2× (not certainly μήτε, not certainly τῷ). Either way the negative in the English is sound: if the word is τῷ, as the cruces read it, the clause stands under the governing ἕως ἂν μή. The finding does not turn on which word it is.
- **Latin:** *nec cum voluntate per naturam bona … humana voluntas, qualitate et quantitate naturali idem exstiterit; quod sane fieri nequit (quomodo enim quod natura participabile est, idem erit …)*.
- **English as it stands:** `is not the same with it in natural quality and quantity — which is impossible (for how could`
- **Proposed:** `is not the same with it in natural quality and quantity — [a sameness] which is impossible (for how could`
- **Why:** ὅπερ ἀμήχανον refers to the sameness (the parenthesis proves it: "how could the participable be the same as the participant?"). In the English, "which is impossible" follows "is not the same", so it reads as "not being the same is impossible", the reverse of the argument.
- **Confidence:** probable (the English inherits the Greek's word order; the defect is in how the English reads).
- **Already in the cruces?** Partly. Cruces 0025 (line 35) logs the damaged connective, reading εἰσί· τῷ τε under the "as long as … not". It does not log the reference of ὅπερ ἀμήχανον. The scope problem is not logged.

### F2. 0002, col. 21 D (heading) — class 1 (scope of the negative)
- **Greek** (file only): `Ὅτι κατὰ πάντα τρόπον οὐκ ἔσται μετὰ τὴν ἀνάστασιν ἓν τὸ θέλημα τῶν ἁγίων πρὸς ἀλλήλους καὶ τὸν Θεὸν, κἂν ἓν πᾶσι τὸ θεληθέν, ὥς τινες λέγουσιν.`
- **Latin:** *Non fore modis omnibus post resurrectionem unam sanctorum inter se, et cum Deo, voluntatem*.
- **English as it stands:** `*That in every way the will of the saints toward one another and toward God will not be one after the resurrection, even if what is willed is one for all, as some say.*`
- **Proposed:** `*That the will of the saints toward one another and toward God will not be in every way one after the resurrection, even if what is willed is one for all, as some say.*`
- **Why:** "In every way … will not be one" reads as "in no way one". But the chapter shows that the will IS one by the principle of nature and differs in mode (col. 25 A: "by the principle of nature the volition of all will be shown to be one, but by the mode of movement, different"). The body renders the same phrase correctly twice: "never will there come to be in every way one volition"; "it is impossible that there be in every way one inclination". The heading should match.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** No (the 0021 entries cover ὑπέρνοια, the mind-ladder, σχέσις and ὑπόστασις, not the heading).

### F3. 0003, col. 32 C — class 3 (main verb moved into the condition)
- **Greek** (file only): `καὶ διὰ τοῦτο φύσεις δύο, ὧν αὐτός ἐστιν ὑπόστασις, εἴπερ οὐκ ἄσαρκος, αὐτὸν καταγγείλωμεν· καὶ δύο θελήματα φυσικὰ, κατὰ τοὺς Πατέρας· εἴπερ οὐκ ἄψιλος [= ἄψυχος] καὶ ἄνους, ἔχειν αὐτὸν παῤῥησιασώμεθα·`
- **Latin:** *duas item voluntates naturales, uti Patres sentiunt (quippe cum non anima ac mente careret) ipsum habere, clare ac libere astruamus*; and before it *(quippe non carnis expertem) ipsum profiteamur*.
- **English as it stands:** `and for this reason let us proclaim two natures, of which he is hypostasis, if indeed we do not proclaim him fleshless; and two natural wills, according to the Fathers, if indeed we boldly declare that he is not soulless and mindless;`
- **Proposed:** `and for this reason let us proclaim two natures, of which he is hypostasis, if indeed he is not fleshless; and let us boldly declare that he has two natural wills, according to the Fathers, if indeed he is not soulless and mindless;`
- **Why:** παῤῥησιασώμεθα is the hortatory main verb, governing ἔχειν αὐτὸν δύο θελήματα. The εἴπερ clauses are verbless ("if indeed [he is] not fleshless / soulless and mindless"). The English moves "boldly declare" into the condition, so the second exhortation loses its verb, and both conditions turn into conditions about what *we* say rather than what Christ *is*. The Latin parses it the same way the proposal does.
- **Confidence:** probable to certain (the second clause certain; the first is the same construction).
- **Already in the cruces?** Only the reading. Cruces 0032 (line 47) logs ἄψυχος for ἄψιλος; the parse of παῤῥησιασώμεθα is not logged.

### F4. 0005, col. 52 A — class 2 (active turned passive: who is beaten)
- **Greek** (file only): `Ὁμοῦ γὰρ τῆς τε θεολογίας καὶ τῆς οἰκονομίας, ὁ Σευήρου βασανιζόμενος καταγωνίζεται λόγος.`
- **Latin:** *Simul enim contra divinitatem, Christique mysterium Severi disputatio ad libram revocata concertat.*
- **English as it stands:** `For the account of Severus, put to the test, is defeated at once in theology and in economy.`
- **Proposed:** `For the account of Severus, put to the test, fights at once against theology and against economy.`
- **Why:** καταγωνίζεται with the two genitives is the deponent "contend against" (κατά- + gen.). The English takes it as passive "is defeated", with the genitives as "in respect of", a construction the passive does not take. The two readings say different things. The Greek says the argument, once examined, is found warring against the Church's doctrine of God and of the incarnation, and the next paragraphs show how: with theology (a Trinity of natures, or of factions, or one person) and with economy (Christ without essence, will or hypostasis). The English makes it say only that Severus loses. Combefis reads it actively (*contra … concertat*).
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** No (0052 entries cover the Zechariah locator and ἀντιστροφή only).

## Candidates considered and rejected

1. **0001 col. 13 C**, `Οὐ πᾶσιν οἷς ἁρμόζει τὸ προαιρεῖσθαι, πάντως ἁρμόζειν καὶ τὸ βούλεσθαι` → "Not to all to whom choosing is fitting is wishing also altogether fitting." Logically the example (we wish health but do not choose it) wants the terms the other way round: not all that is wished is chosen. But the English follows the Greek as printed. This is not an English departure, and if anything it is the source's (or Nemesius's transmission's). Rejected.
2. **0001 col. 13 D**, τὸν τῆς ὑγιείας τόπον rendered "the mode of health": a conjecture (τρόπον) logged in the cruces (0013, line 16; plate prints τόπον). Correct sense. Rejected.
3. **0001 col. 16 B / 0002 col. 21 B**, `Οὐκ ἔστιν οὖν βουλὴ … ἡ προαίρεσις` and `προαίρεσις οὐκ ἔστι τὸ φρόνημα`: the English swaps subject and predicate ("Counsel … is not choice"; "choice is not prudent thought"). Non-identity is symmetric, so the sense is unchanged. Rejected (cf. s11/s13 where the article is followed).
4. **0001 col. 16 B**, Nemesius's opening « (s35) has no quotation mark in the English. This is mechanical (Pattern 6), not this read's class. Noted only.
5. **0001 col. 9 A**, the participles συμπαρεκτείνων / ποιῶν σοι: the σου/σοι show the subject is the longing, not Marinus, and the English attaches them to "the longing". Correct.
6. **0002 col. 24 C**, `εἰ δὲ τότε κατὰ τὸν νῦν κρατοῦντα νόμον τῆς φύσεως, προαίρεσις οὐκ ἔστι` → "And if then, according to the law of nature now prevailing, there is no choice". This can be misread as "under the present law there is no choice", but the Greek and the Latin (*pro legis naturae ratione, quae nunc viget, nulla tunc futura electio est*) have the same order and the same ambiguity. Rejected.
7. **0002 col. 24 A–B**, the ten-step "no one … without" chain (s11) and the eight-adjective list (s12): counted, and they match in full.
8. **0003 col. 32 A**, ἣν φυσικῶς τυπῶν τε καὶ κινῶν → "which rest he, … moving it": the antecedent could be θέλησιν/κίνησιν rather than στάσιν. The Greek is ambiguous and the sense holds. Rejected.
9. **0004 col. 41 C**, ποιότητα rendered "quantity": a conjecture, correct, logged (cruces 0041 line 69; Combefis's own note m). Rejected.
10. **0005 col. 48 B**, `τὴν πρὸς τὸν θάνατον συστολὴν θέλων ἐπισημαίνεται` → "the Saviour is signified as willing …". The middle could be active ("he signifies, willing, the contraction"), parallel to ἐπιδείκνυται in the next sentence. But the Latin also takes it as passive (*Salvator quatenus homo carnaliter velle significatur*) and the sense is the same. Rejected.
11. **0005 col. 52 D**, `ἀνυπόστατος ἔσται … ὁ Χριστός· τῇ ἐνεργείᾳ` → "without hypostasis together with the operation". The dative is loosely taken ("by [the destruction of] the operation"), but the argument is intact. Rejected.
12. **0005 col. 48 C**, `Οὐ γὰρ ἵνα πάθῃ, σώσῃ δέ` → "not in order to suffer, but in order to save": the polarity is right.
13. Every other negative in 0001–0005 was checked against the English and found carried (οὐ, μή, οὐδέ, οὔτε/μήτε chains, οὐδείς, χωρίς/δίχα, πλήν, εἰ μή, the privatives ἀπαθής, ἄτρεπτος, ἀνυπόστατος, ἄβουλος, ἀνούσιος, ἀκατάληπτος).
