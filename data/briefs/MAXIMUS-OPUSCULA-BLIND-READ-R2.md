## Findings

### F1. 0006, col. 0068 D (Opusc. 6, end of the argument)
- **Class:** 3 (mis-parsed grammar: apposition where the Greek has an object)
- **Greek (file only):** ἅτε θέλησιν καὶ ὡς ἄνθρωπος ἔχων αὐτὸς ὁ φύσει Θεὸς, τὴν τοῦ πατρικοῦ θελήματος πλήρωσιν.
- **English as it stands:** "inasmuch as he himself, God by nature, had as man also volition: the fulfilment of the paternal will."
- **Proposed:** "inasmuch as he himself, God by nature, had as man also volition for the fulfilment of the paternal will."
- **Why:** τὴν πλήρωσιν is the object of the volition (he willed, as man, the fulfilling of the Father's will). The colon makes his human volition *be* the fulfilment, an identification Maximus does not make. Small, but it is the thesis sentence of the piece.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** No (checked after: no cruces line on this sentence).

### F2. 0007, col. 0076 A (Opusc. 7, Tome to Marinus)
- **Class:** 5 (fixed term varied)
- **Greek (file only):** ἄνω τε καὶ κάτω διὰ τὴν φυσικὴν πρὸς ἄμφω συγγένειαν κατὰ διαστολὴν ἐξ ἡμισείας μερίζεσθαί τε καὶ τέμνεσθαι
- **Latin:** not material.
- **English as it stands:** "upward and downward, by expansion, because of its natural kindredness with both"
- **Proposed:** "upward and downward, by demarcation, because of its natural kindredness with both"
- **Why:** the brief's fixed table gives διαστολή = demarcation (and 0023 renders the *Opusc.* on the διαστολαί so). "Expansion" is a different idea (spreading out) and breaks the one-word rule for a term Maximus defines later in the same collection.
- **Confidence:** probable (it is a glossary question for the coordinator, not a sense reversal).
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes, in effect: while grepping the English for "expansion" I saw (inadvertently, after forming the finding) a cruces line that defends "expansion" as "the glossary's" rendering here. That contradicts the brief's table; the coordinator should rule which glossary is current.

### F3. 0007, col. 0076 C (Opusc. 7)
- **Class:** 2 (who/what: subject of the clause)
- **Greek (file only):** Πῶς γὰρ καὶ τίνι λόγῳ, μηδεμίαν ἔλλειψιν τῶν τοιούτων ἐχούσης τῆς φύσεως; ἀλλ᾿ ἕτερόν τι ξένον παντάπασι τῆς ἡμετέρας οὐσίας ὑπάρχει καὶ ἄγνωστον· καὶ, ἢ ἀπ᾿ ἀρχῆς συνουσιωμένον αὐτῷ … ἐκεῖνο σαφῶς ὅπερ λέγεται γεγονέναι
- **English as it stands:** "Rather, he is something other, wholly foreign to our essence, and unknown;"
- **Proposed:** "Rather, it is something other, wholly foreign to our essence, and unknown;"
- **Why:** the neuter ἕτερόν τι … συνουσιωμένον … ἐκεῖνο ὅπερ λέγεται γεγονέναι is "that which he is said to have become" (the flesh/manhood), not the Word. "He is … foreign to our essence" makes the Word himself the subject, which blurs the reductio: the point is that *what he assumed* would then be something alien, not human.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** No (checked after: no cruces line on this sentence).

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **0007 col. 0080 C, τελείαν ὑπαρξίαν.** As printed it says "diminution, or rather the perfect *existence*", the reverse of the sense; the English reads "perfect non-existence". Plate-read (p. 50): Migne prints **ὑπαρξίαν**, so the file is faithful, and Combefis's Latin has *perfectam abolitionem*. The English follows the conjecture ἀνυπαρξίαν, which restores the sense rather than reversing it. Not a finding. Checked after: logged in the cruces misprint table (p0050.33, ὑπαρξίαν → ἀνυπαρξίαν). Note that there is no inline note at the point, if the brief's "Migne prints X" note convention is meant to show up in the English.
2. **0006 col. 0057 C, οὐ τὸ ἡγούμενον μαθεῖν** rendered "not to learn what thou art ignorant of". It reads ἀγνοούμενον; Latin *non cujus ignorantia laborares addiscere* agrees. Migne's misprint, sense right. Logged in the cruces (p0035.38).
3. **0006 col. 0060 D, διὰ τὴν ἀπορίαν** rendered "seedlessness" (it reads ἀσπορίαν, which the argument demands). Not a finding.
4. **0009 col. 0093 A–B, τῷ μὴ κατὰ λόγον τινὰ καὶ σοφίαν αὐτὰ ποιῆσαι, καὶ ἑνῶσαι πρὸς ἑαυτὸν**: the μή governs both infinitives. The English "by not having made them … and united them to himself" is loose, since "united" could be read as positive, but the "not" reaches it grammatically. Rejected as below threshold.
5. **0008 col. 0088 C, αὗται … κἀκεῖναι** (dual vs singular expressions): the English "these … those" keeps the Greek's reference (αὗται = the nearer, the dual expressions: difference against confusion). Correct.
6. **0007 col. 0080 B, κατὰ ταύτην … κατ᾿ ἐκείνην**: "by the latter … by the former" maps rightly (deviation → vice; constitution → handiwork).
7. **0006 col. 0057, ἐκεῖνο ποιεῖται … τοῦτο παρέχει** and **ἐκείνῳ μὲν … ἐν τούτῳ δέ**: former/latter rendered rightly.
8. **0009 col. 0097 C, Ὁ μὲν γὰρ ἄψυχον, ὁ δὲ ἄλογον** (Arius soulless, Apollinarius mindless): the order is kept. **Ἑκάτερος … παθητὸν φύσει θεότητος, ἀλλ᾿ οὐ φύσει σαρκός**: polarity kept.
9. **0009 col. 0097 B, Πῶς οὖν … προφασίζονται· … φασί· …** is closed with a question mark in the English where the plate's period has a middle dot and full stop. The sentence is introduced by Πῶς, and the sense is a question. A Pattern 8 nicety, not reported.
10. **0010 col. 0105 B, αὐτοῦ γὰρ ἦν κατὰ φύσιν. Αἵματος γὰρ … κεκοινώνηκε, δύο …**: the English folds the Greek's two full stops into one sentence with a parenthesis (as Combefis does). This is punctuation only (Pattern 8) and the sense is unchanged. Flagged for the coordinator, not reported as a finding.
11. **0010 col. 0105 D, τῆς ἐξομολογήσεως** rendered as the title *Confession*. The Latin has *libro secundo Confessionis*, so it follows the printed text.
12. **0010 col. 0104 C, ἢ δηλονότι σκοπούντων**: the English supplies "[by nature]" in square brackets, visibly marked as supplied, and the sense is right (the examples that follow are by union and by nature).
13. **0007 col. 0073 A, ἢ γνῶναι καθοτιοῦν βουλομένων** rendered "nor are willing in any way to know one": the negative of μηδεμίαν carries over through ἤ. Correct.
