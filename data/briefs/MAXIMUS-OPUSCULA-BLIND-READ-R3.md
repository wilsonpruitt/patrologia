## Findings

### F1 — chunk 0012, col 0113 B (Gregory Naz. quoted in the Sicilian letter, Opusc. 9) — class 1 (polarity)
- **Greek** (file only; the reading is not in doubt): καλὸν γὰρ μήτε ἁμαρτάνοντα, μήτε ὑπονοούμενον, ἕως ἂν οἷόν τε ᾖ, καὶ ὁ λόγος αἱρῇ, τιθέναι πρόσκομμα τοῖς πολλοῖς ἢ σκάνδαλον
- **Latin**: *Pulchrum enim est, nec peccando, nec laborando suspicione, quoad fieri possit ac ferat ratio, offendiculum aut scandalum vulgo ponere* (same structure; no help to the English).
- **English as it stands**: "for it is good, neither sinning nor being suspected, so far as is possible and reason allows, to set an obstacle or a stumbling-block before the many;"
- **Proposed**: "for it is good, so far as is possible and reason allows, to set no obstacle or stumbling-block before the many, either by sinning or by being suspected;"
- **Why**: the Greek μήτε … μήτε negates the whole act (put a stumbling-block neither by sinning nor by being suspected). Rendered word for word, the English negation sticks to the participles and the main clause reads positively, so a reader takes Gregory as saying it is good to set a stumbling-block. The next clause (the penalty for scandalizing the little ones) shows the intended sense.
- **Confidence**: probable (the English is literal, but in English it reads reversed)
- **Already in the cruces?** (checked after) — see the foot of the report.

### F2 — chunk 0014, cols 0133 D – 0137 C (Opusc. 10, to Marinus) — class 5 (addressee's number; brief "Traps", M5, Pattern 17)
Maximus opens in the singular (ὑπερεῖδες, ἐσπούδασας, σὺ τὸν ἐμόν, ἐτίμησας, εἴωθας: "thou", correctly) and then turns to the plural for the rest of the letter. The English keeps "thou/thy" throughout, with no T-note. Five sites, all file only (the number is set by endings and pronouns, and the Latin agrees at every site):
1. col 0133 D — Greek τῇ τῶν **ὑμετέρων** εὐχῶν διατρανώσει · English "by the clarifying of thy prayers" → "by the clarifying of your prayers".
2. col 0133 D / 0136 A — Greek οὐκ ἐν τοσούτοις, ὅσοις **γεγράφατε**, κεφαλαίοις (Latin *quot scripsistis*) · English "not in so many chapters as thou hast written," → "not in so many chapters as you have written,".
3. col 0136 C — Greek κατὰ τὴν **ὑμετέραν** κέλευσιν (Latin *secundum jussionem vestram*) · English "And I besought the Romans, according to thy command," → "And I besought the Romans, according to your command,".
4. col 0137 C — Greek τὴν περὶ τούτων κέλευσιν **ὑμῶν** (Latin *vestram … jussionem*) · English "but since I wished to fulfil thy command about these things" → "but since I wished to fulfil your command about these things".
5. col 0137 C — Greek Ἡ κριθεῖσα … οἰκονομία, τοῖς ἁγιωτάτοις **ὑμῖν** … κἀμοὶ τῷ **ὑμετέρῳ** · English "The economy decided upon by thee, most holy one, for the copying" … "to me also, thy [servant]," → "The economy decided upon by you most holy ones, for the copying" … "to me also, your [servant],".
- **Why**: the brief says "the English follows the Greek pronoun". The plural here may be honorific, but by Pattern 17 a plural takes *you*, and the shift from σύ to ὑμεῖς inside one letter is exactly what that rule keeps visible. (s10 σὲ καὶ φωνῇ is a stray σὲ, probably for γε. It is not an address, and the English rightly does not render it.)
- **Confidence**: certain as to the Greek; whether to keep the plural is the coordinator's call under M5.
- **Already in the cruces?** see foot.

### F3 — chunk 0014, col 0140 A–B (Opusc. 11, from the letter written in Rome) — class 3 (mis-parsed: what was founded, and διά)
- **Greek** (file only; no letter in doubt): Καὶ γὰρ ἅπερ αὐτὸς ὁ τῶν ὅλων δημιουργὸς … οἵ τε τούτου μαθηταὶ … καὶ μάρτυρες, ἱερουργηθέντες οἰκείοις ἔργοις τε καὶ λόγοις, ἀγῶσί τε καὶ ἱδρῶσι, καὶ πόνοις καὶ αἵμασι, καὶ τελευταῖον ἐξαισίοις θανάτοις διὰ τὴν ἡμῶν τῶν εἰς αὐτὸν πιστευόντων καθολικὴν καὶ ἀποστολικὴν Ἐκκλησίαν ἐθεμελίωσαν καὶ ᾠκοδόμησαν, … καταλῦσαι σπουδάζουσι
- **Latin**: *… postremoque stupendis interfectionibus atque necibus immolati ob nostram in eum credentium catholicam et apostolicam Ecclesiam, fundaverunt* (it parses the passage the same way as proposed here).
- **English as it stands**: "consecrated by their own works and words, by contests and sweats, by toils and blood, and finally by extraordinary deaths, founded and built up, the catholic and apostolic Church, for the sake of us who believe in him: this,"
- **Proposed**: "being offered in sacrifice by their own works and words, by contests and sweats, by toils and blood, and finally by extraordinary deaths, for the sake of the catholic and apostolic Church of us who believe in him, founded and built up: this,"
- **Why**: (a) διά governs τὴν … Ἐκκλησίαν, and ἡμῶν τῶν … πιστευόντων is a genitive inside that phrase. The English makes the Church the thing founded, set in apposition to ἅπερ, and moves "for the sake of" onto "us". The object of ἐθεμελίωσαν is ἅπερ, the mystery of the faith that the opponents try to annul "by two words". (b) ἱερουργηθέντες is a passive participle agreeing with the founders (they were offered as sacrifices). The English "consecrated by…" reads as an active main verb taking "what" as its object.
- **Confidence**: probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** see foot.

### F4 — chunk 0014, col 0133 B (Opusc. 10 opening) — class 5 (a silent conjecture against the plate)
- **Greek** (plate-read, pdf p. 83, left column, band B, line 3): καὶ πτωχείᾳ τὸ **ἐντελὲς** μεγαλύνας τῆς ἐμῆς … ἀπειρίας
- **Latin**: *… temeritatis mediocritatem exinanitione, ac tenuitatem paupertate, supra modum extulisti* (Combefis reads or paraphrases it as *tenuitatem*).
- **English as it stands**: "and magnifying by poverty the cheapness, of my inexperience"
- **Proposed**: "and magnifying by poverty the fullness, of my inexperience", with a note: "Migne prints ἐντελές (confirmed on the plate); εὐτελές, 'cheapness', the sense Combefis's *tenuitatem* suggests, is a conjecture." Or keep "cheapness" and add that note.
- **Why**: the plate prints ἐντελές ("complete, perfect"). "Cheapness" renders the conjecture εὐτελές, with no note saying so. The irony survives either way ("the fullness of my inexperience"), so this does not reverse the sense. But the brief and Pattern 7 require a conjecture against the plate to carry a note.
- **Confidence**: certain as to the plate; probable that it needs a note.
- **Already in the cruces?** see foot.

### F5 — chunk 0015, cols 0141–0146 (Opusc. 12, Latin only) — class 5 (addressee's number)
Anastasius's Latin moves between singular address (*dignatus es*, *servo tuo*, *tua … vestigia*, *benedicte domine mi*, *obsecro … praecipere*) and plural address. The English renders every plural as *thou/thy*, with no T-note. Sites, from the Latin text, which is the text here (file only):
1. col 0141 — *Vestris laudabilibus syllabis dignatus es* · English "By thy praiseworthy syllables thou hast deigned" → "By your praiseworthy syllables thou hast deigned".
2. col 0141 — *qui secundum vos pietatis sunt amatores* · English "so that all who, like thee, are lovers of piety" → "so that all who, like you, are lovers of piety".
3. col 0141 — *qui sancte secundum vos vivere proposuerint* · English "but that those who have purposed to live holily, like thee," → "but that those who have purposed to live holily, like you,".
4. col 0141 — *ut ipsi testamini, laudatissimi* (plural verb, plural vocative) · English "as thou thyself bearest witness, most praiseworthy one," → "as you yourselves bear witness, most praiseworthy ones,".
5. col 0144 — *quae bene vobis … visa fuisse dignoscitur* · English "which is known to have seemed good to thee" → "which is known to have seemed good to you".
6. col 0146 — *et vestrae laudem sublimitatis* · English "and the praise of thy sublimity." → "and the praise of your sublimity."
(Also p28 s2 *discite … hortamini* are plural imperatives. The English imperatives "learn … exhort" are unmarked for number and need no change.)
- **Why**: brief, "the English follows the Greek pronoun", and here the Latin is the text. The plural may be honorific, but by Pattern 17 it is rendered *you*. Site 4 also turns a plural vocative into a singular one.
- **Confidence**: certain as to the Latin; keeping the plural is the coordinator's call under M5/R2.
- **Already in the cruces?** see foot.

### F6 — chunk 0015, col 0143 D (Opusc. 12) — class 5 (a term turned)
- **Latin** (the text): *tantumque divinorum lapsum bene valde et compatientissime dogmatum deplorantem*
- **English as it stands**: "and deploring very well and most patiently so great a fall of the divine doctrines,"
- **Proposed**: "and deploring very well and most compassionately so great a fall of the divine doctrines,"
- **Why**: *compatientissime* (from *compatior*, "suffer with") means "with the deepest fellow-feeling", not "patiently". Sophronius is weeping as a second Jeremiah; "patiently" makes his grief sound calm.
- **Confidence**: probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** see foot.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- 0011 s26 μεσίτας Θεοῦ μᾶλλον, ἢ μιμητάς → "mediators of God rather than imitators": Combefis has *non magis … quam* ("no more … than", i.e. both). μᾶλλον ἢ is ordinarily "rather than", so the English is a defensible reading of the Greek. Not reported.
- 0011 s8 "partaking at all in neither…": the participle μετέχουσαν is feminine and belongs to φύσιν μίαν, while the English has it hanging loosely near "Christ". The sense still comes through (the one nature partakes of neither). Rejected as a matter of style.
- 0011 scholion: the stray "Εr." and the reading ἓν καὶ ἓν ἐκ δύο τῶν ἐναντίων are rendered as printed. Not findings.
- 0012 s2 ἡμετέρῳ → "your servant": logged in the cruces (T. read ὑμετέρῳ, Latin *servo vestro*). It does not reverse the sense.
- 0012 s10 ταὐτὸν σὺν Σαβελλίῳ γνωρίζοντες → "are known to hold the same as Sabellius": an active participle rendered with a passive turn. The opponents still hold the Sabellian identity, so who does what is not changed. Minor, and not reported.
- 0012 s31–32: Migne's close-guillemet falls after ὑπολήψιν;, which puts Maximus's retort "And what could this ever contribute…" inside the opponents' quotation. The English closes the quotation after Cyril's words and gives the retort to Maximus. That is the sense the argument requires, so it is a punctuation matter (Pattern 6/8) and not a who-speaks reversal. The coordinator may want a crux line on it.
- 0013 s21 "which those will no longer suffer, nor commit, who prefer the true to the false, our defence…": clumsy but literal, with the polarity intact.
- 0014 s10 "just as we too [cannot express] ours [save] in ours": the supplied negative plus "save" gives the same sense as the Greek's positive comparison (we express ours precisely in our own tongue). Not a reversal.
- 0014 s17 παρεγκληθέντας → "those who have swerved": Combefis's conjecture παρεγκληθέντα is noted in the [n:]. The English sense is plausible.
- 0014 s22 τοῦ τείχους rendered "volume" (τεύχους): this is the sense the sentence needs and it changes nothing. I did not check whether a note exists.
- 0015 p20 s5 *Domini contemnentes inducias, ad conversionem hos benigne cohortantes*: *cohortantes* can agree with *inducias* (the respite, which kindly urged them) as well as with persons. The English "and those who kindly exhorted them" supplies an "and" and adds a second object. The Latin is ambiguous and the argument is unchanged, so it is not reported. If the editor reads *inducias … cohortantes*, the line would be "despising the Lord's respite, which kindly exhorted them to conversion".
- 0015 s3 *sine successu receptionem* [?]: a known crux, excluded by the brief.
- Every negative in the Greek of 0011–0014 and the Latin of 0015 was checked against the English: οὐ/οὐκ/οὐχ, μή, οὐδέ/μηδέ, οὐδείς/μηδείς/μηδέτερος, οὔτε/μήτε, δίχα/πλήν/χωρίς, privatives (ἀνενέργητον, ἀμέτοχος, ἀδιάσπαστον, ἀπρόσληπτον, ἀσυμβάτως…), and *non/nec/neque/nullatenus/nequaquam/nemo* in 0015. The only polarity problem is F1, where the Greek negation is carried over word for word but lands on the wrong clause in English.
