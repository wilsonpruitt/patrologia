### F1. 0017, col. 0156 B — class 2 (whose servant)
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 94 right column, band B):** Τί δὲ κρατεῖσθαί με τὸν **ἡμέτερον** δοῦλον πείρᾳ πρότερον
- **Latin:** *qui me servum **vestrum** … teneri*
- **English as it stands:** `that I your servant am held fast`
- **Proposed:** `that I, our servant, am held fast` (or keep "your" and add a note: "Greek ἡμέτερον, 'our'; Combefis *vestrum*")
- **Why:** the plate prints ἡμέτερον ("our"), not ὑμέτερον. The English gives the Latin's possessor without a note. It may be ἡ/ὑ itacism, and "your servant" is the expected idiom, so the meaning is not reversed. But the English silently goes with the Latin over the Greek.
- **Confidence:** probable (the reading is certain; that it matters is the probable part).
- **Already in the cruces?** YES. The misprint table, row `p0094.19 | ἡμέτερον | ὑμέτερον`, logs it as Migne's misprint, and the English reads the correction. That is the convention, and it does not reverse the sense, so **F1 is WITHDRAWN** and kept here only as a record of the check. (A side note: the identical case at 0113, cruces line 204, is written up as a prose T-note. The same class is logged in two forms.)

### F2. 0017, col. 0160 A — class 3 (relative mis-attached)
- **Greek (file only):** δηλαδὴ τὴν ἐνέργειαν ἐνεργητικὸς ὑπάρχων ἐδείκνυτο, **καθ᾿ ὃ** φύσει δι᾿ ἡμᾶς ὕστερον γέγονεν ἄνθρωπος, ἀλλ᾿ οὐ **καθ᾿ ὅπερ** ὑπῆρχεν ἀνάρχως **δι᾿ ἑαυτὸν** φύσει Θεός.
- **English as it stands:** `he was plainly shown, as being operative, [to have] the operation according to which he later became man by nature for our sake, but not according to that which he was without beginning, through himself, by nature, God.`
- **Proposed:** `he was plainly shown as being operative in operation, in respect of that according to which he later became man by nature for our sake, but not of that according to which he was, without beginning, God by nature for his own sake.`
- **Why:** ὅ is neuter and cannot take ἐνέργειαν (feminine) as its antecedent. It means "in the respect in which" (his manhood against his Godhead, the whole point of §§19–21). The English makes "the operation" the thing "according to which he became man", which is a different Christological claim. Also δι᾿ ἑαυτόν + accusative means "for his own sake", parallel to δι᾿ ἡμᾶς, not "through himself".
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** No. No entry at 0160 deals with καθ᾿ ὃ or δι᾿ ἑαυτόν.

## Candidates considered and rejected

- **0017 col. 157 A, δίχα θελήσεως, ἀλλ᾿ οὐ θελήσεως δίχα.** The plate confirms δίχα. The English reads "by volition", a conjecture logged in the cruces (line 316). It gives the sense the argument needs. Not a finding.
- **0018 col. 165 C, ἡ αὐτὴ ἐλάττων εἶναι καὶ φύσις* note key.** The plate prints **ἴση φύσις** (with a note call). Our Greek FILE has lost ἴση and carries the junk "* note key". The English "less and equal" is correct to the plate. This is a defect in the Greek file only, not a finding about the English. It does not seem to be logged; whoever maintains `src/greek/…/0018.md` may want it.
- **0018 col. 172 C, κεφαλαίῳ βʹ.** The plate reads νβʹ. The English "chapter 52" is right, and the cruces log it (line 350).
- **0018 col. 173 C, καθέστηκεν ἕνωσις.** The plate reads ἕνωσις. The English "the Ekthesis" is a conjecture logged in the cruces (line 355). The sense requires it.
- **0018 col. 173 C, καὶ ψὸ τούτων … τὰ δόγματα → "the doctrines before them".** The first letter is clipped at the plate's left margin, so it could be read as τὰ ("their doctrines") or πρό. Whichever it is, the sense barely moves. Not a finding.
- **0017 col. 156 C, ἵνα μὴ … ἐλλείψῃ τὴν σωτηρίαν, οὐχ ὅλην τῇ φύσει διδούς.** In the English the participles could be read as statements of fact rather than as part of the "lest" clause. The order of the clauses keeps the Greek structure. Not a finding.
- **0017 col. 157 B, ἵνα μὴ τὴν φύσιν μόνον παθοῦσαν.** παθοῦσαν may be a misprint (πᾶσαν?). The English "the suffering nature" follows the printed word, and the argument still holds. Not a finding.
- **0018 col. 168 B, Nestorius, ὁ Θεὸς Λόγος ἀναλαβὼν … προεκρίθη.** The English keeps the Greek's grammatical subject ("God the Word … was preferred"). Nestorius probably means the man, but the English does not resolve an ambiguity that is in the Greek. Not a finding.
- **0019 col. 176 D, ἐλεημοσύνη = "mercy",** where 0018 col. 168 C renders the same Chrysostom sentence "almsgiving". This is an inconsistency within the work, but the term is not in the glossary, and a cruces T-note (line 369) explains "mercy". Not reported.
- **0019 col. 181 C/D, ὡς ἂν μὴ συναπαχθῶμεν … ἀλλ᾿ οὐκ … ἱστάμενοι → "but stand".** The negated participle inside the "lest" clause comes out as a positive purpose. The logic is equivalent and nothing is reversed.
- **0020 col. 193 A, τῷ μὴ ὑπάρχειν ἐκεῖνο → "through that natural will's not existing".** It is correct inside "neither … nor", and ἐκεῖνο does refer to the natural will. It reads awkwardly but does not reverse the sense.
- **0020 col. 188 C, Gregory's quotation in s22.** The closing guillemet takes in Maximus's own apodosis (πῶς ἔμελλεν …). The Greek file sets it the same way, so the English follows the file. Not a finding.
- **0016, Opusc. 13 §5.** Eutyches: "not as not saying the union". It is checked against the Greek, which has the double negative, and is correct. Every other negative in Opusc. 13–14 was checked and all are carried.
