## Findings

### F1. 0021, col. 0200 C–D (s43) — class 1 (negative in the wrong clause)
- **Greek** (plate-read, p. 118, col. 200 C/D: printed as in the file): Ἀλλ᾿ οὐδὲ μίαν μόνον, ἄν περ ᾖ τὴν φύσιν διπλοῦς, τὴν ἔμφυτον ἔχειν ἐνέργειαν τὸν ἐνεργοῦντα, πᾶσα ἀνάγκη· ἐπεὶ καὶ φύσιν μίαν πάντως τὸν ὑφεστῶτα, κἂν σύνθετος ᾖ τὴν ὑπόστασιν.
- **Latin**: Sed nec unam solum operationem naturalem habeat, qui operatur, prorsus necesse est, siquidem ipse duplicis naturae exsistat. Alioqui, prorsus etiam una natura erit ejus qui subsistit …
- **English as it stands**: "But neither is it altogether necessary that the one operating have only one innate operation, if he be twofold in nature;"
- **Proposed**: "But it is altogether necessary, too, that the one operating have not only one innate operation, if he be twofold in nature;"
- **Why**: οὐδὲ negates μίαν μόνον inside the infinitive, not πᾶσα ἀνάγκη: Maximus asserts it is *necessary* that a two-natured agent have more than one operation (the ἐπεί-clause: otherwise he would have one nature). The English makes it merely "not necessary" that he have one, which concedes the monenergist's option.
- **Confidence**: probable.
- **In the cruces?**: No. The 0200–0201 entries cover παρυποστήσει, the marginal rubric, and προσβολή, not this clause.

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **0021 col. 200 B, s31**: Πῶς δὲ καὶ ὁ τοιαύτην ἔχων ἐνέργειαν … τὰ ὅμοια πείσεται; → EN "And how will he too … *not* undergo like things". Plate-read: no οὐ, and Πῶς carries a question mark. But the next sentence (γάρ: the agent must cleave to his operation) shows the implied answer is that he *does* undergo the like. So the English "how … not" keeps the sense, where a literal "how will he undergo" would read in English as a denial. Not a reversal. Possible style note only.
2. **0021 s17**: οὐ πρὸ πολλοῦ … πεποιηκὼς ἀνηγόρευται ("declared to have done this not long before") → EN "is not declared to have done this long before". The negative moves, but the sense does not change (he mentioned his soul only near the passion).
3. **0023 col. 220 C, s15**: ὡς οὐδὲ τὸ παράπαν ἀλλήλαις ἕξις ὑπάρχει καὶ στέρησις → "are not at all the same with one another". Combefis has *idem inter se exsistant*. Acceptable.
4. **0023 col. 220 A, s20**: κατὰ τοῦ θείου θελήματος → "in respect of the divine will" (Latin *una cum divina voluntate*). Loose, but no reversal.
5. **0023 col. 220 C, s26**: τὸ ὑποδύεσθαι → "shrinking back". Considered as "undergoing", but the Latin has *refugam cautionem* and ὑποδύομαι can mean "slip away". Kept.
6. **0023 col. 224 A, s40**: τοὺς … ὀρθοδόξως πιστεύοντας → "with us, those who believe". The accusative is awkward, but the English follows the Latin construction (*nobiscum, qui recta fide … credamus*). No who-does-what flip.
7. **0024 col. 224 C, s4**: κατάγνωσις φύσεώς ἐστι γὰρ τοῦτο → "for this is a condemnation of nature". This looked like it might have lost an οὐ (compare s7 οὐκ ἔγκλημα), but the Latin agrees (*Est enim istud naturae damnatio*). Elliptical: denying the natural shrinking would condemn nature. No finding.
8. **0025 col. 232 C, s10**: μίαν δὲ αὐτῶν τὴν ἰδιότητα λέγειν συγχύσεως ἄνευ → "but to call their property one, without confusion". The English could be read as a necessity, but the Greek and Latin have the same ellipsis. Already in the cruces (0232).
9. **0025 col. 237 A, s34**: τὸ προσαρμόζειν … συμπαραλαμβάνειν, οὐ μὴ θέμις. The English "yet … is not lawful" follows the Latin parse. Already in the cruces (0237, οὐ μὴν θέμις).
10. **0025 col. 228–229, s2**: περιγράφουσά … ὑπερβαίνουσαν … παρασκευάζουσαν. I checked whether the agent is ἥτις (the passing-over) or εὔροια (the flow). Two of the three participles are accusative, so the English "a flow that …" is right.
11. **0023 s17**: the English sets the locator `[n: Joan. x, 36.]` after "My God and your God" (John 20:17). It is a Combefis locator, outside this read's classes, so not reported.
