## Findings

### F1. 0029, col. 0269 [b: A] (Opusc. 24): a "not" moved onto the hypostases of the Trinity
- **Class:** 3 (mis-parsed grammar; it adds a polarity claim the Greek does not make)
- **Greek** (file only; the point is the construal, not the letters): ἀλλ᾿ ὑποστατικόν; καὶ πάλιν ἀλλοτριώσεις τὸν Υἱὸν τοῦ Πατρὸς, καὶ τρεῖς θελήσεις εἰσάγων φανήσῃ μὴ συμβαινούσας ἀλλήλαις, ὥσπερ καὶ τὰς ὑποστάσεις.
- **Latin:** *ac tres voluntates inter se non convenientes, uti et personas, palam induces* (*personas* is the object of *induces*, as θελήσεις is of εἰσάγων)
- **English as it stands:** "and thou wilt appear to introduce three volitions not concurring with one another, as the hypostases also do not."
- **Proposed English:** "and thou wilt appear to introduce three volitions not concurring with one another, just as [thou introducest three] hypostases."
- **Why:** τὰς ὑποστάσεις is accusative, a second object of εἰσάγων beside τρεῖς θελήσεις (Combefis reads it the same way: *uti et personas … induces*). The reductio is that a hypostatic will multiplies wills to match the three hypostases, and the absurdity is that those wills would not concur. "As the hypostases also do not" turns that into Maximus saying himself that the Father, Son and Spirit do not concur with one another, which is the conclusion he is trying to make absurd. (With 0026 rendering συμβαίνω as "concur", meaning agreement, the reader has no other way to take it.)
- **Confidence:** probable. The Greek is elliptical. If the translator meant "do not coincide", the English still says something else.
- **Already in the cruces?** No. Checked after the finding was written: the chunk-0029 entries at 0269 note the theandric will, Severus, the reckoning-pebbles, Ταλμῶν and the Basil source, and nothing on this clause.

## Candidates considered and rejected

1. **0026 s5, col. 0240 C:** ὑπαινίττεσαι (2nd sg.) is rendered "he hints". The subject is plainly Honorius (φάσκων, introducing his quotation), and Combefis has *innuit*. This is a Migne misprint (for -εται) and the English follows the sense. Rejected.
2. **0026 s21, col. 0244 D:** τὴν οἱανοῦν κυρίωσιν ἢ ἐκβολήν is rendered "any annulment or expulsion". κυρίωσις means "ratification", but with ἐκβολήν the sense needs ἀκύρωσιν, and Combefis has *abolendam seu excludendam*. The English follows the sense without a note. This is probably a misprint, and the sense is not reversed. Rejected (worth a crux line at most).
3. **0026 s17, col. 0244 B:** Δέος οὖν πολλὰ βαίνειν ἐπιβαλεῖν is the known `[?]` of 0026 (*verisimile est* in the Latin). Not a finding.
4. **0026 s20:** κατὰ κέλευσιν αὐτοῦ, "at his bidding". Combefis has *jubente Honorio*, and the English "his" could be read as Anastasius. This is an ambiguity, not a flip. Rejected.
5. **0026 s13:** κἂν τῷ φυσικῷ … συνέβαινε μέν is rendered "even if in his natural … he concurred … yet". It could also be read as καὶ ἐν, "and that in his natural [will] too he concurred". Both readings keep the polarity and the μέν/δέ. Rejected.
6. **0027 s3:** τῇ τε πνεύσει is rendered "by your inquiry". The translator has read πεύσει, which fits δι᾿ ἐρωτήσεως earlier in the sentence. Not a reversal. Rejected.
7. **0027 s33:** the alternatives "or he has dissolved it … or he declares" have no expressed "but if after the union". The Greek omits it as well, and Combefis follows the same ἢ … ἢ. The English follows the Greek. Rejected.
8. **0028 s1:** διαπεμπομένην, "dismissing". Combefis has *repudiet* (perhaps reading παραπεμπομένην). It follows the sense. Rejected.
9. **0028 s14:** οἱ κατὰ τὴν Νικαέων … Πατέρες is rendered "the Fathers after the synod of the Nicaeans". Combefis has *post Nicenam synodum*, and the argument demands post-Nicene Fathers, since the 318 are named as the legislators. Rejected.
10. **0028 Opusc. 23 s4, col. 0261 A:** ἐν τῇ κατὰ σύγχυσιν συνόδῳ, "in the concourse by way of confusion". The phrase is theologically odd, but Combefis has *secundum confusionem*, so it is the printed text, and the English is faithful. Rejected (Migne may have σύνθεσιν in mind, but that would need the plate and a conjecture, and the English as printed reverses nothing).
11. **0028 s12:** Παριστᾷν δὲ προτρέπομαι is rendered "I urge you to set forth". Combefis has *Rogo … ut ostendatis*. Rejected.
12. **0029 s10, col. 0268 C:** Νῦν δὲ αὐτὴν οὐκ ἀποκρύπτει is rendered "now he does not hide the operation". The negative is kept, and Combefis has *operationem vimque non celat*. Rejected.
13. **0029 Opusc. 25 c. 3, col. 0272 B:** ἄρα θελήματι τῆς σαρκὸς διαφέρει τοῦ Πατρὸς καὶ Πνεύματος is rendered "therefore by the will of the flesh he differs from the Father and the Spirit". I first suspected a who-differs-from-whom flip, since the premise is that he is united to Father and Spirit in will. But Combefis construes it identically (*ergo carnis voluntate differt a Patre et Spiritu*), and the reading is coherent: it is the converse of c. 2, where the will of the flesh is what sets him apart from them. Rejected.
14. **0029 c. 10 s3:** σαφηνίσαι ὑμῖν, "to make clear to you". This is odd after παρακαλῶ τὴν ὑμετέραν σύνεσιν, but Combefis also has *vobis*. The English follows the Greek. Rejected.
15. **0029 s4:** οὐ γὰρ ὑποστάσει … ἀλλὰ τὸ μὲν φύσει, τὸ δὲ ὑποστάσει. This is rendered literally and matches Combefis (*alterum natura, alterum hypostasi*). Rejected.
16. **0030 Ambrose s3:** Μὴ γὰρ οὕτως ἡ ἥττων ἐνεργεῖ … is rendered as a statement ("does not operate so"). μή may be interrogative, but the file prints no ";" and the sense is the same either way. Rejected.
17. **0030 Gregory of Nyssa:** the English opens a new quotation at Πᾶσα ἀνάγκη, where the file runs on without a stop. This is a layout point only, with no text lost or added. Rejected.
