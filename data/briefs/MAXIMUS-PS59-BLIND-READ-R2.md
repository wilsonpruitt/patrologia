## Findings

### F1. Brief case R2-a, p0438.40: καὶ οὐκ ἐξελεύσῃ … as a statement: KEEP
- **Chunk 0002, col. 869 C/D (lemma, Ps 59:12b).** Class 3 (question vs statement), judged.
- **Greek, plate-read (p0438, left column):** Καὶ οὐκ ἐξελεύσῃ, ὁ Θεὸς, ἐν ταῖς δυνάμεσιν ἡμῶν. — the plate sets a clear **full stop** after ἡμῶν (the italic lemma runs onto a second line, ἡμῶν., centred). The preceding lemma Οὐχὶ σὺ … ἡμᾶς; has the question mark, so the compositor distinguished them.
- **Latin:** *Et non egredieris, Deus, in exercitibus nostris.* (stop, not ?), against *Nonne tu, Deus, qui repulisti nos?* above.
- **Exposition:** Οὐ γὰρ ἐξελεύσεται ὁ Θεὸς ἐν τῷ ἐφ᾿ ἑαυτῷ θαῤῥοῦντι … Ἀλλ᾿ ἐν τοῖς … εἰκότως ὁ Θεὸς ἐξελεύσεται — Maximus expounds the clause as a negative assertion (God does not go forth with our own powers = self-reliance; he goes forth with those who trust in him). The LXX's own sense (a question expecting yes) is not Maximus's reading.
- **English as it stands:** *And thou wilt not go forth, O God, with our powers.* / "For God will not go forth with him who is confident in himself…"
- **Proposed:** no change. Polarity is right (negative in lemma and in the exposition's first sentence; positive ἐξελεύσεται in the ἀλλά-clause, rendered "God will fittingly go forth"). Plate punctuation and exposition agree; Pattern 8 is satisfied.
- **Confidence:** certain.

### F2. Brief case R2-b, p0438.43: κατὰ τὴν ῥομφαίαν ἐπιγραφομένῳ ἑαυτοῦ σωτήριον: CHANGE (small)
- **Chunk 0002, col. 869 D.** Class 3 (mis-parse: reflexive moved to the wrong noun).
- **Greek, plate-read (p0438, left col., lines 2–3 of band D):** κατὰ τὴν ῥομ-|φαίαν ἐπιγραφομένῳ ἑαυτοῦ σωτήριον. Our file matches the plate exactly; no misprint. It construes: ἐπιγραφομένῳ (middle, "claiming / ascribing") with object ἑαυτοῦ σωτήριον "his own salvation", and κατὰ τὴν ῥομφαίαν "by / according to the sword" (Ps 43:7 LXX: ἡ ῥομφαία μου οὐ σώσει με). It does not construe badly; κατά + acc. for the means is loose but fine.
- **Latin:** *qui gladio suo salutem ascribit* — moves the possessive onto the sword; the English follows it.
- **English as it stands:** "and ascribes salvation to his own sword."
- **Proposed:** "and ascribes his own salvation to the sword."
- **Why:** ἑαυτοῦ goes with σωτήριον, not with ῥομφαίαν (there is no possessive on the sword in the Greek). The sense is close, but the English follows the Latin where the Latin departs from the Greek, which the brief rules out. Keeping "ascribes" is right: the same verb (ἐπιγράφοντες ἑαυτοῖς) comes back at col. 872 "who do not ascribe to themselves", and the echo should stay.
- **Confidence:** probable (the change is small; the polarity and the agent are unaffected).

### F4. Brief case R2-c, p0436.39–40: ἄρχει … παντὸς ἀγαθοῦ «rules over» vs *auspicatur* «begins»: KEEP
- **Chunk 0002, col. 865 C/D.** Class 5 (sense of a term), judged.
- **Greek, plate-read (p0436, left column):** Ἰούδας ἐξομολόγησις ἑρμηνεύεται. Ἄρχει γὰρ ἡ δι᾿ εὐχῶν ἐξομολόγησις ἐν τοῖς ἁγίοις παντὸς ἀγαθοῦ. The file matches the plate.
- **Latin:** *Quae enim in precibus confessio est, omne in sanctis bonum auspicatur.*
- **English as it stands:** "For confession made through prayers rules, in the saints, over every good."
- **Proposed:** no change. ἄρχω + gen. can mean either "rule" or "begin", so the γάρ decides. The sentence gives the reason the lemma calls Judah (= confession) *my king*: confession is king because it rules over every good. "Begins every good" would explain nothing in the lemma; it is the Latin bringing in the commonplace *initium bonorum operum confessio*. The next sentence keeps the thread with βασιλεύς ("the true king"). If the double sense is wanted, a T note quoting *auspicatur* would do; the running English should stay.
- **Confidence:** probable (both senses are lexically open; the context favours «rules»).

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **col. 865 κάθαρσις γάρ ἐστιν ὁ λέβης, τῆς ἐλπίδος· τουτέστιν … ἑτοιμότης** — «For the basin of hope is purification: that is, the readiness…». The comma makes the attachment of τῆς ἐλπίδος uncertain. The English is a defensible parse and agrees with the Latin (*Est enim lebes, spei emundatio; quae scilicet … praeparatio*). Rejected.
- **col. 868 τὰ τῆς σαρκὸς πάθη καταπαλαῖσαι τὸν ἀσκητήν** — subject and object of an accusative-plus-infinitive can be reversed. The English («for the ascetic … to wrestle down the passions») is the only reading that fits ἄνευ ἀσκήσεως … ἀμήχανον. The Latin agrees (*ut carnis vitia expugnet, qui pietatis cultor est*). Rejected.
- **col. 869 ἡ ἐκ πάντων … θεωρημάτων περιεχομένη** «enclosed by all the … objects of contemplation». ἐκ marks the source («comprised of»), not the agent. The nuance is real but small, and it echoes col. 868's «enclosed» (περιεχομένη) for the περιοχή wordplay. Not a reversal. Optional: «enclosed from all…» / «comprised of all…». Not filed.
- **πόλις περιοχῆς «fortified city» vs the exposition's περιεχομένη «enclosed»** — the lemma word and the gloss word do not match in English. The term is fixed by ruling, so not reported.
- **col. 869 Ἢ τίς ὁδηγήσει με ἕως τῆς Ἰδουμαίας.** — Migne's full stop is visible on p0438. The English «?» is the known, noted case. Not a finding.
- **col. 868 «the might of his strength»** in italics without a Migne locator. It is an Eph 6:10 echo, not a quoted lemma. Rejected.
- **col. 865 ὁ ὑπὲρ ἡμῶν ἡνίκα γέγονεν ἄνθρωπος, ἐξομολογούμενος** — «for us» could go with «confessing» instead of «became man». The Latin attaches it to *factus homo*, as the English does. Rejected.
- **col. 868 τοῦ ἑνὸς μόνου τε καὶ ἀρχικοῦ λόγου** «the one, sole and sovereign Word» (capitalised) against τοὺς αὐτῶν λόγους «their principles». This is an interpretive choice, defensible for Maximus (logoi → Logos). Rejected.
