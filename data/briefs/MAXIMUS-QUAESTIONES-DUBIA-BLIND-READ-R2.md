## Findings

### F1. Q.65, col 837 B (chunk 0005): the scope of ἵνα μή
- **Class:** 1 (polarity: the negative's reach over a second clause)
- **Greek (file only):** «ἵνα οὖν μὴ οὗτοι βλάπτωνται, καὶ τὸ βλάσφημον εἰς Θεὸν ἀνατρέχῃ, ἐπεξῆλθεν αὐτοῖς ὁ Θεός.»
- **Latin:** «Ne igitur ii labem acciperent, ac blasphemia in Deum recurreret, ipso ultore in illos animadversum est.» (*ne* governs both clauses.)
- **English as it stands:** «so that these might not be harmed, and the blasphemy run back up to God, God went out against them.»
- **Proposed English:** «so that these might not be harmed, and the blasphemy might not run back up to God, God went out against them.»
- **Why:** μή governs both subjunctives. In the English, «the blasphemy run back up to God» is most easily read as a second, positive purpose: God punished the mockers *so that* the blasphemy would rebound to God, which reverses the sense.
- **Confidence:** probable (the Greek is unambiguous; the English is ambiguous, and its easier reading is the wrong one).
- **Already in the cruces?** No. The 0837 cruces cover ἀνώρυξαν and κατ᾿ ἀντιστροφήν, not this clause.

### F2. Q.78, col 852 C (chunk 0006, the brief's p0429.35): the double negative
- **Class:** 3 (mis-parse risk: the English can be read with the sense reversed)
- **Greek (file only):** «Οὐ δύναται γὰρ ἅπερ ἔπραξε, μὴ λογίζεσθαι ὡς μὴ πράξας.»
- **Latin:** «Non enim potest eorum quae egit non meminisse, neque illa cogitare ac si non egisset.»
- **English as it stands:** «For he cannot not take account of what he did, as though he had not done it.»
- **Proposed English:** «For he cannot, as though he had not done them, not take account of the things he did.»
- **Why:** The sense is that the penitent cannot treat his deeds as undone; that is why his conscience stays «coloured» (s6). Read as a unit, the English «cannot not take account of what he did as though he had not done it» says he *must* regard his deeds as undone, the opposite. The comma alone separates the two readings. The proposal keeps both negatives and attaches ὡς μὴ πράξας to δύναται, where the context and the Latin put it. Shorter alternative, if dropping one negative is accepted: «For he cannot reckon what he did as though he had not done it.»
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** Partly. Crux 0852 «…did, as though he had not done it. — T. Two negatives, as printed and as in the Latin … he cannot help reckoning with his deeds as deeds done» records the right sense, so the translator meant what is proposed here. Nothing flags that the English sentence as printed can be read the other way, so the fix is to the wording only.

## Rejected candidates

- **Q.55, col 828 C, εἰκότως (class 3, statement made a question).** Greek file and plate print a full stop: «Ἐὰν σταθμίζεται ἡ ἐλεημοσύνη τοῦ Θεοῦ, καὶ περιγράφεται (j), εἰκότως.» The English «If the mercy of God is weighed, is it then fittingly also circumscribed?» makes it a question (the Latin also: «utique etiam merito circumscribitur?»). Rejected: the editor's note (j) on the same plate reads the sentence the same way: «*Καὶ περιγράφεται*. Quasi certis terminis finitur; quod quasi inconveniens habere videtur Maximus … Exponit itaque circumscribi Dei misericordiam, penes eorum capacitatem, qui illius effectus suscipiunt». Maximus states the conclusion as an apparent absurdity and «μᾶλλον οὖν» corrects it, so the rhetorical question carries the sense without reversing it. A literal statement («…it is also circumscribed, fittingly [one would say]») would be closer to the printed punctuation; editorial preference only, not a finding. Note (j) is not in the English; check whether it is meant to be carried.
- **Q.75, col 849 A, «πᾶς Δαβίδ;»** The Greek file prints a Greek question mark after Δαβίδ in the middle of a statement («τοῦτο σημαίνει, ὅτι πᾶς Δαβίδ; ὁ μὴ κατὰ διάθεσιν …»). The English runs it on as a statement. Right: nothing there is a question, and the mark is a misprint or OCR error for a stop. Not plate-checked, because the English does not depend on it.

## Cases to judge (R2): verdicts
- **Q.54 οὐ μὴ τελέσωσιν:** «they shall not finish». Keep.
- **Q.58 (who has the more):** τὸ πλέον ἔχει has no expressed subject apart from the participle phrase. The English «the abiding has the greater part» agrees with the Latin («hujus partes potiores esse», *hujus* = «the abiding and perfect», the nearer antecedent) and with the closing contrast (the one is examined, the other keeps feast). Keep. Not plate-read.
- **Q.60 οὐδ᾿ ὅλως:** «not having taken any care at all». Keep.
- **Q.66 οὐχ ἤνωτο:** «inasmuch as it was united to the Word, knew all things … but inasmuch as human nature in him was not united, it is said to be ignorant». The contrast ἡνώθη / οὐχ ἤνωτο is kept, the right way round: the knowing goes with the union and the ignorance with the nature taken by itself (editor's note (p), Gregory: as God / as man). It is not reversed. A reader could take «was not united» as a denial of the union. A T note exists in the cruces (p. 0423 (p)), so keep.
- **Q.65 «dug up»:** follows the printed ἀνώρυξαν. Keep (crux 0837).
- **Q.67 left/right:** the Greek prints ἀριστεράν, the English has «left», and the Latin has *sinistra*. The δεξιάν conjecture is Migne's asterisk note (crux 0841). Keep.
- **Q.68–70 gaps:** Q.68 has no question and keeps its answer; Q.69 keeps its question and has no answer; Q.70 has no question and keeps its answer. The English «[…]» matches all three. Keep.
- **Q.73 (the brief's «Q.72») 1 Cor 3:12–15:** the full quotation is rendered, both sentences, the «not» clauses included («he shall suffer loss; but he himself shall be saved»). The six materials are mapped in order (gold/mystagogy, silver/translucent life, stones/pious thoughts, wood/attachment, hay/unreason, stubble/corruption). Keep.
- **p0428.15 παραιτήσασθαι:** «beg to be pursued … and not famine». «Beg for» is right: «καὶ μὴ λιμόν» needs a verb of choosing, and with «beg off» the sentence would say «decline persecution, and not famine», which does not construe. Keep (crux 0849).

- **Q.79 the faculties:** ram = rational power (ruling part), bull = incensive, goat = desiring, turtle-dove = temperance, dove = sanctification. Matches the Greek. Sheep: fleece, milk, lamb. Droppings: 365 (τξεʹ) and 200 (διακοσίους). Keep.
