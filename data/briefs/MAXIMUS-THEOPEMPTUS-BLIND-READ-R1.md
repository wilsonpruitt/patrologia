### Case C. p0711.24–25, col. 1396 B–C (μὴ προορωμένην / οὐκ εἰργομένην): KEEP
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 711, Greek column):** «ἐκείνην τε μὴ προορωμένην τῶν κριμάτων τὸ μέγεθος· καὶ ταυτὴν οὐκ εἰργομένην, ἣν τέως ἡ χρηστότης προσμένουσα τὴν μεταβολὴν ἀναστέλλει, τὴν βάσανον·». Both negatives are on the plate, and the plate agrees with the file (ταυτὴν is accented as printed).
- **Latin:** *quarum illa (dementia …) non praevideat magnitudinem judiciorum; hic autem (id est contemptus) quam hactenus benignitas mutationem exspectans, cohibet, districtionem poenamque non avertat*.
- **English:** «the former not foreseeing the greatness of the judgements, and the latter not keeping itself from the torment which kindness, awaiting the conversion, for the present holds back;»
- **Verdict:** right. ἐκείνη/ταύτη are taken correctly as the former and the latter: folly (ἄνοια, tied to not fearing God) and contempt (καταφρόνησις, tied to not regarding man). Both negatives are carried. οὐκ εἰργομένην with an accusative βάσανον reads naturally as "not restrained / not keeping itself away (as to) the torment". Either way contempt does not shrink from the punishment, and the next clause («αἷς ἑαυτὸν αὐθαδῶς περιέπειρε», «with which it wilfully pierced itself through») confirms that sense. «not deterred by the torment» would be an equally good rendering, but the polarity is the same, so no change is needed.

### Case A. p0712.9, col. 1397 A (οὐ μὴ ποιήσῃ τὴν ἐκδίκησιν): KEEP, a question, and the polarity is right
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 712, Greek column, lines 8–12):** «… Χριστὸς, *Ὁ δὲ Θεὸς οὐ μὴ ποιήσῃ*, φησὶ, *τὴν ἐκδίκησιν ὑμῶν*, δι᾿ ἐμοῦ σαρκωθέντος, καὶ τὴν τοῦ καθαιρέτου τῆς φύσεως ποιουμένου καθαίρεσιν;». The plate sets the quoted words in italic, with φησὶ in roman between them, and it prints the Greek question mark (;) after καθαίρεσιν, closing the sentence.
- **Latin:** *Deus, inquit, non faciet vestri vindictam: per me scilicet, …*. A statement, which as English would make Christ deny that God vindicates: a reversal.
- **English:** «*And shall God not make the vindication of you*, [n: Luc. xviii, 7.] through me who was incarnate and who works the destruction of the destroyer of nature?»
- **Verdict:** keep. The plate's ; and Luke 18:7 (ὁ δὲ θεὸς οὐ μὴ ποιήσῃ τὴν ἐκδίκησιν …;) both make this a rhetorical question expecting "yes". Rendered as a question, «shall God not …?», it asserts that God surely will vindicate, through the incarnate Son, which is what the whole exposition is building toward. The Latin's statement reverses it, and the English rightly does not follow the Latin. The English also matches the plate's italics, which cover exactly the quoted words.

### Case B. p0712.18–20, col. 1397 B (Son and Father, greater and less): KEEP
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 712, Greek column, lines 17–22):** «τὸ μὲν, ὡς ἀσύγκριτον· οὐ γὰρ μείων μὲν Υἱοῦ· μείζων δὲ Πατρὸς, ἡ ἐκδίκησις· ἢ δι᾿ ἄλλου, καὶ ἄλλη τὸ σύνολον, πλὴν τοῦ Υἱοῦ. Καὶ πῶς ἐπὶ τῆς αὐτῆς συγκριτικῶς φησι, τὸ, *Ὁ δὲ Θεὸς*, φησὶν, *οὐ ποιήσει τὴν ἐκδίκησιν ὑμῶν*;». The plate agrees with the file, and there is no Greek behind the Latin's *aut per Filium* (as note 21 says).
- **Latin:** *non enim minor quidem Filii, major autem Patris vindicta est; aut per alium, atque alia prorsus, quam Filii, aut per Filium.*
- **English:** «for the vindication is not less of the Son and greater of the Father, or through another, and wholly another, besides the Son.»
- **Verdict:** right. The single οὐ governs the whole μέν/δέ pair and then the ἢ-member, and the English «is not … or …» carries the negative over all three, which in English reads as "neither … nor". The genitives are assigned correctly: the vindication would be *less* when the Son's and *greater* when the Father's, which is exactly what Maximus denies. ἄλλη (feminine) is rightly taken with the vindication («wholly another»). πλὴν τοῦ Υἱοῦ, «besides the Son», keeps the sense "not through anyone other than the Son". Nothing is reversed or flipped.
- The next sentence (s3) is kept a question with the plate's ;. The future indicative οὐ ποιήσει (not οὐ μὴ ποιήσῃ as in col. 1397 A) sits inside the πῶς question, and the English «*And shall God not*, it says, *make the vindication of you?*» renders the quoted comparison as Luke's rhetorical question, which the word συγκριτικῶς requires. Keep.

### Case D. p0713.18–20, col. 1400 B (εἰ μή τι ἄλλο … τὸ μηδαμῶς ἀντιτείνειν): KEEP
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 713, Greek column, lines 16–21):** «… τῷ ἑτοίμῳ τῆς παραδοχῆς τὸ πρόθυμον τῆς πληγῆς ἀναστέλλοντα, αἰδομένου τυχὸν, εἰ μή τι ἄλλο, τὸ ὑπερβάλλον τῆς ὑποπτώσεως· καὶ τὸ μηδαμῶς ἀντιτείνεν τὸν δυνάμενον τοῦτο ποιεῖν, εἱργόμενον δὲ διὰ τὴν πρόσαξιν.». The plate prints the non-words ἀντιτείνεν and πρόσαξιν exactly as the file does. These are Migne's misprints (for ἀντιτείνειν and πρόσταξιν), and the English reads their sense («resist», «command»), which is right under the brief.
- **Latin:** *… quod … non resistat, cujus id facultatis sit, ac cujus imperio …*.
- **English:** «the striker perhaps feeling shame at, if nothing else, the excess of the submission, and that one who is able to do this does not resist at all, but is restrained because of the command.»
- **Verdict:** right. The genitive absolute αἰδομένου goes with τοῦ πλήττοντος, so it is the *striker* who is shamed, and the English names him. He is shamed by two things: (1) the excess of the submission, and (2) the fact that the man struck, though able to do this (resist), in no way resists, being restrained by the command. τὸν δυνάμενον is the subject of the articular infinitive, which is the struck man, and the English keeps him distinct from the striker. μηδαμῶς («does not resist at all») and εἰ μή τι ἄλλο («if nothing else») are both carried.

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **col. 1396 B, τῆς κατοικίας τὴν παροικίαν ἠλλάξατο → «it exchanged its sojourning for settled dwelling».** With the middle voice ἀλλάσσομαι + acc. + gen. can mean "take the acc. in exchange for the gen.", which would give "took sojourning in exchange for settled dwelling", the opposite direction. Rejected: the context (reasoning *resting* in sense and the visible world κατὰ σχέσιν, which is its "city") requires the sojourn to have been turned into a home. The Latin reads the same way (*habitatione … incolatum mutavit*). The English's direction is the sense. (Plate-read p. 711: the text is as printed.)
2. **col. 1397 B, s4 ἢ διάφορος πάντως δειχθήσεται → «or else it will be shown altogether different».** The Latin has the masculine *diversus* (the Son), and I first suspected a who-is-inferior blur. Rejected: διάφορος has two endings, and the feminine ἄλλη (the vindication) directly before makes "it" (the vindication) a good antecedent. Either way the English keeps that some nature «cannot do the like» and only «images … the one that excels», so the Christological polarity is intact and not reversed.
3. **col. 1397 A, Εἶτα … ἀγάγοι → «until it lead the soul to near-kinship with reason itself».** «the soul» is supplied without a ⟨ ⟩ mark (the Greek gives no object). Rejected as a finding: the supply suits the allegory (the widow-soul who lacks the sowing λόγος is led to kinship with it), and the Latin *provexerit* also needs an object. A supplied object is not a reversal.
4. **col. 1397 C, ἐξ Υἱοῦ → «the provider of all virtue and life, from a Son».** Bare English might suggest that the Father is "from" a Son. The Greek is just as bare, though (it is "known from / through the Son"; Latin *per Filium*), and nothing is reversed. Not reported.
5. **col. 1400 B, δωρούμενον / ἀναστέλλοντα attached to the Lord** («who thence bestows … checking»). ἀναστέλλοντα could be a neuter plural with ταῦτα τελούμενα (the bodily acts check the blow). Both participles can be masculine accusative singular with Κύριον, and the Latin takes the Lord as the subject of *tribuit*. The ambiguity is not a reversal.
6. **col. 1397 C, ὑποπιάζειν** (with omicron, where Luke 18:5 has ὑπωπιάζῃ and the Latin column ὑπωπιάζειν). Plate-read p. 712: Migne prints ὑποπιά-ζειν, and the English carries the printed form. Not a finding.
7. **Apparatus.** The English sets [n: Luc. xviii, 6.] on the title and carries no note for Matt 5:39 or John 20:17 (the plate apparatus under p. 710 prints three notes, the last «Joan. xx, 27»). Outside the reversal class and the subject of the brief's ruling, so not reported.
