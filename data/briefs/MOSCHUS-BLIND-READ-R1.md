### F1. Chunk 0003, col 2873 (band A), ch. 26 — "does not glorify God rightly"
- **Class:** 5 (wrong term/sense; theological point of the story inverted toward worship rather than belief)
- **Greek (file only):** `Λέγω γάρ σοι, ὅτι ἐὰν πάσας τὰς ἀρετὰς ποιήσει ἄνθρωπος, καὶ μὴ ὀρθῶς δοξάζει, εἰς τὸν τόπον τοῦτον ἔρχεται.`
- **Latin:** `si omnes virtutes homo operetur, nec recte credat, in hoc loco cruciabitur.`
- **English as it stands:** `if a man does all the virtues and does not glorify God rightly, he comes to this place.`
- **Proposed English:** `if a man does all the virtues and does not believe rightly, he comes to this place.`
- **Why:** ὀρθῶς δοξάζειν is "to hold right opinion" (the verb behind ὀρθοδοξία), and the whole chapter is about right belief (Nestorius, the Theotokos, s6 τὸ ὀρθῶς φρονεῖν). "God" is not in the Greek; "glorify" shifts the condition from doctrine to worship. The Latin witness reads the same way (`recte credat`).
- **Confidence:** probable (certain that "God" is supplied; "believe/hold the right faith" is the standard sense).
- **Already in the cruces?** (see "Post-check" below)

### F2. Chunk 0003, col 2877 (band B/C), ch. 30 — "accusing myself idly"
- **Class:** 4 (word added without marking)
- **Greek (file only):** `Εἰ δὲ νομίζετε ὅτι ἐμαυτοῦ κατηγορῶ, ἀκούσατέ μου τῆς ἁμαρτίας`
- **English as it stands:** `But if you think that I am accusing myself idly, hear my sin`
- **Proposed English:** `But if you think that I am [merely] accusing myself, hear my sin` (or, unmarked: `But if you think that I am only accusing myself, hear my sin`). Minimal option: drop "idly".
- **Why:** "idly" has no word behind it in the Greek. The implied sense (that he is exaggerating) is fair, but under Tier-2 a supplied word should be bracketed or omitted. Low stakes; it does not reverse anything.
- **Confidence:** certain that the word is supplied; low importance.
- **Already in the cruces?** (see "Post-check")

### F3. Chunk 0005, col 2885 (band D; text runs on to 2888 A), ch. 37 — "it was through him"
- **Class:** 5 / Pattern 16 (English follows Migne's variant and Lipomanus's Latin, not the printed Greek)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0057, left column foot):** `Διὰ τοῦτο⁸¹ οὖν τὰ κτίσματα τὰ δημόσια ἀνῳκοδομοῦντο` — note 81 at the foot: `al. δι' αὐτοῦ et διὰ τούτου.` The file agrees with the plate.
- **Latin:** `ac per ipsum aedificia publica reparabantur`
- **English as it stands:** `So it was through him that the [2888] public buildings were being rebuilt`
- **Proposed English:** `For this reason, then, the [2888] public buildings were being rebuilt`
- **Why:** The text Migne prints is διὰ τοῦτο ("for this reason / so"), which ties the rebuilding to Ephraim being merciful. "Through him" is the reading of the variant δι' αὐτοῦ (and of the Latin `per ipsum`). The variant stands in `[n:]` and should not replace the text. A small change, but it is the printed reading swapped for a witness.
- **Confidence:** certain (plate-read).
- **Already in the cruces?** (see "Post-check")

### F4. Chunk 0005, col 2893 (band C), ch. 40 — the two swords: Greek file drops Ἰδού and has note 18 in the wrong place; English adds "here are"
- **Class:** 4 (text missing in the Greek file; one word added in the English)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0061, left column, band C):** the plate prints `Καὶ αὐτοὶ εἶπον¹⁸· Ἰδοὺ δύο μάχαιραι.` The file has `Καὶ αὐτοὶ εἶπον· δύο μάχαιραι.` (Ἰδοὺ lost). Note 18 at the foot reads `al. τῶν δὲ εἰπόντων.` and is keyed to **this** εἶπον. The file instead attaches `[n: al. τῶν δὲ εἰπόντων.]` to the εἶπον of s10 (`ἀπῆλθον, καὶ εἶπον … ἅπαντα τῷ πατριάρχῃ`), which carries no note mark on the plate. I checked that line: notes 14 and 15 bracket it with nothing between them.
- **Latin:** `Illisque dicentibus: Ecce gladii duo hic`
- **English as it stands:** `And they said, "See, here are two swords."`
- **Proposed English:** `And they said, "See, two swords."` The English "See" is right against the plate. "here are" supplies Luke 22:38's ὧδε, which this plate does not print ("Scripture: translate what Migne's Greek prints"). If an English verb is wanted, `"See, two swords [here]."` or leave it as printed.
- **Why:** The Greek file departs from the plate in two ways (a lost word, a misplaced variant note). Neither reverses the sense, but the Greek file is what ships beside the English. The English "here" is the familiar verse leaking in, probably from the Latin `hic`.
- **Confidence:** certain on the plate facts; low stakes for the English.

Recommend reverting the English and moving the "through him" reading into a crux line.

## Candidates considered and rejected

1. **0001, 2856, ch. 3**: `Παρεβάλομεν ἐν τῇ λαύρᾳ … Σάβα, τῷ Ἀθανασίῳ` → "We visited the laura of … Sabas, and abba Athanasius." The dative is the person visited *at* the laura, so "and" slightly suggests two separate visits. The Latin agrees on the person (`profecti fuissemus ad abbatem Athanasium`). Nothing reverses, and the speaker who follows is still Athanasius. Rejected as style. A tighter version would be "We visited abba Athanasius at the laura of…".
2. **0001, 2856C, ch. 4**: printed `ἀπελθὼν ἐκάθισεν` (3rd person) inside Leontius's 1st-person tale; the English has "I went and settled". Logged in the cruces plate table (`p0042.27`). The speaker is the same either way. Rejected.
3. **0001, 2853C, ch. 2**: `πλήρης ἦν τῆς θείας χάριτος τοῦ Θεοῦ ἄνθρωπος` → "full of divine grace was the man of God". This moves τοῦ Θεοῦ from "grace" to "man". It is trivial and changes nothing in the story. Rejected.
4. **0001, 2860B, ch. 10**: `ὅσον ἔξω πάσχει ἄνθρωπος, τοσοῦτον ὁ ἔξω θάλλει` → English "inner man". Plate-read: Migne prints ἔξω (a misprint the sentence demands correcting, and the Latin has `interior`). Logged (`p0044.27`). The English correctly follows the sense. Rejected.
5. **0002, 2861A, ch. 11**: `ἀντὶ τῶν ἀρχόντων ὑπ' αὐτοῦ` → "in place of those taken up by him". Plate-read: Migne prints ἀρχόντων. As printed, "in place of the rulers by him" does not construe, and ἀρθέντων matches the Latin and Josh 4:9. Logged. Rejected.
6. **0002, 2868B, ch. 20**: `Διηγήσατό μοί τις τῶν Πατέρων, ὅτι δρακονάρις τις διηγήσατό μοι` → "a dragonarius told him". The second μοι belongs to the Father's speech (ὅτι recitative), so turning it into indirect speech as "him" is correct. Rejected.
7. **0002, 2872, ch. 25**: προσκομιδή → "the prayer of oblation". The fixed term "oblation" is kept; "prayer of" makes plain that what the brother learned and recited is a text. Rejected.
8. **0002, 2864–65, ch. 17**: `μετελάμβανεν δὲ τρίτον τῆς ἑβδομάδος` → "three times a week". This could also be read "on the third day of the week", but "thrice" is the normal sense of τρίτον. Rejected.
9. **0003, 2877A, ch. 29**: `ἐδόκει κατακρίνειν αὐτόν` → "he seemed to be getting the better of him". κατακρίνειν strictly means "condemn". The English is a free rendering, but the sense (the heretic was winning the argument) holds, and the Greek may be a misprint for κατακρατεῖν. Borderline. Rejected, but a stricter English would be "he seemed to be condemning him".
10. **0003, 2881A, ch. 32**: `Ὄντως οὐκ ἐᾷς` → "Truly thou dost not leave us so". The form is odd (one might expect "we will not let thee"), but the English keeps the 2nd singular as printed. Rejected.
11. **0005, 2893, ch. 40**: who says "I visited this abba Cosmas at the laura of Pharon; for I stayed in it ten years"? The Greek has no quotation marks. The English assigns it to the narrator, outside Basil's quotation. That is defensible (Moschus's own ten years at Pharan) and not a reversal. Rejected.
12. **0005, 2892–93, ch. 39–40**: every negative was checked (οὔτε σήμερον οὔτε αὔριον, Οὔ ×2, οὐ ψεύδομαι, μὴ ἐξέλθῃ, οὐδὲν βλάπτεται, ἵνα μὴ ἔχωμεν αὐτὸν ὡς ὀρθόδοξον, οὐχ εὕρισκον, μὴ ἔχεις χαρτία, μηδὲ ὅλως καθεζόμενος). All are carried. No finding.
