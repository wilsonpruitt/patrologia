## Findings

### F1. 0006, col 2901 C/D (Chapter 47 opening): city name supplied from the Latin
- **Class:** 4 (added text)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0065):** `Φοινίκης τῆς Λιβανησίας πόλις ἐστίν· ἐν ταύτῃ μῖμος ὑπῆρχεν…`. The plate names no city. The line directly under the Latin chapter title begins `Φοινίκης`.
- **Latin (0006 twin, l. 203):** `Heiopolis Phoenices Libanensis civitas est.`
- **English as it stands:** `Heliopolis is a city of Phoenice Libanensis.`
- **Proposed English:** `There is a city of Phoenice Libanensis [lat: the Latin names it, *Heiopolis* (Heliopolis)].`
- **Why:** The name comes only from Lipomanus. The English gives it as Greek text with no ⟨⟩ and no `[lat:]`. Either the city is left unnamed, as the Greek prints it, or the supply is disclosed.
- **Confidence:** certain (the plate has no name); the remedy is the editor's call
- **Already in the cruces?** No (checked after writing). The only hit is the pass-2 OCR table, line 771 (`p0065.37 | Φοινίκης τῆς Λιβανησίας | Ἡλιούπολις … | pass2: Ἡλιούπολις not printed`). That line confirms the plate omits the name, but no translator crux records that the English supplies it.

### N1. 0007, col 2916 A (Chapter 62): a word dropped from the Greek file. The English is right
- **Not an English finding.** The Greek file reads `Οὐκ ἀκούω σε [n: al. σοῦ.]. σε τίς εἶ.` The plate (p-0072, plate-read) prints `Οὐκ ἀκούω σε ⁸⁹. Οἶδά σε τίς εἶ.` The file has lost `Οἶδά`. The English, "I know thee, who thou art", follows the plate. The Greek file should be patched at the next text pass.

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **0006 col 2897 B (ch. 43), `ψῆφον πάλιν κατέκρινεν, ὡς μὴ ἀρέσκουσαν αὐτῷ`.** It is unclear whose vote this is and whom it pleases. The translator has already flagged it `[?]`, and the English keeps the Greek's ambiguity without reversing it.
2. **0006 col 2905 C (ch. 50), `ἰσταμένου ἐν τῇ θυρίδι`.** The genitive absolute has no pronoun. "As I was standing at the window" is a defensible reading: Anastasius is speaking, and `λέγειν μοι` follows.
3. **0006 ch. 49 s6, `τῷ δὲ παρεστῶτι σταυροφύλακι … λέγουσιν αὐτῷ`.** The Greek has the cross-warden in the dative but makes him the subject of λέγουσιν. Rendering him as subject is the only sense the sentence allows. Not a flip.
4. **0007 col 2908 D (ch. 54) and 0008 ch. 71, `Φύσει` rendered "Truly" (plate-read: `Φύσει` is printed).** It is used the same way at both sites as an affirming particle, and Lipomanus has *revera*. Consistent and defensible.
5. **0007 ch. 56 s7, `οὐκ εὐλόγησάς με … καὶ ἐνετείλω` rendered "nor bid me eat".** Carrying the negative into the second verb is what the sense needs: the disciple is excusing himself for not eating. Not a polarity error.
6. **0007 ch. 56 s14, `σφραγίσαντος τὸ πάθος` rendered "sealed the ailment with the sign of the cross".** A small gloss, but it is the standard sense of σφραγίζω. Not reported.
7. **0008 col 2917 B (ch. 66), `Ὡς οὖν εἰσῆλθεν εἰς τὸ σκάμμα` (3rd singular) rendered "when I went into the arena".** Theodosius is narrating in the first person and the next verb is `ἠρξάμεθα`. The Latin has *ingressus … sum*. Probably Migne's misprint for εἰσῆλθον; the English follows the sense. Not plate-read, since the reading does not change the story.
8. **0008 ch. 66 s6, `εἴσελθε οὖν` with no change of speaker marked.** The English starts a new speech for the young man and flags it `[?]`. The Latin makes the same speaker change explicit (*Ait autem ad me juvenis*). Correct handling.
9. **0008 ch. 67, `διὰ ψήφου ἐδήλου` rendered "by signs".** Literally "by a pebble or token". The Latin has *signo*. Acceptable.
10. **0009 ch. 73, `«Κύριε, ἐκ τῶν κρυφίων μου καθάρισόν με,» ἵνα μὴ αἰσχύνωμαι προσευχόμενος`.** The English puts the ἵνα clause inside the quotation, while Migne's guillemets close before it. The clause is first person, so it is still John's words. This is a quotation-boundary call for the editor, not a change of sense.
11. **0009 ch. 76 nested quotation marks (Palladius › shipmaster › woman › soldier).** Heavy, but who says what is right throughout, including the φησίν "he said" asides.
12. **0010 ch. 79 s12, `οὐχ οἱ δύο, οἱ τρεῖς`.** Already flagged `[?]`, and the sense is kept.
