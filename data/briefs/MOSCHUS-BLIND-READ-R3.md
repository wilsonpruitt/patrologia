## Findings

### F1. 0011, col. 2941 [b: D] (ch. 85) — class 1 (negative left attached to the wrong thing)
- **Greek (file only):** Βλέπετε, ἐντολή ἐστιν τοῦ [b: D] γέροντος, οὐ συμφέρει ἡμῖν.
- **Latin:** *Videte, mandatum senis est, non expedit nobis illud transgredi* (confirms the sense; the Greek leaves the infinitive understood).
- **English as it stands:** `Look, it is the commandment of the elder; it does not profit us.`
- **Proposed:** `Look, it is the commandment of the elder; it is not to our profit [to break it].` (or, without supplied words: `Look, it is the commandment of the elder: it is not expedient for us.`)
- **Why:** the abba is arguing FOR keeping the dole. As set, "it does not profit us" reads as the abba saying the elder's commandment is unprofitable, i.e. the brothers' own argument. The οὐ negates the advantage of cutting the blessing off (Μὴ κόψωμεν, just before), not the commandment.
- **Confidence:** probable (the English is a literal rendering, but in English it lands on the wrong antecedent).
- **Already in the cruces?** see end-of-report check.

### F2. 0012, col. 2948 [b: A] (ch. 90, opening) — class 5 (place: English follows Migne's variant, not his text)
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 88, right column, first line of CAPUT XC):** Δύο ἀναχωρηταὶ ἦσαν ἐπάνω ὄρους Σινᾶ [n: al. Ῥωσσοῦ.] — the plate prints Σινᾶ in the text with note 33 *al.* Ῥωσσοῦ at the foot.
- **Latin:** *Duo anachoretae erant supra Rosum in monte* (follows the variant).
- **English as it stands:** `There were two anchorites above Rhossus [?], on the mountain called Pterygion`
- **Proposed:** `There were two anchorites above Mount Sinai [?], on the mountain called Pterygion` — with the crux recording that the setting (Pterygion, the Piapi, the Scopelos monastery near Rhossus) makes Migne's *al.* Ῥωσσοῦ the evident sense. If the editorial decision is to keep Rhossus, the crux must say the English adopts the variant against the printed text.
- **Why:** the English silently prints the foot-note reading in place of the text reading; the `[?]` flags doubt but does not tell the reader the printed Greek says Sinai. Not a reversal; it is a place swapped. Report so the choice is explicit.
- **Confidence:** certain that the English departs from the printed text; the remedy is an editorial call.
- **Already in the cruces?** see end-of-report check.

### F3. 0012, col. 2953 [b: D] (ch. 96) — class 3 (tense: present request rendered as past)
- **Greek (file only):** Ἀλλὰ δήλωσόν μοι, Πάτερ, τί ὤφειλον ποιῆσαι περὶ τοῦ πορνεύσαντος ἀδελφοῦ
- **Latin:** *Indica mihi, Pater, quid agere debeam de fratre, qui fornicatus est*
- **English as it stands:** `But show me, father, what I ought to have done about the brother who committed fornication`
- **Proposed:** `But show me, father, what I ought to do about the brother who committed fornication`
- **Why:** late-Greek ὤφειλον + inf. is the ordinary "what should I do"; Julian is asking for guidance on whether to keep communion now, and Symeon answers with present commands (Μὴ ἀναχωρήσῃς). "ought to have done" turns it into a retrospective judgment on a past act.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** see end-of-report check.

### F4. 0013, col. 2957 [b: D] (ch. 99 second story, Theodore of Rhossus) — class 5 (proper name varied)
- **Greek (file only):** Θεόδωρος ὁ γεγονὼς ἐπίσκοπος Ῥόσσου [n: al. Ῥωσσοῦ.]

- **Confidence:** certain (consistency).

### F5. 0015, col. 2973 [b: D] / 2976 [b: A] (ch. 110, last saying) — class 2 (speaker of the embedded question)
- **Greek (file only):** Γενοῦ θυρωρὸς τῆς καρδίας σου, ἵνα μὴ εἰσέλθῃ τις ξένος, λέγων· Ἡμέτερος εἶ ἢ τῶν ὑπεναντίων;
- **Latin:** *Esto janitor cordis tui, ne intret peregrinus, dicens: Noster es, an adversariorum?* (as ambiguous as the Greek).
- **English as it stands:** `Be the doorkeeper of thy heart, so that no stranger enters, saying, 'Art thou ours, or of the adversaries?'`
- **Proposed:** `Be the doorkeeper of thy heart, so that no stranger enters: say, 'Art thou ours, or of the adversaries?'` (or keep the participle and move it: `Be the doorkeeper of thy heart, saying, 'Art thou ours, or of the adversaries?', so that no stranger enters.`)
- **Why:** the question is Joshua's challenge to the stranger (Josh 5:13, Ἡμέτερος εἶ ἢ τῶν ὑπεναντίων;), so it is the doorkeeper (the subject of Γενοῦ) who asks it. Set straight after "no stranger enters", the English makes the stranger the one asking, which inverts the image.
- **Confidence:** probable (the Greek participle can grammatically take either; the scriptural source and the doorkeeper's role decide it).
- **Already in the cruces?** see end-of-report check.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- 0011 ch. 80 οὐκ ἔτι κατὰ φύσιν → "It is not by nature": strictly "no longer by nature", but the sense (the spring is God-given, not natural) is unchanged.
- 0011 ch. 80 ἡ τοιαύτη καὶ τηλικαύτη θεοδώρητος πηγή → "that great God-given spring, such as it was": "such as it was" is a little deprecatory for an intensive pair ("so fine and so great"); a style point, not a reversal. Worth a look by the editor.
- 0011 ch. 81 Ἔφερον οὖν ἡμῖν οἱ διασώσαντες τὴν εἰκόνα ἐκ τοῦ ὕδατος: English takes ἐκ τοῦ ὕδατος with διασώσαντες and supplies "some of it" for what was brought; both readings give the story (they drank the water).
- 0011 ch. 83 ὁ Θεὸς ἔχει βοηθῆσαι → "God can help": ἔχει + inf. is closer to "will help"; not a sense change.
- 0011 ch. 85 Οὐκ ἀπαντᾷ τοῦτο ἡμῖν δοῦναι, οὐδὲ παρέχομεν → "nor do we have it to give": the Latin (*Non suppetit nobis quod dare possimus*) reads inability too; no reversal.
- 0011 ch. 85 φαλερῶν εʹ → "five obols"; 0015 ch. 111 φολερά / φόλεις → "obols": the coin is the *follis*; "obols" is a consistent equivalent across the range, not a story change. Possibly a fixed-term question for the editor.
- 0011 ch. 85 οὗτος ὁ σῖτος ἐβλάστησεν → "all the wheat had sprouted": "all" slightly heightens οὗτος; the story requires the whole store lost (5,000 modii), so not reported.
- 0011 ch. 89 φύσει → "Truly": idiomatic ("in fact"), acceptable.
- 0012 ch. 92 Ἤμελλον οἰκοδομεῖν → "I was about to build": 1 sg. vs 3 pl. ambiguous; the abba is the builder throughout, so fine.
- 0012 ch. 91 διὰ τῶν ὑμετέρων εὐχῶν → "your prayers" to a single elder: the Greek plural possessive is printed; under the thou/you rule "your" correctly reflects it.
- 0013 ch. 97 πᾶσαν ἡμέραν ἐνήστευσε → "fasted all day": could be "every day"; the next sentence (καθ᾿ ἑκάστην) carries the daily sense either way. Not reported.
- 0013 ch. 97 who dies in the cave (προκόψας… ἀπεδήμησεν = the fallen brother; ὁ ἀδελφός who stays = the faithful one): English keeps it right.
- 0014 ch. 107 κρούων τὴν κεφαλὴν… ὠρυόμενος: belongs to the lion, and the English "its head" keeps it so.
- 0014 ch. 108 τῇ νυκτὶ τῆς δευτέρας / πρωῒ τῇ δευτέρᾳ → "the second day": in Byzantine usage this is Monday (the story runs Sunday → Monday). Literal and not wrong, but "Monday" would be clearer; editor's call.
- 0015 ch. 109 τὴν γλῶσσαν [n: Lat. γῆν.] κατεφώτισεν → "lit up the earth": the English follows Migne's note against the printed word. The printed γλῶσσαν repeats the word from the previous clause and gives no sense with "sunbeam", so this is the misprint case the brief allows (English follows the sense). It should be in the cruces.
- 0015 ch. 112 Πίστευσον ἀββᾶ, λέγων → "Believe it, abba Leo": λέγων for Λέων is an evident misprint; English follows the sense.
