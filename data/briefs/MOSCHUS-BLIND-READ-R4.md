## Findings

### F1. Chunk 0017, col 2993 band C (ch. 129) — the stylite's country: σου rendered "my"
- **Class:** 2 (whose; pronoun person changed)
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 111):** ὅτι Ἐν τῇ χώρᾳ σου Στυλίτης ἦν — plate prints σου clearly.
- **Latin:** quod in provincia sua fuerit Stylites quidam (indirect; "his own province").
- **English as it stands:** "In my country there was a stylite,"
- **Proposed English:** "In thy country there was a stylite,"  (plus a crux: σου perhaps for μου, cf. Lat. *sua*)
- **Why:** The plate prints σου ("thy"). The English silently turned it into "my". Athenogenes may be speaking to Athanasius, so "thy country" can stand. Even if σου is Migne's slip for μου, the repair should be disclosed in a crux, not made silently.
- **Confidence:** probable (certain that the English departs from the plate; the repair may still be right in sense).
- **Already in the cruces?** Partly. The OCR table logs `p0111.23 | χώρᾳ σου | χώρᾳ μου | ocr | migne | plate σου`, so the repair is recorded, but it is classed `ocr` although the plate itself prints σου. No running crux at 2993 explains the choice. σου/μου is not an itacism, and "thy country" construes if Athenogenes is speaking to Athanasius. My view is that the English should follow the plate, or the crux should argue for the repair.

### F2. Chunk 0017, col 2996 band C (ch. 131) — the Greek's closing ellipsis dropped
- **Class:** 4 (missing text signal; Pattern 8 punctuation)
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 112):** ἀλλὰ καὶ μετὰ δύο ἡμέρας παύει τὸ θανατικὸν ἀπὸ Καισαρείας . . .  (the file has `Καισαρείας..`)
- **Latin:** continues *Quod et factum est juxta verbum senis. Et ista quidem Procopius … enarravit nobis.*
- **English as it stands:** "what is more, after two days the plague ceases from Caesarea.'"
- **Proposed English:** "what is more, after two days the plague ceases from Caesarea . . ."  (the quotation left open as the plate leaves it, or the editor's points kept before the close)
- **Why:** Migne's points mark that the Greek breaks off there while the Latin goes on. The English closes the speech cleanly, so the reader cannot tell that the Greek is truncated. (Same Greek page: in s5–6 the Greek also omits the Latin's "found him in the atrium of St Mary". The plate confirms the Greek as printed, and the English rightly follows it: not a finding.)
- **Confidence:** probable

### F3. Chunk 0020, col 3024 band A (ch. 154): "nearly fifteen years" (ἐγγὺς used twice)
- **Class:** 5 (number qualified, a word made to do double duty)
- **Greek (file only):** καὶ ἐγγὺς αὐτοῦ ἔχων ἔτη πεντεκαίδεκα, νύκτα καὶ ἡμέραν ἐργαζόμενος
- **Latin:** *cum quo mansi annis quindecim* (no "nearly")
- **English as it stands:** "and I have been with him nearly fifteen years,"
- **Proposed English:** "and I have been with him fifteen years,"
- **Why:** ἐγγὺς αὐτοῦ is one phrase, "near him / in his service". The English renders it as "with him" and then again as "nearly" on the number. The Greek gives no approximation, and the Latin agrees.
- **Confidence:** probable

## Candidates considered and rejected
- 0016 ch. 116 s11 Ὡς οὖν οὐκ ἔπεισέ με, "since he could not persuade me": correct (the elder fails to persuade Andrew).
- 0016 ch. 116: the Greek ends ἐγὼ γὰρ αὐτὸ ἔλαβον with no full stop. The Latin ends at the same point (*ego enim illud cepi*), so nothing is lost. Not opened at the plate.
- 0016 ch. 117 s5 εὐθέως θεραπεύει, "at once thou art healed": θεραπεύει is more naturally active 3rd person ("he heals thee"), and the Latin has *statim curabit te*. The English takes it as the 2nd-person passive form (-ει = -ῃ). That reading is possible and it does not reverse the story. Not reported; a translator may want to reconsider.
- 0016 ch. 118 s10 "his eye came out": ἐξῆλθεν ὁ ὀφθαλμὸς αὐτοῦ, and the Latin agrees (*exiit oculus ejus unus*). Kept.
- 0016 ch. 119 ἐκ τρίτου ("a third time") after only one earlier "Pray": the plate (p. 106) prints it so, and the English follows. Not a finding.
- 0016 ch. 123 s6 ὕπαγε σὺ τῶν ἔνθεν: already marked [?] and logged.
- 0017 ch. 127 s7 Μετὰ εὐχὰς ἡμῶν rendered "By your prayers": the plate prints ἡμῶν (p. 109 crop), but it is an ordinary ἡμ-/ὑμ- itacism and the Latin has *Per orationes vestras*. The OCR table logs it (p0109.08). The English follows the sense. Rejected.
- 0017 ch. 127 s5, who gives Communion: the Greek is ambiguous, the Latin makes it the bishop, and the English "he gives him" keeps the ambiguity. OK.
- 0017 ch. 127 s27 ἔχει δὲ χήρα περὶ τὰ ὀγδοήκοντα ἔτη, "a widow of about eighty years": the Latin *vidua quasi annorum octoginta* supports it. OK.
- 0017 ch. 131 οὐχ εὗρον αὐτόν followed by "and I tell him": the Greek as printed is compressed against the Latin. The plate (p. 112) confirms the Greek, and running crux 2996B covers it.
- 0018 ch. 134 Οὕπως, "No [?]": flagged and logged. (οὔπω, "not yet", is also a possible sense; that is a crux matter, not a finding.)
- 0018 φόλλεις, "obols": rendered "obols" consistently across the work (10 instances), so it is a convention. Not reported.
- 0019 sense repairs ἐρήμου πόλεως→Hermopolis, ἀσθενεῖς→"licentious", ναῷ→"ninth milestone", ἔνθεν→"inspired": all four are logged in the OCR table and/or running cruces, each with Latin support. Rejected.
- 0019 ch. 144 s13 μήτε βλέποντες…τῶν ἔμπροσθεν ἐπιλανθανώμεθα, "nor forgetting the things ahead": a single μήτε governing both members is a fair construal. It inverts Phil 3:13, but the Greek as printed says it. Kept.
- 0020 ch. 150 s16 μηκέτι κατὰ τὴν ἁρπαγὴν νοῆσαί τι, "never again to do anything in haste": νοῆσαι is "to think/judge" rather than "do", but the Latin has *agere*, and the sense (no more hasty action) holds. Not reported.
- 0020 ch. 152 s8 ἀλλ᾿ οὐχ οὕτως τούτους λυπεῖ ὡς τὸ Ψαλτήριον, "but nothing grieves them like the Psalter": the subject is recast, but the comparison and its polarity are kept.
- 0020 Ps 70:9 αἰσχύνην, "strength": supported by Migne's own note (i) and logged at 3020. Rejected.
