## Findings

### F1. 0021, col. 3024 B (ch. 155) — place name normalized from the Latin
- **Class:** 5 (name/place)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0126):** `πλησίον τοῦ Ἀννωνὰ καὶ τοῦ Αἰδονὰ`
- **Latin:** `prope Arnonem et Aidonem`
- **English as it stands:** `near the Arnon and the Aidon`
- **Proposed English:** `near the Annona and the Aidona` (or `the Annon and the Aidon`, keeping the article-plus-indeclinable treatment, but with the Greek's double nu)
- **Why:** the plate clearly prints Ἀννωνὰ; "Arnon" is Lipomanus's form (and the biblical one). Register rule: keep Byzantine forms as printed, never normalized; the Latin is not the text. Story unchanged, so low weight. If the translator meant the river Arnon deliberately as an identification, that belongs in a crux, not the text.
- **Confidence:** probable (that the English departs from the Greek is certain; whether the house wants the identification is an editorial call)
- **Already in the cruces?** see below (checked after)

### F2. 0021, col. 3026 D (ch. 158) — mountain name taken from the Latin

- **Already in the cruces?** see below

### F3. 0023, col. 3044 A (ch. 174) — "they spread" for the Greek's singular
- **Class:** 2 (who does what), minor
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0136):** `Χαλάσατε τὰς διφθέρας, καὶ ὡς ἐφήπλωσεν αὐτὰς, ἰδοὺ νεφέλη`
- **Latin:** `Et cum explicuissent eas` (plural)
- **English as it stands:** `And as they spread them out, behold, a cloud`
- **Proposed English:** `And as he spread them out, behold, a cloud`
- **Why:** ἐφήπλωσεν is 3rd singular on the plate (the subject is the shipmaster, the last singular agent); the plural is Lipomanus's. Story barely affected, but it is the Latin's reading, not the Greek's.
- **Confidence:** probable (a loose singular for the crew is conceivable in this Greek, but the English should not silently follow the Latin)
- **Already in the cruces?** see below

### F4. 0023, col. 3045 C (ch. 176) — the "No" party's argument loses its speaker
- **Class:** 2 (speaker of an embedded speech)
- **Greek (file only; a punctuation/structure point, no reading in doubt):** `οἱ δὲ ἔφασκον, Οὔ. Καὶ γὰρ ὁ θεολόγος Γρηγόριος πάντα ἐξαριθμεῖται τὰ βαπτίσματα … οἶδα καὶ πέμπτον ἔτι τὸ τῶν δακρύων. Ποῖον οὖν τούτων ἐβαπτίσατο, ἵνα καὶ κυρώσωμεν αὐτό; Καὶ μάλιστα τοῦ Κυρίου πρὸς Νικόδημον λέγοντος …`
- **English as it stands:** `but others said, No. ⟦s16+17⟧ For Gregory the Theologian counts up all the baptisms,` … `that of tears.* "So with which of these was he baptized, that we should even ratify it?`
- **Proposed English:** `but others said, "No. ⟦s16+17⟧ For Gregory the Theologian counts up all the baptisms,` … `that of tears.* So with which of these was he baptized, that we should even ratify it?` (i.e. open the quotation at "No" and delete the stray opening mark before "So"; the closing mark after "heaven*." then pairs correctly)
- **Why:** καὶ γάρ … continues the reason given by those who said Οὔ; the first-person κυρώσωμεν confirms the clergy are still speaking. As it stands the Gregory argument reads as Andrew's own narration and the quotation that follows has an opening mark with no speaker. (The marks inside Gregory's citation are an editor's call; this is only about who is talking.)
- **Confidence:** probable
- **Already in the cruces?** see below

### F5. 0024, col. 3052 C (ch. 180) — a whole plate line lost from our Greek, and the English papers over it with `[?]` ⭐ the substantive one
- **Class:** 4 (missing text); our-file defect, not Migne's
- **Greek, plate (p-0140, right column, the line beside the band letter C):** `ἐπειδὴ ὁδὸν μέλλω βαδίσαι μακρὰν, πολλῶν ἡμερῶν | προθεσμίαν ἔχουσαν, τῆς κανδήλας σου φρόντισον, | καὶ σβεννυμένην αὐτὴν κατὰ τὴν ἐμὴν πρόθεσιν φύ|λαξαι`
- **Greek, our file:** `ἐπειδὴ ὁδὸν μέλλω βαδίσαι μακρὰν, πολλῶν ἡμερῶν καὶ σβεννυμένην αὐτὴν κατὰ τὴν ἐμὴν πρόθεσιν φύλαξαι` — the line `προθεσμίαν ἔχουσαν, τῆς κανδήλας σου φρόντισον,` is missing (a dropped OCR line). The file's `[b: C]` then sits one line late, after φύλαξαι.
- **Latin:** `quia longum iter iturus sum diebus multis perficiendum, candelae tuae tu curam habe, eamque ne exstinguatur juxta propositum meum serva`
- **English as it stands:** `'Holy Lady Theotokos, since I am about to walk a long road of many days, [?] and keep it from going out, as I intend;`
- **Proposed English:** `'Holy Lady Theotokos, since I am about to walk a long road that has a term of many days, take care of thy lamp, and keep it from going out, as I intend;`
- **Why:** the translator met a sentence with no object for αὐτήν and no main verb before καί, and flagged it; the plate has the clause. Without it the prayer never mentions the lamp, which is the whole point of the miracle. The Greek file needs the line restored too (outside my edit scope; reported for whoever patches `src/greek/…/0024.md`).
- **Confidence:** certain
- **Already in the cruces?** see below

### F7. 0025, col. 3060 B (ch. 185) — the husband's complaint loses its negative
- **Class:** 1 (polarity)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0144):** `Ἀδελφὴ, οὐκ ὀφείλει παρασχεῖν ἡμῖν ὁ Θεὸς τῶν Χριστιανῶν τί ποτε ἐκ τοῦ χρέους ἐκείνου, ὅτι στενούμεθα.` (full stop on the plate, no question mark)
- **Latin:** `Soror, nihil ut video, daturus est nobis Deus Christianorum ex debito illo, et nunc angustiamur inopia.` (then the wife: `Profecto dabit`)
- **English as it stands:** `'Sister, the God of the Christians ought to give us something out of that debt, for we are in want.'`
- **Proposed English:** `'Sister, the God of the Christians is not going to give us anything out of that debt, though we are in want.'`
- **Why:** οὐκ is dropped. ὀφείλω + infinitive is Moschus's periphrastic future ("is to, is going to": cf. 0023 ch. 174 `ὃς ὥφειλεν εὔξασθαι`, "who was to pray"), and Lipomanus reads it the same way (`nihil … daturus est`). The wife's `Ναὶ` then contradicts his complaint ("Yes, he will"), which is how the story turns. The English turns a pagan's grumble that the Christian God has not paid into a statement that he owes, and it loses the doubt the miracle answers. If the translator read it as a rhetorical question, it must at least keep the negative: `'Sister, is not the God of the Christians to give us something out of that debt? For we are in want.'` Either version is closer than the present text.
- **Confidence:** probable (that the negative is lost is certain; which of the two negative readings to use is the translator's call)
- **Already in the cruces?** see below

## Candidates considered and rejected

- **0021 ch. 163**: the English sets `[b: C]` where the Greek has `[b: D]`, and drops `[b: A]` after [3032]. Many other band letters are also absent from or displaced in the English across the range (for example 0021 p4 and p5, and 0025 [3057]). The brief says band markers are not findings, so I only note them here for whoever runs the marker-parity check.
- **0021 ch. 161** `μὴ γένηταί μοί ποτε` → "may it never be mine". This is odd (the sense is "may it never happen to me"), but it is not reversed.
- **0021 ch. 161** `ζημιοῖ ἑαυτὸν` → "thou harmest thyself". ζημιοῖ can be 2nd singular middle, with ἑαυτόν used for the 2nd person in this Greek, so the reading is defensible.
- **0022 ch. 165** `τῷ μὴ ἀνασχεθῆναι τὰς γυναῖκας ψαῦσαι`: who could not bear to touch whom. The English says the men could not bear to touch the women. The Latin agrees (`uxores … recipere passi non sunt`), and τὰς γυναῖκας as the object of ψαῦσαι is normal in late Greek. Rejected.
- **0022 ch. 167** `Φύσει, τέκνον; Ἐγὼ οὐκ ἐρίγωσα.`: the Greek ; might make "Really, child?" a question, where the English has "Truly, child, I was not cold." The sense is the same either way. Not worth a plate read.
- **0022 ch. 171** `ἀναστάσεως` in a list of virtues ("measures of the resurrection"). It looks odd and could be a misprint for ἀσκήσεως, but nothing in the sentence demands the change. Not reported.
- **0023 ch. 174** `ὃς ὥφειλεν εὔξασθαι`: the English "with his wife, who was to pray" leaves it unclear that the person who was to pray is the scribo (ὅς is masculine). The ambiguity is minor and was not reported.
- **0023 ch. 176** `ὡς ἐπὶ τοῦ Κυρίου, ἀδελφοὶ, οὕτως αὐτὸν ἰάσατο` → "as before the Lord, brethren, so did Christ … heal him". The English is awkward, but it keeps the oath formula.
- **0025 ch. 184** φόλεων → "obols". "Obols" is the rendering of φόλλεις used in every chunk of the work (0007, 0011, 0015, 0018, 0027), so it is a convention, not a finding.
- **0025 ch. 184** `ἔπεμψεν αὐτὸν, καὶ ἐδανείσατο` → "he sent and borrowed". The αὐτόν is dropped, but nothing changes.
- **0025 ch. 185** `ὡς ἐνόμιζεν ἰδεῖν … εἰ μὴ μόνον τοὺς πτωχοὺς`: this was already logged (crux 3060A) and is translated as printed.
