## Findings

### F1. 0026, col 3065 C (ch. 188): "the free woman" for ἐλευθερίαν
- **Class:** 5 (wrong term; English follows the Latin, not the Greek)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0147, left col.):** Οὐκ οἶδας ὅτι ὁ ἀδελφός σου ἀπώλεσεν τὴν ἐλευθε-|ρίαν, καὶ εἰς τὴν τοῦ καπήλου ἐξεκυλίσθη;
- **Latin:** *Nescis quia frater tuus perdidit liberam mulierem, et in uxorem cauponis effusus est?*
- **English as it stands:** `has lost the free woman, and has rolled away to the tavern-keeper's?`
- **Proposed:** `has lost his freedom, and has rolled away to the tavern-keeper's?`
- **Why:** the plate prints ἐλευθερίαν (freedom), not ἐλευθέραν (free woman). "The free woman" is Lipomanus's *liberam mulierem*. If the translator meant to emend to ἐλευθέραν (the allegory of the free woman = the Church, opposite the tavern-keeper's wife = Severus), that is a conjecture and needs a note, not a silent change.
- **Confidence:** probable (the English departs from the printed Greek for certain; whether to emend is an editorial call)
- **Already in the cruces?** YES, checked after: cruces 3065B logs it as a deliberate emendation ("ἐλευθέραν, against printed ἐλευθερίαν; see OCR table"), after the Latin. So this is a logged editorial call, not a silent one. Leave it to the adjudicator whether a Greek-against-print emendation resting on Lipomanus alone should stand; "lost his freedom" construes and fits the allegory too.

### F2. 0026, col 3068 C (ch. 189): vocative δέσποτά μου read as apposition to "the Apostle"
- **Class:** 2 (who is addressed)
- **Greek (file only; unambiguous):** Ἤκουσα, δέσποτά μου, τοῦ Ἀποστόλου λέγοντος, ὅτι ἡ γυνὴ οὐκ ἔχει ἐξουσίαν τοῦ ἰδίου σώματος
- **Latin:** *Audivi, domine, dicentem Apostolum*
- **English as it stands:** `I have heard the Apostle, my master, saying that the wife has not authority`
- **Proposed:** `I have heard, my master, the Apostle saying that the wife has not authority`
- **Why:** δέσποτά μου is vocative to the great personage (as δέσποτα in the next sentence). Set between commas after "the Apostle", it reads as "the Apostle, [who is] my master."
- **Confidence:** probable
- **Already in the cruces?** No (the 3068A entries are on ἐντολήν and the 1 Cor 7:4 citation).

### F3. 0027, col 3069 D (ch. 190): τῷ κατ᾿ αὐτὸν μοναστηρίῳ = "his monastery", not "the monastery beside him"
- **Class:** 5 (wrong sense; whose monastery)
- **Greek (file only):** ἀλλὰ καὶ ἄλλα πολλὰ τῷ κατ᾿ αὐτὸν μοναστηρίῳ ἐποίησεν πράγματα
- **Latin:** *sed et alia plurima ministeria exercuit* (no monastery; no help)
- **English as it stands:** `but also made many other things for the monastery beside him.`
- **Proposed:** `but also made many other things for his monastery.`
- **Why:** ὁ κατ᾿ αὐτόν + noun is the ordinary late-Greek possessive ("his own"). "Beside him" suggests a neighbouring house that is not Brochas's; the story has him build a cell that grows into his own foundation.
- **Confidence:** probable
- **Already in the cruces?** No. Cruces 3069 is keyed to this very sentence but is about the band letter only.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **0026 ch. 186 opening**, τῷ ἀββᾷ Εὐσταθίῳ τῷ ἡγουμένῳ rendered "and abba Eustathius": the dative is loose; nothing changes who tells the story. Not a finding.
- **0026 ch. 186** "making me his deputy": "his" is supplied, but it is harmless.
- **0026 ch. 187** "going from holy Gethsemane to the holy Mount of Olives" for ἐν τῷ ἁγίῳ ὄρει: the Greek could put the monastery *on* the mount, but late ἐν = εἰς is ordinary and the Latin reads *ascenderemus in sanctum montem*. Ambiguous, so rejected.
- **0026 ch. 188** διέβαλεν "affirmed": already logged (cruces 3065B, read as διεβεβαίου).
- **0026 ch. 189** ὡς ἐντολὴν ἐλάμβανεν "took it as her charge [?]": flagged and logged. The Latin's negative is a Latin divergence, so not a finding.
- **0026 ch. 189** "Dost thou bid me, my lord? I will go off…": one Greek question split into a question plus a statement. Sense kept.
- **0027 ch. 191** "nor [made anyone] swear [?]": the supplied words are bracketed and logged, and they match the Latin *jurare compulerit*. Six negated members in the Greek, six in the English.
- **0027 ch. 192** ὁ γεγονὼς πάπας "Having become pope" (or "the late pope"): no change to the story.
- **0027 ch. 193** "and put on both witnesses and the completion, and bring it me [?]" (αἴρε μοι αὐτὸ τὸ πρόσωπον): flagged. Doubtful Greek; no polarity or agency issue.
- **0028 ch. 195** "three [?] centenaria" against printed δηνάρια τρία then τρία κεντηνάρια: flagged by the translator. The Latin has *tria centenaria*. Harmonized openly.
- **0028 ch. 196** τὸν τρόπον ὑπέδειξαν rendered "showed them the place" (as if τόπον): the following ἐν ᾧ… and ἴχνη τοῦ πυρός make either reading come out the same. Rejected as immaterial.
- **0029 ch. 198** τὴν μὲν σφραγῖδα… with no δέ-clause: the Greek is like that as printed, and the English follows it.
- **0029 ch. 199** "what is it that he says [?]" for τί ἐστιν ὃ λέγεις: flagged; sense intact.
- **0029 ch. 200** "commissions him" (ἐπιτάσσει αὐτῷ): it is ambiguous whether this is the goldsmith or the young man, and equally so in the Greek. The next clause (the master hands the work over) settles the story. Rejected.
- **0030 ch. 202** "the father threw himself at his feet": the Greek ἥπλωσεν ἑαυτόν has the father as its unexpressed subject, so the English is correct.
- **0030 ch. 206** οὕτως διέλαμψεν μεγάλῃ πραότητι, made narrative ("And so she shone out…") rather than left inside the lady's reply: there is no quotation mark in the Greek, and the chapter break follows. Ambiguous, so rejected.
- **0031 ch. 210** "knowing what he was going to do" (εἰδὼς τί ἔμελλεν ποιεῖν): the English keeps the Greek's ambiguity.
- **0031 ch. 216** "we ought not only not to stand by" for οὐκ ὀφείλομεν μόνον στῆσαι: the English adds a second negative. The context (Herod) demands that sense, and the Latin *Non solum enim non debemus* reads the same. Not a reversal.
- **0031 ch. 219 close** "And being greatly profited, I glorified…" is put outside the elder's quotation, so Moschus is the one speaking. It could equally be the elder. With no quotation marks in the Greek, and the book's doxology following, this is ambiguous and rejected.
