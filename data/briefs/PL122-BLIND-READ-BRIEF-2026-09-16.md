# PL 122 Eriugena — blind read brief (2026-09-16)

You are one of six blind readers on the four Eriugena works: 9176 *Commentarius in Evangelium secundum
Joannem* (15 chunks) and the fragments 9175, 9178, 9181. Your launch message names your chunks. The
English was drafted by Opus stints with the plate read inline; it passes every mechanical check. **Your job
is the class the checks cannot see.**

## ⛔ Rules of the read
- **Do NOT open any `cruces*.md`, any `data/briefs/*PENDING*`, `*PLATE-READS*`, or `*PLATE-NOTES*` file before
  your findings are written.** Blind readers given the cruces file found nothing new; blind readers
  without it found live defects the first pass had missed. You may check a finding against the cruces
  AFTER you have formed it, and say whether it was already logged.
- **Do not edit any file except your report.** The orchestrator adjudicates and applies.
- Read `translation-style.md`: at minimum Pattern 7/7a, Patterns 11–13, 15, 18, and **Eriugena rules
  E1–E12** (E11: where our Latin and the scanned plate differ, the English follows the reading that
  construes; E12: single-letter non-word = sense + crux). Know the markers: `[n:]`/`[nt:]`, `[var:]`
  (English-only Vulgate divergence), `[d:]`, `[sic:]`, `[cj:]`, `[ed:]`; Latin-only `[vl:]` (Floss's
  variant reading: the English writes NOTHING for it) and `[fn:]` (Floss's prose note, twinned by a
  translated `[nt:]`).

## What to hunt, in order of yield (measured on earlier Migne commentaries)
1. **Polarity:** a `non / nec / neque / nisi / haud / nunquam / nullus / nihil / sine` the Latin prints and the
   English drops, or one the English adds; a word rendered as its opposite to rescue a sentence.
2. **Mis-parsed grammar:** wrong subject or object, case misread (genitive vs dative, ablative absolute),
   a relative attached to the wrong antecedent, active vs passive, tense or mood that changes the claim,
   comparative sense lost. **This was the largest real class last time; read clause by clause.**
3. **Received (Douay-Rheims / KJV) wording arriving OVER the plate:** the English giving the familiar
   verse where Eriugena's Latin prints something else. Compare each lemma's English with ITS Latin, not with
   memory.
4. **Missing or added text:** a clause, sentence or list item in one column and not the other. Count
   sentences per paragraph and question marks per paragraph on both sides; a 46-word sentence once went missing
   past every check and was caught only by a question-mark count.
5. **Register-rule breaches** (E1–E12 and Eriugena's standing vocabulary: *theoria* italic; inhumanation /
   incarnation / humanation distinct; Elias, Isaias, latchet, forerunner, Pasch, Holy Spirit). Report
   these only where they change sense or break a rule, not as taste.

**Not defects, do not report:** em-dashes; column-band arithmetic; `(cont.)` heads; literal renderings
the Latin forces (a sentence that does not construe in Latin is carried literally by rule); a
`[vl:]` with no English twin.
