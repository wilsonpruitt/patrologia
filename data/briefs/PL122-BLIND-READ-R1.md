## Findings

### F1 · 0000 · 0297A · class 1 (polarity, by English idiom)
- **Latin:** `Et ne quis putaret, omnes homines eum non recepisse, nam ab initio mundi nullum tempus erat, in quo receptores divini Verbi non essent`
- **English now:** `And lest anyone should think that all men did not receive him, for from the beginning`
- **Proposed:** `And lest anyone should think that all men had failed to receive him, for from the beginning`
- **Why:** In English, "all men did not receive him" is normally heard as "not all men received him", which
  is the opposite of the point. Eriugena is heading off the idea that *nobody* received the Word (the proof
  is that there were always receivers). The printed *omnes … non* means "all failed to". Keeps the Latin's
  shape with no added negation.
- **Confidence:** probable (the Latin is certain, the English is ambiguous rather than flatly wrong).

### F2 · 0001 · 0302D · class 2 (*suis* vs *ipsius* merged)
- **Latin:** `in ea enim unusquisque suam receptionem pro suis meritis et ipsius gratia possidebunt`
- **English now:** `each one shall possess his own reception according to his merits and his grace:`
- **Proposed:** `each one shall possess his own reception according to his own merits and by the grace of that one:`
- **Why:** *suis meritis* (the receiver's own merits) and *ipsius gratia* (the grace of the Son/the house,
  not the receiver's) are two different people. "his merits and his grace" reads as one owner, so it makes
  the grace the saint's own, a doctrinally loaded slip. *ipsius* could also be rendered "his (the Son's)";
  the orchestrator may prefer another way of marking the change of referent, but the two must not merge.
- **Confidence:** probable.

### F3 · 0001 · 0302B · class 2 (a participle phrase split into two recipients)
- **Latin:** `et in eis quaerentibus et diligentibus se Deus manifestat, in quibus veluti quibusdam nubibus rapiuntur sancti`
- **English now:** `and in them God manifests himself to those who seek and love him, in which,`
- **Proposed:** `and in them, seeking and loving him, God manifests himself, in which,`
- **Why:** *quaerentibus et diligentibus* agree with *eis*. They describe the same souls and intellects God
  shows himself *in*, not a second group he shows himself *to*. The English adds a dative and splits one
  referent into two. The sense shift is small, so this is low priority.
- **Confidence:** probable.

### F4 · 0000 · 0297C · class 4 (words added, low)
- **Latin:** `Saepe sapientes viri vocabulo hominem solent appellare.`
- **English now:** `Wise men are often accustomed to call the human being, *homo*, by the word *vir*, man.`
- **Proposed:** `The wise are often accustomed to call the human being by the word "man" (*vir*).` (or keep, if the orchestrator rules the glosses are licensed)
- **Why:** The italic *homo* and *vir* are not printed at this point. They are the translator's disclosure of
  a word-play English can't carry. That is defensible, but it adds words, and *homo* is an extra gloss the
  argument doesn't need (*vir* alone carries it). Flagged only so the addition is a decision, not an
  accident.
- **Confidence:** probable (taste-adjacent. Reject freely.)
