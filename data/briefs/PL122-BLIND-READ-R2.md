## Findings

### F1 — 0005 · @0316B–C · class 1 (polarity)
- **Latin:** `Nisi quis [0316C] symbolum baptismatis acceperit visibiliter, et spiritum, id est, intellectum ipsius symboli non perceperit, non potest introire in regnum Dei.`
- **English now:** `Unless a man [0316C] receive the symbol of baptism visibly, and perceive the spirit, that is, the understanding of that symbol, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God.`
- **Proposed:** `Unless a man [0316C] receive the symbol of baptism visibly, and if he do not perceive the spirit, that is, the understanding of that symbol, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God.`
- **Why:** the plate prints `non perceperit` inside the *nisi* clause; the English drops the `non` to smooth the sense. Medieval *nisi … et … non* (negation re-stated in the second member) construes as "unless X, and if not Y"; the proposal carries the printed `non` without inverting the evident sense. If the orchestrator reads it as not construing, the Pattern 7 fallback is the fully literal "and do not perceive" plus a crux. Either way the dropped negation should not stand silently.
- **Confidence:** certain that a printed `non` is dropped; probable on the best replacement.

### F2 — 0004 · @0312C · class 2 (wrong referent)
- **Latin:** `dum jam non solum negat Christum carnem esse, quam jam in spiritum versam esse intelligit, verum etiam ipsum depellit rationabilem animum esse.`
- **English now:** `but also drives away that he himself is a rational mind.`
- **Proposed:** `but also drives away that Christ himself is a rational mind.`
- **Why:** `ipsum` is Christ (parallel to `Christum carnem esse`; the next sentence proves it: *caro ipsius … rationabilis ipsius anima in intellectum … versa*). "he himself" reads as the spiritual man, the subject of the clause, and makes him deny that *he* is a rational mind. (If adding the name is unwanted: "that he, Christ, is a rational mind" or "that Christ is also a rational mind".)
- **Confidence:** certain.

### F3 — 0005 · @0316C · class 2 / 7a (supplied preposition)
- **Latin:** `Sed et hoc dicendum: quoniam homo ex visibili corpore …`
- **English now:** `But to this also it must be said:`
- **Proposed:** `But this also must be said:`
- **Why:** the plate prints `Sed et hoc dicendum`, not E5's `Sed ad hoc dicendum`; `hoc` is the object ("this must be said"), and "to this" supplies an `ad` that is not printed. If a missing `ad` is suspected in our file, that is a plate question, not a silent repair.
- **Confidence:** probable (certain that "to" has no Latin; probable that it matters enough to change).

### F4 — 0004 · @0310B · class 2 (subject/predicate)
- **Latin:** `Agnus dicitur Christus Dei, quia pro omni mundo immolatus est`
- **English now:** `Christ is called the Lamb of God because he was sacrificed for the whole world`
- **Proposed:** `The Christ of God is called Lamb, because he was sacrificed for the whole world`
- **Why:** `Dei` goes with `Christus` (*Christus Dei*, the Lucan title), and `Agnus` is the bare predicate, as in the preceding sentences (*Verbum Dei ab Joanne Agnus nominatur*; *Agnus quoque nominatur*). The English moves `Dei` onto Lamb, importing "Lamb of God" from the lemma.
- **Confidence:** probable (the word order admits the English reading, but the parallel sentences favour the proposal).

### F5 — 0004 · @0310A · class 3 (received wording over the gloss's argument)
- **Latin:** `*Altera die,* vel, ut in Graeco significantius scribitur, *alia die* …` and then `Nunc autem, veluti secunda notitia, altera die, vel alia die`
- **English now:** `*The next day,* or, as is written more significantly in the Greek, *on another day* …` and `But now, as by a second acquaintance, the next day, or on another day,`
- **Proposed:** `*On the second day,* or, …` and `But now, as by a second acquaintance, on the second day, or on another day,`
- **Why:** "The next day" is the Douay idiom. Eriugena's gloss reads *altera* as ordinal (*Prima enim cognitio … veluti secunda notitia, altera die*), and "the next day" hides the link between *altera* and *secunda*. The contrast *altera/alia* stays visible either way, so this is a softer finding.
- **Confidence:** probable.

### F6 — 0004 · @0311A · class 2 (person)
- **Latin:** `cujus merito mortis et corruptionis omnes homines, excepto Redemptore, debitores sumus;`
- **English now:** `by whose desert all men, the Redeemer excepted, are debtors to death and corruption;`
- **Proposed:** `by whose desert we all, as men, the Redeemer excepted, are debtors to death and corruption;`
- **Why:** `sumus` is first person; Eriugena counts himself and the reader among the debtors. The English moves it into the third person. Minor but real.
- **Confidence:** certain on the grammar; small effect on sense.

### F7 — 0005 · @0314C · class 2 (transitive verb made intransitive)
- **Latin:** `Nicodemus veluti quidam nocturnus discipulus accipitur, conformans eos, qui perfectissime in Christum credunt`
- **English now:** `night disciple, conforming to those who believe most perfectly in Christ,`
- **Proposed:** `night disciple, conforming those who believe most perfectly in Christ,`
- **Why:** `conformans eos` is transitive (Nicodemus gives form to, figures, those believers); "conforming to" turns them into his model rather than the people he figures. It parallels the preceding *in figura catechumenorum ponitur*. (A freer "figuring those who" would also serve.)
- **Confidence:** probable.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **0003 @0308A** `ad integram plenissimamque divinae speculationis` → "into the entire and most full one of divine speculation": the Latin lacks a noun. "one" can take *civitatem* from the sentence before, so this is a literal carry, not a supplied reading.
- **0003 @0308A** `invisibilibus symbolis` "invisible symbols": odd (one expects *visibilibus*), but it is printed and the English carries it. Not a defect for this read; worth a plate glance only if already queued.
- **0003 @0307D** `infra Jordanem` "on this side of the Jordan": a fair sense for *infra* against *ultra*, consistent with *citra* later.
- **0004 @0310C** `Est itaque originale peccatum illud, quo …` → "That, therefore, is original sin, by which …": subject and predicate are slightly re-ordered, but the claim is unchanged.
- **0004 @0313B** `Ad hoc itaque dicendum, quia jam ab originali peccato humana natura neque … neque …, re ipsa abolitio facta est`: this does not construe (nominative *humana natura* with *abolitio facta est*). The English carries it literally with the negation intact, which is correct by rule.
- **0004 @0310C** `mactando vivificatur` "by being slain is made alive": the gerund is used with passive sense, an accepted idiom.
- **0004 @0312A** anchor `[0312A]` sits before `deponit` in the Latin and before "Another" in the English. This is column-band placement, which the brief excludes.
- **0005 @0314B** `ceteris contendentibus` "the rest contending against it": "against it" is a light supply that the ablative absolute implies. Not reported.
- **0005 @0315A** `*denuo*, "anew,"`: the English adds a gloss not printed. Rejected, because E3 requires ἄνωθεν/*desursum* and *denuo* to stay distinct and legible in English. The gloss serves that rule and does not alter the claim.
- **0005** `Regnum Dei inter vos est` "among you": follows the plate's *inter* rather than Douay "within you". Correct.
- **0005** `[var:]` markers at 0315A and 0316B, and in 0003 (Luke 16:16): the wording is sound. Whether they stand on plate-read columns is a ratchet matter, not this read's.
- **0004 notes** (`pessime omnino` "most wrongly of all"; Berengarius formula): faithful. In note a, two Latin sentences are joined with a semicolon in English, with no content lost.
