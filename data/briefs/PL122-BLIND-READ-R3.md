## Findings

### F1 · 0006 · 0319C · class 2 (relative / object misparsed) · probable
- **Latin:** `ut eum aspicientes indubitanter crederent semper in Patre fuisse, ad quem intendebat.`
- **English now:** `that he had always been in the Father, toward whom he was directing them.`
- **Proposed:** `that he had always been in the Father, toward whom he was looking.`
- **Why:** *intendebat* has no object here. It is intransitive: "was directing his gaze", picking up
  *elevabat oculos*. The English adds "them", and the nearest plural antecedent in English is "those
  who beheld him". That turns the clause into Christ directing the onlookers to the Father, which
  the Latin does not say.
- **Cruces (checked after):** see the end of this report.

### F2 · 0006 · 0319B · class 5 (Pattern 3 breach) · certain
- **Latin:** `In divina Scriptura Pater caelestis, qui est principium omnium, nomine caeli crebro nominatur.`
- **English now:** `In divine Scripture the heavenly Father, who is the principle of all things,`
- **Proposed:** `In Holy Scripture the heavenly Father, who is the principle of all things,`
- **Why:** Pattern 3 names *divina Scriptura* explicitly and requires "Holy Scripture". The same
  work's 0008 (0325D, *in divina Scriptura*) already renders it "in Holy Scripture", so as it stands
  the work is inconsistent with itself.

### F3 · 0006 · 0319C · class 5 / Pattern 18 candidate (printed word real, sense against context) · probable (plate question)
- **Latin:** `Saepe in Evangelio legitur, Dominum surrexisse in caelum, id est in Patrem.`
- **English now:** `Often it is read in the Gospel that the Lord rose into heaven, that is, into the Father.`
- **Proposed:** keep the literal "rose" in the running text unless a witness shows otherwise. Flag it
  for a plate read and a crux: the context wants *suspexisse* ("looked up to heaven", as in
  *elevatis oculis in caelum*, Jn 11:41, 17:1). The next two sentences are entirely about his
  *raising his eyes* (*elevasse oculos suos… In Patrem igitur elevabat oculos*), and "often in the
  Gospel the Lord rose into heaven" is not true of the Gospel. If the plate or the other printing
  reads *suspexisse*, then under E11 the English becomes `looked up to heaven`. If both read
  *surrexisse*, this is a `[cj:]` / crux candidate under Pattern 18.
- **Why:** this is the one place in the three chunks where a real word, rendered faithfully, makes
  the argument stop following. I have not seen the plate.

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **0006 0319B `Ascendit, ambiguum est, cujus temporis verbum sit`** → "Hath ascended: it is ambiguous
   of what tense the verb is". The English form settles the tense that Eriugena says is ambiguous in
   Latin. I rejected this as a defect: the Greek (which he cites) is past, so "hath ascended" is right,
   and English cannot keep the ambiguity. The orchestrator may want a crux note or italic *Ascendit*.
   Taste only.
2. **0006 0317C `Si nasceris et tu de spiritu, hoc eris ut ille.`** → "thou wilt be this, like him."
   Augustine's own syntax runs *hoc eris, ut ille qui…* ("you will be such that he who…"), but the
   plate as printed closes the sentence after *ille*. The literal rendering follows the printed
   punctuation (Patterns 7 and 8). Not a defect.
3. **0006 0317C `Sed quoniam ille adhuc non est natus`** → "since he was not yet born". The present
   became a past tense in narrative framing. The claim is unchanged.
4. **0006 0318D lemma `…et non creditis, quomodo…`** → "and you believe not: how…". The colon is
   supplied where the Latin has a comma. This is a minor Pattern 8 point. It does not change the
   sense, so it is not reported as a finding.
5. **0006 0320C `Consepulti sumus cum Christo per baptismum, nisi in mortem`** → "unless into death".
   The odd *nisi* is carried literally, which is correct (Pattern 7, nothing silently dropped).
6. **0007 0321B `neque dimittere ei sua peccata vult`** → "nor wills to forgive him his sins". The
   subject is unclear in the Latin, and the English keeps it equally unclear. Not a defect.
7. **0007 0321B `et non in eo manet, quod judicetur`** → "and there does not remain in him that which
   should be judged". It could also be read "he does not remain in that for which he should be
   judged". The English reading is supported by the parallel *in se detinet digna judicio*. Kept.
8. **0007 0322C `Quamvis quidam existiment, neminem eum baptizasse per seipsum, sed dicitur
   baptizasse…`** → "but that he is said to have baptized". The English puts the indicative *dicitur*
   under *existiment*. Some readers would, but it is a defensible reading of a loose sentence. Borderline.
9. **0007 0322D `enos enim hebraice aqua dicitur`** → "for in Hebrew *water* is called *enos*". Subject
   and predicate are inverted, but the claim is identical (the Hebrew word for water is *enos*). Not a
   defect.
10. **0007 0322D `praecursor discipulus`** → "the forerunner, a disciple". Odd Latin carried literally.
    Fine.
11. **0007 0322C `[var: Vulg. *baptizabat*]`** on the lemma *baptizavit*. The marker's form is correct.
    Whether its column has been plate-read is a ratchet question I did not check, since checking would
    mean opening PLATE-READS.
12. **0008 0324A `sanctificationem quandam Joannis effecisse`** → "a certain sanctification of John".
    The genitive is ambiguous (John's person or John's baptism), and the literal English keeps it so.
    Fine.
13. **0008 0325D `Datum… pro donum`** (ungrammatical *pro* + accusative) → "given… for gift". This is
    a defect in the Latin grammar, not a change of sense. The English is right.
14. **0006 0317D `Quemadmodum ille Spiritus sanctus est per naturam`** → "that Spirit is holy by
    nature". The Latin is a predicate "holy spirit", paralleled by *spiritum sanctum efficit*. The
    English is adequate and "a holy spirit" follows. Taste.
15. Lemma wording in all three chunks was compared against its own Latin, not against Douay:
    *verum ac solum Deum* ("the true and only God", plate order kept), *Si non venissem et locutus
    fuissem* (no *eis*, none added), *O homo, quid habes* ("O man" kept), *desursum de caelo*. No
    received wording arrived over the plate.
