## Findings

### F1 — 9176/0009 · 0327B–C · class 2 (mis-parsed grammar)
- **Latin:** *Id ipsum significat, quod ipse luce inchoante crescere, [0327C] tenebris autem decrescere natus sit; ego vero luce inchoante minui, tenebris autem inchoantibus crescere natus sum.*
- **English as it stands:** "The very same is signified by this, that he was born to increase as the light begins, [0327C] but to decrease with the darkness; but I was born to decrease as the light begins, but to increase as the darkness begins."
- **Proposed:** "The very same is signified by this, that he was born with the light beginning to increase, [0327C] but the darkness to decrease; but I was born with the light beginning to be diminished, but the darkness beginning to increase."
- **Why:** the infinitives depend on *inchoante / inchoantibus* (ablative absolute "the light beginning to increase"), not on *natus*. This is the solstice figure (Christ born as the days lengthen, John as they shorten; cf. Augustine, *natus est Christus cum dies crescere inciperet*). The English makes Christ and John the subjects of increasing/decreasing and has light and darkness merely "begin", which loses the figure and has the Baptist "born to decrease as the light begins", which is not the claim.
- **Confidence:** probable (high).
- **Cruces (checked after):** PARTLY logged. `cruces-0007.md` "Col 0327B–C — the solstice figure" identifies the figure but treats the English construal as correct and only discusses the bare-*tenebris* asymmetry; the misparse (infinitives governed by *inchoante*, not *natus*) is NOT logged. New finding.

### F2 — 9176/0009 · 0326D · class 5 / Pattern 18 (real word, faithful English misleads)

Needs the plate before choosing (E11: does the other printing have *eum*?).

1. **0009 · 0326C** *et tu dicis, quia missus est ante illum* → "thou sayest that he is sent before him". Reads oddly (Jn 3:28 is *missus sum*), but the Latin prints 3rd person with no subject; literal carry is correct under Pattern 7.
2. **0009 · 0327A** *quod jam non sentitis* → "which you do not now perceive". "no longer / not yet" would be sharper, but no polarity or claim changes.
3. **0009 · 0328B** *hominem venisse Christum* → "Christ came as man". Acceptable construal.
4. **0010 · 0330A** *dividens singulis propria* → "dividing to each severally his own". "severally" echoes KJV but only glosses *singulis*; nothing added.
5. **0010 · 0331B** *quos merito suae superbiae salvare neglexit* → "by the desert of their pride". *suae* formally reflexive to the Lord, but the sense is plainly theirs; not a misparse.
6. **0010 · 0331C** *Quoniam vero noluit manere sub lege, neque eos, qui in eum credituri essent* → "nor those who were to believe in him": the Latin is elliptical and the English carries the same ellipsis.
7. **0010 · 0332C** fn c English "It is corruptly for Sichem … usage has obtained that Sichar should be read" and the added glosses "numbers"/"shoulders" in the `[nt:]`. Loose but not sense-changing; glosses help the English reader through a Latin wordplay.

9. **0010 · 0330B** *In principio enim fecit Deus* → "made" (not Vulgate *creavit*): follows the plate, correct.
10. **0011 · 0335B** *temporalem videlicet sitim temporaliter exstinguit* → "for it quenches a temporal thirst temporally". *videlicet* is "namely", not "for"; weak, no sense change.
11. **0011 · 0334D / 0335A** *Sanctus Augustinus* → "Saint Augustine" and nt "Saint Jerome" capitalised: sentence-initial, so not an E4 breach ("Thus saint Jerome" mid-sentence is lower-case).
12. **0011 · 0336A** *animus* kept in Latin italics while *mens*/*intellectus* are Englished: needed, since he lists *animus* as one of its own names beside *mens* and *intellectus*; English "mind" would collapse it into *mens*.
