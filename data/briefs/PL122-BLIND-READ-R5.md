### F1 · 0014 · 0345B and 0345C · class 5 (Pattern 3) · certain
- **Latin (1):** *In his enim divinae Scripturae locis et in multis similibus*
- **English as it stands:** "For in these places of divine Scripture and in many like them"
- **Proposed:** "For in these places of Holy Scripture and in many like them"
- **Latin (2):** *quamvis usus divinae Scripturae sit, symbola pro mysteriis*
- **English as it stands:** "although it is the usage of divine Scripture to put symbols for mysteries"
- **Proposed:** "although it is the usage of Holy Scripture to put symbols for mysteries"
- **Why:** Pattern 3 names *divina Scriptura* explicitly as "Holy Scripture". The same chunk renders *sanctae Scripturae* "Holy Scripture" (0344D), so the two sites are also inconsistent inside the chunk.
- **Cruces:** not logged there (checked after; see below).

### F2 · 0012 · 0337A · class 2 (antecedent) · probable
- **Latin:** *veluti naturali viro errori subditur, a quo et per quem fallitur, … Ideoque de eo dicitur, et nunc quem habes non est tuus vir;*
- **English as it stands:** "And therefore it is said of it, *and he whom thou now hast is not thy husband;*"
- **Proposed:** "And therefore it is said of him, *and he whom thou now hast is not thy husband;*"
- **Why:** *de eo* is masculine: it is the error, the false husband (*errori … a quo et per quem*), that the lemma says "is not thy husband". *anima* is feminine and would be *de ea*. In the English every nearby "it" is the soul ("it is deceived", "its own nature"), so "of it" puts the saying on the wrong referent. "him" matches the preceding "by whom and through whom".

### F3 · 0014 · 0346A · class 4 (added word; English does not construe) · certain
- **Latin:** *Huic multitudini fidem Christi esurienti apponuntur quinque panes et duo pisces*
- **English as it stands:** "To this multitude, hungering for the faith of Christ, are set before them five loaves and two fishes"
- **Proposed:** "Before this multitude, hungering for the faith of Christ, are set five loaves and two fishes"
- **Why:** the dative *huic multitudini* is rendered twice ("To this multitude" and "before them"). The Latin has no second pronoun, and the English sentence as it stands does not parse.

### F4 · 0013 · 0341B · class 4 (dropped word) · probable, minor
- **Latin:** *quod jam Novo clarescente vilescere coepit, adhuc tamen omnino non recessit.*
- **English as it stands:** "has begun to grow cheap, yet has not altogether withdrawn."
- **Proposed:** "has begun to grow cheap, yet has still not altogether withdrawn."
- **Why:** "yet" translates *tamen*, so *adhuc* goes unrendered. It carries the point that the Old Testament is *still* present, which is why the boy is "here".

### F5 · 0013 · 0340B/C · class 1 (polarity), plate candidate, not a change to the English yet · probable that the plate needs reading
- **Latin:** *Deus tentator [0340C] malorum est. Tentat autem electos probandi gratia, non supplantandi.*
- **English as it stands:** "God is the tempter [0340C] of the wicked. But he tempts the elect for the sake of proving, not of supplanting."
- **Proposed:** no change unless the plate reads otherwise. If the plate (or the other printing, E11) reads *intentator*, then: "God is not a tempter of evils. [0340C] But he tempts the elect for the sake of proving, not of supplanting." (anchor placed as the printing has it).
- **Why:** this is Jas 1:13, *Deus enim intentator malorum est*, which every other PL author in our TEI quotes as *intentator* (7736, 7334, 8947 among them). In our TEI *tentator* sits right before the `<pb n="0340C"/>` break, which is where CLAUDE.md warns a lost syllable or break artifact reads as Migne's own text. Both readings construe. *Tentator* fits the next sentence, where one kind of temptation "reprobates the faithlessness of the impious", and *intentator* fits the *autem* contrast. So this needs a plate read, not a guess. If *tentator* stands, "of the wicked" for *malorum* is a defensible choice. Log it as a crux either way.

### F6 · 0013 and 0014, many sites · class 5 (E4 fixed vocabulary) · probable; needs the orchestrator's ruling
- **Latin:** *intellectus* in the sense of a reading: 0343A *invisibilibus eorum intellectibus*; 0343A/B *spirituales intellectus … ex ipsis intellectibus*; 0343B *subtiles ac difficiles intellectus sanctae Scripturae*; 0343B *spiritualesque intellectus colligentium*; 0344A *fragmenta divinorum intellectuum*; 0345D *scientia spiritualium intellectuum*, *infra altitudinem divinorum intellectuum*; 0346A/B *in earum spirituales intellectus*, *divinos ipsius historiae intellectus*, *divini intellectus rerum gestarum*; 0346C *Intellectum vero ipsius litterae*; 0347A *in historiam et intellectum*, *Solus enim intellectus*; 0348A *divinorum ipsius mysterii intellectuum*.
- **English as it stands:** "meaning(s)" at every one of these sites (e.g. "their invisible meanings", "the spiritual meanings of the letter", "the divine meanings of that history", "into history and meaning", "only a meaning is thought of in it").
- **Proposed:** "understanding(s)" (e.g. "their invisible understandings", "the spiritual understandings of the letter", "into history and understanding", "only an understanding is thought of in it"). "sense" is ruled out here because *sensus* (the five bodily senses) is part of the argument in these same paragraphs.
- **Why:** E4 gives *intellectus* two renderings, "intellect" for the mind and "understanding / sense" for a reading. "meaning" is a third word. The chunks are not even consistent with themselves: 0347A *secundum spiritualis intellectus* is "according to spiritual understanding", and 0013 *praeditos divinorum hominum intellectus* is correctly "intellects". If the orchestrator judges "meaning" an acceptable gloss of "sense", this becomes a note to E4 rather than a defect. It is reported because the fixed-vocabulary rule is broken, not as a matter of taste.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **0012 0336C** *laudo, quod dixisti, quae vere dixisti* → "thou who hast said truly": reading *quae* as feminine nominative (the woman) construes. Floss's *forte quia?* is Latin-only, so there is no defect.
- **0012 0337C** *idem:* → "and again:": it introduces a second quotation from the same Apostle. The sense is kept, so this is taste, not a defect.
- **0012 0337C** *ad redarguendam, et gravitatem sacramentorum opprimendam* → "to convict it, and to press down the weight of the sacraments": the Latin is itself awkward (perhaps *gravitate … opprimendam*), and it is carried literally as the rule requires.
- **0012 0339A** *Etenim Pater tales quaerit* → "For the Father also seeketh such": this matches Douay's rendering of the identical Latin *etenim*, and the Latin matches the Vulgate. Not a class-3 intrusion.
- **0012 0338C** *qui me videt, et Patrem meum videt* → "seeth my Father also": the English follows the plate's *meum* over Douay's "the Father". Correct.
- **0013 0340B, 0340C** *panes* → "bread" in the lemmas: identical to the Vulgate Latin, so the Douay wording is licensed. The gloss uses "loaves" where the argument counts them.
- **0013 0340D** *Ordo evangelisantium* → "order of evangelists": close enough, no change of sense.
- **0013 0342A** *distribuit* (perfect) → "does … distribute": a narrative present with no change of claim.
- **0014 0346B** *legatur liber Geneseos, … transitus … per mare rubrum*: Eriugena's (or the codex's) slip for Exodus. The English follows the plate ("Genesis"), which is correct. Possibly worth a crux; not a translation defect.
- **0014 0346A** anchor `[0346A]`: the Latin sets it inside *foenum [0346A] multum*, while the English sets it before "Where he says". This is the anchor displaced a few words by English word order. It is flagged for awareness only, since the brief excludes column-band placement.
- **0014 0346A** *magistris suis* → "his masters": literal and ambiguous in the Latin too. Carried.
- **0014 0347B** *investigatur cerebrum (?)* → "its brain (?) is searched out": Floss's query is carried literally, which is correct.
