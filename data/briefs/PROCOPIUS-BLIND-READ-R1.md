### F1. 0001, col. 2797 [b: B] (§ 2) — class 3 (relation reversed: share vs. rival)
- **Greek (file only):** Οὐκοῦν εἰ τοὺς Ἀθηναίους αὐτόχθονας ἀκούων τεθαύμακας, πάρεστί σοι μετ᾿ ἐκείνων φιλοτιμεῖσθαι
- **Latin:** *Si igitur Athenienses tanquam aborigines admiratus es, licet cum illis gloriari*
- **English as it stands:** "thou mayest vie with them"
- **Proposed English:** "thou mayest take pride along with them"
- **Why:** μετ᾿ ἐκείνων φιλοτιμεῖσθαι is to share their boast (Epidamnus too has an earth-born founder), not to
  compete against them; "vie with" makes the Athenians rivals. Latin *cum illis gloriari* agrees.
- **Confidence:** probable
- **Already in cruces?** No. I checked after writing: there is no line on φιλοτιμεῖσθαι at 2797.

### F2. 0003, col. 2820 [b: B] (§ 21) — class 5 (English follows a conjecture against the printed Greek)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0024, right column, 4 lines above ΚΒʹ):** ἀφέμενον αὐτὸν τῆς μήνιδος τῆς Ἀχιλλέως καὶ μιᾶς
  ἐμπιπλαμέ-|νης ª ὁλκάδος. The plate prints **ἐμπιπλαμένης** ("being filled"), keyed to the Variae lectiones at the
  foot of the same page: *ª ἐμπιπραμένης conj. et vert. R., scilicet Odysseam μ. 415 respiciens.* Our Greek file
  matches the plate.
- **Latin:** *valere jussa Achillis ira et navi una incensa* (Villoison translates R.'s conjecture).
- **English as it stands:** "he would have let go the wrath of Achilles and one ship set on fire"
- **Proposed English:** "he would have let go the wrath of Achilles and one merchantman being filled [lat: the Latin
  gives *navi una incensa*, one ship set on fire, translating R.'s conjecture ἐμπιπραμένης]"
- **Why:** "set on fire" renders ἐμπιπραμένης, the conjecture printed in the variant note, not ἐμπιπλαμένης, the word
  in the text. The English follows the Latin here, against the brief's rule that the English follows the Greek as
  printed with the Latin in a note. The conjecture is attractive: Iliad = wrath of Achilles + the one ship Hector
  fires. So if the coordinator keeps "set on fire", it needs the T note and crux line that say so. No `[?]` or
  marker in the English shows the departure.
- **Confidence:** certain that the English departs from the printed Greek. Whether the fix is the literal rendering or
  a disclosed conjecture is a ruling for the coordinator.
- **Already in cruces?** Partly. I checked after writing. Line 50 (2820) gives the S note for the *Iliad* allusion and says "Villoison *navi una incensa* confirms the reading ἐμπιπραμένης". It treats the conjecture as the text. It never says that Migne's Greek prints ἐμπιπλαμένης, or that ἐμπιπραμένης is R.'s conjecture from the Variae lectiones. So the departure from the printed word is not disclosed, which is the Pattern 7 / 7a′ problem. Also note: that crux cites *Il.* XVI, while Migne's note says R. was thinking of *Od.* μ 415.

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **0001 col. 2800A, Οὐχ οὕτως Εὔμολπος βασιλείας ἀποτυχιεῖ.** ἀποτυχιεῖ is not a regular form (perhaps Migne's or the
   codex's misprint; conj. N. οὐχ ὡς… ἀπέτυχες). The English "Not so Eumolpus, who, having failed of the kingship,
   took the priesthood" restructures the period, but the sense and polarity hold: Eumolpus took the priesthood after
   failing of the kingship, unlike Anastasius. Not material. Not plate-read, because I make no finding about the Greek.
2. **0002 col. 2809A, Οὐχ ὥσπερ Φίλιππος… ἀνεῖλες ὠμῶς.** The printed second person makes the following Σὺ δὲ… οὐχ οὕτως
   μετῆλθες redundant, and ἀνεῖλεν might be expected. But the Latin also has *sustulisti*, and the English renders the
   printed 2nd person with the negation intact ("Not like Philip… didst thou destroy so cruelly"). No departure.
3. **0002 col. 2812A, ἐφ᾿ ᾧ παρῆσαν ἐπράττετο** → "that was done for which they had come". Migne's note glosses it
   *Vendebatur*, and πράττεσθαι can mean "be exacted". This is a lexical shade, not a reversal, and the next clause
   (the governors grew rich) carries the sense. Left alone.
4. **0002 col. 2812B, διὰ τοῦτο** (note: lege τοῦτον) → "for its sake". This leans toward the conjecture, but τοῦτο can
   refer to the same thing (the love of money). Not material.
5. **0002 col. 2812D, § 11 οὐ δυσχεραίνει τῷ πλήθει καὶ παύεται** → "is neither vexed by for their number nor ceases
   from". The negative extends to παύεται, as the sense requires (Latin *non fatigatur*). Kept.
6. **0003 col. 2817A, μείζω γὰρ ταῦτα καὶ τοῦ καιροῦ νικῶντος τὴν χρείαν** → "for they are too great, and the occasion
   prevails over the need". This is acceptable for a genitive absolute. No inversion.
7. **0003 col. 2824A, Καὶ ταῦτα σιδήρῳ καὶ μάχαις ἐπῄει** → "And that done, he fell with iron and battles…". A loose
   rendering of καὶ ταῦτα, with no change of sense.
8. **0003 col. 2820C, ὅπερ Παυσανίαν…** "the very thing" refers to the loftiness of rank (τὸ τῆς ἀξίας ὑψηλόν), and
   the English can bear that reading. Not reversed.

   (2801), ψηφισμένων (2804), δραμάτων/πραγμάτων and αὐτός/ὁ στρατός (2805), Σολῶν/Σολύμων (2808), πόλιν/πάλιν and
