## Findings

### F1. 0004, col. 2835 [b: A] (p-0032, Greek column, lines 1–2) — class 5 (number / structural) — certain
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0032):** Στοαί τέ εἰσιν ἑκατέρωθι δύο, οἰκοδομίαν μὲν τοῦ νεὼ οὐδεμίαν διειργόμεναι … and two sentences on: Ταύταιν δὲ ταῖν στοαῖν, ἁτέρα μὲν τοὺς ἄνδρας … ἡ ἄλλη (dual, "these two colonnades … the one … the other").
- **Latin:** *Utrumque latus duæ circum Ædem porticus ambiunt* … *altera precantibus viris assignata; altera, feminarum* (two in all).
- **English as it stands:** "And there are two colonnades on either side, not separated from the building of the church in any way,"
- **Proposed English:** "And there are two colonnades, one on either side, not separated from the building of the church in any way,"
- **Why:** "two on either side" reads as four. The Greek's dual two sentences later (ταῖν στοαῖν, ἁτέρα … ἡ ἄλλη: one for men, the other for women) fixes the total at two, one per flank; the English itself then says "Of these two colonnades, the one … and the other", contradicting its own count.
- **Confidence:** certain.
- **Already in the cruces?** No (checked after; cruces 0004 has no entry on the colonnades).

### F2. 0004, col. 2835 [b: B–C] — class 2 (who/what) — probable
- **Greek (file only; the sense point does not turn on a reading):** … ὁ νοῦς δὲ οἱ πρὸς τὸν Θεὸν ἐπαιρόμενος ἀεροβατεῖ, οὐ μακράν που ἡγούμενος αὐτὸν εἶναι, ἀλλ᾿ ἐμφιλοχωρεῖν μάλιστα οἷς αὐτὸς εἵλετο.
- **Latin:** *non procul eum abesse, verum sede illa potissimum gaudere, quam sibi ipse delegerit* (the place He chose).
- **English as it stands:** "but delights above all to dwell in those whom He Himself has chosen."
- **Proposed English:** "but delights above all to dwell in what He Himself has chosen."
- **Why:** οἷς is masc. or neut.; the English commits it to persons ("those whom"), but the sentence is about the man standing in the church, and ἐμφιλοχωρεῖν "linger fondly in (a place)" (used of the gaze at S30 in this very chunk) wants the place. The Latin takes it as the chosen *sedes*. The neutral "what" keeps the Greek's ambiguity without turning a statement about the building into one about the elect.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** No (cruces 2836 entry covers only the missing main verb of the same sentence, not οἷς).

### F3. 0004, col. 2833 [b: C] — class 5 (term varied) — probable (low weight)
- **Greek (file only):** ἐκτομὴν δὲ, εἰ μὲν τὰ προὔχοντα ποιεῖσθαι τῶν τοῦ λόφου πλευρῶν μέλλοιεν, ἐγγωνίων
- **English as it stands:** "cut, if they were to make the projecting parts of the pier's sides, with angles;"
- **Proposed English:** "cut, if they were to make the projecting parts of the hill's sides, with angles;"
- **Why:** everywhere else in the chunk λόφος = "hill" ("four man-made hills, which they call piers"; "The hills are made"; "The hills I mentioned just now", the sentence immediately before), and "pier" is reserved for πεσσός. Here alone λόφος becomes "pier". Not a change of sense (they are the same masonry), but a fixed-term slip in the one passage that explains how the "hills" are built.
- **Confidence:** probable (the referent is not in doubt; only the term).
- **Already in the cruces?** No.

### F4. 0005, col. 2840 [b: C] — class 2 (who/what) — probable
- **Greek (file only; the point is syntax, not a reading):** Οὐδὲ κατενύγη ἐπὶ σὲ ὁ εὔσπλαγχνος. Ἄθυτος δὲ ἔσται καὶ ἀκαλλιέργητος· καὶ διαμενεῖ ἀμελῴδητος· ἀνθ᾿ ὧν οὐκ ἔπαθέ τι ἐπὶ τῇ συντριβῇ σου· ἀλλ᾿ ἔθηκε τὸ πρόσωπον αὐτοῦ ὡς στερεὰν πέτραν·
- **Latin:** *nulla de te compunxit miseratio ! sine sacrificiis et pulchris ritibus erit, sine cantibus manebit; nec propterea aliquid passus est in tua eversione* (subject equally unexpressed).
- **English as it stands:** "And it shall be without sacrifice and without its fair rites, and shall remain without song; because He suffered nothing at thy breaking,"
- **Proposed English:** "And He shall be without sacrifice and without fair rites, and shall remain unhymned; because He suffered nothing at thy breaking,"
- **Why:** the unexpressed subject of ἔσται / διαμενεῖ runs on from ὁ εὔσπλαγχνος and on into ἔπαθε and ἔθηκε, all one subject (God): He who felt nothing shall go unsacrificed to and unsung. The English's "it" makes the church the subject while the same sentence addresses the church as "thee" (σου), giving one referent two persons in one clause; and ἀνθ᾿ ὧν ("in return for which") reads as a requital on God, which "it" turns into a mere cause of the church's loss. ἀμελῴδητος = unhymned (cf. ἄθυτος, "un-sacrificed-to"), passive of the honour withheld.
- **Confidence:** probable (the Latin keeps the ambiguity; the run of subjects and the person shift decide it for me).
- **Already in the cruces?** No (cruces 2840A has S/T notes on Isaiah 50:7 and the following ἵν᾿ ἰδῇ clause, nothing on the subject of ἔσται).

## Candidates considered and rejected

- 0004 S25 "Above them a circular structure" after "quite small columns": αὐτῶν = the arches; English "them" is as ambiguous as the Greek order allows and the arches are the natural antecedent. Not a reversal.
- 0004 S23 λογάδην μὲν… ἐναρμοσθεῖσιν δέ rendered "chosen out, and fitted": μέν/δέ slightly flattened, no sense change.
- 0004 S10 ἀποσαλεύουσα "as though riding at anchor": a legitimate rendering of the verb; Latin *velut navis in anchoris* agrees.
- 0004 S27 Ταῦτα δέ, οἶμαι, γλώσσῃ… ἀμήχανά ἐστι: printed text has no negative (Hoeschel's οὐδέ is only in the note); English "beyond the power of a tongue that lisps" follows the printed Greek correctly.
- 0004 S41 οἰκοδομίαν… οὐδεμίαν διειργόμεναι "not separated… in any way": polarity correct.
- 0004 S43 αἰνεῖται (for ἀνεῖται): Migne's misprint (plate-confirmed on p-0032); English "set apart" gives the sense, as the brief directs.
- 0004 S62 κόλαξ "would have seemed flattery": the account seeming a flatterer; noun-for-agent is no change of sense.
- 0004 S69 Φέρεται… μαρτύριον "carries off a certain testimony": correct middle sense; chunk ends here in both Greek and English.
- 0004: the English carries far fewer `[b: X]` marks than the Greek (Greek 22, English 11; e.g. none at [2832], [2836]). Band letters are excluded from findings by the brief; noted only for whoever owns the mechanical check.
- 0004 Greek-file residue `περικέχυται[δικέχ]`, `ἐπαν-Α εστηκυῖα`, `ἀποσκοποῦντεςΒ`, `δια] νοίᾳ`, `ἐντετυχ[ηκ]έναι`: OCR/band-letter debris in our Greek file, no effect on the English; not findings under the brief, but the file should be tidied.
- 0005 S5 ὢ τῆς Ἰλιάδος "O the Iliad of woes!": "of woes" spells out the proverb (Ἰλιὰς κακῶν); allusion, not an addition of fact.
- 0005 S8 κονιορτὸν πρὸς αὐτὴν πάλιν αἰρόμενον "the dust rising again to it": faithful to an awkward Greek (αὐτήν = earth); Latin *ad terram delabentem* differs but the English follows the Greek.
- 0005 S12 σεμνολόγημα "sacred pulpit": interpretive (Latin *venerandum concionatorium*), no polarity or agent issue.
- 0005 S12 τῶν ἀνεφίκτων χρημάτων "riches past reaching": χρήματα could be "matters"; Latin *inaccessis divitiis* agrees with the English.
- 0005 S23 ἢ συντελεσθήσεται "or be brought to completion": the ἢ after a καί-chain of restoration verbs tempted a reading "or be brought to an end", but συντελέω "complete" is the plain sense and the Latin *aut perficietur* agrees. Rejected.
- 0005 S23 τὸ δ᾿ ἐμὸν εἰ ἐπιβιώσω… Θεοῦ ἄρα τοῦτο κἀκεῖνο εἰδότος "my part is whether I shall survive…": slightly loose (it is God, not the speaker, who knows), but the closing genitive absolute is rendered and the sense is recoverable.
