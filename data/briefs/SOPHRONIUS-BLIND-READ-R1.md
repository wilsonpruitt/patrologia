# Sophronius blind read, R1: chunks 0001–0004 (2026-11-05)

Reader R1. Range: `sophronius-hierosolymitanus-orationes` chunks 0001–0004 (Synodical Letter, cols 3148–3173).
Read: every sentence of the Greek in `src/greek/`, against `src/english/`; the facing Latin in `src/pg-latin/` consulted

## Findings

### F1. 0001, col. 3149 [b: D] (s16): "just as they are"
- **Class:** 5 (sense distorted; arguably 3, a participle mis-parsed)
- **Greek (file only):** Δότε γοῦν ὑμεῖς ἐμοὶ πατρικῶς τε καὶ ἀδελφικῶς τὰ αἰτήματα δίκαιά γε τυγχάνοντα
- **Latin:** *Date ergo vos mihi paterne simul atque fraterne, quae postulo, justa quippe sunt*
- **English as it stands:** `my requests, just as they are;`
- **Proposed:** `my requests, since they are indeed just;`
- **Why:** δίκαιά γε τυγχάνοντα is predicative ("being, as they are, just"): Sophronius pleads that the requests are just.
  "just as they are" reads as "as they stand / unaltered" and loses the adjective entirely. The Latin's *justa quippe sunt* confirms the sense.
- **Confidence:** probable (certain that the English is ambiguous; the Greek has only one reading)
- **Already in the cruces?** (see end)

### F2. 0002, col. 3164 [b: B] (s34): which heretic each clause grieves
- **Class:** 2 (who does what: the heretics' targets can be read reversed)
- **Greek (file only):** Οὔτε γὰρ τῇ ἑνώσει τροπή τις ἢ φυρμὸς ἐμεσίτευσεν, οὔτε τῇ διαφορᾷ … διαίρεσίς τις ἢ τομὴ εἰσεκρίνετο· κἂν τοῦτο μὲν λυπῇ τὸν μεμηνότα Νεστόριον, ἐκεῖνο δὲ τήκῃ Εὐτυχῆ τὸν κακόφρονα.
- **English as it stands:** `even if the one grieves the maddened Nestorius, and the other wastes away Eutyches the evil-minded.`
- **Proposed:** `even if the latter grieves the maddened Nestorius, and the former wastes away Eutyches the evil-minded.`
- **Why:** τοῦτο … ἐκεῖνο is "this (nearer) … that (farther)": the denial of division (second clause) grieves Nestorius, the denial of
  change and mixing (first clause) wastes Eutyches. English "the one … the other" is normally read as "the former … the latter", which
  pins the no-change clause on Nestorius and the no-division clause on Eutyches: the heretics swapped.
- **Confidence:** probable (the Greek is unambiguous; the English is at best ambiguous and most naturally read the wrong way)
- **Already in the cruces?** (see end)

### F3. 0003, col. 3169 [b: B] (s23): "were wholly deprived of these": the negative Migne's own note restores
- **Class:** 1 (polarity)
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 199, left col.):** Αἱ γάρ τοι φύσεις ἀμετάβλητοι μένουσαι καὶ ἀσύγχυτοι, καὶ δύο τηλαυγῶς γνωριζόμεναι, καὶ οὐ
  συγκεχυμένως ἑνωθεῖσαι τούτων παντελῶς ἀπεστέρηντο¹⁶, καὶ ἐν ὑποστάσει μιᾷ διεδείκνυτο. Foot of the same page, *Variae lectiones*:
  **¹⁶ L. οὐχ ἐστέρηντο.** The file carries both exactly (`[n: L. οὐχ ἐστέρηντο.]`).
- **Latin:** *Naturae namque incommutabiles manentes et inconfusae, et duae manifeste cognoscendae, et non confusibiliter copulatae, his
  penitus **non** erant frustratae, et in una subsistentia monstrabantur.*
- **English as it stands:** `were wholly deprived of these [?], and were shown in one hypostasis.`
- **Proposed:** `were not at all deprived of these [?], and were shown in one hypostasis.` (with the crux recording that the English follows
  Migne's *L.* note, οὐχ ἐστέρηντο, against the printed ἀπεστέρηντο, and that the Latin version agrees)
- **Why:** The sentence is the ground (γάρ) for "two forms operating in common, each according to its own natural property": natures that stay
  unchanged and unconfused cannot be "wholly deprived" of their natural operations. The printed reading reverses the argument; Migne's *L.*
  (*lege*) is the editor's own correction on the same page, and the facing Latin has *non*. The translator saw the trouble (`[?]`) but rendered
  the reversed reading in the running text.
- **Confidence:** probable (certain that the printed reading reverses the sense; the choice to print the *L.* reading is an editorial call for Wilson)
- **Already in the cruces?** (see end)

### F4. 0003, col. 3172 [b: A–B] (s29): "or essential and natural" can be read as denying that the operations are natural
- **Class:** 3 (mis-parsed: an attribute of the rejected single operation read as a separate denial)
- **Greek (file only):** οὐδὲ μίαν καὶ μόνην αὐτῶν τὴν ἐνέργειαν λέγομεν, ἢ οὐσιώδη καὶ φυσικὴν, καὶ παντελῶς ἀπαράλλακτον, ἵνα μὴ καὶ εἰς
  οὐσίαν μίαν, καὶ φύσιν μίαν αὐτὰς συνελάσωμεν
- **Latin:** *neque unam solam earum operationem dicimus, sive essentialem, [et] naturalem penitusque indifferentem*
- **English as it stands:** `nor do we call their operation one and only, or essential and natural, and wholly without variation,`
- **Proposed:** `nor do we call their operation one and only — one essential and natural operation, and wholly without variation —`
- **Why:** What is rejected is ONE operation that is essential and natural (since that would entail one essence and one nature: the ἵνα μή
  clause). Sophronius affirms in s25 and s30 that each nature has its own operation, "the essential, I mean, and natural". The English "nor
  do we call their operation … essential and natural" reads, out of context, as denying the very thing he asserts two sentences on.
- **Confidence:** probable (the Greek ἢ is loose; the doctrinal sense is not)
- **Already in the cruces?** (see end)

### F5. 0004, col. 3181 [b: A] (s28): "incorruptible" silently adopts Migne's *L.* note against the printed ἄφραστον
- **Class:** 5 (wrong term relative to the printed text; no crux)
- **Greek (plate-read, p. 205, left col., line 2):** οὐ μήν ἐστι τὴν φύσιν ἀθάνατα, ἢ πρὸς οὐσίαν μετελήλυθεν ἄφραστον²⁶. Foot of the page,
  *Variae lectiones*: **²⁶ L. ἄφθαρτον.** The file prints ἄφραστον with NO `[n:]` (the note is listed in cruces.md only among the unplaced notes).
- **Latin:** *neque in essentiam incorruptibilem transeunt*
- **English as it stands:** `nor have they passed over to an incorruptible essence;`
- **Proposed:** keep the English and add a T crux: "Migne prints ἄφραστον, 'inexpressible'; 'incorruptible' follows his own foot-note 26, *L.* ἄφθαρτον,
  with the Latin *incorruptibilem*." Better still, place `[n: L. ἄφθαρτον.]` in the Greek at that point (it is note 26 on p. 205).
- **Why:** The English is right in sense (the argument is about corruption and death), but it departs from the printed word with no trace, the one thing
  the "translate the plate" rule forbids. Not a meaning error; an untraced one.
- **Confidence:** certain (plate-read)
- **Already in the cruces?** Only as an unplaced note ("p. 0205 (numbered, 26): L. ἄφθαρτον."). No crux on the English.

## Candidates considered and rejected

- **0001 s26 (3152D)** καὶ αὐτῷ γνωριζόμενον, "recognized also by him": the Latin *Deum ipsum agnoscendum* implies αὐτό. Plate-read p. 190: Migne
  prints αὐτῷ. The English follows the printed Greek, and the cruces log it (3152). Not a finding.
- **0001 s7 (3148C)** κριτὴς τῶν λεγομένων ἡμῖν προερχόμενος: the English uses ἡμῖν twice ("of what we say, coming forward for us"). It is redundant,
  not reversed.
- **0001 s16** ἣν ἡ πίστις συνδεῖ τοὺς ὁμόφρονας, "which faith binds together for those of one mind": the like-minded are what is bound, not who it is
  bound for. The nuance is soft and the bond is still faith's, so I did not report it. If F1 is fixed, "binds the like-minded together" would be closer.
- **0001 s42** μείνῃ Σαβέλλιον, "remain Sabellian" (variant σεμνύνῃ, "exalt Sabellius", in `[n:]`): a defensible rendering of the printed text, and
  the variant is placed.
- **0002 s8 (3157B)** ἕτερον καὶ ἕτερον, καὶ ἕτερον οὐ λέγεται, "it is not called another and another and another thing": the μέν/δέ contrast reads
  oddly with a negative, but the Latin has the same negative (*aliud et aliud et aliud non dicuntur*). It is the Nazianzen distinction: not ἄλλο
  καὶ ἄλλο, a different thing. The English follows. Not a finding.
- **0002 s4** ἢ τινα τὴν αὐτῆς διαιρεῖν συγχωροῦμεν ἑνότητα, "nor permit anyone to divide its oneness": τινα could also go with ἑνότητα ("any oneness").
  Either way it is a denial, so polarity is unaffected.
- **0003 s4** "Likewise also perfect in Godhead [?]": the clause "and perfect in manhood" is missing in the Greek, and Hardouin's note says so
  (`[n: Deest …]`). The English renders what is printed and flags it. Correct.
- **0003 s26** "just as, indeed, the being of the natures makes that of the natures": awkward, but it is literal for ὥσπερ δὴ καὶ τὸ εἶναι τὰς φύσεις, τῶν
  φύσεων (Latin *sicut et naturas esse naturarum*).
- **0004 s23 (3177D)** τὰ ὑμέτερα ἔργα, "our works": plate-read p. 203. Migne prints ὑμέτερα ("your"), and the Latin has *vere nostra gerens opera*.
  The English follows the sense (Christ underwent *our* works, not Sergius's), as the brief allows for a misprint. I found no crux for it. One line
  would help, given the ἡμεῖς/ὑμεῖς hazard class.
- **0004 s23** file text "ἐν αὐταῖς Ν ἀδιαιρέτως": the stray Ν is the marginal band letter D misread (the plate shows αὐταῖς, then D in the margin).
  Our-file artifact, and the English is unaffected.
- **0004 s17** the « opens at "Whenever thou hearest" in the English but at ἂν μέν in the Greek, which never closes it. This is quotation-mark placement,
  not meaning.
- **0004 s12** the miracle catalogue: I counted 29 items in the Greek, and all 29 are present in the English in order, including the two negated items
  (ἡ φθορᾶς οὐ τελευτῶσα κατάλυσις, ἡ θανάτου μὴ παυομένη καθαίρεσις).
- **Glossary items:** none reported (ἐνέργεια = operation, θέλησις = volition, βούλησις = counsel, Theotokos), per the brief's "do not report" list.
