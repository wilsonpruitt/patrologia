# Sophronius blind read, R2: chunks 0005, 0006, 0007 (2026-11-05)

## Findings

### F1. 0006, col. 3197 (band A–B): the negative of ἵνα μή does not reach the result clause (class 1/3)

- **Greek** (file only): Καὶ διὰ τοῦτο πρεσβεύω καὶ δέομαι, ἵνα μὴ βλάβην ταῦτα τὴν οἱανοῦν ὑπομένοντα παρὰ τὴν ἐμὴν
  ἀπειρίαν … ἀδράνειαν, ὡς αὐτὸς αὐτοῖς λυμηνάμενος ἐν ἡμέρᾳ τῆς κρίσεως κρίνωμαι, καὶ … οὐ τελευτῶσαν ὑφέξω τὴν κόλασιν.
- **Latin** (witness): *ut nullam hic grex noxam sustineat …, et tanquam ego ipse eum læsissem, in die judicii judicer, et
  … immortale tormentum suscipiam*. It is the same construction: everything after *ut nullam* falls under the prayer that it
  not happen.
- **English as it stands:** "so that I be judged on the day of judgement as myself having ravaged them, and undergo the unending punishment"
- **Proposed English:** "and so I be judged on the day of judgement as myself having ravaged them, and undergo the unending punishment"
  (with the sentence opening changed to match: "And for this reason I entreat and beseech that these may not suffer any
  harm whatever … and so I be judged …". Alternatively, recast the sentence as "lest these suffer any harm whatever …, and
  I be judged …".)
- **Why:** In the Greek the ὡς clause is the consequence of the harm, and the whole thing sits under ἵνα μή: he prays that
  the sheep NOT be harmed and that he NOT be judged as their ravager. After "I entreat … that these may not suffer harm",
  the English "so that I be judged" reads as the purpose of the entreaty. That makes him ask to be condemned.
- **Confidence:** probable. The Greek is not in doubt; the English is ambiguous at best.
- **Already in the cruces?** No (the 3197A entries are S/P notes only).

### F2. 0007, col. 3209 (band B): *Adamo tantum … verum Davidis quoque* rendered "To Adam alone" (class 1/5)

- **Latin** (plate-read, p. 219, left column; the plate prints *Adamo tantum primo generis nostri parenti, divinoque Moysi
  misere admodum hodierna luce assimilamur : verum Davidis quoque parentis sortem …*. There is no *non*, so our file is faithful to the plate.)
- **English as it stands:** "To Adam alone, the first parent of our race, and to divine Moses are we likened, wretchedly enough, on this day's light [?]; but we also express exactly, above all, the lot of David our forefather"
- **Proposed English:** "Not only to Adam, the first parent of our race, and to divine Moses are we likened, wretchedly enough, on this day's light [?]; but we also express exactly, above all, the lot of David our forefather"
- **Why:** "alone" contradicts its own sentence. It is followed at once by "and to divine Moses", and then by "but we also …
  David". The *tantum … verum … quoque* is the elliptical late-Latin "not only … but also" (the *non* is understood, not
  lost). The crux sees exactly this ("the *verum … quoque* expects 'not only to Adam'") but leaves "alone" in place, and
  "alone" asserts the opposite of the sentence's point. If a *non* must not be supplied, the fallback is "To Adam … and to
  divine Moses are we likened …", which drops "alone" and supplies nothing.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes, as a T note at 3209A (logged, image check requested). I have now done the image check:
  no negative on the plate. The English choice of "alone" is not discussed there.

### F3. 0007, passim: five of Migne's Scripture locators dropped and one moved (class 4)

The Latin source of 0007 carries 28 `[n:]` locators; the English carries 23. Exact list (file, confirmed for the 3207 and
3209–3210 ones on the plates, p. 218 notes 16–18 and p. 219 notes 31–35):
- col. 3203, *in terra pax [n: Luc. I, 14.] hominibus bonæ voluntatis [n: Luc. II, 14.]*: the English keeps only Luc. II, 14.
  (The first is Migne's own mis-locator. It may have been dropped on purpose, but nothing says so.)
- col. 3207, *in regnum cœlorum … qui est in cœlo [n: Matth. VII, 51.]*: **absent** after "who is in heaven.»".
- col. 3207, *mandata mea servate [n: Joan. XIV, 12.]*: **absent** after "keep my commandments.»".
- col. 3210, *salientis in vitam æternam [n: Joan. IV, 10 seqq.]*: **absent** after "springing up into life eternal.»".
- col. 3210, the first *[n: II Reg. XXIII, 15.]* stands in the Latin after David's words *O si quis mihi daret potum aquæ …
  juxta portam* (p9 s1). In the English it has moved back to p8 s27 ("…from the cistern that was in Bethlehem. [n: II Reg. XXIII, 15.]"),
  and the quotation itself ("…in Bethlehem by the gate!»", first occurrence) now has none.
- col. 3211, *attulerunt ad David [n: II Reg. XXIII, 15.]* … *animarum periculum bibam [n: ibid. 17.]*: the English has a
  single "[n: II Reg. XXIII, 15.]" after "the peril of their souls?»", where the Latin has *ibid. 17*. After "brought it to David.»" there is none.
- **Proposed:** restore each `[n:]` at its Latin point, verbatim: "who is in heaven.» [n: Matth. VII, 51.]"; "keep my
  commandments.» [n: Joan. XIV, 12.]"; "springing up into life eternal.» [n: Joan. IV, 10 seqq.]"; move the p8 s27
  locator to follow the first "…in Bethlehem by the gate!»"; "brought it to David.» [n: II Reg. XXIII, 15.]"; and change the
  final one to "[n: ibid. 17.]". For Luc. I, 14, either restore it or log a deliberate omission. The cruces S notes already
  carry the corrected references (Matt 7:21, John 14:15, 2 Sam 23:16–17).
- **Why:** these are Migne's apparatus, and the brief says it is set into the English. The index harvests scripture from `[n:]`,
  so these citations are lost from it. No meaning is reversed.
- **Confidence:** certain for the count; probable that the omissions are unintended.
- **Already in the cruces?** The references are glossed as S notes at 3207 and 3211. The omission of the `[n:]` markers is
  not logged.

## Candidates considered and rejected

- 0005 col. 3184/3185, ἐνεδρεῦσαν ("lay in ambush") rendered "sat [?]". Translator-flagged; ἑδρεῦσαν is the evident sense. Not a reversal.
- 0005 col. 3188, ᾗ καὶ τὰ δυοκαίδεκα συνῆπται κεφάλαια ("to which the twelve chapters are also joined"). The ᾗ is
  singular (the third letter); the English "to which" could be read as both letters. It is ambiguous but not wrong; rejected.
- 0005 col. 3192, "Origen, another Origen, also called Adamantius". This matches Ὠριγένης, Ὠριγένης ἕτερος ὁ καὶ Ἀδαμάντιος as printed. Not a finding.
- 0005/0006, the `[n:]` Greek variants (κεκτῆσθαι; Ἐλκεσαῖος; ἐνωτικόν; φύσει vel σφίσι; the Hardouin notes) have no English
  twin. By the brief, a Greek-side `[n:]` variant is not a finding, and the English follows the sense (Elchasai).
  "Kenoticon" is logged as P at 3192 as Sophronius's pun.
- 0006 col. 3193, ἀλλήλοις μὲν φησι φιλικῶς συμφερόμενοι, "borne along with one another, as it were, in friendly fashion".
  "Borne along" is weak for συμφερόμενοι ("agreeing"), but the antithesis with "wounded by one another" survives. Logged at 3193A; rejected.
- 0006 col. 3196, τὰς μετὰ τούτους ὀνομασθείσας … αἱρέσεις, "the heresies … named after them". The English could be read as
  "eponymous", but the Greek means "listed after". It is ambiguous, not reversed. A possible polish: "named after these".
- 0006 col. 3200, νέμειν ποθούμενα γράμματα, "to bring letters longed for". Acceptable.
- 0007 col. 3203, *cum lætitia et gaudium Christi natalitia exceperint*. Fine.
- 0007 col. 3207, *Agarenorum arcam confringemus* rendered "break the bow". The plate (p. 218) prints *arcam*, which is Migne's
  misprint for *arcum*, and the English follows the sense. The cruces' plate table logs *arcam* as M. Not a finding.
- 0007 col. 3209, *arens ardensque … bibere desiderabat?* The Latin has a question mark; the English makes it a statement.
  The sentence is narrative, and the "?" is evidently Migne's misprint for a full stop. Rejected.
- 0007 col. 3206, *vel inviti et nocentes*, "even unwilling and guilty". Acceptable.
- 0007 col. 3202 and following: the English segments several notes slightly before or after the Latin point (e.g. Psal.
  LXXXIV after the third verse, Gen. III, 19 moved to the "Earth thou art" quotation, where it belongs). These are not counted
  as findings. Only the outright drops and the one shift are in F3.
