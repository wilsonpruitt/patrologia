## Findings

### F1. Chunk 0009, col. 3237 D (Or. II §18, end)
- **Class:** 3 / 5 (mis-parsed participle; Marian term turned)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0233):** καὶ τοῦτον κυοφοροῦσα καὶ τίκτουσα καὶ Θεοῦ Μήτηρ ἐκ πάσης γεγεννημένη τῆς κτίσεως.
- **Latin:** et sola ex omnibus creaturis Dei mater effecta es.
- **English as it stands:** and Mother of God, born out of all creation.
- **Proposed English:** and having become Mother of God out of all creation.
- **Why:** γεγεννημένη is the common Byzantine double-ν for γεγενημένη ("having become"), the last of a chain of participles (receiving, bearing, giving birth, *becoming* Mother of God). "Born out of all creation" makes the Virgin a product of the whole creation, a sense the sentence does not carry; the Latin *effecta es* confirms the reading. The plate prints γεγεννημένη, so the double ν is Migne's spelling (not an OCR error); the English should give the sense.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes, logged at 3237D: the translator plate-checked γεγεννημένη and rendered it "born" *as printed*, noting that the Latin read γεγενημένη. So the reading is known, but the English follows the letter, not the sense. The brief says Migne's misprints stay in the Greek "and the English follows the sense". Here the sense is "having become", so I recommend the change. (Double ν for γεν- is this plate's habit; see F2, where it is provable from the answering member.)

### F2. Chunk 0011, col. 3253 C (Or. II §30)
- **Class:** 3 / 1 (contrast flattened; the antithesis is lost)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0241):** «Οὗτος ἔσται μέγας·» οὐχ ὡς μὴ μέγας ὢν, μετέπειτα δὲ γεννησόμενος, ἀλλ᾿ ὡς ἀεὶ μέγας ὢν, καὶ μετὰ ταῦτα δὲ γενησόμενος.
- **Latin:** non instar illius, qui, magnus cum non sit, magnus postea sit evasurus, sed tanquam qui semper magnus et est et deinceps permanebit.
- **English as it stands:** not as one who is not great, and afterwards will be born, but as one who is always great, and after these things also will become so.
- **Proposed English:** not as one who is not great and afterwards will become so, but as one who is always great, and after these things also will become so.
- **Why:** The two members are built in strict parallel (μὴ μέγας ὢν … μετέπειτα δὲ γ-ησόμενος / ἀεὶ μέγας ὢν … μετὰ ταῦτα δὲ γενησόμενος). The point is that "shall be great" does not mean he *becomes* great after not being great. "Will be born" breaks the parallel and leaves the denied clause ("not as one who is not great, and will be born") without a meaning. The double ν is the plate's spelling habit (cf. F1), and the Latin *magnus postea sit evasurus* confirms the sense.
- **Confidence:** probable (near certain on sense).
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes, logged at 3253A (the band should be C, per the plate). The translator kept "will be born" *as printed* after a plate check. The objection is the same as for F1: the English should follow the sense, and the crux already holds the evidence.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **0011, 3253 C, πάντως καὶ τὴν σὴν ἕξει κατ᾿ οὐσίαν ταυτότητα → "identity in essence with thee".** At first this looked like a slip for "with him", since the clause follows "connatural with God the begetter". Rejected: the plate prints σήν, and the Latin *eamdem tecum substantiam habebit* agrees. This is the double consubstantiality (with the Father and with his mother), and the English is right.
- **0011, 3256 B, μὴ χοϊκὴν λαχὼν τὴν ἐκλόχευσιν, καὶ χοϊκὴν φορῶν ταπεινότητα.** Considered whether the English lets μή govern only the first participle. The English "who has not been allotted … and borne" carries the negative over both, as the argument needs. Rejected.
- **0010, 3240 C, στέλλων "when he sent me".** The subject and object are supplied from context, correctly. Rejected.
- **0010, 3241 A, καὶ εἰς τῶν ἀνθρώπων.** The file has εἰς where εἷς is meant ("as one of men"). The English already gives the sense. Not a finding.
- **0010, 3241 A, πάντα σου κατόπιν … δευτερεύει; καὶ τίς εὐθὺς οὐχ ἡττηθήσεται, καὶ μὴ μᾶλλον … ἀφήσει.** In the English the rhetorical negative carries over both verbs. Rejected.
- **0008, 3220 A, πάντα μερισμὸν καὶ συζυγίαν ἀρνούμενον → "every pairing".** συζυγία is fixed by the glossary. Rejected.
- **0008, 3224 B, Οὐ γὰρ τῆς Τριάδος ὁ σταυρὸς … τὴν φιλάνθρωπον σάρκωσιν (accusative with no governing verb).** The English construes it loosely ("the Son incarnate with a man-loving incarnation"), but the polarity and the referent are right: the cross belongs to the incarnate One of the Trinity, not to the Trinity. Rejected.
- **0009, 3237 D, ἠνεύρωσας "unnerved" (Cod. ἐνεύρωσας).** The English follows Migne's text, which agrees with the Latin *enervasti*. Rejected.
