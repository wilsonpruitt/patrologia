## Findings

### F1. 0012, col. 3273 [b: B], ⟦p7⟧ ⟦s7⟧. Class 1 (polarity: negative not carried to its second member)

- **Greek** (file only; the point is the English, not the Greek): `καὶ εἰς ἑνὸς προσώπου καὶ μιᾶς ὑποστάσεως συνδρομὴν οὐ τεμνομένην συνθέουσα, οὐδὲ μὴν τὰ ἑνούμενα τρέπουσαν, καὶ εἰς οὐσίας ταυτότητα φέρουσαν.`
- **Latin** (facing, col. 3274): `in unius personæ atque unius hypostasis indivisibilem conveniens unionem, quæ sane neque connexas naturas immutat, neque in unius substantiæ identitatem adducit.`
- **English as it stands:** `nor indeed changing the things united, and bearing them into identity of essence.`
- **Proposed English:** `nor indeed changing the things united, nor bearing them into identity of essence.`
- **Why:** οὐδὲ μήν governs both participles of the concourse (τρέπουσαν … καὶ … φέρουσαν); the English "nor X, and bearing them into identity of essence" reads the second member as an affirmation, i.e. that the union brings the natures into one essence: the Eutychian position the whole sentence denies. The Latin reads the negative across both (*neque … neque*).
- **Confidence:** certain.
- **Already in the cruces?** No (checked after: the 0012 section has nothing at 3273B; the only "identity … essence" crux, at 3253A, is a different passage).

### F2. 0013, col. 3284 [b: A], ⟦p5⟧ ⟦s6⟧. Class 2 (whose: a pronoun drawn to the wrong referent)

- **Greek** (file only): `ἐκείνους δὲ τοὺς μιαροὺς καὶ παράφρονας ἔξω διώκει ταυτησὶ τῆς εὐαγγελίου αὐτῶν πανηγύρεως, ὡς ἀσεβῶς αὐτὰ νοοῦντας ἐκείνους καὶ λέγοντας.`
- **Latin** (facing): `scd a solemni lætitia felicis eorum annuntiationis sceleratos illos ac stultos foras expellunt, quippe qui impie de iisdem et sentiunt et loquuntur.`
- **English as it stands:** `but drive those foul and mad ones out of this festival of their good tidings, as men who understand and speak of them impiously.`
- **Proposed English:** `but drive those foul and mad ones out of this festival of these good tidings, as men who understand and speak of them impiously.`
- **Why:** αὐτῶν is neuter, the εὐαγγέλια that are the subject of the sentence (the same referent as αὐτά in the next clause); standing right after "those foul and mad ones", English "their" is read as the heretics' good tidings, which inverts the point (the heretics are driven out of the Church's feast, they have none of their own).
- **Confidence:** probable (the English is ambiguous rather than flatly wrong, but its natural reading is the wrong one).
- **Already in the cruces?** No.

### F3. 0014, col. 3294 [b: A]–C, ⟦p16⟧ (§ 10). Class 5 (Scripture locators swapped)

- **Latin** (source, file only): at the first *Nunc dimittis* `in pace [n: Luc. ii, 29.]`; at the full canticle closing § 10 `et gloriam plebis tuæ Israel [n: ibid. 31.]`.
- **English as it stands:** `«Now thou lettest thy servant depart, O Lord, according to thy word, in peace.» [n: Luc. ii, 31.]` and, closing the paragraph, `and the glory of thy people Israel.» [n: Luc. ii, 29.]`
- **Proposed English:** `«Now thou lettest thy servant depart, O Lord, according to thy word, in peace.» [n: Luc. ii, 29.]` and `and the glory of thy people Israel.» [n: ibid. 31.]`
- **Why:** the two locators have traded places: the English sends Luke 2:29 (the verse quoted) to 2:31 and the canticle ending at 2:32 to 2:29. Migne's own sequence is right and the English should carry it as printed. In the same paragraph Migne's `[n: ibid. 30.]` at «For,» he says, «mine eyes have seen thy Salvation.» is also absent from the English (see F4).
- **Confidence:** certain (as a file-to-file comparison; the plate was not opened for this, since the finding is not about Migne's text).
- **Already in the cruces?** No.

### F4. 0014, passim. Class 4 (correct Migne Scripture locators dropped, with no crux)

The English carries 29 of the Latin source's 40 `[n:]` locators. Some of the missing ones are Migne's wrong locators, and the cruces give the right reference as an S note (Rom. i, 16 at 3299C → S Rom 10:4; Matth. x, 6 → S Matt 10:16; Psal. xl, 11 → S Ps 45:11). That looks like a deliberate convention and I do not report it. **These, though, are correct as printed, and they are dropped without any crux:**
- § 7, 3292: `uti vitæ præsentis vinculis viso Christo Simeon [n: Luc. ii, 31.]` (English: `as Simeon, when he had seen Christ, from the bonds of the present life.` has no locator);
- § 10, 3294: `[n: ibid. 30.]` at «mine eyes have seen thy Salvation»;
- § 12, 3296: `quia mirabilia fecit [n: Ibid.]` (English `«Let us sing to the Lord a new song: for he has done wonderful things»` has no locator);
- § 14, 3297: `in pace [n: Luc. ii, 29.]` after the second *Nunc dimittis*, and `Israel [n: ibid. 50. …]` (= ii, 30–32) after the canticle;
- § 18, 3299: `[n: Psal. xcvii, 2.]`; § 19, 3300: `[n: Luc. iii, 7.]` (a misprint, probably for ii, 30). The cruces have no S note for either.
- **Proposed:** carry each `[n:]` as printed at its point in the English (Pattern: markers sacred).
- **Confidence:** probable that this is an omission and not a ruling. Nothing in the brief or the cruces says Migne's locators may be dropped.
- **Already in the cruces?** No, apart from the three S notes named above.

### F5. 0014, col. 3300 [b: D]–3301 [b: A], ⟦p28⟧ (§ 20). Class 2 (who acts: the dove made the subject of *exhibuit*)

- **Latin** (source, file only): `Id persuadeat illa peramabilis columba, ad Noe in arcam rediens, oleæ ramum ore afferens, et misericordiam declarans Domini diluviique finem, mystice vero, ut Patri Spirituique videbatur, benignum Christi adventum, quem extremis temporibus per amorem misericordiæ vere plenum, exhibuit.`
- **English as it stands:** `showing forth the kindly coming of Christ, which in the last times, through a love truly full of mercy, it showed.`
- **Proposed English:** `showing forth the kindly coming of Christ, which in the last times, through a love truly full of mercy, he showed.`
- **Why:** the dove declares (*declarans*) the coming mystically, in Noah's day. The one who *exhibuit* the coming "in the last times", out of merciful love, is Christ (or God). The relative clause has its own subject. With "it", the dove itself brings the Incarnation about in the last times. The next sentence keeps the dove to prophecy (*prænuntiavit*).
- **Confidence:** probable (*exhibuit* has no expressed subject).
- **Already in the cruces?** No (the 3301 crux is about *vit*/*vitæ* only).

## Candidates considered and rejected

- 0012 § 38 `Οὐκ ἔσται μοι τοῦτο, καθὼς ἔφησας, ἔφησα`: the English puts "as thou hast said" inside the rejected quotation. That is a defensible reading, and the polarity holds.
- 0012 § 36 `οὐ γυμνὸν ἀνθρωπότητος, καθὰ ἐκ Πατρὸς … γεγέννηται`: "not bare of manhood, as he has been begotten eternally from the Father" can be misread in English, but the sense is the Greek's.
- 0012 § 40 end `τὴν ἐκ σοῦ καὶ μονῆς θαυμαστὴν ἐπιδείξεται`: the Greek lacks its noun, and the translator flags it with [?]. Rejected: it is already flagged.
- 0013 § 46 `ἀλλ᾿ ἐν τῷ [Nic. ἐν αὐτῷ τῷ], τὸ εἶναι σὰρξ`: "in its being flesh … it was also allotted". The "at the same moment" sense is weakened but not lost.
- 0013 § 47 `(Νεστόριοι … συνόμιλοι)`: an odd parenthesis in our Greek file. The English names the Nestoriuses, so nothing is lost.
- 0014 § 5 `Voluit enim procedere duntaxat natum ex Virgine`: "the one born of the Virgin alone go forth" is ambiguous ("Virgin alone" could be read as orthodox). The intended reading is available. A possible polish, not a finding.
- 0014 § 7 `Genitori Patrique lucis` rendered as "Father of lights". This is a plural for a singular (the Jas 1:17 echo). Minor, not a polarity point.
- 0014 § 11 `adorandum esse` (the Arians decreeing Christ "to be adored according to both natures"): the theology is odd, and I checked it at the plate (above). It stands as printed, and the English follows it.
- 0014 § 13 `apparentes nos jam veteris acidique fermenti`: a word is missing in the Latin (e.g. *expertes*). The translator flags it with [?]. Already flagged.
- 0014 `[nt:]` prose notes (18 in the source, 0 in the English): these are deliberate. The cruces section "Migne's notes to the Latin-only orations, in English" carries a working translation of each of the 18. I note only that English 0007 does carry one `[nt:]` inline, so the treatment differs between the Latin-only chunks.
- Dropped `[n:]` locators that are Migne's own errors (Rom. i, 16; Matth. x, 6; Psal. xl, 11): covered by S notes in the cruces, so treated as a convention (see F4).
