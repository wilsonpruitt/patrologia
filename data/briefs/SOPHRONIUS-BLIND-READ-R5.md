## Findings

### F1. 0016, cols 3317–3322 (bands D–A): five of Migne's notes dropped and three moved in the English
- **Class:** 4 (missing text) / 5 (a locator attached to the wrong clause)
- **The Latin (source, file only):** the Latin carries seven `[n:]` notes; the English carries four.
  - Dropped from the English: `[n: Vivi tempore judicii in ip… rituros, sed tantum immutand… … continut, non mo…]`
    (after *in momento*, col. 3318, an editor's doctrinal note on the living at the judgment); `[n: Psal. xlvi, 2.]`
    (after *festum celebrantium*, end of the second Michael prayer); `[n: Luc. i, 28.]` (after *imbuisti*, the
    Annunciation); `[n: Esther. xiii, 10.]` (after *solo nutu condidisti*); `[n: Psal. viii, 6.]` (after *modicum quid ab
    angelis diminutum*).
  - Moved in the English: `[n: Ezech. i, 1 seqq.]` stands after the Elisabeth clause (*"and didst make it fruitful,
    [n: Ezech. i, 1 seqq.]"*), not after *Ezekiel at Chobar*; `[n: Luc. i, 36.]` stands after the Annunciation to Mary,
    not after the Elisabeth clause; `[n: Gen. i, 26, 27.]` stands at the end of the long Fall sentence (*"brought forth.
    [n: Gen. i, 26, 27.]"*), not after *image and likeness*.
- **The English as it stands:** (a) `Thou who didst open to Ezekiel at Chobar that wholly divine vision, dissolve the stain of my barrenness.`
  (b) `and didst make it fruitful, [n: Ezech. i, 1 seqq.] [3319] flood my unhappy`
  (c) `the Theotokos Mary, [n: Luc. i, 36.] and didst transform`
  (d) `from which he had first been taken up and brought forth. [n: Gen. i, 26, 27.] But thou, Lord`
- **Proposed English:** put each note back where the Latin sets it:
  (a) `Thou who didst open to Ezekiel [n: Ezech. i, 1 seqq.] at Chobar that wholly divine vision, dissolve the stain of my barrenness.`
  (b) `and didst make it fruitful, [3319] flood my unhappy` with `[n: Luc. i, 36.]` placed after `from the bond by which it was held,`
  (c) `the Theotokos Mary, [n: Luc. i, 28.] and didst transform`
  (d) `brought forth. But thou, Lord` with `[n: Gen. i, 26, 27.]` placed after `according to thine image and likeness,`
  plus: `[n: Psal. viii, 6.]` after `made him a little less than the angels,`; `[n: Esther. xiii, 10.]` after `by thy nod alone;`;
  `[n: Psal. xlvi, 2.]` after `the sound of those keeping festival.»`; and the editor's note on the living at the judgment
  after `die in a moment,` (as `[nt: …]` per the work's convention for prose notes, carrying the lacunae as printed).
- **Why:** the notes appear to have slid one slot forward through the Gabriel paragraph, so Ezekiel's locator now glosses
  Elisabeth and Elisabeth's glosses the Annunciation, and five notes are simply gone. A reader following the locators is
  sent to the wrong verse. The prose of the translation is not affected.
- **Confidence:** certain (a count of the file; no plate needed, since the claim is about our English against our source).
- **Already in the cruces?** No. The 0016 cruces carry S. lines for these verses in prose (Ezek 1:1–3 and Luke 1:7–24 at
  3318, Gen 1:26 and Ps 8:6 at 3321), so the translator knew the references. But nothing records that the `[n:]` markers
  themselves were dropped or moved, and the 3318 S. line sits under "it was held, and didst make it fruitful", which is
  the moved position.

### F2. 0017, col. 3325 (band A→B), Or. VII §3: a stray negative left standing on the conception of Christ
- **Class:** 1 (polarity) / 3 (mis-parse)
- **The Greek (plate-read, p-0277, left column, the line `ἑνὸς μήτηρ ἀπειρόγαμος ἔσεσθαι οὐ συνελήφθη βε-|βαίως`):**
  `ἵνα τὴν θαυμαστήν σου θεωρήσασα γέννησιν τὸ τῆς Τριάδος ἑνὸς μήτηρ ἀπειρόγαμος ἔσεσθαι οὐ συνελήφθη βεβαίως τε καὶ ἀραρότως πιστεύσειεν;`
  The plate does print `οὐ` with a smooth breathing, so our file is faithful to Migne. It is not an OCR fault.
- **The Latin (witness):** `ut admirabilem ortum tuum videret et firmiorem atque gratiorem ex praesentia carperet fidem intemerata mater illa, quae conjugii expers, unum e Trinitate editura fuit?`. The Latin has no negative and nothing for `συνελήφθη`.
- **The English as it stands:** `that, having beheld thy wonderful birth, she might believe firmly and surely that she, the unwedded one, would be mother of One of the Trinity — was not conceived [?] —?`
- **Proposed English:** `that, having beheld thy wonderful birth, she might believe firmly and surely that she, the unwedded one, would be mother of the One of the Trinity who had been conceived [?]?`
  The cruces line should then say that the plate prints `οὐ`, read here as Migne's misprint for a relative (`οὗ`/`ὃς`).
- **Why:** `οὐ συνελήφθη` does not construe. Left in, "was not conceived" stands in the English as a bare denial beside the
  Virgin's motherhood of One of the Trinity, which is the polarity a reader will take away. The sentence needs the sense,
  as the brief allows: Mary had conceived before the Visitation (Luke 1:31, 39), and the clause is about her faith in what
  she already carried. Migne's `οὐ` for a relative is a one-letter misprint (a breathing and an accent).
- **Confidence:** probable (the rendering of a misprint; the plate reading itself is certain).
- **Already in the cruces?** Yes, as a doubt, at **3325A**: "Kept as printed, negative included, pending the plate". The
  plate has now been read and it prints `οὐ`, so the open item can be closed one way or the other. The translator
  suggested `ὃν συνέλαβε?`, and I agree that a relative stands behind it.

### F3. 0016, col. 3321 (band C), the closing prayer: "the serpent and his wife"
- **Class:** 2 (who does what)
- **The Latin (source, file only):** `dum a serpente, et uxore captus, atque seductus, divina tua mandata praevaricatus est`
- **The English as it stands:** `until, taken and seduced by the serpent and his wife, he transgressed thy divine commandments`
- **Proposed English:** `until, taken and seduced by the serpent and by his own wife, he transgressed thy divine commandments`
- **Why:** in English "his wife" attaches most easily to the nearest masculine noun, the serpent, so the reader is given a
  wife of the serpent. `uxore` is Adam's wife (Eve). One word removes the ambiguity.
- **Confidence:** probable (it is a matter of how the English reads; the Latin itself is unambiguous)
- **Already in the cruces?** No.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **0015 p1 s21** `καὶ τί εἰπεῖν ἀπορῶ τὸ μυστήριον;` → "why am I at a loss to tell the mystery?" `τί` could be the object
  of `εἰπεῖν` ("I am at a loss what to say of the mystery"). The Latin takes it as "why" too (`quid cunctor mysterium
  edicere?`), the polarity is unaffected, and the English is a defensible reading. Rejected.
- **0015 p2 s32** `θεάμονας ἀξίους καὶ προσκυνητὰς τῇ ἀκαταμαχήτῳ ἐξουσίᾳ` → "worshippers, by the invincible authority".
  The dative could be the object ("worshippers of the invincible authority"). The Latin reads it instrumentally
  (`inexpugnabili potestate subnixi`), so the English is supported. Rejected.
- **0015 p1 s3** the English sets `[b: C]` where the Greek has `[b: D]` before «τὸ καθ᾿ ἡμῶν χειρόγραφον». This is a band
  marker, which the brief excludes. Noted only.
- **0015 p2 s2** "All physicians" follows the printed `Οἱ τῶν ἰατρῶν πάντες`, which Migne's own note calls a mistake for
  `παῖδες`. The English follows the printed text, and the note is Migne's variant. Not a finding.
- **0015 p2 s17** `μικρὸν ἀναθέντες τῆς ἡμῶν ταλαιπωρίας` → "having offered up a little of our hardship". The phrase is
  obscure, but no polarity or agent is at stake. Rejected.
- **0017 §1–2** `οὐκέτι οὐδεὶς ὡς οὐχ ὅλῳ κηρύξειε` (a triple negative) → "there is no longer anyone who would not proclaim
  it with his whole mouth". Correct. `μὴ τῇ θειοτέρᾳ σου βοῇ … μὴ … δεχόμενοι` → "if it does not / unless". Correct.
- **0017 §3** `δι᾿ οὗ σε Χριστὸς ἐκαθάρισε` → "through which Christ cleansed thee". This is what the Greek says, so it is not
  an inversion.
- **0017 §6** `ἣν ἑαυτῷ τῷ Θεῷ καὶ Πατρὶ … προσήγαγεν` → "offered to himself, to God and the Father". This is literal, and it
  is orthodox (the offerer who also receives). Rejected.
- **0017 §4** `ὃ μεγέθους ὑπερβολὴν οὐ δεχόμενον` → "admitting no excess beyond its greatness". Opaque, but the negative is
  kept. Rejected.
- **0018 §8** `ἀλλ᾿ ὅτι μηδὲ … πεπίστευκεν` → "but because he did not believe". The "not even" force of `μηδέ` is softened,
  but the polarity is unchanged. Too slight to report.
- **0018 §7** `Πρόδρομος γίγνεται τῆς Ἰουδαϊκῆς ἀπιστίας, καὶ νυκτὸς πόῤῥω καταλείπων τὰ μυστήρια` → "Forerunner, leaving
  far behind the mysteries of the Jewish unbelief and of night". The translator re-groups the genitive with
  `καταλείπων`. Taken with `Πρόδρομος` as printed it would make John "forerunner of the Jewish unbelief", which the
  context excludes. The re-grouping is right. Rejected.
