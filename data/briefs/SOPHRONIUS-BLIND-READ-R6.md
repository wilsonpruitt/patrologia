# Sophronius blind read, R6: chunks 0019–0021 (2026-11-05)

Brief: `SOPHRONIUS-BLIND-READ-BRIEF.md`. Range: 0019 (Or. VII §§10–15, cols 3337–3345), 0020 (Or. VII §§16–20, cols
3345–3353), 0021 (On Peter and Paul §§1–6 plus the John the Theologian excerpts, cols 3356–3364). I read every sentence of the
Greek against the English and checked each negative. I used the Latin as a witness only. I opened `cruces.md` only after the

## Findings

### F1. 0019, §10, col 3337B. Class 3 (attributive for predicative)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0283):** Οὐκοῦν μέγας ὁ ἱερεὺς καὶ μυστικῶς ἀπιστεῖ, καὶ μυστικώτερον ἀφαιρεῖται
- **Latin:** *Igitur sacerdos magnus mystice diffidit* (ambiguous)
- **English as it stands:** `So then the great priest both disbelieves mystically,`
- **Proposed:** `So then the priest is great, and he disbelieves mystically,`
- **Why:** μέγας stands outside the article, so it is the predicate. The sentence draws the conclusion of the argument before it
  (his ecstasy was like Isaac's): Zacharias is great, and even his unbelief is mystical. "The great priest" turns the
  claim into a stock epithet and drops it.
- **Confidence:** probable, leaning certain.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F2. 0019, §10, col 3337C. Class 2 (whose inferiority)
- **Greek (file only):** εἰ γὰρ μὴ οὕτως τις τοῦ πρεσβύτου τὴν ἀπιστίαν νοεῖν ἐθελήσειεν, ἔσται πολὺ τῆς Ἐλισάβετ ἐν ἀρεταῖς ἐλαττούμενος
- **Latin:** *multo minoris virtutis erit Zacharias quam Elisabeth*
- **English as it stands:** `he will be much inferior in virtues to Elisabeth.`
- **Proposed:** `the old man will be much inferior in virtues to Elisabeth.`
- **Why:** In the English, "he" has only one possible antecedent, "anyone". That makes the reader who misunderstands the
  inferior one, which makes no sense. The sense (and the Latin) is that Zacharias would fall below Elisabeth, who believed
  without having been told anything (next sentence).
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F3. 0019, §13, col 3341C/D. Class 2/3 (participle attached to the wrong subject)
- **Greek (plate-read, p-0285, left column):** ἐβόα γὰρ τοῖς σκιρτήμασιν ὡς πάρεστιν ὁ δεσμῶν ἡμᾶς τοὺς ἀνθρώπους λυτρούμενος· καὶ τὸ σκιρτᾷν ἡμῖν ὡς ἐκ δεσμῶν ἀνειμένων δωρούμενος ἠγαλλία κράζων τῷ Πνεύματι
  On the plate a wide space follows δωρούμενος before ἠγαλλία, in the position of a stop that is lost or faint. Our file
  runs the two words together.
- **Latin:** *illum adesse qui homines liberaret a vinculis et nos a vinculis solutos exsilire sineret. Exsultavit clamans spiritu*
- **English as it stands:** `for by his leapings he cried that he was present who redeems us men from bonds; and, granting us to leap as men loosed from bonds, he exulted, crying out in the Spirit`
- **Proposed:** `for by his leapings he cried that he was present who redeems us men from bonds, and grants us to leap as men loosed from bonds; he exulted, crying out in the Spirit`
- **Why:** δωρούμενος pairs with λυτρούμενος: Christ redeems and Christ grants the leaping (Isa 35:6). ἠγαλλία then opens one of
  the asyndetic run of John's actions (ἤπλου, ἐξέτεινεν, ἵστατο). The English makes John the giver of the gift. The gap on the
  plate supports a break after δωρούμενος.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F4. 0020, §20, col 3353B. Class 2/3 (relative pronoun given the wrong antecedent)
- **Greek (file only):** …καὶ σὺν σοὶ πρὸς τὴν φίλην χωρεῖν οὐκ ἀφίησιν ἔρημον· ὃν κἂν πρὸς βραχὺ οὔτε σοὶ ποθητὸν, οὔτ᾿ ἐμοὶ δυνατὸν, οὔτε τῇ σῇ πανηγύρει πρέπον καθέστηκε
- **Latin:** *Si brevius dixerimus quam ipse desideras, quam possumus et haec festivitas expostulat, veniam nobis concede.*
- **English as it stands:** `to the beloved desert; which, even for a short while, is neither desired by thee,`
- **Proposed:** `to the beloved desert; and to leave it, the time of the liturgy, even for a short while, is neither desired by thee,`
- **Why:** ὃν is masculine. It cannot be ἔρημος, which is feminine. In the English, "which" sits right after "the beloved
  desert", so the reader hears that the desert John loves "is neither desired by thee", which contradicts the sentence. The
  crux names καιρός as the antecedent, but the English does not show it. λόγος (τῷ λόγῳ at the start of the sentence) is the
  other masculine candidate, and the Latin's "if we speak more briefly…" points that way. Either antecedent rules out
  "desert". The translator should choose, but the English must not leave the desert as the referent.
- **Confidence:** probable that the current English misleads. The exact replacement is open.
- **In the cruces?** Partly. Line 494 records καιρός as the nearest masculine and calls the clause elliptical. It does not
  record that the English, as worded, attaches the clause to the desert.

### F5. 0021, §4, col 3360C. Class 5 (term turned: mutual love rendered as love of self)
- **Greek (file only):** ὅθεν καὶ πρὸ πάντων τὴν εἰς αὐτοὺς ἀγάπην ἐκτενῶς ἔχειν ἡμᾶς ἐγκελεύεται
- **Latin:** *unde et ante omnia mutuam impense fovere charitatem mandat*
- **English as it stands:** `he bids us to have fervently the love towards ourselves,`
- **Proposed:** `he bids us to have fervently the love towards one another,`
- **Why:** This is 1 Pet 4:8, τὴν εἰς ἑαυτοὺς ἀγάπην, where the reflexive means "mutual". The whole section is about love of the
  neighbour. "Love towards ourselves" reads as self-love, the opposite of the point.
- **Confidence:** certain.
- **In the cruces?** No.

### F6. 0021, John the Theologian excerpt, col 3364C/D. Class 2 (who is addressed)
- **Greek (file only):** καθὰ τὸν Ἰωσὴφ καὶ ἡ πάναγνος αὕτη καὶ πανάχραντος Παρθένος Θεομήτωρ προσαγορεύουσα φαίνεται … «Τέκνον γὰρ, φησὶ, καὶ ἐγὼ καὶ ὁ πατήρ σου…»
- **English as it stands:** `is seen to address Joseph,`
- **Proposed:** `is seen to name Joseph so,`
- **Why:** Here προσαγορεύω means "to call (him) by that title", that is, to call Joseph Jesus's father. The quotation shows this:
  she is speaking to the Child, not to Joseph. "Address Joseph" puts the wrong person on the receiving end of the speech.
- **Confidence:** probable.
- **In the cruces?** No.

- 0019 §15 end: τοῖς τοῦ Πνεύματος τέκνοις ὑμῖν. The English takes "children of the Spirit" as standing with "us". The Greek is
  ambiguous, and the Latin (*nobis filiis suis*) also takes it with "us". Rejected.
- 0019 §10: ἀνέλυσε and τὸ Ἰωάννου … γενέσθαι. Both are already flagged [?]. The English sense is sound.
- 0019 §12: Θεοῦ … προβαδίζειν, rendered "walk before God". It means to go ahead of God (Mal 3:1), but "before" carries that.
  Rejected.
- 0019 §13: "I have need of thee [?] to be baptized" (σοῦ without ὑπό). Already flagged. Rejected.
- 0020 §16: προϊεμένοις rendered "admit". This is a conjecture, logged (cruces 465). Rejected.
- 0020 §19: καταλειψομένην rendered "overtake". This is a conjecture, logged (cruces 493). Rejected.
- 0020 §18: εἰ μὴ πρὸς ἥττους rendered "since it is not with those inferior". The sense requires a causal negative, and the
  English gives it. Rejected.
- 0020 §18: the Moses clause (εἰ καὶ πλείω Μωϋσέως παιδεύεται…). I checked that its subject is John, not Moses. The English is
  right. Rejected.
- 0021 §4: "what is not done according to Christ our God knows how to provide not one benefit". This is a garden path but the
  meaning is correct. A comma after "Christ our God" would help. Style only. Rejected.
- 0021 §6: "the third festival after Christ's saving birth". The count includes the Nativity (title: the fourth day), and the
  English follows the Greek. Rejected.
- 0021 §4: πάντας ἡμᾶς τιμήσατε, "honour all of us". The Greek prints ἡμᾶς and the English follows it. Rejected.
