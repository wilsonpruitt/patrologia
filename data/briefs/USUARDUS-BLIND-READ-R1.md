## Findings

### F1: 9198/0002, col 0707 (29 Jan, Bouillart's note on the octave of St Vincent)
- **Class:** 3 (mis-parsed grammar: the subject of *celebraret*)
- **Latin:** `ut sodales in capitulo congregati lectione Martyrologii praemonerentur quae festa celebraret, quorum sanctorum memoriam die proxime sequenti coleret Ecclesia.`
- **English as it stands:** `should be forewarned by the reading of the Martyrology what feasts they were to celebrate, and the memory of which saints the Church would keep on the day next following.`
- **Proposed English:** `should be forewarned by the reading of the Martyrology what feasts the Church would celebrate, the memory of which saints it would keep on the day next following.`
- **Why:** *celebraret* is singular, and it shares its subject *Ecclesia* with *coleret*. The English moves it to the plural *sodales* ("they") and adds an "and" the Latin does not print. The sense changes very little.
- **Confidence:** probable. The grammar is certain. The stakes are low.
- **In the cruces?** No (checked afterwards: cruces-0000.md and cruces-0004.md).

No other findings. Every number, every negation and every commemoration count checked out in all four chunks.

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **0000, col 0599B, prologue.** *quam multos propriae negligentiae excessus* is rendered "excesses of my own negligence". I wondered whether *propriae* meant the martyrologies' own neglect. The sentence goes on *quos etiam purgari tali cupiebam officio*, and Usuardus's own lapses in keeping the feasts construe cleanly. The English is a defensible reading. Rejected.
2. **0000, col 0599C.** *sive ut condemnetur* differs from Augustine's usual *caveatur*. The English renders the printed text, as Pattern 7 requires. Not a defect.
3. **0001, col 0641, Jan 12 note.** *Colbertinos duos, unum R. R. P. P. Fuliensium, alterum Remigiano monasterio … dono datum*. I asked whether these are four codices or the two Colbertines described. *unum … alterum* makes them appositive to *duos*, and the English keeps that structure. Rejected.
4. **0001, col 0641, Jan 12, Satyrus.** "it at once fell down": the subject of *corruit* is the idol, and the English supplies "it" correctly. Rejected.
5. **0001, Jan 21.** *orantes ut urerentur obtinuerunt* ("praying that they might be burned, obtained it") looks like it asks the opposite of the expected sense. It is literal to the print (Pattern 7). Rejected.
6. **0001, col 0687.** The day head IX *Kal.* carries *Die* 23, where the day should be 24. The English follows `Die n` as printed, which is M1. Not a defect.
7. **0002, col 0709, Matthias.** *de quo mira, et plena fide narrant* is rendered "things wondrous and full of faith". *plenus* takes the ablative, so "full of faith" construes. Rejected.
8. **0002, col 0703, Jan 28 note.** *An non persaepe notum bis etiam usu venit*: the English drops *bis*. This is a garbled word inside a Bouillart note, rendered in its evident sense, which the brief lists as not a defect. Rejected.
9. **0002, col 0739, Feb 7 note.** *Sellerius* is rendered as "Sollerius". Same class as item 8. Rejected.
10. **0002, Feb 10, Sotheris.** *graviter et diutissime* is rendered "most heavily and very long". *graviter* is positive, not superlative, so this overstates slightly. It is register, not one of the five classes. I mention it only for the orchestrator's judgment.
11. **0002, col 0721/0723, Feb 2.** The Latin anchor sits inside the Apronianus sentence (*capitali sententia [0723] accepit*). The English puts `[0723]` after the full stop, before "Also at Rome". This is an anchor position, not a translation class. I note it in case anchor-placement parity matters to the builder.
12. **0002, Jan 13 and Jan 31.** The Bouillart brackets are unbalanced: Jan 13 has no closing `]`, and Jan 31 ends in `]]`. The English mirrors the Latin exactly. Not a defect.
13. **0003, Feb 15.** "The city of Interamna" for *Civitatem Interamnis*, and **0002** "Rome, on the Salarian Way" for *Roma via Salaria*. Both are literal to the printed case. Rejected.
14. **0003, Feb 25.** The `[sic: *Serapionis*]` is correctly placed on a nominative printed as a genitive. It is left as the stints marked it.
