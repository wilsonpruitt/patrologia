# Usuardus blind read: R2

Reader: R2. Chunks: 9198 / 0004, 0005, 0006; 9215 / 0000. Every sentence of both columns was
read, the `[cn:]`/`[nt:]` notes and the Bouillart brackets included. Commemorations were counted
per day on both sides, and every number was checked against the Latin. Findings were formed
before any cruces file was opened. The cruces check came after and is recorded per finding.

## Findings

### F1. 9198 / 0006, col 0987 (30 April). Class 3 (mis-parsed grammar)
- **Latin:** `Civitate Sanctonas, beati Euprobi martyris, quem sanctus Clemens, pontificalis ordinis gratia consecratum, dixerit in Gallias,`
- **English as it stands:** `of blessed Euprobus, martyr, whom Saint Clement, consecrated with the grace of the pontifical order, sent [sic: *dixerit*] into the Gauls,`
- **Proposed English:** `of blessed Euprobus, martyr, whom, consecrated with the grace of the pontifical order, Saint Clement sent [sic: *dixerit*] into the Gauls,`
- **Why:** *consecratum* is accusative and agrees with *quem* (Euprobus). Clement (nominative) is not
  the one consecrated. As the English stands, the participle phrase follows "Saint Clement" and
  reads as describing the pope, so the sentence says Clement was the one consecrated bishop.
- **Confidence:** certain on the grammar. The exact wording of the fix is the orchestrator's call.
- **Cruces (checked after):** not logged. `src/english/9198/cruces-0004.md` records only the `[sic: *dixerit*]` item
  for this site (dixerit for direxit) and says nothing about who *consecratum* refers to.

### F2. 9198 / 0005, col 0941 (17 April). Class 1 (number / quantity)
- **Latin:** `natalis sancti Mappalici martyris, qui, ut beatus scribit Cyprianus in epistola ad martyres et confessores, cum aliis pluribus coronatus est.`
- **English as it stands:** `was crowned with very many others.`
- **Proposed English:** `was crowned with many others.`
- **Why:** *pluribus* is "many / several" and not the superlative. Elsewhere this chunk set renders
  *plurimis* as "very many" (26 Mar *plurimis afflicti tormentis*; 9215 7 May *cum aliis plurimis*),
  so the English here inflates the count and blurs the *plures*/*plurimi* distinction the translation
  keeps everywhere else.
- **Confidence:** probable. It is small, but it is a quantity word.
- **Cruces (checked after):** not logged.

### F3. 9198 / 0005, col 0905 (5 April). Class 5 (one saint, two name forms across the two idnos)
- **Latin:** `Thessalonicae, sanctae Hirenis virginis,`
- **English as it stands:** `At Thessalonica, of Saint Hirenis, virgin,`
- **Proposed English:** `At Thessalonica, of Saint Hirene, virgin,`
- **Why:** the same genitive *Hirenis* is rendered **Hirene** in 9215 5 May (*Hirenei, Peregrini et
  Hirenis* → "Hireneus, Peregrinus and Hirene"). This is the same Thessalonian Irene; the 5 April
  entry names her sisters Agape and Chionia. So one work gives her two English names.
- **Confidence:** probable. It is a name-form consistency issue, not a wrong saint.
- **Cruces (checked after):** ALREADY LOGGED, but on opposite sides. `9198/cruces-0004.md` keeps
  *Hirenis* as "nominative uncertain (the gen. of Hirene would be *Hirenes*)", while
  `9215/cruces-0000.md` C7 reads the same *Hirenis* as the genitive of Hirene, "near-certain", and
  normalizes it. The two cruces files disagree about the same form. The orchestrator should pick one
  ruling and apply it to both idnos. I suggest Hirene, since the 9215 reasoning covers a Greek-
  declension genitive in *-is* and the 9198 note does not consider that.
- ~~Originally drafted with *Niceti* (9215 col 0023) → Nicetius.~~ **Withdrawn after the cruces
  check:** `9215/cruces-0000.md` C7 keeps *Niceti* on purpose, because it could be Nicetus or a
  contracted Nicetius. That is the printed-form ruling, so it stands.

## Candidates considered and rejected
- **27 Mar note:** *debuisse … non male negant Castellanus et Sollerius. Sed collocatum fuisse, nemo
  nisi pertinax … negaverit.* The double polarity is rendered exactly ("not wrongly deny … no one but
  an obstinate man … will deny"). Correct.
- **19 Apr note:** *nihil Sollerium non tentasse* → "left nothing untried". The double negative is
  resolved correctly.
- **12 Apr note:** *ut nisi caecus … effutire possit* → "only a blind man … could blurt out". Correct.
  *non neminem … symmistan* → "someone … a fellow initiate". Correct.
- **29 Mar note:** *Nicomendienses martyres defuisse, adfuisse S. Eustasium* keeps the right polarity.
  *Si mutatum dixisset, nihil reprehenderem* is correct.
- **17 Mar note:** *Nec die hesterna actus erat* → "Nor had it been dealt with on the day before".
  The sense is loose but defensible, with no polarity or number error. Henschenius's list has 8 names
  on both sides.
- **18 Mar note:** the Gertrude chronology (XVI/XV Kal. Apr., 664/659/658, Easter 25 March, cycles,
  letter G) is exact. *Non obiit … anno 664 … aut anno 659* → "did not die in 664 … nor in 659". Correct.
- **16 Apr:** eighteen martyrs of Caesaraugusta. Counted 18 names on both sides.
- **22 Apr:** Gaius's 11 years / 4 months / 12 days; about 250 clerics; Sollerius's nearly eighty codices.
  All correct.
- **26 Apr:** 17,000 crowned in one month; Anacletus 12 years; Marcellinus 9 years 4 months. All correct.
- **9 Mar:** *fratris beati Basilii Caesariensis tam vita quam eloquentia clarissimi*. The English keeps
  the Latin's ambiguity over whom *clarissimi* praises. Not a defect.
- **24 Mar:** *in Tyberim … necatus* → "put to death in the Tiber". It is literal, and the Latin
  (in + acc.) implies being thrown in, but the event is not misstated.
- **12 Apr:** *post mortem ejus* (Constantius's) → "after his death". This is the same ambiguity as
  the Latin and is not a mis-parse.
- **Galatae (19 Apr), Helimenae (22 Apr), Euticetis (15 Apr), Orientis (1 May), Anesi→Anesius
  (31 Mar):** a genitive or uncertain form is carried as the name. Each is a case where the nominative
  is genuinely uncertain, so it falls under the printed-form ruling. Unlike F3, none has an English
  nominative attested elsewhere in these chunks.
- **Agapes (3 Apr, for gen. *Agapis*):** this follows Migne's own nominative *Agapes* printed at 5 April.
  Printed-form ruling; not reported.
- **1 May:** *pene Schytiam* → "almost all Schytia". "all" is an English idiom for *pene* + the whole
  country and adds no content.
- **10 May:** *capite truncatur* → "is cut off by the head". The phrasing is awkward, but the sense is
  correct (beheaded).
- **9 May:** *Castro Vindicino, depositio beati confessoris*. There is no name in the Latin, and the
  English is faithful to that.
- **30 Mar note:** *Filopolum en Achaicum* → "and". A garbled word inside a Bouillart note, rendered
  in its evident sense (fixed by ruling).
- **31 Mar:** *Ozias, rex Israel* is as printed.
