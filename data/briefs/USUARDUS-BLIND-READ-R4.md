## Findings

### F1. 9215 / 0007 / col 0435 (4 September, Marcellus of Chalon). Class 3 (mis-parsed grammar)
- **Latin:** `Cabilonis, sancti Marcelli martyris, qui a praeside Prisco, cum ad profanum convivium fuisset invitatus, et ejusmodi exsecrans epulas, omnes qui aderant, cur idolis deservirent, libera imprecatione corriperet, inaudito crudelitatis genere defodi eum cingulo tenus erectum praeses jussit,`
- **English as it stands:** `At Cabilonis, of Saint Marcellus, martyr, whom the governor Priscus, when he had been invited to a profane banquet, and, execrating feasts of that kind, rebuked with free reproach all who were present, why they were slaves to idols, ordered, by an unheard-of kind of cruelty, to be buried standing upright up to the waist;`
- **Proposed English:** `At Cabilonis, of Saint Marcellus, martyr, who, when he had been invited by the governor Priscus to a profane banquet, and, execrating feasts of that kind, rebuked with free reproach all who were present, why they were slaves to idols, the governor ordered him, by an unheard-of kind of cruelty, to be buried standing upright up to the waist;`
- **Why:** *a praeside Prisco* is the agent of *invitatus*: the governor invited Marcellus. The English
  makes Priscus the subject of the relative clause and then says "when he had been invited", so the
  nearest antecedent of "he" is the governor. The sentence then reads as though Priscus was the
  guest who rebuked the idolaters, and the inviter is lost. The Latin is itself anacoluthic
  (*qui … eum … praeses jussit*), and the proposal keeps that break instead of smoothing it, in the
  same way this translation already handles Cassianus (0357) and Felix/Adauctus (0415).
- **Confidence:** probable. The Latin construal is certain; whether the current English is "wrong"
  or only ambiguous is the call.
- **Already in the cruces?** No. `cruces-0004.md` covers 0435 only for the number (*tertio die*) and
  the place name *Cabilonis*.

### F2. 9215 / 0005 / col 0277 (20 July, Bouillart's keyed note). Formula, outside classes 1–5 (minor)
- **Latin (inside the `[cn: a | …]`):** `*Civitate Corduba sancti Pauli diaconi et martyris. Ipso die sanctae Margaritae virginis.*`
- **English as it stands:** `*In the city of Cordoba, of Saint Paul, deacon and martyr. On the same day, of Saint Margaret, virgin.*`
- **Proposed English:** `*In the city of Cordoba, of Saint Paul, deacon and martyr. On this same day, of Saint Margaret, virgin.*`
- **Why:** the ruled formula is *Ipso die* = "On this same day" and *Eodem die* = "On the same day".
  Everywhere else in chunks 0005–0009 the two are kept apart (0371, 0407, 0411, 0435, 0503, 0529,
  0551). Here the note quotes the Caroline autograph's own formula, and the English collapses it
  into *Eodem die*. Nothing turns on it, but a reader comparing formulae would be misled.
- **Confidence:** certain.
- **Already in the cruces?** No. The formula table at `cruces-0004.md:306` states the rule; this
  instance is not logged.

## Candidates considered and rejected
1. **0389 note, "I will not say that the hand is the same; it is enough to have repeated that a
   hundred times."** (*Non dicam eamdem esse manum, id centies iterasse sat est.*) On its own, the
   first clause could read as Bouillart declining to claim the same hand, which would turn his whole
   thesis round. The second clause makes the sense ("I won't say it again") plain, and the English
   is a literal rendering of what is printed. Adding "again" would supply a word that is not there.
   Rejected. The orchestrator may still prefer "I will not say again …".
2. **0431 note, *quamvis Scripturarum differentia se modo non in oculos in gerat*** → "although the
   difference of the scripts does not force itself upon the eyes". *modo non* can mean "all but".
   But that sense clashes with *quamvis* and with Bouillart's ironic *Solita sua aequitate*: the
   censor condemned the codex *although* no difference shows. The current English is right. The
   split *in gerat* is a garbled word rendered in its evident sense (fixed by ruling).
3. **0487 note, *Caeterum non miror quod Usuardum … hallucinatum fuisse non est credibile*** →
   "For the rest, I do not wonder: that Usuardus … was mistaken is not credible." Both negations
   are kept and the sense is right. The colon is ours, a small Pattern 8 point about a supplied
   mark, not a class 1–5 defect. Rejected as a finding. The orchestrator may want to look at the
   colon.
4. **Names left in the printed genitive or uncertain form:** *Hermogratis* 0301, *Nicomedi* 0325,
   *Cottidi* 0441, *Acuti* 0483 (next to a normalised *Euticus*), *Salonis* 0515. Under the names
   ruling (nominative uncertain, printed form kept) these are not defects. After my read I
   confirmed that all five are logged in the cruces.
5. **0305 note, "For in the latter there is no erasure here"** (*Nam in isto*). "The latter" points
   to the Pratensis, which is the sense the argument needs. Rejected.
6. **0357 Cassianus, "the weaker their hand was, the heavier they made the punishment"** (*faciebat*
   is singular, with *manus* as subject). This is a subject shift with no change of sense. Rejected.
7. **0343 note, "Which saints, pray, …"** ("pray" has no Latin twin). It is a colloquial rendering
   of the interrogative *Quinam*, with no change of sense. Rejected.
8. **0393 "the *Massa candida*, the White Mass"** adds a gloss. It is an italic technical term with
   its gloss, not a defect. Rejected.
9. **0467 Cerealis "was beheaded", 0385 Timotheus "beaten a third time" (*tertio caesus*), 0325
   *ad totius Ecclesiae personam*.** All are correct construals. Rejected.
