## 2. Corrections applied (column 4 of the TSV only)

### 0247 — two real word errors, one of them a **column-crossing duplication**

> **Plate (both printings agree):** *Itaque ultro fateor litura hunc articulum deturpari ab hac
> voce* cruciati *ad finem … Non idcirco tamen ausim affirmare, ut Sollerius, Martianam intrusam
> fuisse.*

- **`lituram` → `litura`.** Archive p. 129 at 600 dpi shows no *m* and no macron; Gallica f124
  agrees. (Our English twin already read *"this article is disfigured by an erasure"* — ablative
  — so the English was right and the Latin was the outlier.)
- **`ut hoc Sollerius` → `ut Sollerius`.** ⚠ This is the interesting one. The stray **`hoc` was
  picked up from the FIRST line of the left column** (*"ut hoc quantulumcunque opus
  susciperem"*), which sits directly above where the right column's *"ut Sollerius"* begins.
  Both printings read *ut Sollerius*. This is the gutter-crossing hazard producing an addition
  rather than a loss. (English twin already read "as Sollerius does" — no change needed.)
- italics added: `ab hac voce *cruciati* ad finem`.

### 0309 — `litura` → `liturae`

> **Plate:** *Praeter ista verba,* civitate Trecas, *tota S. Lupi commemoratio liturae
> superinducta est ; cujus tamen liturae…*

Archive p. 160 at 600 dpi reads `lituræ`; Gallica f155 reads `lituræ`. Our row had `litura` for
the first occurrence and `liturae` for the second. Also italics added: `*civitate Trecas*`.
English twin ("has been written over an erasure") unaffected.

### 0411 — `fuerat` → `erant`

> **Plate (p. 212, col. 413A):** *…dein abbas alterius ex duobus monasteriis, quae tunc temporis
> Augustoduni **erant** ; tandem Parisiis, quo* causa orationis *venerat, vitam morte commutavit*
> die quarto Kalendarum Septembrium.

Our row had `Augustoduni fuerat;` — a singular verb on a plural antecedent (*duobus
monasteriis*). The plate is unambiguous. Italics also added on `*causa orationis*` and
`*die quarto Kalendarum Septembrium.*`
**English twin already read "which were at Autun at that time"** (plural) — so the English was
right and the Latin was the outlier again. No English change.

### Roman numerals: small caps on the plate → capitals (rule 4)

Migne sets every roman numeral in these notes as **small capitals**; our rows carried some as
capitals (`XIII Kal. Augusti`, `IV Idus`, `IX Kal. Sept.`, `VIII Kal.`, `XII Augusti`) and some
as lowercase, inside the same rows. Verified as small caps at 600 dpi on p. 103. Normalised in
my rows only:

- **0163** `sed xx Julii` → `sed XX Julii`
- **0195** `xxxi`→`XXXI` (×3), `xxi`→`XXI`, `xxv`→`XXV`, `xviii`→`XVIII`, `xv`→`XV`,
  `xiii`→`XIII`, `vi`/`vii`→`VI`/`VII` (×5)
- **0209** `Neronis xvi` → `Neronis XVI`
- **0389** `x *Kal. Sept.` → `X *Kal. Sept.`

⛔ **This needs a sweep across the other batches.** Lowercase roman numerals survive in rows
outside 1–31; the rule is corpus-wide and the running Latin of this work already capitalises
(`src/latin/9215/*.md`: `VIII Kal.`, `IX Kal.`, `XIII Kal.`).
