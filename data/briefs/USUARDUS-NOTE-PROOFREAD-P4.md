## Two printings: where they differ (PL 124 rule, Wilson 2026-09-22)

In each case below the **archive** copy prints the defective form and **Gallica** the sound one; our
text already carried the sound reading, so no edit was needed. Recorded here as evidence.

| col | archive prints | Gallica prints | ours |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0487 | `rationen` | `rationem` | `rationem` ✓ |
| 0593 | `Si vero non possit, qua fide quo` | `Si vero non possis, qua fide, quo` | possis + comma ✓ |
| 0597 | `contemplus mundi` | `contemptus mundi` | contemptus ✓ |
| 0597 | `ac perfete ævum` | `ac perfecte ævum` | perfecte ✓ |
| 0665 | `exemplis reperiatu.` | `exemplis reperiatur.` | reperiatur ✓ |
| 0725 | `legisse illam` (over-inked) | `legisse illum` | illum ✓ |

`possit`/`possis` is the one pair where both forms are real words, so the non-word rule does not
decide it; the sentence is second-person throughout (`Tu dicis … oro atque obtestor`), and Gallica's
`possis` with the comma after `fide` is both grammatical and the reading we carry. Followed Gallica.

  and the number disagreement are **Migne's**, identical in both printings. Not touched.

- **0585** `Julii I, XI XVIII` — the archive omits the comma between XI and XVIII in very tight
  setting. Our `Julii I, XI, XVIII` kept; a single crowded comma, not a variant.

  `Hieronymianos`, which is what we carry.

  normalise it — outside the scope of a word-for-word pass and it would collide with other agents'
