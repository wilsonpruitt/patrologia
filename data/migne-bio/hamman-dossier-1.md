# Hamman Dossier 1 — Front matter, Introduction, Ch. 1 ("Le siècle de l'abbé Migne"), Ch. 2 ("L'éditeur du siècle et un curé de village," through p. 66)

**Source:** A.-G. Hamman, *Jacques-Paul Migne: le retour aux Pères de l'Église* (Le Point théologique 16; Paris: Beauchesne, 1975). Chunk read: `raw/bloch-chunks/hamman1.txt` (front matter + pp. 5–66; OCR of the Internet Archive scan, Claremont School of Theology copy). Page citations below are the printed page numbers recovered from running heads. The chunk **cuts off mid-sentence on p. 66**, in the survey of the Scripture *Cours* (list of exegetes: "…le Maistre de Sacy, les abbés Glaire et de Vence, les anglicans Hammond et Ainsworth, les protestants, Jean Drusius…").

**Who Hamman is in this book:** he identifies himself in the Avant-propos as "Continuateur de la plus connue de ses collections" (p. 5) — i.e., compiler of the *Patrologiae Latinae Supplementum* (Garnier, 1958–1974, 5 vols., listed in the "Du même auteur" page). The book was written for the **centenary of Migne's death (1875 → 1975)**. He states the program: not a full biography but to "situer l'homme dans le renouveau théologique de son siècle et à analyser l'élaboration de sa double Patrologie" (p. 5).

**Source-critical note (Hamman's own):** the *only* biography is by a contemporary, **Hippolyte Barbier**, *Biographie du clergé de France* (Paris, 1841, III, xxvii–xxviii; 289–324), which **stops at 1841**; "Nous lui empruntons le grand nombre des précisions" (p. 53 n. 1). So virtually all of Hamman's early-life detail is Barbier at one remove — useful when triangulating against Bloch (1994), who mined the same vein plus archives.

**Divergences / points to check against other accounts:**
- Hamman gives **no birth-day** (just 1800) and no parents' names; other accounts give 25 Oct 1800 and identify the father as a grocer/merchant.
- "il avait trente ans" on arriving in Paris (p. 56) vs. actual age 32–33 in 1833.
- Hamman does **not** date the Puiseaux appointment precisely (arrival at Aillant 1824 + 18 months + brief Auxy ⇒ Puiseaux ~1826).
- The **suspension by Quélen** is undated here; Quélen died Dec 1839, so it belongs to the late 1830s.
- Hamman's fire date is simply "en 1868" (other accounts: night of 12–13 February 1868).

## 2. The century-context chapter (Ch. 1, "Le siècle de l'abbé Migne," pp. 39–50)

Hamman's argument: **the intellectual destitution of the post-revolutionary French Church is precisely what made Migne both necessary and possible.**

- **The Church in ruins (p. 39–40).** After twenty years of war, the Church of France is "secouée, démantelée, appauvrie." Napoleon's system had put it "en liberté surveillée"; the Restoration favors it as a *social force*, which provokes anticlerical opposition fed by 18th-century philosophy (Volney's lay catechism "règne presque partout où celui de l'Église ne fait plus loi"; Voltaire's verve fuels the pamphlets). Persecution, deportation and exile "ont décimé le clergé"; abbeys closed, orders gone; surviving priests "fatigués, vieillis, peu nombreux."
- **Romanticism as a false spring (p. 40).** The religion of Chateaubriand is "plus esthétique que religieux," "plus proche du panthéisme que du christianisme"; emotion stands in for argument; the assault on Enlightenment rationalism ends by "saper les bases intellectuelles de la foi" and opens the road to **fideism**, "dans lequel s'enlisent les meilleures forces de l'Église." The new Fathers of the Church are laymen: "Chateaubriand, J. de Maistre, Louis de Bonald" (p. 40). Maistre and Bonald offer authoritarian restoration — "Thèses qui rassurent à courte-vue, sans résoudre les questions posées."
- **Lamennais as the century's spark (p. 41, 48–49).** His *Essai sur l'indifférence* gives the Church "son second souffle" — 40,000 copies in a year; "Il réveillerait un mort" (Frayssinous); the 35-year-old priest "se trouve investi de la puissance de Bossuet." Condemned **15 July 1834**. His La Chesnaie school (~20 members: Gerbet, Montalembert, Maurice de Guérin, Frédéric de la Provostaye) and the **congrégation de Saint-Pierre (1828, with his brother)** die with his revolt, "mais ses disciples poursuivent son effort." Baudrillart's verdict, quoted approvingly: Lamennais's name "est à l'origine du mouvement intellectuel qui s'est produit dans le clergé français" (p. 49).
- **Clergy education in collapse (pp. 41–43).** Recruitment crisis (Boisgelin: "Dans deux ans, la moitié des diocèses sera sans prêtre et sans culte"). Bishops train clergy in their own seminaries "où n'existent ni maîtres ni bibliothèques"; seminary professors are "Incompétents, autodidactes… Ils veillent plus à la vertu qu'au savoir et conseillent la mortification intellectuelle à leurs élèves." Kant — a fortiori Marx — absent from the manuals. Theology = the pre-revolutionary **manual of Bailly**, then the **cours de Bouvier** (used "dans presque tous les séminaires de France, de 1820 à 1880"), "une compilation, qui mélange dogme, morale, histoire et liturgie." **No church history** in the curriculum: it is absorbed at refectory reading "dans le cliquetis des assiettes et des fourchettes"; the proviseur Gattrey of Besançon calls a chair of history a "luxe inutile." Sulpician **Faillon** resurrects the apostolicity legends of French dioceses (1835) "au mépris de tout sens critique" — and Hamman notes with pained irony that Faillon's Magdalene volume appeared "hélas, aux Ateliers catholiques de Migne" (p. 43 n. 7). **No initiation to the Fathers**; Montalembert (1838): not five seminaries in eighty teach church history. Scripture teaching "inexistant" just as **Strauss** and then **Renan** sap the foundations; Catholic ripostes are "ragoût de vieilles dissertations de séminaristes ou pamphlets sans consistance. Levée de boucliers d'opérette, qui fit la publicité et le succès sans précédent du livre de Renan." Only one exegete of stature: **Le Hir** at Saint-Sulpice — Renan's own master.
- **No higher learning (pp. 44–45).** State theology faculties suspect and moribund (Toulouse dies "sans bruit" ~1830, "à la mort du dernier professeur"); the Paris faculty — Maret, Glaire, Freppel — is "plus littéraire que théologique, gallicane de surcroît." Renewal comes instead from the **Sorbonne**, where **Ozanam** teaches ecclesiastical history — and, Hamman notes, **collaborated with the *Univers* "fondé par l'abbé Migne dès sa création"** (p. 44). No bishop sends students to Germany (Scheeben, Möhler); only **Bautain** does. Apologetics fights the previous century's enemies: Receveur's 1830 book "pourrait avoir été écrit en 1780"; the reigning organ is "le fade *Ami de la religion*" — the very paper Migne would take on.
- **But the ground was ready (pp. 46–47).** Hamman refuses to write the clergy off: "Le prêtre vaut mieux que le manuel qu'il utilise." Classical formation meant Latin (often Greek) was no obstacle; the very mediocrity of the teaching bred autodidact curiosity — "tous les génies de l'histoire n'ont-ils pas été autodidactes?" The Maurist tradition wasn't wholly dead (Caillau & Saint-Yves finish the Maurist Gregory of Nazianzus; Genoude's French translations of the Fathers from 1837). And crucially: "**Ce sont de modestes curés de campagne qui achètent les nouvelles encyclopédies de l'abbé Migne et ses deux Patrologies**" (p. 46). Brémond's arc, endorsed by Hamman: a renaissance beginning with the *Génie du christianisme* "et qui atteint son apogée dans les « bibliothèques de Migne »" (p. 47).
- **Institutional stirrings (pp. 47–49).** Charles X's government (via Frayssinous) budgets **300,000 francs in 1825** for a "Maison des hautes études ecclésiastiques" — the episcopate balks, "le crédit alloué demeure inemployé" (a perfect Hamman emblem of hierarchy vs. learning). Mid-century: **Mgr Affre** buys the Carmes convent, rue de Vaugirard — the **École des Carmes** (six students!): first *licencié* 1848 = future cardinal **Foulon**; first doctor = **Lavigerie**, thesis on *L'école chrétienne d'Édesse*. **Bautain**'s société des prêtres de Saint-Louis sends clerics to Germany, opens a Rome house of studies (1838) — "Initiatives de courte durée, qui meurent toutes avec lui." **1833: Guéranger reopens Solesmes**, welcoming **Pitra**; "C'est à la porte de Solesmes que l'abbé Migne va tout naturellement frapper, au moment d'entreprendre sa gigantesque édition des Pères, sur le conseil de Guéranger" (p. 49) — with the crucial documentary footnote: **Migne to Guéranger, 22 Feb 1855: "c'est à votre instigation et par vos conseils que j'ai entrepris la Patrologie"** (p. 49 n. 21). Pitra, even as cardinal, complains of being "moins lu en France que par les protestants allemands et anglais."
- **The thesis crystallized: the missing thing was books (p. 50).** "Ce qui manquait à l'époque aux séminaires comme aux presbytères, c'était les bibliothèques." Monastic and capitular collections had been dispersed by the Revolution; only city-dwellers could consult anything. Lamennais already in 1808: "avant tout il faut des livres pour étudier." Then the chapter's peroration: "**C'est le mérite de l'abbé Migne, simple desservant de paroisse, de l'avoir compris, et d'y avoir porté remède.**" Migne "connaissait d'expérience la condition du curé de campagne, sa situation précaire, son isolement intellectuel… l'impossibilité de recourir aux sources"; as journalist and editor he "n'oubliera pas sa vie de desservant," defending country curés "contre l'arbitraire des évêques" in the *Voix de la Vérité* and hiring them in his Ateliers. Hence the proud name: "**la Bibliothèque universelle du clergé et des laïques instruits**."

**The Introduction (pp. 9–35)** serves the same argument at longer range: a history of how patristic texts reached the 19th century — author's "commercial copies" (Augustine sending the *City of God* to Firmius, PLS II, 1374); stenographers catching improvised sermons; forgery and pseudepigraphy (53 inauthentic "Jerome" letters crossing the Middle Ages; the pseudo-Ignatian corpus, whose critical dossier "Migne réunit avec l'édition du texte," p. 12); scriptoria (Cassiodorus's Vivarium, Saint-Gall's plan of ~830 with seven scribes); libraries (Pamphilus's catalogue of **30,000 rolls** at Caesarea, p. 17; the Lateran's **649 volumes**, p. 18; Reichenau 822: **25 Augustine vols vs. 22 Jerome**; Cluny 11th c.: **65 vs. 32**, p. 19); the medieval **mediations** — homiliaries, florilegia, catenae, Sentences, canonical collections — through which clergy knew the Fathers only in extracts ("une eau, mais une eau de citerne," p. 30); the picaresque survival of the **Epistle to Diognetus** (found ~early 15th c. serving as **wrapping paper in a Constantinople fish-shop**, bought cheap by Thomas d'Arezzo, passed to Jean Stojkovic de Raguse [in the capital 1435–37 as legate of Basel], to the Basel Dominicans, to Reuchlin, to Marmoutier, to the Strasbourg municipal library — **burned in the Prussian bombardment of 1870**, pp. 21–22); printing (*City of God* editio princeps **Subiaco, 14 June 1467**; Amerbach's complete Augustine from **1506, 9 vols., 2,200 copies**, p. 33; Froben "le roi des imprimeurs" + Erasmus "le prince de la culture," p. 33); and finally the **Maurists of Saint-Germain-des-Prés** — Coustant working **fourteen hours a day** "sans jamais prendre le temps de visiter la capitale," Mabillon "le plus grand érudit de la France" (Le Tellier), **more than 140 libraries across Europe** feeding the effort, the 363 authentic Augustine sermons sorted from 317+ apocrypha (pp. 14, 34). Punchline of the whole Introduction (p. 35): "**Toutes ces éditions des Bénédictins de Saint-Maur trouvent place avec leurs introductions, les variantes, les notes et les tables dans la double Patrologie de l'abbé Migne.**" Note also p. 23 n. 77, a rare first-person aside: Hamman himself helped choose the patristic readings of the post-Vatican-II *Liturgie des Heures* "en faisant la place large aux trois premiers siècles."

## 3. Named people and roles

**Migne's life and enterprise**
- **Abbé Salesse** — principal of the collège of Saint-Flour, then proviseur at Orléans; discovers Migne and takes him there, 1817 (pp. 53–54).
- **Stanislas Julien** — Orléans seminary classmate, later celebrated sinologist, member of the Institut (p. 54).
- **M. de Beauregard** — bishop of Orléans; forbids *De la liberté*; grants the exeat "en termes élogieux" (p. 55).
- **Hippolyte Barbier** — contemporary and only biographer (to 1841); Hamman's chief source (p. 53 n. 1, and quoted pp. 55–64 passim).
- **Victor-Étienne Migne** — the abbé's brother; *prête-nom* for the first volumes and the 1845 printing brevet; founder-director of the *Journal des faits* (1850–53); dies prematurely 1853 (pp. 59–60).
- **Emmanuel Bailly de Surcy** — ex-director of the *Tribune catholique*; publisher of Migne's first volumes (place de la Sorbonne); takes over *L'Univers* in 1836; later refuses Pitra's articles (pp. 58, 60).
- **Vrayet de Surcy** — Paris printer whose name gave legal cover through PL vol. 13 (pp. 60–61).
- **Louis Veuillot** — convert whose editorship from July 1842 finally makes *L'Univers* soar; **E. Veuillot** (his brother) quoted: "La rédaction manquait" (p. 57).
- **Frédéric Ozanam, Philippe Gerbet, Charles Nodier** — prestigious but episodic early collaborators of *L'Univers* (p. 57); Gerbet a bridge from Lamennais's *Avenir* (p. 56).
- **Michel Picot** — director of *L'Ami de la religion*, "légitimiste ardent, défenseur des idées gallicanes," Migne's first press adversary (p. 57).
- **Abbé Romain Prompsaut** — eminent jurisconsult of the *Voix de la Vérité*; author of the legal dictionary for the Encyclopédie catholique; his consultations armed clergy against both civil and episcopal authority (pp. 58–59).
- **Abbé Bonnetty** — "l'ami fidèle mais lucide de l'abbé Migne"; founder (1830) of the *Annales de philosophie chrétienne*; wrote Migne's puff-pieces ("articles publicitaires… fort instructifs," p. 63 n. 27; pp. 46, 63).
- **Dom Prosper Guéranger** — restorer of Solesmes (1833); *instigator of the Patrologia* per Migne's 1855 letter; recipient of the omnibus letter (pp. 49, 61).
- **Dom (later Cardinal) Jean-Baptiste Pitra** — rhetoric professor at Autun, then Solesmes monk; Migne's scholarly right hand for the Patrologies; irritated by the "abominables affiches"; recipient of Migne's 1858 and 1865 letters (pp. 46, 49, 58, 63).
- **Archbishops of Paris in sequence:** **de Quélen** (suspends Migne, then offers to co-opt the enterprise), **Affre** (softens censures; condemns the *Voix de la Vérité*; founds the École des Carmes), **Sibour** (reconciliation; the 5,000-franc gift via **abbé Gratry**, 8 June 1850) (pp. 59, 61–62, 47).
- **Cardinal Mathieu**, archbishop of Besançon — recipient of Migne's most self-revealing sales letters (31 Jan 1853; 20 June 1854); apparently never subscribed (pp. 62–63). Letters communicated to Hamman by **abbé Martinet**, archivist of Besançon (n. 26).
- Episcopal visitors to Montrouge (1853 list): cardinals of Aix, Auch, Bourges, Bordeaux, Cambrai; archbishops of Paris (*sic*), Avignon, Chalcédoine, Tours, Toulouse; 27 French bishops; cardinals **Baluffi, Fornari, Wiseman**; the coadjutor of Montréal and of Québec (p. 62).
- **R. du Merzer** — visitor whose picturesque account of the Ateliers' "streets" is quoted (p. 63).
- **M. Proust** — banker who bought *la Vérité* (p. 59).

**Century-context figures (ch. 1)**
- **Chateaubriand, Joseph de Maistre, Louis de Bonald** — the lay "new Fathers of the Church" (p. 40).
- **Lamennais** — the century's catalyst; *Essai sur l'indifférence*; La Chesnaie; congrégation de Saint-Pierre (1828); condemned 15 July 1834 (pp. 41, 48–49).
- **Mgr Frayssinous** — minister of ecclesiastical affairs; "Il réveillerait un mort"; the aborted 1825 Maison des hautes études (pp. 41, 47).
- **Cardinal de Boisgelin**, **Mgr de Bonald** (of Lyon) — voices of the recruitment panic and anti-intellectualism (p. 41).
- **Cardinal Baudrillart** — 20th-c. judgment quoted twice (pp. 41, 49).
- **Bailly** and **Bouvier** — the theology manuals (pp. 42, 54).
- **Faillon** (Sulpician legend-reviver), **Rohrbacher** (whose works "nuisent plus qu'ils ne servent la cause de l'histoire"), **abbé Gattrey** (Besançon) (pp. 42–43).
- **Montalembert**, **D. F. Strauss**, **Ernest Renan**, **abbé Meignan**, **abbé Vollot**, **M. Le Hir** (pp. 43–44, 46).
- **Mgr Maret, abbé Glaire, Charles E. Freppel** (Paris faculty; Freppel held the chair of sacred eloquence and did honest patristic vulgarization), **Ozanam** (Sorbonne) (p. 44).
- **Scheeben, Möhler** (the German theology France ignored); **Louis Bautain** (Strasbourg; société des prêtres de Saint-Louis) (pp. 45, 48–49).
- **Abbé Receveur**, **E. Vacherot** (whose 1868 *Revue des Deux Mondes* article Hamman calls "tendancieux, qui demanderait à être nuancé") (p. 45).
- **Gerbet and L.-A. de Salinis** (founders of *L'Université catholique*), **Mgr Gaillon** (patrologue averti), **B. Caillau & Saint-Yves** (finished the Maurist Gregory of Nazianzus), **Antoine-Eugène de Genoude** (French translations of the Fathers from 1837), **abbé Brémond** (pp. 46–47).
- **Foulon** (first licencié of the Carmes, 1848), **Charles-Martial Lavigerie** (its first doctor; Edessa thesis; later founded the African catechumenate "sur les règles de l'Église primitive"), **abbé Darboy**, **Mgr Duchesne** (pp. 47–49).

**Introduction (selective — the transmission history):** Jean Gerhard (coined *Patrologia*, 1653), Erasmus, Jean d'Amerbach, Jean Froben, the Maurists (Coustant, Mabillon, Blampin, Montfaucon, Massuet, Garnier, Maran, Touttée, the two de la Rue, Ruinart, de Sainte-Marie), Cardinals **Mai and Pitra** (19th-c. publishers of catena fragments, p. 25), J. C. Th. von Otto, Cunitz & Reuss (Strasbourg collators of the Diognetus MS), R. Étaix (modern reconstruction of Chromatius from homiliaries, p. 23), J. de Ghellinck (the scholarly authority Hamman leans on throughout).

## 4. Numbers exactly as given (with page cites)

**Money**
- **300.000 francs** in the 1825 budget for the stillborn "Maison des hautes études ecclésiastiques"; the credit "demeure inemployé" (p. 47).
- **5.000 francs** from Mgr Sibour via Gratry, 8 June 1850, "pour l'acquisition de livres" (p. 62).
- **Prime de 25 centimes** offered "par chaque faute qu'ils découvriront dans n'importe lequel de nos volumes surtout dans les grecs" (p. 65).
- Correction costs at Montrouge "autant que la composition, tandis qu'ailleurs elle ne coûte que le dixième" (pp. 64–65).
- Grand organ at the fire: insured and appraised at **15.000 francs** (p. 65, citing the Tribunal civil de la Seine, 20 Dec 1871).

**The enterprise**
- *Essai sur l'indifférence*: **40.000 exemplaires** sold in one year (p. 41).
- *L'Univers religieux*: **800 abonnés** after three weeks (p. 57).
- Bibliothèque universelle: planned **2.000 volumes** petit in-4° (p. 59); the 1868 fire stops it "en son milieu" (p. 59).
- Ateliers building: "quelques cents pieds de long," glass roof, "l'aspect d'une église de campagne"; **5 grandes presses mécaniques, mues par la vapeur**, plus foundry, stereotypy, bookshop, bindery (p. 60).
- Workforce: **300** at the start → **596 in 1854** (compositors, printers, conductors, founders, binders, *clicheurs*, editors, *brocheuses*), plus per Migne "un nombre égal à l'extérieur" (pp. 60, 63–64).
- Output: "un puis trois volumes in-4° par semaine" (p. 61).
- Proofreading: **cinq jeux d'épreuves**, "ce qui bat tous les records"; correctors "en plus grand nombre que dans vingt-cinq imprimeries de Paris réunies" (p. 64).
- Vrayet de Surcy's name through **vol. 13** of the PL (p. 61).
- Projected councils collection: **80 volumes** (p. 65).
- Civil unions at Puiseaux: **26 → 3** (p. 55). Aillant: **3 paroisses**, **18 months** (p. 54). Theology cramming: **15 heures par jour** (p. 54); as diacre-censeur trajectory: censeur 1820, **3 years** at Châteaudun (p. 54).
- 1853 visitor list: **5 French cardinals, 5 archbishops, 27 bishops** + foreign prelates (p. 62).
- Six omnibus lines to Montrouge, "à six minutes de la Barrière" (p. 61).
- École des Carmes: **6 étudiants** (p. 47).
- Montalembert: not **5 seminaries in 80** teach church history (p. 43).
- Bouvier's manual reigns **1820–1880** (p. 42).

**Transmission history (Introduction)**
- Patrology's span: to Innocent III (West), to the Council of Florence **1438–1439** (East) (p. 6); term coined **1653** by Jean Gerhard (p. 6).
- Pamphilus's library catalogue: **30.000 rouleaux** (p. 17); Lateran library: **649 volumes** (p. 18); Luxor ostrakon catalogue: **80 titres** (p. 18).
- Reichenau (822): **25 Augustine / 22 Jerome**; Cluny (11th c.): **65 / 32** (p. 19).
- Maurists on Jerome: **150 lettres authentiques** (3 since rejected, 4 more since found) (p. 12); on Augustine's sermons: **363 authentiques**, ~**30 douteux**, **317+ apocryphes** (p. 14).
- Pierre Lombard's Sentences: **1.000 citations** of Augustine, **280** from the *De Trinitate* (pp. 26–27).
- Gratian's Decretum (~**1140**, Bologna): **1.200 citations patristiques** (Augustine ~half; Jerome **201**; Ambrose **148**; Chrysostom **33**, only **14** authentic; Origen credited 2, actually 18); of ~**3.500 canons**, **one-third** from the Fathers (p. 28).
- Incunabula: *City of God* editio princeps **14 June 1467, Subiaco** (9 text + 8 commented editions); Jerome **~50 éditions**; Gregory the Great **~70**; Cyprien **8 éditions** of the Letters; Basil's *Aux jeunes gens* **19 éditions** (pp. 31–32 & nn.).
- Amerbach's Augustine: begun **1506**, **9 volumes**, **2.200 exemplaires** (p. 33).
- Maurists: **140+ bibliothèques** across Europe contributing; Coustant's **14 heures par jour** (p. 34).
- Diognetus MS: in-folio of **200 pages**, 14th-c. copy of a 6th–7th-c. uncial archetype; originally **22 œuvres apologétiques**; burned **1870** (pp. 20–22).

## 5. Direct quotations worth footnoting (French + English)

1. **p. 5 (Hamman's opening):** "Étrange aventure que celle de Jacques-Paul Migne… un cantalien devenu parisien, un curé de campagne, le plus grand éditeur de son siècle, un homme de culture limitée dont le nom s'identifie presque avec les chefs-d'œuvre qu'il a publiés." — "A strange adventure, that of Jacques-Paul Migne… a man of the Cantal become a Parisian, a country curé, the greatest publisher of his century, a man of limited culture whose name is almost identified with the masterpieces he published." (Followed by: "Ce personnage de Balzac est demeuré obscur, méconnu" — "This character out of Balzac has remained obscure, unrecognized.")

2. **p. 41 (Boisgelin, the vocations panic):** "Dans deux ans, la moitié des diocèses sera sans prêtre et sans culte." — "In two years, half the dioceses will be without priest and without worship."

3. **p. 41 (Mgr de Bonald of Lyon):** "Des savants? Que voulez-vous que j'en fasse?" — "Scholars? What do you expect me to do with them?" (Hamman's framing: said "au moment où les savants sapaient les fondements de la foi" — just when the scholars were sapping the foundations of the faith.)

4. **p. 42 (Brugerette, on how church history was learned):** "dans le cliquetis des assiettes et des fourchettes" — "amid the clatter of plates and forks" (i.e., only as refectory reading).

5. **p. 43 (Montalembert, 1838):** "Il n'y a pas à l'heure qu'il est cinq séminaires sur quatre-vingts où l'on enseigne à la jeunesse l'histoire de l'Église." — "At the present hour there are not five seminaries out of eighty where the young are taught the history of the Church."

6. **p. 44 (Lamennais, 1829):** "Autrefois le clergé était à la tête de la société par ses lumières; jamais depuis bien des siècles le clergé pris en masse n'avait été aussi ignorant qu'aujourd'hui, et jamais cependant la vraie science n'a été plus nécessaire." — "Formerly the clergy stood at the head of society by its learning; not for many centuries has the clergy as a body been as ignorant as it is today — and never has true science been more necessary."

7. **p. 49 n. 21 (Migne to Guéranger, 22 Feb 1855 — the origin of the Patrologia):** "c'est à votre instigation et par vos conseils que j'ai entrepris la Patrologie." — "it is at your instigation and by your counsels that I undertook the Patrologia."

8. **p. 54 (young Migne to abbé Salesse, 1817):** "Je vous suivrais en Amérique, et par delà le monde, si c'était possible." — "I would follow you to America, and beyond the world, if it were possible."

9. **p. 56 (Hamman on the arrival in Paris):** "Il arrivait dans la capitale comme les vachers auvergnats, sans argent, sans protection, sans relations… riche en projets — ce qui n'est pas rare — mais obstiné pour les mener à bonne fin, ce qui n'arrive qu'une fois en un siècle." — "He arrived in the capital like the Auvergnat cowherds — no money, no protection, no connections… rich in projects (which is not rare) but obstinate in carrying them through, which happens once in a century."

10. **p. 55 (Hamman on the pivot from Puiseaux to Paris):** "Migne ne voulait pas rester sur un échec. Il allait prendre sa revanche, en publiant non plus une brochure mais des bibliothèques." — "Migne had no intention of resting on a defeat. He would take his revenge by publishing, no longer a pamphlet, but libraries."

11. **pp. 57–58 (Migne's forced insertion in the *Ami de la Religion*, vs. Picot):** "…ce n'est pas de la colère, de la jalousie, des mensonges que le christianisme demande pour sa défense à ses enfants, c'est de la charité." — "…it is not anger, jealousy, and lies that Christianity asks of its children for its defense — it is charity."

12. **p. 58 (Migne to Cardinal Pitra, 27 Jan 1865 — journalism as his other monument):** "Une chose qui ferait… beaucoup de plaisir au Pape, serait de lui apprendre que c'est l'éditeur de la Patrologie qui a fondé le journal l'Univers, et qui l'a ressuscité par le journal le Monde, en sacrifiant la Voix de la Vérité… Il me semble que ceci est quelque chose, indépendamment de toutes mes publications." — "One thing that would give the Pope much pleasure would be to learn that it is the editor of the Patrologia who founded the journal *L'Univers*, and who resurrected it in the journal *Le Monde*, at the sacrifice of the *Voix de la Vérité*… It seems to me that this is something, quite apart from all my publications." (Also the aside on Napoleon III: "L'Empereur voulait absolument une tombe ultramontaine pour laisser créer un berceau de même opinion" — "The Emperor absolutely required an ultramontane tomb before allowing a cradle of the same opinion to be created.")

13. **pp. 62–63 (Migne to Cardinal Mathieu, 20 June 1854, postscript — the salesman):** "Oserais-je dire à Votre Grandeur, que si elle vient à Paris, sans son équipage, je puis mettre tout à fait gratis à son service… une calèche et des chevaux très confortables. La Bourse, le timbre, la poste et les chemins de fer m'ont rendu ce luxe nécessaire." — "May I dare tell Your Grandeur that if you come to Paris without your carriage, I can place entirely free at your service… a barouche and very comfortable horses. The Bourse, the stamp-office, the post and the railways have made this luxury necessary to me."

14. **p. 63 (R. du Merzer's visit — the geography of the Ateliers):** "Entrez dans ce magasin, prenez la rue de la Bible à droite, puis la rue Bossuet à gauche, et au bout vous trouverez M. l'abbé Migne, sur la place des Pères de l'Église." — "Go into that warehouse, take the rue de la Bible on the right, then the rue Bossuet on the left, and at the end you will find M. l'abbé Migne, on the place des Pères de l'Église" — where Migne was pointing out "l'emplacement réservé à l'édifice de Tertullien" ("the site reserved for the edifice of Tertullian").

15. **pp. 63–64 (an anonymous worker-typographer, 1854):** "Là, pas de clients qui harcèlent, pas de concurrence à redouter. Un seul homme pense, gouverne et impose ses volontés." — "There, no clients to harry you, no competition to fear. One man alone thinks, governs, and imposes his will."

16. **pp. 65–66 (Migne's own manifesto, *Démonstrations évangéliques* t. 18, 1849, col. 1351):** "nos Ateliers catholiques ont surtout été fondés pour produire ce que nul éditeur n'oserait entreprendre… C'est l'exploitation perpétuelle et presque universelle d'une mine aussi sacrée qui nous a constamment révoltés, qui nous a mis les armes à la main et nous a fait fonder notre œuvre sur ce double pivot : **du bon, à bon marché**. Que le clergé se serre donc autour de confrères qui ont sacrifié pour lui repos, place et honneur…" — "our Ateliers catholiques were founded above all to produce what no publisher would dare undertake… It is the perpetual and almost universal exploitation of so sacred a mine that has constantly revolted us, put arms in our hands, and made us found our work on this double pivot: **the good, at a cheap price**. Let the clergy therefore close ranks around confrères who have sacrificed for it rest, position, and honor…"

17. **p. 64 (Barbier on the Ateliers as refuge):** "Tout prêtre, quel qu'il soit, qui a faim et envie de s'occuper, peut frapper à la porte de l'Imprimerie catholique… rien ne l'empêchera de dire son bréviaire, d'assister aux offices." — "Any priest whatsoever who is hungry and wants occupation may knock at the door of the Imprimerie catholique… nothing will prevent him from saying his breviary or attending the offices." (And the critics: "De quel droit, disent-ils, recueillir des coupables que l'autorité a jetés à la rue" — "By what right, they say, take in guilty men whom authority has thrown into the street?")

18. **p. 50 (Lamennais, 1808 — the premise of the whole enterprise):** "Qu'on travaille à former des bibliothèques dans les séminaires… c'est le moyen le plus sûr de répandre l'instruction, car avant tout il faut des livres pour étudier." — "Let us work to form libraries in the seminaries… it is the surest means of spreading instruction, for before all else one needs books in order to study."

## 6. Hamman's interpretive stance (sympathetic; contrast Bloch's irony)

Hamman writes as **an heir defending the founder** — he compiled the PL supplements, and his sympathy is structural, not incidental. Marked as HIS view throughout:

- **HIS framing verdict:** Migne is "le plus grand éditeur de son siècle" (p. 5) — stated flatly, in the first paragraph, as fact.
- **HIS explanatory charity:** where Bloch reads Migne's operation as commerce wearing a cassock, Hamman derives the whole enterprise from **pastoral solidarity**: "C'est le mérite de l'abbé Migne, simple desservant de paroisse, de l'avoir compris, et d'y avoir porté remède" (p. 50). The Bibliothèque exists because Migne "connaissait d'expérience la condition du curé de campagne… l'absence tragique de moyens de travail et de culture" (p. 50). The salesman's tricks appear, but as texture, not indictment.
- **HIS defense of the editions' quality:** "ses éditions, qui marquent souvent un progrès, **quoi qu'on en ait dit**" (p. 64) — an explicit push-back against the scholarly tradition of sneering at Migne's texts; he stresses the five proof-stages, the international correctors, the 25-centime error bounty (pp. 64–65).
- **HIS reading of the conflicts with authority:** the bishops come off worse than Migne. Quélen was "abusé" and yielded "aux jalousies cupides des libraires" (p. 61 — Barbier's phrase, adopted without distance); the offer to make the enterprise diocesan under Quélen reads as an attempted takeover. Beauregard condemned a book he hadn't read (p. 55). The chapter-1 gallery (Bonald's "Des savants?", the unused 300,000 francs, Gattrey's "luxe inutile") is built to make Migne's episcopal opposition look like part of a general hierarchical failure of nerve about learning.
- **HIS sentimental identifications:** on Migne's sheltering of irregular priests: "Leur odyssée n'était-elle pas un peu la sienne, la ténacité et la réussite en plus?" (p. 64). On the omnibus letter: "La belle époque" (p. 61). On character: "L'homme et l'œuvre reflètent le paysage natal : rude, solide, obstiné" (p. 53) — the Auvergnat-granite topos taken as explanation. Obstinacy "n'arrive qu'une fois en un siècle" (p. 56).
- **HIS positioning of Migne in a renewal narrative:** endorsing Brémond, the 19th-c. religious renaissance "atteint son apogée dans les « bibliothèques de Migne »" (p. 47). Migne = the practical answer to Lamennais's 1808 call for books. Also "Larousse avant Larousse" (p. 59).
- **Where Hamman concedes the debit side** (so the sympathy is not hagiography): Migne's "culture limitée" (p. 5); he never attended a theology course (p. 41); the "prospectus tapageurs, les « abominables affiches » qui ont irrité dom Pitra" (p. 63); the "hélas" over printing Faillon's legend-mongering Magdalene volume at the Ateliers (p. 43 n. 7); the acknowledgment of "ses démêlés avec la justice dont parlent les frères Goncourt" (p. 5 — flagged as imperfectly known, a thread Bloch would pull hard); "Loin de se concilier tous les partis, il se les aliéna tous" (Barbier, quoted p. 56).
- **HIS scholarly humility:** the archives are burned/lost/scattered, "jamais été dépouillées méthodiquement"; no comprehensive study exists; he wishes the man were given "une thèse de doctorat" (p. 5). Useful to cite as the 1975 state-of-the-question that Bloch (1994) then occupied.

## 7. Texture — personality, habits, appearance, daily life

- **The Auvergnat body:** a mountain vagabond, "grimpeur, chasseur, nageur," truant; parents' shop taught him "le labeur, l'endurance, la ténacité en affaires" (p. 53). "Forte constitution" — which the marshes of Aillant nonetheless broke in eighteen months (p. 54).
- **The autodidact's ferocity:** little French and less Latin at 17; crowned at the seminary concours five months later (p. 54); fifteen hours a day cramming Bouvier by lamplight ("au cours de ses veillées," pp. 41, 54).
- **The pastoral fighter:** faces down national guards ("ignobles plaisanteries," p. 55); walks out of his own Corpus Christi procession over a tricolor in a window (p. 55); civil unions 26 → 3 (p. 55). "Le curé est tenace et réussit" (p. 55).
- **The pugnacious correspondent:** forces the rival *Ami de la Religion* to print his letter accusing Picot of calumny — while claiming such language is "si peu en harmonie avec nos habitudes et notre caractère personnel" (p. 57).
- **The showman-salesman:** every venture opens with a circular to bishops and savants, then "prospectus tapageurs"; he mobilizes "le sentiment, l'intérêt, les lois de la Providence" (p. 63); collects episcopal congratulation letters "soigneusement" and publishes them as a brochure (p. 62); name-drops five cardinals and twenty-seven bishops to a prospect (p. 62); offers a cardinal the free loan of "une calèche et des chevaux très confortables," explaining that the Bourse, stamp office, post and railways "m'ont rendu ce luxe nécessaire" (pp. 62–63) — the one glimpse of personal style/equipage in the chunk.
- **The kingdom at Montrouge:** a glass-roofed building "de quelques cents pieds de long" looking like "une église de campagne" (p. 60); interior "streets" named rue de la Bible, rue Bossuet, opening on the "place des Pères de l'Église," with Migne planning "l'édifice de Tertullien" among canyon-walls of in-4° volumes (p. 63); 596 workers by 1854; "Un seul homme pense, gouverne et impose ses volontés" (pp. 63–64). Six omnibus lines away from the Barrière — les monrougiennes, les favorites, les hirondelles, les parisiennes, les trycicles (*sic*), plus the railway coaches (p. 61).
- **The refuge-keeper:** hires needy priests — provincials, foreigners, men "en rupture de ban, mariés et chargés de famille" — asking only that they promise to live as they ought; free to say the breviary and attend offices (p. 64). This charity itself worsened relations with the archdiocese.
- **The diversifier:** side workshops for altars, religious statues, stations of the cross, "et même d'orgues"; the 1858 letterhead slip touting "de magnifiques Tableaux, ainsi que de puissantes et d'excellentes Orgues" in his "Musées" (p. 65).
- **The grudge-nurser:** decades after leaving *L'Univers*: "On ne peut me pardonner d'avoir été le fondateur de ce journal" (1858, p. 58); still in 1865 wanting the Pope told that the Patrologia's editor founded *L'Univers* (p. 58); and the Puiseaux defeat transmuted into the whole career — revenge "en publiant non plus une brochure mais des bibliothèques" (p. 55).
- **Slogan of the house:** "du bon, à bon marché" — the good, cheap (pp. 65–66); prices "meilleur marché que notre livre de poche" (Hamman's comparison, p. 61); output one, then three quarto volumes a week (p. 61).
