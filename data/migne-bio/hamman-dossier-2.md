## 1. The Portrait Chapter (ch. 3, pp. 73–87)

### Physical presence and constitution
Hamman opens with a contemporary's description (H. Barbier): "Taille superbe, tête large et noblement portée, regard d'aigle où respire cependant la plus onctueuse sensibilité, parole énergique et colorée, facilité d'improvisation peu commune, constitution vigoureuse et capable de supporter les plus longues fatigues" — superb stature, eagle's gaze tempered by "the most unctuous sensibility," uncommon gift of improvisation, a constitution built for fatigue (p. 73). Migne's own gloss: "La Providence pour ainsi dire a chevillé notre âme à notre corps" — Providence has, so to speak, bolted our soul to our body (p. 73). Hamman adds that only "inactivity and chicanery" would wear that health down.

An anecdote from E. Veuillot: leaving his newspaper office late one evening, Migne heard street urchins jeering "couac, couac" at him; he chased them down without difficulty and thrashed them with his umbrella — "une volée de bois ecclésiastique" — shouting "Gredins, je vais vous montrer ce que c'est qu'un prêtre!" ("Scoundrels, I'll show you what a priest is!") (p. 74). To Pitra he wrote, "un peu goguenard": "Bonaparte avait raison de dire que la première qualité d'un général était la santé" — Bonaparte was right that a general's first quality is health (p. 74, letter of 19 April 1864).

### Work habits
- Sixteen hours of work a day; visible "qu'en semaine et d'une heure à deux" (only on weekdays, from one to two o'clock — printed on his letterhead) (p. 73).
- "Plus nous travaillons, plus nous sommes bien portants, heureux… nous ne prenons pas une heure de récréation par an; afin de conserver l'esprit plus libre, nous ne nous nourrissons pas mieux qu'un séminariste ou un ouvrier" — not one hour of recreation per year; he eats no better than a seminarian or a workman, to keep his mind free (pp. 73–74).
- A book a week did not exhaust him: he repeatedly launched new journals, opened a furniture department, and built organs for churches (p. 74). **Hamman's correction of a common reading:** to say "il lui fallait du bruit, du mouvement, du changement" (he needed noise, movement, change) is a myopic view; "Migne était de la race des créateurs, qui ont un dynamisme herculéen à consumer" — Migne was of the race of creators with a Herculean dynamism to burn (p. 74). Cardinal Pitra "voit plus juste" (sees truer): "J'aimais à me rappeler votre énergie d'Hercule, qui ne fit que s'accroître dans la crise de 1848" (letter, Wednesday of Holy Week 1872) (p. 74).
- The overwork, absence of exercise, and sixteen-hour days finally undermine "la robuste constitution du montagnard"; signs of physical decline multiply in the last ten years of his life, with alternations of recovery and discouragement (p. 74).

### Honesty and money
- "Entreprenant mais sans argent, de culture limitée mais curieux d'esprit" — enterprising but penniless, of limited culture but curious mind; with support from neither Church nor State, and no capital but energy and tenacity, he builds "une industrie gigantesque, sainement gérée" — a gigantic, soundly managed industry (pp. 74–75).
- "Ce brasseur d'affaires est honnête, il vit pauvrement et ne s'enrichit pas. Des millions qui passent par ses mains, rien n'y reste collé" — of the millions passing through his hands, nothing sticks (p. 75). At the fire he valued his personal furniture at **662 francs**, against the **six million** his Ateliers were worth (p. 75; Tribunal de la Seine report, in F. de Mély).
- He pays modest but regular salaries, on the 7th of each month; his accounting is exact and current; even the fire "ne lèse ni prêteur ni souscripteur, il ne lésera que lui" — harmed no lender or subscriber, only himself. No detractor ever impugned his honesty (p. 75). To Pitra: "En général, ne vous embarrassez point de vos comptes matériels. Il y a de l'ordre ici; jamais vous ne serez victime de rien" (1843 letter, archives no. 399) (p. 75).
- Baron Henrion received **2,000 francs** per volume of his *Histoire* (p. 75).

### Will and self-conception
- Hamman's most quoted image: "L'abbé Migne tient du bulldozer, il avance, il fonce, il n'a cure ni des fleurs ni des coléoptères sur sa route" — the abbé Migne has something of the bulldozer; he advances, he charges, caring nothing for the flowers or the beetles in his path (p. 75).
- Migne to Pitra (8 Feb 1865): "Ce n'est qu'en voulant ainsi que je suis venu à bout d'édifier la Patrologie, contre tous les obstacles. Mes défauts ont fait mes qualités, et j'ai vaincu" — only by willing like this did I manage to build the Patrology against every obstacle. My defects made my qualities, and I have conquered (p. 75).
- He feels invested with a mission: to save the Church's patrimony, "faire renaître dans le clergé les études fortes" (revive strong studies in the clergy); the disavowal of the archbishop of Paris (M. de Quélen) does not stop him (p. 76).
- He seems to have wanted the episcopate: under the July Monarchy (so before 1848) he figures in a file of candidates "trop âgés ou non sérieux" (too old or not serious) for whom no special dossier exists (p. 76, after P. Poupard, *Correspondance… Garibaldi et Mathieu*, p. 64).
- He is lucid about honors: though he cites his canonries of Rodez and Saint-Flour, the titles he proclaims are self-made — "fondateur de l'Univers," "créateur des Ateliers catholiques, éditeur de la Bibliothèque universelle du clergé." "Ces titres ne se donnent pas, ils se créent" (p. 76). He twice refused the Légion d'honneur (letter to Pitra, 27 Jan 1865) and would accept neither Roman decoration nor pontifical brief, "qui se donnent presque à tout le monde" (which are given to nearly everyone): "L'œil de Dieu et celui de ma conscience me suffisent pleinement pour m'encourager à faire plus encore" (letter to Pitra, 24 May 1864) (p. 76). His donations to the Vatican Library, prompted by Pitra, thereby express real disinterestedness (p. 76).

### Political and commercial shrewdness
- Son of an enriched merchant, he shrewdly gave both parties dividing the French Church a voice in his newspaper. "Au fond de son cœur, le curé de Puiseaux est légitimiste, le fondateur de l'Univers, ultramontain" — at heart, the curé of Puiseaux is legitimist; the founder of *L'Univers*, ultramontane (p. 77). He praises collaborator Jehan de Saint-Clavien to Pitra as "sans contredit un des catholiques les plus ultramontains" (pp. 77 & n. 16; Saint-Clavien compiled nearly twenty of the dictionaries).
- The clergy savings bank (Caisse d'épargne): he offers **5%**, noting "avec adresse et honnêteté": "Plus nous offririons, plus on devrait se méfier de nous" — the more we offered, the more one ought to distrust us; and, from the prospectus "Mode licite, avantageux, solide et méritoire de placer ses fonds": "Une heureuse expérience nous a appris que nos lecteurs ne veulent recevoir rien au-delà du taux légal." Hamman's dry aside: "Heureuse époque!" (p. 77).
- "Habilement, trop habilement il lie les affaires temporelles et spirituelles" — he ties temporal and spiritual affairs together too cleverly: the commercial use of Mass intentions is Hamman's example. Hamman's barb: had he been an advocate at God's tribunal, "il eût troqué… la félicité éternelle contre les deux Patrologies" — he would have bartered eternal felicity for the two Patrologies (p. 77). **But Hamman also cautions (n. 18) not to exaggerate this traffic**, citing Migne to Pitra (4 Oct 1864) on selling a Patrology to the Benedictines of Montreal "avec le consentement exprès de l'Ordinaire."

### The dark side: literary property and lawsuits
- "Honnête en affaires, Migne est moins scrupuleux quand il s'agit de la propriété littéraire" (p. 77). Already in 1835 he was hauled before the tribunal correctionnel by "le sieur Pillet" for usurping the title of his newspaper. "Il y aurait une enquête à faire sur les procès qui jalonnent toute l'existence de l'éditeur" — a whole inquiry could be made into the lawsuits that punctuate the editor's existence (p. 77). Migne to Pitra, in his own hand (11 March 1857): "Je ne pourrai répondre que dans quelques jours… à cause de ces procès qui absorbent tout mon temps" (pp. 77–78).
- The counterfeiting suits became legendary enough for the Goncourts to allude to them (Journal, 21 Aug 1864) in a note Hamman calls "sarcastique et injuste" (p. 78). **Hamman's corrective:** not all the thefts are imputable to Migne — Pitra himself flagged a borrowing by baron Henrion made without the responsible party's knowledge; the dictionary collaborators plagiarized 17th-century works with references "discrètes au point de passer inaperçues, parfois… inexistantes" (p. 78).
- Concrete cases: the abbé Bonnetty noted in a review (Annales de philosophie chrétienne 20, 1849, p. 316) that the Gospels of the Arian Ulfilas simply reproduced the fresh edition of Gabelentz and Loebe, with neither place nor date of printing given — "L'éditeur s'épargnait par là de payer des droits de reproduction" (he thereby spared himself reproduction fees). For the Novels of Leo VI the Wise, Migne went as far as giving inexact references, reproducing Zachariae von Lingenthal's edition "sans manquer aucun des lapsus typographiques" (without missing a single typographical slip) while citing old editions of the Corpus juris civilis (p. 78 & nn. 21–22, citing A. Dain).
- Hamman's verdict, with the pastiche of La Fontaine: "Il leur fit, en les volant, beaucoup d'honneur. Et leur permit de survivre!" — in robbing them he did them much honor, and allowed them to survive (p. 78).
- Self-aggrandizement: "ce dilaté sthénique se réserve tout l'avant de la scène" — this sthenic expansive keeps the whole front of the stage for himself, spreading over his publications as if author rather than editor. To dom Pitra, "le maître d'œuvre des deux Patrologies" (the master-builder of the two Patrologies), he devotes only two lines — and those in a table of contents (PL 218, col. 338). He probably gratuitously credited himself with the "annotations" of the *Démonstrations évangéliques* (p. 78).

### Kindness, generosity, workers
- "Ce chapardage, cette jactance vont de pair avec une grande délicatesse de cœur" — this pilfering and boastfulness go hand in hand with great delicacy of heart (p. 79). His kindness to the poor as a simple desservant was proverbial; he took priests in difficulty into his Ateliers and let them live decently (p. 79). But no soft touch: on père Lacombe (a Benedictine who eventually left his Order), to Pitra (27 Nov 1859): "Croyez-vous que le révérend père Lacombe a eu le courage de vouloir m'emprunter de l'argent et me demander du travail. Je me suis bien gardé d'acquiescer à sa demande" (p. 79).
- He is patient with sick or late collaborators, solicitous of friends' health — delighted to tell Pitra (10 Aug 1858) that he has cured dom Guéranger of his rheumatism (p. 79). Bonnetty gives him admiration and support (p. 79).
- "Le directeur des Ateliers catholiques aime ses ouvriers." His first thought at the fire was not his enterprise but his workforce thrown out of work: "Que vont devenir mes pauvres ouvriers?" — what will become of my poor workers? (Moniteur du Cantal, 15 Feb 1868) (p. 79).
- Munificence: in 1855 the Oblate superior at Nancy asked for a few Patrology volumes; Migne sent all **217 volumes of the Patrologia latina**, adding: "Ces livres seront beaucoup mieux dans la bibliothèque des Oblats qu'empilés dans mes magasins. Quant au paiement, je vous laisse toute latitude d'y pourvoir à votre aise en me donnant quelque chose tous les six mois" (pp. 79–80; from M. Soullier's life of Fr. Soullier). Hamman's coda: "Bon cœur mais aussi bon vendeur" — good heart, but also good salesman (p. 80).

### The prospectuses and publicity machine
- Pitra reproached him for the style of his "abominables affiches." Migne's unrepentant reply (27 March 1858): "Mes prospectus sont l'expression de mes convictions les plus intimes : personne au monde ne serait capable de les faire, pas plus que personne ne pourrait mener la vie que je mène ni exécuter ce que j'exécute. Chateaubriand et Lamennais morts, Guizot et Thiers, Lamartine et Cousin vivants, eussent-ils voulu… faire mes prospectus à ma place que jamais ils n'eussent obtenu mon consentement" (p. 80).
- Hamman: "Poussée à ce point, l'affirmation s'appelle fanfaronnade, la ténacité, entêtement" — pushed to this point, affirmation is called bragging, and tenacity, obstinacy. "Migne est incapable de distinguer les plans, de ne pas se confondre avec son entreprise" (p. 80).
- Everything serves publicity — letterhead margins, pages three and four of his stationery, carry titles and prices even of works never published (p. 81). The stationery invitation: "Si vous êtes curieux de voir s'exercer à la fois et en grand tous les arts relatifs à la typographie, vous êtes prié d'honorer de votre présence les Ateliers catholiques du Petit-Montrouge" (p. 81) — see §8 Texture below for the steam-press boast that follows.
- He compares himself to a new Samson; beside his œuvre, the piercing of Mont Cenis and the building of ten cathedrals are "jeux d'enfants" (child's play) (p. 81; Préface aux Tables, PL 218, col. 1). **Hamman's judgment:** "Ce manque de mesure — et de modestie, a nui à l'entreprise et à l'entrepreneur" — this lack of measure, and of modesty, harmed both enterprise and entrepreneur; caught in his own game, accelerating his output, "l'abbé Migne essouffle son public" — he winds his public (p. 81).
- Deeper need: not just subscribers, but to be taken seriously — recognized not first by foreigners but by France, not only by other Christian confessions but "en catholicité," not only by fellow priests but by the guardians of the faith, the hierarchy (pp. 81–82).

### Faith, churchmanship, ecumenism, orthodoxy
- "L'abbé Migne est d'Église. Sa foi a la santé de ses artères. Elle est comme l'homme : simple, solide, tenace" — his faith has the health of his arteries: simple, solid, tenacious like the man (p. 82). He calls his daily heroism "son martyre" (his martyrdom) (p. 82).
- The modest encouragement of a modest provincial council at Périgueux, 1856, went straight to his heart; he asked Pitra (27 March 1858) to circulate its text in Rome. The council praised "le prêtre si connu, dont le génie et l'heureux dévouement arrachent aux ténèbres par la réimpression, les monuments de la tradition ecclésiastique…" (p. 82).
- Once his work was done, he expected to offer the mission countries the **ten million or so francs** at which he valued the whole of his Ateliers (letter to Pitra, 29 March 1868) (p. 82).
- "Œcuménique à une époque où le fait était insolite" — ecumenical at a time when the fact was unheard-of: he opened his collections to Eastern-church texts and to Anglican and Protestant exegetes and theologians (p. 82). This drew hostility: a priest of Blois had been working "depuis des années avec l'approbation de son évêque, à recueillir toutes les hétérodoxies qui sont dans la Patrologie" — for years, with his bishop's approval, collecting all the heterodoxies in the Patrology (letter to Pitra, 15 Feb 1858) — "Ce prêtre n'était pas le seul" (pp. 82–83).
- "Œcuménique mais orthodoxe, comme pouvait l'être un ultramontain." He insisted on publishing corrective tables on the doctrinal quality of the Fathers "par amour de l'orthodoxie," their absence being "pour mon cœur de catholique une espèce de cauchemar" — for my Catholic heart a kind of nightmare (letter to Pitra, 19 April 1864) (p. 83).
- He appended to the last volume of the *Démonstrations évangéliques* a profession of faith "digne du lyrisme des pères d'Orient": "La sainte Église romaine est pour moi la mère et la reine de toutes les Églises, le centre de l'unité, la source de toute juridiction, l'interprète de la vérité… Je la respecte comme ma mère, moi le moindre d'entre les agneaux du troupeau divin" (p. 83; Démonstrations évangéliques t. 18, col. 1353–1356).
- Rome's coldness wounded him. He refused a papal brief because its administrative formula — "dans l'impossibilité de lire nous-même votre ouvrage" (being unable to read your work ourselves) — annulled its meaning; he would have loved the pope to write that he had plunged at length into "les archives de l'Église" (pp. 83–84). In 1864: "Je serais curieux de savoir si le Saint-Père connaît mon existence. Mon dessein bien arrêté… est d'aller à Rome, dès que j'aurai fini, déposer moi-même un exemplaire de toutes mes grandes publications" (19 April 1864) (p. 84). Later, stung by rumors: "il paraît que j'ai été dépeint à Sa Sainteté comme un spéculateur… une profonde peine ne m'empêchera pas de continuer ma vie de martyr" (27 Jan 1865) (p. 84). And when Pius IX, at Pitra's request, blessed the Patrology and its editor: "J'ai été flatté et même touché… Je ne croyais pas qu'Il me sût au monde, malgré mes accablants travaux pour l'Église" — I did not think He knew I existed, despite my crushing labors for the Church (p. 84).
- Hamman's summary image: "En ouvrant les Ateliers catholiques, il avait simplement changé de paroisse, sa paroisse était désormais le monde de ses lecteurs" — in opening the Ateliers catholiques he had simply changed parishes; his parish was now the world of his readers (p. 84). His objective, stated a week after the 1868 fire: to die "comme le prêtre qui aurait fait le plus de bien à l'Église" — as the priest who had done the most good for the Church (letter to Pitra, 29 March 1868) (pp. 84–85).
- Testament-like letters: (12 April 1858) "Ne craignez pas que je faiblisse… que Dieu me conserve la santé, et j'ose me charger du reste; je marcherai à travers tous les obstacles et j'arriverai, parce que j'ai le sentiment du bien que je fais à l'Église, en ressuscitant sa Tradition" (p. 85). And in terms Hamman calls "presque pauliniens" (27 Jan 1865): "on me dit et même m'écrit de toutes les parts du globe catholique, que je suis sans comparaison le prêtre du monde entier qui ait rendu le plus de service à l'Église. Sans mon énergie, en effet, où serait la tradition…?" (p. 85).

### Decline and the last portrait
- Confessing failing health to the newly created cardinal Pitra (19 April 1864): his most sacred duty is "de terminer ce que j'ai commencé et d'apurer tous mes comptes matériels… pour ne pas scandaliser par de mauvaises affaires" — and his remedies are touchingly homely: "la moutarde blanche pour l'estomac, à l'eau-de-vie camphrée et mélissée pour l'enflure et la paralysie" — white mustard for the stomach, camphorated and lemon-balm brandy for the swelling and the paralysis (pp. 85–86).
- "L'effort finit par avoir raison de cet homme, planté comme un chêne" — effort finally got the better of this man planted like an oak. He confesses: "Nous aspirons à nous recueillir un peu, après une effrayante tension des facultés intellectuelles et physiques"; and, in a movement Hamman calls "romantique": "Je brise ma plume et me contente de gémir" — I break my pen and content myself with groaning (letter to Pitra, 8 Feb 1855) (pp. 86 & n. 48). What overwork prepared, the 1868 fire finished; "Si les forces physiques ont cédé, l'homme est demeuré invincible" (p. 86).
- Three portraits survive (published by F. de Mély): one from before the publishing ventures ("insouciant et conventionnel"); one of 1855, with the just-completed *Cursus completus* of the PL displayed on the inevitable side table; and the last, the best-graven: canon's camail and cross over an austerely white surplice "sans dentelle" (without lace), head still erect, hair curling white at the temples "sous le harnais," the gaze "volontaire, obstiné," the left eye almost closed by the infirmity of the final years — a certain good-natured mischief of the earlier portrait effaced. In the last portrait he holds only a proof-sheet (pp. 86–87).
- Closing line of the chapter: history must keep the portrait of "ce prêtre, curé de campagne, devenu le plus grand éditeur de son siècle" — this priest, a country curé, become the greatest publisher of his century (p. 87).

## 2. The Pre-history of Patristic Collections (ch. 4, pp. 91–106)

Hamman's purpose: to measure the Patrology's novelty one must inventory what filled a monastic or university library in patristics before Migne. Migne intended the integral reproduction of these collections, studies and texts to constitute "la première somme de la tradition catholique" — the first *summa* of Catholic tradition (p. 91). The Patrology was to combine (a) the best editions of the Fathers and ancient Christian writers with (b) the literary, historical, and theological studies indispensable as introduction (p. 91).

### 2a. Literary and theological studies absorbed into the Patrology (pp. 91–95)
The index of modern authors of dissertations/introductions/annotations fills **eighty columns for the PL alone** (PL 218, 307–400); the Benedictines of Saint-Maur "s'y taillent la part du lion" — take the lion's share (pp. 91–92). Key names:

- **Ellies du Pin**, *Nouvelle bibliothèque des auteurs ecclésiastiques* (1686–1711, 47 vols.). An avowed Jansenist; praised by the *Journal des Savants*, attacked by Bossuet, condemned by the archbishop of Paris, put on the Index in 1757. PL borrows his literary-critical study on Optatus of Milevis — "Ce qui attire des difficultés à l'abbé Migne" (which drew difficulties on Migne; cf. Mgr Baillès' letter, Feb 1857) (p. 92).
- **Dom Nicolas Le Nourry** (Saint-Germain-des-Prés team; †1724), *Apparatus ad bibliothecam maximam veterum Patrum* (Paris, 1694–1697, 2 vols.), applying more rigorous criteria to the texts of the great Lyon Bibliotheca; analysis stops at the 4th century. Migne borrows his dissertations on the Apostolic Fathers, Tertullian, Cyprian, Minucius Felix, Arnobius, Lactantius, Ps.-Dionysius (p. 92). Also edited Cassiodorus and Ambrose (p. 100).
- **Le Nain de Tillemont**, *Mémoires pour servir à l'histoire des six premiers siècles* (Paris, 1693–1712, 16 vols.), "qui n'a pas perdu de sa valeur." **Hamman: "Je n'en vois pas de trace dans la Patrologie"** — I see no trace of it in the Patrology (p. 93). (A noteworthy negative finding.)
- **Dom Rémi Ceillier**, *Histoire générale des auteurs sacrés et ecclésiastiques* (Paris, 1729–1763, 23 vols., constantly reissued; re-editions by Caillau 1838–39 and Bauzon 1860–69). Lorraine Benedictine, died "saintement" at Flavigny; gave for each author from the 1st to the 13th century "la vie, le catalogue, la critique, le jugement, la chronologie, l'analyse, le dénombrement des différentes éditions" (p. 93).
- **Dominique Schramm** OSB, *Analysis operum SS. Patrum* (Augsburg, 1780–1796, 18 vols., ending with Epiphanius); Migne borrows his arguments and summaries for Tertullian's works (p. 93).
- **Gottfried Lumper** OSB (†1800), *Historia theologico-critica… Patrum* (Augsburg, 1783–1799); PL and PG make "de généreux emprunts" for the 3rd-century Latins (Tertullian, Minucius Felix, Novatian, Cyprian) and the Apostolic Fathers and apologists (p. 93).
- **Lutherans:** J. Gerhard's manual "a le mérite d'avoir créé le mot *Patrologia*" — has the merit of having coined the word (p. 93); J. G. Walch, *Bibliotheca patristica* (Jena, 1770; revised Danz 1834); C. Oudin, learned Premonstratensian who went over to the Reform (pp. 93–94).
- **The two dominant names: Fabricius and Schönemann.** J. A. Fabricius (†1738), *Bibliotheca graeca* (Hamburg, 1705–1728, 14 vols.) and *Bibliotheca latina* (1697): PL prints his introduction to Philastrius of Brescia's *De haeresibus* and notices on scores of authors from Sedatus of Nîmes (or Béziers) to Pierre de Vaux-Cernai (†1213); PG uses him for the lost books of Justin (p. 94). C. T. Gottlob Schönemann (†1802), *Bibliotheca historico-litteraria* (Leipzig, 1792–1794, 2 vols.): **furnished, outside the Maurists, the greatest number of notices on Latin writers**, from Pope Julius I to Benedict III (p. 94).
- In France, one Reformed name: Jean Daillé (†1670); in Holland, J. Le Clerc (†1736), whose *De l'incrédulité* Migne printed in the *Démonstrations évangéliques* (p. 94).
- **Anglicans**, faithful to "leur génie propre," preferred particular researches: John Usher [Ussher] of Armagh (†1656) on Ignatius and Polycarp; J. Pearson of Chester, whose *Vindiciae Epistolarum S. Ignatii* (Oxford, 1672) stands in PG 5, 37–472; J. E. Grabe (†1706), *Spicilegium SS. Patrum ut et Haereticorum*; Henry Dodwell (†1711), dissertations on Irenaeus and Cyprian, present in the Patrology (PG 7; PL 4); W. Cave (†1713), *Historia litteraria scriptorum ecclesiasticorum* (London, 1688–1698), from which Migne takes a number of notices (pp. 94–95).

### 2b. The collections themselves (pp. 95–106)
The idea of gathering texts of diverse authors goes back to the manuscript age; the East made groupings from the 12th century (the Epistle to Diognetus belonged to an apologetic compilation — "lointain ancêtre des Démonstrations évangéliques") (p. 95). Migne himself acknowledged in a prospectus that he stood in a long history (p. 95). The "Bibliothèques des Pères" appear in the same era and cities as the *opera omnia* movement: **Basel first, Paris later** (p. 95).

- **Precursors at Basel:** Jean Sichard — *Antidotum contra diversas… haereses* (1528), *Orthodoxographa* (1550; 76 writers), *Haereseologia* (1556); Jacques Grynius, *Monumenta ss. Patrum orthodoxographa* (1569; already 85 authors); G. Fabricius' corpus of ancient Christian poets (1564) (p. 96).
- **Marguarin de la Bigne** (†1589), canon of Bayeux, "un des plus savants prêtres de son temps" — "le véritable ancêtre des bibliothèques patristiques" (the true ancestor of the patristic libraries): first to conceive uniting the Fathers' writings in one corpus, the *Bibliotheca sanctorum Patrum supra ducentos* (Paris, 1575–1578), 8 folio volumes + a 9th in appendix. Migne paid homage to it "comme à son archétype"; it "fut pendant cent cinquante ans le pivot de tous les travaux de ce genre" — was for 150 years the pivot of all works of the kind ([Migne], "Réflexions instructives et curieuses sur la Patrologie," *Annales de philosophie chrétienne* 10, 1864, p. 79 — an article Hamman calls "fondamental" for this subject) (p. 96). Its inspiration was apologetic — a Catholic reply to the Protestant *Centuries of Magdeburg* (Basel, 1559–1574) amid the confessional appeal to the Fathers (p. 96).
  - Structure (p. 97): thematic, like medieval florilegia — (I) Letters, (II) History, (III) Parenesis & Morals, (IV) Liturgy, (V) Against heresies, (VI) Scripture commentaries, (VII) Homilies, (VIII) Poems & varia. Two hundred writers, antiquity through the Middle Ages; **all the great classics conspicuously absent** (no Tertullian, Origen, Hilary, Ambrose, Cappadocians, Augustine, Chrysostom — left to the editors of their *opera*); **all texts in Latin only**, the Greeks in translation.
- **Magna Bibliotheca** (Cologne, 1618–1622, ed. Alardus Wyelius, 14 vols.): not just a new epithet but a structural recasting — 100 new authors, chronological ordering; the editor's "astuce" (trick) was to make each tome's number coincide with its authors' century (p. 97). Amplified from hand to hand by Fronton du Duc (Auctarium 1624), G. Morel (Latin supplement 1639), Fr. Combefis (Auctaria 1648, 1672). Retaken at Paris 1618–1624 (adding Hittorp's liturgical collection), then "se figea, comme les santons de Provence, dans sa dernière et définitive forme" — froze, like the santons of Provence, into its last definitive form — at **Lyon, 1677**, superlative added: the **Maxima Bibliotheca veterum Patrum et antiquorum scriptorum ecclesiasticorum**, 27 vols., "de Lyon" (p. 97). (Hamman notes he borrowed its edition of the *Collectio gallicana* for PLS III, 550–697.)
  - Details (p. 98): vol. 1 opens with Philippe Despont's preface; a hundred more minor writers; runs to the 16th century (Palaeologus, Bessarion, ending with Sifiud/Sylaka †1552); four Indices; a 27th volume of addenda; satellites — Jacques Hommey's Supplement, Philippe de Saint-Jacques's one-volume Epitome, Le Nourry's Apparatus.
- **The 18th century:** exhausted by that effort, erudition turned to smaller, specialized collections — "Anecdota, Miscellanea, Monumenta, Bibliothèques se multiplient" across France, Italy, England, Germany, with new, usually critical editions of many unpublished Greek and Latin texts (p. 98). Migne himself gave a state of the question in the *Annales de philosophie chrétienne* article (p. 98).
- **Jacques Sirmond** SJ (1559–1651): Roman researches alongside Baronius; then rector of the collège de Clermont in Paris, forming a research team with Fronton du Duc and Petau. His posthumous *Opera varia* (Paris, 1696, 5 vols.) gave the Patrology numerous unpublished texts, e.g. the introduction to Prosper of Aquitaine (PL 51), the biography and edition of Sidonius Apollinaris (PL 59) and Avitus of Vienne (pp. 98–99).
- **Jean-Baptiste Cotelier**: child prodigy at ease in Latin, Greek, Hebrew, who dazzled Richelieu and the court; chosen by Colbert to catalogue the Greek manuscripts of the Bibliothèque Royale, where he found many patristic inedita; published the "Pères apostoliques" (Paris, 1672, 2 vols.) and *Ecclesiae graecae monumenta* (Paris, 1677–1686, 3 vols.; a 4th being Montfaucon's *Analecta graeca*, 1688) (p. 99). (Hamman notes in passing that the very grouping "Apostolic Fathers" is today contested, n. 49.)
- **Étienne Baluze** (1630–1718): devoted much of his life to hunting manuscripts of the Fathers and supplying correct editions; 7 vols. of *Miscellanea* (Paris, 1678–1715; re-ed. Mansi, Lucca); keeper of Colbert's magnificent library (now in the BN). Migne borrows in part his edition of Cyprian and a study on Marius Mercator — **but not his Mercator edition, "qui valait mieux que celle de Garnier, retenue sans doute pour ses dissertations"** (which was worth more than Garnier's, retained no doubt for its dissertations) — while his editions of Vincent of Lérins and Salvian of Marseille do stand in PL (PL 50; PL 53). He took position on the *De morte persecutorum*, attributing it to Lactantius (revised later by dom Pitra), and annotated Cyprian and popes Cornelius and Gelasius (p. 99).
- **The Maurists** "dominent la scène patristique pendant deux siècles et jusqu'à la Révolution française." **Migne reproduced their editions of the Greek and Latin Fathers integrally — introductions, notes and critical apparatus — from Ambrose to Augustine, from Basil the Great to John Chrysostom** (pp. 99–100). Individually:
  - **Luc d'Achery** (†1685), pioneer and venerated "père et promoteur des études" at reformed Saint-Germain-des-Prés; *Spicilegium* (Paris, 1655–1677, 13 vols.) (p. 100).
  - **Jean Mabillon**, his "génial disciple": *Vetera Analecta* (Paris, 1675–1685, 4 vols.); collaborator on the "publication exemplaire" of Augustine's works (p. 100).
  - **Bernard de Montfaucon**: first destined for arms; illness, retreat, monastic life; learned oriental languages at Saint-Germain; specialist of the Greek Fathers — Athanasius and Chrysostom; *Collectio nova Patrum* (Paris, 1706, 2 vols.), mainly Eusebius and Athanasius; †1741 (p. 100).
  - **Edmond Martène and Ursin Durand**: *Thesaurus novus anecdotorum* (Paris, 1717, 5 vols.) and *Veterum Scriptorum et monumentorum… amplissima collectio* (Paris, 1724, 9 vols.) — more historical than patristic, but easily traced in the Patrology (pp. 100–101).
- **Italy: Louis-Antoine Muratori** (1672–1750): at the Ambrosiana at barely 25; then librarian-archivist of the duke of Modena. "Sa production littéraire est prodigieuse et éclipse presque celle de ses contemporains, Baluze et Mabillon." *Anecdota latina* (Milan, 1697–98 + Padua) and *Anecdota graeca* (Padua, 1700); its newly edited texts remain accessible in Migne (Paulinus of Nola poems/dissertations, PL 61; the *fides Bacchiarii*, PL 20; Gregory of Nazianzus epigrams, PG 38; 45 letters of Firmus of Caesarea, PG 77; plus important dissertations on the agapetae, agape meals, Christian burial) (p. 101).
- **Germany:** Bernard Pez OSB — *Thesaurus anecdotorum novissimus* (Vienna, 1728–29, 6 vols.) and the *Bibliotheca ascetica* (Regensburg, 1723–1740), mostly medieval authors; the Lutheran **Johann Christoph Wolf** [printed "Nolf" in OCR], *Anecdota Graeca, sacra et profana* (Hamburg, 1722–24, 4 vols.) (p. 101).
- **Fr. Oberthür** — the century closes with "l'éditeur d'une double patrologie latine et grecque…, véritable précurseur de l'abbé Migne" — editor of a double Latin and Greek patrology, a true precursor of Migne (Würzburg, from 1777; the Greek series, original text with Latin version, covers the apologists to Clement of Alexandria and Origen — the last 16 of 21 vols. giving Origen after Ch. de la Rue; the Latin series covers Hilary of Poitiers and Optatus of Milevis) (pp. 101–102).
- **André Galland**, Oratorian (1709–1780): his Greco-Latin *Bibliotheca veterum Patrum* (Venice, 1765–1781, 14 vols. + appendix; new ed. 1788; Index Bologna 1863) "marque une date et un progrès" — **the first to supply the Greek text with Latin version**. 380 authors of the first twelve centuries, chronological, mixing Greeks and Latins; complete works only for the Apostolic Fathers, Justin, Minucius Felix's *Octavius*; no Irenaeus, no Clement of Alexandria or Origen, no Tertullian or Cyprian — his purpose being minor works and newly edited fragments missing from previous "bibliothèques" (*quae in editis desiderantur*) (p. 102). Migne's own critique, quoted by Hamman: "Ce vaste travail perdit peu à peu de ses proportions premières; l'éditeur, trop livré peut-être à ses propres forces, se réduisit à un choix arbitraire dont il est difficile de voir le plan" (pp. 102–103). Its careful introductions, choice of editions, corrected texts and explanatory notes gave it value; the Patrology draws on it heavily for notices and critical notes and reprints its editions (p. 103).
- **Chronological frontiers:** all collections hesitated. Galland stops at the 12th century; **Migne overflows into the 13th; he would have liked to go to the council of [Florence]** (p. 103; the word is garbled in OCR — "depireate" — but ch. 5 confirms the PG horizon of Innocent III/Florence).
- Beyond the classics, Migne's edition assembled texts from collections of limited object (polemic, asceticism, preaching, poetry) — "véritable jeu de puzzle" — plus newly discovered catenae, Eastern-church works, liturgical, hagiographical and juridical writings, "et même des diplômes et des chartes, par la grâce — et la faute — de dom Pitra" — and even diplomas and charters, by the grace — and the fault — of dom Pitra (p. 103).
- **The nascent 19th century:** in England, **Martin Joseph Routh**, *Reliquiae Sacrae* (Oxford, 1814–1818, 4 vols.; enlarged 2nd ed. 1846–48, 5 vols.), rich in inedita drawn chiefly from the Bodleian; Migne profited from the 2nd edition for the PG (p. 103). In Rome, **Angelo Mai** (†1854), the future cardinal, "l'infatigable éditeur de textes retrouvés" for forty years across the Ambrosiana and Vaticana; recoverer of books XI–XIV of the Sibylline Oracles; his three collections "fraient la voie à Migne" — *Scriptorum veterum nova collectio* (Rome, 1825–1838, 10 vols. in-4°), *Spicilegium romanum* (1839–1844, 10 vols. in-8°), *Nova Patrum Bibliotheca* (Rome, 1844–1854, 7 vols. in-4°, continued by J. Cozza after his death). Contemporary with Migne's edition, it greatly enriched the PG (long list of borrowings in n. 80: Alexander of Alexandria's sermons, Athanasius' festal letters, Didymus, Gregory of Nyssa on the Holy Spirit, Eusebius epitomes and Psalm catenae, Diadochus, Theodoret on the Trinity, Sophronius homilies, John Damascene, Germanus, Nicephorus, Methodius, Photius…); its Latin — especially Augustinian — texts had *not* yet found place in Migne (Hamman placed the authentic ones in **PLS II**, 443–527, the inauthentic at 1121–1286) (pp. 103–104).
- **Ch. G. Heine**, *Bibliotheca Anecdotorum* (Paris–Leipzig, 1848, posthumous): explicitly mentioned in the Migne–Pitra correspondence; J.-B. Malou had signaled it to Migne; used in PL 99 (p. 104).
- **Armand-Benjamin Caillau (1794–1850)** — "le précurseur immédiat de l'abbé Migne": one of the rare French priests of the early 19th century versed in patristics and eager to spread it; "effacé, modeste — il refusa l'archevêché de Smyrne — efficace" (self-effacing, modest — he refused the archbishopric of Smyrna — effective), "un promoteur du retour aux Pères." He began in 1826 (publication Paris, 1829–1842) a collection planned at **200 volumes**; it **stopped at volume 133 at the moment Migne announced his two Patrologies** (pp. 104–105). Greek Fathers given only in Latin translation; most writers only in selections; Chrysostom and Augustine integral. With Saint-Yves he added to the Maurist Augustine unpublished sermons and writings missing from the Benedictine edition — omitted from the Patrology, they hold pride of place in Hamman's Supplement (PLS II). He also completed the Maurist edition of Gregory of Nazianzus: finding the manuscript among the heirs of M. de Verneuil (a Maurist collaborator), he revised and completed it and published volume II, which stands in PG 35–38, enriched "d'une version latine fort élégante" (p. 105).
- **Migne's entry:** "Toujours circonspect autant qu'entreprenant" — always as circumspect as enterprising — he first floated "un ballon d'essai" (a trial balloon): re-editions of Augustine and John Chrysostom in Latin translation. Encouraged by this first success, he undertook the Patrology. "Il s'en fallut de peu qu'il y ajoutât les œuvres de saint Jérôme pour couper l'herbe sous les pieds d'un concurrent, Parent-Desbarres" — he came close to adding Jerome's works to cut the grass from under a competitor, Parent-Desbarres (letters to dom Guéranger, 11 April and 21 May 1842) (pp. 105–106). Note 88 adds: for the Latin translation of Greek texts, dom Guéranger and the Solesmes Benedictines contributed much; Migne to Pitra (27 March 1858): "Je donnerai la préférence à dom Guéranger."

### 2c. Excursus: the feeder collections (pp. 107–111)
An appendix lists, by genre, the other collections used by the Patrology — polemical bibliothèques (Gesner, Simler, Maugin, Foggini, Oberthür), ascetic (Manutius, Goldast, Chantelou, Poussines, Pez), homiletic/*concionatoriae* (Florimonte, Combefis, Pelt), Apostolic Fathers (Cotelier, Ittig, Russel, Horneman, Hefele, Jacobson), Christian poets (Aldus Manutius 1501–02, G. Fabricius, Arévalo), particular collections (Klotz, Gersdorf), English Church Fathers (Wharton; the Oxford *Bibliotheca Patrum ecclesiae catholicae*, 1835–1855), diverse (Photius' *Myriobiblon*, Assemani's *Bibliotheca Orientalis*, Fabricius, Zacharias), thesauri (Canisius, Basnage, Martène–Durand), spicilegia (d'Achery, Grabe, Fabricius, Mai), analecta (Mabillon, Montfaucon, Kollarius, Amadutius), anecdota (Muratori, Pez, Wolf, Mingarelli, de Levis, d'Ansse de Villoison, Boissonade, Rheinwald), opera (Gretser, Labbe, Sirmond), miscellanea (Baluze, Duelz), monumenta (Hugo, Walchius, Bandinius, **Pertz's Monumenta Germaniae historica, Berlin 1826**), reliquiae sacrae (Schannat, Lami, Fontani, Münter, Routh), catenae (Felicianus, Zephyrinus, Nicephorus, Cramer), collections (Oudin, Zacagni, Martène, Hoffmann, Galeardus, Trombelli, Lorenzana, Mai) (pp. 107–111).

**Attribution note (n. 2, p. 107):** the nomenclature published in *Annales de philosophie chrétienne* 10, 1864, pp. 80–81 (signed [Migne]) "semble faite par dom Pitra" — seems to be dom Pitra's work — since at the same period Pitra wrote Migne (8 Dec 1864, letter communicated by dom Clair of Hautecombe) that he was preparing "une table de toutes les collections patrologiques présentant la série des pièces ainsi recueillies, depuis les plus anciennes chaînes jusqu'à votre patrologie," intending to absorb Dowling and Ittig *ex integro* "mais tout ce qu'ils ont omis." The bibliographic reference tools Migne worked from were **Th. Ittig**, *De bibliothecis et catenis Patrum* (Leipzig, 1707; editions classed by city) and **J. G. Dowling**, *Notitia scriptorum ss. Patrum… post annum 1700* (Oxford, 1839; collections 1700–1839) (p. 95 n. 30; cf. Migne to Pitra, 2 May 1864).

## 3. The Genesis of PG and PL (ch. 5, pp. 115–122 — chunk breaks off here)

### Decision and rationale
- The reception of the Augustine and Chrysostom editions encouraged Migne to take up, on an enlarged basis, the great collections of the 16th–18th centuries: the program formula of the prospectus — "**Reproduction chronologique et intégrale de la tradition catholique, pendant les douze premiers siècles de l'Église, d'après les éditions les plus estimées**" — chronological and integral reproduction of Catholic tradition during the Church's first twelve centuries, after the most esteemed editions (p. 115; prospectus reprinted in *Annales de philosophie chrétienne* 14, 1846, p. 392).
- The venture was the more audacious in that "un certain Castelli" had just failed at the same thing despite the encouragement of Gregory XVI and several sovereigns (per J. de Ghellinck). "Migne était d'une autre trempe" — Migne was of another temper (p. 115).
- Migne's own stated reasons (prospectus): "Le prix démesuré et toujours croissant des anciennes éditions, la tendance historique et traditionnelle des études de notre époque, le besoin de plus en plus sérieux de recourir aux sources, surtout dans les investigations religieuses, le réveil de la science ecclésiastique et la restauration catholique du passé, rendaient indispensable une nouvelle et complète édition des Pères" — the immoderate and ever-rising price of the old editions; the historical bent of contemporary studies; the ever more serious need to go back to the sources; the reawakening of ecclesiastical science and the Catholic restoration of the past (p. 115).
- **The first prospectus was printed in 40,000 copies** (p. 115). Evoking the vanished age of patrons, Migne added in italics a line Hamman rereads "avec un sourire, tant elle est actuelle": "**La célébrité aujourd'hui s'attache à des œuvres moins austères**" — celebrity today attaches to less austere works (pp. 115–116).
- The lyrical dedication: "aux catholiques nos frères, aux frères séparés de toutes les communions protestantes, aux hommes voués aux investigations de la science élevée et forte, au clergé enseignant, évangélisant et militant…," and "au monde avide de progrès, nous donnons la Tradition du passé pour marcher en avant" — to a world avid for progress we give the Tradition of the past, in order to march forward — capped with Tertullian, *Traditio tibi praetendetur auctrix* (De corona 4), which Migne renders "La Tradition te permet de grandir" (p. 116).
- To dom Guéranger (25 Feb 1842): "Pas un éditeur n'aurait le courage d'entreprendre un tel cours" — not one publisher would have the courage to undertake such a course. The first prospectus did *not* get the hoped-for reception. Migne, undeterred: "Quoi qu'il en arrive, j'ai annoncé que je ferai ce cours; or je le ferai, **dussé-je manger tout ce que je possède**, et je ne crois pas être homme à reculer; trop de bien découlera de l'accomplissement de cette œuvre" — I shall do it though I should have to eat up everything I possess (p. 116). He knew the Patrology's success would not match his theology and Scripture *Cursus*. "Peu lui chaut" — little did he care (p. 116).
- **Hamman on Migne's method:** "L'audace ne tient pas lieu de compétence, ni l'artifice publicitaire, d'information scientifique. Migne connaissait ses limites; il savait utiliser les compétences et s'entourer de conseillers" — audacity is no substitute for competence… Migne knew his limits; he knew how to use expertise and surround himself with advisors. He did not print uninformed: his correspondence with Pitra shows he had studied Dowling's book and compared it knowledgeably with Ittig's (letter, 24 May 1864); he arbitrated the disputes over admitting or refusing an unorthodox author (letter, 11 Feb 1857) (pp. 116–117).
- The contract with the Benedictines provided that **literary direction and technical direction be distinct**, without excluding "les conseils réciproques des deux parties contractantes" (p. 117).

### Dom Guéranger's role
- By Migne's own testimony, **dom Guéranger was at the origin of the project**: "C'est à votre instigation et par vos conseils que j'ai entrepris la Patrologie" (letter to Guéranger, 22 Feb 1855) (p. 117).
- Migne offered Guéranger the literary and theological direction, sent him a draft plan (avant-projet) on **25 February 1842**, and offered to supply him "les ouvrages et les éditions" needed. **Guéranger declined.** Twelve years later, the PL complete, Migne let his pique show: "J'ai ainsi réalisé vos idées et vos vœux. Cependant, la Congrégation de Solesmes m'a laissé seul poursuivre la Patrologie latine" — I have thus realized your ideas and your wishes; yet the Congregation of Solesmes left me alone to pursue the Latin Patrology (p. 117).
- Solesmes was nonetheless "sans le savoir" (unknowingly) forming the enterprise's true master-builder: **Jean-Baptiste Pitra**, at that date a Benedictine novice (p. 117).

### Dom Jean-Baptiste Pitra: formation and entry
- Pitra's background: a young priest "féru d'antiquité chrétienne" who had **deciphered the inscription of Pectorius of Autun**; taught rhetoric at Autun (profane and Christian authors); drawn to Solesmes by the memory of the great Maurists. "Dom Guéranger, jaloux des terrains qu'il s'est réservés, lui abandonne la patristique" — Guéranger, jealous of the fields he had reserved for himself, abandoned patristics to him (p. 117). He made profession in **February 1843** and came to Paris to revive the ancient abbey and the work of Saint-Germain — which brought him physically near the Ateliers catholiques and eased the collaboration (p. 117).
- Obliged to make a **begging tour (tournée de quête)** to repair a financial disaster [of Solesmes], Pitra used it for research in the libraries of France, Switzerland, England, Belgium and Holland. At Reims he found a manuscript of Hincmar, "peut-être écrit de sa main" (perhaps autograph); at Metz he rescued in the nick of time parchments **the artillerymen were using to make gargousses** (powder cartridges) (p. 118; per A. Battandier, *Le cardinal J.-B. Pitra*, Paris, 1893, p. 127).
- Fruits gathered in his *Spicilegium Solesmense* (Paris, 1852–1858, 4 vols.), later the *Analecta sacra* (1876–1883, 8 vols.) and *Analecta novissima* (Frascati, 1885–1888). Noticed in Rome, called there, settled **1858**; studied oriental (canon) law; information journeys to Russia and Austria; on return **created cardinal and named prefect of the Vatican Library** (p. 118).
- **The meeting (1843):** when Pitra installed himself in Paris, friends put him in touch with the abbé Migne for his patristic enterprise. Pitra, "conquis" (won over), set to work at once on what he called, writing to Migne, "**votre plus belle œuvre**" — your most beautiful work (letter of July 1843) (p. 118).
- **The Conspectus:** "Au bout de trois jours — et sans doute de trois nuits —" (after three days — and doubtless three nights) Pitra brought the editor a first *conspectus* or Index of the Fathers: the chronological list of Latin writers **from the origins to Innocent III**, with the editions and dissertations to be included, grouping the *dubia* and *spuria* separately. He sent his scheme to esteemed patrologists such as **Jean-Baptiste Malou** and probably **H. Denzinger** (p. 118).
- Duration: the Pitra–Migne collaboration lasted the editor's whole life, loosening somewhat once Pitra settled in Rome; **for the PG it stops at Photius**. The PL mentions him under the rubric *Editores Patrologiae* (PL 218, 338: "…apud Montem Rubrum, luce donatae inter quos praecipuus R. P. Pitra ordinis S. Benedicti") (p. 118).

### The gratitude question and the contract
- Migne has been reproached with ingratitude toward Pitra. **Hamman produces the contract** (communicated by the abbey of Hautecombe) between Migne on one side and dom Guéranger and dom Pitra on the other, whose **Art. 3** stipulates: "Le nom des R.R.P.P. individuellement ou collectivement ne sera mis nulle part, ni dans les prospectus et annonces, ni aux titres et frontispices des volumes. Le nom de M. Migne pourra figurer partout où il le jugera à propos, sauf au bas des notes des éditeurs qu'ils se réservent d'indiquer d'une signature spéciale" — the Reverend Fathers' names shall appear nowhere; M. Migne's name may figure wherever he judges fit, except beneath the editors' notes, which they reserve the right to mark with a special signature (p. 119).
- Hamman's inference: "Dom Pitra s'efface visiblement devant son abbé. L'article en question ne serait-il pas inspiré par ce dernier?" — Pitra visibly effaces himself before his abbot; might not the article have been inspired by the latter [Guéranger]? (p. 119). I.e., the anonymity that fed the "ingratitude" legend was contractual, and possibly Solesmes' own doing.

### Architecture and naming decisions
- **The idea of two separate collections for Latin and Greek Fathers "semble provenir de Pitra"** — seems to come from Pitra (p. 119). The abbé Bonnetty — "qui n'est guère disert sur le mérite du futur cardinal" — criticized the division: "Il semble d'après cette division qu'il existe deux Églises, l'Église latine et l'Église grecque; c'est l'Église grecque qui a en sa faveur les plus anciens documents" (*Annales* 14, 1846, p. 395) (p. 119).
- **The name "Patrologie"** for a collection running to Innocent III and the council of Florence displeased Pitra, who found it improper; "Ses arguments pourtant irréfutables ne firent pas en démordre l'abbé Migne qui sans doute trouvait le titre plus publicitaire" — his nonetheless irrefutable arguments did not budge Migne, who doubtless found the title better publicity (p. 119; per Battandier, p. 124 n. 1).
- **Chronological horizon:** Migne intended to prolong the enterprise to the medieval masters — at least Thomas and Bonaventure — and reach the Council of Trent. To Pitra (letter, 15 Aug 1863): "Mon dessein est de retourner rapidement vers les Latins, non jusqu'au concile de Trente, comme je croyais, mais seulement jusque et y compris saint Bonaventure et saint Thomas. Pourriez-vous me dire à peu près combien j'aurai de volumes à faire pour arriver là" — could you tell me roughly how many volumes I shall need to get there (pp. 119–120). (Eight years earlier, a proposal by M. Cholet to edit the Franciscan doctor Jean de la Rochelle; letter of 9 July 1855.) Pitra, seeing farther, wanted Trent, and as cardinal kept pressing; Migne (15 March 1865): "Je vous promets de nouveau de faire tous les efforts, si je le puis, après moi-même, pour faire continuer la Patrologie jusqu'à Trente" — I promise again to make every effort… to have the Patrology continued to Trent after me (p. 120).

### Launch, dates, editorial method
- **The PL begins to appear in 1844; four volumes came out that same year** (p. 120).
- **Early hesitation between reprinting and re-editing:** the first volumes show it. Pitra, still available, "semble ne pas vouloir se limiter à reproduire des éditions estimées mais à les réviser, au besoin à en faire de nouvelles" — seems unwilling merely to reproduce esteemed editions, wanting to revise them, and where needed make new ones (p. 120).
- **PL 1's preface**, attributed to "les Bénédictins de Solesmes," is Pitra's own work (dated the calends of November 1843; PL 1, 7–8). Thereafter Pitra wrote for every work a **summary or argument** giving its contents, and one for each of its chapters — "Travail considérable qui remplirait à lui seul un gros ouvrage" — a considerable labor that would by itself fill a fat book (p. 120).
- **Case studies of textual work (p. 120):**
  - *Tertullian* — the edition claims to be "entièrement neuve" (entirely new; the Maurists had renounced editing him for lack of manuscripts). **Hamman: "Ce qui n'est pas tout à fait exact"** — not quite exact: the Patrology in fact follows Priorius' edition and adds the *editio variorum*.
  - *Cyprian* — Pitra revised the notes of Baluze and dom Maran.
  - *Minucius Felix* — Pitra established the text from the sole surviving manuscript, at the Bibliothèque Nationale (Codex Parisinus 1661, 9th century).
- **Painful beginnings and correction costs:** Pitra had not prepared the copy carefully enough, forcing major changes at proof stage. Migne to Pitra (7 Sept 1843): "Le premier volume de Tertullien m'a coûté… en corrections et remaniements de toutes sortes, **plus de 600 francs** et au-delà de ce qu'il devait coûter" — the first Tertullian volume cost me over 600 francs in corrections and recastings of every sort, beyond what it should have cost. At that rate correction would cost "plus que la composition" — more than the typesetting (pp. 120–121). Pitra's own admission (article in *L'Univers*, 8 March 1858): "Époque laborieuse, pleine de tâtonnements et d'angoisses que nous avons vue de trop près pour ne pas être indulgent plus que personne" — a laborious epoch, full of gropings and anguishes, which we saw too close-up not to be more indulgent than anyone (p. 121).
- **Division of labor as it settled:** afterwards, Pitra (off on his begging tour) confined himself to indicating the choice of editions, dissertations, commentaries and notes, to assembling texts scattered across many libraries, and to adding diplomas, charters and liturgical texts that overflowed the frame originally set (p. 121).
- **Proofreading system:** in the early days **Pitra corrected one set of proofs out of five; Migne's in-house team of correctors reread the other four sets** (p. 121).
- **The PG:** from Rome, dom Pitra prepared the Conspectus for the Greek Patrology, "**qui commence à paraître en 1857**." He did not draft it in one go: Migne had "la suite" (the continuation), brought from Rome, fetched from the Spiritans' house in Paris — whence Migne's "perle" (gem): "**Je fais prendre le Conspectus chez le concierge du Saint-Esprit!**" — I'm having the Conspectus picked up at the Holy Spirit's concierge! (letter, 16 Feb 1858) (p. 121).

### The councils project
- His Patrology task done, Pitra kept up contact with Migne hoping he would publish **the collection of the councils**, "si urgente à l'approche du concile du Vatican" — so urgent as the Vatican Council approached; he even opened the matter to Mgr Malou (a Malou letter alludes to it as early as 10 June 1844). Migne finally decided, announcing it on his letterhead prospectus in **1861**: "Collection universelle et complète des Conciles, généraux, nationaux, provinciaux et synodaux. **80 gros volumes.** Cette collection sera plus que quadruple de celle de Labbe et Cossart et plus que le double de celle de Mansi et Coletti dont les **31 volumes** sont à peine trouvables au prix de **1.200 francs**. (Bientôt sous presse.)" (pp. 121–122). **The fire of 1868 carried this last project off in the flames** (p. 122).

### Orthodoxy policy and internal tensions
- Relations between Migne and Pitra were "parfois tendus" (sometimes tense). **The prote (print-shop foreman) served as buffer: Paul Durand** oversaw the execution of the Conspectus — which contained semi-Arian authors such as **Théodore d'Héraclée** — while Migne "interdit toute reproduction d'hétérodoxes" (forbade any reproduction of heterodox writers). Durand's letter (19 Feb 1857): "Je suis fort embarrassé entre l'autorité du Conspectus et l'interdiction de M. Migne" — I am greatly embarrassed between the authority of the Conspectus and M. Migne's prohibition. Hamman: "On l'eût été à moins!" In the event, PG 18 does contain catenae of an Isaiah commentary attributed to Theodore (published by cardinal Mai; PG 18, 1307–1378) (p. 122).
- **The Index scare:** a letter of Mgr Baillès, sent from Rome, alarmed Migne — the Patrology was "surveillée et menacée" (watched and threatened). On the copy forwarded to Pitra, Migne added in his own hand (11 Feb 1857): "**Si nous avons le malheur de réimprimer encore des hétérodoxes, nous sommes à l'index. Dès lors adieu la patrologie!**" — if we have the misfortune to reprint heterodox writers again, we are on the Index; and then farewell the Patrology! (p. 122).
- Migne later relaxed the rule, writing in an *Avis* published by the *Annales* (10, 1864, p. 84): "Nous avons cru devoir joindre, surtout pour l'Orient, quelques auteurs hétérodoxes dont les écrits ont une incontestable valeur pour l'histoire de la dogmatique chrétienne" — we believed we ought to include, especially for the East, some heterodox authors whose writings have incontestable value for the history of Christian dogmatics. **Hamman: "Heureusement pour nous. Cette situation explique certaines omissions reprochées parfois injustement à Migne"** — happily for us; this situation explains certain omissions sometimes unjustly reproached to Migne (p. 122).
- **Copy-flow anxieties:** distance from Paris made collaboration harder; Pitra ran late with copy that Migne awaited "nerveusement" — Hamman's quip: "Et pourtant il n'y avait pas encore de grève des Postes!" (and yet there was not yet a postal strike!) (p. 122). Note 34: dom Guéranger was no faster — Migne to him (22 Feb 1855): "**Mon besoin est extrême, les ouvriers sont inactifs**" — my need is extreme, the workers are idle. Pitra complained of the editor's blunders to the bishop of Bruges [Malou], who answered with humor (letter of 1 Nov …) — **the chunk breaks off here**.

### Context from ch. 2's close relevant to PG/PL mechanics (pp. 67–70)
- The wider Bibliothèque universelle within which the Patrologies sat: *Cours complet d'Écriture sainte* (25 vols. + 3 supplements + Index + geographical/iconographic Atlas) and *Cours de théologie* (28 vols.) (pp. 66–67); *Cours complet d'histoire ecclésiastique* (1852; baron Henrion, finished by abbé Vervorst; Guéranger declined the succession) (p. 67); *Démonstrations évangéliques* (1842–1853, 18 vols., 117 authors in French translation, from Tertullian and Origen to Wiseman, including Protestants Jaquelot and Saurin, the Anglican Keith, and even Rousseau's "Apologie de la religion chrétienne" in vol. IX — a letter of Mgr Baillès, Feb 1857, insinuates the collection was denounced to the Holy Office) (p. 67); *Orateurs sacrés* (from 1844, 99 vols., French and Belgian preachers of the 16th–19th centuries classed in 1st, 2nd, 3rd rank — living bishops not neglected, "ce qui était de bonne guerre"; Hamman: "Cette série est la plus caduque de toutes les entreprises de Migne" — the most obsolete of all Migne's undertakings) (p. 67); 117 dictionaries and encyclopedias, 1844–1859, some mere reprints, ranging to astronomy, botany, zoology, agriculture and medicine (pp. 67–68). Total: "La Bibliothèque universelle du clergé et des laïques instruits" = **813 volumes** "si les comptes sont exacts" (if the accounts are right) (p. 68).
- Recruitment of expertise for the dictionaries: Migne wrote **Proudhon** in 1855 to solicit his collaboration; drew on the Académies and the École des Chartes; **Charles Grandmaison** did the *Dictionnaire héraldique* ("qui fait toujours prime en librairie"), **de Mas-Latrie** the *Dictionnaire des manuscrits* (still on the open shelves of the Bibliothèque Nationale, "où son usure prouve son utilité" — where its wear proves its usefulness) (p. 68). The *Dictionnaire des apocryphes* remains the fullest collection and best French translation of OT/NT apocrypha; the *Dictionnaire des hérésies* was reprinted anastatically in Amsterdam in 1969 at 240 florins — "Migne le vendait huit francs le volume" (p. 68).
- Firmin-Didot on the Ateliers' output (1,000+ volumes in thirty years): "**la plus grande entreprise qui ait jamais été conçue depuis l'invention de l'imprimerie**" — the greatest enterprise ever conceived since the invention of printing (p. 69, from his report to the Tribunal civil de la Seine, in F. de Mély).

## 4. Named People and Roles

**The enterprise's inner circle**
- **Dom Prosper Guéranger** (abbot of Solesmes) — instigator of the Patrology idea per Migne himself; declined its literary/theological direction (offered 25 Feb 1842); party to the contract; Solesmes contributed much to Latin translations of Greek texts; slow with copy; Migne once offered him the succession of the enterprise (22 Sept 1865, p. 75 n. 10) and cured his rheumatism.
- **Dom Jean-Baptiste Pitra** OSB (later cardinal, prefect of the Vatican Library) — "le maître d'œuvre des deux Patrologies": author of the Conspectus/Index of Latin Fathers (to Innocent III) in three days (1843) and of the PG Conspectus from Rome (PG begins 1857); author of PL 1's "Solesmes" preface and of the summaries/arguments for every work and chapter; text-critic (Cyprian notes after Baluze/Maran; Minucius Felix from Codex Parisinus 1661; revision of Baluze on *De morte persecutorum*); corrected one proof-set in five; source of the two-collection (Latin/Greek) architecture; opponent of the name "Patrologie"; advocate of extension to Trent and of the councils collection; added charters and diplomas "par la grâce — et la faute" of his enthusiasm; scholarly works: Spicilegium Solesmense, Analecta sacra, Analecta novissima; decipherer of the Pectorius inscription; manuscript rescuer (Hincmar at Reims; Metz gargousse parchments).

## 7. Hamman's Interpretive Stance (marked as his view)

- **Rehabilitating frame.** Hamman consistently reads Migne as a creator-saint of the workshop rather than a huckster: the whole portrait chapter builds toward "ce prêtre, curé de campagne, devenu le plus grand éditeur de son siècle" (p. 87), and its thesis sentence — "L'œuvre est à l'image de l'homme, et l'homme, la mesure de l'œuvre" (p. 70).
- **Corrections of legends and other accounts:**
  1. *The 1019-volume figure.* "Malgré toutes nos recherches nous n'aboutissons pas à trouver le chiffre de 1019 volumes publiés" — the count usually given in notices on Migne derives from Firmin-Didot's report ("environ 1019") and cannot be verified; perhaps one must add a Biblia sacra, Missae pontificales, Pouget's 12-vol. Institutiones catholicae (no trace at the BN), and a Thomas index (p. 70 n. 51).
  2. *The "restless businessman" reading.* Against "Il lui fallait du bruit, du mouvement, du changement": "C'est voir de manière assez myope le personnage" — Migne belongs to "la race des créateurs" with Herculean dynamism to burn; Pitra "voit plus juste" (p. 74).
  3. *The Goncourts.* Their Journal note on Migne is "sarcastique et injuste" (p. 78).
  4. *The plagiarism dossier, nuanced.* Not all thefts imputable to Migne (Henrion's borrowing was behind his back); and with hindsight the larceny "ont servi auteurs lésés et usagers" (p. 78). But Hamman does not whitewash: he documents the missing imprints (Ulfilas) and falsified references (Leo VI's Novels, after A. Dain) as deliberate fee-avoidance (p. 78).
  5. *The Mass-intentions "traffic."* Real but "encore ne faut-il pas exagérer ce trafic" (p. 77 n. 18).
  6. *The ingratitude-to-Pitra charge.* Hamman produces contract Art. 3 showing the monks' anonymity was stipulated, and suggests the clause may go back to Guéranger himself (p. 119) — while still noting acidly that Migne gave the "maître d'œuvre" only two lines in a table of contents (p. 78).
  7. *The "entirely new" Tertullian.* The Patrology's claim is "pas tout à fait exact": it follows Priorius plus the editio variorum (p. 120).
  8. *Unjust reproaches about omissions.* The Index threat of 1857 "explique certaines omissions reprochées parfois injustement à Migne" (p. 122).
  9. *The financial enigma left open.* How Migne could immobilize 582,722 kg of type and 627,855 plates while publishing 1,000+ volumes "demeure une énigme"; the loss of archives and correspondence forbids an answer (p. 70). Hamman refuses to speculate.
  10. *Negative source-finding.* No trace of Tillemont in the Patrology (p. 93); the La Bigne Bibliotheca "semble rarement feuilletée" by scholars, so he analyzes it closely (p. 96).
- **Hamman's criticisms of Migne**, plainly his own: the prospectus bombast is "fanfaronnade," the tenacity "entêtement"; "Ce manque de mesure — et de modestie, a nui à l'entreprise et à l'entrepreneur" (pp. 80–81); Migne "est incapable de distinguer les plans, de ne pas se confondre avec son entreprise" (p. 80); the epigram on trading eternal felicity for the two Patrologies at God's tribunal (p. 77); the *Orateurs sacrés* as "la plus caduque de toutes les entreprises de Migne" (p. 67); the double-edged verdict on Pitra's charters — "par la grâce — et la faute — de dom Pitra" (p. 103).
- **Personal investment**: Hamman writes as the PLS compiler, repeatedly flagging where the Patrology's gaps were filled by his own Supplement (PLS II for Caillau/Saint-Yves and Mai's Latin texts; PLS III for the Collectio gallicana from the Lyon Maxima) (pp. 97 n. 42, 104 n. 81, 105 n. 86). His archive base: the Migne–Pitra correspondence (much of it via dom Clair, abbey of Hautecombe), F. de Mély's publication of the Tribunal de la Seine report, Battandier's life of Pitra, the *Annales de philosophie chrétienne*, and the *Moniteur du Cantal*.

## 8. Texture — Petit-Montrouge, Machines, Daily Operations

- **The invitation to visit** (printed on Migne's own letterhead): "Si vous êtes curieux de voir s'exercer à la fois et en grand tous les arts relatifs à la typographie, vous êtes prié d'honorer de votre présence les Ateliers catholiques du Petit-Montrouge" — the workshops functioned as a showpiece, all the typographic arts "en grand" under one roof (p. 81).
- **Steam power:** "la vapeur y est appliquée à la mécanique" — steam applied to the presses, with a claimed capacity of **2,000 in-4° volumes every 24 hours**; the boast that a medieval monk's hand could not copy in three years what the Imprimerie catholique does in one minute (p. 81).
- **Materials on site:** 582,722 kg of lead type and 627,855 stereotype plates (the *Moniteur du Cantal* says the plates weighed 800,000 kg) — the stereotype plates are the key to the reprint economics, and their bulk is why the 1868 fire was terminal for projects like the 80-volume councils collection (p. 70; p. 122).
- **The product:** in-4° volumes of about a thousand pages, "à deux colonnes très compactes," at 5 francs (8 for Greek) (pp. 69–70).
- **Workflow:** five sets of proofs pulled per volume; a standing in-house team of correctors reading four sets, the outside scholar (Pitra) the fifth (p. 121). Heavy authorial corrections were billed against the volume (the 600-franc Tertullian overrun; correction threatening to cost "plus que la composition") (pp. 120–121). Copy shortages idled the shop floor: "les ouvriers sont inactifs" (p. 122 n. 34). Copy traveled by post from Rome and was retrieved from the Spiritans' porter's lodge (p. 121).
- **Personnel:** the *prote* Paul Durand as operational chief and doctrinal traffic-cop between Conspectus and proprietor (p. 122); salaried workers paid punctually on the 7th of each month (p. 75); priests in difficulty sheltered with decent work (p. 79); an editor whose first thought at the fire was his workers' unemployment (p. 79).
- **Marketing operations:** letterhead margins and pages 3–4 of the stationery carrying catalogue and prices (even of never-published works); 40,000-copy prospectus runs; the clergy savings bank at 5% funneling capital to "l'impression de plus en plus rapide de la Bibliothèque universelle" (pp. 77, 81, 115); subscription mechanics down to the installment plan improvised for the Nancy Oblates ("quelque chose tous les six mois," p. 80); house rule against paying above the legal interest rate (p. 77).
- **The fire (context, from ch. 2's close):** the eyewitness image of Migne "debout au milieu des ruines de son œuvre colossale, songeant déjà aux moyens de relever ce monde détruit" — standing amid the ruins of his colossal work, already pondering the means to raise up this destroyed world (*Moniteur du Cantal*, 15 Feb 1868) (p. 70).
