## 1. 0368D · `[var:]` Jo 3:22 — **CONFIRMED as Migne's**

**Leaf n189, right column (368), band D** (the `D` band letter sits in the gutter on the line
`stitia salvetur qui credit.`).

Migne prints, in italic:

> VERS. 22. — *Post hæc.* Non continuo post disputationem cum Nicodemo quæ facta est in
> Hierosolymis, sed peracto spatio temporis de Galilæa in Judæam rediit. ***In terram Judam,
> et illic demorabatur,*** etc. Moraliter. Ubi est confessio peccatorum vel divinarum laudum,
> illuc venit Jesus, et discipuli

The lemma is set *In terram Judam, et illic demoraba- / tur,* etc. — broken across a line at
*demoraba-tur*, italic throughout, followed by roman `etc.`

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED.** The plate reads *Judam* (not *Judaeam*) and ends the clause at
*demorabatur* with no *cum eis*. The `[var:]` against Clementine Jo 3:22 *in terram Judaeam: et
illic demorabatur cum eis* stands as a claim about Migne's page. Our TEI matches the plate
character for character across the whole column; no patch.

## 2. 0369A · `[var:]` Jo 3:22 quoted a second way — **CONFIRMED as Migne's, and the two really are different**

**Leaf n190, left column (369), band A** (the `A` band letter sits in the gutter on the first
line of the column, `…ubi moratur A`).

Migne prints:

> ejus, id est doctrina et illuminatio ejus, ubi moratur purgando a delictis, unde sequitur,
> ***et ibi morabatur et baptizabat.***

Set in italic *et ibi morabatur / et baptizabat.* across a line break.

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED — and this is the finding worth having.** Both quotations were read on the
plate, eight lines apart across the column turn, and **the plate genuinely prints them
differently**:

| where | Migne's plate |
|---|---|
| 0368D, the lemma | *In terram **Judam**, et **illic demorabatur**,* etc. |
| 0369A, the re-quotation | *et **ibi morabatur** et baptizabat.* |

*illic demorabatur* → *ibi morabatur*: a different adverb and a simplex for the compound, inside
eight lines, both in Migne's italic quotation type, both departing from the Clementine and from
each other. This is not a transcription artifact — our TEI reproduces the plate exactly at both
sites. Both `[var:]` stand, and the stint's refusal to explain the disagreement was correct.

## 6. 0373B · *Panes nostri* — **CONFIRMED as MIGNE'S. Owed a `[var:]`, NOT a TEI patch.**

**Leaf n192, left column (373), band B–C boundary** (the lemma sits two lines below the `C` band
letter's line; the `C` sits beside `ditis proficiens, jam cœperat aliquatenus habere C`).

Migne prints:

> VERS. 20. — ***Panes nostri,*** etc. Propheta es tu, et ideo judica de hac contentione, quæ me
> movet. Judæi se præferebant Samaritanis pro templo quod ædificavit Salomon, in quo adorabant.
> Econtra Samaritani adversum Judæos pro monte, in quo et non in templo **patres adoraverunt**,
> qui Deo placuerunt. Et sic **patriarchæ in montibus**, Judæi in templo adoraverunt,
> præfigurantes modum bene orandi.

**Read at magnification.** Cropped the two-word lemma alone from the source JPEG at native
resolution (145 × 65 px) and scaled it 12× with Lanczos. The letter at issue is unambiguous: a
single italic **n** with two arches of equal weight, its shoulder springing from the stem of the
*a* and closing into the *e*. There is **no ascender** where a *t* would put one and **no
terminal** where an *r* would put one — and the comparison is available on the same page, since
*nostri* two letters later prints a *tr* pair whose *t* carries a plain crossed ascender and
whose *r* carries a clear flag. The forms are not confusable at this magnification.

**VERDICT: CONFIRMED as Migne's type. `Panes` is what the plate prints.**

This resolves the crux's two live outcomes in favour of the second one:

- **NOT a TEI patch.** Corpus Corporum transcribed the plate correctly. `Panes nostri` in
  `sources/pl/tei/8999.xml` is right and must be left alone.
- **A `[var:]` is now owed** against Clementine Jo 4:20 *Patres nostri in monte hoc adoraverunt*,
  and the English "Our loaves" is correct as printed under Pattern 7. Suggested marker, to be
  placed after the rendered lemma in `src/english/8999/0006.md`:

  `[var: Vulg. Jo 4:20 *Patres nostri in monte hoc adoraverunt*, "our fathers adored on this mountain".]`

  The gloss four words later expounds *patres adoraverunt* and *patriarchae in montibus* — which
  is now a fact about **Migne's page disagreeing with itself**, in the same class as items 1–2
  and 4 above, and not evidence about our file.

⭐ The stint's decline was right for the right reason: the corroboration it had (the correct sense
prints four words later) is the reasoning that produced 9004's nine wrong calls, and had it
guessed it would have guessed **wrong** — it leaned toward *Patres* being Migne's.

## 7. Secondary pass — three divergences of OUR file, found on columns already open

All three sit on **leaf n192, left column (373), band D**, within four lines of each other, and
all three were read at magnification against the plate.

### 7a. `id est.` for Migne's `id est,`

**Plate (373D):** `… Quia salus ex Judæis est, etc., id est, quia Christus, qui est salus …` —
a **comma** after *id est*, clearly formed, on a line whose other commas are identical in shape.

**Our TEI:** `etc., id est. quia`

**VERDICT: WITHDRAWN as ours.** The full stop the crux carried into the English ("that is. because
Christ…") is the digitization's, not Migne's. Same class as 9001 @0202D, and the crux guessed
that correctly but declined to assert it.

```
find:    id est. quia
replace: id est, quia
```
(unique in `sources/pl/tei/8999.xml` — 1 occurrence)

**English consequence:** `src/english/8999/0006.md` should read "that is, because Christ, who is
salvation…" — the Pattern 8 carry-through of the stop is no longer warranted.

### 7b. `crede` for Migne's `credere`

**Plate (373D):** `qui potest credere et ideo credere : Quia nisi cre-` — the second *credere*
prints its full `-re` termination, in the same roman type and at the same size as the first
*credere* four words earlier on the same line, which makes the comparison direct.

**Our TEI:** `qui potest credere et ideo crede:`

**VERDICT: WITHDRAWN as ours.** A dropped `-re`, i.e. our file turns Migne's infinitive into an
imperative. Nothing was marked here, but the English rendered the imperative ("and therefore
believe:"); on the plate the sense is *"there is already in thee a man who can believe, and
therefore [can] believe: …"*.

```
find:    et ideo crede:
replace: et ideo credere:
```
(unique — 1 occurrence)

**English consequence:** the imperative "and therefore believe:" in `src/english/8999/0006.md`
should follow the infinitive.

### 7c. `(Isa. VII)` for Migne's `(Isa.)`

**Plate (373D):** the siglum after *non intelliges* is set `(Isa.)` — italic *Isa.*, closing
parenthesis immediately after the abbreviation stop. **There is no numeral.** Read at
magnification on a line where the following words (*Quando neque in monte*) are also italic, so
there is no ambiguity about where the parenthesis closes.

**Our TEI:** `<note>(Isa. VII)</note>`

**VERDICT: WITHDRAWN as ours.** Migne does not print the chapter numeral here.

```
find:    <note>(Isa. VII)</note>
replace: <note>(Isa.)</note>
```
(unique — 1 occurrence)

⚑ **Flagged with a caveat the other two do not need.** 7a and 7b are subtractive/mechanical
(a comma flattened to a stop, a dropped `-re`) — shapes a digitization produces. 7c is an
**addition** of a correct chapter number, which is not an OCR shape and looks like an editorial
supplement somewhere upstream. But the question this pass answers is only what the plate prints,
and the plate prints `(Isa.)`. If the project's convention is that a `<note>` carries Migne's
siglum verbatim, apply the patch; if `<note>` is understood as a normalized citation field, leave
it and record the observation. **The plate reading itself is not in doubt.**
