## 1. 0397C `[var:]` — Jo 10:11 / 10:15 join — **CONFIRMED as Migne's**

**Leaf n204, column 397, band C** (the gutter `C` sits beside the line *…opponit. Ego sum pastor*).

Migne prints, VERS. 14 (italic shown as italic, roman as roman):

> VERS. 14. — *Ego sum pastor.* Post culpam ficti, iterum formam veri pastoris opponit. *Ego sum
> pastor bonus et animam pono pro ovibus.* Quod non ipse unus fecit, nisi quod omnes sui et ipse
> unus sunt, quia ipse sine illis hoc potuit, illi non sine eo sine quo nihil possunt.

**Where the join falls:** after ***bonus***. The lemma runs *Ego sum pastor bonus* — Jo 10:11a
verbatim — and then continues straight into *et animam pono pro ovibus*, which is Jo **10:15b**
(*et animam meam pono pro ovibus meis*) with both possessives dropped. The Vulgate's 10:11b
(*Bonus pastor animam suam dat pro ovibus suis*) is **not on the page at all**: neither *bonus
pastor* as a fresh subject nor *dat* nor *suam*/*suis* appears. The italic span is continuous and
unbroken across the line turn (`Ego sum pastor` / `bonus et animam pono pro ovibus.`), so this is
one lemma, not two set adjacently.

The whole span is set in italic and terminates with a full stop before roman *Quod*. Our chunk
reproduces it exactly. **CONFIRMED — the marker stands.**

## 2. 0397C `[var:]` — Jo 1:18 *nisi Unigenitus … enarraverit* — **CONFIRMED as Migne's**

**Leaf n204, column 397, band C** (six lines below item 1, same band).

Migne prints, VERS. 15:

> VERS. 15. — *Et ego.* Filius per se agnoscit Patrem, non per illum, sicut alibi, *Deum nemo
> vidit unquam, nisi Unigenitus qui est in sinu Patris ipse enarraverit* (*Matth.* XI). Item *nemo
> novit Filium nisi Pater, neque Patrem quis novit Filius, et cui Filius voluerit revelare*
> (*Matth.* XI ; *Luc,* X). *Et animam meam pono.* Hoc est probatio dilectionis ad Patrem, et ad
> oves.

Every element the marker asserts is on the plate: **`nisi`** is printed (a conjunction the
Vulgate does not have), **`Filius`** is absent after *Unigenitus*, and the verb is
**`enarraverit`**, not *enarravit* — the `-erit` is unambiguous at 4× and the word is set in
italic like the rest of the lemma. The parenthetical citation reads **`(Matth. XI)`** on a John
quotation, exactly as the stint reported and reproduced. **CONFIRMED — the marker stands.**

⚑ **Two punctuation departures in our file, found incidentally on this same span** (both minor,
both ours, both offered here rather than silently kept):

- Migne prints **no comma after *Patris*** — the line ends `…qui est in sinu Patris` and turns to
  `ipse enarraverit`. Re-cropped at 4×: the space after the final *s* is clean. Our TEI has
  `in sinu Patris, ipse enarraverit`.
- Migne prints **`Luc,`** with a comma, not `Luc.` — `(Matth. XI ; Luc, X)`, with a space before
  the semicolon. Our TEI has `(Matth. XI; Luc. X)`.

Neither touches the marker's claim, and neither is worth a patch on its own; recorded so the next
reader does not re-derive them. I have not written patches for them.

## 3. 0399C `[var:]` — Mt 16:23 *Redi* for *Vade*, *scandalum es mihi* absent — **CONFIRMED as Migne's**

**Leaf n205, column 399, band C** (the gutter `C` sits beside the line `VERS. 8. — Dicunt ei
discipuli. Territi, volentes`).

Migne prints, VERS. 8, in full:

> VERS. 8. — *Dicunt ei discipuli.* Territi, volentes consilium dare magistro ne moreretur, sicut
> et alibi Petrus : *Absit a te, Domine,* non fiet tibi istud. Sed sicut Petrus laudatur de alieno :
> *Beatus es, Simon Barjona, quia Pater revelavit tibi,* culpatur de suo : *Redi post me, Satana,
> quia sapis non quæ Dei, sed quæ hominis sunt.* Ita et hos corripuit dicens se diem in quo debent
> ambulare ne offendant, et sine eo ambulantes offendant, ut modo nolentes eum mori qui pro
> hominibus venit mori.

**`Redi`** is printed in full — R-e-d-i, in italic, initial capital, with no ambiguity of any
letter; there is no *Vade* anywhere in the clause. ***scandalum es mihi* is absent**: the plate
runs `Satana, quia sapis` with only a comma between, so nothing has been dropped by our
transcription. The rest of the clause reads *quia sapis non quæ Dei, sed quæ hominis sunt* — the
*non* displaced (before *quæ*, not before *sapis*), *hominis* singular, and the æ set as a
ligature where our TEI has *quae*. **CONFIRMED — the marker stands.**

⚑ Also confirmed on the same band, and useful because the stint logged them without marking:
the roman continuation after the *Absit a te, Domine,* lemma really does read **`non fiet tibi
istud`** (not the Vulgate's *non erit tibi hoc*), outside the italic; and the abridged Mt 16:17
lemma really is *Beatus es, Simon Barjona, quia Pater revelavit tibi,* with *caro et sanguis non
revelavit tibi, sed* … *meus, qui in caelis est* absent from the plate. Both declines were right.

## 4. 0399B `impieretur` — **WITHDRAWN as ours**

**Leaf n205, column 399, band B.** Migne prints, VERS. 6:

> VERS. 6. — *Duobus diebus.* Ut quatriduum im-
> pleretur. Prima enim die quo nuntiatum est ei, mortuus est languidus trans Jordanem : per duos
> dies mansit, ecce tres : quarto autem die venit.

The word is **hyphenated across the line turn** — `im-` ends the line, `pleretur.` opens the next —
and at 5× the second element reads **p-l-e-r-e-t-u-r** with a clean ascender on the *l* and no
dot above it. **Migne prints *impleretur*.** The non-word is our digitization's: Corpus Corporum
rejoined the hyphenated word and read the *l* as an *i*. This is the class rule 8a warns about
producing a false claim, and it is why no `[sic:]` was fired — correctly.

**TEI patch** (`sources/pl/tei/8999.xml`; `impieretur` occurs exactly once in the file, so the
find is unique — verified by grep):

- find: `Ut quatriduum impieretur.`
- replace: `Ut quatriduum impleretur.`

⚠ **Downstream, in `src/english/8999/0017.md`:** the English currently reads *"That the four days
might* impieretur *[be fulfilled]."* — the Pattern 7 carry-plus-bracketed-sense device the stint
built precisely because it could not attribute the non-word. With the plate read, the device is
no longer needed and should be unwound to plain **"That the four days might be fulfilled."**

⚑ Incidental, same sentence, not patched: Migne punctuates `trans Jordanem :` with a colon where
our TEI has a semicolon.

## 5. 0400C `Sea` — **WITHDRAWN as ours**

**Leaf n205, column 400, band C** (the last VERS. of the band; the gutter `C` sits eight lines
above, beside `VERS. 38. — Jesus ergo`). Migne prints:

> VERS. 42. — *Sed propter populum,* etc. His verbis se gloriam Patris quærere, sicut in aliis
> operibus ostendit. Nam quia secundum hominem minor erat Patre, ab eo petit …

At 5× the lemma's first word is **`Sed`** — italic *S*, *e*, and a *d* with a full unbroken
ascender; there is no *a*-shaped sort and no damage to the letter. **Migne prints *Sed*.** The
non-word is ours (a d→a misread). Reported as seen, and it happens to match the expected reading.

**TEI patch** (`Sea` occurs exactly once in the whole file — verified by grep, `grep -o "Sea[^a-z]"`
returns one hit — so the shorter find would also be unique; the fuller form is given for safety):

⚠ **Downstream, in `src/english/8999/0017.md`:** VERS. 42 currently opens *"**Sea** for the
people,"* — the untranslated carry. It should become *"**But** for the people,"*.

⚑ Incidental on the same band: Migne prints *Domine, jam **fetet*** (no *oe*), which is what our

## 6. 0396D — *sed in fine perseverant* twice — **CONFIRMED as Migne's** (leaf read: yes, n203)

I completed the primary and secondary lists first, then fetched **leaf n203** and calibrated it
(corners `395` … `396`). **Column 396, band D.**

Migne prints, VERS. 6:

> VERS. 6. — *Illi autem non cognoverunt.* Prædestinatæ oves ad tempus se nesciunt, nec vocem
> pastoris audiunt, sed in fine perseverant, e contra lupi ad tempus audiunt, sed in fine
> perseverant.

**Both clauses end in the identical three words on the plate.** The sheep's clause reads `sed in
fine perseverant, e contra lupi ad` (line end) and the wolves' `tempus audiunt, sed in fine
perseverant.` — the second *perseverant* is the last word of the paragraph, followed by a full
stop and a paragraph break before `VERS. 8. — Omnes,`. There is no negative, no *pereunt*, no
*deficiunt*, and nothing dropped between *audiunt* and *sed*. The commas are Migne's, in the
positions our TEI has them.

**So the defect is Migne's page, not the digitization's.** The stint's judgment — carry it,
mark nothing, log it as the range's strongest plate candidate — was exactly right, and the plate
now settles the attribution: *e contra* contrasts nothing **in Migne**. No marker is needed
(nothing is ill-formed) and no patch is possible; what this read buys is that the sentence can
never again be suspected of being our loss.

⚑ Also confirmed on the same band, three lines below: **`Nec talis dicendus est mercenarius,
ideo lupo veniente non resistit, sed pro mercede cedit.`** — Migne's comma after *mercenarius* is
real and the *ideo* really has no protasis on the plate. The 0396D–0397A crux is Migne's too.
