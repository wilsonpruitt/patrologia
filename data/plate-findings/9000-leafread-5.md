# PART 1 — THE FIVE `[var:]` MARKERS

## 1. @0327A — chunk 0031 — **CONFIRMED**

**Claim:** Vulg. Matt 21:9 ends *hosanna in altissimis*, and *rex Israel* stands only at Jo 12:13.

**Plate prints** (p. 169 left col., band A, settled at **500 dpi**):

> clamabant : *Hosanna filio David : benedictus qui venit in nomine Domini rex Israel*, qui
> constitutus est a Patre rex, cui datæ sunt gentes in hæreditatem.

No *in altissimis* anywhere in the quotation; *rex Israel* is set in the italic lemma. Our Latin
matches the plate word for word. **Marker stands.**

## 2. @0330A — chunk 0032 — **CONFIRMED** ⚑ the conformation is MIGNE'S

**Claim:** Vulg. Luke 2:14 reads *Gloria in altissimis Deo*; *in excelsis* is Luke 19:38, the verse
here glossed.

**Both quotations read on the same page, in full** (p. 170 right col., band A, settled at **400 dpi**):

*First quotation — the nativity song, italic:*

> cœlestia decantabant : *Gloria **in excelsis** Deo. Et in terra pax hominibus*, etc.

*Second quotation — the entry song, two lines later, roman:*

> Sic eidem mox triumphaturo homines concinunt : Pax in cœlo, et gloria **in excelsis**.

Unambiguous at 400 dpi in both places: no ligature ambiguity, no broken sort, *excelsis* spelled
out both times. Our Latin (`src/latin/9000/0032.md` l. 25) reproduces both exactly.

**Verdict: CONFIRMED.** Migne (or his source) prints *in excelsis* at Luke 2:14, the reading of
Luke 19:38 — the verse being expounded two lines below on the same page. The conformation of the
nativity song to the entry song is on the plate. This is a real finding about the printed page and
the marker must stand.

## 3. @0331B — chunk 0033 — **CONFIRMED**, final *t* present

**Claim:** Vulg. Isa 5:7 *Vinea enim Domini exercituum domus Israel est*.

**Plate prints** (p. 171 left col., band B, settled at **500 dpi**, magnified ×1.5):

> *Vineam.* Moraliter : *Vinea Domini **sabaoth**, domus Israel (Isa. v.)* : coloni, operarii…

pair, not a lone *h*). This is not the *sabaoh* shape logged elsewhere in the work. Our Latin
matches. **Marker stands** — Migne prints the Hebrew transliteration where the Vulgate translates.

# PART 2 — DECLINED ODDITIES SETTLED

## 2a. ⛔ THREE OUR-FILE DEFECTS FOUND — the Latin must be patched

### (i) @0347C — ***zelam*** should be ***zelum*** — WITHDRAWN FROM MIGNE

`src/latin/9000/0037.md` l. 69 reads *sed pro eis qui **zelam** Dei habentes*.

**The plate prints *zelum*.** Settled at **500 dpi magnified ×4** on our copy (p. 179 left col.,
band C — a clean column on this leaf) and confirmed independently on the **second witness**
(n179). The letter after the *l* is an unmistakable **u** with two full verticals and a bowl
foot; compare the *u* of *habentes* on the same line and the *a* of *data* nearby.

> crucifigunt, sed pro eis qui **zelum** Dei habentes, sed non secundum scientiam, nescierunt quid
> fecerunt.

**Correct reading for the patch: `zelum`.** This was crux stint-0036 item 11, logged as a possible
gender anomaly in Migne. It is not: it is Corpus Corporum's.

### (ii) @0327D — a dropped ***etc.*** — WITHDRAWN FROM MIGNE

`src/latin/9000/0031.md` l. 55 reads *VERS. 18.-- \*Et alter,\* Bonus ordo, vocaturus gentes…*

**The plate prints** (p. 169 left col., band D, **500 dpi**):

> VERS. 18. — *Et alter*, **etc.** Bonus ordo, vocaturus gentes, et Judæos missurus interfici…

**Correct reading for the patch: `*Et alter,* etc. Bonus ordo, …`** This was crux item 5, logged as
"no punctuation closes the italic lemma, where every parallel address in the work prints *etc.* or
a stop." The parallel *was* right and the plate has the *etc.*; the loss is ours. (Note the very
next entry in our own file, *VERS. 20.-- \*Et alter,\* etc.*, keeps its *etc.* — so this is a
one-site drop, not a class.)

### (iii) @0329A — ***astrictus*** should be ***astritus*** — WITHDRAWN FROM MIGNE

`src/latin/9000/0032.md` reads *in quo addictus vel **astrictus** Domino errore famulabatur*.

**The plate prints *astritus*** — no *c*. Settled at **500 dpi magnified ×4** on our copy (p. 170
left col., band A — a clean column) and confirmed on the **second witness** (n170):

> fidiæ, in quo addictus vel **astritus** Domino ɛrrore famulabatur, sed dominatum sibi vindicare
> non po-

The control is on the same line: *addictus* two words earlier prints a full, unambiguous **ct**
ligature. *astritus* has no *c* between *r* and *i* in either witness.

**Correct reading for the patch: `astritus`.** This one was **not** in any stint's declined list —
it was normalised silently by the digitization and nobody saw it. Flagging for the consolidator:
patching restores an unclassical form, which is exactly what Migne prints.

⚑ Also on that line, both witnesses show a **broken or wrong-fount sort in *ɛrrore*** (the *e*
prints as a narrow epsilon-like shape). Two different physical copies show the same shape, so it
is the type, not the photograph. Our file's *errore* is the right reading of a defective sort and
needs no change; it does not rise to a `[sic:]`.

### (iv) @0344B — TWO VERSE NUMBERS SILENTLY EMENDED (report, do not necessarily patch)

`src/latin/9000/0037.md` prints **VERS. 5.** for *Commovet populum* and **VERS. 11.** for
*Et illusit*.

**The plate prints VERS. 2. and VERS. 14.** — read on our copy at 200 dpi and settled on the
**second witness** (n177, right column, both clean there):

> VERS. **2**. — *Commovet populum*. Non illum, sed se accusant…
> VERS. **14**. — *Et illusit*. Quod in alba veste spernitur…

Our numbers are the **correct** Luke verses (Lc 23:5, 23:11); Migne's printed numbers are wrong.
That is the signature of a silent editorial repair upstream of us, not a transcription slip —
two on one column, both in the same direction. **I make no attribution beyond what I saw:** the
plate prints 2 and 14. Whether to restore Migne's numbers is an editorial call for the
consolidator, but the divergence is real and is presently invisible in our apparatus.

## 2b. ⭐ THE BRIEF'S NAMED CRUX — @0337C ***intimis*** is MIGNE'S (leaf n174)

Our copy **clips the left margin on p. 174** (the printed 337 in the corner is eaten, and 2–4
characters are lost at each line start of the left column) — it cannot settle this word. Settled
on the **second witness** `patrologiaecursu0114mign` n174:

> loco recipit Salvatorem. Qui in littera remanet, **inti-**
> **mis** Pascha facit, sed qui aquæ bajulum, id est, …

**The plate breaks the word across the line WITH A HYPHEN: `inti-` / `mis`.** A printed *in imis*
could not be hyphenated that way — *in* is a whole word and takes no hyphen. So Migne prints one
word, *intimis*.

**Verdict: Migne's. Our transcription is right, no patch.** The conjecture *in imis* is an
emendation of Migne, correctly declined by the stint. ⚑ This is the fused-word class the brief
said no frequency test could find, and the plate settles it against the conjecture.

## 2c. @0332A ***utile cadaver*** — MIGNE'S, no patch

Settled at **500 dpi** (p. 171 right col., band A — legible in our copy despite the clipping,
which begins further right on this line):

> luerant, sed quasi **utile** cadaver projicientes, gentibus suscipiendum dederunt.

No *in-*, no space before *utile*, normal word spacing either side. **The printed reading is
*utile*.** The stint's decline was correct and the English needs no change: *inutile* would be an
emendation of Migne, not a repair of us.
