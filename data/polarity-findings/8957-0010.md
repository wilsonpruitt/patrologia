## Findings — direction 1 (Migne's own defect, faithfully carried into English)

### M1 — 0010, col. 0773B/C. Intrusive `non`; the gloss contradicts its own lemma.
- Lemma: « Succidat me. » / « Let him cut me off. »
- Latin as printed: `Cum enim dolor vel tentatio non [0773C] percutit, elatio mentis succisa cadit,
  ut jam quaerat manum levantis.`
- English as it stands: "For when sorrow or temptation does not strike, the elation of the mind
  falls cut down, so that it now seeks the hand of him who lifts up."
- The lemma asks to be cut down; the whole surrounding argument (0773B: God's crushing breaks a man
  from his vices and makes him solid in virtue) requires the striking to be what fells pride.
  As printed, elation collapses precisely when nothing strikes it.
- Should read (Latin): `Cum enim dolor vel tentatio percutit, elatio mentis succisa cadit` →
  "For when sorrow or temptation strikes, the elation of the mind falls cut down."
- Our English is a faithful rendering of the defect and is **not** an error in our work. Flagged so
  the plate can be checked.

### M2 — 0010, col. 0772B. Doubled `non` in the opening gloss (lower confidence).
- Latin: `Tactus flagellis verbis lacessitur, non ut non erectus in defensionem contumax caderet:
  sed ut percussus tacens gratias egit…`
- English: "…he is provoked by words, not that, not raised up into his own defence, he might fall
  defiant: but that, struck, he kept silence and gave thanks…"
- The contrast wanted is "not that he might rise to his own defence and fall defiant, but that,
  struck, he kept silence" — i.e. one `non`, or `ne non`. As printed the second `non` inverts the
  protasis against the `sed` clause that answers it. English carries both, correctly.
- Lower confidence than M1: `non ut non` is not impossible medieval Latin. Worth a plate look only
  if M1 is being checked on the same column-pair.

### M3 — 0012, col. 0780D. `aeternae` where the gloss's own argument requires the opposite.
- Lemma: « Et tabernaculum. » / « And the tabernacle. »
- Latin as printed: `id est aedificatio aeternae felicitatis, quam supra se casuram multiplicant,
  ut se hic a necessatibus quasi ab aestu et imbre defendant: in qua mens habitat, quam bonus
  viator despicit.`
- English: "that is, the building of eternal felicity, which they multiply though it will fall upon
  them, that here they may defend themselves from necessities … which the good wayfarer despises."
- Its immediate neighbour, one sentence up, says these men "here loved transitory things alone"
  (`sola hic transitoria dilexerunt`), and the same sentence says the building will fall on them and
  that the good wayfarer despises it. Eternal felicity neither falls nor is despised. The word
  wanted is a temporal one (`temporalis` / `praesentis felicitatis`).
- English is faithful to Migne; direction 1 only.

### M4 — 0016, col. 0788A. Word-division that reverses the sense: `in circumscriptione` for `incircumscriptione`.
- Lemma: « Excelsior [coelo est]. » / « Higher. »
- Latin as printed: `Quia in circumscriptione sui spiritus cuncta transcendit.`
- English: "Because in the circumscription of his own spirit he transcends all things."
- A spirit that is circumscribed is precisely what cannot transcend all things; the gloss is
  expounding a lemma about God being higher than heaven, and the neighbouring glosses run on his
  boundlessness (`Longior terra` → "he exceeds the measure of the creature by the perpetuity of
  eternity"). Read `incircumscriptione` ("because by the uncircumscribedness of his spirit he
  transcends all things").
- Faithful to Migne's spacing; report only so the plate can settle the word division.

### M5 — 0018, col. 0791D. `veram` for `vestram` — already carried with a `[var:]`.
- Lemma: « Secundum scientiam veram. » / « According to true knowledge. » `[var: Vulg. Job 13:2
  *vestram*, "according to your knowledge"]`
- The gloss under it reads `et secundum eorum scientiam se nosse confirmat` / "he affirms that he
  knows according to *their* knowledge" — which needs `vestram`, not `veram`.
- This is the lemma-to-gloss contradiction the genre produces, it is Migne's, and the English
  already flags it. Recorded for completeness, not as new work.

## Rejected candidates (looked at, judged not defects)

- **R1 — 0011, col. 0777B, `Parce mihi, Domine`:** `quia contemptu illorum haec melius sperantur` /
  "because by contempt of those these are the better hoped for". The deictics look swapped (contempt
  of *these* present things is how *those* eternal ones are hoped for). Rejected as a report because
  the preceding clause "that thou mayest give eternal things" can carry `illorum` = the present
  things last in view, and nothing in the neighbourhood is actually contradicted. Not raised.
- **R2 — 0016, col. 0788A, `Profundior inferno. Quia transcendendo subvehit`:** "Because by
  transcending he bears upward" reads oddly under a lemma about depth. But the gloss pair
  (higher/deeper) is Gregory arguing that God's height and depth are the same boundlessness, and no
  neighbour is contradicted. Rejected — obscure, not reversed.
- **R3 — 0019, col. 0796B, `« Terra, » quae sanctificat`:** on Job 14:19 `alluvione paulatim terra
  consumitur`, a gloss saying the earth "sanctifies" is unexpected among neighbours that read the
  stones as strength and the waters as constant blandishments. Rejected: there is no contradicting
  neighbour, only an absent one — the gloss is a bare two-word note and could as easily be sound.
- **0014, col. 0785A, `sed beneficiis Dei nemo serviliter timens respondet?`** — mid-sentence `?`
  and the interrogative shape are Pattern 8, carried deliberately. Not a finding.
- Unbalanced guillemets throughout (0010 `« Impleatur risu os tuum, » id est mens gaudio « aeternae
  securitatis`, 0018 `Aut certe loquar. »`), `Nunquid`/`coelum`/`Exspectate` spellings, and lemmata
  diverging from the Vulgate without a gloss quarrel: all excluded by the brief. Not reported.
