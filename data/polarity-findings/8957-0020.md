## Findings (all direction 1 — Migne's own defect, correctly carried into the English)

### 1. col. 0820C, chunk 0029, Job 23:17 — gloss asserts what the lemma denies ⭐ strongest

Lemma (quoted two lines above, col. 0820B): « Non enim perii. » — "For I have not perished."

Latin as printed:
> VERS. 17.-- « Non enim. » Ille in flagello positus a salute corporis propter imminentes tenebras
> **perit**, qui pro transactis percutitur, ut a futuris suppliciis abscondatur.

English as it stands:
> VERS. 17.-- « For not. » He, set in the scourge, **perishes** from the health of the body because
> of the impending darkness, who is struck for the things done in the past, that he may be hidden
> from future punishments.

The gloss on *Non enim perii* says the man **does** perish — and the relative clause that follows
("who is struck for past things, that he may be hidden from future punishments") is plainly the
description of a man who is *not* lost. The sentence contradicts both its own lemma and its own
tail. Reads as a dropped `non` before `perit` (Migne), i.e. `... propter imminentes tenebras **non**
perit, qui pro transactis percutitur ...`. The English renders the printed text exactly, so nothing
is wrong in our work; flagging so the plate can be checked and, if the plate reads as Migne here,
a crux note attached.

### 2. col. 0820A, chunk 0029, Job 23:12 — intrusive `non` inside the condition

Latin as printed:
> Si intus **non** elevatur animus, sed si laus extra quaeritur, tunc sermo Dei in sinu non
> occultatur.

English as it stands:
> If the mind is **not** lifted up within, but if praise is sought without, then the word of God is
> not hidden in the bosom.

Immediate neighbour (same gloss, two sentences earlier): "We hide the words of his mouth in the
bosom of the heart ... and if the words come forth in work, they nevertheless lie hidden in the
heart." The failure case must therefore be *elation within* + praise sought without; as printed the
protasis says the opposite. Expected sense: `Si intus elevatur animus, et si laus extra quaeritur,
tunc sermo Dei in sinu non occultatur.` Again faithfully carried; a Migne-side `non`.

### 3. col. 0799D, chunk 0020, Job 15:35 — conceive/travail transposed

Latin as printed:
> VERS. 35.-- « Concepit dolorem. » Cum perversa cogitat, iniquitatem **parturit**, cum explere
> coeperit, **concepit**.

English as it stands:
> When he thinks perverse things, he is in travail with iniquity; when he has begun to accomplish
> it, he has conceived.

The lemma is *Concepit dolorem, et peperit iniquitatem* — conception precedes birth, and the gloss
as printed reverses the order (thinking = travail; doing = conception), contradicting the verse it
expounds. Expected: `Cum perversa cogitat, concipit; cum explere coeperit, parturit.` A
transposition in Migne, carried straight into the English. Not an error in our work.

### 4. col. 0797D–0798A, chunk 0020, Job 15:16 — "Inutilis, propter justitiam perfectae vitae"

Latin as printed:
> « Inutilis, » propter justitiam perfectae vitae; qui « bibere iniquitatem » dicitur ...

English as it stands:
> « Unprofitable, » because of the justice of a perfect life; who is said « to drink iniquity » ...

A man is called *unprofitable* — and the reason given is the justice of a perfect life. Contradicts
its own lemma; the parallel clause just before gives a privative reason ("abominable because of the
uncleanness of his stain"), so a privative is expected here too (a lost `in-` on *justitiam*, or a
lost word such as *vacuitatem/defectum*). Lower confidence than 1–3 — I could not exclude a
compressed "[unprofitable measured] against the justice of a perfect life" reading — but it is a
lemma/gloss clash and worth a plate look. English is faithful either way.

## Candidates examined and rejected (with reasons)

- **0026, col. 0813B, « Non remansit de civitate ejus » glossed "de cibo ejus nihil remanet"** —
  a real lemma/gloss divergence (*civitate* vs *cibo*), but the English already carries a
  `[var: ...]` marker naming it. Known, not a new site, and not our defect.
- **0021, col. 0801D, `ab impugnatore calido` → "the crafty assailant"** — the English reads
  *callido*. A silent normalisation of a Migne spelling, but not sense-reversing; outside this
  pass's remit. Noting only so the orchestrator can decide whether it wants a `[sic:]`.
- **0026, col. 0813C, lemma « Fugiet arma » → "He shall flee from weapons of iron"** — "of iron"
  has no exponent in Migne's abridged lemma (Vulgate *arma ferrea*). An addition, not a polarity
  flip; the guidance says abridged lemmata are ordinary here, so I do not call it a defect.
- **0022, col. 0804B, `tanto subtilius a judice rationes de ipsis cogitant exquiri`** — the English
  makes *the judge* the one who thinks, where the Latin's verb is plural (*cogitant*, subject
  *cogitationes*). A syntax reading, not a reversal of sense; the Latin sentence is itself broken at
  this point. Not reported as polarity.
- **0029, col. 0819D, `Non elatus comparat se auro`** — English "Not being lifted up does he compare
  himself to gold" is awkward but keeps the negation on *elatus*. Faithful.
- **0023, col. 0806C, `si superbus minime fuisset`**; **0027, col. 0815B, `et hac usi pereunt, et
  flagellati liberantur`**; **0024, col. 0808A, `qui non aeque fieri dicit`** — all checked
  specifically because they invert twice in a short space; all rendered correctly.
- **Unbalanced guillemets** at 0020 col. 0798C (« Sonitus terroris semper.), 0020 col. 0798D
  (VERS. 28.--Habitavit), 0029 col. 0819C (VERS. 5.--Ut sciam) — per the brief, Migne's own habit;
