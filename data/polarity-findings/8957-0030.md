## Site 1 — col. 0833D, chunk 0035, Job 30:6 « Vel super glaream »

**Latin as printed:**
> « Vel super glaream. » Glaream **munitissimos** lapides dicimus, quos aqua fluvialis trahit: haeretici super glaream habitant, quia illas hominum mentes trahunt, quae **nulla stabilitatis gravitate solidantur**.

**English as it stands:**
> « Or upon the gravel. » We call gravel **the most compacted stones**, which the river water drags along: heretics dwell upon the gravel, because they drag those minds of men which are **made solid by no weight of stability**.

**Direction: 1 — Migne's defect, correctly carried.**

The gloss contradicts itself inside one sentence. Gravel is defined as the *most compacted / most fortified* stones, and in the same breath it is (a) what river water drags along and (b) the figure of minds "made solid by no weight of stability." The whole point of the image is instability. `munitissimos` is almost certainly a corruption of **`minutissimos`** ("the tiniest stones, which the river water drags along") — the reading that makes the definition, the dragging, and the application all agree. `minutissimos → munitissimos` is a plausible plate/OCR path (n/u, i/u).

**What the English should read:** nothing, if `munitissimos` is what the plate prints — the rendering is faithful and the edition exists to show the defect. Worth a plate check on 0833D; if the plate reads `minutissimos`, the Latin needs correcting and the English should read "the tiniest stones."

## Site 2 — col. 0838C, chunk 0036, Job 31:26–27

**Latin as printed:**
> « Et lunam incedentem. » Lunam dicit famam … « **Et non est laetatum** in abscondito cor meum. » **Sunt enim qui suis praeconiis extolluntur apud se, et gaudent.** Et hoc est:
> VERS. 27.-- « **Et laetatum est**, etc. »

**English as it stands:**
> « And the moon going. » By the moon he means fame … « And my heart hath **not** rejoiced in secret. » [var: Vulg. Job 31:27 has no negative here, and reads that the heart did rejoice in secret] For there are those who are exalted in their own praises before themselves, **and rejoice**. And this is:
> VERS. 27.-- « **And it hath rejoiced**, etc. »

**Direction: 1 — Migne's defect (intrusive `non`), correctly carried.**

This is the page handing you its own contradicting neighbour. The lemma is printed with a negative; the gloss immediately expounds the *affirmative* ("there are those who … rejoice"), and then Migne re-quotes the same lemma four lines later **without** the negative (« Et laetatum est »). The re-quotation is decisive internal evidence: the `non` at the first quotation is intrusive. Vulg. Job 31:27 has no negative, and Gregory (Mor. XXII) expounds the affirmative.

**What the English should read:** as it stands, if the plate prints `non`. The existing `[var:]` marker flags divergence from the Vulgate but does not record that Migne's *own* second quotation of the lemma contradicts his first — that is the stronger crux, and worth adding to the crux record.

## Candidates examined and rejected (with reasons)

- **0828B, Job 28:9 «Subvertit a radicibus montes»** — "saeculi potestates ad colendum Deum ab intima cogitatione ceciderunt" / "the powers of the world fell from their inmost thought to the worship of God." The English can be read as *falling away from* worship, the opposite of the conversion sense the passage wants. Rejected as a finding: the English reproduces the Latin's own word order and inherits exactly the Latin's ambiguity; nothing was added or reversed by us. (A clarifying rendering — "fell, from their inmost thought, to worshipping God" — would be an improvement in readability, not a polarity repair.)
- **0831A, Job 29:7** — "cum nulla de sua praedicatione metuit, **nullus** pressus terroribus se sub silentio abscondit" / "…no one, pressed by terrors, hides himself in silence." Migne's `nullus` is probably for `nullis` ("pressed by no terrors"). Rejected: both readings land on the same sense (nobody hides), so no polarity is reversed, and the English follows the printed `nullus`.
- **0832B, Job 29:20 «Gloria mea semper innovabitur»** — the gloss says "Idem quasi amissam gloriam deplorat" / "bewails as it were his lost glory," which looks like the reverse of the lemma. Rejected: the whole of Cap. XXIX is framed at VERS. 1–2 as the Church remembering past goods and sighing over the present, so a lemma of past glory glossed as present lament is the chapter's own logic, not a contradiction.
- **0832C, Job 29:22 «Verbis meis addere»** — Vulgate continues "nihil audebant"; the gloss says heretics *do* presume to add. Rejected: the lemma is abridged before the negative, so the printed lemma asserts nothing for the gloss to contradict. Ordinary abridgement habit of this commentary.
- **0834B, Job 30:11 «Et frenum posuit in os me»** — Migne prints `os me`; the English reads "into my mouth." Rejected as a polarity site: a truncated `meum`, normalised in the English, with no effect on sense or negation. Noted only because it is a silent normalisation.
- **0835A, Job 30:15 «Velut nubes pertransiit»** — the lemma is Job's *salus*, the gloss glosses "perversorum gloria." Rejected: the following clause ("quo alta est, fixa non est") coheres with the gloss as printed; the English is faithful; possible `perversorum`/`persecutorum` variation is not decisive and is not a negation issue.
- **Unbalanced guillemets, missing terminal stops, `Nunquid`/`coelum`/`haeritici`/`congnosci` spellings, lemmata diverging from Vulgate/Douay** — per the brief, not defects; observed throughout and not counted.
