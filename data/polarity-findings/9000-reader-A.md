## 2. Findings — OURS (no trace in the Latin)

### F1 · @0270A (chunk 0011, VERS. 22) — a negation that garden-paths into its own opposite ⚠ HIGHEST
- **Latin:** *Interrogatus de se, non verbo aliquo, sed factis se esse signavit, non respondens ad
  ea quae interrogabantur, sed ad scandalum nuntiorum.*
- **English as written:** "Being questioned about himself, he signified **what he was not by any
  word**, but by his deeds…"
- **Direction:** ours.
- **The defect:** the Latin negates the *instrument* (`non verbo aliquo`). English word order puts
  "not" immediately after "what he was", and the first parse a reader takes is *he signified what
  he was-not* — the exact opposite of the sentence's claim, and self-contradictory against its own
  next clause. No word is missing; the force is in the running text but pointed the wrong way. This
  is the John @0415A failure in punctuation form: every particle count scores it clean.
- **Repair:** `…he signified what he was, not by any word, but by his deeds…` (a comma is the whole
  fix).

### F2 · @0272B (chunk 0012, VERS. 49) — a clause the English does not carry
- **Latin:** *Sanata aegra, de salute ejus aliis aegrotant.*
- **English as written:** "The sick woman being healed, **they sicken over her salvation, to
  others**."
- **Direction:** ours (a printed oddity — dative `aliis` where the sense wants nominative `alii` —
  passed through as an English fragment that means nothing).
- **The defect:** "to others" is stranded at the end of an English sentence with no construction to
  attach to; the subject of "they sicken" has no antecedent. A reader gets no proposition at all.
  Compare the parallel Migne prints two lines up (`Sua sententia Pharisaeus convincitur`) — the
  point is that *others* fall sick over her cure.
- **Repair:** `The sick woman being healed, others sicken over her healing.` — with a crux naming
  the printed `aliis`, and asserting nothing about whose it is.

### F3 · @0251A–B (chunk 0003, VERS. 36) — mechanism 5, relative rebound to the easier noun
- **Latin:** *De tribu Aser. Id est beati, descendit, **qui** inter duodecim patriarchas ordine
  nascendi octavus est, propter mysterium resurrectionis.*
- **English as written:** "That is, of the blessed, she is descended, **who** among the twelve
  patriarchas is eighth in the order of birth…" (English: "who among the twelve patriarchs is
  eighth in the order of birth").
- **Direction:** ours.
- **The defect:** `qui` is masculine and its antecedent is **Aser**. In the English the nearest
  candidate is "she" (Anna), and the relative reads as hers — making Anna one of the twelve
  patriarchs. Check case and gender, not sense: the easier reading is the wrong one.
- **Repair:** `…of the tribe of Aser. That is, of the blessed one, she is descended — he who among
  the twelve patriarchs is eighth in the order of birth — on account of the mystery of the
  resurrection.`

### F4 · @0260A (chunk 0007, VERS. 38) — lemma conformed to the familiar verse (7a″)
- **Latin lemma as printed:** *Sed vinum.*
- **English as written:** *But **new** wine.*
- **Direction:** ours.
- **The defect:** Migne prints two words; the English prints three. "New" comes from the Douay of
  Lc 5:38 ("But new wine must be put into new bottles"), not from the page. This is exactly the
  recognizability that 7a″ forbids buying.
- **Repair:** `*But wine.*`

### F5 · @0258A (chunk 0006, VERS. 24) — the distinction the sentence turns on, collapsed
- **Latin:** *…per **missionem** poenae, **remissionem** culpae intellige.*
- **English as written:** "by the **remission** of the penalty understand the **remission** of the
  fault."
- **Direction:** ours.
- **The defect:** the clause is built on *missio* vs *remissio*; one English word for both leaves
  the sentence saying "by the remission of X understand the remission of Y" — a tautology in place
  of an argument. Same class as the epithet rule: two words, two Englishes, fixed.
- **Repair:** `by the sending away of the penalty understand the remission of the fault.`

### F6 · @0258D and @0259A (chunk 0006, VERS. 31 and 34) — untranslated non-words left in the running English
- **Latin:** *Qui hoc medicamento utitur, non remanet **ejunus**.* / *Quandiu sponsus nobiscum est
  et in laetitia sumus, nec **jejumare** possumus…*
- **English as written:** "He who uses this medicine does not remain ***ejunus***." / "neither can
  we ***jejumare***…"
- **Direction:** ours (the underlying non-words are unattributable — `ejunus`/`jejumare` for
  `jejunus`/`jejunare`; both look like the hyphen-rejoin or type class and neither should be
  marked).
- **The defect:** declining to *mark* is right; declining to *translate* is not. The running
  English of both sentences carries no predicate a reader can read, and the second sits inside the
  work's central argument about fasting. Apparatus is not force; neither is a raw Latin word in
  italics.
- **Repair:** render the evident sense (`does not remain fasting`; `neither can we fast`) and log
  both non-words in the cruces file, naming both possible owners and asserting neither.

### F7 · @0262C (chunk 0008, VERS. 20, splice tail) — a verb rendered as something else
- **Latin:** *…sed quia fide **praestitit**, fide prae caeteris meruit principatum.*
- **English as written:** "but because he **showed it** by faith, by faith he deserved the primacy
  before the rest."
- **Direction:** ours.
- **The defect:** `praestare` + ablative = *to excel in*, and `prae caeteris` in the very next
  clause confirms it. "Showed it" invents a referent ("it") that the Latin has not got and drops
  the comparison the sentence is about.
- **Repair:** `but because he excelled in faith, by faith he earned the primacy over the rest.`

## 3. Findings — MIGNE'S (render literally, log, fire nothing)

### M1 · @0276A (chunk 0013, VERS. 37) — a word split, and the English now contradicts its neighbour
- **Latin as printed:** *Conscii fragilitatis, praesentia Domini se indignos judicant, non
  capientes verbum Dei, et **in firma mente** pondus sapientiae non sustinentes…*
- **English as written:** "…and not sustaining the weight of wisdom **in a firm mind**…"
- **Direction:** Migne's type or Corpus Corporum's word-split — **I do not know whose, and I assert
  nothing.** `in firma` is almost certainly `infirma`: the clause's own opening is *Conscii
  fragilitatis*, "conscious of their frailty", and a *firm* mind that cannot bear wisdom's weight
  contradicts it flatly.
- **Handling:** the literal rendering is the right one and I would not change it. What must exist
  is the crux — this is a self-contradicting sentence and the reader is owed the reason. If the
  cruces file does not carry it, the entry is what is missing, not the English.

### M2 · @0270D (chunk 0011, VERS. 25) — printed nonsense, correctly carried
- **Latin:** *Prophetico ergo exemplo ad virtutem **subeundae virtutis** hortatur.* … *Ecce quae in
  veste pretiosa. Quibus fluida divitiis membra solvuntur, **exteros** coelestis regni … consenescunt.*
- **English as written:** "he exhorts to the virtue of a virtue to be undergone" … "strangers to
  the heavenly kingdom … grow old".
- **Direction:** Migne's or ours-the-file; unattributable. `ad virtutem subeundae virtutis` is
  self-referential (one of the two nouns is a repetition of the other — very likely of a word like
  *paupertatis*), and `exteros` is accusative where the syntax wants nominative.
- **Handling:** rendered as closely as the words allow, which is correct. Both belong in the log as
  suspect words with no attribution claim; no `[sic:]`.

- **N4 · @0254A (chunk 0005, VERS. 4):** *humilitati quam potentia* — dative where the parallel
  wants ablative; English smooths to "with humility rather than with power". Same class as N3:
  right reading, no trace. Log.
- **N5 · @0256B (chunk 0006, VERS. 2):** *quorum corda … provenit* — English preserves the number
  disagreement as "whose **hearts comes** forth". Preserving Migne is the policy, but the result is
  ungrammatical *English*, which is a different thing from a faithful one; and `provenit` may be
  a corruption of a transitive (`provehit`), in which case the mercy in "by living mercifully" is

- **N6 · @0272A (chunk 0012, VERS. 43):** *plus fortasse offendit, **cui** plus debuerit* rendered

  The sense survives either way — and the very next clause prints `qui amplius debuit`, so the
  divergence between the two is itself the interesting datum — but the English silently resolves a
  dative into a nominative. Worth a line in the log.

## 5. Candidates considered and REJECTED

These are the sites that looked like the hunt and are not. Each is a place a later reader will
also stop, so the reason is recorded.

1. **@0244D "Abia, Pater Dominus" → "Abia, the Lord is Father".** Subject/predicate of an
   etymological gloss are not fixed by the Latin (no verb); either English is defensible. Reject.
2. **@0245B "paventem suo aspectu blandiendo solari" → "one who is afraid at its appearing".**
   `suo aspectu` can attach either to *paventem* (afraid at the angel's sight) or to *solari*
   (consoles by its own aspect). Genuinely ambiguous in the Latin; the English picks one and the
   sense is unchanged either way. Reject.
3. **@0248D "pro liberatione praecursor donatus est" → "was given a forerunner in place of
   deliverance".** `donatus est` agrees with `praecursor`, so the English makes Zacharias the
   grammatical subject of a verb that is not his. But VERS. 13 above (`Rogasti pro liberatione
   plebis, et donatus est tibi praecursor`) settles the sense, and the English states it correctly.
   Reject as style, not sense.
4. **@0254C "de sua conculcatione … tacet" → "of his own treading down he keeps silence".**
   English "his own treading down" is readable actively, but the psalm quoted two lines earlier
   (*conculcabis leonem et draconem*) makes the passive reading the only available one to any
   reader who has read the sentence before it. Ambiguity, not a mis-binding. Reject — though
   "of his own being trodden underfoot" would be strictly better.
5. **@0253D "ostenderet nobis diabolum ad meliora tendentibus invidere" → "envies those who reach
   out toward better things".** `nobis … tendentibus` can be read as agreeing (the devil envies
   *us*), which the English loses. But `nobis` is also simply the object of *ostenderet*, and then
   `tendentibus` is the object of *invidere* with no pronoun. Both parses are open. Reject.
6. **@0269A "mos est … praeterire quae breviter dicta ab aliis, vel omissa solertius dilucidare".**
   The English splits the compound object across the two verbs. This looked like mechanism 5 until
   I checked the punctuation: Migne's comma falls after *aliis*, which licenses exactly the split
   the English makes. Following the printed comma. Reject.
7. **@0261D "Matthaeus compari suo Thomae … se supponit, cum a caeteris evangelistis praelatus
   sit."** Checked who is set before whom; the English has it right (Matthew puts himself below
   Thomas though the others put him above). Reject.
8. **@0269B "Credente Domino sanatur servus … meritum Domini".** `Domino` here is the centurion,
   not the Lord, and the English says "master" — the harder and correct reading, twice. Explicitly
   *not* a finding; recording it because a checker scanning for "Lord" will stop here.
9. **@0255A "Vere enim erat filius fabri, qui per ipsum in principio omnia fecit".** `qui` binds to
   the *faber* (the Father), not to the son; the English says "of one who through him made all
   things", which is the harder correct binding. Not a finding — recorded as a near-miss the
   translation got right.
10. **@0270C "etiam justitiae formam nec mortis timore mutavit" → "did not change the form even of
    justice through fear of death".** `etiam` has been attached to *justitiae* rather than to
    *nec mortis timore*. Both attachments are open in the Latin. Reject as a placement preference.
11. **@0263C "debet patientiam verberantis sustinere" → "sustain the patient bearing of him that
    beats".** Reads at first as though the *striker* is the patient one; but `verberantis` is an
    objective genitive and English "of" carries objective genitives too. Ambiguous, not reversed.
    Reject.
12. **@0271B "qui nec praedicatione Joannis moti sunt, vel ipsius Christi praedicationem
    despiciunt" → "who neither were moved … or who despise".** "Neither … or" is clumsy English,
    but it mirrors Migne's own `nec … vel` and carries both members. Reject (register, not sense).
13. **@0262A "Non a proximo mari Galilaeae, quia hoc non miraculi loco ponunt".** The Latin clause
    is opaque; the English is opaque in the same way and adds nothing. Correct handling. Reject.
14. **Six or seven `noluit`/`voluit` sites** (0249C *nasci voluit*, 0268D *ire noluit*, 0273D
    *exponere noluit*, 0275D *nolle pati*, 0265C *noli eum tibi obtemperare*): all checked
    letter by letter against the English; all carried with the right polarity. No `noluit`/`voluit`
    swap anywhere in the range.
