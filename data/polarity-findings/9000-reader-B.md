## 3. Findings — OUR direction

### 3.1 `adesse` translated as its opposite — chunk 0022, col. 0302B/C, VERS. 7 (HIGH)

**Latin as printed:**
> In qua quaerit fructum, **non qui adesse nesciret**, sed ostendit in figura, quia in
> Synagoga jam fructum habere deberet.

**English as written:**
> In it he seeks fruit, **not as though he knew not that it was absent**, but he shows
> in a figure that in the Synagogue it ought by now to have fruit.

*adesse* is "to be present". The English prints its opposite, **absent**. The motive is
visible: the fig tree has no fruit, so "did not know that it was there" reads awkwardly
and "did not know that it was absent" reads smooth. This is mechanism 1 exactly — a
word rendered as its opposite so the sentence will run — and it leaves no trace.

**Repair:** "not as one who did not know that it was there" (or "…that it was present").
If the printed reading is judged strained, that goes in the crux, not into the lexis.

### 3.2 The parable's woman silently becomes "he" — chunk 0026, col. 0311D, VERS. 8 (HIGH)

**Latin as printed:**
> *Convocat.* Amicae et vicinae supernae potestates, quae tanto Deo juxta sunt, quanto
> eum vicinius contemplantur. **Quas vocat, dum per exhibitionem gratiae hominibus
> impensae ad amorem sui vehementius accendit.**

**English as written:**
> *She calleth together.* The friends and the neighbours are the powers above… **And he
> calls them, while through the showing of the grace bestowed upon men he kindles them
> more vehemently to the love of himself.**

The subject of this whole gloss is *mulier, id est Dei sapientia*, and our own English
carries her as **she** four times in the preceding six lines ("she created", "she lost
one", "she appeared in the flesh") and prints the lemma as "**She** calleth together".
Then, in the sentence that expounds that very lemma, the subject silently becomes **he**
— i.e. God rather than Wisdom-as-the-woman. Latin `vocat`/`accendit`/`sui` carry no
gender, so nothing on the page flags it; the running English contradicts its own
immediate neighbour and the lemma directly above it.

This is mechanism 5 in its ordinary shape: the contextually easier referent (God) is
substituted for the one the passage is actually built on (the woman of the parable =
Wisdom). Note the contrast: the sheep gloss two paragraphs earlier keeps its shepherd,
and `Qui nec post culpam a Creatore periit` @0311D is correctly bound to *decimus
numerus* — so the drift is local, not systematic.

**Repair:** "**Whom she calls**, while through the showing of grace bestowed upon men
**she** kindles them more vehemently to the love **of herself**."

### 3.3 A lemma left half-untranslated — chunk 0019, col. 0293C, VERS. 45 (MEDIUM-HIGH)

**Latin as printed:** `VERS. 45.-- *Repondens autem.*` (Migne's type for *Respondens*, Lc 11:45.)

**English as written:** `VERS. 45.-- *Repondens, however.*`

The Latin noise-word is passed through untranslated while the particle beside it is
Englished — so the running text hands the reader a Latin typo dressed as an English
lemma. Every other lemma in the range is translated (`*Quare non baptizatus.*` →
"*Why not washed.*" three lines earlier). The right handling is the one used at 0302B,
where Migne's `Vinea Domini **sabaoh**` is preserved *because it stands inside an
italic scripture quotation*; here the word is the lemma itself and must be rendered.

**Repair:** "*And one answering.*" (or "*But answering.*"), with the printed
*Repondens* recorded in the crux.

### 3.4 Untranslated place-name in running prose — chunk 0018, col. 0290B, VERS. 20 (MEDIUM)

**Latin as printed:** *Hunc digitum magi confessi sunt in Aegyto.*
**English as written:** "This finger the magicians confessed in *Aegyto.*"

Migne's *Aegyto* (for *Aegypto*) is carried into the English untranslated **and
italicised**. In this work italics mean "Vulgate words being glossed", so the
formatting makes a printer's typo look like a scriptural lemma, and the sentence leaves
an English reader with no place-name at all. This is the inverse of the
lemma-read-as-prose failure: prose read as a lemma.

**Repair:** "…confessed in Egypt," with the printed spelling logged as a crux
(no `[sic:]`, per the launch brief's prior — our Latin is not Migne's page).

### 3.5 `ignis inopia` parsed the one way the context excludes — chunk 0027, col. 0317A, VERS. 25 (MEDIUM)

**Latin as printed:**
> Nota quod dives iste aliquid boni habuit… et Lazarus aliquid mali habuit, **sed ignis
> inopia purgavit.**

**English as written:** "…and Lazarus had something of evil, **but the want of fire
purged it.**"

Both words are ambiguous (`ignis` nom./gen.; `inopia` nom./abl.), and the English takes
gen.+nom., yielding "the want of fire" — a purging agent the passage cannot mean. The
whole Augustinian point is that **poverty** purged what evil Lazarus had (the standard
text has *ignis inopiae*, the fire of want). The alternative parse of the words exactly
as printed — `ignis` nominative, `inopia` ablative — gives "but fire purged it by want",
which keeps the sense the neighbours require without emending a letter.

**Repair:** "but fire purged it by want" (or "but the fire of want purged it"), with the
printed *inopia* and the likely *inopiae* in the crux. Flagged as medium, not high:
the current reading is *grammatically* available, which is precisely why it needs a
human ruling rather than a silent pass.

### 3.6 Relative re-bound by English word order — chunk 0015, col. 0279D, VERS. 26 (LOW-MEDIUM)

**Latin as printed:**
> Non sufficit ad probationem fidei **vox professionis, quam defendit a verecundia
> professio generalitatis.**

**English as written:**
> "The voice of profession does not suffice for the proof **of faith, which the
> profession of the generality defends from shame.**"

`quam` is feminine accusative and its antecedent is the sentence's subject, *vox
professionis* — the utterance that professing-along-with-everyone shields from
embarrassment. The English relative sits immediately after "faith", which is also
feminine and which any reader will take as the antecedent. The Latin is unambiguous;
our word order is not, and the easier reading is the wrong one.

**Repair:** "The voice of profession, which the profession of the generality defends
from shame, does not suffice for the proof of faith."

## 4. Migne's direction — English already literal, crux owed

These need no change to the English. They are listed because a defect that is rendered
faithfully but never logged is indistinguishable, later, from one nobody noticed.

**4.1 chunk 0022, col. 0301C, VERS. 1 — *petierunt* for *perierunt*.**
> nuntiatur ei de Galilaeis, quod quasi juste ideo **petierunt**, quod non bene se
> praeparaverunt

Our English: "who as it were justly **sought** this for the cause that…". The next
clause of the same sentence supplies the true verb — *illos vere fuisse peccatores et
juste **periisse*** — so the printed *petierunt* is a dropped `r`. English correctly
literal; crux should carry the conjecture *perierunt*.

**4.2 chunk 0026, col. 0312A, VERS. 12 — *negatur* for *regatur*.**
> Sicut Deus non habet alium a quo formetur, **a quo negatur**, sic iste vult sua
> potestate uti, ut nullo formante, **nullo regente** vivat.

The sentence's own second half (*nullo regente*) names the right verb. English "by whom
he is denied" is the faithful rendering of the printed word; crux owed.

**4.3 chunk 0027, col. 0316A, VERS. 16 — *irritus* for *irrisus*.**
> cum linguas etiam **deridentium** contemnit, sicut Dominus **irritus** est a Pharisaeis.

The clause is about mockery ("the tongues of those who **deride**"), and *irrisus* is
one letter away. English "is made void" is literal and right to be so; crux owed.

**4.4 chunk 0027, col. 0316B, VERS. 19 — *praecepta* for *peccata*.**
> quia viscera pietatis ignoravit, quia **praecepta** pretio redimere noluit.

Gregory's point (and the whole context of redeeming by alms — cf. *sua… peccata,
eleemosynis redimere* at 0315C in the same chunk) is that Dives would not redeem his
**sins** with a price. English "redeem the precepts with a price" is literal; crux owed.

**4.5 chunk 0027, col. 0315B, VERS. 13 — dropped verb.**
> Corripiens avaritiam dicit amatores pecuniae **Deum amore non posse**

Our English preserves the lacuna: "the lovers of money are not able God with love."
That is the correct treatment, but a reader will read it as *our* typo unless the crux
says otherwise. (Likely *Deum amare non posse*.)

**4.6 Lexical note, not a defect — *pietas*.** Rendered "family affection" at 0295D
(*pietas debet sequestra dividere*) and "carnal love" at 0309C (*carnalis pietatis
affectibus renuntiare*). At 0309C the kinship sense is the whole point (Lc 14:26), and
"love" loses it. Suggest "the affections of carnal family-love" for consistency. Same
class, lower stakes: *conditor* and *Creator* both → "Creator" at 0287B.

## 5. Candidates considered and REJECTED

- **0294B `nec ipse regnum Dei intrat et illos… excludit`** — the Latin's `nec… et` is
  a defect, but our "neither himself enters… and shuts out those…" reproduces it. Correct.
- **0284A `usque ad`** — checked at all eight occurrences in the range (0284A, 0290C,
  0293A ×2, 0294B, 0300B, 0308A, 0313A, 0316B). Rendered "as far as" every time, per the
  inherited convention. No drift.
- **`turba`/`multitudo`, `daemonium`/`diabolus`, `gentes`/`natio`, `mysterium`/`sacramentum`** —
  spot-checked wherever they occur (0281B, 0282A, 0284B, 0289A–B, 0291A, 0304B, 0313D).
  All hold. `turba` → crowd, `multitudo` → multitude at 0298B; `daemonium` → demon vs
  `diabolus` → devil kept apart at 0282A and throughout 0289–0291.
- **0287D `haec convivium parat Domino, in convivio Domini illa jam jucundatur`** — the
  obvious mechanism-5 trap (Martha/Mary via *haec*/*illa*). English binds *haec* to
  Martha ("the latter prepares a banquet") and *illa* to Mary. **Correct**, not a finding.
- **0290A `Ideo ipsi… eligentes… sedebunt`** — could easily have been rebound to God
  choosing the world's ignoble things (1 Cor 1:27). English keeps the plural participle
  on the apostles, matching `sedebunt`. **Correct.**
- **0301B `ne contemptus te accuset`** — *contemptus* could be the noun ("lest contempt
  accuse thee"); English takes it as the participle bound to *sermo Dei*. The singular
  verb and the sentence's subject support the participle. **Correct.**
- **0311D `Qui nec post culpam a Creatore periit`** — masculine `qui` correctly bound to
  *decimus numerus*, not to *homo*. **Correct.**
- **0317C `Redemptoris praecepta qui ex mortuis surrexit`** — `qui` bound to the
  Redeemer, not to *praecepta*. **Correct.**
- **0298B `nihil mundamini`** ("you are cleansed in nothing") — reads oddly against its
  neighbours and is probably corrupt, but our English reproduces the printed word rather
  than repairing it. **Correct behaviour**; crux may be owed.
- **0292C `oculis mentis tuae… divinarum sensus albescit lectionem`** — the accusative
  *lectionem* is ungrammatical and our English quietly normalises it to "the sense of the
  divine readings". A smoothing, but of a bare case-ending with no effect on sense or
  agency. Reported, not counted.
- **0302C `in Synagoga crebris docet`** — dangling ablative rendered as "often". Same
  class as the above; below the bar.
- **0291D `ex eodem fonte et lacte… et semen… emanat`** — *lacte* for *lac* normalised.
  Same class.
- **0290A `Non potestate, sed comparatione` → "Not by the power of judgment"** — "of
  judgment" is supplied. It is harmonised from the parallel at 0292B where Migne *does*
  print `Non potestate judicii`, so this is a defensible fill, not a rebinding. Noted only.
- **0284D `non fraudatur orans`** — "is not cheated of his prayer" makes the nominative
  participle an object. Cosmetic; sense unchanged.
- **0292B `Non mille annis ante judicium sicut Judaei fingunt`** — checked for a dropped
  negation; none. Faithful.
