## 2. Findings

### 2.1 @0323A–B, chunk 0030, VERS. 13 *Deus, propitius* — OURS, serious
**Latin as printed:** *Publicanus humiliter orans ad illos pertinet de quibus dicitur: Deus
non faciet vindictam electorum ad se clamantium. Pharisaeus merita jactans, ad illos de
quibus subditur:* **Filius hominis veniens, putas inveniet fidem in terra?**
**English as written:** "The publican praying humbly belongs to those of whom it is said:
God will not revenge his elect crying to him. The Pharisee boasting of his merits belongs
to those of whom it is added: *The Son of man coming, thinkest thou, shall he find faith on
earth?*"
**Direction:** ours. The Latin is Luke 18:7 quoted as the **rhetorical question** it is in
the Vulgate (*Deus autem non faciet vindictam electorum suorum clamantium ad se…?* — "And
will not God avenge his elect…?"). Migne prints no question mark, and the English has
rendered it as a flat negative assertion. The running text now says God will **not** avenge
the elect — which destroys the sentence's own point (the humble publican belongs to the
class God *does* vindicate) and contradicts its immediate neighbour, where the parallel
citation is correctly kept interrogative and italicised. Nothing in the marker apparatus
carries the force back; a reader reads a denial.
**Repair:** render as the question, italicised like its twin —
"…of whom it is said: *Shall not God revenge his elect that cry to him?*"
(with a `[var:]`/crux note that Migne prints the clause without its question mark if that
is the house habit).

### 2.2 @0332A, chunk 0033, VERS. 15 — MIGNE'S, log only
**Latin:** *sed quasi **utile** cadaver projicientes, gentibus suscipiendum dederunt.*
**English:** "but, casting him out as it were a **useful** corpse, gave him to the Gentiles
to be taken up."
**Direction:** Migne's — almost certainly a dropped *in-* (*inutile cadaver*). *Projicere*
+ *utile* is self-cancelling, and the whole clause is about contemptuous disposal.
**Action:** the English is correctly literal and must stay so; this needs a crux line, not a
repair. Flagging because the contradiction is visible in the running English and a reader
will trip on it.

### 2.3 @0341B, chunk 0036, VERS. 44 *Sanguinis decurrentis* — OURS (mechanism 5)
**Latin:** *ut fidem scilicet discipulorum quam terrena adhuc fragilitas arguebat, suo
sanguine purgaret, et quidquid **illa** scandali de morte ejus pertulisset, hoc totum ipse
moriendo deleret…*
**English:** "…that he might cleanse by his own blood the faith of the disciples, which
earthly frailty was still convicting; and that whatever of scandal **that frailty** had
endured from his death, all this he might himself blot out by dying…"
**Direction:** ours. Two feminine candidates stand in the sentence, *fidem* and *fragilitas*;
*illa* is the pointer to the **more remote** one, i.e. *fides*, and the sense requires it —
it is the disciples' **faith** that suffered scandal at his death and that his blood
cleanses. The English bound the pronoun to the nearer, easier noun (frailty), which leaves
the cleansing of the faith and the blotting-out of the scandal attached to two different
subjects.
**Repair:** "…and that whatever of scandal **that faith** had endured from his death…"

### 2.4 @0342C, chunk 0036, VERS. 56 *Ancilla quaedam* — OURS (silent voice repair)
**Latin:** *Nisi ut iste sexus ostendatur in nece Domini peccasse, per passionem
**redempturus**.*
**English:** "Unless that this sex might be shown to have sinned in the Lord's slaying,
**being about to be redeemed** through the passion."
**Direction:** ours. *Redempturus* is active (future participle, masc., agreeing with
*sexus*): "about to redeem through the passion" — or, on the other available reading,
attaching to the Lord who was about to redeem by the passion. The English has quietly
turned it passive to make the theology come out even. That is exactly the smoothing the
register forbids: whichever of the two readings is right, the printed form is active, and
the passive rendering leaves no trace that anything was decided.
**Repair:** render the active participle literally ("…to have sinned in the slaying of the
Lord, who was about to redeem through the passion" — or "…this sex, about to redeem through
the passion") and log the difficulty; do not carry the passive.

### 2.5 @0327A–B, chunk 0031, VERS. 13 *Vocatis autem decem* — OURS (mechanism 5, minor)
**Latin:** *Dat decem mnas, dum post resurrectionem surgit, et aperit sensum, ut
spiritualiter intelligat legis dicta*
**English:** "He gives ten pounds, when after the resurrection he rises and opens their
understanding, that **he** may spiritually understand the sayings of the law"
**Direction:** ours. The English has just made Christ the subject twice ("he rises and opens
their understanding"), so "that he may spiritually understand" reads as Christ understanding
the law — which is nonsense against the clause it depends on. The Latin's singular
*intelligat* has the taught disciple as its subject.
**Repair:** "…and opens their understanding, that **each of them** may spiritually understand
the sayings of the law" (or "that it [the understanding] may…", if the stint prefers to keep
the singular tight to *sensum*).

### 2.6 @0329A, chunk 0031, VERS. 30 *Alligatum* — OURS (mechanism 5, moderate)
**Latin:** *Alligatus vinculis perfidiae, in quo addictus vel astrictus Domino errore
famulabatur, sed dominatum sibi vindicare non poterat, quem Dominum fecerat non natura, sed
culpa.*
**English:** "Tied with the bonds of unbelief, in which, made over or bound fast, he was
serving a lord by error; but **he** could not claim the mastery for himself, **whom** not
nature but fault had made lord."
**Direction:** ours. In the Latin the subject of *vindicare non poterat* and the antecedent
of *quem* are the same person — the false lord (the devil), whom fault and not nature made
lord, and who therefore could not truly claim mastery. The English's "he" reads as the colt
/ the enslaved people (the subject of the previous clause), so "he" and "whom" split into two
different people and the point (the devil's lordship is usurped, not natural) is lost.
**Repair:** "…he was serving a lord by error; but **that lord** could not claim the mastery
for himself, since not nature but fault had made him lord."

### 2.7 @0321B, chunk 0028, VERS. 30 — MIGNE'S candidate, log only
**Latin:** *Qui interim **apparens** omnia videt, tunc apparens omnia judicabit…*
**English:** "He who, appearing in the meantime, sees all things, then appearing will judge
all things…"
The *interim … tunc* antithesis collapses if he "appears" in both halves; the sentence wants
*non apparens* (or *latens*) in the first. The English is faithful to the print, which is
correct — recorded here only so the column gets a crux line.

## 3. Candidates considered and REJECTED

1. **@0318B (0028), VERS. 6 *Arbori moro*** — *Evangelium crucis, quae … est eradicata et
   in mare gentium transplantata.* English "which" attaches to "the Gospel of the cross",
   and *Evangelium* is neuter so *quae* cannot be it (it is *morus* or *crux*). Rejected as a
   defect because all three candidates yield one and the same sense — the tree = the Gospel
   of the cross, uprooted from the Jewish stock and transplanted into the sea of the
   Gentiles — and the following clause (*in qua velut in stirpe … tenebatur verbum
   praedicationis*) fixes it. No re-binding of consequence.
2. **@0321A (0028), VERS. 27** — *cum in his successionis, in illis naturae posita sint
   subsidia.* The his/illis assignment is upside-down against the sense (succession belongs
   to marriage, sustenance to food). The English keeps the deixis literally ("in these… in
   those") and does not silently swap them. Correct handling; not a finding.
3. **@0333C (0033), VERS. 43** — *unitatem naturae, qua in altero aliter operatur* rendered
   "whereby **in the one** he works otherwise". *Alter* is "the other (of two)", so "in the
   one … otherwise" is slightly self-cancelling; but the Latin here is elliptical and no
   negation or reference is reversed. Noted, not filed.
4. **@0344B (0037), VERS. 5** — *liberum ab eo judicando cupit reddere*, rendered "desires to
   make himself free from judging him". The alternative ("wishes by his judgment to set him
   free") fits VERS. 14's *quantum studium dimittendi Jesum Pilatus habeat* better, but the
   Latin genuinely admits both and neither reading contradicts a neighbour. Rejected as
   ambiguity, not defect.
5. **Literal renderings of corrupt print, all correct and all left alone:** *fideres* for
   *fideles* @0321A; *Testatamenti* @0324D; the dittography *id est id est* @0326B (carried
   as `[d:]`); *teruisse* @0352B; *significant caecum carnalium vitiorum* @0325A;
   *neque audito Evangelio dormierunt* @0328A; *quae non intus … permissus est haesitare*
   @0342D; *ut, ibi erat area damnatorum* @0347B; *confidentiam ejusdem cum parte potestatis*
   @0349A (very likely *cum Patre*); *per poenitentiam … suspiciendam* @0326D; *humiliatur
   qui … indigent* @0329C; *intimis Pascha facit* @0337C. Each of these is a candidate crux
   line; none is a translation defect, and none should be repaired in the English.
6. **Convention checks in range:** *turba*/*multitudo* kept distinct (@0325D–0326A, @0324B,
   @0346C); *usque ad* rendered "as far as" at every abridgment splice (@0326A, @0338C twice,
   @0353C); *daemonium*/*diabolus* not blurred; commentator's voice not archaised. No drift
   found.
