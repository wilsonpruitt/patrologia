# Out-of-scope observations from the polarity sweep — real, but not polarity defects

Agents are told not to report garbles that produce **nonsense** rather than the
OPPOSITE claim. Several reported them anyway as side-notes, and some are worth
acting on separately. Parked here so they are not lost. **None of these are
polarity findings; do not fold them into REPORT.md's count.**

## Our English carries a nonsense reading through

- **11066 @0452A** (chunk 0006) — Migne prints *baptisma non solum **manibus**, sed
  etiam feminis conveniens*; plainly *maribus* (males), given *quare maribus et non
  feminis* and *Solis maribus ideo praecepta est* a few lines above. Our English
  reads "suited not only to **hands**, but also to women." Not a reversal, but a
  published sentence that means nothing. Candidate for a crux + literal rendering
  with a note.

## Plate/TEI defect where our English is already sound

- **11066 @0443A** (chunk 0003) — *num* for *non* in *de malo adversitatis, num de
  malo perversitatis intelligitur*. Our English already reads "not," so the pair is
  fine; the defect is on the plate/TEI side only. Log as a crux, change nothing.

## A work with a bad plate throughout — 11088, chunks 0000–0007

The u24 agent reported that this stretch is **dense with typographic garbles**, all
excluded as nonsense-not-opposite, all already carried through under `[sic: …]`
markers in our English: `hain domo`/`beamus` (0881C–0883A, a dislocated line),
`veluntatem`, `saecuto`, `montis` for `mentis` (0891C), `domo` for `dono` (0894C),
`it` for `nec`, `divinae` for `divitiae` (0896C), `astu` for `aestu`, `totuin`,
`site`, `cer vices`. The polarity sweep is the wrong instrument for this — it is a
**plate-quality problem for one work**, and worth its own look at whether the TEI or
the scan is the better witness for these columns.

## Our own translation errors that muddle rather than reverse — FIX THESE ANYWAY

Out of scope for the sweep's count, but they are still our bugs and they are still
live on the site.

- **11085 @0876C** — Migne prints *Non enim secus, sed natura disparem in alterutrum
  charitatem amori tribuit*, using `secus` in its archaic noun sense (**"sex"**). Our
  English reads it as the adverb: "For not otherwise, but nature, bestows…" — which
  voids the sex/nature contrast the flanking sentences require. A real translation
  error; produces muddle, not an opposite claim, so it stayed out of the findings.

## Factual errors on the plate, carried into English — not polarity, still wrong

- **11031 heading** — "Conrad **II**" where it should be **Conrad III**. Affects dating
  and attribution of the letter. Entirely outside the sweep's remit; recorded so it
  is not lost with the agent's rejection notes.
- **21425 @1080C** — the Comes list prints *Dominica quarta ante Natale Domini* **twice**;
  the 4-3-2-1 sequence and Amalarius at 1081C require *quinta* first. Faithfully
  carried into our English.
- **11061 @0025A** — `rex Judaeorum` for `rex Medorum` (this one IS in the findings, as a
  migne-side sense reversal, but it is the same species: one word, wrong fact).

- **7427 @1209-ish** — `judicium novissimum ipse suscipere` rendered "himself to
  **undergo** the last judgment" where the sense is "undertake" (i.e. conduct it).
  An agency question, not a polarity flip; no `defectType` fits. Christ presiding
  over the judgment vs. submitting to it is not a small difference — worth a look.
- **11079 @0839C** — Migne drops `posse` from *Deum plus posse quam velle*; our English
  silently supplies "can do." Correct sense, no note. Crux, not a fix.
- **8933 @1425C** — `claritatis` for `charitatis` in a passage otherwise entirely about
  *charitas*; our English follows with "brightness." The u62 agent asked whether a
  plain textual-error queue exists. This file is it.

## The antithesis-distortion class — real, but not a reversal

- **11088 @0896D** — `honorum` for `bonorum`: humility as "the beginning of all
  **honors**" set against pride as "the beginning of all evils." It wrecks the
  antithesis without stating the opposite, so it falls outside the sweep's
  definition. If a later pass wants a wider net, this is the class to add — but
  widening it mid-run would make the counts incomparable, so it stays out here.
