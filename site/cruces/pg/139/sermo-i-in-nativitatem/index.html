⚑ Why this table is the work's spine — the evidence, not the theory

πολυύμνητος and πανύμνητος stand three lines apart in the same section (@0040), and Allatius gives ONE Latin word for both (laudatissima / laudatissima illa). Likewise καλλίπαις/πολύπαις, where the contrast is the sentence's argument, and Allatius flattens καλλίπαις to venustissima puella; and θεουργικώτατον/πλαστουργικώτατον, collapsed the same way. A translator following the Latin as a guide rather than a witness would have silently destroyed three arguments. This is the homily's analogue of 7a″ lemma-conformation, and it is why the epithet rule was written into translation-style.md before this batch ran.

0003 @0032/0033 — Migne's Greek prints:

Ἀλλ᾽ ὃ μήτ᾽ ἐκ γῆς πρῶτον συνέστη, μήτε τι τῶν μελῶν ἀφήρηται, εἴη δ᾽ οὐδ᾽ ἂν ἕν, οὐχ ἕν τι τῶν ἁπάντων πρὸς ταῦτ᾽ οἰκειότερον ἢ ἡ Παρθένος

Work-wide conventions (anchor session, 2026-07-31 — merge into the work-wide section)

  • ΛΟΓΟΣ as a work-division head renders by the work's established English division term — here "SERMON" (registry + the plate's own Latin column: SERMO I) — consistent within the work. (Nicetas uses "Discourse" for the same word; per-work, not per-corpus.)
  • Εὐλόγησον, Πάτερ — the liturgical formula before the sermon renders "Give the blessing, Father."
  • Mariological epithets — fixed equivalences, one per Greek epithet, never interchanged: πάναγνος = all-pure · πανυπέραγνος = all-surpassingly pure · Πανάχραντος = All-immaculate · Πάντιμος = All-honored · πολυύμνητος = much-hymned · Θεομήτωρ = Mother of God · Δέσποινα = Lady · ἀειπάρθενος = ever-virgin · ἡ μακαρία = the blessed one. Glabas builds arguments on the differences between epithets; collapsing two into one English word is the homily analogue of a lemma-conformation.
  • Maker-vocabulary distinctions, fixed within the work: Κτίστης = Creator · Δημιουργός = Maker · πλάστης = Fashioner · πλάσμα = moulded work · δημιούργημα = created work.
  • Rhetorical pile-ups are structure, not padding: doublets/triplets (γυμνὸς, ἐλεεινὸς, αἰχμάλωτος) render 1:1, never compressed; exclamations kept (οἴμοι! = alas!); rhetorical questions stay questions.
  • Bold coinages are rendered boldly, not normalized: ταφόζωος = tomb-bound (existence) · βρεφουργηθέντες = wrought as babes · ἔξαλμα = outleaping · προέτεκε = bore before. Allatius routinely flattens these (sepulcralem vitam, in quadam infantia); his paraphrase is NOT a license to flatten the Greek.

8. Construal cruces — printed reading kept, conjecture here

  • 0001 — καὶ τὸ πρῶτον ἀναιρεῖται ἀξίωμα. ἀναιρέω in the active means destroy; the middle here means takes up for himself, and the Latin confirms it (pristinam resumit dignitatem). Rendered "takes up again his first dignity." Flagged because the naive rendering reverses the sentence — man would be stripped of his dignity in the very clause that says he recovers it. This is a Pattern 7a mechanism-5 trap (agency/voice), and it leaves no lexical trace.
  • 0001 — the unclosed guillemet. Calfa prints καὶ ὁ « καινὸς ἄνθρωπος, … with no closing ». Pattern 8: never supply, never delete. The « is carried into the English at the same point and left unclosed. The plate's own OCR shows no guillemet, so this may be Calfa's artifact rather than Migne's mark; it is not corrected either way, and a leaf render would settle it.
  • 0001 — ἵν᾽ ὅτον ἡ Παρθένος Θεῷ προσωμοίωται. Calfa's ὅτον is not a word; the plate OCR gives ὅτου. Read as ὅσον ("how far") with the Latin (quam similis Deo esset Virgo). Rendered "how far"; the letter is unresolved.
  • 0001 — καὶ τῷ προπάτορι ταύτης ἐδέησεν, ὅσαπερ οἱονεὶ μητρὶ χρήσασθαι. ὅσαπερ is what both Calfa and the plate print, and the clause will not construe smoothly with it (one expects ὥσπερ, or ταύτῃ ὥσπερ). Rendered as closely as the printed words allow — "had need — so far as to use her, as it were, as a mother" — rather than smoothed to just as. Two-witness concurrence: the difficulty is the plate's, not ours.
  • 0001 — τῆς ἔσταξαν λέξεως, a word mentioned as a word. The Nicetas rule keeps a mentioned particle in Greek script; that rule was written for disputed credal particles. Here the word discussed is one the English has just translated, and keeping ἔσταξαν in Greek would make the sentence opaque. Rendered as italic English — "the word dropped down" — matching the quotation four words earlier. Decision recorded so it is not churned; if the convention is later tightened, this is the site.
  • 0002 — τῶν μὲν ἄλλων ἕκαστος … προσῆγον. Singular subject, plural verb. Pattern 9: no English exponent, no emendation to make or undo. Logged so a later sweep does not churn it.
  • 0002 — ὁ δὲ … Ῥουβὶμ … οὔτε λαμβάνει τὰ δῶρα, καὶ ὀνείδεσι βάλλει. The correlative is broken on the plate (οὔτε … καί for οὔτε … οὔτε or οὐ … ἀλλά); the Latin has non acciperet, sed praetereaacerbis eos conviciis impeteret, i.e. it repairs the correlation. Rendered with the break kept — "neither receives the gifts, and pelts …" — under Pattern 7. Plate-verified: the Paris scan's Greek reads οὔτε λαμβάνει τὰ δῶρα, καὶ ὀνεί-. Not a [lat:]: Allatius is smoothing syntax, not asserting a different fact.
  • 0002 — Ῥουβίμ. Kept as printed (transliterated Rubim, not normalized to Ruben). Migne's own note (18) observes that Isidore appears to have taken Rubim seu Ruben for the priest's proper name — the plate has already adjudicated it, so no marker of ours.
  • 0003 — ὁ πάντων τῶν τῆς ἐκείνου μανίας … ἐπέκεινα Θεοῦ χωρισθεὶς … ἀσώματος. The word order permits binding ἐπέκεινα either to χωρισθείς or to the genitive plural; the Latin binds it to the devil's pre-eminence (cunctis insanis ipsius sociis … supereminens). Rendered with the Latin's binding; the choice is recorded rather than silently made, per the "never let the Latin settle a reading without saying so" rule.
  • 0003 — ἔμελλεν/ἐμέλησε and Χαῖρε/χαίρει. Two untranslatable figures; the second is preserved by rendering χαῖρε as Rejoice (§2).
