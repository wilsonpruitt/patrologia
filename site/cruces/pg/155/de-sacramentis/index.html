Genuine cruces

  • 0201 τῶν λυομένων πατέρων. "dead fathers who are being dissolved" — λύεσθαι of dying (the body's dissolution). Latin parentes solutos concurs. Rendered literally.
  • 0201 ἐνίσχυε τὸ ἔργον πρὸς τοῦτο, ὡς καὶ πρὸς τὸ διδάσκειν. The object is "the work," not "them": "that he strengthened the work toward this, as also toward teaching." Rendered as printed; the sense (he equipped the apostles for the monastic life as for preaching) is the reader's. Latin OCR-broken here and settles nothing.
  • 0204 καίτοι γε ταύτην τὴν ἄφεσιν … χαριζομένην ἀεί. An accusative participle with no governing verb (an implied "we see/know"). Rendered as a finite clause, "although indeed this remission of sins is granted always…"; no word added that changes the claim. Latin hanc certe remissionem … concessam has the same shape.
  • 0204 ἐνταφιασμὸν ἦν αὐτοῦ. ἦν is slightly blurred on the plate (Calfa ἐνταφιασλν ύ); it is the only reading that construes ("and that myron was for his burial"). The whole Mary/Simon sentence is anacoluthic (οὐχ ἡ … Μαρία … ὡς καὶ ἡ ἐν τῇ οἰκίᾳ … ἀλλ᾽ ὅπερ ἡ … πόρνη); the English keeps its run, setting the parenthetical clauses off with dashes (anchor precedent).
  • 0205 Βλέπεις, ὅτι τοῦτο δηλοῖ τὸ ἅγιον ἔλαιον; ὅτι read as ὅ τι, "Seest thou what this holy oil signifies?" (Latin Videsne quid hoc sacrum … oleum?). Not an emendation — the plate's spacing of ὅτι/ὅ τι is unreliable in this fount (cf. Ὅ τι δέ at col 201).
  • 0208 ὑμετέραν (plural) after ἀδελφέ (singular) inside one speech. Pattern 17 carried as printed: "brother … on account of your love … your request." The Cleric's δεόμεθά σου is singular ("we beseech thee").
  • 0209 καὶ ἔτι οὐκ ἀπαθὲς ἐξ ἡδονῆς τίκτεσθαι ἡμᾶς. An infinitive clause with no verb of its own; taken as continuing ἐκδιδάσκει: "and further, that our being born from pleasure is not passionless." Calfa had καὶ παι οὐκ; the plate's ἔτι is clear. The οὐκ is carried. Latin quoniam nequaquam absque peccato ex voluptate nascitur (causal) — looseness, not a [lat:].
  • 0209 τὸ Σωτήριον Ἰησοῦς ἐπικληθείς. Neuter adjective (capitalized on the plate) with no noun: rendered "called by the saving name, Jesus" — "name" supplied from τὸ ὄνομα in the same sentence. Latin nomine salutifero concurs.
  • 0209 τῆς νομικῆς δουλείας ἐρρύσατο has no object; "us" supplied from ἀντὶ ἡμῶν just before ("has rescued us from the bondage of the law"). Latin nos eruit.
  • 0212 πλὴν μετὰ προσοχῆς, καὶ ἱερέως εὐλαβοῦς τε καὶ νήφοντος — see [lat:] below; the Greek is clean on the plate and is what the English says.
  • 0213 μήποτε καὶ ληφθείη οὕτω παρὰ Θεοῦ. The sense of the close of ch. 62 is obscure: "it is good to receive and sanctify the infant, lest it should also be taken thus by God." μήποτε may be the later-Greek "perhaps" ("in case it may thus be accepted by God"), which suits the argument better; but "lest" is its first sense and the Latin reads it so (ne sic a Deo deprehendatur). Rendered "lest" — the English does not choose the friendlier sense. The sentence is also anacoluthic (ὁ τὰ τῶν ἀσεβῶν δεχόμενος βρέφη … καλὸν δέχεσθαι), carried.
  • 0213 μετὰ τὴν κοινωνίαν Χριστοῦ βαπτίζειν τε καὶ ἐμπνέειν. Latin post communicationem Christi baptizare et insufflare concurs. Rendered as printed.
