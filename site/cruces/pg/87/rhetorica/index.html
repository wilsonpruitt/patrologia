Procopius of Gaza — read the work →

These are the edition's working notes, published as they were written. They record, work by work, where Migne's plate is defective or the reading uncertain: the printed form, what the English does with it, and why. They are not a groomed apparatus. Where a note argues with itself, withdraws an earlier decision, or leaves a question open, that is the record of how the reading was actually reached, and it is left standing — a conjecture that is visible can be checked, and one that has been tidied away cannot.

The English never silently repairs the page. A construable but wrong reading is rendered literally and the conjecture is recorded here, never applied to the text. How the English is produced and checked →

Chunk 0001 (pr-001)

  • 2796A · …for a requital to contend with thy deeds; — T. ἀμοιβῆς ἀπορεῖ τοῖς ἔργοις ἁμιλλωμένης: the city lacks a requital that could compete with the emperor's deeds. Villoison: quaerit quomodo beneficia repenset gratia referenda.
  • 2796A · …having received through his image the benefactor himself, — P. The occasion is the arrival at Gaza of the emperor's image (εἰκών), set up and greeted by the city.
  • 2796A · …of the others. For Epidamnus the historians sing, — S. Thucydides I.24 opens his account of the Corcyraean affair with Epidamnus, "a city on the right as one sails into the Ionian gulf"; Epidamnus (Dyrrachium, Durrës) was Anastasius's birthplace.
  • 2797A · …is troublesome in both, enjoying either one purely. — T. τῷ μὲν θατέρου ψιλῶς ἀπολαύειν: a μέν with no answering δέ, and θατέρου ("of the other") hangs; the sense wanted is "enjoying the good of each unmixed." Villoison smooths it: quae bona, iis plane frui. Rendered as printed.
  • 2797A · …kindness to strangers, the city is from them. — P. Epidamnus was a colony of Corcyra (identified with Homer's Phaeacia) with a Corinthian founder; Corinth's kings traced themselves to Heracles. The orator stacks every pedigree the city can claim.
  • 2797A · …piety; this thou didst add to thy longing, — T. ταῦτα προσετίθεις τῷ πόθῳ, "these thou didst add to thy longing"; Villoison has Hoc tibi proponebas desiderio, as if προὐτίθεις, "set before". Rendered as printed.
  • 2797A · …thee, and raising thee toward a priest's habit; — P. "The city of the palace" (βασιλείων ἡ πόλις) is Constantinople. The orator has the capital acclaim Anastasius for priestly office before his accession; he is said to have been put forward for the see of Antioch.
  • 2800 · …it confirmed by its vote. Not so Eumolpus, — P. Eumolpus, the Thracian son of Poseidon who fought Athens for Eleusis; his house, the Eumolpidae, held the Eleusinian priesthood.
  • 2801 · …been granted thy soul for a common dwelling; — T. εὐτυχοῦσαι with an accusative, literally "fortunate in thy soul as a common dwelling"; Villoison nactae, "having found". Rendered by sense.
  • 2801 · …decide. Therefore, passing thy days in the palace, — P. Before his accession in 491 Anastasius served at court as a silentiary.
  • 2804 · …great senate added its assent; the empress nodded; — P. The empress (βασιλίς) is Ariadne, Zeno's widow, who chose Anastasius and married him in 491; "the great senate" is the senate of Constantinople.
  • 2804 · …the gift, they set accusers upon the divinity, — T. τῇ δαίμονι, feminine: fortune as a goddess; Villoison deam.
  • 2804 · …empire, certain neighbouring barbarians were throwing into turmoil, — P. The Arabs raiding the eastern provinces; Villoison's argument: Vincit Arabes Orientis provincias infestantes.

Chunk 0002 (pr-002)

  • 2805B · …and, as Homer would say, "servants of Ares"; — S. θεράποντες Ἄρηος, Homer's formula for the Achaean warriors (Iliad II.110 and often).
  • 2805B · …for us close at hand is he himself, — T. αὐτός has no antecedent in the Greek; Villoison supplies ipse exercitus. Probably the commander present at the oration; rendered "he himself".
  • 2808 · …hard to fight, the nation of the Solians, — T. Σολῶν ἔθνος, "the nation of the Solians" (as if of Soli, the Cilician city); Villoison's Latin reads Selorum. The people meant are the Isaurians, whose revolt Anastasius put down in the 490s.
  • 2808 · …from men to whom they did not belong, — T. Βασιλέων δὲ ἦν αὐτοῖς προσταγμάτων, ἐκ μὴ προσηκόντων does not construe as printed, and Villoison's Latin marks a lacuna here (....). Rendered as printed; the sense seems to be that the rebels issued imperial commands without right.
  • 2808 · …generalship and a varied benefaction in the contests, — T. ποικίλῃ τῶν ἀγώνων εὐεργεσίᾳ, "a varied benefaction in the contests", hardly construes; perhaps ἐνεργείᾳ, "varied conduct of the fighting". Villoison paraphrases: imperatoriis artibus.
  • 2809A · …none can tell whether they had ever been; — S. Demosthenes, Third Philippic 26: Philip destroyed thirty-two cities in Thrace so cruelly that a visitor could not tell whether they had ever been inhabited.
  • 2809A · …they dealt with the Melians and the Scionaeans. — S. Thucydides V.116 (Melos) and V.32 (Scione): the Athenians killed the men and enslaved the women and children.
  • 2809A · …seen to be humane, perhaps against his will. — S. Alexander and Porus: Plutarch, Alexander 60; Arrian, Anabasis V.19.
  • 2812 · …one man's judgment the cities were in misery. — P. Lysander set up the decarchies, governments of ten partisans, in the cities after 404 BC.
  • 2812 · …gold and silver come together into one appellation. — P. The chrysargyron, the tax in gold and silver on trades, levied every four years (ἐκ περιόδου); abolished by Anastasius in 498.
  • 2813A · …document concerning it became the work of fire, — P. Evagrius, Church History III.39, tells how Anastasius had the tax records burned.
  • 2813A · …lest it rise again, imitating thine own Heracles. — P. Heracles burned the stumps of the Hydra's necks so that the heads should not grow again; "thine own" because Epidamnus claims him as ancestor (§ 2).
  • 2813A · …naming Aristides would add without fail "the Just". — S. Aristides assessed the first tribute of the Delian League (Thucydides I.96; Plutarch, Aristides 24).

Chunk 0003 (pr-003)

  • 2816 · …from that time the place was named Tax-free. — S. The tax-free farm on Hymettus: [Aristotle], Constitution of the Athenians 16.6, where the tax is likewise a tenth.
  • 2816 · …of a tomb, the bellies of the beasts. — P. The beast-fights of the amphitheatre, which Anastasius abolished.
  • 2816 · …divided by hatred, and thrust against one another. — P. The pantomime dancers, whose rival partisans rioted in the cities; Anastasius banished the pantomimes.
  • 2817A · …common native land of the race of men. — P. Hierapolis (Mabbug) in Syria, the "Holy City" of the Syrian Goddess; hence "from things sacred" just before.
  • 2817A · …the ground that did not rise to them, — T. εἰς ταυτὸν ἀγαγὼν τὰ μὴ διανεστηκότα τοῖς μετεώροις: taken of the aqueduct carrying low ground up to the level of the heights. Villoison reads it of springs gathered from the high ground (collectis fontibus, qui in superioribus locis scaturirent).
  • 2817A · …the open sea, suffered shipwreck in the harbour. — P. Caesarea Maritima in Palestine, Herod's city named for Augustus; its great harbour had silted and sunk by late antiquity.
  • 2820 · …pleasure and safety together upon those who arrive. — P. The Pharos of Alexandria.
  • 2820 · …lavished in rivalry upon the provision of food? — T. φιλοτιμουμένας has no feminine plural to agree with; taken with the abundance of money (Villoison liberaliter insumptam).
  • 2820 · …through the whole of a part of Thrace? — T. δι᾿ ὅλης ἐν μέρει τῆς Θρᾴκης, literally "through the whole, in part, of Thrace"; Villoison per totam fere Thraciam. P. The Long Wall, from the Euxine to the Propontis some forty miles west of Constantinople.
  • 2820 · …opposite directions to each other upon the wall. — S. Thucydides I.93.5: on Themistocles' wall of the Piraeus two wagons, meeting, brought up the stones.
  • 2820 · …far short of the worth of the works. — S. Iliad I.1 (the wrath) and XVI.112–124 (the ship of Protesilaus fired by Hector). Villoison navi una incensa confirms the reading ἐμπιπραμένης.
  • 2820 · …arrogance, when he was convicted by Persian luxury. — S. Thucydides I.130.
  • 2820 · …time has at last shown us in deeds. — S. Hesiod, Works and Days 109–120.
  • 2821A · …whom fortune gave thee together with the empire. — P. Ariadne (see § 5).
  • 2821C · …his child's child, at once child and master. — S. Herodotus III.89 (the Persians call Cyrus "father"); I.107–130 (Astyages, Cyrus's maternal grandfather, deposed and kept by him).
  • 2824 · …be ruled first, and so receiving the rule. — S. Plutarch, Agesilaus 1 (reared in the Spartan training as one not born to rule), 25–26 (his defence of Sphodrias), 31 (Sparta first sees an enemy); Leuctra, 371 BC.
  • 2824 · …son of Zeus, and that Philip was deceived. — S. Plutarch, Alexander 2–3 and 27–28, on his claimed sonship of Zeus.
  • 2824 · …names; and women bear children like their parents. — S. The arms turned to ploughs echo Isaiah 2:4 (Micah 4:3); the last clause is Hesiod, Works and Days 235, verbatim.

Chunk 0004 (pr-004)

  • 2828A · …in Byzantium, brought about the riot called Nika; — P. The Nika riot (January 532), the rising of the circus factions in Constantinople, named from their watchword Nika, "Conquer!", in which the earlier church of Sophia was burnt.
  • 2828A · …with precision, in my books on the wars. — S. Procopius, Wars I.24. The cross-reference is the historian's own, and is one sign that this is Procopius of Caesarea (Buildings I.1), not the Gazan.
  • 2828A · …having made the name most fitting for God). — T. ἐπὶ τὰ καιριώτατα τῷ Θεῷ τὴν ἐπωνυμίαν ἀπεργασάμενοι: Sophia, "Wisdom," as the name most befitting God. Combefis: a potissima velut dote supremo Numini indito nomine.
  • 2833A · …but, hung from heaven by the golden chain, — S. The golden chain let down from heaven, Homer, Iliad VIII.19.
  • 2833A · …by the golden chain, to cover the place. — T. ἐξημμένον (neuter) where the subject is the dome (θόλος, feminine); the received text of Buildings has ἐξημμένη. Rendered with the dome as subject; logged as print.
  • 2836 · …had come upon a meadow in its bloom. — T. ἐντετυχ[ηκ]έναι: the brackets appear to be an editor's supplement on the plate; read ἐντετυχηκέναι.
  • 2836 · …God that this work has been turned out; — T. Ὁπηνίκα δέ τις εὐθὺς, ὡς …: the clause has no main verb. Combefis's Latin supplies precaturus illuc ingressus sit, statim intelligit, the reading of Buildings I.1.61 (εὐξόμενος ἐς αὐτὸ ἴοι, ξυνίησι). The bracketed words follow the Latin; the Greek line on the plate is full length, so the omission seems to be Migne's text, not the OCR.
  • 2836 · …built in this way by the emperor Justinian. — T. Τὰ μὲν οὖν Κωνσταντινουπόλεως ἥνπερ Μεγάλην καλεῖν νενομίκασι: a feminine relative and αὐτήν in the next sentence want a noun that is not printed. Combefis has Constantinopolitanam ecclesiam, quae vulgo Magna dicitur; "church" is supplied from the Latin.
  • 2836 · …mentioned just now (the master-builders call them loroi), — T. λῶροι (the plate's Ἰώρους is an OCR slip for λώρους; Combefis loros): the builders' word, literally "strips, thongs," here applied to the arches themselves and below to the arches' bands upon the piers. Transliterated throughout.

Chunk 0005 (pr-005)

  • 2840A · …slaughtered her own victims and mixed her bowl. — S. Proverbs 9:1–2 (LXX): "Wisdom built herself a house, and set up seven pillars; she slaughtered her victims, mixed her wine in a bowl." The church's name Sophia is read as Wisdom's own.
  • 2840A · …of the evils! O the Iliad of woes! — P. "An Iliad of woes" (Ἰλιὰς κακῶν), the proverb for an endless train of evils.
  • 2840A · …looks upon the earth and makes it tremble — S. Psalm 103:32 (LXX; 104:32): "who looks upon the earth and makes it tremble."
  • 2840A · …word is to thee, O church of God; — S. πρὸς σὲ … ὁ λόγος echoes 4 Kingdoms (2 Kings) 9:5, "I have a word for thee."
  • 2840A · …but set His face like a solid rock, — S. Isaiah 50:7: "I set my face like a solid rock."
  • 2840A · …took upon Him the things of the body. — T. ἵν᾿ ἰδῇ καὶ αὐτὸ εἰ καὶ νῦν ὡς ἔοικεν ἐπιλέλησται: the construction is loose; rendered word for word. Combefis takes it as a conditional, istud si adhuc, ut videtur, oblitus est, and runs it into the following clause.
  • 2840A · …of Sion, the precious, and weighed against gold; — S. Lamentations 4:2: "the precious sons of Sion, who were weighed against gold." The printed ἀντιθέμενοι χρυσίῳ is not the LXX wording (ἐπηρμένοι ἐν χρυσίῳ); rendered as printed (Combefis auro comparati).
  • 2840A · …carry the exaltations of God in your throat, — S. "Delicate" (τρυφεροί) is given to Jeremiah; cf. Lamentations 4:5. "The exaltations of God in their throat" is Psalm 149:6.
  • 2840A · …How has our joy been turned into mourning? — S. Lamentations 5:15: "our dance is turned into mourning." Combefis's facies in squalorem does not answer the Greek.
  • 2841A · …offer first-fruits before thee and to find mercy. — S. Daniel 3:38 (LXX, the Prayer of Azariah), quoted almost word for word; the "sacred hymn" is the canticle of the three youths.
  • 2841B · …darkness, that same darkness, now a second time, — P. The second darkness: the first was the darkness over the earth at the crucifixion (Matthew 27:45).
  • 2841B · …and a bridegroom coming out of his chamber. — S. Psalm 18:6 (LXX; 19:5): "as a bridegroom coming out of his chamber, he will rejoice as a giant to run his course."
  • 2841B · …God; God, then, knowing both this and that. — T. Θεοκλυτήσω, "call upon God"; Combefis's Latin has instead si divina eloquia audio.
