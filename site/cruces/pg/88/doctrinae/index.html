Expositiones et Doctrinae diversae

Dorotheus of Gaza (Dorotheus Archimandrita) — read the work →

These are the edition's working notes, published as they were written. They record, work by work, where Migne's plate is defective or the reading uncertain: the printed form, what the English does with it, and why. They are not a groomed apparatus. Where a note argues with itself, withdraws an earlier decision, or leaves a question open, that is the record of how the reading was actually reached, and it is left standing — a conjecture that is visible can be checked, and one that has been tidied away cannot.

The English never silently repairs the page. A construable but wrong reading is rendered literally and the conjecture is recorded here, never applied to the text. How the English is produced and checked →

Chunk 0003 (dd-002)

  • 1628 · …virtues, and so returns to his own rest. — T. The sentence opens Οὖν διὰ τῆς συντριβῆς, which cannot stand: a word has gone before οὖν (Ἐὰν? Ὅταν?). Migne's Latin reads Igitur si per dolorem cordis. The English keeps the sense and marks the gap.
  • 1628 · …"from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit," — S. 2 Cor 7:1.
  • 1629A · …shalt not bear false witness against thy neighbour," — S. Luke 18:18–20 (cf. Matt 19:16–18).
  • 1629A · …what thou hast and give to the poor," — S. Matt 19:20–21.
  • 1632 · …let that little tool do in us , — T. The Greek has only ἀφοῦμεν τὸ μικρὸν ἐργαλεῖον ἐκείνῳ ποιῆσαι ἐν ἡμῖν, "we let that little tool do in us," with no object. Migne's Latin has ut illud instrumentum fiat nobis loco centenarii, "that the tool take for us the place of a hundredweight of gold," i.e. the attachment once given to great wealth. That phrase is probably missing from the plate or the OCR.
  • 1632B · …the symbols of our habit signify to us. — T. The text reads τὰ σύμβολα τοῦ σώματος ἡμῶν, "of our body." The foot of the page leaked into the OCR as p0846.55 «σχήματος.», evidently Migne's note giving σχήματος, "of our habit," which the Latin (habitus nostri) and the context support. The English follows the note.

Chunk 0004 (dd-003)

  • 1633 · …it says, "Let your loins be girded about." — S. Luke 12:35.
  • 1633 · …faith, as it says again in the Gerontikon, — S. The symbolism of belt, analavos and cowl that Dorotheus credits to the Gerontikon goes back to the prologue of Evagrius' Praktikos.
  • 1636 · …cut gains a long stretch of that road, — T. κοπενδάριον (twice): Latin compendiarium, a short cut, spelled so as to echo κόπτειν, "to cut off," whether as a pun or as a real Byzantine form. Not reported as a misread. Migne's Latin reads scipione, "a staff," which misses the sense.
  • 1637 · …him among all those saints, enjoying their blessedness. — S. The Life of Dositheus, which precedes the Instructions in the tradition.
  • 1637B · …a prostration from there, and takes his leave, — T. λαμβάνει παράθεσιν, "takes his commendation," i.e. his leave. The Latin has a nobis impetrata venia. The phrase is uncertain but the OCR gives no sign of a misread.
  • 1640 · …such a one is worthy to be saved. — T. Printed Τὶς ἄξιος … τὶς ἄξιος, with grave accents on what cannot be the interrogative here. Migne's Latin has hic … hic … dignus est, and the English follows that. Possibly ὁ τοιοῦτος or οὗτος is meant; not settled.
  • 1640 · …we must enter into the kingdom of heaven, — S. Acts 14:22.

Chunk 0009 (dd-008)

  • 1669 · …10. — T. Printed Γʹ (3); read Ιʹ (10): it follows Θʹ (9) and precedes ΙΑʹ (11), and the Latin has X.
  • 1672A · …simplicity, God knows from what, spent a year — T. χρόνον, in later Greek "a year"; the Latin (observato noctis tempore) takes it loosely.
  • 1672B · …as with the melon, if a little splinter — T. βιζάκιον, a rare diminutive; "splinter" is a guess from the sense (Latin vasculum).
  • 1672C · …to be, small or great, let him ⟨not⟩ — T. The Greek has no negative, and the Latin follows it (ita eam permittat et negligat), but what follows ("for indifference is a bad thing. Nor again…") requires one; ⟨not⟩ is supplied.
  • 1672D · …of it, is three and a half eighths. — T. τὸ ἥμισυ τέταρτον τοῦ ὀγδόου, literally "the half-fourth of the eighth". In the Greek idiom a "fourth half" is three and a half (as τέταρτον ἡμιτάλαντον, 3½ talents), so it is three and a half eighths against the work's one. Latin quartum dimidium octavi. Rendered so throughout.
  • 1673A · …one does not do his service ⟨with⟩ knowledge. — T. οὐ ποιεῖ γνῶσιν τὴν ἰδίαν διακονίαν does not construe; ⟨with⟩ (κατὰ or μετὰ) is supplied, with the Latin in cognitione vera non administrare.
  • 1673B · …you be indifferent and throw things away straightaway, — T. Printed ἀφρῇ; read ἀφαρεί "straightaway" (spelled ἀφαρῆ at col. 1656), Latin statim.
  • 1673C · …is stronger than humility. But if one sees — T. Printed ἤδη; read ἴδῃ "sees", Latin si quis viderit, as at col. 1656.
  • 1673C · …you wish, but as it meets the necessity, — T. The sentence is troubled. ἀπαντῶ ("I meet") is read as ἀπαντᾷ "it meets", and the comparative ⟨than⟩ (ἤ) is supplied before "to trouble"; the Latin (melius… quam turbari vos) has the comparison.
  • 1673D · …that from the neighbour come life and death. — S. Gerontikon, Antony 9.
  • 1676C · …evil as to suggest to him straight off — T. Printed οὕτως ἀφαιρῇ τις, which does not construe; conjectured οὕτως ἀφελῶς τι "so crudely, any one", with the Latin statim… quidpiam malorum.
  • 1676D · …wicked one does evil when he mingles self-justification." — S. Prov 11:15 LXX, where the received text has δικαίῳ "with a righteous man"; Dorotheus reads δικαίωμα.
  • 1676D · …one is the devil; and he does evil — T. Printed κακοποιῆσαι (infinitive); read κακοποιεῖ.
  • 1677A · …is a bronze wall between man and God. — S. Gerontikon, Poemen.
  • 1677B · …will, the man is not easily brought round." — T. Printed εὐσυστροφῇ, not a form I can construe; the sense is taken from the Latin non bene convertitur.
  • 1677C · …he mingles self-justification." "And he hates the sound — T. Printed εἶχον "I had"; read ἦχον "sound", as in Prov 11:15 LXX and the Latin sonum.
  • 1677C · …is already being found out through the asking — T. Printed διὰ αὐτοῦ; read διὰ τοῦ (articular infinitive), Latin ex eo quod… interrogant.
  • 1677D · …side, and in him is fulfilled, "Salvation is — T. Printed Ὑπάρχεται; read ὑπάρχει, as the verse is quoted elsewhere in this Instruction.

Chunk 0012 (dd-011)
