Ad Theopemptum scholasticum

Maximus the Confessor — read the work →

These are the edition's working notes, published as they were written. They record, work by work, where Migne's plate is defective or the reading uncertain: the printed form, what the English does with it, and why. They are not a groomed apparatus. Where a note argues with itself, withdraws an earlier decision, or leaves a question open, that is the record of how the reading was actually reached, and it is left standing — a conjecture that is visible can be checked, and one that has been tidied away cannot.

The English never silently repairs the page. A construable but wrong reading is rendered literally and the conjecture is recorded here, never applied to the text. How the English is produced and checked →

Chunk 0001 (th-001)

  • 1393 · …Of Maximus the monk. TO THEOPEMPTUS THE SCHOLASTICUS — P. Theopemptus: a scholasticus (a lawyer), otherwise unknown; the addressee throughout, «God-guarded master».
  • 1393 · …I have not yet ascended to the Father».* — S. Luke 18:6; Matt 5:39; John 20:17.
  • 1393 · …revealer of the word is what is accomplished; — T. The opening plays on Λόγος, the Word worshipped, and λόγος, the word that a life makes manifest; the facing Latin doubles it, Verbum ac Sermo.
  • 1393 · …whom also the deification that comes after grace — T. μετὰ χάριν (with the accusative, "after grace"); the facing Latin has quae gratiae comes est, as if μετὰ χάριτος. The printed form is kept.
  • 1393 · …thou hast incorporeally stirred the sight of thyself; — T. τὸν ὑμέτερον here, and ὑμετέρας, ἐκελεύσατε below, are plurals of respect to the one addressee; by the number rule they stand as you/your, while the singulars stand as thou/thee.
  • 1393 · …stirred the sight of thyself; having shown thyself — T. Read αὑτὸν (reflexive, "thyself") for the printed αὐτὸν, with the facing Latin te; the construal of the comparison that follows («than as much as others …») is uncertain. Migne marks the place with an asterisk after προτερήματα and the note «Locus obscurus, ac forte mendosus» ("an obscure passage, and perhaps corrupt").
  • 1396 · …and not of assertion. The judge of unrighteousness — S. Luke 18:6 (ὁ κριτὴς τῆς ἀδικίας).
  • 1396 · …is, by way of parable, and is called, — T. The three answers are set as accusative and infinitive, depending on «I say those things» above; rendered as plain statements.
  • 1396 · …dwelling; neither fearing God, nor yet regarding man, — S. cf. Luke 18:2, 4.
  • 1396 · …back; with which it wilfully pierced itself through, — S. cf. 1 Tim 6:10.
  • 1396 · …not lawful. And the widow who troubles him — S. cf. Luke 18:3, 5.
  • 1396 · …and her self-chosen leaning toward the things here. — T. λόγος here "reason" (Latin ratio). Migne prints a full stop before Μόνῃ, but the participles that follow (κρατούμενον, προσμένοντα) agree with λόγον; the clause is joined to its sentence.
  • 1396 · …leaning toward the things here. And her adversary — S. cf. Luke 18:3.
  • 1396 · …and way of thinking of the wretched flesh, — S. cf. Rom 8:6–7 (τὸ φρόνημα τῆς σαρκός).
  • 1396 · …into us the shames and passions of dishonour; — S. cf. Rom 1:26 (πάθη ἀτιμίας).
  • 1396 · …the life that truly is. And the bruisings — S. cf. Luke 18:5 (ἵνα μὴ … ὑπωπιάζῃ με).
  • 1397 · …voluntary mortification of the members of the flesh. — S. cf. Col 3:5.
  • 1397 · …lead the soul to near-kinship with reason itself, — T. αὐτοῦ τοῦ λόγου: "reason itself", as in the widow's «reason that sows», with the facing Latin rationis ipsius; the Word cannot be excluded. ἀγχιστεία "near-kinship", kept apart from οἰκείωσις "kinship".
  • 1397 · …shall God not make the vindication of you*, — S. Luke 18:7, adapted: the Gospel has «of his elect» (τῶν ἐκλεκτῶν αὐτοῦ) where Maximus has ὑμῶν, «of you».
  • 1397 · …through another, and wholly another, besides the Son. — T. The facing Latin ends the clause «quam Filii, aut per Filium», with an alternative («or through the Son») the Greek does not print; a phrase may have dropped. Migne prints no such phrase (checked on the plate: the line ends «πλὴν τοῦ Υἱοῦ.»).
  • 1397 · …timidity. But to rule the reading of ὑποπιάζειν — T. The scaffold's ὑποπί-ζειν is read ὑποπιάζειν; Luke 18:5 has ὑπωπιάζῃ, and the facing Latin prints ὑπωπιάζειν. The question is whether the verb is accented as a barytone or circumflexed as a contract.
  • 1397 · …The right cheek: I call it right action, — S. cf. Matt 5:39.
  • 1400 · …none, or perhaps do not possess the equal; — T. Migne prints ἃ μὴ, a misprint for ἢ μὴ («or … not»; checked on the plate; the facing Latin aut).
  • 1400 · …the command. And the saying Touch me not — S. John 20:17.
  • 1400 · …when she supposed him to be the gardener; — S. cf. John 20:15.
  • 1400 · …yet surmounted the tabernacle grown together with it, — S. cf. 2 Cor 5:1, 4 (σκῆνος, the body).
  • 1400 · …divine things as to a kind of Galilee; — S. cf. Matt 28:7, 10; Mark 16:7.
  • 1400 · …shown to have become this, save sin alone, — S. cf. Heb 4:15.
  • 1400 · …nature alone, when we should attain the gift; — T. ὡς καὶ ἡμεῖς ἐκείνῳ: the dative is kept («to him»), as the facing Latin cum illo; ἐκεῖνο, «that», would square with «become that which he was». In τῆς δωρεᾶς τῆς ἐπιτύχοιμεν the second τῆς does not construe (perhaps ταύτης, a conjecture); Migne prints τῆς (checked on the plate); left out of the English.
