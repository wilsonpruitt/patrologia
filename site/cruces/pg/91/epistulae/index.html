Maximus the Confessor — read the work →

These are the edition's working notes, published as they were written. They record, work by work, where Migne's plate is defective or the reading uncertain: the printed form, what the English does with it, and why. They are not a groomed apparatus. Where a note argues with itself, withdraws an earlier decision, or leaves a question open, that is the record of how the reading was actually reached, and it is left standing — a conjecture that is visible can be checked, and one that has been tidied away cannot.

The English never silently repairs the page. A construable but wrong reading is rendered literally and the conjecture is recorded here, never applied to the text. How the English is produced and checked →

Chunk 0001 (me-001)

  • 0364 · …the lord George, the all-praised prefect of Africa. — P. George, prefect (ἔπαρχος, praefectus praetorio) of Africa in the late 630s, the civil governor to whom Maximus, then in the African monasteries, writes Letters 1, 18 (in George's name) and 44–45. Migne's heading (Combefis's) gives the piece both titles: Λόγος παραινετικός, "hortatory discourse", and "in the form of a letter"; the Latin runs Sermo hortatorius epistolari forma*.
  • 0364 · …taught that virtue is a matter of inclination, — T. γνώμη, here and throughout the letters = inclination; γνωμικῶς = by inclination (the scholarship's "gnomic will"). Maximus opposes it to τάξις (rank) and, two lines on, pairs it with διάθεσις (disposition): the three are kept apart in the English as in the Greek. See the glossary.
  • 0365 · …has made the reflections of the divine idioms — T. τῶν θείων ἰδιωμάτων … τὰς ἐμφάσεις: the "idioms" are the characteristic marks proper to God (the glossary's ἰδίωμα, distinct from ἰδιότης = property), their "reflections" (ἐμφάσεις) the appearances of them in a life.
  • 0365 · …to be, be able to adulterate this philosophy, — T. φιλοσοφία: the ascetic life itself, as in the monastic writers; not an argument but a way of living, which is why it can "nurture" a man.
  • 0368 · …soul, and power, and our neighbour as ourselves; — S. Matt 22:37–39 (cf. Mark 12:30–31; Dt 6:5), with "ourselves" (ἑαυτούς) for "thyself".
  • 0368 · …from our own willing? And perhaps the word, — T. ὁ λόγος: the divine word, i.e. Scripture (the Latin has Scriptura); lower case, since the glossary reserves "the Word" for the Son, as just below ("receiving the Word as He mounts").
  • 0368 · …nature, to the things not in our power, — T. τὰ ἐφ᾿ ἡμῖν / τὰ οὐκ ἐφ᾿ ἡμῖν = "the things in our power / not in our power", the Stoic pair; scholia 4–5 gloss it as τὸ αὐτεξούσιον, self-determination. προαίρεσις below = choice.
  • 0369 · …in good order, the course of the virtues. — S. cf. Zech 9:9; Matt 21:2–7 (the colt that bears the Lord).
  • 0369 · …not commixed — let us neither, when rule — T. ἀρχή: rule as an office held (the Latin magistratus), the prefect's own; so "authority" for ἐξουσία below.
  • 0372 · …to have the chastising fire as his portion. — S. Luke 16:19–31 (Lazarus, Abraham's bosom, the chasm, the fire).
  • 0372 · …down in the crowd, and bind sin twice. — S. Sir 7:6–8, as printed (μὴ οὐκ ἰσχύσῃς for the LXX's ἰσχύσεις; καταβάλῃς ἑαυτόν for σεαυτόν).
  • 0372 · …thy head, and many have marvelled at thee; — S. Sir 11:12–13, turned to the second person.
  • 0372 · …the fragrant spreading of thy virtues has come. — S. Ps 18:5 LXX (cf. Rom 10:18), said of the apostles and here bent to George.
  • 0373 · …their mother's womb didst guide them toward righteousness. — S. Job 31:17–18, turned from Job's first person to George's second; the catalogue that follows is Job 29–31 in the same way. The preposition is cut off at the line end (ἐπ…): read ἐπί, "toward", with the Latin ad justitiam.
  • 0373 · …their shoulders with the fleece of thy lambs. — S. Job 31:19–20.
  • 0373 · …didst not set thy gold upon thy dust — S. Job 31:24; "dust" (χοῦς) is glossed at once by Maximus himself.
  • 0373 · …of enemies, and thy heart say: Well done. — S. Job 31:5 (mockers; the foot hastening to deceit); 31:29.
  • 0373 · …price, nor crush the souls of its masters. — S. Job 31:38–39.
  • 0373 · …unjust gifts thou didst draw back thy hands; — S. cf. Isa 33:15.
  • 0373 · …the hardship that is according to the flesh, — T. τὸ κατὰ σάρκα δυσπαθές / δυσπάθεια = hardship (of the flesh), εὐπάθεια = comfort: the pair runs into the next chunk and is kept one word each.

Chunk 0002 (me-002)

  • 0376 · …the procurer of kinship — T. οἰκείωσις = kinship (with God, and in the third chunk with the devil); the Latin necessitudo. Not in the glossary: chosen here.
  • 0376 · …as Gregory, the divine mouth of Christ, said — S. Gregory Nazianzen, Or. 14, On the love of the poor (so Combefis's margin, Orat. de amore pauperum), on the flesh that "will suffer what is its own"; cf., exact place not located.
  • 0376 · …— apart from identity with Him in essence. — T. θεοὶ κατὰ χάριν … χωρὶς τῆς πρὸς αὐτὸν κατ᾿ οὐσίαν ταυτότητος: deification by grace, with identity in essence (ταυτότης, the glossary's identity) expressly excepted.
  • 0376 · …grass has withered, and the flower has fallen. — S. Isa 40:6–7 (cf. 1 Pet 1:24).
  • 0376 · …extremes with one another at the same point, — T. "the extremes … the middle": scholion 6 below explains, the extremes being coming-to-be and corruption, the middle the thing generated.
  • 0376 · …the moment the soul that does not perish; — T. Two gaps on the plate (p0206.48–49). "the soul that does not perish" is sure (Latin animam, quae non sit interitioni obnoxia); the verb survives only as …σωμεν, and "bind" is the Latin's alligemus; "then" stands for a particle read only as τε…, the Latin itaque.
  • 0377 · …not working the like things with the Sodomites: — S. Gen 19:15–17 (Sodom, the mountain, Lot; συμπαραληφθῶμεν echoes 19:15, 17 LXX). Scholion 7 glosses the point.
  • 0377 · …nature the passing flux as their very hypostasis, — T. ὑπόστασις kept as hypostasis by the corpus rule, though here it is the mere reality of passing things: their whole mode of being is flux. The Latin reads substantiam.
  • 0377 · …it beforehand to God through those in need — S. cf. Prov 19:17 (he who pities the poor lends to God).
  • 0377 · …this age passes away, as the Saint said; — S. 1 Cor 7:31. T. ὁ Ἅγιος, "the Saint", here and in "the God of the Saint" just after; the Latin has Apostolus and apostoli Deus both times.
  • 0377 · …the Saint has said. And let us await — T. Migne prints δεχώμεθα "let us receive" with the bracketed alternative [ἐκδεχώμ(εθα)], "let us await", which the Latin follows (exspectemus); the English takes the bracket.
  • 0377 · …alterations, as one of our wise men said, — S. cf. Basil, Hexaemeron 1.3 (the argument from the corruptibility of the parts to that of the whole).
  • 0380 · …to all this, considering exactly how we walk, — S. Eph 5:15.
  • 0380 · …be a new heaven and a new earth, — S. 2 Pet 3:10; Rev 21:1 (Isa 65:17).
  • 0380 · …by Him; and a shuddering threat of blazing — T. αἰθομένου with Migne's bracketed alternative [καιομένου], both "blazing". S. cf. Ps 96:3, 5 LXX (fire round about; mountains melting like wax).
  • 0380 · …of fire, he says, was drawing before Him. — S. Dan 7:10 (Theodotion).
  • 0380 · …and the outer darkness, and the sleepless worm — S. Matt 8:12 (outer darkness); Mark 9:48 (the worm); Luke 16:26 (the chasm).
  • 0381 · …them. And behold, a man and his work; — S. cf. Isa 62:11 (40:10).
  • 0381 · …fire prepared for the devil and his angels. — S. Matt 25:34, 41.
  • 0381 · …inclination, have pierced themselves through with such evils, — S. cf. 1 Tim 6:10 (ἑαυτοὺς περιέπειραν).

Chunk 0003 (me-003)

  • 0384 · …of deceit. Just is thy judgement, O God; — S. Ps 118:137 LXX; what follows is Prov 1:24–31, turned into the confession of the condemned ("thou didst call us, and we did not obey").
  • 0384 · …all that there is a wage of holiness. — S. Wis 2:21–22.
  • 0384 · …trace to find, nor path of its keel — S. Wis 5:7–11. T. τρόπεως, with Migne's bracketed alternative [τρόπιος], the LXX's form; both are "keel".
  • 0385 · …now, our tongue burning with a fearful thirst. — S. cf. Luke 16:24.
  • 0385 · …then, of what we did, do we receive; — S. Luke 23:41.
  • 0385 · …wages worthy of our own disposition by intent: — T. κατὰ πρόθεσιν = by intent; πρόθεσις is not in the glossary and is kept apart from προαίρεσις (choice) and γνώμη (inclination).
  • 0388 · …a gaping depth, and the gloom of cowering. — T. στυγνότητα πτήξεως, "the gloom of cowering": Combefis's note (c) says this is the reading of the Venetian codex, which he prefers; πτῆξις is the crouching of an animal that dares not come out for fear.
  • 0388 · …ask through prayer for the grace of knowledge. — S. Matt 7:7–8.
  • 0388 · …light, nor to see the life of mortals, — S. Matt 22:13; Job 10:21–22.
  • 0389 · …Love, both being it and being called it — S. 1 John 4:8, 16.
  • 0389 · …with our whole power, while we have time, — S. Gal 6:10.
  • 0389 · …away, and all the things in it wither. — S. 1 John 2:17.
  • 0389 · …eyes and ears and understandings mysteries altogether strange. — S. cf. 1 Cor 2:9.
  • 0389 · …becomes, and is called, all things to all, — S. 1 Cor 15:28.
  • 0392 · …who, because of thee, sojourn in this land — P. The monks living in Africa (so Combefis's note 18), among whom Maximus writes; "because of thee" — under George's protection.
  • 0392 · …for us, our all-holy, glorious Lady, the Theotokos — T. Θεοτόκος, "Theotokos", the God-bearer: the Chalcedonian title of Mary, first used in these letters here.
  • 0392 · …ever-virgin Mary, with all the saints. Amen. SCHOLIA — T. The seven Greek scholia printed after the letter (α΄–ζ΄), keyed to the calls (1)–(7) set in the text and not reproduced: (1) "not to rule over men" (col 365); (2) "all that concerns ourselves" (365); (3) "the divine purpose" (368); (4) "as in a definition" (368); (5) "Not in our power" (368); (6) "the middle" (376); (7) "like the blessed Lot" (377). Scholion 4–5's τὸ αὐτεξούσιον = self-determination; εἴτουν = "that is".

Chunk 0004 (me-004)

  • 0392 · …the same, to John the Cubicularius, on love. — P. John the Cubicularius (κουβικουλάριος, chamberlain of the imperial court), Maximus's most frequent correspondent: Letters 2, 3, 4, 10, 12, 27, 43, 44. Letter 2 addresses him in the plural throughout (ὑμᾶς, θεοφύλακτοι), so you*; the plural may take in his household. Combefis's note call (d) after the name is not reproduced.
  • 0393 · …with every spiritual blessing in the heavenly places», — S. Eph 1:3, with ὑμᾶς ("you") for the Apostle's ἡμᾶς ("us"), turned to the addressees.
  • 0393 · …the fulfilment of the law and the prophets; — S. Rom 13:10 (πλήρωμα νόμου ἡ ἀγάπη), with Matt 22:40 ("the law and the prophets"). Scholion 1, at the letter's end, is keyed here.
  • 0396 · …of the virtues, by which we are able — T. The Greek prints δύνανται ("they are able") and Migne gives [δυνάμεθα] ("we are able") in brackets; the Latin has possumus. The English follows the bracket, which agrees with the first persons around it (ῥαπίζομεν, ἀπωθούμεθα).
  • 0396 · …it, as being faithful, and unfailing, and abiding. — S. 1 Cor 13:8 ("love never fails") and 13:13 (faith, hope, love "abide"); the triad expounded next is the Apostle's.
  • 0396 · …be according to the image of the Creator, — S. Col 3:10 (κατ᾿ εἰκόνα τοῦ κτίσαντος), with Gen 1:26–27.
  • 0396 · …bending reason under it; and persuading the inclination — T. γνώμη = inclination, here and throughout the letters; γνωμικῶς = by inclination. Maximus sets it against φύσις (nature) all through this letter: one nature, and, through love, one inclination.
  • 0397 · …he might destroy the works of the devil, — S. 1 John 3:8.
  • 0397 · …has been partitioned, he uproots along with it. — T. The plate's συνακερίζωσε is no word. Combefis's radicitus evellit and the sense, against the plucking-out (ἀποτίλλω) of the next sentence, give συνεξερίζωσε ("he uproots along with it"); logged as a doubt.
  • 0400 · …man, by the perfect principle according to nature; — S. Gen 18:1–8, the three men at Mamre, whom Abraham received as guests. συνένδημον = "dwelling together with", "fellow-resident": God made himself Abraham's guest.

Chunk 0005 (me-005)

  • 0401 · …the hands of the prophets I was likened» — S. Hos 12:10 (LXX 12:11), ἐν χερσὶ προφητῶν ὡμοιώθην. ἐκ προσώπου τοῦ Θεοῦ = "in the person of God", the prophet speaking as God.
  • 0401 · …and strength, and of the neighbour as oneself. — S. Deut 6:5 and Lev 19:18, as joined in Matt 22:37–39.
  • 0401 · …providence for the whole genus according to nature; — T. A definition (ὡς ἐν ὅρῳ): love is "the inward relation (ἐνδιάθετος σχέσις), universal (καθόλου), toward the first good, together with the whole providence for the whole genus according to nature". The genus is mankind, for which love takes thought with God's own providence.
  • 0404 · …inaccessible beauty of the holy and royal Trinity. — S. John 10:9; τὰ Ἅγια τῶν ἁγίων is the inner sanctuary (Heb 9:3).
  • 0404 · …worthy to become a partaker of divine fatness. — S. John 15:1. T. The Greek as printed has θείας … ποιότητος ("divine quality"); Combefis's Latin read divinae … pinguedinis, that is πιότητος ("fatness"), the word that goes with rooting and the vine (cf. Rom 11:17, "the root and fatness of the olive"). The English follows πιότητος.
  • 0404 · …it be said that it is also God — S. Cf. 1 John 4:8, 16, "God is love": the way of love is not only toward God but is God.
  • 0404 · …saints stood firm against sin to the uttermost, — S. Cf. Heb 12:4 (ἀντικατέστητε πρὸς τὴν ἁμαρτίαν).
  • 0405 · …the good fight to come to the end, — S. 2 Tim 4:7; cf. 1 Tim 6:12.
  • 0405 · …all things, endures all things. Love never falls», — S. 1 Cor 13:4–8.
  • 0405 · …is the light of the eyes and peace; — S. Bar 4:1–4, then 3:14. Maximus gives Baruch to Jeremiah (the book stood with Jeremiah in the Greek Bible); «I say to you» is his own frame. The quotation is in the prophet's singular, so thou, though Maximus applies it to "you".
  • 0405 · …it, and hast found sanctification for thy soul.» — S. Jer 6:16, turned into the second person singular of praise.
  • 0408 · …after the image of him who created him — S. Bar 5:1, again given to Jeremiah, with Eph 4:22–24 and Col 3:10 woven in by Maximus ("I mean …").
  • 0408 · …thy head the mitre of the everlasting glory», — S. Bar 5:2, as printed, with Maximus's own gloss set inside the quotation: τῷ τε βασίμῳ τρόπῳ τῶν ἀρετῶν, καὶ τῷ τῆς σοφίας ἀπταίστῳ λόγῳ κοσμούμενος, "adorned both with the firm manner of the virtues and with the unerring principle of wisdom". The Baruch thou stays. T. Col. 408 lines 3–5 are set across the full page width, because the Latin column ends above them. They have been read at the plate, and the English renders them as printed.
  • 0408 · …name Peace of righteousness, and glory of godliness.» — S. Bar 5:3–4.
  • 0408 · …attainable by man, of the divine idioms. SCHOLIA — T. The three scholia are printed by Combefis in the Greek column after the letter; his Latin numbers them 1–3. By their content they answer, in order, "the fulfilment of the law and the prophets" (me-004), the turning from "the movement according to nature" (me-004), and love joining "God and men … about him who has it" (above); the keys themselves were not read with this text. The third scans as iambic verse (Θεὸν βροτούς τε πάντας ἀρρήτῳ λόγῳ).

Chunk 0006 (me-006)

  • 0408 · …Desirableness in the Lord, together with the blessing — T. εὐλογία, "blessing", is a gift sent, here the alms John sent to the monks of the monastery of St George (Latin cum benedictione missa). ποθεινότης, "Desirableness", is an honorific address ("your Longed-for Self"); Letter 3 is in the plural throughout (ὑμεῖς, ἠγαπημένοι), so you.
  • 0408 · …the saving commandments; but the self-love and shrewdness — T. σύνεσις [Fort. αἴσθησις]: the bracketed conjecture is printed in Migne's Greek ("perhaps: sense-perception"). Combefis's Latin translates the printed word (prudentia).
  • 0409 · …by a dexterous scattering of what you have; — S. Cf. Ps 111:9 LXX (ἐσκόρπισεν, ἔδωκεν τοῖς πένησιν). T. παντὶ καὶ λόγῳ καὶ τρόπῳ is the idiom "by every means", rendered by the glossary's pair.
  • 0412 · …the most devout men aforesaid too the mercy — T. τὸν ἔλεον [τὸ ἔλαιον]: the bracket ("the oil") is Migne's, and Combefis's Latin follows it (humanitatis oleum); "let fall" (ἐπεστάξατε, "let drip") suits oil. The English gives both.
  • 0412 · …of the hand, and the word of murmuring», — S. Isa 58:9 LXX (ἐὰν ἀφέλῃς ἀπὸ σοῦ σύνδεσμον καὶ χειροτονίαν καὶ ῥῆμα γογγυσμοῦ). T. For the printed Ἀνέχετε ("hold up") the English reads Ἀνέλετε, "take away" (Latin Auferte). χειροτονία, literally "stretching-out of the hand": Combefis's note (f) argues that Maximus takes the LXX word as delectus, the picking out of some to benefit and the neglect of others, against those who rendered it "ordination".
  • 0412 · …present the abyss of his judgements be covered. — S. Cf. Ps 35:7 LXX ("thy judgements are a great abyss").
  • 0412 · …our bread even upon the water without reckoning, — S. Eccl 11:1. Παροιμιαστής, "the Proverbialist", is Solomon, the author to whom Ecclesiastes was given.
  • 0412 · …who imitate his love for man toward us? — T. τί οὐκ ἂν γένοιτο ἔχων is rendered as printed; the construction is loose, and the Latin at the foot of the page is too damaged to check it. Letter 3 ends here. The line printed after it (οὕτως ἐχειροτονήθη) belongs to Combefis's note (f) and is not translated.
  • 0413 · …repentance unto salvation not to be repented of — S. 2 Cor 7:10. Letter 4 is to the same John, now in the singular (δεσπότην, ἠγαπημένε, σε), so thou.
  • 0413 · …may beat off the wicked sting of sin. — S. Cf. 1 Cor 15:56.
  • 0416 · …the air before the spiritual hosts of wickedness: — S. Eph 6:12 (τὰ πνευματικὰ τῆς πονηρίας). The examination "in the air" is the soul's passage after death past the demons who claim it, the "inquest" of the paragraph's opening.
  • 0416 · …the things written in them with all exactness; — S. Dan 7:9–10. T. [ἀνθρώπων] is bracketed in Migne's Greek; kept in brackets.
  • 0416 · …the gnashing of teeth, and the unceasing tears, — S. Matt 25:33, 41; 8:12; Mark 9:48 (Isa 66:24).
  • 0417 · …reckon himself less than the least of all, — S. Cf. Eph 3:8 (ἐλαχιστότερος, "less than the least").
  • 0417 · …may the whole aim of piety be achieved — T. κατορθωθῇ [κατωρθώθη]: Migne prints the indicative in brackets as a variant; the English renders the subjunctive and gives the bracket.
  • 0420 · …simply, let us become all things to all, — S. 1 Cor 9:22.
  • 0420 · …divine words, and not hearers only, or speakers, — S. Jas 1:22. λέκτας, "speakers": Combefis renders praecones.

Chunk 0007 (me-007)

  • 0420 · …LETTER 5 Of the same, to Constantine. — P. Constantine: otherwise unknown. Combefis's note (h) declines to guess beyond what the letter shows, a layman of standing ("my master") who took Maximus's counsels to heart, written probably while Maximus was in Africa.
  • 0420 · …never, as it is written, restrain my lips — S. Ps 39:10 LXX (τὰ χείλη μου οὐ μὴ κωλύσω).
  • 0420 · …gives to all simply, and does not reproach. — S. Jas 1:5. T. χάριν is the object of ὑποδέξασθαι ("receive the grace"), not the preposition "for the sake of"; the Latin turns the phrase into a clause.
  • 0421 · …eager doers of the works of the commandment; — S. cf. Jas 1:22, 25.
  • 0421 · …are reckoned delightful. For the world passes away, — S. 1 John 2:17; cf. 1 Cor 7:31.
  • 0421 · …like a charioteer snatched away by hard-mouthed horses, — S. cf. Plato, Phaedrus 246a–254e, the charioteer and his horses.
  • 0421 · …and love for man; pursuing peace and love, — S. cf. 2 Tim 2:22; Heb 12:14.
  • 0424 · …made by the evil spirits in this air; — T. The search (ἔρευνα) of the departed soul by the demons of the air, the scene later called the "toll-houses"; cf. Eph 2:2.
  • 0424 · …and keep watch over the guarding of ourselves, — S. cf. 1 Pet 5:8.
  • 0424 · …account to God in the day of judgement — S. Matt 12:36; Combefis's margin adds Rom 14:12.
  • 0424 · …of our all-glorious and all-holy Lady, the Theotokos — T. Θεοτόκος, "God-bearer", kept as Theotokos.
  • 0424 · …the most holy and most blessed archbishop John, — P. John, archbishop: Combefis (note f) identifies him as John, bishop of Cyzicus, to whom Maximus addressed the earlier Ambigua on Gregory Nazianzen. His note (l) reads the letter as aimed chiefly at the Origenists, who held the soul to be a body, or joined to a fine body.
  • 0425 · …injunction, which bids me demonstrate more from nature, — T. φυσικώτερον: "more from nature", by natural reasoning. The five numbered arguments that follow are printed α΄–ε΄ and rendered 1.–5.
  • 0425 · …If body is by its own nature unmoved, — T. ἀκίνητος = unmoved, the glossary's word, here of body that cannot move itself.
  • 0428 · …incorporeal, fearing a fear where there is none, — S. Ps 13:5 LXX (ἐφοβήθησαν φόβον οὗ οὐκ ἦν φόβος).
  • 0428 · …on account of the movement, that is, inclination — T. γνώμη = inclination throughout these letters; paired here with διάθεσις, disposition.
  • 0428 · …we are called beings, and living, and lights, — T. φῶτες as printed means "men"; the series (τὸ φωτίζεσθαι before it, φῶς of God after) and the Latin lumina want φῶτα, "lights", which the English follows.
  • 0429 · …or synonymously, as with things consubstantial, but homonymously, — T. συνωνύμως / ὁμωνύμως: Aristotle's synonyms and homonyms (Categories 1), one name with the same or with a different account.
  • 0429 · …the principle of the "according to the image" — S. Gen 1:26.
  • 0429 · …is the view of the new wise men — T. "the new wise men": Combefis takes them to be the Origenists of the day (see note 12).
  • 0432 · …understood, it remains ungraspable, and being spoken, ineffable. — S. cf. Dionysius the Areopagite, Mystical Theology 5; Divine Names 1.1.
  • 0432 · …of divine witnesses and testimonies that surrounds us; — S. Heb 12:1, the source also of the "weight" (ὄγκος) and the "easily besetting" (εὐπερίστατος) in the next clause.

Chunk 0008 (me-008)

  • 0433 · …7 *Of the same, to John the presbyter, — P. John the presbyter: not otherwise identified. Combefis (note k, on Letter 8) observes that "shepherd and teacher" suits a presbyter set over a monastery, under whom Maximus had lived or been taught, though it would suit a bishop better.
  • 0433 · …month of August, of the present first indiction, — T. The indiction is the fifteen-year fiscal cycle, its year beginning 1 September; the cycle is not named. A damaged line of Migne's apparatus mentions 645 and Maximus's stay in Africa.
  • 0433 · …affirms that it is present, having the very — T. [ἀτοπίαν] is printed in brackets, an editorial supplement in Migne's text; the Latin has it (absurditatem). Kept in brackets.

Apollinarius of Laodicea (d. c. 390), who taught a Word in place of the human mind, and so a mixture into "one nature"; Nestorius, bishop of Constantinople 428–431, deposed at Ephesus for dividing Christ into two. "Jewish" is Maximus's polemical epithet: a Christ who is a mere man, as the Jews hold.
