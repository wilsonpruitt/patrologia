These are the edition's working notes, published as they were written. They record, work by work, where Migne's plate is defective or the reading uncertain: the printed form, what the English does with it, and why. They are not a groomed apparatus. §2 · THE ISAIAH CITATION AT 0694B — Migne's [n: (Isa. LXV)] points at a verse that is not there

The quotation Lux lucernae non lucebit ibi amplius is Apocalypse 18:23, not Isaias. No verse of Isa. LXV — nor of Isa. LX, the chapter that carries the nearest thought (non erit tibi amplius sol ad lucendum per diem) — reads anything like it. The chapter number is plausibly there because the gloss's own preceding sentence is about new heavens and a new earth, which is Isa 65:17, and the compositor (or Bede's source) carried the locator across to the wrong sentence.

Nothing was changed. The note rides verbatim, as every [n:] does. The [var:] marker records only the two readings of the text (ibi against Apoc 18:23's in te), because Pattern 14 says to state the readings and let the crux argue the rest. This is the crux.

The textual divergence itself is real and material in a small way: Apocalypse addresses Babylon in the second person ("in thee"), while Migne's ibi makes it a place-adverb ("there") and so lets Bede apply it to the world that is passing. Rendered "shall shine there no more".

§3a · TWO [cj: …], BOTH AT 0692C, BOTH ADDED AFTER THE BLIND READ

Pattern 18: the printed word is a real Latin form, so nothing is broken for [sic:] to wrap, and the faithful English then carries a relation or a polarity the Latin does not. Both are additive — neither changed a word of the translation.

non boni — an intrusive negation, and the reader needs both readings

Vel torquebuntur ibi, id est apud Deum: ubi angeli non boni, non sustinent, sed vindicant si quid exsecrabile contra illos dicitur.

Rendered, unchanged: "where the angels, not good ones [cj: non boni; read boni, "the good angels"], endure not, but avenge".

Why a marker was needed where §4 originally took none. My first treatment chose the attributive reading silently, on the strength of angeli mali in the construal above. That was a silent choice between two readings that both construe, which is the one thing the apparatus exists to prevent. The evidence runs both ways:

  • For boni (the marker's conjecture): the clause is set apud Deum, and its angels avenge blasphemy — that is the office of the faithful angels, not of the punished apostates. non boni fights its own sentence. And a doubled non … non inside four words (angeli non boni, non sustinent) is the classic signature of an intrusive particle, plausibly a compositor's dittography for ubi angeli boni non sustinent.
  • For non boni as printed: Vel introduces an alternative construal, and an alternative may perfectly well keep the evil angels of the first half — who would then avenge insults offered to themselves (contra illos), which the pronoun allows.

⛔ The translation follows the plate and both nons stand. The marker exists so that the roughness reads as Migne's rather than as ours, which is exactly what it did not do before. A [sic:] was considered and rejected: the words are rendered in English on the page, and Pattern 12's own operational test ("does the reader see our translation or his type?") reserves [sic:] for carried, untranslated runs.

praesumptioni — a stranded dative, no longer papered over

et territi magnitudine poenarum, jam ab illo judicio praesumptioni desistunt

Rendered: "they now desist from that judgment, to presumption [cj: praesumptioni; read praesumptionis, "of presumption"]".

desistere ab illo judicio already takes its ablative, so the dative praesumptioni attaches to nothing. The expected form is the genitive praesumptionis, agreeing with the judicium superbiendi three lines above. My earlier "for presumption" was the defect this edition exists to catch: an English preposition supplying a causal relation the Latin case does not carry, which let the sentence read smoothly straight past a broken form. The bare dative exponent "to" now displays the strandedness instead of concealing it.

§4 · THE UNEXPRESSED SUBJECT — every supplied pronoun, named

The dominant defect class. Each place where the English supplies a person the Latin does not print, with what fixes it. Nothing here was left to construe itself.

Subject = Peter (the epistle's author), fixed by the ARGUMENTUM and by the first-person glosses that surround them. 0689A scribit ("He writes this epistle") — the ARGUMENTUM names Simon Petrus three lines above · 0689A cognoscit ("He acknowledges them as equal to himself"; sibi = Peter, who is the nos of coaequalem nobiscum) · 0689B addit ("Whence he adds") · 0689B scribit twice ("he writes that one to beginners, this one to the more perfect") · 0690B vult monere ("Why does he wish to admonish…") · 0690C vocat ("Beautifully he calls it not death") · 0690D tangit ("Here he touches both the pagans and the heretics") · 0693B ostendit ("he shows how effectual it shall be") · 0694B ait ("He saith not, others") · 0694B dicit ("He says that Paul had written to them").

Subject = God, fixed by the lemma above. 0691D–0692A redegit · contexerit · voluit ("he reduced them to ashes… he willed the surrounding region still to preserve…"). The antecedent is not in the sentence: it is Deus in the VERS. 4 lemma Si enim Deus angelis two glosses up, which the Vulgate's own Et civitates… eversione damnavit continues. Also 0693C videt ("he sees it even as that which is done in one day") and 0694A differt · adimpleat · praevidit, where the printed Dominus of Non tardat Dominus is the subject three words earlier.

Subject = Lot, fixed by the lemma Loth oppressum. 0692B cernens · valebat · reservabat · fuscabat ("which, beholding, he was not able to correct; but he himself kept himself in good works. Nor did he darken himself…").

Subject = the Sodomites among whom Lot dwelt — the one that needed the Vulgate. 0692B quia nihil in illo videbant, nihil de eo audiebant. The sentence prints no subject at all, and English cannot leave the verb bare. It is not the proximi of the sentence before (who are the ones whose deeds tormented him, in the accusative), and it is not Lot. It is 2 Pet 2:8's habitans apud eos — the people he lived among. Rendered "they saw nothing in him, they heard nothing of him". If a blind reader disputes one supplied pronoun in this work, it should be this one.

Subject = David, fixed by the same clause. 0691B prophetavit ("because he prophesied of Christ more clearly than the others") — propheticum sermonem David is printed nine words earlier, and firmiorem inter alios prophetas is the comparative that requires a prophet, not the word, as its subject.

Subject = an individual heretic, and Migne shifts number mid-sentence. 0691C–D: associat (singular) then faciunt · concitant (plural). Rendered as printed, "in those whom, seduced, he associates to his own heresy… whom they stir up" — the number shift preserved rather than smoothed to one or the other. This is a real inconcinnity on the plate and it is not repaired.

⚠ But the English reader has no antecedent for that singular, because the only expressed plural in view is Per haereticos and nothing in the sentence licenses a "he". The reading is almost certainly distributive — whom each of them associates to his own heresy — the singular ranging over the plural haereticos, with the plural verbs then returning to the whole body. Recorded here rather than resolved in the text, so that the roughness reads as Migne's alternation and not as a translator's slip.

⭐ The dative goal of associat is suae haeresi, not the heretic. An earlier draft read "whom he joins to himself, seduced into his own heresy", which kept the sense but moved the government: it made the person the goal and demoted the heresy to an adverbial phrase. Repaired after the blind read. The verse's own logic wants it this way — the men are annexed to the heresy, which is what makes them count as a place where the way of truth is blasphemed.

Two sites where the readings were weighed and both construe:

  1. 0689A quia eadem quam ipsi acceperant Christi gratiam et fidem. Is ipsi the recipients ("the same faith which they themselves had received") or the apostles ("which we ourselves had received")? The Vulgate's coaequalem nobiscum sortiti sunt fidem argues for the apostles as the standard of comparison; the Latin pronoun is third person and argues for the recipients. English "they themselves" preserves exactly the ambiguity the Latin has, so no choice was forced and none was smuggled. Recorded so nobody "fixes" it into "we ourselves."
  2. 0692C ubi angeli non boni, non sustinent, sed vindicant. ⛔ This entry was wrong and has been superseded — see §3a. It set out two construals of non boni, chose the attributive one silently on the strength of angeli mali above, and took no marker, on the reasoning that "neither reading changes a negation." That reasoning was the error: the competing reading is that the non is itself intrusive, so the polarity of the clause is precisely what is at stake. The translation is unchanged and both nons still stand; a [cj: non boni; read boni] now carries the alternative on the page.

One subject that cannot be recovered, and is not guessed. 0693A, the tail of the Bede excerpt: ante Dei oculos sordidas ipsas etiam lacrymas facit. Whatever governs facit was cut away by Migne's own etc. / usque ad. Rendered with a neutral "it makes even those tears themselves foul before the eyes of God" — no antecedent invented.

§7 · LEMMA EXPANSIONS — the words this translator supplied, named

Convention 3 (a fragmentary lemma is rendered as the phrase it stands for, not a word-for-word stump; Ruth's Non te. → "Let no man see thee"). Every place it was invoked, with what was supplied, so a reader can see the edge of our hand:

  • 0691D VERS. 4 Si enim Deus angelis. → "For if God spared not the angels." Supplied: non pepercit, from Vulg. 2 Pet 2:4. "For if God, to the angels." is not English and angelis is a dative that only parcere governs. ⚠ This is the one expansion that supplies a negation, which is why it is named here rather than passed over: the non is the verse's, printed in the Vulgate the fragment opens, and the alternative under convention 3 was a stump that says nothing. In the Beda excerpt two words later the same gap is filled with the corpus's supplied-word brackets instead — "If God [spared not] the apostate angels" — because that is running prose, not a lemma, and brackets are available there.
  • 0690B VERS. 13 Justum autem, → "But it is just,". Supplied: the copula only. arbitror was deliberately not supplied, because the gloss's own next clause renders it (quia justum arbitror → "because I judge it just") and supplying it in the lemma would have pre-empted the gloss.
  • 0694A VERS. 10 Coeli magno. Coeli, → "The heavens with a great violence. The heavens,". Supplied: impetu, from Vulg. 3:10 caeli magno impetu transient. The bare ablative magno has no English exponent standing alone. The gloss expounds only the second lemma (Coeli, id est sancti), so nothing downstream rests on the supplied word.
  • 0694B VERS. 14 Satagite immaculati et inviolati. → "Be diligent to be unspotted and undefiled." Supplied: the infinitive that English needs between satagite and its predicate adjectives (Vulg. satagite immaculati et inviolati ei inveniri in pace).
  • 0693A VERS. 1 Hanc ecce vobis. → "Behold, this to you." Nothing supplied. secundam scribo epistolam was left out on purpose: the gloss immediately says it (hanc etiam secundam scribo Epistolam), so the fragment is intelligible as it stands.
  • Everything else was left as a short lemma because short English carried it: Simon. · Equal. · In justice. · For so. · Perdition. · The unjust. · Into ashes. · Proud things. · The elements. · Difficult things. · They pervert. · It is hidden. No stump in the work is unreadable as English.

Two English commas inside lemmata, declared. In scientia autem abstinentiam. → "But in knowledge, abstinence." (the comma marks an ellipsis English cannot carry otherwise) and Hanc ecce vobis. → "Behold, this to you." Both are ordinary English prose pointing, not sentence-type marks, so Pattern 8 is not engaged; a comma that was going to be added to Caecus est et manu tentans. was removed on review, since "He is blind and groping with the hand." needs none.

§8 · READ CLEAN — sections with nothing to report

Named so that a blind reader can tell "checked and clean" from "not looked at".

  • The ARGUMENTUM (0689A). One sentence, no scripture, no defect. pietas fixed as "piety" here and held to it at 0689C/D (pietatis opera → "the works of piety") — one voice within the work, per the merge-time reconciliation rule. ⚠ One genuinely undecidable construal, raised for the record by the blind read and left as written. Simon Petrus per fidem huic mundo sapientes mortuos esse declarat: per fidem can attach to the accusative-and-infinitive (the wise are dead to this world through faith — what our English gives) or to declarat (Peter declares it through faith). Word order slightly favours the first, the finite verb's proximity slightly favours the second, and there is no evidence either way — nothing in the sentence, and no gloss below picks the phrase up. Recorded because a reader deserves to know the ambiguity is Migne's and not a reading we resolved quietly.
  • CAPUT PRIMUM VERS. 2–8 (0689B–0689D). Six glosses, no divergence, no defective type; the multiplicetur / adimpleatur contrast between the two epistles verified against 1 Pet 1:2 and 2 Pet 1:2 respectively and both are correct.
  • VERS. 14–15 (0690C–0690D). The depositio gloss and the dabo operam gloss: no scripture, three negations, all carried, no ambiguity of subject beyond the Peter voice.
  • VERS. 16's transfiguration gloss (0691A). Elias / Moses / Peter, James and John read as three ages; the doubled Vel, per Moysen mortuum mortuorum, per vivos vivorum is elliptical in the Latin and is rendered elliptically ("by Moses dead, that of the dead; by the living, that of the living"). Not a dittography — the two limbs differ and neither is an eye-skip; Pattern 11 was considered and declined.
  • VERS. 20–21 (0691B–0691C). The four nons of the prophecy argument all carried; the chunk's single question mark is at 0690B (Quare vult monere illos qui sciunt, et confirmati sunt?), a direct question that keeps its mark with no recasting — Pattern 8b is not engaged anywhere in this work.

  • CAPUT III VERS. 2 (0693A–0693B). §9 · WHAT A BLIND READER SHOULD LOOK AT HARDEST

  1. 0692C angeli non boni — §3a. Two readings, evidence both ways, now carried on the page as a [cj:] instead of resolved silently. The likeliest remaining defect in the work, and the one place a reader may reasonably think the marker should have been a decision instead.
  2. 0692B quia nihil in illo videbant, nihil de eo audiebant — the supplied "they", §4. The subject comes from 2 Pet 2:8, not from the sentence, and it is the exact shape of the class that beat Ruth at 0538C.
  3. 0691D VERS. 4's supplied spared not — §7. The only place this translation supplies a negation the run does not print, defensible under convention 3 and named for that reason.
  4. 0689B's John 17:3 allusion — §1c. The declined [var:]; unum for solum is the kind of word an argument could rest on, and this one does not, but the judgment is contestable.
  5. 0692A reservant — §5. Rendered plural against a singular subject. If the house view is that the sentence should read "it reserves", that is a repair and should be argued, not made.
  6. 0694A Quantor — §3. The decision not to write "The four" beside the marker.
  7. 0691C–D's singular/plural shift (associat … concitant) — §4. Preserved rather than harmonized; the distributive reading is argued there but not imposed.
  8. 0689A's per fidem — §8. Undecidable, and recorded as such.

§10 · THE BLIND POLARITY READ — what it found, and the transferable mechanism

A blind reader went over this work after the English was filed. It never saw this cruces file, and it read Jude (8978) in Latin only, so neither our reasoning nor our shipped English of the sibling epistle could steer it. It returned four repairs, all now applied, and — more usefully — two negatives on the two risks this work was launched to guard.

10a · The two negatives, with the evidence

No silent scripture conformation, anywhere. Every scripture span was checked individually against the Vulgate. Two were singled out as the model for the class: 0690D indoctas/doctas and the (Isa. LXV) locator at 0694B (§1a, §2). The reader also confirmed that Migne is followed against the Vulgate at Apoc. VI, 0694A on both points, the transposed adhuc modicum tempus and the omitted et fratres eorum, and called that the highest-risk span on the page. §1c had declined a marker there on transposition grounds and logged it; the decline is confirmed.

No Jude contamination. The reader compared the two plates directly and reached §6's conclusion from the other side: Migne's two recensions share almost no wording. The clearest demonstration is the one place the biblical parallel is tightest, the fallen angels:

  • Jude's plate — Si enim angelis peccantibus non pepercit
  • 2 Peter's plate — Si Deus apostatas angelos traditos poenis inferni

Two different sentences about the same event. Likewise the two Hi sunt glosses, which share a lemma and are about different things (Jude: murmurers envying their brethren; 2 Peter: nihil in eis est nisi vanitas et immunditia). There was, in the end, very little available to import even had the pull been given in to.

10b · ⭐ The mechanism behind two of the four repairs — carry this to the next work

Repairs 1 and 3 are the same error twice: an English preposition supplying a relation the Latin case does not carry.

colLatinthe draft wrotewhat the preposition invented
0693Bdimittatis pro praedicatione illorum pseudo"let go the precepts … for the preaching of those false ones"English "for" defaults to purpose or benefit, so the page said Peter warns against abandoning the precepts in order to preach the false teachers' message. dimittere pro X here is exchange: trading apostolic precept away in favour of their preaching. This clause is the hinge of the paragraph and the sense that survived was the wrong one. Now "in exchange for the preaching of those false ones."
0692Cab illo judicio praesumptioni desistunt"from that judgment for presumption"A causal relation laid over a dative that governs nothing at all, smoothing the reader straight past a broken form. Now "to presumption", with a [cj:] (§3a).

Why it is a class and not two slips. Latin fixes these relations in the morphology; English must choose a preposition, and the reflex reaches for the commonest one, "for", which in English is ambiguous across purpose, benefit, cause, exchange and duration. Nothing looks wrong afterwards: the sentence is smooth, no negation moved, no lemma changed, and every automated check passes. It is Pattern 7a's fifth mechanism (reference or agency mis-bound) wearing a preposition instead of a pronoun, and no particle-counting check will ever see it.

The habit that catches it: when the English needs a preposition the Latin does not print, name the case and name what governs it before choosing the word. pro + ablative after a verb of relinquishing is exchange, not purpose. A dative after a verb that has already taken its ablative is attached to nothing, and the honest English is the bare exponent plus a marker, never the preposition that makes the clause read well.

10c · Confirmed correct, no action

The [spared not] bracket at 0691D (§7); the [sic:] handling of all five carried-type runs, including leaving Quantor wholly untranslated rather than writing "The four" (§3); Coaequalem → "Equal", since the gloss recovers the force with equal to himself; pseudo and pseudoapostoli kept distinct; quia et primo … et cum read temporally; ita videt, sicut folded; and verba doctorum auditum praebere treated as ordinary medieval case looseness (Pattern 9).

Six comma→semicolon promotions (0689B, 0690A, 0690C, 0690D, 0692B, 0694C) were observed and expressly declined for repair: they are real, positional and all in one direction, but a corpus-wide semicolon measurement closed last batch as a rate question, not an absence, with a standing instruction not to sweep it. Nothing was softened in the other direction anywhere in this work — no printed mark deleted, none supplied — so no reading is altered and no parity check is fooled. Left alone rather than churned across six sites.
