§4 · READINGS THAT CONSTRUE ODDLY AND ARE MIGNE'S — rendered as printed

Addendum A: a real, well-formed word that reads oddly is Migne's until a plate says otherwise. None of these took a marker.

  • 0009B · defugatis (postulatione sua defugatis ab eodem loco serpentibus) — hapax in src/latin/, and fugatis is likewise a hapax, so frequency says nothing. But defugare is attested medieval Latin (Du Cange) and the form is well-made. Pattern 9: rendered "driven away", no italics, no marker.
  • 0009A · Non errore neque reprehensione superiori, sed certo consilio — reprehensione superiori is the hardest phrase in the batch. Rendered literally, "nor by any higher censure". Two readings construe and I could not choose: superiori as "from above / on high", or superiori as "the foregoing" (the censure mentioned earlier — except that nothing earlier censures anything, which is what makes it hard). Neither reading was smuggled into the English; the rendering is neutral between them. Negation hosts proved from the sed certo consilio correlative: non is on errore, neque on reprehensione superiori, and neither is on intelliguntur.
  • 0017D · Audiat hoc qui acceptis divitiis luxuriae serviunt — a number disagreement: singular Audiat … qui against plural serviunt. Rendered so that the disagreement survives into English — "Let him hear this who, riches having been received, serve luxury" — rather than being repaired by a plural "them", which is the silent-repair move Pattern 12's second test names (rendering that hides the defect). English does have an exponent here (him/serve), so Pattern 7 governs and no marker is needed.
  • 0013B · Tamen revertere. Si revertebaris ad me, dicit Dominus. — Jr 3:1 reads tamen revertere ad me, dicit Dominus, an imperative; the gloss opens with an imperfect indicative, Si revertebaris, which yields "If thou wert returning to me, saith the Lord" and hangs without an apodosis. Two conjectures weighed: (a) Sive: revertere ad me, on the model of the Sive: that opens the immediately preceding gloss in the same column, and (b) Jerome's alternative rendering si reverteris ad me*. Neither was taken into the text and the printed reading stands, because (a) requires deleting a syllable Migne prints and (b) requires changing tense — and the two conjectures pull in different directions, which is itself the reason to render and log rather than choose. Plate candidate, low priority.
  • 0013C · quae ad nullum declinare erubescit — carried with its negation intact ("who is ashamed to turn aside to no man"), which is the double-negative Latin idiom for shamelessness and reads as a contradiction in English. That is the required treatment: 7a forbids translating a word as its opposite to rescue a sentence.

2d · TWO FRAGMENT-RENDERING RULINGS

(i) The Nunquid fragments are NOT expanded — negation fidelity outranks convention 3. Migne prints VERS. 9.-- Nunquid super his (Jr 9:9) and Nunquid carnes sanctae. (Jr 11:15). Convention 3 would render each as the phrase it opens — but Jr 9:9 continues non visitabo, so "Shall I not visit for these things" would put on the page a non Migne does not print. The rule that every negative in the English answers to one on the page is not suspended by a lemma convention. Both are therefore rendered from the printed words alone and identically as a class: "Shall it be for these things" and "Shall the holy flesh."** The stumpiness is the plate's.

(ii) Where convention 3 DID expand, and why each is safe. Sicut frigidam. → "As a cistern maketh her water cold" (Jr 6:7, and the gloss is entirely about coldness) · Ut quid mihi thus. → "To what purpose do ye bring me frankincense" · Nemo quod bonum, → "No man speaketh that which is good" · Quia ascendit. → "For death is come up" (Jr 9:21) · Si mutare potest, → "If the Ethiopian can change" · Nunquid projiciens, → "Hast thou utterly cast off" · Nunquid avis discolor? → "Is my inheritance a speckled bird?", where the Septuagint reading Migne prints beside it carries the same full clause and the contrast is unreadable without it. None of these imports a negative, and none imports a word the verse's own Clementine text does not have. Complete one-word lemmata (Speciosae. · Pastores. · Nescierunt. · Gladium. · Lignum. · Grex. · Tabernaculum. · Vicinos. · Nudavi. · Agricolae.) are rendered as they stand — a fragment and a complete one-word lemma are different classes and are not to be contrasted.

§4 · THIS BATCH'S CRUCES — sense, subject, and negation

4a · ⛔ THE ONE SITE WHERE THE PRINTED NEGATIVE MAKES THE SENTENCE CONTRADICT ITSELF

0028C · Jr 13:5, resumption tail: …usque ad si non adhaerens Domino, uno spiritu fuerit in Christo.

Englished as printed: "if, not cleaving to the Lord, he shall have been one spirit in Christ." The allusion is 1 Cor 6:17, qui adhaeret Domino unus spiritus est — so the clause as printed says the opposite of the doctrine it is built on. The non attaches to adhaerens and to nothing else (it stands immediately before the participle, and fuerit has its own predicate in uno spiritu). It is carried. Per Pattern 7a and the standing rule, a word is never translated as its opposite to rescue a sentence, and the tail is a resumption whose head the abridgment removed — the missing head may well be a negative of its own (nemo …, nisi …), in which case the sentence is sound and only looks broken. No conjecture is offered and no marker fires, because both candidates (a lost governing negative; a plate slip for adhaerens) require the head of Jerome's sentence, which is exactly what usque ad deleted.

§6 · READINGS, PARSES AND UNEXPRESSED SUBJECTS

6a. Two readings that both construe — named rather than chosen silently

0059C · Non est semper calix aureus Babylonis vel in manu Domini. Two parses: (i) semper … in manu Domini is one predicate and vel is "even" — "The golden cup of Babylon is not always even in the hand of the Lord"; (ii) two predicates — "It is not always a golden cup of Babylon, or in the hand of the Lord." (i) was taken, and the reason is the sentence's own structure rather than what reads well: the two clauses that follow are both, and only, about the Lord's hand — cum … in manu Domini posita fuerit and Nec jugiter in manu Domini continetur; sed tunc tantum. (ii) loses because nothing in the paragraph ever questions that Babylon is a golden cup. The sed tunc tantum correlative also fixes the host of both negatives on the temporal adverbs (semper, jugiter), not on est/continetur — the 2a trigger fired here and the structure, not the reading, settled it.

The Sed is the pivot, and it pivots on a negative that the printed clause has lost.
