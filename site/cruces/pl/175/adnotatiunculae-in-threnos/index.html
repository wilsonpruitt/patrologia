Adnotatiunculae in Threnos

Hugh of Saint Victor — read the work →

These are the edition's working notes, published as they were written. They record, work by work, where Migne's plate is defective or the reading uncertain: the printed form, what the English does with it, and why. They are not a groomed apparatus. Where a note argues with itself, withdraws an earlier decision, or leaves a question open, that is the record of how the reading was actually reached, and it is left standing — a conjecture that is visible can be checked, and one that has been tidied away cannot.

The English never silently repairs the page. A construable but wrong reading is rendered literally and the conjecture is recorded here, never applied to the text. How the English is produced and checked →

Agent stint: chunks 0000–0004 (cols 0255D–0273D). Pattern references are to translation-style.md.

Carried type (Pattern 7 / 10, marked [sic: …] per Pattern 12)

0257C — dilelectionem (chunk 0000). Migne prints quia longe erant per fidem et dilelectionem. Non-word; conjecture dilectionem ("love"), near certain — Hugh's antithesis is bodily nearness vs. distance "by faith and love." Carried untranslated in italics.

0263D — incarnis (chunk 0002). Migne prints Quod si incarnis delectationem resolutus fuerit. Run-together type (Pattern 10) for in carnis; the accusative delectationem requires the preposition, which is glued to carnis. Carried whole. Consequence: the English sentence lacks the preposition and the genitive "of the flesh," because both are inside the carried run — this is the intended cost of Pattern 10, not an omission.

0264B — concucupiscentia (chunk 0002). quando cor ejus concucupiscentia per abrupta vitiorum distrahit. Non-word; conjecture concupiscentia, certain (the word is used correctly a dozen times elsewhere in this stint). Carried.

0266B — fiammae (chunk 0002), inside the Lam. 2:3 lemma Et succendit in Jacob quasi ignem fiammae devorantis in gyro. Long-s/f substitution for flammae. Sits inside an already-italic lemma, so per Pattern 10 it takes no second italic marker: printed [sic: fiammae] without asterisks.

0270C — texitne (chunk 0003). Migne prints Quomodo obtexit, texitne superiora, hoc est coelestia videre posset. The sentence does not construe as printed. Conjecture: texit ne superiora, hoc est coelestia, videre posset — "he covered, that it might not be able to see the higher things, that is, the heavenly." The garbled run texitne is carried inside a [sic:] marker; the clause it governs is translated, and translated negatively, because ne with the subjunctive posset is a negative purpose clause.

CORRECTED 2026-07-30 (blind polarity read, unit 11058-u1). Until now the English read "…[sic: texitne] the higher things, that is, the heavenly, it might be able to see" — an affirmative purpose clause — and this entry defended it on the ground that "the negative is visibly present in the carried Latin." That defence does not hold. Only the two letters ne sit inside the marker; everything they govern was translated, and translated affirmatively, so what the reader met was the affirmative claim. The page therefore said that the covering was so that the soul might see the heavenly things — the opposite of Hugh's argument in the immediately preceding clause (lumen rationis per peccatum in homine obscuratum est, non ablatum) and of the frame (in tenebris ignorantiae reliquit). This is Pattern 7a's own case, in its subtlest form: a crux that describes a repair does not make the repair honest, and a logged column can look handled while the defect is live. Supplying the negative here is not a silent emendation — it is rendering the printed ne that was being dropped.

Printed readings rendered literally (Pattern 7 — no marker, English is ours)

0260C (chunk 0001) — spernuntur for spernunt. dum in pressuris constitutam conspiciunt, persequuntur et spernuntur? The passive breaks the sentence: the powerful of this world are the subject of persequuntur, so the second verb should be active spernunt ("and spurn her"). Rendered as printed, "persecute her and are despised." Real word, so no [sic:] per Pattern 12.

0261C (chunk 0001) — dicetur for dicitur. illi de quibus dicetur in Evangelio — future where the present is expected of a scriptural citation. Rendered "of whom it shall be said in the Gospel."

0262C (chunk 0001) — tribulatione for tribulationem. si forte foris tribulatione sustinet leaves sustinet without an object. Rendered "if perchance it endures without in tribulation."

0262D (chunk 0001) — anacoluthon at the chunk's last sentence. Et attende, quae sint angustiae eorum, qui a Deo recedunt nunquam securi sunt, semper trepidant. The indirect question is abandoned mid-sentence for a direct statement. Rendered as printed, with a colon marking the break: "consider what are the straits of those who depart from God: they are never secure…" No punctuation was added or removed; the colon replaces the printed comma at the identical position. Flagged because it is the only place in this stint where the English clause boundary is looser than the Latin's.

0265D (chunk 0002) — Quid for Quia. Quid omnia speciosa Jacob destruxit, munitiones virginis Juda dejecit. The context is explanatory, not interrogative, and the sentence closes with a full stop, so Quia is the probable reading. Rendered literally, "What: he hath destroyed…". Note that Migne's own punctuation (period, not question mark) is preserved with it.

0266B (chunk 0002) — obsidente for obsistente. nullo sibi obsidente, universa vastabant — "with none besieging them," where "with none resisting them" is required by the sense. The evidence is internal and unusually clean: at 0269A (chunk 0003) Migne prints the same formula correctly, nullo sibi obsistente. Rendered as printed at 0266B; real word, so no marker.

0267D (chunk 0003) — effectu for affectu. quam quasi filiam paterno effectu dilexerat. Migne prints paterno affectu correctly at 0265B in the parallel passage. Rendered "with a father's effect."

0269B (chunk 0003) — pariter expounded but not printed in the lemma. Hugh's comment Ita ambigue positum est pariter turns on the placement of pariter, but the lemma as Migne prints it — Luxitque antemurale, et murus dissipatus est — does not contain the word. Rendered literally ("So ambiguously is it placed, alike…") rather than reconstructing the fuller lemma; the exposition that follows (antemurale pariter cum muro) is where the word surfaces. No emendation made to the lemma.

0270C (chunk 0003) — projecit de coelo terram inclytam Israel diverges from the Vulgate's proiecit de caelo in terram inclitam Israel. The printed text drops in, which converts terram inclytam Israel from a destination into the direct object — and Hugh's whole gloss depends on that reading (quasi de coelo, id est de incorruptione ad corruptionem terram Israel projecit, and the identification of terra Israel with the body of man). Rendered as printed and as Hugh reads it: "he hath cast down from heaven the glorious land of Israel." Recorded here rather than marked, since this stint used only the marker set named in its brief.

Conventions decided in this stint (for the peer agents on 0005–0018)

  1. The ## head reads "Little Notes on Lamentations", and "Little Notes on Lamentations (cont.)" wherever the Latin prints (cont.). The diminutive adnotatiunculae is carried ("little notes"), and Threni is Englished to "Lamentations" per the work-wide rule.
  2. No sense labels occur in chunks 0000–0004. Hugh introduces the senses in running prose (Secundum allegoriae sensum, Secundum intellectum moralem, Secundum moralem sensum, Mystice), never as a printed abbreviated label. These are translated as prose. The MORAL. / ALLEGORICAL. adjective convention therefore has nothing to attach to yet; it applies only where Migne actually prints a label.
  3. Lexical choices held constant across the stint, because Hugh argues from the distinctions: inimicus = "enemy" and hostis = "foe" (he contrasts them explicitly at 0266C–D); dissipare = "lay waste" throughout (tent, walls, strongholds, wall of the daughter of Sion) since he compares the uses; praecipitare = "cast down headlong" against projicere "cast away," which is the point of his gloss at 0265C; speciosa = "the beautiful things," munitiones = "strongholds," moenia = "walls," antemurale = "outwork."
  4. Hic distingue (0265B–C, three times) is the grammarian's instruction to punctuate. Rendered "Here distinguish," "Here distinguish again," "Here once more distinguish."
  5. No guillemets occur anywhere in chunks 0000–0004. Migne marks Hugh's lemmata with italics only. Nothing was supplied; per Pattern 6 the straight quote was not needed either, since the lemmata are already set off.
