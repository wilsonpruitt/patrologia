These are the edition's working notes, published as they were written. They record, work by work, where Migne's plate is defective or the reading uncertain: the printed form, what the English does with it, and why. They are not a groomed apparatus. Where a note argues with itself, withdraws an earlier decision, or leaves a question open, that is the record of how the reading was actually reached, and it is left standing — a conjecture that is visible can be checked, and one that has been tidied away cannot.

The English never silently repairs the page. A construable but wrong reading is rendered literally and the conjecture is recorded here, never applied to the text. How the English is produced and checked →

Garbled / uncertain printed readings, logged per the translation runbook. Column anchor + printed reading + note. Rendered literally-by-sense where the misprint is transparent; flagged where the reading is genuinely uncertain.

Chunk 0001

  • [0414D] printed notun fuerit — evident misprint for notum fuerit ("it has been made known"). Rendered by sense as "made known."
  • [0416A] printed ne praesumas antcire processurum — antcire is a misprint for anteire ("to go before"). Rendered "presume not to go before."
  • [0418B] printed in veritate serva tenorem justitiae — serva (imperative "keep thou") sits oddly in a 3rd-person description of God; likely servat ("he keeps"). Rendered "he keeps the tenor of justice in truth."

Chunk 0002

  • [0418B/opening] printed Ex exsultavit spiritus meus in Deo salutari meo — Ex is a misprint for Et ("And my spirit hath rejoiced"); the lemma elsewhere reads Et exsultavit. Rendered "And."
  • [0419C] printed humano corporis rationalis anima ante formationem non datur — case mismatch (corporis for corpori). Rendered "to the human body."
  • [0419C] printed sensualis tamen et quae animas aciat, nullo modo dicenda est — aciat is a misprint for faciat ("makes"); animas (pl.) is loose. Rendered "such as would make a soul."

Chunk 0003

  • [0422C] printed ut suum gaudium solitum esse demonstraret — solitum ("wonted/customary") is possible but may be a misprint for solidum ("sound"), which fits the following "neither sprung from vanity nor turned to vanity." Rendered literally "her wonted joy"; flagged as uncertain.
  • [0422D] printed Oculi ejus super [respicit Dominus] omnem viam filiorum hominis — the square-bracketed [respicit Dominus] is an editorial alternative reading interpolated into the scripture quotation (Prov. V/Job 34 conflation). Preserved verbatim and untranslated, per the variant-marker rule (treated like a [ al., … ] variant even though unmarked).
  • [0424B] printed Humilitatem eum humiliatione habent — eum is a misprint for cum ("with"). Rendered "with."

Chunk 0004

  • [0427B] printed natura imcomprehensibilis — misprint for incomprehensibilis ("incomprehensible"). Rendered "incomprehensible."
  • [0427D] printed Quatuor timores ... servilem, mundanum, initialem, fiilialem — fiilialem is a misprint for filialem ("filial"). Rendered "filial."

Chunk 0005

  • [0428D] printed Sed eum mutabilitas nulla erit — eum is a misprint for cum ("when"). Rendered "But when."
  • [0429D] printed gentes peccata sua humiliter confidentes — confidentes ("trusting") does not construe with peccata sua; likely confitentes ("confessing/acknowledging"). Rendered "humbly confessing their sins." (Left the participle's syntax as printed in English, matching the Latin's loose grammar.)
  • [0430D] printed qui rursum eam ea, quae agere bona videbantur, deserunt — eam ea is a dittography. CORRECTED 2026-07-28 (Pattern 7 + Pattern 11): the earlier rendering silently dropped the printed eam altogether and rendered only ea quae — a silent emendation. Both printed words are now rendered and the anomalous member is marked: "when they abandon [d: it] those good things which they seemed to do." NOTE for future sweeps: Pattern 11 says the marker wraps the REPEATED occurrence, not the original; here the intrusive member (eam) comes FIRST, so the marker sits on the leading word. Conjecture (NOT applied): ea quae.
  • [0432B] printed verax in exhibend — exhibend is a truncated misprint for exhibendo ("in fulfilling/performing"). Rendered "truthful in fulfilling."

0424B — added-negative (migne, high confidence)

Printed: Illis mundus crucifixus est, et ipsi mundo, quia per humilitatem mundum contemnunt, et per humiliationem a mundo contemnuntur. Istis vero mundus quidem crucifixus non est; ipsi tamen mundo sunt crucifixi, quia gloriam humanam, quam foris non quaesitam accipiunt, intus per virtutem humilitatis oblatam contemnunt.

Conjecture: The negation has migrated to the wrong limb of the Pauline pair (Gal. VI, 14). Read: 'Istis vero mundus quidem crucifixus est; ipsi tamen mundo non sunt crucifixi.'

Hugh has just defined the second class as those 'qui licet coram hominibus foris despicabiles non appareant, intus tamen humilitatis meritum coram Deo inviolatum conservant' — they are precisely NOT despised by the world, so they cannot be the ones 'mundo crucifixi.' And the causal clause the sentence itself supplies proves the other half: 'quia gloriam humanam ... intus per virtutem humilitatis oblatam contemnunt' — they despise the world's glory, which in the immediately preceding sentence is exactly what makes the world crucified to a man ('per humilitatem mundum contemnunt' = 'mundus crucifixus est'). The printed text therefore denies the one thing its own quia-clause establishes and asserts the one thing the preceding definition excludes.

0429D — sense-reversing-letter (migne, low confidence)

Printed: et gentes peccata sua humiliter confidentes, Deique justitiam praeferentes assumpsit.

Conjecture: confidentes for confitentes ('humbly confessing their sins'); the English silently renders the emended word, and its broken syntax betrays the repair.

The clause is the antithesis of the Jews in the same sentence, 'qui ... in sua justitia praesumebant' and 'humilem Christi adventum superbe contempserunt.' To be 'confident in one's sins' is presumption, the very posture for which the Jews are here cast off; the contrasting term must be confession, as 'humiliter' and 'Deique justitiam praeferentes' both require.
