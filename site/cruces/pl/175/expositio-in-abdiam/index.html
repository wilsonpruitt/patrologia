Expositio in Abdiam

Hugh of Saint Victor — read the work →

These are the edition's working notes, published as they were written. They record, work by work, where Migne's plate is defective or the reading uncertain: the printed form, what the English does with it, and why. They are not a groomed apparatus. Where a note argues with itself, withdraws an earlier decision, or leaves a question open, that is the record of how the reading was actually reached, and it is left standing — a conjecture that is visible can be checked, and one that has been tidied away cannot.

The English never silently repairs the page. A construable but wrong reading is rendered literally and the conjecture is recorded here, never applied to the text. How the English is produced and checked →

Work-wide conventions (reconciled at merge)

  • Sense labels normalized per Pattern 9. Migne prints LITTERAL., MORAL., MORALIS., ALLEGORICE., ALLEGOR.; the English prints LITERAL. / MORAL. / ALLEGORICAL. throughout. The two batches disagreed on the third label (ALLEGORICALLY. vs ALLEGORICAL.) and were unified to the adjective at merge, so that the three labels read as one series; the Latin column preserves Migne's own variation, which is what Pattern 9 relies on.
  • The prophet's name is rendered Obadiah in running prose. Abdias stands verbatim wherever it is inside a Latin string (frontmatter incipits, [n:] contents, carried type).
  • Auditum audivimus = "We have heard a hearing," not "a report" — Hugh's gloss at 0375A–B turns on the phrase being an un-Latin Hebraism (locutio quidem minus est Latina, sed ex Graeco fideliter expressa), so an idiomatic English would destroy the thing being glossed.

Carried type — marked [sic: …] in the English

### 0374A (chunk 0001) — in cordo for in corde Printed: dum virus fovit in cordo latitans. Carried, [sic: cordo]. cordo is no form of cor, cordis (dat./abl. cordi / corde); the only Latin word it could be is the ablative of the adjective cordus ("late-born"), which is unconstruable here. Intended: in corde, "in his heart" — near-certain. Because there is nothing renderable, it is carried rather than translated (Pattern 12's "real word merely wrong" exemption does not reach it).

### 0375B (chunk 0001) — sifignicat for significat Printed: Inculcatio vero verbi intentionem sifignicat eloquii. Carried, [sic: sifignicat]. Metathesis/foul type for significat. The sentence is the verb, so the English reads "The repeating of the word, however, [sic: sifignicat] the intention of the utterance."

### 0378A (chunk 0002) — irerverberate for irreverberate Printed: dum aciem mentis in ipsum veritatis radium irerverberate figunt. Carried, [sic: irerverberate]. Adverb irreverberate, "unblinkingly," modifying figunt — the eagle-topos of gazing at the sun without flinching.

### 0380C (chunk 0003) — invaseres for invasores Printed: Deinde eosdem invaseres, illusores, insidiatores nominat. Carried, [sic: invaseres]. The correct invasores stands eleven lines later in the same chunk (0380D, invasores per obedientiam), which is the evidence that this is compositor's type and not a variant form.

### 0386A (chunk 0004) — laetitio for laetitiae Printed: Relinquamus ubique signa laetitio nostrae (Wisd. 2:9). Carried, [sic: laetitio]. Note: the run sits inside an italic scripture lemma, so per Pattern 10 it takes no second italic marker; the [sic: …] content is bare.

### 0387B (chunk 0005) — influmine for in flumine (split/run-together type) Printed: qui convertis mare in aridam, ut influmine sicco pertranseant pede. Carried, [sic: influmine], per Pattern 10 (word-division defect; the whole run is carried, including the real word in). The carried run stands where the prepositional phrase stands: "so that they pass over [sic: influmine] with dry foot."

### 0389A (chunk 0005) — in humanitatem for inhumanitatem Printed: Historialiter ostendit in humanitatem Idumaeorum, et crudelitatem eorum. Carried, [sic: in humanitatem], per Pattern 10 (split type; in stays inside the run). The paired crudelitatem immediately following makes inhumanitatem ("inhumanity") certain — and note that the split reading inverts the sense into "humanity," which is exactly the class of damage Pattern 7 exists to expose rather than repair.

Rendered literally, no marker (real words, wrong readings)

### 0376D (chunk 0002) — vanitatem for unitatem Printed: Per haec enim, quae distinguo, personarum proprietatem ostendo, non naturae vanitatem confundo. Rendered as printed: "I do not confound the emptiness of nature." The argument requires unitatem — the sentence sits between "lest he confound the trinity of the persons, or divide the unity of the divine essence" and "as the Trinity is one in nature or essence." A Trinitarian theologian does not concede an emptiness of the divine nature. High-value defect: this is a doctrinal statement inverted by one letter, and the literal English keeps the witness intact.

### 0377D (chunk 0002) — exsultantem for exaltantem Printed: per exsultantem solium, praedicatio malitiae — but the lemma four lines above (0376D–0377A) and the parallel gloss at 0385C both read exaltantem solium ("that exaltest thy throne," Obad. 3). Rendered as printed, "by the throne exulting." The gloss-list otherwise tracks the lemma word for word, so this is a slip in the gloss, not a variant lemma.

### 0380D (chunk 0003) — subtilitatis … praedicationis Printed: In linteo sindonis, subtilitatis designatur praedicationis. The sentence has no nominative subject for designatur; sense requires subtilitas. Rendered with the printed double genitive: "In the cloth of fine linen is designated of the subtlety of preaching." Deliberately awkward — the doubled "of" is the defect, and smoothing it would hide a case error inside an otherwise unremarkable Gregorian borrowing (Prov. 31:24).

### 0381C (chunk 0003) — induxerunt without an object Printed: Haeretici raptores spirituales terram sanctam invadentes, et Scripturas canonicas pervertentes induxerunt; linguas suas acuerunt… The verb governs nothing. Rendered "have brought them in," supplying only the pronoun English requires. Possibly a dropped noun (errores? schismata?) or a compositor's transposition; nothing in the sentence lets one choose. Flagged for a human eye.

### 0382A (chunk 0003) — cum for eum Printed: Cui hoc Dominus dedit, quando cum talem, judicio fieri permisit, etc. The parallel at 0377C reads dedi te, hoc est justo judicio fieri permisi, i.e. "I permitted him to become such." Rendered with the printed cum as a preposition: "when with such a one he permitted it to come to pass by judgment."

### 0382A (chunk 0003) — lemma variants expellit / deducet Printed: Superbia expellit te … Quis me deducet in terram? against the lemma as quoted at 0376D–0377A and 0385C: Superbia cordis tui extulit te … Quis me detrahet in terram? — and detrahet returns three lines later at 0382A itself ("Quis me detrahet in terram? Dicere istud…"). Both printed readings rendered as they stand ("Pride driveth thee out"; "Who shall lead me down"). Whether Hugh is quoting loosely from memory here or Migne's compositor drifted cannot be settled from the plate.

### 0383A (chunk 0003) — unconstruable clause on the Fathers Printed: quorum nonnulli cum eis foedus iniere pacem, aliquando tenuere panem quoque cum eis comedere. foedus iniere pacem has two accusatives and no conjunction; tenuere … comedere has no construction. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow: "some of whom entered into covenant with them, peace they sometimes held, to eat bread also with them." The sense wanted is plainly the three Obadiah terms Hugh has been working (covenant / peace / eating bread) applied to Augustine among the Manichaeans. A verb has probably dropped out. Flagged.

### 0385B (chunk 0004) — corruptibilis for contemptibilis Printed: parvulus est, quia momentaneus; corruptibilis, quia inhonestus, in the middle of a rhetorical chain that otherwise pairs parvulus with contemptibilis nine consecutive times (0385B–0385C), following the lemma "Ecce parvulum dedi te … contemptibilis tu es valde." Rendered as printed ("corruptible, because dishonorable").

### 0384A–0384B (chunk 0004) — the vice list and the Canaanite-nations list disagree The catalogue at 0384A assigns contra mundum, avaritia; contra negotium acedia (fourth = avarice, fifth = sloth); the interpretation of the seven names at 0384B assigns per quartum acedia; per quintum avaritia (fourth = sloth, fifth = avarice). Both rendered as printed. The etymologies favor the second list (Amorrhaeus "embittered" → acedia; Gergesaeus "driving out the settler" → avarice), so the first list is likelier the slip — but Hugh hands the "fitting of each one" to the reader in the very next sentence, so he may simply not have checked. No emendation attempted.

Translation conventions fixed for this work (for the merge)

  • Lemma rendering. Obadiah is translated from Migne's Latin throughout, never from a conventional English version. In particular Auditum audivimus a Domino is rendered "We have heard a hearing from the Lord," because Hugh's gloss at 0375A–B turns on the phrase being an un-Latin Hebraism ("locutio quidem minus est Latina, sed ex Graeco fideliter expressa"). "We have heard a report" would silently destroy the comment.
  • No guillemets anywhere. Migne marks every quotation in this work by italics, never by « ». The English carries zero guillemets, matching the Latin twin 1:1 (Pattern 6); quoted lemmata repeated inside the gloss are set unitalicized exactly where Migne sets them unitalicized.
  • Sense labels. LITTERAL. → LITERAL.; ALLEG. → ALLEG.; MORAL. and MORALIS. → MORAL.; ALLEGORICE. → ALLEGORICALLY. (the abbreviated and spelled-out forms are kept distinct, since the variance is abbreviation, not a misprint).
  • Proper names follow the plate: Hebrom (not Hebron), Dothain, Sichem, Jezrahel, Jezabel, Idumaea, Chanaanite, Manichaeus.
  • gastrimargia is left in Latin (unitalicized, as Migne prints it) — Hugh glosses it himself with seu gula, so it is a technical term, not a defect.

<!-- corpus-polarity-sweep 2026-07-28 --> ## Corpus polarity sweep — 2026-07-28

Blind Opus read of the Latin/English pair for sense-reversing defects (one word or one letter asserting the opposite of what the context requires). 6 site(s). Migne's defects are recorded here and rendered literally in the English; our own errors were fixed in place. Full context: data/sweeps/corpus-polarity/REPORT.md.

0376D — sense-reversing-letter (migne, high confidence)

Printed: Per haec enim, quae distinguo, personarum proprietatem ostendo, non naturae vanitatem confundo.

Conjecture: non naturae UNITATEM confundo — "I do not confound the unity of nature"

The immediately preceding sentence states the very rule being applied: "Caveat lector, ne personarum confundat trinitatem, nec divinae essentiae dividat unitatem"; the next sentence continues "sicut Trinitas natura vel essentia UNA." Hugh repeats the same formula verbatim at 0392B with the right word: "Haec dicens non divinae essentiae unitatem divido, nec propria personarum confundo." "Vanitas naturae" (emptiness of nature) is not a Trinitarian category at all and asserts the opposite of the unity he is defending.

0377D — sense-reversing-letter (migne, low confidence)

Printed: Tumor praecedit superbiae, tenebrae sequuntur ignorantiae, infidelitas succedit idololatriae.

Conjecture: infidelitatI succedit idololatriA — idolatry succeeds unbelief (case endings transposed)

The very next sentence gives the sequence in fixed order and puts unbelief BEFORE idolatry: "per petram, infidelitas ignorantiae; per scissuras, schismata idololatriae; per habitantem, consensum immunditiae." As printed, the order of the fall is reversed against its own immediately following exposition.

0382A — sense-reversing-letter (migne, medium confidence)

Printed: Superbia expellit te, habitantem in scissuris petrae; qui dicis in corde tuo: Quis me deducet in terram?

Conjecture: Superbia [cordis tui] EXTULIT te — "the pride of thy heart hath lifted thee up" (Obad. 3, as printed twice elsewhere in this same work)

The lemma is quoted correctly twice in the same commentary — at 0377A/0377C ("Superbia cordis tui extulit te, habitantem in scissuris petrarum, exaltantem solium tuum") and again at 0385C. And the exposition attached to THIS occurrence expounds elevation, not expulsion: "Solium, est haeretica doctrina. Solii exaltatio, perversae doctrinae dilatatio," and the answering divine word two lines later is "Si exaltatus fueris ut aquila ... inde detraham te" — a threat that presupposes pride has raised him up, not driven him out.

0385D — dropped-word (migne, low confidence)

Printed: Hic est decalogus inobedientiae, quem in monte Sinai, quod interpretatur humilitas, Dominus dedit Moysi.

Conjecture: a lost antithetical particle, e.g. "decalogus inobedientiae, CONTRA [eum] quem in monte Sinai ... Dominus dedit Moysi"

The ten items just enumerated are "contemptum Dei ... praedicationem peccati ... odium boni ... amorem mali ... oblivionem Dei" — which God manifestly did not give on Sinai — and the very next sentence names its keepers as Pharaoh and the reprobate: "Hunc Decalogum impleverat, qui dicebat: Nescio Dominum; et Israelem non dimittam." As printed, the sentence makes the Lord the giver of the decalogue of disobedience, the opposite of what the surrounding argument requires.

0389A — dropped-word (migne, high confidence)

Printed: Historialiter ostendit in humanitatem Idumaeorum, et crudelitatem eorum adversus populum Israeliticum

Conjecture: INHUMANITATEM Idumaeorum — the word-division defect splits inhumanitas into in + humanitas

The coordinate noun in the same clause is "et crudelitatem eorum adversus populum Israeliticum," and what follows is the charge that they "not only failed him with help in the time of his distress, but even joined themselves to the fellowship of his enemies." The printed division makes the sentence say the prophet displays the Idumaeans' HUMANITY, the exact opposite of the cruelty it is coupled with. (Cf. the same accusation at 0388C: "adversus populum Israeliticum inique egit.")

0403A — dropped-word (migne, medium confidence)

Printed: Cumque huic limo profundi animus fuerit infixus et luto coinquinationis et immunditiae involutus, evelli nequaquam potest, ad illum clamet, et auxilium ejus postulet, de quo Psalmista loquitur

Conjecture: evelli nequaquam potest, NISI ad illum clamet et auxilium ejus postulet — "it can by no means be plucked out unless it cry to him and beg his help"

The Psalm verse quoted in the same sentence asserts precisely that the extraction does happen on such a cry: "Exspectans exspectavi Dominum, et intendit mihi, et exaudivit preces meas, et eduxit me de lacu miseriae, et de luto faecis." With nisi dropped, the clause flatly denies the rescue that the quotation it introduces affirms, and the two hortatory subjunctives (clamet, postulet) are left hanging with no conjunction — the mark of the lost particle.

Du bon, à bon marché — carried to its limit: the good, free.
