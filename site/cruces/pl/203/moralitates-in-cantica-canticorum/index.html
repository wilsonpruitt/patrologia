§1 · THE STRUCTURE OF THE BOOK, AND THE TYPOGRAPHY SEAM

⭐ This work changes how it marks scripture partway through, and the change is a BOOK BOUNDARY, not a printer's whim. Books I–II mark quotations with italics; Book III onward marks them with guillemets « ». The switch falls between chunks 0013 and 0014, at cols 0523/0523B — exactly at Explicit secundus tomus / INCIPIT TERTIUS TOMUS. Corpus-wide counts: 316 italic runs, 286 guillemet runs.

Do not normalize the seam in either direction. Under Pattern 6 a « » is a mark Migne PRINTS and is reproduced 1:1; a " " is ours. Two things look like exceptions and are not:

  • 0013's single guillemet pair is the Sub tuam protectionem antiphon — a liturgical class Migne guillemets even inside the italic books.
  • 0017's single italic run is a printed locator (Exod. [0533D] XXVII), not scripture.

After the seam italics survive by function: words discussed as words (Eleos, Oleos, misericordia), Migne's marginal source-edition page numbers (303–313), and a lemma nested inside a guillemet quotation.

Guillemet asymmetries are Migne's and are mirrored, never balanced (Pattern 5 corollary): unclosed « at 0532C–D, 0565A, 0579D; stray » at 0568D; unopened » at 0572D and 0557C. A 270-word run at 0569A–C swallows Philip's own commentary inside Migne's quotation marks; reproduced as printed.

§2 · WHAT THE MERGE CHANGED

1. tomus = "Tome"; liber = "book". Ruled here, swept 16 places. The seven divisions of the work are tomi; the Song itself is the liber. Five agents collapsed both to "book"; one used "Tome"; that one was right, and was itself inconsistent (Tome at 0019, tome at 0020). ⚑ The decisive text is 0019 @0541C: Ad quartum tomum exponendum hujus libri accedentes. Rendered with one English word that becomes "the fourth book of this book". Migne's own gloss at 0005 settles the sense — Explicit primus tomus, id est primum volumen — a division of a work, not the work. Capitalized as a locator per Pattern 2. Also normalized in the same sweep: INCIPIT = "HERE BEGINS", EXPLICIT = "Here ends" (one agent had "BEGINNETH"/"endeth").

2. Pattern 3, "Holy Scripture" — 11 instances swept across FOUR agents' ranges. The English carried 6 × "divine Scripture", 3 × "divine Scriptures", 2 × "holy Scripture" against 17 Latin instances of divina/sacrae/divinae Scriptura(e). Pattern 3 fixes the standing name as Holy Scripture, both words capitalized, and says explicitly that a capital S alone is not enough. Chunks touched: 0000, 0001, 0002, 0003, 0004, 0014, 0023, 0025, 0028.

3. candidatio — translated, not carried. Libanus candidatio interpretatur occurs at 0005 and 0009 in the same etymological construction; one agent gave "a whitening", the other kept "candidatio". Translated, because both passages argue from whiteness → purity → virginity, and the Latin left standing makes the inference unavailable to an English reader. The register rule reserves italic Latin for technical words with no clean English equivalent; candidatio is an ordinary noun and has one.

§3 · WHAT THE MERGE RATIFIED — no edit needed, do not churn

Migne's lower-case after a misplaced full stop STANDS. Eight instances (0003, 0006, 0011 ×2, 0012 ×2, 0014, 0015), and all three agents who met the construction independently preserved it — there was no drift, only a request to have it ruled. Reasoning, since a later sweep will be tempted: Pattern 9 normalizes only where English has no exponent for the Latin defect (grammatical gender, a typo'd section label). Lowercase-after-a-stop is expressible in English and expresses exactly what the plate shows, so Pattern 8 governs and both the mark and its consequence stand. Capitalizing would make the passage read as a deliberate sentence fragment — a worse misrepresentation than the lowercase.

§4 · FINDINGS NO SINGLE AGENT COULD HAVE MADE

These are the merge's own results: a divergence attested in more than one agent's range is evidence about Philip's copy of the Bible, not a local slip.

⭐ Zech. 4:14 — duo filii olei SPLENDORIS — four chunks, three agents. 0008, 0015 (×2), 0016, 0018. The Vulgate reads duo filii olei; Migne adds splendoris every single time. And Philip knows it: at 0015 @0528C he says so outright — in Isaia simpliciter oleum appellatur, in Zacharia vero cum additamento, splendoris oleum nominatur — and builds a Trinitarian exposition on the addition (Father as oil, Son as splendour, non tamen tres splendores sunt, sed splendor unus). A lemma conformed to the Vulgate here would void a whole chapter, and the consistency across three agents shows the reading is his text, not a compositor's slip on one page.

⭐ Gen. 3:19 — Terra es et in terram ibis — two chunks, two agents. 0020 and 0033, guillemeted both times, against the Vulgate's pulvis es et in pulverem reverteris. The second half of the work expounds terra (we are earth; the earth given the serpent for food; the earth that yields her fruit). On the Vulgate text those chapters would have no proof-text at all.

Cant. 1:4 — sicut pellis Salomonis, singular (Vulg. pelles). Philip raises the singular himself as a quaestio magna, says it stands nowhere else in either Testament, and resolves it a parte totum. Kept singular in all five recapitulations.

The running lemma is re-quoted against itself and both readings stand: the head of Tome VII prints ne vagari incipiam post greges, and 0575B re-quotes it as per greges. Neither was conformed to the other — the within-work merge rule reconciles our vocabulary, never Migne's printed text.

2.1 Marked [var: …]

  1. 0563A — Cant. 1:4, sicut pellis Salomonis (Vulg. sicut pelles Salomonis, plural). 3. ⛔ The Vulgate divergence list — the deliverable

Every printed quotation in 0030–0034 was read word by word against the Vulgate. 17 [var:] markers fired; the divergences that were logged but left unmarked are listed after.

Marked [var:] — material divergences

0030 (2)

  1. 0573C, Coloss. II, 9 — Migne: in ipso omnis plenitudo divinitatis inhabitat. Vulg.: in ipso inhabitat omnis plenitudo divinitatis corporaliter. The word order is inverted and corporaliter is absent. Marked because the missing word is the one Col 2:9 is normally adduced for.
  2. 0573D, Joan. I, 14 — Migne: Vidimus eum quasi unigenitum a Patre. Vulg.: et vidimus gloriam ejus, gloriam quasi unigeniti a Patre. Migne's object is Christ himself, not his glory, and unigenitum is accusative to match. Philip's argument (the topaz "shines with every colour," i.e. Christ is adorned with every virtue) rests on seeing him, so the divergence is load-bearing.
  3. 0575A, Psal. VIII, 6 — Migne adds a vocative Domine the Vulgate does not print. Marked because the added vocative turns a description into an address.

0031 (6)

  1. 0575A–B, the lemma itself — the chapter head and the first re-quotation print Cant. 1:6, ne vagari incipiam post greges sodalium tuorum; the second re-quotation, twenty words later, prints Ne forte vagari incipiam per greges sodalium tuorum. ⚠ This is the range's most important reading: the author re-quotes his own lemma with a different preposition and an added forte, and then expounds vagari. Both forms are rendered as printed and neither has been conformed to the other.
  2. 0575B, Matth. XVI, 13–14 — Migne: Quem dicunt homines esse hominis Filium? Responderunt discipuli: Alii Eliam, alii Jeremiam…. Vulg.: …esse Filium hominis? At illi dixerunt: Alii Joannem Baptistam, alii autem Eliam, alii vero Jeremiam…. John the Baptist is dropped from the list of mistaken identifications — and the list is precisely what the topaz/chrysolite exposition is counting.
  3. 0576A, Joan. XVII, 21 — Migne: Pater, volo ut sicut tu es in me…. Vulg.: ut omnes unum sint, sicut tu, Pater, in me, et ego in te…. Recast as a first-person volition.
  4. 0576A, Joan. XIV, 9–10 — Migne: videt et Patrem meum, non credis quia ego in Patre et Pater est in me [full stop, not a question]. Vulg.: videt et Patrem… Non creditis quia ego in Patre, et Pater in me est? Three divergences at once: meum added, plural → singular, question → statement. Punctuation follows the plate (Pattern 8): the English is a statement.
  5. 0576B, Isa. XVIII, 4 — Migne: Quiescam in loco meo, et considerabo quasi meridiana lux. Vulg.: Quiescam, et considerabo in loco meo, sicut meridiana lux clara est. Migne's word order attaches in loco meo to quiescam, and the next four sentences of commentary interrogate exactly that phrase (Quid est ergo quod dicit in loco meo quiescam?). Conforming to the Vulgate here would have destroyed the exposition.
  6. 0576B, Isa. XXVIII, 12 — Migne: Haec est requies mea, refice lapsum. Vulg.: Haec est requies mea… reficite lassum. Singular imperative for plural, and lapsum "the fallen" for lassum "the weary." The very next sentence glosses peccatorem lapsum … exhortamur — the commentary is built on lapsum, and the familiar "refresh the weary" would have left it unsupported. This is the range's clearest 7a″ trap.
  7. 0576C, Isa. LXVI, 2 — Migne: Super quem requiescam, nisi super humilem et quietum, et trementem verba mea? Vulg.: ad quem autem respiciam, nisi ad pauperculum et contritum spiritu, et trementem sermones meos? An Old-Latin/LXX-shaped form (LXX ἐπὶ τὸν ταπεινὸν καὶ ἡσύχιον). It is adduced under the heading Quae est ergo requies Dei — the divergent verb requiescam is the whole reason the verse is here.
  8. 0576D–0577A, II Cor. V, 10 — Migne: ut recipiat unusquisque prout gessit. Vulg.: ut referat unusquisque propria corporis, prout gessit.

0032 (5)

  1. 0578D, Isa. IX, 2 — Migne: Populus gentium qui ambulabat in tenebris. Vulg. has no gentium; the commentary immediately reads it as populis gentium.
  2. 0579A, Ephes. III, 17 — Migne: in cordibus nostris. Vulg.: in cordibus vestris. A person change, and the gloss that follows continues in the first person plural.
  3. 0579C, Ezech. XVIII, 23 — Migne: Nolo mortem peccatoris. Vulg.: nolo mortem impii; and the whole passage is about the peccator.
  4. 0579D, "Ezech." (ibid.) — Peccator quacunque hora ingemuerit, salvus erit is not in the Vulgate at all, at Ezekiel or elsewhere; it is the medieval adage drawn from Ezech. XXXIII, 12. Marked, because a reader who checks the citation will find nothing there.
  5. 0580A, Heb. VI, 7 — Migne: Terra bibens desuper venientem imbrem, et fructificans opportunam, ab his, a quibus colitur, accipit benedictionem. Vulg.: Terra enim saepe venientem super se bibens imbrem, et generans herbam opportunam illis a quibus colitur, accipit benedictionem a Deo. Note opportunam is left with no noun to agree with — rendered "what is seasonable," not silently supplied with "herb."
  6. 0580C, I Joan. II, 15 — Migne prefixes Filioli (from I Joan. II, 12/18) and prints si quis dilexerit mundum for Vulg. si quis diligit mundum.

0033 (5)

  1. 0580D, Gen. III, 19 — Migne: « Terra es, et in terram ibis. » Vulg.: pulvis es, et in pulverem reverteris. ⚠ The single most important divergence in the range. The entire second half of the seventh book is an exposition of terra — "we ourselves are earth," the earth given to the serpent for food, the earth that yields her fruit. On the received Vulgate text the argument has no proof-text at all. A conformed "dust thou art" would have been smooth, correctly cited, verifier-clean, and would have destroyed the chapter.
  2. 0581A, Eccle. VII, 30 — Migne: Deus fecit hominem simplicem et rectum. Vulg.: quod fecerit Deus hominem rectum. The gloss immediately splits the two words (simplicem, ut malum nunquam faceret, rectum autem, ut…), so simplicem cannot be dropped.
  3. 0581C, Joan. XVII, 3 — Migne: ut cognoscant te, et quem misisti Filium tuum unum et verum Deum esse. Vulg.: ut cognoscant te solum Deum verum, et quem misisti Jesum Christum. A doctrinally loaded recasting: Migne's form predicates "the one and true God" of the Son. Rendered as printed.
  4. 0582A–B, Psal. CI, 26 — Migne: Et tu in principio, Domine, terram fundasti. Vulg.: Initio tu, Domine, terram fundasti. The next sentence turns on it: Filium autem Dei Propheta hic principium appellat.
  5. 0582C, Isa. XXVIII, 21 — Migne: Opus ejus alienum fuit ab eo, ut faceret opus suum, prius fecit opus alienum. Vulg.: ut faciat opus suum, alienum opus ejus: ut operetur opus suum, peregrinum est opus ejus ab eo. Philip himself calls the words valde obscura and says they cannot easily be found in the other prophets — which is a fair description of a text that is not the Vulgate's.

4. Textual cruces — printed readings rendered literally

  1. 0575D (0031), quamvis. Tu me deprecaris quamvis remota omni ambiguitate te certificem ubi… — quamvis ("although") will not construe with the ut-sense the sentence needs; ut is the obvious conjecture. Rendered "that, although with all ambiguity removed I should assure thee where…", i.e. as printed. No emendation.
  2. 0575D (0031), pascor for pascam. Christ's answer prints the passive in meridie pascor et accubo where the lemma, the question, and the parallel answer in 0032 (in meridie pasco et accubo, col. 0578A–B) are all active. Rendered "at midday I am fed and lie down" in 0031 and "at midday I feed and lie down" in 0032 — the two are deliberately different because the plate is. Do not harmonize them at merge.
  3. 0576B (0031), aurae. quare se comparat meridianae luci plus quam aurae vel alteri horae diei? — in a list of hours of the day, aurorae ("dawn") is the near-certain intention; aura ("breeze") is a real word and is rendered as printed ("the breeze"), per Pattern 7 and the requiescere faciam boundary. No marker (a real word merely wrong is not [sic:] matter).
  4. 0578A (0032), Respondeat ergo justum est Christus. An unconstruable run: an impersonal justum est dropped inside the jussive. Rendered "Let Christ therefore answer — it is just — to holy Church"; conjecture Respondeat ergo (justum est) Christus or Respondeat ergo ut justum est.
  5. 0578D–0579A (0032), et sicut coeperunt. …in cordibus eorum est plantata et radicata, et sicut coeperunt in se Christum habere habitatorem — sic is expected. Rendered "and as they began to have Christ as an indweller."
  6. 0575C (0031), eam for cultum. cultum solius divinitatis ab omnibus sanctis removeat, et eam soli Deo … attribuat — feminine pronoun for a masculine antecedent (adorationem understood). Pattern 9: gender has no English exponent, "attributes it"; no marker, no churn at merge.
  7. 0581A (0033), postquam for per quam. rectitudinem mentis quam a Deo acceperat, et postquam ipsi soli placere debuerat … intorsit — per quam is the sense required. Rendered "and after which he ought to have pleased him alone," as printed. The whole period is anacoluthic (the quia clause never reaches its verb before juxta Dei dispensationem actum est takes over) and is rendered as printed, including the shift to possit at the end.
  8. 0583C (0033), insensibile spiraculum vitae. Rendered "the insensible breath of life." Odd of Gen. 2:7; possibly insensibiliter or insensibili. Rendered as printed, no marker.
  9. 0583D–0584A (0033), redemit without an object. Filius ejus Dominus Jesus Christus sanguine suo redemit — the object is supplied in square brackets, "redeemed [thee]," on the ~95-instance corpus precedent for supplied English words.
  10. 0584D (0034), refertur. Daniel propheta … memoria abundantiae suavitatis Spiritus sancti refertur … Christum montem interpretatur — refertur cannot be "is reported" here, because the sentence's finite verb is interpretatur; read as refertus (est) / refercitur, "is filled." Rendered "is filled with the memory of the abundance of the sweetness of the Holy Spirit." Conjecture logged, no emendation and no marker (a real word merely wrong).
