⟦q0.1|1+2⟧ [1260] THE EPISTLE OF PAUL THE APOSTLE TO THE PHILIPPIANS.

⟦q0.1|3⟧ Paul and Timothy, servants of Jesus Christ, to all the saints in Christ Jesus who are at Philippi, with the bishops and deacons: grace to you and peace from God our Father and the Lord Jesus Christ.

⟦q0.1|4⟧ Here, as writing to men of equal honor, he does not set down his dignity — that is, his apostleship — but has used the name common to all. ⟦q0.1|5⟧ A servant, he says, of Jesus Christ. ⟦q0.1|6⟧ «To the saints in Christ Jesus.» ⟦q0.1|7⟧ For there were Jews also who called themselves saints; but, To those in Christ Jesus, he says. ⟦q0.1|8+9+10⟧ «With the bishops and deacons.» ⟦q0.1|8+9+10⟧ Why ever does he write here to the clergy, though this is not his custom? ⟦q0.1|8+9+10⟧ Because it was the clerics themselves who had sent Epaphroditus with the supplies. ⟦q0.1|8+9+10⟧ «With the bishops.» ⟦q0.1|11⟧ Not because there were many bishops in one city; rather, he calls the presbyters bishops. ⟦q0.1|12⟧ For at that time they still shared the names in common, and the bishops were called deacons and presbyters, and conversely the presbyters, bishops.

⟦q0.1|13+14⟧ CHAPTER 1. ⟦q0.1|15⟧ Thanksgiving for the virtue of the Philippians, and a prayer for their perfecting.

⟦q0.1|16⟧ I thank my God upon all my remembrance of you, always in every supplication of mine for you all, making the supplication with joy for your fellowship in the Gospel, from the first day until now.

⟦q0.1|17⟧ Since they had advanced in virtue, he remembered them with thanksgiving toward God. ⟦q0.1|18⟧ «Upon all my remembrance of you.» ⟦q0.1|19+20⟧ Whenever I remember you, he says, I give thanks; and I remember always. ⟦q0.1|21⟧ «With joy.» ⟦q0.1|22⟧ For it is possible also to remember with grief, in the case of those who are not virtuous. ⟦q0.1|23⟧ «For out of affliction, he says elsewhere, and anguish of heart I wrote to you.» ⟦q0.1|24⟧ But to remember with joy was a sign of their virtue. ⟦q0.1|24⟧ «Making the supplication.» ⟦q0.1|25+26+27⟧ And making the supplication for your fellowship — that is, your almsgiving. ⟦q0.1|25+26+27⟧ For what, he says, do I thank God? ⟦q0.1|25+26+27⟧ For your fellowship. ⟦q0.1|25+26+27⟧ Something great and apostolic he attests of them. ⟦q0.1|28⟧ For, he says, from the time you believed until even now, you do everything so as to become fellows of the Gospel. ⟦q0.1|29⟧ As though always present with me, he says, by sending what [1261] met the need. ⟦q0.1|30⟧ «Fellowship.» ⟦q0.1|31⟧ What fellowship? ⟦q0.1|32+33⟧ That from the first day on which you believed, he says, until now. ⟦q0.1|34+35⟧ «For your fellowship in the Gospel.» — [ΦΩΤ.] As for the words From the first day: either, From the first day from which you believed until now I thank my God for you all, making the supplication with joy; or, For your fellowship in the Gospel, ministered from the first day until now. ⟦q0.1|36⟧ And what follows seems rather to agree with the former construal. ⟦q0.1|37⟧ For how were you giving thanks from the beginning, when you had not yet seen them confirmed in the fellowship? ⟦q0.1|38⟧ Trusting, he says, that he who had implanted a beginning so eager and brimming with warmth would himself also carry it forward to the perfection of the work.

⟦q0.1|39⟧ And trusting in this very thing, that he who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ; even as it is right for me to think this on behalf of you all, because I have you in my heart, both in my bonds and in the defense and confirmation of the Gospel, you all being fellow-partakers with me of the grace.

⟦q0.1|40⟧ And I thank God for your fellowship, trusting this: that he who has begun to do a good work in you will both complete it and bring it to fullness until the consummation. ⟦q0.1|41⟧ And see how he has referred everything to God, that they may not think highly of themselves; yet even so it is great praise that they were able at all to draw God to work in them. ⟦q0.1|42⟧ For this would not have come about without the greatest virtue. ⟦q0.1|43⟧ «Until the day of Jesus Christ.» ⟦q0.1|44⟧ For not about you alone, he says, am I confident, but also about those who shall come from you. ⟦q0.1|45⟧ «Even as it is right.» ⟦q0.1|46⟧ Trusting this very thing, even as it is right — that is, since it is right for me, who know exactly the things that concern us [lat: the Latin gives *quae apud vos sunt*, the things that are with you], to think this on behalf of you all. ⟦q0.1|47⟧ What? ⟦q0.1|48⟧ That he who has begun a good work in you will complete it until the day of Jesus Christ. ⟦q0.1|49⟧ «To think this on behalf of you all.» ⟦q0.1|50⟧ For he thinks — and reckons — that his disciples are accomplishing things good and great, who has laid out great zeal and care upon them. ⟦q0.1|51+52+53+54⟧ Or: it is right for me to reckon this, who have such a disposition toward you that not even in my bonds do I forget you, but carry you always in my heart. ⟦q0.1|51+52+53+54⟧ And what is the cause of his holding them in his heart and beyond forgetting? ⟦q0.1|51+52+53+54⟧ Because they are, he says, fellow-partakers with me of the grace — either of the spiritual gift supplied to them through faith, which was then working in them in manifold ways; or simply of grace, that of the rebirth and adoption through baptism. ⟦q0.1|51+52+53+54⟧ Or: I said that I have you in my heart, as being fellow-partakers with me both in my bonds and in the defense and confirmation. ⟦q0.1|55⟧ And of the grace stands for: through the grace. ⟦q0.1|56⟧ For without divine grace no one is counted worthy of such fellowship. ⟦q0.1|57⟧ «Because I have you.» ⟦q0.1|58⟧ He stated the cause according to which it was right for him to think this — that God who has begun will also bring it to fullness: because I have you, he says, in my heart. ⟦q0.1|59⟧ «You in my heart.» ⟦q0.1|60⟧ O what praise, [1264] to be in the heart of Paul! ⟦q0.1|61⟧ For this was no light thing. ⟦q0.1|62⟧ And the words, «And in my bonds, and in the defense,» are greater still. ⟦q0.1|63+64+65⟧ «And confirmation of the Gospel.» ⟦q0.1|63+64+65⟧ By confirmation of the Gospel he means the trials and the afflictions and the bonds. ⟦q0.1|63+64+65⟧ How? ⟦q0.1|63+64+65⟧ Because he chose them. ⟦q0.1|66⟧ And indeed, though it was open to him not to endure them, he confirmed the resurrection and the recompense. ⟦q0.1|67⟧ For I would not, he says, have borne to endure these things, were the things preached by me not true. — [ΧΡΥΣ.] For it is not only the dead being raised, nor lepers being cleansed, but we too, being bound, who confirm the Gospel. ⟦q0.1|68⟧ How, and in what manner? ⟦q0.1|69⟧ Because, suffering countless things and not giving way, but becoming the more eager, we furnish sufficient proof that we are heralds of truth, and that a certain divine power is in us, both easing all these things and not permitting the multitude of the trials to prevail over those who preach. ⟦q0.1|70⟧ For it is not within human power to master so many hindrances. ⟦q0.1|71+72⟧ And these things are a confirmation of the Gospel not for the others only, but for our very selves; for it renders us more approved, and stronger to despise the plots against us. ⟦q0.1|73⟧ «Fellow-partakers with me of the grace.» ⟦q0.1|74⟧ And why do I attest these things to you, and why do I have you in my heart? ⟦q0.1|75⟧ Because, he says, you are fellow-partakers with me of the grace — meaning either of the apostleship, or of the afflictions. ⟦q0.1|76⟧ «For my power, he says, is perfected in weakness.» ⟦q0.1|77⟧ For they were afflicted along with him, as with a teacher and a spiritual father. ⟦q0.1|78⟧ And see how he did not say, partakers, but «fellow-partakers», showing that he himself also is a partaker. ⟦q0.1|79⟧ For he himself says elsewhere, «That I may become a partaker of the Gospel» — that is, that I may share in the good things promised to those who proclaim the Gospel.

⟦q0.1|80⟧ For God is my witness, how I long after you all in the bowels of Jesus Christ. ⟦q0.1|80⟧ And this I pray, that your love may abound yet more in knowledge and all perception, that you may prove the things that excel, that you may be sincere and without offense unto the day of Christ, filled with the fruit of righteousness which is through Jesus Christ, to the glory and praise of God.

⟦q1.1|1⟧ «For God is my witness.» ⟦q1.1|2⟧ Not as though he were disbelieved does he call God to witness, but because he both speaks the utter truth and loves them all exceedingly. ⟦q1.1|3⟧ For they were all virtuous. ⟦q1.1|4⟧ «In the bowels.» ⟦q1.1|5⟧ The intensity of his love he signified through the bowels. ⟦q1.1|5⟧ «Of Jesus Christ.» ⟦q1.1|6⟧ That is, in the love that is for Christ's sake, because you are believers. ⟦q1.1|7⟧ And he says this so that they may not suppose that they are loved by him because they sent him what he needed for his want. ⟦q1.1|8⟧ Or: that I long for you with the bowels given me by Christ. ⟦q1.1|9⟧ «In the bowels of Jesus Christ.» ⟦q1.1|9⟧ Such as are not human, nor feel anything human, but are wholly disposed according to Christ Jesus. ⟦q1.1|10+11⟧ Great indeed it is to be longed for by Paul; [1265] but "in the bowels" is greater; and "in the bowels of Jesus Christ" leaves no room for any surpassing. ⟦q1.1|12⟧ «That your love.» ⟦q1.1|13⟧ Being so loved by them, he prays to be loved yet more and more. ⟦q1.1|14+15⟧ For there is no limit to the love that is according to Christ — or he speaks also concerning the others, that they may love them too. ⟦q1.1|16⟧ «In knowledge and all perception.» ⟦q1.1|17⟧ That is: that you may not love all men indiscriminately, but with testing and knowledge; lest somehow, he says, friendship toward heretics harm you. ⟦q1.1|18⟧ For if you love in this way, he says, you are able to test what is profitable — whom you ought to love, namely the faithful, and whom not, namely the unbelievers and heretics. ⟦q1.1|19⟧ How then does he say elsewhere, "If it be possible, so far as it lies with you, live at peace with all men" [lat: the Latin column omits Εἰ δυνατόν, giving the command unconditionally — *Quantum ex vobis est, cum omnibus hominibus in pace vivite*]? ⟦q1.1|20⟧ And we say that to live at peace is one thing, and to love another. ⟦q1.1|21⟧ And by "the things that excel" he means the things that are profitable. ⟦q1.1|22⟧ «That you may be sincere.» ⟦q1.1|23⟧ That no spurious doctrine, he says, may flow into you under the guise of love. ⟦q1.1|24⟧ Sincere, that is, as toward God; without offense, as toward men. ⟦q1.1|25+26⟧ For it offends many to see a believer having friendship with an unbeliever or a heretic; and to be an offense to anyone falls under punishment. ⟦q1.1|27⟧ «Filled with the fruit of righteousness.» ⟦q1.1|28⟧ For besides rightness of doctrines, care must be taken for one's life as well. ⟦q1.1|29⟧ And he did not say simply, That you be righteous, but, That you be filled in this. ⟦q1.1|30⟧ And by the fruit he means the one that is through Jesus Christ. ⟦q1.1|31⟧ For there is a righteousness in appearance, but not according to Christ — such as that of the Greeks, who for vainglory and to please men make a show of caring for what is just. ⟦q1.1|32⟧ And if you are such, the matter will be to the glory of God, not to my glory. ⟦q1.1|33⟧ For it is not that I the teacher may be glorified, he says, that I exhort these things and pray. ⟦q1.1|34⟧ For what is being done, he says, advances to the glory and praise of God, who also works together toward this.

⟦q1.1|35⟧ CHAPTER 2. ⟦q1.1|36⟧ Narrative of his own contending manner of life, and of his eagerness.

⟦q1.1|37+38+39+40⟧ But I would have you know, brothers, that the things concerning me have fallen out rather to the advancement of the Gospel, so that my bonds have become manifest in Christ in the whole Praetorium and to all the rest, and that most of the brothers in the Lord, being confident by my bonds, are much more bold to speak the word without fear. ⟦q1.1|37+38+39+40⟧ Some indeed proclaim Christ even out of envy and strife, but some also out of good will. ⟦q1.1|37+38+39+40⟧ The one sort announce Christ out of contentiousness, not purely, thinking to bring affliction upon my bonds; but the others out of love, knowing that I am set for the defense of the Gospel. ⟦q1.1|37+38+39+40⟧ What then? ⟦q1.1|37+38+39+40⟧ Only that in every way, whether in pretext or in truth, Christ is proclaimed; and in this I rejoice, yes and I shall rejoice.

⟦q1.1|37+38+39+40⟧ It was likely that they, on learning that he was bound, were troubled, as though the preaching were hindered thereby. ⟦q1.1|41⟧ This, therefore, he says, showing that the bonds have not only been no hindrance to the Gospel, but even an advancement. ⟦q1.1|42⟧ And he states the cause. ⟦q1.1|43+44+45⟧ «That the things concerning me.» ⟦q1.1|43+44+45⟧ It was the part of one who loves, to make plain the things concerning himself. ⟦q1.1|43+44+45⟧ «So that my bonds.» [1268] For neither were my bonds, he says, which are for Christ's sake, hidden, but they have become manifest both in the Praetorium — that is, the palace — and among the rest. ⟦q1.1|46⟧ And what follows from this? ⟦q1.1|47⟧ That from it, he says, courage came to the faithful, and a way of conversion to many of the unbelievers. ⟦q1.1|48+49⟧ «Have become in Christ.» ⟦q1.1|48+49⟧ Either the bonds that are in Christ — that is, those for Christ's sake, so that the construction is by hyperbaton; or he means the bonds that have been made manifest in Christ, that is, made manifest by the good pleasure and dispensation of Christ. ⟦q1.1|50⟧ For there was nothing that Paul suffered or accomplished aright which he did not refer to Christ. ⟦q1.1|51⟧ Or: the bonds of those men are made manifest in Christ who do nothing for display, but act in such a way that those who see are benefited and glorify God who is in heaven. ⟦q1.1|52⟧ «And that most of the brothers in the Lord.» ⟦q1.1|53⟧ Since he had said that the bonds furnished courage, and the saying seemed a great one, he adds, «In the Lord,» that is, For the Lord's sake. ⟦q1.1|54⟧ «Being confident by my bonds.» ⟦q1.1|55⟧ For they took courage, being confirmed toward the faith through Paul's bonds. ⟦q1.1|56⟧ For if the preaching were not divine, he says, Paul would not have borne to be bound for it. ⟦q1.1|57⟧ This he said above also: In the confirmation, he says, of the Gospel. ⟦q1.1|58⟧ For the bonds of holy Paul are a confirmation of the preaching. ⟦q1.1|59⟧ «Much more bold.» ⟦q1.1|59⟧ For they had spoken before as well, but now more. ⟦q1.1|60⟧ «Some indeed even out of envy.» ⟦q1.1|61+62⟧ Since Paul had been arrested, many of the unbelievers, wishing to provoke Nero to anger so that he should by all means kill him, proclaimed Christ, that Nero, hearing the preaching sown abroad, might put to death Paul the author of it. ⟦q1.1|63⟧ Or: that they themselves, wishing to be honored (for great was the honor of Paul), even being unbelievers, proclaimed Christ. ⟦q1.1|64⟧ «But some also out of good will.» ⟦q1.1|65⟧ For faith's sake, for love's sake, out of a good mind and a good purpose. ⟦q1.1|66⟧ «The one sort out of contentiousness,» out of rivalry and envy and strife. ⟦q1.1|67⟧ «Not purely.» ⟦q1.1|68⟧ That is, not from a pure heart, supposing by this to bring him into greater danger and affliction, according to the things written above. ⟦q1.1|69⟧ «Thinking to bring affliction.» ⟦q1.1|70+71⟧ For it did not turn out as they supposed; for I rejoiced the more at these things. ⟦q1.1|72⟧ «But the others out of love.» ⟦q1.1|73⟧ But the others, he says, out of the love toward the Lord and toward me. ⟦q1.1|74⟧ «Knowing, he says, that I am set for the defense of the Gospel.» ⟦q1.1|74⟧ They know, he says, that I have been charged by God to preach the Gospel, and that I shall render account for this; and it lightens for me, he says, the account concerning it, that many are catechized and that the preaching has run its course. ⟦q1.1|75⟧ Knowing this, therefore, he says, those men, in order to lighten for me the account to be rendered to God, catechize many, preaching the word. ⟦q1.1|76⟧ What then? ⟦q1.1|77⟧ Either, For what else, he says, but this? — as though to say, What else, but that I am set for the defense of the Gospel? ⟦q1.1|78⟧ Or, Why, he says, do I speak at length? ⟦q1.1|78⟧ Or, What concern is it of mine, he says, with what aim a man preaches, so long as he preaches rightly? ⟦q1.1|79⟧ «Whether in pretext,» because of envy toward me, «or in truth Christ is proclaimed.» ⟦q1.1|80⟧ From these words certain foolish men have supposed that the Apostle [1269] gave entry to all the heresies, by saying, «Whether in pretext or in truth Christ is proclaimed.» ⟦q1.1|81⟧ But do you attend. ⟦q1.1|82⟧ First, he did not say, Let him be proclaimed, that this might be taken for laying down a law, but, Is proclaimed, narrating what was happening. ⟦q1.1|83⟧ Then, even if he had spoken as one laying down a law — for many of the heretics also falsified the Scripture, as though it read, Let Christ be proclaimed — not even so has he given entry to the heresies. ⟦q1.1|84⟧ How?

⟦q2.1|1⟧ Because these men, of whom he speaks, were not preaching spurious doctrines; rather they preached the doctrine rightly, but with an evil mind, as wishing either to be honored or to cast the Apostle into greater danger. ⟦q2.1|2⟧ But the heretics, besides having an evil mind as well (for the greater number have this too), also introduce spurious doctrines: some not confessing that he who was crucified is God; others saying that there are two hypostases after the union; others that he did not take flesh consubstantial with us; others that the Son is less than the Father, in that he is and is understood to be God; and others telling monstrous tales of a confusion in the holy Trinity. ⟦q2.1|3⟧ «And in this I rejoice.» ⟦q2.1|4⟧ Those men, he says, do these things to grieve me, but I rejoice. ⟦q2.1|5⟧ Why do I say, I rejoice? ⟦q2.1|6⟧ I shall rejoice, he says, with the eternal joy.

⟦q2.1|7⟧ For I know that this shall turn out for me unto salvation through your supplication and the supply of the Spirit of Jesus Christ, according to my earnest expectation and hope, that in nothing I shall be put to shame, but with all boldness, as always, so now also Christ shall be magnified in my mouth [var: Gk σώματί μου, "in my body"], whether through life or through death.

⟦q2.1|8⟧ «This.» ⟦q2.1|8⟧ Either his being bound, or Christ's being preached by them, or even his being plotted against by them. ⟦q2.1|9⟧ «Unto salvation.» ⟦q2.1|10⟧ And by salvation he means the martyrdom that is all but come [lat: the Latin construes the phrase the other way — *quod nondum pateretur martyrium*, that he was not yet suffering martyrdom]. ⟦q2.1|11⟧ «Through your supplication.» ⟦q2.1|12⟧ See his humility: having salvation owed to him from countless right achievements, he says, through your supplication. ⟦q2.1|13⟧ For if, he says, I am well supplied with your prayers, there will be for me an abundance and, as it were, an increase of spirit. ⟦q2.1|14⟧ «According to the expectation.» ⟦q2.1|15⟧ By earnest expectation he means the vehement and strained hope, which a man watches and scans about for, craning his very head. ⟦q2.1|16⟧ «And my hope.» ⟦q2.1|17⟧ From these words he teaches that one must not take courage in the prayers of others alone, unless one contribute something also from oneself. ⟦q2.1|18⟧ Accordingly, he says, I too contribute my hope, than which nothing is greater. ⟦q2.1|19⟧ «For hope, he says elsewhere, does not put to shame.» ⟦q2.1|20⟧ And, «Let thy mercy, O Lord, be upon us, according as we have hoped in thee.» — «That in nothing I shall be put to shame.» ⟦q2.1|21⟧ Because, he says, the plotters shall not prevail; for if they kill me, then most of all have I prevailed. ⟦q2.1|22⟧ «But with all boldness.» ⟦q2.1|23⟧ That is, plainly, openly, not in a veiled manner. — «And now Christ shall be magnified.» — [ΦΩΤ.] Always was Christ magnified in Paul: both when he gave him over to be worn down by bonds and by scourges, and when he drew him out and ransomed him. ⟦q2.1|24⟧ For through the former the preaching was confirmed and made strong, since those who saw [1272] Paul's bonds and afflictions reckoned that, if his preaching were not something divine and wholly true, he would not have given himself over rejoicing to countless evils, when it was open to him to stop and be rid of them. ⟦q2.1|25⟧ Therefore with reason was Christ magnified, the preaching being confirmed. ⟦q2.1|26⟧ And again, when he drew him out and snatched him from the dangers, he was magnified from that very thing. ⟦q2.1|27⟧ For if the grace that delivered Paul were not something divine and above man, how were the bonds loosed? ⟦q2.1|28⟧ How were the doors opened? ⟦q2.1|29⟧ How had those countless wonders come to pass? ⟦q2.1|30⟧ Christ, then, bore witness to Paul by the things in which he delivered him from the dangers; and Paul in turn, by the things in which he endured in the trials, bore witness to the preaching; and through both Christ was magnified. ⟦q2.1|31⟧ So then he says now also: Whether Christ delivers me from death, or does not, he shall be magnified in me. ⟦q2.1|32⟧ For I, both while living, shall not stand off from the boldness that is for the preaching; and if I must die, with this same boldness shall I die. ⟦q2.1|33⟧ «In my body.» ⟦q2.1|34⟧ Those men, by proclaiming Christ out of contentiousness, thought to destroy him. ⟦q2.1|35⟧ But it shall not be so, he says; rather Christ shall be magnified in my body, as one who always delivers me, as is his custom, and ransoms me. ⟦q2.1|36⟧ Then, since having escaped the first defense he was afterwards put to death, Christ, he says, is magnified. ⟦q2.1|37⟧ «Whether through life.» ⟦q2.1|38⟧ Because he snatched me out of the mouth of Nero. ⟦q2.1|39⟧ «Whether through death.» ⟦q2.1|39⟧ Because he put sinew in me to despise death for his sake. ⟦q2.1|40⟧ And this he says, not as one about to die at once, but making them secure, that they be not troubled when this comes to pass.

⟦q2.1|41⟧ For to me to live is Christ, and to die is gain. ⟦q2.1|42⟧ But if to live in the flesh, this is for me the fruit of labor; and what I shall choose I do not make known. ⟦q2.1|43+44⟧ I am constrained by the two, having the desire to depart and to be with Christ, which is much rather better; but to remain in the flesh is more needful for your sake. ⟦q2.1|45⟧ And trusting this I know that I shall abide and continue with you all, for your advancement and joy of faith, that your boasting may abound in Christ Jesus in me, through my coming to you again.

⟦q2.1|46⟧ Elsewhere he says, But the life that I now live in the flesh, I live by the faith of the Son of God who loved me. ⟦q2.1|47⟧ This also he says now: Him I have as my life. ⟦q2.1|48⟧ For neither did he who despised the world live the worldly life, but the life of Christ, the life that Christ willed. ⟦q2.1|49⟧ And he calls dying gain, because of being with Christ and obtaining the crowns. ⟦q2.1|50⟧ «But if to live in the flesh.» ⟦q2.1|51⟧ He had said that to die is gain. ⟦q2.1|52⟧ But that no one may suppose that he is disparaging life, see what he says: «But if to live.» ⟦q2.1|53+54⟧ Since, he says, to live in the flesh is the fruit of labor — for one can use this life for the fruit of good works: For, he says, bearing fruit and increasing — since, then, living also contributes to the bearing of fruit, I do not know, nor do I make known, what I shall choose and prefer: to live, he says, and bear [1273] fruit to God, or to die and be with Christ. ⟦q2.1|55⟧ And observe that he showed himself lord of living and of not living. ⟦q2.1|56⟧ «I am constrained by the two.» ⟦q2.1|57+58⟧ Since, then, what I shall choose I do not make known, with reason am I constrained by the two — both by departing and by continuing in life. ⟦q2.1|59⟧ But, O Paul, you who so love Christ, do you doubt at all and waver as to what you ought to choose? ⟦q13.1|28⟧ As though he were saying: For your advantage I seek these things, that ye may bear fruit. ⟦q13.1|29⟧ «But I have all things.» ⟦q13.1|30⟧ By this again he makes them more eager. ⟦q13.1|31⟧ I have, he says, all things, and abound — that is, the things due for the former time also, in which ye sent not. ⟦q13.1|32⟧ «And abound.» ⟦q13.1|33⟧ That is, I count that I have received more than the things that were passed over. ⟦q13.1|34⟧ «I am filled.» ⟦q13.1|35+36⟧ All things, he says, that were owing to me; for he counts the thing a debt. ⟦q13.1|35+36⟧ I am filled, he says, having received from Epaphroditus. ⟦q13.1|37⟧ Through him they had sent. ⟦q13.1|38⟧ «An odour of a sweet smell.» ⟦q13.1|39⟧ He says that they are an odour of a sweet smell and a sacrifice acceptable to God. ⟦q13.1|40⟧ Ah, to what a place has he carried up the things that were given! ⟦q13.1|41⟧ Ye gave, he says, not to me, but to God. ⟦q13.1|42⟧ For I received the things from you, he says, as being an odour of a sweet smell and a sacrifice to God. ⟦q13.1|43⟧ For indeed incense and a rational sacrifice are offered to God alone. ⟦q13.1|44⟧ And the gifts from you, he says, prove to have been offered to God. ⟦q13.1|45⟧ «And my God shall fill up every need of yours.» ⟦q13.1|46⟧ Since he said above, «But ye lacked opportunity» — that is, Ye were in want — he now prays for them self-sufficiency, so as to be able to supply others also. ⟦q13.1|47⟧ «According to his riches, in glory.» ⟦q13.1|48+49⟧ This he said, showing that it is also easy for him to give; for he is rich. ⟦q13.1|50⟧ And he will give to you in glory. ⟦q13.1|51⟧ That is, so that the giving may advance unto his glory. ⟦q13.1|52⟧ «With Christ Jesus» [lat: the Latin column prints *Per Christum Jesum*, and its note glosses ἐν — *Nam ἐν capitur pro… id est In pro Per* — not σύν]. ⟦q13.1|53⟧ Or: he will give through Jesus Christ. ⟦q13.1|54⟧ For he who has been filled with the things needful gives thanks to him who gave.

⟦q13.1|55⟧ Now unto God and our Father be the glory unto the ages of the ages. ⟦q13.1|56⟧ Amen. ⟦q13.1|57⟧ Salute every saint in Christ Jesus. ⟦q13.1|58⟧ The brethren who are with me salute you. ⟦q13.1|58⟧ All the saints salute you, but chiefly they that are of Caesar's household. ⟦q13.1|59⟧ The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. ⟦q13.1|59⟧ Amen.

⟦q13.1|60⟧ Since he said, Through Jesus Christ he shall fill up every need of yours, he glorifies the Father also, who through the Son gives the self-sufficiency. ⟦q13.1|61⟧ «The brethren who are with me salute you.» ⟦q13.1|62⟧ For there were many with him, save that Timothy alone was like-souled, as he says above. ⟦q13.1|63⟧ Yet see the bowels of Paul, that he calls these also brethren. ⟦q13.1|64⟧ «All the saints salute you.» ⟦q13.1|65⟧ It was a sign that Paul praised the Philippians among them, and therefore they salute them. ⟦q13.1|66⟧ «But chiefly they that are of Caesar's household.» ⟦q13.1|67⟧ He braced them up by indicating that the preaching had reached even the imperial household. ⟦q13.1|68⟧ For had they not believed, they would not have saluted the Philippians. ⟦q13.1|69+70⟧ Besides these things he teaches this also: If they who are in the palaces despised all things and believed in Christ, how much more must ye who are poor do this? ⟦q13.1|71⟧ «The grace of the Lord Jesus Christ be with you all.» ⟦q13.1|72⟧ As is his custom, with a prayer he ends the Epistle now also.
