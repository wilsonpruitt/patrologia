⟦q0.1|8+9⟧ [bid:c177b]CHAPTER 33. ⟦q0.1|10⟧ That the mysteries of the Church are seven.

⟦q0.1|11⟧ *Bishop.* ⟦q0.1|12⟧ Since you seek it, I will speak in Christ according to my power. ⟦q0.1|13⟧ Seven are the gifts of the Spirit, as Isaiah says, and seven the mysteries of the Church that are wrought through the Spirit. ⟦q0.1|14⟧ And these are baptism, chrism, communion, ordination, marriage, repentance, and holy oil. ⟦q0.1|15⟧ And in brief I will make plain to thee concerning each of these also, even though earlier, in the interpretation of the divine liturgy, we spoke of them dimly, as far as there was room.

⟦q0.1|18⟧ Baptism, then, regenerates by the Spirit, since we lost the first passionless birth, and we were conceived in iniquities, [bid:c177c]and in sins, as David sings, did his mother conceive each one of us.

⟦q0.1|19+20⟧ CHAPTER 35. ⟦q0.1|21⟧ On the chrism.

⟦q0.1|22⟧ And the chrism puts in the first seal, and that which is according to the image, which we lost through disobedience; and further also the grace which we received in the soul by the divine inbreathing. ⟦q0.1|23⟧ Wherefore it also bears the power of the Spirit, and is rich in his fragrance, and is a marking and a seal of Christ.

⟦q0.1|24+25⟧ CHAPTER 36. ⟦q0.1|26⟧ On the divine communion.

⟦q0.1|27⟧ And the communion unites us to the Master himself, and we truly partake of his flesh and his blood; and since through eating we died, [bid:c177d]and were parted from paradise and from God, through the communion we take up eternal life again, and casting off corruption we are united to the Immortal, who for our sake became mortal through flesh.

⟦q0.1|28⟧ CHAPTER 37. ⟦q0.1|29⟧ On ordination.

⟦q0.1|29⟧ And ordination affords the authority and power of him who made us. ⟦q0.1|30⟧ And since none of the things that are is without him, and he himself came to lead us to well-being, having taken up this power of his from among us, he afforded it to us through his priesthood; and through it all the rites are wrought for us, and nothing is holy without a priest. ⟦q0.1|31⟧ And further, having from the beginning established us as rulers of the things that are seen, [0180] [bid:c180a]again in the present, in a better manner, he establishes us rulers through the priesthood. ⟦q0.1|31⟧ Wherefore David also says, «Thou shalt establish them rulers over all the earth.» ⟦q0.1|32⟧ For he has delivered to us the keys of heaven.

⟦q0.1|33+34⟧ CHAPTER 38. ⟦q0.1|35⟧ On marriage.

⟦q0.1|36⟧ And marriage is a gift of God's condescension, for the begetting of children, so long as this universe stands together with corruption. ⟦q0.1|37⟧ For God did not will that the irrational constitution, from a flux and filthy, should be in us; but since we died of our own will, he let the succession of the race work in this way, as also among the irrational creatures, that we might know into what we have been brought down. ⟦q0.1|38⟧ And this until the Incorruptible, who both died and rose for us, shall raise up [bid:c180b]and immortalize our nature; wherefore he himself also blesses marriage, that the first-fruits of our life may not come to be without a blessing.

⟦q0.1|39+40⟧ **63** CHAPTER 39. [ed: The plate carries the subtitle Περὶ μετανοίας, "On repentance," which our Greek source omits.]

⟦q0.1|41⟧ And repentance works our setting-upright again after the fall. ⟦q0.1|42+43⟧ And since after baptism there is no other recall, by grace and as a gift and without struggles and toils, except through conversion and tears and the declaring of one's faults and departing from evils, this great gift has been given, in which indeed the habit of the monks also is brought in, as being a pledge of repentance.

⟦q0.1|44+45⟧ [bid:c180c]CHAPTER 40. ⟦q0.1|46⟧ On holy oil.

⟦q0.1|47⟧ And holy oil has been delivered, this too as a sacred rite and a type of the divine mercy, supplied for redemption and sanctification to those who turn back from sin. ⟦q0.1|48⟧ Wherefore it also affords loosing of sins, and raises up from infirmities, and fills with sanctification. ⟦q0.1|49⟧ And all these things Jesus Christ our God delivered, and his divine disciples through him. ⟦q0.1|50⟧ For since we are twofold, of soul and of body, twofold also were these things afforded us by him, since he himself too truly became twofold for our sake, being truly God and having truly been called man, so that by the grace of the Spirit he might intelligibly sanctify our souls, and by the sensible things — water and oil, and bread and cup, and the rest [bid:c180d]that are sanctified by the Spirit — he might hallow our bodies also, and afford entire salvation.

⟦q0.1|51+52⟧ CHAPTER 41. ⟦q0.1|51+52⟧ On the resurrection, that is, the raising of bodies.

⟦q0.1|53+54⟧ He, that he may also make us perfect as he fashioned us, will further raise us up also when we die; and he will restore us with the bodies with which we lived and conducted our lives, that we may receive back what we did. ⟦q0.1|53+54⟧ Since, then, he will raise us perfect (for he fashioned nothing in vain), neither will he leave our body in corruption; for he made all things to be, and wills that nothing perish, being perfect God, and he alone truly being, and he became man like us, taking a body with a rational and intellectual soul, that he might also cleanse [0181] [bid:c181a]our rational souls, and fill them with the grace of the Holy Spirit, and make our bodies incorruptible and at the same time sanctify them. ⟦q0.1|55⟧ For this reason, then, he also gave these mysteries in a twofold manner: sensibly, as being from the things that are seen, yet accomplished by the Holy Spirit, and bestowing the divine grace on souls and bodies alike. ⟦q0.1|55⟧ And he delivered them not in words only, but also in deeds; and the Word of God, having first become flesh in himself, wrought this in his love for man, that, he being for us the first-fruits of good things, all we who are his might receive from him as from a fountain. ⟦q0.1|56⟧ For for this cause also he became man, that we might be united to him, and that we might be sanctified through him. ⟦q0.1|57⟧ Because he who fashioned us from the beginning is this same Word of God, and he again it is who refashions us, by the good pleasure of the Father [bid:c181b]and the co-working of the Holy Spirit. ⟦q0.1|58⟧ For the Trinity, God, who made the whole, also administers the whole, and most of all us who had fallen away, the most precious of things seen and according to the image of God. ⟦q0.1|59⟧ The Word of God, then, the living image and seal and radiance, takes up into himself his own image, us, the rational living creature. ⟦q0.1|59⟧ And not through some other creature does he renew us. ⟦q0.1|60⟧ Since it is not even possible.

⟦q0.1|61⟧ *Cleric.* ⟦q0.1|61⟧ And how, master, was it not possible through an angel also,

⟦q0.1|62⟧ *Bishop.* ⟦q0.1|62⟧ In no way: because every creature is circumscribed, and does not reach everywhere, and does not have by nature the power of saving.

⟦q1.1|1⟧ But by grace; [bid:c181c]and it would have been as one giving the grace through an instrument, and, being partial, not able to extend to all, nor to grant the refashioning to those who first suffered. ⟦q1.1|2+3+4+5+6⟧ But how would he afford life, not being by nature the life? ⟦q1.1|2+3+4+5+6⟧ Or how would he remain unchangeable, not being by nature unchangeable? since some of them fell away, and that though they were not even united to flesh. ⟦q1.1|2+3+4+5+6⟧ For this reason it was necessary that he himself, the one unchangeable by nature, the ever-being **64** Word, who lives always with the living Father together with the life-giving Spirit, should be united to us mortals, so that by what is unchangeable and incorruptible he might stay our corruption, and, being eternal life, might drive death away from us and implant immortality. ⟦q1.1|7⟧ And as being everywhere and eternal, he is able to save both those who died before, and those who believed in the time of his conduct of life upon earth, [bid:c181d]and those who after these things believe, for ever, through his mysteries, and through those that are his own, having his grace. ⟦q1.1|8⟧ And the same one both works for ever and reaches through many. ⟦q1.1|8⟧ Wherefore he also implanted his grace in us, that he might work through us. ⟦q1.1|9⟧ And we who have been deemed worthy of the priesthood are many, and in different places; but he is one, and in heaven and on earth, and in the sea, and in the universe, and everywhere reaching all, and working all things through all. ⟦q1.1|10⟧ Which belongs to no created nature at all. ⟦q1.1|11⟧ Wherefore neither do we work when we perform the mysteries; but we only minister, that is, we serve. ⟦q1.1|12⟧ And he works through us. ⟦q1.1|13⟧ Wherefore, though myriads of celebrants offer each day and at the same time, one is the body [0184] [bid:c184a]and blood, and one the sacrifice, and not many, for the Word of God is living and working, and everywhere, and above the universe.

⟦q1.1|13⟧ *Cleric.* ⟦q1.1|14⟧ Wonderful are the things that have been said, master, and they have put ever so great a pleasure into my soul, and I have come to know more clearly the mystery of the Word of God's becoming man.

⟦q1.1|14⟧ CHAPTER 42. ⟦q1.1|15⟧ Why the Word of God was not united to the better nature of the angels, but to that of men.

⟦q1.1|15⟧ *Bishop.* ⟦q1.1|16⟧ These things are not ours, beloved, but the Fathers'. ⟦q1.1|17⟧ And thou wouldst find them in many places, examining the discourses of these men. ⟦q1.1|18⟧ And further consider this too, which affords at once a very great and a true knowledge of the divine mystery. ⟦q1.1|19⟧ Why the Word of God, having willed [bid:c184b]to be united to his creatures, was not united to the better nature (that of the angels, I mean), which in some way stands as its own and akin toward it by reason of its immateriality, but to this our nature, the corruptible and material? ⟦q1.1|20⟧ According to reason, because it is most great and divine. ⟦q1.1|21⟧ For if he had been united to the angelic nature, it alone would have partaken of his communion, and it would not have been saved; for it is saved, but it would only have been exalted, and led up to something greater, having been deemed worthy of the communion of God. ⟦q1.1|22⟧ And all the other creation would have been profited nothing, but would have remained in corruption, and most of all man, who had fallen away, for whose sake this creation came to be, and through whom it fell under corruption. ⟦q1.1|23⟧ Consider, then, the wisdom of God, and his counsel, truly ancient, on account of which (I think) he made both the world and the angels [bid:c184c]out of things that are not, and last of all man. ⟦q1.1|24⟧ For since he is good, and for this reason brought forth the creation, that the creation might partake of his good things, for he himself, being without need and full and before the ages, would not have needed anything from among the things that are, for he himself is the highest good, since the creation of God is intelligible and sensible, and the union of the two is man [d: intelligible and sensible, and the union of the two is man] alone, consisting of an intelligible soul and a sensible body, God takes this man alone, and is united to the utmost, having made the human nature subsist in himself sinlessly from the holy Virgin, by the Holy Spirit. ⟦q1.1|25⟧ And taking a body with an intellectual and volitive soul, through the intellectual part of the soul he is united to the intellectual nature, that of the angels, and through his [bid:c184d]divine flesh to the creation that is according to sense. ⟦q1.1|26⟧ And thus the sanctification and the grace pass not into man only, who is intelligible from the soul and sensible from the body, but rightly pass also into the whole **65** creation; and the rational creation, that of the angels and of souls, is united to God, and perceives God, and has communion with him, and is made exceedingly bright, receiving in kindred fashion the divine light from the holy soul of the Word, which is intellectual and rational. ⟦q1.1|27⟧ For now angels behold that holy soul of the Saviour according to nature, and now receive more vividly the illumination from the invisible, and are filled with greater wisdom from the divine mind of the living Wisdom. ⟦q1.1|28⟧ And as in some way kindred they draw near to him, and are disposed; and they are filled with wonder; [0185] [bid:c185a]and they are the more humble-minded, having been deemed worthy of such great things; and in like manner also the sanctified souls of the saints. ⟦q1.1|29⟧ And the sensible creation, together with our bodies, through that holy and deified, or rather, so to say, co-divine flesh, sacrificed for us on the cross and risen again, and having received incorruption, had the first-fruits of immortality, and has them. ⟦q1.1|29⟧ And, according to Paul, all this creation eagerly awaits incorruption. ⟦q1.1|30⟧ Which also shall come to pass when the incorruptible and living Word, with his flesh made incorruptible, is revealed together with us at his second and most dread coming; when also the angels will be myriads, cowering and trembling before his beauty, and at the same time rejoicing in the glory which they receive from him. ⟦q1.1|31+32+33⟧ And the elements [bid:c185b]shall be altered, and the righteous shall shine forth as the sun, receiving their shining from the Sun of righteousness who has been united to us, and so shall we ever be with the Lord. ⟦q1.1|31+32+33⟧ Hast thou considered the mystery, O man, so far as there was room for us to speak of it, of the Saviour's becoming man?

⟦q1.1|31+32+33⟧ *Cleric.* ⟦q1.1|31+32+33⟧ I have considered it and have marvelled, and glory to God who wrought these things on our behalf.

⟦q1.1|34⟧ CHAPTER 43. ⟦q1.1|34⟧ That Christ also received the mysteries into himself.

⟦q1.1|35⟧ *Bishop.* ⟦q1.1|36⟧ It remains necessary to say how the Saviour wrought the mysteries in himself also. ⟦q1.1|37⟧ And first, baptism, the first of his spiritual gifts: he himself was baptized in the [bid:c185c]Jordan by John, first, not as himself needing it, for how would he who alone is pure? but for our sake, that we might be cleansed, and wash off the filth that comes from the flux and from the ancestral sin, and, having been fashioned from clay in the beginning, which is earth and water, that our body may be in place of the earth, and in holy water and Spirit we may be regenerated together with the soul, and be born a new and sinless man. ⟦q1.1|38⟧ And second, the chrism, that is, the divine oil and myron, the Saviour received in the baptism, the Spirit descending upon him in the form of a dove. ⟦q1.1|39⟧ And Isaiah, concerning this, speaks as if from Christ himself, «The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because of which he anointed me.» ⟦q1.1|40⟧ Concerning this Peter also says, [bid:c185d]That God made him both Lord and Christ, bodily, that is, the Spirit having come upon him.

⟦q2.1|1⟧ And that God anointed him with the Holy Spirit, Peter says again elsewhere. ⟦q2.1|2⟧ And Paul, «In him, he says, dwelt all the fullness of the Godhead bodily.» ⟦q2.1|3⟧ This chrism, then, is the grace of the Spirit, all of it; which, as has been said in the baptism, he received in human fashion, as was also seen by John. ⟦q2.1|4⟧ And this he had always, as God the Word, it being undivided from him; and he had it by grace also as soon as he became man, since his Spirit, connatural and undivided, was always with him, and by the Holy Spirit he constituted for himself that divine flesh; yet he showed it also at the baptism, descending upon him for our sake. ⟦q2.1|5⟧ Since the pure one also underwent baptism **66** for our sake, that, being baptized, we might [0188] [bid:c188a]receive this. ⟦q2.1|6⟧ We too, then, of necessity need this spiritual myron, and above all in baptism; and no one will be perfectly baptized who has not also received the myron. ⟦q2.1|7⟧ For by baptism he is regenerated, and is loosed from his sins by the Spirit; but he does not yet also have the pledges of grace, nor the odour of life, nor the seal of the Spirit. ⟦q2.1|8⟧ Wherefore, as it is necessary to be baptized, so there is necessity also to be anointed with the myron. ⟦q2.1|9⟧ And for this reason Peter and John laid their hands on those who had been baptized by Philip, as having received baptism only, and they received the Holy Spirit. ⟦q2.1|10⟧ Which indeed is the myron. ⟦q2.1|11+12⟧ For the laying on of hands afforded the myron, as took place also among the apostles, and through them for many others. [bid:c188b]And then it was the laying on of hands, but now, in place of the hands, it is this, the sanctified and so-called great myron, sanctified not by presbyters, but by patriarchs and bishops with sacred prayers and blessings, who are rich in the power of Peter and John; which indeed, being sent out also to the farthest bounds, has the power of the laying on of hands. ⟦q2.1|11+12⟧ And it is necessary that every believer be sealed with this at baptism, that everyone baptized may have the most divine baptism also perfect in himself. ⟦q2.1|13⟧ For if the Saviour received the Spirit when he was baptized, and those baptized by Philip received the Spirit by the laying on of the hands of Peter and John, that they might not be imperfect and unsealed by the Spirit, so also those being baptized, [bid:c188c]the faithful, ought to have been anointed with this myron in baptism, and not (as the infants of the Latins, or of some others) remain imperfect and unsealed, not receiving the grace of the Spirit, nor being marked with the marking of Christ. ⟦q2.1|14⟧ For the myron is the seal of Christ in the Spirit. ⟦q2.1|14⟧ And the priest cries this aloud when he seals with the myron, saying, «The seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit. ⟦q2.1|15⟧ Amen» Without grace, then, and unsealed for Christ is he who has not received the myron. ⟦q2.1|16⟧ And the myron is not simply oil, but that which is sanctified with prayers at the altar by the bishops of Christ, who are rich in the power of the apostles, or rather of Christ himself. ⟦q2.1|17⟧ Nor is the myron that which comes from holy relics, such as that from the body of the divine and myrrh-streaming Demetrius, [bid:c188d]or of some other of the saints, as is noised abroad also, in the land of the Cypriots, of the great martyr Mamas, or of a holy icon, as of the one called Sittē the holy, or from the oil of the lamps that hang before the holy icons or relics. ⟦q2.1|18⟧ For these things are venerable, and have divine grace, and afford healings, but not the power of that most divine myron; ⟦q2.1|27⟧ CHAPTER 44. ⟦q2.1|28⟧ That the Saviour himself performed the celebration.

⟦q2.1|29⟧ And the celebration of his all-holy body and blood, it is plain that he himself celebrated it, and has delivered it to us to celebrate. ⟦q2.1|30⟧ «For this, he says, do for my remembrance.» ⟦q2.1|31⟧ Because he himself is also a priest for ever after the order of Melchizedek (in bread, that is, and wine), in no way ceasing; and rather an eternal priest, because he both sacrificed himself [bid:c189c]willingly through the cross, and sacrifices, and offered to the Father, and brings near. ⟦q2.1|32⟧ And he lies a perpetual victim, and an eternal propitiation for us; whom also we have as a great high priest who has passed through the heavens, Jesus the Son of God; and we hold fast his confession. ⟦q2.1|33⟧ And such a high priest truly became us, holy, harmless, undefiled, separated from sinners, and made higher than the heavens, humbled for us sinners.

⟦q2.1|34+35⟧ CHAPTER 45. ⟦q2.1|36⟧ That the Saviour has been ordained both priest and high priest bodily.

⟦q2.1|37⟧ And that he has been ordained our priest and high priest, the Saviour himself made plain, being the Word of God, [bid:c189d]and made flesh for us, and both borne witness to by the Father and ordained [sic: *χειρονοτονηθείς*] by the Spirit, as Isaiah too foretold: «A rod shall rise up from the root of Jesse, and a flower shall come up from the root. ⟦q2.1|38⟧ And the Spirit of God shall rest upon him,» and what follows. ⟦q2.1|39⟧ And see the matters concerning the ordination, how he received it, this wonderful and most splendid one; though he was God by nature, and had almighty power, all-working and holy; for he is the living Word of God, holy and almighty; but nevertheless for our sake he has also been ordained bodily by the Father. ⟦q2.1|40⟧ And first in the baptism itself; for at that time, being baptized and at the same time touched by the hand, he was as one being ordained, John laying his hand on him, and receiving the Spirit; [0192] [bid:c192a]but being ordained in a special way by the Father, the Spirit descending in place of a hand, and the Father himself saying, «This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased.» ⟦q2.1|41⟧ And the second time he received the ordination more splendidly on the mount of the transfiguration, when he himself shone exceedingly with the light of the divine glory, being the Sun of righteousness; and he came to be in the midst of that luminous cloud, which signified the Spirit, which was always with him,

⟦q3.1|6+7⟧ CHAPTER 46. ⟦q3.1|8⟧ That Christ also blessed honourable marriage, and wrought it in himself through the Church in the Holy Spirit.

⟦q3.1|9⟧ And see also how he, as creator [lat: the Latin gives *prout minister*, "as minister"], established marriage and blessed it. ⟦q3.1|10⟧ For thou hast learned **68** that he came to a marriage in Cana. ⟦q3.1|11⟧ But he was also born of one betrothed to a man [lat: the Latin gives *ut homini socius permanens*, "that, remaining a companion to man," and has no betrothal], that he might sanctify our birth.

⟦q6.1|26⟧ CHAPTER 59. ⟦q6.1|27⟧ A setting-forth, part by part, of the things celebrated before baptism, and in baptism, and after baptism.

⟦q6.1|27⟧ But having also sealed water with the marking of the cross, as a prelude of the divine baptism he sprinkles the house. ⟦q6.1|28⟧ And having sealed the infant also on the forehead, on account of the mind, and on the mouth, on account of the word and the breath, and on the heart, on account of the vital power, so that it may remain guarded for the [0209] [bid:c209a]saving baptism, having thus dismissed them, **75** he departs. ⟦q6.1|29⟧ And on the eighth day the infant is brought to God by certain persons, and before the gates — for it is not yet sanctified by baptism — the priest seals it again on the forehead, and the mouth, and the breast; and gives it a name through a sacred prayer, whatever those who bore it may wish, with which it is also baptized.

⟦q6.1|29⟧ CHAPTER 60. ⟦q6.1|30⟧ That on the eighth day too the Lord received the name, being called Jesus.

⟦q6.1|30⟧ Just as this took place in the Lord also; for on the eighth day, being circumcised, he was called Jesus. ⟦q6.1|31+32⟧ But we do not have circumcision; for the Saviour has fulfilled the things of the law on our behalf, and has rescued us from the bondage of the law. [bid:c209b]And he has given another circumcision in the whole of nature, the divine baptism, which does not cut flesh, but cleanses sins. ⟦q6.1|33⟧ And each of the infants is baptized in its own name, as Jesus the Christ was. ⟦q6.1|34⟧ Not, however, are all baptized John and Mary, as some of the simpler and unlettered folk say. ⟦q6.1|35⟧ On the eighth day, then, circumcision was for the Jews. ⟦q6.1|36⟧ But now we the faithful receive the name on it, because the eighth is a renewal. ⟦q6.1|37⟧ For when the seventh is fulfilled, which this life possesses, revolving through seven of the days, it begins again on the eighth. ⟦q6.1|37⟧ Which is a sign of the resurrection, and a prelude of eternal life. ⟦q6.1|38⟧ For on the eighth the Lord also rose again, and we hope for the last day, that of eternal life. ⟦q6.1|39+40⟧ Which too shall be without end, as we are taught. [bid:c209c]On this eighth, then, the Jews were circumcised as to the males only, as being imperfect, sealed on the member of pleasure, themselves too bearing witness beforehand to the resurrection by the eighth, and signifying by being circumcised that the pleasure of the flesh shall cease at some time, and life shall be incorruptible, and not wrought through the flesh; but not understanding this, nor perceiving the true circumcision, the putting-off of sin, which is the holy baptism. ⟦q6.1|41⟧ For this teaches the being wholly incorruptible and unharmed, and not maimed in any respect; for he who is circumcised in the flesh has been maimed, and does not carry about the entireness of nature; and further, that our being born from pleasure is not passionless. ⟦q6.1|42+43⟧ Wherefore this too has been loosed, the being born from pleasure. [bid:c209d]And there shall be given to us through baptism to be born not of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of man, but of God. ⟦q6.1|44⟧ And this is to be regenerated through the washing of rebirth. ⟦q6.1|45⟧ Whence on the eighth day too the infant receives the name, just as the Saviour was called by the saving name, Jesus; and having been sealed, and having received a sacred prayer, as one brought to the Lord, and signed with the cross on the forehead, as I said, for the intellectual part, on the mouth for the rational part, and on the heart for the life-giving part, and written in the eternal book through the name that was laid upon it, it returns to the mother.

⟦q7.1|1⟧ And on the fortieth day it is brought again by the mother to the temple, and this is presented to God as a gift. ⟦q7.1|2+3⟧ For the priest, standing before the doors of the temple (for it is not lawful to [0212] [bid:c212a]enter before the prayer), and having sealed the mother together with the infant, and sanctified them with prayers, and having afforded to the mother the cleansing from the birth that is pleasure-laden and defiled — she having completed the forty days after the bearing, in which the infant too was perfected in her and began to leap; and having given her the entrance into the temple, she being until then not worthy to enter, nor to have communion with what is pure — himself taking the child into his arms, figuring the part of Symeon, who took the Lord, an infant, in his arms, cries aloud the «Now lettest thou depart,» the priest praying that the infant be loosed from sin, and see the light, Christ, the revelation of the Gentiles and the glory of the new Israel, **76** having itself also been born by the Spirit, and seeing God face to face. [bid:c212b]And if the infant has been baptized, he brings it in even into the altar, and carries it round about, as though making an obeisance too, showing that it is an offering to God; and it does obeisance to him who made it. ⟦q7.1|4⟧ But if the child has not yet been baptized, standing before the rails, and making an obeisance at the altar through the infant, and handing it over to the mother, he dismisses them. ⟦q7.1|5⟧ From this point, then, the infant is a catechumen; and the mother has received leave both to enter the temple, as having been cleansed, and to partake of the mysteries, whenever she is ready and wishes. ⟦q7.1|6⟧ And these things are so.

⟦q7.1|7+8⟧ CHAPTER 61. ⟦q7.1|9⟧ A contemplation of the things celebrated in the divine baptism.

⟦q7.1|10+11⟧ Learn now the things of the most sacred [sic: *ερωτάτου*] baptism; and first, that there is no set time of days or hours for it, lest at any time the infant should depart unenlightened. [bid:c212c]But if there be fear of death, all the things done according to the form are done at once, upon its being born; but with attention, and by a priest both reverent and sober [lat: the Latin gives *nisi quod abest oratio atque pius sanctusque sacerdos*, except that the prayer and a pious and holy priest are lacking]; and if indeed the time allows, and there is no fear of death, at once after the sacred service, that the infant may also partake of the mysteries. ⟦q7.1|12⟧ And concerning this Dionysius says that all the mysteries perfect us by the communion. ⟦q7.1|13⟧ For this is the end of all. ⟦q7.1|14⟧ And the priest ought indeed both to study the sacred prayers well and to say them, and with attention, and slowly, unless there be some necessity, reading them in the hearing. ⟦q7.1|15⟧ For we have heard from the Fathers that those who are often terrified by phantasms suffer this for this reason, because the priests who baptized them did not say [bid:c212d]the exorcisms with attention, and the other sacred prayers. ⟦q7.1|16⟧ If, then, there is leisure, one ought to say the exorcisms from the beginning, and the other prayers, not once only, but many times. ⟦q7.1|17⟧ For indeed in the Euchologion it is written that they be said eight or ten times, because there was also an ancient custom of the Church that these were said to those being baptized on each day, for seven days, by the catechizing priests, and on the eighth again by the bishop, or by the priest who was about to baptize; and so they were baptized by the bishop or the priest. ⟦q7.1|17⟧ But we have seen reverent priests saying these three times without omission, and so baptizing. ⟦q7.1|18⟧ And the most divine baptism is done thus. ⟦q7.1|18⟧ He who is about to be baptized is brought, if an infant, held by a woman, with the sponsor present; but if he is of age, [0213] [bid:c213a]alone, since he must also come of his own will, and the sponsor with him. ⟦q7.1|19⟧ For he is his surety toward Christ, that he will keep the things of the faith, and live as a Christian; and the name too shows this. ⟦q7.1|20⟧ For ἀνάδοχος, sponsor, is as it were one who takes him up into himself.

⟦q7.1|21+22⟧ CHAPTER 62. ⟦q7.1|23⟧ That the sponsor of him who is being baptized must be orthodox and pious.

⟦q7.1|24⟧ Here indeed one must also take heed to make sponsors of lovers of piety, and almost teachers of the faith. ⟦q7.1|25⟧ But I have heard of something exceedingly out of place and grievous. ⟦q7.1|26⟧ For some call persecutors and insulters of the faith, godless men and heretics, to the sponsoring (alas!) of their children, for some human reason; and these set the mystery at nought; and they do not enlighten the children, [bid:c213b]but rather darken them. ⟦q7.1|27⟧ And the priest who ministers is shown to be a partaker with those who are strangers to God. ⟦q7.1|28+29+30+31⟧ And how would the blasphemer against God teach piety? ⟦q7.1|28+29+30+31⟧ But what communion has light with darkness? ⟦q7.1|28+29+30+31⟧ Or what part has a believer with an unbeliever, as it is written? ⟦q7.1|28+29+30+31⟧ He who does such things is condemned; and so is he who receives the infants of the impious, unless these first confess that baptism is holy and divine, and cleansing of sin, **77** and regenerating in Christ, and that he who receives it is a Christian (for if this is so, the one will confess that the infant will be faithful, while he himself hides under some fear), it is good to receive and sanctify the infant, lest it should also be taken thus by God. ⟦q8.1|1⟧ For this reason, then, he stands naked. ⟦q8.1|2⟧ And further, because man became naked through sin, [bid:c216b]and lost the God-woven garment, this figure of nakedness bears witness to it. ⟦q8.1|3⟧ For since he comes to cast off the filth of sin and of unbelief, and to become wholly new, and to put on the form of the new Adam, and to return to what is better than what was before, he shows the nakedness, and bears witness to the shame; ⟦q10.1|23⟧ [bid:c225c]CHAPTER 63. ⟦q10.1|23⟧ What the Alleluia signifies.

⟦q10.1|24⟧ And this song, the Alleluia, the prophets who were inspired by the Holy Spirit used to say. ⟦q10.1|25⟧ Which indeed signifies a sojourning of God, and «God comes, and praise ye him,» having been set down and being sung prophetically, because the Lord was to come, both at his first and at his second coming, and to send his grace always among us in the Holy Spirit. ⟦q10.1|26⟧ Wherefore the Church also ever chants this song, having received Christ, and awaiting him from heaven, as we have said. ⟦q10.1|27⟧ Now the divine Dionysius says that myron is poured into the sacred font [0228] [bid:c228a]; ⟦q10.1|44⟧ CHAPTER 64. ⟦q10.1|45⟧ That «The servant of God is baptized,» differs from «I baptize the servant of God.»

⟦q11.1|1⟧ And *Is baptized*, he says, and not, *I baptize*, as the Latins, innovating even in this — the bishop bearing witness to the willingness of the one being baptized. ⟦q11.1|2⟧ For the *I baptize* does not make plain that the one being illumined is baptized willingly. ⟦q11.1|3⟧ For it is possible, even by some compulsion and against his will, for the one baptizing to baptize him [0229] [bid:c229a]of himself alone. ⟦q11.1|4⟧ But the «Is baptized,» signifies both the willingness, and that it is by me, the bishop or priest, who am already doing the work, and by the only God in Trinity, whose name too is clearly pronounced over the one being baptized. ⟦q11.1|5⟧ Thus, then, regenerated, the one baptized comes out wholly new and illumined and a son of God from the sacred font, our spiritual mother, which stands in place of the virginal pure and holy womb. ⟦q11.1|6⟧ For as Christ came from the Virgin, that he might cleanse the defiled birth, so we too from the pure font; and as there the pure blood and the holy belly of the Virgin in the Holy Spirit wrought the incarnation of the Word, so in us also the pure water of the font and the divine Spirit have accomplished the pure regeneration. ⟦q11.1|7⟧ Only, just as there, at the baptism of the [bid:c229b]Saviour, there was also a descent of the Spirit, that he might show the whole fullness of the Godhead bodily in him who was made flesh, so in us also there is the chrism of the myron after the baptism, that we too may receive the grace of the Spirit. ⟦q11.1|8⟧ «For of his fullness, he says, have all we too received, and grace for grace.»
