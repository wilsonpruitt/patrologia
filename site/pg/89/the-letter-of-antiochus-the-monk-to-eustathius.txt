⟦q0.1|1+2+3+4⟧ THE LETTER of Antiochus, monk of the Laura of Abba Sabas, to Eustathius, superior of the monastery of Attalina, of the city of Ancyra in Galatia, concerning the holy Fathers there; in which are also one hundred and thirty chapters [lat: the Latin title gives *capitula 150*, one hundred and fifty], and a prayer of confession.

⟦q0.1|1+2+3+4⟧ [1421] Since you have written to me, honoured Father Eustathius, that you have endured much affliction, exchanging one place for another and one land for another, for fear of the Chaldean storm that prevails; and that you have been put to the trial of hunger and of thirst — not of bread and of water, but, according to that which has been said, of hearing the word of the Lord; and since you are neither able to carry about with you the weight of books, nor, in the places where you sojourn, to find readily the things you seek: you charged my littleness to draw together in brief the whole of the divine Scripture, both Old and New, so that neither should the burden of the carrying become a weight to us, nor again should anything be left out of the things that tend to the profit and salvation of the soul. ⟦q0.1|1+2+3+4⟧ I yielded readily to your command, and having straightway set in order all the things sought by you out of the divine Scripture, into one hundred and thirty chapters [lat: the Latin gives *centum quinquaginta … capita*, one hundred and fifty], united to one another and yet distinguished from one another, so that the reader should not undergo confusion and jumbling — these I have sent to your God-loving reverence. ⟦q0.1|1+2+3+4⟧ Receiving them, send up to God the fitting glory and thanksgiving, to him who perfects his own power in weakness; and requite us with your acceptable prayers, that we may fulfil the remaining time of our life in a manner thankful to God. ⟦q0.1|1+2+3+4⟧ But if you should find anything, as is likely, grant pardon both to my want of learning and to the haste of the injunction.

⟦q0.1|5⟧ And since you sent this word also, honoured Father, that I should signify to you concerning the holy Fathers of our Laura [nt: The moderns among the Greeks call a notable monastery a *laura*, on account of the great throng of men who, for devotion's sake, flow together there after the manner of many waters.] — whether those things be true which you said you had heard from many, both concerning their manner of life and concerning their slaughter, things hard to believe and passing hearing — I judged it necessary first to signify to you in brief [1424] the things concerning them, and so thereafter to append the chapters of the divine Scripture.

⟦q0.1|6⟧ Now concerning their virtuous manner of life, it belongs neither to the present season nor to my tongue to tell. ⟦q0.1|7⟧ This only do I say: that they were truly divine men — if indeed one ought to call them men, and not angels — grown old from youth in ascetic discipline and in stillness and in the labours borne for godliness; aged, of priestly bearing, grey in the hair and in the understanding, good, humble-minded, orderly, temperate, true, blameless, just, God-fearing, holding themselves back from every evil thing, lacking in none of the good things, and filled full of the divine love. ⟦q0.1|8⟧ Some of them had passed beyond fifty and sixty years [lat: the Latin gives *centum excesserunt annos et quinquaginta sex*, exceeded a hundred years and fifty-six] without going out of the Laura in any way at all; and some neither went forward into the church nor had seen the city from the time they became monks — adorned with virtues of every kind, earthly angels and heavenly men. ⟦q0.1|8⟧ Whence also they carried off such an end, and bore away the prize of the victory.

⟦q0.1|9⟧ For when the Ishmaelites came upon our Laura one week before the holy city was taken, and plundered all the sacred vessels of the church, the multitude of the Fathers withdrew at once; but the steadfast servants of Christ remained behind in the Laura, not willing to leave the place. ⟦q0.1|10⟧ And the barbarians, having seized them and tortured them pitilessly for many days — supposing that they should find money with men in whose keeping was nothing of this age — at last, failing of their purpose and becoming frenzied, cut them all in pieces limb by limb. ⟦q0.1|11⟧ But the blessed ones, rejoicing with a bright countenance, gave up their souls to the Lord with thanksgiving, as having long had the desire to depart and to be with Christ. ⟦q0.1|12⟧ And when their bodies had been flung out unburied for many days, we came upon them from Arabia; and the abba Nicomedes, beholding the piteous spectacle of the Fathers, swooning away, was well-nigh — so to speak — taken up dead. ⟦q0.1|13⟧ But Modestus the most holy, coming forward and gathering up all the tabernacles of the saints, washed them, and having wept much over them and embraced their holy and steadfast relics, laid them in the tombs of the Fathers. ⟦q0.1|14+15⟧ And having performed over them the accustomed rule, he uttered over them the oracle of Isaiah which says: «Just men are taken away, and no one considers; from before the face of iniquity the just man has been taken away; his burial shall be in peace.» [n: Isa. LVII, 1] And: «The souls of the just are in the hand of the Lord, and no torment shall touch them. ⟦q0.1|16⟧ In the eyes of the foolish they seemed to have died; but they are in peace, and their hope is full of immortality; and having been chastened a little, they shall be greatly benefited, because God tried them and found them worthy of himself, and received them as a whole burnt offering of sacrifice, and

⟦q1.1|1+2⟧ [1425] in the time of their visitation they shall shine forth.» [n: Sap. III, 4-6] And having said these things concerning our holy Fathers, he exhorted us all not to forsake our place, but to endure nobly in the trials, remembering the Lord who says: «For strait is the gate and narrow the way that leads to life;» [n: Matth. VII, 14] and the Apostle, that «Through many tribulations must we enter into the kingdom of the heavens.» [n: Act. XIV, 21] [var: the received text of Acts 14:22 reads *τὴν βασιλείαν τοῦ Θεοῦ*, "the kingdom of God"]

⟦q1.1|3⟧ Yielding therefore to his exhortation, we remained in the Laura about two months. ⟦q1.1|4⟧ And when a barbarian report arose again, taking fright we fled for refuge to a monastery near the holy city, about twenty stadia off, called that of the abba Anastasius, which had no one in it at that time. ⟦q1.1|5⟧ And having spent about two years in it, we were again entreated by the aforesaid most holy Modestus to return and to dwell in our own place. ⟦q1.1|6⟧ Persuaded then by his good counsel, some of us returned at once and settled in the Laura; but some a little more slowly, for fear of the Saracens who neighbour upon us; and some of us remained on in that same monastery, together with Justinus their most holy superior, who had renounced the world in the Laura and had spent very many years in it and had been distinguished in many virtues, so that he was honoured also with the dignity of the presbyterate. ⟦q1.1|7⟧ And this most God-fearing Justinus gathered to himself a great company in that same monastery by the grace of Christ, keeping the rule unaltered from that of the Laura, and the rest of its constitution; and one would not find in all Palestine, or in any other coenobium [nt: Of the coenobitic sort.], such a spiritual constitution. ⟦q1.1|8⟧ And it flourishes and increases day by day; and going forward it becomes great after the manner of Isaac, so that from this we rejoice, conjecturing rightly that in every land the seed of our holy Father Sabas shines out after the manner of luminaries.

⟦q1.1|9⟧ But we the lowly, by the grace of God and by the diligence of the aforementioned most holy Modestus, sit in stillness beyond the former years, giving thanks and glorifying God, who kills and makes alive and who alone does great wonders. ⟦q1.1|10⟧ For his own goodness had compassion upon us, and brought our place unto great advancement, so far as the times allowed, on account of the holy servants of his who had served him genuinely therein. ⟦q1.1|11⟧ And his goodness bestowed upon us a Father and shepherd worthy of his providence: Thomas the most holy, one gifted with knowledge and adorned with virtues of every kind, who from a youthful age practised a life of stillness; the gentle and brother-loving and most compassionate, the humble in heart, most fervent about prayer and about the rule. ⟦q1.1|12⟧ Wherefore God also, under his leadership, brought his disciples into a broad place.

⟦q1.1|13⟧ And the number of the Fathers who were slain is forty-four by name. ⟦q1.1|14⟧ And their memory is celebrated in the month of May, on the fifteenth.

⟦q1.1|15⟧ And by the grace of Christ and the diligence of the aforesaid most holy Father of ours [1428] Modestus, the remaining monasteries of the desert also are inhabited. ⟦q1.1|16⟧ Behold then, honoured father Eustathius, in brief the things which I have signified to your God-loving reverence concerning our holy ones and concerning the Laura; and other good tidings also, gladdening your soul and the soul of every Christian who hears them, I bring you. ⟦q1.1|17⟧ For the aforesaid most holy Modestus takes forethought not only for the monasteries of the desert, but also for the city and for all the country round about, God working with him in everything. ⟦q1.1|18⟧ For this man, as truly our new Bezalel or Zerubbabel, filled with the Holy Spirit, has raised up again the venerable churches that were burned: those of our Saviour Jesus Christ — both the holy Skull, and his holy Resurrection, and the august house of the precious Cross, and the mother of the churches, and his holy Ascension — and the remaining venerable houses; so that from this we have good hopes also concerning the things to come, and most of all for those who come from the outer lands to the veneration of the holy places; and that all glorify God, to whom belongs all glory, honour and veneration, now and ever and unto the ages of ages. ⟦q1.1|19⟧ Amen.
