Maximus the Confessor

Maximus Confessor

c. 580 – 662

Monk, theologian and spiritual writer of the seventh century, the most searching Greek theologian of his age. He wrote on prayer, love and the ascetic life, on the difficulties in Gregory of Nazianzus and Dionysius, and on Christ's two natures and two wills against the Monothelite policy of the emperors and the patriarchs of Constantinople. Arrested and tried at Constantinople in 655 and again in 662, he was condemned, mutilated, and died in exile on the Black Sea coast. Migne prints his letters as Combefis edited them, in Greek with Combefis's Latin facing.

1393Of Maximus the monk.

TO THEOPEMPTUS THE SCHOLASTICUS

He had asked about the judge of unrighteousness; and about «If anyone strikes thee on the right cheek»; and about «Touch me not, for I have not yet ascended to the Father». Luc. xviii, 6.

To those for whom the Word is what is worshipped, for these also a life that is the revealer of the word is what is accomplished; so that, having received the experience of practice as the seal of true knowledge, they may learn from what they have undergone, as is right, how much greater than knowing something is becoming it; and, more than this, becoming it ever more and more, until the undoubting kinship toward the Cause be accomplished for them, circumscribing motion by rest and desire by unchangeability of inclination; to whom also the deification that comes after grace is given in return as the prize of the holy and godlike conduct of this present life.

Πρὸς οὓς μάλα σοφῶς καὐτὸς ἀπευθύνων τὸν βίον, θεοφύλακτε δέσποτα, ζητεῖν ἐπείγῃ τὸ πᾶσι προκείμενον δι᾿ ἀγαθότητα, καὶ πάντας δι᾿ ἀπειρίαν διαδιδράσκον· τὸ μὲν, ἵν᾿ ἑλκύσῃ πρὸς ἑαυτὸ, τῷ ἀμηχάνῳ τῆς ὡραιότητος κεντρίζον τὴν ἔφεσιν· τὸ δὲ, ἵνα στομώσῃ, τῇ τῆς ὑπεραπείρου καταπλήξει καὶ θείας ὑπεροχῆς περιστέλλον τὸν κόρον, ὕβρεως ὄντα πατέρα καὶ διαχύσεως γέννημα, τοῖς τὴν φλογίνην ῥομφαίαν οὐ προθεωροῦσι σὺν τοῖς Χερουβὶμ τεταγμένην καὶ στρεφομένην, οὐκ ἄλλοις, ἢ τοῖς δίχα φόβον πρὸς ἀρετὴν ἐπιστρέφουσιν· ἔνθεν τῷ μεγέθει τῆς ἐπὶ τὰ καλὰ προκοπῆς συνεπεκτείνων τὸ μέτριον, ᾧ καὶ μᾶλλον ἐπαύξεις σὺν ἀσφαλείᾳ τὴν πρόοδον· ἠξίωσας ἀγνώτην ὄντα με καὶ ταπεινὸν ἐπίγνῶναι, δι᾿ οὗ τοῖς κατ᾿ αὐτὴν ἡ φύσις τρόπου παρείχετο· φημὶ δὲ, τοῦ γράμματος· ὃ λόγος συνδεκτικὸς τῶν ἀπόντων ἐστὶ, καὶ τῶν οὐ βλεπομένων ἐμφανιστικός· τοσοῦτον νοήσει παραδιδοὺς καὶ συνάπτων τὸν τέως αἰσθήσει μὴ γνωριζόμενον, ὅσον διὰ ταύτης αὐτὸς χαραττόμενος παχύνεται σωματούμενος· ᾧ καὶ τὴν ἐμὴν οὐδένειαν εἰς πόθον ἤγαγες τὸν ὑμέτερον πλέον, ᾗ θέαν τὴν οἰκείαν ἀσωμάτως κεκίνηκας· προδείξας αὐτὸν ἐπ᾿ ἀμφοῖν τούτοιν ἔχειν μᾶλλον τὸ ἶσον περὶ τὸν λόγον, ἢ ὅσον ἕτεροι περὶ σὲ τὴν τοῦ λόγου χάριν παρὰ τοὺς ἄλλους προτερήματα· οἷς διὰ βίου κοσμούμενος, 1396πῶς ποτε τὴν ἐμὴν ἀμβλυωπίαν πρὸς θεωρίαν ἐνάγειν ἐσπούδασας, ἱερῶν καὶ ταῦτα λόγων, καὶ ὧν ἡ ἐπίβασις, κρεῖττον ἢ κατὰ ἄνθρωπον, τὸν οὔπω τελείως ὑπεραναβάντα τὴν τῶν ἀστάτων καὶ διαστατῶν φύσιν καὶ γένεσιν· μὴ ὅτι γ᾿ ἐμοὶ τῷ φορυτῷ τούτων ἑκουσίως συμφυρομένῳ, καὶ σκώληκος δίκην τῇ σήψει τῶν ἀκαθάρτων καλινδουμένῳ καὶ ἡδομένῳ παθῶν. Πλὴν τῆς εὐπειθείας χάριν, καὶ τῆς ὑμετέρας ἀγάπης τιμὴν, τῶν ὑπὲρ ἀξίαν κατατολμῶν, ἐκεῖνά φημι περὶ ὧν ἐκελεύσατε· στοχαστικῶς, ἀλλ᾿ οὐκ ἀποφαντικῶς.

Κριτὴν ἀδικίας ἐν τῷ Εὐαγγελίῳ παραβολικῶς ὑπάρχειν καὶ λέγεσθαι, τὸν ἡμέτερον λογισμὸν, ἀρχῆθεν ἐκ παραβάσεως ἄδικον πεποιημένον τὴν κρίσιν· ὡς τὴν τοῦ Κτίσαντος μὲν οὐ δεξάμενον ἐντολήν· τὴν δὲ τοῦ φθείροντος ἑλόμενον συμβουλήν· ἐξ οὗ καὶ τὸ χοϊκὸς εἶναι, τοῦ εἶναι, καὶ τοῦ θείος γενέσθαι πάμπολυ προετίμησε. Πόλιν δὲ εἶναι τούτου, τὴν αἴσθησιν, ἢ τὸν ὁρώμενον κόσμον· οἷς παραλόγως κατὰ σχέσιν ἐπαναπαυόμενος, τῆς κατοικίας τὴν παροικίαν ἠλλάξατο· μήτε Θεὸν φοβούμενος, μήτε μὴν ἄνθρωπον τὸν αὐτὸν γεγενημένον δι᾿ ἡμᾶς ἐντρεπόμενος· τὸ μὲν, διὰ τὴν ἄνοιαν· τὸ δὲ, διὰ τὴν καταφρόνησιν· ἐκείνην τε μὴ προορωμένην τῶν κριμάτων τὸ μέγεθος· καὶ ταυτὴν οὐκ εἰργομένην, ἣν τέως ἡ χρηστότης προσμένουσα τὴν μεταβολὴν ἀναστέλλει, τὴν βάσανον· αἷς ἑαυτὸν αὐθαδῶς περιέπειρε, τῇ δεδομένῃ παρὰ τοῦ Κτίσαντος εἰς ἐξάνυσιν τῶν καθηκόντων αὐτεξουσίῳ τιμῇ παρὰ τὸ δέον ἀποχρησάμενος· καὶ τοῦ νοουμένου τὸ φανὲν ἀγαθὸν κρίνας, ὃ μὴ θέμις ἦν, αἱρετώτερον.

Χήραν δὲ διοχλοῦσαν, τὴν λογικὴν ψυχὴν, ἅτε δὴ πρὸς καρπογονίαν ἱερῶν καὶ ὁσίων ἔργων οὐκ ἔχουσαν τὸν τὰ καλὰ κατὰ φύσιν σπείροντα λόγον. Μόνῃ τῇ πρὸς αὐτὴν ὁλικῇ ταύτῃ στοργῇ καὶ διαθέσει κρατούμενον· ἀλλ᾿ οὐ προσμένοντα διὰ τὴν ἀπόνευσιν, καὶ τὴν αὐθαίρετον πρὸς τὰ τῇδε ῥοπήν.

Ἀντίδικον δὲ ταύτης, φημὶ τῆς ψυχῆς, τὸ τῆς δυστήνου σαρκὸς ἔνυλον κίνημά τε καὶ φρόνημα, δι᾿ οὗ τῆς ἐν ἡμῖν θείας εἰκόνος καὶ τοῦ κατ᾿ αὐτὴν ἀξιώματος, τό τε κάλλος καταμολύνειν σπουδάζουσι, καὶ τὸ διαυγὲς ἀμαυροῦν οἱ ἀκάθαρτοι δαίμονες, ὁπηνίκα, δι᾿ ἡδονῆς ὑποπίπτουσιν, ἐπεισφρήσωσι δεινῶς ἡμῖν τὰ τῆς ἀτιμίας αἴσχη καὶ πάθη· δι᾿ ἃ καὶ ὁ θάνατος κατεκρίθη τῆς φύσεως, οὐχ ἑλομένης τὸ ἀπ᾿ ἀρχῆς τῆς ὄντως κατατρυφῆσαι ζωῆς.

Toward these, directing thy life most wisely thyself, God-guarded master, thou art pressing on to seek that which through goodness is set before all, and through infinity escapes all: the first, that it may draw them to itself, goading desire by the overwhelmingness of its loveliness; the second, that it may temper them, restraining satiety, which is the father of insolence and the offspring of dissipation, by the astonishment at its more-than-infinite and divine pre-eminence, for those who do not foresee the flaming sword set with the Cherubim and turning, Gen. iii, 24. for none other than those who turn to virtue without fear; whence, extending thy moderation along with the greatness of thy advance in good things, by which thou also increasest thy progress all the more with safety, thou hast thought it worthy to come to know me, who am unknown and lowly, through that mode which nature afforded to those of her kind — I mean, the letter: which is a word that gathers together the absent and makes manifest those who are not seen, delivering to intellection and joining to it one who till now is not known to sense, insofar as the word itself, inscribed through sense, is thickened, being embodied; by which also thou hast led my nothingness into longing for you the more, in which thou hast incorporeally stirred the sight of thyself; having shown thyself in both these respects to hold the equal concerning the word, rather than as much as others, concerning thee, hold the grace of the word as advantages over the rest; 1396adorned with which through life, how thou hast been eager ever to lead my dim-sightedness to contemplation, and that of holy words, and of words whose ascent is beyond what befits man for one who has not yet perfectly surmounted the nature and coming-to-be of unstable and extended things; let alone for me, who am willingly commixed with the refuse of these, and like a worm roll and take pleasure in the rot of unclean passions. Nevertheless, for the sake of obedience, and as honour to your love, venturing upon things beyond my worth, I say those things concerning which you gave command: by way of conjecture, and not of assertion.

The judge of unrighteousness in the Gospel is, by way of parable, and is called, our reasoning, which from the beginning, by transgression, has made its judgement unrighteous; as having not received the commandment of the Creator, but chosen the counsel of the corrupter; from which it also far preferred being earthy to being, and to becoming divine. And its city is sense, or the visible world; resting on which irrationally by relation, it exchanged its sojourning for settled dwelling; neither fearing God, nor yet regarding man, the same who became man for our sake: the one through folly, the other through contempt; the former not foreseeing the greatness of the judgements, and the latter not keeping itself from the torment which kindness, awaiting the conversion, for the present holds back; with which it wilfully pierced itself through, having misused, contrary to what was due, the self-determining honour given by the Creator for the fulfilment of what is fitting, and having judged the good that appeared more choiceworthy than the good that is understood, which was not lawful.

And the widow who troubles him is the rational soul, inasmuch as, for the bearing of fruit of holy and pious works, she does not have the reason that sows good things according to nature, reason held only by this whole affection and disposition toward her, but not abiding with her because of her turning-away and her self-chosen leaning toward the things here.

And her adversary — of the soul, I mean — is the material movement and way of thinking of the wretched flesh, through which the unclean demons are eager to defile the beauty, and to darken the brightness, of the divine image in us and of the dignity that accords with it, whenever, as we fall under them through pleasure, they dreadfully insinuate into us the shames and passions of dishonour; on account of which death too was sentenced against nature, since she did not choose from the beginning to luxuriate in the life that truly is.

Ὑπωπιασμοὺς δὲ τοὺς ἐκ τῆς συνειδήσεως μεθ᾿ ὑποφωνήσεώς τινος παρακλητικοὺς αἰκισμοὺς, οἷς προτεινομένοις ἐκ τῆς ψυχῆς, ἠρέμα πως εἴωθεν κάμπτεσθαι τὸ αὐστηρὸν τοῦ λογισμοῦ, καὶ πρὸς τὰ καλὰ διὰ τὴν χρονιωτέραν ἕξιν ἀνύπεικτον, οὐ φέροντος 1397ἐνδομυχοῦσαν, καὶ οἷον αἰκίζουσάν τε συνεχῶς καὶ διοχλοῦσαν τὴν βάσανον· ἕως λυμῄνηται τὰ τὴν ψυχὴν λυμαινόμενα πάθη, δι᾿ ἐγκρατείας, καὶ τῆς πρὸς τὰ μέλη τῆς σαρκὸς ἑκουσίου νεκρώσεως. Εἶτα καὶ πρὸς ἀγχιστείαν αὐτοῦ τοῦ λόγου δι᾿ εὐσεβείας, καὶ τῆς περὶ τὰ κρείττω τελεωτέρας ἀγάγοι σπουδῆς· οἷς φυσικῶς ἐνεργουμένοις, τὰ οἰκεῖα καὶ πατρικὰ παραβάλλων, ὁ ὑπὲρ οὐσίαν καὶ φύσιν Θεὸς Κύριος ἡμῶν Ἰησοῦς Χριστὸς, Ὁ δὲ Θεὸς οὐ μὴ ποιήσῃ, φησὶ, τὴν ἐκδίκησιν ὑμῶν, δι᾿ ἐμοῦ σαρκωθέντος, καὶ τὴν τοῦ καθαιρέτου τῆς φύσεως ποιουμένου καθαίρεσιν;

Οὕτω μὲν οὖν τὰ περὶ τούτου διεξῆλθον ἐπιτόμως ἐγὼ, καθ᾿ ὅσον τὸ ἐγχωροῦν τῆς διανοίας μέτρον ἐπήρκεσεν· εἰ καὶ ἄλλως ἄλλοι τὸν τόπον κατανοήσαντες· οἱ μὲν εἰς Χριστόν· οἱ δὲ τοὔμπαλιν, εἰς τὸν Ἀντίχριστον ἑξειλήφασι. Δέος δέ μοι νοεῖν, ἢ λέγειν ἑκάτερα· τὸ μὲν, ὡς ἀσύγκριτον· οὐ γὰρ μείων μὲν Υἱοῦ· μείζων δὲ Πατρὸς, ἡ ἐκδίκησις· ἢ δι᾿ ἄλλου, καὶ ἄλλη τὸ σύνολον, πλὴν τοῦ Υἱοῦ. Καὶ πῶς ἐπὶ τῆς αὐτῆς συγκριτικῶς φησι, τὸ, Ὁ δὲ Θεὸς, φησὶν, οὐ ποιήσει τὴν ἐκδίκησιν ὑμῶν; Οὐ γὰρ ἔχει χώραν· ἢ διάφορος πάντως δειχθήσεται, καὶ μετὰ τῆς φύσεως οὐ δυναμένης τὸ ἶσον δρᾷν· καὶ διὰ τοῦτο λοιπὸν, οὐ συντασσομένης· εἰκονιζούσης δὲ μόνον, ὡς ὑπερκειμένην, τὴν ὑπερέχουσαν. Τὸ δὲ, ὡς ἀνέκδεκτον καὶ ἀμήχανον, τὸ καλὸν ποιεῖν τὸν κακίας εὑρετὴν καὶ διδάσκαλον· Οὐ γὰρ δύναται, φησὶ, δένδρον σαπρὸν καρποὺς καλοὺς ποιεῖν· καλὸν δὲ ἡ ἐκδίκησις, καὶ τὸ τὴν ὄχλησιν ἀφοσιοῦσθαι δεόντως τοῦ προσπελάζοντος· ἢ τούτῳ παραβάλλεσθαι τυχὸν, ὃ μὴ θέμις, τὸν Πατέρα, καὶ πάσης ἀρετῆς καὶ ζωῆς χορηγὸν ἐξ Υἱοῦ, καὶ ταῦτα μονογενοῦς, καὶ ἴσου τὴν φύσιν καὶ τὴν ἀγαθότητα. Οὐ γὰρ τὰ καλὰ διὰ τῶν κακῶν μηνύεσθαι πέφυκεν, ὥσπερ οὐδὲ διὰ σκότους τὸ φῶς, ἢ θανάτου ζωή· καὶ τοῦ μηδαμῶς ὄντος, τὸ ὄν. Καὶ οὐ λέγω ταῦτα πρὸς τὴν ἐκείνων ἀθέτησιν· ἔχομαι γὰρ καὶ ἀντέχομαι ζωῆς πλέον, τῆς τῶν φαμένων διδασκαλίας· ἀλλὰ τὸ οἰκεῖον μέτριον δι᾿ ἄκραν δειλίαν ἀσφαλιζόμενος. Τὴν δὲ ἀνάγνωσιν τοῦ ὑποπιάζειν κανονίζειν οὐ πρέπον ἐμοὶ, διὰ τὴν ἐν τούτοις ἀμαθίαν, ἢ τοῖς ἠσκημένοις καὶ σπουδῇ προελομένοις τὴν παίδευσιν· οἳ ταύτην ὀφείλειν φασὶ κανονίζεσθαι βαρυτόνως μᾶλλον, ἢ περισπωμένως, ὅτι τέ φασι τῶν ῥημάτων οὐδὲν περισπᾶται, πλὴν εἰ μὴ ἐκ συναιρέσεως βαρυτόνου καθέστηκεν, ὡς τὸ, νοῶ, καὶ ποιῶ, καὶ χρυσῶ, καὶ ὅσα τοιαῦτα, φυλάσσοντα καὶ μετὰ προθέσεως ἐπὶ τῆς αὐτῆς τὸν τόνον.

And the bruisings are the exhortatory scourgings from the conscience, with a certain murmuring, by which, as they are put forth from the soul, the harshness of the reasoning, unyielding toward good things through a more long-standing habit, is wont somehow gently to be bent, since it does not 1397bear the torment that lurks within and, as it were, scourges it continually and troubles it; until it ravage the passions that ravage the soul, through self-mastery and the voluntary mortification of the members of the flesh. Then, too, until it lead the soul to near-kinship with reason itself, through piety and the more perfect zeal for higher things; to which, enacted according to nature, comparing what is his own and his Father's, the God above essence and nature, our Lord Jesus Christ, says: And shall God not make the vindication of you, Luc. xviii, 7. through me who was incarnate and who works the destruction of the destroyer of nature?

Thus, then, I have gone through the things about this in brief, so far as the available measure of my understanding sufficed; even if others, having understood the passage otherwise, have taken it, some of Christ, others contrariwise of the Antichrist. But I fear to think or to say either: the first, as admitting no comparison; for the vindication is not less of the Son and greater of the Father, or through another, and wholly another, besides the Son. And how, of the same vindication, does it say by way of comparison: And shall God not, it says, make the vindication of you? For it has no place: or else it will be shown altogether different, and with a nature that cannot do the like, and therefore, after all, not ranked with it, but only imaging, as lying above it, the one that excels. The second, as inadmissible and impossible: that the inventor and teacher of vice should do what is good; for a rotten tree, it says, cannot make good fruits; Matth. vii, 18. and vindication is a good, and duly to rid oneself of the importunity of the one who draws near; or else that the Father perhaps, which is not lawful, should be compared to this one, the provider of all virtue and life, from a Son, and that an Only-begotten, equal in nature and in goodness. For good things are not of a nature to be signified through evil things, as neither light through darkness, nor life through death, and what is through what in no way is. And I do not say these things to set those men aside; for I hold to, and cling to, more than to life, the teaching of those who said them; but I secure my own measure out of extreme timidity. But to rule the reading of ὑποπιάζειν is not fitting for me, because of my ignorance in these matters, but rather for those who are trained and have chosen learning with zeal; who say that it ought to be ruled as barytone rather than as perispomenon, because, they say, no verb is circumflexed unless it has arisen from the contraction of a barytone, as νοῶ, and ποιῶ, and χρυσῶ, and as many as are such, which keep the accent on the same syllable even with a preposition.

Τὴν δεξιὰν, σιαγόνα, τὴν δεξιὰν πρᾶξίν φημι, καὶ ὅσην ἔνθεος ἡμῖν διὰ τῶν ἐντολῶν συνιστάμενος βίος χαρακτηρίζει, ῥαπιζομένην ὑπὸ τοῦ πονηροῦ 1400τῇ προλήψει τῆς ἐνεργουμένης καλοκαγαθίας· καὶ τῇ πληγῇ τῆς ἐντεῦθεν οἰήσεως, φλεγμαίνουσάν πως διὰ κενοδοξίας, καὶ ἐπαιρομένην κατὰ τῶν μηδεμίαν, ἃ μὴ τὴν ἴσην τυχὸν κεκτημένων· δι᾿ ἣν ὥσπερ ἄλλην καὶ οἷον ἀριστερὰν στρέφειν προσήκει, τὸν ἤδη πρότερον ἡμᾶς κατὰ τὴν ἀπάτην τοῦ αἰῶνος τούτου καταμολύναντα βίον, τούτῳ τε πλήττεσθαι συνεχῶς διὰ τῆς μνήμης· καὶ ὡς ἁμαρτωλοὺς μᾶλλον συστέλλεσθαι καὶ μετριάζειν, ἢ ὡς ἁγίους μετεωρίζεσθαι, καὶ δι᾿ ἔπαρσιν καταπίπτειν, οὐκ ἔχοντας πῆξιν ἱερὰν τὴν ταπείνωσιν. Τιμάσθω δὲ μετὰ τῶν κατὰ θεωρίαν λαμβανομένων· μᾶλλον δὲ καὶ πρὸ τούτων, εἰς ἑτοιμασίαν ἱερὰν καὶ προκάθαρσιν, καὶ σωματικῶς ταῦτα τελούμενα διὰ τὸν ἐντειλάμενον Κύριον· πραϋπαθείας ἐντεῦθεν ἕξιν τοῖς κατορθοῦσι δωρούμενον, καὶ τῆς τοῦ πλήττοντος περιγραφὴν ἀπιστίας, τῷ ἑτοίμῳ τῆς παραδοχῆς τὸ πρόθυμον τῆς πληγῆς ἀναστέλλοντα, αἰδομένου τυχὸν, εἰ μή τι ἄλλο, τὸ ὑπερβάλλον τῆς ὑποπτώσεως· καὶ τὸ μηδαμῶς ἀντιτείνεν τὸν δυνάμενον τοῦτο ποιεῖν, εἱργόμενον δὲ διὰ τὴν πρόσαξιν.

Τὸ δὲ, Μή μου ἅπτου, πρὸς τὴν Μαγδαληνὴν λέλεκται Μαρίαν παρὰ τοῦ Κυρίου, κηπουρὸν αὐτὸν εἶναι νομίσασαν· δηλοῦντος τοῦ μυστηρίου, καὶ ὑπεκφαίνοντος, ὡς ψυχὴ πᾶσα νοερὰ, μήπω τὸ συμφυὲς σκῆνος ὑπεραναβᾶσα, καὶ διὰ τοῦτο τῶν ἐν γενέσει καὶ φθορᾷ μόνον, οὐδὲν οὐδαμῶς διαφερόντων λαχάνων κηπευομένων· ἴση γὰρ ἐπ᾿ ἀμφοῖν ἥ τε ῥοὴ καὶ ἀποῤῥοὴ, καὶ ἡ τοῦ χρόνου περιγραφὴ καὶ περίοδος· δημιουργὸν εἶναι τὸν Λόγον ὑπολαμβάνουσα, διὰ τὴν περὶ ταῦτα, καὶ ἕως τούτων πρόοδον αὐτοῦ καὶ συγκατάβασιν· οὐκ ἀξία τῆς τούτου κατὰ πνεῦμα καθέστηκεν ἐπαφῆς, καὶ ταῦτα τοῖς φαινομένοις αὐτὸν ἐπιζητοῦσα· καὶ σάρκα δι᾿ ἡμᾶς γεγενημένον γινώσκουσα μόνον αὐτόν· ἀλλ᾿ οὐχὶ πρεπόντως καὶ Θεὸν ἐκ Θεοῦ τοῦ Πατρὸς γεγεννημένον γινώσκουσα. Τοῦτο γὰρ κατὰ τὸ εἰκὸς νοεῖν ὑποβάλλει, τὸ, Οὔπω ἀναβέβηκα πρὸς τὸν Πατέρα μου, τὸ μὴ δι᾿ ἄκρας θεολογίας καὶ πίστεως, οἷς ἡ τῶν ὁρωμένων περιγράφεται φύσις, ὑψωθῆναι πρὸς τῆς οὕτω διακειμένης ψυχῆς· ἀλλ᾿ ἔτι παιδαγωγικῶς δεομένης εἰς ἐντελεστέραν τούτου κατάληψιν, ἐπὶ τὴν ἀποκαλυπτικὴν ἕξιν τῶν θείων ὡσεί τινα Γαλιλαίαν διὰ θεωρίας ἀναδραμεῖν· καὶ σὺν τοῖς ὑπ᾿ αὐτοῦ προτετυπωμένοις καὶ προβεβλημένοις λόγοις, ὅλον ὡς θέμις περιληφθῆναι, καὶ γενέσθαι τοσοῦτον ὅπερ αὐτὸς ἦν ὁ γενόμενος ὅπερ ἡμεῖς, ὅσον ἐδείχθη τοῦτο γενόμενος πλὴν μόνης τῆς ἁμαρτίας, ὡς καὶ ἡμεῖς ἐκείνῳ, δίχα μόνης τῆς φύσεως, ἡνίκα τῆς δωρεᾶς τῆς ἐπιτύχοιμεν· ἣν δι᾿ ἐλπίδος νῦν προσδεχόμενος, καὶ προσήκουσι τῶν ἐντολῶν ἔργοις δεόντως ἀποσεμνύνων, ἀπολαύσαις ὅσον ἐπίστευσας, καὶ τρυφήσαις ὅσον ἐπόνησας· μᾶλλον δὲ πλέον καὶ ἀπειράκις, καθ᾿ ὅσον καὶ ὁ ταύτης δοτὴρ ὑπερουσίως ὑπερανέστηκε καὶ φύσεως κίνησιν, καὶ παντὸς αἰῶνος περίληψιν.

The right cheek: I call it right action, and all that the godly life, established in us through the commandments, characterizes, struck by the evil one 1400with the presumption of the goodness being performed, and with the blow of the conceit that comes from it, somehow inflamed through vainglory and lifted up against those who have none, or perhaps do not possess the equal; on account of which it is fitting to turn, as another and as it were the left, the life which already before defiled us according to the deceit of this age, and to be struck by it continually through memory; and to draw in and be moderate as sinners, rather than to be lifted high as saints and through exaltation to fall, not having humility as a holy fixity. And let these things also, performed bodily, be honoured along with the things taken according to contemplation — or rather even before these, for holy preparation and pre-purification — for the sake of the Lord who commanded them, who thence bestows a habit of meekness on those who accomplish them, and a circumscription of the faithlessness of the one who strikes, checking the eagerness of the blow by the readiness of the reception, the striker perhaps feeling shame at, if nothing else, the excess of the submission, and that one who is able to do this does not resist at all, but is restrained because of the command.

And the saying Touch me not was spoken by the Lord to Mary Magdalene when she supposed him to be the gardener; the mystery showing and hinting that every noetic soul which has not yet surmounted the tabernacle grown together with it, and which therefore supposes the Word to be creator only of the things in coming-to-be and corruption, which differ in no way at all from garden vegetables — for equal in both are the flow and the outflow, and the circumscription and period of time — because of his going forth about these things and as far as these, and his condescension, has not become worthy of touching him according to the spirit, and that while seeking him among the things that appear, and knowing him only as having become flesh for our sake, but not fittingly knowing him also as God begotten of God the Father. For this, in all likelihood, is what I have not yet ascended to my Father suggests to be understood: that he has not been exalted, through utmost theology and faith, by which the nature of visible things is circumscribed, by the soul so disposed; but that she still needs, as under a tutor, for a more complete apprehension of him, to run up through contemplation to the revealing habit of divine things as to a kind of Galilee; and that he be comprehended whole, as is right, together with the principles prefigured and put forth by him; and to become as much that which he was, he who became that which we are, as he was shown to have become this, save sin alone, as we too to him, apart from nature alone, when we should attain the gift; which, receiving it now through hope, and duly adorning it with the fitting works of the commandments, mayest thou enjoy as much as thou hast believed, and delight in as much as thou hast laboured; or rather more, and infinitely many times more, insofar as also its Giver superessentially transcends both the motion of nature and the compass of every age.

On this text

DRAFT (Wilson to read). Maximus the Confessor's short letter to Theopemptus, a scholasticus (a lawyer), as Migne prints it (PG 90.1393–1400), with a Latin version facing; CPG 7696. Theopemptus had asked about three sayings of the Gospels, and Maximus answers each in turn: the judge of unrighteousness (Luke 18), "If anyone strikes thee on the right cheek", and "Touch me not, for I have not yet ascended to the Father" (John 20:17). Each is read contemplatively: the unjust judge is our own reasoning, which by the first transgression made its judgement unrighteous, and his city is sense; the right cheek is right action, struck by the evil one with the conceit of the good one does, so that the other cheek to be turned is the old life of this age, struck by memory; and the word to Mary Magdalene, who took the Lord for the gardener, is said to every soul that has not yet risen above the body. The letter opens, before the questions, on knowledge and practice, and on the deification that is the reward of a life lived by the commandments. On the text.
