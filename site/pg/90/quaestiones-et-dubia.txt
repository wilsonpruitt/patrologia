⟦q1.1|1⟧ [0785] *Of the same.*

⟦q1.2|1⟧ QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS, AND INTERROGATIONS, AND SELECTIONS

⟦q1.3|1⟧ *of various perplexing chapters.*

⟦q1.4|1⟧ QUESTION 1

⟦q1.5|1⟧ Which are the virtues of the soul, and which of the body?

⟦q1.6|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.6|2⟧ The virtues of the soul are these: love, humility, meekness, long-suffering, forbearance, freedom from rancour, freedom from wrath, being without anger, being without envy, not judging, being without vainglory, almsgiving, temperance, freedom from avarice, compassion, being without arrogance, being without pride, compunction. ⟦q1.6|3⟧ And the virtues of the body are these: fasting, sleeping on the ground, vigil, self-control, poverty, freedom from distraction.

⟦q1.7|1+2⟧ [0788] QUESTION 2

⟦q1.8|1⟧ What is the saying in the Apostle: «*I will sing with the spirit, and I will sing with the mind also*»? [n: I Cor. xiv, 15.]

⟦q1.9|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.9|2⟧ One sings with the spirit when he brings forth through the tongue only the utterance of what is sung. ⟦q1.9|3⟧ And he sings with the mind when, knowing the power of what is sung, he is gladdened by contemplation.

⟦q1.10|1+2⟧ QUESTION 3

⟦q1.11|1+2⟧ What is: «*I was conceived in iniquities, and in sins did my mother conceive me with craving*»? [n: Psal. l, 7.]

⟦q1.12|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.12|2+3⟧ Since the leading purpose of God was that we should not be begotten through marriage, out of corruption; but the transgression of the commandment brought in marriage, because Adam acted lawlessly, that is, set aside the law given him by God: all, therefore, who are begotten from Adam are conceived in iniquities, falling under the condemnation of the forefather. ⟦q1.12|4⟧ And the words, *And in sins did my mother conceive me with craving*, signify that Eve, the first mother of us all, conceived sin with craving, as though swelling with desire for pleasure. ⟦q1.12|5⟧ For this reason we too, falling under our mother's condemnation, say that we are conceived with craving in sins.

⟦q1.13|1+2⟧ QUESTION 4

⟦q1.14|1+2⟧ What is that said in the Apostle: «*To be anathema from Christ for my kinsmen*»? [n: Rom. ix, 3.]

⟦q1.15|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.15|2⟧ I prayed, he says, to be handed over from Christ [0789] to the devil, so as to be scourged, and to undergo on Israel's behalf the punishments which by justice they owed for their unbelief toward God, only that they might be saved; in the way in which the Lord also handed himself over, and became a curse for us.

⟦q1.16|1+2⟧ QUESTION 5

⟦q1.17|1⟧ In how many modes does man sin?

⟦q1.18|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.18|2⟧ I think that man sins in four modes: by being carried away, by deceit, by ignorance, by disposition. ⟦q1.18|3⟧ And the first three come easily to full knowledge and repentance; but he who sins from disposition, and comes to repentance neither by experience nor by time, has a punishment that is incurable.

⟦q1.19|1⟧ QUESTION 6

⟦q1.20|1+2⟧ What does the Lord show in the Gospel, saying: «*Two sparrows are sold for an assarion*»? [n: Matth. x, 29.]

⟦q1.21|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.21|2⟧ Some say that the assarion is a piece of ten nummi. ⟦q1.21|3⟧ And by the ten is shown the letter iota; and this is the beginning of the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. ⟦q1.21|4⟧ Through the name of the Lord, then, are bought back the old and the new people, and the inner and the outer man.

⟦q1.22|1+2⟧ QUESTION 7

⟦q1.23|1⟧ What is that taken up by Elisha, said of Elijah: «*Where is the God Aphphu?*»?

⟦q1.24|1+2⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.24|1+2⟧ It is interpreted according to three approaches: either, «Where is the God of my Father?»; or, «Where is the God of my great one?»; or, «Where is the God of the hidden one?»

⟦q1.25|1+2⟧ [0792] QUESTION 8

⟦q1.26|1⟧ In how many modes are allegories, and what is tropology?

⟦q1.27|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.27|2⟧ Allegory is that concerning inanimate things: for example, mountains, hills, trees, and the rest. ⟦q1.27|2⟧ And tropology is that concerning our members: for example, head, eyes, and the rest. ⟦q1.27|3⟧ For tropology is so called from being turned.

⟦q1.28|1+2⟧ QUESTION 9

⟦q1.29|1+2⟧ What is that said in the Psalm: «*Sitting, thou didst speak against thy brother, and against thy mother's son thou didst set a stumbling-block*»? [n: Psal. xlix, 20.]

⟦q1.30|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.30|2⟧ He who speaks evil of the action of one of the same faith, and as it were sits down to the reviling of him, this one is fittingly said also to speak against his brother; and he who, carried along by envy, slanders the one adorned with wisdom and causes all to stumble, this is the one who sets a stumbling-block for his mother's son.

⟦q1.31|1+2⟧ QUESTION 10

⟦q1.32|1⟧ Since in the holy Diadochus, in the hundredth chapter, it is written that some will be judged through fire and purified in the age to come, I beg that the purpose of the Father be uncovered by clarification.

⟦q1.33|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.33|2+3⟧ Those who have acquired the perfection of love toward God, and through the virtues have raised aloft the wing of the soul, are, according to the Apostle, caught up in the clouds, and do not come into judgement. [n: Joan. v, 24] But those who have not wholly acquired the perfection, but have acquired both sins and right deeds, these come into the court of judgement, and there, through the weighing against each other of their good and bad deeds, being as it were tried by fire, [0793] if the scale of the good weighs down, they are cleansed of the punishment.

⟦q1.34|1+2⟧ QUESTION 11

⟦q1.35|1⟧ Concerning different justices.

⟦q1.36|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.36|2⟧ Those wise in divine things say that there are three justices: human, angelic and divine. ⟦q1.36|3⟧ And the human, they say, is the equity and gratitude in the sensible things of this world; and the angelic, the ungrudging imparting of divine knowledge; and divine justice they define as suffering on behalf of those who sin.

⟦q1.37|1+2⟧ QUESTION 12

⟦q1.38|1⟧ For what reason did the Lord, rebuking Peter, say to him, *Satan*? [n: Matth. xvi, 23]

⟦q1.39|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.39|2⟧ It was not, as some suppose, insultingly that the Lord called Peter Satan; but since the Lord's privations became habits for us: for example, his death became life for us; his dishonour became glory for us. ⟦q1.39|3⟧ And not knowing this, the apostle Peter, when the Lord said that he was about to suffer, supposed, following the nature of things, that it is impossible for the Life to die, or for so great a glory to be dishonoured. ⟦q1.39|4⟧ The Lord, then, destroying this supposition, on the ground that one ought not to seek the sequence of nature in things above nature, has deliberated to do these things through their contraries: through death, life; through dishonour, glory. ⟦q1.39|5⟧ So then, as [against] this opposing supposition of his, he says, *Get thee behind me*; that is, Follow me in counsel, and do not go before me, seeking a sequence of things. ⟦q1.39|6⟧ And the name Satan, they say, [0796] is interpreted «adversary»; which the Lord brought out not insultingly, but as though he were saying, «Thou who opposest my purpose».

⟦q1.40|1+2⟧ QUESTION 13

⟦q1.41|1⟧ Since Gregory of Nyssa in his writings appears, to those who do not understand the depth of his lofty contemplation, to hint everywhere at a restoration, I beg thee, say what thou knowest about this.

⟦q1.42|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.42|2⟧ The Church knows three restorations. ⟦q1.42|3⟧ One, that of each man according to the principle of virtue, in which he is restored when he has fulfilled the principle of virtue that lies with him. ⟦q1.42|4+5⟧ The second, that of the whole nature in the resurrection: the restoration to incorruption and immortality. ⟦q1.42|6⟧ And the third, of which Gregory of Nyssa has made most use in his discourses, is this: the restoration of the powers of the soul, which had fallen under sin, back into that for which they were created. ⟦q1.42|7⟧ For just as the whole nature must, in the resurrection of the flesh, receive back incorruption at a hoped-for time, so also the perverted powers of the soul must, in the stretching-out of the ages, cast off the memories of vice lodged in it; and, having passed through all the ages and finding no rest, the soul must come to God, who has no end. ⟦q1.42|8⟧ And thus by full knowledge, not by participation in the good things, it must receive back its powers, and be restored to its original state, and the Creator be shown to bear no blame for sin.

⟦q1.43|1+2⟧ QUESTION 14

⟦q1.44|1⟧ Why is it that, though there are many heavier insults, the Lord in the Gospels determines that he who calls his brother Fool is liable to the gehenna, but says that he who says Raca is subject to the council? [n: Matth. v, 22]

⟦q1.45|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.45|2⟧ They say that the name Fool is [0797] taken of the senseless and witless; and Raca in the tongue of the Hebrews is interpreted «one to be spat upon». ⟦q1.45|3+4⟧ Since, then, the name fool is said of the godless and unbelieving, according to: *The senseless man hath said in his heart, There is no God*; [n: Psal. xiii, 1.] and according to Moses, who says, *This people is foolish and not wise*; [n: Deut. xxxii, 6.] whoever calls his brother, one of the same faith, godless and idolater, or heretic and unbeliever, this one says «fool», and justly becomes liable to the gehenna. ⟦q1.45|5+6⟧ But whoever says Raca, that is, «thou spat-upon» and «unclean», this one slanders his brother's life, and fittingly is subject to a lighter penalty.

⟦q1.46|1+2⟧ QUESTION 15

⟦q1.47|1⟧ How must one understand what is said in the Creed: «Incarnate of the Holy Spirit and of Mary the Virgin»?

⟦q1.48|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.48|2⟧ Some of the saints say that from the Holy Spirit, after the manner of a man's seed, the soul was laid down; and the flesh was formed from the virginal bloods.

⟦q1.49|1+2⟧ QUESTION 16

⟦q1.50|1⟧ Whom does the Apostle mean, saying «*Those who first hoped in Christ*», writing to the Ephesians? [n: Ephes. i, 12.]

⟦q1.51|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.51|2⟧ Every one of the saints before the Lord's sojourn who practised any virtue whatever, even if he did not know the whole mystery of the economy, yet, moved [0800] in part naturally, hoped and expected that he who made nature would himself also save it when it had been corrupted.

⟦q1.52|1⟧ Herod is interpreted «of skin»; one must, then, abhor fleshly thoughts.

⟦q1.53|1+2⟧ QUESTION 17

⟦q1.54|1⟧ What is it, according to the Apostle: That *flesh and blood cannot inherit the kingdom of God*? [n: I Cor. xv, 50.]

⟦q1.55|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.55|2⟧ Flesh is desire; and blood, anger. ⟦q1.55|3⟧ And fittingly, then, he who is not pure of these cannot inherit the kingdom of God.

⟦q1.56|1+2⟧ QUESTION 18

⟦q1.57|1⟧ What is the diapsalma?

⟦q1.58|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.58|2⟧ I think that it is the conversion from concept to concept; or also from one mode of teaching to another mode.

⟦q1.59|1+2⟧ QUESTION 19

⟦q1.60|1⟧ What does the story concerning Lamech show? [n: Gen. iv, 23 seqq.]

⟦q1.61|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q1.61|2⟧ Some of those instructed in divine things say that in the times of Lamech, there being disorder and anarchy, the strong oppressed the weak. ⟦q1.61|3⟧ This Lamech, then, meeting a man with his wife, killed him, and took his wife. ⟦q1.61|4⟧ And meeting a brother with a sister, he killed him too, and took his sister; and the one Scripture called a man, and the other a youth. ⟦q1.61|5⟧ And these things according to the history; but according to contemplation Lamech is taken for the activity of vice. ⟦q1.61|6⟧ For it killed in us the man, the natural law; and the youth, I mean the spiritual

⟦q2.1|1+2+3+4+5⟧ [0801] law; and took from him the understanding, so that the sower of vice, mingling with it, might make it bring forth sin. ⟦q2.1|1+2+3+4+5⟧ From this Lamech, then, vengeance is taken seventy times seven: he pays the penalty for the consent and the activity. ⟦q2.1|1+2+3+4+5⟧ Whence also the Lord, when Peter said, *How often shall my brother sin against me, and I forgive him? till seven times?*, answered him, *Not only until seven times, but until seventy times seven*. [n: ibid. 22.] That is, to him who repents toward thee, thou shalt forgive not only the small things that come about in consent, but the activities also.

⟦q2.2|1+2⟧ QUESTION 20

⟦q2.3|1⟧ Concerning the different wills of God.

⟦q2.4|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.4|2⟧ One must suppose three wills in the case of God: by good pleasure, by economy, by concession. ⟦q2.4|3+4⟧ And the one by good pleasure is shown by what concerns Abraham, when he said to him, *Go forth out of thy land*. [n: Gen. xii, 1.] And the one by economy is shown by what was arranged by economy concerning Joseph, toward the outcome of things to come; and the one by concession is shown by what happened concerning Job.

⟦q2.5|1+2⟧ QUESTION 21

⟦q2.6|1+2⟧ What does the Psalmist mean, saying concerning enemies: «*Of those who beset round about*»? [n: Psal. iii, 6.]

⟦q2.7|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.7|2⟧ Round about is the front, behind, the right, the left. ⟦q2.7|3+4⟧ The demons, then, set upon us in front when they entice us through the appearances of matter; and behind, when through the preconceptions of the thoughts they stir up the memory of vice; and on the left, when through fleshly and licentious passions they lever the soul out of place; and on the right, when through vainglory and pride they set upon the soul.

⟦q2.8|1+2⟧ QUESTION 22

⟦q2.9|1+2⟧ What is: «*Take a psalm, and give the timbrel; a pleasant psaltery with the harp*»? [n: Psal. lxxx, 3.]

⟦q2.10|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.10|2⟧ Take inspired teaching, and give virtuous practice through the mortification of the body. ⟦q2.10|3⟧ And as for *the pleasant psaltery with the harp*: by psaltery he means the spirit; and by harp, the soul; and by timbrel, the body.

⟦q2.11|1+2⟧ QUESTION 23

⟦q2.12|1+2⟧ What is: «*A bruised reed shall he not break, and smoking flax shall he not quench*»? [n: Isa. xlii, 3; Matth. xii, 20.]

⟦q2.13|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.13|2⟧ He who in imitation of the Lord [0804] makes use of the principle of compassion neither makes the one bruised by sin be broken utterly by despair, nor quenches the one who has his rational power smoking through vainglory over certain virtues, but lets him keep his eagerness, until he come to the perfection of knowledge. ⟦q2.13|2⟧ For this, as I think, is also the growing up of the tares together with the good seed: that is, the passion of men-pleasing and vainglory sprouting up together with the virtues. ⟦q2.13|3⟧ Whence the husbandman of souls orders that these not be plucked out until the virtues take firm hold, lest perhaps someone, wishing to pull up such things, pluck out together with them the eagerness for virtue.

⟦q2.14|1+2⟧ QUESTION 24

⟦q2.15|1⟧ What is: «*If anyone strike thee on the right, turn to him the other also*»?

⟦q2.16|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.16|2⟧ If, he says, the demons tempt thee through thoughts on the right cheek, lifting thee up through right-hand works, turn the other: that is, bringing before thy sight the works of left-hand action, as many as have been done by us.

⟦q2.17|1+2⟧ QUESTION 25

⟦q2.18|1+2⟧ How ought we to understand piously what is of the Gospel: That «*The Father will judge no one, but has given all judgement to the Son*»? ⟦q2.18|3+4⟧ And how does he say in another place, that «*I will judge no one; but the word that I have spoken, that shall judge him*»? [n: Joan. viii, 15; xii, 48.]

⟦q2.19|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.19|2⟧ Inasmuch as he is God, neither the Father nor the Son will judge anyone. ⟦q2.19|3⟧ For neither does a man become the judge of irrational animals, but of men. ⟦q2.19|4⟧ But the Father has given judgement to the Son, not inasmuch as the Son is God, but inasmuch as he has become man. ⟦q2.19|5⟧ And he will judge all, comparing his own way of life as man with ours. ⟦q2.19|5⟧ And again his word will judge, that is, the teaching which he displayed through his works, according to what is [0805] written: «*Which Jesus began both to do and to teach*». [n: Act. i, 1.]

⟦q2.20|1+2⟧ QUESTION 26

⟦q2.21|1+2⟧ What is the blasphemy of the Spirit; and how is it that «*Every sin shall be forgiven unto men; but to them that have blasphemed against it, it shall not be forgiven, neither in this age, nor in that to come*»? [n: Matth. xii, 31; Luc. xii, 10.]

⟦q2.22|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.22|2⟧ The perplexity concerning the blasphemy of the Spirit has its solution from the very passage. ⟦q2.22|3⟧ For when the Lord was working various healings, the Jews ascribed these activities of the Spirit to the ruler of the demons. ⟦q2.22|4⟧ But that there is forgiveness neither here nor in the age to come for those who blaspheme must be sought next. ⟦q2.22|5⟧ Some of those who have searched out such things with exactness through the Spirit say that there are four modes through which forgiveness of sins comes about: two here, and two in the age to come. ⟦q2.22|6+7+8⟧ For since the memory does not reach to remembering the faults of the whole of time, so that man may repent of them here, the Master of nature, being a lover of man, arranged by economy modes of repentance even while we do not repent: in the age to come, as has been said, two. ⟦q2.22|6+7+8⟧ When someone who has sinned here indiscriminately, and again has done good indiscriminately, whether moved to pity and compassion toward his neighbour, or whatever else belongs to love for man: these things, weighed in the age to come at the time of judgement, forgiveness comes about for those to which the scale shall have its tilt. ⟦q2.22|6+7+8⟧ This is the one mode. ⟦q2.22|6+7+8⟧ And the second is this: when someone held in sins, yet hearing the Lord say, *Judge not, that you be not judged*, [n: Matth. vii, 1.] judges no one for fear, in the examination of the life he has lived he is not judged, as a keeper of the commandment. ⟦q2.22|9⟧ For the most truthful is not forgetful of his own commandment. ⟦q2.22|10⟧ And the other two modes have their forgiveness here: when someone being in sins is dealt with by providence through economy in misfortunes, in necessities, in illnesses. ⟦q2.22|11⟧ For in a way he does not know, God purifies him through such things; and if he gives thanks when tried, he receives the reward for his thanksgiving. ⟦q2.22|12⟧ But if he is ungrateful, he is purified of the sins for which he is chastened, but pays the penalty for his ingratitude. ⟦q2.22|13⟧ Next: the sins which a man commits against men, as the account has shown, have [0808] many grounds for forgiveness. ⟦q2.22|14⟧ For one sinning against a man, but doing good to another man: the nature against which he sinned, this same has also settled the account. ⟦q2.22|15⟧ But the blasphemy against the Spirit (and it is unbelief) has no other ground of forgiveness than becoming faithful: fittingly, then, for him who has closed his life in unbelief, neither here nor in the age to come will the sin of unbelief and godlessness be forgiven.

⟦q2.23|1+2⟧ QUESTION 27

⟦q2.24|1+2⟧ What does the riddle of the Proverbs show: «*If thine enemy hunger, feed him; if he thirst, give him drink; for in so doing thou shalt heap coals of fire upon his head*»? [n: Prov. xxv, 21; Rom. xii, 20.]

⟦q2.25|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.25|2⟧ We have an enemy against the soul: our body, always warring against us through the uprising of the passions. ⟦q2.25|3⟧ If, then, the way of thinking of the flesh, pressed by the conscience, hungers, that is, reaches for salvation; or thirsts for divine knowledge, one must feed it through self-control and labours, and give it drink by meditation on the divine oracles. ⟦q2.25|4⟧ For thus are heaped upon its head, that is, upon the mind, coals of fire: divine and spiritual thoughts.

⟦q2.26|1+2⟧ QUESTION 28

⟦q2.27|1⟧ What does what is said in the 101st Psalm signify: «*I am become like a pelican of the wilderness*»? [n: Psal. ci, 7.]

⟦q2.28|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.28|2⟧ This pelican is a bird; and the serpent is greatly [0809] hostile to its young. ⟦q2.28|3⟧ And what does it contrive? ⟦q2.28|4+5+6⟧ It fixes its nest on a height, fencing it about on every side because of the serpent. ⟦q2.28|4+5+6⟧ What then does the evil-contriving serpent do? ⟦q2.28|4+5+6⟧ It looks about for where the wind blows from, and from there breathes its venom upon the young, and they die. ⟦q2.28|4+5+6⟧ The pelican, then, comes, and sees that its children have died; and it looks to a cloud, and flies up on high with its wings; and it strikes its sides, and through the cloud drips upon them, and they rise. ⟦q2.28|7⟧ The pelican, then, is taken for the Lord; and its children, Adam and Eve, our nature; and its nest, paradise; and the serpent, the apostate devil. ⟦q2.28|8⟧ The serpent, the author of evil, then, breathed upon the first-formed through the disobedience, and they became dead by sin. ⟦q2.28|9⟧ And so our Lord and God, through his love toward us, is lifted up on the precious cross; and, pierced in the side, through the cloud of the Holy Spirit he has granted us life everlasting.

⟦q2.29|1+2⟧ QUESTION 29

⟦q2.30|1⟧ What is: «*There the sparrows shall make their nests*»? [n: Psal. ciii, 17.]

⟦q2.31|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.31|2⟧ By sparrows he means either the souls or the different virtues.

⟦q2.32|1+2⟧ QUESTION 30

⟦q2.33|1⟧ What does this signify: «*The heron's dwelling leads them*»? [n: ibid. 18.]

⟦q2.34|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.34|2⟧ They say that the heron is a bird, and that it lives in such chastity [0812] that whenever it is about to come together for intercourse, it mourns forty days; and again after that, another forty. ⟦q2.34|3⟧ And it fixes its nest in the trees, where it is not overshadowed by anything, but has the air clean. ⟦q2.34|4⟧ And through these things it signifies chastity. ⟦q2.34|5⟧ For this leads all the virtues; and it stands apart also from sensible things, overshadowed by none of the things of time. ⟦q2.34|6⟧ For the number forty contains the perfection of each of the four elements.

⟦q2.35|1+2⟧ QUESTION 31

⟦q2.36|1⟧ What is: «*Repaying the sins of parents upon children, unto the third and fourth generation, of them that hate me*»? [n: Deut. v, 9.]

⟦q2.37|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.37|2⟧ We have supposed the first generation to be the sowing of evil, that is, the assault; and the second, the desire; and the third, the habit of the generation of evil, that is, the consent; and the fourth, the activity, that is, the action. ⟦q2.37|3⟧ It is repaid, then, unto the third and fourth generation: that is, for the consent and for the action. ⟦q2.37|4⟧ For the assault and the desire are not liable, since the evil has not proceeded to its end.

⟦q2.38|1+2⟧ QUESTION 32

⟦q2.39|1⟧ How ought the regret of God which lies in Scripture to be understood piously?

⟦q2.40|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q2.40|2⟧ Those who have resolved to think piously of divine things say that it is impossible that God, being by nature Creator, should not also be by nature provider for the things created. ⟦q2.40|3⟧ And if this is so, it is of all necessity, since God naturally provides for manhood, that the modes of salvation with him concerning the nature provided for are many. ⟦q2.40|4⟧ For since man is a quick-turning living being, easily converted along with the seasons and the manners, it is altogether necessary that the divine Providence also, though it is the same, should be converted along with our dispositions, finding a mode through the things fitly adapted to the vices that spring up in nature. ⟦q2.40|5⟧ And as in medicine many are the diseases that fall under it; and it is necessary for the skilled physician, when the subject body falls into many and different sufferings, himself also to pass from the lesser to the better modes; so also

⟦q3.1|1⟧ [0813] in the case of God, it is customary in Scripture that the passage from such a mode of providence to another mode be named the regret of God.

⟦q3.2|1+2⟧ QUESTION 33

⟦q3.3|1⟧ What does «*Upon the three or four impieties of Tyre I will not turn away*» signify? [n: Amos i, 9.]

⟦q3.4|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q3.4|2⟧ I think the prophetic word hints at four impieties: of it, the assault, the desire, the habit of evil, and the activity. ⟦q3.4|3⟧ In the case of the first and the second, that is, of the assault and the desire, God shows forbearance, since the evil has not advanced to completion. ⟦q3.4|4⟧ But in the case of the third and the fourth impiety, that is, of habit and activity, or of assent and action, the Word fittingly displays the threat.

⟦q3.5|1+2⟧ QUESTION 34

⟦q3.6|1⟧ Why can «Father of the Spirit» not be said, or «Christ of the Spirit», as, in the case of the Father and of the Son, «Spirit of God» and «Spirit of Christ» are said variously?

⟦q3.7|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q3.7|2⟧ Just as the Mind is cause of the Word, so also of the Spirit, but through the medium of the Word. ⟦q3.7|3⟧ And just as we cannot say that the word is of the voice, so neither can we say that the Son is of the Spirit.

⟦q3.8|1+2⟧ QUESTION 35

⟦q3.9|1⟧ What is shown by what is said by the Lord: «*If the eye or the hand or the foot cause thee to stumble, cut them off, and cast them from thee*»? [n: Matth. v, 29.]

⟦q3.10|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q3.10|2⟧ It is possible, according to the ready sense, to suppose that the parable hints at friends, as at eyes, and at members of our household, as at our hands, they being necessary to us; and at companions, who serve us after the manner of feet; whom, if they cause stumbling and harm the soul, the word has bidden us cut off. ⟦q3.10|3⟧ It is possible also to take the precept otherwise, according to the mode of anagogy. ⟦q3.10|4⟧ If thou hast contemplative knowledge as an eye, and it lifts [0816] thee up to arrogance and pride, cut it off. ⟦q3.10|5⟧ And if thou hast also a refined practice, and it becomes for thee a cause of elation, cut it off. ⟦q3.10|6⟧ Likewise also, if thou hast competence for service, which is the foot, and art puffed up as from this, stand away from it. ⟦q3.10|7⟧ For it is profitable for thee to be without these seeming virtues, and not through elation and pride to go to utter perdition.

⟦q3.11|1+2⟧ QUESTION 36

⟦q3.12|1⟧ What does «*The sun was lifted up, and the moon stood in her order*» show? [n: Habac. iii, 11.]

⟦q3.13|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q3.13|2⟧ When in us the sun of righteousness is exalted through good works and true knowledge, then the moon stands in her order: that is, our nature, which is subject to change and alteration, receives a firm order.

⟦q3.14|1+2⟧ QUESTION 37

⟦q3.16|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q3.16|2⟧ Since these things were said about those in the flood — and these had utter ignorance of God, and lived with wicked practices — such sins as they committed against men he forgave them through the wars and visitations and various mishaps that befell them day by day; but the sins concerning their ignorance of him he did not forgive them, [0817] in order that through faith in him, when out of love for man he came to be in the region of Hades, as many as believed might be forgiven the sin of impiety, and might live according to God in the spirit, having been judged, that is, in the flesh, as has been said, for their offences against men.

⟦q3.17|1+2⟧ QUESTION 38

⟦q3.18|1⟧ What is signified by David playing the psaltery and laying to rest the evil spirit of Saul?

⟦q3.19|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q3.19|2⟧ Everyone who, after the manner of the blessed David, shepherds the sheep, that is, the rational power of the soul, and kills anger and desire, as he did the lion and the bear, [n: I Reg. xvii, 36.] this man, using the word of teaching with a certain lofty contemplation, delights the hearer, and lulls to sleep the passions of vice in him.

⟦q3.23|1+2⟧ QUESTION 40

⟦q3.26|1+2⟧ [0820] QUESTION 41

⟦q3.28|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q3.28|2⟧ All the things performed in the Church have a principle above nature. ⟦q3.28|3⟧ And since these symbols are pre-eminently mysteries and images of the divine essence; and this is incomposite, while every creature is composite; and alone, as has been said, the holy Trinity is simple and incomposite: for this reason, then, the Church sets out the loaves and the chalices uneven, characterizing the Divine in them.

⟦q3.38|1+2⟧ QUESTION 45

⟦q3.50|1+2⟧ QUESTION 49

⟦q4.1|1⟧ [0825] that is, to cleanse the life of every stain of sins; and the head is our mind, which the word commands us to anoint, that is, to make bright with divine knowledge.

⟦q4.8|1+2⟧ QUESTION 53

⟦q4.10|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q4.10|2⟧ Both according to the ready sense, for the mind of the many, because of the uncertainty and the slipperiness of human choice, one must not call anyone blessed until, having passed through every virtue, he closes his life with an end beyond gainsaying. ⟦q4.10|3⟧ But according to the loftier sense, he who has begun, through repentance and ascesis, to humble and make weak the earthly way of thinking that lives in him is not yet to be called blessed, until through the strenuous toil of ascesis it is put to death and receives completion. ⟦q4.10|4⟧ For such a one is blessed, as having died with Christ through inactivity in evils, and rising again with him through the height of the virtues. ⟦q4.10|5⟧ For this the Psalmist also signifies, saying: *Blessed are the blameless*, the pure, that is, from vice; *who walk in the law of the Lord*, [n: Psal. CXVIII, 1.] who journey through good actions.

⟦q4.11|1+2⟧ QUESTION 54

⟦q4.13|1⟧ [0828] *Answer.* ⟦q4.13|2⟧ Cities are for the guarding and safety and keeping of precious things. ⟦q4.13|3⟧ A city, then, according to the mode of allegory, is the ascesis conceived from various modes: such as self-control as to wine, abstention from foods, intense vigil, and suchlike, which are a guard and safety. ⟦q4.13|4⟧ If, then, the demons persecute us from one of these, bringing arrogance or vainglory through such a form of ascesis, it is better to withdraw from such seemingly more exact conduct, lest we fall into pride, and to flee into another virtue free of vainglory, until the principle of passionlessness comes. ⟦q4.13|5⟧ Or also otherwise: he calls the cities human souls. ⟦q4.13|6⟧ For the apostles, sent to these by the Saviour, being driven away by those who judge themselves unworthy, move into the souls of others, which have become receptive of their teaching; and such cities of Israel they shall not finish, truly sojourning in them, and here being received in, and there being persecuted by them, until the Son of man comes in his glorious coming, filling all things.

⟦q4.14|1+2⟧ QUESTION 55

⟦q4.26|1+2⟧ QUESTION 59

⟦q4.27|1⟧ What is the etymology of *gastrimargia*?

⟦q4.28|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q4.28|2⟧ None either of the grammarians or of the orators has mentioned it. ⟦q4.28|3⟧ But Aristotle, in his *On Animals*, mentions an animal called *margos*, which is born from putrefaction, between the earth and the water. ⟦q4.28|4⟧ And from the time it is born it does not cease eating earth, until, having bored through the earth, it comes to the surface; and having come, it dies for three days; and after three days a rain cloud comes and rains upon it, and it comes back to life, no longer an ever-eater. ⟦q4.28|5⟧ And from this, I think, the ancient philosophers took their start in calling the voracious *gastrimargoi*. ⟦q4.28|6⟧ But one who knows how to apply himself piously to the things that are can also take what has been said according to spiritual contemplation. ⟦q4.28|7⟧ For every passion is of a nature to be born from putrefaction; and once it is born, it does not cease eating the heart that gave it subsistence, until through the habit of knowledge it comes to manifestation. ⟦q4.28|8⟧ And having come, it dies to the three powers of the soul. ⟦q4.28|9⟧ And thus the grace of the Spirit, appearing as through a cloud of teaching, gives drops of knowledge and makes alive: not according to the former passionate life, but according to the virtuous one, akin to God.

⟦q4.29|1+2⟧ QUESTION 60

⟦q4.30|1⟧ What is the parable in the Gospel of the workers hired into the vineyard? ⟦q4.30|1⟧ And what is the inequality? [n: Matth. XX, 1.]

⟦q4.31|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q4.31|2⟧ The judgement of God does not come about according to time and body, because the soul too is timeless and bodiless, in that it does not receive its growth and its end according to time, but makes the motions of its disposition timelessly. ⟦q4.31|3⟧ It is good to make clear by an example what is said. ⟦q4.31|4⟧ Often there is someone who has seventy years in the solitary life; and another, one day. ⟦q4.31|4⟧ And since the aim of the profession is that, having drawn the soul up from passionate attachment and relation to material things, one should transfer it wholly to God: then the one [0833] having seventy years, not having taken any care at all about such passionlessness, died; while the one having one day, having drawn up all his passionate understanding toward material things and having enfolded it wholly in God, was perfected. ⟦q4.31|5⟧ In the distribution, then, that comes about at the judgement, the one receives the wage worthily, as having brought the aim of the profession to its end; the other by grace, and only because he endured the toil of ascesis.

⟦q4.32|1+2⟧ QUESTION 61

VI, 4.] when, then, having been released from all things, or rather having let go of all things, our mind comes to rest in God himself, then it receives the fiery tongues, becoming god by grace.

⟦q4.35|1+2⟧ QUESTION 62

⟦q5.2|1+2⟧ QUESTION 64

⟦q5.3|1⟧ What does the paralytic signify, let down through the roof [0837] by four, [n: Luc. V, 19.] and was it possible, according to the history, for the house to be unroofed?

⟦q5.4|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q5.4|2⟧ According to the history, the house was certainly unroofed; since those who have become eyewitnesses of those places say that the roofs of the houses are of pumice stone and very light, so that one wishing to unroof can do this easily and quickly. ⟦q5.4|3⟧ But according to the mode of anagogy, the paralytic is every mind sick in sins and not able, through natural contemplation (for this is the door), to see the Word; but, having through faith unroofed the covering of the thickness of the letter of the law, it is let down through the four from its vain height to the Word who emptied himself, [n: Philipp. II, 7.] and by faith and practice receives its footing.

⟦q5.5|1+2⟧ QUESTION 65

⟦q5.6|1⟧ For what reason, when Elisha the prophet was mocked by the children, did he pray, and bears came out and dug up forty-two children? [n: IV Reg. II, 23.]

⟦q5.7|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q5.7|2⟧ Some say that the children were not Israelites, but of the foreigners; and not children in age, but in prudence. ⟦q5.7|3⟧ Since, then, they heard of the prophet doing many signs, and did not believe, but mocked him; and there were present some who did not share in their vain inclination; so that these might not be harmed, and the blasphemy might not run back up to God, God went out against them. ⟦q5.7|4⟧ But according to contemplation, every mind that is, like Elisha, bald, that is, pure of material concepts, is much mocked in the ascent of knowledge by the thoughts concerned with sensible things, and matter and form; forty signifying the sensible things, while matter and form, added, make up the number forty-two. ⟦q5.7|5⟧ The mind, then, is jeered at by such thoughts, so as to be thrown into reluctance toward virtue. ⟦q5.7|6⟧ These, then, it destroys through prayer, and through the bears, that is, through pleasure and desire; for such passions, handled by inversion toward the better, kill the mocking thoughts.

⟦q5.8|1+2⟧ QUESTION 66

⟦q5.9|1⟧ How ought we to understand the Son's ignorance concerning the consummation? [n: Marc. XIII, 32.]

⟦q5.10|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q5.10|2⟧ Ignorance is twofold: the one blameworthy, the other blameless; and the one is in our power, the other not in our power. ⟦q5.10|3⟧ And the blameworthy and in our power is the ignorance [0840] concerning virtue and piety; but the blameless and not in our power is whatever we do not know though we wish to know: such as the things happening far off, the things about to come to be. ⟦q5.10|4⟧ If, then, in the holy prophets the things far off and not in our power were discerned by grace, how much more did the Son of God know all things, and through him the human, not by nature, but by the union with the Word? ⟦q5.10|5⟧ For as iron made red-hot has all the idioms of fire (for it gives light and burns), yet is not fire by nature, but iron; so too the human of the Lord, inasmuch as it was united to the Word, knew all things, and displayed in itself these things befitting God; but inasmuch as human nature in him was not united, it is said to be ignorant.

⟦q5.11|1+2⟧ QUESTION 67

⟦q5.12|1⟧ What does the monastic garb signify, and, before this, what is the tonsure of the hair?

⟦q5.13|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q5.13|2⟧ As of all the members in the body the head has precedence, so also in the soul the mind holds the type of the head. ⟦q5.13|3⟧ This, then, must be shorn of all worldly concepts. ⟦q5.13|4⟧ And the colobium, [0841] since it covers the whole body but leaves only the hands bare, signifies that we must be clothed with moral philosophy, stripped of the practical activities of sin; and that this must always lie upon us, both upon potency and upon activity. ⟦q5.13|5+6⟧ For the power of the opposing dominion of the evil one is, according to Job, under the navel of the belly; and souls, according to blessed David, are filled with demonic mockeries. [n: Psal. xxxvii, 8.] And the mockeries are the various activities of fornication. ⟦q5.13|7⟧ And the analabos, since it has the cross both before and behind, signifies that we must, according to the Apostle, not only be crucified to the world, but the world also to us; [n: Galat. vi, 14.] so that, as we flee the world, we may have no hindrance, nor be held fast in our relation to it through the deceit that shows itself on its surface; nor again, pursued from behind out of the world by involuntary temptations, slacken the tension of piety; but may remain insensible and dead toward both the involuntary and the voluntary passions. ⟦q5.13|8⟧ And the cowl shows the grace of God guarding and covering our mind. ⟦q5.13|9+10⟧ For he who has shorn off the concepts of the world receives the helmet of salvation. [n: Eph. vi, 17.] And the sandals have this meaning. ⟦q5.13|11⟧ Since they are of dead skins, and lie under a small part of the body, in the same way as the whole body stands to the soles of the sandal, so must the soul use the body, and make it dead to the passions contrary to nature. ⟦q5.13|12⟧ And the mantle, since it is square, and the world too is of four elements, signifies that we must be clothed with natural contemplation, so as not to look at the things seen with a view to sense and passions, but through the principle in them to be led up to their creator. ⟦q5.13|13⟧ And that the left hand is bared signifies that our good actions must shine out in us, according to the Saviour's word: *that men may see our good works, and glorify our Father which is in the heavens.* ⟦q5.13|14+15⟧ And that the garments are black signifies that we must be invisible to the world, as having our *citizenship in heaven.* [n: Philipp. iii, 20.] And if anyone should take the pallium, being square, as standing also for the four generic virtues, he will fittingly not err.

⟦q6.17|1+2⟧ QUESTION 77

⟦q6.20|1+2⟧ QUESTION 78

⟦q6.21|1⟧ Why is it that he who is leprous in part is unclean according to the law, but he who is leprous all over is clean?

⟦q6.22|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q6.22|2⟧ He who is leprous in some part has dead the part in which the leprosy is. ⟦q6.22|3⟧ For as the vital blood withdraws, the place becomes hollow; and when the hollow of the leprosy sank beneath the priest's touch, it defiled such a man. ⟦q6.22|3⟧ So too he who has become leprous in some part of the soul, the soul being hollowed, that is, brought low, through the withdrawal of the vital power of virtue, becomes unclean. ⟦q6.22|4+5⟧ But he who is leprous all over has the colour of the leper, but has the vital blood throughout the whole body sown in the surface. ⟦q6.22|6⟧ This, then, signifies that he who has come to the uttermost limit of vice, and then repented, takes up again the vital power of virtue, and has only his conscience coloured by the former preconceptions. ⟦q6.22|7⟧ For he cannot, as though he had not done them, not take account of the things he did. ⟦q6.22|8⟧ For this reason, then, the law declares such a man clean.

⟦q6.24|1+2⟧ What do the five sacrifices offered according to the law signify: the sheep, the ox, the goat, the turtle-dove, the dove?

⟦q6.25|1⟧ *Answer.* ⟦q6.25|2⟧ According to one approach, the ram is taken, as the ruling part, for the rational power; the bull for the incensive power; the goat for the desiring power; the turtle-dove for temperance; the dove for sanctification. ⟦q6.25|3⟧ But if one must also inquire naturally into the things that belong to each animal, and fit to each of them its proper contemplation, those who have examined such matters say that the sheep offers three things to its owner: [0853] the fleece, the milk, the lamb. ⟦q6.25|4⟧ And the pupil of its eye goes round together with the sun, and each day it voids three hundred and sixty-five droppings. ⟦q6.25|5⟧ And the rational sheep, man, if he has been eager to offer himself as a sacrifice to God who acquired him, owes to give, as fleece, moral practice; as milk, natural contemplation (for this nourishes the mind); and as lamb, begetting through teaching the one who is being discipled, and being eager to bring him to God in all things like himself and perfect. ⟦q6.25|6⟧ And such a man also has the pupil of the eye going round together with the sun: that is, the mind goes round together with the *Sun of righteousness*, who carries our affairs through the provident governance of beings; that is, it follows, giving thanks both in afflictions and in prosperity, and at the providence that leads all things well. ⟦q6.25|7⟧ And such a man casts off each day three hundred and sixty-five droppings: that is, the things under time and coming-to-be, corruptible and superfluous, he casts away at every moment.

⟦q6.26|1⟧ And concerning the ox they say that it possesses a triangular heart, and a five-lobed kidney; and it also has three stomachs; and by day it has eyes like light, but by night like fire; and for a third of the day and a third of the night, looking toward the east, it breathes, blowing. ⟦q6.26|2⟧ Its right nostril is fragrant with musk;
