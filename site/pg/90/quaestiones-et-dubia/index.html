Maximus the Confessor

Maximus Confessor

c. 580 – 662

Monk, theologian and spiritual writer of the seventh century, the most searching Greek theologian of his age. He wrote on prayer, love and the ascetic life, on the difficulties in Gregory of Nazianzus and Dionysius, and on Christ's two natures and two wills against the Monothelite policy of the emperors and the patriarchs of Constantinople. Arrested and tried at Constantinople in 655 and again in 662, he was condemned, mutilated, and died in exile on the Black Sea coast. Migne prints his letters as Combefis edited them, in Greek with Combefis's Latin facing.

QUESTION 1

Which are the virtues of the soul, and which of the body?

Answer. The virtues of the soul are these: love, humility, meekness, long-suffering, forbearance, freedom from rancour, freedom from wrath, being without anger, being without envy, not judging, being without vainglory, almsgiving, temperance, freedom from avarice, compassion, being without arrogance, being without pride, compunction.

788QUESTION 2

What is the saying in the Apostle: «I will sing with the spirit, and I will sing with the mind also»? I Cor. xiv, 15.

Answer. One sings with the spirit when he brings forth through the tongue only the utterance of what is sung. And he sings with the mind when, knowing the power of what is sung, he is gladdened by contemplation.

QUESTION 3

What is: «I was conceived in iniquities, and in sins did my mother conceive me with craving»? Psal. l, 7.

Answer. Since the leading purpose of God was that we should not be begotten through marriage, out of corruption; but the transgression of the commandment brought in marriage, because Adam acted lawlessly, that is, set aside the law given him by God: all, therefore, who are begotten from Adam are conceived in iniquities, falling under the condemnation of the forefather. And the words, And in sins did my mother conceive me with craving, signify that Eve, the first mother of us all, conceived sin with craving, as though swelling with desire for pleasure. For this reason we too, falling under our mother's condemnation, say that we are conceived with craving in sins.

QUESTION 4

What is that said in the Apostle: «To be anathema from Christ for my kinsmen»? Rom. ix, 3.

Answer. I prayed, he says, to be handed over from Christ 789to the devil, so as to be scourged, and to undergo on Israel's behalf the punishments which by justice they owed for their unbelief toward God, only that they might be saved; in the way in which the Lord also handed himself over, and became a curse for us.

QUESTION 5

In how many modes does man sin?

Answer. I think that man sins in four modes: by being carried away, by deceit, by ignorance, by disposition. And the first three come easily to full knowledge and repentance; but he who sins from disposition, and comes to repentance neither by experience nor by time, has a punishment that is incurable.

QUESTION 6

What does the Lord show in the Gospel, saying: «Two sparrows are sold for an assarion»? Matth. x, 29.

Answer. Some say that the assarion is a piece of ten nummi. And by the ten is shown the letter iota; and this is the beginning of the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. Through the name of the Lord, then, are bought back the old and the new people, and the inner and the outer man.

QUESTION 7

What is that taken up by Elisha, said of Elijah: «Where is the God Aphphu?»?

Answer. It is interpreted according to three approaches: either, «Where is the God of my Father?»; or, «Where is the God of my great one?»; or, «Where is the God of the hidden one?»

792QUESTION 8

In how many modes are allegories, and what is tropology?

Answer. Allegory is that concerning inanimate things: for example, mountains, hills, trees, and the rest. And tropology is that concerning our members: for example, head, eyes, and the rest. For tropology is so called from being turned.

QUESTION 9

What is that said in the Psalm: «Sitting, thou didst speak against thy brother, and against thy mother's son thou didst set a stumbling-block»? Psal. xlix, 20.

Answer. He who speaks evil of the action of one of the same faith, and as it were sits down to the reviling of him, this one is fittingly said also to speak against his brother; and he who, carried along by envy, slanders the one adorned with wisdom and causes all to stumble, this is the one who sets a stumbling-block for his mother's son.

QUESTION 10

Since in the holy Diadochus, in the hundredth chapter, it is written that some will be judged through fire and purified in the age to come, I beg that the purpose of the Father be uncovered by clarification.

Answer. Those who have acquired the perfection of love toward God, and through the virtues have raised aloft the wing of the soul, are, according to the Apostle, caught up in the clouds, and do not come into judgement. Joan. v, 24 But those who have not wholly acquired the perfection, but have acquired both sins and right deeds, these come into the court of judgement, and there, through the weighing against each other of their good and bad deeds, being as it were tried by fire, 793if the scale of the good weighs down, they are cleansed of the punishment.

QUESTION 11

Concerning different justices.

Answer. Those wise in divine things say that there are three justices: human, angelic and divine. And the human, they say, is the equity and gratitude in the sensible things of this world; and the angelic, the ungrudging imparting of divine knowledge; and divine justice they define as suffering on behalf of those who sin.

QUESTION 12

For what reason did the Lord, rebuking Peter, say to him, Satan? Matth. xvi, 23

Answer. It was not, as some suppose, insultingly that the Lord called Peter Satan; but since the Lord's privations became habits for us: for example, his death became life for us; his dishonour became glory for us. And not knowing this, the apostle Peter, when the Lord said that he was about to suffer, supposed, following the nature of things, that it is impossible for the Life to die, or for so great a glory to be dishonoured. The Lord, then, destroying this supposition, on the ground that one ought not to seek the sequence of nature in things above nature, has deliberated to do these things through their contraries: through death, life; through dishonour, glory. So then, as [against] this opposing supposition of his, he says, Get thee behind me; that is, Follow me in counsel, and do not go before me, seeking a sequence of things.

Τὸ δὲ Σατανᾶς ὄνομα, φασὶν 796ἀντικείμενον ἑρμηνεύεσθαι· ὅπερ ὁ Κύριος οὐχ ὑβριστικῶς προήγαγεν, ἀλλ᾿ ὡσανεὶ ἔλεγεν, ἀντικείμενέ μου τῷ σκοπῷ.

QUESTION 13

Since Gregory of Nyssa in his writings appears, to those who do not understand the depth of his lofty contemplation, to hint everywhere at a restoration, I beg thee, say what thou knowest about this.

Answer. The Church knows three restorations. One, that of each man according to the principle of virtue, in which he is restored when he has fulfilled the principle of virtue that lies with him. The second, that of the whole nature in the resurrection: the restoration to incorruption and immortality. And the third, of which Gregory of Nyssa has made most use in his discourses, is this: the restoration of the powers of the soul, which had fallen under sin, back into that for which they were created. For just as the whole nature must, in the resurrection of the flesh, receive back incorruption at a hoped-for time, so also the perverted powers of the soul must, in the stretching-out of the ages, cast off the memories of vice lodged in it; and, having passed through all the ages and finding no rest, the soul must come to God, who has no end. And thus by full knowledge, not by participation in the good things, it must receive back its powers, and be restored to its original state, and the Creator be shown to bear no blame for sin.

QUESTION 26

What is the blasphemy of the Spirit; and how is it that «Every sin shall be forgiven unto men; but to them that have blasphemed against it, it shall not be forgiven, neither in this age, nor in that to come»? Matth. xii, 31; Luc. xii, 10.

Answer. The perplexity concerning the blasphemy of the Spirit has its solution from the very passage. For when the Lord was working various healings, the Jews ascribed these activities of the Spirit to the ruler of the demons. But that there is forgiveness neither here nor in the age to come for those who blaspheme must be sought next. Some of those who have searched out such things with exactness through the Spirit say that there are four modes through which forgiveness of sins comes about: two here, and two in the age to come. For since the memory does not reach to remembering the faults of the whole of time, so that man may repent of them here, the Master of nature, being a lover of man, arranged by economy modes of repentance even while we do not repent: in the age to come, as has been said, two. When someone who has sinned here indiscriminately, and again has done good indiscriminately, whether moved to pity and compassion toward his neighbour, or whatever else belongs to love for man: these things, weighed in the age to come at the time of judgement, forgiveness comes about for those to which the scale shall have its tilt. This is the one mode. And the second is this: when someone held in sins, yet hearing the Lord say, Judge not, that you be not judged, Matth. vii, 1. judges no one for fear, in the examination of the life he has lived he is not judged, as a keeper of the commandment. For the most truthful is not forgetful of his own commandment. And the other two modes have their forgiveness here: when someone being in sins is dealt with by providence through economy in misfortunes, in necessities, in illnesses. For in a way he does not know, God purifies him through such things; and if he gives thanks when tried, he receives the reward for his thanksgiving. But if he is ungrateful, he is purified of the sins for which he is chastened, but pays the penalty for his ingratitude.

QUESTION 28

What does what is said in the 101st Psalm signify: «I am become like a pelican of the wilderness»? Psal. ci, 7.

Answer. This pelican is a bird; and the serpent is greatly 809hostile to its young. And what does it contrive? It fixes its nest on a height, fencing it about on every side because of the serpent. What then does the evil-contriving serpent do? It looks about for where the wind blows from, and from there breathes its venom upon the young, and they die. The pelican, then, comes, and sees that its children have died; and it looks to a cloud, and flies up on high with its wings; and it strikes its sides, and through the cloud drips upon them, and they rise. The pelican, then, is taken for the Lord; and its children, Adam and Eve, our nature; and its nest, paradise; and the serpent, the apostate devil. The serpent, the author of evil, then, breathed upon the first-formed through the disobedience, and they became dead by sin. And so our Lord and God, through his love toward us, is lifted up on the precious cross; and, pierced in the side, through the cloud of the Holy Spirit he has granted us life everlasting.

QUESTION 30

What does this signify: «The heron's dwelling leads them»? ibid. 18.

Answer. They say that the heron is a bird, and that it lives in such chastity 812that whenever it is about to come together for intercourse, it mourns forty days; and again after that, another forty. QUESTION 31

What is: «Repaying the sins of parents upon children, unto the third and fourth generation, of them that hate me»? Deut. v, 9.

Answer. We have supposed the first generation to be the sowing of evil, that is, the assault; and the second, the desire; and the third, the habit of the generation of evil, that is, the consent; and the fourth, the activity, that is, the action. It is repaid, then, unto the third and fourth generation: that is, for the consent and for the action. For the assault and the desire are not liable, since the evil has not proceeded to its end.

QUESTION 34

Answer. Just as the Mind is cause of the Word, so also of the Spirit, but through the medium of the Word. And just as we cannot say that the word is of the voice, so neither can we say that the Son is of the Spirit.

QUESTION 54

What does «If they persecute you out of this city, flee into the other» show?

828Answer. Cities are for the guarding and safety and keeping of precious things. A city, then, according to the mode of allegory, is the ascesis conceived from various modes: such as self-control as to wine, abstention from foods, intense vigil, and suchlike, which are a guard and safety. If, then, the demons persecute us from one of these, bringing arrogance or vainglory through such a form of ascesis, it is better to withdraw from such seemingly more exact conduct, lest we fall into pride, and to flee into another virtue free of vainglory, until the principle of passionlessness comes. Or also otherwise: he calls the cities human souls. For the apostles, sent to these by the Saviour, being driven away by those who judge themselves unworthy, move into the souls of others, which have become receptive of their teaching; and such cities of Israel they shall not finish, truly sojourning in them, and here being received in, and there being persecuted by them, until the Son of man comes in his glorious coming, filling all things.

QUESTION 55

How also is «Mercy by weights», Isa. XXVIII, 17. according to the holy Isaiah? For nothing good is without judgement, even if it seemed so in the case of those in the vineyard. Matth. XX, 11.

Answer. If the mercy of God is weighed, is it then fittingly also circumscribed? Rather, then, one must suppose this: that just as we have a power of sight and of hearing and of breathing, receiving not the whole air, or the light, or the voice — since then participation in these would not have been left over for another — but each participates according to the power present to him proportionately; so also the mercy of God gives both the remission and the grace according to the quality of the dispositions of each of the subjects. For example, someone repented perfectly: he is also forgiven perfectly; and likewise in the case of the one who loves.

QUESTION 59

What is the etymology of gastrimargia?

Answer. None either of the grammarians or of the orators has mentioned it. But Aristotle, in his On Animals, mentions an animal called margos, which is born from putrefaction, between the earth and the water. And from the time it is born it does not cease eating earth, until, having bored through the earth, it comes to the surface; and having come, it dies for three days; and after three days a rain cloud comes and rains upon it, and it comes back to life, no longer an ever-eater. And from this, I think, the ancient philosophers took their start in calling the voracious gastrimargoi. But one who knows how to apply himself piously to the things that are can also take what has been said according to spiritual contemplation. For every passion is of a nature to be born from putrefaction; and once it is born, it does not cease eating the heart that gave it subsistence, until through the habit of knowledge it comes to manifestation. And having come, it dies to the three powers of the soul. And thus the grace of the Spirit, appearing as through a cloud of teaching, gives drops of knowledge and makes alive: not according to the former passionate life, but according to the virtuous one, akin to God.

QUESTION 65

For what reason, when Elisha the prophet was mocked by the children, did he pray, and bears came out and dug up forty-two children? IV Reg. II, 23.

Answer. Some say that the children were not Israelites, but of the foreigners; and not children in age, but in prudence. Since, then, they heard of the prophet doing many signs, and did not believe, but mocked him; and there were present some who did not share in their vain inclination; so that these might not be harmed, and the blasphemy might not run back up to God, God went out against them. But according to contemplation, every mind that is, like Elisha, bald, that is, pure of material concepts, is much mocked in the ascent of knowledge by the thoughts concerned with sensible things, and matter and form; forty signifying the sensible things, while matter and form, added, make up the number forty-two. The mind, then, is jeered at by such thoughts, so as to be thrown into reluctance toward virtue. These, then, it destroys through prayer, and through the bears, that is, through pleasure and desire; for such passions, handled by inversion toward the better, kill the mocking thoughts.

Answer. He does not speak of the Pentecost itself, on which the Spirit came down; but since the fifty days are called days of Pentecost, Paul was hastening to keep at Jerusalem the first day after the Pascha; so that it is plain that he bent the knee in the fasts.

QUESTION 74

What is it that David sings, saying, «Give thy might unto thy servant, and save the son of thine handmaid»? Psal. lxxxv, 16.

Answer. Since by nature we are slaves of God, and sons of his handmaid, Wisdom, he prays that the might be given to us first, that is, the dominion over the passions; and then through this salvation comes to us.

QUESTION 77

For what reason are Moabites and Ammanites forbidden to enter into the temple of the Lord, until the third and fourth and tenth generation, and even for ever? Deut. xxiii, 3; I Esdr. xiii, 1.

Answer. Since Moabite is interpreted «father's bowel», and Ammanite «mother's father», it 852is signified by these that whoever takes from another an example of vice, this man has laid bare the bowel of the father; but whoever begets sin from himself, this man is father of a mother, that is, of his own sin. Such men, then, will not enter into the house of the Lord until the third and fourth and tenth generation, and even for ever. That is: he who does not, through good habit and practice (for this is the third and fourth generation of the virtues), and through the name and faith of the Lord Jesus, and for the sake of the ten commandments of the law, and through the regeneration of the age that abides, which is to be, bring himself into kinship with God, will not enter into the house of God: that is, into the heavenly city, in which is the dwelling of all who rejoice. Psal. lxxxvi, 7.

QUESTION 78

Why is it that he who is leprous in part is unclean according to the law, but he who is leprous all over is clean?

Answer. He who is leprous in some part has dead the part in which the leprosy is. For as the vital blood withdraws, the place becomes hollow; and when the hollow of the leprosy sank beneath the priest's touch, it defiled such a man. So too he who has become leprous in some part of the soul, the soul being hollowed, that is, brought low, through the withdrawal of the vital power of virtue, becomes unclean. But he who is leprous all over has the colour of the leper, but has the vital blood throughout the whole body sown in the surface. This, then, signifies that he who has come to the uttermost limit of vice, and then repented, takes up again the vital power of virtue, and has only his conscience coloured by the former preconceptions. For he cannot, as though he had not done them, not take account of the things he did. For this reason, then, the law declares such a man clean.

QUESTION 79

What do the five sacrifices offered according to the law signify: the sheep, the ox, the goat, the turtle-dove, the dove?

Answer. According to one approach, the ram is taken, as the ruling part, for the rational power; the bull for the incensive power; the goat for the desiring power; the turtle-dove for temperance; the dove for sanctification.

Εἰ δὲ δεῖ καὶ τὰ ἑκάστου ζῴου προσόντα, φυσικῶς ζητῆσαι, καὶ ἑκάστου αὐτῶν τὴν πρόσφορον, ἐφαρμόσαι θεωρίαν, φασὶν οἱ τὰ τοιαῦτα ἐπεσκεμμένοι, ὅτι τρία προσάγει τῷ κεκτημένῳ τὸ 853πρόβατον, τὺν χαλλὸν, τὸ γάλα, τὸν ἀμνόν. Ἡ δὲ κόρη τοῦ ὀφθαλμοῦ αὐτοῦ συμπεριπολεῖ τῷ ἡλίῳ, καὶ ἑκάστης ἡμέρας ἀφοδεύει σπυράθους τξεʹ. Τὸ δὲ λογικὸν πρόβατον ὁ ἄνθρωπος, εἴπερ ἑαυτὸν προσφέρειν θυσίαν τῷ κτησαμένῳ Θεῷ ἐσπούδακε, χρεωστεῖ διδόναι ὥσπερ μαλὸν, τὴν ἠθικὴν πρᾶξιν· ὡς δὲ γάλα, τὴν φυσικὴν θεωρίαν (θρεπτικὴ γὰρ αὕτη τοῦ νοῦ)· ὡς δὲ ἀμνὸν, τὸν μαθητευόμενον ἀπογεννῶν διὰ τῆς διδασκαλίας, καὶ πάντα ὅμοιον ἑαυτῷ καὶ τέλειον σπουδάζων προσάγειν τῷ Θεῷ. Ἀλλὰ καὶ τὴν κόρην τοῦ ὀφθαλμοῦ, ὁ τοιοῦτος ἔχει τῷ ἡλίῳ συμπεριπολοῦσαν· τουτέστι τὸν νοῦν, τῷ Ἡλίῳ τῆς δικαιοσύνης, διὰ τῆς προνοητικῆς τῶν ὄντων διοικήσεως φέροντι τὰ ἡμέτερα, συμπεριπολεῖ· τοῦτέστιν, ἐπακολουθεῖ· ἕν τε γὰρ θλίψεσιν, ἕν τε εὐημερίαις εὐχαριστῶν· ἐπί τε τῇ καλῶς ἀγούσῃ τὰ πάντα προνοίᾳ. Ἀποβάλλει δὲ ὁ τοιοῦτος ἑκάστης ἡμέρας σπυράθους τξεʹ· τουτέστι, τὰ ὑπὸ χρόνον καὶ γένεσιν φθαρτὰ καὶ περισσὰ ἑκάστοτα ἀποῤῥίπτει.

Περὶ δὲ τοῦ βοός φασιν, ὅτι τὴν καρδίαν τρίγωνον κέκτηται· τὸν δὲ νεφρὸν πεντάβολον· ἔχει δὲ καὶ τρεῖς στομάχους· καὶ τὴν μὲν ἡμέραν, φωτοειδεῖς ἔχει τοὺς ὀφθαλμούς· τῇ δὲ νυκτὶ, πυροειδεῖς· καὶ τρίτον τῆς ἡμέρας καὶ τρίτον τῆς νυκτὸς κατ᾿ ἀνατολὰς ὁρῶν πνέει φυσῶν. Ὁ δεξιὸς αὐτοῦ ῥὶς, εὐωδιὰ ζει μόσχον· τὸ δὲ αἷμα αὐτοῦ, ἀναιρετικόν ἐστι πάντων τῶν ἡμέρων ζῴων, χωρὶς τοῦ κυνός. Δεῖ οὖν καὶ ἡμᾶς ὥσπερ βοῦν φέροντας, τὸν ζυγὸν τοῦ Χριστοῦ μεταχειριζομένους, τὰς λιθίνας ἀνατέμνειν καρδίας· καὶ τὰς μὲν ἀκάνθας τῶν παθῶν ἐκριζοῦν· πλατύνειν δὲ τὴν γῆν τῆς καρδίας πρὸς ὑποδοχὴν τοῦ θείου λόγου. Ἔχειν δὲ καὶ τὴν καρδίαν τρίγωνον, τουτέστι τὸν περὶ Τριάδος λόγον ὑγιῆ ἔχειν ἐν τῇ καρδίᾳ· ἵνα ὥσπερ ἡ καρδία διαδίδωσι φυσικῶς τὴν ζωὴν τῷ σώματι, οὕτως καὶ ἡ εἰς Θεὸν εὐσέβεια, τὰ μέλη τῆς ψυχῆς συνίστησι. Τὸν δὲ νεφρὸν ἔχειν πεντάβολον, τουτέστι τὸ ἐπιθυμητικὸν μὴ πρὸς τὰ πάθη τετράφθαι, ἀλλὰ διὰ τῶν εʹ αἰσθήσεων τὴν κτίσιν κατοπτεύειν εἰς μόνην ἐπιθυμίαν καὶ ἀγάπην τοῦ Κτίσαντος. Τρεῖς δὲ στομάχους κεκτῆσθαι· ὁ στόμαχος πρῶτον τὰ σιτία δεχόμενος, καὶ διὰ τῆς τεαπτικῆς δυνάμεως τὸ εἰλικρινὲς αὐτῶν ἑκάστῳ τῶν μελῶν παραπέμψας, τὸ λοιπὸν τῇ κοιλίᾳ δίδωσιν. Οὕτως οὖν ἡμᾶς προσήκει διὰ πρακτικῆς καὶ φυσικῆς καὶ θεολογικῆς θεωρίας ἀγομένους, καὶ τὰ προσ φορα πᾶσι τοῖς μέλεσι τῆς ψυχῆς ἀπονέμοντας, καὶ τοῦ ἥττονος προνοεῖσθαι μέρους· τουτέστι, τοῦ σώματος· τοῦτο γὰρ ἡ κοιλία.

But if one must also inquire naturally into the things that belong to each animal, and fit to each of them its proper contemplation, those who have examined such matters say that the sheep offers three things to its owner: 853the fleece, the milk, the lamb. And the pupil of its eye goes round together with the sun, and each day it voids three hundred and sixty-five droppings. And the rational sheep, man, if he has been eager to offer himself as a sacrifice to God who acquired him, owes to give, as fleece, moral practice; as milk, natural contemplation (for this nourishes the mind); and as lamb, begetting through teaching the one who is being discipled, and being eager to bring him to God in all things like himself and perfect. And such a man also has the pupil of the eye going round together with the sun: that is, the mind goes round together with the Sun of righteousness, who carries our affairs through the provident governance of beings; that is, it follows, giving thanks both in afflictions and in prosperity, and at the providence that leads all things well. And such a man casts off each day three hundred and sixty-five droppings: that is, the things under time and coming-to-be, corruptible and superfluous, he casts away at every moment.

And concerning the ox they say that it possesses a triangular heart, and a five-lobed kidney; and it also has three stomachs; and by day it has eyes like light, but by night like fire; and for a third of the day and a third of the night, looking toward the east, it breathes, blowing. Its right nostril is fragrant with musk; and its blood is destructive of all the tame animals, except the dog. We too, then, carrying like an ox the yoke of Christ, handling it, must cut open the stony hearts, and uproot the thorns of the passions, and broaden the earth of the heart for the reception of the divine word. And we must have the heart triangular too, that is, have the account of the Trinity sound in the heart; so that, as the heart naturally distributes life to the body, so too piety toward God may hold together the members of the soul. And we must have the kidney five-lobed, that is, the desiring power not turned toward the passions, but surveying creation through the five senses unto the sole desire and love of the Creator. And we must possess three stomachs: the stomach, first receiving the food, and through its digestive power sending on the pure part of it to each of the members, gives the rest to the belly. So, then, it befits us, being led through practical and natural and theological contemplation, and apportioning what is fitting to all the members of the soul, to take forethought also for the lesser part, that is, the body; for this is the belly.

Τρίτον δὲ τῆς ἡμέρας καὶ τρίτον τῆς νυκτὸς ἀφορῶντας πρὸς ἀνατολὰς πνέειν, τουτέστι τήν· τρισσὴν τοῦ Θεοῦ πρόνοιαν, συντηρητικὴν καὶ ἐπιστρεπτικὴν καὶ παιδευτικὴν ἀφορῶντας, ἕν τε νυκτὶ τῶν πειρασμῶν, ἕν τε ἡμέρᾷ εὐημερίας, εὐχαριστεῖν· ἔχειν δὲ τοὺς ὀφθαλμοὺς, ἐν μὲν νυκτὶ πυροειδεῖς, ἐν δὲ ἡμέρᾳ φωτοειδεῖς, τοῦτέστι, θεωρητικὴν δύναμιν, ἐν μὲν τῇ ἡμέρᾳ τῶν ἀρετῶν, τὰς ἐκ τοῦ Ἡλίου τῆς δικαιοσύνης ἀκτῖνας τῆς γνώσεως ὑποδεχομένην· ἐν δὲ τῇ νυκτὶ τῶν ὑπὲρ ἀρετῆς ἐπαγομένων ἀλλεπαλλήλων πειρασμῶν, τοὺς 856λόγους καταλαμβάνουσαν τῶν ἐγκαταλείψεων· ἔχειν δὲ καὶ τὸν δεξιὸν ῥῖνα εὐώδη. Δεξιὸν μέρος τοῦ ἀνθρώπου ἐστὶν ἡ ψυχή· ἐπειδὴ διπλοῖ ἐσμεν ἐκ ψυχῆς καὶ σώματος. Ὁ οὖν διὰ τὰς τῆς ψυχῆς ἀρετὰς, τὴν χάριν τοῦ ἁγίου Πνεύματος ἐπισπασάμενος, γίνεται τοῖς ἄλλοις εὐώδης. Τὸ δὲ αἷμα τοῦ τοιούτου, ἀναιρετικόν ἐστι τῶν ἡμέρων ζῴων· τουτέστιν, ὁ κατὰ παρατροπὴν θυμὸς, ἀναιρετικός ἐστι τοῖς μιμουμένοις αὐτὸν ἀνθρώποις· τροφὴ δὲ τοῖς ὑλακτοῦσι καθ᾿ ἡμῶν δαίμοσιν.

And to have the right nostril fragrant too. The right part of man is the soul, since we are twofold, of soul and body. He, then, who through the virtues of the soul has drawn to himself the grace of the Holy Spirit becomes fragrant to the others. And the blood of such a man is destructive of the tame animals: that is, anger, by perversion, is destructive to the men who imitate it, but food for the demons that bark against us.

And the goat has such a nature as this: climbing the mountains easily, it walks on cliffs, and takes delight in musical tunes; and its right horn, if it is burned as incense in any place whatever, produces the appearance of an earthquake. And it voids each day two hundred droppings. On this text

DRAFT (Wilson to read). Seventy-nine questions and answers that Migne prints under the name of Maximus the Confessor (PG 90.785–856), with a Latin version facing; the Greek title calls them “questions, and answers, and interrogations, and selections of various perplexing chapters”. Each is a numbered heading, a question, and an answer introduced by the word Answer. They take up hard sayings of the Old and New Testaments and read them literally and then spiritually: the two sparrows sold for an assarion, “Abraham gave tithes to Melchizedek” and why the priests of the law might marry, the paralytic let down through the roof, Elisha and the bears, the five sacrifices of the law and what each animal is said to signify. They take up the Fathers: Gregory of Nyssa and the restoration (the Church, the answer says, knows three restorations), a saying of Diadochus on judgement by fire, Dionysius’s nine orders of angels. They take up the monastic life and the liturgy: the tonsure and the garb of the monk, and the priest’s “Peace” to the people and their answer. And they take up the passions, among them the odd etymology of gastrimargia (gluttony), which the answer takes from an animal in Aristotle and turns to a passion born from putrefaction. The work breaks off in Question 79: Migne’s Greek ends on a row of points and the word “Λείπει” (“it is wanting”), and his Latin says that the end is lacking (Deest finis). In Questions 68 to 70 he prints rows of points where a question or an answer is wanting (the question of 68, the answer of 69, the question of 70); the English marks each as […]. On the text. The Greek is our own OCR of Migne’s plates; every doubtful reading was checked against the page image. Where the plate itself prints a misprint (23 places) the Greek stands as printed, and the cruces page lists each; a few readings (27) cannot be settled because the type is blotted, and these stand as printed. Migne prints his editor’s notes at the foot of the pages: Latin comments, Scripture references, and Greek scholia; they are shown on the cruces page with working translations, and his references to Scripture stand as notes in the English where a translator’s note gives the same reference. His square brackets inside the Greek give variant readings and, in a few places, a supplement or a conjecture; our notes explain each. English. An English translation exists: Despina D. Prassas, St. Maximus the Confessor’s Questions and Doubts (Northern Illinois University Press, 2010); readers who have it should keep it, and this is a new translation, not the first. What this edition adds is the Greek beside the English, line-tagged to Migne’s columns.
