I EPISTOLA AD LEONEM PAPAM III.

  • 1161A

Eximio et orthodoxo a Deo coronato magno viro, gemma sacerdotum, Leoni praesuli sede summa aureaque Romana, cum gloria et omni honestate feliciter regente, ille vilissimus omnium servorum Dei servus immarcescibilis gloriae vestrae coronae beatitudinis salutem et gloriam deposcimus, humiliterque, quasi coram sanctissimis pedibus vestris humano [ Forte, humi prostratus] gloriam et gratiam vestram optamus, et servitium sanctissimum vestrum obnixe prece deposcimus; quia in quantum nobis vires electus Dei Filius dignatus est, in vestro cupimus permanere servitio, sicut dignum et justum est tali vero angelico Deique servo. Nos enim humillimi 1161Bservuli vestri habemus dispositum orationis causa, Domino adjuvante, Romam pergere, et pia oscula ejus ostias [ F., pio osculo . . . ostia] salutare; et ad sanctissimum Petrum calidis buccis oscula in pavimento figere, et veniam delictorum [praeteritorum] indulgentiam rogare. Nunc vero scit almitas vestra, quia propter longitudinem terrae nostrae, et impedimentum paganorum huc ita, ut dispositum [habemus] die pro consule habuimus, occurrere minime valuimus, sicut nobis magna necessitas fragilitatis nostrae expetiit [ Cod. Sal., expetit]. Propterea vestram sanctam postulamus clementiam, ut pro nobis in ejus beatissimam confessionem veniam rogare velitis [ Cod. Sal., rogare delictis], ut per vestram 1162Aintercessionem a vinculis nostrorum delictorum absolutionem aliquantulum nobis pius Dominus largiri dignetur. Nos autem digni non sumus vestrum in aliquibus exercere servitium. Scimus a Deo et sancto Petro vobis retributionem et in hoc saeculo et in futuro mercedem et gloriam adipisci. Alma Trinitas et inseparabilis unitas custodiat Domnum Leonem summum sacerdotem, nunc et semper et in aeternum feliciter cum Dei gloria.

I LETTER TO POPE LEO III.

  • 1161A

To the excellent and orthodox great man, crowned by God, the gem of priests, to Leo the bishop, happily governing the highest and golden Roman see with glory and all honor: that most worthless servant of all the servants of God — we beg salvation and glory for the unfading crown of your glory and blessedness, and humbly, as though before your most holy feet in human fashion [ Forte, humi prostratus], we pray for your glory and favor, and with insistent entreaty we beg your most holy service; because, so far as the chosen Son of God has deigned to grant us strength, we desire to remain in your service, as is worthy and just toward so truly angelic a servant of God. For we, your most humble 1161Blittle servants, have resolved, with the Lord's help, to journey to Rome for the sake of prayer, and to greet his victims with devout kisses [ F., pio osculo . . . ostia]; and to set kisses with warm cheeks upon the pavement before most holy Peter, and to ask pardon and indulgence for our [past] offenses. But now your kindliness knows that, on account of the great distance of our land and the hindrance of the pagans, we have by no means been able to come here in this way, as we have it arranged [habemus], we had it on the day for the consul, as the great necessity of our frailty demanded [ Cod. Sal., expetit] of us. Therefore we beseech your holy clemency to be willing to ask pardon for us at his most blessed confession [ Cod. Sal., rogare delictis], so that through your 1162Aintercession the merciful Lord may deign to grant us some little release from the chains of our offenses. But we are not worthy to render you service in any matter. We know that from God and from Saint Peter you will obtain a recompense both in this world and, in the world to come, a wage and glory. May the kindly Trinity and inseparable Unity guard the lord Leo, the highest priest, now and always and unto eternity, happily and with the glory of God.

II. 449 ANONYMOUS POEM FOR THE POET'S FRIENDS.

Little letter, go quickly, in your course across the level sea; with the winds behind you, make for the strong mouths of the fish-rich Rhine, entering there where it rolls into the deep with rushing waves; 1162Bthen let your prow be drawn by a very long towrope, and let the ship not be swept swiftly backward by the river. If my Albric should come and meet you upon the stream, say to him in haste: hail, cattle-rich prelate. For prior Hadda serves you, for no more than a single night at Traject., honey, and compultim, and butur, inasmuch as Frisia pours out neither oil nor wine. From here lift your sails; in your flight leave Dorsdrada behind; perhaps black Hrotberct prepares no hospitable roof for you; see, the greedy merchant has no love for your song; but turn aside, you, to the shores of my poet Jonah: for there is sure rest for the weary who come thither. Here herbs abound for guests, here fish, here bread. The city of Cologne kindly opens her roofs to you: 1163Ahere greet Father Ricwulf with a humble voice; say: Your praise, beloved, will remain with me always. Hence you will make for the forts, your ship running through the waves, until you reach the calm waters of the longed-for Moselle. After you have furrowed the river's stretch with rowing, then halt your craft here, and let the stern take the sand, and seek on foot the holy places of Father Willibrord; and search out the roof of Samuel the priest; remember to knock at the doors with a Castalian quill, and steadily say to the boy who serves, in a Pythian voice: Publius Albinus sent me from the British world, to bring a sweet greeting to a most sweet Father. If the chance of a word in his presence be granted you, poured out upon the ground as a suppliant, kiss his consecrated feet, and say: Fare well, father Samuel; say: Live long, priest. 1163BUncover your lap now, and bring forth the honorable fathers Priscian and Phocas — and he delights in such a gift, if only Neptune has not drowned them in the sea. If perchance he should wish to lead you to the king's hall, here run through and greet the nobles, the fathers, the brothers. Before the king's feet spread out all your Muses; say many times over: Hail, best of kings, hail: be present to me as protector, guardian, defender, that no envious tongue may have power to tear at me with its harm — of Paulinus, of Peter, of Albric, of Samuel, of Jonah, or of whoever may wish to gnaw my vitals with his bite: let him flee far off at your terrifying, let him depart with nothing. Say with a hushed murmur: Fare well, Peter my teacher — that fierce one strikes with a Herculean club: beware. Rejoicing, embrace the gifts of master Paulinus, 1163Cand ten times take coaxing kisses from his honey-bearing lips. Duly greet Ricwulf, Raefgot, and Rado, and touch their ears little by little with your singing. Say: Companions, brothers, joyful ones, hail and fare well. Perhaps you will come to the distinguished city of Mainz, and there say an everlasting farewell to Lull the teacher: he is held the very model of the Church, the splendor of wisdom, in character and in life fully worthy of so great an honor. O Bassinus, glory of the good people of Speyer, 1164Acommend me, I beg, kindly Father, to Paul my patron, whose kindly house had made us both brothers. Who, devout Fulcrad, will dare to touch you with the lyric quill? By your merits you surpass the songs of the Muses. 450 Yet now allow this ivy to creep about your brows, preserve [ F., sacra], most high Father, and out of your accustomed goodness [bonitate sueta] permit me at least once to say hail to you. Come, on with it, O chaste one [charta], board the ready ship at once; let the curved keel swim beneath you the Ocean and the Rhine. Let no tawny stuff of gold, I beg, hold you back, which the weary settler brings forth from the bowels of the earth; let no forts, houses, cities, nor flowering fields 1164Bdetain you, dazed, for the space of even a single hour; but flee, break off all delay, hasten, run your course in flight. Safe, sound, rejoicing and thriving, may you find our friends, and gladly. May almighty God grant them salvation through the ages, and afterward lead them, glad, into the heavenly hall. When all this is done, return sure of your way to your homeland, and remember to tell what each one says to you; and when in spring the red buds . . . . . out of the bark burst forth, may they see you at play beneath our roof, and bringing us new songs once again. Then I will fashion for you golden garlands of fresh flowers, and you, joined with me, will rest in pleasant meadows.

IV. 456 VERSUS DE CELLA CORMARICENSI.

O mea cella, mihi habitatio dulcis amata, Semper in aeternum, o mea cella vale! 1165AUndique te cingit ramis resonantibus arbos, Silvula florigeris semper onusta comis. Prata salutiferis florebunt omnia et herbis, Quas medici quaerit dextra salutis ope [ Ed., ore]. Flumina te cingunt florentibus undique ripis, Retia piscator qua sua tendit ovans. Pomiferis redolent ramis tua claustra per hortos, Lilia cum rosulis candida mixta rubris. Omne genus volucrum matutinas personat odas, Atque Creatorem laudat in ore Deum. In te personuit quondam vox alma magistri Quae sacro sophiae tradidit ore libros. In te temporibus certis laus sancta tonantis Pacificis [ Ed. pacificos] sonuit vocibus atque animis. 1165BTe mea cella modo lacrymosis plango camoenis, Atque gemens casus pectore plango tuos. Tu subito quoniam fugisti carmina vatum [ Edit., fatum; Mabill., fato], Atque ignota manus te modo tota tenet. Te modo nec Flaccus, nec vatis [ Ed., fatis] Homerus habebit, Nec pueri musas per tua tecta canunt. Vertitur omne decus saecli sic namque repente, Omnia mutantur ordinibus variis. Nil manet aeternum, nihil immutabile vere est, Obscurat sacrum nox tenebrosa diem. Decutit et flores subito hiems frigida pulchros, Perturbat placidum et tristior aura mare. 1165CQuae campis cervos agitabat sacra juventus, Incumbit fessus nunc baculo senior. Nos miseri! cur te fugitivum, mundus, amamus? Tu fugis a nobis semper ubique ruens. Tu fugiens fugias, Christum nos semper amemus, Semper amor teneat pectora nostra Dei. Ille pius famulus [ F., pios famulos] diro defendat ab hoste, Ad coelum rapiens pectora nostra suos; Pectore quem pariter toto laudemus, amemus; Nostra est ille pius gloria, vita, salus.

IV. 456 VERSES ON THE CELL OF CORMERY.

O my cell, dwelling sweet and beloved to me, forever and unto eternity, O my cell, farewell! 1165AOn every side the trees encircle you with resounding boughs, a little wood ever laden with flower-bearing leaves. All the meadows too will bloom with health-bringing herbs, which the physician's right hand seeks for the aid [ Ed., ore] of health. Rivers encircle you with flowering banks on every side, where the fisherman exulting spreads his nets. Your cloisters are fragrant with fruit-laden boughs throughout the gardens, white lilies mingled with little red roses. Every kind of bird sounds out its morning songs, and praises God the Creator with its voice. In you there once resounded the kindly voice of the master, which with holy mouth handed on the books of wisdom. In you, at appointed seasons, the holy praise of the Thunderer sounded with peaceful [ Ed. pacificos] voices and minds. 1165BYou, my cell, I now bewail with tearful songs, and groaning I lament your downfall from my heart. For you have suddenly fled the songs of poets [ Edit., fatum; Mabill., fato], and now a wholly unknown hand holds you. Now neither Flaccus nor Homer the bard [ Ed., fatis] will have you, nor do boys sing the Muses beneath your roof. For thus all the glory of the age is turned about of a sudden; all things are changed in varied successions. Nothing remains eternal, nothing is truly unchangeable; shadowy night darkens the holy day. Cold winter suddenly strikes down the beautiful flowers, and a sadder wind troubles the peaceful sea. 1165CThe holy youth that used to drive the stags across the fields now leans upon a staff, an old man and weary. Wretched are we! Why do we love you, O world, you fugitive? You flee from us, ever rushing headlong everywhere. Flee then, you that flee; let us love Christ always, let the love of God hold our hearts always. May he in his mercy defend his servant [ F., pios famulos] from the dread foe, snatching up to heaven our hearts, which are his own; and let us with our whole heart alike praise him and love him: he in his mercy is our glory, our life, our salvation.

Et tibi per terras, silvas et flumina, montes, Felici rectum tramite donet iter. VI. TO CANDIDUS SETTING OUT FOR ROME.

My mind follows you, dearest Candidus, in sorrow; may your mind, I pray, carry me with it everywhere. Through all the places of God, may the grace of Christ accompany you as you go, sweet son, from the citadel of heaven. And may it grant you, over lands and woods and rivers and mountains, a straight journey on a fortunate path, so that the longed-for day may see you at last in the towers of that excellent City, in which are the holy thresholds of Peter, who may bear your prayers to Christ above the steep 1166Dsummits of heaven — he, the first Father in the world, in his mercy — 1167Aprayers which may flow from the mouth of your heart, with tears, your body prostrate on the ground before the altar of God. Thence, through those airy towers of walls that must one day fall, coming to the consecrated roof of devout Paul, stretched out in the sign of the cross, with groaning breast, there lament the offenses of father and of son together. And straightway, a devout traveler, hasten through the churches of the saints, praising God always with your mouth, who has granted the world to have such patrons, standing before the throne of the Father in the citadel of heaven — who by their rosy blood conquered kingdoms throughout the earth, bearing the cross of Christ upon their devout necks. Beg them again and again with a devoted heart mercifully to be present always to your prayers. Before their devout ashes let the tokens of your tears 1167Bstand always and everywhere upon the sacred stones. You too, throughout the city, with an eager mind, I pray to be present among the sacred orders and the feasts. Observe what befits each person and each season, so that, remembering, you may report it all to the Father, and may profit your own people by your counsels and kindly ways, so that through you the holy Church of God may flourish. If the grace of merciful Christ should go before you into the sacred sight of the apostolic Father, ask for the ashes of the Fathers, their garments, or perhaps the hair of the saints, or the great joys of the holy cross, that it may be a support to our life through the ages, and to our own likewise, always and everywhere devout. When all this has been duly accomplished, Christ having mercy, return prosperously to the Father; go quickly. 1167CLet Phoebus no longer see you lingering in the Hall, but rise with the Morning Star, exulting traveler. Let no flame-flying sun, no wind, no frosts or storm-clouds now delay the journey of one hastening to the Father. Love conquers all things: swifter of foot than the East Wind, go in haste, run, fly, hurry on, so that Jacob may joyfully see his Joseph with his own eyes before the day of dread death, in his own city. Why should I sing you more? Happy, and happily, go now along all your ways, my son, God having mercy. May the Creator of the world grant you all prosperity. Sweet and beloved son, fare well to your Father.

VII. ALBINI ORATIONES AD DEUM ET APOSTOLOS.

IX. ALBINUS DE SEPTEM SIGILLIS.

  • 1169A

Primum sigillum, nativitas. Secundum, baptismum. Tertium, crux. Quartum, sepulcrum. Quintum, resurrectio. Sextum, ascensio. Septimum, judicium. Haec sunt VII dona Spiritus sancti. Primum fuit sapientia, ut Christus de Virgine nasceretur sine virili semine. Secundum spiritus intellectus fuit, ut in baptismo per illas tres undas dimitterentur peccata, ut dicitur: Exspoliantes veterem hominem, induentes novum (Col. III, 9). Tertium consilii fuit, ut Caiphas ait: Melius est ut unus moriatur homo quam tota gens pereat (Joan. XI, 50). Quartum spiritus fortitudinis fuit, quia corpus in sepulcro fuit, et 1169BChristus alligavit diabolum in inferno, et illas animas quae ibi injuste detinebantur, reduxit ad regnum, et Dominus momordit infernum. Quintum spiritus scientiae fuit, quia cum resurrexisset a mortuis, nos fecit credere et resurgere, quia omnes resurgemus, ut dicitur: Exsurge, Domine, tu et arca sanctificationis tuae (Psal. CXXXI, 8). Sextum spiritus pietatis fuit, quia cum Christus ascendit ad coelum, et animae justorum ascendunt ad eum, ut Paulus dicit: Optabam 1170Adissolvi et cum Christo esse (Phil. I, 23). Septimum spiritus timoris fuit, quia quando Christus venturus erit ad judicium, remuneraturus justos, grandis timor peccatoribus erit, ut dicitur: Discedite a me, maledicti, in ignem aeternum (Matth. XXV, 41). Et reliqua.

Ista septem dona Spiritus sancti fuerunt in septem patriarchis. Sapientia in Adam, quia ad omnes feras, vel bestias, et volatilia coeli, et pisces maris posuit nomina, et primus propheta fuit, ut dixit: Hoc nunc os ex ossibus meis, et caro de carne mea (Gen. II, 23). Intellectus in Noe, qui arcam fabricavit in diluvio et gubernavit. Consilium in Abraham ut dicitur: Exi de terra tua et de cognatione tua, et de domo Patris tui (Gen. XII, 1). Fortitudo in Isaac, 1170Bquia inimicos dilexit. Scientia in Jacob, quia benedixit eum angelus Domini, et dixit ei: Non vocaberis ultra Jacob, sed Israel erit nomen tuum (Gen. XXXII, 28). Pietas in Moysen, quia dixit: Dele me de libro viventium (Exod. XXXII, 32), ubi me scripsisti. Timor in David, quia timuit Deum, quando Saul in speluncam introivit ventrem purgare, et ipse dixit: Absit, ut mittam manum meum in unctum Domini (I Reg. XXVI, 11).

IX. ALBINUS ON THE SEVEN SEALS.

  • 1169A

The first seal is the nativity. The second, the baptism. The third, the cross. The fourth, the sepulchre. The fifth, the resurrection. The sixth, the ascension. The seventh, the judgment. These are the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit. The first was wisdom, that Christ should be born of the Virgin without the seed of a man. The second was the spirit of understanding, that in baptism, through those three immersions, sins should be forgiven, as it is said: Stripping off the old man, putting on the new (Col. III, 9). The third was the spirit of counsel, as Caiaphas says: It is better that one man should die than that the whole nation perish (Joan. XI, 50). The fourth was the spirit of fortitude, because the body was in the sepulchre, and 1169BChrist bound the devil in hell, and those souls which were unjustly held there he brought back to the kingdom, and the Lord bit into hell. The fifth was the spirit of knowledge, because when he had risen from the dead, he made us believe and rise again — for we shall all rise again, as it is said: Arise, O Lord, thou and the ark of thy sanctification (Psal. CXXXI, 8). The sixth was the spirit of piety, because when Christ ascended to heaven, the souls of the just also ascend to him, as Paul says: I desired 1170Ato be dissolved and to be with Christ (Phil. I, 23). The seventh was the spirit of fear, because when Christ shall come to the judgment, to reward the just, there will be great fear for sinners, as it is said: Depart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire (Matth. XXV, 41). And the rest.

These seven gifts of the Holy Spirit were in the seven patriarchs. Wisdom in Adam, because he gave names to all the wild animals, or beasts, and the birds of heaven, and the fishes of the sea, and he was the first prophet, when he said: This now is bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh (Gen. II, 23). Understanding in Noah, who built the ark in the flood and steered it. Counsel in Abraham, as it is said: Go forth out of thy land, and from thy kindred, and out of the house of thy Father (Gen. XII, 1). Fortitude in Isaac, 1170Bbecause he loved his enemies. Knowledge in Jacob, because the angel of the Lord blessed him and said to him: Thou shalt no more be called Jacob, but Israel shall be thy name (Gen. XXXII, 28). Piety in Moses, because he said: Blot me out of the book of the living (Exod. XXXII, 32), wherein thou hast written me. Fear in David, because he feared God, when Saul went into the cave to relieve himself, and he said: Far be it from me, that I should put my hand upon the anointed of the Lord (I Reg. XXVI, 11).
