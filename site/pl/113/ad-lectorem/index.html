The theologians of Douai

Theologi Duacenses

1617

The Catholic theologians of Douai who prepared the 1617 Douai edition of the Bible with the Glossa ordinaria and Nicholas of Lyra’s Postilla, printed with Paul of Burgos’s Additions and Matthias Doring’s Replies. Migne reprinted their prefatory matter, addressed to the Catholic reader, at the head of his edition of the Glossa.

DUACENSIUM THEOLOGORUM IN GLOSSAM ORDINARIAM PROLEGOMENA. (Ex editione Duacensi anni 1617.) LECTORI CATHOLICO SALUTEM ET FELICITATEM.

PROLEGOMENA OF THE THEOLOGIANS OF DOUAI TO THE ORDINARY GLOSS. (From the Douai edition of the year 1617.) TO THE CATHOLIC READER, GREETING AND HAPPINESS.

Ad lectorem

11BSunt nonnulli studio novellitatis nimium accensi, qui omnem hunc laborem, quem in conservandis eruditae antiquitatis monumentis ponunt viri docti, repudiant prorsus, et abominantur, velut superstitiosum, quique praeclara ingenia ab excogitandis de novo excellentibus inventis avocet, et absterreat: dum nimirum ita fieri animadvertunt, ut nihil magnopere suspiciant homines probi, in litteris praesertim et bonis artibus, nisi quod aetate ipsa sibi conciliaverit auctoritatem. Hi tales procul dubio nos irridebunt, qui Glossam hanc ordinariam, et Nicolai Lirani Postillam (nomina vel sono ipso, et flexu vocis, eorum delicatis auribus horrendum quid, et barbarum affricantia), tantis vigiliis recudere, studiosorumque manibus obtrudere satagimus. Maluissent enim novum aliquod opus et antehac inauditum, aut saltem novorum hominum commentarios ad hanc rem adaptari: quorum in hoc saeculo polygrapho 12Bet polyhistore tantus est proventus, ut sint pene innumerabiles. Caeterum nos hujusmodi lectoribus placere non studuimus, minime interea dubitantes, modestis et catholicis lectoribus probatum iri hoc institutum nostrum, qui sciant et agnoscant, in caeteris quidem artibus inventa quaeque etiam recentissima, suam gratiam dignitatemque prae multis veteribus invenire posse: in arcanis autem divini verbi sensibus adaperiendis, et esse optima, quae vetustissima; et ea optimis proxima, quae vetustissimis sunt propinquiora, ut pote magis conjuncta purissimo veritatis fonti, ac proinde remotiora a lacunis et coeno perversorum spirituum, unde salutares et illimes aquae Scripturarum crebro solent infici et perturbari. « In antiquis enim esse sapientiam » pronuntiat Sapiens, et in veteribus Ecclesiae tractatoribus secura ponuntur sedulitatis nostrae vestigia, absque falsitatis, saltem damnosae 13Aet perniciosae, periculo: cum in recentioribus istis, qui antiquos illos parum venerantur, non nisi cum maximo animarum periculo, et certissima pretiosi temporis jactura studium nostrum possit versari. Illi enim pietatis et modestiae laudibus insignes, nihil sibi aut scribendum judicarunt, aut sentiendum, quod non Ecclesiae catholicae pontificis, aliorumque in ejus communione perseverantium magistrorum calculo probaretur: agnoscentes, ut animorum, ita ingeniorum esse in Ecclesia ordinatam hierarchiam, et « spiritus prophetarum subditos esse prophetis; » et illud Salomonis oraculum sibi ob oculos semper ponentes: « Verba sapientium sicut stimuli » (qui pungunt veritatis acie, non blandiuntur adulationis flosculis) « et quasi clavi in 13Baltum defixi » (solido fundamento fidei catholicae substrato, non in fluctivago humanae sapientiae sabulo) « quae per magistrorum consilium data sunt » (dum non unicuique licet de rebus divinis prout libet lascivire, sed ad certam regulam loqui coguntur: nec suum quispiam, etsi magnus sit ille, aut maximus, aliorum piorum consensui praeferre audet et propterea submittunt se vicario Christi, per quem Spiritus sanctus regit docentium linguam et calamum, ut mereantur accipere deificam veritatis lucem) « a Pastore uno, » qui est Redemptor noster. Isti autem novitatis admiratores, « sanam doctrinam » antiquorum « non sustinentes, » sed ad sua desideria doctrinam veritatis examinantes et exanimantes, « coacervant sibi magistros, » non attendentes 13Canimarum lucro, sed « prurientes auribus, » ut proinde mirari non sit aequum, si « a veritate » quidem simplici et solida « auditum avertentes ad fabulas » et picturata opinionum feralium lenocinia toto affectu « convertantur. »

Tibi ergo, lector catholice, fidei antiquae et avitae cultor atque admirator, labor hic noster desudavit: ut dum alii excellentes Ecclesiae nunc florentis doctores suorum ingeniorum divitias atque arma tibi expromunt, unde munitus bellum potens et indefessum hostibus fidei catholicae atque animarum praedatoribus indicas: etiam mediae aetatis optimos duces tibi subsidio daremus, qui suo tempore fuere pro muro et antemurali Ecclesiae, inter quos non extremo loco militant auctores istorum commentariorum, 13Dquos iterum detersa nonnulla armorum rubigine, quam contraxerant, in aciem producimus. Hinc enim maxime patefiet quo spiritu majores nostri ducti sunt, cum ad sacrarum litterarum tractationem accederent: divino nempe (non humano) quem ex Salomone superius audivimus magistrorum sibi invicem cum humilitate deferentium consilio, sub uno et ab uno pastore gubernato aperire salutares oraculorum coelestium recessus: quorum harmoniam et consonantiam in divini verbi expositione tanquam concilium et « consilium magistrorum, » primus velut in abaco aut diagrammate mathematico aspectui nostro subjicere tentavit auctor ordinariae Glossae, sic appellatae quia illam posteri omnes tanquam 14Aofficinam ecclesiasticorum sensuum consulere solebant, quotiescunque opus erat ad Scripturae sententiam recurrere; nec putarunt sibi aliquid aliud sapiendum quam quod ab illo « magistrorum consilio » repererant traditum, ut ita acquiescerent sapientis monitioni quam post illa verba subjecit: « His amplius, fili mi, ne requiras, » ubi et addit rationem evidentem: « Faciendi plures libros nullus est finis, » quasi, qui sapientium per consilium magistrorum examinatis verbis, et pastoris unius sententiae non acquiescunt, nunquam per omnes errorum et mendaciorum anfractus divagandi finem faciant, sed interminatis vexentur quaestionibus. Erat autem hoc Glossae opus, in illa librorum paucitate et penuria qua saeculum illud (si cum nostro conferatur 14Bhac in re pauperrimum) laboravit; non solum magnarum vigiliarum, sed etiam expensarum, quas oportebat effundere in sacris doctoribus undique conquirendis et describendis, cum nondum esset inventa typographia haec, thaumaturgia librorum multiplicatrix, quae (sicut ille dicebat de Nilo Aegypti rigatore) devolvit secum infinita ἔσθλα καὶ ὕργα, commoda et incommoda, probos et noxios scriptores. Cujus artis beneficio laudatissimus ille collectoris labor possit illustrior perfectiorque fieri superadditis apteque attextis aliis sanctorum Patrum expositionibus, quae ad sacrarum paginarum facerent elucidationem: quod in evangelica historia praestitit divinum Thomae Aquinatis ingenium, et in multis Scripturae libris recentiores Graeci, et copiosissime 14Cin Genesi atque Exodo doctissimus Veronae episcopus Lypomannus; et nos etiam forte, modo Deus vitam viresque dederit, aliquando tentabimus. Sed interea tamen, non quia aliquid fieri potest perfectius (ut facillimum est inventis addere, difficillimum primo invenire), ideo negligendum fuit aut abjiciendum Glossae hujus opus, quod primum omnium sanctos Patres, quasi in concilio congregatos, Scripturamque collatis symbolis exponentes introduxit: sed omnino erat a nobis suo nitori restituendum, ut possit cum auctorum laude et benedictione a posteris semper revolvi, quod a majoribus nostris jam per octo saecula in summo semper honore fuit habitum. Simul expedire judicavimus ut exstaret 14Dtam luculentum testimonium, non solum catholicae methodi, quam in exponendis Scripturis patres nostri semper sunt secuti, sed etiam diligentissimae eorumdem in sacris litteris industriae; et obstrueretur os impudentissimis haereticis qui ausi sunt jactare apud nostros sepultum neglectumque jacuisse verbum Dei, donec ab illis fax Evangelii (quinti scilicet) accensa fuisset. Quod cum numerosissimae nostrorum hominum in sacras Scripturas theoriae, meditationes, et scripta ante haereticos istos exortos edita, falsissimum esse testentur: tum vel maxime ista Glossae et Lirani commentaria, ex quibus isti alienigenae compilarunt quidquid ab illis recte in Scripturis disseritur, nec quidquam de suo addiderunt, praeter nonnullum splendorem 15Aorationis, et maledictis blasphemiisque oneratas excursiones, in quibus aut sua stabilire nituntur deliramenta, aut nostrae fidei impugnant fundamenta.

Caeterum occasionem huic editioni praebuit, inter caetera, quod a R. P. Antonio Possevino in Bibliotheca sacra fuit adnotatum. Cupientibus enim typographis ad Dei gloriam et communem fidelium utilitatem Glossam ea forma recudere qua a Parisiensibus ex recensione trium doctorum, Francisci Feuardentii, Jacobi de Cuelly et Joannis Dadraei, fuerat anno 1588 evulgata; ostensum est hoc Possevini judicium, quod diligentissimus ille Bibliothecae ecclesiasticae lustrator his verbis conceperat: « Sane vero » (inquit in verbo Glossa ordinaria ), 15B« accuratam illam operam, quam insignes illi theologi in ea posuerunt, integram praestare non potuerunt, urgentibus typographis editionem, et tumultibus Gallicis rem litterariam interturbantibus. » Quae cum non sine justis rationibus ab illo viro tali scripta viderentur, petierunt typographi ut conferremus paulatim exemplaria; inventaque sunt, collatione facta, tam multa hiulca in verbis, in sententiis, in testimoniis, tam multa in eisdem etiam pagellis bis ad longum posita, quaedam etiam dicta auctorum satis periculosa; quamplurima vero parum ad rem facientia, potiusque spatiis paginarum (quae erant satis amplae) replendis, quam sensui illustrando accommodata: ut modestissime Possevinus indicasse videatur quid in ea editione desideraverit, et tantorum 15Cdefectuum non nisi maximam causam, nempe civilium bellorum acerbam calamitatem assignaverit.

Ergo coacti sumus, partitis inter nos vigiliis, totum opus ad exemplaria manuscripta et antiquioris impressionis examinare, Patrum ipsorum originalia consulere: et sua singulis, a quibus desumpta erant, prout ab illis erant scripta, hoc est emendata restituere, indicatis ad singula testimonia auctorum (qui exstant) libris locisque, loco eorum quae erant ad replendas paginarum vacuitates vel repetita, vel superflue posita, vel non satis securo judice electa, alias classicorum Patrum sententias ascripsimus. Praeterea testimonia Rabbinorum (excepto uno aut altero) quae non erant a glossatore antiquo citata, 15Ditemque profanorum scriptorum, ut Plinii, Justini historici, Josephi Judaei, Philonis, quae in Parisiensi editione fuerant sub stellulis adjecta, expunximus; et pro eis, Patrum sanctorum dicta substituimus: his enim solum in catena illa seu harmonia auctoritatum locum esse voluit glossator ipse, et non auctoribus a fide et Christo alienis. Quae non frustra aut jactanter a nobis esse hoc loco dicta quivis per se poterit cernere, qui modo voluerit hanc nostram editionem cum altera Parisiensi aut aliis ex ea factis comparare. Et, si in fine cujusque tomi posuissemus loca restituta, correcta, justis de causis expuncta, vel suppleta: sane non pauci quaterniones addendi fuissent. Neque vero sapientissimorum 16Avirorum (quos supra nominavimus, quique Parisiensi editioni formam praescripserunt) existimationi debet nocere haec nostra animadversio: non solum quia bellici civilesque tumultus omnibus piis eo tempore calamos et libros e manibus excutere potuerunt; verum etiam quia non possumus nobis persuadere, ordinem a magnis illis viris praescriptum, fuisse ab operariis observatum, sed festinante ob causas a Possevino insinuatas prelo, maxima ex parte neglectum. Nam quanta alioquin in illis viris fuerit industria cum summa eruditione conjuncta, quantusque fervor in causa Dei et Ecclesiae propugnanda, alii ipsorum labores in lucem editi et in concionibus doctissimis contra haereticos habitis, exantlati, abunde magnum praestant argumentum.

16BSed neque nobis ipsis tantum assumimus, ut nullum prorsus nos commisisse in recensendo hoc opere erratum putemus: cum etiam in difficultates aeque molestas inciderimus; cum unus e nostris collegis prolixo et gravissimo morbo pene annum integrum ab opere fuerit impeditus, alii duo superiorum auctoritate ad regimen animarum assumpti et ab Academicis umbraculis ad aestum pastoralis curae abstracti, etiam ipsi moram non parvam editioni fecerint, et triennalem (ut de sua editione dixere Parisienses) nobis parturiendi hunc fetum fecerunt esse exspectationem. Caeterum audemus dicere lectorem non inventurum errata magni momenti, aut crebra etiam levioris notae: quorum in aliis editionibus infinitum pene numerum fuisse animadvertimus.

16CCognoscas autem, lector humanissime, nihil prorsus a nobis fuisse in glossa veteri immutatum, non ordinem, non verba ipsa nisi cum falsa aut corrupta esse, ex manuscriptis antiquisque exemplaribus, et originalibus ipsis sanctorum, deprehendimus; ut neque in Lirani postilla litterali et morali quidquam mutavimus; unde aliquas etymologias leviculas, ut quaedam sphalmata grammatica (quia sic videbantur ab ipso auctore conscripta, solent enim in his originationibus non raro postillatores ineptire) prorsus intacta reliquimus. Ea autem quae sunt a Parisiensibus addita Glossae veteri, asterisco seu stellula simplici sunt insignita; quae nos addidimus, duplicem habent sibi praelucentem asteriscum, atque illis notis ab auctoritatibus 16DGlossae veteris distinguuntur; idemque judicium est de nonnullis adnotationibus, quas Lirani postillae alicubi inseruimus, praefixis nempe stellulis, ut sic agnoscantur non esse Lirani verba, sed nostra.

Caeterum in replicis Mathiae Tornigi paulo plus nobis assumpsimus auctoritatis; nempe quaedam non raro expunximus convicia, quae ille in venerabilem Burgensem impotenter nimis et nimis saepe congessit. Neque enim decebat editionem tam necessarii operis, quodque in omnium manibus, etiam hostium Ecclesiae, esset futurum, indecora illa et docto homine indigna petulantia. Sic enim defendit auctor ille suum Liranum ut praeseferat aperte non 17Ase in certamen cum Burgensi descendere veritatis inveniendae causa, sed tantum ne videatur Liranus juste reprehensus; et cum in multis justissime a Burgensi taxetur, et ipse Liranus assumat sibi jure doctoris licentiam alios impugnandi: Tornigus tamen indigne fert Liranum illis in locis argui, ubi arguendus merito venit, et ibi contentiosum trahit funem; cumque parum referat ad Postillatoris gloriam rectene an secus in rebus adiaphoris nec ad fidem nec ad mores spectantibus arguatur; ibi Tornigus venerabilem episcopum, virum sanctum et optime de Ecclesia Hispanica meritum, conviciis onerat, et fideli confessori opprobrium Judaismi (quasi salus a Judaeis non processerit et quasi Liranus suus non fuisset etiam aliquando Judaeus) levissime 17Bobjicit. Illa ergo convicia pleraque omnia delevimus, quia nimis crebro occurrebant: praeterea vero nihil, nec in ipso etiam immutare voluimus.

Atque haec quidem de consilio nostro, deque his quae in hac editione (Deo duce) praestitimus. Forte autem non erit ingratum, si nonnulla attexamus de ipsis Glossarum et Postillarum auctoribus, ex Sixto Senensi praesertim aliisque viris doctis collecta.

Igitur Glossae ordinariae (quae nimirum sacro textui circumjicitur) praecipuus auctor ab omnibus celebratur WALAFRIDUS STRABUS, monachus Benedictinus coenobii Fuldensis in Germania (alius a Strabone monacho Anglo, Venerabilis Bedae fratre) ab oculorum vitio cognomen illud Strabi adeptus, Rabani Mauri Benedictini ejusdem coenobii abbatis, 17Cet archiepiscopi deinde Moguntini auditor et scriba amanuensis: vir saeculari litteratura abunde expolitus, divinae Scripturae peritissimus, et veterum auctorum lectione varia et assidua delectatus, poeta etiam pro illo aevo non infelix. Ejus ergo est haec collectio expositionum in Scripturam, quam tanti fecerunt posteri, ut quasi ordinariam glossam, seu linguam ipsam Scripturae appellarent, qua nimirum ipsam nobis loquentem intelligeremus, cum sine ea legentes sacra verba, linguam, quam non noveramus, audire nos putaremus. Magister sententiarum Glossam hanc κατ' ἕξοχὴν vocat auctoritatem lib. IV, d. 4; adducens enim Epistolam ad Hebraeos, cap. VI, sic ait: « Unde auctoritas dicit, ideo Apostolum pluraliter dixisse, fundamentum baptismatum, quia est 17Dbaptismus in aqua, in sanguine, in poenitentia, » etc.; quae omnia reperies in glossa ordinaria ad illud caput. Caeterum eam alii postea scriptores locupletasse videntur, ut Trithemius in Catalogo censet, et apparet ex apposito saepe Remigii nomine, qui ipso Strabo posterior est aetate; nisi forte dicamus adnotatum esse Remigii nomen pro Augustino, ex quo pleraque more suo desumpsit Remigius. Neque enim in Glossae manuscriptis exemplaribus adnotata sunt ubique auctorum nomina, sed hoc posterorum diligentiae opus fuit. Floruit autem Strabus sub Ludovico Augusto, anno Domini 840.

Glossae vero interlinearis auctor est Anselmus Laudunensis, monachus etiam Benedictinus, cognomento 18AScholasticus, natione Gallus, in divinis Scripturis et sacrorum doctorum voluminibus jugi studio eruditus, et in omnibus disciplinis non mediocriter instructus, ingenio acutus, eloquio pressus, et Laconicae cujusdam brevitatis amator. Unde et novo atque inusitato explanationis modo utriusque Testamenti libros brevissimis quibusdam summariis adnotationum ex Patribus desumptarum exposuit: quas minutissimis characteribus descriptas, inter ipsas Scripturarum lineas majoribus litteris exaratas, non minus curiosa quam utili industria, coaptavit: eumque laborem ad differentiam glossae Strabensis, quae in marginibus sacri textus describitur, glossam interlinearem appellavit. Claruit sub Henrico IV imperatore, anno Domini 1110. Certamen 18Bhabuit aliquando jam senex cum Petro Abelardo, ejusdem instituti monacho, qui tunc philosophiae fastu turgescens, non satis sobrie de rebus fidei loquebatur: licet postea correctus et Cluniacensi in coenobio sub Petro Venerabili sancte vivens laudabili fine quievit. Utraque vera glossa, tam ordinaria, quam interlinearis, maximae semper fuit apud omnes theologos auctoritatis, ut apud Holensem Alexandrum, D. Thomam, aliosque scholae magistros praecipuos qui earum auctoritate passim utuntur, cuivis potest esse manifestum.

De Nicolao Lirano haec habet Sixtus Senensis: « Nicolaus Liranus, natione Anglus vel, ut quidam scribunt, ex Lira Brabantiae oppido, genere Judaeus, et Hebraicis litteris a prima pueritia imbutus, post 18Cconversionem vero Franciscanae religioni addictus, scholasticae theologiae professor, et sententiarum ejus non ignobilis expositor; tanta quoque divinarum Scripturarum vera, pura ac germana intelligentia praeditus, ut in eis exponendis nullum eruditissimorum theologorum sui temporis habuerit parem. Edidit in singula tam Veteris quam Novi Testamenti volumina scholasticae Postillae libros octoginta quinque, studiosis Scripturarum sanctarum, ac praecipue tironibus ad majora properantibus, non utiles solum, sed valde etiam necessarios. In quorum prioribus quinquaginta libris universum sanctae Scripturae corpus absque orationis lenocinio, et verborum involucris, nudo et aperto sermone exposuit: summa in singulis diligentia aperiens et elucidans 18Dlitterae sensum, caeteris divinae Scripturae sensibus ut praestantiorem, ita difficiliorem; allatis in id non modo veterum Ecclesiae Patrum expositionibus, sed Rabbinorum quoque sententiis, quorum apud Judaeos praecipua est auctoritas. Quo expleto opere, iterum repetito labore in triginta quinque posterioribus libris utrumque Instrumentum morali expositione illustravit. Claruit sub Joanne XXII papa, et Ludovico Bavaro imperatore. » Haec Sixtus. Edidit praeterea Liranus tertium opus in Scripturam universam Veteris Testamenti, quod opus Differentiarum inscripsit, in quo diligentissime adnotavit locutiones omnes in quibus interpres vulgatus ab Hebraeo fonte differre videbatur, quod exstat seorsum, impressum, 19Alicet raro admodum inveniatur. Caeterum quod ait Sixtus, Nicolaum a nonnullis Anglum putari, nescio quo nitantur illi fundamento. Qui Brabantinum putant, habent pro se epitaphium ejus quod in Lirae oppido inter Antuerpiam et Mechlineam exstabat, in quo hoc habetur inter caetera distichon: Cui veteris perhumana dabat Brabantia Lirae Cognomen, Lira nam fuit urbe satus. Et clauditur hoc disticho epitaphium Hierophanta Deo sedet hinc diademate fulgens, Et Brabantiaco gloria digna solo. In editione itidem Glossae facta Basileae anno 1520 habetur in fine partis sextae: Biblia sacra cum glossa ordinaria, etc.; et, Postilla Nicolai de Lira Brabantini explicit. Obiit autem Nicolaus Liranus in magno 19BParisiensi Minorum collegio anno 1349, die 14 octobris, ubi etiam epitaphium habetur, ex quo deducunt alii, eumdem Nicolaum fuisse Normannum ex oppidulo Lirae Normannico; et faciet etiam conjectura probabilis, quod crebro utatur exemplis linguae Gallicanae, nusquam autem Brabantinae, vel Anglicanae. Eligat lector quod placuerit.

Sequitur Paulus Burgensis natione Hispanus, cognomento de Sancta Maria, genere Hebraeus, inter suos Hebraeos aliquando Rabbinus praestantissimus; et post suam conversionem ad fidem Christianam, primum Novae Carthaginis, et deinde Burgorum in Hispaniis episcopus, archicancellarius Castellae et Legionis: in gratiam filii sui Alphonsi decani Compostellanae ecclesiae (quem laicus susceperat ex 19Cuxore) ex singulis sacrae Scripturae libris loca plurima disseruit, in quibus conatus est Lirani explanationes corrigere et supplere, et opus Additionum inscripsit. Claruit sub imperatore Sigismundo, anno Domini 1430. Scripsit praeterea insigne opus contra perfidiam Judaicam, quod Scrutinium Scripturarum appellavit. Erat vir maximae in Hispania auctoritatis, tutor regi pupillo ob fidem et prudentiam datus, et notae pietatis. Praecipuo affectu D. Thomae doctrinam et Dominicanorum ordinem prosequebatur, cui et praeclarum in urbe sua coenobium exstruxit et dotavit. Civitatem autem illam tractumque Burgensem vi divini verbi ac zelo ita in fide Christi Domini roboravit, ut nemo deinceps 20Aex iis aut eorum posteris noscatur ab eadem fide recessisse, quemadmodum testatur in suis consiliis Navarrus. Ideoque celebris est adhuc et spectatae nobilitatis ejus familia; vocata, de Sancta Maria, quae sola in Hispania municeps est, et ad omnes reipublicae honores admittitur, exclusis omnibus aliis Hebraei sanguinis familiis.

Contra hunc Paulum Burgensem scribit in his commentariis Matthaeus Dornick, seu Tornigus, natione Saxo Minoritanus, provinciae Saxoniae quondam minister, scholasticae theologiae professor egregius, et inita pro defensione doctoris sui Nicolai Lirani pugna, additiones Burgensis evertere annisus est, editis novis concertationibus, quas commentariis Liranis statim post additiones Burgensis subjecit, et 20BReplicas, seu correctorium corruptorii Burgensis attitulavit. In quibus cum multa docte satis animadverterit, a conviciis tamen, ut diximus, abstinere non potuit, quibus Burgensem immerito satis incessit. Claruit sub Friderico III imperatore, anno Domini 1440.

Admonendus es praeterea, lector benigne, nos ipsum textum sacrum illo prorsus modo reliquisse quo in antiquis glossae et Lirani editionibus ipsum invenimus: quia ita videbatur convenire magis expositionibus ipsius Lirani: qui non eodem modo ubique legit, quo nostra Vulgata nunc a pontificibus emendata. Sed in fine uniuscujusque tomi addidimus correctiones Romanas a D. Francisco Luca Burgensi collectas, et illis attexuimus conjecturas nostras, quibus indicare voluimus quid sequi potuerint 20CRomani Patres in correctionibus illis, et cur ex variis lectionibus has potius quam illas approbaverint.

Indicem etiam, eumque brevem rerum tantummodo praecipuarum, et quae fusius explicantur, unicuique tomo dedimus: nam omnia quae quovis modo exponuntur, in indicem referre, fuisset ipsos commentarios iterum exscribere; et praeterea minutiora et breviora plerumque faciunt ad textus verbalem explicationem, quae proinde facilius invenientur invento textu sacro ad quem dicuntur, quam per indicem; quae autem diligentius et fusius tam in glossa quam a Lirano adnotantur, ea duntaxat in indicem conjecimus.

To the Reader

11BThere are some, too much inflamed with a zeal for novelty, who utterly reject and abominate all this labour which learned men spend on preserving the monuments of learned antiquity, as a superstitious thing, and as one which calls away and frightens off outstanding talents from devising excellent new inventions: since, doubtless, they observe it to come about that upright men esteem nothing greatly, especially in letters and the good arts, except what has won authority for itself by its very age. Such men will without doubt laugh at us, who are busy reprinting with such long vigils, and thrusting into the hands of the studious, this ordinary Gloss and the Postill of Nicholas of Lyra (names which by their very sound and the inflection of the voice rub something horrid and barbarous upon their delicate ears). For they would have preferred some new work, unheard of before, or at least the commentaries of new men adapted to this matter: of which, in this age of many writers 12Band of wide learning, the yield is so great that they are almost innumerable. But we have not been eager to please readers of this kind, while not in the least doubting meanwhile that this undertaking of ours will be approved by modest and Catholic readers, who know and acknowledge that in the other arts, indeed, any inventions, even the most recent, can find their favour and dignity ahead of many old ones: but that in opening up the hidden senses of the divine word, both those are best which are oldest; and those are next to the best which are nearer to the oldest, inasmuch as they are more closely joined to the purest fountain of truth, and hence further removed from the pools and mire of perverse spirits, by which the wholesome and unmuddied waters of the Scriptures are often wont to be infected and troubled. « For wisdom is in the ancients » pronounces the Wise Man, and in the old treaters of the Church the footsteps of our diligence are set securely, without danger of falsity, at least of damaging 13Aand pernicious falsity: whereas among these more recent ones, who little venerate those ancients, our study cannot be occupied except with the greatest danger to souls, and the most certain loss of precious time. For those men, distinguished by the praises of piety and modesty, judged that nothing should be either written or thought by them which would not be approved by the vote of the pontiff of the Catholic Church, and of the other masters persevering in his communion: acknowledging that, as of souls, so of intellects there is an ordered hierarchy in the Church, and that « the spirits of the prophets are subject to the prophets; » and always setting before their eyes that oracle of Solomon: « The words of the wise are as goads » (which prick with the edge of truth, and do not flatter with the little flowers of adulation) « and as nails fixed 13Bdeep » (the solid foundation of the Catholic faith being laid beneath, not in the drifting sand of human wisdom) « which are given by the counsel of masters » (since it is not permitted to each one to run riot concerning divine things as he pleases, but they are compelled to speak according to a fixed rule: nor does anyone dare to prefer his own view, though he be great, or very great, to the consensus of other pious men and therefore they submit themselves to the vicar of Christ, through whom the Holy Spirit rules the tongue and pen of those who teach, that they may merit to receive the deifying light of truth) « from one Shepherd, » who is our Redeemer. But these admirers of novelty, « the sound doctrine » of the ancients « not enduring, » but sifting and stifling the doctrine of truth according to their own desires, « heap up to themselves masters, » not attending 13Cto the gain of souls, but « having itching ears, » so that accordingly it is not right to marvel if, « from the truth » that is simple and solid « turning away their hearing unto fables » and to the painted enticements of deadly opinions, they with all their affection « are turned. »

To thee therefore, Catholic reader, cultivator and admirer of the ancient and ancestral faith, this labour of ours has sweated: that, while other excellent doctors of the Church now flourishing bring forth for thee the riches and the arms of their talents, furnished with which thou declarest a mighty and unwearied war upon the enemies of the Catholic faith and the plunderers of souls, we too might give thee in aid the best leaders of the middle age, who in their own time were a wall and outwork of the Church, among whom the authors of these commentaries fight in no last place, 13Dwhom we bring forth again into the battle line, having wiped away some of the rust which their arms had contracted. For hence it will be made most manifest by what spirit our forefathers were led when they approached the treatment of the sacred letters: namely by the divine (not human) counsel, which we heard above from Solomon, of masters deferring to one another with humility, governed under one and by one shepherd, to open the saving recesses of the heavenly oracles: whose harmony and consonance in the exposition of the divine word, as a council and « counsel of masters, » the author of the ordinary Gloss first attempted to set before our view as on an abacus or in a mathematical diagram, the Gloss being so called because all later men were wont to consult it as 14Aa workshop of ecclesiastical senses, as often as there was need to have recourse to the meaning of Scripture; nor did they think that they ought to savour anything other than what they had found handed down by that « counsel of masters, » so that thus they might acquiesce in the admonition of the wise man which he added after those words: « Further than these, my son, require not, » where he also adds an evident reason: « Of making many books there is no end, » as if to say that those who do not acquiesce in the words of the wise examined through the counsel of masters, and in the judgment of one shepherd, would never make an end of wandering through all the windings of errors and lies, but would be vexed by interminable questions. Now this work of the Gloss was, in that scarcity and penury of books under which that age (if it be compared with ours, 14Bvery poor in this respect) laboured; a work not only of great vigils, but also of expenses, which had to be poured out in seeking out from everywhere and copying the holy doctors, since this printing had not yet been invented, that wonder-working multiplier of books, which (as that man said of the Nile, the waterer of Egypt) rolls down with it infinite ἔσθλα καὶ ὕργα, useful things and harmful, upright writers and noxious. By the benefit of which art that most praised labour of the collector could be made more illustrious and more perfect, other expositions of the holy Fathers which would serve for the elucidation of the sacred pages being added above and fitly woven in: which the divine genius of Thomas Aquinas accomplished in the Gospel history, and in many books of Scripture the more recent Greeks, and most copiously 14Cin Genesis and Exodus the most learned bishop of Verona, Lypomannus; and we too perhaps, if only God grant us life and strength, shall some day attempt it. But meanwhile, nevertheless, it was not because something more perfect can be made (as it is very easy to add to things invented, very difficult to invent first) that the work of this Gloss was therefore to be neglected or cast aside, which first of all brought in the holy Fathers as though gathered in a council, and expounding Scripture by contributions brought together: rather it was altogether to be restored by us to its own lustre, so that it might always be turned over by posterity with praise and blessing of its authors, since it has now for eight centuries been held by our forefathers always in the highest honour. At the same time we judged it expedient that there should stand 14Dso splendid a testimony, not only of the Catholic method which our fathers always followed in expounding the Scriptures, but also of their most diligent industry in the sacred letters; and that the mouth might be stopped of the most impudent heretics who have dared to boast that among our people the word of God lay buried and neglected, until the torch of the Gospel (the fifth, to be sure) had been lit by them. And while the very numerous theories, meditations and writings of our men on the sacred Scriptures, published before those heretics arose, testify that this is most false: so above all do these commentaries of the Gloss and of Lyra, from which those foreigners compiled whatever is rightly discussed by them in the Scriptures, nor have they added anything of their own, save a certain splendour 15Aof speech, and excursions laden with curses and blasphemies, in which they either strive to establish their own ravings, or assail the foundations of our faith.

Moreover, what gave occasion to this edition, among other things, was what was noted by the Reverend Father Antonio Possevino in the Bibliotheca sacra. For when the printers desired, to the glory of God and the common profit of the faithful, to reprint the Gloss in that form in which it had been published by the Parisians in the year 1588 from the recension of three doctors, Francis Feuardent, James de Cuelly and John Dadré, there was shown them this judgment of Possevino, which that most diligent surveyor of the ecclesiastical Library had framed in these words: « Truly indeed » (he says, under the entry Glossa ordinaria ), 15B« those distinguished theologians could not render entire that accurate labour which they put into it, the printers pressing on the edition, and the tumults of France disturbing the republic of letters. » Since these words seemed to have been written by such a man not without just reasons, the printers asked that we should gradually collate the copies; and when the collation was made there were found so many gaps in words, in sentences, in testimonies, so many things even set down twice at length on the same little pages, some sayings of authors also rather dangerous; very many, however, little to the purpose, and suited rather to filling the spaces of the pages (which were quite ample) than to illustrating the sense: so that Possevino seems to have indicated most modestly what he found wanting in that edition, and to have assigned for such great 15Cdefects no cause but the very greatest, namely the bitter calamity of the civil wars.

Therefore we were compelled, dividing the vigils among ourselves, to examine the whole work against manuscript copies and those of older printing, and to consult the originals of the Fathers themselves: and to restore to each his own, from whom the things had been taken, as they had been written by them, that is, emended; having indicated at each testimony the books and places of the authors (which are extant), in place of those things which had been either repeated to fill the empty spaces of the pages, or superfluously set down, or chosen by a judge not safe enough, we have ascribed other sentences of the classic Fathers. Moreover the testimonies of the Rabbis (excepting one or another) which had not been cited by the ancient glossator, 15Dand likewise those of profane writers, such as Pliny, Justin the historian, Josephus the Jew, Philo, which in the Parisian edition had been added under little stars, we have struck out; and in their place we have substituted sayings of the holy Fathers: for the glossator himself willed that there be a place in that chain or harmony of authorities for these alone, and not for authors alien from the faith and from Christ. That these things have not been said by us in this place idly or boastfully anyone can see for himself, who will but compare this edition of ours with the other, Parisian one, or with others made from it. And, had we placed at the end of each tome the places restored, corrected, struck out for just causes, or supplied: truly not a few quires would have had to be added. Nor, however, ought this animadversion of ours to harm the esteem of the most wise 16Amen (whom we named above, and who prescribed the form for the Parisian edition): not only because the warlike and civil tumults could at that time strike pens and books out of the hands of all pious men; but also because we cannot persuade ourselves that the order prescribed by those great men had been observed by the workmen, but, the press hurrying for the causes hinted at by Possevino, for the most part neglected. For how great the industry of those men otherwise was, joined with the highest erudition, and how great their fervour in defending the cause of God and of the Church, their other labours, brought to light and expended in most learned sermons delivered against the heretics, furnish an abundantly great proof.

16BBut neither do we assume so much to ourselves as to think that we have committed no error at all in revising this work: since we too fell into difficulties equally troublesome; since one of our colleagues was kept from the work for almost a whole year by a long and very grave illness, and two others, taken up by the authority of superiors to the governance of souls and drawn away from the shades of the Academy to the heat of pastoral care, themselves also caused no small delay to the edition, and made the expectation of our bringing forth this offspring (as the Parisians said of their edition) a three-year one. Nevertheless we dare to say that the reader will not find errata of great moment, nor even frequent ones of a lighter mark: of which we observed there was an almost infinite number in other editions.

16CBut know thou, most courteous reader, that nothing at all has been changed by us in the old gloss, not the order, not the very words, except when we found them to be false or corrupt, from manuscript and ancient copies, and from the very originals of the saints; as neither did we change anything in Lyra's literal and moral Postill; hence some rather trifling etymologies, and certain grammatical slips (because they seemed to have been so written by the author himself, for postillators are not rarely wont to play the fool in these derivations) we left altogether untouched. But those things which were added to the old Gloss by the Parisians are marked with an asterisk or single little star; those which we added have a double asterisk shining before them, and by those marks they are distinguished from the authorities 16Dof the old Gloss; and the same judgment holds of certain annotations which we have here and there inserted in Lyra's Postill, namely with little stars prefixed, so that they may thus be recognized to be not Lyra's words, but ours.

In the Replies of Mathias Tornigus, however, we assumed a little more authority to ourselves; namely, not rarely we struck out certain abuses which he heaped up against the venerable Burgensis too intemperately and too often. For that unseemly petulance, unworthy of a learned man, did not befit the edition of so necessary a work, and one which was to be in the hands of all, even of the enemies of the Church. For that author so defends his Lyra as to show openly that he does not 17Ago down into the contest with Burgensis for the sake of finding the truth, but only lest Lyra should seem justly reproved; and although Lyra is most justly taxed by Burgensis in many things, and Lyra himself assumes to himself by the right of a doctor the licence of attacking others: Tornigus nevertheless takes it ill that Lyra is censured in those places where he deservedly comes to be censured, and there he pulls a contentious rope; and whereas it matters little to the Postillator's glory whether he is censured rightly or otherwise in indifferent matters which pertain neither to faith nor to morals; there Tornigus loads the venerable bishop, a holy man and one who deserved excellently of the Church of Spain, with abuses, and most frivolously 17Bcasts upon a faithful confessor the reproach of Judaism (as if salvation had not proceeded from the Jews, and as if his Lyra had not also once been a Jew). Those abuses, therefore, we deleted almost all of them, because they occurred too frequently: but beyond this, nothing; nor did we wish to change anything even in him.

And this, indeed, concerning our design, and concerning what we have accomplished in this edition (God leading). But perhaps it will not be unwelcome if we weave in some things about the very authors of the Glosses and Postills, collected chiefly from Sixtus of Siena and other learned men.

Therefore the principal author of the ordinary Gloss (namely that which is set around the sacred text) is celebrated by all as WALAFRID STRABUS, a Benedictine monk of the monastery of Fulda in Germany (another than Strabo, the English monk, brother of the Venerable Bede), who acquired that surname of Strabus from a defect of the eyes, a hearer, and scribe and amanuensis, of Rabanus Maurus, Benedictine abbot of the same monastery, 17Cand afterwards archbishop of Mainz: a man abundantly polished in secular literature, most skilled in divine Scripture, and delighted by varied and assiduous reading of the ancient authors, a poet, too, not unsuccessful for that age. His, then, is this collection of expositions on Scripture, which posterity valued so highly that they called it as it were the ordinary gloss, or the very tongue of Scripture, by which namely we might understand Scripture speaking to us, since, reading the sacred words without it, we should think we were hearing a tongue which we had not known. The Master of the Sentences calls this Gloss κατ' ἕξοχὴν the authority, Book IV, d. 4; for, adducing the Epistle to the Hebrews, chapter VI, he speaks thus: « Hence the authority says that the Apostle spoke in the plural, the foundation of baptisms, because there is 17Dbaptism in water, in blood, in penance, » etc.; all of which thou wilt find in the ordinary gloss on that chapter. Other writers, however, seem to have enriched it afterwards, as Trithemius judges in his Catalogue, and as appears from the name of Remigius often set beside it, who is later in age than Strabus himself; unless perhaps we say that the name of Remigius was noted in place of Augustine, from whom Remigius, after his custom, took most things. For neither in the manuscript copies of the Gloss are the names of the authors noted everywhere, but this was the work of the diligence of later men. Now Strabus flourished under Louis Augustus, in the year of the Lord 840.

But the author of the interlinear Gloss is Anselm of Laon, also a Benedictine monk, surnamed 18Athe Scholastic, a Gaul by nation, learned by continual study in the divine Scriptures and the volumes of the sacred doctors, and instructed not moderately in all disciplines, sharp in intellect, concise in eloquence, and a lover of a certain Laconic brevity. Whence also by a new and unaccustomed manner of explanation he expounded the books of both Testaments by certain very brief summaries of annotations taken from the Fathers: which, written in the tiniest characters, he fitted, with an industry no less curious than useful, between the very lines of the Scriptures inscribed in larger letters: and he called that labour the interlinear gloss, to distinguish it from the gloss of Strabus, which is written in the margins of the sacred text. He was renowned under the emperor Henry IV, in the year of the Lord 1110. He once had a 18Bcontest, when already an old man, with Peter Abelard, a monk of the same institute, who then, swelling with the pride of philosophy, spoke not soberly enough about matters of faith: though afterwards, corrected, and living holily in the monastery of Cluny under Peter the Venerable, he came to rest in a praiseworthy end. Each true gloss, as well the ordinary as the interlinear, was always of the greatest authority among all theologians, as can be manifest to anyone from Alexander of Hales, St Thomas, and the other principal masters of the school who everywhere make use of their authority.

Concerning Nicholas of Lyra Sixtus of Siena has this: « Nicholas of Lyra, English by nation or, as some write, from Lyra, a town of Brabant, a Jew by race, and imbued with Hebrew letters from earliest boyhood, but after 18Chis conversion devoted to the Franciscan religion, a professor of scholastic theology, and a not ignoble expositor of its Sentences; endowed also with so true, pure and genuine an understanding of the divine Scriptures that in expounding them he had no equal among the most learned theologians of his time. He published on each of the volumes of both the Old and the New Testament eighty-five books of scholastic Postill, not only useful to students of the holy Scriptures, and especially to beginners hastening on to greater things, but even very necessary. In the first fifty of these books he expounded the whole body of Holy Scripture without the allurement of oratory and the wrappings of words, in plain and open speech: with the utmost diligence in each opening up and elucidating 18Dthe sense of the letter, which is as much more excellent than the other senses of divine Scripture as it is more difficult; bringing to this not only the expositions of the ancient Fathers of the Church, but also the opinions of the Rabbis, whose authority is chief among the Jews. This work completed, taking up the labour again, in the thirty-five later books he illustrated both Instruments with a moral exposition. He flourished under Pope John XXII, and the emperor Louis the Bavarian. » Thus Sixtus. Lyra published besides a third work on the whole Scripture of the Old Testament, which work he entitled "Of Differences," in which he most diligently noted all the expressions in which the Vulgate interpreter seemed to differ from the Hebrew source; this is extant separately, printed, 19Athough it is very rarely found. But as to Sixtus's saying that Nicholas is thought by some to be English, I do not know on what foundation they rely. Those who think him a Brabanter have in their favour his epitaph which stood in the town of Lyra between Antwerp and Mechelen, in which this distich is found among other things: To whom Brabant most kindly gave the surname of ancient Lyra, For he was sprung from the city of Lyra. And the epitaph is closed with this distich Hence the hierophant sits with God, shining with a diadem, And a glory worthy of the Brabantine soil. Likewise in the edition of the Gloss made at Basel in the year 1520 there is found at the end of the sixth part: The holy Bible with the ordinary gloss, etc.; and, Here ends the Postill of Nicholas of Lyra the Brabanter. Now Nicholas of Lyra died in the great 19BParisian college of the Minors in the year 1349, on the 14th day of October, where also an epitaph is found, from which others deduce that this same Nicholas was a Norman, from the little town of Lyre in Normandy; and a probable conjecture will also favour this, that he often uses examples from the French tongue, but nowhere from the Brabantine, or English. Let the reader choose what he pleases.

There follows Paul Burgensis, a Spaniard by nation, surnamed de Sancta Maria, a Hebrew by race, once a most eminent Rabbi among his fellow Hebrews; and after his conversion to the Christian faith, bishop first of New Carthage and then of Burgos in Spain, arch-chancellor of Castile and León: for the sake of his son Alphonsus, dean of the church of Compostela (whom he had begotten as a layman of 19Ca wife), he discussed very many places from the several books of Holy Scripture, in which he tried to correct and supplement the explanations of Lyra, and he entitled the work Additions. He flourished under the emperor Sigismund, in the year of the Lord 1430. He wrote besides a notable work against Jewish perfidy, which he called The Scrutiny of the Scriptures. He was a man of the greatest authority in Spain, given as tutor to the boy king for his faith and prudence, and of known piety. With special affection he followed the doctrine of St Thomas and the order of the Dominicans, for whom he also built and endowed a splendid monastery in his city. And that city and the region of Burgos he so strengthened in the faith of Christ the Lord by the force of the divine word and by his zeal, that none thereafter 20Aof them or of their descendants is known to have fallen away from that same faith, as Navarrus testifies in his counsels. And therefore his family is still celebrated and of approved nobility; called de Sancta Maria, which alone in Spain holds citizenship, and is admitted to all the honours of the commonwealth, all other families of Hebrew blood being excluded.

Against this Paul Burgensis there writes in these commentaries Matthew Dornick, or Tornigus, a Saxon by nation, a Minorite, once minister of the province of Saxony, an excellent professor of scholastic theology, and having entered the fight in defence of his doctor Nicholas of Lyra, he strove to overturn the additions of Burgensis, publishing new disputations, which he set beneath the commentaries of Lyra immediately after the additions of Burgensis, and 20Bentitled Replies, or the correctory of the Burgensian corruptory. In these, though he noted many things learnedly enough, he nevertheless could not abstain from abuses, as we said, with which he assailed Burgensis quite undeservedly. He flourished under the emperor Frederick III, in the year of the Lord 1440.

Thou must moreover be advised, kind reader, that we have left the sacred text itself entirely in that form in which we found it in the ancient editions of the gloss and of Lyra: because thus it seemed to agree better with the expositions of Lyra himself: who did not everywhere read in the same way as our Vulgate now emended by the pontiffs. But at the end of each tome we have added the Roman corrections collected by D. Francis Lucas Burgensis, and to them we have joined our own conjectures, by which we wished to indicate what the Roman Fathers could have followed 20Cin those corrections, and why from the various readings they approved these rather than those.

We have also given to each tome an index, and a brief one, of the principal matters only, and of those which are explained at greater length: for to bring into the index all things which are expounded in any way would have been to copy out the commentaries themselves again; and besides, the smaller and briefer matters mostly serve the verbal explanation of the text, and will accordingly be found more easily, once the sacred text to which they are spoken has been found, than through the index; but those which are noted more diligently and at greater length, whether in the gloss or by Lyra, those alone we have gathered into the index.
