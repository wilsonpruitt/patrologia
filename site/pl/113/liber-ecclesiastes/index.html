LIBER ECCLESIASTES

One book of the Glossa ordinaria — the whole gloss, book by book.

PL 113, coll. 1115–1126  ·  Latin from the Migne printing  ·  New English translation  ·  column numbers follow the original plates, not the Garnier reprint

Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

CAPUT PRIMUM.

1115D

VERS. 1.-- Verba Ecclesiastae. (HIERON. in Eccle., t. III.) Tribus nominibus vocatus est Salomon, etc. 1116Dusque ad et aequalis magister est, et nescit se esse regem.

(ALBINUS.) Tradunt Hebraei hunc librum Salomonis esse agentis poenitentiam, quia in sapientia 1117Adivitiisque confisus per mulieres Deum offendit. Secum enim omnia vana reputavit esse et ad miseriam devenire eos qui in eis spem ponunt.

(HIERON.) Allegorice. Pacificus et dilectus Dei Patris Filius, etc., usque ad unde: In omnem terram exivit sonus eorum: et in fines orbis terrae verba eorum (Psal. XVIII).

VERS. 2. Vanitas vanitatum. (ID.) Pro vanitas vanitatum secundum quosdam interpretes possumus dicere, etc., usque ad donec veniat quod perfectum est.

Antequam suam ponat sententiam, quemdam apparatum videtur et ornamentum facere, cum dicit: Vanitas vanitatum, et omnia vanitas. Postea a majori, id est ab homine incipit, ostendens ipsum vanitati 1117Bsubjectum, id est mutabilitati. Et hoc est, vanitas, etc. Vanitas omnis homo vivens: quanto magis caetera! Unde, Vanitati subjecta est creatura, non volens, sed propter eum qui, etc. Quidquid enim non esse potest, vanitas dici potest, quia mutari potest. Solus Deus semper idem est quod est. Quod autem mutatur, quodammodo evanescit, et non est quod erat; ad comparationem Creatoris, omnis creatura vanitas potest dici et quasi nihilum computari.

(HIER.) Si cuncta quae fecit Deus, etc., usque ad cogor non semel, sed bis dicere, Vanitas vanitatum, et omnia vanitas.

VERS. 3.-- Quid habet amplius homo. (ID.) Post generalem sententiam, etc., usque ad sed nudi in terram redeunt, unde sumpti sunt.

1117CVERS. 4.-- Generatio praeterit, etc. (ID.). Prima recedit generatio Judaeorum, etc., usque ad nos autem Deum laudamus non in uno saeculo, sed in saeculis saeculorum.

VERS. 5.-- Oritur sol et occidit. (ID.) Ipse sol, etc., usque ad qui oritur fideli, occidit infideli.

VERS. 6.-- Vadit ad austrum, etc. (ID.) Hinc existimare possumus, etc., usque ad et ita ad orientem revertitur.

In circumitu pergit spiritus. (ID.) Ipsum solem spiritum vocat, etc., usque ad alia frigus consumeret.

VERS. 7.-- Omnia flumina intrant in mare. Dicunt philosophi, aquas dulces, quae mari influunt, vel ardente sole consumi, vel salsuginis maris esse pabula. Sed Ecclesiastes noster et ipsarum Conditor 1117Daquarum, dicit eas per occultas terrae venas ad capita fontium regredi et de matrice abysso in sua semper ebullire principia. Possumus per flumina, et mare, humanum genus accipere, quod in terram, unde sumptum est redit, secundum illud: Terra es, et in terram ibis (Gen. III). Nec terra impletur multitudine mortuorum. Et Hieronymus dicit torrentes non flumina, eo quod cito transeant.

(HIER.) Omnes torrentes, etc., usque ad quomodo in proverbiis filiae sanguisugae.

VERS. 8.-- Cunctae res difficiles. Non potest homo causas et naturas rerum explicare, nec oculus ad plenum intueri, nec auris, instruente doctore, ad summam scientiam pervenire. Videmus enim nunc 1118Aper speculum in aenigmate et ex parte, donec veniat quod perfectum est (I Cor. XIII), quod in hoc mortali corpore esse non poterit. Haec sententia contra eos est qui sine labore et dicendi studio sanctas Scripturas se novisse putant, et se sapientes, cum insipientes sint.

VERS. 9.-- Quid est quod. (HIER.) De his videtur dicere, etc., usque ad ut requiesceret Deus ab omnibus operibus suis die septima.

Quid est quod, etc. De naturis in principio mundi creatis, et de successione earum, quomodo veniunt et transeunt; ut homo moritur, homo nascitur, eadem natura est nascentis, quae fuit morientis. Sic de avibus, piscibus, serpentibus et omnibus animantibus; sic de herbis, arboribus et aliis naturis nihil 1118Bnovum invenitur quod antea non fuerit: nihil praeteritum quod post non fiat. Unde, Requievit Deus die septimo ab omni opere suo (Gen. II), a novarum creatione naturarum, non a gubernatione, secundum illud: Pater meus usque modo operatur, et ego operor (Joan. V). Sic ex primordialibus rerum seminibus totius saeculi tempus naturali cursu peragitur.

VERS. 10.-- Nec valet, etc. (HIER.) Estne verbum, etc., usque ad et praedestinatione Dei jam ea facta sunt quae futura sunt.

Jam enim praecessit. Sicut praeterita quae olim fuerunt apud nos, abscondit oblivio, sic quae nunc fiunt, qui nascituri sunt, scire non poterunt; silentio enim peribunt, et quasi non fuerint, abscondentur.

Vanitas enim vanitatum et omnia vanitas, quia 1118Comnia more fluentis aquae transeunt, et non redibunt. Cui sententiae totus mundus acclamat, et maxime genus humanum.

VERS. 11.-- Non est priorum. (HIER.) Seraphin propterea, etc., usque ad unde post, Non est memoria sapienti cum stulto in aeternum.

VERS. 13.-- Et proposui in animo meo, etc. Proposuit ergo Ecclesiastes, etc., usque ad quam sponte incurrerunt.

Hucusque generaliter de vanitatibus saeculi: hinc ad seipsum redit, et quis esset ostendit, quomodo omnia cognoverit, et docuerit, unde: Et proposui in animo meo, et caetera.

Hanc occupationem. (HIER.) Verbum anian Aquila, LXX et Theodotio περισπασμὸν quod latine 1118Ddistensio, etc., usque ad ad superiorem sensum cuncta referuntur.

VERS. 14.-- Vidi, etc. Consideravi omnia mundana, et vidi vanitatem et malitiam esse, id est, miserias spiritus, quibus animus diversis fatigatus cogitationibus laborat cognoscere, quod homini non conceditur.

(HIER.) Verbum Routh Aquila et Theodotio, etc., usque ad quibus anima diversis cogitationibus affligitur.

VERS. 15.-- Perversi. (ID.) Perversus non poterit adornari, etc., usque ad ut non possit comprehendi.

VERS. 17.-- Erroresque et stulti. (ID.) Prima sa 1119Apientia est stultitia caruisse, etc., usque ad supra disseruisse sufficiat.

CHAPTER ONE.

1115D

VERS. 1.-- The words of Ecclesiastes. (HIERON. in Eccle., t. III.) Solomon was called by three names, etc. 1116Das far as and he is an equal master, and knows not that he is king.

(ALBINUS.) The Hebrews hand down that this book is Solomon's, as of one doing penance, because, trusting in wisdom 1117Aand in riches, he offended God through women. For he reckoned within himself that all things are vain, and that those who put their hope in them come to misery.

(HIERON.) Allegorically. The peaceable and beloved Son of God the Father, etc., as far as whence: Their sound hath gone forth into all the earth: and their words unto the ends of the world (Psal. XVIII).

VERS. 2. Vanity of vanities. (ID.) For vanity of vanities, according to certain interpreters, we can say, etc., as far as until that come which is perfect.

Before he sets down his own statement, he seems to make a kind of preparation and adornment, when he says: Vanity of vanities, and all is vanity. Afterwards he begins from the greater, that is, from man, showing him subject to vanity, 1117Bthat is, to mutability. And this is, vanity, etc. Every man living is vanity: how much more the rest! Whence, The creature is made subject to vanity, not willingly, but by reason of him who, etc. For whatever is able not to be can be called vanity, because it is able to be changed. God alone is always the same that he is. But that which is changed in some manner vanishes away, and is not what it was; in comparison with the Creator, every creature can be called vanity and be reckoned as it were nothing.

(HIER.) If all the things which God made, etc., as far as I am compelled to say not once, but twice, Vanity of vanities, and all is vanity.

VERS. 3.-- What hath a man more. (ID.) After the general statement, etc., as far as but they return naked into the earth, whence they were taken.

1117CVERS. 4.-- One generation passeth away, etc. (ID.). The generation of the Jews departs first, etc., as far as but we praise God not in one age, but unto ages of ages.

VERS. 5.-- The sun riseth and goeth down. (ID.) The sun itself, etc., as far as who rises for the believer, sets for the unbeliever.

VERS. 6.-- It goeth to the south, etc. (ID.) [Vulg. Eccl. 1:6 gyrat per meridiem, "goeth round by the south"] Hence we can reckon, etc., as far as and so it returns to the east.

The spirit goeth forward in a circuit. (ID.) He calls the sun itself spirit, etc., as far as cold would consume the others.

VERS. 7.-- All the rivers run into the sea. The philosophers say that the sweet waters which flow into the sea are either consumed by the burning sun, or are food for the saltness of the sea. But our Ecclesiastes, and the Maker 1117Dof those waters themselves, says that they return through the hidden veins of the earth to the heads of the springs, and out of the mother deep boil up ever into their own beginnings. We can understand by the rivers, and by the sea, the human race, which returns into the earth whence it was taken, according to that: Earth thou art, and into earth thou shalt go (Gen. III). [Vulg. Gen. 3:19 pulvis es et in pulverem reverteris, "dust thou art, and into dust thou shalt return"] Nor is the earth filled with the multitude of the dead. And Jerome says torrents, not rivers, because they pass quickly.

(HIER.) All the torrents, etc., as far as as in the Proverbs the daughters of the horseleech.

VERS. 8.-- All things are hard. Man cannot unfold the causes and the natures of things, nor can the eye behold them to the full, nor can the ear, a teacher instructing it, arrive at the highest knowledge. For we see now 1118Athrough a glass in a dark manner and in part, until that come which is perfect (I Cor. XIII), which cannot be in this mortal body. This statement is against those who think that they know the Holy Scriptures without labour and without the study of speaking, and [think] themselves wise, when they are unwise.

VERS. 9.-- What is it that. (HIER.) He seems to speak of these, etc., as far as that God might rest from all his works on the seventh day.

What is it that, etc. Of the natures created in the beginning of the world, and of their succession, how they come and pass away; as a man dies, a man is born, and the nature of the one being born is the same which was the nature of the one dying. So of birds, fishes, serpents and all living things; so of herbs, trees and other natures nothing 1118Bnew is found which was not before: nothing past which is not done afterwards. Whence, God rested on the seventh day from all his work (Gen. II) [Vulg. Gen. 2:2 requievit die septimo ab universo opere quod patrarat, "he rested on the seventh day from all the work which he had done"], from the creation of new natures, not from the governing of them, according to that: My Father worketh until now, and I work (Joan. V). So from the primordial seeds of things the time of the whole age is carried through by a natural course.

VERS. 10.-- Nor is any man able, etc. (HIER.) Is there a word, etc., as far as and by the predestination of God those things are already done which are to be.

For it hath already gone before. As oblivion hides the things past which once were among us, so those who are to be born will not be able to know the things which are now done; for they will perish in silence, and, as though they had not been, will be hidden.

For vanity of vanities and all is vanity, because 1118Call things pass away after the manner of flowing water, and will not return. To which statement the whole world cries assent, and most of all the human race.

VERS. 11.-- There is no remembrance of former things. (HIER.) The Seraphim therefore, etc., as far as whence afterwards, There is no remembrance for the wise man with the fool for ever. [Vulg. Eccl. 2:16 Non enim erit memoria sapientis similiter ut stulti in perpetuum, "for there shall be no remembrance of the wise man any more than of the fool for ever"]

VERS. 13.-- And I proposed in my mind, etc. Ecclesiastes therefore proposed, etc., as far as which they incurred of their own accord.

Thus far generally concerning the vanities of the age: from here he returns to himself, and shows who he was, how he came to know all things, and how he taught them, whence: And I proposed in my mind, and the rest.

This occupation. (HIER.) The word anian Aquila, the LXX and Theodotion [render] περισπασμὸν, which in Latin is 1118Ddistensio, etc., as far as all things are referred to the higher sense.

VERS. 14.-- I saw, etc. I considered all worldly things, and saw that there is vanity and malice, that is, the miseries of the spirit, by which the mind, wearied with diverse thoughts, labours to know that which is not granted to man.

(HIER.) The word Routh Aquila and Theodotion, etc., as far as by which the soul is afflicted with diverse thoughts.

VERS. 15.-- The perverse. (ID.) The perverse man will not be able to be adorned, etc., as far as that it cannot be comprehended.

VERS. 17.-- And errors and the foolish. (ID.) [Vulg. Eccl. 1:17 erroresque et stultitiam, "and errors, and folly"] The first 1119Awisdom is to have been without folly, etc., as far as let it suffice to have discoursed above.

CAPUT II.

VERS. 1.-- Dixi ergo. (HIER.) Postquam in multitudine sapientiae, etc., usque ad tunc non vanitas erit, sed veritas.

VERS. 2.-- Risum reputavi errorem. (ID.) Ubi nos dicimus, etc., usque ad laeta sibi et prospera promittunt.

VERS. 3.-- Cogitavi in corde. (ID.) Ebrietas, etc., usque ad quid vitandum.

VERS. 4.-- Plantavi vineas. In domo divitis, etc., usque ad sine qua caetera ligna siccabuntur.

VERS. 6.-- Et exstruxi mihi piscinas, etc., usque ad et serotinam exspectat de coelo pluviam.

1119BVERS. 7.-- Possedi servos: timore servientes, et spiritualia desiderantes, nec timentes.

Vernaculos eos dicimus, qui sunt quidem de Ecclesia, et praedictos servos et ancillas antecedunt; nec tamen liberi sunt.

Vernaculi dicuntur filii servorum, qui in domo domini sui nati sunt.

Armenta quoque et magnos. Hi sunt, qui absque ratione et scientia Scripturarum laborant quidem in Ecclesia, sed nondum ad hominem perveniunt, id est ad imaginem Dei.

(HIER.) Et quidem possessio armenti. Nota quod, etc., usque ad vel vernaculi.

VERS. 8.-- Substantias regum. (ID.) Dogmata philosophorum, etc., usque ad et prudentias prudentium 1119Creprobat.

Feci mihi cantores. Cantor, etc., usque ad, Cantores et cantatrices, uterque sexus me oblectaret.

Delicias filiorum hominum. Sapientiam scilicet, quae, sicut paradisus, poma habet varia et multiplices voluptates. De qua Psalmista: Delectare in Domino, et dabit tibi petitiones cordis tui (Psal. XXXVI). Et, Torrente voluptatis tuae potabis eos (Psal. XXXV).

VERS. 9.-- Qui ante me fuerunt in Jerusalem. Ad litteram: Non est magna laus. Ante Salomonem enim regnaverunt Saul, cujus tempore regnum coepit, et David, sub quo satis parvum erat regnum.

Perseveravit. Alii, stetit, id est, ei in carne posito sapientia permansit, non proficiens per momenta, sicut in nobis, sed in plenitudine stans.

1119DVERS. 10.-- Et omnia quae. Sed quanto studio ista crescebant, tanto sapientia deerat, quia per omnia desideria rapiebat, putabamque hunc esse fructum laborum meorum, si libidini servirem; sed tandem in me reversus, vidi omnia vana esse.

VERS. 11.-- Sub sole. (HIER.) Christo. In sole posuit tabernaculum suum. Qui ergo ad solis claritatem ordinem constantiamque nondum pervenit, in eo Christus non habitat.

VERS. 13.-- Et vidi quia tantum. Cum hoc cogitarem, quod sapiens, lux, stultus, tenebrae: illius oculi in coelo, hujus, in terra, repente mihi surrepsit, quare communi morte pereant, communi miseria deprimuntur, et hujusmodi.

1120AVERS. 18.-- Rursus detestatus. (HIER.) Spirituali labore sancti die noctuque in lege Domini meditantur, etc., usque ad sapientia enim non est terrenas divitias congregare.

Habiturus. Hoc ipsi Salomoni contigit, qui non sui similem habuit filium Roboam. Ex quo intelligimus filium nec dignum quidem haereditate patris, si stultus sit.

VERS. 21.-- Nam cum alius. (ID.) Supra de incerto haerede, etc., usque ad ex ratione.

VERS. 24.-- Nonne melius. (ID.) Postquam omnia pertractavi, etc., usque ad quantum sufficit?

VERS. 26.-- Homini bono. (ID.) Corrigenti mores suos, etc., usque ad et spiritus praesumptione composita esse.

CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1.-- I said therefore. (HIER.) [Vulg. Eccl. 2:1 Dixi ego in corde meo, "I said in my heart"] After that in the multitude of wisdom, etc., as far as then it will not be vanity, but truth.

VERS. 2.-- Laughter I counted error. (ID.) Where we say, etc., as far as they promise themselves glad and prosperous things.

VERS. 3.-- I thought in my heart. (ID.) Drunkenness, etc., as far as what is to be shunned.

VERS. 4.-- I planted vineyards. In the house of a rich man, etc., as far as without which the other trees will be dried up.

VERS. 6.-- And I made me ponds, etc., as far as and awaits the late rain from heaven.

1119BVERS. 7.-- I got me servants: those serving through fear, and those desiring spiritual things, and not fearing.

We call those homeborn who are indeed of the Church, and go before the aforesaid menservants and maidservants; nor yet are they free.

The homeborn are said to be the sons of servants, who were born in the house of their lord.

Herds also and great flocks of sheep. These are they who, without reason and without knowledge of the Scriptures, do indeed labour in the Church, but do not yet come to the man, that is, to the image of God.

(HIER.) And indeed the possession of a herd. Note that, etc., as far as or homeborn.

VERS. 8.-- The substance of kings. (ID.) The teachings of the philosophers, etc., as far as and he reproves the prudence of the prudent 1119C.

I made me singing men. A singer, etc., as far as, Singing men and singing women, that either sex might delight me.

The delights of the sons of men. Wisdom, namely, which, like a paradise, has various fruits and manifold delights. Of which the Psalmist: Delight in the Lord, and he will give thee the requests of thy heart (Psal. XXXVI). And, Thou shalt make them drink of the torrent of thy pleasure (Psal. XXXV).

VERS. 9.-- Who were before me in Jerusalem. According to the letter: It is not great praise. For before Solomon there reigned Saul, in whose time the kingdom began, and David, under whom the kingdom was small enough.

It continued. Others, it stood, that is, wisdom remained with him while he was placed in the flesh, not increasing by moments, as in us, but standing in fulness.

1119DVERS. 10.-- And all things which. But by how much these things grew with zeal, by so much was wisdom lacking, because it hurried [me] through all desires, and I thought this to be the fruit of my labours, if I should serve lust; but at last, returning into myself, I saw that all things are vain.

VERS. 11.-- Under the sun. (HIER.) Christ. In the sun he hath set his tabernacle. He therefore who has not yet come to the brightness of the sun and to its order and constancy, in him Christ does not dwell.

VERS. 13.-- And I saw that so far. When I was thinking this, that the wise man is light, the fool darkness: the eyes of the one in heaven, of the other on earth, there suddenly stole upon me [the thought], why they perish by a common death, are pressed down by a common misery, and the like.

1120AVERS. 18.-- Again I detested. (HIER.) By spiritual labour the saints meditate day and night in the law of the Lord, etc., as far as for it is not wisdom to gather earthly riches.

Being about to have. This befell Solomon himself, who had a son Roboam not like himself. From which we understand that a son is not even worthy of his father's inheritance, if he be foolish.

VERS. 21.-- For when another. (ID.) Above, concerning the uncertain heir, etc., as far as from reason.

VERS. 24.-- Is it not better. (ID.) After I had gone through all things, etc., as far as as much as suffices?

VERS. 26.-- To the good man. (ID.) To him that corrects his own manners, etc., as far as and to be composed by the presumption of the spirit.

CAPUT III.

1120B

VERS. 1.-- Omnia tempus habent. (HIER.) Incertum et fluctuantem statum conditionis humanae supra docuit, etc., usque ad nec loco nec tempore continentur.

VERS. 2.-- Tempus plantandi, etc. (ID.) Nulli dubium est, etc., usque ad quia perfecta charitas foras mittit timorem.

VERS. 3.-- Tempus destruendi, etc. Non possumus aedificare bona, nisi prius destruxerimus mala, sicut Jeremiae dictum est a Deo, ut ante eradicaret et suffoderet et perderet, et postea aedificaret atque plantaret (Jerem. I).

VERS. 4.-- Tempus plangendi, etc. Plangendum est nunc ut in futuro saltemus, illam saltationem, 1120Cquam David ante arcam testamenti (II Reg. VI). Saulis filiae displicens, magis placuit Deo.

VERS. 5.-- Tempus spargendi lapides, etc. (HIER.) Miror, etc., usque ad et ad poenitentiam vocat.

Tempus amplexandi. Tempus amplexandi sapientiam, et contemplationi strictius inhaerendi; et tempus longe fieri a complexu, scilicet sapientiae, ut curae temporis, et his sine quibus vivere non possumus, serviamus.

VERS. 7.-- Tempus tacendi et tempus. (HIER.) Docemus in Ecclesiis quod nescimus. Omnes artes absque doctore non discimus, sola haec tam vilis et tam facilis, ut praeceptore non egeat. Nihil nobis videtur esse rectum, nisi quod discimus, ut post multum silentium de discipulis efficiamur magistri.

1120DTempus loquendi. Disciplina Pythagorae est per quinquennium tacere, et postea eruditos loqui.

VERS. 8.-- Tempus dilectionis. Tempus amandi post Deum uxores, liberos, propinquos, et tempus odiendi eos, in martyrio. Vel, Quoniam nunc videmus per speculum in aenigmate (I Cor. XIII), etc. Tempus erit diligendi praesentiam Dei, et tempus odiendi, quando, facie ad faciem cernentes, et in melius proficientes, incipiemus odisse et despicere quod amavimus.

Tempus belli. Modo, non agentibus poenitentiam; tempus pacis in futuro, quando omnis Israel salvus fiet; Tempus belli, ne sis securus, accipe armaturam Dei.

1121AVERS. 10.-- Vidi afflictionem quam dedit, etc. (HIER.) Dicitur quod propterea Deus, etc., usque ad usque ad finem saeculi fecerit crescere, manere et mutari.

VERS. 12.-- Et cognovi. (HIERON.) Ideo colonus in mundo datus est homo, etc., usque ad hinc:

VERS. 13.-- Omnis homo qui comedit et bibit, etc., Multae enim nunc tribulationes justorum, in futuro autem non erit labor in Jacob.

Didici, etc. (ID.) Nihil est in mundo novum, solis cursus, lunae vices, terrae et arborum siccitas, vel viror, cum mundo condita sunt; et haec idcirco Deus certa ratione temperavit, ut Dei Providentiam in his intelligamus, et timeamus a facie Dei. Vultus autem Domini super facientes mala.

1121BVERS. 16.-- Vidi sub sole. (ID.) Cum in hoc mundo judicium et veritatem requirerem, etc., usque ad, Omnia enim tempore suo requirentur.

VERS. 17.-- Dixi in corde meo. (ID.) Quasi, etc., usque ad et impleri terram pecoribus et armentis.

VERS. 21.-- Quis novit. (ID.) Non dicit, etc., usque ad et rei quasi dubiae certus assertor sit.

Deorsum. Ad inferos. Fieri enim potest ut cito mutentur, et saepe qui docti videbantur, ad inferos ducuntur; et simplices rustici coronantur.

VERS. 22.-- Et deprehendi. Superiori errore turbatus, etc., usque ad quae in mundo futura sunt postea.

CHAPTER III.

1120B

VERS. 1.-- All things have their time. (HIER.) Above he taught the uncertain and fluctuating state of the human condition, etc., as far as they are contained neither by place nor by time.

VERS. 2.-- A time to plant, etc. (ID.) It is doubtful to none, etc., as far as because perfect charity casteth out fear.

VERS. 3.-- A time to destroy, etc. We cannot build up good things, unless we have first destroyed the evil ones, as it was said to Jeremias by God, that he should first root up and dig under and destroy, and afterwards build and plant (Jerem. I).

VERS. 4.-- A time to mourn, etc. We must mourn now, that in the time to come we may dance that dance 1120Cwhich David [danced] before the ark of the testament (II Reg. VI). Displeasing to the daughter of Saul, he pleased God the more.

VERS. 5.-- A time to scatter stones, etc. (HIER.) I wonder, etc., as far as and calls to penance.

A time to embrace. A time to embrace wisdom, and to cleave more strictly to contemplation; and a time to be made far from the embrace, namely of wisdom, that we may serve the care of the time, and those things without which we cannot live.

VERS. 7.-- A time to keep silence, and a time. (HIER.) We teach in the churches what we do not know. We do not learn any of the arts without a teacher, this alone is so cheap and so easy that it does not need a master. Nothing seems to us to be right, save what we learn, so that after much silence we may be made masters out of disciples.

1120DA time to speak. The discipline of Pythagoras is to keep silence for five years, and afterwards, being learned, to speak.

VERS. 8.-- A time of love. A time of loving, after God, wives, children, kinsfolk, and a time of hating them, in martyrdom. Or, Because now we see through a glass in a dark manner (I Cor. XIII), etc. There will be a time of loving the presence of God, and a time of hating, when, beholding face to face and advancing unto better things, we shall begin to hate and to despise what we have loved.

A time of war. Now, for those not doing penance; a time of peace in the time to come, when all Israel shall be saved; A time of war, be not secure, take the armour of God.

1121AVERS. 10.-- I have seen the affliction which he gave, etc. (HIER.) It is said that God therefore, etc., as far as he made them to grow, to remain and to be changed even to the end of the age.

I have learned, etc. (ID.) Downward. To hell. For it can come to pass that they are quickly changed, and often those who seemed learned are led down to hell; and simple countrymen are crowned.

VERS. 1.-- Ne temere, Deus. Quia omnia audit et videt, et stultitiam tuam arguit quamvis ipse in coelo, et tu in terra.

VERS. 17.-- Hoc itaque mihi visum est bonum, etc. Melius dicit esse, etc., usque ad quam inspirat Dei gratia in adjuvando bona opera.

CHAPTER V.

VERS. 1.-- Be not rash, God. Because he hears and sees all things, and reproves thy folly, although he himself is in heaven, and thou on earth.

VERS. 2. Many cares. As it were, just as he who thinks upon many things frequently dreams of those very things, so he falls back into folly who disputes of God beyond his measure. Or, because we now see through a glass and in a dark manner, as it were through sleep, we reckon that we hold what we do not have, and yet we have nothing.

VERS. 3.-- When thou hast vowed a vow. (HIER.) [Vulg. Eccl. 5:3 Si quid vovisti Deo, ne moreris reddere, "if thou hast vowed any thing to God, defer not to pay it"] It is better not to promise, etc., as far as, he shall be beaten with many stripes.

1122AVERS. 4.-- And much better. As the Jews who said: All things whatsoever God hath commanded us, we will do (Exod. XXIV, XXXII): and they adored the calf. [Vulg. Exod. 24:3 Omnia verba Domini, quae locutus est, faciemus, "all the words of the Lord, which he hath spoken, we will do"]

VERS. 5.-- Give not thy mouth. As if, say not that thou sinnest out of the infirmity of thy flesh, as though turning back the fault upon its author.

Before the angel. Who stands by every man, and announces all things to God: think not that all things lie hidden from God.

VERS. 6.-- Where there are many dreams. As if, Give not thy mouth, etc., because this life is as it were dreams, and many things seem true which are false; so also is thy excuse.

VERS. 7.-- If thou shalt see the oppressions. As if he should say: Say not before the angel, There is no Providence: which is against those who think that all things are done by chance, and deny the providence of God. If nevertheless thou shalt see the oppressions of the needy, 1122Betc.

VERS. 8.-- Of all the earth, etc. God is patient and a repayer, who says, When I shall take a time, I will judge justices.

VERS. 11.-- Sweet is the sleep. (HIER.) The covetous rich man, etc., as far as for they shall see God.

VERS. 12.-- There is also another infirmity. We can [understand] these things of the philosophers, etc., as far as since it is written: I will destroy the wisdom of the wise, etc.

VERS. 17.-- This therefore hath seemed good to me, etc. He says that it is better, etc., as far as which the grace of God inspires in helping good works.

CAPUT VI.

1122CVERS. 1.-- Est et aliud malum. Describit, et aliter divitem avarum, qui cum omnia habeat bona, nihilominus nimia parcitate se cruciat, nesciens cui congreget.

VERS. 2.-- Vir cui dedit Deus divitias. (HIER.) Potest hoc de populo Israel accipi, etc., usque ad ad judicium properabimus omnes.

VERS. 3.-- Sepultura. (ID.) Adeo avarus, ut nec de sepultura cogitet, vel, saepe pro divitiis occisus abjicitur insepultus: vel, quia nullam sibi memoriam relinquat, cum omnibus bonis affluat.

VERS. 7.-- Omnis labor: hoc est, quod laborando didicit homo, id est rationalis, in ore habet, quia alios docet.

Anima ejus non implebitur. (ID.) Quia semper 1122Dcupit discere quod doceat: et hoc habet stulto plus, quia cum beato paupere tendit ad Christum, qui est vita et qui ait: Beati pauperes spiritu, quoniam ipsorum est regnum coelorum.

VERS. 10.-- Qui futurus est. (ID.) Omnium hominum, qui futuri sunt corpore circumdandi, jam Deus vocabulum novit; ne possit homo respondere contra artificem, quare ita, vel ita sit factus. Quanto enim amplius quaesierimus, tanto magis ostenditur vanitas nostra et verba superflua; et non ex praescientia Dei tollitur liberum arbitrium, sed causae ante praecedunt quare unumquodque sit factum.

VERS. 11.-- Verba sunt. Quasi, Dei quidem filius futurus est homo, tu autem noli ultra inquirere 1123Aqualis, quomodo et cujusmodi, sed crede generationem ejus.

CHAPTER VI.

1122CVERS. 1.-- There is also another evil. He describes, in another manner also, the covetous rich man, who, although he has all good things, none the less torments himself with excessive sparingness, not knowing for whom he gathers.

VERS. 2.-- A man to whom God hath given riches. (HIER.) This can be taken of the people of Israel, etc., as far as we shall all hasten to judgment.

VERS. 3.-- Burial. (ID.) So covetous that he does not even think of burial, or, often, being slain for his riches, he is cast forth unburied: or, because he leaves himself no memory, though he abounds in all good things.

VERS. 7.-- All the labour: that is, what a man, that is a rational being, has learned by labouring, he has in his mouth, because he teaches others.

His soul shall not be filled. (ID.) Because he always 1122Ddesires to learn what he may teach: and this he has more than the fool, because with the blessed poor man he tends toward Christ, who is life and who says: Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.

VERS. 10.-- He that is to be. (ID.) Of all men who are to be surrounded with a body, God already knows the name; that a man may not be able to answer back against his maker, why he was made thus, or thus. For by how much the more we shall have inquired, by so much the more is our vanity shown, and our words superfluous; and free will is not taken away by the foreknowledge of God, but causes go before, why each thing was made.

VERS. 11.-- There are words. As if, the Son of God indeed is to be man, but do thou not inquire further 1123Aof what kind, in what manner and of what sort, but believe his generation.

VERS. 2.-- Melius est nomen bonum. (HIER.) Memoria bonorum operum, quae delectet auditorum corda, sicut nares unguenta: Unde, Memoria justorum in laude, nomen impiorum putrescet.

CHAPTER VII.

VERS. 2.-- A good name is better. (HIER.) Prov. 10:7 Memoria justi cum laudibus, et nomen impiorum putrescet, "the memory of the just is with praises"]

And the day. That is, It is better by dying to escape the torments of a wretched life, and the tribulations, and the labours, than to be born unto these things and to endure them.

Than the arrogant. Contrary to this arrogant man is he who in the Gospel is read of as poor in spirit (Matth. V).

That I might find the reason. Whatever thing or discernment there is between men and their manners: who is perfectly good save Christ alone?

Who is such as the wise man, etc. (ID.) He said above that a good man is scarcely found, though men were made good by God. Now, as one giving thanks, he enumerates what good things [God] gave them, wisdom, reason, to know hidden things.

VERS. 2.-- Ego os regis observo. (HIER.) Videtur quidem praecipere juxta Apostolum, etc., usque ad hujus voluntati parendum.

CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 2.-- I observe the mouth of the king. (HIER.) He seems indeed to command according to the Apostle, etc., as far as his will is to be obeyed.

VERS. 3.-- If the tree fall to the south, or to the north, (ID.) as if he should say: The place which thou shalt have prepared for thyself here, thou shalt have in the time to come.

VERS. 4.-- He that observeth the wind soweth not. (ID.) As if he should say: Bestow thyself upon the Almighty, or bodily comfort, or the word of God (lest perchance thou pass by him who is worthy to receive); and at every time. He that observeth the wind. In the midst of the tempest one must 1125Dsow, because we know not what is the will of the spirit, and this is, How, etc.

VERS. 6.-- In the morning sow thy seed, etc. (ID.) 1126AAt every time, namely from morning unto evening, and conversely: or in boyhood and in old age. For he that perseveres shall be saved. [Vulg. Matt. 24:13 qui autem perseveraverit usque in finem, hic salvus erit, "but he that shall persevere unto the end, he shall be saved"]

VERS. 9.-- Rejoice therefore, O young man, etc. (ID.) From here unto the end of the book, etc., as far as they may know that they are to be judged.

CAPUT XII.

Hinc usque ad finem capituli legitur tribus modis, scilicet de judicio, senectute, captivitate, et serviunt quaedam glossae quandoque duabus sententiis, quandoque vero inveniuntur secundum omnes sententias.

VERS. 2.-- Antequam tenebrescant sol et lumen, et stellae et luna, etc. (HIER.) Sol convertetur in tenebras, etc., usque ad nubes et pluviae cessant ei.

1126BVERS. 4.-- Et claudent ostia in platea, ne videant lusus juvenum: omnia enim talia sunt eis importabilia.

Et consurgent. Quia non possunt dormire refrigescente sanguine, siccato humore quibus alitur sopor.

VERS. 5.-- Dissipabitur capparis. (HIER.) Capparis renibus valet, etc., usque ad in adversitate vero desistunt a bono.

VERS. 6.-- Antequam rumpatur funiculus. Redit ad superiora, ubi ait: Memento Creatoris tui, etc., simili fine sententiam concludit.

VERS. 7.-- Et revertatur. Antequam revertaris in Babyloniam unde es egressus in lumbis Abrahae, 1126Cet incipias in Mesopotamia conteri, unde profectus es.

VERS. 8.-- Vanitas vanitatum. Post descriptionem humanae fragilitatis et mortis, pulchre quod proposuerat concludit: Vanitas vanitatum. Quasi, noli luxuriari, noli delicias quaerere, cum scias te celeri fine transire et ad judicium festinare.

VERS. 11.-- Verba sapientium. (HIER.) Ne videatur temere post legem per Moysen, ipso Deo praemonente, datam, nova praecepta cudere, dicit haec verba sapientium esse.

VERS. 13.-- Finem loquendi. Quia diversorum diversas sententias, tanquam concionator, induxerat: hoc fine omnes conciliat, scilicet Deum time. Hoc solum aiunt Hebraei in auctoritate habitum, 1126Dquia hic intentionem suam ostendit, cum videatur in multis ad voluptates hortari.

CHAPTER XII.

From here unto the end of the chapter it is read in three ways, namely of judgment, of old age, of captivity, and certain glosses serve sometimes two senses, but sometimes they are found according to all the senses.

VERS. 2.-- Before the sun and the light, and the stars and the moon be darkened, etc. (HIER.) The sun shall be turned into darkness, etc., as far as the clouds and the rains cease for him.

1126BVERS. 4.-- And they shall shut the doors in the street, lest they see the sports of the young: for all such things are unbearable to them.

And they shall rise up. Because they cannot sleep, the blood growing cold, the moisture dried up by which sleep is nourished.

VERS. 5.-- The caper tree shall be destroyed. (HIER.) The caper avails for the reins, etc., as far as but in adversity they desist from good.

VERS. 6.-- Before the cord be broken. He returns to the things above, where he says: Remember thy Creator, etc., and with a like ending he concludes the sentence.

VERS. 7.-- And return. Before thou return into Babylonia whence thou didst go forth in the loins of Abraham, 1126Cand begin to be worn away in Mesopotamia, whence thou didst set out.

VERS. 8.-- Vanity of vanities. After the description of human frailty and of death, he beautifully concludes what he had proposed: Vanity of vanities. As if, be not wanton, seek not delights, since thou knowest that thou passest away by a swift end and hastenest to judgment.

VERS. 11.-- The words of the wise. (HIER.) Lest he should seem rashly to forge new precepts after the law given through Moses, God himself forewarning, he says that these are the words of the wise.

VERS. 13.-- The end of speaking. Because, as a public speaker, he had brought forward the diverse opinions of diverse men: by this ending he reconciles them all, namely Fear God. This alone, the Hebrews say, was held in authority, 1126Dbecause here he shows his own intention, although in many things he seems to exhort to pleasures.
