Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

CAPUT PRIMUM.

  • 1183C

VERS. 1.-- Omnis sapientia, etc. (RAB. in Eccli., tom. III.) Incipit ab aeterna Dei sapientia, quae Christus est, qui cum Patre est semper ante saecula, secundum illud: In principio erat Verbum, et Verbum erat apud Deum (Joan. I). A Domino Deo est. Quia Christus fons vitae, lux vera, de Patre nascitur, et omnia per ipsum facta sunt, et sine ipso factum est nihil (Ibid.). Quisquis hac sapientia caret, in tenebris ambulat, et nescit quo vadat. Sapientia enim hujus mundi stultitia est apud Deum; et prudentia carnis, mors (Sap. XI).

VERS. 2.-- Arenam maris et pluviae guttas. (RAB. ubi supra ). Corporalia spiritualibus comparat, ut ex difficillimis, quod omnino impossibile est, aestimetur. 1183DSi enim haec quae in certo numero et mensura, et pondere condita sunt, nemo potest dinumerare; quanto minus sapientiam Dei sine initio et sine fine inenarrabilem inaestimabilem poterit investigare?

VERS. 4.-- Prior omnium creata est. Creata secundum humanitatem in consilio Dei patris, scilicet pro salute humani generis praedestinata est incarnari. Notandum autem quod propter unitatem personae aliquando Christus dicitur genitus, aliquando creatus.

VERS. 5.-- Fons sapientiae. Origo omnis sapientiae a Verbo Dei procedit, quod Deus ex Deo semper cum Patre manet in coelis; sed ingressus mundum per dispensationem humanitatis dedit mandata salutis, unde, Verba vitae aeternae habes (Joan. VI).

VERS. 6.-- Radix sapientiae. Ostendit neminem 1184Cposse profunditatem Dei penetrare, vel magnituditudinem bonitatis ejus et misericordiae. Unde ad liberationem et illuminationem humani generis homo fieri dignatus est, unde: Generationem ejus quis enarrabit (Isa. LIII) ?

VERS. 9.-- Ipse creavit illam. Unigeniti scilicet adventum, per Verbum sibi coaeternum cum Spiritu sancto dispensavit: quomodo per Virginis partum ad salutem mundi veniret, et creaturas repararet, et hominem ad agnitionem et dilectionem sui revocaret, dans illi donum Spiritus sancti, ut ejus munere solvetur in aeternum.

VERS. 11.-- Timor Domini gloria, etc. Beatus vir qui timet Dominum, in mandatis ejus cupit nimis. Gloria et divitiae in domo ejus (Psal. III), etc. Timor 1184DDomini. Duae species sunt timoris. De altera dicitur, Timor [non est in charitate] animae in charitate, (Joan. IV), etc. Qui hunc habent propter metum gehennae peccare desinunt, et vitam corrigunt: hic introducit charitatem, sed et foras mittitur. Qui enim sic timet, nondum amat nec desiderat bona, sed cavet mala; sed tamen se corrigit, et bona desiderare incipit. De altera scriptum est, Timor Domini sanctus permanet in saeculum saeculi (Psal. XVIII). Timet enim unusquisque ne amittat bona, et Dei praesentiam, quae in aeternum frui desiderat, de quo dicitur:

VERS. 12.-- Timor Domini delectabit cor. Hic suavitatem tribuit internam, et in futuro vitam aeternam.

VERS. 14.-- Dilectio, (RAB.) Charitas Dei vera 1185Aest sapientia, etc., usque ad ipsa in futuro remuneratio erit; unde: Adimplebis me laetitia cum vultu tuo, delectationes in dextera tua (Psal. XV), etc.

VERS. 17.-- Timor Domini. Sine timore Dei, et observatione mandatorum ejus, nulla religio est; unde: Si quis putat se religiosum esse, non refrenans linguam suam, sed seducens cor suum, hujus vana est religio (Jac. I).

VERS. 19.-- Timenti Dominum. Qui scilicet timore Dei in fide et doctrina et operatione regulam justitiae servat.

VERS. 23.-- Et vidit. (RAB.) In conspectu ejus sunt omnia, unicuique conservat dignam retributionem.

VERS. 25.-- Rami. Per sapientiam, divinam habet 1185Bhomo scientiam vitandi malum, et intellectum faciendi bonum, utrumque enim in auditore operatur in fine; unde: Qui audit me, non confundetur, et qui operantur in me, non peccabunt. Qui elucidant me, vitam aeternam habebunt (Eccli. XXIV). Initium sapientiae, timor Domini. Intellectus bonus omnibus facientibus eum (Psal. CX).

VERS. 26.-- In thesauris. In legis meditatione utrumque declaratur, quomodo scilicet vitanda sunt mala, et quomodo discenda spiritualis sapientia, et exercenda bona opera, in quibus est religio vera. Sapientiae intellectus. Quae prohibet peccata, et carnalia desideria; unde: Non amat pestilens eum qui se corrigit, nec ad sapientes graditur (Prov. XV).

VERS. 27.-- Timor, etc. Quia per poenitentiam 1185Cmundat a praeteritis peccatis, vel prohibet a committendis. Quia qui per timorem Dei sese non corrigit, in peccati voraginem cadit.

VERS. 30.-- Bonus sensus. Unde in Proverbiis, Labia sapientium custodiunt eos (Prov. XIV). Et alibi: Astutus omnia agit cum consilio: qui autem fatuus est aperuit stultitiam (Ibid. XXXI). Sapientis ergo hominis est verba tenere, et omnia modeste peragere. Enarrabunt sensum. Aequanimiter, non mala pro malis, sed bona pro bonis tribuens.

VERS. 33.-- Fili, concupiscens sapientiam. Qualiter ad sapientiam pervenias, ipsa sapientia monstrat, quia sine conservatione justitiae, nemo pervenit ad culmen sapientiae, unde Jacobus: Quis sapiens, inquit, et disciplinatus inter vos? ostendat 1185Dconversationem bonam in mansuetudine sapientiae (Jac. III).

VERS. 34.-- Sapientia enim et disciplina. (RAB). Sicut saepe dictum est, Timor Domini sapientiae et disciplinae custodia est (Prov. XIII), cui convenit fides recta, et morum temperantia, quia sine fide impossibile est placere Deo, et bonorum vita semper tranquilla est; unde in Matthaeo: Discite a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde, et invenietis requiem animabus vestris (Matth. XI). Qui enim vere timet Deum, non potest esse sine fide, et operibus bonis, quae replent hominem virtutibus, et Deo acceptabilem faciunt. Unde: Homini bono in conspectu suo dedit Deus sapientiam et scientiam et laetitiam. Peccatori 1186Aautem dedit afflictionem et curam superfluam (Eccle. II).

VERS. 36.-- Duplici corde. Vir duplex animo, inconstans est in omnibus viis suis, qui scilicet vult gaudere cum saeculo et regnare cum Christo.

VERS. 37.-- Hypocrita.. Aliud corde gestans, et aliud opere monstrans. Unde: Attendite a falsis prophetis, qui veniunt ad vos in vestimentis ovium (Matth. VII).

VERS. 38.-- Attende in illis, etc. Manifestum est, quod qui sequitur haereticos, animae suae acquirit damnationem, quia in judicio revelabuntur occulta cordium, et reddetur unicuique secundum opera sua, Disperdet enim Deus omnes qui loquuntur mendacium (Psal. LXI). Qui abominatur virum sanguinum 1186Bet dolosum; unde: Attendite a fermento Pharisaeorum, quod est hypocrisis. Et alibi: Veniet Dominus servi illius, et dividet eum; partemque ejus ponet cum hypocritis, ibi erit fletus et stridor dentium (Matth. XXIV).

CHAPTER ONE.

  • 1183C

VERS. 1.-- All wisdom, etc. (RAB. in Eccli., tom. III.) He begins from the eternal wisdom of God, which is Christ, who is always with the Father before the ages, according to that word: In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God (Joan. I). Is from the Lord God. Because Christ, the fountain of life, the true light, is born of the Father, and all things were made by him, and without him was made nothing (Ibid.). Whoever lacks this wisdom walks in darkness, and knows not where he goes. For the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God; and the prudence of the flesh is death (Sap. XI).

VERS. 2.-- The sand of the sea, and the drops of rain. (RAB. ubi supra ). He compares bodily things with spiritual, so that from the most difficult a thing altogether impossible may be estimated. 1183DFor if these things, which were established in a fixed number and measure and weight, no one can count, how much less shall he be able to search out the wisdom of God, which is without beginning and without end, unutterable, inestimable?

VERS. 4.-- She was created before all things. Created according to his humanity in the counsel of God the Father, that is, he was predestined to be incarnate for the salvation of the human race. And it is to be noted that, on account of the unity of the person, Christ is sometimes said to be begotten, sometimes created.

VERS. 5.-- The fountain of wisdom. The origin of all wisdom proceeds from the Word of God, which, God from God, abides always with the Father in the heavens; but having entered the world through the dispensation of his humanity, it gave the commandments of salvation, whence: Thou hast the words of eternal life (Joan. VI).

VERS. 6.-- The root of wisdom. He shows that no one is able 1184Cto penetrate the depth of God, or the magnituditudinem of his goodness and mercy. Wherefore, for the liberation and enlightenment of the human race, he deigned to become man; whence: Who shall declare his generation (Isa. LIII) ?

VERS. 9.-- He himself created her. That is, he dispensed the coming of the Only-begotten through the Word coeternal with himself together with the Holy Spirit: how through the Virgin's childbearing he should come for the salvation of the world, and should repair his creatures, and should call man back to the knowledge and love of himself, giving him the gift of the Holy Spirit, that by his gift he should be loosed for ever.

VERS. 11.-- The fear of the Lord is glory, etc. Blessed is the man that feareth the Lord: he desireth exceedingly in his commandments. Glory and riches are in his house (Psal. III), etc. [Vulg. volet nimis, "he shall delight exceedingly," for cupit nimis] The fear 1184Dof the Lord. There are two kinds of fear. Of the one it is said, Fear [is not in charity] of the soul in charity, (Joan. IV), etc. They who have this fear cease from sinning for dread of hell, and amend their life: this fear brings charity in, but it is also cast out. For he who fears in this way does not yet love nor desire good things, but guards against evil ones; yet nevertheless he corrects himself, and begins to desire good things. Of the other it is written, The fear of the Lord is holy, it endureth for ever and ever (Psal. XVIII). [Vulg. permanens, "enduring"] For every man fears lest he lose the good things, and the presence of God, which he desires to enjoy for ever; of which it is said:

VERS. 12.-- The fear of the Lord shall delight the heart. This bestows inward sweetness, and in the time to come eternal life.

VERS. 14.-- Love, (RAB.) The true love of God 1185Ais wisdom, etc., as far as it shall itself be the reward in the time to come; whence: Thou shalt fill me with joy with thy countenance, delights at thy right hand (Psal. XV), etc.

VERS. 17.-- The fear of the Lord. Without the fear of God, and the keeping of his commandments, there is no religion; whence: If any man think himself to be religious, not bridling his tongue, but deceiving his own heart, this man's religion is vain (Jac. I).

VERS. 19.-- To him that feareth the Lord. That is, to him who by the fear of God keeps the rule of justice in faith and in teaching and in work.

VERS. 23.-- And he saw. (RAB.) All things are in his sight; for each one he keeps a worthy recompense.

VERS. 25.-- The branches. Through wisdom man has 1185Bthe divine knowledge of avoiding evil, and the understanding of doing good; for it works both in the hearer unto the end; whence: He that hearkeneth to me shall not be confounded, and they that work by me shall not sin. They that explain me shall have life everlasting (Eccli. XXIV). The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord. A good understanding to all that do it (Psal. CX).

VERS. 26.-- In the treasures. In the meditation of the law both are declared: namely, how evils are to be avoided, and how spiritual wisdom is to be learned and good works to be practised, in which things is true religion. The understanding of wisdom. Which forbids sins and carnal desires; whence: A pestilent man loveth not him that correcteth himself, nor doth he go to the wise (Prov. XV). [Vulg. corripit, "him that rebuketh him"]

VERS. 27.-- Fear, etc. Because through penance 1185Cit cleanses from past sins, or forbids those that are to be committed. For he who does not correct himself through the fear of God falls into the abyss of sin.

VERS. 30.-- Good sense. Whence in the Proverbs, The lips of the wise preserve them (Prov. XIV). And elsewhere: The crafty man doeth all things with counsel: but he that is a fool hath laid open his folly (Ibid. XXXI). [Vulg. aperiet, "shall lay open"] It belongs, therefore, to a wise man to hold his words, and to carry out all things with moderation. They shall declare his sense. With an even mind, rendering not evil for evil, but good for good.

VERS. 33.-- Son, coveting wisdom. How thou mayest come to wisdom, wisdom herself shows, because without the keeping of justice no one comes to the summit of wisdom; whence James: Who is a wise man, saith he, and endued with knowledge among you? let him show 1185Dgood conversation in the meekness of wisdom (Jac. III). [Vulg. ostendat ex bona conversatione operationem suam, "let him show, by a good conversation, his work"]

VERS. 34.-- For wisdom and discipline. (RAB). As has often been said, The fear of the Lord is the keeping of wisdom and discipline (Prov. XIII), to which belong right faith and temperance of manners, because without faith it is impossible to please God, and the life of good men is always tranquil; whence in Matthew: Learn of me, because I am meek and humble of heart, and you shall find rest for your souls (Matth. XI). For he who truly fears God cannot be without faith and good works, which fill a man with virtues and make him acceptable to God. Whence: To the man that is good in his sight God hath given wisdom and knowledge and joy. But to the sinner 1186Ahe hath given affliction and superfluous care (Eccle. II).

VERS. 36.-- With a double heart. A man double of mind is inconstant in all his ways: he, namely, who wishes to rejoice with the world and to reign with Christ.

VERS. 37.-- A hypocrite.. Bearing one thing in his heart, and showing another in his work. Whence: Beware of false prophets, who come to you in the clothing of sheep (Matth. VII).

VERS. 38.-- Attend unto them, etc. It is manifest that he who follows heretics purchases damnation for his own soul, because in the judgment the hidden things of hearts shall be revealed, and it shall be rendered to every man according to his works; for God will destroy all that speak a lie (Psal. LXI). Who abhorreth the bloody 1186Band deceitful man; whence: Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy. And elsewhere: The lord of that servant shall come, and shall divide him, and shall appoint his portion with the hypocrites: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth (Matth. XXIV).

CAPUT II.

VERS. 1.-- Fili, accedens, etc. Demonstravit quid sit vitandum, nunc demonstrat quid sit agendum, dicens: Fili, accedens ad servitutem Dei sta in justitia et timore. Qui enim vult ab illecebris mundi se abstinere, et in Christo pie vivere, persecutionem patitur. Dum enim accingi divino servitio praeparat, bellum diaboli contra se excitat, et dum alia vitia subdit, aliis reluctatur, et divina dispensatione agitur, ut superet et superetur, ne extollatur. Sed 1186Cquia humilitas et patientia illi necessaria est, sequitur, Deprime cor tuum, et sustine, etc.

VERS. 4.-- Patientiam habe. Quasi: In patientia vestra possidebitis animas vestras (Luc. XXI), quia scilicet radix et custos omnium virtutum patientia est. Sciendum autem est, quod patientia tribus modis exercetur. A Deo enim flagellamur, sed cavendum est ne murmuremus; a proximo persecutiones, et damna, et contumelias sustinemus, et cavendum est ne mala pro malis retribuamus; ab adversario tentamur, sed prospiciendum est ne ad delectationem peccati vel consensum flectamur, vel pro his praesentia quaeramus, sed futura exspectemus.

VERS. 6.-- Crede Deo et recuperabit te. (RAB.) 1186DCredere oportet afflictum, et dirigere gressus operum in Dei conspectum, et sperare in illum; unde: Revela Domino viam tuam, et spera in eo, et ipse faciet. (Psal. XXXVI). Hinc Jeremias ait: Beatus qui confidit in Domino, et erit Deus fiducia ejus (Jer. XVII), etc.

VERS. 7.-- Metuentes Dominum, etc. Beneplacitum est Domino super timentes eum, et in eis qui sperant super misericordia ejus (Psal. CXLVI). Qui enim veraciter metuit, patienter sustinet miserationem ejus: Qui autem dereliquerint Deum, confundentur et in terra scribentur (Jer. XVII).

VERS. 8.-- Qui timetis Dominum, credite illi. Hortatur timentes Dominum ut credant illi, qui ad certam mercedem perveniant; unde: Justus enim ex 1187Afide vivit (Rom. I); unde: Confide, fili, remittuntur tibi peccata tua (Matth. IX). Et alibi: Fides tua te salvum fecit (Ibid., X).

VERS. 9.-- Qui timetis Dominum, sperate in illum. Tria commendat timentibus Deum: credere, sperare, diligere; quia fide, spe et charitate vere colitur Deus, et ad contemplationem pervenitur, quae est beatitudo aeterna.

VERS. 11.-- Respicite, etc. (RAB.) Hic quoque tria dicit, etc., usque ad quia sacrificium Deo spiritus contribulatus (Psal. L), etc. Et, Prope est Deus omnibus invocantibus se in veritate (Psal. CXLIV).

VERS. 14.-- Vae duplici corde, etc. Admonuit, ne quis duplici corde accederet ad Deum; nunc autem dicit, Vae duplici corde et labiis scelestis. Qui enim 1187Bprava cogitat, et loquitur, et pravis insistit operibus, nisi poenituerit, in aeternum peribit. Duabus viis terram ingredientis qui facit mala, et sperat bona; vel, qui carnis sectatur opera, et spiritus sanctificationem se arbitratur perficere in timore; vel, quod Dei est opere exhibet, et quod mundi est, cogitatione quaerit. Tales vero inconstantes sunt in omnibus viis suis, quia facile in adversis terrentur, et prosperis irretiuntur.

VERS. 15.-- Dissolutis corde, etc. (RAB.) Adhuc docet errorem cavere, etc., usque ad his, qui non acquiescunt veritati, credunt autem iniquitati, ira et indignatio, etc.

VERS. 17.-- Inspicere, etc. Licet omni tempore inspiciat omnia Deus, tunc tamen inspicere dicitur, 1187Ccum probare vel improbare aliquid dignoscitur.

VERS. 18.-- Qui timent Dominum, non erunt incredibiles, etc. Alternando de amore et dilectione Dei agit, quia timor Domini sanctus, permanens in saeculum saeculi (Psal. XVIII), et omnis qui diligit, ex Deo natus est, et cognoscit Deum (I Joan. IV). Qui vero timet Deum, et diligit, et credit Scripturis, servat operando praecepta Dei, et semper meditando quaerit quae beneplacita sunt illi, replebitur ergo sapientia et charitate Dei. Finis enim praecepti charitas, et qui diligit proximum, legem implevit.

VERS. 20.-- Qui timent Dominum praeparabunt, etc. Felix anima, quae quotidie mundat cor suum ut suscipiat habitatorem Deum, cujus possessor nullo eget bono, quia omnium bonorum auctorem in se 1187Dhabet. Et quia ad hoc necessaria est mandatorum custodia, et boni operis patientia, qua pervenitur ad agnitionem veritatis, sequitur: Qui timent Dominum custodiunt mandata illius, etc. Justus in primis accusator est sui. Sicut enim humilitati praemium, sic superbiae restat supplicium; unde Joannes: Si dixerimus quoniam peccatum non habemus, ipsi nos seducimus (II Joan. I). Qui vere sapiens est, verus justitiae cultor est, quia sapientia dictat justitiae normam.

CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1.-- Son, coming, etc. He has shown what is to be avoided; now he shows what is to be done, saying: Son, when thou comest to the service of God, stand in justice and in fear. For he who wishes to withdraw himself from the enticements of the world and to live godly in Christ suffers persecution. For while he makes ready to be girded for the divine service, he stirs up the war of the devil against himself; and while he subdues some vices, he struggles against others, and by the divine dispensation it is so ordered that he both overcomes and is overcome, lest he be lifted up. But 1186Cbecause humility and patience are necessary to him, there follows, Humble thy heart, and endure, etc.

VERS. 4.-- Have patience. As if to say: In your patience you shall possess your souls (Luc. XXI), because, namely, patience is the root and the keeper of all the virtues. And it is to be known that patience is exercised in three ways. For by God we are scourged, but care must be taken that we do not murmur; from our neighbour we bear persecutions and losses and insults, and care must be taken that we do not render evil for evil; by the adversary we are tempted, but we must look ahead lest we be bent to the delight of sin or to consent to it, or seek present things in return for these, but rather await things to come.

VERS. 6.-- Believe God, and he will recover thee. (RAB.) 1186DThe afflicted man must believe, and direct the steps of his works in the sight of God, and hope in him; whence: Reveal thy way to the Lord, and hope in him, and he will do it. (Psal. XXXVI). Hence Jeremiah says: Blessed is he that trusteth in the Lord, and God shall be his confidence (Jer. XVII), etc. [Vulg. Benedictus vir qui confidit in Domino, et erit Dominus fiducia ejus]

VERS. 7.-- Ye that fear the Lord, etc. The Lord is well pleased with them that fear him, and in them that hope upon his mercy (Psal. CXLVI). For he who truly fears patiently awaits his mercy: But they that forsake God shall be confounded, and shall be written in the earth (Jer. XVII). [Vulg. omnes qui te derelinquunt confundentur; recedentes a te in terra scribentur]

VERS. 8.-- Ye that fear the Lord, believe him. He exhorts them that fear the Lord to believe him, that they may come to a certain reward; whence: For the just man liveth 1187Aby faith (Rom. I); whence: Be of good heart, son, thy sins are forgiven thee (Matth. IX). And elsewhere: Thy faith hath made thee whole (Ibid., X).

VERS. 9.-- Ye that fear the Lord, hope in him. He commends three things to them that fear God: to believe, to hope, to love; because by faith, hope and charity God is truly worshipped, and one comes to contemplation, which is eternal blessedness.

VERS. 11.-- Look ye, etc. (RAB.) Here too he says three things, etc., as far as because a sacrifice to God is an afflicted spirit (Psal. L), etc. And, God is nigh unto all that call upon him in truth (Psal. CXLIV). [Vulg. Prope est Dominus omnibus invocantibus eum … in veritate]

VERS. 14.-- Woe to them that are of a double heart, etc. He warned that no one should come to God with a double heart; but now he says, Woe to them that are of a double heart and of wicked lips. For he who 1187Bthinks and speaks crooked things, and persists in crooked works, unless he repent, shall perish for ever. Going into the land by two ways is he who does evil and hopes for good; or, he who follows the works of the flesh and judges that he is bringing to perfection the sanctification of the spirit in fear; or, he who displays in work what is God's, and seeks in thought what is the world's. Such men indeed are inconstant in all their ways, because they are easily terrified in adversity and entangled in prosperity.

VERS. 15.-- To them that are of a dissolute heart, etc. (RAB.) He still teaches that error is to be avoided, etc., as far as to these, who do not acquiesce in the truth but believe iniquity, wrath and indignation, etc.

VERS. 17.-- To look upon, etc. Although God looks upon all things at every time, yet he is then said to look upon 1187Cwhen he is discerned to approve or to disapprove something.

VERS. 18.-- They that fear the Lord will not be incredulous, etc. He treats alternately of the love and the affection of God, because the fear of the Lord is holy, enduring for ever and ever (Psal. XVIII), and every one that loveth is born of God, and knoweth God (I Joan. IV). But he who fears God, and loves, and believes the Scriptures, keeps the precepts of God by working them, and always by meditating seeks the things that are well pleasing to him: he shall therefore be filled with the wisdom and the charity of God. For the end of the commandment is charity, and he that loveth his neighbour hath fulfilled the law.

VERS. 20.-- They that fear the Lord will prepare, etc. Happy the soul which daily cleanses its heart that it may receive God as its indweller; the possessor of him lacks no good thing, because he has within himself the author of all good things. 1187DAnd because for this the keeping of the commandments is necessary, and the patience of good work, by which one comes to the knowledge of the truth, there follows: They that fear the Lord keep his commandments, etc. The just man is first of all his own accuser. For as a reward remains for humility, so a punishment remains for pride; whence John: If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves (II Joan. I). He who is truly wise is a true keeper of justice, because wisdom dictates the rule of justice.

CAPUT III.

VERS. 2.-- Judicium patris. Dei, ex quo omnis paternitas in coelo et in terra. Quia ex ipsa sunt omnia. Qui diligit ea, quae fecit, et ut in eo maneant, 1188Aquo facta sunt, cujus judicium, qui audierit, et praecepta ejus fecerit, salvus erit: cujus mandatum est, ut etiam patri temporali, et matri reverentia exhibeatur; unde: Honora patrem tuum (Deut. V), etc.; unde sequitur: Qui honorat patrem suum. Cui Deum honorem constituit, non debet a filiis inhonorari: quia Deus judicio suo exquiret qualiter praeceptum suum servetur.

VERS. 4.-- Qui diligit. Qui scilicet mandata servat, et abstinet a peccatis, et tamen pro excessoribus orare non desinit, quia scit misericordem judicem omni tempore preces exaudire.

VERS. 6.-- In filiis. (RAB.) In sobole vel in discipulis, etc., usque ad honorare parentes primum mandatum est in promissione.

1188BVERS. 9.-- In opere et sermone, non in vulva scilicet unde ostenditur, et spiritualis pater maxime dignus honore; unde Paulus: Per Evangelium ego vos genui (I Cor. IV). Et alibi: Qui bene praesunt presbyteri, duplici honore digni habeantur, maxime qui laborant in doctrina et verbo (I Tim. V).

VERS. 11.-- Benedictio patris, etc. (RAB.) Benedixerunt patriarchae filiis, etc., usque ad illusores saevissime laniaverunt.

VERS. 15.-- Eleemosyna. Scilicet quae peccatum delet. Hostia enim quae pro peccato, peccatum vocatur. Qui autem parentibus, vel caeteris pauperibus miseretur, justitiae praemium recipiet a peccatis absolutus. Beati misericordes, quoniam (Matth. V), etc. Et alibi: Feneratur Domino, qui miseretur pauperis. 1188C(Prov. XIX). Hinc Daniel ad Nabuchodonosor: Consilium meum, o rex, placeat tibi: peccata tua eleemosynis redime, et iniquitates tuas misericordiis pauperum (Dan. IV), si forsitan ignoscat Deus delictis tuis. Hinc Dominus ait: Date eleemosynam, et omnia munda sunt vobis (Luc. XI).

VERS. 19.-- Fili in mansuetudine. Mansuetudo et humilitas gratiam, superbia et furor pariunt ruinam; unde: Discite a me, quia mitis sum et humilis corde (Matth. XI), etc. Et alibi: Qui se exaltat, humiliabitur (Luc. XIV), etc.

VERS. 21.-- Quanto magnus es, etc. (RAB.) Docuit superius mansuetudinem, etc., usque ad sufficit homini ea pie cogitare, et opere implere, quae praecepit Deus.

1188DVERS. 24.-- In supervacuis rebus, etc. (RAB.) Id est, ne scruteris supervacue, etc., usque ad disputantes in errores incidunt, et a veritate recedunt.

VERS. 27.-- Cor durum. Quia secundum duritiam tuam thesaurizas tibi iram in die judicii (Rom. II).

VERS. 28.-- Cor ingrediens. Quod non curat, nec studet vitare, et quod ea diligit, quae ducunt ad interitum, ut haereses et profanae novitates; unde sequitur: Cor ingrediens duas vias, non habebit successus, etc.

CHAPTER III.

VERS. 2.-- The judgment of the father. Of God, of whom all fatherhood in heaven and on earth. Because from him are all things. He loves the things which he has made, and loves that they should abide in him in whom they were made; and whoever hears his judgment, and does his precepts, 1188Ashall be saved: whose commandment it is that reverence be shown also to a temporal father and mother; whence: Honour thy father (Deut. V), etc.; whence there follows: He that honoureth his father. He for whom he appointed God as his honour ought not to be dishonoured by his sons: because God will by his judgment search out how his precept is kept.

VERS. 4.-- He that loveth. He, namely, who keeps the commandments and abstains from sins, and yet does not cease to pray for transgressors, because he knows that a merciful judge hears prayers at every time.

VERS. 6.-- In the children. (RAB.) In offspring or in disciples, etc., as far as to honour parents is the first commandment with a promise.

1188BVERS. 9.-- In work and word, not in the womb, namely, whence it is shown that the spiritual father also is most worthy of honour; whence Paul: Through the Gospel I have begotten you (I Cor. IV). And elsewhere: Let the priests that rule well be esteemed worthy of double honour, especially they who labour in doctrine and word (I Tim. V). [Vulg. in verbo et doctrina, in the reverse order]

VERS. 11.-- The blessing of the father, etc. (RAB.) The patriarchs blessed their sons, etc., as far as they most savagely tore the mockers.

VERS. 15.-- Alms. Namely, that which blots out sin. For the victim which is offered for sin is called sin. And he who has mercy on his parents, or on the rest of the poor, shall receive the reward of justice, absolved from his sins. Blessed are the merciful, for (Matth. V), etc. And elsewhere: He lendeth to the Lord that hath mercy on the poor. 1188C(Prov. XIX). Hence Daniel to Nabuchodonosor: Let my counsel, O king, be pleasing to thee: redeem thou thy sins with alms, and thy iniquities with works of mercy to the poor (Dan. IV), if perhaps God may forgive thy offences. Hence the Lord says: Give alms, and all things are clean unto you (Luc. XI).

VERS. 19.-- Son, in meekness. Meekness and humility bring forth grace; pride and rage bring forth ruin; whence: Learn of me, because I am meek and humble of heart (Matth. XI), etc. And elsewhere: He that exalteth himself shall be humbled (Luc. XIV), etc.

VERS. 21.-- The greater thou art, etc. (RAB.) Above he taught meekness, etc., as far as it is enough for a man to think devoutly upon, and to fulfil in work, those things which God has commanded.

1188DVERS. 24.-- In superfluous things, etc. (RAB.) That is, that thou search not superfluously, etc., as far as disputing they fall into errors, and depart from the truth.

VERS. 27.-- A hard heart. Because according to thy hardness thou treasurest up to thyself wrath in the day of judgment (Rom. II). [Vulg. in die irae, "in the day of wrath"]

VERS. 28.-- A heart going in. Which takes no care, nor is at pains to avoid, and because it loves those things which lead to destruction, such as heresies and profane novelties; whence there follows: A heart that goeth two ways shall not have success, etc.

CAPUT IV.

VERS. 1.-- Fili, eleemosynam pauperis, etc. (RAB.) Quantum eleemosyna valet, etc., usque ad qui ex corde corripit verbis, vel factis.

1189AVERS. 3.-- Cor inopis, etc. Non est eleemosyna differenda, cum adsit tribuendi facultas; unde: Ne dicas amico tuo: Vade et revertere, cum cito possis dare (Prov. III). Et alibi: Qui despicit proximum, peccat: qui miseretur pauperis, beatus erit (Ibid. XIV). Eleemosyna autem facienda est, non ex necessitate, Hilarem enim datorem diligit Deus (I Cor. IX). Et alibi: Vidua paupercula plus omnibus misit in gazophylacium, quia non quantitatem muneris pensat Deus, sed devotionem cordis.

VERS. 5.-- Ab inope ne avertas, etc. Non per iram exasperandus est pauper, sed blanda locutione placandus, Ira enim viri justitiam Dei non operatur (Jac. I). Et, Qui obturat aurem ad clamorem pauperis, clamabit, et non exaudiet Dominus (Prov. XXI). 1189BQui enim potest tribuere, debet: quia munus absconditum exstinguit iras, et donum in sinu indignationem maximam. Qui autem non potest, sermo bonus est ei super datum optimum. Qui autem habuerit substantiam mundi: et viderit fratrem suum necesse habentem, et clauserit viscera sua ab eo, quomodo charitas Dei manet in eo? (I Joan. III.) Fides enim si non habeat opera, mortua est. Non ergo diligamus lingua et verbo, sed opere et veritate.

VERS. 7.-- Congregationi pauperum. In his commendatur mansuetudo et humilitas, quia mansuetudo neminem spernit, humilitas se omnibus subjicit. Decet autem Christianum aequalibus et inferioribus affabilem esse et mitem, senioribus humilem; unde: Domine, non est exaltatum cor meum, neque 1189Celati sunt oculi mei (Psal. CXXX).

VERS. 9.-- Libera, etc. (RAB.) Sicut largitionem in pauperes eleemosynam esse dixit, sic liberationem inique oppressi misericordiam esse ostendit; unde, Erue illos qui ducuntur ad mortem.

VERS. 10.-- In judicando. (RAB.) Nullam eleemosynae speciem, etc., usque ad ita et viduae viri solatium.

VERS. 13.-- Et qui vigilaverint, etc. Beatus homo qui audit me, et vigilat ante fores meas quotidie, et observat ad postes ostii mei (Prov. VIII). Per fores autem potes Scripturas et eorum doctores intelligit.

VERS. 15.-- Qui serviunt, etc. Qui diligit eum, qui 1189Dgenuit, diliget eum qui natus est ex Deo, et quem diligit Pater, diligit Filius, unde: Qui diligit me, diligetur a Patre meo, et eum diligam. Sed quia probatio dilectionis exhibitio est operis, sequitur: Qui audit illam, judicabit gentes. Unde: Cum sederit Filius hominis in sede majestatis suae, sedebitis et vos super sedes duodecim judicantes duodecim tribus Israel (Matth. XIX).

VERS. 19.-- Timorem et metum, etc. Sapientia inhabitans dirigit opera, in variis tentationibus praeparat exercendo et probando ad futurae vitae gaudia, quod hac sententia confirmatur: Timorem et metum et probationem, etc. Quia tribulatio patientiam operatur, et patientia probationem, probatio vero spem, 1190Aquod non confundit, quia charitas Dei diffusa est in sanctis per Spiritum sanctum.

VERS. 23.-- Fili, conserva tempus, etc. Supra commendavit sapientiae donum, nunc ostendit ipsius factum. Quicunque enim servat praecepta sapientiae, cautus est in temporum mutatione, nec deserit viam justitiae in tempore persecutionis et angustiae. Ideo sapientia exhortatur filios, ut conservent tempus, quia tempus omni rei sub coelo: et tempus est belli, et tempus pacis.

VERS. 28.-- Non abscondas sapientiam tuam, etc. (RAB.) Non est abscondenda sapientia his, qui audire volunt. Qui enim abscondit frumentum, maledicetur in populis, benedictio autem super caput vendentium; unde: Negotia, dum venio (Luc. XIX).

1190BVERS. 30.-- Non contradicas. Obediendum est majoribus, si veritati concordant et justitiae, nec aliter, sed pro recta fide, et vera religione, in quibus est animae salus, decertandum est; unde: Si oculus tuus scandalizat te, erue eum et projice abs te (Matth. V). Et, Si quis vult venire post me, abneget (Luc. IX), etc.

VERS. 34.-- Noli citatus esse in lingua tua. (RAB.) Mors et vita, etc., usque ad scriptum est: Non in sermone regnum Dei, sed in virtute (I Cor. IV).

VERS. 35.-- Noli esse sicut leo. In Ecclesia sunt haeretici quasi leones, qui per linguae volubilitatem seducunt corda innocentium, et per insolentiam morum conturbant mansuetudinem simplicium.

CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 1.-- Son, the alms of the poor, etc. (RAB.) How much alms avails, etc., as far as he who from the heart rebukes in words or in deeds.

1189AVERS. 3.-- The heart of the needy, etc. Alms is not to be put off, when the means of giving is at hand; whence: Say not to thy friend: Go, and come again, when thou canst give at once (Prov. III). [Vulg. cras dabo tibi: cum statim possis dare] And elsewhere: He that despiseth his neighbour sinneth: he that hath mercy on the poor shall be blessed (Ibid. XIV). And alms is to be done not out of necessity, for God loveth a cheerful giver (I Cor. IX). And elsewhere: The poor widow cast more than all into the treasury, because God does not weigh the quantity of the gift, but the devotion of the heart.

VERS. 5.-- Turn not away from the needy, etc. The poor man is not to be exasperated by anger, but to be appeased by gentle speech, for the anger of man worketh not the justice of God (Jac. I). And, He that stoppeth his ear at the cry of the poor shall cry, and the Lord shall not hear him (Prov. XXI). [Vulg. aurem suam … et ipse clamabit, et non exaudietur] 1189BFor he who is able to give ought to give: because a hidden gift quenches anger, and a present in the bosom quenches the greatest indignation. But he who is not able, a good word is better for him than the best gift. But he that hath the substance of the world, and shall see his brother in need, and shall shut up his bowels from him, how doth the charity of God abide in him? (I Joan. III.) [Vulg. substantiam hujus mundi … necessitatem habere] For faith, if it have not works, is dead. Let us not therefore love in tongue and in word, but in deed and in truth.

VERS. 7.-- To the congregation of the poor. In these things meekness and humility are commended, because meekness despises no one, humility subjects itself to all. And it becomes a Christian to be affable and gentle toward his equals and inferiors, and humble toward his elders; whence: Lord, my heart is not exalted, neither 1189Care my eyes lofty (Psal. CXXX).

VERS. 9.-- Deliver, etc. (RAB.) As he said that bounty toward the poor is alms, so he shows that the deliverance of one unjustly oppressed is mercy; whence, Deliver them that are led to death.

VERS. 10.-- In judging. (RAB.) No kind of alms, etc., as far as so also the solace of a husband to a widow.

VERS. 13.-- And they that watch, etc. Blessed is the man that heareth me, and that watcheth before my doors daily, and waiteth at the posts of my door (Prov. VIII). And by the doors thou canst, he understands the Scriptures and their teachers.

VERS. 15.-- They that serve, etc. He who loves him that 1189Dbegot will love him that is born of God, and whom the Father loves, the Son loves; whence: He that loveth me shall be loved by my Father, and I will love him. But because the proof of love is the showing of the work, there follows: He that heareth her shall judge the nations. Whence: When the Son of man shall sit on the seat of his majesty, you also shall sit on twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel (Matth. XIX).

VERS. 19.-- Fear and dread, etc. Wisdom dwelling within directs works; in various temptations it prepares a man, by exercising and proving him, for the joys of the life to come; which is confirmed by this sentence: Fear and dread and proving, etc. Because tribulation works patience, and patience trial, and trial hope, 1190Awhich confoundeth not, because the charity of God is poured forth in the saints through the Holy Spirit.

VERS. 23.-- Son, keep the time, etc. Above he commended the gift of wisdom; now he shows the deed of wisdom herself. For whoever keeps the precepts of wisdom is wary in the change of times, and does not forsake the way of justice in the time of persecution and of distress. Therefore wisdom exhorts her sons to keep the time, because there is a time for everything under heaven: and there is a time of war, and a time of peace.

VERS. 28.-- Hide not thy wisdom, etc. (RAB.) Wisdom is not to be hidden from those who are willing to hear. For he that hideth up corn shall be cursed among the people, but a blessing is upon the head of them that sell; whence: Do business, till I come (Luc. XIX). [Vulg. Negotiamini, "trade ye"]

1190BVERS. 30.-- Contradict not. Obedience is due to superiors, if they agree with truth and justice, and not otherwise; but for right faith and true religion, in which is the soul's salvation, one must contend; whence: If thy eye scandalize thee, pluck it out and cast it from thee (Matth. V). And, If any man will come after me, let him deny (Luc. IX), etc.

VERS. 34.-- Be not hasty in thy tongue. (RAB.) Death and life, etc., as far as it is written: The kingdom of God is not in speech, but in power (I Cor. IV).

VERS. 35.-- Be not as a lion. In the Church there are heretics like lions, who by the glibness of the tongue lead astray the hearts of the innocent, and by the insolence of their manners trouble the meekness of the simple.

CAPUT V.

1190CVERS. 11.-- Non ventiles te, etc. (RAB.) Levitatem reprehendit, etc., usque ad duplicitas doctrinae eorum, in qua modo vera, modo falsa proferunt, peccatores esse ostendit.

VERS. 13.-- Esto mansuetus. Bonum est mansuete audire, et suscipere verbum veritatis, et modeste ac prudenter proferre; unde: Sit omnis homo velox ad audiendum, tardus ad loquendum et tardus ad iram. (Jac. I). Et paulo post: In mansuetudine suscipite insitum verbum, quod potest salvare animas vestras (Ibid.) In Proverbiis quoque dicitur: Vidisti hominem velocem ad loquendeum? stultitia magis speranda est, quam correctio illius (Prov. XXIX).

CHAPTER V.

1190CVERS. 11.-- Winnow not thyself, etc. (RAB.) He reproves levity, etc., as far as he shows that the doubleness of their teaching, in which they now put forward true things and now false, proves them to be sinners.

VERS. 13.-- Be meek. It is good to hear meekly, and to receive the word of truth, and to utter it modestly and prudently; whence: Let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak and slow to anger. (Jac. I). And a little after: With meekness receive the ingrafted word, which is able to save your souls (Ibid.) In the Proverbs also it is said: Hast thou seen a man swift to speak loquendeum? folly is rather to be looked for than his correction (Prov. XXIX). [Vulg. illius correptio, "his reproof"]

CAPUT VI.

1190DVERS. 1.-- Nolite fieri, etc. (RAB.) Vim verae charitatis ostendit, etc., usque ad allidetur virtus ipsius.

VERS. 4.-- Nequam. Bonae voluntatis succedit boni operis fructus, et malitiosae menti nihil prosperitatis. Si enim desit charitas, frustra operam virtuti impendis; unde: Qui in uno offendit, omnium reus est (Jac. II). Et, ei qui non habet, et hoc quod habet, auferetur ab eo (Luc. XIX).

VERS. 6.-- Multi pacifici, etc. Cum omnibus pax est habenda, quantam permittit nostra fragilitas, sed non cum multis consilium, quia scriptum est: Misericordes vocantur, virum autem fidelem quis inveniet? (Prov. XX.)

1191AVERS. 7.-- Si possides amicum, etc. (RAB.) Per diversas species falsos amicos exprimens, tandem ad verum pervenit amicum: quia multi amicos se esse profitentur in prosperis, et inimici apparent in adversis. Unde: Si humiliaverit se contra te, etc.

VERS. 13.-- Attende. (RAB.) Id est, discretionem habe, etc., usque ad: Rogo autem vos, fratres, ut observetis eos qui dissensiones et offendicula faciunt (Rom. XVI).

VERS. 14.-- Amicus fortis. Amicus amoris consors, cui sic animum tuum applica ut ex duobus unum fiat, cui te tanquam tibi committas, a quo nihil timeas: et nihil inhonestum petas. Non est enim amicitia vectigalis, sed plena decoris; unde: Vos amici mei estis, si feceritis quae ego praecipio 1191Bvobis (Joan. XV), etc. Dedit nobis Dominus formam amicitiae quam sequamur, ut faciamus amici voluntatem, aperiamus ei secreta, et ipse nobis; unde: Vos autem dixi amicos, quia omnia quae audivi a Patre meo, nota feci vobis (Ibid.).

VERS. 16.-- Amicus fidelis. Secundum Deum scilicet. Hic ad medicamentum vitae aeternae et immortalitatis necessaria, cum amico tractat: unde Cicero dicit amicitiam nonnisi inter bonos esse posse, et libera debet esse amicitia, et ad omnem felicitatem proclivis.

VERS. 17.-- Qui timet Dominum. (RAB.) Plena et perfecta amicitiae gratia, etc., usque ad ut de hoc mundo credas te quotidie migraturum.

VERS. 27.-- In omni animo tuo. (RAB.) Corde, non 1191Ccorpore accedendum est ad sapientiam, etc., usque ad perceptio veritatis; unde: Haec est autem vita aeterna ut cognoscant te solum verum Deum (Joan. XVII.), etc.

VERS. 33.-- Fili, si attenderis. Monet sapientia, ut in divina lege assidue meditemur. Primum hortatur ut discamus; post, ut animam ad hoc applicemus, tandem, ut inclinemus; demum, ut diligentiam ad audiendum adhibeamus. Sic enim sapientia sapientem esse promittit. Necesse est enim, ut qui verae sapientiae, quae Christus est, agnitionem desiderat, primum catholicis magistris se humiliter subdat, et omnes affectus animae suae disciplinae eorum. Dehinc intentionem cordis ab omni pravo desiderio avertens ad audiendum verbum Dei adhibeat diligenter, caveatque 1191Dut meditationem legis non pro terrenarum rerum mercede, vel laudis humanae, sed pro ipsius boni amore impendat, ut verae sapientiae fructum percipiat.

VERS. 35.-- In multitudine. Quasi: Ne sis sapiens tibi ipsi. Ne confidas proprio ingenio, haereticos superbiae fastus maxime supplantavit.

VERS. 38.-- Cogitatum habe in praeceptis. (RAB.) Merito illi datur, etc., usque ad et assidue legem meditatur et opere exsequitur.

CHAPTER VI.

1190DVERS. 1.-- Be ye not made, etc. (RAB.) He shows the force of true charity, etc., as far as his own strength shall be dashed to pieces.

VERS. 4.-- Wicked. Upon a good will there follows the fruit of good work, and upon a malicious mind no prosperity at all. For if charity be lacking, in vain dost thou spend labour upon virtue; whence: He that offendeth in one point is become guilty of all (Jac. II). And, from him that hath not, even that which he hath shall be taken away from him (Luc. XIX).

VERS. 6.-- Let many be at peace with thee, etc. Peace is to be kept with all, so far as our frailty allows, but not counsel with many, because it is written: They are called merciful men, but who shall find a faithful man? (Prov. XX.)

1191AVERS. 7.-- If thou wouldst get a friend, etc. (RAB.) Setting forth false friends under various kinds, he comes at last to the true friend: because many profess themselves friends in prosperity, and appear enemies in adversity. Whence: If he shall humble himself against thee, etc.

VERS. 13.-- Take heed. (RAB.) That is, have discernment, etc., as far as: Now I beseech you, brethren, that you mark them who make dissensions and offences (Rom. XVI).

VERS. 14.-- A strong friend. [Vulg. Amicus fidelis protectio fortis, "a faithful friend is a strong defence"] A friend is a sharer of love, to whom thou shouldst so join thy mind that out of two there be made one; to whom thou shouldst entrust thyself as to thyself; from whom thou shouldst fear nothing, and of whom thou shouldst ask nothing dishonourable. For friendship is not a thing of tribute, but full of comeliness; whence: You are my friends, if you do the things that I command 1191Byou (Joan. XV), etc. The Lord has given us a pattern of friendship for us to follow: that we do the friend's will, that we open our secrets to him, and he to us; whence: But I have called you friends, because all things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you (Ibid.).

VERS. 16.-- A faithful friend. According to God, namely. He treats with his friend of the things necessary for the medicine of eternal life and of immortality: whence Cicero says that friendship cannot be except among good men, and that friendship ought to be free, and inclined toward every happiness.

VERS. 17.-- He that feareth the Lord. [Vulg. Qui timet Deum] (RAB.) The full and perfect grace of friendship, etc., as far as that thou mayest believe thyself about to depart from this world every day.

VERS. 27.-- With all thy mind. (RAB.) With the heart, not 1191Cwith the body, must one approach wisdom, etc., as far as the perception of the truth; whence: Now this is eternal life, that they may know thee, the only true God (Joan. XVII.), etc. [Vulg. solum Deum verum, "the only God, true"]

VERS. 33.-- Son, if thou wilt attend. Wisdom admonishes us to meditate assiduously upon the divine law. First she exhorts us to learn; afterward, to apply the soul to this; then, to incline; and lastly, to bring diligence to the hearing. For thus does wisdom promise that a man shall be wise. For it is necessary that he who desires the knowledge of true wisdom, which is Christ, should first humbly subject himself to catholic teachers, and all the affections of his soul to their discipline. Thereafter, turning the intention of his heart away from every crooked desire, let him diligently bring it to the hearing of the word of God, and let him beware 1191Dthat he spend his meditation of the law not for the wages of earthly things, nor of human praise, but for the love of that good itself, so that he may receive the fruit of true wisdom.

VERS. 35.-- In the multitude. As if to say: Be not wise in thine own eyes. Trust not thine own wit; the arrogance of pride has above all supplanted heretics.

VERS. 38.-- Have thy thought upon the precepts. (RAB.) Deservedly is it given to him, etc., as far as and he assiduously meditates the law and fulfils it in work.

CAPUT VII.

Non metes ea in septuplum. (RAB.) Perfectam ultionem, etc., usque ad Assumit septem alios spiritus nequiores se (Luc. XI).

1192AVERS. 4.-- Neque a rege cathedram honoris. Non potest exercere opus justi regiminis, qui sententiam neglexit verae pietatis; unde: Reges gentium dominantur eorum, vos autem non sic, sed qui major est in vobis, fiat sicut minor (Luc. XXII).

VERS. 5.-- Quoniam agnitor. Nulla praesumptio perniciosior, quam de propria justitia, aut scientia superbire; unde: Non est homo super terram, qui faciat bonum, et non peccet (Eccl. VII). Et alibi: Vidisti hominem sapientem sibi videri, et magis illo spem habet stultus (Prov. XXVI).

VERS. 6.-- Noli quaerere fieri judex, nisi valeas. Debet quisque proprias virtutes perpendere, et pro quantitate virium curam aliorum suscipere: ne dum delectatur loco gloriae, fiat subditis auctor ruinae; et 1192Bqui gravatur pondere suarum, velit fieri judex alienarum culparum.

VERS. 7.-- Non pecces in multitudinem. Qui ratione pollet, et liberi arbitrii potestate praevalet, subditorum insolentia non peccet, sed constanter resistat alienae nequitiae: nec se putet impune peccare, si plurimorum acquiescit voluntati; unde: Non solum autem qui ea faciunt, sed qui consentiunt facienti (Rom. I.), etc.

VERS. 13.-- Noli amare mendacium. Omne mendacium malum; et proximis inferre scandalum perniciosum est. Multo magis ergo amico fraudem moliri, crimen imponere: quoniam testis mendax peribit; unde in Proverbiis: Sicut noxius, qui mittit sagittas in mortem, sic qui fraudulenter nocet amico 1192C(Prov. XX).

VERS. 15.-- Noli verbosus esse. Quasi. Noli sensum tuum docere, audi sapientiam seniorum. Tutius est enim audire, quam loqui; unde: Sit omnis homo velox ad audiendum, tardus autem ad loquendum, et tardus ad iram (Jac. I).

Non oderis laboriosa opera. Peccanti homini scientia data est, ut labore manuum quaereret sibi victum; unde: In sudore vultus tui vesceris pane (Gen. III), etc.

VERS. 19.-- Quoniam vindicta carnis, etc. Quia caro peccatorum, quae luxuria defluit, igne inferni et vermibus cruciabitur, ubi vermis non moritur, et ignis non exstinguitur: in verme putredo gehennae, in igne ardor signatur. Vel vermis, est sera scelerum 1192Dpoenitentia, quae in tormentis conscientiam peccatorum mordere non cessat, ut ignis sit poena extrinsecus saeviens, vermis autem dolor interius accusans.

VERS. 20.-- Noli praevaricari in amicum pecuniam. (RAB.) Multi perierunt pro avaritia, etc., usque ad: qui odit munera, vivet (Prov. I).

VERS. 21.-- Gratia enim verecundiae. Oculus non vidit, nec auris audivit, nec in cor hominis ascendit, quae praeparavit Deus diligentibus se. Potest per hanc mulierem sapientia intelligi, quae alibi pretiosa margarita dicitur, quam spiritualis negotiator inveniens vendit omnia, et emit eam. Unde: Vive vitam cum muliere quam dilexisti; quasi, sapientiam 1193Asequere, et scientiam Scripturarum, et quasi conjugem tibi copula.

VERS. 22.-- Non laedas servum. Qualiter servi nutriendi sunt, aperte monstratur, affectu, scilicet, filiorum pro jure naturae; et disciplinandi pro mentis instabilitate; unde in sequentibus, Cibaria et virga et onus asino; panis et disciplina et opus servo.

VERS. 23.-- Neque inopem derelinquas illum. Oppressum violentia servitutis. Sive enim servi, sive liberi, omnes in Christo unum sumus (I Cor. XII).

VERS. 26.-- Filiae, etc. Sicut bonos servos affectu filiorum habendos, sic ancillas, quasi filias habendas esse docet, sed cum disciplina: unde, Lacta filium, et paventem te faciet: lude cum eo, et contristabit te. Hate not laborious works. To sinning man knowledge was given, that by the labour of his hands he should seek his food; whence: In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread (Gen. III), etc.

Or the worm is the late 1192Drepentance for crimes, which in the torments does not cease to gnaw the conscience of sinners, so that the fire is the punishment raging without, and the worm the grief accusing within.

VERS. 20.-- Do not transgress against thy friend for money. (RAB.) Many have perished for avarice, etc., as far as: he that hateth bribes shall live (Prov. I).

By this woman may be understood wisdom, which elsewhere is called the precious pearl, which the spiritual merchant, when he has found it, sells all and buys it. Whence: Live thy life with the woman whom thou hast loved; as if to say, follow wisdom 1193Aand the knowledge of the Scriptures, and join her to thyself as a wife.

VERS. 22.-- Hurt not the servant. It is openly shown in what manner servants are to be nourished: with the affection, namely, of sons, by the right of nature; and to be disciplined, because of the instability of the mind; whence in what follows, Fodder and a rod and a burden for an ass; bread and correction and work for a servant.

VERS. 23.-- Neither forsake him in his need. Oppressed by the violence of servitude. For whether servants or free, we are all one in Christ (I Cor. XII).

VERS. 26.-- Daughters, etc. As he teaches that good servants are to be held with the affection of sons, so also that handmaids are to be held as daughters, but with discipline: whence, Give thy son his way, and he shall make thee afraid: play with him, and he shall grieve thee. Smile not upon him, lest thou be sorry, and at the last 1193Bthy teeth be set on edge (Eccli. XXX).

VERS. 28.-- A wife. He commends conjugal chastity, removing every detestable union. But mystically he admonishes that we, holding firmly the catholic faith in true religion, which we obtained first after the receiving of baptism, cast off the perfidy of heretics.

VERS. 32.-- Love, etc. (RAB.) The fear of God and love, etc., as far as because charity covereth a multitude of sins.

VERS. 35.-- The gift of the arms. Above, for those things which are offered to the ministers of God, he promised the purging of sins: but now, for alms, pardon and the grace of blessing, because as water quenches fire, so alms quenches sins.

1193CVERS 37.-- And to the dead. To alms belongs the burial of the dead; much more, therefore, the consolation of the living: whence Paul teaches to rejoice with them that rejoice, to weep with them that weep. The proof of love is the showing of the work: whence, He that hath the substance of this world, and shall see his brother in need, and shall shut up his bowels from him, how doth the charity of God abide in him? (I Joan. III.)

VERS. 14.-- The beginning of pride. For there is no greater 1195Aapostasy than for a creature to withdraw from the Creator, which is deservedly ascribed to pride. Although elsewhere it is said: The desire of money is the root of all evils (I Tim. VI). There is no covetousness without pride, for no man is proud unless he is covetous, and conversely. Of pride are born heresies, schisms, detractions, envy, wordiness, boasting, contentions and the like, which proceed from covetousness. But covetousness makes men gluttonous, intemperate, drunken, fornicators and the like, who cannot be without pride. Let us therefore beware of covetousness and pride, not two evils, but one from which all draw their beginning.

VERS. 22.-- It was not created. (ID.) Vices were not created, etc., as far as wrath arises, which the constancy of virtue overcomes.

VERS. 23.-- The seed of men shall be honoured. The holy teachers are held in honour among good men, especially those who fear the Lord: whence, Let the priests that rule well be esteemed worthy of double honour (I Tim. V), etc.

VERS. 25.-- The glory of the rich, etc. Truly it is glory to fear God. He who fears him keeps justice in all things: whence, Do not with respect of persons hold the faith 1195Cof Jesus (Jacob. II).

VERS. 27.-- Great is the judge. Although earthly power be great, it cannot excel him that feareth the Lord: for to them that fear God nothing is wanting, but the rich have wanted and have suffered hunger (Psal. XXXIII), etc.

VERS. 28.-- To a wise servant. XVII). But mystically the servant of the Church, who devoutly keeps the Christian religion, is set before the Synagogue; whence, The elder shall serve the younger. And, Jacob I have loved, but Esau I have hated (Gen. XXV).

When the care of governance has been undertaken, every man is found unequal to the several matters, while he is divided among many: nor does he consider his own losses, while he handles those of others.

VERS. 18.-- There is one that is enriched, etc. (RAB.) He layeth up treasure, and knoweth not, etc., as far as but they leave to others who discern truth and falsehood.

VERS. 30.-- Before death, etc. (ID.) It is foolish to praise, etc., as far as who come in the clothing of sheep, but inwardly are ravening wolves.

VERS. 32.-- For as they belch forth, etc. (RAB.) A likeness of the more sagacious animals, etc., as far as they may cast them headlong into the cage of error and the snare of deception.

CAPUT XII.

VERS. 3.-- Non est enim, etc. Gravissimum peccatum est nolle poenitere, nec peccata eleemosynis 1196Dredimere, sed peccata peccatis addere: unde, Super tribus sceleribus Damasci, et super quatuor non convertam eam (Amos, I). Dubiam et dolosam doctrinam, quam in expositione divinae Scripturae expendunt.

VERS. 5.-- Da bono, etc. Simile in Tobia legitur: Panem tuum et vinum super sepulturam justi constitue, et noli ex eo manducare et bibere cum peccatoribus (Tob. IV). Id est pro eo quod peccatores sunt, noli communicare; sicut qui nutriunt histriones, cum esuriant Christi pauperes. Qui autem dat indigenti peccatori, non quia peccator est sed quia homo est, non peccatorem sed justum nutrit: quia culpam non diligit, sed naturam. Non ergo eis obsentiamus, nec potestatem tribuamus, quam significat 1197Bdextera: ne a loco nostro, id est, statu rectae fidei nos dejiciant. Nituntur enim cathedram nostram, id est doctrinam, malitiose subvertere.

VERS. 15.-- In labiis suis. His sententiis notat eos, qui in corde loquuntur et insidiantur ut interficiant innocentem: qui cum aliquem deceperint, movent caput, plaudunt manu, et laetantur cum male fecerint.

CHAPTER XII.

VERS. 3.-- For there is not, etc. It is a most grievous sin to be unwilling to repent, nor to redeem sins by alms 1196D, but to add sins to sins: whence, Upon three crimes of Damascus, and upon four I will not convert it (Amos, I). A doubtful and deceitful teaching, which they expend in the exposition of Holy Scripture.

VERS. 5.-- Give to the good, etc. The like is read in Tobias: Set thy bread and thy wine upon the burial of a just man, and eat and drink not thereof with sinners (Tob. IV). That is, for the reason that they are sinners, do not share with them; as they who nourish players while the poor of Christ hunger. But he who gives to a needy sinner, not because he is a sinner but because he is a man, nourishes not a sinner but a just man: because he loves not the fault, but the nature. If he understands a sinner and an ungodly heretic 1197Awhile they are in error, there is to be no communicating with them: whence, Give not that which is holy to dogs (Matth. VII).

VERS. 8.-- A friend shall not be known in good things. A friend and an enemy are more easily proved in adversity, because there are many friends of the table who do not remain in the day of necessity. He therefore was a true friend who said: Who is weak, and I am not weak? (II Cor. XI.)

VERS. 10.-- Trust not an enemy. Historically he treats of false friends, from whom we ought to beware. But mystically he marks the heretics, who are not truly friends, whose teaching is full of the rust of error and of wickedness: whose speech creeps like a cancer, although they feign themselves faithful and catholic. Let us not therefore obsentiamus to them, nor grant them the power which 1197Bthe right hand signifies: lest they cast us down from our place, that is, from the state of right faith. For they strive maliciously to overturn our chair, that is, our teaching.

Who shall heal. The heretic who bears the office of a teacher, and says that he is a physician of souls: For if the salt shall have become insipid, it is good for nothing more, but to be cast out (Matth. V). [Vulg. Quod si sal evanuerit, in quo salietur? "if the salt lose its savour, wherewith shall it be salted?"]

VERS. 15.-- In his lips. By these sentences he marks those who speak in the heart and lie in wait to slay the innocent: who, when they have deceived anyone, move the head, clap the hand, and rejoice when they have done evil.

CAPUT XIII.

VERS. 1.-- Qui tetigerit picem inquinabitur. (RAB.) 1197CPer similitudinem ostendit, etc., usque ad ne mala quae frequenter aspiciunt, imitari velint.

VERS. 2.-- Pondus super se tollet. (ID.) Haec moraliter nos instruunt, ut humilia sequamur, superba fugiamus: Quia Deus superbis resistit, humilibus autem dat gratiam (Jacob. IV). Mystice autem docet, etc., usque ad quia aeterna confusione dignos ostendent, cum in aeternum cruciabuntur.

VERS. 11.-- Noli esse, etc. Non virtutem humilitatis abhorret: sed auctoritatem firmam contra expugnatores veritatis docet tenere, quibus fortiter resistendum.

VERS. 12.-- Advocatus. Modeste agendum est cum potentibus hujus saeculi, qui frequenter derident innocentium simplicitatem: quibus tamen debitum 1197Dhonorem conferre debemus, sed non contra Deum, vel contra mandata ejus. Unde, Omnes honorate, fraternitatem diligite, Deum timete, regem honorificate (I Petr. II).

VERS. 16.-- Cave tibi, et attende. Quasi: nihil tutum apud amatores mundi, qui mutabilia sequuntur et cum illis mutantur. Unde: Stultus ut luna mutatur (Eccle. XXVII). Possunt in his haeretici et falsi Christiani intelligi, quibus non est credendum, quia inconstantes sunt in omnibus viis suis.

VERS. 18.-- Omni vita. (RAB.) Caeteris refutatis, etc., usque ad quia prope est Dominus omnibus invocantibus eum in veritate.

VERS. 19.-- Omne animal. Ad superiora respicit, 1198Aut divitum, scilicet, et potentum societatem declines, et ei conjungaris, qui studio et religione tibi consors est. Haec est enim amicitia firma, quam similitudo virtutum sociat.

Si communicabit. Rapacitas lupi cum agni innocentia non convenit, nec avari rabies cum modestia simplicis. Nulla enim communicatio munditiae cum immunditia: Sicut enim canis revertitur ad vomitum, ita qui iterat stultitiam suam (Prov. VI). Nulla ergo parilitas superbi divitis cum humilitate pauperis Christi.

VERS. 25.-- Dives commotus. Quod de divite et paupere increpat, exsequitur: quia multi sunt qui divitis facta et dicta extollunt: pauci qui doctrinam humilium et virtutem intelligunt. Arrogantes inveniunt 1198Bpraedicatores, humiles vero contemptores. Unde: Divitiae addunt amicos plurimos, a paupere vero, et hi, quos habuit, separantur (Prov. XIX).

VERS. 31.-- Cor hominis immutat. In judicio vultus, mentis mutatio deprehenditur: unde, Cor gaudens exhilarat faciem, in moerore animi dejicitur spiritus.

VERS. 32.-- Vestigium cordis boni. Quia multi simulati et ficti: ideo difficile invenitur in quo cor et facies, id est cogitatio et operatio atque doctrina, in bono conveniant: cum autem hoc invenies, da gloriam Deo, quia hoc ejus donum est.

CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- He that toucheth pitch shall be defiled. (RAB.) 1197CBy a similitude he shows, etc., as far as lest they should wish to imitate the evils which they frequently look upon.

VERS. 2.-- He shall take a burden upon himself. (ID.) These things instruct us morally, that we should follow humble things and flee proud ones: Because God resisteth the proud, but to the humble he giveth grace (Jacob. IV). But mystically he teaches, etc., as far as because they will show themselves worthy of eternal confusion, when they shall be tormented for ever.

VERS. 11.-- Be not, etc. He does not abhor the virtue of humility: but teaches that a firm authority is to be held against the assailants of the truth, whom we must resist strongly.

VERS. 12.-- Summoned. We must act modestly with the powerful of this world, who frequently deride the simplicity of the innocent: to whom nevertheless we ought to render the due 1197Dhonor, but not against God, or against his commandments. Whence, Honor all men, love the brotherhood, fear God, honor the king (I Petr. II).

VERS. 16.-- Beware of thyself, and take heed. As if: nothing is safe among the lovers of the world, who follow changeable things and are changed along with them. Whence: The fool is changed as the moon (Eccle. XXVII). In these, heretics and false Christians can be understood, who are not to be believed, because they are inconstant in all their ways.

VERS. 18.-- All thy life. (RAB.) The rest being refuted, etc., as far as because the Lord is nigh to all that call upon him in truth.

VERS. 19.-- Every beast. He looks back to the things above, 1198Athat thou shouldst, namely, decline the society of the rich and of the powerful, and shouldst join thyself to him who is thy consort in zeal and in religion. For this is a firm friendship, which a likeness of virtues joins together.

If he shall have fellowship. The rapacity of the wolf agrees not with the innocence of the lamb, nor the rage of the greedy man with the modesty of the simple. For there is no communion of cleanness with uncleanness: For as the dog returneth to the vomit, so he that repeateth his folly (Prov. VI). There is therefore no parity of the proud rich man with the humility of the poor of Christ.

VERS. 25.-- The rich man moved. What he rebukes concerning the rich man and the poor man, he pursues: because there are many who extol the deeds and the sayings of the rich man: few who understand the teaching and the virtue of the humble. The arrogant find 1198Bpreachers, but the humble find despisers. Whence: Riches add many friends, but from the poor man even they whom he had are separated (Prov. XIX).

VERS. 31.-- The heart of a man changeth. In the judgment of the countenance the change of the mind is detected: whence, A glad heart maketh a cheerful countenance; in the sorrow of the mind the spirit is cast down.

VERS. 32.-- The trace of a good heart. Because many are feigned and counterfeit: therefore it is with difficulty found in whom the heart and the face, that is, thought and work and teaching, agree in good: but when thou shalt find this, give glory to God, because this is his gift.

CAPUT XIV.

VERS. 1.-- Beatus vir, etc. (RAB.) Peccando. Hinc 1198CJacobus ait: Qui in verbo non offendit, perfectus est (Jacob. III). Illo, scilicet verbo cujus offensionem humana potest vitare fragilitas.

VERS. 3.-- Viro cupido et tenaci. Avaritiae pestem describit quae excruciat avarum ne consequatur laboris sui fructum, Qui thesaurizat et ignorat cui congregabit ea (Psal. XXXVIII).

VERS. 5.-- Qui sibi nequam. Haec ad litteram mentem avari et invidi describunt, qui de bonis suis non laetantur. Allegorice vero significant philosophos et haereticos qui sine fide Christi congregant divitias pravi dogmatis, quae nihil proficiunt eis, imo poenam conferunt aeternam.

VERS. 7.-- Et si bene. (ID.) Dixerat nihil esse 1198Dnequius, etc., usque ad nunc vero idem repetit.

VERS. 9.-- Insatiabilis oculus. Quia sine fructu bonorum consumit vitam, et sterilem animam gehennae ignibus tradit cruciendam.

VERS. 10.-- Oculus malus ad mala. Haereticorum oculus semper malis est intentus, et panem coelestem, id est intellectum divinae sapientiae non meretur, et in Scripturae mensa marcescit indigentia.

VERS. 11.-- Fili, si habes. Hortatur ut, contempta mundi luxuria, bonorum operum oblationes Deo offeramus: quia breve est spatium vitae in quo operari possumus: post mortem enim, quae festinat, non est tempus operandi, sed mercedem recipiendi: Unde, Operamini dum dies est: venit enim nox, quando jam non licet operari.

1199AVERS. 18.-- Omnis caro. Sicut fenum in terra vel folium in arbore nascitur, crescit, virescit, siccatur et dejicitur, ita caro nostra nascitur ex progenie parentum, crescit infantia et pueritia, florescit in adolescentia, maturescit in juventute, arescit in senectute, dejicitur in morte.

VERS. 22.-- Beatus vir, etc. Beati immaculati in via, qui ambulant in lege Domini. Beati qui scrutantur testimonia ejus. (Psal. CXVIII.)

VERS. 25.-- Statuet casulam. Id est doctoris officium agens, sanae doctrinae opera justitiae conjunget, ut auditores verbo et exemplo instruat.

CHAPTER XIV.

VERS. 1.-- Blessed is the man, etc. (RAB.) By sinning. Hence 1198CJames saith: He that offendeth not in word, is perfect (Jacob. III). In that word, namely, the offense of which human frailty can avoid.

VERS. 3.-- To a covetous and niggardly man. He describes the plague of avarice, which torments the covetous man lest he obtain the fruit of his own labor, Who layeth up treasure, and knoweth not for whom he shall gather these things (Psal. XXXVIII).

VERS. 5.-- He that is evil to himself. These things describe according to the letter the mind of the covetous and of the envious, who rejoice not in their own goods. But allegorically they signify the philosophers and heretics who without the faith of Christ gather riches of depraved dogma, which profit them nothing, nay rather confer eternal punishment.

VERS. 7.-- And if he do well. (ID.) He had said that nothing is 1198Dmore wicked, etc., as far as but now he repeats the same.

VERS. 9.-- The insatiable eye. Because without the fruit of good things he consumes his life, and delivers his barren soul to the fires of Gehenna to be tormented.

VERS. 10.-- An evil eye toward evil things. The eye of the heretics is always bent upon evils, and it merits not the heavenly bread, that is, the understanding of divine wisdom, and at the table of Scripture it withers with want.

VERS. 11.-- Son, if thou have. He exhorts that, the luxury of the world being despised, we should offer to God the oblations of good works: because the space of life in which we can work is short: for after death, which hastens on, there is no time for working, but for receiving the wage: Whence, Work ye while it is day: for the night cometh, when it is no longer lawful to work. [Vulg. Joan. 9:4 Me oportet operari opera ejus qui misit me, donec dies est: venit nox, quando nemo potest operari, "I must work the works of him that sent me, whilst it is day: the night cometh, when no man can work"]

1199AVERS. 18.-- All flesh. As hay in the earth or a leaf on a tree is born, grows, becomes green, dries up and is cast down, so our flesh is born of the offspring of parents, grows in infancy and in boyhood, flowers in adolescence, ripens in youth, withers in old age, is cast down in death.

VERS. 22.-- Blessed is the man, etc. Blessed are the undefiled in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord. Blessed are they that search his testimonies. (Psal. CXVIII.)

VERS. 25.-- He shall set up his little dwelling. That is, performing the office of a teacher, he shall join to sound teaching the works of justice, that he may instruct his hearers by word and by example.

CAPUT XV.

VERS. 3.-- Aqua sapientiae. (RAB.) gratia Spiritus sancti: unde, Qui biberit aquam quam ego dabo vobis, 1199Bfiet in eo fons aquae salientis in vitam aeternam (Joan. XIV), id est non cedet persecutioni, vel haereticae pravitati.

VERS. 5.-- Et in medio Ecclesiae. Apostolos, praedicatores Evangelii significat, quos Dei sapientia, id est Christus, Ecclesiae praeposuit.

VERS. 7.-- Homines stulti, etc. Pagani, et idololatrae et Judaei, qui crucifigunt Filium Dei, sequentes umbram legis, et respuentes veritatem Evangelii: vel haeretici, qui Scripturas pervertunt.

VERS. 8.-- Et viri veraces. Omnis qui est ex veritate, audit vocem meam.

VERS. 9.-- In ore peccatoris. Haereticorum, qui doctrinam Patrum despiciunt, et simplices decipiunt. Donum largitatis non invidet Deus alicui, qui 1199Cvult omnes homines salvos fieri.

VERS. 12.-- Non dicas. (ID.) Non sunt mala nostra Deo imputanda, cui non sunt necessaria: Deus intentator malorum est (Jacob. I).

VERS. 14.-- Constituit hominem ad imaginem et similitudinem suam, et liberum arbitrium dedit ei.

VERS. 16.-- Si volueris. Id est si volueris implere opera et mandata Dei, habebis consolationem: si contempseris gehennae ardorem.

VERS. 19.-- Quoniam multa sapientia. (ID.) Nullus potest praescientiam Dei, etc., usque ad nihil faciat homo, nisi quod dignum sit conspectui divino.

VERS. 21.-- Nemini mandavit. Non est auctor Deus peccandi, nec spatium concedit in quo homo 1199Dimpune peccare possit; semper bona praecipit, et mala interdicit. Solummodo placet ei quod bonum est, infidelitas autem et inutilitas displicet, quoniam justus Deus, et justitias dilexit. (Psal. X.)

CHAPTER XV.

VERS. 3.-- The water of wisdom. (RAB.) The grace of the Holy Spirit: whence, He that shall drink the water that I will give you, 1199Bit shall become in him a fountain of water springing up into life everlasting (Joan. XIV), that is, he shall not yield to persecution, or to heretical depravity. [Vulg. qui autem biberit ex aqua quam ego dabo ei, non sitiet in aeternum: sed aqua quam ego dabo ei, fiet in eo fons aquae salientis in vitam aeternam]

VERS. 5.-- And in the midst of the Church. He signifies the apostles, the preachers of the Gospel, whom the wisdom of God, that is, Christ, set over the Church.

VERS. 7.-- Foolish men, etc. The pagans, and the idolaters and the Jews, who crucify the Son of God, following the shadow of the law and spurning the truth of the Gospel: or the heretics, who pervert the Scriptures.

VERS. 8.-- And truthful men. Everyone that is of the truth heareth my voice.

VERS. 9.-- In the mouth of a sinner. Of the heretics, who despise the teaching of the Fathers and deceive the simple. God envies the gift of his bounty to no one, who 1199Cwilleth all men to be saved.

VERS. 12.-- Say not. (ID.) Our evils are not to be imputed to God, to whom they are not necessary: God is no tempter of evils (Jacob. I).

VERS. 14.-- He made man to his own image and likeness, and gave him free will.

VERS. 16.-- If thou wilt. That is, if thou wilt fulfil the works and the commandments of God, thou shalt have consolation: if thou shalt despise them, the burning of Gehenna.

VERS. 19.-- For the wisdom of God is great. (ID.) No one can [comprehend] the foreknowledge of God, etc., as far as let a man do nothing but what is worthy of the divine sight.

VERS. 21.-- He hath commanded no man. God is not the author of sinning, nor does he grant a space in which a man 1199Dmight sin unpunished; he always enjoins good things, and forbids evil ones. That alone pleases him which is good, but unfaithfulness and unprofitableness displease him, for God is just, and hath loved justice. (Psal. X.) [Vulg. justus Dominus, "the Lord is just"]

CAPUT XVI.

VERS. 5.-- Ab uno sensato inhabitabitur patria. (RAB.) Qui secundum sanam regulam Scripturae fide et opere permanet in semita justitiae.

A tribus impiis descretur. Scilicet qui cogitatione, locutione, opere seipsos sequestrant a societate bonorum.

VERS. 6.-- Multa talia vidit oculus. Quanto quis ardentius legem Dei discit, tanto magis illustratur lumine scientiae: unde, Qui habet mandata mea et 1200Aservat ea, ille est qui me diligit. Qui autem diligit me, diligetur a Patre meo: et ego diligam eum, et manifestabo ei meipsum (Joan. XIV).

VERS. 8.-- Non exoraverunt pro peccatis. (RAB.) Antiqui gigantes, desideriis terrenis irretiti, et sarcina peccatorum gravati; vel haeretici, qui confidunt in virtute sua, et in divitiis dialecticae et rhetoricae gloriantur.

VERS. 12.-- Misericordia enim et ira cum illo. (RAB.) Divina censura aequalitatem servat, etc., usque ad: Qui sequitur justitiam et misericordiam, inveniet vitam et justitiam et gloriam (Prov. XXI)

VERS. 16.-- Non dicas: A Deo abscondar. Reprehendit eos, qui Deum credunt non curare mortalia: unde Eliphaz, Quid enim novit Deus? Et quasi per 1200Bcaliginem judicat. Nubes latibulum ejus, nec nostra considerat (Job. XXII). Hinc Paulus ait, Vivus est sermo Dei et efficax, et penetrabilior omni gladio ancipiti, et pertingens usque ad divisionem animae ac spiritus, compagum quoque ac medullarum, et discretor cogitationum et intentionum cordis (Hebr. IV).

VERS. 18.-- Ecce coelum, et coeli coelorum. Quasi: Si majora pro voluntate sua dispensat, et comprehendit: minora eum latere non possunt, ad cujus nutum omnia pavent et contremiscunt; et non est qui possit ejus resistere voluntati.

VERS. 22.-- Et opera justitiae. Nemo enuntiare potest altitudinem justitiae ejus, nec sustinere justitiae districtionem. A quibusdam; et interrogatia hominum, etc. Idololatris, haereticis, vel falsis catholicis: unde, 1200CNon enim auditores legis justificantur, sed factores (Rom. II).

VERS. 25.-- Et dicam in aequitate. (RAB.) Qui intento corde desiderat, etc., usque ad sed statum sanctae Ecclesiae aperte manifestat.

VERS. 26.-- Distinxit partes illorum. Singularum gentium originem per diversas partes mundi discrevit, ut quaeque gens parentis sui vocabulo denominata proprias sedes possideret. Mystice autem divina sapientia Ecclesiam instituens, per totius orbis partes dilatavit, et in singulis gentibus collocavit: ut in diversis locis diversa nomina vindicaret sibi, unde Joannes in Apocalypsi: Angelo Smyrnae Ecclesiae scribe (Apoc. II), et Pergami et Thyatirae et caeteris.

VERS. 29.-- Non sis incredibilis verbo illius. Dixit 1200Dsuperius, quomodo ab initio Deus in scientia et judicio ordinavit opera sua: nunc interposita sententia auditorem hortatur, ne incredibilis verbo illius a statu rectitudinis flectatur, et ad narrationem redit, ostendens quomodo clementia Dei genus humanum reparavit sic: Post haec Deus in terram respexit, etc.

VERS. 31.-- Denuntiavit, cogitationis arcana: unde, Intellexisti cogitationes meas de longe (Psal. CXXXVIII). Et, Dominus novit cogitationes hominum (Psal. XCIII).

CHAPTER XVI.

VERS. 5.-- By one man of sense a country shall be inhabited. (RAB.) He who according to the sound rule of Scripture continues in the path of justice by faith and by work.

By three ungodly men it shall be deserted descretur. Namely, they who by thought, by speech, by work sequester themselves from the fellowship of the good. [Vulg. tribus impiorum deseretur, "the tribe of the ungodly shall be made desolate"]

VERS. 6.-- Many such things hath the eye seen. The more ardently one learns the law of God, so much the more is he enlightened by the light of knowledge: whence, He that hath my commandments and 1200Akeepeth them, he it is that loveth me. And he that loveth me shall be loved of my Father: and I will love him, and will manifest myself to him (Joan. XIV).

VERS. 8.-- They prayed not for their sins. (RAB.) The ancient giants, entangled in earthly desires and weighed down by the burden of sins; or the heretics, who trust in their own strength, and glory in the riches of dialectic and of rhetoric.

VERS. 12.-- For mercy and wrath are with him. (RAB.) The divine censure keeps an equality, etc., as far as: He that followeth justice and mercy shall find life and justice and glory (Prov. XXI)

VERS. 16.-- Say not: I shall be hidden from God. He reproves those who believe that God has no care for mortal things: whence Eliphaz, For what doth God know? And he judgeth as it were through 1200Ba mist. The clouds are his covert, and he doth not consider our things (Job. XXII). Hence Paul saith, The word of God is living and effectual, and more piercing than any two-edged sword, and reaching unto the division of the soul and the spirit, of the joints also and the marrow, and a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart (Hebr. IV).

VERS. 18.-- Behold the heaven, and the heavens of heavens. As if: If he dispenses and comprehends the greater things according to his will, the lesser cannot lie hidden from him, at whose beck all things quake and tremble; and there is none who can resist his will.

VERS. 22.-- And the works of justice. No one can declare the height of his justice, nor bear the strictness of his justice. From certain men; and the questioning interrogatia of men, etc. From the idolaters, the heretics, or the false catholics: whence, 1200CFor not the hearers of the law are justified, but the doers (Rom. II). [Vulg. interrogatio omnium, "the questioning of all"]

VERS. 25.-- And I will speak in equity. (RAB.) He who with intent heart desires, etc., as far as but openly manifests the state of the holy Church.

VERS. 26.-- He distinguished their parts. He separated the origin of the several nations through the diverse parts of the world, that each nation, named by the word of its own parent, might possess its proper seats. But mystically the divine wisdom, in founding the Church, spread it abroad through the parts of the whole globe, and placed it among the several nations: so that in diverse places it might claim diverse names for itself, whence John in the Apocalypse: To the angel of the Church of Smyrna write (Apoc. II), and of Pergamus and of Thyatira and the rest.

VERS. 29.-- Be not incredulous to his word. He had said 1200Dabove how from the beginning God ordered his works in knowledge and in judgment: now, a sentence being interposed, he exhorts the hearer, lest, being incredulous to his word, he be bent away from the state of rectitude; and he returns to the narration, showing how the clemency of God repaired the human race, thus: After these things God looked upon the earth, etc.

VERS. 31.-- He hath declared, the secrets of thought: whence, Thou hast understood my thoughts afar off (Psal. CXXXVIII). And, The Lord knoweth the thoughts of men (Psal. XCIII).

CAPUT XVII.

VERS. 1.-- Deus creavit. (RAB.) Ut sic vivat, sapiat, etc., usque ad ita homo charitatem habeat, ut sit bonus et justus; etc.

1201AVERS. 2.-- Et secundum se vestivit, etc. Unde: Induite novum hominem, qui secundum Deum creatus est in justitia et veritatis sanctitate (Ephes. IX).

VERS. 3.-- Et dedit illi potestatem. Quia in hoc quoque factus est homo ad imaginem Dei, quo irrationabilibus ratione antecellit, et ea regit et dominatur in eis, et omnium creatorem agnoscit. Sed quia honorem suum non intellexit, comparatus est jumentis insipientibus (Psal. XLVIII).

VERS. 5.-- Creavit ex ipso. Sicut de latere Adae dormientis formata est Eva, sic de latere Christi in cruce morientis exivit sanguis et aqua, de quibus formatur Ecclesia.

VERS. 9.-- Et legem vitae. Naturalem, quam dedit omni homini, ut subjiceretur suo creatori, et bonorum 1201Boperum in se honorificentiam custodiret. Vel, legem litterae, quam ad correctionem morum, et reparationem naturalis legis, scribi voluit; hoc enim naturalis lex et litteralis insinuat, ut Deum toto corde, tota anima, tota virtute diligamus: et mandata ejus in Dei et proximi dilectione custodiamus.

VERS. 13-- Coram illo. (RAB.) Unde: Novit Dominus viam justorum, et iter impiorum peribit (Psal. I).

VERS. 14.-- Rectorem. Angelos, quibus commisit Deus custodiam singularum gentium! Unde, Statuit terminos gentium juxta numerum angelorum Dei. (Deut. XXXII.) Vel, doctores quos ad praedicandum misit.

VERS. 21.-- Convertere. Ostensa conditione hominis, quomodo scilicet ad imaginem Dei creatus sit, 1201Cet caeteris praelatus creaturis: hortatur ut post lapsum peccati revertatur ad factorem suum, relinquens errorem, quo semetipsum decepit; et deprecetur Deum pro offensionibus suis, et tota mente refugiat ad divinam misericordiam, spernens idololatriam, et diabolicae servitutis fallaciam. Discat et mandatorum Dei regulam, ut in sorte sanctitatis, qua electus est, permaneat, et in laudibus Altissimi perseveret.

VERS. 27.-- Confiteberis vivens. (RAB.) In praesenti vita, postquam non est tempus operandi, sed praemium recipiendi: Unde, Currite dum lucem habetis, ne tenebrae vos comprehendant (Joan. XII).

VERS. 30.-- Quid lucidius sole? (ID.) Ostendit mundi machinam transitoriam esse: nec lux corporalis perpetuo eadem manet; quanto minus vana 1201Dcogitatio humanae fragilitatis.

CHAPTER XVII.

VERS. 1.-- God created. (RAB.) That he may so live, be wise, etc., as far as so let a man have charity, that he may be good and just; etc.

1201AVERS. 2.-- And he clothed him according to himself, etc. Whence: Put ye on the new man, who according to God is created in justice and holiness of truth (Ephes. IX).

VERS. 3.-- And he gave him power. Because in this also man was made to the image of God, in that he excels the irrational creatures by reason, and rules them and has dominion over them, and acknowledges the creator of all. But because he understood not his own honor, he is compared to senseless beasts (Psal. XLVIII).

VERS. 5.-- He created out of him. As from the side of the sleeping Adam Eve was formed, so from the side of Christ dying on the cross there went out blood and water, out of which the Church is formed.

VERS. 9.-- And the law of life. The natural law, which he gave to every man, that he should be subject to his creator, and should keep in himself the honorableness of good 1201Bworks. Or, the law of the letter, which he willed to be written for the correction of morals and for the repairing of the natural law; for this is what the natural and the literal law intimates, that we should love God with the whole heart, the whole soul, the whole strength: and should keep his commandments in the love of God and of our neighbor.

VERS. 13-- Before him. (RAB.) Whence: The Lord knoweth the way of the just, and the way of the ungodly shall perish (Psal. I).

VERS. 14.-- A ruler. The angels, to whom God committed the keeping of the several nations! Whence, He appointed the bounds of the nations according to the number of the angels of God. (Deut. XXXII.) [Vulg. constituit terminos populorum juxta numerum filiorum Israel, "he appointed the bounds of peoples according to the number of the children of Israel"] Or, the teachers whom he sent to preach.

VERS. 21.-- Be converted. The condition of man being shown, namely how he was created to the image of God, 1201Cand set before the rest of the creatures: he exhorts that after the fall of sin he return to his maker, leaving the error by which he deceived himself; and that he entreat God for his offenses, and with his whole mind flee back to the divine mercy, spurning idolatry and the deceit of diabolical servitude. Let him learn also the rule of God's commandments, that he may remain in the lot of holiness to which he was chosen, and may persevere in the praises of the Most High.

VERS. 27.-- Thou shalt confess while living. (RAB.) In the present life, after which there is no time for working, but for receiving the reward: Whence, Run while you have the light, lest the darkness overtake you (Joan. XII). [Vulg. Ambulate dum lucem habetis, ut non vos tenebrae comprehendant, "Walk whilst you have the light, that the darkness overtake you not"]

VERS. 30.-- What is brighter than the sun? (ID.) He shows that the fabric of the world is transitory: nor does the bodily light remain the same perpetually; how much less the vain 1201Dthought of human frailty.

CAPUT XVIII.

VERS. 1.-- Qui vivit in aeternum. (RAB.) Rerum substantia simul creata est, etc., usque ad magnitudinem virtutis scire vel enarrare: unde, Nemo novit Filium nisi Pater, neque Patrem quis novit nisi Filius, et cui voluerit Filius revelare (Matth. XI).

VERS. 5.-- Non est minuere. (ID.) Nihil de Scripturis sanctis est minuendum, nihil addendum: unde, Si quis apposuerit ad haec, apponet super illum Deus plagas scriptas in libro isto (Apoc. XXII), etc.

VERS. 8.-- Centum anni. (ID.) Paucitatem dierum nostrorum comparat guttae maris, et calculo 1202Aarenae: quia ad comparationem aeternae felicitatis nimia est brevitas, et miseria maxima: unde, Homo natus de muliere, brevi vivens tempore, repletur multis miseriis (Job. XIV).

VERS. 12.-- Miseratio hominis. Pauci sunt, qui misereantur proximo: cum misericordia Dei redundat per universum mundum, qui solem suum facit oriri super bonos et malos.

VERS. 14.-- Excipiens doctrinam. Hortatur eos qui doctrinam Evangelii accipiunt, ut misericordiam proximis impendant: et festinent diem judicii misericordiae operibus praevenire; unde: Beati misericordes, quoniam ipsi misericordiam consequentur (Matth. V).

VERS. 15.-- Verbi mali. Lingua eucharis gratiosum 1202Bhominem facit; unde: Qui moderatur sermones suos doctus et prudens est (Prov. XVII).

VERS. 16.-- Nonne ardorem. Segetes defendit ros ab ardore solis: et verbum bonum largientem a nequitia irascentis; unde: Sermo durus suscitat rixas, lenis mitigat suscitatas (Prov. XV).

VERS. 18.-- Stultus acriter improperabit. (RAB.) In ore stulti jurgia, etc., usque ad polluunt verbo mendaci.

VERS. 19.-- Para justitiam. Ut videas quid in te Deo placeat vel displaceat, et delicta corrigas.

VERS. 20.-- Ante judicium. (RAB.) Haec monent ut semper parati simus, nec stulta securitate nobis blandiamur: sed in Dei servitute laboremus usque ad finem vitae.

1202CVERS. 23.-- Ante orationm. Cum stabis ad orandum, dimitte si quid habeas adversus aliquem.

VERS. 24.-- Memento. Unde: Justus Dominus, et justitias dilexit: aequitatem vidit vultus ejus (Psal. X.).

VERS. 25.-- Paupertatis. Futurae post hanc vitam, ut facias amicos qui recipiant te in aeterna tabernacula.

VERS. 26.-- Haec omnia. Ver, aestas, autumnus, hiems, dies et nox, nunquam omnia requiescunt; et tamen omnia aeternitati comparata cito transeunt: et omnia coram oculis Domini certo fine clauduntur. Ipse autem semper idem est, et anni ejus non deficient.

VERS. 27.-- Homo sapiens. Beatus est homo qui semper est pavidus ut a peccatis suis caveat, et praecepta 1202DDomini impleat: Quia initium sapientiae timor Domini. Intellectus bonus omnibus facientibus eum (Psal. CX.).

VERS. 28.-- Invenienti. Id est, cum invenerit, et agnitionem ejus perceperit, ipsa dabit confessionem rectae praedicationis, et studium bonae operationis.

VERS. 29.-- Sensati. Quasi astuti agnoscunt sapientiam, et sensati in verbis opere demonstrant prudentiam.

VERS. 33.-- Ne fueris mediocris. Provocat nos ad opera misericordiae. Si enim pigri fuerimus, non modo deerunt nobis solatia temporalia, imo animae nostrae bona futura: quando temporalis eleemosyna remunerabitur mercede aeterna.

CHAPTER XVIII.

VERS. 1.-- He that liveth for ever. (RAB.) The substance of things was created all at once, etc., as far as to know or to declare the greatness of his power: whence, No one knoweth the Son but the Father, neither doth any one know the Father but the Son, and he to whom it shall please the Son to reveal him (Matth. XI).

VERS. 5.-- There is no diminishing. (ID.) Nothing is to be diminished from the holy Scriptures, nothing to be added: whence, If any man shall add to these things, God shall add unto him the plagues written in this book (Apoc. XXII), etc.

VERS. 8.-- A hundred years. (ID.) He compares the fewness of our days to a drop of the sea, and to a pebble 1202Aof the sand: because in comparison with eternal happiness the brevity is exceeding great, and the misery greatest: whence, Man born of a woman, living for a short time, is filled with many miseries (Job. XIV).

VERS. 12.-- The mercy of man. Few there are who have mercy on their neighbor: whereas the mercy of God overflows through the whole world, who maketh his sun to rise upon the good and the bad.

VERS. 14.-- He that receiveth teaching. He exhorts those who receive the teaching of the Gospel, that they show mercy to their neighbors: and that they hasten to forestall the day of judgment by works of mercy; whence: Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy (Matth. V).

VERS. 15.-- Of an evil word. A gracious tongue 1202Bmaketh a man well-favored; whence: He that moderateth his words is learned and prudent (Prov. XVII).

VERS. 16.-- Shall not the heat. The dew defends the standing corn from the heat of the sun: and a good word defends the giver from the wickedness of him that is angry; whence: A hard word stirreth up strifes, a gentle one mitigateth them when stirred up (Prov. XV). [Vulg. sermo durus suscitat furorem (Prov. 15:1) and qui patiens est mitigat suscitatas (Prov. 15:18)]

VERS. 18.-- The fool shall upbraid bitterly. (RAB.) In the mouth of the fool are quarrels, etc., as far as they defile with a lying word.

VERS. 19.-- Prepare justice. That thou mayest see what in thee pleases or displeases God, and mayest correct thy faults.

VERS. 20.-- Before judgment. (RAB.) These things admonish us to be always ready, nor to flatter ourselves with a foolish security: but to labor in the service of God even to the end of life.

1202CVERS. 23.-- Before prayer orationm. When thou shalt stand to pray, forgive if thou have anything against anyone.

VERS. 24.-- Remember. Whence: The Lord is just, and hath loved justice: his countenance hath beheld equity (Psal. X.).

VERS. 25.-- Of poverty. Of that which is to come after this life, that thou mayest make friends who may receive thee into everlasting dwellings.

VERS. 26.-- All these things. Spring, summer, autumn, winter, day and night, all of them never rest; and yet all things compared to eternity pass swiftly away: and all things are shut up before the eyes of the Lord with a fixed end. But he himself is always the same, and his years shall not fail.

VERS. 27.-- A wise man. Blessed is the man who is always fearful, that he may beware of his own sins and may fulfil the precepts 1202Dof the Lord: Because the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom. A good understanding to all that do it (Psal. CX.).

VERS. 28.-- To him that findeth. That is, when he shall have found her, and shall have received the knowledge of her, she herself shall give the confession of right preaching, and the zeal of good working.

VERS. 29.-- Men of sense. As it were: the crafty acknowledge wisdom, and men of sense show forth prudence in words by their work.

VERS. 33.-- Be not mediocre. He provokes us to the works of mercy. For if we shall be slothful, not only shall temporal solaces be wanting to us, but rather the future goods of our soul: whereas a temporal alms shall be repaid with an eternal wage.

VERS. 22.-- Est solertia certa, etc. Incipit dare differentiam inter sanctos et hypocritas. (RAB.) Sic haeretici et hypocritae ostendunt virtutum speciem, sed veritatem ejus non habent: et ut peccandi locum invenerint, ostendunt quod in corde habebant.

VERS. 1.-- A drunken workman. (RAB.) He looks back to the things above where he says, Be not delighted in crowds, by whom, namely, the vice of drunkenness is thought to be a light thing, though it is the loss of good things and the multiplication of crimes. Little things. An idle word, laughter, revellings, drinkings, and the like.

VERS. 2.-- And women. (RAB.) Fornication, by which, the truth of the faith being forsaken, they are joined to demons.

VERS. 3.-- He joineth to harlots. He shows them to be blameworthy; because, true wisdom being left behind, they are made fools, and join themselves to harlots.

He that believeth, etc. The levity of the mind is described, which is infirm and changeable: whence, The fool is changed as the moon. He that believeth. Of charity the Apostle says, She believeth all things, 1203Bhopeth all things, endureth all things (I Cor. XII). But there it is the perfection of a good will, which is always ready for every good work. Here, however, the levity of the mind is described, which has not the state of firmness.

VERS. 7.-- Repeat not a hard and wicked word. (RAB.) That is, after weeping, etc., as far as repeat not the deed to a most wicked man. [Vulg. verbum nequam et durum, "a wicked and hard word"]

VERS. 8.-- To friend and to enemy. He exhorts that we have caution and discretion: lest to friend and to enemy, that is, to catholic and to heretic, we lay open our secrets: because the enemy, although he flatter, attempts to deceive.

VERS. 10.-- Hast thou heard, etc. A foolish word does not move the fool and the wise man equally. For the fool disturbs the sense, so that he may express a groan of grief through an abominable act: but whatever shall have happened disturbs not the blow. 1203CBut he must beware who trusts in the firmness of his heart, lest he retain the memory of a hard word.

VERS. 15.-- Rebuke a friend. We ought to rebuke offending friends with all patience, because they sin sometimes through ignorance, sometimes through negligence, sometimes through disobedience and contempt, if perchance, being rebuked with discretion, they may do penance, and may have diligence and caution in the future: whence, Instruct such a one in the spirit of meekness (Gal. VI).

VERS. 16.-- There is that slippeth with his own tongue. There is a great difference between him who sins through simplicity or ignorance, and him who sins through malice. For it can happen that a man offend in a word which nevertheless 1203Dhe does not think to be evil; but he sins more who is full of guile and of wickedness.

VERS. 20.-- He is lessened in wisdom. In the understanding of the mysteries, yet not in the keeping of God's commandments. Whence there follows:

VERS. 21.-- Better is, etc. He, namely, who in the fear of God fulfils what he knows: whence, For he that knoweth the will of his lord and doth it not, shall be beaten with few stripes (Luc. XII) [Vulg. vapulabit multis, "shall be beaten with many stripes"]: for mercy is granted in small measure.

VERS. 22.-- There is a sure skilfulness, etc. He begins to give the difference between the saints and the hypocrites. (RAB.) So the heretics and the hypocrites show the appearance of virtues, but have not the truth of it: and when they shall have found a place for sinning, they show what they had in the heart.

1204AVERS. 26.-- By the look. (ID.) By outward tokens the affection of the mind is known, whence: A good tree is not that which bringeth forth evil fruits (Luc. VI), etc. And out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh (Matth. XII).

CAPUT XX.

VERS. 1.-- Quam bonum est. (RAB.) Prohibuit iracundiam in judicio, etc., usque ad et doctrina omnis per patientiam dignoscitur.

VERS. 2.-- Concupiscentia. Probat exemplo, quod bonum sit non irasci: quia sicut concupiscentia spadonis maculat virginem, nec generat sobolem: ita violentia iniqui simulat judicium, sed non servat aequitatis modum.

VERS. 5, 6.-- Est tacens. Hic laudatur modestus, et 1204Bculpatur temerarius. Est autem tacens, etc. Exponit quam taciturnitatem laudet. Alii enim tacent, quia nesciunt loqui: alii autem quia sciunt esse tempus tacendi, et tempus loquendi.

VERS. 7.-- Homo sapiens, etc. Prudentes moderantur sermones suos, et tempus servant: stultus vero totum spiritum suum profert.

VERS. 12.-- Est qui multa, etc. (RAB.) Qui scilicet per confessionem humilitatis ab omni reatu solvitur. Et restituens, etc. Quando in locum vitiorum subrogat multiplicem numerum virtutum: Ut ubi abundavit delictum, superabundet gratia (Rom. V).

VERS. 14.-- Oculi enim, etc. Id est, intentio, scilicet variis nequitiis maculata, semper ad nocendum parata. Septenarius enim universitatem significat.

1204CVERS. 16.-- Hodie foeneratur, etc. Instabilitatem haereticorum ostendit: quia sensum sui erroris violenter ab auditoribus extorquent, et ideo odibiles sunt Deo et hominibus. Vel, qui de eleemosyna in praesenti retributionem quaerit, odibilem se facit: unde, Voca pauperes et debiles, caecos, claudos, et beatus eris: quia non habent retribuere tibi. Retribuetur autem tibi in resurrectione justorum (Luc. XIV).

VERS. 17.-- Fatuo non erit amicus, etc. Quia laboris praemium in vita hac quaerit, vel haereticus qui gloriam humanam appetit.

VERS. 18.-- Quoties et quanti, etc. Detestabilis est coetus haereticorum, quia Scripturam Dei pravo sensu interpretantur, et expositionem suam inique 1204Dpraedicant.

VERS. 20.-- Lapsus falsae linguae, etc. Sicut in planitie pavimenti, ubi offendicula non sunt, frustra quis cadit: sic haereticorum sensus propria nequitia excaecatus, in semita veritatis quae aperta est, casum invenit.

VERS. 21.-- Homo acharis, etc. Sine gratia Spiritus sancti; vel amarus, quia amarum et noxium sensum ingerit auditoribus.

VERS. 22.-- Fatui reprobabitur parabola, etc. (RAB.) Judaei, qui adhuc putant, etc., usque ad haeretici, qui sophisticis argumentis nituntur evertere veritatem Evangelii.

VERS. 23.-- Est qui vetatur. Reprehensa falsitate 1205Ahaereticorum, intentionem pravae voluntatis vituperat. Sunt enim, qui cum non possunt actu peccare, nolunt in voluntate quiescere.

VERS. 24.-- Est qui perdet animam suam. Quia timens hujus mundi potestatem, non audet profiteri Evangelii veritatem: unde, Qui erubuerit me et meos sermones, hunc erubescet Filius hominis (Luc. IX).

VERS. 25.-- Est qui prae confusione, etc. Sunt qui plura promittunt, et pauca reddunt, et eos quibus mentiendo promittunt, inimicos sibi faciunt.

VERS. 27.-- Potior fur. Non excusat furem, sed ejus comparatione ingravat mentiendi assiduitatem. Mystice autem fures, sunt Judaei, quibus dicitur, Qui praedicas non furandum, furaris (Rom. II). Haeretici vero mendaces qui pejores sunt, quia post perceptam 1205Bbaptismi gratiam ad vomitum redierunt, et Spiritui gratiae contumeliam fecerunt.

VERS. 29.-- Sapiens in verbis, etc. Quia sapientiam mentis demonstrat in bene dictis: unde, Bonus homo de bono thesauro cordis sui profert bonum (Matth. XII).

VERS. 32.-- Sapientia absconsa. Nulla est utilitas thesauri, qui absconditus est et non scitur, et sapientiae sine discretione absconsae.

CHAPTER XX.

VERS. 1.-- How good it is. (RAB.) He has forbidden anger in judgment, etc., as far as and all teaching is discerned through patience.

VERS. 2.-- Lust. He proves by an example that it is good not to be angry: because, as the lust of a eunuch defiles a virgin, and begets no offspring: so the violence of the unjust man counterfeits judgment, but keeps not the measure of equity.

VERS. 5, 6.-- There is one that keepeth silence. Here the modest man is praised, and 1204Bthe rash man is blamed. But there is one that keepeth silence, etc. He sets forth what kind of silence he praises. For some are silent because they know not how to speak: but others because they know that there is a time of keeping silence, and a time of speaking.

VERS. 7.-- A wise man, etc. The prudent moderate their words, and observe the time: but the fool utters his whole spirit.

VERS. 12.-- There is that redeemeth many things, etc. (RAB.) He, namely, who by the confession of humility is loosed from all guilt. And restoring, etc. When in the place of vices he substitutes a manifold number of virtues: That where sin abounded, grace may more abound (Rom. V).

VERS. 14.-- For his eyes, etc. That is, the intention, namely, stained with divers wickednesses, always ready to do harm. For the septenary signifies universality.

1204CVERS. 16.-- To-day one lendeth, etc. He shows the instability of the heretics: because they violently wrest the meaning of their own error from their hearers, and therefore they are hateful to God and to men. Or, he who from an alms seeks a repayment in the present, makes himself hateful: whence, Call the poor and the weak, the blind, the lame, and thou shalt be blessed: because they have not wherewith to make thee recompense. But recompense shall be made thee at the resurrection of the just (Luc. XIV). [Vulg. voca pauperes, debiles, claudos, et caecos: et beatus eris, "call the poor, the weak, the lame, and the blind: and thou shalt be blessed"]

VERS. 17.-- The senseless man shall have no friend, etc. Because he seeks the reward of his labor in this life, or the heretic who covets human glory.

VERS. 18.-- How often and how many, etc. Detestable is the assembly of the heretics, because they interpret the Scripture of God in a depraved sense, and preach their own exposition wickedly 1204D.

VERS. 20.-- The slip of a false tongue, etc. As on the levelness of a pavement, where there are no stumbling-blocks, one falls without cause: so the understanding of the heretics, blinded by its own wickedness, finds a fall in the path of truth, which lies open.

VERS. 21.-- A man acharis, etc. Without the grace of the Holy Spirit; or bitter, because he instils into his hearers a bitter and harmful sense.

VERS. 22.-- The parable of the senseless man shall be rejected, etc. (RAB.) The Jews, who still think, etc., as far as the heretics, who by sophistical arguments strive to overturn the truth of the Gospel.

VERS. 23.-- There is that is hindered. The falsity 1205Aof the heretics being reproved, he blames the intention of a depraved will. For there are those who, when they cannot sin in act, are unwilling to rest in the will.

VERS. 24.-- There is that shall lose his own soul. Because, fearing the power of this world, he dares not profess the truth of the Gospel: whence, He that shall be ashamed of me and of my words, of him shall the Son of man be ashamed (Luc. IX).

VERS. 25.-- There is that for shame, etc. There are those who promise many things, and render few, and make enemies for themselves of those to whom they promise by lying.

VERS. 27.-- A thief is better. He does not excuse the thief, but by the comparison of him he aggravates the assiduity of lying. But mystically the thieves are the Jews, to whom it is said, Thou that preachest that men should not steal, stealest (Rom. II). But the lying heretics are worse, because after they received 1205Bthe grace of baptism they have returned to the vomit, and have done despite to the Spirit of grace.

VERS. 29.-- A wise man in words, etc. Because he shows the wisdom of the mind in things well said: whence, A good man out of the good treasure of his heart bringeth forth good (Matth. XII). [Vulg. de bono thesauro profert bona, "out of the good treasure bringeth forth good things"]

VERS. 32.-- Wisdom that is hid. There is no profit of a treasure which is hidden and is not known, nor of wisdom hidden without discretion.

CHAPTER XXI.

VERS. 1.-- Son, hast thou sinned, etc. (RAB.) He gives counsel, when we sin: For there is no man that doth good, and sinneth not (Eccle. VII), that we sin not again; but that we do penance for the things past, entreating that they may be forgiven us.

1205CVERS. 2.-- Of a serpent. (ID.) Of the devil, who always haunts the shadows of sin, and reigns in the children of darkness.

VERS. 3.-- The teeth of a lion, etc. Our adversary the devil, as a roaring lion, goeth about seeking whom he may devour (I Petr. V). [Vulg. adversarius vester, "your adversary"]

VERS. 5.-- Chiding and injuries, etc. (ID.) By concord the smallest things increase, etc., as far as because he abominates the truth of the faith and the unity of peace.

VERS. 6.-- The prayer of the poor, etc. Because God will judge the ungodly, who afflict the saints.

VERS. 7.-- Reproof. That is, the judgment of his own sin, because he has hardened his mind, that it should not obey the truth.

VERS. 8.-- Known afar off. Heretical presumption is made manifest in a rash tongue, which speaketh iniquity against 1205DGod.

VERS. 9.-- He that buildeth a house, etc. He signifies the heretic, who gathers testimonies of Scripture, estranged by a depraved understanding, for the confirmation of his own error. But by the vengeance of just retribution the hardness of that abominable action shall be dissolved; or tropologically: the greedy plunderer, who from the goods of others increases his riches, and, empty of all good things, shall be punished in the day of judgment for his iniquity.

VERS. 11.-- The way of them that sin. Therefore is the destruction of the wicked swift, because the way, that is, their life, is full of sorrows: and of the hard works of sins.

VERS. 14.-- He shall not be taught, etc. (RAB.) By these sentences he confounds, etc., as far as and he himself has mingled himself with endless questions.

1206AVERS. 18.-- It displeaseth him, etc. Whence: The children of this world are wiser in their generation than the children of light (Luc. XVI).

VERS. 22.-- Fetters on the feet, etc. (ID.) Because to their steps, wherewith they walk, the ruin of iniquity is contrary, it reproves their actions by condemning them.

VERS. 23.-- The senseless man in laughter, etc. The assembly of the heretics, teaching with plausible workmanship through the art of rhetoric, seeks the favor of its hearers. But the holy teachers so mingle pleasantness of speech with preaching, that they depart not from the track of truth.

VERS. 25.-- The foot of the senseless man, etc. Because the heretics study to imitate the sect of their own authors, lurking in the dwelling-place of their writings.

VERS. 26.-- The fool from the window. (ID.) He marks the craftiness of the heretics, 1206Band the simplicity of the catholics.

VERS. 30.-- While he curseth. Not because he calls the devil accursed, but because he ceases not to imitate him whom he knows to be accursed. For while he had rather imitate the devil than Christ, he is justly condemned.

VERS. 25.-- He that casteth a stone. He shows what preserves a good friend, or rather estranges him. He that casteth a stone. As by the stroke of a stone the flight of birds is brought down, so by a word of reviling the force of friendship is dissolved.

VERS. 26.-- Despair not. Concerning his friendship, because, the profit of correction being acknowledged, he returns to the right of the former friendship.

VERS. 27.-- A friend shall flee away. Not to return, nor after reconciliation; whence: To a friend reconciliatione, once reconciled, trust thou not for ever.

VERS. 29.-- In the time of tribulation. In adversity a friend is the more proved. For there are many friends of the table, 1207Band they remain not in the day of necessity.

VERS. 30.-- Before the fire, etc. As smoke goes before the burning of the fire, so hard words before murder; whence, A hard word stirreth up strifes (Prov. XV). And elsewhere: He that is angry with his brother shall be in danger of the judgment (Matth. V).

VERS. 31.-- To salute a friend. Many good things there are in true friendship: for a true friend rejoices with thee in thy good things, and is saddened in thy evils.

VERS. 32.-- Every one that shall hear. That is, he who knows how great good things are in true friendship, will beware lest he hurt his friend by injury or by reviling.

VERS. 2, 3.-- And in the midst of the people, etc. (RAB.) Because in the showing forth of miracles he stirred up the people to the praise of God. Of his power, etc. Of the angels, of the religious.

VERS. 5.-- I from the mouth. Because the Word is consubstantial, coeternal. Because I am not a creature. Yet he is called the firstborn of every creature, not, that is, in time, but in honour (Exod. IV).

1208CVERS. 6.-- I made in the heavens. (RAB.) In the heavens light arises, by a cloud flesh is covered, because as by its own brightness it surpasses the darkness of a cloud, so the spiritual creature surpasses the bodily in dignity.

VERS. 7.-- My throne. (ID.) The human body weighed down by no weight of sin, but raised on high by the power of God, in which the might of the Godhead exercised its judgments.

VERS. 12.-- He created me, etc. According to the humanity conceived by a singular purity of the Holy Spirit, brought forth of the Virgin.

VERS. 13.-- In Jacob. By the incarnation of the Saviour it was granted to the Jews in a special manner to have the use of him, and he came especially to the Jews. Whence by Jacob the Jews are figured; 1208Dbut by Israel, the Gentiles.

VERS. 14.-- From the beginning. The mystery of the incarnation, foreknown and predestined before the ages, remains unto the age of the age. And he disposed the dwellings of the Church, that is, the rite of the true religion.

VERS. 15.-- And so in Sion. (RAB.) Sion is the Church, which is also Jerusalem, which is built from the first just man unto the last, and in which wisdom daily bears fruit.

VERS. 17.-- In Lebanon. (RAB.) Lebanon is a mountain of Phoenicia, etc., as far as expressing the constancy and the action of those who adorn the Church with the loftier virtues.

VERS. 18.-- As a palm tree. A tree long-lasting in its leaves, and with sweet fruits; it is called palma because it is the adornment of a victorious 1209Ahand: or because it spreads out its branch after the manner of a human palm. This the Greeks call φοινίκον, because the phoenix, a bird, lives a long time. To this the Church is likened, which, despising to be bowed down to earthly things, raises itself up to, heavenly things: that when the contest is finished it may receive the heavenly palm, and, like long-lasting leaves, keeps perpetually the words of right confession.

VERS. 19.-- As an olive tree. In Greek ἐλαἴον, that is, the fruit: for the tree is called ἐλαία, the juice oil. And it is a tree notable for peace: by it the Church is signified, which lights up the fields of the whole world with comeliness, and sanctifies them by a special grace through the anointing of chrism. As a plane tree. The plane tree (platanus) is so called from its height, or because it is spreading and ample. For the Greeks call what is ample πλατός. He expressed, therefore, the name and the form of the tree 1209Bin saying: I am spread abroad as a plane tree in the streets. [Vulg. quasi platanus exaltata sum juxta aquam in plateis, "exalted... beside the water"] And it is most tender, and soft in its leaves, and like to vines, and it signifies Christ, who through his own members is spread abroad in the world, mild and meek.

VERS. 20.-- As cinnamon. Cinnamon and balsam are aromatic trees. Cinnamon is so called because its bark is round and slender after the manner of a reed (canna). It is produced in India and Ethiopia, in a short shrub of only two cubits, of a blackish or ashen colour, of the very slenderest twigs. For what is thick is despised; but the slenderer is the dearer: and when it is broken, it sends out a visible breath after the manner of a cloud or of dust.

VERS. 21.-- Balsam. (RAB.) Pliny says aspaltum; it is born in the same course of heaven, etc., 1209Cas far as mindful of the Lord's passion they are ready to suffer for the Lord. As myrrh. Myrrh is in Arabia a tree five cubits high, like to a thorn, which the Greeks call ἄκανθον, whose drop is green and bitter, whence also it received its name: all which things agree with the mortification of the flesh, which is felt bitter for a time, but is willingly undertaken in hope of the ever-green fatherland. And it is likened to a thorn, because to save the mind it afflicts the body. CHAPTER XXV.

VERS. 2.-- Man and woman. (RAB.) Whence Paul, Husbands, love your wives, as Christ also the Church (Eph. V). We can take the spirit in the man, the soul in the woman, and if these consent together in the fear and love of God, whatsoever they shall ask shall be done for them, whence: If two of you shall consent, it shall be done to them (Matth. VERS. 3.-- Three kinds. (ID.) These things can be transferred to the devil, etc., as far as because he would not be subject to his Maker.

VERS. 6.-- How comely. (ID.) The hoary head is comely, etc., as far as it is not lawful for them to be ignorant of the knowledge and the wisdom of the law.

VERS. 9.-- Nine things unsuspected, etc. With the heart we believe unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation (Rom. X). Nine, therefore, are the things unsuspected, that is, foreign to all suspicion of falsehood, which right faith brings forth for a man.

VERS. 13.-- Wisdom. Which is to beware of evil. Whence, And behold the fear of the Lord, that itself is wisdom, and to depart from evil, understanding (Job. XXVIII). [Vulg. ipsa est sapientia … intelligentia; Migne prints masc. ipse and acc. intelligentiam] Hence it is that one is added over and above to the kinds of the virtues.

1211CVERS. 16.-- The fear of God. (RAB.) Because as the number nine is imperfect, etc., as far as but lest the presence of God forsake thee, which thou dost embrace, and desirest to enjoy for ever.

VERS. 17.-- Every wound. There is no greater grace than the true gladness of the mind, nor a greater wound than the sadness of a deceitful heart in idolatry or in heresy, because in its wickedness is all malice.

VERS. 18.-- And every wound. He sees the wound of the heart who understands the injury of his own depravity, but he does not see it who does not correct it by penance.

VERS. 22.-- There is no head, etc. (RAB.) The wickedness of heretics compared, etc., as far as therefore its dwelling together is hurtful to all.

VERS. 26.-- Every malice is short. Small is every 1211Dmalice in comparison with idolatry and with heretical depravity, because they sin not through the frailty of the flesh, but through the pride of the mind.

VERS. 30.-- If a woman have the pre-eminence. Historically he shows how hurtful is the pre-eminence of a woman, etc., as far as she will be contrary to the man, that is, to the catholic teacher, by thinking perversely and by teaching depraved things.

VERS. 1.-- Of a good woman. (RAB.) As before he blamed the wicked woman, etc., as far as and in the time to come she shall rejoice in the receiving of rewards.

VERS. 4.-- But of the rich man. Rich or poor, coming with a devout mind to the grace of Christ, shall enjoy perpetually the delight 1212Aof the vision of God, because God is no respecter of persons.

VERS. 5.-- Three things. By these likenesses he calls those who are separated from the unity of the true religion, that is, Jews, pagans, heretics, and false Christians.

But mystically it signifies the heretic, who in pride of understanding and in eloquence of words boasts of his fornication, that he may the more easily draw to himself the lovers of novelties, itching in their ears, who turn away their hearing from the truth and are turned unto fables.

VERS. 25.-- In two things. (RAB.) Wisdom marks, etc., as far as or, having forsaken the path of truth, he follows the by-ways of error.

VERS. 28.-- He is hardly stripped. (RAB.) The worst act, not an honest thing, is condemned. That trader is to be abominated who through immoderate ambition loads his wares 1212Dmore with perjury than with price.

VERS. 7.-- As the dressing of a tree. (ID.) Every single tree, etc., as far as the tilling of a tree therefore makes manifest in the fruit the quality of it.

VERS. 15.-- Speech, etc. Because as their action is contrary to the good: so also their speech is prone to the saying of evil things, burdensome to the hearing.

VERS. 17.-- He that discloseth. He shows wherein friendship is most of all wounded, namely in the evil of betrayal, which loses faith, namely, nor keeps the equity of friendship, and therefore breaks up charity.

These, if at any time they have perceived beforehand the hostility of wild beasts or of men, casting themselves down from the high peaks, catch themselves unhurt by their own horns in the branches of the trees.

VERS. 8.-- Memorare. (RAB.) Qui Deum timet, iram vel furorem compescit, et non obliviscitur testamenti Dei, qui ait: Dimittite et dimittetur vobis (Luc. IX).

CHAPTER XXVIII.

VERS. 8.-- Remember. (RAB.) He who fears God restrains wrath or fury, and does not forget the testament of God, who says: Forgive, and it shall be forgiven you (Luc. IX).

VERS. 13.-- Contention. An indiscreet action: whence, Be not over-much just, neither be more wise than is necessary, lest thou be stupefied. Immoderate discipline therefore stirs up the heat of fury, and forward speech the shedding of blood.

VERS. 19.-- The third tongue. Heretical doctrine, which agrees neither with the Old nor with the New Testament, and 1213Dtroubles the hearts of the hearers, and passing from nation to nation, stirs up sedition.

VERS. 21.-- But the wound. Worse is the destruction of the soul, which heretics slay, than any torment whatever of the body.

Blessed is he that is covered. Blessed is he who does not consent to heretics, and whose heart their eloquence does not overturn, because as he is a stranger to their malice, so he is separated from their punishment.

VERS. 28.-- Hedge in thy ears. After he had rooted out the evil of the tongue, he turns himself to the instruction of the hearer.

CHAPTER XXIX.

VERS. 5.-- Until they receive. (RAB.) A sickness of this kind the fear of God better cures. For he who 1214Adreads God as though always present intends to defraud no one.

VERS. 10.-- To many, etc. (ID.) He marks those who wish to injure no one, but through fear of things that will perish are unwilling to do good. Against whom it is said: Lay not up to yourselves treasures on earth (Matth. VI).

VERS. 12.-- For the commandment's sake. Mercy is to be shown to all who are in need, nor is the charity of one's neighbour to be lost for covetousness.

VERS. 13.-- And hide not. For he who shall have seen his brother to be in need, and shall have shut up his bowels from him, how doth the charity of God abide in him?

VERS. 16, 17, 18.-- Above the shield of the mighty, etc. Alms avail more against the darts of the enemy than 1214Bearthly arms: because these bring forth a doubtful, those a most certain victory, that death may be swallowed up in victory.

VERS. 23.-- He that shall lose, etc. That is, he who does not keep the unity of faith and the sincerity of the law, but is entangled in divers errors.

VERS. 20.-- The grace of the surety, etc. The teacher, namely, who promises eternal life for faith and good works. This sentence rebukes Pelagius, who sets free will before grace, whereas the elect are set free by grace alone.

VERS. 27.-- Recover thy neighbour. He counsels that according to thy strength thou shouldst afford comfort to thy neighbour, and so provide for thyself, lest thou fall into a snare.

VERS. 28.-- The beginning of life, etc. (RAB.) He teaches frailty, and hospitality, and honesty, etc., 1214Cas far as and the Lord made coats of skins for our first parents after sin.

VERS. 29.-- Better is the fare of the poor man, etc. To the letter. It is better to take a slender fare beneath a mean hut in safety, than to seek splendid banquets with weariness in uncertain places. (ID.) Allegorically. Better is the fare, etc., as far as where there is labour and superfluous care.

VERS. 30.-- The least thing. (ID.) Let what thou hast suffice thee, etc., as far as a good name is better than many delights.

VERS. 33.-- Pass over. As if: Thou who takest delight in another's lodging, return to thine own, and of thine own labour feed thyself and others. Seek not for thyself superfluous food from another's house. The receiving of thy neighbours sufficeth thee.

1214DVERS. 35.-- A reproach. (RAB.) But mystically the ecclesiastical man is admonished, etc., as far as but that in the judgment to come he may bear the everlasting reproach of confusion.

CAPUT XXX.

VERS. 1.-- Qui diligit filium. (RAB.) Patres tam carnales quam spirituales monet, ut filios suos diligenter erudiant, ut honeste vivant, ne lasciviendo per aliorum dogmata discurrant.

VERS. 19.-- Seth and Sem, etc. Seth the son of Adam, and Sem the firstborn of Noe, were preferred, as well in faith as in manners, before the men of their own time; in whom Christ is signified, who is God and man.

CAPUT L.

VERS. 1.-- Simon Oniae filius. (RAB.) Ad homines in tempore suo pervenit, etc., usque ad cum vitam Simonis et mores tam expresse describat Jesus.

VERS. 3.-- Putei aquarum. Profunditates Scripturarum, quae in divinis libris sub figuris latent, 1229Csed ore praedicatorum redundant, quasi mare ad saturitatem plurimorum.

VERS. 5.-- Et ingressum domus atrii amplificavit. Quia in rudibus, quos signat atrium; et in perfectis, qui signantur per domum, magistrorum excrescit meritum.

VERS. 11.-- In accipiendo ipsum stolam, etc. Ordo praedicatorum accipiendo stolam gloriae, et vestiendo se consummatione virtutum, judicium dignitatis suae, et ornatum fidei, et vestitum bonae operationis demonstrat. Haec enim debent in sacerdotibus apparere, ut digne possint docere.

VERS. 13.-- In accipiendo autem partes de manu sacerdotis. Partes accipit de manu sacerdotis, qui bonae operationis actum exigit a ministris.

VERS. 15.-- Et consummatione fungens in ara. Consummata oblatio datur excelso Regi, quando corpus Christi offertur Deo.

VERS. 17.-- Effudit in fundamento. In commemorationem Christi, qui est fundamentum Ecclesiae, hujus oblationis est odor suavissimus Deo omnipotenti.

VERS. 18.-- Tunc exclamaverunt filii Aaron. Christi sacerdotes praedicationem exhibuerunt per passionem consummatam. Ductiles tubae bene dicuntur, quia praedicatores tanto crescunt quanto tribulationum malleis percutiuntur.

VERS. 19.-- Tunc omnis populus, etc. Cum officium ecclesiasticum per ministros altaris agitur in 1230Amemoriam Christi, populus, ad laudem Dei provocatus, prosternit se in faciem, id est, cordis sui exhibet humilitatem. Dare preces Omnipotenti. Quia quidquid amore Dei agitur, coram Deo est oratio et odor suavitatis; quia omnis Ecclesia satagit ut perseveret in prece usque ad finem mundi, quando corpus Christi in incorruptione honorabitur, et membra capiti suo conjungentur.

VERS. 21.-- Et rogavit populus, etc. (RAB.) Quia quidquid in unitate fidei Deo offertur, apud eum recipitur; quidquid extra, impietati deputatur. Magnus fit in Ecclesia sonus, quando orantibus sacerdotibus et benedicentibus respondet omnis populus: Amen: qui sonus tam ad clamorem vocis quam ad devotionem cordis pertinet.

1230BVERS. 22.-- Tunc descendens, etc. Coetus sanctorum exsultans. Extulit. Quando gratias agit pro perfectione laboris in salute universi populi, tunc laudes multiplicat, orationem iterat, quia nunquam a laudibus cessat.

VERS. 23.-- Ostendere virtutem Dei. Praedicare scilicet misericordiam ejus, qui dat nobis jucunditatem cordis, et pacem in temporibus aeternis, ut omnes viri Israelitae, et amatores Regis aeterni, praedicent potentiam et bonitatem Dei in liberatione populi sui.

VERS. 27.-- Duas gentes, etc. Ruina duplex, gentilitas scilicet, quae idololatria polluta est et conversatione vana.

VERS. 29.-- Doctrinam sapientiae, etc. Opus suum 1230Ccommendat, ostendens quam utile sit legenti, meditanti et operanti.

CHAPTER L.

VERS. 1.-- Simon the son of Onias. (RAB.) He came to men in his own time, etc., as far as since Jesus describes so expressly the life and the manners of Simon.

VERS. 3.-- The wells of waters. The depths of the Scriptures, which lie hid under figures in the divine books, 1229Cbut overflow from the mouth of the preachers, as it were a sea, to the satisfying of very many.

VERS. 5.-- And he enlarged the entrance of the house of the court. [Vulg. ingressum domus et atrii, "the entrance of the house and of the court"] Because in the rude, whom the court signifies, and in the perfect, who are signified by the house, the merit of the masters increases.

VERS. 11.-- In his receiving the robe, etc. The order of preachers, in receiving the robe of glory, and in clothing itself with the consummation of the virtues, shows the judgment of its own dignity, and the adornment of faith, and the vesture of good working. For these things ought to appear in priests, that they may be able worthily to teach.

VERS. 13.-- And in receiving the portions from the hand of the priest. [Vulg. de manu sacerdotum, "from the hand of the priests," plural] He receives the portions from the hand of the priest, who exacts from his ministers the act of good working.

VERS. 15.-- And performing the consummation at the altar. The consummated oblation is given to the most high King, when the body of Christ is offered to God.

VERS. 17.-- He poured out at the foundation. In commemoration of Christ, who is the foundation of the Church, the odour of this oblation is most sweet to God almighty.

VERS. 18.-- Then the sons of Aaron cried out. The priests of Christ set forth their preaching through a consummated passion. Well are the trumpets called beaten out, because preachers grow the more, the more they are struck with the hammers of tribulations.

VERS. 19.-- Then all the people, etc. When the ecclesiastical office is performed by the ministers of the altar in 1230Amemory of Christ, the people, stirred up to the praise of God, prostrates itself upon its face, that is, shows the humility of its heart. To give prayers to the Almighty. Because whatsoever is done for the love of God is before God a prayer and an odour of sweetness; because the whole Church is busied that it may persevere in prayer until the end of the world, when the body of Christ shall be honoured in incorruption, and the members shall be joined to their own head.

VERS. 21.-- And the people besought, etc. (RAB.) Because whatsoever is offered to God in the unity of faith is received with him; whatsoever is outside it, is reckoned to impiety. A great sound is made in the Church, when to the priests praying and blessing all the people answers: Amen: which sound pertains as well to the cry of the voice as to the devotion of the heart.

1230BVERS. 22.-- Then coming down, etc. The assembly of the saints rejoicing. He lifted up. When it gives thanks for the perfection of the labour in the salvation of the whole people, then it multiplies praises, repeats prayer, because it never ceases from praises.

VERS. 23.-- To show the power of God. To preach, namely, his mercy, who gives us joy of heart, and peace in eternal times, that all the men of Israel, and the lovers of the eternal King, may preach the power and the goodness of God in the deliverance of his people.

VERS. 27.-- Two nations, etc. A twofold ruin, namely, heathenism, which is polluted with idolatry and with vain conversation.

VERS. 29.-- The doctrine of wisdom, etc. He commends his own work, 1230Cshowing how profitable it is to him that reads, that meditates, and that works.

CAPUT LI.

VERS. 1.-- Oratio Jesu filii Sirach. Melius ad totam Ecclesiam refertur, quam ad unam personam, quia in ea multae species tribulationum et diversa solatia describuntur.

Confitebor tibi, Domine rex. (RAB.) Jesus Christus adjutor et protector est sponsae suae, qui tradidit semetipsum pro ea, ut liberaret eam a perditione originalis peccati.

VERS. 5.-- Portis tribulationum. Haec sunt ora principum et tyrannorum, unde minae prodeunt et fraudulentiae, quae servos Dei negare fidem compellunt.

1230DVERS. 6.-- A pressura flammae. Hoc tribus pueris contigit in camino ignis corporaliter; sed omnes sancti ab aestu persecutionis liberantur spiritualiter.

VERS. 9.-- Et vita mea appropinquans erat inferno. Quia vita carnis quotidie deficiendo ad mortem tendit. Mortem enim infernum vocat, quia mors carnis, primi peccati vindicta est, sicut infernus peccantium animarum poena perpetua.

VERS. 10.-- Circumdederunt me undique, etc. Videns Ecclesia se ab hostibus circumdari, et humanum deesse solatium, a Deo quaerit adjutorium, a quo est omne bonum, cui attribuit quod liberatur ab inimicis.

1231AVERS. 18.-- Cum adhuc junior essem. (RAB.) In juventute, vel pueritia, etc., usque ad quomodo ab infantia quaesierit sapientiam.

Priusquam oberrarem, etc. Signat se ante errorem pueritiae vel juventutis desiderasse sapientiam, et a Domino postulasse, et usque ad novissimum perseverasse.

VERS. 19.-- Tanquam praecox uva. Decorem sapientiae comparat praecoquae uvae, ut ostendat jucunditatem apud eam esse. Praecoqua enim uva dicitur eo quod cito maturescat.

VERS. 24.-- Consiliatus sum, etc. (RAB.) Prius dicit se consilium inisse ut faceret illam, etc., usque 1232Aad sic enim confirmatus, perveniet ad fructum sapientiae.

VERS. 29.-- Venter meus. Commotus ad poenitentiam per sapientiae notitiam. Propterea. Ad perfectionem sapientiae pervenire non est humani studii, sed divini muneris.

VERS. 30.-- Dedit mihi Dominus, etc. Bona merces est lingua erudita, quae fecit fratres instruere et corrigere.

VERS. 37.-- Laetetur anima. Magna est jucunditas cordis, si recordatur misericordiam superni judicis.

CHAPTER LI.

VERS. 1.-- The prayer of Jesus the son of Sirach. It is better referred to the whole Church than to one person, because in it many kinds of tribulations and divers consolations are described.

I will give glory to thee, O Lord the king. (RAB.) Jesus Christ is the helper and protector of his spouse, who delivered himself up for her, that he might free her from the perdition of original sin.

VERS. 5.-- The gates of tribulations. These are the mouths of princes and of tyrants, whence come forth threats and deceits, which compel the servants of God to deny the faith.

1230DVERS. 6.-- From the pressure of the flame. This befell the three children bodily in the furnace of fire; but all the saints are delivered spiritually from the heat of persecution.

VERS. 9.-- And my life was drawing near to hell. Because the life of the flesh, daily failing, tends toward death. For he calls death hell, because the death of the flesh is the vengeance of the first sin, as hell is the perpetual punishment of sinning souls.

VERS. 10.-- They compassed me about on every side, etc. The Church, seeing herself to be compassed about by enemies, and human consolation to be wanting, seeks help from God, from whom is every good, to whom she ascribes it that she is delivered from her enemies.

1231AVERS. 18.-- When I was yet younger. (RAB.) In youth, or in boyhood, etc., as far as how from infancy he sought wisdom.

Before I wandered, etc. He signifies that before the error of boyhood or of youth he desired wisdom, and asked it of the Lord, and persevered even to the last.

VERS. 19.-- As an early grape. He compares the comeliness of wisdom to an early grape, that he may show that delight is with her. For a grape is called early because it ripens quickly.

VERS. 24.-- I purposed, etc. (RAB.) First he says that he entered into counsel that he might do it, etc., as far 1232Aas for being thus confirmed, he shall come to the fruit of wisdom.

VERS. 29.-- My belly. Moved to repentance through the knowledge of wisdom. Therefore. To come to the perfection of wisdom is not of human study, but of a divine gift.

VERS. 30.-- The Lord hath given me, etc. A good reward is a learned tongue, which made [him] instruct and correct the brethren.

VERS. 37.-- Let the soul rejoice. Great is the joy of the heart, if it remembers the mercy of the judge on high.
