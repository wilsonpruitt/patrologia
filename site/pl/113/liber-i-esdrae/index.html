Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

(BEDA in Esdram, tom. II.) As Cyrus, when the empire of the Chaldeans was destroyed, set the people of God free, etc., as far as to whom he preaches the faith of his own name and the hope of salvation, to all who belong to his kingdom, that is, to the elect.

VERS. 2.-- And he himself hath charged me that I should build him a house, etc. (BEDA ubi supra. ) The house or temple of God in the holy Scriptures, etc., as far as and that they may alike await in time to come the solemnities of its dedication.

VERS. 3.-- Who is there among you. (ID., ibid.) Great is the faith of the king in 692Dthese words, etc., as far as and that he might direct their minds and their hands to works that bring salvation.

Let him go up to Jerusalem. (ID., ibid.) They who sin, or who are busied about worldly things, are in the depth: let those who desire to please God sigh after heavenly things, let them pass beyond all the pomps and allurements of the world by the love of things eternal, and let them build a house for God in Judea, that is, in the confession of their own iniquity and of the divine compassion, let them prepare their hearts, which God may vouchsafe to inhabit and to enlighten, and let them strive to invite their neighbours to the same thing by words and by examples.

693AVERS. 4.-- And all. (BEDA., ibid.) The distinction of the words is to be noted, etc., as far as whence John: We therefore ought to receive such, that we may be fellow-workers of the truth, etc. (III Joan. VIII.)

Let them help, etc. They who abound in temporal things ought not only to minister the necessaries of life to the poor of Christ, but also to work willingly for themselves such good as they can, that by both gains they may deserve to have a part in the temple of God, which is the Church.

VERS. 5.-- And they rose up. (BEDA, ibid.) Cyrus commanded all things, nay rather permitted them, to go up to Jerusalem and to build the house of the Lord, etc., as far as and therefore he deserved to be a partaker of the mercy bestowed.

The princes. (ID., ibid.) For it belongs to the princes of the fathers, that is, to the masters, to build up by work and by teaching the minds of the erring 693Bin the pursuit of good work. Scribes learned in the kingdom of heaven, who, skilfully examining the merits and the understandings of their hearers, know how to judge whom they are to promote and in what grade of ecclesiastical ministry.

That they might go up. (ID., ibid.) By daily advances, as though by certain steps, a man ascends to the height of the virtues, which lie in the vision of eternal peace. The first steps are to correct one's own life. The second, to labour over the errors of one's neighbours. The highest, after good works and teaching, to await the joys of perpetual reward.

VERS. 6.-- They helped their hands with vessels. The hands of those who build are the associates, who, not being able to build, help with monies given, when men of the world commit their children or their household to the preachers to be 693Cbrought up for the Lord, so that what they cannot do of themselves, they may through those who can offer to the Lord as the gift of their devotion.

And with beasts. (BED., ibid.) That is, with the slower sort, etc., as far as and that they may be assigned to the princes of the fathers as it were manifold furniture for the building of the house of the Lord.

And he brought forth. (ID., ibid.) The diverse kinds of vessels, etc., as far as he keeps written in the book of eternal memory, in figure of whom it is fitly subjoined: And he numbered them, etc.

VERS. 9.-- And this is. (ID., ibid.) Because the Lord knoweth the number of the elect, etc., as far as that before they repent they may be snatched away to everlasting punishment.

CAPUT II.

693DVERS. 1.-- Hi sunt, etc. Judaeae scilicet non Babyloniae. Ad hanc enim pertinebant etiam de captivorum stirpe in Babylonia lati. Toto enim animo Judaeam et Jerusalem suspirabant, quorum figuram gerit dux eorum Zorobabel, qui nomine ipso se ortum in Babylone demonstrat, sed intentione et opere civem Jerusalem. Altiori sensu filii Ecclesiae, filii sunt patriae coelestis, non solum qui Ecclesiae sacramentis jam sunt imbuti, sed etiam qui foris, id est inter impios aliquando aberrantes divina electione ante saecula praeordinati ad vitam divinae gratiae ministeriis sunt consecrandi.

Et reversi sunt in Jerusalem. (BEDA in Esdram, tom. II.) Cum enim liberati de potestate Satanae, etc., 694Ausque ad visio autem pacis et confessio in praesenti inchoatur, et in futuro perficitur.

Unusquisque in civitatem. (ID., ibid.) Ideo vigilanter Scriptura distinxit de qua generatione captivorum soluti in patriam redierint, etc., usque ad pro quibus certa mercede remuneret.

VERS. 2.-- Numerus, etc. (ID., ibid.) Populum Israel tribum Juda et Benjamin dicit, etc., usque ad una cum sacerdotibus et Levitis qui inter eos sortem habuerant, debere intelligi.

VERS. 58.-- Nathinaei filii, etc. (ID., ibid.) Nathinaei qui post sacerdotes, Levitas, cantores, et janitores in ordine describuntur, ipsi tunc fuere qui nunc in Ecclesia subdiaconi vocantur, obedientes officiis Levitarum, et oblationes in templo suscipientes 694Ba populo.

Omnes Nathinaei. (ID., ibid.) Hoc loco etiam secundum litteram gratia Dei ostenditur, qua etiam in Veteri Testamento gentiles ad salutem recipiebantur, dum exposito catalogo eorum quos veraciter ad populum Dei pertinere constabat adjuncti sunt, qui utrum ex Israel, an ex proselytis essent, ignorabatur: eorumque simul inter filios Israel numerus exponitur: etsi enim longa separatione patrum suorum a templo vel populo Dei quomodo ad eum pertinerent indicare nequierant: qui tamen data generali licentia de captivitate ascenderant, ut socii recipiebantur; gaudebant etenim caeteri Israelitae eos ob amorem communis fidei et religionis habere quasi fratres et notos, etsi in origine carnis minus 694Ccognoscebant eos.

Allegorice autem inter poenitentes qui liberati a captivitate vitiorum ad aedificandam vel in se, vel in aliis domum Domini ascendunt, sunt nonnulli qui plurimis gravioribus peccatis astricti, qui a Deo ab omni actu pietatis et castitatis videntur alienati, ut nihili prorsus bonitatis et religionis quam a doctoribus acceperant, in eis remansisse videatur, nec possunt indicare utrum ex Israel, id est ex sancta Ecclesia fuerint generati, quia tales peccando facti sunt, quasi nunquam pertinuerint ad Spiritum sanctum, poenitendo tamen corriguntur et convertuntur, ut numerus eorum inter veros Israelitas in quibus dolus non est, conscribatur in coelis.

VERS. 61.-- Et de filiis sacerdotum. (BED., ibid.) 694DEadem cautela filii transmigrationis erga sacerdotes agunt, qua erga populum. Curabant enim, ut distincte patesceret qui vere ad populum Israel vel ad genus sacerdotale pertinerent, qui aut suspecti aut certa proselytorum, id est, advenarum essent stirpe procreati. Sacerdotes ergo suspectos ab altaris moverunt officio, usque dum certius eorum origo claresceret, sed nihilominus in societate transmigrantium, unanimi secum pace servaverunt.

Mystice autem quaerunt filii sacerdotum de captivitate Babylonica ascendentes scripturam genealogiae suae, nec invenientes, de sacerdotio ejiciuntur cum ministri altaris in tanta flagitia vel nefanda dogmata decidunt, ut etsi poenitendo ad salutem 695Aanimae redeant, non tamen digni sunt qui ad sacrum gradum restituantur. Etsi enim inter fideles vitam exspectent aeternam, non tamen scripturam gradus sui, quem repetere nequeunt inter perfectos sacerdotes inveniunt.

Omnis multitudo quasi vir unus, etc. (BED., ibid.) Hi eos significant qui profectu emendatioris vitae vitia superare, et virtutum culmen ascendere satagunt, nondum tamen sibi ad providendam viam regularis vitae sufficiunt, sed eorum qui in Christo praecesserunt industria coercentur et diriguntur.

Et in ipsis. (BED., ibid.) Mystice, in templo vel populo Dei sunt cantores, etc., usque ad et quasi suavitate suae vocis adjuvant aedificantes templum.

VERS. 66.-- Muli eorum. (ID., ibid.) Mulus ex 695Basino et equa gignitur, mala ex equo et asina. Inter homines qui de captivitate ascenderant etiam animalia quibus adjuvabantur, describuntur. Et eorum sicut hominum numerus designatur, quia sunt multi in Ecclesia vel sensu tardiores, vel etiam carnales, qui tamen magistris spiritualibus devote obtemperant, et ad portanda onera fraternae necessitatis, dorsum mentis inclinant, et cum caeteris electis de confusione diabolicae captivitatis erepti, ad supernae civitatis moenia tendunt, quorum etiam numerus memoria Dei integer conservatur; unde: Imperfectum meum viderunt oculi tui (Psal. CXXXVIII). Et alibi: Homines et jumenta salvabis Domino (Ibid. XXXV).

VERS. 68.-- Sponte obtulerunt in domum Dei ad, 695Cetc. (ID., ibid.) Magnae devotionis indicium est, etc., usque ad quanto eos nec licitis uti considerant.

VERS. 69.-- Secundum vires. Haec est enim mensura perfectionis, pro posse suo quemque pro Domino laborare, et statum Ecclesiae primo in se, deinde in proximis confirmare. Qui taliter conversantur, recte principes patrum vocantur, quia perfectionem vitae et doctrinae illis qui in Ecclesia per sollicitudinis studium patres vocantur altius vivendo principantur.

Auri solidos. (BED., ibid.) Aurum et argentum et vestes sacerdotales, etc., usque ad Quo enim gravius se errasse meminerunt, eo ardentius bonis operibus insistunt.

VERS. 70.-- Habitaverunt ergo sacerdotes et 695DLevitae. Levita a Chaldaeis captivatus civitatem recipit, cum diaconus virtutem perfectionis quam a diabolo seductus perdiderat Domino donante reintegrat. In Babylone vero natus civitatem in Judaeam veniens ingreditur, cum regeneratus in Christo et bene institutus in Ecclesia acceptum semel diaconatum regulariter custodit. Sicut enim ille per Christi gratiam quasi de Chaldaea servitute liberatus est, ita hic per exsecutionem bonorum, in quibus praedecessores ejusdem gradus conversati sunt, velut ad moenia propriae virtutis vel civitatis ascendunt.

Universusque Israel. (BEDA ubi supra. ) Universum Israel, non decem tribus tantum dicit, quae quondam vocabantur Israel ad distinctionem duarum quae 696Avocabantur Juda, etc., usque ad de quibus sibi promittunt Judaei, quod regnante Christo suo, cum caeteris suae gentis hominibus sint in Judaeam reversuri, et in toto orbe regnaturi.

Allegorice sacerdotes, Levitae, cantores, janitores, et Nathinaei, omnisque Israel in civitatibus suis habitant, cum ministri altaris et doctores et plebs Christiana in suis gradibus Deo fideliter serviunt, sive qui eodem gradu aliquando vitiis sordidato vel perdito, per poenitentiam correpti sunt, sive qui nuper de regno diaboli ad Ecclesiam venientes bonum sibi gradum acquisierunt. Suas enim civitates introierunt Levitae vel filii Israel, non solum qui eas captivati amiserant, sed etiam qui de captivis geniti ad ipsas se paterna successione pertinere didicerant. 696BQuicunque vero ita captivati sunt, ut nunquam reversi aliis fundos suos possidendos reliquerunt, illos significant, qui sic peccando ab Ecclesia discedunt, ut nunquam resipiscant, et aliis promissa sibi praemia relinquant; unde: Memor esto unde excideris, et age poenitentiam, et prima opera fac: sin autem venio tibi, et movebo candelabrum tuum, etc. (Apoc. II); et alibi: Tene quod habes, ut nemo accipiat coronam tuam (Ibid. III).

CHAPTER II.

693DVERS. 1.-- These are, etc. Of Judea, that is, not of Babylonia. For to her belonged also those brought forth in Babylonia of the stock of the captives. For with their whole soul they sighed after Judea and Jerusalem, whose figure their leader Zorobabel bears, who by his very name shows himself born in Babylon, but by his intention and his work a citizen of Jerusalem. In a higher sense the children of the Church are children of the heavenly country, not only they who are already imbued with the sacraments of the Church, but also they who are without, that is, straying for a time among the ungodly, foreordained to life by divine election before the ages, and to be consecrated by the ministries of divine grace.

And they returned into Jerusalem. (BEDA in Esdram, tom. II.) For when, delivered from the power of Satan, etc., 694Aas far as but the vision of peace and confession is begun in the present and perfected in the time to come.

Every man into his city. (ID., ibid.) Therefore Scripture watchfully distinguished from what generation of the captives they returned, loosed, into their own country, etc., as far as for which he may reward them with a sure recompense.

VERS. 2.-- The number, etc. (ID., ibid.) By the people of Israel he means the tribe of Juda and of Benjamin, etc., as far as is to be understood together with the priests and the Levites who had had their lot among them.

VERS. 58.-- The Nathinaei, the sons, etc. (ID., ibid.) The Nathinaei, who are described in order after the priests, the Levites, the singers and the porters, were then the very men who are now called subdeacons in the Church, obeying the offices of the Levites and receiving the oblations in the temple 694Bfrom the people.

All the Nathinaei. (ID., ibid.) In this place too, according to the letter, the grace of God is shown, by which even in the Old Testament Gentiles were received unto salvation, since, when the catalogue of those who were known truly to belong to the people of God had been set out, there were joined to them men of whom it was not known whether they were of Israel or of the proselytes: and their number too is set out among the children of Israel: for although, by the long separation of their fathers from the temple or from the people of God, they had not been able to show in what way they belonged to him: yet, having gone up out of the captivity when general leave was given, they were received as companions; for the rest of the Israelites rejoiced to have them, for the love of the common faith and religion, as brethren and acquaintances, although in the origin of the flesh they knew them 694Cless.

But allegorically, among the penitents who, delivered from the captivity of the vices, go up to build the house of the Lord either in themselves or in others, there are some who, bound by very many and graver sins, seem estranged from God and from every act of piety and of chastity, so that nothing at all of the goodness and religion which they had received from their teachers seems to have remained in them, nor can they show whether they were begotten of Israel, that is, of holy Church, because by sinning such men have been made as though they had never belonged to the Holy Spirit, yet by repenting they are corrected and converted, so that their number is written in the heavens among the true Israelites in whom there is no guile.

VERS. 61.-- And of the children of the priests. (BED., ibid.) 694DThe children of the transmigration act toward the priests with the same caution as toward the people. For they took care that it should be plainly apparent who truly belonged to the people of Israel or to the priestly race, and who were either suspect or begotten of a certain stock of proselytes, that is, of strangers. The suspected priests therefore they removed from the office of the altar, until their origin should more certainly become clear, but they kept them nonetheless in the fellowship of those passing over, in a peace of one mind with themselves.

But mystically the sons of the priests going up out of the Babylonian captivity seek the writing of their genealogy, and not finding it are cast out of the priesthood, when the ministers of the altar fall into such enormities or abominable doctrines that, even though by repenting they return to the salvation 695Aof the soul, they are yet not worthy to be restored to the sacred grade. For though among the faithful they look for eternal life, yet they do not find, among the perfect priests, the writing of their grade, which they cannot claim again.

All the multitude as one man, etc. (BED., ibid.) [Vulg. Esr 2:64 Omnis multitudo quasi unus, without vir] These signify those who by the advance of an amended life busy themselves to overcome the vices and to climb the summit of the virtues, yet are not yet sufficient of themselves to provide the way of a regular life, but are restrained and directed by the industry of those who have gone before them in Christ.

And among them. (BED., ibid.) Mystically, in the temple or the people of God there are singers, etc., as far as and as it were by the sweetness of their voice they help those who build the temple.

VERS. 66.-- Their mules. (ID., ibid.) A mule is begotten of 695Ban ass and a mare, a mala of a horse and a she-ass. Among the men who had gone up out of the captivity, the beasts also by which they were helped are described. And their number, like that of the men, is set down, because there are many in the Church either slower of understanding or even carnal, who nevertheless obey their spiritual masters devoutly, and bow the back of the mind to bear the burdens of brotherly need, and, snatched with the rest of the elect out of the confusion of the devil's captivity, press toward the walls of the city above, whose number also is kept whole in the memory of God; whence: My imperfect being have thy eyes seen (Psal. CXXXVIII). And elsewhere: Men and beasts thou wilt save for the Lord (Ibid. XXXV).

VERS. 68.-- They offered freely into the house of God for, 695Cetc. (ID., ibid.) It is a token of great devotion, etc., as far as by how much they observe that these use not even lawful things.

VERS. 69.-- According to their strength. For this is the measure of perfection, that each labour for the Lord according to his power, and confirm the state of the Church first in himself, then in his neighbours. They who live in this fashion are rightly called princes of the fathers, because by living more highly they take the lead, in perfection of life and of teaching, over those who in the Church are called fathers through their zeal of solicitude.

Solids of gold. (BED., ibid.) Gold and silver and priestly vestments, etc., as far as For by how much the more grievously they remember that they have erred, by so much the more ardently do they persist in good works.

VERS. 70.-- The priests therefore dwelt, and the 695DLevites. A Levite taken captive by the Chaldeans receives back his city, when a deacon, by the Lord's gift, makes whole again the virtue of perfection which, seduced by the devil, he had lost. But one born in Babylon enters his city by coming into Judea, when, regenerated in Christ and well instructed in the Church, he keeps according to rule the diaconate once received. For as the former was set free by the grace of Christ as it were from the Chaldean servitude, so this latter, by the performance of the good works in which the predecessors of the same grade lived, they ascend as it were to the walls of their own virtue or city.

And all Israel. (BEDA ubi supra. ) By all Israel he means not the ten tribes only, which were once called Israel in distinction from the two which 696Awere called Juda, etc., as far as concerning whom the Jews promise themselves that, when their Christ reigns, they shall return into Judea with the rest of the men of their nation, and shall reign in the whole world.

Allegorically the priests, the Levites, the singers, the porters and the Nathinaei, and all Israel dwell in their own cities, when the ministers of the altar and the teachers and the Christian people serve God faithfully in their own grades, whether they who, that same grade having at some time been defiled or lost by vices, have been recalled through penance, or they who, lately coming out of the kingdom of the devil to the Church, have gained for themselves a good grade. For the Levites or the children of Israel entered their own cities, not only they who had lost them when taken captive, but also they who, born of captives, had learned that they themselves belonged to those cities by paternal succession. 696BBut whosoever were so taken captive that, never returning, they left their lands to others to be possessed, they signify those who so depart from the Church by sinning that they never come to themselves again, and leave to others the rewards promised to themselves; whence: Be mindful whence thou art fallen, and do penance, and do the first works: but if not, I come to thee, and will move thy candlestick, etc. (Apoc. II); and elsewhere: Hold fast that which thou hast, that no man take thy crown (Ibid. III).

CAPUT III.

Jamque venerat mensis septimus. (BEDA in Esdram, tom. II.) Qui apud nos October prae caeteris legali observatione solemnis, in quo etiam celebrata est dedicatio templi. Congrue ergo cum primo civitates 696Csuas intrassent et mansiones sibi providissent, mox in Jerusalem confluxerunt, et altare construxerunt, et eo tempore quo templum cum altari et omnibus vasis olim consecratum est, et quo ad diem consecrationis annuatim venire solebant.

Mystice. Septimus mensis gratiam Spiritus sancti significat. In hoc mense post captivitatem de civitatibus nostris in Jerusalem convenimus, cum ablatas sordes vitiorum post inchoationem bonorum operum majore ejusdem Spiritus gratia illuminamur, et in amore supernae pacis, quae in vera unitate est, accendimur. Jerusalem enim visio pacis dicitur.

VERS. 2.-- Et surrexit Josue. De tribu sacerdotali, quae significat Christum verum sacerdotem, qui in sanguine suo nos a peccatis lavit, et assistit nunc 696Dvultui Dei pro nobis.

Et fratres ejus sacerdotes. De quibus dicitur: Ite, nuntiate fratribus meis (Matth. XXVIII). Hi sunt in Ecclesia altiores, et Christo familiarius inhaerentes, Ecclesiam aedificant. Toti etiam dicitur Ecclesiae: Vos autem genus electum, regale sacerdotium (I Petr. II). Sunt enim electi membra summi sacerdotis, corpora sua offerentes hostiam vivam.

Et aedificaverunt. (BED., ubi supra. ) Magna provisione primo altare aedificaverunt, etc., usque ad sed Christum apud Patrem advocatum non habuerunt.

VERS. 3.-- Bases suas. (ID., ibid.) Corda scilicet electorum ad suscipienda fidei sacramenta praeceptis 697Amagistorum quasi ferramentis fabrorum praeparata, etc., usque ad cum fideles corda et corpora ad exercendum operando quod credunt, humili intentione submittunt.

Et obtulerunt. (BEDA., ibid.) Mane et vespere offerimus holocaustum Domino super altare ejus, etc., usque ad juxta illud: Dirigatur oratio mea sicut incensum in conspectu tuo, elevatio manuum tuarum sacrificium vespertinum (Psal. CXL).

VERS. 4.-- Feceruntque. (ID., ibid.) Qui de Babylone in Jerusalem ascenderant, etc., usque ad et alibi: Non habemus hic manentem civitatem, sed futuram inquirimus (Hebr. XIII).

Solemnitatem tabernaculorum. Quae Graece scenophegia, 697Bid est fixio tabernaculorum dicitur, quae incipit quinto decimo die mensis septimi, et durat septem diebus: in quibus praecepit Dominus populum in tabernaculis habitare, et quotidie legem scrutantes holocausta Domino offerre: quia quondam in tabernaculis scilicet morabantur in solitudine praedicante legem Mosi, et crebro sibi Deo apparente, diu exspectantes promissam requiem, ne unquam scilicet tanti beneficii gratia in oblivionem veniret.

Per ordinem. Quia quidquid inordinate geritur, meritum perfectionis amittit: quia honor regis judicium diligit. Quae enim in honorem summi regis agimus certi, necesse est discretione judicii, quando vel quantum sint agenda, distinguamus.

697CVERS. 5.-- In Kalendis, quam in universis, etc. Scilicet initiis mensium in ortu lunae, a quo semper Hebraei menses incipiebant, nullos menses exceptis lunaribus habentes: unde pro Kalendis Graeci melius neomenias, id est novilunia vocant. Si autem menses propter dies triginta quibus implentur, significant plenitudinem operum lucis, quando in fide sanctae Trinitatis Decalogi praecepta perficimus, quid exordia mensium, in quibus luna, ut denuo luceat, nuper a sole dicitur accendi, nisi initia designant bonae operationis, quae donante conditoris gratia, quasi solis justitiae praesentia percipimus? And now the seventh month was come. (BEDA in Esdram, tom. II.) Which among us is October, solemn beyond the rest by the observance of the law, in which also the dedication of the temple was celebrated. Fitly therefore, when they had first entered their own cities 696Cand provided themselves dwellings, they presently flowed together into Jerusalem and built the altar, and that at the time at which the temple with its altar and all its vessels had of old been consecrated, and at which they were wont to come yearly to the day of the consecration.

Mystically. The seventh month signifies the grace of the Holy Spirit. In this month, after the captivity, we come together out of our cities into Jerusalem, when, the filth of the vices taken away, after the beginning of good works we are enlightened by a greater grace of the same Spirit, and are kindled in the love of the peace above, which is in true unity. For Jerusalem is called the vision of peace.

VERS. 2.-- And Josue rose up. Of the priestly tribe, which signifies Christ the true priest, who washed us from our sins in his own blood, and now stands before 696Dthe face of God for us.

And his brethren the priests. Of whom it is said: Go, tell my brethren (Matth. XXVIII). These are the higher ones in the Church, and, cleaving more familiarly to Christ, they build the Church. To the whole Church too it is said: But you are a chosen generation, a kingly priesthood (I Petr. II). For the elect are members of the highest priest, offering their bodies a living victim.

And they built. (BED., ubi supra. ) With great foresight they first built the altar, etc., as far as but they had not Christ as advocate with the Father.

VERS. 3.-- Its bases. (ID., ibid.) The hearts, that is, of the elect, prepared for receiving the sacraments of faith by the precepts 697Aof the masters as by the irons of smiths, etc., as far as when the faithful submit their hearts and their bodies with humble intent to practise by working what they believe.

Morning and evening we offer a holocaust to the Lord upon his altar, etc., as far as according to that: Let my prayer be directed as incense in thy sight, the lifting up of thy hands an evening sacrifice (Psal. CXL). [Vulg. Ps 140:2 elevatio manuum mearum, "the lifting up of my hands"]

VERS. 4.-- And they made. (ID., ibid.) They who had gone up out of Babylon into Jerusalem, etc., as far as and elsewhere: We have not here a lasting city, but we seek one to come (Hebr. XIII).

The solemnity of tabernacles. Which in Greek is called scenophegia, 697Bthat is, the fixing of tabernacles, which begins on the fifteenth day of the seventh month and lasts seven days: in which the Lord commanded the people to dwell in tabernacles, and daily, searching the law, to offer holocausts to the Lord: because of old they tarried, that is, in tabernacles in the wilderness, while Moses preached the law and God often appeared to them, long awaiting the promised rest, that is, lest the grace of so great a benefit should ever come into forgetfulness.

In order. Because whatever is done out of order loses the merit of perfection: because the honour of the king loveth judgment. For the things which we do in honour of the highest king, being assured, it is needful that we distinguish, by the discernment of judgment, when and how much they are to be done.

697CVERS. 5.-- On the Kalends, as on all, etc. That is, on the beginnings of the months at the rising of the moon, from which the Hebrews always began their months, having no months but lunar ones: whence for Kalends the Greeks better call them neomeniae, that is, new moons. But if the months, because of the thirty days by which they are filled, signify the fulness of the works of light, when in the faith of the holy Trinity we accomplish the precepts of the Decalogue, what do the beginnings of the months designate, in which the moon, that it may shine anew, is said to be lately kindled by the sun, but the beginnings of good working, which by the gift of the Creator's grace we receive as by the presence of the Sun of justice? For the signifying whereof of that divine enlightenment, without which we should be able to begin or to accomplish nothing of good. God commanded all the Kalends, that is, the beginnings of the months, to be kept; whence: Sound ye 697Dat the beginning of the month the trumpet, in the noted day of your solemnity (Psal. LXXX). [Vulg. Ps 80:4 Buccinate in neomenia tuba, "sound the trumpet at the new moon"] And it is well said that, the festivity of tabernacles being finished, the children of the transmigration made a continual holocaust, etc. Because after the mind has once perfectly renounced this world, it behoves that it spend itself wholly in obedience to the divine will.

VERS. 6.-- From the first day. From the beginning, that is, of the divine inspiration, by which the human conscience is changed, and, pleasures being neglected, burns for those things alone which are the Lord's, as the apostles, who, inflamed by the Holy Spirit and made as it were a holocaust, preached the great works of God in all tongues. Hence among the Hebrews the seventh month is called the new year: because it is given by the Holy Spirit to the faithful to fulfil the new 698Acommandment of charity, and to render a new canticle in the building of the house of God.

But it is to be noted that in the seventh month the people was gathered together in Jerusalem, and that, all being gathered, Josue and Zorobabel with their brethren are related to have built the altar and from the first day of the seventh month to have offered a holocaust: whence it is gathered that this altar was put together very swiftly of unpolished stones: not, as of old, wrought of wood and covered with plates of brass. For otherwise, even with a very great crowd of builders pressing on, it could not have been begun and finished in one day, and made ready for the offering of the holocaust. For in the book of the Machabees it is said to have been made of stones: And they took whole stones according to the law, and built a new altar according to that which was before 698B(I Mach. IV).

It is to be noted that the beginning of the fifth age, like that of the four preceding, is consecrated by holocausts offered to the Lord. For in the first, Abel the protomartyr, first of all the elect, offered to God a holocaust of the firstlings of the sheep, and dedicated the entrance of the new-born world first with victims of cattle, at last with his own blood. Noe consecrated the rise of the second age with holocausts offered to God of those things which had been in the ark. Melchisedech and Abraham hallowed the third age, this one in bread and wine, that one by the offering of his own son. David consecrated the fourth age to the Lord, an altar being built in the floor of Horna the Jebusite, and holocausts offered to God, by which he might appease him whom he had offended by numbering the people; where Abraham is reported to have offered his son, and afterwards, 698Cwhen the temple had been built by Solomon, it is agreed that the altar of holocausts was set. The beginning of the fifth age Josue and Zorobabel now offered, an altar being built in that same place and holocausts offered to God, as soon as the yoke of the captivity was shaken off, and afterwards they appeased the Lord toward themselves by a continual holocaust. All these things went before in figure of him who in the sixth age redeemed the whole world by the victim of his own flesh.

For these deservedly are princes of the priests and of the Levites and leaders of the families of Israel, that is, they are understood as men or souls seeing God, of whom it is said: Thou shalt make them princes over all the earth (Psal. XLIV); and elsewhere: Her husband is honourable in the gates.

We set forward. (BEDA, ibid.) All things are full of mysteries, etc., as far as helped whereby we may the more effectually carry over the souls of the faithful to the 710Afellowship of the elect and to the citadel of the life of perfection, as holy vessels to the temple of the Lord.

VERS. 32.-- And we abode. (ID., ibid.) Three days are spent in Jerusalem, etc., as far as as though after the joy of a three days' abiding in Jerusalem they are the more honoured on account of the gifts and the precious vessels which they offered.

VERS. 34.-- And it was written down. (ID., ibid.) This is done when teachers earnestly examine the life of their subjects, etc., as far as that he may be given a worthy retribution in heaven.

VERS. 35.-- But they also who had come. (ID.) Great devotion and religion is shown, when, coming to the temple, they first of all offer holocausts, not only for themselves, but also for all Israel, that is, 710Bfor those who had already returned home, and for those who were still in exile.

But mystically those have truly and perfectly escaped by repenting the captivity of the devil, by which they were held in sinning, who subject themselves with fixed intention to the divine service, who kindle themselves, wholly drawn away from the lowest things, with the flame of heavenly desire. For this is to offer holocausts, that is, wholly burnt sacrifices, to the Lord, to think or to do nothing in all things but his will.

They offered holocausts to God, etc. Not only for themselves. It is a token of a perfect mind when a man immolates for all Israel, that is, for the general welfare of the faithful, and as one mindful of unity and of brotherhood beseeches the mercy above.

VERS. 36.-- And they lifted up the people. (AUG.) By adorning them 710Cwith the various gifts which the king and his counsellors and his princes had sent, and by delivering also his ministers and priests from all straits.

Mystically. The people and the temple of God hold one and the same figure of the Church, which Esdras and the children of the transmigration lift up when the sacred vessels are taken away from Babylon, when preachers, gathering to her, by the Lord's help, new peoples of believers, show her honourable to all and terrible. Again, when those whom they train by examples in good conversation, or whom by words they promote or advance even to the receiving of the heavenly rewards: they lift up the people into the house of God, because they make great joy both for those abiding in the country above and for the elect still journeying on earth.

(BEDA in Esdram, lib. II, tom. II.) But this 710Dtransgression, plainly described in the prophet Malachy, etc., as far as they refer the cause to the prince, that is, to the archbishop, by whose authority the crime may be expiated.

But mystically foreign wives signify heresies and the superstitious sects of the philosophers, which, when they are incautiously admitted into the Church, defile by error the holy seed of catholic truth and of pure action. All sins also with which the heathen are polluted, while Christians are not ashamed to imitate them, as it were through foreign wives degenerate from the seed of the word of God by which they had been begotten, according to that: Of his own will hath he begotten us by the word, etc. (Jac. I), and as it were beget a profane offspring of the daughters of strangers, while, following the allurements 711Aof the erring, they bring forth their perverse acts to the knowledge of all.

He is not separated. (BEDA., ibid.) It is to be noted that, Israel having been led away into captivity, etc., as far as the princes who ought to have corrected confess that they were the first to err.

CAPUT IX.

VERS. 3.-- Scidi pallium. (ID., ibid.) Per vestimenta solent opera designari, etc., usque ad Sedit moerens, ut per lamenta poenitentiae veniam promerendam esse doceret.

VERS. 4.-- Convenerunt. Nota quantum exempla doctorum juvant. Nihil scribitur Esdras locutus esse, sed tantum audito scelere lacrymasse et turbam ad se fidelium non vociferando sed lacrymando traxisse.

711BConvenerunt. Magna mutatio rerum perfidia principum, multi luxuriam secuti, reducti sunt, magno principe moerente, et quid peccatoribus agendum esset innuente: conveniunt ad eum omnes qui timent verbum Dei, quo transgressores puniendos esse minatur.

Scisso pallio. (BEDA, ibid.) Typice, quod scisso indumento, etc., usque ad etiam ea quae illorum fragilitati veraciter conveniebant, misericorditer in se transferre dignatus est.

VERS. 5.-- Curvavi genua, etc. Paravit se per compunctionem cordis, et affectionem corporis, ut dignus efficeretur auditu supernae pietatis: et in haec verba orationis prorumpit. Curvat autem genua, expandit manus, et fundit preces in tempore sacrificii 711Cvespertini, sciens esse gratius quod fit in spiritu humilitatis et in animo contrito, quam quod carnibus et sanguine pecudum offertur.

CHAPTER IX.

VERS. 3.-- I rent my mantle. (ID., ibid.) By garments works are wont to be designated, etc., as far as He sat mourning, that he might teach that pardon is to be earned by the lamentations of penance.

VERS. 4.-- They came together. Note how much the examples of teachers avail. Nothing is written of Esdras having spoken, but only that, the crime being heard, he wept, and drew the crowd of the faithful to himself not by crying out but by weeping.

711BThey came together. A great change of things, by the perfidy of the princes, many having followed luxury, are brought back, the great prince mourning and intimating what sinners ought to do: there come together to him all who fear the word of God, by which he threatens that transgressors are to be punished.

The mantle being rent. (BEDA, ibid.) Typically, that with the garment rent, etc., as far as he vouchsafed mercifully to transfer to himself even those things which truly befitted their frailty.

VERS. 5.-- I bowed my knees, etc. He prepared himself by compunction of heart and by affliction of body, that he might be made worthy of the hearing of the mercy above: and he breaks forth into these words of prayer. And he bows his knees, spreads out his hands, and pours forth prayers at the time of the evening sacrifice, 711Cknowing that what is done in the spirit of humility and in a contrite mind is more acceptable than what is offered with the flesh and blood of cattle.

CAPUT X.

VERS. 1.-- Sic ergo orante Esdra, et implorante Deum et flente . . . . . . . Flevit populus. (BEDA in Esdram, tom. II.) Qui scilicet peccaverunt, poenitentiam agentes, vel qui in castitate permanserant, de casu fratrum dolentes, etc., usque ad Nunc vero etiam ipsi qui peccaverunt cum uxoribus suis et liberis, poenitentiam acturi convenerunt.

De filiis Aelam. Hunc dicit Josephus primum fuisse Jerosolymitarum, qui ut principem decet, maxima auctoritate mox intentionem Esdrae juvabat, 711Dpopulum secum peccasse confitendo, et poenitentiam suadendo abjectis uxoribus alienigenis, cum liberis suis.

Et nunc si est poenitentia in Israel. Quia si populum hujus peccati perfecte poenitet, primo conversi ad Dominum, correctionem promittamus, et veniam postulemus; deinde ad nos reversi, omnem peccati radicem exstirpemus et fruticem, ejectis, scilicet, uxoribus cum omni sobole: hoc est enim veraciter 712Apoenitentiam agere toto corde, et intus ad Dominum converti, et omnem foris peccandi materiam ab ipsa origine resecari.

Surge. Decenter docet quomodo sit apud majores consilio agendum, ut scilicet, quisque pro suo sensu quod optimum intellexerit, vel intellexisse sibi videtur, dicat: et tamen ei qui praeest, locum discernendi relinquat, paratus obtemperare omnibus quae ille secundum voluntatem et legem Dei agenda decreverit.

VERS. 6.-- Filii Eliasib. Qui tunc summus sacerdos erat, post Jesum filium Josedec Joacim filius ejus, et post eum Eliasib filius ejus summo sacerdotio functus est.

Panem non comedit. (BEDA ubi supra. ) Exemplum 712Bhinc habent sacerdotes, etc., usque ad: Si enim refugientes coinquinationes mundi, etc. (II Petr. II).

VERS. 9.-- Ipse est. (BEDA ubi supra. ) Qui ab Hebraeis Casleu, a Romanis vocatur December, etc., usque ad qua arte adversa quae exterius propter peccata desaeviunt, aut evitent, aut separent.

Et sedit omnis populus. (ID., ibid.) Id est, circa atrium sacerdotum quo ipsa domus undique cingebatur, habens circa se ex omni parte per quadrum aedes atriorum amplissimas, in quibus etiam populus quando pro pluvia opus erat, stare poterat, et videre ea quae in templi januis gerebantur, vel circa templum: habebant enim interiores parietes juxta terram in columnis factos exteriores solidos.

VERS. 10.-- Et surrexit Esdras. (ID., ibid.) Hic 712Clocus respondet, etc., usque ad et facta merito in memoria teneretur, ac posteris noscenda traderentur.

VERS. 16.-- Et sederunt in die. (ID., ibid.) Nota numerum ternarium esse mysticum, etc., usque ad quod etiam nos omnibus annis in quadragesima paschae oportuit imitari, instante scilicet Dominica resurrectionis, mundemus nos ab omni inquinamento carnis et spiritus, ut participes resurrectionis esse valeamus (II Cor. VII).

Et inventi sunt. (ID., ibid.) Aptant Hebraei huic loco, etc., usque ad Sed intuendum quod non scribit Esdras Jesum alienigenam duxisse uxorem, sed quosdam de filiis et fratribus suis, etc.

VERS. 18.-- Et fratres ejus. (ID., ibid.) Cognati 712Dscilicet, non enim fratres ejus germani in carne eatenus vivere, et voluptati operam dare poterant, cum centum anni et amplius essent transacti, ex quo Cyrus regnare incipiens, Jesum et Zorobabel cum transmigratione Juda et Benjamin, ad construendam domum Domini, Jerosolymam remisit.

VERS. 19.-- Et dederunt, etc. (ID., ibid.) Primo uxores illicitas abjiciunt, etc., usque ad dignum faciat perpetua mercede in coelis.

CHAPTER X.

VERS. 1.-- So Esdras praying, and imploring God and weeping . . . . . . . The people wept. (BEDA in Esdram, tom. II.) [Vulg. Esr 10:1 et implorante eo et flente, "and he imploring and weeping"] They, that is, who had sinned, doing penance, or who had remained in chastity, grieving for the fall of their brethren, etc., as far as But now those also who had sinned came together with their wives and their children, about to do penance.

Of the children of Aelam. Josephus says that this man was the first of the men of Jerusalem, who, as befits a prince, presently with the greatest authority helped the intention of Esdras, 711Dby confessing that the people had sinned together with him, and by persuading to penance, the foreign wives being cast off, with their children.

And now if there be repentance in Israel. Because if the people perfectly repents of this sin, first, being converted to the Lord, let us promise amendment and ask pardon; then, returning to ourselves, let us root out every root and shoot of sin, casting out, that is, the wives with all their offspring: for this is truly 712Ato do penance with the whole heart, and inwardly to be converted to the Lord, and outwardly to have every matter of sinning cut away from its very origin.

Arise. Fittingly he teaches how counsel is to be taken among elders, namely, that each should say what he has understood to be best, or seems to himself to have understood: and yet leave to him who presides the place of discerning, being ready to obey in all things that he shall have decreed to be done according to the will and the law of God.

VERS. 6.-- The children of Eliasib. Who was then high priest, after Jesus the son of Josedec, Joacim his son, and after him Eliasib his son, held the office of high priest.

He ate no bread. (BEDA ubi supra. ) Hence priests have an example, 712Betc., as far as: If indeed, fleeing the defilements of the world, etc. (II Petr. II).

VERS. 9.-- This is. (BEDA ubi supra. ) Which by the Hebrews is called Casleu, by the Romans December, etc., as far as by which art they may either avoid or put away the adversities which rage without because of sins.

And all the people sat. (ID., ibid.) That is, about the court of the priests, with which the house itself was girt about on every side, having round it on every part, foursquare, most spacious buildings of courts, in which the people also, when there was need because of rain, could stand and see the things which were done in the doors of the temple, or about the temple: for they had the inner walls made upon pillars near the ground, the outer solid.

VERS. 10.-- And Esdras rose up. (ID., ibid.) This 712Cplace answers, etc., as far as and the deeds might deservedly be held in memory, and handed down to be known by those who come after.

VERS. 16.-- And they sat down on the day. (ID., ibid.) Note that the number three is mystical, etc., as far as which we too ought to imitate in every year in the Lent of the pasch, the Lord's day of the resurrection being at hand, let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement of flesh and of spirit, that we may be able to be partakers of the resurrection (II Cor. VII).

And there were found. (ID., ibid.) The Hebrews fit to this place, etc., as far as But it is to be observed that Esdras does not write that Jesus married a foreign wife, but certain of his sons and of his brethren, etc.

VERS. 18.-- And his brethren. (ID., ibid.) His kinsmen, 712Dthat is, for his brethren german in the flesh could not thus far be living and giving heed to pleasure, since a hundred years and more had passed since Cyrus, beginning to reign, sent back Jesus and Zorobabel with the transmigration of Juda and Benjamin to Jerusalem, to build the house of the Lord.

VERS. 19.-- And they gave, etc. (ID., ibid.) First they cast off the unlawful wives, etc., as far as may make him worthy of a perpetual reward in heaven.
