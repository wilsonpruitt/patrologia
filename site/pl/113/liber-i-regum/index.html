Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

VERS. 3.-- The priests of the Lord (GREG., ibid.). How are they priests of the Lord, who below are called sons of Belial? 540BAt that time they were called priests of the Lord, when the priests of idols were reckoned by the names of false gods, so that by this mark they might be distinguished from those. For by these words their faith is proclaimed, not their life, because they both practised depravity of work, but did not err in the faith of the Creator.

Ophni, unshod, or the madness of conversion.

Phinees. (ORIG., hom. de Elcana.) A dumb mouth, it signifies the scribes and Pharisees, etc., as far as deservedly therefore were they sent away and forsaken by God.

And because the oracles of God were first entrusted to them, to Anna, that is, to the Church of the Gentiles, he gave one portion, namely natural wit. Well doth he say.

VERS. 8.-- Anna, why weepest thou? Better to the Church is her own husband, that is, Christ, than the ten sons whom she brought forth from the synagogue, who sometimes loves Christ more, that is, his grace, than those whom she bore carnally.

VERS. 14.-- And he said to her. The Septuagint, καὶ εἶπεν αὐτῇ παιδάριον Ἡλί.

How long wilt thou be drunken? Man seeth in the face, God in the heart. Heli reckoned her drunken, who looked only upon the mouth: but the Lord had regard to her prayer, who saw her heart. Here, moreover, a form of praying is handed down to us, that we should pray not in much speaking, but in compunction 541Aof heart and in the pouring forth of tears: because a sacrifice to God is an afflicted spirit, a contrite heart, etc.

VERS. 17.-- Heli said, Go in peace, etc. And the woman went away, etc. Dost thou see the woman's faith? Before she received what she had asked, she trusted just as though she had already received it. The cause was that she had prayed with much fervour, without any hesitation. She went away with a mind at peace, as though she had obtained the whole. Moreover God had already shaken all her anxiety out of her mind, being about to bestow what she was asking. Let us imitate this woman, and in all afflictions let us flee to God. If we have no children, let us ask them of him, and if we receive them, let us bring them up with much care, etc.

(ID.) For in no wise was the Church changed away from the faith and love of the Redeemer by turning aside into different things.

VERS. 19.-- And the Lord remembered her. (ID.) Because Anna devoutly petitioned the Lord, she conceived and bore. So the Church, praying through natural understanding, conceived the mystery of the incarnation in the heart, and brought it forth with the mouth of confession.

The Jews, filled before, namely with the mysteries of the oracles, hired themselves out for bread, that is, they separated themselves from God: or they tore themselves away from their own seats, lest they should receive the Gospel that treats of the bread which came down from heaven. Filled were those same who were also the mighty, that is, the Israelites to whom the oracles of God were entrusted, but sons of the bondwoman, were made less, who withdrew themselves and believed not in the Saviour, and in those very loaves, that is, in the divine oracles, savoured only earthly things. But the nations, to whom the law was not given, after through the New Testament they came 543Ato those oracles, passed over the earth by much hungering, because in them they savoured not earthly but heavenly things. In the Greek thus: They that were full of bread were made less, and they that were hungry passed over the earth.

And the hungry. That is, the Gentiles, before in want, now taste the bread of the word of God, since it is sweet. So long have they been fed with the knowledge of God.

The barren hath borne. We behold too the earthly Jerusalem fruitful, now made weak: because whosoever were free sons in her were 543Bof her strength: but now, since the letter is there, the spirit is not: her strength being lost, she is made weak.

(ID.) The barren woman, etc., as far as by this his poverty we have been enriched.

Mystically, the preachers, upon whom the globe, that is, the 543DChurch, is made firm by their faith, of whom it is said: Under whom they stoop that bear up the world (Job. IX).

For when the Lord begins to possess us, our adversary becomes his own; and he is conquered by us, not by our strength, because a man is not mighty in his own power. They shall fear the Lord. The Jews, his adversaries, over whom the Lord thundered in the heavens, that is, in the apostles, when he terrified them by threatening hell, confirming his threats by signs: whence many were converted. In the heavens. (RAB.) Because the just retribution is to come in the day of judgment, but meanwhile he thunders out of his clouds, which he filled with the Holy Spirit after he ascended the heavens.

The ends. The last things of a man: because those things will not be judged which in the meantime are changed for the better or for the worse, but the last things in which he shall have been found.

And he will give. That is, to Christ

Dominion. That is, the Church, whence: I will give thee the nations for thine inheritance. Or dominion, that is, the power of judging, because he hath given all judgment to the Son.

To his king. (RAB.) To the Christian people, to whom he gives strength to rule the flesh, that in it he may overcome the world, who for its sake shed his own blood.

544BOf his Christ. (RAB., in lib. Reg. ) Of the Christian people, anointed with chrism: yet the whole body with its head is one Christ. These things Anna the mother of Samuel prophesied, in whom the change of the old priesthood was then figured, and now fulfilled, when the fruitful woman was made weak, that the barren might have a new priesthood in Christ.

VERS. 12.-- Heli. (ID.) He is interpreted a stranger, for he is estranged from God who does not correct those under him. Ophni, the infamy of conversion, and deservedly is he so called who defers to be converted to the better. Phinees, the hardening of the mouth, or sparing the mouth. We read of two priests named Phinees: the one just, the son of Eleazar, the other unjust, the son of Heli. The priests who keep their mouth, lest anything depraved go forth thence, are signified in the son of Eleazar. 544CBut they who have their mouth stopped, either by unskilfulness or by the consciousness of sins, are figured by the son of Heli.

(RAB.) Josephus relates, etc., as far as and the fault of those under them will be imputed to them.

VERS. 19.-- A tunic. The tunic which Anna, that is grace, made and delivered to the boy; it is the beginning doctrine by which the faithful receive the first rudiments, whence: I gave you milk to drink, not meat (I Cor. III). But the linen ephod signifies chastity and continence together with good works. For he who desires to minister to the Lord must needs live chastely and continently, and exercise himself in good works.

VERS. 25.-- If he shall have sinned. (HIERON., ubi supra.) It is not sinned against a man in that manner in which it is sinned against 544DGod, because to sin against a man, that is, against a neighbour, is lighter and more pardonable. But to sin against God, that is, to depart from his worship: it is the sin of impiety, which is more hardly punished and more slowly remitted. And when one sins against a man, the man being appeased, he makes God appeasable to himself. But when one sins against God, who shall pray for him? that is, through whom shall he make God propitious to himself? Not that no pardon is to be hoped for by those who sin in the divine office: but greater is the penalty, and slower the pardon, for those who offend in things consecrated to God, than if they had injured a neighbour by word or deed, because the greater the glory, the graver the offence

For whosoever is of his stock, when he sees the priesthood of the Christians flourishing throughout the world, but withdrawn from himself, pines away with grief and sorrow.

VERS. 32.-- Thy rival. (RAB.) The rival of the house of Heli was Sadoch the priest, who, Abiathar having been cast out by Solomon, took up the priesthood. Whence: Solomon cast out Abiathar, that he might not be priest of the Lord, that the word of the Lord might be fulfilled which he spoke over the house of Heli (III Reg. II).

VERS. 33.-- That they may fail. (RAB.) That there may always, namely, be one of the house of Heli in the house of the Lord, who, seeing another substituted in the dignity of the priesthood and himself left destitute, may fail in mind and pine away. But this change of the priesthood was fully accomplished in Christ, which 545Bwas figured by Samuel: for after Samuel there were priests of the stock of Aaron, Sodoch, and Abiathar, and others until the time of Christ.

VERS. 35.-- And the soul. (ID.) God has not a soul, but is tropically said to have a soul, etc., as far as whence: And the house of Israel rested after the Lord (I Reg. VII).

VERS. 36.-- It shall come to pass. (ID.) This is not said properly of the house of Heli, but of the house of Aaron, of whose stock even to this day they come and are converted.

A morsel. (RAB.) The bread that I will give is my flesh for the life of the world (Joan. VI). Because he had said that he had given food to the house of Aaron from the victims of the old testament, which were the sacrifices of the Jews, he says that a morsel of bread is to be asked for the eating, which is the 545Csacrifice of the Christians and the food of souls.

Nor ought anyone to wonder if he find cities of Juda in the tribe of Simeon or of Benjamin. VERS. 21.-- Substiterant. Ducenti lassi viri, qui cum sarcinis sunt relicti, significant infirmos Ecclesiae, qui cum spiritualibus viris currere nequeunt in via, sed quia fidem habent et opus bonum servant, a Christo praemium beatitudinis cum perfectis praecipiunt.

CHAPTER XXX.

VERS. 1.-- And when the Amalecites. (GREG., lib. XXIX 562CMoral.) It is to be considered what it is that the Amalecites invade Siceleg and take spoil, etc., as far as whom the world in common has despised to have.

VERS. 21.-- They had stayed behind. The two hundred weary men who were left with the baggage signify the weak of the Church, who cannot run in the way with spiritual men, but because they have faith and keep good work, they take beforehand from Christ the reward of blessedness together with the perfect.

CAPUT XXXI.

VERS. 2.-- Percusserunt Jonathan, etc. (RAB. in lib. Reg. tom. II.) Jonathan, Abinadab, etc., usque ad cum filiis periret.

VERS. 4.-- Arripuit itaque. Gladio quo Amalecitis 562Dcontra praeceptum Dei pepercit, merito seipsum occidit: sic qui potestate pro communi utilitate accepta, aut non utitur aut abutitur, suo se nimirum gladio confodit, et quo ab hoste defendi debuerat, hostem potius juvat.

VERS. 11.-- Habitatores. Quos Saul ab adversariis defenderat, rependunt quod possunt. Nos quoque benefactores nostros gladio peccati peremptos, quoad possumus, a Philisthinorum opprobrio eripiamus.

CHAPTER XXXI.

VERS. 2.-- They slew Jonathan, etc. (RAB. in lib. Reg. tom. II.) Jonathan, Abinadab, etc., as far as he should perish with his sons.

VERS. 4.-- He took therefore. With the sword wherewith he spared the Amalecites 562Dagainst the commandment of God, he deservedly slew himself: so he who, having received power for the common good, either uses it not or abuses it, doubtless pierces himself with his own sword, and whereby he ought to have been defended from the enemy, he rather helps the enemy.

VERS. 11.-- The inhabitants. They whom Saul had defended from their adversaries repay what they can. Let us also, as far as we can, snatch our benefactors, slain by the sword of sin, from the reproach of the Philistines.
