Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

For he that diggeth a pit falleth into it (Prov. XXVI). And, The mouth that belieth killeth the soul (Sap. I): and this man was lying that he himself had killed Saul.

VERS. 18.-- The sons. The sons of Juda, sons of confession, 563Bwhom the true David wills to be fortified with the bow of Scripture, lest through ignorance they fall down in the sight of the demons.

The bow. (RAB. in lib. Reg.) Because the Philisthiim are archers, by which art they had killed Saul, he wills them to learn that same art of war, or he commanded that the kings of Juda should teach fortitude, that is, that they should be strong and intent in the fear of the Lord, lest they perish as Saul did.

In the book. (RAB., ubi supra.) It is read, or, it is written. They assert that this book is nowhere to be found, like the book of the wars of the Lord, etc., as far as how Saul, drawing back from the fear of the Lord, perished through obedience [obedientiam; read inobedientiam, "disobedience"].

Consider, Israel. (ID.) Thou who hast remained, for what 563Cfault they were slain, and beware lest thou too perish in their wickedness.

VERS. 20.-- Geth. A winepress, that is, Jerusalem, which killeth the prophets (Matth. XXIII), to whom the death of the Lord and of the apostles was a matter of mockery, and for this cause the true David commanded that his passion should not be announced to them.

VERS. 21.-- The mountains of Gelboe, etc. (ID.) Others, high places, because, that is, O Saul, thy disobedience was accounted as idolatry in the high places, since it is the wickedness of idolatry to be unwilling to acquiesce. The mountains of Gelboe were most rich before the curse, and they say that even until now they lie under the curse, and they testify that it never rains there.

David, king and prophet, who did not render evils 563Dto his persecutors, when Saul and Jonathas had fallen in battle, in his lamentation curses the mountains of Gelboe: Ye mountains of Gelboe, neither dew, etc., as far as and the divine power fulfilled the sentence of him who cursed. And so for the spectacle of the king's slaying the elements paid the penalty.

(RAB.) What have the mountains done amiss? etc., as far 564Aas they who had deserved, iniquity requiring it, to receive the death of their king among themselves.

VERS. 22.-- From the blood of the slain. Blood and fat furnish strength to the body, so those men gave fortitude to their subjects, by whom Saul and Jonathas were slain as though not by rustics. Or thus, Saul was reprobated by God, and slain basely by the uncircumcised, as though he had not been anointed with oil, because of the blood of the slain, that is, of the seventy-five priests who burned fat to the Lord, whom Saul commanded to be slain though they were harmless.

The arrow. It signifies that Jonathan and Saul were warlike and most valiant, and it praises in them the comeliness of form and the constancy of mind.

(RAB.) Allegorically. The holy prophet David figuratively 564Bbewails Saul and Jonathan, etc., as far as lest, being again shamefully overcome, they give joy to their enemies.

CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1.-- Therefore after. (RAB.) The going up of David with his two wives into Hebron signifies the calling together of the two peoples into the Catholic Church. For Hebron is interpreted wedlock. But the Church alone is the bride of the heavenly king. Thither David brought each man with his household, because the faithful are called one by one into that fellowship through the preaching of the Gospel, where there is no distinction of Jew and Greek.

CAPUT V.

VERS. 1.-- Et venerunt. (RAB.) Sic omnes gentes 566Aconveniunt ad Christum, et quasi blandiendo dicunt: Os tuum et caro tua sumus: cum in illo incarnationem quam ex nostra natura in virginis utero suscepit, diligunt et venerantur.

VERS. 3.-- Seniores. (ID.) Prophetae, apostoli pari fide et devotione ad Christum concurrunt.

Unxeruntque. (ID.) David bis unctus dicitur, et Christus prius in Judaeis, post in gentibus principatum sumpsit.

VERS. 4.-- Et quadraginta. (ID.) Quaeritur cur non in summa quadraginta anni et sex menses numerentur? etc., usque ad qui ex quaterdenis constat, temporum et rerum perfectionem significat.

VERS. 5.-- In Hierusalem. Tradunt Hebraei quod Sem filius Noe, quem dicunt Melchisedech, condidit 566BSalem. Hanc postea tenuerunt Jebusaei, e quibus sortita est nomen Jebus. VERS. 6.-- Caecos et claudos. Qui sufficiunt contra te. Hoc dicebant fidentes muris. Aut ita: non ingredieris nisi nos auferas, quos inermes sicut caecos et claudos arbitraris et triumphare putas.

VERS. 7.-- Cepit autem. (RAB.) Sicut Josephus ait, etc., usque ad quae merito arx Sion, id est speculationis vocatur, ut de ea recte dicatur: Factus est in pace locus ejus, etc. (Psal. LXXV).

David arcem Sion cepit, ablatis prius caecis et 566Cclaudis odientibus animam David. Sic Dominus primatum in Ecclesia gentium acquisivit, reprobatis ejus Scribis et Pharisaeis, qui caeci et claudi oderunt animam Christi, id est ejus vitam auferre conati sunt.

VERS. 8.-- Domatum. Id est rectorum. Fistulas appellat aquae ductus de plumbo vel regula factos per quos aqua ibat super murum per totam civitatem.

Fistulas. Falsam scientiam seu haereticorum dogmata figurant, quae Dominus per Joab, id est, praedicatores destruit. Jebusaeus conculcans, haec est gentilitas, quae ante fidem praecepta Dei conculcabat. Item Jebusaeus calcatus, quia diabolus a Domino calcatur, et sub Joab calcandus subditur

VERS. 9.-- Et aedificavit. A loco ita dicto, subaudi 566Dmurum: et intrinsecus subaudi alium murum, hoc est, munivit urbem duplici muro.

VERS. 11.-- Misit quoque. Hiram vivens excelse, haec est gentilitas in Christo excelsa; nuntios mittit ad David, id est doctores ad Christum, et per eos cedrina ligna, id est incorruptos et fortes; et artifices, id est, eos quorum arte mentes sunt incorruptae, et tanquam lapides quatuor virtutibus quadratae, sic domus David, id est Ecclesia Christi aedificatur, et cognoscitur quod Deus nostrum David confirmavit regem super Israel, id est, videntes Deum.

(RAB.) Haereticorum ecclesias quae sub Christiani nominis titulo se manere gloriantur. Sed quia propter 567Alucra carnalia Christum sequuntur, non conjuges, sed concubinae vocantur. Nunc vero si reges plures uxores, vel concubinas habent, peccatum est: quia figurae transierunt, pro quibus hoc aliquando veniale fuit.

VERS. 13.-- Natique sunt. Si per David significatur Christus, non immerito per filios ejus intelliguntur Christiani, qui sunt ex Judaeis et nationibus, quibus congruunt interpretationes nominum: Samua, audacia; Sobab, convertens; Jabahar, elegit; Elisua, Dei mei salus; Nepheg, applicans ori; Japhia, illuminat vel ostendit; Elisama, Deus meus audiens; Elioda, Deus meus scit; Eliphalet, Deus meus salvans.

VERS. 20.-- Baal Pharasim. Interpretatur inimicorum 567Bdivisio. Potest dici sic locus vocatus, quia Philisthiim reliquerunt idola sua in loco ubi divisi sunt.

VERS. 23.-- Pyrorum. In Hebraeo flentium, id est, idolorum, ubi scilicet idola erant in quibus confidebant quae flentium vocantur, quia fletu digna sunt, et cultores suos ad fletum miseriamque perducunt.

In cacumine. (RAB.) Pyrus dicitur ex Greco πῦρ quod est ignis. Pyri sancti: cacumen, sublimitas vitae eorum; in qua flante Spiritu sancto intonuit praedicationis sonitus. Qui est gradientis, id est donum in carne advenientis, in cacumine pyrorum, id est super celsitudinem sanctorum, dum hic sonitus auditur a nostro David, Philisthaei intelligibiles 567Csuperantur.

CHAPTER V.

VERS. 1.-- And they came. (RAB.) So all the nations 566Acome together to Christ, and as though caressing him they say: We are thy bone and thy flesh: when they love and venerate in him the incarnation which he took from our nature in the womb of the Virgin.

VERS. 3.-- The ancients. (ID.) The prophets and the apostles run together to Christ with equal faith and devotion.

And they anointed. (ID.) David is said to have been anointed twice, and Christ took the sovereignty first among the Jews, afterwards among the Gentiles.

VERS. 4.-- And forty. (ID.) It is asked: why are not forty years and six months reckoned in the sum? etc., as far as which consists of four tens, and signifies the perfection of times and of things.

VERS. 5.-- In Hierusalem. The Hebrews hand down that Sem the son of Noe, whom they call Melchisedech, founded 566BSalem. This city the Jebusaeans afterwards held, from whom it obtained the name Jebus. Thence Hierusalem, as it were Jebus Salem, afterwards by Solomon it was called Jerosolyma, as it were Jerosalomonia, which afterwards was called Aelia by Aelius Adrianus.

VERS. 6.-- The blind and the lame. Who are enough against thee. This they said, trusting in their walls. Or thus: thou shalt not enter unless thou take us away, whom, unarmed, thou reckonest as blind and lame, and thinkest to triumph over.

VERS. 7.-- And he took. (RAB.) As Josephus says, etc., as far as which is rightly called the citadel of Sion, that is, of contemplation, so that of it may rightly be said: His place is made in peace, etc. (Psal. LXXV).

David took the citadel of Sion, when first the blind and 566Cthe lame that hated the soul of David had been taken away. So the Lord acquired the primacy in the Church of the Gentiles, when his Scribes and Pharisees had been reprobated, who, blind and lame, hated the soul of Christ, that is, endeavoured to take away his life.

VERS. 8.-- Of the housetops. That is, of the rulers. He calls conduits of water, made of lead or of a rule, pipes, through which the water went over the wall throughout the whole city.

Pipes. They figure false knowledge, or the teachings of heretics, which the Lord destroys through Joab, that is, through the preachers. Jebusaeus is one treading down, this is the Gentile world, which before the faith trod down the commandments of God. Likewise Jebusaeus is one trodden down, because the devil is trodden down by the Lord, and is put under Joab to be trodden

VERS. 9.-- And he built. From the place so called, understand 566Dthe wall: and within, understand another wall, that is, he fortified the city with a double wall.

VERS. 11.-- He sent also. Hiram, living loftily, this is the Gentile world, lofty in Christ; he sends messengers to David, that is, teachers to Christ, and through them cedar timbers, that is, men uncorrupt and strong; and craftsmen, that is, men by whose skill minds are made uncorrupt, and, like stones, squared with the four virtues, so is the house of David built, that is, the Church of Christ, and it is known that God has confirmed our David king over Israel, that is, over those who see God.

(RAB.) The churches of heretics, which glory that they abide under the title of the Christian name. But because for the sake 567Aof carnal gains they follow Christ, they are called not wives but concubines. But now, if kings have many wives or concubines, it is a sin: because the figures have passed away, for the sake of which this was at one time venial.

VERS. 13.-- And there were born. If by David Christ is signified, not undeservedly are Christians understood by his sons, who are of the Jews and of the nations, to whom the interpretations of the names agree: Samua, boldness; Sobab, turning back; Jabahar, he chose; Elisua, the salvation of my God; Nepheg, applying to the mouth; Japhia, he enlightens or shows; Elisama, my God hearing; Elioda, my God knows; Eliphalet, my God saving.

VERS. 20.-- Baal Pharasim. It is interpreted the enemies' 567Bdivision. The place can be said to be so called because the Philisthiim left their idols in the place where they were divided.

VERS. 23.-- Of the pear trees. In the Hebrew of the weepers, that is, of the idols, where, that is, there were idols in which they trusted, which are called weepers, because they are worthy of weeping, and lead their own worshippers to weeping and misery.

On the top. (RAB.) The pear tree is named from the Greek πῦρ, which is fire. The pear trees are the saints: the top is the loftiness of their life; in which, when the Holy Spirit breathed, the sound of preaching thundered. Which is of one walking, that is, the gift of him coming in the flesh, on the top of the pear trees, that is, above the height of the saints, while this sound is heard by our David, the intelligible Philisthaeans 567Care overcome.

(GREG., l. V Moral., cap. 10.) Saepe quia intelligi non valent, etc., usque ad non meliorum facta vel dicta velut infirma judicarent.

CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 1.-- The bridle. There were five cities of the Philisthines which made Israel tributary. These David took to himself and made them tributary, as it is read: David smote the Philisthiim, and humbled them, and took Geth and her daughters out of their hand, etc. (I Paral. 569CXVIII.)

The bridle. Christ, triumphing over the powers of the air, broke the bridle of error which they had laid upon the human race.

VERS. 2.-- Making equal. For men living on the earth were not humbled to such a degree that, when the line was stretched over them, they appeared equal to the earth: but they were so vile and despised that in nothing were they worth more than the earth which has no men. But he put the line for the lot; because it is the custom to measure with a line, according to that word: and by lot he divided to them the land with the line of distribution (Psal. LXXVII). And it signifies that he divided the regions of the Moabites to whom he would as freely as an owner divides his own fields.

(RAB.) And it signifies that he was able to kill 569Dwhom he would, and to spare whom he would. But that he made Moab, which is interpreted from the father, and Syria of Damascus and the other regions tributary, signifies that Christ holds all the nations under his dominion, and presses them, and exacts the tribute of good works. For Damascus, a noble city of Phoenicia, is called by the same name by which also the son of Maseth the handmaid of Abraham is called, and it is interpreted the cup of blood. But Syria, lofty or moist; for she who before, moist with lust, thirsted insatiably for human blood, now, cleansed by the baptism of Christ, lofty by the increase of virtues, desires the cup of his blood.

VERS. 11.-- The king sanctified. And it is signified that Christ, by the strictness of his examination, extinguishes in those who think perversely of him the folly of immoderate labour.

CHAPTER X.

For Hanon, that is, their sorrow, signifies the devil, who of the Ammonites, that is, of the malignant spirits, that is, the ruler of a people of mourning and set always in straitness, who desires to press down or to straiten men. Hanon therefore shaves off the half of the beard of David's messengers, when the devil, by corrupting them, stains the speech or the manner of life of certain preachers. Unde sancti viri cum sinistra delectatione pulsari se sentiunt, ipsa per quae formae species ad mentem ingreditur, disciplinae magisterio lumina restringunt, ne pravae cogitationi visio lenocinata famuletur: quae si unquam subtiliter custodiri negligitur, cogitationis immunditia ad operationem transit.

Tenet ergo David figuram ejus de quo scriptum est: In sole posuit tabernaculum suum (Psal. XVIII). Qui Bethsabee ad se jubet educere, id est legem litterae carnali populo conjunctam spirituali sibi intellectu sociare. Bethsabee enim puteus septem dicitur, quia per cognationem legis infusione 572Bspecialis gratiae perfecta nobis sapientia ministratur. Urias vero Judaicum populum significat, qui interpretatur lux mea Dei: Judaicus enim populus qui de accepta legis scientia extollitur, quasi Dei luce gloriatur. Sed huic David uxorem abstulit sibique conjunxit, quia manu fortis Redemptor apparens, dum de se spiritualiter legem loqui innotuit, per hoc quod juxta litteram tenebantur, hanc a Judaico populo extraneam demonstravit, sibi conjunxit, quia se per illam praedicatum declaravit. Uriam tamen ad domum monet ire, et pedes lavare, quia incarnatus Judaeis praecipit, ut ad conscientiam redeant, et sordes operum fletibus tergant; ut specialiter mandata legis intelligant, et post tantam duritiam praeceptorum, fontem baptismi invenientes, ad aquam 572Cpost laborem currant. Sed Urias, qui arcam Domini esse sub pellibus meminit, respondet, quia domum suam intrare non possit. Quod dicit: Ego mandata Dei in sacrificiis carnalibus video, et redire ad conscientiam per spiritualem intelligentiam non requiro. Quasi enim arcam Dei esse sub pellibus dicit, quia praecepta Dei non nisi exhibendo ministerium carnalis sacrificii intelligit. Hunc tamen redire domum nolentem David ad mensam vocat, quia cum Judaei ad conscientiam reverti contemnant, Christus tamen spiritualia praedicat dicens: Si crederetis Mosi, crederetis forsitan et mihi, de me enim ille scripsit (Joan. Unde et Urias ad Joab cum epistolis ex quibus occidi debeat, 572Dmittitur, quia Judaeus legem portat, qua convincente moriatur. Cum enim mandata legis retinens implere nititur, ipse defert judicium unde damnetur. Quid ergo per factum istud David scelestius? Quid Uria mundius? Sed ad mysterium: quid David sanctius? Quid Uria infidelius? Quia ille per vitae culpam prophetiae signat innocentiam; et istae per vitae innocentiam in prophetia exprimit culpam. Virtus enim sacri eloquii sic transacta narrat, ut ventura exprimat: sic in facto rem approbat, ut in mysterio contradicat. Sic gesta damnat, ut haec mystice gerenda suadeat.

But holy men keep themselves with a circumspection so much the more watchful, as they disdain to be beaten upon by sinister motions even in passing, so that, if perchance the heart should covet anything unlawful, the eye, restrained by the mastery of discipline, would refuse to see: for as temptation is often drawn in through the eyes, so sometimes what has been conceived within compels the eye without to serve it. For very often some thing or other is looked upon with an innocent mind, but in the very looking the soul is confounded by the sword of concupiscence. For David did not look upon the wife of Urias of set purpose because he had coveted her, but 571Drather he coveted because he looked incautiously. But by the just examination of retribution, he who uses the outward eye negligently is blinded in the inward eye. But often concupiscence already lords it within, and the soul, enticed, exacts after the manner of a tyranny that the bodily senses be servants to its own uses, and compels the eyes to serve its own pleasures, and opens the windows of light unto the darkness of blindness. Whence holy men, when they feel themselves beaten upon with sinister delight, restrain by the mastery of discipline those very lights through which the appearance of a form enters into the mind, lest a seductive seeing should be a servant to a depraved thought: and if this is ever neglected to be subtly guarded, the uncleanness of thought passes over into action.

572A(GREG.) Often some thing or other is a virtue in the history, a fault in the signification; just as sometimes a fault in the deed is a virtue in the writing of prophecy. For who would not detest that David, walking on the roof, coveted Bethsabee the wife of Urias, whom nevertheless, when he returned from the battle, he admonishes to go home and to wash his feet? And he straightway answered, saying: The ark of God is under skins, and shall I take my rest in my own house? Whom David received at his own table, and gave him the banquet through which he was to die. David therefore holds the figure of him of whom it is written: He hath set his tabernacle in the sun (Psal. XVIII). Who bids Bethsabee be brought forth to him, that is, to join to himself by a spiritual understanding the law of the letter that was joined to a carnal people. For Bethsabee is called a well of seven, because through the kinship of the law, by the pouring in 572Bof a special grace, perfect wisdom is ministered to us. But Urias signifies the Jewish people, who is interpreted my light of God: for the Jewish people, which is lifted up by the knowledge of the law it has received, glories as it were in the light of God. But David took away this man's wife and joined her to himself, because the Redeemer, appearing strong of hand, while he made known that the law speaks spiritually of himself, showed her to be a stranger to the Jewish people by that whereby they were held according to the letter, and joined her to himself, because he declared himself to be preached through her. Yet he admonishes Urias to go home and to wash his feet, because, being incarnate, he commands the Jews to return to their conscience, and to wipe away with weeping the filth of their works; that they may understand the commandments of the law spiritually, and, after so great a hardness of precepts, finding the fountain of baptism, may run to the water 572Cafter their labour. But Urias, who remembers that the ark of the Lord is under skins, answers that he cannot enter his own house. Which is to say: I see the commandments of God in carnal sacrifices, and I do not seek to return to my conscience through spiritual understanding. For he says as it were that the ark of God is under skins, because he understands the precepts of God not otherwise than by performing the ministry of a carnal sacrifice. Yet David calls this man, who is unwilling to go home, to his table, because, while the Jews scorn to return to their conscience, Christ nevertheless preaches spiritual things, saying: If you did believe Moses, you would perhaps believe me also, for he wrote of me (Joan. V). For the Jew holds the law, which speaks of his divinity, whom he disdains to believe. Whence Urias also is sent to Joab with letters by which he was to be slain, 572Dbecause the Jew carries the law by whose conviction he is to die. For while, holding fast the commandments of the law, he strives to fulfil them, he himself carries down the judgment whereby he is condemned. What therefore is more wicked than David by this deed? What more clean than Urias? But as to the mystery: what more holy than David? What more faithless than Urias? Because he, through the fault of his life, marks the innocence of prophecy; and this man, through the innocence of his life, expresses fault in prophecy. For the power of the sacred eloquence so narrates what is past that it expresses what is to come: so approves the thing in the deed that it contradicts it in the mystery. So it condemns things done, that it urges these same things to be done mystically.

Fortium. De his fortibus ait Josephus: Primus omnium erat Eusebius filius Achimaeae, qui aciem 577Dhostium frequenter irrumpens non cessavit donec nongentos occideret. Post hunc erat Eleazar filius Dodo, qui cum rege fuerat in Sarpha. Hic Israelitis formidantibus Philisthinorum exercitum et fugientibus solus restitit, et multos occidit, ut sanguine occisorum gladius ejus haereret in dextera. Israelitae vero videntes Palaestinos in fugam conversos, descendentes de montibus persecuti sunt eos, et pervenit Israelitis famosa victoria. Tertius fuit Semaeias filius Heli, qui in loco maxillae cum Hebraei fugerent, exercitus Palaestinorum solus portavit alios prosternens, alios in fugam convertens.

Ipse. (RAB.) Virga de radice Jesse absque nuptiali opere florem protulit, qui de uno homine typum gentium 578Apraeferente, uno impetu, id est sermonis imperio legionem expulit daemonum.

Tenerrimus ligni. (ID.) Virtus viri bellica et modesta significatur, etc., usque ad in interfectione octingentorum fortitudo.

VERS. 11.-- Semma filius. Audiens.

Age de, etc. Meditans sive loquens.

Arari. Montanus, qui gestat personam Christi sive praedicatorum ejus.

VERS. 13.-- Descenderant tres. Tres fortes supra 578Bnominati, et Jesboam filius Achamoni, quem Josephus Eusebium filium Achimeae nominat. Et Eleazar filius Ahohi, quem dicit filium esse Dodo, et Semma filius Age, quem Semeiam filium Heli vocat. Hebraei autem tres istos arbitrantur fuisse, Abisai filium Sarviae, et Sobochai Usathitem, et Jonathan filium Sammaa fratris David, qui supra memorati sunt in praeliis Philisthinorum. Cui sententiae congruere videtur quod sequitur: Abisai quoque frater Joab filius Sarviae, etc. Hinc enim apparet quod Abisai princeps erat et nominatus inter tres sequentes.

(GREG.) Cogitandum est, ut qui illicita commisit a quibusdam etiam licitis abstineat, ut per hoc conditori suo satisfaciat, ut qui commisit prohibita, abscindat sibi etiam concessa, et se reprehendat in 578Cminimis, quem meminit peccasse in maximis. Lex Veteris Testamenti uxorem alienam concupisci prohibet: a rege vero fortia juberi militibus vel desiderare aquam, non poenaliter vetat. David vero alienam uxorem concupivit et abstulit, cujus culpam digna verbera sunt secuta, et malum quod perpetravit, per poenitentiae lamenta correxit. Qui cum longe post contra hostium cuneos sedit, aquam ex eorum cisterna desideravit; cujus electi milites intra catervas hostium irrumpentes, aquam quam desideraverat, illaesi pertulerunt. Sed vir flagellis eruditus se protinus cum periculo militum aquam desiderasse reprehendit, eamque Domino fundens libavit. In sacrificium enim Domini effusa aqua conversa est, quia culpam concupiscentiae mactavit per 578Dpoenitentiam suae reprehensionis. Qui ergo concupiscere alienam conjugem non timuit, post etiam quia aquam concupisset, expavit.

VERS. 16.-- Sed libavit. Exemplum fortitudinis et constantiae militibus praebuit, vincens naturam ne sitiens biberet, ut exercitus sitim tolerare disceret.

Mystice vero aquam non sitiebat, sed nasciturum in Bethlehem de virgine Christum praevidebat: cujus lavacrum et sanguinem sitiebat. Aquam ergo non bibit, sed fudit. Sitiebat enim fontem aeternum qui non periculis quaeritur alienis, sed aliena pericula abluit.

VERS. 19.-- Tres primos. Intelligi volunt sapientiam, humilitatem et fortitudinem, David enim 579Asedit sapientissimus: ipse ligni vermiculus, id est, humilis octingentos interfecit velut fortis; ad has tres virtutes nemo fortium David pervenit.

VERS. 20.-- Et Bana. Qui erat magister de Capseel, id est, de congregatione Dei in Cerethi et Pheleti, qui interpretantur occidentes et vivificantes. Ipse percussit quos Paralipomenon duos Ariel Moab nominat. Hi ergo viri fortissimi fuerunt in regno Moab, unde et leones dicuntur. Ariel enim leo Dei interpretatur.

In media. Josephus hic apertius dicit quid fuerit, scilicet, cisterna nimis profunda, tempore hyemis nivis aggere coaequata. In hanc leo inscius decidit, et conclusus grandi rugitu clamavit. Banaias vero qui cum caeteris ad spectaculum venit, in cisternam 579Bdesiliit, et leonem interfecit. Banaias, qui interpretatur aedificator Dominus, Christum significat, qui Ecclesiam aedificat. Unde Paulus: Dei aedificatio estis. Hic in tempore nivis et frigoris, cum scilicet gratia Christi mundo resplenduit, et frigus charitatis homines refrigeravit, leonem, id est, diabolum interfecit. Hebraeus vero hunc leonem Moab intelligit, qui in medio cisternae, id est, in domo Domini, ubi tenebat cornua altaris, occisus est. Altare vero cisterna dicitur, quia sicut aqua cisternae abluit et mundat, ita sanctuarium Domini purgat peccata. In diebus nivis, quia per mortem expiavit peccatum. Unde: Lavabis me, quod impletum est in medio cisternae; et super nivem dealbabor, quod impletum est in eo quod ait diebus nivis.

579CVERS. 21.-- Virum Aegyptium. Imitatorem scilicet Aegyptii Deum blasphemantis, quem Moses jubente Domino interfecit, ille enim blasphemavit Deum, hic prophetam et regem.

Dignum. Spectabatur enim ut si Jerusalem egrederetur, interficeretur.

Hastam. Legem Dei, quam si meditatus fuisset permanendo in Jerusalem non periret, sed quia praeceptum regis contempsit, Banaias in virga, id est in rectitudine justitiae ad eum descendit, et hastam, quam non recte tenebat, extorsit.

VERS. 23.-- Qui erant. Neminem moverat quod in summa non triginta, sed septem et triginta leguntur. Triginta enim et septem leguntur hoc modo. Septem scilicet, fortes hi: David, qui dicitur Adeodatus, 579DAbisai, Sobochai, Jonathan, Eleazar, Semmaa filius Agge de Arari, et Banaias, ecce septem. Triginta autem hi sunt Asael, Eleanam et caeteri.

VERS. 39.-- Urias. Hic ultimus ponitur pro eo quod sequitur: Et addidit furor Domini, etc. Jam enim ultio facta fuerat in David et in domo ejus, sed non in populo qui vel non restitit David vel consensit.

CHAPTER XXIII.

VERS. 1.-- These however are. (HIERON.) They are the last, because after the psalter and the other measures he is said to have composed this measure, in which he says: David the son of Isai said: The man said, for whom a ladder was set up unto the Christ 577Bof the God of Jacob. [Vulg. 2 Reg. 23:1 cui constitutum est de christo Dei Jacob, "to whom it was appointed concerning the Christ of the God of Jacob"]

(RAB.) Because they signify the last praise of confession, which, the enemy being overcome and the victory of death accomplished, the saints shall sing for ever.

Excellent. Because he prophesied most brilliantly of the Christ of God and of his incarnation and the redemption of the human race.

VERS. 3.-- In the fear. He who exercises dominion over his subjects in the fear of God brings forth works of light illumined by the sun of justice, and nothing dark remains in them, but, watered by the shower of heavenly grace, living in the flesh he brings forth the shoots of the virtues as fruit.

VERS. 5.-- Nor is there. (RAB.) He who meditates in the law of the Lord day and night becomes like a tree which, planted beside the waters, gives its fruit in its own 577Ctime, and whatsoever he shall do shall prosper.

VERS. 7.-- And if any man. He, that is, who, communicating in the sins of others because of the hardness of his heart, is pressed down by the weight of iniquities, and is pierced through with the darts of sins, and his works, worthy of eternal fires, shall be brought to nothing.

VERS. 8.-- These are the names. (ISID.) A catalogue of valiant men is woven in the figure of the saints, who, although they advance in the loftiness of the virtues, yet do not reach unto the excellence of the divine Trinity. For this is what is written: Unto the first three he came not. For who among the clouds shall be equal to the Lord among the sons of God?

Of the valiant. Of these valiant men Josephus says: The first of all was Eusebius the son of Achimaea, who, frequently breaking into the battle-line 577Dof the enemy, did not cease until he had killed nine hundred. After him was Eleazar the son of Dodo, who had been with the king in Sarpha. He alone stood fast when the Israelites feared the army of the Philisthines and were fleeing, and killed many, so that with the blood of the slain his sword clave to his right hand. But the Israelites, seeing the Palestines turned to flight, came down from the mountains and pursued them, and a famous victory came to the Israelites. The third was Semaeias the son of Heli, who, in the place of the jawbone, when the Hebrews were fleeing, alone bore the armies of the Palestines, laying some low and turning others to flight.

He himself. (RAB.) The rod from the root of Jesse brought forth a flower without nuptial work, who, from one man bearing the type of the nations, 578Aby one onset, that is, by the command of a word, drove out a legion of demons.

The most tender little worm of the wood. (ID.) The warlike and modest valour of the man is signified, etc., as far as the fortitude in the slaying of eight hundred.

VERS. 11.-- Semma the son. Hearing.

Age of, etc. Meditating or speaking.

Arari. Mountainous, who bears the person of Christ or of his preachers.

VERS. 12.-- He stood. (RAB.) Christ, standing with the Holy Spirit in the field of the Church, protects the weak as barley, or rather he defends with the sword of the mouth the modesty of chastity, as though gleaners, against the incontinent, against luxury.

VERS. 13.-- Three had gone down. The three valiant men named 578Babove, and Jesboam the son of Achamoni, whom Josephus names Eusebius the son of Achimea. And Eleazar the son of Ahohi, whom he says to be the son of Dodo, and Semma the son of Age, whom he calls Semeias the son of Heli. But the Hebrews think that those three were Abisai the son of Sarvia, and Sobochai the Usathite, and Jonathan the son of Sammaa the brother of David, who were mentioned above in the battles of the Philisthines. With which opinion that which follows seems to agree: Abisai also the brother of Joab the son of Sarvia, etc. For hence it appears that Abisai was chief and named among the three that follow.

(GREG.) It is to be considered that he who has committed unlawful things should abstain even from certain lawful things, that thereby he may make satisfaction to his Creator, that he who has committed forbidden things should cut off from himself even things allowed, and should reprehend himself in 578Cthe least things, whom he remembers to have sinned in the greatest. The law of the Old Testament forbids another man's wife to be coveted: but it does not penally forbid that brave things be commanded to soldiers by a king, or that he desire water. But David coveted another man's wife and took her away, and worthy stripes followed upon his fault, and the evil which he committed he corrected by the laments of penance. And when long afterwards he sat over against the squadrons of the enemy, he desired water out of their cistern; and his chosen soldiers, breaking in among the troops of the enemy, brought back unhurt the water which he had desired. But the man, taught by scourges, at once reprehended himself for having desired water at the peril of his soldiers, and pouring it out to the Lord he offered it in libation. For the water poured out was turned into a sacrifice of the Lord, because he slew the fault of concupiscence by 578Dthe penance of his own self-reproof. He therefore who did not fear to covet another man's wife, afterwards was terrified even because he had coveted water.

VERS. 16.-- But he offered it in libation. He gave his soldiers an example of fortitude and constancy, overcoming nature so that being thirsty he did not drink, that the army might learn to endure thirst.

But mystically he did not thirst for the water, but he foresaw Christ who was to be born in Bethlehem of a virgin: for whose laver and blood he thirsted. Therefore he did not drink the water, but poured it out. For he thirsted for the eternal fountain which is not sought at the peril of others, but washes away the perils of others.

VERS. 19.-- The first three. They will have them understood to be wisdom, humility and fortitude, for David 579Asat most wise: he himself was the little worm of the wood, that is, humble, and he killed eight hundred as one valiant; unto these three virtues none of David's valiant men attained.

VERS. 20.-- And Bana. Who was master of Capseel, that is, of the congregation of God in Cerethi and Pheleti, who are interpreted killing and making alive. He himself slew those whom Paralipomenon names the two Ariel of Moab. These men therefore were most valiant in the kingdom of Moab, whence they are also called lions. For Ariel is interpreted the lion of God.

In the midst. Josephus here says more openly what it was, namely, a cistern exceeding deep, which in the time of winter was levelled up with a heap of snow. Into this a lion fell unknowing, and, shut in, cried out with a great roaring. But Banaias, who came with the others to the sight, leapt down into the cistern 579Band killed the lion. Banaias, who is interpreted the Lord is builder, signifies Christ, who builds the Church. Whence Paul: You are God's building. He, in the time of snow and cold, that is, when the grace of Christ shone forth upon the world, and the cold of charity chilled men, killed the lion, that is, the devil. But the Hebrew understands this lion to be Moab, who was slain in the midst of the cistern, that is, in the house of the Lord, where he held the horns of the altar. But the altar is called a cistern, because as the water of a cistern washes and cleanses, so the sanctuary of the Lord purges sins. In the days of snow, because he expiated sin by death. Whence: Thou shalt wash me, which was fulfilled in the midst of the cistern; and I shall be made whiter than snow, which was fulfilled in that he says in the days of snow.

579CVERS. 21.-- An Egyptian man. That is, an imitator of the Egyptian who blasphemed God, whom Moses killed at the Lord's bidding, for that man blasphemed God, this man a prophet and a king.

Worthy. For he was looked upon as one who, if he went out of Jerusalem, would be killed.

A spear. The law of God, on which if he had meditated, remaining in Jerusalem, he would not have perished, but because he despised the king's command, Banaias came down to him with a rod, that is, in the uprightness of justice, and wrested away the spear which he was not holding rightly.

VERS. 23.-- Who were. It had moved no one that in the sum not thirty but thirty-seven are read. For thirty and seven are read in this manner. Seven, that is, these valiant men: David, who is called Adeodatus, 579DAbisai, Sobochai, Jonathan, Eleazar, Semmaa the son of Agge of Arari, and Banaias, behold, seven. And the thirty are these, Asael, Eleanam and the rest.

VERS. 39.-- Urias. He is put last for that which follows: And the fury of the Lord added, etc. For vengeance had already been taken upon David and upon his house, but not upon the people, who either did not withstand David or consented.

CHAPTER XXIV.

(GREG., lib. XXV Moral., cap. 14.) According to the qualities 580Aof the subjects, etc., as far as that, as the deeds of the masters displease, so the mind of the subjects may not withdraw from reverence for the mastership.

(HIERON.) It is to be noted that in Paralipomenon it is read: A thousand thousand a hundred thousand of Israel, and of Juda forty seventy thousand, [Vulg. 1 Par. 21:5 quadringenta septuaginta millia, "four hundred and seventy thousand"] whom it is to be understood that Joab numbered, but that he was unwilling to show any but as many as are written in the book of Samuel.

VERS. 10.-- But his heart smote him, etc. (AMBR., lib. de Poen., cap. 9.) He betook himself to the greater punishment that he might be able to call forth clemency. For the rigour of justice is softened, etc., as far as and the shaking poured forth from heaven upon the people might rest.

VERS. 11.-- The word of the Lord. (RAB.) In Exodus it is written, etc., as far as there were consumed seventy 580Bthousand.

VERS. 13.-- Either. And it seems that they who are not fed with the bread of the word of God through the grace of the sevenfold Spirit endure famine for seven years, and they who have not been regenerated in the name of the Trinity are not strong enough to withstand the enemies, that is, the demons; but the unbelieving die of the pestilence of impiety.

VERS. 16.-- And there was. (RAB.) In Paralipomenon also it is written: Now the angel of the Lord stood by the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite. And David, lifting up his eyes, saw the angel of the Lord between heaven and earth, with a drawn sword in his hand, and turned against Jerusalem. And they fell, both he and the elders, clothed in haircloth, prostrate on the ground, beside the threshing floor. The threshing floor of Areuna is the Church of the Gentiles, in which, after 580Cthe true David raised up the altar of faith, the death of impiety at once gave way. This threshing floor David bought for fifty sicles: because Christ, through the grace of the sevenfold Spirit, all offences being let go, sanctified the Gentile world into an everlasting house for himself.

VERS. 18.-- Areuna. (ID.) It is interpreted a threshing floor: but Ornan, a light to us. Rightly therefore David, wishing to appease the Lord, is bidden to set up an altar in the threshing floor of Areuna, or of Orna, because God is not otherwise appeased by man, unless in the threshing floor of the heart, through the light of right faith and of true love, he set up the altar of humility, in which he may offer the sacrifice of oblation and of praise, whence: The sacrifice of praise shall glorify me, etc. (Psal. XLIX). A sacrifice to God is an afflicted spirit, etc. (Ibid. L). The threshing floor of Areuna, or of Orna, is Jerusalem itself.

580DVERS. 25.-- And he offered holocausts. Hence in Paralipomenon the tabernacle which Moses had made in the desert and the altar of holocausts were at that season in the high place of Gabaon, and David was not strong enough to go to the altar, to beseech the Lord. For he had been terrified with exceeding fear, seeing the sword of the angel of the Lord. And David said: This is the house of God, etc. In this place, according to the Hebrews, Abraham wished to offer his son, when a ram suddenly appeared: which he took and offered instead of his son.
