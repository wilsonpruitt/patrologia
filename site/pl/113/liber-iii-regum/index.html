Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

CAPUT PRIMUM.

  • 581A

VERS. 1.-- Et rex David senuerat. (HIERON., epist. ad Nepotianum, tom. I.) Nonne tibi videtur si occidentem sequaris litteram, figmentum esse vel atellanarum ludicra? Frigidus senex obvolvitur vestimentis, et nisi complexu adolescentulae non tepescit. Vivebat adhuc Bethsabee, etc., usque ad et Raab meretrix in typo Ecclesiae reticulam mysterium sanguinis continentem, ut Jericho pereunte domus ejus salvaretur, appendit, unde de sanctis dicitur: Hi sunt qui venerunt de calore domus Rechab (I Par. II). Et alibi: Ignem veni mittere in terram (Luc. XII), etc., qui scilicet in discipulorum corde succensus cogebat eos dicere: Nonne cor nostrum ardens erat (Ibid., XXIV)?

Cumque operiretur, etc. David nimium frigus pertulit, 581Bvel quia de senioribus natus est parentibus, vel quia multum in praelio sanguinem fuderat, vel potius ex quo angelum caedentem populum vidit, pavore vehementi contabuit, unde in incommoditate frigoris usque ad mortem permansit.

VERS. 5.-- Adonias autem. Judaicum populum significat, qui major filius esse videtur, quia prior legem suscepit, et gentilem populum, qui per gratiam Dei posterior vocatus est, spernens, se solum cum Domino regnare putavit. Adonias enim dominator dominus interpretatur. Sed Ecclesiae populus cum vero Salomone, id est pacifico nostro, cujus ipse corpus est, per divinam ordinationem in regnum substituitur, de quo merito perfidiae major filius, id est prior populus, praecipitatur.

CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- And king David had grown old. (HIERON., epist. ad Nepotianum, tom. I.) Does it not seem to thee, if thou follow the killing letter, to be a fiction, or the sports of the Atellan farces? A cold old man is wrapped about with garments, and save by the embrace of a young girl he grows not warm. Bethsabee was yet living, etc., as far as and Rahab the harlot, in a figure of the Church, hung out the little net containing the mystery of blood, that when Jericho perished her house might be saved, whence it is said of the saints: These are they that came of the heat of the house of Rechab (I Par. II). And elsewhere: I am come to send fire upon the earth (Luc. XII), etc., which fire, kindled in the heart of the disciples, constrained them to say: Was not our heart burning (Ibid., XXIV)?

And when he was covered, etc. David suffered cold exceedingly, 581Beither because he was born of aged parents, or because he had poured out much blood in battle, or rather because, from the time when he saw the angel striking the people, he wasted away with vehement dread, whence he remained in the discomfort of cold even unto death.

VERS. 5.-- But Adonias. He signifies the Jewish people, who seems to be the elder son, because he received the law first, and who, despising the Gentile people, which was called later through the grace of God, thought to reign alone with the Lord. For Adonias is interpreted ruler lord. But the people of the Church is set in the kingdom in his stead, together with the true Solomon, that is, with our peaceable one, whose body it is, by divine ordinance, and from that kingdom the elder son, that is, the former people, is cast headlong by the desert of his perfidy.

VERS. 5.-- Thou also. David gave charge to Solomon concerning the just requital of those who had been a solace to him, or had unjustly harmed him, whom he himself had patiently borne. And the prophecy indicates what evil or what good must come to pass in time to come, which the true Solomon will fulfil. Yet meanwhile the patience of God often grants that the good be afflicted and the reprobate enjoy delights.

VERS. 19.-- Bethsabee therefore came to the king. (RAB.) Adonias, by the intervention of Bethsabee, etc., as far as nor the law which the Jews suppose to be their helper with God.

VERS. 36.-- And thou shalt not go out, etc. The true Solomon commands us who dwell in Jerusalem never to 581Dgo out. But if they who were before subject to us have fled from us, let us not on that account go out to follow the footsteps of the fugitives, lest, while we wish to save them that flee, we ourselves perish, but rather let the dead bury their dead, and let us, while it is lawful, pluck out and cut off from us the eye, the hand, the foot that scandalizeth.

Inter duas mulieres Dominus, dum id quod justum est spiritu oris sui dirimit, unicuique quod debetur restituit.

VERS. 24.-- Dixit ergo. (RAB.) Simulans ignorantiam humanos pro dispensatione carnis metitur affectus, sicut et ibi: Ubi posuisti eum (Joan. XX)? Et alibi: Quis me tetigit (Luc. VIII)?

VERS. 25.-- Dividite. (ID.) Tentat naturam naturae Dominus, et vult secundum utriusque voluntatem, viventem filium, in legem gratiamque dividere: non quod probet, sed ad arguendam Synagogae calumniam hoc se velle dicit.

VERS. 26.-- Dixit autem. (ID.) Ecclesia, quae 583Bscit suum esse, libenter concedit aemulae, ut vivat saltem apud eam, ne inter legem divisus et gratiam, Christi mucrone feriatur, unde: Ecce ego Paulus dico vobis, quia si legem observatis, Christus vobis nihil proderit (Galat. V).

VERS. 4.-- Solomon therefore went. In Gabaon was the greatest high place, where was the tabernacle which Moses made, and the brazen altar, upon which Solomon, offering a thousand victims, deserved a divine oracle.

VERS. 14.-- But if thou wilt walk in my ways, etc. Without the keeping of God's commandments no man can attain long days, that is, blessedness.

(GREG., lib. VIII Moral., cap. 17.) After the Lord lights up the darkness of our error with the light of his own knowledge, etc., as far as that we may be reformed unto the substance of the incorruption that is promised.

(HIER., epist. 131, tom. I.) Some think that this is to be understood of the Synagogue and the Church, etc., as far as it is also to be noted what the Church saith 582Bagainst the Synagogue.

VERS. 17.-- I and the woman. Because after the resurrection of the Saviour one Church was gathered out of both peoples. And elegantly he added: I brought forth beside her in the chamber. For the Church of the Gentiles, which had not the law and the prophets, brought forth in the house of the synagogue, and went not out of the chamber, but entered in. Whence: The king brought me into his chamber (Cant. II) [Vulg. in cellaria sua, "into his storerooms" (Cant. 1:3)]. And: I will bring thee into the house of my mother, and into the chamber of her that conceived me (Ibid., III).

(RAB.) If thou considerest Pilate washing his hands and saying: I am clean from the blood of this just man (Matth. XXVII) [Vulg. Innocens, "innocent" (Matt. 27:24)]: and the centurion confessing: Truly this was the Son of God (Marc. XV), and those who before the passion desired through Philip to see the Lord, 582Cthou doubtest not that the Church brought forth first; and afterward that the people of the Jews was born, for whom the Lord prayed: Father, forgive them, etc., and in one day three thousand believed, and another five thousand.

(GREG.) By the nursing mothers the order of teachers, etc., as far as they themselves receive their sons whole and living, when in the last judgment they obtain a perfect recompense out of their life.

VERS. 19.-- Dead. (RAB.) For while she follows the observance of the law and couples the grace of the Gospel to the law, she is covered over with the error of darkness.

VERS. 20.-- She took the son. The living one, that is: not that she might possess him, but that she might kill him. For she did this not out of love of the son, but out of hatred of her rival.

VERS. 21.-- Whom beholding more diligently, etc. (RAB.) 582DIt were long to show how through Paul and other churchmen the Church understood that he was not her son who was feared in the law, and in the light knew him whom in the darkness she did not see. The two women, of whom the one burned with love, while in the other dissimulation crept in, figure the Church, and the Synagogue or heretical 583Adepravity, of whom each both slays her own by nourishing them wickedly, and by alluring persuades those of another until she destroy them. Between the two women the Lord, while by the spirit of his mouth he decides what is just, restores to each what is owed.

VERS. 24.-- He said therefore. (RAB.) Feigning ignorance, he measures human affections according to the dispensation of the flesh, as also there: Where have you laid him (Joan. XX)? And elsewhere: Who hath touched me (Luc. VIII)?

VERS. 25.-- Divide. (ID.) The Lord tries nature by nature, and wills, according to the will of them both, to divide the living son into law and grace: not that he approves it, but he says that he wills this in order to convict the calumny of the Synagogue.

VERS. 26.-- But she said. (ID.) The Church, who 583Bknows him to be her own, willingly yields him to her rival, that he may live at least with her, lest, divided between law and grace, he be smitten with the sword of Christ, whence: Behold, I Paul say to you, that if you observe the law, Christ shall profit you nothing (Galat. V) [Vulg. si circumcidamini, "if you be circumcised" (Gal. 5:2)].

CAPUT IV.

VERS. 22.-- Cibus Salomonis. (RAB.) Refectio Christi, qui pascitur recta fide et operibus bonis, quae illi quotidie offeruntur ab Ecclesia catholica.

VERS. 23.-- Viginti boves. (ID.) Praedicatores Novi Testamenti, qui quasi geminatum Decalogum in se habent, cum Novum et Vetus Testamentum aequaliter praedicant pleni dilectione Dei et proximi.

Excepta venatione. Venatione diversorum animalium 583Csunt qui ex diversis gentibus quotidie retibus Evangelii per spirituales venatores capiuntur, et in pastum Salvatoris rediguntur.

VERS. 26.-- Quadraginta, etc. Per quadraginta tempora priscae legis designantur, quam Moses accepturus jejunavit quadraginta diebus, et populus qui ea instruebatur, quadraginta annis in deserto fuit.

Et duodecim. Hic numerus praesens tempus significat, in quo apostolica doctrina Novi Testamenti populum nutrit.

VERS. 28.-- Hordeum, etc. Ne quid desit in domo regis, ordo praedicatorum scribendo et loquendo laborat, ut in mensa Domini abundet, unde nutrientur fideles.

583DVERS. 32.-- Et fuerunt carmina ejus. Constat neque carmina neque disputationes Salomonis hodie esse.

Quinque millia. Propter quinque sensus, quos, qui in diversis virtutibus bene regit, beatus est, et per eos quasi carmina quinque millia Domino canit.

VERS. 33.-- Hyssopum. (RAB.) Hyssopus, herba humilis et saxo haerens, etc., usque ad tunc enim cedrum, id est, arrogantiam saeculi inclinavit usque ad humilitatem hyssopi, id est, usque ad stultitiam crucis.

Et disseruit. (ID.) Omnia enim nuda et aperta sunt Christo (Hebr. IV). Disputat autem, cum singulorum rationem nobis proponit in Scripturis suis, 584Amanifestatque sacramenta, quae fuerunt abscondita a saeculis et generationibus, ut consideremus beatitudinem angelorum, utilitatem hominum, astutiam daemonum, et, reprobantes quod malum est, sectemur quod bonum est.

CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 22.-- The food of Solomon. (RAB.) The refreshment of Christ, who is fed with right faith and with good works, which are daily offered to him by the Catholic Church.

VERS. 23.-- Twenty oxen. (ID.) The preachers of the New Testament, who have as it were a doubled Decalogue in themselves, when they preach the New and the Old Testament equally, being full of the love of God and of their neighbour.

Besides the venison. By the hunting of divers animals 583Care meant those who out of divers nations are daily taken by the nets of the Gospel through spiritual hunters, and are brought in for the food of the Saviour.

VERS. 26.-- Forty, etc. By forty are designated the seasons of the ancient law, which Moses fasted forty days that he might receive, and the people that was instructed in it was forty years in the desert.

And twelve. This number signifies the present time, in which the apostolic doctrine of the New Testament nourishes the people.

VERS. 28.-- Barley, etc. That nothing be wanting in the house of the king, the order of preachers labours by writing and by speaking, that there may be abundance upon the table of the Lord, whence the faithful shall be nourished.

583DVERS. 32.-- And his canticles were. It is agreed that neither the canticles nor the disputations of Solomon are extant today.

Five thousand. [Vulg. quinque et mille, "a thousand and five" (III Reg. 4:32)] Because of the five senses, whereof he who governs them well in divers virtues is blessed, and through them sings as it were five thousand canticles to the Lord.

VERS. 33.-- Hyssop. (RAB.) Hyssop, a lowly herb and cleaving to the rock, etc., as far as for then he bowed down the cedar, that is, the arrogance of the world, even to the humility of hyssop, that is, even to the foolishness of the cross.

And he treated. (ID.) For all things are naked and open to Christ (Hebr. IV) [Vulg. oculis ejus, "to his eyes" (Heb. 4:13)]. And he disputes when he sets before us the reason of each several thing in his Scriptures, 584Aand makes manifest the sacraments which were hidden from ages and generations, that we may consider the blessedness of the angels, the usefulness of men, the cunning of the demons, and, reproving what is evil, may follow after what is good.

CAPUT V.

VERS. 3.-- Et quia non potuerit aedificare domum, etc. (BEDA, quaest. in lib. Reg.) Domus quam Salomon aedificavit, Ecclesiae figura fuit, etc., usque ad in quibusdam remunerata cum angelis hominum certamina demonstret.

(ID., lib. de templo Salom., cap. 1.) Hanc domum spiritualem etiam tabernaculum in eremo factum significat, etc., usque ad et salutem omnium gentium in Christo multis modis ostendat et figuret.

584BVERS. 6.-- Praecipe igitur ut praecidant mihi, etc. (RAB.) Servi Hiram praecidentes cedros Salomoni de Libano, etc., usque ad melius gentilium errores noverant, et ideo artificiosius expugnabant.

Scis enim quomodo non est in populo meo vir qui noverit ligna, etc. (BEDA, ut supra. ) Dicendum est prius de operariis templi, qui vel unde fuerunt, et de ipsa materia, etc., usque ad triticum, scilicet, verbi Dei, oleum charitatis, unctionis et Spiritus sancti illuminationis.

VERS. 10.-- Itaque Hiram dabat Salomoni ligna cedrina et ligna abiegna, juxta omnem, etc. Tyrus, unde rex Hiram, insula est dedita negotiationi, ubi erant optimi fabricatores lignorum, qui dabant ligna pretiosa accipientes cibaria. Vivunt enim non agrum 584Ccolendo, sed negotiando. Ibi est mons Libanus, de cujus lignis facta est domus Domini. Unde et Libanus dicitur ibi: Aperi, Libane, portas tuas (Zach. XI).

VERS. 13.-- Elegitque rex Salomon. (BEDA, ut supra, cap. 3.) Non frustra operarios de omni Israel elegit, etc., usque ad in qua mens fidelium a Domino quotidiana illustratione quasi luna a sole respicitur.

Operarios de omni Israel, et erat indictio triginta millia virorum. Hi operatores erant proselyti. Horum magistri de Judaeis, quia non fecit quemquam servire de Israel.

VERS. 14.-- Adoniram erat super hujuscemodi indictionem. (BEDA, ibid.) Dominus meus excelsus. Hic est Christus, qui operariis templi praeponitur, etc., usque ad ut orationibus et jejuniis superno inspectore 584Det visitatore digna sit.

VERS. 15.-- Fuerunt itaque Salomoni septuaginta millia eorum, qui onera portabant, et octoginta millia latomorum in monte. (BEDA, ibid.) Nec praetereundum quod septuaginta et octoginta millia portantium onera et latomorum cum praepositis suis non fuerunt Israelitae, etc., usque ad Quidam utraque virtute praediti ad opus domus Domini conveniunt; unde: Corripite inquietos, consolamini pusillanimes, etc. (I Thes. V).

Latomorum. (BEDA, ibid.). Latomi sunt caesores lapidum, etc., usque ad quia eruti de potestate tenebrarum ad arcem virtutum quae est in unitate Ecclesiae pervenimus.

585AVERS. 16.-- Absque praepositis. Praepositi autem sunt sacrae Scripturae conditores quorum magisterio crudimur inscios docere, contemptores corripere, et invicem onera nostra portare.

Trium millium. Propter fidem Trinitatis, quam sancta eloquia praedicant. Quod in Paralipomenis tria millia sexcenti scripti sunt, ad perfectionem eorum respicit. Senarius enim, in quo mundi completus est ornatus, perfecta bonorum opera significat: et quia sancta Scriptura cum fide veritatis opera justitiae docet habenda, recte praepositi operum templi tria millia et sexcenti fuerunt.

VERS. 17.-- Praecepitque rex ut tollerent. (BEDA, ibid.) Ad aedificandum domum Domini primo ligna et lapides de monte caeduntur, etc., usque ad unde: 585BSuperaedificati super fundamentum apostolorum et prophetarum (Ephes. II).

Lapides grandes. (BEDA, ibid., cap. 4.) Vel lapides pretiosi, grandes, quadrati, primi sunt magistri, qui ab ipso Domino audiere verbum salutis. Superpositi vero ordines lapidum vel lignorum sequentes sacerdotes vel doctores, quorum praedicatione crescit Ecclesia et ornatur virtutibus.

(RAB. ex Bed.) Quali autem colore fuerunt lapides, etc., usque ad marmor candidum ex quo constructa est domus electorum, actionem mundam et conscientiam ab omni naevo corruptionis castigatam significat; unde: Mundemus nos ab omni inquinamento, etc. (II Cor. VII).

In fundamentum templi. (BEDA, ibid.) Fundamentum 585Cest Christus, etc., usque ad ut abjectis verbis, factis cogitationibusque supervacuis, ad portandum onus Ecclesiae digni fiant.

VERS. 18.-- Porro Giblii praeparaverunt ligna et lapides. (RAB. ex Bed., ibid.) Giblos est civitas Phoenicis. etc., usque ad nec sanctuarium Domino, sed ruinam sibi aedificat, qui docere praesumit quod ipse non didicit.

Ad aedificandum domum. (ID., ibid.) Post fundamentum de talibus compositum, aedificanda est domus praeparatis lignis et lapidibus, et ordine collocatis, qui de suo situ vel abstracti sunt, quia post prima fidei rudimenta, post collata in nobis juxta exemplum sublimium virorum fundamenta humilitatis, addendus est in altum paries bonorum operum, 585Det quasi superpositis sibi invicem ordinibus lapidum proficiendum de virtute in virtutem.

CHAPTER V.

VERS. 3.-- And because he could not build a house, etc. (BEDA, quaest. in lib. Reg.) The house which Solomon built was a figure of the Church, etc., as far as it may show in certain men the contests of men rewarded together with the angels.

(ID., lib. de templo Salom., cap. 1.) This spiritual house the tabernacle also that was made in the desert signifies, etc., as far as and may in many ways show forth and figure the salvation of all nations in Christ.

584BVERS. 6.-- Give order therefore that they cut down for me, etc. (RAB.) The servants of Hiram cutting down cedars of Libanus for Solomon, etc., as far as they knew the errors of the Gentiles better, and therefore assailed them more skilfully.

For thou knowest how there is not among my people a man that knoweth timber, etc. (BEDA, ut supra. ) First must be spoken of the workmen of the temple, who or whence they were, and of the material itself, etc., as far as wheat, that is, of the word of God, the oil of charity, of unction and of the illumination of the Holy Spirit.

VERS. 10.-- And so Hiram gave Solomon cedar trees and fir trees, according to all, etc. Tyre, whence came king Hiram, is an island given over to trading, where were the best workers in timber, who gave precious timber and received victuals. For they live not by 584Ctilling the field, but by trading. There is mount Libanus, of whose timber the house of the Lord was made. Whence also Libanus is spoken of there: Open thy gates, O Libanus (Zach. XI).

VERS. 13.-- And king Solomon chose. (BEDA, ut supra, cap. 3.) Not in vain did he choose workmen out of all Israel, etc., as far as in which the mind of the faithful is looked upon by the Lord with a daily enlightening, as the moon by the sun.

Workmen out of all Israel, and the levy was thirty thousand men. These workmen were proselytes. Their masters were of the Jews, because he made not any man of Israel to serve.

VERS. 14.-- Adoniram was over this kind of levy. (BEDA, ibid.) My lord is exalted. This is Christ, who is set over the workmen of the temple, etc., as far as that by prayers and fastings it may be worthy of the beholder 584Dand visitor from on high.

VERS. 15.-- And so Solomon had seventy thousand of them that carried burdens, and eighty thousand stone-hewers in the mountain. (BEDA, ibid.) Nor is it to be passed over that the seventy and the eighty thousand of them that bore burdens and of the stone-hewers with their overseers were not Israelites, etc., as far as Certain men endowed with both virtues come together to the work of the house of the Lord; whence: Rebuke the unquiet, comfort the feeble-minded, etc. (I Thes. V).

Of the stone-hewers. (BEDA, ibid.). Stone-hewers are cutters of stones, etc., as far as because, plucked out from the power of darkness, we come to the citadel of the virtues which is in the unity of the Church.

585AVERS. 16.-- Besides the overseers. Now the overseers are the founders of holy Scripture, by whose teaching we are trained to teach the ignorant, to rebuke the despisers, and to bear one another's burdens.

Of three thousand. Because of the faith of the Trinity, which the holy oracles preach. That in Paralipomenon three thousand six hundred are written, has regard to their perfection. For the senary number, in which the adornment of the world was completed, signifies the perfect works of the good: and because holy Scripture teaches that the works of justice are to be had together with the faith of the truth, rightly were the overseers of the works of the temple three thousand and six hundred.

VERS. 17.-- And the king commanded that they should take. (BEDA, ibid.) For the building of the house of the Lord, timber and stones are first cut down from the mountain, etc., as far as whence: 585BBuilt upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets (Ephes. II).

Great stones. (BEDA, ibid., cap. 4.) Or precious stones, great and squared, are the first masters, who heard the word of salvation from the Lord himself. But the courses of stones or of timbers set above are the priests or teachers that follow, by whose preaching the Church grows and is adorned with virtues.

(RAB. ex Bed.) But of what colour the stones were, etc., as far as the white marble, of which the house of the elect is constructed, signifies action that is clean and a conscience chastened from every blemish of corruption; whence: Let us cleanse ourselves from all defilement, etc. (II Cor. VII).

Into the foundation of the temple. (BEDA, ibid.) The foundation 585Cis Christ, etc., as far as that, casting away superfluous words, deeds and thoughts, they may be made worthy to bear the burden of the Church.

All the works, etc. (GREG.) VERS. 3.-- Elias therefore was afraid, and rising up he went away, etc. (RAB.) Holy men, the spirit lifting them up, are caught away to the highest things: but so long as they are in this life, lest they be proud, they are held down by temptations. Hence it is that Elias, when he had gone forward with so many virtues, afterwards fled from Jezabel, a queen indeed, yet a mere woman: and he who raised the dead, foresaw things to come, did other glorious things also, struck with fear, flees death from the hand of a woman, seeks death from the hand of God, and receives it not.

608AVERS. 5.-- And behold an angel of the Lord touched him. (RAB.) The angel who fed Elias signifies the angel of great counsel; by whose work we subsist both in bodily and in spiritual nature.

VERS. 6.-- And behold at his head, etc. (ID.) Elias twice fed expresses the infirmity of our nature, for whom a single feeding does not suffice, but a double, that we may be able to ascend to things above. For as the body cannot subsist without bodily food, so neither can the soul live without the word of God.

(ID.) So we also, when we shake off from us the sleep of sloth, must needs, being strengthened by divine comfort, hasten with all our might, by the step of good works, through all the time of the present life, to ascend into the mountain of God and into his holy place, that there we may find 608Beternal rest.

Not in the wind. That is, think not that the Lord comes with the wind, but after the wind of commotion, that is, the whirlwind and the rush of the wind, fire shall pass by, then a whistling of a thin air, after this the Lord shall pass by, that he may speak with thee.

VERS. 14.-- I only, etc. (RAB.) What was hard for so great a prophet to know, that servants had remained to God in this world? But he who, being humble, had known even the hidden things of God, being lifted up knew not even the open things. Whence it is certain that he enlightens himself with a ray of humility who subtly weighs the good things of others: because while 608Che beholds outwardly, and in others, the things which he himself has done, he presses down that swelling of pride which strives to break forth within out of singularity. Hence it is that by the voice of God it is said to Elias, who reckoned himself alone. I have left me seven thousand men, [Vulg. III Reg. 19:18 Et derelinquam mihi in Israel septem millia virorum, "And I will leave me seven thousand men in Israel"] that while he knew that not he alone had remained, he might bow down the glory of elation which was rising in him out of singularity.

(RAB.) Not otherwise, save that he foretold that he should be king. Eliseus not otherwise than by casting his mantle upon him. Those two kings neither he himself in person, nor Eliseus his disciple, anointed, but a certain one of the prophets was sent to anoint Jehu.

VERS. 17.-- Whosoever. (ID.) The guilty, divine justice makes some to be punished by kings with the sword, others it pierces through by prophets and priests with the sword of the tongue.

608DVERS. 19.-- He departed. When our Redeemer, coming down from heaven, by the divine judgment acquired a people still gaping after earthly works, in whom he wrought salvation, when he converted him to the faith. For Elias is interpreted the Lord God, Saphat judging, Eliseus the salvation of my God. Upon whom the prophet cast his mantle, when the Lord clothed the people with the catholic faith. Whence the Apostle: You who are baptized in Christ, have put on Christ (Gal. III). Leaving the oxen he ran after Elias: because the choir of the elect, having heard, unless a man renounce all things, he cannot be my disciple (Luc. XIV), straightway ceased to gape after earthly gains, to serve secular desires, and so preached the word of life 609Ato others, for this is to kiss father and mother, to will to correct by speech whomsoever one can, whether of the Jews or of the Gentiles.

(RAB.) The Christian people also, slaying carnal desires, turns its whole army into the work of evangelizing, whence it may be able to furnish sufficient food to its helpers.

VERS. 21.-- He followed. (ID.) He follows our Elias and ministers to him who, following his footsteps, that is, keeping his commandments, busies himself to render worthily ministry and honour.

CAPUT XX.

(RAB.) Benadad significat diabolum, qui diversos exercitus malignorum spirituum ad subvertendum 609Bpopulum Dei contrahit, sed per pueros principum Israel vincitur, id est, per bonos auditores doctorum, quia quod auribus audiunt, factis implent. Antiquus hostis in fugam vertitur, omnisque suus exercitus a militibus Christi perturbatur, qui bene ducenta millia et triginta duo esse dicuntur, quia qui utriusque Testamenti scientiam perfecte tenent, et Trinitatis fidem cum gemina charitate conservant, hi apti militiae summi regis esse comprobantur.

VERS. 10.-- Haec faciant mihi. Samaria morte civitatum habebat terram interius ipsis muris pene aequalem, quo subsidio ictibus arietis resisteret extra, murorum altitudo longe superficiem terrae transcenderat. Ait ergo rex superbus obsessos terrens, quod tantam haberet multitudinem, ut si quisque 609Cmilitum unum lapidem vel stipitem vel cespitem ad construendum contra urbem aggerem apportasset, agger exsurgeret qui superficiei quae erat intra muros esse videretur aequalis, ita ut ex aequo pugnantes contra civitatem tela faces mitterent.

Ne glorietur accinctus. (RAB.) Accinctus est qui cingulo circumdatur, discinctus qui cingulum deposuit. Ait ergo rex Israel, regi Syriae glorianti quasi jam cepisset Samariam quam obsidere coeperat: Ne glorietur accinctus aeque ut discinctus. Ac si dicat: Noli gloriari quasi victor, qui adhuc in acie positus, quem victoria sequatur ignoras: quippe qui vincere credidit, victus est.

VERS. 20.-- Fugit quoque. (RAB.) Id est, diabolus princeps iniquorum, quorum oculi sublimes sunt. 609DSyria enim sublimis in equo suae superbiae confidens cum equitibus scilicet omnibus superbis, quia ipse est caput super omnes filios superbiae, ab exercitu Christi superatus in fugam vertitur, et rex Israel percutit equos et currus, etc., quia rex regum nequitias spirituales obruit humani generis delendo peccata.

(ID.) Diabolus licet a sanctis saepius vincatur, tamen 610Aiterum instaurat praelium contra eos, et dum uno modo vincitur, alio statim vincere conatur

(ID.) Maligni spiritus si in spiritualibus vincuntur, in corporalibus bellum parant, satagentes ut animas de supernis ad ima praecipitent, quo facilius vincant. Si viderint coelestia desiderare, terrena ad amandum ingerunt. Si prosperitate concessa Deo gratiae agentur, student ut per adversa frangantur. Sed sicut Syri, ita daemones ubi se superare confidebant, ibi superati sunt.

VERS. 38.-- Abiit ergo. Michaeas parabola usus est propter sermones quos regi erat dicturus, ut ostenderet hoc ad illum magis quam ad se pertinere.

VERS. 39.-- Erit anima. Providendum est servis Dei, ne secundum exemplum Achab perniciosa 610Bsecuritate sibi blandiantur, quandiu hostis vivit, ne dum se quasi victores arbitrentur per fraudem diaboli pacem promittentis citius, elidantur: quia sicut tunc propheta regi pro inconsiderata pietate pronuntiavit affore ultionem, ita nunc propheticus sermo pronuntiat, si paciscamur cum diabolo, aeternam nobis per hoc imminere poenam.

VERS. 43.-- Et furibundus. Iratus Achab prophetam clausum servari jussit, et confusus verbis Michaeae domum remeavit.

CHAPTER XX.

(RAB.) Benadad signifies the devil, who draws together divers armies of malignant spirits for the overthrowing 609Bof the people of God, but is overcome by the servants of the princes of Israel, that is, by the good hearers of teachers, because what they hear with their ears they fulfil in deeds. The ancient enemy is turned to flight, and all his army is thrown into confusion by the soldiers of Christ, who are well said to be two hundred thousand and thirty-two, because they who perfectly hold the knowledge of both Testaments, and keep the faith of the Trinity with a twofold charity, these are approved to be fit for the warfare of the highest king.

VERS. 10.-- These things may the gods do to me. Samaria, by the death [morte; read more, "after the manner"] of cities, had ground within almost level with the walls themselves, by which support it might withstand outside the strokes of the ram, and the height of the walls had far overtopped the surface of the ground. The proud king therefore said, terrifying the besieged, that he had so great a multitude that, if each 609Cone of the soldiers had brought one stone or stake or turf to build a mound against the city, a mound would rise up which would seem to be level with the surface that was within the walls, so that fighting on equal ground they might cast darts and firebrands against the city.

Let not the girded man glory. (RAB.) He is girded who is encompassed with a girdle, ungirded who has laid the girdle aside. The king of Israel therefore said to the king of Syria, glorying as though he had already taken Samaria which he had begun to besiege: Let not the girded man glory equally as the ungirded. As if he should say: Glory not as a conqueror, thou who, still set in the line of battle, knowest not whom victory will follow: since he who believed that he should conquer, was conquered.

VERS. 20.-- And he fled. (RAB.) That is, the devil, the prince of the wicked, whose eyes are lofty. 609DFor Syria the lofty, trusting in the horse of its pride, with horsemen, namely, all proud ones, because he himself is the head over all the children of pride, being overcome by the army of Christ is turned to flight, and the king of Israel strikes the horses and chariots, etc., because the King of kings overwhelms the spiritual wickednesses by blotting out the sins of the human race.

(ID.) The devil, although he is often overcome by the saints, yet 610Aagain renews the battle against them, and while he is overcome in one way, straightway he strives to conquer in another

(ID.) The malignant spirits, if they are overcome in spiritual things, prepare war in bodily things, busying themselves to cast souls headlong from the things above to the lowest, that they may the more easily conquer. If they see them desire heavenly things, they thrust earthly things upon them to be loved. If, prosperity being granted, thanks shall be rendered to God, they strive that they may be broken by adversities. But as the Syrians, so the demons, where they trusted to overcome, there were overcome.

VERS. 38.-- He went therefore. Micheas used a parable because of the words which he was about to say to the king, that he might show that this pertained rather to him than to himself.

VERS. 39.-- Thy life shall be for his life. It must be provided for by the servants of God that they flatter not themselves after the example of Achab with a pernicious 610Bsecurity, so long as the enemy lives, lest, while they reckon themselves as it were conquerors, they be the more quickly dashed down through the fraud of the devil promising peace: because as then the prophet declared to the king that vengeance would be at hand for his inconsiderate pity, so now the prophetic word declares that, if we make a covenant with the devil, eternal punishment hangs over us thereby.

VERS. 43.-- And in a rage he came into Samaria. Achab, being angry, commanded the prophet to be kept shut up, and confounded by the words of Micheas returned home.

VERS. 17.-- Vidi universum Israelem dispersum, etc. Per haec autem ostendit, quod illius pravitas causa sit stragis. Nam si bonum ac pium habuisset pastorem, vi superasset hostes. Deinde ostendit etiam modum assequendae salutis. Si Dominus est, inquit eis, in Deum, revertatur unusquisque ad locum suum in pace. Si Deo creditis, et vultis ex eo scire quid agendum, dimittite exercitum.

VERS. 19.-- Vidi Dominum, etc. (RAB.) Solium Domini angelicae potestates, quarum mentibus Deus praesidens inferius cuncta disponit. Exercitus coeli ministrantium angelorum multitudo: dextera pars angelorum electa, sinistra pars reproba. Neque enim dextera concluditur Deus, neque sinistra: qui 610Dita est intra omnia, ut sit et extra. Sed quomodo mali de exercitu coeli? Quia quamvis ab aethereo coelo pulsi, adhuc tamen in aereo demorantur coelo; unde Paulus: Contra spiritualia nequitiae in coelestibus (Ephes. VI). Omnis exercitus assistit Deo, quia et voluntas electorum spirituum divinae deseruit potestati, et reproborum sensus suae serviens malitiae, judicio Omnipotentis obtemperat.

CHAPTER XXII.

VERS. 17.-- I saw all Israel dispersed, etc. And by these things he shows that his depravity was the cause of the slaughter. For if it had had a good and pious shepherd, it would have overcome the enemies by force. Then he shows also the manner of obtaining salvation. If the Lord is, saith he to them, for God, let every one return to his own place in peace. If you believe in God, and wish to know from him what is to be done, dismiss the army.

VERS. 19.-- I saw the Lord, etc. (RAB.) The throne of the Lord, the angelic powers, over whose minds God presiding disposes all things below. The army of heaven, the multitude of ministering angels: the right hand part of the angels the elect, the left hand part the reprobate. For God is shut in neither by the right hand nor by the left: who 610Dis so within all things that he is also without. But how are the evil of the army of heaven? Because although driven from the ethereal heaven, they yet still tarry in the airy heaven; whence Paul: Against the spirits of wickedness in the high places (Ephes. VI). The whole army stands by God, because both the will of the elect spirits serves [deseruit; read deservit, "serves"] the divine power, and the sense of the reprobate, serving its own malice, obeys the judgment of the Almighty.
