Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

CHAPTER ONE.

611A

VERS. 2.--(RAB.) And Ochozias fell. The house of Ochozias is the Synagogue, which was hedged about on every side by the precepts of the law as by the lattices of an upper chamber. But as Ochozias, falling through the lattices by which he ought to have been guarded, ran into an exceeding sickness, so also the Jew, going beyond the keeping of the law, fell into a hopeless languor of sins. And there, because he forsook the protection of God, he found no remedy of salvation. For he fled, like an impious king, to false gods, and therefore he is rebuked by the mouth of the prophet, and his death is foretold as near at hand. Ochozias signifies above all the chief men of the Jews, who, given over to vices, persecute the truth; whence: The chief men and the Pharisees sent officers to apprehend Jesus, etc. (Joan. VII).

611BVERS. 9.-- And he sent to him a captain of fifty, and fifty men. (RAB.) The captain of fifty is the confession of penance, by which the remission of sins is declared. The Jews therefore, unwilling that Christ should be God, nor the prince of penance, say to him: Thou art not yet fifty years old, and hast thou seen Abraham? and they are burned in the fire to come. The third captain of fifty, because, being converted to the faith of the Trinity, he acknowledged the sacrament of penance, deserved pardon.

CHAPTER III.

(RAB.) The three kings warring against Moab are the rulers of the faithful, who through faith in the Trinity contend with spiritual arms against the prince of the world and his people, that is, against philosophers, heretics, schismatics, and all the wicked. Moab is interpreted "of the father", it suits those to whom the Lord said: You are of your father the devil (Joan. VIII). These set themselves against the Church with threats, with persecutions, and with guile: but through Christ, who is the head of Christians, they are put to flight.

(ID.) He makes a ditch in the bed of the torrent who seeks out the deep mysteries in the Scriptures. III), that is, they shall receive doctrine, the clever and the simple

VERS. 19.-- The city. (RAB.) The city is worldly prudence, in which philosophers and heretics trust, this is overthrown by the preachers.

VERS. 29.-- And he said. (ID.) While the Lord, etc., as far as through the spirit of love the boy returned to life.

VERS. 39.-- He cut. (RAB.) He who, intent upon the letter of the law or given to the study of philosophy, taking bitterness from 613Cthe law or what is death-bearing from the philosophers, mingles it with the truth of the Gospel, and cooking such a pottage in the pot of the heart, prepares this teaching for his hearers. CHAPTER VI.

VERS. 5.-- And it happened. (RAB.) When the presence of Christ, working through the body, was cutting down the impious Jews like unfruitful trees; whence: Behold the axe is laid to the root of the tree. [Vulg. Matt. 3:10 and Luke 3:9 Jam enim securis ad radicem arborum posita est, "For now the axe is laid to the root of the trees"] From them, the passion intervening, the body itself does service [deservit; read deseruit, "forsook"]: it went down into the deep, in that, laid in the sepulchre, it rose again as to the haft, the spirit returning.

(ID.) Great care must be taken lest the understanding grow numb in idleness, 614Clest in the exercise of work it vanish away through the vice of elation.

(GREG.) The iron in the haft is the understanding in the heart: by this the woods are cut down, when they that act perversely are rebuked. And while this is done loosely, while the fall of vainglory is not shunned; the iron is lost in the water, because out of a dissolute work the understanding is made foolish, which is given for this end, that it may be restored before the eyes of the giver out of good action, whence there follows: This very thing. But the elect, if ever they offend, return swiftly to the heart, and pursue their fault with tears, whence: Alas, alas, etc. They who weep look carefully not only at the evils they have committed, but also at the good things they ought to have rendered out of the gift received, and this is: Alas, alas, alas, and this I had borrowed; as if he should say: That I lost through the looseness 614Dof negligence which I received of the Redeemer's grace, that I might render it again through good works: but God never forsakes the mind which truly acknowledges itself in its sins. Whence Elisaeus sent the wood down, and brought the iron to the surface, because the Redeemer humbles the heart of the sinner, and restores the understanding which he had lost. Whence in another translation it is said: That he broke the wood and cast it in, and so took up the iron. To break the wood is to bruise the heart from elation, to cast it to the depths, to humble it by the thought of its own infirmity. Straightway the iron returned, because the understanding runs back to the use of its former exercise.

VERS. 4.-- And it was broken up, etc. (RAB.) The guard of the peoples was indeed broken up by the divers temptations of demons, etc., as far as who is weighed down by depraved habit and by the multitude of his own iniquity.

VERS. 8.-- In the fifth month. (RAB.) The order of the time agrees with the reason of the vengeance. In the fifth month the city was laid waste, which scorned to keep the Pentateuch of the law, and on the seventh day of the month, because it did not keep the rest of the sabbath, and did things contrary to the precepts of God, 629Bflattering itself in vain concerning its security because of its dwelling in holy places.

(ID.) Nabuzardan came, who is interpreted a winnowing-fan, or the prophecy of another's judgment, etc., as far as but rather the thorns and thistles of vices grow up.

VERS. 13.-- But the brazen pillars which, etc. (ID.) And by the bases we understand the teachers, who by the sonority of their preaching and by the firmness of their faith ought to have upheld others. The brazen sea is the laver of baptism, or the compunction of tears. All these things indeed the spiritual Chaldeans strive, through the sloth of the masters, both to carry away from the house of the Lord, and to break in pieces.

VERS. 14.-- The pots also. (ID.) Because, taking away the divers offices in the ecclesiastical orders from the service of God, they turned them to their own most wicked use.

629CVERS. 15.-- The censers and the vials. When by hindering they overthrow the prayer by which the Godhead could be appeased, and the cup of the word by which the weary ought to have been refreshed.

(ID.) Because the wicked enemy strives to carry away out of the house of the Lord both precious understanding and the comeliness of speech, and to lead them with himself into confusion.

VERS. 18.-- He took also. (ID.) The devil busies himself to deceive not only the common people, but also the strong and the princes in the Church, and to lead sinners captive, whence in Job 629Dof him: His food is choice (Job XXXIII).
