Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

VERS. 5.-- And the people. (ADAM., hom. 6.) They who had not been obedient to the commandments of the Lord are called uncircumcised. From which it is understood that they are called uncircumcised who obey not the commandments of God; and because God loves every soul, he sent Jesus the Son of God, who should circumcise both the worthy and the unworthy, 509Cnot the son of Nave, who did not truly and perfectly circumcise the people, but Jesus the Son of God, who truly cleansed body and soul from every defilement of sin.

VERS. 8.-- But after all were circumcised. And the children of Israel, being circumcised, rested in the same place, sitting in the camp, until they were healed. It is not enough to be circumcised; it is needful after circumcision to be healed, and that a scar be drawn over the wound. To be circumcised therefore by Jesus is to lay aside vices and depraved habits and the worst practices, and to cut away whatever disagrees with the rule of honesty. But in doing this we are held fast by a certain difficulty, since it is with a certain grief of mind that we shun the guilt of habit by a new practice: at this time therefore we are said to sit still, as it were in the pain of our circumcision, 509Duntil, the scar being drawn over, we are healed, that is, until we fulfil the new practices without difficulty, and that which before seemed unwonted and hard is turned into use; and then it is rightly said to us: This day have I taken away from you the reproach of Egypt

Et clangentibus. Unusquisque nostrum debet haec in semetipso complere. Habes in te Jesum ducem per fidem. Fac tibi tubas ductiles ex Scripturis sanctis, inde duc sensus, inde sermones; ideo enim tubae ductiles dicuntur. In ipsis cane, id est in psalmis, in hymnis, in canticis spiritualibus. Si in talibus cecineris tubis, et septies arcam testamenti circumtuleris, id est si legis mystica praecepta ab Evangelicis non separes tubis, sic etiam jubilationis consensum de temetipso exigas, id est si cogitationum 511Aet sensuum tuorum populus concordem semper et consonam proferat vocem; et si non aliquando verum dicas, aliquando adulando mentiaris, si non modo blandus remissionem, modo truculentus iracundia; et si non intra te caro concupiscat adversus spiritum et spiritus adversus carnem, vocem jubilationis emitte, quia tibi victus est mundus.
