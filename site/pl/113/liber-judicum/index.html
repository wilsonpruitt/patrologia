Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

PRAEFATIO.

521A

(ATHANAS., in (Synopsi. ) Vocatur hic liber Judicum, propterea quod post mortem Jesu Nave, populum suum Deus a relictis Chananaeorum gentibus pressum, per judices servavit; quorum hoc volumine singulorum facta et vitae tempus continentur, etc. Fuerunt autem numero tredecim: et anni illorum fuerunt quadringenti et undecim.

(ISID., in lib. Judic., cap. 1.) Historia libri Judicum non parva mysteriorum indicat sacramenta. Post Josue enim succedunt Judices, sicut post Christum apostoli et ecclesiarum rectores ad regendos fideles, quos ipse ad spem promissionis aeternae perducit. Per omnia autem in hoc volumine Judaici populi delicta, et servitus clamorque eorum et miseratio 521BDei continentur. Multis enim annis pro peccatis suis servierunt, et conversi liberati sunt: quia peccata nostra vires hostibus praebent; et quando malum facimus in conspectu Domini, confortantur adversarii nostri, id est daemones. Cum autem convertimur, suscitat Deus judices et salvatores, id est principes et doctores quos mittit in auxilium eorum qui toto corde clamant ad Deum.

(AUG., quaest. 1 in lib. Judic.) In fine libri Jesu Nave breviter narrator porrexit historiam quousque filii Israel ad colendos deos alienos declinaverunt. In hoc autem libro ad ordinem reditur, quomodo consequentia gesta fuerint post mortem Jesu Nave. Non ergo ab illo tempore incipit liber, quo populus ad colenda simulacra defluxit: sed a prioribus, interpositis 521Ctemporibus, quibus ea gesta sunt, postquam ad illa pervenit.

PREFACE.

521A

(ATHANAS., in (Synopsi. ) This book is called Judges, for this reason, that after the death of Jesus Nave God preserved his people, oppressed by the nations of the Chanaanites that were left, by means of judges; and the deeds of each of these and the time of their life are contained in this volume, etc. Now they were thirteen in number: and their years were four hundred and eleven.

(ISID., in lib. Judic., cap. 1.) The history of the book of Judges points to no small mysteries of sacred things. For after Josue there succeed Judges, as after Christ the apostles and the rulers of the churches, to govern the faithful, whom he himself leads on to the hope of the eternal promise. And throughout this volume are contained the offences of the Jewish people, and their servitude and their cry and the compassion 521Bof God. For through many years they served for their sins, and being converted they were set free: because our sins furnish strength to our enemies; and when we do evil in the sight of the Lord, our adversaries, that is, the demons, are made strong. But when we are converted, God raises up judges and saviours, that is, princes and teachers, whom he sends to the aid of those who cry to God with their whole heart.

(AUG., quaest. 1 in lib. Judic.) At the end of the book of Jesus Nave the narrator briefly extended the history as far as the point at which the children of Israel turned aside to the worshipping of strange gods. But in this book there is a return to the order, how the things that followed were done after the death of Jesus Nave. The book therefore does not begin from that time in which the people slipped away to the worshipping of images: but from earlier things, the times being interposed 521Cin which those things were done, after it came to them.

Thither it exhorts us to ascend: thither prophecy strives to move her disciples.

VERS. 30.-- Si tradideris filios Ammon. (AUG., ibid.) Quem autem potuit cogitare qui filios non habebat? An conjugem cogitavit et ut hoc fieret Deus noluit et ut hoc non relinqueret impunitum; ne quis postea id auderet: et ut magna providentia ex hoc quod accidit magnum sacramentum Ecclesiae figuraretur. CHAPTER XI.

(AUG., quaest. 49 in Jud.) Jephte is interpreted opening, etc., as far as because they do not keep the precepts of the law and do not show faith to him as to a husband.

VERS. 3.-- He dwelt in the land of Tob. (ID., ibid.) In a land, namely, good, or rather best. For what in Greek is ἀγάθον, this in Latin is called optimum. And this is what Tob is interpreted, where the resurrection seems to be understood. For what land is more the best than the earthly body clothed with the excellence of immortality and of incorruption.

529C(ID., ibid.) Before the passion he ate with publicans and sinners, etc., as far as that they may be justified and may learn his ways.

In those days. (ID., ibid.) Those who had cast off Christ, etc., as far as that by old age the last times may be signified.

VERS. 8.-- And the princes said. (AUG., ibid.) Some such thing was figured in Joseph, etc., as far as of which kingdom of his there shall be no end.

And that thou fight. (AUG.) That Jephte is sought as leader against the children of Ammon, at whose overthrow those might be set free who desired to make war under him as leader: because Ammon is interpreted 'son of my people', or 'people of mourning'; either those enemies are signified who out of that same nation remained in unbelief; or all who are predestined to hell: where there shall be 529Dweeping and gnashing of teeth, as to a people of mourning. Although the devil and his angels are fittingly understood as the people of mourning: either because they procure eternal misery for those whom they deceive, or because they themselves are appointed to eternal misery.

VERS. 9.-- I will be your prince. (ID., ibid.) That is, a king whom that nation did not yet have in the time of the judges, etc., as far as but Jephte signified him who is the true king.

(ID.) A king, namely, as it is written in the title of the cross, etc., as far as like those who are contained in the books of Kings.

Are not the things which he possesseth, etc. Are not whatsoever things Chamos hath given thee to inherit? [Vulg. Jdc. 11:24 nonne ea quae possidet Chamos deus tuus, tibi jure debentur?, "are not the things which Chamos thy god possesseth due to thee by right?"]

530AVERS. 29.-- And going about Galaad. (AUG., quaest. 49 in Jud.) Galaad is interpreted casting away, etc., as far as which he signifies by saying also from the watch-tower of Galaad of passing over and passing over to the children of Ammon. [Vulg. Jdc. 11:29 Maspha quoque Galaad, et inde transiens ad filios Ammon, "Maspha also of Galaad, and passing thence to the children of Ammon"]

VERS. 30.-- If thou wilt deliver the children of Ammon. (AUG., ibid.) Whomsoever Jephte may have thought of, he does not seem to have thought of his daughter; for he would not have said: alas for me! my daughter, thou hast hindered me, etc. [Vulg. Jdc. 11:35 Heu me, filia mea! decepisti me, et ipsa decepta es, "Alas me, my daughter! thou hast deceived me, and thou thyself art deceived"] For he shows himself to have been hindered, so that he might not fulfil that which he had thought of. But whom could he think of, who did not have sons? Or perhaps he thought of his wife, and God willed not that this should come to pass, and that he should not leave this unpunished; lest anyone afterwards should dare it: and that by a great providence, out of that which happened, a great mystery of the Church might be figured. From both, therefore, was the prophecy fitted? namely, what he thought of in vowing, 530Band what befell him unwilling. For if he thought of his wife, the wife of Christ is the Church. But because the wife of this Jephte could not be a virgin, in that his daughter rather met him, the rashness of the vow did not remain unavenged, and the virginity of the Church was figured, to whom it is said: Daughter, thy faith hath made thee whole, go in peace (Luc. VII). [Vulg. Lc. 7:50 Fides tua te salvam fecit: vade in pace, "Thy faith hath made thee safe: go in peace" — Filia stands at Lc. 8:48, not at Lc. 7:50]

(AUG., quaest. 49.) Concerning the daughter of Jephte there is wont to be a great question, etc., as far as so that all the things which were done afterwards may seem to be understood as works of the Spirit of the Lord who was made upon him.

(ID.) But among praiseworthy men, etc., as far as that it may be altogether avoided, but only that it may be deferred, he labours.

(ISID., in Jud., tom. V.) Jephte signifies Christ, because departing from the face of his brethren, that is, of the Jews, he received the princedom among the Gentiles, because 530Che fulfilled all the mysteries of human salvation as one under oath: and he offered his own flesh, as it were a daughter, for the salvation of Israel. And he swore to the Father and vowed according to that word: As he swore to the Lord, he vowed a vow to the God of Jacob, namely, that he would fulfil the mystery of religion in the passion of his own flesh for the salvation of the human race. These things therefore were so done through Christ that they seem to have been completed for the religion of the oath.

VERS. 37-39.-- And I will bewail, etc. And he did to her, etc. (AUG.) A chaste virgin, that is, the Church, etc., as far as on account of whom the two testaments also are so called.

From thence the custom grew. (AUG., quaest. 49.) But as to what was done by ordinance in Israel, that from days to days they came together to lament the daughter of Jephte, four days in the year, I do not think that it signifies anything after the completed 530Dholocaust, which shall be in eternal life, but the past times of the Church, in which the mourners were blessed. By the four days its universality was figured: because of the four parts of the world through which it is spread far and wide. But as to the proper sense of the history, I do not think that the Israelites decreed this, except because they understood that in her the judgment of God had been shown rather for the striking of the father, lest anyone thereafter should vow such a sacrifice. For why was a lamentation decreed, if that vow is a vow of gladness?

VERS. 20.-- And when they had seen it. (GREG., lib. V Moral., cap. 23.) What is it that at the vision of the angel the man is timid, the woman bold, unless that when heavenly things are shown to us, the spirit indeed shakes itself with dread, but yet hope presumes. For from that source hope raises itself to the daring of greater things, from which the spirit is troubled that sees first the things which are above, because when the mind, thus lifted up, beholds the loftier things of the heavenly secrets, all the solidity of human strength trembles.
