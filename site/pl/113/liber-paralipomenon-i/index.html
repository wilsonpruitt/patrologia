Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

VERS. 4.-- Noe. (RAB. in Paral., tom. III.) That Noe is the tenth 630Dfrom Adam is shown both in Genesis and in Paralipomenon, and fittingly the tenth, because he is perfect. Whence, 631ANoe was a just man and perfect in his generations, etc. (Gen. VI.) He begot Sem, Cham and Japheth, through whom after the flood the offspring of men is spread abroad anew.

VERS. 5.-- The sons of Japheth. (HIER.) These possessed the land in Asia, from Amanus and Taurus, the mountains of Syria Cheles and of Cilicia, as far as the river Thanais. In Europe, as far as Gadara, leaving names to places and to peoples, of which very many were afterwards changed, while the rest remain.

Thubal. (RAB.) The Iberians, who are also the Spaniards, from whom Celtiberia, though some suspect the Italians.

Mosoch. (ID.) The Cappadocians, whence their city is called Masacha to this day. But the Seventy translators judge the Gapthorim to be Cappadocians.

631BVERS. 7.-- But the sons. (GREG.) From these were divided the islands of the nations, the tongues, the kindreds and the peoples. From the Ionians, that is the Greeks, are born the Elisaeans, who are also the Aeolians. Whence that tongue is called in Greek Aeolic.

Tharsis. (RAB.) Josephus judges these to be the Cilicians, whence their metropolis also is called Tharsis, glorious by Paul the apostle (Act. XXII).

Cethim. (ID.) From whom the Cithaeans, otherwise the Cithians, from whom a city of Cyprus is called Cithia, otherwise Cithium, to this day.

Dodanim. (ID.) The Rhodians, for so the LXX translated. Let us read the books of Varro on antiquities, and those of Sinnius Capito, and the Greek Phlegon and the rest, and we shall see that almost all the islands 631Cand the shores of the whole world, and the maritime lands, are occupied by Greek settlers: who from Amanus and Taurus possess all the seaboard as far as the British ocean.

VERS. 8.-- Phut. (ID.) The Libyans, from whom also a river of Mauretania is called Phut, and the region Phutensis. Now many writers, Greek as well as Latin, are witnesses of this thing. But why in one part of the clime the ancient name of Libya remains, and the rest of the land is called Africa, it is not for this time to discuss.

VERS. 9.-- Saba. (ID.) From whom the Sabaeans, it is now interpreted Saba Arabia, whence in Psalm LXXI, where we have: Kings of the Arabians and of Saba; in the Hebrew it stands: Kings of Saba and Saba, the first through Schin, the second through Samech.

631DVERS. 10.-- He began to be mighty in the earth. Because he was the first to seize an unwonted tyranny. He was a strong hunter before the Lord, because he turned men away from the worship of God. Whence Josephus: But he made them lifted up unto the injury and contempt of God. Nemrod, the son of Cham, the son of Noe, who, since he was over-bold and most strong of hand, urged men to ascribe their happiness not to God, but to their own strength. Thus he called men back from the fear of God, that he might set his hope in his own strength. And therefore they thought that God was threatening a flood. Whence they urged that a tower be built which the water could not climb. But the multitude was ready to obey Nemrod, accounting it a heavy servitude to obey 632AGod. Now Nemrod reigned in Babylon and in Areth. This is Edessa and Macha which is Nisibis and in Chalamne, which was called Seleucia from king Seleucus, or which is now called Thesiphon.

VERS. 11.-- And Mesraim begot. (RAB.) Excepting the Laabim, from whom the Libyans were named, who before were the Phutaeans, and the Castium, who afterwards were called Philisthiim, whom we corruptly call Palestinians. The other nations are unknown to us, because, overthrown in the Ethiopic war, they passed into forgetfulness of their former names. And they possessed the land from Gaza as far as the furthest borders of Egypt.

VERS. 14.-- The Gergezaean, the Hevaean. He founded the town Archas over against Tripolis at the roots of Libanus, not far from which was another city by the name of 632BSim, which, overthrown by the varying issue of wars, kept for the place only its former name.

VERS. 16.-- The Aradian also. From whom the Aradians, who possessed the island Aradus, parted by a narrow strait from the Phoenician shore.

And the Samarean. From whom the city Samaria of Syria Cheles, which now also is called, both by the Syrians and by the Hebrews, as it was before among the ancients.

The Hamathaean. (RAB.) From whom the city Emath. The Macedonians, who after Alexander reigned in the East, named it Epiphania. Others think that Antioch was so called. Others, not truly consulting their own opinion with a plausible word, think that Emaus, the first stage from Antioch for those journeying to Edessa, is so called, and that the same was Emath among the ancients.

632CVERS. 17.-- The sons of Sem, etc. These hold the parts of Asia from the Euphrates as far as the Indian ocean. And there is Aelam, from whom the princes of Persia.

Hus and Hul. Or Us, the founder of Trachonitis and of Damascus, held the chief place between Palestine and Coelesyria, from whom the land of Hus, of which Job was; whence, according to the LXX, there was a man in the region of Ausitis by the name of Job, as it were of Husitis.

VERS. 19.-- Phaley. [Vulg. 1 Par. 1:19 Phaleg] (RAB.) By a certain prophecy. For Phaleg is division, because in his days the tongues were divided in Babylon. And Philo relates, in the book of Questions upon Genesis, that from the three sons of Noe, while he himself was yet living, there were born thirteen thousand men and a hundred, besides women and little ones, having leaders over them. The sons of Sem, Jectan as leader; the sons of 632DCham, Nemroth; the sons of Japhet, Suphene. But after the death of Noe the leaders came together with their companies into the plain of Sennaar, that they might there build a tower. And while they were building, God divided their tongues, and changed their features, and no man knew his brother, nor heard the tongue of his neighbour. But when the masters sought stones, the servants brought water, and when those sought water, these brought straw. And so, their design frustrated, they ceased to build and were scattered over the face of the earth.

VERS. 20.-- And Jectan. (HIERON.) The later names of these nations I have not been able to find, but down to the present, because they are far from us, either they are called as at the first, or those that have been changed are unknown; 633Aand they possessed, from the river Cofene, all that region of India which is called Hieria.

VERS. 24.-- Sem, Ar. (RAB.) As in the beginning he set ten generations from Adam as far as Noe; so from Sem as far as Abram, not without the mystery of a sacrament.

VERS. 29.-- The firstborn. (HIERON., Quaest. in Paral., tom. III.) Twelve sons are born to Ismael: the firstborn Nabaioth, from whom the whole region from the Euphrates as far as the Red Sea is called Nabaiothena: which is a part of Arabia. For their families and towns and villages, and their small forts and tribes, are celebrated by the name of these, from one of them, Cedar in the desert: and Duma another region, and Thebaram toward the South, and Cedema toward the Eastern quarter.

633BVERS. 32.-- Of Cetura. (RAB.) As it were, the joined one, whence the Hebrews suspect her to be Agar, who, when Sara was dead, passed over from a concubine into a wife, lest the old man should be charged with having wantoned in a new marriage.

(ID.) And the sons of Caetura, according to the historians of the Hebrews, occupied Troconitis, others Traconitis, and Arabia: which is now called Eidormo, as far as the borders of the Red Sea.

Assurim. Some contend that the Syrians were named from Assurim, and that the regions of India were occupied by very many of the sons of Abraham by Cetura.

VERS. 33.-- Epher. (RAB.) He is said to have led an army against Libya, and to have settled there as victor, and that his posterity named Africa from his name.

VERS. 36.-- Thamna. It is asked: since Thamna is read in 633CGenesis to be the concubine of Eliphaz, of the race of the Horrhaeans, who dwelt among the Idumaeans in the land of Seir, and bore him Amalec, how is she numbered among the sons, when in what follows the same Thamna is read to be the sister of Lotam the son of Seir, who is also Edom? But Seir in Hebrew is called hairy, who was shaggy after the manner of a hide. And Edom in Hebrew is red or tawny. From this, therefore, that he sold his birthright for red food, he was called Edom. But the Hebrews resolve it thus. They say that Thamna was the concubine of Eliphaz, and bore him Amalec, but that she was brought up by him as a daughter among his sons.

VERS. 40.-- Aliam, [Vulg. 1 Par. 1:40 Alian] who is of the race of the Horrhaeans. In Genesis is written Aluham, that is, exalted. But in Paralipomenon Alian, that is, cast out, signifying 633Dthat the Horrhaeans were exalted before Edom took their land, but afterwards cast out.

Sephi. (HIERON.) That is, one-footed. In Genesis Sepho, that is, two-footed; yet both through the letter schin. And he is called two-footed because with the process of time, through the multitude of his offspring, he had greater efficacy than at the first.

VERS. 41.-- Hamran. [Vulg. 1 Par. 1:41 Hamram] In Paralipomenon Hamram, that is, reddened, foul or swollen. In Genesis Amdan, that is, desirable; but both are names of one man.

VERS. 42.-- Jacan. (RAB.) Jacan the son of Aser, of the race of the Horraeans, is named in Genesis Acan, that is, the troubler. In Paralipomenon Jacan, that is, the troubled. 634AFor names are changed by varying events, that varied mysteries may be signified.

VERS. 43.-- Denaba. The city of Hala the son of Beor, king of Edom, after whom Jobab reigned. Yet there is to this day a village Denaba, at the eighth milestone from Areopolis, for those journeying to Armon, and another Denaba above mount Phegor, at the seventh stone from Jebus. Jerome, on Jesbii.

VERS. 44.-- Jobab the son of Zarae, whom they suspect to be Job. But the Hebrews assert that he was begotten of the stock of Nachor; while Philo, in the book of Questions upon Genesis, relates that he lived in these times and took to wife Dina, the daughter of Jacob: and he says that before the contest of his temptation she bore him seven sons and three daughters, who were destroyed 634Bin the temptation. But when he was healed the Lord restored to him all things double, and again the aforesaid wife bore seven sons and three daughters, whose names were the same as those of the former, that is, Eliphaz, Armoe, Diasath, Philasia, Dipharceluth, Thelon, and of the daughters Meru, Lizath, Zeli.

Bosra, etc. Which is Bosor, a city in the desert beyond the Jordan in the tribe of Ruben. Another city of Esau in the mountains of Idumea is also called Bosor, of which it is said: His garments are tawny from Rosor

VERS. 45.-- Of the Themanites. Theman, a region that drew its name from Theman the son of Eliphaz. There is to this day a village Theman, distant five miles from the city Petra, and where a garrison of Roman soldiers has its seat, from which place was Eliphaz king of the Themanites: one 634Cof the sons of Ismael also was called Theman. And it is to be known that every southern region is called Theman in Hebrew.

VERS. 46.-- Madian, a city so called from the least of the sons of Abraham and of Cetura. And it is beyond Arabia in the desert of the Saracens, over against the east of the Red Sea, whence they are called Madianites, and the region Madianaean. The daughters of the father-in-law of Moses are also called daughters of Madian; but this is another city, of the same name with it, near Armon and Areopolis, whose ruins are still shown.

VERS. 48.-- Rohoboth. It is a city beside the river where the king of Edom was, and to this day there is a garrison in the region of Gabalena, and a great village which is so called.

634DVERS. 50.-- Of Matred the daughter, etc. Two mothers are here numbered, to signify a mystery, that the goldsmith should have begotten the stewardess, and the stewardess the loving-kindness of the Lord, because out of depth of understanding is born eloquent speech, which deserves to beget a clean faith, the grace of God, in the hearts of hearers.

VERS. 51.-- Dukes, etc. It is to be noted that he first spoke of kings in Edom, afterwards of dukes, though they were before: of whom he names certain mothers, as Oolibama and Thamna.

Duke Aliha. [Vulg. 1 Par. 1:51 dux Alva] Alva is read in Genesis, whose interpretation is a lifting up; in Paralipomenon Aliha, that is, upon her.

VERS. 1.-- The sons of Juda. For they say that Phares, who is interpreted division, has here his own proper name. But that Hesron is Zaram, though Hesron is interpreted a court, Zaram a rising. That Charmi also is Heber, for Charmi is interpreted my vineyard, Her watching. And Ur they say to be Onam, though Ur is interpreted the left hand, Onam a murmuring, and Sobal to be Sela, the least of the sons of Juda whom Sue bore to him. The order seems confused, because, the sons of Halaa and Naara being numbered, he suddenly brings in the kindred of Arahel the son of Aram, and thereupon narrates of Jabes; but the Hebrews say that Assur of Halaa begot Etam, and he Cos. And Cos begot Soboba, and Soboba Jabes, who was a teacher of the scribes. Between Cos and Jabes are inserted the kindreds 638Dof Aharehel (or Aharahel, otherwise Aral), because he is Obab the son of Aran, that is, of Jetro the kinsman of Moses. And because Jabes taught them; they are inserted in his progeny as though begotten of his forefathers.

And the mother, the Synagogue of whom Christ was born, who bore him in sorrow, because she grieved at his coming through unbelief, whence the Jews, envying, said: If we let him alone so, all will believe in him (Joan. XI).

VERS. 10.-- He called upon. This befits Christ, whom the Father sanctified and sent into the world; of whom it is said: Behold the Lamb of God, who taketh away the sins of the world, [Vulg. Jo. 1:29 peccatum mundi, singular] etc. (Joan. I). Him God blessed with a multiplication of sons, whom through the water of baptism the most holy 639AChurch brings forth (I Cor. I). He enlarged his borders, because he gave him the inheritance of all nations, and extended his possession unto the ends of the earth. With whom was the hand of the Lord, because the power of the Godhead wrought mighty works and miracles through him. He also is the power of God and wisdom, through whom the Father founded all things that are in the heavens and on earth, whom the malice of the Jews could not hurt, who said of himself: The prince of this world cometh, and in me he hath not anything. Through him also his members are blessed, that is, the Church, to whom it is said by Isaias: Enlarge the place of thy tent, etc. (Isa. LIV). With him is the hand of the Lord, because in her the Son of God works, who is the arm and the right hand of the Father. And the gates of hell shall not prevail against him, [Vulg. Mt. 16:18 adversus eam, "against her," of the Church] nor shall the devil have dominion over him, because power 639Bis given to him over all the strength of the enemy

The Hebrews say therefore that Ram, the brother of Caleb, is here called Sua. And Ram is interpreted lofty, Sua lowly, which befits the Jewish people, because it was lofty in the worship of the one God and in the power of the kingdom; but because it would not receive the Son of God, it was made lowly and vile, so that the first become the last.

VERS. 12.--(ID.) Recha. Some think that David is called by the name of Recha. For Recha is tender. Whence David says of himself: I am tender and an anointed king (II Reg. III). And therefore they are called the men of Recha, because they were noted here for the sake of setting forth the genealogy of David.

VIII), [Vulg. 1 Cor. 8:1 Scientia inflat, caritas vero aedificat, with no negative] whom modesty and humility exalt, as Gamaliel and Paul his disciple (Act. V, XXII).

And Jalon and Meldath, who were two of the seventy elders whom Moses received from God for the help of ruling the people: for they hand down that Moses, after the law was received, persuaded his father in the wilderness to put away his mother, because she was his own friend, for she was the daughter of Levi, after which Amram took another, of whom he had Heldath and Meldath.

For they hand down that he was Elimelech the husband of Noemi, in whose time, because of the transgressors of the law, the sun stood still, that on seeing the miracle they might be converted to the Lord; and because they despised this, the famine grew strong, and he who seemed the first in the tribe of Juda, driven by want, was cast out of his country with his wife and his sons, and died in his sojourning together with his sons.

Who returned into, etc. Noemi and Ruth, who returned into Bethlehem when the plenty of bread was heard of.

VERS. 23.-- Potters dwelling. By the potters who labour in plantations and hedges we understand teachers, who form and compose the vessels of the Lord, for to fashion we call to compose, whence we call the composers of clay potters. These abide with the king in their works, because, remaining in the faith of Christ, whatsoever they say, whatsoever they do, they do to the glory of God.

640CVERS. 28.-- They dwelt in, etc. The progeny of Ruth, which is said to have dwelt in those very hedges and plantations, when this plantation came to the royal dignity and to the defence of the people.

And they abode there. In Genesis six are written, but here Sober is passed over: because there is no progeny from him. But certain names are changed for their own reasons. For in Genesis Gemuel, that is, the mother of God; here Hamuel, that is, reading with God. A certain one is called in Genesis Echa, but here Jarib, that is, contending, because from him came forth Zambri, who, fornicating with a Madianite woman, was slain by Phinees the priest. Jacim in Genesis, that is, preparation; but here he is named Zara, that is, a rising.

And all the kindred not, etc. Because Simeon had not 640Dso great an offspring as Juda; whence in Josue it is said: Simeon by his kindreds received an inheritance in the midst of the children of Juda.

These cities, etc. Because Sicelech and Bersabee, which belonged to Juda and were seized by Simeon, returned by David into the former inheritance of Juda.

The stock of Simeon and their acts being described, it is mystically signified that the holy teachers, that is, the princes of the Church, providing pastures for the flocks of Christ, set out into Gador or Gader, which is interpreted his hedge. This is the Church, which is the defence of the faithful: there are pastures as far as the East of the valley, that is, the incarnation of the Word, who emptied himself, taking the form of a servant 641A(Philip. I.) In him are found pastures. For he himself says: By me if any man enter in, he shall go in and go out, and shall find pastures (Joan. X), [Vulg. Jo. 10:9 salvabitur, "he shall be saved," which Migne's text does not print] namely the virtues. In him a land most wide and quiet, that is, the copious grace of the Holy Spirit; in which they of the stock of Cham had dwelt before, which signifies that the multitude of the nations left their idols and flowed together to the worship of the one God. These therefore came, whom we described above by name, in the days of Ezechias king of Juda, that is, at the coming of Christ. For Christ himself is the power of God, whom Ezechias, namely the strength of the Lord, signifies.

They smote the tents: he smites by faith the tents of the wicked and the dwellers among the wicked, when, denying all impiety and worldly desires, he is washed by the baptism of Christ from the filth of sins, 641Bafterwards studying to live soberly, justly and piously in this world, that what is written may come to pass: And it shall be in the place where it was before said: You are not my people: there they shall be called the sons of God (Osee. I).

The Fathers of the New Testament, who went into mount Seir and smote the remnants of the Amalecites, because, trampling down the pride of this world, they press down the deformity of the vices; these are said to be four, because, truly instructed in the evangelical doctrine, they fitly wage spiritual war against the demons; and, the remnants of the Amalecites being smitten, they dwelt there, because, the demons being driven out, they set virtues in the place of the vices, the possession of which makes a man blessed unto this day, which can always be said unto the end of the world, because the Church 641Cremains unto the end of the world, in which daily the ancient enemy is triumphed over.

Aram, which Paralipomenon mentions, Malachim passes over. The Hebrews affirm it to be Rages, a city of the Medes.

CHAPTER X.

VERS. 1.-- Philisthiim, etc. (RAB.) Many things being passed over which the history relates in the book of Samuel concerning Samuel and Saul, he records the fall of Saul and of his household. Hinting mystically that it is not needful to describe the life of one whose impious manner of living deserved to receive a swift end, and figuratively he shows the end of the kingdom of the Jews and of the old priesthood. He passed over to David, 645Cwhose deeds and the ordering of whose kingdom he weaves together, who holds the type of the true David, possessing the kingdom of heaven and of earth. While he is at hand the Philisthiim after their manner fight against Israel and conquer, because, as grace drew near, the gentiles, as before, were assailing the chastity of the Israelite faith, showing now evil works, now the worship of idolatry, as Balaam taught Balaac to cast a stumbling-block before the children of Israel, to eat and to commit fornication.

And the men fled. They who seemed strong in the contemplation of God, while they incautiously follow the works and the teaching of the gentiles; and secular learning has helped not a little the ruin of heretics also. Whence a certain one says: The philosophers, patriarchs of the heretics, have stained the purity of the Church with perverse doctrine.

645DVERS. 2.-- And when they had drawn near. The gentiles rushed in from every side upon the kingdom of Juda and its sons, and corrupted the priests, the scribes and the Pharisees, the sons, that is, and seducers of that kingdom, and the heretics also they forbade, that they should not simply preach or worship the grace of the Holy Spirit.

Abinadab. That we should not praise with worthy confession the Father who adopted us to himself by a spiritual gift, which the slaying signifies, Abinadab, that is, of my willing father.

Melchisua. That we should not believe salvation to come in the kingdom of Christ. For Melchisua is interpreted my king is salvation. The philosopher who seduced Arius slew, as it were, Melchisua: he who seduced Macedonius, Jonathan; he who seduced Manichaeus, 646AAbinadab. Because the master of corruption taught this one to deny the omnipotence of the true King, Christ, that one to detract from the gifts of the Holy Spirit, the other to blaspheme the goodness of the most high Father.

To his armour-bearer. (BEDA in cap. 28 l. I Reg.) The teachers of the law. For as the arms and the arrows of the Philisthiim, etc., as far as that they might not be defiled, but that they might eat the pasch.

But his armour-bearer would not. It is not to be doubted that among 646Bthe reprobate teachers of the law there were some who would not pervert the edge of the word to the slaying of their hearers, and that nevertheless those same hearers, who had received things rightly said by their masters, by despising or by blaspheming turned them to their own destruction: and that, their death being seen, that is, their obstinacy in sinning, the ministers of the word themselves also, grown worse with the times, made the testimonies of the law, which they had feared to interpret ill, an occasion of ruin to themselves. Heretics too, bloodied with the darts of their own sins, when they drag the divine words into an argument for their error, turn their own arms against their own breast, about to die. Any catholic also, when he defiles with the contagion of pride, of vainglory, of the love of money, or of any vice whatsoever, the art which he learned for the common profit, pierces himself with his own sword, because that whereby he ought to have been defended from the enemy 646Crushes upon him.

VERS. 12.-- Let him seek the oak in Jabes, and they fasted seven days, etc. [Vulg. 1 Par 10:12 quercum, quae erat in Jabes, "the oak which was in Jabes"] That they may come to rest. For after the six ages of this world in which we labour in the flesh, there follows the seventh, of the rest of souls.

Mystically, over the blindness of the Jews carnally 647Aobserving the sabbath, their fellow tribesmen, that is the faithful, grieve continually, and as it were fast seven days. But when the mystery of the eighth day of the resurrection has been received, both shall rejoice in a perpetual festival.

Morally, however, holy men detest the death of the impious, and consign their examples to the burial of oblivion. They fast seven days, that is, they lead in continence all the time of this life, which rolls round in the septenary number of days, and they abstain from every evil, lest they die in like manner.

Well, when David wished to appease the Lord, is he bidden to build an altar in the ark of Areuna or Ornam, because God is not appeased by man unless in the ark of the heart, through the light of right faith and of true love, he set up for him an altar of humility, in which he may offer 657Dthe sacrifice of praise.

VERS. 25.-- David therefore gave to Ornam for the place six hundred sicles of gold of most just weight, and built there an altar to the Lord, etc. In Kings thus, etc., as far as which afterward Solomon built, and its courts.

CAPUT XXII.

VERS. 2.-- Ut congregarentur. (RAB.) De Ecclesia Christus artifices domus suae elegit, qui inconditos mores hominum dolarent et polirent, ut in aedificatione spirituali, per diversas juncturas fidei et charitatis sibi convenirent. Aeris. Lectiones sonoras et cantica spiritualia quae sancti Patres ad instructionem Ecclesiae Christo vero pacifico praepararunt.

VERS. 4.-- Ligna quoque. Viros virtutibus claros, quos ad exemplum instruxerunt vel scripturis suis inseruerunt.

Sidonii et Tyrii. Quia per praedicatores, qui de gentibus electi sunt, tales viri ad notitiam Ecclesiae pervenerunt.

658BVERS. 5.-- Parvulus est et delicatus. Tempore Veteris Testamenti, obscura fuit notitia incarnationis Christi: parvus quoque numerus fidelium, qui sunt membra Christi.

Praeparavit impensas. Moyses impensas domus Domini praeparavit dicens: O Israel quid Dominus Deus tuus petit a te? nisi ut timeas Dominum Deum tuum, ut ambules in viis ejus, etc., et aliis similibus.

VERS. 8.-- Multum. Significat Judaeos propter carnalem legis observantiam et sanguinolenta opera, non posse spirituale aedificium Domino praeparare; vel quia pollutas manus habebant sanguine Christi, et prophetarum, unde: Jerusalem, Jerusalem, quae occidis prophetas, et lapidas eos qui ad te missi sunt 658C(Matth. XXIII).

VERS. 14.-- Auri talenta centum millia, et argenti mille millia talentorum. (RAB.) Patres Veteris Testamenti in scripturis suis sensus profunditatem et eloquii venustatem inserebant.

Talentorum. Talentum caeteris praecellit ponderibus: et rei magnitudinem et perfectionem significat.

VERS. 15.-- Artifices, latomos, et caementarios, etc. (RAB.) Praedicatores qui latomi, caementarii et artifices lignorum, et multis aliis modis dicuntur, quia juxta qualitatem auditorum, formatur doctrina praedicatorum. Aliter enim sapientes, aliter insipientes, aliter superbi, aliter humiles docendi sunt.

VERS. 17.-- Praecepit quoque David cunctis principibus 658DIsrael ut adjuvarent Salomonem, etc. Significat legem et prophetas firma auctoritate docere, ubi, qui sanctitate et virtute in Ecclesia principantur, adjuvent verum Salomonem in aedificio ejus, docendo scilicet et operando; unde: Adorate Dominum in atrio sancto ejus (Psal. XVIII).

Sanctuarium Domino Deo, ut introducatur arca foederis Domini, et vasa Domino consecrata in domum, quae aedificatur, etc.-- Anima justi sedes est sapientiae (Sap. VII). In hac domo requiescit arca, id est, fides incarnationis Christi.

CHAPTER XXII.

VERS. 2.-- That they should be gathered together. (RAB.) Out of the Church Christ chose the craftsmen of his house, who should hew and polish the rude manners of men, that in the spiritual building they might come together to one another through the diverse joinings of faith and of charity. Whence: For this cause have I hewed by the prophets, and I have slain them by the words of my mouth (Osee VI)

658AVERS. 3.-- Iron. He ordered the firmness of faith by sacred teachings, that they who enter into the Church might be pierced through with the goads of fear, and so be knit to one another through the faith of love and of fellowship. Whence: Pierce thou my flesh with thy fear (Psal. CXVIII). And elsewhere: Jerusalem, which is built as a city, whose participation thereof, etc. (Ibid. CXXII).

Of brass. The sounding lessons and spiritual songs which the holy Fathers prepared for the instruction of the Church for Christ the true peaceable one.

VERS. 4.-- Timber also. Men renowned for virtues, whom they trained up for an example or inserted in their writings.

The Sidonians and Tyrians. Because through the preachers, who were chosen out of the gentiles, such men came to the knowledge of the Church.

658BVERS. 5.-- He is a little child and delicate. In the time of the Old Testament the knowledge of the incarnation of Christ was obscure: small also was the number of the faithful, who are the members of Christ.

He prepared the charges. Moses prepared the charges of the house of the Lord, saying: O Israel, what doth the Lord thy God ask of thee? but that thou fear the Lord thy God, that thou walk in his ways, etc., and other like things. [Vulg. Deut. 10:12 Et nunc Israel, quid Dominus Deus tuus petit a te, nisi ut timeas Dominum Deum tuum, et ambules in viis ejus, "And now, Israel … and that thou walk in his ways"]

VERS. 8.-- Much. It signifies that the Jews, because of the carnal observance of the law and their bloody works, could not prepare a spiritual building for the Lord; or because they had hands polluted with the blood of Christ and of the prophets, whence: Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets, and stonest them that are sent to thee 658C(Matth. XXIII).

VERS. 14.-- A hundred thousand talents of gold, and a thousand thousand talents of silver. (RAB.) The Fathers of the Old Testament inserted in their writings depth of meaning and comeliness of speech.

Of talents. The talent surpasses the other weights: and it signifies both the greatness and the perfection of the thing.

VERS. 15.-- Workmen, stonecutters, and masons, etc. (RAB.) The preachers, who are called stonecutters, masons, and workers in wood, and by many other names, because according to the quality of the hearers the teaching of the preachers is shaped. For the wise are to be taught in one way, the unwise in another, the proud in one way, the humble in another.

VERS. 17.-- David also commanded all the princes 658Dof Israel that they should help Solomon, etc. It signifies that the law and the prophets teach with firm authority, where they who bear rule in the Church by holiness and virtue may help the true Solomon in his building, by teaching, that is, and by working; whence: Adore ye the Lord in his holy court (Psal. XVIII).

A sanctuary to the Lord God, that the ark of the covenant of the Lord may be brought in, and the vessels consecrated to the Lord into the house which is built, etc.-- The soul of the just is the seat of wisdom (Sap. VII). In this house the ark rests, that is, the faith of the incarnation of Christ.

CAPUT XXIII.

VERS. 1.-- Igitur David, etc. Post completionem 659AVeteris Testamenti, successit incarnatio Salvatoris, cujus regni non erit finis, unde: Ubi venit plenitudo temporis, misit Deus Filium suum (Gal. IV).

Numeratique. Qui ministerio Domini funguntur, fidem sanctae Trinitatis, et mandatorum impletionem servare debent. Unde: Inventa sunt triginta octo millia virorum. Quisquis enim sana fide et recto opere usque ad finem Deo servit, et ad resurrectionis gratiam, et beatitudinem aeternam quae in octonario signantur, perveniet.

Viginti quatuor. Quia veri ministri lucis debent instare, et a via justitiae non declinare. Nam viginti quatuor horis totus per circulum orbis illustratur, et tenebrarum umbra pellitur. Unde septuaginta duo discipuli ad praedicandum missi, quia 659Bfides sanctae Trinitatis cum operibus lucis ad justitiam sufficit: ter enim viceni et quater terni septuaginta duo fiunt, quo numero linguarum diversitas, et librorum utriusque Testamenti series comprehenditur.

Praepositorum. Quia nemo hujus ordinis dignitatem appetere debet, nisi qui perfectioni et doctrinae studet. Senario enim numero mundus factus est, et homo sexto die creatus, quo et redemptus est.

(RAB.) Quia lex et prophetae, etc., usque ad et patienter toleret omnia, amarus et iratus peccatis suis.

VERS. 25.-- Dixit enim David: Requiem dedit Dominus Deus, etc. (ID.) Dum filii Israel in deserto fuerunt, etc., usque ad Evangelium vero stabile et 659Cfixum.

VERS. 28.-- Et erunt sub manu filiorum Aaron, in cultum domus, etc. Ut minores discant majoribus obedire, et majores adjuti a minoribus possint dignius officium explere: sicut in Ecclesia fit hodie.

Exedris et in loco purificationis. Thalamis vel cubiculis in quibus habitant Levitae et sacerdotes. Ipsi quoque veri templi ministri aditum debent diligenter observare, dignos intromittere, indignos vero repellere.

VERS. 29.-- Ad ferventem similam, et ad torrentem, etc. Spicas scilicet primitiarum, quae torrebantur et grana comedebantur, quas vulgo grancas vocant; in quibusdam codicibus habetur: Ad ferventem similam. In aliis ad ferventem, tantum: sed 659Din Hebraeo habetur, ad torrendum.

CHAPTER XXIII.

VERS. 1.-- David therefore, etc. After the completion 659Aof the Old Testament there followed the incarnation of the Saviour, of whose kingdom there shall be no end, whence: When the fulness of time came, God sent his Son (Gal. IV).

And they were numbered. They who discharge the ministry of the Lord ought to keep the faith of the holy Trinity and the fulfilling of the commandments. Whence: There were found thirty-eight thousand men. For whosoever with sound faith and right work serves God unto the end, shall come both to the grace of the resurrection and to the eternal beatitude which are signified in the octonary.

Twenty-four. Because the true ministers of light ought to be instant, and not to turn aside from the way of justice. For in twenty-four hours the whole world is lighted through its circuit, and the shadow of darkness is driven away. Whence the seventy-two disciples sent to preach, because 659Bthe faith of the holy Trinity with the works of light suffices unto justice: for thrice twenty and four times three make seventy-two, by which number the diversity of tongues and the series of the books of either Testament is comprehended.

Of the overseers. Because no one ought to desire the dignity of this order, save he who is zealous for perfection and for teaching. For by the senary number the world was made, and man was created on the sixth day, on which he was also redeemed.

(RAB.) Because the law and the prophets, etc., as far as and let him patiently bear all things, bitter and angry at his own sins.

VERS. 25.-- For David said: The Lord God hath given rest, etc. (ID.) While the children of Israel were in the desert, etc., as far as but the Gospel stable and 659Cfixed.

VERS. 28.-- And they shall be under the hand of the sons of Aaron, for the service of the house, etc. That the lesser may learn to obey the greater, and the greater, helped by the lesser, may be able the more worthily to fulfil their office: as is done in the Church today.

In the chambers and in the place of purification. Bedchambers or cells in which the Levites and priests dwell. These also, the ministers of the true temple, ought diligently to watch the entrance, to let in the worthy, but to drive back the unworthy.

VERS. 29.-- For the seething fine flour, and for the parching, etc. The ears, namely, of the firstfruits, which were parched and whose grains were eaten, which they commonly call grancas; in certain codices it is read: For the seething fine flour. In others for the seething, only: but 659Din the Hebrew it is read, for the roasting. [Vulg. 1 Par. 23:29 et ad similae sacrificium … et ad torrendum, "and for the sacrifice of fine flour … and for the roasting"]

CAPUT XXIV.

VERS. 2.-- Sacerdotioque functus. (RAB.) Potest per Eleazar filium Aaron seniorem, etc., usque ad quia multi filii desertae magis quam ejus quae habet virum.

VERS. 4.-- Per familias sedecim. Duplicatus est octonarius. In lege carnalis circumcisio octava die celebratur, in Evangelio vero in praesenti circumcisio cordium, et in futuro corporum.

VERS. 6.-- Descripsitque. (RAB.) Praedicatorum ordo, etc., usque ad et justitiae nomine merito censetur.

660AVERS. 7.-- Exivit autem sors. Si qui putant in necessitate Deum esse consulendum sortibus apostolorum exemplo, videant apostolos collecto numero fratrum hoc fecisse, et preces Deo ante fudisse.

Prima Joiarib, secunda, etc. David in viginti quatuor sortes Leviticam tribum et sacerdotalem ordinem distribuit, ostendens Ecclesiae sacerdotalem et regiam dignitatem plene habere et legis, et Evangelii doctrinam perfecte servare, vel perfectionem operis, quae senario commendatur, et claritatem evangelicae praedicationis, quae quaternario figuratur. Nam quater seni viginti quatuor faciunt.

CHAPTER XXIV.

VERS. 2.-- And executed the priestly office. (RAB.) It is possible through Eleazar the elder son of Aaron, etc., as far as because the children of the desolate are many more than of her that hath a husband.

VERS. 4.-- By families sixteen. The octonary is doubled. In the law carnal circumcision is celebrated on the eighth day, but in the Gospel the circumcision of hearts in the present, and of bodies in the future.

VERS. 6.-- And he described. (RAB.) The order of preachers, etc., as far as and is deservedly reckoned by the name of justice.

660AVERS. 7.-- Now the lot came out. If any think that God is to be consulted by lots in necessity after the example of the apostles, let them see that the apostles did this when the number of the brethren had been gathered, and had first poured forth prayers to God.

The first Joiarib, the second, etc. David distributed the Levitical tribe and the priestly order into twenty-four lots, showing that the Church fully has the priestly and the royal dignity and perfectly keeps the teaching both of the law and of the Gospel, or the perfection of work, which is commended in the senary, and the brightness of evangelical preaching, which is figured in the quaternary. For four times six make twenty-four.

CAPUT XXV.

VERS. 1.-- Igitur David et magistratus exercitus segregaverunt in ministerium, etc. (RAB.) Quia verus 660BDavid rex noster et Dominus, etc., usque ad et omnem terrenarum rerum cupiditatem transilit, digne laudes persolvit

VERS. 7.-- Cuncti doctores. Hi sunt, qui hodie canones divinorum librorum sensu evangelico exponunt, quater enim septuaginta duo, ducenta octoginta faciunt. Libros autem Veteris et Novi Testamenti septuaginta duo esse dubium non est. Unde quicunque divino fungitur officio, quatuor principalibus virtutibus insistens, evangelicae doctrinae incumbat et secundum ejus normam quidquid didicerit vel docuerit, formare intendat.

CHAPTER XXV.

VERS. 1.-- David therefore and the chief officers of the army set apart for the ministry, etc. (RAB.) Because the true 660BDavid our king and Lord, etc., as far as and overleaps all covetousness of earthly things, he worthily pays his praises

VERS. 7.-- All the teachers. These are they who today expound the canons of the divine books with an evangelical understanding, for four times seventy-two make two hundred and eighty. [ducenta octoginta; read ducenta octoginta octo, "two hundred and eighty-eight" — four times seventy-two, and the reading of 1 Par. 25:7] But that the books of the Old and the New Testament are seventy-two is not to be doubted. Whence whosoever discharges a divine office, being intent upon the four principal virtues, let him apply himself to evangelical teaching, and according to its rule let him aim to shape whatsoever he shall have learned or taught.

CAPUT XXVI.

VERS. 2.-- Meselemiae. (RAB.) Qui interpretatur 660Creddente Domino, et significat ordinem apostolorum et primorum doctorum, quibus orientalis porta, id est, primitiva commendatur Ecclesia, quibus antiquorum Patrum redditae sunt promissiones.

Zachariae. Qui memoria Domini interpretatur, et significat doctores gentium, quia a primis praedicatoribus ad praedicandum ordinati sunt. Unde orantibus apostolis et jejunantibus, ait Spiritus sanctus: Segregate mihi et Paulum et Barnabam (Act. III).

VERS. 4.-- Obededom. Rubeus: qui in servitute erant generati, et rubore sanguinis Christi perfusi, reatum suum in propatulo habuerunt; recte per condescensionem magistrorum reducuntur ad gradum pristinum, unde Paulus: Omnium servum me feci, ut omnes lucrifacerem (I Cor. IX).

660DVERS. 15.-- Ad austrum. (RAB.) Australis porta significat Judaicam plebem lege et prophetis illuminatam, calori fidei et lumini sapientiae percipiendo vicinam.

VERS. 16.-- Ad occidentem, juxta portam, etc. Per occidentalem plagam finis mundi significatur, in quo erit persecutio Antichristi: et abundabit iniquitas, et refrigescet charitas multorum (Matth. XXIV). Hujus portae janitores, sunt doctores illius temporis, qui Ecclesiam docendo et exhortando roborant: unde Sephim humilis vel campestris, et Hosa divinans interpretatur. Humiliabitur enim in persecutione Ecclesia, et in campo lasciviae erit dissoluta. Sed in perfectorum actione elevata, quod divinis praesignatur 661Aeloquiis, unde: Clausi sunt sermones usque ad praefinitum tempus (Dan. XII).

VERS. 17.-- Levitae sex, et ad aquilonem quatuor per diem, etc. Senarius bonum opus ministeriorum Dei significat, quaternarius evangelicam doctrinam et principalium virtutum plenitudinem.

Bini et bini. Janitores binos et binos dicit esse in cellulis atque in via, quia sive in cellulis cordis sui curam gerant, sive in via exterius verbum Dei exponant, duo praecepta charitatis et regulam utriusque Testamenti omnino servent.

VERS. 24.-- Filii Moysi, etc. Qui praecipue a David honorabantur secundum Josephum, et super thesauros constituti erant, doctores de Judaeis significat, quibus ornamentum Ecclesiae, id est, decor 661Banimae commendatur.

VERS. 25.-- Fratres quoque ejus Eliezer. Ostendit donaria principum thesauros esse sanctorum, quae enim hostibus offerebant, in supellectilem templi donabant. Quicunque enim bene laborans aliquid virtutis vel boni operis acquirit, Deo, non sibi bonum attribuat. Unde: Non nobis, Domine, non nobis, sed nomini tuo da gloriam (Psal. CXIII).

VERS. 29.-- Isaaritis vero praeerat Chonenias, etc. Hi sunt qui in Ecclesia humiliter et obedienter, quae a magistris audiunt, devota mente suscipiunt, et qui lingua non possunt, faciendo doctrinam virtutum ostendunt.

VERS. 30.-- Israeli trans Jordanem contra occidentem, etc. Israelitae qui trans Jordanem possessionem 661Cacceperunt, significant eos, qui mediocritate contenti, arcem virtutum, turbulentiam hujus saeculi transcendendo, non appetunt, sed tamen mandata Dei opere exercere non negligunt. Quia vero nemo haec agit nisi dono Dei, is praepositus dicitur esse Asabias, id est faciente domino, ut dicant cum propheta: Omnia opera nostra operatus es in nobis (Psal. XXVI).

VERS. 31.-- Hebronitarum autem princeps fuit Jeria secundum familias et cognationes eorum. In Hebrun coepit David regnare, qui significat eos, qui bene conversati incipiunt, et David in vita sua parant regnum. Hebronitis Jerias, timens Dominum princeps est, quia initium sapientiae timor Domini (Psal. CX). Hebron vero interpretatur conjugium: qui enim 661Dtimet, proficit ut amet, et foras mittat timorem (I Joan. IV).

CHAPTER XXVI.

VERS. 2.-- Of Meselemia. (RAB.) Who is interpreted 660Cthe Lord rendering, and signifies the order of the apostles and of the first teachers, to whom the eastern gate, that is, the primitive Church, is entrusted, to whom the promises of the ancient Fathers were rendered.

Of Zacharias. Who is interpreted the memory of the Lord, and signifies the teachers of the gentiles, because they were ordained to preach by the first preachers. Whence while the apostles were praying and fasting, the Holy Ghost said: Separate me both Paul and Barnabas (Act. III). [Vulg. Act. 13:2 Ministrantibus autem illis Domino, et jejunantibus, dixit illis Spiritus Sanctus : Segregate mihi Saulum et Barnabam, "And as they were ministering to the Lord and fasting, the Holy Ghost said to them: Separate me Saul and Barnabas"]

VERS. 4.-- Obededom. Red: they who were begotten in servitude, and, drenched with the redness of the blood of Christ, held their guilt in the open; rightly are they brought back to their former grade through the condescension of the masters, whence Paul: I made myself the servant of all, that I might gain all (I Cor. IX). [Vulg. 1 Cor. 9:19 omnium me servum feci, ut plures lucrifacerem, "that I might gain the more"]

660DVERS. 15.-- To the south. (RAB.) The southern gate signifies the Jewish people, enlightened by the law and the prophets, near to the receiving of the warmth of faith and of the light of wisdom.

VERS. 16.-- To the west, near the gate, etc. By the western region the end of the world is signified, in which shall be the persecution of Antichrist: and iniquity shall abound, and the charity of many shall grow cold (Matth. XXIV). [Vulg. For the Church shall be humbled in the persecution, and in the field of wantonness shall be dissolute. But raised up in the action of the perfect, which is foresignified by divine 661Awords, whence: The words are shut up until the appointed time (Dan. XII).

VERS. 17.-- Six Levites, and to the north four a day, etc. The senary signifies the good work of the ministries of God, the quaternary the evangelical teaching and the fulness of the principal virtues.

Two and two. He says that the doorkeepers are two and two in the little cells and in the way, because whether in the cells of their heart they bear their own care, or in the way expound outwardly the word of God, they should altogether keep the two precepts of charity and the rule of either Testament.

VERS. 24.-- The sons of Moses, etc. Who were chiefly honoured by David according to Josephus, and had been set over the treasures, signifies the teachers from among the Jews, to whom the adornment of the Church, that is, the beauty 661Bof the soul, is entrusted.

VERS. 25.-- His brethren also, Eliezer. He shows that the gifts of the princes are the treasures of the saints, for the things which they offered to the enemies, they gave into the furnishing of the temple. For whosoever, labouring well, acquires anything of virtue or of good work, let him ascribe the good to God, not to himself. Whence: Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to thy name give glory (Psal. CXIII).

VERS. 29.-- And over the Isaarites was Chonenias, etc. These are they who in the Church humbly and obediently receive with devout mind the things which they hear from their masters, and they who cannot with the tongue, show the teaching of the virtues by doing.

VERS. 30.-- To Israel beyond the Jordan westward, etc. The Israelites who beyond the Jordan 661Creceived a possession signify those who, content with a middle state, do not seek the citadel of virtues by passing over the turbulence of this world, but yet do not neglect to exercise the commandments of God in work. But because no one does these things save by the gift of God, he is said to be set over them, Asabias, that is the Lord doing, so that they may say with the prophet: All our works thou hast wrought in us (Psal. XXVI). [Vulg. Isa. 26:12 omnia enim opera nostra operatus es nobis, "for all our works thou hast wrought for us" — and the words are Isaias', not the psalm's]

VERS. 31.-- And the prince of the Hebronites was Jeria, according to their families and kindreds. In Hebrun David began to reign, which signifies those who, having lived well, make a beginning, and prepare a kingdom for David in his own life. Over the Hebronites Jerias, fearing the Lord, is prince, because the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom (Psal. CX). But Hebron is interpreted a marriage: for he that 661Dfeareth, advanceth that he may love, and cast fear out of doors (I Joan. IV).

CAPUT XXVII.

VERS. 24.-- Non est relatus in fastos. (RAB.) Quia superveniente ira conturbatus est numerus, nec juxta eum fuerunt distributi in ministeria.

VERS. 25.-- Super thesauros, etc. (ID.) Thesaurus Christi scientia, sapientia virtutis, ex his honorat servientes sibi. Balanam Gederites. Balanam, qui interpretatur habens gratiam, super oliveta et ficeta constituitur. Ex Dei enim gratia datur bonorum operum ubertas. Joas vero, qui interpretatur sperans vel Domini robur, apothecas olei custodit, quia nemo pervenit ad praemium nisi qui per robur mentis fiducia spei tendit ad coeleste regnum.

VERS. 29.-- Saphat. Qui interpretatur judicavi, filius Abdi, id est patris gratiae, significat apostolos 662Caliis doctoribus electione Domini praefectos, quibus dicitur: Sedebitis et vos super duodecim sedes, judicantes duodecim tribus Israel (Matth. XIX).

III).

Achitophel (II Reg. XVI). Qui postea cupiditate de pravatus cum Absalon de nece David tractavit, et interpretatur frater meus cadens, vel irruens vel tractans. Hic est Judas Scarioth, qui inter apostolos familiaris Domino fuit, sed postea cum Judaeis avaritia corruptus de morte ejus tractando, perpetuae mortis ruinam meruit, qui etiam, secundum librum Regum, laqueo se suspendit. Possunt etiam per Achitophel Judaei significari, qui Patrum praerogativa aliquando charissimi et collocutione Dei fuere clarissimi: sed postea contra Christum consilium mortis facientes, a Romanis in propria domo sicut Achitophel, id est, in Jerusalem exstincti sunt.

663AVERS. 33.-- Chusai. Id est, Aethiops et David fidelis, per omnia gentiles signat, de quibus dicitur: Populus quem non cognovi servivit mihi, in auditu auris obedivit mihi (Psal. XVII).

VERS. 34.-- Post Achitophel fuit Joiada. Praevaricante Juda non remansit vacuus duodecimus locus: sed successit cognoscens gratiam Dei Matthias humilis et fidelis: qui gratiam quam dono Dei percepit, humiliter servavit. Interpretatur enim Joiada sciens vel cognoscens; Banaias aedificator Domini. Quem enim gratia Dei in apostolum elegit, agnitio sanae fidei in ipsa dignitate solidavit. Abiathar, qui ad tempus est inter consiliarios David, sed in electione a Domino cum Joab contra Salomonem deviavit, significat eos qui dignitatis suae gradum, 663Bquem a Domino perceperunt, cupiditate corrupti non custodiunt. Hi enim a vero Salomone si in carcere poenitentiae juxta timorem Achitophel permanere noluerint, in perpetuum puniendi sunt.

Joab. Id est, inimicus, signat eos ex quorum persona dicitur: Ad omnia mandata tua dirigebar, omnem viam iniquitatis odio habui (Psal. CXVIII), quibus alibi dicitur: Estote perfecti sicut Pater vester coelestis perfectus est (Matth. V). Interpretatur enim Joab idem pater.

CHAPTER XXVII.

VERS. 24.-- It was not put in the records. (RAB.) Because, wrath supervening, the number was disturbed, nor were they distributed into the ministries according to it.

VERS. 25.-- Over the treasures, etc. (ID.) The treasure of Christ is knowledge, the wisdom of virtue, out of these he honours those who serve him. Over these is set Azmoth, who is interpreted strong in death, for the teaching of a man is discerned through patience; whence: Better is the patient man than the valiant (Prov. XVI). Azmoth also the son of Adiel, who is interpreted my father God. For whatsoever of virtues the saints have, they ascribe to God.

662AAnd of these treasures. For he is deservedly set over the treasures of the true Solomon in the cities, villages, and towers, who by faith and by work sets forth the dignity of the name of Jonathan and of his father Ozias. For Jonathan is the house of God, and Ozias the fortitude of the Lord. For he who by the gift of God receives the light of knowledge, and bravely keeps in work the rightness of faith, not undeservedly receives the care of the churches of God.

VERS. 26.-- Ezri the son of Chelub. Ezri, helped by the Lord; Semeias, hearing the Lord; Zabdias, fruit vehement or abounding. For he who hears the commandments of the Lord, helped by the aid of his mercy, gathers an abundant fruit of good work unto life eternal.

VERS. 28.-- The oliveyards and the figyards, which were in the plains, etc. In these are olives and figs, and they signify 662Bthe assemblies of the saints, who have the oil of mercy in the breast, and the sweetness of meekness in mortality. Which virtues are in the plains, which are in the open and at hand for all, and seek for their hearers not the accepting of persons, but the rightness of good will.

Balanam the Gederite. Balanam, who is interpreted having grace, is set over the oliveyards and the figyards. For out of the grace of God is given the abundance of good works. But Joas, who is interpreted hoping or the strength of the Lord, keeps the storehouses of oil, because no one comes to the reward save he who through strength of mind stretches by the confidence of hope toward the heavenly kingdom.

VERS. 29.-- Saphat. Who is interpreted I have judged, the son of Abdi, that is, of the father of grace, signifies the apostles 662Cset over the other teachers by the choice of the Lord, to whom it is said: You also shall sit upon twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel (Matth. XIX).

VERS. 30.-- And over the camels Ubil the Ismaelite, and over the asses Jadias. These animals are misshapen and unclean, and they signify the gentiles distorted by sin and unclean with lust, but coming into the kingdom of our David they have as masters Ubil and Jadias, who, despising philosophy and the empty deceit of the world, chose the simplicity of the Gospel, as humble and despised, whence: If any man among you seem to be wise, let him become a fool (I Cor. III).

VERS. 32.-- Jonathan. (RAB.) When Jonathan was, etc., as far as whence: Upon whom shall my spirit rest, but upon the humble and quiet, and him that trembleth at 662Dmy words? [Vulg. Isa. 66:2 ad quem autem respiciam, nisi ad pauperculum, et contritum spiritu, et trementem sermones meos?, "but to whom shall I have respect, but to the poor little one, and the contrite of spirit, and him that trembleth at my words?"]

Achitophel (II Reg. XVI). Who afterwards, depraved by covetousness, treated with Absalon of the slaying of David, and is interpreted my brother falling, or rushing in, or treating. This is Judas Scarioth, who among the apostles was familiar with the Lord, but afterwards, corrupted by avarice, by treating with the Jews of his death, earned the ruin of everlasting death, who also, according to the book of Kings, hanged himself with a halter. By Achitophel also the Jews can be signified, who by the prerogative of the Fathers were once most dear, and by the converse of God most renowned: but afterwards, making a counsel of death against Christ, were destroyed by the Romans in their own house, as Achitophel was, that is, in Jerusalem.

663AVERS. 33.-- Chusai. That is, an Ethiopian and faithful to David, betokens in all things the gentiles, of whom it is said: A people which I knew not hath served me, at the hearing of the ear it hath obeyed me (Psal. XVII).

VERS. 34.-- After Achitophel was Joiada. Judas prevaricating, the twelfth place did not remain empty: but there succeeded Matthias, knowing the grace of God, humble and faithful: who kept humbly the grace which he received by the gift of God. For Joiada is interpreted knowing or acknowledging; Banaias, the builder of the Lord. For him whom the grace of God chose unto an apostle, the acknowledging of sound faith made firm in that very dignity. Abiathar, who for a time is among the counsellors of David, but in the election turned aside from the Lord with Joab against Solomon, signifies those who, corrupted by covetousness, do not keep the grade of their dignity, 663Bwhich they received from the Lord. For these, if they will not have remained in the prison of penance according to the fear of Achitophel, are to be punished for ever by the true Solomon.

Joab. That is, enemy, betokens those from whose person it is said: I was directed unto all thy commandments, I have hated every way of iniquity (Psal. CXVIII), to whom it is said elsewhere: Be ye perfect, as your heavenly Father is perfect (Matth. V). For Joab is interpreted the same father.

CAPUT XXVIII.

VERS. 1.-- Cum eunuchis et potentes et robustissimos quosque in exercitu Jerusalem. (RAB.) Lex prohibet Israelitas abscindi, et alienigenae non solent ad secreta consilii admitti. Sed tradunt in hoc loco 663Ceunuchos vocari, qui a saeculi actionibus alieni, orationi et lectioni vacabant: et uxores non causa libidinis, sed suscipiendae prolis habebant: quos Josephus Essenos vocat.

VERS. 11.-- Dedit autem David. David Psalmista, et caeteri Patres Veteris Testamenti figuris et aenigmatibus designabant universa quae verus pacificus in Ecclesia facturus erat: quomodo omnes nationes per fidem baptismi introitum in Ecclesiam habuerunt, qualis Ecclesiae status esset futurus, qualis sanctorum perfectio et in regno coelesti remuneratio cujusque pro meritis reddenda per diversas cellas et exedras, quia in regno Dei mansiones multae sunt.

VERS. 17.-- Et leunculos. (RAB.) Neque in Exodo in constructione tabernaculi, etc., usque ad perstrinximus.

663D(ID.) Tradunt Hebraei exemplar templi et vasorum 664AMosi in monte monstrati ad Jesum pervenisse, deinde ad judices et ad Heli et ad Samuel et ad David, tandem ad Salomonem. Vel manus Domini, filius est, per cujus donum David et alii sancti perceperunt quidquid de sanctae Ecclesiae structura protulerunt.

CHAPTER XXVIII.

VERS. 1.-- With the eunuchs and all the mighty and most valiant men in the army at Jerusalem. (RAB.) The law forbids the Israelites to be cut, and foreigners are not wont to be admitted to the secrets of counsel. But they hand down that in this place 663Cthose are called eunuchs who, being strangers to the actions of the world, were free for prayer and for reading: and had wives not for the cause of lust, but for the receiving of offspring: whom Josephus calls Essenes.

VERS. 11.-- And David gave. David the Psalmist, and the other Fathers of the Old Testament, designated by figures and by riddles all the things which the true peaceable one was to do in the Church: how all nations had through the faith of baptism an entrance into the Church, what the state of the Church should be in time to come, what the perfection of the saints and the reward in the heavenly kingdom to be rendered to each according to his merits, through the diverse cells and chambers, because in the kingdom of God there are many mansions.

VERS. 17.-- And little lions. (RAB.) Neither in Exodus in the construction of the tabernacle, etc., as far as we have touched upon.

663D(ID.) The Hebrews hand down that the pattern of the temple and of the vessels 664Ashown to Moses on the mount came to Jesus, then to the judges and to Heli and to Samuel and to David, at last to Solomon. Or, the hand of the Lord is the Son, by whose gift David and the other saints received whatsoever they brought forth concerning the structure of the holy Church.

CAPUT XXIX.

VERS. 6.-- Polliciti sunt itaque, etc. (RAB.) Qui sibi et aliis bene principari didicerit, etc., usque ad lignum, fenum, stipulam superaedificat, detrimentum patietur.

VERS. 10.-- Et benedixit Domino, etc. Quia congregatio fidelium scriptis prophetarum admonita, et sanctorum Patrum dictis et exemplis provocata, laudes et hymnos Deo decantet immolando piae confessionis hostias et sanctae conversationis opera 664Bfructuosa.

VERS. 22.-- Et comederunt et biberunt coram, etc. (RAB.) Hoc est gaudium sanctorum et intentio eorum, ut a fame futura, virtutum operibus sibi caveant, et epulentur, et exsultent in conspectu Dei.

Unxerunt secundo Salomonem filium, etc. Quia Christus regia simul et pontificali dignitate ab omnibus fidelibus sana fide, pia confessione celebratur. Salomon pacificus, Sadoc justus interpretatur. Christus vero est pax nostra, qui fecit utraque unum, ipse est justus Dominus et justitiam diligit: Judex vivorum et mortuorum (Ephes. II), hunc primo ungit omnis fidelis in regem et sacerdotem, cum in professione baptismi, verum regem et sacerdotem confitetur. Ungit et secundo cum docendo et operando 664Cidem aliis praedicat, unde: Oleum effusum nomen tuum (Cant. I).

VERS. 28.-- Et mortuus est in senectute bona, et plenus dierum et divitiis et gloria, etc. Significat cum copia virtutum et operibus lucis vitam finisse praesentem, dignum quiete futura et coelestis regni gloria. Abraham similiter mortuus est in senectute bona perfectaeque aetatis et plenus dierum. Isaac appositus est populo suo plenus dierum. Joseph mortuus est in senectute bona expletis centum decem vitae suae annis, et conditus aromatibus. Moyses centum et viginti annorum mortuus est, cujus non caligavit oculus, nec dentes moti sunt. Viri autem sanguinum et dolosi non dimidiabunt 664Ddies suos (Psal. LIV). Qui sibi videntur in bonis ducere dies suos, et in puncto ad inferna descendunt (Job XXI).

CHAPTER XXIX.

VERS. 6.-- They promised therefore, etc. (RAB.) He who shall have learned to rule well himself and others, etc., as far as he builds upon it wood, hay, stubble, he shall suffer loss.

VERS. 10.-- And he blessed the Lord, etc. Because the congregation of the faithful, admonished by the writings of the prophets, and stirred up by the sayings and examples of the holy Fathers, should sing praises and hymns to God, offering up the victims of pious confession and the 664Bfruitful works of holy conversation.

VERS. 22.-- And they ate and drank before, etc. (RAB.) This is the joy of the saints and their intent, that by the works of virtues they may guard themselves from the hunger to come, and may feast, and exult in the sight of God.

They anointed Solomon the son a second time, etc. Because Christ is celebrated by all the faithful, with sound faith and pious confession, in the royal and at once the pontifical dignity. Solomon is interpreted peaceable, Sadoc just. But Christ is our peace, who made both one, he is the just Lord and loveth justice: the Judge of the living and of the dead (Ephes. II), him every faithful man anoints first as king and priest, when in the profession of baptism he confesses the true king and priest. And he anoints a second time when by teaching and by working 664Che preaches the same to others, whence: Thy name is as oil poured out (Cant. I).

VERS. 28.-- And he died in a good old age, and full of days and riches and glory, etc. It signifies that he ended the present life with abundance of virtues and with works of light, worthy of the rest to come and of the glory of the heavenly kingdom. Abraham likewise died in a good old age and of a perfect age and full of days. Isaac was gathered to his people full of days. Joseph died in a good old age, the hundred and ten years of his life being completed, and was embalmed with spices. Moses died at a hundred and twenty years, whose eye was not dim, nor were his teeth moved. But bloody and deceitful men shall not live out half of 664Dtheir days (Psal. LIV). Who seem to themselves to lead their days in good things, and in a moment go down to hell (Job XXI). [Vulg. Job 21:13 Ducunt in bonis dies suos, et in puncto ad inferna descendunt, "They spend their days in wealth, and in a moment they go down to hell" — without qui sibi videntur]
