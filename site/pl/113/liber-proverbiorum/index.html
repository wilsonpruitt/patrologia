Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

VERS. 1.-- Parabolae Salomonis. Notandum quod vulgata editio parabolas, quae Hebraice misle , Graece παροιμιας paraemias, id est, proverbia dicit; sed satis convenit. Quae enim parabolae (quia occulta sunt) nuncupantur, etiam proverbia jure vocantur: quia talia sunt, quae saepe in ore colloquentium versari ac memoria debent retineri. Etiam proverbia plerumque tam obscura sunt ut parabolae bene dicantur; unde: Haec in proverbiis locutus sum vobis (Joan. VI), etc. Proverbiorum liber, non ut simplices arbitrantur, patentia habet praecepta, sed quasi in terra aurum, in nuce nucleus, in hirsutis castanearum operculis absconditus fructus inquiritur: ita in eis divinus sensus altius est inquirendus. Parabolae 1079DSalomonis secundum Hebraicam veritatem translatae ab Eusebio, Hieronymo presbytero petente Chromatio et Heliodoro episcopis; παράβολη Graece, Latine similitudines; quod vocabulum ideo Salomon huic operi imposuit, ut non juxta litteram intelligamus quae dicit: in quo Dominum parabolice turbis locuturum significat, sicut et nomine suo regno pacifico, regnum Christi et Ecclesiae; de quo: Multiplicabitur 1080Cejus imperium, et regni ejus non erit finis. Super solium David, et super regnum ejus sedebit (Luc. I). Sicut templi constructione et dedicatione, aedificationem Ecclesiae in tempore resurrectionis. Ipse etiam Christus filius David, et rex spiritualis Israel, secundum testimonium turbae, quae occurrit ei cum ramis palmarum et laudibus (Matth. XXI). Quid autem utilitatis habeant, in titulo monstratur, cum dicitur: Ad sciendam sapientiam et disciplinam; id est, quomodo recte credere et vivere oporteat, quemque quo intentionem cordis dirigere.

VERS. 2.-- Ad intelligenda verba prudentiae. Id est, non solum sapientiam, quam perceperunt, amplectantur, et disciplinae virtutum inserviant, sed etiam verba prudentiae, quibus proficiant, auscultent 1080Det solerter intelligant.

VERS. 5.-- Audiens sapiens, sapientior. Pulchre a sapientia incipit, quia optione sibi data sapientiam petiit et accepit.

Audiens sapiens, sapientior. Ostendit neminem tam sapientem in hac vita, qui non possit sapientior esse etiam dictis minorum. Audivit regina Saba Salomonem minor majorem, et sapientior rediit. Audivit 1081AMoyses socerum multo inferiorem, et sapientior factus est.

Et intelligens. (RAB. in Prov., tom V.) Nemo jactet se de sua scientia, etc., usque ad ad sciendam sapientiam et disciplinam, etc.

VERS. 7.-- Timor Domini. (ID.) Duo sunt timores Domini, etc., usque ad Uterque in futuro cessabit, charitas nunquam excidet.

VERS. 8.-- Audi, fili mi, disciplinam. (ID.) Hactenus proverbialiter, etc., usque ad apud Hebraeos enim spiritus, qui gratiam praestat, genere feminino dicitur.

VERS. 9.-- Addatur gratia. His qui legem Domini servabant, etc., usque ad pro gratia rectae operationis.

Et torques collo tuo, etc. Fulgor perfectae operationis, quae praedicationem (quae per collum procedit,) confirmet indeficienti virtutum connexione.

VERS. 10.-- Fili mi. Affectu patris alloquitur filium, blandiens, ne acquiescat peccatoribus.

VERS. 11.-- Insidiemur. (RAB.) Vel generaliter de latronibus, qui innocentibus insidias parant. Vel specialiter de illis qui vitae auctorem neci tradiderunt. (STRAB.) Cum generaliter ad omnes qui sanguini insidiantur hoc pertineat, etc., usque ad et se potius praesumendo perimunt.

Insontem frustra. Qui peccatum non fecit, nec inventus est dolus in ore ejus.

VERS. 12.-- Deglutiamus eum, sicut infernus viventem. Exstinguamus memoriam virtutum et doctrinae 1081Cillius, ut etiam inter nos vixisse non memoretur.

VERS. 13.-- Omnem pretiosam substantiam reperiemus. Homines, scilicet, quos Christo adhaerentes videbant, quos putabant ab ejus amore et comitatu secernere si eum occiderent, et spoliis talium domos suas replere, id est, eorum animabus sua conventicula.

VERS. 14.-- Sortem mitte nobiscum. De latronibus generaliter patet, quia sociis, quos conquirunt, praedae portionem promittunt; sed et Christi persecutores, quoscunque sibi poterant, adjungebant, quos eum sequi videbant, extra synagogam faciebant, id est, sua communione privabant.

VERS. 16.-- Ad malum currunt. Non solum quod 1081Dfacturi, sed quod pro factis passuri. Nam quanto ad facinus implendum properant, tanto ad poenam pro facinore tolerandam propinquant.

VERS. 17.-- Frustra. (RAB.) Caetera de insidiatoribus Christi specialiter, etc., usque ad in pauperibus enim nihil habet causae.

VERS. 20.-- Sapientia. Hucusque persecutiones, quas Christo Judaei intulere; hinc ea, quae per apostolos persecutoribus eisdem post passionem loquitur. Sapientia, id est Christus, loquitur foris, id est in luce palam, ut relicta infantia sint parvuli malitia.

Parvuli. (RAB.) Parvuli dicuntur sensu tardiores, etc., usque ad quae filius ipse veniens perfectis sensu contulit?

VERS. 23.-- Convertimini. Sapientia hortatur converti ad Deum, et profert spiritum.

Ad correctionem. Per apostolos Judaeos corripit, et vel post ascensionem et resurrectionem monet converti.

VERS. 26.-- Ego quoque. (ID.) Similiter in psalmo, etc., usque ad quasi nihil contemnerent.

Quando venerit super vos. (RAB.) Potest haec tota correctio sapientiae, etc., usque ad quod enim sequitur,

VERS. 28.-- Mane consurgent et non invenient me, tempus judicii significat, quo alii in vitam, alii in opprobrium resurgent sempiternum.

Invocabunt. Non leguntur Judaei in obsidione Dominum invocasse; etsi qui invocaverunt, quia vere non poenituerunt, exauditi non sunt.

Suisque consiliis saturabuntur. Quibus dicebant, etc., usque ad potest accipi.

Parvulorum. Sensu, unde supra, etc., usque ad sed quia avertunt, se interficiunt.

VERS. 1.-- The parables of Solomon. It is to be noted that the Vulgate edition calls them parables, which in Hebrew is misle , in Greek παροιμιας paraemias, that is, it calls them proverbs; but it agrees well enough. For the things which are named parables (because they are hidden) are also by right called proverbs: because they are such as ought often to be current in the mouth of those conversing and to be retained in the memory. Proverbs too are for the most part so obscure that they may well be called parables; whence: These things I have spoken to you in proverbs (Joan. VI), etc. The book of Proverbs does not, as the simple suppose, have open precepts, but as gold is sought in the earth, the kernel in the nut, the hidden fruit in the shaggy husks of chestnuts: so in them the divine sense is to be sought out more deeply. The Parables 1079Dof Solomon, translated according to the Hebrew verity by Eusebius, Hieronymus the priest, at the asking of Chromatius and Heliodorus the bishops; παράβολη in Greek, in Latin similitudes; which word Solomon therefore imposed upon this work, that we should not understand according to the letter the things he says: in which he signifies that the Lord would speak to the crowds in parables, as also by his own name and by his peaceable kingdom the kingdom of Christ and of the Church; of which: His empire shall be multiplied, 1080Cand of his kingdom there shall be no end. Upon the throne of David, and upon his kingdom shall he sit (Luc. I). [Vulg. Isa. 9:7 pacis non erit finis, "of peace there shall be no end"; regni ejus non erit finis is Luc. 1:33] As by the construction and dedication of the temple, [he signifies] the building of the Church in the time of the resurrection. Christ himself also is the son of David, and the king of the spiritual Israel, according to the testimony of the crowd which met him with palm branches and with praises (Matth. XXI). But what profit they have is shown in the title, when it is said: To know wisdom and discipline; that is, how one ought rightly to believe and to live, and whither each ought to direct the intention of his heart.

VERS. 2.-- To understand the words of prudence. That is, let them not only embrace the wisdom which they have received, and serve the discipline of the virtues, but let them also hearken to the words of prudence, by which they may profit, 1080Dand shrewdly understand them.

VERS. 5.-- A wise man hearing shall be wiser. Beautifully does he begin from wisdom, because, when the choice was given him, he asked for wisdom and received it.

A wise man hearing shall be wiser. He shows that no one is so wise in this life that he cannot be wiser even by the sayings of lesser men. The queen of Saba heard Solomon, the lesser the greater, and returned wiser. Moses heard 1081Ahis father-in-law, much his inferior, and was made wiser.

And he that understandeth. (RAB. in Prov., tom V.) Let no man boast himself of his own knowledge, etc., as far as to know wisdom and discipline, etc.

VERS. 7.-- The fear of the Lord. (ID.) There are two fears of the Lord, etc., as far as Both shall cease hereafter, charity shall never fall away.

VERS. 8.-- Hear, my son, the discipline. (ID.) Thus far proverbially, etc., as far as for among the Hebrews the spirit, which bestows grace, is spoken of in the feminine gender.

VERS. 9.-- That grace may be added. To those who kept the law of the Lord, etc., as far as for the grace of right working.

And a chain to thy neck, etc. The splendour of perfect working, which may confirm the preaching (which proceeds through the neck,) by an unfailing linking of virtues.

VERS. 10.-- My son. With a father's affection he addresses the son, coaxing him, that he may not consent to sinners.

VERS. 11.-- Let us lie in wait. (RAB.) Either generally of robbers, who prepare ambushes for the innocent. Or specially of those who delivered the author of life over to death. (STRAB.) Since this pertains generally to all who lie in wait for blood, etc., as far as and by presuming they rather destroy themselves.

The innocent without cause. Who did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth.

VERS. 12.-- Let us swallow him up, as hell doth, alive. Let us extinguish the memory of his virtues and of his doctrine, 1081Cso that it may not even be remembered that he lived among us.

VERS. 13.-- We shall find all precious substance. Men, namely, whom they saw cleaving to Christ, whom they thought to sever from his love and company if they should kill him, and to fill their own houses with the spoils of such men, that is, their own conventicles with their souls.

VERS. 14.-- Cast in thy lot with us. Of robbers it is plain generally, because they promise a portion of the booty to the companions whom they seek out; but Christ's persecutors also joined to themselves whomsoever they could, and those whom they saw following him they put outside the synagogue, that is, they deprived them of their own communion.

VERS. 16.-- They run to evil. Not only what they are 1081Dabout to do, but what they are about to suffer for their deeds. For by how much they hasten to fulfil the crime, by so much do they draw near to the punishment to be borne for the crime.

VERS. 17.-- In vain. (RAB.) The rest specially concerning those who lay ambush for Christ, etc., as far as for in the poor he has nothing of a case.

VERS. 20.-- Wisdom. Thus far the persecutions which the Jews brought upon Christ; from here, those things which he speaks through the apostles to those same persecutors after the passion. Wisdom, that is Christ, speaks abroad, that is, openly in the light, that, infancy being left behind, they may be little ones in malice.

1082A(RAB.) For Christ is indeed the Wisdom of God, etc., as far as they should say to anyone.

Abroad. (ID.) Openly, after the resurrection, etc., as far as: in the street his voice.

In the streets she uttereth her voice, etc. The things which he had taught before few he plainly repeated, whence: The things which I say to you in the darkness, speak ye into the light. [Vulg. Matt. 10:27 Quod dico vobis in tenebris, dicite in lumine, "speak ye in the light"]

VERS. 21.-- At the head of the crowds she crieth out, etc. (ID.) Because even to the princes, who seemed to have prevailed against him so that he should be crucified, he openly recalled to memory the guilt of the murder which they had perpetrated, and called them to the remedy of penance.

In the doors of the gates of the city. (ID.) The city of the Lord, the Church gathered into one out of both peoples, etc., as far as he called back his own grace; so that, as Luke says: A great multitude of the priests should obey the faith (Act. VI).

VERS. 22.-- How long. Now you have seen Christ in the flesh working miracles, whom you have despised: now at least love him raised up from death, and he will forgive what you have sinned.

Little ones. (RAB.) Those slower in understanding are called little ones, etc., as far as which the Son himself, coming, conferred upon those perfect in understanding?

VERS. 23.-- Be ye converted. Wisdom exhorts them to be converted to God, and puts forth the spirit.

To correction. [Vulg. Pr. 1:23 ad correptionem meam, "to my reproof"] Through the apostles he rebukes the Jews, and admonishes them to be converted at least after the ascension and the resurrection.

VERS. 26.-- I also. (ID.) Likewise in the psalm, etc., as far as as though they despised nothing.

1082CVERS. 27.-- When sudden calamity shall rush in. He speaks of the sudden siege of Jerusalem and the storming of the whole province wrought by the Romans. Of which, Seeing the city, he wept over it (Luc. XIX).

When it shall come upon you. (RAB.) This whole correction of wisdom can, etc., as far as for that which follows,

VERS. 28.-- They shall rise early and shall not find me, signifies the time of judgment, in which some shall rise again unto life, others unto everlasting reproach.

They shall call upon me. The Jews are not read to have called upon the Lord in the siege; and even if some did call upon him, because they did not truly repent, they were not heard.

VERS. 29.-- They have held it hateful. The just man on the contrary, The unjust, he says, have I hated, and thy law have I loved.

1082DVERS. 31.-- They shall eat therefore. (ID.) Because they would not walk in my ways, etc., as far as and it shall be well with thee.

And they shall be filled with their own devices. By which they were saying, etc., as far as it can be taken.

VERS. 32.-- Turning away. Turning away and prosperity destroys, when the mind turned away from the fear of its Maker seems, by the anger of that same Maker, to suffer nothing adverse in those things wherein it sins. But as Job says: They spend their days in good things, and in a moment they go down to hell.

Of little ones. In understanding, whence above, etc., as far as but because they turn away, they kill themselves.

VERS. 33.-- But he that shall hear me, without terror 1083Ashall rest, etc. It can be understood in this life, and in the life to come, etc., as far as of the resurrection he shall rest.

VERS. 1.-- Fili mi. Duo hic notat, sapientiam esse de coelestibus, prudentiam de saecularibus.

Abscondit mandata Dei penes se, etc., usque ad quasi semen secus viam jactatum volucres tollant.

VERS. 2.-- Audiat sapientiam. Audit non aure, etc., usque ad hoc est enim quod sequitur:

Inclina cor. Inclina, humilia, munda, etc., usque ad et revelasti ea humilibus.

Si enim sapientiam invocaveris. Invocare, etc., usque ad nec liberum debet abesse arbitrium.

VERS. 4.-- Et sicut thesauros. Qui thesauros effodit, 1083Betc., usque ad nec quiescat, donec inveniat.

VERS. 5.-- Tunc intelliges. Timorem amicabilem, etc., usque ad sed etiam scientiam ipsius divinitatis (quae perpetuo beatum facit) invenies.

VERS. 6.-- Quia Dominus dat. Respicit hic versiculus ab hoc quod supra dixerat: Si enim sapientiam invocaveris, etc.

Ideo namque sapientia, etc., usque ad sed a Domino datur.

Ex ore. Aeterna filii nativitate, de qua alibi: Ego ex ore Altissimi prodii, primogenita ante omnem creaturam (Isai. LVIII). Vel, os Domini in sanctis, unde: Os Domini locutum est. Et ex ore ejus scientia nascitur et prudentia, dum Dominus os humanum sanctificat, et suum facit, ut scienter et prudenter loquatur.

1083CVERS. 7.-- Custodiet. Ut in quocunque virtutum gradu rectos juvet et simplices. Job simplex et rectus (Job. I); simplex, per innocentiam mansuetudinis, et rectus, per cautelam discretionis: simplex, nullum laedens, imo cunctis proficiens, rectus, a nullo corruptus.

VERS. 12.-- A via mala. Ad quam vocant, qui dicunt: Veni nobiscum, insidiemur sanguini, perversa loquitur, qui est auctor hujus persuasionis.

VERS. 13.-- Qui relinquunt iter. Pulchre, etc., usque ad finis ejus in tenebris exterioribus.

VERS. 14.-- Qui laetantur. Quia per vias tenebrosas ambulant, etc., usque ad noxiamque laetitiam salubri fletu castigarent.

VERS. 16.-- Ut eruaris a muliere. Pendet hic versus, 1083Detc., usque ad: et ipsi sunt jacula.

VERS. 17.-- Et relinquit ducem. Patet et de mente haeretica, etc., usque ad quam se die baptismi servaturam promiserat.

VERS. 18.-- Pacti Dei sui, etc. Quod cum viro suo tempore nuptiarum pepigit, Domino teste, quod fidem, scilicet castitatis sibi invicem servarent, vel in baptismo.

Inclinata est enim. Omnis conversatio adulterae ad interitum ducit, etc., usque ad id est, ad spirituum tendit tormenta malignorum.

VERS. 19.-- Ad eam non revertentur. Mortem scilicet, non mulierem, etc., usque ad non sibi attribuat, sed gratiae Dei.

1084AVERS. 21.-- Qui enim. Hic rectis jungit simplices, etc., usque ad quae cum Christo in coelis regnat, perveniunt.

VERS. 22.-- Impii. De illis dicitur specialiter, etc., usque ad de fine bonorum, qui autem me audierit, absque terrore quiescet.

CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1.-- My son. He notes two things here, that wisdom is of heavenly matters, prudence of secular.

He hides the commandments of God with himself, etc., as far as as though the birds should take away the seed cast by the wayside.

VERS. 2.-- That thy ear may hear wisdom. He hears not with the ear, etc., as far as for this is that which follows:

Incline thy heart. Incline, humble, cleanse, etc., as far as and thou hast revealed them to the humble.

For if thou shalt call upon wisdom. To call upon, etc., as far as nor ought free will to be absent.

VERS. 4.-- And as treasures. He who digs out treasures, 1083Betc., as far as nor let him rest until he find.

VERS. 5.-- Then shalt thou understand. A friendly fear, etc., as far as but thou shalt find also the knowledge of the Godhead itself (which makes blessed for ever).

VERS. 6.-- Because the Lord giveth. This little verse looks back from that which he had said above: For if thou shalt call upon wisdom, etc.

For therefore wisdom, etc., as far as but is given by the Lord.

Out of his mouth. By the eternal nativity of the Son, of which elsewhere: I came out of the mouth of the Most High, the firstborn before all creatures (Isai. LVIII). Or, the mouth of the Lord is in the saints, whence: The mouth of the Lord hath spoken. And out of his mouth knowledge is born and prudence, while the Lord sanctifies the human mouth, and makes it his own, that it may speak knowingly and prudently.

1083CVERS. 7.-- He will keep. That in whatsoever degree of the virtues he may help the upright and the simple. Job [was] simple and upright (Job. I); simple, through the innocence of meekness, and upright, through the caution of discretion: simple, injuring none, nay rather profiting all, upright, corrupted by none.

VERS. 12.-- From the evil way. To which they call, who say: Come with us, let us lie in wait for blood, he speaketh perverse things, who is the author of this persuasion.

VERS. 13.-- Who leave the way. Beautifully, etc., as far as his end is in the exterior darkness.

VERS. 14.-- Who rejoice. Because they walk through dark ways, etc., as far as and they should chastise their hurtful gladness with wholesome weeping.

VERS. 16.-- That thou mayest be delivered from the woman. This verse hangs, 1083Detc., as far as: and they themselves are darts.

VERS. 17.-- And she leaveth the guide. It is plain also of the heretical mind, etc., as far as which she had promised on the day of baptism that she would keep.

VERS. 18.-- Of the covenant of her God, etc. Which she covenanted with her husband at the time of the nuptials, the Lord being witness, that they should keep faith, namely of chastity, to one another, or in baptism.

For she is inclined. All the converse of the adulteress leads to destruction, etc., as far as that is, it tends to the torments of the malignant spirits.

VERS. 19.-- They shall not return to her. Death, namely, not the woman, etc., as far as let him not attribute it to himself, but to the grace of God.

1084AVERS. 21.-- For they that. Here he joins the simple to the upright, etc., as far as they come to that which reigns with Christ in the heavens.

VERS. 22.-- The wicked. It is said specially of those, etc., as far as concerning the end of the good, but he that shall hear me shall rest without terror.

CAPUT III.

Fili mi, ne. Docet misericordiae operibus insistendum, etc., usque ad ornat actibus bonis, quem instruit.

VERS. 2.-- Pacem, id est, statum beatitudinis, etc., usque ad longo tempore in pacis quiete viveret.

VERS. 7.-- Time Deum et recede a malo. Quale est illud in Job, etc., usque ad divini timoris memoria 1084Ccoercet.

VERS. 12.-- Quem enim diligit Dominus corripit. Tanto ergo minus, etc., usque ad unde ipse ait: Ego quos amo, arguo et castigo.

VERS. 13.-- Beatus homo, etc. Quia positos sub disciplina, etc., usque ad et in futuro ejus visione perfrui.

VERS. 14.-- Melior est. Non metuat de correctione 1085Ainopiae, quisquis opes acquisivit sapientiae, omnibus enim thesauris munus coeleste praecellit.

Primi et purissimi. Quia radix sapientiae timor Domini, hi sunt primi ac purissimi fructus, etc., usque ad a Domino perfectius promittitur.

VERS. 16.-- Longitudo dierum in dextera ejus et in sinistra. Visione suae divinitatis, etc., usque ad et in futuro vitam aeternam.

VERS. 17.-- Viae ejus. Actiones et doctrinae, etc., usque ad pacis et dilectionis exempla pertinent.

VERS. 22.-- And there shall be life to thy soul. Thy soul has life with God, if thou shalt keep his decrees: although the flesh be pressed by the discipline of correction or even of death.

VERS. 27.-- Do not forbid him that is able to do good. Because above he diligently admonished that the law of the Lord and his counsel be kept, and promised eternal rest to those who keep them: now he repeats in manifold ways the things that are to be kept. And first, returning to that which he had passed over, he teaches that mercy is to be shown to the neighbour, and that one must live justly with him. Then he forewarns that an enemy also is to be guarded against. Do not, therefore, he says, forbid. 1085DLest thou hear from the Lord with the Pharisees: You yourselves went not in, and those that were going in you have hindered (Luc. XI).

VERS. 28.-- Say not to thy friend: Go, and come again, to-morrow I will give. Not only of alms, but of all the commandments of Christ, who says: You are my friends, if you do (Joan. XV), etc.

VERS. 30.-- Strive not against a man. [Vulg. Pr. 3:30 Ne contendas adversus hominem frustra, "strive not against a man without cause"; the gloss turns on the frustra the lemma omits] He does not forbid striving against him who does evil, that he may be corrected, because that which a sure necessity compels is not done in vain. Nay rather, he who says: If thy brother shall offend against thee, rebuke him (Matth. XVIII), himself wills that thou shouldst bring back the wanderer, as much as thou canst, to the way of truth.

VERS. 32.-- Because every 1086Amocker is an abomination to the Lord. Every mocker, he says; because he is a mocker who either despises to fulfil the words of God which he knows, or corrupts those same words by understanding and teaching them perversely. He also is a mocker who despises his promises as though they were depraved, and the anger of his strictness, as though tolerable; he spurns. And likewise he who, being shameless, insults the simplicity of his neighbours. And therefore the divine justice deservedly abominates every mocker of this kind. But well is it added:

And his conversation is with the simple, because the proud, while they mock the simple, judge themselves more prudent than those whom they deride. But the prudence of these, as James says: is earthly, sensual, devilish (Jacob. III). But the conversation of the Lord is with the simple, because he enlightens with the secrets of wisdom those 1086Bwhom he considers to have nothing of earthly pride and of duplicity. Whence he says: Thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones (Luc. X).

VERS. 33.-- In the house of the wicked. As of the rich man clothed in purple, who in this life lacked spiritual things, and afterwards came to such want that he did not receive even the drop of water which he sought, whence: Who hath blessed us with all spiritual blessing in heavenly places in Christ, who at the recompense shall hear: Come, ye blessed, (Matth. XV), etc. But according to the letter it often happens that those who plunder what belongs to others are at the last consumed with want; but those who give freely of their own abound even in earthly goods.

VERS. 34.-- Mockers. These verses James and Peter set down according to the ancient translation: Because 1086CGod resisteth the proud, but to the humble he giveth grace (Jacob. IV).

The parables of Solomon. He sets a new title, because he begins a new kind of speech, namely, that he should not, as before, dispute at greater length concerning the several parts of good men or of evil men; but should describe the acts of both in alternating verses.

VERS. 1.-- A wise son. He who keeps well the mysteries of the faith received gladdens God the Father; but he who defiles these by evil action or by heresy saddens his mother the Church.

VERS. 2.-- The treasures of wickedness shall profit nothing. Both from a transitory death, etc., as far as because they were given for life, they profit.

CHAPTER XI.

VERS. 1.-- A deceitful balance. Either of money or of judgment, etc., as far as and it agrees with the action.

VERS. 2.-- Where pride shall be. The proud either bear themselves insolently through contempt or through ignorance of discipline, or they heap reviling upon their neighbours, or he that exalteth himself shall be humbled.

VERS. 7.-- When the wicked man is dead. This Origen 1094Aknew not, etc., as far as and to them perhaps belongs that which follows.

The expectation of the solicitous. The solicitude of good men is to fulfil the precepts of God; but that of evil men who knowingly sin is that they shall be hurried away to everlasting punishment. And therefore the expectation of the former shall be crowned; that of the latter shall be condemned, even before the judgment, their own conscience condemning them.

VERS. 9.-- The dissembler. The heretic, feigning catholic doctrine, deceives his hearer. They who follow the truth of the Gospel shall be set free by catholic wisdom, lest they be taken by the heretical snare.

VERS. 14.-- Where there is no governor, the people shall fall, but safety where there are many counsels. For lest thou shouldst think, etc., as far as that he may be corrected by many.

VERS. 15.-- He shall be afflicted with evil. It is plain according to the letter, etc., as far as he shall render himself worthy of vengeance before the eyes of the strict judge.

But he that is aware of snares shall be secure. He shall be secure in the world to come who now, being rightly fearful, both heals his own sins through penance and keeps himself untouched by the fellowship of the impenitent.

VERS. 27.-- He that seeketh good things riseth well at daybreak, etc., that is, he rises to life in the time of the resurrection, because he now does good things in the time of working. But he who searches out the evil things which he may speak or do is held back from higher things by their weight.

VERS. 28.-- But the just as, etc. As it were, they who by hope of future rewards do good things in the present rightly receive what they hope for. For the green leaf on the tree signifies that the fruits which it does not yet show will come: and the just spring up as a green leaf, who, being saved by hope, cease not to advance in the grace of faith and of the virtues.

VERS. 30.-- He that receiveth souls. He who takes up the care of souls for the Lord, etc., as far as he provides that he may reign more loftily with the Lord.

VERS. 31.-- If the just man receive on earth. If the martyrs suffer so much unjustly, etc., as far as for even if we are not wicked, certainly if we deny that we are sinners, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.

CHAPTER XII.

VERS. 1.-- He that is good, etc. The connexion of these little verses is this; he who is good does not trust in his own thoughts; but seeks the grace of the Lord, 1095Aand through it lives godly. But he who trusts in his own thoughts cannot be good: for he who cares not to seek the grace of God persists in depraved action.

VERS. 5.-- Thoughts. The just their own deeds, etc., as far as having forgotten the fear of the Lord.

VERS. 7.-- Turn the wicked. Much impiety among the gods of the nations, etc., as far as but whatsoever members pass from hence to that place find the joys which they were hoping for.

But the bowels of the wicked are cruel, who do not have compassion on those subject to them; but as the Lord says: They strike 1095Bthe boys and the maidservants, saying: My Lord delayeth his coming. But the root of the just shall prosper, faith, hope, charity, whereby they are rooted in Christ, do not deceive them; but they shall receive in the world to come more than they hope for.

VERS. 16.-- The fool, etc. He who desires to be avenged in the present is a fool; but he who for the Lord's sake despises it is wise.

But he that dissembleth: he does not so wish it to be hidden that it should last, but that it should be utterly blotted out; for it is the nature of anger that, being uttered, it burns the more; being hidden, it languishes.

VERS. 18.-- But the tongue of the wise is health, etc., because both, by themselves fulfilling the good things which they promise, 1095Cthey come to life; and they show to others the things which they are to follow

VERS. 19.-- The lip of truth, etc. The catholic faith shall abide for ever; but he who suddenly brings in new teachings is a minister of falsehood.

VERS. 23.-- A crafty man. [Vulg. Prov. 12:23 Homo versatus celat scientiam, "a practised man hideth knowledge"] Wise and prudent: who sometimes hides knowledge, either not being able to speak to them as to spiritual men, but as to carnal; or not willing to give what is holy to dogs, or to cast pearls before swine, or to send them.

The heart of the unwise provoketh. Either when they desire to be wise more than it behoves to be wise, and, vanishing away in their thoughts, they have and hold folly instead of wisdom; or, forcing upon others the things which they cannot grasp, as it were the light of the sun upon weak eyes, that is, of wisdom, 1095Dthey take away that which they seemed to have.

But that which is slack. They who are loose in mind, etc., as far as for such men are not to be despaired of, when he adds, the sorrow of the just, etc.

VERS. 25.-- Sorrow in the heart, etc., because it is necessary that first the sorrow of penance should healthfully humble the sinner, and afterwards that the word of the reconciliation granted through the judgment of the priest should gladden or refresh him.

He that neglecteth a loss, etc. If this is taken according to the letter, etc., as far as or delivers his very flesh to death?

1096AVERS. 27.-- He shall not find, etc. For money got by fraud, etc., as far as wherewith the brightness of eternal life, more precious than gold, is bought.

VERS. 11.-- Substance, etc. They who wish to become rich fall into temptation, etc., as far as he shall receive the manifold gifts of heavenly blessedness, of whom:

The friend of fools. VERS. 23.-- Much food. Another translator, etc., as far as but that which he subjoins agrees with either sense.

VERS. 24.-- He that spareth the rod hateth his son, etc. For both a good father his son, and a catholic master his disciple, carefully instructs, lest he turn aside to iniquity.

CAPUT XV.

VERS. 1.-- Responsio mollis frangit iram. Qui verba increpantis humiliter suscipit, jam propinquat veniae reatus quem gessit. Qui arguenti resultat, contra se iram judicis provocat.

VERS. 2.-- Ebullit stultitiam. Scripturam pervertendo, suos sensus auctoritati praeponendo; velut fatui philosophi, ut Porphyrius, et Julianus, qui contra 1098AEcclesiae doctores stultitiae suae fluenta fundebant.

VERS. 10.-- Doctrina mala. Qui solverit unum de mandatis istis minimis, et docuerit sic homines, minimus est in regno coelorum. Talibus tamen, si correcti fuerint, patet reditus ad veniam: si autem increpationes non receperint, omnino morientur.

1098BVERS. 15.-- Omnes dies. Omne tempus populi Dei, etc., usque ad quasi jugi delectantur convivio.

VERS. 17.-- Melius est vocari ad olera. Multo enim utilius est simplicis vitae innocentiam cum charitate servare, etc., usque ad, discedite a me operarii iniquitatis.

VERS. 19.-- Pigrorum, quos piget operari justitiam, etc., usque ad sed quia hoc bonos non praepedit, adjungit:

Via justorum absque offendiculo. Justi enim quidquid adversitatis in sua conversatione invenerint, non offendunt, quia aeternae spei et internae contemplationis saltu adversa transiliunt.

VERS. 23.-- Laetatur homo. Humanum est de 1098Csententia quasi prudenter prolata laetari. Sed vere sapiens non solum quod loquitur, sed etiam opportunitatem loci, et temporis, et personae cui loquitur, diligenter inquirit.

VERS. 24.-- De inferno novissimo, etc. Primus infernus caecitas peccantis animae, et verae lucis scientia carentis, de quo: Descendunt in infernum viventes (Psal. LIV).

VERS. 25.-- Viduae Ecclesiae, scilicet sponsae suae, pro cujus vita mori dignatus est.

VERS. 26.-- Firmabitur: in judicio, quando non solum factis, sed et verbis omnibus digna praemia reddet.

VERS. 30.-- Lux oculorum laetificat animam. Jure mentem delectat humanam, etc., usque ad sic enim 1098Dmagis fervet opus.

Lux oculorum. Alia translatio: Videns bona oculus delectat corda bona, scilicet, Domini in terra viventium, quae, quia in oculo mentis contemplatur, merito corde delectatur, quamvis affligatur exterius.

CHAPTER XV.

VERS. 1.-- A mild answer breaketh wrath. He who humbly receives the words of one rebuking him, the guilt which he has borne already draws near to pardon. He who kicks back at one reproving him provokes against himself the wrath of the judge.

VERS. 2.-- Belcheth out folly. By perverting the Scripture, by setting his own opinions before authority; like the foolish philosophers, as Porphyry and Julian, who against 1098Athe teachers of the Church poured forth the streams of their folly.

VERS. 5.-- The fool laugheth at the discipline of his father. Every sinner is a fool who, given over to earthly acts, foresees nothing to come: he laughs at discipline while he disparages the divine correction.

VERS. 7.-- The heart of fools is unlike. Either unlike to itself, by divers thoughts, and it never remains the same. Against which it is said of Anna: And her countenance was no more changed into divers looks.

VERS. 10.-- Evil doctrine. He that shall break one of these least commandments, and shall so teach men, is the least in the kingdom of heaven. [Vulg. Matth. 5:19 Qui ergo solverit unum de mandatis istis minimis, et docuerit sic homines, minimus vocabitur in regno caelorum, "he shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven"] Yet to such men, if they have been corrected, the way back to pardon lies open: but if they receive not reproofs, they shall utterly die.

1098BVERS. 15.-- All the days. All the time of the people of God, etc., as far as they are delighted as with a continual banquet.

VERS. 17.-- It is better to be called to herbs. For it is far more profitable to keep the innocence of a simple life with charity, etc., as far as, depart from me, ye workers of iniquity.

VERS. 19.-- Of the slothful, whom it irks to work justice, etc., as far as but because this does not hinder good men, he adds:

The way of the just is without stumbling-block. For the just, whatever adversity they have found in their manner of life, do not stumble, because by the leap of eternal hope and of inward contemplation they leap over adversities.

CHAPTER XXIV.

VERS. 1.-- Emulate not evil men. Desire not to imitate those whom thou seest entangled in the vices aforesaid; but take care to bestow thy labour upon wisdom, whence he subjoins: By wisdom shall a house be built.

VERS. 4.-- A wise man. Not every strong man is wise, 1107Cbut the contrary: because although he be weak in body, if wisdom be present, he is altogether strong, and overcomes the contests of the adversary, that is, of the devil.

VERS. 7.-- Wisdom is too high for a fool. The fool cannot ascend unto wisdom, because he lies in the lowest places of the vices; because although in the present he thinks that he knows somewhat, in the gate, that is, in the going forth of this life, he shall find that he was nothing.

VERS. 8.-- He that deviseth to do evil. He discloses whom he calls a fool: not one slow of wit, but him who sins even by thinking.

VERS. 10.-- If thou despair in the day of distress. Nothing is more execrable than despair: and he that has had it loses constancy both in the common labours of this life and, what is worse, in the contest of the faith.

1107DVERS. 11.-- Deliver them that are led to death. Deliver them that are deceived by heretics, etc., as far as if those about to perish of cold, refresh thou with food, with raiment.

VERS. 13.-- Eat, my son. He compares the doctrine of wisdom to honey and to the honeycomb, etc., as far as and it is perceived with delay.

VERS. 15.-- Lie not in wait. Seek not occasion, etc., as far as whence he subjoins:

VERS. 5.-- Omnis sermo. Proprietatem Graeci nominis, etc., usque ad vera enim omnia et ignita.

Clypeus est, id est protector omnium sperantium in se. Clypeus contra hostium insidias et omnia adversa.

VERS. 7.-- Duo rogavi te. Haec adhuc vir, etc., usque ad in oblivionem decidat aeternorum.

VERS. 11.-- Generatio quae patri. Superius vitae puritatem, etc., usque ad si natus est, fuit tempus quando non fuit filius.

VERS. 14.-- Generatio quae pro dentibus gladios habet. Haec est, quae suam perfidiam aliis immittere conatur; et sicut corpora gladiis, ita audientium 1113Banimas veneno allocutionis occidit.

VERS. 15.-- Sanguisugae duae sunt filiae. Diabolus, qui siti peccata suadendi perpetuo ardet; Duae filiae, quae ejus imitantur ardorem, luxuria et philargyria. Nam luxuriae, quo liberius frena laxantur, eo noxius delectatur; et Crescit amor nummi, quantum ipsa pecunia crescit.

Tria sunt insaturabilia. Infernus nunquam redundat; meretrix habet similia. Corpus non satiatur deliciis, et avarus non dicit, sufficit; alii infernum dicunt diabolum, qui non satiatur seductione humana.

VERS. 16.-- Os vulvae, et terra quae non satiatur aqua, ut supra ignem, incendium gehennae, qui nunquam desinit.

1113CVERS. 17.-- Oculum qui subsannat. Qui divina judicia reprehendunt, etc., usque ad qui peccati nigredinem humiliter confitentur.

Filii aquilae: quia per gratiam ejus sunt renati, qui in carne ad coelum transvolavit, qui alibi filii sponsi dicuntur.

VERS. 18.-- Tria sunt mihi difficilia. Ita est ad litteram, etc., usque ad difficile est scire quot cogitationibus juvenis agitur, qui viam veritatis deservit.

VERS. 20.-- Talis est et via mulieris adulterae. Adultera quoque mens ut ea quae commemoravit, semper vaga est, quae ubi celare peccata poterit, negat se aliquid patrasse mali.

VERS. 24.-- Quatuor: scilicet minores videntur 1113Djusti in terra, quam reprobi, id est abjectiores; sed tamen sapientiores sunt sapientibus mundi, quia aeterna quaerunt, et pro his pati desiderant.

VERS. 25.-- Formicae populus infirmus. Et Ecclesiae populus, etc., usque ad quibus vivat in aeternum.

VERS. 26.-- Lepusculus, alii choerogryllus, etc., usque ad sed hic quidam pro lepusculo hericium habent.

(HIERON.) Habent enim Hebraea verbum saphan, quod murem et choerogyllum et leporem et hericium interpretantur.

Lepusculus, etc. Haec est plebs Ecclesiae, quae invalida, quia injurias suas ulcisci non quaerit, et quia non suis viribus, sed in Dei auxilio sperare salutem 1114Adidicit, quod est cubile habere in petra, unde dicit cum Psalmista: Esto mihi in lapidem fortissimum, in domum munitam, ut salves me, quia petra mea et munitio mea es tu.

VERS. 27.-- Regem locusta. Locusta, etc., usque ad tamen instanter in suis locis Christo militare, et germina carnalium consuevit absumere voluptatum.

VERS. 28.-- Stellio manibus. Stellio alas non habet, etc., usque ad obtinenda aeterni regni moenia virtus operationis levat.

VERS. 29.-- Tria sunt quae gradiuntur. Non nos moveat, etc., usque ad quia in fallacia sua ad tempus Antichristus prosperatur.

VERS. 31.-- Gallus, scilicet praedicatores, inter hujus saeculi tenebras verum mane nuntiantes.

1114BEt aries. Primus ordo sacerdotum, etc., usque ad postquam elevatus est in sublime.

VERS. 33.-- Qui autem ubera fortiter premit, etc., usque ad quia carnale efficitur hoc quod nimia discussione sentitur.

Et qui provocat iras, producit. Quid prior parabola ad litteram significet, aperit, etc., usque ad elicit sanguinem.

CHAPTER XXX.

VERS. 1.-- The words of the gatherer. Thus far the parables of Solomon, which the men of Ezechias king of Juda copied out. From hence again the words of Solomon, which is discerned from the very name whereby in Greek he is ἐκκλησιαστής, in Latin congregans; for Ecclesia is a convocation.

The vision which. The vision which he spoke, because what he saw with God he laid open, by contemplating the more secret things, 1112Cto men. For whosoever is strengthened by the Spirit of God says with a humble heart the things that follow, I am the most foolish of men, etc.

VERS. 2.-- The most foolish. The children of this world are more prudent than the children of light in their generation (Luc. XVI). And the Apostle: If any man seem to be wise in this world, let him become a fool, that he may be wise (I Cor. III).

I have not learned from man, but, God giving it, that I may know the knowledge of the saints. And the Apostle: We are fools for Christ's sake: again he says, But we speak wisdom among the perfect. Yet wisdom not of men, [Vulg. 1 Cor. 2:6 sapientiam vero non hujus saeculi, "not the wisdom of this world"] namely neither of the princes of this world, who are brought to nought.

VERS. 4.-- Who hath ascended up into heaven. The Son of God after the resurrection, and at the time of our resurrection 1112Dis to descend unto judgment.

Who hath held in. Every spirit, of angels, of men, of living creatures and of storms, lest it fail, is held in by the power of God.

Who hath raised up all. And now God rouses all things which live and grow throughout the world, men, living creatures, seeds, that they may subsist by their own life: and in the day of judgment he will rouse the human race from death from the four winds.

What is his name, and what is the name of his son, he says the name of God the Father and of the Son: whose mystery secular knowledge could not naturally know, although it could understand by the things done that he is God who made all things.

1113AIf thou knowest: thou, namely, who, boasting of thy wisdom, callest the mysteries of our faith foolishness.

VERS. 5.-- Every word. The property of the Greek name, etc., as far as for all are true and fiery.

He is a buckler, that is, the protector of all that hope in him. A buckler against the snares of enemies and all adversities.

VERS. 7.-- Two things I have asked of thee. These things still the man, etc., as far as he fall into forgetfulness of eternal things.

VERS. 11.-- A generation that to its father. Above the purity of life, etc., as far as if he was born, there was a time when he was not a son.

VERS. 14.-- A generation that hath swords for teeth. This is that which strives to send its own perfidy into others; and as bodies with swords, so the hearers' 1113Bsouls it slays with the poison of its address.

VERS. 15.-- The horseleech hath two daughters. The devil, who burns perpetually with the thirst of persuading unto sins; Two daughters, which imitate his ardour, luxury and philargyria. For as to luxury, the more freely the reins are loosed to it, the more harmfully is it delighted; and The love of money grows, as much as the money itself grows.

Three things are insatiable. Hell never overflows; the harlot has the like. The body is not satisfied with delights, and the covetous man says not, It is enough; others say that hell is the devil, who is not satisfied with human seduction.

VERS. 16.-- The mouth of the womb, and the earth which is not filled with water, as above the fire, the burning of Gehenna, which never ceases.

1113CVERS. 17.-- The eye that mocketh. They who reprehend the divine judgments, etc., as far as who humbly confess the blackness of sin.

The young of the eagle: because they are born again through the grace of him who in the flesh flew over unto heaven, who elsewhere are called the children of the bridegroom.

VERS. 18.-- Three things are hard for me. So it is according to the letter, etc., as far as it is hard to know by how many thoughts that young man is driven who serves the way of truth.

VERS. 20.-- Such is also the way of an adulterous woman. The adulterous mind also, like those things which he has recounted, is always wandering, and where it shall be able to hide its sins, denies that it has wrought any evil.

VERS. 24.-- Four: namely the just seem 1113Dlesser in the earth than the reprobate, that is, more abject; but nevertheless they are wiser than the wise of the world, because they seek eternal things, and desire to suffer for them.

VERS. 25.-- The ants, a feeble people. And the people of the Church, etc., as far as whereby it may live for ever.

VERS. 26.-- The little hare, others choerogryllus, etc., as far as but here some have the hedgehog for the little hare.

(HIERON.) For the Hebrew books have the word saphan, which they interpret to be a mouse and a choerogyllum and a hare and a hedgehog.

The little hare, etc. This is the common people of the Church, which is feeble, because it seeks not to avenge its own injuries, and because to hope for salvation not by its own strength, but in the help of God, it hath 1114Alearned, which is to have a couch in the rock, whence it says with the Psalmist: Be thou unto me a most strong rock, a house of defence, that thou mayest save me, for thou art my rock and my fortress. [Vulg. Ps. 30:3–4 Esto mihi in Deum protectorem, et in domum refugii, ut salvum me facias… quoniam fortitudo mea et refugium meum es tu, "a God, a protector… a house of refuge"]

VERS. 27.-- The locust hath no king. The locust, etc., as far as nevertheless it is wont to serve Christ instantly in its own places, and to consume the buds of carnal pleasures.

VERS. 28.-- The stellio supporteth itself on hands. The stellio has not wings, etc., as far as the virtue of working lifts him unto the walls of the eternal kingdom that are to be obtained.

VERS. 29.-- There are three things which walk. Let it not move us, etc., as far as because in his own deceit Antichrist prospers for a time.

VERS. 31.-- A cock, namely the preachers, announcing the true morning amid the darkness of this world.

1114BAnd a ram. The first order of priests, etc., as far as after he was lifted up on high.

VERS. 33.-- But he that strongly presses the breasts, etc., as far as because that becomes carnal which is perceived by too much discussion.

And he that provoketh wrath, bringeth forth discords. He discloses what the former parable signifies according to the letter, etc., as far as draws forth blood.

CAPUT XXXI.

VERS. 1.-- Verba Lamuelis. Lamuel, in quo est Deus, etc., usque ad vidit quam prudenter se mater sua docuerit.

VERS. 3.-- Et divitias tuas. Hoc est, noli terrenis deliciis hominum corda corrumpere, etc., usque ad per illecebras saeculi merentur eradicari de terra 1114Cviventium.

VERS. 4.-- Noli regibus. Haec et sequentia ad litteram patent, etc., usque ad carnales scrutantur.

VERS. 6.-- Date siceram moerentibus. Vinum et sicera, etc., usque ad ne abundantiori tristitia absorbeantur qui hujusmodi sunt.

VERS. 8.-- Aperi os. Pro causa pauperis, etc., usque ad sed et cunctis per orbem nationibus curam salutis impendere.

VERS. 9.-- Aperi os tuum, decerne. Sicut pro causa pauperis et ereptione os aperire praecepit, ita, ubi erraverit, juste castigare admonet ac judicare. Et Moyses: Pauperis in judicio non misereberis (Exod. XIII). Quod utrumque Dominum facere Propheta dicit: Sed judicabit in justitia pauperes, et 1114Darguet inaequitate pro mansuetis terrae.

VERS. 10.-- Mulierem fortem. Hucusque verba Lamuelis regis, etc., usque ad virtutes describantur ac praemia.

Mulierem fortem quis inveniet. Mulier fortis sancta Ecclesia, etc., usque ad per mundum praedicare donavit, addidit:

VERS. 11.-- Confidit in ea cor viri. Vir, redemptor, etc., usque ad cum per praedicatores, quos deceperat, ad veritatem revocat.

Et spoliis non indigebit. Quia nunquam cessabit Ecclesia deceptas a diabolo animas Christi fidei restituere, donec mundi cursu finito numerus justorum compleatur. Sed quia quidquid boni sancta Ecclesia 1115Avel fidelis anima agit, desuper accipit recte subditur:

VERS. 12.-- Reddet ei bonum et non malum. Reddet anima Christo bonum, etc., usque ad cui simile, serviamus illi, etc.

VERS. 13.-- Quaesivit. Quibus operibus, etc., usque ad de hac retributione apertius subditur:

VERS. 14.-- Facta est quasi navis institoris de longe. Navigator dicitur institor, eo quod acquirendis mercibus sedulus instat, etc., usque ad de quibus dicitur: Amen dico vobis, receperunt mercedem suam.

VERS. 15.-- De nocte. Sicut totus praesentis saeculi cursus, etc., usque ad et hujusmodi secreto exercere.

Surrexit. Cum quis fidelis ad agendum proximorum 1115Bcuram sollicite accingitur. Quod duobus modis facit, quia et eos, qui foris errant, ad fidem vocat; et qui in fide sunt, ut amplius operibus insistant; unde subdit:

Deditque praedam domesticis suis. Dat praedam domesticis suis, cum eos, quos diabolus eripuit, eorum societati, qui in fide praecesserunt, conciliat. Dat ancillis suis cibaria, cum humiles quosque, et timore jussa servantes, ne lassescant pio labore, mercedis commemoratione reficit.

VERS. 16.-- Consideravit. Hodie et usque in finem saeculi mulier fortis, etc., usque ad quae sit beatitudinis plenitudo in regno Dei.

De fructu manuum suarum. Quia de fructiferis suorum fidelium factis, etc., usque ad et pro eis quid 1115Cpotest, laborat.

VERS. 17.-- Accinxit. Cum se ad virtutum opera praeparat, etc., usque ad ut bonis operibus fulgeatis.

VERS. 18.-- Gustavit et vidit quia bona est. Agri scilicet illius, etc., usque ad sic sed et nocte accensa lucerna manibus operatur.

VERS. 19.-- Manum suam misit. Fortia dicit, perfecta in charitate opera, etc., usque ad de cujus fortitudine dicitur: Regnum coelorum vim patitur, et violenti rapiunt illud (Matth. XI).

Et digiti ejus, inquit, apprehenderunt fusum. Colus 1115Din sinistra, fusus in dextera, etc., usque ad quid autem mulier fortis, scilicet sancta Ecclesia vel anima perfecta per fusum operata sit; subjungit:

VERS. 20.-- Manum suam aperuit inopi, et palmas suas. Hoc de eleemosynis, etc., usque ad cum late 1116Alongeque praedicatores ad gentes aeternae salutis egentes dispersit.

VERS. 21.-- Non timebit. Frigora nivis, etc., usque ad quia credidit Deo dicenti quod portae inferi non praevalebunt adversus eam (Matth. XVI).

Omnes enim domestici ejus vestiti sunt duplicibus, etc. Sapientia scilicet, ad haereses revelandas, et patientia ad tolerandas apertorum nostium pugnas, etc., usque ad qui merito in frigore religatur, quia vestem pietatis non habebat.

VERS. 22.-- Stragulatam vestem. Stragulata vestis est quae variante textura, solet confici firmissima, etc., usque ad byssinae enim justificationes sanctorum sunt.

VERS. 23.-- Nobilis in portis vir. Vir Dominus 1116Bqui visus est ignobilis, etc., usque ad cunctis apparebit sublimis.

VERS. 24.-- Sindonem fecit et vendidit. In linteo sindonis subtilis, etc., usque ad gentes enim a vitiis ad virtutes, a diabolo migraverunt ad Christum.

VERS. 25.-- Fortitudo. Fortitudo ad tolerandum, etc., usque ad esto fortis adversus detractores.

VERS. 26.-- Os suum. Ad hoc tantum loquens os aperuit, etc., usque ad utrique convenit quod subjungit:

Lex clementiae. Ut non statim puniat ut lex Moysi, etc., usque ad et sic absolvit eam: Vade et amplius noli peccare.

VERS. 27.-- Consideravit. Quia cunctas conscientiae suae cogitationes subtiliter investigat, etc., usque 1116Cad et quae didicit agenda, exercere non desinit.

Panem otiosa non comedit. Quia sacrum eloquium quod percipit, opere exsequitur, etc., usque ad non solum otiosae, sed verbosae et curiosae.

VERS. 28.-- Surrexerunt filii ejus. Quod futurum novit ut propheta, etc., usque ad sed in judicio quanta sit resurrectionis suae gloria demonstrabit.

VERS. 29.-- Multae filiae congregaverunt divitias: tu supergressa es universas. Ecclesiae haereticorum, etc., usque ad quae fide casta et opere perfecto Redemptoris sui vestigia sectatur.

VERS. 30.-- Fallax gratia et vana est pulchritudo. 1116DFallax est gloria laudis, etc., usque ad qualiter enim eam laudet, ostendit, Date ei de fructu.

VERS. 31.-- Date ei de fructu manuum suarum. Hoc vir ille, de quo supra dictum est, etc., usque ad et probante ac remunerante Deo, cui est honor et gloria.

CHAPTER XXXI.

VERS. 1.-- The words of Lamuel. Lamuel, in whom is God, etc., as far as he saw how prudently his mother had taught him.

VERS. 3.-- And thy riches. That is, be not willing to corrupt the hearts of men with earthly delights, etc., as far as through the enticements of the world they deserve to be rooted out of the land 1114Cof the living.

VERS. 4.-- Give not wine to kings. These things and the things that follow are plain according to the letter, etc., as far as carnal men search out.

VERS. 6.-- Give strong drink to them that mourn. Wine and strong drink, etc., as far as lest they who are of this sort be swallowed up by a more abundant sadness.

VERS. 8.-- Open thy mouth. For the cause of the poor man, etc., as far as but also to bestow the care of salvation upon all the nations throughout the world.

VERS. 9.-- Open thy mouth, judge. As for the cause of the poor man and for his deliverance he commanded the mouth to be opened, so, where he shall have erred, he admonishes to chastise and to judge justly. And Moses: Thou shalt not pity the poor man in judgment (Exod. XIII). Which both the Prophet says that the Lord does: But he shall judge the poor in justice, and 1114Dshall reprove with inequity for the meek of the earth. [Vulg. Isa. 11:4 sed judicabit in justitia pauperes, et arguet in aequitate pro mansuetis terrae, "in equity" — two words where Migne's line prints one]

VERS. 10.-- A strong woman. Thus far the words of king Lamuel, etc., as far as the virtues and the rewards be described.

Who shall find a strong woman. The strong woman is the holy Church, etc., as far as he granted to preach throughout the world, he added:

VERS. 11.-- The heart of her husband trusteth in her. The husband, the redeemer, etc., as far as when through the preachers he calls back to the truth those whom he had deceived.

And he shall have no need of spoils. Because the Church shall never cease to restore to the faith of Christ the souls deceived by the devil, until, the course of the world being finished, the number of the just be completed. But because whatsoever of good the holy Church 1115Aor the faithful soul does, it receives from above, it is rightly subjoined:

VERS. 12.-- She will render him good and not evil. The soul will render good to Christ, etc., as far as to which is like, let us serve him, etc.

VERS. 13.-- She hath sought. By what works, etc., as far as concerning this recompense it is more openly subjoined:

VERS. 14.-- She is become as a merchant's ship from afar. A sailor is called institor, because he presses on diligently to the acquiring of wares, etc., as far as of whom it is said: Amen I say to you, they have received their reward.

VERS. 15.-- By night. As the whole course of the present world, etc., as far as and to practise such things in secret.

She hath risen. When some faithful man girds himself solicitously to take 1115Bthe care of his neighbours. Which he does in two ways, because he both calls to the faith those who wander without; and those who are in the faith, that they may press on more in works; whence he subjoins:

And she hath given a prey to her household. He gives a prey to his household, when he reconciles those whom the devil snatched away to the fellowship of them that went before in the faith. He gives food to his handmaids, when he refreshes with the remembrance of the reward all the humble, and those keeping the commands through fear, lest they grow weary in godly labour.

VERS. 16.-- She hath considered. To-day and even to the end of the world the strong woman, etc., as far as what is the fulness of beatitude in the kingdom of God.

Of the fruit of her hands. Because from the fruit-bearing deeds of her own faithful ones, etc., as far as and she labours for them what 1115Cshe can.

VERS. 17.-- She hath girded. When she prepares herself for the works of the virtues, etc., as far as that you may shine with good works.

VERS. 18.-- She hath tasted and seen that it is good. Of that field, namely, etc., as far as so but also by night, the lamp being lighted, she works with her hands.

VERS. 19.-- She hath put out her hand. He means strong things, works perfected in charity, etc., as far as of whose strength it is said: The kingdom of heaven suffereth violence, and the violent bear it away (Matth. XI).

And her fingers, he says, have taken hold of the spindle. The distaff 1115Din the left hand, the spindle in the right, etc., as far as but what the strong woman, namely the holy Church or the perfect soul, has wrought by the spindle; he subjoins:

VERS. 20.-- She hath opened her hand to the needy, and her palms. This concerning alms, etc., as far as when far 1116Aand wide she scattered preachers unto the nations that lack eternal salvation.

VERS. 21.-- She shall not fear. The colds of snow, etc., as far as because she believed God saying that the gates of hell shall not prevail against her (Matth. XVI).

For all her household are clothed with double garments, etc. With wisdom, namely, for the unmasking of heresies, and with patience for the bearing of the battles of open enemies, etc., as far as who is deservedly bound in the cold, because he had not the garment of piety.

VERS. 22.-- An embroidered garment. An embroidered garment is that which, with varying texture, is wont to be wrought most firm, etc., as far as for the fine linens are the justifications of the saints.

VERS. 23.-- Her husband is noble in the gates. The husband is the Lord 1116Bwho was seen to be ignoble, etc., as far as he shall appear sublime unto all.

VERS. 24.-- She made fine linen and sold it. In the cloth of fine linen, etc., as far as for the nations passed over from vices to virtues, from the devil to Christ.

VERS. 25.-- Strength. Strength for enduring, etc., as far as be thou strong against backbiters.

VERS. 26.-- Her mouth. For this only, in speaking, she opened her mouth, etc., as far as to each belongs that which he subjoins:

The law of clemency. That she may not punish at once, as the law of Moses, etc., as far as and so he absolved her: Go, and sin no more.

VERS. 27.-- She hath considered. Because she subtly searches out all the thoughts of her own conscience, etc., as far 1116Cas and ceases not to practise the things which she has learned are to be done.

She hath not eaten her bread idle. Because she fulfils in work the sacred word which she receives, etc., as far as not only idle, but wordy and curious.

VERS. 28.-- Her children rose up. Which he knew as a prophet was to come, etc., as far as but in the judgment he will show how great is the glory of his own resurrection.

VERS. 29.-- Many daughters have gathered together riches: thou hast surpassed them all. The Churches of the heretics, etc., as far as which with chaste faith and perfect work follows the footsteps of her Redeemer.

VERS. 30.-- Favour is deceitful and beauty is vain. 1116DThe glory of praise is deceitful, etc., as far as for he shows in what manner he praises her, Give her of the fruit.

VERS. 31.-- Give her of the fruit of her hands. This that husband, of whom it was said above, etc., as far as and with God approving and rewarding, to whom is honour and glory.
