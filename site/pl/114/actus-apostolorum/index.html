Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

CAPUT PRIMUM.

VERS. 1.-- Sermonem feci. Id est, scripsi: quia scribere est operari. Vel hoc ita dicit, quia factis meruit esse scriptor Evangelii. Feci. Totus liber Evangelii unus sermo dicitur, eo quod habeat unum sensum: aut singulare pro plurali. Evangelium est bona annuntiatio, qua annuntiatur requies post laborem, regnum post servitium, vita post mortem. De omnibus. Dictis et factis Christi quae judicavit officio digna vel idonea suae dispensationi. Theophile. Theophilus idem est quod Dei amicus, vel Dei amator: et si tu sis amicus Dei, tibi scribitur. 425DQuae coepit Jesus facere. A baptismo Joannis quo Jesus baptizatus est, usque in diem quo assumptus est in coelum: quia de toto illo tempore Lucas sermones texuit, qui in fine Evangelii ita scripsit: Cum benedixisset, recessit ab eis et ferebatur in coelum. Facere. Bonum doctorem instituit vel instruit, qui faciat quod docet.

VERS. 2.-- Praecipiens apostolis praedicare Evangelium per Spiritum sanctum, per quem praedicatum est Evangelium, quia verba inspirabat, et fiduciam tribuebat. Vel, praecipiens per Spiritum sanctum, id est, quod Filius praecipit, praecipit et hoc Spiritus 426Csanctus. Vel, propter Spiritum praecepit, quia non praeciperet nisi Spiritus venturus esset. Elegit. Hoc ad laudem apostolorum, quorum actus scribere proponit, vertitur. Assumptus. A Divinitate, vel a nube assumptus, quasi verus homo.

VERS. 3.-- Vivum. Corpus vivum et proprium, non phantasticum quod et apostoli cognoverunt tanquam notissimum. Aut vivum, eo quod post mortem vivebat. Vel vivum, id est amplius non moriturum. Per dies. Non tamen eis per quadraginta dies continuos apparuit: post diem enim resurrectionis alios quatuor dies intervenisse Joannes dicit. Post quos rursus apparuit per quadraginta: quia quadraginta horis mortuus fuerat, quadraginta diebus se vivere confirmat. Et per quadraginta 426Dtypus praesentis saeculi, quo Christus in Ecclesia versatur, potest intelligi: sive quia homo quatuor constans elementis, eruditur contra transgressionem Decalogi.

VERS. 4.-- Convescens. Signa veri corporis annuntiat, quia non uno corporis sensu, sed visu, auditu, tactu, apparendo, loquendo, convescendo probavit se resurrexisse. Promissionem, quam promisit per Joel, ut habetur in sequentibus: et si a Christo facta, a Patre tamen complebitur. Vel Patris dicitur, quia in ejus Verbo, id est Christo, promittitur.

427AVERS. 5.-- Quia Joannes. (BEDA.) Cum diceret Dominus: Quia Joannes baptizavit aqua, vos autem, etc., usque ad quod in baptismo Joannis non fiebat.

Baptisma datur in duabus naturis, corporali scilicet et spirituali: quia homo qui baptizatur, duobus constat: corpore scilicet et anima, tamen unum est baptisma, sicut unus homo.

Vos autem, etc. Quando Petrus negavit, non erant apostoli baptizati; non aqua, sed spiritu: post resurrectionem enim dictum est: Vos vero baptizabimini Spiritu sancto. Nondum enim resurrectione Christi et missione Spiritus confirmati erant. Sicut autem justis circumcisio non fuit necessaria, antequam imperaretur Abrahae et posteritati suae: sic 427Bpostquam sacramentum Novi Testamenti, id est baptisma, circumcisionis loco datum est a Domino dicente: Nisi quis renatus fuerit ex aqua et Spiritu sancto (Joan. III), etc., certum est nullum ad corpus Christi pertinere, nisi baptizatum, praeter eos quibus passio deputatur pro baptismo. Item scriptum est, quando Paulus baptizatus est: et non est scriptum quando alii apostoli baptizati sunt: sed debemus intelligere baptizatos esse propter illud: Nisi quis renatus fuerit, etc.

VERS. 7.-- Non est vestrum nosse. Non ait, non erit, sed non est: notans adhuc esse infirmos, et ideo ad secretum cognoscendum non esse idoneos: Cum vero ait: Non est vestrum nosse, ostendit 427Cquod ipse sciat, cujus sunt omnia quae Patris, sed eis non expedit nosse, sed ita vivant quasi quotidie judicandi. Quae Pater. Illius, inquit, regni tam secretum est tempus, ut tantum scientiae Patris pateat, sed accipietis virtutem Spiritus sancti, ut quod propter infirmitatem carnis non potestis, possitis ex virtute Spiritus sancti. In sua potestate. Hoc ut verus homo excusabiliter dixit, sed tantum una est Patris et Filii et Spiritus sancti potestas: Filius etiam potestas Patris est.

VERS. 8.-- Sed accipietis. Tollitur scientia temporum, sed divinum, quo indigent, adhibetur auxilium: hoc modo, cum pueris aliquid tollitur, aliud datur ne penitus tristentur. Supervenientis Spiritus sancti. Cum superveniet Spiritus in vos, non regnum 427DIsrael, sive regnum Dei in Israel, ut putatis, afferet, sed virtutem testificandi de me vobis praestabit, tantumque illius regni tempus longe est, ut prius non solum Hierosolymam, sed omnes fines Judaeae et Samariae, mundi etiam terminos per circuitum fama Evangelii percurrat. In Jerusalem et in omni Judaea. Consequentia ordinis in vocabulis locorum conservata est: Jerusalem succedit proxima Judaeae provincia, cui Samaria situ conjungitur, dehinc omnis terrae communio subinfertur. In Jerusalem. Mystice: in visione pacis, in confessione verae fidei: custodia praeceptorum Dei: et in his qui se in alienos fecerint a terra praedicaturos eos praedixit, per quos quasi rotae radios de medio terrae ubi salutem 428Aoperatus est, gratia spiritualis et salutaris doctrina per totum orbem defluxit.

VERS. 9.-- Et cum haec dixisset. Aliter Marcus commemorat dicens: Et Dominus quidem Jesus postquam locutus est eis, assumptus est in coelum (Matth. XXVIII). Sed quia Lucas significantius adjecit: Cum haec dixisset, elevatus est, completis illis sermonibus quos commemoraverat Dominum ascendisse coelos ostendit.

VERS. 11.-- Galilaei, quid statis aspicientes in coelum? In quibus debet esse prudentior sensus, quia Galilaei caeteris Judaeis in sapientia praecellebant. Vel, Galilaei volubiles, quia instabilitate sui sensus obliti erunt praecepta Dei.

VERS. 12.-- Tunc reversi sunt, etc. Reversio 428Bapostolorum de monte Oliveti Jerusalem, significat Ecclesiam quae de activa in contemplativam ascendens, iterum descendit in conversationem activalem. Mons enim Oliveti ab antiquis mons trium luminum vocabatur: lucernae scilicet de templo resplendentis, ac solis ex altera parte orientis, et olei in ipso monte naturalis lucis: quae tria conveniunt perfectioni propter divini Verbi lucem de Ecclesia fulgentem, propter solis justitiae fulgorem, propter purae conscientiae nitorem.

VERS. 13.-- Et cum introissent. In Graeco ita ponitur: Cum introissent civitatem, in coenaculum ascenderunt, ubi erant manentes Petrus, etc. In Graeco sic est ordo nominum: Petrus et Andraeas, Jacobus et Joannes, Simon Zelotes et Judas Jacobi. Coenaculum, 428Cetc. (BEDA.) Locus est in superiori, designans quod a terra sublati ad superiora scientiae et virtutis conscendunt.

Coenaculum dicitur tertium tectum, figura charitatis fidei superaedificatae et spei. Charitas autem diffusa est in cordibus nostris per Spiritum. Petrus et Joannes. Petrus primus ponitur, quia princeps et pastor. Petrus et Joannes merito primi ponuntur, qui simul cucurrerunt ad monumentum, quia plus caeteris amabant. Primus (si fas est dici) plus caeteris dilexit: alium, plurimum Jesus amavit. Catalogus apostolorum ideo ponitur, ne extra hos aliqui falsi recipiantur: paria et ponuntur loca singulorum, distinguentibus gradibus meritorum.

Hi sunt duodecim filii Jacob; duodecim fontes 428Ddeserti, quos Israel reperit in Elim; duodecim panes propositionis; duodecim lapides in veste pontificis; duodecim signa zodiaci; duodecim lapides altaris; duodecim lapides de Jordane elevati; duodecim boves sub aeneo mari; duodecim stellae in corona sponsae; duodecim fundamenta; duodecim portae; duodecim menses anni; duodecim horae diei; duodecim fructus ligni vitae.

Zelotes. (BEDA.) Zelotes ipse est qui in Evangeliis dicitur Chananaeus. Chana zelus interpretatur. Erat enim de vico Galilaeae ubi Dominus aquam convertit in vinum. Hic post Jacobum fratrem suum, rexit Ecclesiam Hierosolymorum.

Filio pereunte perditionis, imperfectus numerus 429Aapostolorum remansit, juxta similitudinem enim lunae crescit et decrescit Ecclesia: sed vetera transeunt, et nova fiunt.

VERS. 14.-- Maria, etc. Virgo a mulieribus distinguitur, quae tamen aliquando mulier non pro corruptione, sed pro sexu appellatur. Ecce principium nascentis Ecclesiae adornatur flore virginitatis. In medio fratrum. Id est filiorum, quorum unus est Pater in coelis.

In medio Petrus stetisse dicitur: quasi communi miseratione condescendens. Hoc exemplum sumendum est pastoribus Ecclesiae.

Hic numerus centum et viginti congruit aetati Moysi, ut sicut sub hoc numero, Moyse defuncto, Jesus successit, qui populum in terram promissionis 429Binduxit, sic veteri legi in Ecclesia principatus Christi successit, ducens verum Israel ad regnum coeli.

VERS. 18.-- Possedit agrum. Non quia agrum possederit, sed quia ipsius fuerat pretium agri qui Dominum vitae vendidit. Amissa terra viventium, agrum sanguinis et mortis, aeterna sceleris et nominis sui memoria possidet Judas. Emptum tamen pretio sanguinis agrum figuli non possedit, qui traditionis crimen in se protinus mulctavit, sed possedit dictum est pro possideri fecit. Quidam agrum a Juda possessum ridicule exponunt infernum. Alii possedisse agrum illum existimant, ex eo quod cum peregrinis in eo sepultus fuit. Viscera. Viscera quae sunt sedes fraudis, tanto scelere dirupta, se cohibere non 429Cvaluerunt. Merito autem per sedem doli viscera funduntur, non per locum osculi, id est os quo osculatus est Jesum, quamvis falsa superficie, sed per alium cui virus occultae malitiae inerat.

VERS. 19.-- Eorum, id est habitantium Jerusalem, quia etsi utrique loquebantur Hebraice, distabat tamen proprietas linguae Hierosolymorum a Galilaeis, de quibus apostoli erant: quod in passione Domini ostenditur, ubi etiam Petrus nolens lingua sua proditus est esse Galilaeus.

VERS. 20.-- Deserta, et non sit, etc. Donec electus pro eo assumatur Matthias. Vel, non sit ipse Judas, qui habitet in ea, sed alius assumatur pro eo qui poenitentia indignus judicatus est.

VERS. 23.-- Duos. Clemens refert hos duos qui 429Dad sortem apostolatus statuti sunt, de numero septuaginta discipulorum fuisse. Qui cognominatus. In laude Barnabae immoratur: quia pro laude Matthaeus habet, quod sorte eligitur. Barnabae laus cumulatur, ne velut indignus repelli videretur: quod postea declaratum est; quia cum Paulo gentium apostolus ordinatur, illi officio reservatus. Justus. Aut Latinum est, aut Hebraeum: et interpretatur parcens, vel elevatus. Et Matthiam, qui donatus, vel donum Dei interpretatur: cui donum apostolatus a Domino collatum est, vel Dei pannus interpretatur. Merito parvi transcenditur ille laude hominum qui justus erat, inquit Arator.

CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- I made a discourse. That is, I wrote: because to write is to work. Or he says it thus, because by his deeds he deserved to be the writer of the Gospel. I made. The whole book of the Gospel is called one discourse, in that it has one sense: or the singular stands for the plural. The Gospel is a good announcement, by which is announced rest after labour, a kingdom after servitude, life after death. Of all things. Of the sayings and deeds of Christ which he judged worthy of his office or fit for his dispensation. O Theophilus. Theophilus is the same as friend of God, or lover of God: and if thou art a friend of God, it is written for thee. 425DWhich Jesus began to do. From the baptism of John by which Jesus was baptized, until the day on which he was taken up into heaven: because Luke wove the discourses concerning that whole time, who at the end of the Gospel wrote thus: When he had blessed, he departed from them and was carried into heaven. To do. [Vulg. Luc. 24:51 dum benediceret illis, "while he was blessing them"] He appoints or instructs a good teacher, one who does what he teaches.

VERS. 2.-- Giving commandment to the apostles to preach the Gospel through the Holy Spirit, through whom the Gospel was preached, because he inspired the words and bestowed confidence. Or, giving commandment through the Holy Spirit, that is, what the Son commands, this the Holy 426CSpirit also commands. Or, he gave the commandment on account of the Spirit, because he would not command unless the Spirit were about to come. He chose. This turns to the praise of the apostles, whose acts he purposes to write. Taken up. Taken up by the Divinity, or by a cloud, as true man.

VERS. 3.-- Alive. A living and proper body, not a phantasmal one, which the apostles also recognized as most well known. Or alive, in that after death he was living. Or alive, that is, no more to die. Through days. Yet he did not appear to them through forty continuous days: for John says that after the day of the resurrection four other days intervened. After which he appeared again through forty: because he had been dead for forty hours, for forty days he confirms that he lives. And by the forty 426Da type of the present age may be understood, in which Christ is engaged in the Church: or because man, consisting of four elements, is instructed against the transgression of the Decalogue.

VERS. 4.-- Eating together with them. He announces the signs of a true body, because not by one sense of the body, but by sight, by hearing, by touch, by appearing, by speaking, by eating together, he proved that he had risen. The promise, which he promised through Joel, as is had in what follows: and if it was made by Christ, yet it will be fulfilled by the Father. Or it is called of the Father, because it is promised in his Word, that is, in Christ.

427AVERS. 5.-- For John. (BEDA.) When the Lord said: For John baptized with water, but you, etc., as far as that which was not done in the baptism of John.

Baptism is given in two natures, namely the bodily and the spiritual: because the man who is baptized consists of two: namely of body and of soul, yet baptism is one, as the man is one.

But you, etc. When Peter denied, the apostles were not baptized; not with water, but with the spirit: for after the resurrection it was said: But you shall be baptized with the Holy Spirit. For they had not yet been confirmed by the resurrection of Christ and by the mission of the Spirit. But as circumcision was not necessary to the just before it was commanded to Abraham and to his posterity: so 427Bafter the sacrament of the New Testament, that is baptism, was given in the place of circumcision by the Lord saying: Unless a man be born again of water and the Holy Spirit (Joan. III), etc., it is certain that none belongs to the body of Christ save the baptized, apart from those to whom the passion is reckoned in place of baptism. Likewise it is written when Paul was baptized: and it is not written when the other apostles were baptized: but we ought to understand that they were baptized on account of that: Unless a man be born again, etc.

VERS. 7.-- It is not for you to know. He does not say, it will not be, but it is not: noting that they are still weak, and therefore not fit for the knowing of the secret: but when he says: It is not for you to know, he shows 427Cthat he himself knows, whose are all things that are the Father's, but that it is not expedient for them to know, but that they should so live as though they were to be judged daily. Which the Father. The time of that kingdom, he says, is so secret that it lies open only to the knowledge of the Father, but you shall receive the power of the Holy Spirit, that what through the weakness of the flesh you cannot do, you may be able to do out of the power of the Holy Spirit. In his own power. This he said excusably as true man, but there is only one power of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit: the Son also is the power of the Father.

VERS. 8.-- But you shall receive. The knowledge of the times is taken away, but the divine help of which they stand in need is applied: in this manner, when something is taken from children, another thing is given lest they be utterly saddened. Of the Holy Spirit coming upon you. When the Spirit shall come upon you, he will not bring the kingdom 427Dof Israel, or the kingdom of God in Israel, as you think, but he will bestow upon you the power of bearing witness of me, and the time of that kingdom is so far off, that first the fame of the Gospel shall run through not only Jerusalem, but all the borders of Judaea and of Samaria, and even the bounds of the world round about. In Jerusalem and in all Judaea. The consequence of order is preserved in the names of the places: to Jerusalem succeeds the nearest province of Judaea, to which Samaria is joined by situation, and thereafter the fellowship of the whole earth is brought in. In Jerusalem. Mystically: in the vision of peace, in the confession of the true faith: in the keeping of the commandments of God: and he foretold that they would preach among those who shall have made themselves strangers from the earth, through whom, as through the spokes of a wheel, from the middle of the earth where he wrought salvation, 428Aspiritual grace and saving doctrine flowed out through the whole world.

VERS. 9.-- And when he had said these things. Mark records it otherwise, saying: And the Lord Jesus indeed, after he had spoken to them, was taken up into heaven (Matth. XXVIII). But because Luke added more significantly: When he had said these things, he was lifted up, he shows that, those discourses being finished which he had recorded, the Lord ascended the heavens.

VERS. 11.-- Galilaeans, why stand you looking up to heaven? In whom there ought to be a more prudent understanding, because the Galilaeans excelled the rest of the Jews in wisdom. Or, Galilaeans, the changeable, because by the instability of their sense they will have forgotten the commandments of God.

VERS. 12.-- Then they returned, etc. The return 428Bof the apostles from Mount Olivet to Jerusalem signifies the Church, which, ascending from the active into the contemplative, descends again into the active manner of life. For Mount Olivet was called by the ancients the mount of three lights: namely of the lamp shining out from the temple, and of the sun rising on the other side, and of the oil, a natural light on the mount itself: which three things befit perfection, on account of the light of the divine Word shining out from the Church, on account of the brightness of the sun of justice, on account of the lustre of a pure conscience.

VERS. 13.-- And when they were come in. In the Greek it is put thus: When they had entered the city, they went up into the upper room, where were abiding Peter, etc. In the Greek the order of the names is thus: Peter and Andrew, James and John, Simon Zelotes and Judas of James. The upper room, 428Cetc. (BEDA.) It is a place in the upper part, signifying that they who are lifted from the earth ascend to the higher things of knowledge and of virtue.

The upper room is called the third roof, a figure of charity built up upon faith and hope. But charity is poured forth in our hearts through the Spirit. Peter and John. Peter is put first, because he is prince and shepherd. Peter and John are deservedly put first, who ran together to the tomb, because they loved more than the rest. The first (if it is right to say it) loved more than the rest: the other, Jesus loved most. The catalogue of the apostles is therefore set down, lest outside these any false ones be received: and equal places of each are set down, the degrees of merits distinguishing them.

These are the twelve sons of Jacob; the twelve fountains 428Dof the desert, which Israel found in Elim; the twelve loaves of proposition; the twelve stones on the vestment of the high priest; the twelve signs of the zodiac; the twelve stones of the altar; the twelve stones lifted out of the Jordan; the twelve oxen under the brazen sea; the twelve stars in the crown of the spouse; the twelve foundations; the twelve gates; the twelve months of the year; the twelve hours of the day; the twelve fruits of the tree of life.

Zelotes. (BEDA.) Zelotes is the same who in the Gospels is called Chananaeus. Chana is interpreted zeal. For he was of a village of Galilee where the Lord turned water into wine. He, after James his brother, ruled the Church of Jerusalem.

The son of perdition perishing, the number 429Aof the apostles remained imperfect, for according to the likeness of the moon the Church waxes and wanes: but old things pass away, and new things are made.

VERS. 14.-- Mary, etc. The Virgin is distinguished from the women, who nevertheless is sometimes called woman not for corruption, but for sex. Behold, the beginning of the nascent Church is adorned with the flower of virginity. In the midst of the brethren. That is, of the sons, whose Father in heaven is one.

Peter is said to have stood in the midst: as it were condescending with a common compassion. This example is to be taken by the shepherds of the Church.

This number of a hundred and twenty agrees with the age of Moses, that as under this number, Moses being dead, Jesus succeeded, who led the people into the land of promise, 429Bso to the old law the princedom of Christ succeeded in the Church, leading the true Israel to the kingdom of heaven.

VERS. 18.-- He possessed a field. Not because he possessed a field, but because his was the price of the field, who sold the Lord of life. The land of the living being lost, Judas possesses the field of blood and of death by the eternal memory of his crime and of his name. Yet he did not possess the potter's field bought with the price of blood, who forthwith punished in himself the crime of the betrayal, but he possessed is said for he caused it to be possessed. Some ridiculously expound the field possessed by Judas to be hell. Others think that he possessed that field, from this, that he was buried in it with the strangers. The bowels. The bowels, which are the seat of fraud, burst asunder by so great a crime, were not 429Cable to contain themselves. But deservedly are the bowels poured out through the seat of guile, not through the place of the kiss, that is, the mouth with which he kissed Jesus, though with a false surface, but through the other in which was the venom of hidden malice.

VERS. 19.-- Their, that is, of them that dwell in Jerusalem, because although both spoke Hebrew, yet the property of the tongue of the men of Jerusalem differed from the Galilaeans, of whom the apostles were: which is shown in the passion of the Lord, where Peter also, unwilling, was betrayed by his own tongue to be a Galilaean.

VERS. 20.-- Desolate, and let there be none, etc. Until Matthias, chosen in his stead, be taken up. Or, let it not be Judas himself who dwells in it, but let another be taken up in his stead, who was judged unworthy of repentance.

VERS. 23.-- Two. Clement relates that these two who 429Dwere set up for the lot of the apostolate were of the number of the seventy disciples. Who was surnamed. He lingers in the praise of Barnabas: because Matthew has it for praise, that he is chosen by lot. The praise of Barnabas is heaped up, lest he should seem to be rejected as unworthy: which was afterwards declared; because with Paul he is ordained apostle of the Gentiles, having been reserved for that office. Justus. It is either Latin or Hebrew: and it is interpreted sparing, or lifted up. And Matthias, who is interpreted given, or the gift of God: to whom the gift of the apostolate was conferred by the Lord, or he is interpreted the cloth of God. Deservedly is he passed over at small worth in the praise of men, who was just, says Arator.

CAPUT II.

VERS. 1.-- In eodem loco. Coenaculo scilicet, in quod ascenderant. Qui Spiritum desiderat, carnis domicilium mentis contemplatione transcendens calcat.

VERS. 2.-- De coelo. Datur Spiritus de coelo, ut diligatur Deus; in terra datur, ut diligatur proximus: bis enim post resurrectionem est datus ad duo praecepta charitatis commendanda. Sonus. Potest dici locutio linguarum de ore apostolorum sonans. Spiritus. Ante passionem datus est apostolis Spiritus ad gratiam doctrinae et sanitatum; post resurrectionem insufflavit Jesus spiritum, et ait: Accipite Spiritum sanctum; quorum remiseritis peccata, remittuntur eis (Joan. XX), etc. Quod donum soli Petro 430Bprius dederat: sed ut creditur tunc per anticipationem, nunc cum caeteris per completionem etiam Petro tribuitur: et potestas data est dimittendi illis quos ad officium mittebat praedicationis.

Vehementis. Comparatione praecedentium vehemens dicitur gratia apostolorum, vel in similitudinem venti vehementis Spiritus venit. Totam domum. In una domo sedentibus infunditur Spiritus, ut Ecclesiae unitas commendetur, in coenaculo tertia lege incipiente. In datione legis plebs longe stabat prae timore; hic veniente Spiritu congregati erant in unum ex amore. Sedentes. Ut compleatur quod dictum erat eis: Sedete hic in civitate. Sessio est indicium humilitatis et stabilitatis: quibus praeparatur sedes Spiritui sancto, qui super humiles requiescit.

430C(GREG.) In igne apparuit Spiritus, sed per seipsum, etc., usque ad et voce sive sonitu docta sunt corda.

VERS. 3.-- Dispertitae. Dispersio linguarum est facta in turre, sed quod dispersit superbia, recolligit humilitas: in superbia dispersio, in humilitate fit concordia. Ecce completur sententia Domini quae ait: Non vos relinquam orphanos, sed mittam Spiritum paracletum (Joan. XIV), qui eorum advocatus, pro eis omnibus linguis locutus est. Linguas attulit, qui pro Verbo venit, cognationem enim habet lingua cum verbo, ut ab invicem non possint separari. Sic Verbum Patris, id est Filius, et Spiritus sanctus inseparabiles sunt, imo et unius naturae. Tanquam ignis. Idem in igne Spiritus venit: quia peccatores 430DSpiritus purgat, illuminat, accendit, urit. Ignis enim quatuor habet naturas, urit, purgat, calefacit, illuminat. Similiter Spiritus exurit peccata, purgat corda, torporem excutit, ignorantias illustrat. Ignis incorporeus et invisibilis est in sua natura; sed assumpto aliquo corpore videtur, diversi coloris apparens, propter materias in quibus ardet. Sic Spiritus videri non poterit, nisi per creaturas in quibus operatur.

VERS. 4.-- Et coeperunt. Ecce signum plenitudinis: plenum vas erumpit. Ignis in sinu non potest occultari. Variis linguis. Quia linguis omnibus loquebantur, vel sua, id est Hebraea lingua, loquentes, ab omnibus ita intelligebantur, ac si propriis singulorum 431Aloquerentur, secundum illud Prophetae: In aliis linguis, et labiis aliis, loquar populo huic, etc.

VERS. 5.-- Ex omni natione, etc. (RAB.) Ac si diceretur: Ubicunque nati. Judaeos autem in omnibus gentibus nasci captivitas fecit, quae sub Antiocho facta est non multis temporibus ante.

(RAB.) Super omnem carnem masculi et feminae, vel circumcisi et ethnici, secundum illud: Non est masculus et femina, circumcisio et praeputium, Barbarus et Scytha, servus et liber (Gal. III).

VERS. 19.-- Et dabo. Mystice prodigia in coelo, labores sunt in Ecclesia. Signa, super terram. Vindictae, in terrenos. Sanguis, in martyrio. Ignis, Evangelii vel Spiritus sancti. Vapor fumi, animae vel cordis compunctio. Prodigia in coelo. Etenim Christo nascente, novum sidus apparuit; eo ascendente crucem, sol obscuratus est et coelum tenebris obductum.

Vaporem fumi. Compunctionis et fletuum, scilicet vaporem. Quia sicut fumus ab igne, ita compunctio a Spiritu sancti ardore.

Sol convertetur, etc. Hoc in passione Christi partim factum partim ante judicii diem creditur venturum. Tunc enim sol obscuratus est, sed luna in sanguinem versa hominibus apparere non potuit: quia tunc (utpote in Pascha) quintadecima exsistens, interdiu fuerat visibus humanis objectu terrae occultata.

432BVERS. 23.-- Definito consilio. (RAB.) In hoc solo Judaei fuere notabiles, quia non obedienti animo fecerunt.

Et praescientia Dei, etc. (ID.) In Graeco sic est: Praescientia sic traditum accipientes. Traditus est enim a praeside in potestatem illorum, ut ipsum sive latronem eligerent, sed latroni concessa vita, Jesum per manus militum occiderunt.

VERS. 24.-- Suscitavit. Non usitata cum caeteris, atque communi resurrectione quae in finem differtur, sed quae die tertia celebraretur, ut assertio hujus resurrectionis testimonium esset Deitatis.

(RAB.) Suscitavit, secundum humanitatem, quam maxime oderant Judaei.

Solutis doloribus. Videtur esse sensus hujus sententiae, 432Cquod Domino ad inferos descendente dolores poenarum soluti sint, quia eum tangere non potuerunt, sed juxta Graecum in quo scriptum est: Solvens per ipsum mortis dolores, juxta quod impossibile erat teneri illum ab eo, patet, quia per descensum ejus liberati sunt sancti a locis infernorum; qui licet in sinu Abrahae, id est in consolatione quietis essent, a dolore tamen mortis sive inferi non erant soluti ex toto, quia non meruerant gaudia coeli intrare, donec fieret illud Prophetae: In sanguine testamenti eduxisti vinctos tuos de lacu, in quo non est aqua (Zach. V). Vincti enim erant antequam educerentur.

VERS. 25.-- Providebam Dominum. Vox Christi post resurrectionem exsultantis ad dexteram. Sinistra, est praesens vita; dextera, aeterna. Ne commovear. 432DPost resurrectionem omnia erunt immobilia. Et exsultavit lingua. Quia requies post laborem data est: laboravit enim in spe vitae aeternae. Non derelinques. Non iterum movear corruptione iteratae crucis et mortis. Notas mihi fecisti. Scio me semper esse victurum. Replebis me plena laetitia, id est, iterum non moriar. Quoniam a dextris. Causa quare non moveatur, est quia Dominus a dextris opitulatur, sinistra non praevalente. Si enim a dextris non fuerit, illam partem diabolus occupat, sicut de Juda dicitur: Et diabolus stet a dextris ejus (Psal. CVIII).

VERS. 26.-- Et exsultavit. In passione dixit: Tristis est anima mea usque ad mortem (Matth. XXVI): et coepit pavere et taedere: ut se verum hominem 433Ain anima et corpore, et veraciter passibilem monstraret. Exsultavit vero postea anima et lingua, quia passione sua genus humanum salvandum erat.

VERS. 27.-- In inferno. Constat animam non derelictam in inferno, quae, ablatis illis pro quibus descenderat, mox ad superna rediit, nec caro est corrupta, sed veloci resurrectione glorificata.

VERS. 28.-- Notas mihi fecisti. Non solum haec de illo intelliguntur, qui semel accepta plenitudine divinae virtutis et sapientiae, per se mortem destruxit, resurgens ad vitam; sed et de justis qui per eum via veritatis inventa ad vitam redibunt et jucundabuntur videntes faciem ejus: quia ille visus est super omnia et beatitudo perfecta.

VERS. 33.-- Promissione Spiritus sancti accepta. 433BChristus eam accepit sicut verus homo, et tanquam Deus effundit Spiritum, sicut Joel dicit: Effundam, etc. Item accipiunt apostoli ab ipso promissionem Spiritus qui dicitur a Patre mittendus in Evangelio. Effudit hunc, etc. Qui prius Jesum suscitatum a Deo dixit, nunc fidem auditorum altius erigens, hunc esse Dominum assignat, quem effudisse Spiritum confirmat: hoc enim est signum. Et pulchre verbo effusionis usus est: quod prophetam Joel posuisse supra memoravit, ut ex hoc etiam constet ipsum esse Dominum Jesum, qui ante assumptionem carnis loqui consuevit in prophetis, qui signa et prodigia dat in coelo et in terra, qui omnes invocantes se salvat. Et caetera quae propheta describit, quasi verus Deus, explet.

433CVERS. 34.-- Sede a dextris. (RAB.) Manifeste Petrus ex hoc psalmo regnum Christi non terrenum, sed coeleste, etc., usque ad in altera, ejusdem est Dominus.

VERS. 36.-- Dominum eum. Ponens nomen majestatis et potestatis, ostendit eum esse cui omnis creatura debet subjici. Christus regiae sive pontificalis dignitatis nomen est. In lege ungebantur reges et pontifices, in figura illius. Et Christum. Ille vere est Christus omni plenitudine Spiritus sancti unctus, qui de eodem Spiritu potentialiter dedit quibus voluit. Jesum quem, etc. Jesus est Dominus et Christus, una in duabus naturis exsistente persona, quia in Divinitate aeternaliter ex Patre natus in humanitate temporaliter factus, cum operante Spiritu 433Dsancto in utero virginis est incarnatus.

VERS. 37.-- Compuncti. Impletur prophetia Joelis, quia post ignem Spiritus sancti sequitur vapor compunctionis. Fumus lacrymas excutere solet, incipiunt flere qui irriserant, tundunt pectus, dant Deo precem sicut sacrificium, ut sanguinem illum gustare valeant, per eum salvandi, quem super se et super filios suos prius fuerant imprecati.

VERS. 38.-- In nomine. In honore, vel in hac forma, quia in uno trium fides continetur.

VERS. 39.-- Et omnibus. Hoc ad illud testimonium prophetiae respicitur, quod ultimum posuit, scilicet Omnis quicunque invocaverit nomen Domini salvus erit: quod ad Gentium vocationem pertinet specialiter; 434Aquae a filiis Israel longe factae, per invocationem nominis Domini salvandae erant.

VERS. 40.-- Testificatus est, etc., id est testificatio et exhortatio. Salvamini. Haec est testificationis qualitas et exhortationis, sed Lucas plurima verba Petri brevi conclusit sententia. A generatione. Duae sunt generationes, una bonorum, alia malorum.

VERS. 1.-- In the same place. Namely in the upper room, into which they had gone up. He who desires the Spirit treads upon the dwelling-place of the flesh, transcending it by the contemplation of the mind.

VERS. 2.-- From heaven. The Spirit is given from heaven, that God may be loved; on earth he is given, that the neighbour may be loved: for twice after the resurrection was he given, for the commending of the two precepts of charity. A sound. It may be called the speaking of tongues sounding from the mouth of the apostles. Spirit. Before the passion the Spirit was given to the apostles for the grace of teaching and of healings; after the resurrection Jesus breathed the spirit, and said: Receive the Holy Spirit; whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them (Joan. XX), etc. Which gift he had given to Peter alone 430Bbeforehand: but, as is believed, then by anticipation, now with the rest by completion it is bestowed on Peter also: and the power of forgiving was given to those whom he was sending to the office of preaching.

Mighty. By comparison with what went before, the grace of the apostles is called mighty, or the Spirit came in the likeness of a mighty wind. The whole house. The Spirit is poured into them sitting in one house, that the unity of the Church may be commended, the third law beginning in the upper room. At the giving of the law the people stood far off for fear; here, at the coming of the Spirit, they had been gathered into one out of love. Sitting. That there might be fulfilled what had been said to them: Sit you here in the city. Sitting is a token of humility and of stability: by which a seat is prepared for the Holy Spirit, who rests upon the humble.

430C(GREG.) The Spirit appeared in fire, but through himself, etc., as far as and hearts were taught by the voice or by the sound.

VERS. 3.-- Parted. The dispersion of tongues was made at the tower, but what pride dispersed, humility gathers together again: in pride is dispersion, in humility concord is made. Behold, the sentence of the Lord is fulfilled which says: I will not leave you orphans, but I will send the Paraclete Spirit (Joan. XIV), who, being their advocate, spoke for them in all tongues. [Vulg. Jo. 14:18 Non relinquam vos orphanos: veniam ad vos, "I will come to you"] He brought tongues, who came for the Word, for the tongue has kinship with the word, so that they cannot be separated from one another. So the Word of the Father, that is the Son, and the Holy Spirit are inseparable, nay rather of one nature. As of fire. The same Spirit came in fire: because sinners 430Dthe Spirit purges, enlightens, kindles, burns. For fire has four natures, it burns, purges, warms, enlightens. In like manner the Spirit burns up sins, purges hearts, shakes off torpor, illumines ignorances. Fire is incorporeal and invisible in its own nature; but when some body has been assumed it is seen, appearing of diverse colour, on account of the materials in which it burns. So the Spirit will not be able to be seen, save through the creatures in which he works.

VERS. 4.-- And they began. Behold the sign of fulness: a full vessel bursts out. Fire cannot be hidden in the bosom. With divers tongues. Because they spoke with all tongues, or, speaking in their own, that is the Hebrew tongue, they were so understood by all as if they spoke in the proper tongues of each, 431Aaccording to that of the Prophet: In other tongues and with other lips will I speak to this people, etc.

VERS. 5.-- Out of every nation, etc. (RAB.) As if it were said: Wheresoever born. But the captivity, which was made under Antiochus not many times before, caused the Jews to be born among all nations.

VERS. 6.-- Confounded. That confusion is set over against the confusion of tongues, from which Babel obtained its name. So the humility of the apostles answers to human pride. Every man. All the tongues are said to be seventy-two: whence also the disciples of the second order were chosen under this number, that the number of the preachers might accord with the number of the tongues.

VERS. 8.-- We have heard. In this it was signified that one Church would speak in the tongues of all nations.

431B(BEDA.) It is asked whether those who were speaking, etc., as far as it seemed rather to be a miracle of the hearers than of the speakers.

VERS. 9.-- Cappadocia, etc. These five provinces which are set after Judaea do indeed speak Greek: but from the custom of their country there is a diversity in sound. Whence wonderful was the grace in the apostles, which not only taught them the diversity, but also made in their speech the distance of properties in each of the tongues according to the number of the provinces.

VERS. 10.-- Strangers of Rome. In the Greek, sojourning Romans, that is, Jews who led a pilgrim life at Rome.

VERS. 14.-- Jewish men. That is, there are two causes of prudence, namely that they were Jews, and that they dwelt in 431CJerusalem, which sobriety becomes.

VERS. 17.-- I will pour out. By this word he shows the largeness of the gift, because the Spirit was to be given not, as of old, to prophets and priests only, but to all persons everywhere in either sex. Of my Spirit. He looks to the diverse gifts while he says this, but not to a diminution of the substance. Upon all flesh. What all flesh is he consequently expounds there: And the sons shall prophesy, etc.

(RAB.) Upon all flesh, of male and female, or of the circumcised and the heathen, according to that: There is not male and female, circumcision and uncircumcision, Barbarian and Scythian, bond and free (Gal. III). [Vulg. Gal. 3:28 reads Non est Judaeus, neque Graecus: non est servus, neque liber: non est masculus, neque femina; the clauses on circumcision and on the Barbarian and the Scythian belong to Col. 3:11, not to Galatians]

And they shall prophesy. The law and the prophets until John: from him the kingdom is preached. But it is one thing to prophesy of 431DChrist, another to foretell and to teach things to come.

VERS. 19.-- And I will give. Mystically, wonders in heaven are labours in the Church. Signs, upon the earth. Vengeances, upon the earthly. Blood, in martyrdom. Fire, of the Gospel or of the Holy Spirit. Vapour of smoke, the compunction of the soul or of the heart. Wonders in heaven. For indeed at the birth of Christ a new star appeared; at his ascending the cross, the sun was darkened and heaven was overspread with darkness.

Blood. Not only of the wound, but also of the sweat, when in prayer his sweat became as drops of blood, which is set among the signs of the Deity, because it is not found in human nature. Therefore it signifies 432Athat the whole world is to be washed by the blood of Christ, that as the tabernacle had been consecrated by the blood of victims, so the faithful people might be dedicated by the blood of the same: and that not only at Jerusalem should there be a place of prayer, but that in every place of his dominion the elect should lift up their hands by prayers to the Lord. Vapour of smoke. A vapour, namely, of compunction and of weepings. Because as smoke from fire, so compunction from the ardour of the Holy Spirit.

The sun shall be turned, etc. This is believed to have been done partly in the passion of Christ, partly to be about to come before the day of judgment. For then the sun was darkened, but the moon turned into blood could not appear to men: because then (as being in the Pasch) standing as the fifteenth, it had by day been hidden from human sight by the interposing of the earth.

432BVERS. 23.-- By the determinate counsel. (RAB.) In this alone were the Jews blameworthy, that they did it not with an obedient mind.

And foreknowledge of God, etc. (ID.) In the Greek it is thus: Receiving him thus delivered up by foreknowledge. For he was delivered by the governor into their power, that they might choose either him or the robber, but life being granted to the robber, they slew Jesus by the hands of the soldiers.

VERS. 24.-- He hath raised up. Not by the resurrection usual with the rest and common, which is deferred to the end, but by that which was celebrated on the third day, that the assertion of this resurrection might be a testimony of the Deity.

(RAB.) He raised up, according to the humanity, which the Jews most greatly hated.

The sorrows being loosed. The sense of this sentence seems to be, 432Cthat when the Lord descended to the lower regions the sorrows of the punishments were loosed, because they could not touch him, but according to the Greek, in which it is written: Loosing through him the sorrows of death, according as it was impossible that he should be held by it, it is plain that through his descent the saints were freed from the places of the infernal regions; who, although they were in the bosom of Abraham, that is in the consolation of rest, yet were not wholly loosed from the sorrow of death or of hell, because they had not deserved to enter the joys of heaven, until that of the Prophet should come to pass: In the blood of the testament thou hast led forth thy prisoners out of the pit, wherein is no water (Zach. V). [Vulg. Zach. 9:11 in sanguine testamenti tui emisisti vinctos tuos, "thou hast sent forth"] For they were bound before they were led forth.

That I be not moved. 432DAfter the resurrection all things will be immovable. And the tongue hath exulted. Because rest after labour is given: for he laboured in the hope of eternal life. Thou wilt not leave. Let me not again be moved by the corruption of a repeated cross and death. Thou hast made known to me. I know that I shall always live. Thou shalt fill me with full gladness, that is, I shall not die again. Because he is at the right hand. XXVI): and he began to fear and to be heavy: that he might show himself true man 433Ain soul and body, and truly passible. But afterwards the soul and the tongue exulted, because by his passion the human race was to be saved.

VERS. 27.-- In hell. It is agreed that the soul was not left in hell, which, when those had been taken away for whose sake he had descended, straightway returned to the things above, nor was the flesh corrupted, but glorified by a swift resurrection.

VERS. 28.-- Thou hast made known to me. Not only are these things understood of him who, the fulness of divine power and wisdom being once received, destroyed death by himself, rising again to life; but also of the just who, the way of truth having been found through him, will return to life and will be gladdened, seeing his face: because that sight is above all things and is perfect beatitude.

And beautifully has he used the word of pouring out: which he recorded above that the prophet Joel had set down, that from this also it may be established that he himself is the Lord Jesus, who before the assumption of flesh was wont to speak in the prophets, who gives signs and wonders in heaven and on earth, who saves all that call upon him. VERS. 36.-- Him Lord. Setting down the name of majesty and of power, he shows him to be one to whom every creature ought to be subjected. Christ is the name of royal or of pontifical dignity. In the law kings and pontiffs were anointed, in a figure of him. And Christ. VERS. 39.-- And to all. 434BVERS. 6.-- Silver, etc. Peter was mindful of that precept: Do not possess gold and silver. The money also which was laid at the feet of the apostles they did not lay up for themselves, but kept for the uses of the poor, who had left their own patrimonies.

VERS. 8.-- And leaping up he stood, etc. The order of perfection: first he rises who had lain, then he seizes the way of the virtues, and so with the apostles enters the gate of the kingdom.

VERS. 10.-- With wonder and ecstasy. That is, with dread and with excess of mind. Ecstasy is also spoken of when the mind is not alienated by dread, but is taken up by the inspiration of revelation.

Ran together, etc. (BEDA.) Israel being saved through the apostles, 434Cthe whole world ran, etc., as far as he alone holds a peaceable empire throughout the world.

VERS. 13.-- The God of Abraham, etc. (RAB.) Three are commemorated, with no ungodly man coming between, either on account of the primacy of justice, or, etc., as far as which also is likewise observed in the spiritual generation.

VERS. 15.-- You killed, by will and by word, and this against the law, which says: He that shall kill a man willingly, he also shall be killed.

VERS. 16.-- Perfect soundness. In the two things which have been foretold.

VERS. 17.-- Through ignorance. Two things wrought together: your ignorance, and the foreknowledge of God.

VERS. 18.-- By the mouth of all, etc. Therefore was it necessary that it should be fulfilled, because not one, but all had prophesied.

434DVERS. 19.-- That sins may be blotted out. Then shall sins be fully blotted out, when by the power of the resurrection death shall be destroyed last.

VERS. 20.-- That when there shall come, etc. These things are said continuatively to what precedes, namely, that when the times of refreshment shall come from the presence of the Lord, then your sins may be blotted out.

VERS. 21.-- Whom it behoveth, etc. That is, the Church through faith, or to sit in heaven at the right hand of the Father.

VERS. 22.-- Moses indeed. He sets down examples, not of all who spoke, or of what things, but only of some.

435AThat a prophet, etc. The Son of God took flesh of the people of Israel, appearing in the likeness of Moses, that as he led the carnal Israel, instructed and purified by ceremonies and by sacred laws, into the land of promise, so also Jesus by the grace of the Gospel led the spiritual Israel to heaven. And as the despisers of the law of Moses were cast out of the land of promise by their enemies, so they who despise the doctrine of the Gospel shall be driven from the assembly of the saints by avenging spirits.

Of your brethren. (RAB.) God raised up Josue of Ephraim, Jesus of Juda.

VERS. 23.-- He shall be exterminated from among the people. That is, from the lot of the saints, and he shall be rendered an exile. Or, he shall be cast outside the bounds of beatitude.

435B(RAB.) They that resisted Josue were cast out of the carnal people; but they that resist Christ are exterminated from the spiritual: death follows either disobedience, whether carnal or spiritual.

From Samuel and thenceforth. Under Samuel the times of the kings began in Judaea.

VERS. 25.-- Of the testament. (BEDA.) From that testament which unto Moses, etc., as far as, in thy seed shall all nations be blessed. [Vulg. Act. 3:25 in semine tuo benedicentur omnes familiae terrae, "all the kindreds of the earth"]

Fathers. He set down the plural fathers, and not the singular father, because it was promised not to Abraham alone, but to Isaac and Jacob. In thy seed, etc. (BEDA.) The seed of Abraham is Christ Chistus: in whose faith the blessing is promised to all families, namely to Jews and to Gentiles.

435CAll families. Not that all are, but because none is blessed save in him, as also that: He enlighteneth every man that cometh into this world (Joan. I).

They shall be blessed. Cursed in the sin of Adam, they are blessed in Christ, in whom those also are blessed who went before the incarnation: because the same man God opened the gate of heaven, and at the end will say to all the elect: Come, ye blessed of the Father (Matth. XXV), etc.

CAPUT IV.

VERS. 1.-- Sacerdotes et magistratus, etc. Hi doctores et judices populi esse videbantur. Sadducaei. Hi negabant resurrectionem: et ideo in doctrina apostolorum qui annuntiabant resurrectionem, dolebant.

435DVERS. 2.-- Dolentes, quod ad audiendos apostolos conflueret multitudo.

Resurrectionem. Hoc est maxime contra Sadducaeos: utrique vero dolebant quod Jesum, quem ut hominem occiderant, apostoli a Deo Patre glorificatum affirmabant.

VERS. 4.-- Et factus est, etc. Si in quinque millibus hominum quos in eremo Dominus pavit, populus legis a Christo liberatus accipitur, possunt et hic quinque millia ab apostolis instructa populum gentium designare: ejusdem legis spiritaliter mysteria secuturum. Et bene utique vesperi coelesti munere donantur, quia cum venit plenitudo temporis, misit Deus Filium suum in terris, etc.

436AVERS. 11.-- Hic est lapis. (RAB.) Hic respondens ad interrogata de Christo praedicat, et maxime de David, cui, veluti patriarchae suo, prae caeteris crediderent.

Reprobatus. (AUG.) De lapide reprobato historia non habetur.

VERS. 13.-- Sine litteris, etc. Non quia litteras omnino nescirent, sed quia grammaticae artis peritiam non haberent, haec dicebantur. Idiotae. Idiotae dicebantur, quia proprium ingenium non exercuerant per doctrinam: ἰδίωμα Graece, Latine proprietas.

(BEDA.) Illitterati mittuntur ad praedicandum, ne fides credentium non Dei virtute, sed eloquentia fieri putaretur: secundum illud: Non in sapientia verbi, ut non evacuetur crux Christi (I Cor. I).

436BVERS. 19.-- Petrus vero, etc. (RAB.) Qui estis judices, vestrum facite officium, non sermonem pravum proferendo, sed juste judicando. Ecce plenum frumentum in spica invenitur: quod ante quasi in herba flectebatur.

VERS. 20.-- Non enim possumus. Id est, nolumus, vel in naturam versum est (ut angelis confirmatis) non peccare.

VERS. 21.-- Clarificabant. In Graeco: Omnes clarificabant in eo, quod factum est: et non habetur in eo quod acciderat.

Clarificantur facta vel dicta, quando fama vulgante in notitiam plurimorum deferuntur. Deus autem glorificatur in factis hominum, quod est proprium Christianae pietatis et humilitatis, ipso dicente: 436CVideant opera vestra bona, et glorificent Patrem vestrum, qui in coelis est (Matth. V).

VERS. 22.-- Annorum enim, etc. Unde apparuit amplior gloria vel gratia virtutis, et ideo non aetatem hominis, sed tempus infirmitatis dicit.

VERS. 24.-- Unanimiter, etc. Sine sono. Vel perfectione unanimitatis, secundum illud: Si duo vel tres convenerint in unum in nomine meo, de omni re quam petierint, fiet illis.

VERS. 25.-- Fremuerunt gentes. Fremitus est vox leonis. Unde Jeremias: Facta est mihi haereditas mea quasi leo in sylva: dedit super me vocem (Jer. XII). Meditati sunt inania. De Christo venturo, vel ut in morte detineretur.

VERS. 26.-- Convenerunt. Herodes et Pilatus convenerunt 436Din amicitiam.

VERS. 27.-- Unxisti. Nomen Christi exponunt. Christi, a chrismate, id est unctione, secundum illud: Unxit te Deus Deus tuus oleo laetitiae prae consortibus tuis (Psal. XLIV).

VERS. 29.-- Cum omni fiducia. Hoc Deum postulant, ut cum omni fiducia possint loqui verbum Dei.

VERS. 30.-- Per nomen. In Graeco: Per nomen pueri tui Jesu, quod magis apostolorum votis congruit, qui satagebant, ut homo crucifixus a Judaeis clarescentibus miraculis, per nomen ejus, qui est in coelum assumptus, ipsemet Dei Filius innotesceret.

VERS. 31.-- Motus est locus. Magna virtus orationis 437Afidelium, hoc autem ad suscitandas mentes factum est.

VERS. 32.-- Erat cor unum. Qui mundum reliquerant, non de nobilitate generis gloriantes se aliis praeferebant, sed sicut unius ejusdemque matris Ecclesiae visceribus editi, eodem fraternitatis amore gaudebant. Nec quisquam eorum. Virtutes quas possident justi, non suas judicant esse, sed eorum quorum utilitati dispensant. Communia. Graece κοῖνα· unde coenobitae, id est communiter viventes. Coenobia habitacula eorum.

VERS. 33.-- Reddebant apostoli. Discernit ordinem doctorum et auditorum. Nam multitudo credentium, rebus temporalibus spretis, copula charitatis invicem jungebantur. Apostoli vero, virtute fulgentes, 437Bmysteria Christi pandebant.

VERS. 36.-- Joseph. (RAB.) Auctus Barnabas filius consolationis interpretatur, actus incremento et profectu spiritualis boni.

Quod est interpretatum. Ubicunque Scriptura nomina rerum vel personarum cum interpretatione ponit, sensum sacratiorem eisdem inesse significat. Merito filius consolationis vocatur, qui, spretis praesentibus, spe futurorum se consolatur. Nam et Spiritus sanctus paracletus ideo vocatur, quia in mundo pressuram habentibus infusione sui, paraclesim, id est consolationem tribuit, sicut etiam Petrus propter gratiam Spiritus Barjona, id est filius columbae, vocatus est.

Filius consolationis. (RAB.). Vel propter eleemosynam 437Cqua pauperes consolatus est, vel pro mercedis consolatione quam meruit a Deo accipere, vel quia dignus habitus est qui pro Juda consolaretur apostolos.

CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 1.-- The priests and the magistrates, etc. These seemed to be the teachers and the judges of the people. The Sadducees. These denied the resurrection: and therefore they grieved at the teaching of the apostles, who announced the resurrection.

435DVERS. 2.-- Being grieved, that the multitude flowed together to hear the apostles.

The resurrection. This is most of all against the Sadducees: but both grieved that the apostles affirmed Jesus, whom they had slain as a man, to have been glorified by God the Father.

VERS. 4.-- And there was made, etc. If in the five thousand men whom the Lord fed in the desert the people of the law freed by Christ is understood, here also the five thousand instructed by the apostles can signify the people of the Gentiles: which was to follow the mysteries of the same law spiritually. And well indeed at evening are they endowed with a heavenly gift, because when the fulness of time came, God sent his Son upon the earth, etc.

436AVERS. 11.-- This is the stone. (RAB.) Here, answering the things asked of him, he preaches concerning Christ, and most of all concerning David, in whom, as in their own patriarch, they believed before all others.

Rejected. (AUG.) Of the rejected stone no history is had.

VERS. 13.-- Without letters, etc. These things were said not because they were wholly ignorant of letters, but because they had not the skill of the grammatical art. Idiots. They were called idiots, because they had not exercised their own proper wit through teaching: ἰδίωμα in Greek, in Latin, property.

(BEDA.) Unlettered men are sent to preach, lest the faith of believers should be thought to come about not by the power of God, but by eloquence: according to that: Not in wisdom of speech, lest the cross of Christ should be made void (I Cor. I).

436BVERS. 19.-- But Peter, etc. (RAB.) You who are judges, do your office, not by uttering a crooked speech, but by judging justly. Behold, full grain is found in the ear: which before was bending as it were in the blade.

VERS. 20.-- For we cannot. That is, we will not, or it has been turned into nature (as with the confirmed angels) not to sin.

VERS. 21.-- They made renowned. In the Greek: All made renowned in that which was done: and "in that which had befallen" is not had.

Deeds or words are made renowned when, by the spreading of report, they are brought to the knowledge of very many. But God is glorified in the deeds of men, which is proper to Christian piety and humility, himself saying: 436CThat they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven (Matth. V).

VERS. 22.-- For he was of years, etc. Whence appeared a fuller glory or grace of the power, and therefore he speaks not of the age of the man, but of the time of the infirmity.

VERS. 24.-- With one accord, etc. Without sound. Or by the perfection of unanimity, according to that: If two or three shall come together into one in my name, of every thing which they shall ask, it shall be done for them.

VERS. 25.-- The Gentiles have raged. Roaring is the voice of a lion. Whence Jeremias: My inheritance is become to me as a lion in the wood: it hath given forth its voice over me (Jer. XII). [Vulg. Jer. 12:8 dedit contra me vocem, "gave her voice against me"] They have devised vain things. Of Christ to come, or that he might be held fast in death.

VERS. 26.-- They came together. Herod and Pilate came together 436Dinto friendship.

VERS. 27.-- Thou hast anointed. They expound the name of Christ. Of Christ, from chrism, that is from unction, according to that: God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows (Psal. XLIV).

VERS. 29.-- With all confidence. This they ask of God, that with all confidence they may be able to speak the word of God.

VERS. 30.-- By the name. In the Greek: By the name of thy child Jesus, which agrees better with the desires of the apostles, who were busied about this, that the man crucified by the Jews, the miracles growing bright, might through the name of him who was taken up into heaven become known as himself the Son of God.

VERS. 31.-- The place was moved. Great is the power of the prayer 437Aof the faithful, but this was done for the rousing up of minds.

VERS. 32.-- There was one heart. They who had left the world did not, glorying in the nobility of their race, prefer themselves to others, but, as brought forth from the bowels of one and the same mother the Church, rejoiced in the same love of brotherhood. Neither did any one of them. The virtues which the just possess they judge to be not their own, but of those to whose profit they dispense them. Common. In Greek κοῖνα· whence coenobites, that is, those living in common. Coenobia are their dwellings.

VERS. 33.-- The apostles gave. He distinguishes the order of the teachers and of the hearers. For the multitude of believers, temporal things being despised, were joined to one another by the bond of charity. But the apostles, shining with power, 437Bset forth the mysteries of Christ.

VERS. 36.-- Joseph. (RAB.) Increased, Barnabas is interpreted son of consolation, being driven by the increase and the advance of spiritual good.

Which is, being interpreted. Wherever Scripture sets down the names of things or of persons with an interpretation, it signifies that a more sacred sense is in them. Deservedly is he called son of consolation, who, present things being despised, consoles himself with the hope of things to come. For the Holy Spirit also is therefore called Paraclete, because to those having tribulation in the world he bestows, by the infusion of himself, paraclesis, that is, consolation, as Peter also was called Barjona, that is son of the dove, on account of the grace of the Spirit.

Son of consolation. (RAB.). Either on account of the alms 437Cwith which he consoled the poor, or for the consolation of the reward which he deserved to receive from God, or because he was held worthy to console the apostles in Judas' stead.

CAPUT V.

VERS. 3.-- Tentavit. (RAB.) Praeteritum tentavit ponit, quia perfecta erat tentatio. Tribus modis tentatio agitur, suasione, delectatione, consensu: quod totum impletum est in Anania. Et fraudare. (ID.) In hoc videtur mendacium fuisse, quia totum voverat. Melius est autem non vovere quam post votum non solvere Spiritui sancto.

VERS. 4.-- Sed Deo. Supra dixerat: Spiritui sancto: patet ergo Spiritum sanctum esse Deum.

VERS. 5.-- Cecidit. (RAB.) Legum initia semper 437Dvindictis commendantur. Duo filii Aaron ignem offerentes consumuntur: Oza sustinens arcam mortuus est. Cecidit ille, ut caeteri praevaricatores exemplo ejus terreantur.

Et factus est timor, etc. (BEDA.) Anticipatio: quoniam solius viri morte ante uxoris exitum pauci audierant, quamvis de paucis dici potuerit, quod timuerunt.

VERS. 7.-- Horarum, etc. (RAB.) Parvum tempus poenitentiae datur uxori, cujus culpa magis est venialis quam mariti, utpote quae consensit capiti.

VERS. 8.-- Dic mihi, etc. (ID.) Culpam uxoris, interrogans, declarat ex ipsius confessione, quam maritus suam monstraverat in opere.

438AVERS. 9.-- Quid utique, etc. Increpative dictum? vel sic distinguendum: quid? quasi: Quid est hoc: deinde sibi ipse respondet et dicit: Utique convenit inter vos tentare Spiritum Domini?

Tentare, etc. (RAB.) Tentatio Spiritus in hoc erat quod probaverunt quanta sit ejus scientia.

Sepelierunt. (ID.) Ex hoc multi exemplum sumunt, ut uxores ad viros suos sepeliant. Alii indignum putant, ut de praevaricatoribus exempla capiant, quibus in crimine simul et morte et sepultura meritum, aequale fecerat consortium.

VERS. 11.-- Et factus est. Prius Ananias a Saphira in conspectu Ecclesiae cito mortui sunt, ut apostolica auctoritas, quanta esset, ostenderetur: et quam magnum peccatum esset, quod oblatum est, 438Biterum ab Ecclesia retrahere monstraretur, et caeteri hujus exemplo castigarentur.

VERS. 12.-- Apostolorum. Dicitur pluraliter apostolorum, quia non per manum solius Petri; licet enim pro honore ejus caeteri taceantur, non tamen facta eorum tacentur.

VERS. 13.-- Caeterorum, etc. Exemplo Ananiae et uxoris: poena enim duorum qui fraudulenter eis se conjunxerant, caeteris tribuit exemplum.

VERS. 14.-- Augebatur, etc. (RAB.) Tanta erat multitudo credentium, ut fidei crescente fortitudine, sola sufficeret umbra. Vel: Tot erant tunc infirmi a credentibus allati, ut omnes non possent contingere.

VERS. 17.-- Haeresis. (RAB.) Haeresis scissio interpretatur, quia haereticus, spretis aliorum dictis, 438Cquod sequendum putat eligit.

Sadducaeorum repleti, etc. Sadducaei interpretantur justi, dicti a Sadoch sacerdote, vel propter genus, vel propter mores; justificantes autem seipsos vindicabant sibi quod non erant: corporum resurrectionem negantes, animas cum carne perire dicebant: sed nec angelum, neque spirituum ullum esse credebant, omnes Scripturas praeter libros Moysi respuebant: et ideo maxime principibus in persecutione apostolorum consentiebant qui virtute et signis reddebant testimonium resurrectionis Christi.

VERS. 20.-- Vitae hujus. (RAB.) Christianae, vel aeternae. Hoc autem ideo non additur, quia famosa erat secta Christianorum, pro qua traditi erant carceri.

438DVERS. 23.-- Carcerem quidem, etc. Insani Judaei qui stimulante invidia dicunt furantibus apostolis Jesu de monumento sublatum, cujus furto dicant apostolos de carcere clauso ablatos.

VERS. 24.-- Magistratus. Secundum Graecum magistratus legendum est in singulari numero, sicut et in sequenti ubi dicitur: Tunc abiit magistratus cum ministris. Ambigebant. Nec sic credunt: malitia enim interior cor perfidum signis etiam manifestis obdurat.

VERS. 28.-- Praecipiendo etc. In Graeco: Nonne praecipiendo praecepimus vobis? Nam sic loqui, magis convenit interroganti. Replestis Jerusalem. Principem civitatum, in qua etiam nos conversamur. In hoc ignoranter laudant apostolos, qui nec territi minis 439Acessant praedicare. Super nos sanguinem. (RAB.) Patet per antiphrasim dictum esse: Sanguis ejus super nos.

Temeraria interrogatio pontificis refutatur responsione prudenti.

(ID.) Interrogant, solet enim in verbis esse dubietas, cum in corde firma teneatur sententia.

VERS. 30.-- Deus patrum. (RAB.) Non novus, nec a nobis nuper inventus: hoc autem dicit, ut eorum inobedientia sit inexcusabilis.

VERS. 32.-- Spiritus sanctus. Nos Spiritus sanctus roboravit, ut vobis etiam resistentibus magnalia Dei firmiter praedicemus, in nobis loquens format verba quae auditis.

Omnibus. (RAB.) Non nobis solis, sed omnibus 439Bobedientibus, etiam vobis, si velitis accipere. In hoc ad poenitentiam provocat.

VERS. 34.-- Gamaliel. Hic erat discipulus apostolorum, ut putatur, remanens apud Judaeos consilio Apostolorum, ut pessima destrueret consilia, et furorem eorum mitigaret.

VERS. 36.-- Theodas. (BEDA.) Theodas persuasit multis sublatis ex urbe facultatibus suis ripas Jordanis obsidere, et cum esset magus, prophetam se dicebat, et diviso fluvio se praebere transitum promittebat. Cui jussu Fadi procuratoris equitum turba superveniens, multis peremptis aut captis, ipsius caput Hierosolymam revexit.

VERS. 37.-- Judas Galilaeus, etc. De hoc Josephus dicit quia cogebat populum ne tributa solvendo 439CRomanis libertatem amitteret: proferens ex lege soli Deo serviendum, et eos qui decimas dabant, tributa non dare debere. Quae haeresis intantum crevit, ut Pharisaei et multa pars populi Christum interrogarent, an liceret tributa dare Caesari.

VERS. 40.-- Caesis, etc. (RAB.) Ecce fortitudo fidei patet, quae nec verbis cohiberi poterat, nec flagellis. Prius sunt sermone prohibiti, modo flagellis caesi.

VERS. 41.-- Quoniam digni. Non putabant se laborare, sed maximum munus percipere: quia Beati qui persecutionem patiuntur propter Deum (Matth. VI).

VERS. 42.-- Omni autem die, etc. Saepe talia interponuntur capitula, breviter multa colligentia, ut quasi finis praeterito sermoni, et fundamentum statuatur 439Dsequenti.

CHAPTER V.

VERS. 3.-- He tempted. (RAB.) He sets down the preterite, he tempted, because the temptation was complete. Temptation is carried on in three ways, by persuasion, by delight, by consent: all of which was fulfilled in Ananias. And to defraud. (ID.) In this it seems there was a lie, because he had vowed the whole. But it is better not to vow than after a vow not to pay to the Holy Spirit.

VERS. 4.-- But to God. Above he had said: to the Holy Spirit: it is plain therefore that the Holy Spirit is God.

VERS. 5.-- He fell. (RAB.) The beginnings of laws are always commended by 437Dpunishments. The two sons of Aaron, offering fire, are consumed: Oza, upholding the ark, died. He fell, that the rest of the transgressors might be terrified by his example.

And there came fear, etc. (BEDA.) An anticipation: since by the death of the man alone, before the wife's end, few had heard of it, although it could be said of the few that they feared.

VERS. 7.-- Of hours, etc. (RAB.) A short time of penance is given to the wife, whose fault is more venial than the husband's, inasmuch as she consented to her head.

VERS. 8.-- Tell me, etc. (ID.) By questioning, he declares the wife's fault out of her own confession, the fault which the husband had shown to be his own in the deed.

438AVERS. 9.-- Why is it that, etc. Was it said by way of rebuke? or it is thus to be distinguished: what? as if: What is this: then he himself answers himself and says: Is it indeed agreed among you to tempt the Spirit of the Lord?

To tempt, etc. (RAB.) The tempting of the Spirit lay in this, that they made trial how great his knowledge is.

They buried. (ID.) From this many take an example, that wives should be buried beside their husbands. Others think it unworthy that examples be taken from transgressors, for whom their partnership had made their desert equal alike in the crime and in the death and in the burial.

VERS. 11.-- And there came. First Ananias by [a; read ac, "and"] Saphira died quickly in the sight of the Church, that the apostolic authority, how great it was, might be shown: and that it might be shown how great a sin it is to withdraw 438Bagain from the Church what has been offered, and that the rest might be chastened by this example.

VERS. 12.-- Of the apostles. It is said in the plural, of the apostles, because it was not by the hand of Peter alone; for although for his honour the rest are passed over in silence, yet their deeds are not passed over in silence.

VERS. 13.-- Of the rest, etc. By the example of Ananias and his wife: for the punishment of the two who had fraudulently joined themselves to them gave an example to the rest.

VERS. 14.-- It was increased, etc. (RAB.) So great was the multitude of them that believed, that, the strength of faith growing, the shadow alone sufficed. Or: So many sick were then brought by the believers, that they could not all touch him.

VERS. 17.-- Heresy. (RAB.) Heresy is interpreted a division, because the heretic, despising the sayings of others, 438Cchooses what he thinks is to be followed.

Of the Sadducees they were filled, etc. The Sadducees are interpreted the just, named from Sadoch the priest, either for their race or for their manners; but justifying themselves they claimed for themselves what they were not: denying the resurrection of bodies, they said that souls perish with the flesh: but they believed that there was neither an angel nor any of spirits, and they rejected all the Scriptures except the books of Moses: and therefore they consented most of all with the princes in the persecution of the apostles, who by power and by signs bore witness to the resurrection of Christ.

VERS. 20.-- Of this life. (RAB.) Christian, or eternal. But this is not added for this reason, that the sect of the Christians was famous, for which they had been delivered to prison.

438DVERS. 23.-- The prison indeed, etc. Mad are the Jews who at the goading of envy say that Jesus was carried off from the tomb by the apostles stealing him, by whose theft they may say that the apostles were carried off out of the closed prison.

VERS. 24.-- The magistrates. According to the Greek, magistratus is to be read in the singular number, as also in what follows where it is said: Then the magistrate went away with the ministers. They doubted. Not even so do they believe: for inward malice hardens a faithless heart even against manifest signs.

VERS. 28.-- By commanding etc. In the Greek: Did we not by commanding command you? For to speak thus better befits one who questions. You have filled Jerusalem. The chief of cities, in which we also dwell. In this they praise the apostles unwittingly, who, terrified by threats, 439Acease not to preach. Upon us the blood. (RAB.) It is plain that it was said by antiphrasis: His blood be upon us.

The rash questioning of the high priest is refuted by a prudent answer.

(ID.) They question, for there is wont to be doubt in words, when in the heart a fixed opinion is held.

VERS. 30.-- The God of the fathers. (RAB.) Not new, nor lately found out by us: but he says this, that their disobedience may be inexcusable.

VERS. 32.-- The Holy Spirit. The Holy Spirit has strengthened us, that even though you resist we may firmly preach the great works of God, speaking in us and forming the words which you hear.

To all. (RAB.) Not to us alone, but to all 439Bthat obey, to you also, if you will receive. In this he calls them to penance.

VERS. 34.-- Gamaliel. He was a disciple of the apostles, as is thought, remaining among the Jews by the counsel of the apostles, that he might destroy their worst counsels and mitigate their fury.

VERS. 36.-- Theodas. (BEDA.) Theodas persuaded many, when they had taken their goods out of the city, to beset the banks of the Jordan, and though he was a magician, he called himself a prophet, and promised that, the river being divided, he would afford a passage. A troop of horsemen coming upon him by order of Fadus the procurator, many being slain or taken, carried his head back to Jerusalem.

VERS. 37.-- Judas the Galilean, etc. Of him Josephus says that he compelled the people not to lose their liberty by paying tribute 439Cto the Romans: bringing forward out of the law that God alone is to be served, and that those who gave tithes ought not to pay tribute. Which heresy grew so far that the Pharisees and a great part of the people asked Christ whether it were lawful to give tribute to Caesar.

VERS. 40.-- Having beaten them, etc. (RAB.) Behold, the fortitude of faith is made plain, which could be restrained neither by words nor by scourges. Before they were forbidden by speech, now they are beaten with scourges.

VERS. 41.-- That they were worthy. They thought not that they laboured, but that they received a very great gift: because Blessed are they that suffer persecution for God (Matth. VI). [Vulg. Matth. 5:10 propter justitiam, "for justice' sake"]

VERS. 42.-- And every day, etc. Often such summaries are interposed, briefly gathering many things, so that, as it were, an end may be set to the discourse that has gone before, and a foundation 439Dto that which follows.

CAPUT VI.

VERS. 2.-- Men, brethren, and fathers. Because Stephen was charged with having blasphemed against Moses and God, at the beginning of his discourse he meets their calumny, saying that the God who spoke to the fathers and to the prophets is the God of majesty and of glory. Moses also he praises: but he proves them rebellious and always disobedient. But he used art of speaking toward the rebellious, beginning 440Das though he feared them: thus, Men, brethren, and fathers: a gentle and clement speech wins over the hearer, so that it may commend the pleader. He began smoothly, that he might be heard long, and he who refused to speak against God and the law preaches the true God at the beginning of his oration. The law also he so expounded, that he was the preacher of that whereof he was accused of being the destroyer: in the further course of his discourse also, refuting their errors, he taught fully the vigour of his mind, and that he was free from the fear of the enemy.

The God of glory, etc. Stephen, mindful of that precept: Love your enemies (Luc. VI), etc., announced salvation to his persecutors.

VERS. 38.-- The words of life. (RAB.) That is, the two tables with the Decalogue, and the other commandments, of which it is said: He that shall do them shall live in them.

VERS. 40.-- To Aaron. (ID.) As to a prince. They speak as ignorant of the Divinity. For the true God 443Cwas not made, but made all things, nor is he a creature, but the Creator of all.

Make us gods. (ID.) They say gods, because of the two pillars, etc., as far as the pillar of fire went before the people by night.

VERS. 42.-- The host of heaven. The host of heaven is sometimes said to be the army of angels, but in this place he names the stars the host of heaven, since he presently adds, the star of your God. In the book, etc. Although the prophets are many, yet it is said to be one book, and among the Hebrews there is one book.

Did you offer victims. (RAB.) Although by the necessity of serving God they made offerings, yet in mind, etc., as far as they always returned in heart into Egypt.

It is asked: how did they not offer to God, when it is read 443Dthat they offered certain things to God, not of will, but from the fear of punishments? But God receives not the things which are offered, but the will of those who offer.

VERS. 43.-- Figures, etc. (RAB.) The star of your God is Lucifer, to whom they assigned figures. The same gods were adored in figures and in stars.

(ID.) Some so say "the star of your God," that the word pertains to God, because they received not the Lord himself, but his star.

And I will carry you away. For these sacrileges you shall be led captive beyond Babylon. The first martyr is not to be thought to have erred, who, for that which is written in the prophet, They were led beyond Damascus, said, beyond Babylon: for he set down the meaning rather than the word 444A, because they were led beyond Damascus into Babylon, or beyond Babylon.

VERS. 44.-- The tabernacle. Because they said that he acted against the holy place, he shows that he did not speak blasphemy against the holy place

According to the form. (RAB.) Which he had shown him in Sina, because, since it was made also according to a form, the tabernacle itself is shown to have been a form.

(ID.) Historically it is said with Jesus, because Moses was dead.

VERS. 46.-- That he might find a tabernacle. That is, the true temple, of which that was the figure, which should not be changed in place, that is, the Church which he foresaw would be firm and unchangeable, of which the Apostle says: The firm foundation of the Lord standeth (II Tim. II, 9). [Vulg. 2 Tim. 2:19 fundamentum Dei, "the foundation of God"] And elsewhere: 444BNo man can lay another foundation but that which is laid, which is Christ Jesus (I Cor. III).

VERS. 48.-- But not the Most High. (RAB.) From the saying of Solomon he convicts the Jews, etc., as far as that he does not dwell in the temple of the Jews alone.

As by the prophet, etc. Of the first tabernacle, which was a shadow and not the truth, he spoke before, and afterward set down the testimony of one speaking of the tabernacle which was truer. If I shall enter into the tabernacle of the Lord, etc., at the last sentence he set God. That in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word may stand. [Vulg. Psal. 131:3 tabernaculum domus meae, "the tabernacle of my house"]

VERS. 49.-- Heaven to me. Thus he shows that he encompasses all things; whence elsewhere he asserts that he measures heaven with a span, and encloses the earth with his fist.

And the earth. By the example of the earth he designates every creature 444Cof the Creator.

VERS. 51.-- Hard of neck. (RAB.) Purged of the crime of which the false witnesses accused him, he begins to accuse the Jews, because he who is without crime is worthy of the office of a teacher.

And uncircumcised, etc. He shows them that circumcision, in which they gloried against the grace of the Gospel, avails not to salvation for those who had thought and hearing unclean.

VERS. 52.-- Your fathers. (RAB.) Above he said our fathers, speaking by way of persuasion, now by way of rebuke he calls them the fathers of those men only, to whom they are like in malice.

And they slew. (RAB.) He sets down three degrees of their malice 444D: the first is, that they resisted the spirit; the second, that they persecuted the prophets; the third, that with malice increasing they slew them.

VERS. 55.-- Standing. Immovably, because he is not ite rum to fall, or the bearing is signified of one helping, and judging wrongs, and fighting for his servant.

I see the heavens. (RAB.) About to depart from the earth, he saw the heavens opened, that he who despised the earthly life for Christ might merit the inheritance of heaven, and therefore in his very suffering he sees the reward.

The Son of man. Although Christ is the Son of God, yet he chose rather to call him the Son of man, that the unbelief of the Jews might be confounded, who crucified him as a mere man, whom they would not believe to be God.

445AVERS. 57.-- And casting him out. (RAB.) In this they gloried that they fulfilled the law. For Moses said of the blasphemer: Bring him forth without the camp, and let the people stone him (Lev. XXIV).

VERS. 58.-- Lord Jesus, receive my spirit, into the heaven which I see opened. Christ said to the Father: Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit. Stephen, as a servant to the Lord, Lord, etc.

Because they know not. (RAB.) Many of them, in killing Stephen, thought that they fulfilled the law, because they believed him a blasphemer. This also Christ had foretold: There shall come, he says, an hour, that everyone who killeth you may think that he rendereth a service to God (Joan. XVI). [Vulg. Joan. 16:2 venit hora, "the hour cometh"]

VERS. 59.-- He fell asleep. Beautifully is it said, He fell asleep, and not, He is dead, because he offered the sacrifice 445Bof love, and fell asleep in the hope of the resurrection. The saints do not die, because they are changed for the better, and the temporal life is turned into the eternal.

And Saul, etc. He did not indeed stone him, but consented to them that stoned. Whence he would have perished, unless the grace of God and penance had come to his aid; because they who do and they who consent are worthy of death.

Ecclesia. Collectio vel congregatio, sive vocatio. Dispersi sunt. Hoc est quod Dominus praecepit: Cum 445Cpersequentur vos in civitate ista, fugite in aliam (Matth. X). Ejus enim nutu gerebatur: ut occasione tribulationis fieret seminarium Evangelii.

VERS. 12.-- Philippo. (RAB.) Dicunt quidam istum Philippum fuisse apostolum, et unum de duodecim, sed non videtur verum secundum seriem hujus libri.

Jesu Christi. (ID.) Cujus erant ignari vel (ut praedictum est) in nomine Jesu Trinitatem praedicavit, et qui tantum unam personam de Trinitate perfecte credit, credulitate aliarum duarum carere non potest.

VERS. 14.-- Miserunt. Petrus Joannem sociat sibi, quia virginitas Ecclesiae grata est, et placet.

VERS. 16.-- In nomine. Non est sic legendum, in nomine Jesu tantum, ut aliae personae excludantur, sed sic: tantum baptizati erant, etc., id est necdum confirmati. Jesu. Tamen ante, in Patrem credeban et in Spiritum sanctum, sicut alii Judaei. Vel, plena fides unius personae fidem Trinitatis confirmat.

VERS. 17.-- Imponebant manus. (BEDA.) Philippus, qui Samariae evangelizabat, unus de septem, etc., usque ad cum tradit Spiritum paracletum baptizatis.

VERS. 19.-- Potestatem. (RAB.) Non baptizandi 446Ctantum in aqua et praedicandi, sive virtutes faciendi quam habebat Philippus, sed cuicunque imponendi manus, etc.

VERS. 20.-- Pecunia tua. Non nostra, qui non cupimus, sed tua, quam Spiritui comparas, ut illam pro eo vendas, et illam Spiritu emas. Ideo Spiritum emere volebat, ut aliis vendens eum, plus accipere posset.

Pecunia. (RAB.) Ex hoc exemplum capitur, ut perditorum eleemosyna non recipiatur.

Apostoli nolentes pecuniam, dicunt, sit tecum, id est tuam retine, et hoc, in perditionem, ut, pecunia injuste congregata, poenam aeternam luas.

VERS. 22.-- Poenitentiam age. Pro cupiditate, et pravo sensu quo putasti de Spiritu commercium 446Dfieri posse.

(RAB.) Ut omnes videant id quod es. Non quod ignoret Petrus eum poenitentiam non acturum, sed quia omnibus justum est praedicare, suum facit officium, ut ille sit inexcusabilis. Coram omnibus etiam arguitur ne amplius ab eo fallantur.

Roga Deum. Cui patet cor, de quo Propheta: Quam bonus Israel Deus (Psal. LXXII); quem fallere nemo potest, quia Spiritus sanctus disciplinae effugiet fictum (Sap. I).

VERS. 24.-- Precamini. Causa desperationis non audet ipse precari, sed apostolos rogat, ut pro se precentur, nec pro se audet orare, qui non credit se exaudiendum esse. Apostolis etiam ficte loquitur. 447AQuae dixistis. Non quae credidi, sed quasi infidelis eis imputat quae ventura sunt.

VERS. 26.-- Angelus autem. Post reditum apostolorum, de Philippo historia texitur. Ad viam, etc. Nam via descendit, et per viam descenditur ex natura loci.

VERS. 27.-- Aethiops. Natura et merito. Aethiops enim tenebrosus. Unde: Aethiopia praeveniet manus ejus Deo (Psal. LXVII).

Potens, etc. (BEDA.) Regina Aethiopum thesaurarium praemittens, etc., usque ad, Audi, filia, et vide et inclina aurem tuam (Psal. XLIV), etc.

Candacis reginae. Mos erat illius regionis a feminis regi, et eas Candaces vocari.

VERS. 28.-- Revertebatur. Incipiebat reverti legens 447BIsaiam. Ubicunque in Novo Testamento salutis narratur principium, de Isaia sumitur testimonium, qui interpretatur salus Domini.

VERS. 30.-- Putasne intelligis, etc. (RAB.) Suas invicem linguas agnoscunt, vel per doctrinam eunuchi vel per spiritum Philippi.

VERS. 31.-- Quomodo, etc. Hic arguit illorum audaciam qui existimant sine doctore Scripturam divinam intelligere.

VERS. 33.-- In humilitate. (BEDA.) Etenim judex omnium, veritatem judicii non reperit, sed sine ulla sua culpa, seditione Judaeorum, et voce Pilati, damnatus est.

Humilitate. (RAB.) Passione qua humiliavit se factus obediens usque ad mortem (Philip. II).

447CTolletur. Secundus homo de coelo coelestis, sanctitate segregatus a terrenis, vel, de vita in mortem transibit.

VERS. 35.-- Aperuit. (RAB.) Thesaurum scientiae aperuit. Vel aperuit os, ut Spiritus loquatur in eo, secundum illud: Dilata os tuum, et implebo illud (Psal. LXXX).

Mystice, os Ecclesiae Philippus aperuit, ut ex hoc Gentibus is praedicetur quem prius nesciebant.

Evangelizavit. Solvit quaestionem illius interrogantis: De quo propheta dixit? Demonstrat autem quod de Jesu prophetatum est.

VERS. 36.-- Ad quamdam aquam. Ad hoc enim praedicatio duxerat, ut aqua indigeret, quae de baptismo in via audierat.

447DVERS. 37.-- Si credis. (RAB.) Non ignorabat ejus fidem, sed volebat manifestam confessionem, ut baptizaturis et baptizandis daretur exemplum interrogandi et confitendi.

VERS. 39.-- Spiritus Domini. (RAB.) Vel Spiritus irruit in eum fortiter, sicut et in apostolos, etc., usque ad in quos Spiritus descenderat?

VERS. 40.-- Inventus est. Ab hominibus qui in raptu non inveniebatur, quia ambulavit in solitudine, vel nimia velocitate.

Caesaream. (BEDA.) Caesaream Palaestinae dicit ubi infra domum habuisse describitur, quae usque hodie demonstratur, necnon et cubiculum filiarum quatuor ejus prophetantium virginum.

CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 1.-- In that day. (RAB.) On which Stephen was slain, not because it was made then only, but because from that day the great persecution began, one of the princes of the Church having been slain.

The Church. A collection or a congregation, or a calling. They were dispersed. This is what the Lord commanded: When 445Cthey shall persecute you in this city, flee into another (Matth. X). For by his nod it was carried on: that by the occasion of tribulation there might be made a seed-plot of the Gospel.

VERS. 2.-- And devout men took care of Stephen, that is, men fearing God, either Christians, who were afterwards dispersed, or Jews fearing God though not yet Christians.

VERS. 3.-- Saul. (RAB.) From Saul the persecutor, who was king of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin, of which Paul also was; for indeed according to the custom of the ancients descendants draw their names from their forebears.

He laid waste. (ID.) As a wolf the flock, according to the prophecy of Jacob: Benjamin a ravening wolf, in the morning shall eat the prey, at evening shall divide the spoil (Gen. XLIX).

Dragging. He dragged, he did not kill, because perhaps the Lord was guarding his hands, lest they should be polluted with the blood of the innocent.

Dispersed. (RAB.) Although dispersed by fear, yet they were steadfast in the preaching of the Gospel.

VERS. 5.-- Philip. He was of the number of those that were scattered, who first preached Christ to Samaria. A deacon second from Stephen. He is related as second after him, as it were his heir and of the same degree.

VERS. 6.-- They gave heed. (RAB.) They believed, hearing the words and seeing the signs. Two causes of faith: the words of preaching, and the signs of the miracles. From the present chapter and from the history of the Samaritan woman it is proved that the minds of this people were ready to believe, says Bede.

446AVERS. 11.-- By magic arts. (RAB.) Magicians philosophize about particular things, about the nature of the world, the course of the stars, and their motions. Seducing. Leading apart and away from the truth, or turning them into excess, or into stupor and wonder, by his tricks. He had bereft of mind. To bereave of mind is to make the rational mind absent.

VERS. 12.-- Philip. (RAB.) Some say that this Philip was an apostle, and one of the twelve, but it does not seem true according to the order of this book.

Of Jesus Christ. (ID.) Of whom they were ignorant, or (as has been said before) in the name of Jesus he preached the Trinity, and he who perfectly believes one person only of the Trinity cannot lack the belief of the other two.

446BVERS. 13.-- Then Simon. (ID.) Either he believed the Lord, by the words of Philip and by the power, etc., as far as having fraudulently entered the Church, they steal baptism.

VERS. 14.-- They sent. Peter joins John to himself, because virginity is acceptable to the Church, and pleases.

VERS. 16.-- In the name. It is not to be read thus, in the name of Jesus only, as though the other persons were excluded, but thus: they were only baptized, etc., that is, not yet confirmed. Of Jesus. Yet before, they credeban in the Father and in the Holy Spirit, as did the other Jews. Or, the full faith of one person confirms the faith of the Trinity.

VERS. 17.-- They laid on hands. (BEDA.) Philip, who preached the Gospel in Samaria, one of the seven, etc., as far as when he delivers the Spirit the Paraclete to the baptized.

VERS. 19.-- Power. (RAB.) Not of baptizing 446Conly in water and of preaching, or of working miracles, which Philip had, but of laying hands upon whomsoever, etc.

VERS. 20.-- Thy money. Not ours, who do not covet, but thine, which thou comparest with the Spirit, that thou mayest sell it for him, and buy it with the Spirit. Therefore he wished to buy the Spirit, that selling him to others he might be able to receive more.

Money. (RAB.) From this an example is taken, that the alms of the lost be not received.

The apostles, not willing the money, say, be it with thee, that is, keep thine own, and this, unto perdition, that, the money being unjustly gathered, thou mayest pay an eternal penalty.

VERS. 22.-- Do penance. For the covetousness, and the depraved sense by which thou hast thought that a traffic of the Spirit could 446Dbe made.

(RAB.) That all may see what thou art. Not that Peter is ignorant that he will not do penance, but because it is just to preach to all, he does his own office, that the man may be inexcusable. Before all men also he is rebuked, lest they be any more deceived by him.

Pray to God. To whom the heart lies open, of whom the Prophet says: How good is God to Israel (Psal. LXXII); whom no man can deceive, because the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee the deceitful (Sap. I).

VERS. 24.-- Pray ye. By reason of despair he does not himself dare to pray, but asks the apostles to pray for him, nor does he dare to pray for himself, who does not believe that he will be heard. To the apostles also he speaks feignedly. 447AWhich you have said. Not, which I have believed, but as an unbeliever he imputes to them the things which are to come.

VERS. 26.-- And an angel. After the return of the apostles, the history is woven concerning Philip. To the way, etc. For the way goes down, and by the way one goes down, from the nature of the place.

VERS. 27.-- An Ethiopian. By nature and by desert. For an Ethiopian is dark. Whence: Ethiopia shall prevent her hands to God (Psal. LXVII).

A man of power, etc. (BEDA.) The queen of the Ethiopians sending her treasurer before her, etc., as far as, Hearken, O daughter, and see, and incline thy ear (Psal. XLIV), etc.

Of queen Candace. It was the custom of that region to be ruled by women, and that they should be called Candaces.

VERS. 28.-- He was returning. He was beginning to return, reading 447BIsaias. Wherever in the New Testament the beginning of salvation is related, the testimony is taken from Isaias, who is interpreted the salvation of the Lord.

VERS. 30.-- Thinkest thou that thou understandest, etc. (RAB.) They know each other's tongues, either through the learning of the eunuch or through the spirit of Philip.

VERS. 31.-- How, etc. Here he reproves the boldness of those who think that they understand Holy Scripture without a teacher.

VERS. 33.-- In humility. (BEDA.) For indeed the judge of all found not the truth of judgment, but without any fault of his own, by the sedition of the Jews and by the voice of Pilate, he was condemned.

Humility. (RAB.) By the passion in which he humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death (Philip. II).

447CHe shall be taken away. The second man from heaven, heavenly, separated by holiness from earthly things, or, he shall pass from life into death.

VERS. 35.-- He opened. (RAB.) He opened the treasure of knowledge. Or he opened his mouth, that the Spirit might speak in him, according to that: Open wide thy mouth, and I will fill it (Psal. LXXX).

Mystically, Philip opened the mouth of the Church, that from this he might be preached to the Gentiles whom before they knew not.

He preached the Gospel. He solves the question of him that asked: Of whom did the prophet speak? But he shows that it was prophesied of Jesus.

VERS. 36.-- To a certain water. For to this the preaching had led, that he should need water, the things which he had heard on the way concerning baptism.

447DVERS. 37.-- If thou believest. (RAB.) He was not ignorant of his faith, but wished an open confession, that to those who are to baptize and to those who are to be baptized an example might be given of questioning and of confessing.

VERS. 39.-- The Spirit of the Lord. (RAB.) Or the Spirit rushed upon him mightily, as also upon the apostles, etc., as far as upon whom the Spirit had descended?

VERS. 40.-- He was found. By men, he who in his rapture was not found, because he walked in the wilderness, or with exceeding swiftness.

Caesarea. (BEDA.) He means the Caesarea of Palestine, where below he is described to have had a house, which is shown even to this day, and also the chamber of his four daughters, virgins that prophesied.

VERS. 1.-- And Saul. He returns to that which he had said above: Saul was still laying waste the Church. Of threatenings, etc. Terrifying with threats, he afflicted with slaughter those present: it is doubted whether he killed or scourged, or did both. Breathing. He breathes after the manner of fire, and not incited by another he himself set others on fire.

VERS. 4.-- And falling. Terrified, he flees back to the ground, or as one adoring he falls upon his face.

Why me, etc. (BEDA.) He says not: Why dost thou persecute, etc., as far as, As long as you did it to one of these least ones, you did it to me (Matth. XXV).

VERS. 5.-- I am Jesus. He calls himself not God nor the Son of God, but, Receive, he says, the weak things of my humility, and lay aside the scales of thy pride.

448BVERS. 7.-- Arise. Work untiring as an ox, and be a teacher of the Church. In the law it is written: Thou shalt not muzzle the mouth of the ox that treadeth out the corn (I Cor. IX).

Enter in. (RAB.) About to enter the city he is terrified, lest he bring sorrow upon the saints; thereafter the Lord is bidden to enter, that he may learn the faith which he had assailed.

And it shall be said. An example of learning is given, and pride is rebuked, that no one may think another unworthy to be taught by him what he knows not.

VERS. 8.-- His eyes being opened. (RAB.) There is shown to him in the body what he suffered before in the soul. For as an eye is open without sight, so the law is useless without the faith of Christ.

(BEDA.) He could not with his eyes see well again, unless first blinded, and shutting out his own wisdom, which is troubled 448C, he committed himself to the faith.

VERS. 9.-- And he was. (RAB.) Here a form is given to penitents, that they begin from a space of three days, and in this three days' space also the degree of the catechumens is dedicated.

Three days. It appears that through the three days, being blinded, he was awaiting the light of grace, not growing sluggish in idleness, but, enlightened by God, he was searching into heavenly things. It is credible that even at that time he was fully taught the dispensation of the Gospel.

Not seeing. Because he did not believe that God, rising on the third day, had overcome death, he is instructed by his own example, who changed the darkness of the three days by the returning light.

He did not eat. (RAB.) This had befallen his soul, which had not had the inward food and drink. 448DBut from the fasting of the body and from thirst it is shown that the needy soul must be filled.

VERS. 10.-- Ananias. (ID.) It is a sign of familiarity that one is called by his own name.

VERS. 11.-- Into the village. (ID.) Villages are called lands lying next either to cities, or to one another within a city.

VERS. 15.-- Go, etc. He is not to be feared as a persecutor, but to be embraced as a brother: because the evils which he brought upon the saints, he is ready to endure.

VERS. 17.-- He entered in. The prophecy of Isaias was fulfilled, who says: The wolf shall dwell with the lamb, that is, Saul with Ananias.

VERS. 18.-- There fell, etc. (BEDA.) The body of the serpent 449Ais covered with scales, etc., as far as that he received light in the mind.

(RAB.) That the scales fall is a likeness of the inward sight renewed from the veil of the law.

VERS. 20.-- And immediately. He fulfils the word of the Lord who says: He that will come after me, let him deny himself (Luc. IX), etc.

VERS. 21.-- Is not this he. (RAB.) He gives an example of not blushing, where truth can change its own opinions.

VERS. 24.-- They watched. Not the Jews, but the citizens with their king Aretha, as he himself writes to the Galatians. For the Gentiles agreed with the Jews in the persecution of the Christians.

VERS. 26.-- And when he was come. Not as soon as he was baptized: he came to Jerusalem to the apostles, but 449B(as he writes to the Galatians) he first went away into Arabia, and thence returned to Damascus; then after three years he came to Jerusalem to see Peter, etc. Then, as Luke testifies, he came into the parts of Syria and Cilicia.

VERS. 27.-- Barnabas. (BEDA.) He was a Cyprian by race, a Levite, who is above recorded to have brought the price of a field to the feet of the apostles.

VERS. 29.-- With the Greeks. Jews are called Greeks, because they were born among the Greeks by the dispersion.

VERS. 30.-- To Caesarea. Caesarea Philippi is a city of Syria. Tharsus is a city of Cilicia.

VERS. 31.-- The Church. Such things, as has been said above, are often inserted at the end of narrations and at the beginning, to the praise of the apostles and of the ecclesiastical faith. And with the consolation. Consolation is given in labour, whence it is plain 449Cthat the Church was being tried.

VERS. 33.-- Named Aeneas. (RAB.) Aeneas betokens the human race lying sick, etc., as far as who is made weak by the pleasure of the flesh and by the delight of the world.

VERS. 34.-- Arise. Him whom he had cured of the palsy he commanded to arise and to make his bed, signifying that he who had received the faith in his heart should not only shake off torpor, but should also prepare works in which he may rest.

VERS. 35.-- Of Lydda and Sarona. They were two cities converted to God, for therefore miracles were wrought in them.

VERS. 36.-- Named Tabitha. (RAB.) Tabitha is rendered from the Hebrew into Latin Dorcas, or Damula, as is found in quisbusdam copies.

Dorcas. Allegorically: Dorcas is a doe or a roe: 449Dand it signifies the soul, lofty by the pursuit of virtue, but contemptible in the opinion of men. But Luke, in setting down the interpretation of the name, signifies that a mystery is contained in it. These animals, dwelling in the mountains, see sharply: whence from the sharpness of their sight they are called dorcades in Greek. So they who are lofty by the merits of their works aim at higher things, and watch circumspectly over themselves, they live simply with a discerning degree of work, they walk as it were with a cloven hoof, they ruminate the word of God by assiduous meditation.

VERS. 39.-- In an upper room. (RAB.) She is placed in a higher place, either because of the height of her merit, or because of the sublimity of the miracle which was to be wrought upon her.

VERS. 40.-- All being put forth. (ID.) This was done after the example of Christ 450Araising up the daughter of the chief ruler of the synagogue, to avoid elation, or lest the unworthy should see the sign. She opened. (BEDA.) Right is the order of those that rise again, that first she open the eyes of the mind, afterwards, the voice of Peter being known, she sit up, and receive again the lost light of her circumspection, that she may live unto the teaching of those who help. She opened her eyes. These are the three degrees of the raising up.

VERS. 41.-- And giving. Tabitha rises again at the touch of Peter's hand, because the soul lying sick in sins grows strong again by no better way than by the examples of the saints.

VERS. 1.-- A man, however, etc. (RAB.) By the merit of his perfection and by his manly work this centurion is called a man, according to 450Bthat: There was a man in the land of Hus, whose name was Job.

Cornelius. (BEDA.) Not by virtues did he attain to faith, but by faith he attained to virtues. (GREG.) Cornelius, whose alms before baptism, an angel bearing witness, etc., as far as but in faith he is made solid through works.

VERS. 2.-- Religious, etc. (RAB.) All these things by the good of nature, etc., as far as without manly seed, that is, he may be believed to have conceived by the teaching of a master.

VERS. 3.-- He saw. (RAB.) Not in the night, not in sleep, because the Gentiles, being of a gross understanding, etc., as far as as was shown before in the example of Peter and John.

An angel of God. (ID.) Not of Satan. In this the apostles are excused who preach to the Gentiles and baptize them: for how should that be grudged by a man which was made manifest 450Cby an angel? Entering in, to him perhaps, calling him, and occasion is given to Peter of excusing himself to the Jews who dispute against him and say: Why hast thou gone in to men having the foreskin (Act. XI), etc. [Vulg. Act. 11:3 Quare introisti ad viros praeputium habentes, "Wherefore hast thou gone in"] O Cornelius. A familiarity is shown, by which the name of a gentile man was inserted into the acquaintance of the angels.

VERS. 5.-- And now send men, etc. (RAB.) Here begins the recompense, that God may have mercy on thee who art merciful, because: Blessed are the merciful, for mercy, etc. (Matth. V). [Vulg. Matth. 5:7 quoniam ipsi misericordiam consequentur, "for they shall obtain mercy"; Migne's quotation lacks ipsi]

Joppa. (ID.) It is a city of the land of promise, nearest to the Tyrrhene sea, and situated upon its eastern shore, into which Jonas going down entered a ship, that he might flee.

VERS. 6.-- He lodgeth, etc. Peter lodges in 450Dthe house of Simon, that is, of one obeying, who is a tanner, because a teacher of the Church gladly dwells there where there are obedient men, who chastise the body and bring it into subjection, having crossed over the waves of the world by liberty of mind. For by the constancy of faith they despise the fleeting glory of the world and its terrors.

VERS. 7.-- And when he was departed. (RAB.) He does not say, when Cornelius had awaked, because to him the angel appeared neither through a dream, nor in ecstasy, but manifestly.

A soldier. (ID.) He does not say the household servants fearing the Lord, because at the beginning he had said that he was religious, fearing the Lord with all his house.

VERS. 9.-- As they drew near, etc. Another reading: As they appeared, namely those who were sent by Cornelius, 451Athat is, drawing near in such wise that they could be seen from the house of Simon the tanner.

(RAB.) For into the upper parts, another reading has into the upper room, and rightly: because the gentile people is as it were the upper house: but the Synagogue is beneath, because sunk in the world.

In saying into the upper parts, he signified that the Church, the coveting of earthly things being forsaken, would have her conversation in the heavens. At the sixth hour Peter, amid his prayers, hungers, and that for the salvation of the world, which Jesus came in the sixth age of the world to seek and to save: which he showed when at that same hour he sat over the well of Samaria.

VERS. 10.-- When he was hungry. (RAB.) By compassion he hungers for the salvation of the Gentiles, which is now shown in the vision, which was therefore seen at the sixth hour, because it was wrought in the sixth age 451Bof the world.

VERS. 11.-- Heaven opened. (BEDA.) The opening of heaven is the revelation of the heavenly mysteries shortly to come to the Gentiles. And in this he designates that the heavens are opened to the Gentiles, and that an entrance is to be laid wide.

A certain vessel. (ID.) That vessel designates the Church, etc., as far as through whom the Church, steeped in the heavenly gift, is exalted.

A linen cloth. For the Church, for the whiteness of holiness, and for the mortification of the flesh. From heaven to earth. (RAB.) Thence we are admonished that where our head is and our root (whose branches we are), thither the anchor of our hope may penetrate.

VERS. 12.-- All fourfooted beasts. All the Gentiles who shall believe and who are to be taken up into the unity of the body of Christ, that all may be made his members.

451CVERS. 13.-- Kill. Cleanse, mortify, and slay, that thou mayest make them victims to God, that being plucked out of oldness they may pass over into newness.

VERS. 14.-- I have eaten, etc. (BEDA.) He that eats food set outside transfers it into his own body. He commanded therefore that the Gentiles, set outside through unbelief, be grafted into the fellowship of the Church (which Peter signifies).

(ID.) Peter thought the Gentiles unclean, etc., as far as but may bind fast in the love of God alone.

VERS. 23.-- Bringing them in, etc. Here begins the communion of the apostles with the Gentiles, because he had gone down to them who had heard from the angel the command that they should enter into the house of a gentile, therefore he received the Gentiles into hospitality.

458ATo the Holy Spirit. (RAB.) And to us, with a human understanding indeed, but a sound one.

Than these necessary things. (RAB.) These are necessary things without which there is no salvation, but circumcision is not necessary: but the things which you would not have done to you, do not to another.

VERS. 9.-- Vir Macedo. Angelus illius gentis assimilatus viro Macedoni, proprietate linguae, vel forma speciali, vel judicio verbi.

Adjuva nos. (RAB.) Hoc ex persona hominis dictum, non enim angeli humano auxilio indigent. Vel hoc dicit angelus propter illos quibus praesidet, ut baptizentur et salvi fiant.

CHAPTER XVI.

VERS. 1.-- The son of a widow. (RAB.) Or, of a Jewess. But that she was by race a Jewess appears from this, that he says he was sprung of a gentile father. [Vulg. Act. 16:1 filius mulieris Judaeae fidelis, "the son of a Jewess that believed"; Migne's lemma reads Viduae filius, the reading the gloss then offers as an alternative]

458CVERS. 3.-- And he circumcised him. (ID.) He circumcised Timothy because of the scandal of the Jews, lest he should seem to condemn the law of Moses, but not to impose it upon the Gentiles as though not necessary. Titus he did not circumcise, lest the Gentiles should take from him occasion of being circumcised. And this is what he says: But because of false brethren unawares brought in, etc. [Vulg. Gal. 2:4 sed propter subintroductos falsos fratres; Migne prints fratres falsos]

VERS. 4.-- To keep the decrees, which were. (ID.) They did not preach what they themselves had done, but that which the apostles had decreed for the will of God. And it is an example of rightly teaching even what thou hast not done. In a figure of this deed the priests were clothed with linen breeches, as though about to sow a work, but not as though about to do it.

VERS. 5.-- And the churches indeed, etc. (ID.) This has been 458Dmore often expounded in the earlier chapters, when we said that by a sentence of this kind the earlier narrative is closed, and the beginning of the following is made.

VERS. 7.-- They attempted to go. (ID.) The apostles are permitted to will, but not to go, etc., as far as Luke in Bithynia, etc.

VERS. 9.-- A man of Macedonia. The angel of that nation likened to a man of Macedonia, by the property of the tongue, or by a special form, or by the judgment of the word.

Help us. (RAB.) This was said in the person of a man, for angels do not need human help. Or the angel says this on account of those over whom he presides, that they may be baptized and be saved.

Help us. He shows that the Macedonians, or the angels, are inspired by 459AGod, or that certain teachers had begun to be taught.

VERS. 11.-- Samothracia. Thracia, Neapolis and Philippi are cities.

VERS. 12.-- Certain days, conferring. In certain books is found: Conferring the word of the Lord on the sabbath day.

VERS. 14.-- A seller of purple. A queen clothed in royal purple, or a weaver of purple, or one selling purple, signifies the Church which sells purple while she pours out blood for Christ, that life may be purchased. Purple rightly designates blood by its colour and by its nature, because it is made from the blood of shellfish. Lydda compelled the apostle, with his companions, to enter her house: because the Church received with a devout heart the preaching rejected by the Jews.

459BVERS. 15.-- She constrained us. (RAB.) Here is given an example, or two examples are given: one of constraining guests, the other of entering in to the newly converted.

VERS. 17.-- Following Paul. (ID.) Although she had met them, the woman cries out not to their face, but behind them, keeping modesty.

VERS. 23.-- They cast them into prison. (ID.) By this the power of the deliverer is the greater. To the same purpose looks also that which follows: He put into the inner prison, and made their feet fast in the stocks. [Vulg. Act. 16:24 misit eos in interiorem carcerem, "he put them into the inner prison"; Migne's quotation lacks eos]

VERS. 25.-- And at midnight, etc. Prayer is to be made at midnight, and in dangers the diligence of prayer is to be kept.

VERS. 26.-- A great earthquake was made, etc. The devotion of them that pray and the power of prayer is expressed, 459Cbecause while they sing hymns in the prison their praise moves the earth: it shakes the foundations of the prison, opens the doors, looses the chains of the bound.

VERS. 29.-- He fell down, etc. (RAB.) On account of power and knowledge: power, because by the earthquake the doors were opened: knowledge, because Paul knew why he wished to kill himself: therefore before all he fell down at the feet of Paul and Silas.

VERS. 33.-- He washed their stripes. (BED.) A beautiful variety of things. Those whose stripes of wounding he washed, through them he lost the stripes of his crime. Immediately. (RAB.) The swiftness of obedience is betokened. The grace of the Holy Spirit knows no slow endeavours.

VERS. 34.-- And when he had brought them, he set a table before them, etc. (ID.) An excellent example of both: 459Dthe entering of the house, and the setting of the table.

CAPUT XVII.

VERS. 4.-- Et de colentibus gentilibusque. Id est, et de his qui gentilem ritum Judaismo mutaverunt, et de his qui gentiles permanserant multi ad Christum confluebant.

(RAB.) Prima causa malitiae est quod ii sint de vulgo; secunda quod et mali erant prae vulgo.

VERS. 6.-- Trahebant Jasonem. (BED.) in Graeco: Jasonem et quosdam alios fratres. Unde intelligendum est Jasonem fratrem et fidelem fuisse.

VERS. 7.-- Regem alium. (RAB.) Praeter Caesarem. Hoc contra decretum Caesaris erat, unde in Evangelio: 460AOmnis qui se regem facit, contradicit Caesari (Joan. XVIII). Apostoli vero contradicebant Christum regem esse Judaeorum, id est confitentium, unde et Judaei ipsum Christum accusabant.

VERS. 13.-- A Paulo. Qui erat dux verbi: et ideo postea solus dimissus est, ut iret de Macedonia ad mare.

VERS. 15.-- Profecti. (RAB.) Ab Athenis fratres illi qui duxerunt Paulum, profecti sunt. Quidam de Sila dictum putant et Timotheo, quod de Macedonia ad Paulum profecti sint.

VERS. 18.-- Epicurei. (ID.) Vocantur sic ab auctore suo, qui dicitur voluptatis assertor. Stoici Graece, Latine elementa; qui contendebant paria esse peccata, id est omne peccatum uniforme esse.

460BQuid vult seminiverbius. Seminiverbius est qui verbum seminat; vel seminiverbius, id est sermologus; quia semen est verbum Dei; unde: Si vobis seminavimus, etc.

(AUG.) Legimus Paulum apostolum dictum fuisse seminatorem verborum, quod dictum est ab irridentibus, sed non respuendum a credentibus; erat quippe seminator verborum, sed messor morum. Et nos, licet parvuli, nec illi comparandi in agro Dei (quod est cor vestrum) seminamus verbum Dei, exspectantes uberem frugem, vel fructum de moribus vestris.

Alii vero. (RAB.) A Judaeis audierunt vocabulum daemoniorum esse abominabile, et ideo huic abominationi comparant doctrinam Pauli; vel Judaei sunt 460Cqui hoc dicunt, id est sadducaei.

VERS. 19.-- Ad Areopagum. (RAB.) Nomen est curiae apud Athenas, dictum ab idolo Martis, quod apud eos Arios vocatur.

VERS. 22, 23.-- Viri Atheniensis, etc. Quod ignorantes colitis, hoc annuntio. Respondet ad hoc quod dixerant vel praemiserant: Nova quaedam infers, quasi dicat: Nova quae infero inter vetera vestra habetis. Hoc autem dicit ad persuadendum, ne quasi nova refutent sua verba.

Ignoto Deo. (RAB.) Id est, vero Deo, quem nemo novit ut est, etc., usque ad quod tacetur, vel simpliciter scriptum erat: Ignoto Deo.

VERS. 26.-- Ex uno omne genus hominum. In Graeco: ex uno sanguine, quia sanguis propaginem carnis designat, et per carnem homo intelligitur.

VERS. 28.-- In ipso enim vivimus. (AUG.) Quia id operatur in nobis, quod vivimus, movemur et sumus, etc., usque ad quia quid non ex ipso de quo dicitur: Ex ipso, et per ipsum, et in ipso sunt omnia (Rom. II)?

Sicut et quidam vestrorum poetarum. His qui prophetas non recipiebant, non Moysi, non Isaiae, vel alicujus prophetarum, sed auctorum suorum loquitur 461Atestimonium, versum Arati ponens, et de falsis eorum quibus contradicere non poterant, suam veritatem conservat et confirmat. Ipsius enim, etc. De Arato poeta hoc sumptum est testimonium, non de divina pagina, ne nova videatur inferre.

VERS. 29.-- Genus ergo, etc., non debemus. (RAB.) Homo in medio est, habens Deum superiorem, aurum ex argentum, cum caeteris hujusmodi inferiora, quae non debent ei comparari.

VERS. 30.-- Despiciens Deus. (ID.) Id est, ignoscens ut venialia, et tunc despiciens, id est, indigna judicans sua notitia.

VERS. 31.-- Fidem praebens omnibus. (ID.) Resurrectionis suae exemplo: vel statuit judicare, et post sic jungendum est, fidem praebens omnibus quod 461Bresurgent.

VERS. 34.-- Dionysius. (BED.) Hic est Dionysius qui, postea episcopus Corinthiorum ordinatus, bene rexit Ecclesiam, ad cujus utilitatem etiam multa reliquit volumina, cognomen a loco cui praeerat accipiens. Areopagus est Athenarum curia, nomen trahens a Marte, qui Graece Arios dicitur, pagus, villa.

CHAPTER XVII.

VERS. 4.-- And of the worshippers and of the Gentiles. That is, both of those who had exchanged the Gentile rite for Judaism, and of those who had remained Gentiles, many were flowing together to Christ.

(RAB.) The first cause of the malice is that these are of the common crowd; the second, that they were evil even beyond the crowd.

VERS. 6.-- They dragged Jason. (BED.) in the Greek: Jason and certain other brethren. Whence it is to be understood that Jason was a brother and faithful.

VERS. 7.-- Another king. (RAB.) Besides Caesar. This was against the decree of Caesar, whence in the Gospel: 460AEvery one that maketh himself a king, speaketh against Caesar (Joan. XVIII). But the apostles spoke against it, that Christ was king of the Jews, that is, of them that confess, whence the Jews also accused Christ himself.

VERS. 13.-- By Paul. Who was the leader of the word: and therefore afterward he alone was sent away, that he might go from Macedonia to the sea.

VERS. 15.-- They departed. (RAB.) From Athens those brethren who had conducted Paul departed. Some think it was said of Silas and Timothy, that they departed from Macedonia to Paul.

VERS. 18.-- The Epicureans. (ID.) They are so called from their author, who is said to be the champion of pleasure. Stoics in Greek, in Latin elements; who contended that sins were equal, that is, that every sin is uniform.

460BWhat meaneth this word-sower. A word-sower is he who sows the word; or a word-sower, that is, a sermologus; because the word of God is seed; whence: If we have sown unto you, etc.

(AUG.) We read that Paul the apostle was called a sower of words, which was said by them that mocked, but is not to be rejected by them that believe; for he was indeed a sower of words, but a reaper of morals. And we too, though little ones, and not to be compared with him, sow the word of God in God's field (which is your heart), looking for an abundant crop, or fruit, from your morals.

But others. (RAB.) From the Jews they had heard that the name of demons was abominable, and therefore they compare Paul's teaching to this abomination; or it is the Jews 460Cwho say this, that is, the Sadducees.

VERS. 19.-- To the Areopagus. (RAB.) It is the name of the court at Athens, called from the idol of Mars, which among them is called Arios.

VERS. 22, 23.-- Men of Athens, etc. What you worship in ignorance, that I preach. He answers to that which they had said or had put forward: Thou bringest in certain new things, as if he said: The new things which I bring in you have among your old ones. And this he says for persuasion, lest they refute his words as new.

To an unknown God. (RAB.) That is, to the true God, whom no one knows as he is, etc., as far as that which is passed over in silence, or it was simply written: To an unknown God.

VERS. 24.-- God, who made the world. Note the order of this disputation, because first he teaches that one God is the author 460Dof the world, of all things, in whom we live, and are moved, and are, whose offspring also we are: so that not only for light and life, but also for the kinship of race, he may be shown to be one who ought to be loved.

VERS. 26.-- Of one all the race of men. In the Greek: of one blood, because blood designates the propagation of the flesh, and by the flesh man is understood.

VERS. 28.-- For in him we live. (AUG.) Because he works that in us, which is that we live, are moved, and are, etc., as far as because what is not of him, of whom it is said: Of him, and by him, and in him are all things (Rom. II)?

As also certain of your own poets. To those who did not receive the prophets he speaks the 461Atestimony, not of Moses, not of Isaias, or of any of the prophets, but of their own authors, setting down a verse of Aratus, and out of their own false things, which they could not contradict, he preserves and confirms his own truth. For his, etc. This testimony was taken from the poet Aratus, not from the divine page, lest he should seem to bring in new things.

VERS. 29.-- Being therefore, etc., we ought not. (RAB.) Man is in the middle, having God as higher, gold from silver, with the rest of this kind as lower, which ought not to be compared to him.

VERS. 30.-- God despising. (ID.) That is, pardoning them as venial, and then despising, that is, judging them unworthy of his notice.

VERS. 31.-- Giving faith to all. (ID.) By the example of his own resurrection: or, he has appointed to judge, and afterward it is to be joined thus, giving faith to all that 461Bthey shall rise again.

VERS. 34.-- Dionysius. (BED.) This is that Dionysius who, being afterward ordained bishop of the Corinthians, ruled the Church well, for whose profit also he left many volumes, taking his surname from the place over which he presided. The Areopagus is the court of Athens, drawing its name from Mars, who in Greek is called Arios, pagus, a village.

VERS. 2.-- Eo quod praecepisset Claudius discedere omnes. (BED.) Hoc Josephus nono anno Claudii factum narrat. Suetonius dicit quod Claudius Judaeos Roma expulit.

VERS. 3.-- Sceno factoriae artis. (ID.) Quasi exsules et peregrini tentoria sibi quibus in via utantur aedificant, 461Cetc., usque ad et a ventis insidiarum verbo et opere defendit.

CHAPTER XVIII.

VERS. 2.-- Because Claudius had commanded all to depart. (BED.) Josephus relates that this was done in the ninth year of Claudius. Suetonius says that Claudius expelled the Jews from Rome.

VERS. 3.-- Of the tentmaking craft. (ID.) As exiles and pilgrims build themselves tents which they may use on the way, 461Cetc., as far as and by word and deed protects from the winds of snares.

VERS. 6.-- Your blood. (CHRYS.) This he does, that he may terrify not by word alone, and he discourses the more vehemently, inasmuch as many had been persuaded.

VERS. 7.-- Of Titus Justus, one that worshipped God. Titus Justus was either the man's own name, or he was so surnamed by the merit of his justice. This Titus was not the disciple of Paul to whom he himself wrote the Epistle, and whom he ordained bishop of Crete; with whom Barnabas also, going up to Jerusalem, conferred the Gospel with the apostles, inquiring in their council whether he preached rightly that the Gentiles were saved through the baptism of faith without circumcision, not because he himself doubted of this, but that he might confirm the minds of the doubting by the authority of the apostles.

461DVERS. 15.-- But if they be questions of a word. Now indeed declared and narrated, that is, the Gospel: and of names (Jesus) and (God) or (God the Father). In the Greek: All the Greeks apprehending Sosthenes, etc.

VERS. 18.-- Who had shorn himself. (RAB.) Who shore their own head is uncertain, yet they were Paul or Aquila; whether from fear of the Jews, or from eagerness of converting them? Yet this is said ambiguously, but according to Jerome it is to be understood of Paul.

VERS. 22.-- Caesarea. (ID.) Caesarea is the metropolis of Cappadocia. For he had not yet come into Syria of Phoenicia.

VERS. 27.-- He helped much. Another translation: 462AHe profited much them that believed, by grace, this is what the Apostle writes: I have planted, Apollo watered (I Cor. III), etc.

CAPUT XIX.

VERS. 4.-- Joannes baptizavit. (BED.) Non peccata Joannes suo baptismate remisit, sed poenitentiam docuit. Nam sicut circumcisio in patriarchis signaculum fidei quam habebant fuit. ita et hoc lavacrum populus poenitens quasi signaculum suae devotionis accepit: quod Christi baptisma, in quo fit remissio, figurabat.

(RAB.) Non est baptisma, quod non datur in nomine Trinitatis. Baptizati a Juda non rebaptizantur, quia in fide Trinitatis; sed baptizati a Joanne, quia 462BJoannes in aqua tantum at Christus in Spiritu sancto baptizat. Cum ergo bonus vel malus minister baptizat, si plena est Trinitatis fides, Dominus baptizat; in aqua vero sola a Joanne baptizati, a Paulo sunt baptizati.

(BED.) Quaestio est, utrum ille qui per ignorantiam forte a non baptizato, sed tamen rectae fidei baptizatus est debeat rebaptizari? Quam hoc capitulo expositam reor. Quid enim differt, an tunc ante initium baptismi Christi, an nunc a quopiam sine susceptione ejusdem quisquam baptizaretur? cum quos Joannes in fide et nomine Christi venturi baptizavit, iterum baptizandos praedicavit, dicens: Ego baptizo in aqua, ille baptizabit in Spiritu sancto.

VERS. 7.-- Erant autem omnes. (RAB.) Judicia 462CDei abyssus multa. Ecce Asia apostolorum visitatione indigna, nunc apostolico numero sacrata et prophetiae munere est sublimata. Et notandum quod Spiritus sanctus hic in duodecim discipulos, et supra in centum et viginti (qui numerus perficitur duodenario decies ducto) adventus sui signa monstravit illud in Jerusalem, hoc in Epheso, quae est civitas Graecorum. In quo ostenditur illos tantum (sive de Judaeis, sive de gentibus) spiritum replere, qui sunt in catholica unitate.

VERS. 9.-- Quotidie disputans in schola. (BED.) Ab hora quinta usque ad horam nonam et decimam: sicut traditur a quibusdam: quia horas operi quinque, alias quinque doctrinae, duas dabat cibo et orationi, quod nulla tamen auctoritate firmatur. In 462Dschola tyranni. (RAB.) Tyranni nomen positum est pro dignitate, vel est proprium nomen, et interpretatur continens eos, vel confirmans.

VERS. 10.-- Per biennium. Hoc tempore, Paulus in Epheso scripsit primam Epistolam ad Corinthios.

VERS. 12.-- Semicinctia (RAB.) Vestes ex uno latere pendentes, vel zonae sive vestes nocturnae, vel genus sudarii, quo Hebraei utuntur in capite.

VERS. 13.-- Exorcistis. Excogitavit Salomon, et docuit suam gentem modos exorcismi, id est conjurationis quibus immundi spiritus expulsi non audebant ad homines reverti. Fit autem hoc per reprobos ad condemnationem ipsorum et utilitatem eorum qui vident et audiunt: quia licet illos despiciant 463Aqui signa faciunt, tamen Deum honorant, ad cujus invocationem fit.

CHAPTER XXVII.

VERS. 1.-- And when it was determined that he should sail, etc. In the Greek: When it was determined that we should sail into Italy, they delivered Paul and certain others in bonds to a centurion.

VERS. 2.-- Adrumetine. (RAB.) That is, sailing to Rome, according to some, but it is better derived from the name of the African city which is called Adrumetis.

467DVERS. 5.-- We came to Lystra. (BED.) For Lystra, in the Greek Smyrna is written. But Jerome puts Myrrha.

VERS. 9.-- The fast. (RAB.) A great hunger on the voyage, making the sailors weaker. Others say that it was then the forty days' fast before the birth of the Lord, which is not probable. It is better to be thought the seventh fast, in which Godolias was slain. Or the day of the tenth fast in January, when the captives in Babylon learned that the temple had been destroyed.

VERS. 12.-- A haven, etc. (ID.) That is called a haven of Crete which is in Phoenice over against Crete: but they were seeking the haven of Crete, because they were sailing to Crete.

468AToward Africus, etc. Africus stands between the southern and the western quarter, inclining more to the western; Corus between the northern and the western, more bent toward the west; and the province of Phoenice had Crete to the west.

VERS. 13.-- Asson. (RAB.) It is the name of a place so called, because thence the ships were loosed as they went forward into the sea.

VERS. 14.-- Typhonic. Another translation: a tempestuous wind.

Euroaquilo. (RAB.) That is, Boreas, a wind between Aquilo and the east; this wind was contrary to the ship that was to sail into Phoenice.

VERS. 17.-- Syrtis. (ID.) A narrowness or tribulation is called Syrtis.

468BVERS. 21.-- It behoved indeed, etc. (ID.) As if he should say: You did not hear God, in that Paul above was exhorting them; or, it behoved, me being heard, that is, that you should hear me.

VERS. 26.-- And we must come unto a certain island. (ID.) As if he should say: The prophecy of the truth concerning the island is for a sign, that, when they shall have come to it, they may be certain of their salvation.

VERS. 27.-- In Adria. It is a region to which they were sailing; or, the name of a certain strait in the Tyrrhene sea, or rather it seems to be the name of that land from which the Adriatic gulf is called. Otherwise, Adria is taken in the plural for rocks, and therefore the sailors thought that a region appeared. In the book of Hebrew names, 468CAdria is said to be containing evils, or a place of evils.

VERS. 34.-- To take food. (BED.) No one escapes the tempests of the world, save he who is fed with the bread of life, and who in the night of tribulations leans upon wisdom, fortitude, temperance and justice, and, by the help of the Lord flashing forth, attains the haven of salvation, so that, being disencumbered of the world, he may seek the flame of love by which the inward parts are warmed.

VERS. 37.-- We were all souls. (RAB.) He does not say men, because perhaps there were women also; or, because of the mingling of Christians, soldiers and sailors, it is so said.

VERS. 41.-- Bithalassum. (BED.) [Vulg. Act. 27:41 dithalassum, "having two seas"] The Greeks call the sea Thalassa, etc., as far as and the sea is called by the Greeks Thelasson.
