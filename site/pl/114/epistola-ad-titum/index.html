One book of the Glossa ordinaria — the whole gloss, book by book.

Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

ARGUMENTUM.

637D

Titum commonefacit et instruit de constitutione presbyterii et de spirituali conversatione, et haereticis vitandis, qui in traditionibus Judaicis credunt, scribens ei a Nicopoli.

THE ARGUMENT.

637D

He admonishes Titus and instructs him concerning the constitution of the presbytery and concerning spiritual manner of life, and concerning heretics to be shunned, who believe in Jewish traditions, writing to him from Nicopolis.

CAPUT PRIMUM.

VERS. 1.-- Paulus servus, etc. Conditione non tamen peccati misera servitute. sed Dei. Quidam nobilitate, qualiter Moyses et David servi sunt appellati, et Maria ancilla. Secundum fidem electi. Id 638Dest quam tenent vel qua salvantur electi Dei, et ideo per ea magis laborandum.

VERS. 2.-- In spem vitae aeternae. Haec res. hic fructus apostolici officii. Per hoc commendat officium apostolicum non tepidius agendum. Ante tempora, etc. Non recens promissio, sed ante tempora omnium saeculorum.

VERS. 3.-- Manifestavit. Congruis hoc factum est temporibus, scilicet Verbum, id est Filius: Haec est vita aeterna.

639AVERS. 4.-- Filio secundum communem fidem. Id est catholicam non privatam alicujus. Catholicon Graece, commune vel universale dicitur Latine.

Hujus rei gratia. Primo dicit quid agere debeat et quales ordinare presbyteros, incipiens ita, hujus rei gratia. Dum haec recolit, sicut ei disposuerat monet exsequi.

VERS. 5.-- Presbyteros. Pluraliter dicit presbyteros, non autem singulariter, ut scilicet pastoralis curae onus facilius sustineatur, per multos divisum.

VERS. 6.-- Sine crimine. Si dixisset, sine peccato, nullus in Ecclesia recte posset ordinari minister. Multi enim baptizati fideles sunt sine crimine, sine peccato vero in hac vita neminem dixerim; non quia peccati aliquid remaneat quod in baptismate 639Bnon dimittatur, sed quia in nobis in hujus vitae infirmitate manentibus quotidie fieri non quiescunt, quae fideliter orantibus quotidie remittantur. Quamlibet valde justum discutias in hac vita, non est tamen sine peccato.

Unius uxoris virum. (HIERON. Epist. ad Oceanum. ) Post baptismum, etc., usque ad quia est ab uno Deo apostatare, et in alterius superstitionem ire.

Filios habens fideles. Id est, nec saltem de luxuria accusatos, vel sibi non subditos. Alioquin nec spes in illo est corrigendi alios, nec frontem habet ad alios talium cohabitator.

VERS. 7.-- Oportet enim. Talis debet constitui: necesse est enim ad tractationem officii eum esse sine crimine, id est irreprehensibilem episcopum. 639CHic aperte ostendit presbyterorum nomine episcopos supra fuisse designatos.

VERS. 9.-- Eos qui contradicunt, arguere. Contradicentes non uno modo intelligendi sunt, paucissimi quippe nobis contradicunt loquendo, sed multi male vivendo. Quis enim audet apertissime loquendo contradicere veritati. Non contradicunt multi lingua, sed vita: quos redarguere bonum opus est, grandis sarcina, clivus arduus.

VERS. 12.-- Dixit quidam ex illis. (AUG., lib. II contra adversarium leg. et prophet.) Iste fuit Epimenides Cretensis, in cujus libris hoc invenitur, etc., usque ad ad ministerium domus Dei assumendum est.

VERS. 14.-- Mandatis. Quia mandata legis post manifestam veritatem, jam hominum, non Dei mandata 639Dsunt.

VERS. 15.-- Omnia munda mundis. Hoc ipsi negant ideo, quia in lege quaedam Moyses prohibuit, et ab immundis munda discrevit. Hoc autem non secundum naturas quas Deus creavit, intelligi voluit, sed secundum significationes: Omnia ergo munda sunt mundis secundum naturam; unde in Genesi: Vidit Deus cuncta quae fecerat, et erant valde bona. Secundum significationes quaedam immunda sunt Judaeis: nec nobis omnia apta sunt, vel propter salutem corporum, vel propter consuetudinem humanae societatis.

VERS. 16.-- Factis autem. Si negatio non tantum lingua fit, sed etiam factis, certe multos invenimus 640AAntichristos, qui ore confitentur Christum, et moribus dissentiunt ab eo. Quisquis factis negat Christum Antichristus est. Tales sunt omnes mali catholici, qui non verbis, sed factis negant. Nolite ergo esse tanquam de fide securi, adjungite fidei rectae vitam rectam, ut Christum confiteamini, et verbis vera docendo, et factis bene vivendo. Nam si confitemini verbis et non factis, fides tantum meorum proprie fides est daemoniorum.

CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- Paul a servant, etc. By condition, yet not by the wretched servitude of sin. but of God. Certain men by nobility, in the manner in which Moses and David were called servants, and Mary a handmaid. According to the faith of the elect. [Vulg. electorum Dei, "of the elect of God"] That 638Dis, the faith which the elect of God hold, or by which they are saved, and therefore one must labour the more through these things.

VERS. 2.-- Unto the hope of life everlasting. This is the matter. this the fruit of the apostolic office. By this he commends the apostolic office, as not to be carried on the more lukewarmly. Before the times, etc. Not a recent promise, but before the times of all the ages.

VERS. 3.-- He hath manifested. This was done at fitting times, namely the Word, that is, the Son: This is life everlasting.

639AVERS. 4.-- To a son according to the common faith. That is, catholic, not the private faith of anyone. Catholicon in Greek is called in Latin common or universal.

For this cause. First he says what he ought to do and what sort of presbyters he ought to ordain, beginning thus, for this cause. While he recalls these things, he admonishes him to carry them out as he had appointed for him.

VERS. 5.-- Presbyters. He says presbyters in the plural, and not in the singular, so that, namely, the burden of pastoral care may be more easily borne, being divided among many.

VERS. 6.-- Without crime. If he had said, without sin, no one in the Church could rightly be ordained a minister. For many baptized faithful are without crime, but without sin in this life I would call no one; not because anything of sin remains which is not remitted in baptism, 639Bbut because in us, who remain in the infirmity of this life, they do not cease to be committed daily, which are daily remitted to those who pray faithfully. However exceedingly just a man thou mayest examine in this life, yet he is not without sin.

The husband of one wife. (HIERON. Epist. ad Oceanum. ) After baptism, etc., as far as because it is to apostatize from the one God, and to go over into the superstition of another.

Having faithful children. That is, not so much as accused of wantonness, or not subject to him. Otherwise there is neither hope in him of correcting others, nor has one who dwells with such persons the face to confront others.

VERS. 7.-- For it behoveth. Such a man ought to be appointed: for it is necessary, for the handling of the office, that he be without crime, that is, a blameless bishop. 639CHere he plainly shows that under the name of presbyters bishops were designated above.

VERS. 9.-- To reprove those who contradict. Those who contradict are not to be understood in one manner only, for very few contradict us by speaking, but many by living ill. For who dares, by speaking most openly, to contradict the truth. Many contradict not with the tongue, but with the life: to confute whom is a good work, a great burden, a steep slope.

VERS. 12.-- A certain one of them said. (AUG., lib. II contra adversarium leg. et prophet.) This was Epimenides the Cretan, in whose books this is found, etc., as far as it is to be taken up unto the ministry of the house of God.

VERS. 14.-- Commandments. Because the commandments of the law, after the truth has been made manifest, are now the commandments of men, not of God 639D.

VERS. 15.-- All things are clean to the clean. This they deny for this reason, that in the law Moses forbade certain things, and separated clean things from unclean. But he willed this to be understood not according to the natures which God created, but according to significations: all things therefore are clean to the clean according to nature; whence in Genesis: God saw all the things that he had made, and they were very good. According to significations certain things are unclean to the Jews: nor are all things fitting for us, either for the health of bodies, or for the custom of human society.

VERS. 16.-- But by deeds. If denial is made not only with the tongue, but also by deeds, certainly we find many 640AAntichrists, who confess Christ with the mouth, and in their manners dissent from him. Whosoever denies Christ by deeds is an Antichrist. Such are all evil Catholics, who deny not with words, but by deeds. Do not therefore be as though secure concerning the faith; join to right faith a right life, that you may confess Christ, both by teaching true things in words, and by living well in deeds. For if you confess with words and not with deeds, the faith only of my own is properly the faith of devils.

CAPUT II.

VERS. 1.-- Tu autem loquere quae decent sanam doctrinam. Haec sunt quae ad bonos mores pertinent.

VERS. 2.-- Senes ut sobrii sint. (CHRYS.) Habet senectus vitia quaedam propria, et morbos quibus juventus caret. Et ipsa quoque senectus morbus est, 640Binest illi tamen praeterea segnities quaedam ac tarditas, oblivio profunda, obtusio sensuum et iracundia. Sunt enim plurima quae in hujusmodi aetate vix fieri patiantur.

VERS. 3.-- In habitu sancto. Ut scilicet earum incessus, motus, vultus, sermo praeferant sanctitatem. Non vino multo. Solent enim esse quaedam, quae corporis frigescente luxuria, vino se pro libidine dederunt.

VERS. 4.-- Ut viros suos. Quos naturali affectu naturaliter diligunt, ament etiam pudica dilectione, ut scilicet cum pudore viris debitum potius reddant, quam exigant.

VERS. 5.-- Ut non blasphemetur. Hoc ad senes et ad omnia superiora potest referri, quasi dicat: Hortare senes, et anus, et adolescentulas, ut ita se habeant 640Cut dixi, quod ideo facere debent ut non blasphemetur, etc.

VERS. 7.-- In omnibus, etc. Omnis enim qui male vivit in conspectu eorum quibus praepositus est, quantum in ipso est occidit eos; etiamsi illi vivunt, de misericordia Dei vivunt.

VERS. 10.-- Ut doctrinam Salvatoris. Hoc refertur ad omnia praedicta, scilicet ad senes, et anus, et servos, quasi dicat: Hortare omnes praedictos, ut ita se habeant sicut praescriptum est, ut ita ornent in actibus suis doctrinam Salvatoris, etc. Ornent. Ornamentum doctoris est honesta vita discipuli.

VERS. 11.-- Apparuit enim. Quasi dicat: Omnes monendi sunt, quia omnibus natus est Christus, nullum exclusit. Vel, Gratia, id est Christus qui est 640Dgratia Dei. Vel, gratia Dei et Salvatoris, etc., id est Deus et Salvator noster, scilicet Dei Filius, etsi invisibilis sit in forma Dei, tamen per gratiam apparuit in forma servi.

VERS. 13.-- Et adventum gloriae magni Dei, etc. Qui non in infirmitate ut prius, sed in gloria et potestate veniet praemia daturus secundum merita, qui in primo adventu adeo humilis fuit, quod non solum erudivit, sed etiam dedit semetipsum. Sectatorem bonorum operum, etc. Quia ad hoc redemit nos Christus, ut puram vitam sectantes et repleti operibus bonis, Dei haeredes esse possimus.

VERS. 15.-- Argue cum omni, etc. Ut non aspere accipiatur, cum pro salute fieri scitur.

CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1.-- But speak thou the things which become sound doctrine. These are the things which pertain to good morals.

VERS. 2.-- That the aged men be sober. (CHRYS.) Old age has certain vices of its own, and diseases which youth lacks. And old age itself also is a disease; 640Byet there is besides in it a certain sluggishness and slowness, deep forgetfulness, dullness of the senses, and irascibility. For there are very many things which at an age of this kind scarcely suffer themselves to be done.

VERS. 3.-- In holy habit. So that, namely, their gait, their movement, their countenance, their speech may display holiness. Not in much wine. For there are wont to be certain women who, the wantonness of the body growing cold, have given themselves to wine in place of lust.

VERS. 4.-- That they may love their own husbands. Whom they love naturally with a natural affection, let them love also with a chaste love, so that, namely, with modesty they may rather render the debt to their husbands than exact it.

VERS. 5.-- That the word of God be not blasphemed. This can be referred to the aged men and to all the foregoing, as if he should say: Exhort the aged men, and the aged women, and the young women, that they so conduct themselves 640Cas I have said, which they ought therefore to do that it be not blasphemed, etc.

VERS. 7.-- In all things, etc. For everyone who lives ill in the sight of those over whom he is set, as far as in him lies, kills them; and even if they live, they live of the mercy of God.

VERS. 10.-- That they may adorn the doctrine of the Saviour. This is referred to all the aforesaid, namely to the aged men, and the aged women, and the servants, as if he should say: Exhort all the aforesaid, that they so conduct themselves as has been prescribed, that so they may adorn in their acts the doctrine of the Saviour, etc. Let them adorn. The ornament of the teacher is the honourable life of the disciple.

VERS. 11.-- For there hath appeared. As if he should say: All are to be admonished, because Christ was born for all; he excluded no one. Or, Grace, that is, Christ who is 640Dthe grace of God. Or, the grace of God and of the Saviour, [Vulg. gratia Dei Salvatoris nostri, without et] etc., that is, God and our Saviour, namely the Son of God, who, although he be invisible in the form of God, yet by grace hath appeared in the form of a servant.

VERS. 13.-- And the coming of the glory of the great God, etc. Who will come not in infirmity as before, but in glory and power, about to give rewards according to merits: who in his first coming was so humble that he not only instructed, but even gave himself. A pursuer of good works, etc. Because for this Christ redeemed us, that, pursuing a pure life and filled with good works, we may be able to be heirs of God.

VERS. 15.-- Reprove with all, etc. That it be not taken harshly, since it is known to be done for the sake of salvation.

CAPUT III.

641A

VERS. 1.-- Ad omne opus, etc. Quasi dicat: Admone, ut ad malum tardi sint, sed ad bonum parati, scientes quod singulis persolvi oporteat, quia singulis paratur.

VERS. 3.-- Eramus enim et nos aliquando. Etsi philosophi essent quidam nostrum, vera tamen sapientia carebant.

VERS. 4.-- Benignitas, etc. Per quam dona nobis spiritualia abunde tribuit.

VERS. 10.-- Haereticum hominem. Haereticus est qui per verba legis legem impugnat: proprium enim sensum astruit ex verbis legis, ut pravitatem mentis 641Bsuae legis auctoritate confirmet, qui vitandi sunt: 642Aquia frequentius correcti exercitatiores essent ad malum.

VERS. 11.-- Proprio judicio. Se damnat qui errorem laudat, et veritatem vituperat.

VERS. 13.-- Zenam legisperitum. Hoc dicit, non quia peritior sit Apollo, sed quia hujus professionis erat in Synagoga. Apollo. Episcopus fuit Corinthiorum, qui propter seditionem concitatam adversum se a pseudo-apostolis discesserat cum Zena socio suo ad Titum Cretensem episcopum. Sed Corinthiis jam sedatis per epistolas Pauli mandat Tito, ut priusquam veniat Nicopolim, praemittat eos ita solite, ut nihil in viatico illis desit. Ut nihil, etc. Ideo jubet sumptus illis necessarios dari, quia dignum erat, ut qui spiritualia 642Btradebant, non illis deessent carnalia.

CHAPTER III.

641A

VERS. 1.-- Unto every work, etc. As if he should say: Admonish, that they be slow to evil, but ready to good, knowing that it must be paid out to each, because it is prepared for each.

VERS. 3.-- For we also were once. Even if certain of us were philosophers, yet they lacked true wisdom.

VERS. 4.-- Benignity, etc. Through which he bestowed spiritual gifts upon us abundantly.

VERS. 10.-- A heretical man. A heretic is he who through the words of the law assails the law: for he builds up his own meaning out of the words of the law, that he may confirm the depravity of his mind 641Bby the authority of the law; who are to be avoided: 642Abecause, being the more frequently corrected, they would be the more practised for evil.

VERS. 11.-- By his own judgment. He condemns himself who praises error and blames truth.

VERS. 13.-- Zenas the lawyer. This he says, not because he is more skilled than Apollo, but because he was of this profession in the Synagogue. Apollo. He was bishop of the Corinthians, who, on account of the sedition stirred up against him by the false apostles, had departed with Zenas his companion to Titus the bishop of Crete. But the Corinthians being now quieted through the epistles of Paul, he charges Titus that, before he come to Nicopolis, he send them on ahead so customarily that nothing be lacking to them in provision for the journey. That nothing, etc. Therefore he commands that the necessary expenses be given them, because it was fitting that to those who imparted spiritual things 642Bcarnal things should not be lacking.

On this text

Cruces for this work — where the plate is defective or the reading uncertain →

The Glossa ordinaria is the standard medieval commentary on the Bible — the gloss a reader of Scripture met in the margins of the page, drawn from the Fathers and built up in the schools of the twelfth century. What Migne prints, and what is translated here, is an abridged nineteenth-century recension of it: the marginal glosses only, without the interlinear layer, and 58 of the roughly 73 books — Ezekiel, Daniel, the minor prophets and the Maccabees are absent from it altogether. Migne places the whole under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription scholarship has long since given up; the attributions inside the text — (RAB.) for Rabanus, (ISID.) for Isidore, (CHRYS.) for Chrysostom — are his source's and are not always reliable either. They are printed as he printed them. The gloss on Titus works through the qualifications of the presbyters Titus is to appoint in every city, the Cretan proverb Paul quotes back at the Cretans, and the household instructions of the second chapter. Where Migne's text uses presbyter and episcopus of what looks like one office, both are rendered as they stand rather than resolved. An English of the Rusch text, a different recension, exists for some books of the Bible; none has been found for Titus. The gilt column marks carry Migne's own numbering: migne.app/pl/114/637d resolves to the opening.
