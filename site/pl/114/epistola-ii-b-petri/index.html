EPISTOLA II B. PETRI

One book of the Glossa ordinaria — the whole gloss, book by book.

PL 114, coll. 689–694  ·  Latin from the Migne printing  ·  New English translation  ·  column numbers follow the original plates, not the Garnier reprint

Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

ARGUMENTUM.

  • 689A

Simon Petrus per fidem huic mundo sapientes mortuos esse declarat, eisdemque pietatis quanta sit magnitudo luce ipsa clarius manifestat.

THE ARGUMENT.

  • 689A

Simon Peter declares that the wise are dead to this world through faith, and to those same men he makes manifest, more clearly than the light itself, how great is the greatness of piety.

CAPUT PRIMUM.

VERS. 1.-- Simon. (BEDA.) Istam eisdem quibus et primam scribit epistolam, etc., usque ad non cesserunt, haereticis non cedant.

Servus et Apostolus. Commendatur persona scribentis.

Coaequalem. (BEDA.) Coaequales sibi cognoscit, non pro circumcisione, sed quia eadem quam ipsi acceperant Christi gratiam et fidem et hanc per opera exercebant. In justitia. Hanc justitiam non humana 689Bprudentia reperit, nec legis institutio docuit, sed noster Salvator in Evangelio ostendit: Nisi abundaverit justitia vestra plus quam scribarum et Pharisaeorum, non intrabitis in regnum coelorum (Matth. V).

VERS. 2.-- Gratia vobis. (BEDA.) In prima Epistola scripsit multiplicetur; in hac, adimpleatur: quia illam incipientibus, hanc perfectioribus scribit. Multiplicatur gratia bene proficientibus in hac vita per fidem, adimplebitur pervenientibus in alia vita per speciem. Unde addit, in cognitione Dei, id est ut noscatis unum verum Deum, et quem misit Jesum Christum.

VERS. 3.-- Quomodo omnia. (ID.) Ita adimpleatur gratia Christi, etc., usque ad sed infirmos et inglorios sua virtute reparavit et gloria.

689CVERS. 4.-- Maxima et pretiosa nobis promissa donavit. Id est vitam aeternam quam per ejus cognitionem consequamur. Vel quae promisit nobis, de conversione aliorum per nos, vel de adventu Spiritus sancti, vel de potentia miraculorum donavit.

VERS. 5.-- Ministrate in fide. (BEDA.) Non solum fugiatis concupiscentiam mundi, etc., usque ad ne sitis molles et dissoluti.

VERS. 6.-- In scientia autem abstinentiam. (ID.) Cum bona facere didiceritis, spe vitae aeternae mox a malis abstinete, et in cogitatione et in opere, ne in vacuum scientia coelestium cedat, si se ab illecebris saeculi aliquis non coerceat.

VERS. 7.-- In pietate autem. (ID.) Non alterius 689Drei intuitu pietatis opera adversariis vestris impendatis, nisi fraternae dilectionis, ut pro eis oretis ut convertantur, qui doctrina vestra vel increpationibus nolunt converti.

VERS. 8.-- Haec enim si vobiscum, etc. Ideo haec tenete et aliis ministrate, quia haec omnia si adsint vobis in affectu cordis, et etiam superent, id est adversum vitiorum bella praevaluerint, non vos vacuos virtutibus constituent in hoc saeculo, vel sine fructu in futuro.

690AVERS. 9.-- Caecus est et manu tentans. (BEDA.) Oculus scientiam, manus operationem designat, etc., usque ad praescire nequibat, incidit.

Oblivionem accipiens. Quando homo de peccato in peccatum cadit, videtur oblitus pacti quod in baptismo cum Deo iniit.

VERS. 10.-- Non peccabitis aliquando. Id est, ad vitam aeternam attingetis, ubi amplius non peccabitis. Vel criminalia vitabitis, quae qui egerit, non habebit haereditatem in regno Christi, a quibus immunis juvante Domino permanet, qui supradictis virtutibus se mancipaverit. Sine venialibus vero nullus esse potest.

VERS. 11.-- Sic enim. (BEDA.) Ezechiel loquens de aedificio in monte constituto, etc., usque ad, et videbitur 690BDeus deorum in Sion (Psal. LXXXIII).

VERS. 12.-- Vos incipiam semper commonere de his. Ut sit vobis introitus patens ad Sancta sanctorum, iterum et iterum monebo vos, et semper ero in incoeptione, quia nunquam erit finis monitionis. Quod ergo non est mihi pigrum saepius iterare, vobis non sit onerosum saepius audire.

Et quidem scientes. (BEDA.) Quare vult monere illos qui sciunt, et confirmati sunt? Ideo scilicet ut scientiam quam habent, operibus exerceant, et veritatem cujus praesentia confirmati sunt, fixa mente custodiant, ne quando per magistros erroris excidant a simplicitate et castitate fidei.

VERS. 13.-- Justum autem, etc. Ideo monebo, quia justum arbitror, per hoc quod Dominus benigne vocavit 690Cme, et confirmavit, et alios confirmare monuit, dicens: Confirma fratres tuos, suscitare vos quasi dormientes ad haec praedicta, semper exsistens in commonendo sollicitus.

VERS. 14.-- Depositio tabernaculi mei. (BEDA.) Pulchre non mortem, sed depositionem vocat, quia sic est famulis Dei carnis retinacula eruere, sicut viatoribus confecto itinere domum propriam pro habitatione tabernaculi adire, sicut positis in expeditione prostrato hoste ad patriam redire.

VERS. 15.-- Dabo autem operam. Quia cito sum transiturus, ideo interim dum vivo, dabo operam ut vos frequenter, scilicet quoties instabunt pseudo et volent vos abstrahere, habeatis haec in memoria 690Dpost obitum meum, quando me praesentem non habebitis doctorem, non quolibet modo habere, sed ita ut horum memoriam faciatis opere adimplendo, et alios docendo.

Post obitum, etc. Obitus dicitur sanctorum quando exeunt obviam sponso.

VERS. 16.-- Non enim indoctas fabulas, etc. (BEDA.) Hic et paganos tangit et haereticos. Pagani, quidquid eos delectabat, deos appellare non timuerunt. Haeretici, acceptis Dei veri mysteriis, de caetero non divinis 691AScripturis attendere, sed potius ad sensum erroneum has male interpretando transferre studuerunt.

Sed speculatores facti, etc. Transfiguratio Domini et claritas ejus signum erat resurrectionis suae et nostrae. Per Eliam qui cum eo apparuit, intelligitur resurrectio eorum qui fuerunt ante legem. Per Moysen, eorum qui sub lege; per Petrum, et Jacobum, et Joannem, eorum qui sub gratia. Vel, per Moysen mortuum mortuorum, per vivos vivorum.

VERS. 19.-- Et habemus firmiorem. Quod dixi vobis, non est fabulosum, sed certum, et non tantum his signis declaratum, sed etiam ad confirmationem nostrae praedicationis habemus propheticum sermonem David: Dominus dixit ad me, Filius meus es tu (Psal. II), etc., firmiorem inter alios prophetas, 691Bquia clarius aliis de Christo prophetavit.

Cui benefacitis attendentes. (BEDA.) Ut lucem possitis habere scientiae, etc., usque ad ad comparationem futurae vitae lucerna indigemus.

VERS. 20.-- Hoc primum intelligentes. Attendite prophetico sermoni, nam a Deo est. Quod potest probari per hoc, quod prophetia non est talibus verbis et tali modo locutionis scripta, quali utuntur homines in locutione sua, et quali saeculares scripturae sunt compositae et interpretatae. Et vere non est locutio prophetarum secundum suam, vel audientium voluntatem, sed secundum voluntatem Spiritus sancti qui loquebatur in illis.

VERS. 21.-- Non enim voluntate humana. (BEDA.) Sicut in potestate prophetarum non erat semper habere 691Cspiritum, semper futura praedicere: ita non erat potestatis eorum ut quaecunque vellent, docerent, sed ea sola dicebant quae a spiritu didicerant. Haec ideo dicuntur, ne quis ad libitum suum Scripturas exponat.

CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- Simon. (BEDA.) He writes this epistle to the same persons to whom he wrote the first also, etc., as far as they did not yield, let them not yield to the heretics.

Servant and Apostle. The person of the writer is commended.

Equal. (BEDA.) He acknowledges them as equal to himself, not on account of circumcision, but because the same grace and faith of Christ which they themselves had received, this also they exercised through works. In justice. This justice human 689Bprudence did not find out, nor did the institution of the law teach it, but our Saviour showed it in the Gospel: Unless your justice abound more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven (Matth. V).

VERS. 2.-- Grace unto you. (BEDA.) In the first Epistle he wrote be multiplied; in this, be fulfilled: because he writes that one to beginners, this one to the more perfect. Grace is multiplied to those who advance well in this life through faith; it shall be fulfilled to those who arrive in the other life through sight. Whence he adds, in the knowledge of God, that is, that you may know the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom he sent.

VERS. 3.-- How all things. (ID.) So may the grace of Christ be fulfilled, etc., as far as but he restored the weak and inglorious by his own power and glory.

689CVERS. 4.-- He hath given us most great and precious promises. That is, eternal life, which we may obtain through the knowledge of him. Or he gave the things which he promised us, concerning the conversion of others through us, or concerning the coming of the Holy Spirit, or concerning the power of miracles.

VERS. 5.-- Minister in faith. (BEDA.) Not only should you flee the concupiscence of the world, etc., as far as lest you be soft and dissolute.

VERS. 6.-- But in knowledge, abstinence. (ID.) When you shall have learned to do good things, in the hope of eternal life abstain forthwith from evil things, both in thought and in deed, lest the knowledge of heavenly things go for nothing, if anyone restrain not himself from the allurements of the world.

VERS. 7.-- But in piety. (ID.) Bestow not the works of piety upon your adversaries with a view to any other 689Dthing save that of brotherly love, that you may pray for them that they may be converted, who by your teaching or by rebukes are unwilling to be converted.

VERS. 8.-- For if these things be with you, etc. Therefore hold these things and minister them to others, because if all these things be present with you in the affection of the heart, and also abound, that is, shall have prevailed against the wars of the vices, they will not constitute you void of virtues in this world, or without fruit in the world to come.

690AVERS. 9.-- He is blind and groping with the hand. (BEDA.) The eye designates knowledge, the hand designates work, etc., as far as he could not foreknow, he fell.

Receiving forgetfulness. When a man falls from sin into sin, he seems to have forgotten the covenant which he entered upon with God in baptism.

VERS. 10.-- You shall not sin at any time. That is, you shall attain unto eternal life, where you shall sin no more. Or you shall shun criminal sins, which whoever has committed shall not have an inheritance in the kingdom of Christ; free from which he remains, with the Lord helping, who shall have given himself over to the aforesaid virtues. But without venial sins no one can be.

VERS. 11.-- For so. (BEDA.) Ezechiel, speaking of the building set upon the mountain, etc., as far as, and there shall be seen 690Bthe God of gods in Sion (Psal. LXXXIII).

VERS. 12.-- I will begin always to admonish you of these things. That there may be for you an entrance open unto the Holy of holies, again and again will I admonish you, and I shall always be at the beginning, because there shall never be an end of admonition. What therefore it is not irksome to me to repeat the oftener, let it not be burdensome to you the oftener to hear.

And indeed knowing. (BEDA.) Why does he wish to admonish those who know, and are confirmed? For this reason, namely, that they may exercise in works the knowledge which they have, and may guard with fixed mind the truth by whose presence they are confirmed, lest at any time through masters of error they fall away from the simplicity and chastity of the faith.

VERS. 13.-- But it is just, etc. Therefore I will admonish, because I judge it just, by reason of this, that the Lord graciously called 690Cme, and confirmed me, and admonished me to confirm others, saying: Confirm thy brethren, to rouse you up as though sleeping unto these things foretold, being always solicitous in admonishing.

VERS. 14.-- The putting off of my tabernacle. (BEDA.) Beautifully he calls it not death, but a putting off, because it is so for the servants of God to pluck out the bonds of the flesh, as it is for travellers, the journey finished, to go to their own home instead of the dwelling of a tent; as it is for men set on campaign, the enemy laid low, to return to their fatherland.

VERS. 15.-- But I will give diligence. Because I am soon to pass over, therefore meanwhile, while I live, I will give diligence that you may frequently, namely as often as the false ones shall press upon you and shall wish to draw you away, have these things in memory 690Dafter my decease, when you shall not have me present as a teacher; not to have them in any manner whatever, but so that you may make a memorial of them by fulfilling them in work, and by teaching others.

After the decease, etc. The decease of the saints is so called when they go forth to meet the bridegroom.

VERS. 16.-- For not unlearned fables, [Vulg. doctas fabulas, "learned fables"] etc. (BEDA.) Here he touches both the pagans and the heretics. The pagans feared not to call gods whatsoever delighted them. The heretics, having received the mysteries of the true God, thereafter were zealous not to attend to the divine 691AScriptures, but rather to transfer them by ill interpretation to an erroneous sense.

But made beholders, etc. The transfiguration of the Lord and his brightness was a sign of his resurrection and of ours. By Elias, who appeared with him, is understood the resurrection of those who were before the law. By Moses, that of those who were under the law; by Peter, and James, and John, that of those who are under grace. Or, by Moses dead, that of the dead; by the living, that of the living.

VERS. 19.-- And we have the more firm. What I have said to you is not fabulous, but certain, and not only declared by these signs, but also for the confirmation of our preaching we have the prophetic word of David: The Lord said to me, Thou art my Son (Psal. II), etc., more firm among the other prophets, 691Bbecause he prophesied of Christ more clearly than the others.

Whereunto you do well to attend. (BEDA.) That you may be able to have the light of knowledge, etc., as far as in comparison with the life to come we have need of a lamp.

VERS. 20.-- Understanding this first. Attend to the prophetic word, for it is from God. Which can be proved by this, that prophecy is not written in such words and in such a manner of speech as men use in their own speech, and as secular writings are composed and interpreted. And truly the utterance of the prophets is not according to their own will, or that of the hearers, but according to the will of the Holy Spirit who was speaking in them.

VERS. 21.-- For not by human will. (BEDA.) As it was not in the power of the prophets always to have the 691Cspirit, always to foretell things to come: so it was not of their power that they should teach whatsoever things they would, but they spoke those things alone which they had learned from the spirit. These things are therefore said, lest anyone expound the Scriptures according to his own pleasure.

CAPUT II.

VERS. 1.-- Perditionem. Merito perditionem sibi inducunt, qui Redemptorem suum negant, vel non qualem veritas ostendit, sed qualem sibi ipsi fingunt, praedicant. Et propterea alieni a Redemptore, nil certius quam foveam exspectant.

VERS. 2.-- Via veritatis blasphemabitur. (BEDA.) Per haereticos non solum in eis quos suae haeresi seductos associat, sed in eis quoque quos per impurissima 691Dfacta sua et sacrificia vel mysteria exsecranda quae faciunt, in odium Christiani nominis concitant, existimantibus imperitis omnes Christianos hujusmodi flagitiis mancipatos, via veritatis blasphemari solet.

VERS. 4.-- Si enim Deus angelis. (ID.) Si Deus apostatas angelos traditos poenis inferni, etc., usque ad in abyssi profunda sunt rapti.

VERS. 5.-- Originali mundo. (ID.) Idem mundus est post diluvium qui et ante, etc., usque ad justi gloriam vitae accipient.

VERS. 6.-- Et civitates. Hae civitates dupliciter in ignem sunt redactae, quia et primo eas per incendium cum adjacentibus terris in cinerem redegit, 692Aet cum loca incendii postmodum Mortui maris undis contexerit, servare adhuc voluit circumpositam regionem priscae speciem poenae. Nascuntur enim poma pulcherrima, quae et edendi cupiditatem spectantibus generent. Si carpas, fatiscunt et resolvuntur in cinerem, fumumque excitant quasi adhuc ardeant. In cinerem. Ignis qui Sodomitas semel punivit, significat quod impii sine fine sunt passuri. Quod terra fumigabunda, quod fructus pulcherrimi cinerem intus habent et fetorem, innuit quia dilectio carnalis, etsi stultis arridet, nil tamen in invisibilibus nisi incendium sibi reservant, vel ut fumus tormentorum eis in saecula saeculorum ascendat.

VERS. 7.-- Loth oppressum. (BEDA.) Cruciabant justum iniqua proximorum facta et dicta, quae cernens, 692Bcorrigere non valebat, ipse autem in bonis actibus se reservabat. Nec visis, vel auditis flagitiis se fuscabat. Dicitur item visu, vel auditu justus; quia nihil in illo videbant, nihil de eo audiebant, nisi quod pertinebat ad justitiam.

VERS. 9.-- Iniquos. Pseudo et eorum sequaces, et ideo nolite eos sequi.

In diem judicii. (BEDA.) Et ante diem judicii soluti corpore poenas luunt, et in judicio graviora exspectant, quando corpore et anima cruciabuntur.

VERS. 11.-- Ubi angeli. Ibi cruciabuntur iniqui, ubi angeli mali, qui prius contra Deum superbierant, et inde puniti sunt, jam non portant, sed potius abjiciunt judicium superbiendi contra Deum: quod est exsecrabile adversus seipsos, quia pro illo graviter 692Cpuniuntur, et territi magnitudine poenarum, jam ab illo judicio praesumptioni desistunt: sed isti iniqui nondum desistunt. Vel torquebuntur ibi, id est apud Deum: ubi angeli non boni, non sustinent, sed vindicant si quid exsecrabile contra illos dicitur.

VERS. 13.-- Mercedem injustitiae. (BEDA.) Id est poenam pro malis operibus, et maxime illi qui cum corruptioni carnis serviant, et vesanis teneantur erroribus, conversationem justorum blasphemant, et eis qui sanum sapiunt, detrahere non cessant.

VERS. 17.-- Hi sunt. Non sunt imitandi pseudoapostoli, quia licet videantur utiles, nihil tamen in eis est nisi vanitas et immunditia.

VERS. 18.-- Superba. Superbe dicentes se esse justos, quamvis sit vanum, carnaliter desiderantes 692Det luxuriose viventes pellicunt eos qui paululum refugerint a peccatis; qui cum similes eis sunt, tamen aliquatenus declinant eorum societatem, et a peccatis aliquatenus abstinent. In desideriis carnis, etc. Immundus est omnis qui exaltat cor suum, ut qui paululum refugerint a peccatis, ad suum revertantur errorem et studeant in luxuria.

VERS. 20.-- Si enim, etc., cognitione Domini nostri et Salvatoris Jesu Christi, etc. Si fugientes coinquinationem et animae et corporis quam mundus immittit; rursus implicati (sicut ante baptismum) superantur ab illis coinquinationibus, apparet quod facta sunt posteriora eorum deteriora prioribus: quia magis offendunt Deum quam ante baptismum, 693Aet majores poenas patientur, quia mendaces sunt promissiones factae in baptismo.

VERS. 22.-- Contigit enim illis. (BEDA.) In Proverbiis cum expositione positum est, etc., usque ad ante Dei oculos sordidas ipsas etiam lacrymas facit.

CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1.-- Perdition. Deservedly do they bring perdition upon themselves who deny their Redeemer, or who preach him not such as the truth shows him, but such as they themselves feign him to be. And therefore, being strangers to the Redeemer, they look for nothing more certain than the pit.

VERS. 2.-- The way of truth shall be blasphemed. (BEDA.) Through the heretics the way of truth is wont to be blasphemed, not only in those whom, seduced, he associates to his own heresy, but in those also whom, through their most impure 691Ddeeds and the execrable sacrifices or mysteries which they perform, they stir up into hatred of the Christian name, while the unskilled suppose that all Christians are given over to villainies of this kind.

VERS. 4.-- For if God spared not the angels. (ID.) If God [spared not] the apostate angels, delivered over to the punishments of hell, etc., as far as they were hurried away into the depths of the abyss.

VERS. 5.-- To the world. (ID.) The world after the flood is the same which was before it also, etc., as far as the just shall receive the glory of.

VERS. 6.-- And the cities. These cities were in a twofold manner brought into fire, because first he reduced them to ashes by burning, together with the adjacent lands, 692Aand when he had afterwards covered the places of the burning with the waves of the Dead Sea, he willed the surrounding region still to preserve the appearance of the ancient punishment. For there grow most beautiful fruits, which beget also in those who look upon them a desire of eating. If thou pluck them, they crumble and are dissolved into ashes, and stir up smoke as though they still burned. Into ashes. The fire which once punished the Sodomites signifies that the ungodly are to suffer without end. That the earth is smoking, that the most beautiful fruits have ashes within and a stench, intimates that carnal love, though it smile upon fools, yet they reserve to themselves nothing among things invisible but burning, or that the smoke of their torments may go up for ever and ever.

VERS. 7.-- Lot oppressed. (BEDA.) The unjust deeds and words of his neighbours tormented the just man, which, beholding, 692Bhe was not able to correct; but he himself kept himself in good works. Nor did he darken himself by the villainies seen or heard. He is likewise called just in seeing, or in hearing; because they saw nothing in him, they heard nothing of him, save what pertained to justice.

VERS. 9.-- The unjust. The false ones and their followers; and therefore do not follow them.

Unto the day of judgment. (BEDA.) Both before the day of judgment, loosed from the body, they pay their penalties, and in the judgment they look for heavier things, when they shall be tormented in body and in soul.

VERS. 11.-- Where the angels. There shall the unjust be tormented, where the evil angels, who before had been proud against God, and were punished thereupon, now bear not, but rather cast away, the judgment of being proud against God: which is execrable against themselves, because for it they are grievously 692Cpunished, and, terrified by the greatness of the punishments, they now desist from that judgment, to presumption [praesumptioni; read praesumptionis, "of presumption"]: but these unjust ones do not yet desist. Or they shall be tortured there, that is, with God: where the angels, not good ones [non boni; read boni, "the good angels"], endure not, but avenge, if anything execrable be said against them.

VERS. 13.-- The reward of injustice. (BEDA.) That is, punishment for evil works; and chiefly those who, while they serve the corruption of the flesh and are held by mad errors, blaspheme the conversation of the just, and cease not to detract from those who are of sound understanding.

VERS. 17.-- These are they. The false apostles are not to be imitated, because although they may seem useful, yet there is nothing in them but vanity and uncleanness.

VERS. 18.-- Proud things. Proudly saying that they are just, although it be vain, desiring carnally 692Dand living luxuriously, they allure those who have fled a little from sins; who, though they are like unto them, yet in some measure decline their fellowship, and in some measure abstain from sins. In the desires of the flesh, etc. Every one is unclean who exalteth his heart, so that they who have fled a little from sins return to their own error and are zealous in luxury.

VERS. 20.-- For if, etc., the knowledge of our Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ, etc. If, fleeing the defilement both of soul and of body which the world sends in; being again entangled (as before baptism) they are overcome by those defilements, it appears that their latter things are made worse than the former: because they offend God more than before baptism, 693Aand they shall suffer greater punishments, because the promises made in baptism are lying ones.

VERS. 22.-- For it hath befallen them. (BEDA.) In the Proverbs it is set down with an exposition, etc., as far as it makes even those tears themselves foul before the eyes of God.

CAPUT III.

VERS. 1.-- Hanc ecce vobis. Quasi dicat: Jam aliam Epistolam scripsi ad repellendos pseudo; et ut meam magnam sollicitudinem vobis ostendam, ecce pro eadem causa hanc etiam secundam scribo Epistolam.

VERS. 2.-- Ut memores. Id est ut per illas Epistolas sitis memores eorum verborum quae ego ipse praesens praedixi vobis. Verborum, dico acceptorum, 693Ba sanctis prophetis quae auctoritate sanctorum prophetarum confirmantur, et sitis memores praeceptorum sanctorum apostolorum: ne praecepta quae sancti apostoli dederunt vobis, dimittatis pro praedicatione illorum pseudo, et quae praecepta sunt etiam Domini et Salvatoris quia ipse Dominus, qui venit salvare nos, haec praecepta instituit et servare praecepit.

VERS. 5.-- Latet. (BEDA.) Ideo dicunt omnia eodem modo perseverare, etc., usque ad in diem judicii et perditionis impiorum hominum.

VERS. 8.-- Unum vero hoc. Probato quod judicium vere erit, ostendit quam efficax futurum sit. Si dicitur vobis a pseudo: Quod vultis potestis facere, quia tantum tempus restat usque ad diem judicii, 693Cquod ex longinquitate temporis judex scire multa non poterit; non credatis eis, quia quidquid potest fieri mille annis, ita videt, sicut illud quod fit una die. Si dicunt: Mille annis, poenitendo non assequemini veniam delictorum; non credatis: quia unus dies verae poenitentiae tantum valet ad salutem, quantum mille anni, sicut latroni.

Sicut mille anni. (BEDA.) In agnitione divinae virtutis, etc., usque ad unde et recte subjungitur: 694AVERS. 9.-- Non tardat Dominus. Et quia omnia videt, potestis credere quod Dominus non tardat promissis implendis, quia nondum est tempus quo implenda implebit. Sed ideo differt, ut electorum summam, quam praevidit, prius adimpleat, unde in Apocalypsi: Et dictum est illis, ut requiescerent adhuc modicum tempus, donec compleantur conservi eorum (Apoc. VI).

VERS. 10.-- Coeli magno. Coeli, id est sancti; si quid ergo in eis purgandum est, per ignem purgabitur.

Elementa. (BEDA.) Quatuor, quibus mundus consistit, etc., usque ad et per temporale supplicium, solventur in melius.

VERS. 13.-- Novos vero coelos. (ID.) Non ait, 694Balios, sed veteres et antiquos in melius commutandos, etc., usque ad habemus et in prophetis: Lux lucernae non lucebit ibi amplius (Isa. LXV).

VERS. 14.-- Satagite immaculati et inviolati. Hae sunt vigiliae de quibus dicitur: Beati servi illi quos cum venerit Dominus, invenerit vigilantes. Vigilat qui se a sordibus immunem custodit, qui quantum in se est, cum omnibus hominibus pacem habet, qui omnes illecebras carnis spiritus regimini subdit, in seipso pace felicissima utens.

VERS. 15.-- Frater noster. (BEDA.) Paulum illis scripsisse dicit, in quo ostendit, etc., usque ad et per has se ad profectum excitent.

VERS. 16.-- Difficilia. In Epistolis Pauli quae depravantur ab haereticis, etc., usque ad quidam luxuriae 694Cfrena laxantes, et alia multa, etc.

Depravant. (BEDA.) Nullus Veteris vel Novi Testamenti liber est in quo haeretici aliquid non perverterint, vel demendo, vel addendo, vel mutando. Unicum remedium indoctis, humili stabilitate verba doctorum auditum praebere, quam stabilitatem quia haeretici non habent, vento superbiae quasi palea levis de Ecclesia tolluntur, ad suam ipsorum perditionem.

CHAPTER III.

VERS. 1.-- Behold, this to you. As if he should say: I have already written another Epistle for the driving back of the false ones; and that I may show you my great solicitude, behold, for the same cause I write this second Epistle also.

VERS. 2.-- That you may be mindful. That is, that through those Epistles you may be mindful of those words which I myself, being present, foretold to you. Of the words, I say, received, 693Bfrom the holy prophets which are confirmed by the authority of the holy prophets; and that you may be mindful of the precepts of the holy apostles: lest you let go the precepts which the holy apostles gave you in exchange for the preaching of those false ones; and which precepts are also of the Lord and Saviour because the Lord himself, who came to save us, appointed these precepts and commanded them to be kept.

VERS. 5.-- It is hidden. (BEDA.) Therefore they say that all things continue in the same manner, etc., as far as unto the day of judgment and of the perdition of ungodly men.

VERS. 8.-- But this one thing. It being proved that the judgment shall truly be, he shows how effectual it shall be. If it be said to you by the false ones: You can do what you will, because so great a time remains until the day of judgment, 693Cthat from the length of time the judge will not be able to know many things; believe them not, because whatsoever can be done in a thousand years, he sees it even as that which is done in one day. If they say: In a thousand years, by repenting, you shall not obtain pardon of your offences; believe them not: because one day of true penitence avails as much unto salvation as a thousand years, as it did for the thief.

As a thousand years. (BEDA.) In the acknowledgment of the divine power, etc., as far as whence also it is rightly subjoined: 694AVERS. 9.-- The Lord delayeth not. And because he sees all things, you may believe that the Lord delays not in fulfilling his promises, because the time is not yet in which he shall fulfil the things to be fulfilled. But he therefore defers, that he may first fill up the sum of the elect which he foresaw; whence in the Apocalypse: And it was said to them that they should rest yet a little time, until their fellow servants be filled up (Apoc. VI).

VERS. 10.-- The heavens with a great violence. The heavens, that is, the saints; if therefore there be anything in them to be purged, it shall be purged by fire.

The elements. (BEDA.), of which the world consists, etc., as far as and through a temporal punishment, they shall be dissolved into something better.

VERS. 13.-- But new heavens. (ID.) He saith not, 694Bothers, but that the old and ancient ones are to be changed into something better, etc., as far as we have it also in the prophets: The light of the lamp shall shine there no more (Isa. LXV). [Vulg. Apoc. XVIII, 23 lux lucernae non lucebit in te amplius, "in thee"]

VERS. 14.-- Be diligent to be unspotted and undefiled. These are the watchings of which it is said: Blessed are those servants whom the Lord, when he cometh, shall find watching. He watches who keeps himself free from defilements, who, as much as in him lies, has peace with all men, who subjects all the allurements of the flesh to the governance of the spirit, enjoying in himself a most happy peace.

VERS. 15.-- Our brother. (BEDA.) He says that Paul had written to them, wherein he shows, etc., as far as and by these may stir themselves up unto advancement.

VERS. 16.-- Difficult things. In the Epistles of Paul, which are perverted by the heretics, etc., as far as certain men loosing the 694Creins to luxury, and many other things, etc.

They pervert. (BEDA.) There is no book of the Old or of the New Testament in which the heretics have not perverted something, either by taking away, or by adding, or by changing. The one remedy for the unlearned is, with humble steadfastness, to lend an ear to the words of the doctors; which steadfastness because the heretics have not, they are lifted out of the Church by the wind of pride like light chaff, unto their own perdition.

On this text

Cruces for this work — where the plate is defective or the reading uncertain →

The Glossa ordinaria is the standard medieval commentary on the Bible — the gloss a reader of Scripture met in the margins of the page, drawn from the Fathers and built up in the schools of the twelfth century. What Migne prints, and what is translated here, is an abridged nineteenth-century recension of it: the marginal glosses only, without the interlinear layer, and 58 of the roughly 73 books — Ezekiel, Daniel, the minor prophets and the Maccabees are absent from it altogether. Migne places the whole under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription scholarship has long since given up; the attributions inside the text — (RAB.) for Rabanus, (ISID.) for Isidore, (CHRYS.) for Chrysostom — are his source's and are not always reliable either. They are printed as he printed them. The second letter of Peter shares a great deal of its matter with Jude, and the two are glossed here in the same volume; but Migne's two plates share almost no wording, and this translation was made from its own text rather than from its neighbour. The lemma at col. 0691A prints Non enim indoctas fabulas where the Vulgate has doctas — "unlearned fables" against "learned" — which stacks a second negative on the sentence and inverts its object. It is translated as he prints it. One of his source-marks is wrong in an instructive way: the words quoted at col. 0694B under (Isa. LXV) are not in Isaiah at all but in Apocalypse 18:23, and he prints ibi where that verse has in te. The mark is reproduced as he set it, because the notes that follow refer back to it by sequence and correcting one would misdirect the others. An English of the Rusch text, a different recension, exists for some books of the Bible; none has been found for this one. The gilt column marks carry Migne's own numbering: migne.app/pl/114/0689a resolves to the opening.
