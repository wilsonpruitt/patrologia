Anselm of Laon and his school

Anselmus Laudunensis et schola

twelfth century

The masters of the cathedral school of Laon, gathered around Anselm (c. 1050–1117), to whom the making of the Glossa ordinaria on the Bible is traditionally credited. The gloss is a school's work rather than one man's: it draws its comment from the Fathers — Rabanus, Isidore, Bede, Jerome, Gregory — and names them as it goes, and it grew by accretion over generations. Migne prints it under the name of Walafrid Strabo, an ascription now known to be mistaken.

PRAEFATIO

  • 9A

Haec interpretatio Hieronymi est. Si quid in ea moverit, secundum Hebraeos codices exploretur. Alia est Septuaginta interpretum Ecclesiis usitata: quae, quamvis nonnulla aliter habeat quam in Hebraeis codicibus invenitur, tamen utraque, id est secundum Septuaginta et secundum Hebraeam, apostolica auctoritate firmata est. Non errore neque reprehensione superiori, sed certo consilio Septuaginta nonnulla aliter dixisse vel contexisse intelliguntur. Quod ideo praemonemus, ne quisquam alteram ex altera velit emendare: quod singulorum in suo genere veritas observanda est.

PREFACE

  • 9A

This translation is Jerome's. If anything in it should give trouble, let it be examined according to the Hebrew manuscripts. There is another, that of the Seventy translators, in customary use among the Churches: which, although it has some things otherwise than is found in the Hebrew manuscripts, yet each of the two, that is, the one according to the Seventy and the one according to the Hebrew, has been confirmed by apostolic authority. Not by error nor by any higher censure, but by a settled purpose, the Seventy are understood to have said or woven together some things otherwise. And we give this warning beforehand for this reason, that no one should wish to correct the one out of the other: because the truth of each of them in its own kind is to be observed.

THE ARGUMENT.

  • 9B

Jeremiah the Anathothite, which is a little village three miles distant from Jerusalem, fell asleep at Thamnas in Egypt, overwhelmed with stones by the people. And he lies buried in that place where of old Pharao the king had dwelt. CAPUT PRIMUM.

VERS. 1.-- Verba Jeremiae. (HIER.) LXX: Verbum Dei, quia verba Jeremiae verbum Domini est.

VERS. 2.-- In diebus, etc. (ID). Mirabilis Dei clementia. Jam captivitate vicina, et Babylonio exercitu 9Cvallante Jerusalem, nihilominus populum ad poenitentiam vocat, mallens servare conversos quam perdere delinquentes.

VERS. 5.-- Priusquam te. Non quod ante conceptionem fuerit, ut haeretici suspicantur; sed quia praescivit eum Dominus futurum, cui facienda jam facta sunt; qui vocat ea quae non sunt tanquam ea quae sunt.

10AVERS. 6.-- A, a, a, Domine Deus. Detestatur officium quod pro aetate non potest sustinere. Eadem verecundia, qua Moyses gracilis et tenuis vocis se esse dicit; sed ille quasi magnae et robustae aetatis corripitur, quasi puero huic venia datur, qui verecundia et pudore decoratur.

VERS. 9.-- Et misit, etc. (ID.) Notandum quod hic manus Dei mittitur, etc., usque ad ut confidentiam accipiat praedicandi.

(ID.) Quatuor tristibus duo laeta succedunt, etc., usque ad et propinare jubetur omnibus nationibus.

VERS. 11.-- Virgam vigiliarum. (ID.) Virga vigiliarum, etc., usque ad resurrectione Domini floruisse narratur.

VERS. 13.-- Et factum est. Gradatim peccantes 10Bcorripiuntur; qui noluerunt, percutiente virga, corrigi vel emendari, mittuntur in ollam succensam, de qua plenius Ezechiel.

Ollam succensam. Hinc Dominus ad Job dicit, etc., usque ad sed haec violenta et prava interpretatio est.

VERS. 14.-- Habitatores terrae. De quibus in Apocalypsi, etc., usque ad et Petrus: Electis advenis Ponti, Galatiae, Cappadociae, Asiae et Bithyniae.

VERS. 17.-- Tu ergo accinge, etc. (ID.) Job quoque praecipitur, etc., usque ad sed civitatem totam quae super montem posita latere non potest.

VERS. 18.-- Et in columnam. De qua Apostolus: Columna et firmamentum veritatis (I Tim.). Et Petrus et Jacobus et Joannes qui putabantur esse columnae 10CEcclesiae, et Paulo et Barnabae dederunt dextras communionis.

Regibus Juda. (ID.) Si quando reges Juda, etc., usque ad in Domino speret ut vincat.

CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- The words of Jeremiah. (HIER.) The Seventy: The word of God, because the words of Jeremiah are the word of the Lord.

VERS. 2.-- In the days, etc. (ID). Wonderful is the clemency of God. Though the captivity is now near, and the Babylonian army 9Cis entrenched about Jerusalem, none the less he calls the people to repentance, choosing rather to save the converted than to destroy the offenders.

VERS. 5.-- Before I formed thee. Not that he existed before conception, as the heretics suspect; but because the Lord foreknew that he would be, he to whom the things to be done are already done; who calls those things which are not as those which are.

10AVERS. 6.-- Ah, ah, ah, Lord God. He shrinks from an office which for his age he cannot sustain. With the same modesty with which Moses says that he is of a slender and thin voice; but the one, as being of great and sturdy age, is rebuked, while to this one, as to a boy, pardon is given, who is adorned with modesty and shame.

VERS. 9.-- And he sent, etc. (ID.) It is to be noted that here the hand of God is sent, etc., as far as that he may receive confidence in preaching.

(ID.) Upon four sad things two joyful ones follow, etc., as far as and he is bidden to give it to all the nations to drink.

VERS. 11.-- A rod of watchings. (ID.) [Vulg. Jer. 1:11 virgam vigilantem, "a watching rod"] The rod of watchings, etc., as far as is related to have blossomed at the resurrection of the Lord.

VERS. 13.-- And it came to pass. By degrees sinners are 10Brebuked; they who would not, when the rod smote, be corrected or amended, are sent into the boiling pot, of which Ezechiel speaks more fully.

A boiling pot. Hence the Lord says to Job, etc., as far as but this is a violent and perverse interpretation.

VERS. 14.-- The inhabitants of the earth. Concerning whom in the Apocalypse, etc., as far as and Peter: To the elect strangers of Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia and Bithynia.

VERS. 17.-- Thou therefore gird up, etc. (ID.) Job also is commanded, etc., as far as but a whole city which is set upon a mountain cannot be hid.

VERS. 18.-- And as a pillar. Of which the Apostle: The pillar and ground of the truth (I Tim.). And Peter and James and John, who were thought to be the pillars 10Cof the Church, [Vulg. Gal. 2:9 qui videbantur columnae esse, "who seemed to be pillars", with no "of the Church"] gave to Paul and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship.

To the kings of Juda. (ID.) If ever the kings of Juda, etc., as far as let him hope in the Lord that he may conquer.

CAPUT II.

VERS. 2.-- Adolescentiam. (HIER.) Plenius hoc in Ezechiele legitur ubi sibi Dominus Jerusalem matrimonio 11Acopulat, et sub persona uxoris, suis jungit amplexibus. Sive ut ardentiorem monstret affectum, puellam eam et desponsatam vocat. Quo enim nondum potiti sumus, ardentius appetimus.

VERS. 3.-- Omnes qui devorant. (HIER.) Sicut qui primitias devorant, etc., usque ad primitiae debentur sacerdotibus, non hostibus.

VERS. 4.-- Israel. (ID.) Utrumque nomen ponitur, Jacob et Israel, non secundum duas et decem tribus, sed juxta omnem populum, cum ipse Jacob postea dictus sit Israel.

VERS. 5.-- Quid invenerunt patres. Offensam dicit Deus a patribus factam, non quod peccata patrum filiis imputentur, sed quia filii habentes similitudinem patrum, et suo et parentum scelere puniuntur. Propter 11Bsanctos patres filiorum miseretur Deus.

VERS. 6.-- Neque habitavit homo. (ID.) Qui semper ad majora festinat, etc., usque ad sed certamen et incertus exitus futurorum.

VERS. 7.-- Et induxi vos, quasi diceret: Pro labore durissimi itineris, etc., usque ad et haereditatem illius abominabilem facimus.

VERS. 10.-- Cethim. (ID.) Cethim, vel Italiae scilicet, vel occidentalium partium, quia Judaeae Cyprus insula, in qua urbs hujus nominis vicina est, de qua Zeno princeps Stoicorum.

Et considerate. (ID.) Possumus contra eos hoc dicere, qui majori studio sequuntur vitia quam virtutes, quibus dicitur: Humanum dico propter infirmitatem carnis vestrae; sicut exhibuistis membra vestra 11Cservire immunditiae et iniquitati ad iniquitatem (Rom. VI), etc.

VERS. 11.-- Populus meus. Velut anthropophormitae, qui occasione hujus testimonii: Faciamus hominem ad imaginem et similitudinem (Gen. I), immensam et simplicem divinitatis substantiam lineamentis nostris et humana figuratione compositam pertinaciter contendunt.

VERS. 12.-- Obstupescite, coeli. (ID.) LXX: Obstupuit coelum, etc., usque ad et tam coeli quam coelum eodem dicuntur nomine, ut Thebae, Athenae.

VERS. 13.-- Me dereliquerunt. Qui praeceptum dedi dicens: Ego sum Dominus Deus tuus qui eduxi te de terra Aegypti. Et secundum quod in eodem loco scriptum 11Dest: Non sint tibi dii alieni in conspectu meo, pro quo secutus est daemones.

Fontem aquae vivae. (ID.) Fons perpetuus est et vitales habet aquas. Cisternae et lacus de torrentibus et aquis turbidis complentur impluviis.

VERS. 15.-- Nunquid servus. (ID.) Ex hoc loco Judae in superbiam elati, etc., usque ad Maledictus Chanaam, servus erit fratibus suis (Gen. IX).

Leones. Secundum anagogen, leones intelligimus, etc., usque ad Omnes adulterantes quasi clibanus corda eorum (Osee VII).

VERS. 16.-- Usque ad verticem. Secundum illud: A planta pedis usque ad verticem, etc., usque ad qui puritatem Ecclesiae sua polluunt turpitudine.

12AVERS. 19.-- Arguet te malitia tua. (ID.) Nota quod malitia et praevaricatio, etc., usque ad quia dereliquit Dominum Deum suum.

VERS. 20.-- A saeculo confregisti. (ID.) Potest hoc ad eum dici, etc., usque ad et faciunt divaricare pedes suos omni transeunti.

VERS. 21.-- Vineam electam. In Hebraeo, soreth, quod genus est vitis optimae, de cujus sarculo plantavit Israel, et miratur quomodo semen verum et electa vinea in amaritudinem versa facta sit aliena.

Quomodo ergo conversa es? Nullus securus sit si plantatio, etc., usque ad si permanserit in eo quod plantata est.

VERS. 22.-- Borith. (ID.) Ipsum Hebraeum. LXX vero herbam fullonum, quae in Palaestina in humidis et virentibus nascitur locis, et ad lavandas sordes 12Beamdem vim habet quam et nitrum.

VERS. 23.-- Vide vias tuas, id est respice convallem filiorum Ennon, quae Siloe fontibus irrigatur, ibi cernes delubrum Baal, quem relicto Deo coluisti.

VERS. 25.-- Prohibe pedem tuum. Pascha facturi, etc., usque ad quae deberent evangelico pede calcari et conteri.

VERS. 27.-- Verterunt. Projicientes sermones meos retrorsum. Quando enim magister praecipit, etc., usque ad sed tumorem animi indicant gestu corporis.

VERS. 28.-- Secundum numerum. Vel eosdem, vel diversos, singulae civitates colebant deos, ut nec in impietate viderentur habere consensum; sed pugnans contra se superstitio, errorem sequeretur diversum.

12CVERS. 29.-- Quid vultis? Prona est ad excusationem sui humana perversitas: et quod merito sustinet, sibi injuste videtur sustinere.

VERS. 30.-- Filios vestros. Ut plagis filiorum disceretis, quod austeriori curandi essetis medicamine, et ne forsitan diceretis, peccantes corripere noluisti.

VERS. 31.-- Videre verbum Domini. Moyses videbat vocem Domini. Et Joannes apostolus verbum Dei se vidisse et attrectasse dicit.

VERS. 32.-- Nunquid obliviscetur? (ID.) Ornamentum suum perdit qui a Domino recedit, et intelligentiam doctrinae, quae significatur in pectore: Unde, Joannes supra pectus Domini in coena recubuit (Joan. XIII), et sacerdotibus inter caetera servatur pectusculum 12Dvictimarum.

VERS. 34.-- In omnibus. Istis, sive sub omni quercu alia littera; Hebraeum enim et quercum et ista significat. In amoenis autem locis, umbra et frondibus immolabant.

VERS. 35.-- Et dixisti. His utendum est apud eos, etc., usque ad non lugere, sed excusationes praetendere.

Ecce ego judicio. (ID.) Audiat haeresis nova ex veteri, iram Dei esse vel maximam, nolle peccatum humiliter confiteri, sed impudenter jactare justitiam.

VERS. 36.-- Et ab Aegypto. Ut Aegyptiorum impetum, etc., usque ad increpantur ergo quia, relicto Deo, spem in hominibus ponunt.

VERS. 37.-- Super caput tuum. (ID.) More lugentium, 13Aut Thamar ab Amnon corrupta cinere caput sparsit, et manus supra posuit, et ita reversa est in domum suam.

CHAPTER II.

VERS. 2.-- Thy youth. (HIER.) This is read more fully in Ezechiel, where the Lord joins Jerusalem to himself 11Ain wedlock, and under the person of a wife unites her to his own embraces. Or, that he may show a more ardent affection, he calls her a maiden and betrothed. For what we have not yet obtained, that we desire the more ardently.

VERS. 3.-- All they that devour. (HIER.) Even as they who devour the firstfruits, etc., as far as the firstfruits are owed to the priests, not to enemies.

VERS. 4.-- Israel. (ID.) Both names are set down, Jacob and Israel, not according to the two and the ten tribes, but according to the whole people, since Jacob himself was afterwards called Israel.

VERS. 5.-- What have your fathers found. God speaks of an offence committed by the fathers, not because the sins of the fathers are imputed to the sons, but because the sons, having the likeness of their fathers, are punished both for their own crime and for their parents'. For the sake of 11Bthe holy fathers God has mercy on the sons.

VERS. 6.-- Neither did man dwell. (ID.) He who is ever hastening to greater things, etc., as far as but a struggle, and an uncertain issue of things to come.

VERS. 7.-- And I brought you in, as though he should say: In return for the labour of a most hard journey, etc., as far as and we make his inheritance abominable.

VERS. 10.-- Cethim. (ID.) Cethim, that is, either of Italy or of the western parts, because near to Judaea is the island of Cyprus, in which there is a city of this name, whence Zeno the prince of the Stoics.

And consider. (ID.) We can say this against those who follow vices with greater zeal than virtues, to whom it is said: I speak a human thing, because of the infirmity of your flesh; for as you have yielded your members 11Cto serve uncleanness and iniquity unto iniquity (Rom. VI), etc.

VERS. 11.-- My people. Like the anthropophormitae, who, taking occasion from this testimony: Let us make man to our image and likeness (Gen. I), stubbornly contend that the boundless and simple substance of the Godhead is composed of our lineaments and of a human shaping.

VERS. 12.-- Be astonished, O ye heavens. (ID.) The Seventy: The heaven was astonished, etc., as far as and both the heavens and the heaven are called by the same name, as Thebae, Athenae.

VERS. 13.-- They have forsaken me. I who gave the commandment, saying: I am the Lord thy God, who brought thee out of the land of Egypt. And according to that which in the same place is 11Dwritten: Let there be no strange gods before me, [Vulg. Exod. 20:3 Non habebis deos alienos coram me, "Thou shalt not have strange gods before me"] in whose stead he followed after devils.

The fountain of living water. (ID.) A fountain is perpetual and has living waters. Cisterns and pools are filled from torrents and troubled waters by the rains.

VERS. 15.-- Is he a servant. (ID.) From this passage of Juda, lifted up into pride, etc., as far as Cursed be Chanaam, a servant shall he be to his brethren fratibus (Gen. IX). [Vulg. Gen. 9:25 Chanaan, servus servorum erit fratribus suis, "a servant of servants shall he be to his brethren"]

Lions. According to the anagogy, we understand lions, etc., as far as All that commit adultery, their hearts are as an oven (Osee VII). [Vulg. Osee 7:4 quasi clibanus succensus a coquente, "as an oven heated by the baker"]

VERS. 16.-- Unto the crown of the head. According to that: From the sole of the foot unto the crown of the head, etc., as far as who defile the purity of the Church with their own foulness.

12AVERS. 19.-- Thy own wickedness shall reprove thee. (ID.) Note that wickedness and prevarication, etc., as far as because he hath forsaken the Lord his God.

VERS. 20.-- Of old thou hast broken. (ID.) This can be said of him, etc., as far as and they make their feet to straddle for every one that passeth by.

VERS. 21.-- A chosen vineyard. In the Hebrew, soreth, which is a kind of the choicest vine, from whose hoe [sarculo; read surculo, "shoot"] he planted Israel, and he marvels how the true seed and the chosen vineyard, turned into bitterness, has been made a stranger.

How then art thou turned? Let no man be secure if the planting, etc., as far as if it shall have abided in that whereunto it was planted.

VERS. 22.-- Borith. (ID.) The Hebrew word itself. But the Seventy, the fullers' herb, which in Palestine grows in moist and green places, and for the washing away of filth 12Bhas the same power as nitre.

VERS. 23.-- See thy ways, that is, look upon the valley of the sons of Ennon, which is watered by the springs of Siloe, there thou shalt discern the shrine of Baal, whom, having forsaken God, thou hast worshipped.

VERS. 25.-- Keep back thy foot. About to keep the pasch, etc., as far as which ought to be trodden and crushed by the evangelical foot.

VERS. 27.-- They have turned. Casting my words behind them. For when a master gives command, etc., as far as but they show the swelling of their mind by the gesture of their body.

VERS. 28.-- According to the number. The several cities worshipped gods, whether the same or different, so that not even in impiety might they seem to have agreement; but that superstition, fighting against itself, might follow a divergent error.

12CVERS. 29.-- Why will you? Human perversity is prone to excuse itself: and what it deservedly bears, it seems to itself to bear unjustly.

VERS. 30.-- Your children. That by the stripes of your children you might learn that you were to be healed by a sterner medicine, and lest perchance you should say, Thou wouldst not rebuke us when we sinned.

VERS. 31.-- To see the word of the Lord. Moses saw the voice of the Lord. And John the apostle says that he saw and handled the Word of God.

VERS. 32.-- Will she forget? (ID.) He loses his ornament who withdraws from the Lord, and the understanding of doctrine, which is signified in the breast: whence, John at the supper leaned upon the breast of the Lord (Joan. XIII), and among other things the little breast 12Dof the victims is kept for the priests.

VERS. 34.-- In all. In these, or another reading, under every oak; for the Hebrew signifies both the oak and these things. But in pleasant places, in shade and leaves, they used to sacrifice.

VERS. 35.-- And thou hast said. These things are to be used among those, etc., as far as not to mourn, but to hold out excuses.

Behold I will contend in judgment. (ID.) Let the new heresy sprung from the old hear that it is the very greatest wrath of God, to be unwilling to confess sin humbly, but shamelessly to boast one's righteousness.

VERS. 36.-- And from Egypt. That the onset of the Egyptians, etc., as far as they are rebuked therefore because, having forsaken God, they place their hope in men.

VERS. 37.-- Upon thy head. (ID.) After the manner of mourners, 13Aas Thamar, corrupted by Amnon, strewed ashes upon her head, and laid her hands above it, and so returned into her own house.

CAPUT III.

VERS. 1.-- Vulgo dicitur: si dimiserit. Ecce de fornicata et relicta muliere, etc., usque ad quanto contemptus adhuc vocare non dedignatur.

Si dimiserit vir. (HIER.) Hoc dicere possumus adversus eos, qui fidem Domini relinquentes et haereticorum erroribus irretiti post multas fornicationes et deceptionem suarum animarum, simulant se reverti ad pristinam veritatem, non ut deponant venena pectoris, sed ut insinuent aliis.

Tu autem fornicata es. Sive: Habuisti pastores multos in offensionem tui, ut Deum scilicet offenderes; 13Bet qui magistri esse debuerant, ut alios ab omni errore prohiberent, auctores impietatis exstiterunt.

Tamen revertere. Si revertebaris ad me, dicit Dominus. Arguit impudentiam meretricis, quae post adulterium audet reverti ad virum.

VERS. 2.-- Leva oculos tuos. Juxta anagogen: His qui haereticos errores deserere se promittunt, praecipitur ut levent oculos in directum. Nisi enim recta viderint, pristinam pravitatem damnare non possunt.

Quasi latro. Sicut solent latrones ad vesperam, etc., usque ad et descendentibus de Jerusalem in Jericho obsidet vias.

VERS. 3.-- Frons mulieris. (ID.) Hoc possumus 13Cdicere de haereticis qui in erroribus suis gloriantur. Quia supra dixit: non peccavi, arguit eam ut mulierem procacem quae ad nullum declinare erubescit.

VERS. 4.-- Dux virginitatis meae tu es. Nunquid irasceris in perpetuum, etc., usque ad tanto miserior meretrix quae non vult recipere sanitatem.

VERS. 6.-- Nunquid vidisti quae fecerit? (ID.) Facit comparationem, etc., usque ad et hoc est quod dicit: Nunquid vidisti quae fecerit?

Adversatrix Israel. (ID.) Sub figura duarum sororum loquitur quae de una stirpe Abrahae, Isaac et Jacob, sunt generatae. Priorem adversatricem vocat, quae statuit in Dan et in Bethel vitulos aureos.

VERS. 7.-- Et vidit. (ID.) Aliorum tormenta, aliorum remedia sunt, etc., usque ad et ad zelum 13Det aemulationem Domini collocatum.

VERS. 11.-- Justificavit animam. Justificata est Sodoma soror tua ex te. Et alibi: Descendit hic justificatus in domum suam ab illo (Luc. XVIII).

VERS. 12.-- Et non avertam faciem. Unde: Averte faciem tuam (Psal. L), etc., usque ad de quibus vere dicitur: Qui devorant populum meum sicut escam panis.

VERS. 13.-- Verumtamen scito. Quasi dicat, cum misertus fuero tui, non te justum putes; sed semper memento iniquitatis tuae et superbiae colla dimitte, ut qui per arrogantiam offendisti, per humilitatem placare Deum possis.

VERS. 14.-- Convertimini. (HIER.) Judaei putant 14Ahoc completum esse, etc., usque ad Nisi Dominus sabaoth reliquisset nobis semen, sicut Sodoma essemus, et similes Gomorrhae fuissemus (Rom. IX).

VERS. 16.-- Cumque multiplicati fueritis. (ID.) Alii hoc ad finem temporum referunt, etc., usque ad sed spiritualem cibum sectabuntur.

VERS. 17. Vocabunt. Qui olim dicebant, Qui sedes super Cherubim, manifestare (Psal. LXXIX), etc. Inter duo Cherubim in propitiatorio Domini majestas apparebat, et loquebatur Moysi et sacerdotibus, sed modo aliter tempore fidei.

VERS. 18.-- In diebus illis. Hoc proprie in Christi adventu completur, cum duodecim tribus simul Evangelio credunt, et terram aquilonis relinquunt et diaboli imperium.

14BVERS. 19.-- In filios. (HIER.) i In numero filiorum qui de gentibus crediderunt. Quotquot enim receperunt eum, dedit eis potestatem filios Dei fieri (Joan. I).

VERS. 20.-- Mulier amatorem. (ID) Semel mista, videns eum, etc., usque ad contempsit Dominum Salvatorem, in perniciem suam.

VERS. 21.-- Vox in viis audita est. Libenter suscipit Dominus poenitentem, occurrit filio inopia et squalore confecto, pristinis vestibus induit, gratiam reddit revertenti, si tamen cum ploratu et ululatu redeat ad patrem.

VERS 22.-- Aversiones vestras. Alii conversiones: quamvis propria voluntate ad eum convertamur, nisi tamen ille nos traxerit, et cupiditatem nostram suo praesidio corroboraverit, non possumus esse 14Csalvi. Intelligimus, et de populo Judaeorum ad eum revertente, et de haereticis qui Dominum dereliquerunt.

VERS. 25.-- Dormiemus. Vox Israel, qui Deum suum non audivit et haeretici poenitentis. Pars enim est salutis peccata nosse et confiteri; unde: Dic tu prius iniquitates tuas, ut justificeris (Ibid.).

CHAPTER III.

VERS. 1.-- It is commonly said: if a man put away. Behold, concerning the woman who has played the harlot and been forsaken, etc., as far as how much the more, being despised, he does not yet disdain to call her.

If a man put away his wife. (HIER.) This we can say against those who, forsaking the faith of the Lord and entangled in the errors of the heretics, after many fornications and the deceiving of their own souls, feign that they return to the former truth, not that they may lay aside the poisons of their breast, but that they may instil them into others.

But thou hast played the harlot. Or: Thou hast had many shepherds unto thy giving of offence, that is, that thou mightest offend God; 13Band they who ought to have been masters, that they might keep others from all error, stood forth as authors of impiety.

Yet return. If thou wert returning to me, saith the Lord. [Vulg. Jer. 3:1 tamen revertere ad me, dicit Dominus, "yet return to me, saith the Lord" — an imperative, where Migne prints the indicative revertebaris behind si] He reproves the shamelessness of the harlot, who after adultery dares to return to her husband.

VERS. 2.-- Lift up thine eyes. According to the anagogy: To those who promise that they will forsake the errors of the heretics, it is commanded that they lift up their eyes straight before them. For unless they shall have seen what is right, they cannot condemn their former depravity.

As a robber. Even as robbers are wont at eventide, etc., as far as and he besets the ways for those going down from Jerusalem to Jericho.

VERS. 3.-- The forehead of a woman. (ID.) This we can 13Csay of the heretics who glory in their errors. Because she said above: I have not sinned, he reproves her as a bold woman who is ashamed to turn aside to no man.

Novate. (ID.) Eradicando vepres et sentes, etc., usque ad non potest animus aerumnis mundi plenus semen Dei suscipere et fructum facere. Circumcidimini. (ID.) 15BVERS. 9.-- Principum. (HIER.) Qui legem docere debuerunt, et subjectos a leone defendere.

VERS. 11.-- Ventus urens. (HIER.) Quando scilicet 15Cpervenerit gladius usque ad animam, etc., usque ad nequaquam populo, sed mihi veniet, ut meum triticum dissipetur.

VERS. 12.-- Spiritus plenus. Quidam hunc locum sic exponunt, ut postquam purgata fuerit area, reliquiae salvae fiant. Unde scriptum est: Spiritus plenitudinis veniet mihi; unde evangelista, De plenitudine ejus omnes accepimus (Joan. I). Et nunc ego. Aposiopesis, sicut ait Virgilius (AENEID.): Quos egos; sed motos praestat componere fluctus. Propheta dicturus prospera, retinet se, et tristibus junxit tristia.

Exercitus diaboli, currus scilicet et equites Pharaonis, quod intelligens vir ecclesiasticus dicit, Vae nobis quoniam vastati sumus. Credens illi sententiae. Cum conversus ingemueris, salvus eris. Post tribum enim Dan succedit terra Ephraim per quam venitur in Jerusalem. Allegorice. (HIER.) Venit judicium Domini in terram Domino delinquentem cum omni ubertate supplicii.

VERS. 16.-- Custodes. Adversarios scilicet qui tam diligentes obsideant, et munitionibus urbem claudant, ut magis vinearum agrorumque custodes, quam adversarios putes. Vult Dominus nationes omnes in circumitu suam nosse sententiam, et flagellata Jerusalem cunctos recipere disciplinam.

VERS. 19.-- Ventrem meum. (ID.) Loquitur hoc Dominus, cum seditionem et discordiam cernit in Ecclesia, et in conventiculis suis clamare perdicem, et in bella converti Dei requiem. Unde sequitur:

Vox prophetae, et per prophetam Dei, dolentis super contritionem populi sui, cujus viscera ad instar hominis lacerantur, Salvator in morte Lazari, et super Jerusalem flevit. Nec dolorem celat silentio, et clangor buccinae et strepitus praeliorum illius turbat affectum, dum mala malis cumulantur, et universa duarum tribuum terra vastatur.

Quidquid secundum historiam de Judaea et Jerusalem dicitur, ad Ecclesiam Dei referamus: quae cum Deum offenderit, et vitiis et persecutione vastata fuerit, ubi quondam virtutum cohors et laetitia, ibi multitudo peccatorum et moeror versetur.

Hoc totum ad Ecclesiam potest referri, cum Deum offenderit, et adversariis tempore persecutionis, vel vitiis atque peccatis tradita fuerit.

CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 1.-- If thou wilt return. (HIER.) The Seventy: If Israel shall be converted, saith the Lord, let him be converted to me. Another reading, converteris, that is, if thou desirest salvation, and sayest that thou hast sinned, be fully converted, that is, believe him whom thou hast denied. If thou wilt take away. It is plain, because when we are moved and say: But my feet were almost moved, my steps had well nigh slipped (Psal. LXXII). 14DNot out of the weakness of nature do we suffer this, but because we set our stumbling-blocks and our idols against God.

VERS. 2.-- And thou shalt swear, the Lord liveth, etc. (ID.) For a reproach, namely, of the dead gods, by whom every idolater swears.

Break up anew. (ID.) By rooting out the briers and thorns, etc., as far as a mind full of the troubles of the world cannot receive the seed of God and bring forth fruit. Be ye circumcised. (ID.) It is commanded to the man of Juda and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem that they forsake the letter that killeth, and follow the spirit that quickeneth. Lest perhaps. (ID.) He warns and foretells, lest he be compelled to do that which we approve in the Ninivites, to whom the sentence was foretold, that by repentance they might turn aside the impending wrath.

15AVERS. 7.-- The lion is come up. (HIER.) If any man is a favourer or an author of perverse teachings, of him it can be said, the lion is come up out of his den, etc.

And the robber. Of whom it is said, He shall have dominion over all his enemies (Psal. IX). Who glories, saying: I have gone round about the earth, and walked through it (Job II). For who is there whom the poisons of the devil touch not, save he who says: For the prince of this world cometh, and in me he hath not any thing? (Job. XIV.) Out of his own place. Out of the abyss, in which he is to be bound, into which he entreats that he be not sent.

VERS. 8.-- For this. Without repentance we cannot escape the most savage beast, and unless we be converted to God not only in mind, but also in body. For while the Church is laid waste, the wrath of God is open.

15BVERS. 9.-- Of the princes. (HIER.) Who ought to have taught the law, and to have defended their subjects from the lion.

The priests. Who are thought to be wise: For God hath made foolish the wisdom of this world, because through it they knew not God. The prophets shall be amazed, etc. Aquila, they shall be out of their mind. For who would not go mad, who would not lose heart, when he shall have beheld his princes and kings and priests, once his own, under the lion.

VERS. 10.-- And I said: Alas, alas, alas, Lord God. Because he said above, in that time they shall call Jerusalem the throne of the Lord, etc., and now he says, the heart of the king shall perish, etc., the prophet is troubled, and thinks that God has lied to him, and does not understand that the one is to be long after, this in the time next at hand.

VERS. 11.-- A burning wind. (HIER.) When, namely, 15Cthe sword shall have reached unto the soul, etc., as far as by no means to the people, but to me shall it come, that my wheat may be scattered.

VERS. 12.-- A full spirit. Certain men so expound this passage, that after the threshing-floor has been cleansed, the remnant may be saved. Whence it is written: The spirit of fulness shall come to me; [Vulg. Jer. 4:12 Spiritus plenus ex his veniet mihi, "a full wind out of these shall come to me"] whence the evangelist, Of his fulness we all have received (Joan. I). And now I. An aposiopesis, as Virgil says (AENEID.): Whom I egos; but it is better to settle the troubled waves. The prophet, being about to speak prosperous things, holds himself back, and has joined sad things to sad.

VERS. 13.-- Behold as, etc. He beholds things to come as though present; and he describes the army of the Babylonians, 15Dthe din of whose chariots and wheels is compared to a most savage tempest, and the swiftness of whose horses to eagles. The army of the devil, that is, the chariots and horsemen of Pharao, which the churchman understanding says, Woe to us, because we are laid waste. Believing that saying. When thou shalt have been converted and shalt have groaned, thou shalt be saved. Woe to us, because the prophet has pointed it out as with a finger, the people groans. And it perceives them not as things to come, but as already done, saying: Woe to us.

VERS. 14.-- Wash from wickedness. The prophet answers, nay rather the Lord in the prophet, Wash. Whence: Wash yourselves, be clean (Isa. I), that is, with the water of baptism, with the water of saving repentance.

VERS. 15.-- For a voice of one declaring. He speaks according to the situation 16Aof Judaea. For the tribe of Dan, beside Mount Libanus and the city which is now called Paneas, looks toward the north, whence Nabuchodonosor was to come. From the mountain. For after the tribe of Dan follows the land of Ephraim, through which one comes into Jerusalem. Allegorically. (HIER.) The judgment of the Lord comes upon the land that offends against the Lord, with all abundance of punishment.

VERS. 16.-- Watchers. Adversaries, namely, who so diligently beset it, and shut in the city with fortifications, that thou wouldst think them keepers of vineyards and fields rather than adversaries. The Lord wills that all the nations round about know his sentence, and that, Jerusalem being scourged, all men receive discipline.

VERS. 18.-- Thy ways. (HIER.) Whatever of evil befalls us, etc., as far as how much less against men 16Bonce of the city of God?

VERS. 19.-- My bowels. (ID.) The Lord speaks this, when he sees sedition and discord in the Church, and the partridge crying in her conventicles, and the rest of God turned into wars. Whence there follows:

VERS. 21.-- How long shall I see. The voice of the prophet, and through the prophet of God, grieving over the breaking of his people, whose bowels are torn after the manner of a man, the Saviour wept at the death of Lazarus, and over Jerusalem. Nor does he hide his grief in silence, and the blare of the trumpet and the din of battles disturbs his affection, while evils are heaped upon evils, and the whole land of the two tribes is laid waste.

VERS. 22.-- For my people is foolish. (HIER.) Because 16Cthe princes of my people have not known, etc., as far as whom he ever desired to see? They are wise, etc. (ID.) Malicious wise men, etc., as far as it is called not wisdom, but craft and cunning.

VERS. 23.-- I beheld the earth. The prophet discerns in spirit the things which are to come, that the people, hearing, may be terrified and do penance, that it may not suffer what it dreads. Whatever is said according to the history concerning Judaea and Jerusalem, let us refer to the Church of God: which, when it shall have offended God, and been laid waste by vices and by persecution, where once was the company of virtues and gladness, there may a multitude of sins and mourning be found.

VERS. 27.-- For thus saith. (HIER.) The clemency of the Lord is mingled with wrath: the land is forsaken, but it is not consumed, that there may remain those who understand his clemency 16D.

VERS. 28.-- I have thought and have not repented, etc. That I might take away the sentence foretold, and that the wrath of him that rages might not come to its end; the Lord threatened through Jonas, but the greatness of tears and groanings overcame the impending sword.

VERS. 29.-- At the voice of the horseman. He describes the army of the raging ones, for fear of whom the people left the city and climbed the steep places: but yet it could not turn aside the wrath of God. All this can be referred to the Church, when it shall have offended God, and been delivered over to adversaries in the time of persecution, or to vices and sins.

VERS. 30.-- But thou being laid waste. (HIER.) This same thing is to be said against those who have lost the affections of wedlock and 17Athe chastity of the true faith. When thou shalt clothe thyself. For these things thou preparest even for thy lovers, and a narrow bed cannot hold them both. Nor does God receive the ornaments with which before thou didst please thy lovers.

VERS. 31.-- For a voice as of, etc. (HIER.) Two examples are set down, both of a woman in travail and of one mourning, that whatever a woman suffers, either in her offspring or in the death of her children, Jerusalem may suffer in her peoples.

CAPUT V.

VERS. 1.-- Circuite vias. (HIER.) Grandis amor justitiae ut non juxta interrogationem Abrahae, et responsionem Dei, quod pro decem viris justis liberet Deus civitatem, jamjamque perituram; sed si unum 17Binvenerit, qui faciat judicium, et quaerat fidem.

VERS. 3.-- Domine, oculi tui. (ID.) Damnantur opera Judaeorum (in quibus juxta legis caeremonias exsultabant) per fidem Christianorum, per quam gratia salvi facti sumus. Percussisti eos. (GREG.) Omnis divina percussio, etc., usque ad et est unum flagellum quod temporaliter incipit, et aeternis consummatur suppliciis.

Et non doluerunt. Inferuntur supplicia ut corrigantur vitia, sed non emendatur Jerusalem nec per tormenta.

VERS. 4.-- Forsitan pauperes sunt. (HIER.) Sicut illud, Mittam filium meum, forsitan ipsum verebuntur (Matth. XXI). Utitur Deus sermone dubitantis, ut ex ambiguitate sententiae et verborum suspensione 17Cliberum hominis monstretur arbitrium.

VERS. 5.-- Ecce magis, etc. Juxta tropologiam. Qui magni putantur in Ecclesia, quia solvunt jugum et rumpunt vincula, traduntur in ignominias passionum, ut faciant quae non conveniunt. Vincula. (HIER.) Praeceptorum Dei, non Pharisaeorum, de quibus dicitur: Dirumpamus vincula eorum et projiciamus a nobis jugum ipsorum (Psal. I).

VERS. 6.-- Pardus vigilans. Alexandri impetum significat, qui subditis sibi variis gentibus, quasi variatus pardus contra Medos Persasque arma corripuit. Quia vero non de futuro, sed de praeterito, vel jamjamque de futuris historiam texit, ideo de Romano tacet imperio.

17DVERS. 7.-- Saturavi eos. (HIER.) Audiat hoc qui acceptis divitiis luxuriae serviunt.

VERS. 8.-- Equi amatores. (ID.) Equis, cum non vident jumentum, etc., usque ad Assimilatus est jumentis insipientibus et similis factus est illis (Psal. XLVIII).

VERS. 10.-- Et dissipate. (ID.) Audiat Ecclesia quam cito muri, etc., usque ad propter clementiam judicis, non propter meritum peccantium.

VERS. 12.-- Negaverunt Dominum, etc. (ID.) Audiat hoc Ecclesia negligens: et providentiam Dei refutans, quod et gladium et famem sustineat, nisi ventura crediderit quae dicuntur.

VERS. 14.-- Ecce ego do, etc. (ID.) Ut increduli 18Asermone tuo crementur, etc., usque ad si supra fundamentum Christi aedificaverimus.

VERS. 15.-- Ignorabis linguam, etc. (ID.) In Hebraeo: Non intelliges quid loquatur. Est enim malorum solatium, si habeas hostes, quos rogare valeas, qui intelligant preces tuas.

VERS. 17.-- Et comedet segetes, etc. Vastitatem terrae describit, interfectionem multorum, abactionem pecorum, subversionem urbium et murorum, quod gladio hostili cuncta capiantur.

VERS. 18.-- Verumtamen, etc. (HIER.) Reliquiae scilicet salvae fient, vel eorum qui in Babylonem ducti sunt, vel relicti ad culturam terrae Judaeae; vel eorum qui post persecutionis ardorem, vel fuga, vel confessione fidem Deo servaverunt.

18BVERS. 19.-- Quod si dixeritis. (ID.) Tropologice. Potest hoc super haereticis accipi, etc., usque ad habitate, imo servite his quorum deos colitis.

VERS. 21.-- Audi, popule stulte. Proprie ad Judam loquitur et ad domum Jacob. Israel enim multo jam tempore exsulabat in Assyriis. Qui non habes cor. Dat intelligi quod absque praecepto naturaliter debemus intelligere quae recta sunt. Qui habentes, etc. Cui simile: oculos habent, et non videbunt, etc., Similes illis fiant qui faciunt ea, et omnes qui confidunt in eis (Psal. CXIII).

VERS. 25.-- Iniquitates vestrae, etc. (HIER.) Si quando mare transcendat terminos, etc., usque ad in quibus operariis vineae unum aeternae praemium promittitur.

18CVERS. 27.-- Sicut decipula. Dum invicem se venantur ad mortem, et aliorum damnis atque dispendiis domos suas complent, secundum illam philosophorum sententiam, Omnis dives, aut iniquus, aut haeres iniqui. Atque utinam haec tantum ab eis fiant qui foris sunt, et non a nobis, et venientium ad nos, non manus contemplentur, sed ora.

VERS. 28.-- Incrassati sunt, etc. Cui simile: Incrassatus, impinguatus, dilatatus, dereliquit Deum factorem suum (Deut. XXXII). Et: Incrassatus est dilectus et recalcitravit (Ibid.). Et impinguati, etc. In divinis confisi: quasi dicat: Anima, multa bona habes posita in annos plurimos: requiesce, comede et bibe (Luc. XII).

CHAPTER V.

VERS. 1.-- Go about the ways. (HIER.) Great is the love of justice, that it is not according to the questioning of Abraham and the answer of God, that for ten just men God should set free the city, and that already on the point of perishing; but if he shall 17Bfind one who doeth judgment and seeketh faith.

VERS. 3.-- O Lord, thine eyes. (ID.) The works of the Jews (in which they exulted according to the ceremonies of the law) are condemned through the faith of Christians, through which by grace we have been saved. Thou hast struck them. (GREG.) Every divine striking, etc., as far as and there is one scourge which begins in time, and is consummated in eternal punishments.

And they have not grieved. Punishments are brought in that vices may be corrected, but Jerusalem is not amended, not even through torments.

VERS. 4.-- Perhaps they are poor. (HIER.) As that, I will send my son, perhaps they will reverence him (Matth. XXI). [Vulg. Matt. 21:37 Verebuntur filium meum, without "perhaps"; the word stands at Luc. 20:13] God uses the speech of one doubting, that out of the ambiguity of the sentence and the suspending of the words 17Cthe free will of man may be shown.

VERS. 5.-- Behold rather, etc. According to the tropology. They who are thought great in the Church, because they loose the yoke and break the bonds, are delivered over to the shame of the passions, that they may do the things which are not fitting. Bonds. (HIER.) Of the precepts of God, not of the Pharisees, of which it is said: Let us break their bonds asunder, and let us cast away their yoke from us (Psal. I).

VERS. 6.-- A leopard watching. It signifies the onset of Alexander, who, when divers nations had been subdued to him, took up arms against the Medes and Persians like a spotted leopard. But because he weaves his history not of the future, but of the past, or of things now on the very point of coming, therefore he is silent concerning the Roman empire.

17DVERS. 7.-- I fed them to the full. (HIER.) Let him hear this who, riches having been received, serve luxury.

VERS. 8.-- Lovers of horses. (ID.) To horses, when they do not see a beast of burden, etc., as far as He was likened to senseless beasts, and was made like to them (Psal. XLVIII). [Vulg. Psal. 48:13 Comparatus est jumentis insipientibus, "he was compared to senseless beasts"]

VERS. 10.-- And scatter. (ID.) Let the Church hear how quickly the walls, etc., as far as because of the clemency of the judge, not because of the merit of the sinners.

VERS. 12.-- They have denied the Lord, etc. (ID.) Let the Church, negligent and rejecting the providence of God, hear this: that she shall suffer both the sword and famine, unless she shall have believed the things to come which are spoken.

VERS. 14.-- Behold I give, etc. (ID.) That the unbelieving 18Amay be burned up by thy word, etc., as far as if we shall have built upon the foundation of Christ.

VERS. 15.-- Thou shalt not know the tongue, etc. (ID.) In the Hebrew: Thou shalt not understand what he speaketh. For it is a solace in evils, if thou hast enemies whom thou art able to entreat, who understand thy prayers.

VERS. 17.-- And he shall eat up the corn, etc. He describes the waste of the land, the slaying of many, the driving off of the cattle, the overthrow of cities and of walls, in that all things are taken by the sword of the enemy.

VERS. 18.-- Nevertheless, etc. (HIER.) The remnant, namely, shall be saved, either of those who were led into Babylon, or of those left for the tillage of the land of Judaea; or of those who after the heat of persecution, whether by flight or by confession, kept faith with God.

18BVERS. 19.-- But if you shall say. (ID.) Tropologically. This can be taken of the heretics, etc., as far as dwell, nay rather serve those whose gods you worship.

VERS. 14.-- And they healed the breach. (ID.) As if he should say: When they were doing such great evils, etc., as far as rather making them ready for punishment and for the anger of God.

VERS. 15.-- They were confounded. (ID.) This is to be read more expressly, etc., as far as nay rather they increased their sin by contempt.

They knew not. They would not; but by excessive contempt and by the vice of violent evil they could not even understand. A great impiety, not only not to beware of sins, but not even to be willing to understand them, and to have no distinction 19Dbetween good and evil works.

XIV), whence, Stand ye upon the ways, etc.

The voice of the trumpet, the commandments of the Gospel or the teaching of the apostles: whence, Go up upon a high mountain, thou that bringest good tidings to Sion, lift up thy voice like a trumpet, thou that announcest to Jerusalem. All these. (GREG., Moral., l. XVIII, c. 14). They are princes departing from the Lord and disobedient, etc., as far as he who is at variance with the unity of the Church loveth not his neighbour.

CHAPTER IX.

VERS. 1.-- Who will give. (HIER.) The king of Jerusalem, the city being besieged by the enemies, etc., as far as but this can be understood as well in the person of the Lord as in the person of the prophet.

VERS. 2.-- Who will give me, etc. As if he should say: It is better to dwell in the wilderness than to abide among such great crimes of men. Whence in the Gospel: How long shall I suffer you? (Matth. XVII.) And elsewhere: He that 23Aunderstandeth shall sit still, for the time is very evil (Thren. III; Mich. II).

VERS. 3.-- Because from evil, etc. (HIER.) Sinners pass over from evil to evil, etc., as far as and the tongue of them that arm themselves unto blasphemy.

VERS. 4.-- Every one, etc. (GREG.) The crafty enemy, when he sees himself driven out from the hearts of the good, seeks out those who are greatly loved by them, and, flattering, speaks through the words of those who are loved more than the rest, so that, while the force of love pierces the heart, the sword of persuasion may easily break through to the inmost parts of the inward uprightness. Of his neighbour. (HIER.) Because a man's enemies are they of his own household (Mich. VI). The favour of brethren also is rare. And the father shall deliver up the son, and the son the father. And two against three, and three against two shall be divided (Luc. XII).

23BVERS. 5.-- They have laboured to do wickedly. (GREG.) As if it were openly said: They who could without labour have been friends of the truth, labour that they may sin: and while they refuse to live simply, they exact by labours that they should die.

VERS. 7.-- And I will try them. (HIER.) In the furnace of tribulation. The furnace trieth the potter's vessels (Eccli. XXVII): that whatsoever of adulterate matter is in us may be burnt away. For the silver of the Lord is tried by the fire, purged from the earth, refined seven times (Psal. XI).

VERS. 9.-- Shall it be for these things (ID.) He often repeats this little verse, that, their evils being enumerated, he may infer that he does justly what he does.

VERS. 10.-- Upon the mountains. (ID.) Tropologically. Upon 23Cthe mountains weeping is taken up, etc., as far as they who can ascend on high, even to the simple, have gone back from God.

VERS. 11.-- And I will give Jerusalem. (ID.) When the ecclesiastical teachers fail, etc., as far as and he who saith, I will dwell in them and walk among them (Lev. XXVI).

VERS. 14.-- Of their own heart. Not in our own heart, but in the Lord must we trust. The heart of man is depraved, out of which come forth depraved thoughts.

VERS. 15.-- Therefore thus saith the Lord. It can be prophesied of the near time, in which they were taken captive by the Chaldeans: and properly of this, in which they are dispersed among all the nations and scattered over the whole world.

VERS. 16.-- The sword. (HIER.) Either material or spiritual, by which they are divided, that they may not consent unto evil, 23Dbut that in this, that they are evil, they may perish.

VERS. 17.-- And call ye. Call the mourning women because of the captivity to come, etc., as far as with modulated voice they may melt the people into tears.

VERS. 18.-- Our eyes. Therefore: or God himself joins himself to the affection of one suffering with them, so that what the people endures, he himself is said to endure and to feel.

VERS. 19.-- How are we wasted. Let the crowds of believers say this in persecution, which were therefore laid waste and confounded, because they forsook the law of the Lord, and abandoned their tabernacles.

VERS. 20.-- Hear therefore. Above he said: call the mourning women, etc. Now he addresses them as though present, to the reproach of the priests, and of the teachers, and of 24Aall men: so that, they ceasing from teaching, the women may hear the word of the Lord.

VERS. 21.-- For death is come up. Because so great will be the strength and the swiftness of the enemies, that they will not wait for the door, but will climb up through the windows and the roofs.

VERS. 22.-- Speak: Thus saith, etc. (HIER.) The Hebrew word which is written with three letters, etc., as far as so that there is none to bury them that fall.

VERS. 24.-- Who do mercy. (ID.) Although certain things seem unjust, yet all things, etc., as far as He that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord (II Cor. X).

VERS. 25.-- Behold the days shall come. (ID.) Many of the nations, and chiefly those bordering upon Judaea and Palestine, etc., as far as for circumcision profiteth not, unless thou keep the law.

Confusus est. (RAB.) Demonstrata subversione Babylonis, redit propheta ad auctorem tanti judicii: quia non est potestas nisi a Deo, qui dominatur in regno hominum, et cui voluerit dabit illud.

VERS. 25.-- Ecce ego, etc. Mons pestifer, Nabuchodonosor, et regnum Chaldaeorum; vel diabolus, qui alibi mons caliginosus, propter tenebras erroris, dicitur. Et dabo te. Ad hunc montem pertinent Judaei, haeretici, blasphemi, et omnes impii, qui perdurant in malitia, nec convertuntur ut fiant lapides vivi in aedificio Dei.

60DVERS. 30.-- Cessaverunt, etc. Veniente Cyro et Dario cessavit robur Babylonis et principum ejus.

VERS. 33.-- Filia Babylonis, etc. Fuit plebs Babylonis, quando per potentiam regni et divitiarum abundantiam triturabat et conterebat gentes: sed tempus messionis ejus venit cum cogitur recipere quod gessit, de qua ex persona Judaicae plebis consequenter dicitur: Comedit me et devoravit me Nabuchodonosor, rex Babylonis.

Et desertum faciam mare, etc. (RAB.) Mystice mare hujus saeculi significat turbulentiam, quod Dominus desertum ponit. Faciet enim cessare luxum ejus, et dissipabuntur iniqui, qui confusioni errorum vitiorumque deserviebant.

61AVERS. 41.-- Quomodo capta est Sesach. (RAB.) Sesach, byssus sacci, vel manus sacci interpretatur: sicut Babylon confusio, etc., usque ad ut egrediantur de societate eorum, ne cum illis pereant.

VERS. 45.-- Egredimini de medio ejus. (ID.) Qui vult animam suam salvam facere, etc., usque ad sed in meditatione legis ejus et exercitio virtutum strenui.

VERS. 52.-- Propterea, etc. (ID.) Enumerat plagas Babylonis superventuras et optimatibus ejus, ducibus et magistratibus, et omni populo, etc., usque ad Exite de illa, populus meus, ne participes sitis delictorum ejus, et de plagis ejus non accipiatis (Apoc. XVIII), etc.

VERS. 64.-- Sic submergetur Babylon. Nam secundum 61Bhistoriam Babylon in Euphraten mersa est, quamvis in solitudinem redacta. Sed spiritualis Babylonis aeternum interitum praedicit, quae in profundum inferni cum principe diabolo demergetur. Unde Joannes: Sustulit unus angelus fortis lapidem quasi molarem magnum, et misit in mare, dicens: Hoc impetu mittetur Babylon (Apoc. XVIII).

CHAPTER LI.

VERS. 5.-- For it was not, etc. Because the Lord sent them for the vengeance of his people, whom he chastised not by correcting, but crushed by raging: whence, Woe to Assur, the rod of my fury (Isa. X).

VERS. 6.-- Flee ye out of the midst, etc. After the plagues of Babylon he turns his exhortation 59Cto the Jews historically, to the faithful mystically, that they may flee out of the midst of Babylon, that is, of worldly confusion.

(ORIG.) Not by degrees, that is to say, not step by step, but swiftly go ye out, whosoever have a confounded soul, etc., as far as Likewise, when the sins are lesser, God uses ministers: but when they are graver, then he himself hastens to the retribution.

VERS. 7.-- A golden cup, etc. The golden cup of Babylon is not always even in the hand of the Lord; for when it shall have come for vengeance and shall have been placed in the hand of the Lord, there shall be brought about the earth which was made in Job. Nor is it continually held in the hand of the Lord; but then only, when the Lord shall restore to her what she deserves.

(ORIG.) A golden cup. Nabuchodonosor, wishing to deceive men by the cup of Babylon, mingled it not 59Din an earthen vessel, etc., as far as often have I seen a golden cup in the fair adornment of speech, and, considering the poisons of its doctrines, I have detected the cup of Babylon.

Making drunk, etc. (ID.) See all men drunken; we are made drunk with anger and with sadness: we are made drunk, etc., as far as by him are we all made drunk so long as we are earth.

Of the wine. (ID.) As in those who drink bodily wine beyond measure we see the motion of a drunken body, etc., as far as the nations drank, therefore are they moved.

VERS. 8.-- Suddenly is Babylon fallen. It signifies that the consummation of the world shall be sudden, which shall come to pass not by parts but on a sudden, just as Jericho, falling, perished quickly or suddenly. But if thou come to the advent of Christ, and 60Asee his great work, how he overthrew all the doctrines of the Gentiles, that he might take believers out from the yoke of error, thou shalt see that in his passion Babylon suddenly fell and was crushed; let each man see that Babylon has fallen in his own breast. For in whose heart she has not fallen, to him Christ has not yet come: Babylon falls, Jerusalem the holy city of God is built.

Howl ye. (HIER.) Because no soul is incurable with God, he gives counsel to those who are able, etc., as far as but because she would not be healed, the good physicians do their part and say:

VERS. 9.-- We have healed. (ORIG.) See lest at any time God command the angels to prepare medicines for thy languishing soul, etc., as far as God brings forth his judgment to humble that which is exalted.

60BVERS. 16.-- Who lifteth up the clouds, that is, preachers and prophets, who defend us from the heat of the judgment to come by the shower of their eloquence, when they set forth the things to come. Lightnings. The preachers of the prophets are lightnings, when they strike the enemies: rains, when they wash the devout from sins, and pour in the shower of salvation: whence spiritual fruits may germinate, and grow together into a ripe harvest. And he bringeth forth the winds out of the treasures. [Vulg. Jer. 51:16 et produxit ventum de thesauris suis, "he brought forth the wind out of his treasures"] The winds are the apostles, whose preaching runs through the world like a most swift wind: of whom it is said elsewhere: He hath made his arrows for them that burn (Psal. VII). For to those same winds wings are attributed because of the swiftness of their coursing; whence: He flew upon the wings of the winds (Ibid. XI).

VERS. 17.-- Every man is become a fool. Rightly after the overthrow of Babylon does he rebuke the makers of idols, 60Cbecause through the folly of the human mind works vain and worthy of laughter have been made: because there is neither spirit in them, nor voice, nor any efficacy: whence they can deliver neither themselves nor their worshippers in the day of visitation.

Every founder is confounded. (RAB.) The overthrow of Babylon being shown, the prophet returns to the author of so great a judgment: because there is no power but from God, who rules in the kingdom of men, and will give it to whomsoever he will.

VERS. 25.-- Behold, I, etc. The destroying mountain, Nabuchodonosor, and the kingdom of the Chaldeans; or the devil, who elsewhere is called a dark mountain, because of the darkness of error. And I will make thee. To this mountain belong the Jews, heretics, blasphemers, and all the impious, who persevere in malice, and are not converted that they may become living stones in the building of God.

60DVERS. 30.-- They have ceased, etc. When Cyrus and Darius came, the strength of Babylon and of her princes ceased.

VERS. 33.-- The daughter of Babylon, etc. There was a people of Babylon, when through the power of her kingdom and the abundance of her riches she threshed and ground down the nations: but the time of her harvest came, when she is compelled to receive what she has done, of whom it is consequently said in the person of the Jewish people: He hath eaten me, and devoured me, Nabuchodonosor, king of Babylon.

And I will make the sea a desert, etc. (RAB.) Mystically the sea signifies the turbulence of this world, which the Lord sets as a desert. For he shall make its riot to cease, and the wicked shall be scattered, who were slaves to the confusion of errors and of vices.

61AVERS. 41.-- How is Sesach taken. (RAB.) Sesach is interpreted the fine linen of sackcloth, or the hand of sackcloth: as Babylon, confusion, etc., as far as that they may go out from their fellowship, lest they perish with them.

VERS. 45.-- Go ye out of the midst of her. (ID.) He who wishes to make his soul safe, etc., as far as but strenuous in the meditation of his law and in the exercise of the virtues.

VERS. 52.-- Therefore, etc. (ID.) He enumerates the plagues of Babylon that shall come upon her, and upon her nobles, her leaders and magistrates, and all her people, etc., as far as Go out from her, my people, that ye be not partakers of her sins, and that ye receive not of her plagues (Apoc. XVIII), etc.

VERS. 64.-- So shall Babylon be sunk. For according to 61Bthe history Babylon was sunk [mersa; read non mersa, "was not sunk"] in the Euphrates, although reduced to a wilderness. But he foretells the everlasting destruction of the spiritual Babylon, which shall be plunged into the depth of hell with the devil her prince. Whence John: A strong angel took up a stone, as it were a great millstone, and cast it into the sea, saying: With this violence shall Babylon be cast (Apoc. XVIII).

CAPUT LII.

VERS. 1.-- Filius viginti. Historia quae captum refert Sedeciam, et Jerusalem a Chaldaeis vastatam, in libro Regum plene tota continetur.

VERS. 4.-- Factum est autem. Novenarius minor denario imperfectionem legis significat, etc., usque ad tenebris peccatorum vallati fugiunt, et videntes lupum venientem oves dimittunt.

61CVERS. 7.-- Per viam portae. Latenter arguit inertiam doctorum, qui inter muros duorum Testamentorum constituti, non bellum gerere, sed effugere quaerunt, et deliciis magis affluere (quas hortus signat) quam scuto fidei hostibus resistere.

VERS. 8.-- Persecutus est, etc. (RAB.) In libro Regum ita legitur: Fugit Sedecias per viam, etc. Malignis spiritibus populum circumdantibus, rector non fugit, etc., usque ad Apprehensum ergo regem duxerunt ad regem Babylonis in Reblatha, etc.

VERS. 11.-- Et adduxit eum rex, etc. Quia anima in confusione peccatorum vincta compedibus vitiorum, per malignos spiritus ducitur in domum carceris usque ad diem mortis, et in angustia mentis 61Dincluditur, ut mortis sententiam exspectet.

VERS. 12.-- In mense autem quinto, etc. Congruit ordo temporis cum ratione vindictae: Mense quinto vastatur civitas, quae Pentateuchum despexit, decima die mensis, quia decalogo legis contraria fecit, blandiens sibi de securitate pro sanctorum locorum habitatione; unde supra: Nolite confidere, etc.

(GREG.) Dum Scripturae Sedeciae captivitatem narrat, 62Aordinem captivitatis internae denuntiat, etc., usque ad Quandoque enim et lumen totius rationis clauditur, quia pravo usu et iniquitates suae multitudine gravatur.

Venit Nabuzardan, etc. (RAB.) Justo judicio Dei venit Nabuzardan, princeps exercitus regis Babylonis, super Jerusalem, et succendit, etc., usque ad omnem domum comburit igni, id est, cujusque conscientiam flamma illiciti amoris.

De pauperibus, etc. (ID.) Eos, qui utiles verbo et exemplo esse poterant, etc., usque ad sed spinae et tribuli vitiorum.

VERS. 20.-- Columnas quoque, etc. Per diversa vasa, diversa officia in Ecclesia significantur, quae maligni spiritus de servitio Dei auferunt, et in usum 62Bsuum convertunt.

VERS. 21.-- De columnis, etc. (RAB.) Quod facturam columnarum describit cum capitellis, et retiaculis atque malis granatis, ostendit, etc., usque ad legat opus nostrum super librum Regum, ubi quid de his sancti Patres senserint, explanatum est.

VERS. 24.-- Et tulit magister, etc. Etiam fortes in Ecclesia diabolus decipit et captivat; unde: Escae ejus electae (Habac. I), etc.

VERS. 25.-- De civitate tulit, etc. Continentes, scilicet, oleum gratiae et misericordiae (sicut fatuae virgines) in pectore suo non habentes, qui frustra super viros bellatores constituuntur, cum arrogantiam vitare non praemeditantur.

VERS. 26.-- Septem viros, etc. Septiformi Spiritu, 62Cscilicet, sanctificatos. Sed quia gratiam perdiderunt, septem spiritibus nequioribus traditi, non regis coelestis mandata intuuntur, sed regis inferni voluntati obsequuntur. Et scribam, etc. Quem liber Regum Sophar, qui interpretatur dissipans vel dividens, et significat eos qui rudes in Ecclesia, quos ad militiam Christi nutrire debuerant, pravis exemplis dissipant, et a coetu fidelium per errorem sequestrant.

Et sexaginta viros, etc. Stultos, scilicet, cooperatores, qui merito vulgi nomine appellantur, qui utilitatem divini consilii discernere et implere neglexerunt.

VERS. 27.-- Et percussit eos rex Babylonis, etc. Hoc ambit rex Babylonis, ut quos ab Ecclesia evellit, perducat ad interfectionem perpetuae mortis.

62DVERS. 28.-- Iste est populus, etc. (RAB.) Tertio vastavit Nabuchodonosor Judaeam. Primo, cum Joachim, etc., usque ad et retrudit in carcerem inferni, ubi gemunt in perpetuum damnati.

VERS. 31.-- Et factum est. (ID.) Significat admirandam Dei potentiam per bonos et malos aeque sua, etc., usque ad correctum misericorditer respexit, et de poenis liberans quieti restituit et honori.

CHAPTER LII.

VERS. 1.-- The son of twenty. The history which relates that Sedecias was taken, and Jerusalem laid waste by the Chaldeans, is contained entire and in full in the book of Kings.

VERS. 4.-- And it came to pass. The number nine, less than ten, signifies the imperfection of the law, etc., as far as hedged about by the darkness of sins they flee, and, seeing the wolf coming, they forsake the sheep.

61CVERS. 7.-- By the way of the gate. Covertly he reproves the sloth of the teachers, who, set between the walls of the two Testaments, seek not to wage war but to escape, and rather to abound in delights (which the garden signifies) than to withstand the enemy with the shield of faith.

VERS. 8.-- He pursued, etc. (RAB.) In the book of Kings it is thus read: Sedecias fled by the way, etc. When malignant spirits surround the people, the ruler does not flee, etc., as far as Having laid hold of the king therefore, they led him to the king of Babylon into Reblatha, etc.

VERS. 11.-- And the king brought him, etc. Because the soul, bound in the confusion of sins by the fetters of vices, is led by malignant spirits into the house of the prison unto the day of death, and is shut up in the straitness of mind, 61Dthat it may await the sentence of death.

VERS. 12.-- And in the fifth month, etc. The order of the time agrees with the reason of the vengeance: in the fifth month is the city laid waste, which despised the Pentateuch, on the tenth day of the month, because it did things contrary to the decalogue of the law, flattering itself with security on account of its dwelling in the holy places; whence above: Trust ye not, etc.

(GREG.) While the Scripture Scripturae relates the captivity of Sedecias, 62Ait announces the order of the inward captivity, etc., as far as For sometimes even the light of the whole reason is closed up, because it is weighed down by evil use and by the multitude of its own iniquity iniquitates.

Nabuzardan came, etc. (RAB.) By the just judgment of God came Nabuzardan, prince of the army of the king of Babylon, upon Jerusalem, and set it on fire, etc., as far as he burns every house with fire, that is, the conscience of each man with the flame of unlawful love.

Of the poor, etc. (ID.) Those who could be useful by word and by example, etc., as far as but thorns and thistles of vices.

VERS. 20.-- The pillars also, etc. By the divers vessels are signified divers offices in the Church, which the malignant spirits take away from the service of God, and turn to their own 62Buse.

VERS. 21.-- Of the pillars, etc. (RAB.) In that he describes the fashioning of the pillars with the capitals, and the nets and the pomegranates, he shows, etc., as far as let him read our work upon the book of Kings, where what the holy Fathers thought concerning these things has been explained.

VERS. 24.-- And the master took, etc. The devil deceives and takes captive even the strong in the Church; whence: His meats are choice (Habac. I), etc. [Vulg. Habac. 1:16 cibus ejus electus, "his food is choice"]

VERS. 25.-- He took out of the city, etc. The continent, that is to say, not having in their breast the oil of grace and of mercy (like the foolish virgins), who are set in vain over men of war, since they take no forethought to shun arrogance.

VERS. 26.-- Seven men, etc. By the sevenfold Spirit, 62Cthat is to say, sanctified. But because they lost grace, being delivered over to seven other spirits more wicked, they look not upon the commandments of the heavenly king, but obey the will of the king of hell. And the scribe, etc. Whom the book of Kings calls Sophar, who is interpreted scattering or dividing, and he signifies those who scatter by depraved examples the untaught in the Church, whom they ought to have nourished unto the warfare of Christ, and who sequester them from the assembly of the faithful through error.

And sixty men, etc. Fools, that is to say, fellow workers, who are deservedly called by the name of the common crowd, who neglected to discern and to fulfil the profit of the divine counsel.

VERS. 27.-- And the king of Babylon struck them, etc. This the king of Babylon compasses, that those whom he plucks out of the Church he may bring through to the slaying of everlasting death.

62DVERS. 28.-- This is the people, etc. (RAB.) Three times did Nabuchodonosor lay waste Judaea. First, with Joachim, etc., as far as and he thrusts them back into the prison of hell, where the damned groan for ever.

VERS. 31.-- And it came to pass. (ID.) It signifies the wondrous power of God through good and evil alike, his own, etc., as far as him corrected he mercifully regarded, and, freeing him from punishments, restored him to rest and to honour.
