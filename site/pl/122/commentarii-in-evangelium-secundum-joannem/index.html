John Scottus Eriugena

Joannes Scotus Erigena

c. 800 – c. 877

Irish scholar and master at the court of Charles the Bald, and the one Latin writer of his century who read Greek theology in Greek. He translated the Pseudo-Dionysius, Maximus the Confessor and Gregory of Nyssa into Latin, and out of them built his own system in the Periphyseon. His treatise on predestination was condemned in his lifetime and the Periphyseon long after it. His commentary on the Gospel of John survives only in fragments, from a single manuscript at Laon.

Commentaries on the Gospel according to John

1243

1243BSaint Ambrose enumerates in the following order the prayers that were mentioned: prayers, supplications, requests, thanksgivings. Under prayer he places, when we request good things for ourselves, under supplication, when we request absolution from evils, under request, the fight against all adversities. And he prescribes that we ought to know to do this when we come together into one. Saint Augustine has it thus: precations, prayers, interpellations, or, as your codices have, requests, thanksgivings. And he says: all of which, or nearly all, the church frequents in the solemnity of the sacraments. Precation is every prayer before that which is done at the Lord's table; prayer is whatever is done afterwards up to communion; 1244Binterpellation or request is when the people is blessed; thanksgiving concludes all. Where saint Augustine says precations, our codices have supplications. Under supplication, as has been said, the request for the absolution of sins is placed by saint Ambrose. Cruces for this work — where the plate is defective or the reading uncertain →

A short fragment on the kinds of prayer named in 1 Timothy 2:1, comparing how Ambrose and Augustine divide them and how the prayers of the Mass fall under each. Floss printed it among the additions at the end of his 1853 edition. The apparatus at the foot of the Latin is Floss's, not Migne's: his Variae lectiones give the manuscript's reading where he printed something else, and are marked “Floss, var. lect.”
