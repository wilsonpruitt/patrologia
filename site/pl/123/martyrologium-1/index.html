Usuard of Saint-Germain

Usuardus Sangermanensis

d. c. 875

Monk of the abbey of Saint-Germain-des-Prés at Paris. In 858 he travelled to Spain to bring back relics for his house, and came home with those of martyrs of Córdoba. At the request of Charles the Bald he compiled a martyrology for the whole year, drawing on the Hieronymian lists, Bede, Florus of Lyon and Ado of Vienne, and dedicated it to the king. Its brevity made it the most widely copied martyrology of the Middle Ages, and it is the direct ancestor of the Roman Martyrology of 1584.

599ADomino regum piissimo CAROLO USUARDUS, indignus sacerdos ac monachus, perennem in Christo coronam.

Minime vestram latet celsitudinem, o sapientissime princeps, qualiter monemur imitandis antiquorum studiis, ut quidquid in gestis praecipue ecclesiastico dogmati congruis minus perfectum inventum fuerit, debeamus vel pro posse supplere, vel certe suppleri obtare. Quae res pia quidem sollicitudine (vestro, si recordamini, jussu dignanter admoto) me ad hoc, etsi indignum, compulit, quatenus sanctorum sacras atque anniversario recolendas festivitates ex quibusdam praecedentium Patrum Martyrologiis in quamdam colligerem unitatem. Recolebamque in ipsis eorumdem solempnitatibus 599Bquam multos propriae negligentiae excessus, quos etiam purgari tali cupiebam officio. Praeterea et venerabilium Hieronymi scilicet ac Bedae presbyterorum piis, quamvis succinctis, super hoc provocabar descriptis; quorum prior brevitati studens, alter vero quamplures dies intactos relinquens, 600Amulta inveniuntur hujus operis praeterisse necessaria. Quos tamen secutus censui, et Flori viri memorabilis latiora jam in eo ipso negotio sequi vestigia, praesertim in secundo ejusdem libro. Ibi enim multa, quae in priore omiserat, et correxit et addidit. At si quid praeter quod ab illis accepi in hoc opere actum vel mutatum est, sagaci a me indagine id perquisitum agnosci poterit. Unde quod vestrum est obsecro, ut si justum decernitur, propria tuitionis anchora roboretur, quod videlicet 600Bet praesentibus regalis examinis auctoritate, et futuris tantae auctoritatis firmetur examine. Excellentiam magnitudinis vestrae per multa annorum curricula Rex regum Dominus sua dignetur custodire potentia. Amen.

599ATo the most pious lord of kings, CHARLES, USUARDUS, an unworthy priest and monk: an everlasting crown in Christ.

It is by no means hidden from your Highness, O most wise prince, how we are admonished by the zeal of the ancients, worthy of imitation, that whatever in the acts, especially those agreeing with the doctrine of the Church, has been found less than perfect, we ought either to supply as far as we are able, or at least to desire that it be supplied. Which matter, with pious solicitude indeed (your command, if you remember, having been graciously applied), compelled me, though unworthy, to this: that I should gather into a certain unity, out of certain Martyrologies of the preceding Fathers, the sacred festivals of the saints which are to be recalled every year. And I recalled, in their very solemnities, 599Bhow many excesses of my own negligence, which I also desired to be purged by such an office. Besides, I was also provoked to this by the pious, though succinct, writings of the venerable presbyters, namely Jerome and Bede; of whom the former, studying brevity, and the latter, leaving very many days untouched, 600Aare found to have passed over many things necessary to this work. Having followed them, however, I resolved also to follow the broader footsteps, already in this very undertaking, of Florus, a man worthy of memory, especially in the second book of the same. For there he both corrected and added many things which he had omitted in the first. But if anything, beyond what I received from them, has been done or changed in this work, it may be recognized that it was searched out by me with shrewd inquiry. For the rest, most kind one, I have preferred to insert this little preface of my littleness into this little work for this reason: that it might be known that I did not use levity in this endeavour, but rather obeyed your commands, as was fitting, which are wont to consult the benefit of the catholic faithful. Wherefore I beseech what is yours: that, if it be judged just, it may be strengthened by the proper anchor of your protection, namely that 600Bit may be confirmed both for those present by the authority of royal examination, and for those to come by the examination of so great an authority. May the Lord, King of kings, deign to guard the excellence of your greatness with his power through many courses of years. Amen.

Nemo enim in locis sanctorum corporum assistens altari aliquando dixit: Offerimus 600Ctibi, Petre, aut Paule, aut Cypriane; sed quod offertur, offertur Deo, qui martyres coronavit: ut ex ipsorum locorum amore affectus assurgat ad augendam charitatem, et in illos quos imitari possumus, et in illum quo adjuvante possimus. Colimus ergo martyres eo cultu dilectionis et societatis quo et in hac vita coluntur sancti homines Dei, quorum cor ad talem pro evangelica veritate passionem paratum sentimus. Sed illos tanto devotius, quanto securius. 601AQuos etiam fidentiori laude praedicamus jam in vita feliciore victores, quam in ista adhuc usque pugnantes. At vero illo cultu, qui Graece latria dicitur, Latine uno verbo dici non potest, cum sit propria quaedam Divinitati debita servitus, nec colimus, nec colendum docemus, nisi unum Deum. Cum autem ad hunc cultum pertineat oblatio sacrificii, unde idololatrae dicuntur qui hoc etiam idolis exhibent, nullo modo aliquid tale offerimus aut offerendum praecipimus, vel cuiquam martyri, vel cuiquam sanctae animae, vel cuiquam angelo. Et quisquis in hunc errorem delabitur, corripitur per sanam doctrinam, sive ut corrigatur, sive ut condemnetur; dum etiam ipsi sancti, vel homines, vel angeli exhiberi 602Asibi nolunt, quod uni Deo deberi norunt. Apparuit hoc in Paulo et Barnaba, cum, commoti miraculis quae per eos facta sunt, Lycaonii tanquam diis immolare voluerunt. Conscissis enim vestimentis suis confitentes et persuadentes se deos non esse, ista sibi fieri vetuerunt (Act. XIV, 14). Apparuit et in angelis, sicut et in Apocalypsi legimus angelum se adorari prohibentem atque dicentem adoratori suo: Conservus tuus sum et fratrum tuorum, Deum adora (Apoc. XIX, 10). Honorandi sunt ergo martyres propter imitationem, non adorandi propter religionem. Honorandi sunt charitate, non servitute.

599CThe ancient Fathers ordained that the festivals of the holy apostles or martyrs be celebrated in the mystery of veneration, either to stir up imitation, or that we may be associated with their merits and helped by their prayers (AUG, l. XX, contr. Faust., c. 21). Yet in such a way that we sacrifice to none of the martyrs, but to the very God of the martyrs, although we set up altars in the memorials of the martyrs. For no one standing at the altar in the places of the holy bodies has ever said: We offer 600Cto thee, Peter, or Paul, or Cyprian; but what is offered is offered to God, who crowned the martyrs: so that, from the love of those very places, the affection may rise up to increase charity, both toward those whom we are able to imitate, and toward him by whose help we may be able. We honour the martyrs, therefore, with that worship of love and fellowship with which holy men of God are also honoured in this life, whose heart we perceive to be prepared for such a passion for the truth of the gospel. But those the more devoutly, the more securely. 601AWhom we also proclaim with more confident praise as victors already in the happier life, than as still fighting in this one. But with that worship which in Greek is called latria, and in Latin cannot be said in one word, since it is a certain service properly owed to the Divinity, we neither honour anyone, nor teach that anyone is to be honoured, save the one God. And since the offering of sacrifice pertains to this worship, whence those are called idolaters who show this also to idols, in no way do we offer any such thing, or enjoin that it be offered, either to any martyr, or to any holy soul, or to any angel. And whoever slips into this error is rebuked by sound doctrine, either that he may be corrected, or that he may be condemned; since even the saints themselves, whether men or angels, do not wish 602Athat to be shown to themselves which they know to be owed to the one God. This appeared in Paul and Barnabas, when the Lycaonians, moved by the miracles which were done through them, wished to sacrifice to them as to gods. For, having torn their garments, confessing and persuading them that they were not gods, they forbade these things to be done to them (Act. XIV, 14). It appeared also in the angels, as also in the Apocalypse we read that an angel forbade himself to be adored, and said to his adorer: I am thy fellow servant and of thy brethren; adore God (Apoc. XIX, 10). The martyrs, therefore, are to be honoured for imitation, not adored for religion. They are to be honoured with charity, not with servitude.

The martyrology of Usuard, monk of Saint-Germain-des-Prés, part one: his dedication to Charles the Bald, a second prologue taken from Augustine on how the saints are to be honoured, and the calendar from January to April. For each day it lists the martyrs and confessors commemorated, with place, date of death, and a line or two on the manner of it. The text is Migne’s reprint of the edition by the Bollandist Jean-Baptiste du Sollier (Sollerius), with the readings of the 1718 Saint-Germain edition of Jacques Bouillart set in square brackets. Only Usuard’s text is translated. On Migne’s pages, most of the space goes to Sollerius’s commentary on each day (his notes, variant readings, observations and supplements from other manuscripts); it is present in Migne and not translated here. Bouillart’s own notes, keyed to the text by a raised letter, were recovered from the printed page and are translated: our digital source does not carry them. Many concern a Saint-Germain manuscript that Bouillart believed to be Usuard’s autograph, and they record, entry by entry, which lines stand on an erasure or in a later hand. Each day is headed as Usuard dates it, by the Roman calendar, with the modern date beside it. May to December follows in Migne’s next volume.
