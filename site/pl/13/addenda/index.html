MONITUM.

Absoluta jam impressione communicatae mihi fuerunt annotationes aliquot et observationes studio atque humanitate viri clar. D. abbatis Terribilini, quas hoc loco adjiciendas operi censui, ne lectores earum beneficio fraudarem.

Existimat igitur vir ille eruditus primo loco, Damasum Romae ortum fuisse, utiturque ad hanc confirmandam opinionem rationibus iis, quas ego attigi. Observat etiam plures esse veteres scriptores a Saraziano, et 417CCoustantio memoratos, qui de eo pontifice agunt, illumque laudant: at ex eis ne unum quidem, qui eum Hispanum dixerit ante Catalogum Romanorum pontificum, quem sub Felice IV conscriptum aiunt, aut Anastasium, a quo Catalogum illum exscriptum putant sed utriusque hujus scriptoris haud magnam esse auctoritatem censet. Affert deinde recentiores ex inclyta gente illa auctores, ac eos ait nullo solido fundamento niti, praetereaque de loco digladiari inter se; postremo ex Breviario Eborensi anno 1548 impresso exscribit orationem, et lectiones Festo S. Damasi accommodatas. En vero hoc loco collecta omnia.

NOTICE.

The printing being already finished, there were communicated to me some annotations and observations, by the zeal and kindness of that distinguished man the lord abbot Terribilini, which I judged should be added to the work in this place, lest I should defraud readers of the benefit of them.

That learned man holds, then, in the first place, that Damasus was born at Rome, and he uses to confirm this opinion the same reasons which I have touched on. He observes also that there are many ancient writers, mentioned by Sarazani and 417Cby Coustant, who treat of that pontiff and praise him: but that among them there is not so much as one who called him a Spaniard before the Catalogue of the Roman Pontiffs, which they say was compiled under Felix IV, or Anastasius, by whom they think that Catalogue was copied out — but he judges that the authority of neither of these writers is great. He then brings forward more recent authors from that renowned nation, and says that they rest on no solid foundation, and moreover fight among themselves about the place; lastly, from the Breviary of Évora printed in the year 1548, he copies out the collect and the lessons appointed for the Feast of St. Damasus. Here, then, in this place, is the whole of it gathered together.

Oratio. --Misericordiam tuam, Domine, quaesumus, nobis interveniente beato confessore tuo atque pontifice Damaso, clementer impende: nostrisque delictis ipsius propitiare suffragiis. P.

Lect. I. --Beatus Damasus, hujus nominis primus, natione Hispanus, patria Vimaranensis, ex Bracarensi provincia, patre Antonio, secundum Hieronymum in ecclesiastica Historia Liberio, secundum vero alios Felici II in pontificatu successit, anno Domini 369, sub Valentiniano et Valente imperatoribus. Sedit annis decem et octo, mensibus duobus, diebus decem. T.

Lect. II. --Ursicinus autem, vel, ut alii legunt, 418BUrsinus, Romanae Ecclesiae diaconus, Damasum sibi praelatum non ferens, in tantum furoris erupit, ut persuaso quodam imperito ac agresti episcopo, collecta turbulentorum et seditiosorum hominum manu, in basilica, quae Sicinini appellatur, episcopum se fieri extorqueret: legibus, ordine, et traditione perversis. Itaque cum non tantum suffragiis, sed etiam vi et armis res ageretur: multi utrimque ceciderunt. Ammianus Marcellinus ejus temporis historicus, refert centum triginta septem uno die, in basilica Sicinini 418Cperemptorum reperta fuisse cadavera. Denique sacerdotum et populi consensu, Damasus confirmatus est: et Ursicinus ejectus ab Urbe, atque ad Neapolitanam Ecclesiam traductus. T.

Lect. III. --Damasus autem de adulterio per invidiam accusatus, facta synodo coram quadraginta quatuor episcopis se purgavit: a quibus innocens absolutus est, et accusatores ejus Concordius et Callixtus diaconi, damnati rejectique ab Ecclesia. Certe beatus Hieronymus, in epistola ad Pammachium, non solum virum egregium, et in Scripturis eruditum, sed virginem etiam virginis Ecclesiae doctorem appellat. T.

Lect. IV. --Tandem pacata Ecclesia, Damasus otio litterario delectatus, Vitas Pontificum omnium, 418Dqui ante se fuere, conscripsit, easque ad Hieronymum misit: et cum multa corpora Sanctorum perquirens invenisset, eorum tumbas carminibus adornavit. Elegans enim in componendis carminibus ingenium habuit. Multa brevia opuscula heroicis versibus composuit: Epistolas item multas. T.

Lect. V. --Templa cultumque divinum auxit magnopere. Duas enim basilicas aedificavit, alteram juxta theatrum, alteram via Ardeatina, ad catacumbas, dedicavit: et Platoniam, ubi corpora apostolorum Petri et Pauli aliquando jacuerant, et corpora sanctorum eo loci sepulta, versibus exornavit, ad posteritatis memoriam. T.

Lect. VI. --Basilicam vero, quam in honorem sancti Laurentii non longe a theatro Pompeiano condiderat, 419Amaximis muneribus ornavit: patina argentea pondo librarum viginti, hama argentea librarum quindecim, scypho argenteo anaglypho librarum decem: calicibus argenteis quinque, coronis argenteis quinque. Domos quoque circa basilicam positas, fundum etiam Papyrianum agri Ferentinatis, fundumque Antonianum, et balnea non longe a templo posita, dono dedit. T.

Lect. VII. --Hic celebrato Romae concilio, Apollinarem Laodicensem et Timotheum discipulum ejus haereseos damnavit: quemadmodum legimus in Epistola ad episcopos Orientales ab eo missa: Legimus et alias ejus synodales epistolas: ut ad episcopos per Illyrium, et ad Paulinum Thessalonicensem episcopum, contra diversas haereses. Hic instituit ut psalmi die noctuque alternis vicibus in Ecclesia canerentur, 419Bchoro in duas partes diviso, et ut in fine psalmorum accineretur, Gloria Patri et Filio, et Spiritui sancto. Mandavit etiam, ut in principio celebrationis missarum, confessio diceretur. T.

Lect. VIII. --Ad ejus quoque instantiam, Hieronymus psalterium juxta septuaginta interpretes emendavit, et per vigilias distinxit: cujus scriptis Damasus primus auctoritatem dedit. Tunc enim Biblia Hieronymi legi coepta sunt, cum prius septuaginta interpretum scripta, tantummodo in pretio essent. Tu.

Lect. IX. --Ordinationes quinquies fecit, ex quibus presbyteros triginta et unum, diaconos undecim, episcopos per diversa loca numero sexaginta duos creavit. Mortuus est octogenarius, tertio idus 419CDecembris: anno secundo Arcadii Augusti. Sepultus est in basilica sua, via Ardeatina, juxta matrem suam, et germanam, et cessavit pontificatus dies unum et triginta. Tu.

From the Breviary of the Church of Évora in Lusitania, printed in type in the year 1548, p. 837.ON THE FEAST OF SAINT DAMASUS, POPE AND CONFESSOR.

  • 417D

Collect. --Thy mercy, O Lord, we beseech thee, mercifully bestow upon us, by the intercession of thy blessed confessor and pontiff Damasus: and be favorable to our offenses by his prayers. P.

Lesson I. --Blessed Damasus, the first of this name, a Spaniard by nation, of Guimarães by native place, from the province of Braga, his father being Antonius, succeeded in the pontificate — according to Jerome in the Ecclesiastical History — Liberius, but according to others Felix II, in the year of the Lord 369, under the emperors Valentinian and Valens. He sat eighteen years, two months, ten days. T.

Lesson II. --But Ursicinus, or, as others read, 418BUrsinus, a deacon of the Roman Church, not bearing that Damasus was preferred before him, broke out into such madness that, having persuaded a certain unskilled and rustic bishop, and having gathered a band of turbulent and seditious men, he extorted for himself to be made bishop in the basilica which is called Sicininus, the laws, the order, and the tradition being overthrown. And so, since the matter was carried on not only by votes but also by force and arms, many fell on both sides. Ammianus Marcellinus, a historian of that time, relates that the bodies of one hundred and thirty-seven men slain in one day were found in the basilica of Sicininus. 418CAt length, by the consent of the priests and the people, Damasus was confirmed; and Ursicinus was cast out of the City and transferred to the Church of Naples. T.

Lesson III. --But Damasus, accused of adultery through envy, cleared himself in a synod held before forty-four bishops; by whom he was acquitted as innocent, and his accusers, the deacons Concordius and Callixtus, were condemned and cast out of the Church. Certainly blessed Jerome, in the letter to Pammachius, calls him not only an excellent man and learned in the Scriptures, but a virgin also, and the teacher of a virgin Church. T.

Lesson IV. --At last, the Church being at peace, Damasus, delighting in literary leisure, wrote the Lives of all the Pontiffs 418Dwho had been before him, and sent them to Jerome; and when, searching out many bodies of the saints, he had found them, he adorned their tombs with verses. For he had an elegant talent in composing verses. He composed many short works in heroic verse, and likewise many Letters. T.

Lesson V. --He greatly increased the temples and the divine worship. For he built two basilicas, and dedicated the one beside the theater, the other on the Ardeatine Way, at the catacombs; and he adorned with verses, for the memory of posterity, the Platonia, where the bodies of the apostles Peter and Paul had once lain, and the bodies of the saints buried in that place. T.

Lesson VI. --And the basilica which he had founded in honor of Saint Lawrence not far from the theater of Pompey, 419Ahe adorned with the greatest gifts: a silver dish of twenty pounds' weight, a silver bucket of fifteen pounds, an embossed silver cup of ten pounds; five silver chalices, five silver crowns. He gave also as a gift the houses set about the basilica, and likewise the Papyrian estate in the territory of Ferentinum, and the Antonian estate, and the baths set not far from the temple. T.

Lesson VII. --He, a council having been held at Rome, condemned for heresy Apollinaris of Laodicea and Timothy his disciple: as we read in the Letter sent by him to the bishops of the East. We read also his other synodal letters: as to the bishops throughout Illyricum, and to Paulinus bishop of Thessalonica, against various heresies. He appointed that the psalms should be sung in the Church by turns day and night, 419Bthe choir being divided into two parts, and that at the end of the psalms there should be sung, Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit. He commanded also that at the beginning of the celebration of Masses the confession should be said. T.

Lesson VIII. --At his instance also Jerome corrected the psalter according to the seventy translators, and divided it by vigils: to whose writings Damasus first gave authority. For then the Bibles of Jerome began to be read, whereas before the writings of the seventy translators alone were held in esteem. Tu.

Lesson IX. --He held ordinations five times, in which he created thirty-one priests, eleven deacons, and bishops in various places to the number of sixty-two. He died at the age of eighty, on the third of the Ides of 419CDecember, in the second year of Arcadius Augustus. He was buried in his own basilica on the Ardeatine Way, beside his mother and his sister, and the pontificate was vacant thirty-one days. Tu.

VARIA DE PATRIA S. DAMASI TESTIMONIA.

Lucius Marineus Siculus de rebus Hisqaniae lib. II, pag. 309, typis Francofurti 1603 (edit. 2, nam prima prodiit anno 1552), haec habet de Matritensi oppido:

Quod a nonnullis Madritum, ab aliis Majoritum appellatur. In cujus circuitu turres numeravimus octo et vigniti supra centum. Est praeterea felicissimum sancti Damasi summi pontificis meritis, qui MAJORITANUS 420Afuisse perhibetur a multis. Habet etiam nunc alumnum virum sanctissimum nomine Isidorum, qui fuit agricola.

Ex Chronico Hispaniae Joannis Vasaei Brugensis, quod una cum Marinaeo impressum est iisdem typis Francofurti, haec exscripsi pag. 653:

Sanctus Damasus ordinatur Romanus pontifex, etc. Fuit hic natus VIMARANIS oppido Portugalliae, inter Durium et Minium, tribus leucis a Bracara Augusta, sicut Bracarensis cantat Ecclesia. Vide BREVIARIUM Eborense a RESENDIO nitori suo restitutum.

Ibid., pag. 656:

Damasus papa, etc. Petrus Antonius Beuter TARRACONENSEM fuisse dicit, Marinaeus Siculus MADRITIO civem adscribit. Sed multae, ut diximus, Hispaniae 420BEcclesiae VIMARANENSEM fuisse affirmant.

In tomo II Hispaniae illustratae edit. cit. Francofurti 1603, habetur narratio apologetica de Academiis et doctis viris Hispaniae, auctore Alfonso Garzia, matamoro Hispalensi, qui primum opellam hanc edidit anno 1553: ibique, pag. 809, legitur:

Antecesserunt aetate Osius Cordubensis; et illa unica Hieronymi lux, Damasus MADRITENSIS si qua tamen fides Lucio Siculo citra pudorem debetur, qui ex MANTUA CARPETANUM appellat.

In eodem tomo II Hispaniae Illustratae prostat Responsio I Andreae Resendii (is est auctor Lectionum Breviarii Eborensis) ad Bartholomaeum Kebedium Toletanae Ecclesiae sacerdotem, in qua sic lego, pag. 1004:

Viscella fluvius, Lusitane VIZELLA, dictus, in Avum 420Cmajoris famae fluvium, paulo infra Nervae Trajani Caesaris memoriam, in ipsa Avi ripa perdurantem ingreditur. Intra quorum fluviorum confluentes VIMARANENSIS est civitas, sancti pontificis Damasi quondam patria, si per vestros liceret. Tametsi nondum decennium est, cum prodiit Onuphrii Veronensis viri diligentissimi, de Pontificibus Romanis liber, omnibus Urbis templis, archivis, bibliothecis perquisitis, accuratissime compositus. Is penitus Lusitanum, ac IGAEDITANUM eum facit. SANCTUS, inquit, DAMASUS, ANTONII FILIUS, EGITANENSIS, LUSITANUS HISPANUS. Sed ego EBORENSIS Ecclesiae vetustum Codicem sum secutus, cum ejus vitam BREVIARIO inserui.

Petrus Antonius Beuter, patria Valentinus, edidit Chronicon Hispaniae, Valentino idiomate lucubratum, 420Dquod hactenus non vidi; anno vero 1604 iterum recudit suum Chronicon, et Valentino in Castellanum sermonem translatum, cum titulo hoc: Primera parte de la Cronica General de toda Espana y especialmente del reyno de Valencia, etc. Impressa en Valencia 1604. Ibi, cap. 25, lib. I, pag. 146, legitur:

Despues fue Juliano Apostata treynta y seys emperador, año trezientos sesenta y cinco, y movio persecucion a la Yglesia: mas muriendo el, presto cesso. Era PAPA DAMASO, NATURAL DE TARRAGONA. Siguieron Valentiniano, y Valente, despues Graciano y Valente. Y siguio el gran Theodosio nascido y criado en España.

Henricus Florez Hispanus, Augustinianae familiae 421AAlumnus et magister, tomo IV operis, cui titulus Espana Sagrada, tract. 6, cap. ult., fol. 308, edit. Matritensis, anno 1751, agens de Sanctis Toletanis, haec habet:

VARIOUS TESTIMONIES CONCERNING THE NATIVE COUNTRY OF ST. DAMASUS.

Lucius Marineus Siculus, On the Affairs of Spain, Book II, p. 309, printed at Frankfurt 1603 (edit. 2, nam prima prodiit anno 1552), has these words about the town of Madrid:

Which by some is called Madritum, by others Majoritum. In its circuit we counted one hundred and twenty-eight towers. It is moreover most happy in the merits of Saint Damasus, the supreme pontiff, who 420Ais said by many to have been a MAN OF MAJORITUM. It has even now as its foster son a most holy man by the name of Isidore, who was a husbandman.

From the Chronicle of Spain of Joannes Vasaeus of Bruges, which was printed together with Marineus in the same types at Frankfurt, I have copied these words from p. 653:

Saint Damasus is ordained Roman pontiff, etc. He was born in VIMARANES, a town of Portugal, between the Douro and the Minho, three leagues from Braga Augusta, as the Church of Braga sings. See the BREVIARY of Évora, restored to its own brightness by RESENDE.

Ibid., p. 656:

Pope Damasus, etc. Pedro Antonio Beuter says that he was a man of TARRAGONA; Marineus Siculus enrolls him as a citizen of MADRID. But many Churches of Spain, as we have said, 420Baffirm that he was a man of VIMARANES.

In volume II of Hispania Illustrata, in the cited edition, Frankfurt 1603, there is an apologetic account of the Academies and learned men of Spain, by Alfonso García, of Matamoros of Seville, who first published this little work in the year 1553; and there, on p. 809, one reads:

There went before them in age Osius of Córdoba; and that unique light of Jerome, Damasus OF MADRID — if indeed any credit is owed without shame to Lucius Siculus, who from MANTUA calls him a CARPETANIAN.

In the same volume II of Hispania Illustrata there stands the First Reply of Andrés de Resende (he is the author of the Lessons of the Breviary of Évora) to Bartolomé Quevedo, priest of the Church of Toledo, in which I read thus, on p. 1004:

The river Viscella, in Portuguese called VIZELLA, enters the Ave, 420Ca river of greater fame, a little below the monument of Nerva Trajan Caesar, which still endures on the very bank of the Ave. Between the confluences of these rivers is the city of VIMARANES, once the native country of the holy pontiff Damasus, if it might be allowed by your people. And yet it is not yet ten years since there appeared the book of Onofrio of Verona, that most diligent man, On the Roman Pontiffs, composed most accurately after all the churches, archives, and libraries of the City had been searched through. He makes him thoroughly a Lusitanian, and a man of IGAEDIS. SAINT, he says, DAMASUS, SON OF ANTONIUS, OF EGITANIA, A LUSITANIAN SPANIARD. But I have followed the ancient Codex of the Church of ÉVORA, when I inserted his life into the BREVIARY.

Pedro Antonio Beuter, a Valencian by native place, published a Chronicle of Spain, worked out in the Valencian tongue, 420Dwhich so far I have not seen; but in the year 1604 he printed his Chronicle again, translated too from Valencian into the Castilian speech, with this title: First part of the General Chronicle of all Spain, and especially of the kingdom of Valencia, etc. Printed at Valencia 1604. There, in chapter 25 of Book I, p. 146, one reads:

Afterward Julian the Apostate was the thirty-sixth emperor, in the year three hundred and sixty-five, and he stirred up a persecution against the Church: but when he died, it soon ceased. The POPE WAS DAMASUS, A NATIVE OF TARRAGONA. There followed Valentinian and Valens, and after them Gratian and Valens. And there followed the great Theodosius, born and reared in Spain.

Enrique Flórez, a Spaniard, foster son and master 421Aof the Augustinian family, in volume IV of the work entitled España Sagrada, treatise 6, last chapter, fol. 308, the Madrid edition, of the year 1751, treating of the saints of Toledo, has these words:

XII.

S. DAMASO, a quien algunos hacen natural de Madrid; y por tanto de la diecesi de Toledo: pero le reservamos para otro sitio que juzgamos muy proprio, por no pender de ninguna opinion particular: y entonces se vera, si fue ESPAÑOL.

Ex Vita B. Gonsalvi Amaranthi Ordinis Praedicatorum scripta a Fr. Didaco de Rosario, de mandato Fr. Bartholomaei de Martyribus archiepiscopi Bracharensis, apud Bollandistas, ad diem 10 Januarii, n. 3, pag. 641, edit. Venet.:

In hujus ergo metropolitanae sedis (Bracharensis) districto . . . . . in quo olim divus etiam Damasus ex VIMARANENSI oppido ortus, etc.

XII.

ST. DAMASUS, whom some make a native of Madrid, and therefore of the diocese of Toledo: but we reserve him for another place which we judge very fitting, so as not to depend on any particular opinion; and then it will be seen whether he was A SPANIARD.

From the Life of Blessed Gonsalvus of Amarante, of the Order of Preachers, written by Friar Diogo do Rosário, by the command of Friar Bartolomeu dos Mártires, archbishop of Braga, in the Bollandists, at the 10th day of January, no. 3, p. 641, the Venice edition:

In the district, therefore, of this metropolitan see (of Braga) . . . . . in which of old the divine Damasus also, sprung from the town of VIMARANES, etc.

Ad carm. IX, de SS. AA. Catacumbis, non dubitat beatos Apostolos in iis aliquando habitasse, ut Damasus ait, id est collocatos fuisse: sed eo delatos conjicit ab ipso statim martyrio, atque ibi depositos, secus ac alii sentiunt qui B. Petrum ad Vaticani radices, B. vero Paulum in Ostiensi sepultum scribunt. A Catacumbis vero ad passionis locum translatos putat diu ante Cornelii et Xysti pontificatum, quia Caius Romanus presbyter, secundo nondum elapso saeculo, Apostolorum tropaea Christianorum religioni, in Vaticani, et in Ostiensi occurrisse scribit. Equidem quaestionem hanc, quam viri longe doctissimi vix hactenus extricarunt, non attingam. Hoc adjiciam dumtaxat, nullam perspicere me rationem posse, ob quam versiculus ille Damasianus, de quo in notis monui, exprimere non debeat mirabile illud factum, 421Cquod Gregorius Magnus narrat. Si quis igitur est, qui sic non sentiat, aliam eidem loco sententiam assignet, rogo, aeque aptam. Edicat etiam cui bono priori alio versiculo nos sic monuit: Discipulos Oriens misit, quod sponte fatemur, An forte hoc ignorari unquam potuit? Si vero beatorum Apostolorum tropaea ad se rapere Orientales, et vindicare non studuerunt, proferat rationem aliquam ob quam Roma suos potius meruit defendere cives; ostendatque etiam, quae sit alia ejusdem verbi defendere vis. Scio Pearsonum ad ann. 258, n. 2, temere scripsisse Historiam, quam Gregorius narrat, nulla fide dignam esse. Sed hoc illi liceat dumtaxat, ejusque similibus.

Ad carm. X. Carmen quod ego ex Palatino Gruteriano codice eo loco recensui, ac S. Stephano P. et M. aptari posse existimavi, Damasi esse foetum censet etiam vir eruditus, sed in eo Xystum P. et M. 421Dlaudari conjicit, quem in coemeterio animadversum ex Cypriano scimus. Christi perfectus amicus Ornav Et Damasus tumulum, cum reddit honorem, etc.

Ad Carm. XX, de S. Saturnino. In carminis hujus principio Palatinus codex apud Gruter. p. MCLXXI, sic habet: Horum martyrum cultorum Damasus episcopus servus Dei. OLA NUNC FUERAT CRARTA . . . . . CINIS AN . .

Sic etiam apud Sarazanium Vid. col. 394 huj. edit., not. [ 2 ] not. 20. Deinde ex eodem codice p. MCLXXVI, 422Bhaec affert: NUNC XPI FUERAT CHARTAGINIS ANUS

Supplet igitur ac legit: Horum martyrum cultor Damasus IncOLA nunc XPI FUERAS Carthaginis, ante.

Sic idem Damasus, carm. XX: Sanguine mutasti patriam, etc. Incola nunc Domini.

Verosimilis conjectura, ac lectio, quae si retineatur, Saturninus de quo agitur hoc carmine, idem ille erit, qui Romae detentus sub Decio fuerat, laudatus apud Cyprian. ep. 20, 21, ac deinde martyr. Observat praeterea in Martyrologio Hieronymi, ac Sacramentario Gelasiano ad III kal. Decembris Romae Thrasonis, coemeterio scilicet legi, Saturninum Chrysanthum et Dariam, Maurum, et alios LXXII. Cum igitur Damasus eos omnes laudasset beatos martyres, an illaudatos praeterisset illos LXXII? Ad Carm. XXXIII, de Sepulcro suo, scripserat Cyprianus epistol. 82 Ver. edit., Xystum in coemeterio animadversum, et cum eo diacones quatuor. Sic Baluzius et Vignolius ex mss. codicibus. Contra Fellus legit, et cum eodem Quartum; sigillatque Martyrologia, quod eidem Syxto plures socios adjungant, Felicem scilicet, seu Felicissimum, Agapitum, Donatianum, Faustinum, Praetextatum, aliosque: eaque lectio placuit etiam Ruinario, et Castellano. Observat 422Digitur vir accuratus priorem lectionem Damasi testimonio confirmari, plures Sixto comites attribuentis, quo etiam argumento post Baronium usi sunt Bollandiani ad diem 6 Augusti. Huic profecto sententiae major etiam vis ex eo accedit, quod Quarti hujus nullam ante Adonem et Usuardum mentionem heri observarunt Florentinius et Sollerius. Quidquid igitur de illo sit, quod asseri pro certo potest, illud est, beatum illum pontificem non sine injuria, diuturna sociorum suorum possessione exspoliari posse, quos, ut ait Sacramentarium Leonianum dictum in ejus Missa, doctrinae suae filios ad eamdem gloriam promerendam incitabat: quamquam cum eodem viro clarissimo ex eorum numero excluserim Praetextatum, qui inspectis Martyrologiis caeteris ac Sacramentariis, 423Amemoratur in Martyrologio Florentinii, non ut exprimat martyrem aliquem, sed celebre ab eo dictum coemeterium, in quo Martyrum illorum memoria agebatur. Qui vero, et quot fuerint Sixti in martyrio comites, disquirunt diligenter Bollandiani socii, quos si quis consulat, operae pretium fecerit.

Laborat etiam vir clarissimus, ut caeteros conjectura assequatur, quos Damasus eodem loco sepultos ait, sed ut videtur frustra: Equidem si quis hoc idem a me scire vellet, respondeam cum Prudentio hymn. XI, de Coron: . . . . . . . . . . tu singula quaeris Nomina? difficile est, ut replicare queam, Tantos justorum populos furor impius hausit!

AN ANALYSIS OF CERTAIN OPINIONS OF THE ABBOT TERRIBILINI.

  • 421B

On poem IX, On the Catacombs of the Holy Apostles, he does not doubt that the blessed Apostles at one time dwelt in them, as Damasus says — that is, were placed there; but he conjectures that they were carried thither immediately from the martyrdom itself, and were laid there, otherwise than others hold, who write that blessed Peter was buried at the foot of the Vatican, and blessed Paul on the Ostian Way. But he thinks they were translated from the Catacombs to the place of their passion long before the pontificate of Cornelius and Xystus, because Caius, a Roman priest, before the second century had yet run out, writes that the trophies of the Apostles met the religious devotion of the Christians on the Vatican and on the Ostian Way. For my own part I shall not touch this question, which men far more learned have scarcely disentangled to this day. I will add only this: that I can perceive no reason why that Damasian little verse, of which I gave warning in the notes, ought not to express that marvelous deed 421Cwhich Gregory the Great relates. If, therefore, there is anyone who does not judge so, let him assign to the same passage, I ask, some other meaning equally apt. Let him declare also to what good purpose Damasus warned us thus in another, earlier little verse: The East sent the disciples — which we willingly grant, Or could this perhaps ever have been unknown? But if the men of the East did not take pains to seize for themselves and lay claim to the trophies of the blessed Apostles, let him bring forward some reason why Rome rather deserved to defend her own citizens; and let him show also what other force this same word defend has. I know that Pearson, at the year 258, no. 2, rashly wrote that the History which Gregory relates is worthy of no credit. But let that be permitted to him alone, and to such as he.

On poem X. The poem which I set out in that place from the Palatine codex of Gruter, and judged could be fitted to St. Stephen, Pope and Martyr, the learned man also judges to be the offspring of Damasus; but he conjectures that in it Xystus, Pope and Martyr, is praised, 421Dwhom we know from Cyprian to have been put to death in the cemetery. But the poem itself is against this conjecture, since from it it is plain that Stephen alone then fell a martyr, all the rest, taken with him in the cemetery, being let go unharmed. On the contrary, with Xystus four deacons were slain, and perhaps others, which the learned man himself observes on Poem XXXIII. This poem, therefore, with which we are dealing, suits the former most holy martyr and pontiff rather than the other.

Poem XIII, On Saint Mark the Pope, he supplies thus out of his own wit: Guiltless was the Life of Mark, as we all know Winning in the Love of God, he who could despise the world By his example the People Prayed, that every man might learn Small was the Honor of his Life, great his contempt of possessing 422AAnd skillful Virtue held the inmost chambers of his heart Of justice AE the guardian. The perfect friend of Christ Adorned too by Damasus was the tomb, when he renders honor, etc.

So too in the epitaph of Sisinnius the presbyter, which he thinks was written by the same Damasus, not without probable reason, in Gruter, p. MCLXXIII, no. 9: Here the presbyter Sisinnus wished to lay his limbs, acceptable to all peoples, and a worthy Priest, who knew how to keep the covenants of his holy mother. Winning in the Love of God, he who knew how ever to live, and, content with his own, knew not the hall of the rich man.

On Poem XX, On St. Saturninus. At the beginning of this poem the Palatine codex in Gruter, p. MCLXXI, has thus: Damasus the bishop, servant of God, a cultivator of these martyrs. OLA NUNC FUERAT CRARTA . . . . . CINIS AN . .

So also in Sarazani, See col. 394 of this edition, note [ 2 ] note 20. Then from the same codex, p. MCLXXVI, 422Bhe brings forward these: NUNC XPI FUERAT CHARTAGINIS ANUS

He therefore supplies and reads: Horum martyrum cultor Damasus IncOLA nunc XPI FUERAS Carthaginis, ante.

So the same Damasus, poem XX: By your blood you changed your fatherland, etc. Now a dweller of the Lord.

A likely conjecture, and reading; and if it be kept, the Saturninus with whom this poem deals will be that same man who had been held at Rome under Decius, praised in Cyprian, epp. 20, 21, and afterward a martyr. He observes besides that in the Martyrology of Jerome, and in the Gelasian Sacramentary, at III kal. December, one reads At Rome, of Thraso — that is, in the cemetery — Saturninus, Chrysanthus and Daria, Maurus, and LXXII others. Since, then, Damasus had praised all of them as blessed martyrs, would he have passed over those LXXII unpraised? But because a slip is easy in 422Ccopying out numbers, the distinguished man rightly conjectures that to them belong the little verses which I reported at no. 2 in the Appendix, and that Damasus himself is their author.

For the same reason he thinks that poem XXII, On an uncertain Greek martyr, pertains to St. Hermes, who is known to have been laid in the cemetery of Bassilla, called also that of Hermes, and, as it seems, in nearly the same place as Protus and Hyacinthus. For thus the Anonymous of Mabillon: on the Pincian way, Pamphilus, Bassilla, Protus, Hyacinthus, Hermes; and the Gelasian Sacramentary at V kal. September.

On Poem XXXIII, On his own Sepulchre: Cyprian had written, epistle 82 of the Verona edition, that Xystus was put to death in the cemetery, and with him four deacons. So Baluze and Vignoli, from manuscript codices. Against this Fell reads, and with him Quartus; and he sets his mark upon the Martyrologies for joining several companions to the same Sixtus, namely Felix, or Felicissimus, Agapitus, Donatianus, Faustinus, Praetextatus, and others: and that reading pleased Ruinart and Castellanus also. The exact man therefore observes 422Dthat the former reading is confirmed by the testimony of Damasus, who attributes several companions to Sixtus, which argument the Bollandists also, after Baronius, used at the 6th day of August. To this opinion indeed a still greater force accrues from this, that Florentinius and Sollerius observed that there was no mention of this Quartus before Ado and Usuard. Whatever, then, is the case concerning him, what can be asserted for certain is this: that the blessed pontiff cannot, without injury, be stripped of the long-standing possession of his companions, whom, as the Leonine Sacramentary says in his Mass, he was urging on, as the sons of his teaching, to earn the same glory; although, together with that same most distinguished man, I would exclude from their number Praetextatus, who, when the other Martyrologies and Sacramentaries are inspected, 423Ais mentioned in the Martyrology of Florentinius not so as to denote some martyr, but the famous cemetery called after him, in which the memory of those Martyrs was kept. But who, and how many, were Sixtus' companions in martyrdom, the Bollandist fellows diligently inquire; and whoever consults them will have done a thing worth the labor.

The most distinguished man labors also to reach by conjecture the others whom Damasus says were buried in the same place, but, as it seems, in vain. For my part, if anyone should wish to learn this same thing from me, I would answer with Prudentius, hymn XI, On the Crowns: . . . . . . . . . . dost thou ask the several names? It is hard that I should be able to unfold them: so great peoples of the just has impious fury swallowed up!

424AOn Poem XXXII, the Epitaph of Projecta. Why Sarazani in his notes called Florus, the father of Projecta, a consul, he did not give the reason: perhaps out of Fl., which on that stone is Flavio, he made Florus, as the learned man says. But he suspects that this Florus is the man whose poem stands at the Sepulchre of St. Liberalis the martyr in Gruter, p. MCLXXI, no. 9, which he brings forward: Here rest the limbs of the holy martyr Liberalis, who once on earth was a consul in honor. Florus offered these things to the Lord, framing this hall, that, venerating the saints, he might bear away just rewards.
