DE CASTITATE ET MEDIIS EAM TUENDI.

ON CHASTITY AND THE MEANS OF GUARDING IT.

CAPUT PRIMUM. Quod castitatis virtus junioribus difficilior sit.

Et ut de caeteris virtutibus quae nunc per epistolam enumeratae sunt interim taceam, castitatem, quam ultimam posui, non in membris tantummodo 711Bstudeas servare corporeis, sed haec eadem permaxime regnet ac vigeat in visceribus cordis. Nulla quippe virtus est quae in adolescentiae flore graviora certamina perferat, quam videlicet velut in camino surgentis incendii, titillantis illecebrae genuinus ardor impugnat. Sed cum super hoc themate nobis uberius disputare non liceat, quia prae nimia celeritate non vacat, quid mihi imperatrix, Agnes olim scilicet aureo quidem diademate coronata, nunc autem multo felicius et incomparabiliter eminentius in regis aeterni thalamo collocata, hesterno jam vespertini temporis elabente crepusculo, retulit, hic annotare succincte raptimque non pigeat; ut apud animum tuum, non tam laciniosa loquendi prolixitas quam insignis exempli dignitas convalescat. Porro dum praefata 711CChristi sponsa, et B. Petri filia, in omnium dispositione virtutum delectabiliter pascitur, in tractanda tamen castitatis pudicitiaeque munditia propensius immoratur. Unde factum est ut, dum nonnulla de salute animarum mutuis confabularemur eloquiis, et praecipue quasi quosdam versaremus in manibus vernantis flosculos castitatis, illa hoc in medium protulit: Postquam imperator, inquit, Otto regnum Italiae Berengario victor eripuit, captas illico duas ejus filias in Teutonicas partes exsilio transmigravit. Quae nimirum, dum venusti vultus specie et insigni corporum pulchritudine regum referrent generis dignitatem, coeperunt multi regni proceres Adelaidi Augustae vehementer insistere, ut tam elegantis formae decus dotali sibi mererentur copula 711Dsociare. Quae nimirum puellae dum omnes procos aequa prorsus dedignatione contemnerent, et mortale conjugium sanctae superbiae supercilio fastidirent, una illarum duos pulcinos suis uberibus supposuit, et tandiu inter vestem et carnem, donec omnino putrescerent, occultavit. Cumque conspiceret homines ad suum venire colloquium, laxabat occulte vestem, et naribus colloquentium gaudebat exhalare fetorem. Cumque colloquentes ei hanc quotidie narium paterentur injuriam, tandem cessavit delusa sollicitudo quaerentium, et virgo Christi, per simulatam carnis alienae putredinem, inviolabilem sui corporis 712Aservavit integritatem. Unde factum est ut utraeque postmodum sorores eligerent sanctimoniale propositum, et usque ad finem vitae monachicum servarent irreprehensibiliter institutum. 743

CHAPTER ONE. That the virtue of chastity is more difficult for the young.

And, to say nothing for the moment of the other virtues which have just now been enumerated in the letter, be zealous to keep chastity — which I set last — not only in the bodily 711Bmembers, but let this same virtue above all reign and be vigorous in the inmost parts of the heart. For there is no virtue which in the flower of youth endures heavier contests, assailed as it is by the inborn ardor of a titillating enticement, as though by a furnace of rising flame. But since I am not permitted to discourse more copiously upon this theme, because in my excessive haste there is no leisure, let it not irk me to set down here, briefly and hurriedly, what the empress Agnes — once crowned indeed with a golden diadem, but now far more happily and incomparably more eminently placed in the bridal chamber of the eternal King — told me yesterday, as the twilight of evening was already slipping away; so that in your mind it may not be so much a fringed prolixity of speaking as the worth of a notable example that gains strength. Now while the aforesaid 711Cbride of Christ and daughter of blessed Peter feeds with delight upon the ordering of all the virtues, yet she lingers the more readily upon the treatment of the cleanness of chastity and modesty. Whence it came about that, while we were talking together in mutual discourse of certain matters touching the salvation of souls, and were especially turning over in our hands, as it were, some little blossoms of chastity in their spring, she brought this forward into the midst: After the emperor Otto, she said, as victor snatched the kingdom of Italy from Berengar, he straightway carried off his two captive daughters into exile in the German parts. And since these, by the comeliness of their countenance and the notable beauty of their persons, recalled the dignity of a royal line, many nobles of the realm began vehemently to press the empress Adelaide, that they might be counted worthy to join to themselves in a dowered union 711Dthe grace of a form so elegant. But these girls, since they despised all their suitors with an altogether equal disdain, and loathed a mortal marriage with the lifted brow of a holy pride — one of them placed two chicks beneath her breasts, and hid them between her garment and her flesh until they had utterly rotted. And when she saw men coming to converse with her, she would secretly loosen her garment, and rejoiced to breathe out the stench upon the nostrils of those who spoke with her. And when day by day those who conversed with her suffered this affront to their noses, at length the baffled eagerness of her seekers ceased; and the virgin of Christ, by the feigned putrefaction of another's flesh, preserved the inviolable 712Aintegrity of her own body. Whence it came to pass that both sisters afterward chose the purpose of the consecrated life, and to the end of their days kept the monastic institution blamelessly. 743

CAPUT II. Quod frequens eucharistiae susceptio est praesidium castitatis.

Quamobrem pudeat, vel, o fili, vitio libidinis enerviter, vel in cogitatione succumbere, de quo cernis infirmiorem et fragilem sexum cum tanta gloria triumphare: et, ut frementem bestiam valeas, ut ita loquar, ex agro tui juris expellere, satage te, frater, jam quotidie Dominici corporis et sanguinis perceptione munire. Videat occultus hostis labia tua Christi 712Brubore rubentia, quae territus perhorrescat, et mox in tenebrarum suarum latibula pavescendo diffugiat. Quod enim tu per visibilem panis ac vini speciem suscipis, ille, velit, nolit, Dominici corporis ac sanguinis intelligit veritatem.

Porro autem eadem mihi tunc venerabilis regina narravit quod nuper in Vormaciensi ecclesia, dum longiori per negligentiam tempore eucharistia reservata fuisset in buxide, sola postmodum illic caro fuerit reperta. Ita dum curiosius in eam multorum visus intenderet, nil aliud quam veram ac solidam carnem procul dubio judicaret.

Rainaldus etiam venerabilis Cumanae Ecclesiae pontifex dum adesset, quod sibi paulo ante contigerat, veridicus enarrator exposuit: Presbyter, inquit, in 712Cecclesia cui, Deo auctore, deservio, hodieque nisi fallor advivit, qui tam parvam ac tenuem in litterarum habet doctrina notitiam, ut aperte quoque scriptionis articulum syllabatim vix valeat legere; nimia tamen paupertate constrictus, utcunque missarum cogitur solemnia celebrare. Hic aliquando dum sacri muneris eucharistiam detulisset aegroto, ut erat utique deses ac negligens, aliquantulum Dominici sanguinis remansit in calice. Quod cum ille in ecclesiam rediens comperisset, sed fastidio praepeditus noluisset accipere, mox calicem lavit, et in labrum marmoreum in quo erat aqua sanctificata, projecit. Et, o magnum divinae virtutis indicium! illam labri partem quam id quod effusum est de calice, contigit, in sanguineum ruborem repente convertit, ubi scilicet 712Dduae majores guttae sanguinis exprimuntur; sed neque illae, neque aliae quae juxta videntur esse minusculae, nullo possunt studio vel ablui vel abstergi, quo rubor impressi sanguinis aliquatenus valeat aboleri. Cum ergo purpureus ille cruor tam tenaciter haeserit in vase lapideo, quantum putamus jus sibi potestatis vindicat virtus illa coelestis in pectore Christiano? His itaque coelestibus sacramentis, fili charissime, te frequentius munire non differas, quibus videlicet diabolicae versutiae et subdolae machinationis argumenta repellas. Praecinctus erigere, constanter atque robustus sta semper in acie, nec 713Aignores indeficientem adversarium contra te infatigabiliter dimicare. 744

CHAPTER II. That frequent reception of the Eucharist is a safeguard of chastity.

Wherefore let it shame you, O son, to succumb unmanfully to the vice of lust, or even to succumb in thought, seeing that you behold the weaker and frailer sex triumph over it with so great glory. And that you may be able, so to speak, to drive the raging beast out of the field of your own jurisdiction, take pains, brother, to fortify yourself now daily by the reception of the Lord's body and blood. Let the hidden enemy see your lips red with the redness of Christ, 712Band let him shudder at them in terror, and presently flee away trembling into the lurking-places of his own darkness. For what you receive under the visible appearance of bread and wine, he, willing or unwilling, understands to be the truth of the Lord's body and blood.

Moreover, the same venerable queen then told me that lately in the church of Worms, when through negligence the Eucharist had been kept for a rather long time in the pyx, flesh alone was afterward found there. So that, as the gaze of many was bent upon it rather curiously, one would beyond doubt have judged it to be nothing else than true and solid flesh.

Rainald also, the venerable bishop of the Church of Como, being present, set forth as a truthful narrator what had befallen him a little before: There is a priest, he said, in 712Cthe church which by God's appointment I serve — and unless I am mistaken he is alive to this day — who has so small and slender an acquaintance with the learning of letters that even a plainly written passage he can scarcely read syllable by syllable; yet, constrained by excessive poverty, he is compelled somehow to celebrate the solemnities of the Masses. Once, when he had carried the Eucharist of the sacred gift to a sick man, being as he was slothful and negligent, a little of the Lord's blood remained in the chalice. And when, returning into the church, he discovered this, but, hindered by squeamishness, was unwilling to consume it, he presently washed the chalice and cast the water into a marble basin in which there was hallowed water. And — O great token of divine power! — that part of the basin which what had been poured out of the chalice touched, it suddenly turned to a bloody redness, in the place, namely, where 712Dtwo larger drops of blood stand out; and neither those, nor the others which appear beside them to be smaller, can by any effort be either washed off or wiped away, so that the redness of the imprinted blood might in any degree be done away. Since, therefore, that purple gore clung so tenaciously in a vessel of stone, how great a right of power do we suppose that heavenly virtue claims for itself in a Christian breast? And so, most dear son, do not put off fortifying yourself the more frequently with these heavenly sacraments, whereby you may drive back the devices of diabolical craft and crafty contrivance. Rise up girded; stand ever in the battle line, steadfast and strong; and be not ignorant that 713Aan unfailing adversary fights against you untiringly. 744

CAPUT III. Diabolus transfigurat se in angelum lucis, ut monachum decipiat.

Interea dum haec loquor, et illud nunc ad memoriam redit, quod Hildebrandus Romanae Ecclesiae archidiaconus, eisdem qui superius memorati sunt et episcopo praesentibus retulit: Duos, inquit, sanctos monachos vidi, et non mediocrem apud eos locum familiaritatis obtinui; qui nimirum singulis morabantur in cellulis, apud monasterium quod in loco constructum est qui dicitur Aquisgrani, quorum videlicet alter, qui majoris videbatur esse simplicitatis, Marinus; alter autem Romanus vocabatur proprio 713Bnomine. Sed Marinus vix ulla dies erat in qua diabolum vel per speciem aliquam non aspiceret, vel per somnium non audiret, adeo ut antiquus hostis sibi saepe compsalleret, simulque cum eo divinae laudis officia celebraret. Aliquando vero tenebrarum auctor se transfiguravit in angelum lucis, eique quasi legem dictando mandavit: Cave, inquit, quia qui angelum Dei et aspicere et alloqui meruisti, indignum est ut de caetero cum hominibus loquaris. Protinus ille, tanquam mandatum divinitus accepisset, a fratrum se funditus collocutione removit, seque sub rigida silentii censura constrinxit. Cui cum frater Romanus primo, deinde abbas monasterii, ut sibi loqueretur vehementer insisteret, et ille praedantis labiis ( suppl. responsum vel quid simile ) non 713Cmitteret; abbas, fores cellulae violenter irrumpens, dignis indiscretum fratrem verberibus castigavit, et sic ad reddendum sermonis officium compulit. Cui mox admonendum subjunxit: Idcirco, inquit, antiquus adversarius, bonorum semper operum inimicus, silentium tibi tentavit imponere, ut et tu fructum fraternae aedificationis amitteres, et illi qui a te sublevandi erant tuae consolationis auxilium non haberent. Sic itaque frater, quia non malitia, sed ex simplicitate peccaverat, ad mentem quantocius rediit, et, contempta suggestione diaboli, admonitionibus pii patris humiliter acquievit.

Sed dum te, fili mi, ad evacuandas antiqui hostis insidias provoco, quid praefata regina retulerit audisse ex ore beati Leonis noni Romanae Ecclesiae 713Dpontificis, non omitto. Ait itaque praedictus papa: Amita mea, sanctimonialis effecta, in quodam virorum erat monasterio constituta; ibique in propria degens cellula cum quadam pygmaea, id est brevis staturae femina, quotidianae laudis officia patienter et humiliter persolvebat.

CHAPTER III. The devil transfigures himself into an angel of light, that he may deceive a monk.

Meanwhile, as I say these things, there now returns to memory that also which Hildebrand, archdeacon of the Roman Church, related in the presence of the same persons mentioned above and of the bishop: I saw, he said, two holy monks, and obtained no slight place of familiarity with them; who indeed dwelt in separate cells, at the monastery which is built in the place called Aachen; of whom the one, who seemed to be of the greater simplicity, was called by his own name Marinus, and the other 713BRomanus. But as for Marinus, there was scarcely a day on which he did not either behold the devil in some appearance or hear him in a dream — so much so that the ancient enemy would often chant the psalms along with him, and celebrate together with him the offices of divine praise. But once the author of darkness transfigured himself into an angel of light, and, as though laying down a law, charged him: Take heed, he said; since you have been counted worthy both to behold and to address an angel of God, it is unfitting that henceforth you should speak with men. Straightway that man, as though he had received a command from heaven, withdrew himself utterly from converse with the brethren, and bound himself under a rigid strictness of silence. And when brother Romanus first, and then the abbot of the monastery, vehemently pressed him to speak with them, and he would put forth nothing from his plundering lips ( suppl. responsum vel quid simile ), 713Cthe abbot, violently breaking in the doors of the cell, chastised the indiscreet brother with the stripes he deserved, and so compelled him to render the office of speech. And presently he added this by way of admonition: For this reason, he said, the ancient adversary, ever the enemy of good works, attempted to lay silence upon you: that you might both lose the fruit of fraternal edification, and that those who were to be relieved by you might not have the help of your consolation. And so the brother, because he had sinned not from malice but out of simplicity, returned to his right mind with all speed; and, despising the devil's suggestion, humbly acquiesced in the admonitions of the devout father.

But while I rouse you, my son, to bring to nothing the snares of the ancient enemy, I do not omit what the aforesaid queen related that she had heard from the mouth of blessed Leo the Ninth, pontiff of the Roman 713DChurch. So the aforesaid pope said: My aunt, having been made a nun, was established in a certain monastery of men; and there, dwelling in her own cell with a certain pygmy — that is, a woman of short stature — she discharged the offices of daily praise patiently and humbly.

CAPUT IV. Quod diabolus signo crucis cognoscitur.

Quadam vero nocte cum ante nocturnae synaxis [articulum, vel horam vel aliquid simile ] maturius surrexisset, comitemque suam more solito saepius inclamasset; illaque, gravi sopore depressa, nullatenus responderet, tandem in iram commota, quid diceret non attendit: Diabole, inquit, surge. 745 714AAd quam vocem praesto diabolus, ejus quae dormiebat effigiem repraesentans, accessit; et mutuos reddendo versiculos, psallere simul coepit; cumque ad illum psalterii locum modulatione continua pervenissent ubi dicitur: « Exsurgat Deus, et dissipentur inimici ejus, et fugiant a facie ejus qui oderunt eum: sicut deficit fumus, deficiant (Psal. LXVII), » et reliqua: hic illico malignus spiritus substitit, et hos exprimere versiculos non praesumpsit. Hoc sancta mulier cum apud se miraretur, expavit; et, quod erat, non inaniter suspicans, signum sibi sanctae crucis imposuit, statimque malignus hostis evanuit; et quis esset qui sanctae mulieri psallendo comes exstiterat, se fraudulenter occultando monstravit. Huic etiam venerabili matronae nocturnis horis nequam 714Bspiritus cadaver cujusdam hominis, qui pro suis sceleribus appensus fuerat, attulit; et, ut illi terrorem incuteret, hujusmodi sibi, ut ita loquar, xenium procuravit. Quae mox abbati loci fratrumque conventui quid sibi nequissimus hostis fecisset innotuit; atque ut pro illo misero fratres omnes in commune preces effunderent, obnoxiis [ f. obnixis] precibus impetravit. Postmodum vero cuidam divinitus revelatum est, per orationes fratrum illum misericordiam consecutum. Sic itaque deceptus hostis antiquus, qui dum viventi machinatur illudere, jacturam cogitur de mortuo sustinere.

CHAPTER IV. That the devil is detected by the sign of the cross.

But on a certain night, when she had risen rather early before the [articulum, vel horam vel aliquid simile ] of the night office, and had called out to her companion repeatedly in the accustomed manner, and the other, weighed down by a heavy slumber, made no answer at all, at length, moved to anger, she did not heed what she was saying: Devil, she said, get up. 745 714AAt which word the devil was at hand and drew near, presenting the likeness of her who was sleeping; and, rendering the verses in turn, he began to chant the psalms together with her. And when in unbroken chanting they had come to that place of the psalter where it is said: « Let God arise, and let his enemies be scattered, and let them that hate him flee from before his face: as smoke faileth, let them fail (Psal. LXVII), » and the rest — here the malignant spirit at once stopped short, and did not presume to utter these verses. When the holy woman wondered at this within herself, she took fright; and, suspecting not idly what the case was, she set upon herself the sign of the holy cross, and immediately the malignant enemy vanished; and by fraudulently hiding himself he showed who it was that had stood as the holy woman's companion in the psalmody. To this venerable matron also the wicked 714Bspirit brought, in the night hours, the corpse of a certain man who had been hanged for his crimes; and, that he might strike terror into her, he procured for her, so to speak, a guest-gift of this kind. She presently made known to the abbot of the place and to the convent of brethren what the most wicked enemy had done to her; and with submissive [ f. obnixis] prayers she obtained that all the brethren should pour out prayers in common for that wretched man. But afterward it was divinely revealed to a certain person that through the prayers of the brethren that man had obtained mercy. And so the ancient enemy was outwitted, who, while he contrives to mock the living, is compelled to suffer loss over the dead.

CAPUT V. Christianae charitatis exemplum.

Ad hoc igitur et hujusmodi diabolicae tentationis 714Ctibi proponuntur exempla, ut et ipse contra hostis callidi deceptoris insidias solerter evigiles: et, dum te delectat aliorum cum hoste luctantium audire victorias, ipse te per languidi corporis ignaviam non remittas. Sed jam ista sufficiant. Commenda me fratribus, domno priori ex nostra parte humiliter suggere ut a fervore solito pietatis et misericordiae non tepescat, sed susceptionibus hospitum et subsidiis egenorum, in quantum res domestica patitur, insistere non omittat. Sed et illi conveniens exemplum profero, quod huic rei, quam sibi suadere gestio, non mediocriter profuturum esse confido. Nam et eadem Agnes regina quae supra inquit etiam quia vir quidam in Alemanniae partibus avia saltuum, praerupta montium, opaca sylvarum venandi studio 714Dperagrabat. Hic itaque dum anxius huc illucque discurrit, et sollicitus curiositatis indagine ferarum lustra perquirit, invenit duas mulieres, matrem scilicet ac filiam, per illos hiemalium cumulos pruinarum et profunda nivium rudera miserabiliter oberrantes. Qui protinus misericordia ductus, comiti suo qui eum solum sequebatur, ait: Levemus in equis has feminas, singuli singulam, et ad hominum habitacula devehamus, quia vel oberrantium forte luporum sunt morsibus exponendae, vel vi nimii algoris sunt procul dubio moriturae. Quod comes penitus abnegat, et hanc se ferre posse stomachatur injuriam. Cui dominus: Ego, inquit, si tu fastidis, solus utramque levabo, et hanc quam dicis injuriam 715Aalacriter sustinebo. Cumque unam 746 post se, et alteram, in medio residens, poneret ante se: tandem socius mente compunctus atque confusus, domino suo cessit unam, et ipse ad subvehendum corripuit alteram. Sic igitur eas per difficillima loca et longos anfractus non sine gravi periculo detulerunt, donec scilicet molendinum casu obvium reperirent, ibique eas deponerent. Adjecit etiam dominus ut uni earum vestem traderet, qua vim frigoris aliquatenus temperaret. Hic aliquanto post tempore factus est monachus, et deinceps, ingravescente molestia, ad extrema perductus est. Tunc fratribus, qui astabant, cum magna coepit animadversione clamare, dicens: Videtis innumerabilium daemonum turbam, quae me undique circumvallat, meque terribilibus oculis intueri 715Bet impugnare non cessat? Cumque continuis orationibus et psalmodiae fratres insisterent, ille tamen nihilominus tremefactus visceribus formidaret, tandem ille qui sibi, sicut dictum est in venatione comes exstiterat, assistens propius exclamavit: Nonne vides quod mulier illa quam tecum simul ad aquimolum devectavi, vestimentum illud quod sibi tunc dedi tenens in manibus, ventilat, et omnes in hac domo daemones violenter exturbat? Paulo post, ecce, Deo gratias, ejecti sunt, et a meis obtutibus, mulieris hujus impulsu, prorsus evanuerunt. Sic igitur ille sub bonae spei securitate defunctus est, et misericordiae fructus, quos vivus exhibuit, oblatos sibi, dum moreretur, invenit. Nam saepe contingit ut inter multas carnalium actionum tenebras 715Cunum boni operis lumen erumpat, quod hominem ad bonum finem portumque perducat.

CHAPTER V. An example of Christian charity.

To this end, therefore, are examples of diabolical temptation 714Cof this kind set before you: that you yourself may keep skillful watch against the snares of the crafty and deceiving enemy; and that, while it delights you to hear of the victories of others wrestling with the enemy, you may not yourself grow slack through the sloth of a languid body. But let these things now suffice. Commend me to the brethren; humbly suggest to the lord prior on our behalf that he grow not lukewarm from his accustomed fervor of devotion and mercy, but that he fail not to persist in the receiving of guests and the relief of the needy, so far as the household's means allow. And for him too I bring forward a fitting example, which I am confident will profit not a little toward this matter which I am eager to urge upon him. For the same queen Agnes mentioned above said also that a certain man in the parts of Germany used to range, in his zeal for hunting, the trackless glades, the steeps of the mountains, the shadowy depths of the woods. 714DAnd so, while he ran anxiously this way and that, and with careful searching of his curiosity sought out the lairs of wild beasts, he found two women — a mother, namely, and a daughter — wandering miserably through those heaps of winter frost and the deep wastes of snow. He, straightway led by mercy, said to his companion, who alone was following him: Let us lift these women onto our horses, each of us one, and carry them down to the dwellings of men; for either they will perhaps be exposed to the bites of wandering wolves, or beyond doubt they will die by the force of excessive cold. This his companion utterly refuses, and testily protests that he cannot bear such an indignity. To whom his lord said: If you disdain it, I alone will lift them both, and will cheerfully bear this indignity, as you call it. 715AAnd when he had set one 746 behind him and, sitting in the middle, the other before him, at length his comrade, pricked in mind and put to shame, yielded one to his lord, and himself caught up the other to carry her. And so they bore them through most difficult places and long windings, not without grave peril, until they found a mill that met them by chance, and there set them down. The lord further directed that he should hand over a garment to one of them, whereby she might somewhat temper the force of the cold. Some while afterward this man became a monk, and thereafter, as his sickness grew heavy, he was brought to his last hour. Then he began to cry out with great alarm to the brethren who stood by, saying: Do you see the throng of innumerable demons which hems me in on every side, and does not cease to gaze upon me with terrible eyes 715Band to assail me? And when the brethren persisted in unbroken prayers and psalmody, while he nonetheless, trembling in his inmost parts, was still afraid, at length that man who, as has been said, had been his companion in the hunt, standing closer by, cried out: Do you not see that the woman whom together with you I carried to the watermill, holding in her hands that garment which I then gave her, is fanning it, and violently driving out all the demons in this house? A little after — behold, thanks be to God — they have been cast out, and at this woman's driving they have utterly vanished from my sight. And so that man died in the security of a good hope, and found offered to him, as he lay dying, the fruits of mercy which he had shown while alive. For it often happens that amid the many shadows of carnal deeds 715Cone light of a good work breaks forth, which leads a man through to a good end and haven.

CAPUT VI. Per unum opus bonum Dei pietas peccatores salvat.

Unde non otiosum arbitror si et id quod Stephanus ejusdem reginae capellanus, vir videlicet honestus et prudens, enarravit, attexo. Ait enim quod Stedelandus imperator Galliciae retulit, quod tunc ad unius notitiam, alio postmodum referente, pervenit: Tres, inquit, meretrices erant, obscenis lupanarium foeditatibus deditae, et, ut ita loquar, omni 716Atranseunti turpi lenocinio prostitutae, sed cum regio illa communi Saracenorum et Christianorum esset habitatione permixta, illae se Christianis quidem turpiter exponebant; Saracenorum vero consortia funditus abdicabant. Cumque se dolerent illi in hoc foedo negotio despici, suaeque genti conquererentur injuriam irrogari, tandem eas ad tribunal praesidis pertrahunt, et ut suis quoque, sicut Christianis, in luxuriae permixtione consentiant, vehementer insistunt. Sed dum scelus hoc apud eas, modo terrores, modo blanditias intentantes, aliquatenus obtinere non possent, prolata sententia praesidis, adjudicatae sunt morti. Quid plura? Protinus spiculatores accedunt, nudis cervicibus vibratos pugiones incutiunt; sed ne supremam quidem cutem incidere, cassatis ictibus, 716Bpossunt. Interea dum et istae mori alacriter 747 volunt, et illi occidere nequeunt, carcerali custodiae mancipantur. Nocte vero uni earum, quae quasi superior erat caeteris, Salvator apparuit, eique dixit: Noli timere, hodie certamini vestro finem imponam, vosque cum corona martyrii in gloriae meae amoenitate suscipiam. Sequenti vero die ad tribunalia judicis deducuntur, inquisitione facta, eadem nihilominus sententia perseverat; idem est qui fuerat animus; et Agarenorum, sicut prius, per omnia detestantur amplexus. Protinus ergo carnifices, quoniam in cervicibus earum nil se posse probaverant, earum guttur gladio defaecant [desecant], 748 et sic de meretricibus martyres efficiunt. Sed quoniam longius quam incipiendo decreverat, jam stylus iste 716Cperducitur, cohibendus est calamus, et epistolaris compendii ponendus est modus. Tandem divinae pietatis imploro clementiam, ut inter caetera virtutum et totius sanctae religionis insignia, inviolabile tibi servandae pudicitiae robur infundat, et vasculum tui corporis in sanctificatione et honore custodiat; quatenus is qui dicit in Canticis: « Ego flos campi, ego lilium convallium (Cant. II); » sicut pro te dignatus est fieri filius Virginis, sic etiam vernare te faciat floribus castitatis.

Sit nomen Domini benedictum.

CHAPTER VI. Through one good work God's mercy saves sinners.

Wherefore I judge it not idle if I weave in also that which Stephen, chaplain of the same queen, a man indeed honorable and prudent, related. For he says that Stedelandus, ruler of Galicia, reported what then came to the knowledge of one man, another afterward relating it: There were, he said, three harlots, given over to the obscene foulness of the brothels, and, so to speak, prostituted by base pandering to 716Aevery passer-by; but since that region was mingled by the common habitation of Saracens and Christians, they indeed exposed themselves shamefully to Christians, but utterly renounced the company of Saracens. And when those men grieved that they were scorned in this foul traffic, and complained that an injury was being inflicted upon their people, at length they dragged the women to the tribunal of the governor, and vehemently insisted that they should consent to their own people also, as to the Christians, in the commerce of lust. But when, plying them now with terrors, now with blandishments, they could in no degree obtain this crime from them, the governor's sentence was pronounced, and they were condemned to death. What more? Straightway the executioners approach, and drive their brandished daggers against the bared necks; but their strokes are made void, and they cannot cut 716Beven the topmost skin. Meanwhile, while these women eagerly 747 wish to die, and those men are unable to kill, they are consigned to the custody of the prison. But in the night the Savior appeared to one of them, who was as it were the chief of the rest, and said to her: Fear not; today I will set an end to your contest, and will receive you, with the crown of martyrdom, into the delightfulness of my glory. But on the following day they are led to the tribunals of the judge; the inquiry being made, the same sentence nonetheless stands; their mind is the same as it had been; and, as before, they detest in all things the embraces of the Hagarenes. Straightway therefore the executioners, since they had proved that they could do nothing upon their necks, sever their throats with the sword [desecant], 748 and so make martyrs out of harlots. But since this pen of mine is now carried further than it had determined at the outset, 716Cthe reed must be checked, and a limit set to the compass of a letter. Finally I implore the clemency of the divine mercy, that among the other marks of the virtues and of all holy religion it may pour into you the inviolable strength of a modesty to be kept, and may guard the little vessel of your body in sanctification and honor; so that he who says in the Canticles: « I am the flower of the field, I am the lily of the valleys (Cant. II); » even as he deigned for your sake to become the Son of the Virgin, so may also make you blossom with the flowers of chastity.

Blessed be the name of the Lord.
