Peter Damian

Petrus Damianus

c. 1007 – 1072

Hermit of Fonte Avellana, cardinal-bishop of Ostia, and the fiercest voice of the eleventh-century reform against simony and clerical marriage. His letters and short treatises are vehement, concrete, and often mordantly funny; Dante set him in the highest sphere of contemplatives.

ARGUMENTUM.

Causam affert cur Paulus apostolus ad dexteram, Petrus vero ad sinistram in picturis exprimantur, idcirco scilicet, quia Paulus esset ex tribu Benjamin, qui Latine, filius dexterae interpretatur; probatque hujusmodi nomen aptissime illi convenire. Multas quoque ejusdem Pauli praerogativas adducit. Tum demum exponit cur Hierosolymitana Ecclesia, cum in ea Christus passus fuerit, non sit caput omnium Ecclesiarum.

589BDomno DESIDERIO sanctae conversionis [conversationis] abbati, religiosoque conventui, PETRUS peccator monachus in Domino servitutem.

Ut domnum Martinum ad vos iter arripere didici, notario protinus, ut exciperet, imperavi. Sed quoniam per totum quadragesimalem circulum tecum, venerabilis pater, familiariter degens, quaeque conferenda videbantur, viva voce contulimus, et quae communicanda erant, sive tractanda, saepius intimavimus; jam quid novi ad te scribi valeat, non invenio, dum quidquid necessarium esse poterat, frequentius iteratum alterutra confabulatione perpendo. Quid igitur faciam? Nunquid qui loqui coeperam, deficiente materia, stolide conticescam?

THE ARGUMENT.

He brings forward the reason why the Apostle Paul is represented in paintings at the right hand, but Peter at the left — namely, because Paul was of the tribe of Benjamin, which in Latin is interpreted son of the right hand; and he proves that a name of this sort suits him most aptly. He also adduces many prerogatives of the same Paul. Then at last he sets forth why the Church of Jerusalem, although Christ suffered in it, is not the head of all the Churches.

589BTo the lord DESIDERIUS, abbot of holy conversion [conversationis], and to the religious community, PETER, a sinful monk, offers his service in the Lord.

As soon as I learned that the lord Martin was setting out on the journey to you, I straightway ordered the notary to take down my words. But since, living in familiar company with you, venerable father, through the whole round of Lent, we conferred by living voice on whatever seemed to require conferring, and often made known to each other the things that were to be shared or handled, I now find nothing new that can be written to you, since I weigh how everything that could be necessary has been gone over again and again in our exchange of talk. What then am I to do? Shall I, who had begun to speak, fall stupidly silent for want of matter?

Sed dum armatam penna, atque ad scribendum ex more libratam manum scriptoris aspicio, materia mihi dulcis offertur, de qua ad disputandum mea mens alacriter provocetur. Per istam siquidem notarii dextram repente mihi illud occurrit, quod ipse a me, quaestione proposita, saepenumero requisisti: Cur videlicet in imaginibus picturarum per universas adjacentes Romae provincias, Petrus qui primus est, ad sinistram; coapostolatus autem ejus Paulus constituitur ad dextram; cum juxta vulgarem sensum hoc rerum ordo deposcat, ut Petrus, qui senatus apostolici princeps est, dextrum Domini latus; 589DPaulus vero, qui junior est, sinistrum jure possideat? Sed durum est valde, ut opinemur, quia 590Btam egregium, tamque famosum apostolicae dispositionis ordinem inconsulte, atque inconsiderate devota Deo ac religiosa vetustas admiserit. Neque enim credendum est, ut Constantinus imperator, imo papa Silvester; atque post eos principes et sacerdotes in ecclesiasticae disciplinae littera pervigiles atque solertes, hunc ordinem tantorum principum negligendum ducerent, si correctione dignum aliquatenus aestimarent. Ne ergo sanctae hujus historiae ordo inordinatus appareat, quod nobis videtur, sanctitati vestrae breviter innotescat.

Paulum plane de tribu Benjamin exstitisse quis ambigit? Benjamin autem Hebraice, Latine filius dexterae nuncupatur. Quid ergo mirum est, si ad dexteram constituitur, qui vocabulum dexterae ex 590Cpaterno jure sortitur? Enim vero, ut vetus illa beato Paulo liquido congruere videatur historia, non sine causa Scriptura dicit: « Quia egrediente anima Rachel prae dolore, et imminente jam morte, vocavit nomen filii sui, Benoni, id est filius doloris mei (Gen. XXXV). » Pater vero appellavit eum Benjamin, id est, filius dexterae. Per Rachel namque, quae ovis, vel visum principium dicitur, Ecclesia non immerito designatur. Quae nimirum et ad instar ovis innocenter vivit, et per studium contemplationis ad videndam Redemptoris suis speciem medullitus inardescit. Qui de se requirentibus Judaeis ait: « Ego principium, qui et loquor vobis (Joan. VIII). » Benjamin igitur nascente, Rachel mater emoritur; dum appropinquante ad lucem novae regenerationis Saulo, persecutionibus 590DEcclesia per eum crudeliter impugnatur, Sicut Lucas in Actibus apostolorum: « Saulus, inquit, 591Adevastabat Ecclesiam, per domos intrans, et trahens viros, et mulieres tradebat 607 in custodiam (Act. VIII). » Congrue itaque Rachel Benoni, id est, filium doloris mei vocat: quem Jacob Benjamin, id est, filium dexterae nuncupat; quia Paulus, qui matris Ecclesiae dolor exstitit, qui eam quodammodo, dum nasceretur, impugnando peremit, a Deo Patre filius dexterae est vocatus, cum per eum divina potentia adversus gentes quasi per fortem suam dexteram dimicavit, verborum jacula valenter intorsit, salubres cordium plagas intulit, et per eum devictis atque prostratis hostibus, cum gloria triumphavit. Hinc est quod idem Paulus ad Galatas ait: « Cum autem complacuit ei, qui me segregavit ex utero matris meae, et vocavit per gratiam suam, ut revelaret 591Bfilium suum in me, et evangelizarem eum in gentibus, continuo non acquievi carni et sanguini (Gal. II). » Non ergo immerito filius dexterae Paulus vocatur, per quem omnis gentium multitudo, quae ad dexteram Dei ponenda est, ad fidei sacramenta colligitur Accinctus siquidem verbo Dei, quod est gladius Spiritus (Ephes. VI), hodieque usque ad finem saeculi adversus omnium vitiorum, iniquorumque spirituum rabiem dimicat, et tanquam fulminea Christi manus, vibratis coelestis eloquii gladiis, rigidas resistentium Deo cervices obtruncat. Recte igitur Paulus dextri juxta Redemptorem lateris obtinet dignitatem, qui divinae dexterae non ambigitur exercere virtutem. An non quodammodo dextera Dei adversus diabolum dimicantem se fatetur esse, cum dicit: « Ego autem 591Csic curro, non quasi in incertum: sic pugno, non quasi aerem verberans? » (I Cor. IX.)

CHAPTER ONE. Why Peter is placed at the left, and Paul at the right, in paintings.

  • 589C

But while I look upon the hand of the scribe armed with the pen and poised for writing as usual, a sweet subject offers itself to me, one to which my mind is briskly summoned to dispute. For by that right hand of the notary there suddenly came to my mind the thing you yourself have very often asked of me, putting the question: namely, why, in the images of the paintings throughout all the provinces lying about Rome, Peter, who is first, is placed at the left, while Paul, the partner of his apostolate, is set at the right — whereas, according to the common understanding, the order of things demands this, that Peter, who is the chief of the apostolic senate, should by right hold the Lord's right side, 589Dand Paul, who is the younger, the left? But it is very hard to suppose that 590Ban age devoted to God and religious admitted so distinguished and so famous an ordering of the apostolic arrangement thoughtlessly and without consideration. Nor indeed is it to be believed that the emperor Constantine — nay rather, Pope Silvester — and after them the princes and priests, wakeful and skilled in the letter of ecclesiastical discipline, would have thought this ordering of such great princes something to be neglected, if they had judged it in any degree worthy of correction. Lest, therefore, the order of this holy history should appear disordered, let what seems right to us be briefly made known to your holiness.

That Paul was plainly of the tribe of Benjamin — who doubts it? Now Benjamin in Hebrew is called in Latin son of the right hand. What wonder, then, if he is placed at the right who draws the name of the right hand from 590Chis father's right? Indeed, that the ancient history may be seen to fit blessed Paul clearly, not without cause does Scripture say: « That, as Rachel's soul was departing from her pain, and death already pressing upon her, she called her son's name Benoni, that is, the son of my sorrow (Gen. XXXV). » But the father called him Benjamin, that is, son of the right hand. For by Rachel, who is called a sheep, or a beginning seen, the Church is not undeservedly signified. She indeed both lives innocently after the likeness of a sheep, and, through the pursuit of contemplation, burns inwardly to see the beauty of her Redeemer, who says to the Jews who ask about him: « I am the beginning, who also speak to you (Joan. VIII). » When Benjamin, therefore, is born, his mother Rachel dies: while, as Saul draws near to the light of the new regeneration, the Church is cruelly assailed by him with 590Dpersecutions. So Luke in the Acts of the Apostles: « Saul, he says, 591Awas laying waste the Church, entering into houses, and dragging away men, and delivering women 607 into custody (Act. VIII). » Fittingly therefore does Rachel call him Benoni, that is, the son of my sorrow, whom Jacob names Benjamin, that is, son of the right hand: because Paul, who was the sorrow of his mother the Church, who in a manner slew her by assailing her while he was being born, was called by God the Father son of the right hand, since through him the divine power fought against the nations as though by its strong right hand, hurled the javelins of words with power, inflicted healing wounds upon hearts, and, the enemy being through him overcome and laid low, triumphed with glory. Hence it is that the same Paul says to the Galatians: « But when it pleased him who set me apart from my mother's womb, and called me by his grace, that he might reveal 591Bhis Son in me, and that I should preach him among the nations, immediately I consented not to flesh and blood (Gal. II). » Not undeservedly, then, is Paul called son of the right hand, through whom the whole multitude of the nations, which is to be set at God's right hand, is gathered to the sacraments of the faith. For girded with the word of God, which is the sword of the Spirit (Ephes. VI), he fights to this day and to the end of the age against the madness of all vices and of wicked spirits, and, like the lightning hand of Christ, brandishing the swords of heavenly eloquence, strikes off the stiff necks of those who resist God. Rightly therefore does Paul hold the dignity of the right side beside the Redeemer, who is not doubted to exercise the power of the divine right hand. Or does he not in a manner confess himself to be the right hand of God fighting against the devil, when he says: « But I 591Cso run, not as at an uncertainty; so I fight, not as one beating the air? » (I Cor. IX.)

CAPUT II. Pauli praerogativae enarrantur.

Paulus ad coelum tertium raptus ascendit (II Cor. XII), ubi etiam arcana verba, quae hominibus loqui non licet, audivit. Quis autem nesciat, quia vita terrena dicit sinistram, coelestis autem significat dexteram, sicut sponsa dicit in Canticis: « Laeva ejus sub capite meo, et dextera illius amplexabitur me? » (Cant. II.) Laeva namque esse sub capite dicitur, quia praesens vita apud electum quemlibet sub mente, quae procul dubio cogitationum caput est, despecta calcatur. Dextera vero Dei sponsa amplexari perhibetur, quia dum fidelis et sancta anima 591Daestuanti sui Creatoris igne succenditur, velut divinis quibusdam amplexibus undique confovetur. Qui ergo carnis adhuc fragilitate circumdatus, ad coelestem vitam ascendisse cognoscitur, recte filius dexterae nuncupatur. Hunc dexterae Filium Deus omnipotens tanquam propriam dexteram per totam mundi latitudinem semper extendit, et per eam adoptionis populos ad fidei unitatem colligit. Hanc dexteram summus agricola per Ecclesiae suae aream versat, et per eam spirituales segetes ventilat, ut purgata mystici frumenti grana horreis coelestibus introducat. Hanc dexteram suam Deus huc illucque procul extendit, dum ad colligendas in fidei sacramento gentes Paulus toto terrarum orbe discurrit. Ipse quippe quam valida 592Acharitate flagrat, tam nimia ex locis ad loca se vagatione permutat. 608 Transire ad alia ex aliis appetit, quia ipsius eum, quem implet, stimulus charitatis impellit. Longe namque a Romanis positus scribit: « Memoriam vestri facio semper in orationibus meis, obsecrans, si quo modo tandem aliquando prosperum iter habeam in voluntate Dei veniendi ad vos. Desidero enim videre vos (Rom. I). » Retentus Ephesi, Corinthiis scribit: « Ecce tertio hoc paratus sum venire ad vos (II Cor. XXII). » Rursum Ephesi commorans, Galatis loquitur, dicens: « Vellem me esse apud vos modo, et mutare vocem meam (Gal. IV). » Romae quoque custodia carceris clausus, quia ire per semetipsum ad Philippenses non permittitur, transmittere se discipulum pollicetur, dicens: 592B« Spero in Dominum Jesum, Timotheum cito me mittere ad vos; ut ego bono animo sim, cognitis quae circa vos sunt (Philip. II). » Constrictus etiam vinculis atque Ephesi retentus, Colossensibus scribit: « Nam et si corpore absens sum, sed spiritu vobiscum sum (Coloss. II). » Ecce cor apostolicum quanto fraterni amoris igne decoquitur, quanto ad desudandum pro salute gentium desiderio coarctatur! Hic corpore tenetur; illuc spiritu ducitur: et paterni amoris affectum praesentibus impendit, absentibus porrigit. Coram positis exhibet opera, audientibus exprimit vota. Efficaciter praesens eis, cum quibus non erat: nec tamen illis absens, cum quibus erat. Cujus amorem melius cognoscimus, si ejus adhuc ad Corinthios verba pensamus. Ait enim: « Veniam ad 592Cvos cum Macedoniam pertransiero; nam Macedoniam pertransibo, apud vos autem forsitan manebo, vel etiam hiemabo (I Cor. XVI). » Quid est, quod tam anxie per tot loca partitur, nisi quod circa omnes una charitate constringitur? Charitas enim, quae divisa unire consuevit, unum cor Pauli dividi per multa compellit. Quod tamen tanto ardentius in Deo colligit, quanto latius per sancta desideria spargit. Praedicando igitur Paulus vult simul omnia dicere, amando vult simul omnes videre; quia et in carne permanendo vult omnibus vivere, et de carne transeundo per sacrificium fidei vult omnibus prodesse. Nec mirum si praedicator egregius in fraterna charitate ferventius aestuat, si prae cunctis mortalibus in eo copia verae dilectionis abundat; cum fons vitae 592Dqui eum vas electionis appellat (Act. IX), in eum se decreverit abundanter influere, eumque dignatus sit sacramentorum suorum fluentis incomparabiliter irrigare. Hinc est quod ait: « An experimentum quaeritis ejus qui in me loquitur Christus? » (II Cor. XIII.)

CHAPTER II. The prerogatives of Paul are related.

Paul, caught up, ascended to the third heaven (II Cor. XII), where he also heard secret words which it is not lawful for men to speak. And who does not know that the earthly life bespeaks the left, while the heavenly signifies the right — as the bride says in the Canticles: « His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me? » (Cant. II.) For the left hand is said to be under the head because the present life, in any elect soul, is despised and trodden down beneath the mind, which is beyond doubt the head of the thoughts. But the bride is said to be embraced by the right hand of God because, when the faithful and holy soul 591Dis kindled with the burning fire of its Creator, it is cherished on every side as though by certain divine embraces. He therefore who, still girt about with the frailty of the flesh, is known to have ascended to the heavenly life, is rightly named son of the right hand. This son of the right hand almighty God stretches out perpetually, as his own right hand, through the whole breadth of the world, and by it gathers the peoples of adoption into the unity of the faith. This right hand the supreme husbandman turns over the threshing-floor of his Church, and by it winnows the spiritual crops, that he may bring the cleansed grains of the mystic wheat into the heavenly barns. This right hand of his God stretches far out hither and thither, while Paul runs through the whole globe of lands to gather the nations into the sacrament of the faith. For with as strong 592Aa charity as he burns, with so excessive a wandering does he shift himself from place to place. 608 He longs to pass to some men from others, because the goad of that very charity which fills him drives him on. For, set far from the Romans, he writes: « I make mention of you always in my prayers, beseeching, if by any means at length I may have a prosperous journey, by the will of God, of coming to you. For I long to see you (Rom. I). » Detained at Ephesus, he writes to the Corinthians: « Behold, this is the third time I am ready to come to you (II Cor. XXII). » Again, staying at Ephesus, he speaks to the Galatians, saying: « I would I were with you now, and could change my voice (Gal. IV). » Shut up too at Rome in the keeping of a prison, because he is not permitted to go to the Philippians in his own person, he promises to send a disciple across, saying: 592B« I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you shortly, that I also may be of good comfort, when I know the things concerning you (Philip. II). » Bound also with chains and held at Ephesus, he writes to the Colossians: « For though I am absent in body, yet I am with you in spirit (Coloss. II). » See with how great a fire of brotherly love the apostolic heart is boiled down, with how great a longing it is constrained to sweat for the salvation of the nations! Here he is held in body; thither he is led in spirit: and he spends the affection of a father's love upon those present, and reaches it out to those absent. To those set before him he displays his works; to those who hear him he expresses his prayers. He is effectively present to those with whom he was not, and yet not absent from those with whom he was. We know his love better if we still weigh his words to the Corinthians. For he says: « I will come to 592Cyou when I shall have passed through Macedonia; for I shall pass through Macedonia, but with you perhaps I shall remain, or even winter (I Cor. XVI). » What does it mean that he is so anxiously parceled out through so many places, unless that he is bound about all men by one charity? For charity, which is accustomed to unite what is divided, compels the one heart of Paul to be divided among many things. Yet it gathers that heart in God the more ardently, the more widely it scatters it through holy desires. In preaching, therefore, Paul wishes to say all things at once; in loving, he wishes to see all men at once; because both by remaining in the flesh he wishes to live for all, and by passing out of the flesh through the sacrifice of faith he wishes to profit all. Nor is it a wonder if the distinguished preacher glows more fervently in brotherly charity, if the abundance of true love overflows in him beyond all mortals; since the fountain of life 592Dwho calls him a vessel of election (Act. IX) determined to flow abundantly into him, and deigned to water him incomparably with the streams of his sacraments. Hence it is that he says: « Do you seek a proof of Christ, who speaks in me? » (II Cor. XIII.)

CAPUT III. Paulus prae caeteris omnibus privilegio admirabili est insignitus.

Hoc plane Salvator mundi B. Paulo per singularis gratiae privilegium contulit, qui nulli mortalium post Incarnationis suae mysterium praerogavit. Nam postquam beata illa atque divina Redemptoris humanitas ad paternae majestatis est evecta 593Aconsessum, ut non jam hominibus corporaliter interesset, sed coeli potius virtutibus praesideret, non per magistrum, sed specialiter per se ipsum sic illum efficaciter 609 docuit, omniumque sibi gestorum suorum mysteria delegavit; ut non modo praecessorum suorum magisterio nullatenus indigeret, sed eos insuper omnis coelestis sapientiae profunditate praecelleret. Unde et ipse primus apostolorum Petrus ejus admiratur sapientiam, dicens: « Charissimus frater noster Paulus, secundum datam sibi sapientiam scripsit vobis; sicut et in omnibus epistolis, loquens in eis de his, in quibus sunt quaedam difficilia intellectu, quae indocti et instabiles depravant (II Petr. III). » Quod autem in discendis evangelicae doctrinae mysteriis non magistrum quemlibet hominem, sed Deum 593Bspecialiter habuerit praeceptorem, testatur ipse, qui in principio Epistolae suae ad Galatas loquitur, dicens: « Paulus apostolus non ab hominibus, neque per hominem; sed per Jesum Christum et Deum Patrem, qui suscitavit eum a mortuis (Gal. I). » Fons enim aeternae vitae velut immensus torrens totum se simul in vas suae electionis immersit, qui in caeteros apostolos per longa temporum intervalla, sapientiae suae fluenta profudit. Et paulo post: « Notum enim vobis facio, fratres, Evangelium quod evangelizatum est a me, quia non est secundum hominem; neque enim ego ab homine accepi illud, neque didici, sed per revelationem Domini nostri Jesu Christi (Ibid.). » Quod ergo Petrum Dominus per multa argumenta adhuc mortalis edocuit, hoc 593Ctotum repente beato Paulo constitutus jam in gloria paternae majestatis exhibuit. Ac si doctor quispiam dum privatus est, discipulos doceat, postquam vero ad obtinenda regalis imperii sceptra conscendit, remotis clientium clastibus, unum inter multos egregium, quem nobilius instruat, elegit.

Ut ergo ad id unde coepit sermo recurrat, cum B. Petrus ad dexteram ponitur, primatus ejus, quem inter caeteros est sortitus apostolos, honoratur. Cum vero Paulus ejusdem dextri lateris occupat locum, in Benjamin, cujus est filius, mystice figurae redolet sacramentum. Quanquam et hoc non a mysterio vacet, quod B. Petrus Dominici lateris sinistram tenet. Per illum siquidem activa vita signatur. Et sicut contemplativa vita per dexteram, sic actualis exprimitur 593Dper sinistram. Quod autem per B. Petrum praesens vita, hoc est activa, figuretur, in hoc praecipue demonstratur, quod sicut evangelica frequentissime testatur historia, et Petrus Dominum plus omnibus condiscipulis diligat (Joan. XX); et Dominus non Petro, sed Joanni potius praerogativam specialis omnino dilectionis impendat (Joan. XIII). Per Joannem quippe contemplativa, per Petrum vero, sicut dictum est, activa vita signatur. Quia sancti omnes in hac vita ad similitudinem Petri, Deus plus amant, et quasi minus amantur; dum duras propter eum laborum molestias perferunt; mortali tamen adhuc carne circumdati, ad amplexus ejus dulcedinem non pertingunt. Illi vero qui jam cum Deo sunt, in hoc 594Aeum quodammodo minus amant, et magis amantur; dum et pro eo nullos laborum turbines sentiunt, et tamen intimae familiaritatis ejus oblectationibus perfruuntur. Non ergo mirum, si B. Petrus aliquando partem videatur tenere sinistram, qui 610 et activae vitae continet ex scripturarum auctoritate figuram.

CHAPTER III. Paul was marked out before all others by a wonderful privilege.

This plainly the Savior of the world conferred upon blessed Paul by the privilege of a singular grace, which he granted beforehand to no mortal after the mystery of his Incarnation. For after that blessed and divine humanity of the Redeemer had been carried up to the session 593Aof the Father's majesty, so that he was no longer bodily present among men, but rather presided over the powers of heaven, he taught him so effectively 609 not through a master but specially through himself, and made over to him the mysteries of all his own deeds, that not only did he in no way stand in need of the teaching of his predecessors, but he even surpassed them in the depth of all heavenly wisdom. Whence Peter himself, the first of the apostles, marvels at his wisdom, saying: « Our most dear brother Paul, according to the wisdom given him, has written to you; as also in all his epistles, speaking in them of these things, in which are certain things hard to be understood, which the unlearned and unstable wrest (II Petr. III). » And that in learning the mysteries of the evangelical doctrine he had no man whatever for master, but God 593Bspecially for his teacher, he himself testifies, who speaks at the beginning of his Epistle to the Galatians, saying: « Paul, an apostle not from men, neither through man, but through Jesus Christ and God the Father, who raised him from the dead (Gal. I). » For the fountain of eternal life, like a boundless torrent, plunged itself wholly and all at once into the vessel of its own election — that fountain which poured out the streams of its wisdom upon the rest of the apostles through long intervals of time. And a little after: « For I make known to you, brethren, the Gospel which was preached by me, that it is not according to man; for neither did I receive it from man, nor did I learn it, but by the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ (Ibid.). » What therefore the Lord, while still mortal, taught Peter by many proofs, all this 593Che displayed at once to blessed Paul when he was already established in the glory of the Father's majesty. It is as if some teacher, while he is a private man, should teach disciples; but after he has climbed to the holding of the scepters of royal rule, then, the clastibus of dependents being removed, chooses one distinguished man among many, whom he may instruct more nobly.

That the discourse, then, may run back to the point where it began: when blessed Peter is set at the right, the primacy which he obtained among the other apostles is honored. But when Paul occupies the place of the same right side, he gives off the sacred sign of the mystical figure in Benjamin, whose son he is. And yet neither is this without mystery, that blessed Peter holds the left of the Lord's side. For by him the active life is marked out. And as the contemplative life is expressed by the right, so the active 593Dby the left. And that the present life, that is, the active, is figured by blessed Peter, is shown chiefly in this: that, as the evangelical history very frequently testifies, Peter loves the Lord more than all his fellow-disciples (Joan. XX), and yet the Lord bestows the prerogative of an altogether special love not upon Peter but rather upon John (Joan. XIII). For by John the contemplative life is marked out, but by Peter, as has been said, the active. Because all the saints in this life, after the likeness of Peter, love God more and are as it were loved less, while for his sake they endure the hard troubles of labors, and yet, still girt about with mortal flesh, do not attain to the sweetness of his embraces. But those who are already with God, in this 594Ain a manner love him less and are loved more, since both they feel no storms of labors for his sake, and yet they enjoy to the full the delights of his inmost intimacy. It is no wonder, then, if blessed Peter should sometimes seem to hold the left-hand part, who 610 also holds, on the authority of the Scriptures, the figure of the active life.

CAPUT IV. Cur B. Paulus propriam non habeat cathedram.

Unum vero in B. Paulo videtur, egregium, quia cum omnes apostoli distributi terrarum regionibus, proprias obtineant cathedras; iste, dum nullam specialiter teneat, videtur aliquatenus communiter omnibus praesidere. ( Vide scholia ad calcem opusculi. ) Plane quia ipse toto terrarum orbe universalem 594Bfundavit Ecclesiam, dignum est, ut sicut in omnibus fidei semen sparsit, sic etiam in omnibus jus teneat praesidentis. Dicit enim ad Corinthios: « Gratia Dei sum id quod sum; et gratia ejus in me vacua non fuit, sed abundantius illis omnibus laboravi; non ego autem, sed gratia Dei mecum (I Cor. XV). » Ad Romanos autem: « Habeo, inquit, gloriam in Christo Jesu ad Deum, in obedientia gentium: verbo et factis, in virtute signorum, et prodigiorum, in virtute Spiritus sancti; ita ut ab Hierusalem per circuitum usque ad Illyricum repleverim Evangelio Christi. Sic autem hoc praedicavi Evangelium, non ubi nominatus est Christus, ne super alienum fundamentum aedificarem; sed sicut scriptum est: Quibus non est annuntiatum de eo, videbunt; et qui 594Cnon audierunt, intelligent (Rom. XV; Isa. LII). Haec est igitur causa cur Paulus nullam specialiter cathedram teneat, quia si audemus summis minora conferre, ad instar Christi, salvo caeterorum apostolorum jure, videtur omnibus Ecclesiis praesidere.

Porro autem quantum ad vulgarem pertinet sensum, quoniam Hierosolymis passus est Dominus, universis Ecclesiis non Romana, sed Hierosolymitana potius praeesse deberet Ecclesia; verumtamen cum ex auctoritate canonum, Romana Ecclesia primum teneat locum, Alexandrina secundum, Antiochena tertium, Constantinopolitana quartum, Hierosolymitana jam quintum: constat Dominum Salvatorem non uni cuilibet cathedrae speciali jure praeesse, sed cunctis unum pastorem universaliter praesidere. 594DLiquet ergo Ecclesiarum ordinem esse dispositum juxta privilegium Petri, non secundum incomparabilem excellentiam Redemptoris. Quia quod omnes apostoli juxta mensuram suam obtinent per gratiam collati muneris, hoc totum possidet Christus per naturalis imperium majestatis. Quamdam ergo, si dici liceat, cum Christo similitudinem Paulus tenet, dum non uni duntaxat Ecclesiae, sed omnibus praesidet. Non igitur sine causa B. Paulus dextrum in picturis obtinet latus, dum id non modo deposcat propensior fructuum cumulus, sed et figuralis intelligentiae sacramentum. Gliscit adhuc animus super hoc themate plura conscribere, sed litterarum bajulus, qui ad vos properare festinat, dum potius vellet 595Avolare, quam currere, non me permittit saltem minimas ad scribendum moras habere. Clementia vobis, dilectissimi, divina praecipiat, ut pro me orare sancta vestra fraternitas non desistat.

CHAPTER IV. Why blessed Paul has no chair of his own.

But one thing appears distinguished in blessed Paul: that whereas all the apostles, distributed among the regions of the earth, hold their own chairs, he, while holding none in particular, seems in some measure to preside over all in common. ( Vide scholia ad calcem opusculi. ) Plainly, because he himself founded the universal 594BChurch over the whole globe of lands, it is fitting that, as he scattered the seed of faith among all, so also he should hold among all the right of one presiding. For he says to the Corinthians: « By the grace of God I am what I am; and his grace in me has not been void, but I have labored more abundantly than all of them; yet not I, but the grace of God with me (I Cor. XV). » And to the Romans: « I have, he says, glory in Christ Jesus toward God, in the obedience of the nations: by word and deeds, in the power of signs and of wonders, in the power of the Holy Spirit; so that from Jerusalem round about even to Illyricum I have filled up the Gospel of Christ. And so I have preached this Gospel, not where Christ has been named, lest I should build upon another man's foundation; but as it is written: They to whom it was not announced of him shall see; and they who 594Chave not heard shall understand (Rom. XV; Isa. LII). This, then, is the reason why Paul holds no chair in particular: because — if we dare to compare lesser things with the highest — after the likeness of Christ, saving the right of the other apostles, he seems to preside over all the Churches.

Further, however, as far as concerns the common understanding: since the Lord suffered at Jerusalem, not the Roman but rather the Jerusalem Church ought to be set over all the Churches. Yet, since by the authority of the canons the Roman Church holds the first place, the Alexandrian the second, the Antiochene the third, the Constantinopolitan the fourth, and the Jerusalem Church now the fifth, it is clear that the Lord and Savior does not preside over any one chair by a special right, but that as one shepherd he presides universally over all. 594DIt is plain, then, that the order of the Churches has been arranged according to the privilege of Peter, not according to the incomparable excellence of the Redeemer. For what all the apostles obtain according to their measure by the grace of a gift conferred, all this Christ possesses by the sovereignty of a majesty that is his by nature. Paul therefore holds a certain likeness, if one may say so, with Christ, in that he presides not over one Church only, but over all. Not without cause, then, does blessed Paul hold the right side in the paintings, since not only the weightier heap of fruits demands it, but also the sacred sign of a figural understanding. My mind swells still to write more upon this theme, but the bearer of the letter, who hastens to hurry to you, and would rather 595Afly than run, does not permit me to have even the least delays for writing. May the divine mercy give you commandment, most beloved, that your holy brotherhood cease not to pray for me.

Plainly, because he himself (Paul) founded the universal Church over the whole globe of lands, it is fitting that, as he scattered the seed of faith among all, so also he should hold among all the right of one presiding. In this Epistle the blessed Peter Damian seems to attribute more than enough to Paul: for although the things he reports of that Apostle's deeds are true, they must nevertheless be understood with caution, since the shepherd and prince of the whole world is Peter only, not Paul. Wherefore the eulogy is indeed true 595Bwhich the Church attributes to him when she says: a preacher in the whole world; « true is what he himself affirms: (My daily pressing care, the solicitude of all the Churches (II Cor. XI). » But nevertheless it is most certain that that great bonorem which, at first appearance, Cardinal Damian pays to Saint Paul belongs more fittingly to Saint Peter 612 596A— of whom Leo the Great ( epist. ad Anast. Thess. ) says: « Among the most blessed apostles, he says, in a likeness of honor, there was a certain distinction of power; and although the election of all was equal, yet to one it was given to be preeminent over the rest: and from this pattern the distinction of bishops also arose. » Saint John Chrysostom (hom. 87 in Joan.): Why at last, the others being passed over, does he address Peter alone concerning these things? He was the mouth of the apostles, and the prince, and the summit of that very company; and for that reason Paul too went up to see him rather than the others. And so we shall understand Damian according to the mind of Gregory the Great (GREG. in lib. I Reg., cap. 4), so that, as regards the apostolic office of preaching the Gospel and of loosing sins, we may reckon that all the apostles equally — not Paul alone — were sent into the whole world, 596Byet with a certain subordination to Peter, who was set over the universal flock, as Saint Cyprian teaches at length in On the Unity of the Church, and in Epistle 55 to Pope Cornelius.
