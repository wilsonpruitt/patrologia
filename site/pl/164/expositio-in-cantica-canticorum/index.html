Bruno of Segni

Bruno Astensis

c. 1045–1123

Born at Solero in Piedmont and educated at Bologna, Bruno was made bishop of Segni in 1079 and consecrated by Gregory VII, whose reform he defended in print — his Libellus de simoniacis argues the case against simony. In 1099 he withdrew to Monte Cassino without giving up his see, and was elected its abbot in 1107; in 1111 Paschal II obliged him to resign the abbacy and return to Segni after he criticized the concessions of the Concordat of Ponte Mammolo. He died at Segni on 18 July 1123 and was canonized in 1181. His surviving work is overwhelmingly exegetical.

« Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » The voice of the aforesaid woman.

1234CMany things, she says, you have often announced to me, O envoys of the heavenly King; many things concerning his nobility, his riches, his strength, his beauty, and many things concerning the man's wisdom 1235Ayou have reported. I have seen Moses, I have seen Elias, I have seen Jeremias, I have seen David, and I have seen others too, who announced his coming to me. I believe their words, I languish with love, I am ready; let the envoys cease. Now let him come himself, let him be made manifest, let him fasten kisses upon me, let him give embraces, and let him speak to me with his own mouth. Apostrophe. Thee, she says, I love, and I desire; I long to hear thee, to see thee. Why so? « Because thy breasts are better than wine. » Moses gave me wine, the rest of the prophets gave me wine — bitter and strong, harsh and rough to drink. For the words of the prophets are hard, not only to do, but even to speak. For, to say nothing of uttering and pronouncing them, in certain places they can scarcely be understood. But the preaching of Christ turned the wine into milk. For the two breasts are the two testaments, 1235Bfrom which Christ, by teaching and expounding, drew back a milky and sweet suavity. For those whom the law looked to slay, he mercifully healed. For the Jews wished to stone the woman taken in adultery, but Christ said, « Neither will I condemn thee (Joan. VII, 10). » And these words are better than wine. « Fragrant with the finest ointments. » What is signified by the ointment, if not the sevenfold grace of the Holy Spirit? And with these both the New and the Old Testament are fragrant, and shine, and give forth their scent. This odor the preaching of Christ brought in, since it made some wise, others understanding, others strong, others devout. « And the odor of thy ointments is above all spices. » By the spices we understand whatever things are odor-bearing. But what could be of so great an odor and so great a sweetness that it might be the Spirit himself, filled with whom the Apostle said: 1235C« We are the good odor of Christ unto God? » (II Cor. II, 15.) The voice of the Church to Christ: « Thy name is as ointment poured out. » Thy name, she says, like ointment poured out, has furnished health to all that believe. For Christ is named from the chrism, in which name we are all called Christians, cleansed by the water of baptism. CAPUT IV.

Vox Christi: « Quam pulchra es, amica mea, quam 1255Cpulchra es! » Cur autem bis dixit quam pulchra es, nisi quia in opere et sermone, interius et exterius, in anima et corpore pulchra est? « Oculi tui columbarum, » id est intellectus, ratio, intuitus tuus simplex est et acutus, non ira confusus, non furoris sanguine perturbatus, non in superbiam elatus. « Absque eo quod intrinsecus latet. » Multa inquit, de te dici possunt. Laudabilis es in virtute, in constantia, in sapientia. Magnum est quod videtur, maximum est quod latet. Magna in te fulget operatio, magna in coelesti secretario tibi servatur retributio. Magnus est labor, sed maxima spes. Possumus etiam et per oculos prophetas intelligere, qui ea quae futura erant multo ante praeviderant. Hi columbae dicuntur, 1255Dquoniam in humilitate et simplicitate cordis Domino servierunt. « Capilli tui sicut greges caprarum. » Per capillos eos intelligimus, qui ab Adam usque ad Christum fidem servaverunt. Capillis namque nulla pars 1256Asuperius est. Merito ergo capilli isti dicuntur; siquidem secundum tempus totius Ecclesiae sunt superiores. Sed quare gregi caprarum assimilantur, nisi quia carnaliter vivebant, et luxuriae carnisque voluptatibus serviebant? His enim dictum fuerat: « Crescite, et multiplicamini (Gen. IX, 7). » Ex talibus autem David et Salomon fuisse probantur, quorum concubinarum non erat numerus. Sola igitur fide isti salvantur. Hi sunt, qui per fidem vicerunt regna. « Quae ascenderunt a monte Galaad. » Galaad namque acervus testimonii interpretatur. Ascenderunt igitur de monte Galaad, quia legis et prophetarum testimonia observantes coelorum regna scandere meruerunt. « Dentes tui sicut greges tonsarum ovium. » Dentes apostoli. Sed quare dentes, nisi quia prophetarum 1256Bet legis graviores sententias ruminantes et exponentes, Ecclesiam pascunt et nutriunt? Cur autem greges tonsarum ovium, nisi quia saecularibus omnibus venditis, et pauperibus attributis, nudi, tonsi, vitiis exspoliati in innocentia et simplicitate Domino servierunt? « Quae ascenderunt de lavacro. » De lavacro quidem ascenderunt, quoniam baptismatis aqua mundati, vitiisque omnibus expulsis, ad summum fidei et doctrinae, et ad coelos sublimati sunt. « Omnes gemellis fetibus. » Gemellos quidem fetus habent, quoniam inter medios cleros dormientes Novum et Vetus praedicant Testamentum. Possumus autem et per gemellos fetus doctrinam et operationem intelligere, sive etiam carnis et animae castitatem, vel Dei et proximi dilectionem. « Et sterilis non 1256Cest » inter 617 eas, quoniam multiplici semine fructificant.

« Sicut vitta coccinea labia tua. » Quid per labia, nisi episcopos, et sacerdotes, caeterosque, qui Deum assidue laudant et benedicunt? Hi autem vitta coccinea esse dicuntur. Sed quare vitta? Quia quasi multos capillos, ita multos fideles in una fide ligant; sive quia multas virtutes, multasque sententias simul adunant; sive etiam quia cordis sui cogitationes comprimunt, ne vento vanitatis per inania rapiantur. Cur autem coccineae? Quia et igne sancti Spiritus accensi sunt, et Christi praedicant passionem. Coccus ignis, vel sanguinis speciem habet. « Et eloquium tuum dulce. » Dulcia quidem sunt sanctorum eloquia, et auditu suavia. Unde et illud: 1256D« Quam dulcia faucibus meis eloquia tua, super mel ori meo! » (Psal. CXVIII, 103) -- « Sicut fragmen mali Punici, ita genae tuae. » Genae namque 1257ADei martyres intelliguntur, qui coram et in faciem Christum confidentes pro eo mortui sunt. Hi autem fragmen mali punici esse dicuntur, quoniam proprio sanguine cruentati sunt. Genae igitur dicuntur, quia et de peccatis erubescunt; fragmen autem, quoniam pro Christi nomine trucidantur et franguntur; mali vero punici propter sanguinis effusionem. « Absque eo, quod intrinsecus latet. » Pulchrae, inquit, sunt genae, rosea est facies, virginalis et pudicus est vultus; multo tamen laudabiliora sunt, quae nondum apparent, quae in corde latent, quae in coelesti thesauro tibi reservantur. « Sicut turris David collum tuum. » Per collum Ecclesiae doctores intelligimus. Per collum namque transit cibus, quo venter reficitur et saturatur; per collum 1257Bquoque exeunt verba, omnisque doctrinae documenta; collum denique sustinet scutum, quo totum tegitur corpus. Doctores autem Ecclesiam nutriunt, docent, muniunt et defendunt. Praeterea cum Ecclesia Christum conjungunt, sicut collum cum capite corpus. Hi autem sic totam defendunt Ecclesiam, sicut turris David totam quondam defendebat Jerusalem.

« Quae aedificata est cum propugnaculis suis. » Sic autem et doctores propugnacula habent, sapientiam videlicet et virtutem, quae nulla possunt machina expugnari, nullisque inimicorum instrumentis destrui. « Mille clypei pendent ex ea. » Mille namque clypei pendent a doctoribus, quoniam per innumerabiles sententias et argumentationes ab eis 1257Cdefluentes, Ecclesiam protegunt. « Omnis armatura fortium. » Nihil enim in ea vinci, vel frangi potest. « Duo ubera tua sicut duo hinnulli capreae gemelli. » Una enim caprea, una est Ecclesia, cujus visus acutus, cujus pedes agiles, cujus voluntas altiora petit, cujus cursus coelos ascendit; duo habet ubera, quia duo sunt gemelli. Duo namque ubera duo Testamenta sunt. Duo vero gemelli duos populos designant: Judaicum videlicet et gentilem. Hi autem gemelli dicuntur, quoniam et gemini sunt, et geminam charitatem observant. « Qui pascuntur in liliis, donec aspiret dies, et inclinentur umbrae. » In liliis quidem pascuntur, qui in suavibus et odoriferis Scripturarum sententiis delectantur. « Donec 1257Daspiret dies; » illa scilicet de qua dicitur: Quia « melior est dies una in atriis tuis, super millia (Psal. LXXXIII, 10). » -- « Et inclinentur umbrae. » fugiatque caligo, et tenebrarum omnium princeps cum tota militia sua in infernum praecipitetur. Sponsus 1258Ade sponsa dicit: « Vadam ad montem myrrhae. » Vadam, inquit, Dominus ad montem myrrhae, visitabo terras, carnem suscipiam. Mons namque myrrhae caro est, quam assumpsit. Hic autem est ille mons, qui tantum crevit, ut totam terram compleret. Myrrhae autem ideo dicitur, quia corruptionem non vidit; juxta illud: « Nec dabis sanctum tuum videre corruptionem (Psal. XV, 10). » Possumus autem et per hunc montem Ecclesiam intelligere, quae carnem suam cum vitiis et concupiscentiis mortificans, in amaritudine semper moratur.

« Et ad collem thuris, » id est ad eos, quorum orationes me oblectant, sicut suavis odor thuris. « Tota pulchra es, amica mea, et macula non est in te. » Tota namque pulchra est, quia neque 1258Bmaculam, neque rugam habet. Sponsus ad sponsam: « Veni de Libano, sponsa mea, veni de Libano, veni. » In Libano, inquit, es, dealbata es in virtutibus, florum sanctitate nites; nihil tenebrosum, nihil nigrum, nihil obscurum in te esse conspicitur. Veni igitur ad me, et quae semper pulchritudinem dilexisti, sicut sol in regno meo fulgeas in aeternum. Ter autem dixit « veni, » quia tria sunt, sine quibus non ad eum venitur; id est fides, spes, et charitas; 618 sive quia cogitatione, locutione, operatione Domino servit. Cur autem sponsa vocetur, superius dictum est: « Coronaberis de capite Amana. » Amanus namque mons est Ciliciae, qui et Taurus appellatur.

« De vertice Sanir et Hermon. » Hi autem montes 1258Cin Judaea sunt. In his autem leones et pardi habitant, sicut sequentia manifestant. « De cubilibus leonum, de montibus pardorum. » Per hos autem montes, illos intelligimus, in quibus vitiorum exercitus, leones, et pardi, et ipsi maligni spiritus inhabitant. His autem devictis et expulsis, coronatur Ecclesia: « Nemo enim coronabitur, nisi qui legitime certaverit (II Tim. II, 5). » Coronatur igitur de supradictorum montium capite, quia diabolo devicto, qui illorum caput et dominus est, pro tanto certamine praemium suscipit, et coronam. « Vulnerasti cor meum soror, mea sponsa. » Soror quidem, quia ejusdem patris filia est. Unde Apostolus ait: « Si autem filii et haeredes; haeredes quidem 1258DDei, cohaeredes autem Christi (Rom. VIII, 17). » Sponsa vero, quia carnem nostram sumens de virginali utero Christus processit. Haec autem soror et conjux sponsi sui cor vulneravit, amore scilicet, mortisque timore. « In uno oculorum tuorum. » 1259AMultos namque oculos habet Ecclesia, plena est oculis ante et retro, undique videt, undique latronum propellit insidias; unum tantum oculum splendidissimum habet; una Trinitatis fide illuminatur, sine qua Deo nullus placebit. Hunc autem oculum qui non habent, caeci sunt, et in tenebris ambulant. Unde Apostolus: « Caecitas, inquit, ex parte contigit in Israel (Rom. XI, 25). » -- « Et in uno crine colli tui. » Confirmatio est. Multi namque crines multa doctorum exempla et sententiae sunt. Sed quare crines, nisi quia subtiles et prolixi sunt? Unus autem crinis, una est sententia, quae apud omnes eadem est, in qua Christianorum nullus dissidet, quam apostoli praedicaverunt, et pro qua martyres sanguinem fuderunt, quam sancti Patres 1259Bconfirmaverunt. « Quam pulchrae sunt mammae tuae, soror mea sponsa! » Pulchrae sunt mammae, pulchri sunt et filii. Synagogae namque mammae pulchrae non sunt, quoniam nigro lacte, sordidissima faece, et erroris intelligentia filios nutrivit.

« Pulchriora sunt ubera tua vino. » Metonymia est. Ecce vino lac comparatur, quod utique candidius et suavius est. Natos suos lactat Ecclesia, adulterinos suos inebriat Synagoga. Hi autem sapienter educati, celeriter crescunt; illi vero sicut ebrii, sensum amittunt. De quibus ipse Dominus ait: « Et in me psallebant, qui bibebant vinum (Psal. LXVIII, 13). » -- « Et odor unguentorum tuorum, » id est virtutum, sermonum et gratiarum. Haec enim omnia unguentum 1259Cdicuntur, quia et suavia sunt, et omnium infirmitatum languores sanant. « Super omnia aromata, » id est prae cunctis Judaeorum sacrificiis. « Favus distillans labia tua. » Favus enim mel est in cera, per quem spiritualis intelligentia significatur in littera. Favum distillant labia Ecclesiae, id est dulcia et suavia verba. « Mel et lac sub lingua tua. » Quibus omne genus hominum delectatur. Possumus autem et per mel, quod de rore coeli conficitur, spiritualem et altiorem doctrinam intelligere; de qua Apostolus ait: « Sapientiam loquimur inter perfectos (I Cor. II, 6); » per lac vero mediocrem; unde ipse ait: « Lac vobis potum dedi, non escam (I Cor. III, 2). » -- « Et odor vestimentorum tuorum sicut odor thuris. » Habet enim Ecclesia vestimentum, 1259Dquod ipsa suis duxit et texuit manibus; virtutibus enim virtutes conjungens, operibusque bonis bona opera connectens, admirabilem sibi composuit vestem; in qua non minus delectatur sponsus quam in multo suavissimo thuris odore. « Hortus conclusus 1260Aest soror mea sponsa. » Hortus quidem vocatur, quia quasi diversa florum et arborum genera, ita diversi sanctorum et fidelium ordines in ea continentur. Ibi sancti apostoli, confessores et martyres, virgines et viduae. Conclusus autem, quoniam virtutum et angelorum munita praesidiis. Nulla vitiis patet porta, per quam latrones, et hostes subintrent, qui furentur, et rapiant bona. « Hortus conclusus, et fons signatus. » Fons quidem baptismatis est in Ecclesia, quo et vitiorum sordes auferuntur, et animae et corporis maculae purgantur. Est autem fons vivus in horto isto, cujus aquam qui bibit, non sitiet ultra. De hoc autem fonte dicitur: « Sitivit anima mea ad Deum fontem vivum (Psal. XLI, 3). » Sed cur signatus, nisi quia in Trinitatis 1260Bnomine sanctificatur et benedicitur? Signatus est fons, cognoscitur aqua, ad hanc sancti currunt, hanc et vitia, et maligni spiritus fugiunt. Hoc enim signo fons iste ab aliis discernitur, quoniam quae iste fecit, alii facere nequeunt. « Emissiones tuae paradisus malorum 619 punicorum, cum pomorum fructibus. » Quem hortum superius dixit, paradisum hic appellat; quoniam paradisus est hortus deliciarum. Vocatur igitur Ecclesia paradisus, quoniam omnium deliciarum copiis repleta est. Hic enim est lignum scientiae boni et mali, Novum videlicet et Vetus Testamentum, quae et mala devitare, et bona nos facere docent. Habemus et lignum vetitum; id est Christum, qui est panis vivus, qui de coelo descendit. Quatuor paradisi flumina, quatuor sunt 1260CEvangelia. Sed quae sunt ejus emissiones, quae sunt munera, quae sunt poma, qui sunt flores, quos hic hortus malorum punicorum fructus Domino mittit? Mala, inquit, punica mittit, multosque martyres Domino mandat. Mala namque punica vos estis apostoli, qui pro Christi nomine sanguinem fudistis. « Cum pomorum fructibus; » non solum, inquit, martyres mittit, sed cum aliorum pomorum fructibus, cum virginibus et confessoribus mala punica praesentat. « Cypri cum nardo. Nardus et crocus, fistula et cinnamomum, cum universis lignis Libani, myrrha et aloe cum omnibus primis unguentis, » subauditur, sunt in hoc horto. In his autem intelligimus, quoniam omnium aromatum genera in sancta Ecclesia versantur. Per cyprum namque quo 1260Dregium paratur unguentum, et per nardum, qui suavissimi magnique odoris est, omnes gratiae Spiritus sancti intelligi possunt, quae et suavitate et odore omnibus praeferuntur. Cyprus namque cum nardo ponitur, quoniam qui divino et regio unguento unguntur, 1261Amagnus et suavissimus odor Deo sunt. Unde Apostolus, « Christi, inquit, bonus odor sumus Deo (II Cor. II, 15). » Per crocum namque, fistulam et cinnamomum, quae brevia sunt, nec altius crescunt, simplices et humiles intelligimus, quos, quamvis vulgo homines non venerentur, magnae tamen virtutis et sanctitatis sunt. In his autem persona est humilis, sed virtus excelsa. « Cum universis lignis Libani. » Per haec autem apostolos et prophetas intelligas, qui altitudine, robore et pulchritudine caeteris praesunt; siquidem ligna Libani magnae sunt altitudinis. Myrrha vero et aloe, quae putredini, vermibusque resistunt, castitatem et alias virtutes significant, quae a vitiorum contactu et animam et corpus servant illaesa. « Cum omnibus primis » et maximis 1261B« unguentis, » per quae fides, spes et charitas designari possunt, quae in saecula saeculorum detinent principatum. « Fons hortorum, puteus aquarum viventium. » Hic, inquit, est fons hortorum, hic sunt apostoli et doctores, hic est illa aqua, qua totus irrigatur mundus. Multi enim horti multae sunt Ecclesiae. En puteus viventium aquarum, en profunditas Scripturarum. Una enim est doctrina apostolorum, mortuusque error haereticorum: hanc aquam qui bibunt, non moriuntur; illam qui bibunt, vivere nequeunt. Illi tamen de fonte bibunt, qui Scripturarum superficiem summis tenus labiis delibant. De puteo non sumunt profundiores aquas, id est difficiliores sententias non hauriunt. « Quae fluunt impetu de Libano. » Impetu, inquit, magna scilicet 1261Cconstantia, virtute, et affluentia hae aquae fluunt. Sed unde fluunt? de impetu, de Libano, de monte candidato. Libanus enim, ut dictum saepe est, candidatio interpretatur. Christus gratias convocat. « Surge, aquilo, et veni, auster, perfla hortum meum, et fluant aromata illius. » Dixit superius hortum, dixit arbores et poma, dixit fontem et puteum, quibus irrigatur; nunc autem praecipit ventis ut surgant, hortum perflent, aromata ventilent, quatenus sibi odor reddatur suavior. Venti enim in Christi hortum insurgebant, quando tyrannorum impetus et furor Ecclesiam persequendo sanctos trucidabant. Tunc enim aromata fluebant, tunc martyrum constantia et virtutes redolebant, tunc suavissimus 1261Dodor rationabilis holocausti, et puri sacrificii ante Domini conspectum ferebatur. Possumus tamen per aquilonem, qui asper ventus et frigidus est, tyrannorum crudelitatem intelligere. Per austrum vero, qui serenus est, fallaciae blandimenta designantur; unde dicitur: « Inimici ejus terram lingent (Psal. LXXI, 9). »

Versus: Diximus Ecclesiam Sponsam Regis regiam, 1262ACujus estis oculi, vos prophetae, Vos dentes fortissimi, Petre et Paule, Qui eam reficitis assuete. Genae semper rubeae Martyres sunt utique. Imperfecti quilibet sunt capilli, Et minoris meriti imbecilli, Quia sunt instabiles sicut illi. Collum ferens clypeos Dicimus episcopos, Qui defendunt fortiter suas oves: Persequentes vitia, et leones, Serpentinas furias, et dracones. Hortus est tutissimus Sanctorum sanctissimus, 1262BQui non caret liliis neque rosis; Nec privantur arbores suis pomis. Ibi fons dulcissimus est saporis, Hunc ventorum flamina, 620 Tyrannorum agmina Perturbant et ventilant cum furore; Coelum petunt martyres cum honore; Delectatur Dominus in odore.

CHAPTER IV.

The voice of Christ: « How beautiful art thou, my friend, how 1255Cbeautiful art thou! » And why did he say twice, how beautiful art thou, if not because she is beautiful in work and in speech, within and without, in soul and in body? « Thy eyes are those of doves, » that is, thy understanding, thy reason, thy gaze is simple and keen, not confounded by anger, not disturbed by the blood of rage, not lifted up into pride. « Besides that which lieth hid within. » Many things, he says, can be said of thee. Thou art praiseworthy in virtue, in constancy, in wisdom. Great is that which is seen, greatest is that which lies hid. Great is the work that shines in thee; great is the recompense kept for thee in the heavenly treasury. Great is the labor, but greatest is the hope. We may also understand by the eyes the prophets, who long beforehand foresaw the things that were to come. These are called doves, 1255Dsince they served the Lord in humility and simplicity of heart. « Thy hair is as flocks of goats. » By the hair we understand those who kept the faith from Adam until Christ. For no part is higher than the hair. 1256ARightly therefore are they called hair, since indeed in respect of time they are higher than the whole Church. But why are they likened to a flock of goats, if not because they lived carnally, and served luxury and the pleasures of the flesh? For to them it had been said: « Increase and multiply (Gen. IX, 7). » And of such sort David and Solomon are proved to have been, of whose concubines there was no number. By faith alone therefore are these saved. These are they who by faith conquered kingdoms. « Which came up from mount Galaad. » For Galaad is interpreted a heap of testimony. They came up therefore from mount Galaad, because, observing the testimonies of the law and of the prophets, they deserved to climb the kingdoms of heaven. « Thy teeth as flocks of shorn sheep. » The teeth are the apostles. But why teeth, if not because, chewing over and expounding the weightier 1256Bsentences of the prophets and of the law, they feed and nourish the Church? And why flocks of shorn sheep, if not because, when all worldly things had been sold and given to the poor, they served the Lord naked, shorn, and stripped of vices, in innocence and simplicity? « Which came up from the washing. » From the washing indeed they came up, since, cleansed by the water of baptism and with all vices driven out, they were raised to the summit of faith and doctrine, and to the heavens. « All with twin young. » Twin young indeed they have, since, sleeping among the midst of the lots, they preach the New and the Old Testament. And we may understand by the twin young also teaching and work, or else the chastity of flesh and of soul, or the love of God and of neighbor. « And there is none barren 1256C» among 617 them, since they bring forth fruit with manifold seed.

« Thy lips are as a scarlet fillet. » What is meant by the lips, if not the bishops and the priests, and the others who continually praise and bless God? And these are said to be a scarlet fillet. But why a fillet? Because, as it binds many hairs, so they bind many of the faithful in one faith; or because they gather together at once many virtues and many sentences; or else because they hold in the thoughts of their heart, lest these be snatched through empty things by the wind of vanity. And why scarlet? Because they are both kindled with the fire of the Holy Spirit, and preach the passion of Christ. Scarlet has the appearance of fire, or of blood. « And thy speech is sweet. » Sweet indeed are the sayings of the saints, and pleasant to hear. Whence also that word: 1256D« How sweet are thy words to my palate, above honey to my mouth! » (Psal. CXVIII, 103) -- « As a piece of a pomegranate, so are thy cheeks. » For by the cheeks 1257Athe martyrs of God are understood, who, trusting in Christ openly and to his face, died for him. And these are said to be a piece of a pomegranate, since they were made bloody with their own blood. They are called cheeks, then, because they also blush for their sins; a piece, because they are slaughtered and broken for the name of Christ; and of a pomegranate, because of the shedding of blood. « Besides that which lieth hid within. » Beautiful, he says, are thy cheeks, rosy is thy face, virginal and chaste is thy countenance; yet far more praiseworthy are the things which do not yet appear, which lie hid in the heart, which are kept for thee in the heavenly treasury. « Thy neck is as the tower of David. » By the neck we understand the doctors of the Church. For through the neck passes the food by which the belly is refreshed and filled; through the neck 1257Balso come out words and the teachings of all doctrine; and lastly the neck bears up the shield, by which the whole body is covered. And the doctors nourish, teach, fortify, and defend the Church. Moreover they join the Church to Christ, as the neck joins the body to the head. And these defend the whole Church, as the tower of David once defended all Jerusalem.

« Which is built with bulwarks. » And so the doctors too have bulwarks, namely wisdom and virtue, which can be stormed by no engine, nor destroyed by any instruments of the enemy. « A thousand shields hang upon it. » For a thousand shields hang from the doctors, since by innumerable sentences and arguments flowing down from them 1257Cthey protect the Church. « All the armor of valiant men. » For nothing in her can be conquered or broken. « Thy two breasts are as two twin fawns of a roe. » For the roe is one, and the Church is one, whose sight is keen, whose feet are nimble, whose will seeks the higher things, whose course ascends the heavens; she has two breasts, because there are two twins. For the two breasts are the two Testaments. And the two twins designate two peoples, namely the Jewish and the Gentile. And these are called twins, since they are both born together and keep a twofold charity. « Which feed among the lilies, till the day breathe, and the shadows decline. » They feed indeed among the lilies who take delight in the sweet and fragrant sentences of the Scriptures. « Till 1257Dthe day breathe; » that day, namely, of which it is said: For « better is one day in thy courts above thousands (Psal. LXXXIII, 10). » -- « And the shadows decline. » and let the gloom flee, and let the prince of all darkness with his whole army be cast headlong into hell. The Bridegroom 1258Asays concerning the bride: « I will go to the mountain of myrrh. » I will go, says the Lord, to the mountain of myrrh; I will visit the lands, I will take flesh. For the mountain of myrrh is the flesh which he took. And this is that mountain which grew so great that it filled the whole earth. And it is called of myrrh for this reason, that it saw no corruption; according to that word: « Neither wilt thou give thy holy one to see corruption (Psal. XV, 10). » And we may also understand by this mountain the Church, which, mortifying her flesh with its vices and concupiscences, abides always in bitterness.

« And to the hill of frankincense, » that is, to those whose prayers delight me as the sweet odor of frankincense. « Thou art all beautiful, my friend, and there is not a spot in thee. » For she is all beautiful, because she has neither 1258Bspot nor wrinkle. The Bridegroom to the bride: « Come from Libanus, my bride, come from Libanus, come. » Thou art in Libanus, he says, thou art made white in virtues, thou shinest with the sanctity of flowers; nothing dark, nothing black, nothing obscure is seen to be in thee. Come therefore to me, and thou who hast always loved beauty, shine like the sun in my kingdom for ever. And he said « come » three times, because there are three things without which one does not come to him — that is, faith, hope, and charity; 618 or else because she serves the Lord in thought, in speech, and in work. And why she is called bride has been said above: « Thou shalt be crowned from the head of Amana. » For Amanus is a mountain of Cilicia, which is also called Taurus.

« From the top of Sanir and Hermon. » And these mountains 1258Care in Judaea. And in them lions and leopards dwell, as what follows makes plain. « From the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards. » And by these mountains we understand those men in whom the army of vices, lions and leopards, and the malignant spirits themselves, dwell. And when these are overcome and driven out, the Church is crowned: « For no one shall be crowned except he strive lawfully (II Tim. II, 5). » She is crowned, therefore, from the head of the aforesaid mountains, because, the devil being overcome, who is their head and lord, she receives the prize and the crown for so great a struggle. « Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, my spouse. » Sister indeed, because she is the daughter of the same Father. Whence the Apostle says: « And if sons, heirs also; heirs indeed 1258Dof God, and joint heirs with Christ (Rom. VIII, 17). » And spouse, because, taking our flesh, Christ came forth from a virginal womb. And this sister and wife wounded the heart of her bridegroom, namely with love and with the fear of death. « With one of thy eyes. » 1259AFor the Church has many eyes; she is full of eyes before and behind, she sees on every side, on every side she drives off the ambushes of robbers; she has one only eye that is most shining; she is enlightened by the one faith of the Trinity, without which no one shall please God. And those who have not this eye are blind, and walk in darkness. Whence the Apostle: « Blindness, he says, in part hath happened in Israel (Rom. The Church gives her own children milk; the Synagogue makes her adulterous ones drunk. And these, being wisely brought up, grow quickly; but those, like drunkards, lose their senses. The lips of the Church drop a honeycomb, that is, sweet and pleasant words. « Honey and milk are under thy tongue. » With which every kind of men is delighted. III, 2). » -- « And the odor of thy garments as the odor of frankincense. » For the Church has a garment 1259Dwhich she herself has spun and woven with her own hands; for joining virtues to virtues, and knitting good works to good works, she has fashioned for herself a wondrous robe, in which the Bridegroom delights no less than in the abundant and most sweet odor of frankincense. « My sister, my spouse, is a garden 1260Aenclosed. » She is called a garden because, as diverse kinds of flowers and of trees, so diverse orders of saints and of the faithful are contained in her. There are the holy apostles, the confessors and the martyrs, the virgins and the widows. And enclosed, since she is fortified with the garrisons of the virtues and of the angels. No gate lies open to vices, through which robbers and enemies might steal in, to thieve and to snatch away her goods. « A garden enclosed, and a fountain sealed. » The fountain of baptism indeed is in the Church, by which both the filth of vices is taken away and the stains of soul and body are cleansed. And there is a living fountain in this garden, whose water he who drinks shall thirst no more. And of this fountain it is said: « My soul hath thirsted after God, the living fountain (Psal. XLI, 3). » But why sealed, if not because it is sanctified and blessed in the name 1260Bof the Trinity? The fountain is sealed, the water is known, to it the saints run, and from it both vices and malignant spirits flee. For by this sign this fountain is distinguished from others, since what this one has done, others cannot do. « Thy sendings forth are a paradise of 619 pomegranates, with the fruits of the orchard. » What he called a garden above, he here calls a paradise, since a paradise is a garden of delights. The Church, therefore, is called a paradise, since she is filled with the abundance of all delights. For here is the tree of the knowledge of good and evil, namely the New and the Old Testament, which teach us both to shun evils and to do good. We have also the forbidden tree, that is, Christ, who is the living bread that came down from heaven. The four rivers of paradise are the four 1260CGospels. But what are her sendings forth, what her gifts, what her fruits, what her flowers, what are the fruits of pomegranates which this garden sends to the Lord? She sends pomegranates, he says, and commends many martyrs to the Lord. For the pomegranates are you, O apostles, who shed your blood for the name of Christ. « With the fruits of the orchard; » she sends not only martyrs, he says, but with the fruits of the other trees she presents the pomegranates together with virgins and confessors. « Cyprus with nard. And in these we understand that all kinds of spices are found in holy Church. For by the cyprus, from which 1260Dthe royal ointment is prepared, and by the nard, which is of a most sweet and mighty odor, all the graces of the Holy Spirit can be understood, which are preferred before all things both in sweetness and in odor. Whence the Apostle: « We are, he says, the good odor of Christ unto God (II Cor. And in these the person is humble, but the virtue lofty. « With all the trees of Libanus. » And by these you may understand the apostles and the prophets, who surpass the rest in height, in strength, and in beauty; since indeed the trees of Libanus are of great height. And the myrrh and the aloes, which resist rottenness and worms, signify chastity and the other virtues, which keep both soul and body unharmed from the touch of vices. « With all the chief » and greatest 1261B« ointments, » by which faith, hope, and charity can be designated, which hold the primacy for ever and ever. « The fountain of gardens, the well of living waters. » This, he says, is the fountain of gardens; these are the apostles and the doctors; this is that water by which the whole world is watered. For the doctrine of the apostles is one, and the error of the heretics is dead: they who drink this water do not die; they who drink that other cannot live. For the winds rose up against the garden of Christ when the assaults and fury of tyrants, persecuting the Church, slaughtered the saints. For then the spices flowed, then the constancy and the virtues of the martyrs gave forth their scent, then a most sweet 1261Dodor of a reasonable holocaust and of a pure sacrifice was borne before the sight of the Lord. Yet we may understand by the north wind, which is a harsh and cold wind, the cruelty of tyrants. And by the south wind, which is calm, the blandishments of deceit are designated; whence it is said: « His enemies shall lick the ground (Psal. LXXI, 9). »

1268D« Crura illius columnae marmoreae. » Per crura namque vias et itinera intelligimus, et est causa pro effectu. Crura, inquit, viae et itinera illius non debilia sunt. Non arundo vento agitata; imo columnae. Sed quae columnae? Marmoreae utique, quae frangi 1269Aquidem, flecti autem non possunt. Christus namque occidi potuit, a proposita vero voluntate detorqueri non fuit facultas. « Quae fundatae sunt super bases aureas: » fidem nempe de his quae nos docuit Deus, et sapientiae firmitatem. Christi namque divinitas humanitatem assumptam et regebat, et sustentabat. Possumus autem, et per itinera, incarnationem, passionem, resurrectionem et ascensionem denotare. Quae ideo super aureas bases fundata dicuntur, quia in providentia et consilio Patris (quod nimirum aureum fundamentum est) ante mundi constitutionem inhaeserant. Unde ipse ait: « Non haberes in me potestatem, nisi desuper tibi datum fuisset (Joan. XIX, 11). » Hoc autem tale est ac si diceret: Firmissimum mihi est fundamentum, 1269Bquod nisi se volente me occidi non patietur. « Species ejus ut Libani, electus ut cedri. » Hic enim mons omnes illius terrae montes altitudine transcendit; Christi autem montis majestas omnia complet et continet. Denique Libanus candidatio dicitur, Christus autem prae filiis hominum forma est speciosior. Est igitur species ejus ut Libani, alta, et pulchra, et prae omnibus excellentior. « Electus ut cedri. » Cedrus namque arbor est quae excelsa, odorifera, speciosa, imputribilis naturae esse perhibetur. Haec autem omnia Christo conveniunt. « Guttur illius suavis; » id est, verba quae ab ejus ore prodeunt jucunda et suavia sunt, omnique melle dulciora. Sed quid in singulis moror? « Et totus desiderabilis. » Singularia relinquam: summatim, et breviter 1269Comnia dicam. Quid dicam? Totus amicus meus est desiderabilis, totus est amabilis. Diligamus igitur eum non verbis, sed opere, et veritate, toto corde, tota mente, tota cordis intentione. « Talis est dilectus meus, » cui fideli annulo desponsata sum, nimioque amore et dilectione conjuncta; « et ipse, » et non alius, non Judaeus, non haereticus, « est amicus meus, filiae Jerusalem. » Vere utique amicus, quoniam propter eam sanguinem fudit, qua major dilectio esse non potest.

Yet this is also understood thus in the person of the Church. I sleep, the flesh being overcome and subdued; but the heart watches — that is, the soul, which perseveres in good works. And we may understand by the heart the apostles of Christ. These watch, these guard the holy Church by day and by night. « Quo abiit dilectus tuus, o pulcherrima mulierum: quo declinavit dilectus tuus, et quaeremus eum 1270Ctecum? » Imperfecti sunt enim qui hic loquuntur: idcirco ubi Deus sit, quo declinaverit, et quo in loco moreretur, quasi ignorantes inquirunt. « Dilectus meus descendit in hortum suum. » Quid est, inquit, quod quaeritis? Nonne vobis notum est quod in hortum suum dilectus meus descendit? Venit utique in hortum suum, visitavit me sponsam et amicam suam. Denique et dixit: non te deseram, neque derelinquam. Ex quo igitur in mundum venit, ex quo me dilectionis vinculo sibi conjunxit, mecum est et mecum habitat. Ad Patrem ivit, me non reliquit; coelos ascendit, me non deseruit. Non ergo dicas, o homo, quo abiit? non dicas quo declinavit? Vis eum quaerere mecum? Fac ut sentias mecum: operare quod ego, vigila ut ego. Duc oleum tecum, si vis ascendere mecum. Non habeo dicis; nec mecum longius ibis. Vigila igitur accensis lampadibus, quatenus ei venienti laetus occurras. Cur autem Ecclesia hortus vocetur jam superius dictum est: « Ad areolam aromatis. » Ad areolam, inquit, aromatis. Per areolam intelligitur quaelibet anima fidelis, quae disciplina coelesti exculta, et diligenter composita, ac laterum parilitate, id est quatuor virtutibus praecipuis exornata, in sanctis operationibus, multa aromata Deo incendenda excolit.

« Whither is thy beloved gone, O thou fairest among women? whither is thy beloved turned aside, and we will seek him 1270Cwith thee? » For those who speak here are imperfect: therefore they inquire, as though they knew not, where God may be, whither he has turned aside, and in what place he might die. « My beloved is gone down into his garden. » What is it, she says, that you seek? Is it not known to you that my beloved has gone down into his garden? He has indeed come into his garden; he has visited me, his bride and his friend. In short, he also said: I will not forsake thee, neither will I abandon thee. From the time, therefore, that he came into the world, from the time that he joined me to himself by the bond of love, he is with me and dwells with me. He went to the Father, he did not leave me; he ascended the heavens, he did not forsake me. Do not therefore say, O man, whither is he gone? do not say, whither has he turned aside? Wilt thou seek him with me? See that thou art of one mind with me: work what I work, watch as I watch. Take oil with thee, if thou wilt ascend with me. I have none, thou sayest; then neither wilt thou go further with me. Watch therefore with lamps lighted, that thou mayest go joyfully to meet him when he comes. But why the Church is called a garden has already been said above: « To the bed of spices. » To the bed, he says, of spices. By the bed is understood any faithful soul which, cultivated by heavenly discipline and diligently ordered, and adorned with the evenness of its sides — that is, with the four chief virtues — brings forth in holy works many spices to be burned for God.

« That he may feed in the gardens. » Wilt thou become a garden? III, 20). » -- « And gather the lilies. » For God is always gathering lilies, always joining souls to himself. Thou hast vowed to serve God, thou hast promised to serve God in chastity and humility, in patience and sobriety; these are the lilies which thou presentest to God; these are the lilies which the Lord gathers. « I to my beloved, and my beloved to me. » I, she says, serve my beloved and obey him in all things, and prepare in myself a place for his dwelling. And my beloved helps me, bestows the merits of my service, and brings himself into me as a guest. « Who feedeth among the lilies, » that is, 1271Bhe delights in the odour of the virtues and in the beauty of souls. The voice of the Bridegroom: « Thou art beautiful, my friend, sweet and comely as Jerusalem. » Beautiful indeed, since thou hast been baptized. « And sweet and comely as Jerusalem. » For Jerusalem is interpreted vision of peace. Rightly, therefore, is the Church called sweet as Jerusalem, since she knows and sees peace, follows peace, and believes unto peace. Whence it is written: « And his place is made in peace (Psal. LXXV, 3). » And because she is the daughter of the Jerusalem above, therefore she is said to have its beauty and comeliness; of which the Apostle: « That Jerusalem, he says, which is above is free, which is our mother (Galat. IV, 26). » -- « Terrible 1271Cas an army set in array. » Terrible indeed, because, armed with faith and hope and the other virtues, she puts to flight the ambushes of the malign spirits. For terrible was Peter, and terrible also was Paul. For Peter commanded Ananias with his wife to die; and Paul delivered the adulterer to Satan. Terrible too were the other saints, who forced the malign spirits to go out from the bodies they possessed. But why is she said to be as an army set in array? For the lines of a camp are so ordered that each man, set in his own place, may defend the place entrusted to him. So also in the Church. The bishop stands before the gates, lest the wolf steal in, lest he carry off the sheep. 625 But why say much? Each one fights in his own order. The doctors fight, the continent fight, the married fight also. They are skilled in battle; 1271Dthey destroy vices and overthrow the malign spirits. « Turn away thy eyes from me, for they have made me fly away from thee. » For since above, opening the bolt of her door, the Church both willed to receive Christ into herself and began to pry too far into the secrets of his divinity, therefore it is here said: « Turn away thy eyes from me. » Whence the Apostle also gave this precept: « not to be more wise than it behoveth (Rom. « What shalt thou see in the Sulamitess, but the companies of camps? » For the companies of camps, as the custom is, both rejoice and sing, and adorn them; they burnish their arms, 1276Cthey fortify the bulwarks, they equip themselves for valour and boldness. The voice of Christ to the Church: « How beautiful are thy steps in shoes, O prince's daughter! » Beautiful are the steps, beautiful is the gait, and beautiful too is the working. For patience shoes our feet, since it did not hesitate to shed the blood 1276Dof the innocent, and does not only suffer. We may also understand by the shoes the examples of the prophets who have died. And thus the king's daughter, this daughter of the eternal prince, is shod. « The joints of thy thighs are like necklaces, that are wrought 1277Aby the hand of a craftsman. » What by the thighs, but the begetting of children? For in the thighs generation takes place. Whence Abraham made his servant swear upon his thigh, since he knew that from thence Christ would come forth, by whose name they that fear God do not dare to swear in vain. Let it be said, therefore: The joint of thy thighs is to thee as necklaces — that is, the union, the love, and the concord of thy children. For children are a mother's glory, and by no necklaces is she more adorned than by children, if she has them well-mannered. And so much the greater glory is to be had in these, as the workmanship established by the hand of a higher craftsman is worthier and fairer. « Thy navel is a turned bowl. » What by the navel but the apostles? XI, 2). » Hell subdues all things, charity overcomes all things. Charity does not fear hell, it does not fear its princes and its inhabitants. It is a metonymy. « Its lamps are lamps of fire and of flames. » For hell always burns with an inextinguishable fire. Charity is always kindled with an unconquerable fire. And of this fire the Lord says: « I came to cast fire upon the earth (Luc. XII, 49). » And this fire burns up vices and enlightens hearts. « Many waters could not quench charity. » Rightly, therefore, is charity likened to hell, since its fire, like that one's, is inextinguishable. « Neither shall the floods overwhelm it. » For as the Apostle 1285Bsays (Rom. VIII, 35), neither tribulation, nor augustia, nor death, nor life, nor any persecution shall be able to separate the saints from the charity which is in Christ Jesus. « If a man shall give all the substance of his house for love, he shall despise it as nothing. » What, he says, shall I say of charity? With what praises shall I exalt it? It is greater than can be thought, greater than can be spoken. What price is there for charity, which is matched with the divine love? Do not despise charity; do not say that it can be bought with riches. For if thou givest all that thou hast, if thou distributest all the substance of thy house for love, and thinkest that with these thou hast bought charity, thou despisest charity, which cannot be sold. Thou settest a price upon that which has no price. For it supercellit every price. Riches flee, 1285Cfriends remain; fortune deceives thee, friends die for thee. Let us therefore seek that friend who never dies, never forsakes his friends, never lies, abounds in all things; who, praising friendship, says: « Greater love than this no man hath, than that a man lay down his life for his friends (Joan. XV, 13). » Dying, therefore, for us, he fulfilled this love.

Verses: Who is she that cometh, Flowing with delights? She walks not upon her own feet But is drawn by her husband. Upon the cross I was with thee. 1285DThere I raised thee up; But thy mother fell And, as one violated, came to ruin; Since her firmament fled Set thereon a seal, Upon the heart and the arm. Love conquers all things. Jealousy fears nothing, For which there is no working. Who is not extinguished, Charity does not die; This virtue is ineffable, 1286ASublime, unutterable, Strong and unconquerable. If a man shall deliver over All things that he has had For this virtue, charity Is despised; because it is weighed out, For it is not sold for a price.

Christ to the Synagogue concerning the Church: « Our sister is little, and she hath no breasts. » This sister is understood as the Church of the Gentiles. The apostles say, therefore — the messengers of the Lord say — we are legates; we seek a wife for our Lord. Whom then shall we give him? Our sister is little, lately born, unwise and untaught. She has no breasts: she does not see the Testaments; she cannot yet bear, nor yet nourish 1286Bchildren; she knows not a husband. She must therefore be spoken to and admonished concerning the marriage. But « what shall we do to our sister in the day when she is to be spoken to? » With what words, or with what promises, shall we temper her mind. It follows: « If she be a wall, let us build upon it bulwarks of silver. » If, he says, we shall find in her a wall — constancy, faith, and fortitude — let us build upon it bulwarks of silver and the divine eloquences. For the sayings of the prophets and the discourses of the doctors are bulwarks to the wall and to our faith, since by these we are defended, by these we overcome vices, by these we withstand heretics. And these 633 are called silver, since nothing of lead or of brass, and nothing of falsehood, is found in them, « If she be a door, let us join it together with boards of cedar, » 1286Cetc. Let us paint the door, he says, let us mark the gates, and let us vary them, let us vary them, that the army of God and the multitude of the virtues may find an entrance; that they may recognize the sign, and may delight in comely adornments. Or also let us join it together and shut it, so that, the gates being fastened on every side, vices may be able to enter from no quarter. And let these be fortified with boards of cedar — with prophetic examples, unfading, and with other sayings. The voice of the Church: « And my breasts are as a tower. » I, she says, am a wall; I am strong, I am constant; I resist vices, I storm my enemies. « And my breasts are as a tower. » My breasts, she says — my doctrine and my wisdom — are a tower. The doctrine is lofty, the wisdom is great, well fortified, well armed, fearing neither the sophisms of heretics 1286Dnor those of philosophers. For these breasts nourish and defend her children. Whence they are called both a tower and breasts. But from what time is such fortitude hers? It follows: « Since I am become in his presence as one finding peace; » that is, since Christ loved me and reconciled me to the Father; since the angelic trumpet sounded; « Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will (Luc. II, 14). » The Synagogue says to the Church: « The peaceable one had a vineyard, which hath peoples. » By the vineyard understand the Church; and for the peaceable one, Christ. « For the vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel (Isa. V, 7). » -- « And he 1287Ais our peace, which hath made both one (Ephes. II, 14). » And this vineyard has peoples, because it gathers into one Jews and Gentiles, Greeks and barbarians, and all nations. « He delivered it to keepers, » that is, to the apostles and doctors, that they might cultivate it, and prune it, and guard it from thieves. « A man brings for its fruit a thousand pieces of silver. » For the number of a thousand is put for the highest perfection and fullness. And it is just as if he were to say: The holy and catholic man, that he may enjoy the merit and blessedness of this vineyard, gives away all things whatsoever he possesses. Christ says: « My vineyard is before me. » My vineyard, he says — my inheritance, or my Church — is before me and serves me; and although I had delivered it to keepers, yet I am always present 1287Bto fortify, prepare, and guard it. « A thousand of thine, O peaceable one, » — understand: are kept for thee. And those whom above he called pieces of silver he here calls peaceable ones. But why peaceable? Because they reconcile us to God, they soften his enmity, wrath, and indignation, and prepare peace for us, and lead us into peace. And these are reserved for the Church, and shall be rendered back to her multiplied. « And two hundred to them that keep its fruits. » Two hundred, he says, of the thousand peaceable ones are kept for the apostles and doctors. For to these, as to kings and masters, a double honour is to be had, since they guard the rest, and for the rest 1288Athey watch and die. Christ says to the Church: « Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the friends hearken. » Thou, he says, O Church, dwellest in the gardens, thou delightest in the lilies, thou liest among roses and violets; on every side are flowers, on every side virtues, on every side the sayings of the doctors. « Stay me up with flowers, » thou saidst; thy petition is fulfilled. My friends — namely, the angels and the apostles — hearken to thee; they desire to hear what thou believest, what hope thou hast, to what end thou tendest. « Make me hear thy voice; » namely, praise, jubilation, a canticle of praise and of confession. The voice of the Church to Christ: « Flee away, my beloved, and be like the roe and the fawn of harts upon the mountains of spices. » Flee, she says, leave the lands, fly away to the Father, that they who think thee only a man may believe thee to be true God. 1288BFor my sake thou camest, thou enteredst my house; already thou hast kissed me; to thee I am joined, to thee betrothed. Return therefore, draw me after thee, render me a like return, bring me into thy house. « Be like the roe, » seek the mountains, seek the heavens, seek the things above; « and the fawn of harts, » that thou mayest dwell in the shadow, in Selmon, and in refreshment; that thou mayest abide forever upon the mountains of spices, where the odour is most sweet, the place delightful, the splendour ineffable, the abundance unutterable; there the saints exult, there the angels rejoice; there with the Father and the Holy Spirit praise, honour, and glory are sung to thee forever. Amen.
