Hugh of Saint Victor

Hugo de S. Victore

c. 1096 – 11 February 1141

Theologian and head of the school of the abbey of Saint-Victor in Paris, and the founding master of the Victorine tradition of learning and contemplation. His work ranges across the liberal arts (the Didascalicon), sacramental theology (De sacramentis christianae fidei), and mystical writing. The present piece is a short exposition of the Magnificat, the canticle of the Blessed Virgin Mary.

Adnotatiunculae in Joelem

321C(JOEL. I.) Verbum Domini, quod factum est ad Joel filium Phatuel. HISTORICE. In verbo tria attenduntur: strepitus, forma, intellectus. Strepitus persecutionis, forma vocis, intellectus dictionis. Percussio ad vindictam, vox ad laetitiam, dictio refertur ad gratiam. Siquidem vindicta percutit, vox consolatur, dictio erudit. Erudit filium, consolatur mercenarium: erudit filium avide doctrinam patris sitientem; consolatur mercenarium sub judicio anxie laborantem. Filium erudit gratia obedientiae, mercenarium consolatur merces justitiae, servum affligit poena malitiae. Strepitus ad poenam, vox ad praemium, intellectus ad gratiam refertur. Haec igitur tria considerantur in verbo, quod factum est ad Joel: quia in exitu sequentis prophetiae, 321Det inimicorum ultio, et populi afflicti consolatio, et electorum praedicitur eruditio. Strepitus igitur soni ad adversarium: forma vocis ad populum afflictum: intellectus verbi ad mentem fit propheticam, et electorum chorum. Quia unde adversarius 322Caffligitur, inde populus consolatur: quod mens electorum intelligens in Domino gloriatur. Strepitus igitur fit ad carnales, forma dirigitur ad animales, intellectus ad spirituales. Hoc autem verbum Domini est. In Domino quoque tria considerantur: ultio, zelus, retributio. Ultio culpae, zelus disciplinae, praemium obedientiae. Vivus igitur sermo Dei, et efficax, et penetrabilior omni gladio ancipiti (Hebr. IV). Penetrabilis; quia destruit malitiam; vivus quia reducit ad gratiam; efficax, quia perducit ad gloriam. Penetrabilis est judicio, vivus facto, efficax promisso. Penetrabilis, quia judicium absconditur. Vivus, quia factum ejus fructificatur. Efficax, quia promissum ejus non inficiatur. Penetrabilis, quia destruit fortiter; vivus, quia vivificat 322Dpotenter; efficax, quia reddit efficaciter. Hoc est verbum Domini, quod factum est ad Joel filium Phatuel.

Primo videndum est, quod constructio eclipsim patitur. Dicendo enim: verbum Domini, quod factum 323Aest ad Joel filium Phatuel, aliquid ad perfectionem sensus subintelligendum est vel ita: Hoc est verbum, quod factum est; vel ita: Verbum quod factum est haec alia ostendit. Sequitur:

Audite haec, senes, et auribus percipite, omnes habitatores terrae: si factum est istud in diebus vestris, aut in diebus patrum vestrorum. Super hoc narrate filiis vestris, et filii vestri filiis suis, et filii eorum generationi alteri. Ex magnitudine venturae calamitatis in stuporem vertitur admirationis. Ne vero vilipendenda videantur, auctoritatem senum convocat, ne parvi momenti, vel exigui, habitatores terrae excitat, dicens: Audite, senes, etc. Sapientia senum auctoritatem admirationis, sollicitudo habitatorum fidem dat stupori, scilicet ut de venturo infortunio minime 323Bqueat dubitari: cui auctoritas sapientium, et sollicitudo plebium pro certo publice videtur attestari. Sequitur, partes exprimens infortunii, dicens:

Residuum erucae comedit locusta: et residuum locustae comedit bruchus: et residuum bruchi comedit aerugo. Expergiscimini, ebrii, et flete: ululate omnes, qui bibitis vinum in dulcedine, quoniam periit ab ore vestro. Ostensa superiori parte infortunii, et subsecuta voce lamenti: aggreditur aliud infortunii genus ostendere, et ad illam lamenti speciem conatur descendere. Ait enim:

Gens enim ascendit super terram meam fortis et innumerabilis. Dentes ejus, ut dentes leonis: et molares ejus ut catuli leonis. Posuit vineam meam in desertum: et ficum meum decorticavit. Nudans spoliavit 323Ceam, et projecit; albi facti sunt rami ejus. Ecce lamentum. Nunc subjicit infortunium, dicens:

Plange, quasi virgo accincta sacco super virum pubertatis suae. Et sic alternando infortunium cum lamento decurrit usque ad illud: Ad te, Domine, clamabo. Ubi fit ad Deum supplex invocatio, et calamitatis miseranda expositio, et ipsius delendae humilis, et affectuosa deprecatio. Babyloniorum juxta quosdam designat in Judaeam terribilem adventum, hostilem impetum, ferocem animum: ubique frementem, cuncta vastantem, universa delentem. Vel, quod verius est, Assyriorum superbiam, et ipsius gentis audacem ferociam, sanguinis avidam, 323Dgloriae cupidam, casibus expositam, victoriae pronam. Qui sub Sennacherib universam Judaeam vastantes Jerusalem obsederunt. Et quia non divino judicio, sed propriae fortitudini fortunam negotii ascripserunt: rege cum paucis fugiente omnes una nocte ab angelo percussi perierunt. Joel per erucam designat Assyrios; per locustam Babylonios; per bruchum Persas et Medos: per rubiginem Macedones. Hi omnes per successionem temporum, populum Dei vastaverunt: et si quid unus floridum, aut viride, vel forte inconsumptum reliquerat: alio succedente conculcatum, et ad nihilum redactum est. Josephum antiquum, et traditiones Judaeorum, et caeteras ad id pertinentes historias legat, qui super hoc certificari desiderat.

324AAllegorice vero de persecutione loquitur Ecclesiae generalis, quae quadrifaria legitur: quia primo ab idololatris, secundo ab haereticis, tertio a pseudochristianis impugnata fuisse, quarto ab Antichristo impugnanda legitur. Quod Joel prophetico praevidens oculo, prae magnitudine persecutionis, senes et habitatores terrae in consortium vocat admirationis. Ac si diceret: Ventura sponsae Christi infortunia video, calamitates intueor, varios labores ipsius prospicio: sed sapientiam spiritualium, sollicitudinem quoque carnalium contestor, ut audita per successionem in posterum transfundant: quousque surgat aurora de coelo, veniat Agni sponsa, pullulet Ecclesia: ut nostro praemunita oraculo solida maneat: ne rivalem pro marito recipiat, ne florem sine 324Bfructu, monetam sine argento, manipulum sine grano, umbram sine corpore, decepta eligat. Per erucam designat idololatras; per locustas, haereticos; per bruchos, pseudo et carnales christianos; per rubiginem, Antichristi malitiosam sollicitudinem: primos propter spurcitiam, et varietatem idololatriae; secundos propter volatum scientiae, agilitatem ingenii, elationem superbiae; tertios propter aestum, et immunditiam carnalis concupiscentiae; quartum propter immanitatem saevitiae. Postquam enim Ecclesia idololatriae evasit naufragium, in fluctus et procellas haereticorum decidit: sed dum spirituali flante favonio, procella in leniorem versa est auram, rursus in carnalium aestu sustinendo diu laboravit: demum a filio perditionis, qui extolletur supra omne, 324Cquod dicitur Deus (II Thes. Terram, propter fidei stabilitatem: vineam, propter poenitentiae austeritatem; ficum, propter dilectionis dulcedinem. Vel terram, propter firmamentum justitiae. Vineam, propter laetitiam spiritualis intelligentiae. Ficum, propter suavitatem coelestis doctrinae. Vel terram, propter fructum spiritualium exercitationum: vineam, propter gratiam coelestium donorum; ficus, propter dulcedinem gaudiorum aeternorum. Haec Agnus Christus sponsae contulit suae, scilicet Ecclesiae. Prima in horto; secunda in cellario; tertia in lecto. In horto namque exercitatur; in cellario laetificatur; in lecto felicitatur. Primo operibus poenitentiae; secundo muneribus gratiae; tertio osculis sapientiae. Sed hanc gens praefata decorticavit, nudavit projecit. Membrana 325Asunt exercitia, folia verba, fructus merita. Exercitia gratiae, verba doctrinae, merita gloriae. Sed hanc gens praefata decorticavit, nudavit, projecit, quia persecutione ingravescente tepuerunt exercitia religionis, siluerunt verba praedicationis, virtutum merita attenuata sunt in multis.

Tropologice vero de tentationibus animae propheta loquitur. Gentem spiritualem ostendit super eam ascendisse, catervam vitiorum eam justo Dei judicio invasisse, conculcasse, expugnasse, dicens: Residuum erucae comedit locusta: et residuum locustae comedit bruchus: et residuum bruchi comedit aerugo. Scio quosdam ante me haec quatuor animi perturbationes quatuor significare dixisse, et erucam ad timorem, locustam ad spem, bruchum ad gaudium, 325Brubiginem ad dolorem retulisse. Nos autem eis suam interpretationem relinquimus, et ad alia, quae magis congrua videntur, festinamus. Residuum erucae comedit locusta, etc. Eruca igitur est luxuria; locusta, cenodoxia, id est vana gloria; bruchus gastrimargia; rubigo, ira vel impatientia. Residuum erucae, est castitas; residuum locustae, humilitas; residuum bruchi, sobrietas; residuum rubiginis, mentis lenitas, et patientia. Residuum igitur primi comedit secundum, secundi tertium, tertii quartum: quia saepissime accidit ut de continentia inanis oriatur gloria, humilitatem sequatur ebrietas et crapula, sobrietatem ira, vel 325Cimpatientia. Nonnunquam enim accidit ut mens adhuc imperfecta dum se vicisse et ad culmen justitiae pervenisse existimat, cadat a victoria, et dum aliquem hostium se videt superasse, totum se sustinuisse belli existimat negotium, totumque se putat superasse exercitum. Sed dum mens decepta de triumpho unius exsultat, non videt misera quod gladium alius exerit, et mortem cominus intentat. Quoniam confusum est gaudium a filiis hominum. Merito confusum esse dicitur gaudium ex defectu praecedentium: quia ex eorum inedia periclitatur populus, contemnuntur sacerdotes, infirmantur Ecclesiae.

329CSciendum quoque quod gaudium dividitur quadrifariam. Est enim gaudium iniquitatis, est vanitatis, est charitatis, est felicitatis. De primo legitur: Qui laetantur cum malefecerint, et exsultant in rebus pessimis (Prov. II). De secundo: Tenent tympanum et citharam, et gaudent ad sonitum organi (Job XXI). De tertio: Exsultabunt sancti in gloria; laetabuntur in cubilibus suis (Psal. CXLIX). De quarto: Beati qui habitant in domo tua, Domine; in saecula saeculorum laudabunt te (Psal. LXXXIII). Gaudium igitur charitatis, et felicitatis praemium confusum est a filiis hominum: quia deficientibus praefatis virtutibus, ubi cognitio veritatis, et amor virtutis non praecedunt, cessantibus meritis: nulla vel hic, vel in futuro 329Dsalutis praemia succedunt.

Tropologice vero ea, quae allegorice de Ecclesia diximus super statu animae, interpretari possumus. Anima namque virgo est conditione naturae: virgo privilegio gratiae, virgo quoque praemio gloriae. Animam siquidem meretricem facit inordinata concupiscentia: castitatem ei reddit virilis poenitentia: virginitatem sapiens innocentia. Quasi enim casta est anima, dum per opera, et affectum justitiae cruciatum, et timorem invasit poenitentiae, necdum tamen ad culmen sublimatur innocentiae. Fornicaria est igitur anima in prostibulo: conjugata in atrio, continens in domo: virgo in thalamo. In primo exponitur concupiscentia: in secundo opera facit poenitentiae: in tertio plangens virum desiderio afficitur justitiae: 330Ain quarto virgineis jucundatur amplexibus et osculis sapientiae. Brief Notes on Joel

321C(JOEL. I.) The word of the Lord that came to Joel the son of Phatuel. HISTORICALLY. In a word three things are attended to: noise, form, meaning. The noise of persecution, the form of the voice, the meaning of the utterance. The striking is referred to vengeance, the voice to gladness, the utterance to grace. Since indeed vengeance strikes, the voice consoles, the utterance instructs. It instructs the son, it consoles the hired servant: it instructs the son who thirsts eagerly for his father's teaching; it consoles the hired servant who labors anxiously under judgment. The grace of obedience instructs the son, the wage of justice consoles the hired servant, the penalty of malice afflicts the slave. The noise is referred to punishment, the voice to reward, the meaning to grace. These three things, then, are considered in the word that came to Joel: because in the outcome of the prophecy that follows, 321Dboth the vengeance upon enemies, and the consolation of the afflicted people, and the instruction of the elect are foretold. The noise of the sound, then, is made toward the adversary; the form of the voice toward the afflicted people; the meaning of the word is made toward the prophetic mind and the choir of the elect. For whence the adversary 322Cis afflicted, thence the people is consoled: in that the mind of the elect, understanding, glories in the Lord. The noise, then, is made to the carnal, the form is directed to the animal, the meaning to the spiritual. Now this word is the Lord's. In the Lord also three things are considered: vengeance, zeal, retribution. The vengeance of fault, the zeal of discipline, the reward of obedience. Therefore the word of God is living, and effectual, and more piercing than any two-edged sword (Hebr. IV). Piercing, because it destroys malice; living, because it leads back to grace; effectual, because it brings through to glory. It is piercing in judgment, living in deed, effectual in promise. Piercing, because judgment is hidden. Living, because its deed bears fruit. Effectual, because its promise is not denied. Piercing, because it destroys mightily; living, because it quickens 322Dpowerfully; effectual, because it repays effectually. This is the word of the Lord that came to Joel the son of Phatuel.

First it must be seen that the construction suffers an ellipsis. For in saying: the word of the Lord that came 323Ato Joel the son of Phatuel, something must be supplied for the completeness of the sense, either thus: This is the word that came; or thus: The word that came shows these other things. There follows:

Hear this, ye old men, and give ear, all ye inhabitants of the land: whether this hath been done in your days, or in the days of your fathers. Tell ye of this to your children, and let your children tell it to their children, and their children to another generation. From the greatness of the coming calamity he turns to the stupor of wonder. But lest these things seem to be held cheap, he calls together the authority of old men; lest they be thought of little moment or slight, he rouses the inhabitants of the land, saying: Hear, ye old men, etc. The wisdom of the old men gives authority to the wonder, the concern of the inhabitants gives credence to the stupor — namely, that of the misfortune to come it may in no way 323Bbe possible to doubt: to which the authority of the wise and the concern of the common people seem publicly to bear certain witness. There follows, expressing the parts of the misfortune, saying:

What the caterpillar hath left, the locust hath eaten: and what the locust hath left, the grub hath eaten: and what the grub hath left, the rust hath eaten. Awake, ye that are drunk, and weep: howl, all ye that drink wine in sweetness, for it is perished from your mouth. The upper part of the misfortune having been shown, and the voice of lament having followed after, he undertakes to show another kind of misfortune, and strives to descend to that form of lament. For he says:

For a nation is come up upon my land, strong and without number. Its teeth are as the teeth of a lion: and its molars as a lion's whelp. It hath laid my vineyard into a desert: and hath barked my fig tree. Stripping it bare it hath despoiled 323Cit, and cast it away; its branches are made white. Behold the lament. Now he subjoins the misfortune, saying:

Lament, like a virgin girded with sackcloth for the husband of her youth. And so, alternating misfortune with lament, he runs on as far as that place: To thee, O Lord, will I cry. Where there is made a suppliant invocation to God, and a pitiable setting forth of the calamity, and a humble and affectionate entreaty that it be blotted out. According to some he designates the terrible coming of the Babylonians into Judaea, their hostile onset, their fierce spirit: raging everywhere, laying all things waste, destroying all things. Or, what is truer, the pride of the Assyrians, and the bold ferocity of that nation, greedy for blood, 323Dcovetous of glory, exposed to disasters, prone to victory. Who under Sennacherib, laying waste all Judaea, besieged Jerusalem. And because they ascribed the fortune of the affair not to divine judgment but to their own strength, the king fleeing with a few, all perished in one night, struck by the angel. Joel designates by the caterpillar the Assyrians; by the locust the Babylonians; by the grub the Persians and Medes; by the mildew the Macedonians. All these, through the succession of the times, laid waste the people of God: and if one of them had left anything flowering, or green, or by chance unconsumed, at the coming of another it was trodden down and reduced to nothing. Let him read the ancient Josephus, and the traditions of the Jews, and the other histories pertaining to this, who desires to be made certain about the matter.

324AAllegorically, however, he speaks of the general persecution of the Church, which is read to be fourfold: because it is read to have been assailed first by idolaters, secondly by heretics, thirdly by false Christians, and fourthly to be assailed by Antichrist. Which Joel, foreseeing with a prophetic eye, on account of the greatness of the persecution calls the old men and the inhabitants of the land into fellowship of wonder. As if he said: I see the misfortunes to come upon the bride of Christ, I behold her calamities, I look out upon her various toils: but I call to witness the wisdom of spiritual men, and the concern of carnal men too, that they may pour on what they have heard, by succession, into after times: until the dawn arise from heaven, the bride of the Lamb come, the Church spring forth: that, forearmed by our oracle, she may remain firm: lest she receive a rival in place of a husband, lest, deceived, she choose a flower without 324Bfruit, coin without silver, a sheaf without grain, a shadow without a body. By the caterpillar he designates idolaters; by the locusts, heretics; by the grubs, false and carnal Christians; by the mildew, the malicious solicitude of Antichrist: the first on account of the filthiness and the variety of idolatry; the second on account of the flight of knowledge, the agility of wit, the elation of pride; the third on account of the heat and the uncleanness of carnal concupiscence; the fourth on account of the monstrousness of its savagery. For after the Church escaped the shipwreck of idolatry, it fell into the waves and storms of the heretics: but while, with a spiritual west wind blowing, the storm was turned into a gentler breeze, again it labored long in enduring the heat of carnal men: at last, by the son of perdition, who shall be lifted up above all 324Cthat is called God (II Thes. II), or that is worshipped: wearied in many ways, variously tormented, most grievously distressed: at length, fair weather being restored, it will return to the harbor of liberty: it will enter the dwelling of peace. And this is what he says: What the caterpillar hath left, the locust hath eaten, etc. This is the nation which is said to have come up upon the land, that is, upon the Church, strong and without number: strong in greatness, without number in multitude; strong in cruelty, without number in persistence; the lion is the devil, or a prince highest in earthly power: as some heresiarch, or Antichrist himself. The teeth are his satellites. The whelps, the nobles, or the petty kings. The molars, the executioners. That which above he called the land, he now calls the fig tree and the vineyard, that 324Dis, the Church. The land, on account of the stability of faith: the vineyard, on account of the austerity of penance; the fig tree, on account of the sweetness of love. Or the land, on account of the firm ground of justice. The vineyard, on account of the gladness of spiritual understanding. The fig tree, on account of the sweetness of heavenly teaching. Or the land, on account of the fruit of spiritual exercises: the vineyard, on account of the grace of heavenly gifts; the fig tree, on account of the sweetness of eternal joys. These things Christ the Lamb conferred upon his bride, namely the Church. The first in the garden; the second in the storeroom; the third in the bed. For in the garden she is exercised; in the storeroom she is gladdened; in the bed she is made happy. First by the works of penance; secondly by the gifts of grace; thirdly by the kisses of wisdom. But the aforesaid nation barked it, stripped it cast it away. The bark 325Ais the exercises, the leaves the words, the fruit the merits. The exercises of grace, the words of teaching, the merits of glory. But the aforesaid nation barked it, stripped it, cast it away, because, as the persecution grew heavier, the exercises of religion grew lukewarm, the words of preaching fell silent, the merits of the virtues were thinned in many.

Tropologically, however, the prophet speaks of the temptations of the soul. He shows that a spiritual nation has come up upon it, that a troop of vices has by the just judgment of God invaded it, trodden it down, stormed it, saying: What the caterpillar hath left, the locust hath eaten: and what the locust hath left, the grub hath eaten: and what the grub hath left, the rust hath eaten. I know that certain men before me have said that these four signify the four perturbations of the mind, and have referred the caterpillar to fear, the locust to hope, the grub to joy, 325Bthe mildew to grief. Of which is that verse of Boethius: Drive off joys, and fear, and put hope to flight, and let not grief be present. Clouded is the mind, and bound with reins, where these reign. But we leave them their own interpretation, and hasten on to other things which seem more fitting. What the caterpillar hath left, the locust hath eaten, etc. The caterpillar, then, is lust; the locust, cenodoxia, that is, vainglory; the grub, gastrimargia; the mildew, anger or impatience. What the caterpillar leaves is chastity; what the locust leaves, humility; what the grub leaves, sobriety; what the mildew leaves, gentleness of mind, and patience. What the first leaves, therefore, the second eats; what the second leaves, the third; what the third leaves, the fourth: because it very often happens that from continence empty glory arises, that drunkenness and surfeit follow humility, and anger or 325Cimpatience follow sobriety. For not seldom it happens that the mind, still imperfect, while it reckons that it has conquered and has come to the summit of justice, falls from its victory; and while it sees that it has overcome one of the enemies, it reckons that it has sustained the whole business of the war, and supposes that it has overcome the whole army. But while the deceived mind exults over the triumph of one, it does not see, wretched thing, that another draws his sword and threatens death at close quarters. Hence it is that, insulting over the slaying of lust, now as it were secure and less provident, it is struck by the sword of cenodoxia. But if it should chance to have overcome this by humility of mind, gastrimargia at once thrusts itself in. For while the incautious soul sees chastity restraining the flesh, while it beholds humility repressing the mind, it straightway hastens to condescend to pleasure, speaking thus: 325DSince neither does the wind of empty glory stir the mind, nor does the enticement of carnal concupiscence tickle the flesh, it would be a kind of cruelty if I tortured my fellow citizen with the goads of dire necessity. The peace restored to me in both admonishes me, and allows me to minister pleasant things. Thus surfeit is brought in, and the citizen is slain. Further, if it happens to have overcome this too by divine help and its own zeal: now supposing that it has wholly escaped shipwreck, it falls back into that misfortune. For because the soul considers that continence is rising in the flesh, humility in the mind, abstinence in the body, now presuming upon perfection, and despising the rest in comparison with itself, if ever its endeavor is resisted, its onset opposed, its desire thwarted: straightway 326Ait loosens the reins to anger, it leaps out into words of impatience, saying: Thou wast born wholly in sins, and dost thou teach us? (Joan. IX.) The order of things requires that the prior things hold the prior place, and that the lower lie lower. It is written: The spiritual man judgeth all things; and he himself is judged by no man (I Cor. II). And in the law the mouth of the leper is commanded to be shut (Levit. XIII). First, then, learn rightly, that you may teach soundly; first live well, that you may reprove justly. Hence the Lord through Job: Remember the battle, and add no more to speak (Job XL). As if he said: The mind must show itself the more solicitous against the spiritual things of wickedness (Ephes. VI), the more subtle their arms prove to be. Therefore, you who are turned away, fearing his justice, be converted and flee to his mercy. From him flee to him; from the rigor of justice to the bosom of mercy. The Lord, who is to be feared, speaks: he who is truth commands that which is just, which is useful, which is honorable. Be converted, he says, to me, etc. There are four things, two evils and two goods, the one evil, the other worse, the one good, the other better. Turning away and overthrow, lo, two evils. Conversion and returning, lo, two goods. Turning away 344Cfrom God through negligence, overthrow by the Devil through malice. Conversion to God through penance, returning to him through innocence. And turning away comes about in three ways: by vanity, by pleasure, by curiosity: by the vanity of the world, by pleasure in oneself, by curiosity about one's neighbor. Overthrow also in three ways: by headlong falling, by malice, by despair: by the headlong falling of fault, by the malice of envy, by despair of pardon. But conversion comes about in three ways: by confession, by compunction, by mortification: by confession of the mouth, compunction of the mind, mortification of the flesh — namely, that in the mouth there be truth, in the mind purity, in the flesh a chaste sobriety. Returning also comes about in three ways: by devotion, by love, by contemplation: by the devotion of grace, the love of justice, the contemplation 344Dof glory. You therefore who have turned away from God through the vice of negligence, who have been overthrown and drowned in the flood of malice, be converted, he says, to me with all your heart. There is a conversion of the heart, and there is also a conversion of work. And of the conversion of the heart, one kind is with the whole heart, another is in part. But the Lord seeks that which is with the whole heart, because it suffices for salvation. But he rejects the other, which is in part, because it is feigned, and far from salvation. Hence it is written: The Holy Spirit will flee from the feigned of discipline (Sap. I). For in the heart there are three things: reason, will, memory: reason of things to come, will of things present, memory of things past. For reason seeks things to come, the will loves things present, memory retains things past. Reason enlightens, the will loves, memory preserves. When therefore reason 345Aseeks and finds the highest good, the will receives and loves it, and memory carefully keeps it and binds it more tightly; then the soul converts itself to God with its whole heart. But when reason, laid to sleep, forbears to seek heavenly things, or the lukewarm will cares not to love them, or the sluggish memory despises to guard them, then the soul becomes feigned, falling first into the vice of ignorance, secondly into the offense of negligence, thirdly into the sin of malice. In either case it is agreed that the soul is feigned, because otherwise ignorance could be driven off by the light of reason, and negligence shut out by the zeal of the will, and malice lulled to sleep by the diligence of memory. Reason, then, in seeking, brings forth instruction; the will, in embracing, love; memory, in binding fast, edification. Between the porch and the altar the priesthood supplicates for the people, because it is both pressed by fear lest the divine mercy forsake the people, and is governed by love, so that it may presume to approach the throne of grace with a humble conscience. Between the porch 348Aand the altar God is appeased, because he whom fear guards from unlawful things, and whom love raises up on high, is reconciled entirely. Between the porch, I say, and the altar there comes about a fruitful and humble penance, which fear pricks, lest it grow numb; which love kindles, lest it grow lukewarm. Useful, I say, and fruitful, because fear has shut out rash presumption, and love has cast off the confusion of faintheartedness. They say therefore: Spare, O Lord, spare thy people, etc. There are three chief judgments of a man: the first about himself, the second about his neighbor, the third about himself and his neighbor. In the first he ought to be strict, in the second kindly, in the third just. For each ought to accuse himself strictly, to judge his neighbor kindly, to examine himself and his neighbor justly. Since therefore we are slack and kindly to ourselves, but to our neighbor 348Bstrict and harsh, we say: Spare, O Lord, our own slackness; spare our strictness toward our brother; spare, I say, us for what we have done unjustly; spare what we have wickedly done in our neighbor. Or, because there are two places, and two perils joined to them: the places, the world and hell; the perils, vanity and calamity. It is said therefore: Spare, O Lord, from the vanity of the world, spare from the calamity of the pit; spare, I say, from the first, lest it affect us, spare from the second, lest it swallow us up. Or, because the person of man consists of a twofold nature, namely body and soul,--in which nature indeed we have frequently sinned, for in the soul we have acted impiously, in the body we have done wickedly,--let us therefore say: Spare, O Lord, the impiety of the mind, spare the iniquity of the body. Or, because a twofold law has been given us, namely the law of nature and 348Cthe written law; and we are transgressors of both; let us therefore say: Spare, O Lord, the first transgression, spare the second breach. Or, because action is given us for the perfecting of the present life, and contemplation as a pledge of beatitude for the foretaste of the life to come; and we have been slack in action, and puffed up out of contemplation; let us say: Spare our slackness, spare our elation. Spare thy people: the people, namely, the gathering of men assembled for thee unto living justly. Thine: founded by thee, redeemed by thee, in either case to be set by thee. Truly a contrite and humbled heart God despiseth not (Psal. L). Truly at a fitting time he looks upon the quiet and humble man who trembles at his words. Truly, the valleys abound with corn; truly he wills mercy more than sacrifice, and the knowledge of Del more than holocaust. Which the prophetic utterance shows in the present passage. For above, in 349Bthe sequence of the supplication, he lamentably set forth in lamentation the coming and the cruelty of the enemies, the crushing of the land and the wasting of the animals, the pestilence and the destruction, the confusion of the people, the affliction of the ancients the squalor of the virgins; and he proclaimed the rigor of penance, the dryness of abstinence, the depth of sobs and groans, and discipline. She acted, she received, she returned. She did penance, she received mercy, she returned to grace. O fruitful and manly penance! O heroine, amplectanda, most faithful mediatrix of sinners! O second plank after shipwreck! O refuge of the poor, help of the wretched, hope of exiles, kindling of the weak, light of the blind, solace of the bereaved, rod of the wanton, bar of the vices, storehouse of the virtues — thou who alone dost bend the judge, 349Caccuse the Founder, lay low the Almighty; while thou art conquered thou conquerest, while thou art tormented thou tormentest, while thou woundest thou healest, while thou wholesomely succumbest, thou gloriously triumphest! Thou alone, while the rest are silent, boldly mountest the throne of grace. Leading David by the hand thou reconcilest him, thou restorest Peter, thou enlightenest Paul, thou confidently insertest into the choir of the apostles the publican taken from the toll booth. Thou raisest Mary from the brothel into the upper air and joinest her to Christ; the thief fastened to the gibbet, still fresh with blood, thou insertest into paradise. What more? The heavenly court is under thy jurisdiction: which the prophetic page plainly teaches in the present passage, since through labor and the fruit of penance the people not only escaped the shipwreck of misfortune, 349Dand not only returned to the former state of grace; but rather, rejoicing at the coming of the Saviour, enlightened by his teaching, shaped by his life, healed by his passion, made firm by his resurrection, glorified by his ascension, endowed in the present with the same Paraclete Spirit, it doubled its crown and carried off double. Let the dogged fluency, secular eloquence, be struck dumb, which asserts that the divine utterances are rustic and uncultivated. Behold, in the present passage a sentence is set down with color, by the law of antithesis rendering contraries to contraries. For against the wasting of the land he sets its abundance; against famine, plenty; against reproach, the security of glory; against the incursion and the credulity of the enemies, their own stench and destruction; against the barrenness of fruits and 350Athe dryness of trees, their buds and abundance; against the famine of the word and the thirst for teaching he brings in the fountain of life and the teacher of justice; against sadness, joy; against confusion, comfort; against reviling, glory; against death, life; against ashes, a crown. The fitting of each of these to each I leave to the reader, because I hasten to unfold the winding folds of the allegory. But it must be known that in the present passage there is a plain prophecy about the coming of the Saviour: through whom the spiritual enemy is quenched, the spiritual famine is cast out, abundance is restored, grace is given back. Behold, he says, I will send you corn, and wine, etc. The corn is the teaching of life; the wine, the fervor of spiritual understanding; the oil, the sweetness of conscience. Or the corn 350Bis the grace of the Eucharist; the wine, spiritual gladness; the oil, which floats above all liquids, the grace of contemplation. Or by the wine are understood the works of penance: by the corn, the works of wisdom; by the oil, because it anoints, the works of mercy; because it shines, the works of grace; because it feeds, the works of justice. We must see, therefore, how the letter hangs together. For the prophet had above set forth in many ways the common misfortune of the human race; he had foretold the vows of the people, the laments of the priests, the squalor of the virgins, the sighs of the fathers. Then he set beneath the coming of Christ, the incarnation of the Word, the dispensation of the mystery for the liberation of the people, for the removal of the misfortune; and afterward he subjoined, for the illumination of the Gentiles, for the perfection of all things, saying: And it shall come to pass after this: I will pour out my spirit, etc. We read that the Lord took away of the spirit of Moses, and set what had been taken away upon the seventy elders. For first he poured, secondly he poured in, thirdly he poured out. He poured in paradise, he poured in in the desert, he poured out in the upper room. In paradise by giving the law of nature, in the desert the precepts and ceremonies of the written law, in the upper room the fullness of grace. There are also three kinds of visions. The first is material, the second spiritual, the third intellectual. The first is with matter and form; the second without matter, but with form; the third without matter and without form. The first conceives things composed of the elements, the second things imagined, the third is a stranger to every circumscription, conceiving God in whatever way, and the virtues also and the vices.

There are also three kinds of dreams: one of a mind full of dregs, another of a sober mind, the third of a mind purged of dregs. The first kind is a slave to falsehood; the second serves either the one or the other, either truth or falsehood; the third contemplates truth. And, that I may insert something out of the secrets of natural science, 356Athe first kind is before the digestion of real phantasms, the second in the digestion of them, the third after their digestion. In the first kind the soul is overwhelmed by the mass of phantasms: thence the error of falsehood. In the second, because the soul begins to be purged of dregs, it begins to contemplate some light; yet because for the greatest part it remains full of dregs, it quickly yields, deceived, to falsehood. In the third the load of dreggy phantasms is strained away to purity: thence the splendor of truth. The first kind of dream, therefore, is deceptive, the second revelatory, the third contemplative. There are also three kinds of prophecy. The first is admirable, the second doubtful, the third lowly. Prophecy is a divine inspiration, declaring with unmoved truth the outcome of hidden things. That prophecy therefore is lowly 356Bwhich is made concerning the elements and things composed of the elements; doubtful, that which is made concerning the manners of men and their counsels; admirable, that which is concerning the truth of God's judgments and his own secrets. That is lowly which is concerning what is near and known; doubtful, which is concerning what is near and unknown; admirable, which is concerning what is hidden and unknown. The first therefore and the second belong to the daughters, the third to the sons. At the coming of the Paraclete, therefore, sons and daughters prophesy with every kind of prophecy; the old men dream with the contemplative kind of dream; the young men see with the intellectual and revelatory kind of vision. And you shall know that I am the Lord your God, etc. Let us follow up the vengeance begun, and the explanation concerning the last coming. When the brightness of the sun and moon and of all the stars shall have been exchanged for darkness, the Lord will roar out of Sion after the manner of a lion, or will cry out, and his voice will be so lofty and so terrible that the hinges of the heavens and the foundations of the lands are shaken. And although he will be so severe toward those who are to be punished, he will nevertheless be merciful toward his own people, and will give them 366Cstrength — those who are called children of Israel, namely as seeing God with the mind: who have not walked by crooked ways, but, walking in the way of Christ, have done all things aright. Then both those who will be punished in Gehenna and those who will be taken up into glory will know that the Lord dwells in his watchtower Sion, and in Christ his holy mountain: namely in him who had prepared himself a dwelling worthy of God.

But if it be read of the first coming, the explanation will be easy and fitting, thus. It is no wonder if in the lowliness of judgment, through the power of the word and the discernment of faith, the nations subject themselves to the Son of God, who is the living word of God, and effectual, and more piercing than any two-edged sword (Hebr. IV). Living, because he is not changed: effectual, because 366Dhe does not fail: piercing, because he is not deceived. He is not changed in his promise: he does not fail in his deed: he is not deceived in his judgment. His promise does not die by forgetfulness, his working is not overcome by difficulty: his judgment is not deceived by ambiguity. He promises truly, he acts mightily, he discerns subtly. He is living, that thou mayest believe: effectual, that thou mayest hope: piercing, that thou mayest fear. He is living in precepts and prohibitions: effectual in promises and threatenings: piercing in judgments and condemnations. Since therefore the word of God is living, it must be believed that he promises true things: since he is effectual, it must be believed that he brings his promises to fulfillment: since he is piercing and cannot be deceived, it must be mourned that one has offended him, and for the rest one must beware of offending. In that word three things are to be considered: sound, voice, 367Aunderstanding. In the sound, the noise; in the voice, the sense; in the understanding, the perfection of the intellect. The noise looks to threatening: the voice to consolation: the understanding to the perfection of knowledge. The noise therefore to the torment of penitence, the voice to the grace of pardon, the understanding to the brightness of glory. And this is what he says: The Lord shall roar out of Sion, etc. The Lord's roaring is the noise of the word. Whence these three: roaring, voice, knowledge. The roaring is made by threatening, the voice by consolation, the knowledge by contemplation. By threatening he draws to penitence, by consoling he calls back to grace, by contemplating he raises up to glory. Through the roaring the earth is moved to penitence, through the voice the heavens are moved to grace, through the understanding we know thee, the one only and true God, and Jesus Christ whom thou hast sent. 367BAnd this is what is said: And you shall know that I am the Lord your God, dwelling in my holy mountain.

But if the present prophecy be understood of the coming of Christ into the mind, this will be the sum of the tropology. One must go into the valley of judgment; there the cause of salvation, the form of penitence, the rule of righteousness must be sought. There the enemy is destroyed, the citizen set free, he that is raised up is exalted, he that is exalted is made perfect. There are indeed three judgments: namely one's own, the human, and the divine. One's own is made out of compunction of heart and human penitence: the human out of public discipline, the divine making manifest things hidden and secret. Through one's own the enemy is destroyed and the citizen set free. Through the human he is polished and exalted. Through the divine he is perfected to a nicety. Each of these judgments is deservedly said to be set in 367Ca valley, because each presses down and humbles the mind, each strikes the heart of man with fear and torments it in various ways. The first humbles the mind by the terror of Gehenna, the second presses down the spirit by the weight of discipline, the third lays the soul low by the love of righteousness. In the first judgment, therefore, comes the victory of the nations and the death of the strong; in the second, the abundance of the presses and the grace of gifts; in the third, the hope of the people and the strength of the children. The day of the Lord is said to be near the valley of judgment. The day of the Lord is the day of penitence, the day of discipline, the day of glory. Near the first valley is the day of penitence, near the second the day of discipline, near the third the day of glory. Hence the Psalmographer: Show forth from day to day his salvation (Psal. XCV). 367DFrom the day, namely, of penitence, the day of discipline: from the day of discipline, the day of glory. And this is what is said: And you shall know that I am the Lord your God, dwelling in Sion my holy mountain. There follows:

And Jerusalem shall be holy, and strangers shall pass through it no more. And it shall come to pass in that day: the mountains shall drop down sweetness, and the hills shall flow with milk. And through all the brooks of Juda waters shall go: and a fountain shall come forth out of the house of the Lord, and shall water the torrent of thorns. These things the Jews and our judaizers refer to the fables of the thousand years: when they suppose that Christ will dwell in Sion, and that in a golden and jewelled Jerusalem the peoples of the saints will be gathered together, so that they who in this present age have been oppressed by all the nations may in this same age rule over all nations. This the fable of the Jews 368Adreams: while the truth furnishes us with something far otherwise. Jerusalem signifies the Church of this present time: it signifies also the faithful soul: and also that which is above, free, our mother (Gal. IV).

The letter can be taken of the threefold coming, and what is said can fittingly be understood of each one. For after the sending of the Paraclete, after the preaching of the apostles, after the conversion of the nations in the valley of judgment, through the discernment of faith, Jerusalem becomes holy: and strangers shall pass through it no more. Strangers are said to be idolaters, heretics, schismatics. All of whom, at the beginning of the primitive Church, came with one accord to assail her, but did not pass through her, because, although they strive to assail the chastity of her faith, 368Bthey can nevertheless in no way break her unity, nor violate her charity. They came indeed armed with phantasmal reasonings, with sophistic arguments: but they were unable to pass through, driven back by the truth of the word, overcome by the reason of judgment, laid low by the testimonies of the Scriptures and by the heat of faith. They came indeed prepared to engage, but did not pass through, being compelled to go back. And from the very name of holiness her manifold power is shown, and the kind of her holiness. What is called sanctum in Latin is called ἄγιος, that is agios, in Greek. Agios is a name compounded of α and γῆ, that is gi Gi, means earth, a without. Whence that too is called holy which comes to be without earth, and is lifted up from earth. Holy were they of whom it is written: Our conversation is in heaven (Philipp. III). Holy too were those of whom 368Cit is said: Among whom you shine as lights in the world: holding forth the word of life (Philipp. II). Jerusalem then is holy in her sacraments, in her precepts, in her judgments, in her counsels, in her promises. For her sacraments are without dregs, her precepts without burden, her judgments without strife, her counsels without envy, her promises without the paint of deceit — without the envy of jealousy, without the strife of controversy, without the burden of forced service, without the dregs of concupiscence. She is therefore holy, whose understanding is contemplative, whose affection is heavenly, whose sense is spiritual, whose act is angelic. Whence John in the Apocalypse: I saw, he says, the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven, adorned as a bride for her husband (Apoc. XXI). There follows: And strangers shall pass through 368Dher no more. Hence Nahum: Keep, he says, thy festivals, O Juda: and pay thy vows, because Belial shall no more pass through thee: he is utterly perished (Nahum I). There follows: And it shall come to pass in that day: the mountains shall drop down sweetness, etc. That day is the coming of the Paraclete; the mountains, the apostles; the hills, the disciples; the little brooks, the subject peoples; the sweetness, wisdom; the water, teaching. There are three kinds of teaching: the contemplative, the allegorical, the moral. The contemplative pure, the allegorical bare, the moral alone. Alone, from matter; bare, from form; pure, from every kind of circumscription. For the moral kind forms life without matter. The allegorical illumines knowledge without form. The contemplative, without phantasy, raises up to wisdom. Otherwise: Water is the historical condition; 369Amilk, moral teaching; sweetness, spiritual edification. They shall drop down from the higher places, they shall flow in the level places, they shall go in the valleys. The higher things of God are his invisible judgments: the level places, his manifest counsels; the valleys, humble penitence, obedience to the precepts, works of mercy. There follows: And a fountain shall come forth out of the house of the Lord, and shall water the torrent of thorns. The house of the Lord is the beauty of the primitive Church: the fountain, the fullness of evangelical teaching; the torrent of thorns is the onrush, the heat, the cruelty of the nations. Further, the fountain that came forth from the house of the Lord watered the torrent of thorns when, through the apostles and their successors, evangelical teaching initiated the Gentile people unto the end, converted it to belief, and led it to the grace of baptism. There follows:

369BEgypt shall be in desolation: and Idumaea in a desert of destruction: for that which they have done unjustly against the children of the Jew, and have shed innocent blood in their own land. And Judaea shall be inhabited for ever; and Jerusalem from generation to generation. Nothing on earth comes to pass without a cause: nor does the Lord without desert rain upon one city, nor without reason leave another unrained-upon. For behold, on the one hand Egypt and Idumaea remain desolate for the causes subjoined; on the other, Judaea and Jerusalem, for that they lived innocently, continently, obediently, are inhabited for ever, and rejoice. Egypt is the portion of the Gentile people that is reprobate and blinded through ignorance. Idumaea, the portion of the Jewish people that is reprobate and deserted by God through depraved and unclean concupiscence. Judaea is the wife of the Lamb, the bride of Christ, 369Cconfessing her ignorance, pricked with compunction and wearing herself away because of disordered concupiscence. Confessing to herself the darkness of ignorance, the filth of concupiscence, the horror of malice. Confessing to God the grace of pardon, the purity of innocence, the sublimity of glory. There follows:

And I will cleanse their blood, which they had not cleansed [quem non mundaveram]; and the Lord shall dwell in Sion. Here there is secretly brought in, through the coming of Christ, the remedy of grace against the shipwreck of original sin. For that blood, namely original sin, could in no way be cleansed or blotted out by anyone's righteousness or power, save by the blood of the immaculate Lamb, who knew no sin. Whence 369Dthe Apostle: Without the shedding of blood there is no remission (Hebr. IX). Likewise the same: For it was fitting that we should have such a high priest, holy, innocent, undefiled, separated from sinners, and made higher than the heavens (Hebr. VII). And this is what in the present passage the Lord says through the prophet: And I will cleanse their blood, which they had not cleansed, that is, which they had not been able to cleanse. And then the Lord shall dwell in Sion. Then, namely, when all things shall be subjected to him, and he himself shall subject the kingdom to God and the Father. Then he shall dwell in the watchtower, because the sight of him shall be ministered to the elect unto glory. Then he shall be all in all (I Cor. XV): life, strength, bread, drink, form, salvation, light, fountain of life, leader, law, medicine. So shall he be all in all, according to that word: I shall be satisfied when thy glory shall appear (Psal. XVI). 370AHence is that word: This is, he says, life eternal, that they may know thee, the only true God, and Jesus Christ whom thou hast sent (Joan. XVII). Hence is that word of the wise man: To behold thee is the end, the beginning, the bearer, the leader, the path, the same the term. So much for the coming of Christ in the flesh; now let us speak of his coming to judgment.

And Jerusalem shall be holy, and strangers shall pass through her no more. After the sentence of judgment has been duly given, after the separation of the elect from the reprobate, the assembly of the saints shall be joined to the angelic choirs; and then Jerusalem, the wife of the Lamb, shall be lifted up from earthly things: Then God shall wipe away every tear from the eyes of the saints; and now there shall be no more either mourning or crying; nor any sorrow, for the former things are passed away (Apoc. 370BXXI). There shall be, I say, no more the crying of suggestion, the sorrow of temptation, the mourning of compunction; for the former things are passed away. What are the former things? The serpent's suggestion, the woman's delight, the man's consent. But the suggestion has passed away, impassibility being given; the delight, charity being made perfect; the consent, felicity being made full. Hence Isaiah: Arise, he says, arise, put on thy strength, O Sion; put on the garments of thy glory, O Jerusalem, city of the holy one, because the uncircumcised and the unclean shall no more pass through thee (Isa. LII). There follows: And it shall come to pass in that day: the mountains shall drop down sweetness, etc. By the mountains and the hills and the brooks the diversity of merits is shown. By the sweetness, the milk and the water, the variety of rewards. And that a fountain comes forth out of the house of the Lord watering the torrent of thorns, 370Cthat is what is said elsewhere: Everlasting joy shall be upon their heads (Isa. XXXV). And that: The Lamb, who is in the midst of them, shall rule them, and shall lead them to the fountains of the waters of life (Apoc. VII). Then Egypt and Idumaea shall be in a desert of destruction, and Jerusalem with Judaea shall be inhabited unto the years of generation; because to those it shall be said: Go, ye cursed, into everlasting fire (Matth. XXV); and to these: Come, ye blessed of my Father; receive the kingdom (ibid.). And then the Lord shall cleanse the blood of the elect, which they had not cleansed before; because, as the doctors assert, the elect, seeing the apostate angel punished by an irrevocable sentence, bound fast in an indissoluble chain, condemned to a horrible penalty, shall be shaken with fear, and by that very fear shall be purged of that which they had cleansed only with difficulty. 370DAccording to that word of Job: When he shall be taken away, he says, speaking of the apostate angel, the angels shall fear, and being terrified shall be purified (Job XLI). And after these things the Lord shall dwell in Sion, that is, in the watchtower of vision; because then he shall gird himself, and shall make them sit down, and passing shall minister unto them. Morally, concerning the coming of the Bridegroom to the bride of Christ, to the dove, to the only one, the beloved and faithful soul. When the Lord, dwelling in Sion, in the holy mountain, in the height, namely, of contemplation, enters more closely with the bride into the bond of union, when the bridegroom also proffers heavenly kisses, when with angelic service he ministers to her the dishes of paradise, then Jerusalem becomes holy — holy indeed, because lifted up from earthly things. Lifted up, I say, from iniquity, lifted up from curiosity, 371Aheld back from pleasure, set apart from vanity. Then strangers pass through her no more, because neither the suggestion of the evil one, nor the elation of the spirit, nor the titillation of the flesh, nor the harassment of the world dare in any way to provoke her, whom the felicity of the Bridegroom, shut up in the bridal chamber of contemplation, sets in the little couch of union. Strangers do not transcunt, I say, through her any more; because she who is joined to the impassible and set within the blessed is not struck by any trouble of things, even for a moment. By the grace of the Bridegroom, I say she is made impassible, not created so substantially. There follows:

And it shall come to pass in that day — that is, in that brightness of contemplation — the mountains shall drop down sweetness, and the hills shall flow with milk, and through all the brooks of Juda waters shall go. The mountains are the sublime things of contemplations; the hills, 371Bthe works of innocence; the brooks, the exercises of penitence; the sweetness is perfect love; the milk is holy devotion; the water, devout compunction. In that day, therefore — that is, in that felicity of union — the bride, through the joy of contemplation, receives the sweetness of perfect love: descending from which, and returning to herself, she flows with the milk of devotion. Whence, crucifying herself out of love for the things above, she is plunged into the waters of compunction, and coming forth thence is exercised in harsh penitence; after these things she is drawn to the works of innocence, and at last she flies away to those of contemplation, saying. For what have I in heaven? and besides thee what have I desired upon earth? (Psal. LXXII.) Likewise: My soul hath chosen hanging (Job VII). Then the fountain comes forth out of the house of the Lord, and waters the torrent of thorns; because the abundance and the privilege of spiritual 372Agrace flows out of the secret joy of a most pure conscience, blunting the stings of vices, restraining the movement of temptations, calming the gust of desires, and polishing the seat of the affections. Then ignorance is put to flight, then carnal concupiscence is brought to ruin; because the mind is illumined by the brightness of contemplation, and concupiscence is healed by the perfection of love. For Egypt is interpreted darkness, by which ignorance is designated: Idumaea, earthly things, by which carnal concupiscence is designated. But Judaea and Jerusalem shall be inhabited for ever. Judaea, confession; Jerusalem is called the vision of peace. For the devout and perfect mind, either descending to the knowledge of itself, confesses its offenses to God through the humility of compunction; or, ascending to the knowledge of God through the grace of contemplation, 372Bis raised up to the vision of peace, and then in the voice of exultation and of confession there comes to be in her the sound of one feasting. Never does the faithful soul forsake Judaea; because either, pricked with compunction, she confesses her crimes, or, devout, she gives thanks for the gifts of grace, or, raised on high, she pours forth a hymn as she receives her rewards. Then too the Bridegroom cleanses the blood which hitherto could not be cleansed, because through the fellowship of union the Bridegroom bestows upon the bride the privilege of perfection, so that nature may feel against nature, that which nature naturally withstands. And then the Lord of nature, by the privilege of grace, dwells in Sion, that is, in the watchtower of glory. May he deign to purge our nature, and to bestow upon us his own grace, unto the glory of that same sublimity. Who, three and one, liveth and reigneth Amen.
