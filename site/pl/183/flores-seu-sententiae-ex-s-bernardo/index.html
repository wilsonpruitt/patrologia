Flowers, or Sentences, from St. Bernard

1197B1569 Chastity is in peril amid delights, humility amid riches, piety amid business, truth amid much speaking, charity in this wicked world. De convers. ad cler., num. 37

Nothing is more precious than time; but, alas, nothing today is reckoned cheaper. Tr. de cont. mun. ad cler., n. 55

The days of salvation pass, and no one thinks of it again; no one pleads that moments which will not return have perished for him. Ibid.

Nothing is so hard that it does not yield to something harder. IV, De consid., c. 3. Hence habit is conquered by habit.

Charge to yourself whatever you suffer from him who can do nothing without you. IV De cons. n. 9.

The scorpion has nothing in its face for you to dread, but it stings from the tail. Ibid.

1197CWhat good is it to be wise, if you have not been wise for yourself? II, De consid. 2.

All things are lacking to him who thinks that nothing is lacking to him. Lib. II, De consid. cap. 7.

An ape on the roof — a foolish king sitting on the throne. Ibid.

Keep the middle, if you do not wish to lose measure. II, De consid. cap. 10.

Prosperity is to the incautious what fire is to wax, and the sun's ray to snow. II, De consid. cap. 12.

It is no great thing to be humble in abasement; humility that is honored is a rare virtue. Hom. 4 sup. miss.

There is no truer misery than false gladness. De lib. arb., n. 14.

We are plainly convicted of willing what would not happen if we did not will it. 1197DIbid.

A mind poured out abroad does not feel its inward losses. De conv. ad. cler., cap. 4, n. 5.

To flee persecution is not the fault of the one fleeing, but of the one persecuting. Epist. 1.

The will is held for the deed, where necessity shuts out the deed. Tract. ad Hugon., n. 9.

1198BNo one deserves anger more than an enemy feigning to be a friend. De conv. n. 33.

There is no reason for those to fear thieves who lay up treasure for themselves in heaven. De conv. ad cler., n. 41.

He does not please who is not himself pleasing. Ibid., 33.

The mother of the insolence of clerics is the negligence of bishops. Epist. 152.

One ought to humor one's friends, but not to one's own death. Epist. 215.

If the water of a river begins to stand still, it rots. Serm. 1 de Quadrag.

All things that are in the world have an end; but their end will not be an end. Serm. 9 in Cant.

You will give to your voice the voice of power, if you have first persuaded yourself of what you urge. Serm. 6 in Cant., n. 3.

1198CI gladly hear the voice of that teacher who stirs not applause for himself, but lamentation in me. Ibid.

The voice of the turtledove does not sound sweetly, but it signifies sweet things. Namely, love of its mate. Ibid.

Will you or will you not, the Jebusite dwells within your borders: he can be subjugated, but not exterminated. Serm. 57 in Cant., num. 10.

It is little to have pruned once; one must prune often — indeed, always. Ibid. For the vices idemtidem sprout.

Virtue cannot grow together with the vices; let cupidity be pruned, that virtue may be strengthened. Ibid.

Take away what is superfluous, and wholesome things rise up. Whatever you take from cupidity accrues to usefulness. Ibid.

1198DA shepherd who is learned but not good does not so much nourish by his rich teaching as harm by his barren life. Serm. 76 in Cant., num. 10.

Ambition that has obtained its wishes does sometimes less harm than ambition frustrated. Epist. 126: For it turns to violent means.

1570 To hearing or reading the song of love 1199Ahe comes in vain who does not love. Serm. 79 in Cant., n. 1.

A cold breast cannot take in fiery speech. Ibid. Just as one who does not know Greek does not understand a man speaking Greek.

Reputation cannot claim for virtue what conscience convicts of being vice. Serm. 71 in Cant., n. 2.

Virtue is content with the brightness of conscience, even if the fragrance of reputation does not follow. Ibid.

Many things which you spit out when idle, you will take up with longing after labor. Epist. 1, n. 11. For hunger is the best seasoning of food.

The enemy presses more boldly from behind than he withstands you to the face. Ibid., n. 12.

1199BTo do evil, even at anyone's command, is not obedience but disobedience. Epist. 7. n. 3. Namely, toward God.

Whatever each man worships above the rest, he is shown to have set up as his God. Tract. de contempt. mund. ad cler., cap. 5, num. 17.

Many would not run to honors with such eagerness, if they felt that they are also burdens. Tract. de off. episc., cap. 7.

Would that the Noahs of our day might leave us something by which we could be covered by them. Ibid., n. 19, cap. 8. So openly and shamelessly is sin now committed.

For one set on high, not to be high-minded is difficult. Ibid.

If it delights you to be higher rather than better, expect not a reward but a headlong fall. Epist. 27 ad 1199CArdut.

It is foolish to store up treasure in a place from which you cannot take it back when you wish. Tract. de off. episc., cap. 5.

Tribulation proves what each man's faith is. Ibid., c. 4.

He who is commanded to love his neighbor by his own measure should know first how to love himself. Ibid., cap. 4, n. 13.

To repent of evils, and to abstain from evils, makes the conscience good. Ibid., cap. 4.

Physicians use the same knife for cutting kings as for common men. Ibid., cap. 2.

It belongs to the good shepherd not to seek his own, but to spend himself. IV, De consid., cap. 2.

Stewardship without necessity and usefulness is not faithful 1199Dstewardship, but cruel squandering. III, De consid., cap. 4.

Since all men have you, be also yourself one of those who have you. Lib. I De consid., cap. 5. n. 6.

A lover sometimes seems out of his mind — but only to one who does not love. Praefat. in lib. De consid.

The measure of loving God is to love without measure. Tract. de dilig. Deo, cap. 1 et 6.

I do not approve a man who knows many things, if he has not known the measure of knowing. Serm. 36 in Cant., n. 3.

There are those who wish to know only that they may know; and this is curiosity.

There are those who wish to know that they themselves may be known; and this is vanity.
