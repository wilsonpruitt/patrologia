Nicholas of Clairvaux

Nicolaus Claraevallensis

d. after 1176

Monk of Montieramey and then of Clairvaux, where he served as secretary to Bernard until a breach of trust over the abbot’s seal ended his position there. His sermons and letters circulated widely, some of them long transmitted under Bernard’s name.

Corde creditur ad justitiam, ore autem fit confessio ad salutem (Rom. X, 10).

1049B714 1. Et quietum studium, et attentum silentium, et tanta tanti viri solemnitas monent, imo compellunt aliquid dicere ad ostendendum gloriam deitatis, gratiam apostolicae dignitatis, confessionis virtutem. Demus gloriam laudi ejus, quem laudant omnes gentes, qui ministros suos et laudat, et laudabiles facit. Ipse est cui assistunt Angeli ad audiendam vocem sermonum ejus: qui stellas vocat et dicunt: Adsumus: cujus manus extendit coelos, qui fundavit terram super stabilitatem suam, qui graditur super fluctus maris, qui fecit mirabilia solus, in quem desiderant Angeli prospicere. Ipse est qui fecit omnia, et in omnibus quae fecit, quaecunque voluit fecit. Huic servire regnare est, qui servivit pro nobis dura et gravi necessitate, et purpuram 1049Csuae divinitatis cilicio nostrae mortalitatis operiens, pauper et mendicus venit in regionem nostram. Numquid non ludus vobis videtur, Judaei, pagani, haeretici, et omnis illa subsannantium, imo insanientium turba, cum dicimus Filium Dei carnem, mortem, clavos et lanceam tolerasse? Ludus profecto est, unde sic nobis luditur, ut vobis illudatur. Unicus ille Sapiens hujus mysterii non ignarus, ludum istum eleganti sermone praelusit. Delectabar, inquit, per singulos dies, ludens coram eo in orbe terrarum (Prov. VIII, 30). Lusit quippe coram Deo, cum carnem ex ejus praecepto suscepit; et in orbe terrarum, quia ad redemptionem orbis terrarum delectatus, per singulos dies in singulis Apostolis jucundatus, dum uni tradit regnum coelorum, alteri de 1049Dpectore suo reclinatorium facit.

With the heart it is believed unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation (Rom. X, 10).

1049B714 1. Both your quiet attention, and your intent silence, and the great solemnity of so great a man admonish — nay, compel — me to say something toward showing the glory of the Godhead, the grace of apostolic dignity, the power of confession. Let us give glory to the praise of him whom all nations praise, who both praises his ministers and makes them praiseworthy. He it is to whom the Angels stand by to hear the voice of his words; who calls the stars, and they say: We are here; whose hand has stretched out the heavens, who founded the earth upon its own stability, who walks upon the waves of the sea, who alone has done wonderful things, upon whom the Angels desire to look. He it is who made all things, and in all the things he made, he did whatsoever he willed. To serve him is to reign — him who served for us under a hard and heavy necessity, and, covering the purple 1049Cof his divinity with the sackcloth of our mortality, came poor and a beggar into our country. Does it not seem to you a game, you Jews, pagans, heretics, and all that throng of mockers, or rather of madmen, when we say that the Son of God endured flesh, death, nails, and the lance? A game it truly is, whereby the play is ours, and the mockery is turned on you. That one Wise Man, not ignorant of this mystery, played this game beforehand in an elegant saying. I was delighted, he says, every single day, playing before him in the world of the earth (Prov. VIII, 30). For he played before God, when at his command he took on flesh; and in the world of the earth, because he was delighted unto the redemption of the world of the earth; every single day, being made glad in each of the Apostles, while to one he hands over the kingdom of heaven, and of his own 1049Dbreast makes a resting place for another.

2.

Et deliciae meae esse cum filiis hominum. Putabam ego, Domine Jesu, quod deliciae tuae essent esse non cum filiis hominum, sed cum choris Angelorum, ubi divinitatis gloria Thronis et Potestatibus admirandus superiorum oculos reverberas naturarum; ibi lucem inhabitas inaccessibilem, et circumstantiam clarissimorum luminum tui luminis obtegis majestate. Sed illa clementissima tua pietas, et benignitas adoranda, delicias suas dicit esse cum filiis 1050Bhominum. Isti sunt filii hominum, qui speraverunt sub tegmine alarum tuarum, quos elegisti de plebe, quorum lavisti pedes, quibus tuum spiritum infudisti, qui fidem tui nominis quadrigis evangelicis per orbem quadrifidum portaverunt. Nota tamen quod dicit, non, 715 Cum hominibus; sed, cum filiis hominum. Ego puto homines ad veterem hominem; filios hominum ad filium hominis pertinere, juxta quam intelligentiam puer citharista sic intonat: Quid est homo, quod memor es ejus? aut filius hominis, quoniam visitas eum? (Psal. VIII, 5.) Videsne quia Deum hominis esse memorem, filium vero hominis se visitare confirmat? Praesumimus et speramus, quia faciet nobis misericordiam suam in conspectu filiorum hominum, si interim protegat nos 1050Cin abscondito faciei suae a conturbatione hominum. Igitur et Dominus noster, cujus schola est in terris; et cathedra in coelo, ex his omnibus quos elegit et praeelegit, Andream elegit apostolum, cujus solemnitatem et vocibus, et cordibus inclamamus. O dulcis Apostolus et primitiva vocatio Domini Salvatoris, qui in ipsum apostolicum chorum tanti prioratus insignitur fastigio! Non subterfugit nos, quia quodammodo tenerrimus est, et dulcior in cordibus fidelium, cujus passio tanta spiritus devotione distillat, ut mentes audientium adipe et pinguedine replere videatur. Unde non immerito in singularem auctoritatis arcem suscepta est, quam qui viderunt scripserunt orbi, et ministri fuere sermonis. Vocatio ejus, vita ejus et passio cognitae sunt vobis: et nunc 1050Dquid superest ut a me amplius exspectetis?

2.

And my delights are to be with the sons of men. I used to think, Lord Jesus, that your delights would be to be not with the sons of men, but with the choirs of Angels, where in the glory of the Godhead, to be wondered at by the Thrones and Powers, you beat back the eyes of the higher natures; there you inhabit light inaccessible, and cover the standing circle of the brightest luminaries with the majesty of your own light. But that most merciful devotion of yours, and your adorable kindness, says its delights are with the sons 1050Bof men. These are the sons of men who have hoped under the covering of your wings, whom you chose out of the people, whose feet you washed, into whom you poured your spirit, who carried the faith of your name by the four-horse chariots of the Gospels through the four-parted world. Note, however, that he says not, 715 With men, but, with the sons of men. I think that men pertain to the old man, and the sons of men to the Son of man; according to which understanding the boy harper sounds forth thus: What is man, that thou art mindful of him? or the son of man, that thou visitest him? (Psal. VIII, 5.) Do you see that he affirms that God is mindful of man, but that he visits the son of man? We presume and hope that he will do us his mercy in the sight of the sons of men, if meanwhile he protect us 1050Cin the hiding of his face from the disturbance of men. Therefore our Lord also, whose school is on earth and whose chair is in heaven, out of all those whom he chose and chose beforehand, chose Andrew as an apostle, whose solemnity we acclaim both with our voices and with our hearts. O sweet Apostle, and first calling of the Lord and Savior, who in that very apostolic choir is marked out by the height of so great a precedence! It does not escape us that he is in a manner most tender, and sweeter in the hearts of the faithful, whose passion drops with so great a devotion of spirit that it seems to fill the minds of the hearers with fatness and richness. Whence not undeservedly has it been taken up into a singular citadel of authority — which those who saw it wrote for the world, and were ministers of the word. His calling, his life, and his passion are known to you: and now 1050Dwhat remains that you should expect further from me?

3.

Verumtamen movet me lectio apostolica, ut aliquantisper de ea vestrae reverentiae colloquamur. Ait enim: Corde creditur ad justitiam, ore autem confessio fit ad salutem. Dicit enim Scriptura: Omnis qui credit in ipsum non confundetur. Tria proponuntur hic: et corde credere ad justitiam, et ore confiteri ad salutem, et in illum credere, in quem qui crediderit non confundetur. Solum cor hominis capax est orationis et susceptibile mysteriorum Dei, 1051Aquod purgatum atque politum ipsam divinitatis substantiam considerat: etsi non comprehendit, apprehendit tamen. Cor est in pectore, anima in corde, mens in anima, fides in mente, Christus in fide. Et hoc est, puto, quod dicit Apostolus, habitare Christum per fidem in cordibus nostris (Ephes. III, 17). Hoc igitur in meracissimam eliquatum est puritatem, cum omnia mysteria christianae fidei fide percipis inconcussa, nihilque est quod tibi dubitationem parit, licet non videas rationem. Haec est consummata justitia, perfecta veritas, et vera perfectio, si tamen oris confessio subsequatur. Fides enim cordis sine oris fide, aut nulla est, aut parva, Salvatore dicente: Qui me erubuerit et meos sermones, hunc Filius hominis erubescet (Luc. IX, 26), etc. Et 1051Bpropheta: Periit fides et ablata est de ore eorum. Non dixit, De corde; sed, de ore: quia nihil valet fides cordis sine fide oris, nec fides oris sine fide cordis; cum ista justitiam, illa donet salutem; quia salus a justitia, vel justitia a salute separari non possunt. Crede ergo et confitere; nihilominus confitere et crede: quia fides ad justitiam, confessio fit ad salutem.

3.

Nevertheless the apostolic reading moves me to speak with your reverence a little while concerning it. For it says: With the heart it is believed unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation. For the Scripture says: Everyone who believes in him shall not be confounded. Three things are here set forth: to believe with the heart unto justice, to confess with the mouth unto salvation, and to believe in him in whom whoever believes shall not be confounded. The heart of man alone is capable of prayer and receptive of the mysteries of God, 1051Awhich, when purged and polished, considers the very substance of the Godhead: and even if it does not comprehend it, yet it lays hold of it. The heart is in the breast, the soul in the heart, the mind in the soul, faith in the mind, Christ in faith. And this, I think, is what the Apostle says, that Christ dwells by faith in our hearts (Ephes. III, 17). This, therefore, is refined into the purest purity, when you perceive all the mysteries of the Christian faith with unshaken faith, and there is nothing that breeds doubt in you, though you do not see the reason. This is consummate justice, perfect truth, and true perfection — if, however, the confession of the mouth follows after. For the faith of the heart without the faith of the mouth is either nothing or little, since the Savior says: He who is ashamed of me and of my words, of him shall the Son of man be ashamed (Luc. IX, 26), and so forth. And 1051Bthe prophet: Faith has perished and is taken away out of their mouth. He did not say, Out of the heart, but, out of the mouth: because the faith of the heart avails nothing without the faith of the mouth, nor the faith of the mouth without the faith of the heart; since the one grants justice, the other salvation; because salvation cannot be separated from justice, nor justice from salvation. Believe therefore and confess; and no less, confess and believe: because faith is unto justice, confession is made unto salvation.

4.

Et quandoquidem in haec devenimus loca, et occasio confessionis se obtulit, reddendum est quod alibi promisimus, totumque conferendum quod de confessione sentimus. Patientes autem estote, et si forte protensius sermonem extendero, vos intendite quae dicuntur, quia vestrae utilitati proferuntur. Via enim est sine qua nemo venit ad Patrem: quam qui perdidit, perdidit Deum. Cum enim de vitiorum 1051Cabysso disponis emergere, ad illum praecipue sit refugium tuum, cui tua anima commissa est, quem imposuit Deus super caput tuum. Nec te moveat si illiteratus sit, vel indiscretus, quia hoc ipsum signum humilitatis est, quae generat confessionem. Memento quia non est potestas, nisi a Deo. Super cathedram, inquit, Moysi sedent Scribae et Pharisaei. Et tu attende non ad sedentem, sed sedem; cathedram, non personam. Quod si forte vel licentia ejus ad alium transfugere permittatur, tu tamen prius ei reveles tui cordis arcanum: quia non est plena salus, si ille fugitur aut contemnitur, cui adhaerere, et quem honorare debueras. Quod si minus sufficiens tibi videtur ejus instructio, patet ad libertatem via, ut coram 1051Dalio litterato atque discreto effundas cor tuum; reservato illi privilegio suo, cui tu tuam animam commendasti.

4.

And since we have come into these regions, and the occasion of confession has offered itself, we must render what we promised elsewhere, and bring together the whole of what we think concerning confession. But be patient, and if perhaps I extend the sermon at greater length, do you attend to what is said, since it is brought forth for your profit. For it is a way without which no one comes to the Father: and he who has lost it has lost God. For when you set about emerging from the abyss of vices, 1051Clet your refuge be chiefly to him to whom your soul has been committed, whom God set over your head. And let it not move you if he be unlettered or lacking discernment, because this very thing is a sign of humility, which begets confession. Remember that there is no power but from God. Upon the chair of Moses, he says, sit the Scribes and Pharisees. And do you attend not to the one sitting, but to the seat; to the chair, not the person. But if perhaps, even by his leave, it be permitted you to flee across to another, do you nevertheless first reveal to him the secret of your heart: because salvation is not full if he is fled from or despised to whom you ought to have cleaved and whom you ought to have honored. But if his instruction seems to you insufficient, the way lies open to freedom, that before 1051Danother, lettered and discerning, you may pour out your heart — his own privilege being reserved to him to whom you commended your soul.

5.

Nec tibi blandiaris, si graviter peccanti levior poenitentia, vel a nesciente, vel a dissimulante dictatur, cum 716 in purgatoriis ignibus perficiendum sit, quidquid hic minus feceris, quia dignos fructus poenitentiae quaerit Altissimus. Licet autem non evaserit manus Omnipotentis, qui tibi non plene consulit, tibi tamen ratio magistra praesidet, quae te doceat tantum a licitis abstinere, quantum te memineris illicita perpetrasse. A vestibulo igitur hujus sacratissimae virtutis omnes illos excludimus, quos ad confessionem vel timor cogit, quia extorta confessio non est confessio; vel aliorum accusatio trahit, 1052Aquia non est confessio, sed offensio; vel sui excusatio ducit, quia non est confessio, sed defensio; vel jactantia inanis rapit, quia non est confessio, sed illusio; et si quae aliae pestes sunt, quae obumbratorio velamine tanti sacramenti simulant sanctitatem. Cor in semetipso, et ex semetipso contritum et humiliatum, ad hujus purificationis potest ascendere puritatem. Ostendam tibi unum justorum justissime confitentem. Quaeris quisnam sit? Psaltes Domini est. Dixi, inquit, Confitebor adversum me injustitiam meam Domino (Psal. XXXI, 5). Dixi, hoc est, in corde proposui, non coactus, non compulsus, sed spontaneus. Injustitiam meam, ait, non alienam, adversum me arguens et accusans. Vides quia non timore alterius, vel ad alium accusandum, sed se 1052Bipsum sanctus Domino confitetur. Pro hujusmodi confessione remittes tu, Domine, impietatem peccati mei. Pro hac orabit ad te omnis sanctus in tempore opportuno. Haec primum in corde germinat ad justitiam, post haec in ore nascitur ad salutem.

5.

Nor should you flatter yourself if to one sinning gravely a lighter penance is prescribed, whether by one who does not know or by one who dissembles, since 716 whatever you do here too little must be completed in the purgatorial fires, because the Most High seeks worthy fruits of penance. And although he who does not fully counsel you has not escaped the hand of the Almighty, yet reason presides over you as mistress, to teach you to abstain from things lawful as much as you remember that you have committed things unlawful. From the vestibule, therefore, of this most sacred virtue we shut out all those whom either fear compels to confession — because an extorted confession is not confession; or whom the accusation of others draws on, 1052Abecause that is not confession but offense; or whom the excusing of themselves leads, because that is not confession but defense; or whom empty boasting carries off, because that is not confession but mockery; and whatever other plagues there are, which under the shadowing veil of so great a sacrament counterfeit holiness. A heart contrite and humbled in itself and out of itself can ascend to the purity of this purification. I will show you one of the just confessing most justly. Do you ask who it is? He is the psalm-singer of the Lord. I said, he says, I will confess against myself my injustice to the Lord (Psal. XXXI, 5). I said — that is, I purposed in my heart, not forced, not compelled, but of my own accord. My injustice, he says, not another's, arguing and accusing against myself. You see that the holy man confesses to the Lord not from fear of another, nor to accuse another, but 1052Bhimself. For a confession of this kind, thou, O Lord, wilt forgive the impiety of my sin. For this shall every holy one pray to thee in a seasonable time. This first sprouts in the heart unto justice, and after this is born in the mouth unto salvation.

6.

Primus ejus gradus est, cognitio peccati. « Initium salutis, » ait quidam, « notitia peccati (SENEC. in Epist.). » In hac cognitione videndum est quid egeris, quantis contumeliis affeceris corpus tuum, ambulans in passione desiderii, sicut et gentes quae ignorant Deum, possidens vas tuum in immunditia et vilitate, non in sanctificatione et honore. Considerandum est etiam quid merueris; ignem scilicet illum, qui paratus est diabolo et angelis ejus, et omnia genera poenarum, quae in illis poenalibus locis 1052Cemulantur et crescunt. Inspiciendum vero quid amiseris, bona videlicet illa quae oculus non vidit, et auris non audivit, nec in cor hominis ascenderunt, quae praeparavit Deus diligentibus se.

6.

Its first step is the knowledge of sin. « The beginning of salvation, » says a certain writer, « is the knowledge of sin (SENEC. in Epist.). » In this knowledge one must see what you have done, with how many outrages you have afflicted your body, walking in the passion of desire, as do the nations also that know not God, possessing your vessel in uncleanness and vileness, not in sanctification and honor. It must also be considered what you have deserved: namely that fire which is prepared for the devil and his angels, and every kind of punishment which in those places of punishment 1052Cvie with one another and grow. And it must be looked at what you have lost: namely those good things which eye has not seen, and ear has not heard, nor have they ascended into the heart of man, which God has prepared for those who love him.

7.

Horum igitur omnium consideratione perterritus transi ad poenitentiam, ultricem vitiorum, altricem virtutum. Poeniteat te, quod tot et tantis flagitiis involutus, in luto faecis et miseriae diutius jacuisti. Poenitentiam agens corpus tuum contere, et furibundo carnis asello incentivorum semina subtrahens, poenitenda amplius non admittas, ne vulnus vulneri frequenter ingeminans, confringas ossa animae tuae, quae custodit in justis Dominus: Unum ex his, inquam, non conteretur (Psal. XXXIII, 21). 1052DNam haec duo ita sibi invicem conjuncta sunt, ut quis se cognoscere non possit, nisi poeniteat; et poenitere non possit, nisi se cognoscat.

7.

Terrified therefore by the consideration of all these things, pass over to penance, the avenger of vices, the nurse of virtues. Let it repent you that, wrapped in so many and so great disgraces, you have lain too long in the mire of dregs and misery. Doing penance, wear down your body, and, withdrawing the seeds of incitements from the raging little ass of the flesh, admit no further things to be repented of, lest, frequently redoubling wound upon wound, you break the bones of your soul, which the Lord keeps in the just: Not one of these, I say, shall be broken (Psal. XXXIII, 21). 1052DFor these two are so joined to one another that no one can know himself unless he repent; and he cannot repent unless he know himself.

8.

Sic ergo tibi cognitus, et in te poenitens transvola ad tertium gradum, dolorem videlicet cordis; et jam plantata erit justitia plantatione illa, quam plantavit Pater tuus coelestis. Dole igitur quia Creatorem offendisti, cujus legem coelestia et terrestria praeter te indefessa statione conservant: et in tanta republica Dei tu solus peregrinus es, et imperatoriae majestatis decreta non curas. Surgat et acrior dolor, quod cum Creatore etiam Patrem offenderis, et illum patrem, qui tibi ministrat siderum cursus, fecunditatem terrae, fructuum ubertatem. Accedat et acerrimus dolor, qui pertranseat animam 1053Atuam quod tantum benefactorem contempseris, qui dolorem crucis sustinuit, ne dolores inferni amplius sustineres. Sic igitur in interiori homine justitia germinante, surculus erumpat in arborem, et loquatur lingua quod scientia loquebatur.

8.

Thus therefore, being known to yourself and repentant in yourself, fly over to the third step, namely sorrow of heart; and now justice will have been planted with that planting which your heavenly Father planted. Grieve, therefore, that you have offended the Creator, whose law things heavenly and earthly, except you, keep with unwearied standing; and in so great a commonwealth of God you alone are the stranger, and care nothing for the decrees of the imperial majesty. Let a sharper sorrow also arise, that along with the Creator you have offended the Father as well — and that Father who furnishes you the courses of the stars, the fruitfulness of the earth, the abundance of fruits. Let a most bitter sorrow come too, to pass through your soul, 1053Athat you have despised so great a benefactor, who bore the sorrow of the cross that you should not bear the sorrows of hell any longer. So therefore, justice sprouting in the inner man, let the shoot break forth into a tree, and let the tongue speak what knowledge was speaking.

9.

Quartus itaque gradus est confessio oris. Haec pure facienda est: quia non est pars una peccatorum dicenda, et altera reticenda; neque levia confitenda, et gravia diffitenda. Nec alter accusandus et ipse excusandus, sed cum justo dicendum est: Non declines cor meum in verba malitiae ad excusandas excusationes in peccatis (Psal. CXL, 4). Haec enim sunt verba malitiae, et gravis malitiae, qua gravior vel pejor vix esse possit. Confitendum est et humiliter, ut idem sit in corde, quod sonabit in ore. Sunt enim 1053Bnonnulli, qui narrare in confessionibus solent, quae vel 717 argute litteratorio, et fortiter gladiatorio gessere conflictu, proh dolor! sub humilitatis pallio superbiam inducentes, et putantes se posse vitare oculos Judicis cuncta cernentis. Nonne utique reges Saul et David redarguti a prophetis Samuele et Nathan, dixerunt, Peccavi? et tamen Samuel audivit, Transtulit Dominus regnum a te, et dabit illud aemulo tuo (I Reg. XV, 28); et David, Transtulit Dominus peccatum tuum, et non morieris (II Reg. XII, 13). Quid est hoc, nisi quia quod Saul habuit in ore, non fuit in corde; et humilitas quae in ore David, exstitit in corde, etiam radiavit? Dicendum est et fideliter, ut in fide Ecclesiae credat sibi peccata dimitti, quia Cain et Judas pure et humiliter confessi 1053Csunt, sed nequaquam fideliter: quia alter eorum desperans ad laqueum cucurrit (Matth. XXVII); alter illud grande scelus evomuit, Major est iniquitas mea quam ut veniam merear (Gen. IV, 13). Mentiris, latro, quia major est pietas Dei quam quaevis iniquitas: quia miserationes ejus super omnia opera ejus.

9.

The fourth step, accordingly, is confession of the mouth. This must be made purely: because it is not the case that one part of one's sins should be told and another kept silent; nor light things confessed and grave ones denied. Nor is another to be accused and oneself excused, but with the just man it must be said: Incline not my heart to words of malice, to make excuses in sins (Psal. CXL, 4). For these are words of malice, and of grave malice, than which scarcely any could be graver or worse. It must be confessed humbly also, so that the same thing be in the heart which will sound in the mouth. For there are 1053Bsome who are accustomed to relate in their confessions what things they have done, whether 717 shrewdly in the conflict of letters or bravely in that of the sword — alas the grief! — bringing in pride under the cloak of humility, and thinking they can escape the eyes of the Judge who discerns all things. Did not the kings Saul and David, rebuked by the prophets Samuel and Nathan, say, I have sinned? And yet Samuel heard, The Lord has taken away the kingdom from thee, and will give it to thy rival (I Reg. XV, 28); and David, The Lord has taken away thy sin, and thou shalt not die (II Reg. XII, 13). What is this, except that what Saul had in his mouth was not in his heart; and the humility which was in David's mouth existed in his heart, and shone out as well? It must be said faithfully too, so that one believe in the faith of the Church that his sins are forgiven him, because Cain and Judas confessed purely and humbly, 1053Cbut by no means faithfully: since one of them, despairing, ran to the noose (Matth. XXVII); the other spewed out that great crime, My iniquity is greater than that I should deserve pardon (Gen. IV, 13). You lie, you robber, because the devotion of God is greater than any iniquity whatever: because his mercies are over all his works.

10.

Sequitur quintus gradus, maceratio carnis, quae jejuniis, vigiliis attenuanda est; ne nova prurigine ad voluptuosam revolet pravitatem. Haec autem facienda est licentiose; quia quod sine licentia fit, vanae gloriae deputabitur, non mercedi. Occulte, ut nesciat sinistra tua quid faciat dextera tua. Discrete, propter debilitatem corporis, et multimodas infirmitates. Plerosque enim vidimus in principio conversionis ita discretionis infregisse repagula, ut 1053Dlaudum solemniis inhabiles redderentur, et apparatu deinceps lautiori diuturnis foveri temporibus indigerent. Jam sic illuminatus procedat ad sextum, qui est correctio operis. Prohibe ergo linguam tuam a malo, operare quod bonum est, et sensuales motus tuos continentiae frenis infrena, et sic erit pax in terra carnis tuae. Argue malos pro persona quam habes: et te praesente justitiam non sinas sepeliri. Veni ad septimum gradum: et hic est perseverantia bonitatis. Et in hoc septimo vocabit te Dominus cum Moyse de medio caliginis (Exod. XXIV, 16): et in ipsam divinitatis substantiam interiores oculos infiges. Perseverantiam autem faciunt exspectatio praemiorum, poenarum recordatio, primitiae spiritus 1054Aet donum coeleste, de quibus loquitur Apostolus, quod nec expertus ignorat, nec inexpertus intelligit (Hebr. VI). Stultitia est enim illi, et non potest intelligere quae sunt spiritus Dei (I Cor. II, 14).

10.

There follows the fifth step, the mortifying of the flesh, which is to be thinned by fastings and vigils, lest with a new itch it fly back to voluptuous depravity. But this must be done by leave, since what is done without leave will be reckoned to vainglory, not to reward. Secretly, so that your left hand know not what your right hand does. Discreetly, on account of the weakness of the body and its manifold infirmities. For we have seen very many at the beginning of their conversion so break down the bars of discretion that 1053Dthey were rendered unfit for the solemnities of praise, and thereafter needed to be cherished for long periods with a more sumptuous provision. Now, so enlightened, let him proceed to the sixth, which is the correction of works. Restrain therefore your tongue from evil, work what is good, and bridle your sensual motions with the bridles of continence, and so there will be peace in the land of your flesh. Rebuke the wicked according to the person you bear: and in your presence do not suffer justice to be buried. Come to the seventh step: and this is perseverance in goodness. And in this seventh the Lord will call you with Moses out of the midst of the darkness (Exod. XXIV, 16): and you will fix your inner eyes upon the very substance of the Godhead. Now perseverance is made by the expectation of rewards, the recollection of punishments, the firstfruits of the spirit 1054Aand the heavenly gift, of which the Apostle speaks — which he who has experienced it does not fail to know, nor does he who has not experienced it understand (Hebr. VI). For it is foolishness to such a man, and he cannot understand the things that are of the Spirit of God (I Cor. II, 14).

11.

Audite et breviter quem oporteat ordinari ad dispensationem mysterii hujus. Oportet eum esse non minus litteratum quam religiosum, ut zelum Dei habeat, et secundum scientiam; cum indiscreta religio magis obesse soleat quam prodesse. Litteratum ideo, ut sciat quid injungat, cui parcat, quando parcere debeat, quam consolationem proferat de Scripturis. Religiosum vero idcirco, et ut puras manus levet ad Deum, et pro peccatoribus fiducialiter intercedat. Videat autem ne unquam de his quae sub signaculo confessionis accepit, aliquam faciat 1054Bmentionem, vel alicui loquenti consentiat. Caveat etiam ne vilescat in conspectu ejus, qui suam vilitatem ostendit: nec sit suspectus, nec de praeterita futuram vitam aestimet peccatoris. Quod si fortuito de his quae in confessione suscepit, causa coram eo fuerit ventilata, non tanquam conscius in partem declinet, sed quasi nescius judiciarium ordinem permittat exerceri. Habeat autem in voluntate compati, et liberare eum: et nitatur aliquid detrahere severitati, imitans dulcedinem Domini sui, de quo dicit sanctus: Quoniam fecisti judicium meum, et causam meam (Psal. IX, 5). Sed proh dolor! temporibus nostris, per universum mundum pestilentialis morbus excrevit, ut praeferantur ecclesiis, quos nec scientia, nec vita commendet, caeci 1054Cduces caecorum, et praecipitati et ad praecipitium perducentes.

11.

Hear also briefly what sort of man ought to be ordained to the dispensation of this mystery. He must be no less lettered than religious, that he may have the zeal of God, and that according to knowledge; since religion without discernment is wont to hinder rather than to profit. Lettered, therefore, that he may know what to enjoin, whom to spare, when he ought to spare, what consolation to bring forth from the Scriptures. But religious for this reason: both that he may lift pure hands to God, and that he may intercede with confidence for sinners. And let him see to it that he never make any 1054Bmention of those things which he received under the seal of confession, nor consent to anyone speaking of them. Let him beware also lest the man who has shown his own vileness become cheap in his sight; and let him not be suspicious, nor judge the sinner's future life from his past. But if by chance a case concerning those things which he received in confession be aired before him, let him not turn aside to one side as one who is privy to it, but as one not knowing let him permit the judicial order to be carried out. But let him have in his will to suffer with the man and to free him: and let him strive to take something away from severity, imitating the sweetness of his Lord, of whom the holy man says: For thou hast made my judgment and my cause (Psal. IX, 5). But alas the grief! In our times, through the whole world a pestilential disease has grown up, that men are set over the churches whom neither knowledge nor life commends — blind 1054Cleaders of the blind, both fallen headlong and leading others to the headlong fall.

12.

Audivimus de confitente, et de confessionem suscipiente: audiamus nunc quae impediant confessionem. Quantum colligere possum, quatuor praecipue occurrunt: pudor, timor, spes et desperatio. Nihil fortius ad expugnandum gratiam Dei quam pudor humanus; quia dum erubescimus confiteri quae commisimus, Deum minus quam homines reveremur. Haec est confusio adducens peccatum (Eccle. IV). Opponamus ei considerationem rationis, reverentiam intuentis Dei, comparationem majoris confusionis: ut enim confiteamur ratio monet, Deus cogit, qui vidit. Et si confundimur hic dicere, quid 1054Derit in illa magna confusione, ubi omnibus omnia patebunt? Timor plerosque opprimit, ne gravi poenitentia percellantur, et perferre non possint. Vere qui timet pruinam, irruet super eum nix (Job. VI, 16). Hic considerandum, quam gravis sit poena gehennae, quam longa, quam infructuosa: e contrario autem poenitentia praesens levis, brevis et fructuosa. Spes autem multos obruit, qui dum bona praesentium cupiunt, aperire conscientias suas nolunt; ne, si quales sint hominibus appareant, ad nulla praesentis vitae bona conscendant. Quibus inculcandum est quia bona hujus saeculi parva sunt, incerta et momentanea: et illa illius vitae maxima, et certissima et aeterna. Plurimos desperatio interficit, qui hoc solum metuunt, ne possint continere. Verum 1055Aest, quia peccator cum venerit in profundum malorum, contemnit (Prov. XVIII, 5). Hanc obterere debemus vigore confessionis, gratia humilitatis, et compassione illius cui aperimus conscientiam nostram. Sciendum autem quia alia est confessio quae peccatum plangit, alia quae laudem canit. Bonum animae ornamentum confessio, quae et peccatorem purgat, et justum reddit purgatiorem.

12.

We have heard concerning the one confessing, and concerning the one receiving the confession: let us now hear what things hinder confession. So far as I can gather, four chiefly present themselves: shame, fear, hope, and despair. Nothing is stronger to storm the grace of God than human shame; because while we blush to confess what we have committed, we revere God less than men. This is the confusion that brings on sin (Eccle. IV). Let us set against it the consideration of reason, reverence for God who looks on, the comparison of a greater confusion: for reason admonishes that we confess, and God, who has seen, compels it. And if we are confounded to speak here, what 1054Dwill it be in that great confusion, where all things shall lie open to all? Fear oppresses very many, lest they be struck with a grave penance and be unable to bear it through. Truly, upon him who fears the frost, the snow shall rush (Job. VI, 16). Here it must be considered how grave is the punishment of Gehenna, how long, how fruitless: but on the contrary, present penance is light, brief, and fruitful. But hope overwhelms many, who, while they desire the goods of things present, are unwilling to open their consciences; lest, if they appear to men as they are, they should climb to none of the goods of the present life. To whom it must be pressed home that the goods of this world are small, uncertain, and momentary: and those of that life greatest, most certain, and eternal. Despair kills very many, who fear this alone, that they may not be able to hold out. It is true 1055Athat the sinner, when he has come into the depth of evils, despises (Prov. XVIII, 5). This we ought to crush by the vigor of confession, by the grace of humility, and by the compassion of him to whom we open our conscience. But it must be known that one confession is that which bewails sin, and another that which sings praise. Confession is a good ornament of the soul, which both cleanses the sinner and renders the just man more cleansed.

13.

Sequitur: Qui credit in illum non confundetur. Credere Deo, est verbis ejus fidem adhibere. Credere Deum, est confiteri ubique esse. Credere in 1056ADeum, est omnem spem suam in illum dirigere. Deum et Deo credunt daemones: sed in illum non credunt, in quem qui credit non confundetur; quia spem suam non ponunt in illum. Ecce quantum potui, etsi non quantum volui, de confessionis disserui Sacramento. Sed sermonem in longum protraximus, finem loquendi pariter audiamus. Det nobis credere ad justitiam, ore confiteri ad salutem, et in se credere, Sponsus Ecclesiae, Filius Mariae, qui est Deus benedictus in saecula. Amen.

13.

There follows: He who believes in him shall not be confounded. To believe God is to give credence to his words. To believe that God is, is to confess that he is everywhere. To believe in 1056AGod is to direct all one's hope toward him. The demons believe that God is, and believe God: but they do not believe in him, in whom whoever believes shall not be confounded; because they do not place their hope in him. Behold, as much as I have been able, though not as much as I wished, I have discoursed concerning the Sacrament of confession. But we have drawn out the sermon at length; let us likewise hear the end of speaking. May he grant us to believe unto justice, to confess with the mouth unto salvation, and to believe in himself — the Bridegroom of the Church, the Son of Mary, who is God blessed forever. Amen.
