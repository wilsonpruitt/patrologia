## NOTICE.

[0623C]In that old collection of Lucca, of which I have treated more than once, there occurs, in an epitome of very many councils and of certain decretal letters, an extract *from the Letter of Pope Innocent from the council of Nicaea,* which letter no prudent man would doubt to be Pope Innocent I's, since the age of this codex, of at least nine hundred years, surpasses every other pontiff named Innocent. But what this letter of Innocent may be, from what codex it was copied, whether Innocent issued a single letter on the Nicene canons, or, as occasion was given, adduced and inculcated them in his various letters, and the author of this epitome then continued them into one series of canons, I plainly do not know. And although nothing new is presented in these, it is nevertheless useful to give this, such as it is, since this is the most ancient Latin edition of the Nicene canons. For the one which bears Dionysius Exiguus as its author is certainly later than Innocent I. And whether the one which is put forward in the collection of Isidore Mercator is older or more recent has not yet been investigated. [0623D] Finally, the one that is Rufinus's is of nearly the same antiquity as Innocent.

## ON THE LETTER OF POPE INNOCENT I FROM THE COUNCIL OF NICAEA.



## I.

He who has cut himself off, let him not enter the clergy.

## II.

A Gentile who has been converted and baptized, let him not be promoted in the clergy tacitly.

## III.

Let a bishop or the other clerics not dwell with women who are strangers.

## IV.

Let a bishop be ordained by all the bishops, or by three with the consent of the metropolitan.

## V.

[0624C] Let a bishop not receive the cleric of another bishop, or one excommunicated. Let councils be held twice in the year on account of the complaint of divers persons.

## VI.

Let the Alexandrian and the Roman popes have the primacy.

## VII.

With the consent of very many bishops, especially with the consent of the metropolitan, let presbyters be chosen (perhaps, however, *bishops* was written).

## VIII.

Let the bishop of Jerusalem have a primacy, saving the privilege of the metropolitan.

## IX.

[0624D] Cathari, that is, Novatians, converted clerics, let them be received in the clergy. But let a bishop be received in the place of a presbyter.

## X.

In one city let there not be two bishops.

## XI.

If he has been detected after ordination to the priesthood, let him be deposed; in like manner it is with the lapsed.

## XII.

Let the lapsed without torments do penance seven years.

## XIII.

If after military service he has been converted, and has served as a soldier again, let him do penance XIII years.

## XIV.

[0625A] If in necessity he has received communion, has survived, let him keep his penance.

## XV.

Catechumens, if they have lapsed, let them do penance three years.

## XVI.

Let a bishop, or the other orders, not depart from city to city.

## XVII.

Let a wandering cleric not be received into communion by another.

## XVIII.

Let no one promote another's cleric in honor.

## XIX.

[0625B] Let clerics give neither usury, nor *renovo* ( *sic* ).

## XX.

Let deacons not be preferred to presbyters, or sit, nor let them hand the Eucharist to presbyters; but when those are absent, let them distribute it.

## XXI.

*Paulianeste* let them be baptized ( *sic* ).

## XXII.

Deaconesses let them be held among laywomen ( *sic* ).

## XXIII.

Let Easter be kept on one day by the bishops.

## DISSERTATION *On the following Epitome of the letter of Innocent.*

Although I have no new letters of Innocent I to bring forth, yet I think it not beside the point that there should be communicated to my readers [0625C] the things which our breviary of the canons in the Lucca manuscript codex of nine hundred years presents. In it there are extant canons drawn from several letters of Innocent and reduced to an epitome. But in the epitome which is drawn from the letter to Exsuperius, canon 3 is lacking, concerning those who after baptism, holding magistracy, have pronounced sentence in a capital case. For this omission I can devise no other reason, except that the Pontiff judges that nothing is to be decreed in that matter. Canon 4, which follows, which is 3 in our epitome, is badly expressed; for the married are not deprived of communion if they have committed adultery, but the innocent party is separated from the adulterer. The rest agrees with the letter. The addition about the *Lamentations of Jeremiah* is not to be neglected, which is supplied by our manuscript alone and is not read in the printed editions.

After this epitome there follows another letter of Innocent, to the bishops of Toledo. Some things in it are plainly worthy of remark. For first, in our [0625D] breviary, just as in all the ancient codices, on the witness of Hardouin, is read *Tolosanos, not Toletanos.*

There follows a synopsis of the letter of Innocent to Victricius (our codex has *Victorium*), in which all is plain and agrees with the printed editions, if you except one, the second part of canon 3, in which the printed letter indeed rules that the cases of churchmen are to be heard each in their own provinces, *without* prejudice, however, to appeal *to the Roman* church, or unless the gravity of the case refer the judgment *to the apostolic see*. So the printed editions. But the manuscript codex not so, but: *Let the Roman clerics not dare to plead their cases to other bishops.* Assuredly these two are far apart from one another, so that they seem drawn from one codex and another. But that the things read in the printed editions are Pope Innocent I's no one will rightly doubt, since they are cited as from Innocent by Nicholas I in a twin letter in the Roman council VII, to be read in Labbe, vol. X. But [0626A] since Nicholas neither writes that he took them from the letter to Victricius, nor is our abridger always so happy in rendering the sense of the canons, one or the other of two things necessarily follows: either that canon, as in the printed editions, has been badly stitched on from some other letter of Innocent to this one of his to Victricius, as it is agreed has happened to other letters of the ancient Pontiffs; or our anonymous author has by no means grasped the Pontiff's mind.

In the epitome of canon 8 it is proclaimed that monks are not to be admitted into the clergy; but this Innocent does not command, but warns that, promoted to a higher grade, they should not cast off the modesty and the other virtues which they had cultivated in the monastery. Wherefore the drowsiness of our abridger is to be rebuked.

The synopsis of the letter of Innocent to Decentius is plainly barbarous, but its first chapter expresses a certain old liturgical rite which the printed editions do not touch, although it agrees in sense with the printed editions. The Pontiff forbids in canon 1 that the peace be given before the end. But [0626B] our anonymous author expressed this thus: *The order of the mass: to give the peace after the fourth oration.* By the name of the fourth oration I judge that here is designated that which the old Roman order in Mabillon, *Museum Italicum*, vol. II, calls the *oration at the completion*. For in the mass the first oration was that which was read before the *Apostle*, that is, before the epistle. The second followed, *over the offerings*. Then the Lord's prayer was in the third place; the fourth, finally, was held to be the *oration at the completion*. Behold, then, our abridger reconciled with Innocent.

Why the fifth canon of this letter has been cut into two, especially since both of them come back to one and the same thing, I would assign no other reason than the drowsiness of our abridger. The rest is plain.

Last of all is read the epitome of the letters of Innocent *to Aurelius* (the bishop of Carthage, certainly). There is extant in the Labbe collection, num. 12, a letter of Innocent to Aurelius, to which the things contained in this synopsis can be referred.

## I. FROM THE LETTER OF INNOCENT TO EXSUPERIUS.

[0626C]

## I.

Let an incontinent presbyter and deacon be deposed.

## II.

Those who are continent, let them receive the viaticum at every time, and communion through mercy.

## III.

The married, if they have committed adultery, let them be excommunicated.

## IV.

It is lawful to accuse a homicide.

## V.

He who has put away his wife and married another, let him be excommunicated; likewise the woman also.

## VI.

[0626D] The books which are to be received:

Of Moses, books V: Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Deuteronomy; Jesus Nave I; Judges I; Kings IV; Ruth I; of the Prophets XVI; of Solomon V; the Psalter I; Job I; Tobias I; Esther I; Judith I; of the Maccabees II; of Esdras II; *Chenib*, that is, the Lamentations of Jeremiah. Paralipomenon II; of the New Testament; Gospels IV; of Paul the apostle XIV; of John IV; of Peter II; of James I; the Acts of the Apostles, the Apocalypse of John.

## II. FROM THE LETTER OF INNOCENT TO ALL THE BISHOPS IN TOLOSA.

Let a bishop not ordain bishops in another's church. [0627A] He will draw away the Spaniards from secular military service, not ordaining them swiftly to the priesthood, nor curials, who ought not to be clerics. Those blame the Spaniards who have been engaged in forensic practice, or from some military service, or from the curials, so that they come to the priesthood or to the lesser offices.

## III. OF INNOCENT TO VICTORIUS THE BISHOP.



## I.

Without the consent of the metropolitan let a bishop not be ordained.

## II.

He who has had the belt of secular military service, let him not come to the office of the clerical state.

## III.

[0627B] Among clerics let the bishop hear. Let the Roman clerics not dare to plead their cases to other bishops.

## IV.

Let a cleric not take a widowed woman as wife.

## V.

Let no one take up another's cleric.

## VI.

Novatians or Montenses, let them be taken up through the imposition of the hand. Those of ours who have gone over to them, and are called back, let them do penance long.

## VII.

Let deacons not have intercourse with a wife.

## VIII.

Let a monk not come into the clergy, nor take a wife.

## IX.

[0627C] Let curials not be ordained clerics.

## X.

A veiled woman, if she has gone astray, let her not receive penance.

## XI.

Women consecrated to the Lord, not veiled, if they have married, let them do penance.

## IV. OF INNOCENT TO THE BISHOP ( *sic* ).



## I.

Let no bigamous cleric be ordained.

## II.

He who before baptism had a wife, and after baptism [0627D] has married another, is a bigamist.

## V. OF INNOCENT TO DECENTIUS.

[0628A]

## I.

The order of the mass: to give the peace after the fourth oration.

## II.

Let the names be recited after the commendation of the offerers.

## III.

Let presbyters not seal infants, except bishops.

## IV.

Let there be fasting on every sabbath.

## V.

Let presbyters hold mass in the city when the bishop is present.



## VI.

[0628B] Without the bishop's command let a presbyter hold masses, in the city when the bishop is present.

## VII.

Without the bishop's command let others also lay a hand on one possessed.

## VIII.

Let all penitents be reconciled on the fifth feria of Easter.

## IX.

Let it be lawful for a presbyter to visit the sick, and to anoint chrism; a sick penitent let it not be lawful to anoint with chrism.

## VI. OF INNOCENT TO AURELIUS THE BISHOP.

[0628C]

Let a hand not be laid easily on a Bishop; let him not be ordained from a layman.

## OBSERVATION OF PHILIPPE LABBE, S. J.

The following canons, sent long ago by some Roman synod to the bishops of Gaul, the Reverend Father Sirmond first brought to light from several manuscript codices in the appendix to volume I of the councils of Gaul; and the Royal collector threw the same, out of their place, into the end of the XXXVII volume, after the indices, with the treatise on the ancient promotions of bishops. We, however, following the not improbable conjecture of the same most illustrious editor, have placed them under the letters of Innocent the first, since neither the form of the decrees nor that of the writing is foreign to them. But in what year they were issued is plainly not established, although, as everyone [0628D] sees, they bear on their face the greatest antiquity.
