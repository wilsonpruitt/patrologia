## Annals



## 96.

[0987B]The passion of Saint Dionysius. Saint Sanctinus, making his way to Rome with Antoninus after the martyrdom of blessed Dionysius, by that same man's command, was instructed by the visitation of an angel and turned aside to the city of the Clavi, which is called [0987C] Verdun; preaching the word of God to the citizens, he converted many, and, building and consecrating a church in honor of blessed Peter, he was chosen first bishop by those very Christians whom he had converted.

## 360.

In these times, after the devastation by the Huns, the churches flourished again, and Pulchronius, a disciple of Lupus bishop of Troyes, is set over the church of Verdun as its 5th bishop.

## 503.

The passing of Saint Firminus, bishop of Verdun. In whose place blessed Vitonus is appointed by King Clovis, and is enthroned in the pontifical see.

## 518.

The annunciation of Saint Agericus.

## 528.

The passing of blessed Vitonus, bishop of Verdun.

## 554.

The ordination of Saint Agericus to the episcopate.

## 602.

Saint Agericus died; Caramerus succeeds. [0987D]

## 688.

Pepin, son of Ansegisus, began to reign. From whose times blessed Madelveus continued down to the times of Charlemagne.

This Pepin, duke and illustrious prince of the Franks, together with his wife, the illustrious matron Plectrude, gave to the church of Saint Vitonus the estate of Parida with its appurtenances, together with the spacious wood which is called the Nauca of Saint Vitonus, in the seventh year of King Childebert.

## 758.

[0988B]Saint Madelveus, bishop of Verdun, died.

## 771.

Peter is installed as bishop of Verdun.

## 795.

Peter, bishop of Verdun, died; [0988C] Austrannus succeeds.

## 800.

Austrannus, bishop of Verdun, died; Herilandus succeeds.

## 847.

Hildinus, bishop of Verdun, died; Hatto succeeds.

## 915.

The church of Saint Vitonus of Verdun was burned, [sic: *liba*]: the memorials of the saints.

## 920.

Dado, bishop of Verdun, died; Bernoynus succeeds.

## 941.

Bernoinus, bishop of Verdun, died; Berengar succeeds, a kinsman of the emperor Otto.

## 952.

The clerics are driven out of the monastery of Saint Vitonus, and monks are put in their place under Abbot Hubert. [0988D]

## 958.

Berengar, bishop of Verdun, died; Wicfrid succeeds, who founded the church of Saint Paul of Verdun.

## 986.

Wicfrid, bishop of Verdun, died; Adalbero succeeds.

## 990.

Adalbero, bishop of Verdun, died; Heymo succeeds, who founded the monastery of the nuns of Saint Maurus. [0989A]

## 1005.

Fingenius, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died: Richard succeeds.

## 1025.

Heymo, bishop of Verdun, died; Raymbert succeeds, who founded the monastery of Saint Agericus.

## 1039.

Raymbert, bishop of Verdun, died; Richard succeeds.

## 1046.

Richard, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; Waleran succeeds. Richard, bishop of Verdun, died; Theoderic succeeds.

## 1048.

The city of Verdun is taken by Duke Godfrey and by Baldwin, is plundered, and is burned together with the monastery of Saint Mary.

## 1049.

Blessed Odilo, abbot of Cluny, died. The church of blessed Mary Magdalene was dedicated by [0989B] Pope Leo IX.

## 1056.

The emperor Henry died; Henry the boy succeeds.

## 1060.

Abbot Waleran died; Grimold succeeds.

## 1064.

The chapel of Saint Bartholomew, with its forecourt, is dedicated. And the star which is called a comet appeared; in which year also Harold, king of the English, is slain, William succeeding him.

## 1069.

Duke Godfrey, son of Duke Gozelo, died; his son Godfrey, called the Hunchback, succeeds.

## 1071.

Ermenfrid died, canon and builder of the church of blessed Mary Magdalene.

## 1075.

Duke Rudolf invaded Saxony. [0989C]

## 1076.

Godfrey the younger, duke, died; Godfrey who is called of Bouillon succeeds.

## 1077.

Rudolf succeeds Abbot Grimold. A hard frost, continuous for 4 months.

## 1078.

Abbot Grimold died.

## 1079.

A battle in Saxony, where Duke Rudolf is slain by Henry the younger.

## 1080.

The body of the lord bishop Richard was translated, and was buried before the choir in the new basilica of Saint Mary.

## 1082.

A great strife between the kingdom and the priesthood. On account of which the monks were expelled from the monastery of Saint Vitonus for seven years, namely until after the death of Theoderic, bishop of Verdun

## 1086.

A battle at Sathanacum. [0989D]

## 1087.

William, duke of the Normans and king of the English, died. Pope Victor. The translation of Saint Nicholas to Bari.

## 1088.

Theoderic, bishop of Verdun, died; Richer succeeds, and Pope Urban.

## 1091.

Herimann, bishop of Metz, died.

## 1095.

The consecration of Bishop Richer. A council of the bishops of almost the whole world, except the Lotharingians, the Germans, and the Bavarians, was assembled at Clermont in Auvergne.

## 1097.

The star which is called a comet appeared.

## 1098.

Pope Urban died; Pope Paschal succeeds. [0990A]

## 1099.

Abbot Rudolf died; Laurence succeeds. Jerusalem is taken by Duke Godfrey of Bouillon and by the Christians.

## 1106.

The emperor Henry the father having died, Henry succeeds.

## 1107.

Bishop Richer died; whom Richard, the bishop-elect, succeeds.

## 1111.

The monastery of Saint Vitonus deserted, the monks having been expelled by Bishop Richard. Henry, son of Henry the said king, coming to Rome as though to give satisfaction to Pope Paschal, seized him with the cardinals within the church of blessed Peter by treachery, and thrust him into a filthy place.

## 1114.

The monks of Saint Vitonus return to the monastery after the death of Bishop Richard. The [0990B] bishopric was vacant for three and a half years.

## 1116.

Henry succeeds as bishop of Verdun.

## 1117.

A battle before Verdun of the counts Raynald and William. Pope Paschal died; Gelasius succeeded.

## 1118.

Baldwin, king of Jerusalem, died.

## 1119.

Pope Gelasius died; Calixtus the Second succeeds.

## 1120.

The city of Verdun is taken by Count Raynald, and the greatest part of the city, with the monasteries of Saint Saviour, of Saint Peter, and of Saint Agericus, is burned and despoiled.

## 1122.

The citizens of Verdun handed over this city by treachery to Count Henry of Grandpré; and he, having held it for some time, committed many [0990C] evils there.

## 1124.

Concord was made between the kingdom and the priesthood.

## 1125.

Pope Calixtus died; Honorius succeeds. Henry the Fifth, emperor, died; Lothar, duke of Saxony, succeeds.

## 1129.

Henry gave back the bishopric. Ursio, abbot of Saint Denis of Reims, is elected.

## 1130.

Pope Honorius died; Innocent succeeds.

## 1131.

Ursio renounced the bishopric, and Adalbero the archdeacon is elected and is consecrated at Paris on holy Easter day by Pope Innocent.

## 1132.

The tower of Verdun is taken and overthrown.

## 1138.

The emperor Lothar died; whom [0990D] Conrad succeeds.

## 1139.

Laurence, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; Segard succeeded.

## 1142.

Abbot Segard died; whom Cono succeeded.

## 1143.

Pope Innocent died; Celestine succeeds.

## 1144.

Pope Celestine died; Lucius succeeded.

## 1145.

Pope Lucius died; Eugenius the Third succeeds.

## 1147.

Pope Eugenius, on the day of his feast, transferred the body of the most blessed Father Vitonus into a new bier, [0991A] and on the day of the feast of Saint Martin dedicated the church of blessed Mary.

## 1150.

Pope Eugenius died; Anastasius succeeds, and afterward Adrian. The monastery of Saint Mary Magdalene and the greatest part of the city were burned.

## 1152.

Conrad, king of the Romans, died; Frederick, duke of Alsace, succeeds.

## 1153.

A battle before Mouzon, where the men of Metz perished. Ascalon taken by the Christians. Frederick, king of the Lotharingians, set out for Rome to enlarge the empire.

## 1156.

The ordination of Bishop Albert, two years before the death of Adalbero.

## 1158.

Adalbero, bishop of Verdun, died.

## 1161.

Pope Adrian died; Alexander [0991B] III succeeds.

## 1163.

Albert, bishop of Verdun and monk of Saint Vitonus, died; Richard called the Child succeeds.

## 1171.

Richard, bishop-elect of Verdun, died; Arnold succeeds. Saint Thomas, bishop of Canterbury, suffered.

## 1178.

Cono, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died, who ordered the stone towers and the place called the Treasury to be made, and the bier of Saint Vitonus to be completed. Whom Richer succeeds.

## 1179.

Richer is brought to an end by his own free will; Allestannus the younger succeeds, and in that same year he died; whom Allestannus the elder succeeded. [0991C]

## 1181.

Arnold, bishop of Verdun, died, he struck by an arrow in the head before the castle of Sainte-Menehould; Henry succeeds.

## 1182.

Pope Alexander died; Pope Lucius succeeds.

## 1183.

Allestannus the elder renounced the abbacy; Thomas, abbot of Mureau, is put in his place.

## 1186.

Pope Lucius died; Urban succeeds. Henry renounced the bishopric, and Albert is elected.

## 1187.

Abbot Thomas renounced; Hugh succeeds. Pope Urban died, and Gregory succeeded: who died in the same year; Clement succeeds.

## 1189.

The emperor Frederick died; his son [0991D] Henry succeeds.

## 1190.

Pope Clement died; Celestine succeeds.

## 1194.

A famine so severe that grain was sold at the price of 4 pounds.

## 1197.

*Pope Celestine died*; *Innocent the Third* succeeds. Abbot Hugh gave up the prelacy; whom Stephen succeeds; who, struck with paralysis, renounced the abbacy in the same year. Ludwig is chosen abbot, [0992A] the brother of Albert, bishop of Verdun.-- *The emperor Henry died*; to whom *succeeds* Philip.

## 1200.

Constantinople was taken by the Franks and by the Venetians, and they appoint Baldwin, count of Flanders, emperor.

## 1208.

*Philip*, king, not yet emperor, *was slain* by the palatine . . . . . . . ; Otto the *count* succeeds . . . . . i; what after he *was consecrated*, ruling by the man*dates of Pope* Innocent, excommunicated . . . . . . dis, he *was deposed* from the roy*al* dignity, and Frede*rick, duke of Ap*ulia, *was raised* to the empire. A great sedition arose at Verdun, the citizens rising against the clerics, the clerics against the citizens, and perpetrating many evils; at the last Bishop Albert died, slain by a lance; whom Robert the precentor succeeds. [0992B]

## 1216.

A general *council was convoked* at Rome, and there the emperor Frederick *was signed* with the cross. Pope Innocent died; Honorius succeeds.

## 1217.

Robert, bishop of Verdun, died; John of Aspremont succeeds, who founded the monastery of Saint Nicholas in the Meadow. A fire of this city of Verdun took place, from the steps of Saint Mary and from the gate of Ancelin's quarter as far as the bridge of Brachiolum, generally from wall to wall.

## 1222.

Saint Dominic died, the founder of the Order of Preachers.



## 1224.

John, bishop of Verdun, renounced the bishopric, passing over to the bishopric of Metz; whom Rudolf of Torte succeeds, cantor of [0992C] Lyons. Ludwig, abbot of Saint Vitonus, ordered a silver table to be made before the high altar. Pope Gregory admonished the emperor Frederick that he should set out for Jerusalem with his army, which had long since been signed with the cross, to destroy the enemies of the Christian faith.

## 1226.

*Honorius IX, pope, died*; *Gregory succeeds.*

1227 . . . . . a fight between Rudolf, *bishop* of Verdun, and the ci*tizens* . . . . . ii.

## 1232.

Saint Dominic, founder of the Friars Preachers, canonized.

## 1237.

On the 8th of the Ides of March, Abbot Ludwig, worn out with old age, resigned the abbacy — he who had begun the costly work of the monastery [0992D] — and died in the following year. Whom William, abbot of Saint-Mansuy, succeeds by election, formerly a monk of Saint Vitonus; who in that same costly work, in the buildings of the houses and of the chapel, in the shrines of the saints, in bells of the best and largest sort, and in the acquisition of lands, was found most faithful, just as his predecessor also was.

## 1241.

Pope Gregory died; Celestine succeeds, who sat 18 days; the see is vacant for three years. [0993A]

## 1243.

Innocent succeeds in the papacy.

## 1245.

Rudolf, bishop of Verdun, died; Guy of Trainel succeeded; who died in the same year; whom Guy of Mello succeeds. A great strife arose between him and the citizens of Verdun; peace having been made, in the same year he passed over to the bishopric of Auxerre; whom John of Aix succeeds.

## 1253.

Pope Innocent died; Alexander succeeds. John of Aix, bishop-elect of Verdun, a deacon, died; whom, by the provision of the lord pope, James, archdeacon of Laon, succeeds, a Frenchman by birth, from the city of Troyes.

## 1256.

James, bishop of Verdun, was translated by the pope's command to the patriarchate of Jerusalem; whom Robert of Milan succeeds. [0993B]

## 1259.

Abbot William died. The see was vacant for eight years, while a suit lasted between two men elected, namely Dominic the provost and Rudolf, prior of Mont-Saint-Martin, who nevertheless prevailed; and on this account the costly work of the monastery ceased, many attacks and damages having been done to this church by him. Pope Alexander died; the aforesaid James, bishop of Verdun and patriarch of Jerusalem, succeeded, called Pope Urban IV, who instituted the solemnity of the most holy Sacrament.

## 1264.

Pope Urban died; Clement succeeds.

## 1265.

Charles, count of Anjou, brother of Louis king of France, leader of the army of the Christians, overcame Manfred, the enemy of all Christendom. [0993C]

## 1267.

Clement died; Gregory succeeds.

## 1270.

Saint Louis, king of the Franks, died.

## 1271.

Robert II, bishop of Verdun, died; Ulric of Sathanacum succeeded, provost of blessed Mary Magdalene.

## 1272.

Pope Gregory [died]; Innocent succeeds.

## 1273.

Ulric, bishop of Verdun, died; after him the see was vacant for three years.

## 1276.

Pope Innocent [died]; Pope Adrian [succeeds], and then John. And in the bishopric there succeeds Gerard of Grandson.

## 1277.

Pope John died; Nicholas succeeds.

## 1278.

Gerard of Grandson, bishop of Verdun, died; his brother Henry of [0993D] Grandson succeeds.

## 1281.

Abbot Rudolf died; John succeeds.

## 1286.

Abbot John died; Thierry succeeds. Henry of Grandson, bishop of Verdun, died; it was vacant for 4 years.

## 1290.

James of Revigny, most general of clerics, succeeds in the bishopric of Verdun. [0994A]

## 1292.

Abbot Thierry died; Gobert the almoner is elected; but Philip Dorne, prior of Corbigny.

## 1296.

James II, bishop, died in the Roman curia, who had gone thither on account of the discord which he had with the citizens of Verdun. There succeeds John of Richericourt, called of Aspremont [sic: Ill u s .]

## 1297.

Abbot Philip died; Hugh of Reims succeeds.

## 1302.

John of Richericourt, bishop of Verdun, died; Thomas of Aubemont succeeds.

## 1303.

Hugh, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; Baudoc of Failly succeeds, prior of Munau.

## 1305.

Abbot Baudoc died; Nicholas [0994B] Maisel the cellarer succeeds. Thomas, bishop of Verdun, died; Nicholas of Neuville-sur-Orne succeeded.

## 1312.

Nicholas, bishop of Verdun, resigned the burden of his bishopric; whom Henry of Aspremont succeeds.

## 1314.

Henry of Luxembourg, [sic: *imperarator*], died, who at the receiving of the Eucharist and in the administration of the blood of Christ was poisoned in Italy, poison having been put into the chalice.

## 1316.

Nicholas, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; whom Simon of Nancy succeeds, prior of Flavigny. A great famine and a very great mortality of men.

## 1318.

Simon of Nancy, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; whom Theobald of Bazeilles succeeds, prior of [0994C] Munau.

## 1319.

Theobald, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; his kinsman Erard of Bazeilles succeeds; who wiped out the debts and pledgings of his predecessors.

## 1325.

The archdeacon of Trier, the king of Bohemia, the count of Bar, and the duke of Lorraine with their following were three times in the year before Metz. On this account Patries was wholly burned

## 1332.

There were thunderings and lightnings on the morrow of the Lord's Nativity. The greater men of the city of Verdun were banished by the community of the city.

## 1336.

The citizens of Verdun burned the vill of Buxey, and as they were returning, at the bridge of Way many were slain and wounded by the men of Bar, [0994D] and the bishopric of Verdun by treachery.

## 1340.

Erard, abbot of Saint Vitonus, ordered the two larger middle bells to be made.

## 1346.

The king of the English near Crécy overcame the king of the Franks, and there fell the king of Bohemia, the duke of Lorraine, and many other princes.

## 1349.

Henry, bishop of Verdun, died; [0995A] Henry of Germiny is elected. He was not confirmed. Erard, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; Bastiavus, prior of Chaudefontaine, is elected; but Raymond Dachic obtained it by favor of the apostolic see.

## 1353.

Gerard of Vaudenay succeeds Abbot Raymond by favor of the apostolic see. Who squandered the goods of the monastery of Mary, encumbered its rents and revenues, pawned letters and charters of the utmost necessity, impoverished its men and subjects and despoiled them of their goods, diminished its revenue by a third part, and damaged it in other things to the amount of 26 thousand francs and more, and reduced the monastery itself to such poverty that scarcely twelve times in the year were matins sung in it. Who all things, may God spare him and forgive. [0995B]

## 1381.

Gerard of Vaudenay, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; whom John of Tric succeeds, prior of Flavigny.

## 1382.

John of Tric, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; Henry of Passavant, called le Champenoy, succeeds, prior of Chaudefontaine, who greatly reduced the pledgings and debts of his predecessor.

## 1391.

Henry, a good governor, died as abbot; Reginald Pallardelli succeeds by favor of the apostolic see, a monk of Saint Michael.

## 1403.

Liébaud of Cusance, bishop of Verdun, died; John of Sarrebonne, son of the lord John of Commercy, knight, succeeds; who suffered many troubles from robbers and from some of the vassals of the bishopric of Verdun, inflicted upon him wickedly [0995C] and without cause.

## 1413.

A fire, on the vigil of the feast of Saint Vitonus, left unattended in the southern bell tower, melted the two heavier bells; which Abbot Reginald restored in the following year.

## 1417.

Abbot Reginald died; his nephew succeeds. Stephen of Bourges, almoner of Saint Vitonus, a most faithful ruler, [died].

## 1420.

John of Sarrebonne was translated to the bishopric of Châlons by an exchange made between him and Louis of Bar, cardinal, bishop [sic: *Portunensem*], duke of Bar, marquis of Pont, lord of Cassel, and administrator of the bishopric of Verdun. [0995D]

## 1430.

Abbot Stephen, the lesser bell of Abbot Reginald having been broken up, made a greater one. Louis of Bar, cardinal and bishop of Verdun, died; Louis of Haraucourt, cantor of Verdun, succeeds.

## 1431.

Stephen, abbot of Saint Vitonus, out of that magnificent [0996A] and costly old work of the church of Saint Vitonus, begun by the abbots Ludwig and William and brought to utter ruin, lacking a roof, taking the better stones, began another new one, and had it completed as far as the southern portal of the common entrance on either side, with its [sic: *verevis*], its [sic: *volturis*], and a fitting covering.

## 1436.

The body of Abbot Ludwig was translated from the old work into the new, under the altar of Saint Mary at the head.

## 1437.

The 4 first altars of the new work were dedicated on the 6th of the Kalends of June. Louis, bishop of Verdun, obtaining the bishopric of Toul, William, abbot of Saint-Thierry near Reims, succeeds by apostolic authority in the bishopric of Verdun [0996B]

## 1438.

A severe famine.

## 1448.

Abbot Stephen bought 4 copper columns.

## 1449.

By an exchange William, bishop of Verdun, passed over to the bishopric of Toul. And Louis, bishop of Toul, returned to Verdun, which he ruled well.

## 1451.

A mortality of the epidemic at Verdun, of 2500 men.

## 1452.

Stephen, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died. John of Arency the chamberlain is elected; but, 15 months having passed, he is cast out by the apostolic see at the instance of the lord William Hugh, cardinal, to whom the administration is granted; after nine months Anthony of Serrières, prior of Flavigny, extracted it from him, giving an annual pension of 400 gold ducats. [0996C]

## 1454.

The work of the new church of Saint Vitonus ceased.

## 1455.

The same lord William, cardinal, died, and his pension with him.

## 1456.

Louis, bishop of Verdun, died; William of Haraucourt, his kinsman, provost of Mont-faucon, succeeds.

## 1461.

Charles, king of the Franks, died; his son Louis succeeds. Abbot Anthony, the greater bell of Abbot Stephen having been broken up, restored it. And he bought two silken copes woven with gold and silver. John of Arency died, once elected of Saint Vitonus, chamberlain and prior of Chaudefontaine.



## 1463.

The lectern was renewed, and the whole burial place [0996D] of eight bishops of Verdun resting in the cemetery of this monastery of Saint Vitonus, formerly and of old before the high altar.

## 1475.

Anthony, abbot of this place, died.

## 1476.

The death of Charles of Burgundy at Nancy.

## 1481.

Abbot Matthew died; Gerard succeeds.
