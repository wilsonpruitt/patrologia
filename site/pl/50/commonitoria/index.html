Vincent of Lérins

Vincentius Lirinensis

d. before 450

Monk of the island monastery of Lérins, off the coast of Provence, and one of the sharpest Latin minds of the semi-Pelagian milieu of southern Gaul. Around 434, three years after the Council of Ephesus, he composed the Commonitorium under the pen-name Peregrinus, 'the Pilgrim' — a handbook for telling catholic truth from heresy by the test of what has been believed everywhere, always, and by all. Migne prints the surviving first book together with the recapitulation that is all that remains of the lost second.

S. VINCENTII LIRINENSIS COMMONITORIUM PRIMUM.(Ex editione Baluziana.)

637CINCIPIT TRACTATUS Peregrini pro Catholicae fidei antiquitate et universitate adversus prophanas omnium Haereticorum novitates.

THE FIRST COMMONITORY OF ST. VINCENT OF LÉRINS.(Ex editione Baluziana.)

637CHERE BEGINS THE TREATISE of Peregrinus, for the antiquity and universality of the Catholic faith against the profane novelties of all heretics.

I.

DICENTE Scriptura et monente, Interroga patres tuos, et dicent tibi; seniores tuos, et adnuntiabunt tibi, Deut. 32, et item, Verbis sapientium adcommoda aurem tuam, Eccli. 6, et item, Fili mi, hos sermones ne obliviscaris, mea autem verba custodiat cor tuum, Prov. 3, videtur mihi minimo omnium servorum Dei Peregrino 638Cquod res non minimae utilitatis, Domino adjuvante, futura sit, si ea quae fideliter a sanctis patribus accepi litteris comprehendam, infirmitati certe propriae pernecessaria, quippe cum adsit in promptu unde imbecillitas memoriae meae adsidua lectione reparetur. Ad quod me negotium non solum fructus operis, sed etiam consideratio temporis et opportunitas loci adhortatur. Tempus: propterea quod cum ab eo omnia humana rapiantur, et nos ex eo aliquid invicem rapere 639debemus quod in vitam proficiat aeternam; praesertim cum et appropinquantis divini Judicii terribilis quaedam exspectatio augeri efflagitet studia Religionis, et novorum Haereticorum fraudulentia multum curae et attentionis indicat. Locus autem, quod urbium frequentiam turbasque vitantes, remotioris villulae et in ea secretum monasterii incolamus habitaculum, ubi absque magna distractione fieri possit illud quod canitur in psalmo: Vacate, inquit, et videte quoniam ego sum Dominus, Psal. 45. Sed et propositi nostri ratio in id convenit; quippe qui cum aliquandiu variis ac tristibus secularis militiae turbinibus volveremur, tandem nos in portum Religionis, cunctis semper fidissimum, Christo adspirante condidimus; ut ibi depositis vanitatis ac superbiae flatibus, Christianae humilitatis sacrificio placantes Deum, non solum praesentis vitae naufragia, sed etiam futuri seculi incendia vitare possimus. Sed jam in nomine Domini quod instat adgrediar, ut scilicet a majoribus tradita et apud nos deposita describam, relatoris fide potius quam auctoris praesumptione; hac tamen scribendi lege servata, ut nequaquam omnia, sed tantum necessaria quaeque perstringam, neque id ornato et exacto sed facili communique sermone, ut pleraque significata potius quam explicata videantur. Scribant ii laute et accurate qui ad hoc munus vel ingenii fiducia vel officii ratione ducuntur. Me vero sublevandae recordationis vel potius oblivionis meae gratia Commonitorium mihimet parasse suffecerit: quod tamen paulatim, recolendo quae didici, emendare et implere quotidie, Domino praestante, conabor. Atque hoc ipsum idcirco praemonui, ut si forte elapsum nobis, in manus sanctorum devenerit, nihil in eo temere reprehendant, quod adhuc videant promissa emendatione limandum.

I.

Since Scripture says and admonishes, Ask thy fathers, and they will tell thee; thy elders, and they will declare to thee, Deut. 32, and again, Incline thine ear to the words of the wise, Eccli. 6, and again, My son, forget not these words, and let thy heart keep my words, Prov. 3, it seems to me, Peregrinus, the least of all the servants of God, 638Cthat a matter of no small usefulness will result, the Lord helping, if I set down in writing those things which I have faithfully received from the holy fathers — a thing surely most necessary to my own weakness, since I would then have ready at hand that by which the frailty of my memory might be repaired by constant reading. To which task not only the fruit of the work, but also the consideration of the time and the opportunity of the place, urges me on. The time: because, since all human things are snatched away by it, we too ought in turn to snatch from it something that may profit unto 639life eternal; especially since a certain terrible expectation of the approaching divine Judgment demands that the zeal of Religion be increased, and the fraudulence of the new heretics gives sign of much need of care and attention. The place, moreover: because, shunning the throng and crowds of the cities, we dwell in the retreat of a rather remote little farmstead, and in it the secluded lodging of a monastery, where, without great distraction, that may come to pass which is sung in the psalm: Be still, he says, and see that I am the Lord, Psal. 45. And the plan of our purpose accords with this too; for we, who for some while were tossed about in the various and sorrowful whirlwinds of secular soldiery, at last, Christ breathing upon us, found harbor in the port of Religion, ever most faithful to all; so that there, laying aside the blasts of vanity and pride, and appeasing God by the sacrifice of Christian humility, we might be able to escape not only the shipwrecks of the present life, but also the fires of the age to come. But now, in the name of the Lord, I will approach what is at hand, namely, to set down the things handed down by our elders and deposited with us, with the fidelity of a reporter rather than the presumption of an author; keeping, however, this law of writing: that I touch by no means upon all things, but only upon such as are necessary, and that not in an ornate and polished but in an easy and common speech, so that most things may seem indicated rather than expounded. Let those write elegantly and carefully who are led to this office either by confidence in their talent or by the reckoning of their duty. But for me it will be enough to have prepared a Commonitory for myself, for the sake of relieving my recollection, or rather my forgetfulness — which nevertheless I shall try, little by little, by recalling what I have learned, to amend and fill out daily, the Lord granting it. And I have given this very warning beforehand for this reason: that if perchance it should slip from us and come into the hands of holy men, they may not rashly find fault in it with anything which they may yet see is to be filed down by the promised amendment.

II.

Saepe igitur magno studio et summa attentione perquirens a quamplurimis sanctitate et doctrina praestantibus viris quonam modo possim certa quadam et quasi generali ac regulari via catholicae fidei veritatem ab haereticae pravitatis falsitate discernere, hujusmodi semper responsum ab omnibus fere retuli, quod sive ego, sive quis alius vellet exsurgentium haereticorum fraudes deprehendere laqueosque vitare, et in fide sana sanus atque integer permanere, duplici modo munire 640fidem suam, Domino adjuvante, deberet: primùm scilicet divinae legis auctoritate, tum deinde Ecclesiae catholicae traditione. Hic forsitan requirat aliquis: Cum sit perfectus Scripturarum Canon, sibique ad omnia satis superque sufficiat, quid opus est ut ei Ecclesiasticae intelligentiae jungatur auctoritas? Quia videlice Scripturam sacram pro ipsa sua altitudine non uno eodemque sensu universi accipiunt, sed ejusdem eloquia aliter atque aliter alius atque alius interpretatur; ut pene quot homines sunt, tot illinc sententiae erui posse videantur. Aliter namque illam Novatianus, aliter Sabellius, aliter Donatus exponit, aliter Arius, Eunomius, Macedonius; aliter Photinus, Apollinaris, Priscillianus, aliter Jovinianus, Pelagius, Caelestius; aliter postremo Nestorius. Atque idcirco multùm necesse est, propter tantos tam varii erroris anfractus, ut propheticae et apostolicae interpretationis linea secundum Ecclesiastici et Catholici sensus normam dirigatur. In ipsa item Catholica Ecclesia magnopere curandum est ut id teneamus quod ubique, quod semper, quod ab omnibus creditum est. Hoc est etenim vere proprieque catholicum, quod ipsa vis nominis ratioque declarat, quae omnia fere universaliter comprehendit. Sed hoc ita demum fiet, si sequamur universitatem, antiquitatem, consensionem. Sequemur autem universitatem hoc modo, si hanc unam fidem veram esse fateamur quam tota per orbem terrarum confitetur Ecclesia; antiquitatem vero ita, si ab his sensibus nullatenùs recedamus quos sanctos majores ac patres nostros celebrasse manifestum est: consensionem quoque itidem, si, in ipsa vetustate, omnium vel certe pene omnium sacerdotum pariter et magistrorum definitiones sententiasque sectemur.

II.

Often, therefore, inquiring with great zeal and the utmost attention from very many men eminent in holiness and learning, in what way I might discern, by some sure and, as it were, general and regular path, the truth of the Catholic faith from the falsehood of heretical depravity, I brought back from nearly all of them almost always an answer of this kind: that whether I or anyone else wished to detect the frauds of the heretics as they arose, and to avoid their snares, and to remain sound and whole in a sound faith, he ought to fortify his faith in a twofold manner, the Lord helping: 640first, namely, by the authority of the divine law, and then, secondly, by the tradition of the Catholic Church. Here perhaps someone may ask: Since the Canon of the Scriptures is perfect, and abundantly and more than sufficient of itself for all things, what need is there that the authority of ecclesiastical understanding be joined to it? Because, evidently, owing to its very depth, not all men receive Holy Scripture in one and the same sense, but one interprets its utterances one way and another another, so that almost as many opinions can seem to be drawn out from it as there are men. For Novatian expounds it one way, Sabellius another, Donatus another; Arius, Eunomius, Macedonius another; Photinus, Apollinaris, Priscillian another; Jovinian, Pelagius, Caelestius another; and lastly Nestorius another. And therefore it is very necessary, on account of so many windings of such varied error, that the line of prophetic and apostolic interpretation be directed according to the standard of the ecclesiastical and Catholic sense. In the Catholic Church itself, moreover, great care must be taken that we hold that which has been believed everywhere, always, and by all. For this is truly and properly Catholic, as the very force and reason of the name declares, which comprehends nearly all things universally. But this will at length come to pass, if we follow universality, antiquity, consent. And we shall follow universality in this way, if we confess that one faith to be true which the whole Church throughout the world confesses; antiquity, moreover, thus, if we in no wise depart from those senses which it is manifest our holy elders and fathers celebrated; and consent likewise, if, in that very antiquity, we follow the definitions and opinions of all, or certainly of almost all, the priests and teachers alike.

III.

Quid igitur tunc faciet Christianus catholicus, si se aliqua Ecclesiae particula ab universalis fidei communione praeciderit? Quid utique nisi ut pestifero corruptoque membro sanitatem universi corporis anteponat? Quid si novella aliqua contagio non jam portiunculam tantum, sed totam pariter Ecclesiam commaculare 641conetur? Tunc item providebit ut antiquitati inhaereat, quae prorsum jam non potest ab ulla novitatis fraude seduci. Quid si in ipsa vetustate, duorum aut trium hominum, vel certe civitatis unius aut etiam provinciae alicujus error deprehendatur? Tunc omnino curabit ut paucorum temeritati vel inscitiae, si qua sunt, universaliter antiquitus universalis Ecclesiae decreta praeponat. Quid si tale aliquid emergat ubi nihil hujusmodi reperiatur? Tunc operam dabit ut collatas inter se majorum consulat interrogetque sententias, eorum duntaxat qui diversis licet temporibus et locis, in unius tamen Ecclesiae Catholicae communione et fide permanentes, magistri probabiles exstiterunt; et quicquid non unus aut duo tantum, sed omnes pariter uno eodemque consensu aperte, frequenter, perseveranter tenuisse, scripsisse, docuisse cognoverit, id sibi quoque intelligat absque ulla dubitatione credendum.

III.

What, then, will a Catholic Christian do, if some little portion of the Church has cut itself off from the communion of the universal faith? What indeed, but prefer the soundness of the whole body to the pestilent and corrupt member? What if some new contagion attempts to defile not merely a little portion, but the whole Church alike? 641Then likewise he will provide to cleave to antiquity, which now can in no way be seduced by any fraud of novelty. What if in that very antiquity an error be detected of two or three men, or certainly of one city or even of some province? Then he will altogether take care to prefer the decrees of the universal Church, established of old universally, if there be any, to the rashness or ignorance of a few. What if some such thing should emerge where nothing of this kind is found? Then he will give his effort to consult and question the opinions of the elders, compared among themselves — of those, at least, who, though in diverse times and places, yet remaining in the communion and faith of the one Catholic Church, stood forth as approved teachers; and whatever he shall have found that not one or two only, but all alike, with one and the same consent, openly, frequently, perseveringly held, wrote, and taught, let him understand that this too is to be believed by himself without any doubt.

IV.

Sed ut planiora fiant quae dicimus, exemplis singillatim illustranda sunt et paulo uberius exaggeranda; ne immodicae brevitatis studio rapiantur rerum pondera orationis celeritate. Tempore Donati, a quo Donatistae, cum sese multa pars Africae in erroris 642sui furias praecipitaret, cumque immemor nominis, religionis, professionis, unius hominis sacrilegam temeritatem Ecclesiae Christi praeponeret, tunc quicumque per Africam constituti, profano schismate detestato, universis mundi Ecclesiis adsociati sunt, soli ex illis omnibus intra sacraria catholicae fidei salvi esse potuerunt; egregiam profecto relinquentes posteris formam, quemadmodum scilicet deinceps, bono more, unius aut certe paucorum vesaniae universorum sanitas anteferretur. Item quando Arianorum venenum non jam portiunculam quamdam, sed pene orbem totum contaminaverat, adeo ut prope cunctis Latini sermonis Episcopis partim vi, partim fraude deceptis, caligo quaedam mentibus offunderetur, quidnam potissimum in tanta rerum confusione sequendum foret, tunc quisquis verus Christi amator et cultor exstitit, antiquam fidem novellae perfidiae praeferendo, nulla contagii ipsius peste maculatus est. Cujus quidem temporis periculo satis superque monstratum est quantum invehatur calamitatis novelli dogmatis inductione. Hunc siquidem non solum parvae res, sed etiam maximae labefactatae sunt. Nec enim tantum adfinitates, cognationes, amicitiae, domus, verum etiam urbes, populi, provinciae, nationes, universum postremo Romanum Imperium funditus concussum et emotum est. Namque cum prophana ipsa Arianorum novitas, velut quaedam Bellona aut furia, capto primo omnium Imperatore, cuncta deinde palatii culmina legibus novis subjugasset, nequaquam deinceps destitit universa miscere atque vexare, privata ac publica, sacra prophanaque omnia, nullum boni et veri gerere discrimen, sed quoscumque conlibuisset, tanquàm de loco superiore percutere. Tunc temeratae conjuges, depullatae 643viduae, prophanatae virgines, monasteria demolita, disturbati clerici, verberati levitae, acti in exilium sacerdotes, oppleta sanctis ergastula, carceres, metalla: quorum pars maxima, interdictis urbibus, protrusi atque extorres, inter deserta, speluncas, feras, saxa, nuditate, fame, siti affecti, contriti et tabefacti sunt. Atque haec omnia numquid ullam aliam ob causam, nisi utique dum pro coelesti dogmate humanae superstitiones introducuntur, dùm bene fundata antiquitas scelesta novitate subruitur, dum superiorum instituta violantur, dum rescinduntur scita patrum, dum convelluntur definita majorum, dùm sese intra sacratae atque incorruptae vetustatis castissimos limites prophanae ac novellae curiositatis libido non continet?

IV.

But that what we say may become plainer, they must be illustrated one by one with examples and amplified a little more fully; lest, by a zeal for excessive brevity, the weight of the matters be carried off by the speed of the discourse. In the time of Donatus, from whom the Donatists are named, when a great part of Africa was hurling itself into the frenzies of its 642error, and, forgetful of its name, its religion, its profession, was preferring the sacrilegious rashness of one man to the Church of Christ, then whoever throughout Africa, detesting the profane schism, were associated with all the Churches of the world — these alone out of all those were able to be saved within the sanctuary of the Catholic faith; leaving indeed to posterity an excellent pattern, namely, how thenceforth, by a good custom, the soundness of all should be preferred to the madness of one, or certainly of a few. Likewise, when the poison of the Arians had contaminated not now some little portion, but almost the whole world, so that, nearly all the bishops of the Latin tongue being deceived partly by force, partly by fraud, a certain fog was poured over their minds as to what above all was to be followed in so great a confusion of things, then whoever stood forth a true lover and worshiper of Christ, preferring the ancient faith to the new perfidy, was stained by no plague of that contagion. And by the peril of that time it was more than sufficiently shown how great a calamity is brought in by the introduction of a new doctrine. For by it not only small things, but even the greatest, were shaken to their foundations. For not only affinities, kinships, friendships, households, but even cities, peoples, provinces, nations, and finally the whole Roman Empire were utterly shaken and moved from their place. For when that profane novelty of the Arians, like some Bellona or fury, having first captured the Emperor of all, then subjected all the heights of the palace to new laws, it never thereafter ceased to confound and vex all things, private and public, sacred and profane alike, to make no distinction of the good and the true, but to strike down whomsoever it pleased, as from a higher place. Then were wives dishonored, 643widows despoiled, virgins profaned, monasteries demolished, clerics thrown into confusion, deacons beaten, priests driven into exile, workhouses, prisons, and mines filled with saints: the greatest part of whom, the cities being forbidden them, thrust out and banished, among deserts, caves, wild beasts, and rocks, afflicted with nakedness, hunger, and thirst, were worn down and wasted away. And all these things — for what other cause, save indeed while human superstitions are brought in for the sake of a heavenly doctrine, while well-founded antiquity is undermined by wicked novelty, while the institutions of our forebears are violated, while the decrees of the fathers are rescinded, while the definitions of the elders are torn up, while the lust of profane and new curiosity does not contain itself within the most chaste bounds of a hallowed and incorrupt antiquity?

V.

Sed forsitan odio novitatis et amore vetustatis haec fingimus. Quisquis hoc aestimat, beato saltem credat Ambrosio, qui, in secundo ad Imperatorem Gratianum libro, acerbitatem temporis ipse deplorans, ait: « Sed jam satis, inquit, omnipotens Deus, nostro exilio nostroque sanguine Confessorum neces, exilia Sacerdotum, et nefas tantae impietatis eluimus. Satis claruit eos qui violaverint fidem tutos esse non posse. » Ambros. lib. 2 de fide cap. 4, alias 16. Item in tertio ejusdem operis libro: « Servemus igitur, inquit, praecepta majorum, nec haereditaria signacula ausi rudis temeritate violemus. Librum signatum illum propheticum non Seniores, non Potestates, non Angeli, non Archangeli aperire ausi sunt: soli Christo explanandi ejus praerogativa servata est. Librum sacerdotalem quis nostrum resignare audeat, signatum a Confessoribus, et multorum jam martyrio consecratum? Quem qui resignare coacti sunt postea 644tamen damnata fraude signarunt; qui violare non ausi sunt, Confessores et martyres exstiterunt. Quomodo fidem eorum possumus denegare quorum victoriam praedicamus? » Ibid. lib. 3. cap. 7, alias 15. Praedicamus plane, inquam, ô venerande Ambrosi! praedicamus plane, laudantesque miramur. Nam quis ille tam demens est qui eos, etsi adsequi non evaleat, non exoptet sequi quos a defensione fidei majorum nulla vis depulit, non minae, non blandimenta, non vita, non mors, non palatium, non satellites, non Imperator, non imperium, non homines, non daemones? quos, inquam, pro religiosae vetustatis tenacitate tanto munere Dominus dignos judicavit ut per eos prostratas restauraret Ecclesias, exstinctos spiritales populos vivificaret, dejectas sacerdotum coronas, reponeret, nefarias illas novellae impietatis non litteras, sed lituras, infuso coelitus Episcopis fidelium lacrymarum fonte, deleret, universum postremo jam pene mundum saeva repentinae haereseos tempestate perculsum ad antiquam fidem a novella perfidia, ad antiquam sanitatem a novitatis vesania, ad antiquam lucem a novitatis caecitate revocaret. Sed in hac divina quadam Confessorum virtute illud est etiam nobis vel maxime considerandum, quod tunc apud ipsam Ecclesiae vetustatem non partis alicujus, sed universitatis ab iis est suscepta defensio. Neque enim fas erat, 645ut tanti ac tales viri unius aut duorum hominum errabundas sibique ipsis contrarias suspiciones tam magno molimine adsererent, aut vero pro alicujus provincio lae temeraria quadam conspiratione certarent, sed omnium sanctae Ecclesiae sacerdotum apostolicae et catholicae veritatis haeredum decreta et definita sectantes, maluerunt semetipsos quam vetustae universitatis fidem prodere. Unde et ad tantam gloriam pervenire meruerunt, ut non solum Confessores, verum etiam Confessorum principes jure meritoque habeantur.

V.

But perhaps we invent these things out of hatred of novelty and love of antiquity. Whoever thinks this, let him at least believe blessed Ambrose, who, in his second book to the Emperor Gratian, himself lamenting the bitterness of the time, says: « But now, almighty God, we have sufficiently washed away, by our exile and our blood, the slayings of Confessors, the exiles of Priests, and the abomination of so great an impiety. It has become sufficiently clear that those who have violated the faith cannot be safe. » Ambrose, On the Faith, Book 2, ch. 4, otherwise 16. Likewise in the third book of the same work: « Let us therefore keep, he says, the precepts of our forefathers, and not, with rude rashness, dare to violate the seals of our inheritance. That sealed prophetic book neither Elders, nor Powers, nor Angels, nor Archangels dared to open: to Christ alone was reserved the prerogative of expounding it. Who of us would dare to unseal the priestly book, sealed by Confessors, and already consecrated by the martyrdom of many? Those who were afterward compelled to unseal it 644nevertheless sealed it again, the fraud being condemned; those who did not dare to violate it stood forth as Confessors and martyrs. How can we deny the faith of those whose victory we proclaim? » Ibid., Book 3, ch. 7, otherwise 15. We proclaim it plainly, I say, O venerable Ambrose! We proclaim it plainly, and, praising them, we marvel. For who is so out of his mind as not to long, even if he cannot avail to attain them, to follow those whom no force drove back from the defense of the faith of our forefathers — not threats, not blandishments, not life, not death, not the palace, not the guardsmen, not the Emperor, not the empire, not men, not demons? — whom, I say, for their tenacity in a devout antiquity, the Lord judged worthy of so great a gift, that through them he restored the prostrate Churches, gave life again to the spiritual peoples that had been extinguished, set back in place the cast-down crowns of the priests, and — a fountain of the tears of the faithful being poured from heaven upon the bishops — blotted out those nefarious things of the new impiety, not writings but blottings, and at last called back nearly the whole world, stricken by the savage tempest of a sudden heresy, from a new perfidy to the ancient faith, from the madness of novelty to the ancient soundness, from the blindness of novelty to the ancient light. But in this certain divine virtue of the Confessors there is also this above all to be considered by us, that then, in the very antiquity of the Church, the defense undertaken by them was not that of some party, but of the whole. For it was not right 645that men so great and of such a kind should assert, with so great an effort, the wandering suspicions of one or two men, contrary even to themselves, or truly should contend, by some rash conspiracy, for the sake of some provincio lae, but, following the decrees and definitions of all the priests of the holy Church, the heirs of apostolic and Catholic truth, they chose rather to betray themselves than the faith of the ancient universality. Whence also they merited to arrive at so great a glory, that they are rightly and deservedly held to be not only Confessors, but even the chief of Confessors.

VI.

Magnum hoc igitur eorumdem beatorum exemplum, planeque divinum, et veris quibusque Catholicis indefessa meditatione recolendum, qui modum septemplicis candelabri septenâ sancti Spiritus luce radiante, clarissimam posteris formulam praemonstrarunt quonam modo deinceps per singula quaeque errorum vaniloquia, sacratae vetustatis auctoritate prophanae novitatis conteratur audacia. Neque hoc sane novum. Siquidem mos iste semper in Ecclesia viguit ut quo quisque foret religiosor, eo promptius novellis adintentionibus contrairet. Exemplis talibus plena sunt omnia. Sed ne longum fiat, unum aliquod, et hoc ab Apostolica potissimum Sede sumemus; ut omnes luce clarius videant beatorum Apostolorum beata successio quanta vi semper, quanto studio, quanta contentione defenderit susceptae semel Religionis integritatem. Quondam igitur venerabilis memoriae Agrippinus Carthaginensis Episcopus, primus omnium mortalium contrà divinum Canonem, contra universalis Ecclesiae regulam, contra sensum omnium consacerdotum, contra morem atque instituta majorum, rebaptizandum esse censebat. Quae praesumptio tantum mali invexit, ut non solùm Haereticis omnibus formam sacrilegii, sed etiam quibusdam Catholicis occasionem praebuerit erroris. Cùm ergo undique ad novitatem rei cuncti reclamarent, atque omnes quaquaversum sacerdotes pro suo quisque studio retinerentur, tunc beatae memoriae Papa Stephanus apostolicae Sedis antistes, cum 646caeteris quidem collegis suis, sed tamen prae caeteris restitit, dignum, ut opinor, existimans si reliquos omnes tantum fidei devotione vinceret quantum loci auctoritate superabat. Denique in Epistola quae tunc ad Africam missa est, his verbis sanxit: NIHIL NOVANDUM NISI QUOD TRADITUM EST. Intelligebat etenim vir sanctus et prudens nihil aliud rationem pietatis admittere, nisi ut omnia, qua fide a patribus suscepta forent, eâdem fide filiis consignarentur; nosque Religionem, non qua vellemus ducere, sed potius quà illa duceret sequi oportere; idque esse proprium Christianae modestiae et gravitatis, non sua posteris tradere, sed a majoribus accepta servare. Quis ergo tunc universi negotii exitus? Quis utique nisi usitatus et solitus? Retenta est scilicet antiquitas, explosa novitas. Sed forte tunc ipsi novitiae adinventioni patrocinia defuerunt? Imo vero tanta vis ingenii adfuit, tanta eloquentiae flumina, tantus adsertorum numerus, tanta verisimilitudo, tanta divinae legis oracula, sed plane novo ac malo more intellecta, ut mihi omnis illa conspiratio nullo modo destrui potuisse videatur nisi sola tanti moliminis causa ipsa illa suscepta, ipsa defensa, laudata novitatis professio destituisset. Quid postremo? Ipsius Africani Concilii sive decreti quae vires? Donante Deo, nullae; sed universa tanquam somnia, tanquam fabulae, tanquam superflua, abolita, antiquata calcata sunt. Et, o rerum mira conversio! Auctores ejusdem opinionis Catholici, consectatores vero Haeretici judicantur; absolvuntur magistri, condemnantur discipuli; conscriptores librorum filii regni erunt, adsertores vero gehenna suscipiet. Nam quis ille tam demens est qui illud sanctorum omnium et Episcoporum et Martyrum lumen beatissimum Cyprianum cum caeteris collegis suis in aeternum dubitet regnaturum esse cum Christo? Aut quis tam contrà sacrilegus qui Donatistas et caeteras pestes, quae illius auctoritate Concilii 647rebaptizare se jactitant, in sempiternum neget arsuros esse cum diabolo?

VI.

Great, therefore, and plainly divine is this example of those same blessed men, and to be recalled by all true Catholics with unwearied meditation, who, like the seven-branched candlestick shining with the sevenfold light of the Holy Spirit, showed forth to posterity a most clear pattern of how thereafter, through each and every empty talk of errors, the audacity of profane novelty may be crushed by the authority of hallowed antiquity. Nor is this indeed anything new. For this custom has always flourished in the Church, that the more devout each one was, the more readily he opposed himself to new inventions. All things are full of such examples. But, lest it grow long, let us take some one, and this above all from the Apostolic See; that all may see, clearer than light, with how great force, with how great zeal, with how great contention the blessed succession of the blessed Apostles has always defended the integrity of the Religion once received. Once, then, Agrippinus of venerable memory, bishop of Carthage, first of all mortals, against the divine Canon, against the rule of the universal Church, against the judgment of all his fellow priests, against the custom and institutions of our forefathers, held that rebaptism ought to be practiced. Which presumption brought in so much evil, that it furnished not only to all heretics a pattern of sacrilege, but even to certain Catholics an occasion of error. When, therefore, all cried out on every side against the novelty of the thing, and all the priests everywhere held back, each according to his own zeal, then Stephen of blessed memory, Pope, prelate of the Apostolic See, together 646indeed with his other colleagues, but yet more than the rest, resisted, judging it worthy, as I suppose, that he should surpass all the rest as much by devotion to the faith as he excelled them by the authority of his place. In short, in the Epistle which was then sent to Africa, he decreed in these words: LET NOTHING BE INNOVATED SAVE WHAT HAS BEEN HANDED DOWN. For the holy and prudent man understood that the reckoning of piety admits of nothing else than that all things which had been received from the fathers with the same faith should be consigned to the sons; and that it behooves us to follow Religion, not to lead it whither we would, but rather whither it leads; and that it is proper to Christian modesty and gravity, not to hand down our own things to posterity, but to keep the things received from our elders. What, then, was the outcome of the whole affair? What indeed, but the usual and accustomed one? Antiquity, namely, was kept, and novelty was hissed off. But perhaps at that time patronage was wanting to the new invention? Nay rather, there was present so great a force of talent, so great rivers of eloquence, so great a number of maintainers, so great a semblance of truth, so many oracles of the divine law — but plainly understood in a new and evil fashion — that to me all that conspiracy seems in no way to have been able to be destroyed, unless the very undertaking, the very defended and praised profession of novelty which was the cause of so great an effort had itself failed. Finally, what force had that same African Council or decree? By God's gift, none; but all things, as though dreams, as though fables, as though superfluous, were abolished, made obsolete, trodden underfoot. And, O wondrous turning of events! The authors of that same opinion are judged Catholics, but the followers heretics; the masters are absolved, the disciples condemned; the writers of the books shall be sons of the kingdom, but hell shall receive the maintainers. For who is so out of his mind as to doubt that that most blessed light of all the saints and bishops and martyrs, Cyprian, with the rest of his colleagues, shall reign forever with Christ? Or who is so sacrilegious to the contrary as to deny that the Donatists and the other plagues, who by the authority of that Council 647boast that they rebaptize themselves, shall burn forever with the devil?

VII.

Quod quidem mihi divinitus videtur promulgatum esse judicium propter eorum maxime fraudulentiam qui cum sub alieno nomine haeresim concinnare machinentur, captant plerumque veteris cujuspiam viri scripta paulo involutius edita, quae pro ipsa sui obscuritate dogmati suo quasi congruant; ut illud nescio quid quodcunque proferunt, neque primi neque soli sentire videantur. Quorum ego nequitiam duplici odio dignam judico, vel eo quod haereseos venenum propinare aliis non pertimescunt, vel eo etiam quod sancti cujusque viri memoriam tanquam sopitos jam cineres prophana manu ventilant, et quae silentio sepeliri oportebat, rediviva opinione diffamant, sequentes omnino vestigia auctoris sui Cham, qui nuditatem venerandi Noe non modo operire neglexit, verum quoque irridendam caeteris enuntiavit. Unde tantam laesae pietatis meruit offensam ut etiam posteri ipsius peccati sui maledictis obligarentur, beatis illis fratribus multum longeque dissimilis, qui nuditatem ipsam reverendi patris neque suis temerare oculis, neque alienis patere voluerunt, sed aversi, ut scribitur, texerunt eum: quod est erratum sancti viri nec approbasse nec prodidisse; atque idcirco beata in posteros benedictione donati sunt. Sed ad propositum redeamus. Magno 648igitur metu nobis immutatae fidei ac temeratae religionis piaculum pertimescendum est; a quo nos solum constitutionis Ecclesiasticae disciplina, sed etiam censura Apostolicae deterret auctoritatis. Scitum enim cunctis est quam graviter, quam severe, quam vehementer invehatur in quosdam beatus Apostolus Paulus, qui mira levitate nimium cito translati fuerant ab eo qui eos vocaverat in gratiam Christi, in aliud Evangelium, quod non est aliud; 2 Tim. 4, qui coacervarant sibi magistros ad sua desideria, a veritate quidem auditum avertentes, conversi vero ad fabulas; 1 Tim. 5, habentes damnationem quod primam fidem irritam fecissent; quos deceperant ii de quibus ad Romanos fratres scribit idem Apostolus: Rogo autem vos, fratres, ut observetis eos qui dissentiones et offendicula, praeter doctrinam quam ipsi didicistis, faciunt; et declinate ab illis. Hujusmodi enim Christo Domino non serviunt, sed suo ventri, et per dulces sermones et benedictiones seducunt corda innocentium, Rom. 16. Qui intrant per domos, et captivas ducunt mulierculas oneratas peccatis, quae ducuntur variis desideriis; semper discentes, et ad scientiam veritatis nunquam pervenientes, 2 Tim. 3. Vaniloqui et seductores, qui universas domos subvertunt, docentes quae non oportet, turpis lucri gratia, Tit. 1. Homines corrupti mente, reprobi circà fidem, 2 Tim. 3, superbi, et nihil scientes, sed languentes circa quaestiones et pugnas verborum; qui veritate privati sunt, existimantes quaestum esse pietatem: simul autem et otiosi discunt circumire domos; non solum autem otiosi, sed et verbosi, et curiosi loquentes quae non oportet, 1 Tim. 5: qui bonam conscientiam repellentes, circa fidem naufragaverunt, 1 Tim. 1: quorum prophana vaniloquia multum proficiunt ad impietatem, et sermo eorum ut cancer serpit, 2 Tim. 2. Bene autem quod de iis item scribitur: Sed ultra non proficient. Insipientia enim eorum manifesta erit omnibus, sicut et illorum fuit. 2 Tim. 3.

VII.

Which judgment indeed seems to me to have been divinely promulgated chiefly on account of the fraudulence of those who, since they contrive to concoct a heresy under another's name, generally seize upon the writings of some ancient man, published a little too obscurely, which, by reason of their very obscurity, seem, as it were, to agree with their own doctrine; so that whatever that unknown something is which they put forward, they may seem neither the first nor the only ones to hold it. Their wickedness I judge worthy of a twofold hatred: both because they do not dread to offer to others the poison of heresy, and also because they fan with profane hand the memory of each holy man as though ashes now laid to rest, and defame by a revived opinion the things that ought to have been buried in silence, following altogether the footsteps of their author Ham, who not only neglected to cover the nakedness of the venerable Noah, but also proclaimed it to the rest for mockery. Whence he earned so great an offense of injured piety that even his descendants were bound by the curses of his sin — very far unlike those blessed brothers, who wished neither to violate with their own eyes the very nakedness of their revered father, nor to let it lie open to those of others, but, turned away, as it is written, covered him: which is neither to have approved nor to have betrayed the error of the holy man; and therefore they were endowed with a blessed benediction upon their posterity. But let us return to our purpose. With great 648fear, therefore, must the guilt of a changed faith and a violated religion be dreaded by us; from which only the discipline of ecclesiastical ordinance, but also the censure of apostolic authority deters us. For it is known to all how gravely, how severely, how vehemently the blessed Apostle Paul inveighs against certain men who, by a wondrous fickleness, had been too quickly carried over from him who had called them into the grace of Christ, unto another Gospel, which is not another; 2 Tim. 4, who had heaped up to themselves masters according to their own desires, turning away their hearing indeed from the truth, but turned unto fables; 1 Tim. 5, having damnation because they had made void their first faith; whom those had deceived concerning whom the same Apostle writes to the Roman brethren: I beseech you, brethren, that you observe those who make dissensions and scandals contrary to the doctrine which you yourselves have learned; and avoid them. For such do not serve Christ the Lord, but their own belly, and by sweet speeches and blessings seduce the hearts of the innocent, Rom. 16. Who make their way into houses, and lead captive silly women laden with sins, who are led away with various desires; ever learning, and never arriving at the knowledge of the truth, 2 Tim. 3. Vain talkers and seducers, who subvert whole houses, teaching things which they ought not, for the sake of filthy lucre, Tit. 1. Men corrupt in mind, reprobate concerning the faith, 2 Tim. 3, proud, and knowing nothing, but languishing about questions and strifes of words; who are deprived of the truth, esteeming gain to be godliness: and at the same time, being idle, they learn to go about from house to house; and not only idle, but also full of words, and curious, speaking things which they ought not, 1 Tim. 5: who, casting away a good conscience, have made shipwreck concerning the faith, 1 Tim. 1: whose profane vain babblings advance much unto impiety, and their speech spreads like a canker, 2 Tim. 2. And it is well that of them likewise it is written: But they shall proceed no further. For their folly shall be manifest to all, as also that of those men was. 2 Tim. 3.

VIII.

Cum ergo tales quidam circumeuntes provincias et civitates, atque errores venalitios circumferendo, etiam ad Galatas devenissent, cùmque his auditis Galatae nausea quadam veritatis affecti, Apostolicae Catholicaeque doctrinae manna revomentes, haereticae novitatis sordibus oblectarentur, ita sese Apostolicae potestatis exeruit auctoritas, ut summa cum severitate 649decerneret: Sed licet nos, inquit, aut angelus de coelo evangelizet vobis praeterquam quod evangelizavimus vobis, anathema sit, Gal. 1. Quid est quod ait, Sed licet nos? Cur non potius, Sed licet ego? Hoc est: Etiamsi Petrus, etiamsi Andreas, etiamsi Joannes, etiamsi postremo omnis Apostolorum chorus evangelizet vobis praeterquam quod evangelizavimus, anathema sit. Tremenda districtio, propter adserendam primae fidei tenacitatem, nec sibi, nec caeteris coapostolis pepercisse! Parum est: Etiamsi angelus, inquit, de coelo evangelizet vobis, praeterquam quod evangelizavimus, anathema sit. Non suffecerat ad custodiam traditae semel fidei, humanae conditionis commemorasse naturam, nisi angelicam quoque excellentiam comprehendisset. Licet nos, inquit, aut angelus de coelo. Non quia sancti coelestesque angeli peccare jam possint; sed hoc est quod dicit: Si etiam, inquit, fiat quod non potest fieri, quisquis ille traditam semel fidem mutare tentaverit, anathema sit. Sed haec forsitan perfunctorie praelocutus est, et humano potius effudit impetu, quam divina ratione decrevit. Absit. Sequitur enim, et hoc ipsum ingenti molimine iteratae insinuationis inculcat: Sicut praediximus, et nunc iterum dico: Si quis vobis evangelizaverit, praeterquam quod accepistis, anathema sit. Non dixit: Si quis vobis annuntiaverit praeterquam quod accepistis, benedictus sit, laudetur, recipiatur; sed, anathema sit, inquit, id est, separatus, segregatus, exclusus, ne unius ovis dirum contagium innoxium gregem Christi venenata permixtione contaminet.

VIII.

When, therefore, certain such men, going about the provinces and cities, and carrying around their venal errors, had come even to the Galatians, and when the Galatians, having heard these things, seized by a certain nausea of the truth, vomiting up the manna of apostolic and Catholic doctrine, took delight in the filth of heretical novelty, the authority of apostolic power so exerted itself that with the utmost severity 649it decreed: But although we, he says, or an angel from heaven preach a gospel to you besides that which we have preached to you, let him be anathema, Gal. 1. What is it that he says, But although we? Why not rather, But although I? This is to say: Even if Peter, even if Andrew, even if John, even if finally the whole choir of the Apostles should preach to you besides what we have preached, let him be anathema. A tremendous strictness, that for the sake of asserting tenacity in the first faith he spared neither himself nor his fellow apostles! It is little: Even if an angel, he says, from heaven should preach to you, besides what we have preached, let him be anathema. It had not sufficed, for the keeping of the faith once handed down, to have mentioned the nature of the human condition, unless he had also comprehended the angelic excellence. Although we, he says, or an angel from heaven. Not because the holy and heavenly angels can now sin; but this is what he says: If even, he says, that which cannot be done should be done — whoever he be that shall attempt to change the faith once handed down, let him be anathema. But perhaps he spoke these things perfunctorily, and poured them out by human impulse rather than decreed them by divine reason. God forbid. For he goes on, and drives home this very thing with the immense effort of a repeated insinuation: As we have foretold, and now I say again: If anyone preach a gospel to you besides that which you have received, let him be anathema. He did not say: If anyone announce to you besides what you have received, let him be blessed, praised, received; but, let him be anathema, he says, that is, separated, set apart, excluded, lest the dire contagion of one sheep should, by a poisoned intermixture, contaminate the innocent flock of Christ.

IX.

Sed forsitan Galatis ista tantum praecepta sunt. Ergo et illa solis Galatis imperata sunt quae in ejusdem Epistolae sequentibus commemorantur: qualia sunt haec: Si vivimus spiritu, spiritu et ambulemus. Non efficiamur inanis gloriae cupidi, invicem provocantes, invidentes, Gal. 5, et reliqua. Quod si absurdum est, et omnibus ex aequo imperata sunt, restat ut sicut haec morum mandata, ita etiam illa quae de fide cauta sunt omnes pari modo comprehendant, et sicut nemini licet invicem provocare aut invidere invicem, ita nemini liceat, praeter id quod Ecclesia Catholica usquequaque evangelizat accipere. Aut forsitan tunc jubebatur, si quis annuntiasset praeterquam quod annuntiatum fuerat, anathematizari; nunc vero jam non jubetur. Ergo et illud quod item ibi ait: Dico autem, spiritu ambulate, et desiderium carnis non perficietis, tunc tantum jubebatur, modo vero jam non jubetur. Quod si impium pariter et perniciosum est ita credere, necessario sequitur ut sicut haec cunctis aetatibus observanda sunt, ita illa quoque quae de non mutanda fide sancita sunt cunctis aetatibus imperata sint. Annuntiare ergo aliquid Christianis catholicis, praeter id quod acceperunt nunquam licuit, nusquam licet, nunquam licebit; et anathematizare eos qui annuntiant aliquid praeterquam quod semel acceptum est, nunquam non oportuit, nusquam non oportet, nunquàm non oportebit. Quae cùm ita sint, estne aliquis vel tantae audaciae qui praeter id quod apud Ecclesiam annuntiatum est annuntiet, vel tantae levitatis qui praeter id quod ab Ecclesia accepit accipiat? Clamat et repetendo clamat, et omnibus, et 650semper, et ubique per litteras suas clamat ille, ille vas electionis, ille magister gentium, ille Apostolorum tuba, ille terrarum praeco, ille coelorum conscius, ut si quis novum dogma annuntiaverit, anathematizetur. Et contra reclamant ranae quaedam, et cyniphes, et muscae moriturae, quales sunt Pelagiani, et hoc Catholicis: Nobis, inquiunt, auctoribus, nobis principibus, nobis expositoribus, damnate quae tenebatis, tenete quae damnabatis, rejicite antiquam fidem, paterna instituta, majorum deposita; et recipite: quaenam illa tandem? horreo dicere: sunt enim tam superba, ut mihi non modo afirmari, sed ne refelli quidem sine aliquo piaculo posse videantur.

IX.

But perhaps these precepts were given to the Galatians only. Then those things too were enjoined on the Galatians alone which are mentioned in the following parts of the same Epistle: such as these: If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit. Let us not be made desirous of vainglory, provoking one another, envying one another, Gal. 5, and the rest. But if that is absurd, and they were enjoined upon all equally, it remains that, just as these commands concerning morals, so also those which are prescribed concerning the faith comprehend all in like manner; and just as it is lawful for no one to provoke one another or to envy one another, so let it be lawful for no one to receive anything besides that which the Catholic Church everywhere preaches. Or perhaps it was then commanded that if anyone announced besides what had been announced, he should be anathematized; but now it is no longer commanded. Then that too which he likewise says there: And I say, walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the desire of the flesh, was commanded then only, but now is no longer commanded. But if to believe so is at once impious and pernicious, it necessarily follows that, just as these things are to be observed in all ages, so also those things which have been sanctioned concerning the faith not to be changed have been enjoined upon all ages. To announce, therefore, to Catholic Christians anything besides that which they have received was never lawful, is nowhere lawful, will never be lawful; and to anathematize those who announce anything besides what has once been received was at no time not fitting, is nowhere not fitting, will never not be fitting. Since these things are so, is there anyone either of so great audacity as to announce anything besides that which has been announced in the Church, or of so great fickleness as to receive anything besides that which he has received from the Church? He cries out, and by repeating cries out, and to all, and 650always, and everywhere through his letters he cries out — he, that vessel of election, that teacher of the nations, that trumpet of the Apostles, that herald of the lands, that one privy to the heavens — that if anyone announce a new doctrine, let him be anathematized. And on the contrary there cry back certain frogs, and gnats, and flies about to die, such as are the Pelagians, and this to Catholics: On our authority, they say, with us as leaders, with us as expounders, condemn what you were holding, hold what you were condemning, reject the ancient faith, the institutions of the fathers, the deposits of the elders; and receive — what, then, at last? I shudder to say: for they are so arrogant that to me they seem able not only not to be affirmed afirmari, but not even to be refuted, without some guilt.

X.

Sed dicet aliquis: Cur ergo persaepe divinitus sinuntur excellentes quaedam personae in Ecclesia constitutae res novas Catholicis annuntiare? Recta interrogatio et digna quae diligentius atque uberius pertractetur: cui tamen non ingenio proprio, sed divinae legis auctoritate, Ecclesiastici magisterii documento satisfaciendum est. Audiamus ergo sanctum Moysen; et ipse nos doceat cur docti viri, et qui propter scientiae gratiam ab Apostolo etiam prophetae nuncupantur, proferre interdum permittantur nova dogmata, quae vetus Testamentum allegorico sermone deos alienos appellare consuevit, eò quod scilicet ita ab haereticis ipsorum opiniones sicut a gentibus dii sui observentur. Scribit ergo in Deuteronomio beatus Moyses: Si surrexerit, inquit, in medio tuî propheta, aut qui somnium vidisse se dicat, Deut. 13, id est, magister in Ecclesia constitutus, quem discipuli vel auditores sui ex aliqua revelatione docere arbitrentur. Quid deinde? et praedixerit, inquit, signum atque portentum, et evenerit quod locutus est. Magnus profecto nescio quis signatur magister et tantae scientiae qui sectatoribus propriis non solum quae humana sunt nosse, verum etiam quae supra hominem sunt praenoscere posse videatur; quales fere discipuli sui jactitant fuisse Valentinum, Donatum, Photinum, Apollinarem, caeterosque ejusmodi. Quid postea? et dixerit, inquit, tibi: Eamus, et sequamur deos alienos quos ignoras, et serviamus eis. Qui sunt dii alieni, nisi errores extranei quos ignorabas, id est, novi et inauditi? Et 651serviamus eis, id est, credamus eis, sequamur eos. Quid ad extremum? non audies, inquit, verba prophetae illius aut somniatoris. Et quare, oro te, a Deo non prohibetur doceri quod a Deo prohibetur audiri? Quia, inquit, tentat vos Dominus Deus vester, ut palam fiat utrum diligatis eum an non, in toto corde et in tota anima vestrâ. Luce clarius aperta causa est cur interdum divina Providentia quosdam Ecclesiarum magistros nova quaedam dogmata praedicare patiatur. Ut tentet vos, inquit, Dominus Deus vester. Et profecto magna tentatio est cum ille quem tu prophetam, quem prophetarum discipulum, quem doctorem et adsertorem veritatis putes, quem summa veneratione et amore complexus sis, is subito latenter noxios subinducat errores, quos nec cito deprehendere valeas, dum antiqui magisterii duceris praejudicio, nec facile damnare fas ducas, dum magistri veteris praepediris affectu.

X.

But someone will say: Why, then, are certain eminent persons set in the Church very often divinely permitted to announce new things to Catholics? A right question, and worthy to be treated more diligently and more fully: to which, however, satisfaction is to be given not by one's own wit, but by the authority of the divine law, by the teaching of the ecclesiastical magisterium. Let us hear, then, holy Moses; and let him himself teach us why learned men, and those who on account of the grace of knowledge are even called prophets by the Apostle, are sometimes permitted to put forward new doctrines, which the Old Testament is wont in allegorical speech to call strange gods, namely because the heretics observe their own opinions just as the Gentiles do their gods. The blessed Moses, then, writes in Deuteronomy: If there arise, he says, in the midst of thee a prophet, or one who says that he has seen a dream, Deut. 13, that is, a teacher set in the Church, whom his disciples or hearers think to teach from some revelation. What next? and shall have foretold, he says, a sign and a wonder, and there shall have come to pass what he spoke. Assuredly some great teacher, I know not who, is signified, and of such great knowledge that to his own followers he may seem able not only to know the things that are human, but even to foreknow the things that are above man; such as their disciples boast that Valentinus, Donatus, Photinus, Apollinaris, and the rest of that kind were. What afterward? and shall have said, he says, to thee: Let us go, and follow strange gods whom thou knowest not, and let us serve them. Who are the strange gods, but foreign errors which thou didst not know, that is, new and unheard of? And 651let us serve them, that is, let us believe them, let us follow them. What at the last? thou shalt not hear, he says, the words of that prophet or dreamer. And why, I pray thee, is it not forbidden by God to be taught that which by God is forbidden to be heard? Because, he says, the Lord your God tries you, that it may be made manifest whether you love him or not, with all your heart and with all your soul. Clearer than light the cause is laid open why divine Providence sometimes suffers certain teachers of the Churches to preach certain new doctrines. That the Lord your God may try you, he says. And assuredly it is a great temptation when he whom thou thinkest a prophet, a disciple of the prophets, a teacher and maintainer of the truth, whom thou hast embraced with the utmost veneration and love, suddenly and secretly brings in harmful errors, which thou canst neither quickly detect, being led by the prejudgment of the ancient teaching, nor easily deem it right to condemn, being hindered by affection for the old master.

XI.

Hic forsitan efflagitet aliquis ut ea quae sancti Moysi verbis adserta sunt, Ecclesiasticis aliquibus demonstrentur exemplis. Aequa expostulatio, nec diù differenda. Nam ut a proximis et manifestis incipiam, qualem fuisse nuper tentationem putamus, cùm infelix ille Nestorius, subito ex ove conversus in lupum, gregem Christi lacerare coepisset, cum eum hi ipsi qui rodebantur ex magna adhuc parte ovem crederent, ideoque morsibus ejus magis paterent? Nam quis eum facile errare arbitraretur quem tanto Imperii judicio electum, tanto sacerdotum studio prosecutum videret; qui cum magno sanctorum amore, summo populi favore celebraretur, quotidie palam divina tractabat eloquia, et noxios quosque Judaeorum et Gentilium confutabat errores? Quo tandem iste modo non cuivis fidem faceret se recta docere, recta praedicare, recta sentire, qui ut uni haeresi suae auditum patefaceret, cunctarum haereseon blasphemias insectabatur? Sed hoc erat illud quod Moyses ait: Tentat vos Dominus Deus vester si diligatis eum an non. Et ut Nestorium praetereamus, in quo plus semper admirationis quam utilitatis, plus famae quam experientiae fuit, quem opinione vulgi aliquamdiù magnum humana magis fecerat 652gratia quam divina, eos potius commemoremus qui multis profectibus multaque industria praediti non parvae tentationi Catholicis hominibus exstiterunt. Velut apud Pannonias majorum memoria Photinus Ecclesiam Sirmitanam tentasse memoratur: ubi cum magno omnium favore in sacerdotium fuisset ascitus, et aliquandiu tanquam Catholicus administraret, subito, sicut malus ille propheta aut somniator quem Moyses significat, creditam sibi plebem Dei persuadere coepit ut sequeretur deos alienos, id est, errores extraneos, quos antea nesciebat. Sed hoc usitatum. Illud vero perniciosum, quod ad tantum nefas non mediocribus adminiculis utebatur. Nam erat et ingenii viribus valens et doctrinae opibus excellens et eloquio 653praepotens; quippe qui utroque sermone copiose et graviter disputaret et scriberet: quod monumentis librorum suorum manifestatur, quos idem partim graeco, partim latino sermone composuit. Sed bene quod commissae ipsi oves Christi multum pro catholica fide vigilantes et cautae, cito ad praemonentis Moysi eloquia respexerunt, et prophetae atque pastoris sui licèt admirarentur eloquentiam, tentationem tamen non ignorarunt. Nam quem antea quasi arietem gregis sequebantur, eumdem deinceps veluti lupum fugere coeperunt. Neque solum Photini sed etiam Apollinaris exemplo istius Ecclesiasticae tentationis periculum discimus, et simul ad observandae diligentius fidei custodiam commonemur. Etenim ipse auditoribus suis magnos aestus et magnas generavit angustias; quippe cum eos huc Ecclesiae traheret auctoritas, huc magistri retraheret consuetudo; cumque inter utraque nutabundi et fluctuantes, quid potius sibi seligendum foret non expedirent. Sed forsitan ejusmodi ille vir erat qui dignus esset facile contemni. Imo vero tantus ac talis cui nimium cito in plurimis crederetur. Nam quid illo praestantius, acumine, exercitatione, doctrina? Quam multas ille haereses multis voluminibus oppresserit, quot inimicos fidei confutaverit errores, indicio est opus illud triginta non minus librorum, nobilissimum ac maximum, quo insanas Porphyrii calumnias magna probationum mole confudit. Longum est universa ipsius opera commemorare; quibus profecto summis aedificatoribus Ecclesiae par esse potuisset, nisi prophana illa haereticae curiositatis libidine novum nescio quid adinvenisset quo et cunctos labores suos velut cujusdam leprae admixtione foedaret et committeret ut doctrina ejus non tam aedificatio, quam tentatio potius Ecclesiastica diceretur. 654

XI.

Here perhaps someone may demand that the things which have been asserted in the words of holy Moses be demonstrated by some ecclesiastical examples. A fair demand, and not to be long deferred. For, to begin from things near and manifest, of what kind do we suppose the temptation lately was, when that unhappy Nestorius, suddenly turned from a sheep into a wolf, had begun to tear the flock of Christ, while those very men who were being gnawed still for the most part believed him a sheep, and therefore lay the more open to his bites? For who would easily think him to err whom he saw chosen by so great a judgment of the Empire, attended by so great a zeal of the priests; who, while he was celebrated with great love of the saints and the highest favor of the people, daily handled openly the divine utterances, and refuted every harmful error of the Jews and Gentiles? In what way at last would this man not have made anyone believe that he taught rightly, preached rightly, held rightly, who, that he might open a hearing for his one heresy, assailed the blasphemies of all the heresies? But this was that which Moses said: The Lord your God tries you, whether you love him or not. And, to pass over Nestorius — in whom there was always more of admiration than of usefulness, more of fame than of experience, whom for some while human 652favor rather than divine had made great in the opinion of the crowd — let us rather call to mind those who, endowed with many advances and much industry, stood forth as no small temptation to Catholic men. As in Pannonia, within the memory of our elders, Photinus is recorded to have tempted the Church of Sirmium: where, when he had been taken into the priesthood with the great favor of all, and for some while administered it as a Catholic, suddenly, like that evil prophet or dreamer whom Moses signifies, he began to persuade the people of God entrusted to him to follow strange gods, that is, foreign errors, which before they knew not. But this is usual. That, however, is pernicious, that for so great a wickedness he used no mean supports. For he was both strong in the powers of talent and excellent in the riches of learning and most mighty in eloquence 653; inasmuch as he disputed and wrote copiously and gravely in either tongue: which is made manifest by the monuments of his books, which he composed partly in the Greek, partly in the Latin tongue. But well it is that the sheep of Christ committed to him, much watchful and wary for the Catholic faith, quickly looked back to the words of the forewarning Moses, and, though they admired the eloquence of their prophet and shepherd, yet did not fail to recognize the temptation. For him whom before they followed as though the ram of the flock, the same they thereafter began to flee as though a wolf. And not only by the example of Photinus, but also of Apollinaris, do we learn the peril of this ecclesiastical temptation, and are at the same time admonished to the more diligent keeping of the guard of the faith. For he too generated great heats and great straits for his hearers; inasmuch as, on this side, the authority of the Church drew them, on that side the custom of the master drew them back; and, wavering and tossing between the two, they could not make out what they ought rather to choose for themselves. But perhaps he was a man of such a kind as to be worthy of being easily despised. Nay rather, he was so great and of such a kind that in very many things he was too quickly believed. For what was more excellent than he in acuteness, in exercise, in learning? How many heresies he crushed in many volumes, how many enemy errors of the faith he refuted, that work of no fewer than thirty books is witness, most noble and greatest, in which he confounded the mad calumnies of Porphyry with a great mass of proofs. It is long to recount all his works; by which assuredly he might have been equal to the highest builders of the Church, had he not, by that profane lust of heretical curiosity, invented I know not what new thing whereby he both befouled all his labors as though by the admixture of a certain leprosy, and brought it about that his doctrine should be called not so much an edification as rather an ecclesiastical temptation. 654

XII.

Hic a me forsitan deposcatur ut horum quos supra commemoravi haereses exponam, Nestorii scilicet, Apollinaris, et Photini. Hoc quidem ad rem de qua nunc agimus non attinet. Propositum etenim nobis est, non singulorum errores persequi, sed paucorum exempla proferre, quibus evidenter ac perspicue demonstretur illud quod Moyses ait, quia scilicet si quando Ecclesiasticus aliquis magister, et ipse interpretandis prophetarum mysteriis propheta, novi quiddam in Ecclesiam Dei tentet inducere, ad tentationem id nostram fieri Providentia divina patiatur. Utile igitur fuerit, in excursu, quid supra memorati haeretici sentiant breviter exponere, id est, Photinus, Apollinaris, Nestorius. Photini ergo secta haec est. Dicit Deum singulum esse et solitarium, et more Judaico confitendum. Trinitatis plenitudinem negat, neque ullam Dei Verbi aut ullam Spiritus sancti putat esse personam. Christum vero hominem tantummodo solitarium adserit, cui principium adscribit ex Maria: et hoc omnimodis dogmatizat, solam nos personam Dei Patris et solum Christum hominem colere debere. Haec ergo Photinus. Apollinaris vero in unitate quidem Trinitatis quasi consentire se jactitat, et hoc ipsum non plena fidei sanitate; sed in Domini incarnatione aperta professione blasphemat. Dicit enim in ipsa Salvatoris nostri carne aut animam humanam penitus non fuisse, aut certè talem fuisse cui mens et ratio non esset. Sed et ipsam Domini carnem non de sanctae Virginis Mariae carne susceptam, sed de coelo in Virginem descendisse dicebat; eamque nutabundus semper et dubius modo coaeternam Deo Verbo, modo de Verbi divinitate factam praedicabat. Nolebat enim in Christo esse duas substantias, unam divinam, alteram humanam, unam ex Patre, alteram ex matre; sed ipsam Verbi naturam putabat esse discissam, quasi aliud ejus permaneret in Deo, aliud vero versum fuisset in carnem; ut cum veritas dicat ex duabus substantiis unum esse Christum, ille contrarius veritati ex una Christi divinitate duas adserat factas esse substantias. Haec itaque Apollinaris. Nestorius autem contrario Apollinari morbo, dum sese duas in Christo substantias distinguere simulat, duas introducit repente personas, et inaudito scelere duos 655esse vult filios Dei, duos Christos, unum Deum, alterum hominem, unum qui ex Patre, alterum qui sit generatus ex matre. Atque ideo asserit sanctam Mariam non Theotocon, sed Christotocon esse dicendam: quia scilicet ex ea non ille Christus qui Deus, sed ille qui erat homo, natus sit. Quod si quis eum putat in litteris suis unum Christum dicere et unam Christi praedicare personam, non temere credat. Aut enim istud fallendi arte machinatus est, ut per bona facilius suaderet et mala, sicut ait Apostolus: Per bonum mihi operatus est mortem, Rom. 7. Aut ergo, ut diximus, fraudulentiae causa quibusdam in locis scriptorum suorum unum Christum et unam Christi personam credere se jactitat, aut certe post partum jam Virginis ita in unum Christum duas perhibet convenisse personas, ut tamen conceptus seu partus Virginei tempore, et aliquantò posteà duos Christos fuisse contendat; ut cum scilicet Christus homo communis primum et solitarius natus sit, et necdum Dei Verbo personae unitate sociatus, postea in eum adsumentis Verbi persona descenderit; et licet nunc in Dei gloria maneat adsumptus, aliquandiu tamen nihil inter illum et caeteros homines interfuisse videatur.

XII.

Here perhaps it may be demanded of me that I set forth the heresies of those whom I have mentioned above, namely of Nestorius, Apollinaris, and Photinus. This indeed does not pertain to the matter of which we now treat. For our purpose is not to pursue the errors of each, but to bring forth the examples of a few, by which may be evidently and clearly demonstrated that which Moses says: namely, that if ever some ecclesiastical teacher, himself also a prophet in interpreting the mysteries of the prophets, attempt to bring some new thing into the Church of God, divine Providence suffers this to be done for our temptation. It will therefore be useful, in a digression, briefly to set forth what the above-mentioned heretics hold, that is, Photinus, Apollinaris, Nestorius. The sect of Photinus, then, is this. He says that God is single and solitary, and is to be confessed after the Jewish manner. He denies the fullness of the Trinity, and thinks there is no person of the Word of God nor any of the Holy Spirit. But Christ he asserts to be merely a solitary man, to whom he ascribes a beginning from Mary: and this he teaches in every way, that we ought to worship only the person of God the Father and only Christ as man. This, then, is Photinus. Apollinaris, indeed, boasts that he in a manner consents to the unity of the Trinity, and this itself not with the full soundness of faith; but in the incarnation of the Lord he blasphemes by open profession. For he says that in the very flesh of our Savior either there was no human soul at all, or certainly it was such as had no mind and reason. And he said that the very flesh of the Lord was not taken from the flesh of the holy Virgin Mary, but had descended from heaven into the Virgin; and, ever wavering and doubtful, he preached it now coeternal with God the Word, now made out of the divinity of the Word. For he would not have there be in Christ two substances, one divine, the other human, one from the Father, the other from the mother; but he thought the very nature of the Word to be cleft, as though one part of it remained in God, but another had been turned into flesh; so that, whereas the truth says that Christ is one out of two substances, he, contrary to the truth, asserts that out of the one divinity of Christ two substances were made. This, then, is Apollinaris. But Nestorius, with a disease contrary to Apollinaris, while he feigns to distinguish two substances in Christ, suddenly introduces two persons, and by an unheard-of wickedness will have there be two 655sons of God, two Christs, one God, the other man, one who is from the Father, the other who is begotten from the mother. And therefore he asserts that holy Mary is to be called not Theotocon, but Christotocon: namely because from her was born not that Christ who is God, but he who was man. But if anyone thinks that in his writings he says one Christ and preaches one person of Christ, let him not rashly believe it. For either he has contrived this by the art of deceiving, that through good things he might the more easily persuade evil ones as well, as the Apostle says: Through that which is good it wrought death in me, Rom. 7. Either, then, as we have said, for the sake of fraud, in certain places of his writings he boasts that he believes one Christ and one person of Christ, or certainly, after the Virgin's childbearing, he alleges that two persons so came together into one Christ, that nevertheless at the time of the Virgin's conception or childbearing, and for some while after, he contends that there were two Christs; so that, namely, Christ was first born a common and solitary man, and not yet joined to the Word of God by unity of person, and afterward the person of the assuming Word descended into him; and, although now he who was assumed abides in the glory of God, yet for some while there seems to have been no difference between him and other men.

XIII.

Haec ergo Nestorius, Apollmaris, Photinus adversus Catholicam fidem rabidi canes latrant: Photinus, Trinitatem non confitendo; Apollinaris, convertibilem Verbi dicendo naturam, et duas in Christo substantias non confitendo, et aut totam Christi animam aut certe mentem atque rationem in anima denegando, et adserendo pro sensu mentis fuisse Dei Verbum; Nestorius, duos Christos aut semper esse, aut aliquandiu fuisse adseverando. Ecclesia vero Catholica et de Deo et de Salvatore nostro recta sentiens, nec in Trinitatis mysterio nec in Christi incarnatione blasphemat. Nam et unam divinitatem in Trinitatis plenitudine et Trinitatis aequalitatem in una atque eadem majestate veneratur, et unum Christum Jesum, non duos, eumdemque Deum pariter atque hominem confitetur. Unam quidem in eo personam, sed duas substantias; duas substantias, sed unam credit esse personam: duas substantias, quia mutabile non est Verbum Dei, ut ipsum verteretur in carnem; unam personam, ne duos profitendo filios, quaternitatem videatur colere, non Trinitatem. Sed operae pretium est ut id ipsum etiam atque etiam distinctius et expressius enucleemus. In Deo una substantia, sed tres personae; in Christo duae substantiae, sed una persona. In Trinitate alius atque alius, non aliud atque aliud; in Salvatore aliud atque aliud, non alius atque alius. Quomodo in Trinitate alius atque alius, non aliud atque aliud? Quia scilicet alia et persona Patris, alia Filii, alia Spiritus sancti; sed tamen Patris et Filii et Spiritus sancti non alia et alia, sed una eademque natura. Quomodo in Salvatore aliud atque aliud, non alius atque alius? Quia videlicet altera substantia divinitatis, altera humanitatis; sed tamen deitas et humanitas non alter et alter, sed unus idemque Christus, unus idemque filius Dei, et unius ejusdemque Christi et filii Dei una eademque persona; sicut in homine aliud caro, et aliud anima, sed unus idemque homo anima et caro. In Petro et Paulo aliud 656anima, aliud caro; nec tamen duo Petri, caro et anima; aut alter Paulus anima, et alter caro; sed unus idemque Petrus, unus idemque Paulus, ex duplici diversaque subsistens animi corporisque natura. Ita igitur in uno eodemque Christo duae substantiae sunt; sed una divina, altera humana; una ex Patre Deo, altera ex matre Virgine; una coaeterna et aequalis Patri, altera ex tempore et minor Patre; una consubstantialis Patri, altera consubstantialis matri; unus tamen idemque Christus in utraque substantia. Non ergo alter Christus Deus, alter homo; non alter increatus, alter creatus; non alter impassibilis, alter passibilis; non alter aequalis Patri, alter minor Patre; non alter ex Patre, alter ex matre; sed unus idemque Christus Deus et homo, idem non creatus et creatus, idem incommutabilis et impassibilis, idem commutatus et passus, idem Patri et aequalis et minor, idem ex Patre ante secula genitus, idem in seculo ex matre generatus, perfectus Deus, perfectus homo. In Deo summa divinitas, in homine plena humanitas. Plena, inquam, humanitas: quippe quae animam simul habeat et carnem, sed carnem veram, nostram, maternam, animam vero intellectu praeditam, mente ac ratione pollentem. Est ergo in Christo verbum, anima, caro; sed hoc totum unus est Christus, unus filius Dei, et unus Salvator ac Redemptor noster. Unus autem, non corruptibili nescio qua divinitatis et humanitatis confusione, sed integra et singulari quadam unitate personae. Neque enim illa conjunctio alterum in alterum convertit atque mutavit (qui est error proprius Arianorum), sed ita in unum potius utrum que compegit, ut manente semper in Christo singularitate 657unius ejusdemque personae, in aeternum quoque permaneat proprietas uniuscujusque naturae; quo scilicet nec unquàm Deus corpus esse incipiat, nec aliquando corpus, corpus esse desistat. Quod etiam humanae conditionis demonstratur exemplo. Neque enim in praesenti tantum, sed in futuro quoque, unusquisque hominum ex anima constabit et corpore, nec tamen unquam aut corpus in animam aut anima vertetur in corpus; sed unoquoque hominum sine fine victuro, in unoquoque hominum sine fine necessario utriusque substantiae differentia permanebit. Ita in Christo quoque utriusque substantiae sua cuique in aeternum proprietas, salva tamen personae unitate, retinenda est.

XIII.

These things, then, do Nestorius, Apollmaris, Photinus, rabid dogs, bark against the Catholic faith: Photinus, by not confessing the Trinity; Apollinaris, by calling the nature of the Word convertible, and by not confessing two substances in Christ, and by denying either the whole soul of Christ or certainly the mind and reason in the soul, and by asserting that the Word of God was in place of the sense of the mind; Nestorius, by affirming that there are two Christs always, or that there were two for some while. But the Catholic Church, holding right views both of God and of our Savior, blasphemes neither in the mystery of the Trinity nor in the incarnation of Christ. For it venerates both one divinity in the fullness of the Trinity and the equality of the Trinity in one and the same majesty, and confesses one Christ Jesus, not two, and the same both God and man alike. One person indeed in him, but two substances; two substances, but it believes there is one person: two substances, because the Word of God is not mutable, that it should be turned into flesh; one person, lest, by professing two sons, it should seem to worship a quaternity, not a Trinity. But it is worth the labor that we unfold this very thing again and again more distinctly and more expressly. In God one substance, but three persons; in Christ two substances, but one person. In the Trinity, one person and another, not one thing and another; in the Savior, one thing and another, not one person and another. How, in the Trinity, one person and another, not one thing and another? Because, namely, the person of the Father is one, that of the Son another, that of the Holy Spirit another; but nevertheless of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit there is not one nature and another, but one and the same nature. How, in the Savior, one thing and another, not one person and another? Because, evidently, one substance is of the divinity, the other of the humanity; but nevertheless the deity and the humanity are not one person and another, but one and the same Christ, one and the same Son of God, and of the one and the same Christ and Son of God there is one and the same person; just as in man the flesh is one thing, and the soul another, but one and the same man is soul and flesh. In Peter and Paul the soul is one thing, 656the flesh another; nor yet are there two Peters, flesh and soul; nor one Paul the soul, and another Paul the flesh; but one and the same Peter, one and the same Paul, subsisting from the twofold and diverse nature of soul and body. So, therefore, in one and the same Christ there are two substances; but one divine, the other human; one from God the Father, the other from the Virgin mother; one coeternal and equal to the Father, the other in time and less than the Father; one consubstantial with the Father, the other consubstantial with the mother; yet one and the same Christ in either substance. Not, therefore, one Christ God, another man; not one uncreated, another created; not one impassible, another passible; not one equal to the Father, another less than the Father; not one from the Father, another from the mother; but one and the same Christ, God and man, the same uncreated and created, the same unchangeable and impassible, the same changed and suffering, the same both equal to the Father and less, the same begotten of the Father before the ages, the same generated in the age from the mother, perfect God, perfect man. In God the highest divinity, in man full humanity. Full humanity, I say: inasmuch as it has at once a soul and flesh, but true flesh, ours, maternal, and a soul endowed with intellect, strong in mind and reason. There is, therefore, in Christ word, soul, flesh; but this whole is one Christ, one Son of God, and one our Savior and Redeemer. One, however, not by some corruptible confusion of divinity and humanity, but by a certain whole and singular unity of person. For that conjunction did not convert and change the one into the other (which is the error proper to the Arians), but rather so compacted both into one, that, the singularity of one and the same person ever remaining in Christ, 657the property of each nature also abides forever; so that, namely, God may never begin to be body, nor may the body ever cease to be body. Which is also demonstrated by the example of the human condition. For not only in the present, but also in the future, each of men will consist of soul and body, nor yet will either the body ever be turned into the soul or the soul into the body; but, since each of men will live without end, in each of men the difference of both substances will of necessity remain without end. So in Christ too the property of each substance, proper to each, is to be retained forever, the unity of the person nevertheless being preserved.

XIV.

Sed cum personam saepius nominamus, et dicimus quod Deus per personam homo factus sit, vehementer verendum est ne hoc dicere videamur quod Deus verbum sola imitatione actionis quae sunt nostra susceperit, et quidquid illud est conversationis humanae, quasi adumbratus, non quasi verus homo fecerit; sicut in theatris fieri solet, ubi unus plures effingit repente personas, quarum ipse nulla est. Quotiescumque etenim aliqua suscipitur imitatio actionis alienae, ita aliorum officia aut opera patrantur, ut tamen hi qui agunt, non sint ipsi quos agunt. Neque enim (ut, verbi gratia, secularium et Manichaeorum utamur exemplis), cum actor tragicus Sacerdotem effingit aut regem, sacerdos aut rex est. Nam desinente actu simul et ea quam susceperat persona desistit. Absit hoc a nobis nefarium scelestumque ludibrium. Manichaeorum sit ista dementia; qui phantasiae praedicatores, aiunt Filium Dei Deum, personam hominis non substantia extitisse, sed actu putativo quodam et conversatione simulasse. Catholica vero fides ita Verbum Dei hominem factum esse dicit, ut quae nostra sunt, non fallaciter et adumbrate, sed vere expresseque susciperet, et quae erant humana, non quasi aliena imitaretur, sed potius ut sua gereret; et prorsus quod agebat, hoc etiam esset. Sicut ipsi nos quoque in eo quod loquimur, sapimus, vivimus, subsistimus, non imitamur homines, sed sumus. Neque enim Petrus et Joannes, ut eos potissimùm nominem, imitando erant homines, sed subsistendo. Neque enim Paulus simulabat Apostolum, aut fingebat Paulum; sed erat Apostolus, et subsistebat Paulus. Ita etiam Deus Verbum adsumendo et habendo carnem, loquendo, faciendo, patiendo per carnem, sine ulla tamen suae corruptione naturae, hoc omnino praestare dignatus est ut hominem perfectum non imitaretur aut fingeret, sed exhiberet, ut homo verus non videretur aut putaretur, sed esset atque subsisteret. Igitur sicut anima connexa carni, nec in carnem tamen versa, non imitatur hominem, sed est homo, et homo non per simulationem, sed per substantiam, ita etiam Verbum Deus, absque ulla sui conversione, 658uniendo se homini, non confundendo, non imitando factus est homo, sed subsistendo. Abjiciatur ergo tota penitùs personae illius intelligentia quae fingendo imitatione suscipitur, ubi semper alius est et aliud simulatur, ubi ille qui agit, nunquam is est quem agit. Absit etenim ut hoc fallaci modo Deus Verbum personam hominis suscepisse credatur; sed ita potius ut incommutabili sua manente substantia, et in se perfecti hominis suscipiendo naturam, ipse caro, ipse homo, ipse persona hominis existeret, non simulatoria, sed vera, non imitativa, sed substantiva, non denique quae cum actione desisteret, sed quae prorsus in substantia permaneret.

XIV.

But since we often speak of a person, and say that God was made man through a person, we must beware most earnestly lest we seem to say that God the Word took upon himself the things that are ours by a mere imitation of action, and that whatever belongs to human life he performed as one shadowed forth, not as a true man — as is wont to happen in the theaters, where one actor suddenly counterfeits several persons, of whom he himself is none. For as often as any imitation of another's action is undertaken, the offices or works of others are so performed that nevertheless those who play them are not themselves those whom they play. For neither (to use, for example's sake, the cases of the secular stage and of the Manichaeans), when a tragic actor represents a Priest or a king, is he a priest or a king. For when the acting ceases, the person which he had taken up ceases along with it. Far be from us this wicked and impious mockery. Let that madness belong to the Manichaeans, who, preachers of a phantom, say that the Son of God is God, and that the person of a man existed not in substance but was feigned by a certain putative action and manner of life. But the Catholic faith declares that the Word of God was so made man that he took upon himself what is ours not deceitfully and in shadow, but truly and expressly, and that he did not imitate what was human as though it were another's, but rather bore it as his own; and that altogether what he did, this also he was. Just as we ourselves too, in that we speak, know, live, and subsist, do not imitate men, but are men. For neither Peter nor John — to name them above all — were men by imitating, but by subsisting. Nor did Paul feign an Apostle, or counterfeit Paul; but he was an Apostle, and subsisted as Paul. So too God the Word, by assuming and possessing flesh, by speaking, acting, and suffering through the flesh, yet without any corruption of his own nature, deigned wholly to bring this to pass: that he did not imitate or counterfeit a perfect man, but exhibited one; that he was not seen or reckoned to be a true man, but was such and subsisted as such. Therefore, just as the soul joined to the flesh, and yet not turned into flesh, does not imitate a man but is a man — and a man not by pretense but by substance — so also the Word, God, without any conversion of himself, 658by uniting himself to man, not by confounding, not by imitating, was made man, but by subsisting. Let there be cast off, then, entirely and to the depths, that understanding of a person which is taken up by imitative feigning, where there is always one man and another thing is counterfeited, where he who plays is never the one whom he plays. For far be it that God the Word be believed to have taken the person of a man in this deceitful manner; but rather in this way — that, his own unchangeable substance remaining, and by taking up in himself the nature of a perfect man, he himself was flesh, he himself man, he himself the person of a man, not a feigned but a true person, not an imitative but a substantial one, not one, finally, that would cease with the action, but one that would wholly abide in substance.

XV.

Haec igitur in Christo personae unitas nequaquam post Virginis partum, sed in ipso Virginis utero compacta atque perfecta est. Vehementer enim praecavere debemus, ut Christum non modo unum, sed etiam semper unum confiteamur: quia intoleranda blasphemia est ut etiamsi nunc eum unum esse concedas, aliquando tamen non unum, sed duos fuisse contendas, unum scilicet post tempus baptismatis, duos vero sub tempore nativitatis. Quod immensum sacrilegium non aliter profecto vitare poterimus nisi unitum hominem Deo, sed unitate personae, non ab ascensu, vel resurrectione, vel baptismo, sed jam in matre, jam in utero, jam denique in ipsa virginali conceptione fateamur: propter quam personae unitatem indifferenter atque promiscue et quae Dei sunt propria tribuuntur homini, et quae carnis propria adscribuntur Deo. Inde est enim quod divinitus scriptum est et filium hominis descendisse de coelo, et Dominum majestatis crucifixum in terra, Joan. 3. 1 Cor. 2. Inde etiam est ut carne Domini facta, carne Domini creata, ipsum Verbum Dei factum, ipsa sapientia Dei impleta, scientia creata dicatur; sicut in praescientia manus ipsius et pedes ejus fossi esse referuntur. Per hanc, inquam, personae unitatem illud quoque similis mysterii ratione perfectum est, ut carne Verbi ex integra matre nascente, ipse Deus Verbum natus ex Virgine catholicissime credatur, impiissime denegetur. Quae cum ita sint, absit ut quisquam sanctam Mariam divinae gratiae privilegiis et speciali gloria fraudare conetur. Est enim singulari quodam Domini ac Dei nostri, Filii autem sui, munere verissime ac beatissime theotocos confitenda; sed non eo modo theotocos quo impia quaedam haeresis suspicatur, quae adserit eam Dei matrem sola appellatione dicendam, quod eum scilicet pepererit hominem qui postea factus est Deus; sicut dicimus Presbyteri matrem, aut Episcopi matrem, non jam Presbyterum aut Episcopum pariendo, sed eum generando hominem qui postea Presbyter vel Episcopus factus est. Non ita, inquam, sancta Maria theotocos, sed ideo potiùs quoniam, ut supra dictum est, jam in ejus sacrato utero sacrosanctum illud mysterium perpetratum est, quod propter singularem quamdam atque unicam personae unitatem, sicut Verbum in carne caro, ita homo in Deo, Deus est.

XV.

This unity of person in Christ, then, was by no means compacted and perfected after the Virgin's childbearing, but in the very womb of the Virgin. For we must most earnestly take heed to confess Christ not merely as one, but also as always one: for it is an intolerable blasphemy that, even if you now grant him to be one, you should nevertheless contend that at some time he was not one but two — one, that is, after the time of his baptism, but two at the time of his nativity. And this immense sacrilege we shall assuredly not be able to avoid in any other way than by confessing that man was united to God — but by a unity of person — not from the ascension, or the resurrection, or the baptism, but already in the mother, already in the womb, already, in short, in the very virginal conception: on account of which unity of person the things proper to God are ascribed to the man indifferently and interchangeably, and the things proper to the flesh are ascribed to God. For hence it is that it is written by God's own word that both the Son of man descended from heaven, and the Lord of majesty was crucified upon earth, John 3, 1 Cor. 2. Hence also it is that, the flesh of the Lord being made, the flesh of the Lord being created, the Word of God itself is said to be made, the very wisdom of God to be filled, knowledge to be created; just as in prophecy his hands and his feet are reported to have been pierced. Through this unity of person, I say, that too was accomplished by a like reckoning of mystery, that, the flesh of the Word being born of an undefiled mother, God the Word himself is most catholically believed, and most impiously denied, to be born of the Virgin. And since these things are so, far be it that anyone should attempt to defraud holy Mary of the privileges of divine grace and of her special glory. For she is, by a certain singular gift of our Lord and God, who is however her own Son, most truly and most blessedly to be confessed theotocos; yet not theotocos in the way that a certain impious heresy imagines, which asserts that she is to be called mother of God by mere appellation, because, that is, she bore him as man who afterward was made God — just as we speak of a Presbyter's mother, or a Bishop's mother, not because she bore a Presbyter or a Bishop, but because she brought forth as man him who afterward was made a Presbyter or a Bishop. Not so, I say, is holy Mary theotocos, but rather for this reason: because, as was said above, already in her consecrated womb that most sacred mystery was wrought, whereby, on account of a certain singular and unique unity of person, as the Word in the flesh is flesh, so the man in God is God.

XVI.

Sed jam ea quae supra de memoratis haeresibus 659vel de catholica fide breviter dicta sunt renovandae causa memoriae brevius strictiusque repetamus; quo scilicet et intelligantur iterata plenius, et firmius inculcata teneantur. Anathema igitur Photino non recipienti plenitudinem Trinitatis, et Christum hominem tantummodo solitarium praedicanti. Anathema Apollinari adserenti in Christo conversae divinitatis corruptionem, et auferenti perfectae humanitatis proprietatem. Anathema Nestorio neganti ex Virgine Deum natum, adserenti duos Christos, et explosa Trinitatis fide, quaternitatem nobis introducenti. Beata vero Catholica Ecclesia, quae unum Deum in Trinitatis plenitudine et item Trinitatis aequalitatem in una Divinitate veneratur; ut neque singularitas substantiae personarum confundat proprietatem, neque item Trinitatis distinctio unitatem separet Deitatis. Beata, inquam, Ecclesia, quae in Christo duas veras perfectasque substantias, sed unam Christi credit esse personam, ut neque naturarum distinctio unitatem personae dividat, neque item personae unitas differentiam confundat substantiarum. Beata, inquam, Ecclesia, quae ut unum semper Christum esse et fuisse fateatur, unitum hominem Deo, non post partum, sed jam in ipso matris utero confitetur. Beata, inquam, Ecclesia, quae Deum factum hominem non conversione naturae, sed personae ratione intelligit, personae autem non simulatoriae et transeuntis, sed substantivae ac permanentis. Beata, inquam, Ecclesia, quae hanc personae unitatem tantam vim habere praedicat ut propter eam miro ineffabilique mysterio et divina homini et Deo adscribat humana. Nam propter eam et hominem de coelo secundum Deum descendisse non abnegat, et Deum secundum hominem credit in terra factum, passum, et crucifixum. Propter ea denique et hominem Dei filium et Deum filium Virginis confitetur. Beata igitur ac veneranda, benedicta, et sacrosancta, et omnino supernae illi angelorum laudationi comparanda confessio, quae unum Dominum Deum trina sanctificatione glorificat. Idcirco etenim vel maxime unitatem Christi praedicat, ne mysterium Trinitatis excedat. Haec in excursu dicta sint, alias, 660si Deo placuerit, uberius tractanda et explicanda. Nunc ad propositum redeamus.

XVI.

But now let us, for the sake of renewing the memory, more briefly and closely repeat those things which were said above in brief concerning the aforementioned heresies 659or concerning the Catholic faith; that so they may both be understood more fully by repetition, and be held more firmly by being driven home. Anathema, therefore, to Photinus, who does not receive the fullness of the Trinity, and who preaches Christ as only a solitary man. Anathema to Apollinaris, who asserts in Christ a corruption of the divinity turned into flesh, and who takes away the property of a perfect humanity. Anathema to Nestorius, who denies that God was born of the Virgin, who asserts two Christs, and who, the faith of the Trinity being exploded, brings in upon us a quaternity. But blessed is the Catholic Church, which venerates one God in the fullness of the Trinity, and likewise the equality of the Trinity in one Divinity; so that neither may the singularity of substance confound the property of the persons, nor again may the distinction of the Trinity separate the unity of the Deity. Blessed, I say, is the Church, which believes in Christ two true and perfect substances, but one person of Christ, so that neither may the distinction of natures divide the unity of the person, nor again may the unity of the person confound the difference of the substances. Blessed, I say, is the Church, which, that it may confess Christ to be and to have been always one, confesses that man was united to God not after the childbearing, but already in the very womb of the mother. Blessed, I say, is the Church, which understands that God was made man not by a conversion of nature, but by reason of the person — a person, moreover, not feigned and transient, but substantial and abiding. Blessed, I say, is the Church, which preaches that this unity of person has such power that, on account of it, by a wondrous and unspeakable mystery, it ascribes both the divine things to the man and the human things to God. For on account of it, it does not deny that the man, according to God, descended from heaven, and it believes that God, according to the man, was made on earth, suffered, and was crucified. On account of it, finally, it confesses both the man to be the Son of God, and God to be the Son of the Virgin. Blessed, therefore, and to be venerated, blessed and most holy, and altogether to be compared to that heavenly praising of the angels, is the confession which glorifies the one Lord God with a threefold sanctification. For it is for this very reason above all that it preaches the unity of Christ: lest it exceed the mystery of the Trinity. Let these things be said in passing, to be treated and set forth more fully at another time, 660if it shall please God. Now let us return to our purpose.

XVII.

Dicebamus ergo in superioribus quod in Ecclesia Dei tentatio esset populi, error magistri, et tanto major tentatio quanto ipse esset doctior qui erraret. Quod primum Scripturae auctoritate, deinde ecclesiasticis docebamus exemplis, eorum scilicet commemoratione qui cum aliquandiu sanae fidei forent habiti, ad extremum tamen aut in alienam decidissent sectam, aut ipsi suam haeresim condidissent. Magna profecto res, et ad discendum utilis, et ad recolendum necessaria; quam etiam atque etiam exemplorum molibus illustrare atque inculcare debemus; ut omnes vere Catholici noverint se cum Ecclesia doctores recipere, non cum doctoribus Ecclesiae fidem deserere debere. Sed ego ita arbitror, quod cum multos in hoc tentandi genere proferre valeamus, nemo pene sit qui Origenis tentationi valeat comparari; in quo plura adeo praeclara, adeo singularia, adeo mira exstiterunt, ut inter initia habendam cunctis adsertionibus ejus fidem quivis ille facile judicaret. Nam si vita facit auctoritatem, magna illi industria, magna pudicitia, patientia, tolerantia. Si genus vel 661eruditio, quid eo nobilius, qui primum in ea domo natus est quae est illustrata martyrio, deinde pro Christo non solum patre sed omni quoque facultate privatus, tantum inter sanctae paupertatis profecit augustias ut pro nomine Dominicae confessionis saepius, ut ferunt, adfligeretur. Neque vero haec in illo sola erant quae cuncta postea tentationi forent, sed tanta etiam vis ingemi, tam profundi, tam acris, tam elegantis ut omnes pene multum longeque superaret; tanta doctrinae ac totius eruditionis magnificentia ut pauca forent divinae, pene fortasse nulla humanae philosophiae quae non penitus adsequeretur: cujus scientiae cum Graeca concederent, Hebraea quoque elaborata sunt. Eloquentiam vero quid memorem, cujus 662fuit tam amoena, tam lactea, tam dulcis oratio ut mihi ex ore ipsius non tam verba quam mella quaedam fluxisse videantur? Quae non ille persuasu difficilia disputandi viribus elimpidavit, quae factu ardua non ut facillima viderentur effecit? Sed forsitan argumentorum tantummodo nexibus adsertiones suas texuit? Imo plane nemo unquam magistrorum fuit qui pluribus divinae legis uteretur exemplis. Sed, credo, pauca conscripsit? Nemo mortalium plura; ut mihi sua omnia non solum non perlegi, sed ne inveniri quidem posse videantur: cui ne quicquam ad scientiae instrumenta deesset, etiam plenitudo exabundavit aetatis. Sed forsitan discipulis parum felix? Quis unquam felicior? Nempe innumeri ex sinu suo doctores, innumeri sacerdotes, confessores et martyres extiterunt. Jam vero quanta apud omnes illius admiratio, quanta gloria, quanta gratia fuerit, quis exsequi valeat? Quis non ad eum paulo religiosor ex ultimis mundi partibus advolavit? Quis Christianorum non pene ut prophetam, quis philosophorum non ut magistrum veneratus est? Quam autem non solum privatae conditioni, sed ipsi quoque fuerit reverendus imperio declarant historiae, quae eum a matre Alexandri Imperatoris accitum ferunt, coelestis utique sapientiae merito, cujus et ille gratia et amore illa flagrabat. Sed et ejusdem Epistolae testimonium perhibent quas ad Philippum Imperatorem, qui primus Romanorum Principum Christianus 663fuit, Christiani magisterii auctoritate conscripsi. De cujus incredibili quadam scientia si quis referentibus nobis Christianum non accepit testimonium, saltem testificantibus philosophis gentilem recipiat confessionem. Ait namque impius ille Porphyrius excitum se fama ipsius Alexandriam puerum fere perrexisse, ibique eum vidisse jam senem, sed plane talem tantumque qui arcem totius scientiae condidisset. Dies me citius defecerit quam ea quae in illo viro praeclara exstiterunt vel ex minima saltem parte perstringam: quae tamen omnia non solum ad Religionis gloriam, sed etiam ad tentationis magnitudinem pertinebant. Quotus enim quisque tanti ingenii, tantae doctrinae, tantae gratiae virum aut facilè deponeret, ac non illa potius uteretur sententia, se cum Origene errare malle, quam cum aliis vera sentire? et quid plura? Eo res decidit ut tantae personae, tanti doctoris, tanti prophetae, non humana aliqua, sed, ut exitus docuit, nimium periculosa tentatio plurimos a fidei integritate deduceret. Quamobrem hic Origenes tantus ac talis, dum gratia Dei insolentius abutitur, dum ingenio suo nimium indulget, sibique satis credit, cum parvi pendit antiquam Christianae Religionis simplicitatem, dum se plus cunctis sapere praesumit, dum ecclesiasticas traditiones et veterum magisteria contemnens, quaedam Scripturarum capitula novo more interpretatur, meruit ut de se quoque Ecclesiae Dei 664diceretur: Si surrexerit in modio tuî propheta, Deut 13. Et paulo post: Non audies, inquit, verba prophetae illius. Et item: Quia tentat vos, inquit, Dominus Deus vester utrum diligatis eum an non. Vere non solum tentatio, sed etiam magna tentatio deditam sibi atque in se pendentem Ecclesiam admiratione ingenii, scientiae, eloquentiae, conversationis, et gratiae, nihil de se suspicantem, nihil verentem, subito a veteri Religione in novam prophanitatem sensim paulatimque traducere. Sed dicet aliquis corruptos esse Origenis libros. Non resisto; quin potius et malo. Nam id a quibusdam et traditum et scriptum est, non Catholicis tantum, verum etiam Haereticis. Sed illud est quod nunc debemus advertere etsi non ipsum, libros tamen sub nomine suo editos, magnae esse tentationi: qui multis blasphemiarum vulneribus scatentes, non ut alieni, sed quasi sui et leguntur et amantur; ut etsi in errore concipiendo Origenis non fuit sensus, ad errorem tamen persuadendum Origenis auctoritas valere videatur.

XVII.

We were saying, then, in the earlier passages, that in the Church of God there is a testing of the people, an error of the teacher, and a testing all the greater the more learned is he who errs. Which we were teaching first by the authority of Scripture, then by ecclesiastical examples — namely, by the recalling of those who, though for some while they were held to be of sound faith, yet in the end either fell away into another sect, or themselves founded their own heresy. A great matter, assuredly, and useful for learning, and necessary for pondering; which we ought again and again to illustrate and drive home with masses of examples, so that all who are truly Catholic may know that they ought to receive teachers together with the Church, not to desert the faith of the Church together with the teachers. But I judge thus: that although we are able to bring forward many in this kind of testing, there is almost no one who can be compared to the testing of Origen; in whom things so many and so illustrious, so singular, so wondrous, existed, that anyone would easily have judged that faith was to be placed in all his assertions from the very outset. For if a life makes authority, great was his industry, great his chastity, patience, endurance. If birth or 661learning, what more noble than he, who was first born in that house which was made illustrious by martyrdom, then, for Christ's sake, deprived not only of his father but of all his substance as well, made such progress amid the straits of holy poverty that, for the name of the Lord's confession, he was, as they report, often afflicted? Nor indeed were these the only things in him which would afterward all become matter of testing, but also so great a force of intellect, so profound, so keen, so refined, that he far and greatly surpassed almost all; so great a magnificence of learning and of all erudition that there were few points of divine philosophy, and perhaps almost none of human, that he did not thoroughly master: and, the Greek disciplines yielding to his knowledge, the Hebrew too were elaborated by him. But why should I mention his eloquence, 662whose speech was so charming, so milk-sweet, so pleasant, that to me not so much words as certain honey seems to have flowed from his mouth? What things difficult of persuasion did he not clarify by the powers of disputation? What things hard in the doing did he not make to seem most easy? But perhaps he wove his assertions together only by the connections of arguments? Nay, plainly there was never any of the teachers who used more examples of the divine law. But, I suppose, he wrote little? No mortal wrote more; so that to me all his works seem not only never to have been read through, but not even able to be found: to whom, lest anything at all be lacking for the instruments of knowledge, even the fullness of years abounded. But perhaps he was little happy in his disciples? Who was ever happier? Indeed from his bosom there came forth teachers innumerable, priests innumerable, confessors and martyrs. Now truly, how great was the admiration of him among all, how great his glory, how great his favor, who could recount? Who did not, being somewhat religious, fly to him from the farthest parts of the world? Who among Christians did not venerate him almost as a prophet, who among philosophers not as a master? And how reverend he was not only to private station but to the empire itself, the histories declare, which report that he was summoned by the mother of the Emperor Alexander, by the merit surely of his heavenly wisdom, with the grace and love of which she too burned. And they bear witness also to the testimony of his Epistles, which, to the Emperor Philip, who was the first of the Roman Princes to be a Christian, 663I wrote with the authority of a Christian teacher [conscripsi; read conscripsit, "he wrote"]. And if anyone, when we report of his certain incredible knowledge, does not accept the testimony of a Christian, let him at least receive the confession of a Gentile from the testifying philosophers. For that impious Porphyry says that, roused by his fame, he went as almost a boy to Alexandria, and there saw him already an old man, but plainly such and so great a one as had founded the citadel of all knowledge. The day would fail me sooner than I could touch, even in its smallest part, upon those things which existed illustrious in that man: all of which, however, pertained not only to the glory of Religion, but also to the magnitude of the testing. For how few would either easily set aside a man of such intellect, of such learning, of such favor, and not rather use that saying — that he would rather err with Origen than think truly with others? And why say more? To this the matter came: that a testing — not any human one, but, as the outcome taught, an exceedingly perilous one — of so great a person, so great a teacher, so great a prophet, led very many away from the integrity of the faith. Wherefore this Origen, so great and so notable, while he abuses the grace of God too insolently, while he indulges his own genius too much, and trusts himself enough, while he reckons of little worth the ancient simplicity of the Christian Religion, while he presumes to be wiser than all, while, despising the ecclesiastical traditions and the teachings of the ancients, he interprets certain chapters of the Scriptures after a new manner, he deserved that of him too it should be said to the Church of God 664: If there arise within your measure [Vulg. in medio tui, "in the midst of thee"] a prophet, Deut. 13. And a little after: You shall not hear, he says, the words of that prophet. And again: Because the Lord your God tests you, he says, whether you love him or not. Truly not only a testing, but even a great testing, suddenly, little by little, and by degrees, to lead the Church — devoted to him and hanging upon him in admiration of his intellect, knowledge, eloquence, manner of life, and favor, suspecting nothing about him, fearing nothing — from the ancient Religion into a new profanity. But someone will say that Origen's books have been corrupted. I do not resist; nay rather I even prefer it. For this has both been handed down and written by certain persons, not only Catholics, but also Heretics. But this is what we must now observe: that even if not he himself, yet books published under his name are of great testing; which, teeming with many wounds of blasphemies, are read and loved not as another's but as his own; so that, although Origen's mind was not in conceiving the error, yet for the persuading of the error Origen's authority seems to have force.

XVIII.

Sed et Tertulliani quoque eadem ratio est Nam sicut ille apud Graecos, ita hic apud Latinos nostrorum omnium facile princeps judicandus est. Quid enim hoc viro doctius, quid in divinis atque humanis rebus exercitatius? Nempe omnem philosophiam et cunctas philosophorum sectas, auctores, adsertoresque sectarum, omnesque eorum disciplinas, omnem historiarum ac studiorum varietatem mira quadam mentis capacitate complexus est. Ingenio vero nonne tam gravi ac vehementi excelluit ut sibi nihil pene ad expugnandum proposuerit quod non aut acumine inruperit, aut pondere eliserit? Jam porro orationis suae laudes quis exsequi valeat, quae, tanta nescio qua rationum necessitate concerta est ut ad consensum suî quos suadere non potuerit impellat; cujus quot pene verba, tot sententiae sunt; quot sensus, tot victoriae. Sciunt hoc Marciones, Apelles, Praxeae, Hermogenes, Judaei, Gentiles, Gnostici, caeterique; quorum ille blasphemias multis ac magnis voluminum suorum molibus, velut quibusdam fulminibus, evertit. Et tamen hic quoque post haec omnia, hic, inquam, Tertullianus, catholici dogmatis, id est, universalis ac vetustae fidei parum tenax, ac disertior multo quam felicior, mutata deinceps sententia, fecit ad extremum quod de eo beatus confessor Hilarius quodam loco scribit: Se quenti, inquit, errore detraxit scriptis probabilibus auctoritatem, Hilarius in Matth. c. 5. Et fuit ipse quoque in Ecclesia magna tentatio. Sed de hoc nolo plura dicere. Hoc tantum commemorabo, quod contra Moysi praeceptum exsurgentes in Ecclesia novellas Montani furias et insana illa insanarum mulierum novitii dogmatis somnia veras prophetias adseverando, meruit ut de se 665quoque et scripturis suis diceretur: Si surrexerit in medio tuî propheta. Et mox: Non audies verba prophetae illius. Quare? Quia, inquit, tentat vos Dominus vester utrum diligatis eum an non.

XVIII.

But of Tertullian too the same reckoning holds. For as the former is among the Greeks, so this man among the Latins is easily to be judged the chief of all our own. For what is more learned than this man, what more practiced in things divine and human? Indeed he embraced all philosophy and all the sects of the philosophers, the authors and defenders of the sects, and all their disciplines, all the variety of histories and studies, with a certain wondrous capacity of mind. And did he not excel in a genius so weighty and vehement that he proposed to himself almost nothing to overthrow which he did not either break through by acuteness or crush by weight? Now further, who could recount the praises of his speech, which is knit together with such — I know not what — necessity of reasonings that it drives to agreement with itself those whom it could not persuade; of whom there are almost as many judgments as words, as many victories as meanings? This the Marcions, the Apelleses, the Praxeases, the Hermogeneses, the Jews, the Gentiles, the Gnostics, and the rest know; whose blasphemies he overthrew with the many and mighty masses of his volumes, as with certain thunderbolts. And yet this man too, after all these things — this Tertullian, I say — holding too little firmly to the catholic doctrine, that is, to the universal and ancient faith, and much more eloquent than fortunate, having afterward changed his opinion, did at the last what the blessed confessor Hilary writes of him in a certain place: By a following error, he says, he detracted authority from his approvable writings, Hilary on Matthew, ch. 5. And he too was a great testing in the Church. But of this I do not wish to say more. This only will I recall: that, rising up in the Church against the precept of Moses, and asserting to be true prophecies the newfangled ravings of Montanus and those insane dreams of insane women concerning the novel doctrine, he deserved that of him too and of his writings it should be said 665: If there arise in the midst of you a prophet. And soon: You shall not hear the words of that prophet. Why? Because, he says, the Lord your God tests you, whether you love him or not.

XIX.

His igitur tot ac tantis caeterisque ejusmodi Ecclesiasticorum exemplorum molibus evidenter advertere, et secundum Deuteronomii leges luce clarius intelligere debemus, quod si quando aliquis Ecclesiasticus magister a fide aberraverit, ad tentationem id nostram fieri Providentia divina patiatur, utrum diligamus Deum an non in toto corde et in tota anima nostra.

XIX.

By these, then, so many and so great masses of ecclesiastical examples, and by the rest of the same kind, we ought evidently to perceive, and, according to the laws of Deuteronomy, to understand more clearly than light, that if ever some ecclesiastical teacher has strayed from the faith, divine Providence suffers it to happen for our testing, whether we love God or not with all our heart and with all our soul.

XX.

Quae cum ita sint, ille est verus et germanus Catholicus qui veritatem Dei, qui Ecclesiam, qui Christi corpus diligit, qui divinae Religioni, qui catholicae fidei nihil praeponit, non hominis cujuspiam auctoritatem, non amorem, non ingenium, non eloquentiam, non philosophiam; sed haec cuncta despiciens, et in fide fixus, stabilis, permanens, quicquid universaliter antiquitus Ecclesiam Catholicam tenuisse cognoverit, id solum sibi tenendum credendumque decernit; quicquid vero ab aliquo deinceps uno praeter omnes vel contra omnes sanctos novum et inauditum subindici senserit, id non ad Religionem sed ad tentationem potiùs intelligit pertinere, tum praecipue beati Apostoli Pauli eruditus eloquiis: hoc est enim quod in prima ad Corinthios Epistola scribit: Oportet, inquit, et haereses esse, ut probati manifesti fiant in vobis, 1 Cor. 11; ac si diceret: Ob hoc haereseon non statim divinitus eradicantur auctores, ut probati manifesti fiant, id est, ut unusquisque quam tenax et fidelis et fixus catholicae fidei sit amator appareat. Et revera, cùm quaeque novitas ebullit, statìm cernitur frumentorum gravitas et levitas palearum; tunc sine magno molimine excutitur ab area quod nullo pondere intra aream tenebatur. Namque alii illico prorsus avolant; alii vero tantummodo excussi, et perire metuunt, et redire erubescunt saucii, 666semineces ac semivivi; quippe qui tantam veneni hauserint quantitatem quae nec occidat nec digeratur, nec mori cogat nec vivere sinat, heu miseranda conditio! quantis illi curarum aestibus, quantis turbinibus exagitantur? Nunc etenim, qua ventus impulerit, incitato errore rapiuntur; nunc in semetipsos reversi, tanquam contrarii fluctus, reliduntur, nunc temeraria praesumptione et ea quae incerta videntur adprobant; nunc irrationali metu, etiam quae certa sunt expavescunt; incerti qua eant, qua redeant, quid adpetant, quid fugiant, quid teneant, quid dimittant. Quae quidem dubii et male penduli cordis afflictio divinae erga se miserationis est medicina, si sapiant. Idcirco etenim extra tutissimum catholicae fidei portum diversis cogitationum quatiuntur, verberantur, ac pene enecantur procellis, ut excussa in altum elatae mentis vela deponant, quae male novitatum ventis expanderant, seseque intra fidissimam stationem placidae ac bonae matris reducant et teneant, atque amaros illos turbulentosque errorum fluctus primitus revomant, ut possint deinceps vivae et salientis aquae fluenta potare. Dediscant bene quod didicerant non bene; et ex toto Ecclesiae dogmate quod intellectu capi potest capiant, quod non potest credant.

XX.

And since these things are so, he is the true and genuine Catholic who loves the truth of God, who loves the Church, who loves the body of Christ, who sets nothing before the divine Religion, before the catholic faith — not the authority of any man, not his love, not his genius, not his eloquence, not his philosophy; but, despising all these things, and fixed, stable, and abiding in the faith, he determines that whatever he has come to know the Catholic Church has held universally and from antiquity, this alone is to be held and believed by him; and whatever, from that point on, he perceives to be introduced as new and unheard-of by some one man, apart from all or against all the saints, this he understands to pertain not to Religion but rather to testing — being taught above all by the sayings of the blessed Apostle Paul: for this is what he writes in the first Epistle to the Corinthians: There must, he says, be also heresies, that they who are approved may be made manifest among you, 1 Cor. 11; as if he were saying: For this reason the authors of heresies are not at once rooted out by God, that they who are approved may be made manifest — that is, that each one may appear how tenacious and faithful and fixed a lover of the catholic faith he is. And in truth, when any novelty boils up, at once the heaviness of the wheat and the lightness of the chaff are discerned; then, without great effort, that which was held by no weight within the threshing-floor is shaken off from it. For some fly right away at once; but others, only shaken, both fear to perish and blush to return, wounded, 666half-dead and half-alive; inasmuch as they have drunk down such a quantity of the poison as neither kills nor is digested, neither compels to die nor suffers to live. Alas, pitiable condition! By what surging tides of cares, by what whirlwinds, are they tossed about? For now, wherever the wind has driven, they are carried off by the error let loose; now, returned into themselves, they are dashed back like contrary waves; now by rash presumption they approve even those things which seem uncertain; now by irrational fear they dread even those things which are certain; uncertain which way to go, which way to return, what to seek, what to flee, what to hold, what to let go. And this affliction of a doubtful and ill-poised heart is the medicine of the divine compassion toward them, if they are wise. For it is for this reason that, outside the most safe harbor of the catholic faith, they are shaken, beaten, and almost slain by the diverse storms of their thoughts, that they may lower the sails of a mind lifted up and borne on high, which they had ill spread to the winds of novelties, and may draw themselves back and hold themselves within the most faithful anchorage of their calm and good mother, and first of all vomit up again those bitter and turbulent floods of errors, that they may thereafter be able to drink the streams of living and leaping water. Let them unlearn well what they had learned ill; and of the whole doctrine of the Church, let them grasp what can be grasped by understanding, and let them believe what cannot.

XXI.

Quae cum ita sint, iterum atque iterum eadem mecum revolvens et reputans, mirari satis nequeo tantam quorumdam hominum vesaniam, tantam excaecatae mentis impietatem, tantam postremo errandi libidinem ut contenti non sint tradita semel et accepta antiquitus credendi regula, sed nova ac nova in diem quaerant, semperque aliquid gestiant Religioni addere, mutare, detrahere; quasi non coeleste dogma sit quod semel revelatum esse sufficiat, sed terrena institutio, quae aliter perfici nisi assidua emendatione, imo potius reprehensione non possit, cum divina clament oracula: Ne transferas terminos quos posuerunt patres tui, Prov. 22; et: Super judicantem ne judices, Eccli. 8; et: Scirdentem sepem mordebit eum serpens, Eccles. 10, et illud apostolicum, quo omnes omnium haereseon sceleratae novitates velut quodam spiritali gladio saepe truncatae semperque truncandae sunt: O Timothee, depositum custodi, devitans prophanas vocum novitates et oppositiones falsi nominis scientiae, quam quidam promittentes, circa fidem exciderunt, 1, Tim. 6. Et post haec inveniuntur aliqui tanta inveteratae frontis duritia, tanta impudentiae incude, tanto adamante pertinaciae, qui tantis eloquiorum coelestium molibus non succumbant, tantis ponderibus non fatiscant, tantis malleis non conquassentur, tantis postremo fulminibus non conterantur? Devita, inquit, prophanas vocum novitates: Non dixit antiquitates, non dixit vetustates; imo plane quid e contrario sequeretur ostendit. Nam si vitanda est novitas, tenenda est antiquitas; et si prophana est novitas, sacrata est vetustas. Et oppositiones, inquit, falsi nominis scientiae. Vere falsum nomen apud doctrinas Haereticorum; ut ignorantia scientiae, et caligo serenitatis, et tenebrae luminis appellatione fucentur. Quam quidam, inquit, promittentes, circa fidem exciderunt. 667Quid promittentes exciderunt, nisi novam nescio quam ignoratamque doctrinam? Audias etenim quosdam ipsorum dicere: Venite, o insipientes et miseri, qui vulgo Catholici vocitamini, et discite fidem veram, quam praeter nos nullus intelligit, quae multis ante seculis latuit, nuper vero revelata et ostensa est; sed discite furtìm atque secretìm: delectabit enim vos. Et item: Cum didiceritis, latenter docete; ne mundus audiat, nec Ecclesia sciat: paucis namque concessum est tanti mysterii capere secretum. Nonne haec verba sunt illius meretricis quae apud Salomonis Proverbia vocat ad se praetereuntes viam qui dirigunt iter suum? Qui est, inquit, vestrum insipientissimus, divertat ad me. Inopes autem sensu exhortatur dicens: Panes occultos libenter attingite, et aquam dulcem furtim bibite. Quid deinde? At ille, inquit, nescit quoniam terrigenae apud eam pereunt, Prov. 9. Qui sunt isti terrigenae? Exponat Apostolus: Qui circa fidem, inquit, exciderunt, 2 Tim. 2.

XXI.

And since these things are so, revolving and pondering these same matters within myself again and again, I cannot sufficiently wonder at the great madness of certain men, the great impiety of a blinded mind, the great lust, in short, for erring — that they are not content with the rule of belief handed down once and received from antiquity, but seek new things upon new by the day, and always long to add something to Religion, to change, to take away; as though it were not a heavenly doctrine, which it suffices to have been once revealed, but an earthly institution, which cannot be perfected otherwise than by continual amendment, nay rather by continual censure — whereas the divine oracles cry out: Remove not the bounds which your fathers have set, Prov. 22; and: Judge not against him who judges, Ecclus. 8; and: Him who breaks down Scirdentem a hedge, a serpent shall bite him, Eccles. 10; and that apostolic word, by which all the wicked novelties of all the heresies have often been, and are always to be, cut down as with a certain spiritual sword: O Timothy, keep the deposit, avoiding the profane novelties of words and the oppositions of knowledge falsely so called, which certain men professing have erred concerning the faith, 1 Tim. 6. And after these things, are there found some of such hardness of an inveterate brow, of such an anvil of shamelessness, of such adamant of obstinacy, that they do not succumb to such masses of the heavenly sayings, do not weary under such weights, are not shattered by such hammers, are not, in short, crushed by such thunderbolts? Avoid, he says, the profane novelties of words: he did not say antiquities, he did not say the things of old; nay, plainly he showed what would follow to the contrary. For if novelty is to be avoided, antiquity is to be held; and if novelty is profane, the old is sacred. And the oppositions, he says, of knowledge falsely so called. Truly the name is false among the doctrines of the Heretics; so that ignorance is decked with the appellation of knowledge, and fog with that of clear sky, and darkness with that of light. Which certain men, he says, professing, have erred concerning the faith. 667Professing what, did they err, but some new and unknown doctrine? For you may hear certain of them saying: Come, O foolish and wretched, who are commonly called Catholics, and learn the true faith, which no one but us understands, which for many ages before lay hidden, but has lately been revealed and shown; but learn it by stealth and in secret: for it will delight you. And again: When you have learned it, teach it in hiding; lest the world hear, and the Church know: for to few is it granted to grasp the secret of so great a mystery. Are not these the words of that harlot who, in the Proverbs of Solomon, calls to herself those passing by the way, who direct their journey aright? Whoever is, she says, the most foolish among you, let him turn aside to me. And those void of sense she exhorts, saying: Touch gladly the hidden loaves, and drink stolen sweet water. What then? But he, she says, knows not that the earth-born perish with her, Prov. 9. Who are these earth-born? Let the Apostle expound: Who, he says, have erred concerning the faith, 2 Tim. 2.

XXII.

Sed operae pretium est totum ipsum Apostoli capitulum diligentius pertractare. O Timothee, inquit, depositum custodi, devitans profanas vocum novitates. O! Exclamatio ista et praescientiae est pariter et charitatis. Praevidebat enim futuros, quos etiam praedolebat, errores. Quis est hodie Timotheus nisi vel generaliter universa Ecclesia, vel specialiter totum corpus Praepositorum, qui integram divini cultûs scientiam vel habere ipsi debent vel aliis infundere? Quid est, depositum custodi? Custodi, inquit, propter fures, propter inimicos, ne dormientibus hominibus, superseminent zizania super illud tritici bonum semen quod seminaverat filius hominis in agro suo, Depositum, inquit, custodi. Quid est depositum? id est, quod tibi creditum est, non quod a te inventum; quod accepisti, non quod excogitasti; rem non ingenii, sed doctrinae, non usurpationis privatae, sed publicae traditionis; rem ad te perductam, non a te prolatam: in qua non auctor debes esse, sed custos; non institutor, sed sectator; non ducens, sed sequens. Depositum, inquit, custodi; catholicae fidei talentum inviolatum illibatumque conserva. Quod tibi creditum, hoc penes te maneat, hoc a te tradatur. Aurum accepisti, aurum redde: nolo mihi pro aliis alia subjicias: nolo pro auro aut impudenter plumbum aut fraudulenter aeramenta supponas: nolo auri speciem, sed naturam plane. O Timothee, O Sacerdos, O Tractator, O Doctor, si te divinum munus idoneum fecerit, ingenio, exercitatione, doctrina, esto spiritalis tabernaculi Beseleel, pretiosas divi dogmatis gemmas exsculpe, fideliter coapta, adorna sapienter, adjice splendorem, gratiam, venustatem. Intelligatur, te exponente illustrius, quod ante obscurius credebatur. Per te posteritas intellectum gratuletur quod ante vetustas non intellectum venerabatur. Eadem tamen quae didicisti doce, ut cum dicas nove, non dicas nova.

XXII.

But it is worth the labor to treat the whole chapter of the Apostle itself more carefully. O Timothy, he says, keep the deposit, avoiding the profane novelties of words. O! That exclamation is at once of foreknowledge and of charity. For he foresaw the errors that were to come, over which also he grieved beforehand. Who is Timothy today, but either generally the whole universal Church, or specially the entire body of the Prelates, who ought themselves both to have the whole knowledge of divine worship and to pour it into others? What is, keep the deposit? Keep it, he says, because of thieves, because of enemies, lest, while men sleep, they oversow tares upon that good seed of the wheat which the Son of man had sown in his field. The deposit, he says, keep. What is the deposit? That is, what has been entrusted to you, not what has been invented by you; what you have received, not what you have devised; a thing not of genius, but of teaching; not of private usurpation, but of public tradition; a thing brought to you, not brought forth by you: in which you ought to be not the author, but the keeper; not the founder, but the follower; not the leader, but the one who follows. The deposit, he says, keep; preserve the talent of the catholic faith inviolate and unimpaired. What has been entrusted to you, let this remain with you, let this be handed on by you. You have received gold, render gold: I do not want you to substitute for me one thing for another; I do not want you to put down, in place of gold, either shamelessly lead or fraudulently base metal: I do not want the appearance of gold, but plainly its nature. O Timothy, O Priest, O Expositor, O Teacher, if the divine gift has made you fit, by genius, by practice, by learning, be the Bezalel of the spiritual tabernacle; carve out the precious gems of the divine doctrine, fit them together faithfully, adorn them wisely, add splendor, grace, beauty. Let that be understood more clearly, by your setting forth, which before was believed more obscurely. Through you let posterity rejoice to have understood what before antiquity venerated without understanding. Yet teach the same things which you have learned, so that, though you speak in a new way, you speak not new things.

XXIII.

Sed forsitan dicit aliquis: Nullusne ergo in Ecclesiâ Christi profectus habebitur Religionis? Habeatur plane, et maximus. Nam quis ille est tam invidus hominibus, tàm exosus Deo, qui istud prohibere conetur? Sed ita tamen ut verè profectus sit ille fidei, 668non permutatio. Siquidem ad profectum pertinet ut in semetipsum unaquaeque res amplificetur; ad permutationem verò, ut aliquid ex alio in aliud transvertatur. Crescat igitur oportet et multùm vehementerque proficiat tam singulorum quam omnium, tam unius hominis quam totius Ecclesiae, aetatum ac seculorum gradibus, intelligentia, scientia, sapientia, sed in suo duntaxa: genere, in eodem scilicet dogmate, eodem sensu, eademque sententia. Imitetur animarum religio rationem corporum: quae licet annorum processu numeros suos evolvant et explicent, eadem tamen quae erant permanent. Multum interest inter pueritiae florem et senectutis maturitatem; sed iidem tamen ipsi fiunt senes qui fuerant adolescentes; ut quamvis unius ejusdemque hominis status habitusque mutetur, una tamen nihilominus eademque natura, una eademque persona sit. Parva lactentium membra, magna juvenum, eadem ipsa sunt tamen. Quot parvulorum artus, tot virorum; et si qua illa sunt quae aevi maturioris aetate pariuntur, jam in seminis ratione proserta sunt; ut nihil novum postea proferatur in senibus quod non in pueris jam antè latitaverit. Undè non dubium est hanc esse legitimam et rectam proficiendi regulam, hunc ratum atque pulcherrimum crescendi ordinem, si eas semper in grandioribus partes ac formas numerus detexat aetatis quas in parvulis Creatoris sapientia praeformaverat. Quod si humana species in aliquam deinceps non sui generis vertatur effigiem, aut certe addatur quippiam membrorum numero vel detrahatur, necesse est ut totum corpus vel intercidat, vel prodigiosum fiat, vel certe debilitetur: ita etiam Christianae Religionis dogma sequatur has decet profectuum leges, ut annis scilicet consolidetur, dilatetur tempore, sublimetur aetate, incorruptum tamen illibatumque permaneat, et universis partium suarum mensuris cunctisque quasi membris ac sensibus propriis plenum atque perfectum sit, quod nihil praeterea permutationis admittat, nulla proprietatis dispendia, nullam definitionis sustineat varietatem. Exempli gratia: Severunt majores nostri antiquitus in hac ecclesiastica segete triticeae fidei semina: iniquum valde et incongruum est ut nos eorum posteri pro germana veritate frumenti subdititium zizaniae legamus errorem. Quin potiùs hoc rectum et consequens est ut primis atque extremis sibimet non discrepantibus, de incrementis triticeae institutionis triticei quoque dogmatis frugem demetamus; ut cùm aliquid ex illis seminum primordiis accessu temporis evolvatur, et nunc laetetur et excolatur, nihil tamen de germinis proprietate mutetur: addatur licet species, forma, distinctio, eadem tamen cujusque generis natura permaneat. Absit etenim ut rosea illa catholici sensus plantaria in carduos spinasque vertantur. Absit inquam, ut in isto spiritali paradiso de cinnamomi et balsami surculis lolium repente atque aconita proveniant. Quodcumque igitur in hac Ecclesia Dei agricultura fide patrum satum est, hoc idem filiorum industriâ decet excolatur et observetur, hoc idem floreat et maturescat, hoc idem proficiat et perficiatur. Fas est etenim ut prisca illa coelestis 669philosophiae dogmata processu temporis excurentur, limentur, poliantur; sed nefas est ut commutentur, nefas ut detruncentur, ut mutilentur. Accipiant, licet, evidentiam, lucem, distinctionem; sed retineant necesse est plenitudinem, integritatem, proprietatem. Nam si semel admissa fuerit haec impiae fraudis licentia, horreo dicere quantum exscindendae atque abolendae Religionis periculum consequatur. Abdicata etenim qualibet parte catholici dogmatis, alia quoque atque item alia, ac deinceps alia et alia, jam quasi ex more et licito, abdicabuntur. Porro autem sigillatìm partibus repudiatis, quid aliud ad extremum sequetur, nisi ut totum pariter repudietur? sed e contra, si novitia veteribus, extranea domesticis, et profana sacratis admisceri coeperint, proserpat hic mos in universum necesse est ut nihil posthac apud Ecclesiam relinquatur intactum, nihil illibatum, nihil integrum, nihil immaculatum, sed sit ibidem deinceps impiorum ac turpium errorum lupanar ubi erat antea castae et incorruptae sacrarium veritatis. Sed avertat hoc a suorum mentibus nefas divina pietas, sitque hic potiùs impiorum furor. Christi vero Ecclesia, sedula et cauta depositorum apud se dogmatum custos, nihil in his unquam permutat, nihil minuit, nihil addit, non amputat necessaria, non apponit superflua, non amittit sua, non usurpat aliena; sed omni industria hoc unum studet ut vetera fideliter sapienterque tractando, si qua sunt illa antiquitus informata et inchoata, accuret et poliat; si qua jam expressa et enucleata consolidet, firmet; si qua jam confirmata et definita, custodiat; denique quid unquam aliud Conciliorum decretis enisa est nisi ut quod antea simpliciter credebatur, hoc idem postea diligentius crederetur, quod antea lentius praedicabatur, hoc idem postea instantius praedicaretur, quod antea securius colebatur, hoc idem postea sollicitiùs excoleretur? Hoc, inquam semper, neque quicquam praeterea, Haereticorum novitatibus excitata, conciliorum suorum decretis catholica perfecit Ecclesia, nisi ut quod prius à Majoribus sola traditione susceperat, hoc deinde posteris etiam per Scripturae chirographum consignaret, magnam rerum summam paucis litteris comprehendendo, et plerumque, propter intelligentiae lucem, non novum fidei sensum novae appellationis proprietate signando. 670

XXIII.

But perhaps someone says: Shall there then be held to be no progress of Religion in the Church of Christ? Let there be, plainly, and the greatest. For who is he so envious of men, so hateful to God, who would attempt to forbid this? But in such a way, nevertheless, that it be truly a progress of the faith, 668not a change. Since indeed it pertains to progress that each single thing be enlarged within itself; but to change, that something be turned from one thing into another. It behooves, therefore, that there grow and greatly and vigorously advance, of individuals as of all, of one man as of the whole Church, according to the stages of ages and centuries, the understanding, the knowledge, the wisdom — but only within its own kind, that is, in the same doctrine, the same meaning, and the same judgment. Let the religion of souls imitate the reckoning of bodies: which, although with the advance of years they unfold and unroll their numbers, yet remain the same that they were. There is much difference between the flower of boyhood and the maturity of old age; yet the very same become old men who had been youths; so that, although the state and condition of one and the same man be changed, nevertheless there is one and the same nature, one and the same person. The small limbs of nurslings, the large of young men, are yet the same. As many are the members of little ones as of grown men; and if there are any which are brought forth at the age of a riper time, they were already put forth in the reckoning of the seed; so that nothing new is afterward produced in old men which did not already lie hidden beforehand in children. Whence there is no doubt that this is the lawful and right rule of advancing, this the sure and most beautiful order of growing: if the number of the ages ever unfolds in the greater those parts and forms which the wisdom of the Creator had preformed in the little ones. But if the human shape be afterward turned into some figure not of its own kind, or if certainly something be added to the number of the members or taken away, it must needs be that the whole body either perish, or become monstrous, or certainly be weakened: so also it befits the doctrine of the Christian Religion to follow these laws of progress, that it be consolidated by years, dilated by time, exalted by age, yet remain uncorrupted and unimpaired, and be full and perfect in all the measures of its parts and in all its members, as it were, and its own senses, admitting thereafter no change, sustaining no loss of its property, no variety of its definition. For example: our forefathers of old sowed in this ecclesiastical field the seeds of the wheaten faith; it is exceedingly unjust and unfitting that we, their posterity, should gather, in place of the genuine truth of the grain, the counterfeit error of tares. Nay rather this is right and consequent, that, the first and the last not being at variance with themselves, from the increases of the wheaten planting we reap also the fruit of the wheaten doctrine; so that when something from those first beginnings of the seeds is unfolded in the course of time, and is now made glad and cultivated, yet nothing be changed of the property of the shoot: let the appearance, the form, the distinction, be added, yet let the same nature of each kind remain. For far be it that those rosy nursery-plants of the catholic understanding be turned into thistles and thorns. Far be it, I say, that in that spiritual paradise, from the shoots of cinnamon and balsam, darnel and aconite should suddenly spring up. Whatever, then, has been sown in this husbandry of the Church of God by the faith of the fathers, this same it befits should be cultivated and observed by the industry of the sons, this same should flourish and ripen, this same should advance and be perfected. For it is lawful that those ancient doctrines of the heavenly 669philosophy be, in the course of time, refined, filed, polished; but it is unlawful that they be changed, unlawful that they be lopped off, that they be mutilated. Let them receive, indeed, clarity, light, distinction; but they must needs retain their fullness, integrity, and property. For if once this license of impious fraud be admitted, I shudder to say how great a peril of Religion to be cut down and abolished follows. For, any part of the catholic doctrine being renounced, another too, and again another, and thereafter another and another, will be renounced, as it were now by custom and by leave. Furthermore, the parts being repudiated one by one, what else will follow at the last, but that the whole likewise be repudiated? But on the contrary, if things newfangled begin to be mingled with the old, foreign things with the domestic, and profane with the sacred, this custom must needs creep on into everything, so that thereafter nothing be left among the Church untouched, nothing unimpaired, nothing whole, nothing unspotted, but there be there thenceforth a brothel of impious and base errors where before was the sanctuary of chaste and incorrupt truth. But may the divine mercy avert this wickedness from the minds of her own, and let this rather be the madness of the impious. But the Church of Christ, a careful and cautious keeper of the doctrines deposited with her, never changes anything in them, diminishes nothing, adds nothing; she does not cut off what is necessary, does not append what is superfluous, does not lose her own, does not usurp what is another's; but with all industry she is intent on this one thing: that, by treating the old faithfully and wisely, if there be any things anciently shaped and begun, she may perfect and polish them; if any things already expressed and unfolded, she may consolidate and strengthen them; if any things already confirmed and defined, she may guard them; in short, what else has she ever striven for by the decrees of the Councils, but that what before was believed simply, this same might afterward be believed more diligently; what before was preached more slackly, this same might afterward be preached more urgently; what before was cultivated more securely, this same might afterward be cultivated more solicitously? This, I say, and nothing else, has the Catholic Church, roused by the novelties of the Heretics, ever accomplished by the decrees of her Councils, but that what she had first received from her Elders by tradition alone, she should afterward consign also to posterity by the handwriting of Scripture, comprehending a great sum of matters in few letters, and, for the most part, for the sake of clearness of understanding, marking a not-new meaning of the faith by the property of a new appellation. 670

XXIV.

Sed ad Apostolum redeamus. O Timothee, inquit, depositum custodi, devitans prophanas vocum novitates. Devita, inquit, quasi viperam, quasi scorpionem, quasi basiliscum, ne te non solum tactu, sed etiam visu afflatuque percutiant. Quid est devitare? cum hujusmodi nec cibum sumere, 1 Cor. 5. Quid est, devita? Si quis, inquit, venit ad vos, et hanc doctrinam non affert, 2 Joann. Quam doctrinam, nisi catholicam et universalem, et unam eamdemque per singulas aetatum successiones incorrupta veritatis traditione manentem, et usque in secula sine fine mansuram? Quid tum? Nolite, inquit, recipere eum in domum, nec ave ei dixeritis. Qui enim dicit illi ave, communicat operibus ejus malignis. Prophanas, inquit, vocum novitates. Quid est prophanas? Quae nihil habent sacri, nihil religiosi, ab Ecclesiae penetralibus, quae est templum Dei, penitus extraneas. Prophanas, inquit, vocum novitates. Vocum, id est, dogmatum, rerum, sententiarum novitates, quae sunt vetustati atque antiquitati contrariae: quae si recipiantur, necesse est ut fides beatorum patrum, aut tota, aut certe magnâ ex parte violetur; necesse est ut omnes omnium aetatum fideles, omnes sancti omnes casti, continentes, virgines, omnes clerici, levitae et sacerdotes, tanta confessorum millia, tanti martyrum exercitus, tanta urbium, tanta populorum celebritas et multitudo, tot insulae, provinciae, reges, gentes, regna, nationes, totus postremo jam pene terrarum orbis, per catholicam fidem Christo capiti incorporatus, tanto seculorum tractu ignorasse, errasse, blasphemasse, nesciisse quid crederet, pronuntietur. Prophanas, inquit, vocum novitates devita: quas recipere atque sectari nunquam Catholicorum, semper vero Haereticorum fuit. Et revera, quae unquam haeresis nisi sub certo nomine, certo loco, certo tempore ebullivit? Quis unquam haereses instituit nisi qui se prius ab Ecclesiae catholicae universitatis et antiquitatis consensione discreverit? Quod ita esse luce clarius exempla demonstrant. Quis enim unquam ante profanum illum Pelagium tantam virtutem liberi praesumpsit arbitrii ut ad hoc in bonis rebus per actus singulos adjuvandum necessariam Dei gratiam non putaret? Quis ante prodigiosum discipulum ejus Coelestium reatu praevaricationis Adae omne 671humanum genus denegavit adstrictum? Quis ante sacrilegum Arium trinitatis unitatem discindere, quis ante sceleratum Sabellium unitatis Trinitatem confundere ausus est? Quis ante crudelissimum Novatianum crudelem Deum dixit, eo quod mallet mortem morientis quam ut revertatur et vivat? Quis ante magum Simonem, apostolica districtione percussum (a quo vetus ille turpitudinum gurges usque in novissimum Priscillianum continua et occulta successione manavit) auctorem malorum, id est, scelerum, impietatum, flagitiorumque nostrorum ausus est dicere creatorem Deum? Quippe quem adserit talem hominum manibus ipsam suis creare naturam, quae proprio quodam motu et necessariae cujusdam voluntatis impulsu nihil aliud possit, nihil aliud velit nisi peccare, eò quòd furiis vitiorum omnium exagitata et inflammata in omnia turpitudinum barathra inexhausta cupiditate rapiatur. Innumera sunt talia, quae brevitatis studio praetermittimus: quibus tamen cunctis satis evidenter perspicueque monstratur hoc apud omnes fere haereses quasi solemne esse ac legitimum, 672ut semper profanis novitatibus gaudeant, antiquitatis scita fastidiant, et per oppositiones falsi nominis scientiae a fide naufragent. Contra vero Catholicorum hoc vere proprium, deposita sanctorum patrum et commissa servare, damnare profanas novitates, et sicut dixit atque iterum praedixit Apostolus, si quis annuntiaverit praeterquam quod acceptum est, anathema sit, Gal. 1.

XXIV.

But let us return to the Apostle. O Timothy, he says, keep the deposit, shunning the profane novelties of words. Shun it, he says, as a viper, as a scorpion, as a basilisk, lest they strike you not only by touch, but even by sight and by their breath. What is "to shun"? With such a one not even to take food, 1 Cor. 5. What is "shun"? If anyone, he says, comes to you, and does not bring this doctrine, 2 Joann. What doctrine, but the catholic and universal, one and the same through the several successions of the ages, abiding by the incorrupt tradition of the truth, and destined to abide unto the ages without end? What then? Do not, he says, receive him into the house, nor say Ave to him. For he that saith Ave to him communicateth with his malignant works. Profane, he says, novelties of words. What is profane? Those things which have nothing sacred, nothing religious, wholly foreign to the inner recesses of the Church, which is the temple of God. Profane, he says, novelties of words. Of words — that is, novelties of doctrines, of things, of opinions — which are contrary to what is old and ancient: which, if they be received, it is of necessity that the faith of the blessed fathers be violated, either wholly, or at least in great part; it is of necessity that all the faithful of all ages, all the saints, all the chaste, the continent, the virgins, all the clergy, the levites and priests, so many thousands of confessors, so many armies of martyrs, so great a throng and multitude of cities and of peoples, so many islands, provinces, kings, tribes, kingdoms, nations, in short by now well-nigh the whole round world, incorporated into Christ the Head through the catholic faith, be pronounced to have been ignorant, through so great a course of the ages, to have erred, to have blasphemed, not to have known what it believed. Profane, he says, novelties of words: shun them — which to receive and to follow was never the mark of Catholics, but ever of heretics. And indeed, what heresy ever boiled up except under a certain name, in a certain place, at a certain time? Who ever established heresies except one who had first separated himself from the agreement of the universality and antiquity of the catholic Church? That this is so, examples show clearer than light. For who ever, before that profane Pelagius, presumed so great a power of free will that he did not think the grace of God necessary to aid it, in good works, through the several acts? Who, before his monstrous disciple Coelestius, denied that the whole 671human race was bound by the guilt of Adam's transgression? Who, before the sacrilegious Arius, dared to sunder the unity of the Trinity; who, before the wicked Sabellius, to confound the Trinity of the unity? Who, before the most cruel Novatian, called God cruel, in that he would rather the death of the dying than that he should return and live? Who, before Simon Magus — smitten by apostolic severity (from whom that old whirlpool of foulnesses has flowed, by a continuous and hidden succession, down to the most recent Priscillian) — dared to call God the Creator the author of evils, that is, of our crimes, impieties, and shameful deeds? For he asserts that such a one creates with his own hands the very nature of men, which, by a certain motion of its own and by the impulse of a certain necessary will, can do nothing else, wills nothing else, than to sin, in that, driven and inflamed by the furies of all vices, it is swept with insatiable desire into all the abysses of foulness. Innumerable are such things, which for the sake of brevity we pass over; by all of which, however, it is shown clearly and plainly enough that this is, as it were, customary and lawful among well-nigh all heresies, 672that they ever delight in profane novelties, loathe the decrees of antiquity, and, by the oppositions of knowledge falsely so called, make shipwreck of the faith. But on the contrary, this is truly proper to Catholics: to keep the deposits committed to them by the holy fathers, to condemn profane novelties, and, as the Apostle said and again foretold, if any man announce anything besides that which has been received, let him be anathema, Gal. 1.

XXV.

Hic fortasse aliquis interroget an et Haeretici divinae Scripturae testimoniis utantur. Utuntur plane. et vehementer quidem. Nam videas eos volare per singula quaeque sanctae legis volumina, per Moysi, per Regum libros; per Psalmos, per Apostolos, per Evangelia, per Prophetas. Sive enim apud suos, sive alienos, sive privatìm, sive publice, sive in sermonibus, sive in libris, sive in conviviis, sive in plateis, nihil unquam pene de suo proferunt quod non etiam Scripturae verbis adumbrare conentur. Lege Pauli Samosateni opuscula, Priscilliani, Eunomii, Joviniani, reliquarumque pestium; cernas infinitam exemplorum congeriem, prope nullam omitti paginam quae non novi aut veteris Testamenti sententiis fucata et colorata sit. Sed tanto magis cavendi et pertimescendi sunt, quanto occultius sub divinae legis umbraculis latitant. Sciunt enim faetores suos nulli fere cito esse placituros, si nudi et simplices exhalentur; atque idcirco eos coelestis eloquii velut quodam aromate aspergunt, ut ille qui humanum facile despiceret errorem, divina non facile contemnat oracula. Itaque faciunt quod hi solent, qui parvulis austera quaedam temperaturi pocula, prius oras melle circumlinunt, ut incauta aetas cum dulcedinem praesenserit, amaritudinem non reformidet. Quod etiam iis curae est qui mala gramina et noxios succos medicaminum vocabulis praecolorant, ut nemo fere ubi suprascriptum legerit remedium, suspicetur venenum. Inde denique et Salvator clamabat: Attendite vobis a pseudoprophetis, qui veniunt ad vos in vestitu ovium, ab intus autem sunt lupi rapaces, Matth. 7. Quid est vestitus ovium, nisi Prophetarum et Apostolorum proloquia, quae iidem, ovili quadam sinceritate, agno illi immaculato, qui 673tollit peccatum mundi, tanquam vellera quaedam texuerunt? Qui sunt lupi rapaces, nisi sensus Haereticorum feri et rabidi, qui caulas Ecclesiae semper infestant et gregem Christi quaqua possunt dilacerant? Sed ut fallacius incautis ovibus obrepant, manente luporum ferocia, deponunt lupinam speciem, et sese divinae legis sententiis velut quibusdam velleribus obvolvunt, ut cum quisque lanarum mollitiem praesenserit, nequaquam aculeos dentium pertimescat. Sed quid ait Salvator? Ex fructibus eorum cognoscetis eos: id est, cum coeperint divinas illas voces non jam proferre tantum, sed etiam exponere, nec adhuc jactare solum, sed etiam interpretari, tunc amaritudo illa, tunc acerbitas, tunc rabies intelligetur, tunc novitium virus exhalabitur, tunc profanae novitates aperientur; tunc primum scindi sepem videas, tunc transferri patrum terminos, tunc Catholicam fidem caedi, tunc Ecclesiasticum dogma lacerari. Tales erant ii quos percutit Apostolus Paulus in secunda ad Corinthios Epistola, dicens: Nam ejusmodi, inquit, pseudoapostoli, sunt operarii subdoli, transfigurantes se in Apostolos Christi? 2 Cor. 11. Quid est transfigurantes se in Apostolos Christi? Proferebant Apostoli divinae legis exempla; proferebant et illi. Proferebant Apostoli psalmorum auctoritates; proferebant et illi. Proferebant Apostoli sententias Prophetarum; et illi nihilominus proferebant. Sed cum ea quae similiter protulerant, interpretari non similiter coepissent, tunc simplices a subdolis, tunc infucati a fucatis, tunc recti a perversis, tunc postremo veri Apostoli a falsis Apostolis discernebantur. Et non mirum, inquit. Ipse enim Satanas transfigurat se in angelum lucis. Non est ergo magnum si ministri ejus transfigurantur sicut ministri justitiae. Ergo secundum Apostoli Pauli magisterium, quotiescumque vel pseudoapostoli vel pseudoprophetae vel pseudodoctores divinae legis sententias proferunt, quibus male interpretatis errores suos adstruere conentur, non dubium est quin auctoris sui callida machinamenta sectentur, quae ille nunquam profecto comminisceretur, nisi sciret omnino nullam esse ad fallendum faciliorem viam, quam ut ubi nefarii erroris subinducitur fraudulentia, ibi divinorum verborum praetendatur auctoritas.

XXV.

Here perhaps someone may ask whether heretics too make use of the testimonies of divine Scripture. They use them plainly, and very vigorously indeed. For you may see them flying through each and every volume of the sacred law, through Moses, through the books of Kings; through the Psalms, through the Apostles, through the Gospels, through the Prophets. For whether among their own or among strangers, whether privately or publicly, whether in discourses or in books, whether at banquets or in the streets, they bring forth well-nigh nothing of their own which they do not attempt to shade over with the words of Scripture as well. Read the little works of Paul of Samosata, of Priscillian, of Eunomius, of Jovinian, and of the rest of those plagues; you will discern an endless heap of examples, well-nigh no page omitted which is not painted and colored with the opinions of the New or the Old Testament. But they are the more to be guarded against and dreaded, the more secretly they lurk under the shades of the divine law. For they know that their stenches will be pleasing quickly to well-nigh no one, if they be breathed out naked and plain; and therefore they sprinkle them, as it were, with a certain aroma of heavenly eloquence, so that he who would easily despise a human error may not easily contemn divine oracles. And so they do what those are wont to do who, being about to temper certain bitter draughts for little children, first smear the rims round about with honey, so that the unwary age, when it has felt the sweetness beforehand, may not dread the bitterness. This too is the care of those who color over evil herbs and noxious juices with the names of medicines, so that well-nigh no one, when he has read the remedy written above, may suspect the poison. Hence at last the Savior too cried out: Beware of false prophets, who come to you in the clothing of sheep, but inwardly are ravening wolves, Matth. 7. What is the clothing of sheep, but the utterances of the Prophets and Apostles, which the same, with a certain sheeplike sincerity, wove, as it were, into fleeces for that immaculate Lamb which 673taketh away the sin of the world? Who are the ravening wolves, but the fierce and raging senses of heretics, who ever infest the folds of the Church and tear the flock of Christ in whatever way they can? But that they may more deceitfully creep upon the unwary sheep, the ferocity of the wolves abiding, they lay aside the wolfish appearance, and wrap themselves in the opinions of the divine law as in fleeces, so that when anyone has felt beforehand the softness of the wool, he may in no wise dread the points of the teeth. But what says the Savior? By their fruits you shall know them: that is, when they have begun no longer merely to bring forth those divine words, but even to expound them, and no longer only to vaunt them, but even to interpret them, then that bitterness, then that harshness, then that madness will be understood, then the novel venom will be breathed out, then the profane novelties will be laid open; then first you may see the hedge broken, then the boundaries of the fathers removed, then the catholic faith cut down, then the ecclesiastical dogma mangled. Such were those whom the Apostle Paul strikes in his second Epistle to the Corinthians, saying: For such, he says, false apostles are deceitful workers, transforming themselves into the Apostles of Christ, 2 Cor. 11. What is transforming themselves into the Apostles of Christ? The Apostles brought forth examples of the divine law; these too brought them forth. The Apostles brought forth the authorities of the psalms; these too brought them forth. The Apostles brought forth the opinions of the Prophets; and these no less brought them forth. But when they had begun to interpret in unlike manner the things they had brought forth in like manner, then the simple were distinguished from the crafty, then the uncolored from the painted, then the upright from the perverse, then at last the true Apostles from the false Apostles. And no wonder, he says. For Satan himself transformeth himself into an angel of light. It is therefore no great thing if his ministers be transformed as the ministers of justice. Therefore, according to the teaching of the Apostle Paul, as often as either false apostles or false prophets or false teachers bring forth the opinions of the divine law, by which, ill interpreted, they endeavor to build up their errors, there is no doubt that they follow the crafty devices of their author — which he assuredly would never have contrived, unless he knew that there is altogether no easier way to deceive than that, where the fraud of nefarious error is insinuated, there the authority of divine words be held forth.

XXVI.

Sed dicet aliquis: Unde probatur quia sacrae legis exemplis Diabolus uti soleat? Legat Evangelia, in quibus scribitur: Tunc assumpsit illum Diabolus, id est, Dominum Salvatorem, et statuit illum super pinnam templi, et dixit ei: Si filius Dei es, mitte te deorsum. Scriptum est enim quod angelis suis mandavit de te ut custodiant te in omnibus viis tuis; in manibus tollent te, ne forte offendas ad lapidem pedem tuum, Matth. 4. Quid hic faciet misellis hominibus qui ipsum Dominum majestatis Scripturarum testimoniis appetivit? Si, inquit, filius Dei es, mitte te deorsum. Quare? Scriptum est enim, inquit. Magnopere nobis doctrina loci istius attendenda atque retinenda est, ut tanto evangelicae auctoritatis exemplo, quando aliquos apostolica seu prophetica verba proferre contra catholicam fidem viderimus, diabolum per eos loqui minime dubitemus. Nam sicut tunc caput capiti, ita nunc quoque membra 674membris loquuntur, membra scilicet diaboli membris Christi, perfidi fidelibus, sacrilegi religiosis, Haeretici postremo Catholicis. Sed quid tandem dicunt? Si, inquit, filius Dei es, mitte te deorsum; hoc est, si filius esse vis Dei et haereditatem regni coelestis accipere, mitte te deorsum, id est, ex istius te sublimis Ecclesiae, quae etiam templum Dei putatur, doctrina et traditione demitte. Ac si quis interroget quempiam haereticorum sibi talia persuadentem: Unde probas, unde doces quod Ecclesiae Catholicae universalem et antiquam fidem dimittere debeam? statim ille: Scriptum est enim. Et continuo mille testimonia, mille exempla, mille auctoritates parat de lege, de Psalmis, de Apostolis, de Prophetis, quibus novo et malo more interpretatis, ex arce catholica in haereseos barathrum infelix anima praecipitetur. Jam vero illis quae sequuntur promissionibus miro modo incautos homines Haeretici decipere consueverunt. Audent etenim polliceri et docere quod in Ecclesia sua, id est, in communionis suae conventiculo, magna et specialis ac plane personalis quaedam sit Dei gratia, adeo ut sine ullo labore, sine ullo studio, sine ulla industria, etiamsi nec petant, nec quaerant, nec pulsent, quicumque illi ad numerum suum pertinent, tamen ita divinitus dispensentur ut angelicis evecti manibus, id est, angelica protectione servati, nunquam possint offendere ad lapidem pedem suum, id est, nunquam scandalizari.

XXVI.

But someone will say: Whence is it proved that the devil is wont to use examples of the sacred law? Let him read the Gospels, in which it is written: Then the devil took him, that is, the Lord and Savior, and set him upon the pinnacle of the temple, and said to him: If thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down. For it is written that he hath given his angels charge over thee, that they keep thee in all thy ways; in their hands they shall bear thee up, lest perhaps thou dash thy foot against a stone, Matth. 4. What will he do here to poor wretched men, who assailed the very Lord of majesty with the testimonies of the Scriptures? If, he says, thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down. Why? For it is written, he says. Greatly is the teaching of this passage to be heeded and retained by us, that, by so great an example of Gospel authority, when we see any men bring forth apostolic or prophetic words against the catholic faith, we may in no wise doubt that the devil speaks through them. For as then the head spoke to the Head, so now too the members speak to the members 674— the members, that is, of the devil to the members of Christ, the perfidious to the faithful, the sacrilegious to the religious, the heretics in short to the Catholics. But what at length do they say? If, he says, thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down; that is, if thou wilt be the Son of God and receive the inheritance of the heavenly kingdom, cast thyself down — that is, let thyself down from the teaching and tradition of this lofty Church, which is even reckoned the temple of God. And if anyone should ask any one of the heretics who persuades him of such things: Whence dost thou prove, whence dost thou teach, that I ought to forsake the universal and ancient faith of the Catholic Church? — straightway he replies: For it is written. And at once he prepares a thousand testimonies, a thousand examples, a thousand authorities from the law, from the Psalms, from the Apostles, from the Prophets, by which, interpreted in a new and evil manner, the unhappy soul may be cast headlong from the catholic citadel into the abyss of heresy. But now, by those promises which follow, the heretics have been wont to deceive the unwary in a marvelous manner. For they dare to promise and to teach that in their Church — that is, in the little conventicle of their communion — there is a certain great and special and plainly personal grace of God, so that, without any labor, without any zeal, without any diligence, even though they neither ask, nor seek, nor knock, whosoever belong to their number are nevertheless so divinely dispensed that, borne up by angelic hands — that is, kept by angelic protection — they can never dash their foot against a stone, that is, never be scandalized.

XXVII.

Sed dicet aliquis: Si divinis eloquiis, sententiis, promissionibus et Diabolus et discipuli ejus utuntur, quorum alii sunt pseudoapostoli, alii pseudoprophetae et pseudomagistri, et omnes ex toto Haeretici, quid facient Catholici homines et matris Ecclesiae filii? Quonam modo in Scripturis sanctis veritatem a falsitate discernent? Hoc scilicet facere magnopere curabunt, quod in principio Commonitorii istius sanctos et doctos viros nobis tradidisse scripsimus, ut divinum Canonem secundum universalis Ecclesiae traditiones et juxtà Catholici dogmatis regulas interpretentur; in qua item Catholica et apostolica Ecclesia sequantur necesse est universitatem, antiquitatem, consensionem. Et si quando pars contra universitatem, novitas contra vetustatem, unius vel paucorum errantium dissensio contra omnium vel certè multo plurium Catholicorum consensionem rebellaverit, praeferant partis corruptioni universitatis integritatem: in qua eadem universitate, novitatis profanitati antiquitatis religionem, itemque in ipsa vetustate, unius sive paucissimorum temeritati primum omnium generalia, si qua sunt, universalis Concilii decreta praeponant; tunc deinde, si id minus est, sequantur quod proximum est, multorum atque magnorum consentientes sibi sententias magistrorum. Quibus, adjuvante Domino, fideliter, sobriè, sollicite observatis, non magna difficultate noxios quosque exsurgentium Haereticorum deprehendemus errores.

XXVII.

But someone will say: If both the devil and his disciples — of whom some are false apostles, others false prophets and false teachers, and all wholly heretics — make use of the divine eloquences, opinions, and promises, what shall Catholic men and the sons of mother Church do? In what manner shall they discern the truth from falsehood in the holy Scriptures? This assuredly they will take great care to do, which at the beginning of this Commonitorium we wrote that holy and learned men had handed down to us: that they should interpret the divine Canon according to the traditions of the universal Church and according to the rules of catholic dogma; and in that same Catholic and apostolic Church they must needs follow universality, antiquity, consent. And if at any time a part should rebel against the whole, novelty against what is old, the dissent of one or of a few in error against the consent of all — or at least of far the greater number — of Catholics, let them prefer the soundness of the whole to the corruption of a part; and within that same universality, let them prefer the religion of antiquity to the profanity of novelty; and likewise, within antiquity itself, let them set, before the rashness of one or of a very few, first of all the general decrees, if there be any, of a universal Council; and then next, if there be none such, let them follow what is nearest — the mutually agreeing opinions of many and great masters. Which being faithfully, soberly, and carefully observed, with the Lord's help, we shall without great difficulty detect the noxious errors of any heretics that arise.

XXVIII.

Hic jam consequens esse video ut exemplis demonstrem quonam modo prophanae Haereticorum novitates prolatis atque collatis veterum magistrorum concordantibus sibimet sententiis et 675deprehendantur et condemnentur. Quae tamen antiqua sanctorum patrum consensio non in omnibus divinae legis quaestiunculis, sed solum certe praecipue in fidei regula magno nobis studio et investiganda est et sequenda. Sed neque semper neque omnes haereses hoc modo impugnandae sunt, sed novitiae recentesque tantummodo, cum primum scilicet exoriuntur, ante quam infalsare vetustae fidei regulas ipsius temporis vetantur angustiis, ac priusquam manante latius veneno, majorum volumina vitiare conentur. Caeterum dilatatae et inveteratae haereses nequaquam hac via adgrediendae sunt, eo quod prolixo temporum tractu longa iis fruendae veritatis patuerit occasio. Atque ideo quascumque illas antiquiores vel schismatum vel haereseon profanitates nullo modo nos oportet nisi aut sola, si opus est, Scripturarum auctoritate convincere, aut certe jam antiquitus universalibus sacerdotum catholicorum Conciliis convictas damnatasque vitare. Itaque, cum primum mali cujusque erroris putredo erumpere coeperit, et ad defensionem suî quaedam sacrae legis verba furari, eaque fallaciter et fraudulenter exponere, statim interpretando Canoni majorum sententiae congregandae sunt; quibus illud quodcumque exsurget novitium, ideoque profanum et absque ulla ambage prodatur, et sine ulla retractatione damnetur. Sed eorum duntaxat patrum sentententiae conferendae sunt, qui in fide et communione Catholica sancte, sapienter, constanter viventes, docentes, et permanentes, vel mori in Christo fideliter vel occidi pro Christo feliciter meruerunt. Quibus tamen hac lege credendum est ut quicquid vel omnes vel plures uno eodemque sensu manifestè, frequenter, perseveranter, velut quodam consentiente sibi magistrorum concilio, accipiendo, tenendo, tradendo firmaverint, id pro indubitato, certo, ratoque habeatur. Quicquid vero, quamvis ille sanctus et doctus, quamvis Confessor et Martyr, praeter omnes aut etiam contra omnes senserit, id inter proprias et occultas et privatas opiniunculas a communis et publicae ac generalis sententiae auctoritate secretum sit; ne cum summo aeternae salutis periculo, juxta sacrilegam Haereticorum et Schismaticorum consuetudinem, universalis dogmatis antiqua veritate dimissa, unius hominis novitium 676sectemur errorem. Quorum beatorum patrum sanctum catholicumque consensum, ne quis sibi temere contemnendum forte arbitretur, ait in prima ad Corinthios Apostolus: Et quosdam quidem posuit Deus in Ecclesiâ primum Apostolos (quorum ipse unus erat), secundo Prophetas (qualem in Actibus Apostolorum legimus Agabum), tertio Doctores, qui tractatores nunc appellantur, quos hic idem Apostolus etiam Prophetas interdum nuncupat, eo quod per eos Prophetarum mysteria populis aperiantur. Hos ergo in Ecclesia Dei divinitus per tempora et loca dispensatos quisquis in sensu catholici dogmatis unum aliquid in Christo sentientes contempserit, non hominem contemnit, sed Deum: a quorum veridica unitate ne discrepet, impensius obtestatur idem Apostolus dicens: Obsecro autem vos, fratres, ut id ipsum dicatis omnes, et non sint in vobis schismata, sitis autem perfecti in eodem sensu et in eadem sententia, 1 Cor. 1. Quod si quis ab eorum sententiae communione desciverit, audiet illud ejusdem Apostoli: Non est Deus dissensionis, sed pacis, 1 Cor. 14 (id est, non ejus qui a consentiendi unitate defecerit, sed eorum qui in consentiendi pace permanserint) sicut in omnibus, inquit, Ecclesiis sanctorum doceo, id est, Catholicorum: quae ideo sanctae sunt, quia in fidei communione persistunt. Et ne quis forsitan, praetermissis caeteris, se solum audiri, sibi soli credi adrogaret, paulo post ait. An a vobis, inquit, verbum Dei processit, aut in vos solos devenit? Et ne hoc quasi perfunctorie acciperetur, adjecit: Si quis, inquit, videtur propheta esse aut spiritalis, cognoscat quae scribo vobis, quia Domini sunt mandata. Quae utique mandata, nisi ut si quis est propheta aut spiritalis, id est, spiritalium rerum magister, summo studio aequalitatis et unitatis cultor existat; ut scilicet neque opiniones suas caeteris praeferat, et ab universorum sensibus non recedat. Cujus rei mandata qui ignorat, inquit, ignorabitur: id est, qui aut nescita non discit, aut scita contemnit, ignorabitur, hoc est, indignus habebitur qui inter unitos fide et exaequatos humilitate divinitùs respiciatur: quo malo nescio an quicquam acerbius cogitari queat. Quod tamen juxta apostolicam comminationem Pelagiano illi provenisse cernimus Juliano, 677qui se collegarum sensui aut incorporare neglexit, aut excorporare praesumpsit. Sed jam tempus est ut pollicitum proferamus exemplum, ubi et quomodo sanctorum patrum sententiae congregatae sint, ut secundum 678eas ex decreto atque auctoritate Concilii ecclesiasticae fidei regula figeretur. Quod quo commodius fiat, hìc sit jam hujus Commonitorii modus; ut caetera quae sequuntur, ab alio sumamus exordio.

XXVIII.

Here now I see it to be consequent that I should demonstrate by examples in what manner the profane novelties of heretics may be both detected and condemned by bringing forward and comparing the mutually agreeing opinions of the ancient masters. And 675this ancient consent of the holy fathers is to be sought out and followed by us with great zeal, not in all the little questions of the divine law, but certainly, above all, only in the rule of faith. Nor are all heresies always to be assailed in this manner, but only the novel and recent ones — at the very moment, that is, when they first arise, before they are hindered by the straits of the time itself from falsifying the rules of the ancient faith, and before, as the venom spreads more widely, they attempt to corrupt the volumes of our elders. But heresies now widespread and grown old are by no means to be attacked in this way, because through the long lapse of the times a long occasion of assailing the truth has lain open to them. And therefore, whatsoever those more ancient profanities, whether of schisms or of heresies, it behooves us in no wise to do otherwise than either to convict them, if need be, by the sole authority of the Scriptures, or at least to shun them as already, of old, convicted and condemned by the universal Councils of the catholic bishops. And so, as soon as the rottenness of any evil error begins to break out, and to steal certain words of the sacred law for its own defense, and to expound them falsely and fraudulently, straightway the opinions of our elders are to be gathered together for the interpreting of the Canon; by which whatsoever novel thing shall arise, and therefore profane, may be laid bare without any ambiguity, and condemned without any retractation. But only the opinions of those fathers are to be brought together who, living, teaching, and abiding holily, wisely, and constantly in the catholic faith and communion, deserved either to die faithfully in Christ or to be slain happily for Christ. Who nevertheless are to be believed on this law: that whatsoever either all, or the greater number, have — with one and the same sense, manifestly, frequently, perseveringly, as though by a certain mutually agreeing council of masters — in receiving, holding, and handing down, confirmed, that is to be held for undoubted, certain, and ratified. But whatsoever anyone, though holy and learned, though a Confessor and Martyr, has held apart from all, or even against all, let that be set aside among his own private and hidden and personal little opinions, from the authority of the common and public and general opinion; lest, with the utmost peril of eternal salvation, after the sacrilegious custom of heretics and schismatics, the ancient truth of the universal dogma being forsaken, we follow the novel 676error of a single man. And lest anyone should rashly think that the holy and catholic consent of these blessed fathers is perchance to be contemned by him, the Apostle says in the first Epistle to the Corinthians: And some indeed God hath set in the Church: first Apostles (of whom he himself was one), secondly Prophets (such as we read Agabus to have been in the Acts of the Apostles), thirdly Doctors, who are now called expositors, whom this same Apostle also sometimes names Prophets, in that through them the mysteries of the Prophets are opened to the peoples. Whosoever therefore contemns these — dispensed by God through times and places in the Church of God, agreeing in some one thing in Christ according to the sense of catholic dogma — contemns not man, but God: from whose truthful unity, that no one may depart, the same Apostle more earnestly adjures, saying: Now I beseech you, brethren, that you all say the same thing, and that there be no schisms among you; but that you be perfect in the same sense and in the same opinion, 1 Cor. 1. But if anyone should fall away from the communion of their opinion, he shall hear that word of the same Apostle: God is not the God of dissension, but of peace, 1 Cor. 14 (that is, not of him who has fallen away from the unity of consenting, but of those who have remained in the peace of consenting), as, he says, in all the churches of the saints I teach — that is, of Catholics; which are therefore holy, because they persist in the communion of the faith. And lest anyone perhaps, the rest being passed over, should claim that he alone be heard, that he alone be believed, a little after he says: Or, he says, did the word of God go forth from you, or did it come to you only? And lest this be taken as it were carelessly, he added: If any man, he says, seem to be a prophet or spiritual, let him know the things I write to you, that they are the commandments of the Lord. Which commandments — what, but that, if anyone be a prophet or spiritual, that is, a master of spiritual things, he should show himself with the utmost zeal a cultivator of equality and unity; so, namely, that he neither prefer his own opinions to the rest, nor depart from the senses of all? And he who knows not the commandments of this thing, he says, shall be unknown: that is, he who either learns not things unknown, or contemns things known, shall be unknown — that is, shall be held unworthy to be regarded of God among those united in faith and made equal in humility; than which evil I know not whether anything more bitter can be conceived. Which nevertheless we perceive to have befallen, according to the apostolic threat, that Pelagian Julian, 677who either neglected to incorporate himself with the sense of his colleagues, or presumed to unbody himself from it. But now it is time that we bring forth the promised example — where and in what manner the opinions of the holy fathers were gathered together, that according to 678them, by the decree and authority of a Council, the rule of ecclesiastical faith might be fixed. And that this may be done the more conveniently, let there here now be the measure of this Commonitorium; that the rest which follow we may take up from another beginning.

XXIX.

677Quae cum ita sint, jam tempus est ut ea quae duobus his Commonitoriis dicta sunt, in hujus secundi fine recapitulemus. Diximus in superioribus hanc fuisse semper et esse hodieque Catholicorum consuetudinem, ut fidem veram duobus his modis adprobent, primum divini Canonis auctoritate, deinde Ecclesiae Catholicae traditione; non quia Canon solus non sibi ad universa sufficiat, sed quia verba divina pro suo plerique arbitratu interpretantes, varias opiniones erroresque concipiant, atque ideò necesse sit ut ad unam ecclesiastici sensûs regulam Scripturae coelestis intelligentia dirigatur, in his duntaxat praecipue quaestionibus quibus totius Catholici dogmatis fundamenta nituntur. Item diximus in ipsa rursus Ecclesia universitatis pariter et antiquitatis consensionem spectari oportere; ne aut ab unitatis integritate in partem schismatis abrumpamur, aut a vetustatis religione in haereseon novitates praecipitemur. Item diximus in ipsa Ecclesiae vetustate duo quaedam vehementer studioseque observanda, quibus penitus inhaerere deberent quicumque haeretici esse nollent: primum, si quid esset antiquitus ab omnibus Ecclesiae Catholicae sacerdotibus universalis Concilii auctoritate decretum; deinde si qua nova exsurgeret quaestio, ubi id minimè reperiretur, recurrendum ad sanctorum patrum sententias, eorum duntaxat qui suis quisque temporibus 678et locis in unitate communionis et fidei permanentes, magistri probabiles exstitissent, et quicquid uno sensu atque consensu tenuisse invenirentur, id Ecclesiae verum et catholicum absque ullo scrupulo judicaretur. Quod ne praesumptione magis nostra quam auctoritate ecclesiastica promere videremur, exemplum adhibuimus sancti Concilii quod ante triennium fermè in Asiâ apud Ephesum celebratum est VV. CC. Basso Antiochoque Consulibus: ubi cum de sanciendis fidei regulis disceptaretur, ne qua illic forsitan profana novitas in modum perfidiae Ariminensis obreperet, universis sacerdotibus, qui illo ducenti fere numero convenerant, hoc catholicissimum, fidelissimum, atque optimum factu visum est ut in medium sanctorum patrum sententiae proferrentur, quorum alios martyres, alios confessores, omnes vero catholicos sacerdotes fuisse et permansisse constaret; 679ut scilicet rite atque solemniter ex corum consensu atque decreto antiqui dogmatis religio confirmaretur et prophanae novitatis blasphemia condemnaretur. Quod cum ita factum foret, jure meritoque impius ille Nestorius catholicae vetustati contrarius, beatus 680vero Cyrillus sacrosanctae antiquitati consentaneus judicatus est. Et ut ad fidem rerum nihil deesset, tam nomina et numerum (licèt ordinem fuissemus obliti) edidimus eorum patrum juxtà quorum ibidem concinentem sibi concordemque sententiam et legis sacrae proloquia exposita sunt, et divini dogmatis regula constabilita est: quos, ad confirmandam memoriam, hìc quoque recensere nequaquam superfluum est.

XXIX.

677Since these things are so, it is now time that we recapitulate, at the end of this second one, the things which have been said in these two Commonitoria. We said in the foregoing that this has ever been, and is today also, the custom of Catholics: to prove the true faith by these two ways — first by the authority of the divine Canon, then by the tradition of the Catholic Church; not because the Canon alone does not of itself suffice for all things, but because most men, interpreting the divine words according to their own judgment, conceive various opinions and errors, and therefore it is necessary that the understanding of the heavenly Scripture be directed to the one rule of the ecclesiastical sense — in those questions especially on which the foundations of the whole of catholic dogma rest. Likewise we said that in the Church herself the consent alike of universality and of antiquity ought to be regarded; lest either we be torn away from the soundness of unity into the part of schism, or be cast headlong from the religion of what is old into the novelties of heresies. Likewise we said that in the very antiquity of the Church two things are to be observed with great earnestness and zeal, to which whosoever would not be heretics ought wholly to cleave: first, if anything was of old decreed by all the bishops of the Catholic Church by the authority of a universal Council; and then, if any new question should arise where this is by no means found, recourse must be had to the opinions of the holy fathers — of those only who, each in his own times 678and places, abiding in the unity of communion and of faith, had stood forth as approved masters; and whatsoever they should be found to have held with one sense and one consent, that should, without any scruple, be judged the true and catholic doctrine of the Church. And that we might not seem to bring this forth by our own presumption rather than by ecclesiastical authority, we adduced the example of the holy Council which was celebrated about three years ago in Asia at Ephesus, in the consulship of the most illustrious men Bassus and Antiochus: where, when there was debate about the sanctioning of the rules of faith, lest perchance some profane novelty should there creep in after the manner of the Ariminian treachery, it seemed to all the bishops — who had assembled there to the number of about two hundred — most catholic, most faithful, and best to do, that the opinions of the holy fathers should be brought forth into the midst, of whom it was established that some had been martyrs, others confessors, but all catholic bishops, and had so remained; 679that so, duly and solemnly, out of their consent and decree, the religion of the ancient dogma might be confirmed, and the blasphemy of profane novelty condemned. And when this had been so done, by right and desert was that impious Nestorius judged contrary to catholic antiquity, but the blessed 680Cyril in accord with sacrosanct antiquity. And that nothing might be lacking to the credibility of the matter, we published both the names and the number (though we had forgotten the order) of those fathers, according to whose mutually harmonious and concordant opinion, in that place, both the utterances of the sacred law were expounded, and the rule of divine dogma was established: whom, to confirm the memory, it is by no means superfluous to recount here also.

XXX.

Sunt ergo hi viri quorum in illo Concilio vel tanquam judicum vel tanquam testium scripta recitata sunt. Sanctus Petrus Alexandrinus Episcopus doctor praestantissimus et martyr beatissimus. Sanctus Athanasius, ejusdem civitatis antistes, magister fidelissimus et confessor eminentissimus. Sanctus Theophilus, ejusdem item urbis Episcopus, vir fide, vita, scientia satis clarus, cui successit venerandus Cyrillus, qui nunc Alexandrinam illustrat Ecclesiam. 681Et ne forsitan unius civitatis ac provinciae doctrina haec putaretur, adhibita sunt etiam illa Cappadociae lumina, Sanctus Gregorius Episcopus et confessor de Nazianzo; Sanctus Basilius, Caesareae Cappadociae Episcopus et confessor; Sanctus item alter Gregorius Nyssenus Episcopus, fidei, conversationis, integritatis et sapientiae merito, fratre Basilio dignissimus. Sed ne sola Graecia aut Oriens tantum, verum etiam Occidentalis et Latinus orbis ita semper sensisse adprobaretur, lectae sunt quoque ibi quaedam ad quosdam Epistolae sancti Felicis martyris et sancti Julii, urbis Romae Episcoporum. Et ut non solum caput orbis, verum etiam latera illi judicio testimonium perhiberent, 682adhibitus est à Meridie beatissimus Cyprianus Episcopus Carthaginensis et martyr; a Septentrione sanctus Ambrosius Mediolanensis Episcopus. Hi sunt igitur omnes apud Ephesum sacrato Decalogi numero magistri, consiliarii, testes, judicesque producti; quorum beata illa Synodus doctrinam tenens, consilium sequens, credens testimonio, obediens judicio, absque taedio, praesumptione, et gratia de fidei regulis pronuntiavit. Quanquam multo amplior majorum numerus adhiberi potuerit, sed necesse non fuit; quia neque multitudine testium negotii tempora occupari oportebat, et decem illos non aliud vere sensisse quam caeteros omnes collegas suos nemo dubitabat.

XXX.

These, then, are the men whose writings were read in that Council, either as of judges or as of witnesses. Saint Peter, Bishop of Alexandria, a most excellent teacher and most blessed martyr. Saint Athanasius, prelate of the same city, a most faithful master and most eminent confessor. Saint Theophilus, likewise Bishop of the same city, a man renowned enough in faith, in life, in knowledge, whom the venerable Cyril succeeded, who now illumines the Church of Alexandria. 681And lest perchance this teaching should be thought that of a single city and province, there were adduced also those lights of Cappadocia: Saint Gregory, Bishop and confessor, of Nazianzus; Saint Basil, Bishop and confessor of Caesarea in Cappadocia; and likewise another Saint Gregory of Nyssa, Bishop, by the merit of his faith, conversation, integrity, and wisdom most worthy of his brother Basil. But lest Greece alone, or only the East, but also the Western and Latin world, should be shown to have ever thought thus, there were read there also certain Epistles, to certain persons, of Saint Felix the martyr and of Saint Julius, Bishops of the city of Rome. And that not only the head of the world, but also the flanks might bear testimony to that judgment, 682there was adduced from the South the most blessed Cyprian, Bishop of Carthage and martyr; from the North, Saint Ambrose, Bishop of Milan. These, then, are all the masters, counselors, witnesses, and judges brought forward at Ephesus in the consecrated number of the Decalogue; whose doctrine that blessed Synod holding, whose counsel following, believing their testimony, obeying their judgment, it pronounced concerning the rules of faith without weariness, without presumption, and without favor. Although a far greater number of the elders might have been adduced, yet it was not necessary; because neither ought the times of the business to be taken up by a multitude of witnesses, nor did anyone doubt that those ten had in truth thought nothing other than all the rest of their colleagues.

XXXI.

Post quae omnia, adjecimus etiam beati Cyrilli sententiam, quae gestis ipsis ecclesiasticis continetur. Namque cum lecta esset Sancti Capreoli, Episcopi Carthaginensis Epistola, qui nihil aliud intendebat et precabatur nisi ut, expugnata novitate, antiquitas defenderetur, ita Episcopus Cyrillus prolocutus est et definivit. Quod hìc quoque interponere non abs re videtur. Ait enim in fine gestorum: Et haec, inquit, quae lecta est Epistola venerandi et multum religiosi Episcopi Carthaginensis Capreoli, fidei gestorum inseretur; cujus aperta sententia est. Vult etenim antiquae fidei dogmata confirmari, novitia vero et superflue adinventa et impie promulgata, reprobari atque damnari. Omnes Episcopi adclamaverunt: Hae omnium voces sunt, haec omnes dicimus, hoc omnium votum est. Quae tandem omnium voces, aut quae omnium vota, nisi ut quod erat antiquitus traditum teneretur, quod adinventum nuper, exploderetur? Post quae admirati sumus et praedicavimus quanta Concilii illius fuerit humilitas et sanctitas, ut tot numero sacerdotes, pene ex majori parte Metropolitani, tantae eruditionis tantaeque doctrinae ut prope omnes possent de dogmatibus disputare, quibus propterea ipsa in unum congregatio audendi ab se aliquid et statuendi addere videretur fiduciam, nihil tamen novarent, nihil praesumerent, 683nihil sibi penitus adrogarent, sed omnimodis praecaverent ne aliquid posteris traderent quod ipsi a patribus non accepissent, et non solum in praesenti rem bene disponerent, verum etiam post futuris exempla praeberent ut et ipsi scilicet sacratae vetustatis dogmata colerent, profanae vero novitatis adinventa damnarent. Invecti etiam sumus in Nestorii sceleratam praesumptionem, quod sacram Scripturam se primum et solum intelligere, et omnes eos ignorasse jactaret, quicumque antè se, magisterii munere praediti, divina eloquia tractavissent, universos scilicet sacerdotes, universos confessores et martyres, quorum alii explanassent Dei legem, alii verò explanantibus consensissent vel credidissent, totam postremo etiam nunc errare et semper errasse adseveraret Ecclesiam, quae, ut ipsi videbatur, ignaros erroneosque doctores et secuta esset et sequeretur.

XXXI.

After all these, we added also the opinion of blessed Cyril, which is contained in the ecclesiastical acts themselves. For when the Epistle of Saint Capreolus, Bishop of Carthage, had been read — who aimed at and prayed for nothing else than that, novelty being vanquished, antiquity might be defended — thus Bishop Cyril spoke forth and defined. Which it seems not out of place to insert here also. For he says at the end of the acts: And this Epistle, he says, which has been read, of the venerable and most religious Bishop of Carthage, Capreolus, shall be inserted into the record of the faith; whose opinion is plain. For he wills that the dogmas of the ancient faith be confirmed, but that things novel, and superfluously devised, and impiously promulgated, be reproved and condemned. All the bishops cried out: These are the voices of all; this we all say; this is the wish of all. What at length are the voices of all, or what the wishes of all, but that what had been handed down of old should be held, and what had lately been devised should be rejected? After which we marveled and proclaimed how great was the humility and holiness of that Council: that so many bishops in number, well-nigh for the greater part Metropolitans, of such erudition and such learning that nearly all of them could dispute about dogmas — for which reason the very gathering of them into one might have seemed to add to itself confidence of daring and of establishing something — yet innovated nothing, presumed nothing, 683arrogated nothing at all to themselves, but in every way took care not to hand down anything to posterity which they had not themselves received from the fathers; and not only ordered the matter well in the present, but also furnished examples to those to come, that they too, namely, should cherish the dogmas of consecrated antiquity, but condemn the devices of profane novelty. We inveighed also against the wicked presumption of Nestorius, in that he boasted that he first and alone understood the sacred Scripture, and that all those had been ignorant, whosoever before him, endowed with the office of teaching, had handled the divine eloquences — all the bishops, namely, all the confessors and martyrs, of whom some had expounded the law of God, others had consented to or believed the expounders — and asserted, in short, that even now the whole Church errs, and had always erred, which, as it seemed to him, both had followed and would follow ignorant and erroneous teachers.

XXXII.

Quae omnia licet cumulate abundeque sufficerent ad profanas quasque novitates obruendas et exstinguendas, tamen ne quid deesse tantae plenitudini videretur, ad extremum adjecimus geminam Apostolicae Sedis auctoritatem, unam scilicet sancti Papae Sixti qui nunc Romanam Ecclesiam venerandus 684illustrat, alteram decessoris sui beatae memoriae Papae Coelestini, quam hìc quoque interponere necessarium judicavimus. Ait itaque sanctus Papa Xis tus in Epistola quam de causa Nestorii Antiocheno misit Episcopo: Ergo, inquit, quia, sicut ait Apostolus, fides una est, quae evidenter obtinuit, dicenda credamus et tenenda dicamus. Quae sunt tandem illa credenda et dicenda? Sequitur, et ait: Nihil ultra, inquit, liceat novitati, quia nihil addi convenit vetustati. Perspicua majorum fides et credulitas nulla coeni permixtione turbetur. Omnino apostolice: ut majorum credulitatem perspicuitatis lumine ornaret, novitias vero profanitates coeni permixtione describeret. Sed et sanctus Papa Coelestinus pari modo eademque sententia. Ait enim in Epistola quam Gallorum sacerdotibus misit, arguens eorum conniventiam quod antiquam fidem silentio destituentes, profanas novitates exsurgere paterentur: Merito, inquit, causa nos respicit, si silentio foveamus errorem. Ergo corripiantur hujusmodi; non sit his liberum habere pro voluntate sermonem. Hic aliquis fortasse addubitet quinam sint illi quos habere prohibeat liberum pro voluntate sermonem, vetustatis praedicatores, an novitatis adinventores. Ipse dicat, dubitationem legentium ipse dissolvat. Sequitur enim: Desinat, inquit, si ita res est (id est, si ita est ut apud me quidam urbes et provincias vestras criminantur, quod eas quibusdam novitatibus consentire noxia dissimulatione faciatis), desinat itaque, inquit, si ita res est, incessere novitas vetustatem. Ergo haec fuit beati Coelestini beata sententia, ut non vetustas cessaret obruere novitatem, sed potius novitas desineret incessere vetustatem.

XXXII.

All which things, although they would abundantly and superabundantly suffice to overwhelm and extinguish any profane novelties whatsoever, yet, lest anything should seem to be lacking to so great a fullness, at the last we added the twin authority of the Apostolic See — one, namely, of Saint Pope Sixtus, who now, venerable, illumines the Roman Church, 684the other of his predecessor of blessed memory Pope Coelestine, which we judged it necessary to insert here also. So then Saint Pope Xis tus says, in the Epistle which he sent concerning the case of Nestorius to the Bishop of Antioch: Therefore, he says, because, as the Apostle says, there is one faith, which has evidently prevailed, let us believe the things to be said, and say the things to be held. What at length are those things to be believed and to be said? He continues, and says: Let nothing further be permitted to novelty, because it is fitting that nothing be added to what is old. Let not the clear faith and belief of our elders be troubled by any admixture of filth. Wholly apostolic: to adorn the belief of the elders with the light of clearness, but to describe novel profanities by the admixture of filth. But Saint Pope Coelestine likewise, and with the same opinion. For he says, in the Epistle which he sent to the priests of the Gauls, reproving their connivance in that, forsaking the ancient faith by silence, they suffered profane novelties to arise: Deservedly, he says, does the cause regard us, if by silence we foster error. Therefore let such men be corrected; let them not be free to have speech according to their pleasure. Here perhaps someone may be in doubt who they are whom he forbids to have speech free according to their pleasure — the preachers of what is old, or the devisers of novelty. Let him himself say; let him himself dissolve the doubt of the readers. For he continues: Let it cease, he says, if the matter is so (that is, if it is so as certain persons charge before me concerning your cities and provinces, that you make them consent to certain novelties by a harmful dissimulation), let it cease then, he says, if the matter is so, let novelty cease to assail what is old. Therefore this was the blessed opinion of blessed Coelestine: that not what is old should cease to overwhelm novelty, but rather that novelty should cease to assail what is old.

XXXIII.

Quibus apostolicisque catholicis decretis quisquis refragatur, insultet primum omnium necesse est memoriae sancti Coelestini, qui statuit ut desineret incessere novitas vetustatem, deinde irrideat definita sancti Xysti, qui censuit ne ultra quicquam liceat novitati, quia nihil addi convenit vetustati, sed et beati Cyrilli statuta contemnat, qui venerandi Capreoli zelum magna praedicatione laudavit quod antiqua fidei dogmata confirmari cuperet, novitia vero adinventa damnari, Ephesinam quoque synodum, id est, totius pene Orientis sanctorum Episcoporum judicata proculcet, quibus divinitus placuit nihil aliud posteris credendum decernere nisi quod sacrata sibique in Christo consentiens 685Asanctorum patrum tenuisset antiquitas, quique etiam vociferantes et adclamantes, uno ore testificati sunt has esse omnium voces, hoc omnes optare, hoc omnes censere; ut sicut universi ferè antè Nestorium haeretici, contemnentes vetustatem, et adserentes novitatem, damnati fuissent, ità ipse quoque Nestorius, auctor novitatis, et impugnator vetustatis, condemnaretur. Quorum sacrosanctae et coelestis gratiae munere inspirata consensio si cui displicet, quid aliud sequitur, nisi ut profanitatem Nestorii adserat non jure damnatam? Ad extremum quoque universam Christi Ecclesiam et magistros ejus Apostolos et prophetas, praecipuèque tamen beatum Apostolum Paulum, velut quaedam purgamenta contemnat; illam, quòd à Religione colendae et excolendae semel sibi traditae 685Bfidei nunquàm recesserit; illum verò, qui scripserit: O Timothee, depositum custodi, devitans profanas 686Avocum novitates, et item: Siquis vobis annuntiaverit praeterquàm quod accepistis, anathema sit. Quòd si neque apostolica definita neque ecclesiastica decreta temeranda sunt, quibus secundùm sacrosanctam universitatis et antiquitatis consensionem cuncti semper haeretici, et ad extremum Pelagius, Coelestius, Nestorius, jure meritoque damnati sunt, necesse est profectò omnibus deinceps Catholicis, qui sese Ecclesiae matris legitimos filios probare student, ut sanctae sanctorum Patrum fidei inhaereant, adglutinentur, immoriantur, profanas verò profanorum novitates detestentur, horrescant, insectentur, persequantur.

Haec sunt ferè quae duobus Commonitoriis latiùs disserta, aliquantò nunc breviùs recapitulandi lege constricta sunt; ut memoria mea, cui adminiculandae 686Bista confecimus, et commonendi assiduitate reparetur, et prolixitatis fastidio non obruatur.

XXXIII.

Whosoever gainsays these apostolic and catholic decrees must needs first of all insult the memory of Saint Coelestine, who ordained that novelty should cease to assail what is old; then deride the definitions of Saint Sixtus, who decreed that nothing further be permitted to novelty, because it is fitting that nothing be added to what is old; and let him also contemn the ordinances of blessed Cyril, who with great proclamation praised the zeal of the venerable Capreolus, in that he desired that the dogmas of the ancient faith be confirmed, but the devices of novelty condemned; let him trample too the Ephesine synod, that is, the judgments of the holy Bishops of well-nigh the whole East, to whom it was divinely pleasing to decree that nothing else be believed by posterity than what the consecrated antiquity of the holy fathers, agreeing with itself in Christ, 685Ahad held; who also, crying out and acclaiming, testified with one mouth that these were the voices of all, that this was the desire of all, that this was the judgment of all; so that, as well-nigh all the heretics before Nestorius, contemning what is old and asserting novelty, had been condemned, so Nestorius himself too, the author of novelty and the assailer of what is old, should be condemned. If their consent, inspired by the gift of sacrosanct and heavenly grace, displeases anyone, what else follows but that he asserts the profanity of Nestorius not to have been justly condemned? At the last too, let him contemn the whole Church of Christ, and her masters the Apostles and prophets, but especially the blessed Apostle Paul, as though they were certain refuse: her, in that she has never departed from the religion of the faith once handed to her to be honored and cherished; 685Bhim, in that he wrote: O Timothy, keep the deposit, shunning the profane 686Anovelties of words, and likewise: If any man announce to you anything besides that which you have received, let him be anathema. But if neither apostolic definitions nor ecclesiastical decrees are to be violated, by which, according to the sacrosanct consent of universality and antiquity, all heretics ever, and at the last Pelagius, Coelestius, Nestorius, have been by right and desert condemned, it is assuredly necessary for all Catholics thenceforth, who are eager to prove themselves lawful sons of mother Church, that they cleave to, be glued to, and die in the holy faith of the holy Fathers, but that they detest, shudder at, pursue, and persecute the profane novelties of profane men.

These are, in sum, the things which, discussed more at large in the two Commonitoria, are now bound somewhat more briefly by the law of recapitulating; that my memory, for the aiding of which 686Bwe have composed these things, may both be restored by the assiduity of reminding, and not be overwhelmed by the tedium of prolixity.

The Commonitorium — a title best rendered 'reminder' or 'memorandum' — was written on the island of Lérins about 434 by the monk Vincent, who signs himself Peregrinus, the Pilgrim. It is the classic Western statement of how the Church holds its faith across time: the catholic rule is what has been believed everywhere, always, and by all (quod ubique, quod semper, quod ab omnibus creditum est), and doctrine grows only as a body grows, developing without ever becoming something other than itself. Migne prints the surviving first book together with the recapitulation that is all that remains of the lost second (PL 50, coll. 637C–686B), from the Corpus Corporum transcription of Baluze's edition. This is not the first English of the Commonitorium — it has been translated before, notably by C. A. Heurtley in the Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers and by Rudolph Morris in the Fathers of the Church — and we make no claim otherwise. We render it afresh because a text this central to how the tradition understands itself belongs in migne.app's own voice, set beside its Latin column for column. The gilt marks carry Migne's numbering: migne.app/pl/50/637 resolves to the opening.
