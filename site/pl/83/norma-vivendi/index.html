Norma vivendi

1247B341 1. Age, fili, ut oportet, age ut decet, age ut dignum est. Propone tibi ut non pecces, ut culpas tuas non reiteres, post lapsum denuo ne delinquas; bona et mala prout eveniunt tolera; quaecunque eveniunt mente libera sustine; patientiae et mansuetudini ante omnia operam des; irrisionem despiciendo supera; sagittas contumeliae patientiae clypeo frange; praepara contra omnem aspectum clypeum patientiae; contra linguae gladium tolerantiae praebe scutum, tacendo siquidem melius vinces (Vide Isidoriana, cap. 82).

The Rule of Living

1247B341 1. Act, my son, as you ought; act as is fitting; act as is worthy. Set it before yourself not to sin, not to repeat your faults, not to offend again after a fall; bear good things and bad as they come; endure with a free mind whatever comes; give your effort before all else to patience and gentleness; overcome mockery by despising it; break the arrows of insult upon the shield of patience; make ready the shield of patience against every look; against the sword of the tongue hold out the buckler of endurance — for by keeping silent you will conquer the better (Vide Isidoriana, cap. 82).

2.

Disce a Christo modestiam, disce et moderantiam, 1247Ctolerantiamque Christi attende, et non dolebis injurias. Maxima est virtus, si non laedis a quo laesus fueris, vel etsi laesus permittis. Maxima est virtus, si cuicunque nocere potuisti parcis; quando enim cruciaris propterea, peccata tua praecingunt te; quando minaris, mala tua iter faciunt; quidquid tibi adversi evenit, propter peccata tua tibi evenit. Consideratione igitur justitiae dolorem tempera; melius dolorem portabis, si pro quibus tibi eveniunt intendas.

2.

Learn modesty from Christ, learn moderation too, 1247Cand give heed to the endurance of Christ, and you will not grieve at injuries. It is the greatest virtue if you do not wound the one by whom you have been wounded, or if, even when wounded, you let it pass. It is the greatest virtue if you spare anyone you had the power to harm; for when you are tormented on this account, your own sins are girding you about; when you are threatened, your own evils are making their way. Whatever adversity comes upon you, it comes upon you because of your sins. Temper your grief, therefore, by considering justice; you will carry the grief better if you attend to the things for which it comes upon you.

3.

Cum igitur tibi derogatur, ora; cum maledicitur, tunc benedic; maledictioni benedictionem oppone, et irascentem patientia delinire stude; blandimento iracundiam fervescentem dispelle; nequitiam bonitate verbi vince; malitiam benignitate, inimicos omnes modestia placa, aliorum mala tua bonitate supera. Tranquilla mente illatas contumelias perde, aperi tranquillo corde dolores; vulnus in imo corde, quamvis grave sit, apertum evaporat, valde autem comedit animum inclusum. Quanto enim magis tegis, 1247Dtanto magis auges; aperi igitur grato animo, et non te excruciet.

3.

When, therefore, you are disparaged, pray; when you are cursed, then bless; set a blessing against the curse, and be eager to soothe the angry man by patience; drive off boiling wrath with a gentle word; conquer wickedness by goodness of speech; conquer malice by kindness, appease all your enemies by modesty, overcome the evils of others by your own goodness. Undo with a tranquil mind the insults brought against you; open up your griefs with a quiet heart. A wound in the depth of the heart, however grave it be, evaporates when opened, but eats away the soul terribly when shut in. For the more you cover it, 1247Dthe more you increase it; open it, therefore, with a grateful spirit, and it will not torture you.

4.

Si tristaveris in aliquo fratrem tuum, satisfac ei. Si peccaveris in illum, poenitentiam age cum eo. Si offenderis aliquem, propitia eum prece, parce velociter, ad reconciliationem offensioni tuae cito veniam postula; non dormites, nisi convertaris ad pacem; non requiescas, nisi reconciliatus fueris fratri tuo. Revoca eum celerrimo dilectionis affectu. Revoca eum humilitate ad gratiam; humilitatis affectu illi te prosterne, et supplici modo veniam deprecare; petenti quoque tibi veniam libenter indulge, et poscenti indulgentiam placatus dimitte.

4.

If you have made your brother sad in any matter, make it good to him. If you have sinned against him, do penance with him. If you have offended anyone, win him over by entreaty, spare him swiftly, quickly ask pardon in order to be reconciled for your offense; do not sleep unless you have turned back to peace; do not rest unless you have been reconciled to your brother. Call him back with the swiftest affection of love. Call him back to favor by humility; prostrate yourself before him in the affection of humility, and beg pardon in a suppliant manner; and when you yourself are asked for pardon, grant it freely, and, being appeased, release the one who asks for indulgence.

5.

Reverentiam sanctam amplectere, et confestim benigna suscipe charitate; peccanti juste non dimittas, sed culpam sciturus, quia in te est venturum judicium; non habebis indulgentiam, nisi dederis. Etsi ille non supplicet, sibique humiliter dimitti non postulet, si peccatum suum mala consuetudine non 1248Bcognoscat conscientia, tu tamen ex corde relaxa, veniam quoque propria voluntate concede; aufer a corde fraternam offensam, vel offensionem, et alienae nequitiae non servito; odium enim a Deo separat, nec sanguine fuso deletur.

5.

Embrace holy reverence, and receive it at once with kindly charity; do not lightly dismiss the sinner justly, but know the fault, since the judgment is coming upon you; you will not have indulgence unless you have given it. And even if he does not entreat you, and does not humbly ask that it be forgiven him — if by a bad habit his conscience does not 1248Brecognize its own sin — do you nevertheless release it from the heart, and grant pardon of your own will as well; take the brotherly offense, or the taking of offense, away from your heart, and do not be a slave to another's wickedness; for hatred separates a man from God, and is not blotted out even by the shedding of blood.

6.

Pacem ama, pacem dilige, pacem cum omnibus habe et tene, 342 omnesque in mansuetudine et charitate complectere; praepara amplius amare quam ameris ipse; non sis in pace infidus, non sis levis in amicitia, retine semper vinculum constantiae; odientes ad pacem invita. 1248C

6.

Love peace, cherish peace, have and hold peace with all men, 342 and embrace all in gentleness and charity; make ready to love more than you are loved yourself; do not be faithless in peace, do not be fickle in friendship, hold always to the bond of constancy; invite those who hate you to peace. 1248C

7.

Discerne te tuo, non alieno judicio; nec ex alieno sermone, sed ex tua te mente metire. Nemo melius et magis scire potest quid tu scis quam tu, cum conscius tui es. Quibus vero prodest, si malus, si bonus praediceris; aut quae laus hominum pertinet ad te, si alius es quam laudaris? Quapropter vita simulationem, vita hypocrisin, sub obscuriori veste non simula sanctitatem. Qualis haberi vis, talis esto professione tua et habitu; et in incessu tuo sit simplicitas, et in motu puritas; nihil dedecoris, nihil lasciviae, nihil petulantiae, nihil insolentiae in incessu tuo appareat; gestus enim corporis signum est mentis; non praebeas de te aliis spectaculum, nec aliis obtrectandi locum.

7.

Judge yourself by your own judgment, not another's; and measure yourself not by another's talk, but by your own mind. No one can know better or more fully what you know than you, since you are conscious of yourself. And to whom is it any profit if you are foretold bad, or good? Or what does the praise of men have to do with you, if you are other than you are praised for being? Wherefore shun pretense, shun hypocrisy, do not counterfeit holiness beneath a drabber garment. Be, in your profession and your habit, such as you wish to be reckoned; and let there be simplicity in your walk and purity in your movement; let nothing unseemly, nothing wanton, nothing impudent, nothing insolent appear in your walk; for the bearing of the body is the sign of the mind. Do not offer yourself to others as a spectacle, nor give others occasion to disparage.

8.

Non te adjungas levibus personis, nec te admisceas vanis; vita malos, cave iniquos, fuge malignos, sperne ignaros, fuga a te turbas hominum, maxime illius aetatis qui ad vitia proni sunt. Bonis jungere, et sanctis individue sociare. Si feceris, socius 1248Dconversationis illius eorum eris et virtutum; periculum enim est pessimis sociari, melius est eorum odium quam consortium.

8.

Do not attach yourself to frivolous persons, nor mingle with the empty; shun the bad, beware of the wicked, flee the malicious, spurn the ignorant, drive from yourself the crowds of men, especially of that age which is prone to vices. Join yourself to the good, and be inseparably allied with the holy. If you do so, you will be a companion 1248Dof their manner of life and of their virtues; for it is a peril to be allied with the worst, and their hatred is better than their fellowship.

9.

Claude aures, ne audias aliquid impudicum; vanus enim sermo cito polluit mentem, et facile laeditur turpibus, et malis omnibus, agiturque libenter quod auditur. Nemo enim creditur odisse quorum relatione non laeditur; nihil ex ore tuo procedat quo turpescas, vel quod turpe sit; sed haec erumpant ex ore tuo, et ex labiis, quae aures non polluant audientium; sermo enim vanus conscientiae vanae est index. Mores hominis lingua pandit, et qualis sermo ostenditur, talis animus comprobatur. Ab otioso sermone compesce linguam, nec inania verba loquaris; sermo enim otiosus non erit sine judicio. Unusquisque enim redditurus est rationem sermonum suorum, ante uniuscujusque hominis faciem stabunt verba sua. Qui verba mala non reprimit, ad noxia cito transit; et qui minima non respuit, in 1249Amaxima valde prorumpit; minorum namque culpa majorem generat.

9.

Close your ears, that you may hear nothing shameless; for empty talk quickly pollutes the mind, and the mind is easily injured by base things and by all evils, and what is heard is gladly done. For no one is believed to hate those by the report of whom he is not injured. Let nothing proceed from your mouth by which you may be made base, or which is itself base; but let those things burst forth from your mouth and from your lips which do not pollute the ears of the hearers; for empty speech is the index of an empty conscience. The tongue lays open a man's character, and as his speech is shown to be, such is his mind proved to be. Restrain your tongue from idle talk, and do not speak empty words; for idle speech will not be without judgment. For each man is to render an account of his words; before the face of every man his own words shall stand. He who does not check evil words passes quickly on to harmful ones; and he who does not reject the least things breaks out utterly into 1249Athe greatest; for guilt in lesser matters begets guilt in greater.

10.

Particulatim crescunt vitia, et dum parva non cavemus, in magnis prolabimur; minora itaque devita, et ad majora non devenies. Ea quae loqueris gravitate atque doctrina existant culta, sitque sermo tuus irreprehensibilis, ad exspectationem audientium utilis; stude loqui omnino quodlibet secundum quod oportet; discerne quid loquaris, quid taceas. Intende, et loquere, et loquendo peritus esto. Multum autem delibera quod loqui vis, ne revocare non possis quod dixeris; fuge casus linguae, nec pateat injuriae jaculis os tuum. Cum amico semper habeto silentium, vide opportunitatem loquendi, et proferendi sermonem, et expendendi. Maneat in ore tuo mensura, in sermone tuo sit statera, abscinde et a lingua tua vitium detrahendi, et de alieno malo os tuum, non coinquines; quod in alio detrahis, hoc in te potius pertimesce.

10.

Vices grow bit by bit, and while we do not guard against small ones, we slip and fall in great ones; avoid the lesser, therefore, and you will not arrive at the greater. Let the things you say stand out well cultivated by weight and by learning, and let your speech be beyond reproach, useful to the expectation of the hearers; be zealous to speak whatever you speak wholly as you ought; discern what you should say and what you should keep silent. Give heed, and speak, and be skilled in speaking. But deliberate much on what you mean to say, lest you be unable to call back what you have said; flee the slips of the tongue, and let your mouth not lie open to the darts of injury. With a friend always keep silence; watch for the right moment for speaking, and for bringing forth a word, and for weighing it. Let measure remain in your mouth; let there be a balance in your speech; cut away from your tongue also the vice of disparaging, and do not defile your mouth with another's evil; what you disparage in another, this rather fear in yourself.

11.

Quod inter se loquuntur homines, hoc nunquam 1249Bscire desideres, 343 nec quaeras aliquem qui dicat tibi; omitte causam quae ad animam tuam non pertinet, nec oblitus morum tuorum alienos perscruteris; tanto animo corrige vitia tua, quanto studio prospexeris aliena.

11.

What men say among themselves, never 1249Bdesire to know, 343 nor seek out anyone to tell you; leave aside the matter that does not pertain to your own soul, and do not, forgetful of your own character, scrutinize the characters of others; correct your own vices with as much spirit as the zeal with which you have looked into another's.

12.

Omne genus mendacii summopere fuge; non studium loquacium fratrum, nec qualibet fallacia vitam alicujus defendas; nullum mendacium justum, quod a veritate discordat, in quo etiam prohibetur duplex juramentum, assiduitas enim perjurii assuetudinem facit. Fac bonum quod spopondisti, non in verba facilis, sed etiam factis et operibus difficilis: sine consideratione unquam nil facias, vel praesumas; quando poteris facere, non pollicearis facere. Multum Deo reus eris, si non reddideris quod voves; in malis autem promissis rescinde fidem. Non est promissio quae dolore adimpletur.

12.

Flee every kind of lie with the utmost care; do not defend anyone's life by the zeal of talkative brethren, nor by any deceit whatever; no lie is just, since it is at variance with truth — and in this matter a double oath is likewise forbidden, for the constant practice of perjury breeds a habit of it. Do the good that you have promised, being not ready with words but slow rather in deeds and works too: never do anything, or presume anything, without consideration; when you will be able to do a thing, do not promise to do it. You will be greatly guilty before God if you do not render what you vow; but in evil promises, break your word. That is no promise which is fulfilled with grief.

13.

Si vis tuas adimplere virtutes, prodere noli; 1249Cocculta virtutes pro elatione; absconde bona facta pro arrogantia; fuge videri quod esse meruisti; qui manifestando poteras amittere, tacendo custodi. Vitia vero cordis tui revela, pravas cogitationes manifesta illico, peccatum enim revelatum cito curatur, simulata culpa crescit. Si patet vitium, ex magno fit pusillum; si latet peccatum, ex minimo fit maximum. Melius est autem ut vites vitium quam ut emendes, ne forte cum incurreris, pessima consuetudine revocare non possis. Consuetudinis vincula vix solvuntur, et inolita diu tardius corriguntur.

13.

If you wish to bring your virtues to completion, do not betray them; 1249Chide your virtues, on account of pride; conceal your good deeds, on account of arrogance; flee to be seen as what you have deserved to be; what by making it known you could lose, keep safe by keeping silent. But reveal the vices of your heart, disclose evil thoughts on the spot — for a sin revealed is quickly cured, while a fault dissembled grows. If the vice lies open, it becomes small from great; if the sin lies hidden, it becomes greatest from least. But it is better that you avoid a vice than that you amend it, lest perchance, when you have run into it, you be unable, through a very bad habit, to call yourself back. The chains of habit are loosed only with difficulty, and things long ingrained are corrected more slowly.

14.

Incipientem diu delibera sententiam, et quod vis diu agere, diu exquire, proba, et age; nil enim in agendo praecipiti consilio, sed mora melius est. In rebus autem bonis tarditas removeatur; in bonis enim dilatio nocet, et quod expedit differre impedit. In bonis absit remissa segnities, absit negligentia, carnis vitia cito captant inertes; per torporem enim vires et ingenium defluunt, et desidia natura corrumpitur; hebetudo scientiae lumen exstinguit, solertia 1249Dautem meliorem reddit; ardentius fit ingenium subjuncto studio, et tardiora ingenia studiis acuuntur. Torporem naturae industria excitat, spiritus tarditatem assiduitas acuit. Expergiscentia perficit spiritum, expergiscentia plus scitur, saepe namque consuetudinem superat, saepe ex consuetudine naturae est effectus; assiduitas enim mores facit, jugis usus in naturam se vertit, quod cum difficultate per usum sine difficultate perficies.

14.

Deliberate long on an opinion at its beginning, and what you intend to do at length, search out at length, test, and do; for in acting, nothing is better than delay, and nothing worse than headlong counsel. But in good matters let slowness be put away; for in good things delay does harm, and to put off what is expedient is to hinder it. In good things let slack sluggishness be absent, let negligence be absent; the vices of the flesh quickly seize the idle; for through torpor strength and talent flow away, and nature is corrupted by sloth; dullness puts out the light of knowledge, while skill 1249Dmakes it better; talent becomes more ardent when zeal is joined to it, and slower talents are sharpened by studies. Industry rouses the torpor of nature; persistence sharpens the slowness of the spirit. Wakefulness perfects the spirit; by wakefulness more is known — for it often overcomes habit, and often what belongs to nature is the effect of habit; for persistence makes character, and continual practice turns itself into nature, and what you now do with difficulty you will accomplish, through practice, without difficulty.

15.

Disce quod nescis, ne doctor inutilis inveniaris; bonum autem quod didiceris doce; sapientiam cum caeteris impertieris, tibi hanc auges, sapientia exinde longior fit; retinendo minor est, et dum plus confertur, abundat. Verba tua cum praecedunt opus, quae ore promis, quae ore promittis, opere adimple; quae verbis doces, exsecutione exhibe et ostende. Esto non solum magister, sed imitator virtutum. Si doces, et facis, tunc gloriosus eris. Non enim satis est laudare quod dixeris, nisi dictis facta commisceas, 1250AIn doctrina tui ipsius ab humana te laude tempora; sic instrue alios, ut teipsum custodias, sic doce, ut humilitatis gratiam non amittas.

15.

Learn what you do not know, lest you be found a useless teacher; and teach the good you have learned. When you share wisdom with others, you increase it for yourself; wisdom thereby grows longer; by holding it back it grows less, and while it is bestowed more widely, it abounds. When your words go before your work — what you utter with the mouth, what you promise with the mouth, fulfill in deed; what you teach in words, display and show in execution. Be not only a master of the virtues but an imitator of them. If you teach and also do, then you will be glorious. For it is not enough to commend what you have said, unless you mingle deeds with your words. 1250AIn the teaching of your own self, hold yourself back from human praise; so instruct others that you may keep guard over yourself; so teach that you may not lose the grace of humility.

16.

Cave, ne, dum alios docendo erigis, ipse laudis appetitione derogeris. 344 Cum autem instruis eos, obscuritate noli uti, sed ita dic ut intelligaris, ne loquendo simplicibus displiceas, ne prudentes offendas; juxta spiritum audientium erit sermo doctorum. Secundum mores incipienda est doctrina; juxta vulnus adhibenda sunt remedia, variae enim voluntates diversam disciplinam desiderant. Unusquisque igitur secundum suam professionem est docendus. Inspicienda est utilitas personarum, et unumquemque taliter in erudiendo tracta. Committe omnibus secreta professionis tuae, loquere aperte.

16.

Beware lest, while you lift others up by teaching them, you be yourself disparaged through an appetite for praise. 344 And when you instruct them, do not use obscurity, but speak so as to be understood, lest by your speech you displease the simple or offend the prudent; the discourse of teachers will be according to the spirit of the hearers. Teaching must be begun according to men's characters; the remedies must be applied according to the wound, for different wills call for different discipline. Each man, therefore, is to be taught according to his own profession. The profit of the persons must be looked to, and you must handle each one accordingly in instructing him. Entrust to all the secrets of your profession; speak openly.

17.

Cunctis operta paucis annuntia; ne erubescas loqui quod nosti defendere; et quod tibi scientiae sentis deesse, ab aliis quaere; quaerendo enim obtecta clarescunt, et difficilia conferendo aperiuntur. 1250BNulla sit tibi curiositas sciendi latentia, nec ultra quam scriptum erit quaeras scientiam. Non cupias quod scire non licet. In disputatione non contradicas justitiae; nec contendas evacuare quod rectum est; in omni disputatione rectum tene, et plus dilige audire quam loqui. In principio audi, in fine loquere. In omni re finis attendatur, extrema quaerantur, meliorque est sermo novissimus quam primus.

17.

Announce to all what is hidden from a few; do not blush to speak what you know how to defend; and what you feel to be lacking to your knowledge, seek from others; for by seeking, covered things become clear, and hard things are opened up by comparing. 1250BLet there be in you no curiosity to know hidden things, nor seek knowledge beyond what shall have been written. Do not desire what it is not permitted to know. In debate do not speak against justice; do not strive to make void what is right; in every debate hold to what is right, and love to hear more than to speak. At the beginning, listen; at the end, speak. In every matter let the end be attended to, let the last things be sought out — and the last word is better than the first.

18.

Venerare omnes scientia et vita meliores, et unumquemque pro suo merito sanctitatis potiori gradui competentem reverentiam tribue, nec te aequalem sanctiori exhibeas. Senioribus praesta obedientiam, et eorum senio obsequio obedi. Cunctis in praeceptis sic obtempera, ut obtemperando hominum votis Deum non offendas. Malum visus facere, non consuescas, nec adhuc si poenae immineant, peccanti cuiquam non aquiescas; melius namque est pati mortem quam perniciosa mala adimplere. Non solum quippe factorum, sed conscius peccati tenetur obnoxius; 1250Cneque immunis est a scelere qui, ut faceret male, obedivit. Similis est qui obtemperat malo ei qui facit malum; facientem et obsequentem pari poena constringe.

18.

Reverence all who are better in knowledge and in life, and to each one render the reverence befitting a higher rank according to his own merit of holiness, and do not present yourself as the equal of a holier man. Render obedience to your elders, and obey their old age with deference. Comply with all in their precepts in such a way that by complying with men's wishes you do not offend God. Do not grow accustomed to doing the evil of sight, nor, even if punishments hang over you, do not give consent to anyone who is sinning; for it is better to suffer death than to carry out ruinous evils. For a man is held liable not only as a doer of deeds, but as one privy to the sin; 1250Cnor is he free from the crime who obeyed in order that evil be done. He who complies with evil is like the one who does evil; bind the doer and the compliant under an equal penalty.

19.

A subditis magis venerari quam timeri stude, ut tibi plus dilectionis officio quam contradictionis necessitate adhaereant. Subjecti te plus revereantur quam timeant; ex reverentia enim amor procedit, et odium tremor confert; fidem metus tollit, affectus restituit; timor non servat diuturnam fidem; ubi timor audet, eum sequitur robur; ubi timor, audentia sequitur; et ubi metus, desperatio occurrit. Quapropter tempera oris sementem, summaque bonitate subditos convenit regere; non sis terribilis in subjectis, sed sic dominare, ut tibi delectent servire.

19.

Be zealous to be revered by those under you rather than feared, so that they may cleave to you more by the service of love than by the necessity of opposition. Let your subjects revere you more than they fear you; for love proceeds from reverence, and trembling brings hatred; fear takes away trust, affection restores it; fear does not preserve a lasting fidelity; where fear grows bold, strength follows it; where there is fear, boldness follows; and where there is dread, despair comes on. Wherefore temper the sowing of your mouth; and it is fitting to rule your subjects with the utmost goodness; do not be terrible toward those set under you, but so rule that they delight to serve you.

20.

Tene modum in omni tempore, ut nihil intemperatum agas, nec minus, nec ultra quam oportet, nec in bonis nisi moderatus esse non debes. Omnia enim medicamenta utilia sunt, et cum tempore omnia fiunt utilia; recta enim immoderato usu corrumpuntur, 1250Domnis nimietas in vitio est; prospice igitur quid aptum sit tibi, et tempori, et ubi, quando, quare, quandiu facere debeatur; causas rerum et tempora inspice, et singulorum operum discretiones diligenter distingue.

20.

Keep measure at every season, so that you do nothing immoderate, neither less nor more than you ought — nor ought you to be anything but moderate even in good things. For all medicines are useful, and with the right occasion all things become useful; for right things are spoiled by immoderate use, 1250Dand every excess lies in vice; look forward, therefore, to what is fitting for you and for the occasion, and where, when, why, and how long a thing ought to be done; examine the causes and the occasions of things, and carefully distinguish the discriminations proper to each work.

21.

Dignosce omnia quae agis, quare bonum incipias, quare peragas; in omni operatione discretionem tene, nec in aliquo indiscretus sis. 345 Cum bene discreveris opus tuum, multa sunt consuetudine vitiata, multa pravo usu praesumpta, et multa contra pudicos mores usurpata; tunc minime consuetudinem serva et legem, usus auctoritati cedat, pravum usum lex et ratio vincant. Testimonio tuo nulli noceas, nec ad alicujus periculum vocem testificationis adhibeas. Sermo tuus nec animam cujusquam, nec res impediat, nullum contra veritatem defendas.

21.

Discern all that you do — why you begin a good thing, why you carry it through; in every operation hold to discretion, and be indiscreet in nothing. 345 When you have well discerned your work, you will find that many things have been corrupted by custom, many presumed upon through bad practice, and many usurped against chaste morals; then keep custom and the law least of all — let practice yield to authority, let law and reason conquer bad practice. Harm no one by your testimony, nor lend the voice of witness to anyone's peril. Let your speech obstruct neither anyone's soul nor his goods; defend no one against the truth.

22.

Cum judicas, ne ullius personae affectu deflectaris, a viro paupere, aut a divite, causam prospice, non personam. Sperne munus, ne per hoc justitia 1251Acorrumpatur; munera enim semper veritatem praevaricant, et cito a viro violatur justitia. De justo judicio temporalia lucra non appetas, pro justitia nullum tibi praemium quaeras dum judicas, pro futuro serva mercedem, nec quaeras in terra rependi quod tibi in futuro debetur. Excute manus tuas ab omni munere, si in coelis velis habitare; in judicio quoque sine misericordia sedeas. Custodi justitiam discretionis, et noli plus justus esse quam oportet. Omne enim quod nimium est vitium est.

22.

When you judge, do not be turned aside by partiality toward any person, whether from a poor man or from a rich; look to the case, not the person. Spurn a gift, lest through it justice 1251Abe corrupted; for gifts always play the traitor to truth, and justice is quickly violated by a man. Do not seek temporal gains from a just judgment; seek no reward for yourself for justice while you are judging; keep your wage for the future, and do not seek to be repaid on earth what is owed you in the world to come. Shake your hands free of every gift, if you wish to dwell in heaven; in judgment, too, sit without mercy. Keep the justice of discretion, and do not be more just than you ought. For everything that is excessive is a vice.

23.

Impia justitia est fragilitati humanae non ignoscere; non igitur ames damnare, sed emendare potius quam corrigere. Tene rigorem in discretione justitiae, judicii examen sequatur pietas, disciplinae rigorem temperet indulgentia, ita clemens esto in alienis delictis, sicut in tuis, nec quemquam districtius judices quam sicut teipsum. Sic alios judica, ut te judicari cupis; dum enim indulges aliena peccata, tibi misereris; judicium quod aliis imponis ipse portabis. In 1251Beo enim in quo judicas es judicandus; in qua mensura mensus fueris, remetietur tibi.

23.

It is an impious justice not to pardon human frailty; do not, therefore, love to condemn, but rather to amend than to correct. Keep rigor in the discretion of justice; let mercy follow upon the scrutiny of judgment; let indulgence temper the rigor of discipline; be as merciful in others' offenses as in your own, and judge no one more strictly than you would yourself. So judge others as you desire to be judged; for while you pardon another's sins, you have mercy on yourself; the judgment you lay upon others you will yourself carry. For in 1251Bthat in which you judge, you are to be judged; in what measure you shall have measured, it will be measured back to you.

24.

Omnia quae inquisieris cum justitia definias, nunquam judices suspicionis arbitrio; ante proba, tunc judica; non enim qui accusatur, sed criminator reus est; periculum est aliquid praesumptuose judicare. In ambiguis de judicio serva sententiam. Qui nosti tua, quando nescis, divino serva judicio; non nitaris damnare judicio humano quod Deus suo examini servat; incerta non judicemus, quoadusque novissime veniat Dominus, qui latentia producat in lucem. Quamvis enim non credenda sunt, ideo tamen dimittentur, nisi quae certis indiciis approbantur, et non quae manifesto examini committuntur, et non quae ordine judiciario comprobantur.

24.

Determine with justice all the things you have inquired into; never judge by the verdict of suspicion; first prove, then judge; for it is not the one accused, but the accuser, who is guilty; it is a peril to judge anything presumptuously. In doubtful matters, hold back your sentence in judging. You who know your own affairs, when you do not know, leave the matter to the divine judgment; do not lean upon a human judgment to condemn what God keeps for his own scrutiny; let us not judge uncertain things, until at the last the Lord shall come, who will bring hidden things into the light. For although they are not to be believed, yet on that account they will be dismissed — except such as are proved by sure evidences, and not such as are committed to open scrutiny, and not such as are established by the order of judicial process.

25.

In summo honore tibi suprema sit veritas; quamvis sublimis sis potestatis, in te celsitudinem reprime; non te extollat honor, sed praesideat humilitas culmen sublimitatis. Tanto esto majori humilitate conspicuus, quanto es magna dignitate praeclarus. 1251CImpositas tibi curas humiliter exple, et traditum tibi ministerium mente subdita suscipe. Esto obediens divinae dispositioni, et voluntati ejus contraire non audeas. Jura potestatis adeptae moderanter exerce, et omnia non turbulento, sed tranquillo corde dispone. Cave honores, quos sine culpa tenere non poteris. 1252ASublimitas enim honorum magnitudo scelerum, in majori gradu sine dubio poena major.

25.

Let the highest truth be held by you in the highest honor; however lofty the power you hold, press down loftiness in yourself; let honor not exalt you, but let humility preside over the summit of your high station. Be conspicuous by so much greater humility as you are illustrious with great dignity. 1251CFulfill humbly the cares laid upon you, and take up with a submissive mind the ministry handed over to you. Be obedient to the divine dispensation, and do not dare to go against his will. Exercise with moderation the rights of the power you have obtained, and set all things in order with a tranquil, not a turbulent, heart. Beware of honors which you will not be able to hold without guilt. 1252AFor loftiness of honors is a greatness of crimes, and in a higher rank the punishment is without doubt greater.

26.

Si vis esse quietus, nihil saeculi appetas. Semper requiem mentis habebis, si a te curam mundi abjicias, semperque quiete aeterna frueris, 346 si te a strepitu terrenarum rerum abstraxeris. Abjice a te quidquid bonum propositum impedire poterit, et toto animo odies damna quae diligit mundus. Quidquid habes, habeto ad misericordiam. Suffragatur virtus tua in operibus. Non eligas cui miserearis, ne forte praetereas illum qui meretur accipere. Omnibus da, ne forte cui non dederis ipse sit Christus; et cui largiris, cum hilaritate largire; et quidquid tribuis, cum affectu tribue; tribue misericordiam sine murmuratione, praebe eleemosynam sine taedio.

26.

If you wish to be at rest, desire nothing of the world. You will always have rest of mind if you cast the care of the world away from you, and you will always enjoy eternal quiet 346 if you withdraw yourself from the din of earthly things. Cast away from you whatever will be able to hinder a good purpose, and hate with your whole soul the losses which the world loves. Whatever you have, have it for mercy's sake. Your virtue lends its support in works. Do not pick out the one on whom you will have mercy, lest perchance you pass by the one who deserves to receive. Give to all, lest perchance the one to whom you have not given be Christ himself; and to whomever you give generously, give with cheerfulness; and whatever you bestow, bestow with affection; bestow mercy without murmuring, offer alms without weariness.

27.

Major autem sit benevolentia quam quod datur. Major sit gratia quam quod impenditur; tale enim erit opus tuum qualis fuerit intentio tua. Quod enim affectu bono dispensatur, hoc accipit Deus; qui 1252Bautem cum taedio dat, perdit mercedem suam. Qui cum tristitia manum porrigit, fructum amittit remunerationis; non est misericordia, ubi non est benevolentia. De tuis justis laboribus ministra pauperibus. Non auferas uni unde tribuas alteri. Nihil prodest si alium inde reficis unde alium inanem facis. Condemnat miseratio ista, non propitiat. Bonum quod facis misericordiae causa, non jactantiae, facito; nil propter temporalem opinionem, nihil propter famam, sed propter vitam aeternam et Dei charitatem. Quidquid agis, pro futura mercede age, aeternae remunerationis exspectatio te teneat. Amplius quidquid egeris causa gloriae, hoc est facti tui.

27.

But let the good will be greater than what is given. Let the favor be greater than what is laid out; for your work will be such as your intention has been. For what is dispensed with a good affection, this God receives; but he who 1252Bgives with weariness loses his reward. He who stretches out his hand with sadness forfeits the fruit of the recompense; there is no mercy where there is no good will. Minister to the poor out of your own just labors. Do not take from one in order to bestow on another. It is of no profit if you refresh one from that whereby you leave another empty. Such compassion condemns; it does not win favor. The good that you do, do for mercy's sake, not for boasting; nothing for the sake of temporal reputation, nothing for the sake of fame, but for the sake of eternal life and the charity of God. Whatever you do, do for the reward to come; let the expectation of eternal recompense hold you fast. Beyond that, whatever you have done for the sake of glory — this is the whole of your deed.

28.

Si dederis ita, ut hominibus innotescat, non homini, sed propriae personae praestitisti, nec quod ad aeternae, sed quod ad hujus [ Forte, hujus vitae] gloriam proficit. Ecce accepisti modica, data est tibi norma vivendi; nulla te ignorantia excusat, non es jam vitae nescius, non es imprudens, aut ignarus, legem 1252Cquam debes sequi, dispositionis qualis esse debeas, descripsi. Cognitiones mandatorum habes. Jam scis quid est recte vivere, vide ne ultra offendas, vide ne quod recipis legendo contemnas vivendo. Bonum scire, acceptum retine, et imple opere quod didicisti praeceptione. Vide deinceps ne bonum quod accepisti despicias. Amen.

28.

If you give so that it becomes known to men, you have given not to a man but to your own person, and not to what profits toward eternal glory, but toward the glory of this [ Forte, hujus vitae]. Behold, you have received small things; a rule of living is given you; no ignorance excuses you; you are no longer unacquainted with life, not imprudent or unknowing: I have described the law 1252Cyou ought to follow, and what manner of ordering you ought to have. You have knowledge of the commandments. Now you know what it is to live rightly; see that you offend no further; see that you do not despise in living what you receive in reading. It is good to know; keep what you have received, and fulfill in work what you learned by precept. See henceforth that you do not despise the good you have received. Amen.
