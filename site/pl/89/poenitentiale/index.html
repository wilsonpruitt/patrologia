Egbert of York

Egbertus Eboracensis

d. 19 November 766

Bishop of York from 732 and, when Pope Gregory III sent him the pallium in 735, its first archbishop since Paulinus a century before. Bede wrote him a long letter of advice in 734, the last thing Bede is known to have written, urging reform of the Northumbrian church. Egbert was the brother of King Eadberht of Northumbria, and he founded the cathedral school at York in which Alcuin was trained. A short dialogue on church discipline is securely his. The penitential material that circulated under his name, in Latin and in Old English, is largely later compilation, and how much of it goes back to him is disputed.

PRAEFATIO.

  • 401A

Cum quis ad poenitentiam suam abit, tunc cum maximo timore Dei et humilitate coram ipso se extendat; et oret flente voce, ut eum poenitentiam facere doceat omnium eorum delictorum quae adversus Dei voluntatem fecerit; et confiteatur illi malefacta sua, ut sacerdos sciat quam poenitentiam ipsi imponere debeat. Tunc sacerdos eum interroget, quamnam fidem in Deum habeat; et ipsum vario modo exhortetur ad animae suae necessitatem, et admoneat, ac ita dicat: Credisne in Deum omnipotentem, et in Filium, et in Spiritum sanctum? Credisne quod omnes homines a mortuis resurgent in die judicii? Num te poenitet omnium eorum quae tu malefecisti, dixisti, et cogitavisti? Visne remittere 401Bomnibus iis qui unquam in te peccaverunt? Si dixerit Volo, tunc ei dicat: Deus omnipotens tui misereatur, et mihi concedat quod debeo. Jejuna quolibet die hoc tempore quadragesimali ad Nonam, et abstine ab albo; et cave tibi ab octo capitalibus vitiis, 402Ane ea committas; quae sunt homicidium, et furtum, et perjuria, et avaritia, et injustus concubitus, et luxuria, et detractio, et falsum testimonium; et observa duodecim quadragesimae dies, qui quovis anno occurrunt, et cave tibi a sacrificio barbaro, et veneficio, et concubitu cum virgine, et biloquio, et superbia, et ambitione aliorum hominum possessionis: cave tibi ab his omnibus, et dilige Dominum tuum cum toto animo, et cum omni virtute, et omnibus viribus, et toto corde intimo firmiter; et esto benignus erga pauperes, et mansuetus, et eleemosynas distribuens, et diligenter ecclesiam visitans, et diligenter decimas dans Ecclesiae Dei, ac pauperibus: tunc Deus tibi benevolus, et beneficus, et misericors erit, et tu regnabis cum ipso in saecula saeculorum 402Babsque fine, si ita feceris; et oro te, ut mihi dicas omnia quae unquam malefecisti, quoniam tibi expedit pudefieri nunc hic coram me solo misero, quam deinde coram Deo in magno judicio, ubi coelicolae, et terricolae, et inferi omnes erunt congregati.

PREFACE.

  • 401A

When anyone goes away to his penance, then let him stretch himself out before him with the greatest fear of God and humility; and let him pray with weeping voice that he teach him to do penance for all those offences which he has committed against the will of God; and let him confess to him his evil deeds, so that the priest may know what penance he ought to impose on him. Then let the priest ask him what faith he has in God; and let him exhort him in various ways to the need of his soul, and admonish him, and speak thus: Dost thou believe in God almighty, and in the Son, and in the Holy Spirit? Dost thou believe that all men will rise from the dead on the day of judgment? Dost thou repent of all those things which thou hast done ill, said, and thought? Wilt thou forgive 401Ball those who have ever sinned against thee? If he says, I will, then let him say to him: May God almighty have mercy on thee, and grant me what I ought. Fast every day in this Lenten season until None, and abstain from white food; and guard thyself against the eight capital vices, 402Athat thou commit them not; which are homicide, and theft, and perjuries, and avarice, and unlawful lying-together, and lust, and detraction, and false witness; and observe the twelve days of Lent, which occur in every year, and guard thyself against barbarous sacrifice, and sorcery, and lying with a virgin, and double speech, and pride, and covetousness of the possession of other men: guard thyself against all these, and love thy Lord with all thy mind, and with all thy strength, and all thy powers, and with thy whole inmost heart, steadfastly; and be kind toward the poor, and gentle, and distributing alms, and diligently visiting church, and diligently giving tithes to the Church of God, and to the poor: then God will be kind to thee, and beneficent, and merciful, and thou shalt reign with him for ever and ever 402Bwithout end, if thou doest so; and I pray thee to tell me all the evil that thou hast ever done, since it is more profitable for thee to be put to shame now, here, before me alone, a wretched man, than hereafter before God at the great judgment, where the dwellers in heaven, and the dwellers on earth, and those below will all be gathered together.

THE FIRST PART OF THE FIRST BOOK.

CAPUT PRIMUM. De presbyteri prudentia.

  • 401

Convenit cuilibet sacerdoti, cum poenitentiam jejunii aliquibus imponit, ut sciat quisnam ille sit, an sanus, an infirmus; dives, an pauper; quam juvenis, vel quam senex sit; utrum sit ordinatus, an laicus, et qualem poenitentiam faciat; an sit caelebs, an uxoratus: 401Comnes homines decet discretio, licet studio adulterium non committant. Divites severius judicandi sunt quam pauperes, juxta jus canonicum.

CHAPTER ONE. On the prudence of the presbyter.

  • 401

It befits every priest, when he imposes a penance of fasting on any persons, that he know who that person is, whether healthy, or sick; rich, or poor; how young, or how old he is; whether he is ordained, or a layman, and what penance he may do; whether he is unmarried, or married: 401Cdiscretion befits all men, although they do not commit adultery of set purpose. The rich are to be judged more severely than the poor, according to canon law.

CAPUT II. Jus de variis peccatis.

Theodorus sanctus et pius episcopus constituit hanc lectionem in exemplum et doctrinam omnibus illis qui delicta sua apud Deum emendare volunt, et ita dixit: Legimus in Poenitentiali quod poenitentia facienda sit pro capitalibus criminibus annum, vel duos, vel tres (in) pane et aqua; et pro minoribus criminibus hebdomadam, vel mensem, omnes eodem modo. Sed hoc apud nonnullos res dura, et difficilis est, ideo docebimus quibus rebus hoc redimere debeat qui jejunium suum servare nequit; hoc est ut psalmodia et eleemosynis per longum spatium poenitentiam 402Bagat, et si omnia implere possit quae in hoc libro scripta sunt, valde bonum et legitime ordinatum est; et si non possit, cantet quinquaginta psalmos juxta ordinem in ecclesia, vel in alio secreto loco; et qui unam septimanam jejunat cum pane et aqua, cantet trecentos psalmos genibus flexis, vel trecentos et viginti absque genibus flexis, prouti 402Csupra dictum est; et si quis per mensis spatium poenitentiam agere debet cum pane et aqua, cantet mille et ducentos psalmos flexis genibus: et absque genibus flexis mille sexcentos et octoginta; ac deinde quolibet die se recipiat ad meridiem praeter dies Mercurii, et Veneris; tunc jejunare debet ad Nonam, et abstineat a carne et vino, hoc est omnis generis potu quo quis inebriari possit, et utatur omnibus aliis cibis quos Deus ei dederit, postquam cantaverit; et si nec psalmos norit, nec jejunare possit, distribuat quolibet die obolum, vel valorem ejus pauperibus; et qualibet hebdomada jejunet unum diem ad Nonam, et alium ad vesperas, et utatur iis quae habet, prouti supra dictum est. Et si quis annum integrum poenitentiam agere debet, distribuat viginti sex solidos ad eleemosynas, et qualibet hebdomade jejunet duos dies, prouti supra dictum est; et consideret in tribus 403Aillis quadragesimis quid cibus ejus fuerit, dimidiumque ejus ad eleemosynas distribuat. Secundo anno poenitentia inchoanda est a Nativitate Domini ad Epiphaniam, et a Paschate ad Pentecosten; et homo qui non potest peccatorum suorum poenitentiam agere, uti supra dictum est, distribuat primo anno viginti sex solidos ad eleemosynas, et secundo viginti, et tertio octodecim, qui sunt sexaginta quatuor solidi. Et sciat dives quod Deus ipsi multas opes et possessiones hujus vitae concrediderit, quo modo crimina sua capitalia emendare debeat. Sumat Evangelium et ibi legat quomodo Zacchaeus cum Domino locutus sit, cum ad prandium eum in domum suam susceperit, tunc invocabat eum, ita dicens: Domine, omnium bonorum meorum trado dimidiam partem pauperibus; 403Bet si per injustam fraudem aliquibus aliquid abstuli, reddo quadrifariam. Et tunc a Domino promeruit audire: Hodie domui huic salus facta est; hoc est omnium peccatorum remissio. Et ad augenda haec bona opera, de quibus supra diximus, liberet, et redimat eos qui in captivitate sunt, et ab illo die, quo injustitiam suam derelinquit, non renovet illam deinde, ne forte similis sit cani qui devorat quod prius evomuit, neque negligendum est quod Apostolus dixit: Qui per corpus suum peccat, per corpus suum hoc compenset; hoc est, in jejunio, et in vigiliis, et in orationibus, et in precibus ad Deum, in compunctione cordis, et cum effusione lacrymarum.

Prima abstinentia est baptismus ignis, secunda est amor Dei, tertia est desiderium eleemosynarum, 403Cquarta est effusio lacrymarum in vera poenitentia, quinta est confessio flagitiorum, sexta est afflictio cordis et corporis in jejuniis, septima est ut mores corrigantur coram Deo, octava est preces sanctae pro flagitiis illis, nona est misericordia et bona fides, decima est ut alii a flagitiis ad Dei voluntatem ducantur, undecima est remissio Dei, ut per hoc Deus peccata ipsi condonet, duodecima est martyrium, uti fiebat ad Christi passionem.

CHAPTER II. The law concerning various sins.

Theodore, a holy and pious bishop, established this reading as an example and a teaching for all those who wish to amend their offences before God, and spoke thus: We read in the Penitential that penance is to be done for capital crimes for a year, or two, or three (on) bread and water; and for lesser crimes a week, or a month, all in the same manner. But this with some is a hard and difficult thing; therefore we will teach by what things he who cannot keep his fast ought to redeem it; that is, that he do penance with psalmody and alms over a long space of time 402B, and if he can fulfil all the things which are written in this book, it is very good and lawfully ordered; and if he cannot, let him sing fifty psalms in order in church, or in another secret place; and he who fasts one week on bread and water, let him sing three hundred psalms on bended knees, or three hundred and twenty without bended knees, as 402Cwas said above; and if anyone ought to do penance for the space of a month on bread and water, let him sing one thousand two hundred psalms on bended knees: and without bended knees one thousand six hundred and eighty; and thereafter every day let him take refreshment at midday, except Wednesdays and Fridays; then he must fast until None, and let him abstain from flesh and wine, that is, from every kind of drink by which one can be made drunk, and let him use all the other foods which God has given him, after he has sung; and if he neither knows the psalms nor can fast, let him distribute every day an obolus, or its value, to the poor; and every week let him fast one day until None, and another until Vespers, and let him use the things which he has, as was said above. And if anyone ought to do penance a whole year, let him distribute twenty-six solidi in alms, and every week let him fast two days, as was said above; and let him consider in those three 403ALents what his food has been, and let him distribute half of it in alms. In the second year the penance is to be begun from the Nativity of the Lord to the Epiphany, and from Easter to Pentecost; and the man who cannot do penance for his sins, as was said above, let him distribute in the first year twenty-six solidi in alms, and in the second twenty, and in the third eighteen, which are sixty-four solidi. And let the rich man know that God has entrusted to him many riches and possessions of this life, by which means he ought to amend his capital crimes. Let him take up the Gospel and read there how Zacchaeus spoke with the Lord, when he had received him into his house to dinner; then he called upon him, saying thus: Lord, of all my goods I hand over the half part to the poor; 403Band if by unjust fraud I have taken anything from anyone, I restore it fourfold. And then he merited to hear from the Lord: Today to this house salvation is come; that is, the remission of all sins. And to increase these good works, of which we spoke above, let him free and ransom those who are in captivity, and from that day on which he forsakes his injustice, let him not renew it thereafter, lest perhaps he be like the dog which devours what it vomited before; nor is that to be neglected which the Apostle said: He who sins through his body, let him make amends for this through his body; that is, in fasting, and in vigils, and in prayers, and in supplications to God, in compunction of heart, and with the shedding of tears.

The first abstinence is the baptism of fire, the second is the love of God, the third is the desire of alms, 403Cthe fourth is the shedding of tears in true penance, the fifth is the confession of misdeeds, the sixth is the affliction of heart and body in fastings, the seventh is that one's ways be corrected before God, the eighth is holy prayers for those misdeeds, the ninth is mercy and good faith, the tenth is that others be led from misdeeds to the will of God, the eleventh is the remission of God, that through this God may forgive him his sins, the twelfth is martyrdom, as was done at the passion of Christ.

CAPUT III. De episcopi adulterio.

Episcopus, si adulteretur, juxta jus canonicum jejunet duodecim hiemes, et eleemosynas reddat multas, et cum effusione lacrymarum Dei honorem 403Dexoret. Aliqui volunt ut se deinde ordinibus privet.

CHAPTER III. On the adultery of a bishop.

A bishop, if he commits adultery, according to canon law let him fast twelve winters, and render many alms, and with the shedding of tears 403Dlet him entreat the honour of God. Some wish that he should thereafter deprive himself of his orders.

CAPUT IV. De presbyteri adulterio.

Presbyter cum fornicatus fuerit, jejunet tres hiemes, et tria jejunia per hebdomadam jejunet duobus diebus ad Vesperam. Si cum moniali fornicatus fuerit, multum auctum sit jejunium ejus, hoc est septem annos.

CHAPTER IV. On the adultery of a presbyter.

A presbyter, when he has fornicated, let him fast three winters, and three fasts through the week let him fast on two days until Vespers. If he has fornicated with a nun, let his fast be much increased, that is, seven years.

CAPUT V. De sacerdote qui seipsum impuris sermonibus vel aliquibus aliis vitiis polluit.

Sacerdos qui impure loquitur, vel ex visu aut aspectu mulieris se polluit, et se non purgat, jejunet viginti dies; qui propria voluntate admodum pollutus fuerit, jejunet centum dies. Si invitus in somno fuerit pollutus, surgat et cantet psalterii psalmum, et 404Acrastino die maxillam suam triginta vicibus ad terram inclinet. Si invitus fuerit pollutus, vel si volens in somno moechatus sit, cantet viginti quatuor psalmos. Si quis in ecclesia dormiens semen suum effuderit, surgat et cantet psalterium. Si sacerdos cum cupidine mulierem osculetur, poeniteat ejus viginti dies. Si sacerdos polluatur, cum mulierem tetigerit, jejunet quadraginta dies, nonnulli volunt triginta. Si sacerdos cogitationis voluntate pollutus sit, jejunet hebdomadam; si tactu manus, jejunet tres hebdomadas. Si quis crebro fornicationem commiserit, canon judicat ut decem hiemes jejunet; nonnulli volunt annum, et cum discretione tres.

CHAPTER V. On a priest who pollutes himself with impure speech or with any other vices.

A priest who speaks impurely, or pollutes himself from the sight or look of a woman, and does not cleanse himself, let him fast twenty days; he who has been greatly polluted by his own will, let him fast a hundred days. If unwilling he has been polluted in sleep, let him rise and sing a psalm of the psalter, and 404Aon the next day let him bow his jaw to the ground thirty times. If unwilling he has been polluted, or if willing he has committed adultery in sleep, let him sing twenty-four psalms. If anyone sleeping in church has shed his seed, let him rise and sing the psalter. If a priest kisses a woman with desire, let him repent of it twenty days. If a priest is polluted when he has touched a woman, let him fast forty days; some wish thirty. If a priest is polluted by the will of his thought, let him fast a week; if by the touch of his hand, let him fast three weeks. If anyone has frequently committed fornication, the canon judges that he fast ten winters; some wish a year, and with discretion three.

CAPUT VI. De presbytero qui baptizare detrectat ob itineris sui necessitatem.

  • 404B

Quicunque sacerdos in propria sua provincia, vel in aliqua alia proficiscatur, et si in itinere ejus baptisma ab ipso exoretur, et ille neget, detineatur in itinere suo, et si infans ethnicus moriatur, ordine suo spolietur.

CHAPTER VI. On a presbyter who refuses to baptize because of the necessity of his journey.

  • 404B

Whatever priest travels in his own province, or in any other, and if on his journey baptism is begged of him, and he refuses, is held back on his journey, and if the heathen infant dies, let him be stripped of his order.

CAPUT VII. De non baptizato presbytero et de eo quem ipse baptizat.

Quicunque presbyter si norit quod non sit baptizatus, baptizetur, et omnes illi quos antea baptizaverat. Attamen papa Romanus constituit, si missae administrator vitiosus sit, vel paganus, quod servitium Spiritus sancti esset in dono baptismi, non tamen in hominis alicujus.

CHAPTER VII. On a presbyter not baptized, and on him whom he himself baptizes.

Whatever presbyter, if he knows that he is not baptized, let him be baptized, and all those whom he had baptized before. Yet the Roman pope decreed, if the administrator of the Mass is vicious, or a pagan, that the service of the Holy Spirit was in the gift of baptism, not however in that of any man.

CAPUT VIII. De episcopo et presbytero qui ordinem suum perdit.

  • 404C

Si quis ordinem perdat, presbyter, vel episcopus, poenitentiam agat usque ad mortem, ut ejus anima vivat. Sacerdos si hominem occidat vel fornicetur, ordine suo spolietur; si aliquem vulneret, jejunet centum dies.

CHAPTER VIII. On a bishop and a presbyter who loses his order.

  • 404C

If anyone loses his order, a presbyter, or a bishop, let him do penance until death, that his soul may live. A priest, if he kills a man or fornicates, let him be stripped of his order; if he wounds anyone, let him fast a hundred days.

CAPUT IX. De iis quibus indutus esse debet sacerdos cum missam celebrat.

Cum sacerdos missam celebrat non portet caracallam cassiatam, sed cum Evangelium legit, imponat eam humero.

CHAPTER IX. On the things in which a priest ought to be clothed when he celebrates Mass.

When a priest celebrates Mass let him not wear a caracalla cassiata, but when he reads the Gospel, let him put it on his shoulder.

CAPUT X. De sacerdote, cum calicem effundit, et de cibo ante Eucharistiam.

Cum sacerdos calicem effuderit, postquam missam 404Dcelebraverit, jejunet triginta dies; si comedat antequam ad Eucharistiam abeat, et postquam Eucharistiam acceperit, jejunet septem dies.

CHAPTER X. On a priest, when he spills the chalice, and on food before the Eucharist.

When a priest has spilled the chalice, after he has celebrated Mass 404D, let him fast thirty days; if he eats before he goes to the Eucharist, and after he has received the Eucharist, let him fast seven days.

CAPUT XI. De presbytero, si aliquid magni pretii auferat.

Si presbyter furtum magni pretii commiserit, jejunet quinque hiemes, vel prouti episcopus ipsi adnotaverit.

CHAPTER XI. On a presbyter, if he carries off anything of great price.

If a presbyter has committed a theft of great price, let him fast five winters, or as the bishop has appointed for him.

CAPUT XII. De diacono et monacho, si fornicentur.

Si diaconus fornicatus fuerit, jejunet tres hiemes, et monachus totidem; si infantem genuerit, jejunet magis, prouti episcopus vel jura adjudicaverint ipsi: homo non ordinatus, et qui non est monachus, si fornicatus fuerit, jejunet duodecim menses; si infantem 405Agenuerit, jejunet tres hiemes; si eum occiderit, jejunet septem hiemes.

CHAPTER XII. On a deacon and a monk, if they fornicate.

If a deacon has fornicated, let him fast three winters, and a monk as many; if he has begotten a child, let him fast more, as the bishop or the laws have adjudged to him: a man not ordained, and who is not a monk, if he has fornicated, let him fast twelve months; if he has 405Abegotten a child, let him fast three winters; if he has killed it, let him fast seven winters.

CAPUT XIII. De monachis et monialibus, si fornicentur.

Monachus vel femina consecrata si fornicati fuerint, jejunent septem hiemes, nonnulli volunt tres. Juveni non est danda uxor, si prius ordinem monachalem in animo habuerit.

CHAPTER XIII. On monks and nuns, if they fornicate.

A monk or a consecrated woman, if they have fornicated, let them fast seven winters; some wish three. A wife is not to be given to a young man, if he has previously had the monastic order in mind.

CAPUT XIV. De laicis, si fornicentur.

Laicus uxorem habens, si alterius viri feminam foedet, vel virginem, jejunet unam hiemem; si infantem habeat, jejunet tres hiemes; si autem caelebs sit, jejunet septem hiemes, aliqui volunt decem. Si quis cum matre sua, vel sorore, vel filia coeat, jejunet 405Bduodecim hiemes, et videat tamen sacerdos, in quo ordine ipse sit. Quilibet homo cum bestia rem habens, jejunet decem hiemes, vel septem; aliqui volunt tres hiemes, aliqui unam, aliqui centum dies; videat quid sit homo, quid sit bestia. In omnibus rebus sacerdoti convenit prudentia, uti antea diximus. Quicunque in os semen effuderit, jejunet septem hiemes. Quilibet homo qui cum verna sua fornicatus fuerit, jejunet unam hiemem; si ipsa infantem habuerit, redimat eum, et jejunet tamen unam hiemem.

CHAPTER XIV. On lay people, if they fornicate.

A layman having a wife, if he defiles the woman of another man, or a virgin, let him fast one winter; if he has a child, let him fast three winters; but if he is unmarried, let him fast seven winters; some wish ten. If anyone lies with his mother, or sister, or daughter, let him fast 405Btwelve winters, and yet let the priest see in what order he himself is. Any man having dealings with a beast, let him fast ten winters, or seven; some wish three winters, some one, some a hundred days; let him see what the man is, what the beast is. In all things prudence befits the priest, as we said before. Whoever has shed seed into the mouth, let him fast seven winters. Any man who has fornicated with his bondmaid, let him fast one winter; if she has had a child, let him redeem it, and yet let him fast one winter.

CAPUT XV. De injustis operibus juniorum.

Si parvus puer a majori opprimatur in coitu, jejunet quinque noctes; si eum patiatur, jejunet viginti noctes; si furetur, vel animal per se mortuum 405Cedat, et ipse hoc norit, jejunet septem dies. Si viginti annos habeat et tale quid fecerit, jejunet viginti dies. Si idem manu sua se polluat, jejunet viginti dies. Si hoc in virili aetate accidat, jejunet quadraginta dies; nonnulli volunt centum.

CHAPTER XV. On the unlawful deeds of the young.

If a little boy is overpowered by an older one in coition, let him fast five nights; if he submits to him, let him fast twenty nights; if he steals, or eats an animal dead of itself 405C, and he himself knows this, let him fast seven days. If he is twenty years old and has done such a thing, let him fast twenty days. If the same pollutes himself with his hand, let him fast twenty days. If this happens in manly age, let him fast forty days; some wish a hundred.

CAPUT XVI. De menstruae consuetudinis fornicatione, et de alia injusta fornicatione.

Quilibet vir, qui cum uxore sua in consuetudine ejus menstrua rem habuerit, jejunet quadraginta dies: et quilibet qui sanguinem suum, vel viri semen biberit, jejunet quinque hiemes. Quilibet homo in ecclesia fornicans judicetur quod episcopo justum videtur, et juxta hoc puniatur. Si quis cum bestia vel cum masculo coiverit, jejunet decem hiemes. Si 405Dpueri inter se fornicationem committant, suspendantur. Bestia si a masculo sit polluta, occidatur, et detur canibus; si dubitetur, vivere permittatur.

CHAPTER XVI. On fornication in the menstrual custom, and on other unlawful fornication.

Any man who has had dealings with his wife in her menstrual custom, let him fast forty days: and anyone who has drunk his own blood, or a man's seed, let him fast five winters. Any man fornicating in a church, let him be judged as seems just to the bishop, and let him be punished accordingly. If anyone has lain with a beast or with a male, let him fast ten winters. If 405Dboys commit fornication among themselves, let them be suspended. A beast, if it has been polluted by a male, let it be killed, and given to the dogs; if it is doubtful, let it be permitted to live.

CAPUT XVII. De ethnicorum fornicatione, et de eo qui nesciens esse bis baptizatus, et de eo qui ab illegitime fornicante presbytero baptizatus est, et de eo qui bis sua sponte baptizatus est.

De ethnicorum fornicatione, et de eo qui invitus bis fuerit baptizatus, et de eo qui baptizatus fuerit ab injuste fornicante presbytero, et de eo qui sua sponte bis baptizatus fuerit. Quilibet ethnicus deserat uxorem suam ethnicam in potestate sua, post baptisma ejus, sive ipse (baptisma) habeat sive non; ita etiam si uter eorum sit ethnicus, vel baptizatus. Ethnicus 406Aa baptizato discedere debet, uti apostolus dicebat: Infidelis si discedit, discedat. Qui bis baptizatus fuerit, ita ut nesciret, non imponatur ei aliquod jejunium nisi juxta canonicum jus sit consecrandus, licet necessitatem suam celet. Si vir a presbytero adultero baptizatus sit, statim rebaptizetur. Quilibet qui sciens aliquem secunda vice baptizat, hoc est quasi Christum suspenderet, jejunet septem hiemes; duas dies jejunii per hebdomadam, et tres plenario jejunet.

CHAPTER XVII. On the fornication of heathens, and on him who unknowing was baptized twice, and on him who was baptized by a presbyter unlawfully fornicating, and on him who was baptized twice of his own will.

On the fornication of heathens, and on him who has been baptized twice against his will, and on him who has been baptized by a presbyter unjustly fornicating, and on him who of his own will has been baptized twice. Let any heathen desert his heathen wife in his own power, after his baptism, whether he himself have (baptism) or not; so also if either of them is a heathen, or baptized. A heathen 406Aought to depart from a baptized person, as the apostle said: If the unbeliever departs, let him depart. He who has been baptized twice, so that he did not know it, let no fast be imposed on him, unless according to canon law he is to be consecrated, although he conceal his necessity. If a man has been baptized by an adulterous presbyter, let him be rebaptized at once. Anyone who knowingly baptizes someone a second time, this is as if he were hanging Christ, let him fast seven winters; two days of fasting through the week, and three let him fast in full.

CAPUT XVIII. De eo quod vir mulierem in baptismate suscipere debet et mulier virum.

Masculus feminam in baptismo suscipere debet, et femina masculum. Baptizatus [ Lege baptizans] non comedat in domo cum baptizatis, nec eos osculetur, 406Beo magis quia hoc ei non licet apud ethnicos.

CHAPTER XVIII. On this, that a man ought to receive a woman in baptism, and a woman a man.

A male ought to receive a female in baptism, and a female a male. The baptized [ Lege baptizans] let him not eat in the house with the baptized, nor kiss them, 406Bthe more because this is not lawful for him among heathens.

CAPUT XIX. De viri et mulieris promissis.

Si aliquis vir vel femina caelibatum servare voverit, et deinde vir in legitimo matrimonio copulatus sit, non negligatur hoc quod jejunet tres hiemes, quoniam stultum et impossibile votum violatum est. Si uxor viri cujusdam adulteretur, maritus eam potest deserere, et aliam ducere, si ea prima fuerit uxor; si autem secunda vel tertia fuerit, non potest aliam ducere; si uxor flagitia sua committere velit intra quinque annos, alii viro nubere debet. Si mortuus maritus sit, uxor intra annum alium sumere potest. Quicunque maritus uxorem suam deseruerit, et se injusto matrimonio (alii) adjungat, jejunet septem 406Chiemes severum jejunium, vel quindecim leviora. Quicunque multa mala perpetraverit in homicidium, et occisionem hominis, et injustum concubitum cum bestiis, et cum mulieribus, eat ad monasterium, et semper jejunet usque ad finem vitae, si valde multa commiserit.

CHAPTER XIX. On the vows of a man and a woman.

If any man or woman has vowed to keep celibacy, and then the man has been coupled in lawful matrimony, let this not be neglected, that he fast three winters, since a foolish and impossible vow has been violated. If the wife of any man commits adultery, the husband can desert her, and take another, if she was his first wife; but if she was the second or the third, he cannot take another; if the wife wishes to commit her misdeeds within five years, she ought to marry another man. If the husband is dead, the wife can take another within a year. Whatever husband has deserted his wife, and joins himself in an unlawful marriage (to another), let him fast seven 406Cwinters a severe fast, or fifteen lighter ones. Whoever has perpetrated many evils in homicide, and the killing of a man, and unlawful lying with beasts, and with women, let him go to a monastery, and fast always until the end of his life, if he has committed very many.

CAPUT XX. De eo quod virum non deceat uxorem suam nudam videre; et de eo quod cum ipsa rem habere nequeat.

Non decet virum uxorem suam nudam videre. Cum vir et mulier matrimonio juncti sunt, et vir cum ea coire nequeat, mulier eum deserere potest, et alii nubere, si hoc in viro manifestum sit. Parentes debent mulieres matrimonio jungendas viris tradere; excipe si ipsa omnino contradixerit, quod eum nolit, tunc illa, si ipse velit, hoc omittere potest, et ad 406Dmonasterium aditum eligere, si ipsa velit. Si femina cum viro habitare nolit, cui antea nupta erat, reddatur ei pecunia quam pro illa tradidit, et tertia etiam pars haereditatis ejus; et si ipse hoc nolit, perdat pecuniam, et femina abeat ad monasterium, vel alibi in castitate se contineat.

CHAPTER XX. On this, that it does not befit a man to see his wife naked; and on this, that he cannot have dealings with her.

It does not befit a man to see his wife naked. When a man and a woman have been joined in matrimony, and the man cannot lie with her, the woman can desert him, and marry another, if this is manifest in the man. Parents ought to hand over women who are to be joined in matrimony to husbands; except if she herself has altogether refused, that she does not want him, then she, if he himself wishes, can let this go, and choose entrance to a 406Dmonastery, if she herself wishes. If a woman does not wish to live with the man to whom she was previously married, let the money be returned to him which he paid for her, and also the third part of her inheritance; and if he himself does not wish this, let him lose the money, and let the woman go away to a monastery, or elsewhere keep herself in chastity.

CAPUT XXI. De injusta fornicatione.

Si maritus cum uxore sua canino more coeat, jejunet quadraginta dies; si cum extis ejus coeat, jejunet decem hiemes. Quilibet homo Solis die vel nocte coiens, jejunet tres dies. Maritus non coeat cum uxore sua quadraginta dies ante Pascha, nec septem dies ante Pentecosten, nec quatuordecim dies ante Natale Domini.

CHAPTER XXI. On unlawful fornication.

If a husband lies with his wife in the manner of dogs, let him fast forty days; if he lies with her entrails, let him fast ten winters. Any man coupling on Sunday, by day or by night, let him fast three days. Let a husband not lie with his wife forty days before Easter, nor seven days before Pentecost, nor fourteen days before the Nativity of the Lord.

CAPUT XXII. De diversis homicidiis.

  • 407A

Si quis alterum occidat in homicidio in ira et in secreto, jejunet tres annos, nonnulli volunt septem. Quicunque homicidio consentit, et illud deinde committat, jejunet quinque hiemes; nonnulli volunt septem, et quadraginta dies ecclesiam non ingrediatur. Quicunque servum suum occiderit, jejunet unum annum. Quicunque in genitalibus alteri imbecillitatem intulerit, vel ei vulnus inflixerit, compenset ei vulnus illud, et opus ejus operetur, usque dum vulnus sanetur, et praemium medico tradat, et jejunet duo vel tria jejunia; si nesciat quomodo solvat, jejunet duodecim menses. Quicunque aliquem occiderit, jejunet tres hiemes; si autem cognatis 407Bejus ipsum compensare voluerit, jejunet annum et dimidium. Si quis alterum vulneret, pro sanguinis effusione jejunet quadraginta dies.

CHAPTER XXII. On diverse homicides.

  • 407A

If anyone kills another in homicide, in anger and in secret, let him fast three years; some wish seven. Whoever consents to a homicide, and afterwards commits it, let him fast five winters; some wish seven, and for forty days let him not enter the church. Whoever has killed his own slave, let him fast one year. Whoever has brought weakness upon another in the genitals, or has inflicted a wound on him, let him compensate him for that wound, and do his work until the wound is healed, and hand over the fee to the physician, and let him fast two or three fasts; if he does not know how to pay, let him fast twelve months. Whoever has killed someone, let him fast three winters; but if he has been willing to compensate 407Bhis kinsmen for him, let him fast a year and a half. If anyone wounds another, for the shedding of blood let him fast forty days.

CAPUT XXIII. De episcopi, et presbyteri, et monachi occisione.

Si quis episcopum vel presbyterum occiderit, hoc ad regis vel episcopi jus pertinet: quicunque presbyterum vel monachum occiderit, hoc sit juris episcopalis an arma deponat, et ad monasterium abeat, vel septem hiemes jejunet.

CHAPTER XXIII. On the killing of a bishop, and of a presbyter, and of a monk.

If anyone has killed a bishop or a presbyter, this pertains to the law of the king or of the bishop: whoever has killed a presbyter or a monk, let it be of episcopal law whether he lay down his arms, and go away to a monastery, or fast seven winters.

CAPUT XXIV. De variis homicidiis.

Si quis alterum in pugna publica occiderit, vel ex necessitate, cum domini sui mercem eriperet, jejunet quadraginta dies. Si quis alterum occiderit, nequaquam 407Cin homicidio, duodecim menses jejunet. Si quis patrem suum, vel matrem, sororem, vel fratrem, filium, vel filiam occiderit, hoc est juris episcopalis, nonnulli volunt ut poenitentiam agat in terra peregrina decem hiemes. Homo qui odit fratrem suum homicida est, uti Joannes evangelista dicit.

CHAPTER XXIV. On various homicides.

If anyone has killed another in public battle, or out of necessity, when he was rescuing his lord's goods, let him fast forty days. If anyone has killed another, by no means 407Cin homicide, let him fast twelve months. If anyone has killed his father, or mother, sister, or brother, son, or daughter, this is of episcopal law; some wish that he do penance in a foreign land ten winters. The man who hates his brother is a murderer, as John the evangelist says.

CAPUT XXV. De fratribus, quomodo cognatam uxorem sumere possunt, et de variis rebus aliis.

Duo fratres duas sorores in uxores ducere possunt, et pater ac filius matrem ac filiam. Si servus et serva mutua voluntate se conjungant, et si deinceps aliquis eorum manumittatur, et nequeat servam redimere, quocunque modo liber sit, servae libertatem 407Dlucrari debet. Quicunque homo liber servam in uxorem duxerit, non debet illam deinde relinquere, si mutuo consensu antea serva fuerat. Quicunque parturientem uxorem liberam fecerit, infans tamen est servus. Qui in legitimo matrimonio est, tres dies ante jejunium quadragesimale, non congregetur, ita etiam quadraginta dies totos usque ad nonum diem in Paschate. Quicunque maritum vel uxorem habuerit, licet illegitime, attamen quomodocunque potest cibos illi coemere debet, quoniam propheta ait: Domini est terra. Maritus et femina si in matrimonio conjuncti sunt, si alter velit Dei servus esse, et alter nolit, vel alteruter eorum sit infidelis, vel sit sanus, attamen cum consensu mutuo separentur si velint.

CHAPTER XXV. On brothers, how they can take a kinswoman as wife, and on various other matters.

Two brothers can take two sisters as wives, and a father and a son a mother and a daughter. If a slave and a bondwoman join themselves by mutual will, and if afterwards one of them is manumitted, and cannot redeem the bondwoman, in whatever way he is free, he ought to gain the bondwoman's freedom 407D. Whatever free man has taken a bondwoman as wife, he ought not afterwards to leave her, if by mutual consent she had previously been a bondwoman. Whoever has made his wife free while she is in labour, the infant is nevertheless a slave. He who is in lawful matrimony, for three days before the Lenten fast, let him not come together, so also for the whole forty days until the ninth day in Easter. Whoever has had a husband or a wife, although unlawfully, yet in whatever way he can he ought to buy food for that one, since the prophet says: The earth is the Lord's. A husband and a woman, if they are joined in matrimony, if the one wishes to be a servant of God, and the other does not wish it, or either of them is an unbeliever, or is healthy, yet with mutual consent let them be separated if they wish.

CAPUT XXVI. De viri lotione post coitum.

  • 408A

Si maritus cum propria sua uxore coeat, lavet se antequam ad ecclesiam abeat; si mulier maritum suum a se rejiciat, et dein nolit resipiscere, et cum eo in quinque annis pacem inire, maritus cum consensu episcopi, aliam uxorem ducere potest. Si maritus uxoris in captivitatem ducatur, exspectet eum sex annos, et ita vir uxori faciat, si ei captivitas accidat; si maritus aliam uxorem ducat, et captiva post quinque annos redeat, deserat posteriorem, et captivam sumat, quam antea eodem modo duxerat. Cum vir in adulterio conjunctus sit uxori suae familiae, post uxoris suae mortem, legitimo jure uxori illi conjungatur.

CHAPTER XXVI. On the washing of a man after coition.

  • 408A

If a husband lies with his own wife, let him wash himself before he goes to church; if a woman rejects her husband from herself, and then will not come to her senses, and make peace with him within five years, the husband, with the consent of the bishop, can take another wife. If the husband of a wife is led into captivity, let her wait for him six years, and let the man do so for the wife, if captivity befalls her; if the husband takes another wife, and the captive returns after five years, let him desert the later one, and take the captive, whom he had formerly married in the same manner. When a man has been joined in adultery to a wife of his household, after the death of his own wife let him be joined to that wife by lawful right.

CAPUT XXVII. De adolescentibus et puellis, in qua aetate seipsos gubernare possunt.

  • 408B

Puella quatuordecim annorum corporis sui potestatem habere potest. Adolescens cum quindecim est annorum, in propria sua potestate sit; deinde se monachum potest facere, si velit, et non ante. Femina usque dum sit tredecim vel quatuordecim annorum, sit in parentum suorum potestate; post hanc aetatem dominus ejus illam capere potest, cum sua voluntate. Pater filium suum, si valde pauper sit, ad servitium tradere potest, usque dum septem anni praeterierint; absque filii voluntate tamen non tradat ipsum. Si inimicus vir aliquem uxore privet et ipse eam obtinere nequeat, aliam sibi sumat, quoniam 408Choc melius est quam adulterii flagitium committere.

CHAPTER XXVII. On youths and girls, at what age they can govern themselves.

  • 408B

A girl of fourteen years can have power over her own body. A youth, when he is fifteen years old, let him be in his own power; then he can make himself a monk, if he wishes, and not before. A woman, until she is thirteen or fourteen years old, let her be in the power of her parents; after this age her lord can take her, with his own will. A father can hand over his son into servitude, if he is very poor, until seven years have passed; yet let him not hand him over without the son's will. If a hostile man deprives someone of his wife, and he himself cannot obtain her, let him take another for himself, since 408Cthis is better than to commit the crime of adultery.

CAPUT XXVIII. De eo quod cognati coeant, et quandiu mulier gravida a viro abstinere debet.

In quinto gradu permittantur homines matrimonium inire, et in quarto, si hoc occurrerit, non separentur, in tertio gradu separentur. Abstineat mulier a marito suo septem menses antequam eam cognoscat, et post partum ejus quadraginta dies. Quicunque maritus cum uxore sua in illo tempore coeat, jejunet viginti vel quadraginta dies. Mulier in consuetudine sua menstrua ad ecclesiam non confugiat, nec ad eucharistiam accedat, nec monialis nec mulier laica; sicubi hoc fecerint jejunet viginti dies.

CHAPTER XXVIII. On this, that kinsfolk lie together, and how long a pregnant woman ought to abstain from a man.

In the fifth degree let men be permitted to enter matrimony, and in the fourth, if this has occurred, let them not be separated, in the third degree let them be separated. Let a woman abstain from her husband seven months before he knows her, and after her giving birth forty days. Whatever husband lies with his wife in that time, let him fast twenty or forty days. A woman in her menstrual custom, let her not take refuge in the church, nor approach the Eucharist, neither a nun nor a lay woman; if anywhere they have done this let her fast twenty days.

CAPUT XXIX. De uxore quae artem magicam exercet.

  • 408D

Si mulier artem magicam, et incantationem, et veneficium, et simile quid committat, jejunet duodecim menses, vel tria jejunia, vel quadraginta dies: sciat, quantum flagitium sit. Si illa cum venefico coeat jejunet septem hiemes. Mulier quae ecclesiam intrat antequam a sanguine suo se purgat, jejunet quadraginta dies. Mulier si semen viri cum cibo suo miscet, et hoc facit ut masculis eo charior sit, jejunet tres annos.

CHAPTER XXIX. On a wife who practises the magic art.

  • 408D

If a woman commits the magic art, and incantation, and sorcery, and anything similar, let her fast twelve months, or three fasts, or forty days: let her know how great a crime it is. If she lies with a sorcerer let her fast seven winters. A woman who enters a church before she cleanses herself from her blood, let her fast forty days. A woman, if she mixes a man's seed with her food, and does this so that she may be dearer to males by it, let her fast three years.

CAPUT XXX. De muliere si infantem suum recens natum arte magica occidit.

Si mulier abortum promoveat conceptu sui in utero 409Aet in quadraginta diebus occidatur id quod semine conceptum est, antequam animatum sit, quasi pro homicidio jejunet tres annos, et qualibet hebdomada duos dies ad vesperam, et tres jejunium legale; si ipsa abortiat, annum, vel tria jejunia legalia.

CHAPTER XXX. On a woman, if she kills her newly born infant by the magic art.

If a woman brings about an abortion of her conception in the womb 409Aand within forty days that which was conceived of seed is killed, before it is ensouled, let her fast three years as though for homicide, and every week two days until evening, and three the lawful fast; if she herself miscarries, a year, or three lawful fasts.

CAPUT XXXI. De muliere quae infantem suum occidit, et de eucharistia ac esu sanguinis.

Si mater infantem suum occidat, jejunet quindecim hiemes absque cessatione, exceptis diebus Solis. Si pauper sit, jejunet septem annos; si ea cum aliis fornicetur, jejunet decem annos. Si mulier aliqua arte coitum suum adjuvat, uti ipsa novit, jejunet duos annos, quoniam ipsius est pollutio. Unum jejunium competit viduis et feminis. Majus competit uxori maritum 409Bhabenti, si adulterium committat. Uxor non deserat maritum suum, licet adulteretur. Uxor ad eucharistiam accedat antequam pariat, cum maxime ei necessarium sit. Mulier quae mariti sui sanguinem pro aliquo medicamento tradit, jejunet quadraginta dies.

CHAPTER XXXI. On a woman who kills her infant, and on the Eucharist and the eating of blood.

If a mother kills her infant, let her fast fifteen winters without cessation, except on Sundays. If she is poor, let her fast seven years; if she fornicates with others, let her fast ten years. If a woman aids her coition by some art, as she herself knows how, let her fast two years, since the pollution is hers. One fast befits widows and women. A greater befits a wife having a husband 409B, if she commits adultery. Let a wife not desert her husband, although he commit adultery. Let a wife approach the Eucharist before she gives birth, when it is most necessary for her. A woman who gives her husband's blood for some medicine, let her fast forty days.

CAPUT XXXII. De immolatione inimicorum, et de fascinatione ubi frumentum ardet.

Si quis praemium aliquod magnum saltem diabolis immolet, jejunet per annum; si aliquid magnum sacrificet, jejunet decem hiemes: quicunque inimicis assignatum cibum sacrificat, et deinde sacerdoti confiteatur, videat sacerdos cujus ordinis homo sit, vel cujus aetatis, quam peritus sit; et deinde ita judicet, prouti ipsi justissimum videbitur. Quicunque frumentum 409Curit In domo vertendum est, WILKINS. in loco ubi mortuus est, in salutem vivorum, et masculi, jejunet quinque hiemes.

CHAPTER XXXII. On the sacrificing of enemies, and on the enchantment where grain is burned.

If anyone sacrifices some great reward at least to devils, let him fast for a year; if he sacrifices anything great, let him fast ten winters: whoever sacrifices food assigned to enemies, and then confesses to the priest, let the priest see of what order the man is, or of what age, how experienced he is; and then let him judge thus, as shall seem most just to him. Whoever burns grain 409CIt is to be rendered in domo, "in the house." Wilkins. in the place where one has died, for the health of the living, and of the male, let him fast five winters.

CAPUT XXXIII. De fascinatione mulieris, et de eo si illa fornicetur, et de voto ejus post mariti sui obitum.

Si mulier filiam suam super domum vel fornacem collocet, ideo ut febrim ejus curare velit, jejunet septem annos. Si ea in illegitimo adulterio sit, et maritus illius cum ipsa habitare nolit, abeat ea ad monasterium, si velit; si nolit, sumat quartam partem haereditatis. Si femina aliqua injustum adulterium committat, poena ejus sit in potestate mariti sui. Uxor quae pro uno marito est, si Deo promittat quod post mariti sui mortem alium nolit, et ipsa 409Dalium sumat, tunc poeniteat ipsam, quod promissum suum non impleverit; emendare deinde velit circa undecimum annum, postquam in mariti sui potestatem pervenerit; hoc est utrum ipsa id impleat, vel utrum cum necessitate cum viro quocum est vivere debeat. Si quis absque consensu episcopi promittat, promissum sit in episcopi potestate, hoc est utrum illum mutet, vel non.

CHAPTER XXXIII. On the enchantment of a woman, and on this, if she fornicates, and on her vow after the death of her husband.

If a woman places her daughter upon a house or an oven, for this reason, that she wishes to cure her fever, let her fast seven years. If she is in unlawful adultery, and her husband will not live with her, let her go away to a monastery, if she wishes; if she does not wish, let her take the fourth part of the inheritance. If any woman commits unjust adultery, let her punishment be in the power of her husband. A wife who is for one husband, if she promises God that after her husband's death she wants no other, and she herself 409Dtakes another, then let it repent her that she has not fulfilled her promise; let her wish then to amend about the eleventh year, after she has come into her husband's power; that is, whether she fulfil it, or whether of necessity she ought to live with the man with whom she is. If anyone promises without the consent of the bishop, let the promise be in the bishop's power, that is, whether he change it, or not.

CAPUT XXXIV. De perjurio.

Si quis perjurium faciat in ecclesia vel in libro Christi, vel super sanctas reliquias, jejunet quatuor hiemes. Si quis perjurium faciat in manu episcopi, 410Avel sacerdotis, vel diaconi, vel ad sanctificatam Christi crucem, jejunet annum unum. Si quis ad juramentum ducatur, et nesciat aliud jus praeter hoc, et ille tamen cum aliis juret, et ita decipiatur, et deinde sciat quod falsum esset, jejunet tria legalia jejunia. Quicumque sua sponte pejeret, et norit deinde quod perjurus sit, jejunet tres vel duas hiemes. Si quis in laici manibus juret, nihil obstat an cum astutia, jejunet juxta jus canonicum, antequam ad eucharistiam abeat, antequam jejunium fixerit. Nonnulli volunt ut statim circa duodecim menses, vel circa septem menses adeat; nonnulli volunt ut cum jejunium suum fixerit.

CHAPTER XXXIV. On perjury.

If anyone commits perjury in a church or on the book of Christ, or over holy relics, let him fast four winters. If anyone commits perjury in the hand of a bishop, 410Aor of a priest, or of a deacon, or at the sanctified cross of Christ, let him fast one year. If anyone is led to an oath, and knows no other law besides this, and yet he swears with others, and so is deceived, and afterwards knows that it was false, let him fast three lawful fasts. Whoever of his own will forswears himself, and knows afterwards that he is perjured, let him fast three or two winters. If anyone swears in the hands of a layman, nothing hinders whether with cunning, let him fast according to canon law, before he goes to the Eucharist, before he has fixed his fast. Some wish that he approach at once about twelve months, or about seven months; some wish that when he has fixed his fast.

CAPUT XXXV. De injustis operibus diei solis.

  • 410B

Graeci eum qui die Solis laborat prius verbis minantur; si deinde faciat, tunc aliquid operis ejus illi saltem adimitur; tertia vice suspenditur, vel jubent jejunare septem dies. Graeci volunt ut quolibet die Solis ad eucharistiam abeant tam laici quam sacerdotes; et qui tribus diebus dominicis non abit ad eucharistiam, excommunicatus est juxta jus canonicum. Ita Romanus abit ad eucharistiam, nisi quod excommunicet eum qui nequeunt. Diaconi non frangant panem consecratum cum Graecis, nec legant collectas, nec dicant: Dominus vobiscum. In uno eodemque altari quotidie duas missas celebrare debes. Qui non adest priori missae non debet ad osculum accedere ante eucharistiam. Qui ad eucharistiam accedere 410Cnon vult, non accedat deinde ad presbyterum pro pane, vel pro eucharistia, vel pro osculo. Non debet quis ad presbyterum eucharistiae gratia accedere, qui consecrationem vel lectionem non potest juxta consuetudinem implere.

CHAPTER XXXV. On unlawful works of Sunday.

  • 410B

The Greeks first threaten with words him who labours on Sunday; if he does it afterwards, then something of his work is at least taken away from him; the third time he is suspended, or they order him to fast seven days. The Greeks wish that on every Sunday laymen as well as priests go to the Eucharist; and he who for three Lord's days does not go to the Eucharist is excommunicated according to canon law. So the Roman goes to the Eucharist, except that he excommunicates him who cannot. Let deacons not break the consecrated bread with the Greeks, nor read the collects, nor say: The Lord be with you. On one and the same altar thou oughtest to celebrate two Masses daily. He who is not present at the earlier Mass ought not to approach the kiss before the Eucharist. He who does not wish to approach the Eucharist 410C, let him not approach the presbyter thereafter for bread, or for the Eucharist, or for the kiss. No one ought to approach the presbyter for the sake of the Eucharist who cannot fulfil the consecration or the reading according to custom.

CAPUT XXXVI. De missae celebratione pro monacho et laico.

Pro mortuo monacho statim tertio die missa cantanda est, et juxta id quod abbas voluerit. Pro pio laico tertio die, vel circa septimum diem missa cantanda est; pro poenitenti homine circa triginta, vel circa septem dies missa celebranda est, si pro eo cognati et amici ipsius fidejubeant, et pro anima ejus saltem aliquid benefaciant altari. Apud Romanos 410Dconsuetudo est corpus consumpti hominis et mariti in ecclesia sepeliri, et chrismate corpus ejus ungere, et missam celebrare super eum, et cum cantu ad sepulcrum portare; cum in sepulcro collocatus sit, terram super eum jacere, tunc primo, et tertio, et nono, et trigesimo die pro illo missam cantare, et postea uti quisque voluerit.

CHAPTER XXXVI. On the celebration of Mass for a monk and a layman.

For a dead monk Mass is to be sung at once on the third day, and according to what the abbot has wished. For a pious layman Mass is to be sung on the third day, or about the seventh day; for a penitent man Mass is to be celebrated about thirty, or about seven days, if his kinsmen and friends stand surety for him, and for his soul do at least some good to the altar. Among the Romans 410Dthe custom is for the body of a man departed and a husband to be buried in the church, and to anoint his body with chrism, and to celebrate Mass over him, and to carry him with chant to the tomb; when he has been laid in the tomb, to throw earth over him, then on the first, and the third, and the ninth, and the thirtieth day to sing Mass for him, and afterwards as each one wishes.

CAPUT XXXVII. De mulieris perceptione sanctae eucharistiae.

Mulieres sub Brunonis regulis ad eucharistiam accedant, uti Basilius docebat. Apud Graecos uxores sacrificium immolare debebant, uti apud Romanos. Tria jejunia legalia sunt quotannis, unum pro omni 411Apopulo, nempe quadraginta dies ante Pascha; deinde cum decimum obolum annuum solvimus, et hoc quadraginta dies ante Natale Domini: tunc omnes pro exercitu orent, et orationes legant, et quadraginta dies post Pentecosten.

CHAPTER XXXVII. On a woman's receiving of the holy Eucharist.

Let women approach the Eucharist under the rules of Bruno, as Basil taught. Among the Greeks wives were to offer the sacrifice, as among the Romans. There are three lawful fasts every year, one for the whole 411Apeople, namely forty days before Easter; then when we pay the annual tenth obolus, and this forty days before the Nativity of the Lord: then let all pray for the army, and read prayers, and forty days after Pentecost.

CAPUT XXXVIII. De vulpibus, et de avibus, et equis, ac feris bestiis quae comedendae sunt.

Pisces emi possunt, licet mortui occurrant, quoniam alius speciei sunt. Caro equina non est prohibita, licet multae familiae eam emere nolint; aves et aliae bestiae, quae in retibus sunt suffocatae, non sunt comedendae; neque quas accipiter momorderit, si mortuae inveniantur, non sunt edendae; quoniam ita exstat in Actibus apostolorum injunctum: Abstinete 411Bvos a fornicatione, et a suffocato, sanguine et idololatria. Halices comedi possunt et boni sunt in febre, et dirrhaea, elixi in aqua, et fel eorum cum pipere misceri potest ad scabiem oris (sanandam).

CHAPTER XXXVIII. On foxes, and on birds, and horses, and wild beasts which are to be eaten.

Fish may be bought, although they are found dead, since they are of another kind. Horse-flesh is not forbidden, although many households are unwilling to buy it; birds and other beasts which have been suffocated in nets are not to be eaten; nor are those which a hawk has bitten, if they are found dead, not to be eaten [non sunt edendae; read sunt edendae after neque, "nor are to be eaten"]; since it stands thus enjoined in the Acts of the apostles: Abstain 411Bfrom fornication, and from what is suffocated, blood and idolatry [Vulg. Acts 15:29 ab immolatis simulacrorum, et sanguine, et suffocato, et fornicatione, "from things sacrificed to idols"]. Herrings can be eaten and are good in fever, and diarrhoea, boiled in water, and their gall can be mixed with pepper for scab of the mouth (to be healed).

CAPUT XXXIX. De apibus quae pungunt homines et de variis rebus.

Si apes hominem occiderint, ita occidantur antequam ad mel perveniant, et saltem ut non per noctem ibi habitent; et edatur mel quod fecerunt. Si porcellus ceciderit in aquam, et vivus extrahatur, spargatur super aquam illam aqua consecrata, et suffiatur cum thure, et vendatur aqua illa; si tunc mortuus sit, et tunc aqua vendi nequeat, effundatur. Si quis impuris manibus cujuscunque generis tangat, vel canem, vel catum, vel murem, vel purum aut 411Cimpurum aliquod animal, Theodorius dicebat quod nihil ei doleat. Si in parvam aliquam aquam mus, vel mustela inciderit, et ibi moriatur, aspergatur cum consecrata aqua, et vendatur (aqua). Quicunque impurum animal pro sua necessitate vendit, nihil ei doleat. Infirmis hominibus permissum est quovis tempore, et quando iis placuerit, cibum sumere. Si quis bis confirmetur, et hoc sciat, jejunet septem hiemes; si hoc nesciat, jejunet tres hiemes.

CHAPTER XXXIX. On bees which sting men, and on various matters.

If bees have killed a man, let them be killed thus before they come to the honey, and at least so that they do not dwell there through the night; and let the honey which they have made be eaten. If a piglet has fallen into water, and is drawn out alive, let consecrated water be sprinkled over that water, and let it be fumigated with incense, and let that water be sold; if it is then dead, and then the water cannot be sold, let it be poured out. If anyone with impure hands touches, of whatever kind, either a dog, or a cat, or a mouse, or a clean or 411Cunclean animal, Theodorius said that it should grieve him nothing. If a mouse or a weasel has fallen into some small water, and dies there, let it be sprinkled with consecrated water, and let (the water) be sold. Whoever sells an unclean animal for his own need, let it grieve him nothing. It is permitted to sick men to take food at any time, and when it pleases them. If anyone is confirmed twice, and knows this, let him fast seven winters; if he does not know this, let him fast three winters.

CAPUT XL. De violatis porcis, et de aliis impuris animalibus.

  • 412A

Si sus edat carnem mortuam, vel sanguinem humanum absorbeat, permittimus tamen ut non abjiciatur, licet comedi non debeat, usque dum purus sit. Si gallina hominis sanguinem bibat, post tres menses in usum converti potest, licet vetus de hoc non habeamus testimonium. Si quis aliquid cruentum edat, in dimidio elixo cibo, si sciat, jejunet septem dies; si hoc nesciat, jejunet tres dies, vel psalterium cantet. Qui proprium sanguinem in saliva nesciens devocet, non est hoc aliquod damnum. Qui aliquid edit ejus quod canis vel mus comedit, vel cui mustela adjacuit, et ipse hoc sciat, cantet centum et decem psalmos. Qui alteri aquam vendit in qua mus 412Bvel mustela fuerint submersi, si sit rusticus, jejunet tres dies; si sit religiosus, cantet trecentos psalmos; si ante nesciverat, et postea sciat, cantet psalterium.

CHAPTER XL. On violated pigs, and on other unclean animals.

  • 412A

If a pig eats dead flesh, or swallows human blood, we nevertheless permit that it be not cast away, although it ought not to be eaten, until it is clean. If a hen drinks a man's blood, after three months it can be turned to use, although we do not have old testimony about this. If anyone eats something bloody, in half-boiled food, if he knows it, let him fast seven days; if he does not know this, let him fast three days, or sing the psalter. He who unknowing draws down his own blood in his saliva, this is not any harm. He who eats anything of that which a dog or a mouse has eaten, or by which a weasel has lain, and he himself knows this, let him sing a hundred and ten psalms. He who sells to another water in which a mouse 412Bor a weasel has been drowned, if he is a countryman, let him fast three days; if he is a religious, let him sing three hundred psalms; if he had not known before, and afterwards knows, let him sing the psalter.

CAPUT XLI. De sancti Dyonisii et sancti Augustini dictis.

Dionysius Areopagita dicebat quod cantus apud Deum odiosus esset, si quis pro malis hominibus missam celebret; at Augustinus dicebat quod pro omnibus hominibus celebranda sit, sive id in commodum fiat hominibus mortuis, sive orantibus, et hostiam liberaliter immolantibus. Qui pro mortuo jejunat, hoc ei ipsi sit solatium, si mortuum non adjuverit. Deus solus scit quid mortuus commiserit.

Sanctus Sylvester dicebat: Acolythus non diffamet subdiaconum aliquem, nec exorcista diffamet aliquem 412Cacolythum, nec ostiarius lectorem aliquem aliquo jure; et nolumus ut subdiaconus, vel acolythus, vel exorcista, vel ostiarius, vel lector, licet infantes habeant, ejiciantur, et legem conjugalem ac Christi recte annuntient: nisi excipe, si omnia non sint quam certissime pura, loquatur septem linguis; nec diaconus aliquis ejici potest, nisi triginta et sex (testium) numero, et presbyter, quadraginta et quatuor numero sint.

CHAPTER XLI. On the sayings of saint Dionysius and saint Augustine.

Dionysius the Areopagite said that the chant would be hateful before God, if anyone celebrates Mass for evil men; but Augustine said that it is to be celebrated for all men, whether it be done for the benefit of dead men, or of those praying and liberally offering the host. He who fasts for a dead man, let this be a consolation to himself, if he has not helped the dead man. God alone knows what the dead man has committed.

Saint Sylvester said: Let an acolyte not defame any subdeacon, nor an exorcist defame any 412Cacolyte, nor a doorkeeper any lector by any law; and we do not wish that a subdeacon, or an acolyte, or an exorcist, or a doorkeeper, or a lector, although they have children, be cast out, and rightly proclaim the conjugal law and that of Christ: unless except, if all things are not as certainly pure as can be, let him speak with seven tongues; nor can any deacon be cast out, unless there be thirty-six in number (of witnesses), and a presbyter, forty-four in number.

LIBRI PRIMI PARS ALTERA.

THE SECOND PART OF THE FIRST BOOK.

CAPUT PRIMUM. Hic incipit primum caput (quomodo) aliquem deceat sibi praecavere juxta peccatorum rationem.

  • 411

Decet sacerdotem considerare peccatorum emendationem juxta jus episcopale, et non respicere divites nec pauperes, ut eis praescribat, prouti liber ipsi praescribit; quoniam Salvator dicit, si sacerdos nolit peccatori emendationem peccatorum suorum praescribere, quod animam illam ab ipso deinde requiret.

CHAPTER ONE. Here begins the first chapter (how) it befits one to take precaution for himself according to the reckoning of sins.

  • 411

It befits the priest to consider the amendment of sins according to episcopal law, and not to regard rich nor poor, so that he prescribe for them as the book prescribes for him; since the Saviour says, if the priest will not prescribe to the sinner the amendment of his sins, that he will afterwards require that soul of him.

CAPUT II. De eo qui in extremo (vitae) suae die ad poenitentiam peccatorum suorum se convertere velit, ut hoc illi non negetur.

Sanctus Isidorus dicit, quod opinio esset hominis veram confessionem et conversionem Deo acceptam 412Dfore in extremo ejus die; quoniam Salvator dicit: Quocunque die homo ad piam vitam revertitur, vivet, et non morietur: ideo non est rejicienda vera resipiscentia, quocunque tempore fiat; quia sacerdos nullam habet necessitatem illis hominibus veram confessionem denegandi, quia Deus contemplatur cujuslibet hominis cogitationes interiores, et novit secreta cordium omnium hominum.

CHAPTER II. On him who on the last day of his (life) wishes to turn to penance for his sins, that this be not denied him.

Saint Isidore says that it was the opinion that a man's true confession and conversion would be acceptable to God 412Don his last day; since the Saviour says: On whatever day a man returns to a pious life, he shall live, and shall not die: therefore true repentance is not to be rejected, at whatever time it happen; for the priest has no necessity of denying true confession to those men, for God contemplates the interior thoughts of every man, and knows the secrets of the hearts of all men.

CAPUT III. De eo qui desiderat poenitentiam agere, et viaticum, hoc est Christi corpus, ex timore mortis; et de eo cui sermo ademptus est antequam confessarius venerit.

Furi necesse est ut sacerdos hujus conditionis hominem consoletur, et confessionem ejus audiat, et ei 413Asaltem eucharistiam non deneget; et licet cum illa infirmitate adeo labefactatus sit uti loqui nequeat, uti ante poterat; tamen si testimonium habeat illorum qui cum ipso sunt hominum, quod confessionem fecerit et eucharistiam perceperit, tunc sacerdos ei remissionem det, et judicium ipsius faciat.

CHAPTER III. On him who desires to do penance, and the viaticum, that is, the body of Christ, out of fear of death; and on him whose speech has been taken away before the confessor came.

For a thief it is necessary that the priest console a man of this condition, and hear his confession, and at least not deny him 413Athe Eucharist; and although he be so shaken with that infirmity that he cannot speak, as he could before; yet if he has the testimony of those men who are with him, that he has made confession and received the Eucharist, then let the priest give him remission, and make his judgment.

CAPUT IV. De eo qui peccatorum suorum confessionem meditatur.

Multi homines poenitentiam aggredi vellent, ast dubitant deinde de peccatorum suorum multiplicitate, et desperant posse se portare id quod confessarius ipsis praescribit, et relinquunt eam; tunc desperatio haec gravius peccatum esse putatur quam peccata quae confiteri cogitabat, et quod jam negligit. Ast qui cum compunctione peccata sua confiteri 413Bvelit, suscipiatur a sacerdote, et assignet ei poenitentiam, quae ad id pertinet ut anima vulnerata, quae per peccata vincta et vulnerata est, pervenire possit ad verum medicamentum, hoc est remissionem Dei.

CHAPTER IV. On him who meditates the confession of his sins.

Many men would wish to undertake penance, but they then doubt because of the multitude of their sins, and despair that they can bear that which the confessor prescribes for them, and they abandon it; then this despair is thought to be a graver sin than the sins which he was thinking to confess, and which he now neglects. But he who with compunction 413Bwishes to confess his sins, let him be received by the priest, and let him assign him a penance, which pertains to this, that the wounded soul, which through sins has been bound and wounded, may come to the true medicine, that is, the remission of God.

CAPUT V. De eo quod quis in multis rebus a desiderio vitae suae abstinere debeat, si peccatorum suorum emendationem facere velit.

Unum est ut justum hominem poeniteat omnium quae erga Deum delinquit, et alterum est ut omittat illa quae corpori suo charissima sunt, ex amore vitae aeternae; quoniam Paulus apostolus dicit: Omnia mihi licita sunt, sed non mihi expediunt omnia: hoc est desiderium mundi, quod nihil iis conducat qui 413Cplenario illud committunt; omnibus etenim hominibus valde necessarium est, ut non omittant peccata sua confiteri, vel emendare, quia omnibus hominibus expedit peccata sua hic corrigere, quam in aeternis tenebris permanere.

CHAPTER V. On this, that one ought to abstain in many things from the desire of his life, if he wishes to make amendment of his sins.

One thing is that a just man repent of all the things in which he offends against God, and the other is that he let go those things which are dearest to his body, out of love of eternal life; since Paul the apostle says: All things are lawful to me, but not all things are expedient for me: that is, the desire of the world, which profits nothing to those who 413Ccommit it fully; for it is very necessary for all men that they not omit to confess their sins, or to amend them, since it profits all men to correct their sins here, rather than to remain in eternal darkness.

CAPUT VI. De eo quod poenitentiam agenti non est licitum aliquam mercaturam exercere.

Sanctus Isidorus, qui librum hunc composuit, dicit de eo qui poenitentiam auspicatur pro capitalibus criminibus, et qui ad tempus terra expulsus est; et ait quod illi non sit permissum in loco mercatorio mercaturam aliquam exercere, nec occupari circa aliqua mundana negotia, antequam adimpleverit id quod confessarius ejus illi praescripsit.

CHAPTER VI. On this, that it is not lawful for one doing penance to practise any trade.

Saint Isidore, who composed this book, speaks of him who begins penance for capital crimes, and who has been expelled from the land for a time; and he says that it is not permitted to him to practise any trade in a market place, nor to be occupied about any worldly business, before he has fulfilled that which his confessor has prescribed for him.

CAPUT VII. De eo quod poenitens post poenitentiae suae contritionem, non debeat reverti ad eam.

  • 413D

Sacra Scriptura dicit quod indecorum sit poenitentem, postquam emendaverit uti confessarius ipsi praescripsit, se aliquo modo mundanis rebus adjungere, et nunquam cogitare de poenitentia, quam antea poenituit, quoniam sanctus Paulus dicit: Qui Deo vult servire non debet occupari in mundanis negotiis.

CHAPTER VII. On this, that the penitent, after the contrition of his penance, ought not to return to it.

  • 413D

Holy Scripture says that it is unseemly for a penitent, after he has amended as the confessor prescribed for him, to join himself in any way to worldly things, and never to think of the penance of which he formerly repented, since saint Paul says: He who wishes to serve God ought not to be occupied in worldly affairs.

CAPUT VIII. De eo quod poenitens non observet id quod confessarius ipsi annotat.

Sanctus apostolus Petrus dicit quod qui post poenitentiam peccatorum ad mundi hujus vanitatem convertitur, 414Asimilis sit omnino cani qui vomit, et deinde devorat. Hae sunt vanitates hujus mundi; primo superbia, et nequitia, et invidia, et durities cordis, et furtum, et ebrietas, et ira, et adulterium, et sacrificium ethnicum, et avaritia, et praeda, et incantatio, et homicidia, et multae aliae his similes: certe homo qui haec committit, non est dignus aliqua communione cum honestis hominibus, et licet cum aliis ad ecclesiam abeat, non praesumat (tamen) Dei altare appropinquare, vel Christi corpus accipere, antequam ad poenitentiam se converterit; deinde faciat prout confessarius ipsi praescribit et ostendit.

CHAPTER VIII. On this, that the penitent does not observe that which the confessor notes for him.

The holy apostle Peter says that he who after penance for sins is turned to the vanity of this world, 414Ais altogether like the dog which vomits, and then devours. These are the vanities of this world; first pride, and wickedness, and envy, and hardness of heart, and theft, and drunkenness, and anger, and adultery, and heathen sacrifice, and avarice, and plunder, and incantation, and homicides, and many others like these: certainly the man who commits these things is not worthy of any communion with honourable men, and although he go to church with others, let him not (yet) presume to approach the altar of God, or to receive the body of Christ, before he has turned himself to penance; then let him do as the confessor prescribes and shows him.

CAPUT IX. De eo quod poenitenti per episcopi intercessionem remissio non detur.

414BEpiscopo et sacerdoti non convenit illis confessionem denegare qui eam desiderant, licet multifariam in peccatorum negotiis occupati sint; quoniam Dei misericordia tanta est, ut nullus homo eam assequi non possit; et propheta per Spiritum sanctum dixit: Cum peccator a peccatis suis ad poenitentiam convertatur, tunc sanus erit, et deinde dicit: Confitere injustitiam tuam, ut sis justificatus; et Psalmista dicit: Quod cum Domino sit innumerabilis misericordia, ita episcopus et sacerdos misericorditer peccatorem judicare debent, quoniam nemo vitiorum exspers est.

CHAPTER IX. On this, that remission is not given to a penitent through the intercession of a bishop.

414BIt does not befit a bishop and a priest to deny confession to those who desire it, although they have been occupied in manifold ways in the business of sins; since the mercy of God is so great that there is no man who cannot attain it; and the prophet said through the Holy Spirit: When the sinner is turned from his sins to penance, then he will be healed, and then he says: Confess thy injustice, that thou mayest be justified; and the Psalmist says: That with the Lord there is innumerable mercy; so the bishop and the priest ought to judge the sinner mercifully, since no one is free of vices.

CAPUT X. De eo qui sacramentum desiderat in extremitate vitae, et de malo quod inde oritur.

414CIn sanctorum apostolorum doctrina dictum est: Si homo in extremo suo die desiderat corpus Christi accipere, non denegetur ei: si tamen in contritione antea fuerit, et licet hoc non plenario emendaverit: quoniam hoc est viaticum ejus, et omnium illorum qui ad regnum Dei perveniunt. Et si deinceps ad hanc vitam revertatur, ut hoc observet quod Deo et confessario suo promisit, portione sua bona utatur, et habitet cum morigeris hominibus.

CHAPTER X. On him who desires the sacrament at the extremity of life, and on the evil which arises from it.

414CIn the doctrine of the holy apostles it is said: If a man on his last day desires to receive the body of Christ, let it not be denied him: if, however, he has previously been in contrition, and although he has not fully amended this: since this is his viaticum, and that of all those who come to the kingdom of God. And if afterwards he returns to this life, so that he observes this which he promised to God and to his confessor, let him use his own good portion, and let him dwell with well-mannered men.

CAPUT XI. De eo quod presbyter poenitentem non debeat suscipere absque venia episcopi, nisi cum episcopum adire nequit.

Sacerdotem quam maxime decet diligenter considerare necessitatem animae illius qui poenitentiam 414Dagere quaerit, quomodo justissime eum praeparare possit ad Dei voluntatem, et ad animae illius commodum, ipsi etiam poenitentiam semper praescribat, juxta delicti rationem: et si delictum adeo capitale sit, ut ab episcopo praescribi debeat, illuc ducatur: quoniam sanctus Augustinus dicit in alio loco: quod si quis per capitalia crimina vinctus esset, ut ad jus episcopale ducatur, et si ad episcopum pervenire nequeat, ut presbyter in illis rebus episcopi vicario munere fungatur.

CHAPTER XI. On this, that a presbyter ought not to receive a penitent without the leave of the bishop, unless when he cannot reach the bishop.

It most of all befits the priest diligently to consider the necessity of the soul of him who seeks to do penance 414D, how most justly he may prepare him for the will of God, and for the benefit of that soul; let him also always prescribe penance to him, according to the reckoning of the offence: and if the offence is so capital that it ought to be prescribed by the bishop, let him be led thither: since saint Augustine says in another place: that if anyone were bound by capital crimes, he should be led to episcopal law, and if he cannot reach the bishop, that the presbyter in those matters should discharge the vicarious office of the bishop.

CAPUT XII. De poenitentibus, quibus temporibus remissio eis danda sit.

Hi mores trans mare apud Christianos observantur, nempe, quod quilibet episcopus sit in sede episcopali 415Adie Mercurii, quem caput jejunii vocamus ante Quadragesimam; tunc quilibet eorum, qui capitalibus criminibus pollutus est, in provincia illa eo die ad ipsum accedere debet, et peccata sua ipsi confiteri, et ipse tum praescribit peccatorum suorum emendationem cuilibet, pro ratione delicti sui; et ita, cum ipsius venia domum redit. Ac deinde die Jovis ante Pascha ad eumdem locum omnes congregat, et episcopus super eos cantat, et remissionem dat (illis), et illi ita domum redeant cum episcopi benedictione. Hoc ita observandum est omnibus Christianis, et diligenter sacerdos considerare debet utrum cum aliqua compunctione, et cum aliqua perfectione poenitens emendaverit id quod ipsi praescriptum erat, et ita ei juxta illud remissionem det.

CHAPTER XII. On penitents: at what times remission is to be given them.

These customs are observed among the Christians beyond the sea, namely, that every bishop be in his episcopal see 415Aon the Wednesday which we call the head of the fast, before Lent; then every one of them who is polluted with capital crimes in that province ought on that day to come to him, and confess his sins to him, and he then prescribes to each one the amendment of his sins, according to the measure of his offence; and so, with his leave, he returns home. And then on the Thursday before Easter he gathers them all to the same place, and the bishop chants over them, and gives remission (to them), and so let them return home with the bishop's blessing. This is to be so observed by all Christians, and the priest ought diligently to consider whether the penitent has amended with some compunction, and with some perfection, that which was prescribed to him, and so let him give him remission according to that.

CAPUT XIII. De eo cui eucharistia prohibita est et sub hoc (interdicto) obit.

  • 415B

Si alicui eucharistia denegata sit, et ipse interea moriatur, de his rebus nihil aliud edicere possumus, nisi quod ad Dei judicium pertineat; quoniam in Dei potestate erat quod absque eucharistia obierit.

CHAPTER XIII. On him to whom the eucharist is forbidden and who dies under this (interdict).

  • 415B

If the eucharist has been refused to anyone, and he meanwhile dies, concerning these things we can declare nothing else, except that it belongs to the judgment of God; since it was in the power of God that he died without the eucharist.

CAPUT XIV. De eo qui pro diversis peccatis suis ad poenitentiam convertere velit cum plena compunctione.

  • 416A

Quicunque per varia peccata vinctus est, et ea cum animo compuncto, et ex amore aeternae vitae, confessario suo confiteri, et prouti ipsi praescribit emendare velit; permittimus ut a Deo remissionem habeat.

CHAPTER XIV. On him who, for his divers sins, wishes to turn to penance with full compunction.

  • 416A

Whosoever is bound by various sins, and is willing to confess them to his confessor with a compunct mind, and out of love of eternal life, and to amend as he prescribes to him; we allow that he have remission from God.

CAPUT XV. De Jacobi sancti apostoli Epistola, in qua dicit quomodo pro aegroto homine orandum, et ipse oleo ungendus sit.

Hic notat sanctus Jacobus: quod si quis infirmatus sit, ut invitet ad se sacerdotem suum, et alios Dei ministros, ut eum admoneant, et infirmus necessitatem suam ipsi indicet, et illum ungant in Dei nomine 416Bsancto oleo, et per fidelium preces, ac per unctionem conservari potest, et Dominus ipsum erigit; et si plenus peccatorum est, illa ipsi remittentur. Hanc unctionem quilibet fidelis, si possit, acquirere sibi debet, et statuta quae ad eam pertinent, quoniam scriptum est quod quicunque hanc disciplinam habuerit, anima ejus aeque pura sit post obitum ac infantis qui statim post baptisma moritur.

CHAPTER XV. On the Epistle of the holy apostle James, in which he says how one is to pray for a sick man, and he himself is to be anointed with oil.

Here saint James notes: that if anyone is sick, he should invite to himself his priest, and other ministers of God, that they may admonish him, and let the sick man declare his need to him, and let them anoint him in the name of God 416Bwith holy oil, and through the prayers of the faithful, and through the anointing he can be preserved, and the Lord raises him up; and if he is full of sins, they shall be remitted to him. Every faithful man, if he can, ought to obtain this anointing for himself, and the ordinances which pertain to it, since it is written that whosoever has had this discipline, his soul is as pure after death as that of an infant who dies straightway after baptism.

If any wife, out of some light fury, hangs her bondwoman, and she dies by the hanging, and is innocent, let the mistress fast seven years. But if she has witnesses that she was guilty, let her nevertheless fast three years.

Si quis seipsum occidit armis, vel aliis rebus diversis, 416Ddiaboli instinctu, non est permissum ut pro tali homine missa cantetur, vel cum aliqua psalmodia corpus terrae committatur. Idem jus faciendum est illi qui pro delictorum suorum tormento vitam suam amittit.

CHAPTER V. On him who kills himself out of some carelessness; and on him who is punished for his sins.

If anyone kills himself with weapons, or with other divers things, 416Dat the instigation of the devil, it is not permitted that a mass be chanted for such a man, or that his body be committed to the earth with any psalmody. The same law is to be done to him who loses his life in torment for his offences.

CAPUT VI. De eo qui indecenter fornicatur, hoc est cum bestiis, vel se cum juvenibus polluit, vel masculus cum masculo.

Vir qui se cum bestia polluit, vel masculus cum masculo in rebus indiscretis, si habeat viginti annos, ut intelligere possit quod rem turpem et indecoram commiserit, resipiscat, confiteatur, et jejunet quindecim annos; et si maritus uxorem suam habeat, et tale quid perpetret, resipiscat, et jejunet quandiu vivit, et non praesumat Domini corpus accipere ante 417Afinem dierum suorum. Juvenes et imprudentes omnino suspendendi sunt, qui tale quid commiserint.

CHAPTER VI. On him who commits fornication indecently, that is, with beasts, or pollutes himself with youths, or male with male.

A man who pollutes himself with a beast, or male with male in indiscriminate things, if he is twenty years old, so that he can understand that he has committed a foul and unseemly thing, let him come to his senses, confess, and fast fifteen years; and if he is a husband having his wife, and perpetrates any such thing, let him come to his senses, and fast as long as he lives, and let him not presume to receive the body of the Lord before 417Athe end of his days. Youths and the imprudent who have committed any such thing are by all means to be suspended.

CAPUT VII. De eo qui adulteratur vel adulterium commiserat.

Qui adulterium committit, jejunet septem annos, tres dies per hebdomadam, in pane et aqua, et si mulier praeter verum suum dominum alium virum habeat, eodem sit digna.

CHAPTER VII. On him who commits adultery or had committed adultery.

He who commits adultery, let him fast seven years, three days in the week, on bread and water, and if a woman has another man besides her true lord, let her be worthy of the same.

CAPUT VIII. De marito qui conjugem suam deserit, et de uxore quae maritum suum deserit et alium eligit.

Vir qui veram conjugem suam deserit, et aliam uxorem ducit, est adulter; non tradat presbyter ei eucharistiam, nec ullum eorum jurium quae Christianos decet; et si obire illum contingat, non sepeliatur 417Bcum Christianis. Et si uxor verum suum maritum deserat, et alium eligat, sit eodem jure digna, uti supra dictum est; et cognati, qui ad hoc dispositi erunt, perdant idem jus, nisi prius ad poenitentiam se convertere velint, prouti confessarius eorum ipsis praescripserit.

CHAPTER VIII. On a husband who deserts his wife, and on a wife who deserts her husband and chooses another.

A man who deserts his true wife, and takes another wife, is an adulterer; let no presbyter give him the eucharist, nor any of those rights which befit Christians; and if it happens that he dies, let him not be buried 417Bwith Christians. And if a wife deserts her true husband, and chooses another, let her be worthy of the same law, as was said above; and the kinsmen who shall have been party to this, let them lose the same right, unless they first be willing to turn themselves to penance, as their confessor shall have prescribed to them.

CAPUT IX. De marito qui uxorem et pellicem habet.

Viro qui legitimam uxorem et pellicem etiam habet, non tradat ei presbyter aliquis eucharistiam, nec aliquod illorum jurium quae Christianis fiunt, nisi ad poenitentiam se convertat. Et si pellicem, et non legitimam uxorem habeat, illud quod ipsi videtur faciendum est, sciat tamen quod uni earum alligatus sit, sive sit pellex, sive conjux.

CHAPTER IX. On a husband who has a wife and a concubine.

To a man who has a lawful wife and also a concubine, let no presbyter give him the eucharist, nor any of those rights which are done for Christians, unless he turns himself to penance. And if he has a concubine, and not a lawful wife, that which seems good to him is to be done, yet let him know that he is bound to one of them, whether it be the concubine, or the wife.

CAPUT X. De eo qui legitimam uxorem habet et adhaeret extraneis mulieribus.

CHAPTER X. On him who has a lawful wife and cleaves to strange women.

If any man lies with another's lawful wife, or a wife with another's true consort, let him fast seven years; three on bread and on water, and four as his confessor shall have taught him.

CHAPTER XI. On a man who takes two sisters in marriage, or on a wife taking one brother after another.

If any wife takes two brothers, one after the other, into consortship with herself, let them be liable to judgment, and let them be in penance as long as they shall live, as their confessor shall have prescribed to them, and after their death, let the priest do for them that right as is to be done 417Dfor Christians, if they promise that, if they should live longer, they would amend themselves. If anyone shall have remained in some unspeakable offence up to his end, we cannot prescribe to him any penance, except that it looks to the law of God.

CAPUT XII. De eo, si aliqua uxor desponsata sit, quod non permissum sit alteri unquam viro rebus suis eam spoliare.

Si uxor aliqua desponsata sit, non est permissum, ut aliquis alius homo eam spoliet; si quis hoc fecerit, sit excommunicatus.

CHAPTER XII. On this: if any wife has been betrothed, that it is not permitted to any other man ever to despoil her of her things.

If any wife has been betrothed, it is not permitted that any other man despoil her; if anyone does this, let him be excommunicated.

CAPUT XIII. De viro qui uxorem vel puellam assumit cum injustis rebus.

Si quis per fraudem uxorem, vel puellam servam 418Ainvitam ad injustum concubitum coegerit, sit excommunicatus.

CHAPTER XIII. On a man who takes a wife or a girl with unjust things.

If anyone by fraud has forced a wife, or a girl who is a bondwoman, 418Aunwilling, to unlawful intercourse, let him be excommunicated.

CAPUT XIV. De viro qui ob injustum concubitum servam procuratricem a domino suo allicit.

Si quis per versutias alterius hominis pedisequam ab eo propter fornicationem alliciat, et cum ea invita coeat, si sit vir ordinatus perdat ordinem suum; si laicus sit, excommunicatus.

CHAPTER XIV. On a man who, for the sake of unlawful intercourse, entices a bondwoman, a stewardess, away from her lord.

If anyone by wiles entices another man's handmaid away from him for the sake of fornication, and lies with her unwilling, if he be an ordained man let him lose his order; if he be a layman, excommunicated.

CAPUT XV. De eo si puella viro desponsata sit, et alius vir illam spoliet invitam.

Si puella aliqua desponsata sit, et interea vastetur, vel aliquibus casibus pellatur, sit ejus cui desponsata erat; et si deinde accidat ut proximi fiant, cum 418Bvenia conjungatur, quoniam ipsa invita ab eo fuerat (separata).

CHAPTER XV. On this: if a girl has been betrothed to a man, and another man despoils her unwilling.

If any girl has been betrothed, and meanwhile is laid waste, or driven off by any mischances, let her be his to whom she had been betrothed; and if afterwards it happens that they become near, 418Blet her be joined with leave, since she, unwilling, had been (separated) from him.

CAPUT XVI. De eo, si puella consecrata ad fornicationem alliciatur. De non consecrata puella quae ad injustum concubitum allicitur.

Si uxor aliqua ordinetur communi ordine, et ea deinde despiciat sponsum cui antea desponsata erat, hoc est Christus, et ad mundanas vanitates revertatur, et familiam suscipiat, et cogitet possessionibus suis et mundi substantiis iram Dei quam excitavit placare; ast non potest aliquid eorum facere quae corpore pio digna sunt, nec ullus presbyter ab ea confessionem postulare potest, antequam peccatum suum deseruerit, et ad Christum reversa sit, ac 418Cdeinde vivat vitam prouti confessarius ipsius illi praescripserit.

Si puella quaedam in societate cum ordinatis habitet, et ad eumdem ordinem tendat, et interea per diaboli tentationem ad fornicationem instigetur, non sit apud Deum innocens, licet non sit ordinata, quoniam antea cogitabat Dei sponsa fieri; tunc ipsi quam maxime necessarium est ut peccata sua confiteatur et emendet, prouti confessarius ejus illam docuerit.

CHAPTER XVI. On this: if a consecrated girl is enticed to fornication. On an unconsecrated girl who is enticed to unlawful intercourse.

If any wife is ordained with the common order, and she afterwards despises the bridegroom to whom she had formerly been betrothed, that is Christ, and returns to worldly vanities, and takes up a household, and thinks with her possessions and the substance of the world to appease the wrath of God which she has stirred up; yet she cannot do any of those things which are worthy of a pious body, nor can any presbyter ask confession of her, before she has forsaken her sin, and has returned to Christ, and 418Cthen lives her life as her confessor shall have prescribed to her.

If any girl dwells in fellowship with the ordained, and tends to the same order, and meanwhile through the temptation of the devil is incited to fornication, let her not be innocent before God, although she is not ordained, since she was formerly thinking to become a bride of God; then it is most of all necessary for her that she confess her sins and amend, as her confessor shall have taught her.

CAPUT XVII. De viro cognatam in uxorem ducente.

Si quis proximam cognatam, vel patris aut fratris viduam, vel matrem suam uterinam in uxorem duxerit, sit excommunicatus ab omnibus Christianis; et si ad poenitentiam se convertat, prouti necesse habet, 418Dcompenset semper, et poeniteat quandiu est, juxta jus episcopale.

CHAPTER XVII. On a man taking a kinswoman to wife.

If anyone has taken to wife his nearest kinswoman, or the widow of his father or of his brother, or his own mother by the womb, let him be excommunicated by all Christians; and if he turns himself to penance, as he has need, 418Dlet him make amends always, and do penance as long as he is, according to episcopal law.

CAPUT XVIII. De eo quod sanctus Gregorius de injusto concubitu dicit.

Si quis monialem, quae Dei sponsa vocatur, sibi in uxorem ducit, sit ea excommunicata ab omnium fidelium communione. Sacri libri dicunt quod eodem modo obnoxius sit apud Christum, ac regis minister qui cum regis consorte coiret. Et si hoc alicubi accidat, uti non fit, ut aliquis per diaboli tentationem in id inciderit, separentur illi, et uterque eorum semper poenitentiam agat, et precetur quandiu in vivis est semper, juxta jus episcopale.

CHAPTER XVIII. On what saint Gregory says concerning unlawful intercourse.

If anyone takes to himself as wife a nun, who is called the bride of God, let her be excommunicated from the communion of all the faithful. The sacred books say that he is liable before Christ in the same way as a king's minister who should lie with the king's consort. And if this happens anywhere, as it does not happen, that anyone should have fallen into it through the temptation of the devil, let them be separated, and let each of them always do penance, and pray always as long as he is among the living, according to episcopal law.

CHAPTER XIX. On a man committing frequent couplings.

It is not permitted to any Christians that they contract marriage more than twice; since saint Paul forbids this in his teaching, and teaches all men how they ought to conduct themselves in this matter; that is, if the first wife of some husband is dead, that with leave he may take another wife; and if he survives her, let him dwell always thereafter without a wife.

CAPUT XX. Prior et posterior sententia eadem est, licet ita divisa appareat.

Ita etiam mulier, si primum maritum suum mortuum sepeliverit, cum venia sumat alium, si hoc eligere velit; et si illa tunc supersit, maneat semper 419Bin posterum in ordine viduae, quandiu vivit. Et si aliquis vir vel femina hoc violaverit, non diffidant Dei misericordiae, sed convertantur, et emendent, prouti confessarius eorum illis praescripserit et ostenderit.

CHAPTER XX. The former and the latter sentence are the same, although it appears so divided.

So also a woman, if she has buried her first husband dead, let her with leave take another, if she wishes to choose this; and if she then survives, let her remain always 419Bthereafter in the order of a widow, as long as she lives. And if any man or woman has violated this, let them not despair of the mercy of God, but let them turn, and amend, as their confessor shall have prescribed and shown to them.

CAPUT XXI. De eo quomodo conjuges se gerere debeant coram Deo.

Sacri libri annotant quid singulis fidelibus faciendum sit, cum legitimam suam conjugem prius domum duxerit; hoc est, juxta libri praescriptum, ut tres dies et noctes primas castitatem suam servent, et tunc tertio die missa eorum fiat, et absque eucharistia, sumatur, ac deinde conjugium suum teneant coram Deo, et coram mundo, ut ipsis necesse est. 419CEt omnes familias decet castitatem suam servare quadraginta dies et noctes ante Pascha, et totam hebdomadam paschalem. Et deinde, noctes diei Solis, et diei Mercurii, et diei Veneris; quaelibet etiam uxor puritatem suam servet tres menses ante partum, et post partum, sexaginta dies et noctes, sive sit masculus infans, sive femina.

CHAPTER XXI. On how spouses ought to conduct themselves before God.

The sacred books note what each of the faithful is to do when he has first brought his lawful spouse home; that is, according to the prescription of the book, that for the first three days and nights they keep their chastity, and then on the third day let their mass be done, and without the eucharist, let it be received, and then let them hold their wedlock before God, and before the world, as is needful to them. 419CAnd it befits all households to keep their chastity forty days and nights before Easter, and the whole Easter week. And then, the nights of Sunday, and of Wednesday, and of Friday; let every wife also keep her purity three months before childbirth, and after childbirth, sixty days and nights, whether the infant be male, or female.

CAPUT XXII. De eo qui rem seducentem committit.

Si aliquis voveat, vel offerat ad torrens aliquod, vel ad lapidem, vel ad arborem, vel ad alias aliquas creaturas, praeter ad ecclesiam Dei, in Dei nomine, jejunet tres annos in pane et in aqua. Et licet praesumat in aliquo (hujusmodi) loco edere vel bibere, et nullam hostiam offerre, nihilominus tamen jejunet 419Dunum annum in pane et in aqua.

CHAPTER XXII. On him who commits a seducing thing.

If anyone vows, or makes an offering at any torrent, or at a stone, or at a tree, or at any other creatures, except at the church of God, in the name of God, let him fast three years on bread and on water. And though he presume to eat or drink in any place (of this kind), and to offer no victim, nevertheless let him still fast 419Done year on bread and on water.

CAPUT XXIII. Et de eo cui permissum non est vanam divinationem exercere.

Certe non est permissum Christianis vanas divinationes committere, uti ethnici faciunt; hoc est quod credant in solem, et lunam, et in cursum stellarum, et constituant divinationes temporum res suas ordiendi. Non fiat congregatio cum aliqua incantatione, nisi cum Pater noster et cum Credo, vel cum aliquibus precibus quae ad Deum pertinent. Si quis hanc rem vanam committat, resipiscat et confiteatur, et jejunet quadraginta dies; et si deinde se vanitati illi rursus adjunxerit, jejunet tres quadragesimas.

CHAPTER XXIII. And on this: to whom it is not permitted to practise vain divination.

Assuredly it is not permitted to Christians to commit vain divinations, as the heathen do; that is, that they believe in the sun, and the moon, and in the course of the stars, and appoint divinations of times for beginning their affairs. Let no gathering be made with any incantation, except with the Pater noster and with the Credo, or with any prayers which pertain to God. If anyone commits this vain thing, let him come to his senses and confess, and fast forty days; and if afterwards he has joined himself again to that vanity, let him fast three Lents.

Et si quis coactus sit pejerare, vel si nesciat, jejunet tres annos, unum in pane et aqua, et duos prouti confessarius ejus ipsi praescripserit, et si non sit ordinatus libertati restituat aliquem ex amore Dei. Et si quis ex timore alicujus vel amore, vel pro usura aliqua pejeret, distribuat possessionem suam pauperibus, et abeat ad monasterium, et emendet, prouti confessarius ejus ipsi adnotaverit, et poenitentiam agat semper quandiu in vivis est.

CHAPTER XXIV. On perjury, how it ought to be amended.

  • 420A

If any layman forswears himself, and knows that he is perjured, let him fast four years; if he be a man of common condition, let him fast five years: a subdeacon six years, a deacon seven, a presbyter ten, a bishop twelve. And if anyone has been forced to forswear himself, or if he does not know it, let him fast three years, one on bread and water, and two as his confessor shall have prescribed to him, and if he is not ordained, let him restore someone to freedom out of love of God. And if anyone forswears himself out of fear of someone, or love, or for some usury, let him distribute his possessions to the poor, and go away to a monastery, and amend, as his confessor shall have set down for him, and let him do penance always as long as he is among the living.

CAPUT XXV. De furto.

Si aliquis rem pretiosam furetur, si sit laicus, jejunet quinque annos, subdiaconus sex, diaconus septem, presbyter decem, episcopus duodecim. Et si quis rem mediocrem auferat, reddat furtum ei qui illud possidebat, et jejunet annum in pane et in aqua; et si non habeat unde furtum reddat, jejunet tres annos in pane et in aqua.

CHAPTER XXV. On theft.

  • 420B

If anyone steals a precious thing, if he be a layman, let him fast five years, a subdeacon six, a deacon seven, a presbyter ten, a bishop twelve. And if anyone takes away a thing of middling value, let him return the theft to him who possessed it, and fast a year on bread and on water; and if he has not the means to return the theft, let him fast three years on bread and on water.

CAPUT XXVI. De falso testimonio.

Si quis in falso testimonio (inventus) fuerit, non est ei permissum eucharistiam accipere antequam confiteatur, et emendet prouti confessarius ejus ipsi 420Cpraescripserit.

CHAPTER XXVI. On false witness.

If anyone has been (found) in false witness, it is not permitted to him to receive the eucharist before he confesses, and amends as his confessor shall have 420Cprescribed to him.

CAPUT XXVII. De discordia cum proximo suo.

Certe nulli Christiano licitum est in ecclesia Dei munus Deo offerre, quandiu invidiam, vel furorem, vel aliquam discordiam habeat in corde suo erga proximum suum; quoniam munus ejus non est acceptum Deo, antequam seipsum reconciliet, et emendet apud proximum suum. Quoniam Salvator in Evangelio suo dicit: Si munus offeras ad altare Dei, et ibi recorderis quod frater tuus, hoc est: quilibet Christianus inimicitiam foveat tecum, desere ibi munus tuum, et abi ad fratrem tuum, et reconcilieris cum eo, et deinde veni ad altare et offer Deo acceptum munus.

CHAPTER XXVII. On discord with one's neighbour.

Assuredly it is lawful to no Christian to offer a gift to God in the church of God, so long as he has envy, or fury, or any discord in his heart toward his neighbour; since his gift is not acceptable to God, before he reconciles himself, and makes amends with his neighbour. Since the Saviour says in his Gospel: If thou offer a gift at the altar of God, and there remember that thy brother, that is: any Christian, cherishes enmity with thee, leave there thy gift, and go to thy brother, and be reconciled with him, and then come to the altar and offer to God an acceptable gift.

CAPUT XXVIII. De furore.

  • 420D

Si quis adeo furiosus et duro corde sit, ut ad nullum jus mundi et pacem redire nolit, cum eo qui erga ipsum deliquit, sit excommunicatus.

CHAPTER XXVIII. On fury.

  • 420D

If anyone is so furious and hard of heart that he is unwilling to return to no law of the world and peace with him who has offended against him, let him be excommunicated.

CAPUT XXIX. De eo qui juramentum praestat quod in gratiam cum proximo suo redire nolit.

Quicunque pro aliqua discordia inimicus fiat proximi sui, et adeo rigidus sit, ut juramentum praestet se in gratiam aliquam cum eo qui erga ipsum deliquit non esse rediturum, sit excommunicatus. Si autem reverti velit, et in gratiam redire, jejunet unum annum propter juramentum, et tres quadragesimas cum pane et aqua; et illo anno praeterea jejunet, prouti confessarius ejus illi praescripserit.

CHAPTER XXIX. On him who takes an oath that he will not return into favour with his neighbour.

Whosoever for some discord becomes the enemy of his neighbour, and is so rigid that he takes an oath that he will not return into any favour with him who has offended against him, let him be excommunicated. But if he wishes to turn back, and return into favour, let him fast one year on account of the oath, and three Lents with bread and water; and in that year let him fast besides, as his confessor shall have prescribed to him.

CAPUT XXX. De eo cui permissum non est ut aliquis pecuniam suam ad injustum tributum tradat.

  • 421A

Cuilibet fideli prohibitum est, pecuniam, vel possessionem suam, ad aliquod injustum tributum mutuo dare, hoc est, ut non offerat plus reddere quam antea mutuo acceperat; sed ex amore, et propter 422Anecessitatem quisque mutuo det aliis pecuniam suam, et possessionem, prouti velit ut ipsi fiat. Si quis hoc ex avaritia quadam faciat, sacri libri ei praescribunt triennale jejunium; unum annum cum pane et aqua, et duos prouti confessarius ipsi praescripserit.

CHAPTER XXX. On this: that it is not permitted that anyone hand over his money for unjust tribute.

  • 421A

To every faithful man it is forbidden to lend his money, or his possessions, for any unjust tribute, that is, that he not offer to return more than he had before received on loan; but out of love, and on account of 422Aneed, let each lend his money to others, and his possessions, as he would wish it to be done to himself. If anyone does this out of some avarice, the sacred books prescribe for him a fast of three years; one year with bread and water, and two as the confessor shall have prescribed to him.

CAPUT II. De iis qui sacerdotalem ordinem rite servare desiderant.

Si presbyter accusatus sit capitalium criminum, antequam ordinatus esset, et ea prius confessus sit, et correxerit, prouti confessarius ejus ipsi praescripserit; si deinde, postquam ordinatus sit, eadem vitia committat, non debet servitium aliquod ad altare Dei facere, sed maneat alias cum communis conditionis hominibus; et si reverti velit, emendet, prouti episcopus ipsi praescripserit.

CHAPTER II. On those who desire to keep the priestly order duly.

If a presbyter has been accused of capital crimes before he was ordained, and first confessed them, and corrected them, as his confessor prescribed to him; if afterwards, after he has been ordained, he commits the same vices, he ought not to do any service at the altar of God, but let him remain elsewhere with men of common condition; and if he wishes to turn back, let him amend, as the bishop shall have prescribed to him.

CAPUT III. Deinde, de presbyteris.

421CSi presbyter vel diaconus hominem occidat vel pejeret, perdat ordinem suum, et si ad poenitentiam se convertere velint, emendent juxta jus episcopale.

CHAPTER III. Next, on presbyters.

421CIf a presbyter or a deacon kills a man or forswears himself, let him lose his order, and if they wish to turn themselves to penance, let them amend according to episcopal law.

CAPUT IV. Deinde, de ministris Dei.

Sacerdotes Dei, et diaconi, et alii Dei ministri, qui in Dei templo Deo servire debent, et sanctuarium ac sacros libros manu tractare, semper castitatem suam servent. Ast si aliquis hujus generis homo in fornicationem inciderit, confiteatur confessario suo et emendet prouti ipsi praescripserit; quia Salvator dicit: Estote sancti, quia ego Dominus Deus et Dominus vester sum sanctus.

CHAPTER IV. Next, on the ministers of God.

The priests of God, and deacons, and other ministers of God, who ought to serve God in the temple of God, and to handle with their hands the sanctuary and the sacred books, let them always keep their chastity. But if any man of this kind has fallen into fornication, let him confess to his confessor and amend as he shall have prescribed to him; because the Saviour says: Be ye holy, because I the Lord God and your Lord am holy.

CAPUT V. De eo quomodo sacerdos et diaconus se gerere debeant.

  • 421D

Sacerdotes, et diaconi, et Dei ministri, qui ipsi nolunt nec possunt coram Deo castitatem servare, quae ipsis injuncta est, detrudantur a ministerio suo, quod homines puri in domo Dei Deo praestant; et si deinceps reverti et confiteri velint, emendent, prouti confessarius eorum ipsis praescripserit.

CHAPTER V. On how a priest and a deacon ought to conduct themselves.

  • 421D

Priests, and deacons, and ministers of God, who themselves are neither willing nor able to keep before God the chastity which is enjoined upon them, let them be thrust out from their ministry, which pure men render to God in the house of God; and if thereafter they wish to turn back and confess, let them amend, as their confessor shall have prescribed to them.

CAPUT VI. De episcopis et de aliis Dei ministris.

Certe omnibus prohibitum est in sacris libris singulis episcopo, et abbati, et presbytero, ut nullius uxoris proximam secum habeant; ita etiam cuilibet ministro Dei, qui in castitate Deo servire debet, prohibitum est ut neque cognatam suam, neque aliam uxorem propter alicujus rei negotium intus 422Asecum habeant, ne forte per diaboli tentationem cum illis peccent.

CHAPTER VI. On bishops and on other ministers of God.

Assuredly it is forbidden to all in the sacred books, to each bishop, and abbot, and presbyter, that they have near them the neighbour of no wife; so also it is forbidden to every minister of God who ought to serve God in chastity, that they have within 422Awith them neither their kinswoman, nor any other wife, for the business of any matter, lest perhaps through the temptation of the devil they sin with them.

CAPUT VII. De hominibus ordinatis qui possessionem suam mercede aliis exponunt.

422BSi episcopus, vel abbas, vel presbyter, vel similis Dei minister, pecuniam suam ad injustum tributum mutuo dederit, et non recordetur ejus quod Salvator per Davidem psalmistam dicit: Tunc divitiis ejus uti debetis, qui pecuniam suam ad nullum injustum tributum tradit. Si quis contra hoc fecerit, non est dignus ut eucharistiam accipiat, antequam hoc emendaverit, prouti hic supra dictum est, hoc est per triennale jejunium.

CHAPTER VII. On ordained men who put out their possessions to others for hire.

422BIf a bishop, or an abbot, or a presbyter, or a like minister of God, shall have lent his money for unjust tribute, and does not remember that which the Saviour says through David the psalmist: Then you ought to use the riches of him who hands over his money for no unjust tribute. If anyone has done contrary to this, he is not worthy to receive the eucharist, before he has amended this, as has been said here above, that is, by a fast of three years.

CAPUT VIII. De ordinatorum procurationibus.

Nulli presbytero permissum est, nec diacono, ut sint praefecti, vel procuratores, vel circa negotia quaedam mundana occupati, nisi circa ea ad quae intitulati sint.

CHAPTER VIII. On the stewardships of ordained men.

It is permitted to no presbyter, nor to a deacon, that they be reeves, or stewards, or occupied about any worldly business, except about those things to which they have been titled.

CAPUT IX. De eo quod ordinatus complacitum sumat cum muliere.

  • 422C

Verum Dei ministrum, qui circa Dei servitia occupatus est, non decet de loco in locum, de domo in domum proficisci, proximi uxorem desiderans, absque doctoris sui venia. Ast si quis hanc consuetudinem habeat, resipiscat, et emendet, prouti confessarius ejus ipsi praescripserit.

CHAPTER IX. On this: that an ordained man makes a compact with a woman.

  • 422C

It does not befit a true minister of God, who is occupied about the services of God, to go from place to place, from house to house, desiring his neighbour's wife, without the leave of his teacher. But if anyone has this custom, let him come to his senses, and amend, as his confessor shall have prescribed to him.

CAPUT X. Deinde, de eodem.

Monachum qui seipsum Deo vovit, et sanctas regulas suscipit, non decet aliquem mundanum honorem desiderare, vel illud quod Deo vovit violare; si hoc fecerit, resipiscat, et emendet, prouti episcopus 422Dei praescribit.

CHAPTER X. Next, on the same.

It does not befit a monk who has vowed himself to God, and takes up the holy rules, to desire any worldly honour, or to violate that which he has vowed to God; if he has done this, let him come to his senses, and amend, as the bishop 422Dprescribes to him.

CAPUT XI. De monachi et monachae delictis.

Si monachus et monialis, qui Deo seipsos consecraverunt, et votum suum Deo voverunt, per diaboli tentationem hoc violent, separentur ab omnibus ordinatis hominibus; et si ad Deum reverti velint, prius cum lacrymis confiteantur, et emendent, quandiu in vivis sunt, prouti episcopus provinciae iis praescripserit.

CHAPTER XI. On the offences of a monk and of a nun.

If a monk and a nun, who have consecrated themselves to God, and have vowed their vow to God, violate this through the temptation of the devil, let them be separated from all ordained men; and if they wish to turn back to God, let them first confess with tears, and amend, as long as they are among the living, as the bishop of the province shall have prescribed to them.

CAPUT XII. De mulieris ministerio ad altare, quod ad eam non pertinet.

Saepe legimus in sacris libris, non esse justum quod aliqua laica uxor impura Dei mysteria in altari 423AChristi tractet, nec sacros libros, nec sanctuarium, quod consecrati homines manu versari debent, quoniam sanctus Paulus hoc admodum prohibuit.

CHAPTER XII. On a woman's ministry at the altar, which does not pertain to her.

We often read in the sacred books that it is not just that any lay wife, unclean, should handle the mysteries of God on the altar 423Aof Christ, nor the sacred books, nor the sanctuary, which consecrated men ought to handle with their hands, since saint Paul has greatly forbidden this.

CAPUT XIII. De eo quod quilibet sacerdos statuta sua nosse debeat.

Quemlibet sacerdotem decet nosse doctrinam quae ad sanctum ejus ordinem pertinet, et eam populum docere, et praeparare illum ad Dei judicium, et ad animae suae commodum.

CHAPTER XIII. On this: that every priest ought to know his ordinances.

It befits every priest to know the doctrine which pertains to his holy order, and to teach it to the people, and to prepare them for the judgment of God, and for the profit of their soul.

CAPUT XIV. De eo quod sanctus Augustinus Romam miserit ad sanctum Gregorium, et sanctus Gregorius ei responsum miserit.

Simul ac sanctus Augustinus in Anglorum natione baptisma introduxisset, et Dei servos ubique constituisset, 423Bet ordinavisset, mittebat epistolam Romam ad sanctum Gregorium, qui papa ibi erat, et rogabat eum ut per epistolam ipsi mitteret quid faciendum sit circa nocturnam pollutionem quae saepe hominibus in somno accidit; utrum crastino die ad eucharistiam abire, vel eucharistiam consecrare possit, si presbyter esset. Ille etiam ei prudens responsum misit, et dixit ut quilibet minister Dei sedulo inquirat de quibus rebus nocturna pollutio oriatur, quoniam ex tribus causis suboritur animo dormienti: interdum ex naturali infirmitate et imbecillitate, interdum ex superfluitate cibi et potus, interdum etiam cum quis die cogitat et meditatur inania et prohibita, quae cum vigilans die cogitat, haec dormienti noctu obveniunt. Itaque, cum quis in 423Csomno pollutus sit ex carnali imbecillitate, non fuit hoc in ejus potestate; oret sedulo ad Deum, et a Deo remissionem petat. Et si hoc ex superfluitate cibi vel potus oriatur, sit obnoxius; attamen non est ei eucharistia deneganda, nec missae celebratio, si presbyter sit, si maxime necessarium sit, et alius non adsit sacerdos, qui hoc ministerium peragat; si autem alius adsit qui hoc ministerium praestare queat, melius ei est, si eo die hoc omittat, et diligenter 424Apsalmodia et eleemosynis remissionem petat. Si autem tertio modo accidat, quod homo die videat, vel cogitet, vel meditetur rem prohibitam, et noxius amor accidat, et cupido ardens, et tunc per hoc, ac per diaboli simulationem polluatur in somno, non est ei permissum eucharistiam sumere crastino die, postquam hoc ei acciderit; sed opus habet illud confiteri et emendare, prouti confessarius ejus ipsi praescripserit.

CHAPTER XIV. On this: that saint Augustine sent to Rome to saint Gregory, and saint Gregory sent him an answer.

As soon as saint Augustine had introduced baptism among the nation of the English, and had everywhere established servants of God, 423Band ordained them, he sent a letter to Rome to saint Gregory, who was pope there, and asked him that he would send him by letter what is to be done concerning the nocturnal pollution which often befalls men in sleep; whether on the morrow a man may go to the eucharist, or consecrate the eucharist, if he were a presbyter. He too sent him a prudent answer, and said that every minister of God should diligently inquire from what things nocturnal pollution arises, since it springs up in the sleeping mind from three causes: sometimes from natural infirmity and weakness, sometimes from superfluity of food and drink, sometimes also when one thinks and meditates by day on vain and forbidden things, which, when he thinks on them waking by day, come upon him sleeping by night. And so, when anyone has been polluted in 423Csleep from fleshly weakness, this was not in his power; let him pray diligently to God, and seek remission from God. And if this arises from superfluity of food or drink, let him be liable; yet the eucharist is not to be refused him, nor the celebration of mass, if he be a presbyter, if it be most needful, and no other priest be present who may perform this ministry; but if another be present who can render this ministry, it is better for him if on that day he omits this, and diligently 424Awith psalmody and alms seeks remission. But if it happens in the third manner, that a man by day sees, or thinks, or meditates a forbidden thing, and a harmful love befalls, and burning desire, and then through this, and through the deceit of the devil, he is polluted in sleep, it is not permitted to him to take the eucharist on the morrow, after this has befallen him; but he has need to confess it and amend, as his confessor shall have prescribed to him.

CAPUT XV. De eo quod sacerdoti nihil prosit quod justus sit, si injustos ab injustitia sua deducere nolit.

Hic dicitur, uti sanctus Paulus docet, quemlibet presbyterum, qui ad populum Dei docendum constitutus est, ut nullius eorum parcat ex alicujus hominis 424Btimore, nec pro pecunia aliqua, ita ut non praedicet omnibus hominibus quid illis faciendum et quid omittendum sit, si ipse velit esse salvus in die judicii coram Deo ipso.

CHAPTER XV. On this: that it profits a priest nothing that he is just, if he will not lead the unjust away from their injustice.

Here it is said, as saint Paul teaches, that every presbyter who has been appointed to teach the people of God, that he spare none of them out of fear of any man, 424Bnor for any money, so that he does not preach to all men what they are to do and what to leave undone, if he himself wishes to be saved on the day of judgment before God himself.

CAPUT XVI. De conversatione presbyterorum.

Quilibet presbyter sit, uti vocatus est. Sacerdos est vox Graeca, et Latine significat sacrum dans; ipse sacrum dare debet populo, cui doctor et pastor constitutus est, hoc est, ut sanctos mores et exempla illis ostendat; admoneat illos, ne de iis dicatur verbum terribile quod Salvator dicebat de pastoribus populi Israelis, inquiens: Vae vobis pastoribus, qui gregis lacte utimini, et eorum lana vos vestitis, et cum pingues essent, eos dissecatis, et cum infirmi 424Cessent, eos non corroboratis, neque aegros medemini, et cum fracti essent, eos non restituitis, et cum dispersi essent, eos non colligitis, et dispersos non quaeritis. Nunc omnis grex meus seditiosus est, quoniam non habent pastorem aliquem, et cibus facti sunt omnibus bestiis feris. Omne hoc dicitur de episcopis et de presbyteris, qui Dei populum in die judicii ad judicium ducere debent, quemlibet ad portionem quae ei antea in vita assignata erat.

CHAPTER XVI. On the manner of life of presbyters.

Let every presbyter be as he is called. Sacerdos is a Greek word, and in Latin signifies sacrum dans, "giving the holy"; he ought to give the holy to the people for whom he has been appointed teacher and shepherd, that is, that he show them holy conduct and examples; let him admonish them, lest there be said of them the terrible word which the Saviour said concerning the shepherds of the people of Israel, saying: Woe to you shepherds, who use the milk of the flock, and clothe yourselves with their wool, and when they were fat, you cut them up, and when they were sick, 424Cyou do not strengthen them, nor heal the ailing, and when they were broken, you do not restore them, and when they were scattered, you do not gather them, and you do not seek the scattered. Now all my flock is seditious, because they have not any shepherd, and they have become food for all the wild beasts. All this is said of bishops and of presbyters, who ought on the day of judgment to lead the people of God to judgment, each one to the portion which was assigned to him beforehand in life.

With one mass the fasting of seven days can be redeemed, and with ten (masses) the fasting of four months can be redeemed, and with twenty masses the fasting of seven months can be redeemed; and with thirty masses the fasting of twelve months can be redeemed. If anyone wishes with true love of God to intercede for himself, it is said in the Holy Scriptures that almighty 429AGod gives men remission of sins in twelve ways. The first remission of sins is through the washing of baptism. The second remission is through the love of God and of men, that one love God with the whole heart, and then his neighbour, that is, any Christian, as himself. The third remission is through the distribution of alms: because it is written that just as fire is quenched with water, so alms blot out the sins of men. The fourth remission is through psalmody, and the shedding of tears, that one do penance for his sins and weep, just as one does for the death of his friend. The fifth remission is through the confession of sins, that one confess his sins to his confessor, and reveal his secrets to him, and then make amends, just as he 429Bshall have prescribed for him. The sixth remission is that one, out of love of God, and for the salvation of his soul, overcome the unjust desires of his body, and so subdue himself through fasting and through other virtues, that the inner man, that is the soul may be held for godly. The seventh remission is that one forsake his possession, and his children, and his fatherland out of love of God, and go away into a foreign land, and there end his life. The eighth remission is when one departs from this life through punishment; and his friends, while he was among the living, can redeem him, and obtain remission for him with God through pious services, and through their worldly possessions. The ninth remission is mercy and good faith. The tenth remission is when we turn someone from his sins to the will of God. The eleventh remission is 429Cthat one, out of love of God, forgive the offences of those who have offended against him, because the Saviour says in his Gospel: Forgive and it shall be forgiven you. The twelfth remission is martyrdom, as was done for the thief before the passion of the Lord, when the Saviour said to him: It is true, what I tell thee, now today thou shalt be with me in the kingdom of my Father.

These are the divine precepts which are to be observed by us, that is, first, true love of God and of men, and chastity, and fasting, and constancy, and that we be humble, and moderate, and kind, and patient, and meek, and hospitable, and full of alms and of holy vigils, and that we be merciful, and peaceable; and let us leave these aside, namely pride, and avarice, and envy, and vainglory, and 429Dtheft, and robbery, and fornication, and surfeiting, and homicide, and perjuries, and lying, and cursing, and contention. The time of services comes in every year, that each one should speak with his confessor, and with his confessor's leave begin his fast, and confess to God and to his confessor the offences which he has committed; whether homicide, or the slaying of a man, or fornication, or any one of those things in which we can offend against God: then mayest thou have (O man) true faith toward God, and at this devout time pray diligently (since thou knowest what thou hast done) with thy fastings, and with thy alms, and with thy prayers, which thou knowest as best thou mayest, and on every Lord's day come to church 430A, and there pray diligently for thyself, and for all the baptized people, and for the confessor, and then thou shalt have prayed for us all. My beloved, I ask thee to consider how thou wast brought forth into this world; and how, and what thou givest to this world in return; and how thy body and soul are to be separated; and afterwards in what place of abode thy soul must await the day of judgment; and also the time when the soul, and thy body are to be gathered together, and then led to the judgment of God: and then thou, and every man, shall be justly recompensed for his works, and shall receive from that judgment; and then with soul and body thou shalt obtain eternal life, or eternal death, according as thou hast offended before, as well eternal life as endless punishment. Then when first in the morning 430Bthou hast risen, sign thyself as diligently as thou canst, and pray to God; when thou hast betaken thyself to bed, the same, that he may remit thee thy sins through God, and, after death, give thee eternal rest and his mercy. Come now, my beloved, consider what our parents were before, and what sort we now are; or which of those now appear, who a hundred years ago were covered with the dust of the earth, such as we shall soon be, when the soul has gone out of the body. Come now, my dear one, since God grants us (time), let us beware of sins, and of those vices which the devil suggests to us: all rage comes from the devil, and all contention, and all misfortune. Come then, let us resist him, and calm our mind, and implore the mercy of God, 430Cand his help, that we may be able to observe his precepts. Come now, let us frequent our churches on Sundays and feasts, and often between these times, the more often the better, and let us beware of envy, and of anger, and of useless words, and of surfeiting, and of blasphemy, and of double speech, and of false witness, and of homicide, and of perjuries, and of frequent fornications, and of any impurity of our body. And come now, let us consider how we ought to prepare our soul, and our body, since God grants us (grace) that we be occupied in that whereby at least after this world we may have rest from the mercy of God. My Lord, I ask thee, who in thy Gospel sayest to all the truly faithful: Ask and it shall be given you: I ask thee, my Lord, 430Dhumbly, that thou grant me that which I beseech, if it please thee, and that the salvation of my soul may endure for ever with the blessing of my worldly life.

A confessor’s handbook: how a priest should receive a penitent, and the fast he should set for each sin, reckoned in days, Lents and winters. It runs through the clergy’s faults, marriage and sexual sin, killing, theft, perjury, magic, and what may be eaten. Migne reprints it from Mansi, who took it from Wilkins’s Concilia Magnae Britanniae. The Latin here is not an eighth-century original. As Migne’s first note says, the manuscripts present these chapters as ones Egbert “translated from Latin into English, so that the unlearned might understand them easily”, and what Wilkins printed is a Latin version of that Old English text. The English idiom still shows through the Latin, and the translation keeps it: years are counted in winters. The closing canons are a separate excerpt from an Oxford manuscript. Most scholars now doubt that the Old English penitential is Egbert’s own work. The notes marked as Migne’s were recovered from the printed page: our digital source does not carry them.
