English

Preface. To my most glorious lords, the kings of the Franks, Hugh, and his son Robert, who bears a presence worthy of the imperial dignity: Abbo, the humble ruler of the monks of Fleury, wishes the gift of everlasting salvation in Christ. After you received the summit of the kingdom by the provident mercy of God, you suffered many misfortunes for the profit of your testing, by a judgment of God hidden but not unjust — and this not from foreign nations, but from the great men of the realm, so that the first days of your rule might be leavened. But because the almighty Lord has delivered you out of them all, remember the goodness and the tender mercy of God; remember your predecessors, the good kings; remember just judgments; and keep it always in your mind to spare the subject and to war down the proud.

Assuredly, most serene lords, because I have found you well disposed toward our order, I have set out in a single sequence the chapters written below, from the books of the canons and of the laws, partly in my own words and partly in the judgments of others; and consecrating them to your name — having first answered my rivals in an Apology — I have published them, briefly gathered, in one volume. In them I have expressed the sum of your ministry, and I have not kept silent as to how the magnates of the realm owe you their fidelity. I have gathered much, too, for the defense of the monastic order…

On this text

The Latin is Migne's printing (PL 139), from the Corpus Corporum transcription, verified against the page scans. Parcere subjectis et debellare superbos is Virgil, Aeneid VI, 853 — Abbo sets the kings of the Franks under Anchises' charge to Rome. Every gilt column mark is an address: this paragraph is migne.app/pl/139/473a.

Du bon, à bon marché — carried to its limit: the good, free.
