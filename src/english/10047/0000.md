## PROLOGUE.

[1025A]

We have learned, on the Psalmist's testimony, that God is wonderful in his saints [n: (Psal. LXVII)], not only in this, that he works wonderful signs through them upon those who are sick, but also in this, that they themselves, recovering through him from the common weakness, were made strong in war. For the weakness of men longs to flee death, and most of all if it is to be inflicted with torments. But the holy martyrs, overcoming this weakness through the grace of God, not only endured the torments offered them, and through them poured out their life, but also of their own accord offered themselves to tyrants and executioners for the most savage punishments. Of such a kind of men I would say, with the best right, that by a divine gift they had exchanged their strength: since what is loved by nature they lay down with none compelling them, [1025B] and what is naturally shuddered at, they themselves willingly pursue. And indeed this strength of mind, however wonderful it is in men themselves, and worthy of the highest praise, is far more wonderful and more praiseworthy in the *501* female sex, which by the coldness of its nature is timid and soft; and as it is most unfit for wars, so it hates swords and arms, and shudders not only at the sight but even at the mention of blood. I have set these things first so that you may understand, dearest brothers, of what kind and how great are the virgins to whose feast you are gathering: in whom we ought to venerate not only their religion, nor their virginity only, but their martyrdom as well — and not any sort of martyrdom, but such a one as they themselves ran to of their own accord from distant regions, no differently than to the highest delights. In [1025C] which act of the highest fortitude they not only shone out, but also, by the example of their virginity, brought a king of the barbarians, not yet reborn in the water of baptism, to the same contest, to be undertaken with the same eagerness in a most holy emulation. And if we compare these virgins and the king with one another in an act of such great virtue, in sex indeed they surpass him by their devotion and their example; but they are surpassed by the king in this, that he, not yet regenerated in Christ, began his first service at so great a height of perfection that he became a martyr before he received the sacrament of the faith; and he who had not been washed with water was washed in his own blood. O truly a very great spectacle, [1026A] to behold him who had not been a Christian stretch out his neck for Christ; and him who had not yet been reborn in baptism be set before very many of the baptized by the palm of martyrdom! But let the history itself now speak to us; let it set before us the origin of these saints, the course of their life, and the glory of the contest they completed.

## CHAPTER ONE. *The devotion of these holy virgins, their captivity, and their freedom in captivity.*

That the city of Rome went before all cities in every kind of nobility, and was the head of the world, is manifest. For if you consider the time before the birth of Christ the Lord, you find the Romans masters of affairs; but if after, you see her made head of the world through the see of blessed Peter; [1026B] if you turn over in your mind the men who came forth in her, you will find them cautious in counsel, vigorous in arms, highest in fortitude, outstanding in justice, moderate in all things. The one thing that had been lacking before Christ — namely the devotion of faith — brought in through Peter, so ennobled the city which until then had surpassed the others in all else, that in this too she goes before the rest. For what would suffice to count how the native-born of this city have been renowned in Christian times in war and in peace? Their number indeed overcomes estimation, and overcomes belief as well. But among those who purchased the heavenly kingdom by their torments, these women assuredly, to whose feast *502* you are gathering, hold a special dignity. For putting on a manly spirit, born at Rome, they deserved to live most religiously at Rome, and at last at Rome to die [1026C] most steadfastly. The glorious virgins Flora and Lucilla, then, sisters, in the times — so far as can be understood, and as it is given us to know — of Antoninus surnamed Pius, when at Rome they burned in the divine sight with their holy manners, and outwardly, according to the propriety of their names, flowered and shone, chose the apostolic life, by which they might withdraw themselves from the love of earthly things, and offer up the sanctified vessels of their body by the sanctification of chastity; and they who had learned that the angels fell through pride, might prepare for themselves through humility an ascent to the heavens. When therefore by these and other good deeds they were pleasing to God and to men, the Lord willed [1027A] to test them through tribulations, as gold through fire, that he might make them more glorious in his sight. For when God had furnished the holy virgins with the virtue of fortitude, he did not will that they should lie hidden, but that they should go forth into the field of contest: so that they who had conquered the sufferings of their body and the temptations of the devil might overcome the world itself. Taking his beginning therefore from lighter things, by his holy counsel, in the way in which he draws good even out of evils, he permitted the holy women to be carried off from their own region, that they might both receive the merit of patience, and announce the name of Christ to foreign nations, and bring them to his worship.

Now there was in those times in the parts of Africa a barbarian king named Eugegius, who, hostile to the Roman regions and attempting to lay them waste, brought the holy virgins, among the rest of the captives, [1027B] God so willing, into his own region: and when he saw that they were outstanding in youth and beauty, he burned with love for them. And when he was taking care that women's finery should be furnished them with royal splendor, the virgin Flora, prudently perceiving what the barbarian intended, said to him: See what you do. For we have a most powerful bridegroom, who, stirred by injuries to us and to himself, will drive his fury against you. The barbarian rages on hearing this; and swollen with the vanity of pride, thinking that no one could be found who could exercise vengeance upon him, with troubled mind and arrogant voice he answered: And who is this bridegroom of yours, and of what power, that he can avenge his injuries or yours upon me? But Flora, the virgin of the Lord, in no way trembling, and setting a manly confidence into a woman's thought, said: We have as bridegroom God [1027C] and the Lord Jesus Christ, who founded heaven and earth out of nothing, and all things that are contained within the compass of heaven. Whom all things serve, to whom nothing can be difficult; to this Lord God we have pledged our very selves, him we serve with unstained heart and body. And how discordant it is, how impious, and how impossible, that this should be violated, should be defiled, should serve your lust — *503,* you may see. For it cannot persuade you that, if you set about violating the temple of God, which we are, you will go unpunished [n: (I Cor. III, VI)]; rather you ought to persuade yourself, more surely than surely, that you will straightway be destroyed by him whom you provoke in injuries done to us. Hearing this, the most unconquered king, marveling at so great a steadfastness in a woman, at her eloquence and her love of chastity, is struck with fear at the Lord's prompting; and changing into religion the impure [1027D] desire with which he had prepared violence for the virgins, he venerates the highest and true God in their hearts; and at once he ordered a private and adorned chamber to be prepared for them, to which no entrance should lie open to any of the men, but to maidens alone, whom he had appointed to the perpetual service of the holy women. He commanded these also not to withdraw from their service, but to serve them with the utmost reverence, [1028A] as handmaids of the God who dwells in the heavens.

The holy sisters therefore, seeing so great a providence of God concerning them, by which he had made a protector out of a robber and a defender of modesty out of a corrupter of it, and had changed a prince's court into a monastic cloister, marveled at the divine counsel and gave great thanks to the author of all good things, who had made them, even set in captivity, find so great favor with a foreign king; for whose safety and likewise perpetual salvation they frequently prayed to God, as was just, lest they should be marked with any stain of ingratitude. Meanwhile the king's mind is being formed toward the faith, and he so trusted in their holy prayers that, when about to set out for war, he most urgently [1028B] commended himself to them. And when he was bidden by them to be of good courage, or heard that he would return home safe, then, as though fortified by a heavenly oracle, conceiving a sure hope of victory, he went down into battle and always, at God's prompting, carried back the victory. By these most evident signs of their holiness the king, understanding that he was protected by God through their prayers and helped in all his doings, pursued the virgins with greater honor, and held them as mothers, judging himself blessed, and his kingdom too, which was protected by the merits of the handmaids of Christ and made firm by their prayers.
