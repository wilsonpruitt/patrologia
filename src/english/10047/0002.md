## CHAPTER III. *The burial and translation of the holy bodies.*

Their bodies were gathered by the faithful and buried with Christian devotion in the suburb of the city of Ostia, where they rested for *506* seven hundred years and more, that is, until the year of the Lord nine hundred and one. At which time the religious father John, bishop of Arezzo, chancellor of the palace, sought and received the holy bodies of Saints Flora and Lucilla and of king Eugegius from Benedict III, Roman pontiff of that name. And taking religious care that they be carried by his own household [1030D] toward Arezzo, he himself returned by another way, lest he suffer any hindrance from the Romans. But since God willed to spread abroad the names and merits of the virgins for his own glory and theirs, it happened on a certain day that the bishop's household with the holy bodies lodged between a lake, Disortium by name, and the marsh of Arezzo. And the dwellers of the place, and chiefly its lords, recognizing the holy [1031A] relics from the reverence of the place and the religion of the household, obtained by God's prompting a certain particle of them. And in order that, according to the apostolic admonitions, they might be grateful for such great benefits of God, they founded a basilica in honor of God and of blessed Mary and of Saints Flora and Lucilla, and had it dedicated by their holy relics. They ordered also that the name of their town and of their lordship be named from the name of Saint Flora, whereas before the town was called Amia, and they themselves were called the counts of Amiata.

Departing therefore from the town of Saint Flora and from its religious counts, they were pursuing the journey they had begun, and were already drawing near to the plain of Arezzo, when behold, by God's counsel, the beast of burden that was carrying the holy bodies would allow itself to be led no further by any urgings or blows. The attendants were astonished, and looking to one another [1031B] and not knowing what the martyrs meant by this, do not fail to make known to the bishop, who had already reached Arezzo, what had befallen them. Then the devout prelate, having received the message and accompanied by a very great multitude of clergy and people, went out to meet the saints, and, holy prayers having been sent up first and sprinkled with tears, tried by himself to move that beast. And when he too could not, taking counsel with his men, they let the beast go, knowing for certain that that place had been chosen by the saints to which of its own [1032A] accord the pack animal should hasten. And this being done, the brute animal, led by the angels of God and by the holy martyrs, as though breaking out from a prison, climbed at a most swift course the mountain which was then called Titanus, and halted in the very place appointed for the saints from heaven, which is distant from the city of Arezzo by nearly two miles. There, by the brief devotion of the faithful, a basilica was made, and a monastery of monks, which by the holiness of those dwelling in it and by the intercession of the virgins grew up wonderfully for many years with frequent and great miracles. Because of the renown of this monastery and the devotion to the holy women, a castle was founded there, and from Saint Flora — as also the whole mountain itself — it received the name by which it flourished for very many years.

*But the monastery in the year of the Lord one thousand [1032B] one hundred and ninety-three was destroyed through the Guelf and Ghibelline factions of the men of Arezzo. And this* *507* *desolation being accomplished, it was translated with the holy relics into the city of Arezzo, where to this day the holy virgins are venerated with great reverence of the people. But the castle afterward, in the year of the Lord one thousand one hundred and ninety-six, was destroyed on account of the same factions, our Lord Jesus Christ reigning, who lives and reigns forever and ever. Amen.*
