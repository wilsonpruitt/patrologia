## CHAPTER II. *The translation of the body; miracles.*

These things, therefore, which could come to our knowledge concerning the life of the blessed man, were not indeed seen by those who reported them to us, but were made known by the well-known memory of succeeding generations.

But there was in our own recent times a certain very ancient old man, already trembling with a body wasting away, who testified that he had seen the nephew of this blessed Maurus — Constantius by name — bishop of the Church of Cesena. *422* But since [0948D] we have set out with running pen those things concerning the life of blessed Maurus which it fell to us to hear, let us now briefly explain how almighty God revealed his merits to the glory of his own name.

There is now a well-known monastery on the aforesaid mountain on which the man of God had been accustomed to pray, established in honor of the blessed Mother of God and Virgin, where by the ancient custom of the people it is called [0949A] the mountain of Maurus, since that place is not without reason adorned with the holy man's name.

When therefore that chest in which the blessed man's poor body was enclosed was lying hidden beside the aforesaid church, the heap of earth growing up on every side, and scarcely the top of the stone itself stood out level with the ground, it happened that a certain man, coming with the litanies, the lace of his boot having come undone, suddenly set his foot there and, thinking nothing of the sort, tied it without concern. His ankle then being bound, when he now wished to go away, he could in no way move his foot. And when he had long tried, and, attempting everything, turned himself this way and that, his foot nonetheless stood unmoved and was held fast to the stone as though by nails. The wretched man stood, guilty of an unknown offense, and he who had wished to tie his own strap grieved that he had rather tied himself to the stone. [0949B] He wished to stand, but leaning on one sole he could not; he longed to lie down, but was not able to fall away from his own foot. At length, a sudden concourse of people coming up, some with mallets, some with axes, eagerly cut away part of the stone, and after much labor were scarcely able to carve the man out of the rock. Soon a shout of the rejoicing crowd arises, the wonderful power of God is exalted, and the tomb of blessed Maurus is judged worthy of reverence.

The same monk of the aforesaid monastery — Teuzo by name — now advanced in years and venerable by his very gray hair, from whose account we have learned these things, related another miracle which we by no means pass over in silence. At one time this same aforesaid brother was pressed by a most bitter force of fevers. And when by no medicinal care, by no human effort at all, he had been able to return to a state of soundness, [0949C] blessed Maurus appeared in the silence of the night to a certain religious aunt of his — a nun, that is — and said to her: If you wish, he said, that your nephew recover from the sickness he suffers, tell him to fix the sign of the holy cross over my burial place, and to vow that he will keep a feast for me all the year. And when the man gladly fulfilled the blessed man's oracle, which he learned from the nun's report, as soon as he fixed an iron cross over the tomb, then, his inward parts being stirred, he vomited violently, and so at once cast off all the trouble of the fiery sickness, as though the vomiting itself were expelling it; and now wholly restored to his former health, he rendered worthy thanks to God his Savior, and took care to make known to many, for the glory of God's name, [0949D] the miracle of blessed Maurus which he had experienced in himself.

*423* And now, as the well-known report of both signs flew about among the people, many began to run devoutly to the holy relics of the blessed man, and to commend their souls more earnestly to his devout merits; and some also, laboring under various troubles of ill health, when they either drank the filings ground from the marble of the tomb, [0950A] or implored the mercy of the blessed confessor by poured-out prayers alone, returned to their own homes sound and whole, by whatever bond of sickness they had been bound. You might have seen the slack corpse-like bodies of pitiable sick men carried in, held up first by another's support, who soon were making their way back with great exultation and dancing, restored to their former health.

And when, with so many miracles flashing out, the more renowned fame of blessed Maurus grew ever more frequent, and the powers which the Lord showed forth through him resounded not only through all the neighboring places but through more distant tracts as well, at length, the bishops of the nearby cities having been called together and many crowds of peoples flowing in from every side, that chest in which the blessed man's protecting relics are laid away is eagerly [0950B] dug up, and, the wall of the church being pierced, is placed with hymns and praises with the utmost care between the apse and the altar. But in the course of time that same basilica was made larger, and, as is now seen, more festively adorned; where, to declare the merits of the blessed confessor, many miracles are done, God working, even to the present day — of which we touch on a few, which assuredly those who report them to us testify that they saw.
