## CHAPTER III. *Various miracles at the relics.*

Pope Gerbert had encamped beside Cesena, and the armies poured around were hemming in his town with a siege. At that time there was brought to the tomb of blessed Maurus a certain countryman, weighed down with a twofold [0950C] affliction of diseases, being namely crippled and blind; but after prayer had been made, both the lost light returned to his eyes, and the sickness, put to flight, released his stiffened limbs. Straightway the man, seeing himself so wonderfully restored to health, giving such thanks as he was able, handed himself over as a servant to blessed Maurus by perpetual right, and vowed that he would remain in his service as long as he lived. And he, enjoying the gift of the health he had received for nearly ten years, afterward weakly gave way to the frailty of the flesh, and joined a wife to himself in an ill-omened marriage. For as soon as he had got possession of the pleasure of indulgence, he wholly lost the light of his eyes, and *424* as long as he lived, he remained in that same blindness. And surely it was fitting that he who, withdrawing himself from the right of blessed Maurus, to whom he had made himself over, shamefully put his neck under the yoke [0950D] of lust, should lose the gift with which he had been enriched by his good Lord: and that he who had of his own accord fallen into the inner darkness should unwillingly run into the outer.

At another time also a certain woman possessed by a demon was led to the altar of the blessed man, whom the pestilent spirit had filled with such fury that, making her as it were his instrument, [0951A] he uttered many sacrilegious and mad things through her mouth, sent out dreadful cries, and barked out like a rabid dog with repeated bayings. But on the third day after she had been brought there, she vomited blood with a certain violent rush, and so cast out the malignant spirit by which she was held.

A certain other woman, intent more than she ought upon the necessity of household affairs, did not fear to clean wheat on the feast of Saint Rufillus; but since she did not show the reverence of worthy honor to the blessed confessor, she did not escape the sentence of a worthy chastisement. For on the spot, struck by the divine judgment, she stiffened with her arms drawn up, and, in the same manner as if she still seemed to be winnowing with the sieve, she began wretchedly to tremble. And so she is led before the altar of blessed Maurus, and most urgently promises that on the aforesaid [0951B] feast she will never again do any such thing; and soon the most bountiful mercy of almighty God was graciously at hand, and fully restored the woman's numbed arms to the freedom of their own strength.

Again another woman was suffering an issue of blood; and when she was being continually harassed by the incurable wound, and, trying this thing and that, had been able to find no remedy of health, at length, set in the utmost straits, necessity itself found the counsel. And so with a clean linen cloth, so far as her means allowed, she covered the tomb of blessed Maurus, and, being thus delivered by God, escaped altogether every trouble of the ghastly sickness. For indeed, since the Apostle says: *Cleansing their hearts by faith [n: (Act. XV, 9)]* — because with a clean heart, being namely full of faith, [0951C] she touched the blessed man's tomb, she worthily deserved to be freed from the uncleanness of the flowing blood.

Likewise another woman was so twisted by the monstrousness of a most violent sickness that, the order of nature being undone, she held her face behind her and carried her neck before her. She likewise placed a linen cloth over the memorial of blessed Maurus, and straightway, health being received, nature restored to each of her limbs its own right, and tempered the vessels of the arteries to holding their proper vigor.

Another woman also, having lost her sight, was brought from the borders of Aquitaine, *425* who, keeping the temper of Gallic spiritedness, and after the manner of her country breaking her words out violently, testified most obstinately that she would never depart [0952A] from there unless through the merits of blessed Maurus she had received back the sight she had lost. And so, kindled with a great fervor of faith, she put together a small hut beside the wall of the church, so that in a manner she seemed to be preparing a dwelling rather than a lodging. And when prayer had been made most urgently for eight days, she received, by the bestowal of divine grace, the sight she sought, and rendered worthy thanks to God her enlightener and to Maurus his servant.

A crowd from the diocese of Forlimpopoli was making its way to the threshold of blessed Arduinus for the sake of prayer; and when it had passed through the oratory of blessed Maurus, it happened that a certain woman of that company, having humbly kissed the knee of the abbot of that monastery, asked leave to go on; and she, having advanced scarcely the shot of a single arrow, [0952B] was soon seized by a demon and is thrown to the ground, shaken, rolled about, and she kept bitingly spewing out many insults against that same abbot. And so she was led before the altar of blessed Maurus, and not long after was freed by the command of the divine power, so that, when her companions were returning, being now restored to herself, she went back together with them to her own home sound and whole.

These few things, therefore, dearest brothers, we have written concerning the powers which the almighty Lord has deigned to work through his blessed confessor Maurus, that we may stir up the zeal of your charity to glorify the might of the divine majesty. He, while he deigns to show the power of his miracles to us still set upon the way, through these invites and calls us on to lay hold of the inheritance of the heavenly country; [0952C] the Psalmist bearing witness, who says: *He will declare,* he says, *the power of his works to his people, that he may give them the inheritance of the nations [n: (Psal. CX, 6, 7)]*; so that, while we behold the very tombs of the saints shining with so many miracles, we may in no way doubt of their immense glory, in which they already reign with Christ; that, earthly things being despised, our mind may now transfer itself by desire to that place where it hopes to rejoice forever, and our spirit may freely direct itself there where Christ the emperor of heaven reigns unspeakably with the senators of the court above; to whom be glory and dominion with the co-eternal Father and the Holy Spirit through all ages of ages. Amen.
