## ARGUMENT.

To his nephew Damian he commends chastity especially among the other virtues; and, that he may guard it the more easily, he urges him to receive the Lord's body daily. Then he instructs him against the snares of the demon. Lastly, he asks that he commend him to the prior, whom he is to admonish concerning the keeping of hospitality.

[0709D]To DAMIAN, his most dear son, PETER, a sinful monk, the affection of a father's love.

[0710D]Because the bearer of this letter is anxiously making ready to set out, I cannot write out what must be sent with any polished ordering of style; [0711A] and I would have you consider the sense of the truth rather than go hunting after the bedizened allurements of words. For the present, therefore, while I cannot write back at leisure, let it suffice that this be plainly written to you, which the Apostle enjoins by letter upon Timothy as upon one of like age with you: « Till I come, he says, attend unto reading, unto exhortation, and unto doctrine [n: (I Tim. IV)]. » To whom, assuredly, he had already sent these words before: « Be thou an example of the faithful in word, in conversation, in charity, in faith, in chastity [n: (Ibid.)]. »

## ON CHASTITY AND THE MEANS OF GUARDING IT.



## CHAPTER ONE. *That the virtue of chastity is more difficult for the young.*

And, to say nothing for the moment of the other virtues which have just now been enumerated in the letter, be zealous to keep chastity — which I set last — not only in the bodily [0711B] members, but let this same virtue above all reign and be vigorous in the inmost parts of the heart. For there is no virtue which in the flower of youth endures heavier contests, assailed as it is by the inborn ardor of a titillating enticement, as though by a furnace of rising flame. But since I am not permitted to discourse more copiously upon this theme, because in my excessive haste there is no leisure, let it not irk me to set down here, briefly and hurriedly, what the empress Agnes — once crowned indeed with a golden diadem, but now far more happily and incomparably more eminently placed in the bridal chamber of the eternal King — told me yesterday, as the twilight of evening was already slipping away; so that in your mind it may not be so much a fringed prolixity of speaking as the worth of a notable example that gains strength. Now while the aforesaid [0711C] bride of Christ and daughter of blessed Peter feeds with delight upon the ordering of all the virtues, yet she lingers the more readily upon the treatment of the cleanness of chastity and modesty. Whence it came about that, while we were talking together in mutual discourse of certain matters touching the salvation of souls, and were especially turning over in our hands, as it were, some little blossoms of chastity in their spring, she brought this forward into the midst: After the emperor Otto, she said, as victor snatched the kingdom of Italy from Berengar, he straightway carried off his two captive daughters into exile in the German parts. And since these, by the comeliness of their countenance and the notable beauty of their persons, recalled the dignity of a royal line, many nobles of the realm began vehemently to press the empress Adelaide, that they might be counted worthy to join to themselves in a dowered union [0711D] the grace of a form so elegant. But these girls, since they despised all their suitors with an altogether equal disdain, and loathed a mortal marriage with the lifted brow of a holy pride — one of them placed two chicks beneath her breasts, and hid them between her garment and her flesh until they had utterly rotted. And when she saw men coming to converse with her, she would secretly loosen her garment, and rejoiced to breathe out the stench upon the nostrils of those who spoke with her. And when day by day those who conversed with her suffered this affront to their noses, at length the baffled eagerness of her seekers ceased; and the virgin of Christ, by the feigned putrefaction of another's flesh, preserved the inviolable [0712A] integrity of her own body. Whence it came to pass that both sisters afterward chose the purpose of the consecrated life, and to the end of their days kept the monastic institution blamelessly. *743*

## CHAPTER II. *That frequent reception of the Eucharist is a safeguard of chastity.*

Wherefore let it shame you, O son, to succumb unmanfully to the vice of lust, or even to succumb in thought, seeing that you behold the weaker and frailer sex triumph over it with so great glory. And that you may be able, so to speak, to drive the raging beast out of the field of your own jurisdiction, take pains, brother, to fortify yourself now daily by the reception of the Lord's body and blood. Let the hidden enemy see your lips red with the redness of Christ, [0712B] and let him shudder at them in terror, and presently flee away trembling into the lurking-places of his own darkness. For what you receive under the visible appearance of bread and wine, he, willing or unwilling, understands to be the truth of the Lord's body and blood.

Moreover, the same venerable queen then told me that lately in the church of Worms, when through negligence the Eucharist had been kept for a rather long time in the pyx, flesh alone was afterward found there. So that, as the gaze of many was bent upon it rather curiously, one would beyond doubt have judged it to be nothing else than true and solid flesh.

Rainald also, the venerable bishop of the Church of Como, being present, set forth as a truthful narrator what had befallen him a little before: There is a priest, he said, in [0712C] the church which by God's appointment I serve — and unless I am mistaken he is alive to this day — who has so small and slender an acquaintance with the learning of letters that even a plainly written passage he can scarcely read syllable by syllable; yet, constrained by excessive poverty, he is compelled somehow to celebrate the solemnities of the Masses. Once, when he had carried the Eucharist of the sacred gift to a sick man, being as he was slothful and negligent, a little of the Lord's blood remained in the chalice. And when, returning into the church, he discovered this, but, hindered by squeamishness, was unwilling to consume it, he presently washed the chalice and cast the water into a marble basin in which there was hallowed water. And — O great token of divine power! — that part of the basin which what had been poured out of the chalice touched, it suddenly turned to a bloody redness, in the place, namely, where [0712D] two larger drops of blood stand out; and neither those, nor the others which appear beside them to be smaller, can by any effort be either washed off or wiped away, so that the redness of the imprinted blood might in any degree be done away. Since, therefore, that purple gore clung so tenaciously in a vessel of stone, how great a right of power do we suppose that heavenly virtue claims for itself in a Christian breast? And so, most dear son, do not put off fortifying yourself the more frequently with these heavenly sacraments, whereby you may drive back the devices of diabolical craft and crafty contrivance. Rise up girded; stand ever in the battle line, steadfast and strong; and be not ignorant that [0713A] an unfailing adversary fights against you untiringly. *744*

## CHAPTER III. *The devil transfigures himself into an angel of light, that he may deceive a monk.*

Meanwhile, as I say these things, there now returns to memory that also which Hildebrand, archdeacon of the Roman Church, related in the presence of the same persons mentioned above and of the bishop: I saw, he said, two holy monks, and obtained no slight place of familiarity with them; who indeed dwelt in separate cells, at the monastery which is built in the place called Aachen; of whom the one, who seemed to be of the greater simplicity, was called by his own name Marinus, and the other [0713B] Romanus. But as for Marinus, there was scarcely a day on which he did not either behold the devil in some appearance or hear him in a dream — so much so that the ancient enemy would often chant the psalms along with him, and celebrate together with him the offices of divine praise. But once the author of darkness transfigured himself into an angel of light, and, as though laying down a law, charged him: Take heed, he said; since you have been counted worthy both to behold and to address an angel of God, it is unfitting that henceforth you should speak with men. Straightway that man, as though he had received a command from heaven, withdrew himself utterly from converse with the brethren, and bound himself under a rigid strictness of silence. And when brother Romanus first, and then the abbot of the monastery, vehemently pressed him to speak with them, and he would put forth nothing from his plundering lips [n: ( suppl. responsum vel quid simile )], [0713C] the abbot, violently breaking in the doors of the cell, chastised the indiscreet brother with the stripes he deserved, and so compelled him to render the office of speech. And presently he added this by way of admonition: For this reason, he said, the ancient adversary, ever the enemy of good works, attempted to lay silence upon you: that you might both lose the fruit of fraternal edification, and that those who were to be relieved by you might not have the help of your consolation. And so the brother, because he had sinned not from malice but out of simplicity, returned to his right mind with all speed; and, despising the devil's suggestion, humbly acquiesced in the admonitions of the devout father.

But while I rouse you, my son, to bring to nothing the snares of the ancient enemy, I do not omit what the aforesaid queen related that she had heard from the mouth of blessed Leo the Ninth, pontiff of the Roman [0713D] Church. So the aforesaid pope said: My aunt, having been made a nun, was established in a certain monastery of men; and there, dwelling in her own cell with a certain pygmy — that is, a woman of short stature — she discharged the offices of daily praise patiently and humbly.
