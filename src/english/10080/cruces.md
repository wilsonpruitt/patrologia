# Cruces — 10080, *De castitate et mediis eam tuendi* (Peter Damian)

## Migne's own bracketed apparatus (passed through untranslated)

Three bracketed editorial supplies appear in the Latin and are reproduced verbatim
in the English, per the variant rule:

- **chunk 1, before col 0714A** — *ante nocturnae synaxis [articulum, *vel* horam
  *vel aliquid simile* ]*. Migne flags a gap in the text and offers *articulum* /
  *horam* / "or something similar" as candidate supplies. Rendered as "before the
  [articulum, *vel* horam *vel aliquid simile* ] of the night office," leaving the
  supply unresolved.
- **col 0714B** — *obnoxiis [ *f.* obnixis] precibus*. The printed reading
  *obnoxiis* ("submissive") is what I have translated; Migne's *forte* conjecture
  *obnixis* ("earnest, insistent") is the likelier original and fits *impetravit*
  better. Bracket retained; **flagged for a human decision.**
- **col 0716B** — *earum guttur gladio defaecant [desecant]*. The printed
  *defaecant* ("they strain / cleanse") is not sense here; the bracketed
  *desecant* ("they cut through") is plainly right and is what I have rendered
  ("sever their throats with the sword"), with the bracket kept in place.

## Grammatical roughness rendered per the intended sense

- **col 0714C** — *Nam et eadem Agnes regina quae supra inquit etiam quia vir
  quidam…* The connective is tangled: *quae supra* wants a participle
  (*quae supra memorata est*), and *inquit … quia* mixes direct and indirect
  speech. Rendered "For the same queen Agnes mentioned above said also that a
  certain man…"
- **col 0712D** — *sed neque illae, neque aliae … nullo possunt studio vel ablui
  vel abstergi.* A triple negative (*neque … neque … nullo*) which in late Latin
  reinforces rather than cancels. Rendered as a single negation in English.
- **col 0713C** — *Cui mox admonendum subjunxit.* Gerundive *admonendum* standing
  where a noun or adverbial phrase is expected. Rendered "he added this by way of
  admonition."

## Point of substance for a human read-through

- **col 0715A** — *Adjecit etiam dominus ut uni earum vestem traderet, qua vim
  frigoris aliquatenus temperaret.* The subject of *traderet* is ambiguous — the
  lord himself, or the companion at the lord's direction. At col 0715B the
  companion says *vestimentum illud quod sibi tunc dedi* ("that garment which I
  then gave her"), which settles it: the lord **directed the companion** to hand
  over the garment. I have rendered 0715A accordingly ("The lord further directed
  that he should hand over a garment to one of them") so the two passages agree.
  Rendering 0715A as "the lord gave" would put the narrative at odds with itself.
- **col 0713D → chunk 1** — the work's chunk boundary falls mid-anecdote (Leo IX's
  aunt and the pygmy nun), and chunk 1 opens *Quadam vero nocte…* with the subject
  carried over from chunk 0. Frontmatter `colContext: "0713D"` on chunk 1 records
  this; no text is lost, but the two chunks must be read continuously.
