## CHAPTER ONE. *What it is for a priest to go out from the holy places.*

It plainly belongs to a holy priest both to think the things that are holy, and also to express plainly in his works what he has conceived in his mind: otherwise what does it profit [0492B] to think anything good, unless he skillfully keeps watch to bring this very thing to completion in action as well?

The priest must therefore labor that his head be consecrated through the regard of a devout will, and that his hands be anointed with the charism of spiritual oil through the plain evidence of holy work. He is also said to be clothed with holy vestments, because it is fitting that the priest be always adorned with the garments of devotion *497* and of justice, as it is written: « Let thy priests put on justice [n: (Psal. CXXXI)]. » He is commanded not to uncover his head, that is, not to spread his heart open to the desires of this world, but to keep it always hidden in the intention of gaining the heavenly kingdom. He is bidden also not to rend his garments — namely, that he walk always so covered with the vesture of honorable and devout [0492C] conduct that he never, which God forbid, tear it apart by the interpretation put upon any perverse deed. He is forbidden besides to go in to a dead man: namely, that he take care warily to withdraw his foot from the evils by which the soul dies, and to guard himself from dead works with ever-wakeful concern. Of which the Apostle says: « Christ has rescued us from dead works, to serve the living God [n: (Hebr. IX)]. » It is added also that he be not defiled over his father and mother, that is, that he be polluted neither by the affairs of the world nor by desire for this mortal life. Whence it is well added, he shall not go out from the holy places, lest he pollute my sanctuary. For a priest goes out from the holy places when, the care of souls neglected, he wanders about through earthly desires. He goes out from the holy places when, [0492D] ecclesiastical discipline set aside, he entangles himself too immoderately in the business of passing affairs. Where [0493A] there is at once fitly added: « Lest he pollute my sanctuary. » For the sanctuary is polluted when any priest, scattered abroad by earthly desires and dissipated by secular causes, does not fear boldly to stand at the most holy altars; when, namely, that comes to pass which is said through the prophet: « And it shall be, as the people, so the priest [n: (Isa. XXIV)]. » Wherefore, that the Church of Christ may be strong to flourish in her own cleanness, and may not grow filthy with the obscene contagion of those who minister ill, there is still fitly added in that place: « He shall take a virgin to wife, not a widow, not one put away; and a defiled woman and a harlot he shall not take, but a maiden of his own people, lest he mingle the stock of his own race with the common folk of his nation; for I am the Lord, who sanctify him [n: (Levit. XXI)]. » The priest's wife is not unfittingly taken to be his life and his manner of life [0493B]; which is commanded to be a virgin, in that it should be kept unharmed and uncorrupted from the worn-out wantonness of the unclean spirit, who is the violator of souls. Let her not be a widow, but joined to the heavenly bridegroom by a happy covenant. She is forbidden also to be one put away, or defiled, or even a harlot: namely, lest, if she is polluted by the filthy defilement of lust and of riotous vices, she be separated by a deadly divorce from that same bridegroom of souls. To this defiled wife the Apostle had given a bill of divorce when he said: « The things that were gains to me, these I have counted losses for Christ, for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and count them as dung [n: (Philip. III)]. » As if he were to say: That defiled and harlot wife, to whom I had been joined in Judaism, I forsake by divorce [0493C], and, a bill of repudiation intervening, I cast her off like dung. For from a priest's wife of this kind there are not begotten such children as may offer sacrifice to God, but rather such as incur the severity and vengeance of divine chastisement. Hence it is that, when it is said by the divine voice to the prophet Hosea: « Go, take to thyself a wife of fornications, because the land, committing fornication, shall commit fornication away from the Lord, » he, as Scripture testifies, « went, and took Gomer, the daughter *498* of Debelaim; and she conceived, and bore a son. And the Lord said to Hosea: Call his name Jezrahel [n: (Osee I)]. » Which name, although it seems to signify something prosperous, in that it is interpreted *the seed of God,* here nevertheless sounds *the vengeance of God* and *fury,* since there is straightway subjoined: « For yet a little while, [0493D] and I will visit the blood of Jezrahel upon the house of Jehu, and I will make the kingdom, the house of Israel, to rest [sic: *requiescere*], and in that day I will break the bow of Israel, in the valley of Jezrahel *(Ibid)*. » But that here by Jezrahel not salvation or prosperity, but rather the anger of God is signified, is intimated in this also, since there is straightway subjoined: « She conceived again, and bore a daughter, and the Lord said: Call her name, Without mercy, because I will no more add to have mercy on the house of Israel, but with forgetfulness I will forget them. And when she had borne a third also, he says: Call his name, Not my people; because you are not my people, and I will not be your God. » In which names of the sons of the fornicating woman we plainly learn that whoever is joined as a husband to an unclean life as to a wife [0494A], and takes delight in her ill-flattering embraces, cannot beget from her children who may please God; because neither can sweet fruit be brought forth from a bitter root: « For a bad tree cannot bring forth good fruits [n: (Matth. VI)]. » Whence the priest must beware of taking a defiled wife or a harlot, but must always display the honorableness of a chaste and modest manner of life. From her, assuredly, he may be able to beget not bastard but lawful sons, while he takes pains, by the bounty of divine grace, to bring forth the freeborn fruits of good works. It is necessary therefore that a priest take a priestly wife, from whom he may beget offspring that will answer to its father's stock. Whence there too it is fittingly added: « Let him not mingle the stock of his own race with the common folk of his nation [n: (Levit. XXI)]. » For the priest [0494B] joins the stock of his own race to the common folk of his nation when, living in a common way, he is not at variance with the people by the merit of a more shining life: when, in short, he walks along the broad and spacious way of worldly conduct, and does not separate himself from the vulgar crowds by the honorableness of [sic: *spritiualium*] character. But that a priest may beget offspring worthy of his race, let him too not stray from the footsteps of his fathers, that is, of the holy priests who went before: lest, if by living ignobly he degenerates from their distinguished lineage, he lose the right of the priestly office, which, plainly, he was discharging unworthily.
