## CHAPTER II. *That the nobility of priests is a holy life*

Hence it is that in the book of Esdras, when it has first been set down [0494C] concerning the sons of the priests — the sons of Hobia, the sons of Accos, the sons of Berzellai, who took a wife of the daughters of Berzellai the Galaadite, and their name was called after them — there is straightway added: « These sought the writing of their genealogy, and found it not, and they were cast out from the priesthood [n: (I Esdr. II)]. » Plainly, those who do not find the writing of their genealogy are driven back from the priesthood; because when men consider that they have degenerated, through the ignobility of a secular life, from the freeborn lineage of the fathers who went before, it is surely fitting that the vigor of canonical censure expel them from the priesthood *499*. The priest of the Church must therefore be noble, so that he who is the minister of the Lord may blush to be the slave of sin. To which nobility he has advanced us of whom the Apostle says: « Who has rescued us from the power of darkness, [0494D] and has carried us over into the kingdom of the Son of his love, and whose blood has wiped away from us the slavery of sin [n: (Coloss. I)]; » of whom it is said through John: « Who loved us, and washed us from our sins in his own blood [n: (Apoc. I)]. » But that the priest may be able to possess this cleanness in himself beyond the rest, let him take pains to express in himself, in a marked way, the brand of the blood of Christ; as the Apostle says: « I always bear the marks of Jesus in my body [n: (Gal. VI)]. » Whence in the law also it was commanded that with the blood of the sacrificed ram there be touched the tip of the right ear of the high priest, and likewise of the right hand and of the foot [n: (Levit. II)]. And so, that the discourse may quickly disengage itself from this point: the priest bears in his ear the blood of the sacrificed ram when it delights him [0495A] to hear with eager longing the mystery of Christ's passion. The same blood is smeared upon his right hand also when he effectually carries out what, as an earnest hearer, he understands. The right foot too is marked with the same gore when the priest's life is fortified against the temptations of the devil by the remembrance of Christ's passion; so that, as he has received the power, he may walk upon serpents and scorpions, and yet not fear their bites and the poisons of their feeble rage; or else surely, because the foot is the body's extremity, what is designated by this but the end of a good work? Namely, that every good which we begin we should also perseveringly carry through even to a happy end. Hence it is that the divine voice says to the people of Israel: « If thou shalt hear, he says, the voice of the Lord thy God, so as to do and to keep his commandments, [0495B] the Lord thy God will make thee higher than all the nations [n: (Deut. XX)]. » In which words there is plainly expressed what is figured in the members: « If thou shalt hear, he says, the voice of the Lord thy God, » behold, the blood of the sacrificed ram grows red on the ear of the priest. But what follows, « so as to do, » behold the hand of one at work. And what is subjoined last, « and to keep his commandments, » behold the perseverance of good work. For it profits no one to begin a good work, unless he takes pains also to keep it until he brings it to completion.

And it is to be noted that only the right ear, hand, and foot of the priest are commanded to be smeared with that blood; whereby, namely, he should so always hold in all things the rightness of a right-hand manner of life and of faith, that he may not, with his steps straying [0495C] from the way, run into the wandering turns of the left. Or else surely that he should so always hold the mystery of the Lord's passion for the right hand, that he may despise all the allurements of this world as utterly left-handed and worthless. For indeed, because the priest is the leader and standard-bearer of the Lord's army, he is bidden to walk adorned with these badges of honor, and ought to carry before him the banners of the blood and of the cross of Christ for the squadrons of the ecclesiastical soldiery that follow. This the prophet Zacharias figured when he said: « In that day, that which is upon the bridle of the horse shall be holy to the Lord [n: (Zach. XIV)]. » But further, before the coming of the Savior, what was the human race but like an unbridled horse, which unrestrained liberty *500* was snatching away everywhere through the fields of its own pleasures? But our Redeemer [0495D] applied a bridle to its mouth when he set before it the holy Gospel, by which, namely, all the vices of wanton licentiousness are curbed. And upon this bridle is placed the holy thing of the Lord, that is, the cross, because all the precepts of the holy Gospel breathe the cross, and invite that the cross be carried after Christ.
