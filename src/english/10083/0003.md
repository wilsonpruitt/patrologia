## CHAPTER III. *That even the least suspicion of a crime is to be fled by a priest.*

This same thing that golden plate also signified, which the high priest bore upon his head for glory and for beauty, on which, namely, the tetragrammaton name of the Lord was inscribed [n: (Exod. XXVIII)]. For as there were four letters there, so none the less are there four [0496A] horns of the cross, embracing the parts of the fourfold world. This the priest bore for glory and for beauty; since the Apostle also says: « But God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ [n: (Gal. VI)]. » Let the priest therefore bear the golden plate upon his head, that he may continually turn the cross of Christ over in his mind. Let him likewise, as a consequence, apply the holy thing of the Lord to the bridle of his horse, that he may crucify his flesh with its vices and concupiscences. For then the priest applies both the golden plate to his head and the holy thing of the Lord to the bridle of his horse, when he directs the gaze of his mind to the cross of Christ, and restrains his body, like a fierce horse, from all liberty of wanton vices. Assuredly it is on account of this twofold mystery of bearing the cross that it is written that both Elias [n: (IV Reg. II)] and John the Baptist [0496B] had leathern girdles about their loins [n: (Matth. III)]. But of our Savior John says in the Apocalypse: « That he had a golden girdle about his breasts [n: (Apoc. I)]. » Now it is agreed that between the two breasts the heart of man is set. Whence the bride says in the Canticles: « My beloved shall abide between my breasts [n: (Cant. I)]; » that is, he shall continually rest in my heart. What then is it to gird the loins, where lust is most of all vigorous, with leathern girdles — which, namely, are made from dead animals — but to bridle on every side, through the affliction of the body, all the unlawful motions and kindlings of carnal passions? And what is it to have a golden girdle about the breasts, but to keep watch always with anxious guard against the troublesome phantasms of assaulting thoughts as well? We gird our loins, therefore, with the leathern [0496C] girdle when we check the wantonness of the flesh by the continence of fasting. But we have the golden girdle about our breasts when we fight with implacable struggle against the onslaught of harassing thoughts. And so then will come to pass what follows there; for as soon as the prophet had premised that the holy thing of the Lord would be upon the bridle of the horse, he at once subjoined: « And there shall be caldrons in the house of the Lord, like bowls before the altar, and every caldron in Jerusalem and in Judah shall be sanctified to the Lord of hosts [n: (Zach. XIV)]. » Plainly the Lord was commanding in the Gospel that caldrons be turned into bowls, and that a golden girdle be had about the breasts, when he said: « Take heed lest your hearts be weighed down with surfeiting and drunkenness, and the cares of this world [n: (Luc. XXI)]: » in surfeiting, namely, and drunkenness understand the caldrons; in the cares of this world [0496D] *501* understand the girdle that is to be had about the breasts. Caldrons, plainly, were the breasts of those who sighed after the fish of Egypt, who, sick of the manna, hungered for melons and gaped after onions and garlic. Or were they not caldrons, who cried out, seething, that « we were sitting over pots of flesh? » [n: (Num. XI.)] For those hearts are caldrons in which gluttony reigns, and in which, through the boiling heat of greed, dainties and a varied abundance of foods are cooked down. But these caldrons are turned into bowls, and are before the altar, and sanctified to the Lord, when the Holy Thing of the Lord is set upon the bridle for horses, when, in short, the golden girdle is applied to the inner breasts. Then assuredly the caldrons and the pots of flesh are turned into golden bowls, namely full of sweet odors, [0497A] which are the prayers of the saints [n: (Apoc. V)]. And these are set as bowls before the altar which before, while they were caldrons, were rather busied in the reek of the kitchen.

*502* But then the mind of the priest is a golden bowl, and full of the sweet odors of spiritual perfumes, when it is watchfully intent upon prayers and devout works. And this bowl is said to be before the altar, because it does not cease to offer to God, as a sacred shrine, everything that it thinks. And then that caldron is sanctified to the Lord, and therefore the grace of consecration, which he himself received inwardly [0498A] from the gift of the highest bestower, he now deservedly, [sic: *e*], pours over into others by the imposition of hands applied to them. But I, forgetful as it were of who it is that speaks, while I draw out the discourse too long, exceed the bound of epistolary brevity. Let these things therefore now suffice, only that I, who had begun from a blessing, may conclude my word in a blessing.

May the right hand of God, venerable father, fill thee with its blessings; and, with the flock committed to thee accompanying thee, bring thee into the heavenly kingdoms.

*Blessed be the name of the Lord.*
