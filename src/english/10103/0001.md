## CHAPTER II. *The prerogatives of Paul are related.*

Paul, caught up, ascended to the third heaven [n: (II Cor. XII)], where he also heard secret words which it is not lawful for men to speak. And who does not know that the earthly life bespeaks the left, while the heavenly signifies the right — as the bride says in the Canticles: « His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me? » [n: (Cant. II.)] For the left hand is said to be under the head because the present life, in any elect soul, is despised and trodden down beneath the mind, which is beyond doubt the head of the thoughts. But the bride is said to be embraced by the right hand of God because, when the faithful and holy soul [0591D] is kindled with the burning fire of its Creator, it is cherished on every side as though by certain divine embraces. He therefore who, still girt about with the frailty of the flesh, is known to have ascended to the heavenly life, is rightly named *son of the right hand*. This son of the right hand almighty God stretches out perpetually, as his own right hand, through the whole breadth of the world, and by it gathers the peoples of adoption into the unity of the faith. This right hand the supreme husbandman turns over the threshing-floor of his Church, and by it winnows the spiritual crops, that he may bring the cleansed grains of the mystic wheat into the heavenly barns. This right hand of his God stretches far out hither and thither, while Paul runs through the whole globe of lands to gather the nations into the sacrament of the faith. For with as strong [0592A] a charity as he burns, with so excessive a wandering does he shift himself from place to place. *608* He longs to pass to some men from others, because the goad of that very charity which fills him drives him on. For, set far from the Romans, he writes: « I make mention of you always in my prayers, beseeching, if by any means at length I may have a prosperous journey, by the will of God, of coming to you. For I long to see you [n: (Rom. I)]. » Detained at Ephesus, he writes to the Corinthians: « Behold, this is the third time I am ready to come to you [n: (II Cor. XXII)]. » Again, staying at Ephesus, he speaks to the Galatians, saying: « I would I were with you now, and could change my voice [n: (Gal. IV)]. » Shut up too at Rome in the keeping of a prison, because he is not permitted to go to the Philippians in his own person, he promises to send a disciple across, saying: [0592B] « I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you shortly, that I also may be of good comfort, when I know the things concerning you [n: (Philip. II)]. » Bound also with chains and held at Ephesus, he writes to the Colossians: « For though I am absent in body, yet I am with you in spirit [n: (Coloss. II)]. » See with how great a fire of brotherly love the apostolic heart is boiled down, with how great a longing it is constrained to sweat for the salvation of the nations! Here he is held in body; thither he is led in spirit: and he spends the affection of a father's love upon those present, and reaches it out to those absent. To those set before him he displays his works; to those who hear him he expresses his prayers. He is effectively present to those with whom he was not, and yet not absent from those with whom he was. We know his love better if we still weigh his words to the Corinthians. For he says: « I will come to [0592C] you when I shall have passed through Macedonia; for I shall pass through Macedonia, but with you perhaps I shall remain, or even winter [n: (I Cor. XVI)]. » What does it mean that he is so anxiously parceled out through so many places, unless that he is bound about all men by one charity? For charity, which is accustomed to unite what is divided, compels the one heart of Paul to be divided among many things. Yet it gathers that heart in God the more ardently, the more widely it scatters it through holy desires. In preaching, therefore, Paul wishes to say all things at once; in loving, he wishes to see all men at once; because both by remaining in the flesh he wishes to live for all, and by passing out of the flesh through the sacrifice of faith he wishes to profit all. Nor is it a wonder if the distinguished preacher glows more fervently in brotherly charity, if the abundance of true love overflows in him beyond all mortals; since the fountain of life [0592D] who calls him a vessel of election [n: (Act. IX)] determined to flow abundantly into him, and deigned to water him incomparably with the streams of his sacraments. Hence it is that he says: « Do you seek a proof of Christ, who speaks in me? » [n: (II Cor. XIII.)]

## CHAPTER III. *Paul was marked out before all others by a wonderful privilege.*

This plainly the Savior of the world conferred upon blessed Paul by the privilege of a singular grace, which he granted beforehand to no mortal after the mystery of his Incarnation. For after that blessed and divine humanity of the Redeemer had been carried up to the session [0593A] of the Father's majesty, so that he was no longer bodily present among men, but rather presided over the powers of heaven, he taught him so effectively *609* not through a master but specially through himself, and made over to him the mysteries of all his own deeds, that not only did he in no way stand in need of the teaching of his predecessors, but he even surpassed them in the depth of all heavenly wisdom. Whence Peter himself, the first of the apostles, marvels at his wisdom, saying: « Our most dear brother Paul, according to the wisdom given him, has written to you; as also in all his epistles, speaking in them of these things, in which are certain things hard to be understood, which the unlearned and unstable wrest [n: (II Petr. III)]. » And that in learning the mysteries of the evangelical doctrine he had no man whatever for master, but God [0593B] specially for his teacher, he himself testifies, who speaks at the beginning of his Epistle to the Galatians, saying: « Paul, an apostle not from men, neither through man, but through Jesus Christ and God the Father, who raised him from the dead [n: (Gal. I)]. » For the fountain of eternal life, like a boundless torrent, plunged itself wholly and all at once into the vessel of its own election — that fountain which poured out the streams of its wisdom upon the rest of the apostles through long intervals of time. And a little after: « For I make known to you, brethren, the Gospel which was preached by me, that it is not according to man; for neither did I receive it from man, nor did I learn it, but by the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ [n: (Ibid.)]. » What therefore the Lord, while still mortal, taught Peter by many proofs, all this [0593C] he displayed at once to blessed Paul when he was already established in the glory of the Father's majesty. It is as if some teacher, while he is a private man, should teach disciples; but after he has climbed to the holding of the scepters of royal rule, then, the [sic: *clastibus*] of dependents being removed, chooses one distinguished man among many, whom he may instruct more nobly.

That the discourse, then, may run back to the point where it began: when blessed Peter is set at the right, the primacy which he obtained among the other apostles is honored. But when Paul occupies the place of the same right side, he gives off the sacred sign of the mystical figure in Benjamin, whose son he is. And yet neither is this without mystery, that blessed Peter holds the left of the Lord's side. For by him the active life is marked out. And as the contemplative life is expressed by the right, so the active [0593D] by the left. And that the present life, that is, the active, is figured by blessed Peter, is shown chiefly in this: that, as the evangelical history very frequently testifies, Peter loves the Lord more than all his fellow-disciples [n: (Joan. XX)], and yet the Lord bestows the prerogative of an altogether special love not upon Peter but rather upon John [n: (Joan. XIII)]. For by John the contemplative life is marked out, but by Peter, as has been said, the active. Because all the saints in this life, after the likeness of Peter, love God more and are as it were loved less, while for his sake they endure the hard troubles of labors, and yet, still girt about with mortal flesh, do not attain to the sweetness of his embraces. But those who are already with God, in this [0594A] in a manner love him less and are loved more, since both they feel no storms of labors for his sake, and yet they enjoy to the full the delights of his inmost intimacy. It is no wonder, then, if blessed Peter should sometimes seem to hold the left-hand part, who *610* also holds, on the authority of the Scriptures, the figure of the active life.
