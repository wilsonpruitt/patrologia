# Cruces — 10103, Peter Damian, *De picturis principum apostolorum* (PL 145)

Garbled or doubtful type in Migne's plate, rendered literally per the intended
reading and logged here.

- **col. 0589B (chunk 0000), head** — `DE PICTURIS PRINCIPUM APOSTLORUM.` The
  head as printed reads *APOSTLORUM* for *APOSTOLORUM* (dropped **O**). The
  Latin head is preserved verbatim in `heads:`; the English head is translated
  from the intended reading, "ON THE PAINTINGS OF THE CHIEF APOSTLES."

- **col. 0589B (chunk 0000)** — `sanctae conversionis [conversationis] abbati`.
  Migne prints the variant in square brackets in the text. *conversatio* (monastic
  way of life, the Benedictine term) is almost certainly right; *conversio* is the
  printed lemma. Rendered from the printed lemma ("holy conversion") with the
  bracketed variant passed through verbatim, as with `[ *al.,* …]` variants.

- **col. 0589C–0590B (chunk 0000)** — the column sequence jumps 0589D → 0590B;
  there is no 0590A anchor in the TEI. Reproduced as printed; not an error on our
  side. (Migne's 0590A is occupied by the facing-page note block.)

- **col. 0592A (chunk 0001), note** — `[n: (II Cor. XXII)]` for the quotation
  *Ecce tertio hoc paratus sum venire ad vos* = 2 Cor. 12:14 (or 13:1). Migne's
  numeral **XXII** is impossible — 2 Corinthians has 13 chapters; the intended
  reading is XII. Note contents pass through **verbatim untranslated** per the
  marker rule; flagged here only so the citation index does not treat it as a
  real locus.

- **col. 0593C (chunk 0001)** — `remotis clientium clastibus`. *clastibus* is
  not a Latin word; the intended reading is *classibus* ("the throngs/companies
  of dependents being removed") or possibly *catervis*. Rendered per the intended
  reading, "the crowds of dependents being removed."

- **col. 0595B (chunk 0002), Scholia** — `illum tantum bonorem` for *honorem*
  (dropped **h**). Rendered per the intended reading, "that great honor."

- **col. 0595B (chunk 0002), Scholia** — punctuation is broken around the 2 Cor.
  XI quotation: Migne opens `« verum est quod idem affirmat: (Instantia mea
  quotidiana…` — an unmatched `(` and a guillemet pair that does not close where
  the sense requires. Reproduced as printed (guillemets kept per the marker rule);
  a human read-through may want to note the intended punctuation in an apparatus
  rather than silently repair the plate.

- **col. 0594B–C (chunk 0002)** — the Romans quotation opens `« Habeo, inquit,
  gloriam in Christo Jesu…` and **never closes**: Migne prints no `»` before
  *Haec est igitur causa*. The English reproduces the plate exactly (opening
  guillemet kept, no closing mark supplied), consistent with the marker rule and
  with the Scholia punctuation crux below. The quotation plainly ends at
  *intelligent [n: (Rom. XV; Isa. LII)]*.
