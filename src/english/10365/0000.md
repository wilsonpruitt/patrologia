## On Keeping Confession Secret

*379* ARGUMENT.--It seems to be asked in this little book, when a penitent's own priest has made his confession public, to whom the penitent ought then to confess. I. Having proved the virginity and the unity of the Church, the author shows of how many evils the breaker of the seal of confession has been the cause.--II. That in this case — namely, where the penitent suffers so great a scarcity of priests that none at all can be found — he may confess to clerics, or even to clean laymen, that is, to the faithful; not that such a confession (to set forth in this matter the opinion of the theologians) confers grace *ex opere operato* (for it is not sacramental), but *ex opere operantis,* namely from contrition, and from the humility which the one confessing attests in the disclosure of his own offense, so that he may draw upon himself a greater blush of shame; whence in some manner God supplies the office of a priest.--III. That where neither priest, nor cleric, nor layman is to be found, one must not despair of salvation, but must confess to God alone: which he proves from various authorities of the holy Fathers.

[0625C]Being asked concerning the virginity of mother Church — how she is a virgin, who consists not only of virgins but also of the married, of the corrupted and of the incorrupt in flesh — I ought not to have denied to charity the giving of a testimony concerning that virginity. For she is a virgin, and truly a virgin; for, if we gaze into the mirror of purity and truth, she is also truly a virgin, having the image [ *al.,* hujus] of that virginity which the blessed Virgin Mary is in the flesh: mother Church is a virgin in conscience. And we bring this matter the more quickly into line if we diligently examine the four ecclesiastical sacraments — faith, baptism, the consecration of the Lord's body and blood, and the remission of sins. For mother Church is by faith what blessed Mary is in the flesh, because what the one conceived in the Holy Spirit, the other believes in the Holy Spirit. In the sacraments [0626C] of baptism, of the Lord's body and blood, and of the confession of sins she is not divided; and because she is truly one, she is truly a virgin, since every virgin, in that she is a virgin, cannot be divided in two; for flesh which is virgin is perfectly virgin. Blessed [ *al.,* vero] mother Church, therefore, is one and the same in the sacraments and in faith, seeing that she suffers nothing corrupt in her conscience, even as neither does the Virgin Mother of God; and as the latter is a virgin in body, so she also is a virgin in mind. And so in the mirror of this virginity, for the beholding of the face of truth in the sacraments of confession, certain men are darkened — and, what is graver, men who seem to have the image of religion. Let us look upon these things the more diligently; and let everything that is not one in *380* holy Church vanish from the eye of truth.

That there are three sacred things in confession the Lord [0627A] declared in his own person: the figure of baptism; one conscience made out of two; and God and man in the same judgment. In the figure of baptism, he who confesses renounces through penitence and believes through faith, just as he who is baptized renounces and believes. Whence the Lord also received from the Father the same testimony in the transfiguration as in the baptism, and there was added: *Hear ye him [n: (Math. XVII, 6)]*. For of him concerning whom John had heard: *This is he that baptizeth [n: (Joan. I, 33)]*, the disciples heard: *Hear ye him;* because he who baptizes in baptism is himself heard in the one who gives penance. Two consciences pass into one: namely, that of him who confesses into that of him to whom he confesses, and that of him who judges into that of the same man who is judged. This unity the Son of God prays for with the Father, saying: *That they also [0627B] may be one in us, as thou, Father, in me, and I in thee [n: (Joan. XVII, 21)]*. God and man come together in the same judgment, because he who judges himself already acts together with God [n: (Aug. tract. 12, in Joan. sub finem)]. Whence the thief also on the cross, when he had said: *We indeed receive things worthy of our deeds [n: (Luc. XXIII, 41)]* — because he judged himself together with the Lord, he is with the Lord in paradise.

In these sacred things they are darkened who in any way knowingly cause any suspicion to arise publicly concerning those who have confessed to them, or who defame those who have confessed; and those sin who in confession betray anyone, or a partner in their own crime; and those [ *f.* confessione; *vel sic:* peccant, et illi in confessionem, qui facta, etc.] who in confession search out the deeds of others. For since confession holds the figure of baptism, [0627C] we know that we are baptized in the renunciation of our own sin, not in the disparagement of another. They are reckoned also as sharing in Judas, the betrayer of the Lord; because, although he who is disparaged does not, by the fact that he has sinned, signify the person of the Lord, yet by the fact that he is betrayed he can signify him. For the Lord, although he did not sin, was nevertheless betrayed, of whom Judas said: *Whomsoever I shall kiss, that same is he; hold him fast [n: (Marc. XIV, 44)]*; and he to whom, with the appearance [ *al.,* imaginem] of familiarity, he offers or has offered as it were a kiss, he asks to be held fast for his crime in confession.. And the Apostle John: Everyone *who committeth sin is* born *of the devil, because the devil sinneth from the beginning [n: (I Joan. III, V. 8)]*; and this sin we understand to be the violation of charity, because from the [0627D] beginning the devil sins [ *al.,* perdidit]. But it is exceedingly horrible that he who ought to have been reborn of the Holy Spirit through a pure confession is reborn of the devil in the disparaging of another, through the violation of charity. And the Scripture: *The just man is at the beginning the accuser of himself [n: (Prov. XVIII, 17)]* — of himself, not of another. And likewise: *Confess your sins one to another [n: (Jac. V, 16)]* — your own, not another's.

Wherein let us call to memory that Judas hanged himself alone with a noose; for he who disparages another in confession harms himself alone, since he who is disparaged is neither made public, nor can in any way be brought under any suspicion whatever, so long as the sacraments of confession are kept unbroken. Whence, in the sacred canons and in ecclesiastical cases, we speak openly and bear witness only concerning those things which we have heard in open treatment and see to be handled [0628A] in effect. And in the Old Testament the goring ox is commanded to be shut up [n: (Exod. XXI, 29)]; for if, not being shut up, it kills someone in the field of publication — that is, brings him under the suspicion of a mortal crime — then its owner (namely, he to whom the man entrusted himself through confession), because he did not shut it up, that is, because he brings in any way into a public case some sin spoken in confession, or anyone simply named by the one who disparages him, is commanded to die the death. For the revealer of a confession is guilty of a mortal crime; for he lies against faith, he does not observe the sacraments of confession, he sins against the unity of the Father and the Son, he blasphemes against the Holy Spirit, who is the true remission, and with the Jews he says to the Lord: *Do we not say well that [0628B] thou art a Samaritan, and hast a devil? [n: (Joan. VIII, 48.)]* He lies against faith when he does not keep what was entrusted to the faith of his Christianity; concerning which blessed Augustine says in Homily 10 on the Epistle of John: « They who do not believe, that is, who have not faith, are worse than the demons, and duller. »

He violates the sacraments of confession, because he does not believe that the penitent has been baptized in confession; and he destroys, so far as in him lies, that second baptism, that is, confession; nor does he guard the unity of conscience, nor does he stand in awe that God has met with him in his judgments. He sins against the unity of the Father and the Son when by any publication he divides the unity which is between God the Father and the Son, represented in the man confessing to him. For since the Son says to the Father: [0628C] *And may they also be one in us, as thou, Father, in me, and I in thee [n: (Joan. XVII, 21)]* — he denies either that God is one, or that he himself is in him who is one, or that the Almighty is able to work unity in him. But since God is one, and the members of Christ come together into one, it is assuredly plain that, while by the violation of so great a sacrament he cuts himself off from the unity of the Church, which is the body of Christ, he is neither in God, nor of God, nor with God. He blasphemes against the Holy Spirit when he reveals what was entrusted to him in the faith and in the invocation of him who is the remission of sins, showing thereby that with him not even the Holy Spirit is an advocate. In Solomon also *381* he is called fraudulent who reveals secrets, and faithful who conceals what is committed to his mind *(Prov. [0628D] XI, 13)*. And in Ecclesiasticus: *He that discloseth the secrets of a friend loseth his credit [n: (Eccli. XXVII, 17)]* — which is the origin and foundation of all the virtues — and he runs into despair, which is the blasphemy against the Spirit, and the last and greatest of all crimes. With the Jews he says to the Lord: *Do we not say well that thou art a Samaritan, and hast a devil? [n: (Joan. VIII, 48.)]* By "devil" sin can be understood, as the evangelist witnesses, who says of Mary Magdalene that seven devils were cast out; and the Lord is said to be a Samaritan and to have a devil when the fault of one who has confessed is brought up against him again; because he who is a Samaritan — that is, a keeper through confession — reproaches the man whom he keeps with having a devil, through his own sharing in the sin.

But if he who confesses persists in his fault, and [0629A] even is anathema, let him to whom the confession was made bear with his Judas, together with the Lord, even to the end; nor let him ever [aliquam omnino vel] exact any vengeance, by himself or by another, upon those who have been denounced to him as being in a crime, whether through the malice or through the simplicity of those confessing, lest the sacraments of confession be violated. For there are some who do not think that they obtain pardon unless they have disclosed the names of their associates.

And there are others who most studiously search out in confession the deeds of others — which seems to us a thing most vicious, because, according to Pope Gregory [n: (in Job, l. v, c. 3, vers. 40)], curiosity is a great vice. Now as to those who either betray others in confession, or in any way bring what has been confessed into a public case, or inquire of those confessing after the crimes of their associates [0629B] — rarely indeed, and not according to the rigor of the Scriptures, do the ears endure to hear how horrible these things are: let them shudder at them.
