## On Keeping Confession Secret (cont.)

Yet, however little has been said, let us see to whom confession is then to be made. Those first of all of whom we have spoken are to be avoided, since, if the first Epistle of blessed John be consulted [n: (cap. II, 2)], in all the sacraments they are of the manners of Antichrist. These being passed over: in the Old Testament leprosy is shown to the priests *(Levit, XIII, 49)*; the Levites carry the tabernacle of the covenant, set it up, and take it down. They set a boundary for the tents of the people away from the tabernacle of the covenant, lest they approach the tabernacle without discretion and die [n: (Num. I, 50, 53)]. The tabernacle of the covenant is the body of the Mediator of God and men, from which [0629C] the Levites set a boundary for the people, when they teach for what sins a man ought to abstain from the table of the Lord. Of subdeacons it is said: *Be ye clean, you that carry the vessels of the Lord [n: (Isai. LII, 11)]*. The vessels signify hearts; whence in the Gospel: *But the wise took oil in their vessels with the lamps [n: (Matth. XXV, 4)]*. And so subdeacons wash the vessels of the Lord when they cleanse consciences; acolytes kindle the lights of the Church. It is the office of exorcists to drive demons out of possessed bodies; of readers, to deliver the word of God; of doorkeepers, to open the church. And if all these things be diligently considered, they are done visibly to this end: that by those who do them they may be done invisibly, through the cleansing of consciences. Whence blessed Augustine, in Tractate 6 on the Epistle of St. John the Apostle, thus speaks of [0629D] Baptism: « The water of the sacrament is visible, the water of the Spirit invisible; the former washes the body, and signifies what is done [ *al.,* quod sit] in the soul. » For visible sacraments both effect and signify [ *al.,* signant] invisible things. By this we know that concerning hidden things we ought to confess to every ecclesiastical order; but concerning open things it belongs to priests alone, through whom the Church both looses and binds what she publicly knows.

But if you do not find even among the ecclesiastical orders one to whom you may confess, let a clean man be sought out, wherever he may be, as is commanded in the Old Testament: *This is the law of a man that dieth in a tent. All that enter [0630A] into his tent, and all the vessels that are there, shall be defiled seven days. The vessel that hath no cover, nor binding over it, shall be unclean. If any man in the field touch the corpse of a man that was slain, or that died of himself, or his bone, or his grave, he shall be unclean seven days. They shall take of the ashes of the burning and of the sin, and shall pour living waters upon them into a vessel; and when a clean man hath dipped hyssop therein, he shall sprinkle with it all the tent, and all its furniture, and the men that are defiled with such a contagion; and in this manner the clean man shall purify the unclean on the third and on the seventh day [n: (Num. XIX, c. 14-19)]*. It would be long to run through the mysteries of the allegories one by one. But it must be diligently considered what this means, that without the determining of any order a clean man [0630B] is said to purify the unclean; and we read of certain of the holy Fathers who governed souls, and yet we do not know their orders.

But if no one is found to whom you may confess, do not despair, since in this the judgments of the Fathers agree — that you should confess to the Lord. Whence blessed John Chrysostom on Psalm 50 [n: (homil. 2, ad hunc versum: Et peccatum meum contra me est semper, ante medium)]: « If you are ashamed to tell anyone the things wherein [ *al.,* quia] you have sinned, say them daily in your soul. I do not say that you should confess to your fellow servant, that he may reproach you; but confess to God, who will heal them. » Likewise the same, on the Epistle to the Hebrews [n: (super cap. 12 hom. 31, post medium)]: « I do not tell you to betray yourself in public, nor to accuse yourself before others; but I wish you [0630C] to obey the Prophet who says: » *Reveal thy way to the Lord [n: (Psal. XXXVI, 5)]*, *382* and then at last hope that you can obtain mercy. And in the last seven Conferences John Cassian [n: (collat. 20, cap. 8)]: « If, drawn back by shame, you blush to reveal your sins before men, cease not to confess them with unceasing supplication to him from whom they cannot lie hidden — who is wont both to heal without the publishing of any shame, and to forgive sins without reproach. Maximus says in an Easter sermon: « I find that Peter wept; I do not find what he said: I read of his tears, I do not read of his satisfaction. » And Leo, the tongue of the Holy Spirit: « That offense is washed away with tears which it is shameful to confess with the voice. For the merciful [0630D] Lord looked upon Peter even without any confession, and pardoned him the guilt of his threefold denial. »

But it must be known above all by the lesser priests, and most of all by prelates, that they must not punish in public, under the name of another fault, a fault told in secret. Whence in the line of truth — namely, in the evangelical seal — when the Son of God had said: *That they may be one in us, as thou, Father, in me, and I in thee [n: (Joan. XVII, 21)]* — not without a great mystery did he keep silent the name of the Holy Spirit, although the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one. For the unity of him who confesses ought not to be brought before a third person, nor ought this purity to be defamed under the name of another fault, [0631A] but committed secretly to the Holy Spirit.

I forbear to speak only of those things which have been confessed, since the Gospel says concerning those things which we see and suffer: *If thy brother shall offend against thee, rebuke him between thee and him alone [n: (Matth. XVIII, 15)]*. Of which sentence blessed Augustine speaks thus in his words on the Gospel [n: (Serm. 16 de verb. Domini, c. 4, tom. X)]: « Rebuke thy brother between thee and him alone, looking [ *al,* intendens] to his correction, sparing his shame; for perhaps out of embarrassment he will begin to defend his fault, or his sin; and you who wish to make a correction [ *al.,* quem vis facere correctiorem] make him worse. Rebuke him, therefore, between thee and him alone. » Likewise the same [n: (ibid.)]: « You know that he has sinned; for it was secret [0631B] when he sinned against you; seek secrecy when you correct what he has sinned: for if you alone know that he has sinned against you, and you wish to correct him before all men, you are not a corrector but a betrayer. « Likewise the same [n: (ibid.)]: « Those things are to be rebuked before men which are sinned before men; those things are to be rebuked more secretly which are sinned more secretly: distinguish the occasions, and the Scripture is in accord. So we act [ *al.,* agamus], and so it must be done, not only when men sin against us, but when anyone sins in such a way that it is not known by another. » [n: (Ibid. 8.)] « In secret we ought to rebuke, in secret to reprove, lest, wishing to reprove publicly, we betray the man. We wish to rebuke and to correct; but what if an enemy is seeking to hear, and to seize upon [quod puniat] something to punish? For the bishop knows some [0631C] murderer or other, and no one else knows him. I would rebuke him publicly, but you seek to indict him: I neither betray him at all, nor neglect him. I rebuke in secret, I set the judgment of God before his eyes, I terrify his bloodstained conscience, I urge penitence. With this charity [0632A] we ought to be endowed. Whence men sometimes find fault with us, as though we did not rebuke: either they think that we know what we do not know, or they think that we are silent about what we know. But perhaps what you say [ *al.,* scis] I too know; yet I do not rebuke him in your presence, because I wish to heal, not to accuse. There are men who are adulterers in their own houses; they sin in secret; sometimes they are betrayed to us by their wives, for the most part out of jealousy, sometimes by wives seeking their husbands' salvation. We do not betray them openly, but reprove them in secret; where the evil happened, there let the evil die. Yet we do not neglect that wound, showing above all to the man set in such a sin, and bearing a wounded conscience, that that wound is deadly. » Blessed Jerome speaks thus on Matthew *(in cap. 18, [0632B] in medio, ad illa verba: Si peccaverit in te, etc.)*: « The brother must be rebuked apart, lest, if once he has lost shame and modesty, he remain in his sin. »

From these things it is sufficiently plain that, as for the things which we see and suffer, we can — saving the Lord's precept — keep them secret only between ourselves and our sinning brethren. If they are willing to be corrected, and we nevertheless by every means uncover what has been confessed, then, the Holy Spirit being witness, we are accounted antichrists and betrayers. Concerning whom Pope Felix [n: (FEL. 1, ep. 1, in medio tom. I Conciliorum editionis regiae)]: « Let neither the voice nor the accusation of betrayers be heard. »

But that the things which have been confessed ought not to be punished under the name of another fault, nor things seen in secret to be uncovered, Pope Innocent thus says: « Let hidden sins not [0632C] have punishment; and, proof being wanting, let the reckoning of vengeance be at rest. » In all these things it is plain that mother Church is one with her Bridegroom, and brings forth her members not unto division but unto unity, world without end. Amen.
