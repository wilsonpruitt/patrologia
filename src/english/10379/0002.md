## 14.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions.* [1368B] Truly, he in whom Christ does not lie down wanders, because the mind, which that sweet and weighty Spirit of Christ does not fill, is carried about, dissolved into many thoughts. ⟦s2⟧ But who are the companions of Christ, on whose account the Bride so dreads to wander after their flocks, if not certain false Christians? ⟦s3⟧ Who are called brothers of Christ either merely on account of the name, or even on account of a shared communion of the sacraments, because they are joined, by dwelling together in the Church, to the elect whom he himself has called brothers. ⟦s4⟧ Whose multitudes are rightly called, by way of reproach, flocks, because, while they live without order like irrational animals, they are handed over to seducing spirits to be devoured. ⟦s5⟧ Through flocks of this kind wanders whoever, driven out from inward desire, imitates the folly of the reprobate [1368C] with like desires. ⟦s6⟧ For while the elect suffer these grievously, being proved through their persecutions, they are led, made purer, to the eternal goods. ⟦s7⟧ Whence the Bride is admonished, when it is said by way of reproof:

## 15.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 7. ⟦s2⟧ *If thou know not thyself, O fairest among women, go forth, and go by the footsteps of the flocks.* ⟦s3⟧ If the Bride does not know herself, let her go forth and depart, because whoever does not know that he is placed in this life so that he may overcome temptations through endurance withdraws from familiarity with Christ, because, though placed in warfare, he refuses to fight alongside his Lord. ⟦s4⟧ For it must be known by all that when we are in temptation we are commanded to fight against the powers of the air, because we cannot be crowned without victory. ⟦s5⟧ Even if the adversary [1368D] is absent, and there is no one for us to conquer, the crown too will be absent, which we shall receive only by conquering. ⟦s6⟧ Whoever does not know this is in a state of war and yet exults as though in peace, and is wounded all the more often the more secure he feels, because he does not know how to see the one against whom he must be on guard. ⟦s7⟧ Rightly, then, is he cut off from the fighters, that he may follow the footsteps of the flocks, because, while he neglects to imitate the prudent, he necessarily follows the reprobate, whom folly has made like brute animals.

## 16.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And feed thy kids beside the tents of the shepherds.* ⟦s2⟧ By the kids, unclean thoughts are designated; by the tents of the shepherds, the conventicles of heretics are expressed. ⟦s3⟧ Whoever, then, does not know how to fight [1369A] feeds his kids beside the tents of the shepherds, because whoever does not resist manfully with the swords that oppose temptation nourishes unclean thoughts in his heart like wanton kids, and becomes like the man who even falls away from the faith. ⟦s4⟧ But how the soul who longs to be made the Bride of Christ ought to conduct herself, Christ teaches her, when he straightway tells her by way of comparison:

## 18.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 9. ⟦s2⟧ *Thy cheeks are beautiful, as the turtledove's.* ⟦s3⟧ Once the turtledove has lost her mate, she never joins herself to another, but always dwells alone and perseveres in mourning, because, not finding him whom she loved, she seeks him. ⟦s4⟧ So every holy soul, while she is absent from her Bridegroom, does not withdraw from love of him, but always sighs and yearns after desire of him. ⟦s5⟧ And since she does not find him whom she greatly loves — because she withdraws herself from every strange love — she shows the chastity of her heart, as it were in the modesty of her cheeks, by that very outward habit and [1369C] bearing. ⟦s6⟧ It follows:

## 19.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thy neck is like jewels.* ⟦s2⟧ By the neck of the Bride, the preachers of the holy Church are designated. ⟦s3⟧ Preachers are compared to jewels, either because they are an ornament to the Church, or because they defend the Church, as a necklace defends the breast — or from its roundness, that is, its perfection. ⟦s4⟧ In necklaces, gems are set in gold. ⟦s5⟧ For by gold wisdom is expressed, and by the stones, ornaments. ⟦s6⟧ The neck of the Bride, then, is like jewels, because every upright preacher of the Church both clothes himself inwardly with wisdom, and outwardly carries out the works which he has seen in wisdom. ⟦s7⟧ To whom it is also further said:

## 20.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 10. ⟦s2⟧ *We will make thee chains of gold, inlaid with silver.* ⟦s3⟧ The moray is a fish which, when caught, turns itself into a circle, after whose likeness is made the earring which is called a *murenula*, by which is designated preaching, which clings to the ears and penetrates them. ⟦s4⟧ By these little chains the jewels are bound to the neck, because both wisdom and religion are joined to preachers from the Holy Scriptures. ⟦s5⟧ For by the little chains Holy Scripture is understood. ⟦s6⟧ Which is rightly said to be golden and inlaid with silver, because Holy Scripture both shines with wisdom, and is heard through resounding preaching throughout the whole world. ⟦s7⟧ For as wisdom is shown by gold, so holy preaching is shown by silver. ⟦s8⟧ To these words of the Bridegroom the Bride replies, and through bodily images of things she makes known [1370B] the love with which she burns spiritually within, saying:

## 21.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 11. ⟦s2⟧ *While the king was at his repose, my nard gave forth its odor.* ⟦s3⟧ Nard is a lowly herb which, when tasted, drives away cold, and warms the limb. ⟦s4⟧ The king entered his repose then, when our Lord Jesus Christ, in the body, penetrated the inward parts of heaven. ⟦s5⟧ While he rests there, the Bride's nard gave forth its odor, because the power of the holy Church scattered far and wide the sweet fame of her goodness. ⟦s6⟧ For the Lord ascended into the heavens, and sent his Holy Spirit upon the disciples, that, filled with him, they might preach the words of salvation to the world, and spread abroad on every side, through holy works, the fame of a good odor. ⟦s7⟧ Every faithful soul, indeed, receives this Spirit in baptism, [1370C] that through him she may compound for herself ointments from the making of virtues, and refresh her neighbors, as it were with the odor of ointment, by the showing of example. ⟦s8⟧ It follows:
