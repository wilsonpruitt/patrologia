## On the Beginnings and Founding of the Sacred Order of Jerusalem (cont.)

Yet another great miracle came to pass, namely, that Accamanus, the Roman emperor, with certain other honorable men, for the sake of receiving homage from the Jews — it happened, when they were at sea near a certain island of Crete which is called Rhochas, that the stern of their ship was broken, and all who were left aboard were lost, except Julian the Roman, who was carried to land by the Son of God [1102A] kindly. And when Julian saw the disaster which had happened, he asked the Lord: « Who are you who have drawn me out of this peril? » And our Lord answered and said: « I am the Son of God, who have ordained you Hospitaller of the house of the poor of Jerusalem, and in time to come I shall come bodily into that house; » and he blessed him. That Julian received with joy the blessing of our Lord, and came to Jerusalem to holy Zacharias, where Zacharias himself and the other brothers received him as a father. Thereafter, when our Lord came upon earth for the salvation of the human race, and willed to manifest his glory to the people, he called his apostles and disciples, and came to Jerusalem, as he had foretold through the Holy [1102B] Spirit to the providers of the dwelling of the Hospital. And, being at that time a bodily man, he worked a miracle in the presence of his disciples, and by the apostles he was working the salvation of souls. God, who did great good things to this house, in that he himself willed to show himself to them, stretched out his hands over his apostles and over the poor of the aforesaid dwelling; and then he said: « He who honors you will honor me, and he who receives you receives me. Cursed be the man who despises one of them. » We make it known that this is the dwelling in which our Lord said to that wise man: « You shall love God with your whole heart, and with your whole mind, » and many other things according as is found in the Gospel. Likewise this is the house in which the apostles hid themselves and shut the doors, [1102C] for fear of the Jews, when our Lord Jesus Christ had been crucified. Into this holy house the Virgin Mary and St. John the Evangelist came to our Lord, when he was upon the cross for us sinners, and he said to St. John: « Behold here your mother. » And afterwards he said to his mother: « Behold here your son. »

This, further, is that house of the Hospital into which Jesus came on the eighth day of his resurrection, the doors being shut, and entered into the midst of his disciples, and said to them: « Peace be to you. » And he showed them his hands and his breast; but Thomas was not with them when Jesus came. And the disciples came to St. Thomas and said to him: « We have seen our Lord. » [1102D] And Thomas answered and said: « I do not believe. I do not touch, and I see the hole of the nails of his hands and of his feet, and I shall put my hand into his breast. » Near these eight days, as St. John the Evangelist testifies, when the disciples were in this holy house, and St. Thomas with them, Jesus came, the doors being shut, and said: « Peace be to you. » And he said to Thomas: « Put your finger here into the hole of my hands and of my side, and be not unbelieving, but faithful. » And in that holy house St. Thomas acknowledged his Creator. To whom our Lord said: « Because you have seen me, you have believed. Blessed are they who have not seen and have believed. »

At the same time Ananias and Sapphira left [1103A] the world, and gave themselves to the fellowship of Christ and of the disciples; and because they did not render to God and to the sacred house of the Hospital and to the poor, and did not carry to the feet of the disciples what they had, they died a sudden death; and St. Peter did not permit their bodies to be laid in the cemetery, but rather had them buried outside, as excommunicate. And in the time when God sent us his apostles and disciples to preach, and they were of the ancient sort — such as St. Stephen, who was the first martyr, and Philip the protomartyr, Nichauome, Thymocenn Permanan, Nicholas of Antioch. These kept the houses of the Hospital and served the poor by the command of our Lord Jesus Christ. In that time there were in the same place many and diverse races [1103B] of men — sometimes Greeks, sometimes Caryseni, who held the city of Jerusalem and the land of promise; and this notwithstanding that the said house was in great poverty, yet they received the poor and the sick. And when it came about finally that the Saracens had the house of Jerusalem, and the Greeks lost it, one little servant of Jesus Christ, who was called Conrad, kept the holy house of the Hospital; and the alms which were given him by the Saracens he gave to the poor, and served them kindly.

At that same time, when it pleased God that the Christians should have the land of promise, Godfrey of Bouillon and a great multitude of barons and pilgrims, who had been instructed by divine grace [1103C] and by preaching, came through long lands, over mountains and valleys, and through the land of Romania, to Antioch; and they besieged it, and by the power of God took it. In it they destroyed a great multitude of the faithless Saracens. From there they went on to the city of Tripoli, and took it. And thence they took Acre; and from there they went to the land of Jerusalem. And in their army they had so great a famine that they had almost no bread to eat. Then that Conrad, who served the poor of the Hospital of Jerusalem, three or four times a day would put bread in his lap, and from above the walls of the land would throw the bread down to the Christians in place of stones; and this Conrad, who kept the poor of the Hospital, was accused before the Sultan [1104A] by the Saracens who guarded the walls. And the Sultan commanded the Saracens who had accused him that, when he should throw the bread down to the Christians, they should seize him and bring him to him, with all the bread, and otherwise he did not believe him. Then came a day when Conrad — or Gerard — was doing as he was accustomed, throwing bread down to the Christians; and the Saracens seized him, with the bread which he had in his lap, and led him before the Sultan; and when the Saracens showed the bread to the Sultan, then that bread, by the power of God, was made stone. And when the Sultan saw the malice of the Saracens, he let Conrad go, and commanded him that without fear he should fight the Christians, as he had been accustomed. And when Conrad, or Gerard, heard this, every day he threw bread down to the Christians of God.

[1104B]And Godfrey of Bouillon stood so long with his army before Jerusalem that he took the city and killed many Saracens, and he had great joy at the victory obtained, which God had given them; and they came to the holy sepulchre of our Lord, singing and rendering thanks to God. Then they went to all the other places. And thereafter the barons divided the possessions of Jerusalem, and gave to the said Conrad — or Gerard — master of the said Hospital of Jerusalem, a great part of the possessions, in honor of St. John the Baptist, patron of that same Hospital. Then, in the time of the said master Conrad, or Gerard, God greatly multiplied the said dwelling of the Hospital in revenues, possessions, and lordships throughout the kingdom of Jerusalem. [1104C] And when Conrad was dead, his soul went into paradise. And brother Raymond du Puy was appointed to succeed him in his place; and to the praise of God he built farmsteads, dwellings, and castles; and greatly improved the dwelling with great possessions.

That brother Raymond made the ordinances of the dwelling of the Hospital, which are kept in that same house even now; and the Rule; and he had it confirmed by Pope Innocent II, etc.

May God, the Creator of all things, deliver the brothers of the Hospital from all evils and from their sins, and grant them the grace of coming to heavenly glory with all their benefactors. Amen.
