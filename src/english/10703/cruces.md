# Cruces — 10703, Odo of Cambrai, *Homilia de villico iniquitatis*

Garbled or corrupt printed readings, rendered literally in the English and logged here.

## Chunk 0

- **1122D** — Anchor sequence anomaly, not a textual crux: the column following [1121D] is printed as **1122D**, skipping 1122A–C. Reproduced verbatim per the marker rule, but a human should confirm against the plate whether this is a Migne typo for 1122A.
- **1123B** — *melior est finis orationis quam principium [n: (Eccl. XVII, 9)]*. The verse is Ecclesiastes 7:8, and the Vulgate reads *finis orationis* ("of the speech/discourse") — Odo builds his argument on the "prayer/discourse" ambiguity. Rendered "the end of prayer is better than the beginning" as printed; the citation *Eccl. XVII, 9* is wrong (chapter XVII for VII) but passes through verbatim per the marker rule. Cf. the same quotation at 1126C in chunk 1, where Migne cites it correctly as *Eccles. VII, 9*.
- **1124D** — *Si quidem per speciem saepe genus subintelligitur*: "the genus is often understood through the species." The illustration Odo gives runs the other way (wheat, a species, standing for bodily goods generally), so *genus* and *species* appear transposed. Rendered as printed.

## Chunk 1

- **1126B** — *Oro iniquitate vidi tentoria Aethiopiae*. *Oro* is a misprint for *Pro*: Odo's own exposition in the next clause reads *pro divitiis captandis*, and he repeats the lemma correctly at 1126C as *pro iniquitate tentoria Aethiopiae*. Rendered with the intended reading ("for iniquity").
- **1126D** — *Hostiae impiorum abominabiles [n: (Prov. II, 2)]*. The verse is Proverbs 21:27; the printed citation *Prov. II, 2* is wrong. Citation passes through verbatim per the marker rule.
- **1127B → 1128A** — Anchor sequence gap: columns 1127C and 1127D are not marked, the text running straight from [1127B] to [1128A] mid-sentence. Reproduced as printed; worth a check against the plate for dropped text.
