## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « How fair thou art, my love, how fair thou art! thy eyes are of doves, besides that which lies hidden within. ⟦s2⟧ Thy hair is as a flock of goats that have gone up from Mount Gilead. ⟦s3⟧ Thy teeth as a flock of shorn sheep that have gone up from the washing. ⟦s4⟧ All with twin offspring, and there is none barren among them. ⟦s5⟧ As [1205D] a scarlet ribbon are thy lips, and thy speech is sweet. ⟦s6⟧ As a piece of a pomegranate, so are thy cheeks, besides that which lies hidden within. ⟦s7⟧ As the tower of David is thy neck, which is built with bulwarks. ⟦s8⟧ A thousand shields hang from it, all the armor of valiant men. ⟦s9⟧ Thy two breasts are as two twin fawns of a roe, which feed among the lilies until the day break, and the shadows decline. »

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *How fair thou art.* The voice of the bridegroom, considering the affection of that gentile Church, which would wish to draw to itself as friends any whom it could, commends her on that account. ⟦s3⟧ The eyes, that is, thy overseers who diligently gaze upon the Scriptures, are dove-like, that is, gazing with simplicity, and shining with every inner simplicity: [1206A] dove-like eyes, I say, *besides that which lies hidden within,* that is, apart from the intention, which is far more fair. ⟦s4⟧ *Thy hair,* that is, those cleaving close to the head, and subtle and slender like true poor men, are *as a flock of goats.* ⟦s5⟧ A flock, on account of the multitude: they are called goats because they both seek the heights and have the other virtues — goats, as we said before; *which go up from the mountain,* that is, from the eminence of the virtues, striving toward perfection. ⟦s6⟧ The mountain, I say, of *Gilead,* that is, where there is the heap of testimony; for the saints do not keep their virtues in silence, but bring forth in manifold ways the testimony of Christ. ⟦s7⟧ Gilead is called *the heap of testimony* for this reason, that Jacob and Laban his uncle there built a heap of stones, as a testimony of their peace. ⟦s8⟧ *Thy teeth,* namely those that are set in the jaws [1206B] and by which food is ground, are those saints who grind the Scriptures and render them intelligible to the lesser ones; and such are they *as flocks of shorn sheep:* flocks, because many; shorn, for this reason, that just as a sheep in shearing lays aside its old growth, so these have laid aside all their oldness, *which go up from the washing.* ⟦s9⟧ The saints indeed go up to the virtues from the washing of baptism, and all, according to their greater part — the eyes as well as the head and the teeth — are begetting sons to God from either people, namely the Jewish and the gentile. ⟦s10⟧ *All* are *with twin offspring,* because each one has faith and works. ⟦s11⟧ *As a scarlet ribbon are thy lips.* ⟦s12⟧ The office of a ribbon is to bind the hair about the head; so the lips, that is, the preachers through whom God speaks, bind the hair to Christ, [1206C] lest it flow away from the head. ⟦s13⟧ He calls it scarlet on account of the redness of the Lord's passion. ⟦s14⟧ Or by the hair may be understood the thoughts which depend from the mind and adorn it. ⟦s15⟧ For these thoughts are held in check by the preaching of the divine word, and are bound about the mind. ⟦s16⟧ *Thy speech,* that is, thy teaching, *sweet,* nourishing like milk. ⟦s17⟧ *As a piece of a pomegranate.* ⟦s18⟧ The pomegranate is red on the outside, but when it is broken it appears white within: *so are thy cheeks,* that is, those more perfect saints are within like the teeth, on the outside they are worn down by many persecutions, and are vile to the world. ⟦s19⟧ But within they are found, when known, white with many virtues, *besides that which lies hidden within,* that is, the intention, which is far more fair. ⟦s20⟧ *As the tower of David is thy neck,* that is, the saints, through whom the [1206D] Scriptures come down to the lesser ones, have also the other offices of the neck; and they are as *a tower,* that is, a defense of the others who flee to them, in which the true David dwells and does battle; *which* tower *is built with bulwarks,* that is, with firm and excellent judgments, with which they drive far off the assaulting enemies. ⟦s21⟧ *A thousand shields* are those lingering about the neck; those have the bulwarks; but those going forth to wars receive from those who are the neck all the judgments with which they may protect themselves, and this she says by the thousand shields; and they receive also other judgments with which they may vanquish others and draw them to themselves: and this is what she adds, *all the armor of valiant men. ⟦s22⟧ Thy two breasts,* that is, thy preachers, in whom is contained the holy teaching from the gentile and Jewish [1207A] people; or even the peoples themselves who are nourished by the teaching, are *as two twin fawns of a roe,* that is, the fawns have the virtues that the roe has, and they are twins, that is, like one another, and agreeing in the same ordinance. ⟦s23⟧ Or we may understand by the two breasts the two Testaments, which now are not said to disagree with one another; or it may be said of all the aforesaid. ⟦s24⟧ *They feed,* that is, are refreshed and delighted, *among the lilies,* that is, in the whiteness and sweet odor of good works. ⟦s25⟧ Meanwhile, I say, *until the day break,* namely eternal blessedness, *and the shadows decline,* that is, until these ignorances pass away.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « I will go to the mountain of myrrh, and to the hills of frankincense. ⟦s2⟧ Thou art all fair, my love, and there is no spot in thee. ⟦s3⟧ Come from Lebanon, my bride, come from Lebanon, [1207B] come, thou shalt be crowned, from the top of Amana, from the summit of Sanir and Hermon, from the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards. ⟦s4⟧ Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, my bride, thou hast wounded my heart with one of thine eyes and with one hair of thy neck. ⟦s5⟧ How fair are thy breasts, my sister, my bride. ⟦s6⟧ Thy breasts are fairer than wine, and the odor of thy ointments above all spices. ⟦s7⟧ Thy lips, my bride, are a dropping honeycomb, honey and milk are under thy tongue, and the odor of thy garments as the odor of frankincense. ⟦s8⟧ A garden enclosed, my sister, my bride, a garden enclosed, a fountain sealed. ⟦s9⟧ Thy shoots are a paradise of pomegranates, with the fruits of apples, of camphire with spikenard, spikenard and saffron, the sweet cane and cinnamon with all the trees of Lebanon, myrrh and aloes with [1207C] all the chief ointments. ⟦s10⟧ A fountain of gardens, a well of living waters, which flow with a rush from Lebanon. ⟦s11⟧ Arise, north wind, and come, south wind, blow through my garden, and the spices thereof shall flow. »
