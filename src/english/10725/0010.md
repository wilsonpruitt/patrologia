## Expositions on the Song of Songs (cont.)

[1217A]*Thou art fair.* The voice of the Bridegroom. Christ, hearing the affection of the Church, therefore commends her when he says: *Thou art fair, my love,* etc. He calls her fair according to this, that she has so great a will to know him; sweet because she invites others to the same, not begrudging that her Bridegroom has other loves; he calls her comely according to her works. *As Jerusalem,* that is, as fair as the heavenly Jerusalem, and comely as thy mother, namely that Church which begot thee and reared thee, *terrible* to enemies, *as an army set in array,* because I do battle in thee, and thou dost battle in my service, as one wars in a camp, and thou art *set in array* through the institution of the saints. *Turn away thine eyes.* Thou art so praiseworthy, but nevertheless cease to think what thou now believest, that thou canst attain to the perfect knowledge [1217B] of me; and this he says, *before thine eyes,* that is, the understanding which thou exercisest toward the full knowledge of me, or those very eyes gazing more subtly, *because they, to fly away,* that is, to be separated from all human knowledge, *have made me,* namely because the more subtly and loftily one searches out God, the more lofty and incomprehensible he knows him to be. Now, because he said: Turn away thine eyes, lest she should complain that she is more unworthy than her mother, that is, Jerusalem, he commends her as he did the mother above, when he says: *thy hair,* etc. I said: Turn away thine eyes, and yet thou hast eyes, etc., equally fair as that Church which preached thee. He did not say that she should turn away her eyes because it is a sin to wish to know God fully, but it is a sin to think one can attain it. *There are threescore queens.* Queens or wives are all those in the holy Church who [1217C] beget sons to God out of the love of God alone, not from hope of worldly gain; but concubines are those who preach not out of the love of God, but for the sake of temporal good, and yet beget good sons to God. The concubines, however, are cast out of the house of the head of the household, but the sons are kept. The maidens are those who are begotten. And this he says because of these beauties; thou hast both queens and the rest, who are said to be threescore. By threescore, which is made up of ten and six, namely those perfect in the ten precepts of the law according to the six works of mercy. By fourscore, which is made up of eight and ten, we have those who, although they keep and preach the ten precepts somewhat, nevertheless are engaged in worldly affairs. But the number eighty, when it is taken in a [1217D] bad sense, designates the cares and entanglements of temporal things, because both the course of the world is turned about through four seasons, and the world itself is divided into four regions — east, west, north, and south. Concubines, therefore, are those who announce Christ out of love of temporal things. *My dove is one.* Since he names so many members of the Church, someone might suppose that discord and never union is found in the Church, and therefore, removing this, he says: Although I have named so many parts of the Church, she herself is nevertheless one, that is, united in faith, and she is one with her mother, that is, she is as much one as her mother, that is, that Church which nourished and reared this one with sweet doctrine. And she is *the one of her that bore her,* that is, like her who bore her, and like those preachers who converted her, [1218A] and he takes "mother" as regards instruction, "she that bore her" as regards conversion; and because she is such, as has been shown through all that has been said above, therefore whatever other *daughters saw her* admired her, *and the queens and concubines proclaimed* her *most blessed* on account of the glory to come, and *the queens and concubines praised her* for her virtues in the present life.

« Who is she that goeth forth like the rising dawn, fair as the moon, chosen as the sun, terrible as an army set in array? »

*Who is she.* The voice of the Jews, who, seeing so great an advance of the Churches among the Gentiles, beginning to be moved to compunction, marveling, not envying, say: *Who is she,* that is, of what sort and how great, *who goeth forth [1218B] like the dawn,* that is, is spread abroad through the earth, like the *rising* dawn, in whom are some *as the moon,* that is, enlightening those in darkness; some *as the sun,* that is, enlightening even certain bright ones, and there are some in her terrible to enemies, some are *an army of the camp,* namely fortified with the rampart of faith, and set in order with many virtues.

« I went down into the garden of nuts to see the apples of the valleys, and to look whether the vineyard had flourished, and the pomegranates had budded. I knew not: my soul troubled me for the chariots of Aminadab. Return, return, O Shulamite; return, return, that we may behold thee. »

*I went down.* The voice of the Bridegroom, so that he thus answers the Jews: You ask who she is? She indeed is my love and the garden into which I went down. *Of nuts,* for this reason: because [1218C] in a nut there is bitterness on the outside, within a certain fragile shell, and inside is the sweet kernel; in the same way the saints endure on the outside many bitternesses within the body, which is the fragile shell; but inside indeed is what is most sweet, which is the soul adorned with many virtues. I went down, I say, *to see,* that is, to test and to bestow help through grace on those who are *apples,* that is, offering ripe works to God, and are valleys, that is, humble; *and to look.* For God to look is to bestow grace and to test the faithful, *whether the vineyards,* that is, these on whom great cultivation is spent, *had flourished,* that is, had put forth the beginnings of virtues, *and* whether *the pomegranates,* that is, those who show themselves ready for any suffering, *had budded,* that is, had gained some [1218D] fruit for God. *I knew not.* The voice of the Jews stricken with compunction. Thou sayest that this is thy love, and lookest upon her by bestowing on her spiritual gifts; but I indeed knew not, because *my soul,* that is, the knowledge which I had in the law according to the carnal understanding, *troubled me,* that is, rendered me troubled away from the knowledge of God, *for the chariots of Aminadab,* that is, on account of the four Gospels of Christ which seem to me to destroy the law which I had received before. Aminadab is interpreted *the willing one of the Lord,* or *for the people,* by which is signified Christ, who of his own will offered himself to God, and this for the redemption of the people. By the chariots which are borne on four wheels, we understand the Gospel which is carried through the whole world by the four evangelists. [1219A]*Return, return.* The voice of the Church from the Gentiles. She indeed recognizes the error of the Jews, and admonishes them to conversion. And she sets down *return* four times, because she calls them back from the four parts of the world. He calls her Shulamite, that is, taken captive by the devil into Babylon, that is, into the confusion of vices. There was indeed a certain Shulamite taken captive in Babylon. *Return,* I say, *that we may behold thee,* that is, that we may see our life in thee, or that we may marvel at thee for thy exceeding goodness.

« What wilt thou see in the Shulamite, but the choirs of the camps? How beautiful are thy steps in shoes, O prince's daughter! The joints of thy thighs are like jewels that are wrought by the hand of a craftsman. Thy navel is like a turned bowl, never wanting [1219B] cups. Thy belly is like a heap of wheat, set about with lilies. Thy two breasts are like two young twin fawns of a roe, thy neck like a tower of ivory. Thine eyes like the fishpools in Heshbon, which are in the gate of the daughter of the multitude. Thy nose is like the tower of Lebanon, that looketh toward Damascus. Thy head is like Carmel, and the hairs of thy head like the king's purple bound in the channels. How fair art thou, and how comely, dearest one, in delights! Thy stature is likened to a palm tree, and thy breasts to clusters of grapes. »

*What wilt thou see.* The voice of Christ, and he admonishes the Church of the Gentiles to labor for the conversion of the Jews, because their labor and preaching will not be useless, as if he should say: Thou askest her to return, from which one must not cease, because thou wilt see *in the Shulamite,* that is, in the [1219C] Jews of the captivity by the devil, *choirs,* that is, those singing and chanting psalms to the Lord, choirs, I say, *of the camps,* that is, in whom God does battle, and who are well fortified. *How beautiful!* Again the Church from the Gentiles is admonished to the preaching of the Jews for another reason, namely, because it will be very useful, and this last Church, in which will be the converted Jews, will bear great fruit for God. Yet it is not said that all the Jews will at last be converted, but the greater part. Therefore labor, thou Church from the Gentiles, for the conversion of the Jews, because those *thy steps* will be very *beautiful,* that is, the last faithful who will carry thee and thy doctrine, beautiful, I say, *in shoes,* that is, in the mortification of the flesh, because shoes are made from dead animals, or in shoes, that is, in the apostles and other saints [1219D] by whose examples and authority the last will be protected, as feet by shoes. *O prince's daughter,* that is, of God. Or this may here be the voice of Christ to the Jews themselves by apostrophe. *The joints of the thighs,* that is, the joining together of the two peoples, *like jewels,* because they both fortify and admonish others, so that, considering their beauty and the spiritual gifts which flourish in them, they may wish to cleave to them; and they fortify their subjects against the snares of the devil, and teach them chastity. Jewels, I say, *wrought* by the power and working of the highest *craftsman,* that is, God himself.
