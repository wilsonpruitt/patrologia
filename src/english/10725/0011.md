## Expositions on the Song of Songs (cont.)

*Thy navel.* In this part of the belly a woman has the seedbed of generation, and by this part, which is in the belly, we have those who, though they are among the carnal, [1220A] as the married, nevertheless have the seedbed of preaching and of begetting sons to God. And such as these are *a bowl,* that is, giving drink to and inebriating others with the love of God. A bowl, I say, *turned,* that is, so smoothed according to the will of God that the hand of God may find no roughness there, which bowl *never* wants *cups,* because to saints of this kind the doctrine with which they may give drink to others will never fail. Or holy preachers of this kind are called a navel for this reason: because, as the navel is in the middle of the belly, so these are in the middle of the people, preaching and reconciling them to God. *Thy belly,* that is, that softer part of the Church, who do not beget others, *like a heap of wheat set about with lilies.* He says a heap, because there are many such in the Church. He says of wheat, because such are also the sweet and delightful food [1220B] of God, and they are set about, that is, well fortified, *with lilies,* that is, in virtues. *Two breasts,* that is, preachers, who are breasts, namely vessels of milk, that is, of the doctrine that sweetly nourishes. Such as these are *fawns,* because light and nimble for running, not weighed down by the burden of the flesh; he says *twins,* because, as twin brothers, though they are different, yet are like one another, so these two peoples of the Jews and of the Gentiles will be like one another, because they will both follow and teach the same instruction of the law; *of a roe,* because they will have the virtues of a roe. *Thy neck,* that is, those who in the last Church will have the office of the neck, will be *like a tower,* because those who flee to them will find defense, he says *of ivory* on account of chastity. *Thine eyes,* that is, those holy foreseers who subtly gaze into the depth of the Scriptures, [1220C] are *like fishpools,* that is, waters refreshing with the doctrine of the word of God all who thirst. *In Heshbon,* which is interpreted *the girdle of sorrow,* are those saints who are eyes and are called waters, and in Heshbon, because they are always girded with sorrow both for their own and for others' offenses, which fishpools, that is, doctrines, are *in the gate,* that is, ready for the refreshment *of the daughter of the multitude,* that is, of many sons. For they are, as it were, in the gate, because they are ready for all who wish to approach, to refresh them with spiritual water. *Thy nose.* In the nose is contained the discernment of a good and a bad smell, and by this are signified those who have the discernment of the good and the wicked, so that they may distinguish who are of good will, and instruct them, and who are dissemblers and deceitful, and reject them. Such as these are *the tower of Lebanon,* a tower, because [1220D] they are a timely stronghold for those who flee, of Lebanon, because they are made white with virtues. Or a tower, because as a tower is a strong stronghold, so the saints in their discernment are not overcome, because they are strong, just as in their choosing they are not deceived, so as to judge good to be evil, or evil good, *that looketh toward Damascus.* Damascus was indeed always opposed to Jerusalem, and they mutually drew away one another's citizens, and Damascus is interpreted *a drink of blood,* by which are signified bloodthirsty persecutors who bring in persecution or rejoice in the evils of others, against whom the aforesaid saints look, so as to draw away some citizens from them, and bring them over into their own body. *Thy head,* that is, thy mind, or the higher ones in the Church, are *like [1221A] Carmel,* that is, having the knowledge of circumcision. For they understand, by the foreskin cut off, where the seat of lust is, and through which all generation follows, that all the old generation is to be cut off from us, namely the putting away of all filth, which will at last be made perfect in the common resurrection. Carmel is interpreted *the knowledge of circumcision.* *And the hairs of the head,* that is, those who cleave most closely to the head of the Church, namely the true poor who cleave most closely to Christ, are also, as it were, the adornment of the head itself. Such as these are *like the king's purple,* that is, imitators of the passion of Christ, who is the true king, purple, I say, *bound in the channels,* that is, that their suffering be joined to and depending from the channels, that is, from their thoughts and hearts, namely, that when they set themselves forth as imitators of the passion of Christ, [1221B] this may not be according to outward things only, but also according to inward things. Those vessels in which purples are dyed are called channels, and as purple, if it cleave well to the channel, is dyed better, so these are better fitted to the passion of Christ, if through outward and inward things they show themselves compassionate. *How fair.* Why go through each one? *Thou art all fair,* according to the inner virtues, *and comely* according to works, *in delights,* that is, in every spiritual refreshment. *Thy stature,* that is, *thy* uprightness, like a palm tree. For as the palm is always borne on high, so these faithful ones always strive toward the height of virtues, *and thy breasts,* that is, thy doctrines, are like *clusters of grapes,* that is, wine; for as wine does, so thy doctrines inebriate.

« I said: I will go up into the palm tree, and will take hold of its fruits. And thy breasts shall be like the clusters of the vine, and the smell of thy mouth like the smell of apples. Thy throat like the best wine, worthy for my beloved to drink, and for his lips and teeth to ruminate. I am my beloved's, and his turning is toward me. Come, my beloved, let us go forth into the field, let us abide in the villages. Let us rise up early to the vineyards, let us see if the vine hath flourished, if the flowers bring forth fruits, if the pomegranates have flourished. There will I give thee my breasts. The mandrakes have given their smell in our gates. All the fruits, new and old, my beloved, I have kept for thee. »

*I said: I will go up.* He sets down another reason why the Church from the Gentiles ought to preach to the Jews, as if he should say thus: Thou oughtest to preach, because thy preaching [1221D] will be useful, and many will be converted, both because when converted they will be such and so worthy, as has been said, and because I myself came properly to redeem them, whence it is said, Matthew 15: « I am not sent but to the sheep that are lost of the house of Israel, » and this he says, *I said,* that is, from eternity I disposed, *I will go up into the palm tree,* that is, onto the cross, in which a certain part is said to have been from that tree, or into the palm because there he obtained the victory over the devil, and triumphed over the winning of the world. I said I will go up *and will take hold of its fruits,* that is, they will beget sons for me, and will bring forth good works. Now the Bridegroom himself turns to her, when he says, *and thy breasts,* that is, thy doctrines, *like the clusters* not of the ivy, but *of the vine,* that is, both sweet to drink, [1222A] and inebriating some who diligently hear them, *and thy smells,* that is, the fame and devotion of thine own, *like* the smells *of apples;* for as apples by their smell attract some, so these also by their fame attract some to themselves and associate them. Those last Jews will indeed be very good, as the Apostle shows, who says, Romans 2: « And if the diminishing of them is the riches of the Gentiles, how much more their fullness? » For although not all will be converted, yet the greater part of them will be converted. *The throat,* that is, the inner words, namely the meaning of thy words, is *like the best wine,* that is, nourishing and inebriating men, so that they make them forget their whole former life. *Worthy for the beloved.* The voice of the Church, and she commends those words which are even so worthy that Christ received them from God the Father, [1222B] so that it is said thus: I call thy throat like the best wine, and so best that it is *worthy for my beloved,* that is, for Christ, *to drink,* that is, that Christ may drink them, that is, without labor and with delight, *for the lips,* that is, the preachers, *and the teeth,* that is, the stronger ones, who grind the Scriptures, worthy *to be ruminated,* that is, worthy that the lips and teeth should ruminate, namely grind and labor to understand. *I am my beloved's,* as if she should say: I am to be believed, because I am always present to him as a familiar friend from the knowledge of his secrets, and he loves me, and this he says: *His turning is toward me,* that is, he turns his love toward me. *Come, my beloved.* Now the Church, having heard the admonition of Christ, and being ready to preach to the Jews, invites the Bridegroom to be with her in that preaching, bestowing help upon her, as if she should say: Thou hast commanded me to preach to the Jews, which will profit nothing without thy help, and therefore, *my beloved, come,* that is, breathe upon them inwardly, *and let us go forth,* that is, let us step beyond the Gentiles *into the field,* that is, to the preaching of those who without great labor bear fruit, and *let us abide in the villages,* that is, in the building up and ordering of them. *Let us rise up early,* that is, let us labor zealously; for those who are zealous rise early to their works. By the vineyards we understand those on whom labor is spent before they yield fruit, and *let us see if the vineyards,* that is, those cultivated with such labor, *had flourished,* that is, had put forth the beginnings of virtues, *if the flowers,* that is, those in whom the beginnings of virtues already appear, bring forth *fruit,* that is, ripe works, and let us see *if the pomegranates,* [1222D] that is, the future martyrs, had yet *flourished.* I said: Come, my beloved, to whom indeed thou oughtest to come with me, because *there will I give thee,* that is, to thy honor, *my breasts,* that is, my doctrine, by which I will gain for thee the best sons. *The mandrakes.* I say that there I will give thee my breasts, which I shall well be able to do, because the Jews themselves have already given me a good expectation of their conversion, namely, showing themselves fit to receive the faith. Mandrakes are herbs, and they have a likeness to human limbs without a head; by which are signified the Jews lacking a head, that is, Christ. By the gates we understand faith, because it is the gate and the entrance for all to salvation. *All the fruits.* I said that I would give thee my breasts, which I shall truly do, because *I have kept for thee,* [1223A] that is, to thy honor, the sentences of the New and Old Testament, by which I will make for thee some fruits, which sentences both sweetly give off fragrance, and so sweetly attract those who hear as apples do.

« Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother, that I may find thee alone without and kiss thee, and now no one may despise me? I will take hold of thee and lead thee into the house of my mother, and into the chamber of her that bore me. There thou shalt teach me, and I will give thee a cup of spiced wine, and the new wine of my pomegranates. His left hand under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me. »
