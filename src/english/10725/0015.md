## CHAPTER VIII.

« Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother, that I may find thee alone without and kiss thee, and now no one may despise me? I will take hold of thee and lead thee into the house of my mother, and into the chamber of her that bore me. There thou shalt teach me, and I will give thee a cup of spiced wine, and the new wine of my pomegranates. His left hand under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me. »

*Who shall give me.* Here is shown the effect of the preaching of the Church, because the Jews speak, hearing that Christ [1223B] is to be announced to them, and, kindled with love of him, they burst forth into these words, *Who shall give me,* that is, would that someone might announce to me *my brother,* that is, Christ born of my flesh, *sucking the breasts of my mother,* that is, brought up in the doctrine of the Synagogue. Now out of excessive love she turns to him. Who shall give me, I say, *that I may find thee without,* that is, that I may know thee as man, in whom I have shamefully erred, *and kiss thee,* that is, that I may delight in thy humanity now acknowledged, *and now no one* shall think me contemptible, who before was a contempt to all. *I will take hold of thee,* that is, we who before had been separated from thee will now firmly cleave to thee. I will take hold, I say, and lead into the house of my mother, that is, those who now serve the Synagogue I will make to serve Christ, and *into the chamber of her that bore me,* that [1223C] is, I will make certain ones of the Synagogue such that God may lie down and rest in them without offense. *There,* that is, while I announce thee to the sons of the Synagogue, *thou shalt teach me,* because I will open my mouth, and thou wilt fill it, as thou hast promised [n: (Psal. LXXX)], *and I will give thee a cup,* that is, I will convert for thee many of the Jews, who will be to thee a cup, that is, a delightful drink and refreshment through their good works and in many virtues. Such a drink God thirsts for, as he himself said on the cross, « I thirst [n: (Joan. XIX)], » namely, I bring over the human race into my body. A cup, I say, *of spiced wine,* that is, that the cup should be wine, that is, men glowing with love of God and inebriating others with the love of God. Which wine is spiced, that is, compounded with many spices, that is, with the diverse virtues [1223D] of the saints themselves, and I will give also a cup, *new wine,* namely wine that is hot, fervent, and boiling, by which are signified the holy martyrs fervent for the love of martyrdom, who are also called pomegranates, because outwardly red on account of their suffering, inwardly white on account of their virtue. *His left hand,* and I ought well to labor to gain such for him, because he himself bestows on me solace, both through temporal things and through the hope of eternal things.

« I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, till she please. Who is she that cometh up from the desert, flowing with delights, leaning upon her beloved? Under the apple tree I raised thee up: there thy mother was corrupted, there she that bore thee was violated. Set me as a seal [1224A] upon thy heart, as a seal upon thy arm, for love is strong as death, jealousy as hard as hell. »

*I adjure you.* The voice of the Bridegroom. For Christ, hearing so great an affection of the Church of the Jews, commends her as he did the former ones, and charges those subject to him not to disturb her. *Who is she.* The voice of the Church from the Gentiles. For seeing her daughter, the Church from the Jews, come to such perfection, rejoicing, not envying, she says with admiration, *Who is she that cometh up.* Through the steps of virtues, *from the desert,* and from her unbelief, in which she was deserted by God, *flowing with delights,* that is, abounding in many virtues, *leaning upon her beloved,* that is, Christ, whom everyone walking rightly has as a foundation. *Under the apple tree.* The voice of Christ [1224B] to that Church from the Jews now converted, and he commends to them his love. For it was fitting that, since this whole book is about the love of God, at the end of its treatise it should commend love. Continuation. The Church from the Jews has leaned upon me, her beloved. *Set* therefore, thou Church from the Jews, *me upon thy heart,* that is, as ruling thy heart and thy thoughts. And otherwise, *upon,* that is, as the suppressor of all thine iniquity; set me, I say, *as a seal,* that is, that it may be clear to friends that thou dost keep my secrets, and likewise that it may be clear to enemies that thou art not of their sect, or that the secrets which thou hast are closed and unknown from them. Or a seal, namely, so set me that thou keep in thy heart the image of me, namely, that thou dispose thyself like me both in suffering and in all other things according to thy measure, and set [1224C] me *upon thy arm,* that is, thy working, that thou mayest have me as ruler in all thy works. Set me, I say, *as a seal,* that is, that through thy very works it may be clear to friends that thou art a familiar friend to me, and that the enemies of their fellowship may understand thee to be estranged from their life and manners; and that they ought to do this, the cause has been set forth beforehand, when it was said: *Under the apple tree,* as if he should say: Deservedly oughtest thou to set me upon thy heart, because I freed thee when thou wast dead in sins, and this he says, thee lying and languishing *under the tree,* that is, under the sin committed by the tree, *the apple,* that is, in the sin of the first parent. Or, languishing, under the sin of the cross, which likewise was from this tree, that is, the apple tree. For it is read in the books of the Greeks that from the same [1224D] tree in which Adam sinned, a certain branch was carried to Jerusalem, and there, being planted, it grew up into a great tree, from which afterward the cross of the Lord was made, so that from that whence death had come, by the same life might be restored. It is also said that Adam was buried in the same place in which afterward the cross of the Lord was fixed; *there,* that is, in that tree, *the mother was corrupted and violated,* that is, the Synagogue. By "corrupted" he marks the penalty of sin in whomever it be, in the sentence by which from the crucifixion of the Lord the Jews had punishment, namely that destruction which Titus and Vespasian brought upon them. When he says "violated," he marks the eternal damnation which from that sin all who did not repent received. This verse is also continued otherwise thus: The Church has called me her beloved, and deservedly oughtest thou to love me, because [1225A] I freed thee from the sin of the first parent. *For it is strong.* That is, therefore oughtest thou to set me upon thy heart, because my love is strong as *death.* For as death cannot be hindered from coming, so the love of God cannot be hindered by any persecution from working salvation, as Paul deduces [n: (Rom. VIII)]. Or as death destroys the bodily senses, so does the love of God. For he who is well occupied with the love of God no longer draws any delight of the flesh from sight, nor from the other senses. Or as death, that is, the devil himself, who is the cause of all death. For as the devil, in enmity, confers damnation upon his own, so the love of God confers salvation. *Jealousy,* that is, the disposition of love, namely to accomplish those things which love demands — these are a care and unbearable to the flesh; *as hell,* [1225B] that is, that jealousy is insatiable. For to no one in whom love abounds does it seem enough to give his own or to do the other things which love demands; nay, he would always wish to do more.

« The lamps thereof are lamps of fire and of flames. Many waters could not quench charity, neither shall the floods overwhelm it. If a man should give all the substance of his house for love, he shall despise it as nothing. »

*The lamps thereof.* Again he describes the nature of that love, as if he should say: You ought to hasten to that love, because *its lamps,* that is, the ardors of this love, are like *lamps of fire,* that is, the ardors of the Holy Spirit, and this is what he says, *of fire.* For as fire supplies heat, so the Holy Spirit [1225C] supplies the fervor of love, and because there could be a fire that did not shine, he says *and of flames,* that is, the lamps of love are such that they also shine, namely, those who have these loves shine to others, because they enlighten others by word and example. We can also by lamps understand those men in whom love dwells as fire in a lamp. *Many waters.* Again he shows another nature of love, that we too may wish to embrace it, so that it is said thus: Such is love. But this love neither threats nor persecutions, small or great, will be able to quench. By water we understand threats and small tribulations of this kind, because they quench human loves. By floods we have greater adversities. *If a man should [1225D] give.* Again he commends this love, that he may strongly invite us to preserve it, when he says that this is such that, if anyone give for it *all his substance,* that is, his whole possession, and himself besides, he despises it, that is, he will judge it contemptible in comparison with what he will obtain.

« Our sister is little, and hath no breasts. What shall we do to our sister in the day when she is to be spoken to? If she is a wall, let us build upon it silver bulwarks. If she is a door, let us join it together with boards of cedar. »

*Our sister,* says the Bridegroom. The law of love is so heavy; but the sister, that is, the Church from the Jews, who is called the sister of Christ, because she is both of the same flesh and a coheir of the kingdom with Christ, as she is newly converted, [1226A] is *little,* still tender in faith, *and hath* not *breasts,* that is, doctrine, of herself, and therefore, unless we furnish her help, she will not be able to bear the law of love. *What shall we do?* The Bridegroom says to his friends: Because our sister is still little, we must bestow help upon her, and *what shall we do to her in the day when she is to be spoken to?* In this present time in which she is to be instructed and spoken to, so that after this life she may be found worthy to be joined to Christ in marriage. What shall we do, I say this, namely: *if she is a wall,* that is, if there are any in this Church who are a wall and a defense of others. *Let us build upon it silver bulwarks,* that is, sentences by which they may know how to defend themselves and to assail others, and these *silver,* that is, ringing with the composition of eloquence, namely let us make them [1226B] wise and eloquent. *If she is a door,* that is, if there are any in this Church who bring others in, *let us join it together,* that is, let us add something that may be to their honor, let us join it together, I say, *with boards,* that is, let us add such as are boards, that is, subtle and well endowed for the will of Christ, that these such may be a help to those who are a door. With boards, I say, *of cedar,* that is, let such be added as are of cedar, that is, incorruptible, and by their smell killing worms.

« I am a wall, and my breasts are like a tower, since I am become before him as one finding peace. The Peaceable One had a vineyard in that which hath peoples. He delivered it to keepers: a man brings for its fruit a thousand pieces of silver. My vineyard is before me. A thousand are for thee, O Peaceable One, and two hundred for them that keep its fruits [1226C]. »
