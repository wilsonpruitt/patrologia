## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « They have set me a keeper in the vineyards. » Who, then, sets keepers in the vineyards, except to ward off thieves? ⟦s2⟧ God planted one vineyard, and because it was turned into bitterness, many other vineyards were planted. ⟦s3⟧ Whence also that saying: « And they sowed fields, and planted vineyards [n: (Ps. CVI, 37)]. » In these the apostles were set as keepers, lest all they that pass by the way should gather her vintage. ⟦s4⟧ « My vineyard I have not kept. » One vineyard, she says, I have left, but many [1238C] vineyards I have kept. ⟦s5⟧ And the many vineyards are the many churches. ⟦s6⟧ But the synagogue is one. ⟦s7⟧ And the apostles left her, as it is written: « To you it behooved us first to speak the word of God; but because you have rejected it, and have judged yourselves unworthy of eternal life, behold, we turn to the Gentiles [n: (Act. XIII, 46)]. » This can also be read as a question. ⟦s8⟧ Have I not kept my vineyard? ⟦s9⟧ I have indeed kept it. ⟦s10⟧ And in that he set the vineyard first in the plural and then in the singular, he figured the unity of the Church, which, however much it be divided according to the character of places, is nevertheless one in faith and love. ⟦s11⟧ And the apostles and the doctors kept it, lest Arius, Sabellius, and the other thieves should craftily steal away some cluster, or separate any of the faithful from the Church. ⟦s12⟧ The voice of the Church [1238D] to Christ: « Show me, O thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest down at midday. » Show me, she says, make me to know and firmly to understand, O shepherd, O ruler, whom with my whole mind and with all my strength [1239A] I have loved and do love; show me where thou feedest thy flock, in what place, and with what food thou nourishest thy sheep. ⟦s13⟧ For God's sheep are fed in the assembly of preaching and in the sacred volumes. ⟦s14⟧ And this has been shown also to those bishops who, being dumb dogs unable to bark, suffer God's animals to perish of hunger. ⟦s15⟧ « Where thou liest down at midday. » What is meant by midday, if not the too greatly burning affliction of the saints? ⟦s16⟧ Say, she says, say, O shepherd, where thou liest down at midday, where thou art when the saints are afflicted, where thou art when thy lambs are torn in pieces by wolves? ⟦s17⟧ Art thou not he who, as a good shepherd, layest down thy life for the sheep? ⟦s18⟧ The heat is heavy, the sheep thirst, they are burned with the warmth. ⟦s19⟧ Lead them to the harbor, lead them to the shade. ⟦s20⟧ I know; thou wilt say: I am in thee, in thee I lie down and rest. ⟦s21⟧ « For upon whom doth my spirit [1239B] rest, but upon the humble, and the quiet, and him that trembleth at my words? » [n: (Isa, LVI, 2.)] And likewise: « Know you not that you are the temple of God, and that the Holy Spirit dwelleth in you? » [n: (Cor. III, 16.)] Why then am I troubled? ⟦s22⟧ I know, thou wilt say, because through many tribulations we must enter into the kingdoms of heaven. ⟦s23⟧ The answer is true; now I fear nothing, since so great a guest guards the city. ⟦s24⟧ I do not shudder at the heat, since the living fountain is with me. ⟦s25⟧ There follows: « Lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions. » What is meant by the companions, if not heretics and philosophers? ⟦s26⟧ But why companions? ⟦s27⟧ Because they too are shepherds, and feed a flock. ⟦s28⟧ And through their flocks and their conventicles those men wander who know not the way of the true pasture [1239C] and of the divine meadow. ⟦s29⟧ Let us ask our Lord, therefore, both to show us the sheepfold, and by his mercy to set us in a place of pasture.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Likewise verses: Why shouldst thou read me so many prophets, O royal Bridegroom?
⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Let the envoys cease, who are ready only to give words.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Now have mercy on me, now suffer thyself to be seen.
⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And now let it be thy care to give kisses to thy bride,
⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And to our ears the strains of thy sweet voice.
⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Now let it sound forth, I beg thee, if there be any love of thine toward me.


⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The voice of Christ to the Church: « If thou know not thyself, O most beautiful among women, go forth. » If thou, he says, most beautiful of women, fair of form, knowest not thyself; if, forgetful of thyself, unmindful of thy nobility, go forth, depart, *608* [1239D] and wander, as thou hast said, after the tracks of the flocks. ⟦s2⟧ Follow the heretics, imitate the philosophers. ⟦s3⟧ For we know not our own selves, when, forgetful of our dignity, of our baptism, and of our newness, we are made subject to error and to vanity, and follow the tracks of the flocks of [1240A] [sic: *caparrum*] and of he-goats.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And feed thy kids beside the tabernacles of the shepherds. » if thou, he says, shalt not have known thyself, and shalt have departed from me, thy lambs will soon become kids. ⟦s2⟧ For just as in baptism roes are turned into lambs, so, by sinning and departing from God, we turn pure souls and most gentle lambs into butting and stinking he-goats. ⟦s3⟧ And these kids, beside the tabernacles of the shepherds and the conventicles of the heretics, having scorned the sweet food of the Scriptures, are fed with poisonous grass, that is, they drink in depraved teachings. ⟦s4⟧ The voice of Christ: « To my cavalry among the chariots of Pharao have I likened thee, my friend. » Neither ought thou, he says, to leave me nor to forget thyself. ⟦s5⟧ For I have likened thee to my cavalry among the chariots of Pharao And the [sic: *Eeclesia*] is likened to it for this reason, namely, [1240B] that the chariots and horsemen of Pharao pursued the people of Israel. ⟦s6⟧ But as Pharao, pursuing the aforesaid people with chariots and horsemen, was drowned in the sea, so also the devil, lying in wait with a multitude of vices for the souls of men, perishes in baptism. ⟦s7⟧ Having therefore received so great a benefit, let no one forget himself or God. ⟦s8⟧ The young maidens to the bride:

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Thy cheeks are beautiful as the turtledove's. » But when she had heard these words, as it were of rebuke, on account of the words spoken above, she drew upon herself the blush of shame. ⟦s2⟧ For she had said, « lest I begin to wander. » For wandering belongs to harlots. ⟦s3⟧ Recalling therefore this word and this rashness, she blushed. ⟦s4⟧ But the Bridegroom, when he saw this, [1240C] praised her virginal modesty. ⟦s5⟧ For he says: « Thy cheeks are beautiful, » — thy face is bashful and chaste. ⟦s6⟧ Thou blushest to speak, or to have done, or even to have thought base things. ⟦s7⟧ For thou art not of those women who take their strength from crime. ⟦s8⟧ But how beautiful? ⟦s9⟧ As the turtledove's, as a virgin's, as of one who keeps great chastity and conjugal faith. ⟦s10⟧ For the Church serves no husband but one. ⟦s11⟧ « Thy neck as necklaces. » As the other virgins, he says, delight in necklaces, so thou delightest in the beauty of thy neck alone. ⟦s12⟧ What is meant by the neck, if not the doctors? ⟦s13⟧ For through the neck food is sent down into the belly, and out of it words also come forth. ⟦s14⟧ But the Church is nourished and instructed by the doctors. ⟦s15⟧ The office of the neck, therefore, is fulfilled by the doctors. ⟦s16⟧ Moreover, the weakness of the neck is the ruin of the whole body. ⟦s17⟧ The voice of the friends: « Golden [1240D] *murenulae* will we make thee. » *Murenulae* indeed are ornaments of a virgin's neck, so named from the fish *murena*. ⟦s18⟧ And they signify the discourses of the prophets, lengthy, and [sic: *dictinctos*], and praiseworthy. ⟦s19⟧ *Murenulae* [1241A] were made for us by Moses, Isaias, Jeremias, David, and by him indeed who wrote these things, Ecclesiastes. ⟦s20⟧ « Inlaid with silver. » They are *murenulae* indeed, because they are slippery; but golden, because they shine forth with the light of wisdom; and they are said to be inlaid with silver, because they are marked out by the purity of eloquence.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1241B]The voice of the bride: « While the king was at his repose, my nard gave forth its odor. » While my king, she says, my bridegroom, my beloved, was at his repose, upon the little bed of the flesh, in the humanity which he took from a virginal womb, « my nard gave forth its odor. » And this can be understood according to the letter of Mary, who poured out the alabaster box of ointment upon the head of him as he reclined. ⟦s2⟧ For straightway the whole house was filled with the odor of the ointment. ⟦s3⟧ But spiritually it is understood of the Church. ⟦s4⟧ For before our Savior had come in man, the nard gave forth its odor in few, since the virtues and prayers of few were pleasing to the Lord. ⟦s5⟧ But after he came in the flesh, and the Lord went forth as a bridegroom out of his chamber, [1241C] straightway the virtues and prayers of the saints gave forth their odor. ⟦s6⟧ Whence the Apostle: « We are, he says, a good odor unto God [n: (II Cor. II, 15)]. » And the Psalmist: « Let my prayer be directed as incense in thy sight [n: (Ps. CXL)]. » Hence it is that John saw golden vials full of odors [n: (Apoc. V, 8)]. ⟦s7⟧ For after the incarnation of Christ both the virtue of the saints and their number increased. ⟦s8⟧ The nard therefore gave *609* forth its odor to Christ the Lord when he was made flesh. ⟦s9⟧ « A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me. » He set myrrh for bitterness. ⟦s10⟧ For Christ was to the Church a bundle of myrrh, and indeed a gathering of great grief and great bitterness. ⟦s11⟧ For how could she be otherwise than in grief and bitterness, widowed of so great and such a husband? ⟦s12⟧ So too it can [1241D] be said: « My beloved is to me. » For my sake and for my profit was he made a bundle of myrrh, since he submitted himself for me to death and to the bitterness of the passion. ⟦s13⟧ Was he not carrying a bundle of myrrh [1242A] and of bitterness when he said: « My soul is sorrowful even unto death? ⟦s14⟧ « [n: (Matth. XXVI, 38.)] He was indeed a bundle, because he both drew many to himself and slew many with himself. ⟦s15⟧ For all the martyrs are gathered, as it were into one bundle, for his death. ⟦s16⟧ « He shall abide between my breasts. » For the excellent Bridegroom abides between the breasts of the bride, since he is not separated from her memory and her embrace. ⟦s17⟧ For the place of the heart is between the breasts. ⟦s18⟧ And where the heart is, there also is the memory. ⟦s19⟧ He therefore who abides between the breasts, since he lies in the heart, cannot escape the memory. ⟦s20⟧ Happy is that soul, and exceedingly happy, between whose breasts Christ abides, in whose heart he lies, and who never forgets him. ⟦s21⟧ « My beloved is to me a cluster of Cyprus. » For the wine of the island of Cyprus is the best [1242B]. ⟦s22⟧ And wine gladdens the heart of man. ⟦s23⟧ And Christ is to his saints a cluster of Cyprus, since they are gladdened by the draught of his blood. ⟦s24⟧ And in short, if wine signifies gladness, then he who had been a bundle of myrrh in dying is rightly called a cluster of Cyprus in rising again. ⟦s25⟧ Whence the Psalmist: « In the evening, he says, weeping shall tarry, and in the morning gladness [n: (Psal. XXIX, 6)]. » He is therefore a cluster of Cyprus. ⟦s26⟧ Where? ⟦s27⟧ There follows: « In the vineyards of Engaddi. » Engaddi is interpreted *the fountain of the kid*. ⟦s28⟧ And the fountain of the kid is in the churches. ⟦s29⟧ And the churches are the vineyards of Engaddi. ⟦s30⟧ For the fountain of baptism is in the Church, and he who enters it is a kid, but he who comes out is a lamb.
