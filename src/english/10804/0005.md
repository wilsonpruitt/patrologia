## CHAPTER III.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The voice of the Church: « In my little bed by night I sought him whom my soul loveth; I sought him, and found him not. » Of old, says the Church, in my little bed, in my heart and in the leisure of searching, I sought my Lord, my bridegroom, whom my soul loves above all. ⟦s2⟧ But by night, without faith, in darkness, in human wisdom, in blindness of mind. ⟦s3⟧ And therefore I found him not. ⟦s4⟧ For this the heretics, this the philosophers sought; but seeking by night and trusting in their own wisdom [1250D] they found him not. ⟦s5⟧ Whence the Lord himself says: « I confess to thee, O Father, Lord of heaven and earth, because thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, [1251A] and hast revealed them to little ones [n: (Matth. XI, 25)]. » -- « I will rise, and will go about the city. » I will rise, says the Church. ⟦s6⟧ But what Church? ⟦s7⟧ The Church of the Gentiles, the people that sat in darkness and was given over to sleep, which had not yet said: « O God, my God, to thee do I watch at break of day [n: (Psal. LXII, 1)]. » I will rise, she says, I will sleep no more; *614* I will go about the city, that is, the congregation of the saints and of the apostles, that they may teach me concerning God those things which I could not know by myself. ⟦s8⟧ « Through the streets and the broad ways, and in all places I will seek him whom my soul loveth; I sought him, and found him not. » But this was by night, this in my little bed. ⟦s9⟧ I will seek him therefore by day, that I may find him « The watchmen who go about the city found me. » I arose, she says, and went about the aforesaid city. [1251B] And while I was doing this, the watchmen who guard the city found me. ⟦s10⟧ The city is the Church, and the watchmen are the apostles. ⟦s11⟧ And these find those who seek God by faith and by baptism. ⟦s12⟧ For in this the care of the guardians is set about her, who not she them. but they also are said to have found her. ⟦s13⟧ And when these were found, I said (the Church speaks of Christ): « Have you seen him whom my soul loveth? » And this is as if she should say: You who have the knowledge of God, show me God himself and the way of truth. ⟦s14⟧ « When I had a little passed by them, I found him whom the soul loveth. » When, she says, by hearing and understanding I had passed by them, and when I ran through their admonitions and their preaching (for by this manner of speech we are wont to say, I run through a book), I found, by their showing, [1251C] him whom my soul loveth. ⟦s15⟧ « I held him, nor will I let him go. » I found him, she says, and sweetly embraced him. ⟦s16⟧ « I held him. » How? ⟦s16⟧ In mind, and in work, and in perfect faith: « nor will I let him go, » but with most burning love I will cleave to him. ⟦s17⟧ « Till I bring him into my mother's house, » that is, into the dwelling of this earthen vessel, which he himself formed with his own hands, the earth being its parent. ⟦s18⟧ For the saints are the dwelling of God. ⟦s19⟧ Whence he himself says: « I will dwell in them and walk among them [n: (II Cor. VI, 16)]. » -- « And into the chamber of her that bore me. » That is, into the secret place of my heart. ⟦s20⟧ And we may understand by this mother also the Synagogue, from whose house Christ went out, from the time when he said: « Your house shall be left to you desolate [n: (Matth. XXIII, 38)] » [1251D] -- « But when the fulness of the Gentiles shall have come in, then shall Israel be saved [n: (Rom. XV, 25)]; » then will he bring the Church into the house of her that bore her. ⟦s21⟧ « My beloved to me, and I to him. » For he does not indeed tarry either here or there, but dwells with the saints, until both the day breathe and the end of the shadows come.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verses: To whom I said: Be thou like to a roe,
⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Or upon the mountains of Bether to a little hart,
⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1252A]In my little bed through the nights I sought him;
⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But since through the nights I found him not,
⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ I arose to go about the city.
⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ While I was passing again through the streets and the broad ways,
⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The watchmen of the gates found me.
⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Where my beloved was, they told me.
⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ I found him therefore, and I will not let him go
⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Until I bring him into the little bed of her that bore me,
⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ That is, into the secret matter of this vessel,
⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Or into the last people of the Jews.


⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The Bridegroom to faithful souls: « I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, » and the rest. ⟦s2⟧ By the roes, he says, and by the harts, by the apostles and the doctors, by virtue and faith and good work, by which you both run to heaven and to the promised prize [1252B] and overcome the venom of serpents; you, daughters of Jerusalem, you who have the knowledge of God, I adjure and beseech, that you rouse not my beloved, my bride, nor part her from that work in which she delights. ⟦s3⟧ For one delights in fasting, another in vigils, another in giving alms, another in building churches. ⟦s4⟧ To sunder these from so great a sleep, from this delight, from this purpose of their own soul, is a great sin. ⟦s5⟧ For the graces are diverse, the wills are diverse, and diverse too are the works by which we can serve the Lord. ⟦s6⟧ Let each one therefore abound in his own sense. ⟦s7⟧ The Synagogue concerning the Church: « Who is she that goeth up by the desert. » Who, she says — of what sort, how beautiful, how wonderful is she, who mounts up to me by hastening [1252C] from virtue to virtue? ⟦s8⟧ Of what virtue, I say, and of what daring is she, who comes to me through the desert? ⟦s9⟧ For through the desert and through the adversities of this world the Church passed over to the Lord, fearing not the ambushes of robbers, nor the swords of tyrants, nor the teeth of lions, nor the sophisms of Jews and heretics. ⟦s10⟧ But how did she pass? ⟦s11⟧ « As a little rod of smoke made of spices, namely of myrrh and of frankincense, and of all the powder of the perfumer. » As if she should say: So am I delighted in her [sic: *inhujus*] coming, as in the odor and sweetness of all perfumes. ⟦s12⟧ By the myrrh and the frankincense which are here named we understand the mortification of the flesh and the prayers of the saints.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Who ascends as it were smoke,
⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Whom the earth has not weighed down.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The narrow way she found,
⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But she passed through the desert
⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Bearing a pleasing spice.


⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The voice of the Church: « Behold, threescore valiant ones of the most valiant of Israel surround the little bed of Solomon. » [1253A] For Solomon is interpreted *peaceable*. ⟦s2⟧ « And he is our peace, who hath made both one [n: (Ephes. II, 14)] » By the little bed of Solomon, therefore, we understand *615* the Church, whom Christ has joined to himself with devoted love, not having spot or wrinkle. ⟦s3⟧ Of whom he says through the Prophet: « This is my rest for ever and ever; here will I dwell, for I have chosen it [n: (Psal. CXXXI, 14)]. » And threescore valiant ones surround her; the apostles and the doctors guard her, lest perchance the Jews, the heretics, and the false apostles, adulterating the word of God, should defile the bed of Solomon and the conscience of the saints. ⟦s4⟧ But why are they said to be threescore, if not because the number sixty is made up of six and of ten? ⟦s5⟧ For six times ten, or ten times six, make sixty. ⟦s6⟧ And [1253B] since God finished his work in six days, we rightly understand by six the perfection of works. ⟦s7⟧ And by ten we signify the ten precepts of the law. ⟦s8⟧ Those therefore who guard the little bed of Solomon, that is, holy Church, ought to be both perfect in works and wise in the teachings and institutions of the law. ⟦s9⟧ And these are said to be not only valiant, but of the most valiant of Israel, since the doctors of the Church must be stronger than the rest. ⟦s10⟧ « All holding swords. » Whence also the Psalmist: « And two-edged swords in their hands [n: (Psal. CXLIX, 6)]. » And the Apostle: « And the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God [n: (Ephes. VI, 17)]. » Let the bishops therefore hold swords, with which they may both destroy vices and defend the Church. ⟦s11⟧ « And most expert in war. » Whence is that saying: « Blessed [1253C] be the Lord my God, who teacheth my hands to fight, and my fingers to war [n: (Psal. CXLIII, 1)]. » For they are most expert in battle, since by suffering, not by defending themselves, they conquered in humility and patience. ⟦s12⟧ « Every man's sword upon his thigh. » For they have swords upon their thigh who drive back the assaults of lust with the bridle of chastity. ⟦s13⟧ Whence the Apostle: « I chastise, he says, my body, and bring it into subjection [n: (I Cor. IX, 27)]. » -- « Because of fears in the night. » The doctors, he says, are armed and girt with swords, lest the heretics and the malignant spirits should by night, by guile and in secret, invade the bed of Solomon, that is, holy Church. ⟦s14⟧ The Church. ⟦s15⟧ « King Solomon hath made himself a *ferculum* of the woods of Libanus. » What is meant by the *ferculum*, if not the Church? ⟦s16⟧ And this *ferculum*, this refectory. [1253D] this house of sacred banqueting the Lord made of the woods of Libanus, since no one dwells in the Church who has not been made white by the water of baptism. ⟦s17⟧ For Libanus is interpreted *whitening*. ⟦s18⟧ And of this *ferculum* it is said in the Psalms: « He hath set me there in a place of pasture. [1254A] [n: (Psal. XXII, 2)]. » And those who sit at this *ferculum* must have wedding garments. ⟦s19⟧ And here Christ set forth his own flesh to us to be eaten. ⟦s20⟧ Here we eat the living bread, here the prefigured lamb and the fatted bull; here we drink the chalice of the New Testament, here we are made drunk with the divine and most sweet wine. ⟦s21⟧ She says of Christ: « He made its pillars of silver. » What is meant by the pillars, if not the apostles and the doctors? ⟦s21⟧ And why pillars, if not because they are the supports of the whole Church? ⟦s22⟧ Of these it is said in the Psalms: « I have established the pillars thereof [n: (Psal. LXXIV, 4)]. » And why of silver, if not because they are pale with the affection of simplicity, modesty, and sobriety? ⟦s23⟧ These are those feathers of the pillar which are said to be silvered over: « A resting-place of gold. » He made, she says, [1254B] a table and a resting-place of gold, since the New and the Old Testament shine forth with the light of wisdom. ⟦s24⟧ And of this table it is said: « Thou hast prepared a table before me [n: (Psal. XXII, 5)]. » Let us draw near, therefore, to this golden table, that we may be refreshed with divine and spiritual feasts. ⟦s25⟧ « An ascent of purple. ⟦s26⟧ « For no one eats the food of this table unless he ascend by the purple way. ⟦s27⟧ For the Jews do not take this food, since they have not known the ascent of purple. ⟦s28⟧ Of whom it is said: « Let their table become before them a snare [n: (Psal. LXVIII, 22)]. » For the way is purple which Christ made bloody with his own blood. ⟦s29⟧ And we may understand by the resting-place that heavenly blessedness. ⟦s30⟧ And it is called golden because it is more precious than all things; whose [1254C] rest is eternal, whose ascent is of purple. ⟦s31⟧ No one ascends thither unless, redeemed by the death of Christ, he be led by his blood. ⟦s32⟧ The martyrs also rejoice that they ascended thither by the purple way.
