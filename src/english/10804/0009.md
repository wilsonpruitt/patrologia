## CHAPTER V.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The Church says of Christ: « Let my beloved come into his garden. » Let him come, she says, my beloved, my Bridegroom, my friend, into his garden, into his Church, into the congregation of the saints. ⟦s2⟧ To what end? ⟦s3⟧ « That he may eat the fruit of his apple trees. » Let him eat, she says, the fruit of the [sic: *pomerum*]; let him receive the sheaf, [1262C] the homage, and the good works of the saints. ⟦s4⟧ The apples are mellow, the trees are shaken, the martyrs are slain. ⟦s5⟧ Let the Lord therefore command that their souls be gathered into the heavenly treasury and into Abraham's bosom. ⟦s6⟧ Christ says: « Come into my garden, my sister, my bride. » Thou askest me, he says, to come; but I have already come.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ « I have gathered my myrrh with the spices. » By the myrrh we understand the martyrs, chaste and incorrupt; but by the spices, the fragrance of good works. ⟦s3⟧ And there is myrrh with spices when, the concupiscences of the flesh being overcome, a man lives with the virtues alone. ⟦s4⟧ Then the flesh is incorrupt, then the virtues give forth their fragrance, then the Lord bids [1262D] the saints come to him. ⟦s5⟧ For when the Apostle said: « I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ [n: (Philip. I, 23)], » was he not longing that his own myrrh should be gathered? ⟦s6⟧ « I have eaten my honeycomb with my honey. » With what sweetness and delight God receives his saints is declared in these words. ⟦s7⟧ « I have eaten, he says, my honeycomb with my honey » — I have received my love, I have taken up my sweetest and exceedingly beloved friends. ⟦s8⟧ Confirmation. ⟦s9⟧ « I have drunk my wine with my milk. » The wine indeed are you, O apostles, who, made drunk [1263A] with the divine cup, had all skill. ⟦s10⟧ But the milk are those who are adorned with a middling and imperfect knowledge. ⟦s11⟧ Christ therefore came into his garden, and carried away with him, with exceeding love, the myrrh which the wind had blown through — that is, the saints whom the tyrant had afflicted. ⟦s12⟧ Christ says to the apostles: « Eat, my friends, and drink. » Apostrophe to the apostles. ⟦s13⟧ You, he says, are my friends; do you eat the doctrine of the faith and my flesh, do you drink my blood. ⟦s14⟧ « For unless you shall have eaten my flesh and drunk my blood, you shall not have life in you [n: (Joan. VI, 54)]. » Eat therefore the living bread; drink the cup of the new testament. ⟦s15⟧ « And be inebriated, my dearly beloved. » For he who is inebriated with this cup is inebriated with true knowledge. ⟦s16⟧ For he who spurns riches, desires [1263B] death, forgets himself, neglects what he sees, believes what he does not see — does he not seem to be drunk? ⟦s17⟧ « I sleep, and my heart watcheth. » He slept indeed according to the flesh, since he died and was buried; but the heart watches, because the divinity cannot sleep. ⟦s18⟧ Yet this is also understood thus in the person of the Church. ⟦s19⟧ I sleep, the flesh being overcome and subdued; but the heart watches — that is, the soul, which perseveres in good works. ⟦s20⟧ And we may understand by the heart the apostles of Christ. ⟦s21⟧ These watch, these guard the holy Church by day and by night. ⟦s22⟧ The Church: « The voice of my beloved knocking. » I hear, she says, the voice of my Lord; for he knocks at his saints, that they may not sleep, and exhorts them to watch. ⟦s23⟧ For he stands at the door and knocks. ⟦s24+25⟧ If any man open, he will come in and will sup with him. [1263C] « Open to me, my sister. » Sister indeed, because both Bridegroom and bride are children of the same Father — he by nature, but she by adoption. ⟦s26⟧ « My friend, » for whom I did not hesitate to die, than which love there is none greater. ⟦s27⟧ « My dove, » lacking gall and bitterness. ⟦s28⟧ « My undefiled, » who, cleansed from all vices, art without spot or wrinkle. ⟦s29⟧ Open to me, he says, thy heart; open to me the gate by which I may enter both thy conscience and the consciences of others: « for my head is full of dew. » Confirmation. ⟦s30⟧ « And my ringlets with the drops of the nights. » All the night, he says, I have run; for love of thee I have kept watch. ⟦s31⟧ « Behold, I stand at the door and knock [n: (Apoc. III)]. » Open to me, my sister. ⟦s32⟧ Christ indeed was in many adversities, in the night, and in death itself, for love [1263D] of her. ⟦s33⟧ Whence also that word: « Moreover, even until night my reins have chided me [n: (Psal. XV, 7)]. » It is unjust, therefore, not to open to him, and not to receive so great a Bridegroom into the bridal chamber of the heart. ⟦s34⟧ But spiritually the dew upon the head is understood both of the heavenly blessing and of the grace of the Holy Spirit in him. ⟦s35⟧ Of which it is said [1264A]: « Like the ointment upon the head, which ran down upon the beard, the beard of Aaron [n: (Psal. CXXXII, 2)]: » The voice of the faithful soul. ⟦s36⟧ The bride to herself: « I have put off my tunic; how shall I put it on? » Rightly, she says, hast thou called me undefiled, since I have put off my tunic, have fled my former life, have stripped off the old man, and have driven away the concupiscences of the world, the cares of the age, and the vices in which I was wrapped and ensnared. ⟦s37⟧ I am undefiled; and clothed with thee, the new man, I shall walk in newness of life, and I shall no more put on the aforesaid tunic, nor shall I return to secular things; but what I have begun I shall work at without ceasing. ⟦s38⟧ « I have washed my feet; how shall I defile them? » I have washed my feet, she says, and have put to flight those vices in which human frailty more frequently offends. ⟦s39⟧ For this part *621* of the body, because it is nearer [1264B] to the earth, is more often defiled. ⟦s40⟧ For there are certain sins from which scarcely anyone will be immune, as elation of mind, vainglory, idle words, and vain thoughts. ⟦s41⟧ The voice of the Church concerning Christ: « My beloved put his hand upon the opening. » My beloved, she says, put his hand — his doctrine and the spirit of his fortitude, and the very commemoration of his passion — upon the opening and the gate of my heart, which is never shut to him.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And my belly trembled at his touch. » For who is there whom the Lord has touched and inspired with the Holy Spirit — who, calling his passion to mind, would not tremble? ⟦s2⟧ Whence is that word. ⟦s3⟧ « Touch the mountains, and they shall smoke [n: (Psal. CXLIII, 5)]. » The Lord therefore puts his hand through the opening, he touches our heart; [1264C] it remembers the passion, it is gripped with wonder and fear that the Lord should suffer such things for his servants, the just for the unjust, the creator for the creature. ⟦s4⟧ « I rose up to open to my beloved. » I heard, she says, the voice; from the little bed of my delight I rose up, and forsook the blandishments of the world. ⟦s5⟧ I opened to my beloved, I believed his words, I cleansed of vices and of error my heart, in which so great a guest was to dwell. ⟦s6⟧ Whence it is said through the Psalmist: « At the hearing of the ear he hath obeyed me [n: (Psal. 17, 45)]. » -- « My hands dropped with myrrh. » What by the hands but works? ⟦s7⟧ And what by the myrrh but incorruption? ⟦s8⟧ The hands therefore drop myrrh, since good works make both body and soul incorrupt. ⟦s9⟧ « And my fingers are full of the most [1264D] approved myrrh. » For what do we understand by the fingers but the distinct performance of individual works? ⟦s10⟧ As to watch, to pray, to fast, and the like. ⟦s11⟧ And these are not undeservedly said to be full of the most approved myrrh, since they both mortify the flesh — and that not without a great recompense — and prepare an eternal incorruption. [1265A] « I opened the bolt of my door to my beloved. » My heart, she says, was greatly hardened; it had been barred with many obstacles and fastenings. ⟦s12⟧ But after I heard his voice, and conceived in my mind the sayings of truth, I opened the whole understanding of my heart, and removed all the obstacles of error by which my heart was shut as by a bolt. ⟦s13⟧ I opened indeed, that I might receive him, and might understand most truly, perfectly, and reasonably who he is, and of what sort, and in what manner. ⟦s14⟧ But in vain. ⟦s15⟧ Why so? ⟦s16⟧ It follows. ⟦s17⟧ « But he had turned aside and had passed on. » For when men presume to search out the things that are God's, he indeed turns aside from them and passes on, since in what manner he is can in no way be comprehended. ⟦s18⟧ Whence is that word: « Man shall come to a deep heart, and God shall be exalted *(Psal. [1265B] LXIII, 7)*. » -- « My soul melted when he spoke. » My soul, she says, which was harder than iron and stone, when the most sweet voice of the Bridegroom had been heard, melted; and, softened as it were like wax, received the evangelical preaching written upon it. ⟦s19⟧ In short, when we have heard our doctors preaching the divine precepts, if perchance, pricked with fear and love, we dissolve our eyes into tears, then we have a soul that is melted. ⟦s20⟧ But the souls of the Jews have not melted, since their hearts, hardened beyond measure, cannot be inscribed with the stylus of the spirit. ⟦s21⟧ Which indeed their law signified, being shaped upon stones. ⟦s22⟧ « I sought him, and found him not. » For many seek the Lord, since they desire both to leave the world, and to be freed from necessities, and to be with him. ⟦s23⟧ Whence one says: « O that thou wouldst rend [1265C] the heavens and come down! » [n: (Isa. LXIV, 1.)] And the Apostle: « I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ [n: (Philip. I, 23)]. » Yet some seek, and do not at once find. ⟦s24⟧ « I called, and he did not answer me. » For they call and cry out, they beg to be freed from this flesh, and the Lord does not hear them. ⟦s25⟧ « The keepers that go about the city found me. » The city is the Church, the keepers the apostles; the keepers, I say, the apostles and doctors, who go about the Church, who defend her from her enemies, « found me. ⟦s26⟧ They struck me, » namely with the sword of the spirit. ⟦s27⟧ « For the word of God is living and effectual, and more piercing than any two-edged sword [n: (Hebr. IV, 12)]. » -- « And they wounded me, » namely with the fear of compunction and of love, and with the sayings [1265D] of the Scriptures, which like the sharpest arrows put the army of the enemies to flight. ⟦s28⟧ « The keepers of the walls took away my cloak from me. » My cloak, she says — the depraved will and the multitude of vices in which I was wrapped and covered — the apostles and doctors took from me, and clothed me with the garment of faith, with a white and nuptial robe. ⟦s29⟧ Apostrophe. ⟦s30⟧ « I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, if you find my beloved, that you tell him that I languish with love. » For by the daughters of Jerusalem the souls of the apostles are understood. ⟦s31⟧ And these have already ascended into Jerusalem [1266A], and dwell with the Lord, and have found their desire. ⟦s32⟧ And therefore God's beloved, that is, the Church, who still dwells in this world *622*, adjures them to announce her love and her desire to God. ⟦s33⟧ For there are faithful witnesses in heaven.
