## CHAPTER VI. (cont.)


⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Who is she that goeth forth as the morning [1274C] rising? » Who, he says, and of what sort, and how beautiful is she who goes forth, who does not linger in idleness, but climbs up to me by passing from virtue to virtue, and fills the world with her greatness, and is spread abroad in every land? ⟦s2⟧ « As the morning rising, » shining with virtues, gleaming with serenity, announcing the true and highest day to all nations. ⟦s3⟧ « Beautiful as the moon, » since she is illumined by the light of her brother and Bridegroom alone. ⟦s4⟧ « Chosen as the sun. » For what is chosen is preferred to the rest. ⟦s5⟧ The Church, therefore, is chosen, since she imitates the brightness of the sun. ⟦s6⟧ In short, since Christ is called the sun and the Church the moon, not undeservedly is she called chosen as the sun. ⟦s7⟧ For as the Church chose the sun, so also the sun of justice, Christ our Lord, [1274D] chose the Church. ⟦s8⟧ Whence is that word: « And with the elect thou wilt be elect [n: (Psal. XVII, 17)]. » -- « Terrible. » Why terrible? ⟦s9+10⟧ Because she is prudent, because strong, because wise, because she is « as [1275A] an army set in array. » Ever watchful, ever armed, she is never off her guard. ⟦s11⟧ She says therefore: « And thou hast girded me with strength unto battle, and hast supplanted under me them that rose up against me [n: (Psal. XVII, 40)]. » -- « I went down into the garden of nuts. » What by the garden of nuts, but the Church? ⟦s12⟧ But why the Church is called a garden has already been said above: yet why of nuts? ⟦s13⟧ For the nut yields a pleasant odour. ⟦s14⟧ And its fruit is clothed with a double covering. ⟦s15⟧ What is outermost is bitter; but the second is tough and hard. ⟦s16⟧ But what lies hidden within is sweet and pleasant. ⟦s17⟧ So too the Church has an outer garment, namely the flesh: bitter, wretched, tearful, rough and shaggy. ⟦s18⟧ But beneath this is covered that strong, white, and singular garment which she wove with her own [1275B] hands: for joining virtues to virtues and knitting good works to good works, she has composed for herself an admirable robe; clothed with which, she scorns the arrows of her enemies. ⟦s19⟧ And beneath this is covered the soul, of wondrous sweetness and pleasantness, free from gall and from all bitterness. ⟦s20⟧ « That I might see the fruits of the valleys. » Into this garden Christ went down. ⟦s21⟧ But why did he go down? ⟦s22⟧ That he might see the fruits of the valleys, that is, the humility and patience of the faithful. ⟦s23⟧ And of this valley it is said: « Every valley shall be filled [n: (Luc. III, 5)]. » He went down into the garden, he saw the valley, he knew and chose the humble. ⟦s24⟧ « And that I might look whether the vineyard had flourished. » For the vineyard of God flourishes when the saints prepare themselves for good work. ⟦s25⟧ « And whether the pomegranates had budded. » The pomegranates [1275C] are the martyrs, the pomegranates are the virgins, the pomegranates are all who blush for their sins. ⟦s26⟧ And these *627* are always budding; these bring forth and nourish not thorns and briers, but rather flowers and virtues. ⟦s27⟧ « I knew not. » The voice of the Synagogue: Thou, she says, camest into the garden. ⟦s28⟧ But I knew not; I was ignorant of thy coming. ⟦s29⟧ Why so? ⟦s30⟧ Because « my soul troubled me; » and this « on account of the chariots of Aminadab. » I saw, she says, Aminadab; I saw and heard Christ. ⟦s31⟧ For Aminadab is interpreted *the willing one of his people*. ⟦s32⟧ Christ, therefore, is Aminadab, since he did not hesitate to shed his own blood for us. ⟦s33⟧ And the Synagogue saw his chariots. ⟦s34⟧ She saw Mary, who bore him in her womb and in her hands. ⟦s35⟧ She saw also the very flesh in which the Lord lay hidden. ⟦s36⟧ She saw also the apostles, [1275D] whom Christ ruled and guided as a good charioteer. ⟦s37⟧ Hence it is that it is said: « Thou who wilt mount upon thy horses, and thy chariots are salvation [n: (Habac. III, 8)]. » This the Synagogue saw, and therefore she was troubled. ⟦s38⟧ She was troubled on account of Mary, and troubled also on account of [1276A] the flesh, since she said that God could neither be a man nor have a mother. ⟦s39⟧ She heard the apostles also preaching: she was troubled, since they seemed to speak against the law. ⟦s40⟧ For they preached « Christ crucified, unto the Jews indeed a stumbling block, but unto the Gentiles foolishness [n: (I Cor. I, 23)]. » The consoling voice of the Church: « Return, return, O Sulamitess; return, return, that we may behold thee. » The voice of the chariots, that is, of the apostles: Thou, they say, O Synagogue, being foolish and unwary, didst not know him who went down into the garden, nor didst thou lose thy hardness for the din of his chariots. ⟦s41⟧ But thou, Sulamitess, thou people of the Gentiles, thou Church, return. ⟦s42⟧ Whither? ⟦s43⟧ To the Lord, into the fatherland, into the promised inheritance. ⟦s44⟧ And Sulamitess is interpreted *captive* or [1276B] *despised*. ⟦s45⟧ But the Church, formerly despised, is now chosen by a husband. ⟦s46⟧ Which indeed Rachel signified. ⟦s47⟧ And she is a captive, since, taken in battle, she was carried off by the malign spirits. ⟦s48+49⟧ And « return » is said four times, since there are four Gospels, which do not cease to say « return, return. » For they always admonish us to return, to do penance, to return to the Lord. ⟦s50⟧ « That we may behold thee, » that is, that thou be not plunged into darkness, but that in the heavenly Jerusalem we may see thee with us, and thy beauty, forever.
