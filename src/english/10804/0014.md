## CHAPTER VII. (cont.)


Christ, or the Church: « I said: I will go up into the palm tree. » For this Christ said; this he foreknew beforehand, [1280A] that he was to ascend into the palm and the virgin, into the cross. Mary is the palm, because she is ever green and a virgin. The cross is the palm, because it is the instrument of victory. It always grows, always is green; its praise is eternal, its power never grows old. « And I will take hold of its fruits. » The womb of Mary gave fruit, namely the restoration of the world. The cross of Christ gave fruit also, since, made purple with his blood, it made the dead to live. You are its fruits, O apostles, martyrs, and confessors. O palm! O victorious and admirable tree, that art adorned with such great fruits! Christ went up into the palm, he took hold of the fruits, since he drew all things to himself. « And thy breasts shall be as the clusters of the vine. » For the breasts of the Church contain both wine and milk: with milk they nourish the simple, with wine they inebriate the wise. [1280B] With this milk the Church nourishes her sons; with this wine she gladdens both minds and hearts. This wine gives wisdom; these fruitful cups have made the tongues of infants eloquent. « And thy odours are as of apples, » that is, pleasing and sweet. The odour is pleasing, the fruits are delightful. The sayings are sweet and weighty. The voice of the Church: « Thy throat is like the best wine; thy words are sweet and pleasant. » They are wine, but the best, since even if they be deep, yet they announce sweetness and gladness; they preach not death, but life. For thou willest not the death of the sinner, but life. For Moses was giving wine when, « an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth [n: (Deut. XIX, 21)], » he said. But this wine seems heavy, and harsh to drink. « Worthy for my beloved to drink. » It is worthy, she says, [1280C] a life-giving wine, so sweet, so pleasant; let my beloved drink; it is worthy that words so honey-flowing should drop from his own mouth. For from this throat, from this precious vessel, went forth that wine with which the Church is inebriated. She has tasted the wine; she praises both the cupbearer and the wine. And as though asked whether the wine were good, she answered that it was worthy of a king. « And for his lips and teeth to ruminate. » What by the lips and teeth, but the apostles and doctors? These are they who both drink and ruminate the aforesaid wine. These are they who subtly search out the divine secrets. For to ruminate wine is to turn over the testimonies of God always both on the lips and in the heart. Whence the Apostle says: « Pray without ceasing [n: (I Thess. V, 17)]. » The bride concerning [1280D] the Bridegroom: « I to my beloved, and his turning is toward me. » I, she says, speak to my beloved, and confess my wishes to him; and he turns to me, answers me, and hears me. « Come, my beloved. » *630* And this is the voice of the prophets, and of all those who were awaiting the coming of Christ. « Come, my beloved, » look upon the lands, take flesh. « Let us go forth into the field, » that we may root out from it the thorns and briers. Let us till the field, let us preach to the nations, let him go forth [1281A] [sic: *jar*] that sows, to sow his seed. « Let us abide in the villages; » that is, let us dwell among the nations. For we were country folk and unlearned. We knew not the law, nor judges, nor anything of judgments. But the [sic: *Flius*] of God, coming into the world, left the cities, left Jerusalem. Whence he himself says: « Lo, I have gone far off, fleeing, and I abode in the wilderness [n: (Psal. LIV, 8)]. » Now we have the law, now we speak in city fashion, since the universal king abides in the villages with the law and with judges. » « Let us get up early to the vineyards. » -- « The night has passed, and the day has drawn near [n: (Rom. XIII, 12)]. » Let the doctors rise, because it is day; let them not sleep, because the night has passed. Rise thou, O Paul, who admonishest the rest to watch; prune the vineyard of God; prepare and cleanse thy planting. For thou hast planted, Apollo has watered. [1281B] « Let us see if the vineyard has flourished, » that is, whether it has already received the faith and the teachings of the doctors. For this he saw, these flowers he desired, upon whom was the solicitude of all the Churches. « Whether the flowers bring forth fruits. » For the flowers bring forth fruits when good things come to their effect. Or when we carry through to the very end those good things which we have begun. « Whether the pomegranates have flourished, » that is, whether the martyrs still do not hesitate to die for Christ. In short, the pomegranates flourish when the humble and the poor shine with virtues and with holiness. For the pomegranate tree is low and unshapely. « And there will I give thee my breasts. » Where? In the vineyards I will give thee my breasts, for thee I will keep my chastity, to thy praise and honour I will suckle my children. Let the Jews flee, and let the heretics flee too, [1281C] to whom it is not lawful even to touch these breasts. « The mandrakes have given an odour in our gates; » that is, the apostles and doctors, because to some indeed they were the odour of death unto death, to others the odour of life unto life [n: (II Cor. II, 16)]. And these in our gates — that is, in the hearing of the faithful — gave their odour and their knowledge. But why are these called mandrakes? For the mandrake fights against barrenness. And it was said above of the apostles and doctors that there is none barren among them. Moreover, the mandrake affords sleep to those who cannot sleep. And these both afford to all the rest of eternal life, and very often make those who were harassed by the solicitude of the world forget all earthly things. They give sleep and rest, they give [1281D] forgetfulness of the former life. « All fruits, new and old, my beloved, I have kept for thee. » The old indeed, because the patriarchs and prophets; but the new, because the apostles and confessors. And this is understood also of new and old [1282A] sayings. The Church assuredly guards the fruits, and renders to her beloved those which the malign spirits long to steal.
