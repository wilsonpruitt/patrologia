## CHAPTER VIII

« Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother. » Who, she says, shall give me this; who shall bestow on me this honour, this dignity, this blessedness, that thou, the Son of the most high King, taking flesh, shouldst become man and be called my brother, and shouldst say with thine own mouth: « I will declare thy name to my brethren [n: (Psal. XXI, 23)]. » And Christ drew out the breasts of his mother the Synagogue, since he himself fulfilled the commandments of the law and of the prophets; or also because, according to the flesh, as it is written, he « advanced in age and wisdom before God and men [n: (Luc. II, 52)]. » [1282B] -- « And I may find thee without: » she says — for this reason she said it — that I may find thee without, that I may see thee in a man, that thou mayest proceed from the Father, come into the world, and be made manifest to the nations. « And kiss thee. » And this is what is written at the head of this book. « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » -- « Let no man despise me. » Thou hast loved me, she says; for me thou hast assumed flesh; let no man despise me, since I am betrothed to so great a husband. « I will take hold of thee and lead thee into my mother's house. » Confirmation. « And into the chamber of her that bore me. » For she takes hold of him, since she cleaves to him with devout love. And she led him into her mother's house, namely into the flesh, which was made from the earth, the universal mother. For out of love for the Church *631* Christ our Lord willed to become man. « There thou shalt teach me. » In this [1282C] house, she says, in this tabernacle, in this place of sleeping and of death, thou shalt teach me by what way and doctrine I may be able to come to thee. And rightly is the humanity of Christ called a chamber, since there he was seen to sleep who according to the divinity can neither sleep nor die. « And I will give thee a cup of spiced wine. » For the primitive vineyard, turned into bitterness, when it ought to have given grapes, brought forth wild grapes. But the Church gives wine well spiced, delightful to God and pleasant to drink. But why spiced, unless because the spiritual understanding is mingled with the letter. For Paul gave spiced wine, since as one delights in spiced wine, so in his sound doctrine and sweet preaching [1282D] Christ was delighted. For he gave spiced wine when, to the praise and honour of God, he preached wisdom among the perfect. « And the new wine of my pomegranates. » For no one doubts that must is new wine; [1283A] which indeed does not cease to ferment until the whole has been purged. And the pomegranates are the apostles and the other martyrs. And their must, their new canticle, put off its fermenting until both the error of the heretics and the unbelief of the pagans had ceased. The Church, therefore, gave the must of pomegranates when the martyrs of Christ both preached the New Testament and poured it out with their own blood.

[1283C]Thy mandrakes, O Christ,
Give forth a great odour.
Fruits old and new
Thy bride lifts up for thee.

That one mother might be ours!

« His left hand under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me. » The voice of Christ: « I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, until she herself please. » By the roes, [1283D] he says, and the harts — by the apostles and doctors, by the virtues and faith and good working, whereby you run to the promised prize and overcome the venom of serpents. And these things, because they were expounded above, need no further exposition. The Synagogue concerning the Church: « Who is this that cometh up from the desert, [1284A] flowing with delights, and leaning upon her beloved? » Who, she says, and of what sort, how beautiful, how strong is she who always ascends, always hastens to higher things, never descends, but by the steps of the virtues seeks heavenly things? But whence does she come? « From the desert, » from a land pathless and waterless, and once forsaken by God. But how does she come? « Flowing with delights, » abounding in virtues, with full lamps. Who carries her? It follows: « Leaning upon her [sic: *dilectem*], » joined to him, cleaving to him whom she has loved with her whole mind and with all her strength, and who promised to draw her after him. The voice of the Bridegroom to the bride: « Under the apple tree I raised thee up. » Thou wast dead, he says, thou wast lying in sins; but I, taking flesh, raised up again under the apple tree. And of this [1284B] apple it is said above: « As the apple tree among the trees of the woods, so is my beloved among the sons. » And this tree, this flesh of our Saviour, overshadows those whom he raised up. And this may also be understood of the cross, which by reason of Christ's blood had the colour and the appearance of this fruit. « There was thy mother corrupted, » namely the Synagogue, which crucified Christ the Lord: according to that word: « They are corrupt and are become abominable [n: (Psal. XIII, 1)]. » -- « There was she violated that bore thee, » because she forsook her lawful Bridegroom. The Church says to Christ: « Set me as a seal upon thy heart, as a seal upon thy arm. » For Christ is both the seal and the key and the firm signet of the heart of the saints. The bodies of the saints cannot be broken, *632* nor [1284C] can their secrets be violated, sealed with so great and so well-guarded a signet. We have made a sign; let us remember our friends. Christ has given us his troth, the sign and pledge of love. They carry this sign upon the arm who do all that they do in the name of the Lord. The seal is on the heart, the seal is on the hand, since the saints are mindful of Christ always both in their thoughts and in their works. Let Christ therefore be the sign and the doctor of our heart, that we may readily believe his sign and his admonition. « For love is strong as death. » -- « For perfect charity casteth out fear [n: (Joan. IV, 17)]. » Love does not fear death, it is not overcome by love. Death is strong, love is strong. The disciples of Christ do not fear death, since their love is strong as death. [1284D] They fight with death, they run to meet death. For they remember, and they have the seal, since the Lord's love over them was most firm. For the Father « spared not his own Son, but delivered him up to death for us all [n: (Rom. VIII, 32)]. » And the Son says through the Prophet: « The zeal of thy house hath eaten me up [1285A] [n: (Psal. LXVI, 10)]. » Confirmation: « Jealousy is hard as hell. » Of this jealousy the Apostle says: « For I am jealous of you with the jealousy of God [n: (II Cor. XI, 2)]. » Hell subdues all things, charity overcomes all things. Charity does not fear hell, it does not fear its princes and its inhabitants. It is a metonymy. « Its lamps are lamps of fire and of flames. » For hell always burns with an inextinguishable fire. Charity is always kindled with an unconquerable fire. And of this fire the Lord says: « I came to cast fire upon the earth [n: (Luc. XII, 49)]. » And this fire burns up vices and enlightens hearts. « Many waters could not quench charity. » Rightly, therefore, is charity likened to hell, since its fire, like that one's, is inextinguishable. « Neither shall the floods overwhelm it. » For as the Apostle [1285B] says [n: (Rom. VIII, 35)], neither tribulation, nor [sic: *augustia*], nor death, nor life, nor any persecution shall be able to separate the saints from the charity which is in Christ Jesus. « If a man shall give all the substance of his house for love, he shall despise it as nothing. » What, he says, shall I say of charity? With what praises shall I exalt it? It is greater than can be thought, greater than can be spoken. What price is there for charity, which is matched with the divine love? Do not despise charity; do not say that it can be bought with riches. For if thou givest all that thou hast, if thou distributest all the substance of thy house for love, and thinkest that with these thou hast bought charity, thou despisest charity, which cannot be sold. Thou settest a price upon that which has no price. For it [sic: *supercellit*] every price. Riches flee, [1285C] friends remain; fortune deceives thee, friends die for thee. Let us therefore seek that friend who never dies, never forsakes his friends, never lies, abounds in all things; who, praising friendship, says: « Greater love than this no man hath, than that a man lay down his life for his friends [n: (Joan. XV, 13)]. » Dying, therefore, for us, he fulfilled this love.
