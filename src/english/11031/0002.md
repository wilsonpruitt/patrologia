## III. *To the emperor Conrad II.--He congratulates him on his election as emperor, and exhorts him to rule the Roman empire vigorously and in a Christian manner, and to protect the monastery of Cassino.* [n: (Anno eod.)]

To CONRAD, most excellent emperor and most vigorous enlarger of the Roman empire, PETER THE DEACON wishes that he may be unceasingly protected by the shield of the Lord, and may triumph perpetually by his conquering right hand over enemies both spiritual and bodily.

Blessing and brightness and wisdom and thanksgiving [1141D] be to our almighty God for ever, who through you and in you has raised up for us the miracles of old, and by your recent election renews for the Roman empire its ancient joys. For neither the death of Hannibal, nor the extermination of the Goths, nor the slaughter of Lucius Catiline, nor the death of Attila and Totila so delights us as to have heard of your magnificent election, and to proclaim unceasingly the divine power in your successes. For through you the glory and honor of the eternal city is stretched out even to the sun's rising, and is enlarged, and, unfaithfulness being driven off, receives its sovereignty over the nations that rebel against it. Through you the dignity of the Roman empire is repaired, is lifted up, and is barely raised again to the ancient state of the dominion of the consuls — of Pompey, Scipio, Julius, Octavian, and [1142A] Trajan. Let all Christendom, then, utterly rejoice in your election; let east and west be glad; let the south echo back with the north; let the eternal city, mistress after God of all earthly things, celebrate festal calendars and solemn days, and pay her vows in the praises of our Lord Jesus Christ, bring forth songs, and with harmonious voices of gladness renew the ancient harmony, saying: *Let us sing to the Lord, for he is gloriously glorified.* For truly God is honored in you, who has chosen such a man as may, being one, pursue a thousand and put to flight ten thousand. Behold, through the four bounds of the Roman world your deeds, your labors, your notable struggles are reported; almost every tongue speaks of the glory of your empire. Indeed you have been made, as the [1142B] Apostle says, a spectacle to the world and to angels and to men; you have been made a champion of the Lord, the glory of the Roman empire, its increase, its ornament, and its marvelous diadem, the defense of the apostolic see, the solace of the sorrowful, the refreshment of the poor. At last the city of Rome has, by God's mercy, received back her own son, and rejoices that she keeps an emperor from the ancient stock of the Caesars. Yet for you, whom the divine majesty has chosen for so great an empire, it is fitting and becoming that, the more you have been raised by him above other mortals, so much the more you should be subject to him in all humility. For those whom he has given to rule in time, if you ascribe all your acts to his mercy and his power, he will grant to triumph perpetually. Guard therefore, unconquered emperor, before all things and above all things [1142C] the good of peace; let no spite, no envy creep into your breast. And treat justly and mercifully the Roman empire subjected to you, that above all the Roman church, through which you have received the seat of the Roman empire, may perceive — and perceiving may rejoice — that they have deserved so great and so good an Augustus, and have come under the rule of one who fears God. Let no man's wealth ever so please you that justice does not always outweigh it in your breast, so that justice should lose the place of equity. For what does it profit to have taken up the helm of the Roman empire, if you do not strive to excel all mortals in mercy, in justice, and in short in every goodness? For uprightness of mind ought to be a pattern and an example of virtue not only to you, but also to those who come after. And by doing so [1142D] you will always have the grace of almighty God as your fellow-worker; by whose supports you will both subdue all enemies, and will rejoice in peace both present and to come. Know, moreover, that the Church of Cassino daily entreats the almighty Lord for the standing of your empire, that he may bring the works of his mercy begun through you to a worthy end, and that he may protect and defend you from all evils, and for so great a labor may grant you hereafter the citizenship of eternal life. And we ask the loftiness of your nobility, most exalted conqueror, that you protect the Church of Cassino, the mother of all monasteries; for it is known to have been once defended by the protections of your predecessors and enriched by their wealth. You [1143A] therefore, as you are shown to be their equal in the nobility of your wisdom, in power, in prudence, in all the things that are of this world, and — what is more distinguished — in upright character, so also strive to be their equal in the exaltation of the monastery of Cassino. Make blessed Benedict your debtor on earth, that whatever you bestow on him you may deserve to receive a hundredfold in heaven. For to this end has heavenly Providence, which governs all things and orders all things from the beginning of the world, chosen a man of such vigor and prudence in the Roman commonwealth — especially at this time, when, our sins requiring it, [1144A] soundness has failed, and truths have been diminished among the sons of men — that you should exalt the Churches. And may God our Lord, who freely lavishes the gifts of his goodness on those who ask, protect and bless you with the right hand of his strength; and, when the enemies of both substances have been separated off, may he open to you the doors of the heavenly life as you return to him with the palm of the contest, and may he grant you to rejoice perpetually with himself in heavenly goods.
