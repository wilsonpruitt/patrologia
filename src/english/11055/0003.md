## BRIEF NOTES ON THE SECOND BOOK OF KINGS.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0100D] *[n: (II REG. I.)] And David made a lamentation of this kind over Saul and over Jonathan his son; and he commanded that they should teach the children of Juda the bow,* that is, the art of shooting; because Saul and Jonathan his son had perished by the arrows of the Philistines, so that, if war should arise anew, they might be able to defend themselves against their adversaries. ⟦s2⟧ *As it is written in the book of the just.* ⟦s3⟧ Some think that by the book of the just this same book is to be understood, on account of Samuel and David, of whom it principally treats. ⟦s4⟧ Others interpret the book of the just as the law of Moses, in which the form of justice is set before men. ⟦s5⟧ From which they think it may be gathered that in not a few places the art of shooting is useful, as perhaps that place in Genesis where Jacob says that he took a portion from the hand of the Amorrhite with sword and bow. ⟦s6⟧ Others interpret the book of the just not of just men [0101A] but of judgments or precepts. ⟦s7⟧ As if it were said, in the book of right — so that the sense may be: he commanded that they should teach the children of Juda the bow, that this should be held as it were for a law and be observed inviolably, according to what is written in the book of right and set forth by the precept of the law. ⟦s8⟧ *The shield of the mighty,* to be pronounced in the plural; which nevertheless, expounding in the singular, he adds: *the shield of Saul.* ⟦s9⟧ A manner of speaking, when something is set forth in common, to which a particular carrying-out is to be added. ⟦s10⟧ This occurs in the heightening of matters, when something is narrated with strong emphasis. ⟦s11⟧ *The shield of the mighty is cast away, the shield of Saul,* as though he had not been anointed with oil. ⟦s12⟧ As of a worthless man, as of a man not having divine sanctification. ⟦s13⟧ Then there follows: *From the blood of the slain, from the fat of the mighty, the arrow of Jonathan [0101B] never went back.* ⟦s14⟧ As if he said. ⟦s15⟧ The arrow of Jonathan did not go back in the battles, so that, once shot, it should ever leap back empty from the blood of the mighty, from the fat of the marrowy ones; but it pierced mightily, pouring out blood and grazing the fat of the mighty whom it slew. ⟦s16⟧ *Jonathan is slain in thy high places, O Israel. ⟦s17⟧ I grieve over thee, my brother Jonathan.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (II REG. V.)] It was said to David by them, that is, by the Jebusites. ⟦s2⟧ Thou shalt not enter here, unless thou take away the blind and the lame.* ⟦s3⟧ They set blind and lame men upon their walls, that they might say to him as he came. ⟦s4⟧ *Thou shalt not enter here* — for contempt, namely of him, as though such men were enough to repel his violence. ⟦s5⟧ And therefore they said to him: *Thou shalt not enter here* unless thou take away those, namely the blind and the lame, who have been set up to resist thee. [0101C] *For David had offered a reward on that day,* to him, namely, *who should strike the Jebusite and touch the pipes of the housetops.* ⟦s6⟧ That is, the pipes of the channels projecting along the top of the wall from the housetops, that is, from the roofs of the houses, for leading out the water of the eaves. ⟦s7⟧ To him, then, who first, climbing up as far as the channels projecting from above, should enter the city, David had offered a reward — which reward, however, he does not here determine, but sets forth in the book of Paralipomenon, namely that he would make him leader of the army. ⟦s8⟧ Whence Joab, climbing up first, was made prince of the soldiery. ⟦s9⟧ *From Mello, and inward.* ⟦s10⟧ Mello was a place in the city, beginning from which to build, he built round about and inward.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0101D] *[n: (II REG. VII.)] Thou shouldst do before the face of thy people, namely terrible things;* that is, on account of thy people. ⟦s2⟧ For from it thou didst take the cause of doing. ⟦s3⟧ *Whom thou hast redeemed to thyself out of Egypt.* ⟦s4⟧ Here one must stop; then add: *A nation, and its God,* whereby is understood: where shall we find such a nation, and such a God of it, as thy people Israel, and as thou its God.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (II REG. XI.)] She was sanctified from her uncleanness,* that is, she washed herself after intercourse, or because her monthly courses had ceased on account of conception.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (II REG. XIV.)] Upon me, my lord king, be the iniquity, and upon the house of my father;* but let the king and his throne be innocent. ⟦s2⟧ As if she said: Beware lest perhaps thy promise be empty, since it does not befit thee, who art king and lord, that iniquity be found in thee, as it is in [0102A] me and in the house of my father, that is, in such as I am. ⟦s3⟧ For the greater thou art, the more deformed is thy iniquity. ⟦s4⟧ *Let the king remember the Lord his God,* that is, let him fear the Lord his God. ⟦s5⟧ *That they be not multiplied,* that is, that he not permit to be multiplied and to rise up in manifold ways *the next of kin by blood,* that is, the kinsmen of the slain man, for taking vengeance; and then they will not kill my son. ⟦s6⟧ *Let thy handmaid speak,* etc. ⟦s7⟧ Having received assurance concerning the case which she had feigned as her own, she now brings in that by which she may compel the king by his own judgment to spare, saying: *Why hast thou thought such a thing;* that is, that thou hast cast out thy son for the killing of his brother, which indeed was against the people of God; because this example will be able to harm the people of God hereafter, if the people imitate it, doing likewise. ⟦s8⟧ *Now therefore I am come, because it is good [0102B] to spare and to have mercy.* ⟦s9⟧ Therefore I have come, and I will speak to thee, the king my lord, this word which I have done concerning my son. ⟦s10⟧ What follows, *the people being present,* is found more expressly in the Hebrew: chi, chire, , that is, because they have terrified me; as if she said: because those who wished to kill my son have terrified me, therefore I have come to obtain pardon. ⟦s11⟧ Which nevertheless can also be fittingly understood in the earlier words, so that if the sense; I have come that I may speak the word, the people being present, that is, pressing and urging me — who demand my son for death. ⟦s12⟧ *And thy handmaid said. ⟦s13⟧ I will speak to the king, if in any way the king may do the word of his handmaid;* that is, if perchance he may hear me pleading for my son. ⟦s14⟧ *And the king heard. ⟦s15⟧ Let therefore thy handmaid speak, that the word of my lord the king may be done,* that the judgment [0102C] which thou hast made concerning my cause thou mayest keep in thine own; because thou, like an angel of God, oughtest to be moved from the truth neither for anger which thou mayest have against anyone, nor for favor. ⟦s16⟧ *By the health of thy soul, my lord king, it is neither to the right nor to the left;* that is, nothing of all that thou hast spoken deviates anywhere, but that it is as thou sayest, namely that the hand of Joab is with me. ⟦s17⟧ Thou art wise in all these things, so as to understand all: that is, what is expedient to be done in all the things that are to be done upon the earth. ⟦s18⟧ It is a winning of favor, that he may attend, as a wise man, to what is to be done.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (II REG. XV.)] Carrying the ark of the covenant of the Lord, and they set down the ark of God.* ⟦s2⟧ Some think the ark of the Lord was set down for this reason, that David might consult the Lord. [0102D] But because he then was unwilling to answer him, therefore David said to Sadoc: *Carry back the ark of God into the city,* namely Jerusalem. ⟦s3⟧ Others think it was set down that it might await the people passing over. ⟦s4⟧ Whence it is that there follows: *Abiathar went up,* that is, he stood apart in a more eminent place, waiting until all the people should be gathered.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (II REG. XVII.)] He went in to Abigail the daughter of Naas.* ⟦s2⟧ Naas is the same as Isai and Jesse, the father of David.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (II REG. XXI.)] From the beginning of the harvest until, water dropped upon them.* ⟦s2⟧ Note that in those regions, in summer, especially at the time of harvest, neither do thunders sound nor do rains fall, until about the Kalends of September. ⟦s3⟧ *And there was again a battle of the Philistines against Israel; and David went down,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ She whom in [0103A] this place Scripture names Arepham [Arafam] is believed to have been Orpha the Moabitess, the daughter-in-law of Noemi, the companion of Ruth, from whose offspring were born these four giants, who here are said to have fallen by the hand of David and of his servants. ⟦s5⟧ But Scripture goes back over earlier matters, recalling those wars perhaps in another order than that in which they were done. ⟦s6⟧ In the first, Abisai the son of Sarvia killed Jesbidenob; in the second, Sobochai killed Sephi [Zap]; in the third, David, who here is called Adeodatus, because he was given by God for the deliverance of Israel, and son of the forest, because he was taken from the pastures and the forest, and the weaver of many colors, because with manifold beauty he adorned the worship of God in psalms and canticles: and the Bethlehemite, on account of his native place. ⟦s7⟧ David himself, namely, killed Goliath the Gethite. ⟦s8⟧ Which war seems to have been the first, [0103B] although here it is recalled as the third. ⟦s9⟧ In the fourth, Jonathan killed a man having six fingers on each of his hands and feet, who likewise was of the stock of Arepha [Arafa].
