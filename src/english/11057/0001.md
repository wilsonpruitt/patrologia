## Brief Notes on Joel (cont.)

ALLEGORICALLY. Awake, ye that are drunk, and weep. Howl, all ye that drink wine in sweetness, for it is perished from the mouth. As if he said [sic: *diecret*]: The day of perdition is at hand, and the times of temptation hasten to be present. Awake, therefore, from the [sic: *sumno*] of deadly negligence, from the bed of carnal concupiscence, because you are made drunk with the prosperity of a passing pleasure; pour out the tears of compunction, utter the howl of confession, you who burn with the wine of a passing delight. For it is perished, he says, from your mouth. Hence James: *Let the brother of low degree glory in his exaltation, and the rich in his lowness, for [0326C] he shall pass away as the flower of the grass. For the sun is risen with a burning heat, and hath dried up the grass; and its flower is fallen; and the beauty of its countenance hath perished; so also shall the rich man wither away in his ways [n: (Jac. I)]*. Awake, unto penance; weep, unto pardon; howl, unto grace, you who drink wine in the sweetness of a passing pleasure, you who are made drunk with the glory of a momentary vanity; because it is written: *They have slept their sleep, and all the men of riches have found nothing in their hands [n: (Psal. LXXV)]*. There follows: For it is perished from your mouth. Hence James: *Go to,* he says, *now, ye rich men, weep, howling in your miseries which shall come upon you. Your riches are corrupted, and your garments are moth-eaten. Your gold and silver is rusted: and their rust shall be for a testimony [0326D] against you; and shall eat your flesh like fire. Ye have treasured up* indeed *for yourselves wrath in the last days [n: (Jac. V)]*. The wine, I say, is perished from your mouth, because it is written: *For laughter shall be mingled with sorrow, and mourning taketh hold of the end of joy [n: (Prov. XIV)]*. Whence there follows: For a nation is come up upon my land, strong, and without number. Their teeth are as the teeth of a lion; and its molars as a lion's whelp. It hath laid my vineyard into a desert, and hath barked my fig tree. Stripping it bare it hath despoiled it, and cast it away: its branches are made white. That nation is the throng of iniquity, the multitude of superfluity, the troop of curiosity, the cohort of pleasure, the people of vanity. Strong is that nation in the storming of the virtues, without number in the persistence of temptations; strong [0327A] it is by the victory of wickedness without number, by the insistence of suggestion.

The lion is the devil, and his teeth are the depraved motions of the soul; the molars, consent; the whelps, acts; the land is the soul: which is also a fig tree by the sweetness of heavenly contemplation. Or it is a land by the humility of penance, a vineyard by the subtlety of understanding, a fig tree by the sweetness of eminent grace. Or it is a land by the rigor of abstinence, a vineyard by the fervor of justice, a fig tree by the sweetness of heavenly wisdom. But the aforesaid nation barked it, stripped it, cast it away. It barked it in act, stripped it in sense, cast it away in affection. In the act of uprightness, in the sense of honorableness, in the affection of goodness. Or in the act of justice, in the sense of continence, in the affection of mercy. The branches are the desires of the soul, by which it is stretched out as far as [0327B] works: which are made white when they do not lose the native color of virtue. There follows:

Lament like a virgin girded with sackcloth for the husband of thy youth. *The sacrifice and the libation is perished from the house of the Lord. The priests, the ministers of the Lord, have mourned. The country is laid waste. The ground hath mourned, for the wheat is destroyed. The wine is confounded, and the oil hath languished. The husbandmen are confounded, and the vinedressers have howled over the corn and the barley, because the harvest of the field is perished. The vineyard is confounded, and the fig tree hath languished. The pomegranate, and the palm tree, and the apple tree, and all the trees of the field are withered, because joy is confounded from the sons of men.* HISTORICALLY. He names the Synagogue a virgin, on account of the faith of the one God, which she received. But the nations were harlots and fornicators, on account of the varied worship of idolatry. [0327C] This Synagogue, namely, he invites to lamentation and to sackcloth over the husband of her youth: because, namely, before the multitude of the disaster, and before the fury of the enemy's devastation, the rite of the ceremonies and the worship of the law ceased. For the worship of the law was the Synagogue's husband. Whence the Apostle: *The woman is bound to the law of her husband as long as her husband liveth. But if he be dead, she is loosed from the law of her husband [n: (I Cor. VII)]*. The Synagogue too, until Christ, held the worship of the law as a debt. Whence Christ himself: *The law and the prophets until John [n: (Matth. I)]*. But at the coming of Christ she hears: *If you be circumcised, Christ shall profit you nothing [n: (Galat. V)]*.

ALLEGORICALLY. He calls the Church a virgin, on account of the faith of the one God and the integrity of the sacraments. A virgin [0327D] is the Church, a virgin is her husband Christ. Who is deservedly called the husband of her youth, because it is thus read of him in Jeremiah: *The Lord hath created a new thing upon the earth. A woman shall compass a man [n: (Jer. XXXI)]*, and that other place: *A man of sorrows, and acquainted with bearing infirmity [n: (Isa. LIII)]*. Likewise in Isaiah, in the person of the Father: *I,* he says, *am the Lord calling a bird from the east, and from a far country the man of my own will [n: (Isai. XLVI)]*. The husband, assuredly, of her youth. Whence the bride in the Canticles: *I am,* she says, *my beloved's, and his turning is toward me [n: (Cant. VII)]*. As if she said: As I alone am his, so he alone is mine; and as I am his alone, so he is mine only. This virgin is called forth to lamentation and to sackcloth over the husband of her youth, because on account of the misfortunes contained below the Church is worthily warned to weep. For, [0328A] sins requiring it, the order, the state, the splendor, the worship of the Church is neglected, altered, deformed, darkened. The order of the precepts, the state of the counsels, the splendor of the judgments, the worship of the sacraments. The order through negligence or heedlessness, the state through concupiscence, the splendor through avarice, the worship through irreverence. Whence the Church is most worthily incited through Joel to lamentation over the contempt of her husband: Lament, he says, like a virgin girded with sackcloth over the husband of thy youth, for the sacrifice and the libation is perished from the house of the Lord. That is called a sacrifice which is offered of a solid thing, and a libation what is of a liquid. By these the sacraments of the Church are designated, which are handled partly in a solid, partly in a liquid. But both perish from the house of the Lord: not in substance, but in reverence, [0328B] because, with the malice of the vices prevailing, one is scarcely found in the Church who worthily handles or receives the sacraments. Whence there follows also: The priests, the ministers of the Lord, have mourned. They are called priests, because they give the sacraments; ministers, because they minister the word of life. The priests mourn the contempt of their subjects in the precepts, or their disobedience, and also the irreverence toward themselves in the sacraments, which they justly deserve. Hence the Lord through Malachi: *Behold,* he says, *I come to you, O priests, that despise my name, and offer polluted bread upon my altar, and say: Wherein have we polluted thee? [n: (Matth. I.)]* In that ye say (that is, ye cause it to be said) the table of the Lord is polluted. If therefore, since thou art a minister and a priest, *and restest in the law, and gloriest in God, and knowest his will, and approvest the more profitable things, being instructed [0328C] by the law, thou art confident that thou thyself art a guide of the blind, a light of them that are in darkness, an instructor of the foolish, a teacher of infants, having the form of knowledge and of truth in the law. Thou therefore that teachest another, teachest not thyself? Thou that sayest men should not steal, stealest. Thou that preachest men should not commit adultery, committest adultery. Thou that abhorrest idols, committest sacrilege. Thou that gloriest in the law, by transgression of the law dishonorest God. The name of God is blasphemed among the Gentiles through you [n: (Rom. II)]*. Hence Jeremiah: *From the priests,* he says, *iniquity is gone forth [n: (Jer. XXIII)]*. And Sophonias: *The priests,* he says, *have polluted thy sanctuary; they have done unjustly against the law [n: (Sophon. III)]*. And Malachi: *But you,* he says, *have departed out of the way, and have caused very many to stumble at the law; and [0328D] you have made void my covenant, saith the Lord [n: (Malach. II)]*. Behold their depraved deservings. There follows concerning their mourning and their irreverence, of which the Lord through Malachi: *I will send,* he says, *want upon you, and I will curse your blessings, and I will curse them, because you have not laid it to heart [n: (ibid.)]*. Likewise through the same: *Behold, I,* he says, *will cast the arm at you, and I will scatter upon your face the dung of your solemnities [n: (ibid.)]*. Likewise the same: *Wherefore,* he says, *I have made you contemptible, and base before all people, as you have not kept my ways, and have accepted persons in the law [n: (ibid.)]*. There follows:

The country is laid waste, the ground hath mourned, for the wheat is destroyed. The wine is confounded, and the oil hath languished. The husbandmen are confounded, the vinedressers have howled [0329A] over the corn and the barley, because the harvest of the field is perished. The vineyard is confounded, and the fig tree hath languished. The pomegranate, and the palm tree, and the apple tree, and all the trees of the field are withered. The country of the Church is the breadth of obedience; the ground, the humility of penance; the wheat, the soundness of teaching; the wine, the subtlety of spiritual understanding; the husbandmen and the vinedressers, the priests and the preachers. But they are called husbandmen because of the shaping of morals, vinedressers because of the gladness of eternal things which they pour into the minds of their hearers; husbandmen, because they shape men toward the action of justice; vinedressers, because they stir up the souls of their hearers to the love of wisdom; or husbandmen, because by their teaching they overturn earthly works and desires; vinedressers, because they promise spiritual gifts, and bring the gladness of eternal things into the minds of their hearers [0329B]. The barley is plain and historical teaching; the harvest for the fields, the fertile simplicity of good works; the vineyard, the abundance of divine knowledge; the fig tree, the sweetness of contemplation and the pleasantness of eternal things. The pomegranate is the fervor of martyrdom; the palm, contempt of the world; the plain apple, the work of faith; all the other trees are the works of penance or of mercy. But hastening on to other matters we leave off fitting each thing to its own place: which, as easy and useful, we leave to the reader's exercise. There follows:

*Because joy is confounded from the sons of men.* Deservedly is joy said to be confounded from the failure of the things preceding: because from the want of them the people is endangered, the priests are despised, the churches are made weak.

[0329C]It is also to be known that joy is divided in four ways. For there is a joy of iniquity, one of vanity, one of charity, one of felicity. Of the first it is read: *Who are glad when they have done evil, and rejoice in most wicked things [n: (Prov. II)]*. Of the second: *They take the timbrel and the harp, and rejoice at the sound of the organ [n: (Job XXI)]*. Of the third: *The saints shall rejoice in glory; they shall be joyful in their beds [n: (Psal. CXLIX)]*. Of the fourth: *Blessed are they that dwell in thy house, O Lord; they shall praise thee for ever and ever [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)]*. The joy of charity, therefore, and the reward of felicity is confounded from the sons of men: because, when the aforesaid virtues fail, where the knowledge of truth and the love of virtue do not go before, merits ceasing, no rewards of salvation follow either here or in the [0329D] time to come.
