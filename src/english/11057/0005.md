## Brief Notes on Joel (cont.)

Their appearance is as the appearance of horses, and they shall run like horsemen. In the first is marked the neighing and the fervor of preaching; in the second the struggle for the faith and the labor of the encounter. There follows: Like the noise of chariots it shall leap upon the tops of the mountains, like the noise of a flame of fire devouring the stubble: as a strong people is prepared for battle. Here is briefly inserted the labor and the victory of the evangelists, who by their life, by miracles and by teaching broke the elation of the proud. The chariots are the evangelists, and the mountains the proud, the noise the preaching of faith, the flame [0341B] the miracles, the stubble sins. There follows: At its face the peoples shall be tormented; all faces shall be brought into a pot. The tormenting of the peoples is its wholesome compunction; the bringing of the faces into a pot is a man's own and sincere knowledge of himself. There follows: They shall run like valiant men, like men of war they shall scale the wall. In the first is marked the prosperity of preaching, in the second the victory of the contest, and, for the faith, the obedience of mortification. There follows: The men shall march in their own ways, and they shall not turn aside from their paths. In the first is marked the concord of the preachers, in the second the perseverance of the virtues and of faith. There follows: No one shall press upon his brother; every one shall walk in his own track. The prophet still pursues the unanimity of the apostles, and their most concordant will, who so sowed [0341C] the Gospel in the world that no one should usurp for himself another's seed. Hence the Apostle: *I dare not speak of any of those things which Christ worketh not by me, unto the obedience of the Gentiles, by word, by deeds, by the virtue of signs and wonders, in the powers of the Holy Ghost, so that from Jerusalem round about as far as illyricum I have filled up the Gospel of Christ [n: (Rom. XV)]*. The same, in the same place: *And I have so preached this Gospel, not where Christ was named, lest I should build upon another man's foundation [n: (ibid.)]*. There follows: Yea, and they shall fall through the windows, and shall not be broken. There are five windows: the dispensation of the incarnation, or the five modes of divine contemplation. The first mode of contemplation is the consideration of fault and of hell; the second, the contempt of present things and the hope of things to come; the third, [0341D] judgment and the kingdom; the fourth, the state of the body and its glorification — the state, according as it will be passible or impassible; the glorification, according as it will be of ineffable beauty, or of most resplendent brightness; the fifth, of one spirit with God, which is conformity in all things. Through these windows the apostles and apostolic men wholesomely fall, when, by the devout consideration of these things, they humbly prostrate themselves before their Creator. Falling thus they are not broken, but rather are raised up and made firm, according to that word: *Every one that exalteth himself shall be humbled [n: (Luc. XIV)]*. Whence in the Apocalypse: *And when,* says John, *those four living creatures gave glory, and honor and blessing to him that sitteth upon the throne, that liveth for ever and ever, the four [0342A] and twenty ancients fell down before him that sitteth on the throne, and adored him that liveth for ever and ever. And they cast their crowns before the throne, saying: Thou art worthy, O Lord our God, to receive glory, and honor, and power [n: (Apoc. IV)]*, etc. There are five windows of the incarnation: namely, the nativity, the manner of life, the teaching, the resurrection, the ascension. Through which those five things which have been said about contemplation are seen. For through his nativity, sins and hell were made manifest to many. His manner of life openly taught men to despise present things and to direct their hope to eternal ones: his teaching indeed deals sufficiently with judgment and the kingdom; in the resurrection the state of the body and its glorification were shown, because as he himself rose into glory, so also do we through him; and in the ascension it was in some measure made known to us how [0342B] our spirit is to be united with God. For the human body could not be lifted upward or carried across without any vehicle except through the divinity united to itself. Through these windows also apostolic men fall, because by the careful meditation of the aforesaid things they humble themselves utterly and wear down their flesh, they mortify their members while they truly and particularly desire them. But they are not broken, because in him on whom they lean kindness knows not how to injure, nor truth to deceive, nor justice to despise, nor chastity to corrupt; and in him to whom they are joined wisdom knows not how to be deceived, nor strength to be weakened, nor power to be overcome, nor eternity to be changed. There follows:

They shall enter the city, they shall run upon the wall, they shall climb up the houses. The city is in this place the gathering of men assembled to [0342C] live in common. The wall is the obstinacy of the mind; the houses, the consciences of individuals hardened by sins. So great will be the grace and the fortitude of the apostles and of apostolic men that they will storm even the gathering of malicious men, dissolve their obstinacy by the spirit of fortitude, and obtain the consciences of individuals, the spirit of grace and of prayers. There follows: They shall come in at the windows like a thief. The windows are in this place fear, namely of eternal punishment, sorrow for the present exile, hope of the heavenly reward, the shortness of this life, the mutability of fortune. The first looks to the west, the second to the north, the third to the east, the fourth to the south, the fifth considers the center of this world. Through these they enter [0342D] stealthily the consciences of individuals, which they easily overcome while they gather the aforesaid things before the eyes of the mind. For fear pricks, sorrow drives out, hope draws, shortness urges on, the instability of fortune puts to flight; and so the mind of the newly baptized, fleeing, hastens to its homeland. There follows: At its face the earth trembled, and the heavens were moved. The earth is sinners, the heavens the just. But the earth trembled at the presence of the apostles unto penance. The just, as many as were in the world, hearing their teaching, their life, their fame, were moved, accounting their own justice as nothing. There follows: The sun and the moon are darkened, and the stars have withdrawn their shining. The sun is the wisdom of the age; the moon, the power of this world; the stars, the sects of the philosophers; the shining of the stars, the cultivation and the fame of the sects. But the sun and the moon were darkened [0343A] at the face of the apostles, because through the foolishness of God the wisdom of the age was destroyed, and through the weak thing of God the power of the world was trodden underfoot; and through the virtue of signs, through the brightness of miracles, through the naked truth of words, the composition of the colors of rhetoric was blotted out, the rhetorical splendor of discourses was darkened, the cultivation and gleam of empty sects was made void. Hence the Psalmist: *Thou hast broken up the fountains and the torrents; thou hast dried up the rivers of Etham [n: (Psal. LXXIII)]*. By the fountains the philosophers are understood, by Etham the devil, which is interpreted *strong,* and his flame are the kinds of perverse sects. There follows: And the Lord hath uttered his voice before the face of his army: because his camps are exceeding many: because they are strong and execute his word. The voice of the Lord is the divine inspiration. The voice of the Lord, I say, [0343B] is that heavenly unction which teacheth of all things. Of which it is said through the Psalmist: *The voice of the Lord breaking the cedars. The voice of the Lord preparing the stags. The voice of the Lord coming between the flame of fire. The voice of the Lord shaking the desert [n: (Psal. XVIII)]*. This voice makes camps, because it joins and orders the companies of the saints. Which indeed are many, and strong, and executing his word. They are many in the dignity of their merits, strong in the virtue of signs and the power of miracles, executing his word through the obedience of his commandments, through the eminence of their exercises, through the excellence of their counsels. There follows: For the day of the Lord is great, and terrible. Because the coming of Christ magnified believers and the just; it terrified and condemned the unfaithful [0343C] and the reprobate. Great indeed, *because whom he predestinated, them he also called; and whom he called, them he also justified; and whom he justified, them he glorified [n: (Rom. VIII)]*. *Terrible, because it shall be more tolerable for the Ninevites in the day of judgment than for this most wicked generation [n: (Luc. XI)]*. For they believed at the preaching of Jonas: and behold, more than Jonas here. There follows: Who shall bear it? For who will be able worthily to receive into himself the Founder of nature, the fountain of life, the bridegroom of grace, the coming of the Saviour? Or who will be able to bear, the weight of wrath, the burden of misery, the punishment of vengeance, *the worm that dieth not; the fire that is not quenched? [n: (Marc. IX.)]* There follows:
