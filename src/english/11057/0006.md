## Brief Notes on Joel (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Now therefore, saith the Lord, be converted to me with all your heart, in fasting, and in weeping, and in mourning; and [0343D] rend your hearts, and not your garments. ⟦s2⟧ And be converted to the Lord your God, because he is gracious and merciful, patient, and of much mercy, and prevailing over malice. ⟦s3⟧ Who knoweth whether God will turn and forgive, and leave a blessing behind him, sacrifice and libation to the Lord our God.* ⟦s4⟧ HISTORICALLY. ⟦s5⟧ Under the person of the Lord the prophet exhorts his people to importune the divine mercy, to beat upon the ears of the highest clemency with laments and tearful cries, saying: Be converted to me with all your heart, in fasting, and in weeping, and in mourning, etc. ⟦s6⟧ As if he plainly said: Since the time of the captivity draws near, and the Babylonian presses on, do you then press on in supplication, apply yourselves to prayer; be converted to me, that the Babylonian may be turned back upon himself; return to [0344A] me, that the enemy may be turned back. ⟦s7⟧ For *with the holy thou wilt be holy, and with the elect elect, and with the perverse thou wilt be perverse [n: (Psal. XVII)]*. ⟦s8⟧ But allegorically, under the person of the Lord he admonishes in common every rank of the Church to flee from the wrath to come, to quench the flood of sins with the tears of penance, to appease the wrath of the judge on high with works of mercy, saying: Now therefore saith the Lord: Be converted to me, etc. ⟦s9⟧ As if he said: Since the day of perdition is at hand, and the times hasten to be present; since a day exceedingly terrible and great presses on; since scarcely will anyone flee from the wrath to come; since the strict judge cannot be conquered, because he is power; cannot be deceived, because he is wisdom; cannot be corrupted, because he is justice; cannot be outlasted, because he is eternal; [0344B] cannot be avoided, because he is everywhere. ⟦s10⟧ Yet he can be entreated, because he is mercy; he can be appeased, because he is kindness; he can cleanse, because he is the fountain of grace; he can satisfy, because he is the bread of life; he can calm, because he is unction; he can adorn, because he is fullness; he can make happy and blessed, because he is beatitude. ⟦s11⟧ Therefore, you who are turned away, fearing his justice, be converted and flee to his mercy. ⟦s12⟧ From him flee to him; from the rigor of justice to the bosom of mercy. ⟦s13⟧ The Lord, who is to be feared, speaks: he who is truth commands that which is just, which is useful, which is honorable. ⟦s14⟧ Be converted, he says, to me, etc. ⟦s15⟧ There are four things, two evils and two goods, the one evil, the other worse, the one good, the other better. ⟦s16⟧ Turning away and overthrow, lo, two evils. ⟦s17⟧ Conversion and returning, lo, two goods. ⟦s18⟧ Turning away [0344C] from God through negligence, overthrow by the Devil through malice. ⟦s19⟧ Conversion to God through penance, returning to him through innocence. ⟦s20⟧ And turning away comes about in three ways: by vanity, by pleasure, by curiosity: by the vanity of the world, by pleasure in oneself, by curiosity about one's neighbor. ⟦s21⟧ Overthrow also in three ways: by headlong falling, by malice, by despair: by the headlong falling of fault, by the malice of envy, by despair of pardon. ⟦s22⟧ But conversion comes about in three ways: by confession, by compunction, by mortification: by confession of the mouth, compunction of the mind, mortification of the flesh — namely, that in the mouth there be truth, in the mind purity, in the flesh a chaste sobriety. ⟦s23⟧ Returning also comes about in three ways: by devotion, by love, by contemplation: by the devotion of grace, the love of justice, the contemplation [0344D] of glory. ⟦s24⟧ You therefore who have turned away from God through the vice of negligence, who have been overthrown and drowned in the flood of malice, be converted, he says, to me with all your heart. ⟦s25⟧ There is a conversion of the heart, and there is also a conversion of work. ⟦s26⟧ And of the conversion of the heart, one kind is with the whole heart, another is in part. ⟦s27⟧ But the Lord seeks that which is with the whole heart, because it suffices for salvation. ⟦s28⟧ But he rejects the other, which is in part, because it is feigned, and far from salvation. ⟦s29⟧ Hence it is written: *The Holy Spirit will flee from the feigned of discipline [n: (Sap. I)]*. ⟦s30⟧ For in the heart there are three things: reason, will, memory: reason of things to come, will of things present, memory of things past. ⟦s31⟧ For reason seeks things to come, the will loves things present, memory retains things past. ⟦s32⟧ Reason enlightens, the will loves, memory preserves. ⟦s33⟧ When therefore reason [0345A] seeks and finds the highest good, the will receives and loves it, and memory carefully keeps it and binds it more tightly; then the soul converts itself to God with its whole heart. ⟦s34⟧ But when reason, laid to sleep, forbears to seek heavenly things, or the lukewarm will cares not to love them, or the sluggish memory despises to guard them, then the soul becomes feigned, falling first into the vice of ignorance, secondly into the offense of negligence, thirdly into the sin of malice. ⟦s35⟧ In either case it is agreed that the soul is feigned, because otherwise ignorance could be driven off by the light of reason, and negligence shut out by the zeal of the will, and malice lulled to sleep by the diligence of memory. ⟦s36⟧ Reason, then, in seeking, brings forth instruction; the will, in embracing, love; memory, in binding fast, edification. ⟦s37⟧ The first brings forth the light of knowledge, the second the affection of justice, the third preserves the treasure [0345B] of grace. ⟦s38⟧ This is the conversion of the heart which God requires. ⟦s39⟧ This is that which altogether suffices for salvation, There follows the conversion of work. ⟦s40⟧ In fasting, he says, and in weeping, and in mourning. ⟦s41⟧ The mourning is referred to confession, the weeping to compunction, the fasting to mortification. ⟦s42⟧ There follows:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And rend your hearts, and not your garments. ⟦s2⟧ The rending of hearts is the scattering of depraved affections. ⟦s3⟧ The garments are the examples of the saints, And so we are commanded to rend our hearts, but to keep our garments unharmed, because both the impure will is to be chastised and purified by the austerity of penance, and the examples of the saints are to be taken as a lesson of justice. ⟦s4⟧ Hence Job: *Thou renewest,* he says, [0345C] *thy witnesses against me, and multipliest thy wrath upon me, and pains war against me (Job X)*. Likewise the same: *He shall look upon men, and shall say: I have sinned, and indeed I have offended: and I have not received as I deserved. ⟦s5⟧ Then shall he see the face of the Lord with jubilee, and he will render to man his justice [n: (Job XXXIII)]*. ⟦s6⟧ Of great usefulness indeed are the examples of the saints: which instruct us unto knowledge, draw us to pardon, approach [ *an* accendunt?] unto grace, shape us unto justice, advance us unto glory. ⟦s7⟧ Hence [sic: *Hincvoce*] in the voice of the Psalmist: *He hath made a remembrance of his wonderful works [n: (Psal. CX)]*, etc. ⟦s8⟧ There follows: And be converted to the Lord your God. ⟦s9⟧ The Lord, he says, who is to be feared; God, who is to be loved. ⟦s10⟧ Your own, who is the Founder. ⟦s11⟧ Your own, he says, because he is the founder of nature; God, because he is the giver of grace; Lord, [0345D] because he is the avenger of fault. ⟦s12⟧ There follows: Because he is gracious and merciful, patient, and much in mercy [multae misericordiae], prevailing over malice. ⟦s13⟧ He is gracious, bearing with the sinner's wrath; merciful, bearing with his savagery; patient, bearing with his contempt; much in mercy, bearing with his hatred; prevailing over malice, bearing with the shipwreck of his despair. ⟦s14⟧ Gracious, I say, because he turns the wrath of enemies into meekness; merciful, because he turns savagery into piety; patient, because he turns contempt into compunction; much in mercy, because he turns hatred into love; prevailing over malice, because he advances the abyss of despair into the grace of contemplation. ⟦s15⟧ These things have been set forth in the natural order; now let them be set forth in the artificial order. ⟦s16⟧ He is prevailing, therefore, over malice, [0346A] while he draws despair to penance; much in mercy, while he draws hatred to pardon; patient, while he draws contempt to grace; merciful, while he draws cruelty to innocence; gracious, while he draws wrath and rancor to the glory of kisses. ⟦s17⟧ There follows: Who knoweth whether he will turn, and forgive, and leave a blessing behind him? ⟦s18⟧ Who, in this place does not signify doubt, but charity. ⟦s19⟧ As if he said: Who knoweth whether a man will be converted, and God forgive? ⟦s20⟧ Only let the conversion go first, and no delay of pardon follows. ⟦s21⟧ But of the day and hour of conversion no one knoweth but the Son alone, and he to whom the Son shall be willing to reveal it. ⟦s22⟧ For *the Spirit breatheth where he will; and thou hearest his voice, but thou knowest not whence he cometh, or whither he goeth [n: (Joan. III)]*. ⟦s23⟧ There follows: And leave a blessing behind him. ⟦s24⟧ There are three kinds [0346B] of divine blessing: the first he gives before him; the second he ministers beside him; the third he leaves behind him. ⟦s25⟧ There are also three kinds of good men: the first are the innocent; the second those who at once come to themselves again; the third those who at length repent. ⟦s26⟧ The innocent are those who always live without crime, who stand constantly before God, who walk continually in his presence. ⟦s27⟧ Those who at once come to themselves again are those who indeed fall, but soon the divine hand is stretched out to them, and they at once rise again. ⟦s28⟧ Those who at length repent are those who, resting long in their own dregs, at length by the divine nod rise up to penance. ⟦s29⟧ To the innocent the Lord gives the blessing before him; to those who at once come to themselves again, he ministers it beside him; to those who at length repent, he leaves it behind him. ⟦s30⟧ To the innocent [0346C] he gives the firstfruits of the blessing; to those who at once come to themselves again, its riches; to those who at length repent, its leavings. ⟦s31⟧ There follows: Sacrifice and libation to the Lord our God. ⟦s32⟧ These are the leavings of the blessing, namely the works of sacrifice and of libation. ⟦s33⟧ The sacrifice looks to the mortification of the flesh, the libation to the compunction of the mind. ⟦s34⟧ But because we have committed the wicked works of the flesh, because we have followed the unlawful joys of the mind, let us render contraries to contraries, so that we may both extinguish the works of the flesh through mortification, and the unlawful joys through compunction.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Otherwise: The sacrifice can be understood as right working, the libation as holy contemplation. ⟦s2⟧ In which sense the libation is attributed to God, the sacrifice to the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ For one must act rightly because the Lord is to be feared: of God [0346D] there is a tasting, whence he is also to be loved. ⟦s4⟧ There follows:

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Blow the trumpet in Sion; sanctify [sic: santificate] a fast, call an assembly, gather together the people, sanctify [sic: santificate] the Church, assemble the ancients, gather together the little ones and them that suck the breasts. ⟦s2⟧ Let the bridegroom go forth from his bed, and the bride out of her bridal chamber. ⟦s3⟧ Between the porch and the altar the priests, the ministers of the Lord, shall weep, and shall say: Spare, O Lord, spare thy people: and give not thy inheritance to reproach, that the nations should rule over them. ⟦s4⟧ Why do they say among the peoples: Where is their God?* ⟦s5⟧ The prophet still charges his people to press on in devout prayer, and every age, order, sex, condition humbly to apply itself to supplication, showing that they are subject to peril. ⟦s6⟧ Whence he counsels also that supplication be made in common. ⟦s7⟧ ALLEGORICALLY. ⟦s8⟧ The perils of the Church having been shown, [0347A] he exhorts them to hasten to the harbor of penance, lest perhaps they feel the hand of the Lord grow heavy upon them, *For it is a fearful thing to fall into the hand of the living God [n: (Hebr. X)]*. ⟦s9⟧ He says therefore: Blow the trumpet in Sion, etc. ⟦s10⟧ The exposition of these things we leave aside, because we have said a good many things about them elsewhere. ⟦s11⟧ The ancients are those perfect in virtue; the little ones, those endowed with simplicity and innocence; those that suck the breasts, the unlearned set under teaching. ⟦s12⟧ The ancients are assembled, inasmuch as they use reason; the little ones are gathered together, and those that suck, inasmuch as they are set under teaching, and serve under the rod.
