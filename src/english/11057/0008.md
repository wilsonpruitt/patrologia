## Brief Notes on Joel (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ ALLEGORICALLY. ⟦s2⟧ The discourse is of the ancient enemy, of the apostate angel, who by the incarnation of Christ, by the word of faith, by the grace of baptism was driven out into the dryness of the Gentile and of the Jewish people. ⟦s3⟧ The eastern sea is the elect; the utmost, the reprobate; the face, the intention; his hindmost part, the shutting-in. ⟦s4⟧ Or the eastern sea is the first-fruits of the election of the Gentiles; the utmost, the belief [0351B] of the Jews; his stench is the death of Antichrist; his rottenness, the sentence of judgment upon him.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ MORALLY. ⟦s2⟧ The land is the reprobate soul; pathless, that is, without the visitation of the elect angels; deserted, without the fruit of the virtues and the seed of good behavior. ⟦s3⟧ The casting out of the demon is the confession of sin; the eastern sea is the mind made bitter by compunction, made a rising sun by the devotion of contemplation. ⟦s4⟧ The utmost sea is the mind made bitter by the stench of conscience, swallowed up by the despair of pardon. ⟦s5⟧ Its face is the cunning intention of doing harm; its hindmost part, the fury and madness of triumphing. ⟦s6⟧ Its stench is the hatred of sin; its rottenness, the contempt of the world. ⟦s7⟧ There follows:

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Fear not, O land* (behold the consolation of the land), *rejoice, [0351C] and be glad; for the Lord hath magnified, that he should do.* ⟦s2⟧ Rejoice, O Judaea, over the confounding and the death of thine enemy. ⟦s3⟧ Rejoice, O Church, over the coming of thy Savior into the flesh. ⟦s4⟧ Rejoice, O faithful soul, over the coming of thy bridegroom into the mind. ⟦s5⟧ Be glad in the merit of the virtues; rejoice in the reward of things heavenly; be glad in the gifts of grace; rejoice in the crowns of glory. ⟦s6⟧ There follows:

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Fear not, ye beasts of the country; for the beautiful places of the desert have sprung forth; for the tree hath brought forth its fruit: the fig tree and the vine have yielded their strength.* ⟦s2⟧ HISTORICALLY. ⟦s3⟧ After the destruction of the enemy, after the shipwreck of the captivity, Judaea received back the face of the sun's former serenity, the covenant of plenty. ⟦s4⟧ But ALLEGORICALLY [0351D] the tree of the Lord's passion brought forth its fruit; the fig tree, the strength and sweetness of the resurrection. ⟦s5⟧ At the coming of the Paraclete the vine of the apostles gave its fruit of propagation. ⟦s6⟧ Thence the beautiful places of the desert sprang forth, because out of the imitation of the passion, out of the hope of the resurrection, out of the apostolic teaching and life of religion, glorious assemblies were propagated. ⟦s7⟧ The beasts of the country are men of the secular life. ⟦s8⟧ Let the beasts of the country therefore not be afraid, because the beautiful places of the desert have sprung forth, since through the teaching and the holiness of religious men secular men are drawn to reverence for the sacraments, to obedience to the precepts, to innocence of behavior, to eminence in counsels, to the imitation of the Lord's passion, to the hope of the heavenly resurrection, [0352A] to the solicitude and the fruit of religious propagation. ⟦s9⟧ There follows:

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And, ye daughters of Sion, rejoice in the Lord your God; for he hath given you a teacher of righteousness, and he will make the early and the latter rain to come down to you, as in the beginning. ⟦s2⟧ And the floors shall be filled with wheat, and the presses shall overflow with wine and oil. ⟦s3⟧ And I will restore to you the years which the locust, the grub, the mildew, the caterpillar hath eaten: my great strength, which I sent upon you. ⟦s4⟧ And ye shall eat, feeding, and shall be filled; and ye shall praise the name of the God of Israel, who hath done wondrous things with you; and my people shall not be confounded for evermore. ⟦s5⟧ And ye shall know that I am in the midst of Israel. ⟦s6⟧ I am the Lord your God, and there is none besides, and my people shall not be confounded for ever.* ⟦s7⟧ HISTORICALLY. [0352B] After the consolation of the land, of the fruits, of the trees, of the beasts, he passes over to the consolation of men, saying: And ye daughters of Sion, rejoice, etc. ⟦s8⟧ For I have given you a teacher of righteousness. ⟦s9⟧ The Hebrews assert that the teacher of righteousness is Hezekiah, of whom it is written: *He destroyed the high places, and broke the statues; and cut down the groves, and brake in pieces the brazen serpent which Moses had made [n: (IV Reg. XVIII)]*. ⟦s10⟧ And so after him there was none like him among all the kings of Juda, nor yet among those who were before him. ⟦s11⟧ And he clave to the Lord, and departed not from his footsteps, and did his commandments which the Lord had enjoined. ⟦s12⟧ Others of the Hebrews say Josiah, of whom it is written: *He did that which was right before the Lord, and walked in all the [0352C] ways of David his father: and turned not aside to the right hand or to the left [n: (II Par. XXXIV)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ ALLEGORICALLY. ⟦s2⟧ The teacher of righteousness is Christ; a teacher, who teacheth man knowledge; a teacher indeed, who found out all the way of discipline, and gave it to Jacob his servant, and to Israel his beloved. ⟦s3⟧ Of righteousness, whence it is written: *Master, we know that thou art true, and teachest the way of God in truth, and thou carest not for any man: for thou dost not regard the persons of men [n: (Matth. XXII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Of righteousness assuredly, because he shook his hands from every bribe; of righteousness truly, because *he rendereth to every man according to his works [n: (Matth. XVI)]*. ⟦s5⟧ Of righteousness, I say, because he justly blinds, justly justifies, justly reprobates, justly glorifies, justly damns. ⟦s6⟧ The early rain is the spiritual knowledge of the law; the latter, [0352D] the grace of the Gospel. ⟦s7⟧ Or the early rain is the utterances of himself and of his apostles; the latter, the expositions of the spiritual Fathers, the canons and the decrees; the floors are the minds of the faithful, made used to the yoke of discipline, worn by the paths of religion, polished by the traditions of regular discipline. ⟦s8⟧ The presses are those hearts which are set between hope and fear, having constancy below and patience above; constancy in temptation, patience in tribulation — in the temptation of vices, in the tribulation of scourges. ⟦s9⟧ For constancy presses down and holds off from above; patience lies beneath and bears the weight. ⟦s10⟧ Constancy holds off vices, patience bears up under conflicts. ⟦s11⟧ Between these two the holy mind, set as it were in a press, is pressed, is purged of dregs, is strained out: it is pressed by scourges, [0353A] purged of vices, strained free of idleness. ⟦s12⟧ It is pressed by calamity, purged of iniquity, strained free of vanity. ⟦s13⟧ For hence is drawn out the groan of pure confession, hence flow the tears of anxious compunction, hence stream the sighs of joyful devotion, hence the desires of the sweetest love are melted, hence are drawn out the droplets of the clearest contemplation. ⟦s14⟧ The wheat is the perfection of righteousness; the wine, the brightness of spiritual understanding; the oil, the sweetness of the purest conscience. ⟦s15⟧ There follows: And I will restore to you the years which the locust hath eaten, etc. ⟦s16⟧ For at the coming of Christ there are restored to us the years which the aforesaid pest had devoured; because in him he gave us, against the locust, true humility, saying: *Learn of me, for I am meek and humble of heart [n: (Matth. XI)]*. ⟦s17⟧ Against [0353B] the grub, perfect sobriety, saying: *Take heed lest your hearts be weighed down with surfeiting and drunkenness and the cares of this life [n: (Luc. XXI)]*. ⟦s18⟧ Against the mildew, perfect patience, saying: *If any man strike thee on thy right cheek, offer him the other also [n: (Matth. V)]*. ⟦s19⟧ Against the caterpillar, perfect chastity, saying: *Let your loins be girt and lamps burning [n: (Luc. XII)]*, etc. ⟦s20⟧ The locust therefore he exterminated, saying: *Blessed are the poor in spirit. ⟦s21⟧ Blessed are they that mourn [n: (Matth. V)]*. ⟦s22⟧ The grub, saying: *Blessed are the merciful [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s23⟧ The [sic: *Rubignem*], saying: *Blessed are the peacemakers. ⟦s24⟧ Blessed are the meek [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s25⟧ The caterpillar, saying: *Blessed are the clean of heart [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s26⟧ There follows: My great strength, which I sent upon you. ⟦s27⟧ Great indeed was the strength of Christ, whom the Father sent into the world, since he is *reaching from end [0353C] to end mightily, and ordering all things sweetly [n: (Sap. VIII)]*. ⟦s28⟧ Who overcame the devil, despoiled hell, blotted out the handwriting, brought back the captive, led the servant by the hand, kept his very self unharmed. ⟦s29⟧ There follows: And ye shall eat, feeding, and shall be filled, etc. ⟦s30⟧ Ye shall eat assuredly the food of sweetness, namely the teaching of righteousness; the food of strength, namely the bread of life. ⟦s31⟧ There follows: And my people shall not be confounded for ever. ⟦s32⟧ As if he should say: Because with me in the present they bear confusion and reproach, everlasting gladness shall be upon their heads: hence the Savior to his disciples: *You are,* he says, *they who have continued with me in my temptations; and I dispose to you, as my Father hath disposed to me, a kingdom (Luc. [0353D] XXII)*, etc. ⟦s33⟧ There follows:

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And it shall come to pass after this, I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh; and your sons and your daughters shall prophesy. ⟦s2⟧ Your old men shall dream dreams, and your young men shall see visions. ⟦s3⟧ Moreover upon my servants and my handmaids in those days I will pour out my spirit. ⟦s4⟧ And I will show wonders in heaven and in earth, blood, and fire, and vapor of smoke. ⟦s5⟧ The sun shall be turned into darkness, and the moon into blood, before the great and dreadful day of the Lord come; and it shall be: whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.* ⟦s6⟧ Here let the Jew Apella blush; here let him blush for his blindness and his madness, so that he who boasts himself a leader of the blind and to hold the lamp of the law may in this place catch out the darkness of his own error and the gloom of his own ignorance. ⟦s7⟧ Let my ass [0354A] lay down his packs for me; let him wait until we go up to the mountain, and after we have worshipped we shall return to him; and seeing rest, that it is good, and the land, that it is very good, let him bow his shoulder to carry. ⟦s8⟧ Let him tell me, I say, where the Lord poured out his spirit; let him tell where the sons, the old men, the young men saw prophecies, dreams, visions. ⟦s9⟧ But since he will somehow be able to unroll these things, let him at least tell where the Lord gave blood, fire, and vapor, where the sun was turned into darkness and the moon changed into blood. ⟦s10⟧ And when he shall not be able to show it, let the wisdom of Christ reign, glorified by her sons. ⟦s11⟧ Of the coming of Christ, therefore, the present letter is properly understood; the prophecy, closed as to the sending of the Spirit the Paraclete, is clearly unloosed. ⟦s12⟧ But first we must see what is said: And it shall come to pass [0354B] after this. ⟦s13⟧ What is *after this*? ⟦s14⟧ For what had he said above, to which what is subjoined ought to be referred? ⟦s15⟧ We must see, therefore, how the letter hangs together. ⟦s16⟧ For the prophet had above set forth in many ways the common misfortune of the human race; he had foretold the vows of the people, the laments of the priests, the squalor of the virgins, the sighs of the fathers. ⟦s17⟧ Then he set beneath the coming of Christ, the incarnation of the Word, the dispensation of the mystery for the liberation of the people, for the removal of the misfortune; and afterward he subjoined, for the illumination of the Gentiles, for the perfection of all things, saying: And it shall come to pass after this: I will pour out my spirit, etc. ⟦s18⟧ We read that the Lord took away of the spirit of Moses, and set what had been taken away upon the seventy elders. ⟦s19⟧ We read likewise that Elisha received the double spirit [0354C] of Elijah; but thus far we do not read that Christ poured out his Spirit. ⟦s20⟧ Here indeed he poured out; he did not take away from Moses, nor snatch the double portion from Elijah, but distributed his own bountifully. ⟦s21⟧ But it is to be noted what is said: He poured out. ⟦s22⟧ For first he poured, secondly he poured in, thirdly he poured out. ⟦s23⟧ He poured in paradise, he poured in in the desert, he poured out in the upper room. ⟦s24⟧ In paradise by giving the law of nature, in the desert the precepts and ceremonies of the written law, in the upper room the fullness of grace. ⟦s25⟧ Whence of the first it is said: *The Lord breathed into Adam [n: (Gen. II)]*. ⟦s26⟧ And of the second: *God called Moses out of the midst of the darkness [n: (Exod. XXIV)]*, etc. ⟦s27⟧ Whence *infula* is so called as though *intus fula*; because the spiritual understanding was shut up within the veil of the law, and overshadowed by the gloom of the rind. [0354D] But in the third, the Holy Spirit came down in tongues of fire, *because of his fullness we all have received [n: (Joan. I)]*. ⟦s28⟧ First then he poured, watering the floor of nature; secondly he poured in the torrent of teaching; thirdly he poured out the river of grace.
