## Brief Notes on Joel (cont.)

MORALLY. After the wonders given in heaven, after the light of the knowledge of oneself, after the smoke of devout compunction, after the blood of just mortification, after the fire of perfect love, there remains only the sweetness of contemplation. He says therefore: And it shall be — then, namely — everyone, whosoever shall call upon the name of the Lord shall be saved: because on the flowering little couch of contemplation the calling upon the name of the Lord is nothing else than the longed-for [0358D] attaining of salvation, the most desired uniting of bridegroom and bride. So had the bride been saved, so had she called upon the name of the Lord, who said: *Thy name is oil poured out [n: (Cant. I)]*. The oil, I say, of the name poured out the bride had received: which, because it is light, she had been illumined by its presence; because it is an unction, she had stood healed by its touch; because it is the bread of life, she had been satisfied by its embrace. Illumined by the knowledge of the spirit, healed from the vanity of the world, satisfied by the kiss of the Word. And this is the calling upon the name. This is the attaining of salvation, the receiving of kisses, the sharing of the little couch, the uniting of the Word and the soul. In which indeed everyone is saved; because with this light no one is blinded, with this strength none is made weak, with this salvation no one comes into peril. There follows:

[0359A] *For in mount Sion and in Jerusalem shall be salvation, as the Lord hath said, and in the residue whom the Lord shall call [n: (Joel. III)]*. *For behold, in those days and in that time, when I shall have turned the captivity of Juda and of Jerusalem, I will gather together all nations, and will bring them down into the valley of Josaphat: and I will plead with them there for my people and for my inheritance Israel, whom they have scattered among the nations, and have divided my land, and have cast lots upon my people. And they have set a boy in the brothel, and have sold a girl for wine that they might drink.* Let us discuss this chapter first morally; then let us pass over to the dark places of allegory and the pleasant things of tropology. The Jews in this place promise themselves — nay rather, they dream — that in the last time they will be gathered by the Lord and led back into Jerusalem. And not [0359B] content with such felicity, they assert that God himself will deliver into their hands the sons and daughters of the Romans, so that they may sell them, not to the Persians and the Ethiopians and the other nations which are near, but to the Sabaeans, a most remote people, because the Lord has spoken that he will avenge the injury of his people. So they, and our judaizers, who promise themselves a kingdom of a thousand years within the borders of Judaea, and a golden Jerusalem, and the blood of victims; and sons and grandsons, and incredible delights, and gates picked out with a variety of gems.

This place is most difficult according to anagogy, and admits of a manifold explanation, so that under tropology we refer all those things which have been said to what the apostles Peter and Paul referred them, that is, to when the Lord suffered and rose again. For it cannot [0359C] be that we should understand what goes before of the time of the passion and what follows of the day of judgment: especially since there follows, For behold, in those days and in that time,--and this little verse, joining what is below to what precedes, says that all things are to be accomplished in one time. Let it be said therefore: In mount Sion and in Jerusalem shall be salvation, as the Lord hath said, etc. The residue are the Apostles and the other disciples. Whence Isaiah: *Unless the Lord of Sabaoth had left us seed [n: (Isai. I)]*, etc. As the Lord hath said. Where, or through whom, did the Lord say it? Through Isaiah: *Out of Sion,* he says, *shall go forth the law, and the word of the Lord out of Jerusalem [n: (Isai. II)]*. And through the Psalmist: *The Lord loveth,* he says, *the gates of Sion above all the tabernacles of Jacob [n: (Psal. LXXXVI)]*. There follows: For behold, in those days, etc. For in the days [0359D] of the passion and of the resurrection, when the Lord turned the captivity of Juda and Jerusalem; when, that is, choosing out for himself some of the sons of Abraham he called them, and by calling justified them, and by gathering them together brought down all the nations into the valley of Josaphat; because, that is, by a just and gratuitous mercy *he made some vessels of mercy unto glory; others vessels of just wrath unto destruction and dishonor [n: (Rom. IX)]*. For he said to Moses: *I will have mercy on whom I will, and I will show mercy to whom I shall be merciful [n: (Exod. XXXIII)]*. Josaphat is interpreted *the judgment of the Lord*, by which the damnation of the reprobate is designated. In the time therefore of the conversion of the elect the multitude of the ungodly is reprobated; because in the moment and mystery of the Lord's passion stands both the cause and the sum of salvation and of perdition. [0360A] Whence the Apostle: *Whosoever we are that have been baptized in Christ Jesus, we have been baptized in his death [n: (Galat. VI)]*. Likewise the same: *For I,* he says, *bear about the marks of Jesus Christ in my body [n: (Ibid.)]*. Here the Lord to Moses: *When the angel shall see,* he says, *the blood upon the lintel and upon both the door posts, he shall pass over the door; and shall not suffer the destroyer to come into your houses and to hurt [n: (Exod. XII)]*. Hence the Lord through Ezekiel: *Everyone,* he says, *upon whom thou shalt see Thau, slay not [n: (Ezech. IX)]*. And the Apostle: *All things,* he says, *are almost cleansed with blood, and without the shedding of blood there is no remission [n: (Hebr. IX)]*.

There follows: And I will plead with them there, namely in the valley of Josaphat. For his people and for his inheritance, for the causes subjoined, the Lord pleads against the nations; [0360B] because thence the nations receive the judgment of damnation, whence they fell into the crimes that followed. This testimony looks to idolaters and heretics, who, seducing the people of the Lord, divided that people, and, dividing his land and parting it among themselves by many errors, compelled it to the [sic: *culturom*] of idols, so that some worshipped Jupiter, others Phoebus, others Juno, others Minerva, Rubigo, Anubis, the crocodile, and the ibis, owls, hawks, and storks. The heretics too divided the land of the Lord, that is, the Church, and cast lots upon the ecclesiastical people, when, seducing them and leading them away into the error of heresies, Arius made Arians, Sabellius Sabellians, Manes Manichaeans, and others made others disciples of their own error. There follows: And they have set a boy [0360C] in the brothel, and have sold a girl for wine that they might drink. Here he convicts each of them otherwise, namely the heretic and the idolater, upon three counts: of avarice, of lust, and of gluttony. Of avarice, because he cast lots upon the people of the Lord. Of lust, because he prostituted the boy. Of gluttony, because he sold the girl for wine.

ALLEGORICALLY. Each of them, deceiving the boy — that is, the ecclesiastical people, pure in faith, upright in hope, sincere in charity — by malignant persuasion and fraudulent promise, the idolater set him in the brothel of idols, and the heretic shut him up in the hovel of errors. And they sold a girl for wine that they might drink: because the soul still tender in faith and delicate in behavior, the idolater corrupting her by gifts and promises, the heretic [0360D] circumventing her by the poisoned coloring of words and seducing her by sophistic arguments, they made her over as chattel to heresy or to idolatry, receiving from her defeat the wine of gladness.

MORALLY. There are two spiritual captivities: namely the captivity of Juda, that is, of confession; and the captivity of Jerusalem, that is, of contemplation. When contemplation is taken captive, at once the soul is assailed by cares, occupied with business, harassed by tumults and unlawful desires. But when confession goes into captivity, then, unhappy, it goes down into the darkness of vices and the calamity of despair. Then the demons divide the land of the Lord, that is, the faithful soul, by vices; they cast lots upon the people of the virtues, the lots of sins. Then they prostitute the boy, that is, the purity of the mind, [0361A] to unlawful desires. Then the [sic: *pucilam*], that is, the innocence of the heart, they sell for wine, that is, for temporal gladness and earthly delight. But when in the [sic: *dic*] of salvation and in the acceptable time the Dayspring from on high deigns to visit, he pours in the knowledge of truth, and then confession is called back; he bestows the love of virtue, and contemplation is brought back. And then the nations of the vices are led down into the valley of judgment: and because they acted wickedly against the choir of the virtues, they snatch up the road of destruction. We can refer the foregoing to the day of judgment; but since we hasten on to other matters, it seems good to leave these things to the reader's own discretion. There follows:

*But what have I to do with you, O Tyre and Sidon, and all the coast of the Philistines? Will you render vengeance to me? And if you shall avenge yourselves against me, quickly and speedily [0361B] will I return your recompense upon your own head. For you have taken away my silver and my gold: and my desirable things and most beautiful you have carried into your temples. And the children of Juda and the children of Jerusalem you have sold to the children of the Greeks, that you might [sic: faceritis] them far from their own borders. Behold, I will raise them up out of the place wherein you have sold them, and I will turn your recompense upon your own head. And I will sell your sons and your daughters into the hands of the children of Juda; and they shall sell them to the Sabaeans, a far-off nation, because the Lord hath spoken.* These things they judge to be said for the Jews against Tyre and Sidon and the coasts of the Philistines, because in the time of the Jewish captivity, when they were conquered by the Romans, they persecuted the people of God — nay rather, in that people God himself, who was set over the people. I therefore, he says, will render [0361C] to you what you have done to my people, nay rather to me, because you have taken away my silver and my gold, that is, the vessels of the temple, and whatever was most precious, and have consecrated them to your idols. But history relates that the Chaldaeans did this rather, who set the vessels of the temple of the Lord in their own temple. Whence Belshazzar afterward drinks out of the bowls; and straightway his kingdom is transferred to the Medes and the Persians. But since these things are said to be about to come to pass after the great and dreadful day of the Lord, which the apostles interpret of the resurrection of the Lord, and the Hebrews defer to the future time of judgment, it is rather to be understood of the Romans: that Vespasian and Titus, having built at Rome the temple of peace, consecrated in its shrine the vessels of the temple and all the offerings: [0361D] which the Greek and the Roman history relates.
