## Brief Notes on Joel (cont.)

But mystically the matter is of the coming of Christ into the flesh. By Tyre and Sidon and the Philistines may be understood idolaters and heretics; if it be of his coming to judgment, demons; if of his coming into the mind, vices and carnal passions. And to all of them the Lord will speedily and quickly render their recompense, since of the first coming too it is read: *He that believeth not is already judged [n: (Joan. III)]*. Likewise: *Now is the judgment of the world [n: (Joan. XII)]*, etc. And of the second: *Go, ye cursed, into everlasting fire [n: (Matth. XXV)]*. And of the third: *O poor little one, tossed with tempest, without any comfort. Behold, I will lay thy stones in order, and will lay thy foundations with sapphires [n: (Isai. LIV)]*, etc. The silver is eloquence; the gold, wisdom; the desirable things, the most religious; [0362A] the most beautiful, the most honorable. What is honorable looks to reputation, what is religious to conscience. The temples are the worships of idols, or the sects of heretics, or the habits of vices. The children of Juda, the actives; the children of Jerusalem, the contemplatives. The Greeks, liars, that is, men who speak falsely, or the motions of the mind. For the Greeks are interpreted *is and is not*, by which are signified deceit and lying; and by these things which we have said above, their company, or the choir of the virtues, is set far from its own borders. But God, the Lord of vengeance, will quickly and speedily render the recompense: he who turns back the retribution of his enemies. Whence there also follows: Behold, I will gather them together, etc. And I will sell your sons and your daughters into the hands of the children of Juda; and they shall sell them to the Sabaeans, a far-off nation. The children of the aforesaid, [0362B] allegorically, are the disciples of the Gentiles or of the heretics; morally, the motions of the vices. The hands of the children of Juda are confessions and the works of penitence. Wholesomely, therefore, are the aforesaid delivered into these hands: because both these return through the confession of faith to the grace of baptism, and those, through the confession of sin and the works of penitence, fly up to the pardon of guilt, to the grace of obedience, to the purity of innocence. And hence they sell them to the Sabaeans, a far-off nation, because they apply the aforesaid, purified in the aforesaid manner, to the hatred of sin, to the abjection of self, to the contempt of the world. For Saba is [sic: *intepretatur*] *captivity.* First then iniquity is taken captive by the hatred of sin, secondly curiosity by the abjection of self, thirdly vanity by the contempt of the world. Otherwise, the aforesaid sell the aforesaid, when they have been exercised, to the Sabaeans, because after the works of penitence, after the devotion [0362C] of obedience, after fruits worthy of righteousness, they lift up the aforesaid to the rest of contemplative gladness. Saba is called a far-off nation. Whence also the contemplative household is called a remote nation: because it is set apart far from the crowds of vices, from the cares of business, and from the exercise of actions. There follows:

*Proclaim ye this among the nations: Sanctify a war, rouse up the strong. Let all the men of war draw near and come up; cut your ploughs into swords and your mattocks into spears. Let the weak say: For I am strong; break forth, and come, all ye nations round about, and gather yourselves together. There the Lord will make thy strong ones to fall. Let the nations arise and come up into the valley of Josaphat, for there I will sit to judge all nations round about. [0362D] Put ye in the sickles, for the harvest is ripe. Come and go down, for the press is full. The presses overflow, for their malice is multiplied. Peoples, peoples in the valley of destruction, for the day of the Lord is near in the valley of destruction. The sun and the moon are darkened; and the stars also have withdrawn their brightness.* The Jews refer this place to Gog and Magog, most savage nations (of whom we have spoken above), thinking that in the last time, when Jerusalem shall have been restored under the empire of a thousand years, they will come against the people of God, and will fall in the valley of Josaphat, which is situated toward the eastern quarter. For they say that the time of their slaying has come, and that the vintage of the shedding of blood is at hand. These things the Jews dream in vain. But let us, laying open the truth [0363A] of the matter, pursue these things allegorically, and then pass over to searching out the pleasant secrets of tropology. If the present passage treats of the coming of the Lord into the flesh, the letter is fittingly read thus. The prophet, then, in the present passage exhorts the divine seers and his fellow-prophets to declare from day to day the salvation of the Lord, to announce among the nations his glory, among all peoples his wonders: to announce, I say, the incarnation of the Word, the passion and resurrection of Jesus Christ; upon the apostles the coming of the Paraclete, their preachings, the power of signs, the grace of miracles, the conflicts of words, the struggles of contests, the sufferings of bodies: in all things a manly constancy, and a swift victory over the nations. And this is what he says: Proclaim ye this among the nations. As if he should say: [0363B] Spare not, be not silent. Thus for the nations to be conquered will be glory: to succumb in this way is indeed a glorious victory. Sanctify a war. For if that whose end is good is itself also good, this war too is without doubt holy, whose end is holy. And to sanctify this war is to endow the war with holiness. Is not a war sanctified where virtue triumphs and vice is slain? Is not, I say, a war sanctified where the victory is reckoned to the vanquished, the crown to him that succumbs, where the prize is ascribed to him that flees, the kingdom to him that is overcome? Rouse up therefore the strong, namely the apostles and apostolic men. What is it to rouse up, but to stir upward? What is it to stir upward, but to announce men lifted up on high, fortified with virtues, adorned with miracles? Let the men of war therefore, namely the preachers [0363C] of the word, the leaders of the heavenly warfare, draw near to preaching, and come up upon the necks of the nations. Let them come up, I say, upon the yoke of the nations' faith through the power of prodigies, through the flashing of miracles. Then the prophetic discourse turns to them, saying: Cut your ploughs into swords, and your mattocks into spears. What are the ploughs, but mortified members? What is it to cut ploughs into swords, but often to set mortified members against death for the care of the faith, for the power of the word? This those men had done of whom it is read in the Acts of the Apostles: *The apostles went rejoicing from the presence of the council, because they were accounted worthy to suffer reproach for the name of Jesus [n: (Act. V)]*. Hence the Apostle: *For I think that God hath set forth us apostles the last, and as it were appointed to death, [0363D] because we are made a spectacle to the world, and to angels, and to men [n: (I Cor. IV)]*. Likewise, the same: *Even unto this hour,* he says, *we hunger and thirst, and are naked, and are buffeted, and are unsettled, and we labor, working with our own hands: we are cursed, and we bless; we suffer persecution, and we bear it; we are blasphemed, and we entreat; we are made as the refuse of this world, the offscouring of all even until now [n: (ibid.)]*. Behold how the plough is cut. Now let us see how it is turned into a sword: *It is good,* he says, *for me rather to die, than that any man should make void my glory. For if I preach the Gospel, it is no glory to me: for a necessity lieth upon me. For woe is unto me if I preach not the Gospel! But if I do this willingly, [0364A] I have a reward. But if against my will, a dispensation is committed to me [n: (I Cor. IX)]*. The mattocks are the various compunctions of the mind. The spears are severe threatenings. And what is it to cut mattocks into spears, but out of the various compunction of the mind to conceive the ardor of faith, the power of the word, the force of sentences? Hence the Bridegroom to the bride in the Canticles: *Thine eyes,* he says, *are as the fishpools in Hesebon, which are in the gate of the daughter of the multitude [n: (Cant. VII)]*. The eyes of the Bride are the brightness of faith and the understanding of the divine utterance. Hesebon is interpreted *the girdle of grief:* the fishpools of Hesebon are the different kinds of compunction. The eyes of the Bride therefore are as the fishpools in Hesebon: because they are the more made bright from that source in which they are the more frequently washed in the aforesaid pools. The sword is a manly conception [0364B] concerning the present: the spear, a wholesome threatening concerning the future. Teachers therefore, through contempt of self and mortification of the flesh, receive from God the confidence of fraternal reproof. Through devotion: the power of compunction and the grace of threatening There follows: Let the weak say: For I am strong. Because it is written: *When I am weak, then am I stronger and powerful [n: (II Cor. XII)]*. Likewise it is said: *Strength is made perfect in weakness [n: (ibid.)]*.
