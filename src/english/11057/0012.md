## Brief Notes on Joel (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Break forth from the lurking places of the temples, from the corners of the idols, and come by the steps of faith and the fruits of penitence, out of the circuit in which you have walked as ungodly men: you who, despising the truth, have long groped after falsehood. ⟦s2⟧ Gather yourselves together [0364C] within the nets of the divine word, within the sheepfolds of faith, within the enclosures of the Lord's eye. ⟦s3⟧ There the Lord of faith will make his strong ones to fall, because, that is, through the ardor and the power of the divine utterance, teachers will subject to themselves kings, magnates, wise men, philosophers. ⟦s4⟧ Hence Isaiah: *All the flocks of Cedar,* he says, *shall be gathered together to thee: the rams of Nabaioth shall minister to thee [n: (Isa. LX)]*. ⟦s5⟧ Hence elsewhere: *The glory of Libanus is given to him, the beauty of Carmel and of Saron [n: (Isa. XXXV)]*. ⟦s6⟧ Likewise another: *The nation and kingdom that will not serve thee shall perish [n: (Jer. XXVII)]*. ⟦s7⟧ But Isaiah: *The glory of Libanus shall come to thee, the fir tree, the box tree, the pine together, to adorn the place of my sanctification [n: (Isa. LX)]*. ⟦s8⟧ The same in the same place: *There shall come to thee bowed down the children of them that humbled thee, and shall adore the steps of thy feet, all [0364D] they that detracted thee [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s9⟧ Since then it is of such moment to yield to the victor, of such profit for men of war to succumb, let the nations therefore arise and come up into the valley of Josaphat, that is, into the lowliness of judgment. ⟦s10⟧ For Josaphat is interpreted *the judgment of the Lord; for there I will sit,* saith the Lord, *to judge all the nations round about [n: (Joel. IX)]*. ⟦s11⟧ Hence he himself in the Gospel: *For judgment,* he says, *I came into this world, that they who see not may see, and they who see may become blind [n: (Joan. IX)]*. ⟦s12⟧ There follows the voice of the Savior exhorting his apostles to the gathering of the new crops, to the conversion of the nations, and saying: Put ye in the sickles, for the harvest is ripe. ⟦s13⟧ See how great is the agreement of the prophecy with the evangelical sentence. ⟦s14⟧ For the Savior in the Gospel speaks thus to the apostles: *The harvest indeed is great, [0365A] but the laborers few [n: (Matth. IX)]*. ⟦s15⟧ Likewise in the present prophecy: When the harvest shall be ripe, put ye in the sickles. ⟦s16⟧ For there are three sickles of those who take up peoples into the faith of the Trinity, who send new crops into the Lord's barns. ⟦s17⟧ The first is the preaching of the word; the second, the manner of catechizing; the third, the sacrament of baptism. ⟦s18⟧ As if he should say: Preach, instruct, wash. ⟦s19⟧ Preach the rule of faith, instruct unto penitence, wash unto pardon. ⟦s20⟧ There follows the voice of the Lord himself exhorting his disciples, in the care of faith and the teaching of the word, to the struggle of martyrdom. ⟦s21⟧ Come, he says, and go down. ⟦s22⟧ Come with the steps of preaching, go down to the labor and the humility of a most fruitful passion, because through the suffering of their bodies and the power of miracles many thousands of neophytes [0365B] will receive with you the [sic: *gloriossima*] rewards of the glorious contest; and they who shall be sharers of the sorrow shall be made partakers also of the joy. ⟦s23⟧ And this is what he says: For the press is full: the presses overflow. ⟦s24⟧ But what he says, Their malice is multiplied, if it is a matter of the neophytes, is rightly understood thus, as if he should say: Malice is multiplied, and carried through even to its end, beyond which it ought not to have gone: for which a different sentence has here set a limit. ⟦s25⟧ But if it is of the reprobate, thus: Malice is multiplied, that is, brought to its heap and to its point, so that, namely, they may be cast out from the glory of the kingdom and from the sheepfold of faith; so that the elect, harassed by the malice and cruelty of these men, may be brought into joys, crowned with glory and honor through the merit of martyrdom. ⟦s26⟧ Behold the agreement of the Gospel and the prophecy. ⟦s27⟧ The Savior says to the apostles in [0365C] the Gospel: *Lift up your eyes, and see the countries, for they are already white unto the harvest [n: (Joan. IV)]*. ⟦s28⟧ He himself in the present passage through the prophet: Come, he says, and go down, for the press is full: the presses overflow, etc. ⟦s29⟧ There follows: Peoples, peoples in the valley of destruction. ⟦s30⟧ The repetition of *peoples* is an urging of the work, a commending of the business. ⟦s31⟧ As if he should say: Peoples, peoples in the valley of destruction, that is, in the lowliness of judgment, in the discernment of faith, life is to be sought, the cause of salvation, the sum of righteousness. ⟦s32⟧ And this is what follows: For the day of the Lord is near in the valley of destruction: for the lowliness of right confession is followed by the prosperity of eternal retribution and the brightness of blessed contemplation. ⟦s33⟧ There follows: The sun and the moon are darkened, and [0365D] the stars have withdrawn their brightness. ⟦s34⟧ The sun is the philosophy of the world; the moon, secular power; the stars, the other powers The sun and the moon indeed are darkened in the lowliness of judgment: because from the moment when the wise of this world and the powerful of this age came to the brightness of faith, they at once acknowledged the darkness of their own error. ⟦s35⟧ And the stars withdrew their brightness, because when they beheld the disciples of Christ, in the cause of faith, snatching up contempt of the world, abjection of themselves, thirsting for the struggle of martyrdom; when they beheld them shining with signs, adorned with prodigies, glorious with miracles, then at once, turned from their elation, cast down from earthly dignity, following the humility of Christ, they gave their necks to the faith. ⟦s36⟧ And this is for the stars to withdraw their brightness: for the powerful of this age, the magnates of this world, by embracing the humility [0366A] of faith, to despise in every way the flower of the world. ⟦s37⟧ So much for the coming of Christ in the flesh. ⟦s38⟧ But if it be read of the last coming, as it pleases some, the interpretation will in part be easy: but the assignment made by certain men will be difficult, and absurd, and unfitting. ⟦s39⟧ For in that strict judgment what will the ploughs be? what the swords? what the mattocks? what the spears, the sickles, the presses? ⟦s40⟧ But those who assert this run to the place of allegory. ⟦s41⟧ We, however, that we may humor them, say that the aforesaid nations are for this reason brought down into the valley of judgment: that they may be slain and fall, that they may be judged by the Lord. ⟦s42⟧ The sorrow of which day, and the torments of those who perish, neither the sun nor the moon nor the other stars will be able to look upon; but they will withdraw their brightness, and will not dare to look upon the severity of him who judges and renders each man's work upon his own [0366B] head. ⟦s43⟧ Not because they are more merciful than the judgment of God, but because every creature, in the torments of others, is greatly afraid concerning its own judgment. ⟦s44⟧ There follows:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the Lord shall roar out of Sion, and utter his voice from Jerusalem, and the heavens and the earth shall be moved. ⟦s2⟧ And the Lord is the hope of his people and the strength of the children of Israel. ⟦s3⟧ And you shall know that I am the Lord your God,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Let us follow up the vengeance begun, and the explanation concerning the last coming. ⟦s5⟧ When the brightness of the sun and moon and of all the stars shall have been exchanged for darkness, the Lord will roar out of Sion after the manner of a lion, or will cry out, and his voice will be so lofty and so terrible that the hinges of the heavens and the foundations of the lands are shaken. ⟦s6⟧ And although he will be so severe toward those who are to be punished, he will nevertheless be merciful toward his own people, and will give them [0366C] strength — those who are called children of Israel, namely as seeing God with the mind: who have not walked by crooked ways, but, walking in the way of Christ, have done all things aright. ⟦s7⟧ Then both those who will be punished in Gehenna and those who will be taken up into glory will know that the Lord dwells in his watchtower Sion, and in Christ his holy mountain: namely in him who had prepared himself a dwelling worthy of God.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But if it be read of the first coming, the explanation will be easy and fitting, thus. ⟦s2⟧ It is no wonder if in the lowliness of judgment, through the power of the word and the discernment of faith, the nations subject themselves to the Son of God, who is the living word of God, and effectual, and more piercing than any two-edged sword [n: (Hebr. IV)]. ⟦s3⟧ Living, because he is not changed: effectual, because [0366D] he does not fail: piercing, because he is not deceived. ⟦s4⟧ He is not changed in his promise: he does not fail in his deed: he is not deceived in his judgment. ⟦s5⟧ His promise does not die by forgetfulness, his working is not overcome by difficulty: his judgment is not deceived by ambiguity. ⟦s6⟧ He promises truly, he acts mightily, he discerns subtly. ⟦s7⟧ He is living, that thou mayest believe: effectual, that thou mayest hope: piercing, that thou mayest fear. ⟦s8⟧ He is living in precepts and prohibitions: effectual in promises and threatenings: piercing in judgments and condemnations. ⟦s9⟧ Since therefore the word of God is living, it must be believed that he promises true things: since he is effectual, it must be believed that he brings his promises to fulfillment: since he is piercing and cannot be deceived, it must be mourned that one has offended him, and for the rest one must beware of offending. ⟦s10⟧ In that word three things are to be considered: sound, voice, [0367A] understanding. ⟦s11⟧ In the sound, the noise; in the voice, the sense; in the understanding, the perfection of the intellect. ⟦s12⟧ The noise looks to threatening: the voice to consolation: the understanding to the perfection of knowledge. ⟦s13⟧ The noise therefore to the torment of penitence, the voice to the grace of pardon, the understanding to the brightness of glory. ⟦s14⟧ And this is what he says: The Lord shall roar out of Sion, etc. ⟦s15⟧ The Lord's roaring is the noise of the word. ⟦s16⟧ Whence these three: roaring, voice, knowledge. ⟦s17⟧ The roaring is made by threatening, the voice by consolation, the knowledge by contemplation. ⟦s18⟧ By threatening he draws to penitence, by consoling he calls back to grace, by contemplating he raises up to glory. ⟦s19⟧ Through the roaring the earth is moved to penitence, through the voice the heavens are moved to grace, through the understanding we know thee, the one only and true God, and Jesus Christ whom thou hast sent. [0367B] And this is what is said: And you shall know that I am the Lord your God, dwelling in my holy mountain.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But if the present prophecy be understood of the coming of Christ into the mind, this will be the sum of the tropology. ⟦s2⟧ One must go into the valley of judgment; there the cause of salvation, the form of penitence, the rule of righteousness must be sought. ⟦s3⟧ There the enemy is destroyed, the citizen set free, he that is raised up is exalted, he that is exalted is made perfect. ⟦s4⟧ There are indeed three judgments: namely one's own, the human, and the divine. ⟦s5⟧ One's own is made out of compunction of heart and human penitence: the human out of public discipline, the divine making manifest things hidden and secret. ⟦s6⟧ Through one's own the enemy is destroyed and the citizen set free. ⟦s7⟧ Through the human he is polished and exalted. ⟦s8⟧ Through the divine he is perfected to a nicety. ⟦s9⟧ Each of these judgments is deservedly said to be set in [0367C] a valley, because each presses down and humbles the mind, each strikes the heart of man with fear and torments it in various ways. ⟦s10⟧ The first humbles the mind by the terror of Gehenna, the second presses down the spirit by the weight of discipline, the third lays the soul low by the love of righteousness. ⟦s11⟧ In the first judgment, therefore, comes the victory of the nations and the death of the strong; in the second, the abundance of the presses and the grace of gifts; in the third, the hope of the people and the strength of the children. ⟦s12⟧ The day of the Lord is said to be near the valley of judgment. ⟦s13⟧ The day of the Lord is the day of penitence, the day of discipline, the day of glory. ⟦s14⟧ Near the first valley is the day of penitence, near the second the day of discipline, near the third the day of glory. ⟦s15⟧ Hence the Psalmographer: *Show forth from day to day his salvation [n: (Psal. XCV)]*. [0367D] From the day, namely, of penitence, the day of discipline: from the day of discipline, the day of glory. ⟦s16⟧ And this is what is said: And you shall know that I am the Lord your God, dwelling in Sion my holy mountain. ⟦s17⟧ There follows:

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And Jerusalem shall be holy, and strangers shall pass through it no more. ⟦s2⟧ And it shall come to pass in that day: the mountains shall drop down sweetness, and the hills shall flow with milk. ⟦s3⟧ And through all the brooks of Juda waters shall go: and a fountain shall come forth out of the house of the Lord, and shall water the torrent of thorns.* ⟦s4⟧ These things the Jews and our judaizers refer to the fables of the thousand years: when they suppose that Christ will dwell in Sion, and that in a golden and jewelled Jerusalem the peoples of the saints will be gathered together, so that they who in this present age have been oppressed by all the nations may in this same age rule over all nations. ⟦s5⟧ This the fable of the Jews [0368A] dreams: while the truth furnishes us with something far otherwise. ⟦s6⟧ Jerusalem signifies the Church of this present time: it signifies also the faithful soul: and also that which is above, free, our mother [n: (Gal. IV)].
