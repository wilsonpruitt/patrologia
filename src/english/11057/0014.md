## Brief Notes on Joel (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And it shall come to pass in that day — that is, in that brightness of contemplation — the mountains shall drop down sweetness, and the hills shall flow with milk, and through all the brooks of Juda waters shall go. ⟦s2⟧ The mountains are the sublime things of contemplations; the hills, [0371B] the works of innocence; the brooks, the exercises of penitence; the sweetness is perfect love; the milk is holy devotion; the water, devout compunction. ⟦s3⟧ In that day, therefore — that is, in that felicity of union — the bride, through the joy of contemplation, receives the sweetness of perfect love: descending from which, and returning to herself, she flows with the milk of devotion. ⟦s4⟧ Whence, crucifying herself out of love for the things above, she is plunged into the waters of compunction, and coming forth thence is exercised in harsh penitence; after these things she is drawn to the works of innocence, and at last she flies away to those of contemplation, saying. ⟦s5⟧ *For what have I in heaven? and besides thee what have I desired upon earth? [n: (Psal. LXXII.)]* Likewise: *My soul hath chosen hanging [n: (Job VII)]*. ⟦s6⟧ Then the fountain comes forth out of the house of the Lord, and waters the torrent of thorns; because the abundance and the privilege of spiritual [0372A] grace flows out of the secret joy of a most pure conscience, blunting the stings of vices, restraining the movement of temptations, calming the gust of desires, and polishing the seat of the affections. ⟦s7⟧ Then ignorance is put to flight, then carnal concupiscence is brought to ruin; because the mind is illumined by the brightness of contemplation, and concupiscence is healed by the perfection of love. ⟦s8⟧ For Egypt is interpreted *darkness,* by which ignorance is designated: Idumaea, *earthly things,* by which carnal concupiscence is designated. ⟦s9⟧ But Judaea and Jerusalem shall be inhabited for ever. ⟦s10⟧ Judaea, *confession;* Jerusalem is called *the vision of peace.* ⟦s11⟧ For the devout and perfect mind, either descending to the knowledge of itself, confesses its offenses to God through the humility of compunction; or, ascending to the knowledge of God through the grace of contemplation, [0372B] is raised up to the vision of peace, and then in the voice of exultation and of confession there comes to be in her the sound of one feasting. ⟦s12⟧ Never does the faithful soul forsake Judaea; because either, pricked with compunction, she confesses her crimes, or, devout, she gives thanks for the gifts of grace, or, raised on high, she pours forth a hymn as she receives her rewards. ⟦s13⟧ Then too the Bridegroom cleanses the blood which hitherto could not be cleansed, because through the fellowship of union the Bridegroom bestows upon the bride the privilege of perfection, so that nature may feel against nature, that which nature naturally withstands. ⟦s14⟧ And then the Lord of nature, by the privilege of grace, dwells in Sion, that is, in the watchtower of glory. ⟦s15⟧ May he deign to purge our nature, and to bestow upon us his own grace, unto the glory of that same sublimity. ⟦s16⟧ Who, three and one, liveth and reigneth Amen.
