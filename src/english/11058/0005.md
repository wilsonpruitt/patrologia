## Little Notes on Lamentations (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The Lord is become as an enemy. ⟦s2⟧ We have made [0274A] the reason why he should be an enemy, who before was not an enemy, because he hates not that which he himself created, but that which we have made. ⟦s3⟧ There follows: He hath cast down her ramparts; he hath destroyed her fortifications. ⟦s4⟧ The ramparts of the soul are one thing, her fortifications another. ⟦s5⟧ The ramparts pertain to comeliness, the fortifications to safekeeping. ⟦s6⟧ So there are certain gifts of graces which adorn, as is prophecy, kinds of tongues, the interpretation of speeches; and certain which fortify, as are faith, hope, charity. ⟦s7⟧ The former, if they be present, furnish an ornament to the virtues; the latter, if they be lacking, a peril. ⟦s8⟧ The former, even if they be lacking, do not for all that hinder salvation; without the latter men cannot attain to salvation. ⟦s9⟧ And well is it first said: He hath cast down the ramparts, and then subjoined: he hath destroyed the fortifications; because, when a sinful soul is abandoned, first [0274B] there are taken from her the gifts which were given for manifestation, and then the foundations of faith, hope and charity are overthrown. ⟦s10⟧ He hath cast down, he says, the ramparts. ⟦s11⟧ What is lofty is cast down; what is solid is destroyed: because, as has been said, the ramparts pertain to glory, the fortifications to protection. ⟦s12⟧ There follows: And he hath filled in the daughter of Juda the humbled man and the humbled woman — that is, he humbled the spirit and the flesh in man by filling them with misery and grief, so that they who would not be humbled well through virtue might be humbled through affliction. ⟦s13⟧ Or by the humbled man and the humbled woman we may understand sense and thought, so that she is driven with reproaches within and without, until, confounded, she return to penitence. ⟦s14⟧ This may also be referred especially to the Jewish people. ⟦s15⟧ For the prophet, considering how [0274C] God, after the casting out of the first man, willing to have mercy upon the human race, chose this people alone, that in it he might prepare the beginnings of our salvation; how he tended it with the commandments of the law that was given, and everywhere exalted it by his own protection; and after all these things cast it off when it sinned and transgressed — and so, reckoning a second to the first adjection, he laments, saying: The Lord is become as an enemy, he hath cast down her ramparts, he hath destroyed her fortifications. ⟦s16⟧ For when he took away from the people of the Jews prophecy, and doctrine, and the signs of miracles, he cast down as it were her ramparts. ⟦s17⟧ But when he permitted them to be blinded through unbelief, and to be strangers from the hope of his permission and of his inheritance, he destroyed as it were her fortifications. ⟦s18⟧ There follows: [0274D] And he hath filled in the daughter of Juda the humbled man and the humbled woman. ⟦s19⟧ What shall we understand by the humbled man, but the order of prelates; and what by the humbled woman, but the subject people? ⟦s20⟧ For both were humbled at the coming of Christ, because the truth which they had preached and believed was to come, they denied when it was present. ⟦s21⟧ Concerning whom it is further subjoined.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And he hath destroyed his tent as a garden, etc. ⟦s2⟧ The tent is without, the tabernacle within: as also the tabernacle of the covenant, into which Moses entered to consult the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ Rightly therefore by the tent is signified the people, by the tabernacle the order of prelates. ⟦s4⟧ For when God so exalted that ancient people with temporal glory, he spread out as it were a tent for comeliness. ⟦s5⟧ But when, enlightening certain [0275A] of them unto the knowledge of his secrets, he held them worthy also of his familiar converse, he built as it were a tabernacle to dwell in. ⟦s6⟧ But because afterward, coming in the flesh, he both deprived that people of the knowledge of the truth, its sins requiring it, and then scattered it by the Romans among all the nations of the world, rightly is it now said: He hath destroyed his tent, he hath demolished his tabernacle. ⟦s7⟧ And it is to be noted that he says the tent is not demolished but destroyed, and asserts that the tabernacle is not destroyed but demolished. ⟦s8⟧ For what is demolished is pulled down gradually and secretly. ⟦s9⟧ What therefore in this place is expressed by demolition, but hidden hatred? ⟦s10⟧ The tabernacle therefore was demolished and the tent destroyed, because unless envy had driven the Scribes and Pharisees, the teachers of the people, [0275B] to the slaying of the Saviour, the vengeance of captivity would not have destroyed that people so cruelly. ⟦s11⟧ And see by what likeness he says the tent was destroyed: as a garden, he says. ⟦s12⟧ As a garden is laid waste when, its fruits having been gathered out of it, the hedge is destroyed and the guard removed, so from that people, after it ceased to bear the fruit of justice, the guard and protection of God at once withdrew; according to the prophecy of Isaias saying: The daughter of Sion shall be left as a shade in a vineyard, and as a hut in a cucumber-field. ⟦s13⟧ And the Lord himself in the Gospel speaks to the Jews, saying: *The kingdom of God shall be taken away from you, and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruit thereof [n: (Matth. XXI)]*. ⟦s14⟧ For the barren garden, and not bearing fruit, that is the Synagogue of the Jews, is laid waste; and the garden bearing [0275C] fruit, that is the Church of the Gentiles, is fortified and enclosed, as is said in the Canticle of Canticles: *A garden enclosed, my sister, a garden enclosed, a fountain sealed up [n: (Cant. IV)]*. ⟦s15⟧ And well does he call her sister whom he names an enclosed garden, because the Church of the Gentiles was born by faith out of the Synagogue, from which also Christ took flesh: yet nevertheless that same Synagogue, because it had not the fruit of justice, he laid waste as a barren garden. ⟦s16⟧ The mother therefore of Christ, that is the Synagogue, is a garden laid waste. ⟦s17⟧ The sister of Christ, that is the Church, an enclosed garden. ⟦s18⟧ There follows: The Lord hath delivered over to oblivion in Sion the festival and the sabbath. ⟦s19⟧ For the observances of the law were certain shadows of things to come; and therefore, after the [0275D] Truth itself came, God no longer admits the former things unto his worship. ⟦s20⟧ For he calls him as it were forgetful, because he no more commands those things to be rendered to him. ⟦s21⟧ There follows: *Unto reproach and unto the indignation of his fury, the king and the priest.* ⟦s22⟧ Understand: he hath delivered them over. ⟦s23⟧ By these two persons was that people governed, namely the legal and the priestly. ⟦s24⟧ And the words are fitting: honour to the king, devotion pertains to the priest; but now against honour is set reproach, against devotion indignation. ⟦s25⟧ And by their sacrifices God is not appeased, but is angered. ⟦s26⟧ As it is said by Isaias: *The blood of he-goats, and of calves, and of rams I would not: incense is an abomination to me. ⟦s27⟧ When you came before my sight, who required these things at your hands? [n: (Isa. I.)]* And by the Psalmist: *I will not take,* [0276A] he says, *calves out of thy house, nor he-goats out of thy flocks. ⟦s28⟧ Offer to God the sacrifice of praise, and pay thy vows to the most High [n: (Psal. XLIX)]*.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But if we turn the things said above to the moral sense, by the tent we may not unfittingly understand good action, which is spread abroad without for comeliness. ⟦s2⟧ But by the tabernacle, conscience, in which each one abides with himself in a certain familiar and domestic converse. ⟦s3⟧ For that the tabernacle signifies conscience, the Psalmist makes plain when he says: *The voice of rejoicing and of salvation is in the tabernacles of the just [n: (Psal. CXVII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ For the just utter their voice as it were in tabernacles, because concerning their good acts they wish to become known within, in conscience, to God alone. ⟦s5⟧ As it were a tent, therefore, is destroyed [0276B] when the brightness of a good work, shaken by the wind of flattery, is driven from its state of uprightness. ⟦s6⟧ But the tabernacle is demolished when the cleanness of conscience is secretly corrupted by unlawful delight. ⟦s7⟧ And rightly is it said: He hath destroyed his tent as a garden. ⟦s8⟧ For in this each one begins to be barren, that he seeks to glory in his good work in the eyes of men, so that for the rest he is now unworthy of God's protection for working well, because he does not keep with God the fruit of his own seed, that is the reward of his own work. ⟦s9⟧ This too we may note in the tent: that as a tent is first stretched out by cords, and then those cords are held by stakes fixed in the earth, so also good working ought to be stretched by the perseverance of intention, lest it be slackened; and that intention itself, lest it grow slack from its purpose, [0276C] ought to be confirmed by the hope of eternal reward, as it were by certain stakes fixed in the solidity of the earth. ⟦s10⟧ There follows: The Lord hath delivered over to oblivion in Sion the festival and the sabbath. ⟦s11⟧ What is understood by the festival, but inward joy; and what by the sabbath, but the rest of the mind? ⟦s12⟧ For he had said two things above, namely: he hath destroyed his tent, and he hath demolished his tabernacle. ⟦s13⟧ To these two he seems to answer. ⟦s14⟧ As if he said: Because he hath destroyed his tent, therefore he hath delivered over to oblivion the festival. ⟦s15⟧ But because he hath demolished his tabernacle, therefore he hath delivered over to oblivion the sabbath. ⟦s16⟧ For how great is the sweetness of inward joy he does not remember, whoever seeks joy in the praises of men and in transitory things; and in no wise [0276D] does the mind enjoy true peace within, when it is troubled by the incentives of vices and by the affections of carnal desires. ⟦s17⟧ There follows: Unto reproach and unto indignation the king and the priest. ⟦s18⟧ Our soul ought to be both king and priest. ⟦s19⟧ King, because it ought to rule its own flesh. ⟦s20⟧ Priest, because it ought unceasingly to offer to God its own devotion. ⟦s21⟧ But the king is delivered over unto reproach when the soul, made subject to her flesh, is servant to base desires. ⟦s22⟧ The priest is delivered over unto indignation when the obstinate mind is hardened by the habit of sin away from that devotion which it was wont to have toward God. ⟦s23⟧ God therefore destroys us, demolishes us: he is said to deliver us over unto oblivion, unto reproach, unto indignation, because the afflicted mind complains that this is done as it were by God, which by him, his own judgment [0277A] permitting, is not hindered. ⟦s24⟧ For he permits that we be destroyed, that being destroyed we forget him, that having forgotten him we come unto reproach and unto indignation. ⟦s25⟧ There follows:
