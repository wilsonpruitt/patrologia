## Little Notes on Lamentations (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Her gates are sunk into the ground; he hath destroyed and broken her bars. ⟦s2⟧ In the gates the loftiness of the kingdom, in the bars [0280C] the strength, is noted. ⟦s3⟧ The gates therefore are sunk into the ground: loftiness humbled and cast down. ⟦s4⟧ The bars destroyed and broken: strength lost and shattered. ⟦s5⟧ How this was done he adds, saying: Her kings and her princes among the Gentiles — understand: the Lord destroyed, that is, scattered, and broke, that is, reduced to nothing. ⟦s6⟧ By what desert this also came about he subjoins: There is no law, namely among them, because they keep not the law of the Lord: and her prophets have found no vision from the Lord. ⟦s7⟧ With bitterness must it be pronounced, *her prophets.* ⟦s8⟧ As if he said: She loved those more who prophesied false things out of their own spirit, than those who found a vision from the Lord. ⟦s9⟧ Her gates are sunk into the ground, etc. ⟦s10⟧ What ought we to understand by the gates [0280D] and the bars of the city, but the prelates of the people? ⟦s11⟧ For the same men are both gates through word, and bars through counsel, and ways through example. ⟦s12⟧ Through eloquent word, gates of brass; through firm counsel, bars of iron; through good example, level ways. ⟦s13⟧ For they are gates when they lead in unto the faith, through the word of preaching, those placed without. ⟦s14⟧ They are bars when they confirm and direct, through the counsel of salvation, those established within through faith. ⟦s15⟧ They are ways when they become to such as are already beginning an example of walking rightly through good working. ⟦s16⟧ Rightly therefore by the gates of Jerusalem may we understand the Scribes and Pharisees, the teachers of the Jews. ⟦s17⟧ Of whom it is here said: Her gates are sunk into the ground. ⟦s18⟧ Gates, if indeed they are upright and have solid bars, can according to the time [0281A] be shut and opened — shut, namely, to enemies, and opened to friends. ⟦s19⟧ But gates loosed from their bars, and taken away, do not fortify the entrance, nor do they withstand those wishing to enter. ⟦s20⟧ But gates which, their bars being broken, are [sic: *delixae*] in the ground, do not indeed furnish a defence, and yet they hinder those wishing to enter. ⟦s21⟧ What therefore are upright gates, but teachers preaching heavenly things, which hang well upon bars, because the word of preaching is made firm out of the counsel of the heart? ⟦s22⟧ And what are gates destroyed and taken away, but negligent and lukewarm preachers, whom the Lord rebukes by the prophet, saying: *Dumb dogs, not able to bark? [n: (Isa. LVI.)]* And elsewhere: *Thou hast not gone up over against them in the day of the Lord, that you might set yourselves a wall for the house of the Lord [n: (Ezech. XIII)]*. ⟦s23⟧ Of whom also [0281B] he had said above: Her gates are destroyed. ⟦s24⟧ But here, wishing to intimate something further, he says: Her gates are sunk into the ground. ⟦s25⟧ For to such the Lord says in the Gospel: *Woe to you, Scribes and Pharisees, because you have taken away the key of knowledge. ⟦s26⟧ You yourselves have not entered in, and others who wished to enter you have forbidden [n: (Luc. XI)]*. ⟦s27⟧ Let it be said therefore: Her gates are sunk into the ground. ⟦s28⟧ For by the earth either the love of earthly things, or the humanity of Christ, or the carnal observances of the law may be understood. ⟦s29⟧ Well therefore are the gates said to be sunk into the ground, because the Scribes and Pharisees, the teachers of the people, for this cause that they had fixed their mind upon earthly desires, not only would not receive Christ, but rather, lest they should lose that land, killed Christ. ⟦s30⟧ Or they are sunk into the earth, because, regarding in Christ the flesh alone, [0281C] they fell into the snare of faithlessness. ⟦s31⟧ Or they are sunk into the earth, because, the spiritual understanding of the law having been transferred to the Gentiles, they themselves remained in carnal observances. ⟦s32⟧ Whence it is well subjoined: He hath destroyed and broken her bars. ⟦s33⟧ For what do the bars designate but the hidden and strong counsels of the Scribes and Pharisees, by which they took counsel to kill Christ, lest they should lose the land? ⟦s34⟧ But he destroyed and broke the bars, because he reduced their counsel to nothing. ⟦s35⟧ The counsel was destroyed, because by killing Christ they could not extinguish him. ⟦s36⟧ The counsel was broken, because for this they lost the land, that they killed Christ. ⟦s37⟧ Wherefore it is subjoined: Her kings and her princes among the Gentiles — understand: God appointed, namely that [0281D] the Gentiles should reign and rule over them. ⟦s38⟧ So therefore were the bars destroyed and broken, that they not only paid tribute to the kings of the Gentiles, but also that the prefects and governors of kings dwelt among them and oppressed them, and at length cast them out, rebels, from the land, and scattered them through the whole world. ⟦s39⟧ Concerning whom it is further subjoined: There is no law, namely among them, because, even though they have the books of the law, yet because they believe not in Christ, they do not observe the law. ⟦s40⟧ But because they still promise themselves that their Christ is yet to come, against this it is rightly answered, when it is said:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Her prophets have found no vision from the Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he said: What they preach concerning the coming of Christ, falsely flattering themselves, this they have found from themselves, not from the Lord revealing it. ⟦s3⟧ *Sunk in the ground [0282A] are her gates.* ⟦s4⟧ What do we understand by the gates of the soul, but the senses of the body? ⟦s5⟧ Which gates, namely, we then sink into the ground when we incline our very senses to earthly desires. ⟦s6⟧ But because the sense without never succumbs to base desires, unless first within the guard of the mind has been broken and destroyed through negligence, it is rightly subjoined, when it is said: He hath destroyed and broken her bars; for he destroys the bars when, good fear being taken away, he leaves the mind wandering and dissolute. ⟦s7⟧ He breaks the bars when, the vices entering in, he permits the constancy of the mind to be broken and softened. ⟦s8⟧ Whereupon there follows: Her kings and her princes among the Gentiles. ⟦s9⟧ For by the Gentiles the vices are signified. ⟦s10⟧ The kings therefore and princes of the soul are among the Gentiles when those vices themselves through consent begin [0282B] to lord it over her. ⟦s11⟧ Or by the kings and princes we may understand the very virtues of the soul, by which she was formerly governed. ⟦s12⟧ Kings, because within they preside over the wills; princes, because without they move the mind to act well. ⟦s13⟧ And it is to be referred to what he had premised: he hath destroyed and broken her bars, namely the kings and princes, that is, he destroyed and broke the virtues among the Gentiles, that is, he permitted them to be broken and oppressed by the vices. ⟦s14⟧ For hence it is that he adds, saying: There is no law. ⟦s15⟧ For the mind ought to have a law within, namely the fear and the love of God, and to govern its senses without according to the judgment of this law. ⟦s16⟧ Fear, namely, lest it dissolve through wantonness along with the concupiscence of the flesh; and love, lest sloth render it torpid in good work. ⟦s17⟧ But if perchance the mind fixes its gates, that is its own senses, [0282C] in earthly delights, it is itself a judgment to itself, that, its bars being broken — that is, its virtues lost — it has lost also the law of life within, namely the fear of God and love. ⟦s18⟧ To whom it is well further said by way of reproach:

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Her prophets have found no vision from the Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ For after the mind, the governance of the understanding being lost, begins to follow the judgment of the senses, it now shuns those things only which the affection of the senses reports to be evil, and follows those which the affection of the flesh approves. ⟦s3⟧ Whence it must needs often be deceived: which, while it considers present delights only, does not avoid future bitternesses. ⟦s4⟧ Hence it is that Moses so often runs back to the tabernacle, to consult the Lord; [0282D] because a man ought rashly neither to seek nor to shun anything without, unless he first discern within, by the judgment of the mind, what is pleasing to the will of God. ⟦s5⟧ Or to the perverse soul, which everywhere follows the affection of the flesh, not the judgment of the mind: rightly here by way of reproach is it said: Her prophets have found no vision from the Lord. ⟦s6⟧ Because assuredly he casts himself headlong into the pit of destruction, who always considers what is sweet to his own flesh, and attends not to what pleases God.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The ancients have sat upon the ground, they have held their peace,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Thus far he has described as it were the destruction of buildings: henceforth he laments the misery and grief of men. ⟦s3⟧ The ancients have sat upon the ground, they have held their peace. ⟦s4⟧ The sitting designates dejection, the silence stupor. ⟦s5⟧ And fittingly [0283A] are the ancients, in whom the authority of counsel ought to have been, said to have kept silence; because so great was the calamity that it could be relieved by no counsel. ⟦s6⟧ There follows:

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The virgins have sprinkled their heads with ashes.* ⟦s2⟧ Of their own accord they defile the beauty of their comeliness, that by that very squalor without they may show the vehemence of inward grief. ⟦s3⟧ And beautifully in tribulation do the ancients keep silence, while the virgins sprinkle their heads with ashes; because evil, when it presses vehemently, turns mature senses into stupor, and wanton ones to impatience: concerning whom it is further subjoined:

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They are girded with haircloth.* ⟦s2⟧ This to the ancients.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They have cast down their heads to the ground.* ⟦s2⟧ ALLEGORICAL. ⟦s3⟧ This to the virgins. ⟦s4⟧ They wish to hasten death, who to pressing evils add voluntary afflictions besides. [0283B] The ancients have sat upon the ground, they have held their peace, etc. ⟦s5⟧ The prophet's mind, considering all the evils of the present life, leading the eye of compunction from the beginning — from the very fall of the first man, unto the blinding of the Jewish people, and then descending through the evils of the present Church — extends its lamentation unto the end of time. ⟦s6⟧ For in the foregoing he described buildings pulled down, ramparts destroyed, beginning as it were from the lesser: hence now, advancing as it were to greater things, he laments the misery and grief of men these things therefore, which follow, may not unfittingly be referred to the present Church. ⟦s7⟧ The ancients of the daughter of Sion have sat upon the ground, they have held their peace. ⟦s8⟧ What is understood by the daughter of Sion, but the present Church: which was born of the Synagogue through faith? ⟦s9⟧ The ancients therefore of the daughter of Sion are called the prelates of the Church, [0283C] whose old age is sought not in the number of years, but in the advancement of wisdom and in maturity of manners. ⟦s10⟧ And well ancients, because not as little ones set under the law, as it were under a pedagogue. ⟦s11⟧ For the ancient people was as it were a little one; because it was nourished by the rod of the law and by earthly promises, until it should climb to a mature sense, that it might serve God not for earthly but for heavenly things. ⟦s12⟧ These ancients therefore of the daughter of Sion ought not to sit upon the ground, that is, to seek earthly and transitory things; but to be in the heavens by their converse and their desire, so that what they preach by word they may show by their manners. ⟦s13⟧ But because the prophet foresaw that there would be many in holy Church who, obtaining the place of prelacy, would stain by perverse manners the dignity of their office, they will burst forth into a voice of grief, thus [0283D] he says: The ancients of the daughter of Sion have sat upon the ground. ⟦s14⟧ For what is it to sit upon the ground, but by leaning upon earthly delights to cease from good work? ⟦s15⟧ But because they who, ceasing from good, do perverse things, blush to speak the word of truth lest they become witnesses of their own depravity, he rightly went on and added: They have held their peace; because assuredly, where the guilt of crime gnaws the conscience, the fear of confusion binds the tongue from preaching. ⟦s16⟧ There follows:
