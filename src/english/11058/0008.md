## Little Notes on Lamentations (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The virgins of Jerusalem have sprinkled their heads with ashes.* ⟦s2⟧ What do we understand by the virgins of Jerusalem, but the good subjects in the Church, who, preserving the integrity of faith by sincerity of manners, hasten to the vision of the peace above? ⟦s3⟧ And what by the head, but spiritual action? ⟦s4⟧ What [0284A] is signified by the ash, which is left behind by fire, but earthly thoughts? ⟦s5⟧ For ash is born as it were from fire, when the leavings of empty thoughts are generated out of carnal concupiscence. ⟦s6⟧ Well therefore, after the ancients sit upon the ground and are silent, do the virgins sprinkle their heads with ashes; because whenever the simple in the Church see their prelates, the zeal of preaching being set aside, leaning upon earthly delights, they too begin to be carnally delighted even in the good things which they do. ⟦s7⟧ And rightly did he name the ancients of the daughter of Sion, and the virgins of Jerusalem, because prelates in holy Church ought to be lofty through the providence of circumspection. ⟦s8⟧ But subjects, in the good things which they humbly perform, ought always through the intention of the mind to look toward the vision of eternal peace; so that both [0284B] they, being set on high through virtue, may guard the things that are beneath, and these, being placed below through humility, may always strive by intention toward the things above. ⟦s9⟧ This therefore the prophet laments, that they sit who ought to have stood upright, and that they who were wont to contemplate heavenly things cast ash upon themselves, that they may not be able to lift their sight to lofty things. ⟦s10⟧ And note that even the perfect are said to have dust upon their feet; but they who sprinkle their heads with dust are now numbered among those to be lamented, because in earthly actions the good do indeed sometimes contract light stains through weakness, whereas the evil grow foul, even in those things which they seem to do bravely, through the intention of praise. ⟦s11⟧ There follows:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They are girded with haircloth.* ⟦s2⟧ What is signified by the haircloth, but the care [0284C] of earthly things, which pricks the conscience with the continual stings of occupations? ⟦s3⟧ First therefore are the ancients described as sitting upon the ground, and afterward as girded with haircloth, because where the mind has first been loosened in the love of carnal delights, presently thereafter care follows, which pricks it by making it solicitous for the fulfilling of its own desires. ⟦s4⟧ The virgins likewise are said first to sprinkle their heads with ash, and afterward to cast down those same heads to the ground, because this is the order of destruction: that each one first darkens the good things which he does through the intention of human praise, and then changes even those good works themselves into depraved actions. ⟦s5⟧ MORAL. ⟦s6⟧ The ancients of the daughter of Sion have sat upon the ground, they have held their peace. ⟦s7⟧ By the diverse ages, and sexes, and offices of persons, [0284D] the inward motions of the soul are distinguished. ⟦s8⟧ For what the offices of persons distinguish without, this the motion of the affections performs distinctly within. ⟦s9⟧ What therefore ought we to understand by the ancients of the daughter of Sion, but the counsels of a faithful soul? ⟦s10⟧ But it must be known that the counsels of the elect are one thing, and those of the reprobate another. ⟦s11⟧ For the counsels of the elect are to despise the present life and its delights, to keep patience in adversities, through present tribulations to reach [sic: *per tingere*] unto future glory. ⟦s12⟧ The counsels of the reprobate are to flee adversities, to love the present life, to give tarrying to carnal desires; to despise or to despair of the life to come. ⟦s13⟧ Of the counsel of the ungodly it is said by the Psalmist: *Blessed is the man who hath not walked in the counsel of the ungodly [n: (Psal. I)]*; and of the counsel of the just, in the same psalm afterward, [0285A] it is consequently added: *Therefore the ungodly rise not again in judgment, nor sinners in the counsel of the just.* ⟦s14⟧ If therefore they who are converted to the counsel of the just rise, deservedly are they said to sit who have slipped down into the counsel of the ungodly; because the one tends upward and the other downward, the one seeks heaven and the other earth. ⟦s15⟧ The ancients therefore of the daughter of Sion sit upon the ground, when the affections of a soul forgetful of the eternal promise cleave to earthly and transitory delights. ⟦s16⟧ Of whom it is rightly said, they have held their peace. ⟦s17⟧ For inward desire is as it were a certain cry in the ears of God. ⟦s18⟧ And therefore the more a depraved mind seeks transitory things without, so much the more does it fall silent within from the desire of things eternal. ⟦s19⟧ There follows: The virgins of Jerusalem have sprinkled their heads with ashes. ⟦s20⟧ By the virgins of Jerusalem [0285B] we ought to understand the clean thoughts of the soul. ⟦s21⟧ What is it therefore that, after the ancients sit upon the ground, the virgins are said to sprinkle their heads with ash, but that when the affections of the soul have been depraved, straightway a mist of thoughts is generated? ⟦s22⟧ Whence it is further subjoined: They are girded with haircloth. ⟦s23⟧ For after the sitting and the sprinkling of ash the haircloth follows, because the depraved delight and the confusion of the mind a harsh compunction out of the guilt of its own crime is generated to the sinning conscience. ⟦s24⟧ But last of all, at the end, the heap of unhappiness is added, when it is subjoined: The virgins of Juda have cast down their heads to the ground. ⟦s25⟧ For to cast the heads to the ground after the girding of haircloth is, after the anguish and trembling of an evil conscience, to despair also of pardon. ⟦s26⟧ Wherefore also they are rightly called the virgins [0285C] of Juda. ⟦s27⟧ For to the increase of grief confession is named last in them, because that too, the last of all, after all the other virtues have been lost, is shut out from the despairing.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *My eyes have failed for tears,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ LITERAL. ⟦s3⟧ The prophet, in the person of the Church lamenting her own members, having enumerated the evils of the lost, adds the compassion of the good; for the evil are lamented by the good before they are lamented by themselves; and while the ill-sound still exult in their own destruction, the sighs of others indicate how much they are to be made. ⟦s4⟧ He says therefore:

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ My eyes have failed for tears, *my bowels are troubled: my liver is poured out upon the earth.* ⟦s2⟧ The eyes in holy Church are the provident, the bowels the merciful, the liver the zealous and those fervent in good work. ⟦s3⟧ For as [0285D] the natural philosophers report, in the liver is the heat of digestion. ⟦s4⟧ As therefore the [sic: *jecut*], or liver, by its own heat digests raw food in the stomach itself, and thence transmits the nutritive juice into all the parts of the body, so assuredly do they who rouse others by the fervour of their devotion from torpor to work well minister nourishment, as it were out of their own digestion, to the body of Christ. ⟦s5⟧ What is it therefore that, sin laying waste the faithful people, the eyes of the Church fail, and the bowels are troubled, and the liver is poured out upon the earth, but that they who truly have the charity of Christ always suffer with the miseries of others? ⟦s6⟧ As the Apostle says: *Who is weak, and I am not weak? who is scandalized, and I am not on fire? [n: (I Cor. XL)]*. ⟦s7⟧ For there is no surer sign of true charity [0286A] than the affection of fraternal compassion. ⟦s8⟧ And well did he say that the eyes fail for tears, because often fault ulcerates the life of subjects with so vehement a blow that all the reason and diligence of prelates succumbs, and now amid such great evils there is no other counsel save only to flee to tears and to implore the divine clemency. ⟦s9⟧ This the Psalmist beautifully signifies, saying: *They were troubled, and were moved like a drunken man; and all their wisdom was swallowed up. ⟦s10⟧ And they cried to the Lord when they were in tribulation: and he delivered them out of their necessities [n: (Psal. CVI)]*. ⟦s11⟧ Well therefore do the eyes fail for tears, because often, when evil presses vehemently, sorrow makes the eye of discretion grow dim. ⟦s12⟧ The bowels are troubled, because within the affection of piety is shaken. ⟦s13⟧ The liver is overspread upon the earth, [0286B] because often in the elect, through sorrow at another's peril, the alacrity of the mind is bound up, so that they are rendered the more lukewarm to do good things even in themselves, the more they are afflicted in compassion for a brother's tribulation. ⟦s14⟧ By the eyes therefore, and the bowels, and the liver, are signified they who lament, not they who are lamented. ⟦s15⟧ Whence also presently, joining on a sentence by the words following concerning those who are lamented, he sets forth the cause of grief, saying:

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For the destruction of the daughter of my people.* ⟦s2⟧ For what is signified by the female sex, but the weak common people? whom nevertheless, when he names her daughter, he shows to have the profession of faith; and by the destruction an irreparable calamity is shown, as what is crushed is no more repaired. ⟦s3⟧ There follows

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0286C] *When the little one and the suckling fainted in the streets of the town.* ⟦s2⟧ He determines the destruction: by the little one, the untaught people and simple in faith. ⟦s3⟧ By the suckling, depraved teachers. ⟦s4⟧ By the streets of the town we ought to understand the pleasures of this world. ⟦s5⟧ The little one therefore and the suckling faint in the streets of the town, when all the carnal and foolish go with perverse teachers along the broad way of pleasures unto destruction; and note that he says, fainted. ⟦s6⟧ For those are more to be lamented who fail from good, than those who never were good.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They have failed,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ ALLEGORICAL.: It is proper to the elect to lament others' evils as their own. ⟦s3⟧ For as by rejoicing together with the good they become partakers in their merits, so also by grieving together with the evil through compassion they make gain for themselves out of [0286D] their destruction. ⟦s4⟧ Let the sinner therefore learn how he ought to weep for his own evils, when the just man afflicts himself with so great contrition for the offences of others. ⟦s5⟧ My eyes, he says, have failed for tears. ⟦s6⟧ How greatly, think you, was that man pricked, who so wept? ⟦s7⟧ For he does not show that he poured out tears once only, who says that his eyes have failed for tears. ⟦s8⟧ But because many pour out tears and are not pricked in heart, he went on and added: My bowels are troubled, that is, the arrow of grief penetrated even to the inmost parts of the heart, grief touched the sense, and the affection of piety trembled. ⟦s9⟧ There follows: My liver is poured out upon the earth. ⟦s10⟧ What is signified by the pouring out, of the liver, but the affliction of the flesh? ⟦s11⟧ For because in the liver the digestion [0287A] of the stomach receives its heat, they who in the affliction of their own body set care for it aside, what else do they but pour out the liver upon the earth. ⟦s12⟧ Lest therefore the tears be believed empty, the sighs feigned, the groans simulated, behold, a manifest affliction of the flesh follows. ⟦s13⟧ But perhaps this man grieves for parents or kinsfolk. ⟦s14⟧ Hear what he says. ⟦s15⟧ For the destruction, he says, of the daughter of my people: not of a father, not of a mother, not of a brother, not of a sister, not of a kinsman, not of a relation by marriage, but of my people, he says, lest the goad of his grief should be thought the affection of the flesh and not of charity. ⟦s16⟧ But I suppose this man honours with his lament the famous and the rich among the people? ⟦s17⟧ Attend to what he says. ⟦s18⟧ When there fainted, he says, the little one and the suckling. ⟦s19⟧ First of all mark his discretion. ⟦s20⟧ He laments that they fail, and does not lament that they are not rich. ⟦s21⟧ Who a little one, he says, and [0287B] a suckling. ⟦s22⟧ See that he has compassion on weakness, and does not venerate strength or pomp. ⟦s23⟧ But perhaps he still honours, in the weakness of sex and of age, nobility of birth: In the streets, he says, of the town. ⟦s24⟧ Not in a hall, not in a city, not in a commonwealth, not even in a house, but in the streets of the town; because the greater the abjection of the crushed, the heavier, and therefore the more precious, is the compassion of him who grieves.
