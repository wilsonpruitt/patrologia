## Little Notes on Lamentations (cont.)

[0301A]They have clapped, etc. MORAL. He who provokes a sinner to penitence by a gentle exhortation, what else does he do than feel the sick man's wounds before the cutting? As therefore a wound which cannot bear even the gentle touch of remedies is believed to be altogether incurable, so the sinner who spurns even a gentle admonition is reckoned as though incorrigible. This is what the prophet here casts up to the sinful soul with grievous sorrow, saying: They have clapped over thee, etc. As if he were to say: Formerly the suggestion of the flesh could easily prevail with thee to persuade thee to iniquity; but now all the solicitude and industry of good men has failed in thy correction, and as rash as thou wast formerly, through contempt, for perpetrating iniquity, yet now art thou hardened [0301B] through despair in that very iniquity of thine: All they that pass by the way have clapped their hands over thee. That her ruin may be shown the graver, he recalls that she had already entered upon the way of good work, and had, as it were, collapsed in the middle of the journey. Whence also he calls the others passers-by, that he may plainly show that those who now begin to go before her by the merit of virtues were once behind her. But because in the ruin of their neighbors charity always provokes the elect to compassion, and the example to fear, they are rightly described as both passing by alike and clapping. For by advancing they pass by; by suffering with others they clap, so that they so rejoice over their own advance that yet they do not presume to despise their neighbors lying in infirmity. They have clapped, he says, their hands. The clapping of the hands designates the example of good work; the hissing, the word of consolation; [0301C] the wagging of the head, the affection of compassion; because all spiritual men both provoke by example and confirm by word those whom they see to have fallen through iniquity, and show by the very grief of their own compassion how much they would rejoice over their salvation. There follows: Is this, they say, the city of perfect beauty? They therefore recall what she ought to have been, that from the memory of her past dignity she may the more quickly recognize into what misery she has slipped. They call her a city, as being lofty and fortified with virtues, who formerly had God reigning within her, when she was not yet subjected by consent to the dominion of the vices. There follows: The joy of all the earth. As if it were said: The more there were who ought to have rejoiced over her advance, the more are there now whom her ruin and desolation saddens, so that if [0301D] she will not spare herself, she may at least blush to afflict others by her own perdition. By all these means the hardened conscience is dealt with, that, so manifold a medicine having been applied, it may at length soften to penitence.

*All thy enemies have opened their mouth over thee,* etc. LITERAL. As if he should say: If it does not move thee that thou hast been mocked by false prophets, that, having fallen into calamity, thou hast been set for a sign and a portent to them that pass by; let this at least prick thy insensibility, that thou hast been made unto thy enemies a prey and a trampling, a joy and a derision. They have opened, he says, their mouth over thee, all thy enemies. The opening of thy mouth designates cruelty and derision. [0302A] The greedy have opened the mouth to devour. The proud have opened the mouth to deride.

*They have hissed, and gnashed with their teeth.* The hissing expresses contempt, the gnashing wrath and indignation. All thy enemies, the Chaldeans, the Romans. Grievous is the pressure where all press and all prevail. There follows:

*And they said: We will devour.* They wish not to harm a little, but to blot out even to consuming; nor only to blot out, but to devour — they who feed and take delight in thy ruin. Whence there follows:

*Lo, this is the day that we have looked for in desiring; we have found it in seeking; we have seen it in exulting.* So that the greater is the gladness of those who afflict thee, the more bitter and intolerable is thy misery.

[0302B]They have opened, etc. ALLEGORICAL. The enemies of holy Church are the heretics, because they assail the constancy of her faith with depraved dogmas. But these do not presume to open their mouth against her, so long as they behold both wisdom and discipline flourishing together in her conduct. For against wisdom they close their mouth, because from those whom they see stable in the knowledge of truth they hide the proofs of their error. Against discipline likewise they close their mouth, when in the conduct of the faithful they do not find what one might blaspheme. But if perchance wisdom should have lost discipline, they open their mouth against her, that they may blaspheme; or if discipline should not have wisdom, they open their mouth against her, that they may disseminate errors; but if neither wisdom nor discipline be in her, now they open their mouth not only against her but [0302C] over her, that they may devour, and, as it were by incorporating them into themselves, may consume all the remnants of truth in those whom they are able to draw into their own fellowship There follows: They have hissed. In the hissing we can note the crafty and coaxing persuasions by which they deceive the unwary, and, like serpents after a soft hiss, pour in the venom of death. But because the blandishments of the heretics proceed not from piety but from cruelty: rightly is it subjoined: They have gnashed with their teeth. As if it were said: Coaxing was that which the speech hissed, but exceedingly cruel that which the intention gnashed. There follows: Lo, this is the day that we have looked for, have found, have seen: the oppression of the good they name not night but day, because from that whence the darkness of unbelief blinds others, they themselves shine by an iniquitous gladness. [0302D] We have looked for it, they say, namely desiring; we have found it seeking; we have seen it exulting. And note that what is hidden is found; what is seen, is open. As if they should say: What formerly we desired to find, even in secret, now we see made manifest.

They have opened over thee, etc. MORAL. The demons open their mouth against the soul: when they beat with the voices of suggestion upon it as it stands upright in good desires. But they open their mouth above it, when to those lying in depraved delight they no longer persuade iniquity, but command it. Or they open their mouth above her whom, having first drawn her to guilt by coaxing, they afterwards by raging demand for punishment. Whence it is added: They have hissed, [0303A] they have gnashed with their teeth. For first they hiss, and then they gnash with their teeth, because, as we have said, after the coaxing suggestion they cruelly demand the punishment. Of which punishment, namely, it is yet further subjoined, when it is said: And they said: We will devour. For what else is it to devour, unless to swallow up unto eternal damnation the soul going forth from the body? But it must be known that they never avail to devour a soul going forth from the body which they have not first, while it was set in the body, boiled down with the fire of the vices. And therefore great confidence is born in them concerning a man's future damnation, when they lord it over him in the present life through iniquity. After therefore he had said, they have opened their mouth, they have hissed, he rightly added at once, saying: They have gnashed with their teeth; they said: We will devour; because from the moment a man is subjected to their suggestion [0303B] by consent to sin, they are straightway girded with the fury of the cruelty to come. And because they see the food already softened by long boiling, they promise themselves a devouring close at hand. In which name of devouring, namely, both their vehement desire is shown, and at the same time the bitterness of the damnation is expressed. There follows: Lo, this is the day that we have looked for, have found, have seen. They who say that they have found intimate also that they had sought before they found. First therefore the demons seek, when they take pains to try by suggestion the secret intention of our heart; then they wait, because, although they sometimes receive license to tempt, yet they can never bring force upon the tempted man to consent. But after the search and the waiting [0303C] they find, when, after the suggestion has been sent in, they at last behold manifest signs of a spontaneous consent coming forth from the heart. Last of all they also see, when the consent to the crime proceeds even to the effect of the work.

*The Lord hath done that which he purposed, he hath fulfilled his word,* etc. LITERAL. Next, after the rebuke, he subjoins consolation, saying: The Lord hath done that which he purposed, etc. First he consoles the mourners by the justice of the judge; then by the malice of the enemy. By the justice of the judge, that they may not grieve over the past; by the malice of the enemy, that they may not despair of the future. For it is a great consolation to the afflicted, that the punishment has been brought upon them by him whom injustice cannot please. Likewise it affords great confidence of obtaining mercy, that [0303D] he is our adversary who is known not to please our judge either. He says therefore: The Lord hath done that which he purposed. As if he were to say: Our enemies exult, and ascribe our ruin to their own strength; but we ought to bear their insult the more lightly, the more truly we know that our evils proceed not from their strength but from the just judgment of God. The Lord hath done that which he purposed. That is shown to have been ordained which is recorded as done with premeditation, especially because it had been foretold long before, so that they may have no occasion of murmuring against God who were able to foreknow the peril and would not beware of it. Whence there follows:

*He hath fulfilled his word, which he had commanded from the days [0304A] of old.* He had commanded his prophets to declare this word, and that they who had been long waited for might at least repent, not newly, but from the days of old, that is, long before it should come to pass. But the longer they were waited for through mercy, the more justly did they, persevering in evil, deserve to be grievously punished. Whence there follows:

*He hath destroyed, and hath not spared.* These things are properly referred to the last captivity.

*And he hath made thine enemy to rejoice over thee.* The greater the gladness of the oppressor, the graver is the misery of the oppressed. But yet the arrogance of the enemy is easily despised, if his power be ascribed not to himself but to God. And it is to be noted that I said, over thee he hath made thine enemy to rejoice. For against us, yet not [0304B] over us, does the enemy rejoice, when he is indeed permitted to harm but not to oppress; but he rejoices over us when he not only afflicts but also oppresses. There follows:

*He hath exalted the horn of thy foes.* In the horn strength and power are signified; by the foes, the Chaldeans or the Romans are understood.

The Lord hath done that which he purposed, etc. ALLEGORICAL. Through the heretics the Lord does that which he purposed, because, while they draw away certain men from the Church to their own errors, others become more approved in faith and in the knowledge of truth. Whence there follows: He hath fulfilled his word, which he had commanded from the days of old. For the word of God, which he had commanded from the days of old, is understood to be Holy Scripture, which was long since published to the world; but [0304C] is still for a great part hidden. Which is daily fulfilled in the minds of the faithful, when they, exercised by the questions of heretics, grow up to greater pursuits of the virtues, and advance to a loftier understanding of the divine oracles. Thus assuredly do the wicked serve the divine will even then when they strive to go against it, because he so fulfills his disposition through them that by their errors he both permits the reprobate to be ensnared and exercises the good. There follows: He hath destroyed, and hath not spared. When he suffers certain men to be cut off from the unity of the Church through unbelief; he does not spare, because the sorrows of compassion afflict, in the ruin of their brethren, even those who persevere. There follows: He hath made thine enemy to rejoice over thee. The singular enemy of the Church is the devil, who pursues the faithful [0304D] with perpetual hatred, as we read in the Apocalypse of the dragon who persecutes the woman [n: (Apoc. XII)]. But this enemy then rejoices over holy Church, when he receives even out of her those through whom he afflicts her. There follows: He hath exalted the horn of thy foes. The foes of the Church are the heretics, who daily lead forth a battle-line to storm her. Whose horn is then assuredly said to be exalted, when, many deserting the faith and passing over to their fellowship, the people of the faithful is found lesser in number.
