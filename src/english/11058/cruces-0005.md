# Cruces — 11058 *Adnotatiunculae in Threnos*, chunks 0005–0009

Hugh of St Victor, PL 175. Agent stint covering chunks 0005, 0006, 0007, 0008,
0009. Entries are ordered by column.

## Conventions decided in this stint

- **Head.** `## Adnotatiunculae in Threnos (cont.)` rendered
  `## Little Notes on Lamentations (cont.)`, matching the wording the peer agents
  landed in 0000–0003 and 0015–0018. `(cont.)` left literal.
- **Sense labels.** Rendered as adjectives, uniformly: `MORAL.` (Migne prints
  `MORALIS.`), `LITERAL.` (Migne prints `LITTERAL.`), `ALLEGORICAL.` (Migne
  prints `ALLEG.`, and at 0286D `ALLEG.:` with a following colon — the colon is
  kept). Identical to the 0015–0018 stint's ruling.
- **Quotation marks.** Migne prints no guillemets in any of these five chunks.
  None supplied; none of Hugh's very frequent `Ac si diceret:` paraphrases has
  been quoted. No straight quotes were needed either.
- **Lemma wording.** Hugh's Lamentations lemma is translated from Migne's page at
  every recurrence. Fixed renderings kept consistent across the stint:
  *moenia* = "ramparts", *munitiones* = "fortifications", *tentorium* = "tent",
  *tabernaculum* = "tabernacle", *antemurale* = "bulwark", *funiculus* = "line",
  *vectes* = "bars", *senes* = "the ancients", *oppidum* = "town".

## Chunk 0005 (0274A–0277A)

- **0274C — REAL WORD, WRONG: *primae adjectioni*.** *et post haec omnia peccantem
  et praevaricantem **abjecit**, et ita primae **adjectioni** secundam adnumerans.*
  The preceding verb is *abjecit*, and the two things counted are two castings-off
  (of Adam, then of the Jews); sense requires *abjectioni*. *adjectio* ("addition")
  is a real Latin word, so it is rendered as ordinary English — "reckoning a second
  to the first adjection" — with **no** `[sic: …]` marker (Pattern 12). The
  conjecture lives here.
- **0274C — REAL WORD, WRONG: *a spe permissionis*.** *et a spe **permissionis** et
  haereditatis suae alienos.* Sense requires *promissionis* ("of his promise"),
  which is what an inheritance is hoped from. Rendered as printed, "from the hope
  of his permission and of his inheritance." Real word, no marker.
- **0275B** — Isa. 1:8 is quoted **without** italics and **without** an `[n: …]`
  note, unlike the four scripture citations around it, which carry both. Migne's
  typography reproduced as printed; recorded so the index's low citation count for
  this column is not read as an omission on our side.
- **0275D** — *Quasi enim **oblitum** dicit.* The masculine accusative has no
  expressed noun; the subject of the whole passage is *Deus*, so it is rendered
  "he calls him as it were forgetful." No emendation.
- **0276A** — the Isaias citation at 0275D–0276A conflates Isa. 1:11 (*Sanguinem
  hircorum… nolui*) with 1:13 (*incensum abominatio est mihi*) and 1:12 (*quis
  quaesivit haec de manibus vestris*) in one run under a single `[n: (Isa. I.)]`.
  Rendered as one continuous quotation, as printed. Not marked `[var: …]`: the
  wording of each limb agrees with the Vulgate; only the stitching is Migne's.

## Chunk 0006 (0277B–0280B)

- **0278D — CARRIED TYPE `[sic: *autemurale*]`, beside a real word that is wrong.**
  Migne prints *suxitque **autemurale**, et murus dissipatus est*. Both words are
  defective against Lam. 2:8 *Luxitque antemurale*, which Migne himself prints
  correctly twice nearby (0279A *Luxit antemurale*; 0280B *Luxitque antemurale*).
  The two halves are treated differently, per Pattern 12's boundary:
  - *suxitque* is a **real word** (perfect of *sugo*, "sucked") and is therefore
    rendered as ordinary English — "and it sucked" — with no marker. The English
    clause is absurd because the plate is.
  - *autemurale* is a **non-word** and is carried untranslated in italics inside
    `[sic: …]`.
  Conjecture: *Luxitque antemurale*, "and the bulwark mourned."
- **0279A — MISSING PUNCTUATION.** *quasi antemurale in civitate Dei foris stant
  dicatur ergo: Luxit antemurale…* No mark between *stant* and *dicatur*, so two
  finite verbs collide. None supplied (Pattern 8); the English reads "…they stand
  without as it were a bulwark in the city of God let it therefore be said:".
- **0279B–C** — the Luke 19:41–43 quotation is abridged past construing: *Quia si
  cognovisses, et tu, quia venient dies in te…* has a protasis (*si cognovisses*)
  with no apodosis, the Vulgate's *et quidem in hac die tua, quae ad pacem tibi*
  having dropped out. Rendered as printed, apodosis and all missing. Not marked
  `[var: …]`: this is an abridgement of the quotation, not a divergent reading, and
  Pattern 14 was introduced for the latter.
- **0279C** — *Quid namque per murum filiae Sion, nisi virtutes animae accipimus**:***
  A question closed with a colon. Preserved (Pattern 8).
- **0279D, 0280A** — *Quod etiam evidentius explanatur, cum dicitur**.*** and
  *Unde recte subinfertur, cum dicitur**.*** Full stops where a colon introduces
  the lemma. Preserved. The same habit recurs at 0290A in chunk 0009.
- **0278D** — Isa. 9:3 printed *Multiplicasti gentem, non magnificasti laetitiam*
  (Vulg. *et non magnificasti*). The negation is present and is carried; only the
  connective is missing. Sense unaffected, so no `[var: …]`.

## Chunk 0007 (0280C–0283D)

- **0281A — CARRIED TYPE `[sic: *delixae*]`.** *Portae vero, quae contritis vectibus
  in terra **delixae** sunt.* Non-word for *defixae*, which Migne prints correctly
  five times in the same chunk (0280C, 0280D, 0281B ×2, 0282A). Carried into the
  English untranslated, in italics, inside `[sic: …]`; the English clause therefore
  has no participle, which is the plate's condition.
- **0281A** — Ezech. 13:5 printed *Non **ascendisti** in die Domini ex adverso, ut
  **poneretis** vos murum pro domo **Domini**.* Singular verb then plural verb in
  one sentence, and *Domini* for the Vulgate's *Israel* (Vulg.: *Non ascendistis ex
  adverso, neque opposuistis murum pro domo Israel*). All rendered as printed. Not
  marked `[var: …]`: nothing in Hugh's gloss turns on the divergence.
- **0282D — REAL WORD, WRONG: *Perversae aut animae*.** The context requires
  *autem* ("but to the perverse soul…"); *aut* is a real word and is rendered as
  printed, "Or to the perverse soul." No marker.
- **0283B — MISSING PUNCTUATION.** *hominum miseriam et dolorem, **plangit haec
  igitur**, quae sequuntur, non incongrue ad praesentem Ecclesiam referri possunt.*
  No mark between *plangit* and *haec igitur*, so the sentence runs two clauses
  together. None supplied; the English reads "…he laments the misery and grief of
  men these things therefore, which follow, may not unfittingly be referred to the
  present Church."
- **0283C–D — NUMBER MISMATCH: *erumpent*.** *Sed quia plerosque propheta… praevidit,
  qui… macularent, in vocem doloris **erumpent**, sic ait.* A plural future
  indicative where the singular subject (*propheta*) and the syntax want a
  participle (*erumpens*) or *erumpit*. Rendered as printed, "they will burst forth
  into a voice of grief, thus he says." Real word, no marker.

## Chunk 0008 (0284A–0287B)

- **0284D — SPLIT TYPE `[sic: *per tingere*]`.** *per praesentes tribulationes ad
  futuram gloriam **per tingere**.* A word-division defect for *pertingere* ("to
  reach unto"); the whole broken run is carried per Pattern 10, in italics inside
  `[sic: …]`, at its printed position (clause-final). The English limb therefore
  has no verb, matching the plate.
- **0285A** — Ps. 1:5 printed *Ideo non **resurgunt** impii in judicio* (Vulg.
  *resurgent*). Present for future; rendered "rise not again." The negation is
  present and carried. Sense unaffected, no `[var: …]`.
- **0285B — DOES NOT CONSTRUE.** *quia **pravam delectationem et confusionem mentis**
  aspera peccatrici conscientiae de reatu sui sceleris compunctio generatur.* Two
  accusatives stand with no verb to govern them (*generatur* is passive and already
  has its subject, *compunctio*). Something on the order of *post pravam
  delectationem* is wanted. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow, with the
  accusatives left dangling: "because the depraved delight and the confusion of the
  mind a harsh compunction out of the guilt of its own crime is generated to the
  sinning conscience." No emendation, no marker (nothing here is a non-word).
- **0285D — CARRIED TYPE `[sic: *jecut*]`.** *Sicut ergo **jecut**, vel hepar, suo
  calore crudum cibum… decoquit.* Non-word for *jecur*, which Migne prints correctly
  three times within twenty lines (0285C, 0285D, 0286A). Carried in italics inside
  `[sic: …]`; the appositive *hepar* is a real Latin word for the same organ and is
  rendered "liver," so the English reads "the *jecut*, or liver."
- **0285C — REAL WORD, WRONG: *quantum fiendi sint*.** *quantum **fiendi** sint
  aliena suspiria indicant.* Sense requires *flendi* ("how much they are to be wept
  for"); the whole sentence is about lamenting the lost. *fiendi* (gerundive of
  *fio*) is a real form, so it is rendered as ordinary English — "how much they are
  to be made" — and takes **no** marker (Pattern 12). The conjecture lives here.
  This is the entry most likely to look like our error rather than Migne's.
- **0286A — REAL WORD, WRONG: *offunditur*.** *Jecur in terram **offunditur***, in a
  paragraph that prints *effusum* / *effunditur* / *effusionem* on either side of
  it. *offundo* ("to overspread, pour over") is a real word and is rendered
  literally, "The liver is overspread upon the earth." No marker.
- **0285D** — the note `[n: (I Cor. XL)]` attaches to *Quis infirmatur, et ego non
  infirmor?*, which is **2 Cor. 11:29**; I Corinthians has no chapter XL. Migne's
  citation reproduced verbatim per the marker rule; recorded only so the index's
  `refDisplay`/`refKey` split is not mistaken for our error.
- **0286C** — *Per lactentem, **pravos doctores**.* The suckling is glossed as
  "depraved teachers" here, but as *virtus mentis* ("the virtue of the mind") at
  0287C in chunk 0009, and the whole surrounding argument is about the untaught
  people. Rendered as printed at both places, unharmonized.
- **0287A** — *Quid per effusionem**,** jecoris, nisi afflictio carnis signatur?* A
  comma between the noun and its dependent genitive. Preserved (Pattern 8); the
  English reads "What is signified by the pouring out, of the liver, but…".
- **0287B — DOES NOT CONSTRUE.** *Quis parvulus, inquit, et lactens.* The preceding
  sentence has just quoted the lemma as *Cum deficeret, inquit, parvulus, et
  lactens*, so *Quis* is unaccounted for; it may be an interrogative standing alone
  (*Quis? parvulus, inquit, et lactens* — "Who? 'a little one,' he says, 'and a
  suckling'"), but supplying the question mark would be supplying punctuation the
  plate does not print. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow: "Who a
  little one, he says, and a suckling." Real words throughout, so no marker.
- **0286B** — anchor sequence 0286A → 0286B → 0286C is continuous; noted here only
  because 0285C–D carries an unusual density of defects and a later reader may
  suspect a lost line. No text is broken across any anchor in this chunk.

## Chunk 0009 (0287C–0290C)

- **0288B — ANCHOR SET TIGHT.** Migne prints `[0288B]Omnia cooperantur ad malum`
  with no space after the column mark. Reproduced exactly — anchor and word run
  together in the English too.
- **0288D — INCOMPLETE DISJUNCTION.** *qui **aut** plus debito exigunt**;** minus,
  vel nihil, debito impendunt.* The *aut* has no answering limb, the second half of
  the antithesis being introduced by a bare semicolon. Rendered as printed —
  "who either exact more than is due; bestow less than is due, or nothing" — with
  the semicolon kept.
- **0289B — NUMBER MISMATCH: *isti*.** *Vide quomodo **isti** hordeum legis
  **abjicit**; et non nisi triticum… edere **quaerit**.* The subject is Paul, and
  both verbs are singular, so sense wants *iste*. Rendered by the verbs, "See in
  what manner this man casts away…". Real word, no marker.
- **0289D — CARRIED TYPE `[sic: *hibere*]`.** *vinum, et non aquam **hibere**
  debemus.* Non-word for *bibere*. Carried in italics inside `[sic: …]`; the English
  clause therefore has no infinitive — "we ought to *hibere* wine and not water" —
  which is the plate's condition. Both negations (*non*, and *ne… coquamus* in the
  next sentence) are carried.
- **0290A** — *Unde recte subinfertur, cum dicitur**.*** Full stop before the lemma
  where a colon is expected, as at 0279D and 0280A above. Preserved.
- **0290C — MISPLACED PUNCTUATION.** *Quasi morientes**;** namque filios matres in
  sinu tenent, quando…* The semicolon detaches *Quasi morientes* into a fragment,
  and *namque* is postponed to second position in a way the sense does not want.
  Preserved as printed: "As it were dying; for mothers hold their sons in the
  bosom, when…".
- **0290B → 0290C** is the chunk's last anchor pair and is continuous; the chunk ends
  mid-argument at 0290C, which is our chunk boundary, not a defect.
