# Cruces — 11058 *Adnotatiunculae in Threnos*, chunks 0010–0014

Hugh of St Victor, PL 175. Agent stint covering chunks 0010, 0011, 0012, 0013,
0014. Entries are ordered by column.

## Conventions decided in this stint

- **Head.** `## Adnotatiunculae in Threnos (cont.)` is rendered
  `## Little Notes on Lamentations (cont.)`, matching the wording the peer agents
  landed in 0000–0009 and 0015–0018. `(cont.)` left literal.
- **Sense labels.** Rendered as adjectives, uniformly: `MORAL.`, `LITERAL.`,
  `ALLEGORICAL.` Migne prints `MORAL.`, `LITTERAL.`, `LITTER.` (0307C),
  `ALLEG.` Normalized per Pattern 9 (section labels normalize in English; the
  misprint stays visible in the facing Latin column). No label invented where
  the plate prints none — the second `Cui comparabo te` gloss at **0292C** has
  no label and is left unlabelled.
- **Quotation marks.** Migne prints no guillemets in these five chunks; none
  supplied. Hugh's constant `Ac si diceret:` / `Quasi diceretur:` paraphrases
  and his re-quoted lemmata are left unquoted, matching the plate. No straight
  quotes were needed either.
- **`assumptio` / `ejectio`.** The Lam. 2:14 lemma *assumptiones falsas et
  ejectiones* is rendered "takings-up" and "castings-out" throughout, because
  Hugh's whole gloss at 0293D–0294A turns on *assumptio* being a *burden* taken
  up (*ad similitudinem oneris, quod assumptum portantem gravat*) and on
  Symmachus and Theodotion having used the word where Jerome wrote *onus*.
  Douay's "revelations / banishments" would have destroyed the argument. At
  0294A the cited Greek-translator word *assumptio* is itself left in Latin
  italics, since it is being quoted as a word.
- **Lemma wording** translated from Migne's page at every recurrence; no
  conventional English Lamentations consulted.

## Chunk 0010

- **0291D** — *Quod est aperte dicere. Mala tua…* and, a few lines later, *Quod
  est dicere. Alia mala…* Migne closes both with a **full stop** where a colon
  is expected before the quoted sense. Preserved (Pattern 8): "Which is openly
  to say. Thy evils surpass…"
- **0291D** — *Quemadmodum mare super omnes, alias aquas et mole et amaritudine
  excellens est.* Migne prints a comma inside the noun phrase, after *omnes*.
  English word order does not reproduce the phrase, so the comma is not carried:
  rendered "Just as the sea excels all other waters both in bulk and in
  bitterness." The only punctuation departure in this stint; logged per
  instruction.
- **0292D** — *Cui bene dicitur. Magna velut mare contritio tua.* Again a full
  stop where a colon introduces the lemma. Preserved.
- **0293C** — *et tamen credere non timuisti merito punitur insipientia tua.* No
  punctuation between *timuisti* and *merito*; none supplied. The column anchor
  falls between them in the plate, and the English keeps the same join.
- **0294A** — *ubi beatus Hieronymus transtulit onus Babylonis, sive Tyri, sive
  Damasci, etc. Simmachus et Theodotion assumptio interpretati sunt.* Migne's
  *etc.* is followed by a full stop and a new sentence with no connective;
  rendered exactly so. Also note *Simmachus* for *Symmachus* — an attested
  spelling of the name, not a defect (Pattern 9); rendered "Symmachus."

## Chunk 0011

- **0294D** — *Qui sunt prophetae peccatricis animae, qui falsa et stulta **eis**
  vident.* Plural *eis* against singular *animae*. Rendered with the plural
  visible, "who see false and foolish things **for them**." Real word, no
  marker; the number mismatch is the plate's.
- **0295A** — the note `[n: (Luc. X)]` attaches to *Filii hujus saeculi
  prudentiores sunt filiis lucis in generatione sua*, which is Luke 16:8.
  Reproduced verbatim per the marker rule; recorded here only so the index's
  `refDisplay`/`refKey` split is not mistaken for our error.
- **0295D–0296A — CARRIED TYPE `[sic: *divilias*]`.** *ut ab amantibus divilias
  omnia virtutum merita tollat.* Non-word for *divitias*, which Migne prints
  correctly twice in the immediately preceding lines (*Possidentibus divitias*,
  *ad quaerendas divitias*). Carried into the English untranslated, in italics,
  inside `[sic: …]`; the English phrase therefore reads "from those who love
  *divilias*."
- **0296C** — *quando vero etiam ad irrationabiles actus cupiditate victam
  impellebant, **non solum** falsa et stulta viderunt.* The correlative *sed
  etiam* limb is absent, so the sentence as printed says the prophets saw "not
  only false and foolish things" and stops. Compare the parallel a line earlier
  (*falsa viderunt*), which expects a climax here. Rendered exactly as printed,
  `non` carried, nothing supplied (Pattern 7a). Conjecture: a dropped clause, or
  *non solum* for *non solum falsa, sed etiam stulta*.
- **0296D — MISSING TERMINAL STOP.** *…ostenduntur et aliunde venire et aliorsum
  tendere **Per hoc enim** quod aliunde veniunt…* No point between *tendere* and
  *Per*. None supplied (Pattern 8): the English reads "…and to tend elsewhere For
  by this, that they come from elsewhere…"
- **0297A — CARRIED TYPE `[sic: *lespiciunt*]`.** *quod isti quasi extranei
  dejectam lespiciunt.* Non-word for *despiciunt* ("look down on"), which the
  answering limb confirms (*illi vero … non solum despectum, sed odium … 
  ostendunt*). Carried in italics inside `[sic: …]`; the English clause is
  therefore left without its verb, which is the plate's condition.

## Chunk 0012

- **0299B — REAL WORDS, WRONG DIVISION: *per gentes*.** *et **per gentes** pro
  his, quos adhuc carnalis affectus in hoc mundo obligat, mugitus compassionis
  edunt?* Sense and the sentence's own earlier phrase (*vaccae … pergentes, et
  mugientes*, twice in this paragraph) require *pergentes* — "going on, they
  utter lowings of compassion." But *per gentes* is two real Latin words and
  construes ("through the nations"), so under the Pattern 12 boundary it is
  rendered as ordinary English and takes **no** `[sic: …]` marker. Conjecture
  recorded here: *pergentes*.
- **0299B** — the same sentence, a statement running from *Vaccae igitur … 
  vadunt, quando fideles … edunt*, is closed with a **question mark**.
  Preserved (Pattern 8); the following relative clause (*qui nec propter
  compassionem…*) resumes after it in lower case, exactly as printed.
- **0300B** — *Ore sibilavit, quando Corinthios post poenitentiam consolans:
  Scripsi vobis…* The participle *consolans* has no finite verb; the sentence is
  an anacoluthon. Rendered as printed, "when consoling the Corinthians after
  their penitence:".
- **0300C** — *Tanto graviorem **electis quibuslibet** de vestri perditione
  luctum facitis.* Rendered "You make for any of the elect a grief… the
  heavier"; *quibuslibet* kept in its indefinite force rather than smoothed to
  "all the elect."
- **0300D** — *nisi quia dum patitur unum membrum omnia membra compatiuntur:* a
  colon closes the sentence, and the next clause (*propter quod valde
  pertimescere debent…*) runs on from it. Preserved.

## Chunk 0013

- **0301A** — the column anchor is set flush against the first word,
  `[0301A]Plauserunt`, with no space. Mirrored exactly in the English
  (`[0301A]They have clapped`), as also at **0302B** and **0308A**.
- **0301A–B** — *quantumque prius ad perpetrandum iniquitatem per contemptum
  fuisti temeraria, **tamen** nunc in ipsa iniquitate tua per desperationem es
  obdurata.* The correlative of *quantum* should be *tanto*; Migne prints
  *tamen*. Rendered with both visible ("and as rash as thou wast formerly…, yet
  now art thou hardened…"). Real words, no marker.
- **0301D** — *Quanto plures de ejus profectu gaudere debuerant, tanto plures
  nunc ejus ruina et desolatio contristat.* Nominative *ruina et desolatio* with
  singular *contristat* governing accusative *plures*: rendered "the more are
  there now whom her ruin and desolation saddens," keeping the singular verb.
- **0301D — CASE, RENDERED AS PRINTED: *os suum* / *oris tui*.** The lemma at
  0301D prints *Aperuerunt super te **os suum**, omnes inimici tui*, and the
  gloss two lines later prints *Apertio **oris tui** crudelitatem et
  subsannationem designat.* As printed the gloss makes the opened mouth **thy**
  mouth, not the enemies' — which contradicts the lemma it is glossing and the
  two sentences that follow (*Aperuerunt os avidi… Aperuerunt os superbi…*).
  Conjecture: *oris sui*. Rendered exactly as printed, "The opening of **thy**
  mouth designates cruelty and derision" (Pattern 7a — no silent repair). Real
  word, no marker.
- **0301D** — *quod in calamitatem **collapso**, in signum et prodigium
  transeuntibus posita es.* Masculine/neuter *collapso* where the addressee is
  feminine (*posita es*). Latin gender has no English exponent (Pattern 9);
  rendered "having fallen into calamity," English left alone, logged so a later
  sweep does not churn it.
- **0302C — MISSING TERMINAL STOP.** *…quos ad suum consortium trahere **valent
  Sequitur:** Sibilaverunt.* No point between *valent* and *Sequitur*. None
  supplied (Pattern 8).
- **0302D** — *Daemones contra animam os aperiunt: quando **erectum** in bonis
  desideriis vocibus suggestionis pulsant* and, next sentence, *quando
  **jacentes** in prava delectatione…* Both participles disagree with feminine
  singular *animam*. Gender/number, no English exponent; rendered "upon it as it
  stands upright" and "to those lying," English left alone.
- **0303D** — *Ordinatum esse ostenditur quod cum praemeditatione factum
  memoratur.* Rendered literally, "That is shown to have been ordained which is
  recorded as done with premeditation."
- **0304C** — *quia eos etiam, qui persistunt in ruina fratrum, moerores
  compassionis affligit.* Singular *affligit* with plural *moerores*; rendered
  with the plural subject ("the sorrows of compassion afflict…"), and *qui
  persistunt* left without its complement, as printed.

## Chunk 0014

- **0305C** — *Recte ergo **afflictio** dicitur, complevit sermonem quem
  praeceperat a diebus antiquis, **ut eo patientius … toleret**.* Nominative
  *afflictio* where the following purpose clause has the afflicted soul as
  subject; *afflictae* would construe. Rendered as printed, "Rightly therefore is
  the affliction spoken of: he hath fulfilled the word…". Real word, no marker.
- **0306A** — *Tantoque humilius mens humana **justitia Dei** se in flagella
  subjiciat.* Nominative/ablative *justitia* where the sense wants *justitiae
  Dei* as the thing submitted to. Rendered as an ablative, "let the human mind
  the more humbly subject itself to the scourges **by** the justice of God."
  Real word, no marker.
- **0306A — TRUNCATED TYPE `[sic: *contradica*]` — the most important entry in
  this stint.** The chunk's first paragraph ends:

  > *Valde enim in afflicto animo pondus tribulationis allevat, si nec in sua
  > poena aequitati judicis **contradica***

  The word is cut off mid-form and there is **no terminal stop**. That the form
  is truncated and not a variant is confirmed inside this same work: at
  **0278A** (chunk 0006) Migne prints the full *contradicat* (*quod ejus
  voluntati contradicat*). Carried into the
  English untranslated, in italics, inside `[sic: …]`, and the sentence is left
  unterminated: "…if not even in its own punishment it *contradica* the equity of
  the judge". The printed *nec* is carried. Conjecture: *contradicat.*
  Whether the loss is Migne's plate or our TEI cannot be settled from our files,
  so it is marked `[sic: …]` (the plate's, on the evidence we have) rather than
  `[ed: …]`; a plate check would settle it.
- **0306C** — *et hoc **eum prohiberi** non posse existimant, quod ipso
  permittente, et disponente fieri ignorant.* The accusative *eum* with passive
  *prohiberi* does not construe (*eum prohibere* would). Rendered as closely as
  the printed words allow: "and they judge that this cannot be hindered, which
  they know not comes to pass by his own permitting and disposing."
- **0306C** — *Murus filiae Sion unumquemque veritatis defensorem significare
  potest, **si** per quem cor inimicorum clamat ad Dominum.* Printed *si* leaves
  the clause without an apodosis and without a construable link. Conjecture:
  *sic* ("thus, through whom the heart of the enemies cries…"), on the model of
  the same *si*-for-*sic* slip logged at 0310A in chunk 0015. Rendered as
  printed, *si* carried ("if through whom…"). Real word, no marker.
- **0306D — DROPPED VERB, RENDERED AS PRINTED: *clausit*.** Migne prints:

  > *tunc nimirum cor hostium ad Deum intrinsecus praesidentem **clausit**, ut
  > jam ipse importuno strepitu commotus de illa sua quiete exsurgens dicat.*

  The whole passage is expounding the lemma *Clamavit cor eorum ad Dominum*, the
  preceding sentence has *ad aures usque Dei non pertingunt*, and the *ut*
  clause makes God rise up *stirred by the din* — so the verb required is
  *clamavit*, and *ad Deum … clausit* ("closed toward God") does not construe
  with *ad*. Rendered exactly as printed, "then assuredly the heart of the foes
  **closed** toward God presiding within" (Pattern 7a: a word is not translated
  as something other than what it means to rescue a sentence). Real word, no
  marker. Conjecture: *clamavit.*
- **0306D** — *Rursumque per murum filiae Sion; quia tunc delectatio carnis…* A
  fragment with no verb, closed with a semicolon. Rendered as printed, "And
  again by the wall of the daughter of Sion; because…".
- **0308C** — *peccant revera si de illorum salute etiam plus ipsis **solliciti,
  non sunt**.* Migne prints a comma between the predicate and its negated verb.
  English reorders the clause, so the comma is not carried; the negation is.
- **0307C** — the sense label is printed `LITTER.` here, not `LITTERAL.`
  Normalized to `LITERAL.` per the label convention above.
