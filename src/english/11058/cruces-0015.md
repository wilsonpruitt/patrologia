# Cruces — 11058 *Adnotatiunculae in Threnos*, chunks 0015–0018

Hugh of St Victor, PL 175. Agent stint covering chunks 0015, 0016, 0017, 0018
(0018 is the last chunk of the work). Entries are ordered by column.

## Conventions decided in this stint

- **Head.** `## Adnotatiunculae in Threnos (cont.)` is rendered
  `## Little Notes on Lamentations (cont.)` throughout, conformed to the wording
  the peer agents had already landed in chunks 0000–0012. `(cont.)` left literal
  per the marker rules.
- **Sense labels.** Rendered as adjectives, uniformly: `MORAL.`, `LITERAL.`,
  `ALLEGORICAL.` Migne prints `MORAL.`, `LITTERAL.`, `ALLEG.` — and at **0310D**
  he prints `ALLEG` with **no terminal point** (`de circuitu. ALLEG Quid per
  noctem`). Normalized to `ALLEGORICAL.` under Pattern 9 (section labels
  normalize in English; the misprint stays visible in the facing Latin column).
- **Quotation marks.** Migne prints no guillemets anywhere in these four chunks.
  None supplied. Hugh's very frequent `Ac si diceret:` / `Quasi diceret:`
  paraphrases are left unquoted, matching the plate; straight quotes are used
  once only (0309A, the term "leading down").
- **Lemma wording.** Hugh's Lamentations lemma is translated from Migne's page
  each time it recurs, including where his own repetitions differ from one
  another (see 0310C below). No conventional English Lamentations was consulted.
- **`deduc` / `educere`.** The whole gloss at 0308D–0310A turns on *deducere* =
  "lead DOWN" (*hoc est deorsum ducat*), so the lemma is rendered "Lead down thy
  tears like a torrent," not "Pour out"/"Let tears run down." From 0309B Migne
  switches to *educunt / educendae* ("lead out") while still glossing the same
  lemma; the switch is preserved, not harmonized.

## Chunk 0015

- **0309D–0310A** — *Sed quia multi coeptum quidem poenitendi studium non
  deserunt, sed tamen ab illo fervore suo, quo coeperant, paulatim tempore
  succedente tepescunt.* The *quia* clause has **no apodosis**: Migne closes it
  with a full stop and begins a new sentence, *Adjungit, dicens*. Rendered as
  printed, stop and all (Pattern 8: never delete, never supply). Probably the
  apodosis was absorbed into *Adjungit*.
- **0310A** — *Cum ergo requies non datur, pupilla quoque oculi clamat: si dum
  caro foris affligitur, intus mentis devotio orat.* Printed *si* gives "cries
  out: **if**, while the flesh is afflicted without, within the devotion of the
  mind prays" — which does not resolve. Conjecture: *sic* ("thus the pupil of the
  eye also cries out: while the flesh…"). Rendered as printed; *si* is a real word
  and takes no `[sic: …]` marker (Pattern 12 boundary).
- **0310A** — *quia item instantiam laboris devotio humilitatis commendabilem Deo
  reddit.* Printed *item* rendered "likewise"; possibly *ipsam*. Construable as
  printed, so rendered literally, no marker.
- **0310C** — *Et vide quomodo in arcto reposita est.* The feminine subject is
  unexpressed (*causa*? *res*?). Rendered "And see how it is set in a narrow
  strait."
- **0310C–D — DITTOGRAPHY, marked `[d: …]`.** Migne prints:
  *…quando parentes pro anima parvulorum suorum exorare jubentur. **Idcirco autem
  parentes pro anima parvulorum suorum exorare jubentur;** idcirco autem parentes
  pro parvulis suis supplicare debent…* The middle clause repeats the end of the
  preceding sentence and the opening of the following one; the sentence resumes
  correctly after it. Both occurrences are rendered, the second wrapped in
  `[d: …]` per Pattern 11. Looks like a compositor's doubling (eye-skip between
  two *idcirco autem* line-starts), not a genuine textual doubling.
- **0310C vs 0311C — lemma divergence inside the work.** At 0310C Migne prints the
  lemma *qui fame pereunt in capite omnium compitorum*; at 0311C, glossing the
  same words, he prints *Qui defecerunt fame in capite omnium compitorum* (Vulg.
  Lam. 2:19 *qui defecerunt in fame*). Both rendered as printed ("who perish with
  hunger" / "who have fainted with hunger"). Not marked `[var: …]`: the sense does
  not turn on it and the divergence is internal to Migne's own repetitions.
- **0309A** — the note `[n: (Psal. I)]` attaches to *Cor contritum, et humiliatum
  Deus non despicies*, which is Ps. 50:19. Migne's citation reproduced verbatim
  per the marker rule; recorded here only so the index's `refDisplay`/`refKey`
  split is not mistaken for our error.

## Chunk 0016

- **0312C** — *ille quasi aquas cor suum ante conspectum Domini effundit.* Plural
  *aquas* against the lemma's *sicut aquam*. Rendered "like waters"; real word,
  no marker.
- **0313B — CARRIED TYPE `[sic: *videmiaveris*]`.** *Vide, Domine et considera quem
  videmiaveris ita.* Non-word for *vindemiaveris*, which Migne prints correctly at
  the head of the same section (0313A) and again at 0314D. Carried into the
  English untranslated, in italics, inside a `[sic: …]` marker; the English
  sentence therefore lacks its verb, which is the plate's condition.
- **0313C** — *Ergone, Domine, mulieres comedent fructum suum.* A direct question
  closed with a **full stop**; the same question at 0314D is closed with `?`.
  Punctuation preserved as printed (Pattern 8).
- **0314A** — *et in libro Regum scriptum est [n: (II Par. XXVI)]*: the prose names
  Kings, the note cites Paralipomenon. Note reproduced verbatim; not adjudicated.
- **0314D** — *Unde cum magno pietatis affectu pronuntiandum est hoc, quod dicitur.
  Vide, Domine…* Full stop where a colon is expected before the lemma. Preserved.
- **0315B** — *Si occiditur in sanctuario Domini sacerdos, et propheta?* A
  conditional protasis closed with a question mark (as also at 0316A). Preserved.

## Chunk 0017

- **0316A — CARRIED TYPE `[sic: *praeteritotorum*]`.** *nisi prius per negligentiam,
  et praeteritotorum malorum memoria…* Non-word for *praeteritorum*; carried in
  italics inside `[sic: …]`.
- **0316B — Migne's own bracketed variant `[senex]`.** The lemma prints *puer, et
  senes [senex]*. Reproduced verbatim and untranslated, on the same footing as
  `[ *al.,* …]`; the head noun is rendered from the printed *senes* ("the old
  men"), and the whole gloss that follows in fact expounds the singular *senex*.
- **0316C — CARRIED TYPE `[sic: *cansam*]`.** *de nostra miseria tecum cansam
  inimus.* Non-word for *causam*; carried in italics inside `[sic: …]`.
- **0316C — Migne's bracketed variant, malformed italics.** The lemma prints
  `*Interfecisti in die furoris, percussisti, nec misereris [misertus es* ].` —
  the closing italic asterisk falls **inside** the bracket, before the `]`. The
  English mirrors the printed asterisk placement exactly rather than rebalancing
  it. Also note *in die furoris* without *tui* (Vulg. Lam. 2:21 *in die furoris
  tui*): rendered "in the day of fury," and kept so at both later repetitions
  (0317C, 0318C).
- **0317A — SPLIT TYPE `[sic: illuminabi te]`.** *et exsurge a mortuis, et
  illuminabi te Christus* (Eph. 5:14, Vulg. *illuminabit te Christus*). A
  word-division defect: the run is carried whole per Pattern 10, so the English
  clause has no finite verb. It sits inside an already-italic scripture lemma and
  therefore takes **no second italic marker** (nested italics not expressible), as
  Pattern 10 directs.
- **0317B — CARRIED TYPE `[sic: aculus]`.** *Lingua eorum gladius aculus* (Ps.
  56:5, Vulg. *gladius acutus*). Non-word; carried inside the italic lemma, so
  again without a second italic marker.
- **0317B — MISSING NEGATION. The most important entry in this stint.** Migne
  prints:

  > *Per virgines autem et juvenes, fideles signantur, qui et virgines sunt, quia
  > integritatem fidei pravis operibus **corrumpunt**…*

  As printed this says the faithful are called virgins *because they corrupt the
  integrity of faith by depraved works* — the opposite of the argument, whose
  parallel limb immediately follows in the correct polarity (*et juvenes sunt,
  quia per constantiam mentis adversis ingruentibus **non** cedunt*), and whose
  continuation a few lines later assumes that heretical doctrine is what
  *violates* that chastity of faith. Near-certain conjecture: **non corrumpunt**.
  Rendered exactly as printed — no negation supplied, no verb weakened — per
  Pattern 7a. The English is self-contradictory because the plate is.
- **0317C** — *quia cum, Deus, superbos despicias, ii qui haereticorum erroribus
  subvertuntur, constat quod nequaquam a cognitione veritatis caderent…* An
  apostrophe to God breaks into a third-person exposition and *ii qui…* is left
  without a governing verb (*constat quod* takes over). Rendered as closely as the
  printed words allow, with the anacoluthon intact.
- **0317D — MISSING TERMINAL STOP.** *…spinas et tribulos vitiorum germinare coepit
  Parvulus autem est sensus carnis…* No point between *coepit* and *Parvulus*.
  None supplied (Pattern 8): the English reads "…began to bring forth the thorns
  and thistles of the vices But the little child is the sense of the flesh."
- **0318A** — *quando id etiam nostrae corruptionis, quod vindicta praevaricationis
  intulit; carnalis mens…* A semicolon where the relative clause closes and the
  main subject begins. Preserved.
- **0318B** — *dum ex corruptis cogitationes corrumpuntur.* *ex corruptis* has no
  expressed noun (*affectibus*, from the preceding clause). Rendered "while
  thoughts are corrupted from corrupt ones."
- **0319A** — *ne evaderent manus inimicorum quando Sedechias…* No punctuation
  before *quando*. Preserved.
- **0319B** — *in Reblata* (Riblah) kept as Migne prints it.

## Chunk 0018

- **0319D** — *Quando multos, qui hic in disciplina Ecclesiae educati… inimicus
  consumet et secum ad perditionem trahet.* A *quando* clause standing as a whole
  sentence, with no main clause. Rendered as a sentence, as printed.
- **0320C — REAL WORD, WRONG: *in altare*.** *terretur quidem, sed non de circuitu,
  quia **in altare** peccatrix conscientia ex memoria sui reatus stringitur; ex
  altera parte in fiducia boni operis dilatatur.* The answering limb *ex altera
  parte* shows the sense required is *ex una parte* / *ex altera parte* — "on the
  one side… on the other side." *altare* ("altar") is a real Latin word, so under
  Pattern 12 it is rendered as ordinary English ("because **on the altar** the
  sinful conscience is straitened…") and takes **no** `[sic: …]` marker. The
  conjecture lives here.
- **0320D — REAL WORD, WRONG: *dies vocationis*.** *Dies enim solemnis, est dies
  quietis; dies vocationis quando operari non licet.* Sense requires *dies
  vacationis* ("a day of leisure"), which is what the following clause defines.
  Rendered as printed, "a day of calling" — which is not absurd here, since the
  paragraph is expounding *Vocasti*, and that may be why the error survived.
  No marker (real word).
- **0320D** — *Peccatum meum coram [contra] me est semper.* Migne's bracketed
  variant `[contra]` reproduced verbatim and untranslated inside the lemma
  (Vulg. Ps. 50:5 *contra me*).
- **0321B** — *quae prius tempora poenitenti indulta sibi neglexit.* Printed
  *poenitenti* (dat. sing. participle) where *poenitentiae* would be expected;
  rendered as a dative in apposition, "the times formerly granted to it as a
  penitent." Real word, no marker.
- **0322B — CASE MISMATCH: *ipsa carni desideria*.** *Vel per educatos, et
  enutritos, ipsa carni desideria accipere possumus.* Printed dative *carni* for
  genitive *carnis*. Rendered with the dative kept visible — "the very desires
  **belonging to** the flesh" — rather than smoothed to "of the flesh." Real
  word, no marker.
- **0322B** — *Novissime inimica destruetur mors* (1 Cor. 15:26, Vulg. *novissima
  autem inimica destruetur mors*). Migne prints the adverb *novissime*; rendered
  "Last of all the enemy death shall be destroyed." Sense unaffected, so no
  `[var: …]`.
- **0321B → 0322A.** The anchor sequence runs 0321B then 0322A, with no 0321C/D.
  Not reported as a defect: band marks are positional quarter-guides, and no text
  is broken across the anchor (a sentence closes at *Recte ergo dicitur.* before
  it). Logged only so a later reader does not re-open it.

## Polarity read, 2026-07-29 — one accuracy fix (OURS)

- **@0316A `instantia mala` — "present" → "impending". FIXED in `0017.md`.** Hugh sets
  prudence against *instantia mala prospiciendo* and devotion against *mala praeterita
  supplicando*, then names the pair himself three lines later: *praeteritorum malorum
  memoria, et futurorum cautela* (0316B). The antithesis is past against **future**, so
  *instantia* here is *impending*, not *present* — and "foreseeing present evils" does
  not cohere on its own terms either. Not a polarity defect (it flattens an antithesis
  rather than reversing a claim), which is why the blind reader correctly declined to
  report it as one and passed it on as an accuracy nit. Recorded here because a rejected
  candidate that is nonetheless a real error is exactly the kind of finding that
  otherwise evaporates.
