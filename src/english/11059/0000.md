## Little Notes on the Book of Judges

[0087A]The Book of Judges, which in Hebrew is called *Sopthim*, is placed seventh, after the five books of Moses and the book of Joshua. It describes the times of the judges, who after Joshua, down to Eli the priest, judged the people of Israel. In the surface of the letter it appears more open than the rest.

*[n: (JUD. I.)] Judah shall go up.* By Judah in this place we ought to understand not the person, but the people — namely the tribe of Judah — just as by Simeon [we understand] the tribe of Simeon, whom the tribe of Judah, about to fight against the Canaanites, calls to its aid. *Whom they followed and seized, the tips of his hands and feet having been cut off.* Certain men thought it more properly interpreted from the Hebrew truth: "the thumbs having been cut off," because, the thumb being cut off, a man is thereafter [0087B] not fit to bear arms. *They brought him to Jerusalem.* Since the Jebusites are read to have held Jerusalem down to the times of David, how are the children of Israel said to have brought Adonibezek into Jerusalem, as though into their own city? But what follows — *The children of Judah, therefore, assaulting Jerusalem, took it, and smote it with the edge of the sword* — solves the question. For it is to be understood thus: that first they took the city, then to the city, captured and subjected to their dominion, they brought the captured king. *Caleb, therefore, gave her the upper watered land and the lower watered land.* By "upper" and "lower" we understand the mountainous and the level regions. *And the children of the Kenite, the kinsman of Moses, went up from the city of Palms with the children of Judah into the wilderness of his lot, which is to the south of Arad, and they dwelt with him.* The Kenite is Jethro himself, [0087C] and Raguel, the father of Sephora, the wife of Moses, from whom the Kenites are named, who here are said to have gone up to the children of Judah and to have dwelt with them. *But the children of Benjamin did not destroy the Jebusite, the inhabitant of Jerusalem; and the Jebusite dwelt with the children of Benjamin in Jerusalem unto the present day.* Since above the children of Judah are read to have assaulted and taken Jerusalem, and to have given the whole city over to burning, how is it in this place recorded that the children of Benjamin did not destroy the Jebusite, the inhabitant of Jerusalem, but rather that the Jebusite himself dwelt with the children of Benjamin in Jerusalem unto the present day? It is understood, therefore, that the children of Benjamin — whose possession lay wholly next to the tribe of Judah, and touched Jerusalem — after the children of Judah [0087D] had taken and laid waste the city, began to dwell in it, and allowed the Jebusite himself, the inhabitant of the place, subdued by the children of Judah, to dwell with them; and in this way Jerusalem, down [0088A] to the times of David, was the dwelling of the Jebusites and of the children of Israel alike, until at last, by David himself, the Jebusite being fully cast out, Jerusalem was called to the lot of the children of Judah, and was named the city of David.

*[n: (JUD. II.)] And the angel of the Lord went up from Gilgal to the place of weepers:* by anticipation it is now called the place of weepers. For the name was given afterward. *Joshua, therefore, dismissed the people.* How is it that, when he has already said that Joshua was dead, and that after his death Judah went up to battle before the children of Israel, and the rest that has been said up to this point, he now afterward records that Joshua dismissed the people? But the narrative returns to what precedes, as if it said: in that, after the death of Joshua, the children of Israel preserved those nations which the Lord [0088B] had commanded to be destroyed, it is clear, after Joshua dismissed them, how, left to themselves, they scorned to keep the commandments of God. For after Joshua dismissed the people, and died, and the other elders who had known the law of God departed, the following generation, the worship of God being abandoned, began to serve idols and to provoke the wrath of God against themselves by depraved works. Thus what he says — *Joshua, therefore, dismissed the people* — is to be referred to what precedes.

*[n: (JUD. III.)] Who raised up for them a savior, Aioth, the son of Gera, the son of Jemini.* Jemini is Benjamin himself. For Benjamin is interpreted *son of the right hand.* The "ben," therefore, being removed — which is interpreted *son* — what remains, "jamini," makes "jeminum," that is, "of the right side," [0088C] or "right-hand," or "right" — however else it be formed — named from the right hand: whence "Jeminus" is not to be written with a *g,* like "geminus," which means "double," but with a *j* and an *e,* so that it be "jeminus," said from "jemin" or "jamin," as "dexter" [right] from "dextera." Hence the sons of Jemini are called the sons of Benjamin.

*[n: (JUD. IV.)] But he himself dwelt in Haroseth of the nations.* This region is called "of the nations" for this reason: that it was inhabited not by one people alone, but by many nations. *And she sent and called Barak, the son of Abinoem.* This Barak is thought to be the same as Lapidoth; and we believe this opinion arose from the fact that each name is found to have one and the same interpretation. For Barak, or Lapidoth, is interpreted *laughter* and *lightning.* Barak, therefore, [0088D] is himself Lapidoth, her husband, whom the woman, about to go to battle, calls as being her husband. *But Aber the Kenite had formerly withdrawn from the other Kenites, his brethren, the sons of Hobab, the kinsman of Moses.* We read in Exodus how [0089A] Jethro, the father-in-law of Moses, came to him in the wilderness, bringing to him Sephora his wife, and his sons; then, when Jethro himself returned to his own land, Moses kept with him his son Hobab, his kinsman — the brother, namely, of his wife — that he might set out together with the children of Israel to the land of promise. From the progeny of this Hobab, then, this Aber, descending from his other brethren, the sons of Hobab dwelling among the children of Israel — as we read above in this book of Judges, that *the children of the Kenite went up from the city of Palms with the children of Judah into the wilderness of the lot, and dwelt with* them — departing to another place of that same region, that is, to the valley of Sennim, dwelt in it; whose wife slew Sisera.
