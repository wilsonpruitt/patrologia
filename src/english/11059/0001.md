## Little Notes on the Book of Judges (cont.)

*[n: (JUD. V.)] And Deborah and Barak, the son [0089B] of Abinoem, sang in that day, saying: You who of Israel have willingly offered your lives to danger, bless the Lord.* This canticle Deborah and Barak sang after the victory, in praise of God. *Lord, when thou wentest out from Seir, and passedst through the regions of Edom, the earth was moved, and the heavens and the clouds dropped down water. The mountains flowed away before the face of the Lord, and Sinai before the face of the Lord, the God of Israel.* She recalls to memory the past marvels of God, for the increase of the present ones. And she signifies that once, when God, about to lead his people out of Egypt, went before them, furnishing guidance through the wilderness which is joined to the land of Edom and Seir, even the mute elements felt the presence of the Creator, and, as though terrified and disturbed by the majesty near at hand, by that very motion rendered [homage] to the present divine power of the Godhead. *The earth was moved.* Behold the sign of the present [0089C] Godhead. *He who founded it was alone able to move it.* We read in Exodus [n: (cap. XIX)] how Mount Sinai, when the Lord descended upon it, wholly smoked: and the whole mountain was terrible, and there went up from it as it were smoke from a furnace, and a most dense cloud covered the mountain. There, then, from the presence of the Creator, the moved earth smoked; there the heavens, dropping down water in a most dense cloud, attested their dread and reverence by a kind of sweat of their own; and the mountains, covered by the very overshadowing of the clouds, flowed away in the fluctuation of the flitting clouds and the descent of the waters. "Sinai before the face of the God of Israel" — by this one clause she comprehends all that precedes. Sinai was moved before the face of the Lord. Sinai dropped down water. Sinai flowed away before the face of the Lord. For what she had set forth, as it were generally, on account of [0089D] the excellence of the miracle — the earth was moved, the heavens and the clouds dropped down water, the mountains flowed away — she determines, specially adding where all this was fulfilled, saying: Sinai before the face of the Lord. But what she says — *The mountains flowed away* — can be understood as said by hyperbole, as though melted by excessive terror. And what she says — the earth was moved — can fittingly be referred to the disturbance of the men dwelling in the land, who, on hearing such great marvels, were moved and troubled. There follows: *In the days of Samgar, the son of Anath, in the days of Jahel, the paths rested; and they that went in through them walked through byways.* After the ancient marvels she proceeds to narrate new miracles. And she signifies that the children of Israel, before her times, were so hemmed in on every side by the terror of enemies [0090A] that the paths rested, no one walking through them; and if any were perchance compelled to go by them, they would avoid the assaults of enemies by going secretly, not by the public road, but through byways. And this happened even in the days of Samgar, who, though he was of such strength that he laid low six hundred men with a single ploughshare, nevertheless could not reach so far as to establish in the land so great and such a peace as God has now given to his people through this victory. This is what follows: *The strong men of Israel ceased and were at rest,* namely, not being able to resist their enemies. *Until Deborah arose: arose,* I say, *a mother in Israel.* Therefore *the Lord chose new wars,* destroying strong things through weak, laying low proud enemies through a woman. And that the miracle of God might be the greater, this was done not only with a woman as leader, but also [0090B] with a small band fighting. For there follows: *If a shield and a spear should have appeared among forty thousand of Israel.* There was not among so many thousands one who lifted the shield of Israel or a spear against the enemies, except a few, that is, ten thousand, who gave themselves to danger for the salvation of their people, of whom she adds: *My heart loves the princes of Israel;* to whom again: *You who of your own will have offered yourselves to danger, bless the Lord. You who ride upon sleek asses, and sit in judgment, and walk on the road.* You who now, the enemies having been laid low by the aid of God, are both in glory, and have power and peace, do not forget the works of the Lord by which these things have been bestowed on us. *Speak where the chariots were dashed together, and the army of the enemies was choked.* By the sleek asses she signifies glory; [0090C] by the sitting in judgment, power; by the walking on the road, peace. *There,* then, *let the justices of the Lord be told,* as regards the choking of the enemies; *and his clemency toward the strong men of Israel,* as regards the deliverance of his own; *Then the people of the Lord went down to the gates* — they who before, through fear of enemies, did not dare to go out, once the enemies were laid low not only went out, but even held dominion. Hence the joy and rejoicing that follows: *Arise, arise, Deborah; the Lord has fought among the strong: out of Ephraim,* or "in Ephraim," *he has destroyed them in Amalek, and after him;* or (as some think it more rightly interpreted from the Hebrew) *and after thee in Benjamin, against thy peoples, O Amalek.* The sense is this: for the increase of the present joy, she reckons up the past and future marvels of God [0090D] alongside those at hand. "The Lord, fighting among the strong, out of Ephraim." The Lord fought among the strong out of Ephraim; and through them he destroyed those who were in Amalek. Or: the Lord fought among the strong who were in Ephraim, and through them destroyed those who were in Amalek — so that the sense is: "I destroyed those who were in Amalek," as above. She notes how, in the time of Moses, Joshua (who was of Ephraim), the Lord fighting through him and in him, overcame Amalek; and this concerns the past. But concerning the future she adds, signifying that the Lord will yet, through Saul who is to be born of Benjamin, destroy Amalek; this is what follows: *And after him,* that is, Ephraim, or "after thee, O thou Ephraim," *the Lord fought among the strong in Benjamin,* that is, through the strong who will be in [0091A] Benjamin — namely, through Saul, who will be born of Benjamin; he will fight, I say, "against thy peoples," that is, against thy peoples, O Amalek. There follows: *From Machir princes came down, and from Zabulon those who would lead the army to make war.* This pertains to the present history. For she praises those who, from Machir, or from Zabulon, or from Issachar, did not hesitate to undergo the peril of war for their people. On the contrary she reproaches those who withdrew themselves, that is, Reuben and Gad, or Gilead, and Dan, and Asher, and marks them as worthy of derision for their dissembling. This is what follows: *Reuben being divided against himself, the contention of the great-hearted was found.* In the Hebrew it stands thus: Biflagoth ruben gotholib helchiche lem ; that is, *the divisions of Reuben, a great cunning of heart.* Which they think is [0091B] to be understood thus. Out of a great cunning of heart Reuben divided himself from the others, and set his borders far from the frequency of wars, in such a division on either side that, from wherever, whether on this side or that, wars might arise, to him resting securely there would come, not the weight of battle, but only the whisper of a slight report. Whence she adds: *Why dost thou dwell,* namely, O thou Reuben, *between two borders?* — that is, so that thou mayest hold to neither side; and thou doest this for this reason, *that thou mayest hear the whistlings,* that is, only a slight report, secure from afar; the whistlings, I say, *of the flocks,* that is, of the camps and armies — that thou mayest hear, I say, and do nothing; whence again she repeats, to his derision: the divisions of Reuben, a great cunning of heart. But if it be read thus: *The division of Reuben against himself is the found contention of the great-hearted* — it can be understood [0091C] in this way. When Reuben was divided against himself — that is, when he wavered, and fluctuated within himself with various thoughts and contrary desires, and quarreled with himself whether he should go to war or not, and made delay in this hesitation — the great-hearted of Zabulon and Nephthalim were found ready for the contest and the struggle. Or: Reuben being divided from the others, and withdrawing lest he go to battle — which nevertheless was against himself, that is, against his honor — the contention of the great-hearted was found. Afterward she turns her derision to others also: Gilead, Dan, Asher. These were idle, but *Zabulon and Nephthalim offered their lives to death in the region of Romae [Merome* ]. She notes the place where the battle was to be fought. In the Hebrew it stands: Romae Zachae, which is interpreted *upon the height [0091D] of the field.* And it is signified that in the field where the battle was fought they were made superior to their enemies, where they gave themselves to danger. So, then, "Romae" is not to be understood as the name of a place, but signifies a height — in which the translation erred. *The stars, remaining in their order and course, fought against Sisera.* Scripture does not affirm, as the astrologers think, that Sisera was conquered by the fate of a constellation, but figuratively says that the heavenly bodies themselves, as it were, bent their effort to his overthrow; because the Lord of heaven, by his providence, arranged that he be conquered. Just as, therefore, on earth the mountains, or valleys, or any other earthly things are blamed or praised when, by the men remaining in them and presiding over them, something blameworthy [0092A] or praiseworthy is done, and these very places are said to have done what was done in them by men; so the stars too are said to have done from heaven what God, presiding over them in heaven, did. *The stars, remaining in their order, fought.* She shows [their] intention and reason in that she says: Remaining in their order. For they remain where they make delay and strongly bend their effort. And they remain in order, where in what they do they do not forsake reason. *Curse the land of Meroth [Meros* seu *Merotz], said the angel of the Lord.* Either a messenger of God, coming to me, said [it], or I myself, being an angel of God, say to you on his behalf, that you curse the land of Meroth. It is the name of a region, or of the man its possessor, from whom the land was called Meroth. *Curse its inhabitants.* She adds the cause: *because they came not to the help of the Lord,* [0092B] that is, *to the aid of the most valiant*

*[n: (JUD. VI.)] Joas, the father of the family of Ezri [Ozri* ]. Here he calls "father" the greater, the elder, the more honorable among the others of that family, of which he himself also was.

*[n: (JUD. IX.)] From the temple of Baalberith.* Berith is interpreted *a conspiracy*; hence Baalberith, the idol of conspiracy. *He slew his brethren, the sons of Jerobaal, seventy men.* If he slew all seventy, how did one of them remain? But it is said thus: *he slew seventy, and one remained* — as if it were said: he slew seventy except one, who remained. *My wine, which gladdens God,* when it is sacrificed, and men, when it is drunk. *What is this Abimelech?* As if he should say: he is of no worth. *And yet another thing:* that he set his servant Zebul, a certain abject and vile man, as prince [0092C] over noble men.
