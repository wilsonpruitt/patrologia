## CHAP. XV.-- *On the mystical numbers of Holy Scripture*.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0022A]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Now they signify in these nine ways.*
⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to the order of position.* | 1
⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to the quality of composition.* | 2
⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to the manner of extension.* | 3
⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to the form of arrangement.* | 4
⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to reckoning.* | 5
⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to multiplication.* | 6
⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to the aggregation of parts.* | 7
⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to multitude.* | 8
⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *According to exaggeration.* | 9

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Numbers, therefore, signify in nine ways in the divine utterance: according to the order of position, according to the quality of composition, according to the manner of extension, according to the form of arrangement, according to reckoning, according to multiplication, according to [0022B] the aggregation of parts, according to multitude, according to exaggeration.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ According to the order of position: as unity, because it is first among numbers, signifies the beginning of all things. ⟦s2⟧ The number two, because it is second and is the first to withdraw from unity, signifies the sin by which there was a turning aside from the first good. ⟦s3⟧ Numbers signify according to the quality of composition, as the same number two, which admits of division and can be divided into two, signifies things corruptible and transitory. ⟦s4⟧ But the number three, because, a unit intervening in the middle, it does not admit of division so as to be divided into two equal parts, denotes things indissoluble and incorruptible.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Numbers signify according to the manner of extension, [0022C] as the number seven beyond the number six signifies rest after work. ⟦s2⟧ The number eight beyond the number seven, eternity after mutability. ⟦s3⟧ The number nine before the number ten, defect within perfection. ⟦s4⟧ The number eleven beyond the number ten, transgression beyond measure.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ According to the form of arrangement, as the number ten, which is stretched out lengthwise, signifies the rectitude of faith. ⟦s2⟧ The number one hundred, because it is spread out breadthwise, the amplitude of charity. ⟦s3⟧ The number one thousand, which is raised on high, denotes the height of hope. ⟦s4⟧ Rectitude toward oneself, breadth toward one's neighbor, height toward God. ⟦s5⟧ To the first and principal unity, therefore, out of these three members, the number ten is nearest by the order of position; the number one thousand by the form of arrangement. ⟦s6⟧ The one is nearer in place, the other more like in perfection.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0022D]According to the reckoning of number, as the number ten signifies perfection, because in it the extension of reckoning makes an end.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Numbers signify according to multiplication, as the number twelve is a sign of universality, because it is completed from three and four multiplied by one another; since four is the form of bodily things, three of spiritual.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Numbers signify according to the aggregation of parts, as six is the form of perfection, for this reason, that its parts — three, two, unity — gathered together fill up the whole; and they neither overflow beyond it, [0023A] nor fall short below it, which befits perfection, in which there ought to be neither more nor less than is right.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Numbers signify according to the multitude of parts, as two, on account of its two unities, the love of God and of neighbor. ⟦s2⟧ Three, on account of its three, the Trinity. ⟦s3⟧ Four, on account of the four seasons, temporal things, since the year and the world are divided into four parts. ⟦s4⟧ Five, the five [sic: *senius*]. ⟦s5⟧ Seven, the present age, which rolls round in seven days.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Numbers signify according to exaggeration, when the case demands that a thing be aggravated, and that answer be made with a certain exaggeration to those things which have gone before, such as that in Leviticus: *I will add [sic: Ad Adam] to your chastisements sevenfold for your sins [n: (Lev. XXVI)]*, where nothing else is signified than the multiplicity of the punishment, expressed [0023B] through the number seven. ⟦s2⟧ From few things let the wise man learn to weigh many.

## CHAP. XVI.-- *On the mystical places, times, and deeds of Holy Scripture.*

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ These things concerning numbers we have pursued somewhat more at length, on account of their manifold signification. ⟦s2⟧ But now let us turn our discourse to the fourth circumstance, that is, place. ⟦s3⟧ Places signify, whence the Lord willed certain matters to be transacted in certain and determined places, for the sake of the signification: as, for example, the children of Israel, going down into Egypt under the compulsion of famine, were oppressed with grievous servitude; but being led out thence by the Lord, and making their journey through the desert for forty years, they came into the land of promise, which is situated between [0023C] Babylon and Egypt. ⟦s4⟧ And both nations, that is the Egyptians and the Assyrians, led them captive; but the Egyptians first: all these things are apt for signification. ⟦s5⟧ Egypt, which is a voluptuous land and flowing with delights, signifies the world — not this frame of it, but the pleasures of the world and secular desires. ⟦s6⟧ The desert signifies the religious life, through which, as though returning to our homeland, we fast from the vices and concupiscences of this age. ⟦s7⟧ Babylon is set toward the north, where there is perpetual cold and darkness, since that region is never touched by the sun. ⟦s8⟧ Through the Assyrians, therefore, that is the Babylonians, the demons are fittingly denoted, who chose for themselves a seat toward the north, as being numb with the cold of unbelief and deprived of the light of truth. ⟦s9⟧ First [0023D] the Egyptians oppress Israel, then the Assyrians; for the devil can do nothing in us unless we are first drawn away by our own concupiscences. ⟦s10⟧ Whence: *Give me not over, O Lord, from my desire to the sinner,* that is, to the devil [n: (Psal. CXXXIX)].

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Times signify. ⟦s2⟧ For example, Jesus was in the porch of Solomon, and it was winter. ⟦s3⟧ Mention was made of winter for this reason, that through the quality of the season the quality of minds might be denoted, that is, the torpor and unbelief of the Jews.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Deed signifies, as is plain in the Gospel. ⟦s2⟧ Jesus came into Bethany and raised up Lazarus; then through the Mount of Olives he came into the valley of Josaphat, and sent his disciples into the city for the ass, etc. ⟦s3⟧ Bethany is the house of obedience. ⟦s4⟧ Only to the obedient [0024A] does Christ come, that he may raise up Lazarus, that is, the soul before dead in sins. ⟦s5⟧ Since, then, there are six circumstances which are said to signify, whichever of them signifies, either a deed signifies a deed and it is allegory; or a deed signifies a thing to be done, and it is morality. ⟦s6⟧ In these two we are instructed unto the knowledge of truth, that is, the integrity of faith, and unto the love of goodness, that is, unto the perfection of good works. ⟦s7⟧ For which two things divine Scripture must be read, namely that we may believe sincerely and work well.

## CHAP. XVII.-- *On the matter of Holy Scripture.*

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The matter of divine Scripture is the incarnate Word with all its sacraments, both those preceding from the beginning of the world and those to come up to the end of the age. [0024B] And it must be known that this whole series and extension of time is to be divided into two states, the old and the new; and three times, of the natural law, and of the written law, and of grace; and six ages. ⟦s2⟧ The first age from Adam to Noe. ⟦s3⟧ The second from Noe to Abraham. ⟦s4⟧ The third from Abraham to David. ⟦s5⟧ The fourth from David to the transmigration of Babylon. ⟦s6⟧ The fifth from the transmigration of Babylon to the coming of Christ. ⟦s7⟧ Likewise the five preceding ages, that is from Adam to Christ, are divided into four successions. ⟦s8⟧ The first, of the patriarchs, was from Adam to Moses. ⟦s9⟧ The second was from Moses to David, which is that of the judges. ⟦s10⟧ The third, which is that of the Kings, from David to the transmigration of Babylon. ⟦s11+12⟧ The fourth from the transmigration of Babylon to Christ; and this was the succession of the priests. [0024C] They are called states, because they pertain [sic: *adesse*] of man. ⟦s13⟧ The state is called old, because it was in fault and punishment up to the resurrection of Christ. ⟦s14⟧ But it is called new on account of the renewal of human life, which was made through the grace of Christ, up to the end of the age. ⟦s15⟧ Likewise it is called the time of the natural law, for this reason, that man was left to his own natural sense without a common precept. ⟦s16⟧ It is called the time of the written law, for this reason, that then the written law gave precepts of living among the people of God. ⟦s17⟧ The time of grace, because Christ freely gave it to fulfill what the law had commanded. ⟦s18⟧ The ages are called six after the likeness of the ages of men. ⟦s19⟧ For the world was both an infant and a boy, etc. ⟦s20⟧ And it must be noted that these ages are not divided according to equal spaces of time, [0024D] but according to common renewals of things; such as were the flood, and the election of Abraham, and the institution of kings, and the transmigration into Babylon, and the coming of Christ. ⟦s21⟧ It is called the succession of the patriarchs, because at that time the fathers alone ruled over their own sons: which lasted up to Moses, who, first holding the principate among the people of God, was appointed judge not only over his own sons, but over the whole people of Israel, although there were already many kings among the nations. ⟦s22⟧ Or it is named the succession of the patriarchs for this reason, that at that time those first fathers succeeded one another in turn, from whom the human race was disseminated and families derived and countries named, as from Edom the Edomites, from Levi the Levites, from Juda the Jews
