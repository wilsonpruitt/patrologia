## HERE BEGINS THE EXPLANATION.

[0413]

[0413D] *My soul doth magnify the Lord.* If we are willing to weigh the circumstance of the event, it will appear clearer than light of how great consideration these words are worthy. For a sacred interpretation is then more conveniently drawn out to clarity, [0414D] when it has been made known either through whom the mystical account was brought, or by what cause the narrator himself was impelled, so that he was either able or willing to relate such things. Let us therefore see how blessed Mary came [0415A] to the saying of these things which have been set forth. It is read in the Gospel of Luke [n: (Luc. I)], that *the angel Gabriel was sent from God into a city of Galilee, Nazareth, to a virgin espoused to a man whose name was Joseph, of the house of David; and the virgin's name was Mary:* to proclaim the new coming of the Son of God into flesh. He, entering, venerates the virgin with a new homage of salutation, saying: *Hail, full of grace, the Lord is with thee, blessed art thou among women. And when* Mary *had heard this*, not without great wonder *she was troubled at the saying of the angel, and thought what manner of salutation this should be.* But the angel, setting forth the cause of such a salutation and so great a veneration, comforted the holy virgin, that is, soothed her with sweet words, saying: *Fear not, Mary. For thou hast found grace with the Lord. Behold, [0415B] thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and shalt bring forth a son, and shalt call his name Jesus. He shall be great, and shall be called the Son of the Most High: and the seat of David his father shall be given to him, and he shall reign in the house of Jacob for ever; and of his kingdom there shall be no end.* And Mary: *How,* she says, *shall this be done, because I know not a man?* To whom at once the angel, setting forth the cause and manner of so ineffable a sacrament: *The Holy Spirit,* he says, *shall come upon thee, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow thee: and therefore also that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God:* and, that all ambiguity of believing might be taken away, by adding another miracle — wonderful, yet unlike — he makes manifest the efficacy of the divine power, saying: *And behold, Elisabeth thy kinswoman, she also hath conceived a son in her old age: and this is the sixth month with her that is called barren: because [0415C] no word shall be impossible with God.* Straightway the virgin, full of faith and exultation, answered to this with great rejoicing, saying: *Behold the handmaid of the Lord, be it done to me according to thy word.* At once, therefore, when the Holy Spirit came upon the Virgin, and the grace of all virtues filled the most sacred dwelling-place at the coming of the Son of God, there is no doubt that the Virgin herself conceived a wondrous and unspeakable sweetness of heavenly joys and of eternal sweetness, when that eternal light with all the splendor of its majesty descended into her; and that which the world does not contain placed itself wholly within the virgin's womb.

Filled therefore with such and so great a presence of divinity, what she saw, or what she felt, who can tell? I boldly declare that not even she herself could fully explain [0415D] what she was able to grasp. Amid such great wonders, then, how could a human tongue have kept silent, unless the same Spirit who had filled the virgin were tempering the torrent-rush of his own abundance with a most sweet embrace? The wisdom of God cried out then from afar in that blessed soul: *The Spirit breatheth where it will, and thou hearest its voice: but thou knowest not whence it cometh, or whither it goeth [n: (Joan. III)]*. For thou, it says, didst suddenly receive the Holy Spirit as it entered into thee; nor didst thou by thy knowledge anticipate its coming, so as either to seek it as about to come, or to love it as it came, or to open to it as it entered. Suddenly it glided into thee, freely offered itself, came unsought, unforeseen poured itself in. Thou perceivest the infusion, but thou dost not reach out [0416A] to the fountain of its immensity. And therefore thou knowest not whence it comes, because thou canst feel how much has been given to thee, but out of how great a source it has been given thou canst not search out. If therefore thou couldst not go before it as it came into thee, presume not to go before it as it will proceed through thee; for thou knowest not whither it goes, just as thou art ignorant whence it comes. *Keep the secret, guard what is committed, hide what is entrusted. It is not for thee to know the times or the moments, which the Father hath put in his own power [n: (Act. I)]*. He himself knows when, and to whom, and in what manner he may reveal the secrets of his magnificence; be thou only ready to obey him who commands, to render service to him who enjoins. Governing herself, therefore, by such consideration, Mary prudently chose meanwhile to keep silent, until the giver of the gift should by his wisdom deign to become the author of the revelation.

[0416B]But because, taught by the same Holy Spirit, she had learned, as to cover her own things through humility, so to rejoice together in the goods of others through charity, she rises straightway, and with the abundance of so great grace hastens to feast at the banquet of one lesser. She climbs up into the hill country of Judaea to see and to congratulate Elisabeth: that what, believing, she had heard about her she might see present in her, and receive it with worthy exultation. But she who had already run devoutly to proclaim the good things of another, deserved rightly to hear her own things proclaimed by others: so that her glory might grow also from this, that she did not envy the exaltation of another. Whence Elisabeth, filled with the Holy Spirit, recognized of what sort and how great she was who had come, and disclosed how unworthy she judged herself of her visitation, saying: *Whence is this to me, that the mother of my Lord should come [0416C] to me? For behold, as soon as the voice of thy salutation came in my ears, the infant in my womb leaped for joy. And blessed art thou that hast believed, because those things shall be accomplished in thee that were spoken to thee by the Lord [n: (Luc. III)]*. Then Mary said:

My soul doth magnify the Lord. She could therefore no longer contain herself, when she perceived that the Spirit, which she felt overflowing with such fullness within the secret places of her heart, had burst forth through the barriers of another's mouth. Then therefore, at the manifestation of the Spirit, she opened her mouth, and pouring forth the good word which she had conceived, cried out in praise of the Savior, saying: My soul doth magnify the Lord. Let no one, then, think these words to be lightly esteemed. For the things which were uttered out of so profound a conception cannot worthily be penetrated [0416D] without profound investigation. And would that it might befall us, as we seek their secrets, to advance under the leading of that Spirit, filled with which Mary merited to conceive the Word of the Father, and to magnify the Father of the Word with a word of exultation. She says, therefore: My soul doth magnify the Lord.

*And my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Savior.*

Truly beloved, and only one, and led into that wine-cellar by the king thy spouse, made drunk with the plenty of his house, and given to drink of the fountain of life (which is with him), thou hast poured forth the memory of the abundance of his sweetness, and hast exulted in his justice. Thou hast seen and hast tasted; thou hast seen majesty, thou hast tasted sweetness. And therefore what thou hadst drawn within, thou hast poured out abroad. My soul [0417A] doth magnify the Lord. See what she says: My soul, she says, doth magnify. And my spirit hath rejoiced. Two and two: soul and spirit, magnifies and hath rejoiced. The soul magnifies, the spirit exults; and again two: Lord and Savior; two words, one thing, and yet two: Lord and Savior; by Lord mark power, by Savior mercy. Let us therefore see the distinction of the words. First the soul magnifies the Lord; then the spirit exults in the Savior — she does not say the soul exults. Nor did she say: the spirit magnifies; but the soul, she says, magnifies, and the spirit exults; nor does she say it magnifies the Savior and exults in the Lord; but it magnifies the Lord, and exults in its Savior. First let us discern why she set them distinctly, magnifies and exults; or why [0417B] first it magnifies, afterward exults. For nothing is without reason; because everything that was said flowed forth from that inmost light of the highest truth, to which the virgin's mind excellently clung. Nor could she say otherwise — she who spoke not by meditating, but by tasting; whom not a thought running through diverse things taught, but a devotion of mind clinging through contemplation to the one fountain of wisdom. My soul doth magnify the Lord, she says, and my spirit hath rejoiced. For there are two things which the blessed spirits of angels and of men draw in eternal contemplation from that fountain of good. Namely, the incomprehensible majesty of God, and his ineffable goodness; of which the one begets chaste fear, the other brings forth love. For on account of majesty they venerate God, and on account of goodness they love him, lest either love [0417C] without reverence be dissolute, or reverence without love be penal. For wondering they love, and loving they wonder, so that love may burn inextinguishably through wonder, and wonder may glow sweetly in love.

On account of this reverence it is said that the pillars of heaven tremble before him; because doubtless even the powers of the heavens cannot behold so great a majesty without wonder. But the trembling of the blessed spirits is not a shaking of their tranquillity, but the unceasing and life-giving intentness of perpetual contemplation. For because they can never perfectly comprehend him whom they see, they ever look above themselves; wherein, as it were, they keep awake through wonder, lest they grow numb [0417D] at that which they can never comprehend. But the more keenly they behold, the more ardently they love; because to see him is to taste, and that which is seen is sweetness. But true sweetness, the more perfectly it is felt, the more desirably it is sought; because if what is perceived is truly sweet, it must needs be sweeter still, if it be more fully perceived. To this light of contemplation, therefore, the mind of Mary had been lifted up — she who expressed the sweetness of the heavenly homeland in her words as wonderfully as she comprehended it ineffably. For when she declared that she magnified the Lord, she manifestly showed that she beheld with inward vision that majesty of the eternal Godhead, venerable and to be revered by all. But when she asserted that she exulted in her Savior, [0418A] she showed that she had perceived a taste of inward sweetness. Whence she professed both, namely Lord and Savior: that on account of the power by which he rules over all his creation she might show him to be rightly feared even by those by whom he is not loved; but on account of the goodness by which he mercifully saves some, she might demonstrate him worthy of love. Of course, because *all the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth [n: (Psal. XXIV)]*, it is perfect praise to confess him Lord and Savior, since truth is commended in the Lord and mercy in the Savior. For truth pertains to the Lord, and mercy to the Savior. For, because he governs all his works with so great and so perfect a justice that even that which is found in them to have been done apart from justice he does not leave disordered; and because what has been done wrongly [0418B] can neither escape his judgment nor run counter to the laws of the eternal disposition, he keeps the tenor of justice in truth.
