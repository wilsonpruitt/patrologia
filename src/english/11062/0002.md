## HERE BEGINS THE EXPLANATION. (cont.)

But because he gathers certain wandering things freely unto life, and restores them unto salvation in his judgment, he tempers justice by the gentleness of mercy. Therefore we magnify the Lord, and exult in the Savior; because the justice of the Lord is to be revered by everyone, and the mercy of the Savior to be observed with exultation. And therefore, Mary says: My soul doth magnify the Lord: And my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Savior. Why does the soul magnify, and the spirit exult? Perhaps the same thing is repeated in another word. For soul and spirit in man are the same, although soul denotes one thing and spirit another. For spirit is said with reference to [0418C] substance, soul with reference to vivification. Nevertheless, since the occasion has offered itself, I think it not out of place to recall here the error of certain men. For there are those who contend that in each man there are two souls: one rational and one sensual, devoid of reason, such as are the souls of brute animals. And this opinion they attempt to confirm by certain reasonings and authorities. For they say that the rational soul is infused only into a body already formed in the womb, just as it is read that in the first man the body was first formed, and then the breath of life breathed in. And Moses says in the law: *But if anyone strike a pregnant woman and she miscarry: if the abortive fetus were formed, let the striker [0418D] render a life for a life; but if it were not formed, let him be fined a sum of money [n: (Exod. XXI)]*. Certain of the holy Fathers also are found to have asserted this in their treatises.

And therefore, since it is established that the rational soul is given only to a formed body, and again that matter, like the body itself, is moved and grows before it receives human form, and by the very vital motion which is in it is led to this very form, they think it must be conceded without any contradiction that, before the human body receives a rational soul, it has a sensual soul, by which it lives and is quickened and takes on growth and form, so much so that, if to the seed once conceived and formed a rational soul were not given, then, with that irrational soul which it has from [0419A] the first conception, there would be born of the man, in human form, a brute animal, differing in nothing from the other irrational creatures, except that it had drawn its substance from human seed. For since this belongs naturally to the seed of brute animals, that of itself, when the time comes, it receives vivification, it seems unworthy to deny this to human seed, which is established to be more excellent in its nature. After all this they also bring forward, in testimony of their assertion, that in the catholic Scriptures we frequently find, in one person, soul and spirit named by a doubled term; and in the prayers of the Church daily, without any suspicion of error, when we render the office at the funeral of the faithful, we commend the soul and spirit of the deceased to the Lord. By this [0419B] reasoning, then, they wish to prove that each man has two souls: one by which he lives, another by which he knows; and both to be beatified hereafter in the elect — the one, that is, the rational, through the vision of the Creator; the other, that is, the sensual, through the incorruption of the body. Likewise in the reprobate, both to be tormented: the one by fire, the other by an evil conscience.

But the catholic faith does not accept an assertion of this kind, but most truly testifies that the soul is one and the same, which in man both furnishes the life of the body through sense, and lives in itself through the intellect. Nor, if a rational soul is not given to the human body before its formation — although it be moved and grow before it receives human form — is it therefore necessary [0419C] that we say this comes about through some soul, since we manifestly see shoots and herbs move and have growth without a soul. Unless perhaps someone should wish to call this very vegetation and natural motion a soul. But this force, although in some respect it may be called a soul, is nevertheless in no way to be called sensual, or such as would make a soul. For it is ridiculous, and altogether beyond reason, that we should say a human body is born a beast without a rational soul, and not rather that it neither lives nor is born, if it has not been vivified by a rational soul. For as to what they say — that it is unworthy that human seed, according to the natural conception, should be believed weaker unto vivification than the seeds of the other animals, and less [0419D] efficacious — it is plain that this is of no weight, since we see nearly all brute animals surpass man in vigor of sensation; nay rather, from this very fact it is more probably proved that the seed is vivified and perceives sense only from a rational soul; because it was perfectly just that to brute animals, to which nothing was to be given in intellect, something more should be given in sense, and, on the contrary, that so much the greater necessity of exercising reason should be laid upon man, the greater the defect he suffered in his bodily senses. But that too — that in Holy Scripture we sometimes find the words spirit and soul in designating one and the same person — was not done for the sake of signifying diverse essences, but for the sake of a diverse property of the same essence. For one and the same spirit [0420A] is called spirit with reference to itself, and soul with reference to the body. Whence also those spirits which were first created, that they might persist in their purity and not be mingled with bodies, can be called spirits, but cannot be called souls; because they have a spiritual nature, but have not bodily animation. But the spirits of brute animals, because they are essentially body, and have no being outside bodily vivification, are more properly called souls than spirits. But the human soul, because it has being both in the body and outside the body, is properly called both soul and spirit. But it is called soul in so far as it is the life of the body; and spirit in so far as it is a spiritual substance endowed with reason. Therefore in this life the soul is lost that the spirit may be saved, when this life [0420B] is despised for God's sake, that afterward eternal life may be granted by God. But because that which we lose is, as to essence, the very same as that which we receive, therefore the Lord in the Gospel by no means commanded us to lose the soul that we might receive the spirit safe, but said that this very same soul must be lost here, that in the future it might be received safe [n: (Matth. X)], signifying assuredly this: that whoever for God's sake willingly despises this life, which now consists of the body's vivification from the soul and is temporally mortal, shall in the future receive that same life of the body (not only of the soul) as eternal and immortal. Whence also the holy Church, which most faithfully believes the resurrection of the flesh, prays not only for the spirits, but also for the souls of her faithful [0420C]: asking indeed this, that in the visitation of the just, together with that beatitude which shall belong to clean hearts from the vision of God, they may also receive, unto the glory of eternal life, as immortal and incorruptible through the resurrection of the flesh, that which now, through the death of the flesh, they lay down as corruptible. And these things indeed concerning the difference of soul and spirit we have said beside the point, but perhaps not without profit. Now let us return to the order of our narration, as we began.

We ask, then, what such a distinction of words means; whether it hints something to us that she says not the spirit, but the soul magnifies the Lord, and testifies that the spirit, not the soul, exults in its Savior. And perhaps someone may think it is to be ascribed to curiosity rather than to diligence [0420D] thus to scrutinize each single thing, and not to pass over even the least without its own consideration. I know that many things in this manner, for the sake of stirring the affections, are in the Scriptures either said by way of emphasis, or repeated by way of inculcation. And if anyone should say that the same was likewise done in this case, there is nothing unfitting. For it was said thus: My soul magnifies and my spirit exults, as if it were said: I, out of my soul and my spirit, that is, out of my whole heart and my whole will, praise God, and over his salvation, which I now see being prepared for the human race, I exult with all my heart. For now I see that which I believe ought to be offered for me being taken from me. But nevertheless, that I may confess my Savior, who, although he has not yet offered the victim of his flesh, has nevertheless already taken on the flesh [0421A] which, when the time comes, he may offer as a victim. Therefore he is Savior even now; and not only now, but from eternity Savior, who has now come to save through the flesh he has assumed, but has from eternity purposed to give that salvation which he will manifest in its own time. Or certainly, according to the distinction of the words, the soul is fittingly said to magnify God, and the spirit is declared to exult in its Savior. For often in the Scriptures a certain affection and tenderness of mind is wont to be designated by the soul; and therefore, when she asserts that she magnifies out of her soul, she shows that she venerates God by no means with servile fear, but with the affection of love. And again, when she declares that her spirit exults in its Savior, she manifestly shows that this is not a salvation over which the flesh [0421B] may rejoice, but rather an eternal one, prepared in invisible goods, over which the spirit exults. Because, therefore, that blessed soul had conceived the sweetness of chaste fear, she said that she magnified the Lord not with servile dread, but with filial love. For in the soul, as has been said, the affection and devotion of one who loves is expressed, since to praise out of the soul is nothing other than to venerate out of affection, and to revere out of love. But because again she had drawn the certainty of eternal salvation from that fountain of piety, therefore she exulted in spirit in her Savior.

Nor is it to be passed over in silence that, when she named the Lord, she added nothing. But when she said Savior, she named not simply Savior, but her Savior. For almighty God, by the power [0421C] by which he rules over all his creation, is the Lord of all; but by the mercy by which he restores to life only some, and not all, he is not the Savior of all. For his dominion regards all equally; but his goodness sets apart only some for salvation. And therefore his lordship is said specially of no one; but of the salvation which is from him, the elect alone (as of a gift proper to them) rightly glory in a singular way. This is why in Holy Scripture he willed himself to be called specially the God of certain ones: because he who granted to all that they might be, gave himself as a reward to the good alone, that they might be blessed. *I am,* he says, *the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, the God of Jacob [n: (Matth. XXII)]*, because, whereas the rest have from me only that they may be, these, chosen by grace that they may be blessed, [0421D] are what they are from him from whom they have their being; that is, I gave them my very self; and therefore I will to be called their God: because I am theirs whom they have taken, or received, through grace — whom they could not have through nature, nor deserved through fault. But now they possess me, and behold I am their inheritance, I am their God and Savior, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, the God of Jacob. Rightly, therefore, the blessed virgin, who saw herself singularly chosen because she had singularly received grace, as if confirmed by a certain privilege of divine election, confidently calls him whom she had conceived as a son for the salvation of the world her own Savior too, with gladness and exultation.
