## HERE BEGINS THE EXPLANATION. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Because he hath regarded the lowliness of his handmaid.* ⟦s2⟧ This is [0422A] the cause of her exultation, because he regarded the lowliness of his handmaid. ⟦s3⟧ As if she should say: Rightly do I exult in him, because it is from him that I exult; and because I love his gifts for his own sake, therefore I exult in him. ⟦s4⟧ Let us distinguish these two: Some exult neither from God nor in God: for those who exult in the pleasure of the flesh, or who, according to Solomon, *rejoice when they have done evil, and exult in the most wicked things [n: (Prov. II)]*: these exult neither from God nor in God. ⟦s5⟧ For, because that whence they exult is evil, it is plainly evident that that whence they exult is not from God. ⟦s6⟧ And because again they exult from evil to evil, and set their joy in malice, therefore they in no way exult in God. ⟦s7⟧ There are others who abuse the gifts of grace they have received, and turn those things which were given for the salvation of the soul to the use of the flesh and the glory [0422B] of the world. ⟦s8⟧ They rejoice in the gifts of God they have received, and are glad to have what God has bestowed: not that by this they may be helped to reach him, but that they may be shown to go before others in the reception of grace. ⟦s9⟧ These, although they may seem to have from God that whence they rejoice, nevertheless by no means rejoice in God, because they love neither in God nor for God's sake that which they have received from God. ⟦s10⟧ But those who, having received grace, turn that very thing which they receive from him to the love of God, these assuredly are proved to exult both from God and in God. ⟦s11⟧ Wherefore we must carefully consider, while we feel our mind perhaps touched by some gladness, lest that very thing which lifts up the mind through joy either arise from evil, or, having arisen from good, drive the mind toward evil through its intention. ⟦s12⟧ Mary therefore, that she might show her joy to be her wonted joy [0422C], showed it to be neither sprung from vanity nor turned to vanity: but declared that she loved God's gifts unto God, and that on account of the regard of grace by which she had been prevented, she exulted in her Savior, saying: My spirit hath rejoiced in God my Savior; because he hath regarded the lowliness of his handmaid. ⟦s13⟧ Of course, the regard of God in Holy Scripture is wont to be taken in three ways: namely, according to knowledge, according to grace, according to judgment. ⟦s14⟧ Concerning the regard of divine knowledge the Apostle says: *All things are naked and open to his eyes [n: (Hebr. IV)]*. ⟦s15⟧ Therefore through knowledge God regards all things; but through grace he does not regard all. ⟦s16⟧ For concerning the regard of grace it is said: *The eyes of the Lord are upon the just, and his ears unto their prayers [0422D] [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]*. ⟦s17+18⟧ Which regard, namely, those do not merit to whom at the end it shall be said: *I know you not [n: (Matth. XXV)]*. ⟦s19⟧ Concerning the regard of judgment it is said: *The eyes of the Lord behold the good and the evil [n: (Prov. XV)]*. ⟦s20⟧ And again: *His eyes are upon [respicit Dominus] every way of the sons of man, and he considereth all their steps. ⟦s21⟧ There is no darkness, and there is no shadow of death, that they who work iniquity may be hid there [n: (Prov. V)]*. ⟦s22⟧ Therefore for God to see through knowledge is to be ignorant of none of the things that are. ⟦s23⟧ To see through grace is to bestow the gifts of mercy. ⟦s24⟧ To see through judgment is to destine each one according to his works, either to punishment or to glory. ⟦s25⟧ But because in this place it is the regard of grace that is treated, let us yet more diligently consider how God regards man through grace. ⟦s26⟧ For the very word [0423A] *regard* marks a certain emphasis, so that to regard seems to be something more than to see. ⟦s27⟧ For to regard is, as it were, to visit those previously cast off and forsaken. ⟦s28⟧ For God is said to be, as it were, turned away from man when, through the severity of judgment, he withdraws the gifts of his grace. ⟦s29⟧ But when, appeased through mercy, he restores what was withdrawn, he again turns himself toward him through the regard of grace. ⟦s30⟧ Well, therefore, does Mary testify that the Lord regarded in her humility alone, because the propitiation of the Godhead, which human nature lost in the first parents through pride, it recovered in Mary through humility. ⟦s31⟧ For, because in her the Word of the Father took on the substance of flesh, which he would unite to himself, he looked, as it were, through mercy [0423B] toward exalting that nature which he had previously cast off. ⟦s32⟧ God, therefore, regarded the humility of Mary: to whom, on account of the merit of humility, he granted that she should conceive his Son in her flesh, and of her flesh bring forth true God and man, the Savior of all men (as far as in him lies). ⟦s33⟧ The virtue of which humility, determining it straightway, she subjoins: of his handmaid. ⟦s34⟧ For, because she humbly recognized herself to be what she was, a handmaid, therefore she merited to be sublimely what she was not, a mother; but because we have said that in this, that she called herself a handmaid, the virtue of humility is expressed, that it may appear how this saying commends humility, we ought to distinguish the kinds of servitude. ⟦s35⟧ For servitude is varied in four ways: according to condition, according to necessity, according to fear, according to love. ⟦s36⟧ According to condition, all things [0423C] ought to be subject to the divine servitude, because a work owes this to its maker, from its own condition, that it obey his dispositions and follow his ordinances: so that, just as it was made by him, so it may proceed only under him and according to him. ⟦s37⟧ But according to necessity, perverse wills are said to serve God, which, although they strive to run counter to his commands, are nevertheless constrained by his ineffable disposition, so that they can bring nothing to effect without his nod. ⟦s38⟧ They serve his disposition unwillingly, who are not willingly subject to his precept. ⟦s39⟧ There follows the third servitude, which is done through fear, when we fulfill the divine precepts not out of love, but out of dread. ⟦s40⟧ But the fourth servitude is when we voluntarily obey his commands; because we love him who commands, [0423D] and seek no other advantage in our servitude outside himself, than that, walking according to him, we may be able to reach him. ⟦s41⟧ For this is to do for his sake what he commands: to do what he commands for the sake of attaining him. ⟦s42⟧ But of these four servitudes, that one especially seems to me to be commended in this place, which is according to condition. ⟦s43⟧ This indeed our parents in paradise refused to render to their Creator, when, lifted up into pride, they disdained to be under him by whom they had been created, and perversely wished to be like him in majesty, who were not equal to him in nature. ⟦s44⟧ Fittingly, therefore, does grace answer to fault. ⟦s45⟧ Eve, through pride, not considering that she was a creature of God and the work of God, [0424A] therefore wished to be made equal; but Mary, humbly submitting herself to her maker, called herself a handmaid: and therefore the one was cast off, and this one chosen. ⟦s46⟧ The proud he disdained, and the humble he regarded: and what the proud woman lost, the humble one received. ⟦s47⟧ Therefore she says: He hath regarded the lowliness of his handmaid. ⟦s48⟧ He regarded the humility, he regarded the humiliation. ⟦s49⟧ He regarded the humility, rewarding the humble; he regarded the humiliation, exalting her who was humbled. ⟦s50⟧ There are two things, humility and humiliation: humility is within, in the virtue of the mind; humiliation is without, in the abjection of human esteem. ⟦s51⟧ But the servants of God sometimes have, along with humility, humiliation also. ⟦s52⟧ Sometimes they have humility, but do not have humiliation. ⟦s53⟧ But never do they have humiliation without humility. ⟦s54⟧ They have humility with humiliation [0424B] who are both humble before God and despicable before men. ⟦s55⟧ They have humility without humiliation who, although before men outwardly they do not appear despicable, nevertheless inwardly preserve the merit of humility inviolate before God. ⟦s56⟧ To these the world is crucified, and they to the world, because through humility they despise the world, and through humiliation they are despised by the world. ⟦s57⟧ But to these others the world indeed is not crucified; yet they themselves are crucified to the world, because the human glory which they receive outwardly unsought, they inwardly despise, offered through the virtue of humility. ⟦s58⟧ Therefore Mary, who was humble before God and, for God's sake, cast off before men, testifies that in both she was regarded by God, because both her humility was made acceptable before God [0424C], and her humiliation before men was changed into glory. ⟦s59⟧ Whence there follows:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For behold, from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed.* ⟦s2⟧ For until then she had borne among men the reproach of barrenness, because she preferred the integrity of virginity to the marriage-bed. ⟦s3⟧ But whence, in the former carnal generation, she bore as it were a sentence of malediction, thence now she is praised by every generation with a merited benediction: to whom alone among all women this was granted, that she should both have the fruit of fecundity, and not lose the integrity of virginity. ⟦s4⟧ For God regarded her humility, and took away her humiliation, and therefore, she says, from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed. ⟦s5⟧ All the generations, which shall recover through the fruit [0424D] of my womb the lost beatitude of which they were deprived through the fruit of the forbidden tree; all shall call me blessed, so that it is now a small thing to have borne in the past generation the reproach of barrenness — I who by every future generation, on account of the fruit of my fecundity, shall be called blessed. ⟦s6⟧ *From henceforth,* she says. ⟦s7⟧ As if Elisabeth should say: From the time when through thy mouth he opened his great deeds, which he wrought in me, from henceforth he will magnify the same, making them manifest unto all generations.
