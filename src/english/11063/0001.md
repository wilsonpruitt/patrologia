## HERE BEGINS THE EXPOSITION.

[0371]

[0371D] *[n: (ABDIAS I.)] The vision of Obadiah.* Obadiah is interpreted *the Lord's servant,* by whom the Savior is signified. Whence also in Isaiah, under the person of the Father speaking to the Son, it is read thus: *Hear, O Jacob, my servant, and Israel whom I have chosen [n: (Isa. XLI)]*. Likewise to the same: *Thou art my servant, Israel; I have given thee for a light of the nations, that thou mayest be my salvation [0372D] even to the farthest part of the earth [n: (Isa. XLIX)]*. Hence the Savior himself, reproaching the human race, says thus: *Thou hast made me,* he says, *to serve in thy sins [n: (Isa. XLIII)]*. But since there are four kinds of servitude — for one is necessary, another conditional, another for hire, another free — let us see under which kind of servitude [0373A] liberty served, strength was weakened, blessedness humbled itself, Deity bowed itself down. For he did not serve under the first kind, who did nothing of necessity; nor under the second, who led captivity captive; nor under the third, who gave gifts to men; but under the fourth. Hence that word: *Holocaust and sin offering thou didst not require: then said I, Behold I come [n: (Psal. XXXIX)]*. Likewise: *In the head of the book it is written of me, that I should do thy will: O my God, I have willed it, and thy law in the midst of my heart [n: (ibid.)]*. Truly a free servitude, by which neither was nature corrupted, nor condition changed, nor a bargain struck, but an identity of will bowed to obedience. Hence that word: *You were sold for nothing; and you shall be redeemed without money [n: (Isa. LII)]*. So much for the name. Now let us speak of the vision. It is the idiom of prophetic speech that [0373B] a consolation to come is intimated there where a vision is set down. Whence Obadiah too set down, at the opening of his prophecy, as a vision the destruction of the adversary nation, that is, of Idumaea — a destruction which brought great consolation to the Jewish people — saying: The vision of Obadiah. For the guarding of humility, the prophet speaks of himself as of another; because he deserves to lose a privilege who abuses a gift granted him, or a power permitted. The vision of Obadiah is the incarnation of the Word. Of which vision it is said through the Psalmist: *The Lord hath looked down from heaven; he hath beheld all the sons of men [n: (Psal. XXXII)]*. The eyes of the Word were judgment and mercy. With these eyes he saw the form of our redemption, when he set forth the incarnate truth of judgment and the mercy of reward; the judgment of discernment, and the mercy of remission. With these eyes [0373C] he looked upon Peter, when both by the truth of judgment he called him to weeping, and by his mercy blotted out his sin [n: (Luc. XXII)]. By the first, in opening it, he washed away the fault; by the second, he restores grace. These are the eyes the bride praises, saying in the Canticles: *Thy eyes are as doves upon the brooks of waters [n: (Cant. V)]*. The waters are the heavenly gifts of wisdom; the brooks are the varieties of the gifts; the dove is the grace of the giver. The eyes therefore are doves upon the brooks of waters; because both by the truth of judgment he freely enlightens, and by the kindness of mercy he freely adorns with gifts. The vision therefore of Obadiah is the incarnation of Wisdom, which looked upon the world with both eyes. According to that word in the Gospel of John: *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, [0373D] and we saw his glory, the glory as of the only-begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth [n: (Joan. I)]*. There follows:

*Thus saith the Lord to Edom.* That is, against Edom. What is it to say, but to bring forth a word? And what is it to bring forth a word, but to come forth from what is hidden? The Lord therefore says, that is, the Father brought forth the Word, when the Son came forth from the Father and came into the world. But because [he had come] to triumph over the devil, therefore the Lord; because to free the human race freely, therefore God; because he had come to tread down the world and its pomp, therefore he who speaks against Edom: the Lord who is to be feared, God who is to be loved, against Edom which is to be trodden down. To be feared by the devil, to be loved by his own people, the world to be trodden down by him. The Lord by reason of the kind of his victory, and God by reason of the gifts of grace, against [0374A] Edom by healing the languor of concupiscence. The Lord therefore, because he laid the enemy low manfully; God, because he freed the citizen mercifully; against Edom, because he stormed the world's concupiscence as a foe. Edom is Esau himself, who was also called Seir; the brother of the simple Jacob, who, for a craving after lentils, lost the glory of the birthright, and through envy of his brother lost the grace of his father's blessing. Hence, roused against his brother with grievous hatred, while he nursed the venom lurking in his [sic: *cordo*], corrupting the covenants of love, he embittered his inward parts to the murder of his brother, seeking a place and a time for the mischief. Whence he said: *The days of mourning for my father will come: and then I will slay him [n: (Gen. XXVII)]*. By him therefore is designated the prudence of the flesh, the concupiscence of the world, the [0374B] worst seed, wicked and criminal children, enemies of the grace of God. Edom, *earthy;* Esau, *bloody;* Seir, *hairy.* Bloody in sins, hairy in businesses, earthy in cares. Bloody, the world assuredly, in idolaters; hairy, in heretics; earthy, in carnal men and false Christians. But against Edom the Lord sent the Word, which both blotted out the handwriting of sin, and bestowed spiritual rest, and promised eternal happiness. Pardon destroyed the handwriting, grace the business, glory the care: the pardon of sinners, the grace of the devout, the glory of the eternal. Hence David: *He sent his word, and healed them, and delivered them out of their destructions [n: (Psal. CXVI)]*. This Word, I say, *enlighteneth every man that cometh into this world [n: (Joan. I)]*. This *catcheth the little foxes [0374C] that destroy the vines [n: (Cant. II)]*; this *maketh poor and maketh rich; he humbleth and he lifteth up [n: (I Reg. II)]*. There follows:

*We have heard a hearing from the Lord, and he hath sent an ambassador to the nations.* LITERAL. What he had said obscurely before, he now sets forth more plainly: namely the coming of Christ for the destruction of sin, for the salvation of the world, for the knowledge of the true, for the love of the highest good. The ambassador is he of whom Haggai speaks: *Behold there cometh,* he says, *the desired of all nations, and his glory filleth the whole earth [n: (Agg. II)]*. This ambassador, under the habit of poverty, brought us the riches of immortality. Whence the Apostle: *Who,* he says, *for us became poor, that by his poverty we might be enriched [n: (II Cor. VIII)]*. This ambassador brought three things with him: light, ointment, [0374D] bread. Light for the blind, ointment for the sick, bread for the hungry. He set forth the light on the mountain, the bread in the upper room, the ointment on the gibbet of the cross. The light in the prison, the bread in the desert, the ointment he laid in the theater: in the prison of ignorance, in the theater of carnal concupiscence, in the desert of human misery. For Christ enlightened the world, ignorance being driven out; he himself filled it, concupiscence being set in order; the same changed it for the better, misery being banished. Whence he himself in the Gospel: I am, he says, the way, the truth and the life [n: (Joan. XIV)]. The way by enlightening, the truth by healing, the life by making blessed. First against ignorance, secondly against concupiscence, thirdly against misery. But it must be asked where, or from whom, the prophet had received anything about this embassy. [0375A] Let Moses leap forth into the midst, and disclose whatever he had received of it. *A prophet,* he says, *will the Lord raise up of your brethren: him you shall hear as myself [n: (Deut. XVIII)]*. Isaiah too: *From the ends of the earth,* he says, *we have heard praises, the glory of the Just One [n: (Isa. XXIV)]*. Consider, reader, the praises and the glory of the Just One. The glory in the future, the praises in the present. Manifold praise indeed he has earned; because he gave both health to the sick, and plenty to the needy, and liberty to captives. Let his praise therefore be in my mouth, who has both strengthened me with health, and enriched me with plenty, and endowed me with liberty. After this earnest, if I receive it purely and faithfully, I in no way doubt that I shall receive the glory of the Just One. For we have heard a hearing from the Lord, and he hath sent an ambassador to the nations. But in that he says we have heard a hearing, the expression [0375B] is indeed less than Latin, but faithfully rendered from the Greek. The repeating of the word, however, signifies [sic: *sifignicat*] the intention of the utterance. Historically, moreover, the coming of the king of Babylon upon the Idumaeans is signified: for whom the Lord sent, when by a just judgment he permitted him to come. For this man was called the hammer of the whole earth, through whom the Lord deigned to execute the judgments of a most just vengeance, and through whom, by the just judgment of God, the pride of countless peoples was ground down. Whence it is read in another prophecy: *Son,* he says, *of man, what shall be given to the king of Babylon for his labor, wherewith he served me at Tyre? Give,* he says, *him the kingdom of Egypt.* Consider, reader, the form of divine providence and the order of vengeance and the rule of justice. The Babylonians, indeed, overcame the Assyrians, the Medes the Babylonians, [0375C] the Macedonians the Medes, the Romans the Macedonians, Christ the Romans; but the last victory was not wrought out of cruelty, but out of kindness and clemency. Upon the others, however, according to the quality of their deserts, the vengeance of punishments was worthily brought. These things we have said by way of digression, according to the history.

Now let us discuss them allegorically. *Being sent,* he says, *as an ambassador from the vale of Hebrom he came into Sichem [n: (Gen. XXXVII)]*; because Christ, mighty in Deity, and increasing the kingdom of everlastingness by the orders of the heavenly hosts, came to the labor of the passion, but wandered in the field, and found in the world none free from sin. *Whence, seeking his brethren, he went down into Dothain [n: (ibid.)]*, because, thirsting for our reconciliation, he humbled himself even to the failing of death. Hebrom is interpreted [0375D] strength or *everlasting increase;* Sichem, *shoulders;* Dothain, *failing.* We have heard therefore a hearing from the Lord, and he hath sent an ambassador to the nations; because in the incarnation of the Word we received sure tidings of our redemption. How promptly, how devotedly he fulfilled his embassy, and wrought our redemption, he himself will make plain.

*Arise ye,* he says, *and let us rise up together against him in battle.* LITERAL. Great was the dignity of our creation; but greater the reverence of our restoration. For man is read to have been created with divine counsel; but restored with the same, to which grace was added. For there it was said out of counsel that man should be made; but here it is both said in counsel, and secretly the Son is sent to redeem him. There was shown the dignity [0376A] of man in the manner and form of the counsel; here the privilege of grace, and the excellence of nature, in the quality of the mystery and the greatness of the punishment. In both transactions we are stirred to love; but in the second we are kindled to a greater devotion. ALLEG. Hence the Son: Arise ye, he says, and let us rise up together against him in battle. Arise ye, he says, to the angels; let us rise up together, Father — I and the Paraclete: in saying this, I do not divide the unity of the essence of the Deity, but faithfully distinguish the property of the persons. Against him. Against the devil, that is; against the world, against sin, against the flesh. Arise ye, he says, to the angels. Hence Isaiah under the same person: *Go,* he says, *ye swift angels, to a nation rent and torn in pieces, to a terrible people, after whom there is no other* [0376B] [n: (Isa. XVIII)]. Hence in the same prophet, concerning the Son: *To a deceitful nation,* he says, *I will send him, and against the people of my wrath I will give him a charge, to take away the spoils, and to seize the prey, and to make it to be trodden down like the mire of the streets [n: (Isa. X)]*. Hence the Son to the Father through the Psalmist: *My heart is ready,* he says, *my heart is ready [n: (Psal. CVII)]*. Hence, concerning the Spirit, the Father to the Son: *Behold,* he says, *my servant, whom I have chosen; I have given my spirit upon him, he shall bring forth judgment to the nations [n: (Isa. XII)]*. Likewise, the Father concerning the Son and the Paraclete Spirit: *Cease ye,* he says, *from the man, whose breath is in his nostrils; for he is reputed high [n: (Isa. II)]*.
