## HERE BEGINS THE EXPOSITION. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ MORAL. ⟦s2⟧ Attend diligently how the angels, [0376C] how promptly, are sent to the storming of the enemies; how the blessed and holy Trinity works redemption. ⟦s3⟧ I am most certain of the victory, where power joins battle, wisdom orders the business, kindness supplies patience and promises wages. ⟦s4⟧ Let us rise up together, says the Son: the Father by mightily thrusting out the adversary; I by wisely hiding the mystery — for if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory [n: (I Cor. II)]; the Spirit by kindly receiving and healing the wretched: *For the spirit of wisdom is benevolent, and will not free the evil speaker from his lips [n: (Sap. I)]*. ⟦s5⟧ Let the Father arise, mightily laying the enemy low for the Son; I will arise, wisely teaching him that knows not. ⟦s6⟧ Let the Father's power shine forth in the storming of the enemies. ⟦s7⟧ Let the Father loose the chains of human captivity. [0376D] Let the Son illumine the darkness of human blindness. ⟦s8⟧ Let the Spirit supply the gifts and the desires of charity. ⟦s9⟧ Let the reader beware lest he confound the trinity of the persons, or divide the unity of the divine essence. ⟦s10⟧ For by these things which I distinguish, I show the property of the persons; I do not confound the emptiness of nature. ⟦s11⟧ Otherwise, as the Trinity is one in nature or essence, so also its operation is in every way one. ⟦s12⟧ For it is written: *Whatsoever the Father doth, these same things the Son also doth in like manner [n: (Joan. V)]*; nor yet is the Spirit drawn aside from this, who is, and is called, and is believed to be, the same essence, the same power, the same wisdom, the same God, etc. ⟦s13⟧ There follows:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Behold I have made thee small among the nations. ⟦s2⟧ Thou art exceeding contemptible. ⟦s3+4⟧ The pride of thy heart hath lifted thee up, that dwellest in the clefts of the rocks, that exaltest thy throne, who sayest [0377A] in thy heart, Who shall bring me down to the ground?* ⟦s5⟧ LITERAL. ⟦s6⟧ Historically the prophet, under the person of the Lord, argues and rebukes, threatens and commands against the Idumaean: that he rose up against his brother, namely the Jewish people; that he insulted him in the time of his calamity; that he taught, upheld and helped his enemies to storm him — showing what and of what sort he was by the debt of nature or the merit of his race, and what he has become by his own vice. ⟦s7⟧ These three things God shows him: namely the debt of nature, the vice of fault, the punishment of penalty. ⟦s8⟧ That he is, namely, both contemptible in nature, and filthy in his pursuits, and detestable in his punishment. ⟦s9⟧ The first he shows where he says: Behold I have made thee small among the nations, etc. ⟦s10⟧ The second there: *If thou be exalted as an eagle,* and the rest. ⟦s11⟧ The third there: *They have sent thee out even to the border [0377B].* ⟦s12⟧ But in that he calls the Idumaean people dwellers in the clefts of the rocks, he touches a custom proper to that nation, which on account of the scarcity of timber and the burning of the sun dwells in the caverns of the crags and in caves of earth.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ ALLEGORICAL. ⟦s2⟧ The Lord inveighs against the gentile people; who, spurning the Creator, and the comeliness of their own nature, and the honor of divine grace, and the gift of knowledge, and the worship of justice, turned to the filthiness of idolatry, to uncleanness of morals, to the dirges of errors and to vain philosophy — and whence they ought devoutly to have prostrated themselves before the Creator, thence, armed with a fat neck, they rather swelled up against him. ⟦s3⟧ He says therefore to him: Behold, little in understanding, gentile in worship, contemptible in act, I have given thee, [0377C] that is, by a just judgment I permitted thee to become so. ⟦s4⟧ Little, I say, in the understanding of truth; gentile in rite and in the ceremony of unbelief; contemptible in the action of iniquity. ⟦s5⟧ But to the heaping up of malice the wind of pride puffed thee up, and by its deserving thou didst fall into the darkness of unbelief and the gloom of errors. ⟦s6⟧ And not content with these alone, but by preaching errors, by asserting light to be darkness, and by declaring good evil and truth a lie, thou hast led others also to ruin. ⟦s7⟧ Hence that which follows: The pride of thy heart hath lifted thee up, that dwellest in the clefts of the rock, that exaltest thy throne: who sayest in thy heart, Who shall bring me down to the ground? ⟦s8⟧ Consider the order of misery, the course of falling, and the formless form of departing from God. ⟦s9⟧ The swelling of pride goes first, the darkness of ignorance follows, unbelief [0377D] succeeds to idolatry. ⟦s10⟧ This is followed by delight and consent to uncleanness, thereafter the preaching of malice, and at last contempt of virtue and hatred of justice. ⟦s11⟧ For in that he says: Pride hath lifted thee up, the swelling of pride is shown; by the rock, the unbelief of ignorance; by the clefts, the schisms of idolatry; by dwelling, consent to uncleanness; by the throne exulting, the preaching of malice. ⟦s12⟧ In that he says, in his heart: Who shall bring me down to the ground? he signifies contempt of virtue and hatred of justice. ⟦s13⟧ But the Lord God of vengeance, the God of vengeance, acts freely [n: (Psal. XCIII)]; he is exalted in judging the earth, while he renders retribution to the proud. ⟦s14⟧ Hence it is that it is said:

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ If thou be exalted as an eagle; *And if thou set thy nest among the stars: thence will I bring thee down, saith the Lord.* [0378A] LITERAL. ⟦s2⟧ Their deities seemed to the gentile people to be as it were stars. ⟦s3⟧ ALLEG. ⟦s4⟧ But the sophists and philosophers are as the eagle. ⟦s5⟧ For in some respect philosophers and sophists have seemed eagles, while they lift the eyes of the heart to the sun of justice, while they fix the gaze of the mind [sic: *irerverberate*] upon the very ray of truth. ⟦s6⟧ But the eagle is at once drawn back from thence; because after receiving the implanted knowledge of truth, the philosopher and the sophist, by the desert of their pride, return to the gloom of errors. ⟦s6⟧ Hence the Apostle: *Who, when they had known God,* he says, *glorified him not as God, nor gave thanks; but became vain in their thoughts [n: (Rom. I)]*. ⟦s7+8⟧ The gentile people set its nest among the stars, when it placed the hope of its salvation either in angels or in men: whom it falsely believes to have shone with the light of wisdom and the brightness of justice. [0378B] There follows:

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *If thieves had gone in to thee; if robbers by night, how wouldst thou have held thy peace? ⟦s2⟧ Would they not have stolen enough for themselves? ⟦s3⟧ If the grape-gatherers had come in to thee, would they not [nunquid] at least have left thee a cluster?* ⟦s4⟧ LITERAL. ⟦s5⟧ The robbers were the mighty of the world; the thieves, the flamens and the other worshippers of idols; the philosophers and the sophists, the grape-gatherers. ⟦s6⟧ For these three chiefly flourished in idolatry: namely philosophy, empty superstition, infamous power. ⟦s7⟧ The worldly philosophers boasted that they possessed the understanding of truth; the worshippers of idols, the sanctity of religion; the mighty, the lordship of liberty. ⟦s8⟧ But of the first it is read: *Their wine is the gall of dragons, and the venom of asps, which is incurable [n: (Deut. XXXII)]*. ⟦s9⟧ Of the second: *Let them all be confounded [0378C] that adore graven things: and that glory in their idols [n: (Psal. LXXVI)]*. ⟦s10⟧ Of the third. ⟦s11⟧ *The mighty shall be mightily tormented: and a stronger punishment presses upon the stronger [n: (Sap. VI)]*. ⟦s12⟧ As if the Lord were to say: See ye that I am alone, and that there is no other God besides me. ⟦s13⟧ *I will kill and I will make to live; I will strike, and I will heal; and there is none that can deliver out of my hand. ⟦s14⟧ If I destroy, no man buildeth; if I shut up, there is none that openeth [n: (Deut. XXXII)]*. ⟦s15⟧ There is none like me among the strong, none among the lawgivers; I am magnificent in holiness, terrible and praiseworthy, and doing wonders. ⟦s16⟧ What neither worldly power, nor pestilent superstition, nor empty philosophy could do, my power, my wisdom, my kindness has availed to do. ⟦s17⟧ My power [0378D] destroyed, my wisdom built, my kindness adorned. ⟦s18⟧ It destroyed the falsehood of errors; it built the truth of doctrines; it adorned by pouring in the bounty of graces. ⟦s19⟧ Of the first it is read: *God, whose wrath no man can resist [n: (Job IX)]*. ⟦s20⟧ Of the second: *Wisdom hath built herself a house [n: (Prov. IX)]*, and the rest. ⟦s21⟧ Of the third: *His spirit hath adorned the heavens, and his obstetric hand brought forth the winding serpent [n: (Job XXVI)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ ALLEG. ⟦s2⟧ The power of the world and the superstition of the idol both seized enough for themselves. ⟦s3⟧ But philosophy left some of the clusters behind it; because neither could the first give or show liberty, nor the second security, nor the third truth, unto salvation. ⟦s4⟧ It had a shadow, not a body; it exhibited an image, not the thing. ⟦s5⟧ But the incarnate Word laid all things bare to clearness; [0379A] it possesses all things entire; it searches out the hidden things of Esau: it leads each one to his own border, while by a just judgment it lays upon each singly his due end according to his desert. ⟦s6⟧ Hence the Psalmist: *There is none,* he says, *that can hide himself from his heat [n: (Psal. XVIII)]*. ⟦s7⟧ The heat of God is read to be threefold. ⟦s8⟧ For one is of extreme damnation; another of merciful vengeance; another of pleasing love. ⟦s9⟧ Of the first it is read: *A fire is kindled in my wrath, and shall burn even to the lowest depths of hell [n: (Deut. XXXII)]*. ⟦s10⟧ Of the second: *Our God is a consuming fire [n: (Hebr. XII)]*. ⟦s11⟧ Of the third: *The Lord liveth, whose fire is in Sion, and his furnace in Jerusalem [n: (Isa. XXXI)]*. ⟦s12⟧ None therefore shall be hidden from his heat; because the impenitent shall be tormented by the fire of damnation, and the penitent purged by the fire of vengeance, and the innocent [0379B] gladdened by the fire of love. ⟦s13⟧ Hence the Savior of himself. ⟦s14⟧ *Revenge to me, and I will repay [n: (Rom. XII)]*. ⟦s15⟧ Likewise the same: *I live,* saith the Lord, *for every knee shall bow to me, and every tongue shall confess to me [n: (Rom. XIV)]*. ⟦s16⟧ And this is what is added:

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *How have they searched Esau, they have sought out his hidden things; they have sent thee out even to the border.* ⟦s2⟧ LITERAL. ⟦s3⟧ For Christ and his apostles and their successors have searched out the hidden things of Esau; because by life, by conduct, by doctrine, they confuted and condemned the pomp of the world, its concupiscence, its foolish wisdom, and its idolatry. ⟦s4⟧ The first Christ condemned in the manger; the second in the desert; the third on the mountain; the fourth in the Jordan, when the King of glory willed to be wrapped in swaddling clothes; when the Bread [0379C] of life willed to be afflicted with hunger; when the Wisdom of God willed to be made foolish; when the Deity willed to be upheld by a servant. ⟦s5⟧ But how the apostles and their successors contended against the world and the error of the gentiles, both their words teach and the marks of the sufferings of Christ bear witness. ⟦s6⟧ Whence there follows: They have sent thee out even to the border.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ ALLEG. ⟦s2⟧ The aforesaid men sent the world out even to the border; because when they broke the little pitchers of their bodies, when they sounded with the trumpets of their preachings - when they shone with the lamp of their miracles, the world was terrified, was overcome and sank down, and offered its neck to the bonds of faith. ⟦s3⟧ Hence the Psalmist: *They themselves seeing, so wondered, they were troubled, they were moved; trembling took hold of them [n: (Psal. XLVII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ They wondered at the preaching of an unwonted newness; [0379D] they were troubled at the hearing, they who suffered from the fervor of charity; they were moved by the miracles of a brightness unseen till then: and therefore they sent Esau out even to the border; that is, they led the world down to the predestined article of faith. ⟦s5⟧ Whence there also follows:

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *All the men of thy covenant have mocked thee; the men of thy peace have made an inroad against thee. ⟦s2⟧ They that eat bread with thee shall lay snares under thee.* ⟦s3⟧ LITERAL. ⟦s4⟧ These men had entered a covenant with the devil and the world through the worship of idolatry; they had made peace through the affection of concupiscence. ⟦s5⟧ They ate bread with him through the preaching of errors and the sprouting of malice. ⟦s6⟧ But the men of the covenant mocked him; because those who had served the world more curiously, who had shown the worship of idolatry more devoutly, [0380A] afterwards raised against the world the sword of preaching, set against the worship of idolatry the shield of faith, against the whirlpool of carnal concupiscence the most secure harbor of religion.
