## HERE BEGINS THE EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There is therefore no prudence in him. In the assembly, namely, [0383B] of heretics, where both reason convicts falsehood, and the divine word establishes truth, and the grace of conversion discloses perfidy. But these things may be referred tropologically to the flesh. For the flesh is not undeservedly signified by Esau; because it is hairy with concupiscences, and bloody with vices, and earthy with hurtful works — with the concupiscences of vanity, the vices of curiosity, the works of iniquity. Whence the Apostle: *I know,* he says, *that there dwelleth not in me* (that is, in my flesh) *that which is good [n: (Rom. VII)]*. Likewise the same: *They who are in the flesh cannot please God [n: (Rom. VIII)]*. But it is to be known that when the divine discourse inveighs against the flesh, it is not nature but fault that is arraigned; not the condition of nature, but the defect of justice; not the joining of the elements, but the motion of vices. For as in man there are two [0383C] natures, namely spirit and flesh, so there are two motions by which each of them is moved: one toward the affection of justice, the other departing from the affection of grace. Thence are born virtue and vice: so that if it be a motion of the spirit, it is virtue; but a motion of the flesh is nothing else than a motion of the mind not obeying the spirit. That one, being spiritual, makes men spiritual. This one, being carnal, makes men carnal. That one is called spirit; this is designated by the name of flesh. Further, since occasion has offered itself concerning the origin of virtue and of vice, it is well to take it up a little more deeply, and to discuss more subtly all the general motions of the mind. The general motion of the mind, then, is divided fivefold. For sometimes the heart of man is moved only by the devil, sometimes by man alone, sometimes by God alone, sometimes by man and the devil, sometimes by man and God. The first is [0383D] through the suggestion of fault; the second through a simple consideration of nature; the third through a momentary inspiration of grace; the fourth through unlawful delight; the fifth through the affections of the virtues and the contemplation of the Bridegroom. The first and the second are harmless to man; the third glorious; the fourth penal; the fifth rewarding. The fourth is vicious and to be condemned. The fifth is virtuous and to be crowned. The third is to be received eagerly. The second is indifferent along with the first. The fourth is referred to the flesh, the fifth to the spirit; the third to God; the first and the second to neither. But the general motion of the flesh is sevenfold. For sometimes it is against God, sometimes against the neighbor, sometimes against itself, often against the world, [0384A] many times against business. Sometimes upon the neighbor, sometimes upon itself. Against God, pride; against the neighbor, envy; against itself, anger; against the world, avarice; against business, sloth. Upon the neighbor, lust; upon itself, gastrimargia, or gluttony. These seven vices are that household which the Lord left in the midst of his people, that in it he might train Israel; whence also from the allotment of their names their property may be designated. And these are the names. The first is called the Pherezite; the second the Hethite; the third the Hevite; the fourth the Amorrhite; the fifth the Gergesite; the sixth the Jebusite; the seventh the Chanaanite. The first is interpreted *surpassing;* the second, *wasting away* or *enduring a voyage;* the third, *gathering stones;* the fourth, *embittered* or *embittering;* the fifth, *driving out the [0384B] settler;* the sixth, *trodden down* or *treading down;* the seventh, *changed* or *changing.* By the first is designated pride; by the second envy; by the third anger; by the fourth sloth; by the fifth avarice; by the sixth lust; by the seventh is designated gluttony. But we, since we purpose to pass on to what follows, leave the fitting of each one to the easy exercise of the reader. It is to be known, however, that that motion is called carnal specially which serves the flesh alone and is administered through the flesh, which here is designated by Esau. He prepares snares for his brother Jacob; because he assails virtue in three ways: namely through the incontinence of lust, through the immoderation of gluttony, and through the wantonness of the senses. By the first the flower of chastity is stormed; by the second the honor of sobriety; by the third [0384C] the comeliness of decency. Whence Esau too was allotted a threefold name, by which the aforesaid mischief is signified. For earthy is gluttony. Bloody, lust. Hairy, wantonness. Hairy through the superfluity of curiosity. Bloody through the vicious flux of lust. Earthy through both; burdensome through the appetite of surfeit and drunkenness. Obadiah therefore, under the person of the Lord, inveighs against the flesh; nay rather, the Lord undertakes to storm it through his friends, saying to them

Arise ye, and let us rise up together against him in battle. MORAL. *For they that are Christ's have crucified their flesh with its vices and concupiscences; but you are not in the flesh, but the Holy Spirit dwelleth in you [n: (Gal. V)]*. *That which is born of the flesh, is flesh: and that which is born of the spirit, is spirit [n: (Joan. III)]*. [0384D] *For he that soweth in the flesh, shall reap corruption; but he that soweth in the spirit, of the spirit shall reap life everlasting [n: (Gal. VI)]*. *The spirit indeed is ready: but the flesh is weak [n: (Marc. XIV)]*. It is read in the Psalm: *In a desert land, and where there is no way and no water: so in the sanctuary have I appeared to thee [n: (Psal. XIV)]*. And through Solomon: *I thought,* he says, *to withdraw my flesh from wine, that I might carry it over to wisdom [n: (Eccle. II)]*. And the Bridegroom in the Canticles: *I will go,* he says, *to the mountain of myrrh, and to the hill of frankincense [n: (Cant. II)]*. And through Job: *The deep,* he says, *saith: It is not in me: and the sea speaketh: It is not with me: neither is it found in the land of them that live pleasantly [n: (Job. XXVIII)]*. And in Jeremiah: *They that fed delicately have perished in the ways: and they that were brought up in scarlet have embraced [0385A] the dung. Arise therefore, my friends, to the avenging of the enemy, to the mortification of the flesh. And as much as she hath been made ready and hath been in delights, so much give ye to her of torment and of mourning [n: (Thren. IV)]*. Concerning the outcome of the business, therefore, you have nothing whatever to fear: concerning the hope of triumph you must not in the least distrust. For he who helps you cannot be conquered, because he is omnipotence; he who teaches you cannot be deceived, because he is wisdom; he who promises cannot be corrupted, because he is justice; he cannot be withstood, because he is eternal; he cannot be avoided, because he is everywhere. Arise, I say, friends, because the enemy is weak, the place fitting, the helper unconquered, the victory certain, the merits many, the rewards worthy. The enemy, the weak flesh: the place, the present life: the helper, the highest Deity: the reward, the vision of the Trinity. Arise from the sleep of torpor, [0385B] from the bed of grief, from the languor of desolation. First through the vigilance of discretion, secondly through the mortification of the flesh, thirdly through the joy of spiritual consolation. Let us rise up together against him in battle: because the enemy is contemptible. Whence, turning to the enemy, he says: Behold I have made thee small among the nations, thou art exceeding contemptible. For the motion of the flesh, among those vices which we have named above, is small, because momentary; corruptible, because dishonorable: small, because where it has poured out pleasant honey, it flees, and strikes the smitten heart with too tenacious a bite. It is contemptible, because pleasure bought with grief does harm. It is small, because it neglects good things. It is contemptible, because it accomplishes evil things. It is small because it seeks transitory things. Contemptible, because [0385C] it despises eternal things. Small in affection, contemptible in act. Small in work, contemptible in desert. There follows:

The pride of thy heart hath lifted thee up, that dwellest in the clefts of the rock: who sayest in thy heart: Who shall bring me down to the ground? The rock is here understood as the soul, which is called whole when no carnal passion has mastery over it. It is cleft when it is broken up by the assault of vices. Therefore the clefts are carnal concupiscences. For he exalts his throne who preaches the works of the flesh to the contempt of God. But to say in the heart: Who shall bring me down to the ground? is, God being despised, to place the hope of salvation and of eternity in the pleasure of the world and of oneself. In which three vices are noted: hatred of good, love of evil, forgetfulness of God. Attend diligently [0385D] how the unhappy soul falls swiftly, suddenly fails from the state of justice, in a moment sinks into the depth of malice. For first it inwardly conceives a pestilent pleasure, then places its glorying in it. In this it fixes consent, and afterwards applies deliberation: thereupon it displays operation, afterwards contempt of God, then the preaching of sin, thereafter hatred of good, afterwards love of evil, at last forgetfulness of God. This is the decalogue of disobedience, which the Lord gave to Moses on Mount Sinai, which is interpreted *humility.* This decalogue he had fulfilled who said: *I know not the Lord; and I will not let Israel go [n: (Exod. V)]*. And others too, saying: *Who is the Almighty, that we should serve him? [n: (Job XXI.)]* And: *What doth it profit us if we pray to him? [n: (Ibid.)]* Others [0386A] also: *Let us crown ourselves,* they say, *with roses, before they wither. Let us leave everywhere the tokens of our [sic: laetitio]. Let us eat and drink: for tomorrow we shall die. For this is our lot, and our inheritance [n: (Sap. II; Isa. XXII)]*. There follows:

If thou be exalted as an eagle, and if thou set thy nest among the stars: thence will I bring thee down, saith the Lord. The eagle was, by condition, the apostate angel: but through pride he was made Leviathan. The stars are the kinds of men shining in the world above others by the lamp of nobility, the brightness of knowledge, the splendor of riches. The nest is the pomp of dishonorable pleasure. The Lord therefore says to Esau, that is, to the flesh: If thou be exalted as an eagle, etc. As if he were to say: If the dowry of Egypt fall to thee, if the flower of the world smile upon thee, if pomp answer thee [0386B] to thy wish: if thou hast fittingly tended thy skin, if thou hast feasted splendidly, if thou art fattened with riches, made sleek with delights, spread wide with attendance, *yet under thee shall the moth be strewn, and thy covering shall be worms [n: (Isa. XIV)]*. Whence the Apostle: *Meat for the belly, and the belly for meats: but God shall destroy both this and these [n: (I Cor. VI)]*. Hence the Lord through John in the Apocalypse: *Because thou sayest,* he says, *I sit a queen, and am not a widow, and I see no mourning: therefore in one day shall thy plagues come, death, and mourning, and famine; and fire shall burn thee [n: (Apoc. XVIII)]*: for strong is the Lord who judgeth her. Likewise in the same place: *Render to her as she also hath rendered to us: and double ye the double: according to her works, in the cup wherein she hath mingled, mingle ye to her double [n: (ibid.)]*.

[0386C]If thieves had gone in to thee, and robbers, how wouldst thou have held thy peace? Would they not have stolen enough for themselves? If the grape-gatherers had come in to thee, would they not at least have left thee clusters? The thieves are the dissemblers and the crafty, who provoke the wrath of God. The robbers are passions and diseases. Further, the demons are the grape-gatherers. The first waste the flesh under a pretense. The second torment it most grievously. The third disfigure and humble it: but all seize enough for themselves; because neither demon, nor disease, nor hypocrite does anything except what the divine dispensation permits by its ordering. Hence the Lord through Job: *I encompassed,* he says, *the sea with my bounds, and I set a bar and doors, and I said: Hitherto thou shalt come, and shalt go no further, [0386D] and here thou shalt break thy swelling waves (Job XXXVIII)*. Now the Lord encompasses the sea when he moderates the assaults of those that afflict the flesh by the dispensation of his judgments: so that the swollen wave of a mad fervor may be broken secretly upon a level shore. The Lord says therefore: If thieves had gone in to thee, etc. As if he were to say: If those who can do nothing without my judgment afflict thee so far in straitness and against thy advantage, what shall my friends do, who by afflicting thee shall please me, providing for thy profit?
