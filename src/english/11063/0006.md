## HERE BEGINS THE EXPOSITION. (cont.)

*How thou hast drunk upon my holy mountain: all the nations shall drink continually; and they shall drink, and shall be swallowed up, and they shall be as though they were not.* Historically the prophetic word is directed to the Idumaeans; allegorically against idolaters and heretics; tropologically against the flesh and its animal senses, against concupiscence and its carnal onsets. The mountain, historically, is Jerusalem, that earthly city; allegorically, Christ and the Church; tropologically, holy contemplation. Now to drink upon the mountain is to take delight and to rejoice at the overthrow of the aforesaid city, or at the contempt of Christ, or at the casting down of the Church, or at the fall of a soul adorned with virtues and raised up to the contemplation of the Bridegroom. Let us read [0390A] Herodotus and the Greek and barbarian histories, and we shall see how under the Assyrians and Babylonians was fulfilled what is said: *The day of the Lord is near upon all the nations [n: (Psal. CXXXVI)]*. But as to what follows — As thou hast done, so shall it be done to thee; he shall render thy recompense upon thy own head — this is the sense which we read in the psalm: *Be mindful, O Lord, of the children of Edom, in the day of Jerusalem: who say, empty it out, Empty it out even to the foundation therein [n: (ibid.)]*. For as thou hast drunk upon my holy mountain with the Babylonians and hast rejoiced, so all the nations which thou hadst with thee in the garrison of Babylon, turned against thee, shall drink and rejoice; and not only shall they drink, but they shall also swallow thee up, so that the Idumaeans shall be as though they were not. Or certainly those same nations, when they have swallowed thee up, shall themselves be swallowed up by the Medes. And this [0390B] vengeance shall go forward for this end: that thou [consume] Jerusalem, that the Babylonian consume thee, and the Mede and the Persian the Babylonian. Let us follow the order of interpretation. The day of the Lord is near, O heretic, upon all the nations: the time of judgment is at hand, in which all nations are to be judged; as thou hast done against churchmen, so shall it be done to thee; for as thou, rejoicing at their slaughter, didst keep festival, and upon my holy mountain, that is, in the Church, didst drink not my cup but the devil's — of which it is said in Habakkuk: *Woe to him that giveth drink to his friend, and maketh him drunk, mingling his gall, that he may look upon his nakedness [n: (Habac. II)]* — so all the nations, or the opposing powers assigned to punishments, or the adversary virtues, shall drink and swallow up thy blood; and at the last, when the torment comes upon all, [0390C] they too shall be as though they were not. For he who perishes to him who is — and who says to Moses: *He who is hath sent me to you [n: (Exod. III)]* — is, according to the rule of the Scriptures, said not to be. Whence also in Esther we read: *Deliver not, O Lord, thy kingdom to them that are not [n: (Esth. XIV)]*. Likewise through Isaiah: *As though they were not, so are they before him; and they are reckoned by him as nothing and emptiness [n: (Isa. XL)]*. We can interpret this passage otherwise. Because you have rejoiced in the ruin of my servants, the same persecution shall come against you also; and as you rejoiced together with the rest of the nations against my people, so all the nations shall come against you also, and shall devour, and drink, and grind you down with a like blow. Tropologically, against the flesh, which had said: *I sit, and I shall not see mourning* [0390D] [n: (Apoc. XVIII)]. Hence to her: *As much as she hath been prepared and hath been in delights, so much prepare for her torments and mourning [n: (ibid.)]*. Hence the Psalmist: *O wretched daughter of Babylon: blessed is he that shall repay thee thy recompense [n: (Psal. CXXXVI)]*, etc. Hence through Nahum, saying to the spirit: *Go in under the clay and tread it; working it, take hold of the brick [n: (Nahum III)]* — calling the flesh a brick. There follows:

LITERAL. *And in mount Sion shall be salvation; and it shall be holy. And the house of Jacob shall possess those who had possessed them. And the house of Jacob shall be a fire, and the house of Joseph a flame, and the house of Esau stubble. And they shall be kindled among them, and shall devour them; and there shall be no remnants of the house of Esau, for the Lord hath spoken.* Idumaea being overthrown, and devoured by the hostile nations with which it had before entered into a covenant against Jacob, upon mount Sion there shall be remnants, [0391A] and there shall be salvation, and it shall be holy — that is, either the Lord himself will return to the temple which he had abandoned because of the people's sin, or say "holy" absolutely, that is, the Holy of holies. And the house of Jacob, under Zorobabel, Esdras and Nehemiah, shall possess those who had possessed them by inheritance; and the house of Jacob, that is, the house of Judah, shall be a fire. And the house of Joseph, of the ten tribes, a flame. But the house of Esau, that is, of the Idumaeans, who had shown themselves so savage and cruel against their own brother, shall be turned into stubble. And as fire and flame consume stubble, so the two kingdoms, leagued together in the bond of a single rod for themselves, according to Ezechiel, shall lay Idumaea waste and devour it. And there shall be none left over of the people who might announce the overthrow to the neighboring nations. All the things we have said and are [0391B] about to say, he promises will be seen by him in the time to come, when in place of Christ they shall receive Antichrist, the Saviour's prophecy being fulfilled: *I came in the name of my Father, and you [sic: accipistis] me not; if another shall come in his own name, him you will receive [n: (Joan. V)]*. Whatever we have interpreted against Idumaea, they dream against the Roman kingdom.

MORAL. What we have said was accomplished according to history under Zorobabel, we assert to be done daily in the Church according to prophecy and the mystical understandings, and to be accomplished in every man in the kingdom of the soul against the flesh. Mount Sion, allegorically, is the Church; tropologically, the soul set in the watchtower of heavenly things, lifted up by the joy of contemplation. Therefore, when Christ enters into Egypt upon a light cloud and goes down into Dothain, [0391C] while by the washing of saving water through the word the Saviour sanctifies the Church — he who is in very truth her sanctification — that he may present her to himself without spot and without wrinkle, mount Sion is said to be sanctified; because the Saviour, who is, as I have said, her sanctification, is joined to her by grace and by mystery. Hence the Son himself to the Father: *Father,* he says, *sanctify them in truth [n: (Joan. XVII)]*. Thy word is truth. Likewise he himself: *I,* he says, *sanctify myself for them [n: (ibid.)]*. See, reader, a new and wondrous kind of sanctification in the manner of our redemption: of what dignity the making of man was, and of how much greater moment his restoration was. For the Father sanctifies, the Word sanctifies, the Spirit sanctifies. For what is it to sanctify, if not to make holy? What is it to make holy, if not to set free from earthly [0391D] appetite, from earthly affection, from earthly understanding, from earthly contagion? For *agios* in Greek, that is, "holy," is in Latin called "without earth." Now in each single man there are by nature three things which, pressed down by earthly contagions, defiled with the filth of vices, were brought under the proud tyranny of demons, These however are: to be able, to will, to know. For man's power had been weakened, his will wounded, his knowledge troubled. The Apostle had felt his power weakened, who said: *I am delighted with the law of God according to the inward man; I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members [n: (Rom. VII)]*. He had known his will wounded, when he said: *For the good which I will, [0392A] that I do not; but the evil which I hate, that I do [n: (ibid.)]*. He had seen his knowledge troubled too, saying: *For we know in part, and we prophesy in part [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*. This also the Psalmist briefly intimates, saying. *My heart is troubled [n: (Psal. XXXVII)]* — behold the will wounded; *And my strength hath forsaken me [n: (ibid.)]* — behold the power weakened; *The light of my eyes, and even that is not with me [n: (ibid.)]* — behold the knowledge darkened. Furthermore, in mount Sion, that is, in the loftiness of the watchtower, in the perfection of the Church, there shall be salvation: through the power of the Most High, through the incarnation of the Word, through the coming of the Paraclete. And that mountain shall be holy — namely the Church, in act, in sense and in affection wholly lifted up from earthly things, receiving power in the sacraments, wisdom in the precepts, fruitfulness in the gifts: from the Father power, [0392B] from the Son wisdom, from the Spirit fruitfulness, the charity poured into us by him. In saying these things I do not divide the unity of the divine essence, nor confound the properties of the persons; but in the divine operation I show, soundly and faithfully, the distinction of the persons and the unity of the essence. Mount Sion, then, is holy at the coming of the Saviour; because the Church, at the coming of her Bridegroom, received from him the holiness of the sacraments, the sanctity of the precepts, the sanctification of the gifts; by him, I say, she is sanctified, that is, she is lifted up from earthly things by the faith of the sacraments; by him she is lifted up by the hope of the precepts; by him she is raised on high by the charity of the heavenly gifts. Sanctified, therefore, she sanctifies and heals; illumined, she illumines; what she has received, she bestows, that all may be of one, both he who is sanctified and he who sanctifies.
