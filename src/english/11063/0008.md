## HERE BEGINS THE EXPOSITION. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they that are toward the south shall inherit the mount of Esau, and they that are in the plains the Philistines: and they shall possess the country of Ephraim, and the country of Samaria: and Benjamin shall possess Galaad.* ⟦s2⟧ LITERAL. ⟦s3⟧ Judah having returned into his own kingdom — he who dwelt in the south and possessed the whole region according to the division of Jesus the son of Nave, which slopes toward the scorpion, that is, the whole [sic: *Achartumnam*]. ⟦s4⟧ These, who before were hemmed in by narrow bounds, shall possess the mount of Esau, that is, the mountains [0396C] of Seir and the highlands which Edom had formerly possessed. ⟦s5+6⟧ But those who dwell in Sichela, that is, in the plains of Lidan and Emmaus — meaning Diospolis and Nicopolis — shall possess the Palestinians, that is, the five cities of the Philistines: Gaza, Ascalon, Azotus, Acharon, Geth, or that whole region which, according to the Acts of the Apostles, is called Salonas. ⟦s7⟧ The border of the sons of Judah shall also be extended as far as Ephraim, where Neapolis now is; and as far as the country of Samaria, where Sebaste was founded. ⟦s8⟧ But Benjamin, whose borders are extended from Jerusalem toward the north, shall possess the whole of Arabia, which was formerly called Galaad and is now named Gerasii, according to the Seventy Interpreters; and the mount of Ephraim, and the fields of Samaria, and Benjamin, and Galaad, those who [0396D] were in the south shall possess. ⟦s9⟧ Whether this has come to pass, let God see to it. ⟦s10+11⟧ For it can in part have been fulfilled through the five hundred years down to the coming of Christ; and after the coming of Christ, more truly to have been fulfilled through the spreading of the faith — which I know most certainly, because it is fulfilled daily in us and is confirmed in the kingdom of the Church. ⟦s12⟧ There follows:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the removal of this host of the children of Israel shall possess all the places of the Chanaanites as far as Sarepta; and the removal of Jerusalem, which is in the Bosphorus, shall possess the cities of the south. ⟦s2⟧ And saviours shall go up into mount Sion, to judge the mount of Esau; and the kingdom shall be the Lord's.* ⟦s3⟧ Those who, according to the volume of Esdras and Nehemiah, shall have returned from Babylon into Judaea shall rightly [0397A] be called the removal. ⟦s4⟧ That whole host of the children of Israel, toward the south as well as toward the west and toward the north, shall possess the Idumaeans, and the Palestinians, and mount Ephraim and Samaria. ⟦s5⟧ Benjamin, because he borders upon the wilderness, shall in particular obtain Galaad. ⟦s6⟧ But toward the east they shall rule over all who are in the land of the Chanaanites, as far as Sarepta of the Sidonians, where once the widow fed Elias. ⟦s7⟧ Furthermore, those who shall have been carried over from Jerusalem herself, the mother city, to the Bosphorus, shall possess the cities of the south, which are in the tribe of Judah. ⟦s8⟧ For having returned into their own city, they shall obtain the places that are near the city. ⟦s9⟧ And when these things had been fulfilled, as it is written in the book of Judges, the Lord used to send saviours who should save the people from captivity. ⟦s10⟧ So they shall approach and shall come into [0397B] mount Sion, that they may judge and discern the mount of Esau — that is, the Idumaeans — as subject to them and serving them; and when all have been subjugated, the kingdom shall be the Lord's. ⟦s11⟧ Jerome: We, he says, have learned from the Hebrew who instructed us in the Scriptures. ⟦s12⟧ According to the Hebrew verity, Sapharat in the present passage is so called, that is, Bophorum [Bosphorum], and not Ephrata, as it pleased the Septuagint. ⟦s13⟧ And it is, he says, as it were "seeing" — the region to which Hadrian carried over the captives. ⟦s14⟧ When therefore our Christ shall have come, then that captivity too shall return into Judaea. ⟦s15⟧ But we can understand any place whatever of the kingdom of Babylon, which I judge to be something else. ⟦s16⟧ For it is the custom of the prophets, when they speak against Babylon, the Ammonites, the Moabites, the Philistines and the other nations, to make free use of many of their words [0397C] and to keep the idioms of the provinces. ⟦s17+18⟧ Since therefore in the tongue of the Assyrians a border, which in Hebrew is called Jebul, is called *sapharat*, I conjecture the sense to be this: The removal of Jerusalem, which has been divided among all the borders and regions, shall receive the cities of the south, that is, of its own tribe. ⟦s19⟧ Thus Jerome from the Hebrew; nay rather, the Hebrew from Jerome. ⟦s20⟧ But since we have interpreted according to history, as best we could, and have steered our little boat among the [sic: *confragrosos*] rocks, let us spread the sails of spiritual understanding, so that, with the Lord breathing upon us and unlocking his mysteries, we may come joyfully to harbor.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ ALLEGORICAL. ⟦s2⟧ Behold, Obadiah in the present passage describes the land historically and divides it spiritually. ⟦s3+4⟧ We read of many divisions in the divine utterance: the divisions, namely, of the Red [0397D] Sea, whence comes that word: *Who divided the Red Sea into divisions [n: (Psal. CXXXV)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And in the Apostle: *Now there are divisions of graces, but the same Spirit. ⟦s6⟧ And there are divisions of ministries, but the same Lord. ⟦s7⟧ And there are divisions of operations, but the same God, who worketh all things in all [n: (I Cor. XII)]*. ⟦s8⟧ But we read that there have also been four divisions of the land. ⟦s9⟧ The first was made by Josue; the second by Ezechiel; the third here in Obadiah. ⟦s10⟧ And the fourth by the Saviour. ⟦s11⟧ The first is the sacrament of the second, the second of the third, the third of the fourth. ⟦s12⟧ Whence the first, by the same number, so stands historically as the second does spiritually according to that law; the third to the fourth. ⟦s13⟧ Consider, reader, the form of the order and the splendor of the proportion. ⟦s14⟧ For as the first [0398A] is to the second, so the third stands to the fourth. ⟦s15⟧ And as the first to the third, so the second to the fourth, so the second to the third. ⟦s16⟧ As to the judgment of proportions, I commit the reader to arithmetic for his instruction. ⟦s17⟧ But our purpose is to search out the hiding-places of allegorical obscurity, and to bring its darkness out into the light of explanation. ⟦s18⟧ The first division of the land, then, is made when the storming of the enemies has gone before, or their subjugation; the second without hostile surrender or civil upheaval; the third with a voluntary subjugation of the enemies; the fourth without condition or sinew of subjection, but with the glory and joy of mutual love. ⟦s19⟧ The first division is made with punishment and misery; the second with punishment without misery; the third without punishment and misery, though the shame of it is retained; the fourth without punishment and misery, every weakness being removed. ⟦s20⟧ The battle is to be unwilling [0398B] to consent to sin; the misery is that the unwilling man be harassed by an unlawful incitement; the shame of the misery is to be titillated by it; the removal of the weakness is the shutting out of temptation. ⟦s21⟧ There are four liberties: liberty of choice, liberty of exercise, liberty of counsel, liberty of joy. ⟦s22⟧ The first division was made with liberty of choice, but not with liberty of exercise. ⟦s23⟧ For the will, moved by its own choice, stormed whom it could, but did not subjugate all whom it wished. ⟦s24⟧ Whence it had liberty in the faculty and motion of judgment, but did not have it in the business of exercise; for if it had truly had it, it would have subjected all men to itself at a nod. ⟦s25⟧ The second had the first liberty and the second, but lacked the third; for moving itself by its own will, it uses the wished-for faculty of exercise; but liberty of counsel is wanting to it, because it did not know how to reach the bounds of knowledge and of order, [0398C] of the outcome, of the beginning. ⟦s26⟧ The third had all these three but lacked the fourth; because it obtained both will in motion, and faculty in act, and brightness in understanding, but did not have security in affection. ⟦s27⟧ But the fourth possesses all things; for it possesses both free will in choosing, and swift faculty in acting, and sincere brightness in intention, and secure perpetuity in enjoyment. ⟦s28⟧ The first liberty, then, is common to all; the second is general to the converted, but special to the imperfect; the third is general to the perfect, and special to those who soldier for God in this world; the fourth is singular to those who contemplate the Bridegroom and rejoice with him. ⟦s29⟧ The first belongs properly to servants; the second belongs properly [0398D] to hirelings; the third properly to friends; the fourth properly to sons. ⟦s30⟧ The first division is made through the grace of compunction; the second through the discipline and purpose of conversion; the third through the ecstasy of mind and the eminence of contemplation; the fourth through the taking up of glory and honor, and of immortality, straightway unto the vision of God. ⟦s31⟧ The first comes to pass when we withdraw from the darkness and servitude of sin; the second, when we follow the fleeing Bridegroom to the mountain of myrrh and to the hills of Lebanon; the third, when we go up through the desert like an army set in array; the fourth, when we enter the courts of the heavenly city with hymns. ⟦s32⟧ There are four things which have been given to the elect for their profit and salvation and happiness: namely exercises, joys, merits, rewards — the exercises of religion, the joys of contemplation, the merits [0399A] of perfection, the rewards of the most blessed vision; the exercises, I say, of discipline, the joys of spiritual union and of the heavenly bond, and the merits of justice, the rewards of glory. ⟦s33⟧ The first division, then, pertains to the exercises, the second to the joys, the third to the merits, the fourth to the rewards. ⟦s34⟧ Whence also in the first division it is said by Josue to the children of Israel: *These are the nations which the Lord thy God left in the midst of thee, that in them he might instruct Israel [n: (Judic. III)]*. ⟦s35⟧ Hence again it is written: *But the sons of Judah could not destroy the Jebusite, the inhabitant of Jerusalem [n: (Judic. I)]*. ⟦s36⟧ Likewise in the same place: *The sons of Manasses could not overthrow these cities, but the Chanaanite began to dwell in his own land. ⟦s37⟧ But after the children of Israel grew strong, they subjected the Chanaanites and made them their tributaries, and did not slay them [n: (Ibid.)]*.
