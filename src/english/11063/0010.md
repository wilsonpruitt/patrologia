## HERE BEGINS THE EXPOSITION. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And so there follow seven petitions against the seven vices, by which he is entreated to come to our aid who both taught us to pray and promised that he would give to those who pray the good that would serve to heal our wounds and to loose the yoke of our captivity. ⟦s2⟧ But before we come to the explanation of these, we wish first to show by yet another likeness how great a corruption the aforesaid vices beget in us, so that the more dangerous the sickness is shown to be, the more necessary the medicine may be proved. ⟦s3⟧ Through pride, then, the heart is puffed up; through envy it withers; through anger it bursts; through sadness it is ground down and, as it were, reduced to dust; through avarice it is scattered; through gluttony it is tainted and, as it were, made sodden; through lust it is [0403A] trampled underfoot and reduced to mud, so that the wretch may now say: *I am stuck fast in the mire of the deep, and there is no sure standing. ⟦s4⟧ I am come into the depth of the sea, and a tempest hath overwhelmed me [n: (Psal. LXVIII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And when the soul has been stuck fast in this mire of the deep, and wrapped about in the mud of defilement and uncleanness, and can by no means be plucked out, let it cry to him and beg his help of whom the Psalmist speaks, saying: *With expectation I have waited for the Lord, and he was attentive to me, and he heard my prayers, and he brought me out of the pit of misery and out of the mire of dregs [n: (Psal. XXXIX)]*. ⟦s6⟧ For this reason, then, he himself taught us to pray, that we might understand our whole good to be from God. ⟦s7⟧ The first petition, therefore, is against pride, in which we say to God: *Hallowed be thy name.* ⟦s8⟧ For here we ask that he grant us to fear and venerate his name, [0403B] so that we may be subject to him through humility, because through pride we have shown ourselves rebellious and stubborn. ⟦s9⟧ To this petition is given the spirit of the fear of the Lord, that he, coming to the heart, may create in it the virtue of humility, which may heal the disease of pride, so that a humble man may be able to attain to the kingdom of heaven, which the proud angel lost through elation.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The second petition is against envy, in which it is said: *Thy kingdom come.* ⟦s2⟧ For the kingdom of God is the salvation of men: because God is then said to reign in men when men themselves are made subject to him, both now by cleaving to him through faith, and afterward by inhering in him through sight. ⟦s3⟧ He therefore who asks that the kingdom of God may come assuredly seeks the salvation of all; and in that he petitions for the common salvation of all, [0403C] he shows that he reproves the vice of spite. ⟦s4⟧ To this petition is given the spirit of piety, that he, coming to the heart, may kindle it to kindness, so that a man may himself attain to that same possession of the eternal inheritance to which he desires others to attain.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The third petition is against anger, in which it is said: *Thy will be done, as in heaven and on earth.* ⟦s2⟧ By this he declares that whatever the will of God dispenses, whether in himself or in others, according to the judgment of his own good pleasure, is pleasing to him. ⟦s3⟧ To this petition, then, is given the spirit of knowledge, that he, coming to the heart, may instruct it and wholesomely prick it, so that a man may know that the evil which he suffers comes from his own fault, but that whatever good he has proceeds from the mercy of God; and that by this he may learn, whether in the evils which he endures or in the goods which he does not have, [0403D] not to grow angry against the Creator, but in all things to show patience. ⟦s4⟧ Most excellently, then, is the anger and indignation of the soul mitigated by compunction of heart, which is born of humility, the spirit of knowledge working inwardly; because, on the contrary, anger kills the foolish man, when, driven and blinded by the vice of impatience, he does not recognize either that he has deserved the evil which he suffers, or that he has received through grace the good which he has. ⟦s5⟧ And this virtue — that is, compunction or grief — the reward of consolation follows, so that he who here of his own accord afflicts himself before God with lamentations may there deserve to find true joy and gladness.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The fourth petition is against sadness, or accidie, in which it is said: *Give us [0404A] this day our daily bread.* ⟦s2⟧ For sadness is a weariness of mind joined with sorrow: when the mind, in a manner wasted away and embittered by its own vice, does not desire inward goods, and, all vigor being dead, grows glad at no desire of spiritual refreshment. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore, to heal this vice, we must entreat the mercy of the Lord, that he with his wonted kindness may set the food of inward refreshment before the soul languishing in its weariness; so that what she, being absent from it, knows not how to desire, she may, admonished by the taste of it when present, begin to love. ⟦s4⟧ To this petition, then, is given the spirit of fortitude, to raise up the fainting soul, so that she, having received back the strength of her former vigor, may grow strong out of the disposition of her weariness into the desire of the inward savor. ⟦s5⟧ The Spirit, therefore, creates in the heart a hunger for justice, so that while here it is strongly kindled by the desire of piety, there [0404B] it may obtain for its reward the full satiety of blessedness.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The fifth petition is against avarice, in which it is said: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors.* ⟦s2⟧ For it is just that he should not have to be anxious in paying his debt who has been unwilling to be grasping in exacting it. ⟦s3⟧ And therefore, when the vice of avarice is taken from us by God's grace, it is granted us — the condition of salvation being set forth — how we ought to be absolved from our own debt. ⟦s4⟧ To this petition, then, is given the spirit of counsel, which may teach us in this world willingly to bestow mercy upon him who sins against us, so that in the world to come, when we are to render an account for our sins, we may deserve to find mercy.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The sixth petition is against gluttony, in which it is said: *And lead [0404C] us not into temptation* — that is, do not permit us to be led into temptation. ⟦s2⟧ This is the temptation which by the allurement of the flesh often strives to draw us through natural appetite into excess, and secretly slips pleasure beneath us while openly it flatters us with talk of necessity. ⟦s3⟧ Into which temptation we are assuredly in no way led, if we so strive to furnish nature its support according to the measure of necessity that we nevertheless always remember to restrain the appetite from the allurement of pleasure. ⟦s4⟧ And that we may be able to fulfill this, the spirit of understanding is given to us when we ask, that the inward refreshment of the word of God may check the outward appetite, and the mind be strengthened by spiritual food, so that neither bodily want may be able to break it, nor the pleasure of the flesh to overcome it. ⟦s5⟧ For this reason too the Lord himself, [0404D] when his tempter was practising deceit upon him in his hunger by the suggestion of outward bread, answered, saying: *Not in bread alone doth man live, but in every word that proceedeth from the mouth of God [n: (Matth. IV)]*; that he might plainly show that when the mind is refreshed inwardly by that bread, it does not greatly care if outwardly it suffers for a time the hunger of the flesh. ⟦s6⟧ There is given, therefore, against gluttony the spirit of understanding: and he, coming to the heart, cleanses and purifies it.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The seventh petition is against lust, in which it is said: *Deliver us from evil.* ⟦s2⟧ For although, as we have said elsewhere, evil is manifold — as the evil of the body and the evil of the soul; the evil which is fault, the evil which is punishment; the evil of this world, and the evil of the world to come — nevertheless [0405A] lust can in a manner be reckoned the chief evil, which reduces to [0406A] servitude the man it has caught with its allurements. ⟦s3⟧ Whence through the gift of wisdom we ask to be delivered, saying: *Deliver us from evil.* ⟦s4⟧ Amen.
