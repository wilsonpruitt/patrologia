## Carried type — marked `[sic: …]` in the English

### 0374A (chunk 0001) — *in cordo* for *in corde*
Printed: `dum virus fovit in cordo latitans`. **Carried**, `[sic: *cordo*]`.
*cordo* is no form of *cor, cordis* (dat./abl. *cordi* / *corde*); the only Latin
word it could be is the ablative of the adjective *cordus* ("late-born"), which
is unconstruable here. Intended: *in corde*, "in his heart" — near-certain.
Because there is nothing renderable, it is carried rather than translated
(Pattern 12's "real word merely wrong" exemption does not reach it).

### 0375B (chunk 0001) — *sifignicat* for *significat*
Printed: `Inculcatio vero verbi intentionem sifignicat eloquii`. **Carried**,
`[sic: *sifignicat*]`. Metathesis/foul type for *significat*. The sentence is the
verb, so the English reads "The repeating of the word, however, [sic: *sifignicat*]
the intention of the utterance."

### 0378A (chunk 0002) — *irerverberate* for *irreverberate*
Printed: `dum aciem mentis in ipsum veritatis radium irerverberate figunt`.
**Carried**, `[sic: *irerverberate*]`. Adverb *irreverberate*, "unblinkingly,"
modifying *figunt* — the eagle-topos of gazing at the sun without flinching.

### 0380C (chunk 0003) — *invaseres* for *invasores*
Printed: `Deinde eosdem invaseres, illusores, insidiatores nominat.`
**Carried**, `[sic: *invaseres*]`. The correct *invasores* stands eleven lines
later in the same chunk (0380D, `invasores per obedientiam`), which is the
evidence that this is compositor's type and not a variant form.

### 0386A (chunk 0004) — *laetitio* for *laetitiae*
Printed: `Relinquamus ubique signa laetitio nostrae` (Wisd. 2:9). **Carried**,
`[sic: laetitio]`. Note: the run sits *inside* an italic scripture lemma, so per
Pattern 10 it takes no second italic marker; the `[sic: …]` content is bare.

### 0387B (chunk 0005) — *influmine* for *in flumine* (split/run-together type)
Printed: `qui convertis mare in aridam, ut influmine sicco pertranseant pede`.
**Carried**, `[sic: *influmine*]`, per Pattern 10 (word-division defect; the whole
run is carried, including the real word *in*). The carried run stands where the
prepositional phrase stands: "so that they pass over [sic: *influmine*] with dry
foot."

### 0389A (chunk 0005) — *in humanitatem* for *inhumanitatem*
Printed: `Historialiter ostendit in humanitatem Idumaeorum, et crudelitatem
eorum`. **Carried**, `[sic: *in humanitatem*]`, per Pattern 10 (split type;
`in` stays inside the run). The paired *crudelitatem* immediately following makes
*inhumanitatem* ("inhumanity") certain — and note that the split reading inverts
the sense into "humanity," which is exactly the class of damage Pattern 7 exists
to expose rather than repair.

## Rendered literally, no marker (real words, wrong readings)

### 0376D (chunk 0002) — *vanitatem* for *unitatem*
Printed: `Per haec enim, quae distinguo, personarum proprietatem ostendo, non
naturae vanitatem confundo.` Rendered as printed: "I do not confound the
**emptiness** of nature." The argument requires *unitatem* — the sentence sits
between "lest he confound the trinity of the persons, or **divide the unity** of
the divine essence" and "as the Trinity is one in nature or essence." A Trinitarian
theologian does not concede an *emptiness* of the divine nature. High-value defect:
this is a doctrinal statement inverted by one letter, and the literal English keeps
the witness intact.

### 0377D (chunk 0002) — *exsultantem* for *exaltantem*
Printed: `per exsultantem solium, praedicatio malitiae` — but the lemma four
lines above (0376D–0377A) and the parallel gloss at 0385C both read *exaltantem
solium* ("that exaltest thy throne," Obad. 3). Rendered as printed, "by the throne
exulting." The gloss-list otherwise tracks the lemma word for word, so this is a
slip in the gloss, not a variant lemma.

### 0380D (chunk 0003) — *subtilitatis … praedicationis*
Printed: `In linteo sindonis, subtilitatis designatur praedicationis.` The
sentence has no nominative subject for *designatur*; sense requires *subtilitas*.
Rendered with the printed double genitive: "In the cloth of fine linen is
designated of the subtlety of preaching." Deliberately awkward — the doubled "of"
is the defect, and smoothing it would hide a case error inside an otherwise
unremarkable Gregorian borrowing (Prov. 31:24).

### 0381C (chunk 0003) — *induxerunt* without an object
Printed: `Haeretici raptores spirituales terram sanctam invadentes, et Scripturas
canonicas pervertentes induxerunt; linguas suas acuerunt…` The verb governs
nothing. Rendered "have brought them in," supplying only the pronoun English
requires. Possibly a dropped noun (*errores*? *schismata*?) or a compositor's
transposition; nothing in the sentence lets one choose. Flagged for a human eye.

### 0382A (chunk 0003) — *cum* for *eum*
Printed: `Cui hoc Dominus dedit, quando cum talem, judicio fieri permisit, etc.`
The parallel at 0377C reads `dedi te, hoc est justo judicio fieri permisi`, i.e.
"I permitted **him** to become such." Rendered with the printed *cum* as a
preposition: "when with such a one he permitted it to come to pass by judgment."

### 0382A (chunk 0003) — lemma variants *expellit* / *deducet*
Printed: `Superbia expellit te … Quis me deducet in terram?` against the lemma as
quoted at 0376D–0377A and 0385C: `Superbia cordis tui extulit te … Quis me
detrahet in terram?` — and *detrahet* returns three lines later at 0382A itself
("Quis me detrahet in terram? Dicere istud…"). Both printed readings rendered as
they stand ("Pride driveth thee out"; "Who shall lead me down"). Whether Hugh is
quoting loosely from memory here or Migne's compositor drifted cannot be settled
from the plate.

### 0383A (chunk 0003) — unconstruable clause on the Fathers
Printed: `quorum nonnulli cum eis foedus iniere pacem, aliquando tenuere panem
quoque cum eis comedere.` *foedus iniere pacem* has two accusatives and no
conjunction; *tenuere … comedere* has no construction. Rendered as closely as the
printed words allow: "some of whom entered into covenant with them, peace they
sometimes held, to eat bread also with them." The sense wanted is plainly the
three Obadiah terms Hugh has been working (covenant / peace / eating bread) applied
to Augustine among the Manichaeans. A verb has probably dropped out. Flagged.

### 0385B (chunk 0004) — *corruptibilis* for *contemptibilis*
Printed: `parvulus est, quia momentaneus; corruptibilis, quia inhonestus`, in the
middle of a rhetorical chain that otherwise pairs *parvulus* with *contemptibilis*
nine consecutive times (0385B–0385C), following the lemma "Ecce parvulum dedi te
… contemptibilis tu es valde." Rendered as printed ("corruptible, because
dishonorable").

### 0384A–0384B (chunk 0004) — the vice list and the Canaanite-nations list disagree
The catalogue at 0384A assigns `contra mundum, avaritia; contra negotium acedia`
(fourth = avarice, fifth = sloth); the interpretation of the seven names at 0384B
assigns `per quartum acedia; per quintum avaritia` (fourth = sloth, fifth =
avarice). Both rendered as printed. The etymologies favor the second list
(*Amorrhaeus* "embittered" → *acedia*; *Gergesaeus* "driving out the settler" →
avarice), so the first list is likelier the slip — but Hugh hands the "fitting of
each one" to the reader in the very next sentence, so he may simply not have
checked. No emendation attempted.

## Translation conventions fixed for this work (for the merge)

- **Lemma rendering.** Obadiah is translated from Migne's Latin throughout, never
  from a conventional English version. In particular *Auditum audivimus a Domino*
  is rendered "**We have heard a hearing** from the Lord," because Hugh's gloss at
  0375A–B turns on the phrase being an un-Latin Hebraism ("*locutio quidem minus
  est Latina, sed ex Graeco fideliter expressa*"). "We have heard a report" would
  silently destroy the comment.
- **No guillemets anywhere.** Migne marks every quotation in this work by italics,
  never by « ». The English carries zero guillemets, matching the Latin twin 1:1
  (Pattern 6); quoted lemmata repeated inside the gloss are set unitalicized
  exactly where Migne sets them unitalicized.
- **Sense labels.** `LITTERAL.` → `LITERAL.`; `ALLEG.` → `ALLEG.`; `MORAL.` and
  `MORALIS.` → `MORAL.`; `ALLEGORICE.` → `ALLEGORICALLY.` (the abbreviated and
  spelled-out forms are kept distinct, since the variance is abbreviation, not
  a misprint).
- **Proper names follow the plate**: *Hebrom* (not Hebron), *Dothain*, *Sichem*,
  *Jezrahel*, *Jezabel*, *Idumaea*, *Chanaanite*, *Manichaeus*.
- ***gastrimargia*** is left in Latin (unitalicized, as Migne prints it) — Hugh
  glosses it himself with *seu gula*, so it is a technical term, not a defect.

## Carried type — `[sic: …]` in the English (Patterns 7, 10, 12)

### 0391B — `accipistis`
Printed: *Ego veni in nomine Patris mei, et non accipistis me* (John 5:43).
`accipistis` is not a form of *accipio*; the perfect is *accepistis*, which the
Vulgate prints and which the sense requires. A compositor's slip (i for e).
Carried untranslated inside the scripture lemma, so it takes no second italic
marker (Pattern 10): `you [sic: accipistis] me not`.

### 0396B — `Achartumnam`
Printed: *quae vergit ad scorpionem, id est omnem Achartumnam.* No such Latin or
transliterated word. Hugh is abbreviating Jerome *In Abdiam* on v. 19, where the
territory Judah holds is the *Daroma* — the Hebrew *darom*, "south," which Jerome
elsewhere transliterates and glosses. `Achartumnam` looks like a badly set
transliteration of that (or of another Hebrew regional name) rather than a Latin
word, so it is carried, not translated. **Worth a human's eye** — a reader with
Jerome's text at hand could probably fix this in one look, and if it is
recoverable it belongs in `data/tei-patches/11063.json` with provenance, not here.

Related, same clause: *vergit ad scorpionem* ("slopes toward the scorpion"). This
is real Latin and is rendered as printed under Pattern 7, no marker. It is
nonetheless suspect: a compass bearing is wanted here, and *scorpio* is not one.
Possibly a garbling of a wind-name (*Africum*, *Notum*) in Jerome's geography.
Flagged, not emended.

### 0397C — `confragrosos`
Printed: *inter confragrosos scopulos nostram naviculam reximus.* The word is
*confragosus* ("broken, rugged"); `confragrosos` has an intrusive *r* and is not
attested. Carried under Pattern 7. (Contrast the Pattern 9 exemption: this is not
a period spelling, it is a slip unique to this plate.)

### 0400B — `Ab dias`
Printed: *Dicit ergo Ab dias:* — the prophet's name split across a word space.
Pattern 10 split type; the whole run is carried, including the fragment `Ab`
which is a real word. The name is *Abdias* and is set correctly everywhere else
in the work (e.g. 0394A, 0397D).

### 0402A–B — `hisistere`
Printed: *Sed neque hisistere potest semel coepta corruptio.* Not a Latin word.
The sense requires *subsistere* ("come to a stand, stop") — the argument is that
corruption once begun does not halt but breeds the next vice; *insistere* is the
other candidate but gives the wrong sense. Carried under Pattern 7.

## Rendered as printed (English on the page is ours — no marker)

### 0390B — `ut tu Hierusalem` with no verb
Printed: *Et haec idcirco vindicta procedet, ut tu Hierusalem: te Babylonius,
Babylonium Medus ac Persa consumat.* The first limb of the tricolon has lost its
verb; the shared verb *consumat* arrives only in the third. Rendered with a
supplied bracketed verb — "that thou [consume] Jerusalem" — on the existing
`[it]`/`[them]` convention for words we supply. The bracket is ours; the gap is
Migne's.

### 0390A — `exinanite, Exinanite`
Printed: *qui dicunt, exinanite, Exinanite usque ad fundamentum in ea.* NOT
dittography: Ps 136:7 genuinely doubles the imperative, and the Vulgate prints it
doubled. No `[d: …]`. The mid-sentence capital *E* is Migne's and is reproduced
("empty it out, Empty it out"). Pattern 8.

### 0391D — comma before a capitalized new sentence
Printed: *…superba daemonum tyrannide fuerunt subjugata, Haec autem sunt posse,
velle, nosse.* A comma where a full stop is wanted, with the following word
capitalized. Preserved exactly under Pattern 8 (never delete a printed mark).

### 0392A — full stop before a quotation
Printed: *Hoc etiam Psalmista breviter insinuat dicens. Cor meum turbatum est…*
A stop where a colon is wanted. Preserved; the English reads "…intimates,
saying. *My heart is troubled*".

### 0392C–D — Jacob's fourth wrestling assigned to the brother
Printed: *Ter cum fratre suo, semel cum angelo. Primo in utero; secundo de lentis
edulio; tertio de benedictionis paternae oraculo. Cum fratre de Mesopotamia
rediens, ut praefatum est.* The fourth item is introduced *cum fratre*, but the
schema Hugh has just announced (and the biblical narrative, Gen. 32) makes the
return from Mesopotamia the wrestling **with the angel**; Hugh himself then
writes *Cum angelo quarto loco…* in the next sentence. Probably a slip for *cum
angelo*, possibly a compressed reference to the reconciliation with Esau on the
same journey. Rendered as printed.

### 0392D — `Hebr. III` lemma, *usque ad firmum*
Printed: *si tamen initium spei usque ad firmum teneamus.* The Vulgate has
*fiduciam et gloriam spei usque ad finem firmam retineamus*; Migne's text has
lost *finem* and left *firmum* as a bare substantive. Rendered literally — "right
up to that which is firm" — per the scripture rule (translate the wording quoted,
never a conventional version).

### 0393B — `salvabit` for `salvavit`
Printed (Isa. 63:5): *sed salvabit mihi brachium meum, et indignatio mea ipsa
auxiliata est mihi.* Future beside a perfect in the same verse. Rendered as
printed ("my own arm shall save for me"), which preserves the tense clash.

### 0393A — `per te passus est` for `pro te`
Printed: *quid pro te sustinuit sol justitiae, quid pro te egit Dominus virtutum,
quid per te passus est rex gloriae.* Two *pro te* then a *per te* in a
deliberately parallel tricolon. It may be rhetorical variation ("suffered
*through* thee," i.e. at thy hands) rather than a slip — Hugh is berating the
reader — so it is rendered as printed, "suffered through thee." A real word,
merely possibly wrong: no marker (Pattern 12 boundary).

### 0395C — `antequam ad persecutionem quis evadat`
Printed thus, paired with *antequam ad arcem contemplationis transeat*. The
parallel wants *perfectionem*, and *evadere ad* ("get clear to") fits an
attainment, not a persecution. Rendered as printed. A near-certain
*persecutionem*/*perfectionem* confusion, but a real word either way, so no
marker.

### 0397B — `quem aliud arbitror`
Printed: *Possumus autem locum quemlibet regni Babylonis intelligere, quem aliud
arbitror.* Unconstruable as it stands (*quem* … *aliud*). Rendered as closely as
the printed words allow: "which I judge to be something else."

### 0397A–B — `Bophorum [Bosphorum]`
Printed: *id est, Bophorum [Bosphorum] vocari non Ephrata.* The bracketed
correction is **Migne's own**, and it is reproduced verbatim and untranslated,
like `[ *al.,* …]`. Per Pattern 12, no `[sic: …]` is added on top of it: the
nineteenth-century editor had already caught the misprint and flagged it, and
ours marks only defects he passed over.

### 0398A — `sic secunda ad quartam, sic secunda ad tertiam`
Printed: *Et sicut prima ad tertiam, sic secunda ad quartam, sic secunda ad
tertiam.* The third limb repeats *secunda* and produces a proportion that
contradicts the second limb. Not dittography (the two runs differ), so no
`[d: …]`; rendered as printed. The whole proportion passage (0397D–0398A,
*Unde prima eodem numero sic historialiter…*) is muddled in the transmission and
Hugh's own arithmetic is hard to recover — he then sends the reader to
"arithmetic" for instruction. **Worth a human's eye.**

### 0400C–D — `Fiat voluntas tuas`
Printed thus in the list of petitions (accusative plural for nominative
singular). Latin case has no English exponent — "thy will" is identical either
way — so, as with the gender mismatches of Pattern 9, there is no emendation to
make and none to undo. Logged; the English is left alone. The same petition is
printed correctly as *Fiat voluntas tua* at 0403C (chunk 0010).

### 0400D–0401A — `succedunt quatuor virtutes`, then seven are listed
Printed: *Deinde quarto loco succedunt quatuor virtutes*, followed by *Prima* …
*septima* — seven items, in a passage whose entire architecture is sevenfold
(seven vices, seven petitions, seven gifts, seven beatitudes). *quatuor* is
almost certainly caught from the immediately preceding *quarto loco*. Rendered
as printed ("there follow four virtues"), and the seven items follow it on the
page, which is the defect made visible. **Worth a human's eye** — this is the
clearest single-word error in the batch.

### 0401A — `Avaritia … per montem Esau significatur`
The list of vice-to-place correspondences begins at the fifth vice (avarice) and
gives only two members (avarice/mount Esau, envy/Philistines) before breaking
off into the physician simile. The promised seven-part mapping onto Obadiah's
seven place-names is never completed. Rendered as printed; noted because a reader
will expect the missing five and may suspect our text of a hole. It is not a hole
in the transcription — the Latin runs continuously.

### 0376D — sense-reversing-letter (migne, high confidence)

Printed: *Per haec enim, quae distinguo, personarum proprietatem ostendo, non naturae vanitatem confundo.*

Conjecture: **non naturae UNITATEM confundo — "I do not confound the unity of nature"**

The immediately preceding sentence states the very rule being applied: "Caveat lector, ne personarum confundat trinitatem, nec divinae essentiae dividat unitatem"; the next sentence continues "sicut Trinitas natura vel essentia UNA." Hugh repeats the same formula verbatim at 0392B with the right word: "Haec dicens non divinae essentiae unitatem divido, nec propria personarum confundo." "Vanitas naturae" (emptiness of nature) is not a Trinitarian category at all and asserts the opposite of the unity he is defending.

### 0377D — sense-reversing-letter (migne, low confidence)

Printed: *Tumor praecedit superbiae, tenebrae sequuntur ignorantiae, infidelitas succedit idololatriae.*

Conjecture: **infidelitatI succedit idololatriA — idolatry succeeds unbelief (case endings transposed)**

The very next sentence gives the sequence in fixed order and puts unbelief BEFORE idolatry: "per petram, infidelitas ignorantiae; per scissuras, schismata idololatriae; per habitantem, consensum immunditiae." As printed, the order of the fall is reversed against its own immediately following exposition.

### 0382A — sense-reversing-letter (migne, medium confidence)

Printed: *Superbia expellit te, habitantem in scissuris petrae; qui dicis in corde tuo: Quis me deducet in terram?*

### 0385D — dropped-word (migne, low confidence)

### 0403A — dropped-word (migne, medium confidence)

Printed: *Cumque huic limo profundi animus fuerit infixus et luto coinquinationis et immunditiae involutus, evelli nequaquam potest, ad illum clamet, et auxilium ejus postulet, de quo Psalmista loquitur*

Conjecture: **evelli nequaquam potest, NISI ad illum clamet et auxilium ejus postulet — "it can by no means be plucked out unless it cry to him and beg his help"**

The Psalm verse quoted in the same sentence asserts precisely that the extraction does happen on such a cry: "Exspectans exspectavi Dominum, et intendit mihi, et exaudivit preces meas, et eduxit me de lacu miseriae, et de luto faecis."
