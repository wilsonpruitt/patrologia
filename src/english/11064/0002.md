## CHAPTER II. *What is the subject-matter of the hierarchies, and their arrangement.*

[0927]

[0927C] Dionysius the Areopagite, from a philosopher made a Christian theologian and describer of the hierarchies, shows the order of the divine arrangement in the governance of all things: how God made the rational creature a partaker of his power, appointing sacred magistracies, and powers, and principalities in heaven among the angels, and on earth among men, that they might have dominion over his creation. For it had been fitting that that part of his work should become a partaker of his power in its own arrangement — the part which in its own creation had received a participation of the likeness, so that, being the only one made to the likeness of its Maker, it alone should retain his image in its own ordering. God himself, therefore, the Maker of all things, whose ineffable majesty and unfailing power was able alone to govern [0927D] what it alone had created, willed to have partakers and fellow-workers in the governance of the things made by him — not that he himself might be helped by their ministry, but that they themselves might be made exalted by fellowship in his power. The Lord, therefore, alone and prince of all, established dominions and principalities beneath him and after him, for the accomplishing of the ministry which the order of the universe demanded, so that his works might be fulfilled through orders and arrangements, by a command running down from the highest into all things. The majesty, therefore, and the dominion, [0928C] which in the Lord and governor of all is adored universally, and most omnipotently, and super-excellently, and ineffably, has been distributed among those who have been made partakers of grace and glory, consorts and companions of the majesty, through parts, and divisions, and degrees, and orders — descending from him and looking back to him, and ordered beneath him who is the fount and cause of all things, and the first beginning: « One work, and one maker? one dominion, and one ruler, one prince, and one commonwealth: one Lord, and Father, from whom is all fatherhood in heaven and on earth [n: (Ephes. III)]: one in all, all things: and all things one in one. » For the highest good, by the participation of graces and the distribution of gifts, remaining one in itself, [0928D] is divided among all the partakers of its bounty, and all who partake of the one are gathered together into the form of its unity and its true simplicity. For they could not have been partakers of the power, unless first through grace they became consorts of the virtue. Nor could they, together with him, do what he himself can do, unless first they deserved to be, from him, what he himself is. But the omnipotent Maker is named governor of all things made by him, not by a foreign usurpation, nor by a perfunctory title; but by a power innate in him, and by an ingrown goodness cherishing and nourishing all things, [0929A] ruling and disposing all things, he has dispensed that same goodness and power, in manifold ways, to those who rule beneath him, according to the measure of participation and the reckoning of ministry, through orders and degrees — to the more excellent indeed, and those set above in dominion, imparting greater and super-excellent gifts; but to the lower, and to the subordinate degrees, granting lesser graces, and things freely serving, set over their subjects too without oppression. These are the distributions of lights, descending into all things, to which it has been given to partake of them from the Father of lights and the Sun of justice — made bright mirrors, that they may shine and give light. Subordinate indeed in that they shine, and set above in that they give light. And there is one light, and there is one good; and there are very many things that shine, and that partake of the one [0929B] good and the one light; and in that they partake, they are one, gathered together into the one, and led back to the one, and conformed to the one. These are the hierarchies, that is, the sacred principalities, which the highest hierarchy formed after its own likeness, and appointed beneath itself to have dominion and to preside over its works according to the orders marked out, [0930A] under one beginning and one power, from which is all power, and all virtue, and all light spiritually shining and spiritually giving light to all things that shine. This is the loftiness of the rational creature, and its sublimity, and its dignity to be marveled at, whereby it deserved to have dominion in the works of its Maker, having received virtue from him, and holding power together with him. Which virtue, since according to the measure of the bounty and the participation it is distributed variously and manifoldly, for the comeliness and beauty of those works which wisdom has adorned, from one virtue and one power, becomes many virtues and many powers. But, lest again the multitude beget schism and division, and the world war against itself with contrary rule, there is one beginning, and one moderator of all, [0930B] from whom they have what they are, and under whom they moderate what they can do, and they refer to him everything that they accomplish, so that unity may remain in all things, and peace endure in the kingdom of God, who creates all things and governs all.

## CHAPTER III. *On the three hierarchies*

[0929]

[0929C] There are three hierarchies, in the description of which Dionysius — theologian and narrator of the hierarchies and of the sacred powers that are in heaven and on earth — set forth a finished work. The first, chief of all, and the form and exemplar of the rest, is the highest and ineffable power of the Trinity — simple, and one, and uniform, without degree and difference and comparison, highest, and eternal, and perfect, and true, Omnipotent by its own virtue in creating and ruling all its works, receiving nothing external, losing nothing of its own. The second hierarchy is formed in the angelic nature, by adoption, and participation, and deigning — from the first, and beneath the first, and toward the first, second, sublime by likeness, having degree, and admitting difference, and allowing comparison, second after the highest Trinity in emulation, [0930C] distinguished by a threefold division. The third and last hierarchy is ordained in human nature, imitating the second in the first place and the first in the second, and receiving, through the middle one, the highest image and the super-excellent likeness in the things that partake, and referring it through that very thing, so that the whole may be from the one, and the whole toward the one, and the whole one. But theology took up the angelic hierarchy first, as the exemplar of the human hierarchy — to whose likeness the whole hierarchy that is among men has been made and formed — to be explained; and after it, the human, which stands according to its likeness; in the third place setting the highest, and ineffable, and super-exalted hierarchy, so that, from the illumination of the preceding ones — nearer to human minds — that which is exceedingly intelligible and obscure might grow clear.
