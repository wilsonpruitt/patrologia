## TITLE OF CHAPTER I. *That the divine illumination, coming forth variously unforeseen according to goodness, remains simple; and not this only, but it also unifies the things illumined.*



## THE TEXT.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0933B]

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « *Every best gift, and every perfect gift, is from above, coming down from the Father of lights [n: (Jac. I)]*. » *But also every procession of the manifestation of lights, the Father being moved, coming forth into us most excellently and abundantly: again, as a unifying power restoring us, fills us anew, and turns us back to the unity of the gathering Father, and to the deifying simplicity. ⟦s2⟧ For from him are all things, and unto him [n: (Rom. XI)]*, *as the divine word says. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore, calling upon Jesus, the paternal light, that which is, that which is true, that which « illumines every man coming into this world [n: (Joan. I)]* » *through whom we have had access to the principal light, the Father [n: (Ephes. II)]*: *upon the illuminations handed down in the Father of the most holy oracles, we shall look, as far as is possible. ⟦s4⟧ And the hierarchies of the heavenly minds, manifested to us from them symbolically and anagogically, [0933C] we shall consider, as far as we are able. ⟦s5⟧ And beholding [0934B] the principal, and super-principal brightness of the divine Father — which manifests to us in figured symbols the most blessed hierarchies of the angels — with immaterial and untrembling eyes of the mind, we are again restored from it into the single ray of that light. ⟦s6⟧ For neither is the ray itself anywhere, nor is it ever, forsaken by its own singular unity. ⟦s7⟧ But toward the anagogic and unifying tempering of the things that have been provided, it is most excellently and beautifully multiplied, and comes forth; and it remains within itself, securely, uniformly fixed in an unchangeable likeness; and toward those who look upon it, as far as is lawful, it extends itself proportionally to them, and unifies them according to the simple unity of itself. ⟦s8⟧ For neither is it possible for the divine ray to shine upon us otherwise than anagogically wrapped about with a variety of sacred veils, and connaturally and properly prepared, by the paternal providence, for those things that are according to [0934C] us. ⟦s9⟧ On account of which [sic: *quoae*], judging our most holy hierarchy [0935A] worthy of the celestial hierarchies by a supra-mundane imitation, the most perfect arrangement of sacred things both handed down the said immaterial hierarchies, varying them by material figures and formal compositions, so that we might proportionally ascend, for our very selves, from the most sacred formations into the simple and unfigured heights and likenesses. ⟦s10⟧ For neither is it possible for our mind to ascend to that immaterial imitation and contemplation of the celestial hierarchies, unless it use the material hand-leading that is according to itself. ⟦s11⟧ Deeming, indeed, the visible forms to be imaginations of the invisible beauty, and the sensible sweetnesses to be figures of the invisible distribution, and the material lights to be an image of the immaterial refulgence, and the sacred disciplines run through according to the intellect to be an image of the contemplative fullness, [0935B] and the ordered habits of the dispositions of the things that are here to be an image of things united and ordered toward the divine, and the very reception of the most divine eucharist to be an image of the participation of Jesus — and whatever other things are handed down to the heavenly essences in a supra-mundane way, but to us symbolically. ⟦s12⟧ On account, therefore, of this our co-rational deification, the merciful beginning of perfection, both manifesting to us the heavenly hierarchies, and making our hierarchy their fellow-minister unto virtue and unto our likeness, described the deiform sanctification of those super-celestial intellects in sensible images, in the sacred compositions of the oracles, that he might lead us back through sensible things to intelligible things, and from sacredly figured symbols to the simple summits of the celestial hierarchies.*
