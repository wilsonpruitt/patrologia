## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

« Therefore, invoking Jesus, the paternal light, which is, [and] true, which illuminates every man coming into this world [n: (Joan. I)]; through whom we have had access to the principal light, the Father; we shall look, as far as is possible, upon the illuminations handed down by the Father in the most sacred oracles, and we shall consider, as far as we are able, the hierarchies of the celestial spirits made manifest by them to us symbolically and anagogically. » In the preceding chapter he has briefly comprised the sum of the following work, namely [0939A] that every good is from the highest good, and every good is toward the highest good. Now, before he enters upon the text of his subject-matter, he makes an invocation to Jesus, who is the paternal light, by whose mediation all spiritual illuminations and gifts of graces are bestowed on those to be illuminated, so that, with him illuminating and helping, according to the traditions of the divine oracles — which themselves too were handed down by the Father for the illuminating of the hearts of men — he may be able both truly to recognize and worthily to narrate the invisible illuminations and sacred dispositions of the celestial spirits. « Therefore, invoking Jesus; » as if he said: Every illumination indeed is from the Father, but without Jesus the mediator no illumination can be had. « Therefore » we, who ask to be illuminated, « invoking Jesus, » namely that we may merit to be illuminated [0939B]. Then there follows, concerning Jesus Christ himself, that it is « the paternal light; » instead of what he ought to have said, "who is the paternal light," the reference has been made to the following expression, "which is the paternal light." Or, « invoking Jesus, the paternal light, » which, namely, is light; that is, it has true and eternal and unchangeable being, and is light from that which it is. For it is as with the illuminated lights, which are lights not from that which they are, but from that which they have received. For which phrase "true light" — the word "light" too is understood — because from that which it is, it shines, and it shines not only for itself, but also illuminates others. Which « illuminates every man coming into this world. » Not because all are illuminated; but because of all men [0939C] there is none who has of himself why he should merit to be illuminated rather than another. For this illumination is of grace, not of nature; a gift, not a debt; the benefit of the giver, not the reward of the receiver. For either this light illuminates every man coming into this world inasmuch as all were made by nature capable of reason, and received the light of understanding. Or this light illuminates every man coming into the world; not because all, as has been said, are illuminated; but because all who are either illuminated or not illuminated have no means of being illuminated from elsewhere. For to illuminate belongs to none but the light; as to be illuminated belongs to none but the one receiving the light. Wherefore, as it belongs to all to receive light, so it belongs to the light alone to illuminate all.

[0939D] There follows: « Through whom we have had access to the principal light, the Father. » Through the light Jesus we have had access to the light the Father. For Jesus is light, and the Father of Jesus is light; and the Son, light, was begotten of the Father, light; and the Father and the Son are one light. And the Father is called the principal light, not because he is a greater light or a better light, since he is the same light; but because he is light not from light, therefore the principal light. The Son is light from light, the Father is light not from light; and yet the Father and Son are one light, as the Father and Son are one God. And therefore the Father is the principal light, because from the Father, light, the Son, light. It can, moreover, be understood also in another way, that the Father is called the principal light. For I see that the Son himself too is light in a certain respect, in which he cannot be equal [0940A] to the Father. For the Father of Jesus is God, and God only; but the Son is God, and not only God, but also man and God; and Jesus is light in that he is God; and the same light which the Father, God, is. But in that he is man, he is light, since the very humanity of Jesus too was light in that it demonstrated the truth, and revealed the principal light, the Father. But the light of the humanity of Jesus was less than the light of the deity; and in the light of humanity indeed Jesus was inferior to the Father, but in the light of divinity the same as the Father. And we come through him — through the light of his humanity, in which was the Son alone — to the light of the divinity, in which was the Father and the Son. Therefore we have had access through the Son to the Father; and not to the Father alone, but both to the Son and to the Father. I see also another access [0940B] through Jesus to the principal light, the Father. For Jesus is the wisdom of the Father, and this wisdom revealed the Father, and the wisdom of the Father went forth, the Father remaining in the hidden place: and the Father remained invisible, and his wisdom was made visible, that it might lead through to the invisible Father; and when it was made visible, it did not cease to be invisible, because it came thither where it was not: thence, where it was, it did not withdraw. And it went forth first through the creature of the world, and manifested itself in its works: and, invisible, it began to be seen visibly in that which was visible, and a certain light was shown, that it might lead to a greater light: and the first light was of wisdom, and the second light was wisdom. And Jesus was made for us the way to the Father; from the inferior light to the principal light. Then wisdom came a second time [0940C], and went forth, as has been said, through the flesh, and became light in an earthen vessel, that it might illuminate us, and accustom us to the greater light, and lead us through to the principal light. But thus also we have had access through Jesus to the principal light, the Father. « Therefore, invoking Jesus, the paternal light, which is, which is true, which illuminates every man coming into this world: through whom we have had access to the principal light, the Father. » Up to this point the sentence hangs, until it is completed by what follows; and the sense is this. We, invoking Jesus, shall look, as far as is possible for us, upon the illuminations of the most sacred oracles — that is, upon the most sacred oracles, which illuminate us by the teaching of truth and the understanding of secrets — handed down [0940D] by the Father. For the illuminations of the divine oracles were handed down by the Father, that we might be illuminated by them, until we are made capable of the contemplation of the principal light; and, with them illuminating us, we now recognize the celestial secrets which we are not yet able to comprehend by sense. We can also take the "illuminations of the most sacred oracles" to mean the descriptions of the sacred oracle, and the configurations drawn from visible forms, in which it demonstrates to us, from the proposed likeness, the state of invisible things and the reason and manner of the celestial secrets. For by these very visible signs the human mind is more conveniently instructed and illuminated to the knowledge of invisible things. Therefore almost everywhere in the sacred oracle the appearances of visible things are employed for signification, [0941A] when the human mind is instructed concerning those things which are hidden from human understanding, so that through the things it knows it may be able to grasp and understand the things it does not know. These illuminations, set before us outwardly for instruction, we ought — invoking Jesus, who inwardly illuminates hearts — to examine, and according to their demonstration to learn the celestial secrets.

This is what follows: « And we shall consider, as far as we are able, the hierarchies of the celestial spirits made manifest by them to us symbolically and anagogically. » The order, then, is this: that first we examine the illuminations of the Holy Scriptures with the invocation of Jesus, through whom the inward illumination is given and without whom the outward is empty; and then, with Jesus illuminating, from these illuminations and demonstrations of Holy Scripture [0941B] we consider the sacred principates and powers of the celestial spirits, that is, of the celestial spirits (which, unless they were made manifest to us through the traditions of Holy Scripture, would be altogether invisible and unknown to us). For this is what he says: « We shall consider, as far as we are able, » or as far as we can. For our ability is small and scanty for the magnitude of such great things. « We shall consider the hierarchies, » that is, the sacred principates of the celestial spirits, that is, of the angelic spirits. The hierarchies, I say, made manifest to us, that is, demonstrated, or revealed by them, namely by the illuminations, that is, by the demonstrations of the sacred oracle, symbolically and anagogically. A symbol is the bringing-together of visible forms for the demonstration of invisible things. But anagogy [0941C] is the ascent, or elevation, of the mind for contemplating the things above. Here he notes a twofold mode of divine revelation, which was poured into the minds of the theologians and prophets through visions and demonstrations, which the Greeks call theophanies, that is, divine apparitions. Since sometimes invisible things were demonstrated through signs like to sensible things, sometimes through anagogy alone, that is, the ascent of the mind, purely contemplated in the things above. From these two kinds of visions, two kinds of descriptions also were formed in the sacred oracle. One, by which the truth of hidden things is shadowed forth by forms and figures and likenesses. The other, by which it is expressed nakedly and purely, as it is, without covering. When, therefore, [0941D] that which is hidden is manifested by forms and signs and likenesses, or that which is manifest is described, it is a symbolic demonstration. But when it is shown by pure by pure and naked revelation, or taught by plain and open narration, it is anagogic.
