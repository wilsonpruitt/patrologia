## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « And, looking with immaterial and untrembling eyes of the mind upon the principal and super-principal brightness of the divine Father, which manifests to us in figured symbols the most blessed hierarchies of the angels, again from it we are restored into the single ray of it. » Here he plainly teaches by what grades the procession of the divine illumination takes place down to us; and again by what advances our mind is led back to the contemplation of the highest brightness. For the divine light first descends into the angelic nature [0942A], and from it, by revelations and divine demonstrations and the mystical narration of the sacred oracle, transfuses itself even to our understanding and participation. But the human mind, again ascending by the same grades to the things above, by the sacred inspection of the divine oracle weighs the celestial secrets and that illumination of the divine brightness which is in the angels: from which, gradually growing up into the recognition of invisible things, it at last becomes strong enough to contemplate the very splendor of the highest light; and there comes about what was said at the beginning, that the one light both divides itself for the illuminating of many, so that it may reform all the illuminated to the sight and likeness of one brightness. Therefore, the brightness of the celestial spirits being known from the sacred traditions of the divine oracle, he shows how the contemplation [0942B] of the highest light follows upon this knowledge, saying: « And, looking with immaterial and untrembling eyes of the mind upon the principal and super-principal brightness of the divine Father, which manifests to us in figured symbols the most blessed hierarchies of the angels, again from it we are restored into the single ray of it. » The order of the words is: We, looking upon the principal and super-principal brightness of the divine Father with immaterial and untrembling eyes of the mind — which brightness manifests to us the most blessed hierarchies of the angels in figured symbols — again from it we are restored into its single ray. For, looking upon the brightness of the Father, we are restored, that is, reformed again — that is, being turned back reductively to that whence we came — into his single ray, [0942C] that in one light we may be one, we who shine from one light, and that there may be no discrepancy of the shining ones, just as there is no diversity of the lights. But the brightness of the Father is called principal and super-principal, because it is superior to all shining things not only in dignity or in being their cause, but also loftier in nature. And this brightness, because it is not corporeal but spiritual, and shines spiritually: therefore it can be seen only by immaterial, that is, incorporeal, eyes. Which eyes must also be untrembling, that is, sound and clear-sighted and unbeaten-back, lest, gazing upon so great a light with bleary keenness, they bring darkness upon themselves out of the very brightness. And rightly, after the brightness of the angels has been contemplated through the sacred oracles, [0942D] we are illuminated to the brightness of the divine Father; because the brightness of the angels itself too is made manifest to us, from the revelation of the sacred oracle, by the brightness of the Father descending through them into us. This is what he says: « *Which, in figured symbols,* » that is, in signs and conformations figured according to visible appearances, manifests to us the most blessed hierarchies of the angels, that is, the principates, dignities, and powers. The orders of the angels, and their powers, and their divine operations are altogether invisible: and unless they are demonstrated by visible signs and by likenesses drawn from visible things, they can in no way be understood by those who have known only visible things. Therefore the brightness of the Father, wishing to restore us to his principal light, first arouses us by the illuminations of the sacred oracle to contemplate the brightness of the angels [0943A], and, illuminated from that brightness, at last reforms us to the recognition of his own brightness and light, that we may be one in it, we who have received one thing from it. But this brightness of the Father, by the emission and illumination of one simple ray, diffuses itself through all things, and penetrates all things, since the Father begot one wisdom, through which he made all his works. For the Word of the Father is light from light; one Word, and one ray, and the Word itself is wisdom, and this wisdom is a proceeding light, by which it is known: one from one, and therefore one ray from one brightness, by which they are illuminated who are reformed toward it, that they may shine from it, and be light with it, just as he is light. And although there are many illuminated lights, shining variously and dissimilarly, yet there is one [0943B] light illuminating in all the illuminated.

This is what follows: « For neither anywhere, nor ever, is the ray itself forsaken by its own singular unity. » For the wisdom of God, although it pours itself variously and dissimilarly into the minds to be spiritually illuminated according to the capacity and possibility of those receiving it, yet in itself remains one and simple. Neither greater in itself in those who abound from it, nor less in those who, according to a lower grace of participation, receive to be united in it; but whole in the whole, and whole in each, transcending the highest and condescending to the lowest, offering itself to be partaken manifoldly, and remaining one in itself. Therefore it divides itself among many partakers, but by the participation of itself it restores the many partakers to unity. For we [0943C] can be gathered in it; but it cannot be divided in us. For the ray itself, that is, the illuminating wisdom itself, going forth from the principal light, the Father, and proceeding even to all things to be illuminated, is never forsaken by its own proper unity, since it is always with it; nor anywhere forsaken, since it is everywhere in it. For even when, through diverse times, it offers itself dissimilarly to the minds to be illuminated, it is the same and undissimilar. And when it shows itself present at once in diverse places and to diverse partners of participation, it is not manifold.
