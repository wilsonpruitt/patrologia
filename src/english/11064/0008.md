## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « But for the anagogic and unifying tempering-together of the things that have been provided, it is multiplied and comes forth most excellently and beautifully: and it remains within [0943D] itself securely, uniformly fixed in the unchangeable likeness of its nature: and, to those looking upon it, as far as is right, it extends itself proportionally to them, and unifies them according to the simple unity of itself. » As if he said: The ray of divine brightness, which illuminates those who shine spiritually, although it remains one in itself, is nevertheless variously multiplied by the participation and distribution of gifts, since it is distributed and multiplied in many and diverse ways. But this multiplication and variation is the beauty of all things; since, unless the several things were beautiful dissimilarly, all things together would not be supremely beautiful. For no one thing out of all the diverse things could contain what was the whole of beauty: and therefore the highest beauty was distributed by varied participation among the several things, that [0944A] it might be able to be perfect in all things together. But this distribution itself is multiplied most excellently and beautifully: most excellently in all things, and beautifully in the several, or, most excellently according to the disposition of the partakers. For it is multiplied most excellently, that the good which is perceived by many may be greater; and it is multiplied beautifully, that the comeliness of the whole may be greater — in that it is poured into the several partakers in varied and diverse ways, so that from the numerous multitude in the partakers there may come about the consummation of good, and from the dissimilar distribution the beauty of the partakers. Or the multiplication of the simple ray, by which we are illuminated, can suitably be understood according to the many and diverse figures and likenesses of the divine oracles: by which, taken according to the measure of us, the divine [0944B] wisdom — which in its purity and simplicity is altogether incomprehensible — is declared to human minds variously and manifoldly, although it is itself always one and the same. It is multiplied, therefore, because it is declared in many ways: most excellently indeed as regards itself, and beautifully as regards us. It is multiplied most excellently, because from the very multiplication of declarations it is more perfectly recognized; it is declared beautifully, because it is manifested by the beautiful and seemly appearance and dissimilarity of the forms that declare it. And this multiplication takes place for the tempering-together of those things that have been provided — that is, of the elect and of those provided for life — that they may be tempered together and united by the concord of participation, like many members in one body, so that the very diversity of gifts may confirm among them the fellowship [0944C] of unity and peace: so that each single member may not presume to divide itself from the framework of the whole body, since the fullness of good, which it has less in itself, it possesses in the fellowship of the others. For because each one has what another does not have, therefore each one has in the other what it does not have in itself, or has more in the other what it has less in itself. And therefore the several members tend toward one another and keep concord and peace among themselves: and this concord and peace is the tempering-together by which they agree with one another, that they may stand as one and be reformed to one. For this reason, therefore, he calls the tempering-together of those provided-for anagogic and unifying. Unifying indeed, that they may stand as one; anagogic, that they may be reformed [0944D] to one. Unifying, because it gathers the dispersed into one; anagogic, because it leads back the cast-down to higher things. Unifying, through the love of neighbor; anagogic, through the love of God. Therefore it is beautiful in congregation, best in elevation. Therefore the ray of divine illustration itself, multiplied whether through the distribution of spiritual gifts, by which it is partaken inwardly, or through the variety of mystical demonstrations, by which it is declared outwardly in the sacred oracle, comes forth — that is, proceeds into the hearts to be illuminated — whether from within filling them by inspiration, or from without pouring itself into them by the instruction of the sacred word: and yet it remains within itself securely, so that, while it offers itself to be partaken variously and manifoldly by the minds to be illuminated, it nevertheless does not flow out beyond the simplicity of its [0945A] unity. For its unity itself therefore always remains within itself, because a coessential unchangeableness guards and keeps it, so that, although a plurality of partakers approaches to the communication of it, yet the diversity of participation does not prevail to the division of it. It remains, therefore, within itself securely, that it may always be the same, and may not go out from that which it is into that which it is not: but remaining uniformly fixed in its unchangeable likeness, so that, namely, it may not differ from itself, as though made unlike itself. Nor does it alone remain one, but also to those looking upon it and turned toward it — as far as is right, that is, permitted and possible for them — it extends itself to them, that is, it reaches out so that it may come even to those who are far off, and yet does not withdraw from itself. It extends itself, namely, proportionally, [0945B] that is, so that some receive more, others less, according to the dispensation of the distribution of gifts, or the possibility of the capacity of the partakers. For those who are nearest receive more abundantly from it: and so then each one, the more they approach, the more they receive; and the more they withdraw, the less they receive. Yet all receive; each one according to his part. This is what he says: « It extends itself proportionally, because nevertheless it extends itself to all, and pours itself into all, illuminates all, and makes them to be lights from its own light, and through this makes them to be one, just as it itself is one. And this is what he says: « It unifies them according to the simple unity of itself. » For the one illumination of all is one likeness and one form, in which all are one from it — they who [0945C] are illuminated by it — and one with it, since the illuminated are lights, just as it itself is light. Yet those who are one from it are one by unification, not by unity, because what they are, one out of many, they are one in one. For they themselves are many, and therefore they are not one in that which they are; but, because they are many in one, from one the many are one, and they are one by unification, not by unity. But it itself is one not by unification but by unity, because it is one from that which it is, which is one. And therefore he said "simple unity," because it is one from one, and the same one from which it is, it itself is. Therefore unity in one is simple; but unification is manifold in one, because those are one from one, and one in one: but here [0945D] one out of many, because they are many in one, and one in many. Then he adds the reason why the divine ray, that is, the divine wisdom, which is one from the Father and simple with the Father, is variously multiplied through the likenesses and forms of demonstrations in the sacred oracle: namely, because the invisible wisdom of God could not otherwise become known to us, unless it conformed itself unto likeness to those forms of visible things which we know, and through them expressed to us, by signifying, its invisible things which we do not know. Therefore the divine ray, which in itself is one and simple, coming to us, is multiplied by the variety of significations and demonstrations, because the human mind is not able to grasp it in its purity and simplicity.

[0946A] « For neither is it possible for the divine ray to shine upon us otherwise, unless, by the variety of sacred things, or the mind, [it be] anagogically veiled about, and prepared, connaturally and properly, by the fatherly providence, for those things which are according to us. » The sacred veils, in which the divine ray shines for us, are the mystical descriptions in the sacred oracle, which bring forward visible forms and likenesses of invisible things for their declaration. By which veils, namely, the divine ray itself is anagogically veiled about. For anagogy, as has been said, is called the ascent of the mind, or elevation, into the contemplation of the things above. It is therefore anagogically veiled about, because it is veiled for this end, that it may grow more fully bright; it is covered for this, that it may appear the more. Its overshadowing, therefore, is our illumination; and its veiling-about [0946B] is our elevation. Just as weak eyes freely behold the sun covered by a cloud, which cannot gaze upon its flashing light: so too it is impossible for the divine ray to shine upon the bleary eyes of the mind, unless it be veiled about by the variety of sacred veils and prepared, connaturally and properly, by fatherly providence, for those things which are according to us. For unless fatherly providence had provided for us in this with ineffable goodness, the light of it could not shine at all to our eyes: and therefore it provided for us fatherly and devoutly, that it might prepare that light for us and fit it to those things which are according to us — to things and likenesses and forms: and so, connaturally and properly, that is, according to our nature and the property of those things which belong to our nature, it might show it to us prepared [0946C] and fitted. It conforms itself to our things, that through our things it may become known to us, so that those things which are connatural and proper to us, set forth in demonstration, may more easily be understood by us.
