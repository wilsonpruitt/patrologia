## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « For which reason also the most perfect disposition of sacred things, judging our most holy hierarchy worthy of the supermundane imitation of the celestial hierarchies, handed down the said immaterial hierarchies too, varying them by material figures and formal compositions. » As if he said: Because invisible things are suitably demonstrated through visible things, therefore the divine Wisdom, which disposes all things to the likeness of the angelic hierarchy, which had been invisibly [0946D] ordered in the heavens, formed the human hierarchy visibly on earth, so that in men too, as in the angels, there might be sacred powers and principates, by which human conduct, proceeding in time, might be governed, so that from the visible disposition of men the invisible disposition of the angels might become known. For this is what he says: For which reason the most perfect disposition of sacred things — that is, the highest hierarchy, the ineffable Trinity itself, from which, and according to which, all sacred things, that is, all sacred ordinations, are disposed — judging our most holy hierarchy, that is, the human, worthy of the supermundane, that is, spiritual or intellectual, imitation of the celestial hierarchies, so that it might imitate them spiritually and be disposed according to them. And therefore "varying," that is, variously and manifoldly [0947A] declaring those same already-mentioned immaterial, that is, spiritual and angelic hierarchies by material, that is, corporeal and visible, or figures taken according to visible things, and by formal compositions, that is, fittingly and competently expressed according to the plan of the formations, "he hands down," that is, institutes, namely our most holy hierarchy itself, so that in this too the reason of visible demonstrations to the invisible truth might be preserved, that we may ascend from these most sacred formations, which are set forth outwardly for demonstration, to the unfigured heights and likenesses, that is, the simple and spiritual ones. Let us ascend, I say, proportionally to our very selves, that is, according to the proportion and measure of spiritual gifts, illuminated [0947B] through the things that are outward, into the recognition of invisible things, and to the sublime and simple truth that ever remains the same. For the heights and likenesses themselves are the invisible powers. Heights indeed by dignity, likenesses by unchangeableness, unfigured by simplicity. To the knowledge of which we ascend proportionally to our very selves, according to the difference of progressions and the increments of illuminations, by the grades of spiritual advancements in us, by the difference of gifts among us. For according to that proportion by which we now perceive the gifts of graces differently, we deservedly afterward pass over into the number of the angels — either here through the contemplation of the mind, or there through retribution. For thus we ascend proportionally to our very selves, when, according to [0947C] the fact that in the present we are disposed differently through the gifts of graces and the grades of dignities, we are lifted up to the participation of invisible truth by a secret and invisible promotion. But the truth itself, in us indeed, is figured figuratively and materially through the offices of spiritual dignities and the grades and distributions of the orders, and is formed according to habits and actions and visible signs, which are set forth in our hierarchy, as it were, for declaration — all which things, in the celestial and angelic hierarchy, according to the simple truth, in a manner sublime and superexcelling our nature, subsist purely and uniformly without signs and figures: according to which excellence theology calls the angelic orders themselves "heights" [0947D] and "likenesses," either because through no mutability do they differ from themselves, or because those things which are in them according to the simple truth seem to be, as it were, certain likenesses and exemplars of the things which subsist through figures and images. For unless there were some likeness between these and those, through these there would be no declaration of those. But now, since the divine wisdom formed these visible things, for the emulation of invisible things, in the first creation, in the second disposition it also established from these same things, for the rational soul ascending to the recognition of invisible things, signs and examples of demonstrations.

For this is what follows: « Since it is not possible for our soul to ascend to that non-material imitation and [0948A] contemplation of the celestial hierarchies, unless by that, » which is according to it, it make use of material hand-leading. By "non-material" he means the spiritual imitation, or contemplation, and the incorporeal; since everything that is corporeal is from matter. But the spiritual nature is rightly called immaterial, since it neither is from matter, so as to be from another, nor can be matter, so that something else be from it. But by "material hand-leading" he understands corporeal signs, by whose hand-leading, as it were, the human mind makes use, so that from visible things it may be directed to the imitation and contemplation of invisible things. To imitation indeed through the exercise of virtue; but to contemplation through the knowledge of truth. But note a new composition and a new likeness in the fact that he says "hand-leading." For the mind [0948B] of man, wrapped about by the darkness of its own ignorance, cannot go out to the light of truth unless it be directed and, like a blind man using a hand to lead him, walk on where he does not see. But these hand-leadings and directions, which the mind uses in tending toward invisible things, are taken from visible signs and from demonstrations formed according to visible things. And all this was disposed and ordered by the most perfect disposition of sacred things, which in Greek is called hierothesia, that is, the placing of sacred things — namely the highest Trinity, from which and according to which all sacred things are disposed and ordered in heaven and on earth — and by another name is called teletarche, that is, the beginning of purgation, since from it is every cleansing and beginning of purgation — not only that the things which are evil may be made good; but also of illustration [0948C] and deification, that the things which were good may be made better. Therefore this highest priesthood, or highest fatherhood, cleansing and sanctifying and illustrating all things, instituted and ordained this: that in the first place, according to it, the angelic nature might be invisibly formed, and in the second place the human nature, aroused through visible things, might be reformed to the same image; because the human soul cannot be led back to the knowledge or imitation of invisible things unless instructed through visible demonstrations, since the visible things themselves too were so made by God that, according to that likeness and emulation which they received toward those invisible things, they can suitably declare the same, so that our soul, using the leading of these, may be directed to those [0948D] according to these, estimating and weighing those things by the likeness of the demonstration.
