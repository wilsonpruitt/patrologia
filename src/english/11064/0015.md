## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

For this is what follows: « So that neither may we too, in the same fashion as the many, impurely suppose the heavenly and deiform minds to be certain many-footed and many-faced things, and formed after the herd-nature of oxen, or after the bestial imagination of lions, and shaped after the appearance of eagles with a curved beak, or after the threefold beating of the wings of flying things. And that we should imagine certain fiery wheels above the heaven; and material thrones necessary to the Divinity for its reclining, and certain horses of many colors, and arms-bearing supreme commanders; and whatever other things have been handed down to us out of the sacred words, sacredly and in a form-giving way, in the variety of manifesting [0964C] symbols. » So that neither may we too, then, in the same fashion as the many — that is, in the way that the many suppose — suppose the heavenly and deiform minds, that is, the invisible, incorporeal spirits, which have the likeness and form of God, not of bodies, to be many-footed; namely, in the way in which they are figuratively described in the sacred word under the figures and forms of animals, as in Ezekiel, in Isaiah, and Zechariah, and Micah, and other prophets, the angelic spirits are figured by four-footed animals, and flying things, and the rest in this manner. And lest we also suppose certain of those spirits to be many-faced, that is, to have many countenances or faces, as it is written in Ezekiel concerning the holy living creatures: « Four [0964D] faces had one of them [n: (Ezech. I)]: » which, although it may be fittingly referred to the holy evangelists, or the apostles, or any of the just, is nevertheless also not inconveniently taken of the holy angels, who announce the incarnation of the Word, and the passion, and the resurrection, and the ascension, and in all these things attend upon the incarnate Word in the service of ministry.

There follows: « And after the herd-nature of oxen, » — it is to be understood, and lest we suppose certain of them formed after the herd-nature of oxen, that is, after the likeness and form of oxen, which is that of the herd, not angelic, as in Ezekiel the form of an ox, or of a calf, is expressed in the figuration of the living creatures. Or again, lest we suppose the same spirits formed after the bestial imagination of lions, that is, after the imagination or [0965A] image of lions, which is bestial, and unworthy of a spiritual and rational nature, as in Ezekiel the face of a man and the face of a lion is mentioned in the figuration of the living creatures. And after the appearance of eagles with a curved beak, again it is understood, let us not suppose them formed. But in that he says « with a curved beak, » he plainly stirs a fitting derision, as in the rest, against those who think these things assigned to a spiritual nature according to property. It is known, moreover, that in Ezekiel's distinction the face of an eagle also is mentioned.

There follows: « Or shaped after the threefold beating of the wings of flying things, » — it is understood, let us not suppose. For as it is written in Isaiah of the throne of the one sitting and of the temple: « The seraphim stood above it: [0965B] six wings to the one, and six wings to the other. With two they veiled the head, with two they veiled the feet; and with two they flew, the one to the other [n: (Isa. VI)]. » And elsewhere: « For with two they covered their bodies [n: (Ezech. I)]. » Where indeed, in the six wings, by twos and twos in their distinctions, a threefold or tripartite beating is found. For six, joined two and two together, make three pairs of wings.

There follows: « And let us imagine certain fiery wheels above the heaven. » The negative particle is supplied from above, namely, let us not imagine certain fiery wheels above the heaven, and let us not imagine also material thrones as though necessary to the Divinity for its reclining, since the divine nature, which alone bears all things, has no need of a support. But of the fiery wheels and thrones [0965C] we have testimony in the book of Daniel [n: (Dan. VII)], and in Isaiah [n: (Isa. VI)], and Micah, concerning the one sitting upon a lofty and high throne.

There follows: « And certain horses of many colors » (as in Zechariah there are read white horses, and black, and black, and dappled [n: (Zach. VI)]; and in the Apocalypse likewise [n: (Apoc. VI)]) — it is understood, let us not imagine. « And arms-bearing supreme commanders, » likewise let us not imagine, that is, princes and leaders of the army bearing arms, or clad in arms, as we read in Zechariah, and in Micah. Likewise the Lord sitting upon a lofty throne, and the host of heaven at his right hand and his left; and in the book of Kings the chariots, and horses, and horsemen of fire shown upon the mountains to Elisha, and coming to the aid of his servant [n: (IV Reg. VI)]. They are called supreme commanders [0965D] as it were leaders, or conductors of the princes of the army. It is a compound name, from that which is ἀρχός *archos*, that is, prince, and στρατηγος *strategos*, which is leader and commander of an army; and so supreme commanders are understood as the leaders of the princes of the army, who rule over the princes themselves, or, being superior, afford them their leadership. All these things, therefore, taken by likeness from those things which are according to us, and attributed to those heavenly spirits for the signifying of those things which are invisible to themselves, are so to be received by us that we do not suppose these things to be in them in this way, but that through these things we may study to weigh other things, which are in them and are invisible to us, visibly set forth. For no corruptible appearance attains to their purity; and therefore in a certain manner [0966A] it is an impure supposition which, without discretion, attributes things so lowly and unworthy to that excellent nature according to property. Whether therefore these things, or whatever other things out of the words, that is, the Scriptures, have been handed down to us sacredly and in a form-giving way — that is, by a sacred fashioning or figuring, or the figuring of a sacred thing — in the variety of manifesting symbols, that is, of sacred figures and descriptions, by which secret and hidden things are made manifest: let us so receive them all that, according to their likeness and signification, we believe all those things of which they are made to be true; and yet let us think none of these things to be in them according to property.

There follows: « For very artfully theology has used poetic sacred formations in unfigured intellects [0966B] — revealing, as has been said, our mind, and providing for it its own and connatural leading-back, and reforming unto it the anagogical holy Scriptures. » To this he responds to what he had said above, that it is to be considered with what sort of formations sacred Scripture would figure the heavenly orders. As if he said: For this reason the very formations of the heavenly orders in the sacred word are diligently to be inspected; because they were made for this end, that they might reveal, that is, illuminate, our mind unto the knowledge of invisible things through these visible and known things. For very artfully, or prudently, or with consideration, theology, that is, the divine Scripture, has used poetic sacred formations, that is, descriptions of forms or figures set forth for the representing of sacred or holy things — [0966C] poetic formations, that is, ones devised, or invented by reason and set forth through the likeness of mystical tradition — for the signifying of unfigured intellects, that is, of spirits subsisting without figure and corporeal form in their own simplicity. « Revealing, as has been said, » namely in the foregoing chapter, « our mind » from the deep veil of ignorance, so that with bared and open face it may contemplate heavenly things: « and providing for it, namely for the mind, its own and connatural leading-back, » so that, instructed, it may be led back to the knowing of invisible things through those things which are connatural and its own; and reforming, namely, or adapting, or tempering unto the mind itself the anagogical holy Scriptures, so that first, being conformed to us, they might descend to us, and afterward, by leading back to higher things, [0966D] might lift up the illumined. This, then, theology did very artfully, that, speaking to us, it might take up our things, and, through our things, might lift up those admonished and instructed unto its own. But if perchance it seem to anyone that this is to be approved insofar as theology itself, for the signifying of visible things, took beautiful and seemly formations from those things which are ours; but that it is reprehensible and unfitting that it also appended likenesses unseemly and unworthy of so great a purity and majesty: to him it is to be answered that theology took up not only like, but also unlike, forms for the demonstration of divine things for this reason — that through that which is unlike and, as it were, alien, said manifestly of them by figure, even that which [0967A] could seem proper might be recognized as figured and alien to property:
