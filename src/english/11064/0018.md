## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « For this, as I suppose, is more powerful in it. » This, namely, from which is signified not what it is, but what it is not, is more powerful, that is, more efficacious, and more proper, and more expressive in it; since he who says what it is not, says that which in some manner can be understood; but he who says what it is, says that which in no manner can be comprehended. But it is more powerful and more excellent, as regards the expression of truth, to say what God is not, than what he is. « Since indeed, as the hidden and priestly tradition has introduced, we truly say this to be it — not according to any of those things which are; but we are ignorant [0973C] of its superessential, and invisible, and ineffable infinitude. » As if he said: As the authority of sacred Scripture testifies, introduced from the hidden unto manifestation, and handed down for correction and information. As, then, that tradition, that is, that authority which is handed down, hidden as regards the mysteries of hidden sacraments, and priestly as regards the dignity and holiness of the divine writings themselves, and its own sanctification, because it was both handed down by divine priests and prophets, and divinely sanctified for the sake of those to be sanctified through it. As, then, that hidden and priestly tradition introduced, we learned that this, that is, such a thing according to which [0973D] we truly say it, that is, the divine nature, to be, is not of all those things which are; because no species of created things so approaches to its likeness as to show expressly and according to property that which truly is in it. Or thus we learned that it is not truly what we say it to be according to something, that is, according to any of those things which are: for that which truly is according to any of those things which are, cannot be said to be the whole; and therefore when we say it to be something according to those things which are, we do not yet manifest by expression that which it truly is. But we are ignorant of its superessential, and invisible, and ineffable infinitude. For that which is infinite cannot be estimated by human knowledge: which, because it is ineffable, is not said; and because it is invisible, is not known; and [0974A] because it is superessential, is not comprehended. Of it, then, the human mind can grasp something, itself it cannot; and the human tongue can say something of it, itself it cannot; nor for that reason, however, is what is said of it to be supposed false; since it is only of it, and it itself is not this which is said; nor is what is thought of it vain, since it is only of it, and it itself is not this which is thought; since what is said is true, and what is thought is truth: which so leads unto it, although it subsists more sublimely and loftily in itself.

There follows: « If, then, the negations in divine things are true, but the affirmations incompact: the manifestation through unlike formations is more apt to the obscurity of hidden things. » As if he said: Since more expressly and more [0974B] properly we say God to be nothing that is, when we say both to be something, and not to be, truly, it is manifest that in divine things, that is, in those things which are said of God and attributed to God, the negations are true, that is, proper; but the affirmations incompact, that is, improper and not cohering, since they try to join and fit together unlike things, according to that mode of speaking by which human speech cannot otherwise be formed concerning God. But if the negations in divine things are true, that is, proper, and the affirmations incompact, that is, improper, it is manifest that the manifestation made through unlike formations is more apt to the obscurity of the hidden things to be revealed than that made through like ones; since the former strives to demonstrate, as it were by negation, by removing, what God is not, [0974C] but the latter tries to show, as it were by affirmation, by positing, what he is. « And now, therefore, the sacred descriptions of the words do not, as base, fill up the heavenly adornments, manifesting them by unlike fashionings of forms; and through these showing the excellences of the supermundane things above all material things together. And now therefore, » — since unlike figurations stand more properly in divine things, according to that mode by which, out of all things which are, nothing is properly named of God; according to this mode, therefore, they do not fill up, that is, assert to be filled up, the heavenly adornments, that is, the heavenly orders or dispositions, the base, that is, deformed, formations themselves which are set forth in the sacred word; manifesting [0974D] them, namely the adornments, by unlike fashionings, that is, by compositions of forms unlike and alien from their excellence; and through these fashionings showing the excellences of the supermundane, that is, of the heavenly and invisible things, above all material things together; this is, their excellences in relation to all material things; that is, showing that the supermundane and spiritual things themselves excel all material things. For in this, that they attribute unlike figures to them, they show that even those things too, which seem to be said of them according to likeness, do not rise up to their property.
