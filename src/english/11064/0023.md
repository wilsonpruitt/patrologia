## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « For perhaps indeed we would not, from need, come into inquiry, and into anagogy through a diligent scrutiny of divine things, unless the deformity of the manifesting formation of angels wrested us thereto. » As if he were saying: From our need [0988C] into this inquiry — that is, to seeking these things as regards the composition of visible images — or into anagogy as regards the investigation of invisible truth; from our need, I say — we who, knowing the truth, do not need these inquiries — into this inquiry, I say, we would not come, unless (on account of others who might be scandalized thereby, if they were not instructed) the deformity wrested us to it. First, note the doubling of the negation, by which the expression is made: or in this it is manifest that deformed formations are useful in divine things, because we too would not scrutinize the truth of divine things so diligently, unless the deformity of the manifestations wrested us, that is, compelled us. This is what he says: « Perhaps we would not indeed [0988D] come into an inquiry leading into anagogy, » that is, the contemplation of things above; from need, namely, of the understanding of truth: we would not come, I say, through a diligent scrutiny of divine things, « unless it wrested us, » that is, compelled us « to come into inquiry from need, and through inquiry into anagogy, » — the very deformity of the manifesting deformation of the angels, that is, unless the deformity of the formation through which the angels are manifested in Holy Scripture should compel us; I say: « Not allowing our mind to remain in the dissimilar makings of forms, » that is, in the deformed compositions of representations, which are dissimilar from the truth of spiritual things. Unless, then, the very deformity of the representations should compel our mind from the visible figures to [0989A] seek the truth, our mind itself would remain in those things which are set forth outwardly, as things only to be believed and venerated, and would not recognize its own need, so as to think that other things, beyond these, were to be desired and sought by it. But now the very deformity, intervening, compels the mind to go forth from the figure to the truth — the mind, I say, unable to remain in those things which are in themselves unworthy, and incongruous with the truth of divine things. « But the mind struggling to deny » of the divine things themselves those « material possibilities » which are set forth outwardly in signs; and through the fact that it thus holily abhors them, « growing accustomed purely to extend itself through visible things, » roused « into the super-mundane heights »: that is, the angelic excellences, which are supereminent over and excel all worldly things. For unless, roused from these [0989B] by the advancement of contemplation, it should wholly leave these very things behind, it would not be able purely to behold those things which it strives to contemplate.

There follows: « So many things indeed have been said by us, » etc. He himself connects the preceding matters to the following narration. « So many things indeed, » as many as we have said thus far, « have been said by us on account of the angelic descriptions of images, » that is, on account of the descriptions of angels, which are made through images and visible forms, and forms taken from visible things — which descriptions are « of the divine discourses, » that is, made through the divine discourses, and set forth in the divine discourses, which descriptions are also material, that is, formed according to the nature and likeness of material and corporeal things; and they are also unfitting [0989C] on account of the incongruous and base figurations of those things to which excellences have been attributed. For on account of such a thing the inquiry which has been ventilated thus far was set forth. « But next it behooves us to set apart what we consider the hierarchy itself to be, and what benefit those who obtain the hierarchy receive from the hierarchy itself. It behooves us, he says, next, » that is, after the aforesaid, to set apart, that is, to distinguish what we consider the hierarchy itself, taken generally, to be, its definition being set forth. « Next » also « it behooves us to set apart what benefit they receive, » that is, what of usefulness those who obtain the hierarchy receive from the hierarchy itself, namely, which each one obtains. And in saying this, he says, I pray that « my Christ » may be the « leader » of my discourse [0989D]; yet only if it is lawful for me, that is, permitted, to say « my. » For this is a great thing, and seems as it were close to presumption, that a sinner should receive the reward of the just, and one cast down should be confident of the familiarity of the Most High. Therefore I say [0990A] let « Christ » (and if I dare more, I say « my ») be the leader of my discourse. Without whom neither can discourse be right, because he is the Word; nor understanding be true, because he is Wisdom. For all who savor the true, savor through him; and he himself is the inspiration « of every hierarchical manifestation, » since he himself, being Wisdom breathed into the minds of the holy theologians, manifests the whole account of the hierarchical disposition — whether that which is in heaven, or that which is on earth — to those to whom he wills, in the ways he wills. « But you, O boy, » etc. He speaks to Timothy, the disciple of Paul, to whom he is said to have written these things — whom he also calls a boy for this reason: either because he preceded him in age, or because he held toward him the place and dignity of a teacher and master. « But you, O boy, hearken. » As if he says: [0990B] Because the things which are to be said are great, therefore hearken holily and becomingly, as befits the very holy things which are to be said. « Hearken, » I say; according to the holy legislation of our priestly tradition, which bids us not to contend, nor to resist in doctrine, but reverently and humbly to hearken. Or hearken to these things which have been said « according to the holy legislation of our priestly tradition, » that is, which have been said according to the holy law, that is, the Holy Scripture, which has been brought to us by God through the priestly tradition, that is, through the tradition of the holy and the sanctified. Thus, then, « hearken to the wondrously said things, » that is, those things which have been wondrously said: « you, » I say, who have been made divine in the divine doctrine, which makes those who hear and do it divine; [0990C] or, having been made divine in the divine doctrine of the wondrously said things, hearken to the things I propose to be said. And not only hearken, so as to receive the things said reverently, but also « in the secret of your mind covering about the things which are holy, keeping them from the unclean multitude, » that is, from those who are both unclean in conduct and divided in desires: « as uniform things, » that is, undivided and untouched, « guard them, » lest you imprudently expose them to be torn and defiled. « For it is not lawful, as the discourses say, to cast to swine the unconfused, and light-formed, and beneficent adornment of the invisible pearls. » For those are that unclean multitude who are signified by swine in Holy Scripture, who defile the Word of God by living wickedly, and defile the invisible pearls, that [0990D] is, the spiritual understandings — which adorn the understandings with morals, shine through pure truth, are unconfused through the affluence of grace, and are beneficent toward those who touch them with reverence and honor.
