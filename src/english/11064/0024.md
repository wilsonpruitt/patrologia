## THE TEXT.

[0989D]

*The hierarchy is, according to me, a divine order, and a knowledge, and an activity assimilating itself, as far as possible, to the deiform, [0990D] and, proportionally to the illuminations divinely implanted in it, ascending into the likeness of God. The divine beauty, as simple, and best, as consummative. It is pure [0991A] indeed, universally, from all dissimilarity; distributive according to the worth of each one's proper light; and perfective, in the most divine sacrifice, according to the compactly immutable formation of those perfected unto it. The interpretation, therefore, of the hierarchy is, unto God, as far as is possible, a likeness and unity, having Him as the leader of every holy action and knowledge; and unto its own most divine comeliness immutably defining them; but, as far as possible, it reforms, and perfects its own praisers as divine statues, mirrors most clear, and pure, receptive of the principal light and of the divine ray, and filled indeed with the implanted sacred brightness: and pouring it out again copiously into the things which follow, declaring the divine laws. For it is not lawful for the perfecters of the holy, and for the holily perfected, to do anything at all [0991B] apart from the + mysteries of the sacrificial victims, or the sacred ordinations; but neither to subsist otherwise, if they seek His divine brightness; and they look unto it holily and becomingly, and are reformed, according to the anagogy of each of the holy intellects. Does not, therefore, he who says "hierarchy" declare universally a certain sacred disposition, an image of the divine beauty, in the hierarchical orders and knowledges celebrating the mysteries of its own illumination, and assimilated to its own principle, as is permitted? For to each of those who obtain the hierarchy this is the perfection: to ascend, according to one's own analogy, into the imitation of God, and — most divine of all, as the discourses say — to become a fellow-worker of God [n: (I Cor. III; III Joan. I; Matth. V)]*, *and to show forth the divine action shining again in oneself, according [0991C] as is possible. Inasmuch as the order of the hierarchy is that some be purged, but some purge; and some be illumined, but some illumine; and some be perfected, but some perfect; for each in a deiform union, in whatever manner. The divine blessedness — as far as it is to be spoken of among men — is indeed pure, without any dissimilarity; full of eternal light; perfect and not needy, at once of all perfection; purging, and illumining, and perfecting; but a great purgation, and holy illumination, and perfection, above purgation, above light, perfect beforehand; through itself perfect, the principle of perfection; and the cause indeed of every hierarchy, and of everything sacred, according to its supereminent loftiness. [0991D] It behooves, therefore, as I consider, that those to be purged be wholly perfected by the pure, and be freed from all confusion of dissimilarity. But that those to be illumined be filled with divine light, and be led back unto a contemplative habit and power in the most chaste eyes of the mind. That from the imperfect, being restored, they become partakers of the perfective knowledge of the sacred things searched out. But that the purgers, by the greatness of purgation, impart to others out of their own chastity. But that the illuminators — having more luminous minds, and being properly suited both for the participation of light and for its distribution, and most richly filled with holy charity — convey their own super-excellent light entirely upon those who are worthy of light. But that the perfecters, as teachers of the perfective tradition, perfect those to be perfected by a most sacred doctrine, through the knowledge of the sacred things beheld. Is not, therefore, [0992A] each order of the hierarchy's disposition led back, according to its own analogy, to divine cooperation — accomplishing those things by grace and by the power given by God, which are present in the divinity naturally and supernaturally, and, by it superessentially enacted, are manifested unto the imitation of the hierarchy, as far as is possible, by minds loving God.*
