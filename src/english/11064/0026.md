## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

« For it is not lawful for the perfecters of holy things, and for the holily perfected, to work anything at all beyond their proper mysteries of victims, and their sacred ordinations. » As if he should say: For this reason those who in the order of the hierarchies are superior transfuse their lights to the inferiors, according to the divine laws; because it is not lawful, that is, not at all permitted, to work anything either for the perfecters of holy things, that is, for those who perfect others in holiness, or for the holily perfected, that is, for those who are perfected by others in holiness, beyond the proper mysteries of victims, that is, the proper gifts of graces, and the proper [0996A] sacred ordinations, that is, the sacred orders proper and assigned to each one. For to work without grace is vain; to work beyond order, or against it, is perverse. For individuals have their proper gifts, according to which they are able to work; and their proper orders, according to which they ought to moderate their working. Therefore it is necessary that each one strive to recognize the grace which he has received, lest he begin to presume in that which he cannot do; and to attend to his proper office and order, lest he dare to transgress in that which he ought not. For that this order of the divine disposition must be diligently observed, the Apostle Peter admonishes, saying: « Each one, as he has received grace, ministering it one to another » [n: (I Petr. IV)]. And the Apostle Paul reproves those who were unwilling to hold this divine ordination [0996B] and dispensation, saying: « Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all doctors? Are all workers of powers? Have all the grace of healings? Do all speak with tongues? Do all interpret? Be zealous for the better charisms. » [n: (I Cor. XII.)] Therefore, then, it is not lawful for those who have been made partakers of divine grace, whether they have received it immediately from God, or through man, to work anything beyond their proper gifts and offices, so that the divine beauty may be conserved in all, and the order of the supreme dispensation may persevere. He calls perfecters of holy things those who have received so great a grace from God that, by illuminating and instructing, they perfect others also unto holiness. The holily perfected are those who, [0996C] illumined and instructed by those very superiors, are perfected in holiness. By mysteries of victims he wishes gifts of graces to be understood: which are for this reason called mysteries, because they are secretly inspired; but victims, because they are bestowed for the cleansing and expiation of those who receive them. Or by ministries of victims he means the exhibitions of holy works, and the administrations of the divine sacraments: which are both victims, because they are offered through the exhibition of action, and mysteries through the sacraments of signification; because through that which is done visibly without in the sacrament, the invisible power of truth is signified. So, then, beyond the proper mysteries of victims, that is, the proper gifts or ministries, it is not lawful to work anything, either for the perfecters of holy things, that is, for those who administer the grace they have received to others to be perfected, [0996D] or for the perfected themselves, that is, for those who, through the grace received, are perfected unto the perfection of holiness. Nor is it lawful either for these or for those to work anything beyond their sacred ordinations, that is, beyond that which pertains to their sacred ordinations, so that, namely, each one may presume to work that alone which is proved to pertain to the order and office assigned to him.

There follows: « But neither can they subsist otherwise, if they desire his divine brightness, and look toward it sacredly and becomingly, and are reformed, according to the analogy of each of the holy intellects. » As if he should say: Not only is it a debt for them to keep the sacred mysteries and the sacred ordinations, but it is also necessary, [0997A] since they cannot subsist otherwise, namely in that state in which they imitate the divine beauty, unless, by keeping their orders, they proceed according to the divine laws. If, therefore, they desire the divine brightness of their Creator himself, and look toward the receiving of it by imitating and desiring — sacredly by affection, becomingly by habit — and we are reformed unto his likeness by imitating and following it: each of the holy intellects, namely, that is, of the angels, « according to the analogy of each, » that is, according to the manner and measure of his possibility, which each has according to his order, and rank, and property; otherwise they can in no way subsist in that good which they desire, unless they proceed according to the divine laws, [0997B] keeping their ordinations, and executing their proper ministries.

There follows: « Does not, therefore, he who says a hierarchy universally declare a certain sacred disposition, an image of the divine beauty, in the hierarchical orders and knowledges sacrificing the mysteries of its proper illumination, and, as far as is allowed, assimilated unto its proper principle? » Since, he says, it is necessary that every hierarchy desire the divine likeness, nor can it subsist otherwise, unless it persevere in his imitation. Therefore he who says a hierarchy declares a certain sacred disposition, which is an image of the divine beauty, that is, comeliness. A disposition, I say, sacrificing the mysteries, that is, exercising or exhibiting the mystical works, or the divine ministries [0997C] of its proper illumination, that is, according to its proper illumination, in the hierarchical orders and knowledges — as far, namely, as is given to each for the work, according to his order, and rank, and the gift of illuminating grace; and, by working, assimilated, as far as is allowed, that is, as far as is possible for the creature according to its manner and dignity, unto its proper principle, from which also it was made that it might be something, and according to which it was disposed that it might be such. « Does not, therefore, he who says a hierarchy declare a certain sacred disposition universally, » that is, so holding itself in every order and rank of it; because, namely, it is disposed and ordained after the image of the divine beauty in the hierarchical orders and knowledges, and sacrifices [0997D] the mysteries of its proper illumination, so that in all its disposition, and order of dignity, and knowledge of discernment, and imitation of work, it may emulate its principle. He sacrifices the mysteries of his proper illumination who, out of the hidden gift of inspiration, represents good work; he also sacrifices the mysteries of his proper illumination who, transfusing the grace he has received to others, multiplies the talent committed to him; he also sacrifices the mysteries of his proper illumination who administers those things alone which befit his order and office. « For the perfection of each of those who are allotted a hierarchy is this: namely, according to his proper analogy, to ascend into the imitation of God, and to become, most divinely of all [divinissime], as the words say, a fellow-worker of God, and to show forth the divine action in himself, shining, as far as is possible, [0998A] relucent. » For this reason he says: He who says a hierarchy signifies a certain general disposition, imitating the divine beauty in its ordination, because this is the perfection of each order, namely, or person, of all who are allotted a hierarchy: to ascend, namely, according to his proper analogy, that is, manner and measure, into the imitation of God, and to become a fellow-worker of God, as the words say, most divinely of all, that is, than which the words say nothing more divine, or, most divinely of all, to become a fellow-worker of God, that is, than which nothing makes men more divine than, namely, to become a fellow-worker of God, and to show forth in himself the divine action relucent: so that, namely, by being relucent to others, he may transfuse through the example of work that which he first merited to receive through the gift of hidden inspiration. Thus, then, [0998B] the perfection of a hierarchy stands, that those who are purged may purge, and those who are illumined may illumine, and those who are perfected may perfect. « Inasmuch as the order of a hierarchy is thus, namely: that some indeed are purged, but some purge; and some indeed are illumined, but some illumine; and some indeed are perfected, but some perfect: for each one a deiform union in whatever mode. » First they are purged, afterward they are illumined, then they are perfected. For unless purgation preceded, illumination would not follow; and unless there were illumination, consummation would not come. For just as he cannot be illumined who is not purged, so he cannot be consummated who is not illumined; because the knowledge of truth illumines none but the pure, and the perfection of virtue [0998C] approaches none but those illumined by truth. But there are the superior and sublime, who themselves also, approaching the divinity, receive immediately from it both purgation, that they may be pure, and illumination, that they may be clear, and perfection, that they may be holy. And from those in turn, according to the order of the divine disposition, those who follow, and are subject in order, are both purged, and illumined, and perfected. And thus, according to this mode, for each union — that is, order and distribution — in whatever mode the deiform, that is, deiformity or likeness, is: namely, this very being purged, illumined, and perfected, so that in this their mode and measure the individuals may have deiformity and the likeness of God: they who are pure by purgation, illumined by truth, perfected by goodness.
