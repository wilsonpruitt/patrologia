## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

Whence there follows: « Is not, therefore, every order of the hierarchic disposition, according to its proper analogy, that is, » manner, and measure, and order, « led back unto the divine cooperation: doing and perfecting those things through the grace and power given by God, [1001D] which are in the divinity naturally and supernaturally, and, having been » — namely by the divinity — « superessentially wrought by it, were afterward manifested to the hierarchy for the possible imitation of the souls, that is, » of the spirits « that love God? » For unless those goods which are in God by nature descended to these by grace, they would not be like him; and unless they themselves, by acting, obtained those things which he possesses not by acting but by having, they would not be his imitators. Finally, unless those goods had been led forth from his invisible secret unto manifestation, they would in no way have been imitable by the possibility of the creature; nor would there be dignity according to him, unless grace had gone forth from him. Behold what charity does. To those souls alone that love God are the hidden [1002A] divine things said to be made manifest, and possible to imitate. For rational souls perceive the interior and eternal goods through charity alone: those goods, through love, both by tasting that they may understand, and by following that they may apprehend. For unless they loved they would not understand, because they are not understood except when they are loved; and again, unless they loved they would not seek, and unless they sought they would not find, because they are not found if they are not sought. For hence it is written: « Now I will not call you servants, because the servant knows not what his Lord does. But I have called you friends, because all things whatsoever I have heard from my Father, I have made known to you [n: (Joan. XV)]. » And again: « Father, I give thee thanks, because thou hast hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones [n: (Luc. X)]. » Behold, [1002B] therefore, how charity alone reveals the things of God that are hidden, and likewise it too leads unto possibility the things that are ineffable, and superessential, and supernatural to every creature, according to the incomprehensible sublimity of God. « If anyone, he says, loves me, he will keep my word; and my Father will love him, and we will come to him, and will make our abode with him [n: (Joan. XIV)]. » Thus, therefore, every order of the disposition of the hierarchy, the grace and power having been received from God, is perfected through charity alone, by acting and imitating God, so that by action and imitation there may begin to be present in them those things which are in the divinity naturally, since they are from it; and supernaturally, since they are the same with it: naturally, since coeternal; supernaturally, since [1002C] coessential: for that which is always present is natural; but that which is the same is supernatural, since it is nature itself, and it itself is nature.

There follows: « And superessentially wrought by it; and manifested to the hierarchy for the possible imitation of the souls that love God. » What are those things which are supernaturally in the divinity, and were superessentially wrought by it, and finally were manifested to the hierarchy for the possible imitation of the souls that love God. He wishes certain goods to be understood, invisible and ineffable, which were with God, and were in God, and had not yet come into knowledge through participation, so as to be known and possessed except by him alone whose they were, in whom they were: and afterward they were made by him, when it was brought about that they should come to be in [1002D] us; and they were superessentially made, since first they were made by him, for us, so that afterward through him they might come to be in us. Made for us by predestination, that they might come to be in us by reception; made for us when they are bestowed, that they might come to be in us when they are received. Made above us, when they begin to come to us; made in us, when they begin to be had by us. Made unto us, when they descend from him; made in us, when they are bestowed through him. These, therefore, are the things which are supernaturally in the divinity before us, and were superessentially made from it unto us: finally manifested in us through it. « They were manifested, he says, to the hierarchy, » that is, to the sacred disposition which is ordained according to God, and proceeds: and this was done [1003A] « for the possible imitation of the souls that love God, » that is, so that the souls that love God might be able to imitate him; because, unless they had been manifested, they would not be imitable. For unless they were known they would not be sought, and unless they were sought they would not be known. But this is to be diligently attended to, that those goods are said to be manifested not to any single individuals, but to the hierarchy, that is, to the whole; yet in such a way that they are exercised by the individuals [1004A] within the whole by the zeal of imitation, because grace is poured out upon all and works in individuals. Outside unity none can receive it, and, given to another within unity, it can suffice for no one. For this reason they are manifested to the hierarchy, to bring forth the possible imitation of the souls that love God.
