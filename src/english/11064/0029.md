## THE TEXT.

[1003B]

*What a hierarchy is, therefore, having been, as I suppose, well defined by us — the angelic hierarchy — is then to be praised, and its wondrous makings of forms in the words are to be beheld with supermundane eyes, that we may ascend into their most deiform simplicity through the mystical formations. And at the same time we shall praise the principle of all hierarchic knowledge in divine religiosity, and in the most perfect givings of thanks: first of all, at once, to say this true thing — that by universal goodness the superessential divinity, setting up the essences of the things that are unto being, led them forth. For this is proper to the cause of all things, and to the goodness that is above all: to call unto its own communion the things that are, as for each one of the things that are it is defined out of its proper analogy. All things, therefore, [1003C] that are participate the providence flowing from the superessential and most causal divinity. For perhaps they would not be, save by the assumption of the principle of the essences of the things that are. All existing things, therefore, participate its being. For the being of all things is the superbeing of the divinity: but living things participate the same vivific power above all life. Rational and intellectual things participate the same wisdom, above all reason and intellect, perfect through itself, and before perfect. And it is clear that about it are those of the essences which have innumerably received from it. The holy dispositions, therefore, of the heavenly essences, above the things which merely are and live irrationally (according to which we are rational), have been made in the participation of the hierarchic tradition. For, invisibly looking supermundanely [1003D] into the divine likeness, and desiring to form their intellectual appearance, they beautifully have the more copious communions toward it. For they are attentive to all life. They, therefore, are first, and manifoldly, and ever to the utmost, as far as is lawful, intent upon the conformation of the divine and inflexible love, and receiving the principal illuminations immaterially and purely, and ordained unto them, and having all intellectual life. They, therefore, are first, and manifoldly made in the participation of God: and first, and manifoldly the makers-manifest of the divine hiddenness. For this reason also, beyond [1004B] all things, they have been made worthy of the angelic appellation, and by selection: in that they first bring forth into themselves the divine illumination, and through themselves convey to us the manifestations which are above us. Thus indeed, as theology says, they were given to us through angels. And our glorious fathers also, both before the law and after the law, the angels led back unto the divine: introducing what was to be done, and leading them back unto the right way of truth from error and from an unclean life; or revealing prophetically the sacred orders of the supermundane mysteries, or the hidden visions, or certain divine prior proclamations [praedictiones] [n: (Gen. XIX, XXII, XXXI; Judith. VI; Dan. VII; Matth. II; Act. X; Apoc. IV)]*. *But if anyone should say that from thence also there were, immediately, theophanies to certain of the holy ones, let him learn this too wisely from the most sacred [1004C] words: how this indeed is a certain thing — no one sees the hidden of God, nor will see [n: (Num. XII; I Tim. VI)]*. *But theophanies were made to the holy ones, [sed] according to what befits God, and through certain sacred manifestations of proportional visions to those who see. Therefore the most wise theology itself revealed that vision which is described in it to be divine, so that, as it were, a likeness in the form of the formless, from the leading-back of the seers into the divine, is beautifully called a theophany, as though through it divine things were made to the seers by illumination, the divine ones themselves indeed holily perfecting them. But these divine visions our glorious fathers accomplished through the mediating heavenly powers. Does not also the tradition of the words say that the sacred legislation was given, as it were of itself indeed, from God to Moses, so that it may truly teach us also [1004D] that it and the sacred characters are divine? [n: (Deut. IX.)] But theology also wisely teaches that it came unto us through angels, as though the divine legal order legally set that down — that is, that the second things are led back into the divine through the first. For not only in the superposed and subject souls, but also in the equally powerful, is the law itself defined out of the superessential principle of the ordination of all things. This is: through each single hierarchy there are the first, and the middle, and the last, and ordinations, and powers, and the teachers of the least are the more divine, and the guides-by-hand unto the divine leading, and illumination, [1005A] and communication. But I see that the angels first taught the divine mystery of the humanity of Christ, and then through them the grace of knowledge descended unto us [n: (Matth. I; Luc. I, II)]*. *Thus, therefore, the most divine Gabriel taught the mysteries to Zacharias, the high priest [n: (Luc. I)]*, *that is, that from him a prophet should come to be, against hope — a boy to be born divinely by divine grace — and, for the salutary manifestation to the world of the manly divine operation of Jesus. And Mary too, how in her there should be the divine mystery of the ineffable divine formation [n: (ibid.)]*. *But another of the angels was instructing Joseph, how the things divinely promised to the forefather David were truly to be fulfilled [n: (Matth. I; Luc. II)]*. *But another evangelized the shepherds, as those purged, as it were, by the return of the many and by silence, and with him a multitude of the heavenly host [1005B] was delivering to those who are on earth that greatly laudable doxology. And let us look also to the most excellent apparitions of the light of the words. For I see that Jesus himself, the superessential essence of the supercelestial essences, coming unchangeably unto that which is according to us, does not recoil from the human ordination ordained and assumed by himself, but, obedient, is made subject to the dispositions of the Father, and of God, through angels. And through those very mediators there is announced to Joseph the withdrawal of the Son to Egypt, disposed by the Father, and again the leading-across into Judaea out of Egypt [n: (Matth. II)]*. *And through angels we see him ordained under the paternal legislations [n: (Luc. XXII)]*. *For I press on to say (as to one who knows the things expressed in our priestly traditions) also concerning the angel strengthening Jesus [sic: Vesum] himself; or because Jesus himself too, coming through our [1005C] salutary, beneficent, and manifesting ordination, is called the Angel of great counsel. For indeed, as the angel himself said, whatsoever things he heard from the Father, he announced to us [n: (Joan. XV; Isai. IX)]*.
