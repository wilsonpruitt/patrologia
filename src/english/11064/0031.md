## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

This is what he says: « The holy dispositions, therefore, of the heavenly essences, » that is, the holy orders of the heavenly spirits, « have been made by participation in the tradition of the hierarchy, » that is, by participation in the grace which is handed on to the hierarchy ordered according to God, « above those things which only are, » because they live; « and above those things » which « are living irrationally, » because they discern; in comparison with which irrationally living things we men are called, and are, rational animals. Nor is it any wonder, he says, if the angelic spirits have been made superior in dignity not only to existing and non-living things, and to living and non-discerning things, but even to rational and corporeal things; because rational [1009B] corporeal things, that is, men, although they are reformed unto the divine likeness, nevertheless look toward it only when instructed and roused by the mediation of corporeal things. But the angelic sublimity, established above all corporeal things, invisibly and immediately, no other creature set between it and God, is conformed to the divine likeness more copiously, drawing grace from it more manifoldly, and contemplating it more nearly, as being close at hand. « Invisibly, » he says, that is, conforming themselves unto the divine imitation without material and corporeal instruments and visible signs — namely the dispositions themselves of the heavenly essences. « And unto the divine likeness, » through imitation and conformation, « supramundanely, » that is, spiritually, « looking, they have beautifully more copious [1009C] communions with it, » that is, they take more manifold perceptions of spiritual gifts from it, namely from the divine likeness, in which they have communion with it; because, by perceiving and sharing its good in those very spiritual gifts, they possess it in it and with it. Beautifully indeed, and purely, and without corporeal contagion and material contact; being simply taken up into it. « They are attending » indeed « unto all life, » that is, unto the highest life, in which is all life; which is wholly life, not living from elsewhere, but is itself life; and through this, perceiving all life from the highest life, so that, by the very fact that they receive the fullness of life immediately in themselves, in them life may never fail.

[1009D]There follows: « They themselves, therefore, are — firstly, and manifoldly, and always, unto the highest, as far as is lawful — intent upon the conformation of divine and inflexible love; and receiving the principal illuminations immaterially and purely, and ordered unto them, and having all intellectual life. » They themselves, he says, are intent — namely upon the highest good — firstly because immediately, and manifoldly because perfectly, and always because intent without intermission, « namely as far as is lawful, » that is, permitted or possible to a creature so placed, « in the conformation of divine and inflexible love, » that is, of a creature loving God inflexibly or immutably, and turning itself and reforming itself toward God through inflexible and divine love. « They themselves also are, » namely the heavenly powers, « receiving the principal [1010A] illuminations, » that is, those given firstly and principally — immaterially, without body, and purely, without contagion. Immaterially, without visible signification, and purely, without the contagion of error. « They are also ordered unto them, » namely unto the illuminations to be received, so that according to the difference of order there may be a difference of perception; or, ordered unto them, they are by justice subjecting themselves and fitting themselves to the divine will in all things, so that in this very thing they may perceive the illumination of grace without impediment, in that they do not disagree from its truth through desire of iniquity. « They are » also « having all intellectual life, » because the very thing that they are, they are life, and their spiritual substances have this very same thing — to live — that they have to be. Because, therefore, in them there is not one thing which gives life [1010B] and another which is given life, but the whole is one and the same; they are said to have all life, or to be all life, because the whole of what they are, they are life — just as the highest life itself also, in which they are and in which they live, is all life; because it lives of itself, and is life, and the whole of what it is, is life.

There follows: « They themselves, therefore, have been made — firstly, and manifoldly — in the participation of God, and are — firstly, and manifoldly — the manifesters of the divine hiddenness. » They themselves, he says, the heavenly dispositions, « have been made in the participation of God, » that is, so that they may participate in God and in the divine grace; firstly, because no creature is before them; « and manifoldly, » because no creature is above them. « Firstly, » because before all; « and manifoldly, » because more than all. And they are « manifesters of the divine hiddenness, » [1010C] that is, of the divine grace invisibly and secretly breathed into them, while that which they inwardly perceive from a hidden inspiration they afterward transfuse to others by manifesting it. Or « of the divine hiddenness, » that is, they are manifesters of the hidden and invisible divinity; because in them and through them the invisible things of God come forth unto manifestation, when the divine brightness both first shines in them and afterward, through them, illumines the subject orders of those provided for. Therefore, he says, « they have been made worthy, by selection, » that is, specially or singularly, « beyond all things, of the angelic appellation, in that they first put forth into themselves the divine illumination, and through themselves bring down to us the manifestations which are above us. » Therefore, because the divine light pours itself into [1010D] them in the first place, and afterward descends through them to illumine us, they have been made worthy, beyond all things, of the angelic appellation. For 'angel' is interpreted *messenger*. Those, therefore, who bring down the grace they have received by ministering it to others, what else are they than messengers of the author and bestower of that same grace? But there are messengers — some prior, some posterior. For the angels, who, perceiving the divine grace in the first place, afterward bring it down to the knowledge of men, are, as it were, the first messengers of that same grace. But men themselves, when they bring down the grace they have received to others by preaching and announcing it, are indeed called messengers, but are not the first messengers, because they have perceived what they announce from others first illumined and first announcing. Therefore those heavenly and invisible [1011A] spirits are named angels both by a singular dignity and by a proper appellation; because to them first, through hidden inspiration, is manifested that which they behold in themselves of the invisible light of the divinity, and through them in the first place is transfused to us that which they themselves carry to be manifested to our knowledge. For in the first place, as though giving birth out of the hidden womb of divine conception, they put forth into themselves the divine illumination — not by drawing it from without, but by conceiving from within the light of eternal brightness — so that there may come forth from within, to themselves, that which they see in themselves, and that they may transfuse to us through themselves. For thus, by first perceiving the divine illuminations in themselves through themselves, they afterward bring down to us the manifestations of those very illuminations, which are above us. For « thus indeed the manifestations, » namely the divine ones, « have been given [1011B] to us through the angels, as theology, » that is, Holy Scripture, « testifies. And the angels led back also our glorious fathers, both before the law and after the law, unto the divine, introducing that which was to be done, and leading them back from error and unclean life unto the right way of truth; either revealing the sacred orders of the supramundane mysteries, or the hidden visions, or certain divine fore-preachings prophetically. » Thus, he says, as theology, that is, Holy Scripture, testifies and manifests, the divine manifestations have been given to us through the angels; because both before the law, namely before it was given, and after the law was given, that is, under law and under grace, the angels led back our glorious fathers, that is, the elect and just, by imitating whose life we are sons, unto the divine, that is, [1011C] unto the divine knowledge: introducing, namely, that which was to be done, that is, leading them to live justly and piously, and leading them back from error and unclean life unto the right way of truth, that is, teaching them to believe rightly, so that both their faith might be right and their life clean. Or also revealing the sacred orders of the supramundane mysteries, that is, of the heavenly secrets — namely concerning the blessed life, and the joys of the heavenly fatherland, and the invisible ordination of the blessed spirits: how all are happy in the one good, yet not co-equal; and how the superior are set above without pride, the inferior subjected without misery; or revealing the sacred orders of the supramundane mysteries, that is, of the divine secrets: namely how it must be fulfilled visibly [1011D] and temporally by men, which God disposed to be done by an invisible and secret dispensation; or also revealing certain hidden visions, signifying something concerning things past, or present, or future; or revealing certain divine fore-preachings made prophetically, that is, divine preachings in which something, before it came to pass, was prophetically foretold — ministering, namely, to those who spoke them the knowledge of things to come, or to those who heard them the understanding of what was said, and in all these things, by instructing holy men either unto right faith or unto good working, leading them back unto divine knowledge and participation.

There follows: « But if anyone should say that there have also been theophanies to certain persons immediately [1012A] from thence, let him learn this too wisely from the most sacred words: how this indeed is a certain thing: No one has seen, nor will see, the hidden thing of God. » If anyone, he says, against what I have said — that divine revelations come to the knowledge of men through the angels — should say that sometimes, without the angels mediating, certain of the holy Fathers received revelations from the divinity itself, let him learn from the most sacred words this: that the hidden thing of God itself no one has seen, nor will see. If, then, anyone — that is, some person — should say that from thence, that is, from the divine secret, theophanies were made to certain of the holy ones, that is, divine apparitions immediately, that is, descending into them in the first place with no other creature mediating; if anyone, I say, should say this, let him [1012B] also learn to understand this wisely from the most sacred words: how « no one » ever « has seen, nor will see, the hidden thing of God, » that is, the divine nature, which is hidden and removed from every sense. He has seen, I say — namely what this very hidden thing is; that is: even if he has seen it in a figure, he has not seen it in essence; he has not seen it in its proper form, even if he has seen it in signification. And when he has come to know this from the most sacred words, let him understand that the human mind cannot by itself approach immediately unto the contemplation of the inaccessible light, unless, roused by theophanies, that is, by divine apparitions, it be lifted up. « But theophanies have been made to the holy ones [but] according to what befits God: and through certain sacred manifestations of proportional visions to those who see. » Theophanies, he says, that [1012C] is, divine revelations, or revelations of the divinity, have been made to the holy ones — yet not in this manner, that the human mind, placed in mortal flesh and established within human sense, should approach immediately to touch the incomprehensible nature; but they were made according to certain sacred manifestations, whether of visible forms applied outwardly to the sense, or of imaginations brought inwardly into the mind according to visible things, or impressed spiritually upon human reason in some other, higher and more excellent way whatsoever — fitted with such suitability that they both befitted God himself, concerning whom they were made, and were congruous to those very seers to whom they were made, according to the proportion and capacity of each.
