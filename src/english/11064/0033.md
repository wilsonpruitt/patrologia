## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

« For I see, he says, that Jesus himself too, coming immutably — in the superessential essence of the supercelestial essences — unto that which is according to us, does not recoil from the human ordination ordained and assumed by himself. » Jesus himself, he says, who according to his divinity, by which all things subsist, is also the essence of the supercelestial essences, that is, of the angelic spirits, who transcend not only earthly things by purity, but also [1016C] the heavenly things by subtlety; and not only their essence, because in him they subsist, but the superessential essence, because they do not attain to his equality. Jesus himself, whose ineffable majesty and power of divinity both bears up the angels themselves through grace and transcends them through nature, coming unto that which is according to us, that is, unto the taking-on of flesh, which was of our nature and was like unto us; and coming immutably, that is, without diminution or change of his divinity, does not recoil from the human ordination, that is, from the ordination or law owed to humanity. An ordination, I say, first ordained by him, when he earlier instructed man; afterward assumed by him, when he took on the form and nature of humanity. But if he had assumed the human [1016D] nature and yet spurned the human condition, he would recoil from the human ordination. For it would be, as it were, a recoiling for him to be unwilling to endure by condition that which he had assumed by nature. But now, coming unto the human nature through the assumption of flesh, he does not recoil from the human ordination, by the keeping of humility. « But, obedient, he is made subject to the dispositions of God the Father through the angels, » administered about his humanity: « and through those very » angels as intermediaries, that is, by the mediation of the angels themselves, « there is announced to Joseph, » as to the guardian of his infancy, « the withdrawal » of the « Son » of God himself « into Egypt, » disposed by God the Father, « and again the leading-across, or leading-back, from Egypt into Judea. » « But we see him too, he says, [1017A] ordained through the angels under the paternal legislations. » We see him ordained through the angels, that is, disposed, « under the paternal legislations, » that is, under the legal commandments, which commandments those whom he had as fathers and forefathers according to the flesh had received to be observed. Under these legal commandments, therefore, he was ordained through the angels, when, according to the precepts of the paternal law, he was both circumcised on the eighth day, and on the fortieth day presented in the temple with gifts and victims — the Lord of the angels observing the law given through the angels.

There follows: « For I am urgent to say, as to you who know the things expressed in our priestly traditions, also concerning the angel comforting Jesus himself. » It is a small thing, he says, that I say, that Jesus in his humanity was ordained by the angels under the paternal [1017B] laws: he who, in the same humanity, drawing near to the passion, was even comforted by an angel. I am urgent to say what perhaps, if it had not been said before, would not be credible at all. I am urgent to say, also concerning the angel comforting Jesus himself. I am urgent to say what I myself too cannot utter without wonder — what I certainly believe and pursue: which, if I were about to say it to one who did not know, I would not presume to say at all. I am urgent to say what is so great that it cannot be comprehended by the little in knowledge; so wondrous that it cannot be believed by the little in faith — concerning the angel comforting him. I am urgent to say what, if it were thought to have been said by me, would not be believed. But to you, who know the things which are expressed, that is, manifestly set forth by our priestly traditions, that is, by the Scriptures, [1017C] which were handed on to us by priestly men who minister sacred things and are sanctified, to you I boldly am urgent to say the truth, hesitating nothing about your faith, which is firm and admits no scandal, having discernment of what was done, what was said. To you, therefore, O Timothy, son, as to one who knows the things expressed in our priestly traditions, to you confidently « I am urgent to say, also concerning the angel comforting Jesus, » so that in this it may become plain without doubt how greatly the humanity [1018A] was subjected, according to the divine ordination, to the angelic dispensation — the humanity which, in the man, at the time of the passion, willed to be comforted by an angel; yet not seeking aid, but forming an example; not having necessity, and nevertheless showing the condition. Therefore, then, « I am urgent » to « say to you, also concerning the angel comforting Jesus himself. » Or also I « am urgent » to « say this to you: that Jesus himself, coming through our salvific, beneficent, and manifestative ordination, is called the angel of great counsel. » This is that Jesus himself is called « the angel of great counsel, coming, » that is, in that he came to us, through our ordination, that is, according to what he had ordained, or what had been ordained — to come in our nature: which ordination was salvific, and beneficent, and manifestative. [1018B] Salvific in redemption, beneficent in the bestowal of grace, manifestative in glorification. Salvific, because he redeemed from death; beneficent, because to the redeemed he conferred the gifts of graces unto justification; manifestative, because he first set forth God, in his humanity, to be seen visibly by those to be justified, and afterward leads the justified, through the vision of his humanity, unto the contemplation of the divinity. According to this « manifestative ordination, therefore, Jesus, coming, is called the angel of great counsel. » Because, « as the angel himself says, whatsoever he heard from the Father, he announced to us. » If, then, an angel is called a messenger, deservedly the Savior himself too is called a messenger. Who announces to us the will of God the Father, and reveals the inward goods to our minds by breathing them in through his Spirit. In [1018C] which the angelic dignity is also manifestly shown: that the Savior himself too, bringing down to us the commands of the Father, is surnamed an angel. For since the sum of our salvation through redemption is accomplished not without the angelic appellation, it is clear that the rest too, which pertain to that same salvation, are dispensed by angelic administration. And these things indeed have been said concerning the angelic appellation.

## TITLE OF CHAPTER V *Why all the heavenly essences are commonly called angels, and specially have their own proper appellations, except those of the first and second orders.*



## THE TEXT.

[1017D]

*This indeed is, as far as concerns us, the cause of the angelic surname in the oracles. But it is fitting, as I think, to search out for what cause the theologians call all the heavenly essences at once angels, yet, coming to the manifestation of the supramundane dispositions themselves, they specially name the angelic order, which completively terminates the divine and heavenly things; but before it they set in order, placed above, the archangelic adornments, and the princes, and the powers, and the virtues, and whatever essences the manifestative traditions of the oracles know to be established above these. But we say that, throughout every holy disposition, the excelling orders have the illuminations and virtues of the inferior dispositions, but they that do not participate [1018D] in the things that excel them are last. Therefore the theologians call the most holy orders of the most excellent essences angels; for these too are manifesters of the divine illumination [n: (Hebr. I)]*. *But the lowest order of the heavenly minds have no ground for being named principalities, or thrones, or seraphim. For it is not in the participation of the most lofty virtues. But just as he himself leads back our divine high priests to the brightnesses of the divinity known to him, so also the wholly sacred virtues of the essences [still unclean] altogether lead back that which is before it, and they are ordered unto the divine, unto the disposition that consummates the angelic hierarchies. Unless indeed someone should say this too: that all the angelic namings are common, according to the subjection of all the heavenly virtues unto the deiform and God-given light, and their supereminent [1019A] communication. But that the matter may be the more discerned by us, we shall consider the holy and comely properties of each heavenly disposition, expressed in the sacred oracles.*
