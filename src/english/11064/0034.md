## EXPOSITION.

« This indeed is, » etc. In this fifth chapter it is inquired why all the heavenly essences are surnamed angels. But this question seems to have arisen from this: that in the sacred word certain of the heavenly spirits are declared always to stand by God and always to see his face; but certain others are said to be sent forth outward, to fulfill exterior ministries. For hence it seems to follow that those who always stand by the divine countenance do not go forth to announce exterior things; but those [1019B] who are sent forth do not stand by the divine sight immovably, or immutably. For it is written in the book of the prophet Daniel: « Thousands of thousands ministered to him, and ten thousand times a hundred thousand stood by him [n: (Dan. VII)]. » In which distinction, by those who stand by seem to be signified those who do not go forth to exterior things; but by those who minister, those who, by going forth to exterior things, do not always stand by. On which account, because the inferior and lowest orders together go forth to exterior things, while only the superior and more excellent stand by the divine contemplation without intermission, those who stand by are declared to be fewer, but those who minister more. For since there are nine orders of angels, only two, that is, angels and archangels — inasmuch as they specially, from the distribution of their office, [1019C] are appointed to be sent — have received their surname from the very thing. Angels, namely, are called messengers, but archangels chief messengers; since the former announce lesser things, the latter greater, as it were from the dignity of their ministry. But because again, in Holy Scripture, we read that certain ones of the superior orders were sent — as in Isaiah one of the seraphim is recorded to have flown to the prophet and to have touched his lips with a coal which he had taken with tongs from the altar [n: (Isa. VI)]; and in the Epistle to the Hebrews the Apostle testifies that all are ministering spirits, and sent into ministry, for the sake of those who receive the inheritance of salvation [n: (Hebr. I)]: a great difficulty of ambiguity arises for us, and what is rather to be asserted here is not easily found. The author solves this question in this manner. He testifies that the name of angels belongs [1019D] only to the inferior orders, and to those which, from their office, are properly appointed to announce exterior things. But because the divine secrets, which by those inferior ones are brought down outwardly to the knowledge of men, are announced to those same ones by the superior: the name of angels too is communicable to the superior: who, although they do not go forth to announce exterior things, nevertheless — themselves receiving from above the things which are to be announced outwardly, according to the law of the divine ordination, and by announcing carrying them across to the inferior orders that follow after them — and the superior participate in every virtue, and grace, and property of the inferior: and therefore the surnames of the inferior also pass over to the superior; but the virtue, and grace, and [1020A] property of the superior are not all communicable to the inferior, and therefore their surnames too, by a like reasoning, cannot universally be brought down to the inferior. Yet sometimes the inferior, when they take up the property of the superior from the quality of an office, assume their name too in the execution of that same office. Hence is that which we commemorated above, that the angel who had come to kindle and purge the lips of the prophet is called a seraph, because in the quality of this work he was carrying out the property of the one who kindles or inflames. But that which the Apostle says, that all are ministering spirits and sent into ministry, they think is to be understood in this way: that, as we said above, those too are not unfittingly said to be sent; [1020B] because, although they do not go forth to exterior things, nevertheless they bring the things which are to be announced outwardly, by announcing them to the inferior and following orders. Others think that all the heavenly orders, both superior and inferior, are directed to exterior things according to time, and place, and cause; yet that those which have this as proper from their office are specially surnamed angels or archangels. For that all are sometimes sent, the Apostle seems to assert by testimony, in that which we commemorated above, saying: « Are they not all ministering spirits, sent into ministry, for the sake of those who receive the inheritance of salvation? » But that even certain superior ones are sometimes directed to exterior things not from office, but from an accidental cause, the Psalmist seems to assert, when he says: « Who [1020C] makes his angels spirits, and his ministers a burning fire [n: (Psal. CIII)]." Thus, then, there are some who stand by from office; and others who go forth to fulfill exterior things; and yet those who stand by, being sometimes sent into ministry, while they take up the property of the inferior, assume their name too in signification. Who, nevertheless, when they go forth to exterior things, do not withdraw from the interior contemplation, because they behold him who is present to them wherever they go. And in this way indeed, before we should examine the words of the book, we thought that mention of a question of this kind should be made briefly, for the clearness of the things to be said — defining nothing rashly, but venerating the secret, of which perhaps to have been ignorant is a venial infirmity, [1020D] but to assert presumptuously is a damnable rashness. This, however, must be known: that the author seems rather to lend his assent to that part which holds that only certain, not all, of the heavenly orders are sent to exterior things; and yet that, on account of the participation of the same property, by which the superior share the grace and virtue of the inferior, the names of the inferior too are assumed by the superior. Just as also sometimes the inferior, when they take up the property of the superior from the quality of an office, assume their name too in signification. Now to the text: « This indeed is, as far as concerns us, the cause of the angelic surname in the words. » « This, he says, is » — which we have said above — « the cause of the angelic surname; » namely because the divine secrets are announced to us through them, [1021A] and therefore, because they bring down to us the divine precepts by announcing them, as far as concerns us — that is, as far as regards that, or on account of that, which they work toward us — they assume the name of angels. But because this — that is, to carry the messages of the interior teacher outward to the knowledge of men — whether it befits all is not yet proved by manifest reason or authority, it must now be inquired, he says, why in the sacred word he attributes the name of angels commonly to all the heavenly virtues.

This is what he says: « It is fitting, as I think, to search out for what cause the theologians call all the heavenly essences at once angels; but, uniting unto the manifestation, they specially name the angelic order of the supramundane dispositions themselves, [1021B] which completively terminates the divine and heavenly things; » as if he should say: This now remains to be inquired: why the theologians universally name all those heavenly natures angels, when nevertheless they specially name one order of them — namely of the spirits who come to us to manifest the supramundane dispositions themselves, that is, the spiritual, and divine, and invisible ordinations — the angelic order; « completively terminating the divine and heavenly things, » that is, leading through, unto completion and end, the things divinely disposed in a heavenly and secret ordination. For the divine precept, running down from the highest, is brought through the superior orders to the inferior, until at last, coming to the knowledge of men, it is completed in work: on which account [1021C] that heavenly order too, to which the command of the divinity descends last of all through the powers placed above, and then through it is announced outwardly, is said to terminate it completively, or unto completion, because through its announcement it is brought to that place where it is last to be completed by visible operation. This, therefore, must be inquired: why, namely, although this last order is specially marked with the angelic surname, all the orders of the heavenly essences are named angels. « But before it, those placed above set in order the archangelic adornments, and the princes, and the powers, and the virtues, and whatever essences established above these the manifestative traditions of the words know. » As if he should say: The theologians indeed establish the angelic order itself [1021D] as the last and latest, and nearest to human things, and as carrying forth the divine visions by a final announcement, and determining them; but before it they set in order those placed above, that is, they describe those placed above in the order. « The archangelic adornments, » that is, the choirs of the archangels, ordered and beautifully disposed, who in order are both superior to the angels themselves in dignity, and prior in announcement. Then the theologians themselves also establish — not only above the angels, but even above the archangels — the princes, that is, the principalities, and the powers, and the virtues, and other essences, that is, « whatever spiritual natures » the manifestative traditions of the sacred words « know, » that is, set forth to knowledge. Essences, I say, established above these, namely [1022A] the angels, or whatever others established above all these aforesaid, so that they are not only more sublime in dignity, but also stronger in firmness of perfection, and more stable unto eternity and immutability.

There follows: « But we say that, throughout every holy disposition, the excelling orders have the illuminations and virtues of the inferior dispositions; but they that do not participate in the things that excel them are last. » As if he should say: Since in that disposition of the heavenly spirits some are superior, others inferior, the superior indeed have every illumination and virtue of the inferior; but the inferior do not have all the illuminations and virtues of the superior orders. And therefore the names of the inferior [1022B] are sometimes transferred to the superior orders, inasmuch as these participate with them in the same properties of names. But the names of the superior cannot be so assumed by the inferior, since the properties of the names do not universally pass over to them: this is what he says: « But we say that, throughout every holy disposition » — namely of the heavenly spirits — « the excelling, » or superior, « orders have » all the illuminations and virtues « of the inferior dispositions, » that is, of the subject orders. But the last, that is, the inferior, and those following in order, do not participate — "in all," it is understood, the illuminations and virtues of those that excel them, that is, of those who are more excellent than they. We understand the illuminations [1022C] as being in the knowledge of truth; but the virtues as being in the love of goodness and the perfection of work.
