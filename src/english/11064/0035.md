## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « Therefore the theologians call the most holy orders of the most excellent essences angels. For they too are manifesters of the divine illumination. » Since he says that the higher orders have all the properties of the lower ones: therefore the theologians call the most holy orders of the most excellent essences — that is, of the most excellent spirits — angels; because they too partake of the angelic property, in this respect, that although they do not go out to announce outward things, nevertheless to those orders which follow after them they manifest, by transfusing and as it were by announcing, the divine illumination which they receive concerning themselves. « But the lowest order of the heavenly minds does not have [1022D] the ground for being named archas, or thrones, or seraphim. » — « They do not have, he says, the ground, » those same theologians, « for naming the lowest order of the heavenly minds, » that is, the last order of the heavenly spirits, namely the angels, « archas, » that is princes, or thrones, or seraphim, because that lowest order of the angels is not in participation of the most exalted powers. For this reason the angels cannot be named princes, or thrones, or seraphim, as princes and thrones and seraphim are named angels; because the higher ones partake of the angelic property universally, whereas the angels do not partake universally of the illuminations and powers of the higher ones; and those very ones especially, for which the higher ones received their special surnames, the angels possess only in part [1023A], and yet they do not have the dignity and the property of that surname. For not everyone who has some power or property by partaking of it ought at once to be surnamed according to that property, unless he holds it either singularly among the rest or excellently above the rest. For not everyone who knows something is at once called wise; nor is he who has done something right forthwith called just. But he alone who holds wisdom and justice either singularly or excellently ought to be called wise and just by a proper and express surname. So then those heavenly orders of the holy spirits have proper surnames, in which is signified not what they have received singularly, but what they possess excellently. For the seraphim [1023B] — because out of love of their Creator, as neighbors and those nearest, they both are burning in themselves and set others alight from themselves — are interpreted *burning* or *kindling*, not because they alone among the rest have this singularly, but because with the rest, and above the rest, excellently. For all burn with the love of God, and yet they especially ought to be called burning, who both first conceive the fire of that love and, burning more strongly, send out the flame of love to set the rest also alight. So too the cherubim (which name is interpreted *fullness of knowledge*) — because they have a greater knowledge of God than the rest — take their name from that alone which, possessing it with the rest, they merited to perceive more excellently than all the rest. The thrones too are so called, not because in them alone does God sit and discern [1023C] his judgments, but because they received this in their office more excellently than the rest, and on that account they draw their surname specially from it. And in this manner indeed the other orders too must be understood, namely, that each is believed to receive its proper surnames from that which it is proved to possess more excellently by the gift of grace.

But there arises a question not to be despised, and one which would bring great confusion to the mind if it were not examined by applying reason. For if, as has been said, in that heavenly arrangement each and every order draws its own surnames from that property in which they are proved to be more excellent than the rest, since the higher ones possess universally and excellently all the illuminations and [1023D] powers of the subject orders, nothing singular is left to the orders placed beneath and following, from which they might obtain a surname proper to them and distinct among the rest. Whence it behooves us to consider more diligently in what way both are at once true: namely, that the higher ones possess universally and excellently the illuminations and gifts of the subject orders, and yet that each and every one retains something proper and special from which it can be marked by a proper and distinct appellation. For the seraphim by their name express a singular love. The cherubim, however, intimate a more excellent knowledge. But the thrones show a greater force of discernment. Yet it is established that those who love more ardently look more deeply, and discern more subtly. [1024A] For those who look from nearer at hand undoubtedly recognize more evidently. How then is the order of the cherubim said to be surnamed from the privilege of a singular grace, if another retains this more excellently in its office, from which the cherubim obtains its appellation? But this question is perhaps not unfittingly solved by this reasoning: yet only so, if the secret of truth be violated by no rashness of prejudgment. For it can come to pass that, although the higher orders possess more excellently all the gifts of the powers, nevertheless they draw their surname only from those which among the gifts of the powers themselves are the more sublime; and they leave the others, which follow in the order of dignity, to those following after them for their denomination, so that the first may obtain its name from the first, the second from the second, the third from the third. For we know, [1024B] Scripture bearing witness, that among all the gifts of the powers charity excels; wherefore it was consequent that that order which is the most eminent of all should take its singular appellation, in its own distinction, from charity alone, although it might possess the other gifts of the powers too more excellently, from which it could have taken its appellation and name. But because the knowledge of truth is recognized as next in dignity after the love of virtue: therefore from that same dignity those who are second are, after the first, deservedly surnamed angelic spirits. But the judgment of discerning truth, because it seems to have a verdict still as it were doubtful, and appears in a certain manner suspended by a less perfect knowledge; and therefore because it, after full contemplation, in which truth is not sought but [1024C] possessed, looks back to that same knowledge of the highest truth, gave its name to the third order after the two former: this, however, must be understood according to man, for whose sake the names were given to those spirits themselves. For there judgment is not the settling of ambiguity, but the discernment of truth; nor there, where all things are manifest, is something that lies hidden discussed, but what is certain is esteemed according to its merit. Wherefore in this judgment too we hold that the knowledge of truth is to be preferred, since wisdom judges simplicity and unity; but judgment proves itself, through the force of discernment, to look toward diverse and contrary things. Whence the more excellent surname was to be taken from wisdom itself, by which the more excellent order was to be designated [1024D]: which order, although, as more sublime and more perfect, it possessed both wisdom and judgment more fully, yet, taking its name from wisdom alone, left judgment as the surname of the order following after it. According to this consideration, therefore, whoever should wish to interpret the surnames of the angels, there will perhaps be no unfittingness, if the higher orders possess more perfectly all the gifts of the powers, and yet the lower orders have their proper surnames from certain gifts specially. But concerning the orders of the angels themselves, in the first and the last at least, the same opinion of all is established. For that the seraphim are placed in the highest place, and after them the cherubim, and then the thrones, can be unknown to no one who knows the testimonies of the holy Scriptures. [1025A] It is manifest too that, for the lower ones ascending from the bottom upward, the angels and archangels are placed first. The four following orders certain men arrange in this manner, that from the thrones downward they set first the dominations, then the principalities, then the powers, then the virtues, so that in this manner the nine orders are distinguished by three triads: of which the first and highest contains the seraphim, cherubim, thrones; the second and middle comprises the dominations, principalities, and powers; the third and lowest arranges together the virtues, archangels, and angels. And so from the bottom upward the angels are numbered first; then the archangels, then the virtues, then the powers, then the principalities, then the dominations, then the thrones, then the cherubim, then the seraphim. But the Theologian [1025B] places first the angels, then the archangels, then the principalities, and these he assigns to the first triad. But in the second he sets first the powers, then the virtues, then the dominations. And in the third he judges that first the thrones, afterward the cherubim, afterward the seraphim are to be placed, the progression being made from the lower to the higher. But in this threefold triple distinction it especially behooves us to consider this: that the highest three orders, that is, the seraphim, and the cherubim, and the thrones, by the power of their singular excellence and the force of their denomination, seem to look only toward the interior. For to love and to know and to judge is proper to those presiding within, and having their conversion toward the interior. But the last three, and lowest orders, by the property of their surname [1025C], are proved to be arranged toward outward things only according to the office of their ministry; whether the angels and archangels, in that they announce outwardly whatever is to be done and manifested to men; or the principalities, in that they dispense by invisible power whatever things are to be administered and arranged concerning men. But the middle orders, as in arrangement, so also in office, seem to be borne between the invisible and the visible things; and to administer, according to their dignity and office, the things which are to be brought down from the higher ones to the lower. And among these the dominations are first, which by a singular excellence form by command alone the invisible announcement in the virtues; but the virtues are second, which, executing the enjoined command, publish it in [1025D] the powers. But the powers are third, which perfect the mandate conceived in the principalities, archangels, and angels, subjected to them for the operation. But those who set the virtues after the angels and archangels understand thereby, of course, those spirits through whom signs and miracles are more frequently done. In this too let the property of the last arrangement be preserved, whose ministry we hold to have been ordained for dispensing outward things alone. But the powers, they say, are those spirits so called who have the adverse virtues subjected to them, and freely restrain them according to the power given, lest they be able to harm as much as they will. But the principalities, they hold, are those spirits so named who are set over even the good spirits of the angels themselves: to whom, while they command whatever is to be done, [1026A] they act as princes over their subjects for the fulfilling of the divine ministries, and stand as superiors. But the dominations are those so called who surpass even the principalities by a more excellent power, so that they have as subjects even those who merited to command others for the fulfilling of the ministry. These things briefly concerning the orders of the angels, and their names, we thought had to be tasted beforehand as necessary for the coming narration, so that the reader, once these things are said, may take up the several matters — as reason and cause shall demand, to be recalled in what follows — that concern these things, and perhaps, without these, the obscure things that are to be said. Now let us return to explaining the sequence of the text itself. Above he said that the theologians do not have the ground for naming the angelic order archas, or thrones, or seraphim; because that angelic order [1026B] is not in participation of those most exalted powers, so as to partake with them in name, when it does not partake in the property of the name. Now he proves this same thing by the following words, namely, that the angelic order does not partake of the most exalted powers. It does not, he says, partake with them; but what it partakes, it partakes from them. For it is one thing to partake in the fullness, and another to receive participation from the fullness. And so the angelic order is subjected to the most exalted powers in such a way that it does not partake with them, but from them; because, just as it, by illuminating and instructing, leads back those men who are led back to divine knowledge, so also it receives divine illumination from those powers which are before it.
