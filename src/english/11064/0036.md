## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

[1026C]This is what he says: « As it, » namely the angelic order, « leads back our divine highest priests, » that is, the holy men who received the sacred divine things immediately from the angels and handed them on to us, « to the splendors of the divinity known to it, » that is, the divine knowledge which it received, and, having received, bestowed, « so also it is led back by the virtues of the essences which are before it, » that is, before it, or above it, « the virtues, I say, pure, » that is, exceedingly sacred, or wholly sacred, so that by the excellence of holiness they can be a form of perfection to those subject to them. And these same « virtues are also sacred toward the divine, » namely, toward what is to be conferred; that is, toward the knowledge of divinity to be given, « to the arrangement consummating the angelic hierarchies, » [1026D] that is, to the arrangement which perfects, or contains as perfected, or terminates the angelic hierarchies; this is, namely, the last arrangement, in which both the angelic order, in that it is perfected, is consummated, and, in that it is last, the hierarchies of all spirits are terminated. Indeed, here the execrable error of the interpreter must be guarded against, who supposed that the celestial virtues are called unclean. For the Greek πανιερὸν panieron, which is interpreted *exceedingly sacred,* or *wholly sacred,* or *universally sacred,* he thought was here to be understood as ἀνιερὸν anieron, that is, *unsacred,* or *not sacred,* or *without the sacred.*

There follows: « Unless indeed someone should also say this: that all the angelic namings are common, according to the subjection of all the celestial virtues to the deiform, and [1027A] God-given light, and their supereminent communication. » The lower orders cannot, he says, take up the names of the higher, because they do not partake in the same excellence of virtues with them, unless one should wish to say that, for this reason, all the namings of the angels ought to be common, because all alike, as subjects, perceive the one light given by God, and from that same perceived light all are made conformed to God, and exist as partakers of one supereminent communication in one light. Unless, he says, someone should also say this: that all the angelic surnames are common, according to the subjection of all the celestial virtues into the light, or toward the deiform and God-given light to be perceived, and according to the supereminent communication of spiritual or special grace: so that, just as one [1028A] grace diffuses itself for the participation of all, so one surname may reasonably be attributed to all. « But, that the reason may be more judged by us, we shall consider the holy and comely properties, expressed in the sacred oracles, of each celestial arrangement. » That we may be more able, he says, to judge this reason — namely, whether the angelic surnames ought to be common or not — we shall consider the holy and comely properties of each celestial arrangement, expressed in the sacred oracles. For from the properties of each, diligently considered, it will perhaps be able to be recognized whether the communication of the naming can be the same: to whom, even if one grace is infused, yet it is not given in one measure and proportion.

## TITLE OF CHAPTER VI. *What is the first arrangement of the celestial essences: what the middle, and what the last.*



## THE TEXT.

[1027B]

*How many indeed and of what sort are the adornments of the supercelestial essences, and how according to them the Hierarchies are perfected, I say that she alone diligently knows, the contemplative sovereign of their perfection; and moreover that they themselves are ignorant of their own proper virtues and illuminations, and of their own sacred and super-adorned ordination. For it is impossible for us to know the ministries of the supercelestial minds, and their most holy perfections, unless one should tell to himself whatever mysteries the divinity taught us through them, as through those who well know their own. Not therefore do we ourselves say anything of our own motion. But whatever of the angelic contemplations have been contemplated by the holy theologians, these, teaching us, as much as we are able, we shall expound. All theology [1027C] called the celestial essences by nine manifesting surnames [n: (Colos. I)]*. *These our divine holy perfecter separates into three ternary arrangements. And the first indeed he says to be that existing about God always, and attentively united to him, and, before the others, immediately — so it has been handed down. For the most holy thrones, and the eyed and winged orders, named cherubim in the voice of the Hebrews, and seraphim, according to a nearness set above all things, are placed immediately about God, he says, as the manifestation of the divine oracles hands down. This threefold adornment therefore, as one and equally ordered and truly the first hierarchy, our common master calls it: than which [n: (Ephes. III; Coloss. I)] there is no other more deiform, and, of itself, more immediately intent upon the pre-operating illuminations of the divinity. [1027D] But the second he says to be completed from the powers, and dominations, and virtues. And the arrangement, last of the celestial hierarchies, of the threefold company of the angels, archangels, and principalities [n: (I Thess. IV)].*
