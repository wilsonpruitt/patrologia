## EXPOSITION

The title of the sixth chapter is: « What is the first arrangement of the celestial essences, what the middle, and what [1028B] the last. » For in this chapter he treats of the threefold arrangement of the nine orders: of which the first, which is also the highest, contains three orders, the seraphim, cherubim, and thrones; the second, which is also the middle, likewise three, the dominations, virtues, and powers; the third, which is also the last, likewise three, the principalities, archangels, and angels: in which the arrangement of the nine orders is consummated. But being about to treat of things so sublime and remote from human sense, he first modestly confesses his own ignorance, showing that those celestial secrets are not only unknown to men, but cannot be wholly comprehended perfectly, as they are, even by the angelic spirits themselves, and that the divine power alone, from which they are, perfectly knows what they are.

[1028C]This is what he says: « How many indeed, and of what sort, are the adornments of the supercelestial essences, and how according to them the hierarchies are perfected: I say that she alone diligently knows, the contemplative sovereign of their perfection, » that is, the divine wisdom alone, which they have both as principle that they may be, and toward which they look through contemplation that they may be perfect. That divine wisdom therefore, he says, I say alone knows how many and of what sort are the adornments of the supercelestial essences, that is, of the invisible natures: because, of course, not even they themselves can perfectly comprehend of what sort they were created by God. For this reason, he says, I say moreover that they too are ignorant of their own proper virtues, and illuminations, and their own sacred and super-adorned ordination. [1028D] Therefore the divine wisdom alone, which made them, perfectly comprehends both of what sort it made them and how it arranged them, so that in this it may be openly shown how much the immensity of the Creator transcends all the possibility of the creature, since the creature does not at all suffice even to comprehend itself.

There follows: « For it is impossible for us to know the ministries of the supercelestial minds, and their most holy [1029A] perfections. » For it is not greatly to be wondered at, if we cannot know this concerning them, which they themselves are unable to comprehend concerning themselves, since we can know even that which we know concerning them only through what is given and ministered by God through them. For this reason it is impossible for us to know anything concerning them, except what the divinity taught us through them. « Unless, he says, one should there say, whatever mysteries the divinity taught, as through those well knowing their own. » It is impossible for us to know, unless one should say there, that we know only there where we have been taught through them whatever mysteries, that is, secrets, the divinity taught us through them, as through those well knowing their own ministries. Well therefore do they know their own ministries, as much as suffices for our instruction; and well are they ignorant, [1029B] as much as suffices for full and perfect comprehension.

There follows: « Not therefore do we ourselves say anything of our own motion: but whatever of the angelic contemplations have been contemplated by the holy theologians, these, teaching us, as much as we are able, we shall expound. » Since, he says, we of ourselves can know nothing concerning them, which we have not learned from them: therefore in those things which we wish to say concerning them, we do not say of ourselves, that is, of our own motion, or sense, or thought — we say nothing. But whatever the holy theologians have contemplated through contemplation concerning the angels themselves, we, following their authority as much as we can, teach this. « All theology called the celestial essences by nine [1029C] manifesting surnames. » All the celestial essences, he says, that is, the spirits, theology called by nine manifesting surnames, that is, distinguished them by nine manifest and evident surnames, namely angels, archangels, principalities, powers, virtues, dominations, thrones, cherubim, and seraphim, marking them off by their proper names. « These, he says, our divine holy perfecter separates into three ternary arrangements. » It is believed that no one is here better signified as his divine and holy perfecter, or teacher, than the apostle Paul, by whom he had been baptized and instructed in the catholic faith; who, caught up as far as the third heaven into the paradise of God, and there hearing secret things which it is not lawful for a man to speak, is thought to have revealed — as much [1029D] as it was either useful or possible to know of these things for this mortal life — by transmitting them to the memory of posterity, through this holy man as well as through others who by the Spirit of God had passed beyond human understanding. Relying therefore on his authority, and supported by the testimonies of the holy Scriptures, he brought down the knowledge of the celestial things to earth. These, he says, namely the essences, that is, these nine orders, « our divine holy perfecter separates, » that is, distinguishes, « into three ternary, » that is, threefold, « arrangements. And the first indeed he says to be that existing about God, always, and attentively united to him, and, before the others, immediately, as has been handed down. » He says the first arrangement to be about God, always [1030A] existing, and never at any time going out to outward things; and handed down, that is, ordained and arranged, to be united, namely, to God himself, that is, that it may be attentively united to him, being always intent, of course, upon him; and that it may be united to him before the other celestial virtues, which are after it, and united immediately, so that there are none before it. « For the most holy thrones, and the eyed and winged orders, named cherubim in the voice of the Hebrews, and seraphim, according to a nearness set above all, are placed immediately about God, he says, as the manifestation of the divine oracles hands down. » He himself therefore, our perfecter and teacher, says that the manifestation, or authority, of the divine oracles hands down or attests the most holy thrones, and the eyed and winged orders, named in the voice of the Hebrews [1030B] cherubim and seraphim — the eyed, namely, the cherubim, on account of contemplation, the winged, namely the seraphim, on account of love. These orders, I say, that is the thrones, cherubim, and seraphim, he himself says to be placed immediately about God according to the nearness which they have set above toward God, and more excellent than all the other orders. « This threefold adornment therefore, as one, and equally ordered, and truly the first hierarchy, our common master calls it. » These three orders therefore he says to constitute one hierarchy, first in relation to the others, equally ordered in itself. In which hierarchy, namely, although they are more exalted than the others in singular grace, yet all are in a certain manner equal in one arrangement: and from this, according to something, they [1030C] exist as equal, that all look immediately upon God: the first in love, the second in knowledge, the third in discernment. « Than which, » namely hierarchy, « there is no other more deiform, and, of itself, more immediately intent upon the pre-operating illuminations of the divinity. » No other hierarchy, he says, is more deiform than this one, nor more nearly approaching the likeness of God, nor more intent upon the illuminations of the divinity pre-operating in it alone, because before the others; and operating of itself, because not through others. For the divine illuminations operate in this hierarchy alone before the others, because all after this hierarchy are illuminated by those same divine illuminations; and of themselves the divine illuminations operate in this one alone, because through it all the other hierarchies [1030D] are consequently illuminated by the divine illuminations. « But the second he says to be, » namely hierarchy, « completed from the powers, and dominations, and virtues: » the order however being changed, so that the dominations are understood as first, the virtues as second, the powers as third. « And the arrangement of the threefold, last, of the celestial hierarchies, of the angels, archangels, and principalities. » It must be supplied from what precedes, our master himself says, to have known the arranged arrangement of the threefold celestial hierarchies, of the angels, archangels, and principalities. Where, if it be asked why Paul in his Epistles, when he was enumerating the arrangement of the angels by distinguishing it, did not preserve this order, since [1031A] he had asserted to others that such was the order among them. It can be said that there he wished to explain not so much the order of the celestial arrangements as their number, especially since in one place he enumerates only certain ones; but in another he explains those which he had there passed over in silence, without [1032A] repetition of the others. In which it is manifest that, if he were attending to order and number, he would have been zealous to explain all of them at once in one series of narration. And if there is any other reason which can be fittingly answered to this objection.
