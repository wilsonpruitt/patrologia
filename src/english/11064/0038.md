## THE TEXT.

[1031B]

*We, being about to receive this order of the holy hierarchies, say that all the surnames of the celestial intellects have a declaration of the deiform property of each one. And indeed the holy naming of the seraphim — which are of the Hebrews — [we, ] knowing, [know it] to signify either the intent ones, or the heating ones; but that of the cherubim, a multitude of knowledge, or a fusion of wisdom. Beautifully, therefore, the first of the celestial hierarchies is sanctified by the most excellent essences, having an order higher than all, that is, it is placed immediately about God; and the first-operating theophanies and perfections are borne down into it, as into the nearest, more principally. Heating ones therefore are they named, both the thrones, and a fusion of wisdom, by a name manifesting their deiform [1031C] dispositions. For their ever-moving about the divine things, and unceasing, and hot, and sharp, and super-fervid — of an intent, and perhaps innermost, and ever-inflexible motion, and reductive of the things placed beneath, and active — the exemplative [property], as it were re-heating those things, and resuscitating them into a like heat, and fiery from heaven, and purgative by whole-burnt-offering, and unveiled, and inextinguishable, and having thus ever a light-form and illuminative property, persecutrix of every dark obscuring, and manifestatrix — this the manifestation, or surname, of the seraphim teaches. But that of the cherubim [teaches] their knowable, and bared [property], and receptive of the highest giving of lights, and contemplative, filled in the first operatrix power of the divine beauty, and of the wisdom-making tradition, and communicative [1031D] copiously to the second things, of the wisdom given for outpouring. But that [naming] of the highest and compacted seats [teaches] the being diligently exalted above every ignominy of subjection, and bearing upward to the summit in a supermundane way, and, by every extremity ineffably, into the most sublime, and, about the truly exalted, placed with all its powers unchangeably and stably, and receptive of the divine super-advent in all impassibility and immateriality, and God-bearing, and, in a servant-like manner, open unto the divine receivings. This indeed is the declaration of their names, as far as pertains to us. But it must be said what hierarchy of theirs we deem it. That the speculation of every hierarchy hangs ineffably upon a deiformity imitating God, and that every hierarchic action is divided into a sacred participation, and a tradition [1032B] of pure purgation, and of the divine light, and of perfective knowledge, I judge to have been already sufficiently said by us. But now let us worthily proceed to speak of the most excellent intellects, how now, according to them, the hierarchy is manifested by the oracles. In the first essences, which, placed after their substance-making divinity, and, as it were, ordained in its vestibules, super-excel every made power visible and invisible, it must be deemed that there is a proper, and wholly equiform, hierarchy. It must therefore be deemed that they are pure, not as freed from unclean stains and defilements, nor as receptive of material phantasies, but as clean by every diminishment, and higher, and, above every super-firmed temple, according to the most exalted chastity, super-placed above all the most deiform powers [1032C], and, by their own motion of themselves according to the unconvertible order of one loving God, ineffably received, and knowing wholly no contumely among the things subjected, but having a non-casual, as also an unchangeable, most pure placing of their proper deiform specialty. And [they are] again contemplative of the sensible symbols, or speculative of the intellectual ones, not as led back into the divine by the variety of a sacred-writing theory, but as filled with the higher light of all immaterial knowledge, and stuffed, as much as is lawful, with the contemplation of the form-making, and principal beauty, and superessential, and thrice-shining; and made worthy likewise of the communion of Jesus. Not in sacredly feigned images do they formatively figure the deific likeness, but as truly approaching him in the first participation [1032D] of knowledge [they figure] the deific [thing] of his lights, and because what is like God has been substantially given to them. But he communicates such a thing, as far as possible, in the pre-operatrix power, to his deific and human virtues. But [they are] perfect likewise, not as illuminated by a sacred variety in an analectic [analyticum] knowledge, but as filled with a first and supereminent deification, according to the most excellent knowledge of the divine works, as far as is in angels. For not through other holy essences, but sanctified by the divinity itself, they are extended immediately into it, by a virtue and order supereminent to all, and are placed unto a wholly most chaste fortitude, and are led into contemplation of the immaterial and invisible beauty, as much as is lawful, and are bent toward the knowable reasons of the divine works, as first, and about [1033A] God, essences, and are most excellently [excelsissime] sanctified by the very sovereign of perfection. This therefore the theologians also openly declare: that the subordinate arrangements of the celestial essences are ornately instructed in the deific sciences by their super-firmed [superiors], but that the higher of all are illuminated in doctrine by the divinity itself, as much as is lawful. For they introduce certain of them, sacredly instructed by the former ones, [saying] that the Lord is [Lord] of the celestial powers, and the King of glory received into the heavens in a human manner [n: (Psal. XXIII)]*. *But certain [others] inquiring with Jesus himself, and learning for us the knowledge of his divine action, and [they introduce] Jesus himself immediately teaching them, and, lavishing upon them, manifesting his human benignity. For I, he says, dispute justice, and the judgment of salvation [n: (Isa. LXIII)]*. *But I wonder that even [1033B] the first of the celestial essences, and — so many all together — supereminent by divine illuminations, reverently seek, as it were, mediated questionings. For indeed they do not thereupon ask: Why are thy [tibi] garments red? But among themselves they deliberate before asking, showing indeed that they learn, and seek the deific knowledge, yet not leaping before the illumination imparted through the divine procession. Is not, then, the first hierarchy of the celestial intellects, sanctified by the very sovereign of perfection, in that it is extended immediately into it — by a most holy purgation, by much light, by a perfect consummation beforehand, filling it proportionally — purged, and illuminated, and perfected; pure indeed by every diminution, but full of the first light, and partaking of the knowledge first given, and of the perfect knowledge [1033C]? But comprehending (and this I would say perhaps not undeservedly) that both purgation, and illumination, and perfection, is an assumption of the divine knowledge. Purging, namely, ignorance, inasmuch as the knowledge of the perfect doctrines is imparted according to order; but illuminating by that same divine knowledge through which it also purges the one not first contemplating, before it manifests through the higher illumination; and again perfecting, by that same light, according to habit, by the knowledge of the most lucid doctrines. This therefore is, as far as pertains to our knowledge, the first arrangement of the celestial essences, standing in the circuit of God, and immediately about God, and simply and unceasingly circling his eternal knowledge, according to the most excellent, ever-moving placing, as far as is in angels. Seeing indeed many [1033D] and blessed contemplations purely, and illuminated by the simple and immediate splendors, and filled with divine nourishment; by much given at first in outpouring, and by the sole domestic and unific unity of the divine refection, and made worthy of much communion of God and of cooperation toward it, by a likeness, as far as possible, of good dispositions and actions; and knowing many of the divine things in a super-placed manner, and made a partaker of the divine knowledge and cognition, according to what is lawful. Therefore theology handed down its praises also to those who are on earth, in which the eminence of its most excellent illumination is wonderfully manifested. For certain of them, speaking sensibly, re-echo as the voice of waters: Blessed be the glory of the Lord from his place. [1034A] But others cry back that very laudable and most devout theology: Holy, holy, holy, the Lord God of Sabaoth; full is all the earth of his glory [n: (Ezech. I; Ezech. III; Isa. VI; Apoc. IV)]*. *But these most exalted hymnologies of the celestial minds we have already, indeed, opened as much as possible in those [treatises] which are on the divine praises, and it has been said of them in those [places], as far as pertains to us, sufficiently. From which, for remembrance, it suffices to say only, according to the present time, that the first arrangement itself was illuminated in the theological [sic: *seientiam*], as far as is lawful, by the divine goodness, through which it then handed itself down also to others as a deiform hierarchy, introducing that [truth] by way of brevity in saying: that it is lawful for that most devout, and supremely blessed, and wholly blessed divinity to be blessed [1034B] — as far as is possible — to be known and praised by the intellects that receive God. For they themselves are, as it were, deiform divine places, of divine, as the oracles say [n: (Psal. IX, XLVI, LXXIX; Isa. LXVI)]*, *rest. And because it is a monad and unity, three substantially, and, from the supercelestial essences even to the last things of the earth, extending its goodness into all things that are, [and its] providence, as the super-principal principle of every essence, and cause, and binding all things around by a super-essentially immeasurable containment.*
