## EXPOSITION.

The title of the seventh chapter is: On the interpretation of the seraphim, and cherubim, and thrones; and on the first hierarchy, which is theirs. For after he enumerated [1034C] the orders of the celestial hierarchies, he now begins to treat of the interpretation and signification of the surnames. And first concerning those which are counted in the first hierarchy, that is, concerning the seraphim, and cherubim, and thrones, demonstrating their virtue according to the property of the appellation.

« We, being about to receive this order of the holy hierarchies. » We, he says, being about to receive and approve this order of the holy hierarchies, which we spoke of above: « We say » consequently, « that all the surnames of the celestial intellects have a declaration of the deiform property of each one. » For all the surnames of the holy intellects, that is, of the spirits, have a declaration [1034D] of the deiform property of each order. The surname declares what is proper to it, and singular, conferred through the excellence of the gift in deiformity. For every grace reforms the mind to the likeness of God; and yet what is conferred as proper to each in that one form, just as it is distinguished in the office, so was to be distinguished in the appellation.

There follows: « And indeed the holy naming of the seraphim — which are of the Hebrews — knowing [it] to manifest either the kindling ones, or the heating ones; but that of the cherubim, a multitude of knowledge, or a fusion of wisdom. We say, he says, that all the surnames of the celestial intellects have a declaration [1035A] of the deiform property of each one. » We, I say, knowing the naming of the seraphim: which seraphim are of the Hebrews, that is, are so called in Hebrew, [know it] to manifest either the kindling ones, or the heating ones; but that naming — supply it — which is of the cherubim, [we, ] knowing, [know it] to manifest a multitude of knowledge, or a fusion of wisdom, so that in the very expression of the spiritual names may be declared the grace of the gifts, which, even if they are not singular, yet are established as excellent. « Beautifully, therefore, the first of the celestial hierarchies is sanctified by the most excellent essences, having an order higher than all, that is, it is placed immediately about God: and the first-operating theophanies and perfections are borne down into it, as into the nearest, more principally. » This, he says, is beautiful [1035B] and fitting, that the first hierarchy should have more excellent spirits, since it too is by its order higher than all the other hierarchies, and nearer to God, placed immediately about him, that is, with no other standing between it and God. And therefore, because it is nearest to God, on that account the theophanies — that is, the divine apparitions, or manifestations, or illuminations first-operating, namely from the Creator into the creature, not through a creature (for those are the first operations of the divine illumination into the creature, or [rather] the creature; the second are through a creature into a creature): and the perfections too of the spiritual gifts are borne down more principally into that first hierarchy than into the others which follow and are subject: inasmuch as they conceive every participation of spiritual grace [1035C] only by its mediation. « Heating ones therefore are they named, both the thrones, and a fusion of wisdom, by a name manifesting their deiform dispositions. » Since the divine illuminations and perfections are borne down more principally into it, on that account the orders constituted in it — some, by conceiving and furnishing the fire of love, are named heating ones, like the seraphim; others, powerful by the judgment of truth, thrones; others, shining and illuminating by the knowledge of science, a fusion of wisdom, like the cherubim — are called by a name manifesting their deiform dispositions. For from the very name of the appellation is manifested the virtue of the deiform disposition, so that by it they may be known to be endowed with the divine likeness, by which they are known to be marked with a distinct [1035D] and singular appellation. For that in each order the distinction of the name marks a certain singular and proper disposition of the divine participation, he proves from the sentence set below, saying:

« For their ever-moving about the divine things, and unceasing, and hot, and sharp, and super-fervid — of an intent, and perhaps innermost, and ever-inflexible motion, and reductive of the things placed beneath, and active — the exemplative [property], as it were re-heating those things, and resuscitating them into a like heat, and fiery from heaven, and purgative by whole-burnt-offering, and unveiled, and inextinguishable, and having thus ever a light-form and illuminative property, persecutrix of every dark obscuring, and manifestatrix — this the naming, [1036A] or manifestation, of the seraphim teaches. » If I should say what I feel, first I confess this: that I have heard words either not spoken to a man, or not spoken by a man. For that they should even be spoken through a man seems to me so great a thing that nothing more could be given to a man. And perhaps, because these words were born from those which could be heard, they ought not to have been spoken. For he indeed who suggested or taught these things had reached even to the third heaven, and had entered into the paradise of God, and there had heard certain words concerning the Word, wholly secret, and next to silence, [reaching] even to those which the human ear could not touch: which no one would hear, until he knew. For within they were heard where they were spoken, and could not go forth outside where man was. Therefore by him who was within, and deeply within, they could be heard within and inwardly [1036B]; but to those who were outside they ought not to have been spoken. Yet lest even those who were outside should be abandoned, if they were not called by him who was within: words were born from words, just as the words were born from the Word: from the words which had to be kept within, words which could be brought forth outside; from immense things, great ones; from hidden things, obscure ones; from impenetrable things, profound ones — which, though heard by us, whether they were understood, I do not know. These are the very words which the disciple of the master, and the master of disciples, set before us to be searched out, or rather to be wondered at. For the first disciple of the Word heard words from the Word, and he, made a teacher by other words, had a disciple, and made a teacher. When this at length descended to our hearing, the voice, as if from heaven, resounded upon the earth, and already filled our ears [1036C] with amazement; yet it had not yet illuminated our hearts with manifest truth. Therefore those who were men, and could not yet grasp divine things, said that a thunder had come to pass, because they were only dismayed, and not instructed. Others, illuminated a little, not yet consummated, thought it an angel; they did not understand it to be God. And so we too have heard the thunder of the heavenly voice, and have begun to wonder, not yet to be illuminated. Yet if our wonder be an awakening, by that same wonder we shall be converted, that by conversion we may be illuminated. And then those very words will be sweet, not only to be wondered at, but to be loved, when they begin to be heard and known, if only we shall be gracious toward them. [1036D] For if they are not loved, they are not understood; nor are they loved, if they are not tasted. What then? Why have we heard, if we do not understand; or how do we understand, if we do not love? I, for my part, answer: If I do not presume upon love, I do not depart from wonder. Perhaps by that same wonder I shall wake up to knowledge: and if I am less roused to knowledge, I shall be incited to love. And meanwhile love itself will be a refreshment, until from it there arises contemplation, through which illumination may come to be.

What is that [saying] of the angels — « the ever-moving about the divine things, and unceasing, and hot, and sharp, and super-fervid, of an ever-intent, and perhaps innermost, and ever-inflexible motion? » If we should say that this is love, perhaps we may seem to have said little, [1037A] to those not knowing what love is. For never does he say little who says love, unless perchance he means a small love. But this one did not wish to mean a small love, who said so many things about love. « Moving, he says, and unceasing, and hot, and sharp, and super-fervid. » Moving, because it is life; unceasing, because it is perpetual; hot, because it is love; sharp, because it is wisdom. Is this enough? He said life, he named it perpetual, he set down love, and he added wisdom. And all this is in one love, and it is one love. Do you wish to know that love is life? Hear that beloved one, and the lover, commending love. « He who does not love, he says, remains in death [n: (Joan. I)]. » Therefore love is life; and what kind of life? « Charity never falls away [n: (I Cor. XIII)]. » But if [1037B] charity never falls away, love is perpetual life. And what of love? Where shall we be able to show that hot and fervid quality in love? Where did love have fervor and heat; or rather, where was love without heat and fervor? Walking and loving, kindling and fervent, what did they say of Jesus, whom they heard, and did not recognize on the way? For they were walking and being moved, driven by the impatience of love, because if they stood still they would not love. For the moving quality belongs to love as does the hot, so that true love may not grow numb. They were walking therefore in the moving quality of love, and were burning in the hot, and were saying: « Was not our heart burning within us concerning Jesus, while he was speaking to us on the way? [n: (Luc. XXIV.)] » For because they were walking, they had the moving quality; and the hot, because they were burning; but the sharp [1037C] they did not have, because they did not recognize [him]. For this reason, because they did not have the sharp, they heard: « Foolish ones, and slow to believe in all the things that the prophets have spoken [n: (ibid.)]. » Therefore they were dull, and slow to know; but not lukewarm, or sluggish to love. Yet because they first loved, afterward they knew, so that the sharp might be in love just as the hot also. First the hot, afterward the sharp. Therefore he did not love the sharp and the hot, but the hot and the sharp: just as first the moving, afterward the unceasing, so that the moving may rouse to inquiry, the unceasing may confirm to perseverance; the hot, that it may vivify the sense; but the sharp, that it may penetrate to comprehension. For the sharp signifies a certain onrush of love, and the vehemence of an ardent desire, bearing [1037D] itself into the beloved, and entering, and penetrating, so that it may be there where the very thing which is loved is, with it, and in it, so that not only may the warmth be from it, but the sharp may pass over into it. For it could be hot, and, as it were, be warmed from afar: for which it would be enough to love one so absent, and not to see him present, or to possess him most present. But the love of the hierarchy was not perfect, nor much to be loved, unless it made the sharp for itself, and passed through all things, and penetrated, until it reached the beloved, nay rather went into the beloved. For if you do not go into the beloved, you still love outside, nor do you have the sharpness of love. But you have [only warmth], and, growing numb, you remain divided, and outside him, so that you are not made one [with him]. But love wishes to make you one with [1038A] him: and therefore it penetrates all things, and approaches as much as it can, unto him who is one.
