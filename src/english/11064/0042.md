## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

And that very surname of the Seraphim teaches also their light-formed and illuminative property, holding itself always thus: thus, that is, in one and the same mode, and remaining without variety and mutability, and the pursuer of all dark obscuration, so that it does not draw near to it; and the manifester, so that nothing lies hidden outside it: all these things, therefore, the surname or manifestation of the Seraphim teaches. And all these things, as we have said, are in one love, and are one love: which is given to them from above, and is derived through them to the subjects. Of which love the author distinguishes a threefold power in those highest spirits by this enumeration: above them, in them, and beneath [1043D] them. Above them, mobile; in them, vital; beneath them, operative. Above them, through desire; in them, through sense; beneath them, through affection. Above them, seeking; in them, perceiving; beneath them, gathering. Above them, in that which they desire; in them, in that which they perceive; beneath them, on behalf of those whom they draw along with themselves to that which they perceive in themselves, and to that which they desire above themselves. On account of such wondrous operations of love he said so many things about it: in which he would perhaps have said the whole, if the whole could have been said. But we fear both, [1044A] if we be either negligent or fastidious. It is hard for us, in a matter so sweet, to deny anything which we have received: and again it seems to us rash to add anything which we ought not. What, do you think, is love? When shall the whole be told? Behold, we have said the « mobile » that is theirs, « and the unceasing, and the hot, and the keen, and the superfervent, and the intent, and the inmost, and the inflexible, and the exemplative, and the reductive, and the active, and the rewarming, and the re-arousing: » and this seemed to be much, and perhaps enough: except that other wondrous things still followed, I know not whether more wondrous. « Fiery, » he says, « from heaven, and purgative by holocaust. » Two things are to be noted, that he named it fiery, and that same fiery from heaven. For the fiery too is one thing from the earth, but is not like that which is [1044B] fiery from heaven. For it burns, and consumes, and lays waste, and destroys; nor can there be any fellowship with it for another. For those who draw near to it are harmed; and if they touch it at all, they already begin to be consumed. But that which is fiery from heaven burns sweetly; and it kindles indeed, but does not consume; and if it consumes anything, yet not unto harm, but unto purgation: for it consumes this, which would harm, if it were not consumed. Therefore after « fiery from heaven » there follows « purgative by holocaust; » because that fiery thing purges, and purges the whole, and purges from the whole, not only from the corruption of evil, but also from the defect of good. For there were certain things which had contracted the corruption of evil; there were certain things which had not yet received the perfection of good: and [1044C] both were to be purged; the one from corruption, the other from imperfection. Those which were on earth were to be purged from corruption; those which were in heaven were to be purged from imperfection. The former, because in their first condition they were not perfect; the latter, because after their first condition they were corrupted. The former have been purged from imperfection, when they are consummated in glorification; the latter have been purged from corruption, when they are freed from sin. Of the former, therefore, the purgation was not the amendment of an evil which they did not have; but the consummation of a good which they had in lesser measure. But of the latter the purgation was first amendment; afterward consummation. Such a purgation, therefore, we understand in that spiritual and heavenly nature. But even if anyone should understand in those beings a purgation, [1044D] not of the corruption which was in them, because they were always clean, but of the perfect cleanness, to which no corruption could be present, this too he will understand fittingly. That « fiery from heaven, » therefore, by which they are inflamed, that they may burn, and are purged either from imperfection unto consummation, or unto full cleanness against all corruption, is « purgative by holocaust, » that is, universally purgative, or wholly purgative, because it purges the whole and purges from the whole; whether that no corruption may be present, or that no perfection may be lacking. For a holocaust is that which is wholly kindled, and wholly burned up.
