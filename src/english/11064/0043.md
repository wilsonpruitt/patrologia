## BOOK SEVEN. *Exposition of the remaining part of the seventh chapter of the divine Dionysius the Areopagite on the celestial hierarchy, the text of which has been set out above.*

[1045]

[1045A]Long leisures demand a new beginning. A little above we had entered upon the discourse concerning the surname of the angels, where the author shows why such surnames or appellations have been attributed to the several orders of the angels. And concerning the first, and highest, order indeed, which is surnamed Seraphim, we have pursued, according to our capacity, the account of that appellation which has been given by theology. Now it remains that we also consider the account which he renders of the surname of the following order. He had first said that the surname, or manifestation, of the Seraphim teaches « their mobile, and the unceasing, and the hot, and the keen, and the superfervent, » which were added after these: but now he brings in and says that the surname, [1045B] or manifestation, of the Cherubim teaches « their knowable, and the deiform, » etc. For he says: « But the Cherubim itself. » Two things are to be repeated from what precedes, "the surname" and "teaches." As if he should say: The very surname of the Cherubim teaches, that is, in that they are surnamed Cherubim, which is interpreted *fullness of knowledge,* there is taught and signified « their knowable, » that is, the knowledge or acquaintance and science which they have; and by this name there is signified also « their deiform, » that is, the vision of God which is in them, because through the light of wisdom implanted in them they clearly contemplate the majesty of their Creator. There is signified also « the receptive of the highest bestowal of lights: » that is, it is signified that they have received the lights divinely given most highly [1045C] and most perfectly. For in that they are signified, by their very surname, to have the fullness of knowledge, they are assuredly taught to have received the light of that same wisdom from elsewhere; because, according to the saying of the Apostle: « If they had not received it, they could not have had it at all [n: (I Cor. IV)]. » But in that they have it, reception is noted; in that they have the fullness, advance is noted. And the order is this, that first they receive the light of wisdom divinely given, and afterward, illumined by that same light, they see and know God, the author of the light. Well, therefore, are they illumined, who are so illumined that they see and know him by whom they are illumined. Many are illumined that they may see other things, and see not him himself through whom they see. But it is no great thing to see a work, if you [1045D] know not the craftsman. A thing made cannot beatify, if you do not reach to the beauty of the one who wrought it. Therefore the surname of the Cherubim teaches « their knowable, and the deiform, and the receptive of the highest streaming-in of light. » Note the unusual compositions in that he says "deiform," and "streaming-in of light." The surname of the Cherubim teaches [1046A] also, namely, their contemplative in the first working power of the divine beauty, and their repleteness of wise handing-down, and their communicative of the copious wisdom given for the outpouring unto the second beings. Distinguish thus: The surname of the Cherubim teaches the contemplative of the divine beauty in the first working power. For in that they are called Cherubim, that is, full of knowledge, it is shown that through the light of the wisdom given they contemplate the divine beauty; since assuredly, whatever they knew, they would not be full of wisdom, if they did not know the divine beauty, from which, and in which, all things are beautifully and reasonably ordered. Because, therefore, they are full of wisdom, they assuredly contemplate the divine beauty. Nor do they contemplate it in any way whatever, [1046B] but in the first working power, so that, namely, being first and principally illumined by God, they may illumine the rest that come after them. For the divine power works first, and principally, and of itself in those who are nearest; but afterward through them in the others who follow. This, therefore, the surname of the Cherubim teaches, namely that the contemplation of the divine beauty is in them through the first working power; because God works first in them, that afterward he may work through them. It teaches also the repleteness of the wise handing-down, that is, it teaches that they are replete with divine grace: which by the wise handing-down of the Creator is distributed in participation to some more, and to some less. A wondrous excellence of theirs, therefore, is shown, because of that good which the wisdom [1046C] of the Creator handed down unequally unto the beauty of the universe, they are proclaimed to have not a part but the fullness. It can also be distinguished otherwise, so that it be said that the surname of the Cherubim teaches their contemplative replete, that is, their contemplation replete with the divine beauty, and with the wise handing-down, in the first working power. And it can be understood, not unfittingly, by the divine beauty and the wise handing-down, that the divine wisdom effects a greater beauty in its works from this very thing, that it did not settle its gifts to be participated by all in one and the same mode. Of which beauty, indeed, and of which handing-down, those highest spirits are for this reason replete; because the gifts which were given in part to the lower and subject beings, they have merited [1046D] to possess according to the fullness.

There follows: « And the communicative of the wisdom given copiously for the outpouring unto the second beings. » Again it must be repeated from what precedes. The surname of the Cherubim teaches their communicative, that is, the communication, or participation, of the wisdom given copiously for the outpouring, [1047A] that is, that in communicating they participate not slightly, but copiously, in the wisdom given to them for the outpouring. Which outpouring, namely, was first made in them copiously, that through them it might afterward be made unto the second beings: inasmuch as the light of wisdom, which is first poured copiously into them from above, is afterward through them transfused unto the second, that is, the following orders, to be illumined. This fullness, therefore, and dignity, and excellence in the gift of wisdom, and in the light of truth, the surname of the Cherubim teaches.

There follows: « But it itself, of the highest and compacted seats, diligently to be exalted from all the ignominy of subjection, and bearing upward supramundanely unto the summit, and by every extremity ineffably unto the most sublime, and about the truly lofty placed with all its powers unchangeably [1047B] and stably: and receptive of the divine coming-upon in all impassibility and immateriality, and God-bearing, and in a servile manner opened unto the divine receptions. » After the surname of the Seraphim, and of the Cherubim, he passes to explaining the surname of the Thrones. As if he should say: As the surname of the Seraphim signifies the ardor of love, and the surname of the Cherubim the brightness of knowledge, so also the surname of the Thrones signifies the loftiness of dignity, for the reason that the invisible Founder, sitting in them, through them disposes all subject things by judging. For on account of this dignity and excellence of the divine judgment, which is exercised through them, he named the Thrones themselves the highest and compacted seats; the highest, [1047C] on account of their dignity; compacted, on account of their truth. For it is manifest that thrones are the seats of those who reign and judge. And to the one reigning belongs sublimity, but to the one judging belongs truth. And therefore he deservedly named the Thrones themselves the highest seats, because he who reigns in them has no superior; and compacted, because he who judges in them does not depart from the truth. What is "compacted"? Fitly and suitably joined together. See the joining of the seats of God. The joining of the seats of God is the fittingness of judgments. Every judgment infers one thing from another. From fault, punishment; from justice, glory; from merit, reward; from the quality of the work, the quality of the retribution. It finds fault, it adjudges punishment. It finds justice, it adjudges glory. Well is it joined, that seat is compacted. Well does it accord and [1047D] fitly cohere. Punishment to fault, glory to justice. If glory were joined to fault, and punishment to justice, they would not accord with one another, nor would the judgment have a compacted seat. The compaction, therefore, of the seats is the truth of the judgments. But it must be known that every lower judgment, when it comes into question or contradiction, is wont to take from a higher either testimony or confirmation. But the highest judgment, because it has not another above itself by which it may be confirmed; by right the seats of God are named not only compacted through truth, but also highest through dignity. But if we read not "compacted," but "raised up," which from the ambiguity of the Greek word can likewise be understood: this is signified, that those heavenly spirits, over whom God presides for judging, have merited to be singularly high [1048A] in glory, not through the loftiness of nature, but through the raising-up of grace. Let him say, therefore: « But it itself, of the highest and compacted seats, » that is, of the Thrones (again from what precedes "the surname" must be supplied) teaches this, namely that those same Thrones are exalted, or have been exalted, diligently, that is, perfectly, from all the ignominy of subjection, that is, from all ignominious subjection. For the more perfectly they are subject to the presiding God, the more truly they have merited to be raised above all other things through him, and in him. Because, therefore, thrones signify not subjection, but dominion, when the word of God names those heavenly spirits thrones, in that very thing he demonstrates them to be perfectly ruling, and free from all subjection. So, therefore, the surname of the Thrones teaches them to be exalted from all [1048B] the ignominy of subjection; it teaches also their bearing upward, that is, their raising-up unto the summit: and this supramundanely, that is, spiritually or invisibly, namely by a spiritual and invisible exaltation, and one transcending all mundane and visible loftiness. The surname of the Thrones, therefore, not only teaches them to be preferred to the lower beings through dominion; but also, through sublimation, to be exalted unto the summit, so that, namely, between them and him who is the highest there be nothing in the middle.
