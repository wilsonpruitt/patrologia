## BOOK SEVEN. *Exposition of the remaining part of the seventh chapter of the divine Dionysius the Areopagite on the celestial hierarchy, the text of which has been set out above.* (cont.)

There follows: « They are therefore to be reckoned pure: not as freed from unclean stains and defilements, nor as receptive of material phantasms, but as clean of all diminution, and higher; and, above every over-established temple, set on high above all things according to the most exalted chastity, with all their most God-formed virtues; and by their own proper motion through themselves, and by that same motion, according to the unturnable [character] of the order that loves God, ineffably received; and altogether knowing no contumely in things subordinate, but holding the incapable-of-falling and also unchangeable [1051D] most pure placement of their own God-formed specialty. » Above he said that in those three most excellent orders of angels there is one, and an altogether similar, hierarchy — that is, a sacred power; now, consequently, he adds that those things which pertain to hierarchical action — that is, to the operation of the sacred power — are perfectly present in them, namely purgation, illumination, perfection. These three every sacred power ordered under the supreme power exercises in two ways, namely by receiving from what is above and imparting to what is below. He says, therefore, that those first essences, in which the first hierarchy is ordered, have purgation, which they both receive from the divinity above, that they may be purged, and confer on those below, that they may purge. But they receive purgation not as though they were to be freed from some [1052A] uncleanness or corruption, but as to be perfected from all imperfection and diminution — this is what he says: They are to be reckoned pure, namely those first essences; pure, I say, yet not as though freed from certain unclean stains and defilements, since they never had any uncleanness or defilement; nor pure as though receptive of material phantasms, since they do not at all receive material phantasms, and therefore do not need to be purged of them, since they do not have them at all; but rather pure as clean, and estranged from all diminution and imperfection. Indeed, by unclean stains and material phantasms he has expressed two kinds of spiritual defilement: one which is in depraved desires, the other [1052B] which is in vain thoughts. For depraved desires defile the cleanness of the heart like mud, while vain thoughts, like a certain dust sprinkled over it, becloud its brightness. Since therefore those most pure essences of the highest spirits take on no corruption either in depraved desires or in vain thoughts, they consequently do not require to be cleansed either from unclean stains or from material phantasms. They are therefore to be understood as pure, not as purified from corruption, but as clean from imperfection; and not only estranged from imperfection, but also, through the excellence of their perfection, higher than all the rest that are perfect, and set on high above them.

[1052C]For this is what follows: « And higher » — supply: they are to be reckoned to be, those first essences — « and set on high above the over-established temple of all, » that is, above every rational creature in which God dwells, and which, through him dwelling within it, is confirmed toward the highest good. Above all these those first essences are set on high according to their most excellent chastity — that is, more excellent than the chastity of all the rest; a chastity, I say, existing in all their virtues, that is, in their virtues of every kind, the most God-formed, that is, drawing nearer to conformity with God than the virtues of others draw near. Thus, therefore, those first essences are to be reckoned both to be pure, and, through the excellence of their purity, higher than the rest, and set on high [1052D] above them. And they are also to be reckoned to have been ineffably received, for the participating of that purity in God, by their own motion through themselves — that is, without a mediator; and by that same motion — that is, ever straining without deviation toward the selfsame thing; according to the unturnable — that is, according to the unturnableness of the order that loves God. For those who never grow lukewarm in love are never bent aside or turned away from their straining.

There follows: « And in things subordinate knowing no contumely at all — supply: they are to be reckoned. For just as they participate the purity of God from above without diminution, so they offer it to be participated below without elation; in this very thing imitating God, « who gives to all abundantly, and does not upbraid [n: (Jac. I)]. » [1053A] For this reason, he says, « they are to be reckoned as knowing no contumely in things subordinate, but rather as holding the incapable-of-falling and also unchangeable placement of their own God-formed specialty. » By 'their own God-formed specialty' he understands the excellent and singular beauty of divine conformation, which is in those highest essences; in which they, most pure and most perfect, are so placed and grounded that they have a placement incapable of falling, over which a fall cannot hold sway; and rightly incapable of falling, inasmuch as it is unchangeable, or immutable. The sum, then, of this chapter is this: that those first and principal essences participate their purgation, or purity, without diminution, offer it to be participated without elation, and possess it uncorrupted without mutability. [1053B] And these things, indeed, have been said concerning their purgation.

Afterward he adds concerning illumination, saying: « And again, contemplative of sensible symbols, or speculative of intellectual things, not as brought back into the divine by the variety of sacred-writing theory; but as filled with the higher light of every immaterial knowledge, and, as far as is lawful, replete with the contemplation of the form-making and principal beauty, both super-essential and thrice-shining; and likewise made worthy by the communion of Jesus: they do not figure the deiform likeness formatively in sacredly-fashioned images; but as truly drawing near to him himself in the first participation of the knowledge of his deiform lights; and because what is like to God has been substantially bestowed on them. And they communicate [1053C] in this manner, as is possible, in his pre-operative power, in his deiform and human virtues. » All this has been said concerning illumination. As if he said: Not only are those first essences to be reckoned to have purgation and to be pure; but again — that is, further — they are to be reckoned to have illumination, and to be contemplative of sensible symbols, and speculative of intellectual things. Sensible symbols are material signs — whether in creatures, or in the Scriptures, or in the divine sacraments — set forth for the demonstration of invisible things; whose mystical signification and invisible truth those highest angelic spirits recognize by contemplating through divine illumination. They also behold, through that same illumination, intellectual things [1053D] — supply: symbols — that is, spiritual theophanies, that is, divine manifestations, through which the nature of the hidden and invisible divinity is manifested to them within. Or it can be read thus: They — namely the essences — are to be reckoned to be contemplative of sensible symbols, and speculative of intellectual things, and not (supply) of symbols. For outwardly, in sensible things, where there are material signs, there are symbols; but inwardly, in intellectual things, where there are no signs but truth, there are no symbols. Therefore in sensible sacred symbols they contemplate the signs of truth, but inwardly, in intellectual things, they behold the naked truth without signs. Therefore they are to be reckoned to be contemplative of sensible symbols, and likewise [1054A] speculative of intellectual things; yet not as brought back into the divine — that is, into the divine cognition — by variety, that is, by the manifold teaching of theory, that is, of divine Scripture; theory, I say, of the sacred-writing [kind], because, namely, it writes and speaks about divine and sacred things. For, instructed by the simple illumination of divine contemplation, not by the varied and manifold teaching of the Scriptures, they contemplate all the sacred figurations and sensible signs which, whether in the Old or in the New Testament — such as the tabernacle of the covenant, and the ark of the testament, and other things of this kind, which Scripture sets forth for the demonstration of invisible things; the visible appearances of creatures too, through which invisible things are demonstrated; the mystical revelations also made through sensible formations — all these, namely, [1054B] sacred symbols they contemplate. And not only these things that are outward, but intellectual things too, which shine within through pure and naked truth, they behold. But to the beholding of all these things, and to the divine cognition, they are not to be reckoned to have been brought back by the variety of sacred-writing theory — that is, by the manifold teaching of the divine Scriptures, which is necessary only for this: that the minds of men, estranged from God, may be brought back to the knowledge of truth, and, scattered through diverse things, may be gathered into one. Therefore those highest essences are not to be reckoned to have the cognition of all these things as though instructed through the teaching of the Scriptures, but rather as filled with a higher light — that is, with a more excellent cognition of every immaterial, that is spiritual, knowledge. For higher and [1054C] more worthy is the light of cognition that is poured in within through invisible inspiration than that which is possessed from without through the instruction of teaching. And so those highest essences are to be reckoned to have the cognition of things visible and invisible, inasmuch as they are filled with such a light; and also as replete with the contemplation of the form-making, principal, super-essential, and thrice-shining beauty. Replete, I say, as far as is lawful — that is, as is granted to the possibility of the creature. Now he signifies the divine beauty, which is form-making, because according to itself it formed all things made by itself; and is principal, because, though it is the form of all things, it itself received its form from no other; and is super-essential, because it not only goes before through [1054D] form, in that it is the exemplar of all things, but also transcends through essence, in that it is the creatress of all things.

There follows: « And of the thrice-shining » — namely beauty — « they are to be reckoned replete with the contemplation. » By 'thrice-shining beauty' he signifies that same divine beauty, which shines in three, though it itself is not triple, but one: Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit are three persons; but God is one, the Godhead one, the nature one, the essence one, the beauty one. You see the Father: you see that shining beauty in the Father; you see the Son: you see that shining beauty in the Son; you see the Holy Spirit: you see that shining beauty in the Holy Spirit. So it, as it were, shines thrice, because it shines in three, [1055A] though it itself is not triple; but it is one that shines. The Father is another in person: there it shines. The Son is another in person: there it shines. The Holy Spirit is another in person: there it shines. And yet, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, as the Godhead is one and the nature one, so the beauty is one. Therefore, he says, « they are to be reckoned replete with the contemplation of the thrice-shining beauty. » What wonder, then, is it if those highest essences perfectly know the divine works, visible and invisible — they who are full of the contemplation of the Creator himself, as far, namely, as is possible for a creature.
