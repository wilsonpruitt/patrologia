## BOOK SEVEN. *Exposition of the remaining part of the seventh chapter of the divine Dionysius the Areopagite on the celestial hierarchy, the text of which has been set out above.* (cont.)

There follows: « And likewise made worthy by the communion of Jesus, » — supply: they are to be reckoned. Note [1055B] that there is a compound there, « made-worthy, » that is, made worthy. Not only, he says, did they merit to receive the contemplation and cognition of the divinity, but they were also made worthy — by the communion of that salvation which was accomplished in the humanity of Jesus, both by knowing and by participating. For they had communion with Jesus: the mystery of his incarnation — both by foretelling it before it came to pass, and by ministering when it came to pass, and, after it was accomplished, by rejoicing together with man, restored to eternity, in the same fellowship of blessedness. Afterward he adds, showing that they receive the divine contemplation without any figures, illumined immediately by the divinity itself. For just as above he demonstrated that they read and draw the cognition of all things visible and invisible not through outward teaching, [1055C] but through the inner [teaching] in the very wisdom of God; so now he demonstrates that they receive that same divine contemplation not through any intervening forms or images, but from the divinity itself, in the first place, immediately, nakedly, and purely. « They do not, he says, figure the deiform likeness formatively in sacredly-fashioned images; but rather they figure it as truly drawing near to it, » namely to the deiform likeness, or to the divinity, « drawing near, » I say, in the first participation of the knowledge « of his deiform lights, » that is, of his deifying illuminations. The deiform likeness is the divine contemplation itself; because, while, illumined through it, they are shining, they in a certain manner receive the likeness of the illuminating light itself. But in this [1055D] deiform likeness they do not figure themselves, nor do they acquire it for themselves through any sacred images formed and fashioned — that is, composed for the demonstration of spiritual things; as do men, who through visible and material demonstrations in the sacred discourse are instructed unto the cognition of invisible things. Therefore those highest essences are not illumined with the divine contemplation by means of such things. But, as truly drawing near to it — that is, drawing near to truth itself not through another, but through truth itself, since between them and truth there is nothing in between; and therefore they are drawing near in the first participation of knowledge, because in the first place they participate the knowledge of his deiform lights; because immediately they contemplate, and know, and recognize his deiform — that is, deifying — lights; [1056A] because those illumined by them through the divine likeness they in a certain manner make gods.

There follows: « And because what is like to God has been substantially bestowed on them. » For this reason too, he says, they are truly, and in the first participation, drawing near to the Divinity itself; because what is like to God has been bestowed on them — that is, because the likeness of God has been substantially bestowed on them. For that they are like to God they have not from another, or through another; but because they receive the divinity itself substantially, nakedly, and purely. Or, what is like to God has been bestowed on them most subordinately, because in the nearest place they are subordinate, so that they receive the likeness of God itself by first participation.

There follows: « They communicate in this manner, as is possible, in his pre-operative power, in his deiform and [1056B] human virtues. » He concludes what was said above. For because they are replete in this manner — that is, those highest essences [are replete] with the contemplation of the principal beauty — therefore they communicate in the deiform virtues; and because they are made worthy by the communion of Jesus, therefore they communicate in the human virtues; and because they participate in the first and nearest place, therefore they communicate in the pre-operative power of God, which, namely, first operates in them, and afterward operates through them. Or he calls 'the human virtues of Jesus' his virtues [shown] from his humanity — that is, shown from clemency or kindness; so that the sense is: Since, just as they communicate in his virtues in the contemplation of his majesty, so also they communicate in the co-operation of his kindness. They communicate with him through contemplation in his majesty; they communicate [1056C] with him through the ministry of co-operation in our redemption. Thus, therefore, they communicate in his deiform and human virtues; and this is as far as is possible for them, according to the measure of grace given. Thus far it has been said concerning their illumination. Now, in the third place, he adds concerning perfection.

There follows: « And likewise perfect, not as illumined unto analytic knowledge by the sacred variety, but as filled with the first and supereminent deification, according to the most excellent knowledge — as far as [is possible] in angels — of the divine works. » As if he said: Not only are they to be reckoned pure and illumined; but also perfect in the knowledge received from the habit of virtue, yet not as illumined, or instructed [1056D] [in] analytic knowledge or discipline — that is, resolutory — by the sacred variety, that is, by the manifold sacred teaching of Scripture. For they are not, like men, instructed outwardly by the multiplicity of speech, so that for them, through disputations and distinctions, the things that are hidden and intricate are resolved in knowledge and discipline; but rather they receive to be illumined through simple contemplation within, so that from their cognition nothing of the things that can be known is hidden. Thus, therefore, they are to be reckoned to be perfect, not as illumined unto a resolutive and explicable knowledge through the variety of the sacred discourse; but as filled with the first and supereminent deification — that is, with the divine illumination by which they are illumined before all the other celestial essences, according to the knowledge of the divine works, [1057A] a most excellent knowledge, as far, namely, as it can be excellent in angels.

« For not through other holy essences, but sanctified by the Divinity itself, they are extended immediately into it; they are placed, with a virtue and order supereminent above all, unto an altogether most chaste fortitude, and are led into the contemplation of the immaterial and invisible beauty, as far as is lawful; and unto the knowable reasons of the divine works, as the first essences and [those] around God, they are bent, and by the very Prince of perfection are most exaltedly sanctified. » He proves in sum that those essences receive the divine illumination before all others, and above all other celestial essences; because, not through other holy essences, but sanctified by the Divinity itself, they are extended immediately into it through love and [1057B] exaltation, according to the virtue and order — that is, the grace and dignity — which they have supereminent above all. And so, thus immediately joined to the Divinity, they receive from it alone both purgation, and illumination, and perfection — this is what he says: they are placed, that is, established, unto an altogether most chaste fortitude, or inflexibility — that is, a strong and inflexible and incorruptible chastity. They are also led, through illumination, as far as is lawful — that is, as is permitted or granted — into contemplation, unto the contemplating of the immaterial and invisible beauty of the Creator. They are also bent, as into soft wax, or informed, or instructed, or even perfected unto the knowable reasons of the divine works, as the first essences [1057C] and [those] standing in the nearest place around God, and which are most exaltedly sanctified by the very Prince of perfection — namely the Divinity.

There follows: « This, therefore, the theologians also openly declare: that the subordinate dispositions of the celestial essences are indeed instructed in the deiform sciences by those set over them in an ordered way; but that those higher than all are illumined unto teaching by the Divinity itself, as far as is lawful. » By authority he proves what he had said: namely, that the supreme orders of angels are illumined by the Divinity alone; but that the lower are instructed by the higher. For this the theologians — that is, the prophets, and those holy men who spoke about celestial and divine things — openly declare: that the subordinate dispositions — that is, the lower orders of the celestial essences — [1057D] are instructed in an ordered way, that is, beautifully and fittingly, unto the deiform sciences, by those set over them, that is, by the orders placed above; but that those which are higher than all are illumined unto teaching by the Divinity itself, as far as is lawful — that is, as is possible for them.

There follows: « For some of them they introduce as sacredly instructed by those before them, that the Lord of the celestial powers, and the King of glory, has been received into the heavens according to his humanity. » He sets forth an example where the lower celestial essences were instructed by the higher: namely, where the theologians introduce some of them as instructed by those before them, or by the higher, so that they might know that God, the Lord of the celestial powers, and the King of glory, was received into the heavens according to his humanity. For thus [1058A] it is written that, when the Savior, according to the flesh he had taken, was ascending into heaven, some of the angels, still ignorant of the exaltation of his humanity, and therefore marveling, and saying: « Who is this King of glory? » it was said by others, more fully illumined: « The Lord of powers, he himself is the King of glory. » [n: (Psal. XXIII.)] In which it is proved that at times some are instructed by others.

There follows: « But some [they introduce as] inquiring of Jesus himself, and learning the knowledge of his divine action [performed] for us; and [they introduce] Jesus himself as immediately teaching them, and, bestowing it beforehand upon them, manifesting his human kindness. For I, he says, dispute justice, and the judgment of salvation. » This example is taken from Isaiah, to prove that the supreme orders [1058B] of angels are illumined by God alone unto the knowledge of truth. But some celestial essences alone the theologians themselves introduce as inquiring — that is, making inquiry of Jesus himself; and learning, not from another, but from him who is God, the knowledge of his divine action displayed for us. By the divine action of Jesus himself he means the passion and death which he underwent for us. Which action is therefore called divine, because it was possible for God alone that, through death, he should destroy the power of death. The knowledge of this action, then, they introduce the celestial essences inquiring of Jesus himself, where, beholding the garment of the assumed flesh made bloody with the blood of the passion, they say: « Who is this that comes from Edom, with dyed garments from Bosra? [n: (Isai. LXIII.)] » And thereafter: « Why [1058C] then is thy garment red, and thy raiment like theirs that tread in the winepress? [n: (ibid.)] » Thus, therefore, they introduce them as inquiring and desiring to learn. They also introduce Jesus himself as teaching them immediately, and through his very self, and manifesting to them his human — that is, clement — kindness, which he displayed to us. Which manifestation he confers on them, bestowing it beforehand — that is, granting before all others the knowledge of his operation. « I, » he says, « dispute justice, and the judgment of salvation [n: (ibid.)]. » Justice and the judgment of salvation — that is, of the Savior, or of salvation — signifies the redemption of the human race. In which there was both justice, inasmuch, namely, as the Maker called back his own creature from an alien domination; [1058D] and judgment, inasmuch as he powerfully cast out the devil, the invader of another's right, from the man whom he possessed. And he says that he disputes this justice and judgment for this reason: because that [work] which pertained to the sufferings of the assumed flesh he fulfilled with labor, and as it were with a certain struggle.
