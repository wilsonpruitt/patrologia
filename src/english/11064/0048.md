## BOOK SEVEN. *Exposition of the remaining part of the seventh chapter of the divine Dionysius the Areopagite on the celestial hierarchy, the text of which has been set out above.* (cont.)

There follows: « This, therefore, as far as regards our knowledge, is the first disposition of the celestial essences, standing in the encircling of God, and around God, immediately, and simply, and incessantly encircling his eternal knowledge, according to a most excellent — as far as [is possible] in angels — ever-mobile placement. » He infers from what was said above. As if he said: Since that hierarchy of the highest spirits is first and principally both purged, and illumined, and perfected by the Divinity itself; therefore it is the first and principal [1061D] disposition, or the principal order, of the celestial essences, standing immediately, both in the encircling of, and around, God. For it stands through the immutability of contemplation; but it encircles through the life-giving and unceasing desire of eternal love. Therefore incessantly, because it does not fail from love; therefore simply, because, encircling, it does not withdraw from unity. That unity in the midst is the simplicity of the Divinity, around which and in the encircling of which they are, inasmuch as they immediately draw near to it. In the encircling too, because to his ineffable and hidden incomprehensibility they too are in a certain manner outside, into which they do not at all penetrate. Yet they go about it and desire it; and they strive toward the inner things; and, drawing near by cognition and by love, they become nearest, according to the most excellent placement which [1062A] they have beside God; most excellent, I say, as far as [is possible] in angels — that is, in comparison with that [placement] which is found in all the other angels. And I call the placement 'ever-mobile,' because both by contemplation they do not withdraw, and by desire they ever draw near. For all these reasons, then, this disposition is principal among all the other celestial essences; principal, I say, as far as regards our knowledge — namely, as far as it can be comprehended through our knowledge. He further adds, in praise of this hierarchy, one thing after another, heaping up many lofty and sublime things worthy of divine wonder, and certain profound things, and [things] known only to the pure. Such, he says, is that disposition, or order, of the first hierarchy — nearest in placement, most ardent in desire and intention; [1062B] which, although it cannot penetrate to the whole of what belongs to God, [which is] incomprehensible, yet with eternal love does not cease to go about [it] as far as it can be grasped and comprehended by a creature. But lest perhaps it should seem a pursuit of toil, not of happiness, if it were always to go about [it] and never attain [it], always to seek and never to grasp, always to desire and never to taste, he adds the fruit of the inquiry and the effect of the desire, saying: « Seeing indeed many and blessed contemplations purely, and simple and immediate splendors, illumined, and filled with divine nourishment. [Filled] with many things, indeed, by the first given outpouring, and by the unity — sole, domestic, and unifying — of the divine refreshment; and made worthy by much communion with God, and co-operation with him, unto that [likeness], as is possible, by the likeness of good dispositions [1062C] and actions; and knowing many of the divine things super-positively, and made a partaker, according to what is lawful, of the divine knowledge and cognition. » He said, as best he could say it: Three stand about the Trinity — the Seraphim by love, the Cherubim by cognition, the Thrones by domination. Here is the whole Trinity. Domination in the Father, wisdom in the Son, love in the Holy Spirit. It seemed that he had set the Seraphim above, because charity is supereminent; but behold, we also see that the throne belongs to the Father, and the Thrones are joined with the Father, and love is given to the Holy Spirit. If you think the Thrones subordinate, because they are named after the Seraphim and Cherubim, then think them also set above, because they pertain to the Father, who is named before the Son and the Holy Spirit. But in [1062D] the Trinity there is no degree. Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit is one God. Unity cannot be lower than itself. Therefore that hierarchy of the highest spirits, which is formed first and principally by the highest and supereminent domination of the Divinity, as far as concerns the most excellent likeness by which it imitates the simple unity, has no degree in domination — although, nevertheless, according to that measure of participation and distribution of grace (by which a creature cannot be made equal to the Creator) it has a difference. Therefore the domination of the three is one, by which they are universally set before all things subordinate — after the supreme and supereminent domination of the Deity — [but] are not subordinated to one another. Therefore they all together hold the first place, and they stand about [him], [1063A] each in the nearest placement, so that one does not come between another by position, nor anticipate [another] by participation, nor surpass [another] by domination. Thus is this first disposition, standing in the encircling of God, and around God, immediately — thus properly. And what is the fruit of so great a familiarity?
