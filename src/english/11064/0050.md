## BOOK SEVEN. *Exposition of the remaining part of the seventh chapter of the divine Dionysius the Areopagite on the celestial hierarchy, the text of which has been set out above.* (cont.)

There follows: « Filled with divine nourishment. » What is the divine nourishment? The divine refreshment. And this refreshment, what is it but the love of God? Therefore, when he had said, « that surpassing angelic ordering filled with divine nourishment, » he added also, saying: « filled with the unity of the divine refreshment. » But what is the divine refreshment, if not the divine nourishment? [1066A] Therefore the unity of refreshment and the unity of nourishment is one refreshment and one nourishment. Therefore at the table of God only one dish is set. But do not despise it. The satiety is great. « I shall be satisfied, » says the Psalmist, « when thy glory shall appear [n: (Psal. XVI)]. » There are many things in this world, and all these cannot satisfy the heart of man. But there is one good with God, and when this alone is received, satiety is found. Therefore satiety is not in multitude, but in unity. When is there satiety? When the satiety is enough. Suppose now that of these many things which are in the world you have some few that you love, and you will see that it is not enough. Let more come: still you will not say, it is enough. Let all things be set before you; and you will find yourself still in need, and not yet having enough. In all these things, therefore, there [1066B] can be no satiety, where there can be no enough. But that one good will come; and there will be satiety, because there will be enough. Do not wonder. All these are many, but they are not much. That is one, and it is much. Therefore that sinful woman, because she had many things to be forgiven, loved not many things but much. She let go many things, and chose much. She was wise; she did not regard the heap, but weighed the value. In many things, little; in one thing, much. Therefore do not attend [sic: *atttendas*] to the number, but seek the fruit. There is one good, which God has prepared for you. Do not be afraid when you hear "one"; it is one good, but in that good is every good. He has prepared one refreshment, one food, one dish, one bread — but do not despise it. Hear what is written: « Having every taste, and [1066C] every delight of sweetness [n: (Sap. XV)]. » This is the divine nourishment, and the great outpouring, or abundant effusion, by which outpouring, namely, that blessed society, granted the first place, is filled. There is also the unity of the divine refreshment, by which unity alone — that is, singular and domestic and unifying — it has been made specially worthy. But this one and simple divine refreshment is called singular for this reason, that it is not received with any foreign delight or outside sweetness; domestic, because it is prepared only for friends and members of the household; unifying, because it makes one with itself all those to whom it grants itself to be taken and shared.

There follows: « And by much communion with God and cooperation: » — understood, the ordering is filled, or the [1066D] first hierarchy of the celestial essences. This, I say, has been made worthy of that communion and cooperation with God, as far as is possible for it; by the likeness of good dispositions, that is, of virtues and actions. For by good dispositions it has been made worthy of communion; by good actions, worthy of cooperation. And the order is right. First it is illumined by the brightness of knowledge; then it is refreshed by the sweetness of love, so that thus it may both commune with God in virtue and cooperate in action, as it is written: « If any one love me, he will keep my word; and my Father will love him, and we will come to him, and make our abode with him [n: (Joan. XIV)]. »

There follows: « And knowing many divine things in a supereminent way, and made a partaker of divine knowledge and cognition, [1067A] according to what is lawful. » That is, this hierarchy knows many of the divine things, that is, many things concerning the divine, or concerning God, in a supereminent way, that is, more excellently than the other hierarchies « and it has been made in participation, » that is, it has been made a partaker of divine knowledge and of divine cognition, according to what is lawful for it, that is, granted to it.

There follows: « Therefore theology has handed down its praises also to those who are on earth, in which the eminence of its most exalted illumination is wondrously made manifest. » As if he said: Because so great is the excellence of the hierarchy of the first essences, therefore theology, that is Holy Scripture, has handed down to those who are on earth, that is, to men, its praises or laudations. In which laudations, namely, [1067B] the eminence of its most exalted illumination is wondrously made manifest. As if he said: Because so great is its sublimity, therefore theology has made manifest to men its laudations, by which it praises God, so that through the excellence of the laudation the eminence of the illumination might be made manifest.

There follows: « For some of it, speaking perceptibly, resound like the voice of waters: Blessed be the glory of the Lord from his place. » As if he said: Truly excellent are the laudations of this hierarchy, which are made manifest by Holy Scripture, because some of it, that is certain of them, sounding perceptibly resound, or re-echo, or cry out like the voice of waters: Blessed be the glory of the Lord from his place. Or it can be read thus. Theology has handed down its praises perceptibly, that is, [1067C] by the likeness of sensible and corporeal things, speaking of spiritual things, namely that « some of it. » That is, of its number « resound like the voice of waters. Blessed be the glory of the Lord from his place. » This testimony is taken from Ezekiel, where the prophet records a voice of great commotion made behind him. Which voice, although it is not said there by whom it was uttered, yet this author testifies without ambiguity that it was made by the highest spirits of the celestial orders.

There follows: « But others cry back that most laudable and most holy theology: Holy, holy, holy: the Lord God of Sabaoth. All the earth is full of his glory [n: (Isa. VI)]. » This testimony is taken from Isaiah, where two seraphim, flying, are recorded to have proclaimed this theology, that is, this divine address or laudation, one to [1067D] another, back and forth.

There follows: « But these most exalted hymnologies of the celestial minds we have already, indeed, opened up as far as possible in those things which concern the divine praises. And it has been said of these, in those places, sufficiently as far as we are concerned. » These aforesaid hymnologies, that is, the divine laudations of the celestial minds, that is, of the celestial spirits, he says that he has opened up, or expounded, as far as was possible for him, in other writings, which were made by him concerning the divine praises; and concerning these hymnologies, namely, he testifies that it has been said sufficiently in those (understood) writings, as far as pertained to him, that is, to his capacity. But this [1068A] writing, which he asserts that he made concerning the divine praises, is nowhere found among these his writings which are with us. Yet from this statement we can know that he wrote concerning the divine praises, since in the book *On the Divine Names* he introduced certain things from the amatory hymns of Saint Hierotheus, in which something of the aforesaid praises is treated.

There follows: « Of which it suffices, for a reminder, to say only this much according to the present occasion: that this first ordering has been illumined in theological knowledge, as far as is lawful, by the divine goodness, through which, as a deiform hierarchy, it then handed itself down to others also. » As if he said: Of which, namely of the divine praises sufficiently [1068B] expounded in the aforementioned book, now, according to the present occasion, it suffices to say only this much for a reminder or admonition, namely that this first ordering, that is, the first hierarchy of angels, has been illumined in theological, that is divine, knowledge by the divine goodness itself. Note the construction in that he says, "illumined knowledge." For it is said thus: But I am instructed, I am taught a discipline. He says, therefore, that the first ordering has been illumined unto knowledge by the divine goodness alone; through which knowledge, namely, this ordering handed down itself; then, that is, consequently, to the other hierarchies following after it. « It handed down, » I say, « itself as a deiform hierarchy, » that is, having conformity and likeness to God, and imitating God in this, that, being illumined by him, [1068C] it illumines others, and sets forth itself, as it were, as a form and exemplar of divine conformity. This, therefore, now suffices to say concerning the aforesaid praises: that in them the first ordering of angels, praising its Creator, handed down itself as a form of praising to the other hierarchies following after, « introducing, » that is, suggesting to them, or insinuating, « that by speaking briefly. » For it introduced, or suggested, as it were something hidden, and manifest to few. And it suggested by speaking briefly the other thing which it suggested. And rightly was that suggested only in a hidden way which was invisible, and that spoken only briefly which was ineffable. For however much it were expounded unto knowledge, that would remain hidden which was impenetrable; [1068D] and however much it were extended through speech, it would be brief in relation to it, which was without end. Yet it suggested, and insinuated, and expounded, so that through it there might come to the knowledge of others that which it itself had perceived with no intermediary. What did it suggest?
