## THE TEXT.

[1071B]

*But now we must pass over into the middle ordering of the celestial intellects, exploring those dominations, as far as is possible, with supermundane eyes, and the truly powerful contemplations of the divine powers and virtues. For each appellation, among the essences above us, signifies their deiform properties, imitative of God. Therefore I judge that the manifesting naming of the holy dominations declares a certain absolute anagogy, free from all pedestrian diminishment, and in no way whatsoever universally inclined by any of the tyrannical dissimilitudes, a dominion liberally severe, set above all diminishing servitude, superior to every subjection, and remote [1071C] from every dissimilitude, and unceasingly desirous of dominion, and conforming, as far as is possible, both itself and the things that are after it, in the best and most beautiful way, to the likeness of that very virtue subsisting naturally, turned toward none of the things seen as one, but toward the properly* ὄν *universal, and made, as far as is possible for it, ever in participation of the Lordly deiformity. But that certain strong and unchangeable manliness of the holy virtues, in all their operations according to their deiformity, in no way feebly weakened toward any reception of the divine illuminations imparted to it, powerfully led back into the imitation of God, not abandoning by its own feebleness the deiform motion, but firmly bearing itself into the superessential [1071D] and power-making virtue, and made its image like to the virtue, according to what is permitted, and powerfully turned toward it indeed as toward the principal virtue: but toward the second things, giving [dans et] virtue, coming forth deiformly. But that ordination of the holy powers, equal in power to the divine dominations and virtues, well-ordered and unconfused about the divine receptions, and the ordered rank of the supermundane and intellectual power, not tyrannically [1072B] hurled down by its power-wielding virtues into the things that are lower, but powerfully led back into the divine things after the well-ordered ones, and leading back deiformly the things that are after it, and assimilated, as far as is lawful, to the power-making causal power, and revealing it, as is possible, to the angels in the ranks well-ordered through it by its power-wielding virtue. Having these deiform properties, the middle ordering of the celestial minds is indeed purged, and illumined, and perfected, as has been said, by the divine illuminations imparted to it in the second place through the first hierarchical ordering, and conveyed through that middle one by a second manifestation. And so, by the utterance passing from one angel [and] heard in another angel, we shall make a symbol of the perfection which is from afar above [all], and [1072C] hidden through the procession into the things that follow. For those wise concerning our sacred immolations say that the plenitudes of the divine things, shining through themselves, are more perfect than [those] through other contemplative participations. So too I judge that the immediate participation of the angelic orders first extended into God is more perfect through the mediation of the perfected. On which account, by our priestly tradition, the first intellects are named the perfective, light-bringing, and purgative virtues of the lower ones, as of those led back through them into the superessential principle of all, and made partakers of the mystical purgations, and illuminations, and perfections, according to what is lawful for them. For *Poc* has been divinely promulgated altogether by the divine ordination: that the second things participate in the divine [1072D] illuminations through the first. And you will find this too expressed many times by the theologians. For when the divine and paternal humanity, correcting Israel nurturingly for its sacred salvation, and handing it over into correction to avenging and monstrous nations, by an all-kinds conveyance of things provided for unto the better, both dismissed the captivity and clemently led it back to its former beneficence, one of the theologians, Zachariah, saw one of the first — as I judge — and of the angels about God (for this angelic appellation is common [1073A] to all, as I said) learning from God himself concerning this the consoling, as has been said, words. But another of the subject angels coming forth to meet the first, as to the reception and perception of illumination, then instructed by him in the divine counsel as by a high priest, and turned to teach this to the theologian, namely that Jerusalem shall be fruitfully inhabited by a multitude of men [n: (Zach. I)]*. *But another of the theologians, Ezekiel [n: (Ezech. VIII)]*, *[says] that this too, he says, was most sacredly promulgated by the most glorious divinity established above the cherubim. For Israel itself, as has been said — the leader of the host, the humanity, conveying it unto the better through disciplines, divine justice being rendered to those who correct — justified the corrected. This [1073B] is taught: the first after the cherubim, girt about the loins with sapphire, who was clothed in the podere according to the hierarchic symbol. But the rest of the angels, who had axes, the divine ordination commands to be taught by the prior one concerning this divine judgment [n: (Ezech. X)]*. *For to him indeed it said to pass through the midst of Jerusalem, and to give a sign on the foreheads of the corrected men; to the others: Go out into the city after him, and strike, and spare not your eyes; but to all, upon whom the sign is, do not draw near [n: (Ezech. IX)]*. *What, perhaps, would one say of the angel saying to Daniel: The word went forth; or of that first one receiving fire from the midst of the cherubim [n: (Dan. IX)]*. *Or that [saying] of the same, which [is] more abundantly toward the showing of the angelic order, namely that [1073C] the cherub also puts fire into the hands of the one clothed in the holy stole [n: (Ezech. X)]*; *or of the one calling the most divine Gabriel, and saying to him: « Make him understand the vision [n: (Zach. I)]*; » *or whatever other things have been said by the sacred theologians concerning the deiform adornment of the celestial hierarchies. To which the ordination of our hierarchy, assimilated as far as is possible, will have the angelic beauty, in so far as it is formed in characters through it, and led back to the superessential of the whole hierarchy at once.*
