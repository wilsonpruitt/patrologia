## THE EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « But that certain strong and unchangeable manliness of the holy virtues, in all their operations according to their deiformity, in no way feebly weakened toward any reception of the divine illuminations imparted to them, powerfully led back into the imitation of God: not abandoning by its own feebleness the deiform motion, but firmly bearing itself into the superessential and power-making virtue, and made its image like to the virtue, according to what is permitted, and powerfully turned toward it indeed as toward the principal virtue. But toward the second virtue giving and coming forth deiformly. » That, he says, of the holy virtues (understood: the appellation) [1076D] I judge to declare a certain strong and unchangeable manliness in all their operations: a manliness, I say, imparted to them according to their deiformity, because in this they are like, or conformed, to God, that they have merited to receive a strong and unchangeable manliness, or virtue, for the doing of all things. For the divine virtue is both strong for acting, so that it is not broken by the difficulty of any work, and unchangeable for persevering, so that it does not grow faint or be weakened by the length of duration. In this, therefore, the virtues are deiform, and therefore they are alike in that for the doing of all things they have a strong and unchangeable vigor. Nor do they only have a manliness strong and unchangeable for doing all things beneath them, but they have also a manliness or virtue not feebly weakened [1077A] toward that reception of the divine illuminations imparted to it. For they are strong in the execution of works, and strong also in the reception of gifts, because their virtue, even if it be weak when compared to the divine virtue, is nevertheless not weak when conformed to the divine. It is weak by its condition for grasping the whole that is proper to the Creator; but it is not weak by corruption for receiving the whole that is owed to a creature. It is not, therefore, feebly weakened: for even if it does not have a virtue equal to the Creator, it nevertheless has a strength sufficient for a creature. Therefore it has a manliness strong and unchangeable for the execution of things to be done, and not weakened for the reception of gifts; and powerfully led back, that is, turned or [1077B] reformed in the imitation of God, that is, that it may imitate God; led back indeed through illuminations, into imitation through operations. And they have a manliness not abandoning the deiform motion by any feebleness of their own. He calls it "deiform motion," that these celestial virtues are moved unceasingly, that they may imitate God; either from above, namely by desiring to receive what he has, that they may be the same with him; or downward, by doing what he does, that they may not depart from him. And so they have a manliness not abandoning the deiform motion; but through that deiform motion firmly bearing or exalting itself into the superessential and power-making virtue of the Creator: and through this made an image like to the virtue of the Creator himself, according to what is permitted, that is, according to what [1077C] is granted to them, or possible. For in this, that their manliness is raised through the deiform motion into the superessential and power-making virtue of the Creator, it is proven to become the image and likeness of that same divine virtue. Therefore, he says, I judge their manliness to be made an image like to the divine virtue, and turned toward that divine virtue, inasmuch as toward the principal virtue; but toward the second things giving virtue, and coming forth deiformly. Thus, he says, the appellation of the virtues teaches that their manliness is turned toward the higher virtue of God, that from it, as from the first, it may receive deiformity; and it teaches also that it comes forth or proceeds toward the second things, that is, toward the subsequent orders, that it may come forth to them deiformly, giving them virtue, and in that virtue [1077D] conformity to God, so that it both receives that deiformity from the superior and bestows it on the inferiors: receiving through gift, bestowing through example.

There follows: « But that of the holy powers, equal in power to the divine dominations and virtues, well-ordered and unconfused about the divine receptions by its ordination, and the ordered rank of the supermundane and intellectual power, not tyrannically hurled down by power-wielding virtues into the things that are lower, but powerfully led back into the divine things after the well-ordered ones: and leading back deiformly the things after it, and assimilated, as far as is lawful, to the power-making causal power: and revealing it, as is possible, to the angels in the ranks well-ordered through it by its power-wielding [1078A] virtue. » After the appellation of the virtues he passes on to the appellation of the powers. But that of the holy powers (understood: the naming) I judge to declare a certain ordination of theirs, equal in power to the divine dominations and virtues, that is, an ordination — I say — of equal power with the dominations and virtues, well-ordered and unconfused about the divine receptions, that is, about the divine gifts to be received, because the gifts of God, which it receives for the adornment of spiritual beauty, it guards without confusion, humbly persisting in its own degree and order. I judge also that this naming of the powers declares the ordered rank, that is, the ordination of the supermundane and intellectual power: a power, I say, not tyrannically hurled down into the things [1078B] that are lower, with power-wielding virtues, that is, with violent force. But rather a power led back, or powerfully turned, into the divine things after the well-ordered ones, that is, after the virtues, which are ordered before it: and a power, I say, leading back deiformly, that is, according to the divine likeness, the things that are after it, that is, those orders of angels which follow after it. For just as it itself is turned to the likeness of God by the superiors meditating, so, with it mediating, the things that are lower are called back to the same likeness of God. There follows further: The powers assimilated, as far as is lawful, that is, either permitted or granted, to the power-making, causal power; because it can do all things, and is the cause of all. And a power, I say, revealing, namely that divine [1078C] power, as far as it is possible to reveal it to the angels. Revealing how? By its power-wielding virtue. Where revealing? In the ranks well-ordered through it, that is, in the subsequent orders, which it adorns, by revealing to them the divine power by its own example.

There follows: « Having these deiform properties, the middle ordering of the celestial minds is indeed purged, and illumined, and perfected, as has been said, by the divine illuminations imparted to it in the second place through the first hierarchical ordering, and conveyed through that middle one by a second manifestation. » The middle ordering of the celestial minds, he says — of which, namely, treatment has thus far been made, which consists of dominations and virtues and powers: this, having the deiform properties, namely those [1078D] which we said above, is purged and illumined and perfected, just as was said above concerning the first hierarchy; purged, I say, by the divine illuminations imparted to it in the second (understood: place) through the first hierarchical ordering. The first hierarchical ordering consists of the three first orders, that is, seraphim, cherubim, and thrones: which in the first place receives the divine illuminations from the divinity itself: which illuminations afterward being conveyed by a second manifestation, through that middle one, to the second hierarchy, this second hierarchy is in the second place purged, and illumined, and perfected, through those same divine illuminations.

There follows: « And so, by the utterance passing [1079A] from one angel [and] heard in another angel, we shall make a symbol of the perfection which is from afar above [all] perfect, and hidden through the procession into the things that follow. » By example and authority he proves what he had said, namely that the middle ordering of angels is illumined by the first, or through the first, that is, what he says: We shall make a symbol, that is an example, or a demonstration, of the perfection, namely the divine perfection perfect from afar above all things: and hidden, through the procession, into the things that follow; because, although in itself it is hidden and invisible, yet through the procession it goes forth and is made manifest in the things that follow, that is, in those who are immediately after it in the first place, or in those who follow after them. Of this kind of divine perfection, I say — perfect in itself and hidden, and yet, through the bestowal of grace, proceeding into the things that follow [1079B] — we shall make a symbol: the utterance, that is, the saying or word brought forth by one angel, and afterward passing into another angel, and heard by him. For in this, that one angel spoke, another heard, one brought forth the teaching, another received it: a symbol has been made, that is a demonstration or argument, that the divine perfection proceeds, to be participated, through the prior and superior ones to the following and inferior ones. « For those wise concerning our sacred immolations say that the plenitudes of the divine things, shining through themselves, are more perfect than [those] through other contemplative participations. » As if he said: It is no wonder if among the angels one taught and another was taught, since one is more learned than another. For those who are wise concerning our sacred immolations, [1079C] that is, concerning our sacrifices or sacraments, they say that the plenitudes of the divine things, that is, those angels who are full of divine gifts, or illuminations, and shine through themselves, are more perfect than the contemplative participations through others, that is, than those participating by contemplative participations through others. For he put the genitive for the ablative. Those who are wise, he says, or skilled in our sacraments, testify that those angelic spirits who receive the divine contemplation immediately through themselves are more perfect than the rest, who, with the superiors mediating, participate in the same contemplation.

There follows: « So too I judge that the immediate participation of the angelic orders first extended [1079D] into God is more perfect through the mediation of the perfected. » Because, he says, the wise testify this, therefore I too judge that the immediate participation of the angelic orders first extended into God — that is, those angelic orders which, in the first place and principally extended into God, participate in him immediately — is more perfect, that is, that they are more perfect in participation than the perfected who participate through mediation, that is, than the perfected participating through mediation, that is, that they are more perfect than those who are made perfect by others mediating.

There follows: « On which account, by our priestly tradition, the first intellects are named the perfective, light-bringing, and purgative virtues of the lower ones; as of those led back through themselves into the superessential principle of all, [1080A] and made partakers of the mystical purgations, and illuminations, and perfections, according to what is lawful for them. » As if he said: Because the superior spirits are more perfect than the inferior and following ones, therefore by our priestly tradition the first intellects, that is, the supreme orders of angels, are called the perfective, and light-bringing, and purgative virtues of the inferiors, because through them the inferiors are perfected and purged and illumined: they are named the virtues of the inferiors, inasmuch as these are led back through themselves, that is, through their own selves, into the superessential principle of all, that is, God; and the inferiors, I say, made in participation, because they have been made partakers, as far as is lawful for them, of the mystical purgations, and illuminations, and perfections. [1080B] Through those superiors, therefore, by whom they are purged, and illumined, and perfected.

There follows: « For this has been divinely promulgated altogether by the divine ordination: that the second things participate through the first in the divine illuminations: this, he says, has been divinely promulgated, » or made manifest, « altogether, » that is, universally, concerning the divine ordination — that the second things everywhere participate in the divine illuminations through the first.

There follows: « But you will find this too expressed many times by the theologians: » this, he says — that I say the second things participate in the divine illuminations through the first — you will find expressed, that is, manifestly magnified and marked in the Scriptures, namely the sacred ones: by the theologians, that is, those who have reasoned [1080C] concerning divine things and written.
