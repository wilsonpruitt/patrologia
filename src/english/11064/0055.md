## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « For when the divine and paternal humanity, in a nurturing way correcting Israel for the sake of its holy salvation, and handing it over for correction to avenging and savage nations, by a universal translation of the things provided-for unto the better: both dismissed the captivity, and mercifully led it back to its former beneficence: one of the theologians, Zechariah, saw one of the first angels, as I suppose, and of those about God (for this angelic surname is, as I have said, common to all), learning from God himself, concerning this matter, consoling words, as has been said. But another of the subject angels coming to meet the first, as if unto the reception and perception of illumination; [1080D] then instructed by him in the divine counsel as by a high priest: and this angel, being turned, teaches the theologian, that Jerusalem is fruitfully inhabited by a multitude of men. » He proves by an example what he had said. In the prophet Zechariah it is read that an angel, having received a command from God, went forth, and gave a command to another angel meeting him, that he should run and announce to the prophet that Jerusalem was as yet inhabited without walls. For this is what he says: « When the divine and paternal humanity, » that is, the paternal mercy of God, « in a nurturing way correcting, » that is, in the manner of a foster-father who scourges his little son for this end, that he may chastise him, correcting Israel with the scourge of the Babylonian captivity for the sake of its holy salvation, namely so that, while for a time he outwardly dispersed it in bodily fashion, he might inwardly [1081A] save it by restoring it unto holiness. And so thus the mercy of God, in a fatherly way correcting Israel and handing it over unto correction — that is, handing it over to the nations that it might be corrected, namely to the Chaldeans: nations, I say, both avenging and savage; because they both avenged the injury done to God, and yet did this not out of zeal for justice but out of the fury of cruelty. Handing over, I say, by a translation; namely that they might be carried over from their homeland into exile, from their own land into a foreign land, from Jerusalem into Babylon, from the vision of peace into the captivity of confusion: by a translation, I say, that is universal, that is, general, of all the things provided-for unto the better — that is, unto the salvation of the predestined. For indeed through this captivity and leading-back, which was done bodily in one people, [1081B] there was shown forth a figure of all the elect, and of those to be freed from the captivity of sin. When therefore the divine mercy, in a fatherly way handing over Israel by such a translation, afterward both dismissed the captivity, and mercifully led Israel itself back to its former beneficence. Then « one of the theologians, namely Zechariah, saw one of the first angels, » that is, one of the first angels, that is, one of the excellent and superior angels, and of those set about God. He says, « one of the first, as I suppose: » for theology does not say this, that that one was of the first. But I suppose that that one was of the first, because he did not receive a command from another prior to him, but gave a command to the second. Therefore, he says, « I suppose » that that one was of the first angels. How [1081C] of the first angels, since the angels are not first, seeing that only those who are last and lowest are named angels? Not so, he says, do I mean 'angel.' But according to this, that this angelic surname is sometimes common to all, as was said above, I now call the supreme orders angels. And so thus one of the theologians, Zechariah, saw one of the first angels learning from God himself consoling words, namely concerning this matter of the captivity and the leading-back of Israel, as was said in the prophecy, namely of Zechariah himself. He saw also another of the subject angels coming forth to meet the first angel, that is, going out, or coming before, that is, running on ahead before he is called, as one ready and [1081D] devoted of himself unto the reception and perception of his illumination, which he was about to receive from him. Then Zechariah himself saw that second angel instructed in the counsel by the first angel himself as by a high priest, namely as by a superior, and one giving holy things: and he saw also that same second angel afterward turned, teaching the theologian this — that is, turned away from the first angel, to whom he was attending, toward the theologian, that is, toward Zechariah himself, that he might teach him this, namely the divine counsel which he himself had learned from the first angel: but the first angel from God — and this is the counsel which he himself announced, « that fruitfully, » that is, abundantly, « Jerusalem shall be inhabited » by a multitude of men. Another translation says: « Without walls [1082A] shall Jerusalem be inhabited. » Under the same sense assuredly, in that after the slaughter of the captivity that people would, by divine grace, be multiplied to such a degree that the multitude of those dwelling in Jerusalem could not be enclosed within the circuit of its walls.

There follows: « But the other of the theologians, Ezekiel: and, he says, this was most sacredly promulgated to the cherubim by that same most glorious divinity, established firmly above. » By the testimony of the other theologian he proves what he said, namely that the divine counsels, immediately received by the first angels, are then carried through the lower angels even unto the minds of the theologians — that is, what he says: « The other of the theologians, Ezekiel, » namely that this divine counsel was most sacredly promulgated to the cherubim by the most glorious divinity itself, established firmly above [1082B]: this, he says. Ezekiel says that the most glorious divinity itself, established firmly above in majesty, in eternity, most sacredly promulgated or revealed its counsel to the cherubim, from whom it was afterward manifested more fully to the following orders? « For humanity itself — as has been said, the leader of the army — translating Israel unto the better through chastisements, is answered by divine Justice to those who correct, [and] justified the corrected. » Humanity, he says, that is, mercy, the leader of the army, or the mistress of the exercise, translating Israel itself unto the better through disciplines, condemned the guilty, justified the corrected; and this, namely the judgment, is answered out of divine justice to the correcting angels, who had been sent to punish: because for this reason the divine mercy brought in the scourges, that [1082C] it might condemn the reprobate, and justify the corrected. Or the divine justice itself is answered to those who correct, in that which is just, that the reprobate be condemned, and the corrected be justified. Or, to the correcting Chaldeans, that is, the persecutors through whom God scourged and corrected his people, it is answered out of divine justice, that they may receive as they have deserved; but the divine mercy justified the corrected Israelites. « This is taught to the first after the cherubim, girded about the loins with sapphire, who was clothed with the *podere* according to the hierarchical symbol: » this, he says, divine counsel of condemnation and justification « is taught to the one set first after the cherubim, who was clothed with the *podere* according to the hierarchical symbol. » For the fact that after the cherubim a certain one [1082D] appeared girded about the loins with sapphire, and clothed with the *podere*, was a hierarchical symbol, that is, a hierarchical figure, because the hierarchy uses visible figures for the demonstration of invisible things. And not only is he who was first after the cherubim taught the divine counsel, but also the rest who follow. For this is what he subjoins: But the divine ordinance commands that the rest of the angels, who had axes, be taught concerning this the divine judgment by the one prior to them. Namely concerning this counsel, or concerning this work, the divine ordinance commands the cherubim themselves « that the rest of the angels be taught by him » who was first after the cherubim, so that in that order the divine precept might first descend into the cherubim, then through the cherubim to him who was next, clothed with the *podere*, through whom it might at last come to the following [1083A] angels, who were made ready with axes.

There follows: « For to him indeed he said to pass through the midst of Jerusalem, and to give a sign upon the foreheads of the corrected men: » — either 'to him,' that is, the divine ordinance said to the cherubim, that he should command the one following him to pass through the midst of Jerusalem, etc. Or 'to him,' that is, to the one following after the cherubim, through the cherubim itself he « said to pass through the midst of Jerusalem, » etc. And not only did he give the precept to that one through the cherubim, but also to the others following after him — this is what is added: « To the others: Go forth into the city after him, and strike; and do not spare your eyes. But to all upon whom is the sign, draw not near. » But all these things, which have been taken from the prophetic visions, [1083B] avail for this, that it may be shown that the first and highest angels are instructed by God himself, the second by the first, the third by the second.
