## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « What perhaps might someone say of the angel saying to Daniel: The word went forth; or of that first one himself receiving fire from the midst of the cherubim? or that saying of the same, which is more abundantly for the showing of the angelic order; namely that the cherubim also sends fire into the hands of the one clothed with the holy stole; or of the one calling the most divine Gabriel, and saying to him: Make him understand the vision. Or whatsoever other things have been said by the sacred theologians concerning the deiform adornment of the celestial hierarchies, unto which the ordination of our hierarchy, assimilated according to what is possible, will have the angelic beauty, as far as in figures, [1083C] being formed through it and led back to the superessential at once of every hierarchy. » In these prophetic testimonies, as we said above, he intends to prove this alone: that among the angels some teach others, and the lower receive precept and doctrine from the higher; or also when men are instructed by the angels, and the lower receive knowledge from the higher. For what might one — that is, someone — say of the angel speaking to Daniel, except that the lower is instructed by the higher, and receives doctrine? Or again, what might someone say of that first one, mentioned above after the cherubim, clothed with the *podere*, and receiving fire from the midst of the cherubim, except this likewise, that one is shown to receive from another that which [1083D] he cannot have of himself? Or what else also is that saying of the same — namely of the vision, or of the example — which is more abundantly, or more copiously, for the showing of the angelic order, that is, for showing that among the angels some superior orders are set over others, as I say, that the cherubim sends fire into the hands of the one clothed with the holy stole, except that manifestly a dispensation is demonstrated to be made through [1084A] one into another? That testimony, indeed, in the vision of Ezekiel, from which this is taken, is rather conjectured than expounded; because from the fact that that first one after the cherubim, clothed with the holy stole, or *podere*, is bidden to receive fire from the midst of the cherubim, he is believed, not unfittingly, to have received that same fire from the cherubim itself. Concerning this, therefore, what will anyone be able to say, except that it is manifestly shown that the divine bounty passes through some unto others? Or again, of the angel calling the most divine Gabriel, and saying to him: Make him understand the word [visionem]: what will anyone be able to say, except that, according to the ordination of the same disposition, the divine precept descends through some unto others; and those who are superior [1084B] command to the inferior the things to be done, even as they insinuate the things to be known? Concerning these things therefore, or also concerning all the others, whatsoever other things have been said by the sacred theologians concerning the deiform adornment of the celestial hierarchies, what will anyone be able to say, except what has been said above? Unto which adornment of the celestial hierarchies, namely, the ordination of our — that is, of the human, or Ecclesiastical — hierarchy being assimilated, as far as is possible for it (namely, to be assimilated), will have from that very likeness the angelic beauty, as far as in figures, that is, in figures and likenesses. For as much as it now has in the figure of likeness, so much will it afterward have in the loftiness of glorification. Now indeed it has the character and the sign in figure; then it will have the reality and the truth in glory. Certain men have tried to interpret these characters [1084C] in a strange way; setting before those who contemplate, in that future glory of the supernal blessedness, certain phantasms and images of errors in place of God, asserting that God is never visible to anyone in his own substance. But we do not await blessedness in the contemplation of figures, to whom the very truth has been promised. Therefore the disposition of our hierarchy, formed unto the angelic adornment, as much as it now has of likeness in characters and figure, so much will it afterward have in the glory of beauty. Now indeed it is formed through it: that is, both according to it, namely the angelic adornment, and by the mediation of that same angelic adornment, led back to the superessential at once of every hierarchy — that is, [1084D] restored unto the likeness of God, who is the superessential of every hierarchy; because from him every hierarchy has not only form, but also substance; because he himself not only instituted every hierarchy, but also created it, so that it might be after him in likeness, under him in condition.
