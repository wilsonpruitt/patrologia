## THE TEXT.

[1083D]

*The adornment remaining to us for contemplation, [1084D] concluding the angelic hierarchies disposed by the deiform principalities, archangels, and angels. And first indeed [1085A] I judge it necessary to speak, according to what is impossible for me, the manifestations of their sacred surnames. For it manifests, indeed, that deiform principal eductive of the celestial principalities, together with the sacred order and the most becoming principal powers, and that they are universally converted unto the superprincipal principle, and lead others hierarchically, and are, as far as possible, formed unto that very principial principle, and that it manifests his superessential ordination and the adornment of the principal powers. But that of the holy archangels is indeed of equal power with the celestial principalities. For there is, both of them and of the angels, as I have said, one hierarchy and disposition. Nevertheless, since indeed there is no hierarchy not having also first, and middle, and last powers, the [1085B] holy order of the archangels is received into the communicative hierarchical middle of the extremes. For it communicates with the most sacred principalities, and with the holy angels. With the former indeed, because it is principally converted unto the superessential principle, and is, as far as possible, reformed unto it, and unifies the angels according to its well-adorned, and ordered, and invisible leadings. But with the latter, because it is to them also a prophetic order, receiving the divine illuminations hierarchically through the first powers, and announcing them deiformly to the angels, and manifesting them through the angels to us according to the sacred analogy of each of those divinely illumined. For the angels themselves, as we have said before, completively consummate all the dispositions of the celestial minds, according to what is to be consummated: inasmuch as, in the celestial essences, [1085C] having the angelic property, and more fittingly named angels among us than the prior ones, in so far as, concerning that which is more manifest, there is to them a hierarchy, and more of the circum-adornment. For it is to be judged that the most excellent disposition, as itself firstly and orderly approaching the hidden one, secretly, forming [formantem], ordains the second. But the second, which is completed by the holy dominations, and virtues, and powers, presides over that hierarchy which is of the principalities, and archangels, and angels. The first hierarchy indeed more manifestly; but that which is after it, more hiddenly. But the manifestative disposition of the principalities, archangels, and angels commands the human hierarchies through the consequent things, so that there be through order an ascent unto God, and conversion, [1085D] and communication, and unity. And indeed also, from God, best imparted to all the hierarchical ones, and communicatively supervening, and a procession with most sacred adornment. Thence theology distributes our hierarchy to the angels, naming Michael the prince of the people of the Jews [n: (Dan. X)]*, *and others, diverse, of the nations. For the Most High established the bounds of the nations according to the number of the angels of God [n: (Deut. XXXII)]*. *But if anyone should say, and how was the people of the Hebrews alone led back into the divine illuminations? it must be answered that the right dominations of the angels are not to be accused of the error of the other nations unto non-existent gods, but those very ones, by their own inflexions, falling away from that right leading-back which is unto the divine, by their own love, and by pride [1086A] of the things divinely seen by them, and, in a co-rational manner, unto worship. This is reported that the people of the Hebrews itself also suffered. For thou hast repelled the knowledge of God, he says, and hast gone forth after thy own heart [n: (Ose. IV; Jer. II)]*. *For neither do we have a coerced life; nor are the divine lights of provident illumination revealed through the proper power of those who provide, but the dissimilarity of the intellectual visions makes the donation of light, out of the superfull paternal goodness, either wholly not participated, not distributed unto their reformation, or makes the participations differing — small or great, obscure or clear — of the one and simple, and ever holding itself in the same mode, and superexpended fontal ray. Then, because also to the other nations, out of which we also have looked back unto that, [there is] prepared unto tradition and open the [ray] of divine light, and the great, [1086B] and copious sea, no alien gods indeed ruled: but the one principle of all, and unto it the angels, exercising the priesthood according to each several nation, led back their followers. Melchizedek is to be understood as a high priest, existing most friendly to the Lord — not of the non-existent, but of the truly existent, most high God. For thus simply the theosophists themselves called Melchizedek not only the friend of God, but also priest [n: (Gen. XIV)]*. *Or, that to the wise they might openly signify, that he converted not only out of them unto that God who truly is. But still also to others, as a high priest, he brings forth those things which are of the leading-back unto the true and sole divinity. And this, moreover, we shall admonish thy most-priestly understanding: that both with the angel ruling over Pharaoh among the Egyptians themselves [1086C] [n: (Gen. XLI)]*, *and with the prince of the Babylonians having his own presiding one — with all providence, both provident and empowered of domination, distributed according to the divisions — and for those nations there were established leaders, ministers of the true God, by the manifestation of the vision of the formations from the angels; the [visions] revealed continually by the angels, from God through the angels, to the holy men Joseph and Daniel [n: (Dan. II)]*. *For there is one principle and providence of all. And in no way is it to be judged that the Jews indeed spoke of the divinity fully; but that the angels specially, or equally honorably, or oppositely, or that certain gods, one over another, rule the other nations. But that saying also is to be received according to the sacred understanding itself, not as though God, together with other gods, or angels, contending, suffered our leadership — the principate in Israel, and the leadership of the nation Israel. [1086D] But as, that same one and lofty providence at once salutarily distributing all men by the restorative hand-leadings of their proper angels, Israel almost alone beyond all being converted unto the illumination and knowledge of the man of God. Whence theology, signifying that he possessed Israel unto the service of the true God, said, He became the portion of the Lord [n: (Psal. CXXXIV; Deut. XXXII; Isa. XIX)]*. *But showing that he also distributed, man by man, to the rest of the nations, [and] to a certain one of the holy angels, Michael, for the knowing through him of the one principle of all, said that he led the Jewish people [n: (Dan. X)]*. *openly teaching us that there is one providence of all, superessentially set above at once all the invisible and visible powers. [1087A] And all the angels ruling throughout the several nations extend their several powers unto him, as unto their proper principle, following him by an essential will*
