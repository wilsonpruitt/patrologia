## EXPOSITION.

« The adornment remaining to us for contemplation, concluding the angelic hierarchies disposed by the deiform principalities, archangels, and angels. » The words are plain. The adornment, he says, concluding the angelic hierarchies, disposed by the deiform principalities, archangels, and angels, is remaining to us — that is, it now remains for us to contemplate or consider the adornment, that is, the beautiful disposition of the angelic hierarchy, which is, as it were, the conclusion of the two preceding, and the end, in which three orders are disposed, that is, principalities, angels, and archangels.

[1087B]There follows: « And first indeed I judge it necessary to speak, according to what is possible for me, the manifestations of their holy surnames. » I, he says, judge it to be necessary that I first speak the manifestations of their surnames, so that through the interpretation of the surnames their properties may be manifested. This therefore he judges must first be expounded: why in this last hierarchy some are named principalities, others archangels, others angels, because from the very distinct surname the properties of each are manifested. This is what he adds: « For it manifests, indeed, that deiform principal eductive of the celestial principalities, together with the sacred order and the most becoming principal powers, [1087C] and that they are universally converted unto the superprincipal principle, and lead others hierarchically; and that they are, as far as possible, formed unto that very principial principle, and that it manifests his superessential ordination, and the adornment of the principal powers. » He expounds what the several surnames in this last hierarchy signify: and first he treats of the surname of the principalities, showing that this very surname manifests that principal eductive, that is, that principal leadership which they have, deiformly, that is, [in] the likeness of God; because just as God is the one and highest prince of all, so they themselves are the princes and leaders of the subject angels and men. This [1087D] leadership, therefore, the surname itself manifests together with the sacred order and the most becoming principal powers, because they have that leadership both from the sacred order and from the principal powers. For that they are leaders and princes of others, they have both from this, that they have been ordained to this by office, and from this, that by their principal powers they are more excellent than the rest.

There follows: « And that they are universally converted unto the superprincipal principle; » this also, he says, the surname of the principalities manifests, namely those powers which they themselves have: that they are universally converted unto the superprincipal principle, that is, God. And their surname also signifies that they lead other powers, or the powers of others, unto that very [1088A] principial principle, [and], as far as is possible, that those powers are formed unto it, or conformed to it: to lead, I say, hierarchically, that is, according to the manner and measure of their own precedence. For their own powers they lead unto their principle by universally converting; the powers of others they lead unto their principle by hierarchically preceding; because they have to lead only in so far as they ought to precede or preside. Therefore they lead hierarchically, because in that in which they are not set above, they cannot rightly be leaders. This therefore their surname manifests: namely that they have a principate in the likeness of God, from office and from power; and that they convert both their own powers unto their principle, and lead the powers of others. Their surname also manifests the adornment [1088B] of the principal powers themselves — that is, the disposition of the principalities themselves manifests the superessential ordination of that first principle. For from the very surname of the principalities it is intimated that in their precedence the first Principate is manifested, because by ruling they both imitate it and manifest it.

There follows: « But that of the holy archangels is indeed of equal power with the celestial principalities. For there is, both of them and of the angels, as I have said, one hierarchy and disposition. » That surname (it is understood) « of the holy archangels is of equal power, » that is, signifies an equal power, or an equal potency, « with the celestial principalities; » because there is one hierarchy and one disposition of the three orders, [1088C] that is, of the angels, and of them, namely the archangels, and of the principalities. And although from this — that the hierarchy is one — there is also one and equal power of the three: nevertheless, because there is no hierarchy which does not have first, and middle, and last orders, therefore the archangels, set in the middle place between the principalities, who are first, and the angels, who are last, participate in the property of both. This is what he says: « Nevertheless, since indeed there is no hierarchy not having also first, and middle, and last powers, the holy order of the archangels is received communicatively into the middle of the extremes of the hierarchy. » Since, he says, there is no hierarchy which does not have first, and middle, and last powers: [1088D] therefore « the holy order of the archangels is received communicatively into the middle of the extremes of the hierarchy: » that is, it is received so as to be in the middle of the extremes, communicating with the hierarchy, that is, with their sacred power; or it is received into the hierarchical middle, that is, for the possessing of the hierarchical middle, which is communicative of the extremes — this is what follows: « For it communicates both with the most sacred principalities, and with the holy angels. » But in what it communicates with each, he shows subsequently, saying: « With the former indeed, because it is principally converted unto the superessential principle, and is, as far as possible, reformed unto it; and it unifies the angels according to its well-adorned, and ordered, and invisible leadings. » In this, he says, the order of the archangels participates with [1089A] the principalities in the same power, because « it is converted unto its superessential principle, and is reformed unto it, » as far as is possible for it: and because it also unifies — that is, gathers unto the unity of that same principle — by leading the angels after itself according to invisible and ordered leadings, by which it has to lead those subject and following ones. In this, therefore, the order of the archangels communicates with the power of the principalities: that in the likeness, or imitation, of them it both converts itself unto its first principle, and gathers those following it. And not only with the principalities set above, but also with the angels established below, does the order of the archangels itself communicate. This is what he adds: « But with the latter, because it is to them also a prophetic order, receiving the divine illuminations hierarchically through the first [1089B] powers: and announcing them deiformly to the angels: and manifesting them through the angels to us according to the sacred analogy of each of those divinely illumined. » With the former, he says, that is, with the principalities, the order of the archangels communicates, as we said above. But with the latter, that is, with the angels, it communicates in this: that it is to them also a prophetic order — that is, an order prophetic, and ordained for prophesying — prophesying to them, as to inferiors, the divine secrets, receiving indeed the divine illuminations through the first powers, that is, through the principalities, who are prior and superior: itself receiving, I say, « hierarchically, » that is, according to what the hierarchical order and disposition requires, namely that the inferior receive their illuminations from the superior. « Receiving, » [1089C] I say, « and announcing them, » namely the illuminations which it receives from the superiors, to the inferior angels: but announcing deiformly, so that in this — that it receives from the superiors — it may keep the hierarchical disposition; but in this — that it imparts to the inferiors — it may not depart from conformity to God, who, the highest of all, bestows his gifts upon all subject things to be participated. For to receive illumination from a superior is a hierarchical thing, but to give to an inferior is a divine thing. And through this the archangelic order, established in the middle according to the disposition of the hierarchy, participates both with the superiors and with the inferiors — receiving the divine illuminations from the superior principalities, and announcing them to the inferior angels: and through the angels [1089D] announcing to us, as though to the last and lowest, those same divine illuminations according to the sacred analogy of each of those divinely illumined, that is, according to the manner and measure of the capacity of each of us for perceiving that same divine illumination; or, announcing to us through the angels according to the sacred analogy of each of them, namely of the angels divinely illumined — that is, they announce the divine illumination through each one only in so far as each is fit to receive it in himself, and to announce it to others through himself.

There follows: « For the angels themselves, as we have said before, completively consummate all the dispositions of the celestial minds, according to what is to be [1090A] consummated. » As if he should say: Fittingly does the archangelic order announce the divine illuminations to us through the angels, because the angels themselves are last in the celestial disposition, and nearest to men, and in them are completed, or terminated and consummated, all the dispositions or orders of the celestial minds, that is, of the angels. Thus therefore the angels consummate all the celestial orders, according to what is to be consummated, so that it may be understood concerning them; or, that it may be said more expressly, according as they themselves, namely the orders of the angels, are to be consummated. But in what way the angels themselves perfect, or consummate, the celestial orders, he subjoins, saying: « Inasmuch as, in the celestial essences, having the angelic property, and more fittingly named angels among us than the prior ones, in so far as, [1090B] concerning that which is more manifest, there is to them a hierarchy, and more of the circum-adornment. » It is not, he says, wondrous if the celestial orders are terminated in them, because among all the celestial essences it is their property to announce the divine secrets to us; and in this they have the angelic property, that it is their property to be messengers to us; and therefore they are named angels more fittingly among us than the prior or superior ones, namely in so far as there is to them a hierarchy, that is, a power or office concerning that which is more manifest to us, and in so far also as their adornment, or disposition, or order is more 'circum-,' that is, is more circumscribable and intelligible to us, approaching visible things because of their ministry. Therefore to them belongs more the circum-adornment — that is, a knowable or comprehensible [1090C] order — because it is nearest to us.

There follows: « For it is to be judged that the most excellent disposition, as itself firstly approaching to order the hidden one, secretly forming [forma], ordains the second. But the second, which is completed by the holy dominations, and virtues, and powers, presides over that hierarchy which is of the principalities, and archangels, and angels. The first hierarchy indeed more manifestly, but that which is after it, more hiddenly. But the manifestative disposition of the principalities, and archangels, and angels commands the human hierarchies through the consequent things, so that there be through order an ascent unto God, and conversion, and communication, and unity. » It is to be judged, [1090D] he says, that the most excellent disposition — that is, the supreme hierarchy, as firstly, that is, principally, approaching the hidden one, that is, God — ordains the second, secretly, that is, hiddenly, or incomprehensibly forming it. But the second hierarchy, which is completed by the holy dominations, and virtues, and powers, is to be judged to preside over that — namely the hierarchy which is of the principalities, and archangels, and angels; and the first hierarchy, that is, the supreme, is to be judged to perceive the divine illuminations more manifestly, or to preside more manifestly over the third; but that which is after it, that is, the second, to preside, namely, more hiddenly. But the disposition — that is, the hierarchy of the principalities, and archangels, and angels — [1091A] the manifestative, that is, the known and notifying one, is to be judged to command the human hierarchies through the consequent things, that is, consequently, after a precept has been given to it by the superiors: so that thus there may be through order an ascent unto God, and conversion, and communication, and unity — that is, so that human minds, turned through the lower hierarchy unto the middle, and from the middle unto the supreme, may ascend unto that one thing to be communicated which God is.
