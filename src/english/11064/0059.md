## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « And indeed also, from God, best imparted to all the hierarchical ones, and communicatively supervening, and a procession with most sacred adornment. » By such an order, he says, the one good is communicated by all, proceeding from God into all the hierarchical orders, so that it may adorn them, supervening upon them and imparted [1091B] through communication. This is what he says: *And indeed also,* that is, even so, there comes to be from God a procession of that highest good, namely, best imparted to all the hierarchical — it is understood, orders: upon them « supervening communicatively, » that is, unto communicating, and with most sacred adornment. For in this is the great adornment of the divine disposition: that, although all participate in the one good, yet it comes forth to be communicated not in one mode, but some perceive it through others, and in turn offer it to be communicated to others after them. Just as, among those spirits, the first hierarchy offers the divine illuminations to be communicated to the second, the second to the third, the third to ours, that is, to the human. This is what he subsequently adds, saying: « Thence theology distributes our hierarchy [1091C] to the angels, naming Michael the prince of the people of the Jews, and others, diverse, of the nations. » Thence, he says, that is, for this reason — because the inferior are ruled by the superior — theology, that is, Holy Scripture, distributes our hierarchy to the angels to be ruled, naming Michael the prince of the people of the Jews, and naming other diverse angels as princes of the nations, namely: as in Daniel we read the prince of the Persians, and the prince of the Greeks named. And not only in those testimonies is it proved that the angels have rule over men, but Scripture also testifies elsewhere, saying: « The Most High established the bounds of the nations according to the number of the angels of God [n: (Dan. X)]. » The bounds — that is, the divisions, or distributions, of the nations — the Most High established according to the number [1091D] of the angels of God: because he fitted the number of the nations to the number of the angels, so that individual angels might preside over individual nations; although, because according to another translation there not 'angels' but 'sons of God' are named, something else seems to be signified.

There follows: « But if anyone should say: And how was the people of the Hebrews alone led back into the divine illuminations? It must be answered that the right dominations of the angels are not to be accused of the error of the other nations unto non-existent gods, but those very ones, by their own inflexions, falling away from that right leading-back which is unto the divine, by their own love, and by pride — [pride] of the things divinely seen by them, and, in a co-rational manner, unto worship. » As if he should say: It seems indeed wondrous. If [1092A] anyone, he says, wonders why the good angels suffered those nations over which they presided to fall away from the knowledge of the true God, it must be answered that this was not done by the fault of the angels, and that the right dominations of the angels ought not to be accused for the error of the other nations, which were not of Israel, unto non-existent gods — that is, because by erring they worshipped those who were not gods; but that those very ones, by their own inflexions, that is, by their own will, falling away from the right leading-back, that is, from the knowledge by which man is led back unto the divine, so that they believed [to be] gods those who were not gods; those very ones, I say, are to be accused, who by their own will, in so far as they were of free choice, slipped from the truth into error, and this out of their own love, because they loved themselves more than God, and sought their own glory, [1092B] and were therefore proud; and out of the pride of the things divinely seen by them, that is, of those things which were divinely revealed to them, and which were revealed to them also in a co-rational manner unto worship, namely the divine — that is, from which they might reasonably or probably have understood in what manner they ought to worship God. For as the Apostle says: « When they had known God, they glorified him not as God; but they became vain in their thoughts. For saying themselves to be wise [behold, pride], they were made fools, and their senseless heart was darkened [behold, the error unto non-existent gods], because they served the creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed for ever [n: (Rom. I)]. » Or, 'divinely and co-rationally' [means] with God recompensing — it is understood, to them: that is, wisely and worthily [1092C] unto worship; that is, as was fitting for the worshippers of false [gods], namely gods seen by them — that is, who seemed to them gods, and were not. For in this God recompensed them wisely and reasonably: that, as the Apostle says, because they approved not to have God in [their] knowledge, God delivered them up unto a reprobate sense, that they should do the things which are not fitting, in their very selves. For they were to be punished in their very selves, who had sinned in their very selves.

There follows: « This is reported that the people of the Hebrews itself also suffered — namely this, that it withdrew from the worship of God, just as the other nations also had withdrawn unto the cult of idols; or this, that is, a like divine vengeance, because it likewise withdrew from the worship of God, and cast off the knowledge of God. For the divine voice says to Israel itself [1092D]: « Thou hast repelled the knowledge of God: and hast gone forth after thy own heart. » Namely, God being forsaken, who is worshipped within by a pure mind. Thou hast gone forth outward through the love of visible things, whither thy own heart — that is, thy desire and depraved will — was drawing thee. In all which the power of free choice appears, because without coercion man — whether aided unto good, or permitted unto evil — is inclined by his own will. This is what follows: « For neither do we have a coerced life. » For the will can be given free to a rational creature, but it cannot be coerced at all. And just as it is not in our power that the gift of divine illumination be offered to us, so it is only in our power that, once offered, it be received. For sometimes it is offered even when we are unwilling, [1093A] but it is never received except when we are willing. And when we are willing, we are willing indeed from God, because a good will is the gift of God. But when we are unwilling, we are unwilling from ourselves, because to be unwilling is nothing other than not to will: which is a desertion of the good unto non-being, and nothing, which was made without it. And so « the divine lights of provident illumination, » because through them God providently illumines, are indeed not « revealed through the proper power of providence: » that is, of those who, having received them, become provident; or 'of the things provided-for,' that is, of those who have been provided for, or predestined by God to receive them. It is to be noted that where we have *revelantur* ('are revealed'), another text has *obcaecantur* ('are blinded'); which, although they are different, yet pertain to establishing the same truth: namely that it may be shown [1093B] that whether the divine lights be revealed, that is, manifested, or blinded, that is, hidden and concealed, this stands not in the power of those receiving, but in the choice of the giver. But nevertheless, although the manifestation or concealment itself is not in their power, yet the reception never comes to be except with their will, because a rational good is that which cannot be perceived except by one who knows and wills. Therefore either it comes about, by a depraved will, that they are wholly repelled, or, by a difference of good will, that they are participated dissimilarly. This is what he says: « But of the intellectual visions, » that is, of the spiritual eyes, namely of the rational minds, « the dissimilarity makes the donation of light, » coming namely « out of the superfull, » that is, out of the exceedingly full and eminently full paternal [1093C] goodness; that donation, I say, the dissimilarity itself makes either wholly non-participating, and not distributed unto the reformation of those visions: « or it makes the participations differing, that is, small or great, obscure or clear, of the one fontal ray, and simple, and ever holding itself in the same mode, and superexpanded. » For the ray of divine illumination, descending from the fount of the good, in itself one and simple, and ever holding itself in the same mode, is superexpanded upon all rational minds, and is participated by them differently, not according to its own simplicity, but according to their diversity. For either they are depraved, and wholly repel it, and cause that it be in no way participated, nor [1093D] distributed unto their reformation; or they are dissimilarly good, and receive it differently.

There follows: « Then, because also to the other nations — out of which we also have looked back unto that — [there was], prepared unto tradition and open to all, the great and copious sea of the divine light; no alien gods indeed ruled, but the one principle of all; and unto it the angels, exercising the priesthood according to each several nation, led back their followers. » As if he should say: That good angels were set over the nations can be proved not only from this, that the Most High is said to have established the bounds of the peoples according to the number of the angels of God, but also from this, that over those nations no alien gods ruled from the beginning, but one God. For it is altogether probable [1094A] that the good Lord established in his commonwealth good ministers under himself: by whose ministry this too was afterward done, that we, who have believed out of the nations, looked back through faith unto God, and unto the fullness of his illumination, being led back through them: which assuredly they would not have done, if they had not been good. This is what he says. Then also from this it can be proved that good angels were set over the nations: namely because to those other nations, which were not of Israel — out of which nations we also, who afterward believed, looked back, turned through faith unto that great and copious sea of the divine light, which is prepared and open to all unto tradition, or bestowal, because it bestows itself and hands itself over to all who will and desire to receive it. Over those nations, namely, [1094B] no alien gods ruled, although this might seem [so], because they worshipped idols, and adored false gods. But one God ruled over them, and had dominion, because just as there is one principle of all, from whom are all things, so there is one Lord and ruler, under whose power all things are established. Who, as he is good in himself, so established good ministers under himself, as rulers and leaders, [namely] angels, and those angels led us back, following through faith, unto that very principle of ours. They led back, I say, according as they were exercising the priesthood — that is, the sacred precedence — according to each several nation; that is, each one, out of that nation in which he exercised the priesthood, that is, the sacred power, converted unto the faith those who believed, and looked back unto their principle.
