## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

[1094C]There follows: « Melchizedek, it is to be understood, being the highest priest, most beloved by the Lord of those that exist — nay, not of those that exist, but of the truly existing most high God. » Treating of the ministry and office of the angels, he suddenly introduces a narrative about Melchizedek: not because, as certain men have supposed, he holds that Melchizedek himself was an angel, or that on this account his priesthood is to be commemorated among the ministries of the angels, but that he may show that it was not only by the work and ministry of angels, but also of the saints and of men pleasing to God, and set over other men in the things that were divine, that unbelievers from among the Gentiles were converted to the faith and worship of the true God. According to this manner, then, [1094D] by which the angels, from the office of their prelacy, exercised the priesthood among the Gentiles, converting them to the faith of the true God, it is to be understood that Melchizedek was the highest priest, being most beloved by the Lord of all that exist — nay rather, of those that do not exist, but of the truly existing most high God. The letter is intricate. But the sense is this. Since he had said that Melchizedek was most beloved by the Lord of all that exist, as though correcting his own statement — because he had called those who truly are not "existing" — he adds "of those not existing," according to what is written: « All the nations are before him as though they were not [n: (Isa. XL)]. » And, as though it were asked who then exists, he adds: « The most high God alone, who has true existence. » But that he does not say "the truly existing most high [1095A] God," « but of the truly existing most high God, this was done because of the likeness of the preceding word, so that by the same case it might answer to "of those not existing," « if of the truly existing most high God. » Or it may be read thus. It is to be understood that Melchizedek, being most beloved by the Lord of all that exist, was a priest not of any existing things whatsoever, but of the most high God who truly exists, as it is written: « For he was a priest of the most high God [n: (Gen. XIV)]. »

And there follows: « For indeed thus simply the theosophers themselves called Melchizedek not a friend of God, but also a priest. » Rightly, he says, did I say that Melchizedek is to be understood a priest, since the theosophers, that is, those who write of divine things, called him not thus simply a friend of God only, but also a priest. And why [1095B] they called him a priest he adds, saying: « Was it that they might signify openly to the wise that he converted not only from among them into him who truly is God? But moreover also others, that as highest priest he may lead them forth by that leading-back which is unto the true and sole divinity. » That Melchizedek, he says, who was not of the Jews, they called not only a friend of God, but also a priest of God — did they do this for this reason? Assuredly they did this for this reason: that they might openly signify to the wise that he converted not only from among them, that is, from Israel, who alone then seemed to be led back and converted to divine knowledge, into God himself, that is, to the faith and knowledge of that same God who truly is, but that he would also lead forth many from other nations out of the darkness of ignorance, namely by that leading-back which is unto the true [1095C] and sole divinity, that is, unto the knowledge of God. Not that Melchizedek himself is read to have converted any from Israel, which was not yet a people: unless perhaps we take Israel in the earlier fathers, of whom some perhaps through Melchizedek himself, in his own time, were led to the knowledge of God. Or so that it be read thus, namely that for this reason he was called not only a friend of God, but also a priest, because he converted — understand: the ignorant and those who knew not — unto God himself, in whom truly one must believe. And as though someone should object that Melchizedek himself, who was not of Israel but a foreigner, converted no one to the faith of God, since Israel alone knew God, he answers that not only from Israel were men converted to the faith of God, but also from other nations: which conversion of the others, namely, was accomplished through Melchizedek himself. Who then indeed, that is, when he blessed Abraham, was called a priest of the most high God, though he was not of Israel, that it might be shown that he was to lead forth many unto God, and that God had both a priest and a people not only in Israel, but also among the Gentiles. Therefore he does not say "he led forth," but « he shall lead forth, » he says. Signifying indeed something future to that time when these things were said — when either Melchizedek himself afterward by himself converted men from the Gentiles unto God, or Christ, of whom he was a type, led forth many, not only from Israel but also from all nations, through faith, unto his Church out of the darkness of unbelief.

There follows: « And this also we shall bring to the notice of thy highest [1096A] priestly understanding: that — the angel ruling over Pharaoh among the Egyptians themselves, and its own president presiding over the prince of the Babylonians, by all providence both provident of dominion and endowed with power, distributed according to the divisions — for those nations also the ministers of the true God were established as leaders of the formations by the manifestation of the vision from the angels, the angels forthwith revealing to the holy men Joseph and Daniel from God through the angels. » He adds yet another authority, from which he wishes to prove that good angels were set by God over the Gentiles: namely, that among the Egyptians the angel shows a vision to Pharaoh, and through it enjoins upon him a precaution concerning the coming barrenness; the same vision being then made manifest through Joseph; and likewise upon the prince of the Babylonians, namely Nebuchadnezzar, his own president, that is, his own [1096B] appointed one, namely his own angel, forms a vision, and afterward makes it manifest by unlocking it through Daniel. In all which it appears that God has both providence, and dominion, and power over all things, according to which, through those visions, it was discerned, or distinguished, or distributed: which visions were first revealed to the angels by God, and then forthwith through the angels to the holy men Joseph and Daniel. This is what he says: O Timothy, not only the higher things, but this also shall we bring to the notice of thy highest priestly understanding, that is, thy understanding — thou who art the highest priest — we shall bring this to thy notice, that, the angel ruling over Pharaoh among the Egyptians, and its own president ruling over the prince of the Babylonians, that is, the angel who was set over [1096C] him; by this, I say, that the angels are read to have ruled over them, there was distributed the provident and powerful quality of the divine providence and dominion according to those, namely, visions — that is, it was shown that God distributes his providence and power for the governing of the several nations; and it was also shown that for those nations the ministers of the true God were established as leaders; and how it was shown, this he adds: by the manifestation, namely, of the vision of the formations by the angels, that is, of the vision in which were displayed the formations, or figures, made by the angels in the minds of the prophets, to signify things to come — such as were the cows and the ears of grain which Pharaoh saw [n: (Gen. XLI)], and the tree and the statue which Nebuchadnezzar saw [n: (Dan. II)]. By the manifestation, therefore, of such a vision was displayed the divine providence and power, disposing the nations through the angels; the vision, I say, first revealed to the angels by God, and then forthwith revealed through the angels to the holy men, namely Joseph and Daniel, from God.
