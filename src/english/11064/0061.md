## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « For one is the principle and providence of all; and by no means is it to be supposed that the Jews indeed led the divinity fully, but that the angels, either specially, or equally honorably, or as opposites, or certain other gods, ruled over the other nations. » The repetition proves what he said above, that one is the principle and providence of all, that is, one God, from whom are all things, and by whom all things are ruled; and therefore by no means [1097A] is it to be supposed that the divinity led the Jews, that is, that it furnished leadership to the Jews fully, or absolutely, that is, through itself, namely without the intervening ministry of angels; but that over the other nations the angels ruled specially, that is, particularly, or with a less excellent power; or equally honorably, that is, with a power equal and like to the divine dominion; or as opposites (understand: with opposing sovereignties), just as the apostate angel is read to have said that he would set his seat toward the north and would be like to the Most High in power [n: (Isa. XIV)]. Or, again, it is not to be supposed that certain other gods ruled over the other nations, as though all things were not subject to the power and dominion of the one and true God. It is not, he says, so to be supposed, as though the true God had power [1097B] over the Jews alone, and over the other nations either angels, or certain other gods, ruled with a power either equal or unequal, yet contrary and foreign to the divine dominion. Or let it be read thus: By no means is it to be supposed that the Divinity, through itself, without an interposed subject angelic power, furnished leadership to the Jews; but that the angels ruled over the other nations specially, that is, particularly, namely among nations of a power and dignity inferior to that of the Jews themselves; or equally honorably, that is, among nations, namely, equally powerful and honorable; or even among opposing nations, which, namely, were opposed and contrary to the Jews themselves, assailing and oppressing them. It is not, he says, so to be supposed — namely that God for this reason led the Jews through himself [1097C] as though they were more honorable than all the other nations, and left the other nations, as inferior and baser, to the angelic dominion — since in other peoples many are not doubted to have been not only equally honorable, but even stronger and more powerful, oppressing the Jews themselves by their power and strength. For what has been said: « When the Most High divided the nations, he set the bounds of the peoples according to the number of the angels of God; but the Lord's portion is his people, Jacob the cord of his inheritance; » is not to be so understood, as though he had given the other nations to be ruled by angels, retaining Israel alone to himself, that is, to his own providence and governance — as if either Israel itself, without angels, or the angels, without him, [1097D] disposed the remaining nations; since both Michael is read to be set over Israel, and God is recorded in the sacred eloquence to possess and govern all nations. Not thus, therefore, is what is written to be understood: « But that eloquence, » in which, namely, this is said to be taken, « is according to the sacred understanding itself, » that is, according to such an understanding as does not disagree with the sacred, that is, with the truth. And this is the sacred understanding: that we understand this saying not as if, that is, as though, God dividing our leadership, that is, the leadership of the human race, with certain other gods or angels — dividing it, I say, into the principate of Israel and into the leadership of the nation, God being content, namely, with the principate over Israel, that is, retaining it to himself, so that [1098A] he alone should be to that people prince and leader. Not, I say, is it so to be understood. But so as, that is, as though, the exalted providence itself, or the providence of the Exalted One, which is one and at once of all, because it provides for all — that providence, I say, salutarily distributing all men by the restorative hand-leadings of their own angels, that is, by leaderships, by which as blind men in the darkness of ignorance they were led by the hand, and restored to the light of truth; and Israel alone being converted to the illumination and knowledge of the true God almost beyond all nations. For on this account Israel alone is read to have been received by God into a portion, and led; because it alone then, out of all nations, was called back to the knowledge of God, and established in the divine worship. « Whence, » he says, « theology, signifying that he, » [1098B] that is God, « possessed Israel unto the service of the true God, » says: « It became the portion of the Lord, » namely Israel itself. Thus, therefore, Israel was his portion, which was so possessed by him that it possessed and had him.

There follows: « But showing that he distributed even it, man by man, to the other nations, he said that Michael led the Jewish people, unto a certain one of the holy angels, that through him the one principle of all might be known. » In this, he says, that Holy Scripture said Michael led the Jewish people, it plainly shows that he, that is God, distributed also among the other nations, namely, leadership man by man, that is, one by one, to a certain one, that is, to some one of the holy angels — or, to say it more expressly, one nation to one, or each nation [1098C] to each, or the several to the several: distributed it, I say, that through him, namely the angel, that is, that each nation, through its own angel, might know the one principle of all, from whom are all things, and under whom all are ruled; so that, in order that Holy Scripture might show this, he says, it said that Michael was the leader of the Jewish people, whom God had specially taken up to be guarded under his own providence and governance. For, in saying this, it plainly taught us that there is one providence which presides over all in governing, under which the angels ruling over the several nations extend, or promote, those same nations that follow them by essential will, that is, by free choice naturally implanted, [1098D] into their own proper principle, namely by suggesting and helping; Scripture, he says, said that Michael led the Jewish people, whose leader it had elsewhere recorded him to be from God. In this « plainly teaching us that there is one providence of all, superessentially set above, » that is, presiding not only potentially, but even naturally, over all invisible and visible virtues at once, namely of angels and of men. For this is what stands as good in both. Showing also « that all the angels under that providence, ruling over the several nations, extend each, » or the several virtues of those nations, namely, into (understand: God) himself, « as into their own proper principle: » the virtues, I say, « following [1099A] by essential will, » that is, by free natural choice. For the angels arouse human minds to love God [1100A] by suggesting virtues, not compelled by necessity, but « following by will. »

## TITLE OF CHAPTER X. *A recapitulation of the ordering of the angelic synagogue.*



## THE TEXT.

[1099A]

*Thus, therefore, is the most honorable disposition of the minds about God connected, ordered from the perfective illumination, immediately ascending into it, more hidden: and by the more manifest illumination of the divinity it is purged, and illuminated, and perfected. More hidden indeed [1099B] as being more invisible, and more simplified and unified. But more manifest as given before, and shining first, and more universal, and having form more poured out into it, as is fitting. But from it again proportionally the second, and from the second the third, and from the third the hierarchy according to us, according to that same law of the well-adorning ordination, in the divine harmony and analogy unto the whole adornment of all good at once, is led back to the sovereign principle and consummation of the hierarchy. And all are manifesters, even the angels of those who are before them. They themselves indeed the most honorable of God who moves them, but proportionally the rest of those moved by God. For the superessential harmony of all foresaw for each of the rational and intellectual ones, with sacred adornment [1099C] and ordered leading, as much as each order of the hierarchies itself is sacredly and becomingly placed. And we see every hierarchy divided into first, and middle, and last virtues. But it also distinguished the very disposition — speaking specially, through each one — by those divine harmonies themselves. For which reason the theologians say that the most divine seraphim themselves cry one to another [n: (Isai. IV)]*, *openly declaring this, as I suppose: that the first themselves hand on the theological utterances to the second. But I would add perhaps, and this not incongruously, that each celestial and human mind, according to itself also, has special first, and middle, and last ordinations and virtues, manifested proportionally through each of the hierarchic illuminations, unto the aforesaid proper anagogies: [1099D] through which each is made, insofar as this itself is both lawful and possible, in participation of the most superunknown purgation, of the fullest light, of the fore-perfected perfection. For nothing is perfect through itself, needing the universal perfection, save only the truly most perfect and fore-perfect.*
