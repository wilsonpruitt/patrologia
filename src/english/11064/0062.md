## EXPOSITION.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The title of the tenth chapter is: A recapitulation of the ordering of the angelic synagogue. ⟦s2⟧ For here is briefly repeated what was said above, namely, the ordered synagogue, that is, the congregation, or angelic multitude, in all its first, and second, and third members. ⟦s3⟧ And the chapter thus begins:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Thus, therefore, is the most honorable disposition of the minds about God connected, ordered from the perfective illumination, immediately ascending into it, [1100A] more hidden and more manifest; and by the illumination of the divinity it is purged, and illuminated, and perfected. » He speaks of the first hierarchy, which consists of three orders — seraphim, cherubim, and thrones. ⟦s2⟧ This disposition, then, or ordination, of the minds, that is, of the spirits, the most honorable or most excellent of all, [1100B] is connected about God, that is, both joined among themselves in concord, and immediately associated to God; thus ordered from the perfective illumination, that is, the divine, which alone perfectly shines and illuminates. ⟦s3⟧ For whosoever are illuminated by it are indeed, in shining, made like to it, and in illuminating others, imitate it. ⟦s4⟧ Yet neither in that they shine are they made equal to it, nor in that they illuminate are they compared to it. ⟦s5⟧ Therefore this hierarchy, which alone is illuminated by it immediately, is singularly ordered from the perfective illumination. ⟦s6⟧ Others, indeed, who receive the lower illuminations mediately, are also fittingly disposed and ordered lower down. ⟦s7⟧ But this hierarchy, which is ordered from the perfective, or teletarchic, and divine illumination, which is the sovereign [1100C] of sanctification, that is, the first and greatest and singular cause of sanctification — or, to say it more expressly, is made pontiff, that is, is raised into the pontifical order, or dignity — is fittingly set above all in dignity, namely by immediately ascending into that illumination, being more hidden, and more manifest, and by that same illumination of the divinity being purged, and illuminated, and perfected. ⟦s8⟧ But how the first hierarchy, from the fact that it receives the divine illumination immediately, is both more hidden and more manifest, he sets forth in the following words, saying: « More hidden indeed as being more invisible, and more simplified and vivified. ⟦s9⟧ But more manifest as that which, given before, shines, and shines first, and is more universal, and has form [1100D] more poured out into it, as is fitting. » It is more hidden, he says, in that it is more invisible, and more simplified, and unified. ⟦s10⟧ In this it is more hidden, that it is more invisible, and nearer to the simplicity and unity of the Godhead. ⟦s11⟧ But it is more manifest, inasmuch as it shines from the light given to it before, that is, before it was given to others: therefore it shines first, and more excellently. ⟦s12⟧ It is also more manifest because it is more universal, in which one likeness stands in many, since that is more known which is more common and suits more. ⟦s13⟧ And whence it is more universal and more alike, he consequently shows in turn, namely, because the form, that is, the divine illumination, from which it is formed so as to shine, is more, that is, more expressly, or more abundantly, poured out into it than into the other hierarchies below it. ⟦s14⟧ But the sense is this: because, namely, the divine illumination is poured first into this hierarchy, therefore [1101A] it is itself more perfectly illuminated. ⟦s15⟧ And by how much the more perfectly they who are in it, the spirits, receive the one illumination, by so much the more perfectly and clearly do they, shining in that one form of one light, become one; and what in them is diverse by nature and manifoldly distinguished, when the one form of clarity comes upon all, does not appear unlike. ⟦s16⟧ Thus, therefore, this hierarchy, because it shines first, shines more perfectly; and because it shines more perfectly, it shines more universally, or more alike, or more expressly; because it shines more expressly, it shines more manifestly. ⟦s17⟧ That, then, which is essentially more hidden, is formally more manifest; and that which is subtler by nature is clearer by grace.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ There follows: « But from it again proportionally the second, and from the second the third, and from the third the hierarchy according to [1101B] us, according to that same law of the well-adorning ordination, in the divine harmony and analogy, unto the whole adornment of all good at once, is led back to the sovereign principle and consummation of the hierarchy. » From it, he says, the first hierarchy, is led back or converted the second, and by the second the third is converted, and by the third is converted that hierarchy, namely, which is according to us. ⟦s2⟧ For the first angelic hierarchy converts the second, the second the third, the third angelic converts the human. ⟦s3⟧ But how it converts he shows, saying: Unto the sovereign principle, and the consummation of the whole good adornment at once, that is, unto God, who is the principle and perfection of every good and beautiful disposition. ⟦s4⟧ And it leads back hierarchically, that is, according to the office, or [1101C] ordination, or law of the hierarchies, in which it is ordained that the higher, according to their manner and virtue, by illuminating and suggesting to the lower, convert them to their principle. ⟦s5⟧ And they convert according to that same law of the well-adorning ordination, that is, of the divine ordination, which adorns all things: whose law and institution it is, that some be illuminated by others, and converted in divine harmony and analogy, that is, in the concord and proportion conferred by God upon the universe. ⟦s6⟧ For in this, that the participation of the good is transfused through some to others proportionally, the concord in the universe is perfected and consummated. ⟦s7⟧ But that he says, "is again led back," is to be so understood as by a second leading-back. ⟦s8⟧ For the first leading-back is when [1101D] the first, illuminated by God himself, are converted unto God himself. ⟦s9⟧ The second, when the second are converted by the first, or the third by the second. ⟦s10⟧ « And all are manifesters, even the angels of those who are before them. » All the celestial spirits, he says, are manifesters, and angels, that is, messengers, of those who are before them. ⟦s11⟧ For what they receive from those preceding, they announce and manifest to those following after them.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ There follows: « They themselves indeed the most honorable of God who moves them, but proportionally the rest of those moved by God. » He had said that all are messengers of those preceding to those following: now he subdistinguishes, that they themselves indeed, the most honorable, that is, the most excellent spirits, who have God alone before them, are messengers of God himself moving them by inspiration, that through them his secrets [1102A] he may reveal to those following. ⟦s2⟧ The rest, who follow after them, are messengers of those same, who are moved by God. ⟦s3⟧ Messengers, I say, they are proportionally, that is: the second, of the first, and of God through the first; the third, of the second, and of the first through the second; but of God, through the second and the first; and in this manner the rest that follow after this. ⟦s4⟧ But how the several proportionally reveal the divine secrets, he sets forth in the following words, showing that to each is given only so much power as befits the order and place in which it is set. ⟦s5⟧ This is what he says: « For the superessential harmony of all foresaw for each of the rational and intellectual ones, with sacred adornment and ordered leading, as much as each order of the hierarchies itself is sacredly and becomingly placed. » [1102B] The superessential harmony, he says, that is, the divine providence, which disposed all things concordantly, foresaw only so much, namely of virtue or power, for each of the rational or intellectual ones, with sacred adornment and ordered leading, namely sacredly adorning and orderly leading; foresaw only so much, I say, as each order of the hierarchies is sacredly and becomingly placed — this, as much as befits the holiness and becomingness of the order of each. ⟦s6⟧ Concerning which order in each hierarchy he adds distinctions, saying: « And we see every hierarchy divided into first, and middle, and last virtues, or orders. » And not only each hierarchy, but also each order through the several hierarchies. ⟦s7⟧ This is what follows: « But it also distinguished the very [1102C] disposition — speaking specially through each one — by those divine harmonies themselves. » The divine providence itself, he says, distinguished, or distinguished apart, the very disposition, namely each one, that is, the order, by the divine harmonies, so that, namely, there might be distinction, and there might be no discrepancy. ⟦s8⟧ And this is to be said specially through the several hierarchies: the several orders have special distinctions.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ There follows: « For which reason the theologians themselves say that the most divine seraphim themselves cry one to another: openly, as I openly suppose, declaring that the first themselves hand on the theological utterances to the second. » For which reason — namely, because the several orders are distinct within themselves — therefore the theologians themselves say that the seraphim themselves, [1102D] who are most divine, that is, nearest to the divinity, cry one to another, as is read in Isaiah [n: (Isai. VI)]. ⟦s2⟧ In this, namely, that they say they cry one to another, openly declaring that the theological, that is, divine utterances, they who are first in that order hand on to those who are constituted second in the same order. ⟦s3⟧ In which it is clear that one and the same order has first and second. ⟦s4⟧ But if this is truly taken of the first and highest order, of those following it can by no means be doubted.
