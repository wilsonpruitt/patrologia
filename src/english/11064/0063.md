## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « But I would add perhaps, and this not incongruously, that each celestial and human mind, according to itself also, has special first, and middle, and last ordinations and virtues, assigned through each of the hierarchic [1103A] illuminations, manifested proportionally unto its proper anagogies: through which each is made, insofar as this itself is both lawful and possible, in participation of the most superunknown purgation, and of the fullest light, and of the fore-perfected perfection. » The sense is this: that not only is the universal hierarchy divided into a first, and a middle, and a last hierarchy, and the several hierarchies into first, and middle, and last orders, and the several orders into first, and middle, and last spirits; but even the several angelic, or human, spirits themselves are, within themselves, virtually distinct, containing first, and middle, and last virtues: through which, by their own anagogies, that is, upward-leadings, or ascensions, from the lowest to the middle, and from the middle to the highest, ascending proportionally according to the hierarchic [1103B] illuminations, they are made partakers, as far as is possible for them, of the divine purgation, which is most unknown, or most hidden, because it purges inwardly; and of the divine light, which is fullest, because it puts all darkness to flight; and of the divine perfection, which is fore-perfected, because it is prior to all in eternity, and higher in dignity, and fuller in truth. But he calls the hierarchic illuminations proportional for this reason, because in the sacred powers, where some are constituted higher, others lower, they do not all perceive the divine illuminations in one manner. For to the higher and more capacious, more is bestowed in the gifts of grace; to the lower, according to the measure of their capacity, less is granted to be participated. According to this manner, then, by which, whether in the several [1103C] hierarchies to the diverse orders, and to the diverse minds, the divine illuminations are differently bestowed, each mind also within itself, in those same illuminations, growing by a reasonable difference from lesser to greater, is advanced. This is what he says: « But I would add this also, not incongruously, » as if he should say: Not only concerning the several hierarchies or orders can I fittingly say that they have first, and middle, and last orders, or spirits; but this also perhaps I can, not incongruously, add to what has been said above, namely, that each celestial and human mind, according to itself, or in itself, has special, that is, proper, first, and middle, and last ordinations and virtues, that is, virtues ordained in the first, and middle, and last place within it. To [1103D] what end it has differing virtues, he adds, [1104A] namely, unto its proper anagogies — that each, namely, may have its own proper ascensions, and advancements unto the better, as it is written: « He hath disposed ascensions in his heart [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)]; » and again: « They go from virtue to virtue: and the God of gods shall be seen in Sion. » And unto these anagogies, that is, ascensions, are disposed the virtues, some lower, some higher, so that, by ascending from the lower to the higher, they may at length be made partakers of the highest good, which is the God of gods in Sion. Each spirit, he says, has its own proper virtues, first, and middle, and last, unto its proper anagogies, that is, ascensions, to be made. Anagogies, I say, already spoken of, that is, which we have already spoken of above, namely, through each order of the hierarchic illuminations: [1104B] because, when we showed how, in the distribution of the hierarchies, the distinct orders perceive differing illuminations, we also demonstrated in what manner among them the lower ascend proportionally, that is, by a reasonable difference of advancement, through the higher unto the highest. Therefore he said the anagogies of the hierarchic illuminations manifested proportionally, or manifested in proportions, or from the preceding treatment, in which this was demonstrated, that those ascensions are made proportionally; or manifested proportionally in this, because by how much the more the ascension grows, by so much the more the knowledge grows. On account of these ascensions, therefore, the grades of virtues are disposed, by which ascensions, namely, each thing, that is, each mind ascending [1104C] through them, is made in participation, that is, is made partaker of the most superunknown purgation, and of the fullest light, and of the fore-perfected perfection. Partaker, I say, it is made "as this itself," that is, that it may be made partaker, is both lawful and possible: lawful, as to the dignity of the good to be participated; possible, as to its own capacity.

There follows: « For nothing is perfect through itself, needing the universal perfection, save the truly most perfect, and fore-perfect. » Rightly, he says, did I call the divine perfection fore-perfected; because all things that are perfect are perfect from it; because nothing is perfect through itself, and there is nothing which is not through itself imperfect, and which is not through itself needy of the invisible, or of every perfection, save that highest good: [1104D] which is both truly most perfect, because nothing is lacking to it; and fore-perfect, because its fullness is eternal.

## TITLE OF CHAPTER XI. *Why all the celestial essences are commonly named celestial virtues.*



## THE TEXT.

[1103D]

*But these things being defined, it behooves to understand that which is worthy: for what cause we are wont to call all the angelic essences alike celestial virtues. For it is not to be said, as in the angels, that the very disposition of the holy virtues is the last of all. And indeed the dispositions of the superposed essences partake the holy and comely illumination of the last; [1104D] but the last in no wise the illumination of the first. And for whose sake are all the divine intellects indeed named celestial virtues; but the seraphim, and thrones, and dominations in no wise. For the extremes are partaken of by the most excellent, in the universal properties. For the angels themselves, and before the angels the archangels, and the principalities, and the powers, ordered after the virtues by theology itself, are often commonly called by us, together with the other [1105A] holy essences, celestial virtues. But we say that, using commonly in all the naming of celestial virtues, we do not introduce a certain confusion of the properties of each disposition; but, because they are divided into three according to themselves by a supermundane reason — all the divine intellects — into essence, and virtue, and operation, when we call all of them at once, or certain of them unobservantly, either celestial essences, or celestial virtues, it is to be supposed that we signify periphrastically those of whom the discourse is, from that essence or virtue which is through each of them. For neither is it easy for us now to annex the superposed property of the holy virtues, already distinguished by us, altogether to the lesser essences, in a turning-about of the unconfused ordination of the angelic adornments. For according to the reason often rightly [1105B] rendered by us, the superfirmed dispositions indeed have abundantly the sacred properties even of the lesser; but the last do not have the superposed universalities of the greater, the illuminations first appearing particularly into them, distributed to them proportionally through the first.*
