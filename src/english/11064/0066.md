## THE TEXT.

[1111B]

*Come, let us consider this also according to our power: for what reason one of the seraphim is said by the theologians to have been sent [n: (Isai. VI)]. For someone may answer that it is not one of the subordinate angels, but a certain one of the greatest and innermost essences, that purges the priest. Some therefore say that, according to the definition of the society of all the intellects already rendered before, the oracle does not name one of the first minds about God to have come unto the purgation of the theologian; but that a certain one of the angels excelling us, performing the sacrifice of the prophet's purgation, was called seraph by equivocation, on account of the fiery and celestial washing away of the aforesaid sins, and the raising up of the purged one into the divine obedience. And they say the oracle said 'one of the seraphim' simply, [1111C] not one of those set about God, but of the purgative virtues excelling us. But another furnished me a certain not too unfitting apology concerning this kind of state. For he says that the great angel who then was, forming the vision to teach the theologian divine things, referred his own purgative sacrifice unto God, and after God unto the pre-operative hierarchy. And is not this reason then truthful? For he who said this says: How the divine virtue, coming into all things, fills them, and immeasurably penetrates through all, and again is invisible to all — not only as being superessentially remote from all, but also as secretly letting into all things its provident operations. But nevertheless it also shines above proportionally in all the intellectual beings, and, bringing its own [1111D] illumination into the most precious essences, through those as the first ones distributes itself in well-ordered fashion into others established under them, according to the contemplative commensuration of each disposition. Which, that I may say it more openly, also through examples proper — though deficient in relation to God, remote from all, yet more manifest to us. The distributions of the solar ray, well distributed into the first matter, fill it, the most lucid of all, and through it more manifestly it declares its own splendors. But approaching grosser matters it has a dimmer distributive over-appearance, from the matters to be illuminated in relation to the distributive habit of illumination in point of opportuneness; and a little afterward, from this, it is contracted to what is almost perfectly undistributed. Again the heat of fire distributes itself more into the more capacious things, and into those well suited and easy [1112B] to its own likeness. But toward the essences contrary to its re-formings, it manifests no hidden trace of its primal operation. And this the more, because to those things which are not cognate it is admitted through the things possessing what is opportune to it — first, inasmuch as it makes things easily movable ignited from the fiery ones, and through these heating proportionally either water, or something else of the things not easily kindled. According to this reason then of natural ordination, the supernatural ordination itself of every good adornment, visible and invisible, fittingly manifests to the most exalted essences the brightness of illumination first appearing, as in the most copious outpourings; and through those the essences which are after partake the divine ray. For these first ones, knowing God, and supereminently desiring the divine [1112C] virtue, and to become pre-operative, as far as is possible, with a virtue like to God, have been made worthy also in action; and they themselves extend deiformly the essences after them unto a like virtue, as virtue, copiously delivering to them from the brightness supervening upon them, and those again to their subjects; and through each one the first delivers to that which is after it. Does not the water itself also proportionally reach into all? God then is at once, to all the illuminated, the principle of illuminating — by nature indeed, and truly and properly, as the essence of light, and causal of its very being and seeing. Then, deiformly and God-likely remaining superposed, transferring through itself the divine lights after it into each one, into that. Therefore the essences of all the remaining ones at once [1112D] marvel at the most excellent disposition of the celestial minds, as, according to what is consequent, the principle after God of all sacred and divine knowledge and of divine imitation — the divine illumination being distributed, as it were, through those into all, and into us. Wherefore they also refer every sacred and God-like operation unto God as the causal one; then unto the first deiform intellects as the first operators of divine things, and masters. Does not then the first disposition of the holy angels have, more than all together, the fiery property, and the poured-out tradition of the divine wisdom, and the mystical property of the most exalted knowledge of the divine illuminations, and, bearing the sedent property, signifying the divine reception. But the dispositions themselves of the subordinate essences do indeed partake the fiery, the wise, and the knowing receptive virtue of God — but below, and looking to the first [1113A] ones, and through them, as made worthy by divine imitation to be pre-operative, brought back into what is possible of deiformity. The said holy properties, therefore, of which the participation comes about through the first ones for those subsisting after them, they place back in those very ones, after God, as in hierarchies. He says therefore these things, saying [n: (ibid.)]: That the vision descending from her was received by him for the theologian, through one of the holy and blessed angels ruling over us, and that he referred back his illuminative leading-by-the-hand into that holy contemplation before. And he saw the most exalted essences sitting above — as far as is to be said in symbols — placed after God, and about God, and with God; and, more sublime than all these themselves in a super-arcane way, the super-principal summit super-placed in the midst of the super-firmed virtues. The theologian himself therefore learned by the visions [1113B] that, according to every superessential super-excellence, the divine is incomparably super-placed above every invisible and visible virtue of all things. And that it is remote from all as universal, nor like even to the first essences of the things that are. Moreover, that it is itself the principle of all, and the substance-making cause, and, in secret singularity, the immutable foundation of the things that are — from which being, and well-being, is even to the supremely established virtues themselves. Then he was taught the same deiform virtues of the most holy seraphim; by their sacred surname indeed — which is the fiery, concerning which we shall speak a little later, as far as is possible for us — to introduce, into the deiform, the anagogies of the fiery virtue. But by the sacred formation of the expanded wings, the absolute and highest extension into the divine, in the first, and in the middle, [1113C] and in the last intellects. But the intellectual theologian, seeing also their manifold and multiform character, and that the vision was distinguished by wings beneath the feet, and it beneath the faces, and its ever-motion in the midst of the wings, was brought back to the invisible knowledge of the things which were seen, the many-wayed and many-seeing virtue of the highest intellects being manifested to him; and by their sacred awe, which they have supermundanely against the proud, and bold, and impossible scrutiny of higher things and of lower; and, in the commensuration of the actions imitating God, the unceasing and high-flying character of their ever-motion. But he was also instructed in that divine and much precious hymnody, the angel forming the vision according to virtue for the theologian himself, and [1113D] delivering his own sacred knowledge. He taught him therefore this also, that purgation is, for those purged however much, the participation, as far as is possible, of the divine brightness itself, unknown. But this brightness, from the remote causes themselves of the divinity, by which it perfects all sacred intellects with superessential occultation, is somehow more manifest to the highest virtues about itself, and more manifests and distributes itself. Then to the second, or the last, or our intellectual virtues — as far as each one has come forth from it according to the deiform — thus it conducts its manifest illumination unto the praising of the unknown of its own occultation. But it shines through the several ones, to the second through the first. And if it behooves to say it briefly, it is first led from the hidden to the manifest through the first virtues. This therefore the theologian learned from the light-[1114A]leading angel, namely purgation, and that all the divine operations, shining back through the first essences, are distributed into all the rest, according to the analogy of each one unto the deifying participations. Wherefore he consequently placed back the fierily purgative property in the seraphim themselves, after God. Nothing therefore is inordinate, if a seraph is said to purge the theologian. For thus God purges all, of whose whole purgation he is the cause. But let us more closely use an example: just as he who according to us is the highest priest, purging or illuminating through his ministers or priests, is himself said to purge and to illuminate — the orders purged through him referring back through themselves unto him their own sacred operations; so also the angel accomplishing the purgation of the theologian places back his own purgative knowledge and virtue [1114B] unto God as the causal one, then unto the seraph itself as the highest priest first acting: as perhaps someone, with angelic reverence, teaching the purged one, might say that the principle of the purgation to be accomplished in thee, before me, is indeed the exalted one, and essence, and creator, and causal, bringing the first essences to be present, and containing them about himself in their collocation; and keeping them unturnable and free from falling; and moving himself into the first participations of his own provident operations; for this, teaching me these things, he says the mission of the seraph itself manifests. But the highest priest, and after God the leader himself, the adornment of the excelling essences, from whom I have been deiformly instructed to purge, he therefore it is who through me purges thee — he through whom the very cause and artificer of the whole purgation brought forth from the [1114C] hidden even into us his own provident actions. These things indeed he taught me; but to thee I deliver them, and I would grant to thy intellectual and discerning knowledge either to absolve one part of the said causes from doubt, and to honor the same before the other as having what is consequent, and reasonable, and equally true; or, from thyself — which is truly nearer to the truth — to find it, or to learn it from another; God, namely, giving it, and the angels first receiving it, and to us, the friends of the angels, revealing it, through his more lovable contemplation.*
