## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « Then, deiformly and God-likely remaining superposed, » etc. In God, he says, the light itself is nothing other than the essence itself, to him for whom to be and to be light are the same, who from his very self is the principle of illuminating, because those who first shine he illuminates from his very self. But then, that is, thereafter, after him, namely God, each thing « remaining superposed deiformly and God-likely, » that is, according to the conformation and likeness of God — understand: it becomes the principle of illuminating after itself, « transferring through itself the divine lights into that, » namely into what is constituted after itself. The sense is this: That God, who is superposed to all, illuminates through himself those who are nearest; [1121D] and thus he becomes the principle of illumination, illuminating the first shining ones; then each one of those first shining ones, superposed to all the other subjects according to the conformity and likeness of God, becomes the principle of illumination to each one of the subjects, transferring through himself the divine lights into that, namely the subject. But that he put 'superposed' in the neuter, in place of what he should have said, 'superposed' in the masculine, namely order, or angel, was done after the manner of Scripture, especially when unknown and wonderful things are treated of, when it is said 'that,' as if it were said, 'whatever that is.' But that he subjoined 'to each one after itself,' this seems said as though that superposed thing, whatever it is, being one, is the principle of illumination to the several [1122A] subjects, so that it may furnish a light not as many to many, but as one to many. But in that which after this he again added, « transferring the divine lights into that, » it seems to minister illumination not as one to many, but as one to one. Whence it is plain that, according to the manner of Scripture, whether number be put alternately for number, or gender for gender, the truth of the same understanding is not changed.

There follows: « Therefore the most exalted disposition of the celestial minds. » He infers from the aforesaid, as if he said: Since the first disposition has received the divine things, and furnishes illumination to all its subjects; « therefore the essences of all the remaining ones marvel at the disposition, » that is, the spirits of all the remaining orders [1122B] contemplate with admiration and astonishment « that most exalted disposition of the celestial minds itself, the principle after God, according to what is consequent. » For thus it is consequent, that they should know God as the first principle; then also should venerate those who are first after God, as in their own manner a principle unto the things that follow, through whom the divine grace descends to those who are subject. And this is what follows: « They marvel that these are the principle of all sacred and divine knowledge and of divine imitation: » which, namely, flow to the subjects through them, as the divine illumination distributed through them into all the other celestial virtues, and not only into them, but also into us; namely men, who receive the divine illuminations by their mediation. And because all the remaining celestial [1122C] essences are illuminated by the mediation of the first ones, therefore whatever sacred thing they can perform, and like to God, this indeed they first attribute to God, who is the cause of all; then to those first spirits, by whose operating and mediation they receive the divine grace. This is what he says: « Wherefore, » namely, because through those illuminated by God the rest are illuminated, therefore the rest themselves « refer every sacred and God-like operation of theirs unto God as the causal one, » because he is the first cause of all; « then they refer it unto the first intellects, » that is, the supreme spirits, who are deiform, or conformable to God, in this, that they bestow the spiritual gifts: not however as God, who is the first cause from whom they are, but [1122D] as the first effects, through whom they are. Therefore they refer their good to those, through whom it is, « as through the first operators, and masters of divine things, » namely of gifts. Operators indeed in that they move by effecting, but masters in that they preside by directing.

There follows: « Does not then the first, » etc. *Num* stands for *nonne*. « Does not then, » he says, « the first disposition of the holy angels, » which is so greatly more sublime than all the rest, « have more than all » the highest and « fiery property, » that is, charity, as in the seraphim; « and the poured-out, » that is, abundant « tradition of the divine wisdom, and the mystical of the most exalted knowledge of the divine illuminations, » that is, the mystical, or hidden, or profound, most exalted [1123A] knowledge, which it perceives from the divine illuminations, as in the cherubim; and the sedent property, as in the thrones, bearing, namely, God, and « signifying the divine reception: » because in that they are called thrones, it is openly signified that they receive God sitting and resting in them. The sense is: Does not the first hierarchy, more excellently and more abundantly than all the rest, have both the fire of charity, and the light of knowledge, and the judgment of discernment? It has assuredly all these more than all the rest. « But the » dispositions themselves « of the subordinate essences, » that is, of the subject orders, do indeed themselves also partake the « fiery virtue, » that is, charity, as the seraphim; the wise and knowing « virtue, » that is, knowledge, as the [1123B] cherubim; and the virtue receptive of God, as the thrones: « they partake, » I say; but « below, » that is, more imperfectly than the first ones: « and looking to the first ones, » that is, imitating the first ones, « and through them, » namely the first ones, brought back into that of deiformity which is possible for them, that is, brought back to as much deiformity as is possible for them; through them, namely the first ones, I say, « made worthily, » that is, made worthy, « by divine imitation, » that is, that they may imitate God, « pre-operative, » that is, operating, before and more excellently than all the others, from him and according to him.

There follows: « The said holy properties therefore, » etc. Since, he says, through the first ones the subsequent ones receive the gifts of graces, therefore the holy properties already said above — such as was to set the prophet's lips on fire with a coal, [1123C] which seems to be proper to the seraphim, and to teach knowledge, which seems proper to the cherubim. Such properties therefore, « of which the participation comes about through the first essences, » the other essences « subsisting after them, » these properties, I say, « place back in them, » that is, attribute to those first ones, namely through whom they partake them: « as in hierarchs, » that is, in princes, and superiors, so that they may refer them to those from whom they received them. « They place back, » I say, « in them after God: » because they first attribute them to God, then to those who are nearest after God. But that he says that the subsequent essences become the participation of the properties of the first ones, the manner of speaking is such as if he said: They become partakers [1123D] of the properties of the first ones, because that which the first possess through property, the second share through participation.
