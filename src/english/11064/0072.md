## TITLE OF CHAPTER XIV. *What the handed-down angelic number signifies.*



## THE TEXT.

[1129C]

*What the handed-down angelic number signifies. And this too is worthy, as I judge, of intellectual cognition, because the tradition of the oracles concerning the angels says there to be « a thousand times a thousand, » and « ten thousand ten thousand times [n: (Dan. VII)]*, *according to us the most sublime of numbers, revolving them into itself, and multiplying them, and through this openly signifying the innumerable ordinations of the celestial essences. For many are the blessed hosts of the supermundane intellects, surpassing the weak and constricted commensuration of material numbers according to us, and defined by the gnostic supermundane and celestial intelligence and knowledge alone, according to the very health most richly bestowed on them of the divine and manifold knowledge, sapiential of all things at once which are — of the principle superessentially [1129D] subsisting, and of the substance-making cause, and of the containing virtue, and of the environing consummation.*

## EXPOSITION.

« What the angelic number handed down signifies, » that is, given, or defined by the Holy Scriptures? Not only, he says, the aforesaid things; but « this too is worthy of intellectual cognition, » namely, why a certain and defined number is attributed to the angelic spirits: [1130C] « because the tradition » of the divine « words concerning the angels says there to be a thousand times a thousand, and ten thousand times ten thousand » angels, « revolving the most sublime of numbers according to us, » that is, the numbers which according to us are most sublime. Revolving, I say, into itself, that is, in the utterance of the words — the words which are contained in it — revolving them into words, and multiplying some through others, just as thousands are revolved into "a thousand times" and ten thousands into "ten thousand times" by multiplication. Which he would have said more plainly as "revolving the numbers into themselves," although this was nevertheless said more expressly, because not the numbers, but the names of the numbers, placed into themselves by multiplication, are revolved. If therefore the tradition of the words, signifying the multitude of the celestial spirits, says there to be a thousand times a thousand, and ten thousand [1130D] times ten thousand angels, revolving into itself the most sublime numbers according to us, or comparing them among themselves, and multiplying some by others. « And through this openly signifying that the ordinations of the celestial essences are innumerable. » For in that he set down the greatest numbers, he plainly intimates that he ought still to have said more, if he had been able to say more. Truly it must be known that this, which he says — a thousand times a thousand, and ten thousand times ten thousand — our translation does not contain in this manner; [1131A] but a thousand of thousands, and ten thousand times a hundred thousand, which nevertheless pertains to the same truth. Therefore, wishing to signify infinite numbers, he set down the greatest finite numbers, because « many are the blessed hosts, » that is, the blessed multitudes warring for God, « of the supermundane intellects, surpassing the weak and constricted commensuration of material numbers according to us, and defined by the sole gnostic, that is, cognitive knowledge; or, namely, by the divine cognition, which knows all things, and alone knows the number of those supernal virtues, and alone discerns them, and fully knows them according to that health, that is, the purity, or integrity, of their simple and incorruptible nature, bestowed on them most richly and most abundantly by the supermundane and [1131B] celestial sapiential intelligence and knowledge of the divine and manifold knowledge — of the principle of all things which are superessentially and existing, and of the substance-making cause of all, and of the virtue containing all things, and of the consummation environing all things. » The sense is such: that the wisdom of God alone, which is gnostic, that is, cognitive of all things, definitely and perfectly knows the number [1132A] of those celestial essences in that purity and integrity in which it created and formed them. Which knowledge, namely, is both celestial, and divine, and manifold. Celestial, by purity; divine, by truth; manifold, by incomprehensibility. And though it be such in itself, it is also sapiential in its work, making all things wisely, and establishing nothing worthy of reproof. And this such wisdom, through which all things were created, and all things are known, is the wisdom of him who is the principle of all things which are (because from him are all things), subsisting superessentially through himself, namely perfect, and needing nothing; and who is the substance-making cause of all, because he makes all things to subsist, that they may be; and is the virtue containing all things, that they may remain in that which they are; and is the consummation [1132B] environing all things, lest they flow apart, or fall away from that whereby they thus are. It is no wonder therefore, if those spirits whose number is incomprehensible to us can be discerned through so great a knowledge, and the knowledge of so great a one: through which all things, when they were not, were created; and now, lest the things made revert to nothing, subsist in it.
