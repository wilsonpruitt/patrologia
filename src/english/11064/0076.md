## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

« But there is, » etc. As if he should say: Not only on account of these things which I have said is the human form fittingly adapted to the angels by signification, but also it « is, » that is, it happens, possible « to find the celestial virtues through the several parts of our corporeal manifold partition. » For in the several parts of the human body, through mystical signification, spiritual virtues can be found; because all things which have been visibly made have some [1143B] signification unto the invisible. Thus those who consider rightly can weigh the invisible from the visible. Whence it happens to find this, « saying, » that is, they who say, that « that clearest gaze, » which is given to man « toward the divine luminaries, » such as the sun, and the moon, and the stars, « signifies the conspective virtues, » that is, the contemplative, which are in the rational. Likewise saying that that same gaze of the exterior sight further signifies a certain « reception » of « the divine illuminations, tender, » that is, easily perceiving, and liquid, that is, clearly seeing through, « and not beaten back, » that is, effectually comprehending, « but acutely mobile, » because not beaten back; « pure, » because liquid; « fully impassibly, » because tender. [1143C] So indeed speaking of corporeal sight: « But the smell, » they say, signifies the « discretive virtues » of corporeal things, « that receptive of the distribution, » or bestowal, or effusion, of spiritual grace, « sweetly smelling above the intellect. » Receptive, I say, as far as it is possible to be receptive; « and of those things which are not so, » that is, of those which do not smell sweetly, « discretive by knowledge, and altogether refugitive. » That is, they say that smell signifies specially that force or virtue of the soul which spiritually discerns between those things which spiritually smell well, and those which do not; and desires the well-smelling, but flees the ill-smelling. « But the virtues of the ears » they say signify « that » which is in the soul « participant; [1143D] and gnostic, » that is, cognitive and receptive of the divine inspiration. « The gustative, » etc. He says the same, that is, they say that the gustative virtues signify « the fulness of the invisible and divine foods, » by which the celestial essences are refreshed; signify also « that receptive of the nourishing promotions, » that is, that which receives for them the nourishing promotions, that is, the nurturing increases. « But the tactive virtues » they say signify that which is in the soul, « discretive by knowledge of the fitting, or the harmful. Then the eyelids, and the eyebrows » they say signify « that custoditive of the intelligence of the divine visions; but the youthful and the adult age » they say signify « that » which is in the soul « of the vital virtue ever renewing » it. « But the teeth, » [1144A] by which food is divided, and broken small, and passed over into the body to be nourished, they say signify « the divisive of the nourishing perfection put into, » or engrafted in, the rational spirit itself. « For each intellectual essence divides and multiplies, by a provident virtue, the uniform intelligence given to it by a diviner essence » as it were whole food: breaking small, and adapting it « to the leading analogy of the inferior, » that is, to the measure by which it may be conveyed to the participation of the inferior knowledge. For unless it broke small to measure that which it receives great, it could by no means pass over to the participation of the inferior intelligence. « But the shoulders, and the arms, and likewise the hands » they say signify « the factive, and operative, and active. But the heart » [1144B] they say « is a symbol, » that is, a figure, « of the deiform life, » sowing, or scattering, or diffusing, « its own vital virtue into those things which have been foreknown: » just as the heart diffuses vital motion to the other members. Therefore they say that the heart is, in God, or in the rational spirit, that virtue from which vital grace is diffused into others, who have been foreseen by God to receive it. For it is given only to those who have been foreseen to receive it, and predestined unto life. « The breasts again » they say « signify the hard, and the firm; and the custoditive » of that « life-giving distribution proceeding from the heart, » inasmuch as « from a thing placed beneath, » because the heart is placed beneath the breast, that by it it may be fortified and guarded. « But the backs, » they say, signify « the continuous, » [1144C] that is, the continuation « of all the fertile virtues; » because, just as in the back spine is coupled to spine, so in the virtues one coheres to another. « But the feet » they say signify « the mobile, and swift, and cursive of the motion ever going into the divine. On account of which, » because they have swift motion, « theology has figured the feet of the holy intellects as winged. » And rightly, because « the winged signifies the anagogic, » that is, upward-leading, « swiftness; » and it signifies also « the celestial active, » that is, the celestial action, or impulse, or promotion of the journey, which is upward: and it signifies also « the removed, » that is, the removal, « from all that is lowly, » that is, from lowliness, or abjection, or depression: [1144D] which « removed » is in them « by the bearing upward, » because in that they are borne upward, they are removed from all things which are in the depth. But the very lightness of the wings signifies that there is nothing earthly or heavy in them, « but the whole ascending on high purely, and without weight. The naked, too, and the unshod, » which is attributed to them, signifies « the lowered » of them, « and the loosed, and the immeasurable, and the pure from » all « apposition of those things which are without; » that is, that the angels are described naked and unshod signifies that they are lowered, that is, loosed, and absolved, and pure, from all « apposition, » or conjunction, « of those things which are without, » that is, of visible things, and by this immeasurable. For by how much the less they are constrained by corporeal mingling, or apposition, [1145A] by so much the more they are, through the purity of intelligence, immeasurably dilated. It signifies also « the naked, and unshod, assimilative of them to the divine simplicity, as far as is possible, » because, pure from corporeal admixture, they do not subsist out of a double substance. By which sentence they are struck who say that the angels have bodies. For the author testifies that they are pure from all apposition of the things which are without, and that through their naked and pure essence, in which they subsist, they imitate the divine simplicity.

There follows: « But since again, » etc. As if he should say: I have said that divine Scripture, in that it describes the angels as naked, signifies their purity and simplicity. But since again that very wisdom, which is both simple by truth, and much varied [1145B] through the diversity of formations — « since, » I say, that same such « wisdom both again clothes the naked, and gives them certain vessels, » that is, instruments, « to carry about: come, » that is, up now, « let us open, according as it is possible for us, the sacred garments of the celestial spirits, and their organs, » that is, instruments; because, as has been said, they are described both with clothing, and certain vessels, or organs, are attributed to them to be carried about in that mystical description. And I well say let us open what these signify, because all are significative. First, then, « the bright and fiery garment, » which is attributed to them, « I esteem to signify the deiform, » which is in them, « according to the image of fire, » which is the likeness of deiformity; « and the luculent, that is, the clear, I esteem to be attributed to them, on account of the reposes, » which are [1145C] « in heaven, where light is, » but not any light whatever, but « altogether invisible » (for so it must be said, that that light which is there is altogether invisible), « either intellectually illuminating, » as in God, « or intellectually illuminated, » as in the angels. « But the sacerdotal garment » I esteem signifies « the leading, » or leadership, or direction, « to the divine and mystical contemplations, » and signifies « the vow of the whole life. » For in the sacerdotal garment is signified that they are leaders unto the divine, mystical contemplations, that is, unto the divine and mystical contemplations, instructing their subjects unto knowledge, and that they dedicate their life to God, exhorting, and confirming the devotion of a good work. « The girdles, too, » they say signify « the custoditive of those fecund virtues: » [1145D] and signify « the habit gathering them, » namely the virtues, « to be converted unitedly into itself, and to be carried about circularly around itself, with a faculty free from falling, from the likeness of nature. » The sense is: that the girdle, which surrounds and constrains the loins and the navel, in which is the seminative of propagation, signifies the guardianship of the fecund virtues; which guardianship surrounds those virtues, and guards, or constrains them, lest in their multiplication they be relaxed beyond the bounds of justice, or flow apart. It is also signified in the girdle itself, in that by surrounding the body after the manner of a circle it is turned into itself, and united: namely, that the habit of the virtues, gathering those virtues into one, lest they flow apart or recede from the unity of concord; [1146A] namely, that that habit, I say, is bent about circularly around itself through the coherence of all things and through the mutual consideration of those virtues: bent about, I say, with ease, because there is made an easy progression from virtue to virtue on account of the likeness and concord: which ease is free from falling, from the likeness of nature. For the progression of the virtues is not the corruption, but the reparation, of nature. « The rods, too, » or scepters, they say signify « the regal and principal, and defining all things right; » just as a rod is straight, and a royal bearing. « But the darts, and the axes, » by which whole things are wont to be cut, or divided, or severed, they say signify « the separative of dissimilitudes, » that is, that force which separates dissimilitudes, and discerns between good and evil; [1146B] « and » they say it signifies that same « acumen of the discerning virtues, » that is, subtlety, namely in inspecting; « and the efficacious, and the actuous, » that is, the efficacy of acting by perfecting: « But the geometric and tectonic vessels, » that is, the instruments of measuring and building, they say signify « the fundative, and edificative, and perfective, and whatsoever else there are of the providence reducing and converting the second things. » For the providence of the first and higher things, which converts and reduces unto the better the second and lower things, founds, and builds, and perfects in them a spiritual edifice. Of which work the signs are the geometric and tectonic vessels, formed in the vision according to a corporeal appearance.
