## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « It itself also, » etc. As if he should say: Not only in the appearance of a cloud does theology itself describe the angels, « but it also puts about the appearance of air, and of electrum, and of many-colored stones about them itself by » signification [1149A] « on the celestial essences. » And rightly. For « electrum » indeed, inasmuch as « gold-formed, and at once silvern, signifies, » inasmuch as in gold, the splendor « imputrescible, and at once ample, and not diminished, and uncontaminated, » as gold does not at any time rust, but has a « splendor » pure, and perpetual. « And » it signifies also, inasmuch as « in silver, brightness open, and light-formed, and celestial, » as silver is bright and shining. So then electrum, according to each appearance, namely of gold and of silver, signifies the bright and the fulgid, that is, the clear and the fiery, because the supernal wisdom, which shines in those spirits, is both clear through knowledge, and fiery through love. This, then, through the appearance of electrum is signified in the supernal spirits. « But in the air there is to be attributed » [1149B] to them « either the fiery or the gold-formed, according to the reasons » already « delivered, » namely in the exposition of electrum, where according to something a similar appearance is found. « But the many-colored appearances of stones it is to be esteemed signify either the light-formed, as it were the white ones, » that is, as the white appearances, namely, signify, « or the gold-formed, as it were the red ones, or the youthful and new, as it were the pallid ones. » And, that I may say it briefly, « through the several appearances thou wilt find an anagogic, that is, spiritual, dijudication of the typic, » that is, figurative, images. « But since indeed I judge these things to have been said by us sufficiently according to our virtue, therefore there must be a passing over » — it is now — « into the sacred unlocking, » that is, exposition, « of the sacredly-figured bestial formation of the celestial spirits, [1149C] namely that we may expound the bestial forms which are sacredly figured to the celestial spirits for signifying them. For the lion is to be reckoned to signify the principal, and robust, and indomitable, » which subsists in them spiritually, and to signify also « the assimilative of the intellectual footprints » going « to the hidden, » that is, secret, « of the ineffable divinity. » Which footprints, as, that is, as the virtue, that is, the spiritual thing, is able, imitate the footprints of the lion, because, just as the lion wipes out his footprints with his tail and in a certain way reforms and restores the level ground which he had impressed with his foot, that his going may be hidden, so the invisible step of the celestial spirits is in a certain way figured, « its journey restored into itself, » that is, brought back to [1149D] the prior state, and reduced to the level, the signs of the footprints being wiped out, « by a veiling-about, that is, through a veiling-about; and, that so it may perhaps rather be mystically said, it restores in a journey with a pompous garment, » that is, a distinguished, or excellent, or splendid and glorious garment coming to them « according to the divine illumination. » For to those going inward to contemplate the secret light of the divinity, from the supervening splendor the footprints are wiped out; because everything which before adhered through knowledge is wiped from the memory, when to the souls desiring, the desired brightness is manifested. Hence it is that Paul says: « One thing — forgetting the things indeed which are behind, I stretch myself unto those things which are before. » As therefore to go with the footprints wiped out is, the thought of all [1150A] *imagitionum* being wiped from the mind, to set forth with the mind to contemplate the *incircumpscritum* light. For there indeed the intellect, while it is surrounded with the splendor of the supervening brightness, as though covered by a certain garment, whatever besides that light is in itself, even that it cannot see. All these things, then, it is to be esteemed the form of the lion signifies. « But that of the ox » (understand: the form) it is to be esteemed signifies « the firm and new, » and recalling or renewing, « and repairing the intellectual furrows unto the reception of the celestial and generative showers; and » it is to be esteemed to signify also « the custoditive and most strong. » For because the ox firmly fixes its foot, it signifies the firmness and stability of immutability. But in this, that furrowing the earth it recalls it, and renews it unto the reception [1150B] of the celestial showers by which it is made fruitful for generating, it signifies the interior newness, by which the mind is tempered and adapted to the reception of the celestial grace, which, being made fruitful, brings forth the germs of the virtues. In this, then, that the ox is eminent by the strength of its neck, and bearing the yoke keeps guard, is signified the guardianship of the mind, by which in every act it perseveres in its own guardianship without defect. « That of the eagle thereafter » (understand: the form) it is to be esteemed signifies « the regal, » because it is preeminent over the other birds; « and the high-bearing, » because by flight it bears itself on high; « and the swift-flying, » because it flies swiftly; « and, unto potent nourishment, acute and sober, » because flying in the height it acutely descries its nourishment, soberly desires it, potently seizes it. [1150C] The form of the eagle also signifies « the agile, » because it is expeditious for moving itself; « and » it signifies « the well-contrived, » because in its motion it fittingly coadapts itself; « and » it signifies also the contemplative « unto the ray of the divine sun copious, and much-shining, » that is, much shining, one word. « Contemplative, » I say, « by desire, » that is, through the desire « of the inspective virtues, » that is, of those beholding within, or the interior things: and I say the contemplation « with sound gazes, and immediately, and rightly, and inflexibly, » just as the eagle with unbeaten eyes beholds the ray of the sun. All these things, then, in the contemplative spirits, it is to be esteemed the form of the eagle signifies. « But that of horses » (understand: the form) it is to be esteemed signifies « obedient, and bridleable, [1150D] and white indeed, » that is, the white form is to be esteemed to signify « truly lucid, and as it were most cognate to the divine light. But of those, » namely of horses, « which are black, » the form is to be esteemed to signify « the hidden, » that is, the occult, namely of the divine incomprehensibility, as the black color is dark, and repels the sight. « But the red » color it is to be esteemed signifies « the fiery and active, » because it intimates the fervor of a good will, which is exercised in work. « Of the mingled ones, too » (understand: horses), « from white and black » color, it is to be esteemed to signify « the conjunctive of the extremes with a perfective virtue: » which associates through [1151A] charity « the first to the second, and the second to the first, convertibly, and consequently connecting » them, namely that they be connected to one another through mutual love and providence. This, therefore, can be understood in the aforesaid formations. And perhaps, « if we did not terminate the commensuration of the discourse, » that is, if it were not this that we wish to terminate the discourse by such measure, and not to go further through the several things to be expounded: and unless we also terminated all the properties, « and all the corporeal conformations » of the aforesaid animals « through their parts » already said — that is, unless by this, that we have already said of them in part, we wish to signify by one likeness whatever can universally be said of them, whether in the interior properties, or in the exterior conformations: [1151B] unless this, I say, be that through the several things by expounding we are unwilling to go, « perhaps not incongruously we should have united to the celestial virtues, » even the furious, « according to dissimilar likenesses, » as was said above, that some dissimilitudes are like, but some are dissimilar. According to this manner, then, not incongruously we should have adapted « the furious » to the celestial virtues, for signifying « their intellectual fortitude: » « of which » fortitude, namely, « the fury is the lowest image, » because, namely, through the contrary and from afar it signifies. « But the concupiscence » of the irrational, not incongruously we should have adapted « unto the divine love, » for signifying it. « And, that we may say it universally, » as it is to be said summarily, fittingly we should have adapted « at once [1151C] both all the senses, and all the manifold parts of the irrational animals, » namely to those spiritual beings by signification — the senses and the parts, I say, « reducing » us by signifying « into the immaterial, » that is, spiritual « intelligences, and uniform virtues of the essence of the celestial; » because through those things which are corporeally and materially described in the senses and members of the irrational animals we are stirred up to the immaterial, that is, spiritual intelligence of those virtues which uniformly and purely are in the essence of the spiritual beings. Many other things could be said, « but there suffice to the wise not only these things themselves » which have been said, « but » also « the dijudication of one significative image unto the declaration of the neighboring, » that is, similar images, « to be made in like manner. » And therefore [1151D] « this too is to be inspected, » namely that the angels themselves « are said to have been called rivers, and wheels and chariots connected, » that is, adapted by signification « to the celestial essences, » or it is to be inspected that in the celestial essences they were said, that is, named, to be rivers, and wheels and chariots connected, that is, coupled and conjoined to one another. For « the fiery rivers signify the divine promotions, » that is, the promotions or effusions of divine grace, « giving » to those celestial essences « a copious, » that is, « not failing affluence, and nourishing them » with a life-giving fecundity. But the chariots, » in which many things coupled are rolled together, signify « the conjunctive society of the likenesses » which are in them, through [1152A] which they are moved concordantly by one assent unto all things. « But the wheels, » since they are indeed « winged, » that is, agile; « and since they are going inconvertibly, » that is, without retrogradation, and inflexibly, that is, without deviation, « into those things which are before the sight, » that is, the anterior things, signify « the virtue of the operation of them, namely of the essences: of the operation, I say, going forth by a right and just way, » by the intellectual rotation of all those essences at once, « supermundanely, » that is, spiritually « directed » into the same right way, that they may at once and concordantly go the proposed journey, without bending, that is, without deviation, « and rightly, » that is, without retrogradation. This, then, the wheels signify. And not only this, but « there is also, » that is, it happens, or is fitting, « by another [1152B] anagogy, » that is, spiritual interpretation, « to dijudicate the imaginary description of the intellectual wheels. For it was cried out to them, » namely the angels, « as the theologian says, » that is, the prophet: « Gel, gel, gel. » For Ezekiel says that, the wheels appearing and running, himself hearing, the angel called them voluble. In this, then, was cried out and shouted gel, whether to them, or by them. « But this signifies, » that is, gel, « according to the Hebrew word, revolutions and revelations. » The cause of each interpretation he subjoins: « For the fiery and Deiform wheels have indeed revolutions about the same best thing, with a motion ever movable, » that is, by a motion ever movable. Even as a circle for this reason is revolved into itself, because around one and [1152C] the same thing itself, that is, the center, it is ever moved; so the spiritual wheels as it were are revolved into themselves, while about that same thing which is best, that is, God, as though about their center, they are moved by ever surrounding it by contemplation. And therefore they are moved with an ever-movable motion, because both the desire is inextinguishable, and that which is desired is incomprehensible. On account of all these things the spiritual wheels have revolutions. « But they have revelations » in the manifestation « of the secret things, and in the circum-reduction of the subjects, » because to the subjects indeed they descend, illuminating them: but again they return, reducing them through contemplation to the higher things; and so they have revelations in « the deductive perfection of the high illuminations into those things which are subject; » because the illuminations [1152D] which they receive from on high they lead down unto the perfection of the subjects, through which they lead back those same subjects again to the higher things.
