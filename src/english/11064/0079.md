## EXPOSITION. (cont.)

There follows: « But there remains for us, » etc. After the explanation of the material figures and descriptions, he turns again to the interior things, about to contemplate last of all the joys of the angels. And well is the discourse concerning the celestial ones terminated in joy, because joy is the end of all celestial things. « There remains for us » a « discourse unto the explanation concerning the joy of the celestial dispositions, » because this alone now remains, that we make some discourse, for the sake of explanation, concerning the joy of the celestial ones. And well did he say that there remains a discourse not simply concerning joy, but concerning the joy of the celestial ones [1153A], because the celestial dispositions « are not at all receivers of that passible delectation which is according to us, » but « are said to rejoice together with God, » that is, to rejoice with a joy like the divine joy, at the « finding of the lost, » as when, the lost sheep being brought back, and the drachma found, they are invited to rejoice together. And « according to that deiform exultation » which, at the return of the prodigal son, was figured and perfected in the fatted calf. Or it can be read thus: They are said to rejoice together with God at the finding of the lost, not affected by any temporal and suddenly, passibly arisen gladness, but « according to a deiform feasting, » that is, the delectation and pleasantness which they impassibly enjoy, imitating the eternal joy of God. And [1153B] « they are said to rejoice together with God » according to deiformity, « in the providence and salvation of those returning unto God, » because after the likeness of God they provide for the salvation of those returning to God. They are said also to rejoice together with God according to « the gladness of incorruption, » that is, the incorrupt, or fully and perfectly sweet gladness, to which no bitterness is mingled; and according to that « ineffable beneficence » of divine grace, through which, an interior sweetness being poured forth, it refreshes the holy hearts, « in the participation of which, » that is, of which, namely, sweetness or beneficence, « holy men » also, that is, even holy men, « have been made partakers, through the deific advents of the divine illuminations, » coming, namely, from above. « So much by me, » etc. [1153C] He forms a clause in which by a brief recapitulation he establishes the end of the narration. « So much, » he says, « has been said by me concerning the sacred formations: » said, I say, « deficient from a diligent manifestation, » that is, from a diligent manifestation « of them, » namely the formations; because I confess that those things which have been said by me are less than sufficient unto the diligent and perfect manifestation of the sacred formations. But nevertheless, although unto this they are not perfect, they are yet « perfect, » « as I esteem, » unto another thing, namely « unto our not remaining lowlily in the figurative [1154A] phantasies, » that is, unto this, that they may instruct us how (at least altogether) we may not remain « lowlily, » that is, abjectly, « in the figurative phantasies, » believing nothing else to be than what we find written according to the formation of visible things. But rather, being instructed by the figurative things, let us ascend to the knowledge of those things which are true. « But if thou also shalt say this, » thou, some one, or thou, Timothy, « how » we « have not made » memory, or « mention, of all the angelic virtues » and « operations » and « images » which « are found in the oracles: » then, that is, after those things which we have said, « we answer the truth, » that is, « that » which is true; and that in those very things, namely virtues, or operations, or images, « of which the supermundane, » [1154B] that is, the too-lofty, « knowledge we have been ignorant, we desire another leader of light teaching » us, that is, that he may teach us, and lead us to the light of knowledge. « But certain things, as being equipotent to the things said, » that is, equal and not greater to those which we have said, « we have passed over; » yet « providing for the commensuration of the discourse, » that is, providing that our discourse should not pass its measure, and that we should say too much, if we said even this whole which we could say. Likewise also « honoring the secret » which is « above us » « with silence, » that is, by being silent, or through silence. For silence honors the secret, because to that which is kept silent a greater reverence is sometimes shown. And [1154C] some things which could be said must sometimes be covered, that there may always remain over what is to be sought within, and lest it grow cheap if the whole be expounded.

These things indeed upon the hierarchy of blessed Dionysius, according to the possibility of our understanding, I have presumed. But I fear lest, adding my own things to his, I be convicted of having overlaid a marble wall with mud. Therefore let the presumption be turned aside, let devotion implore. But it brings me solace, that of him whom he undertook to expound, in whom wisdom transcends, holiness condescends.
