## PREFACE. *On the varied exposition of Holy Scripture, and on Solomon's intention.*

[0113C]

What I lately discoursed before you concerning the book of Solomon which is called Ecclesiastes, [0114C] I have now marked down with the pen, briefly running over it (because certain things there seemed worthy of remembrance). All Scripture expounded according to its own proper interpretation both shines out more clearly, [0115A] and opens to its readers an easier approach to the understanding of itself. Many, not understanding the power of the Scriptures, becloud their comeliness and beauty with foreign expositions; and when they ought to have unlocked hidden things, they darken even the manifest ones. But to me they seem to lie under a like fault, both those who obstinately deny that the mystical understanding and the profundity of the allegories is to be sought in Holy Scripture where it is, and those who superstitiously contend that it is to be applied where it is not. Wherefore in this work I judge that I must not labor much over tropologies, or over mystical senses of allegories to be searched out through the whole sequence of his narrative at least: especially since the author himself here aims not so much at movements to be instructed, or at mysteries to be recounted, as at stirring the human heart [0115B] to contempt of worldly things by the manifest truth of his reasonings and by evident exhortation. Nor yet do I deny this, that many uncertain mystical things belong to this narrative, which require their own proper explanation . especially in what follows, since always, in the advance of the narrative, he touches more and more upon spiritual things according to the increase of contemplation, and is lifted up from things visible. But it is one thing to attend to that by which the whole intention of the writer and the whole sequence of the narrative is led; and another to hold that certain things said mystically by accident, and to be understood spiritually, are not to be passed over negligently. Now therefore we undertake to explain the surface of the narrative, which is strong with so great a loveliness of speech and of sentiments, so that the things which you now read written (this poor little lucubration, whatever it be, [0115C] affording a way to the understanding) you may henceforth rejoice not only to have written for you, but understood by you.
