## HOMILY THE FIRST. *On the title of the work, on Solomon's names, and books, and on the beginnings of this work, on the explanation of the vanities.* (cont.)

*What hath a man more of all his labour that he laboureth under the sun?* What hath he more — it is understood — than that which has been said of all things: Vanity of vanities, vanity of vanities, and all is vanity? As [0125B] if he were saying: If all things are subject to vanity, how can the works of men be alien from vanity? If according to something that is vain which God created, how is that not much more vain which man makes? If that is temporal which the Eternal made, what is that which the temporal makes? Therefore what hath a man more of all his labour that he laboureth under the sun? He laboureth under the sun, namely either as acting or as suffering. Distinguish these two: he who acts and he who suffers labors under the sun; each labors, both he who acts labors, and he who suffers labors. But this is the difference, that the one labors as though unwilling, the other willingly. For he who acts labors, and himself makes that from which he labors. But he who suffers labors, and does not himself make, but sustains, that from which he labors. These two kinds of men live in this world, namely those who labor [0125C] and act, and those who labor and suffer. Who are they that labor and act? Hear the Psalmist: *Nevertheless universal vanity, every man living. Nevertheless man passeth as an image; yea and he is disquieted in vain. He storeth up, and knoweth not for whom he shall gather these things [n: (Psal. XXXVIII)]*. Who are they that labor and suffer? Hear Paul the apostle: *The creature,* he says, *was made subject to vanity, not willingly; but by reason of him that made it subject, in hope [n: (Rom. VIII)]*. Therefore both labor: both those, namely, to whom this life is sweet, and those to whom this life is bitter. Those labor to enjoy its delights; these labor to be freed from its misery. Those labor in fear lest they go hence quickly; these labor in dread lest they remain here long. The labor of the former is reproved for vain solicitude. The labor [0125D] of the latter is crowned for patience. To the former it is said: *Sufficient for the day is the evil thereof [n: (Matth. VI)]*. And that: *Be not solicitous, saying: What shall we eat, or what shall we drink, or wherewith shall we be clothed? [n: (Ibid.)]* To the latter it is said: *Be you also patient, strengthen your hearts [n: (Jac. V)]*. And that: *If we suffer with him, we shall also reign with him. If we die with him, we believe that we shall live also together with him [n: (II Tim. II)]*. To the former it is said: *Ye sons of men, how long will ye be dull of heart, why do ye love vanity, and seek after lying? [n: (Psal. IV.)]* To the latter it is said: *Blessed are all they that fear the Lord, that walk in his ways. For thou shalt eat the labours of thy hands, blessed art thou, and it shall be well with thee [n: (Psal. CXXVII)]*. Therefore the labor of the former has a penalty in the work, and a penalty in the recompense. [0126A] But the labor of the latter has indeed a penalty in the work, but a reward in the recompense. In the labor of the former there is true misery. In the fruit of their labor, a false and vain consolation. In the labor of the latter, a temporal and transitory affliction. In the fruit of their labor, eternal blessedness. Therefore the labor of the former is empty in its fruit, true in its misery. The labor of the latter is transitory in its penalty, eternal in its fruit. But there, for the evil man, what of good seems to be present is vain; the evil of these men which appears is as it were vain, because it is transitory; but the good which does not appear is perpetual. Let it not therefore seem wondrous that we call the evil in this life laboring and acting, but the good only laboring and suffering, since if we look more closely upon the truth, we shall find the evil always laboring by acting, and the good always by suffering. [0126B] For as incontinence harasses the evil even then when they seem to be at rest, so patience keeps the good tranquil even when they are set in labors. Wherefore in a certain wondrous manner all the good and the just, when for God's sake they waste themselves with voluntary affliction, then also labor by suffering; because, setting themselves against themselves, what outwardly they inflict by raging in strictness, inwardly, remaining quiet and without disturbance, they bear in patience. But all the perverse and lovers of the carnal life, even then when they sustain evils brought upon them from without by another's violence, because inwardly they disturb themselves with the goads of fury and of impatience, they rather themselves make that from which they labor. Therefore the good labor in this life, but the evil labor even for the sake of this life; because the temporal labors to which all the depraved [0126C] and perverse subject themselves for the obtaining of temporal sweetness, the good bear patiently for the sake of eternal consolation. But because this mortal life, in which and for which men labor, tends daily through the spaces of living to the end of life, rightly is all the labor of man reckoned in the number of the vanities, when it is said: What hath a man more of all his labour that he laboureth under the sun? [nt: It is understood: apart from vanity.]
