## HOMILY V. *On the literal and moral exposition of the words of Ecclesiastes already spoken.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0153D]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I Ecclesiastes was king of Israel in Jerusalem.* ⟦s2⟧ First this seems to me by no means to be passed over: that some judge that Solomon wrote this book in penitence, and that he therefore so studiously reproves in it the vanity of worldly things, and most of all of carnal pleasure, because he himself had learned all things by experience, corrupted by the luxury of delights and seduced by the blandishments of women. ⟦s3⟧ And that therefore he was unwilling to say: I am king of Israel in Jerusalem; because, the purple of the kingdom laid aside, he had already come down from his throne, and, despising the power which he had ill held, he was bewailing his guilt in the habit of penitence — and therefore he says: he was king in Jerusalem. ⟦s4⟧ And would that it stood established that this is true, and that we had as certain a testimony concerning [0154A] his penitence as we have an undoubted one concerning his fault! ⟦s5⟧ But now, since we cannot affirm doubtful things for certain, let us pursue those which are not doubtful. ⟦s6⟧ I Ecclesiastes. ⟦s7⟧ He sets himself forth, as we have said, as an example of all the things he is about to say, so that hearers may the sooner give credence to his words, in that they understand that he learned what he taught not by hearing only, but by experiencing. ⟦s8⟧ As if he were to say. ⟦s9⟧ I who teach you have made trial of all the things I say. ⟦s10⟧ For who could better know what vanity there is in the summit of earthly power? ⟦s11⟧ For I was king of Israel in Jerusalem. ⟦s12⟧ A loftiness great enough: to reign over so great a people and in so noble and famous a city, and to see oneself upon the throne of the kingdom, lord over all in power and set above all in honor. ⟦s13⟧ And to what end these things? [0154B] I was, he says. ⟦s14⟧ As though he should say: Now I am not; because even if I am, I now recognize that that very thing which I am is nothing. ⟦s15⟧ And how he came to this, that he should recognize himself to be nothing, he then pursues, saying:

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I proposed in my mind to seek and to search out prudently concerning all things which are done under the sun.* ⟦s2⟧ As though he should say: I, outwardly swollen with power and inwardly puffed up with knowledge, attempted to seek things higher than myself. ⟦s3⟧ But where by vainly presuming I swelled above myself, there by failing I truly recognized the direction of myself. ⟦s4⟧ I proposed in my mind to seek and to search out wisely concerning all things which are done under the sun. ⟦s5⟧ A great purpose, if it could have come to effect; but how should the human mind suffice for so great things? ⟦s6⟧ Great pride therefore it is that mortal man should presume concerning so many things, and [0154C] again great curiosity to desire so many. ⟦s7⟧ I proposed, he says. ⟦s8⟧ See how all things sound of elation. ⟦s9⟧ He does not say, I thought; but, I proposed — whereas the temptation would at least have been venial and human if a swollen thought had touched the soul suddenly and in passing, as it is sometimes wont to do, and yet had not been received or approved by consent. ⟦s10⟧ But now the mind, not only lifted up but also blind, fell down into so great a depth of error that it not only thought to do that which was disordered, but even presumed that it could fulfil that which had been impossible. ⟦s11⟧ I proposed in my mind. ⟦s12⟧ A huge attempt. ⟦s13⟧ And what things? ⟦s14⟧ To seek, and to search out. ⟦s15⟧ To seek things unknown, and to search out things deep. ⟦s16⟧ How? ⟦s16⟧ Wisely. ⟦s17⟧ O foolish heart! [0154D] Therefore thou dost not seek wisdom, but professest only to seek by wisdom: for this thou hast said: I proposed in my mind to seek and to search out wisely concerning all things which are done under the sun. ⟦s18⟧ If therefore thou seekest wisely, thou wilt seek by wisdom. ⟦s19⟧ And thou sayest: Just so; I will seek by wisdom. ⟦s20⟧ For I should seek ill, if I did not seek by wisdom. ⟦s21⟧ For if I should seek without wisdom, even if I sought wisdom, I should seek foolishly. ⟦s22⟧ But to seek wisdom foolishly — that very thing would now be not to be wise, but to be without sense. ⟦s23⟧ Wherefore I will seek wisely, and I will seek by wisdom, and I will seek wisdom. ⟦s24⟧ Tell me then the wisdom by which thou wilt seek: where wilt thou seek? and by what wisdom wilt thou seek wisdom. ⟦s25⟧ By which dost thou seek? ⟦s26⟧ Already, he says, I have the wisdom [0155A] by which I may seek; and therefore by the wisdom which I have I will seek; and the wisdom by which I may seek, I will not seek. ⟦s27⟧ What wilt thou seek? ⟦s28⟧ Concerning all things which are done under the sun. ⟦s29⟧ What is "concerning all things"? ⟦s30⟧ Why didst thou not say, all things which are done under the sun? ⟦s31⟧ Because, he says, I proposed something greater, and much greater. ⟦s32⟧ For the things which are done under the sun are all visible, and therefore, though it be a great thing, yet it is not the highest, to seek and to search out those things which can be comprehended by the eye. ⟦s33⟧ Therefore I will not seek those things, but I will seek concerning those things. ⟦s34⟧ To seek and to search out concerning all things which are done under the sun. ⟦s35⟧ Not those things which are seen, but, concerning those which are seen, those things which are not seen. ⟦s36⟧ Not those things which are done under the sun, which are seen; but, concerning those which are done under the sun, the reasons and causes of those things which are not seen. ⟦s37⟧ These things therefore I proposed: [0155B] to seek and to search out wisely concerning all things which are done under the sun. ⟦s38⟧ Surely thou hast great wisdom. ⟦s39⟧ Who gave thee so great wisdom? ⟦s40⟧ Art thou a man, and wilt thou seek wisely concerning all things which are done under the sun? ⟦s41⟧ Whence hast thou such wisdom: God, thou sayest, gave it. ⟦s42⟧ Let us now see. ⟦s43⟧ For thou shalt not pass by unexamined. ⟦s44⟧ For we wish first to know whether thou canst render an account of thyself, thou who hast come with so great a purpose that thou thinkest to search out the reason of all the things which are done under the sun. ⟦s45⟧ Say then: in that wisdom by which thou proposest to search out the reason of all things, hast thou been able as yet to recognize at least thine own possibility: I, he says, unless I had hoped that I could bring the thing itself to effect — what I should believe altogether impossible, even if I thought to do it, yet I should by no means [0155C] propose that I would do it. ⟦s46⟧ O foolish heart! ⟦s47⟧ This is what I said: because thou hast not known thyself, therefore thou hast presumed to desire what was above thee — in the one blind, in the other swollen, in both foolish. ⟦s48⟧ Thou therefore wilt search out wisely concerning all things which are done under the sun. ⟦s49⟧ Search now, bestir thyself, give effort. ⟦s50⟧ Seek, and thou shalt find. ⟦s51⟧ What wilt thou find? ⟦s52⟧ Thou wilt find wisdom. ⟦s53⟧ What wisdom? ⟦s54⟧ That thou mayest know thine own folly. ⟦s55⟧ For thou shalt seek and shalt not find what thou seekest; thou shalt labor and shalt not profit, and vexation alone shall give understanding to the hearing.
