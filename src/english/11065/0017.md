## HOMILY VI. *What it is to be distended in the worst occupation.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0157D] *This worst occupation therefore God hath given to the sons of men, that they may be distended therein.* ⟦s2⟧ What is, that they may be distended? ⟦s3⟧ That outwardly they swell, inwardly they become empty. ⟦s4⟧ For what is distended appears indeed greater without; but whence it is seen to have taken increase from without, thence it is truly proved to have lost solidity within. ⟦s5⟧ For while it strives to reach beyond itself into that which is not, it begins to fail in itself and to grow empty of that which is. ⟦s6⟧ So indeed the human mind, while through curiosity it is stretched toward searching out those things which are outside itself, is hindered by the very occupation of its own searching, so that it cannot consider itself. ⟦s7⟧ This worst occupation therefore God hath given to the sons of men, that they may be distended therein. ⟦s8⟧ O worst [0158A] occupation! ⟦s8⟧ O ruinous distension! whither dost thou drag the mind? ⟦s9⟧ How much dost thou promise, and how much dost thou take away? ⟦s10⟧ Thou promisest to man the whole of what he himself is not, and thou takest away the whole of what he himself is. ⟦s11⟧ What if thou didst give the whole that thou promisest, and take away the whole that thou takest away? ⟦s12⟧ *For what doth it profit a man if he gain the whole world, but suffer the loss of his own soul? [n: (Matth. VI.)]* How much more now, since falsely thou promisest the whole, and truly thou takest away the whole? ⟦s13⟧ Thou art worst, distending and scattering, afflicting and torturing minds; suggesting to those whom thou rulest that those things are always to be desired whose searching out is either harmful or vain, and whose finding is much more either useless or ruinous. ⟦s14⟧ If therefore thou wert sweet, and couldst possess minds without labor, who would ever detect thy malice? ⟦s15⟧ But now [0158B] the very labor and affliction which thou bringest upon minds make thee hateful, and what thou promisest is the more easily recognized to be vain, while what thou bringest in is felt to be hard. ⟦s16⟧ Thus thou trainest many unto wisdom, who then at last fully detect how great a vanity follows after thee, when they have begun to feel by experience how great a difficulty lies hid in thee for those who labor. ⟦s17⟧ Therefore he added and said: *I saw all things that are done under the sun, and behold all is vanity and affliction of spirit [n: (Eccle. I)]*. ⟦s18⟧ I saw, he says, where, didst thou see? ⟦s19⟧ Who taught thee? ⟦s20⟧ By doing, he says, I saw; by experiencing I came to know. ⟦s21⟧ What didst thou do? ⟦s22⟧ *I set before,* he says, *in my mind to seek and to search out wisely concerning all things which are done under the sun [n: (Ibid.)]*. ⟦s23⟧ And behold, I had begun to seek and to search out, and I thought that in so laborious a study I should find some issue. ⟦s24⟧ I stretched out [0158C] therefore my mind as far as I could; I poured out my thought upon surveying all things. ⟦s25⟧ I took sleep away from my eyes, nor did I give rest to my heart night and day, until I might find what my soul was seeking, that I might find the reason concerning all things which are done under the sun. ⟦s26⟧ But what end could there have been in such attempts? ⟦s27⟧ I did and I failed, I strove and I was driven back, until my soul wasted away and my senses were darkened; since, in so great a multitude of things and in the depth of hidden truth, while the understanding began both to be scattered and to be beaten back, I was no longer able to bear the weight of labor and the straitness of an inextricable struggle. ⟦s28⟧ Then, returned into myself and as it were driven back from that dense and impassable pathlessness of hidden nature against which I was striving to press, I began to think that it is foolish [0158D] and vain that mortal man should seek with so obstinate a zeal the knowledge of those things which not only were made outside his nature, but also can neither add anything to his salvation if they be known, nor take anything away if they be not known. ⟦s29⟧ There therefore I saw that God hath given this worst occupation to the sons of men; that they may be distended therein, because all things which are done under the sun are all vanity and affliction of spirit. ⟦s30⟧ For because they have no fruit, they are vain; and because they afflict, they are harmful. ⟦s31⟧ For they do not help those who possess them, and they afflict those who seek them. ⟦s32⟧ For they cannot be sought without labor, but they can be possessed without profit. ⟦s33⟧ For the mind, as soon as it has been occupied with the care and desire of seeking and of finding them, and has poured itself out into them through the zeal of curiosity [0159A] and the solicitude of cupidity — to be afflicted with a punishment worthy of its own rashness — is both scattered by their multiplicity and beaten back by their difficulty. ⟦s34⟧ I saw therefore all things which are done under the sun, and behold all is vanity and affliction of spirit. ⟦s35⟧ I saw that this very thing also had been vain, that I proposed in my mind to seek and to search out wisely concerning all things which are done under the sun. ⟦s36⟧ I saw that it is vain that the sons of men are occupied with the worst occupation, that they may be distended therein; and all this I saw when I had made trial of it, and I saw how great labor and affliction of spirit there was in this, that I proposed in my mind to seek and to search out wisely concerning all things which are done under the sun. ⟦s37⟧ And I saw that all is vanity and affliction of spirit in all things which are done under the sun. ⟦s38⟧ These things therefore I saw. ⟦s39⟧ But why do not all the sons of men likewise [0159B] see the worst occupation which God hath given them, that they may be distended therein, so as to understand and see the vanity and affliction of spirit in all things which are done under the sun [sic: *sole*]? ⟦s40⟧ Why do they labor and not feel it; are afflicted and scattered; and are distended in the worst occupation, and do not understand their own vanity and the affliction of spirit in all things which are done under the sun? ⟦s41⟧ Because, he says, the perverse are corrected with difficulty. ⟦s42⟧ Of which let us speak more at large.
