## HOMILY VII. *That the perverse are corrected with difficulty,* etc.

*The perverse are corrected with difficulty.* Therefore they cannot understand, because they are perverse; and therefore they labor and do not feel it, and they take delight in their own affliction *(Job [0159C] XXX)*, and think it is well with them while it is ill. For they are perverse to such a degree that the judgment of truth is not in them, thinking *good* to be what is *evil*, and what is *good* to be *evil, putting darkness light, and light darkness;* and esteeming *bitter sweet, and sweet bitter [n: (Isa. V)]*. For they reckon that there are delights beneath the senses; and therefore they love their own griefs, and thrust themselves willingly upon the worst occupation, and are distended therein; because, while with blinded mind they desire what falsely seems to delight, they do not attend to what truly tortures and afflicts. Therefore then they do not see their own vanity and the affliction of spirit in all things which are done under the sun, because they are perverse and are corrected with difficulty. And *the number of fools is infinite.* For they are not only turned away, but also [0159D] perverse. Turned away from God; in themselves perverse. And being turned away they are not easily converted, because being perverse they are corrected with difficulty. For if they were corrected in themselves, they would be converted through themselves. Because if they first received the judgment of truth in themselves, they would afterward see by their own judgment that vanity must be fled and truth followed. But now, being turned away, they do not see the light of truth; nor, being perverse, do they see by the light of truth; and therefore they are corrected with difficulty, because they know neither how to seek rightness and truth when absent, nor how to approve them when present. Therefore the perverse are corrected with difficulty, and the number of fools is infinite. For they would not be so many if they could easily be corrected. And in this it is manifestly shown how difficult the correction of the perverse is, that the multitude of fools [0160A] is infinite Now therefore the perverse and [sic: *stultitam*] are as hard to correction by their perversity as they are bold against truth by their multitude; and therefore the perverse are corrected with difficulty, and the number of fools is infinite. Wilt thou know, he says, how hard it is for the perverse to be corrected? Take me. I will this day become for thee an example of all. For I too was once perverse, and I myself did not understand my own perversity; but I thought myself to be upright, and to be acting rightly, when *I proposed in my mind to seek and to search out wisely concerning all things which are done under the sun;* nor was there then any consideration of truth in me which might correct and restrain me, lest I should attempt to exceed my own measure, nor presume to desire those things which have not been given to men to know and to search out. I went down therefore into [0160B] the *worst occupation* which *God hath given to the sons of men, that* they may be distended *therein,* and I scattered my mind upon all things, and began to run through all things with thought, that I might seek and search out concerning all things which are done under the sun: but I was not able. And I began to labor and to be vehemently afflicted with weariness; and I saw that my mind, whithersoever it turned itself, was filled with grief and bitterness and anxiety, and the labor of seeking was immense, and the fruit of finding scanty. It came to pass therefore that at length the punishment itself and the great affliction made me prudent. *And I saw all things which are done under the sun, and behold all is vanity and affliction of spirit.* I knew therefore the truth, and I rejected vanity, because I felt the affliction. And I seemed already wholly corrected of my perversity; but there was still another entanglement; [0160C] and I did not know that the perverse are corrected with difficulty. I was then already corrected, so that I might know truth out of vanity, and I was not yet corrected so that I should not be proud of the knowledge of truth. One chaff of error had already been shaken off, and there still remained another, more tenacious and clinging, the chaff of elation. For the perverse are corrected with such difficulty that they either do not admit correction and purgation at all, or do not receive it to perfection. Therefore I had already begun to be corrected, but I was not yet perfectly corrected; and therefore I knew the labor of my presumption, but I did not know the use of my finding. I knew that it is a vain affliction to seek things not profitable to salvation; but I did not yet know that it is a vain elation [0160D] to be proud of things found.

Therefore *I spoke in my heart, saying: Behold, I am become great.* For I knew not that I had swollen with puffing up, and therefore I seemed great to myself, and I said in my heart: Behold, I am become great. In my heart I said: for out of the heart I swelled, and therefore in the heart I said it; because in the heart I saw whence I swelled, so that the elation might be there where the cause of the elation was. I spoke in my heart: Behold, I am become great. How great!

*I went before,* he says, *in wisdom all who were before me in Jerusalem.* Little by little one proceeds into corruption, and the depraved mind, as the swelling grows, is always cast headlong into the worse. First it thought of itself more than it ought; then it came even to the contempt of others. [0161A] I went before in wisdom all who had been before me in Jerusalem. Therefore then thou didst swell and wast puffed up, because thou didst go before in wisdom all who were before thee in Jerusalem. And how great a thing was this, that thou shouldst therefore lift up thy neck, because thou didst go before in wisdom all who were before thee in Jerusalem? Do not wonder, he says. Often pride rejoices more in singularity than in comparison. For elation does not always care to attend to what sort those are whom it goes before in the comparison; because sometimes this alone abounds for its exaltation, that among all who were before it in Jerusalem it recognizes none equal to itself. For this reason therefore I too, seeing that I went before in wisdom all who were before me in Jerusalem — even if I discerned that there had been few wise men before me in Jerusalem, [0161B] yet I gloried vehemently, considering myself superior to them in wisdom; since, although those before me, in comparison with whom I was made pre-eminent, were neither very many nor great, yet I saw that many wise men could come after me in Jerusalem, to whom I should become the first by the example of wisdom. That therefore I preferred myself in wisdom in comparison with those who went before looked also to this, that I gloried that I was established by a pre-eminent example into an authority for those to come, so that to those who went before I might be found the highest, but to those who came after the first author of wisdom. And therefore I spoke in my heart, saying: Behold, I am become great, and I went before in wisdom all who were before me in Jerusalem.

*And my mind hath contemplated many things wisely, and [0161C] I have learned.* Therefore thou wast already somewhat corrected, because thou didst not say, my mind hath contemplated all things wisely, as thou hadst above proposed *to seek and to search out wisely concerning all things which are done under the sun;* but thou didst bring in something more moderate, saying: My mind hath contemplated many things wisely — that thou mightest show thyself taught by the worst occupation, and to have seen the vanity and affliction of spirit, so as not to presume any further concerning all things; but yet not fully corrected, so as not to be proud concerning very many. By seeking all things therefore thou didst find very many, not all; and by failing toward all thou didst understand that man ought not to presume concerning all things; but by finding very many thou didst not consider that man ought not to be proud even concerning very many. Therefore in some respect thou wast corrected, and [0161D] in some respect uncorrected, when thou didst say: My mind hath contemplated many things wisely and I have learned; because, although they were many in comparison with few, yet those very things were few in comparison with all. But this looked rather to humility: that thou, who hadst already made trial of thine own failing with regard to all things, shouldst call the very many things thou hadst found not many in comparison with the few of others, but rather few in comparison with those things, all of them, which thou couldst not attain. But because the rashness of curiosity had already been repressed through its own failure and affliction in the searching out of all things, but the swelling of elation had grown in the contemplation of very many things, therefore thou didst still swell and say: Behold, I am become great, and I went before in wisdom all who were before me in Jerusalem, and my mind hath contemplated many things wisely [0162A] and I have learned. But, I ask thee, if thou didst go before in wisdom all who were before thee in Jerusalem, from whom didst thou learn, that thou shouldst say: My mind hath contemplated many things wisely and I have learned? If thou wast wiser than all, from whom couldst thou learn what thou thyself didst not know? Or wast thou therefore also wiser than all, because thou didst learn from all, and the wisdom of all was gathered in thee alone, so that thence thou mightest go before in wisdom, out of all men, each one severally — in saying which thou hadst all men as teachers, from each severally? Or perhaps thou didst not learn from men, but the trials of things taught thee what thy mind contemplated wisely, so that thence the contemplation of wisdom might become more certain to reason, in that the very outcome of things manifestly proved it to one making trial? For it does not seem to accord with thy character that thou shouldst learn from men, [0162B] thou who hadst been raised in esteem so far above men that thou didst think to seek and to search out not only many things wisely, but even all things which are done under the sun: therefore thou didst contemplate and learn, because thou didst think and prove, so that thou mightest demonstrate the force and power of thy understanding to be so much the greater, the more evidently thou didst bring forth all the arguments of thy reason to the visible trials of things. And perhaps this man willingly became a fool in his own wisdom, that he might teach fools wisdom. For he would not so diligently set forth to us the ways of his own error, unless in that same error of his he were thinking of our instruction. Therefore he still redoubles and commends that evil diligence of his, and subjoins how he obtained that wisdom of which he swelled, [0162C] saying:

*And I gave my heart that I might know prudence and doctrine, and errors and folly.* Which words can be joined to those above in two ways under the same sense; for, being about first to show this elation, he had thus begun. I spoke in my heart; and then he subjoins what he spoke. Behold, I am become great, and I went before in wisdom all who were before me in Jerusalem; and my mind hath contemplated many things wisely and I have learned; and afterward he brought in that which we have just now set forth. And I gave my heart that I might know prudence and doctrine, errors and folly. This whole series can be comprehended under one continuation, so that he is understood to have said all this in his heart: namely, that he was made great; and that [0162D] he went before in wisdom all who were before him in Jerusalem; and that his mind contemplated many things wisely and learned many things; and that he gave his heart that he might know prudence and doctrine, and errors and folly; and that after all these things, when he had thus spoken in his heart and had exalted himself concerning the searching out and the consideration of so great things, at last, coming to his senses, he recognized that this glorying also was vain, and on this account subjoins:
