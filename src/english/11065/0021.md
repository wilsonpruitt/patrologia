## HOMILY VIII. *That man flees away from the truth: as Adam in paradise fled, and hid himself.* (cont.)

*I thought* therefore *in my heart to withdraw my flesh from wine, that I might carry over my mind to wisdom, and might shun folly, until I should see what was useful for the sons of men: with what deed there is need under the sun, in the number of the days of his life.* O human heart, where art thou? How hast thou come hither, that thou knowest not this — what is useful for the sons of men, with what deed there is need under the sun, in the number of the days of his life? Where art thou, that thou art ignorant of this? *Where art thou, Adam, where art thou? I heard,* he says, *thy voice, and I hid myself [n: (Gen. III)]*. O thou hidden one, from whom hast thou hidden thyself? O [0166A] hidden one, where art thou? How far off art thou, and how near art thou? How far art thou from him, and how near art thou to him? Therefore, where art thou? Behold, thy God seeks thee and cries: Where art thou? Thou turnest thyself away that thou mayest lie hid, and closest thine eyes that thou mayest not see. But he, seeing one who does not see — and therefore does not see, because he desires not to be seen — cries and says, on account of thy affection and not on account of any defect of his own: Adam, where art thou? He seeks thee, therefore, that thou mayest find him; and he seeks thee that thou too mayest seek him and say: Lord, where art thou? But thou, what dost thou do? For he, if he has lost, yet seeks; if he has lost, he has not lost the whole, because he sees what he has lost; and therefore he seeks what he has lost, because he sees what he has lost. For he would not seek unless he saw. But thou, what? Adam, where art thou? Thou hast lost, and the whole; thou hast lost, because thou hast both lost, [0166B] that thou mightest not have, and hast ceased to see, that thou mightest not seek. For if thou seest what thou hast not, thou understandest what thou art to seek. But now thou art far off and art exiled from the truth, so that thou canst not see whence thou hast come and whither thou must return. Therefore thou seekest what thou oughtest to seek; because thou knowest not what thou hast lost. Thou seekest what is useful for the sons of men: with what deed there is need under the sun, in the number of the days of his life! Oh, how thou oughtest to have known this! But now thou art hidden from the truth, lying hid under the shadow of thine own ignorance; because thou hast withdrawn far off and hast been unwilling to be with her, without whom thou canst not be. For because thou canst not be without her, therefore thou seekest; and because thou art turned away and hidden, therefore thou knowest not what thou oughtest to seek — though yet in this thou hast by no means been able to be hidden from her, so as not to understand that thou must seek, [0166C] since thou seest that this too is lacking to thee which thou art seeking. Understand, therefore, that at least in this thou art utterly not hidden; because she follows him who flees, and presses herself upon him who is turned away, and teaches that she must be sought by him to whom she does not offer herself to be seen. Although this very thing too, outside the truth, thou dost not see — that thou seest it must be sought by thee, that thou seest it is lacking, granted that what it is thou dost not see. Therefore in the truth thou seest that thou must seek what thou dost not see to be the truth. Why is this? Because thou art hidden. For if thou wert not hidden, thou wouldest see that nothing besides the truth must be sought; and the truth itself thou wouldest not seek, but wouldest have. Now thou art hidden, and thou dost not see; and yet thou art not forsaken, because thou art admonished to seek what thou dost not see. Therefore it is said to thee: Adam, where art thou? — that thou mayest return to the truth and find the truth. [0166D] Thou wert unwilling to stand in the truth; now return to the truth, because thou canst not stand outside the truth. A wanderer and a fugitive and unstable shalt thou be all the days in which thou art not with her and in her; nor shall thy heart find where it may rest, if it be unwilling to stand in her; because neither will it stand outside her so as to seek, nor will it find, besides her, anything in which to rest. Upward, downward, far off and near, wherever it may go, it will not find rest until it finds her. Why art thou in tumult, unhappy one? Thou laughest, thou weepest, thou art cherished, thou art afflicted: what is that to wisdom? For because in wantonness and pleasure thou hast been foolish, in affliction thou shalt be wretched. What more will they be able to confer upon thee? *Vanity of vanities: vanity of vanities, and all things vanity [n: (Eccle. I)]*. [0167A] But thou hadst not yet come to know these things. Therefore, by thine own sense and thine own wisdom — which in her sight is folly — puffed up rather than solid, thou wert trying to struggle on, and didst suppose that thou wouldest do something when thou wert nothing. Thou hast tried all things, and everywhere thou hast failed. Soft things and hard, smooth and rough, prosperous and adverse: outside the truth they can corrupt, they cannot amend. Therefore thou wavest and art tossed; thou fallest and art thrown down and art driven on; thou desirest and dost not obtain. Groping and not seeing, thou goest forward in vain against what is opposite, seeking the truth where it is not. And thou, where art thou? She is within, and thou art without; and therefore she cries to thee and says: Adam, where art thou? Thou seekest, that she may reprove thy ignorance; she walks, that she may show thy instability. When the Lord was walking about *in paradise at the hour after midday, he called and said: Adam, [0167B] where art thou? [n: (Gen. III.)]* He did not walk, but walked about. What is "he walked about"? Hither and thither, as if wandering and roving, not going straight forward, but proceeding wherever — this is to have walked about. And why did he wish to walk about thus? That he might show himself outwardly such as he (had begun to be) within. For already the truth had been moved, and was wavering, so as to withdraw from the heart of the sinner. Nay rather, the truth stood and the sinful conscience wavered; and therefore the truth walked about without, because within the sinner wavered away from the truth. Yet it walked about and did not depart, nor did it go off straight on, drawing far away as though irrevocable and never to return again; but circling near and walking about close by, it went away and did not go away, now going and now returning, and surging with a certain great uncertainty, as though unwilling to desert its own seat and yet not [0167C] able to endure a polluted dwelling. What doest thou, Adam? Why art thou silent? Call her as she departs, follow her as she flees; since she is still near and waits to see whether perchance she be called back, and therefore she makes delay and gently threatens her departure, grieving that she has been injured and violently cast out; and she signifies that she wishes to return, if by chance she be invited to come. Therefore she signifies that she has not gone quickly away, but is waiting still: that she will go, nevertheless; and now, because there is no one to call her back, that she will go; and behold, that she walks in order to depart — since there is no one mindful of love, who remembers charity, who has at heart the affection of fellowship. O unhappy Adam, where is thy remembrance? Where hast thou fixed thy soul, that thou endurest these things? O hard one, hardened and obdurate, could not so great kindness, so great a flame, so vast an ardor of love soften thee, that thou shouldest melt and run [0167D] after her? But thou, what doest thou? *I heard,* he says, *thy voice, and I hid myself.* Why? *Because I was naked [n: (ibid.)]*. Therefore thou fleest the truth, because thou lovest depravity. But she, what does she do? She follows him who flees, she who forsook him as he departed. She who was turned away from him who did wicked things calls back him who persists in iniquity. Adam, where art thou? I, he says, know where thou art; thou knowest not. Therefore, where art thou? Attend to where thou art, that thou mayest correct what thou hast been made, and return to him by whom thou wert made. Adam, where art thou? I heard, Lord, thy voice, and I hid myself. O hidden and not hidden, not seeing and yet seen, from whom hast thou hidden thyself? I heard the voice, and the face thou didst not see. Why? Because hidden. For this thou hast gained by hiding thyself, that thou shouldest not see him by whom thou wast seen. Whom indeed [0168A] thou wast able to flee, but wast not able to escape; because not even hidden didst thou lie concealed from him. Therefore, being sought, thou wast found, because lying hid thou wast not hidden. But thou, by thy malice, fleest again, and again fleest, and art found and fleest; and always thou fleest, and dost not escape. Held, thou fleest; sought, thou fleest; searched out, thou deniest. Therefore, being sought, thou art found; having denied, thou art convicted; convicted, thou art condemned. Let us see, therefore, now, lest perchance we have made so great a digression without cause. Let us see our Adam — that ancient one, and new still by a repeated malice. Let us see him renewing still his old pursuits, and, a deserter from the truth, seeking the shade and the hiding-places and the coverings of leaves; convicted on every side, never willing to be reproved. Not able to lie hid, and always fleeing. Let us see, therefore, what he does. [0168B] For what does he do? *He saw* the food and gazed upon it, since it is delightful to the sight and sweet *to eat [n: (ibid.)]*; and he opened his jaws and said: I will go, and I will abound in delights, and I will enjoy good things. And behold, behind his back wisdom and truth cried out and said: Adam, where art thou? But he, hearing her voice and convicted by the truth crying within, could not deny the truth, and said: I heard, Lord, thy voice; and I saw truly that this was vanity, and therefore laughter I counted an error, and to joy I said: Why art thou deceived in vain? Behold a man hearing the truth and confessing the truth. Who, think you, is this man? He seems altogether great, and drawing near to the truth, who thus professes the truth. Truly great, if he were not hidden. For he hears, and does not see the truth; because he himself is without through concupiscence, [0168C] but the truth is within through wisdom. And the truth wishes to win him, and calls him back to herself, and again and so again cries from within and says: Adam, where art thou? But he, feeling himself betrayed and convicted, when one approach is blocked up, seeks other hiding-places for flight; and, held in check from pleasure, pours himself out toward avarice For indeed he first sought after the fruit for eating; now he seeks the leaves for shade, and hides himself from the light of the truth the more deeply, the more wickedly he covers the vice of ambition under the pursuit of thrift. Therefore he adds and says: I thought in my heart to withdraw my flesh from wine. Why? That I might, he says, carry over my mind to wisdom, and might shun folly. Well hast thou done this. *For no man can serve two masters [n: (Matth. VI)]*. Therefore thou hast done well, in cutting away [0168D] from thee the cloudy and darkening conscience, the delight of the flesh, so that thou mightest be able to find the luminous delight of truth, having nothing of impurity. Well hast thou done in this, that thou didst propose to withdraw thy flesh from wine, that thou mightest the better make thy soul drunk with wisdom, and gather it inward to its very self: where wisdom shines, where truth is known and prudence is found. For this very thing too was to shun folly: not to pour oneself out after empty and vain things to be desired, but to conform to the truth the whole intention of the mind and all the endeavors of the heart, restraining them under the governance of reason. This, therefore, thou hast done well. But how long? Until, he says, I should see what was useful for the sons of men: with what deed there is need [0169A] under the sun, in the number of the days of his life. See therefore that thou persevere, that thou seek in the truth, and that thou seek perseveringly; because the truth is not found save by those who seek her in the truth. Who are they who seek her in the truth? Those who approach her with the whole heart. Those who do not come half and withdraw half, but come whole and come wholly: these seek the truth in the truth. But those who gather their heart in one place and scatter it in another — these do not approach whole, nor do they approach wholly, and therefore they do not find the truth; because what they seemed to have gained of the truth in one place, they lose in another. Who gather wages and put *them into a bag with holes [n: (Agg. I)]*. If thrift gathers and avarice scatters, what does it profit? What does it confer if, binding the gullet, thou hast gathered through continence of pleasure, [0169B] and, opening the eyes, hast scattered through ambition? Thou markest that thou hast entered; but that thou hast gone out, thou markest not. If thou enterest and goest out, thou art without; just as, if thou goest out and enterest, thou art within. But if thou art without, wherever thou art thou art not with the truth; because the truth is within. And what does it matter where thou art, if thou art not there where the truth is? From wherever thou comest and by whatever way thou enterest, if thou art within, thou art well; because thou art with the truth. And wherever thou goest, by whatever way thou goest out, if thou art without, thou art ill; because thou art not with the truth. If, therefore, thou seekest in the truth, seek whole, approach whole, enter whole; because the truth is within. Why dost thou seek so long, and dost not find what thou seekest? Because thou seekest ill, because thou seekest there where what thou seekest is not. For thou seekest without, and what thou seekest is within. Therefore thou seekest ill, and [0169C] therefore thou canst not find what thou seekest, and for that reason thou wilt not be able to find rest there, where art thou? because the truth is not there where thou art. Where art thou? Under the shade, under the leaves; because thou hast lost the fruit, and therefore appearance deceives thee and the truth is not in thee. Wherever he shall have gone, thou wilt be defrauded: shadow is the whole of what thou seest, and the truth lies hidden. What is the shadow? The appearance of visible things is the shadow, and thou hast poured out thy soul into them, and hast hidden thyself under the leaves that thou mightest lie hid. And how dost thou seek the light while entering the darkness: in the fruit thou hast beheld vanity, and in the leaves dost thou think the truth to be? If all earthly things are vain for enjoying: how are they not much more vain for the seeing? For if they do not confer the true good on those who enjoy them, what can they confer on those who possess [0169D] and do not enjoy? Why then, after the good purpose of continence and the zeal for seeking wisdom, art thou so quickly turned again to vain occupation? Why? Save because thy mind has been cast forth without, and made a wanderer and a fugitive upon the earth. And therefore it cannot stand, because it is not fixed in the truth. Therefore, thou sayest, *I made my works great,* etc.
