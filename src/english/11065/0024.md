## HOMILY X. *On the remaining vanities, as far as that place: « The fool walketh in darkness. »* (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *I passed over to contemplate wisdom.* "Hebrew" is interpreted *one passing over*. ⟦s3⟧ This man, therefore, was made a Hebrew in order to contemplate wisdom. ⟦s4⟧ And indeed he himself was a Hebrew before too according to the flesh, but he was not a Hebrew according to the truth, until he began to pass over to contemplate wisdom. ⟦s5⟧ Just as one is called a Jew according to the flesh, and a Jew [0174B] according to the truth, so one is a Hebrew according to the flesh and another a Hebrew according to the truth. ⟦s6⟧ But he who is openly a Jew according to the flesh, and he who is openly a Hebrew according to the flesh, is not truly a Jew, and is not a true Hebrew. ⟦s7⟧ But he who in secret is a Jew according to the spirit, and according to the spirit is a Hebrew, is truly a Jew and truly a Hebrew. ⟦s8⟧ For many pass over according to the flesh and do not pass over according to the truth, because they do not pass over in order to arrive at the truth. ⟦s9⟧ They would more easily cross the seas and traverse far-off regions than reach the truth — and she is near. ⟦s10⟧ *Near is,* says Scripture, *the word in thy mouth [n: (Rom. X)]*; and the truth of the word is in the heart, and the word itself is truth, because the truth is the word. [0174C] And though the truth herself is so near, yet nothing is farther from those who love folly. ⟦s11⟧ But what is more foolish than always to look upon the lowest things, and to have countenances bent down to the earth? ⟦s12⟧ For this was given to the beasts, to whom it is granted to desire nothing beyond. ⟦s13⟧ But wisdom dwells in the things above; and to her they are unwilling to be lifted up, who are compared to the beasts and gaze upon the earth. ⟦s14⟧ This is that very folly out of which this man passed over to contemplate wisdom, when he despised the false goods which he had loved on earth, and lifted himself up to contemplate and to love the true goods which are above; because in all these things which are below under the sun he beheld vanity. ⟦s15⟧ Therefore, when he had seen in all things vanity and affliction of mind, and that nothing remains under [0174D] the sun, he passed over to contemplate wisdom above the sun. ⟦s16⟧ O how difficult a passage, and a way of how many days, for those who have weak steps! ⟦s17⟧ And I know not whether the whole life of a man can suffice to accomplish this journey. ⟦s18⟧ For who, living in this mortal life, will be able to lay hold of wisdom fully? ⟦s19⟧ For wisdom is drawn from hidden things and is found in hidden things; and wisdom always loves the secret place, nor does she offer herself to be contemplated save to those who enter in to her. ⟦s20⟧ But this is very grievous to weak souls: that they should leave what they have loved and forsake what is beloved, and pass over to the desiring of things which they do not know unless they be instructed by their own griefs, and that things which before were sweet should begin to be bitter; and thus in a certain manner many are drawn back, and are compelled to enter and to pass over to contemplate [0175A] wisdom. ⟦s21⟧ For when they have begun to taste and to understand the sweetness and pleasantness of wisdom, then they have no more need to be compelled, but follow willingly; and they gladly despise all things and leave all things, that they may pass over to contemplate wisdom; and then they arrive quickly, because they run swiftly and seek ardently, and wisdom begins to be near to them, because they themselves draw near to her and do not go far off from her into alien desires. ⟦s22⟧ So when Moses was in the desert a shepherd of animals, and had an Ethiopian woman for a wife, he did not yet know any fire but a consuming one; therefore he wondered that the bush was burning and was not burned up [n: (Exod. III)]. ⟦s23⟧ For as long as the soul feeds its own desires upon the lowest pleasures, and, bound by carnal concupiscence, has not yet come to the embraces [0175B] of wisdom, it is in a fire consuming and devouring, even to perdition. ⟦s24⟧ For the flame of lust and the love of the flesh consume those whom they kindle. ⟦s25⟧ But the love of wisdom has light, and has no consuming; and if it kindles the cold, yet it does not burn up those who are burning. ⟦s26⟧ Therefore Moses wondered how the bush was burning and was not burned up. ⟦s27⟧ For he too was burning, because he had an Ethiopian wife; nor was this a miracle to him, but to burn and not be burned up: what he had not experienced, this was a miracle to him. ⟦s28⟧ And the miracle itself drew him and urged him to pass over and see the greatest vision — how the bush was burning and was not burned up; and he began to run into a sweeter desire, and to forget the old longings where he was burning and was burned up, [0175C] and to loathe the love of his Ethiopian wife, because she was black and was not beautiful, nor like that Shunamite woman who warms old men and does not burn the young. ⟦s29⟧ For that fire, by which the bush was burning and was not burned up, did not signify the love of this Shunamite, that is, of wisdom; and therefore Moses, after he had passed over to see the vision, and had begun to feel how sweetly her flame burned, could no longer be held by the love of his Ethiopian wife, but, his heart kindled with a better fire, came even to converse with the Most High, and was sent for the salvation of the people. ⟦s30⟧ For in fire God spoke to him, and out of the fire he was heard, and through fire he was understood. ⟦s31⟧ And there was within a flame which had set his heart alight, by which, like the bush, he was burning and was not burned up. ⟦s32⟧ The mind itself had conceived God in fire, [0175D] and through fire it knew God. ⟦s33⟧ For unless it had burned, it would not have seen nor known; because fire itself is love, and love itself is knowledge. ⟦s34⟧ He knew, therefore, and loved; he saw and burned; he tasted and loved; and fire conquered fire, love overcame delight. ⟦s35⟧ The Ethiopian woman was despised, deformed and black, whom her own flame had darkened, and her very love made her not lovable. ⟦s36⟧ She was despised in comparison with the fairer one; nor did he wish to serve any longer for her, who recognized that with her he would always be a slave. ⟦s37⟧ For the carnal life and the concupiscence of the flesh is not from God the Father, but from the devil the father; and it consigns its own lovers to the servitude of its father and subjects them to his domination. ⟦s38⟧ And Moses serves and feeds [0176A] the cattle, and buys the love of a foul wife with base attendance; nor can he be free until God comes in fire and appears in love. ⟦s39⟧ For then he easily despises what he had loved wickedly, when he has begun to taste what he may desire more sweetly. ⟦s40⟧ And now Moses does not deign to serve for the Ethiopian woman; and although he does not cast her off afterward when she follows, yet he does not receive her into love. ⟦s41⟧ For, made a lover of wisdom, he ought so to have admitted the care of the flesh as not to subject himself by affection to it when it wantoned shamelessly, but yet to condescend by compassion to it when it was weak and compliant. ⟦s42⟧ For it is one thing to love for joy, another to endure for use. ⟦s43⟧ It is one thing to receive into the fellowship of love, and another to endure in the part of compassion. ⟦s44⟧ The life of the flesh is to be borne in necessity; wisdom [0176B] is to be joined to us in love. ⟦s45⟧ That one is to be sought after for her own sake; this one is to be fled for its own sake to be endured for our sake. ⟦s46⟧ Therefore Moses after the vision does not serve in Midian the father of the Ethiopian woman, nor does she any more bear sons, as one driven back from the bed, where the place of love and of affection is; and another fairer and more lovable having been received, from whose fellowship the face of Moses would be made not dark but luminous, whose love was shown in that fire, when the bush was burning and was not burned up. ⟦s47⟧ But Moses ran that he might pass over and contemplate the vision, because he had not yet conceived the fire of wisdom nor felt its sweetness. ⟦s48⟧ Therefore he wondered, and by that very wonder was drawn, so that drawing near he might grow warm and burn. ⟦s49⟧ And the fire itself, which enlightened him while absent, kindled him as he drew near. ⟦s50⟧ Therefore, drawing near, [0176C] he loosed his shoe, gladly renouncing now the carnal desires, the sweetness of that love having been tasted and its pleasantness recognized. ⟦s51⟧ For what could the concupiscence of the flesh have availed, compared with the love of wisdom? ⟦s52⟧ Therefore Moses hastened to run and to see the vision, and to pass over to contemplate wisdom. ⟦s53⟧ And it has been made manifest how he passed over, and how he arrived at contemplating wisdom. ⟦s54⟧ How much he saw in her, and how much he came to know of him, and how he was made a friend of wisdom, penetrating even into her depths and privy to her secrets. ⟦s55⟧ And how many are there from the beginning who wished thus to pass over to contemplate wisdom? ⟦s56⟧ First Abraham, that great father, went out from the fire of the Chaldeans, and passed over to contemplate [0176D] wisdom, and came into the land of vision which God showed him, and saw the brightness of wisdom and rejoiced. ⟦s57⟧ And Isaac had gone out to meditate, and passed over to contemplate wisdom; and God spoke with him. ⟦s58⟧ Jacob too, going on to contemplate wisdom, passed over as he came from Mesopotamia, and saw the Lord face to face, and his soul was made safe [n: (Gen. XXXII)]. ⟦s59⟧ After these, all the patriarchs and all the prophets passed over, and all wished to contemplate wisdom. ⟦s60⟧ And last of all wisdom herself came, and passed over, and her friends contemplated her; since she had come for this, that she might be seen, lest she should be sought in vain if she were never seen; and she said to those who had passed over, that they might contemplate [0177A] her. ⟦s61⟧ *Blessed are the eyes that see what you see. ⟦s62⟧ Many kings and many prophets have wished to see what you see, and have not seen; and to hear what you hear, and have not heard [n: (Matth. XIII)]*. ⟦s63⟧ This she said, and passed over, that after her those might pass over who desired to contemplate her, lest perchance she should now not be sought, if she were always seen. ⟦s64⟧ And many others followed after her, and very many passed through, and wisdom was made manifold. ⟦s65⟧ And many wished to pass over and could not arrive, because they did not hold the way. ⟦s66⟧ Others passed over and arrived. ⟦s67⟧ Others wished to pass over and could not pass through, because they thought to go by themselves. ⟦s68⟧ For the philosophers of the nations also, they too wished to pass over to contemplate wisdom, but they went astray and became vain, because they thought to lead [0177B] themselves. ⟦s69⟧ And yet they had begun to pass over, and had arrived as far as something, and came up to the way; but they did not enter the way, because they did not believe in Christ. ⟦s70⟧ Do you wish to know how far they had come? ⟦s71⟧ They were on earth in body, and they ascended in mind and passed over all earthly things, searching out the heavenly. ⟦s72⟧ Even to the moon and even to the sun, even to the stars of heaven, finally even to heaven itself and even to the firmament itself they ascended by their own wit, and arrived by their understanding. ⟦s73⟧ And what was there beyond, whither they could have gone? ⟦s74⟧ But wisdom is above all things, nor can she be found within all things, she through whom all things were made. ⟦s75⟧ He is none of these things, from whom is everything that is. ⟦s76⟧ But where could they have found that? ⟦s77⟧ It was not seen, and yet it was sought; nor could it lie hid so as not to speak, [0177C] nor be searched out so as to be found. ⟦s78⟧ Wisdom was within, and they were without; and they entered in mind after her, that they might pass over to contemplate wisdom. ⟦s79⟧ And they investigated and searched through wisely, and comprehended the secrets of nature, and found many hidden things; and none of these was wisdom, because all this was made through wisdom. ⟦s80⟧ Above all things, whither they ascended; within all things, whither they entered; and there was nothing beyond where they could have sought wisdom. ⟦s81⟧ And they fell and failed, searching out searchings; and it seemed to them that there was something divine above all things, and yet they did not know it nor understand it; but seeing, they grew the more dim, because with weak eyes they struck against a splendid light. ⟦s82⟧ And they began to reckon many false things concerning the true, and to liken God [0177D] by their own perverse heart, not to what he was, but to what seemed to them, who could not see what he was. ⟦s83⟧ But they reckoned that they saw well, and became vain in their own thoughts; and they were the worse blinded, and were found to wander the more deeply the more subtly they searched. ⟦s84⟧ But others thought that wisdom was to be sought more subtly by morals; and very many became despisers of their own goods, but not correctors of their own vices. ⟦s85⟧ And these too arrived as far as something, because they had the appearance of truth, but did not hold the truth. ⟦s86⟧ They cast away money, but retained malice. ⟦s87⟧ What was without they cleansed, and what was within they kept polluted. ⟦s88⟧ But this was not wisdom, because it was not truth, [0178A] but a false image alone. ⟦s89⟧ But they thought that they were walking along the road of the virtues; yet whither they were tending they did not know, because they did not know their own direction and end. ⟦s90⟧ And there were yet others who had certain virtues and were borne into them by a natural leading; and they passed over and arrived as far as something; and from a certain part of nature, by which she did not lie wholly subject to corruption, they saw the good and desired it. ⟦s91⟧ And it was given them to be capable of much in the pursuit of the virtues, and with an upright disposition inclined to goodness. ⟦s92⟧ Who does not know this — how many men of virtues they too had, and how great things, wonderful and worthy of imitation, they did: pursuing justice, cultivating piety, keeping chastity, confirmed in patience, steadfast in fidelity, circumspect in prudence — and what shall we say? ⟦s93⟧ Were not all these things [0178B] good? ⟦s94⟧ They were indeed, but of nature, not of grace; of creation, not of restoration: goods which nature had kept, lest she should be wholly corrupted; not goods which grace had given, that nature might be freed from corruption. ⟦s95⟧ Goods which grace had indeed given to nature when she was first created, and afterward had left to her when she was corrupted; but had not added over and above to her when she was to be purged from corruption. ⟦s96⟧ Goods by which the beauty of a nature well created might be proved; not by which the loftiness of a nature to be glorified might be acquired. ⟦s97⟧ All these, therefore, were goods for their own part, but they were not true goods, because they did not direct the mind by intention to the highest good. ⟦s98⟧ And by all these wisdom was not found, because she was not sought in truth; and she drew near neither to those, that she might enlighten their eyes to the truth, nor [0178C] to these, that she might direct their steps to virtue. ⟦s99⟧ And therefore neither could the blind find her, nor the lame arrive at her. ⟦s100⟧ But let this our Hebrew say what he has done, and how much he has profited by his passing over; since he too testifies that he passes over with the rest to contemplate wisdom, saying: I passed over to contemplate wisdom. ⟦s101⟧ This, therefore, is what you ought to consider. ⟦s102⟧ For he who passes over to wisdom leaves folly behind. ⟦s103⟧ And what is it that follows?
