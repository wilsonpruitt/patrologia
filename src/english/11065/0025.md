## HOMILY X. *On the remaining vanities, as far as that place: « The fool walketh in darkness. »* (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Errors also, and folly.* ⟦s2⟧ If he passes over to wisdom who passes over to contemplate wisdom, how does he withdraw from folly, or leave error behind, who passes over to contemplate errors and folly? ⟦s3⟧ But wisdom is light; error, however, and folly are darkness. ⟦s4⟧ And he who is in darkness sees neither the darkness [0178D] nor the light. ⟦s5⟧ But he who is in the light sees both the darkness and the light, because all things that are reproved are reproved by the light. ⟦s6⟧ Since therefore he cannot see his own darkness, because he himself is still in darkness, but comes from the darkness to the light, that he may see through the light both the darkness and the light — not the darkness through darkness and the light through light, but darkness and light through the light alone. ⟦s7⟧ No one goes to darkness that he may see darkness, but comes to the light that he may see through the light not only light, but both darkness and light. ⟦s8⟧ He, therefore, who wishes to see his own darkness withdraws from it, lest he himself be in darkness and see nothing; and he passes over to the light, that through the light he may see darkness and light alike. ⟦s9⟧ Rightly therefore Ecclesiastes, when he passes [0179A] over to contemplate wisdom, comes to her — about to contemplate errors and folly also — as to a light, that in her he may see both the darkness and the light. ⟦s10⟧ That is, that in her he may see also that she is wisdom; and that through her he may see errors and folly, which is not wisdom. ⟦s11⟧ For he who sees wisdom sees that she is wisdom; and he who sees errors and folly sees that this is not wisdom; and yet he can see wisdom, and can see errors and folly, only through her — and she is wisdom. ⟦s12⟧ He, therefore, who passes over to contemplate errors and to contemplate folly passes over to wisdom, just as he who passes over to contemplate wisdom passes over to wisdom. ⟦s13⟧ Yet the one — that is, errors and folly — he contemplates in order to flee it; but to the other, that [0179B] is, to wisdom, he draws near in order to contemplate her; because neither of the two is wisdom, but to contemplate both is wisdom; and he who passes over to contemplate both passes over to nothing other than wisdom, nor does he leave behind anything other than errors and folly. ⟦s14⟧ Let him say, therefore: He passed over to contemplate wisdom, and errors and folly. ⟦s15⟧ What is error: not to know what is right. ⟦s16⟧ What is folly? ⟦s16⟧ To know and not to follow. ⟦s17⟧ What is wisdom? ⟦s17⟧ To know [sic: *cognocere*] and to love the good. ⟦s18⟧ But to know evil and to hate it — this too is wisdom, and the first wisdom; because to have hated evil is to have loved the good, and to have known evil is to have understood the right. ⟦s19⟧ Wherefore to know and to love the good is wisdom, and this same is to hate and to have known evil. ⟦s20⟧ And all this is known in wisdom, [0179C] when wisdom is seen, and what she is, that it may be loved; and what she is not, but is made manifest through her, that it may be hated. ⟦s21⟧ O how good this passing over is, and how happily he removes who removes to contemplate wisdom! ⟦s22⟧ What wonder is it if the God of the Hebrews is the Lord God, who makes such passers-over, and makes them pass over to such things? ⟦s23⟧ *Pass over to me,* says Wisdom, *all you who desire me, and be filled with my generations. ⟦s24⟧ For my spirit is sweet above honey, and my inheritance above honey and the honeycomb. ⟦s25⟧ They that eat me shall yet hunger, and they that drink me shall yet thirst; they that make me clear shall have eternal life [n: (Eccli. XXIV)]*. ⟦s26⟧ Since I give light of doctrine to all as it were before the dawn; and I will enlighten all [0179D] who hope in the Lord. ⟦s27⟧ For do you wish to know how wisdom enlightens those who have passed over to contemplate her? ⟦s28⟧ Question this very man, who passed over to contemplate wisdom; and consider how he has been enlightened from the time that he began to see her. ⟦s29⟧ For how blind he was before he had passed over to contemplate her, you have heard sufficiently above, when he thought to go where there was no way for man: to seek out and to investigate all things that were under the sun; and believed it to be something great to investigate the nature of those things which stand outside the nature of man — things which, even if they be known, so far as lies in them, cannot confer salvation, nor, if they be unknown, take it away. ⟦s30⟧ There, therefore, he was blind, in not seeing nor knowing in himself what he could, nor desiring for himself what he ought. ⟦s31⟧ But [0180A] now, enlightened by wisdom, attend to what he says.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *What is man, I say? ⟦s2⟧ What is he, I say?* ⟦s3⟧ I do not ask what he is as though admiring his dignity, but as knowing his infirmity. ⟦s4⟧ What is man? ⟦s4⟧ Man is nothing. ⟦s5⟧ Man is not anything such that he may be found sufficient for this. ⟦s6⟧ For what?

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *That he may be able to follow the king, his maker.* ⟦s2⟧ For if he cannot attain the thing made, much more can he not follow the maker. ⟦s3⟧ If he cannot investigate what has been made, how can he comprehend him who made it? ⟦s4⟧ What is man, that he may be able to follow the king, his maker? ⟦s5⟧ His king and his maker. ⟦s6⟧ The very same who is king is maker. ⟦s7⟧ He is maker because he created; king, because he governs in that which he created, and directs it [0180B] to that for which he created it. ⟦s8⟧ For he created in a great good, and directs to the highest good. ⟦s9⟧ Who is he that follows his maker? ⟦s10⟧ He who lives as he was made. ⟦s11⟧ Who walks according to that for which he was instituted? ⟦s12⟧ He who keeps the good which he received when created, and guards unpolluted what nature conferred. ⟦s13⟧ This man follows his maker. ⟦s14⟧ And who is he that follows his king also? ⟦s15⟧ He who tends toward that to which he is directed; who hastens to arrive whither he is invited; who obeys the laws of one who presides well; who at the command of him who orders bends himself to all things with a spontaneous will. ⟦s16⟧ This man follows his king. ⟦s17⟧ But who is the man who can answer God? ⟦s18⟧ Who will be found sufficient to render a return to God? ⟦s19⟧ If he himself goes before, and so that you may follow: then one thing is his, another yours. ⟦s20⟧ Nay, if he himself begins the good and you complete it, what is less [0180C] is his, and what is greater is yours. ⟦s21⟧ If God goes before by beginning the good, and you follow after by perfecting it, you are now found not only equal to God, but greater. ⟦s22⟧ But perhaps he goes before by showing, so that you may follow by imitating. ⟦s23⟧ And where then is what is said: *His mercy shall go before me and shall follow after? [n: (Psal. LVIII.)]* Where is: *Of him, and through him, and in him are all things? [n: (Rom. XI.)]* Where is: *What hast thou that thou hast not received? ⟦s24⟧ And if thou hast received, why dost thou glory, as if thou hadst not received? [n: (II Cor. IV.)]* For if to show is his and to follow is yours, then you have something which you have not received; nor are all things of him and through him and in him, but certain things are man's which are not of him, if a man can of himself do some things which are not [0180D] through him. ⟦s25⟧ But wisdom does not enlighten for this, that a man should think thus; or that he should say that a man can have anything as it were from himself, who could not have even this from himself, that he is a man. ⟦s26⟧ What, then, does he say? ⟦s26⟧ What is man, that he may be able to follow the king, his maker? ⟦s27⟧ For how far do you think man is from God, that he should reach where he is? ⟦s28⟧ For what is it to reach where he is, unless it be to be what he is? ⟦s29⟧ And what is God? ⟦s30⟧ Or what is man? ⟦s31⟧ See how far apart they are. ⟦s32⟧ God is just and blessed; man unjust and wretched. ⟦s33⟧ See how greatly the just and the unjust, the wretched and the blessed, stand apart. ⟦s34⟧ But misery cannot be together with blessedness, nor can injustice arrive at blessedness. ⟦s35⟧ Justice is the way, blessedness the fatherland. ⟦s36⟧ But the servant of sin cannot be just, unless he has been freed from sin. ⟦s37⟧ See [0181A] therefore that man cannot follow the king, his maker, unless he be first visited by the king, his maker. ⟦s38⟧ Man cannot go to him; but he can, if he will, come to man. ⟦s39⟧ Therefore the king and maker of man came to man. ⟦s40⟧ He came to that which man was, and he himself was made what he was not. ⟦s41⟧ He was made man for man, taking on our misery, retaining his own justice. ⟦s42⟧ And there appeared between God and men, as the mediator of God and men, the man God: as God drawing near to men through misery, and as man not withdrawing from God through justice. ⟦s43⟧ Thus God descended to one, and man ascended to one; and in the one, two were found, the one from what is ours, the other from what is his: for there were, as it were, three things. ⟦s44⟧ God was just and blessed, and man was wretched and unjust; and because these could not come together, [0181B] the man God set himself in the midst, joining the wretched and the just, that through justice he might conquer misery, and might restore justice to blessedness. ⟦s45⟧ Therefore, when Emmanuel was with us through the justice which he had brought to us, and through the misery which he had taken up from us, he began to return whence he had come. ⟦s46⟧ That in man he might conquer misery through justice, and might call man back, with justice, through misery to blessedness. ⟦s47⟧ He held justice, therefore, and endured misery, until he should conquer misery and crown justice. ⟦s48⟧ And he ran, and went before us. ⟦s49⟧ *Suffering for us, and leaving us an example, that we may follow his steps [n: (I Petr. II)]*. ⟦s50⟧ Freeing us from the servitude of sin, that we too may have justice in misery, [0181C] and through misery, with justice, may pass over to blessedness. ⟦s51⟧ Promising us what he received, if we suffer what he endured; that we may reign with him, if we suffer for him. ⟦s52⟧ Thus, therefore, the incarnate Word, our maker and king, because we could not follow him in his own majesty, went before us in our humility, and out of what is ours established a way, that we might come to what is his. ⟦s53⟧ But not even so could man follow him, unless he were helped by him; because not even a just man would run through death to life, unless he who gave justice to the sinner should also give constancy to him who suffers. ⟦s54⟧ Rightly therefore he who had contemplated wisdom, and had recognized how utterly nothing man is by himself, said: What is man, that he should follow the king, his maker? ⟦s55⟧ For truly this man had passed over to contemplate wisdom, and [0181D] truly had been enlightened by wisdom: much more now, in being wise, truly recognizing the infirmity of man, than before, vainly stretching himself beyond the possibility of man. ⟦s56⟧ For there he was lifted up so as to fail; here he was humbled so as to advance. ⟦s57⟧ There he swelled outside himself through elation, so as to be emptied within himself; here through humility he gathered himself to himself, that he might be given to the One alone. ⟦s58⟧ And wisdom did all this after he had contemplated her, showing him in her own light first his darkness, and afterward her own light also — and the whole in her light and through her light. ⟦s59⟧ And the darkness was not light, nor was there darkness in the light; but through the light the darkness was seen, and was divided from the light. ⟦s59⟧ And he says:
