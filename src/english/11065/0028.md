## HOMILY XI. *What the ends of the earth are in which the eyes of fools are: and how wisdom reaches from end to end mightily,* etc. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ What do we say? ⟦s2⟧ Was the wisdom of God, then, diminished in the making? and did it pursue its work feebly to the end? ⟦s3⟧ And where is that which reaches from end to end mightily? ⟦s4⟧ If Wisdom mightily reaches from end to end, then the difference of beauty in the creation is not from a failing in the maker. ⟦s5⟧ Why then are not all things equally beautiful, unless it be that all things together may be more beautiful? ⟦s6⟧ For if individual things were not beautiful in differing degrees, all things together would not be incomparably beautiful; because the highest beauty would be in individual things — whatever one there were would be surpassing that one, and more eminent than all the rest — [0186B] not every beauty would be in all things together. ⟦s7⟧ Therefore Wisdom mightily reaches from end to end; because she so pursues her works down to the very bottom of things that she even orders the defect of lower things toward the beauty of the whole, so that the universe is the more beautiful for this, that in the universe certain things suffer defect by comparison with others and in respect of them. ⟦s8⟧ For this very thing, which in certain of her beautiful works seems not beautiful, is nevertheless not ugly where it is; but it has less of beauty in the part, because in the whole it is beautiful for that to which it is ordered. ⟦s9⟧ Therefore in no part is there ugliness, but nevertheless in certain parts there is greater and in certain parts lesser beauty; because the beauty in the whole of the parts is greatest [0186C] for this reason, that in the parts it is differing, and manifoldly varied and distinguished in every way. ⟦s10⟧ Then follows, and he says: Ordering all things sweetly. ⟦s11⟧ For she orders all things sweetly, because in kinds of things so many and so diverse, advancing each single one [sic: *promonens*] to its own term, she permits nothing to disturb the concord and peace of the universe; but she so assigns to each what is its own that she leads things running unlike to one end, and so that the working of one does not hinder the force and effect of another. ⟦s12⟧ O Wisdom, how widely thou art poured out, and yet thou art not scattered! ⟦s13⟧ How far into the distance thou stretchest thyself, and sufferest no failing! ⟦s14⟧ How great is thy course, from the highest even to the lowest! ⟦s15⟧ By as much as heaven is distant from earth, and hell [0186D] beneath the earth where the end of the earth is, and beyond there is no earth; by as much as the highest is distant from the lowest, so much does Wisdom penetrate through. ⟦s16⟧ And yet this way of Wisdom is short, and her stretching-forth is compendious, and she counts all that is a small thing for her to pass over and to lay hold of and to penetrate; nor is anything far off that is stretched out in space, if it be near in goodness. ⟦s17⟧ But iniquity is far more distant from justice, and farther than the two, heaven from earth, and hell beneath the earth. ⟦s18⟧ For justice is higher than heaven, and not only than heaven, but even than those who are in heaven. ⟦s19⟧ The angels are in heaven, and from the beginning they are in heaven; and from thence they ascended, through justice, above that which they had been made. ⟦s20⟧ And justice is higher than heaven, iniquity deeper than hell. ⟦s21⟧ For it is deeper than everything that is lowest in creation, because that which has fallen through iniquity [0187A] is beneath every creature; nor can anything be deeper than that than which nothing is worse. ⟦s22⟧ By as much, therefore, as he ascends who ascends into heaven, by so much does he ascend who advances toward justice. ⟦s23⟧ And by as much as he descends who descends to hell, by so much does he descend who falls to iniquity. ⟦s24⟧ And by as much as he ascends who ascends from hell to heaven, by so much does he ascend who returns from iniquity to justice. ⟦s25⟧ But by as much as he descends who descends from heaven to hell, by so much does he descend who rushes down from justice to iniquity.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And in all these things *they ascend even to the heavens, and they descend even to the depths, and their soul amid evils* wastes away [n: (Psal. CVI)]. ⟦s2⟧ Therefore heaven belongs to justice, and hell to iniquity; and the motions of souls are snatched along [0187B] by an alternating surge without ceasing, as though driven by contrary blasts, ascending and descending; and there come to be liftings up, and pressings down, and great commotions in that invisible sea of billowing desires. ⟦s3⟧ The good spirit comes, and drives upward. ⟦s4⟧ The evil spirit comes, and drives downward: and they ascend even to the heavens, and they descend even from the depths, their soul wasting away in the uncertainty of its own evils. ⟦s5⟧ And what does he say? ⟦s6⟧ If I shall ascend into heaven, thou art there; if I shall descend to hell, thou art at hand. ⟦s7⟧ For if you ascend into the heaven of justice, you have there with you wisdom holding you fast lest you fall; but if you descend to the hell of guilt, wisdom is there laying hold of you lest you escape. ⟦s8⟧ Above she has her right hand, and below [0187C] her left, reaching from end to end mightily, and ordering all things sweetly. ⟦s9⟧ Above she is strong, below she is strong, everywhere unconquerable. ⟦s10⟧ Above she is strong, so as to keep from evil those who trust in her, and to cast headlong into evil, by forsaking them, those who presume upon themselves. ⟦s11⟧ Below she is strong, so as to condemn for evil those who despise, and to justify from evil those who repent. ⟦s12⟧ Behold how many she has kept safe at the summit, how many she has cast down from the summit, how many she has raised from the depth, how many she has forsaken in the depth. ⟦s13⟧ Manifest are her judgments: and he who knows himself knows how Wisdom works all these things daily in the secret of the human heart, always presiding at judgment: and by hidden requital and invisible dispensation, weighing merits, now she takes up through grace, now she forsakes through justice, now through gentleness [0187D] she spares, now through severity she punishes. ⟦s14⟧ And when she has forsaken she takes up again, and when she has taken up she again abandons, that a man may not know his own end, and may walk anxiously all the time of his life, so that those who lie prostrate may not despair, and those who stand may not presume. ⟦s15⟧ If they are in the heaven of justice, let them fear a fall. ⟦s16⟧ If in the hell of guilt, let them seek mercy. ⟦s17⟧ Therefore justice herself first descended to hell; afterward she ascended to heaven, that in descending she might give hope to those to be set free, and in ascending might show the way to those to be glorified. ⟦s18⟧ Wisdom, then, reaches from end to end mightily, and orders all things sweetly. ⟦s19⟧ For she comprehends the end of both — of the good, namely, which is in her; and equally of the evil, which is not in her. ⟦s20⟧ But in that which is from her it flows away from her, yet not altogether without [0188A] her, because what nature does not beget out of her own, she restrains by power within her own. ⟦s21⟧ For evil has no proper seat of its own, but by nature a stranger to what is its own, it lodges in what belongs to another, so that it is plain that it is not in its own; because if there were not good things in which evils were, evils would not be at all. ⟦s22⟧ For since evil is nothing other than the corruption of a good, and everything that is corrupted is good, that from which it falls away is one thing, that in which it subsists another. ⟦s23⟧ And that in which it subsists is good; but that which falls away, from which it falls away from the good, is evil: a falling-away from good can be nowhere else at all except in that which subsists in some good. ⟦s24⟧ Therefore if there were no good, there would be no evil at all; because there would be nowhere that malice could be, if Wisdom, which did not make it, did not suffer it within her own. ⟦s25⟧ For Wisdom did not make [0188B] malice; but in that which she made she permitted malice to be, so that Wisdom, set against malice, might conquer, and being compared might appear the more beautiful. ⟦s26⟧ For Wisdom sets herself against malice and measures herself with it in her works, and conquers it on both sides, so that she may reach from end to end; because malice, running toward defect, does not escape Wisdom, but Wisdom, rising toward advancement, transcends malice. ⟦s27⟧ For malice running toward defect does not escape Wisdom for this reason, that malice cannot find what it may corrupt except in those things which Wisdom created. ⟦s28⟧ But Wisdom, rising toward advancement, transcends malice for this reason: that malice cannot corrupt the whole of what Wisdom was able to create. ⟦s29⟧ So that malice may be compared, then, nothing can be brought through malice [0188C] to non-being which has not received being from Wisdom; but that malice may be overcome, malice does not corrupt the whole of what has been made by wisdom.
