## HOMILY XI. *What the ends of the earth are in which the eyes of fools are: and how wisdom reaches from end to end mightily,* etc. (cont.)

Wisdom runs, therefore, that she may reach from end to end, transcending malice in the incorrupt goods, whither malice does not come; and following after malice in those things which have been corrupted, whither malice does not go before her. For malice cannot go before Wisdom, so as to be found either prior, before her rising, or posterior, after her failing; because that also is found prior to corruption which at some time admitted corruption, and posterior to corruption that which could not be wholly corrupted, and above corruption that to which corruption did not come. And for this reason Wisdom conquers malice, and goes before folly; because she reaches from end even to [0188D] end, that is, from the rising of every good, that she may transcend malice, beyond the failing of a certain good, that she may shut malice in. For in that in which every good at some time stood without corruption, Wisdom is acknowledged as prior. But in that good also which is corrupted, in which after the corruption something of good remains over which cannot be corrupted at all, Wisdom is found posterior. Wisdom therefore conquers malice; because she reaches from the end in which every good received its beginning, even to the end in which even a corrupted good, after the failing of the good, subsists in some good. And she reaches from end to end mightily, so as to hold malice itself within measure by her power, and to restrain it to order by her reason, lest either corruption, destroying the whole, stretch itself unto the end, or confusion, deforming the whole, [0189A] pour itself out to the uttermost parts. For this reason, therefore, she reaches mightily everywhere, subjecting malice to her dominion: and whatever of corruption malice brings in toward confusion, this Wisdom, by a wondrous law and a secret disposition, turns to the beauty of her own works, bringing it about out of what is not beautiful, that what is beautiful becomes most beautiful, and that what stands as good in itself rises up, out of that which is not good, into the consummation of goodness. She reaches, then, from end to end mightily, and orders all things sweetly. Nothing is excepted from her sweetness. She orders all things sweetly. What things all? Crooked and straight, good and evil, the subject and the adverse — she orders all things sweetly: not only good things, but evil ones too; not only straight things, but crooked ones too; not only subject things, but adverse ones too. She orders all things [0189B] sweetly. How sweetly? Do you wish to know how sweetly she orders all things? Without violence she subjects adverse things; without tumult she sets crooked things straight, and in her most peaceable kingdom, which justice holds within, she makes even evils and the motions of the vices that oppose her to serve, without compulsion, by a secret order and a most wise disposition. For she orders all things, even things not sweet, so sweetly that all the things which raise themselves against her will she neither powerfully extinguishes, so that they are nothing, nor violently compels, so that they can do nothing; but, allowing them to be and granting them to be able, she manifestly permits things running against her will, and secretly leads them to the fulfilling of her will. And when the wicked will those things against her, they will them without her; but when they can do what they will, they can do it from her. And when they do [0189C] what they can, they think indeed and wish to do what she does not will; and yet they do not do otherwise, nor are they able to do otherwise, than what she wills. For they are manifestly permitted and secretly led, that they may be sweetly ordered; and Wisdom reaches even to them mightily, so as to restrain them to her own disposition; and she orders sweetly, so as not to compel them against their own will. But even if at times she outwardly applies violence to crooked wills to be applied to, or to be chastised; nevertheless she never forsakes the sweetness of her disposition; because, remaining tranquil in herself, from that very source whence she disturbs the ill-quiet peace of those who oppose her, she thence composes the kingdom of her justice the more solidly unto peace. For this reason, therefore, she reaches from end even to [0189D] end mightily, and orders all things sweetly.

There remains now that greatest and highest course of Wisdom, from end to end: which ends have no end. Above, the sublimity of contemplation, and below, the depth of damnation. For these ends have no other ends beyond them by which they themselves might be bounded; but all things reach as far as these, which were made for these, and beyond them nothing. To these two ends wisdom, beginning from the middle, ordering on this side and on that, whether by advancing [sic: *promovende*] or by forsaking, leads the course of all things; strewing justice as a way for those ascending above; leaving iniquity as a way for those descending below, so that through justice one may go to blessedness [sic: *ab beatitudinem*], but through iniquity and injustice one may come to damnation. And when it has come thither, she will set all things in their own ends; [0190A] and the ends themselves will never have an end. And then too Wisdom herself will reach from end to end mightily, so that never more will she either fail in the requital of the just, or desist from the punishment of the iniquitous: ordering all things sweetly, on this side to joy, on that to eternal torment. But, I ask, what sweetness will there be in the regions below? what sweetness will there be able to be in eternal torments, that it should be said, ordering all things sweetly? Is it sweetly for this reason, that she who orders remains sweet herself, although those whom she orders are not sweet, nor is that sweet in which she orders? Yet how does she order sweetly? Is it sweetly for this reason that she orders, because the ordering itself is sweet, even if those things are not sweet which she orders: and, just as she orders evils not evilly but well, so she orders unsweet and bitter things sweetly? [0190B] For therefore evils exist, and good things have been permitted by the Omnipotent to be evils, not that evils may become gifts; but that out of evils good may come to be, and that the good things themselves may shine out more beautifully by comparison with evils; not that good things may be increased by evils joined to them, but that they may be commended by things compared with them. But who is wise, that he may understand these things — what evils do in the kingdom of the good: and yet, if they did nothing in the kingdom of wisdom, they would not be evils at all? Therefore some do evils on account of which they have been permitted to be: and both they themselves and evils do good, and evils themselves do not make good, but he makes good out of the evils themselves: he who knows how to use both good things and evil for good. And this is Wisdom, which reaches from end to end mightily, and orders all things sweetly.
