## HOMILY XII. *On that passage, according to another translation: The eyes of fools are in the ends of the earth; where our copy has: The fool walketh in darkness, and on the remainder of the second chapter.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0190C]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ We have perhaps lingered longer over the discussion of these matters than the brevity of a treatise would require; but not fully so long as the difficulty of the subject would demand. ⟦s2⟧ Now therefore, since we have found an end, and an end, concerning which Scripture spoke when it said that Wisdom reaches from end to end mightily, and orders all things sweetly; and since it was our purpose to seek these ends on account of the ends of the earth, where the eyes of fools are, who walk in darkness, lest perchance Wisdom, who reaches from end to end, should reach to the ends [0190D] of the earth, and the light shine in the darkness in the ends of the earth, if Wisdom reaches to the ends of the earth, she who reaches from end to end mightily, and orders all things sweetly. ⟦s3⟧ Since therefore we have found these ends. ⟦s4⟧ If nevertheless the things we have said about these ends are able worthily to fit their own truth to the interpretation of Scripture, and there be no other more excellent interpretation to which this one should by right give place, it now remains to inquire what these ends of the earth are that have been mentioned, and where they are; and also what that earth itself is, and where it is, in whose ends are the eyes of fools, who do not look back to their own head that they may see, and not walk in darkness. ⟦s5⟧ First, then, let us ask what this earth is, and where this earth is. ⟦s6⟧ Now we know that in this world there is earth and there is heaven, [0191A] and between earth and heaven is whatever is in this world. ⟦s7⟧ For in the body of this world heaven is highest and earth lowest; and just as above heaven there is nothing of this kind which is not in heaven itself, so beneath the earth there is nothing, save perhaps what is in the earth. ⟦s8⟧ And if we say of this earth that the eyes of fools are in its ends, how this will not be able to stand. ⟦s9⟧ For we proclaim eyes keen-sighted rather than blind, which we assert are stretched out by their own vision even to the ends of the earth. ⟦s10⟧ Which of the just men has been able to do this, to survey the compass of the earth with his eyes, and to run through in a single seeing such far-off spaces as could scarcely be traversed by the labor of many years? ⟦s11⟧ It is ridiculous even to think this, that Scripture said it of bodily vision. ⟦s12⟧ Of this visible earth: The eyes of fools [0191B] are in the ends of the earth, unless perhaps someone believe that in these words this is signified: that all fools and lovers of the carnal life, who with the whole intention of the mind always go about after earthly goods [sic: *a terrena bona*], run about in pursuit and search of them even to foreign nations, and often even to the uttermost bounds of the lands: and that they are therefore said to have their eyes there, because by thought and desire they always strain thither. ⟦s13⟧ But the wise man, who has raised his mind to the things that are above and directs his intention thither, he himself may have his eyes in his own head, in that he strains toward the attaining of that which is highest of all and chief. ⟦s14⟧ This could, so far as pertains to the truth of the interpretation, be brought in suitably enough, if Wisdom did not call us to a higher understanding, and to another earth, and to other ends, and to other eyes [0191C] to be thought of. ⟦s15⟧ For if the head of the wise man is rightly understood spiritually, it is necessary that we interpret the earth also and its ends mystically and not bodily, on account of the eyes of fools. ⟦s16⟧ As, therefore, this visible world has its own earth and its own heaven, so man has his own earth and his own heaven. ⟦s17⟧ For man himself also is a world, and man has been called the lesser world. ⟦s18⟧ His earth is his flesh, and his heaven is his soul. ⟦s19⟧ But in this greater world the earth is within the heaven; in the lesser world, however, the heaven is within the earth. ⟦s20⟧ And yet the heaven of the lesser world is greater than the heaven of the greater world. ⟦s21⟧ But what is its earth compared to the earth of that other? ⟦s22⟧ How small it is? and yet its heaven is within its earth. ⟦s23⟧ What is heaven within [0191D] earth? ⟦s24⟧ The soul within the body. ⟦s25⟧ The soul is heaven, the body is earth. ⟦s26⟧ The soul within the body; heaven within earth. ⟦s27⟧ And yet heaven is always above, earth below. ⟦s28⟧ For as this heaven is loftier than earth by its place, so that heaven is more sublime than its earth by its nature. ⟦s29⟧ Therefore in the greater world, the things that are in the earth are beneath the earth; the things that are outside the earth are above the earth. ⟦s30⟧ In the lesser world, the things that are within are above, the things that are without are beneath. ⟦s31⟧ What are the things within, and what the things without? ⟦s32⟧ Invisible things are within, visible things are without. ⟦s33⟧ Therefore invisible things are above, visible things beneath; and invisible things are above our heaven, invisible things beneath our earth. ⟦s34⟧ All earthly things are beneath our earth, because they were made for its service and ordained for its obedience. ⟦s35⟧ Therefore our earth is above the earth. ⟦s36⟧ And what shall I say? [0192A] Is not our earth also above heaven? ⟦s37⟧ Did he not carry it thither, and place it there, taken from us, he who took it for us? ⟦s38⟧ Therefore our earth is above heaven. ⟦s39⟧ Our earth had first been made by nature above the earth; afterward our earth went, through guilt, into the earth, and beneath the earth; but now, through grace, our earth has been raised above heaven. ⟦s40⟧ Therefore all earthly things are beneath our earth. ⟦s41⟧ And yet this same earth is joined to earthly things and coupled to them by a kind of friendship, as heaven is joined and allied to heavenly things; and earth reaches as far as earthly things, and heaven as far as heavenly things. ⟦s42⟧ Therefore earthly things are the ends of the earth, and heavenly things the ends of heaven; because earth reaches as far as earthly things and no farther, and heaven as far as heavenly things and no farther. ⟦s43⟧ Earth stretches itself through concupiscence as far as earthly things, and [0192B] heaven stretches itself through wisdom as far as heavenly things. ⟦s44⟧ For *the flesh,* as the Apostle says, *lusteth against the spirit; and the spirit against the flesh [n: (Galat. V)]*. ⟦s45⟧ For the spirit lusts upward and the flesh lusts downward: and therefore each of the two stretches itself in a direction contrary to the other. ⟦s46⟧ And when each has come whither it stretches, there it makes an end and stretches itself no farther. ⟦s47⟧ Therefore the ends of the earth are earthly things, whither the earth of the flesh is stretched by carnal desire. ⟦s48⟧ And our earth has indeed another end, that of judgment, but of desire it has no other end. ⟦s49⟧ Thus far it is stretched by desire, but by judgment it is led farther. ⟦s50⟧ Through desire, indeed, concupiscence is stretched toward earthly things; but through judgment, after earthly things it is led into the earth, after the earth to gehenna. ⟦s51⟧ Through concupiscence to pleasure, after [0192C] pleasure to iniquity, after iniquity to death, after death to damnation. ⟦s52⟧ *Every man,* says Scripture, *is tempted, being drawn away and enticed by his own concupiscence. ⟦s53⟧ For concupiscence, when it hath conceived, bringeth forth sin; but sin, when it hath been completed, begetteth death [n: (Jac. I)]*. ⟦s54⟧ *He hath conceived sorrow, and brought forth iniquity. ⟦s55⟧ His sorrow shall be turned upon his own head, and his iniquity shall come down upon his crown [n: (Psal. VII)]*.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And so the delights of carnal pleasures are the ends of the earth, and the death of the flesh follows upon the delights of the flesh. ⟦s2⟧ But the vengeance of damnation follows upon the death of the flesh. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore the ends of the earth have one end, death, by which they are received and consumed; but death [0192D] has damnation for its end, by which it is received and consummated. ⟦s4⟧ For carnal delights are consumed by death, so that they are no more; but the death of the flesh is consummated by damnation, so that it may be for ever. ⟦s5⟧ These ends fools are unwilling to consider, who have their eyes in the ends of the earth. ⟦s6⟧ For they gaze only at the first ends of the earth, round about the earth, where the earth itself is ended; not beyond, at the other end, where these ends are ended. ⟦s7⟧ They strain toward the ends of their own desire, not toward the ends of their own judgment. ⟦s8⟧ To the delight of their flesh, and to the enticements of their pleasures, they open their eyes, and they do not attend to what is to be thereafter beyond. ⟦s9⟧ Therefore they are fools who make an end in the middle of the way, where there is no end: and they do not look forward to the end where the end is. ⟦s10+11⟧ *The heart of fools is where mirth is, and the heart of the wise where sadness is (Eccle. [0193A] VII)* And another Scripture says: *In all thy works all the days [sic: omnis diebus] of thy life, remember thy last end, and for ever thou shalt not sin [n: (Eccli. VII)]*. ⟦s12⟧ For this reason fools do not fear to sin, because they look to the delight of sin alone, but do not look to what follows thereafter, the damnation of the delight. ⟦s13⟧ The eyes of fools are in the ends of the earth; the eyes of the wise man are in his head. ⟦s14⟧ What is the head of the wise man? ⟦s15⟧ Whatever is highest for anyone, this is his head. ⟦s16⟧ For the head is the highest part of a man. ⟦s17⟧ The head of the wise man, then, is that which is highest for the wise man. ⟦s18⟧ And what is highest for the wise man, save that which is highest of all. ⟦s19⟧ For he would not be wise who held for highest what was not the highest. ⟦s20⟧ And so that which is highest of all is the head of the wise man: and perhaps Wisdom herself is this head, whom [0193B] he who is wise sets before all things that seem to have anything to be desired, and in comparison with her he despises all things whatsoever. ⟦s21⟧ And rightly, because Wisdom is the highest of all; she herself is the head of the wise man, since this very thing, that he is wise, is from Wisdom herself, and what the wise man desires to be is wholly in her. ⟦s22⟧ Wherefore the head of the wise man is understood as nothing else more rightly than Wisdom herself; since from her is the origin of goodness, and in her consists the consummation of every good.
