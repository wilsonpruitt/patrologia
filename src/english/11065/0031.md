## HOMILY XII. *On that passage, according to another translation: The eyes of fools are in the ends of the earth; where our copy has: The fool walketh in darkness, and on the remainder of the second chapter.* (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And so that which is highest of all is the head of the wise man, and this head is nothing other than Wisdom herself; since Wisdom is the highest of all. ⟦s2⟧ The highest good, then, is the head, and the highest evil the end. ⟦s3⟧ For there are two, the highest good and the highest evil, [0193C] set on either side over against each other, and from the middle all goods and evils run back to these two ends. ⟦s4⟧ Every good to the highest good, and every evil to the highest evil. ⟦s5⟧ For there is no true good unless it leads to the highest good, nor a true evil unless it runs back to the highest evil. ⟦s6⟧ Therefore in like manner every evil is toward the highest evil, just as every good is toward the highest. ⟦s7⟧ But not in like manner is every evil from the highest evil, as every good is from the highest good. ⟦s8⟧ For *every* *best giving, and every perfect gift, is from above, coming down from the Father of lights [n: (Jac. I)]*. ⟦s9⟧ And what of evils? ⟦s10⟧ *The beginnings,* he says, *of sorrows are these [n: (Matth. XXIV)]*. ⟦s11⟧ Therefore every good is from the highest good, and every good is toward the highest good. ⟦s12⟧ In like manner [0193D] every evil is toward the highest evil, but not every evil from the highest evil. ⟦s13⟧ Therefore the highest good is both the head and the end of all goods. ⟦s14⟧ The highest of all evils is not a head, but only an end. ⟦s15⟧ Therefore every good, apart from the highest good, is good just so far as it leads to the highest good; and every evil, apart from the highest evil, is evil just so far as it leads to the highest evil. ⟦s16⟧ For there have been many who had these middle goods, and they were not true goods to them, because through them they did not come to the highest good. ⟦s17⟧ And there have been many who bore grievous middle evils, and they were not evils to them, because through them they did not descend to the highest evil. ⟦s18⟧ And so the former, lifted up by false goods, fell headlong to the highest evil; the latter, exercised and humbled by false [0194A] evils, advanced to the highest good. ⟦s19⟧ For this reason the wise man lifts his eyes as far as his own head, and nothing that is below suffices him, until he comes to that which is highest. ⟦s20⟧ For certain men have had their eyes upward and yet have not raised their eyes as far as the head, but have remained in the middle of the body. ⟦s21⟧ But what would the members do without the head? ⟦s22⟧ Could the members live, cut off from the head? ⟦s22⟧ So every good, without the highest good, is not a true good. ⟦s23⟧ You have seen a man abstaining from those things which are forbidden by the law, not committing murder, not committing adultery, not plundering, not bearing false witness, not coveting the goods of his neighbor, and you have called him a good man, of good life, of good conversation, worthy of praise, worthy of reward. [0194B] And how many have done these things and have not pleased God; because they did not do for God's sake what they did: and therefore they did not even do well, because they did not do it for the sake of the highest good. ⟦s24⟧ You have seen another climbing yet higher and doing greater goods, not only abstaining from evil, but even exercising himself in good, fasting, praying, bestowing many alms, showing compassion to the wretched, coming to the aid of those set in straits and tribulation, and you have proclaimed him great and worthy of imitation, as one near to God and chosen. ⟦s25⟧ And yet many have done all these things and have profited nothing; since they did not set God in the case. ⟦s26⟧ They did not have their eyes in their own head like the wise man, but bending downward into their own temptation, desiring to please either themselves or others, [0194C] and glorying in their own good without God, they lost their own good, and did not come to the highest good. ⟦s27⟧ For deservedly could they not keep the members alive, who cut off the head from the body. ⟦s28⟧ Therefore the eyes of the wise man are in his head, that he may first go forward to the highest good by intention and desire of the mind, afterward follow after it by zeal and action, and then come to it by reward. ⟦s29⟧ Let him go forward by intention, so that all he does he may do for the attaining of it; let him follow after by zeal, so that he may persevere in it; let him come to it by reward, so that he may receive it as his prize. ⟦s30⟧ In this manner, therefore, the eyes of the wise man are in his head.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For this reason, indeed, when our head was below with us, lest perchance our intention also should remain below, [0194D] the head ascended above to the head, that it might draw us after it: and behold, above there are not two heads, but one head. ⟦s2⟧ For the head below was Christ the man in the world, and the head above was God the Father in heaven. ⟦s3⟧ Do you wish to know that Christ is the head? ⟦s4⟧ *The head of the woman is the man, the head of the man is Christ [n: (I Cor. XI)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And Christ has been appointed by God head of the whole Church. ⟦s6⟧ And the Church herself, the bride of Christ and the body of Christ, what does she say of her bridegroom Christ? and of her head Christ? ⟦s7⟧ For she it is, the bride, who in Canticles, commending her beloved, after many such things and many others, adds this to his praise, saying: *His head is the finest gold [n: (Cant. V)]*. ⟦s8⟧ Therefore Christ is the head, and Christ has a head. ⟦s9⟧ And what is the head of Christ? ⟦s10⟧ Hear the Apostle: The head of the woman is the man; the head [0195A] of the man is Christ: *The head of Christ is God [n: (I Cor. XI)]*. ⟦s11⟧ And so the head of Christ is God: and Christ himself, about to ascend into heaven, said: *I ascend to my Father and your Father: my God and your God [n: (Joan. XX)]*. ⟦s12⟧ If therefore he ascends to God, he ascends to his own head; because the head of Christ is God. ⟦s13⟧ And this head is that finest gold which no rust consumes, no age wears away; because immortal divinity does not admit corruption. ⟦s14⟧ Behold, therefore, our head above is Christ, and our head above is God, and our one head is Christ and God; because Christ also is God, and the one God is Christ: and the head of Christ is God, and the one head is Christ, and the head of Christ is God; as Christ is the one beginning, and the head of Christ is God: *I,* he says, [0195B] *am the beginning, who also speak to you [n: (Joan. VIII)]*. ⟦s15⟧ For this beginning is itself our head: to which the wise man has raised his eyes, now straining toward him through faith, that afterward he may be able to have his eyes upon him, beholding him through sight. ⟦s16⟧ That we may lift our eyes to this beginning and to this head, Scripture admonishes us, saying: *If you have risen again with Christ, savor the things that are above, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God; seek the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth. ⟦s17⟧ For you are dead, and your life is hidden with Christ in God [n: (Coloss. III)]*. ⟦s18⟧ For where we are dead, there we ought not to savor; but there we ought to savor, where we have life. ⟦s19⟧ If we are dead upon the earth, how do we savor upon the earth? ⟦s20⟧ For what dead man perceives? ⟦s21⟧ There, [0195C] therefore, we ought to perceive, where we have life; there to savor, where our life is hidden with Christ in God, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God. ⟦s22⟧ What is it to savor? ⟦s22⟧ To love him, that is to savor. ⟦s23⟧ For love itself is savor, and love itself is wisdom. ⟦s24⟧ And so to love the things that are above, this is to savor the things that are above; because love itself draws thither where that is which is loved: and meanwhile it has its own savor by which it is refreshed, until one comes to that which is loved. ⟦s25⟧ Since, therefore, what is loved through wisdom is above, for that reason Wisdom herself draws upward. ⟦s26⟧ And because what is loved through concupiscence, it is necessary that concupiscence itself also draw downward. ⟦s27⟧ But from that whereby Wisdom draws upward, and concupiscence downward, there is as it were a division of darkness from light: and the hearts which [0195D] descend downward fall away from the light; but those which ascend are in the light. ⟦s28⟧ For they who descend to vanity fall away from truth, and they who fall away from truth fall away from the light; because truth is light. ⟦s29⟧ And the light itself does not fail, but they themselves fall away from the light, when they become darkness; because darkness is not light, nor is it illumined by the light so as to shine. ⟦s30⟧ Nevertheless the light itself shines even in darkness, but the darkness does not comprehend it [n: (Joan. I)], so as to receive it into itself and become partakers of it, since darkness cannot have communion with light. ⟦s31⟧ These, then, are the darkness in which fools walk: they who savor earthly things and, not understanding their own honor, *are compared to senseless beasts, and are become like to them [n: (Psal. XLVIII)]*. ⟦s32⟧ For [0196A] because after the manner of beasts they seek only earthly things, and seek after heavenly things with no affection of the mind, at length they are so blinded by the delight of the flesh, and so lulled asleep by the enticements of pleasures, that they are neither able [sic: *valcant*] to think from what goods they have fallen, nor to look forward to what evils they are to come. ⟦s33⟧ What, then, do these do but walk in darkness, who are so blinded by a crooked delight that they can neither know true goods nor foresee true evils? ⟦s34⟧ What darkness do they suffer within, who do not see the light that is present, and cannot recognize that good whose presence they are able by no turning away to avoid? ⟦s35⟧ Why is this? unless because folly has blinded their eyes: which they have fixed in the ends of the earth, determining with themselves to turn their eyes down into the earth.
