## HOMILY XII. *On that passage, according to another translation: The eyes of fools are in the ends of the earth; where our copy has: The fool walketh in darkness, and on the remainder of the second chapter.* (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But to the sinner he gave affliction, and superfluous [sic: surperfluam] care, that he may labor, that he may be distended, and gather, and not possess, but hand it over to him who pleases God.* ⟦s2⟧ Therefore good things to the good, and evil things to the evil. ⟦s3⟧ Consider how great is this perversity. ⟦s4⟧ *Iniquity hath lied to itself (Psal. [0202D] XXVI)*. ⟦s5⟧ Iniquity has lied both about itself and about God; and yet it has lied to none but itself. ⟦s6⟧ Iniquity said: *Good things to the good, and evil things to the evil [n: (Eccli. XXXIX)]*. ⟦s7⟧ And where is that word: *Mercy and truth have met each other? [n: (Psal. LXXXIV.)]* Where is that: *All the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth? [n: (Psal. CXVIII.)]* Where is that: *Men and beasts thou wilt save, O Lord: how hast thou multiplied thy mercy, O God? [n: (Psal. XXXV.)]* Where, in short, is that which Truth herself and mercy says: *Be ye merciful, as your Father also is merciful: who maketh his sun to rise upon the good and the bad, and raineth upon the just and the unjust? [n: (Matth. V.)]* If good things have been given to the good alone, what is it that Scripture says of the good: *The kiln trieth the potter's vessels, and the furnace of tribulation just men? [n: (Eccli. XXVII.)]* [0203A] And: *All who wish to live godly in Christ must needs suffer persecution [n: (II Tim. III)]*. ⟦s7⟧ *They went about in sheepskins, in goatskins, being in want, naked, straitened, afflicted, of whom the world was not worthy [n: (Hebr. XI)]*. ⟦s8+9⟧ *In hunger and thirst, in cold and nakedness? [n: (II Cor. XI.)]* And so iniquity lied about God, when it said: That God gives good things to the good alone, and evil things to the evil alone. ⟦s8+9⟧ For he both gives good things to the good, and gives evil things to the evil; and gives evil things to the good, and good things to the evil. ⟦s10+11⟧ For to the good he both gives good things that they may be cherished, and gives evil things that they may be exercised; and to the evil he gives evil things that they may be chastised, and gives good things that they may be called forth. ⟦s12⟧ And so iniquity lied about God, when it said: God gives good things to the good and evil things to the evil. ⟦s13⟧ But it would have spoken truth if it had spoken of true goods and of true evils, namely that true goods and true evils are goods to none but the good, and [0203B] evils to none but the evil. ⟦s14⟧ For this reason, then, iniquity lied about God. ⟦s15⟧ But iniquity has also lied about itself. ⟦s16⟧ When did iniquity lie about itself? ⟦s17⟧ When it called itself goodness, then did iniquity lie about itself, because iniquity is not goodness. ⟦s18⟧ And where did iniquity call itself goodness? ⟦s19⟧ When it said: Who shall so devour, and abound with delights, as I? ⟦s20⟧ Then it called itself goodness: for iniquity said this, since this saying against goodness and honor belongs to iniquity and impurity. ⟦s21⟧ Therefore iniquity said this, and in this it called itself goodness: because it said that this alone belongs to goodness which it said it would do, since it presumed itself to be goodness. ⟦s22⟧ For it said:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To a good man God gave, in his own sight, wisdom and knowledge and gladness.* ⟦s2⟧ First he gave wisdom and knowledge, that he might understand and know how to seek gladness [0203C] in his sight: because God made it that those who please him might exult in it. ⟦s3⟧ As though indeed it were not wisdom, and much the greater wisdom, to seek and embrace that sadness which is according to God, rather than a foolish gladness, since on the contrary Scripture says: *The heart of [sic: stulterum] is where mirth is, and the heart of the wise where sadness is [n: (Eccle. VII)]*, This indeed is true, that God has prepared gladness for his elect in his sight, and has set wisdom and knowledge in their heart, that they may know and understand how to seek it where it is. ⟦s4⟧ But this is not that gladness with which they are glad who do evil: *who rejoice in the worst things [n: (Prov. II)]*. ⟦s5⟧ For that gladness is not in his sight, because of those who embrace it Scripture said: [0203D] *Thou shalt set them downward [var: Vulg. dorsum, "the back"], in thy remnants thou shalt prepare their countenance [n: (Psal. XX)]*. ⟦s6⟧ Therefore God gave gladness to the good man, but that gladness which is in his sight. ⟦s7⟧ Of which it is written: *Let the just feast, and exult in the sight of God: let them be delighted with gladness [n: (Psal. LXVII)]*. ⟦s8⟧ And again: *Visit us with thy salvation, that we may see the good of thy elect: that we may be glad in the gladness of thy nation, that thou mayest be praised with thine inheritance [n: (Psal. CV)]*. ⟦s9⟧ And again he says: *Thou shalt fill me with gladness with thy countenance: delights at thy right hand even unto the end [n: (Psal. XV)]*. ⟦s10⟧ This gladness, then, God gave to the good man in his sight.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But to the sinner he gave affliction, and superfluous care.* ⟦s2⟧ Not by bringing in malice, but by descending to malice into the worst occupation.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0204A] *That he may add and gather, and hand it over to him who hath pleased God.* ⟦s2⟧ Yet for this reason, because the one gathered riches through avarice, and the other, having received them, scattered them to the use of mercy. ⟦s3⟧ Otherwise neither is he who gathered rightly rebuked, nor is he who possesses commended. ⟦s4⟧ But now all things are said perversely, when it is said that he who devours and abounds with delights is good, and that to him God gave wisdom and knowledge and gladness in his sight — whereas it was said above that they are fools who have their eyes in the ends of the earth, looking to earthly delights and serving the pleasure of the flesh. ⟦s5⟧ And so iniquity lied, which said that pleasure is the reward of justice, and that the fruit of wisdom consists in it, and judged that bodily delights contain the sum of happiness. ⟦s6⟧ But a false judgment cannot long [0204B] stand in glory. ⟦s7⟧ For truth comes forth, and lays falsehood low. ⟦s8⟧ He says, in fact.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But this also is vanity, and an empty carefulness of the mind.* ⟦s2⟧ What is this? ⟦s3⟧ Not only is this vanity and empty carefulness, that the sinner should add and gather and not possess; but this too is vanity and empty carefulness, that the just man should seek after and covet the riches of the sinner, or, if he has received them, should set his heart upon them. ⟦s4⟧ The whole, then, is vanity and empty carefulness, because nothing under the sun can remain, either for him who uses or for him who seeks riches and pleasures and joy and gladness. ⟦s5⟧ For all things pass away and flow off, and nothing subsists under the sun, so that the sentiment may be fulfilled: *Vanity of vanities, vanity of vanities, and all things vanity [n: (Eccle. I)]*. ⟦s6⟧ For vanity is filled full in this also, [0204C] that the very heart of man does not remain in the same state, but, always other than itself and adverse to itself and a stranger outside itself, always removes far from that which is into that which is not; and it passes over and is snatched away with the greatest vanity, whether from vain things to true or from true things to vain, everywhere vain and unstable, now seeking the highest things, now plunging itself into the lowest. ⟦s7⟧ There approving truth; here setting vanity before it. ⟦s8⟧ And we now see this greatest wavering of the human mind, contemplating in one man the whole race of men, and we were wondering how quickly the sense of mortals is brought to ruin, and, after so sublime a contemplation of truth, suffers itself to go into the alien things of baseness. ⟦s9⟧ From which indeed nothing else is given us to understand, [0204D] except that we should know our own wretched vanity, and that in this, that we reach to high things and approve the better, we should consider what sort of being man was made by nature: but in this, that we seek after things so wretched and abject, we should understand what sort of being man has been made by his own guilt, so that at least in this we may begin to be partakers of truth, that we truly acknowledge our own vanity.
