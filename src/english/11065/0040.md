## HOMILY XIV. *A declaration of the remaining things that have their own time, and a repetition of what has been said.* (cont.)

*A time to scatter stones and a time to gather.* But what this means according to the letter is rightly [0219A] asked, since such a thing, if it be thought to have a time among human actions, seems altogether idle and unfitting. Unless perhaps it is to be understood thus: that, because he is going to say in what follows, *A time of war and a time of peace,* he fittingly sets before it here a time to scatter stones and a time to gather; namely, that when a time of war is at hand the stones may be gathered for building fortifications and the ramparts of walls against sieges; but in a time of peace, because no perils of wars are threatening, the mounds of the fortifications may be scattered, so that access may lie open to all without hindrance. Nor ought it to seem superfluous, on the ground that he had said above, A time to destroy and a time to build, as though the same thing were repeated a second time; because that is taken as more fittingly said of the buildings [0219B] of houses and the dwellings of men, whereas this refers more properly to the defenses of walls. Yet perhaps another sense, more suitable, may be understood under these words. For because he had set before, A time to kill and a time to heal, lest perhaps it should be thought that only the evil, and those who are to be punished for their sins, are exposed to slaughter, he now shows that the just also are sometimes, by God's permitting, for an example and chastisement of the evil, subjected together with the impious and with sinners to deaths as well, and scattered by exiles and proscriptions, as is said through Peter: *It is time that judgment begin at the house of God [n: (I Petr. IV)]*. And that by the name of stones the just are signified is manifestly shown through Jeremiah, [0219C] when he says: *The stones of the sanctuary are scattered at the head of all the streets [n: (Thren. IV)]*. And Isaiah again: *I will make all thy walls of graven stones, all thy sons taught of the Lord [n: (Isa. LIV)]* [var: Vulg. *omnes terminos tuos in lapides desiderabiles*, "all thy borders of desirable stones"]. Therefore it is a time to scatter stones, that the just may understand from the very labor of their scattering and shaking that there is no true rest in this life. And again it is a time to gather stones, that after the wearing down of bodily exile, lest they fail in mind, when peace has been restored they may again grow strong by a temporal benefit unto things eternal. Thus what is hidden can with difficulty be judged toward any one side: since the divine word for this reason dissembles itself from us in certain places, that even there where it was thought to be manifest it may show itself worthy [0219D] of searching out.
