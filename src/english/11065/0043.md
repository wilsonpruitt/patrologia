## HOMILY XVI. *On the spiritual understanding of those things which have been said concerning time.*

But now it is time that, after the running through of the times according to the things that roll outwardly in time, we should also call to mind the spiritual understanding. For little by little [0226A] the discourse of wisdom advances toward the touching of spiritual things, that it may draw the exercised mind to inward things. We distinguished above, then, two lives: one according to the flesh, the other according to the spirit. Now the carnal life is prior in time, in that every man is born in the flesh before he is reborn in the spirit; and everyone who lives in the flesh either walks according to the flesh and has not yet attained the spiritual life, or walks according to the spirit and has renounced the carnal life. Wherefore every spiritual man has been made spiritual out of a carnal man; but not every carnal man has been made carnal out of a spiritual man. For the carnal life is always either alone or first, so that every man either holds it alone, so as never to begin the spiritual life, or, having had it first, exchanges it by a following pursuit of the spiritual life [0226B]. Each of these two lives has its own followers: the carnal indeed those who love the joys of the present life; the spiritual those who seek out the pleasantness of the life to come. Of these, the former cherish the flesh with the blandishments of earthly delights, while the latter exercise the spirit by the pursuit of virtues in the hope and desire of things to come. With these two peoples the world runs down to its end through the mutability of the times, until it come that by the appointed covenant of justice the judgment of both be made. For it pleased God, that he might show the riches of the glory to come upon the vessels of mercy, to lead along also the vessels of wrath in forbearance through a temporal mercy; and likewise to exercise those same vessels of mercy, that justice and truth may be fulfilled, meanwhile now with the troubles of temptations: so that every life may have [0226C] both what it may receive freely of mercy, lest goodness be left unknown, and what it may feel as strict of justice, that truth may be made manifest. Therefore God set before every man both good things and evils, that he might show forth both, namely mercy and truth: the one, by which without merit he freely bestows a benefit; the other, by which for evil merit he repays a worthy punishment. Therefore he disposed that man should receive both; and he left it in man's power which he would first receive. But he set a condition of choosing upon both, lest perhaps depraved concupiscence be excusable: so that if man chose temporal goods in this life for his pleasure, he should not be able to obtain the eternal; and if he willingly submitted himself here to the scourge of chastisement in the judgment of truth, in the [0226D] life to come he should not feel the torments of damnation. Now the reprobate, who love the temporal life and set its pleasantness before the joys of the everlasting life, embrace present goods with all their desire, and set all their happiness in them; but the elect patiently endure temporal griefs, that they may be able to come to the joys of eternity that have been promised; and in regard to those joys, whatever is transitory, if it seem bitter, they do not fear; if sweet, they do not covet. Whence it comes about that the lovers of the carnal life have good things first and afterwards receive evils; but the spiritual are first exercised with adversities and afterwards attain to good things. To the former the first things are good, the last evil; to the latter the first things evil, the last good. But because [0227A] again all things that pass away in time are confused and mingled, so that they keep no order, whether from good into evil or from evil into good, therefore now meanwhile between the beginning and the end a manifold vicissitude runs in: namely, that now after good things evils, now after evils good things, should befall by an uncertain lot both the evil and the good alike. All these things, while Ecclesiastes was setting forth a certain distinction concerning the rolling of the times and the vicissitude of the things that are varied in time, he carefully expressed for an example of all things, that he might show the lot of both the good and the evil together, and their advance running into contraries. For when he had said that all things that are under heaven are varied by the turning of time and are borne into contraries, he subjoined examples, and enumerated many things out of all, that [0227B] the nature of all might lie open. And among those things which he enumerated, there are certain in which good things and evils are recalled - which indeed in which evils and good things, so that the state and condition both of the carnal and of the spiritual may alike be recognized. For there are seven in which good things are first recalled, afterwards evils; and again seven in which evils are first spoken of, afterwards good things, so that the whole may run down on either side unto the end. For by the number seven this whole present life is expressed, which rolls on by the course of seven days unto its own end. The evil, therefore, have their sevenfold number, and the good likewise have their own sevenfold number; because this whole life of the evil is from good into evil, and the whole life of the good from evil into good, of those going [sic: *cuntium*]. Of which [0227C] life the beginning of the evil is in good and the end in evil; but of the good the beginning is in evil and the end in good, with the middle things fluctuating by turns. For the middle things are uncertain: and for this reason also that which has been set in the middle has been pronounced doubtfully, whether from good into evil or from evil into good. For the middle was a time to scatter stones and a time to gather: and this itself has received an ambiguous exposition, signifying either of war or of peace, so that the middle things may be left uncertain. For if in this the thing that is said first is evil, and what comes afterwards good, then there will be eight of those in which evils are named first and good things afterwards, and six in which good things are recalled first and evils afterwards; because the life of the good rightly passes over to the number of beatitude, while the intention of the evil, together with their life, does not exceed the labor of temporality. Thus the truth is [0227D] one, whithersoever you may twist the sentiment by interpretation. There are many other things which perhaps might fittingly have been said in this enumeration or distinction. But we, avoiding weariness as far as is possible, let us come to the setting forth spiritually of the things that have been said.

*All things have their time, and all things under heaven pass away in their own spans.* If man had persisted in obedience to the commandment, he would not be of the number of those things which have their time under heaven and pass away. For if he had not flowed down in mind through iniquity, he would have remained stable in body also through immortality. Nor would he then be under heaven, so as to be rolled about with the things that pass away with time; but cleaving to the highest and true good, he would not be subjected [0228A] to the moments of the times, being fixed by contemplation in the solidity of things eternal. For eternity itself was the heaven, where, fixed in mind, he could not be changed by time; because, lifted above the times, he looked down upon all things gliding away beneath him. But after he turned aside his heart, so as to subject it through concupiscence to mutable things, then, as though fallen from heaven, he began to flow downward together with the things to which he clung, and, led through the rollings of his own mutability, to feel the moments of the times. Hence the human mind was made liable to times, so that in it now evils have their turn along with good things — the mind which good things alone before possessed, quiet and unshaken, without vicissitude. Therefore all things have their time, and all things under heaven pass away in their own spans, so that he too who through love subjected himself [0228B] to mutable things, agitated by the law of others with the waves of his own mutability, cannot stand at rest. Thus therefore the times came into the human heart, when many desires entered [sic: *introicrunt*] into it, and mutability began to hold sway there; and some came and others departed, and there arose a great fluctuation and inconstancy. For now the invisible world was rolling along with the visible world, and the moments of the times began to run their course within it, and there began to be years, and months, days and nights, sowings and harvests, rains and droughts, fruitfulnesses and barrennesses. For all these things are carried out in the nature of spiritual beings, the likeness of corruptible things having been brought in by the harm of sin, so that a nature made for eternity, wrongly associated with that which passes away temporally, likewise fails into alteration. For the shoots [0228C] of the virtues rise from the first root, and go on to grow and to come to the effect of consummation. And against them come the risings of vices and the cockle sown over them; and they lift themselves up to choke the things that are right, that they may not prosper; and there comes a great expectation, and a heavy peril mingled with hope and with fear. For if God pour out the rain of grace and the droppings of his truth upon a thirsting soul, there rises a harvest of virtues with manifold increase; and years of fruitfulness come, and calm seasons shine out, the cloud of ignorance being driven away. But if in anger he take away his benefits, and hold back his blessing that he may exercise his judgments, then sad seasons follow, and the good [sic: *genimima*] wither in the drought, and the thistles and thorns of vices are multiplied for labor and grief to sinful [0228D] man. And in all these things the sinful soul suffers its own times, looking out upon the heaven above, which it has lost, unmoved in its eternity, while it itself is rolled downward, temporal; and in it all things which cannot stand pass through by their own spans and measures. And therefore all things have their time, and all things under heaven pass away in their own spans.

*A time to be born and a time to die.* This is the lot of sinners in time: that if by chance any good things are born in them, afterwards they die, and do not persevere unto the end. For sometimes even in the evil some good things are born, and begin as it were to rise and put forth a shoot; but once born they soon dry up, because they have no moisture. For they believe for a time, and in the time [0229A] of temptation they fall away, so that they have a time and do not come through to eternity. A time to be born and a time to die. For the depraved there is a time to be born in good, when through a sudden fervor of mind they begin some right things; but there follows them straightway a time to die, because, not having perseverance in the good, by the same lightness of mind with which they had begun, in the time of impending pressure they abandon the good things begun.

*A time to plant and a time to pluck up.* Good things are planted in the evil, when through exhortation or right admonition they sometimes take up good things; but they are plucked up afterwards, when, shaken by a depraved suggestion, they lose the good things they had begun. They are born, therefore, by their own will; they are planted by another's admonition; but they die when by their own desires [0229B] they grow lukewarm toward the good; they are plucked up when they fall by another's supplanting. And in all these things the evil have their own times, in which by a just judgment they are disposed, whether to this or to that; and everywhere good things go before, and evils follow after for those foreseen unto evil. Then follows the life of the good, in which evils go before that good things may follow after, and those very evils that run before work together unto good.

*A time to kill, a time to heal.* In the elect the old man is first killed, and the carnal life which lives according to the affection of the flesh; then he is healed who according to the spirit is the new man, that he may live unto God.

*A time to destroy and a time to build.* Every man first makes an evil building, when [0229C] he works in the flesh, and daily adds evils to evils, so that a depraved building rises; and this must be destroyed and overthrown in those who are saved, that justice may be built in them.

*A time to weep and a time to laugh.* First a man ought to weep for the depraved things he himself has committed, that afterwards he may laugh securely in the good things he has received from God. For unless a preceding weeping wash the conscience in contrition, in vain does laughter exult over the things that are glad outwardly, without inward pleasantness.
