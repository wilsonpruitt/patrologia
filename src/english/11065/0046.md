## HOMILY XVII. *On the confusion of minds arising from the passing of times.*

[0236D]

Just now we have come out from the times, but would that we had come through to eternity! For many things still remain, and very many are rolled about us: and again the times repeat their circuit on every side, so that there is no end until all things shall have been consummated. For upon the mutability of things there follows a grievous confusion of minds: since hearts go along upon the rollings and passings of times, so that they do not stand in truth, but flow away with the things which go that they may pass. Therefore he ceases from all these things which totter without, and fluctuate, as though all these were small and not working greatly unto peril: and he turns himself inward, and there redoubles his complaint concerning the vanity [0237A] which is hidden: and he examines that one with grievous expostulation over the one which appears without as alien. For the mutability of things would not have harmed, if the mutability of minds had not been: since this very thing was good, that transitory things should change their appearance for a time, but it was not good that the judgments of human minds should desert truth. And so we do not lay the blame that times are various, since it is good: but we lay the blame that human judgments are various: since this is not good. For truth cannot of itself be other: because what is good in truth is not evil: and what is not good in truth, is evil. Why therefore is it judged that this is good, and this is evil; and is it judged that this is evil, and this is good: when in truth either [0237B] it is not good, if it is evil, or it is not evil, if it is good. One says, It is good, and another says, It is evil: and he who says, This is good, says That is evil: and he who says This is evil, says That is good: and all speak ill, because the whole is good. But this alone is not good, that they speak ill of that which is good. Wherefore all things are good: and those alone are evil who beyond all the rest ought to have been good. For all the rest are what they ought to have been, and they alone are not what they ought to have been: because they are not truthful, and are not in truth; and therefore all things are good in that which they are, but they themselves are not good in that which they are, because they are not what they ought to have been. This therefore, among the other vanities, is itself also reckoned for a great vanity, [0237C] and alone is set before all vanities by comparison. And he says:

*I saw the affliction which God gave to the sons of men, that they should be distended in it.* For what is that affliction?
