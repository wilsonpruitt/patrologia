## HOMILY XVII. *On the confusion of minds arising from the passing of times.* (cont.)

*He hath made all things good in their time: and he hath delivered the world to their disputation; that man may not find the work which God hath wrought from the beginning unto the end.* Therefore the mutability of things does not afflict man, but his own disputation afflicts him. And rightly: because disputation always signifies restlessness and struggle. And therefore the mutability of things is not man's labor; but that he himself has been made restless, this is his labor. And that restlessness itself is the great disputation [0237D] which man has in his own instability, so that he does not feel the same thing: since he is divided, and made another, so that he is not one whole. Consider now the great disputation which man carries on upon the earth. For it is much, and long, and exceedingly involved: so that it cannot easily have an end, until man himself receives his end. And now, that you may believe, see how many sentiments concerning the works of God those curious of wisdom have formed from the beginning: and there has been no one until now who could impose an end upon this so great business. And they wrangle still daily, and fight with struggles as they dispute: and one says This is so, and another says It is not, but it is otherwise; and many things are said, and lies of opinions are feigned as though wrought by craft concerning the judgment of things. And very many assert [0238A] that there is nothing except that very thing which is seen, and others come, and testify that nothing truly is except that very thing which is not seen. And in all these things they heap up and multiply their arguments, and weave reasons, and as it were reasons: and each man's reason is his own estimation. For God has set forth his work before the eyes of men, that he may question their hearts concerning it: and he himself lies hidden behind, until they may make trial of our exercise in it. And they draw with sense, and judge with heart, and bring forth very many things out of the treasury of their judgments, and out of the deep abyss of their fantasies, and of their false opinions and empty thoughts. And some say that nature alone is, and there is nothing else; and God is nothing, but empty fear has devised all things; and so it always was, [0238B] what is from the beginning, and before the beginning without beginning. And the ages revolve, and nature works itself, and renews itself, and cannot be other than it always was. Others on the contrary cry out and wrangle for the wrong done to the Creator, and think that they defend him whom they themselves assail with their lies. They say that there is a craftsman who from matter co-eternal with himself has fashioned all things, that he might give a better form; and these men do not know the power of the Creator, but deny that anything has been made from nothing: and they deny that any of these things which are can slip back into nothing: but that the Founder's work consists only in the alterations of things. After these others follow, wrangling and disputing, and they promise to take away error and to make truth manifest; and these themselves confess that all things were made from nothing, and that he was not made [0238C] through whom all things were made; and that there was once a time when nothing was created, and that then he was who always was. And these men have in their teachings as it were a good beginning, but an evil end follows. For those who think as it were truly concerning the creation of things, lie exceedingly concerning the subsistence of things, and there is no end of the disputations and inventions of men. They feign essences, and forms, and atoms, and ideas of the principal constitutions, and very many elements, and infinite births, and invisible motions and procreative efficiencies. And in all these things the likenesses of reasons are multiplied, and very many disputations are made, and truth is not in them all. For likewise he who speaks, speaks falsely, and he who contradicts, [0238D] speaks falsely, because by disputing and by lying they have all by one vanity gone far away from truth. For neither shall man be able to find the work which God hath wrought from the beginning unto the end. Out of all the things which have been made from the beginning and which are to be made unto the end, man will not be able to find any one thing so as to comprehend it, and know the whole of what it is. For even if certain men seem to have found very many things, all things had been set forth, and the things which were hidden could in no way be found. For God delivered the world to their disputation, *and set before them his works, that they might see them and judge.* And in these he manifested many things which he willed to be known, and the things which he willed to be hidden could not be found. Therefore God manifested in them all the things which are known; and man did not find them [0239A] by his own sense, until they were revealed in him so far as God willed. For certain things were set forth without, and certain things were revealed within, and man found nothing, so as to go by his own sense to those things which were hidden, until they were made manifest in him. And if he should wish to contend that he himself found some things, could he nevertheless find all the things which God hath wrought from the beginning unto the end? And in the very things which he found, could he find the whole of what was, and did there not remain over something hidden and secret to which his sense was not admitted, so that he might find the whole? For if man had found the whole, there would be no disputation at all, nor would opinions run in opposite directions concerning all those things which are in the world. For we all alike see this world: and it is placed before our eyes, [0239B] that it might be delivered to our disputation; and we see this whole before us, and we do not know whence it came hither. For he who made it lies hid, and stretches his work out before himself, that he may be hidden, and be made manifest in it. For the image that has been made, receiving human contemplation from near at hand, brings it about that one can neither see his Creator manifestly, nor likewise be wholly ignorant of him. Therefore the understanding of man, exercised by the appearance of things, rises up, and from that which offers itself to manifestation strives, according to its emulation of truth, to search out inward things; and it has in the works of God the beginning of first contemplation, but it does not come through unto the end of the works of God, so as to grasp the whole of what is hidden. For if it had been able to come through to the end, it would assuredly have found God, since from him from whom [0239C] all things are, there do all things have their end and consummation. It does not therefore come to the end, since it could not find God, until he himself should manifest himself in us; and they that search searchings have been confounded, since they have failed [n: (Psal. LXIII)], and have not availed to comprehend and to make an end of their disputations. Therefore *God hath made foolish the wisdom of the world [n: (I Cor. V)]*, because he placed the world in their disputation, himself remaining hidden until the disputers and those seeking empty things should fail. And man shall not find every work which God hath wrought from the beginning unto the end. After these things God revealed himself and came forth that he might be found, when man could no longer glory as though he himself by his own sense and wisdom had known God, who had failed in [0239D] searching out the work of God. To this end therefore it profited that the world was for a time delivered to their disputation, that man might not find [sic: *invenirit*] every work which God hath wrought from the beginning unto the end. These are the disputations of men seeking with vain curiosity the hidden things of the works of God, which they multiply for themselves by fabricating lies concerning the things they do not know, and they fortify their errors with [sic: *concortationibus*]. And there are yet certain other disputations in the desires of men, exceedingly manifold and various, and out of them arise the pursuits of many vanities. For every man prefers what he himself loves, and the hearts of all are drawn into infinite rendings, so that they never agree in one. Some follow wantonness and luxury and pleasure: to be drunken and to play and to laugh and to dance, and [0240A] they reckon this to be the happiness of man and the sweetest delight. Others court honors, and dignities, and great fame among the peoples, and commend their own pursuits, and dispute with grievous struggle, that this portion is better in the life of man. Others heap up moneys and pile up wealth, and think to procure security, and great confidence against oncoming want in the time of straitness. And these men praise foresight and say that solicitude is better, though they wish nevertheless to be secure themselves. And thus indeed all are carried away into their own appetites, each one apart with contrary approval; and they dispute by quarreling, and by dissenting with importunate desires. And the world is delivered to their disputation, that in its affluence each one may make trial of his own pleasures and prove them, and [0240B] see, and consume them into arguments one against the other about themselves out of its plenty, each man of that thing which he himself shall have chosen in the approval of his own desires. And each one snatches with great obstinacy what he has chosen for himself; and each praises his own part, and there comes to be a great disputation and altercation of wills in the various estimations which many desires bring forth. And in all these things man does not find the work which God hath wrought from the beginning unto the end. For the beginning of the works of God is the use of temporal things; but the end and consummation, the fruit of eternal things. Therefore he who establishes his disputation in the things of the world, and seeks out the approval of his own desires, cannot find in the works which God hath wrought anything from the beginning unto the end; since, [0240C] although he is allowed to make trial how great delight there can be in the goods of this world, yet that ineffable pleasantness which confers consummated happiness he does not lay hold of in the contemplation of God. For this reason the world has been delivered to the lovers of the world, to whom it is not given to deserve to find the works of God from the beginning unto the end; because to those from whom the sweetness of eternal goods is taken away, carnal delights have been given, and the joys of the world set forth unto the dissipation of their evil desires. We find yet other disputations in the judgments of men, grievous and manifold, and horrendous stirring up quarrels [sic: *rigas coacitantes*], so that they grow even into blasphemy of the Creator, and men say the things they ought not. For men violently seize the place [0240D] of God, and set themselves up as judges of the world, and dispute concerning the world and concerning the works of God, and his providence, and the judgments by which he disposes the whole world. And some say, God has done this well; and others murmur, and say, That was not well done. For these are the complaints of men, and the presumptions by which they provoke the wrath of God; because they will not be subjected to his laws, but dispute concerning his works, and reprehend his judgments. Someone has perished by a cruel death or by grievous loss, and has been crushed by a miserable chance; and his enemy comes and says: God has done well; he has judged an impious and sinful man according to his malice; and his friend murmurs, and raises a complaint against God, why he permitted an innocent man to perish, and did not guard the just man. And that man is condemned [0241A] for his malice; and this one is judged for his rashness. And yet men do not cease to dispute concerning the works of God, and to weigh his judgments by their own estimation; and God is reprehended by the thoughts of malignant men. For men say that God ought not to have created harmful things, nor to have placed among his works the things which are hostile and do harm; and they curse the good creatures of God, and blaspheme their Creator. Frogs, and flies, and serpents, and all venomous things, and all adverse and pestilential things — why did God make them? For he would have done better, if he had made all things good. For evil things are very many, and all evil things are not good. And he indeed made all things good in their time; but man alone is evil, not understanding goodness nor retaining the judgment [0241B] of truth, and with an evil will disagrees with goodness: and when that comes to pass which he himself evilly does not will, he says that it is ill done; whereas it is well done, and he himself evilly wills. Therefore evil wills and wicked concupiscences blind the hearts of men, so that they do not understand what is right, because they do not love nor will what is good. And they reprehend God because he made harmful things; and yet no one reprehends when his own enemy is hurt, though they themselves all are enemies of God, and adversaries of truth. For this reason man does not understand the work which God hath wrought from the beginning unto the end. For *the carnal man perceiveth not the things that are of God [n: (I Cor. II)]*. For he follows his own concupiscence, and praises the delight of his own desires, and when what he loves is given him, he thinks that it goes well with him. when rather this comes to pass [0241C] unto his harm; and when his desires are withstood, he murmurs and raises a complaint; and he lays blame, as though it were ill done when it is justly ordained; and he does not understand the works which God works from the beginning unto the end. From the time when he is brought forth out of the earth, until he is called back into the earth: he does not understand the works which God hath wrought from the beginning unto the end. For God works many things about man from his beginning and unto the end of him, and man does not find to what end all these things are done, until he himself receives his end. But then he shall understand, and then he shall find the things which God hath wrought in him from his own beginning unto his own end, whether in mercy or in judgment, that he might lead him to such an end as he approved by his works from the beginning unto the end, that [0241D] he should come into consummation. But meanwhile, until the end comes, from the beginning unto the end all things lie hidden, so that man does not find the work which God hath wrought from the beginning unto the end. Therefore he can dispute concerning the works of God, which God works from the beginning unto the end; but he cannot find the work which God hath wrought from the beginning unto the end. But if man cannot find the work which God works from his own beginning unto his own end, so as to understand at least this which he himself has seen, how then shall he find the works which God hath wrought from the beginning of the world unto the end of the age, which he himself could not see? Let man therefore cease to dispute concerning the judgments of God, by which he governs and disposes the whole world from the beginning unto the end; because man was placed by God neither as ruler [0242A] nor as judge of the world, but as possessor; nor that by his own strength or power he should rule the elements of the world or dispense the outcomes of times at his own choice, but that he should receive the fruits of the world, and the changes of times, according to the Creator's dispensation, for the use of nourishing his own weakness.
