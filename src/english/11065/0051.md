## HOMILY XVIII. *On the perverse morals of men: and what Ecclesiastes judged concerning them.* (cont.)

*They were made of earth, and into earth they alike return.* And if anyone should wish to say: that man has this more than the beast, that the spirit of the sons of Adam goes upward, so that, the body falling into death, it remains surviving in life; and that the spirit of the beasts descends downward, that is, together with the death of the body [0250D] flows away into corruption: who knows this? Yet not because it is not true, but because it is hidden: therefore:

*Who knoweth this?* For he did not say, This is false, but, Who knows this? No one knows this. For this cannot be known by man, and yet it can be believed. And it is true, because it is believed: and what is believed is true, and that belief itself is not doubtful but firm knowledge; and yet no man knows this in the manner in which those things are known which are seen, and heard, and touched, and perceived by the other senses; and in the manner in which those things are known concerning which one naturally cannot doubt, and which are demonstrated by indubitable reason even to unbelievers who doubt. For by faith alone is this perceived, and therefore there is great doubt for those who do not have faith concerning [0251A] this, because they do not know it, nor can it be demonstrated to them by those who lay hold of it by faith, because they themselves do not lay hold of faith. Therefore they themselves think that man has nothing at all more than the beast, because they do not know that the spirit of the sons of Adam goes upward, and the spirit of the beasts downward. And when it is said to them that man has more than the beast, because the spirit of man goes upward to life, and the spirit of the beasts downward to death, they say: Who knows this? For they do not think that anything can be known unless it be seen with the eye of the flesh, and touched by sense, and therefore they do not receive the knowledge of faith, by which alone man is called back to that in which he has more than the beast. And because they cannot see that, nor touch it in the open so as to comprehend what this is which man has more than the beast: [0251B] they despair utterly of perpetual life, and become unbelieving toward the things which are said, and cast themselves with their whole intent into the delights of the present life, as though this alone were their portion, and they were to receive nothing more afterward. For they do not know that for this reason it is hidden, that it may be believed that man has more than the beast, and that man himself may be proved by God in faith through his own ignorance. For if it were seen it would not be believed, but known; nor would there be merit, nor would man be proved, nor would the iniquitous be convicted, nor the good exercised. Therefore it is hidden that it may not be seen, that which man has more than the beast, so that faith may have merit, and unbelief its place. And there are many unbelievers, and they say: Who knows this? And they prove that things certain are not to be forsaken for things uncertain; and they begin to embrace present things, [0251C] and to snatch the things which are seen, that they may hold what is certain; and they are mocked in the uncertain, because these pass away, and slip out while they are thought to be held; and the things which are certain follow after, which were thought uncertain. All these things this man demonstrates, and he shapes his narrative this way and that, so as to follow the minds of men, since in this manner they themselves totter, and fluctuate in the uncertainty of a darkened life. For he told of the doubt of men concerning their own life, since they do not know whether man has more than the beast, and they do not find who has known whether after the death of the body the spirit of man remains surviving in life; now he takes up their own voice in approval of present delights, in accordance with this very doubt of theirs and despair of the life to come, saying:

*I have found out: that there is nothing better than that a man be glad [0251D] in his own work: and that this is his portion.* For those who do not believe that a life to come exists, these think that this alone is man's portion, that he be glad in his own work, and in this life enjoy his own labor; since those who do not reckon that a wage will follow after the work is completed, these judge that the profit of the work is to be taken not after the work, but in the work, and that he alone is happy who prepares the fruit of his own labor for use in the present, not he who reserves the wage of his own work to be taken hereafter.

*For who shall bring him, that he may know the things to come after him?* From the time when man has once gone out from this life, he is no more brought back, nor called back again into this life, that he may make trial and feel again the things which [0252A] are done in this world, so that he may anew enjoy its delights, and perceive the pleasantness of that which is to come after him. Therefore, as long as he lives, let him take as much as he can, and use this world before he departs, and is led forth from it; because he shall not return any more, nor be brought back, that he may know the things to come after him. And it can come to pass that another after him may abuse the things which he himself was unwilling to use, and a stranger rejoice and exult in his goods, and they can no more be brought back to his own use, once he has been taken away. For this reason, and for reasons of this kind, men think that this is their portion, that they should enjoy the pleasure of the world while they live; and for this alone they reckon that they were made, and that they will be nothing after these things, when this life shall have received its end. And they multiply reasonings and arguments, and heap up the things which are reasons to them, one [0252B] after another, that they may deceive themselves, and that their hearts may be confirmed in evil, and that they may believe what is false. And they build with lies a wall between themselves and truth, that they may not see it; and they set forth all things, and diligently search out all things by which they may plausibly demonstrate that there is no other life, and that this alone is good: and that man was made for this life only, and that there will be no other after it. And all this shaking rises up out of the darkness of the judgments of God; because he placed man in doubt that he might prove him, and that he might not see what man has more than the beast. And yet he himself again pursues that error with other judgments, and shows that this life is not good, in which very many evils reign; nor can true delight or tranquil rest be found in it. And therefore he sows over it [0252C] adverse things, and turns himself back, and goes far off, so that he does not free the oppressed, that they may be greatly afflicted, and that by grief and sadness the evil sweetness and the iniquitous delight may be wiped away. And therefore those who wish to be gladdened in this life, and make a covenant of love with the world, and iniquitously purpose not to seek the truth, are compelled by true judgments to see the evils which are under the sun, so that they may not please themselves overmuch in their turning away. Therefore this man, when he had said that it was good for man to be glad in his own work, and that this was his portion, and had given a definition for resting in these things, is moved by another consideration, that this life is not so, that rest can be in it. Therefore he adds and says:

[0252D] *I turned me to other things [n: (Eccles. IV)]*. He is rightly said to have turned to other things, because by those things which he is now about to see he is admonished to believe something other than what he had before reckoned. For all these things, which shall then be seen, may teach that the true good of man is elsewhere than in this life, which is subject to so great miseries and griefs, in which innocence is oppressed, and grief does not deserve consolation: this thing therefore other, and far other than that which before was seen and thought, was this man considering, and he said:
