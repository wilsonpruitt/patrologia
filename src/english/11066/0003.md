## I. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE ROMANS. (cont.)

QUESTION XLIII. *God gave them up to the desires of their heart, and to the passions of shame, and to a reprobate sense.* From these authorities, and many others, such as this: *He gave them the spirit of compunction, that seeing they might not see, and hearing they might not hear [n: (Rom. XI)]*. And this: *The heart of Pharaoh was hardened [n: (Exod. VII)]*. And this: *Whom he will he hardens, on whom he will he has mercy [n: (Rom. IX)]*. From these and many others certain evils seem to be brought about by God's operation. Augustine too heaps up many things into one for the proof of a matter of this kind. Afterward he infers. From which it is manifest [0442C] that God works in the hearts of men, in the good by inclining them to good things out of mercy, or in the evil by inclining them to evil things by his judgment, sometimes hidden, sometimes manifest, but always just. Likewise: *Is it not just, that he who is in filth should be filthy still? [n: (Apoc. XXII.)]* By whom is this just? Is it not by God, from whom is all that is just? Therefore God seems to work this. Likewise that sin which is the penalty of a preceding sin, from whom is it? All that is just is from God, and that sin is a just penalty. Therefore it seems to have its origin from God. Solution. To this indeed some respond, saying that all sin is from God, not only that which is the penalty of another, but also that which is only fault, conceding that theft, robbery, [0442D] adultery are from God, according to that saying of the prophet: *There is no evil in the city, which God does not make [n: (Amos. III)]*. Because also by such a reasoning they attempt to prove the same. Every essence is from God, but an evil will and an evil action are sin, and have essence: whence it is gathered that sin is from God according to the opinion of these men. Against whom the objection is made thus. What is it to make sin, but to sin? What is it to make adultery, but to make to be committed in adultery? to make theft, but to steal? Whence if it be conceded that he makes sin, it follows that God sins, steals, commits adultery, kills: which it is not only wicked to say, but even to think. That which is objected concerning the evil will and the action not good, that they are something, and thus from God. Thus it is resolved. Of sins one is according to [0443A] itself, another according to another. Sin according to itself is a certain disorder, as the privation of justice; and it itself is nothing but the absence of justice, whence it is not from God, since it is nothing. For God is not the author of that which is nothing. An evil will, and an action not good, is sin according to another, namely according to the disorder, and these are something, and are called sins not from that which they have, but from that which they do not have: for they are sins for this reason, that they do not have order or measure. That saying of the prophet, namely that he says: There is no evil in the city, which God does not make, is understood of the evil of adversity, not of the evil of perversity. Or perhaps we are not even compelled to understand this of the evil of adversity, if we look more diligently at the context of this Scripture. For thus [0443B] it stands in the prophet: There is no evil in the city, which the Lord does not make known to his servants the prophets. Likewise they object concerning the power of sinning, that it is from God: and it itself is sin, and thus sin is from God, as it seems. Solution. This is nothing, because the power of sinning is neither sin, nor a sufficient cause of sinning without the will.

QUESTION XLIII. Again it is asked thus. Whether every will is from God, since nothing is, which does not have its being from God? Solution. It is not to be conceded that every will is from God, since nothing is, which does not have its being from God: for this would be both good and evil alike. The same is understood of action. For when an evil action or will is spoken of, the expression redounds more upon the quality than upon the essence.

[0443C] Wherefore, although it has that whence it is said to be from God, yet since its quality is not from him, an evil action or will is not to be said to be from God. Note that disorder is said to be the quality of an evil action, on account of the similar manner of response; or "quality" is taken broadly for that by which it is assigned of what sort something is, or is not. Likewise this proposition: The essence of this action is from God, is understood in two ways: either that it itself is from God, or that from which it has being is from God. But others say that not every sin is from God; but only according to that which is the penalty of another, is done by God's operation; saying: If what is said, God gave them up to a reprobate sense, be referred to permission alone, this could be said generally of every sin, that God works [0443D] it; but God is never said to give anyone up to any sin, except that which is the penalty of sin. Likewise, if it be referred to the withdrawal of grace, from this it is plain that God works it, since he withdraws grace. Likewise since that follows necessarily from the withdrawal of grace, who is the cause of it but the withdrawer? as if a house had some prop without which it could not stand, if anyone took away the prop, who would become the cause of the ruin, but he who removed the prop? Or if some were in a little boat on the sea, if anyone sank it, since without it men could not live, who is the cause of death but the sinker? By these likenesses, then, they attempt to assert that God works certain sins, if not [0444A] all. But there does not seem to be a likeness between a house, which can merit nothing, and a man, by whose fault is offered that without which he cannot stand. But a more fitting likeness would be where, if a house were about to fall, and someone put his hand under and held it up lest it fall, if afterward, reason requiring it, he withdrew his hand, he would indeed not be the cause why the house fell, but he was the cause why it did not fall sooner. As if someone were naked, and another gave him clothes, and that clothed man then offended the one with whose clothes he is clad, so that he reasonably took away the clothes, and thus he died naked, who is the cause of death? Is it not he himself who dies naked? And if anyone, cause requiring it, withdraws teaching from someone to whom he before displayed it, he does this not by doing something, but rather by not doing what he was doing. [0444B] So too we say that God, by withdrawing grace, our fault requiring it, is not himself the cause why grace is withdrawn according to us: nor did he do this by doing something, but by not doing what he before did. Thus therefore it is by no reasoning to be conceded that sin is done by God's operation, whether it be the penalty of sin, or not; this therefore, which Augustine says, that God works in the hearts of men, either by inclining to good or to evil, is said to work either by the likeness of the diction of this verb *to withdraw,* which is construed with the accusative, as if it signified to do something; or, what is better, *to work* is taken for to work, or not to work, as here: *For what I work I do not understand [n: (Infra, c. 7)]*. For otherwise, if it were not so taken, to do evil, and not to do good, [0444C] would not be parts of working. And that: *He will render to each according to his works,* namely for those which he did, and for those which he did not do, when he ought to have done them. But that which is said: *He who is in filth, let him be filthy still [n: (Apoc. 22, infra, c. 2)]*, is understood thus: the justice is not from the quality of the defilement, but from the judgment of God, by which it is done. Whence, if it be called just, not the justice which is in it, but in the rule of God, not that its being defiled is from God, or that God does it. Likewise concerning any sin whatever which is the penalty of a preceding sin. For that such a penalty is called just, this is not from the quality of itself, but from the judgment of God.
