## I. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE ROMANS. (cont.)

QUESTION LVII. How is it rendered to each according to his own works, since this man works good things through his whole life and falls at the end, and another works evil things through his whole life and rises at the end, through penance; [0448B] will not he who worked good things be damned, and he who worked evil things be saved? How then according to works? Solution. A man's works are said to be those with which he departs from this life, by which alone God will requite him; but those good things which he did who fell at the end are mortified, and become not his own through the supervening evil. So too, in the case of him who lived badly and rose at the end through penance, the evil things he did become not his own through the supervening good things; or else judgment is made with respect to works, so that he is punished less, because he did good things, or will have less glory, because he sinned.

QUESTION LVIII. It is likewise asked how [it is] according to works, since this man has the will to do evil but not the power to carry it out, and another has the will to do good but not the power to fulfill it. Solution. [0448C] Their works are said to be theirs from the fact that they are in their wills, and it does not rest with them that they are not done. Whence the Lord in the Gospel: *Whoever shall look upon a woman to lust after her has already committed adultery with her in his heart [n: (Matth. V)]*; nor yet do we say that he is now punished as much as he would be punished for the works themselves fulfilled, but as for works, though he would not be punished as much as for the will alone.

QUESTION LIX. It is asked concerning an unbaptized child how requital is made to him according to his works, since he dies at once before he does anything. Solution. The works of his parents come to be in his conception and in his birth, and for them he will be damned as though he had actually done them. Likewise concerning a baptized child we say that [0448D] he is saved by the grace of baptism without his own merits.

QUESTION LX. It is asked concerning him who suffers for Christ and does not work, how it is rendered to him according to work? Solution. "Works" are taken both for sufferings and for works, as "deed" is taken for what is done and for what is not done: when that is done which ought not to be done, it is called a sin; but when that is not done which ought to be done, it is a fault.

QUESTION LXI. Since guilt is temporal and the punishment eternal, how will he render according to works? Solution. The guilt of the wicked, so far as lies in themselves, is eternal, for it was in their will to remain always in evil: whence by the just judgment of God they will be punished for eternity. Or else the evil will, and the guilt of fault, and the impenitence of heart will be eternal in him. Whence the Lord [0449A] is to say to them: *Depart from me, all you who work iniquity [n: (Luc. I)]*. He will not say, who have worked, but who work. For not to repent is a sin, and they cannot repent. And so they will sin for eternity, and thus for eternal guilt the punishment will be everlasting.

QUESTION LXII. Likewise, since the good are to receive more than they have merited, and the evil are punished less than they might be according to justice, how [is it] according to works? Solution. There is no comparison between merit and reward, but the sense is: he who has merited more will receive more, and he who less will receive less, according to quality and quantity: for the merit of one and the reward is at one time equal and unlike, at another time like and unequal, with the merit and reward of another; as two martyrs, or two confessors, who are of the same [0449B] and equal merit and reward, are equal and alike in justice and glory; but if one martyr and one confessor are equal, they are nevertheless unlike according to quality, because the one is a martyr and the other a confessor, etc.

QUESTION LXIII. It is asked concerning that which is said: *Tribulation and anguish upon every soul,* etc. [n: (Rom. II.)] Shall a man be punished in the soul alone, and not also in the body? Why then does he say, upon every soul, and not upon the body, since he sinned in both? Solution. There are certain sins of the soul, for which it alone will be punished, such as anger, envy, unbelief, of which the Apostle treats especially in this place: and therefore he makes mention of the punishment of the soul, not the punishment of the body. There are also certain sins of the body, for which [0449C] it will be punished, such as gluttony, lust, murders, contentions, which are performed by the ministry of the body; whence it is clear that they will be punished in both, according to that word: *With a double destruction destroy them [n: (Jerem. XVII)]*.

QUESTION LXIV. It is asked concerning the glorification of the body, what it confers upon the man or upon the soul, since the sole beatitude of man is the vision of God, of which the soul alone is capable, whence it seems to confer nothing upon the glory which will be in the body. Solution. The glorification of the body will be unto the increase of glory and joy for the soul itself. For it will greatly rejoice, when it sees the body, formerly so weak and feeble, thus made solid, that it can suffer no further injury.

QUESTION LXV. It is asked, since the glory of the soul is to contemplate God, and to enjoy the vision of him, and in him [0449D] to know all things — for *God will be all in all [n: (I Cor. XV)]*, being the mirror of every creature, just as every creature now is a mirror of God — since this, I say, is so, how will the glorification of the body be said to befit it, so that its beatitude is increased? Solution. It will know this glorification already in God himself, and the contemplation will be a part of its beatitude.

QUESTION LXVI. Since the Apostle says: *God is no respecter of persons [n: (Act. X)]*, it is asked what it is to accept persons. Does not the Apostle say: *To whom honor, honor; to whom fear, fear [n: (Rom. XIII)]*? Ought we not to show greater reverence to one than to another? Solution. He accepts persons who venerates a man on account of something that is the man's, and [0450A] prefers him to others — which God does not do. For when we show fear or honor to those who are established in dignity, we do not do this except on account of God, whose person they bear, or whose ministers they are.

QUESTION LXVII. *The doers of the law shall be justified.* It is asked whether the fulfilling of the law justifies, which seems so from these words, when a lawyer asked of the Lord: *What shall I do to possess eternal life?* The Lord answered: *What is written? How do you read? And he: Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart, and with thy whole soul, and with thy whole mind, and thy neighbor as thyself; and the Lord said to him: Do this, and thou shalt live [n: (Luc. X)]*. Behold, the commandment of the law, when observed, confers eternal life. Whence it is read that the law fulfilled justifies [n: (Rom. II)]. The same in another place, when the young man [0450B] says to him: *What shall I do to possess eternal life?* And the Lord to him: *Honor father and mother; thou shalt not kill, thou shalt not steal, thou shalt not commit adultery, thou shalt not bear false witness, thou shalt not covet thy neighbor's wife,* nor his property. And the *young man says: All these things* from *my* youth *have I kept.* And the Lord *looked upon him, and loved him [n: (Matth. XIX; Exod. XX; Marc. X; Luc. XVIII)]*: which he would not have done, unless he were just from the observance of the commandments of God. But that which is added: *If thou wilt be perfect [n: (Matth. XIX)]*, etc., pertains to the perfection of justice. Likewise Paul: *The commandment of the law, which was unto life, was found by me to be unto death [n: (Rom. VII)]*. Likewise Bede: The law, kept in its own time, conferred not only temporal goods, but also eternal; whence it is manifest that the law fulfilled [0450C] justifies. But the Apostle asserts that the law brought no one to perfection, saying: *by the works of the law shall no flesh be justified, and if justice is from the law, then Christ died in vain [n: (Galat. II)]*: and many other things in this manner. And therefore some say that those precepts whose fulfillment confers justice are the precepts of the Gospel, even though they are written in the law. For he who does them is at once made an evangelical man; nor is he a man of the law. These also say that those precepts, according as they are understood to be fulfilled in the law, do not justify immediately, but only make one fit to receive the faith of Christ, through which alone salvation is had: for they say that he who shows outwardly that he loves God and neighbor, even if inwardly he does not love in his heart, keeps the law so far as the law goes [0450D]; it restrains the hand indeed, and not the mind. To which solution it is objected thus: If by the observance of those precepts, made only outwardly, they become fit and worthy to receive the faith of Christ, faith is given not from grace, but from merits. Likewise, if they are fit, how do they know that they will receive it? Whence they do not seem suitably to resolve the foregoing question. For what is a greater perfection than to love God with the whole heart, and one's neighbor as oneself? And therefore we say that the fulfilling of the aforesaid precepts justifies immediately, yet not the law, which does not suffice but is weak without grace for its own fulfilling; nor does it give grace, nor does it openly show the faith of Christ, without which the precepts of the law [0451A] are not fulfilled: it shows indeed the homeland, but does not point out the way by which one must go, nor whither. Whence the Apostle: By the works of the law shall no flesh be justified.

But someone will say: This authority manifestly contradicts your solution. Are they not contrary — that there is justice by the fulfilling of the legal precepts, as you asserted above, and that no one is justified by works? Solution. The Apostle calls "works of the law" those which are done from fear alone, which the law instills, from which there is no justice. It is to be noted that "law" is said in several ways: for sometimes "law" is called the book of Moses, sometimes the ceremonies and legal observances, sometimes the ten precepts written on the two tablets, sometimes the book of psalms, sometimes also "law" is said [0451B] of any observance whatever, as when it is said: This is the law of this or that matter. Natural reason too is called law. In the New Testament also "law" is taken in several ways; whence the law of faith, the law of the spirit, the law of grace, the law of the flesh, the law of the members, the law of sin, the law of death.
