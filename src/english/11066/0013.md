## I. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE ROMANS. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CLXXXI. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked whether it is consequent that he does not work [it] simply, if he does not work [it] according to the mind; just as it is consequent that, if he does [it] according to the flesh, he also does [it] simply. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ It is indeed true that upon a determinate affirmation [0476B] there follows a simple affirmation almost always, but a simple negation does not usually follow a determinate negation; whence one must lay stress on the pronoun when it is said: *I do not work [it] [n: (ibid.)]*, as it were the inner I, in which the truth of the man consists, because [it is] the image of God; and thus the negation, which seems to be simple, is not simple, but rather determinate.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CLXXXII. ⟦s2⟧ *But the sin that dwells in me.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how sin, which is nothing, but only a privation of good, is said to dwell in someone. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ By this name 'sin' is signified the tinder of sin, which is called the law of the flesh, or the law of the members, just as consent or operation is called the law of death.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CLXXXIII. ⟦s2⟧ *I know that there dwells not in [0476C] me,* that is, *in my flesh, good.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether he says universally of all good, or indefinitely of a certain good, that it does not dwell in the flesh? ⟦s4⟧ But of all good how can he say this, since the flesh, even corrupted and subject to languor, is the work of God, nor deprived of all good, especially since evil is not a thing existing by itself, nor can it be except in a good thing. ⟦s5⟧ Whence it is consequent that where there is no good, neither is there any evil; and where there is any evil, there is necessarily also some good. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ He says of health, or of the integrity of nature, that it does not dwell in the flesh: whence he fittingly proves that sin is the tinder of sin, which is the privation of health, or of the integrity of nature; it dwells in the flesh: for where there is not health [0476D] and there ought to be, there is the privation of the same, which the Apostle signifies, saying: Good does not dwell in the flesh.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CLXXXIV. ⟦s2⟧ *To will lies near to me,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked concerning that will which the Apostle says lies near to him, what it is to be called? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Nothing other than the natural affection of the soul, which from creation is in the soul, and by which the soul naturally wills good; but this affection always lacks effect, unless it be helped by the grace of God.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CLXXXV. ⟦s2⟧ *To will lies near to me, but to accomplish I find not; since evil lies near to me.* ⟦s3⟧ By these words the Apostle seems to intimate that, if evil did not lie near, not only would 'to will' lie near to him, but he would also find 'to accomplish,' since nothing would impede toward [0477A] good, or impel toward evil. ⟦s4+5⟧ But in this very thing he intimates the first state of the first man before sin, to whom 'to will' good lay near, but evil did not lie near, whence he could not say, 'to will lies near to me, but to accomplish I find not, because evil lies near to me.' But whether that good, which he naturally willed, he could accomplish without the addition of greater grace, might be asked. ⟦s6⟧ Was not that willing, which Adam had before sin, much more intense than that natural willing which the pagans now have? but the pagans do great and many good things (even if they are not worthy of eternal life, because done without faith) from the natural affection which they have: how then could Adam, before sin, from a greater will, not advance even a little, as almost all assert? [0477B] Likewise, if God did not confer greater grace, but left [him] in that state in which he created him, could not God justly exact from man that, for the good already conferred, he should love him perfectly? but God could not justly exact what man could not render: and thus it seems that man before sin could advance from the good already received, which many deny.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CLXXXVI. ⟦s2⟧ But someone will say: Can not God now justly exact from man what man cannot render, as has already been said above? ⟦s3⟧ For unless God could justly exact that he covet nothing against reason, and that he love God with his whole heart, he would not have commanded man both. ⟦s4⟧ In a similar way it seems that man before sin could not render the whole which God could justly exact. [0477C] Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Man before sin was not made weak or impotent by any fault; therefore he could easily render what God could exact: after sin, and through sin, he was made such that he cannot pay every debt, which nevertheless God can exact, and does exact from those who presume upon themselves, and do not take refuge in grace. ⟦s6⟧ It is to be known that this text, from that place: I am carnal, up to that: *therefore there is now no condemnation [n: (Rom. VIII)]*, etc., is wont to be read both of the man of the law and of the man of grace; and how it ought, or can, be expounded of the man of grace has been said according to Augustine. ⟦s7⟧ But how it is to be read of the man of the law, they will be loved, is to be considered. ⟦s8⟧ Now he is called the man of the law who, instructed by the law, knows through sin; to which, unwilling to resist, [0477D] he is conquered and succumbs, and consents to the tinder — who is called carnal, not only feeling the tinder, but even consenting to it. ⟦s9⟧ Since, therefore, consent is of reason.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CLXXXVII. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked how it befits the man of the law: *I will the good, I hate the evil, now* I myself do not work *it, and I consent to the law of God.* ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Although consent is of reason, yet because carnality drew it to consent, it is ascribed not to reason but to the flesh.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CLXXXVIII. ⟦s2⟧ *That the justification of the law might be fulfilled in us [n: (Rom. VIII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what he calls the justification of the law, since from the law there is no justice. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He calls the justification of the law not that which the law conferred, [0478A] but that which the law commanded and promised, which is fulfilled in us through the grace of Christ, who fulfilled the law fully and perfectly, and gave us the grace of fulfilling [it], and what we do too little: he himself supplies, and answers for us.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CLXXXIX. ⟦s2⟧ Whether it is the same thing to walk according to the flesh, and to be according to the flesh, and to savor the things that are of the flesh. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ It is not the same. ⟦s5⟧ For to walk according to the flesh is to fulfill in deed the things which the flesh covets. ⟦s6⟧ To be according to the flesh is to consent to the concupiscences, or to be disposed toward carnal things. ⟦s7⟧ To savor the things that are of the flesh is to take delight in such things as the flesh judges highest, or not to perceive the things that are of God. ⟦s8⟧ For in two ways is one called carnal, namely by life and by doctrine: either who [0478B] indulges the flesh, or who binds the divine power to the natures of things, that is, who believes that God can do nothing except what he sees in the nature of things. ⟦s9⟧ Similarly, to walk according to the spirit is to fulfill in deed the things that are of the spirit. ⟦s10⟧ To be according to the spirit is to consent to the spirit, or to be disposed according to spiritual things. ⟦s11⟧ To perceive the things that are of the spirit is to take delight in spiritual things, according to which three [things] one is called truly spiritual. ⟦s12⟧ One is also called spiritual through understanding; and there is one spiritual by life, and not by understanding; another by understanding, and not by life; another in both ways; another in neither, etc.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CXC. ⟦s2⟧ *But you are not in the flesh,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how the Apostle says that they are not in the flesh, since he wrote to them to repress their proud contention. [0478C] Solution. ⟦s4+5⟧ He calls them in the spirit and not in the flesh, because they did not walk according to the flesh, or, what is better, among the Romans there were certain spiritual ones, and of perfect faith: on account of which he says: You are not in the flesh. ⟦s6⟧ And there were others contending among themselves, and of imperfect faith, looking to whom he subjoins: *If yet the Spirit of God dwells in you [n: (ibid.)]*, whom he exhorts to perfection.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CXCI. ⟦s2⟧ *But if Christ is in you.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what it is for Christ to be in someone, or the spirit of Christ? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Augustine says: Christ in a man is faith in the heart. ⟦s6⟧ But according to this, in whomever [there is] faith [there is] also Christ; but in the evil [man] there is faith, therefore also Christ, or the Spirit of Christ is in the evil [man]. ⟦s7⟧ For it is the same [thing], for Christ, or the spirit of Christ, to be [0478D] in someone; but the spirit of Christ is not in anyone except in whom there is love. ⟦s8⟧ Solution. ⟦s9⟧ Not everyone in whom there is faith has faith in the heart; for faith is not said to be in the heart, except when it sits in the heart and pleases [it], that is, except where it works through love; and according to this it is the same [thing], for Christ to be in someone, and for the faith of Christ to be in his heart.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CXCII. ⟦s2⟧ *The body indeed is dead on account of sin.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked on account of what sin the body is dead, that is, subject to the necessity of dying. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ On account of the guilt of original fault: but since such — nay, every — fault is forgiven in baptism, it is asked why such a penalty, inflicted for such a fault, is not taken away. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ Although the fault for which such a penalty [0479A] is inflicted is forgiven, yet such an infirmity remains, lest men should hasten to the reception of that sacrament more for the advantage of temporal benefit than for the faith and love of the future life, and thus it would not profit the soul to be reborn in Christ. ⟦s8⟧ Or *the body is dead on account of sin [n: (Rom. VIII)]* to be avoided. ⟦s9⟧ For the human spirit is such that, unless it were liable to infirmities, it would be exalted beyond measure, and thus would not be saved — which cannot come about without humility. ⟦s10⟧ Not cruelly, therefore, but mercifully; nor from impotence, but from a great dispensation, is our mortality, with the other penalties, not taken away in baptism.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CXCIII. ⟦s2⟧ *For you have not received the spirit of servitude,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether the spirit of fear [0479B] and the spirit of adoption are the same? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The same Spirit, on account of various effects, is called by different names. ⟦s6⟧ Note that the gift and the author of the gift are called by the same name.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CXCIV. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked how, speaking to the Romans, he says: You have not again received the spirit of servitude, again in fear, etc. ⟦s3⟧ For the spirit of servitude was not before given to them as to the Jews. ⟦s4+5⟧ Solution. 'Again' does not denote an iteration made in the same persons, but in different ones, as if he said: To the Jews indeed, in the giving of the law, was given the spirit of servitude in fear, and again *to you is given a spirit not of fear, but of adoption.* ⟦s6⟧ Fear is of one kind worldly, of another servile, of another initial, of another filial. ⟦s7⟧ Worldly fear is, according to some, when we forsake good or do evil, yet keeping the will [0479C] good, on account of the shame of others, lest we be held cheap — which, according to the same [authors], is also called human. ⟦s8⟧ But according to Cassiodorus, worldly fear is when we fear the perils of the flesh, or to lose the goods of the world, on account of which we transgress; and this fear is evil, and in the first degree is forsaken along with the world: which the Lord forbids, saying: *Fear not those who kill the body [n: (Matth. X)]*. ⟦s9⟧ Servile [fear], according to the earlier [authors], is that which restrains the hand from an evil work, the evil will being kept.
