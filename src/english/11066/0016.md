## I. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE ROMANS. (cont.)

QUESTION CCXX. Whether the number of the predestined can be increased or diminished? The same is asked concerning the number of the reprobate. That it can, they seek to prove thus. God can not apply grace to him to whom he applies it, and if he did this, the predestined would be damned; and he can apply it to him to whom he does not apply it, and if he did this, the reprobate would be saved. And thus he who is predestined can be damned, and he who is reprobate can be saved: and someone can pass from the number of the predestined to the number of the reprobate, and conversely. Solution. This they resolve after the likeness of the aforesaid, by conjunction and disjunction. But we say that it is possible according to lower causes, [and] perhaps impossible [0486D] according to higher causes. Likewise it does not seem to follow, God can save or damn this man, therefore this man can be saved or damned. For our power does not follow from God's power, as [it would] if God could redeem the human race otherwise, that therefore the human race could be redeemed otherwise than through the death of the Son of God: and if God had this in his power, that man therefore had in his power to be saved otherwise. Cannot God, if he willed, save Judas? Can Judas therefore be saved? It is to be noted that "lower causes" are called those which God implanted in creatures at the first constitution of things, according to which like are born of like, as from such a grain such a tree or fruit proceeds. But "higher causes" are called the divine power, will, disposition, and predestination: [0487A] which sometimes work with the lower, sometimes without them: which always have their effect, but the lower not always; rather they are sometimes impeded by the higher.

QUESTION CCXXI. *To be made conformable to the image of his Son,* etc. It is asked why the Son is called the image of the Father. Solution. Because he is most like him, and because he is of the same essence.

QUESTION CCXXII. But since the Holy Spirit is equally like the Father, being of the same substance, why is the Son and not the Holy Spirit called the image of the Father? Solution. Because image pertains more to the property of generation than of procession: for those things which are generated are wont to be more alike than those which proceed.

[0487B]QUESTION CCXXIII. Whether there are two images in the Son, because he is the uncreated image insofar as he is God, and the created image insofar as he is man. Solution. Not for that reason two images, because in Christ the uncreated image and the created image are not two images, but one: just as he is the Son of God and the son of man; yet not two sons, but one Son of God and son of man.

QUESTION CCXXIV. *Whom he called, them he also justified,* etc. It is asked how the Apostle distinguishes between calling and justifying, since in calling he justifies. Solution. By calling he understands the infusion of charity; by justification the grace that follows and preserves.

QUESTION CCXXV. *Whom he predestinated, [them] he also called,* etc. It is asked whether all who are called are predestined, [0487C] which seems [so], because calling is the effect of predestination. But some are called who are not chosen, because it is written: *Many are called, few chosen [n: (Matth. XXII)]*; but only the chosen are predestined; therefore not all who are called are predestined. Solution. Calling is of one kind common, which is made outwardly by preaching, and also sometimes inwardly by inspiration: of another kind special, which is made according to purpose, according to which none is called except the chosen and predestined. But according to the common calling it was said: Many are called, but few chosen.

QUESTION CCXXVI. *Who shall accuse against the elect?* None, because what God does not will, another cannot [do]. But it is asked: what is it for God to accuse someone? Solution. For God to accuse is to permit [0487D] a man to fall into sin. But for the devil to accuse is to cast [him] into sin through temptation, and by casting [him down] to make him liable to accusation.

QUESTION CCXXVII. It is asked how Christ intercedes for us. Whether by voice, or only by mind and desire? or in some other way? Solution. For Christ to intercede for us is, by the merits of his obedience shown to God the Father in his humanity, to reconcile us to him, [we] adhering [to him] by faith and love.

QUESTION CCXXVIII. It is also asked whether the saints, whose patronage we ask, intercede for us, and how? Solution. For the saints to intercede for us is nothing other than that God, for their merits, [0488A] rewards the good affections which we have toward them for God's sake; and therefore it makes no difference whether they hear us or do not hear us.

QUESTION CCXXIX. *Who shall separate us from the charity of God,* etc. It is asked whether the Apostle was of such great perfection that he could not sin mortally; for if he could not be separated from God, neither could he sin mortally: for this is to be separated. But since he says elsewhere: *Lest the greatness of the revelations should exalt me, there was given me a sting of my flesh [n: (II Cor. 12)]*, he intimates that if he could be exalted, he could also sin mortally, and thus be separated. Solution. No other than himself could separate the Apostle; yet he could separate himself, because in this he merited, that he could transgress, and did not transgress. For when he confessed Christ before Nero, then he could deny him, if [0488B] he willed: what therefore neither life nor death could [do], he himself could.

QUESTION CCXXX. It is asked how the cause makes the martyr, and not the punishment, since none is a martyr without punishment, just as neither without a cause. For as punishment without a cause does not suffice for the crown of martyrdom, so neither the cause without punishment. Solution. That which is said, the cause makes the martyr and not the punishment, is namely to be understood as "alone." But someone will say that by the same reasoning it can be said, punishment makes the martyr, not the cause, so that it be understood as "alone." Response. The cause without punishment profits; punishment without a cause not only does not profit, but harms; and that punishment profits, when it profits, this it has from the cause; therefore this is said rather than that.

[0488C]QUESTION CCXXXI. *Great is my sadness, [and] continual sorrow to my heart [n: (Rom. IX)]*. It is asked why the Apostle says that he grieves, that formerly, erring with those who erred, he persecuted the Church: for since great advantage came from that persecution, he ought not to grieve over it, but to rejoice? Solution. The Apostle did not grieve over the good which followed from his evil, but over the sin which he himself committed, over which it is always good to grieve.

QUESTION CCXXXII. *I myself wished to be anathema from Christ for my brethren.* It is asked how the Apostle wished to be separated from Christ, whether according to glory, or according to justice. If according to glory, he seems to have loved his brethren more than God, which is in no way to be done; if he wished to be separated according to [0488D] justice, this he could not [do] without sin and without offense against God, which cannot reasonably be wished by anyone. Solution. He did not wish to be separated from Christ in this way or that, but by these words he shows his wonderful affection which he had toward the Jews, which kind of expression Moses used, saying: *Either blot me out of the book of life, or forgive them this offense [n: (Exod. XXXII)]*. Or it can be said that each, both Moses and the Apostle, preferred in his desire and wish the salvation of so great a multitude to his own salvation, nor in this did they set the love of God after; rather, preferring the glory and honor of God to their own salvation, [they were] willing rather that the glory of God be magnified in so many saved, than [0489A] diminished in one saved; and this perfection exceeds every perfection, because a greater cannot be conceived.

QUESTION CCXXXIII. *What then shall we say? Is there injustice with God?* It is asked whether God is unjust in reprobating, and in hardening, and finally in damning this man who cannot act well without the grace of predestination, especially since he cannot merit that predestination itself? Solution. There is assuredly no injustice with God. But that it may be plain that there is no injustice with God, it must be seen what predestination is and what its effect, and what reprobation and its effect. Predestination is the preparation of grace: by the name of grace are here signified the gratuitous goods by which in the present we are justified, or in the future [0489B] are crowned; but what "preparation" signifies must be seen. It was said above that the divine essence, though it is one and simple, obtains diverse names on account of the various effects of things: of which one is predestination. Preparation therefore is nothing other than God himself preparing, and discerning, and appointing, and purposing, or choosing in himself, that he confer these gifts on these, or those in time; and this purpose or this preparation is the cause of the future goods by which we are adopted as sons of God. Behold, it has been said what predestination is, and what its effect, namely calling, justification, and glorification. But is reprobation something that was from eternity in God, or God himself, that it be the cause of future evils just as predestination [is the cause] of goods? Which [0489C] if it be conceded, it follows that God is thus the cause and author of evils. Whence it must be known that reprobation does not posit anything; because for God to reprobate someone is nothing other than not to choose, and not to predestine, [and] not to prepare gratuitous goods. Whose effect is to harden: which is nothing other than not to apply grace; such as the cause is, such is the effect. But there is no injustice or unrighteousness, if God does not give something to him to whom he owes nothing. God is therefore not unjust in that he reprobates or hardens someone.

QUESTION CCXXXIV. But it is asked why God did not reprobate all, or why he did not predestine all; but predestined some and reprobated some. Solution. If he predestined all, the divine justice would lie hidden, [0489D] because it would not be known what was justly owed to fault; if he reprobated all, the goodness of God would not appear. But the wisdom of God judged it better that there be good and evil, than only good, although it itself made nothing but good.
