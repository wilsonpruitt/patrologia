## I. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE ROMANS. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXXXV. ⟦s2⟧ But it is asked why he chose Jacob rather than Esau; likewise the same can be asked concerning any elect and reprobate whatever. ⟦s3⟧ For it cannot be said that one was chosen and the other reprobated on account of future good or evil merits: for thus that which is temporal would be the cause of that which is eternal. ⟦s4⟧ Likewise, if it be said that Jacob was chosen by grace alone, [and] Esau reprobated on account of original sin, it is asked why on account of the same sin Jacob was not reprobated, or why Esau was not chosen out of the same [0490A] grace? ⟦s5⟧ Solution — if indeed it ought to be called a solution to show that something is insoluble. ⟦s6⟧ They say it was so done because God willed it to be done; but if it be asked why he so willed, the question is foolish, because the cause of the divine will is sought, of which there is none; rather it itself is the first and principal cause of all things. ⟦s7⟧ But blessed Jerome says that God did nothing because he wills, but because there is a reason for it to be so done. ⟦s8⟧ And therefore it can not incongruously thus he asks why he willed this, and be answered: Because the judgments of God are a great deep. [n: (Psal. XXXV)]. ⟦s9⟧ Yet we can say, saving the reverence due to secret things and without the arrogance of assertion, that he chose Jacob rather than Esau for this reason: because he foreknew that greater benefit would come to the good of the universe from the election of Jacob than of Esau. ⟦s10⟧ But someone will say: Therefore [0490B] that which is temporal is the cause of that which is eternal, namely the good which he foresaw would then be future is the cause of predestination. ⟦s11⟧ To which we say that this is not true; yet the cause looks to that, namely the eternal reason: for that he should choose that from which greater benefit would come to the good of the universe — this reason too was eternal, which looks to that temporal good that would be future from the election of Jacob.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXXXVI. ⟦s2⟧ But again it is asked why greater benefit came from the election of Jacob than of Esau? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Because grace is more commended by the election of Jacob than of Esau. ⟦s5⟧ Likewise it is asked why it is more commended by the election of Jacob than of Esau. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ Because he was younger in birth. ⟦s8⟧ For if the elder-born were chosen, it would seem that this [0490C] was done by the privilege of birth.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXXXVII. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked why Jacob was not the firstborn and Esau the later-born, so that thus at least Esau might be chosen? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ This is to ask why Jacob is not Esau, and conversely: and therefore it is a question without reason.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXXXVIII. ⟦s2⟧ From those things which come to pass in time, the same difficulty arises: for it is wont to be asked why God confers grace on this man rather than on that, since they are indifferent, and neither can merit grace, without which he cannot be saved. ⟦s3⟧ For it seems that it is not to be imputed to me if I do not do what I cannot do without grace, since grace has not been conferred on me, but rather to him who does not confer on me the necessary [0490D] grace, since he cannot [do so] without harm to himself. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Concerning grace different men think differently. ⟦s6⟧ Some said that God cannot do [anything] except what he does: which is certainly not true. ⟦s7⟧ Others say: There is a certain seed-bed of virtue (which they call the root of charity) in this man, from which he is apt, so that by grace conferred on him he may merit eternal life: which because it is not in that man, he can merit neither charity nor eternal life. ⟦s8⟧ But because this is against the Church, let it be wholly passed over. ⟦s9⟧ Others say that God offers his grace to all in common, which he who lays hold of will be saved; he who does not lay hold of will be damned, as if someone had prepared good food for you in a tower, and said: Come up to take the food: but because you cannot go up without a ladder or some help of this [0491A] kind, therefore they say: There is need of another grace, that you may ascend to that higher one. ⟦s10⟧ Likewise for laying hold of that, another, and for that another, and so on to infinity. ⟦s11⟧ But these attribute the whole to grace, and leave nothing to merit. ⟦s12⟧ There are others who say that grace is offered, even by being held out to the man, that he himself may lay hold of it, without which, once laid hold of, he cannot be raised up, just as if a rope be let down to one who is in a pit, without which he cannot get out of the pit; but if he stretch out his hand to the rope and lay hold, he is drawn out, and something is from the man, although merit cannot exist without grace. ⟦s13⟧ But because this opinion divides between merit and grace, [holding] that something good is there from the man, at least that he raises his hand and lays hold of the rope, which because it [0491B] cannot be done without grace, this opinion too, together with the aforesaid, is to be guarded against. ⟦s14⟧ But it can be said that the grace of God is offered equally to the good and to the evil, that is, to the predestined and to the reprobate, which yet not every one lays hold of or follows as it draws; rather he, into whom the ray of grace is poured, closes his eyes; and thus repels the ray by which he is touched, whence grace is deservedly withdrawn from him, because he withdraws himself. ⟦s15⟧ For it is in grace as in the ray of the sun, which presents itself to the eye, by which the eye, being touched, exercises sight. ⟦s16⟧ For the eye is of such a nature that through it sight is exercised, if it be struck by the ray of the sun, without which there is no vision in the eye: yet unless it were of such a nature, even when touched by the ray it would not see, as a wall or a stone does not see, even if it be flooded with the ray of the sun; so the soul [0491C] has naturally the power of meriting, which yet it cannot exercise unless it be flooded with the splendor of grace. ⟦s17⟧ But when it is touched, it is moved and merits, whence the whole is from grace, yet in such a way that merit is not excluded, just as if a boy who cannot yet walk be led by someone, and does indeed walk, which yet he could not [do] by himself, nor even with another leading, unless he had the natural power of walking: yet the whole is said to be from the one leading, that he walks; so for this, that the soul may merit, two things are required, both grace and natural power: yet the whole authorship of meriting belongs to grace alone, because natural power posits nothing without grace.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXXXIX. ⟦s2⟧ *For to this same purpose have I raised [0491D] thee, that I may show in thee my power,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether God inclines the mind of a man to this, that the man be made worse? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Pharaoh, in that blinding, was not made worse than he was before; but through the signs set forth outwardly, the malice which had before been conceived in the mind was stirred up, and broke forth into deed, just as the envy of the Jews broke forth when the Lord entered Jerusalem with such great glory, and brought to ripeness the crime already conceived. ⟦s6⟧ Or for God to stir up is nothing other than to permit [one] to be cast headlong by a just judgment: concerning this question it has been said more carefully in the foregoing.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXL. ⟦s2⟧ *On whom he will he has mercy, and whom he will he hardens.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether the will of God, as it is the cause of mercy, is likewise the cause of hardening: which seems [so], [0492A] since the Apostle says: On whom he will, etc. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The will of God is not the cause except of that which is something; hardening does not posit something, but rather removes [it]; yet [the Apostle says this] so that he may show that God can justly give to whom he will, and not give to whom he wills not to give: for God does not act by necessity, but by will alone. ⟦s6⟧ But that it is the cause of good is certain, whence the Apostle above: *It is not of him that wills, nor of him that runs, but of God that shows mercy [n: (ibid.)]*.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXLI. ⟦s2⟧ But it can be asked how willing is not of him that wills, since no one can [will] this without will. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ The good will is both man's, but as of one receiving; and God's, but as of one giving and [as] author.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXLII. ⟦s2⟧ *Has not the potter power [0492B] over the clay,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked: to what end this likeness is brought in; does God form *one vessel unto honor, another unto dishonor?* ⟦s4⟧ Or does the dishonorable have its being from God? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ That one vessel is unto honor is from God; but that it is dishonorable is from the vessel itself. ⟦s7⟧ Yet the likeness is good, because just as the potter makes one vessel unto honor, another unto dishonor: so God is said to form one vessel unto honor, another unto dishonor; or, what is better, God is said to form one unto honor and another unto dishonor for this reason: because, God being author, even those things which are vessels of wrath or of dishonor have their being, yet the vessels of dishonor do not have their being from God, but from their own vice.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXLIII. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked what it is to be written in the book [0492C] of life according to foreknowledge. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ To be foreknown and foreordained to life, just as to be written according to justice is to be in a state in which, if he departed from this life, he would be saved. ⟦s5⟧ There are therefore some written according to foreknowledge and not according to justice, as those who do not yet have virtue, yet are foreordained to life. ⟦s6⟧ But some are written according to justice and not according to foreknowledge, as those who have charity, yet are foreseen unto death. ⟦s7⟧ But some are written according to foreknowledge and according to justice, as those who have charity and are predestined to life. ⟦s8⟧ But some neither according to foreknowledge nor according to justice, as those who were never good nor [0492D] are predestined. ⟦s9⟧ But those things which are there written according to justice and not according to foreknowledge are said to be blotted out thence; but those which are written there according to foreknowledge are never blotted out thence.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXLIV. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked whether those who are written in the book of life according to merit, and not according to foreknowledge, are written by disposition. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ It can be said that they are written by disposition on account of merit, yet not simply according to disposition; because if this were so, they could not be blotted out thence; with such a qualification it can be said [that they are written] by disposition on account of merit, [in the sense] that God disposed [it] as [he does] every good.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXLV. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked how that is to be understood which is said concerning this authority: *The elder shall serve [0493A] the younger [n: (Gen. XXV)]*, this is [said] concerning foreknowledge. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ The sense is: by this Scripture it is shown that God was foreknowing of future things.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXLVI. ⟦s2⟧ *But the justice which is of faith,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what it is for justice to be of faith. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ This is for justice to be through grace, because not only does one come to faith from grace, but also after faith grace is necessary, that faith may be fulfilled by good works, whose fulfillment is called justice.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCXLVII. ⟦s2⟧ Likewise it is asked how that is to be understood which is said concerning this authority, *Jacob I have loved, Esau I have hated [n: (Malach. I)]*, this is [said] concerning judgment. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ By this prophecy it is shown to have been fulfilled, what was in God's foreknowledge. ⟦s5⟧ Note, [0493B] that this authority, Jacob I have loved, Esau I have hated, can be expounded concerning the eternal predestination of the one, and the reprobation of the other, or concerning the temporal application of grace, or the withdrawal of the same: whence that gloss, In Jacob he found nothing to be loved except the gift of his mercy, is thus understood, that is, out of grace alone God disposed to confer on Jacob grace in time whereby he would be saved. ⟦s6⟧ And that gloss: In Esau nothing to be hated [sic: *odiendium*] except original sin, that is, he foreknew that on account of original sin grace was not to be conferred on Esau, by which he would be saved in time.
