## I. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE ROMANS. (cont.)

QUESTION CCXLVIII. Someone says: Why on account of original sin was grace not conferred on Esau, since it was conferred on Jacob, although he had original sin? [0493C] Solution. Because the same grace was assuredly offered, but Esau withdrew himself from grace and closed his eye, while in Jacob grace blotted out sin, because he yielded to grace: he followed it as it drew, God working and disposing all things according to the judgment of his most just will.

QUESTION CCXLIX. *That he might show the riches of [his] glory,* etc. It is asked whether the torments of the evil profit the good in the future life? Solution. They say that they profit, because God would never allow evils to be, unless they conferred some benefit on the good of the universe.

QUESTION CCL. It is asked what benefit comes to the good from this, that they see the evil punished. Do they therefore love or praise God the more; or do they take delight in the punishment of the evil? Solution. They take delight not in the punishment of the evil, but in the justice of God; and their own [0493D] grace shines forth the more by comparison with the evil, especially when they see themselves freed from the same punishments by mercy alone. But someone will say: Although the evil were not punished, would not the good know that they are freed from the same punishments as now? Solution. Perhaps not so effectively, and in this very thing they would love less. Likewise, if none were to be damned, would not God requite the good according to their merits? And thus it seems, although all were saved, it would be no less good than now — rather it would be more good; because the good would rejoice more over their salvation then, than they now do over their damnation. Likewise the good in the future either will have compassion on the evil, or will not have compassion; if they have compassion, how [are they] blessed: if they do not have compassion, [they are] cruel. Solution. [0494A] Augustine resolves this question thus: It was more good that there be goods and evils, than only goods, so that God might be praised more wonderfully from that very diversity.

QUESTION CCLI. *Following after the law of justice,* etc. Since the law does not justify, it is asked how it is called the law of justice. Solution. Because it is a certain preparation for justice, therefore it is called the law of justice.

QUESTION CCLII. *They stumbled at the stone of stumbling* — concerning which, because the Lord [says] in the Gospel: *Whoever shall fall upon this stone shall be broken; but upon whomever it shall fall, it will grind him to powder [n: (Matth. XXI)]*, it is asked what it is to fall upon the stone. Solution. To offend God imprudently, just as to be ground by the stone [is] to be eternally punished by Christ: whence Christ is called the rock of scandal and the stone of stumbling, [0494B] because [being] humble, [he was] therefore apt for the proud to stumble against.

QUESTION CCLIII. *I bear witness to them, that they have a zeal, or emulation, of God,* etc. [n: (Rom. X.)] It is asked whether that zeal is good, and what it is, and what its effect? That it is good is clear from this, that the Apostle says this to the commendation of the Jews, and the expositors also say that it is the love of God: but if this were so, they would seem to have something that were worthy of eternal life; but were they not worthy of death, who killed Christ? How then did they have the zeal of God, that is, love? Likewise, if it was good, it had a good effect, but was not its effect that which they did out of that zeal? But this was mortal [0494C] sin, namely the persecution of the martyrs and the killing of Christ: how then did the good produce evil? Solution. That good zeal was an affection toward God, by which they were ready to do what their conscience dictated must be done for God's sake: yet it was not so great as to make them worthy of eternal life, nor ought it to be called charity, nor simply the love of God: yet if it is sometimes called the love of God, this is for the reason that they had it toward God: but its effect was not the persecution of the martyrs, or the death of Christ, but the avoidance of contempt of God. For they were placed in such a strait that whether they killed Christ or not, they would sin mortally: yet not [that], if they did not kill Christ, they would sin in this very thing; but because by not killing [him] they would despise God: for often by one [0494D] sin another is avoided.

QUESTION CCXLIV. Likewise, by killing Christ they were not acting against conscience; rather [they did] that which they believed must be done for God's sake: how then did they sin? Solution. Although they were not acting against conscience, yet they sinned mortally, because they were blinded: for they did what their conscience ought to have dictated to them must not be done.

QUESTION CCLV. *Wishing to establish their own justice,* etc. It is asked how the justice of the law is called the Jews'. Solution. Because in this, that they believed they fulfilled it by their own powers, they made [it] their own justice, but not according to knowledge, that is, they did not exercise that good affection as it [0495A] was to be exercised by the judgment of reason, and in this they erred.

QUESTION CCLVI. *Christ is the end of the law.* It is asked how Christ is the end and consummation of the law, since the justice of the law is without assisting grace, and they had no merit with God. Solution. Christ is not called the end or consummation of the law according to that by which it was kept by the Jews, but because he spiritually fulfills it in himself and in his own.

QUESTION CCLVII. *But the justice which is of faith speaks thus: Say not,* etc. *In thy heart, Who shall ascend into heaven?* etc. It is asked how the Apostle brought in this authority: for it is taken from Deuteronomy, where Moses uses it in another sense: for he forbade the Jews to say, Who has ascended into heaven, [0495B] to bring us the law, or who has crossed the sea to carry the law over, or who has descended into hell to bring it forth to us, because the word is near, in thy heart, that is, to have the law in the present. Solution. It is fittingly brought in, although the Apostle does not here express through it the literal sense of Moses: for it can come to pass that, just as to the Jews that was literally a sin, so in it there is figuratively a precept to us, that we not ask, Who has ascended, etc.; whence the Apostle brings in the words of Moses under such a sense, [and] fittingly also brings in Moses as speaking about it.

QUESTION CCLVIII. But someone will say: If Moses speaks of the justice of faith in the law, it seems that the justice of faith was in the law, and thus the law truly justified. Solution. It does not follow, if in the law there is speech about the justice [0495C] of faith, that it was therefore in the law, nor is it even to be admitted that Moses speaks of the justice of faith, unless "to speak" be taken for "to signify." For by saying this he figures the justice of faith [as] near, that is, not far from the nature of minds; because, as the gloss says, it is agreeable to reason to believe.

QUESTION CCLIX. *The word is near, in thy mouth and in thy heart.* It is asked how this is true. Do we not believe many things which reason did not provide for, whence it is written: *Faith has no merit, for which human reason furnishes proof.* Solution. If anyone consider how all things concerning Christ were foretold beforehand in the law and the prophets, and how, [being] fulfilled, they were approved by signs and prodigies; how true, how holy and pious, and how powerful in deed and word [0495D] he himself was in his own person, what remains, except that he proclaim: *Thy testimonies are become exceedingly credible? [n: (Psal. XCII.)]* And reason is in no way permitted to doubt even a little: or [it is] for this reason called agreeable to reason; because to speak and to believe concerning that which pertains to the salvation of the soul pleases reason itself, rather it desires this above all things.

QUESTION CCLX. *With the heart one believes unto justice.* The gloss says that a man can [do] the rest unwilling; but he cannot believe except willing. But how is this true? Can no one hope and love except willing? How then does it say, a man can [do] the rest even unwilling, since these are other than to believe? Solution. By "the rest" it understands external things. But again it seems [0496A] false that anyone does external things unwilling: for if he in no way willed, how would he do [them]? Solution. "To will" is put for "to approve": for we often do many things which we do not approve.

QUESTION CCLXI. *For everyone, whosoever shall call upon.* It is asked how this is true, since many call upon, and yet are not saved, whence the Lord says: *Not everyone who says, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven [n: (Matth. VII)]*. Is not to say this, to call upon the name of the Lord? Solution. To call upon is to call inwardly, that is, unto the honor of God and for God's sake, or to desire knowledge [of him], which is not done without faith, hope, and charity.

QUESTION CCLXII. *They cannot believe, because Isaiah had foretold,* etc. It is asked concerning this prophecy, whether [0496B] the prophet willed that what he said be fulfilled? For if he willed [it], he seems to have willed that the blinding of the Jews come to pass: for in the blinding of the Jews the prophecy was fulfilled. Solution. It is not to be conceded that he willed the Jews to be blinded, although he willed his prophecy to be fulfilled, just as Christ willed to be true what he said to Peter: *Before the cock crow, thou shalt deny me thrice [n: (Matth. XXVI)]*: yet he did not will Peter to deny [him]: for many things are so joined that one cannot come to pass without the other, yet we can will one without the other.

QUESTION CCLXIII. *Lord, who has believed our report,* etc. It is read in the Gospel concerning the Jews, who *believed not, that the word of Isaiah might be fulfilled [n: (Joan. XII)]*: whence they object thus: What fault of the Jews [is it], that they [0496C] believed not, since it was necessary that the prophecy be fulfilled, and thus it was necessary that they not believe? Solution. God foretold through the prophet the sins of the Jews, but did not do [them]: for the foreknowledge of them does not impose necessity on them, according to that [saying]: so that when it is said, they believed not, that the word, etc., it only notes that that prophecy was fulfilled in the blindness of the Jews, and that it was foretold before [it was] fulfilled.

QUESTION CCLXIV. It is asked how the Jews sinned by not believing in Christ, since it is written: *Therefore they could believe* not because *Isaiah said: God has blinded their eyes, and hardened [their] heart [n: (ibid.)]*, or if they sinned by not believing in Christ, therefore they could believe, and did not believe: and if this is so, [0496D] how is this true: Therefore they could not believe because God blinded [them], etc. How [is it] at once true [that] they could believe and could not believe? Likewise he seems to place the cause of their unbelief with God, saying: Therefore they could not, because Isaiah said: God blinded [their] eyes, etc. Solution. It is true that they could not believe, as the Gospel says, and yet by not believing they sinned; because they came to that impossibility from the vice of their own will, and therefore such impossibility has no excuse. Both therefore are true, that they could not believe and that they could believe, but not in the same respect: they could [believe] by nature, and could not by fault: for they were so made, that they had the possibility not only of believing, but even of seeing God [0497A] face to face: and therefore it is said that they could believe by nature; but because through fault they were made such, that it was just with God not to have mercy on them, therefore it is said that by fault they could not believe. A like expression is had concerning the good angels: [namely] that they are mutable by nature, immutable by grace; but this which is said, God blinded [them], etc., is to be understood thus, that is, he permitted [them] to be blinded, not by imparting malice by which they would be made worse, but by not conferring grace by which they would be made better.
