## I. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE ROMANS. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is to be noted [regarding] that which is said, They could not believe, because they were unwilling: this exposition seems to be too general; for the same can be said of all who are in sins unto death, whence according to this exposition nothing special is said of the blinded. [0497B] Solution. ⟦s2⟧ It can be said that the blinded merit that they be unwilling to believe, and that God not have mercy on them: which not all who sin mortally do. ⟦s3⟧ Likewise that which is added: *While [they are] proud,* etc., seems to be said too generally and simply: for this too can be said generally of all. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Because "while" too is to be taken causally: for through pride and the other vices they merit that it be unjust for God to have mercy on them.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6+s7⟧ QUESTION CCLXV. It is asked how this prophecy is brought in: was he not speaking to those who were of the present state, and yet the prophecy was not yet fulfilled, and if this is so, how by this authority did he reprove those who then were, concerning that which was still future, how did it pertain to those then present? [0497C] For this seems to be the force of the proof, that all who were in the world heard the word of Christ, and therefore are to be reproved, as hearing and not believing. Likewise, if the prophecy was not yet fulfilled, how can he use a verb of past time in the sense of present time? for if it be taken in the sense of future time, the Apostle's proof seems to be ridiculous. Solution. He was speaking to those present, but not on account of those present only; rather he brought in this prophecy also on account of all of the future state, and he well reproves those present of that time, because although in their time the prophecy was not fulfilled, yet there was begun in them what the prophecy had foretold would be future. Note that certain Scriptures seem to mean that Israel [0497D] did not know [him]; others seem to mean that it knew Christ, which seem to be contrary: but they are not, because they are understood of different [persons]. For in that people there were some knowing, others not knowing.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXVI. ⟦s2⟧ *But if of grace, then not now of works,* etc. [n: (Rom XI)]. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked, if the whole is of grace, what [is] of merits, or if [there is] something of merits, how [is] the whole of grace? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The whole is of grace, although something is of merits, because the merits themselves are of grace. ⟦s6⟧ Yet it seems that justice is from preceding merits, as in Cornelius, whose prayer obtained this, that he be converted by the preaching of Peter, [and] that he receive faith: which faith is of grace, wherefore grace was obtained from works. ⟦s7⟧ Solution. ⟦s8⟧ It is not to be said [0498A] that grace or justice is from works; because this would be, that someone be justified from works without grace: which cannot be. ⟦s9⟧ But that Cornelius prayed, that he might receive faith, this was from preceding grace: for which also a greater grace, namely justifying grace, was conferred. ⟦s10⟧ For he was not without all grace, when he believed in one God and, as the doctors say, had the faith of the incarnation, but did not yet know the Word of God [to be] incarnate, which afterward he learned by the preaching of Peter. ⟦s11⟧ Note that compunction is of one kind, of envy, by which one is pricked and grieves, and pines away at another's goods; of another kind, of fault, by which one is tormented in his own conscience, as Judas, who hanged himself with a noose for his crime. ⟦s12⟧ Of another kind is the compunction of grace, which is either of beginners, who repent of evils, [0498B] that they may abstain, or of the perfect, who fear God out of love.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXVII. ⟦s2⟧ This which it sought, Israel did not obtain. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how this is true: did it not seek external justice, but obtained that: therefore what it sought, this it obtained. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ This is said for the reason that they lacked the end for which they were acting: for they wished to be reputed just with God, which did not befall them.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXVIII. ⟦s2⟧ *That they hear not, unto this present day.* ⟦s3⟧ That the Jews of this time are blinded, this we plainly hold, and the saints in many places say the same, establishing the persecution of Christ as the cause of the blinding, and still more that they killed James the Just. ⟦s4⟧ But it does not seem agreeable to reason, [0498C] that the present Jews should be punished for this, since the crimes which their fathers committed displease many [of them]. ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ Not undeservedly are the modern Jews too blinded, because if the power were given them, what their fathers did to Christ, the same they would do also to the members of Christ, even if it could be done to the Head himself.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXIX. ⟦s2⟧ *I will honor my ministry,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what it is to honor one's office. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He honors his ministry who expends something beyond what he owes from his office, as Paul, who from his office ought only to preach to the Gentiles, did more, by preaching also to the Jews. ⟦s6⟧ But since Paul believed that some of the Jews could be converted to the faith by his preaching, and knew that this must be done [0498D] for God's sake, if he did not do [it], he would sin: wherefore he ought to do this — how then does he say that he honors his office, since he owed also that which is superadded? ⟦s7⟧ Solution. ⟦s8⟧ The debt of office is one thing, that of occasion another: beyond the debt of office Paul preached to the Jews, and thus honored his ministry; yet he owed to preach to them as regards the debt of occasion, since he knew it must be done for God's sake.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6⟧ QUESTION CCLXX. *For if the loss of them be the reconciliation of the world,* etc. It is asked how the unbelief of the Jews and [their] blinding was the cause of the salvation of the Gentiles, would the Gentiles not be saved, unless the Jews were blinded? Could not God save the Gentiles otherwise? Solution. Their unbelief was not the efficient cause why the Gentiles should be saved, yet from the fact that [0499A] those blinded ones repelled the word of preaching, the Apostles took occasion of preaching to the Gentiles; and thus the salvation of the Gentiles followed, God fulfilling his disposition through evils, from which he draws out a good effect.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXXI. ⟦s2⟧ *If the firstfruit be holy, so is the lump; and if the root be holy, so are the branches,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked concerning this consequence, whether it is true? ⟦s4⟧ Cannot the antecedent be true without the consequent, how then is the consequence true? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ The consequent of each hypothetical, although it be uttered simply, is nevertheless not to be understood simply, but with such a qualification, "naturally": for although the branches be broken, yet they are naturally good: and those few who were converted to the faith, if they were so constant, it is plain that those who will be converted at the end will be constant.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0499B]QUESTION CCLXXII. ⟦s2⟧ But since the nation of the Jews is often called in the Scriptures most hard, how is it here called naturally holy, as though having a fitness for holiness? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ The Apostle treats of the holiness by which is understood the inconstancy of faith; not of the fitness which they would have for holiness.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXXIII. ⟦s2⟧ It can be asked what the firstfruit is? ⟦s3⟧ It is answered. ⟦s4⟧ The taste of a small part of some thing, for the testing of the whole lump. ⟦s5⟧ But the lump is the multitude of those to be converted after the death of Antichrist. ⟦s6⟧ The Jews are called natural branches; because [they were] born of the patriarchs, in whom was the origin of faith, and of whom Christ was born according to the flesh. ⟦s7⟧ Note that "nature" is called the accustomed course of nature, against which God often works. ⟦s8⟧ Sometimes also the divine [0499C] disposition is called "nature," against which God does nothing; but does all things according to it.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXXIV. ⟦s2⟧ *For without repentance, that is, without change, are the gift and calling of God.* ⟦s3⟧ God sometimes changes his sentence, but not his counsel. ⟦s4⟧ What is it to change a sentence? ⟦s4⟧ To do something which he did not seem about to do; or not to do something which he seemed about to do.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXXV. ⟦s2+3⟧ The expositor says that the evil, while they act against the will of God, [in that very act] the will of God is fulfilled by them: but if the will of God is fulfilled by them, do they not themselves fulfill the will of God? and if this is so, how do they act against the will of God? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. "Will" is said in two ways: the precept [0499D] of God, and his disposition. ⟦s5⟧ While therefore they act against the precept of God, they do what God disposed to be done: for often good things are done through the evil; but always from the evils which the evil do, God draws out some good.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXXVI. ⟦s2⟧ *He who hears the Father comes to the Son.* ⟦s2⟧ For no one comes to the Son, unless the Father draw him [n: (Joan. VI)]. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked, then, what it is for the Father to draw to the Son? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ From the disposition which is from the Father, to come to the knowledge of the Son, that one may be saved by him: the Father draws to the Son, when the Father reveals the Son to be equal to himself.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXXVII. ⟦s2⟧ *God has shut up all things under sin, that he may have mercy on all.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how this is to be understood: is not to shut up under sin an evil? how does God do this, or how [0500A] is this the cause of good? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ God shut up all things in sin, that is, permitted [them] to be shut up, and this is the occasion of good, not the efficient cause.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXXVIII. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked whether God worked our salvation in this way rather than in another, namely by shutting up all things under sin? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ It so pleased God, that seeing themselves unable to be justified, they might be humbled, and seek grace, and thus ascribe the whole to God. ⟦s5⟧ *O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God,* etc. ⟦s6⟧ Here a threefold kind of causes is wont to be distinguished. ⟦s7⟧ Some are formal, some judicial, some final; some according to which, others through which, others on account of which things come to pass. ⟦s8⟧ Through judgments, judicial causes; through counsels, final causes; through which [they are] formal, or through wisdom and knowledge, formal.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0500B]QUESTION CCLXXIX. ⟦s2⟧ Concerning the foregoing exclamation it is wont to be asked. ⟦s3⟧ For the Apostle seems to exclaim less advisedly concerning the blinding of the Jews and the entry of the Gentiles, saying: O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God! since he himself knew how to render a cause for this, whence he says: *I would not have you ignorant of the mystery [n: (ibid.)]*, etc., and afterward adds: *God has shut up all things under sin, that he may have mercy on all [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s4⟧ But if he exclaims for this reason, that under this [matter] human understanding does not suffice to render a reason perfectly, by like reasoning would he have had to exclaim concerning many other things? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ He exclaims concerning that rather than concerning another, because there one more quickly errs in rendering reasons, and sins unto death.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXXX. ⟦s2⟧ *For from him, and through him, [0500C] and in him are all things.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether sins, insofar as they are, are from God: for if all things [are], then sins [are], or sins are not of the number of all things. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Some say that sins, insofar as they are, have their being from God. ⟦s6⟧ But Augustine, in *On the Nature of the Good*, says that only those things which are naturally ought to be understood, when it is said that all things are from God, not sins, which corrupt nature; therefore sins are not from God in any way.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION CCLXXXI. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked whether all things which have their being from God ought to be said to be of God. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Only those things which have their being of the substance of God ought to be said [to be] of God, as the Son and the Holy Spirit are from God the Father in such a way that [they are] of him, because they are of his essence, [0500D] consubstantial with him. ⟦s5⟧ But creatures are from God, not of the substance of God, but out of nothing. ⟦s6⟧ Note that by the threefold preposition, namely from, through, in, the trinity of persons is here intimated; by the same pronoun the identity of nature is signified, which is whole in each person: for saying, from whom, he understands the Father; through whom, the Son; in whom, the Holy Spirit.
