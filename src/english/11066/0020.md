## I. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE ROMANS. (cont.)

QUESTION CCLXXXII. But it is asked how, by this that he says, from whom are all things, he understands the Father, since just as all things are from the Father, so also from the Son and the Holy Spirit? Solution. On account of the authority of the origin, because all things so have their being from the Father that he himself does not have it from another; but the Son and the Holy Spirit, although they are one principle of all creatures, yet have a principle, or author, namely the Father himself, [0501A] from whom they have being: and this very thing, that they are the principle of all, they have from the Father, as all else that they possess. The expositor says that in the Trinity there is the highest origin of all, the most perfect beauty, the most blessed delight, referring origin to the Father, beauty to the Son, delight to the Holy Spirit: and thus these three — origin, beauty, and delight — are determinate, because they are referred, each determinately to each person, as has already been said. But because again the whole Trinity is the highest origin of all things, the most perfect beauty, the most blessed delight, these same three are called infinite. And this is, as I judge, what is said: that the aforesaid three are both determinate in relation to one another, and infinite in themselves.

[0501B]QUESTION CCLXXXIII. Likewise it is said: in the Father unity, in the Son equality, in the Holy Spirit the concord of unity and equality: but since the same unity, and equality, and concord belongs to the three, it is asked why unity is said to be in the Father, and equality in the Son, and concord in the Holy Spirit? Solution. Saving the reverence due to hidden things, we say that unity is said to be in the Father for this reason, that as unity is the principle of numbers, but itself has no being from any other number, so also the Father, since he is the cause of all, has no cause. But equality is said to be in the Son, because in him is the first distinction, and discretion, and the first plurality, and the second personal unity, which, that it may be shown undifferentiated and unequal in nothing from the Father himself, is not unfittingly marked by the name [0501C] of equality, wherein it is declared that the Son so has his being from the Father alone that he is neither the Father, nor diverse from him in nature, but in all things equal and consubstantial with him. But the Holy Spirit is for this reason called the concord of unity and equality, that it may be intimated that the Holy Spirit is so from both that he is equal to both — this I say saving the catholic faith, whose hidden meaning I would rather hear from another than say anything about these matters out of my own slenderness.

QUESTION CCLXXXIV. Likewise it is read: All things are one on account of the Father, all things equal on account of the Son, all things connected on account of the Holy Spirit. It is asked, then, how these words are to be understood? Are not the three persons one by the same unity, or [0501D] equal by equality, or concordant by concord? Solution. All things are one on account of the Father, that is, the three persons are one, having the same natural unity, which is wont to be referred to the Father, though it is common to the three persons: in like manner it is to be understood of equality and concord: for the three persons are equal on account of the ineffable and natural equality which they have, being thus connected. In creatures a vestige of the Trinity shines forth, because they show that they hold in themselves unity, and species, and order, since each single thing is both some one thing, and is formed by some species, and holds some order, whence it is said: *He made all things in number, and weight, and measure [n: (Sap. XI)]*. For number pertains to unity, weight to order, measure to species. For of anything whatever [0502A] it is true that, from the moment it begins to be, it at once falls under number, because it is either one or many. Weight is said to pertain to order for this reason, that single things hold their order according to the nature of weight. For there are two kinds of weights: one which tends downward, as the nature of lead; the other which tends upward, as oil. Likewise order is considered according to place and time: whence also the angels, according to their greater or lesser affections and delights [dilectiones.], obtain their orders; but the species of things, which is a certain mode of them, and measure.

QUESTION CCLXXXV. It is likewise asked whether the Holy Spirit proceeds from himself, or is sent as from the Father and the Son. Solution. The procession, or mission, of the Holy Spirit is twofold, [0502B] eternal and temporal: according to the eternal procession he proceeds only from the Father and the Son, not from himself; temporally he proceeds also from himself, because his temporal procession is the operation of the Trinity. In the same way it is to be understood of the temporal procession or mission of the Son. For just as he sanctified himself for us and did not deliver himself up, so also he sent himself, that is, his own mission or incarnation (which is the same) he wrought. But the eternal procession of the Son from the Father ought to be called, not mission, but generation.

QUESTION CCLXXXVI. It is asked further how the Holy Spirit is the love by which the Father loves the Son, and the Son the Father. Is it not the same for the Father to love, and to be, and by that whereby he has being, also to love? If [0502C] then the Father loves by the Holy Spirit, how does he not have being from the Holy Spirit? Solution. It is not said that the Father loves by the Holy Spirit, but that the Holy Spirit is the love by which the Father loves the Son; because the Holy Spirit is the divine nature, and the Father loves by the divine nature; but if by this term, the love by which the Father loves the Son, a personal property is signified, it is not true that the Holy Spirit is the love by which the Father loves, just as the Holy Spirit is not the Father.

QUESTION CCLXXXVII. Hilary says: As it is impious to preach two gods, the Father and the Son, so to preach a singular God is sacrilegious. Likewise the expositor says, speaking of the Trinity, that it is singularly [0502D] one according to substance, which seems to be contrary. Solution. Singularity sometimes excludes a plurality of persons, and according to this it is sacrilegious to preach the Father and the Son a singular God. Sometimes singularity is put for unity, and thus it is taken when it is said to be singularly one according to substance.

QUESTION CCLXXXVIII. *Not to be more wise than it behooves to be wise,* etc. [n: (Rom. XII.)] It is asked who is wise more than he ought. For no one in this life has so great a knowledge that he could not have a greater, and that he ought not even to desire more: how then does the Apostle command not to be more wise than it behooves to be wise, as though anyone could, by investigating concerning God, comprehend more than is necessary? Solution. The Apostle does not forbid the investigation [0503A] of truth, but lest anyone through excessive investigation fall into doubt of those things which ought to be firmly and undoubtingly believed. Or lest anyone ascribe to himself, by proud presumption, that which he knows, which is to be proud of one's knowledge: for this is not to be wise, but to be foolish.

QUESTION CCLXXXIX. *He who shows mercy, in cheerfulness,* etc. It is asked, then, whether God punishes anyone without mercy, that is, whether he punishes those whom he punishes as much as they deserved to be punished. Which seems so, because *judgment without mercy to him who has not done mercy [n: (Jac. II)]*. But again it is written: *All the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth [n: (Psal. XLII)]*. Whence it seems that he does not even punish the wicked without mercy, according to that: *Neither did he kindle all his wrath* [0503B] [n: (Psal. LXXVII)], that is, he did not punish them as much as they deserved; because he might justly have punished more if he willed. Solution. God punishes no one without mercy, because he punishes none as much as he deserved, and yet judgment without mercy to him who does not show mercy, because he is not further corrected to this end, that he attain eternal life. And so it is true that, just as to the good God will confer of grace more good in the future than they merited, so to the wicked less evil than they merited, and in both God is just and merciful.

QUESTION CCXC. It is asked why rather, judgment without mercy, etc., he says this of this than of another, since it could be said of any criminal whatever. Solution. Understand it to be said of this one for this reason, that there are many fallen into this error: that they believe it enough for them, in order to [0503C] attain eternal life, to harm none, and to turn away from evil, although they do not bestow their goods on the poor, or show forth works of mercy: which error the Apostle here openly represses.

QUESTION CCXCI. *Rejoice with them that rejoice,* etc. It is asked whether one should rejoice over temporal prosperity, as over the abundance of someone's riches. Solution. Since they are given to the good and to the wicked, and more often to the wicked than to the good, it does not seem that one should rejoice if they are given to anyone; nor grieve, if they are taken away: but God is always to be praised in his gifts.

QUESTION CCXCII. *Weep with them that weep,* etc. It is asked concerning the weeping which the Apostle commanded, whether it is reasonable, since over the adversities which befall the good or the wicked [0503D] one should rather rejoice than grieve? For adversities are either for a greater crown to the good, or for the correction of the wicked. It is read also that they wept imprudently over the death of the martyrs, when they should rather have rejoiced than wept, if the reason of grief admitted it. Solution. One weeping is of piety, another of compassion: that which is of piety is, as it were, of nature, and according to some does not merit; but that which is of compassion, merits. For we ought to have compassion on the infirmities of the brethren, that we may gain those whose salvation we ought to desire, and give our labor that they be saved.

QUESTION CCXCIII. *Rendering to no one evil for evil, and love your enemies.* It is asked how the saints do not render evil for evil, since it is written: [0504A] *The saints cry out: Avenge, O Lord, our blood [n: (Apoc. VI)]*. And that: *The just shall rejoice when he shall see the vengeance [n: (Psal. XXXVII)]*; therefore since equally the good and the wicked wish to be avenged by the Lord, wherein are they distinguished? Solution. The good do not rejoice over the punishment as the wicked do, but over the judgment of God.

QUESTION CCXCIV. It is asked whether Christ observes what the Apostle above commanded to all, that he render evil for evil to none. They wish to prove thus, that Christ does not observe it. The wicked man deserved that evil be rendered to him for evil: unless therefore God render him evil, he will not render him what he deserves. Likewise God renders him something for evil, therefore either good or evil; if not evil, therefore good, and thus the wicked deserved good. Likewise, does not God render punishment for fault, and [0504B] thus evil for evil? Solution. Neither a good judge nor God renders evil for evil; because this would be to exercise vengeance with an evil intention: whence it must be diligently guarded against, lest this be inferred in all the aforesaid matters.

QUESTION CCXCV. It is asked whether, from what has been said, the justice by which someone is hanged for theft, and the like, is of the Gospel. Which seems so, since the Church hands him over to the prince, and all justice of the Church ought to be that which is there taught. *Behold, here are two swords [n: (Luc. XXII)]*. But if it is of the Church, as it seems, how can it be denied that the justice of the Gospel is heavier than the justice of the law, since by the law it was commanded, *a tooth for a tooth, an eye for an eye [n: (Matth. V)]*; but here the head of a man is given for a she-goat [0504C]? Solution. This is in no way the justice of the Gospel, that a man be slain for a horse or an ox, nor is this precept found in the whole Gospel, nor does the Church do it, but only permits it.
