## II. ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0513]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0513A]QUESTION I. *Paul, called to be an Apostle,* etc. [n: (I Cor. I.)] This Epistle, which is addressed to the Corinthians, was not written by the Apostle in the second place, as in the body of the epistles it is arranged in the second place. ⟦s2⟧ For both epistles sent to the Corinthians were written before [0513B] that which is to the Romans. ⟦s3⟧ Which is clear from this, that in the Epistle to the Romans he says that he will set out for Rome after he has assigned the collection made by the Achaeans for the poor who are at Jerusalem. ⟦s4⟧ Of which collection, to be made, he makes mention in these. ⟦s5⟧ But it is not without a rational cause that that to the Romans is set before the others in the body of the epistles, but on account of the dignity of the Romans. ⟦s6⟧ Or rather because in it first is taken away and destroyed that vice which is pride. ⟦s7⟧ But the Corinthians, converted to the faith by the Apostle, but afterward in manifold ways subverted and seduced by false apostles, were erring in the virtues of the sacraments, and especially in the virtue of baptism, thinking that, when conferred by an evil man, it had no virtue, a greater from a better, a lesser from a less good. [0513C] They also thought little of the Apostle, and held him in contempt: he who had not come to them in the words of human wisdom, but in the simplicity of faith for the instruction of the little ones. ⟦s8⟧ Concerning the resurrection also they did not think rightly, saying that it had already taken place: some also among them altogether denied it. ⟦s9⟧ Certain ones also wished to hold the law together with the Gospel as though necessary to salvation. ⟦s10⟧ In marriage also they sinned, as in many other things, and made schisms: in which the Apostle intends to correct them. ⟦s11⟧ For after pride is cut down in us, it remains that we cut down also the other vices. ⟦s12⟧ From these things, then, it appears what is the matter of this epistle, what the intention, what the mode and order of proceeding. ⟦s13⟧ But the special matter of this [0513D] epistle is the state of the Corinthians, in which they then were, when the Apostle wrote to them from Ephesus. ⟦s14⟧ But the intention is to recall them from contentions to the unity of faith. ⟦s15⟧ But the mode is such, that he instructs, rebukes, confirms, praises, according to the qualities of persons. ⟦s16⟧ The order is such: he sends beforehand a salutation, after the manner of those writing epistles, lest at the outset he seem to have chided them. ⟦s17⟧ Then he sets before them concerning good things, that others may be conformed to them, and thus he comes to reproving contention. ⟦s18⟧ He begins therefore from the salutation, saying, Paul, using a name of humility against pride; Apostle, which is a name of dignity and office.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION II. ⟦s2⟧ *Through the will of God,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked: what does he here call the will of God? ⟦s4⟧ For the will of God is sometimes called his counsel, sometimes [0514A] his precept, sometimes his disposition, sometimes his approbation, sometimes his permission. ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ He calls the will of God his good pleasure.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION III. ⟦s2⟧ *Grace to you, and peace from God.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked: what is the grace of God? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The grace of God is said to be [0514B] God giving freely, and grace, that is, divine inspiration, namely the operation of God, from which the soul is moved to love God and neighbor.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION IV. ⟦s2⟧ *To them that wait for the revelation,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether to every just man it is revealed at once after his death concerning his salvation. ⟦s4⟧ For it seems not: for since that revelation is a full knowledge, which is eternal beatitude, it seems, if this be granted, that no one is in purgatorial punishments: for can anyone be at once in punishment and in glory? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ The perfect pass at once to the knowledge of the highest good, in which is eternal beatitude. ⟦s7⟧ But the less perfect, before they come to eternal beatitude, pass through purgatorial punishments, yet certain of their [0514C] rest at last. ⟦s8⟧ But that there are purgatorial punishments, Augustine makes manifest, saying: The mildest purgatorial punishment is heavier than any temporal punishment whatever that is among us: and so there cannot be at once punishment and glory in such a fire.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION V. It is asked how that is to be understood which is read: *The angels shall tremble in the day of judgment;* for if then there will be trembling in them, how are they blessed? ⟦s2⟧ Solution. ⟦s3⟧ That trembling notes veneration of a higher power, not that the punishment of fear can be there where there is eternal beatitude. ⟦s4⟧ It is to be noted that not every mortal sin is called a crime, but only that which is worthy of accusation and damnation.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VI. ⟦s2⟧ *Unto the day of the coming of the Lord,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether the Lord will come on a day to judgment; [0514D] because often the time of his coming is called a day. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The time of the coming of the Lord is called a day, not for the illumination of the air, but for the revelation of hidden things: for all things which now lie hidden will then be laid open. ⟦s6⟧ But it is not known: will he come by day, or by night? ⟦s7⟧ For not only by the name of day, as above, but also by the name of night is the time of judgment designated, as it is written in the Gospel: *At midnight there was a cry made: Behold, the bridegroom cometh [n: (Matth. XXV)]*; but it is called night on account of its exceeding hiddenness: the day of judgment is so called on account of the discerning of the good and the wicked.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VII. ⟦s2⟧ *Now this I say, that every one of you saith: I am of Paul,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether the Apostle speaks truly, saying, Now this, etc., when [0515A] none of them said this. ⟦s4⟧ For they did not glory in the true apostles, but in the false. ⟦s5+6⟧ Whence in what follows he is about to say: *But these things, brethren, I have transferred in a figure to myself, and to Apollo [n: (I Cor. IV)]*. ⟦s7⟧ Solution. ⟦s8⟧ The Apostle speaks truly, and the sense of the words is: Now this I say, that every one, etc., that is, this which I say is like that which every one of you says. ⟦s9⟧ But he did not wish to designate those in whom they gloried by their proper names, lest he should seem to envy them; and that in greater matters he might show that in lesser ones there is no glorying.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VIII. ⟦s2⟧ Augustine says that Christ retained to himself the power of baptizing, which, if he willed, he could have given to his servants. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked, therefore, what that power is, which Christ retained to himself, and could have given to his servants, [0515B] and yet did not give. ⟦s4⟧ Whether it is the power of outwardly dipping bodies; but this he gave to the apostles, on whom he conferred the ministry of baptizing; but if he calls the power of baptizing the inward washing of souls, this power God alone can have, whose alone it is to infuse himself into souls, and to wipe away the stains of sins: how then could he give this to his servants? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ In answering this question many labor, not knowing what Augustine signified by the name of power. ⟦s7⟧ But we say that he calls power a dignity: by which, through the invocation of the name of Christ, or of the whole Trinity, baptism is given: which dignity he could have given to his chief servants, so that baptism might be designated by their name, and be called the baptism of Paul, the baptism [0515C] of Peter, and baptism designated by their names might have as great a force as it has when designated by the name of Christ himself: yet he did not wish to give this to them, lest anyone place his hope in man.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5⟧ QUESTION IX. *Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?* etc. What it is to be baptized in the name of Paul is wont to be asked. Solution. The faithful are not baptized in the name of Paul; because those who are baptized are not bidden to believe in Paul, but in Christ, nor is the name of Paul invoked over them or over the element, that the sacrament may be made, but the name of Christ, who alone baptizes in the Spirit.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION X. *Lest the cross of Christ should be made void by the wisdom of speech.* ⟦s2⟧ It is asked how by the wisdom of speech [0515D] the cross of Christ should be made void. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ The wisdom of the world, which is called the wisdom of speech, because it is commended by no virtue, but by the composition of words alone, supposes that natural genius suffices for knowing, and free will for living rightly: which if it were so, the cross of Christ would be made void, that is, Christ would have died in vain.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XI. ⟦s2⟧ *I will destroy the wisdom,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Is not all wisdom from God? how then is it said: I will destroy the wisdom *of the wise?* ⟦s4⟧ Does God destroy that which is from himself? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ Their wisdom in themselves is not the wisdom of God; because they ascribe it to themselves, and abuse it: whence while they think to comprehend the highest things, they become vain in their thoughts, and thus from their wisdom incur damnation to themselves. ⟦s7⟧ Therefore their false [0516A] opinion, by which they believed that God could not do anything against the accustomed course of nature, and by which they supposed that free will sufficed for salvation, is called by the prophet the wisdom of the wise, which God destroys by acting against it, according to the reasons above, working our salvation in the midst of the earth.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XII. ⟦s2⟧ *The world by wisdom knew not God.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how this is true which he here says, since elsewhere it is written of the wise of the world: *When they had known God, they glorified him not as God [n: (Rom. I)]*: how are both true, the world knew not, and when they had known God? etc. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ That: When they had known God, etc., is understood of the possibility of knowing. ⟦s5⟧ But this, [0516B] the world knew not God, is taken of the act of knowing. ⟦s6⟧ Or rather that, when they had known God, is understood of a few of the wiser and more excellent. ⟦s7⟧ But this, the world knew not, is expounded of all, or the greater part. ⟦s8⟧ Whence he here says the world: by which name the whole, or the greater part, is wont to be understood. ⟦s9⟧ Or the world, that is, the wise of the world, even if they had known God according to the works of restoration, in power, not in piety; in majesty, not in humility. ⟦s10⟧ It is to be noted that there are theophanies in creatures, that is, divine apparitions, as in the world, whose greatness demonstrates the highest power of God; but the fullness or beauty of the world demonstrates the wisdom of God; but the usefulness of the world intimates the benignity of God: in power the Father, in wisdom the Son, in [0516C] benignity the Holy Spirit is understood.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIII. ⟦s2⟧ *He that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what it is to glory in oneself, or of oneself. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He glories in himself, or of himself, who thinks that to be of himself which is of God, and therefore attributes praise to himself, who, so far as in him lies, takes from God what is his own: for God alone is the author of good things. ⟦s6⟧ Whence it follows that whoever says any good to be of himself, in a certain manner makes himself God. ⟦s7⟧ He also glories in himself who, although he know that what he has is from God, yet turns back to himself the end of it, which he ought to refer to God.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIV. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked also what it is to glory in the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ He glories in the Lord who by the grace [0516D] of God alone judges himself worthy of glory.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XV. ⟦s2⟧ *And I came not to you in loftiness of speech, or of wisdom,* etc. [n: (I Cor. II.)] It is asked how the Apostle says that he came not to the Corinthians in wisdom, but preached to them Christ, and him crucified: as though it were less difficult to understand that God assumed in the flesh died, than that God is one in essence, and three in persons? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ In Christ there are two natures, human and divine: but those things which are according to the human nature are more akin to us than those which are according to the divine nature: wherefore by us they are more easily believed and understood. ⟦s5⟧ But those which are according to the divine nature, as concerning the three persons, and the same essence, and the like, are more remote, [0517A] and therefore more difficult to understand. ⟦s6⟧ Whence he well said that he came not to them in wisdom, when he discoursed to them of the death of Christ; because he preached those lesser things, and not those loftier ones.
