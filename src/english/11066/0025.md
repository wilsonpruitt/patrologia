## II. ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVI. ⟦s2⟧ Is it not required, in order that anyone be perfect, that he believe God to be one and three? ⟦s2⟧ And thus the Apostle seems to have handed down to them an imperfect doctrine, not preaching those loftier things, and thus to have deceived them. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Faith is spoken of in two ways: either when it is simply set forth what is to be believed; or when the distinction of the things which are believed is set out distinctly — what is to be believed, and how, and why. ⟦s5⟧ But thus the Apostle set before them, generally and without distinction of the several articles, what they ought to believe. ⟦s6⟧ For thus it behooves that in the first place [0517B] believers be initiated, and afterward that the advancing be perfected.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVII. ⟦s2⟧ *We speak wisdom among the perfect.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whom the Apostle here calls perfect. ⟦s4⟧ For some are perfect in faith and in knowledge; others perfect in knowledge, and less in faith; others perfect in faith, and less in knowledge. ⟦s5⟧ In faith and knowledge the apostles are perfect; perfect in knowledge, and less in faith, are certain clerics, who are less constant in faith than the country folk; in faith and not in knowledge are very many country folk, who have attained little of knowledge, and yet believe perfectly. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ It seems to us that the Apostle was speaking wisdom among those perfect in knowledge, because those who are perfect in faith alone seem incapable of the higher things: yet deservedly to such faithful ones many things are often revealed: [0517C] and therefore, according to some, he speaks among those perfect in faith.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVIII. ⟦s2⟧ *Neither of the princes of this world, which is brought to nought [who are brought to nought].* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how the wisdom of the philosophers is brought to nought, since it seems to be itself a comprehension of the truth. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ It is brought to nought when it is shown to be false or nothing: for they thought, as has already been said, that God could do nothing against nature. ⟦s6⟧ And so, among the great and many things which they truly comprehended, they asserted many false ones, and that truth which they had from God they attributed not to God but to themselves, whence their wisdom was turned into darkness.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIX. ⟦s2⟧ *Which none of the princes of this world.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked first whom he calls the princes of the world? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Either the wise of the world, or the demons, or even [0517D] those learned in the law.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XX. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked, therefore, secondly, how that is true, that none of the demons knew him, since it is written of those who said: *Jesus, thou Son of David, why art thou come to torment us before the time? [n: (Matth. VIII.)]* And again: *And there were many devils going out, and crying: Thou art the Son of God; and he suffered them not to speak, for they knew him to be the Son of God [n: (Marc I)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ The demons are said to have known on account of their supposing: who rather supposed Christ to be God than truly knew it: for God always left the devil in doubt, so that always after some humble things he did lofty ones, and after the sublime returned to the humble: whence after that notable [0518A] miracle concerning Lazarus, when the devil altogether thought him to be God, he allowed himself to be seized, bound, scourged: whence again the devil judged him to be a mere man; but when he was now led to the governor, and the Jews themselves confirmed in evil, it was in some manner revealed to him that through him he would lose his own right, and he wished to persuade Pilate through his wife to release him.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXI. ⟦s2⟧ Likewise it is asked, thirdly, concerning the Jews, how it is true that they did not know him, since it is written of them in the Gospel parable: *Behold the heir; come, let us kill him [n: (Marc. XII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ And elsewhere: *That through envy they had delivered him up [n: (Matth. XXVII)]*: but if they knew him, and yet killed him, incredible cruelty was in them. ⟦s4⟧ Likewise, if they knew him to be God, they knew him to be immortal, and thus unable [0518B] to die: how then did they seek his death, if they knew that he could not die? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ Some say that the Jews knew Christ, which they could not deny. ⟦s7⟧ Or in truth some of them knew him to be that one who had been promised in the law and the prophets. ⟦s8⟧ But they did not believe him to be God; yet that they killed him whom they knew to be just, was envy. ⟦s9⟧ Whose nature is such, that against its own goods it seeks the harm of others: therefore they were utterly ignorant of the dispensation of the divine counsel.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXII. ⟦s2⟧ *That we may know the things that are given us.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what it is to know the things that are given us. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ To know from whom they are given, and not to be ungrateful to the giver: for he is said truly to have who knows whence he has; he does not have, who knows not whence he has, and is ungrateful [0518C] to him from whom he has: for no one is blessed by the gifts of God who is ungrateful to the giver.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXIII. ⟦s2⟧ *Comparing spiritual things with spiritual.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whom he calls spiritual: for some are perfect in life, and not in understanding; others in understanding, and not in life. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Those whom above he named perfect, here he calls spiritual.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXIV. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked whom he calls the animal man. ⟦s3⟧ For a man is animal according to life, and according to understanding. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He calls animal the man intent upon earthly wisdom, who, considering earthly things, reckons the things that are of God to be foolish.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXV. ⟦s2⟧ *But the spiritual man discerneth all things that are necessary to salvation, and he himself is judged of no man. [0518D] For the animal man cannot understand what he doeth, or why.* ⟦s3⟧ It is objected. ⟦s4⟧ Peter was rebuked by Paul, therefore a spiritual man is judged by a spiritual man. ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ The spiritual man is judged of no man, that is, he is shown by no one to be damnable and worthy of damnation: for although he sin in something, yet this is not damnable to him.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXVI. ⟦s2⟧ *I could not speak to you as to spiritual,* etc. [n: (I Cor. II)]. ⟦s3⟧ Was not the Apostle spiritual, both in life and in knowledge, and thus able to speak spiritual things, which he also did? ⟦s4⟧ Whence above: *We speak wisdom among the perfect [n: (I Cor. II)]*; how then does he say, I could not speak to you as to spiritual? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ He does not say this as though he did not inwardly have the faculty of teaching greater things, but because they themselves were not fit hearers; yet often, when the perfect and imperfect [0519A] are mingled, the same things are said, even if they are not understood in the same way.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXVII. ⟦s2⟧ *I have planted, Apollo watered, but God now gave the increase.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how the Apostle divides between himself, and God, and Apollo, since Paul or Apollo does nothing except from God and through God, and thus whatever either Paul or Apollo works, God also works. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The Apostle divides, that he may show what God works through Paul himself, and through Apollo, and through himself: for the Apostle, by preaching, was working unto the faith of Christ, which God also did through Paul. ⟦s6⟧ For in vain would the Apostle speak outwardly, unless the Holy Spirit were within, working, and moving the mind.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXVIII. ⟦s2+3⟧ *He that watereth, and he that planteth, are one. [0519B]* It is asked how one, since they are divided both in substance and in accident? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ They are one, that is, indifferent. ⟦s6⟧ For neither does Paul confer more from that which is Paul's than Apollo from that which is Apollo's. ⟦s7⟧ For in the conferring of gifts this one does no more than that one: for God alone confers the gifts; yet Paul labors more than Apollo, and a greater gift is conferred through the office of Paul than of Apollo, because through the faith which is from preaching one comes to baptism. ⟦s8⟧ Or, through the sacraments themselves no more is conferred from this one than from that one, or through this one than through that one.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6⟧ QUESTION XXIX. *For we are God's helpers,* etc. Does God need anything? How then were the apostles God's helpers? Solution. They are called God's helpers because God works through them, although he could work that same thing [0519C] through himself.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXX. ⟦s2⟧ *As a wise architect, I have laid the foundation,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what he calls the foundation. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The faith of Christ, which works through love, which they obtained from the preaching of Paul himself: this foundation remaining, no one can perish, although some stains adhere, which are purged by fire. ⟦s6⟧ For although here they possessed some worldly things by desire, yet they did not prefer these to God, but God to all. ⟦s7⟧ But to prefer God to all is, if the option or choice were given of dying or of denying Christ, to choose to die rather than to deny Christ. ⟦s8⟧ Upon this foundation one builds this, another that.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6⟧ QUESTION XXXI. It is asked, what is to be understood by *wood, hay, [0519D] and stubble?* Solution. Some say that by these three is understood venial sin: but how do these build upon this foundation, since rather any sin whatever pertains to destruction than to building? Likewise, how shall he who builds these be saved yet so as by fire, since faith without works is dead? Likewise, *if any man have all faith, so as to remove mountains, and have not charity, it profiteth nothing [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*: therefore, if there is no salvation without charity, how can anyone be saved building these three alone upon this foundation?

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXII. ⟦s2⟧ Others say otherwise, that by wood, hay, stubble are understood good works, but imperfect ones. ⟦s3⟧ According to which opinion it is asked how a good work is to burn, or how [0520A] it suffers loss? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Not the good work, because it is good; but because it is imperfect it will be purged of its imperfection, and especially of certain carnal affections which the builders of such things have, which seem to be venial sins.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXIII. ⟦s2⟧ *All things are yours; and you are Christ's; and Christ is God's,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked according to which nature we are Christ's, that is, whether we are his according to the human nature, or according to the divine. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ It can be said that we are his not only according to the divine nature, according to which he is our Creator, but also we are his according to the human nature, according to which he is our Redeemer; but he himself, according to both natures, is of God the Father, from whom he has whatever [0520B] he has; but in one way and in another: for according to that which he is God, he has by nature all things which are the Father's; and all the same things he has according to that which he is man, but by grace: this is the norm of right faith.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXIV. ⟦s2⟧ *I am conscious to myself of nothing,* etc. [n: (I Cor. IV)]. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how the Apostle says that he is conscious to himself of nothing, and yet is not hereby justified: and John in his Epistle says: *If our heart do not reprehend us, we have confidence toward God [n: (I Joan. III)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And Paul himself elsewhere: *For our glory is this, the testimony of our conscience [n: (II Cor. I)]*: wherefore we ought to think ourselves just, and thence to have glory, if our conscience does not reprehend us. ⟦s5⟧ For what is a greater perfection of justice than to have so pure [0520C] a conscience? ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ Something can always lie hidden from us: and therefore, in order that we may have true justice, it does not suffice that our conscience does not gnaw at us: yet we can glory in this, that our conscience is clean and pure.
