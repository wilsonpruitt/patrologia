## II. ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS. (cont.)

QUESTION XXXV. Likewise it is asked how the Apostle was not conscious to himself, since he knew that he was not without sin. Whence John: *If we say that we have no sin, we are liars, and the truth is not in us [n: (I Joan. I)]*. And the child, whose life is of one day upon the earth [n: (Job II)], is not without sin. Whence he himself says: *And I do that which I will not [n: (Rom. VII)]*. Solution. The Apostle was of such perfection that whatever his conscience dictated to him was to be done, [0520D] this he did. Whence, if at any time as a man he had sinned, either through ignorance, or by stealth, or by frailty, the force of divine love consumed the whole like stubble.

QUESTION XXXVI. *Judge not before the time,* etc. It is one thing to judge concerning ambiguous matters, which is forbidden, because damnable: it is another to suspect, which is of human infirmity, of which perhaps no one is free. But since the Apostle forbids judging, and the Lord himself forbids the same in the Gospel, what is it that elsewhere the Lord says: *Beware of false prophets, who come to you in the clothing of sheep [n: (Matth. VII)]*. And how they may also be discerned, he himself adds, saying: *By their fruits you shall know them [n: (ibid.)]*. Hence he seems to intimate that we ought to judge concerning such. Solution. [0521A] It is one thing to discern the evil, that they may be guarded against; another to judge them worthy of damnation: whom we do not definitely know whether they are good or evil.

QUESTION XXXVII. Likewise it is asked how false prophets can be discerned from their works, since they do all the same things that the good also do: for by fruits are understood works. Solution. By the virtue of perseverance the false are proved and discerned from the true.

QUESTION XXXVIII. It is asked whether that is true which the Apostle says: *You are now filled [n: (I Cor. IV)]*, since he speaks ironically: which does not seem to be, since the contrary is true. Solution. As often as such a kind of speech occurs, as to the surface of the word and the first signification of the utterance, what is said is false: yet he himself who speaks does not lie; [0521B] but rather speaks the truth, which happens from the nature of the trope.

QUESTION XXXIX. *Not to confound you.* Is it not good to bring it about by a word that a sinner be confounded, and blush for his fault, and thus be corrected: how then does the Apostle say, I say *not this,* to confound you? Solution. Confusion is twofold. There is a confusion which is unto death, from which some are made worse; and there is a confusion which is of correction, when one blushes from correction over his sins, that a man may abstain the more.

QUESTION XL. *Shall I come to you with a rod, or in charity?* It is asked how the Apostle divides, saying, whether with a rod, or in charity. Solution. The name "charity" is here, from the adjunct, restricted to the sweet and delightful things [0521C] which come from charity; but sometimes charity signifies all things, the harsh and the sweet, which pertain to salvation.

QUESTION XLI. The expositor says that in those who are punished by an inflicted penalty, sin is diminished: but it is asked how this is true, since they bear the inflicted penalty unwillingly, and in this are contrary to justice, and thus sin the more, and thus their sin is increased in this, that they are punished. Solution. It can be said of such, that at least they avoid, through the penalty which they suffer, the sins into which they would fall if they lived longer: for if they bore the penalty willingly, even the sin already committed would be forgiven.

[n: (I COR. V)]. *I have judged to deliver such a man [0521D] to Satan,* etc. From this place of the Apostle the Church takes the sentence of anathema, that is, of separation; and separation is made now of known and manifest persons, now of unknown; but whether of these or of those, they are first to be summoned before the Church. Some also are to be awaited longer, and more often admonished, according to the diversity and quality of persons and of sins, as if a king, through whose anger slaughter threatens the Church. But after they have been separated, if they are known, in no way is one to communicate with them: if unknown, if anyone communicate unknowingly, he does not sin; but if he suspect, if he can avoid it without scandal, let him avoid; if he cannot, let him communicate. But this vengeance ought to be done for the sake of the common utility, both of him and of others.

[0522A]QUESTION XLII. It is asked, then, whether to excommunicate someone is good, which seems so, because just. Solution. It is good, that is, expedient, yet not in itself and from its own quality is it good: but when such a thing is called just, it is understood thus, that is, requited according to desert, not that it is informed by the quality of justice.

QUESTION XLIII. Likewise it is asked whether to be excommunicated is good. Solution. According to the aforesaid reasoning it seems to be good, because excommunication seems to be expedient not only to the rest but even to him: for if he should resist the admonition of the Church, he would sin.

QUESTION XLIV. But it is asked, if it happen that he be excommunicated who did not deserve it, but someone is struck with the sentence of excommunication out of the hatred or [0522B] envy of a prelate, whether he ought to resist, or to yield. Solution. If without scandal he can resist, let him resist, and by good zeal repress the swelling of his prelate; if without scandal he cannot, let him yield, and for God's sake patiently endure: whence the sentence of a prelate is always to be feared, not always to be held. When someone is excommunicated, he is delivered into the power of Satan, that he may vex his flesh, so that at last he may thus come to his senses: yet Satan does not always vex. For the crafty enemy spares one, that he may gain many, or that he may keep him for himself.

QUESTION XLV. *Purge out the old leaven,* etc. It is asked what he calls the old leaven? Solution. Vain boasting, or the oldness of life, which is to be purged by [0522C] newness of life.

QUESTION XLVI. *Such cannot be gained to Christ,* etc. This is from the gloss. It is asked: Since the human mind is prone to evil rather than to good, and especially when it is urged to return to accustomed things, why did the Apostle grant to those newly converted to the faith to have table and conversation in common with the gentiles, since Christians would more easily come to idolatry by the persuasion of the gentiles than the gentiles come to the faith by the admonition of Christians? Solution. In the primitive Church those converted to the faith, fervent with charity, could not easily be torn from the foundation: which the Apostle knowing, granted to it only fellowship with the gentiles.

QUESTION XLVII. *If he who is named a brother among [0522D] you is a fornicator,* etc. Since there is now so great a multitude in the Church, it is asked whether we act against the precept of the Apostle when we take food with such, or whether all such are to be cast out of the Church and cut off with the sickle of anathema. Solution. So indeed it would be to be done, if it could be done without scandal: but yet, because so great and so many would rather make a schism, since they spurn correction and amendment, they are tolerated: and therefore nothing else remains but that Rachel weep for her children. It is to be noted that of fornications some are bodily, others spiritual: bodily is every use of those members apart from the lawful; spiritual is that which separates the soul from God, and takes it away, as a bride from the bridegroom.

QUESTION XLVIII. *Know you not that the saints shall judge this [0523A] world? [n: (I Cor. VI.)]* Here the Apostle shows that it is lawful for the saints to judge concerning secular matters: but if this is so, how does he afterward subjoin: *there is now plainly a fault among you, that you have lawsuits among yourselves [n: (Ibid.)]*: if it is lawful to judge, how is this a fault? Solution. It is lawful for the saints to have causes under discipline, but it is not lawful to contend among themselves.

QUESTION XLIX. It is asked how this is true, that it is lawful to demand back one's own, since the Lord says: *If anyone take away what is thine, ask it not again? [n: (Luc VI.)]* Solution. To demand back one's own, although it be not evil, yet is an occasion of evil: therefore the Lord counsels not to demand back one's own, for the sake of avoiding contentions. This is what the Apostle says: *All things are lawful to me, but all things are not expedient [n: (I Cor. X)]*.

[0523B]QUESTION L. It is asked in what manner the saints are to judge in the future? And it is said, not only by the comparison of a better deed, but also by authority and power. Whence not without reason it is asked what that power is, since it is written: *The Father judgeth no man, but hath given all judgment to the Son [n: (Joan. V)]*. Solution. As Daniel says: *The judgment sat, and the books were opened,* namely of the eternal reasons, according to which the judgment shall be made, and the books of consciences, concerning which the judgment shall be made [n: (Dan. VII)]. These books of the eternal reasons and of consciences will be open and manifest to those who are to judge with the Lord. And this revelation of hidden things, according to some, is their power and authority: according to others, the saints are therefore said to be about to judge with the Lord because by their comparison the evil [0523C] will appear damnable. Whence it is read that the saints shall judge the nations.

QUESTION LI. It is asked whether it is a precept, or a counsel, or a permission, this which he says concerning judgments? Now it is a counsel concerning the highest things, as that: *Concerning virgins I have no precept; but I give counsel [n: (I Cor. VII)]*. A precept concerning middle things, as: *Thou shalt not kill, thou shalt not commit adultery [n: (Exod. XX)]*, etc.; a permission concerning the lowest things, as concerning the taking of food. But it seems that it is a precept: for if I do not demand back my own, I foster in error him who has taken it away; wherefore for the sake of the common utility I ought to demand it back: wherefore I demand it back from precept; because every evil is to be fled from precept. Solution. It is a permission to demand back; but a counsel [0523D] not to demand back. The Apostle therefore shows what is lawful to the weak; but the Lord shows what befits the perfect, namely not to demand back: but to contend, and to carry on lawsuits before a judge, especially an unbeliever, is altogether a fault.

QUESTION LII. *He who is joined to a harlot is made one body.* It is asked concerning this unity, what it is, and according to what it is to be understood. Solution. He notes that such are one not only by bodily conjunction, but in the ardor of lust, and this is most foul, thus to be made one body with a harlot, who ought to be one spirit cleaving to God.

QUESTION LIII. *For they shall be,* he says, *two in one flesh.* It is asked how the Apostle brings forward this authority, which is concerning that conjugal union which is holy, [0524A] to show this foul union, which is between the fornicator and the harlot, since in no way can that union which is there be this. Solution. The sense is: If in that conjugal union one body is made of both, much more is this made one flesh on account of the greater delight of the vice.

QUESTION LIV. *Bear God,* etc. What is it to bear God? Solution. To live soberly, and justly, and piously. They therefore bear God who live well in him: for this is to put on his image and the form of justice, just as they trample God under foot who by living ill deny him; but they lay God down who grow lukewarm in charity and are made more lax and remiss.
