## II. ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LV. ⟦s2⟧ The gloss says: It is graver to sin against God than against the neighbor; but do not those who sin against the neighbor [0524B] offend God? ⟦s2⟧ How then is this distinguished, both the one and the other? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ To sin against God is, through contempt in those things which pertain specially to him, as in the sacraments, and faith, and the like, to offend God. ⟦s5⟧ To sin against the neighbor is to injure him by some wrong, which is less grave than the first. ⟦s6⟧ Or, to sin against God is to violate the mind inwardly. ⟦s7⟧ To sin against the neighbor is outwardly to injure a man by word or example: not therefore is everyone who offends him said to sin against God.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LVI. ⟦s2⟧ *Now concerning the things whereof you wrote to me,* etc. [n: (I Cor. VII.)] He treats of marriage in three ways: according to precept, according to permission, according to counsel, which several things let the diligent reader distinguish in their places. ⟦s3⟧ What marriage is must be seen, [0524C] what the efficient cause, what the cause on account of which it is contracted, who are the legitimate persons, and what are the goods of marriage. ⟦s4⟧ And so marriage or matrimony is the marital conjunction of male and female, retaining, between legitimate persons, an undivided manner of life. ⟦s5⟧ This definition befits only the marriage of the faithful. ⟦s6⟧ According to others: Marriage is the power of lawfully being joined, contracted from lawful consent. ⟦s7⟧ The efficient cause is the material consent expressed through words of the present. ⟦s8⟧ The consent, which is in the soul, ought to be shown before the Church, without which there is no marriage, whence it is read: Matrimony is made not by the union of bodies, but by the will of souls. ⟦s9⟧ The cause on account of which it is contracted is the procreation [0524D] of offspring, and the avoidance of fornication. ⟦s10⟧ The legitimate persons are those whom neither a vow of continence, nor blood, nor order, nor disparity of worship, nor condition, nor frigidity of nature impedes. ⟦s11⟧ Three are the goods of marriage: faith, offspring, sacrament — namely inseparability, which is twofold, of the sacrament and of matrimony itself: for this third good, namely the sacrament, is not marriage itself, although it is itself a sacrament, as is that also: but whether both are of the same thing can be asked, which here we defer to resolve for the sake of brevity, whose traces we follow: for in the [treatises on] sacraments and the sentences of our elders we pursue these things more diligently.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LVII. ⟦s2⟧ *But this I say by way of indulgence.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what indulgence is, or is called? [0525A] Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Indulgence is the concession of a laxer life; so that what would be unlawful and a sin, where there was no concession, becomes lawful. ⟦s5⟧ For the carnal union of man and woman would be a sin, unless it had taken up the state of the married, and thus through concession the unlawful becomes lawful, which is wont to be done for the sake of avoiding a graver sin.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LVIII. ⟦s2⟧ *But I would that all of you were as myself.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked: how does the Apostle say that he wishes all chaste, since he knew that God does not? ⟦s3⟧ For if this were so, how would the propagation of the human race come about? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ There is an implicit condition, as if he said, I wish, and it is good, or it would please me, that all were such: perhaps, if all were good who now are, the number of the predestined would be filled from them.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0525B]QUESTION LIX. ⟦s2⟧ *Let not the husband put away,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether a husband can put away his wife, if she be convicted of adultery? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He can put her away. ⟦s6⟧ Likewise it is asked whether he, or she, can be joined to another while the other is living. ⟦s7⟧ Some say that he cannot.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LX. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked whether marriage still remains, namely after divorce; if it remains, then the husband has power over the body of his wife, and conversely. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ It remains, and he has the power; but he cannot use this power, unless a reconciliation has been made.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXI. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked whether [sic: *au*] among unbelievers, or between a believer and an unbeliever, there is matrimony: which does not seem to be, since it is written that no marriage which was not made in God is ratified. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. [0525C] They say that there is marriage among unbelievers, as between Priam and Hecuba, and between Philip and Herodias. ⟦s4⟧ Whence John reproved Herod for adultery; for it is not lawful for thee to have thy brother's wife [n: (Matth. XIV)]: because, if there was adultery and marriage there, as Chrysostom says.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXII. ⟦s2⟧ *But if the unbeliever depart, let him depart.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked, if between a believer and an unbeliever there is marriage, how the Apostle says: *If he will depart, let the unbeliever depart, and let the believer be joined to another [n: (I Cor. VII)]*, when above he says that she who is dismissed should remain unmarried: for if it is a true marriage, how can it be dissolved? ⟦s3⟧ If it is not a marriage, how does he persuade them to remain together, since every union apart from the lawful is fornication? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The believer has power [0525D] over the body of the unbeliever, but not conversely; because, as is read, the injury done to the Creator dissolves the right of matrimony: the believer therefore can both remain and depart. ⟦s6⟧ Because also that debt which was owed was previously paid; but the power which she had, she did not lose. ⟦s7⟧ But the unbeliever departing, if he join himself to another, commits adultery.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXIII. ⟦s2⟧ Concerning the marriages of the ancients it is wont to be asked whether they were true marriages, seeing how one having several wives conducted himself toward each by marital law. ⟦s3⟧ How was there lawful consent there, retaining an undivided manner of life. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Some say that they were not marriages, but held the place of marriage. ⟦s6⟧ But others say that there were true marriages among some, and that there are among the Jews and unbelievers: [0526A] for he who says that the marriage of such is not ratified does not deny it to be a marriage, but asserts it not to be ratified.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXIV. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked whether marriage is a greater good than virginity. ⟦s3⟧ And it seems that it is; because there are greater straits, and pains, and labors in marriage than in virginity, and to each one the reward will be rendered according to his labor. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ It does not follow, if the labor is greater here than there, that therefore the glory is greater. ⟦s6⟧ For virginity is a greater good in itself than marriage. ⟦s7⟧ Yet certain married persons are not of less merit than certain virgins.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXV. ⟦s2⟧ *Be not made the servants of men.* ⟦s3⟧ Above he said: *Wast thou called being a servant? care not for it* [0526B] [n: (I Cor. VII)]: how then does he here forbid that we be servants of men? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He warns that men be not served for the sake of men: which is done when the hope of salvation is placed in them, as those who said: *I am of Paul, but I of Apollo [n: (I Cor. I)]*.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXVI. ⟦s2⟧ *But he that committeth fornication sinneth against his own body,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ From these words fornication seems graver than the other sins: for where there is greater delight and greater contempt, there is also greater offense. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ A certain delight, from the penalty inflicted for original sin, is present in our members: which although it be greater in delight than the other sins, yet does not seem to be greater than all other sins in guilt or fault. ⟦s6⟧ Likewise they wish to prove by the authority of Jerome that fornication is graver than the other sins. ⟦s7⟧ For he says: [0526C] As much delight as preceded in the deed, so much bitterness of mind ought to follow in the satisfaction, and thus it seems that where there is greater delight, there is greater fault. ⟦s8⟧ Solution. ⟦s9⟧ This, as I judge, is not said in comparison with all the others; but in any kind whatever the quantity of the crime is understood according to the quantity of the delight.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXVII. ⟦s2⟧ *Each one hath his first gift,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how this is true, since there are many to whom nothing has been given whereby they can be saved? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He speaks only of the faithful, of whom there are diverse grades, as continence, marriage, virginity: in which they can be saved, as if he said: He who would not contain, let him descend to the marriage [0526D] granted to him by God, that in it he may be saved.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXVIII. ⟦s2⟧ *For the figure passeth away.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how this is true, since it is written: *The earth standeth for ever [n: (Eccle. I)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He does not say the world passeth away; but the figure of the world, that is, the form and appearance which it now has, will change into a better form: yet if anywhere it be read that the world passeth away, this is to be understood according to form, not according to substance, which will always be.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXIX. ⟦s2⟧ *He that joineth not, doth better.* ⟦s3⟧ On account of a greater aptitude for meriting. ⟦s4⟧ But it is asked how there is a greater aptitude for meriting in virginity than in marriage: since the fight is greater here than there? ⟦s5⟧ For if there is greater difficulty of meriting in marriage, there seems to be a greater reward. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ In virginity, [0527A] with less labor, greater profit, and therefore a greater aptitude for meriting. ⟦s8⟧ In marriage, great labor, and little profit: in which grade it is a great thing at least to stand.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXX. ⟦s2⟧ *But I think that I also have the Spirit of God.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what spirit of God the Apostle says he had, that the Corinthians might acquiesce in his counsel? ⟦s4⟧ For if we say charity, it does not suffice. ⟦s5⟧ For he could have charity, and not knowledge, like very many; if knowledge, it does not suffice: for many have knowledge, but their life does not answer to their words. ⟦s6⟧ Whence the counsel of such can deservedly be reproved. ⟦s7⟧ Solution. ⟦s8⟧ By the Spirit of God he understands both knowledge and charity; because in both the Apostle excelled. ⟦s9⟧ Whence also in his counsel one ought to acquiesce. [0527B]

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXXI. ⟦s2⟧ *Knowledge puffeth up,* etc. [n: (I Cor. VIII.)] Of itself, without charity, not from its own quality; but by occasion, as the law works wrath. ⟦s3⟧ But the same seems able to be said of charity. ⟦s4⟧ For many, remaining in the advance of charity and of the other virtues, sometimes grow proud, and thus fall. ⟦s5⟧ As therefore the occasion of transgressing is taken from knowledge, so it seems to be taken from charity, which is not to be granted. ⟦s6⟧ For from charity no one takes an occasion of evil. ⟦s7⟧ For *it dealeth not perversely; it is not puffed up [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*. ⟦s8⟧ Solution. ⟦s9⟧ Knowledge is such a thing that it puffs up: and yet it is from God, just as riches offer an occasion of evil, and yet are from God; just as also wood is apt for burning, yet is not burned unless fire be applied; [0527C] so also knowledge never puffs up unless the heart of man be kindled. ⟦s10⟧ But can charity ever be so kindled? ⟦s10⟧ For can anyone have charity, and be proud?

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXXII. ⟦s2⟧ *An idol is nothing.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how this is true, since anything artificial is something. ⟦s4⟧ Augustine says: The matter is from God, but the folly of men gave the form. ⟦s5⟧ But again, since it is written: *Every substance, every form, every conjunction, and every joining is made by God:* how is the form of the idol not from God, since it is something? ⟦s6⟧ Origen says: *An idol is nothing,* that is, it has the likeness of no thing that exists. ⟦s7⟧ Or an idol is nothing, that which they think to be the idol, that is, a person made of the image and [0527D] of a presiding spirit: Or (which is better) an idol is nothing in the world, that is, it has no power in the things of the world, to change them for better or worse. ⟦s8⟧ Whence things sacrificed to idols, as far as concerns themselves, it is lawful to eat; but not in the presence of a weak person, lest he do this with veneration of the idol.
