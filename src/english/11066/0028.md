## II. ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS. (cont.)

QUESTION LXXIII. *There is no God but one.* Is not the Father God, and the Son God, and the Holy Spirit God, and the Father is not the Son, or the Holy Spirit? How then is there but one God? Likewise the Father is unbegotten God, and the Son is begotten God, how then is there one God? Solution. There is one God in nature, not in person: but what is the sense of these words, God one in nature, not in person? A great question among the moderns. But to speak what I feel, many seem to me to savor of the Sabellian heresy, being ignorant of the manifold signification of this word, *unus,* or *unum,* or even *singulus,* or [0528A] *substantia.* For these three words are taken otherwise in theology, otherwise in the common usage of speaking, concerning which one must treat by itself.

QUESTION LXXIV. It is asked whether the Son, or the Holy Spirit, is to be called our Father? Solution. Not simply, lest the unbegotten person be understood: but it can be so with such a determination, namely according to the grace of regeneration: for the whole Trinity in common by its grace regenerates us into sons of adoption.

QUESTION LXXV. Likewise it is asked whether God the Trinity is that Father who is eternally Father, or another. [0528B] Solution. Not that one, nor another, but one and the same with the same.

QUESTION LXXVI. It is asked whether the angels are to be called gods, as holy men are called gods, as that: *I have said: You are gods, and all of you sons of the most High [n: (Psal. LXXXI)]*. Solution. The angels are not to be called gods, lest they seem to be worshipped with that service which is called latria. In three ways is one called God: substantively, as the Trinity; by adoption, as the saints; by appellation, as the gods of the nations.

QUESTION LXXVII. *For their conscience, being weak, is defiled.* It is asked how he says that their conscience is defiled: for if they believed themselves to do this for God, namely to eat things sacrificed to idols with veneration, would they not, if they left this off, [0528C] sin, since all that is not of faith is sin? Whence also Paul, if he did not persecute the Church, then when he believed that it was to be persecuted for God, would sin. Solution. Because they did this to a creature, as to the Creator, they had no excuse on account of ignorance: for so perverse and blinded was their mind, that they thought a wooden or stone image to be God, whence fittingly the Apostle says: *Their conscience, being weak, is defiled [n: (I Cor. VIII)]*, not that it is defiled on account of God. Note that it is lawful for a preacher to take necessary things from his subjects, yet let him do this without scandal.

QUESTION LXXVIII. *Doth God take care for oxen?* [0528D] [n: (I Cor. IX.)] Does not God take care of all things? and if of all, how does he not take care of oxen? Solution. One is the care of providence, which is had generally of all things; another is the care of precept, which God does not have concerning oxen. For he does not give precepts to men, that he may teach them how they ought to nourish oxen; for this care he has concerning men alone.

QUESTION LXXIX. *I became to the Jews as a Jew.* It is asked how, as a Jew? or truly a Jew? Solution. He truly used the rites of the Jews, as Augustine says; not by dispensation, as Jerome says. But it would be done by dispensation, if it were evil, and yet were done for a time for the sake of a greater good, namely, that through that feigning they might be converted to the true [0529A] faith of Christ. But those two pillars of the Church were diverse in this: yet we do not say that either of them lied, since to believe this or that is no peril of faith: concerning which controversy it shall be spoken in what follows.

QUESTION LXXX. *All ate the same spiritual food [n: (I Cor. X)]*. It is asked, how the same? Solution. The same in signifying, or the same in effecting. For that food was of the same efficacy as this, as they hold: yet certain sacraments of the Old Testament have their virtue and efficacy from the sacraments of the New Testament: just as from the body of Christ, which is a sacrament, the manna taken by the faithful had its virtue.

QUESTION LXXXI. *Neither let us tempt Christ, as [0529B] some of them.* God tempts, that he may prove; the devil tempts, that he may cast down; man tempts, that he may explore and know. And so temptation is threefold. The first is of proving, which is good, as that of Job. The second is of deceiving, which is the trying of the mind unto deceiving. The third is of distrust and despair, as that of the Jews saying: *Can God prepare a table in the desert? [n: (Psal. LXXVII.)]* It is asked, therefore, how the Jews are said to have tempted Christ, since they seem to have worshipped only God the Father and God the Trinity. Solution. The Jews are therefore said to have tempted Christ, because all the preceding things figured Christ; hence it is that this temptation was rather said to be of Christ than of the Father, although it is not more of the Son than of the Father, although it is so said.

[0529C]QUESTION LXXXII. *And they perished by the destroyer,* that is, by the angel striking them outside the borders of the promised homeland. But if it be asked whether that angel was good or evil, we answer that the evil are sometimes punished by the good, sometimes by the evil; but the good are not punished except by the evil.

QUESTION LXXXIII. *Upon whom the ends of the ages are come.* But were the ages ended at the coming of Christ; but if they are not ended, how is it the end of the ages? Solution. They are ended in us, because, whereas there preceded diverse states of life, in all of which there was a variation and a certain expectation of the coming of Christ, but we expect no other state of life, because between this life and the future there is nothing [0529D] intermediate; but to those who still expect the Messiah, the end of the ages has not yet come, as to the wretched Jews.

QUESTION LXXXIV. *God is faithful, who will not suffer,* etc. It is asked how he says that God does not permit anyone to be tempted above that which he can bear, since many fall from temptation, and are separated from God, and thus are overcome by the devil, and so are tempted more than they can endure. Solution. As long as they are with God, he does not suffer them to be tempted above that which they can. But that some of them yield is because they are unwilling to resist temptation when they can; whence it is just that they be tempted to that degree, their fault demanding it. But God sometimes permits someone to be tempted for the sake of proving, and for the preservation of virtues: which temptation is of providence, [0530A] as with Paul, to whom *there was given a sting of the flesh [n: (II Cor. XII)]*, for the preservation of humility.

QUESTION LXXXV. *The chalice of benediction, which we bless, is it not the communion of the blood of Christ.* It is asked concerning the name of this sacrament, why it is called the Eucharist. Solution. The sacrament of the body and blood of Christ is called, on account of its excellent virtue, the Eucharist, that is, good grace, in which sacrament there is not only an increase of virtue and grace, but he himself is received who is the fountain and origin of all virtue and grace. In which sacrament there are three things: namely, the visible species of bread and wine, and the body and blood of Christ, and spiritual grace. The first is the sacrament of the second, the second is the reality of the first and the sacrament of the third, the third is the virtue of the first and the reality of the second. [0530B] And so the first is only a sacrament, the second is both sacrament and reality, but the third is only reality. The first sacrament therefore has two realities, one signified and contained, namely the true body and blood of Christ; the other signified and not contained, namely the unity of the Church.

QUESTION LXXXVI. It is asked likewise why, after the sacrament of the typical lamb, the Lord gave his disciples the sacrament of his body and blood? Solution. That he might show that the legal sacraments, among which the chief was the sacrament of the Paschal lamb, ought to cease, and that the sacrament of the new law be substituted: among which the Eucharist holds the first place: therefore also that he might imprint this sacrament more closely, [0530C] and commend it more tenaciously to the memory of the disciples.

QUESTION LXXXVII. Then it is asked why he gave this sacrament under another species, and not under its own? Solution. That faith might have merit, which is of invisible things, because faith has no merit when human reason offers proof; and lest the eye shudder at what the hand holds, and lest we be insulted by unbelievers.

QUESTION LXXXVIII. Likewise it is asked why under this species rather than under another. Solution. Because a thing of this species has an express likeness with both realities of this sacrament, because, as bread is from many grains, and wine from many grapes, so the material body of Christ is from many members, and the spiritual from many [0530D] faithful. And as in bread and wine there is a full and principal refreshment of bodies, so in this sacrament there is a full and principal refreshment of souls, because through the eating of the flesh one comes to the taste of the divinity.

QUESTION LXXXIX. Likewise it is asked: since the whole Christ is received under either species, why is it received not under one only, but under a twofold species? Solution. Therefore under two species, that it may be shown that he assumed the whole man, that he might heal the whole man; for he assumed body for the sake of body, soul for the sake of soul, and bread is changed into flesh, and wine into blood. Therefore indeed it is received under two species, that the taking-up of soul and body in [0531A] Christ, and the liberation of both in us, may be signified.

QUESTION XC. Likewise, since flesh and blood are under either species, it is asked whether the substance of either species is changed into the flesh and blood of Christ? Solution. Although either be under either species, yet the substance of bread alone is changed into flesh alone, and the substance of wine alone into the substance of blood alone. Nor ought they to be called two sacraments, but one. Nor is the sacrament therefore said to be repeated, because the benediction is not repeated over the same species. Nor ought other substances to be offered in the sacrifice of truth, because from others the body and blood of Christ cannot be consecrated, but from a grain of wheat reduced into bread, and from wine.

[0531B]QUESTION XCI. It is asked why water is put with wine in the chalice of the Lord. Solution. The water signifies the people: whence neither ought the wine, by which Christ is signified, to be offered without water, because Christ suffered only for the people, nor water without wine in any way; because the people is not redeemed except through Christ.

QUESTION XCII. But it is asked whether the sacrifice becomes void if the water be omitted. Solution. If anyone, not intending to introduce heresy, has omitted the water through forgetfulness or ignorance, it does not seem to be void: whence neither do the Churches of the Greeks add water. But water alone can in no way be offered in sacrifice, nor bread except of grain, that is, of wheat, nor the grain except reduced into bread.

[0531C]QUESTION XCXIII. But it is wont to be asked whether the water with the wine is changed into blood. Solution. Some say that it is changed; but to us it seems that it is not changed: which we received from master Achard.

QUESTION XCIV. It is asked whether Judas received the body of the Lord in the dipped morsel. Solution. Not then, but before with the others.

QUESTION XCV. It is asked concerning the accidents, which remain in species, taste, weight, and form, in what subject they are. Solution. Many doctors agree in this, that they are without a subject, just as the substances of flesh and blood are there without accidents of this kind.
