## II. ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS. (cont.)

QUESTION XCVI. It is wont also to be asked concerning the breaking [0531D] and partition which is there, in what thing it takes place; because there is no other substance than the substance of flesh and blood, which remains whole. Solution. The body of Christ remains whole in itself, and yet is broken and divided in the sacrament.

QUESTION XCVII. It is asked whether the evil man receives the true body of the Lord and the true blood. Solution. He truly receives both, but to his own harm; because unworthily — for he is unworthy who receives otherwise than Christ instituted, or who is in mortal sin.

QUESTION XCVIII. Likewise it is asked whether Christ is immolated daily. Solution. He who was once slain on the altar of the cross is immolated daily in memory of his passion in the sacrament, nor is it repeated from his infirmity, but from ours, who daily sin, and obtain the remission of venial sins, [0532A] and an increase of virtues if we participate worthily. It is objected concerning that which Augustine says, that the good man receives the sacrament and the reality of the sacrament; but the evil man only the sacrament, and not the reality of the sacrament, and it has already been said above that the evil man receives the true body and the true blood: therefore the evil man receives not only the sacrament, but also the reality of the sacrament, which is to be believed without doubt. Solution. In the aforesaid words Augustine calls the sacrament the true body and true blood of Christ; he calls the reality the spiritual grace, which the good man alone and not the evil receives.

QUESTION XCIX. It is asked what kind of body Christ gave to his disciples, mortal or immortal? If mortal, how could it without injury be ground [0532B] or broken by the teeth; if immortal, then he gave such as it was not yet. Solution. Soundly we say that he gave such as he willed, to whom nothing was impossible. Yet some assert that he gave a mortal one, which, as we do not assert, so we do not deny.

QUESTION C. It is asked what is the virtue of this sacrament? Solution. The remission of venial sins, the perfecting of virtues, and it is instituted for the increase of virtues, and as a medicine of daily infirmity.

QUESTION CI. It is asked whether one ought to communicate daily. Solution. Augustine says: to receive the Eucharist daily I neither praise nor blame: yet if anyone is in the disposition of sinning, he is more burdened by receiving than [0532C] purified; and if anyone is gnawed by mortal sin, let him make satisfaction with tears; and if for the rest he have the will of not sinning, let him approach securely.

QUESTION CII. It is wont to be asked whether depraved priests can confect this sacrifice? Solution. Although some be depraved in life, if they are within by name and by sacrament, it is believed that they truly consecrate: but those who are excommunicated, and manifestly marked with heresy, do not seem able to do this. In this sacrament three things must be observed, namely form, order, and intention: the form instituted by the Lord; the order, that he be a priest; the intention, that he intend to do this.

QUESTION CIII. It is asked whether the body of the Lord is touched or received by brute animals? Solution. In no way is it received either by a mouse, or by another little beast.

[0532D]QUESTION CIV. What then does the mouse receive, which eats it? Solution. God knows — perhaps nothing but the accidents which are there.

QUESTION CV. *Eat not for the sake of him who judged, and for conscience' sake,* namely of the weak, etc. It is asked how he says: Eat not for the sake of the unbeliever, or the weak believer, is it not better to eat for the sake of both than to abstain? For if one abstains, the unbeliever is offended. But the weak is rather to be left to himself, as was said above: *He that is weak, let him eat herbs [n: (I Cor. XIV)]*. For in this opinion, he who thinks that thou dost venerate the idol, if thou eatest things consecrated to the idol, he is to be left to himself: for although he judge that thou sinnest by eating, yet thou dost not eat with veneration of the idol, [0533A] nor is he himself, by thinking this, turned away from the faith, but rather by zeal for the faith believes thee to err in this that thou eatest, and therefore is indignant. Solution. It is better to instruct by abstaining him who ate, than by eating to foster him in error. Likewise, if thou shouldst eat in the presence of a weak brother, he himself would be scandalized, and from the scandal would perish. Therefore one ought rather to abstain than to eat.

QUESTION CVI. *Whether you eat, or drink,* etc. It is asked how it can be fulfilled, that we do all things to the glory of God, since we do many things naturally, which we do not do for this, that we may please God? Solution. Let all our works be done so circumspectly, that nothing be done against God.

QUESTION CVII. *As I also please all men in all things.* Elsewhere he says: *If I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ [0533B] [n: (Galat. I)]*. Was he, pleasing all men in all things, not the servant of Christ? Solution. That which he says: If I yet pleased men, is understood thus: If I pleased men so far as in me lies, so as to put the cause and end in man, I should not be the servant of Christ. But he who pleases for the sake of the truth, not he himself, but rather the truth itself pleases.

QUESTION CVIII. *If any man seem to be contentious, we have no such custom, neither the Church of God.* Here we have authority that the customs of holy Church are to be held, even if we are ignorant of the reason why they were thus established by her: for God does not permit his Church to err in those things which pertain to himself. Note: The Church is called the convocation of the faithful, and the house in which one assembles to receive the Eucharist, [0533C] not only for the commemoration of the death of Christ, but also that we may be conformed to Christ in morals and life.

QUESTION CIX. *The man is the image and glory of God, but the woman is the image and glory of the man,* etc. It is asked how the man is the image of God and not the woman, since in Genesis it is written of both, that they were made to the image of God? [n: (Gen. I.)] Solution. One is the image common to man and woman, of which it is treated in Genesis; another is that which befits the man alone and not the woman. The first consists in the natural power of knowing God; the second is understood in this, that, as from God are all things, so from one man are all men. Or it is to be taken morally: that by the man reason be understood; by [0533D] the woman, sensuality, and according to this the man and not the woman is the image of God.

QUESTION CX. *There must be heresies,* etc. Concerning the authority of the Church. It is asked who is to be called a heretic? Solution. A heretic properly is one who, for the sake of some temporal advantage, and especially of glory, or of his own principality, invents false and new and depraved sects, alien from the truth, or holds, follows, and defends sects invented by others.

QUESTION CXI. *But let a man prove himself,* etc. If anyone is in mortal sin, let him not approach, but say: *Lord, I am not worthy that thou shouldst enter under my roof [n: (Matth. VIII)]*. What then is it for anyone to prove himself, but to see whether his conscience gnaws at him concerning a mortal sin, and whether he is in the purpose of remaining still in [0534A] sin? But the priest ought to admonish him who is in mortal sin, though not manifest, not to approach; yet he cannot withdraw communion from him. But if public report or a crime accuse him, let him in no way approach, lest the priest give it to him. For if, either for favor of him, or convicted by money, he gives to such a one, so far as in him lies, he kills Christ: which is greatly to be feared.

QUESTION CXII. *Now there are diversities of graces, but the same Spirit,* etc. [n: (I Cor. XII.)] Since the works of the Trinity are undivided, it is asked why he attributes graces to the Holy Spirit, ministrations to the Son, operations to the Father? Solution. In grace especially appears benignity, which pertains to the property of the Holy Spirit: therefore he attributes graces to the Holy Spirit. In ministrations [0534B] indeed wisdom shines, which is wont to be referred to the Son. In operations, power, which is specially of the Father: therefore he refers operations to the authority of the Father.

QUESTION CXIII. *To one is given the word of wisdom,* etc. It is to be noted that wisdom and knowledge are taken here otherwise than there: *O the depth of the riches of the knowledge and wisdom of God [n: (Rom. XI)]*. For when he says: O the depth of the riches of the knowledge and wisdom of God, by wisdom and knowledge he calls the divine essence. But when he says, to one is given the word of wisdom; *to another the word of knowledge,* by wisdom he calls the knowledge of eternal things, but by knowledge, the knowledge of human things.

QUESTION CXIV. *All these things one and the same Spirit worketh.* It is asked why the Father is not called gift, [0534C] as the Son or the Holy Spirit, since he gives himself, as also do the other persons: for to no one is the Son or the Holy Spirit given without the Father. Solution. On account of the authority of the origin, lest he be understood to be from another, who is from none.

QUESTION CXV. *For as one body,* etc. It is asked whether the good alone participate in these gifts? Or also the evil? But that even the evil have these gifts of the Spirit is plain from that which is read in the Gospel, that they will say to the Lord in the day of judgment: *Lord, have we not prophesied in thy name, and cast out devils? [n: (Matth. VII)]* to whom the Lord will say: *Amen I say to you, I know you not,* etc. [n: (Matth. XXV.)] Likewise John the evangelist, when he saw a certain man, who did not follow the Lord, casting out devils in the name [0534D] of Christ, wished to forbid him; to whom the Lord said: *Forbid not; he that is not with me is against me [n: (Marc. IX)]*. From which it is plain that the evil also often use these gifts, and thus it seems that they are of the body of Christ, and are his members; but the same, since they are evil, are members of the devil. Solution. The Apostle does not say that all having the gifts of the Holy Spirit are established in the unity of the body; or the Church is taken broadly, namely the multitude of all participating in the sacraments of the Church. Among whom are certain rotten members, and many grains with chaff, which are said to be in the body, but not of the body. Whence John: *They went out from us, but they were not of us [n: (I Joan. II)]*. Note that the Apostle says all the members of the body, since they are many, are one body. This I say on account of [0535A] certain ones unwilling to concede that all the parts of some whole joined together receive the name of the whole, since it is also said in the creed of Athanasius, soul and flesh are one man.
