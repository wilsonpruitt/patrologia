## II. ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS. (cont.)

QUESTION CXXXVIII. It is likewise wont to be asked, since man before sin was said to be mortal, and in a certain manner immortal, whether he had both from nature, or neither, or one only. Solution. Saving the reverence due to hidden things, and without prejudice to a better opinion, we say that man before sin was naturally mortal and passible; [0542B] but by the benefit of the tree of life he would become immortal: whence the doctors do not say simply that he was then immortal, but they add "in a certain manner," and determine in what manner; but mortal they pronounce him simply to have been, according to this that is said: The first man was made into a living soul, that is, into a soul which should quicken and vivify the body, not so as to have no need of foods.

QUESTION CXXXIX. *Now this I say, brethren, that flesh and blood cannot possess the kingdom of God; neither shall corruption possess incorruption.* It is asked therefore what bodies we are going to have. Solution. The same bodies which we now have we shall have after the resurrection, but changed not according to substance, but [0542C] according to quality: these dissoluble, but those indissoluble; but those of the good impassible, but those of the wicked passible: whence they will be as if in perpetual death. But that they will be indissoluble, the Apostle teaches, saying: Flesh and blood shall not possess the kingdom of God [n: (Ibid.)]. But that they are going to have impassible bodies, the Apostle intimates, saying: *but we shall all be changed [n: (Ibid.)]*.

QUESTION CXL. *For the trumpet shall sound,* etc. It is asked, what is signified by the name of the trumpet? Solution. The doctors say that the Apostle so calls some evident and manifest sign, by which the mystery of the future resurrection will be fulfilled: which trumpet is elsewhere called a cry, elsewhere the voice of an archangel or the voice of Christ.

[0542D]QUESTION CXLI. It is asked also concerning the voice of the trumpet, whether it will be material? Solution. It is manifest that there will be a material voice made by the ministry of an angel; because as by the trumpet the people of the Jews was called together to a feast or to war, so then they will be called to judgment; or some evident sign, by which the same is done that is wont to be done by the voice.

QUESTION CXLII. *Death is swallowed up in victory.* It is asked, what death, and in what victory it is thus swallowed up? For by the name of death is signified sometimes the devil, who is the author of death, sometimes sin, which separates from God, sometimes the dissolution of soul and body. Solution. It can well be understood both of the devil, and of sin, and of the dissolution of soul and body. It is established that in the victory of the Lord's resurrection the devil [0543A] is swallowed up: that he may not domineer, as before, when by the fear of death men were driven to any crime whatever; but now the saints despise death: but chiefly in the future general resurrection of all shall all death be swallowed up, when this mortal shall put on incorruption.

QUESTION CXLIII. *And the strength of sin is the law.* How this is to be understood may easily be weighed from those things which were said upon the Epistle to the Romans: for the law being given, both carnal concupiscence grew strong, and transgression came on. For the law by forbidding increases concupiscence, unless the Holy Spirit infuse charity.

QUESTION CXLIV. *Therefore, brethren, be ye steadfast and unmovable.* It is asked in what these two differ. Solution. [0543B] Steadfast in faith, lest of themselves they be moved by the foot of pride; unmovable in temptations, lest by the impelling hand of sins they forsake the faith. There are three kinds of temptations: one violent, another fraudulent, the third violent and fraudulent. The first is done through open persecutions; the second through false brethren and heretics; the third will be done through Antichrist. But every [0544A] kind of temptation the devil sends: whence he is also called a lion, by raging openly, and a dragon, by seducing secretly and stealthily: whence it is written: *Under his tongue is labour and sorrow [n: (Psal. X)]*.

QUESTION CXLV. *Concerning Apollo, I make known to you, that I greatly besought him to come unto you, but it was not his will to come now [n: (I Cor. XVI)]*. It is asked which of the two acted unreasonably — whether Paul in asking that he should go, or Apollo in not consenting? Because it seems either that this one did not ask what was fitting, or that the other omitted what he ought to have done. Solution. It is true that both acted reasonably, because the Apostle asked that he should do this; whence Apollo, because he was thus asked, saw that the Apostle's petition should rather be set aside, wherefore [0544B] he did not consent.

QUESTION CXLVI. *Salute one another with a holy kiss.* It is asked why he added, "holy"? Solution. There is a kiss of wantonness; there is a kiss of betrayal, as of Judas; there is a kiss of holiness and concord, so that what is within may appear outwardly, namely the bond of charity, etc. Of this therefore he speaks, that he may exclude the rest.
