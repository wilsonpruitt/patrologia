## III. ON THE SECOND EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIII. ⟦s2⟧ *Who hath given us the pledge of the Spirit,* etc. [n: (II Cor. V)]. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how the Holy Spirit is called a pledge, and of what thing he is the earnest? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The Holy Spirit is love, and from the love which we have toward God, we are certain of his promise; and because we have this certitude from the Holy Spirit, therefore the Holy Spirit is given to us by God as it were an earnest and a pledge. ⟦s6⟧ Now a pledge is the certitude of a thing believed, or promised, or to be believed. [0547D] *Knowing therefore the fear of the Lord, we persuade men.* ⟦s7⟧ Fear is divided into five species, as was already said in the Epistle to the Romans, nor is there need to go back over it.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIV. ⟦s2⟧ *For whether we be transported in mind,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what are the excesses of mind. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ There are two excesses, either dread, or straining toward things above: so that in a certain manner lower things slip from the memory. ⟦s6⟧ In this excess of mind were all the saints: to whom the secret things of God, surpassing the world, were revealed.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XV. ⟦s2⟧ *Christ died for all.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how Christ died for all: since his death does not profit all: for it does not profit those to be damned, but only the elect. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. [0548A] According to Jerome it is thus understood, for all who are to be saved. ⟦s5⟧ For universality is sometimes restricted, and this in many ways. ⟦s6⟧ For sometimes the universal sign gathers the individuals of the kinds, sometimes the kinds of the individuals, sometimes neither, but the greater or the more worthy part of the universality itself. ⟦s7⟧ Or according to Augustine, Christ died for all, because his death merited this, that through him all should be saved, unless it remained in themselves: for it was sufficient for the salvation of all.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVI. ⟦s2⟧ *God was in Christ, reconciling the world to himself.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how the Father is said to be in the Son, or the Son in the Father: or how that [0548B] is to be understood: *He that seeth me, seeth the Father also [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The one is said to be or to be seen in the other for this reason, that their substance is naturally one. ⟦s6⟧ Which I add, that I may exclude the Sabellian heresy. ⟦s7⟧ There is unity where there is no diversity, but in every way indifference, and equality, and identity. ⟦s8⟧ Whence Hilary says: The Father is seen in the Son on account of the united likeness of nature: for so do we detest the pest of the Arians, that we no less execrate the madness of the Sabellians; so do we confess God to be three, that he is one, and so one, that he is three. ⟦s9⟧ For many in our days are Sabellians as to their understanding, who confess three persons: but when it is said that the three persons are one substance, they understand nothing else than what Sabellius understood: [0548C] which comes about because they do not attend to the manifold signification of this name, *substance.* ⟦s10⟧ For Hilary says that when it is said: The Father and the Son are one substance, such a manner of speaking has both the conscience of faith, and a fraud ready at hand. ⟦s11⟧ Then he opens up both, saying: If you signify the Father and the Son to be a singular God, the understanding is false; but if you say that the Father and the Son are one substance, or one simply, so as to understand one, equal and indifferent, through all things equal, in no part dissimilar, the understanding is true. ⟦s12⟧ By which words he most manifestly distinguishes between personal unity and natural unity: for the Father and the Son are one in nature, not in person.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0548D]QUESTION XVII. ⟦s2⟧ *Behold, now is the day of salvation,* etc. [n: (III Cor. VI)]. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked why the time of grace is called the day of salvation, since also in the time of the natural and written law many were saved? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ This time of grace is called the day of salvation for this reason, that in this time the victim was offered, through which alone entrance lies open into the kingdom, through which also those who went before merited salvation. ⟦s6⟧ Whence also it is called the time of grace, on account of the greater powers given to us through the manifestation of faith and love: whence also the powers of the devil are diminished, and because now all things are done freely, not for the sake of any earthly advantage, and because those things which in other times were promised are in this time fulfilled.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [0549A]QUESTION XVIII. ⟦s3⟧ *In charity unfeigned,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ It is asked what charity is called feigned. ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ That which does not persevere, or which is not sufficient for salvation, or that which is simulated, namely some sign of love shown outwardly, when within it is not in the heart, and this charity is not charity.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIX. ⟦s2⟧ *As dying, and behold we live.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked, in view of these words, how the yoke of the Lord is sweet, and the burden light, since the saints suffer so many and so great hard and difficult things, and how those laboring and burdened, coming to him, find rest, since they seem to pass not from labor to rest, but rather from rest to labor. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ To the saints sustaining grievous and harsh things there is present the Holy Spirit, who in the corruption of the outward man renews the inward man, [0549B] from day to day, and, spiritual rest being tasted, by the hope of future beatitude relieves all harsh things, and thus in so many hard things the burden of Christ is lighter. ⟦s6⟧ For the love of God and of our Lord Jesus Christ makes all savage and monstrous things easy and well-nigh nothing.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XX. ⟦s2⟧ *That there may be an equality, as it is written: He that had much had nothing over,* etc. [n: (II Cor. VIII.)] It is asked whether the lesser, who (as it were provincials) minister stipends to the soldiers of Christ, are equal to them in merits: which the Apostle seems to mean, saying, that there may be an equality. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ That equality is not of piety, but because both sustain, and are sustained by, one another. ⟦s5⟧ For the lesser sustain the greater in carnal things, and are sustained in spiritual things. ⟦s6⟧ And the greater sustain the lesser, that is, the spiritual the carnal, in [0549C] spiritual things, and are sustained in carnal things by the same.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXI. ⟦s2⟧ *For we provide good things not only before God,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how the Apostle divides, namely before God, and before men, since it cannot be done before God, unless it be also done before men? ⟦s4⟧ Yet the outward work is not always required, namely when the faculty is lacking. ⟦s5⟧ But it is always required that the conscience be clean. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ That the Apostle may show that the conscience cannot be clean, unless good things also be provided before men, therefore he distinguishes between these two.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXII. ⟦s2+3⟧ It is asked: What is it to provide good things before God? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ So to dispose the mind, that [0549D] nothing be done against God, which cannot be done, unless the scandal of the brethren be avoided outwardly; or therefore he divides between the aforesaid, that he may show that there are certain things which, as far as pertains to God, are lawful to be done, because in themselves they are good, although it seem otherwise to men, and therefore they can be passed over. ⟦s6⟧ What therefore is expedient and fitting, let that be done which is expedient for us unto merit, which is fitting for an example to the rest. ⟦s7⟧ Therefore for the sake of conscience we ought to provide good things before God. ⟦s8⟧ For the sake of reputation we provide good things also before men: for he who, trusting in his conscience, neglects his reputation, is cruel, which he does who cares not whether what he does please or displease, and for the scandal of the brethren omits nothing.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXIII. ⟦s2⟧ *He who soweth sparingly, shall also reap sparingly.* [0550A] [n: (II Cor. IX.)] It is asked concerning the poor, who sow sparingly, or nothing, whether they too shall therefore reap sparingly. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ He does not sow sparingly who bestows little, if the mind be ready to give, if he had more. ⟦s5⟧ He therefore is to be said to sow sparingly who has little love, whether he give more or less: and this man shall reap sparingly, that is, he shall receive a small retribution unto life eternal.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXIV. ⟦s2⟧ *For we dare not insert ourselves,* etc. [n: (II Cor. II.)] That is, we do not usurp to ourselves power, but we use the power given to us by God. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked therefore what it is to usurp power. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He usurps to himself power who, not elected, or not called, takes to himself honor, who thrusts himself in, and, power not being received, wishes to domineer.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0550B]QUESTION XXV. ⟦s2⟧ *But we will not glory beyond measure.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked: What is it to glory beyond measure? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ To glory more than one ought, and in that in which one ought not, which he does who extends himself into that to which he has no right. ⟦s6⟧ But to abuse power is to flatter, and to caress the vices of sinners. ⟦s7⟧ To use power is to reprove sinners, and to do the other things which pertain to edification.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXVI. ⟦s2⟧ *He that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what it is to glory in the Lord, since elsewhere he says: *God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ [n: (Galat. VI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And that: *Not only do we glory in the hope of the sons of God, but also in our tribulations [n: (Rom. V)]*. ⟦s5⟧ Is it the same to glory in the Lord, and in the cross of the Lord, and in tribulations? [0550C] What is it to glory? ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ To glory is to rejoice in praise, and to judge oneself worthy of glory. ⟦s8⟧ To glory in the Lord is to attribute all confidence not to oneself, but to the Lord as author, and to exult in Christ with spiritual joy. ⟦s9⟧ To glory in the cross of the Lord can be understood in two ways. ⟦s10⟧ For he is rightly said to glory in the cross of the Lord who with joy, and with hope of the future life, imitates the passion of the Lord, and this is to glory in tribulations. ⟦s11⟧ Someone also is said to glory in the cross of the Lord who does not judge himself worthy of salvation, except through the merit of the Lord's passion, saying with the apostle Peter: *Because there is no other name under heaven whereby we must be saved [n: (Act. IV)]*: these things I say without prejudice to a better opinion.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0550D]QUESTION XXVII. ⟦s2⟧ *For I am jealous of you with the jealousy of God,* etc. [n: (II Cor. XI.)] It is asked: What is jealousy? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Jealousy is a motion of the mind toward good or toward evil on account of another's state. ⟦s5⟧ When it is toward good, then it is of love; when it is toward evil, then it is of envy.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXVIII. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked likewise: What is it to be jealous with the jealousy of God? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ To love unto the honor of God, or with that jealousy which God inspires.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXIX. ⟦s2⟧ *For I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you a chaste virgin to Christ.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked concerning this espousal of the Apostle, since not all who are in the Church are virgins, as the married, how then can he present those who are not virgins as a virgin, as it were one, and moreover chaste? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Virginity is twofold: [0551A] of body and of mind: the virginity of the flesh is the body untouched; the virginity of the mind is the incorrupt integrity of the inward man. ⟦s6⟧ But this is required of every believer, without which that which is of the flesh does not profit. ⟦s7⟧ But all are called one virgin on account of the unity of entire faith, of solid hope, of sincere love. ⟦s8⟧ Chaste, not having the heat of an evil will.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXX. ⟦s2⟧ *Satan transformeth himself into an angel of light,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether it is perilous to believe Satan to be an angel of light, when he says or does those things which befit the good. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ If, at the time when he says or does those things which are fitting, there is no error, it is not perilous; but when he begins to lead in his deceits, that no one may go after him, there is need of vigilance: which is not done without God.
