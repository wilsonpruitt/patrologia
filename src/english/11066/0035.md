## III. ON THE SECOND EPISTLE TO THE CORINTHIANS. (cont.)

[0551B]QUESTION XXXI. [ *In hunger, and thirst,* etc. It is asked where is the promise of God who says: *First seek the kingdom of God, and all these things shall be added unto you [n: (Matth. VI)]*. For the promise seems to have wavered, since the Apostle says that he labored in hunger, and thirst, in cold and nakedness. Solution. That physician, to whom we have once committed ourselves wholly, and from whom we have the promise of the present life and of the future, knows when to apply these aids, or to withdraw them, as he judges to be expedient for us?

QUESTION XXXII. *Through a window in the gate by the brethren.* It is asked whether this deed was praiseworthy, or worthy of reproach: which seems [so] to some, because he was not freed by the help of God. Solution. The help of God is not needed before human aid has failed; nor ought anyone to await [0551C] the help of God, while he has something he can do, lest he seem to tempt God.

QUESTION XXXIII. *And thus I escaped his hands.* It is asked whether the Apostle acted, in fleeing, as a good shepherd, or as a hireling. Did he not, when the wolf came, desert the sheep, and flee? and thus it seems that he was not a good shepherd, but a hireling. Solution. When any shepherd is specially sought by persecutors, it is permitted him to give way, and to avoid the fury of the persecutors, and by fleeing to preserve himself for the benefit of the whole flock, and meanwhile let the others, who are not so sought after, furnish food to their fellow-servants. But when a common peril presses upon all, let those, who need others, not be deserted by those whom they need.

QUESTION XXXIV. *Whether in the body, or out of the body, [0551D] I know not, God knoweth,* etc. [n: (II Cor. XII.)] It is asked how the Apostle could have doubted whether he was caught up in the body, or out of the body, since no one existing in this life can see God as he is; whence he says to Moses: *Man shall not see me, and live [n: (Exod. XXXIII)]*. Likewise, if he was caught up out of the body, namely so that the soul was separated from the body, was his body meanwhile dead? Likewise, if he saw God with an intellectual vision, then he saw him truly, and had that knowledge in which is the highest beatitude, and thus he was in beatitude; but beatitude once had is never lost; or if God took from him what he had already received, it seems that God acted unjustly. Solution. Either could have happened: either that the soul separated from the body [0552A] saw God in itself, or that the soul, free from the senses of the body, contemplated God thus in the body itself; and since either of these could rightly have happened, the Apostle, in doubt, says whether in the body, or out of the body. Whence, whichever of these it was, that authority will be preserved: *Man shall not see me, and live,* because in either way he could have put off the man. But if he was out of the body, then the body was dead, and separated from the soul, and again quickened when the soul returned. And since he had full knowledge of God, and thus was in beatitude, if again the same glory was withdrawn from him, it is not to be wondered at, nor incredible, because thus God could show beforehand to his most beloved servant the beatitude and joy to come, which he was to receive, [0552B] that he might endure more firmly in his love and service, and desire more vehemently the very time of receiving it: just as, transfigured on the mountain, he showed the glory of his humanity to his three disciples.

QUESTION XXXV. But by the third heaven, and paradise, into which he was caught up, he understands the same thing, namely the full understanding, or knowledge, of the divinity. Note the fourfold opinion concerning the three heavens. According to the first opinion, the first heaven is the airy, whence [are] the birds of heaven. The second is the firmament, whence also he called the firmament heaven. The third is the empyrean, which as soon as it was made was filled with angels, where the angels, and the holy souls, enjoy the contemplation of God. According to the second opinion, the first heaven is corporeal vision, by which [0552C] heaven, and earth, and all things visible to the eyes are discerned. By the same vision certain things are sometimes seen by the gift of God, as Elisha [saw] the fiery chariots [n: (IV Reg. II)], and Balthasar the hand writing on the wall, *mane, thecel, phares [n: (Dan. V)]*. The second heaven is imaginary, or spiritual, vision, by which are seen not bodies, but the images of them: as is wont [to happen] in dreams, or in ecstasy, as Pharaoh [saw] the ears of grain [n: (Gen. XLI)], and Peter the vessel [n: (Act. X)]. The third is intellectual vision, by which neither bodies, nor images of bodies are seen, but incorporeal and immaterial things are beheld by the instinct of the mind, as substance, the Godhead, and every affection of the soul. The third opinion calls the three heavens the threefold hierarchy of angels according to Dionysius. The first, in the ascent, is that which contains the angels, and archangels, [0552D] and virtues. The second, powers, principalities, and dominions; the third, thrones, and cherubim, and seraphim. This third hierarchy, therefore, in the ascent, and the first in the descent, the Apostle calls the third heaven, or paradise: to which, when he says he was caught up, he shows that he saw God immediately, face to face. The fourth opinion is, that the first heaven is called the knowledge of heavenly bodies, the second [the knowledge] of heavenly spirits, the third the knowledge of the Godhead.

QUESTION XXXVI. *I know such a man,* etc. It is asked how he says a man was caught up whether in the body, or out of it, since man subsists in body and soul, how then does he say that a man can be caught up out of the body. Solution. The truth of man consists there, where is [0553A] the image, and likeness of God. Whence [comes] that saying: The mind of each is the man himself. By the name of man, therefore, he calls the inner man.

QUESTION XXXVII. *There was given to me a thorn of my flesh, an angel,* etc. It is asked by whom this thorn was given to him, whether by God, or by the devil. If by God, how is it called an angel of Satan, as though sent by him? Likewise, if sent by the devil, how is that true which follows: *Lest the greatness of the revelations exalt [me],* etc. Did Satan then buffet the Apostle through an angel sent by himself, lest he be exalted into pride? Solution. Both by God, and by the devil, was he sent who vexed the Apostle, but for one purpose by God, and for another by the devil. By God he was sent for this, lest the greatness of the revelations exalt him. By the devil, for this, that he might drag him to [0553B] failing. By God also he was sent for this, that virtue might be perfected in weakness.

QUESTION XXXVIII. *For which thing I besought the Lord thrice,* etc. The devil sought to tempt Job, and was heard [n: (Job I)]. The Apostle asked that the angel of Satan might depart from him, and was not heard. Where then is divine justice? Was it just that the devil be heard, and not the Apostle? Solution. Those whom God has purposed to heal, he does not always hear according to their will, but according to their health. But some, in wrath, he sometimes hears according to their will, as the devil.

QUESTION XXXIX. Likewise, since the Apostle knew that this infirmity was given him for his profit, and for the preservation of humility, it is asked whether he reasonably prayed [0553C] that such a temptation might depart from him. Solution. Although he knew this, yet as a man he feared a fall from the affliction, [0554A] and so from fear he was humbled; but humility drove out the disease of proud elation, and by this dispensation of divine providence, that thorn was given him.

QUESTION XL. *And they did not do penance,* etc. It is asked whether penance is necessary for one amending his morals for the better; and in what ways penance is performed. Solution. It does not suffice to change one's morals for the better, and to withdraw from evils, unless by the sorrow of penance, and the groaning of humility, and the sacrifice of a contrite heart, satisfaction be made for the fault. Likewise it is to be noted that penance is performed in three ways: before baptism, and after baptism, for the graver [sins], and daily for the lighter and more frequent, according to that saying: You have avoided great things, see that you be not crushed by the sand.

QUESTION XLI. *We pray God, that you do no evil* [0554B] [n: (II Cor. XIII)]. Here the Apostle intimates that only by the grace of God is one turned aside from evil, saying: We pray, etc. For outward planting and watering avail nothing without the inward increase: which God gives by grace alone. It is asked, then, why in the sacred Scriptures we are often commanded both to turn aside from evil, and to do good: since free will is sufficient for neither of these, since it is the work of grace alone, proper to it, both this and that. Solution. The will does something, but does not do it alone. Therefore when it is commanded that this or that be done, we ought to recognize free will; but when prayer is made, the benefit of grace is sought. Concerning grace and free will it has already been said in what is above, and likewise what grace works of itself without the will, [0554C] and what it does not work without it; nor is there need that the same things be repeated again.
