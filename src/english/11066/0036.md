## IV. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE GALATIANS.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0553]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0553C] *[n: (Galat. I.)] Paul the apostle,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This Epistle the Apostle sends to the Galatians: who, coming from Gaul, mingled themselves with the Greeks in a certain province of Greece. ⟦s3⟧ Whence that province was first called Gallograecia, then Galatia. ⟦s4⟧ Whence, since the Greeks are of sharp intellect, these [being] foolish, and slower to understand, are held to be like the unteachable Gauls. ⟦s5⟧ These were first instructed by the Apostle in the faith, [0553D] and in evangelical doctrine, [and] afterwards were subverted in many ways by false apostles, so that they believed the grace of Christ did not suffice for salvation without the law of Moses. ⟦s6⟧ Whence it is plain what the special matter is, namely the state of the Galatians in which they then were; but the general matter [is] the common state of the Church. ⟦s7⟧ But the intention of the Apostle in this Epistle is to recall the Galatians, deceived by the tricks of the false [apostles], to the truth of the catholic faith and of evangelical doctrine. ⟦s8⟧ The manner [is] such: he prefaces a greeting, where against the detractors, and those glorying in the works of the law, he briefly touches upon his own dignity, and the grace of Christ: commending his own person, when the false [apostles] disparaged [him]. ⟦s9⟧ After the salutation he reproves them for their fickleness: afterwards he commends his own person more fully. ⟦s10⟧ Then he disproves the law of Moses: [0554C] teaching that it is not to be kept after Christ, because not only does it not profit unto salvation and justice, but even hinders. ⟦s11⟧ Afterwards he commends the Gospel and the faith of Christ, which suffices for salvation.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION I. *Who gave himself for our sins, that he might deliver us from the present world,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Since the world or age is the work of God, who is the author of good things [0554D] only, it is asked here how the whole world is set in evil, or how the age is called wicked. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Not only places, but also times and instruments of evils contract the infamy of those [evils] which are done in them: whence [there are] evil days, and perilous times [so] called. ⟦s5⟧ Woodland passes too, full of robbers, are called evil; and the sword, by which blood is shed, and the cup, by which poison is administered; and thus the world, or age, obtains the name of wickedness on account of the things which are done in it.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION II. ⟦s2⟧ *But although we, or an angel, preach to you beside that,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Were there not many things which he had not yet preached to them, which are to be believed and held by little ones, who are to be nourished with the milk of simple doctrine? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He does not say more than you have received, but beside [0555A] that, he says, by which he understands the contrary. ⟦s6⟧ Whence he promised he would come to some, that he might fulfill the things that were lacking to them.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION III. ⟦s2⟧ *If I yet pleased men,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ The question, which is wont to be raised here, has been resolved in the first Epistle to the Corinthians. ⟦s4⟧ But someone will say: Whatever has been said elsewhere, I would like to hear how both are true, *if I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ;* and that: *Please all men in all things [n: (I Cor. X)]*. ⟦s5⟧ He himself does not wish to please men, that he may be the servant of Christ. ⟦s6⟧ But to us he commands that we please all men. ⟦s7⟧ Does he not wish us not to be servants of Christ, since we cannot [do] both, both please them, and be servants of Christ? ⟦s8⟧ Whence it is written: *He hath scattered the bones of them that please men* [0555B] [n: (Psal. XXV)]. ⟦s9⟧ The same question arises from the diverse words of the Lord. ⟦s10⟧ For in one place he says: *Let your light shine before men [n: (Matth. V)]*; and in another: *Do not your justice before men [n: (Matth. VI)]*. ⟦s11⟧ Solution. ⟦s12⟧ We are admonished nothing else, whether by the words of the Lord, or by the words of the Apostle, save that we place not the end of good works in the praise of men, and that we not desire the same as it were for the reward of good works.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION IV. ⟦s2⟧ *I persecuted the Church of God,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether the Apostle sinned by persecuting the Church of God, since he had zeal for the law, and in doing this believed it ought to be done for God's sake. ⟦s4⟧ For everyone is bound to do that which conscience dictates [0555C] ought to be done for God's sake, and so if he did not do it, he seems to offend God by contempt. ⟦s5⟧ Likewise, who would doubt that to persecute the Church is evil? ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ Some say that, whether he did this or that, he would sin. ⟦s8⟧ But others say that the zeal, which the Apostle had, was good, but that work was evil, and of error, namely the persecution of the Church.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION V. *But I saw none other of the apostles, save James the brother of the Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ Why James the less, son of Alphaeus, is called the brother of the Lord is wont to be asked. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4+5⟧ Some say that he was called the brother of the Lord because he was the son of Joseph by another wife, who was thought to be the father of the Lord: but this is not established, since Joseph was believed to be a virgin; another solution therefore is to be sought. [0555D] It is to be known, then, that Mary the mother of the Lord was the daughter of Joachim and Anna, who married Joseph, and so Joseph was the putative father of the Lord. ⟦s6⟧ But when Joachim was dead, Cleophas the brother of Joseph took the same Anna to wife, and begot of her a daughter, whom he called Mary, who married Alphaeus, who begot sons, namely James, Joseph, Simon, and Jude. ⟦s7⟧ But when Cleophas was dead, a certain Salomas married the same Anna, and begot of her a daughter by name Mary, who married Zebedee: and had of him sons, James, who was called the Greater, and John the evangelist. ⟦s8⟧ Three husbands, therefore, Anna had, and three daughters. ⟦s9⟧ Now it is to be seen why James [son] of Alphaeus was also called the less, [and] the brother of the Lord. ⟦s10⟧ He was called the less in comparison with the other, who first clung to the Lord, [0556A] and therefore was called the greater, not according to the time of birth, but of conversion. ⟦s11⟧ But he was called the brother of the Lord, according to some, because he was the son of his maternal aunt, or on account of the likeness of his sanctity, or rather because he was the nephew of the paternal uncle of Christ, that is, of Cleophas. ⟦s12⟧ For the Hebrews call those joined by natural consanguinity on the fathers' side brothers. ⟦s13⟧ It is to be noted that in the Scriptures men are called brothers in four ways. ⟦s14⟧ By nature, as Esau and Jacob; by nation, as all the Jews are called brothers among themselves; by kinship, as all those who are of the same family; when from one root a multitude spreads out, as Scripture calls Abraham, and Lot, and Jacob, and Laban brothers. ⟦s15⟧ By affection, all Christians [are brothers], partakers of the same grace, having the same heavenly Father.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0556B]QUESTION VI. ⟦s2⟧ *Thou compellest the Gentiles to judaize [n: (Galat. II)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ Concerning this rebuke it is wont to be asked: was it true, or dispensatory, and did Peter sin, and was he truly reprehensible? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ In the answering of this question those two illustrious lights, Jerome and Augustine, seem to disagree. ⟦s6⟧ Jerome says that that rebuke was dispensatory, and not true, and that Peter did not sin, nor was reprehensible. ⟦s7⟧ But Augustine asserts that the rebuke was true, and not feigned, and that Peter was truly reprehensible; nor did he walk according to the truth of the Gospel; not in this, that he became weak to the weak, but because by his example he compelled the Gentiles to judaize: otherwise the consequence will be that Paul wrote what was false, which is by no means to be believed. [0556C] Likewise concerning the abolition of the legal [observances] after Christ, the same two masters nonetheless do not think the same. ⟦s8⟧ For Jerome says that after Christ those legal observances are deadly. ⟦s9⟧ Augustine says that it was permitted the Jews then, in the primitive Church, to observe them, only let them not put hope in them. ⟦s10⟧ For before the coming of Christ they seem to have been necessary; on the very border of law and grace [they were] indifferent, if hope be not put in them; but now they are deadly. ⟦s11⟧ Note that a dispensation is the concession of an inferior state for the sake of avoiding scandal, in which a lesser evil is done, that a greater may be avoided. ⟦s12⟧ Saving the reverence of [these] secret [matters], we prefer the opinion of blessed Augustine to the opinion of blessed Jerome upon the aforesaid rebuke and abolition of the law. [0556D] Whence to the objections of blessed Jerome we answer thus. ⟦s13⟧ The first is: Christ is the end of the law, that is, the consummation and fullness fulfilling the law, and consummating [it] in himself, and in his own. ⟦s14⟧ Yet not so that through no interval of time could the law, after the coming of Christ, lawfully be kept by anyone. ⟦s15⟧ Likewise, *the law and the prophets until John.* ⟦s16⟧ Venerable old man Jerome, answer me with pure understanding, how is this to be understood, the law and the prophets until John? ⟦s17⟧ Is it thus, that after John it was not lawful to keep the law? ⟦s18⟧ Which seems [to follow] according to your disputation. ⟦s19⟧ But did not Christ even after John keep the law, celebrating the old pasch? ⟦s20⟧ Did Christ do what was not fitting? ⟦s21⟧ It is therefore to be understood thus: *the law and the prophets until John,* that is, from John the grace of the New Testament [0557A] began both to be preached and to be shown forth: and from then the Old Testament began to cease. ⟦s22⟧ Likewise at that time it was not heresy to observe the legal ceremonies, though it would be now, especially if anyone believed that grace did not suffice for salvation without the law. ⟦s23⟧ Likewise if they are to be observed, they bring salvation. ⟦s24⟧ Do we not observe certain things, which do not confer salvation, but which we can, at our own discretion, use, and not use?

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VII. ⟦s2⟧ *Man shall not be justified by the works of the law, but by the faith of Christ.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how faith justifies and not works, since God renders to each according to his works. ⟦s4⟧ Is there not, from the fact that there is merit and reward, [also] crown and justice? ⟦s5⟧ If then the crown is from works, it seems that justice is from the same. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ In the Epistle to the Romans it has been disputed [0557B] according to our measure concerning this question: here let it now suffice to say: faith is said to justify for this reason, because from the certitude of things invisible the eternal goods are loved; and love justifies. ⟦s8⟧ He says therefore that we are justified from faith, because it is first, from which the rest are obtained. ⟦s9⟧ Nor, when he says that we are justified from faith, are good works made void, but he says this for the reason that the works themselves are from the grace of faith.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VIII. ⟦s2⟧ *If I build again the things which I destroyed, I make myself a prevaricator.* ⟦s3⟧ But someone will say: Did he not destroy the faith which he assailed, and again build it up by preaching, and thus seem to be a prevaricator? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He who destroys a false thing, which can be destroyed, if he build it again, is a prevaricator. ⟦s6⟧ But faith cannot be destroyed, though [0557C] it can be assailed. ⟦s7⟧ Although, therefore, Paul first tried to destroy our faith, and again to build it up, yet he was not a prevaricator.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION IX. ⟦s2+3⟧ Likewise it is objected concerning the same thing thus: *If I build again the things which I destroyed.* ⟦s4⟧ Behold he manifestly says that he destroyed the legal [observances]; but elsewhere he says: *Do we then destroy the law? ⟦s5⟧ God forbid! but we establish the law [n: (Rom. III)]*. ⟦s6⟧ How then is each true? ⟦s7⟧ Solution. ⟦s8⟧ In two ways is anyone said to destroy the law. ⟦s9⟧ He destroys the law who says it useless in its state before the incarnation of the Word, and asserts it was not given by God, and in this way Paul did not destroy the law. ⟦s10⟧ He also is said to destroy the law, who shows that after the coming of Christ it is not to be kept according to the carnal [sense], [0557D] and in this way Paul did destroy the law, saying: *If you be circumcised, Christ shall profit you nothing.*

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION X. Is Christ then the minister of sin, since the law is good, and the commandment good, just, and holy? how [is] Christ [the minister of sin], if he administers the law, and [that] sin? ⟦s2⟧ Did God, when he gave the law, administer sin? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ The law indeed [is] good, yet the occasion of sin: according to which reasoning the minister of the law is called the minister of sin.
