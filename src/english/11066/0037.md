## IV. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE GALATIANS. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XI. ⟦s2⟧ *For through the law I am dead to the law,* etc., that is, through the authority of the law I have dismissed it: but by what authority, it is asked. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Moses says: *I will raise up for you a prophet from your brethren, whom you shall hear as me [n: (Deut. XVIII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And Jeremiah: *I will complete a new testament with the house of Israel.* ⟦s6⟧ And David: [0558A] *And holocaust for sin thou didst not require: then said I: Behold I come [n: (Psal. XXXIX)]*. ⟦s7⟧ From this too, that it is written in the law: *Thou shalt hate thine enemy [n: (Matth. V)]*, since no one can be saved with hatred of an enemy, it is established that the law justifies no one. ⟦s8⟧ And therefore from it one must flee to the grace which justifies. ⟦s9⟧ And because the law restrained only the hand, and not the mind, in the doing of outward things, and set all its worshippers under a curse, it is clear that through it one must die to it, that one may live in God.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XII. ⟦s2⟧ *Who loved me, and delivered himself for me,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Recall to memory that question, how the Father, the Son, and Judas concur in the delivering up of the Son, treated upon the Epistle to the Romans according to our ability. ⟦s4⟧ For if [0558B] justice [is] through the law, then Christ died in vain: but Christ did not die in vain, that is, without cause, or benefit: therefore justice is not from the law. ⟦s5⟧ Reader, recall the authority to memory as often as it is needful for you to prove that justice is not from the law: this I say, because there are authorities by which it seems it could be shown that justice is from the law: as was shown above.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIII. ⟦s2⟧ *Who hath bewitched you that you should not believe the truth,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what bewitching is. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Magical mockery, by which things are shown to the eyes of men otherwise than they are: [this] is called a bewitching, or bewitchment: or commonly [it is called] fascination, which harms infants. ⟦s6⟧ For the eyes of certain [persons] are said to burn by their gaze, and this act is reckoned a bewitching. ⟦s7⟧ Thus envy harms not only [0558C] the envious man, pining away at another's happiness, but also those in whom some good things begin to be. ⟦s8⟧ Whence it is written: *The bewitching of trifling obscureth good things [n: (Sap. IV)]*.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIV. ⟦s2⟧ *They who are of faith shall be blessed: they who are of the works of the law are under a curse.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what it is to be of faith, [and] what it is to be of the works of the law. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ They are of faith whose being depends on faith, that is, who through faith tend to true being, and who through the grace of faith seek to be justified. ⟦s6⟧ And these alone shall obtain from God the blessing of eternal life. ⟦s7⟧ But they are said to be of the works of the law, who seek to be justified from them; and therefore they are under a curse, as transgressors of the law.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XV. ⟦s2⟧ *Cursed is everyone who abideth not [0558D] in all things which are written in the book of the law, to do them.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether God commanded anything in the law which cannot be fulfilled? ⟦s4⟧ If it be said nothing, how are all, who are of the works of the law, under a curse? ⟦s5⟧ Likewise, if God commanded man something which he himself would not [be willing to] do, God seems unjust and cruel. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ Some say that nothing was commanded in the law which man cannot fulfill. ⟦s8⟧ Against whom the expositor says, that God commanded many things, all of which no one could fulfill. ⟦s9⟧ Whence the apostle Peter: *Why tempt you God, to put upon us a yoke, which neither we, nor our fathers were able to bear [n: (Act. XV)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVI. ⟦s2⟧ *Cursed is everyone that hangeth on a tree.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether Christ is contained under this universality of the curse [0559A]? ⟦s4⟧ But he is the one who is over all things God blessed forever; through whom every curse is taken away, and who blessed us with every blessing in the heavenly [places]. ⟦s5⟧ For he himself [is] the promised seed of Abraham, in whom is [made] the blessing of all nations. ⟦s6⟧ How then can he be cursed? ⟦s7⟧ Solution. ⟦s8⟧ There is one curse of fault, which is far from Christ: there is another of punishment, which Christ voluntarily took upon himself, made for us a curse, or sin, that is, a victim for sin: that he might take away from the midst the curse both of fault and of punishment. ⟦s9⟧ It can also be said that Christ was cursed with the curse of fault, not truly, but according to the opinion of men: whence he was reputed as a sinner with the wicked.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0559B]QUESTION XVII. ⟦s2⟧ *For if the inheritance [be] from the law, [it is] not from the promise.* ⟦s3⟧ Concerning this consequence it is asked whether it is true. ⟦s4⟧ For its truth it requires that to be from the law and [to be] from the promise be opposites: otherwise it would not follow, if from the law, [then] not from the promise. ⟦s5⟧ What is it, then, [for] the inheritance to be from the law: what is it to be from the promise? what also does he call the inheritance? ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ By inheritance he calls eternal life: to be from the law, is to be from the works of the law, for the fulfilling of which man seems to himself to suffice, nor to need the grace of God, which is [for] the inheritance to be from merits. ⟦s8⟧ But for the inheritance to be from the promise, this is, to be from grace. ⟦s9⟧ See then, that to be from the law, and [to be] from the promise are opposites. ⟦s10⟧ Whence elsewhere the Apostle says: *If from works, [then] now not from grace [n: (Rom. XI)]*, that is, if from debt, [0559C] [then] not from grace.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVIII. ⟦s2⟧ *What then [is] the law?* ⟦s3⟧ Since the former saints, who were before the law, were just through the grace of faith, and obtained the promise, it is asked why the law was given? and what usefulness it conferred? ⟦s4⟧ The solution of this question the Apostle sets down, saying: *The law was set because of transgression,* which can be understood in two ways, namely for the restraining of transgression, that at least through fear men might cease to transgress, so that at some time they might do the same voluntarily; or the law was set because of transgression, that is, that it might make men transgress, and thus they might be humbled, and seek a physician, and implore the help of grace. ⟦s5⟧ It was given therefore that it might humble the proud, and betray [their] weakness, [0559D] and [be] a scourge to the hard and a sign of things to come.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIX. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked why the law was not given immediately after the fall of man? or why the Son himself did not come at once? or why he permitted so many men to perish? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ By great counsel this was done, that after the fall of man the law was not immediately given, or the Son sent. ⟦s5⟧ For unless the pride of man first made trial of the powers of his own choice, it would arrogate sufficiency to [its] liberty, and would judge that the law was given superfluously, and that the Son came in vain. ⟦s6⟧ Nor did all, who then were, perish: just as neither are all, who now are, made saved. ⟦s7⟧ And so this was done, that man might recognize the disease of his own weakness, and implore the grace necessary for him. ⟦s8⟧ For in the natural law he was left to himself, [0560A] that thus he might make trial of the powers of nature, and was convicted of ignorance, and confessed [it], because the light of his eyes failed: yet still he believed that he had a virtue, by which he could fulfill whatever was necessary for salvation. ⟦s9⟧ Whence also he said: *There is not lacking one who fulfills, but there is lacking one who commands.* ⟦s10⟧ As if he said: I need knowledge, not virtue. ⟦s11⟧ And so the law was given, which might illuminate ignorance, but not aid weakness: which uncovered sin, but did not consume it, by the giving of which the disease grew strong, and concupiscence was increased, not by the fault of the law, but of nature, and by the instigation of the devil, that thus, the insufficiency of both laws being recognized, and his own weakness, [man] might cry to the physician, and seek the help of grace: and thus, with weaknesses multiplied, they hastened to the physician, who coming in the form [0560B] of a servant healed the wounds of the sick. ⟦s12⟧ For this is that Samaritan, who drew near to the wounded man, who fell among robbers, and bound up his wounds: whom the priest, and the levite, that is the law and the priesthood, passed by [n: (Luc. X)]: because *the law brought no one to perfection [n: (Hebr. VII)]*. ⟦s13⟧ This is Elisha, who after sending the staff not effective of health, came to raise up the dead son of the Shunammite [n: (IV Reg. IV)]. ⟦s14⟧ This is the Angel of great counsel, who descended into the pool, and when the water was moved one was healed [n: (Joan. V)]. ⟦s15⟧ This is the omnipotent Word, who came from the royal seats, while a middle silence held all things [n: (Sap. XVIII)]. ⟦s16⟧ Note that there are three silences. ⟦s17⟧ The first silence is the ignorance of languor, which was under the natural law. ⟦s18⟧ The second silence is the despair of salvation, [0560C] which was under the written law, and this silence is the middle one. ⟦s19⟧ The third silence is the attainment of health: which will be in the glory of eternal beatitude. ⟦s20⟧ While therefore all things held a middle silence, that is, despaired of salvation: the Son of the most high King descended from the light of heaven to the darkness of the world or of hell. ⟦s21⟧ And coming he spoke peace, gave grace, set forth mercy, promised pardon. ⟦s22⟧ And thus, the middle silence being broken, the sick began with pure faith, and true confession, as with great cries to demand a remedy, and to hasten to the physician, by whom the sick might be healed, and [their] wounds cured.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XX. ⟦s2⟧ *The law was set in the hand of a Mediator.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked why Christ is called a Mediator? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. [0560D] Because he mediates us, that is, reconciles [us] to God.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXI. ⟦s2⟧ But since not only the Son reconciles us to himself, but also God the Father, as the Apostle says: *God was in Christ, reconciling the world to himself [n: (II Cor. V)]*, it is asked why the Son alone is called the Mediator of God and men. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ The whole Trinity by the exercise of [its] power reconciles us to itself: but the Son alone by the fulfilling of obedience, and by the undertaking of the sacraments, justifies and reconciles us. ⟦s5⟧ Whence not undeservedly he alone is called Mediator.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXII. ⟦s2⟧ Since Christ [is] God, and man, the mediator of God and men, it is asked according to which nature, whether according to the divine, or according to the human, or according to both, he is Mediator. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ The authors say that he is not mediator according to [0561A] that which is God, but only according to that which is man, drawing near to us by mortality, [and] to God by justice.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXIII. ⟦s2⟧ *But scripture hath concluded all under sin.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what he calls scripture. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The law, which elsewhere he calls the letter, here the Apostle names scripture: because it only commands, [and] does not help: it convicts of disease the sick man, who seemed to himself whole, and thus by showing sins, and not taking [them] away, concludes all under sin. ⟦s6⟧ The law was given therefore, that grace might be sought: and grace was conferred, that through it the law might be fulfilled: to this accords what he says elsewhere: *God hath concluded all in unbelief, that he might have mercy on all [n: (Rom. XI)]*.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXIV. ⟦s2⟧ *As many of you as have been baptized [0561B] in Christ, have put on Christ.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked concerning him, who feignedly approaches baptism, whether he is baptized in Christ, and has put on Christ. ⟦s4⟧ For if it be said so, it follows that he is conformed to Christ: but if he is not baptized, [and] if he repent of his feigning, he will be able to be baptized, but this is not granted him: it is established therefore that if baptized. ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ To be baptized in Christ, and to put on Christ is understood in two ways, either by the reception of the sacrament, which is common to good and bad; or by inward sanctification, and conformity of life, which belongs to the good alone.
