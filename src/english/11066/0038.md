## IV. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE GALATIANS. (cont.)

QUESTION XXV. It is asked how Christ is the garment of the saints, and whether the saints are the garment of Christ? Solution. Christ is called the garment of the saints, and the saints also the garment [0561C] of Christ. But in one way and another. For Christ is called the garment of the saints through the overshadowing of the Holy Spirit, protecting them from the heat of vices: but the saints [are] the garment of Christ, as it were having him within themselves, and surrounding [him]. Also they honor him by their holy manner of life: as the wicked, by living wickedly, blaspheme [him].

QUESTION XXVI. *If you [be] Christ's, then [you are] Abraham's,* etc. Christ is called the seed of Abraham, and the faithful are called the seed of Abraham. Whence it is asked whether according to the same signification both Christ and the faithful are called the seed of Abraham? Solution. Christ is the seed of Abraham corporally, because born of his stock: the faithful are the seed of Abraham spiritually, that is, just through [0561D] faith, as he was.

QUESTION XXVII. *We were serving under the elements of the world [n: (Galat. IV)]*. If then the Jews also served under the elements, in what did they differ from the pagans? Solution. The Jews under the elements served God, not the elements themselves: but the pagans exhibited divine worship not to God, but to the elements themselves.

QUESTION XXVIII. *When the fullness came,* etc. It is asked why the coming of the Savior is called the fullness of time? Solution. For this reason, because at this time are fulfilled the things which in preceding times had been foretold, and it would seem more properly said the time of fullness, than the fullness of time, and the end of the age is called the same.

QUESTION XXIX. *He sent his Son,* etc. It is asked [0562A] whence and whither the Son was sent. Hear: *I came forth from the Father, and came into the world [n: (Joan. XVI)]*.

QUESTION XXX. But someone will say: Was he not *in the world, and the world was made by him [n: (Joan. I)]*. Was he then sent thither, where he was before? Solution. He was before in the world by power, and by essence, but he began to be otherwise in the world, made visible through the undertaking of the servile form. As therefore when he came forth from the Father, he did not desert the Father; so he came into the world, in which he was before. But why the mission of the Son, or of the Holy Spirit, since it is the work of the Trinity, is attributed to the Father, has already been said in what precedes.

QUESTION XXXI. *Made of a woman.* Since the Lord was born of a virgin, it is asked why the Apostle asserts [him] made of a woman? Solution. He used the Hebrew [0562B] manner of speech, which puts woman for female, as Eve in Genesis, not yet having suffered intercourse, is called a woman. Note that the expositor says that the Creator, who always was, was made, that he might be a creature; because he was made man, that he might become what he was not; not that what he was might perish; this I say on account of certain [persons], who deny that he was made anything.

QUESTION XXXII. *Made under the law, that he might redeem them, who were under the law,* etc. Did he not by his death redeem both those who were under the law, and those who were without the law? How then does the Apostle say Christ [was] made under the law, that he might redeem those who were under the law, as though he redeemed them through the observance of the law? Solution. He was made under the law, that he might fulfill it, and having fulfilled [it] make it cease; and thus also [0562C] redeem the Jews from the prevarication of the law. For unless he kept the law, in which the promise was made, who would believe that he was the seed of Abraham promised to Abraham, in whom not only the Jews, but also all the nations should be blessed?

QUESTION XXXIII. *That we might receive the adoption of sons.* What does he call adoption; whether the gratuitous goods pertaining to present justice, or to future glory, through which we are made sons? Solution. Some think a distinction is to be made between the sons of adoption and the sons of grace, but I judge them to be the same.

QUESTION XXXIV. *God sent the spirit of his Son,* etc. Note that the Trinity is here manifestly signified. There are some, who say, that as the eternal generation of the Word [0562D] is from the Father alone, so also is his temporal mission; because according to these, for the Father to send the Son, is [for the Son] to be from the Father, and to appear to us in the flesh assumed. Similarly they assert concerning the Holy Spirit, that as his eternal procession is from the Father, and the Son, and not from himself, so also [is] the temporal: but Augustine manifestly says that the temporal mission both of the Son and of the Holy Spirit is the work of the Trinity; whence the Son himself says that he was sent by the Holy Spirit.

QUESTION XXXV. *Crying: Abba, Father.* It is asked why the Apostle set down two words signifying the same thing; for one seems to be set down superfluously. Solution. He does this for this reason, that he might intimate two peoples joined by one faith. For the Hebrew name signifies the Jews, the Greek word the gentile people; [0563A] the same signification of both words figures the unity of faith and of the Spirit.

QUESTION XXXVI. *To those, who by nature are not gods.* Here he intimates that the nature of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit is one. For if the Son is not by nature God, then [he is] neither to be worshipped, nor adored. But it is objected to us thus: Is not the humanity of Christ worshipped and adored? and yet it is not by nature God. Solution. That which is assumed is adored not on its own account, but on account of the one assuming; therefore we adore the humanity of the Savior not alone and bare, but united to the Godhead.

QUESTION XXXVII. *But rather, being known by God.* Does not God know all things from eternity? How then is God said then as if first to know us, when we begin to believe in him? Solution. By a tropical [0563B] manner of speech what we do by God's authorship, is attributed to him; whence it is said: He asketh for the saints, because he maketh them ask, so [he is said] to know us, because he bestoweth on us the knowledge of himself, and to rest, because he maketh us rest in himself.

QUESTION XXXVIII. *How are you turned again to the weak and needy things?* The Galatians did not before keep the law, how then does he say: *you are turned again to the needy things,* etc. Solution. That he might show that legal observance after the coming of Christ differs little, or nothing, from idolatry. Or he says this for this reason, because they wished not only to keep the law, but were also turned to their former errors; thus by a double error they had been deceived by the false [apostles].

QUESTION XXXIX. *And the seventh of the seventh decade, [0563C] which is called jubilee.* What is this that the expositor says? was not the fiftieth year called jubilee in the law? how then is the seventh year of the seventh decade called jubilee? for the seventh of the seventh decade is the sixty-seventh. Solution. The children of Israel, sometimes oppressed by captivity, could not keep the jubilee year in its order. But in the sixty-sixth year license of returning was given by Cyrus, and Darius, and in part they returned, and kept the sixty-seventh year for the jubilee. But someone will say that before the seventy years were fulfilled, they did not return from captivity. Solution. Not indeed generally and wholly did they return before the seventieth year; yet in the [0563D] sixty-sixth year, as we said before, license of returning was granted to some, which is signified by the Alleluia, which is sung on the Saturday after Good Friday, which is the sixty-sixth day of Septuagesima. But since some were still detained in captivity, there follows the tract, which is a sign of labor, as the Alleluia is a sign of joy. But on the following Saturday there is sung also a second [Alleluia] together with the first Alleluia, prefiguring the general return of the Jews which is [at] the completed seventy years.

QUESTION XL. *If it could have been done, you would have plucked out,* etc. Was not that able to be done which the Apostle says? Solution. Holy Scripture says that thing cannot be done, which is not justly done; whence Job says: *Would that I could kill myself [n: (Job X)]*. And the Lord to Lot: *I cannot [0564A] do anything, until thou enter thither [n: (Gen. XIX)]*. He said that he could not [do] what without doubt he could through power, but not through justice.

QUESTION XLI. *Abraham had two sons.* Did he not have more sons of Cetura after the death of Sara? How then does the Apostle say he had two, as though not more than two? Solution. If more than two, then two; for he does not say only two, but yet rather speaks of these than of the others, because Scripture follows out the particulars concerning these, intimating that something excellent is prefigured.

QUESTION XLII. *But these are the two testaments.* Concerning this and like expressions it is asked how they are true. Is not the figure one thing, the truth another; the signifier one thing, the signified another? how [0564B] then is the name of truth predicated of the name of figure, when it is said: These are the two Testaments, and *the rock was Christ? [n: (I Cor. X.)]* Solution. This word, to be, in places of this kind, is set for to signify.

QUESTION XLIII. Likewise why holy Scripture in such a place uses the substantive verb, can be asked. Would it not be plainer, if it were said: But these signify the two testaments, and the rock figured Christ? Solution. It belongs to words to signify, to things to be, properly. But in theology not only do words have signification, but also things are set for the signification of other things, that is, certain things are signified by other things. As often, therefore, as holy Scripture wishes to show, what thing has to signify what thing, it does not say: this signifies that; but this is that, as the rock [0564C] was Christ; for if it were said the rock signifies Christ, it would seem that the signification of this name rock, and not of this thing the rock, was pointed out.

QUESTION XLIV. Note that above he says, that Ishmael was born according to the flesh; Isaac not according to the flesh, but through promise, but was not Isaac, like Ishmael, born by the intercourse of both sexes? how then was he not born according to nature or according to the flesh? Solution. Ishmael was born by the accustomed law of nature, namely from the concurrence of natural causes; but the generation of Isaac was the operation not of nature, but of divine power, and grace. For to such intercourse, as could be at such an age between old Abraham, and Sara aged and [0564D] barren, nature does not grant children; but what nature denied, grace conferred.

QUESTION XLV. But how then did he, who was born according to the flesh, persecute him who was born according to the spirit, or promise; so also now. It is asked: Where this is found, that Ishmael persecuted Isaac. For in Genesis it is read, that the elder played with the younger [n: (Gen. XXI)]. What evil then did he do? what did he sin? how by merely playing did he persecute him? Solution. The play of the elder with the younger Sara understood to be a mockery. Whence also, indignant, she said: *Cast out the handmaid, and her son. [n: (Ibid.)]* And the Apostle calls such mockery persecution. So also mockers persecute us more than open persecutors.

QUESTION XLVI. *If you be circumcised, Christ [0565A] shall profit you nothing. [n: (Gal. V.)]* Did not the Apostle circumcise Timothy? Then he deceived him, and made [it so] that Christ profited nothing? Solution. He says this of those who sought to be justified from circumcision; and therefore circumcised themselves, and thus fell from grace, which they believed to be insufficient. But to those, who received it out of a certain reverence, yet not putting hope in it, it was not pernicious according to Augustine, but according to Jerome it harmed all who received [it], unless a dispensation were made. Concerning which controversy it has been more fully said above.
