## IV. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE GALATIANS. (cont.)

QUESTION XLVII. *I testify to every man circumcising himself, that he is a debtor to do the whole law.* Will he, if he fulfill the whole law, even so be able to obtain justice? Which seems [to follow] from these words, but [0565B] elsewhere it is manifestly held that justice is not from the law. Likewise, is not every man bound to fulfill the law, at least according to the spirit? What is it then that every man circumcising himself owes this, [and] not another? Likewise: Is not everyone bound to love his neighbor as himself? but he who loves his neighbor fulfills the whole law. Does it not seem from these things, that everyone is bound to fulfill the whole law? What is it then that he says: I testify to every man circumcising himself, etc. Solution. He who seeks to be justified from circumcision or from the law, is bound to this, that, in order to have justice, or be worthy of life, he fulfill whatever is commanded in the law, namely that he covet nothing; and love God with his whole heart, in which two consists perfect justice; which, if anyone had, he would not need the grace [0565C] of faith, as neither [do] the angels of God, [a justice] which is granted to no one in the present [life], save only to the Mediator of God and men: whence it is necessary, that if anyone wishes to be justified or beatified, he flee to grace alone; for if he seeks to be justified in the law, that is required of him which cannot be discharged by man.

QUESTION XLVIII. *In Christ Jesus neither circumcision availeth, nor uncircumcision.* The expositor says that for those who are in Christ Jesus vices are to be fled, virtues to be sought; but the middle things [are] neither to be fled, nor to be sought, among which he places circumcision. Whence it seems that it neither profits, nor hurts; but above it was said that circumcision not only does not profit, but even hurts. And the Apostle says: *If you be circumcised, Christ shall profit you nothing.* If then [0565D] it takes Christ from us, it hurts much. Solution. Circumcision simply received, in that the cause of salvation is not placed in it, seems to be indifferent, and so neither profits, nor hinders; but if [received] with this intention, and this mind, that from it salvation be sought, [it] hurts [when] received.

QUESTION XLIX. *The whole law is fulfilled in one word: Thou shalt love thy neighbor as thyself.* Since there are two precepts of love, namely of God and of neighbor, it is asked how in the love of neighbor the whole law is fulfilled? Solution. In the love of neighbor is contained the love of God; for who can love his neighbor for God's sake, unless he love God? Likewise no one can love his neighbor as himself, unless he love [0566A] himself; but no one loves himself, unless he love God: for what is it to love oneself, but to love one's own good? but the true and highest good is Christ. Otherwise he who loves iniquity, hates his own soul.

QUESTION L. It is asked who is [one's] neighbor? Solution. Every man. We are bound therefore by precept to love every man, and to exhibit the offices of piety to everyone in need.

QUESTION LI. *As thyself,* etc. It is asked whether we ought to love [our] neighbor as much as ourselves? Which seems [so], since it is said, as thyself. Solution. "As" is not of quantity, but of quality: this indeed we are bound to do to others, which we wish according to reason to be exhibited to us by others. Note: Since each precept of love is contained in [0566B] each [other], often one is set down rather than two, for if both were set down together, it would seem that one could be had without the other, or could be had.

QUESTION LII. *The flesh lusteth against the spirit.* It is asked how the flesh is said to lust, since the soul alone lusts. Solution. What the soul does from the flesh, this is attributed to the flesh, as the ear is said to hear, the eye to see, since the soul does these things through instruments of this kind.

QUESTION LIII. Since therefore the soul and the spirit are the same according to substance, by what does the soul lust against the spirit; is it against itself? Solution. He calls the lusting flesh the carnal delight, which the soul has from the flesh. But by the name of Spirit he signifies the spiritual delight. [0566C] And on account of these two delights the inner man is divided, and obtains diverse names. For according to the lower delight, he is called now flesh, now man, now soul, or animal, or carnal; but according to the higher he is called spirit, or spiritual, or new man.

QUESTION LIV. *If you be led by the spirit, you are not under the law.* It is asked what it is to be under the law? Solution. To abstain from every evil work by the fear of punishment, not by the love of justice, this is to be under the law. As he is said to be under the law, who seeks to be justified from it, who is a debtor to fulfill the whole law.

QUESTION LV. *But manifest are the works of the flesh, which are fornication,* etc. It is asked how the Apostle [0566D] enumerates among the works of the flesh certain things which are not vices of the flesh, but rather of the soul, as anger, envy, etc. Solution. By the name of flesh he signifies the whole man, who by living according to himself has fallen into these things. For to believe that all vices are from the flesh, is an error, lest the devil, who has no flesh, seem immune from these.

QUESTION LVI. *But the fruit of the spirit is charity.* The expositor says that the Apostle calls the works of the spirit fruits, because they are to be sought for their own sake. But Augustine says that the virtues are to be sought for the sake of beatitude alone, but for their own sake nothing is to be loved, save the highest good, whose fruition makes us blessed. What then is to be said? what is to be held? Solution. The virtues are to be sought for their own sake, because they delight their possessors [0567A] with a sincere delight, but not only for their own sake, but also for the sake of beatitude, which is the supreme end.

QUESTION LVII. *If a man be overtaken in any fault [n: (Gal. VI)]*, etc. It is asked what it is to be overtaken, and what he calls a fault. Solution. He is overtaken, who falls into some fault, or sin, either because he is ignorant of what is to be done or to be omitted, or when he knows [it], but yet weakness impels [him] to sinning. A fault is the desertion of good, as sin is the perpetration of evil, or a fault is what is done ignorantly; sin what [is done] by one knowing, yet indifferently the one is set for the other.

QUESTION LVIII. *Every one shall bear his own burden,* etc. It is asked how both are true. [0567B] *Bear ye one another's burdens,* etc., and this, which he says here: Every one shall bear his own burden? Solution. There are some burdens of the weakness to be shared, of which he treated above; there are others of the account to be rendered to God concerning our acts, of which he treats here. It is true therefore that in the present we ought to help one another; and [that] those who are stronger [ought] to sustain the weaknesses of others; and yet in the future everyone shall bear his own burden, that is, for his own sin and not for the sin of another.

QUESTION LIX. *Let us work good to all,* etc. It is asked whether we are bound by precept to bestow the offices of piety even on enemies? Which seems [so], since the Apostle says: Let us work good to all; and the Lord [0568A] in the Gospel: *Love your enemies; do good to them that hate you [n: (Luc. VI)]*. But Augustine says: To love enemies is not of so great a multitude, as I believe to be heard in the Lord's Prayer, where it is said: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors [n: (Matth. VI)]*. And Gregory says: It suffices not to hate enemies, that is, to will their salvation; even if works of mercy be not bestowed on them. Solution. By precept we are bound to will the salvation of all; but to bestow also the offices of piety on enemies is, as regards the weak and less perfect, counsel, [but] as regards the perfect, precept. Diligently therefore is it to be noted what is said according to counsel, [and] what according to precept.

QUESTION LX. Likewise since it is established, that it is good [0568B] to do good to the wicked, and to sinners, and to the impious, what is it that Scripture says: *Give to the merciful, and receive not the sinner, and to the impious, and to sinners render vengeance, and do good to the humble, and give not to the impious [n: (Eccles. XII)]*. Solution. The sense of the aforesaid authorities is that you do good to no sinner for this reason, because he is a sinner, but because [he is] a man, that is, we ought to foster no one's fault, but [his] nature. In each let us love what God loves, and hate what he hates; but if we cannot help all, both good and bad, we ought to bestow the good things which we can on the servants of God; whence the Apostle: *But most of all to those of the household of the faith.*
