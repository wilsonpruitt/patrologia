## V. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE EPHESIANS.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0567]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0567C] *[n: (Ephes. I.)] Paul, an apostle,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This Epistle he writes to the Ephesians, whom Paul did not found in the faith, but, having been founded by John the apostle, he confirmed — men who firmly persisted in faith and good works, whom he exhorts to advance in good things, writing to them from Rome, from prison. ⟦s3⟧ And so the matter of the apostle in this epistle is the state of the Ephesians, in which they then were. ⟦s4⟧ But the intention is to confirm them in the good things they possess, to spur on [sic: *adulteriora*], and also to form them to humility and the giving of thanks. ⟦s5⟧ The mode of proceeding is such: after his manner he sends a salutation beforehand; then he gives thanks, setting forth the manifold benefits of God, conferred by grace alone — now upon the human race, now upon the apostles themselves, now upon the Ephesians themselves. ⟦s6⟧ Then he shows the dignity and preeminence of Christ. ⟦s7⟧ Afterward he invites them to patience [0567D] and charity, commending the unity of faith and of the Church, and enumerating the gifts of grace; at last, exhorting them to the contest, he describes the armor of the Christian soldiery against the princes of darkness.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION I. *Blessed be God, who hath blessed us,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Since "to be blessed" is said of God and of man, it is asked whether it is said in the same manner, and according to the same signification. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Otherwise and otherwise of each. [0568C] For God is blessed by man when he is extolled with worthy praises; but man is blessed by God when God imparts to him the gifts of his grace.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION II. ⟦s2⟧ The expositor says that the Apostle here sets down two preordinations: one concerning present justice, the other concerning the future crown. ⟦s3⟧ But since the predestination of God is one, which is God himself, it is asked how they are said to be two? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ They are not said to be two as though it were not one; but because it has two effects, namely through present justice and through future glory. ⟦s6⟧ To which solution it is thus objected: If the number of predestinations is to be assigned according to the number of effects, then there will be infinite predestinations, since the effects of predestination are infinite. ⟦s7⟧ Solution. ⟦s8⟧ All instances of justice, because they differ in number and not in species, [0568D] are said to be one effect; so also all crowns, because they are distinguished not in species but in number alone, are said to be one effect of predestination. ⟦s9⟧ But since present justice and future glory differ not only in number but also in species, they are not incongruously said to be two effects of predestination. ⟦s10⟧ It is to be noted that there are many things of this kind which, though they be many in number and one in species, are said to be simply not many, but [0569A] one, according to the usage of speaking; as my faith and his faith, though they be two in number, are nevertheless said to be not two simply, but one, because they are one in species; so of grass, of voice, of understanding, of sight, and of many other things — letter, figure, element, vowel — the like distinction is to be made.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION III. ⟦s2⟧ *As he chose us, that we should be holy,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ The Pelagian says that those whom God chose from eternity, he chose them for this reason, because he foreknew that they would be good, and through the choice of free will, not because he was going to sanctify them. ⟦s4⟧ But the Apostle destroys this heresy of his when he says: He chose us, that we should be holy *and unspotted;* he does not say because we were going to be holy, but that we should be. ⟦s5⟧ For if it were as the Pelagian says, our justification would be the cause [0569B] of the divine election, not the divine election the cause of our justification; and thus that which is temporal would be the cause of that which is eternal, and not conversely: which cannot be.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION IV. ⟦s2⟧ *In whom we have redemption,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether he calls redemption and the remission of sins the same thing? for if they seem the same, then one of them is superfluously set down. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ By redemption he calls that price by which we were redeemed, through which the power of returning is given to us; but the remission of sins, which is conferred upon us in baptism, is the effect of that redemption itself.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION V. *To restore [sic: omnio], which are in the heavens,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Since Christ did not die for the angels, it is asked how the things which are in the heavens were restored through Christ? [0569C] Solution. ⟦s3+4⟧ This is said for this reason, because those who are saved by grace fill up the diminished number of the angels; or by the things which are in the heavens he understands the souls which are already in heaven; by the things which are on the earth, the saints still dwelling in this life.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VI. ⟦s2⟧ *You are sealed with the Holy Spirit, who is the pledge of the inheritance.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how the Holy Spirit is called the pledge of our inheritance; is not a pledge that which is given for a time for some price, and again, when the price is paid, is taken away? ⟦s3⟧ Is the Spirit then taken away from us when the inheritance itself is given to us? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Here "pledge" is set down for "earnest," which is part of the price itself, and is not taken away when the price is paid; whence certain manuscripts have "earnest," not "pledge."

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0569D]QUESTION VII. ⟦s2⟧ *Setting him at his right hand,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ God is spirit, and is not bounded or enclosed by the form of a body: how then is the Son said to sit at the right hand of the Father, since the Father has no right or left side, because he has no body? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ By the right hand of God in Holy Scripture is sometimes signified eternal beatitude, sometimes the equality of the divine nature, sometimes judiciary power. ⟦s6⟧ For Christ is at the right hand of the Father, because in the majesty of the Father he remains equal, and because *the Father judgeth no man, but hath given all judgment to the Son [n: (Joan. V)]*. ⟦s7⟧ By the right hand of God therefore, or by hand, or arm, or finger, or eye, or ear, and the like, nothing corporeal is to be understood, but the whole spiritually.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0570A]QUESTION VIII. ⟦s2⟧ *Above every name, which is named not only in this world,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Are there not nine orders of angels? ⟦s4⟧ Angels, archangels, virtues, powers, principalities, dominations, thrones, cherubim, seraphim — and there are no more than these, which are all named in the present. ⟦s5⟧ How then does he say: Set above everything that is named not only in this world, *but also in that which is to come,* as though there were some to be named in the future who are not named in the present. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ The denominations of certain angels are given to them according to the offices which they exhibit toward us in the present, as angels, archangels, virtues, powers, principalities, dominations; therefore they are said to be names in the present, because in this manner their denomination looks to the present. [0570B] Whence in the future they will be made void, when Christ shall deliver up the kingdom to God and the Father, when God *shall be all in all.* ⟦s8⟧ But the denominations of certain angels look to the future state, as thrones, cherubim, and seraphim; whence they will not be made void even in the future, but above all these orders of the supercelestial spirits the humanity of the Savior has been exalted.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION IX. ⟦s2⟧ *And him he gave to be head over all the Church.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked according to which nature, namely the divine or the human, Christ is the head of the Church. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ It can be said that it is according to the divine, according to which he is the head, the beginning, and the author of all the faithful. ⟦s6⟧ To which solution it is thus objected: By the same reasoning it can be said that he is the head of a stone, and of every creature, of which he is the author; and nothing would be said [0570C] of the Church. ⟦s7⟧ Therefore we say that Christ is properly the head of the Church according to his humanity.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION X. Why Christ is called the head of the Church. ⟦s2⟧ Why he is said to be the head may not unfittingly be asked. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Christ is called the head of the Church for this reason: because as in the head of a man are fully all the carnal senses, namely sight, hearing, smell or scent, taste, and touch; so in Christ is the fullness of all the spiritual senses, namely the fullness of grace, *of whose fullness we all have received [n: (Joan. I)]*; whence we are also called members, as not all having some one sense, just as the other members of the body have but a single sense; none has all except the head.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XI. ⟦s2⟧ *We were by nature the children of wrath (Ephes. [0570D] II),* etc. ⟦s3⟧ God is the author of nature; if therefore we are by nature children of wrath, it seems that we are this with God as author. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Nature is taken in three ways in Holy Scripture, namely for that whole and interrupted good in which man was created, according to which acceptation it is said that every creature of God is good. ⟦s6⟧ Nature is also called the corruption of sin, in which every man is conceived, and with which he is born, and thus it is taken here when it is said: We were by nature children of wrath. ⟦s7⟧ For the vice has grown ingrained in place of nature: because therefore, on account of the guilt of original sin, with which we are born, we are worthy of Gehenna, therefore we are called by nature children of wrath. ⟦s8⟧ Nature is also taken for the remnants of that natural good which remained in us after sin, and thus [0571A] it is taken where it is read that *the Gentiles do by nature the things that are of the law [n: (Rom. II)]*.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XII. ⟦s2⟧ *To me the least of all the saints is given this grace [n: (Ephes. III)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Since the Apostle was not the least in the number of all the saints, but rather among the apostles was one of the first, how does he speak truly in naming himself the least of all. ⟦s4⟧ Seek the solution of this question upon that passage: *I am the least of the apostles,* in the First Epistle to the Corinthians.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIII. ⟦s2⟧ *The unsearchable riches of Christ,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how the Apostle understood or evangelized, if the riches are unsearchable. ⟦s4⟧ For those things are called unsearchable which cannot be comprehended. ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ Those things which are by their nature unsearchable were made searchable, through the grace [0571B] and revelation of the Holy Spirit, not only to the Apostle, but also to the rest of the faithful, whom the anointing itself teaches concerning all things.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIV. ⟦s2⟧ *Of the hidden mystery,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked, what things are called hidden. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ In the world the causes are hidden of all things that come to be naturally. ⟦s6⟧ But in God alone are hidden the causes of all things that come to be through grace. ⟦s7⟧ Concerning these two kinds of causes something has already been said above.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XV. ⟦s2⟧ *That it may be made known to the principalities and powers in the heavenly places through the Church,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether the mystery of the incarnation was revealed to the supercelestial essences before its fulfillment. ⟦s4⟧ In the solution of this question Jerome [0571C] and Augustine seem contrary. ⟦s5⟧ For the blessed Jerome says that the angelic dignities did not understand purely the aforesaid mystery; whence they thus ask: *Who is this that cometh from Edom? [n: (Isa. LXIII.)]* And elsewhere: *Who is this king of glory? [n: (Psal. XXIII.)]* But the blessed Augustine says that the mystery of the kingdom of heaven was not hidden from the angels; whence the text is expounded in diverse ways, That it may be made known, etc. ⟦s6⟧ But this contrariety, which seems to be between the aforesaid doctors, can be resolved thus: to those who are of greater dignity the aforesaid mystery was revealed; but to others not purely, as the blessed Jerome says. ⟦s7⟧ But that the angels grow daily in knowledge, from those things which come to be in the world, many doctors assert with concordant voice. [0571D] Whence we hold this as if for certain, since canonical Scripture seems manifestly to intimate it.
