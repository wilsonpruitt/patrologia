## V. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE EPHESIANS. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVI. ⟦s2⟧ *Of whom all paternity in heaven and in earth is named.* ⟦s3⟧ From this passage we have that not only is God our Father, but also the angels and men are called our fathers. ⟦s4⟧ But elsewhere the Lord says: *One is your Father, who is in heaven [n: (Matth. XXIII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ If therefore there is one, how are there many? or if many, how one? ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ As God, who alone truly is, and who alone is truly good, imparts the name of his essence and of his goodness to the rest, so that they too are said both to be and to be good; so also he, who alone is truly Father — of all by creation, and of the faithful by regeneration — has granted the name of paternity to the rest. ⟦s8⟧ It is to be known thus, that God the Trinity is the Father of all [0572A] by creation and of the faithful by regeneration. ⟦s9⟧ But God unbegotten alone is the Father of the Only-begotten by nature. ⟦s10⟧ But the angels are called our fathers by the authority of example, by reason of benefit, by care, and by providence. ⟦s11⟧ But men are fathers both by nature, and by the authority of example, and by reason of benefit, and by care and providence.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVII. ⟦s2⟧ *What is the breadth, and length, and height.* ⟦s3⟧ What is the breadth of love, what the length of eternity, what the height of power, what the depth of knowledge — according to this, which is expounded of God, such can be the understanding.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVIII. ⟦s2⟧ *To know also the charity of Christ, which surpasseth knowledge.* ⟦s3⟧ If the charity of Christ surpasses knowledge, how can it be known? if it cannot be known, how does the Apostle pray that the disciples may know it? [0572B] Solution. ⟦s4⟧ That which human knowledge cannot comprehend is known in part through grace, which the Apostle prays may come to pass.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIX. ⟦s2⟧ *That you may be filled unto all the fullness of God,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Shall we not have less than God himself? how then can we be filled unto all the fullness of God? how can anyone have the fullness of God, and not be full God and equal to God? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The sense is, that you may be full of God, not full God. ⟦s6⟧ This is to be filled unto all fullness: to have in the present the fullness of virtues, and in the future the fullness of glory; for Paul does not wish that the fullness of the divine nature be conferred upon anyone, but that we be full of God in the present and in the future.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0572C]QUESTION XX. ⟦s2⟧ *One Lord, one faith, one baptism [n: (Ephes. IV)]*. ⟦s3⟧ How are they to be understood? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ One Lord: the Father, and the Son, and the Spirit in nature, not in person. ⟦s6⟧ One faith not in number, but in kind, because it is alike in all, just as of all who will the same thing there is said to be the same will. ⟦s7⟧ Concerning faith it has already been spoken. ⟦s8⟧ It is called one baptism, because it is equal, and of the same essence by whomsoever it is given, and therefore also because it cannot be reiterated.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXI. ⟦s2⟧ *He gave gifts to men.* ⟦s3+4⟧ But does David say: *Thou hast received gifts in men [n: (Psal. LXVII)]*? if he gave, how did he receive? or if he received, how did he give? ⟦s3+4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ As God he gave; he received not only in himself according to that he is man, but also in his members, in which he is, of which receiving [0572D] the Prophet treats: whence he says: Thou hast received gifts in men.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXII. ⟦s2⟧ *What gifts he gave in ascending.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what gifts he gave in ascending. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The Holy Spirit, and also the gifts which are not the Holy Spirit, which when he gives, in them also he gives the Holy Spirit; he does not give separately the Holy Spirit and his gifts which are not himself, but in giving the Holy Spirit he gives the gifts; and in giving the gifts of the Holy Spirit he gives him. ⟦s6⟧ This I say on account of those who will not grant that the Holy Spirit is given, since he is immutable, but grant nevertheless that his gifts are given. ⟦s7⟧ For the Holy Spirit is then said to be given to us by the Father, and the Son, and even by himself, when he infuses into our hearts through charity this power, [0573A] to the end that we may love God and neighbor.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXIII. ⟦s2⟧ Since Christ is God and man, it is asked according to which nature he gave the gifts? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ He gave according to that, as the expositor says, according to which he ascended and descended, and thus he gave according to that he is man; because the divinity, which is everywhere, neither descends nor ascends. ⟦s5⟧ But this seems to be contrary to the aforesaid, where I asserted that he gave as God, not as man. ⟦s6⟧ When therefore it is read that he gave as man or according to his humanity, it ought to be understood thus: he who is man, not because he is man, but because he is God.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXIV. ⟦s2⟧ Whether the Deity descended, and how. ⟦s3⟧ Likewise another exposition says that Christ descended according to [0573B] his Deity, and ascended according to his humanity, which seems contrary to the foregoing. ⟦s4⟧ For it was said that the divinity which is everywhere neither descends nor ascends; but here it is said that the Deity descended: what then is to be held? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ When it is said that God descended, this is understood of the descent of the incarnation, by which God was made man. ⟦s7⟧ When it is said that man descended, this ought to be understood of the descent to the lower regions, which was only according to the soul, not according to the body, which lay in the sepulcher, nor according to the Deity, which was everywhere.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXV. ⟦s2⟧ *Unto a perfect man,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked, by what perfection. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Perfection can be understood neither of the whole Christ, that is, of body and head, nor [0573C] of the individual members. ⟦s6⟧ For Christ, consummated in himself, grows and advances in others, but in the future at last he will be perfected, so that nothing be added either in powers or in number. ⟦s7⟧ Each one also will then be a perfect man, as much by the consummation of virtues as by the stature of the body. ⟦s8⟧ Whence there follows: *Unto the measure of the age of the fullness of Christ.* ⟦s9⟧ For each one will rise again in that perfection in which he was, or to which he was going to come, when he was thirty years of age; for not all will be of the same magnitude, nor of that of which Christ was when departing from this life.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXVI. ⟦s2⟧ *Be angry, and sin not,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ The Apostle permits anger, which cannot be avoided, but the Lord forbids anger, saying: *Whosoever [0573D] is angry with his brother shall be in danger of the judgment [n: (Matth. V)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Whence it seems that what the Lord forbids, the Apostle permits. ⟦s5⟧ Likewise it was said above: that nothing is forbidden in the New Testament which cannot be avoided, just as nothing is commanded which cannot be fulfilled; which does not seem to be true, if the Lord forbids anger, and no one can avoid being angry. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ The first motion of anger, which is not in our power, is permitted by the Apostle: but the Lord does not forbid the first motion, which is called pro-passion, but the will and the purpose of harming another. ⟦s8⟧ And so one thing is permitted by the Apostle, and another is forbidden by the Lord, which can be avoided with grace helping.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXVII. ⟦s2⟧ What are the gates of death and of life. ⟦s3⟧ Note that concupiscence and the fear of the world are the two [0574A] gates of death, through which the devil enters; as the fear and love of God are the gates of life, through which Christ enters. ⟦s4⟧ Each stands at the door; and both Christ knocks and the devil: but the enemy is driven out, Christ is brought in.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ QUESTION XXVIII. ⟦s3⟧ *Grieve not the Holy Spirit,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ It is asked how anyone can grieve the Holy Spirit, since he is impassible and cannot receive into himself the passion of sadness. ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ He has used a certain trope, sufficiently customary in theology, by which those things which come to be in us with God as author are attributed to him; because therefore those whom he fills with charity, as they rejoice over the progress of others, so are grieved over the lapses of those who fall, the Apostle says: Grieve not the Holy Spirit, that is, those in whom [0574B] the Holy Spirit dwells through charity.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6+s7⟧ QUESTION XXIX. Forgive *one another,* etc. Scripture says: Unless we forgive our fellow servants, God requires again what was remitted; but it is asked how God requires it again? Will God punish again for sins for which satisfaction has already been made through the contrition of the heart, and the confession of the mouth, and through worthy fruits of penance? Will he punish twice for the same thing? Solution. To the end that anyone may bring forth worthy fruits of penance, it is required that henceforth he sin not mortally; whence whoever sins after penance renders his former satisfaction useless to himself.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXX. ⟦s2⟧ What is it to punish twice for the same thing? ⟦s3⟧ Now to punish twice for the same thing is to punish again in another life for a sin destroyed by penance and not repeated, [0574C] which God does not do. ⟦s4⟧ But if any penitent is afflicted for some sin and again repeats the same; if in this life or in another he is punished for it again, this is not to punish twice for the same thing.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXI. ⟦s2⟧ *For an odor of sweetness [n: (Ephes. V)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ If the death of Christ was a sweet odor to God, then he willingly accepted his death; then the Jews did not sin, who crucified him; because they did that which was good, and which pleased God. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The action of the Jews was evil, nor did that which was done by them please God; but the passion of Christ was good, and the cause of our salvation.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXII. ⟦s2⟧ *The days are evil,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Do not the days have their being from God as author, as it is written: *Thine [0574D] is the day, and thine is the night? [n: (Psal. LXXIII.)]* How then are they called evil? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ On account of the malice and misery of men the days are called evil; otherwise, as to the spaces of the hours, they are ordered, and good.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXIII. ⟦s2⟧ *No man ever hated his own flesh,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Do not holy men hate the flesh, persecute, crucify, and mortify it, not nourish nor cherish it? how then is it true, No man ever hated his own flesh? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The saints hate and persecute not the flesh, but the vice of the flesh.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXIV. ⟦s2⟧ *For this cause shall a man leave his father.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how this pertains to the Son of God, who never forsook the Father, but always remained inseparably with him? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Because the Son did not [0575A] appear to us in the form in which he is equal to the Father, he is said to have forsaken or left the Father; yet he never departed from him: he came into the world, and did not forsake the Father.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXV. ⟦s2⟧ *He that loveth his wife.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked of what love he here treats; for if of the love by which we love one another for the sake of God, the sense is: He that loveth his wife *loveth himself,* that is, does what is useful to himself: according to this he could say the same of any neighbor whatever. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The Apostle says this for this reason, because it is more incumbent upon a man to provide for his wife than for another: whence if for the sake of God he acquires for her the necessaries, and governs her, he also loves himself, that is, does what is useful to himself; because thereby he merits with God.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXVI. ⟦s2⟧ *Servants, obey your masters [n: (Ephes. VI)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Here it is asked whether it is lawful for a Christian to have a servant, [0575B] since the Apostle permits this. ⟦s4⟧ The Catholic Church too, and especially the Church of the Gauls, receives this. ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ It would be better not to exact servitude of this kind; nor does the Church receive it as a good, but tolerates it as an evil.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXVII. ⟦s2⟧ *Children, obey your parents; and you, fathers, provoke not your children to anger.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked why the Apostle commands parents to [0576A] love their children, and children to honor their parents, since nature compels them to love one another mutually. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Natural love has no merit unless it be done for the sake of God: therefore he commands that they love one another for the sake of God, and so have merit.
