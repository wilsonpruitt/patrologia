## VI. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE PHILIPPIANS. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XV. ⟦s2⟧ *Not having my own justice,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Did not God give the law, and command obedience to it? [0579D] how then does he call the justice which is from the law his own? ⟦s4⟧ The solution of this question is contained in the foregoing. ⟦s5⟧ For while he was under the law, seeking to be justified from the works of the law: in this, that he believed he could fulfill the law itself by his own powers, he made the justice of the law his own.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVI. ⟦s2⟧ *In faith unto knowing,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ This is understood of the faith which has not the time of working; but is not to love a work of faith? ⟦s4⟧ But faith without love, after one is grown, is never of avail. ⟦s5⟧ How then does the expositor say that this ought to be understood of the faith which has not the time of working, that it avails for knowing God, and for other things? ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ This is said for this reason, lest anyone think it [0580A] suffices for salvation, namely to believe and to have faith without works, when he has the faculty of working well.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVII. ⟦s2⟧ *Whose names are written,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what the book of life is. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The book of life is the predestination of God, in which all who are to be saved are written, whether at least through predestination alone, or also through justice and predestination.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVIII. ⟦s2⟧ *Rejoice in the Lord always [n: (Philipp. IV)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ The expositor says that joy in the world and joy in God are contrary, and cannot be at the same time in the same person. ⟦s4⟧ But is there not someone who is divided, and rejoices partly in the world and partly in the Lord, just as he loves partly the world and partly God? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ Anyone is said [0580B] to rejoice with that joy which is known to preponderate in him; and that part of the inner man which the joy of the world occupies, the joy which is in the Lord does not hold, because they cannot be in the same person concerning the same thing.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIX. ⟦s2⟧ *Let your petitions be made known with God,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Does not God know all things fully and perfectly before they come to pass? how then does the Apostle say, That the petitions be made known with God? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The sense is: Let your petitions be so vehement and not lukewarm that they be worthy to be heard and fulfilled. ⟦s6⟧ For when they are fulfilled, it becomes known to us that they reach to God.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XX. ⟦s2⟧ The expositor says that the angels offer [0580C] our prayers to God: therefore it is asked how this offering is to be understood. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Saving the reverence due to hidden things, we say that for our prayers to be offered to God by the angels is nothing other than for them to be made worthy through them, that they may be heard by God. ⟦s5⟧ Likewise the angels are intermediaries between us and God: for as they announce the divine secrets to us, so the things which are done here among us they are said to announce to God.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXI. ⟦s2⟧ But since God's own perfection suffices for knowing all things, it is asked to what end the angels announce anything to him? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Not that they may teach him, but that they may consult his eternal and unchangeable truth, and that they may know what is to be done by them, what, and to whom, and when it is to be announced.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0580D]QUESTION XXII. ⟦s2⟧ *I seek not the gift, but the fruit,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what difference there is between gift and fruit. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He calls the gift that which is given, as food, coin, garment. ⟦s6⟧ But the fruit is the intention of the giver: which is therefore called fruit, because according to it the work is judged useful and fruitful. ⟦s7⟧ Or he calls the fruit the reward itself. ⟦s8⟧ Whence a good work is called a flower, from which is born the fruit of eternal life. ⟦s9⟧ Note that by the raven which fed Elijah is understood the gift, where the good work was without a good intention. ⟦s10⟧ By the widow who fed the same prophet is understood the fruit; where there was a good work with a good intention.
