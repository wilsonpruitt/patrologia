## VII. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE COLOSSIANS.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0581]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0581A] *[n: (Coloss. I)] Paul an apostle,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Apostle writes this epistle to the Colossians, who are Asians: to whom the Apostle himself did not preach, but his disciples, namely Archippus and Epaphras. ⟦s3⟧ But Archippus had received a ministry among them; Epaphras indeed was sprung from them, and, instructed by the Apostle, confirmed the teaching of Archippus. ⟦s4⟧ But afterward, when false apostles came in and preached carnal observances, it had come into doubt among them whom they ought to believe. ⟦s5⟧ Whence the Apostle, whose authority was renowned, judges as it were a mediator which side ought rather to be held, writing to them from Ephesus. ⟦s6⟧ The intention therefore of the Apostle in this epistle is to confirm the Colossians in that faith and doctrine which they received from his disciples, and he teaches that hope is to be placed in nothing besides [0581B] Christ. ⟦s7⟧ The manner of treatment is such: after his wonted fashion he sends greeting beforehand; then he gives thanks for their good things, commending their faith and love. ⟦s8⟧ He prays that they may be perfected in Christ, whose benefits, and primacy according to both natures, he commends, and afterward recounts the dignity of his own ministry, and warns them not, seduced by philosophy or by the ceremonies of the law, to depart from Christ. ⟦s9⟧ Finally he instructs all together, and separately — namely the sexes, and ages, and conditions — morally. ⟦s10⟧ At the end he warns Archippus to be solicitous of his ministry.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION I. *Giving thanks,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The expositor says that here the Apostle begins to show that the law [0581C] does not profit, but harms. ⟦s3⟧ Is not the law good, and given by God, and did it not only not harm, but profit in its own state? how then is it true that it does not profit, but harms? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ It is true that the law before grace profited; but after grace it was useless. ⟦s6⟧ To which solution it is thus objected: Does not the law even now bear witness to the truth, and through it are we not illuminated in the knowledge of the Deity? therefore even now in the time of grace the law profits us. ⟦s7⟧ How then is it true: The law does not profit, but harms? ⟦s8⟧ Solution. ⟦s9⟧ The law read, and spiritually understood, profits, and does not harm: but observed according to the letter it does not profit, but harms.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION II. ⟦s2⟧ *Who hath made us worthy to be partakers of the lot of the saints.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what he calls the lot, of which [0581D] we have been made partakers. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The future inheritance, which is given by lot — that is, by divine grace and will — without merits, the Apostle here calls the lot.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION III. ⟦s2⟧ Likewise it is asked how the future inheritance is given to the saints without merits, since through faith, and love, and good works they merit it. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. [0582A] It is to be understood without merits preceding faith, not without those following: which are of grace, and therefore are not excluded, when it is said that by grace alone future glory is given. ⟦s4⟧ Of these we have already spoken above.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION IV. ⟦s2⟧ *He hath translated us into the kingdom of the Son.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what he means by *the kingdom of the Son of God.* ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The kingdom of heaven, or of the Son, is taken in several ways, namely for future glory, as there: *Thy kingdom come [n: (Matth. VI)]*; or for the present Church, as there: *When he shall have delivered up the kingdom to God and the Father [n: (I Cor. XV)]*; or for the faith of Christ, as there: *The kingdom of God is within you [n: (Matth. XII)]*; or for Holy Scripture, as there: *The kingdom of heaven shall be taken away from you, and shall be given to a nation yielding fruit [n: (Matth. XXI)]*.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0582B]QUESTION V. *Of the Son of his love.* ⟦s2⟧ Is not the Holy Spirit the love by which the Father loves the Son, and the Son the Father, and is Christ the son of love? ⟦s3⟧ It seems therefore that he is the son of the Holy Spirit: which faith does not receive. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Love is commonly taken for the divine nature, or substance, or essence, when he is proclaimed to be the son of love. ⟦s6⟧ For the Son is consubstantial and coessential with the Father in nature, substance, and essence. ⟦s7⟧ But what love signifies, when it is said: The Holy Spirit is the love by which the Father loves the Son, and conversely, has been expounded elsewhere. ⟦s8⟧ Concerning redemption likewise, and the image, and that Christ is called the head of the Church, it has been already said: nor is it needful that we do things already done.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0582C]QUESTION VI. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked, however, whether Christ according to his humanity was the head of the saints who preceded the incarnation. ⟦s3⟧ It seems that he was: but he who was not yet [head] according to his humanity, how could he be their head? ⟦s4⟧ Or if [he was], how could they have [a head] that was not yet head? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ Christ even according to [sic: *humanitatom*] can be called the head of those who were from Abel, because through faith all who are to be saved, as many as are saved, [are saved]. ⟦s7⟧ Note that Christ is proclaimed to be of the same nature with the Father, where he is shown to be the son of love. ⟦s8⟧ But where [he is called] the image, not only the identity of the same substance, but also personal property is intimated. ⟦s9⟧ Where he is said to be the firstborn of every creature, there he is asserted to be coeternal with the Father. ⟦s10⟧ Where [0582D] it is read that all things were founded through him, his omnipotence is declared. ⟦s11⟧ Where it is said that *all things consist in him [n: (Coloss. I)]*, his immensity is made manifest, whose eternity is intimated in John, where it is written: *In the beginning was the Word [n: (Joan. I)]*; and personal property when it is subjoined: *And the Word was with God [n: (ibid.)]*; and identity of substance, [0583A] when it is said: *And the Word was God [n: (Joan. I)]*. ⟦s12⟧ And so the Son is coeternal, consubstantial, and co-omnipotent with the Father; another in person, the same with the Father the beginning of all creatures.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VII. ⟦s2⟧ *And he,* namely Christ, *is the head of the Church.* ⟦s3⟧ It is said that the Church began first from Abel, who was the first just man, but was not Adam just? ⟦s4⟧ For it is believed that after the fall he was justified through penance and faith: why then is the Church not said to have begun from Adam? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ From Adam the Church contracted the stain of original sin, from Abel none, and therefore it is better said to have begun from Abel than from Adam; because Scripture makes mention of the sin of Adam, and not of his justice, and of the justice of Abel, and not of his sin. ⟦s7⟧ Christ [0583B] therefore is always the head of all who have been from the beginning, as of those who followed him: through whom alone we have access to the Father.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VIII. ⟦s2⟧ *See that no one deceive you through philosophy and vain deceit [n: (Coloss. II)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ Was not the knowledge of natural things, which the philosophers handed down, useful for knowing the invisible things of God? ⟦s4⟧ For through the visible forms of visible things, the teaching of which is taught in mathematics, one comes to the invisible causes of visible things, which physics teaches: through which one comes to know invisible substances, and the invisible natures of invisible substances. ⟦s5⟧ How then does the Apostle call such knowledge vain deceit? ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ The Apostle does not reprehend the philosophers for [0583C] this, that they inquired into the natures of things; but for this, that they endeavored to confine the power of God, though it be infinite, under natural causes, saying that God does nothing against nature. ⟦s8⟧ Whence they said that for God to become a creature, for a virgin to bear, for a dead man to live again, was impossible. ⟦s9⟧ This error of theirs therefore the Apostle rightly reprehends: not the true knowledge which they had concerning natures.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION IX. ⟦s2⟧ *In whom dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead.* ⟦s3⟧ Since the fulness of the Godhead is one and simple, how does he say all the fulness of the Godhead, since 'all' is wont to gather a multitude? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ It is said 'all the fulness of the Godhead,' that may be noted [sic: *I otetur*]: that it is there in every mode of indwelling, even personally. ⟦s6⟧ Or otherwise: All the fulness is said, that it may be intimated [0583D] that in him is the fulness not only of knowledge, but also of power and goodness. ⟦s7⟧ Whereby is destroyed the error of those who say that the man assumed has all the knowledge through grace which the Word has through nature, but not all the power.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION X. *The fulness of the Godhead bodily.* ⟦s2⟧ Since the Godhead is incorporeal, it is asked how it can dwell bodily in Christ. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ He says 'bodily,' that is, completively, solidly, and truly, with regard to the legal figures, which were the shadow of things to come, whose body — that is, fulfillment and truth — is Christ. ⟦s5⟧ Or otherwise: 'Bodily' signifies not the nature of the existing thing, but rather [0584A] the mode of existing. ⟦s6⟧ For just as a body so has being naturally in one place that it cannot at the same time be in another: so the fulness of the Godhead has being in the man assumed: in which mode it is in no other creature. ⟦s7⟧ In every creature it is by essence, in the just by grace, in Christ alone according to personal property. ⟦s8⟧ This then is for the Godhead to dwell bodily in Christ, that is, personally. ⟦s9⟧ Just as a body is in several places at once, not bodily but spiritually. ⟦s10⟧ In saying this I do not deny the truth and the essence of the Lord's body to be at once in several altars, but by this I intimate that it does not have that mode of existing common with other bodies, or from the nature of a body, but rather common with a spirit — not created, but uncreated. ⟦s11⟧ Both are [0584B] wonderful, and true: that a created spirit is somewhere bodily, and that the sacramental body of Christ is in several places spiritually.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XI. ⟦s2⟧ *Blotting out the handwriting of the decree, which was contrary to you,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what he calls the decree, what the handwriting. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ By 'decree' he names either the law of Moses itself, or the precept of God which he gave to the first man in paradise, saying: *Of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil thou shalt not eat [n: (Gen. II)]*; by 'handwriting,' the record of each violated decree: and the handwritings of all faults have been blotted out by the blood shed without fault.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XII. ⟦s2⟧ *Which are a shadow of things to come, but the body is Christ's.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how shadow, body, and spirit are distinguished. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ One thing is shadow, [0584C] another body, another spirit: which three are called by other names figure, thing, truth, so that the same is shadow and figure; the same body and thing; the same spirit and truth. ⟦s6⟧ The legal ceremonies were called the shadow and figure of things to come. ⟦s7⟧ The sacraments of grace are the body, or thing, of those shadows or figures: the spiritual grace, namely, which the sacraments of the New Testament confer and signify, is called the spirit or truth: but the sacraments of the Old Testament only signify spiritual grace, and do not confer it: and this is the difference between these sacraments and those. ⟦s8⟧ Likewise it is to be known that those things which in the time of the law were precepts, now in the time of grace are not precepts; but only testimonies of truth, whence then it was a sin not to [0584D] observe even one. ⟦s9⟧ But now it is not a sin not to keep them; nay, he who keeps them, offends.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIII. ⟦s2⟧ *Mortify your members [n: (Coloss. III)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what, or what things, he calls members, which are to be mortified. ⟦s4⟧ Are the eye, the hand, the foot to be mortified? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ Here the Apostle calls members the concupiscence of the members, as he himself subjoins, and expounds *fornication,* etc.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIV. ⟦s2⟧ *Covetousness, which is the service of idols,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Is not covetousness one thing, idolatry another? how then does he call covetousness the service of idols? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He therefore compares covetousness to idolatry, because the malice is not unlike. ⟦s6⟧ For just as the idolater worships a block, so the covetous man his coin. ⟦s7⟧ And just as the idolater strives to take away from God his honor, so the covetous man usurps to himself alone the things [0585A] which God created in common for all.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XV. ⟦s2⟧ *Putting off the old man,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what he here calls the old man. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The old man is sometimes called Adam the first man, who by sinning clothed himself and all his posterity with the tunic of oldness. ⟦s6⟧ The old man is sometimes called any man who bears the image of the earthly; but sometimes the old man is called oldness itself, which consists in fault and in punishment; sometimes the old man is named the habit of vices, as here, according to the expositor.
