## VII. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE COLOSSIANS. (cont.)

QUESTION XVI. *And put on the new man.* It is asked likewise what he here calls the new man? For the new man is sometimes called Christ, from whom [0585B] is all newness; sometimes the new man is called anyone renewed in Christ; sometimes also the habit of virtues; sometimes specially the mind of reason. But above, that which is called the inner man was said to be the mind; here, however, it is said that the inner mind is called the new man: which seems to be contrary, since the new man is one thing, the inner another, as has already been shown. Solution. The expositor says that he calls the rational mind the new man, nor is it contrary to what was said above, that the inner mind is called man: for the same thing, on account of diverse properties, obtains diverse names.

QUESTION XVII. *Who is renewed unto the knowledge of God according to his image,* etc. Likewise it is asked how [0585C] the mind is said to be renewed according to the image of God, since it is itself the image of God — can it be renewed according to itself? Solution. It is the same image which is renewed, and that according to which it is renewed, just as we say that someone is dead according to the body, that is, in body, not according to the spirit. And so the mind is renewed according to itself, that is, in itself. In the Epistle to the Ephesians, however, it is said that this very renewal is made according to God: therefore according to God, lest it be said to be according to a creature. For through such a renewal it is made like and conformed to God: therefore it is said to be according to him. Note that this image, which is renewed in the knowledge of God, is found equally in the man and in the woman: which is other [0586A] than that of which it was said above: *The man is the image of God, and the woman of the man.*

QUESTION XVIII. *Forgiving one another, if any have a complaint against another.* This same thing the Lord commanded, saying: *Forgive your debtors [n: (Matth. VI)]*, but what is it that can be forgiven by us? what is the debt that can be exacted by us from him who has done us injury? Solution. This is to forgive the injury inflicted, to demand for ourselves in no way satisfaction on account of the injury: certain other things upon this have been said by us elsewhere.

QUESTION XIX. The expositor says that he who abstains from unlawful things has praise; but he who tempers himself from lawful things, praise and reward — as if he said: The one avoids punishment, the other also merits a crown. But does not also he [0586B] who abstains from unlawful things have not only praise, but also reward? Solution. He who abstains from unlawful things always avoids punishment, but does not always merit a crown, except only then, when he is tempted and assailed.

QUESTION XX. *Do all things in the name of the Lord.* By 'all things' he understands nothing but good things, which are to be done by us. Whence it is asked whether he prescribes that all things to be done be done for God's sake. Am I by the precepts constrained to give my cloak to a poor man whom I see to be in need, and which I know to please God? For if I withhold it, I seem to despise what I know he wishes to be done. Solution. It is to be understood thus: Do all things in the name of the Lord, that is, do nothing against God, and in good things seek not your own glory, but God's. Not [0586C] however in all things that we do do we merit; because certain things are indifferent, as to sit, to stand, to draw breath, to close the hand, to open the eyes, and the like. But certain things are such as have merit if they be done; and are not a sin if they be not done, as to give a cloak to a poor man, and to sell all things, and give to the poor: which is of the perfect, who follow Christ naked.

QUESTION XXI. *Say to Archippus: Take heed to the ministry which thou hast received in the Lord, that thou fulfil it [n: (Coloss. IV)]*. It is asked whether subjects may admonish prelates. Solution. Behold authority, that subjects may admonish prelates, lest they be slothful in foreseeing those things which pertain to salvation.
