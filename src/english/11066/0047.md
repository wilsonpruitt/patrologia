## VIII. ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO THE THESSALONIANS.

[0585]

[0585D] *[n: (I Thess. I.)] Paul and Silvanus,* etc. The Apostle writes this epistle to the Thessalonians. Thessalonica is the metropolis of Macedonia, which is a province of the Greeks. The Thessalonians therefore are Macedonians, or Greeks, who, converted by the Apostle, could be moved from the truth of the faith neither by tribulations nor by false preachers. These the Apostle praises together, [0586D] because they had drunk in the spirit of so great a faith that, even in the hope of things to come, with devoted mind for the name of Christ he sustained perils from his fellow-citizens who did not believe. Yet there were some among them idle and curious; some also thinking less rightly about the resurrection, and therefore grieving too tenderly over the death of their friends. These therefore the Apostle corrects in this epistle, and warns the perfect [0587A] not to yield to adversities and false apostles, and to correct others. He wrote, moreover, from Athens. And the intention of the Apostle in this epistle is to correct the depraved and the uncorrected, and to exhort the good to perseverance and to the correction of others. The manner is such: after his own fashion he sends greeting beforehand: which sent beforehand, he gives thanks for their good things, recounting not their faith and works only, but also the manner of their conversion and their endurance of evils, that he may provoke them to persevere; and he inserts also somewhat concerning his own labors, and the truth of the Gospel, and his prudent conduct among them, and with how great affection he desires to see them. Then he beseeches the depraved to restrain themselves from luxury, and idleness, and curiosity, and he confirms that the dead shall rise again. Toward the end is moral instruction. And so he says, Paul and Sylvanus, etc. [0587B] Timothy, etc. Note that it was the custom of the Apostle to join to himself in the salutation those who either had been with him among them, or were to be.

QUESTION I. *To the living and true God,* etc. It is asked why he says, true. Solution. There is one God naturally; many by participation: to distinguish from whom he is called the true God, inasmuch as he is God of himself, not from elsewhere. For the others are gods not from the fact that they are, but from the fact that they have received.

QUESTION II. *For the wrath of God is come upon them. [n: (I Thess. II)]*. It is asked how, on account of their sins, the wrath of God and punishment came upon them, that is, before death. Is eternal punishment lightened by temporal punishment? for if it is not lightened, God seems merciless, who punishes here, and there. Likewise, if the eternal [0587C] is lightened by the temporal, it seems that such punishment proceeds not from the wrath of God, but from mercy; and therefore it is to be called not wrath, but mercy. Solution. Great indeed is the mercy of God to call to penance through temporal punishment; but they, unwilling to repent, turn the mercy of God for themselves into wrath, and the punishment inflicted, which could have been a means of salvation, becomes for them the beginning of eternal punishment.

QUESTION III. *We hastened to see your face with much desire,* etc. The expositor says wherein evils would come upon them; but it is asked how from the presence of the Teacher evils would come upon the disciples: since rather good things than evils seem to come from it: whence he himself desired to see them for the profit of his own presence, [0587D] namely that he might confirm them, and add over what was lacking. Solution. Greater is the crown, and more praiseworthy the victory, if they should stand immovable, the master being absent: yet the Apostle humanly feared lest they fall, and therefore desired to come to them.

QUESTION IV. *At his coming,* namely of the Lord. It is asked, at what coming. Solution. Threefold is the coming of the Lord, one in the flesh, one in the spirit, one in the flesh. He came in the flesh made man. He comes in the spirit when he pours his Spirit into our spirit, healing and justifying it. He shall come in the flesh at the judgment. He comes also secretly to each one at death, either to purge them, or to open the gate of the kingdom: to the evil, to punish them.

QUESTION V. *That you be not sorrowful, even as the rest [0588A] who have no hope. [n: (I Thess. IV)]*. It is asked whether to be sorrowful and to weep for the death of friends is a sin, since the Apostle says: That you be not sorrowful? Solution. It is necessary that, when death seizes one beloved, it sadden the affection of love; it is therefore not a fault, if we be saddened by the necessity of losing, if we be consoled by the hope of receiving again: whence the Apostle does not say simply, that you be not sorrowful, but says, that you be not sorrowful as the rest who have no hope, as if he said: It is lawful to be sorrowful, but with hope.

QUESTION VI. It is asked how the Lord wept for Lazarus, dead but presently, at his own bidding, about to live? Solution. Not the dead man, but death, which he had not deserved by sinning, did he bewail: to which, and to the miseries of the world, Lazarus was again to be recalled: and thence was the Lord [0588B] moved.

QUESTION VII. It is asked whether prayers, alms, the sacrifices of the altar, profit all for whom they are done. Solution. The aforesaid profit after death only those who so lived before death that they are worthy that these be profitable to them after death: for they profit not those who went forth from this life without faith working through love; because, living here, they lacked the pledge of salvation, namely unto salvation.

QUESTION VIII. Since Christ can do all things, it is asked why he did not straightway grant immortality to his faithful, that they should never at all experience death. Solution. If this were done, a certain felicity indeed would be given to the flesh, but the fortitude of faith would be diminished. No one then would hasten to the grace of Christ for the sake of the future [0588C] life; but only for the sake of fleeing the trouble of death, and so in a certain manner delicately would men believe in Christ. Where then would be the glorious triumph of the martyrs?

QUESTION IX. *And those who have slept.* It is asked, since he calls Christ, who now lives, dead, why he calls the faithful dead not dead, but sleeping? Solution. He therefore says Christ died, and rose again, that, while we hear this, we may hope the same, namely that we shall rise again through the power of the Godhead. But the elect he therefore calls sleeping; because no one can be so easily wakened from *sommo*, as easily as God will waken them all from the *sommo* of death.

QUESTION X. *At the voice of the archangel,* etc. Concerning this voice [0588D] it is asked, whether it will be material. Solution. Some say that it will be material, formed by the ministry of angels, just as that was material which was heard when the Lord was baptized, which is therefore called God's; because God will give it efficacy, that through it the dead may rise. Others say that it will be some evident sign. Concerning the uncertain, an ambiguous solution. For all things future at the last day are uncertain to us.

QUESTION XI. *Then we who live.* It is asked how the Apostle says that those *first shall rise* who were *dead* long since, and then those who shall then be found alive, since he himself elsewhere says that the resurrection shall be *in the twinkling of an eye and in a moment [n: (I Cor. XV)]*; wherein there is nothing prior, nor posterior, but all things at once? Solution. It can be said that the resurrection of the dead is therefore said [0589A] to be made at once, because there will be a little delay.

QUESTION XII. It is wont, however, to be asked whether those whom Christ shall find alive are to die, or to pass without death to immortality? Solution. Concerning this we have nothing certain; yet it seems to some that in the very rapture they die, and revive, since elsewhere the Apostle says: *In Christ all shall be made alive [n: (I Cor. XV)]*, and elsewhere: *that which thou sowest is not quickened, except it first die [n: (ibid.)]*. Nor ought this to seem incredible, since in a moment, and in the twinkling of an eye, the common and general resurrection is believed to be about to be.

QUESTION XIII. *We shall be caught up to meet Christ in the air.* It is asked whether this is said of all? Solution. The good, as it were light, shall run to meet Christ in the air; but the evil, [0589B] as it were weighty, shall cleave not to meet Christ in the air, but to the earth, which they always loved. Note that there are four kinds of those who shall come to the judgment; for some shall come to judge only, as the very good, not to be judged, of whose salvation it is settled. Others are the less perfect, of whose salvation it is not yet settled; these shall come to the judgment, to be judged. So there are two kinds of the evil; some shall come to the judgment, to be judged, as the less evil, of whose damnation it is not settled. But others, not to be judged; [0590A] because they are already judged, since concerning their damnation it is already certain. Why then shall they come? shall it be to judge? God forbid! Why then? that they may hear with the devil: *Go, ye cursed, into everlasting fire [n: (Matth. XXV)]*, etc. Concerning the manner of the judgment it has already been said in the first epistle to the Corinthians; namely how the saints shall judge, whether by comparison alone, or also by power.

QUESTION XIV. *The day of the Lord shall so come as a thief in the night [n: (I Thess. V)]*, etc. Since no one knows whether by day or by night the Lord shall come to the judgment, it is asked how the Apostle calls the time of the Lord's coming day. Solution. Day in such a place is not put for the illumination of the air as elsewhere; but for revelation, that is, manifestation, because then all things shall be manifested. For each one shall know why the Lord saves or damns him [0590B] or another.

QUESTION XV. *To pray without ceasing.* It is asked how this precept of the Apostle can be fulfilled. For who can always pray? Is it not needful sometimes to sleep? Solution. It is thus understood: Without ceasing at the fixed hours. Or by prayer is understood holy desire, and pious affection, and so the just man never ceases to pray, unless he cease to be just; for he who always does well, always prays well.
