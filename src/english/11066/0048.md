## IX. ON THE SECOND EPISTLE TO THE THESSALONIANS.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0589]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0589C] *Paul, and Silvanus [n: (II Thess. I)]*, etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Apostle writes this epistle to the Thessalonians. ⟦s3⟧ For, a graver tribulation having arisen among them, he warns them to patience, showing the just judgment of God, that the good may attain glory, the evil punishment. ⟦s4⟧ And because in the first epistle he says certain things concerning the Coming of the Lord and concerning the resurrection of the dead, whence the day of the Lord was thought to be at hand; now he writes another epistle, in which he signifies, though obscurely (for he cannot do so openly), concerning the abolition of the Roman kingdom, concerning the appearing of Antichrist, and his damnation, and concerning the disquiet of certain brethren. ⟦s5⟧ He writes also that the day of the Lord is not at hand, as by occasion of the former epistle it seemed to some. ⟦s6⟧ And so the intention of the Apostle in this epistle is to move the good and the quiet [0589D] to patience, and to correct the unquiet; and to open somewhat here what he had said obscurely in the former epistle. ⟦s7⟧ The manner is such: first he salutes, then he gives thanks for their good things; afterward he warns to patience, and to constancy; thence he asserts that Antichrist shall forestall the coming of Christ, and announces some signs of the coming of Antichrist, though obscurely, treating of the abolition of the Roman kingdom and of the slaying of Antichrist. ⟦s8⟧ But toward the end he beseeches them to rebuke the curious and the idle.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION I. *In a flame of fire, giving vengeance to them who have not known God.* ⟦s2⟧ It is asked, whether the Lord [0590C] shall come to the judgment surrounded in fire? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ He is said to be about to come in a flame of fire, because he shall appear terrible to the ungodly, to burn and to torment them. ⟦s5⟧ Fire indeed shall precede his coming, by which the elements shall be dissolved, that they may be renewed.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION II. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked how those shall be kept alive whom the Lord shall find alive. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Just as the fire did not hurt the three children in the furnace of the oven; so that fire preceding the coming of the judge shall not hurt the good.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION III. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked what punishments the souls of the ungodly now suffer among those below, whether material, or only tortures of conscience? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ It is believed that they suffer material punishments, as fire and cold. ⟦s5⟧ Whence it is said: They shall pass *from the waters of snows to [0590D] excessive heat [n: (Job. XXIV)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION IV. ⟦s2⟧ Likewise it is asked how they are tormented by these punishments, since spiritual things cannot be touched by bodily. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ It can come about that they be punished by those things by which they are not touched, just as one contracts a great horror from something seen or imagined, although he be not touched by it.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION V. Likewise, their bodies being resumed, since these shall thereafter be immortal, how shall they be punished in them, since they cannot be dissolved? ⟦s2⟧ Solution. ⟦s3⟧ The bodies of the evil shall indeed be immortal, but passible, [0591A] that is, such in which the evil may suffer, yet without harm to the natural quantity, or essence, of the bodies themselves.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VI. ⟦s2⟧ *Except there come a departure first [n: (II Thess. II)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked: how shall that which he says concerning the departure come to pass? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ This can be understood in four ways, either of the earthly Roman empire, or of the spiritual empire of the Roman Church, or of the faith, or of Antichrist. ⟦s6⟧ For another translation says the Runaway. ⟦s7⟧ Note that it is read that Antichrist shall be born in Babylon of the tribe of Dan. According to which Jacob says: *Let Dan be a serpent in the way, and a horned snake in the path [n: (Gen. XLIX)]*. ⟦s8⟧ Who, when he shall first manifest himself, shall come to Jerusalem, and shall circumcise himself, saying: I am Christ promised to the Jews.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0591B]QUESTION VII. ⟦s2⟧ *Who is lifted up above all that is called God,* as the gods of the nations or the saints; *or that is worshipped,* as God the Trinity. ⟦s3⟧ But it is asked how he shall be able to lift himself up above God the Trinity? ⟦s4⟧ Will not this be his greatest pride and self-exaltation, that he shall say himself to be Christ, and thus equal to God — for he will not say himself greater; how then is he lifted up above all that is worshipped as God? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ As it seems to me, this self-exaltation is understood in this, that that wicked one, *the man of sin, the son of perdition,* shall cause the veneration and worship due to God the Trinity alone to be exhibited to himself and not to God. ⟦s7⟧ It is read that just as in Christ dwelt the fulness of the Godhead, so in Antichrist there shall be the fulness of malice, and of all iniquity, and, so to say, the fulness of devilishness.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0591C]QUESTION VIII. ⟦s2⟧ Whence it can be asked whether he will be a man simply, or whether the devil will assume him personally, as God man. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ The devil cannot so assume a man that he be personally united to himself; he will therefore be a pure man, whom nevertheless the devil will so possess that he shall fill him with all the power of his wickedness, and with all iniquity, so that, utterly given over and devoted to him, he shall wish nothing, be able to do nothing, save what the devil wishes and is able to do.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION IX. ⟦s2⟧ *The mystery of iniquity already worketh.* ⟦s3⟧ The expositor says that in Nero and in other evil men the devil already works secretly, who in Antichrist shall openly rage. ⟦s4⟧ Whence it is asked how he works secretly in Nero. ⟦s5⟧ Did not Nero deny Christ, and [0591D] openly persecute those who preached Christ? ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ He is therefore said to work secretly in Nero; because Nero did this by the fraudulent suggestion of certain men. ⟦s8⟧ Or, which is better, the devil is therefore said to work secretly in Nero, not because that persecution was not manifest, but because it is a shadow, and figure, and a certain image of that which shall be done through Antichrist, which shall be much graver than all which have gone before. ⟦s9⟧ For he shall reign three years and a half; and, sitting in a pavilion on the Mount of Olives, he shall be slain by the power of the Holy Spirit through Michael, as the doctors say, or the Lord himself, or some other angel.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION X. *Whom the Lord shall destroy with the brightness of his coming,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Shall not Antichrist first be slain [0592A] before the Lord come to the judgment? ⟦s3⟧ For from the book of Daniel it is understood that forty-two days shall be granted to the elect for penance after the death of Antichrist. ⟦s4⟧ But how long after the Lord shall come is utterly unknown not only to men, but even to the angels. ⟦s5⟧ How then does the Apostle say: With the brightness of his coming (since before the coming he is to be slain) the Lord shall destroy him? ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ Antichrist shall indeed be slain, as is believed, before the coming of the Lord, by the dissolution of body and soul; yet the Lord shall destroy him, now raised again with his whole body, with the brightness of his coming, damning him, and sending him into everlasting fire, saying to him and to the other reprobate: *Go, ye cursed, into everlasting fire [n: (Matth. XXV)]*.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0592B]QUESTION XI. ⟦s2⟧ *According to the working of Satan,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ The expositor says, yet not without sense, as the frenzied, who have no fault for the evils which they do. ⟦s4⟧ Whence it can be asked, if Antichrist shall do the whole with the devil instigating and cooperating, and in a manner compelling, why it is imputed to him rather than to the frenzied, or if the frenzied do this with the devil instigating, why it is not imputed to them. ⟦s5⟧ And how are they without fault for the evils which they do, rather than that wicked one? ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ The frenzied, who are without sense, are rather to be said to suffer something than to do something; and evils are done rather through them than by them; since they themselves are not the authors, seeing they do not apply the will to this. ⟦s8⟧ But Antichrist shall so do all things, with the devil instigating, that he shall apply his whole will and [0592C] sense to the same; and therefore all things shall be imputed to him unto eternal punishment.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XII. ⟦s2⟧ *With signs, and lying wonders,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked concerning those signs which Antichrist shall do through the devil, whether they are therefore called lying, because they are not true as they seem, but phantastic; that is, because he is about to deceive mortal senses through phantasms, or whether they are therefore called lying, because by God's permission they shall draw men to a lie? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ It seems to us to be probable, that all those signs which the devil shall be able to do, Antichrist shall also do truly. ⟦s6⟧ But those which the devil has not in his power, neither shall that wicked one do truly, but by magic art alone he shall delude the eyes of mortals, that he may seem to do what [0592D] he shall not truly do. ⟦s7⟧ Whence the Apostle says: Whose coming shall be in all power, as regards those things which he shall do truly, and in all seduction, as regards those things which he shall not do truly. ⟦s8⟧ Yet all avail unto the seduction of the ungodly. ⟦s9⟧ But what those things are which the devil shall be able [to do], and what things lie not subject to his power, it is not for our littleness to unfold.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIII. ⟦s2⟧ *God shall send them the operation of error, to believe a lie, that they may be judged,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked, however, concerning those who, moved by the signs and wonders of that wicked one, shall believe him to be good, and the Son of God: whether they have any excuse? ⟦s4⟧ For if they do not exhibit divine worship to him, since their conscience dictates to them that he is to be adored as God, they shall sin unto death, and thus they seem to be inexcusable [0593A]. ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ The Apostle says that because they received not the brightness of the truth, that they were saved — that is, Christ formerly manifestly preached — God shall send them the operation of error, that they may believe a lie and be judged: wherein he sufficiently teaches that, on account of their preceding sins, they shall have no excuse, even if they judge themselves to render obedience to Christ, that is, to the true God, by believing in Antichrist.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XIV. ⟦s2⟧ Augustine says that to be able to have faith, or charity, is of the nature of the faithful: to have faith, or charity, is of the grace of God. ⟦s3⟧ Whence it can be asked how this ought to be understood. ⟦s4⟧ Is not that which is natural in our own faculty? but since we cannot have faith, or charity, without grace, how is to be able to have faith of the nature [0593B] of the faithful? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ To be able to have faith, that is, the potency of having, is natural; but to have the use of this potency is of grace alone, and not of nature.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XV. ⟦s2⟧ *He that will not work, let him not eat* [0594A] [n: (II Thess. III)]. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked of what work he here treats. ⟦s4⟧ For if of the outward, how does he prescribe that he not eat who will not work? ⟦s5⟧ For many are worthy to eat, though they work not. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ Augustine says that the Apostle wills the servants of God to work bodily, that they be not compelled by want to seek necessaries; yet they do not act ill who postpone outward works for spiritual ones when they have whereof to live, because *Mary hath chosen the best part [n: (Luc. X)]*. ⟦s8⟧ But those who, given over to curiosity, will not work, are reprehended; not those who forsake lesser goods for greater.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XVI. ⟦s2⟧ *If any obey not our word by an epistle, note that man, and do not keep company with him, that he may be confounded.* ⟦s3⟧ The Apostle says thus, that he [0594B] who resists the admonition of the Church is to be excommunicated. ⟦s4⟧ Whence it can be asked in what manner this is to be done. ⟦s5⟧ And it is certain that it is to be done with great discretion, lest the Church incur detriment, nor is such punishment to be exercised for a light cause.
