## X. ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO TIMOTHY. (cont.)

QUESTION XV. *Adam was not seduced, but the woman.* It is asked which of the two sinned more, whether Adam or Eve? Solution. The doctors say that the woman not only sinned, but also made the man sin. Against which solution it is thus objected: Adam was seduced, because he did not believe to be true what the enemy urged; but the woman was seduced, believing to be true what the serpent was saying; and thus it follows that he sinned knowingly, and she through ignorance; but it is graver to sin knowingly than through ignorance. Whence it follows that the man sinned more than the woman. Solution. [0597D] That ignorance did not have excuse, because it proceeded from fault; for doubting she answered, saying: *Lest perhaps we die [n: (Gen. III)]*. But the man thought of the mercy of God, and of penitence; and therefore he sinned less.

QUESTION XVI. It is asked how it is true that he was not seduced? Did he not believe that to be committed as venial, which was mortal? Therefore he too was in some way deceived. Solution. He was not seduced first, and in that in which the woman was, as the authority says. But is he not seduced, if he was not seduced first, and in that in which the woman was? For if someone is not wounded in the head, is he therefore not wounded? Does a simple negation follow upon a determinate negation? Solution. The doctors of the New Testament sometimes say [0598A] that something was not, which the Scripture of the Old Testament does not say was. Whence, because the scripture of Genesis, speaking of the seduction of the woman, says nothing of any seduction of the man, therefore the Apostle says: Adam was not seduced, but the woman. According to the same manner of speaking, elsewhere the same Apostle asserts that Melchisedech had no beginning, nor end, no father, no offspring, although in truth he had a beginning, and an end, and a father, and perhaps offspring; but because the scripture so introduces him that it said nothing either of his genealogy, or birth, or death, therefore the Apostle asserts that he had none of the aforesaid things. And Ambrose says that the man is blessed with the first woman, and not with the second, as if he were to say: In Holy Scripture we read the blessing of the man with the [0598B] first woman, but we do not read where God blesses the man with the second.

QUESTION XVII. *But the woman shall be saved through the generation of children.* If the generation of children saves, what will the continent woman or the virgin do? Will she not be saved, because she has no children? What is it that elsewhere the Apostle says: *But she will be more blessed, whether widow or virgin, if she so remain?* Solution. He does not place the cause of salvation in the generation of children, but rather in faith and love, whence he adds: *If she remain in faith and love.* And the sense is: even if the woman was the cause of sin, nevertheless she shall be saved — not only the continent woman, or the virgin, but even the married woman, though, never ceasing from the generation of children, but going forward through the generation of children, she should depart from this world, provided that they remain [0598C] in faith and love. Or it will avail her for an increase of salvation, if her children, through her teaching and diligence, remain in faith and love. Or rather it is understood mystically. The woman is a type of the flesh, which elsewhere is signified by the turtledove. Adam is a figure of reason, which is figured by the sparrow. The children are good works, which are understood by the young of the turtledove, which live only in the nest of the Catholic faith; outside they do not thrive, nay they are trodden down, because good works do not profit pagans, Jews, heretics, schismatics. Whence he says here: If she remain in faith and love, namely the children of the woman, the young of the turtledove, the good works.

QUESTION XVIII. *If any man desire the office of a bishop, he desireth a good work [n: (I Tim. III)]*, etc. But this ambitious man [0598D] desires the episcopate; can it be inferred, therefore he desires a good work? Solution. The episcopate is the attention over one's own and others' life, and thus it is the name of a work, which the good man desires, who, Rachel of beautiful countenance being set aside for a time, but not abandoned, with Jacob enters in to Leah bleary-eyed, on account of the fruitfulness of children, and descends from the mountain of contemplation with Moses to the fields of action. For no one ought so to be at leisure that in that same leisure he does not think of the profit of his neighbor, nor so busy that he does not seek the contemplation of God. The episcopate is also the name of a dignity, which the ambitious man desires, who wishes rather to preside than to profit.

QUESTION XIX. *The husband of one wife,* etc. It is asked whether he ought to be called a monogamist, and can be ordained, who [0599A] before baptism had a wife; and, she being dismissed, was reborn in Christ and afterward married a believing woman. Solution. Jerome says that such a man is not a bigamist, and that he can be ordained; to whom, now made new, neither debaucheries nor other things are imputed. But Augustine says that he is not a monogamist, nor ought to be ordained, although what he had done before does not stand against him; on account of the mystery which ought to be preserved in those to be ordained; which in the aforesaid man cannot be preserved.

QUESTION XX. But do Jerome and Augustine hold contrary opinions in the solution of the aforesaid question? Solution. Jerome says that such a man is a monogamist as regards merit, and it is not a sin if he be ordained. But Augustine says that he is not a monogamist as regards the force of the sacrament, nor can he be [0599B] ordained, where the sacrament of monogamy is to be preserved; nor is it required that it be preserved in every one to be ordained, otherwise a virgin could not be ordained, since he is not the husband of one wife. The Church is the convocation of many to the worship of the one God.

QUESTION XXI. *Great is the sacrament of piety,* etc. It is asked what is here called a sacrament. For sometimes a sacrament is called a sign of a sacred thing, as the sacrament of baptism and of the altar; sometimes it is called something hidden and secret, or a mystery. Solution. Christ, hidden according to his deity, is called a sacrament, who is the cause of all religion, and of true worship. For who was ever pious and devout in faith, except the knowledge of this sacrament having first been received?

QUESTION XXII. *Which was manifested in the flesh,* etc. [0599C] It is asked of what manifestation this treats. Solution. Of that which was made by preaching, by powers, and by signs shown in the assumed flesh.

QUESTION XXIII. *Justified in the spirit.* Did not the discourse precede concerning the Word of God? but this was not justified, but rather born just from eternity. Solution. When discourse is made about Christ, it must be diligently observed what is said, and in respect of what; this therefore, justified in the spirit, is understood of Christ according to the man assumed, according to whom he is free from all sin, having justice in every mode.

QUESTION XXIV. *He appeared to the angels.* Here we have authority that the angels advance in knowledge through [0599D] those things which are done in the Church, which we have already diligently pursued above upon the passage: that the manifold wisdom may become known, etc.; and the expositor testifies the same in this place.

QUESTION XXV. *Nothing is to be rejected that is received with thanksgiving [n: (I Tim. IV)]*. It is asked whether this is contrary to the legal doctrine, which distinguishes certain foods, calling some clean, others unclean? Solution. In the law certain animals were called unclean not by nature, but by signification: so that, if concerning the pig and the lamb it be inquired, each is clean in nature, because every creature is good; yet in signification the lamb is clean, the pig unclean.

QUESTION XXVI. *Piety is profitable to all things, having the promise [0600A] of the life that now is,* etc. It is asked what he calls the promise of the present life; for concerning the promise of the future life it is established. Solution. The sufficiency of temporal things and the abundance of spiritual things. For God promises both to his worshippers. The sufficiency of temporal things is promised, where it is said: *Seek first the kingdom of God, and all these things shall be added unto you [n: (Matth. VI)]*. The abundance of spiritual things, where it is written: *Whosoever shall leave these or those things for my sake, shall receive a hundredfold [n: (Matth. XIX)]*, that is, in the present the delightfulness of spiritual goods exceeds the affluence of temporal goods by as much as a hundred surpasses unity.

QUESTION XXVII. *Which was given to thee by prophecy,* etc. It is asked, what does he call prophecy? Solution. [0600B] He calls prophecy the choice of the holy ones who chose him for bishop. Or rather the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, through which the Apostle knew him to be worthy of the episcopate.

QUESTION XXVIII. *With the imposition of hands.* What is it? Solution. He calls the imposition of hands the mystical words, by which the one chosen is confirmed for this work, receiving authority, his conscience being witness, that he may dare to offer sacrifices in the Lord's stead.

QUESTION XXIX. *The laborer is worthy of his hire [n: (I Tim. V)]*, etc. It is asked whether that which is given by the people to the preacher is a due and worthy wage; and whether he sins, if he preaches for it; and whether he who does this sells the Gospel. Solution. Who would dare to say that is not a wage, which Truth itself calls a wage? [0600C] Yet he ought not to preach for such a wage, but that he may receive an eternal wage from the Lord. It is therefore of necessity to receive that whereby one lives; it is of charity to bestow, nay a debt, that those who receive spiritual things should minister carnal things. The preacher ought to receive, that he may preach; not to preach, that he may receive.

QUESTION XXX. *But them that sin reprove before all.* To this seems to be contrary what the Lord says in the Gospel: *If thy brother shall offend against thee, rebuke him between thee and him alone [n: (Matth. XVIII)]*. Does so good a disciple disagree with so good a Master? Solution. Both are true, and sometimes that which the Lord says with his own mouth, namely when thou art alone, is to be done; for a hidden fault [0600D] ought to be reproved in secret, as the just Joseph did concerning Mary, alone suspecting adultery; otherwise, if thou do otherwise, thou wilt be a betrayer, not a corrector. Sometimes also that is to be done which Truth said by the mouth of Paul, namely when one sins openly. For a public offense needs a public satisfaction.

QUESTION XXXI. *Use a little wine.* It is asked how he counseled this. Solution. He gives a special counsel, that he govern himself by wholesome instruction; for God wishes to be served prudently, not that by excessive abstinence they become weak, and afterward require the help of physicians for themselves.

QUESTION XXXII. *Languishing about questions, and strifes of words [n: (I Tim. VI)]*, etc. The question was, whether anyone was a slave, since all have arisen [0601A] from the same fathers, and Christ has redeemed all. Likewise there was a strife of words whether he had freed them, because the Lord says: *If the Son hath freed you, you are free indeed [n: (Joan. VIII)]*, which and similar things seem to dissent from the apostolic doctrine, which exhorts servants to be subject to their masters, and likewise from the words of the Lord saying: *Render therefore the things that are Caesar's to Caesar,* etc. [n: (Luc. XX.)] But such a controversy of words is to be resolved thus: this — if the Son shall have freed you, you are free indeed — is to be understood of spiritual liberty, not of carnal. But Paul speaks of carnal liberty and servitude, which the spiritual does not take away.
