## X. ON THE FIRST EPISTLE TO TIMOTHY. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXIII. ⟦s2⟧ *The root of all evils is cupidity.* ⟦s3⟧ Elsewhere Scripture says: *The beginning of every sin is pride [n: (Eccle. X)]*. ⟦s4⟧ But here, that it is avarice or [0601B] cupidity. ⟦s5⟧ For if avarice is held as a species for a genus, how is each true? for if pride is the beginning of every sin, therefore also of cupidity; and if cupidity is the root of all evils, therefore also of pride; and thus the same thing is the cause and the effect of the same. ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ When it is said that the root of all evils is cupidity, or that pride is the beginning of every sin, if the individual genera are understood, it is false; but if the genera of individuals, it is true. ⟦s8⟧ For there is no genus of sin which does not sometimes arise from pride, sometimes from cupidity. ⟦s9⟧ For there are some who covet riches, that through them they may be able to reach the summit of honor; there are others who seek dignity for this reason, that they may become richer.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXIV. ⟦s2⟧ *Alone powerful.* ⟦s3⟧ Are not the angels powerful [0601C], whence they are also called Virtues and Powers? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ God alone is powerful by nature, from that which he is; but the angels are powerful by grace, from that which they have received.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXV. ⟦s2⟧ *Who alone hath immortality,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Are not souls and angels also by nature immortal? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ God alone hath immortality, that is, immutability, because he neither could, nor can, nor will be able to sin. ⟦s6⟧ This immortality neither the angels nor the souls of the saints have; for although they are immortal by grace, yet they are truly believed to be mutable by nature.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXVI. ⟦s2⟧ *Who inhabiteth light inaccessible.* ⟦s3⟧ The prophet says: *Come ye to him, and be enlightened [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]*: is not God himself the very light which he is said to inhabit? ⟦s3⟧ How are we admonished to come [0602A] to this light, if it is itself inaccessible? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ No one comes to it of himself, but he to whom it is given by his gift.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXVII. ⟦s2⟧ *Whom no man hath seen, nor can see.* ⟦s3⟧ Did not Abraham see God, and Moses, and the other fathers? ⟦s4⟧ How then is it true that no one has ever seen God? ⟦s5⟧ Likewise, do not we too hope for the vision of him? ⟦s6⟧ But how, if no one can see him? ⟦s7⟧ Solution. ⟦s8⟧ God in his own nature is invisible to the eye of flesh; but those visions were exhibited to the holy fathers in a subject creature. ⟦s9⟧ Whence Moses, after those figures in which God was seen, said: *Lord, show me thy face [n: (Exod. XXXIII)]*. ⟦s10⟧ But in the future God shall be seen by the clean of heart, and now he is seen by the holy angels.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0602B]QUESTION XXXVIII. ⟦s2⟧ *Avoiding the profane novelties of words,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whether all novelties of words are to be avoided. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ No, because not all are profane, as this very name "Christian," "homoousion," "the new commandment," and "the new testament," and "the new song" — they call these novelties not profane, but sacred, and befitting religion. ⟦s6⟧ But "hypostasis," in the time of the heretics, denoted a profane novelty, which name the heretics used, now in the signification of person, now in the signification of substance, for the deception of the simple, so that, if they granted the Trinity to be one hypostasis, they would infer: Therefore they are one person; but if they said the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit to be three hypostases, they would conclude: Therefore they are three substances. ⟦s7⟧ But now [0602C] this word does not denote a profane novelty, because it has been reduced to the signification of person. ⟦s8⟧ Whence we now simply grant the Trinity to be three hypostases, and not one; which formerly was not to be granted without a qualification, when it still retained a manifold signification.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION XXXIX. ⟦s2⟧ *And the oppositions of knowledge falsely so called,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked what the Apostle calls knowledge of a false name. ⟦s4⟧ Is it logic? ⟦s4⟧ But if it is this, how does Augustine call dialectic the science of sciences, which not only makes one knowing, but even demonstrates the knower? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ As I judge, he calls knowledge of a false name the sophistical art, whose oppositions are to be avoided by the pious and meek, because they avail for nothing but the subversion of the simple.
