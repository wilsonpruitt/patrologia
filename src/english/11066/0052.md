## XI. ON THE SECOND EPISTLE TO TIMOTHY.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0601]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0601D] *Paul an apostle [n: (II Tim. I)]*, etc. ⟦s2⟧ Now about to pass from the world, he writes this second epistle from Rome, from prison, to Timothy, wearied by infirmities and adversities, that he may labor constantly in the grace of God entrusted to him, exhorting him to martyrdom in many ways, [0602D] and that he may persevere in the office of right preaching, and in holy working: and foretelling what shall be in the last times, and concerning his own death. ⟦s3⟧ And the intention of the Apostle in this epistle is to exhort Timothy to the diligent execution of his office, and to the palm [0603A] of martyrdom, and he adds still some things about the episcopal office. ⟦s4⟧ The manner is such: first he greets, then he gives thanks for the good which he has, where he shows his desire of seeing him: afterward he admonishes to preaching, and to the endurance of martyrdom, by his own example and in other ways: thence he says what men will be like in the last days: finally, concerning the time of his own dissolution which is at hand.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION I. *God hath not given us the spirit of fear.* ⟦s2⟧ It is asked of what fear this is said. ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Of the fear which the love of God expels.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION II. ⟦s2⟧ *Which was given us before the secular ages,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ When could anything be given to us, we not yet existing? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ It was given, that is, foreseen to be given: whence he does not say simply "it was given," but added "in Christ Jesus."

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0603B]QUESTION III. ⟦s2⟧ Likewise it is asked why he does not say [sic: *sempliciter*] *before the ages,* but adds, *secular.* ⟦s3⟧ Are not all times secular? ⟦s4⟧ To the difference of what, then, does he say "secular"? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ Some say that the angels were created before the creation of the world, and those spaces which were from the creation of the angels up to the creation of the world the Apostle elsewhere calls eternal times, to the difference of which he here says "secular times." ⟦s7⟧ But someone will say: Does not time denote mutability, and eternity immutability? ⟦s8⟧ How then can any times be eternal? how be at once mutable and immutable? ⟦s9⟧ Solution. ⟦s10⟧ The aforesaid doctors understand this thus: In those spaces which are called eternal times there was succession, and no passing-away: and thus a certain immutability, [0603C] and according to this a certain eternity. ⟦s11⟧ To this opinion Jerome seems to accord. ⟦s12⟧ But nearly all the doctors say that the angels were created not before, but with the world; but what, according to them, ought to be understood by "eternal times," let him see and inquire who follows their opinion. ⟦s13⟧ But to me the opinion of the former seems the stronger, saving the reverence due to hidden things: this I say, asserting nothing rashly.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION IV. ⟦s2⟧ *No man being a soldier to God entangleth himself with secular businesses [n: (II Tim. II)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ Do not active men war for God, and yet are entangled in secular businesses? how then does the Apostle say: No man being a soldier to God, etc.? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He calls businesses "secular" when the mind is occupied with the care of gathering money: [0603D] which no one can do at the same time, and war for God. ⟦s6⟧ But active men do not devote themselves to outward things for this, that they may gather money, but only for this, that they may provide necessary things for themselves and for others.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION V. *Of the seed of David according to my Gospel, wherein I labor even unto bonds.* ⟦s2⟧ It is asked of what tribe Christ and Mary were. ⟦s3⟧ But from this passage note that Christ was born of David: as in the beginning he says: *Of the seed of David according to the flesh.* ⟦s4⟧ And the archangel Gabriel of the same: *The Lord God shall give unto him the throne of David his father [n: (Luc. I)]*. ⟦s5⟧ These things I say on account of those who will not have the mother of the Lord to be of the seed of David, who was of the tribe of Judah, but say that she was only of the tribe of Levi, and on account of Joseph the husband of Mary, who was of the family of David, they say Christ is called of [0604A] the seed of David: and Origen (as it is said) thought thus. ⟦s6⟧ Others say — which also seems so to us — that Mary too proceeded from the stock of David, and so from the stock of Judah, not only from the stock of Levi. ⟦s7⟧ For the two tribes, the royal and the priestly, were intermingled, and therefore it is no wonder that the Virgin drew her origin from both, as one about to bring forth a true King and Priest. ⟦s8⟧ *Contend not in words.* ⟦s9⟧ Among the servants of God there ought to be no contention, but conference, and a modest inquiry into truth.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VI. ⟦s2⟧ *Saying that the resurrection is already made.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked of what resurrection the Apostle speaks: whether of that which is of minds, or of that which is of bodies? ⟦s4⟧ For if this be said of that which is of minds, which is made in baptism, how did those who said this [0604B] to be already made subvert the faith of some, since this is true and to be believed? ⟦s5⟧ Likewise, if they said this of the resurrection of bodies, how did they by this subvert the faith of some, since it is established to all that this is false? ⟦s6⟧ Solution. ⟦s7⟧ This can be understood of both resurrections: for by this, that they said the resurrection of minds to be already made, and no other of bodies to be future, they deceived, namely because they slyly denied the resurrection of bodies. ⟦s8⟧ Or also in this they subverted faith, that they said the resurrection of bodies to have been made: taking thence the occasion of their error, that it is written in the Gospel: *Many bodies of the saints arose, and appeared in the holy city [n: (Matth. XXVII)]*, [0604C] denying that the general resurrection, which we await, is to come.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VII. ⟦s2⟧ *In a great house there are not only vessels of gold and of silver,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked whom he understands by the vessels of gold and silver, and whom by the vessels of wood and of earthenware? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ By the vessels of gold and silver he understands the predestined; by the vessels of wood and earthenware, the reprobate. ⟦s6⟧ Or by the golden vessels are understood the good, whether they be predestined or not; by the wooden and earthen vessels, the evil, whether they be foreknown unto life, or reprobate. ⟦s7⟧ And thus according to this opinion, of both there are some unto honor, some unto contumely. ⟦s8⟧ But according to the former, unto honor only are the golden and silver vessels; unto contumely, the wooden and earthen. ⟦s9⟧ *Avoid foolish questions, and without discipline,* [0604D] etc. ⟦s10⟧ Here he hints that not all questions are to be avoided, but only those in which there is no fruit. ⟦s11⟧ *Having an appearance of piety, but denying the power thereof.* ⟦s12⟧ Such are many in this time, as the Apostle here describes, who love themselves, not God: having the name and habit of sanctity, while within they are full of all uncleannesses. ⟦s13⟧ He calls the power of piety charity, of which it is written: *Love is strong as death [n: (Cant. VIII)]*: this contempt of the world necessarily precedes.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VIII. ⟦s2⟧ *All who wish to live piously in Christ shall suffer persecution [n: (II Tim. III)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Does not the Church have a time of peace in which many live piously, and yet do not suffer persecution? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The saints suffer in many ways, as in heart and body: [0605A] now from the devil, now from evil men, now from their own concupiscences; to whom also the manner of life of the weak is a persecution. ⟦s6⟧ For these say with the Apostle: *Who is weak, and I am not weak? who is scandalized, and I am not on fire? [n: (II Cor. XI)]*. ⟦s7⟧ Therefore those who live piously in Christ are not without persecution, even in time of peace.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION IX. ⟦s2⟧ *I have finished my course [n: (II Tim. IV)]*, [0606A] etc. ⟦s3⟧ Did there not still remain, for the consummation of his course, a sharper and crueler enemy, namely the sword of Nero? ⟦s4⟧ How then was it true that Paul had finished his course before the triumph of his passion! ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ He says this not with the thing complete, but with sure hope. ⟦s7⟧ For he who made him victor in the contest had already, through revelation, rendered him certain and secure concerning the consummation.
