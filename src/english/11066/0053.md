## XII. ON THE EPISTLE TO TITUS.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0605]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0605B] *Paul the servant of God [n: (Tit. I)]*, etc. ⟦s2⟧ This epistle he writes to Titus, left as bishop of Crete, too forbearing out of humility and simplicity, from Nicopolis, concerning the episcopal office to be handled commandingly and with authority, having prescribed to him by his own useful authority. ⟦s3⟧ For a bishop ought to have maternal tenderness and paternal severity: that he be strong toward the proud, and gentle toward the modest: having neither a corner of fear, nor the eyebrow of haughtiness. ⟦s4⟧ *Let him burn and shine:* whence in the vestment of the legal high priest there was scarlet twice-dyed, which has the appearance of fire. ⟦s5⟧ But fire does two things: it burns and it shines: so also the bishop, with the sword of preaching, namely with fiery speech, ought to burn with biting rebuke and with fearful threatening: and to shine with soothing words, by cherishing and by promising delightful things. ⟦s6⟧ And therefore it is said of the manna that it hardened [0605C] at the fire, and melted at the sun. ⟦s7⟧ And the pontifical staff pricks from the lower part, and at the top is extended toward the front, returning upon itself: because the ecclesiastical doctor ought with the sword of the word to prick, that is, to reprove sharply those who sin, which is from the lower nature, and to direct those corrected toward the things before, yet so that by consideration of himself he return to his own conscience, if perchance he have in himself that which he announces by reproaching others. ⟦s8⟧ The form of the staff therefore figures this, that the bishop ought to prick the rebellious, and to draw the meek to himself, whence a certain one says: *The curved part draws the meek, the sharp point pricks the rebellious.*
⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The intention of the Apostle in this epistle, then, is to instruct Titus about the episcopal office, and to admonish him to handle it commandingly, and to shun heretics. ⟦s2⟧ The manner is such: [0605D] First he greets, then he instructs him about the episcopal office, teaching him what he ought to do, and what kind of bishops to constitute throughout the cities: then how he ought to instruct those differing whether in sex, or in age, or in condition: afterward he admonishes him about avoiding heretics.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION I. *Which he promised before the secular ages,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked how God promised eternal life before all times, since there were not yet men to whom he might promise? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ He promised, that is, in himself the eternal one he immutably purposed, that in time he would give eternal life to those whom he had already predestined.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0606B]QUESTION II. ⟦s2⟧ *Without crime,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ He does not say "without sin": because, as the authority says, no one, however piously he live, even if he be worthy of the name of just, is without sin. ⟦s4⟧ But someone will say: Is there not in baptism, and through true penitence, a plenary remission of all sins? ⟦s5⟧ And if this is so — nay, because it is so — it is established that this baptized man, and the truly penitent, is without sin. ⟦s6⟧ How then is it true, No one is without sin? ⟦s7⟧ Solution. ⟦s8⟧ When it is said, No one is without sin, it is thus understood: No one, however good he be, can pass through this life without sin; yet he can live without crime, that is, without a graver sin, and without a ground of complaint, that is, without such a sin as is worthy of accusation and of damnation, as adultery, homicide, theft, and the like.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0606C]QUESTION III. ⟦s2⟧ *The Cretans are always liars,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked why the Apostle interwove into his own teaching, in which there is divine authority, the words of a Gentile and unbelieving author, and, speaking to the Athenians, says: *In him we live, and move, and are [n: (Act. XVII)]*; and elsewhere he says: *I found an altar on which it was written, To the Unknown God, which is certain to have been taken from the writings of the heathen [n: (ibid.)]* Solution. ⟦s4⟧ It is permitted to the divine authority to take a testimony of truth from whence it will, which it judged to be necessary. ⟦s5⟧ For it does not on that account approve all the other things which are there, and judge them to be true. ⟦s6⟧ In figure of which thing the Hebrews despoiled the Egyptians of gold, and silver, and other things which were necessary for the divine worship. ⟦s7⟧ And in the law it was commanded to the Jews that, if they bought a Gentile slave, his hairs should be shaved, and the growths of his nails [0606D] cut off, and then he should be taken up for domestic uses. ⟦s8⟧ So, the vain and superfluous superstitions of the Gentiles being shaved off and cut away, whatever is found pure is to be taken up for the ministry of the house of God. ⟦s9⟧ It is to be noted that the divine authors put testimonies of this kind in a signification other than that in which they were put by their own author, as that: *It is expedient that one man die for the people, [and not] that the whole nation perish [n: (Joan. XI)]*; Caiaphas understood it one way, the evangelist another. ⟦s10⟧ For he by whose power and might the she-ass, as by an instrument, spoke with the voice of a man, used Caiaphas in the utterance of the aforesaid words.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0607A]QUESTION IV. ⟦s2⟧ *They confess that they know God, but in their works they deny him.* ⟦s3⟧ Note, that some confess God with words only, others also with works; some deny God with works only, some both with words and with works; those who confess God with words and works are good. ⟦s4⟧ But those who deny both with words and with works are unbelievers; but those who confess with words, and deny with works, are they to be called simply confessors, or deniers? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ Hear. ⟦s7⟧ *By their fruits you shall know them [n: (Matth. VII)]*, he does not say "by their words." ⟦s8⟧ All the evil, therefore, deny God by their works; all are antichrists who deny Christ by their life. ⟦s9⟧ Antichrist has not yet come, and yet already there are many antichrists in the world.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6⟧ QUESTION V. *A heretical man, after the first and second correction, avoid [n: (Tit. II)]*, etc. He calls a heretic [0608A] one who by the law assails the law. But why is such a one to be avoided? Would it not be better to confer with him often, and to recall him from error to truth? Solution. Since he is incorrigible, it is better to avoid him, because if he were more often corrected, it would be an exercise unto evil.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION VI. ⟦s2⟧ *Since he is condemned by his own judgment.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how such a one is condemned by his own judgment, for he believes what he says to be true, and thus does not judge himself damned. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Of him who knowingly sins, who recognizes the error and knows the truth, and yet praises the error and blames the truth — of this man the Apostle seems here to speak, and such a one, his conscience being witness, is damned, as is clear to all.

## XIII. ON THE EPISTLE TO PHILEMON.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0607]

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0607B] *Paul, a prisoner of Christ Jesus,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This epistle he writes to Philemon of Colossae, who was endowed with no dignity of ecclesiastical ministry, but was a praiseworthy man among the people, to whom he sends a familiar letter on behalf of Onesimus his slave, who had fled to his loss, but, having heard the Gospel from the Apostle, was baptized, for whom the Apostle both prays pardon, and deprecates his fault, writing to him from Rome, from prison. ⟦s3⟧ And the intention of the Apostle is to implore pardon for Onesimus with Philemon. ⟦s4⟧ The manner is such: first he greets him with his wife and son; then he gives thanks to God for their good things, commending their faith and charity, afterward he beseeches Philemon, though he could command him, to spare Onesimus, and to give thanks to God, because he received him such, that he should esteem him not a slave, but a most beloved brother. ⟦s5⟧ Then he says that he should prepare him a lodging, as he hopes to come to him.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION I. *To Philemon, and to Appia, and to Archippus,* etc. [0608B] It is asked why in this salutation he does not preserve the dignity of persons, namely why he does not put first Archippus, who was bishop of Colossae, but puts him after, and puts Philemon first, who was endowed with no ecclesiastical dignity — is it perhaps because the one was father, and the other son? ⟦s2⟧ Solution. ⟦s3⟧ He puts the layman before the ordained man for this reason, that a domestic matter is being treated.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION II. ⟦s2⟧ *So may I enjoy thee, brother, in the Lord,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ What is it to enjoy? ⟦s3⟧ Is it not to cleave to something for its own sake through love? but thus God alone is to be enjoyed, because God alone is to be loved for his own sake; how then does the Apostle say to Philemon: So may I enjoy thee, brother, in the Lord? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ He does not say simply, "I shall enjoy thee, brother"; but he adds "in the Lord," by this hinting that he has placed the end of his love in the Lord. ⟦s6⟧ Or "to enjoy" is said to be "to use with a certain delight," and thus it can be taken here.
