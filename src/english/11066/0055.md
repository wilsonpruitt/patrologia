## XIV. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE HEBREWS. (cont.)

QUESTION XI. And again he says: *Therefore to him for a father, and he himself shall be to me for a son,* etc. It is asked concerning this authority how it pertains to Christ, since neither the things preceding nor the things following that place, whence this authority is taken, seem to admit this. For in the book of Kings God the Father is brought in speaking to David: *Thou shalt not build me a house, because thou art a man of blood; but thy son, who shall reign after thee: [0612B] who if he shall have done wickedly, I will correct him with the rod of men, and in the blows* [stripes] *of the sons of men, and I will establish his kingdom for ever and ever, and I will be to him for a father, and he himself shall be to me for a son [n: (II Reg. VII)]*, etc. All these things can be fitted neither to Solomon nor to Christ. Solution. In divine Scripture often in the same series certain things are set down which refer to the history alone; certain things to the mystical sense alone; certain things also which can be taken both ways, as in the aforesaid; certain things refer to Solomon, certain to Christ.

QUESTION XII. Likewise it happens in many others. For if anyone, following the history, considering the things preceding and following, where it is written: *Behold a virgin shall conceive, and shall bear a son [n: (Isa. VII)]*: it will seem to him [0612C] rather that this authority refers to that young woman of Isaiah than to the Virgin, since there are there certain things which can in no way agree with the mother of our Emmanuel, which pertain to the history alone, although a certain mode of speech and property of relation sufficiently indicate that all things refer to the same person: Thy son, he says, who shall reign after thee, etc. Solution. It is to be known that there are three kinds of relations: for one is personal, as Saul, who is also Paul; another general and simple, as the woman who condemned, saved; another vocal, as my hands, which made you, are fastened with nails.

QUESTION XIII. Likewise it is asked according to which [0612D] nature the Father says: I will be to him for a father. For according to the human he is not father; according to the divine he was father from eternity; how then does he say: I will be father to him? Solution. Then a thing is said to be made, when it begins to be known; because therefore through the resurrection it was made plain to all the faithful, and in the future will be made plain even to the unbelieving, that he himself is Father, and that one Son, therefore he says: I will be to him for a father. Or thus: I will be to him, that is to the man assumed, for a father, and that man himself shall be to me for a son, yet not according to humanity, but according to divinity.

QUESTION XIV. *And when again he bringeth in the first-begotten,* etc. It is asked how the Apostle calls "entrance" that coming which the Lord himself calls "going forth," saying: *I came forth from the Father, and am come into the world [n: (Joan. XVI)]*, calling it entrance [0613A] when he says, *And again when he bringeth in [n: (Hebr. I)]*. Solution. As regards the Father, who was within, the coming of the Lord is called a going forth; but as regards us, who were without, it is called an entrance, or conversely.

QUESTION XV. *And let all the angels of God adore him,* etc. Concerning the man assumed it is wont to be asked whether he is to be adored with that adoration which is called latria: for latria is exhibited to God alone, and not to a creature; but the man assumed is a creature, and thus it seems that latria is not to be exhibited to him. Solution. Latria is exhibited to that man not because he is man, but because he is God; concerning this it has already been said above; but that adoration which is exhibited to men or to angels is called dulia.

[0613B]QUESTION XVI. *Who maketh his angels spirits,* etc. It is asked how he does this? Solution. "Spirit" is the name of a nature, as "man"; "angel" is the name of an office, as "soldier": therefore of spirits are made angels, as of men are made soldiers; not of angels are made spirits, just as neither of soldiers are made men.

QUESTION XVII. *And his ministers a flame of fire,* etc. It is asked how he makes a flame of fire, that is, the seraphim, his ministers, since the seraphim always stand by: how then is that order said to minister, if it always stands by? For they are not said to minister except from the fact that they minister. Solution. It can be said that the seraph receives immediately from God that which he reveals to an inferior, who is sent to us: whence also the inferior [0613C] is reckoned by the name of the superior, whose office he bears, or rather from whom he receives the office. Whence Isaiah says: *One of the seraphim flew to me, and touched my lips [n: (Isa. VI)]*. Or it can be said that, when some great things are to be done or announced, then those superiors are sent, whose office nevertheless is not to minister, but rather to stand by. Note that nothing is done which is not done either by God commanding or by God permitting: by which words it is intimated that not everything which is done is a work of God: for what he only permits, he does not do. This I say on account of those who say that whatever is, in that which it is, is from God. If this be so, the aforesaid distinction is null, that some things [sic: *finnt*] by God commanding, others are done by God permitting.

[0613D]QUESTION XVIII. *Therefore he anointed thee,* etc. The expositor says that Christ was anointed for this, that he might love justice. But did he not, from the moment he existed, love justice, nor was he anointed before he was full of love? how then is he said to be anointed for this, that he might love justice? Solution. By the oil of anointing is understood grace itself, and virtue: by "to love" he insinuates the very act proceeding from virtue. Causally therefore the anointing precedes the act of loving, and not in time. Or pursue another exposition.

QUESTION XIX. *Therefore he anointed,* that is, therefore because thou hast loved justice, he anointed thee. But did he not from his very cognition have the fullness of anointing? what anointing then did he receive as it were in reward, because he loved justice? Solution. By the oil of gladness, [0614A] as it were anointed by the merit of love, according to certain men is understood the stole of the mature resurrection.

QUESTION XX. *God, thy God, hath anointed thee,* etc.— the other, that is the former, is the vocative case, as if he should say: O Son, God, thy God hath anointed thee. But what God has a God? what God is anointed? Solution. Christ is God, and has a God, not inasmuch as he is God, but inasmuch as he is man, and according to that he is anointed.

QUESTION XXI. *They shall perish,* etc. It is asked concerning which heavens he says that they are to perish. Solution. Concerning the airy heavens, as the expositor says, which perished through the flood, and are to perish by fire. Whence it is asked, if they already perished through the flood, how are they again to perish through [0614B] fire? If they have already perished, have they not ceased to be? and if they have already ceased, how shall they again perish by fire? Solution. They perished through the flood, that is, they were changed for the worse; and those same are to perish by fire, that is, they are to be changed into a better state. But concerning the earth and the higher heavens there is no question but that they are to be changed for the better: whence Peter says: *We look for new heavens and a new earth [n: (II Pet. III)]*. Concerning water and air it is doubted whether they are to be changed into a better state, like earth and heaven, because in the Apocalypse it is written: *And the sea was now no more [n: (Apoc. XXI)]*. And here concerning the airy heavens it is written: They shall perish. Whence it seems to some that water and air will lose those properties from which these names belong to them, [0614C] and with them will lose also the names, yet they will not be wholly annihilated; but neither water nor air will they any longer be called, these things being taken away and annihilated whence before they were so called.

QUESTION XXII. *They are all ministering spirits,* etc. It was said above that it is not the office of the superiors to minister, how then are they all ministering? Solution. It can be said that all spirits minister to us either immediately, as the inferiors; or through the mediation of others, as the superiors and the middle ones; or by "all" he gathers only those who are of the lowest order, who are properly called angels, whose office it especially is to minister, and therefore they alone are properly ministering.

QUESTION XXIII. *What is man, that thou art mindful [0614D] of him [n: (Hebr. II)]*, etc. It is asked how this is to be expounded. The solution is expounded in three ways. First thus: That by "man" is understood the old man, by "the son of man" the new man is understood. Secondly thus: That by "man" is understood any good man, by "the son of man" Christ. Thirdly thus: That by "man" and "the son of man" the same is understood, namely Christ.

QUESTION XXIV. *Thou hast made him a little less than the angels,* etc. Above he said that he was made better than the angels: here he says that he was made less than they: and we have said in what way both are true. But here it is asked whether it is to be granted absolutely that Christ is less than the angels. Which certain men wish thus to prove: Christ is all that he was made, but [0615A] he was made less: therefore he is less than the angels. Solution. It is not to be said absolutely that he is less than the angels; but that "a little less" he was made less, is thus understood, that is, according to something he was made less, namely according to the infirmity of the flesh and the passion of death, as the authority says: nor is it consequent that, if he is less according to something, he is therefore absolutely less.

QUESTION XXV. *Thou hast subjected all things under his feet [n: (Psal. VIII)]*. "All" sometimes gathers the whole, as all the angels of heaven are just. Sometimes by determination it is reduced to a part, as *all mine are thine [n: (Joan. XVII)]*, for this God the Father says to the elder son, that is to the Jewish people: therefore by "all" here it behooves to understand, not those things which belong to God according to [0615B] nature, but only those which are necessary for salvation. Sometimes also "all" has the force of a negation, as *all things which I have heard from my Father, I have made known to you [n: (Joan. XV)]*, that is, none except those which I have heard. Whence it can be asked how this is to be taken when it is said, thou hast subjected all things. Solution. Some say that it is reduced to a part, and by "all" they understand only angels and men. But if the more excellent things are subjected to Christ, how not the lesser? Since Ambrose says that just as nothing is excepted from the work of God, so neither from the power of Christ. And Augustine says: No creature will be not subject, to whom the chief angels are subjected. And the Apostle himself shows that nothing is excepted, when he subjoins: *For in that he subjected all things to him, he left nothing not subject to him.*
