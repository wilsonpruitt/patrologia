## XIV. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE HEBREWS. (cont.)

QUESTION XLI. *As I swore in my wrath,* etc. It is asked what here is called the wrath of God: for it is agreed that such a passion does not fall upon God. Solution. He calls the wrath of God the immovable sentence of eternal justice, by the figure which is called anthropopathos: which occurs whenever those things which, said of men, signify passion, are figuratively attributed to God. God therefore swears in wrath, when he firmly determines to punish for the obstinacy of sin. Note that there are four kinds of symbols, that is of signs, that is of those things which are figuratively said of God. For some symbols are like, and are taken from corporeal things, as fire, light, sun and others of this kind. Others are like and taken from incorporeal things, [0619B] as reason, intellect, spirit and the like. Others are unlike and taken from corporeal things, as lion, bear, worm and such like. Others are unlike, and taken from incorporeal things, as wrath, fury, grief, repentance and the like.

QUESTION XLII. *That none of you be hardened by the fallacy of sin,* etc. It is asked what he calls the fallacy of sin. Solution. The fallacy of sin is when one presumes upon the grace of God: and secure in this confidence lies in sins. Or when one promises that in the coming year he will amend, that in the present he may sin more securely, which is done at the instigation of the devil. There can also be a threefold fallacy noted here, so that the first is when we consent to a depraved motion; the second, when we break forth into an evil work; the third, when in the habit of sin [0619C] we take delight.

QUESTION XLIII. *But with whom was he offended for forty years,* etc. It is asked how this is to be understood. Solution. The number forty indicates the entirety of years: therefore he is said to be offended with them for those forty years, because he is angry with sinners even to the end of their life.

QUESTION XLIV. *And indeed the works from the foundation of the world [n: (Hebr. IV)]*, etc. It is asked where mention is made of that eternal rest, which is signified by the land of promise: for the rest of the Sabbath is there treated of: And God rested on the seventh day *from all the work which he had done.* But of the rest of the land of Palestine mention seems to be made there, where it is said: If they shall enter into my rest [0619D]. Solution. Where the rest of the Sabbath, or the rest of the land of Palestine according to the letter, is treated of, there also the true rest is treated of anagogically, which is signified by those two.

QUESTION XLV. *God rested on the seventh day,* etc. Scripture says that in six days God made all his works, so that he made nothing new afterward; but does he not daily create new souls? how then does he make nothing new? Solution. God makes nothing except from matter made in the first condition, as any corporeal thing: or after the likeness of things then already made, as incorporeal spirits, namely human souls.

QUESTION XLVI. *Again he determineth a certain day, To-day,* etc. It is asked how, by that which the Apostle here [0620A] brings in, he compels the Jews to understand a rest other than the other, which was that of the land of promise, saying: *To-day if you shall hear his voice,* etc. Solution. Those to whom he wrote had faith, and believed David to be a prophet: according to which it was necessary to understand another day, and another rest than that rest of the land of promise, from the words of the prophet there set down: and thus the proof of the Apostle proceeds:

QUESTION XLVII. *For the word is living,* etc. The expositor says that the Son of God sees how reason and sensuality agree in their differences: but do they not differ in their differences? how then do they agree in their differences? can any things agree and differ in the same? Solution. He does not say that in the differences by which reason differs from sensuality, [0620B] or conversely, these two agree, but that reason has differences by which it is distinguished from itself, while it gazes upon God, thinking of the divine *usia*, or lower, considering the heavenly things, it contemplates the natures of invisible spirits, or on earth it deals with the things of the world rightly to be handled. Likewise sensuality too has differences by which it is divided from itself, while, more given to the lowest things, it is lower, or, called back from them, it is worthier. So then the Son of God sees how the superior difference of sensuality, by consenting, agrees with the differences of reason; or the inferior difference of reason, pressed and captive, sometimes consents to the inferior difference of sensuality. Note that "soul" is put either for sensuality, or for carnal sins, or for carnal [0620C] thoughts: so also "spirit" for reason, or for spiritual sins, or for good thoughts. When it is said that the word of God reaches even to the division of the soul and the spirit [n: (Hebr. IV)], whence thou wilt find a threefold exposition in the gloss on account of the threefold acceptation of soul and spirit.

QUESTION XLVIII. *Tempted in all things for the likeness, without sin.* In that he was tempted, he knows how to compassionate. In that he is without sin, he is able to deliver. The expositor says that it is impossible for a man to know afflictions, unless he has experienced them. But do not many from sight alone know the afflictions of others, which they have nowhere suffered: so much even that they compassionate them? how then is it true, *it is impossible for a man,* etc.? Solution. He who has not experienced does not know so perfectly [0620D] as he who knows by experiment: which manner of knowing the Apostle notes, saying: *We have not a high priest who [cannot compassionate,* that is, has known.

QUESTION XLIX. *Neither doth any man take the honor to himself [n: (Hebr. V)]*, etc. Do not many very great men in this time thrust themselves forward: and, not called, sometimes even by violence take the honor to themselves? how then is it true: *Neither doth any man take the honor to himself?* Solution. No one acting piously and religiously thrusts himself forward. *Neither did Christ glorify himself,* etc., since this glorification of the Father was made concerning the Son according to the human nature.

QUESTION L. It is asked how this authority: [0621A] *This day have I begotten thee,* which according to Augustine is expounded of the eternal generation, is in this place brought in by the Apostle: how does the Father, saying, *This day have I begotten thee,* testify that Christ, according as he is man, is his Son, and the redeemer of the world? Solution. Jerome says that this authority is thus understood of the human generation of Christ: *This day,* that is in the time of grace and light, *have I begotten thee,* that is, I have incarnated thee: or "I have begotten," that is, I have shown thee to be begotten: according to which this authority is well brought in.

QUESTION LI. *According to the order of Melchisedech,* etc. The expositor says that Melchisedech was not temporal: but was he not only a man, and thus temporal, because every man is temporal? Solution. It is therefore said that he is not temporal: because Scripture [0621B] was silent as to the beginning and end of his life, in figure of Christ, who lacks beginning and end. Or he can be said a priest not temporal on account of his priesthood, which remains in Christ for ever.

QUESTION LII. *He was heard for his reverence,* etc. It is asked what it is that Christ was heard for his reverence. Was not Stephen likewise heard for his reverence, and the other saints? What then is great concerning Christ if he was heard for his reverence? Solution. It is no wonder if certain things are said of Christ common to the martyrs, since certain things also common to the legal priests are here set down. Yet it can be said that Stephen was heard for the reverence of Christ rather than for his own: for Christ entered by himself, others through him: [0621C] whence also *of his fullness we all have received [n: (Joan. I)]*. And thus something special is said of Christ when it is read that he was heard for his reverence: which is noted in this, that without sin and by charity alone he died and suffered.

QUESTION LIII. *He learned obedience by the things which he suffered,* etc. We learn those things which we do not know: since therefore Christ knew all things as God, how did he learn what he was not ignorant of? Solution. This question has already been sufficiently discussed above. For it was said that cognition is twofold, one of comprehension, the other of experience: that which is of comprehension he had from eternity, but the other he learned in time.

QUESTION LIV. *Which are the elements of the beginning of the words of God,* etc. It is asked what is the difference between these [0621D] three: elements, beginning, word of God? Solution. He calls the word of God the evangelical doctrine; the beginning, the Creed and the Lord's Prayer: the elements are the matter which the Creed contains, as the nativity, the passion, and the other articles of faith.

QUESTION LV. *Every one that is a partaker of milk,* etc. The expositor says: He who does not grasp, *The Word was made flesh [n: (Joan. I)]*: how will he grasp, *In the beginning was the Word [n: (ibid.)]*? It is asked therefore how it is lighter to understand, The Word was made flesh, than that, In the beginning was the Word, since that concerning the incarnation of the Word is against human reason: but the former are higher, and concerning the Trinity of persons, and the unity of essence, investigated by reason, as from the prophets [0622A] it appears? Solution: those things concerning the incarnation are therefore said lighter, because they were visibly exhibited: but these more difficult, because comprehended by the intellect alone of few.

QUESTION LVI. Likewise it is asked how those to whom the Apostle here speaks needed to be taught what are the elements of the beginning of the words of God. Were they not faithful? how were they ignorant of the nativity, the passion, and the other articles of faith: since without these no one is faithful? Solution. They knew these things, but not fully.

QUESTION LVII. *Wherefore, leaving the word [n: (Hebr. VI)],* etc. Since the word of beginning seems necessary to them, how does he say, leaving the word of beginning? Solution. The sense is: [0622B] you ought not always to tarry in those things which pertain to the beginning, but to tend to perfection.

QUESTION LVIII. *Not again laying the foundation,* etc. This seems contrary to the aforesaid. For if they still needed the doctrine of the elements, the foundation was necessary to them. Solution. The glosses suffice for this question, expounding how "not again" is to be understood.

QUESTION LIX. *From dead works,* etc. He calls dead works either mortal sins, or good works which have been mortified by an ensuing evil: it is asked therefore how it is true, that no good is unrewarded, as no evil unpunished. Solution. Such goods are therefore called dead, because they [0622C] do not profit unto eternal life: and yet in this they shall be rewarded, because they who did them shall be less punished in the future. Or perhaps a temporal advantage was given for them. Likewise such goods are called dead, because through penance they can revive according to certain men: not that they be any more rewarded for them: but because from goods before done they will more easily attain grace. But it seems to us that, the sin being blotted out by penance, through which they were mortified, they are, as before, before they were mortified, worthy of eternal life.
