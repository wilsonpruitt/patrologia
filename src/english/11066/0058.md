## XIV. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE HEBREWS. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LX. ⟦s2⟧ *Of baptisms,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how he here says in the plural "of baptisms," since elsewhere he says: *One Lord, one faith, one baptism [n: (Ephes. IV)]*, etc. ⟦s4⟧ If one, how many? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. [0622D] As faith is said to be one not in number, but in kind, as the authority teaches, so baptism can be said to be one not in number, but in kind, because it is one form: nor can it be repeated: and because there is there one operation of the three persons, many are spoken of as regards the several washings. ⟦s6⟧ Or he therefore says "baptisms" in the plural, because there is baptism in power, in blood: and not for this reason, that the sacrament of baptism is celebrated except in water: but because the shedding of blood, faith, and penance supply the place of baptism: there only where the moment of necessity, not contempt of religion, excludes the sacrament of baptism. ⟦s7⟧ To which solution it is thus objected: *Unless a man be born again of water and the Holy Spirit, he shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven [n: (Joan. III)]*. [0623A] According to this authority it seems that no one is worthy of salvation without the reception of this sacrament. ⟦s8⟧ Solution. ⟦s9⟧ The doctors thus expound the aforesaid words of the Lord, *Unless a man be born again of water,* etc., that is, unless a man be born again by that spirit and regeneration by which are reborn those who are reborn of water, and by the Holy Spirit he can be saved. ⟦s10⟧ But by this spirit men are reborn in diverse ways. ⟦s11⟧ Some are reborn through penance, others through the shedding of blood, others through baptism. ⟦s12⟧ Note that the foundation of the Christian religion is divided into six, which are here set down by the apostles, namely penance, faith, baptism, imposition of hands, resurrection, future judgment: which pertain to the instruction of neophytes.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0623B]QUESTION LXI. ⟦s2⟧ *It is impossible for those who were once illuminated,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Here the Apostle seems to deny penance to those gravely fallen, as in the following, where he says: *To those who sin voluntarily there is now left no sacrifice for sin.* ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ It seems to us that in both places he describes the sin against the Holy Spirit, into whose pit whoever once falls, it is impossible that he attain penance or salvation. ⟦s6⟧ But the doctors say that this "impossible," etc., does not deny all renovation, but only the repetition of baptism. ⟦s7⟧ Or it can be understood of the future state: to which opinion certain glosses are of service.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXII. ⟦s2⟧ The expositor says that baptism avails even against subsequent sins. ⟦s3⟧ But it is asked how sins which are not yet done are already [0623C] forgiven. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Some say that baptism is therefore said to avail against subsequent sins, because through the grace conferred in baptism future sins are avoided. ⟦s6⟧ But according to this solution what is this, which in the same gloss follows, "if penance be done concerning them"? ⟦s7⟧ It seems to us to be thus understood, that baptism avails against subsequent sins: because by the virtue of baptism they can be blotted out with less satisfaction: so that if it happen that a faithful man and an unfaithful commit the same sin, the penance which suffices for the reborn unto salvation, the same does not suffice for the unfaithful or not yet reborn. ⟦s8⟧ This I say without prejudice to a better opinion.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXIII. ⟦s2⟧ *The Lord entered in by himself,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Since the Lord says in the Gospel: *Let your speech [0623D] be: Yea, yea; No, no: and that which* over and above *is, is of evil [n: (Matth. V)]*, it is asked how the Lord then swore to Abraham. ⟦s4⟧ Would not Abraham believe, or would he believe more slowly, God, if he promised without an oath? ⟦s5⟧ Solution. ⟦s6⟧ Not on account of Abraham did the Lord swear, since without an oath he firmly believed him: but on account of those who were to come after him, who would either not at all believe, or would more slowly believe, the promise made to Abraham, unless it were confirmed by an oath, and thus that oath was not from the evil of Abraham, but of those who were to come.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXIV. ⟦s2⟧ If it be asked: Why did the Lord swear by himself? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. ⟦s4⟧ Because men are wont to swear by one greater than themselves, but God had not [0624A] a greater: whence it is agreed that they sin who swear by something inferior. ⟦s5⟧ For that by which they swear they in a manner constitute above themselves.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6+s7+s8+s9+s10⟧ QUESTION LXV. *And an oath for confirmation is the end of all their controversy.* What then shall we say of the judgment of fire, or of water, or of others of this kind, which the Church seems to receive? Is not in those also the end of controversy? Solution. These are not judgments of the Church: whence also the guilty are many times thereby absolved, and the not-guilty are sometimes condemned: which would by no means happen, if mother Church held these. Whence in certain places she rather tolerates these than commends them. But someone will say: The same judgment ought to be made concerning an oath. This we grant. For the Church did not institute that, just as neither the aforesaid.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0624B]QUESTION LXVI. ⟦s2⟧ *According to the order of Melchisedech [n: (Hebr. VII)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Christ is said to be a high priest according to the order of Melchisedech in many ways. ⟦s6⟧ Both because that man was king and priest alone, so is Christ; both because he was instituted not with visible oil, as Moses, but was anointed with the oil of gladness and the purity of faith; nor did he immolate animals, but in bread and wine offered dedicated the sacrifice of Christ; so also Christ was anointed by the Father with the Holy Spirit; who offered himself on the altar of the cross to God the Father; and because he gave his true body and blood to his disciples at the supper, and because his priesthood remains for ever according to rite and dignity.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXVII. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked what that oblation of Melchisedech in bread and wine profited those who received it? ⟦s3⟧ Solution. [0624C] Some say that it profited those who received with faith as much as now the body of Christ profits. ⟦s4⟧ Which does not seem to us to be true: for the sacraments of the natural law, or of the written law, did not profit, nor could profit, as much as the sacraments of grace.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXVIII. ⟦s2⟧ *That which is less is blessed without any contradiction by the better.* ⟦s3⟧ Is not often a holy man blessed by one who is of less sanctity: as when a monk is blessed by some secular priest, how then is it true, that which is less is blessed by the greater? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Someone can be less than another in excellence of merits, and greater than the same in dignity: but here it is treated of the majority and minority which consists according to dignity. ⟦s6⟧ That therefore which [0624D] is less in dignity is blessed by that which is greater in excellence of dignity. ⟦s7⟧ Nor is it here treated of any blessing whatever, but only of that which belongs to the consecrated.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXIX. ⟦s2⟧ *Through Abraham, even Levi, who received tithes, was tithed,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Augustine says that just as, Adam sinning, those who were in his loins sinned; so, Abraham giving tithes, those who were in his loins were tithed. ⟦s4⟧ But did Christ either sin, Adam sinning; or was he tithed, Abraham giving tithes, since he was in the loins of both, according to the flesh? ⟦s5⟧ Since therefore Levi and Christ were alike in the loins of Abraham, how was Levi tithed, and not Christ? ⟦s6⟧ Or [0625A] if Christ was tithed like Levi, how does the Apostle prove the priesthood of Christ to be greater than the levitical priesthood: by this, that the levitical order in Levi was tithed in Abraham? ⟦s7⟧ Solution. ⟦s8⟧ There were some, and perhaps still are, saying that the flesh of Christ from Adam even to the Virgin was kept entire and incorrupt, who are not to be heard. ⟦s9⟧ Levi therefore was tithed, and not Christ, although both were there, because Levi contracted thence that whereby he lay subject to tithing, that is, fault. ⟦s10⟧ But Christ contracted nothing thence whereby he might be subject to tithing, whose flesh contracted thence not a wound, but the medicament of a wound. ⟦s11⟧ To which solution it is thus objected: All the flesh which was in Isaac descended from Abraham according to the common law, namely through concupiscence; [0625B] but the flesh of Christ was in Isaac: therefore the flesh of Christ descended through concupiscence from Abraham. ⟦s12⟧ Solution. ⟦s13⟧ It is not to be granted absolutely that the flesh of Christ so descended thence through concupiscence: for this would be, that the flesh of Christ was made through concupiscence; yet it can be granted by division: a certain flesh, which afterward was Christ's, descended thence according to the common law, which from the first man even to Mary was under original sin.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXX. ⟦s2⟧ *The priesthood being translated, it is necessary that a translation also be made of the law.* ⟦s3⟧ It can be asked how, upon the translation of the priesthood, the translation of the law necessarily follows. ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Because both were given at once by the same one under [0625C] the same promise: what is asserted of the one is necessarily understood also of the other. ⟦s6⟧ Or therefore, the priesthood being translated, the law is necessarily translated; because the translation of the priesthood is made for this, that by its ministry no one was justified, and thus on account of its insufficiency and infirmity it was translated; but the law is equally insufficient and infirm. ⟦s7⟧ Whence the Apostle: *The law brought nothing to perfection* [n: (Hebr. )]. ⟦s8⟧ By the same necessity therefore is it translated. ⟦s9⟧ Because neither therefore could consummate, of both is made a translation: which was figured in the priest and levite, the languishing man, who fell among robbers, passing by and not conferring the mercy of healing [n: (Luc. X)].

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXXI. ⟦s2⟧ *For it is manifest that our Lord sprang [0625D] out of Juda.* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked therefore whether his mother also? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ Call back to memory that question which we set down above upon the Epistle to Timothy, which the words of Origen make, saying that Mary was only of the tribe of Levi, and on account of Joseph only was the Lord said to be of the tribe of Juda. ⟦s6⟧ But, as I said, two tribes were intermingled, the royal and the sacerdotal, and the Virgin was on the father's side, of her father, of the tribe of Juda, and on the mother's side of the tribe of Levi.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXXII. ⟦s2⟧ The law brought nothing to perfection, etc. ⟦s3⟧ Were not certain men perfect in the time of the law, as David, and many others; and had they not that justice, since they were just, from obedience to the divine law? how then were they not just from the law? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The just of that time had justice not from the law, but from faith in him who was to come: [0626A] for the law showed sins, it did not take them away.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXXIII. ⟦s2⟧ *To make intercession for us,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is asked how Christ makes intercession for us? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ The expositor says that it is by the representation of himself, which is thus to be understood, that the merit of his passion, which he exhibited in his humanity, reconciles us believers to the Father.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ QUESTION LXXIV. ⟦s2⟧ *Who hath not daily necessity [n: (Hebr. VIII)].* ⟦s3⟧ It is asked concerning the sacrifices of the law what utility they conferred: perhaps the remission of sins? ⟦s4⟧ Solution. ⟦s5⟧ For certain sins the sinners were forbidden entrance, and through the sacrifices they were so reconciled that it was permitted them to enter into the temple: but not through such remission were they made [0626B] worthy of eternal life.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6+s7+s8⟧ QUESTION LXXV. *If then he were upon earth, he would not be a priest,* etc. It is asked how these things are to be understood. Solution. The text of this chapter is less coherent, and cut off: and therefore brings in obscurity. Whence it is expounded in five ways, as is had in the glosses. Note that in every sacrifice four things are considered: namely, to whom it is offered, and by whom it is offered, what is offered, and for whom it is offered. The same one mediator of both, reconciling us to God through the sacrifice of peace, would remain one with him to whom he offered, would make one in himself those for whom he offered; he himself would be one who offered and what he offered.
