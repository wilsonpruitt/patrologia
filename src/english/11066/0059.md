## XIV. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE HEBREWS. (cont.)

QUESTION LXXVI. *Who serve the exemplar and shadow,* [0626C] etc. It is asked what is here called "exemplar." Solution. The truth shown on the mount to Moses is called the exemplar of the figures made according to it. Likewise the figures themselves are called the exemplar of the truth, which was afterward fulfilled: and thus the figures themselves, in a different respect, can be called both example and exemplar. "Exemplar" *onim* is properly said that to whose likeness something is made; "example," that which is drawn from it.

QUESTION LXXVII. *For if that former had been without fault,* etc. Is not the law good, holy and given by God? how then is it not without fault? Solution. For that reason the Old Testament is said not to be without fault, for which the law is said to work wrath, namely, because it does not justify, and because it commands what cannot be done without grace.

[0626D]QUESTION LXXVIII. It is asked why we do not worship God with that rite with which the Hebrew fathers worshiped him? Solution. Because God commanded us another thing through the fathers of the New Testament: nor are these things which we observe against the Old Testament, but foretold and pre-announced in it.

QUESTION LXXIX. Likewise, why do we hold the authority of that testament? Solution. Lest we extinguish the prophets, and take away the testimony of truth from our midst. When therefore something is read which is not observed by us, we must only ask what it signifies, not reprehend it; because by the very fact that it is now not observed, it is proved not condemned, but fulfilled.

QUESTION LXXX. Likewise it is asked whence that is called the Old Testament, this the New, since the law is fulfilled [0627A] through the New Testament? Solution. The Old Testament is called "old" for the old harm, which is not healed through the letter commanding and threatening. But this is called "new" on account of the newness of spirit, which heals a man from the vice of oldness. Note diligently what is the difference between the two testaments: that old, this new. There the letter, which alone kills, here the spirit giving life. That is written on tables of stone, this on minds and hearts. That promises earthly things, this heavenly. That has sacraments signifying salvation, not conferring it; this has sacraments conferring salvation. Likewise note that "testament" is said both of the promise itself, temporal or eternal, and of the writing containing the promise itself. The Old Testament therefore contains the promise [0627B] pertaining to the old man. But the New Testament, which contains the promise of the eternal inheritance which belongs to the new man.

QUESTION LXXXI. *For if the ashes vitulaeaspersus sanctify the defiled [n: (Hebr. IX)]*, etc. It is asked what he calls the defilement, from which the sprinkled ashes of a heifer sanctify? Solution. He so calls the touch of the dead; or leprosy, from which those ashes cleanse, that is, from the punishment which according to the law was due to such uncleanness: by which touch is signified the consent of sin.

QUESTION LXXXII. *And as it is appointed unto men once to die.* The expositor says that Christ died by the same necessity and right of nature by which necessity, and in fact, other men die. [0627C] Whence it is asked whether Christ died by necessity, and whether he had the necessity of dying? Which seems manifest according to the aforesaid authority. But if by necessity, how did he die by will alone, as it is written: *He was offered because he willed it? [n: (Isa. LIII.)]* Solution. Some say that among the other penalties which the Lord took up with our nature without fault, he took up also this penalty, namely the necessity of dying, voluntarily: which does not exclude the will, nor is excluded by it. Nor yet is it to be understood but that Christ could by the power of divinity lay aside this penalty without the dissolution of soul and body, and clothe over the nature assumed with the stole of immortality; but if he conferred no more [0627D] than was conferred on it before death, he would necessarily die.

QUESTION LXXXIII. *To them that expect him unto salvation,* etc. Upon this place the expositor says that Christ died not by necessity, but by will for the sin of those expecting him unto salvation: but above it was said that he died by necessity: which seems to be contrary. Solution. What he says here, understand thus. Christ died not by necessity, that is, in him there was no sin for which it was necessary that he die: whence, as it were expounding what he had said, he subjoins: but he died for their sin, namely of those who expected him unto salvation.

QUESTION LXXXIV. *Otherwise they would have ceased to be offered [n: (Hebr. X)]*, etc. The legal victims, as the Apostle says, if [0628A] they made perfect would cease to be offered: whence it is asked why the salutary victim of the New Testament, since it perfects, and consummates the sanctified, is more often offered, and does not cease to be offered? Solution. Once indeed it was offered through the passion of death on the altar of the cross in human form; nor is it again so offered through death, but yet in the sacrament it is more often offered, not by reason of its own infirmity, but rather of ours, who daily sin: and especially for the remembrance of the death of Christ, that his love may be more deeply fixed in our hearts through this, that we are mindful of so great a benefit.

QUESTION LXXXV. *But a body thou hast fitted to me,* etc. It is asked in what he fitted the body of Christ? Solution. This fitness consists in two things, namely in cleanness and in mortality: for unless it were clean, through [0628B] it the unclean could not be redeemed, unless it were mortal it could not be immolated; it is necessary therefore that the body of the Lord be clean through immunity from sin, and mortal.

QUESTION LXXXVI. *Not forsaking your assembly.* It is asked whether the Apostle here condemns what the Lord permits, saying: *If they shall persecute you in one city, flee into another [n: (Matth. X)]*, of which thing he himself also gave an example by fleeing into Egypt. And Paul likewise is found many times to have done the same: what is it then that here he blames those who forsake the assembly? Solution. The Apostle in this place blames those who, as it were for the cause of their own sanctity, cannot dwell among other infirm or imperfect ones: and therefore by forsaking the assembly they rend unity, and thus [0628C] sin unto death. When some are specially sought by persecutors, then it is lawful to decline the rage of the persecutors, if it can be done without detriment to the assembly.

QUESTION LXXXVII. *To those who sin voluntarily,* etc. It is asked who are those who sin voluntarily, to whom there is left no sacrifice for sin. Solution. The gloss says that he calls those who sin voluntarily those remaining in sin out of the will of sinning: to whom Christ, who is the sacrifice for sin, does not profit, who profits only the penitent.

QUESTION LXXXVIII. But again it is asked why the Apostle says *after having received the knowledge of the truth,* since Christ does not profit those remaining in the will [0628D] of sinning even before the knowledge of the truth? Perhaps someone will say that such can be profited through the grace of baptism, so that no punishment even of satisfaction is enjoined, whereas after the grace of regeneration he cannot be renewed even through penance. Saying this we do not exclude penance, as certain men wish, taking occasion from these words of the Apostle: *to those who sin voluntarily,* etc. But to certain men it seems that he calls those "sinning voluntarily" who, knowingly assailing the truth out of envy, or hatred, or some such cause, sin against the Holy Spirit, and therefore sin irremissibly; because this sin has remission neither in the present nor in the future, as the Lord says. Which also seems to be intimated above, where the Apostle says: *It is impossible for those who were once illuminated* [0629A] [n: (Matth. XII)], etc. And subsequently in this place he supposes: Who hath trodden underfoot the Son of God, and hath esteemed the blood of the testament polluted, and hath offered an affront to the spirit of grace. By which words the sin against the Holy Spirit seems manifestly to be signified.

QUESTION LXXXIX. It is asked why baptism cannot be repeated: whose repetition is here denied by the Apostle, according to certain men? Solution. Because baptism at once takes away both fault and punishment. Whence if it were repeated, not only would it become cheap, but would even invite to sin. But someone will say: by the same reasoning neither ought penance to be repeated, lest it become cheap or provoke to sinning. Solution. There is another judgment concerning penance, which by the infliction of punishment restrains from sin, [0629B] than concerning baptism, by which both fault and punishment are wholly remitted.

QUESTION XC. *And a certain jealousy of fire.* It is asked what punishment he here signifies through fire? Solution. The vehemence of eternal punishment here the fire signifies, because no element has such efficacy as fire.

QUESTION XCI. *Which shall consume the adversaries,* etc. Shall the fire reduce Christ's enemies to nothing? Solution. Therefore it is said that the jealousy of fire shall consume the adversaries, because no part of the body or soul shall be free from punishment, but shall be tortured by the fire.

QUESTION XCII. *Revenge to me, and I will repay, saith the Lord.* It is asked whether vengeance is always to be reserved to the Lord; is it not lawful for a man [0629C] to take vengeance? Are not robbers lawfully hanged? Solution. It is not lawful for a man to take vengeance for himself, nor ought he to desire it; but if it is necessary to take it, let God alone be in the cause, and the zeal of justice; but he who takes it otherwise, sins mortally. According to that: *He that shall take the sword, shall perish by the sword [n: (Matth. XXVI)]*.

QUESTION XCIII. *Faith is the substance of things to be hoped for, the evidence of things that appear not [n: (Hebr. XI)]*. Concerning this definition of faith it is asked whether it fits all faith, and whether it can also be fitted to faith alone. But it seems that this definition fits only faith which is concerning future things; but since faith is concerning present and past things, as concerning the nativity and the passion, how is the faith of the passion the substance of things to be hoped for? [0629D] Likewise, is not hope and charity the substance, that is the thing and cause, which makes the things to be hoped for subsist in us? how then is faith alone the substance of things to be hoped for? Solution. According as the gloss expounds this definition, it seems that it does not fit faith alone. But although part of this definition seems to be assigned to other virtues, yet not the whole; for faith alone is the evidence of things that appear not, through which alone we are certain concerning things eternal, that they are; but through hope we are confident that we shall have them. And therefore properly faith is called the substance of future things, because through it we know that they are. Faith also, concerning present or past things, can be called the substance of things to be hoped for, that is, the cause which [0630A] sometimes makes those things which we hope for subsist in us, because through faith in the passion one comes to the future goods, and it is the evidence of things that appear not, so much that those who saw him suffer believed something, namely that Christ, who hung on the cross, was God.

QUESTION XCIV. *That from invisible things,* etc. It is asked what he calls invisible things? Solution. Either the unformed and invisible matter of the four elements, which in Greek is called chaos; or the invisible world, which is called the archetype, which was in the mind of God, according to whose exemplar this sensible and visible world was made.

QUESTION XCV. *Abel by faith being dead yet speaketh,* etc. It is asked how he who does not live, speaks? [0630B] Solution. He speaks, that is, is matter of speaking; he speaks because by his example he admonishes us to be just.

QUESTION XCVI. *Without faith it is impossible to please God,* etc. It is asked of what faith he says this; is it of the faith of the incarnation? Solution. It is said that without it no one from the beginning pleased God.
