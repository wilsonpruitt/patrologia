## XIV. ON THE EPISTLE TO THE HEBREWS. (cont.)

QUESTION XCVII. *He that cometh to God must believe that he is,* etc. It is asked of what faith he treats, whether of perfect, or of imperfect? If of imperfect, how could anyone please God through it; if of perfect, how can this suffice for salvation, to believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder to them that seek him? for in these are not contained all the articles which are necessary for salvation. Solution. He seems [0630C] here to lay down the order of the faith to be held, that is, to show what one must believe in the first place, not to assign the sufficiency of faith.

QUESTION XCVIII. But whether there was any time in which this believing only, namely, that God is, and that he is a rewarder, etc., sufficed, can be asked. Which does not seem [to be so].

QUESTION XCIX. *By faith also concerning things to come Isaac blessed Jacob,* etc. Did he, when he blessed him, understand what was there signified by this, that he was blessing the younger? If he understood, then he knew that it was Jacob, which the history in no way allows. For he even felt the hands, to prove whether he was Esau, and afterward, when Esau came, he said: *Who was it that came deceitfully, whom I blessed, and he shall be blessed?* Solution. [0630D] Although he did not then understand, yet afterward he understood, and had faith of this thing, that the greater people shall serve the lesser.

QUESTION C. Likewise it is asked whence Isaac was deceived in blessing? Solution. He was not deceived, but that he knew what effect the blessing would have in him whom he was blessing; yet he did not yet know that it was Jacob whom he was blessing. Now deception is properly wont to be taken in those things which ought not to be done. Is he to be said to be deceived, who buys gold for brass, or silver for tin? But if deception be taken broadly, for all things which happen otherwise than they are thought, then here too there was a certain deception.

QUESTION CI. *And he adored the top of his rod;* or, as another reading has it, *upon the top,* etc. [0631A] It is asked what it is that Jacob adored upon the top of Joseph's rod. Solution. Perhaps he had taken the rod from his son, when that same son was swearing, and while he holds it after the words of the one swearing, it not yet being returned, he straightway adored God: thus Augustine resolves it. Or it can be said that he adored the top of his rod, that is, the kingdom of Christ to come among the nations: which was signified by it, so that by the name of the sign the thing signified may be understood.

QUESTION CII. *Jephthah,* etc. Concerning this it is asked whether he made a rational vow, and whether he sinned in fulfilling it, in offering his only daughter to the Lord. If a dog had met him, would he have offered it to God? Solution. It seems that he acted foolishly in vowing, more foolishly in fulfilling the vow: [0631B] yet if he did this by the hidden admonition of the Holy Spirit, he is excused, like Samson, who crushed the Philistines together with himself, since it is not lawful to lay hand upon oneself. Whence also Jonah said: *Cast me into the sea.*

QUESTION CIII. It is asked why, in memory of the death of that girl, the Hebrew virgins make lamentation every year? Solution. For the sake of example, lest anyone again so foolishly vow or do something whence such grief might follow.

QUESTION CIV. It is asked whether Abraham sinned by will, in willing to sacrifice his son, since, according to some — nay, in very truth — Jephthah sinned in killing his daughter, unless this was done by divine instinct. Solution. Abraham is not only not blamed with the charge of cruelty, but is even praised under the name [0631C] of piety, because he willed to kill his son not wickedly, but obediently.

QUESTION CV. *Moreover we have had fathers indeed of our flesh [sic: quidemcarnis] as instructors, and we reverenced them: shall we not much more be subject to the Father of spirits, and live? [n: (Hebr. XII.)]* If it be asked whether human souls are by traduction, note diligently this authority, by which it is manifestly proved that souls are not by traduction as flesh is; for if this were so, the Apostle would in no way distinguish between the fathers of our flesh, and the Father of spirits.

QUESTION CVI. *Looking diligently lest any man be wanting to the grace of God,* etc. It is asked what it is to be wanting to the grace of God? Solution. To close the eyes of the mind to the grace offered, and to spurn the grace, just as, when my eyes [0631D] are open to the ray of the sun, I can at my pleasure close them, or hold them open, which is from free will; and thus grace does not exclude free will, nor does the will itself exclude grace.

QUESTION CVII. *He sold his birthright,* etc. It is asked what the Apostle calls the birthright of Esau. Solution. Here he calls the birthright the honor and dignity of the priesthood: because before the priesthood of Aaron all the firstborn were priests, as was Shem. And this was a great dignity, because he took a greater portion of the paternal substance and inheritance, and shone with a richer adornment of garments, and to him the blessing was given.

QUESTION CVIII. It is asked whether the sin of Esau was [0632A] irremissible, since the Apostle says that *he found no place of repentance.* Did he not repent? nay, he wept: how then is it true, he found no place of repentance? Solution. He found no place of repentance, that is, of pardon, and place of blessing through repentance — not that he did not repent, but because he did not repent as he ought. For those tears were rather from indignation against his brother, and from grief at the honor lost, than from humility and true repentance.

QUESTION CIX. *Jesus the mediator of the new testament, and the sprinkling of blood speaking better than that of Abel.* It is asked in what the blood of Christ speaks better than the blood of Abel? Solution. The gloss says [it speaks] better for this reason, because this, that is, [the blood] of Christ, sought pardon, [0632B] that one vengeance; this, salvation, that one, damnation. But someone will say: Did not the shedders of the blood of Christ perish, like the shedders of the blood of Abel? Therefore the blood of Christ speaks vengeance, like the blood of Abel. How then does this speak better than that? Solution. The blood of Christ not only cries vengeance to those who despise it, but also pardon to all, even to persecutors, if they are willing to repent. But the blood of Abel speaks only vengeance, and pardon to none. Or the blood of Christ speaks better than [that of] Abel, that is, makes us speak better, namely, that Jesus is the Son of God, by whom we are redeemed; than the blood of Abel, which makes us speak [only] that Abel was a just man immolated in a figure of Christ. Note that Abel, who was the first unjustly slain, is put for all [0632C] the others whose blood could effect no pardon.

QUESTION CX. *By this indeed they pleased God,* etc. It is asked whether Abraham and Lot knew them to be angels. If they knew, how did they offer the offices of humanity, which are not necessary except to the infirmity of men? Likewise, if they did not know them to be angels, but thought them only men, how did they venerate them more than men? Solution. At first they thought them men sent from God, and in whom God was; but afterward they discovered them to be angels.

QUESTION CXI. It is asked how the angels could eat in the bodies they had assumed? For they did not have those bodies united to them as the souls of men have [0632D] bodies united to themselves, whence those bodies were neither animate nor sensible; nor could the angels be harmed in those bodies. Solution. The angels did not eat after the manner of animals, but as fire consumes what is put to it, and reduces it to nothing; so also that food was consumed by their operation, or they made of it what they willed.

QUESTION CXII. Likewise it is asked how, since three angels were seen, Abraham could adore one of them in the faith of the Trinity and unity? Solution. By divine revelation he knew in them the mystery of the Trinity and unity.

QUESTION CXIII. *For the bodies of those animals,* etc. The expositor says that by a double allegory he proves, [0633A] that the body of Christ is not to be eaten by those who serve the tabernacle; but since there is one sense of both histories, how does he prove this by a double allegory? What is allegory? Is it not the spiritual sense? Therefore where there is one sense, how is the allegory double? Solution. He says by a double allegory, [0634A] that is, by an allegory arising from a double history. Or allegory is said [both] of the signifying and of the signified; but here it is put for the signifying: And what is "by a double allegory" but "by a double history," namely one of the Old, the other of the New Testament?
