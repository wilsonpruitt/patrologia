## CHAP. VII. *On sadness, or sloth, and its retinue.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Sadness is the anxiety of a troubled mind arising from a wish frustrated by adverse things, [1001A] and a weariness at the good performing of a good thing. ⟦s2⟧ Its companions are despair, rancor, torpor, fear, sloth, complaint, faintheartedness.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Despair is a broken steering of hope toward the state of life or the return of the virtues; or it is the casting-away of hope of obtaining salvation or pardon.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Rancor is the enervation and corruption of mind and body, arising from black bile or from an excessive slackness of the powers.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Torpor is the sluggishness of a languishing mind, a slackened motion, a dullness of the whole body.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Fear is that by which a wavering anguish is engendered in the mind from the expectation of adverse things.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Sloth is a sadness or weariness born from an excessive confusion of mind, or an excessive bitterness of soul, [1001B] by which spiritual gladness is extinguished, and, by a certain beginning of despair, the mind is overthrown within itself.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Complaint is to open with impatient words a hidden trouble of the heart, and to show oneself able to do less than one can.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Faintheartedness is the inert and unconsidered dejection of a broken mind, arising from weariness at adverse things.

## CHAP. VIII. *On avarice, and its retinue.*

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Avarice is the insatiable and dishonorable coveting of glory, of riches, or of any other things whatever. ⟦s2⟧ Its companions are philargyria, perjury, violence, usury, fraud, rapine, and deceit.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Philargyria is that by which, through a singular appetite for scraping money [1001C] together, the reins of continence are loosened: for it is interpreted *love of silver.*

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Perjury is the denial of an acknowledged truth, made fraudulently by forswearing, for the sake of gaining advantage or of avoiding loss.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Violence is that by which malice or injury, for the sake of advantage, is exercised mightily and by force upon an inferior.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Usury is the receiving-back, with interest, of money or of another thing given on loan.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Fraud is that by which, by a clandestine stealth, provision is made for one's own property, by reason of want or of malice.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Rapine is the violent plundering of another's goods, proceeding from the impulse of a greedy mind.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Deceit is that by which, by deceptive movements, the truth [1001D] of a matter is cloaked, and either the hatred of another or one's own advantage is aimed at.

## CHAP. IX. *On the gorging of the belly, or gluttony, and its retinue.*

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Gluttony of the belly is the immoderate satisfaction of a gaping concupiscence for foods or drinks of whatever kind. ⟦s2⟧ Its companions are drunkenness, surfeit, dulling of the mind, languor, daintiness, forgetfulness.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Drunkenness is that by which a certain forgetfulness of itself is engendered in the mind from the indulgence of superfluous drinks.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Surfeit is the enticing and greedy appetite for foods, for the sake of the body alone.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The dulling of the mind is the blunting of keen reason, brought on by the gross senses of carnal intemperance.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1002A]Languor is the idleness of a sick mind, contracted by the vice of voracity.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Daintiness is the desire for lavish, delicious, and costly food or drink beyond one's condition and need.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Forgetfulness is that by which, the soul being buried in vices on account of gluttony, a neglect of honor and of salvation is brought in.

## CHAP. X. *On lust, and its retinue.*

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Lust is the slippery and unbridled prostitution of mind or body, descending from unclean desires. ⟦s2⟧ Its companions are pleasure, wantonness, slothfulness, petulance, staggering, blandishments, delights.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Pleasure is the inclination of a foul mind toward things unlawful, with a certain slippery sweetness.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Wantonness is the indecent motion of a dissolute body in a shameful part, issuing from carnal intemperance.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1002B]Slothfulness is the languid dejection of both the inner and outer man from the praiseworthy exercise of the virtues.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Petulance is the wanton onrush of the whole body toward the thing loved, from the conceiving of carnal thoughts.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Staggering is a vice of a broken mind issuing outwardly at the urging of slippery carnality, or a wavering of the limbs from immoderate lust.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Blandishments are the sensual display, made outwardly, of vicious delights with a certain sweetness.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Delights are the usurpation of bodily enticement with a certain superfluity.

## CHAP. XI. *On humility, and its companions.*

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1002C]

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Now that the dreadful array of pride, which draws to the left, has been made known, attend to the army of humility, which leads to the right. ⟦s2⟧ And now that the citizens of Babylon are known, attend to the citizens of Jerusalem.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Humility is the voluntary inclination of the mind, from the regard of one's own condition, or of the Creator. ⟦s2⟧ Its principal retinue are these: prudence, justice, fortitude, temperance, faith, hope, and charity.

## CHAP. XII. *On prudence, and its retinue.*

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Prudence is the provident and shrewd discernment of good and of evil, in the keeping of oneself. ⟦s2⟧ Its companions are the fear of the Lord, alacrity, counsel, memory, understanding, providence, deliberation.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The fear of the Lord is a vigilant keeping of the divine [1002D] precepts in faith and in good morals. ⟦s2⟧ For the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Alacrity is the glad and circumspect readiness of the mind for completing a good work.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Counsel is the subtle foresight of the mind for examining and governing affairs.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Memory is a certain imaginary representation of past things before the gaze of the mind.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Understanding is to weigh and discern the state of present things with reasonable liveliness.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Providence is that by which, with shrewd subtlety, the outcomes of future things are gathered.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Deliberation is, before anything begun, a consideration full of maturity and skill.

## CHAP. XIII. *On justice, and its retinue.*

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1003A]

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Justice is that by which the grace of the community is maintained, and to each his own dignity is not denied. ⟦s2⟧ Its companions are law, severity, equity, correction, the keeping of an oath, judgment, truth.

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Law is that by which the things to be done are commanded, and the things not to be done are forbidden.

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Severity is that by which judicial punishment, in following the rigor of the true, is exercised more strictly upon the sinner.

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Equity is the most worthy repayment of deserts, weighed out evenly on the balance of justice.

⟦p48⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Correction is the restraining, by the bridle of reason, of an error inborn or introduced by habit.

⟦p49⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The keeping of an oath is that which, once a decree has been promulgated to the citizens, [1003B] wards off the rash transgression of it by an oath taken concerning its perpetual observance.

⟦p50⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Judgment is that by which, from the hearing of deserts, a fitting punishment or reward is rendered.

⟦p51⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Truth is that by which, by probable reasoning, something said or done is set forth unchanged.

## CHAP. XIV. *On fortitude, and its companions.*

⟦p52⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Fortitude is the unmoved undertaking by the mind, amid adversities, of labors and of dangers. ⟦s2⟧ Its companions are magnanimity, confidence, endurance, rest, stability, constancy, perseverance.

⟦p53⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Magnanimity is a certain noble excellence of a mind that manages difficult and arduous matters beautifully and bravely.

⟦p54⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1003C]Confidence is, from the awareness of one's virtues, to establish for oneself an unmoved mind amid adversities, and to trust in a good outcome.

⟦p55⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Endurance, or patience, is the long-lasting sufferance of another's wickedness or annoyance.

⟦p56⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Rest is that by which a certain security is brought to the mind from the contempt of perfunctory changeableness.

⟦p57⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Stability is the holding-firm of a mind — fixed by no changeableness of places or times — though it be tossed through diverse things.

⟦p58⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Constancy is a course of life like itself, worthy of its state, perpetual, and unmoved.

⟦p59⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Perseverance is that which, by a certain perfection of the virtues (longsuffering bringing it to completion), strengthens the mind, lest before the attained end it desist from what was begun.

## CHAP. XV. *On temperance, and its companions.*

⟦p60⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1003D]Temperance is the firm and discreet mastery of reason over the unlawful impulses of the mind. ⟦s2⟧ Its companions are discretion, compliance, taciturnity, fasting, sobriety, affliction of the flesh, contempt of the world.

⟦p61⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Discretion is the provident reason that moderates human impulses, for judging the causes of things.

⟦p62⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Compliance is, one's own virtue being preserved, to attune oneself justly and devoutly to the manners of those dwelling with one.

⟦p63⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Taciturnity is the restraining of one's tongue even from useful words, with a certain fruitful quiet of mind.

⟦p64⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Fasting is a vigilance of the inner things, a discreet keeping of frugality.

⟦p65⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1004A]Sobriety is the temperance of both the inner and outer man, pure from enticements, so called because it is set apart and separated from drunkenness.

⟦p66⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Affliction of the flesh is that by which the seed-beds of a wanton mind are pressed down by discreet chastisement.

⟦p67⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Contempt of the world is a love of eternal things, brought in by reason from the regard of things that perish.

## CHAP. XVI. *On faith, and its companions.*

⟦p68⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Faith, in holy zeal, is that which, from the truth of things premised, makes the mind hang upon the invisible things to come. ⟦s2⟧ For it is the evidence of things that do not appear. ⟦s3⟧ Its companions are religion, cleanness, obedience, chastity, reverence, continence, affection.

⟦p69⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Religion is that by which, with reverent service, the ceremonies of divine worship are performed.

⟦p70⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1004B]Cleanness is the consummate integrity of both the inner and outer man, in the regard of divine love or fear.

⟦p71⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Obedience is the voluntary denial of one's own will, resting upon devout zeal.

⟦p72⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Chastity is the clean and honorable condition of the whole body, through the furies of the vices being tamed.

⟦p73⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Reverence is a chaste worship rendered to God on account of the faith we have concerning him.

⟦p74⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Continence is that by which the impulses of carnal affections are curbed by the governance of counsel.

⟦p75⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Affection is the outpouring of devout love toward one's neighbor, a holy joy having been conceived through faith.

## CHAP. XVII. *On hope, and its companions.*

⟦p76⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Hope is a motion of the mind, immovably suspended toward the receiving of those things which it seeks with sure reason. ⟦s2⟧ Its [1004C] companions are contemplation of things above, joy, modesty, confession, patience, compunction, longsuffering.

⟦p77⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Contemplation of things above is, through the jubilation of an uplifted mind, the death of carnal affections.

⟦p78⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Joy is the spiritual gladness of a hastening mind, coming from the contempt of present things.

⟦p79⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Modesty is the bashfulness, over praiseworthy progress, of a chaste and honorable bearing.

⟦p80⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Confession is that by which the hidden disease of the soul is disclosed, unto the praise of God, in the hope of obtaining pardon.

⟦p81⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Patience is the voluntary and insuperable endurance of adverse things, in view of eternal glory.

⟦p82⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Compunction is a certain burning of a sighing soul, born either from the fear of punishment or from the love [1004D] of reward.

⟦p83⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Longsuffering is an untiring sustaining of labors, for the fulfilling of just desires.
