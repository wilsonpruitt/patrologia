## Standing terminology decision (applies to all chunks)

The treatise is built on a borrowed **rhetorical** vocabulary, and its whole argument
turns on the terms staying distinct from one another:

- *oratio* → prayer · *precatio* → entreaty · *petitio* → petition
- *supplicatio* → **supplication** · *postulatio* → **postulation** ·
  *insinuatio* → **intimation** (the three *species orationis*)
- *captatio* → kept in **italic Latin**, per the register rule for technical terms
  with no clean English equivalent: it is the rhetor's *captatio benevolentiae*,
  and "winning of goodwill" would be a gloss, not a rendering.
- *exactio* → **exaction** · *pura oratio* → **pure prayer** (the three
  *genera supplicationum*)
- *affectus* → **affection** throughout (never "emotion" / "feeling"), because
  chapter VII treats it as a taxonomic term.

"Postulation" rather than "petition" for *postulatio* is deliberate: Hugh uses
*petitio* alongside it in the same definitions, so collapsing the two would erase the
distinction his three-fold scheme rests on. Cheap to reverse if a human prefers
"request."

## Chunk 0000

- **Col 0977D** — `Cogitemus quam brevis sit vita nostra, quam lubrica, via quam mors
  incerta.` The comma has slipped one word to the left: the members are *quam lubrica
  via, quam incerta mors*. Rendered "how brief our life is, how slippery the way, how
  uncertain death."
- **Col 0978C** — `si quemadmodum in praeterito nostri misertus, sit cogitemus.` The
  comma falls inside the perfect *misertus sit*. Rendered as one verb.
- **Col 0979B** — `Postremo nisi eam plus quam mala sua diligeret … refugium sibi in
  ipso eligeret.` The feminine `eam` has no clean antecedent in the sentence, whose
  other pronouns (*eo*, *ipso*) are masculine and refer to God; the nearest feminine
  is *misericordiam* implied by the preceding *speraret eum misereri velle*. Rendered
  "unless he loved that mercy more than his own evils," keeping the feminine.
  **A human may prefer** emending to *eum* and reading "loved him."

## Chunk 0001

- **Col 0980B** — `Hoc genus orandi in forma est, et quam prae caeteris omnibus
  unicum, tam est apud Deum prae caeteris omnibus pretiosum.` `in forma est` is
  obscure as it stands (candidates: *informe est*, "is without form," which suits pure
  prayer forgetting its own petition; or *in summa forma est*). Rendered neutrally,
  "is in a form of its own." **Flagged for a human** — the sense of the sentence turns
  on it.
- **Col 0981B** — `quia nos obstinere posse sola insinuatione speramus`. `obstinere`
  for *obtinere*. Rendered "obtain."
- **Col 0981B** — `quod in nuptiis deficiente vino Domino mater dicit. *Vinum non
  habent*` — a full stop, not a colon, before the quotation. Reproduced as printed.
- **Col 0981C** — `Maria enim a Christo non quasi mater exigere Filio, sed quasi
  mulier … miraculum humiliter postulare, debebat a Deo.` The construction is tangled
  (`a Christo … Filio` with a bare dative, and a comma before `debebat`). Rendered per
  the evident sense: "Mary ought not, as a mother, to exact a miracle of Christ as her
  Son, but, as a woman … humbly to postulate it of God."

### 0980B — sense-reversing-letter (migne, low confidence)

Printed: *Hoc genus orandi in forma est, et quam prae caeteris omnibus unicum, tam est apud Deum prae caeteris omnibus pretiosum.*

Conjecture: **Read *informe est* ("is formless") for the plate's word-divided *in forma est*; the privative in- has been split off and the claim inverted from "has no form" to "has form."**

Pure prayer is defined immediately before as the prayer that loses its own content: "prae amoris ejus magnitudine etiam petitionis suae obliviscatur ... ejus etiam, pro quo venit, curam libenter postponat" — it forgets even its own petition and sets aside the very business it came for. A few lines later the same chapter makes the outward formlessness explicit and grades it as the mark of the highest devotion: "quod per sola nomina fit, quo magis est foris significatione imperfectum, tanto magis intus est abundantia dilectionis plenum ... quanto major et ferventior intus est, tanto minus foris per vocem explicari potest", and "Illud igitur, quod solis nominibus fit, ad puram orationem pertinere videtur." Pure prayer is precisely the species with the least outward form, so "in forma est" asserts the opposite of what its own neighbours require. The English "in a form of its own" is a silent repair that smooths the defective plate rather than translating it.
