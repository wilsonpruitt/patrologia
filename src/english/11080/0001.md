## On the Four Wills in Christ (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ David, when his son was sick, poured out tears; when he was dead, he washed his face [n: (II Reg. XII)], to show that the misery of compassion is to be borne so long as the mercy of suffering is not to be despaired of. ⟦s2⟧ There is, however, a certain affection of compassion which pursues with commiseration even a nature that is lost, according to which, in the likeness of our own kind, just as we are angry at a fault not to be corrected, so we have pity on a nature not to be extinguished. ⟦s3⟧ But now mortal life, subject to mutability, for the meanwhile bears its share of misery; but afterward, translated to incorruption, it will lay it aside. ⟦s4⟧ Now [0844D] weak in both — namely in suffering in the flesh, and in compassion in the mind — then in both it will be healed, so that it may be strengthened against suffering through the immortality of the flesh, and against compassion through the immutability of the mind. ⟦s5⟧ For just as the sickness of the flesh is to suffer, so the sickness of the mind is to have compassion. ⟦s6⟧ Therefore the God-man, who came to take away both, endured both. ⟦s7⟧ He took up suffering in the flesh; he took up compassion in the mind. ⟦s8⟧ In both he willed to be sick for our sake, that in both he might heal us who were sick. ⟦s9⟧ He was made weak by suffering in his own penalty; he was made weak by compassion in another's misery. ⟦s10⟧ So far did he bear suffering, that he died for those about to die; so far did he take up compassion, that he wept for those about to perish. ⟦s11⟧ On account of misery he delivered his flesh to suffering; [0845A] on account of mercy he troubled his soul to compassion. ⟦s12⟧ In his flesh he grieved for us by suffering, in his mind he grieved together with us by having compassion; and just as the weakness of the flesh in him, according to the affection of nature, willed not its own penalty, so the piety of the mind, according to the affection of mercy, willed not another's misery. ⟦s13⟧ For just as, according to the will of the flesh, he asked that the cup of suffering be carried away from him, which he foreknew was not to be carried away, so, according to piety, he prayed that the sentence of condemnation be mitigated for others, which he immutably foresaw. ⟦s14⟧ For piety wrought what was its own by showing mercy, and reason followed what was its own by obeying the divine disposition. ⟦s15⟧ Therefore there was nothing of contrariety. ⟦s16⟧ If Christ as man, according to the affection of piety which [0845B] he had assumed in his humanity, willed something which nevertheless, according to the divine will in which he disposed all things with the Father, he foreknew was not going to be; because both this pertained to true humanity, that he should be moved with piety, and this pertained to true divinity, that he should not be moved from his own disposition. ⟦s17⟧ And so according to both he did what he ought. ⟦s18⟧ Nor for that reason was he contrary to himself, if according to piety befitting his humanity he willed what according to justice due to his divinity he willed not; because both this willing and that not-willing he willed, both of them. ⟦s19⟧ It is written: *Jesus* however *groaned in spirit and troubled himself [n: (Joan. XI)]*. ⟦s20⟧ Here give heed: *He troubled himself [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s21⟧ How did he trouble himself? ⟦s22⟧ What was that trouble wherewith Jesus troubled [0845C] himself? ⟦s23⟧ Piety itself, commiseration itself was the trouble. ⟦s24⟧ A good trouble: was he well troubled who was moved with piety? ⟦s25⟧ He troubled himself. ⟦s26⟧ Commiseration troubled him, and he himself troubled himself, who of his own accord took up [0846A] commiseration. ⟦s27⟧ Just as he delivered himself up, when Judas delivered him, so he troubled himself, when commiseration troubled him. ⟦s28⟧ This is well known and proved by frequent usage. ⟦s29⟧ It is said to a man when he suffers something: You have troubled yourself; you have done this evil to yourself. ⟦s30⟧ So Jesus troubled himself, when commiseration, which he had of his own accord taken up, troubled him; and he delivered himself up, when that man delivered him up to whom he had granted the power to deliver him. ⟦s31⟧ And so Jesus, in the assumed humanity, so long as he willed to bear it as passible, bore, according to the property of humanity, both suffering in the flesh and compassion in the mind. ⟦s32⟧ In which, however, now translated to immutability, he can neither suffer unto corruption, nor have compassion unto trouble. ⟦s33⟧ Since we have known Christ according to [0846B] the man, we have known him both as suffering and as compassionating [n: (II Cor. V)]. ⟦s34⟧ *My days,* he says, *have failed like smoke, and my bones have dried up like a dry stick [n: (Psal. CI)]*. ⟦s35⟧ The days failed on account of suffering; the bones dried up on account of compassion; on account of suffering the flesh died, on account of compassion the soul was dried up. ⟦s36⟧ The grief of compassion was like a kind of frying of the soul, whereby it was burned with commiseration, pressed with compassion, dried with desperation — desperation, I say, not on its own account, but on account of those who were neither to be corrected in their evil, nor to be freed from evil. ⟦s37⟧ In all these things we have known Christ according to the flesh, when he bore a true humanity, and in true humanity a true mortality [n: (II Cor. V)]. ⟦s38⟧ But now we no longer know it according to the flesh, after that [0846C] *rising from the dead he dies no more; death shall no longer have dominion over him. ⟦s39⟧ But in that he lives, he lives unto God [n: (Rom. VI)]*.
