## On the Sacraments of the Natural Law and of the Written Law (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0031C]D. What did it pertain to us that Christ conquered the devil? ⟦s2⟧ M. Christ is called the Son of man for this reason, that human nature, which in the first parent had grown old through sin, renewed in Christ flowered again [sic: *refleruit*] as it were unto the strength of youth; and it was just, even according to law, that on behalf of a decrepit father, weak as it were through the failing of age, a son of greater strength should make the encounter against the tyrant, and that his victory should avail unto liberty not for himself but chiefly for him for whom he had fought. ⟦s3⟧ For on this account also the Son of God willed to become the Son of man, that he might take from man that in which man might overcome the devil for man. ⟦s4⟧ D. Had not the devil also been cast out by certain men even before the passion of Christ? ⟦s5⟧ M. He had been, yet not save through [0031D] the passion. ⟦s6⟧ D. How can this be? ⟦s7⟧ M. As the passion of Christ, now past, sanctifies believers, so, being then to come, it sanctified believers. ⟦s8⟧ D. By what reasoning will you be able to confirm this? ⟦s9⟧ M. What should I have called the incarnate Word, save a king who entered this world through the humanity he assumed, to do battle with the devil, and thereafter to drive him out as a tyrant and one violently lording it over what was another's. ⟦s10⟧ And what should I have called all the earlier saints who from the beginning before the incarnation were elect, save certain most excellent soldiers going before the king who was to come in the battle-line, fortified and girded as with arms by those very sacraments by which they were then sanctified. ⟦s11⟧ What, likewise, should I call those who after the incarnation unto the end follow as elect, save other soldiers, following with one mind and eagerly not another but [0032A] their own king himself going before them, they too, being new, having new arms, yet armed by the same one, and about to fight against the devil. ⟦s12⟧ They are, therefore, whether going before or following after, bearing the sacraments of one king, serving as soldiers of one king, and overcoming one tyrant; those going before him who was to come, these following after him who went before. ⟦s13⟧ Do you see, then, how all from the beginning unto the end who are good are Christ's? ⟦s14⟧ Nor do any overcome the devil save those alone who are Christ's. ⟦s15⟧ D. Therefore from the beginning they were Christians? ⟦s16⟧ M. Nothing is truer. ⟦s17⟧ D. But many judge otherwise. ⟦s18⟧ M. Do you wish the same to be shown you more evidently? ⟦s19⟧ D. I do. ⟦s20⟧ M. There are three times through which the space of the present age runs. ⟦s21⟧ The first is the time of the natural law; the second, the time of the written law; the third is the time [0032B] of grace. ⟦s22⟧ The first is from Adam unto Moses; the second from Moses unto Christ; the third from Christ unto the end of the age. ⟦s23⟧ Likewise there are three kinds of men, that is, men of the natural law, men of the written law, men of grace. ⟦s24⟧ Men of the natural law can be called those who direct their life by natural reason alone; or rather, men of the natural law are called those who walk according to the concupiscence in which they were born, Men of the written law are those who are formed unto good living by outward precepts. ⟦s25⟧ Men of grace are those who, breathed upon by the inspiration of the Holy Spirit, are both enlightened, that they may know the good which is to be done, and inflamed, that they may love it, and strengthened, that they may accomplish it. ⟦s26⟧ And, that we may signify the same by a plainer distinction, men of the natural [0032C] law are openly evil; men of the written law, feignedly good; men of grace, truly good. ⟦s27⟧ In the first kind are contained the pagans; in the second, the Jews; in the third, the Christians. ⟦s28⟧ These three kinds of men have from the beginning at no time been lacking, yet the time of the natural law pertains to the openly evil; who then were both more in number and more excellent in state. ⟦s29⟧ The time of the written law, to the feignedly good; because then men, serving in fear, cleansed the work, not the mind. ⟦s30⟧ The time of grace pertains to the truly good; who now, even if they be not more in number, are nevertheless more excellent in state, and by the grace of God are publicly set first even by those who contradict it in their manners. ⟦s31⟧ Know, therefore, that at whatever time from the beginning of the world unto [0032D] the end there has been and is none truly good save one justified through grace; and that grace could never be obtained save through Christ, so that you may acknowledge that all, whether going before or following after, were saved by one remedy of justification. ⟦s32⟧ Look, then, upon the camp of our king and the battle-lines of his army shining in spiritual arms, and with how great a multitude of preceding and following peoples he goes attended.
