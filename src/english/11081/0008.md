## On the Sacraments of the Natural Law and of the Written Law (cont.)

D. What were those things which the natural law had set forth to be done or to be shunned? M. Those seven which the written law afterwards commanded in the second table concerning the love of one's neighbor. The first was: *Honor thy father and thy mother, that it may be well with thee, and that thou mayest be long-lived upon the earth;* the second: *Thou shalt not kill;* the third: *Thou shalt not commit adultery;* the fourth: *Thou shalt not steal;* the fifth: *Thou shalt not speak false testimony;* the sixth: *Thou shalt not covet thy neighbor's wife;* the seventh: *Thou shalt not covet thy neighbor's goods [n: (Deut. V)]*. The first was made according to precept; the six others according to prohibition. D. Why was one precept only given concerning things not to be done, and more concerning things to be done? [0039D] M. The first sin of man was disobedience; and therefore this life, which is granted to man for penitence, is wholly assigned to obedience. Since therefore man, in doing evil things, is drawn in divers directions by his own concupiscence, but in doing good things is governed by the rule of obedience alone; therefore in guarding against evils, which he has of himself and can do through himself, he had to be instructed one by one, but for doing good things he had only to be recalled to obedience, that there, through the judgment of another, he might more healthfully learn what he himself ought to do. For when man is bidden to render honor to father and mother, this is not to be referred to carnal parents only; but rather this is commanded him, that what he once despised in his own commandments, [0040A] he should now, for God's sake, not disdain to show forth toward a man set above him in obedience and honor.

D. Behold, you have shown three things, that is, faith, and the sacraments of faith, and works. Now it remains that you show what and how great a restoration has been wrought in those who had these things. M. If the highest good of man were the contemplation of God present, especially since the first man is believed to have been founded for the full enjoyment of this, deservedly is faith, through which he begins in a certain way to see him who is absent, called the beginning of good and the first principle of his restoration. For since man, who by sinning lost the knowledge of God, is again enlightened through faith, that he may be able to see and to know him; it is plain that that through which the first good which he had lost is given back to him is rightly called the outset of his restoration. [0040B] And this restoration grows, of course, according to the increases of faith, while man is the more enlightened through acknowledgment and inflamed *perdilectionem*. Faith, therefore, is that itself through which man is repaired, and in which the lost good is given back to him, so that through it, growing gradually alike in knowledge and in love, he may deserve to attain to that full felicity of vision. For thus the just man, so long as, existing in this body, he journeys away from the Lord, has to live by faith, just as the blessed man, when he shall have been led out of this workhouse and brought into the joy of his Lord, will have to live by contemplation. But by a wondrous dispensation of God it comes about that now for the meanwhile the malice of the ancient enemy is let loose to assail and persecute the faithful, so that, namely, this may be reckoned to man for merit, if [0040C] now, walking through faith, he forsake not the way of truth even when assailed — he who before, strengthened by the vision of God present, was laid low by persuasion alone. But that he may be able to stand unconquered in this battle and to guard his good unharmed, there are given to him, as has already been said, arms in the sacraments with which to fortify himself, and darts in good works with which to lay low the enemy; namely, that with charity and hope joined to faith he may alike be strengthened inwardly and conquer. Now (as I judge) it has been sufficiently shown you what faith and what sacraments or what works were necessary to those first saints for the obtaining of salvation.

D. How long did that state of the earlier men last? M. Unto Abraham. For then God, willing to gather his people into one and to set them apart from the fellowship of the unbelieving, [0040D] that they might be not only in one faith but also in one society, called Abraham, that he might lead him out of his own land, so that through that one man and in that one man he might, leading out from their former manner of life all who were to be born of him or to follow him, gather them into one society of a new life. To whom also he commanded that he should circumcise the skin of his foreskin as a sign for himself, as an example for those who should come after; adding to the former state only so far as that through this little sign the people of God might be distinguished from the unbelieving, until he should live who would gather the faithful not only from the sons of Abraham but even from all the nations, and through this would set them apart no longer by the little sign of the flesh in the race, but by the little sign of baptism in cleansing. [0041A] D. Were not the faithful also distinguished from the unbelieving of old before the flood? M. They were; but after the flood the human race, brought back again to one people, began a second time to be distinguished in Abraham. D. In what manner was circumcision performed? M. It was commanded that a male child on the eighth day of his birth should be circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin with a little knife made of stone. D. What was the reason that it was commanded to be done thus? M. There are three circumcisions: one in the flesh only, outwardly, which is the sacrament; two others which are the reality and the power of the sacrament. The one which is done in the present, when through the laying aside of iniquity the mind is circumcised. The other which will be done in the future, when through the laying aside of corruption the body will be circumcised. The first, therefore, is in the flesh; the second in the mind; the third [0041B] in the body. But the number eight in Holy Scripture sometimes signifies the time of the resurrection, which follows after the present life (which runs through seven days); sometimes the time of grace, in which eternal goods are promised to those who serve God. Deservedly, therefore, that first circumcision, which was the sacrament of the other two, was commanded to be done on the eighth day, that it might show that in the time of grace hearts were to be circumcised [0042A] through the cleansing away of iniquity, and in the time of the resurrection bodies through the laying aside of corruption. And the circumcision of the flesh was given to males alone for this reason, that Holy Scripture is wont to signify the soul by the male sex, but the flesh by the female; so that, namely, it might be shown that that outward circumcision conferred justification upon souls, but did not take away corruption from the flesh. The little knife made of stone signifies Christ, of whom the Apostle says: *But the rock was Christ [n: (I Cor. X)]*, *who taketh away the sins of the world [n: (Joan. I)]*. It was commanded to be done in the flesh of the foreskin for this reason, that it was given as a remedy against original sin, which we contract from our parents, being propagated through that member. D. What remedy, then, had women, who were not circumcised? [0042B] M. Faith and oblations or sacrifices: circumcision itself also; yet not by receiving it, but by believing it. D. What is to be said of infants dying before the eighth day, since they could not attain to that remedy — had they been saved or damned? M. This seems to me to be left to the judgment of God.
