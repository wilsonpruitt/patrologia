# Cruces — 11081 (Hugh of St Victor, *De sacramentis legis naturalis et scriptae*)

## Convention — speaker labels (chunk 0000, col 0017C onward)
- **Reading (as printed):** `D. Quid fuit priusquam mundus fieret? M. Solus Deus.`
- **Note:** The work is a dialogue and Migne labels the speakers with bare initials, `D.` (*Discipulus*) and `M.` (*Magister*). The English initials of the house terms coincide exactly — Disciple / Master — so the labels are reproduced as `D.` and `M.` unchanged in all nine chunks. Nothing is expanded and nothing is invented; the plate's abbreviation stands. Fixed here per the 11066 speaker-label precedent and held to the end of the work.

## Convention — sacramental vocabulary (chunks 0000–0008)
- **Note:** Equivalents fixed at first occurrence and held across all nine chunks: *sacramentum* = sacrament; *signum* = sign; *signaculum* = little sign; *res* = reality (technical: *sacrae rei signum* = "the sign of a sacred reality"; *res sive virtus sacramenti* = "the reality or the power of the sacrament"; *res et virtus sacramenti* = "the reality and the power of the sacrament"); *virtus* = power; *figura* = figure; *umbra* = shadow; *imago* = image; *institutio* = institution; *similitudo* = likeness; *efficacia* = efficacy; *conferre* = to confer; *significatio* = signification; *sanctificatio* = sanctification; *gratia* = grace; *remedium* = remedy; *medicamentum* = medicine; *lex naturalis* = the natural law; *lex scripta* = the written law; *tempus gratiae* = the time of grace; *oblatio* = oblation; *sacrificium* = sacrifice; *circumcisio* = circumcision. The four *opera* of 0021A are kept distinct: *opus creationis* = work of creation, *opus dispositionis* = work of disposition, *opus propagationis* = work of propagation, *opus gubernationis* = work of governance; *opera conditionis* (0020C) = "the works of the founding," to keep it separate from *creationis*. In the anthropology: *appetitus justi/justitiae* = appetite for the just / for justice, *appetitus commodi* = appetite for the advantageous, *commodum* = advantage, *praeceptum naturae / disciplinae* = the precept of nature / of discipline, *concessio* = concession.

## Col 0020A (chunk 0000)
- **Reading (as printed):** `unde et nunc ad omnes terrarum partes per thracones et alveos suos derivantur`
- **Note:** `thracones` is not a word I can attest in medieval Latin lexica (the sense required is "channels, conduits," parallel to *alveos*; possibly a compositor's mangling of *tracones* or of a Greek-derived term). Per Pattern 7's non-word carve-out it is carried into the English untranslated and in italics: "through their *thracones* and their channels." If it is later shown to be an attested form, this entry should be reversed under Pattern 9, not churned silently.

## Col 0020D (chunk 0000)
- **Reading (as printed):** `tribus primis diebus disponens et ordinans, tribus sequentibus exordinans`
- **Note:** `exordinans` is a formed Latin word (*ex-* + *ordino*) and so is rendered rather than carried through, but the sentence's own continuation supplies the intended reading: `quarta exornans coelos` — the three "following" days are the days of *adorning* (*exornans*), not of a second ordering, and *disponens et ordinans* has just been used of the first three. Rendered as printed: "setting out in order in the three following." Intended reading: *exornans*, "adorning."

## Col 0021C (chunk 0000)
- **Reading (as printed):** `neque ordinem unversitatis in aliquo commutavit`
- **Note:** `unversitatis` is not a word — a dropped *i* in *universitatis*, and the correct form stands in the immediately preceding clause (`numero universitatis`). Per Pattern 7 the non-word is carried into the English untranslated and in italics: "nor did he change the order of the *unversitatis* in anything."

## Col 0021C (chunk 0000)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Nihil tamen novum addici dicitur`
- **Note:** `addici` is a real Latin form (present passive infinitive of *addico*, "to be adjudged, awarded"), so Pattern 7 governs and the non-word carve-out does not apply: rendered "nothing new is said to be adjudged." The argument plainly wants *addi* ("to be added") — the question being answered is whether daily-created souls **add** anything to the universe, and the preceding sentence reads `neque aliquid postmodum numero universitatis addidit`. The conjecture stays here.

## Col 0021C (chunk 0000)
- **Reading (as printed):** `D. Et quid quod adhuc quotidie novae creantur animae.`
- **Note:** A full stop closes an interrogative (`quid quod…`). Preserved per Pattern 8; no `?` supplied.

## Col 0022C–D (chunk 0001)
- **Reading (as printed):** `D. Qnare hoc voluit? M. Ad [0022D] retundendam superbiam diaboli.`
- **Note:** `Qnare` is not a word — an n/u inversion of *Quare*, which is printed correctly in the surrounding questions. Per Pattern 7 it is carried into the English untranslated and in italics: "D. *Qnare* did he will this?"

## Col 0023B (chunk 0001)
- **Reading (as printed):** `cum illud ejus quod de nihilo factum esse multo majus sit quam id quod de viri substantia in corpus illius transivit`
- **Note:** `factum esse` (infinitive) stands where the relative clause requires a finite verb (*factum est*); as printed the clause has no governing verb of saying to hold the accusative-and-infinitive. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow — "since that part of her which was made out of nothing is much greater than that which passed over from the substance of the man into her body" — with the defect recorded here rather than marked in the text.

## Col 0025A (chunk 0002)
- **Reading (as printed):** `tanto gravius plectendus crat, quanto leviori impulsu fuerat prostratus`
- **Note:** `crat` is not a word — e/c substitution for *erat*. Per Pattern 7 it is carried into the English untranslated and in italics: "*crat* so much the more grievously to be punished, by how much the lighter the impulse by which he had been laid low."

## Col 0025D (chunk 0002)
- **Reading (as printed):** `quatenus homo vel illo solo amisso hujus et illius affectus perderet; vel illo solo retento hujus et illius effectum retineret`
- **Note:** `affectus` in the first limb against `effectum` in the second breaks a deliberately exact antithesis, and the whole preceding sentence turns on *effectum suum* twice (`illi effectum suum… isti vero effectum suum`). `affectus` is a real word, so Pattern 7 governs and it is rendered: "should lose the affection of both the one and the other… should keep the effect of both the one and the other." Intended reading: *effectum*.

## Col 0027B (chunk 0002)
- **Reading (as printed):** `D. Non potest inordinare non appetere? M. Non potest.`
- **Note:** As printed the question is not construable: `inordinare` is the infinitive of *inordino* ("to disorder"), which cannot govern or be governed by `non appetere` here. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow — "Is he not able to disorder, not to desire?" The intended reading is the adverb *inordinate*, which stands correctly in the Master's immediately preceding answer (`sed quod inordinate appetit`): "Can he not avoid desiring in a disordered way?" No emendation made in the text.

## Col 0028A (chunk 0003)
- **Reading (as printed):** `M. Quatuor sunt loquar: unus in quo est solum bonum…`
- **Note:** Unconstruable as printed — a finite `loquar` ("I shall speak") stands where the four following masculine members (`unus… unus… unus… unus`, answered by *in coelo… in inferno… in paradiso… in mundo*) require a noun, i.e. *Quatuor sunt loca*, "There are four places." Rendered as printed with a dash marking the break: "There are four — I shall speak:". Intended reading: *loca*.

## Col 0029C (chunk 0004)
- **Reading (as printed):** `injuste autem quantum pertinet ab diaboli fraudulentiam`
- **Note:** `ab` + accusative, against the exactly parallel preceding clause `quantum pertinet ad culpam suam`. `ab` is a real word, so Pattern 7 governs and the printed preposition is rendered: "but unjustly so far as pertains **from** the deceitfulness of the devil." Intended reading: *ad*.

## Col 0030A (chunk 0004)
- **Reading (as printed):** `sicut Isaias ait: *Puer natus est nobis [n: (Isa. II)]*, etc.`
- **Note:** Migne's note locates *Puer natus est nobis* at Isaiah II; the verse is Isaiah 9:6. The note is reproduced verbatim per the marker rule (`refDisplay` keeps what Migne prints); no correction is made in the text or in the note. Recorded so the indexer's resolution is a documented inference, not a silent fix.

## Col 0030B (chunk 0004)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Ideo in nativitate Christi angeli pacem munde nuntiant`
- **Note:** `munde` is a real Latin adverb ("cleanly, purely") and is therefore rendered rather than carried through: "the angels announce peace purely." The sense the passage requires — and the quotation immediately following (*in terra pax hominibus bonae voluntatis*) — is the dative *mundo*, "announce peace **to the world**." This is a case where Pattern 7 produces a genuinely misleading English sentence; the conjecture is here and not in the text by design.

## Col 0031B (chunk 0004)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Judicium secundum operationem imago quodammodo est sacramentum judicii secundum retributionem`
- **Note:** Two nominatives (`imago… sacramentum`) share one copula with no connective. Rendered as apposition without supplying a conjunction (Pattern 8 forbids adding the mark or word the plate lacks): "is in a certain way an image, a sacrament of the judgment according to retribution." A reader may prefer *imago quodammodo et sacramentum*; that would be a supplement, so it stays here.

## Col 0031C (chunk 0005)
- **Reading (as printed):** `humana natura… in Christo renovata quasi ad juventutis robur refleruit`
- **Note:** `refleruit` is not a word — for *refloruit*, "flowered again" (the metaphor is fixed by *senuerat* … *ad juventutis robur*). Per Pattern 7 it is carried into the English untranslated and in italics: "renewed in Christ *refleruit* as it were unto the strength of youth."

## Col 0032B (chunk 0005)
- **Reading (as printed):** `qui secundum concupiscentiam in qua nati sunt ambulant, Homines scriptae legis sunt ii qui…`

## Col 0032C (chunk 0005)
- **Reading (as printed):** `tempus naturalis legis ad aperte malos pertinet; qui illi tunc et numero plures et statu excellentiores fuerunt`
- **Note:** `qui illi` doubles the pronoun where the parallel clauses later in the passage carry a bare relative (`qui modo etiamsi numero plures non sint`). Rendered as printed: "who then were both more in number and more excellent in state." Whether `illi` is a stray or an emphatic is not decidable from the plate; no word is dropped.

## Col 0033B (chunk 0006)
- **Reading (as printed):** `« Proposuit et diabolus sacramenta sua… una Christi, altera diaboli. »`
- **Note:** Migne opens and closes a guillemet pair around a stretch of Hugh's own continuing exposition — there is no named source, no *inquit*, and the sentences before and after it are in the same voice. It is most likely a set-off of a much-excerpted passage rather than a quotation proper. Per Pattern 6 the marks are reproduced 1:1 exactly where Migne places them; they are not demoted to straight quotes and nothing is added or removed. This is the work's only guillemet pair.

## Col 0033C (chunk 0006)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Verum haec maligni versutia machinatio, divinae misericordiae dispensatio fuit`
- **Note:** `versutia machinatio` is two nominatives in asyndeton where the sense wants a genitive (*versutiae machinatio*, "the contrivance of the malign one's craft"). Rendered as apposition without supplying a connective: "But this contrivance, the craft of the malign one, was the dispensation of divine mercy."

## Col 0034D (chunk 0006)
- **Reading (as printed):** `D. Ergo in ommni sacramento plus esse credendum est`
- **Note:** `ommni` is not a word — doubled *m* for *omni*. Per Pattern 7 it is carried into the English untranslated and in italics: "in *ommni* sacrament."

## Col 0035A (chunk 0006)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Si quis sacramenta priore effectum sanctificationis habuisse negaverit`
- **Note:** `priore` is an ablative singular attached to the accusative plural `sacramenta`; the required form is *priora*. English adjectives carry no exponent for Latin case or number, so — as with the gender class in Pattern 9 — there is no emendation to make and none to undo: "that the earlier sacraments had the effect of sanctification." Marked so a later sweep does not churn it.

## Col 0035A (chunk 0006)
- **Reading (as printed):** `ut videlicet simul et ex institutione significat, et ex similitudine repraesentet, et conferat ex sanctificatione`
- **Note:** Indicative `significat` inside a `ut videlicet` clause whose two other members are subjunctive (`repraesentet`, `conferat`). English has no exponent for the Latin mood contrast in this construction, so all three are rendered alike ("signifies… represents… confers"). Nothing emended; logged so the mismatch is attributed to the plate.

## Col 0035B (chunk 0006)

- **Note:** The comma that the three-member series requires after `robur` is absent, though the second one is present. Per Pattern 8 the printed punctuation is reproduced exactly: "receiving strength in faith arms in the sacraments of faith, darts in good works." No comma supplied.

## Col 0038D (chunk 0007)

- **Note:** `ot` is not a word — o/e substitution for *et*. Per Pattern 7 it is carried into the English untranslated and in italics: "(which in it *ot* through it the sacrament obtains)."

## Col 0039B (chunk 0007)
- **Reading (as printed):** `cum lex subintrasset, et disciplinam vivendi arctiorem proponeret coepit ex iis quae media erant quaedam excipere`
- **Note:** No comma before the main verb `coepit`, closing the two-member *cum* clause. Preserved per Pattern 8; no mark supplied: "…and was setting forth a stricter discipline of living it began to except…".

## Col 0039C (chunk 0008)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Non maechaberis`
- **Note:** **Not a defect.** *maechaberis* for *moechaberis* is the routine medieval ae/oe interchange, attested throughout the period, and falls under Pattern 9's first class: translated normally, no italics, no carry-through — "Thou shalt not commit adultery." Logged only so a later sweep does not mistake it for a Pattern 7 case.

## Col 0039C–D (chunk 0008)
- **Reading (as printed):** `D. Quare de non faciendis unum tantum praeceptum datum est, et de faciendis plura?`
- **Note:** The two halves are inverted against the sentence immediately preceding, which has just enumerated **one** precept (*Honora patrem tuum…*, `secundum praeceptionem`) and **six** prohibitions (`sex alia secundum prohibitionem`) — so the single precept concerns things **to be done** and the many concern things **not** to be done. The Master's answer confirms the correct direction (`in malis cavendis… singulatim instruendus erat, ad bona vero agenda ad obedientiam solummodo revocandus`). Rendered exactly as printed: "Why was one precept only given concerning things not to be done, and more concerning things to be done?" The transposition is Migne's; it is not smoothed.

## Col 0040B (chunk 0008)
- **Reading (as printed):** `dum homo per agnitionem amplius illuminatur et inflammatur perdilectionem`
- **Note:** `perdilectionem` is a run-together of *per dilectionem* ("through love") — Pattern 10's word-division class, the same defect as a split run. The whole broken run is carried into the English untranslated and in italics, spacing as printed: "the more enlightened through acknowledgment and inflamed *perdilectionem*."
