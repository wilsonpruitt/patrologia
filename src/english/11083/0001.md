## THERE FOLLOWS *The determination of the question concerning the soul of Christ, whether it had knowledge equal with the divinity.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ You ask concerning the soul of Christ, that is, concerning that rational spirit which quickened the human flesh in Christ the man, which the Word united to itself, together with the flesh and in the flesh itself, into the unity of a person, so that Christ might be one in one person, in two natures, in three essences: in one person, namely, because the same is man, on account of him whom he took up, and God, on account of that which he was; in two natures, that is, divine and human, in God and man being one, not several; in three essences, divinity, [0847D] flesh, and soul — distinguished without separation, joined without confusion. ⟦s2⟧ Of that soul, I say, by whose mediation *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us [n: (Joan. I)]*, you ask whether it had knowledge equal with the divinity. ⟦s3⟧ You add also that, if that soul — which, although it had been ineffably united to the creating nature, was nevertheless beyond doubt a creature — had knowledge equal with the divinity, then assuredly a creature was equal to the Creator; but that a thing he has made cannot be made comparable in equality to the Creator, even if it could be taken up into the fellowship of unity. ⟦s4⟧ That it is one thing altogether to be united by his condescension, and another to be compared in majesty. ⟦s5⟧ There are many other things which, in this manner, you were perhaps able to show to be absurd. ⟦s6⟧ But I desire, together with you, so to refute what is false as to profess what is true. ⟦s7⟧ For how [0848A] would God be immeasurable, if any creature, however excellent, however singular, could reach to equality with him? ⟦s8⟧ *Great,* says the Psalmist, *is the Lord, and great is his power; and of his wisdom there is no number [n: (Psal. CXLVI)]*. ⟦s9⟧ And Solomon: *There is no wisdom, there is no prudence, there is no counsel against God [n: (Prov. XXI)]*. ⟦s10⟧ Where there is no "how much," what can be said to be "so much"? ⟦s11⟧ A portion is sought where number is not found. ⟦s12⟧ Of his wisdom there is no number. ⟦s13⟧ What therefore do I say? ⟦s14⟧ Either the soul of Christ was not a creature, or it could not be made equal to the Creator. ⟦s15⟧ Nay rather, because it is a creature, it is not — I do not say to be made equal to the Creator — but I dare to say more: it is not even to be compared with him. ⟦s16⟧ Therefore, you say, it had less wisdom than the Word? ⟦s17⟧ But I ask, what do we say when we call a soul wise [0848B]? ⟦s18⟧ Do we not call it wise by wisdom? ⟦s19⟧ For who is wise, if wisdom is not wise? ⟦s20⟧ And yet in one way he is wise in whom wisdom is, in another way he is wise who is wisdom. ⟦s21⟧ The creature is wise because wisdom is in it. ⟦s22⟧ But the Creator is wise because he himself is wisdom. ⟦s23⟧ Yet no one can be wise without [sic: *sapentia*], whether by that which he has, or by that which he himself is. ⟦s24⟧ If therefore the soul of Christ is wise — nay rather, because it is — is it wise, beyond doubt, by that wisdom which it has, or by that which it itself is? ⟦s25⟧ But a creature, even if it is wise by participation in wisdom, cannot itself be wisdom. ⟦s26⟧ What then is that wisdom by which the soul of Christ is wise? ⟦s27⟧ What does John say of the Word? ⟦s28⟧ *He was,* he says, *the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world* [0848C] [n: (Joan. I)]. ⟦s29⟧ What is the Word but wisdom? ⟦s30⟧ For the same who is called the Word of God by John is himself named the wisdom of God by Paul. ⟦s31⟧ For *Christ,* says Paul the apostle, *the power of God and the wisdom of God [n: (I Cor. I)]*. ⟦s32⟧ Christ therefore is himself both the Word, and himself is wisdom. ⟦s33⟧ The Word is wisdom, and wisdom is the Word. ⟦s34⟧ The Word is wisdom, because, *My heart has uttered forth a good word [n: (Psal. XLIV)]*. ⟦s35⟧ Wisdom is the word, because, *I, wisdom, came forth from the mouth of the Most High, the firstborn before every creature [n: (Eccli., XXIV)]*. ⟦s36⟧ Wisdom therefore is the word of the heart, and the word is the wisdom of the mouth; because, while the word is said to come forth from the heart and wisdom from the mouth, wisdom and the word are shown to be the same. ⟦s37⟧ And so, if the word enlightens, wisdom enlightens; because the word could not [0848D] enlighten, if it were not wisdom. ⟦s38⟧ For what does a word do without wisdom? ⟦s39⟧ The word sounds, wisdom enlightens. ⟦s40⟧ For wisdom itself also is light, and God is light, because God is wisdom; and when God enlightens, wisdom enlightens, and enlightens with light; nor does he enlighten with another light, but with that light which it itself is; because it is light, and the word is light, and wisdom is light; because the word is wisdom, and wisdom is God. ⟦s41⟧ He was, he says, the true light which enlightens every man coming into this world. ⟦s42⟧ What then? ⟦s43⟧ Does this light enlighten the wicked, and does God enlighten the wicked? ⟦s44⟧ How then are they blind? ⟦s45⟧ Hear what follows: *And the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it [n: (Joan. I)]*. ⟦s46⟧ What is this, the light shines in the darkness? ⟦s47⟧ And what does the darkness do, if the light is there? ⟦s48⟧ And [0849A] the darkness does not comprehend it. ⟦s49⟧ Yet that light shines and enlightens, but not all who see by it see it. ⟦s50⟧ The light shines, and let us know through itself that it shines through itself, and enlightens from itself. ⟦s51⟧ It shines: it shines through itself, and enlightens from itself. ⟦s52⟧ For as he was, he says, the true light which enlightens every man coming into this world. ⟦s53⟧ John was a light, because *you are the light of the world [n: (Matth. V)]*; but he was not the true light, because *a lamp* he was, *burning and shining [n: (Joan. V)]*; not shining through itself, and enlightening from itself, like the light which enlightens every man coming into this world. ⟦s54⟧ Whom did he name? ⟦s55⟧ He did not say the good, nor the spiritual; but every man coming into this world. ⟦s56⟧ For there is one sun, and every eye is irradiated by it, but not all who see by it recognize [0849B] it. ⟦s57⟧ In like manner the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world, is poured out upon all, grows bright upon all, illumines all; but one man sees by it, another sees it. ⟦s58⟧ The wicked are enlightened that they may see other things; the very one by whom they see, they do not see, because the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it. ⟦s59⟧ But the good are enlightened that they may see him from whom and by whom they see, that they may refer to him, and love in him, everything that they see, and love him above everything that they see. ⟦s60⟧ All therefore who see are enlightened by him, but more excellently those who see him. ⟦s61⟧ Those who see are enlightened by him, and from him they are enlightened, because he himself is the light from whom one is enlightened. ⟦s62⟧ But from what would the light enlighten, if it did not enlighten [0849C] from its own self? ⟦s63⟧ From what would it enlighten, if it did not offer for sharing that which it itself is? ⟦s64⟧ But perhaps it must be feared that it be polluted by what is unclean, that it be darkened by what is shadowy; and that it be unworthy that the holy be called communicable to the profane, the unpolluted to the defiled, the true to those who err. ⟦s65⟧ These things are thought to no purpose, because the light which shines in the darkness, just as it is not darkened by what is shadowy, so is not corrupted by what is defiled, remaining bright in the darkness and undefiled amid filth. ⟦s66⟧ For, since all things are in it and it is in all things, yet to it the foulness of the whole creation is not foul, nor does its malice harm it, nor does its error err. ⟦s67⟧ It shines therefore and enlightens, neither through another nor from another; but that which it itself is, and by which it itself shines, it offers to be shared with all who are to be enlightened, and itself possesses the whole. [0849D] Hear again: *The Lord,* says Scripture, *gives wisdom [n: (Dan. II)]*. ⟦s68⟧ Which wisdom he gives, perhaps, as some accident by which we are affected, that we may be made wise.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6+s7+s8+s9+s10+s11+s12+s13+s14+s15+s16+s17+s18+s19+s20+s21+s22+s23+s24+s25+s26+s27+s28+s29+s30+s31+s32+s33+s34+s35+s36+s37+s38+s39+s40+s41+s42+s43⟧ For what is wisdom? Is it not an accident? The Lord therefore gives wisdom. What wisdom? *All wisdom is from the Lord God [n: (Eccli. I)]*. Well said, because all accidents are from the Lord God, from whom are all the things that are. Or is some accident perhaps not from God, since sin is not from him? Yet all wisdom is from God. How is it from him? Perhaps because it is his gift, it is from him; because it is a thing made by him, it is from him; and all substance is from him? What great thing? what new thing? what wonderful thing has been said? All wisdom is from [0850A] the Lord God — if some accident is from him from whom all substance is? How then is all wisdom from the Lord God? Either because it is his work, or because it is his gift? If wisdom is a creature, what is folly? Did God create wisdom, and not create folly? Therefore God created certain accidents, while others have being, and do not have it from him. Who would presume either to say this, or to approve it when said, that something has being and does not have it from God? What do we say? Did God then not create accidents, and is some other the Creator of wisdom, and God the giver? Was God then able to give wisdom, and not able to create it? Did he then receive from elsewhere what he gave, and give what was not his own, and was he able to receive what he had not had? Who would say this? Who of sound head would admit this even into his own [0850B] thought? In what way then is all wisdom from the Lord God? Whether because it is his gift, or because it is a thing made by him? For if it is a thing made, it is not always, because what once began has not always had being. What then does it say? All wisdom, it says, is from the Lord God; *and it has been with him always, and is before all time [n: (ibid.)]*. It is a wonder: accidents therefore have always been with God; and if accidents have always been with God, they were not substances. Or is all wisdom from God, and not all wisdom with God? God forbid. But all wisdom is from him, and all wisdom is in him, and all wisdom is with him, and always is. And what do we make of the wisdom which is an accident? Is not it too always with God? How then is All wisdom from the Lord God, and has it been with him [0850C] always, and is before all time? What then do we make of it? Let us shut out from the inner shrine of wisdom the fictions and phantasms of folly — these I know not what sort of things, and empty dreams, which truly are accidents to us, because they were not created with us. Let us acknowledge the true wisdom by which we are wise, whoever we are that are wise, whatever we are wise about, even if we are not wise about it. Let us attend to the light shining in the darkness, lest we be darkness; because the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it. And the darkness, he says, did not comprehend it; nay rather, did not apprehend it. For how could they have comprehended the incomprehensible? And the darkness did not comprehend it.
