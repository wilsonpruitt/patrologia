## THERE FOLLOWS *The determination of the question concerning the soul of Christ, whether it had knowledge equal with the divinity.* (cont.)

What then did the light do? Light comprehended light. [0850D] And what is this light? Certainly, if light comprehends light, then they have comprehended the light who from the light have been made light. They have comprehended the light who have been enlightened by the light. They have comprehended the light who took up into themselves that light which is above them. They comprehended it by likeness; they did not comprehend it by equality. This therefore is the one and true light of all rational spirits, and chiefly of the holy ones, which enlightens every man coming into this world. Which is shared by all without diminution of itself; and although it is the same in all, it is nevertheless not perceived by all in the same manner. For it, whatever it is, is whole, and is everywhere whole; nor is there less or more in it, and yet, according to the difference of participation, all perceive less and more of it who, according to [0851A] their own condition or the gift of the bestower's grace, are less and more capable of this same communication and participation. Participation, therefore, is at one time greater, at another less. But he who is participated is not at one time greater or less; rather, he himself is everywhere the same, whole and full and perfect. When therefore we call souls wise, and call them wise by wisdom, let us think of that wisdom, and believe that it alone is that by which they are wise, whatever rational minds have received the power of understanding and of discerning. *This is,* he says, *life eternal, that they may know thee, the only God; and Jesus Christ whom thou hast sent [n: (Joan. XVII)]*. And so the knowledge of God is life eternal; and what is the knowledge of God? Is it an accident? Then the love of God is an accident, and beatitude is an accident, and our beatitude [0851B] lies in accidents. Why should not God rather be our beatitude? He himself is the wisdom by which we know, he himself the love by which we love, he himself the joy in which we exult. There is therefore one wisdom by which all are wise; nor yet are they wise in one manner, because they are wise by participating. But if all who are wise are wise by this wisdom, much more is that soul wise by this wisdom which was united to wisdom itself. Which did not flourish from it by the lot of participation, but possessed the fullness of it by the privilege of unity. For what could it have less of wisdom, to which the very fullness of wisdom had been embodied? *In whom,* he says, *dwells all the fullness of the divinity bodily [n: (Coloss. II)]*. Of whom did he say this — was it not of Christ? How then does all the fullness of the divinity dwell in Christ? There are three essences in [0851C] Christ: flesh, soul, divinity. What then dwells in what? Divinity in divinity, or divinity in the soul, or divinity in the flesh? But what is this, divinity dwells in divinity? Was this either fitting to say or necessary to hear? Therefore the fullness of Christ's divinity dwells in the humanity of Christ. And if the fullness of Christ's divinity, then the fullness of the wisdom of God also. For what is the divinity of Christ but the wisdom of God? Therefore the fullness of the wisdom of God dwells in the humanity of Christ. Where? In the flesh or in the soul, or at once in the flesh and in the soul? Or in the flesh through the flesh? Then wisdom would dwell in the flesh, and would not dwell in the soul. God forbid! The seat of wisdom is the soul of the just man, and the house of wisdom is the soul of Christ. And [0851D] what have I said — the house of wisdom is the soul of Christ? I ought to have said more, if I could have said more; for because wisdom dwells in it, therefore I called it a house; but because it dwells there bodily, therefore I ought to have said more. What is it, that it dwells bodily? It has been made one with that in which it dwells, and through that one also with him in whom that dwells. How one? Not by a turning of nature, but by the unity of person; one person, not one essence. Therefore the divinity has been embodied in its own dwelling-place, into which it pours fullness, and to which it has joined truth.

*Wisdom has built herself a house, she has hewn out seven pillars [n: (Prov. IX)]*. Of what kind? *The Spirit of the Lord shall rest upon him: the spirit of wisdom and of understanding, the spirit of counsel and of fortitude, the spirit of knowledge and [0852A] of godliness; and the spirit of the fear of the Lord shall fill him [n: (Isa. XI)]*. Behold the seven pillars in the house of wisdom. She hewed out seven pillars. Whence did she hew them? From a mountain she hewed them; from what mountain? From the shady and thick-set mountain. From what mountain? From the mountain of the divine majesty she hewed out seven pillars. She hewed them out, but she did not cut them off. She took, but she did not take away. The pillars hewn from the mountain she set in the building of the house, and yet she did not diminish the mountain. And what wonder, if wisdom, to build her house, raised up pillars hewn from the mountain and not separated from the mountain — she who came to inhabit the house, and yet remained there whence she had come? Wisdom therefore built herself a house. What kind of house? That of which it has been said: In whom dwells all the fullness of the divinity bodily. And again: [0852B] *In Christ it has pleased that all the fullness of the divinity should dwell, and that through him all things should be reconciled, making peace unto himself through the blood of his cross, whether the things that are on earth or the things that are in heaven [n: (Coloss. I)]*. Do you see what we are doing? The Apostle testifies that the fullness is there; and we say that the whole of wisdom is not in her own house. Then half of her dwells within, and half without. And where shall she be sought, if she is not found at home? In whom dwells all the fullness of the divinity bodily. Where there is fullness nothing is lacking; where nothing is lacking, the whole is there. But perhaps the fullness of the divinity is there, and the fullness of wisdom is not.

But he says: *In whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge [n: (Coloss. II)]*. But you say, well. All the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are there; but [0852C] [d: All the treasures of wisdom and knowledge are there; but] they are hidden. He carries them and does not see them. But far be it from our understanding that we should hold such a view. Rather they are hidden, not that they should be hidden from him, but lest in him they be seen by the impious. They are hidden, that at the opportune time they may be brought forth. They are hidden, and they are laid up. Hidden, lest through him they be opened to the unworthy. Laid up, that in him they may be worthily kept. They are laid up, lest they be darkened by any rust of sins, lest they be polluted by any filth of crimes or contagion of vices. They are laid up as in an unspoiled vessel, as in a clean dwelling, lest perhaps anyone be able to bring a stain upon them, and then begin to bear reproach. *Seven women shall lay hold of one man in that day, saying: We will eat our own bread, and be covered with our own [0852D] garments; only let thy name be invoked upon us; take away our reproach [n: (Isa. IV)]*. Therefore they are laid up in him, but they are not hidden from him. *God,* he says, *no one has ever seen. The only-begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father, he has declared him [n: (I Joan. IV; Joan. I)]*; if he declared him, he also saw him. Whence do we prove this? *He who is of the earth speaks of the earth; he who comes from heaven is above all; and what he has seen and heard, this he testifies [n: (Joan. III)]*. Therefore he saw. In what did he see? In the divinity or in the humanity? Perhaps he saw in the divinity, and declared in the humanity. Those then who heard, in what did they see? Or if they did not see, why did they hear? He saw therefore in the divinity, he saw also in the humanity, yet are they from the divinity? and those things which they heard they could not see [0853A] except by the illumination of the divine light. There was therefore the fullness of the divinity in the soul of Christ; there was the fullness of wisdom also in the soul of Christ; and out of the fullness of wisdom the soul of Christ was fully wise. Therefore, they say, the soul of Christ has as much wisdom as God has. What is comparison doing? where there is only one? One is the wisdom of God, by which the soul of Christ is wise; nor is it wise by participating, so as to be wise about this or that in it and through it, but by having the fullness, so that it possesses the whole. Let us not say therefore "so much" or "how much"; but let us say that the whole wisdom of God is in the soul of Christ, and that out of the whole wisdom of God the soul of Christ is wise; and yet the soul of Christ is not equal to God, because it is not the wisdom of God. Further, it is one thing altogether to be wise by wisdom, and another to be wisdom; because he who is wise by [0853B] wisdom has received from elsewhere what he has; he who is wisdom has from his own self that by which he is wise. There grace, here nature; and the same is grace there which is nature here; and yet there grace does not change nature into immensity, nor here does nature confine grace to measure. He therefore who dares to say that the fullness of wisdom was not in the soul of Christ, let him say that the soul of Christ was not fully wise. The whole wisdom of God therefore was in the soul of Christ, because the whole wisdom of God had been united and embodied to the soul of Christ; and yet the soul of Christ was not equal to God. For it could not be compared to that nature, although it had received grace without measure, because together with immeasurable grace the nature was passible.
