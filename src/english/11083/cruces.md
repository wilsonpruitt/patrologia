# Cruces — 11083, Hugh of St Victor, *De sapientia animae Christi* (PL 176)

Garbled or doubtful type in Migne's plate, rendered literally and logged here.

- **col. 0846C (chunk 0000)** — `Nunquid nomina est veritas?` *nomina* (nom./acc.
  pl.) cannot stand as predicate of the singular *est*. The likely reading is
  *Nunquid non una est veritas?* ("Is truth not one?"), which the surrounding
  argument wants. Rendered from the printed text as "Is truth a matter of
  names?"; not emended.

- **col. 0846D (chunk 0000)** — `Sed narrant quinque somnia sua`. *quinque*
  ("five") is unmotivated; the expected reading is *quique* / *quisque* ("each
  one relates his own dreams"). Rendered literally as "their five dreams."

- **col. 0846D (chunk 0000)** — `jam non solum secundum morituram, sed exortam
  quoque a se sapientiam putant`. The allusion is Job XII, 2 (*vobiscum
  morietur sapientia*), so *secundum* stands for *secum*. Rendered from the
  sense ("will die with them"); the printed *secundum* is not emended in the
  Latin.

- **col. 0847A (chunk 0000)** — `In nobis quippe quod de rerum cognitione
  percipimus, et eo modo, cordis intellectus de iis quae extra sunt ad veritatem
  indicant, quo se interius animus in repraesentatione figurat.` The sentence has
  no finite main verb for its opening clause and the *eo modo … quo* correlation
  straddles a plural verb with a singular subject. Rendered clause-for-clause
  without repair; the English is as unresolved as the Latin.

- **col. 0847C (chunk 0000)** — the preface breaks off at `quam cor perversum`,
  with no verb (*habere*) supplied and **no final period**. Mirrored exactly: the
  English chunk also ends unpunctuated. Chunk 0001 opens with a new head, so
  nothing has been lost at our chunk boundary.

- **cols. 0845D → 0846C (chunk 0000)** — the column sequence jumps from 0845D
  straight to 0846C; there are no 0846A/0846B anchors in the TEI. Reproduced as
  transmitted.

- **col. 0848A (chunk 0001)** — `Ubi quantum non est, tantum dici quod potest?`
  The word order is disturbed (expected *quid potest dici tantum*). Rendered
  literally, "Where there is no 'how much,' what can be said to be 'so much'?"

- **col. 0848B (chunk 0001)** — `Nullus tamen sine sapentia sapiens esse potest`.
  *sapentia* for *sapientia*. The English renders the intended word.

- **col. 0849A (chunk 0001)** — `Lux lucet, et per se sciamus quod per se lucet`
  and, two lines later, `Nam ut erat, inquit, lux vera`. Both are suspect
  (*scilicet* for *sciamus*; *et* for *ut*). Rendered from the printed text
  without emendation.

- **col. 0852C (chunk 0002)** — **dittography.** The plate prints
  `Omnes thesauri sapientiae et scientiae ibi sunt; sed` twice, once immediately
  before the 0852C anchor and once immediately after it — a compositor's repeat
  across the column break. Reproduced verbatim in both Latin and English; not
  silently collapsed.

- **col. 0853A (chunk 0002)** — `Vidit ergo in divinitate, vidit et in
  humanitate, tamen ex divinitate sunt? et illi quae audierunt non nisi ex
  illustratione divini luminis videre potuerunt.` The interrogative clause
  *tamen ex divinitate sunt?* has no construable subject and the following *et
  illi quae audierunt* mixes masculine plural with neuter plural. Rendered
  literally; the incoherence is Migne's.

- **col. 0854A (chunk 0003)** — `Sicut non potest creatura aequali Creatori`.
  *aequali* for the infinitive *aequari*. Rendered from the intended reading
  ("cannot be made equal to").

- **col. 0855A (chunk 0003)** — `et quae termimum habet immensitatem`.
  *termimum* for *terminum*. English renders the intended word.

- **col. 0855A (chunk 0003)** — `Quibus verbis (fere) adjungendum esse putatis`.
  The parenthetical *(fere)* is Migne's, sitting oddly mid-clause; kept in the
  English as "(well-nigh)".

- **col. 0856B (chunk 0003)** — `Noluit igitur ille unionem solvere, ne nos
  praesumatur unitati comparare.` The impersonal passive *praesumatur* with an
  accusative-and-infinitive is irregular (expected *praesumamus*). Rendered "lest
  anyone presume to compare us to that unity."

- **col. 0856D (chunk 0003)** — `Hoc credenti qui non credit, non bene credit.`
  The dative *credenti* forces "he who does not believe one who believes this,"
  which is what the English gives; some may prefer *hoc credendum qui non
  credit.* Not emended.

## Not cruces — flagged for a human

- **col. 0848A (chunk 0001)** — `[n: (Psal. CXLVI)]` on *Magnus Dominus … et
  sapientiae ejus non est numerus*. This is Ps. 146 (147):5 in Vulgate
  numbering; correct as printed, noted only because modern readers will look for
  147.

- **col. 0848A (chunk 0001)** — `[n: (Prov. XXI)]` on *Non est sapientia, non est
  prudentia, non est consilium contra Deum* = Prov. XXI, 30. Correct.

- **col. 0848C (chunk 0001)** — `[n: (Eccli., XXIV)]` prints an internal comma
  after the abbreviation that the work's other notes do not use. Preserved
  verbatim.

- **col. 0852D (chunk 0002)** — `[n: (I Joan. IV; Joan. I)]` on *Deum nemo vidit
  unquam. Unigenitus Filius …* The compound note is Migne's own (1 John 4:12 for
  the first clause, John 1:18 for the second). Preserved verbatim; flagged
  because a naive indexer will read it as one locus.

- **col. 0854B (chunk 0003)** — the quotation tagged `[n: (ibid.)]` prints *sed
  Spiritum* **Christi**, with *Christi* set outside the italics. The Vulgate of
  1 Cor. II, 12 reads *sed Spiritum qui ex Deo est*; **Christi** is the reading
  Hugh's argument requires, and Migne's roman type appears to mark it as the
  author's substitution. Left exactly as printed, italic runs and all.

- **col. 0854B (chunk 0003)** — likewise `*Nobis autem revelavit Deus per
  Spiritum* sanctum?`, where *sanctum* falls outside the italic run. Rendered
  with the same split ("through the Spirit,* the holy one?"); an English reader
  will find the seam awkward, but the run boundary is Migne's.

- **col. 0856B (chunk 0003)** — `*Cujus non sum dignus ut solvam corrigiam
  calceamenti* ejus [n: (Joan. I)]` has *ejus* outside the italic run. The
  English preserves the split ("… the latchet of the shoe* of him"), which reads
  stiffly; a normalizing editor would move the italic close after *ejus.*
  Flagged rather than silently fixed.

- **heads (chunks 0001–0003)** — all three carry the same head, `SEQUITUR
  Decisio quaestionis …`, with `(cont.)` appended by the chunker on 0002 and
  0003. The `(cont.)` marker is passed through in the English heads unchanged;
  it is our apparatus, not Migne's, and a human should confirm the renderer
  suppresses it.

- **addressee** — the preface is directed only to `G.`; the initial is not
  expanded here.
