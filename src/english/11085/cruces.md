# Cruces — 11085 (Hugh of St Victor, *De virginitate B. Mariae*, PL 176)

## Col 0857A (chunk 0000) — head, and all six chapter-head repeats
- **Reading (as printed):** `CAP. I. Quod Maria in consensu conjugali virginitatis propositam non mutavit.` (chunk head; repeated with `(cont.)` in chunks 0002–0004)
- **Note:** `propositam` (fem. acc.) cannot agree with anything in the clause; the sense and the body of the chapter require *propositum* (neut., "purpose"), which is what Hugh writes throughout the text itself (*virginitatis propositum non mutavit*, cols 0858C, 0865C, 0870C). Per Pattern 9 a Latin gender/case mismatch has no English exponent — there is no way to render "propositam" differently from "propositum" — so the English head reads "the purpose of virginity" and the defect stays visible in the facing Latin column. No emendation made; flagged so a later sweep does not churn it.

## Col 0857A (chunk 0000)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Ego igitur (sicut placitum fuerat, quae super hoc dicenda videbantur) stylo signans legenda transmitto.`
- **Note:** Migne's parenthesis swallows the object of *signans … transmitto* (`quae super hoc dicenda videbantur`), so the sentence outside the brackets has no object. Punctuation follows the plate (Pattern 8): the parenthesis is reproduced where Migne prints it, and the English is left with the same syntactic hole — "I therefore (as had been agreed, the things which seemed to need saying upon this) am setting down with the pen and sending them to be read." The intended punctuation is almost certainly `(sicut placitum fuerat)` alone.

## Col 0858A (chunk 0001)
- **Reading (as printed):** `matrimonium non facit coitus, sed consensus` … `sed intelligendum putaveras, consensus coitus, additum fuisset`
- **Note:** Migne marks neither the quoted maxim nor the phrase *consensus coitus* typographically — no guillemets, no italics. Per Pattern 6, marks we supply because English needs them take straight quotes, so the maxim is rendered `"intercourse does not make a marriage, but consent."` *consensus coitus* is left unquoted and set off with dashes, since Hugh is naming the missing words rather than quoting a source. No guillemets supplied anywhere in this chunk (Latin twin has none).

## Col 0859B (chunk 0001)

- **Note:** An exclamation mark closes what is grammatically a statement completing the *Quid igitur restat nisi ut dicamus …* construction; a full stop is expected. Preserved as printed per Pattern 8.

## Col 0860A (chunk 0001)
- **Reading (as printed):** `ait Apostolus: Quae sub viro est mulier, vivente viro, *alligata est legi. Si autem* fuerit mortuus *vir ejus, liberata est a lege viri [n: (I Cor. VII)]*`
- **Note:** Migne's italic runs interleave with plain type mid-quotation (Rom. 7:2, cited as I Cor. VII — see below), and the plain run `fuerit mortuus` sits between the italic `Si autem` and the italic `vir ejus`. English word order cannot keep the subject after the verb, so `vir ejus` ("her husband") has been pulled forward out of the italic run: "*is bound to the law. But if* her husband be dead, *she is freed from the law of her husband*." Italic-run **count** (3) is preserved; the boundary of run 2/3 shifts by two words. No other change.

## Col 0860A (chunk 0001) — citation
- **Reading (as printed):** `[n: (I Cor. VII)]` attached to *alligata est legi … liberata est a lege viri*
- **Note:** The verse is Romans 7:2, not 1 Corinthians 7. Note contents are reproduced verbatim and untranslated per the marker rule; the misattribution is Migne's and is recorded here rather than corrected.

## Col 0861D–0862A (chunk 0002)
- **Reading (as printed):** `ut mihi respondeas an aut propter hoc, duo in carne una sociati sunt`
- **Note:** `an aut` is a doubled interrogative particle with no second limb — the disjunctive `aut` has no answering member anywhere in the sentence. Rendered literally ("whether or on this account they were allied…") rather than smoothed to "whether on this account"; per Pattern 7 the printed reading stands. Probably a compositor's doubling.

## Col 0863A (chunk 0002)
- **Reading (as printed):** `quanto magis quae neque fuit neque esse potuit, secundum carnalem commistionem non relinquet homo patrem suum et matrem suam`
- **Note:** `quae` has no antecedent and the relative clause `quae neque fuit neque esse potuit` sits unattached inside the comparative. Rendered as printed ("how much more that which neither was nor could be: according to carnal mingling a man does not leave…"). The argument requires something like *quanto magis [secundum illam] quae neque fuit neque esse potuit*. Not emended.

## Col 0863A (chunk 0002)
- **Reading (as printed):** `ut affectum uxoris illi praeferat; ut eum non solum sed singularem haberet?`
- **Note:** `non solum sed` without a following *etiam* or a balancing adverb; `solum` is here taken adjectivally with *eum* (= *affectum*), giving "not merely sole, but singular." An alternative reading takes `solum` as the adverb and supposes a dropped word after `sed`. Rendered on the first reading; the ambiguity is recorded, not resolved in the text.

## Col 0865A (chunk 0003)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Nequaquam ergo ex eo pensandum est conjugii sacramentum, in quo tunc sanctificaretur, sed exerceretur conjugalis castitas; et nunc sanctificaretur conjugium, sed excipitur conjugatorum infirmitas.`
- **Note:** Both limbs run `sanctificaretur … sed …` with no negative, which reverses Hugh's argument (his whole point is that the sacrament is *not* sanctified in the carnal office). A dropped `non` before each `sanctificaretur` is near-certain. Per Pattern 7 the printed reading is rendered as it stands ("in which then conjugal chastity would be sanctified, but exercised; and now marriage would be sanctified, but the infirmity of the married is allowed for"), producing English that asserts the opposite of the surrounding paragraph. Not emended.

## Col 0865D (chunk 0003)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Et ait Maria: Quomodo fiet istud: quoniam virum non cognosco?`
- **Note:** A colon stands where the sense (and Luke 1:34) requires a comma or nothing; the question mark then closes a clause introduced by a colon. Both marks preserved per Pattern 8, including the second occurrence a few lines below (`quomodo ergo fiet istud, ut filium pariam, et virum non cognoscam: Quia virum non cognosco.`), where a colon interrupts the sentence and is followed by a capital.

## Col 0866A (chunk 0003)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Tu vovistvirum te non cognituram`
- **Note:** `vovistvirum` is run-together type for *vovisti virum* ("you vowed that you would not know a man"). Per Patterns 7 and 10 the whole broken run is carried into the English untranslated and in italics — "You *vovistvirum* that you would not know" — which necessarily leaves the object of *cognituram* unexpressed in the English, since `virum` is inside the broken run. The intended reading is not in doubt.

## Col 0867A (chunk 0003)
- **Reading (as printed):** `cur ei postea dictum est: *Joseph, fili David, noli timere accipere Mariam conjugem tuam? [n: (Matth. I.)]*`
- **Note:** The question mark falls **inside** the italic scripture run and inside the quotation, although the question is Hugh's frame (*cur … dictum est*), not the angel's. Preserved exactly as printed (Pattern 8); no mark supplied outside the run.

## Col 0867C (chunk 0003)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Lex, inquit, dixit: « Maledicta sit sterilis, quae non parit; et quae non relinquit semen in Israel [n: (Deut. VII, 14; Exod. XXIII, 26)]. »`
- **Note:** The only guillemet pair in the work. Migne places the citation note **inside** the quotation, before the closing `»`, and leaves *inquit* outside the guillemets (it belongs to Hugh's frame, not the quoted Law). Both reproduced 1:1 per Patterns 5 and 6 — the note stays inside, *inquit* stays outside. Neither Deut. 7:14 nor Exod. 23:26 contains this wording; the "curse of barrenness" is Hugh's paraphrase of the Law, and Migne's note is an approximate locator. Note contents verbatim, untranslated.

## Col 0867D (chunk 0003)
- **Reading (as printed):** `At tamen audiamus, quod paret.`
- **Note:** `paret` (from *pareo*, "appears") gives "let us hear what appears," which is odd as a transition into the opponent's next objection; *parat* ("what he is preparing") or *pariat* would both fit better. `paret` is a real Latin form, so it is translated rather than carried through in italics (Pattern 7 / Pattern 9): "But let us nevertheless hear what appears."

## Col 0868D–0869A (chunk 0004)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Et quia se *in honore* esse *intellexit, non est* comparata mutis *insipientibus,* nec *similis* facta *est illis [n: (Psal. XLVIII)]*`
- **Note:** Hugh negates Ps. 48:13 and reapplies it to Mary, and Migne italicizes only the words that survive verbatim from the psalm, leaving Hugh's connective tissue in plain type — five italic runs interleaved with four plain ones. English word order will not carry the interleave word-for-word (`intellexit` must precede "in honor"). The **count** of italic runs (5) is preserved and each run holds its corresponding English words, but runs 1 and 2 exchange position: "she *understood* that she was *in honor,* she *was not* compared to the mute *senseless ones,* nor *was she made like to them*."

## Col 0869C (chunk 0004)
- **Reading (as printed):** `elegit in terra abjectio plebis fieri et opprobrium hominum`
- **Note:** `abjectio` is nominative where *fieri* after *elegit* requires the accusative *abjectionem* (Ps. 21:7, *opprobrium hominum et abjectio plebis*, is quoted with its own case intact). English has no exponent for the case (Pattern 9); rendered "chose to become on earth the abjection of the people and the reproach of men." Not emended.

## Col 0869C (chunk 0004)
- **Reading (as printed):** `maledictum legis in carne sua pro nobis suscipiens a maledicto legis liberavit`
- **Note:** `liberavit` is transitive and has no expressed object. English cannot leave the verb bare, so "[us]" is supplied in square brackets per the corpus convention for supplied words: "…set [us] free from the curse of the law." No Latin word is emended.

## Col 0870D (chunk 0005)
- **Reading (as printed):** `quomodo in elligi oporteat quod dictum est`
- **Note:** `in elligi` is split-and-doubled type for *intelligi* ("to be understood") — a word-division defect with a doubled *l*. Per Pattern 10 the whole broken run is carried into the English untranslated and in italics, spacing included: "…ought to be *in elligi*." The intended reading is not in doubt.

## Col 0871B (chunk 0005)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Quod enim concepit, hoc genuit?`
- **Note:** A question mark closes what the argument requires as a statement (it restates the premise established two sentences earlier, *quod concipit, hoc parit*). Preserved as printed per Pattern 8.

## Col 0872A (chunk 0005)
- **Reading (as printed):** `in altero quidem de carne viri sumitur, in altero autem per amorem viri monstratur`
- **Note:** `monstratur` ("is shown") against the expected *ministratur* ("is supplied") — the whole passage is about nature *supplying* substance (*naturam substantiam ministrare*, repeated three times in the surrounding lines). `monstratur` is a real word and is translated as printed ("is shown"), not emended.

## Col 0872A (chunk 0005)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Nam quia in corde, quo amor Spiritus sancti singulariter ardebat, ideo in carne ejus virtus Spiritus sancti mirabilia faciebat.`
- **Note:** `in corde` lacks the possessive (*in corde ejus*) that the answering `in carne ejus` has, and the `quia … ideo` correlation leaves the *quia*-clause without a verb of its own beyond the relative. Rendered as printed. The intended reading is *quia in corde ejus … ardebat*.

## Col 0872C (chunk 0005)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Sic etiam posse astrui, quod in concipiendo libidinem non sensisse perhibetur.`
- **Note:** A bare infinitive `posse astrui` with no governing verb — an anacoluthon carried over from the preceding *putamus*. Rendered with the minimum English needs ("Thus also it can be established that…"), which supplies a finite verb the Latin does not print; no Latin word emended.

## Col 0872D (chunk 0005) — head
- **Reading (as printed):** `CAP. III. Quod sine doloris et sine virginitatis detrimento Maria Christum peperit.`
- **Note:** `sine doloris … detrimento` ("without the detriment of pain") is a strained genitive — the body of the chapter says simply *sine dolore peperisse*. Rendered literally in the head ("without detriment of pain and without detriment of virginity") so that the parallelism Migne prints is visible.

## Col 0873D (chunk 0006)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Quid enim impedit ut vir virum, et femina feminam tali sibi pactionis foedere et societatis amore non astringat?`
- **Note:** *impedit ut … non astringat* is a double negative that inverts the objection Hugh is reporting (what hinders them from binding themselves, not from *not* binding themselves). Rendered as printed per Pattern 7 ("what hinders that a man should not bind a man to himself"); a dropped or intrusive `non` is the likely defect.

## Cols 0874A–0874B (chunk 0006)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Cogunt igitur, ut nos [n: (si assensum coitus in conjugii pactione necessarium omnino non esse volumus … proptereaque in eodem sexu (ut si fiat)] ita a conjugii sacramento foret aliena, quemadmodum ob id ipsum fiat illicita;`
- **Note:** The single largest defect in the work. Migne has bracketed a whole limb of Hugh's argument (roughly 60 words, running from *si assensum coitus* to *(ut si fiat)*) as an editorial note, and the bracket does not close where the note ends — the marker terminates with `]` immediately after `(ut si fiat)`, leaving the main sentence to resume at `ita a conjugii sacramento foret aliena` with no subject and no main verb for `Cogunt igitur, ut nos …`. Per the marker rule the note contents are reproduced **verbatim and untranslated**, exactly as printed, including the unbalanced parenthesis; the English resumes mid-sentence as the Latin does. The material inside the note is running argument, not apparatus — Wilson may want this one looked at against another edition.

- **RESOLVED 2026-07-28 (Wilson): it was never a note.** The `<note>` element is a **Corpus Corporum markup error**, not something Migne prints, so the marker rule never applied to it. Evidence: (1) the host sentence does not construe without the note's contents — `Cogunt igitur, ut nos … ita a conjugii sacramento foret aliena` leaves the `ut` clause with no verb, and the verb it needs, *demonstremus*, is **inside** the supposed note; apparatus cannot supply the main clause of the text it annotates. (2) The parentheses do not balance: the marked span opens `(si assensum` and closes `(ut si fiat)`, beginning and ending mid-construction. (3) The content is Hugh's own voice, continuous on both sides, and looks nothing like this work's actual notes, which are short parenthetical locators (`(Gen. II)`, two lines below).
  **Action:** patched at source, not in the chunk — `data/tei-patches/11085.json` drops the spurious `<note>` element and `chunk-work.mjs` applies it to the XML before the transform, so a re-harvest reproduces the fix (same mechanism as `data/calfa-patches/` on the PG side). **Migne's plate is not emended: not one word added, removed, or changed.** The ~60 words are now translated as the running text they are; chunk 0006 `noteCount` 7 → 6, `words` 1339 → 1338, and every chunk boundary in the work is unchanged (verified by diff against a pre-patch copy). This also removed a phantom `fontes` record — the indexer had been reading the passage as a cited source.

## Col 0876B (chunk 0006)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Sponsus quoque ipse uniti amoti amoris praerogativam commendans. *Sexaginta* inquit, *sunt reginae …*`
- **Note:** Two defects in one sentence. (1) `uniti amoti amoris` — `amoti` (perf. part. of *amoveo*, "removed") is a real Latin word but nonsensical here; the expected reading is *uniti amoris* ("of a united love"), with `amoti` an anticipatory doubling of `amoris`. Because it is a real word it is translated rather than carried in italics (Pattern 7): "the prerogative of a united, removed love." It is not marked `[d: …]`, since the run is not identical and so is not a clean dittography (Pattern 11). (2) A full stop closes `commendans`, leaving the participle without a main verb and stranding the Song quotation as a fragment. The stop is preserved per Pattern 8.

## Col 0876B (chunk 0006)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Propter sacramentum ergo dilectionis aeternae Deus conjugium sancire voluit propter utilitatem propagationis humanae sexum creavit`
- **Note:** Two independent clauses run together with no conjunction and no punctuation between `voluit` and `propter`. Punctuation follows the plate (Pattern 8): nothing is supplied, and the English runs on in the same way — "God willed to ratify marriage for the sake of the advantage of human propagation he created sex."

## Col 0876C (chunk 0006)
- **Reading (as printed):** `Non enim secus, sed natura disparem in alterutrum charitatem amori tribuit, et tamen sexus inaequalitatem naturae discernit.`
- **Note:** `secus` ("otherwise") gives no usable contrast with `sed natura`; the sentence immediately restates the previous one (*amor conjugalis non propter sexum dissimilem sacramentum est, sed propter naturam differentem*), which requires `sexus` — "for it is not **sex**, but nature, that bestows…". `secus` is a real word, so it is translated as printed ("For not otherwise, but nature, bestows…") and the conjecture is left here. This is the clearest single-word emendation candidate in the work.

## Whole work — quotation marks
- **Note:** Migne prints exactly one guillemet pair in 11085 (col 0867C, the Law's curse of barrenness), reproduced 1:1. Everywhere else — and this work is almost entirely quotation and re-quotation of Gen. 2:24, Luke 1, Ps. 48, Ps. 72 and the Song — Migne marks quotation by italics or by nothing at all, most often by nothing: the Genesis lemma *Relinquet homo patrem suum…* is italicized on first citation (0861C) and then repeated more than twenty times in plain type as Hugh dissects it. Per Pattern 6 no guillemets were supplied for any of these; the plain-type repetitions are left plain, and straight quotes were used sparingly and only where English genuinely needs a mark the plate does not print — six instances in all: col 0858A (the maxim *matrimonium non facit coitus, sed consensus*), cols 0862A–0862B (the phrase *propter hoc* twice, and the clauses *adhaerebit uxori suae* / *erunt duo in carne una* once each, where Hugh is naming them as phrases rather than quoting them), and col 0866D (the word *desponsatio* named as a word). Guillemet counts match the Latin twin 1:1 in all seven chunks.
