## LETTER III. TO JOHN, ARCHBISHOP OF SEVILLE. *That the Christian faith is to be held not only in the heart, but also confessed with the mouth.*

To JOHN, archbishop of Seville, HUGH the servant of the cross of Christ.

What, dearest brother, what shall I say to you? If we begin to speak to you, perhaps you will take it ill. That man was strong and great, and a despiser of all torments, *and no one spoke a word to him, because they saw that his grief was very great [n: (Job II)]*. How, then, shall we be able to speak to you in the vehemence of so great a grief? If yet you grieve as much as you ought [1014C] to grieve [ *al.,* tu dolendus es]? what shall we do for you? Shall we hold back the conceived speech which our heart and our soul — not (as in those men) from impatience of speaking, but from vehemence of grieving — has now begun to bring forth? Shall charity, then, be able to keep silent, so as not to burst forth and cry out in its griefs, and in the anguish of its tribulation? For now the sword has reached even to the soul, and fraudulent death has come, sparing the flesh, that it may extinguish the spirit. You know, brother, what I wish? The cause of your soul is at issue. See what you do. Christ sets against you his death, the Christian his redemption. He seeks the purchased, this one the redeemed. He complains that he gave himself as a price for you in vain; this one laments the redeemed lost. But you say: I know my own conscience. Let no one [1014D] terrify me: it is not the tongue that makes the Christian, but the conscience. I love Christ: it suffices me. He seeks nothing more. What is chiefly mine, I have given to him. He has the heart; let him possess it. I will say to man whatever he pleases. The Lord himself knows that I deny unwillingly. The tongue says this, not the conscience. With the mouth indeed I deny, but with the heart I confess. Hear, brother. Scripture says: *With the heart one believes unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation [n: (Rom. XVI)]*. How, then, do you think to have salvation, if you do not have confession? Do you deny Christ and say that you have the Holy Spirit? What, then, is it that the Apostle says: *No one speaking in the Spirit of God says anathema to Jesus? [n: (I Cor. XII.)]* If you say anathema to Jesus, how do you have the Spirit of Christ? But if [1015A] you do not have the Spirit of Christ, you are not Christ's. *He who has not,* says Scripture, *the Spirit of Christ, he is none of his [n: (Rom. VIII)]*. Hear again: *He who,* he says, *has been ashamed of me and of my words, of him shall the Son of man be ashamed, when he shall come in his majesty [n: (Luc. IX)]*. But you say well: He who denies Christ out of shame is justly damned, is justly not acknowledged by Christ. For it is a small thing this — to prefer bashfulness to Christ. I have more that I may put forward in excuse. For I am not ashamed, but afraid. Greater is that which terrifies me. He himself knows that I am not able to suffer. He spares, then, my weakness, he condescends to my devotion. He attends not to the voice, but looks to the charity. *No one ever hated his own flesh [n: (Ephes. V)]*. I fear for my flesh, which I cannot hate. O deceitful deception! [1015B] Will you, then, love the flesh, and blaspheme the Creator? What, then, does that mean which he says: *He who loves his soul more than me is not worthy of me [n: (Luc. XIV)]*. If you cannot rightly love the soul more, can you the flesh? But you say: I do not love the flesh more: for I love our God more than my flesh. Let us now see. What you love more, this you rather choose. Your God says: *Fear not those who kill the body, but have nothing they can do to the soul [n: (Matth. X)]*. This, then, God says; this the flesh gainsays; see now. What you love more, this you rather choose. O what a shepherd! How would you lay down your life for your sheep [n: (Joan. X)], you who do not lay down your life even for your own soul? You are unwilling to give your flesh for your soul, and would you give your life for your sheep? O what a shepherd! [1015C] Not so did that Good Shepherd, who laid down his life for his sheep, and deigned to die for his flock. What does it seem to you? If he had feared death as you do, what sheep, do you think, would yet have been freed from death? He thought to choose a good shepherd for his sheep, one who, when the wolf came, would not flee, nor, hiding in a covert of trembling, conceal himself, setting aside the peril of the sheep. The wolf came, he could not seize the sheep; the shepherd he not only seized, but dragged away. A wondrous thing! The sheep dares, and the shepherd trembles. O what a shepherd! If you were to be such, why did you take up the care of the sheep? If you received the dignity, why do you not expend the goodness: *The good shepherd lays down his life for his sheep [n: (ibid.)]*. But you say: Peter denied with his mouth; yet, because he did not deny with his heart, the Lord looked upon him, and [1015D] he was called by his own name to the joy of the resurrection with the other apostles. This, as I hear, is the greatest thing that you put forward in your excuse. The Lord, then, looked upon Peter denying. Why? that he might make him confessing: first weeping, afterward confessing. He looked to compunction, he called to confession. If, then, you have the regard of Christ, where are the tears? But if you weep, why do you not confess? If you follow Peter denying, why do you not imitate him confessing? Lastly, brother, if you are a Christian, where is the sign of your King? I see an alien mark upon your forehead. Do you know what shall be over those who bear the mark of the beast? The servants of our God are sealed in their foreheads, and they cannot [1016A] escape the sentence of extermination, save only those who in the figure of the Tau are fortified with the seal of the cross of Christ. The cross on the breast, faith in the heart; the cross on the forehead, confession in the mouth: both are owed, both are required. Christ claims the whole for himself. The heart for faith in him, the mouth for confession of him. But the tormentor stands by, the drawn sword threatens. Hear: *He who loves his soul shall lose it; and he who shall have lost his soul in this world for my sake shall keep it unto life eternal [n: (Marc. VIII)]*. He who loses for my sake shall receive from me. I will keep what is entrusted, that I may the better restore it. Do not tremble at all. *A hair of* your *head shall not perish [n: (Luc. XXI)]*. Why are you anxious for the soul, you who have received security even for a hair? But perhaps you will say to me that many today within the bosom of the Church [1016B] live in the faith and confession of Christ who, if they were so interrogated, would in no way confess Christ. To which I answer you with the prophetic voice, that *the judgments* of the Lord *are a great deep [n: (Psal. XXXV)]*. We cannot search out the depth of the judgments of God, and penetrate his unsearchable ways. *I will have mercy,* he says, *on whom I have had mercy; and I will show mercy to whom I shall be merciful [n: (Rom. IX; Exod. XXXIII; Rom. IX)]*. If, then, it shall be pleasing in his eyes to spare certain of his little ones, whom mother Church either bears conceived or nurses when brought forth, and not to permit these graver interrogations of temptations to come upon them, who are you to say to him: *Why do you do so [n: (Job IX)]*. It does not befit your excellence to reckon yourself to be counted in the number of such. Among the great [1016C] servants you had received your place, not as a little one, but as guardian and provider of the little ones. To you, therefore, as to one great and strong, and fit for the first or nearest attainment, Jesus said: *Follow me [n: (Joan. XI)]*. You, then, having obtained the place next after himself, he admonished to follow him, so that you afterward might be able to say confidently to your followers, to whom you ought to have furnished a leading unto life: *Be imitators of me, as I also of Christ [n: (I Cor. XI)]*. If, then, the Lord willed to call you, as a great and faithful servant, to the glory of his imitation, see what kind of thing this is, that you, looking to his little ones who meanwhile are to be cherished, not pressed down, should say: *Lord, but what of these? [n: (Joan. XXI.)]* This he said whom you think to imitate — though nevertheless you ought not in all things — not understanding [1016D] the dignity of that calling, nor rightly regarding the mercy of the divine dispensation. And therefore he heard the sentence of just rebuke, that he might learn to hearken, not to judge. *So,* he says, *I will that he remain, until I come. What is that to you? Do you follow me [n: (ibid.)]*. This, then, brother, is the word which you ought most intently and most diligently to hear, that you may follow your King and attain him. Follow to the penalty, attain to the glory. If it must be suffered for Christ, you have no excuse. *The sufferings of this time are not worthy of the future glory that shall be revealed in us [n: (Rom. VIII)]*. But if even without suffering you deny, I not only say that you are not excused, but I say further that you are accused. Wretch, where is your soul, that you do not consider [1017A] what you have become? what glory you have lost, and into what misery and confusion you have fallen? Look upon yourself, what you are: where is your crown and glory? You have forsaken your King: every nation follows its own gods: *And surely these are not gods [n: (Jer. II)]*. You have forsaken your Lord and your God, and have become base, the reproach of the worst men. O what a shepherd [1018A] of Christians! How will you feed the sheep of Christ, you who have destroyed your very self? The sheep to be fed have been given to a wolf-to-be. O what a shepherd! Blush, wretch, and be confounded! The eyes of Christians are upon you. From afar you are seen. You cannot lie hidden. Your error has made you known. You cannot escape confusion, unless you show forth confession.
