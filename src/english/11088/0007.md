## CHAPTER VI. ON THE OUTWARD AND THE INWARD HABIT (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ From which words it is plain that, if we fix the eye upon a woman, we are abominable to our God, whom we ought to have pleased. ⟦s2⟧ And from him from whom we ought to have received glory, [0901A] we shall receive punishment. ⟦s3⟧ Wherefore it is necessary that, if divine love does not separate us from our vices, at least fear may restrain us; and if the joys on high do not delight us, at least the eternal punishments may terrify us. ⟦s4⟧ So does a man begin to withdraw from sin, if he fears to displease his Maker.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ If someone says: How shall I fear God? ⟦s2⟧ If thou wilt fear God, consider that he sees all things. ⟦s3⟧ An evil conscience tickles thee: consider that God sees thee. ⟦s4⟧ Whatever evil there shall be in thee, weigh it that God beholds it all. ⟦s5⟧ Whatever we do, or say, or turn over in the mind, let us always attend to the eyes of God above us; let us always fear to displease him. ⟦s6⟧ This is the way of justice, these are the first beginnings of wisdom, as it is written: *The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord [n: (Eccli. I)]*. [0901B] There follows: When therefore you are together in church, or wherever women are, guard your chastity toward one another.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Great is the virtue of chastity, and much commended in the divine Scriptures. ⟦s2⟧ This virtue is heavenly and spiritual. ⟦s3⟧ For it lifts a man from the earth, makes him the companion of angels, joins him to God, makes him heavenly from earthly, spiritual from carnal. ⟦s4⟧ This virtue transcends even human nature. ⟦s5⟧ For that a man is chaste, that he is pure, no human virtue so much brings about as divine. ⟦s6⟧ Through this the invisible bridegroom loves us. ⟦s7⟧ For a soul does not please God if it is not pure, if it is not chaste. ⟦s8⟧ For if it serves uncleanness and lust, it is abominable to him and reprobate. ⟦s9⟧ The virtue of chastity is acceptable to God and to men. ⟦s10⟧ This the ancient enemy of the human race envies us, [0901C] this he ceases not to assail. ⟦s11⟧ For he himself is the persuader of uncleanness and lust; he corrupts very many, he spares not clerics, he indulges not monks, and — what is the more to be groaned over — he lays low even the religious themselves.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is necessary therefore with the highest vigilance to guard our chastity toward one another. ⟦s2⟧ For if we lose it, we lose [sic: *totuin*], even our very selves. ⟦s3⟧ He who loses chastity loses his soul, loses God, loses himself also. ⟦s4⟧ But to this end we dwell together in one, that we may guard one another, correct one another, be instructed by one another, so that what each cannot do through himself he may be able to do through another. ⟦s5⟧ And because our guarding is not from ourselves but from God, it is well added.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0901D] *For God, who dwells in you, will in this way also guard you from among yourselves.*

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For our own guarding is not sufficient for us unless the divine guarding be present also. ⟦s2⟧ Whence it is written: *Unless the Lord shall guard [n: (Psal. CXXVI)]*, etc. ⟦s3⟧ There is no one who can guard chastity, either in himself or in another, save through God. ⟦s4⟧ But if he himself shall dwell in us, we shall be able through him what we cannot through ourselves. ⟦s5⟧ So then let us be zealous to live in such a way that he may deign to dwell — we in him and he in us — that we may be able to be just and chaste through him, who lives and reigns God through all ages of ages. ⟦s6⟧ Amen.
