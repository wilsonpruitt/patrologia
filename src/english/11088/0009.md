## CHAPTER VIII. ON THE KEEPING OF COMMON THINGS.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0905A]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Have your clothes in one place under one keeper, or two, or as many as may suffice to shake them out lest they be damaged by the moth. ⟦s2⟧ And as you are fed from one storeroom, so let you be clothed from one clothes-room.*

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ That he commands the clothes to be laid up under one keeper or several, there seem to be two reasons. ⟦s2⟧ The first, namely, lest, neglected through carelessness, they be ruined by moths. ⟦s3⟧ The second, lest a brother should believe that he has as his own this very garment which he has, if it were permitted him to lay it up for himself in a place of his own. ⟦s4⟧ And with this sentence the words that follow seem to agree; for he says:

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And if it can be done, let it not concern you what clothing is brought out to you according to the fitness of the seasons: [0905B] whether each one receive that which he had laid aside, or another which another had had; provided only that to each one what he needs be not denied.*

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ He says, if it can be done, for this reason: that if it seem hard to anyone, he may be allowed to take back the very garment which he had laid aside. ⟦s2⟧ For it would belong to a greater perfection if anyone had come to so great a contempt of himself as to be content with that alone which the body needs, according to what the Apostle says: *Having food and clothing, we ought to be content with these [n: (I Tim. VI)]*. ⟦s3⟧ He does not determine how much or of what kind, but only marks out those things which suffice for nature. ⟦s4⟧ For in the imperfect, pleasure always seeks more than necessity does. ⟦s5⟧ And as the blessed Augustine says, *Human nature is content with little, but custom has added many things; will and [0905C] pleasure have joined on more.* ⟦s6⟧ If anyone loved God perfectly, if he wholly renounced carnal desires, he would cut away many superfluous things which he now holds as though necessary. ⟦s7⟧ Let us learn, therefore, to love God with the whole heart, so that through love of him we may be eager both to be content with what is necessary and to cut away what is superfluous, so that, while what is needed is denied to no one, nothing beyond what is needed be sought by anyone. ⟦s8⟧ Charity seeks not the things that are her own; charity sets common things first; charity makes a man to be abject in the house of God. ⟦s9⟧ If anyone has perfectly possessed her, he will cut away from himself even out of what will be needful.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But if from this contentions and murmurings arise among you, when someone complains that he has received worse than he had before, and that he is unworthy to be clothed [0905D] as another brother of his was clothed, then from this test yourselves — how much you lack in that inward holy habit of the heart, you who quarrel over the habit of the body.*

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The habit of your heart is sanctified by the grace of God, by the indwelling of the Holy Spirit, when there is within peace, charity, goodness, humility, patience, concord, meekness, and other things of this kind. ⟦s2⟧ These are our inward riches, namely good character and the virtues. ⟦s3⟧ But if we begin to contend, to murmur and to quarrel among ourselves, at once we are left empty of these spiritual goods. ⟦s4⟧ For virtues cannot remain together with vices. ⟦s5⟧ For *a little leaven corrupts the whole lump [n: (I Cor. V)]*. ⟦s6⟧ From this each one ought to test himself, and carefully to weigh how much he lacks within, [0906A] and how great a loss he incurs who, for the sake of passing things, loses that whereby he ought to be blessed. ⟦s7⟧ Quarrels and contentions, therefore, are greatly to be guarded against by us, because these are works of the flesh; and, as the Apostle says, *They who do such things shall not obtain the kingdom of God [n: (Galat. V)]*. ⟦s8⟧ Therefore he admonishes us in another place, saying: *Nothing through contention or vainglory; esteeming one another as superiors, each one considering not the things that are his own, but the things that are another's [n: (Philipp. II)]*. ⟦s9⟧ For these two things avail very much to remove quarrels and to beget concord: when one judges himself lower than all, and weighs the things that are another's more than his own. ⟦s10⟧ For in these two, humility and charity are commended; and these are wont not only not to fall into discord, but even to bring those who are in discord back to concord [0906B]. ⟦s11⟧ And because the things that have been said pertain to the more perfect, he tempers the sentence for the sake of the less perfect when he adds:

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Yet if your weakness is borne with, so that you receive what you have laid aside, still keep in one place, under common keepers, what you put away.*

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Namely that, while the very thing which is taken for one's own use is laid up in a common place, we may believe it to be not our own but common.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In such wise, indeed, that no one work anything for himself, but that all your works be done in common with greater zeal and more frequent eagerness than if you were making your own things for yourselves singly.*

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And thereupon he sets down the apostolic sentence and expounds it, saying:

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For charity, of which it is written, That it « does not [0906C] seek the things that are its own, » is thus understood: that it sets common things before its own, not its own before the common.*

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ This is the reason why no one should work for himself, but that things be done in common with greater zeal and more frequent eagerness than if they were his own. ⟦s2⟧ This is the reason, I say, because *charity seeks not the things that are its own, but sets common things before its own.* ⟦s3⟧ If the charity of Christ seeks not the things that are its own, none of us ought to work for himself And if the charity of Christ sets common things before its own, then with greater zeal and more frequent eagerness we ought to do the common things than if they were our own. ⟦s4⟧ For the fruit of charity is greater than that of ownership. ⟦s5⟧ For *what eye has not seen nor ear heard, this God has prepared for them that love him [n: (I Cor. II)]*.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And therefore, the more you care for the common thing than for your own [0906D], the more you may know that you are advancing, so that in all the things which passing necessity uses, that charity which abides may stand out above them.*

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For passing necessity uses temporal things and temporal pursuits, in which charity ought always to stand out, because no one can please God without charity. ⟦s2⟧ In all things, therefore, that we do, charity must always stand out, because she is the mother of the virtues, she is the root of all good things. ⟦s3⟧ This is the loftier way by which men press toward heaven, of which the apostle Paul says: *I show unto you yet a more excellent way [n: (I Cor. XII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ He advances much in this charity who for the love of God despises all things that are of this world, seeks to retain nothing for himself, [0907A] and divides in common whatever he has. ⟦s5⟧ Truly he walks by the lofty way who, treading earthly things underfoot, is raised in mind and desire to the heavenly. ⟦s6⟧ Charity consists in the love of God and of neighbor. ⟦s7⟧ But the love of God is expressed in three ways, so that nothing may remain in man which is not made subject to divine love. ⟦s8⟧ For we are commanded to love God *with the whole heart,* namely that we refer all our thoughts to God; *with the whole mind [n: (Deut. VI)]*, that we hold toward him all the reason by which we understand and discern; *with the whole soul [n: (ibid.)]*, that we direct all the affections of the soul to God. ⟦s9⟧ In the love of neighbor two things are to be kept. ⟦s10⟧ *That what you do not wish to be done to you, you should not do to others [n: (Tob. IV)]*. ⟦s11⟧ And *what you wish men to do to you, do you also to them [n: (Matth. VII)]*. ⟦s12⟧ From this twofold power of love toward [0907B] the neighbor all the virtues arise, by which we both usefully desire the things that are to be sought and avoid the things that are to be avoided. ⟦s13⟧ By these two articles the neighbor is fittingly loved, while he is both cherished with benefits and injured by no malice. ⟦s14⟧ In all things, therefore, that passing necessity uses, charity ought always to stand out. ⟦s15⟧ Because whatever we do or speak ought to look to the charity of God or of neighbor, so that in all our manner of life the sum of our intention may always turn upon charity. ⟦s16⟧ *And on these* moreover *two commandments hangeth the whole law and the prophets [n: (Matth. XXII)]*.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It follows therefore that also, when someone has bestowed some garment, or anything else to be reckoned among necessaries, upon his own sons or upon any who belong to him by some necessity and are established in the monastery, it be not received secretly, [0907C] but be in the power of the prior, so that, being brought into the common stock, it may be supplied to him who has need of it.*

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For in the whole of life it is lawful to receive nothing secretly, nor for anything to be so received, nor for anything to be retained as one's own; but if anything is given to anyone, it must be handed over to the prior, so that it may be supplied to him who has need of it. ⟦s2⟧ Thus is avarice extinguished, thus is superfluity cut away. ⟦s3⟧ Thus is charity nourished and holy poverty kept, because, while it is not lawful to retain earthly things, the mind is compelled to think on heavenly ones. ⟦s4⟧ But there are some who are gravely troubled when the things they receive from others are not permitted to be retained by them. ⟦s5⟧ They love little gifts; they take delight in things of their own. ⟦s6⟧ And often over small and worthless things they burn with so great a desire that, on occasion of them, they are wont to disturb the concord of the brethren. ⟦s7⟧ And that this happens by diabolical persuasion there is no [0907D] doubt. ⟦s8⟧ For the crafty enemy knows the characters and qualities of all, and discerns their affections, and tempts each one in that in which he knows himself to prevail the more. ⟦s9⟧ Nor does he care whether he deceives by a great thing or a small, so long as he may be able to corrupt the charity of a brother. ⟦s10⟧ We ought to be cautious and watchful against the crafty enemy, and to guard beforehand against his snares in every matter of things, and to tread down by strength of mind all earthly things by which he is able to deceive, that we may be able to sing with the Prophet: *The snare is broken, and we are delivered [n: (Psal. CXXIII)]*. ⟦s11⟧ But because some are caught in this, therefore it is added:

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But if anyone conceals a thing bestowed upon him, let him be condemned by the judgment for theft.*

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0908A]The Apostle says: *Thieves and robbers shall not possess the kingdom of God [n: (I Cor. VI)]*. ⟦s2⟧ For theft is a criminal sin, as it is written: *Thou shalt not commit adultery, thou shalt not steal, thou shalt not bear false witness,* etc. [n: (Exod. XX.)] Since therefore it is reckoned among criminal evils according to the sentence of the canons, everyone ought so to judge concerning theft as concerning adultery, as concerning homicide, as concerning false witness. ⟦s3⟧ The brother who conceals a thing bestowed upon him holds in the holy Church the place of Judas the traitor, who was a thief and a robber and had the purse, and carried off and hid the things that were sent to the Lord. ⟦s4⟧ He blazed with so great a vice of cupidity and avarice that he sold and betrayed his own Lord, as the devil had put it into his heart. ⟦s5⟧ But in seeking gain he found death, as it is written: *While [0908B] he thirsts for gain he comes to the [sic: loqueum].* ⟦s6⟧ Such are the fruits of avarice. ⟦s7⟧ Let the brethren therefore take care not to seek gains, not to hide anything whatever, lest, while at the devil's prompting they perpetrate so detestable a fault, they incur by the just judgment of almighty God the penalty of death and everlasting damnation.
