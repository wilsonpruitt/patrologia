## CHAPTER IX. ON THE CLEANNESS OF CLOTHES AND OF BODIES.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let your garments be washed according to the judgment of the prior, whether by yourselves or by fullers, lest too great an appetite for clean clothing contract an inward filth of soul.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Why he has ordered the garments to be washed according to the judgment of the prior, he himself sets out the reason: namely, lest [0908C] a brother contract filth in his mind if he desires beyond measure to have clean clothes; for each one is polluted within in the same measure as he is cleansed without for vainglory. ⟦s2⟧ Prelates ought therefore to take care that their subjects do not go astray through the sheen of clothing. ⟦s3⟧ For it is not the sheen of clothes but uprightness of character that adorns a cleric. ⟦s4⟧ Whence the Apostle admonishes us, saying: *Be not conformed to this world, but be reformed in the newness of your mind [n: (Rom. XII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ For those who seek the form of this world do not seek the form of the inner man. ⟦s6⟧ And the more outward beauty is loved, the less is the inward cared for. ⟦s7⟧ But this is the true and spiritual cleanness which God requires in us, saying: *Wash yourselves, be clean; take away the evil of your thoughts from my eyes [n: (Isa. I)]*. ⟦s8⟧ For he does not say: Blessed are the clean [0908D] in body, but: *Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God [n: (Matth. V)]*. ⟦s9⟧ And lest anyone should think that care of the flesh must likewise be cut away, he adds:

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let the bathing of the body also by no means be denied when the necessity of sickness compels it. ⟦s2⟧ But let it be done without murmuring, on the counsel of medicine; so that even if he be unwilling, he do at the prior's command what must be done for health. ⟦s3⟧ But if he wishes it and perhaps it is not expedient, let his own desire not be obeyed.*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ This sentence seems to agree with the words of the Apostle, which forbid care of the flesh to be taken in its desires, not in necessity. ⟦s2⟧ For there is a great difference between necessity and desire. ⟦s3⟧ For what is from necessity is useful; but what is from desire is a vice. [0909A] But there are some who say that bodily medicine does not befit religion. ⟦s4⟧ But if they attended diligently, they would themselves recognize that this — that we eat and drink, that we cover ourselves with clothes and use earthly things for necessity — is medicine of the body.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let it be done, therefore, without murmuring, on the counsel of medicine.*

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And well does he say: Let the unwilling be compelled in necessity, let the willing be restrained from desire, because necessity, not desire, is to be obeyed. ⟦s2⟧ For sometimes, even if it harms, what pleases is believed to profit. ⟦s3⟧ See why a man ought not to obey his own desire: because he is often so deceived by his own desire that he believes that to profit him which harms him. ⟦s4⟧ So does evil desire deceive men; so does it pretend that to be good which is evil and [0909B] pernicious. ⟦s5⟧ See why the Holy Scriptures so condemn desire and carnal delight: because they draw men to death while the men themselves know it not.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Sometimes, even if it harms, what pleases is believed to profit.*

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But according to the Apostle's word we must abstain from carnal desires, which war against the soul, because the flesh indeed lusts against the spirit. ⟦s2⟧ But by the discipline of the spirit the concupiscence of the flesh must be restrained. ⟦s3⟧ But there are some who pursue beyond measure the rigor of the spirit; others, the softness of the flesh. ⟦s4⟧ The former, while they seek to live spiritually, sometimes extinguish the flesh along with the vices. ⟦s5⟧ But [0909C] it must be known that virtue, if it has no measure, is turned into vice. ⟦s6⟧ But those who wish to live carnally are eager, together with care of the flesh, to nourish their own desires also. ⟦s7⟧ A measure must therefore be set for both, and the former must be compelled to do what must be done for health. ⟦s8⟧ But these latter must be restrained, lest they do what they carnally desire. ⟦s9⟧ The health of the body indeed is to be preserved for serving God; but the desire of the flesh is to be extinguished. ⟦s10⟧ And because he has spoken of manifest sickness, he adds concerning hidden pain, saying:

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Finally, if the pain is hidden, let the servant of God who tells what pains him be believed without hesitation.*

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For rightly is he to be believed whom good character and a holy manner of life commend as a servant of God. [0909D] For truth or falsehood is not known from the mouth of those who speak, but by their fruits you shall know them, says the Lord. ⟦s2⟧ They are not deceitful, they are not double; they are not of those who in heart and mouth have spoken false things. ⟦s3⟧ The servants of God are in mouth such as they are in heart. ⟦s4⟧ A good life gives credit to words. ⟦s5⟧ An evil life makes even truth itself to be doubtful. ⟦s6⟧ Let those, therefore, be believed without hesitation who without hesitation are good. ⟦s7⟧ In another way: If the pain is hidden, let him be believed without hesitation. ⟦s8⟧ For the servant of God fears to offend God not only in deed but also in speech. ⟦s9⟧ We ought to show ourselves such that whatever we say may be believed without hesitation. ⟦s10⟧ Life ought to commend speech, and good works to bear witness to the truth, [0910A] as our Saviour says of himself: *If you do not believe me; believe the works. ⟦s11⟧ The works,* he says, *which I do, they bear witness of me [n: (Joan. X)]*. ⟦s12⟧ For there are many who are not believed even if they speak the truth, because they are evil, or are held under suspicion of being evil. ⟦s13⟧ But if our life has begun to be good without suspicion, presently our words will be believed without hesitation to be true. ⟦s14⟧ The less good anyone is, the less is he believed; the more religious he has begun to be, the greater credit is granted to his words. ⟦s15⟧ But because the necessity of sickness sometimes seeks the things that are contrary to itself, therefore he adds:

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But yet, whether what pleases be expedient for the healing of that pain, if it is not certain, let a physician be consulted.*

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0910B]Their words are to be believed, yet their desires are not at once to be complied with; for unlawful desires tempt even the servants of God; therefore, if it is not certain, let a physician be consulted. ⟦s2⟧ Let him be sought who may make certain out of the uncertain. ⟦s3⟧ Nor is it a wonder if we seek the counsels of physicians, since we read that the apostle Paul made use of the counsel of medicine. ⟦s4⟧ For through his Epistle he enjoins his disciple Timothy, saying: *Use a little wine for thy stomach's sake and thy frequent infirmities [n: (I Tim. V)]*. ⟦s5⟧ The discerning master therefore tempers his disciple away from the rigor of abstinence — namely, lest, broken by frequent sufferings, he grow faint from the good of preaching. ⟦s6⟧ For it was good that the disciple should render the fruit of patience out of his infirmity; but the Apostle foresaw it to be more useful if, growing somewhat stronger [0910C] by the use of medicine, he should gain souls for God. ⟦s7⟧ But out of diverse goods the more useful are to be chosen. ⟦s8⟧ Supported by this authority, we ought to seek bodily medicine to this end, that from it we may render a greater fruit to the Lord.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And let them not go to the baths, or wherever it shall be necessary to go, fewer than two or three.*

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let us provide good things,* says the Apostle, *not only before God, but also before all men [n: (II Cor. VIII)]*. ⟦s2⟧ For our own sake a good conscience suffices us; for the sake of others our reputation ought not to be polluted, but to prevail in us. ⟦s3⟧ He who, trusting in his own conscience, neglects his reputation is cruel — most of all one set in this place, of which the Apostle says, writing to his disciple: *In all things show thyself an example of good works* [0910D] [n: (Tit. II)]. ⟦s4⟧ Again elsewhere he says: *We are made a spectacle to the world, and to angels, and to men. ⟦s5⟧ Those who love us seek what they may praise in us; but those who hate us disparage us. ⟦s6⟧ And we, set in the midst between the two, with the help of our Lord Jesus Christ, ought so to guard both our life and our reputation that we may not blush to be praised by our detractors.* ⟦s7⟧ Two or three, therefore, ought to go together, both for comfort and for the testimony of good repute. ⟦s8⟧ Whence Solomon says: *Woe to him that is alone; if he fall, he has none to lift him up; if there be two, they shall keep each other warm. [n: (Eccle. IV)]*. ⟦s9⟧ And well does he say, he ought to go with those whom the prior shall have commanded — namely, that by the prelate the safety of the person may be provided for, and by the subject the virtue of obedience be kept.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0911A] *The care of the sick, whether of those to be restored after sickness, or of those laboring under some weakness or even under fevers, ought to be laid upon someone, so that he himself may ask from the storeroom what he has foreseen to be needful for each one.*

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ A brother fearing God is to be chosen, who may bear the greatest care about the sick, and be eager to minister to them what shall be needful for them, and serve them with such zeal and affection as if he were serving the Lord Jesus Christ himself. ⟦s2⟧ For he himself will say at the judgment. ⟦s3⟧ *I was sick, and you visited me [n: (Matth. XXV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And again: *What you have done to one of the least of mine, you have done to me [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s5⟧ The stronger, therefore, ought to bear the weakness of the infirm. ⟦s6⟧ For thus is divine charity fulfilled, the Apostle bearing witness, who says: *Bear ye one another's burdens, and so you shall fulfil the law of Christ [n: (Galat. VI)]*. ⟦s7⟧ The law of Christ is love. [0911B] The office of love is to bear one another's burdens. ⟦s8⟧ And diverse times and diverse infirmities bring it about that we bear one another's burdens. ⟦s9⟧ But nothing so proves a friend as the bearing of a friend's burden. ⟦s10⟧ But the sick man himself, who is served for the love of God, ought to weigh it and to take the utmost care lest by his own excess or impatience he grieve the one who serves him. ⟦s11⟧ But let him be eager to give thanks to God, lest the infirmity which was given him for the purging of the soul be turned, by fault, to an increase

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And whether those who are set over the clothes or those who are set over the books, let them serve their brethren without murmuring.*

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Let them show themselves such, and serve their brethren with such charity, that they may not themselves sin by murmuring in the ministry committed to them, nor by their negligence make others [0911C] murmur. ⟦s2⟧ Murmuring is a grave sin, Gregory bearing witness, who says: *No one who murmurs receives the kingdom of heaven; no one who receives it will be able to murmur.* ⟦s3⟧ And Solomon: *The heart of a fool is like a cart wheel [n: (Eccli. XXXIII)]*, it carries hay and murmurs. ⟦s4⟧ Such, then, are those in the congregation who, being subject in the flesh, do not cease from murmuring. ⟦s5⟧ The Apostle: *Neither murmur ye, as some of them murmured, and by* serpents *perished [n: (I Cor. X)]*. ⟦s6⟧ Why by serpents, unless because the serpent is venomous, and everyone who murmurs has the venom of the devil on his tongue. ⟦s7⟧ James.: *The tongue is a restless evil, full of deadly venom [n: (Jac. III)]*. ⟦s8⟧ *We must beware,* therefore, *of murmuring, lest we perish by the deadly and diabolical [0911D] venom.*

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let the books be asked for at a fixed hour; whoever shall ask outside the hour, let him not receive.*
