## CHAPTER XI. ON OBEDIENCE.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let the prior be obeyed as a Father, much more the priest who bears the care of you all.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Obedience is the one virtue that engrafts the other virtues in the mind and guards them once engrafted. ⟦s2⟧ Samuel: *Better [0920A] is obedience than a victim [n: (I Reg. XV)]*; because by victims another's flesh, but by obedience one's own will, is bound. ⟦s3⟧ Solomon: *An obedient man* shall speak of victories [n: (Prov. XXI)], because while we humbly submit to another's voice we overcome ourselves in the heart. ⟦s4⟧ For obedience is enjoined upon us to be kept even unto death. ⟦s5⟧ Through obedience evil is never to be done; but sometimes even the good that is being done ought to be broken off through obedience. ⟦s6⟧ But because sometimes adverse things are not even commanded, it must most especially be known that obedience is sometimes nothing if it has anything of its own, and sometimes is least if it has not something of its own. ⟦s7⟧ For when success in this world is enjoined, when a higher place is commanded, he who pants out of his own desire to obtain these things empties for himself the virtue of obedience [0920B]. ⟦s8⟧ Again, when contempt of the world is enjoined, when reproaches and insults are commanded to be sought, unless the mind desires these of itself it lessens for itself the merit of obedience, because it descends unwilling and reluctant to the things that are despised in this life. ⟦s9⟧ Obedience, therefore, ought both in adversity to have something of its own, and again in prosperity to have nothing at all of its own: so that in adversity it may be the more glorious the more it is joined to the divine ordinance even by desire, and in prosperity the more lowly the more it is utterly separated in mind from the present glory which it receives from God. ⟦s10⟧ But to harmless minds the ornament of obedience is always joined, the Lord bearing witness, who says: *My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me [n: (Joan. X)]*. [0920C] Assuredly he who is harmless obeys the Redeemer; and he cannot be harmless who scorns to obey.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *That all these things, therefore, may be kept, and that, if anything shall have been less kept, it be not negligently passed over but cared for as a thing to be amended and corrected, this will pertain chiefly to the prior: that he refer to the priest, whose authority among you is the greater, what exceeds his own measure or his strength.*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For to this end have these precepts of the rule been given, that all things be kept; and if anything has been less kept, it must be quickly amended. ⟦s2⟧ For we cannot negligently pass over anything without peril to our soul. ⟦s3⟧ For indeed we have vowed to hold them especially, and we have sworn to live according to them. ⟦s4⟧ And the Psalmist says: *Thou hast commanded thy commandments to be kept exceedingly [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And [0920D] the apostle James: *But if anyone shall keep the whole law, yet offend in one point, he is become guilty of all [n: (Jac. II)]*. ⟦s6⟧ It will pertain, therefore, to the prior, and to him who is keeper of the order, that he refer to the priest whose authority is the greater — that is, to the abbot — whatever he cannot determine by himself according to the canonical rule. ⟦s7⟧ But if in a congregation where there is the canonical order there is no abbot but a bishop, that bishop himself is designated by the name of priest. ⟦s8⟧ For to this end have prelates been appointed in the Church: that they keep what is well ordered, correct what has gone wrong, and by word and example compose the life and morals of their subjects, following the pattern of the Apostle, who says: *Reprove, entreat, rebuke [n: (I Tim. IV)]*. ⟦s9⟧ Mingling times with times, blandishments with terrors, [0921A] they ought to reprove the undisciplined and the restless more harshly, and to entreat the obedient, the mild and the patient, that they may advance to better things. ⟦s10⟧ The negligent, the contemptuous and the proud let them either rebuke or punish. ⟦s11⟧ Nor let them dissemble the sins of offenders, but as soon as these have begun to arise let them cut them off by the roots, so far as they prevail, mindful of the peril of Heli the priest concerning his sons. ⟦s12⟧ Let them rebuke the more honorable and the more intelligent by a first and a second admonition; let them restrain the wicked and hard of heart, the proud and disobedient, with the lash and with chastisement, calling this back to memory: A fool is not corrected by a word; strike thy son with a rod, and thou shalt deliver his soul from death [n: (Prov. XXIII)].

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But let him who presides over you not think himself happy in a lordly power, but in a serving charity.*

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0921B]For those who so preside as to rejoice in the power of lordship are not true shepherds but hirelings; these seek rather to be over others than to profit them; they seek not the fruit of souls but temporal gain, not heavenly glory but earthly honor. ⟦s2⟧ Of such the Lord says in the Gospel: *Amen I say to you, they have received their reward [n: (Matth. VI)]*. ⟦s3⟧ But those who so preside as to rejoice in the servitude of charity — these hold the example of the true shepherd, who came not to be ministered unto but to minister, and to give his life a redemption for many. ⟦s4⟧ Those truly are prelates and exercise the care of rule according to God who labor for this only, to draw many with themselves to God — men whom the burden delights and not the honor, charity and not power, service spent upon others and not service received from others. [0921C] According to what the Apostle says: *Whereas I was free in all things, I made myself the servant of all, that I might gain all [n: (I Cor. IX)]*. ⟦s5⟧ Yet he must be honored by his subjects on account of the authority of his rule. ⟦s6⟧ Whence it is also added:

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In honor before you let him be set over you.*

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For this reason, namely, he ought to be honored: because he holds the voice of Christ in the monastery. ⟦s2⟧ Whence the Lord himself also says to shepherds: *He who hears you hears me, and he who spurns you spurns me [n: (Luc. X)]*. ⟦s3⟧ The servants of God, therefore, ought to honor their master, whose mastery over themselves they have taken up for the love of God. ⟦s4⟧ Yet the more he is honored outwardly, the more he must fear, and, despising himself, set before himself in thought those by whom the honor is paid him. ⟦s5⟧ Whence it is subjoined:

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0921D] *In fear before God let him be laid beneath your feet.*

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Hence it is written: *The greater thou art, humble thyself in all things [n: (Eccli. III)]*. ⟦s2⟧ And again: Son, they have made thee a leader, *be not lifted up,* but *be among* them *as one of* them [n: (Eccli. XXXII)]. ⟦s3⟧ And the Lord in the Gospel: *Everyone who exalts himself shall be humbled, and he who humbles himself shall be exalted [n: (Luc. XVIII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ For each one is the greater with God the humbler he has been with himself. ⟦s5⟧ But let us hear what he ought to be in his character.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In all things let him show himself an example of good works.*

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For as he excels in honor and in dignity and in rank, so he ought to go before them in holiness, that by his example his subjects may learn how they ought to live.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0922A] *Let him rebuke the restless, console the fainthearted, receive the weak, be patient toward all.*

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ He who holds the place of rule ought not to inflict evils but to bear them, that out of his own meekness he may be able to soften the wrathful. ⟦s2⟧ Because the physician of souls ought to set the medicines of the virtues against the passions of the vices — namely so that he first diligently inquire into the characters of all, that he may recognize what he ought to apply to each.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let him hold discipline willingly, and, being one to be feared, impose it. ⟦s2⟧ And although both are necessary, yet let him seek rather to be loved by you than to be feared, always thinking that he will render an account to God for you.*

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The ecclesiastical teacher must be wise and disciplined, because he then rightly imposes discipline upon others when he himself lives in discipline, when he draws from himself [0922B] the mastery which he exercises upon another. ⟦s2⟧ These are the words of the Apostle saying to the Hebrews: *Persevere in discipline; God offers himself to you as to sons. ⟦s3⟧ And what son is there whom his father does not correct? [n: (Hebr. XII.)]* But these two are necessary for the ruler to show to his subjects: namely fear and love. ⟦s4⟧ Fear to those who are proud, by this rebellious, and of hard mind. ⟦s5⟧ But love to those who willingly, humbly and devoutly serve the Lord God.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And although both are necessary, yet let him seek rather to be loved by you than to be feared.*

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Because the more a prelate is loved, the more he is heard. ⟦s2⟧ And that service pleases God more which is done out of will and love than that which is done out of fear. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore the sweet master says to his disciples: *Now [0922C] I will not call you servants, but friends [n: (Joan. XV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ For the Lord does not wish that we fear him as a Lord, but wishes that we love him as sons love a father, and that we do his precepts not from fear of punishment but from love of justice. ⟦s5⟧ But he who is judge of souls ought always to think on this, that he has God as judge over himself, so that he may exercise the care of rule the more solicitously the more strictly he weighs that he will render an account for those committed to him before the strict Judge. ⟦s6⟧ For each one ought to be greatly solicitous in good work, so that in all that he does he may always think of the last end. ⟦s7⟧ Whence it is written: *In all thy works remember thy last end, and thou shalt never sin [n: (Eccli. VII)]*.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Wherefore, by obeying the more, have mercy not only on yourselves but also [0922D] on him, who among you is engaged in the greater peril in proportion as he is in the higher place.*

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ In great peril is he engaged, whoever is raised up to the place of rule. ⟦s2⟧ For the higher the step, the more perilous the fall; and from him to whom more is committed, more is required. ⟦s3⟧ It is a great peril to live for oneself and for others at once, to provide for temporal and for spiritual things together. ⟦s4⟧ He ought so to take care of himself as not to neglect others; so to think of others as not, by forgetting, to abandon himself; so to see to temporal things as not to grow cold toward the spiritual. ⟦s5⟧ So to burn toward spiritual things as to be solicitous about the temporal. ⟦s6⟧ Subjects, therefore, ought to obey their prelate the more from that wherein they see him labor the more for them. ⟦s7⟧ And when they do this, by obeying the more they show mercy not on themselves, but also [0923A] on him. ⟦s8⟧ On themselves, namely, because they save their own souls. ⟦s9⟧ For it is written: *Have mercy on thy own soul, pleasing God [n: (Eccli. XXX)]*. ⟦s10⟧ They show mercy also on him who presides, because by obeying they lighten his burden. ⟦s11⟧ For since it is written: *A wise son maketh a father glad [n: (Prov. X)]*, spiritual Fathers rejoice the more and labor the more freely for their sons, the more they see them comply with spiritual disciplines.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *May the Lord grant that you observe all these things as lovers of spiritual beauty, and fragrant with the good odor of Christ from a good manner of life, not as servants under the law, but as sons established under grace.*

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Fittingly is prayer added after the reading. ⟦s2⟧ For to read and to preach is as it were to sow a field; [0923B] to pray, as it were to cover the seed. ⟦s3⟧ As those who sow in a field, if they neglect to cover it, the birds come and snatch away all that seemed to have been sown, so indeed do those who shall have scattered in the field of their own heart the seeds of the word. ⟦s4⟧ Of God, by psalming, by reading, by preaching, unless afterwards by praying they have shut them up in the heart and in a manner buried them, the birds come — that is, the flighty, empty and vain thoughts of this world — and they snatch away what has been sown in the heart. ⟦s5⟧ Therefore after reading and preaching one must always pray, that the power of the word of God may remain in the heart, and spiritual fruit grow in work. ⟦s6⟧ And well does he say:

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As lovers of spiritual beauty.*

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For they cannot fulfil all these precepts of the rule except through the love of justice, which is the spiritual [0923C] beauty of the soul. ⟦s2⟧ For no one acts well unwillingly, even if what he does is good. ⟦s3⟧ But as to what he adds:

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And fragrant with the good odor of Christ from a good manner of life.*
