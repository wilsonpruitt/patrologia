## CHAPTER XI. ON OBEDIENCE. (cont.)

This pertains to a good life and a good reputation. Whence the Apostle: *We are the good odor of Christ unto God in every place [n: (II Cor. II)]*. They are servants under the law who serve the Lord out of fear; free men under grace, who fulfil the divine commandments out of love. *The grace of God is poured forth in our hearts by the Holy Spirit who is given to us [n: (Rom. V)]*: it fulfils the law: *The law is not laid down for the just man [n: (I Tim. I)]*; *for where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty [n: (II Cor. III)]*. And thus shall we run freely the way of the commandments of God, if we have our heart enlarged in the charity [0923D] of God.

## CHAPTER XII. ON THE CONTINUAL MEDITATION OF THE RULE.

*And that you may be able to look into this little book as into a mirror, [0924A] lest through forgetfulness you neglect anything, let it be read to you once in the week.*

Therefore it is commanded to be read, that it may be held often by memory. For the blessed James said: *He who has looked into the law of perfect liberty and has remained in it, not become a forgetful hearer but a doer of the work, he shall be blessed in his deed [n: (Jac. I)]*. And well does he call this little book a mirror; because in it, as in a mirror, we can look upon what sort we are — whether fair or foul, whether just or unjust; whether any one of us lives according to rule; whether he advances or falls short; whether he pleases God or displeases him. For Holy Scripture represents to us our inward image; it shows what is shapely, what is misshapen in the soul, and how the beauty of justice ought to be kept, how [0924B] the comeliness of the virtues ought to be composed, how the stain of vices ought to be wiped away. It is the practice of the depraved always to cover their evils, and never to review what they have done. Whatever they do, they pass over with a blind mind. But on the contrary holy men do not cease daily to look upon their life in the Holy Scriptures, and to weigh their own conduct watchfully. And if they find anything misshapen or uncomposed or disordered, at once, according to what they see written, they are eager to reform and compose themselves, that they may be found holy and unspotted before God as much in body as in soul. Therefore the Psalmist says: *Then shall I not be confounded, when I shall have looked into all thy commandments [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*. And the Apostle admonishes, saying: *Be renewed in the spirit of your mind [n: (Ephes IV)]*. And again: *Our inward man is renewed from day to [0924C] day [n: (II Cor. IV)]*. Whence it is openly subjoined:

*And where you find yourselves doing the things that are written, give thanks to God, the bestower of all good things.*

Thanksgivings are always owed by us to the Father *of lights,* from whom *every best giving and every perfect gift [n: (Jac. I)]*. Scripture being witness, comes down. For no one is blessed by the gifts of God who stands ungrateful toward the giver. And the Apostle: *What hast thou that thou hast not received? [n: (I Cor. IV.)]* Deservedly, therefore, are we admonished to give thanks to the Lord our God, so that a man may acknowledge, if anyone lives well, that he has it by the grace of God, and that, for progress to be made in the love of justice, he will obtain it from nowhere else. Which thought makes a man pious. For this is the wisdom which is called piety, by which the Father of lights is worshipped. And he is worshipped with the sacrifice of praise and with thanksgivings, so that [0924D] his worshipper may glory not in himself but in the Lord.

*But where any one of you sees that something is lacking to him, let him grieve for the past, beware for the future, praying that his debt be forgiven him, and that he be not led into temptation.*
