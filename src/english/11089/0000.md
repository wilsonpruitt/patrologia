## CHAP. I.-- *On the three precepts of the former tablet.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0009A]

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Hear, O Israel: thy God, God is one; him shalt thou adore, and him alone shalt thou serve [n: (Deut. VI)]*. ⟦s2⟧ Not the whole of what is said belongs to the precept, because before the precept the mind of the hearer is prepared for obedience by admonition. ⟦s3⟧ *Hear, O Israel.* ⟦s4⟧ Here mark obedience. ⟦s5⟧ *Thy God,* here mark grace. ⟦s6⟧ *God is one,* here doctrine; as though it were said: Show obedience, understand grace, acknowledge truth. ⟦s7⟧ That you hear — this is the obedience which is required of you. ⟦s8⟧ That he is your God is the grace of the one who goes before. ⟦s9⟧ That God is one is the truth by which you are illumined and perfected. ⟦s10⟧ Hear your God, who has already shown mercy to you, and yet has not yet received service from you, so that you may know that from him is both — namely, both that he requires of you the work of virtue, and that he [0009B] promises you the reward of recompense. ⟦s11⟧ *Hear, O Israel,* that is, hearken, acquiesce. ⟦s12⟧ For every virtue begins from obedience, just as every vice proceeds from disobedience. ⟦s13⟧ For in vain would grace be given to the disobedient, or doctrine to the stubborn, or a precept to one who does not understand. ⟦s14⟧ Therefore first *hear, O Israel,* that you may be obedient; *thy God,* that you may be devout; *God is one,* that you may acknowledge the truth. ⟦s15⟧ Then follows the precept: *Him shalt thou adore,* this pertains to faith. ⟦s16⟧ *And him alone shalt thou serve,* this pertains to work. ⟦s17⟧ For faith working through love makes the perfect man, because neither work without faith, nor faith — for which there is time enough — is acceptable without work. ⟦s18⟧ Therefore adore by believing rightly, serve by working well. ⟦s19⟧ But it may be asked whether what he said, *him alone,* is to be referred [0009C] to both, so that it be said, Him alone shalt thou adore and him alone shalt thou serve? ⟦s20⟧ But how will this be able to stand, since we often read in the Old Testament that holy men adored not only [0010A] angels, but even men, and yet we nowhere find them reproved on that account? ⟦s21⟧ And in the New Testament the Apostle indeed most earnestly exhorts even the faithful to serve their superiors and prelates. ⟦s22⟧ It must therefore be known that men are adored in one way, and God is commanded to be adored in another way: for by ancient usage any superiors whatever were said to be adored by their inferiors, when reverence was shown to them by these, whether in the bending of the knees or in the inclination of the head. ⟦s23⟧ But to adore God is to lay down the whole mind beneath him through humility and devotion, and to believe him the beginning and end of all good. ⟦s24⟧ That we owe this to him alone, there is no one of sound mind who would doubt. ⟦s25⟧ Whence he himself also testifies concerning himself through the prophet, saying: *My glory I will not give to another: [0010B] nor my praise to graven images [n: (Isa. XLII)]*. ⟦s26⟧ But concerning service, the same thing can fittingly be understood, since undoubtedly every man is most rightly said to serve God alone who both, in accordance with God, humbly subjects himself to a superior man, and, where the cause of God is assailed, confidently gainsays it; who carefully takes heed lest in his service he either do anything against God for fear of man, or set the gain of earthly recompense before the heavenly reward in his intention. ⟦s27⟧ But in the Greek the meaning is expressed more plainly. ⟦s28⟧ For the Greek diction distinguishes divine service from human by its own word, which the Latin tongue does not have: for there the service of God is called *latria,* and human service *dulia.* ⟦s29⟧ And on this account those were called idolaters who rendered to idols the service which they owed to God. ⟦s30⟧ But the Latin tongue embraces both [0010C] services under the same name, and therefore the signification has become more obscure when it is said, *Him alone shalt thou serve,* which in the Greek is not [0011A] ambiguous. ⟦s31⟧ But since this precept pertains to the Father, why is it said, *Him alone shalt thou serve,* as though one ought not to serve also the Son and the Holy Spirit? ⟦s32⟧ But what is said, *alone,* does not exclude the communion of the Son and of the Holy Spirit: but whatever is not God. ⟦s33⟧ Now the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit is one God, and he alone is to be adored, and to him alone, and to no other, is service to be rendered — namely, with that service which is owed to him alone. ⟦s34⟧ For also in the Gospel, when it is said, *No one knows the Father, but the Son [n: (Matth. XV)]*, etc., the Holy Spirit is surely not excluded from the knowledge of the Father, since he too searches the deep things of God, as the Apostle testifies [n: (I Cor. II)]. ⟦s35⟧ But it is a manner of speaking of this kind, so that it is said, no one but he — namely, no one who is not that which he is.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0011B]These things having been passed over for brevity's sake, let us pass on to the discussing of the following commandment, which is this: *Thou shalt not take the name of thy God in vain [n: (Exod. XX)]*. ⟦s2⟧ This, according to the simple sense of the letter, ought to be understood thus: that a man take not the name of God in vain, that is, either for confirming a lie or for venerating an idol — namely, that he neither honor idols with the divine name, nor associate the name of God with falsehood. ⟦s3⟧ But mystically, to take the name of God in vain is to reckon the Son of God, made visible through his humanity, to be a creature. ⟦s4⟧ For he takes the name of God in vain who believes that the eternal Son of God began in time. ⟦s5⟧ For the Son is called the name of the Father, because in the Son, and through the Son, the invisible Father is made manifest — not only when through him the world was made, but [0011C] also when through him man was redeemed. ⟦s6⟧ Surely those who confess the Son of God made man in no way take the name of God in vain, because him whom they believe to be man through that which he assumed in time, they acknowledge to be true God through that which he was from eternity. ⟦s7⟧ It can still be understood in another way, what was said: *Thou shalt not take the name of thy God in vain.* ⟦s8⟧ For they take the name of God in vain who despise the Son of God, sent to them and incarnate for their salvation. ⟦s9⟧ What then is it to say, *Thou shalt not take the name of thy God in vain,* except: As you venerate and adore him because you were made by him, so adore him and venerate him because you were redeemed by him, who conferred upon you no less when he redeemed than when he created? ⟦s10⟧ To the same sense pertains [0011D] what the Son himself says in the Gospel to the disciples, and through the disciples to all who believe in him: *You believe in God, believe also in me [n: (Joan. XIV)]*: admonishing us indeed with a modest suggestion (as befitted the merciful one), that we may understand that he is to be adored by us not only in his divinity, by which he created, but also in his humanity, by which he redeemed.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The third precept is: *Observe the day of the Sabbath [n: (Deut. V)]*. ⟦s2⟧ Scripture seems to commemorate four Sabbaths. ⟦s3⟧ The first, that in which God is said to have rested, his works being finished. ⟦s4⟧ The second, that which is commanded to the children of Israel to be kept carnally. ⟦s5⟧ The third, that which is enjoined upon the people of God to be observed spiritually. ⟦s6⟧ The fourth, that which in the promise [0012A] God pledges to those who love him, a Sabbath for a Sabbath. ⟦s7⟧ There are therefore two Sabbaths outwardly, one of God and one of man; and two inwardly, one of God and one of man. ⟦s8⟧ The Sabbath of God, that in which outwardly he is said to have ceased from work, is the sacrament of that inner Sabbath of his, in which his eternal immortality and immortal eternity, enjoying a perpetual and unshaken blessedness, is never wearied by any labor. ⟦s9⟧ The Sabbath of man, which is enjoined upon him to be observed outwardly and visibly, is the sacrament of the inner Sabbath, where the holy mind, resting through a good conscience from the servitude of sin, is gladdened in the joy of the Holy Spirit. ⟦s10⟧ Whoever in the present life shall so keep this Sabbath as to consent to no evils, shall come in the life to come to that inner and eternal [0012B] Sabbath of God, where he shall feel no evils, as it is said: *And there shall be a Sabbath for a Sabbath, a month for a month.* ⟦s11⟧ And so this precept seems to be referred specially to the person of the Holy Spirit, when it is said: *Observe the Sabbath,* namely, that in the present we may receive him and keep him within us unto the joy of a good conscience, so that in the future we may deserve to receive and possess him unto the joy of eternal life. ⟦s12⟧ These, then, are the three precepts of the first tablet, in which especially the love of God is commended, and the whole Trinity, one God, is enjoined to be equally adored and worshiped.
