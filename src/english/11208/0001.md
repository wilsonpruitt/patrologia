## Flowers, or Sentences, from St. Bernard (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Gladly will I go without any spiritual gain, however great, which cannot be acquired except with scandal. [f: *Epist.* 82.]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ It is a fair and wholesome order that you should first carry yourself the burden which you lay on another to carry. [f: *Epist. 221, n.* 3.]

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The power of the voice is the deed; but prayer earns grace and effectiveness both for the deed and for the voice. [f: *Ibid.*]

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ A false Catholic does more harm than a true heretic. [f: *Serm. 65 in Cant., n.* 4.]

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ It is no unprofitable exchange to have left all things for the sake of him who is [1202B] above all things. [f: *Tract. de cont. mun. ad cler., c.* 1.]

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The coveting of the world does more harm than its substance. [f: *Ibid.*]

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The reason for fleeing riches is that they can scarcely, or never, be possessed without love of them. [f: *Ibid.*]

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The human heart easily clings to everything it frequents. [f: *Ibid.*]

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ He who purposes to leave all things should remember to number himself also among the things to be left. [f: *Ibid., n.* 2.]

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ True love has its reward — but that reward is the thing loved. [f: *Tract. de dilig. Deo, n.* 17.]

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ If one of the people goes astray, he perishes alone; the error of a prince involves many. [f: *Epist.* 117.]

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ True joy is that which is conceived not from the creature, but [1202C] from the Creator. [f: *Epist.* 114.]

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The vicious man does not shun the consciences of the vicious. [f: I, *De consid., cap.* 10.]

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Where all are filthy, the stench of one is not noticed. [f: *Ibid.*]

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ To those who dwell on them, honors are alluring; to those who weigh them, they are a thing of dread. [f: *Tract. de off. et mor. episc., cap.* 7.]

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ True charity is not empty of reward, and yet it is not mercenary. [f: *Tract. de dilig. Deo, n.* 17.]

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ He keeps a good stronghold who guards his own body. [f: *Serm. 2 de Assumpt., num* 2.] *A common proverb.*

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ Misery alone is free from envy. [f: *Serm 5 de verb. Isa.*]

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Cursed is he who makes his own portion worse. [1202D] [f: Lib. I *De consid. cap. 5, et serm. de septem panib.*]

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ He gives his hand to the seducer who makes a pretense of giving it to his teacher. [f: *Serm. 77 in Cant.*]

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ He who lets the sheep go into the pastures without a keeper is a shepherd not of sheep but of wolves. [f: *Ibid., num.* 6.]

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The life of the poor is sown in the streets of the rich. [f: IV, *De cons., cap.* 2.]

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Ambition, the cross of the ambitious, tormenting all, pleases all. [f: III, *De consid., cap.* 1.]

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Trifles in the mouth of a layman are trifles; in the mouth of a priest they are blasphemies. [f: Lib. II, *De consid., cap.* 15.]

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ A vain heart forces the mark of vanity upon the body. [f: *Apol. ad Guil., cap.* 9.]

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Soft garments indicate softness of mind. [f: *Ibid.*]

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [1203A]The Church shines in her walls, and goes cold in her poor. [f: *Apol. ad Guil., cap.* 11.]

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *1573* At the expense of the needy, the eyes of the rich are served. [f: *Ibid.*]

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ I fear the teeth of the wolf more than the rod of the shepherd. [f: *Tract. de vita et off. episc., n.* 35.]

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Hippocrates teaches men to save their souls in this world; Christ, to lose them. [f: *Serm. 35 in Cant.*]

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The pleasure of the throat, which today is reckoned at so high a price, scarcely takes up the breadth of two fingers. [f: *De conv. ad cler., n.* 13.]

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The insatiable love of riches torments far more by desire than it cools by use. [f: *Ibid.,* 14.]

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The use of riches falls to others, generally; to the rich falls only the name and the anxiety. [f: *Ibid.*]

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [1203B]The demon lies in wait chiefly for perseverance alone, which alone of the virtues he knows to be crowned. [f: *Epist.* 24.]

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ A guard upon the mouth is always useful — provided it does not shut out the grace of affability. [f: IV, *De consid., cap. 6, n.* 23.]

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The headlong tongue is to be bridled everywhere, but most of all at a banquet. [f: *Ibid.*]

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ That [sic: *babitus*] is more fitting, if you are severe indeed in act, serene in countenance, and grave in word. [f: *Ibid.*]

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ There is no need of ladders for one who already holds the throne. [f: V, *De consid., n.* 1.]

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ To disparage, or to listen to one disparaging — which of these is the more damnable, I could not easily say. [f: II, *De consid., n.* 22.]

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Deliberating foresight holds judgment in suspense. ⟦s2⟧ *Serm.* [1203C] [f: *de S. Magdal., n.* 1,] now in volume V.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Religious sorrow mourns either another's sin or its own. [f: *Ibid., n.* 2.]

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [1204A]Learn from those things which he was made for you, how highly God has valued you. [f: *Serm. 1 de Epiph.*]

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *1574* It is the utmost madness that we are unashamed toward obscene things, and blush at penitence: wrongly prone to wounds, and bashful toward remedies. [f: *Serm. in Circumc.*]

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ For the sake of preserving humility, the divine mercy is wont so to order things that the more a man advances, the less he reckons himself to have advanced. [f: *Serm. 15 de div., n.* 4.]

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Both are pleasing to God: the sinner stricken with compunction, and the just man devout; but the ungrateful just man is as displeasing as the sinner untroubled. [f: *Serm. de div.,* 4.]

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The healthy man does not know what the sick man feels, nor the full man what the fasting man suffers. [f: *Tract. de grad. hum., n.* 6.]

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Humility is a glorious thing — with which pride itself also [1204B] seeks to cloak itself, that it may not become cheap. [f: *Ibid., n.* 47.]

⟦p48⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Unhappy is that victory by which, overcoming a man, you succumb to a vice. [f: *Exhor. ad mil., n.* 2.]

⟦p49⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ The impious walk round about, naturally seeking that by which they may end their appetite, and foolishly spurning that by which they might draw near to the end. [f: *De dilig. Deo, n.* 19.]

⟦p50⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The soul that loves God seeks no reward of its love other than God; or if it seeks something else, it loves that, not God. [f: *Ibid., n.* 17.]

⟦p51⟧ ⟦s1⟧ By natural desire everyone seeks the highest good. ⟦s2⟧ Do you wish to arrive? ⟦s3+4⟧ Begin to leap across. [f: *Tract. de cont. mun. ad cler., n.* 33.] *For if you are entangled in the lowest things, you will not arrive.*

⟦p52⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ The way is winding, and cannot be walked; you will arrive more easily by despising all things than by obtaining them. [f: *Ibid.,* 33.]

⟦p53⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ [1204C]A wretched exchange, and full of the utmost madness: to shun human labor, and to choose everlasting gnashing with the devil. [f: *Ibid.,* 27.]
