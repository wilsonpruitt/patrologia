## 5.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They* therefore *all slumbered and slept. ⟦s2⟧ And at midnight there was a cry made: Behold, the bridegroom cometh! go ye forth to meet him.* ⟦s3⟧ O cry sweeter to the prudent virgins than every melody, than all measures of music! ⟦s4⟧ *Behold, the bridegroom cometh!* ⟦s5⟧ Do you suppose this is the voice of the Son of God, which, when that blessed hour has come, all who are in the tombs shall hear, and they who have heard shall live? [n: (Joan. V, 25.)] Lord Jesus Christ, Son of the living God, [1046C] grant in that hour joy and gladness to my hearing, and the bones that have been humbled shall exult. ⟦s6⟧ Let me not fear then, I beseech thee, Lord, at the evil hearing which they shall hear to whom thou art to say: *Go, ye cursed, into everlasting fire [n: (Matth. XXV, 41)]*. ⟦s7⟧ Perhaps this will be the voice of that trumpeting archangel, at whose voice and at the last trumpet the Lord shall come down from heaven [n: (I Thess. IV, 15)]. ⟦s8⟧ For the trumpet shall sound, at whose blast — to use Jerome's words — the whole world shall fear. ⟦s9⟧ The crash of that trumpet, then, while the angel trumpets, while men are waking, shall shake even the shadowy nether regions. ⟦s10⟧ The wretched who are there shall hear, who are held in that prison-house; then they shall hear who now hear and refuse to do: *Go ye forth to meet him.* ⟦s11⟧ A great cry indeed: since, as the Sibyl says, when the trumpet [1046D] sends forth its sad sound from the height of the world, and the Lord shall call heaven from above and the earth, wishing to sift his people before him, and to separate the prudent virgins from the foolish; he shall call heaven from above, so that those powers of the heavens may be moved from their seats, and all the Angels may be gathered with him for the judging. ⟦s12⟧ The Psalmist: *He shall call heaven from above [n: (Psal. XLIX, 4)]*; that is, the spirits and souls of the just, to whom it had before been said that they should rest yet a little time, until the number of their brethren should be fulfilled [n: (Apoc. VI, 11)]. ⟦s13⟧ Then, that heavenly dwelling being left behind, those holy souls, restored to their own bodies, shall rejoice the more safely as they are the more [1047A] incorrupt. ⟦s14⟧ Then in a moment, in the twinkling of an eye, according to Ezekiel, in a field full of bones, the bones of the dead shall be gathered again, bone to bone, and sinews to sinews, and joints to joints [n: (Ezech. XXXVII)]; because there shall rise a spiritual body which was sown a natural one [n: (I Cor. XV, 44)]: and rising from the dead it dies no more, death shall no longer have dominion over it. ⟦s15⟧ Then at that moment, in the dawning of that day, when the bridegroom comes, a great cry shall rouse all who sleep, and it shall cry with great and most effectual power: *Go ye forth to meet him.* ⟦s16⟧ Go forth, you who sleep, and hasten to meet the Judge. ⟦s17⟧ Then shall all the tribes of the earth bewail themselves. ⟦s18⟧ *Then shall they say to the mountains, Fall upon us; and to the hills, Cover us [n: (Luc. XXIII, 30)]*. ⟦s19⟧ Go forth from your tombs, you whom the memory of conscience does not accuse, and behold the Judge, [1047B] rejoicing with a good conscience and good desert.

## 6.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But the reprobate the Judge's power rather than their own will shall bring forth, nor shall they so much go out of the tombs as be dragged out. ⟦s2⟧ Deservedly is that day proclaimed a day of wrath, a day of calamity and misery, a day of darkness and gloom [n: (Sophon. I, 15)], on which

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *713* *At the sign of judgment, the earth shall grow wet with sweat:*
⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *There shall fall from the heavens a river of fire and of brimstone,*
⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the earth, gaping open, shall show the chaos of Tartarus*.


⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For fire shall go before the Lord, and shall set his enemies ablaze round about [n: (Psal. XCVI, 3; Luc. XII, 49)], whom it shall then find, like the Sodomites, naked and uncovered. ⟦s2⟧ For so shall the flooding shower of fire and brimstone set them alight as they rise and burn them up. ⟦s3⟧ It will then be altogether pitiable to see that the incorruptible and unburnable [1047C] body shall burn and not perish: it shall be burned up and not diminished. ⟦s4⟧ For a hair from the head shall not perish; because nothing of a body which has put on incorruption can either be cut or be scorched. ⟦s5⟧ Look at the salamander walking with unharmed body over fiery and burning coals. ⟦s6⟧ Further, the stone asbestos is reported to be of such a nature that, once kindled, it burns unquenchably, the fire clinging to the stone and yet not consuming it. ⟦s7⟧ In such a way, I think, will that sulphurous fire set the Lord's enemies ablaze. ⟦s8⟧ But the just, like the three Babylonian youths, it shall not touch at all [n: (Dan. III)], nor sadden, nor bring any trouble upon, when the Lord shall refashion the body of our lowliness, conformed to the body of his brightness. ⟦s9⟧ I ask, amid these things what [1047D] will be the mind of sinners, who now are glad when they have done evil and exult in the worst of things? [n: (Prov. II, 14.)] what spirit, I say, shall they then have, with the elements burning, the earth gaping open, and with its terrible chasm showing the chaos of Tartarus, the dead rising up, and that Judge, to whom the Father has given all judgment [n: (Joan. V, 22)], coming from heaven with all the Angels? ⟦s10⟧ Wretched me! while I think and write these things, my heart throbs with fear, my eyes grow wet, and I would be troubled beyond measure, did I not know the mercies of the Lord.

## 7.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But let us return to the matter proposed. ⟦s2⟧ *Then all those virgins arose, and trimmed their lamps,* that is, their consciences, with thoughts accusing [1048A] or even defending: *I will reprove thee,* he says, *and set thee before thy own face [n: (Psal. XLIX, 21)]*. ⟦s3⟧ We have put our sins behind our back while we lived here, and — as though no examination of our acts were to be made before the tribunal of Christ — through negligence we have handed over many sins to forgetfulness. ⟦s4⟧ But what does he say? ⟦s5⟧ *I will set thee before thy own face;* that is, thy whole life, whatever thou hast sinned in thought and in deed, I will bring before the face of thy mind: and when thou hast seen all these things at once, I will say: *These things hast thou done, and I kept silence.* ⟦s6⟧ So I judge it will be in that hour, such a judgment will be made, with Truth, which is Christ, judging in the hearts of each. ⟦s7⟧ Alas! how many things shall then come into mind which now, thinking them nothing, we leave unconfessed and unpurged! ⟦s8⟧ Fearing which, the devout and prudent king Hezekiah said: [1048B] *I will think over again all my years in the bitterness of my soul [n: (Isa. XXXVIII, 15)]*. ⟦s9⟧ *I will think over again* — that is, I will think a second time; lest perchance I have forgotten something, and lest anything remain unjudged and unexamined.

## 8.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The prudent virgins, therefore, trim their lamps with the oil which they had laid by in vessels; but the lamps of the foolish virgins are put out, and because they had done the works of darkness, they remained in darkness. ⟦s2⟧ *Give us,* they say, of *your oil.* ⟦s3⟧ A foolish request. ⟦s4⟧ The just man shall scarcely be saved [n: (I Petr. IV, 18)], and scarcely does the oil of their own righteousness suffice even for the saints unto salvation: how much less for themselves and for their neighbors too? ⟦s5⟧ Noah, Daniel, and Job shall not free even a son: but as the soul that has sinned shall itself die, so the soul that has done righteousness shall be saved alone [1048C] [n: (Ezech. XVIII, 4, 20-30)]. ⟦s6⟧ *The prudent answered, saying: Lest perhaps there be not enough for us and for you, go rather to them that sell, and buy for yourselves.* ⟦s7⟧ It is too late: for now the oil of righteousness is by no means for sale. ⟦s8⟧ Now the Prophets and Apostles, and the other teachers, who used to deal in this and to have it for sale, have ceased from such merchandise, nor do they any longer say: *Take up a psalm, and bring hither the timbrel [n: (Psal. LXXX, 3)]*. ⟦s9⟧ What more? ⟦s10⟧ *They that were ready went in with him to the marriage.* ⟦s11⟧ The virgins go in to the marriage, whom the King shall bring into his chamber: because he has desired their beauty. ⟦s12⟧ There from the heavenly wine-cellar the new wine is drawn and poured out, which the Son of man shall both drink and display. [1048D]

## 9.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the door was shut.* ⟦s2⟧ What door? ⟦s3⟧ That which now stands open to those coming from the East and the West, that they may recline with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven: that door which says, *Him that cometh to me I will not cast out [n: (Joan. VI, 37)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Behold how it now stands open, which shall then be shut forever. ⟦s5⟧ Murderers come, and are let in; publicans and harlots come, and are received; sodomites come, the soft, adulterers, thieves, and whoever are of this kind, and the open door is not refused them. ⟦s6⟧ For he is abundant in pardoning, mighty over malice of whatever kind and however great. ⟦s7⟧ But then what does he say? ⟦s8⟧ *The door was shut.* ⟦s9⟧ The door of Christ's mercy and compassion, which now stands easily open to the tears, [1049A] groans, sighs, and prayers of the penitent, is shut. ⟦s10⟧ No one's penitence, no one's prayer, no one's groaning shall be admitted any more. ⟦s11⟧ That door is shut which received Aaron after his idolatry, which let David in after adultery, after murder, which not only did not [1050A] repel Peter after his threefold denial, but handed over its own keys to him to keep. ⟦s12⟧ *Watch ye therefore, because ye know not the day nor the hour:* because to those who watch and pray, not to those who sleep and are slothful, the kingdom of heaven is granted through our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be honor and glory forever and ever. ⟦s13⟧ Amen.
