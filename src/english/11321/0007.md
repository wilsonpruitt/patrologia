## 22.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *After the flocks.* ⟦s2⟧ For he who departs from the perfection of truth into the byway of error, does not even go with the wandering flocks, but after the flocks; because he is found worse than all these. ⟦s3⟧ But it must not be passed over, that the flock of the Lord is fed by its shepherds in three ways: by example, [0425A] by word, by prayer. ⟦s4⟧ By example, against the concupiscence of the flesh; by word, against the concupiscence of the eyes; by prayer, against the ambition of the world. ⟦s5⟧ *If thou know not thyself, O fairest among women.* ⟦s6⟧ The bride was seeking the Bridegroom at noon, that is in the manifest light of truth — namely, lest one thing should meet her instead of another; she was seeking where he would lie down at noon, that is in which affection *275* of noonday perfection, that she might see which affection was to be chosen and when, where the Bridegroom would lie down with her, and she with the Bridegroom. ⟦s7⟧ And so the Bridegroom answered: O fairest among women — namely, between Mary and Martha, because I do not wish that thou choose one of these, but that thou hold both, namely the contemplative life and the active; O fairest among women, if thou wouldst know me, be not ignorant of thyself. ⟦s8⟧ This is the answer of the Delphic Apollo: [0425B] « Know thyself. » There are two things that make us ignorant of ourselves: either too much rashness about ourselves, or too much timidity of humiliation. ⟦s9⟧ According to these two the Bridegroom answered the bride: either recalling her from rashness to humility, through experience of herself, or from timidity to confidence, from the perception of past gifts. ⟦s10⟧ For there are two kinds of ignorance of oneself: either when one esteems more, or when one esteems less of oneself; and there are two things that produce this, presumption and pusillanimity; and two causes of these effects, pride and too little experience of oneself. ⟦s11⟧ But experience of oneself makes humility; humility, knowledge of oneself. ⟦s12⟧ And so the Bridegroom, restraining the rash one: Too soon, he says, and impatiently dost thou desire the noon of angelic beatitude; because first thou must do much, and labor in the [0425C] morning of virtue. ⟦s13⟧ *If* therefore *thou know not thyself, go forth* from me, or from the good of thy conscience, and stand not with me, *but go after the steps of the flocks and feed thy kids,* that is the depraved motions of the flesh, or the lost hearers, who shall be set on the left, *beside the tents of the shepherds;* that is, having followed the doctrines of reprobate masters, not in the Church, but in the tents of their conventicles teaching depraved things. ⟦s14⟧ But what he says, *Go forth,* he permits, he does not command — which also he does not even seem to permit wholly, but recalls her to labor.

## 23.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I have likened thee, my love, to my company of horsemen in the chariots of Pharaoh [n: (Cant. I, 8)]*. ⟦s2⟧ O my love, though I restrain thee as rash, though I recall thee to labor, yet not on that account less my love. ⟦s3⟧ Already thou wouldst sit with me in my kingdom; I should be indignant at the presumption, but I pardon the love. ⟦s4+5⟧ O my love, thou must not now reign, but fight; because *I have likened thee to my company of horsemen in the chariots of Pharaoh.* [0425D] Thou art set in the midst of the chariots of Pharaoh: not only must thou go forth, but thou hast to lead forth my people out of the land of bondage. ⟦s6⟧ Or, that I may raise up the pusillanimous one: Thou shalt not perish, O my love, because *in the chariots of Pharaoh I have likened thee to my company of horsemen,* that is, I have made thee like to the company of horsemen of the Angels: so that as they have charity, and chastity, and humility, and order, so also thou, set in the midst of the chariots of Pharaoh. ⟦s7⟧ For as long as we are here in readiness for battle, and our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against the [0426A] spiritual wickednesses in the heavenly places, both armies, both the chariot of God and the chariot of Pharaoh, are joined as in war: for as the chariot of Pharaoh, that is of the devil, so also the chariot of God is manifold with ten thousand. ⟦s8⟧ It is pleasing to describe more subtly these very chariots both of God and of the devil, and to show more evidently who serve as soldiers of God or of the devil. ⟦s9⟧ The chariots of the devil are three, with which he traverses the whole world: luxury, pride, malice. ⟦s10⟧ The chariot of luxury has two wheels, gluttony and softness, which the impulse of the flesh draws like an untamed horse; upon which sits love of self in the saddle of pleasure, having want for its bridle, and for its spur, the spur of lust. ⟦s11⟧ The chariot of pride has two wheels, presumption of self and rashness: the horse that draws is the appetite of vainglory, upon which sits love [0426B] of the world, in the saddle of adulation, having avarice for its bridle, and for its spur, the spur of spite. ⟦s12⟧ The chariot of malice has two wheels, feigning and dissembling: the horse that draws is malevolence, upon which sits the relish of evil, in the saddle of fraud, having tergiversation for its bridle, and for its spur, the spur of wrath. ⟦s13⟧ Against these three chariots, there are three chariots of God. ⟦s14⟧ The chariot of continence has two wheels, prayer and bodily exercise: the horse that draws is devotion, upon which sits the soldier of God, contempt of self, in the saddle of purity; having discipline for its bridle, and for its spur, the spur of the fear of the Lord. ⟦s15⟧ The chariot of innocence has two wheels, the humility of him who is unwilling to be over others, and the meekness of him who is willing to be under: these the horse draws, that is love of peace, having contempt of the world for its rider, *276* in the saddle [0426C] of benevolence; for its bridle, zeal of truth; for its spur, the spur of the love of God and of neighbor. ⟦s16⟧ The chariot of wisdom has two wheels, prudence and simplicity; for its horse, fortitude; for its rider, the love of God; for its saddle, vigilance lest it fall; for its bridle, moderation, that it may govern; for its spur, zeal of justice. ⟦s17⟧ Since therefore, my love, thou hast chariots as Pharaoh, and I have likened thee to my company of horsemen in the chariots of Pharaoh, thou must now be busy about victory, not about the reward of victory: for what thou seekest is too much, and too soon. ⟦s18⟧ Thou shalt not now see my face, but thou shalt see my back parts. ⟦s19⟧ For though thou dost not now receive the noon which thou askest, yet many things meanwhile thou dost receive from me, in which thou art deservedly praised. [0426D]

## 24.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For already by my grace *thy cheeks are beautiful as the turtledove's;* that is, great are both the outer and the inner gifts with which I have enriched thee. ⟦s2⟧ For what thou hast received within, the honesty and maturity of thy manner of life shows outwardly, which are the two cheeks of the turtledove, that is of the soul, mourning in the absence of the Bridegroom, and receiving no consolation, and dwelling alone. ⟦s3⟧ As in the pallor or ruddiness of the cheeks the quality of the outer man is discerned, so in the honesty and maturity of the manner of life the sincerity of a well-composed mind is shown. ⟦s4⟧ And not only this, but also *thy neck is as jewels.* ⟦s5⟧ Purity of soul is commended in thee, as I have said, before men, by the honesty and maturity of thy manner of life; but before God, by the directing of thy intention. ⟦s6⟧ For thy intention, which like a neck [0427A] joins the whole body of thy work to the head, Christ, is as a jewel, when it is made manifest to all that it serves God and the neighbor. ⟦s7⟧ For this is what the jewel signifies, the ornament of the neck, which outwardly has gold, but inwardly a gem — namely that the intention be affected with the love of God, and the love of neighbor. ⟦s8⟧ When the Bridegroom declares this to men, he adorns the bride's neck with a jewel; nay, he makes it to be as jewels. ⟦s9⟧ Since I have given thee all these things, says the Bridegroom, I will add yet another ornament: *We will make thee chains of gold.* ⟦s10⟧ So it is plainly, so it is wont to be. ⟦s11⟧ When the bride seeks glory in this world, she does not attain it; or if she attains it, it is useless. ⟦s12⟧ But when she seeks glory in the Bridegroom alone, even unknowing she incurs glory, and glory that is useful. ⟦s13⟧ For as in the ornament of the neck glory [0427B] offered itself from example, so in the ornament the gold from word. ⟦s14⟧ For glory ought to seek thee, not thou it. ⟦s15⟧ If thou seek it, it flees; if thou flee, it comes upon thee; if thou seek it and find it, it is harmful or useless.
