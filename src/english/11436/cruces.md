# Cruces — 11436, *Vita et passio S. Thomae Cantuariensis* (PL 190)

Garbled or doubtful type in Migne's plate, rendered literally per the intended
reading and logged here.

## Prologue and chap. I (chunk 0000)

- **col. 0345C** — `brevitate competenti studuimus consulere deficatis`.
  *deficatis* is not a Latin word. The sense of the sentence (brevity for one
  class of reader, *veritas plena* for the *studiosi*) requires a dative plural
  naming the impatient reader: *delicatis* ("the fastidious") is the likeliest
  reading, *fatigatis* / *defessis* ("the weary") possible. Rendered per the
  likeliest intended reading, "for those who are fastidious"; the parallel at
  0346A (*taediosis tollat fastidium*) supports it.

- **cols. 0345A, 0346A–B** — this text is a *quadrilogus* whose editor prints only
  the incipit and explicit of passages he takes over verbatim from Edward Grim
  (`Professores artium, etc.`; `Electus igitur ante mundi, etc., usque ad
  veritati`). These stubs are translated as stubs; the bracketed cross-reference
  that follows is Migne's apparatus, and its locator (`apud Edwardum Grim.,
  supra, col. 3`) is kept in locator form with the embedded `[0346B]` anchor
  in place. A human read-through should confirm that this hybrid treatment
  (comment translated, locator kept) is what the project wants for this
  bracket-form apparatus, which is neither `[n: …]` nor `[ *al.,* …]`.

## Chap. II (chunk 0001)

- **col. 0346C** — `in captivitate Sclavorum`. The captors throughout the chapter
  are Saracens (*gentiles*, *pagani*, an *Amiraldus* = emir, in the Holy Land);
  *Sclavorum* ("of the Slavs") is almost certainly a corruption of
  *Saracenorum*. Rendered literally as printed ("in the captivity of the
  Slavs") per the corrupt-Latin rule; flagged here rather than emended.

- **col. 0347A** — `consequenter et ipsa ab eo scistitavit` — *scistitavit* for
  *sciscitata est* / *sciscitavit*. Rendered per the intended reading.

- **col. 0347B** — `quasi verbi ex virtute tota mutata`: the word order is
  disturbed (*quasi ex virtute verbi*). Rendered per the intended reading, "as
  it were wholly changed by the power of the word."

- **col. 0349A** — `Cumque interrogaretur … si vellet baptizari, respondit:` The
  sentence has a stray comma-for-period after *baptizari,* in the printed line
  (`in quo debuerat baptizari, Cumque interrogaretur`) with a capital following.
  Sentence division taken per the capital; noted only so no one reads it as a
  dropped clause.

## Chaps. III–IV (chunk 0002)

- **col. 0350D** — `didicit a matre timorem Dimini` for *Domini*. Rendered per
  the intended reading, "the fear of the Lord."

- **col. 0351A** — `motam de flumine anam accipiter insequeretur`. *anam* is an
  unclassical accusative of *anas* (for *anatem*); the sense ("duck") is not in
  doubt and it is so rendered.

- **col. 0351B** — `ne exstingueretur lucerna futurus in Israel`. The participle
  is masculine (*futurus*, agreeing with the boy) while the noun it stands with
  is feminine (*lucerna*); read either as *lucerna futura* or as an anacoluthon
  in which *futurus* still looks back to Thomas. Rendered as "lest the lamp that
  was to be in Israel should be put out," which keeps the printed sense of both.

- **cols. 0351B → 0352A** — the column sequence skips 0351C–D; the sentence runs
  across the jump without a break in sense. Reproduced as printed.

## Attribution

Migne's `authors: ["Auctor incertus"]` is right as far as it goes, but the
prologue names its own method and sources explicitly (cols. 0345A–B): this is
the **first *Quadrilogus***, a harmony woven from four eyewitness biographers —
Herbert of Bosham, John of Salisbury (here "John of Chartres," from his later
see), William of Canterbury, and Alan, abbot of Tewkesbury. The compiler is
conventionally identified as **Elias of Evesham** (working c. 1198–1200); Migne
does not name him, and the text itself does not, so the frontmatter is left as
Migne has it. Migne's own closing note (0352B) distinguishes this "*quadrilogus
prior*" from a "*secundus quadrilogus*" in five books.
