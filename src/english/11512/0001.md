## DEDICATORY EPISTLE OF ABBOT WOLBERO.

[1005B]To his most beloved sisters on the Island of the Rhine, serving as soldiers to God and to blessed Mary, WOLBERO, a sinner.

While I was often treating with myself, and frequently turning over with a doubtful mind, what sweet thing, what pleasant, what useful thing I might sing to the inward ears of your holy charity, O most holy sisters, by whose sweetness and incitement your mind would be raised to a greater love of the heavenly life, there suddenly occurred to my mind that singable epithalamium of the bridegroom and the bride, which for its excellence is called the *Canticle of Canticles.* This kind of song, indeed, greatly befits the dignity of your profession, because this surpasses the order of all professions, as that surpasses every kind of canticles.

[1006B]For there are three kinds of professions: one of the married, another of widows, the third of virgins. Now we read that there is *a canticle of praise, a canticle of gladness, a new canticle.* Let us assign therefore, as by certain steps, the canticle of praise to the married, the canticle of gladness to widows, the new canticle to virgins, which *no man* can *say, save they who follow the Lamb whithersoever he shall go [n: (Apoc. XIV)]* [var: Vulg. Apoc. 14:3 *nemo poterat dicere canticum, nisi illa centum quadraginta quatuor millia*, "no man could say the canticle but those hundred forty-four thousand"; *Hi sequuntur Agnum* is 14:4]. For the *canticle* of *praise* the married sing, who have received some beginning of religion and of good conversation, but have not yet escaped the tribulation of the flesh; while the *canticle* of *gladness* widows sing, who have attained something higher, excelling in greater religion, and, having now experienced the tribulation of the flesh, are set free from it, and therefore can [1007A] sing both praise and gladness. But virgins sing the *new canticle,* who hold the highest step, never having experienced the aforesaid tribulation of the flesh, and therefore able to sing both praise, and gladness, and a newness singular and unutterable to the rest.

But great heed must be taken, in those who are called virgins, of which part they are; for the Lord divided this profession into two parts, that is, into wise and foolish virgins [n: (Matth. XXV)]; because this new canticle they cannot say, save those who are of the part of the wise: and the wise are those who are virgins both in body and in mind; but the foolish, those who, although they are virgins in body, yet live corrupted in mind. Such, assuredly, cannot sing [1007B] the *new canticle,* nor even the canticle of the last profession; who do not even attain to its merit, because they have no oil in the vessel; nay rather, for this negligence of theirs, they do not enter the bridegroom's chamber.

Wherefore your charity, you who are virgins in body, ought greatly to take heed and be solicitous that you be so in mind also; because virginity of the flesh confers nothing on you, if you have already married in mind. If, then, you hold the right rule of the virginal profession, you sing a singular canticle, and, so to speak, not simply a canticle, as married women and widows do, but the *Canticle of Canticles*, which it is possible to sing only for those *who are before the throne of God without spot, because they have not defiled their garments [n: (Apoc. XIV)]* [var: Vulg. Apoc. 14:5 *sine macula enim sunt ante thronum Dei*; the clause *qui non inquinaverunt vestimenta sua* is Apoc. 3:4], that is, their [1007C] bodies.

Authorities for these three professions are indeed found in Holy Scripture, because conjugal chastity is proclaimed in Elizabeth, widowly holiness in Anna, virginal perfection in the blessed Mother of God, Mary. And the merit of each the Lord himself shows in the Gospel, where the *seed* is said to have fallen *into good ground and* to have made *fruit, some thirtyfold, some sixtyfold, and some a hundredfold [n: (Luc. VIII)]* [var: Vulg. Luc. 8:8 *fecit fructum centuplum*, "made fruit a hundredfold", with no thirty or sixty; these stand in Matt. 13:8 and Mark 4:20].

For the thirtyfold fruit is that of the married living chastely, whom the reckoning of the fingers shows by this number, the forefinger being laid over the thumb; it indicates the weight of the tribulation and depression of the widows themselves, destitute of the support and consolation of a man. [1007D] But the hundredfold number, which is put for perfection, shows the perfection of holy virgins; which, carried over from the left hand to the right, the tip of the forefinger being joined to the tip of the thumb, expresses the crown of virginity.

Weigh therefore the excellence of this profession, whose reward is the hundredfold fruit, whose merit is to say the *new canticle,* to whose dignity, by reason of the special divine conjunction of grace, these *Canticles of Canticles* which we intend to sing to you according to our small measure are most fitting. Not even with you, whichever of you are truly virgins, can I, I say not sing these canticles, but not even whisper them, I who differ far [1008A] from your holiness; but as someone who can convey the knowledge of some art by word only, but cannot express it in act, so I too, although I cannot sing these things with you, my fault standing in the way, will nevertheless try with God's help to say something from which you may in some measure know the excellence and singular dignity of these Canticles. Therefore, as the gentile poet says: *I will serve as a whetstone, which knows how to render*
*iron sharp, itself without share in cutting:*
[n: (HORAT., Ars poet., 304.)] and since it is not permitted to sing, let it at least be permitted to give counsel for singing. For holy Moses also, with whom God spoke face to face, heard and did the counsel of Jethro his kinsman, a gentile man, although he himself was full [1008B] of wisdom and holiness, while the other stood very far distant from him in gentilehood and in the quality of his life. But since God has given to will this, may he give also to be able. I know indeed that it will be unworthy in the eyes of many, who receive only the writings of the ancients, *And admire nothing save what Libitina has hallowed.*
[n: (HOR., Ep. II, I, 49.)] that after the sayings of the elders, which charity has confirmed, I have shamelessly laid bare my own follies as well; but your love will excuse me before them, whose desire alone I have set myself to satisfy. And if they reproach the speech as too simple for the majesty of the cause, let them remember that in the construction of the tabernacle not only gold and silver, byssus [1008C] and purple, but also goats' hair were offered, [n: (Exod. XXV)], and the two small mites of the widow were more praised by the Lord than the immense gifts of the rich [n: (Luc. XXI)]. And if they cavil at the arrangement of the words, let them nevertheless venerate the cause of the matter, and let both the dignity of the matter and the obedient charity of devout supplication excuse the thinness of the speech or composition. For we have in the Church very many books composed by metrical art, on the Gospels and the deeds of the saints, in a humble style and simple speech, which nevertheless, on account of the dignity of their matter, have already obtained authority. Whence Arator, when about to write the Acts of the Apostles, says: *In fasting speech indeed, but deeds that are rich*
*Have we written, and a drop flows with the weight of the sea*.
[1008D] Let no one therefore despise our simplicity, because *discourse with the simple* is ours [n: (Prov. III)]; nay rather, let him indulge us sinners the more, in that it pleased [us] more to write something, however simply, who in empty leisure, torpor, since we even wish this work to go forth nowhere, but to lie hidden only with the holy sisters alone to whom it has been sent; unless perhaps it shall please someone, out of a regard of kindness, to take this up for correcting, and out of a shapeless and soft composition to work out some beautiful and masculine work.

Receive therefore this work of my littleness, in which it has not irked me to labour for the sake of your [1009A] love, for your profit indeed, but for my own perpetual monument; so that when you look upon these things [1010A] and read them, you may be mindful of me, and, whether I am living or dead, entreat our common God for me.
