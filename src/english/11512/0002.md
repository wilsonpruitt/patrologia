## CENSURE OF THE ABBOT OF GERODHEN On the Commentaries of Abbot Wolbero on the Canticle of Canticles.

[1009]

[1009A]To the venerable and inwardly lovable lord WOLBERO, abbot of Saint Pantaleon, brother, called abbot of the monastery of GERODHEN: [may you,] led not only into the cellar but also into the chamber of the bridegroom, be inebriated with consummate charity *from the plenty of the house of God, and with the torrent of the pleasure* of him be given to drink for ever [n: (Psal. XXXV)].

For you write, and with a humility familiar to all and to those who use the wisdom of the spirit, you admonish by asking and [1009B] ask by admonishing, that I should certify you in writing concerning your work on the *Canticles of Canticles,* considered with more diligence, every courting of your good will being wholly put away, what nevertheless it may seem to me, namely whether I think it should be brought forth into the light, or understand that it should be hidden and laid aside; because you would trust no one better than me, in that both kinship binds [me] to good will and the wisdom of correction would not deny a verdict. And indeed, as every man of understanding recognizes that all judgments are contained in nothing but these two, prudence and good will, so, since I reflect not without grief that good will is surely present and prudence absent, following him who prayed, saying: *That I may know what is wanting to me [n: (Psal. XXXVIII)]*, not [1009C] being sufficient to bear the burden that you lay on me, I not undeservedly shrink from it.

But since this is the excuse even of all who are torpid in leisure, and your charity, trusting me and hoping that this can be done by me, admonishes me to do it, not as a judge, but as a friend of the bridegroom by desire: while I hear the voice [1010A] of him ministering his doctrine to your heart in the liberty of the spirit, at the voice of the bridegroom using you as an organ I rejoice together in charity rejoicing with the truth, and I judge that this work is not to be abolished, nay, to the glory of God, for it is glory to reveal the word [n: (Tob. XII)], and for the profit of the Church of God and the instruction of those for whom it is written, it is by all means to be made manifest. Truly both catholic in faith, and, for morals [1010B] to be instructed, most apt, every Christian disposing himself to live well in the house of God will judge it.

I have learned that you took it somewhat ill that I did not at once send back the codex, finished; but some things were an impediment: first, because no thing can be at once hurried and examined, which I learned from you also, since you pressed on almost into the new year; wherefore what was written with diligence [should], as it is read, not be judged with negligence. To this was added that our brothers, against your will indeed, but with me willing and rejoicing over it, eagerly snatched it and read it sedulously and diligently, and by the study of reading made it commendable; yet so that they withdrew from me, even unwilling, some of the reading of [1010C] it. But now, read through, and not without diligence, I send it back, to be communicated to all of you in Christ for reading; it remains that you not deny a share in the fruit to him whom you judged fit to share the labour. Farewell.
