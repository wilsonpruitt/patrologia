## GENERAL PREFACE OF WOLBERO ON THE CANTICLE OF CANTICLES OF SOLOMON. (cont.)

This threefold progress of the soul the Psalmist prays for, saying: *Goodness and discipline and knowledge teach me [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*: for first he puts goodness, then discipline, in the third place knowledge. For God, being naturally good, created man naturally good, since he made him to his own image and likeness, reason being given to him by which he could discern good and evil; but he himself, understanding that he can [do] the one of these, the good, not from himself but from God, asks to be taught goodness, that through the goodness of naturality, by the affection of holy morality, discipline, [1014D] through which he may be able to advance to that incomprehensible and divine knowledge of rationality; and, ever demanding the order of this progress with the whole desire of his mind, he cries out: *goodness,* he says, *and discipline and knowledge teach me.* As if he said in other words: Since thou, O Lord God, hast created me naturally good; how that very good of nature may not be corrupted by my fault, but propagated by thy grace, teach [me], so that in this way I may will and be able by thy help to live rightly, and, living rightly, to penetrate the secrets of thy mysteries with the citadel of the mind.

To this third part, then, which we have called rationality, this book is subscribed, on account of the excellence of the sacraments which are sung therein in Christ and the Church, or in any whatever, beloved of God, of the mind; which [1015A] is also deservedly inscribed with this name which is the *Canticles of Canticles,* because, marked as by a certain privilege of spiritual harmony, more perfectly and more sublimely than all canticles, it intones mystical things. For if you consider more subtly, you will understand that almost all the sacraments of Christ and the Church are sung in it. For, to speak by way of example, in the very beginning of the canticle, when it is said in the person of the Church: *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth,* the coming of Christ through the flesh is longed for, as if the Church said in other words: Thou who once spokest to me through the mouths of thy prophets and of the other Fathers, now at length, born according to the flesh, offer thyself in presence, and with thine own mouth hold out to me the decrees of thy commandments. The passion also of Christ and the resurrection it denotes in that place where the beloved [1015B] speaks: *A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me, he shall abide between my breasts,* and presently after, *a cluster of cypress is my beloved to me [n: (Cant. I)]*, where by the myrrh the bitterness of the passion, by the cluster the sweetness of the resurrection is expressed. And elsewhere: *Flee,* she says, *my beloved, and be like to a roe or to a young hart upon the mountains of spices [n: (Cant. VIII)]* [var: Vulg. *capreae, hinnuloque cervorum*, "to a roe and to a young hart"]; by the roe and the young hart suggesting the power of the Lord ascending into heaven. In the flock of shorn ones coming up from the washing [n: (Cant. IV, VI)], the people of the baptized laying down the burdens of sins is understood; in the twin offspring, the twin things of charity; and in the eyes, the teeth, the cheeks, the hair, and the other arrangements of members which are named there, the spiritual adornment and arrangement of holy Church, or of any holy soul, is described. There the depravity of heretics, [1015C] the sweetness of doctors is denoted; and, to comprehend the whole, whatever in holy Church is done, generally or specially, or is celebrated as done, is sung with most beautiful allusions, so that deservedly this book is called by such a name, which is the *Canticles of Canticles*, for its excellence.

Our purpose, therefore, in this work is to investigate, as we are able, the rationality of these sacraments; but chiefly, for your edification, holy sisters, to look into morality and to stir up your mind in the love of your bridegroom, the Lord Jesus Christ.

We have divided the whole book into four tomes, on account of the four subdistinctions of the recapitulations of the periochae, [1015D] which are marked by this chapter thrice repeated: *I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the goats, that you stir not up nor make the beloved to awake, till she herself will.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 2:7 *filiae Jerusalem, per capreas cervosque camporum*, "daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields"]

Of these periochae, then, the first is from where it says: *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth,* up to: *I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem,* the second from where it says: *The voice of my beloved; behold he cometh, leaping upon the mountains, skipping over the hills,* up to, likewise: *I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem.* The third, longer, which begins thus: *Who is she that goeth up by the desert,* up to the third adjuration: *I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem.* The fourth. *Who is she that goeth up from the desert, flowing with delights,* up to the end.

The singings of these four parts we have distinguished into four [1016A] little books: either on account of the evangelical chariot of Aminadab, or the moral one, which consists of the four cardinal virtues, which the bride, troubled, dreaded; or that the mind studious of morality may be admonished that it ought to be squared by the virtues, so that, as by squareness it stands on whatever [face] it uses, so he is neither lifted up by prosperity nor cast down by adversity, but, like a squared stone placed in the building of the Lord, is conformed to the passion of Christ by the mortification of the carnal life, the bearer of his cross, *that* he may be able *to comprehend with all the saints what is the length, breadth, sublimity and depth [n: (Ephes. III)]* [var: Vulg. *latitudo, et longitudo*, "breadth, and length", the reverse of the order the exposition follows]. The virtue of which comprehension belongs to the perfect alone, who, *rooted and founded in charity [n: (ibid.)]*, so cleave to their Creator that by the amplitude of charity they continually sing the *Canticle of Canticles*; in whom [1016B] this squareness of charity consists. For charity in the saints is long, because it is not for an hour, but persevering and continual; broad, because it extends even to enemies; high, because it is had for the sake of the hope and reward of eternal life; deep, because it penetrates even the secrets of God; *no man*, however, *hath known the things which are of God, save the Spirit of God [n: (I Cor. II)]*, through whom charity itself is poured forth in the hearts of the saints [n: (Rom. V)].

In the head himself also, our true exemplar, the Lord, that is, Christ, the beauty of this squareness shines forth delightfully, figured in the manner of his passion, namely in the form of the cross. For from the top of the cross down to the bottom, the length of his charity is expressed, of whom John says that *having loved [1016C] his own who were in the world, he loved them unto the end [n: (Joan. XIII)]*: and the breadth, stretched from the right to the left, is extended to the love of enemies, for whom, supplicating, *Father,* he says, *pardon them, because they know not what they do [n: (Luc. XXIII)]*; the sublimity is regarded in the inscription of the title: for scarcely can the height of his kindness, by which he rules, cherishes and guards us, consider; the depth, through the part fixed in the earth, which sustains the rest, hides the abyss of the judgments of God, because, though God has done and suffered so much for the salvation of men, why he takes up this one, repels that one, is inscrutable and unsearchable.

You therefore, O beloved sisters, crucified to the world with the Lord, learn to comprehend with all [1016D] the saints the length, breadth, sublimity and depth of charity, so that, led into the chamber of our Lord, your bridegroom, with lighted lamps, that is, good works, you may be able to sing continually before the throne of God that new canticle to be sung by virgins alone, truly the Canticle of Canticles.

But now let us come to the course of the book itself, forewarning this: that if it moves anyone that the discourse is stretched beyond measure, and the speech overflows the mode of explanation, let him know that we have undertaken this work not only for the sake of explaining; but rather with this intention, that thence we might overcome the matter for ourselves, whence we might satisfy, at least by writings, the religious desire of the holy sisters, whom we could not by words.
