## CHAPTER ONE.

[1017]

* 2* VERS. 1.-- *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.*

If the Son of God is the Word of God, of whom John says: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was [1017B] with God, and the Word was God [n: (Joan. I)]*, assuredly the same Son of God is also amply understood as the mouth of God, because a word is brought forth by the mouth. Not unfittingly can we take the kiss to be the Holy Spirit, because the peace and charity which are signified by a kiss are given through the Holy Spirit, in whom the remission of sins is made, through whom we are reconciled to God, through whom is given the distribution of gifts by which we are joined to God [n: (Rom. V)]. But as the substance of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit is undivided, so too is their operation undivided; and in the whole Trinity, although there is distinction in the persons, yet the will and the operation are one and the same. The one kissing, then, and the mouth, and the kiss, the three work one thing, that is, charity in the hearts [1017C] of those who share in this kiss; because, as we have said, the operation of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit is one; and the kiss of the mouth, that is, of the Word, which is the Son, is also the kiss of the one kissing, that is, of the Father, because the Holy Spirit is of the Father and of the Son; and the kiss is the grace and peace and charity of this undivided Trinity. Through the kiss of the mouth of the one kissing, then, that is, through the Holy Spirit, of the Father and of the Son, whose substance is one, one majesty, one virtue, one power, one will, one operation: first indeed is given the remission of sins, by the kiss of peace, the reconciling kiss; then the distribution of gifts, by the kiss of charity, the contemplative kiss.

This kiss, then, of a twofold grace, holy Church universally, or [1017D] any holy soul particularly, seeks in its own order from the Lord her Creator, pondering and considering that the creating Trinity, which is everywhere present through the power of the divinity, is not present through the grace of indwelling, which is not everywhere save through love, through which the indwelling of the divinity is known, and conversely love through the indwelling of the divinity: and therefore she asks that the kiss be held out to her, first, as we have said, the reconciling kiss through the remission of sins, so that she may then be able to merit the contemplative kiss through the grace of gifts. But since the proof of love is the showing of the work, assuredly through the showing of the work the grace of the indwelling divinity is recognized; because love working and working loving is the kiss of the mouth of the one kissing, that is, the grace of the Trinity dwelling wholly in the mind [1018] *3,* Whence the Lord says in the Gospel: *If anyone love me he will keep my word, and my Father will love him, and we will come to him and will make our abode [1018B] with him [n: (Joan. XIV)]*.

She says, then: *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.* As if she should say in other words: May my Creator himself, by his grace, so kindle this heart of mine that I may love him, that loving I may please him by working, that pleasing him I may be made worthy of his indwelling.

Or, that we may refer the mystery of his incarnation to the person of the Son alone: the Church longs that she may hear him speaking in his own person, whom until then she had heard through the mouths of the Fathers. For the Word that was to be incarnate first promised the secrets of his precepts or sacraments in the law and the prophets; which, coming afterwards, he himself fulfilled, and sealed as things to be observed unchangeably, as it were by the setting of his hand to them: whence he says through the prophet: *Therefore my people shall know [1018C] my name in that day, because I who spoke, behold, I am here [var: Vulg. Is 52:6 *ego ipse qui loquebar*, "I myself who spoke"] [n: (Isa. LII)]*.

That speech indeed, by which he spoke through the law and the prophets as through an instrument, was obscure and indeterminable; but when he himself came and spoke in his own person, it was made determinable and plain; because, the truth being present, the shadow had to cease. But because the preceding speech, whether through an angel, or through a man, or through some other creature, was made in many ways, that is, either by voice, or by sign, or by operation, or by vision, or by the touch of the heart alone; the following one through the very mediator of God and men; the blessed Apostle says *that in many ways and in many manners God, speaking of old to the fathers in the prophets, last of all [1018D] in these days hath spoken to us in the Son [n: (Hebr I)]*; assuredly, as a craftsman is far better than his instrument, so this last speech, since the Son of God brought it forth himself in his own person, is far worthier and more to be desired than that speech which he used in the Fathers as in instruments. It is last for this reason, because after it nothing could be, nor will be able to be, said or written more firmly or more certainly.

The sweetness of this speech, then, as being the true truth of truth, to be revealed to her understanding, holy Church, burning with the whole ardour of her soul, cries out for: *let him kiss me,* she says, *with the kiss of his mouth.* Now a kiss is the sign of peace or of love: and of this truly peaceable Lord Jesus Christ it is written that *coming he preached peace to those who were far off and to those [1019A] who were near [n: (Ephes. II)]*. But what peace? That, surely, which dissolves the enmities between God and man, which the holy prophets did indeed announce, but could not give. But he, *the Angel of great counsel* [var: Vulg. Is 9:6 *Admirabilis, Consiliarius*, "Wonderful, Counsellor"] and *Prince of peace [n: (Isa. IX)]*, both announced it and gave it. Wherefore holy Church, sighing for his presence, his address and his affability, rightly desires with all her prayers to enjoy to the full his converse, as the kiss of a peaceable mouth sowing and giving true and whole peace, saying: *Let him kiss me with the kiss of thy mouth,* [var: Vulg. Ct 1:1 *oris sui*, "of his mouth"] as if she should say: May he himself show forth to me in his own person what the holy Fathers *4* of old confirmed by sayings, writings and signs as something to be shown forth.

And why she desires this rather, she adds the cause: *for thy breasts are better,* she says, *than wine,* that is, the nourishments [1019B] of evangelical gentleness provide better and more quickly for my salvation than the austerity of the legal sentence, or the swelling and empty loquacity of the philosophers; of which it will be treated more fully under the second verse.

Morally, however, the soul beloved of God, having now attained that noble and incomparable celibacy, and having been brought into the Holy of holies of her bridegroom, her Creator and her Redeemer, secret and inaccessible to the human mind, rejoicing; *let him kiss me,* she says, *with the kiss of his mouth.* But whence comes this proclamation, so great, of such a kind, so confidently and sublimely presuming [sic: *praesumes*]? surely *from a pure heart and a good conscience and faith unfeigned [n: (I Tim. V)]*. For this is the method of attaining perfection: to know what, in what manner, why, that is, the matter, the intention, the usefulness of working. For it must be [1019C] greatly considered what thou doest, with what intention, with what fruit. For every action is conceived in the heart, nourished by intention, perfected by faith. For indeed if thou beginnest anything good, the intention of that good itself will be its judge, because *if thy eye be single, thy whole body will be lightsome; but if evil, it will be darksome [n: (Luc. XI)]*. Moreover faith either destroys or advances the work itself. For a man either believes or does not believe God, who promises his worshipper those things *which neither eye hath seen, nor ear heard, nor into the heart of man* have they ascended [n: (I Cor. II)]. For he who is unfaithful acts unfaithfully, and he who is faithful acts faithfully.

For faith can be divided in three ways, so that it is called faith dead, feigned, proved. *Dead faith* may be called that which, [1019D] according to the Apostle, *is without works [n: (Jac. I)]*, which, insensible, as though having no soul, that is, charity, is quickened or moved to no good work. Feigned faith is that which, not rooted in charity, according to the Lord's word, *believeth in time, and in time of temptation falleth away* [var: Vulg. Lc 8:13 *ad tempus credunt*, "believe for a while"] [n: (Luc. VIII)]. But proved faith is that which fails under no tribulations, under no adversities, because it is firmly rooted in charity.

Let the faithful and prudent soul, then, be zealous with *faith unfeigned* to keep charity *from a pure heart and a good conscience [n: (I Tim. I)]*, which is the perfection of the interior habit, which habit *charity* alone produces, that charity which *being perfect casteth out fear [n: (I Joan. IV)]*; which these three approve: a pure heart, a good conscience, [1020A] faith unfeigned, which nevertheless proceed from charity itself. For who *keepeth his heart with all watchfulness [n: (Prov. IV)]*, that he may have a good conscience, save from charity? And who keeps faith firm and unshaken, save from charity? These three, then, are probative of charity, and true charity itself is effective of them.

These are, in the Gospel, the oil, the vessels, the lamps, adorned with which the virgins, not foolish but wise, await the coming of their Bridegroom, our Lord *5* Jesus Christ, that they may enter with him to the wedding of weddings. For what is the oil, save a good work? And what the vessels, save the consciences, which, namely, carry those same works? And what the lamps, save the fervour and splendour of those same works? For if holy men are fervent in charity, they are splendid in the showing of it; so that to those who see [1020B] they may be for an example, to those who work, for a light. They carry oil in their vessels with their lamps, then, who cover a good work in a glad and secure conscience, with this intention, that they both may not seek [sic: *quaerat*] human praise, and may afford their neighbours an example of working well.

*From a heart,* then, *pure, and a good conscience and faith unfeigned [n: (I Tim. I)]*, let the soul beloved of God set up a ladder for herself, on which she may dispose ascents, by which, *from virtue to virtue [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)]*, she may be brought through charity [sic: *charita em*] to perfection in that highest good which is God, taking her form from her Bridegroom himself, Jesus Christ, who, *the Lord high above all nations [n: (Psal. CXII)]*, came humble among the nations, that he might teach us to climb his humility thither whence our first parents had descended through pride. For humility leads [1020C] her through charity to that vision of the Deity, as it were *to the third heaven,* to which the Apostle asserts that he was *caught up,* *and* that he heard ineffable *words, which it is not lawful for a man to speak [n: (II Cor. XII)]*.

Let us posit that, as it were, the first heaven is humility, the second charity, the third the sublimity of contemplation. When, then, the mind, lifted up, rises to the virtue of humility as to the first heaven, all the pleasures of the world being now despised and trodden underfoot, through *charity, which many waters* cannot *quench; for love is strong as death [n: (Cant. VIII)]*; it ascends to the second heaven; and when it has advanced so far that it thinks of nothing else, desires nothing else but God, it is lifted up through contemplation to the third heaven, [1020D] where, set above itself, and marvelling at the hidden things of the divine wisdom, it says: *My secret to myself, my secret to myself [n: (Isa. XXIX)]*. In which secret, now inebriated by the torrent of that ineffable delight, her face washed and her lips purged *with desolating coals [n: (Psal. CXIX)]*, she lingers in the longed-for embraces of her Bridegroom, saying: *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.*

But what is this confidence, that she so suddenly seeks the kiss of her Lord's mouth, as if reason did not permit one to approach, without order and some degree, a thing so eminent and supreme?
