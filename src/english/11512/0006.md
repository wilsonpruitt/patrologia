## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

Whence it is to be considered that certain lower kisses also went before that one: first indeed the kiss of redemption, then the kiss of reconciliation, and at the summit the kiss of love. And the kiss [1021A] of redemption is as it were the kiss of the Lord's feet; the kiss of reconciliation is as it were the kiss of the hands; but the kiss of love is as it were the kiss of the mouth. For by the feet, with which we are moved from place to place, the humanity of the Saviour is designated, the dispensation of which humanity, namely *6* that he was crowned with thorns, suffered, died, and was buried, and rose again, lay in movement and passage: for *Christ,* *rising from the dead, dieth now no more, death shall no more have dominion over him [n: (Rom. VI)]*; because through this [man] went out from the captivity of the devil into the liberty of Christ, and according to the prophet there rotted *the yoke before the face of the oil [n: (Isa. X)]*. For since in Greek ἔλεος is called in Latin *mercy,* and it is the nature of God to be borne above all liquids, and *the mercy* moreover *of the Lord is above all [1021B] his works [var: Vulg. Ps 144:9 *miserationes ejus*, "his tender mercies"] [n: (Psal. CXLIV)]*; when God looked upon the world in mercy, the yoke of its captivity rotted [sic: *computrui*] at the coming of the Saviour.

Now we kiss the feet of our Redeemer when we embrace the mysteries of his humanity with faith and devotion, and imitate, as far as we can, the footsteps of his humility, patience and obedience. But, the captivity being taken away, did man forthwith merit to be joined to God? For by his own fault, through free will, he had come into the captivity of the devil; because, though he had been created good by nature, he slipped into evil by his own vice. Because, then, he had rebelled against God in will and in deed, and by this had withdrawn far from his grace, he had need of reconciliation.

The ministers of God, our holy Fathers, went before, [1021C] pleading the cause of this reconciliation by preaching, exhorting, teaching and frequently announcing his precepts; and they could indeed free him from error, but could not bestow perfect liberty on him. His Creator himself came after, taking on his mortality, that he might give him his own immortality; and *having destroyed the middle wall, dissolving the enmities in his flesh through his blood [var: Vulg. Eph 2:14 *medium parietem maceriae solvens, inimicitias in carne sua*, without *destructo* or *per sanguinem*] [n: (Ephes. II)]*, he presented him with liberty by the kiss of Redemption, and then presently joined the same man to himself by the kiss of reconciliation: he who, bending himself downward from that height of his divinity, having torn up the old handwriting of sin, wrote on the earth the new testament of this peace and reconciliation through the sacrament of baptism, in which, as though the grace of manumission [1021D] were given, he restored perfect liberty; and adopting him as a son, whom before he had not even deigned to have as a servant because of the voluntary yoke of sin, he gave him the deed of such an inheritance as *neither eye hath seen, nor ear heard, nor hath it ascended into the heart of man [n: (I Cor. II)]*. And this testament or deed holy Church holds now and will hold, or any faithful soul, so that, if she does not cease to know, to have and to love God as her true Father, she may always be secure of such an inheritance.

We hold out a kiss to the hands of the Lord, then, when we observe the covenant or decree of this reconciliation in faith and work, and, consenting even in the way of this life with our adversary, that is, the word of God, we have perpetual [1022A] peace with him, by living justly and piously.

*7* But after reconciliation, as though security had been received, man began to draw near to the divinity more freely and more joyfully; and according to what James says: *Draw nigh to God and he will draw nigh to you [n: (Jac. IV)]*, the more he drew near to God through zeal for good work, the nearer he made God to himself through contemplation, so that, having set aside all the business of the world and trodden down earthly desires, he says, ascending the mountain with Moses: *Lord, show me thy face [n: (Exod. XXXIII)]*; and, cleaving delightedly in the vision of him, he advances so far in the ardour of divine charity that he seeks the kiss of his mouth, saying: *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.*

Not suddenly, therefore, but little by little, and as it were by certain [1022B] steps, he comes to the ineffable sweetness of that supreme charity, as to the kiss of the Lord's mouth: so that the first step is the redeeming kiss, the second the reconciling kiss, the third the contemplative kiss. For this is also the third heaven, to which the Apostle, asserting that he was caught up, received the grace of a special knowledge from that principal kiss of the Lord's mouth, and came to all the churches throughout the world, preaching the sweetness of that same kiss and exhorting them to the perfection of so great a holiness.

The grace of this threefold kiss that woman in the Gospel prefigured by her dutiful service, when first, falling devoutly at the feet of the Lord, kissing them, watering them with tears, wiping them with her hair, she obtained pardon of her sins, as though the redeeming kiss [1022C] had been given her, when she heard: *Thy sins are forgiven thee [n: (Luc. VII)]*. And presently, having received as it were from the hand of the Lord the written tablets of liberty, she is presented with the reconciling kiss, when, reconciled to God through the remission of sins, it is said to her: *Go in peace [n: (ibid.)]*. Thereafter, by the progress of a good way of life, she cleaved to the Lord with such ardour of charity, as though by the kiss of his mouth, the contemplative kiss, that she was praised by the proclamation of the Lord himself, saying: *Mary hath chosen the best part, which shall not be taken away from her [n: (Luc. X)]*.

We can also, not absurdly, take this threefold kiss as faith, hope, charity, so that faith is as it were the kiss of the Lord's feet, hope of his hands, charity of his mouth; for by faith we are saved, by hope we are lifted up, [1022D] by charity we are glorified. By faith we embrace the feet of the Lord, that is, the sacraments of his humanity, which pertains to salvation; by hope we reach the hands of the Lord, by which we are led to hope for the heavenly promises, which pertains to patience; for *if we hope for the things we see not, we wait for them through patience [n: (Rom. VIII)]*; but by charity we hold out a kiss as it were to the mouth of the Lord, when we are presented by him with eternal beatitude, which pertains to reward.

*8* Let the whole Church, then, say generally, or any holy soul specially: *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth;* with the kiss, she says, of the mouth, not as before of the prophets or of any other teachers whatever, but *his;* because he himself by his teaching and example has established for me a form of living, through which, by his own help, all the obstacles of this world being repulsed [1023A] and cast away, and perfect liberty now attained, they may cleave to him continually by the kiss of love, until, immortality and incorruption of body and soul having at length been taken on, I may be joined to him unchangeably and inseparably, and may say truly, *let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth:* that what *now* I see *through a glass in a dark manner, then* I may see *face to face [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*, *when God shall be all in all [n: (I Cor. XV)]*. These things, therefore, I desire, this I seek, this I long for with all my prayers. Why?

VERS. 2.-- *For thy breasts are better than wine, fragrant with the best ointments.*

What are these breasts of thine? The remission of sins, the distribution of gifts. These console me when I weep, these nourish me when I am little and bring me forward into manly strength: [1023B] these call me, draw me, soothe me; because they cry to me through thy Gospel: *Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven will draw near [var: Vulg. Mt 3:2 *appropinquavit*, "is at hand"] [n: (Matth. III)]*. But the wine of the biting law terrifies me by its authority, turns me away and exasperates me; because it cries: *Eye for eye, tooth for tooth, and whoever does this or that, let him die the death [n: (Exod. XXI)]*. These breasts of thine, I say, greatly invite me, animate me, and are strengthened, because of the exceeding abundance of thy mercy and the prerogative of thy gifts: but since from one of these lashes [cj: *verberum*; read *uberum*, "breasts"] of thine flows the remission of sins, from the other the distribution of gifts, the order is that the remission of sins be given first, then the distribution of gifts. But the greatness of his mercy sometimes makes that which is later, that [1023C] is, the distribution of gifts, and that which is earlier, that is, the remission of sins, run before in certain persons, for this reason, namely, that they may be the more roused and may labour with thy grace which calls them to receive the remission of sins.

Which, for example's sake, may be considered in Cornelius the centurion, in whom the gift of thy grace in kinds of tongues went before; so that, the more animated by it and as it were assured, he might come more readily to receive the sacrament of faith unto the remission of sins. In Paul the apostle also the same gift of thy grace ran before, in the gift of understanding and discernment, to whom, when Ananias had been sent and had come: *Saul,* he said, *brother; the Lord Jesus hath sent me, who appeared to thee in the way by which thou camest, that thou mayest see and be filled [1023D] with the Holy Spirit [n: (Act. II)]*. Did this seeing of his pertain only to the illumination of his bodily eyes, and not rather to the illumination of the mind, by which he might see and know *9* the truth which he was attacking? Was not this also to be filled with the Holy Spirit. For to this end thou gavest the Holy Spirit to him while not yet baptized, that he might know how to discern between the shadow of the law, which he was defending, and the truth of the Gospel, which he was attacking; and, having left the shadow, might come the more eagerly and fervently, by the help of the same Holy Spirit, to receive the truth, unto the remission of sins. These evangelical breasts of thine, then, which draw to salvation because of their gentleness, are better than those legal institutions which frighten away from salvation because of their austerity.

[1024A]But that I may consider something more deeply: what also, O beloved, *are* these *breasts of thine,* save the ineffable sweetness and glory of those heavenly secrets of thine, of which the Psalmist says: *I shall be satisfied while thy glory shall appear? [var: Vulg. Ps 16:15 *cum*, "when"] [n: (Psal. XVI.)]* Into this wondrous secret chamber of thine, then, after I shall have ascended through contemplation by the gift of thy grace, I shall be able to be torn from thy embraces neither by any blandishments of alluring prosperities nor by any scourges of frightening adversities, *for thy breasts are better than wine;* better, nay rather incomparable, are that paradise of thy delight and the manifestation of thy glory than the pleasures and passing honours of this world. And of what kind are these breasts of thine? *Fragrant with the best ointments.*

For these ointments are the signs not of any breasts whatever, [1024B] but of thine, redolent assuredly with such sweetness that by their very odour it becomes known what they are and whose they are. But what are these ointments? Surely those which that most excellent perfumer or spice-maker of thine, the Holy Spirit, has compounded and compounds. And what are the spices of which these ointments are made? Assuredly those which the Apostle, enumerating them in a certain place, calls spiritual fruits: *the fruits,* he says, *of the spirit are: peace, patience, goodness, kindness, faith, mildness, obedience, continence, chastity [var: Vulg. Gal 5:22–23 *caritas, gaudium, pax, patientia, benignitas, bonitas, longanimitas, mansuetudo, fides, modestia, continentia, castitas*; no *obedientia*] [n: (Gal. V)]*.

How good and useful a tree in the garden of the Lord, producing such kinds of fruits, and, rejoicing for its fruitfulness, proclaiming: *But I, as a fruitful olive tree [1024C] in the house of the Lord [var: Vulg. Ps 51:10 *in domo Dei*, "in the house of God"] [n: (Psal. LI)]*. The cultivator of this tree is assuredly the Holy Spirit, who takes such fruits to himself for compounding the ointments with which the beloved's breasts are fragrant, which are conferred on those who shall have been anointed with these ointments and can say with the Apostle: *We are the good odour of Christ unto God in every place [var: Vulg. 2Cor 2:15 *in iis qui salvi fiunt*, "in them that are saved"] [n: (II Cor. II)]*; because to such is given the remission of sins and the distribution of graces.

But to the sweetness of ointments of this kind, seasoned from the aforesaid kinds of virtues, no human reason makes progress without the aid of grace; because man, although capable of reason, begins or perfects nothing holy, nothing reasonable, unless that compounder of those same ointments, the Holy Spirit, works it by his interior unction, as John says of him to his hearers: [1024D] And *the unction* *10* *of him will teach you concerning all things [var: Vulg. 1Jo 2:27 *docet*, "teacheth"] [n: (I Joan. II)]*.
