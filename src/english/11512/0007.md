## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

Such an unction, assuredly, is the true medicine of the inner man and the strongest antidote against the diseases of the soul, by which it heals the soul wounded by the wound of sin and quickens her, enlightens the mind, rouses her to merit the gifts of divine grace, and lastly pervades her with such vigour of this sweetness that, forgetting the things behind and stretching forth to those before, she raises herself even above herself, saying with the Apostle: *But our conversation is in heaven [n: (Philip. III)]*, where, liberty from all misery having been attained, she may be satisfied *from the breasts of the consolation of God,* [var: Vulg. Is 66:11 *ab ubere consolationis ejus*, "from the breast of her consolation"] and may feast from the entrance of his glory [n: (Isa. LXVI)].

The breasts of this beloved can also, not unfittingly, be taken as the teaching and the working of our Lord [1025A] Jesus Christ, by which, in his humanity, he made himself known to the world with the best ointments of the virtues, fragrant now as well: then indeed nourishing the first-fruits of the new faith with the milk of teaching and confirming them by the power of working, but now strengthening the fullness of that same faith as with the solid food of teaching, and forming it by the example of working to the perfection of an honourable life.

These *breasts,* assuredly, fragrant with the ointment of so great a mercy and grace, *are better than wine* by far, that is, than the austerity of the royal ceremonies, or of things done: as truth is better than the shadow, the quickening spirit than the killing letter: *For the law,* as the Apostle says, *added nothing to perfection* [var: Vulg. Hbr 7:19 *adduxit*, "brought"]; but *all things happened to them in figure; and they are written for our correction, upon whom the ends of the ages are come [n: (I Cor. X)]*. Or [1025B] they are better, or rather incomparable, than wine, that is, than the pleasure of worldly doctrines, which, when they infect their hearers with the ointment of carnal delight, drag them to the death of the soul; and thus, from the opposite side, as much as the soul advances toward thy grace by the consolation of thy breasts, O beloved, so much does she fall away from that grace of thine by the deadly concoction of those others.

*Thy name is oil poured out: therefore the young maidens have loved thee.*

They who behold me wonder, O beloved, that I praise thee so, that I proclaim so great a grace of thy mercy and sweetness, that I desire thy countenance, thy embraces, with such avidity; and they long to know of what name thou art, or of what dignity, whom I esteem so highly, judge so highly; to whom, lest they excuse themselves on the ground of ignorance, because [1025C] thy name has not been announced to them, I say that *thy name* is not hidden, is not concealed, but rather manifest, and as it were *oil poured out,* whose nature is that when it is poured out it spreads the more.

How, then, has thy name been poured out and spread abroad? Not as once thou wast *known in Judaea* only, and only *in Israel great is thy name [n: (Psal. LXXV)]*, but as thou sayest through another prophet *11*: *From the rising of the sun even to the going down, great is thy name among the nations [n: (Malach. I)]*. *The name which is above every name, so that in thy name every knee should bow, of those in heaven, on earth and under the earth [n: (Philip. II)]*. *And there is no other name under heaven given to men, through [1025D] which we must be saved [n: (Act. IV)]*.

And that I may draw them up with me to higher things, that they may understand and know why I so greatly desire thy kiss, why I delight in the sweetness of thy breasts, let them hear something further, and let them weigh why I have said, *thy name is oil poured out.* For oil is borne above all liquids. What, then, is understood by oil, save charity, which excels all the virtues? For no virtue can be called a virtue, or be one, without charity; because where charity is not, there is no virtue. For *if I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, and have not charity, I am become as sounding brass or a tinkling cymbal; and if I should have prophecy and should know all mysteries and all knowledge, and if I should have faith so that I could remove mountains, and [1026A] have not charity, it profiteth me nothing; and if I should distribute all my goods to feed the poor, and if I should deliver my body to be burned, and have not charity, I am nothing [var: Vulg. 1Cor 13:2–3 ends v. 2 *nihil sum* and v. 3 *nihil mihi prodest*; v. 2 *omnem fidem*] [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*.

Behold of how great virtue, of how great excellence, do you think, charity is, so that without it neither prophecy, nor any knowledge, nor faith, nor almsgiving, nor martyrdom is anything? And what is this charity? Surely thou art it, O beloved, as that beloved one of thine testifies, cleaving to thy holy kiss, and, reclining on thy breast, drinking in the sweetness of thy breasts; he, I say, the heavenly eagle, flying with the wings of holy contemplation into thy very secrets, and directing the keenness of his mind upon thee, the sun of justice. And what does he say? *God is charity [n: (I Joan. IV)]*. [1026B] Thou, then, art called charity and art charity, O beloved, because charity made thee take on flesh and come into this world, and be humbled for human salvation even unto the death of the cross; and *for his exceeding charity with which God loved us [n: (Ephes. II)]*. Thee, his Son, he sent in the likeness of sinful flesh, that of sin thou mightest condemn sin [n: (Rom. VIII)], made a curse for our sakes, that thou mightest free us from the curse [n: (Gal. II)]. And how has this name of thine, the name of charity, been poured out and spread abroad? *Like the ointment on the head, that ran down upon the beard, the beard of Aaron, that ran down to the skirt of his garment [n: (Psal. CXXXII)]*. Thou art Aaron, that is, the mountain of strength, in whom, namely in our head, the distinction of this charity abounded specially, with which *God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of exultation [1026C] above thy fellows [n: (Psal. XLIV; Hebr. I)]*, namely of that exultation with which thou didst exult both to have shown [sic: *ostendi se*] so great an abundance of thy charity and to have fulfilled the order of our redemption.

The beard of this head were the apostles, or apostolic men, into whom the grace of this ointment flowed down so abundantly that they cleaved to thee, as a beard *12* to the head, firmly, with the ardour of holy charity, even unto death.

*That ran down to the skirt of thy garment,* which thy faithful have known, from the first age of the world up to this one in which we now are, we *upon whom the ends of the ages are come [n: (I Cor. X)]*, who are the skirt, that is, the end, of this garment of thine; in all of whom, namely both the former and the present and the future, the strength of this [1026D] ointment, that is, of charity, has laboured and labours [sic: *labora*], and will labour even in the very end of the world under the persecution of Antichrist for thy name. *Thy name,* then, *is oil poured out,* because *thy charity is poured forth in our hearts, through the Holy Spirit who is given to us [n: (Rom. V)]*.

And *Therefore the young maidens have loved thee exceedingly.* [var: Vulg. Ct 1:2 *dilexerunt te*, without *nimis*] Because, she says, so great is the sweetness of thy breasts, so great the abundance of thy charity, that gentleness rather than harshness is found in thee, rather a cherishing madness [cj: *dementia*; read *clementia*, "clemency"] than a striking sentence, *therefore the young maidens have loved thee exceedingly.*

But who are these young maidens, assuredly faithful souls, removed indeed from the milk of infancy, but not yet having attained the solidity of perfection; therefore [1027A] still weak, and needing to be sustained by divine help; and on that account they have greatly loved thee, O beloved, and love thee, because they attend to this in thee, that they in no way flee from it, but rather by it may at length grow strong more and more to the sublimity of perfection.

Now it is customary in Holy Scripture for the growths of the mind to be designated by the growths of bodies; because, as men advance by nature to greater ages, so the faithful advance by grace to greater virtues of the soul; whence Peter exhorts his hearers to infancy, that is, to innocence of life, saying: *As newborn infants, desire the rational milk without guile, that thereby you may grow unto salvation [n: (I Petr. II)]*. In the Gospel also the Saviour, exhorting his disciples to purity [1027B] and simplicity of life: *Unless,* he says, *you be converted, and become as little children, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven [n: (Matth. XVIII)]*. And the Apostle: *In malice,* he says, *be children, that in understanding you may be perfect [var: Vulg. 1Cor 14:20 *sensibus autem perfecti estote*, "but in understanding be perfect"] [n: (I Cor. V)]*. The Psalmist too, desiring the youth of his soul to be renewed after the example of the eagle, *shall be renewed,* he says, *like the eagle's thy youth [n: (Psal. CII)]*. So that, stripping off the old man with his deeds, he may put on the new [n: (Colos. III)], who according to God is created in justice and holiness [n: (Ephes. IV)]. And in the book of Wisdom gravity of character is expressed by old age of mind, when it is said: *Old age is venerable, not that of long time, nor counted by the number of years; for the grey hairs are the understanding of a man, and a spotless life is old age [n: (Sap. IV)]*.

[1027C]Growth, then, can be divided in two ways, so that one speaks of *natural* growth and *affectual* growth. And natural growth is ascribed to every bodily creature which is moved naturally with vital vegetation according to its own property: *13* but affectual growth is granted in the present life to the rational creature alone, according to the measure of the giving grace, by the help of which grace it is promoted, against the vices of the flesh, by the strength of the spirit, to good things, and from good things to better.

But for the most part in divine Scripture natural growth is significative of affectual, as the Psalmist: *And it shall please,* he says, *God better than a young calf that bringeth forth horns and hoofs [n: (Psal. LXVIII)]*. For if thou understandest it according to the letter, what care has God [1027D] for an irrational beast naturally bringing forth horns and hoofs? but if thou considerest the young calf more deeply according to the mystical sense, thou canst take it as the mind newly converted to the Lord, and budding with the flower of good action, which the Apostle calls the new man, saying: *Put on the new man, him who is renewed unto knowledge, according to the image of him that created him [n: (Col. III)]*. Enumerating within the constitutive parts of which man: *Put on,* he says, *as the elect of God, the bowels of mercy, benignity, humility, modesty, patience, bearing with one another and forgiving one another [n: (ibid.)]*:

The horns of this young calf, that is, of the new man, can not absurdly, as I judge, be taken as constancy of mind and fortitude of spirit, or the spirit of fortitude, [1028A] resisting vices and sins and winnowing the adversaries of this new man

Further, as for its hoofs, with which it stirs the earth, nothing prevents thee from taking them as contempt of the world and love of God, by which, casting away all earthly things, it may bring its body into servitude, subjecting it to the spirit, so that it may co-operate and labour together with it by consent in the discipline of good works; so that in the future renewal, as it was a sharer of the labour, so it may become a sharer also of the reward; which is for the new man himself to be presented with a double stole, namely with the felicity of body and of soul.

Or understand the horns and hoofs of this calf as the sublime and special transports of the theoretic life; because, as horns and hoofs proceed indeed from the flesh but go beyond the flesh, so the theoric man, though he is in [1028B] the flesh, yet transcends carnal affections in mind, saying with the Apostle: *For walking in the flesh, we do not walk according to the flesh [var: Vulg. 2Cor 10:3 *militamus*, "we war"] [n: (II Cor. X)]*. And again: *But our conversation is in heaven [n: (Philip. III)]*; and elsewhere: *Whether we be sober, or whether we fall out of our mind, it is to God [var: Vulg. 2Cor 5:13 *sive enim mente excedimus Deo: sive sobrii sumus, vobis*, "whether we be transported in mind, it is to God; or whether we be sober, it is for you"] [n: (II Cor. V)]*.

But this calf, still not yet having horns and hoofs, but bringing them forth, is young, and has not yet advanced to the strength of a bull; because the believing [sic: *credentes*] soul of each one, already seizing the rudiments of holy newness, but not yet having attained the perfection of the holy Fathers, is called not *adolescens*, a youth, but by diminution *adolescentula*, a young maiden, because on account of her weakness she cannot rise to the sublimity of perfection, but brings forth the first little flowers of good working, [1028C] as far as she is able, until, growing up little by little, she shows the fruits of the virtues now full and ripe.
