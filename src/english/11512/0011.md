## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

Therefore, for that gracious bringing in of the grace of God into the ineffable subtlety of the divine mysteries, [1038C] the holy soul and bride of Christ ought to render to God the thanksgiving that is due, by the jubilation of heart and mouth and by the showing of works, which is to take oil with the lamps and to go out to meet the bridegroom, saying: *We will exult and we will rejoice in thee,* because we bear the gladness of good works in our conscience, and this in thee, when we ascribe every good that we do not to our own strength but to thy grace. But after that bringing in, gloriously celebrated, for which she gives thanks by exulting and rejoicing, she adds the cause of this exultation and gladness, namely the *remembrance* *of the divine breasts, more desirable than wine.* For what would that exultation or gladness of the religious mind be, thinking and working things worthy of God, if it had not [1038D] the remembrance of these breasts, *breasts better than wine,* that is, sweeter than every pleasure of the world? But of what breasts? Surely either of the two Testaments, of which the whole of Holy Scripture consists, or of the two precepts of charity, on which *the whole law dependeth, and the prophets [n: (Matth. XXII)]*. Truly, meditation on the Holy Scriptures begets the remembrance of the divine commandments, according to which the rule of living rightly consists, to whose rightness the life of each is directed: *in thy justifications,* says the Psalmist, *I will meditate; I will not forget thy words [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*.

For how can anyone be or be called just unless he is mindful of the things which God has commanded, and above all of these breasts, namely the two precepts [1039A] of the charity of God and of neighbour, without which, as the Apostle says, neither kinds of tongues, nor prophecy, nor the knowledge of all mysteries, nor faith, nor almsgiving, nor martyrdom [n: (I Cor. XII)], nor any virtue will be able to profit anyone? for *whosoever shall keep the whole law, but shall offend in one precept of charity, is become guilty of all [var: Vulg. Jac 2:10 *offendat autem in uno*, "but offend in one point", without *praecepto charitatis*] [n: (Jac. II)]*.

Wherefore the bride of Christ, wishing to show that the whole rightness of her life consists in a remembrance of this kind, goes on, saying: *The right love thee;* as if she should say: the proof of rightness *25* is love; because if they are right, they love thee; if they do not love, neither are they right. But because they conceive this rightness from the remembrance indeed of thy precepts, but take it up perfectly from the love of thee, let it rightly be said that [1039B] *mindful of thy breasts, better than wine, the right love thee.*

For this middle little part of the sentence, which is *mindful of thy breasts, better than wine,* can not absurdly be joined, by a subdistinction, to the parts placed on either side of it; so that it may be uttered either in this way: *We will exult and we will rejoice in thee, mindful of thy breasts, better than wine.* Or in this way: *We will exult and we will rejoice in thee:* and here, a subdistinction having been made, let there be added after, *mindful of thy breasts, better than wine, the right love thee.* For those who have remembrance of the commandments of God, and settle the manner of their life according to them, become right; and none but the right can love, nor are they right unless they love.

Moreover, it delights not a little the eyes of our inner [1039C] man to consider the most beautiful progression of this canticle, from that place where it is said: *Draw me after thee,* up to this little sentence, *the right love thee.* For the order of this progression becomes a kind of sequel to the kiss that was asked for.

For here that kindly sevenfold Spirit, as by certain ascents of the heart, intimates the sevenfold graces of his condescension, by which the holy soul, the bride of Christ, may rise little by little, until she advances to the inestimable bridal chamber of her Lord, her bridegroom and king, to be coupled to him with eternal charity. For first she is drawn by the compassion of God; then, as it were moving her foot to the race, she offers the first-fruits of a good intention; and soon, having applied zeal, she merits to be brought into [1039D] the storerooms of God, and when she has entered, she exults in understanding and rejoices in believing and working.

Further, from this active life she passes to a certain beginning of the contemplative life by meditation and remembrance of the precepts of God, and directing to their line whatever strength is still in her, she attains the perfection of rightness; and so at last, all cares and business set aside, cleaving to God through contemplation, she is so near to him by burning charity that she can say: *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.*

VERSE 4.-- *I am black but beautiful, O daughters of Jerusalem, as the tents of Cedar, as the skins of Solomon.*

Universally indeed the whole Church, and particularly the individual Churches, and singly also each soul beloved of God, have endured adversities, and it is endured [1040A] from the contrary side, because the qualities of the good and of the evil have been set against them, who are divided into diverse and contrary pursuits, of which parts the one is called the heavenly city, the other the earthly; the one God's, the other the devil's.

Of these, moreover, *26* two women bore the type: the one free, who was Sara; the other a bondwoman, who was Agar, the mother of Ismael, whose son was Cedar, which is interpreted *darkness,* to which is likened that blackness which the Church, or each holy soul, says she has according to the judgment of those who live evilly, whereas right consideration judges this very thing to be not blackness but rather beauty. And what is this blackness? Plainly persecution, which is inflicted on the sons of the bondwoman; which is therefore thought black by the perverse and by those who do not believe the good things [1040B] to come, because it is heavy and troublesome, and because it makes those who suffer it as it were abject and wretched, whereas they rather become glorious, and inwardly, because of the hope of rewards to come, fair and beautiful with spiritual joy; for the sons of that city which is signified by Sara, who was free, are persecuted by the sons of the other city, which is shown by Agar the bondwoman; now indeed by inflicting evils bodily, but now by afflicting the soul spiritually through malice.

Lastly, that play which went on between the son of the free woman and the son of the bondwoman the Apostle calls persecution; as he says: *He that was born according to the flesh persecuted him that was after the spirit; so also now [n: (Gal. IV)]*: because assuredly the sons of the bondwoman persecute the sons of the free woman, [1040C] not only by carnal persecution but also by spiritual, by which they try to deprive them of that true liberty with which Christ has made them free [n: (ibid.)], that they may not serve sin, as these do who are the sons of the bondwoman. Which persecution is surely as much heavier than every bodily persecution as that spiritual portion is better than the carnal, the soul, namely, than the body.

Whence, indignant at such persecution, that free woman and mother of the free says: *Cast out,* she says, *the bondwoman and her son, for the son of the bondwoman shall not be heir with the son of the free woman [n: (Gen. XXI)]*; because surely the servants of sin do not belong to that heavenly inheritance to which the free of justice belong. The Church, then, or any [1040D] holy soul, makes light of persecution of this kind, inasmuch as, being established upon the rock of faith, she is often shaken only by the storms of the persecution [sic: *persecutionem*] of outward men, but is not thrown down, having patience and awaiting glory and honour and incorruption unto eternal life.

Let her say, then: *I am black but beautiful:* black indeed to those who judge of me by reason of temporal affliction, but to you, O daughters of Jerusalem, who know and believe the good things to come, *beautiful,* because the humbler I am through temporal affliction, the more I am exalted by the greater beauty of the virtues. But how am I judged *black*? *As the tents of Cedar.* Cedar was the son of Ismael, of whom the divine voice says: *This man,* it says, *shall be a slave man [var: Vulg. Gn 16:12 *ferus homo*, "a wild man"], and the hand of all against him; over against the country of his brethren he shall pitch his tents [n: (Gen. XVI)]*.

[1041A]But why did she not say *as the tents* of Ismael, but *of Cedar?* Because, namely, Ismael is interpreted *the hearing of God;* but Cedar *darkness,* and *27* this name of darkness agrees better with the opinion of those who think *light darkness, and darkness light [n: (Isa. V)]*, and, according to the likeness of his father, that is, Ismael, who was a slave and pitched his tents not among his brethren but over against his brethren, they judge the abasement of this world and the labour of true philosophy to be a certain wildness and contrariety.

But how *beautiful? as the skins of Solomon; beautiful,* she says, *as the skins;* not of any other very mighty man, but *of Solomon.* For Solomon, as he was wiser and richer than all the men of his time, so also he had his tents more beautiful and [1041B] more adorned, of choice and elaborately wrought skins.

Now skins are of dead animals; but what are the skins of dead animals but the virtues and examples of the Fathers who went before? And from the virtues and examples of the holy Fathers who went before, as from skins shining with varied beauty, I, compacted as it were into one skin, the elaborate of my king, namely of the true Solomon, that is, of the *peaceable* one, our Lord Jesus Christ, making peace by his blood with all things that are in heaven and on earth [n: (Coloss. I)]; therefore the tent; so that it may worthily be said of me by the grace of God; that is, the tabernacle which God pitched, and not man.

[1041C]VERSE 5.-- *Do not consider me that I am brown, because the sun hath discoloured me: the sons of my mother have fought against me, they have set me the keeper in the vineyards; my own vineyard I have not kept.*

Beautiful am I as that which I am, and I am the comeliness of the skin of the true Solomon, which you, O daughters of Jerusalem, know well, who daily proclaim that same beauty of mine to the praise of God, of whose grace that beauty is.

Wherefore, *do not consider me that I am brown, because the sun hath discoloured me:* do not, I say, consider my outward disposition and habit, which I have contracted by the burning of the sun, that is, by the heat of temptation and of worldly affliction, or from the kindling charity of the true sun, of whom it is written: *But unto you that fear my name the sun of justice shall arise* [1041D] [n: (Malach. IV)], who has as it were burnt me with so great a fervour of his love, as with an inseparable accident, that *neither tribulation, nor distress, nor persecution, nor famine, nor nakedness, nor peril, nor the sword can separate me from his charity [n: (Rom. VIII)]*. Such a discolouring, surely, is the great and glorious beauty and adornment of my inner man.

Further. Sometimes a holy man does certain things which those who discern badly interpret in the sinister sense, since they do not know the intention of the one working, which God alone judges. *For there are right ways which seem crooked to men [var: Vulg. Pr 16:25 *Est via quae videtur homini recta*, "there is a way which seemeth to a man right"] [n: (Prov. XVI)]*; just as, on the contrary, crooked ones which seem right; because they do not heed that eye of intention according to which every work is either *28* set upright or made crooked. Let the faithful soul, then, say [1042A] after this manner, *I am black but beautiful;* as if she should say: Black indeed I seem in some act of mine to those who, not having the single eye, take it in the contrary sense, whereas I am rather beautiful because of my single eye, from which my whole body is full of light [n: (Matth. VI)]. As you too judge, O daughters of Jerusalem, who look upon me with a single and dovelike eye: *do not therefore consider me that I am brown, because the sun hath discoloured me:* that is, do not think my body, that is, my work, to be dark; because surely what proceeds from the luminous grace of the true sun is wholly full of light, not having any part of darkness.
