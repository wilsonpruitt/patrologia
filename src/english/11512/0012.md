## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Further. ⟦s2⟧ Any soul whatever, turned to the Lord through penitence, seems indeed, from the [1042B] austerity of penitence, as it were black, because of her sad and mournful habit: but because *this sorrow is according to God, and worketh penitence unto salvation that is steadfast [n: (II Cor. VII)]*; and: *Blessed are they that mourn, for they shall be comforted [n: (Matth. V)]*, she is beautiful; having it in hope that, according to the prophet, she may receive from the Lord *a crown for ashes, the oil of joy for mourning, a mantle of praise for the spirit of grief [n: (Isa. LXI)]*. ⟦s3⟧ And as a certain wise man says: *The heart that knoweth the bitterness of its own soul, in its joy the stranger shall not intermeddle [n: (Prov. XIV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Let such a one say, then: *I am black but beautiful, O daughters of Jerusalem, as the tents of Cedar, as the skins of Solomon;* because from the blackness of penitence I seem at first to have belonged to the sons of the bondwoman, whose works are of darkness, [1042C] but afterwards that very penitence has conferred on me such beauty that I now belong to the sons of the free woman, who, by the mortification of the carnal life, know both how to put together and how to be the tents of the true Solomon [sic: *Salomanis*]. ⟦s5⟧ Wherefore *do not consider me that I am brown, because the sun hath discoloured me,* that is, do not despise my penitence, because that true sun made me take it up, who has pierced and enlightened my heart with the rays of his grace and mercy, so that by repenting I might recover that most general good, namely God himself, whom by sinning I had lost.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The chanting of the present Canticle not unfittingly looks to you, most holy sisters, who are and ought to be brides of Christ, in accordance with the quality of your purpose and order, because you display in your outward [1042D] habit a guise that is despised indeed, but preserve in the inward habit of the mind one beautiful to God because, as we believe, acceptable. ⟦s2⟧ Whence let the whole body of your holy society proclaim with a concordant and sweet harmony of heart and mouth; *I am black but beautiful, O daughters of Jerusalem, as the tents of Cedar, as the skins of Solomon: do not consider me that I am brown, because the sun hath discoloured me.* ⟦s3⟧ Which is to say in other words more expressly, *for we are made a spectacle to this world, and to angels, and to men [n: (I Cor. IV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ The sons of this world judge us black and misshapen, *as the tents of Cedar,* that is, as the gentiles who by the blackness of evils are dead both to God and to the world. ⟦s5⟧ But we, seeking neither the favour nor the glory *29* of the world, but carrying *oil in vessels* of our own [n: (Matth. XXV)], that is, the gladness of good [1043A] works in our consciences, are beautiful and fair in the eyes of our Bridegroom, *as the skins of Solomon,* that is, as those who, *bearing about the mortification of Jesus in our bodies [n: (II Cor. IV)]*, build in our hearts tabernacles beloved of him, of which he can say: *I will dwell in them and walk among them [n: (II Cor. VI)]*. ⟦s6⟧ Do not therefore consider us that we are brown, in that we do not have the passing and misshapen beauty of this world, because that true sun, who has shone upon us by his grace, has brought this about by the strong fervour of his love, so that, all the pomp of this corruptible life being spurned, we choose rather to please him in act and habit than the world. ⟦s7⟧ *The sons of my mother have fought against me, they have set me the keeper in the vineyards; my own vineyard I have not kept.*

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1043B]After the Church, or any holy soul, has set forth beforehand her outward discolouring and the cause of the discolouring, for *all* indeed *that will live godly in Christ shall suffer persecution [n: (II Tim. III)]*; she now adds the aggravation of the persecution, that she has suffered it and suffers it not only from strangers but also, which is heavier, from brothers. ⟦s2⟧ For those evils which are inflicted by strangers bring less pain, but those torments rage more fiercely which are inflicted by those of whose minds better was presumed.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Now the chanting of this little part seems to demand not a simple sense, because the Synagogue and the Church and the heavenly Jerusalem, the goodness or power of the Creator, can all be called equivocally by the name of Mother. [1043C] The same equivocation, too, embraces under the name of sons the apostles and the successors of the apostles, the Jews, heretics and the adverse powers. ⟦s2⟧ The fight of such sons, too, is not uniform, because it is taken both in the good sense and in the bad. ⟦s3⟧ And the several parts ought to have their several expositions.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For the primitive Church, which was from the Jews, suffered persecution from those Jews who did not believe; as we read in the Acts of the apostles, that *filled with zeal they laid hands on the apostles, and put them in the public prison;* and in another place: *When they had beaten them,* moreover, *they charged them that they should not speak in the name of Jesus [n: (Act. V)]*; and in that persecution in which Stephen suffered, it is written *that there was raised a great persecution [1043D] in the Church which was at Jerusalem, and they were all dispersed through the regions of Samaria [var: Vulg. Act 8:1 *per regiones Judaeae et Samariae*, "through the regions of Judea and Samaria"], except the apostles [n: (Act. VIII)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Since therefore both parts, that of the believers, namely, and that of the unbelievers, were sons of the Synagogue by nature or by race, but the unbelieving persecuted the faithful on account of the faith of Jesus Christ, that part of the believers, which was at that time the Church of Christ, could plainly say: *The sons of my mother have fought against me, they have set me the keeper in the vineyards, my own vineyard I have not kept* *30.* ⟦s2⟧ As if she should say in other words: When the Lord had commanded, saying: *Go ye not into the way of the gentiles, and into the cities of the Samaritans enter ye not, but go ye rather to the lost sheep of Israel [n: (Matth. X)]*, I, desiring to fulfil this precept, preached to the sons *of my mother* the faith which I received from the Lord and through which they [1044A] ought to be saved; but they, making light of my preaching, *fought against me* by words and deeds, and driving me from their borders, *set me the keeper in the vineyards,* that is, in the churches of the gentiles, who received the word of faith with joy. ⟦s3⟧ And while I was spending on them the diligence of my labour and keeping, I neglected to keep *my own vineyard,* that is, my own nation, because they despised my keeping and refused to receive the word of salvation: as one of my own said to them, *To you indeed it behoved [var: Vulg. Act 13:46 *Vobis oportebat primum*, "to you it behoved first"] the word of God to be spoken, but because you reject it and judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life [sic: judicati saeternae], behold, we turn to the gentiles [n: (Act. XIII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ For as the same says elsewhere: *By their offence, salvation to the gentiles, that they may be emulous of them [n: (Rom. XI)]*: and presently: *I would not have you ignorant, brethren, of this mystery, lest [1044B] you be wise in your own conceits, that blindness in part befalls Israel, until the fulness of the gentiles should come in, and so all Israel should be saved [sic: fierit] [n: (ibid.)]*.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Because of this contempt, then, the prophet foretold that this vineyard was to be left without a keeper: *And the daughter of Sion,* he says, *shall be left as a covert in a vineyard, and as a lodge in a garden of cucumbers, as a city that is laid waste [n: (Isa. I)]*; and these things indeed the primitive Church could say, whose mother was the synagogue, as also of the contrary part, of which she says that the sons of her mother have fought against her.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Further, the universal Church could and can say of the apostles and their successors: *The sons of my mother have fought against me, they have set me the keeper [1044C] in the vineyards, my own vineyard I have not kept.* ⟦s2⟧ For the heavenly Jerusalem is the mother of believers, because of the faith, hope and charity which they have towards her; because, while they hope and believe that they are made partakers of her blessedness, of which the saints who went before have been made partakers, they work and labour through the charity by which they love so great a good, so that they may be able to come to that heavenly fatherland: for the sake of which to live holily and justly, what else is it than to be spiritually begotten from her unto her?

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Her sons, too, were the apostolic ones: and apostolic men, who, while for the meriting of this most glorious fatherland they were afflicted with many labours and wearied even unto death, surely, by believing, hoping and loving, were themselves being born unto her, [1044D] and no less were being begotten, as with a mother's affection, by her who was the cause of this labour of theirs.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Such sons, then, fought against the Church when they intended to fight her out of her error, just as even now they daily fight every faithful soul out of vices unto virtue by words and examples. ⟦s2⟧ Did not *31* one of them fight in this manner against the churches, when he said to one of them: *What will you? shall I come to you with a rod, or in the spirit of meekness? ⟦s3⟧ It is absolutely heard that there is fornication among you, and such fornication as is not among the gentiles, so that one should have his father's wife [n: (I Cor. IV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ See how he first fought against these, whom, after he had beaten them out of this sin by rebuking, he relieves with a consoling letter: *Although I made you sorrowful,* he says, *by [1045A] the letter, I do not repent: though I did repent, seeing that the same letter (though but for a time) made you sorrowful: now I am glad; not because you were made sorrowful, but because you were made sorrowful unto penance: for you were made sorrowful according to God, that you might suffer damage by us in nothing [n: (II Cor. VII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ Writing also to another Church: *O senseless Galatians, who hath bewitched you, that you should not obey the truth? [n: (Gal. III.)]* While he fights against her in this manner, he fights her out of unbelief.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But what is it that these sons appoint as keepers in the vineyards those whom they assail in such a manner and fight out of vices, but that they teach and persuade them to keep the buds and fruits of the virtues? ⟦s2⟧ But those do not keep their own vineyard, because by the fighting of these men they have advanced to so great a keeping of the virtues that they now [1045B] neglect to keep the errors of their former custom, nay rather, by the grace of God, they drive them further from themselves by the spirit of fortitude. ⟦s3⟧ Whence the prophet says in the person of such: *I will stand upon my watch [n: (Habac. II)]*: he stands upon his watch who, through the skill of discipline, does not succumb to earthly desires but rises above them, so that, while he seeks the ever-standing eternity, everything that passes away may be beneath him.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ In yet another way the Church also cries out over the false brothers who rebel against her, heretics and schismatics, who, as long as they were in the Church and shared in the same sacraments, were thought to be sons of that heavenly Jerusalem which is the mother of the faithful, and to belong to the city of God; but after they were seen to blaspheme against the divinity [1045C] and the humanity of Christ, and to rend the holy unity, they were cast out of the inheritance of the sons, and it was proved that they had not truly been sons, but rather bastards; to such the Apostle says: *But if you be without discipline, whereof all sons are made partakers, then are you bastards, and not sons [n: (Hebr. XII)]*. ⟦s2⟧ Of these, too, John says: *Because they went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would no doubt have remained with us [n: (I Joan. II)]*. ⟦s3⟧ And the Lord through the prophet: *My sons,* he says, *are gone out from me, and they are not [n: (Jer. X)]*.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ At that time, then, when these sons, not legitimate but bastards, assailed those who were of right sonship by the assertion of a different faith, since heretical [1045D] princes favoured the assertors of the perverse faith, holy and catholic men, driven from their sees, suffered losses and exiles and many perils, because they preached the right dogma of faith and would not communicate with the heretics.
