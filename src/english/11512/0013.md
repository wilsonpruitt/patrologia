## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

Since, therefore, both were distinguished with ecclesiastical offices *32* and held the same sacraments, and for this reason were believed to belong to the bosom [sic: *grem um*] of one Mother, they were divided by the sole difference of faith, and that society of the wicked persecuted the catholic society; tainted by the heat of whose persecution, this society calls herself black, and says that the sun has discoloured her, the sun for whom she was contending, that he should be said to be equal to the Father in divinity, and of one substance, power and will with him, and in humanity should be believed true [1046A] man, which the contrary party blasphemously said was not so.

The catholic party, then, adds the cause of her discolouring, because, she says, *the sons of my mother have fought against me,* and since, under the persecution of such sons, not legitimate but bastards, that is, heretics, the party of sound faith was compelled to move to other sees and to leave its own cities, she adds that *they have set me the keeper in the vineyards, my own vineyard I have not kept;* for many Churches at that time, deprived of their own pastor, were stained by the heretics with perverse dogma; and holy priests, exiled, sowed the word of faith in foreign places, which they were prevented by the necessity of the times from doing in their own.

Otherwise. Since the mother of every creature is the goodness and [1046B] power of the Creator, for it was of his goodness that he made us when we were not; of his power, because he could do all things that he would [n: (Psal. CXIII)]; and since in all creation there are two rational ones, the angel and man, because of their sharing in reason, and since both were made by the same goodness and power, they seem to have as it were a certain fellowship of brotherhood, so that they are called sons of the same mother. But because that part of the angels which fell, seeing and envying the human creature rise by the grace of God to that glory from which it had been cast down, persecuted it in every way and laboured to destroy it, let man himself say in the person of the weak: *The sons of my mother have fought against me, they have set me the keeper in the vineyards, my own vineyard I have not [1046C] kept.*

While, he says, these rational spirits, the sons of my mother, fought against me, they compelled my frail mind to love earthly actions and to desert the keeping of my soul. For by vineyards, whose work is under the open sky, the occupations of outward actions also can be designated: for the more the mind is scattered abroad about earthly actions through the outward senses, the more the soul is neglected inwardly in the keeping of itself.

For since the soul is invisible, it is in no way touched by delight in bodily things, except that, cleaving to the body, it has the senses of that same body as certain openings for going out. For sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch are as it were certain ways of the mind, by which it may come abroad and desire those things which [1046D] are outside *33* its substance. For through these senses of the body, as through certain windows, the soul looks upon each outward thing, and looking, desires it: hence Jeremias says: *Death is come up through your windows, it is entered into your houses [var: Vulg. Jr 9:21 *per fenestras nostras … domos nostras*, "through our windows … our houses"] [n: (Jerem. IX)]*. For death comes up through the windows and enters the houses when concupiscence, coming through the senses of the body, enters the dwelling of the mind.

The adverse powers, then, make the mind that is busied with earthly actions careless of itself, while through the slackness of outward works they force it by no means to stand in the keeping of its own soul, so that it can deservedly say: *The sons of my mother have fought against me, they have set me the keeper in the vineyards, my own vineyard I have kept [var: Vulg. Ct 1:5 *vineam meam non custodivi*, "my own vineyard I have not kept"].* But because in all adversities [1047A] or perils brought upon her, not so much by strangers as by brothers, the Church universal or particular, or any faithful soul, is afflicted with a certain heat of doubt, because many hold the faith in name only, and it is not easy to discern who are truly faithful, she turns to the Lord and her bridegroom, and, begging his help for the grace of discernment of this kind, goes on with a prayer of entreaty, saying: *Show me, thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest in the midday.*

VERSE 6.-- *Show me, thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest in the midday, lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions.*

God is said to show in three ways, namely by thing, by understanding, by voice. By thing, indeed, when, the mind being doubtful, [1047B] he makes manifest by a sign of some power of his what is to be chosen or what is to be held; for in this manner, in order to restrain a seditious people from murmuring, when among the twelve rods which he receives from the several tribes, and commanded to be placed in the tabernacle of the covenant, the rod of Aaron alone, in the tribe of Levi, had blossomed and brought forth fruit [n: (Num. XVII)], in presage of the most noble succession to come, from which that singular birth through the Virgin Mary was to be accomplished, he showed by this sign that he had chosen Aaron himself with his offspring for the priesthood, to draw near in a special manner to offer sacrifices to God and to enter into the Holy of holies.

Further, he shows by understanding when, touching the mind of someone [1047C] with a hidden inspiration, he reveals to his understanding what better thing he should choose to do. Whence, when David the king, the name forewarning, was thinking to build a house for the Lord, it did not displease God, but rather pleased him; because, although he forbade him to do this in his own time, because he was *a man of blood* [n: (II Reg. XVI)], yet he did not disapprove the cause, nay rather judged it worthy of a reward so great, and of reverence, that he promised him that he would raise up a Son who should be exalted by the prerogative of so great *34* peace, innocence and wisdom, as to be fit to accomplish such a work. Moreover, God, wishing to reward this good will of his, which was pleasing to him: *Foretelling,* he says, *I foretell to thee that the Lord will build thee a house [n: (I Reg. VII)]*. So then, that God might show that what [1047D] he had shown him through the understanding was pleasing to him, he not only did not forbid what he had thought, but also, that that very house might become of great esteem and power in the whole world, he approached it with his grace.

But God shows by voice, now indeed by himself, now through others: by himself indeed, as he spoke to Abraham and Moses and the other prophets; but through others, as when, first through the prophets themselves, then through the apostles and the other believers, to whom he committed his things to be announced to others, he showed what was of his will, what was of their salvation.

Again, according to these ways, God is said to show in four ways, namely sometimes by thing and understanding, sometimes by thing and voice, very often by understanding and voice; but sometimes all at once, both by thing and by understanding [1048A] and by voice. For he shows by thing and understanding when, namely, he foreshows something by the showing of things, and at once foreshows through the understanding to him to whom he shows it. Hence it is that when the diversities and qualities of the kingdoms to come were shown to Daniel through the diverse likenesses of beasts, some things he did not understand except by the teaching of an angel; but some, after the things were seen, he understood at once by himself, as in a certain place, after the likenesses of things were shown to him, he at once adds: *And I,* he says, *understood,* and in another place: *A word was revealed to Daniel surnamed Balthasar, and a true word, and great strength: and he understood the word [n: (Dan. VIII et X)]*.

But he is said to show by thing and voice when he foretells something [1048B] by a thing and pronounces by voice what it signifies; as when he had given Peter the astonishing catch of fishes, he forthwith opened the cause of this miracle: *Fear not,* he says, *from henceforth thou shalt catch men [n: (Luc. VIII)]*.

Further, he shows by understanding and voice when he gives someone [sic: *alicu*] to understand his will, and proves by an open voice that he has understood this by his inspiration. Hence it is that Jacob, when about to go down into Egypt, having understood that it had not been done without the will and disposition of God that Joseph should be promised, God not having been consulted about this, as the Scripture relates: *He set out with all his house, and came to the well of the oath, and there, having slain victims to the God of his father, he heard him in a vision saying to him: Jacob, Jacob; who [1048C] answered: Here I am; and he said: Go down into Egypt; fear not, and I will make thee there a great nation [n: (Gen. XXXI)]*. Behold, what *35* he had understood to be of the divine will, the divine voice also attested that it had judged this to him through the understanding.

But he seems to judge all at once, both by thing and by understanding and by voice, when he intimates something by some foregoing sign, by the causation of which sign set beneath him the capable sense of the one seeing weighs through the Spirit of understanding, which the author of that cause confirms also by a voice following after. Lastly, when Isaias, fearing to take up the office of preaching, said: *Woe is me, because I have held my peace [sic: tacu], because I am a man of polluted lips, and I dwell in the midst of a people that hath polluted lips [n: (Isa. VI)]*; by a sign given him by God, which he shows, saying: *And one of the seraphim flew to me, [1048D] and in his hand a live coal, which he had taken with the tongs from the altar, and he touched my lips, saying: Behold, I have touched [var: Vulg. Is 6:7 *tetigit os meum … Ecce tetigit hoc labia tua*, "touched my mouth … Behold, this hath touched thy lips"] thy lips with this, and thy iniquity shall be taken away, and thy sin shall be cleansed,* he understood the condescension of the grace calling him; and when the Lord said, *whom shall I send, and who shall go for us?* he reverently attended to the divine will, and presently, offering himself humbly and dutifully, *behold me,* he said, *send me [n: (Isa. VI)]*. And the Lord, what he had foreshown by the thing and had opened through the understanding, proved also by voice, so as to appoint the prophet to the office of preaching: *Go,* he says, *and thou shalt say to this people: Hear, you that hear me, and do not understand, and see the vision, and do not know [n: (ibid.)]*.

In some one, then, of the ways set down above, the Church, or any soul pleasing to God, asks that there be shown [1049A] to her the grace of the needful discernment, where she can and ought to recognize the well-pleasing dwelling of the divine condescension. *Show,* she says, *me, thou whom my soul loveth.* Not at random, she says, not lightly, and as though it should hold but little weight, is this showing of thine to be taken, O Lord; and therefore grant the gift of this grace not to just anyone, but to me, who love thee; because to the perception of this subtlety only the power of contemplative charity avails.

And what wilt thou show? Namely, *where thou feedest, where thou liest in the midday.* Who are those whom God feeds, but these whom he himself leads forth and sets out in the green places of the virtues, where they may gather the diverse kinds of holiness, whose wholesomeness may confer health on the soul, drive away diseases, and make a man effective [1049B] unto every good work. But what these kinds are, of such great vigour that they make a man so strong, so fair, so glorious, is clearer than light: namely, humility, patience, obedience, chastity, cleanness of body and soul, incorruption of mind and mortification of the flesh, contempt of the world and the fear of God, and finally the very charity of God and of neighbour, without which the compounding of all these kinds will be of no efficacy. These things are for a judgment to one desiring to know who and of what kind are those whom God feeds, *36* namely, that they are the sheep of that true Shepherd, of whom he himself says: *My sheep hear my voice, and I* the Lord *know them [n: (Joan. X)]*, so that he proves those to be his sheep who hear his voice, and they are sheep for this reason, because they hear, whose such [1049C] hearing and being is his knowing; just as, on the contrary, those who do not hear are not sheep either, since they are rather *ravening wolves under the clothing of sheep,* of whom he says that *by their fruits you shall know them [n: (Matth. VII)]*, whose not hearing and not being is his not knowing; if, as he himself says to such: *I know you not; depart from me, you cursed, into everlasting fire [n: (Matth. XXV)]*.
