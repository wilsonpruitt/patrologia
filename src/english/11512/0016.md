## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

[1056D]For he truly knows himself who weighs with subtle searching how far he excels every visible creature in that he was created after the likeness of God, which likeness is regarded not in the body but in the mind, according to the prerogative of a reason that understands *44* and discerns; how the creature too, serving his use, is subject to him; that his bodily substance is stretched upward as a kind of reminder to the mind, which ought to despise earthly things and raise itself to heavenly ones; that God so washed his nature that he deigned to shed his blood to redeem it; lastly, that he predestined that same nature to so great a glory that he [1057A] decreed to make him at some time partaker of his immortality, unchangeableness and eternity, and therefore made him capable of receiving all uprightness, virtue and discipline; and so from this knowledge of himself, as by the light too of a certain day [sic: *dici*], he is led [sic: *dicitur*] to desiring and obtaining justice. As, on the contrary, he is drawn to injustice by a certain blindness of mind, as by the darkness of a certain night, when he neither knows nor can consider in himself all those things set down above, which is to be ignorant of oneself. Knowledge of self, then, is as it were a certain good that disposes to justice, as ignorance of self also is a certain evil that deprives of that same justice. The Bridegroom, then, that is, Christ, chiding his bride, the Church or any holy soul you please, who had complained, who suffered persecution not only from strangers but also from her brothers, and that it was therefore very difficult [1057B] to discern among the companions of the bridegroom who are true, who false, chiding her for faint-heartedness of this kind, and because, as if she were forsaken, she did not show the largess of the grace of God in herself: *If thou know not thyself,* he says, *O fairest among women, go forth*; if, he says, thou dost not at all take the consideration of thyself, and knowest not that thou wast made after my likeness, that I made thee capable of reason, so that thou mayest understand what thou oughtest to seek and what to flee, that I redeemed thee with my blood, and among the synagogues of divers doctrines, or the other souls, made thee alone fair without spot and wrinkle, both by the washing of baptism and by the special gift of my sacraments, and also by the distribution of divers graces; that I made thee such in this manner that rightly [1057C] thou oughtest not to be troubled, but rather to be comforted, and to glory in me, since it is made impossible that thou be utterly forsaken, whom I have consecrated to myself by so great a condescension of my grace and mercy; if, he says, thou art ignorant of this and refusest to know it, *go forth,* that is, go out of doors from my company and from the fellowship of my secret, because *every one that is ignorant shall be ignored, and there is no participation of light with darkness [n: (I Cor. XIV; II Cor. VI)]*, *and go away after the steps of the flocks;* that is, imitate the acts of the peoples who are not one flock, nor one fold, because they have not one shepherd, since, cutting themselves off from the unity of the faith, they follow divers masters of manifold errors. *And feed thy kids,* that is, nourish and cherish thy carnal and wanton acquaintances, *or thy kids,* that is, sinners made [1057D] rotten by thy word and example, and to be set at the left hand in the judgment; *45* *beside the tents of the shepherds,* that is, beside the conventicles of erring masters, who have many tents, because they set up divers synagogues on account of the various sects of doctrines, departing from that one tent of mine in which is *one Lord, one faith, one baptism [n: (Ephes. IV)]*, and there is no schism in the catholic unity.

Now this voice of the Bridegroom is not that of one commanding but of one foretelling, because, according to his own voice: *No man can serve two masters [n: (Matth. VI)]*, that is, God and the world, nor love God and the world at once; and by the voice of John it is said: *for every one that loveth the world, the charity of the Father is not in him [n: (I Joan. II)]*. Wherefore, if to love the world is not to love God, but [1058A] to love God is to be just, surely to love the world is to be unjust. Now two contraries cannot be at one and the same time together in one and the same thing, so that anyone could be at once just and unjust. Again, in one and the same thing, yet at different times, it is possible for two contraries to be, so that now someone doing something good is declared just, but at another time, sinning, unjust. Further, no one can be called definitely at once just and unjust at one and the same time, as we have already said; which is taught from the words of the Gospel, where it is said to certain men: *Either make the tree good and its fruit good, or make the tree evil and its fruit evil [n: (Matth. XII)]*; as if he said: Because you cannot be called definitely both good and evil at once at one and the same time, it is necessary that [1058B] one of these, existing in you, destroy the other, that is, either goodness malice, or malice goodness. This indeed may be said according to the state of the inward man, who is always set either in good or in evil; but if you consider the whole man, who consists of soul and body, this rule of the dialecticians fails, because two contraries can be together at one and the same time in one and the same thing. For good and evil are contraries, but man and sinner are two things. And man indeed, according to substance, is a certain good, *for all things that God made were very good [n: (Gen. II)]*; but the sinner is a certain evil, which man himself made. And these two contraries are in one and the same at one and the same time, because this, that [1058C] he is man, is good, that he is a sinner, evil.

The Bridegroom, then, addresses the bride more severely, as one wandering and uncertain and less confident, and, showing a certain thing with no mean between, from this one proposition in which he says: *If thou know not thyself, O fairest among women, go forth, and go away after the steps of the flocks,* he teaches that one of the two must necessarily be chosen, namely, that either, taking up by knowledge of herself the good of justice, she cleave specially to God with contemplative charity, or, falling by ignorance of herself into the evil of injustice, she depart from it, by loving the world, through the privative action. for the evil of injustice deprives man of the good of justice.

*46* The Bridegroom, therefore, says to the bride, that is, Christ to each faithful soul, that if she has sunk herself into such blindness of heart that she neither will nor can [1058D] recognize how gracious God has endowed her above others with beauty of mind for considering and understanding the purpose of the divine goodness in her, she is to go forth from the secret place of her mind and go away by the broad and spacious way of erring peoples, where she may nourish carnal motions according to the doctrine of masters who teach contrary things, and who will not have the tabernacle of the Lord, in which are the golden urn having the manna, and the rod of Aaron, and the tables of the testament, because in it are preached and venerated the divinity and humanity of Christ, and the priestly dignity, and the sublimity of the divine commandments.

For heretics or schismatics have many tents, on account of the various doctrines by which they seduce their followers, and they think their assembly [1059A] to be a church, when they are a synagogue of Satan. But there is no place of salvation outside the catholic Church, nor are other churches to be spoken of besides this one, which, although it is divided into many particular churches, yet because of the unity of the faith is one holy universal Church. Whence also in this same Canticle, when the Bridegroom had said that *there are sixty queens, and eighty concubines, and of young maidens there is no number,* he added: *One also is my dove, my perfect one, she is the one of her mother, the chosen of her that bore her [var: Vulg. Ct 6:8 *Una est columba mea … electa genetrici suae*, "one is my dove … chosen to her that bore her"] [n: (Cant. VI)]*. Hence it is that the first Shepherd himself of the same Church, not yet having attained perfect knowledge, when, the glory of the Lord having been seen on the mountain, he said: *Lord, it is good for us to be here; let us make here three tabernacles, one for thee, one for Moses, and [1059B] one for Elias [n: (Matth. XVII)]*, was not heard, nor was such counsel of his approved [sic: *approba um*], because neither in the law nor in prophecy was any Church to be set up apart, but that of the New Testament, in which the law and the prophets are summed up, was alone and one throughout the whole world the holy and universal Church to be received and venerated. For the Gospel is nothing other than the law and the prophets announce as future by things and by words; the Gospel represents it as already done. Whence Ezechiel, in the vision of the glory of God: *The appearance,* he says, *of the wheels and the works, as if it were a wheel in the midst of a wheel [n: (Ezech. I)]*. The wheel in the midst of a wheel, namely, is the New Testament within the Old Testament, because what the Old Testament prefigured, this the New Testament [1059C] displayed; and the exposition of the Old Testament is the New Testament. For the Old and New Testament are one, and hold themselves a judge, because what that one represents in figure this one represents in truth; and what that one shows as to be done, this one shows as already done, so that they are one tabernacle of God.

*47* The Church of God, therefore, neither ought nor can be ignorant of herself, she who is adorned with so great a beauty [sic: *pulchritudinem*] of mysteries and sacraments, whose whole praise is the most seemly variety itself of venerable ordinances.

Further, singly, any soul you please ought not to be ignorant of herself, lest, while she does not at all weigh in herself the gifts of her Creator, by flowing away through unlawful things she become estranged from him. But if she considers any good things in herself through his grace, [1059D] she ought to attend to them gratefully and seemly, and to attend to them with zeal for good action, lest she be rebuked for the buried talent, but rather be presented with the reward of eternal recompense for the talent multiplied. Accordingly you too, O most holy [sic: *santissimae*] sisters, who are gathered together in the house of discipline, be not ignorant of yourselves, but rather look diligently about you, and attend to what or of what kind your bridegroom has made you: how he has set you apart from other men like wheat from the chaff, how he has fitted you specially to himself by the beauty of a singular way of life, with how manifold an adornment of virtues he has decked you, when, with pursuits alternating on this side and that, now by chastity, now by continence, now by obedience, now by humility, now by the frequency of prayers and vigils, you make a spectacle delightful to God and his saints.

[1060A]When, then, you behold yourselves such through the grace of God, do not cast your pearl before swine, that they trample it, that is, do not go out from that contemplative and most safe secret chamber of your Bridegroom, and go away after the din of worldly business, where you may fulfil carnal desires according to the doctrine of impious men, from whose tents you ought to flee, lest the earth of worldly conversation swallow you up; but rather, when you have rightly recognized yourselves, strive in every way to render thanks to the divine condescension, whose gift you are, and to advance day by day from good things to better, so that from this present tabernacle you may be able to pass over to that heavenly one, saying with the Psalmist: *How lovely are thy tabernacles, O Lord of hosts; my soul longeth and fainteth for [1060B] the courts of the Lord [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)]*.

VERSE 9.-- *To my company of horsemen, in Pharao's chariots, have I likened thee, my love.*

After the Lord has rebuked the Church in general, or specially the soul devoted to him, because in some adversity she doubted of his help, by whose grace she had merited the benefits of many good things, since, proved by adversities, she ought rather to have been stronger, he himself begins to show that she ought to recognize herself, so that the good of the grace of God which she does not know in herself may, by his showing, become manifest to her *48* and from this she may stand more constant in all adversities. He says, then: *To my company of horsemen, in Pharao's chariots, have I likened thee, my love.*
