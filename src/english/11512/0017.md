## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

[1060C]First it must be said what that company of horsemen of the Lord was, and what the chariots of Pharao; then what is the signification of these two opposites, and also what the proportion of the things signifying and the things signified. The company of horsemen of the Lord was that army of the Israelite [sic: *Israelitci*] people, once accepted by God, which he led out of Egypt with a mighty hand and a lofty arm, Egypt having been struck with ten plagues; the terrible showing of whose tenth plague, and the sacrifice of the Paschal lamb at evening, was the swift deliverance of that people. For so says Scripture: that when the Lord was about to strike Egypt with this last plague, namely the death of all the first-born, he commands the children of Israel that by their families they should sacrifice a lamb at evening, whose blood they should put upon both posts of the house and upon the lintels [1060D] of the houses in which they ate it, so that, this sign being seen, the smiting angel should pass over, no one in that house being harmed. And when they had done this, and in that night the Lord struck every first-born in the land of Egypt, yet every house being safe which had been sprinkled with the blood of the lamb, all that company of horsemen of the Lord went out from the land of Egypt; and Pharao, when he pursued it with his chariots and his horsemen, was drowned in the Red Sea, that company of horsemen having crossed the same sea with dry foot [n: (Exod. XII-XV)].

If we apply the eyes of the mind, it is a most beautiful thing to consider what of mystery is contained in that going out from Egypt, the sacrifice of the evening lamb, the sprinkling of blood on the posts and the lintels of the houses, the pursuit of Pharao and his drowning, [1061A] the deliverance of the people also, and at last the bringing into the land of promise; and how far the likening of all these pertains to the universal Church. At the same time also it is pleasing to begin how the Lord went before that people by a pillar of cloud in the day, in the night by a pillar of fire, and bore before them that rod which by divine command stood forth as the worker of so many signs.

After the likeness, then, of this company of horsemen, which went before in figure, is set in order, or is being set in order, that wonderful company of horsemen of the Lord in which there have served and serve, first indeed the apostles and martyrs, then the priests and the orders of divers professions, then their successors, clothed in the armour of God, that is, with the breastplate of justice, the shield of faith, and the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God [n: (Ephes. VI)]. What then is this company [sic: *equitus*] [1061B] of horsemen? Surely the holy universal Church, in which whatever then went before in the thing only is now daily performed in the sacrament and in the thing of the sacrament. For what is the sacrifice of the lamb at evening but the passion of Christ at the end of the ages; the blood of which Lamb is put upon both posts when the sacrament of Christ's passion is both taken by the mouth unto redemption and is gathered with an intent mind unto *49* imitation. To put the blood of the lamb upon the lintels of the houses too is both to direct the intention of the mind, in which we dwell by thought, to the imitation of the Lord's passion, and to fortify with the sign of that passion the front of the body in which we dwell by living.

That ancient company [sic: *antiquitatus*] of the Lord, then, freed by the sacrifice [1061C] of the lamb from Egyptian slavery, the Red Sea having been crossed and the enemies drowned, after the law had been given and received in the desert, entered the land of promise. But this new company of horsemen of the Lord, after the passion of Christ, the yoke of diabolical damnation having been shaken off, the enemies of the soul, that is, vices and sins, having been drowned through the washing of baptism, has received through faith and the observance of the Gospel the land of the promised eternal inheritance. This his company of horsemen the Lord goes before, in the day by the pillar of cloud, in the night by the pillar of fire: who shows himself mild and gentle, with the lenity of mercy, to the saints, who are understood by the day; but to sinners, who are taken for the night, he shows himself terrible with the terror of judgment.

[1061D]Truly wonderful and glorious according to the mystery was the carrying of the rod in that company of horsemen, by which God is recorded to have shown among his people the prodigies and signs of his power. Could not the wood of the Lord's passion, which is the only hope and the sum of our whole salvation, be prefigured fittingly enough by this rod?

Plainly the Church of the Lord, which by his own voice is likened to this company of horsemen, bears this wonderful sign of the cross venerably before her in all the ordinances of her offices, in the doctrines of all sacraments and mysteries, in the uses of all things to be consecrated [sic: *sacra dandarum*]; nothing will be able to be ratified or holy in them except what has been confirmed by the foretaste of this sign.

[1062A]Of this Church, namely, which not unfittingly is also called the company of horsemen of the Lord, the suffering Christ is as it were the standard-bearer, who with this victorious sign of his death goes before her continually, against those chief horsemen and chariots of Pharao, and against all the onsets of his depraved suggestion and the incursions of his wicked contrivance. For this chief Pharao, as we say, who is the head of the wicked and of which head all the wicked are members, is opposed and contrary to that chief leader and author of this company of horsemen, Christ the Lord, who is the head of all the elect and of which head all the elect are members; and therefore these two companies of horsemen, namely of God and of the devil, always fight against each other, as being citizens of two cities contrary to each other.

[1062B]The city of God is the congregation of the faithful which stretches from Abel the just to the last elect, who have *not here a lasting city, but* seek *one to come* [n: (Hebr. XIII)]. Further, the city of the devil is the synagogue of the unfaithful, which from Cain the reprobate will be bounded at the *50* last of the reprobate, who, like Cain, who first built a city, seek and love earthly things, but make little of heavenly ones. And they are called cities by that mode of speech in which that which contains is put for that which is contained; not that they are contained in buildings, but because they are local and circumscribed: the one by the receptacle of blessedness, the other of misery; the one and the same thing, whether blessedness or misery, being disposed on both sides with a manifold disparity of retributions, [1062C] according to the diverse quality of merits.

But this city, which is taken for the present Church [sic: *Eccelsia*], is mingled of good and bad, but it itself is also separated by the certainty of the future sifting, so that two cities are therefore spoken of in this one, because the part of the good belongs to that receptacle of blessedness which is interpreted the heavenly Jerusalem, that is, *vision of peace*; but the part of the bad to that receptacle of misery which is named Babylon, that is, *confusion*.

That city indeed which is the congregation of the just is the company of horsemen and the chariots of God, of which it is said: *The chariot of God is manifold with ten thousands, thousands of them that rejoice in them [n: (Psal. XLVII)]*; in which chariots are also those of whom it is said to God by the prophet: *who mountest upon thy horses; and thy company of horsemen is health* [1062D] [var: Vulg. Hab 3:8 *qui ascendes super equos tuos, et quadrigae tuae salvatio*, "thy chariots are salvation"] [n: (Habac. III)]. For his horses are the saints, over whom he himself presides, who, the first seal having been opened, that is, the mystery of the incarnation having been revealed, went out on a white horse, that is, in a body shining and immaculate, as being conceived and born of the Virgin not in human wise but by the power of the Holy Spirit; and he was the leader of this his company of horsemen in time, by word and example, being to be its rewarder, after the warfare is completed, without time, with the reward of an unspeakable [sic: *inedicibilis*] good and a share in his eternal kingdom. Assuredly the glory of this procession is celebrated in the whole world to the praise of that leader who goes before: how, the same leader sounding the trumpet of the Holy Spirit, these horses, set in the chariot of God, went boldly to meet those armed with depravity and unfaithfulness, so that their sound went forth into all the earth; at the sound of whose [1063A] preaching they run together daily from all the ends of the world, and, coming to the end and joining themselves to this company of horsemen, cry out with the prophet: *From the ends of the earth we have heard praises, the glory of the just one [n: (Isa. XXIV)]*.

But on the contrary, from the opposite side, that city which is the synagogue of the unfaithful. They are the chariots and the company of horsemen of Pharao, that is, of the devil, under whose chariot are those horses red, black, white and dappled: red indeed with the blood of the saints whom they have slain, black with the deformity of sins, white with feigned faith and working, dappled with the diversity of doctrines and sects; over all of whom presides that one who, the *51* fourth seal having been opened, that is, the Lord's passion being at hand, went out on a pale horse, namely with the sign of persecution or of death, to fight through those who were dead to God against those who lived [1063B] to God, as is also added there, that *hell followed him, and power was given him over the four parts of the earth to kill and to slay with the sword, and with famine, and with death, and with the beasts of the earth [n: (Apoc. VI)]*. For hell is rightly compared to his company of horsemen because of the insatiable cruelty which after the Lord's passion raged through the four parts of the world with the four kinds of afflictions named above, namely the sword, famine, death, the beasts of the earth; in whose wretched company of horsemen the first and better known followers of the present leader were: in the passion indeed of that leader who came above on the white horse. Herod and Pilate, and after them many emperors of the Romans, slaying that most holy company of horsemen of the glorious leader by divers kinds of death through all the parts of the world; after whom, last [1063C] of all, the greater Antichrist: against him madness will be stirred, because in him principally will preside that rider of the pale horse, to seduce those who dwell on the earth and even, if it can be, the elect [n: (Matth. XXIV)]. For of this name exalting itself *above all that is called God, or that is worshipped [n: (II Thessal. II)]*, it is to be known that, as the incarnate Truth in his preaching chose for his company of horsemen the poor, the unlettered and the simple, so on the contrary he, to preach his falsehood, will choose the cunning and the double and those having the wisdom of this world. Whence it is said through Isaias: *Woe to the land, the cymbal of wings, which is beyond the rivers of Ethiopia, which sendeth ambassadors by sea, and in vessels of bulrush upon the waters [n: (Isa. XVIII)]*.

[1063D]The land, namely, to which *woe* is said, is principally that damned man who is called the cymbal of wings, because those who through pride fly up into the height of thought sound forth that same perverse man by preaching. Which land is rightly declared to be *beyond the rivers of Ethiopia.* For Ethiopia nourishes a black people, and this world, because it brings forth every man a sinner, gives birth as it were to a black people, Ethiopia; but the land to which *Woe* is said is declared to be *beyond the rivers of Ethiopia [sic: Aethiopia]*, because that damned man is wicked with such perversity that he surpasses the sins of all sinners. *Which sendeth ambassadors by sea,* because he scatters his preachers into the ages; of whom it is rightly added in what manner they are sent, when it is said, *in vessels of bulrush upon the waters.* [1064A] For of bulrush is paper made. For what is designated by bulrush but worldly knowledge? The vessels of bulrush, then, are the hearts [sic: *corde*] of worldly doctors. *In vessels* of bulrush, then, to send *ambassadors upon the waters* is to put one's preaching in the senses of the carnally wise and to call the peoples who flow down into guilt.
