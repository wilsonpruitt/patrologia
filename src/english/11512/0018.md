## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

Under what prince, then, and with what cohort this company of horsemen is set in order, Ezechiel *52* the prophet intimates when he says: *There is Assur and all his multitude, and round about him his graves [n: (Ezech. XXXII)]* For what is expressed by the name of Assur, the proud king, but him of whom it is written: *And he is king over all the children of pride? [n: (Job XLI.)]* Where *however Assur* is, there is *all his multitude,* because surely the whole body of the reprobate cleaves to its head when, binding itself by his perverse laws, [1064B] it shows itself in his warfare. *And round about him his graves:* graves cover the corpses of the dead; what then are his graves but the hearts of the perverse, which receive him, dead from the blessedness of eternal life, to be buried in themselves through carnal desires. *Round about him,* then, *are his graves,* because those in whose minds he now buries himself through desires, he afterwards joins to himself as a tomb through torments.

But what the fruit of such a company of horsemen is, the same prophet announces a little after, saying: *Who go down to hell with their weapons [n: (Ezech. XXXII)]*. The weapons of sinners are the members of the body with which they carry out the evil desires that they conceive in the mind; [1064C] whence it is said through Paul: *Do not yield your members as weapons of iniquity unto sin.* To go down to hell with their weapons, then [sic: *ego*], is to endure the torments of eternal judgment with those very members also with which they fulfilled the desires of pleasure.

By the opposition, then, of this Pharaonic company of horsemen, the Lord's: that company of horsemen is recognized, because if he never was good whom the depravity of the wicked did not exercise, deservedly will they be judged to belong to this company of horsemen of the Lord, or to this city of the Lord, who, cleaving constantly to their leader, fight against that company of horsemen opposed to them, or the contrary city, with patience and the spirit of fortitude, adorning themselves throughout with such comeliness of humility, patience, long-suffering and the other virtues that as the bride of their Lord both universally [1064D] the whole Church and singly any holy soul whatever may fittingly be proclaimed

Of which universal Church, namely, John in the Apocalypse: *I saw,* he says, *the holy city Jerusalem coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband [n: (Apoc. XXI)]*. The Lord, then, says to this his bride that she deservedly ought not to fear this city of the devil, or company of horsemen, which is opposed and contrary to her, because he has likened her *to* his *company of horsemen in Pharao's chariots:* so that, as that one of old, freed from Egypt after the sacrifice of the lamb, after the crossing of the Red Sea, after the drowning of the enemies, a hymn of praise having been celebrated for the victory, after the law had been given and kept in the desert, entered the land of promise; so she, to be freed out of the Egypt of this world, [1065A] after the grace of baptism or of compunction, after the receiving of the body and blood of the Lord, after the mortification of vices and the observance of the divine law. Triumphed over *53* at last the whole company of horsemen of diabolical oppression, with thanksgiving for the largess of so great a gift bestowed on her, she was to enter from the solitude of this life into the inheritance of the eternal promise.

And not beside the purpose, for stirring up the minds of spiritual warriors, to consider in such a company of horsemen what the proportion or comparison is between the truth and the figure, and then between the fight of each and the cause of the fight, between victor and vanquished. For according to the likeness which we demand between the signifying and the significative there can indeed be some comparison, but according to the dignity of the thing [1065B] none at all. Now this is the proposition among the things we have set down: as truth stands to the figure, so let the victor stand to the vanquished, so also the cause of the fight to the fight, so that it is as it were the proportion of a hundred to one, namely a hundredfold. For this is the promise of the Lord in the Gospel, that *he who shall leave house, or father, or mother, or children, or lands for my name's sake shall receive a hundredfold [n: (Matth. XIX)]*: not that he shall receive the very things which he left multiplied a hundredfold, « but that in comparison and in their own merit they are such as if a hundred were compared with a small number. » [n: (HIERONYM.)] Although a hundred is also wont to be taken for perfection, so that, according to what we have said, truth to figure, the cause of the fight to the fight, victor to vanquished, are of such dignity that between the perfection of the better part and the [1065C] imperfection of the other there is no comparison at all. For the fight is the life of man upon earth; the cause of the fight is the eternal and unchangeable life, for which in the present life one must fight and labour with the whole strength of the soul. The vanquished: the devil and his company of horsemen; the victor: he himself, triumpher over the devil and his company of horsemen. The figure is the thing apart from the sacrament, the truth the thing with the sacrament. For that circumcision of the ancient God, and the crossing through the Red Sea, whether the food of manna or the giving of the law, whether the flight and victory of those fighting [sic: *pugnativum*] carnally, were the figure of those things which are now done in the sacrament of baptism and of the body and blood of the Lord and in the observance of the Gospel precepts, and of the war also of the Lord's warriors fighting [1065D] spiritually against the enemies of the soul.

Let us compare, then, each company of horsemen of the Lord, the signifying and the signified, that which is according to the spirit, and let us say that the proportion between them is that which is between flesh and spirit, between corruption and incorruption, between temporality and eternity; and the truth is so much greater [sic: *proportior*] than the figure as spirit than flesh, incorruption than corruption, eternity than temporality. But on either side, that is, in figure and in truth, the fight and the cause of the fight, the victor and the vanquished, are far unlike. For in that one, that is, in the figure, there was fighting carnally for a carnal, corruptible and temporal thing; in this one, that is, in the truth, there was fighting *54* spiritually for a spiritual, incorruptible and eternal thing. There the carnal victor [1066A] overcame the carnal vanquished, but here the spiritual overcomes the spiritual; there by strength of body, here by strength of mind.

The proportion, then, of truth to figure, in comparison and in its own merit, can be as it were that of a hundred, to one, that is, a hundredfold: whether because of its perfection, which is signified by a hundred, or because a hundred, carried over from the left hand to the right, expresses the crown of blessedness, which is the strongest reward in this company of horsemen of the Lord's warriors.

The fight and the cause of the fight having been set forth in the contrary intention of this two-formed company of horsemen, it must be considered with what force and fortitude each side is armed, so that from an affection of this kind a place of argument may be taken, if the question arises who is to be assigned to the Lord's army, who to the adversary's. [1066B] Now we have said that the fight is the life of each man, as it is written in Job [n: (cap. VII)]: *The warfare of man is his life*; but life itself is constituted according to the affection with which the soul is affected, according to which the whole force and fortitude of those who fight consists.

Let us suppose, then, that that force with which the soul is affected in the company of horsemen of the Lord fighting is the love of God: for every one who labours and fights for justice and truth labours and fights for that thing which he loves; but justice and truth is God; therefore he loves God. Further: he who labours and fights for eternal life to be gained, loves, for a thing which he desires to have; but eternal life is God himself; therefore he loves God: and because he loves him he desires to have him, and therefore labours and fights for him.

[1066C]Wherefore, if we ask what it is to love God, we know it from a definition of this kind: *To love God is to labour and fight with the whole soul for justice and truth and eternal life to be gained.* The definition is equal to the thing which it defines; for it is converted, since to labour and fight with the whole soul for justice and truth and eternal life to be gained is to love God.

Let the place, then, be taken from the definition, so that we may prove him to be in the company of horsemen of the Lord whose soul through grace is affected with such love of God that for justice and truth and eternal life to be gained he does not shrink even from dying. For it is a maximal proposition: to whatever the definition is present, that too is present which is defined. For it is defined, that is, *to love God is for justice [1066D] and truth and eternal life to be gained to labour and fight,* and there is present also that which has been defined, namely to love God. The love of God and the company of horsemen, then, are joined inseparably. of the Lord, since it is agreed that no one serves as a soldier to God but he who is proved *55* to love him, nor is it agreed that any loves God but he who is proved to serve him as a soldier.

But contrary to this company of horsemen of the Lord is the company of horsemen of the devil, in which that force by which the soul of one serving on this side is affected is envy, which also cleaves inseparably to its subject. For on this contrary side there is always envy, and conversely where envy is, there is this contrary side. Let a place of argument, then, be taken here also from the definition, if it is asked who deservedly is to be reckoned on this side. [1067A] For to envy is to be tormented by another's happiness, and conversely to be tormented by another's happiness is to envy, so that the definition is equal to the thing which it defines. Such an argument, then, is proposed, that it be said that every one who envies does not love his neighbour, because he would not wish another's good to be; but not to love one's neighbour, this is not to love God. And since it is agreed that to love God is to be in his company of horsemen, but not to love one's neighbour, as has been said, is not to love God. To envy, then, is to be on the contrary side, that is, in the company of horsemen of the devil; they are therefore contrary to each other, as the companies of horsemen, so both the affection of that company of horsemen and the cause divine: for the affection on the one side is of love, on the other of envy; the cause for which there is fighting is on the one side eternal life, on the other the transitory.

But neither side can so pass over into the other [1068A] that at one and the same time they could be one and the same together: for it does not follow that he who wastes away with the disease of envy could be in the company of horsemen of the Lord, and that he who is impelled by the love of God should be reckoned in the company of horsemen of the devil; but he who fights for the transitory life wishes also to drag his neighbour with him, for whom he does not desire eternal goods: *For by the envy of the devil death entered into the world [n: (Sap. II)]*; but he who fights for eternal life wishes to lead his neighbour with him, for whom, as one who truly loves, he desires eternal goods as for himself, so that he fulfils that which is said: *He that heareth, let him say: Come [n: (Apoc. XXII)]*. For he who, hearing and understanding something of the everlasting goods, communicates the same to his neighbour also by teaching and exhorting, shows that he truly loves him whom he invites with himself to understand and to merit those very goods.
