Now it has been said that in the root of the nard there are diverse qualities of nature, which we can refer to the manifold virtues of charity. *For charity is grace*, because it outweighs all virtues; which the Apostle calls *the more excellent way*, saying: *Yet I show unto you a more excellent way [n: (I Cor. XII)]*. [1078C] And it is thick, as being of a livelier sap according to the inner vigour of the mind, which gives to the other virtues the effect of holiness, since without it every virtue remains as it were dry and arid: whose fatness the Psalmist considering, who declared that he watched unto God with thirsting desire: *As with marrow,* he says, *and fatness let my soul be filled [n: (Psal. LXII)]*. It is short, because it is written of it that *an abbreviated and consummated word shall the Lord make upon the earth [n: (Rom. IX)]* [var: Vulg. Rom 9:28 *verbum enim consummans, et abbrevians … verbum breviatum faciet Dominus super terram*, "a word finishing and cutting short … a shortened word shall the Lord make upon the earth"]; the consummated and abbreviated word is charity, because in it consists the perfection of all the virtues, and this one saying is the conclusion of this holy universe: *This word of charity is consummated, because love is the fulness of the law [n: (Rom. XIIII)]*, of which [1078D] the Psalmist says: *I have seen an end of all consummation [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*, not an end which makes that which is, not to be, but which makes that which is not, to be, and brings it to perfection: it is abbreviated, because the precepts of the whole law are renewed in this one: *Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart, and thy neighbour as thyself; on these two precepts dependeth the whole law and the prophets [n: (Matth. XXI)]*. How short a word, consisting of three syllables and seven letters, but containing the whole fulness of holiness: for *God* is *charity [n: (I Joan. IV)]* *68*, who is triune in the distinction of persons, one in the equality of majesty, and sevenfold is the grace of the Holy Spirit, through whom charity itself is poured forth into the hearts of the saints. If, then, *God is charity,* and nothing of good cannot [cj: *esse non potest*; read *esse potest*, "nothing good can be without God"] be without [1079A] God, he who is convicted of not having charity, which is God, is without doubt concluded not to be good.

The root of the nard is also said to be black, because charity, which is, as we have said, the root of all virtues, willingly bears for God and for the salvation of the brethren the blackness of all persecutions and adversities, because, as it is written: *Charity is patient [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*. And it is brittle on account of the exceeding tenderness of brotherly compassion, because it is compelled through the bowels of mercy to be troubled with the troubled, to be weak with the weak, to weep with those who weep, to grieve with those who grieve, to be sad with the sad; for it is written: *Charity is patient, is kind [n: (ibid.)]*: for charity abounds in such sweetness of kindness that it thinks every need of its neighbour its own, it is of a small [1079B] and dense leaf because charity always shows humility, while it abounds in a copious density of all the virtues. It spreads out into ears, because the holy mind, growing up with the increase of divine love, is lifted up into heavenly things by the keenness and subtlety of understanding.

These seven natural things, then, in the root of the nard can not absurdly be taken as these praises which we have set down of the charity proceeding from the sevenfold grace; because, as from the little root of the nard, with the adding of leaves and spikes, is made the confection of a most precious ointment, so from charity, which is the root of all good things, and from the diverse and most choice species of the virtues, the Holy Spirit being the craftsman, is made the confection of holiness; with which holy Church, or any holy soul whatever, as it were anointed, sends forth from herself a most sweet [1079C] odour by the repute and fame of good action; of which odour the Apostle says *that we are the good odour of Christ unto God in every place [n: (II Cor. II)]*, and a certain wise man: A good fame, he says, attacks the bones [var: Vulg. Prov 15:30 *fama bona impinguat ossa*, "a good name maketh the bones fat"]: because every virtue of the soul is enervated unless it is filled with the fatness of a compound of this kind.

Such an odour, then, the Church, or the holy soul, says that she gives from this pistic nard, that is, faithful, wherein that King and bridegroom [d: and bridegroom] heavenly may be delighted *at his reclining,* that is, in that unutterable secret of the Father's bosom. For this is his reclining: to be in the bosom of the Father through the same essence and through the same power of divinity; his reclining is also, as we set down above, to incline himself to the nature of human infirmity through the wonderful order of the consummated [1079D] dispensation. His reclining is also to dwell in the thought of the holy mind, through the most bountiful condescension of his manifold grace.

The odour of this precious pistic nard *69* that woman of the Gospel who is written to have anointed the Lord showed forth in deed and in sacrament: in deed according to the history, in sacrament according to tropology; because to anoint the feet of the Lord was to consider the loftiness of his divinity by the uplifting of the mind, albeit through a glass and in a dark manner. To anoint the head and the feet of the Lord, therefore, is to believe and to preach his divinity and his humanity; whence also of the power of that same anointing with which Mary anointed the Lord it is written that *the house was filled with the odour of the ointment [n: (Joan. XII)]*. For the house [1080A] is the Church, which is filled with the odour of this anointing, because she believes and confesses Christ God and man, and by the rightness of this faith and confession is spread through the whole breadth of the world. Through this house, that is, the whole body of the Church, John the Evangelist sprinkles the sweetness of this odour from the head and from the feet of the Lord: from the head indeed when he says: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God:* and from the feet when he says: *The Word was made flesh and dwelt in us [n: (Joan. I)]*.

The anointing of the head and feet of the Lord can also be understood in this way: that he who follows the footsteps of Christ through the pursuit of the active life should lie prostrate at his feet by living well, and so, advancing from day to day to better things, [1080B] through the virtue of the contemplative life should raise himself up, as it were, to the head of Christ, by cleaving to God continually. The anointing of the feet, then, is the operative virtue; further, the anointing of the head is the contemplative virtue. For the offering of good action and the exercise of holy meditation is of a most sweet odour, in which that heavenly King and Bridegroom delights at his reclining, waiting for and desiring those fragrant with such an odour, to place them with himself in the eternal reclining-place of that reclining of his own.

But that holy Church, or the faithful soul, may be able to come to that eternal reclining of his, in this reclining of his temporal dispensation, in which he willed to recline for her salvation by being born and by dying, by following the examples of his humility, patience and obedience, let her prepare for him in her mind a reclining redolent [1080C] with the odour of the virtues, so that afterwards he may lead her, as bridegroom the bride, the immaculate the immaculate, into the bridal chamber, or reclining, of his kingdom; so that, as he now rests in her temporally through the diversity of graces, so she hereafter may rest in him eternally through everlasting glory. Whence by what follows the same bride shows that this is the sentence fixed beforehand by the merciful Bridegroom, that he who shares in his sufferings through patience shall also partake of his eternal kingdom through consolation.

VERSE 12.-- *A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me, he shall abide between my breasts.*

*70* By the myrrh, namely, is expressed the bitterness of the passion, by the cluster the sweetness of the resurrection. While, [1080D] she says, my bridegroom and my king, under whose rule it is mine both to will and to be able the good, had reclined in the passion of the cross and, made obedient unto death, was already laid in the sepulchre, I, who before complained that I was black because not only strangers but my brothers also fought against me, as if compounded as a certain most precious nard out of his most ardent charity, and also out of the example of his humility, patience and obedience, have sprinkled the odour of good repute through the whole world, ready to succumb for his love not by one death only, but by a thousand kinds of deaths; so that *to me my beloved* is not myrrh alone, but a bundle of myrrh, since for the one kind of death which he underwent for me, I desire to gather a bundle of many and diverse deaths.

[1081A]What is this bundle? Perils of rivers, perils of robbers, perils from the Gentiles, perils in the city, perils in the wilderness, perils in the sea, perils among false brethren; labour and hardships, many watchings, hunger and thirst, many fastings, cold and nakedness: mockings and stripes, chains and prisons, stoning, being sawn asunder, lastly slaughter by the sword [n: (II Cor. XI; Hebr. XI)]. Behold *the bundle of myrrh.* But since martyrdom is twofold, one in the body, the other in the mind, let us divide this bundle and suppose one to be the bundle of myrrh, that is, of bodily suffering, and another the bundle of mental myrrh; and the bundle of bodily suffering we have enumerated above; further, the bundle of mental myrrh, that is, of suffering, is virginity of the flesh, renunciation of the world and of all [1081B] worldly pleasure, or the labour of manifold continence. The cause of this twin bundle *is my beloved to me,* says the Church, or the holy soul, because for it, for his name and love, I have put the shoulders of mind and body under the bearing of this bundle: of the mind indeed by the mortification of vices, and of the body by the endurance of diverse sufferings. Why this? Because the beloved himself also, through himself too, was made *to me a bundle of myrrh*, when, taking upon himself for me in his own body scourges, spittings, blows, mockings, the draught of gall, the cross, the piercings of the hands, feet and side, and lastly death itself, he gathered as it were a bundle of manifold sufferings; and *a bundle, my beloved to me,* being made such, *shall abide between my breasts,* because all these signs of his passion I daily [1081C] recall in mind, and while I have the memory of them, I too gather more ardently for love of him the bundle of my own sufferings.

In the situation of the body the breast is placed between the breasts set on either side, and therefore by the commemoration of the bundle of myrrh between the breasts we rightly *71* understand that memory of the Lord's passion which ought to be in the breast.

Or let us suppose the breasts to be the two Testaments, namely the Old and the New, and consider that in these too the beloved abides by the commemoration of his passion, the former Testament foretelling that very passion as to come, the following one presenting it as already done. *Between my breasts,* she says, *he shall abide,* because in the two [1081D] Testaments, set before me for the knowledge of him and for my salvation, I gather the bundle of his myrrh: foreshown in the Old Testament through figures, and in the New shown forth to the whole world through the saving sap of eternal redemption. For the sap of myrrh is said to heal many infirmities, and the sap of this myrrh of the Lord has driven out every sickness of the soul, and has established the medicine of perpetual health in the whole world.

Or *he shall abide between my breasts,* that is, between the two peoples established in my body out of the Jews and the Gentiles, who receive this bundle of the Lord's myrrh by faith, follow it by their manners, and make it in the sacrament by the daily commemoration of him, recall it in mind, and with the mouth take their own perpetual.

[1082A]Or *between my breasts*, that is, between the two thieves, between whom *my beloved*, hanging, composes the bundle of his passion; who prefigured the same two peoples, one believing, the other crucifying; one chosen, the other reprobate. And they were my breasts, the former on account of the natural and the written law, the latter on account of the natural alone, both thus having the nourishment of reason, so that they are inexcusable. And the latter indeed, through my beloved abiding in the midst and holding out the bundle of his myrrh, took on over and above a new law enlightening the natural; but the former, by the hidden but just judgment of God, after the law was given, lost even the natural, on account of unbelief.
