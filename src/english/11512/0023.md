VERSE 13.-- *A cluster of Cyprus is my beloved to me, in the vineyards of Engaddi.*

And because it willed [cj: *voluit*; read *noluit*, "was unwilling"] to take up the healing of this, it went without the sweetness of the cluster, and therefore it neither died together with the beloved to sin, nor rose together with him to justice. But to me, who in the other people, coming to the faith, receive, venerate and adore the sacraments of the passion, he has been made *a cluster of Cyprus*, because when he himself rose from death, he quickened me too with the hope and sweetness of the resurrection to come.

For he is the cluster carried from the land abiding with milk and honey, by the two spies signifying those very [1082C] peoples; who, begotten of Mary his mother, flowing with the sweetness and fatness of the Holy Spirit, *72* was hung upon the cross for the salvation of both peoples. But as it is the custom of those who carry that one goes before and the other follows, so the former people was that of the Jews, known to God through the observance of the law, and the following people that of the Christians, taken up through the religion of the new law. And as he who goes before does not see that which he carries, and puts it behind him by a certain turning away of the back, while he who follows always has it before his face, always guards it with his gaze, and enjoys a certain nearness of body: so the Jewish people, carrying Christ in the law, does not understand him, but as it were turning away despises him. But the Christian people understands him, carries him on the forehead [1082D] and in the mind, and guards him by worshipping and by obeying his commandments.

That people abhors and despises this cluster hanging on the cross on account of the curse of the law; this people receives the saving testament of his blood, pressed out in the winepress of the cross, drinking with spiritual sweetness the unique remedy of body and soul; and in hope, the desirable and incomparable joy to come after the general resurrection of the same twin substance, namely of body and soul.

In this people, then, for I am this people, the bride of God, the Church of God, *a cluster of Cyprus is my beloved to me,* because he who was *a bundle of myrrh* in the bitterness of the passion will be a cluster in the sweetness of the resurrection; and he for whose sake I have gathered a bundle of many sufferings, [1083A] both in body and in mind, will be to me a cluster in the eternal refreshment of the soul and in the most sweet recompense of this labour.

Whence he is rightly called *a cluster of Cyprus*. For Cyprus is interpreted *sadness* or *mourning*; he is called, then, *the cluster of Cyprus*, because he himself is the true and sweet consolation of all my mourning and sadness; and as the cluster of Cyprus surpasses the clusters of all lands in size, so my beloved, *the Father of mercies, and the God of all consolation [n: (II Cor. I)]*, surpasses in his dignity and grace all things that are in heaven and earth.

But where has the beloved been made a cluster of Cyprus? *In the vineyards of Engaddi.* Engaldi is said to be a very large village in Judaea, beside the Dead Sea, where there are vineyards, not indeed of wine but of balsam, little trees which are therefore [1083B] called vineyards, because they are propped up in the manner of vineyards. And from balsam [sic: *basalmo*] mixed with the liquor of the olive the chrism is made, by which certain greater institutions of ecclesiastical consecrations are performed; for in it the laver of baptism is consecrated, and the priestly, its own imperial, dignity is confirmed. A certain special holiness is assigned to churches, or altars, and to sacramental vessels. And typically, in the anointing with a liquor of this kind are signified the gifts of spiritual charisms, by which the hearts of the faithful are made fit by the grace of the Holy Spirit for the divine ministries.

And Engaddi is interpreted *the fountain of the kid*. *73* And who is the fountain of the kid but baptism, or the grace of tears? [1083C] Now the kids are the sinners who are to be placed on the left, and all the just are to be set on the right. The vineyards of Engaddi, then, that is, of the fountain of the kid, are the spiritual gifts which are granted only to those who have been washed from the filth of sins by holy baptism, or through the tears of penance: for the fountain of the kid is baptism, by which sinners, that is, kids, are washed and become just, that is, sheep of Christ; or, if after baptism they have fallen into sin, they are restored to their former state through the grace of tears, as through a second baptism.

*A cluster,* she says, *of Cyprus is my beloved to me in the vineyards of Engaddi,* because he is indeed to me the food and drink of consolation and salvation in those who through the grace of baptism have been made worthy to receive the knowledge of the divine sacraments [1083D] and the abundance of spiritual gifts; or who through the fountain of tears merit the absolution of sins, and, dead to sins, rise again to the life of eternal salvation, so that if, according to the Apostle, they are *made planted together* *in the likeness of the death of Christ,* they may become *likewise also of his resurrection [n: (Rom. VI)]*.

The order of these four predicaments is certainly to be noted, namely *the odour of the nard, the bundle of myrrh, the cluster of Cyprus, the vineyard of Engaddi.* For in the same order in which they follow one another here in the series of the words, they are related as fulfilled in the Lord Jesus Christ: when before the passion he was anointed by Mary with precious spikenard, *and the house was filled with the odour of the ointment [n: (Joan. XII)]*: and in the passion he was given myrrhed wine to drink [n: (Marc. XV)], and after the passion, anointed with myrrh and other kinds of spices, [1084A] he was laid in the sepulchre [n: (Joan. XIX)]; whence, rising again after three days [n: (Marc. XVI)], he offered to believers the sweetness of the spiritual cluster in the principal joy of the resurrection: into whom, a little afterwards, he poured the grace of the Holy Spirit, which has all virtue, like balsam *in the vineyards of Engaddi*, giving to it power, through water and the spirit [n: (Joan. XX)] or the imposition of the hand, to give remission of sins and to make of kids the sheep of Christ.

But the things which in this manner, according to the history, went before in themselves in the head can also be shown in figure in his members, according to morality, in like order: so that, the odour of the virtues going before, the mortification of vices should consequently follow; because the affliction of the body and the contempt of worldly glory are the instruments [1084B] of this mortification. Are not hunger, thirst, nakedness, watching and continual prayers the instruments or the matter of this mortification? [n: (Rom. VIII.)] Now it is a principal and maximal proposition that where the instrument is lacking the cause of the instrument also is lacking; and where the matter is not, that also which is made from the matter is not; and that therefore it is consequent that where virtues are lacking, vices rule; because upon the steadfastness of the virtues going before there follows the fall of the vices. *74* Whence the Apostle, describing two things adverse to one another, namely the flesh and the spirit, first says: *Walk in the spirit,* and then, *and you shall not fulfil the desires of the flesh [n: (Gal. V)]*, to show that the flesh is subjected by the strength of the spirit, and that unless virtue goes before, vice cannot give way.

[1084C]In this order, then, in imitation of the head, that is, of Christ, his members, that is, the saints, offer first nard, then myrrh, because they ought first to have the works of holiness, by whose virtue the works of the flesh may be mortified. But because the same Apostle says: *If by the spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you shall live [n: (Rom. VIII)]*, in a fitting order, doubtless, spiritual resurrection follows spiritual mortification, so that those who have died together with Christ by dying to sins may rise again together with him by living. to justice, saying with the same Apostle: If we die together, we shall also live together [n: (II Tim. II)]. For as there is a twin death, namely of the soul and of the body, so there is also a twin resurrection of each, so that those who now through justification rise from the death of the soul, in the resurrection to come [1084D] may rise through incorruption from the death of body and soul. Whence John says in the Apocalypse: *Blessed is the saint that hath part in the first resurrection, in these the second death hath not power [n: (Apoc. XX)]* [var: Vulg. Apoc 20:6 *Beatus, et sanctus, qui habet partem*, "blessed and holy is he that hath part"]. This resurrection indeed is signified by the name of the cluster, so that, because for a time they have suffered a certain bitterness by spiritually mortifying the flesh, afterwards they may perceive eternally the ineffable sweetness of joy, here indeed by resting spiritually from the corruption of the mind, but there both from the corruption of the mind and from that of the body.

Truly this order also pleased the disposing grace as fitting and seemly: that, with the nard of holy repute being fragrant, and by the myrrh of inmost mortification, those already existing there as a living victim, and through the cluster [sic: *hotrum*] spiritually [1085A] inebriating, gladdened by the newness of the soul rising from the death of sin, made superior to the flesh through the spirit, it should show worthy of the coming of the Holy Spirit, through the balsams of diverse graces which Engaddi spiritually brought forth, that is, the fountain of the kid, namely the baptism of the twofold laver, both of washing and of compunction, by which the kids, that is, sinners, are washed and are made sheep of Christ.

Therefore, after the bridegroom rebuked the bride for her ignorance of herself, on account of which she would have received a deserved repulse from the fellowship of the bridegroom, the bride, by his wholesome chiding, at length recognizing herself, she who before had distrusted that she could persevere by herself, now with full faith learned not to fail in tribulations, even unto death, for his love; and the bridegroom, congratulating her on such [1085B] progress of hers and now judging her fair, flatters her with a proclamation of praises of this kind, so that by this she may be provoked to greater works of virtues.

* 75* VERSE 14.-- *Behold, thou art fair, my love, behold, thou art fair, thine eyes are doves'.*

Because, he says, considering thyself at first, thou didst call thyself black and dusky on account of the various heats of temptations, and, being still of little faith, didst cry out for my help, which nevertheless he had not said to thee; but afterwards, recognizing thyself as I showed thee, thou didst advance in such strength of spirit that thou didst not hesitate to offer me not only the myrrh of the passion but a bundle of myrrh: *behold*, I declare to thee that so *fair art thou*, and because fair, therefore *my love*; and conversely, because *my love*, therefore *fair*; and not [1085C] once *fair*, but again *fair*: for repetition is confirmation, because thou art so perfectly fair that thou pleasest me with a doubled beauty, namely of faith and of working, or of soul and of body.

Now in a beautiful order, between two predicates of beauty set on either side, one subject is constructed in the middle place, because the middle itself takes its being from the two; so that if one and of the two, doubly, of the beauty disposed on both sides, is lacking, namely, so that there be faith without works, or works without faith; or chastity of body without chastity of mind, or of mind without chastity of body, no soul is joined to God by the bond of spiritual friendship.

For since it is written: Faith, if it have not works, [1085D] dead in itself [n: (Jacob. II)], and since again that soul is called foolish by the Lord which carries lamps without oil, being about to go forth to meet the bridegroom [n: (Matth. XXV)], it is manifest that these parts of beauty, doubled on both sides, are constitutive of this most holy friendship. For it is not of small estimation before the judgment of almighty God to be perfected by so great a grace of God that he deigns him worthy of his friendship; and to be named, for the special gift of the virtues, the friend of God is certainly a great proclamation. Whence the Lord to the apostles: *Now I will not call you servants,* he says, *but friends, because all things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you [n: (Joan. XV)]*. For the sign of this friendship is the revelation of the Father's secret, and that which is specially conferred on those who love him and keep faith with this friendship, [1086A] the excellence of the contemplative dignity.
