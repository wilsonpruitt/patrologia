[1093A]And well are both, as cedar and cypress, described as odoriferous and incorruptible, because both the prelates are raised aloft by the good odour and incorruption of life, doctrine and faith, and the subjects are daily provoked by their erudition to the same graces of the virtues. Whence also the cypress, which is set in the part of the subjects, has this by nature, that, planed by the hand of the craftsman, it presents a comely appearance, because this is proper to good subjects, that when they have been touched by the hand of holy admonition or rebuke by their prelates, they do not recoil insolently, but submit themselves obedient to their admonitions with a cheerful countenance and a patient mind.

But that we may look at all these things generally in the universal Church too, which is as it were a certain commonwealth of the bridegroom and the bride, [1093B] these ensigns of the adornment of the house of the Lord are daily venerated and proclaimed, it contains principally the head, that is, Christ in his deeds and precepts, and after him his members, among whom, above all, eminent and of singular merit, is the blessed Virgin Mary, who in the signification of the flowery bed is held most beautiful: then the apostles or apostolic men and their hearers and followers, set in the very house of the Lord as beams and panelled ceilings, by whose life, erudition, institution, authority in all things this whole commonwealth of God is venerably confirmed and adorned, so that to those who look rightly nothing more honest, nothing more pleasant, nothing surely sweeter can be esteemed in the pilgrimage of this life. For this is as it were a certain way and concourse of future felicity [1093C] and a contemplation, far off, in faith, hope and charity, of that great and incomparable commonwealth.

But that this glorious state of the nobly instituted Church has stood at the nod of him *84* who said: *Without me you can do nothing [n: (Joan. XV)]*, the same bridegroom and ruler of it himself confesses, truth confirming truth, not that he needs our attestation, but that he may instruct us unto greater faith, and that he may teach no one to presume of his own justice in any good.
