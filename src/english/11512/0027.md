## CHAPTER TWO.

VERSE 1.-- *I am the flower of the field and the lily of the valleys.*

Rightly, he says, O beloved, dost thou judge, who ascribest all thy beauty to me, because *I* in truth am [1093D] *the flower of the field,* that is, the honour and beauty and adornment of the whole universal Church, spread through the whole world, or most joyful field, and diverse in species of holiness and grace, germinating and flowering from my gift. *I* also *am the lily of the valleys,* that is, the cleansing, refreshment and medicine of the humble, or of those cast down by some sorrow and affliction of this world for the sake of my name.

*85* For the nature of the lily has in it three delightful things: whiteness, odour, medicine against burning; for whoever in this valley of tears, with humbled spirit and contrite heart, bewail their excesses, recognizing themselves infirm and always imploring my aid, without doubt shall obtain cleansing and whiteness of soul, and then [1094A] shall be refreshed by the odour of good conversation, I inspiring and helping, and against all the incentives of the flesh and against the burning of affliction and of the world as it were setting on fire, shall be cooled by the opposing of my love and of the consolation of hope.

Another too, O beloved, *I am the flower of the field,* that one, namely, of whom Isaiah says: *There shall come forth a rod out of the root of Jesse, and a flower shall rise up out of his root [n: (Isa. XI)]*. For thou didst say above that our bed is flowery, namely the womb of my Mother, from whom I willed to be incarnate; but I say that it is flowery for this reason, because I am its flower, from which flower many other flowers proceed, that is, spiritual grace, which the prophet mentioned enumerates. *And there shall rest,* he says, *upon him,* without doubt upon the flower, *the spirit of the Lord, the spirit [1094B] of wisdom and of understanding, the spirit of counsel and of fortitude, the spirit of knowledge and of piety, and the spirit of the fear of the Lord shall fill him [n: (ibid.)]*. I am the flower, then, of the field, namely of that earth of which it is said: *Truth is sprung out of the earth [n: (Psal. LXXXIV)]*; which is therefore called a field, because it is not rough, nor tortuous, a village, but level and even, as being levelled by the power of the Most High after the manner of a plain from every unevenness of morals; which brought me forth, the flower of flowers, that is, the Holy of holies, without the seed of man, just as a field without seed produces the shoots of diverse flowers and herbs.

*The lily* too am I *of the valleys,* that is, the whiteness and odour and salvation of those who imitate the humility of my mother, and not of those who glory in the merit of chastity; for those who glory, and contain themselves for human favour only, [1094C] have not progress, the lily of virginal whiteness, and, by inward purity, the sign; but rather they are unclean with the blackness of vainglory before the sight of my purity, and therefore I neither am nor can be called their lily: but of those who imitate me and her, I am the lily by the gift of grace, she is the lily by example, so that they too may become a lily by imitation. Whence follows: *as the lily among thorns.*

VERSE 2.-- *As the lily among thorns, so is my love among the daughters.*

As I, he says, among the Jews, whom I expected to make grapes, but they made thorns [n: (Isa. V)], shone with humility, meekness, patience and the manifold grace of the virtues, while they, standing like a thorn against [1094D] me, pricked me with many stings of words and of manners, so thou, O my Church, my bride, or any soul whatever that loves me, you ought to be among the daughters *86* of this world, that is, souls thorny with malice and unbelief, in humility and patience, so that, when thou hast imitated me in this manner, thou mayest be proved truly my love, and let such imitation of me be the proof of true friendship.

Or as my mother according to the flesh, Mary, who is a lily by the whiteness of virginity and the odour of holiness, went her way with humility and patience among the daughters of the Synagogue, who like thorns pricked her, putting upon her the stain of infamy, because they did not believe in the virtue and power of God. Because she recognized through the spirit the dispensation of God, and felt fulfilled in herself what she had formerly heard through the prophet and afterwards through the angel. [1095A] So also, by her example, whatever soul is a friend to me through faith and love ought, among the daughters whom the corruption of malice and impiety has begotten, to keep humility, patience and honesty, and on account of the hope of future remuneration never to fall away from them.

Or let us say simply that the soul which wishes to show itself worthy of the friendship of God ought always to stand among depraved men *as the lily among [sic: liliuminter] thorns,* so that, as *the lily among thorns,* being set there, does not lose the dignity of its whiteness and odour, so she, among depraved manners, may not lose the integrity of faith and patience, or of any holiness granted her, but rather may learn by tolerating that, the more she is afflicted by their perversity, so much the more are the merits of greater virtues heaped up for her; [1095B] for, as blessed Gregory says, whoever does not tolerate the wicked, he himself through his intolerance is witness to thee that he is not good. Hence blessed Job speaks of himself, saying: *A brother* am I *of dragons and a companion of ostriches [n: (Job XXX)]*; and the Lord to Ezekiel: *The sons of man are unbelievers and subverters with thee, thou dwellest with scorpions [var: Vulg. Ez 2:6 *fili hominis … increduli et subversores sunt tecum*, "O son of man … unbelievers and subverters are with thee"] [n: (Ezech. II)]*. Peter also approves the life of Lot, saying: *And he delivered just Lot, oppressed by the injury and the luxurious conversation of the wicked; for in sight and hearing he was just: dwelling among them who from day to day vexed the soul of the just man with wicked works [n: (II Petr. II)]*. Hence John writes to the Church of Pergamum, saying: *I know where thou dwellest, where the seat of Satan is, and thou hast my name, and hast not denied my faith [n: (Apoc. II)]*; Paul also [1095C] praises and strengthens his disciples, saying: *In the midst of a depraved and perverse nation, among whom you shine as lights in the world. Holding the word of life [n: (Philip. II)]*.

By these authorities it is sufficiently established how great a virtue it is to tolerate the depraved manners of men, since from the greater malice of the reprobate greater gains of merits grow for the elect, because it is written: *The more Pharaoh oppressed the people of God, the more it was multiplied [n: (Exod. I)]*.

But who the daughters are among whom the Church or holy soul is said to dwell *as the lily among thorns* is gathered from the comparison itself, because as for those which are compared to thorns, it is evident *87* that they are taken in the adverse part; and they are therefore called daughters, because [1095D] they are generated from the wicked seed of perverse doctrine, or from an evil conception of the mind. But the Church, or any faithful soul whatever, which through faith and operation is the bride of Christ, that she may be able to be as the lily among thorns, comes to meet him who rules and protects her with gracious praise through another comparison, saying: *As the apple tree among the trees of the woods,* etc.

VERSE 3.-- *As the apple tree among the trees of the woods, so is my beloved among the sons. Under the shadow of him whom I had desired I sat, and his fruit was sweet to my throat.*

My beloved, she says, has compared me to the lily among thorns, keeping its virtue, which, although he has granted it me by his gift, yet he taught me by his own example that I should become this. For *as the apple tree,* fruit-bearing, having in itself three useful things by which it refreshes, [1096A] this being fruit, odour and shade, *among the trees of the woods* too, which certainly are unfruitful, is held incomparable, *so my beloved,* by the fruit and odour of holiness and of justice and of doctrine, and by the shade of his protection, with which he refreshes, teaches and defends those who believe in him, *among the sons,* that is, the Jews, stood forth; who at first indeed were sons, and now wish to be, but are not, because of them God said: *I have nourished and exalted sons, but they have despised me [n: (Isa. I)]*; and elsewhere: *The strange sons have lied to me, the strange sons have grown old and have halted from their paths [n: (Psal. XVII)]*: who boast themselves to be sons of Abraham, but are not, because they have not the works [n: (Joan. VIII)]; and therefore they are unfruitful and useless trees, and on this account to be cut down and cast into the fire. For among such [1096B] trees, caught by eternal burning, he hung on the tree of the cross, from which he poured out for me the sweetest fruit of his body and blood, with which he daily refreshes those who believe in him: but they, like wild trees, not only brought forth no fruit of good work, but even poured out the most bitter sap of their malice and impiety, crying: *Crucify, crucify [n: (Joan. XIX.)]*, and: *His blood be upon us and upon our sons [n: (Matth. XVII)]*.

Under the shade of this tree, then, that is, the refreshment and consoling defence of the New Testament, which I was desiring now for a long time in the ancient fathers, who sighed with great desire for the times of softening and refreshing grace, now at length after the advent, passion, resurrection and ascension of Christ [1096C] I sat, with faith unshaken and stable. For the Law, with a certain heat of inclemency and of incurable punishment, when it said: *If anyone shall have done this or that, let him die the death [n: (Exod. XIX)]*, had long afflicted me; but grace moved such heat with the shade of mercy and the remedy of penance, under which desirable shade I did not walk, did not stand, but rather sat and rested, because *88* I received this decree of piety, confirmed by the authority of my Lord himself, to be held immutably.

This tree indeed is the tree of life, whose shade and divine protection, which its branches pour out, branches of honour and grace, which namely are the oppositions of diverse virtues against the heats of worldly temptations: as, against pride, [1096D] the opposition of humility is necessary; against anger, of patience; against lust, of chastity and continence; against contumacy, of obedience; against avarice, of largesse and mercy; against strifes and contentions, of peace and concord; against envy and hatred, of love; and that we may comprehend it briefly, against all the delights and temptations of this world surely there must be opposed the remembrance of the future misery of the wicked and the glory of the just.

The outpourings of such shades indeed protect the soul from the heat of carnal suggestions and delights, under which, because I desired them, *I sat,* that is, I rested, since indeed I found in them the grateful and wholesome refreshment of protecting grace.

*And his fruit,* without doubt that of that apple tree, [1097A] that is, of Christ, my bridegroom and Lord, *sweet to my throat,* because the utility and suavity of his precepts is sweet and pleasing to the palate of my heart and to the capacity of my mind. Or the fruit of this apple tree hanging on the tree of the cross is sweet to my throat, because the receiving of the body and blood of Christ pours sweetness and health into my breast, and it is plainly the perfect refection of my body and soul [sic: *ani mae*].

Otherwise also the sons among whom the bridegroom is said to dwell *as the apple tree among the trees of the woods* are taken to be the elect, who are called sons of God by name, on account of the observance of his commandments, for which they shall obtain from their heavenly Father himself the inheritance of eternal life; among whom Christ dwells *like the apple tree among the trees of the woods,* because as the apple tree [1097B] excels all the trees of the woods in beauty and in the odour and sweetness of its fruits, so Christ goes before all the saints in the comeliness of virtues and the sweetness of doctrine, as it is written of him: *Beautiful in form above the sons of men, grace is poured abroad in thy lips; therefore God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows [n: (Psal. XLIV)]*.
