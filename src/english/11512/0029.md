## CHAPTER TWO. (cont.)

Whence he sends consolatory things to certain of his disciples, saying words: *I give thanks to my God always for you, in the grace of God which is given you in Christ Jesus, because in all things you are made rich in him, in all utterance and in all knowledge, as the testimony of Christ was confirmed in you, so that nothing is wanting to you in any grace, waiting for the revelation of our Lord Jesus Christ [n: (I Cor. I)]*. Behold, he who desired to be dissolved [1101B] and to be with Christ, how much he rejoiced and gave thanks, that, because he could not yet attain this, yet by the advancements of the sons whom he had begotten in Christ he greatly relieved the inconvenience of this delay.

*93* In another way also the Church desires the utility and progress of her sons through faith and operation, so that she is then said to be stayed up with flowers when she is adorned with the faith of believers, which is the beginning of religion and as it were the first rising flower of holiness. But because *faith without works is dead [n: (Jac. II)]*, she is said to be compassed about with apples when she is said to rejoice in the pious works of those same believers, bearing fruit manifoldly, as in fruits already ripe.

And since these two are so joined to each other that the one [1101C] cannot be useful without the other; for it is written that *without faith it is impossible for anyone to please God [n: (Hebr. XI)]*, but faith, if it have not works, is dead in itself [n: (Jac. II)], great surely is the consolation of the soul languishing in the love of God when she sees her neighbours advance in faith and operation.

But since this bride of Christ, who by that love so magnificently, as we have said, ordered is made weak and languid, is not [cj: *non*; read *non solum*, "not only"] consoled by the progress and utility of her neighbours, but is also lifted up by the reality of present goods and the hope of future ones, she adds, saying: *His left hand under my head.*

VERSE 6.-- *His left hand under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me.*

[1101D]The left hand of Christ, that is, the *sinistra*, are temporal goods, but his right hand eternal goods; but certain temporal goods in this world God grants to his elect for the expectation of future goods, so that while they receive his present gifts they may be prepared more ardently and more securely for desiring the eternal.

But the *left hand* of Christ is *under the head* of her, because she presses down these very temporal goods by the intention of the highest love, so that she may look upon them as lower, and always attend to eternal goods as higher. For under the head, that is, under the mind, not above, ought all these temporal things to be, namely that she may never set them before divine love, but may so use them that, not for pleasure but rather for necessity, she may moderate them by the reasonable equity of the mind [1102A] and may always admire the more, by loving and sighing, those things which ought to be above the mind. For this is *his right hand* which may embrace him, namely those everlasting goods, which, when she shall have obtained him, she may be able to rest in secure blessedness and blessed security.

She says, therefore: *His left hand under my head and his right hand shall embrace me,* because all things that he has ordered in creatures for my utility I shall hold in thought for thanksgiving, but those on account of which these things have been furnished me for a time, as being to abide with me eternally, I shall be zealous to love and to desire with the whole devotion of my mind.

Or, *his left hand under my head,* because that he was born and suffered, that he rose again and ascended into heaven, [1102B] will be in my memory; *and his right hand shall embrace me,* because the memory of these things will animate me to the ensigns of the future resurrection and *94* glory, to be loved and desired.

Or the left hand of God is, in this world, a certain small knowledge of him, but the *right hand* *of him,* after this life, the full and true knowledge of him; but the *left hand under* our *head* is, but the *right hand* *of him shall embrace* us, because *we see now indeed through a glass in a dark manner, but then face to face [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*. For the *left hand* is *under the head* because it is granted to us in this life to think something of God and to see it with the mind, but the *right hand* *of him shall embrace* us, because in the future life he will fill and surround us with so great a vision of himself that what this may be *neither eye* can see, [1102C] *nor ear* hear, *nor into the heart of man* can it ascend [n: (I Cor. II)].

This charity, then, ordered in the bride by the condescension of ordering grace, sets the left hand under her head and embraces her with its right hand, so that, all temporal gifts being put in the second place, she may rest principally in the desire of eternal life; and that in this way the grant of present things may be also the certainty of future things, the consolation of the mind weak and languid in the love of God.

And you, sisters beloved of Christ, who by despising the world languish with the love of God, ought to give such a sign of this kind of love that you may be stayed up with flowers by believing, compassed about with apples by working. Let the left hand of Christ be under your head and his right hand embrace [1102D] you, so that you may always attend to the temporal goods somehow granted you by God as lower, but to the eternal as higher. *For length of days is in his right hand, in his left hand riches and glory [n: (Prov. III)]*; because in this present life he sometimes grants to his elect to foretaste the riches of his goodness, but in the future life to possess them eternally without end. *Delights in the right hand* of him are *even unto the end [n: (Psal. XV)]*, which you can surely obtain, if you so languish in his love that you persevere in him firm and stable even unto the end. His right hand indeed shall embrace you, because not in part as now, but whole and perfect knowledge of himself he will give, and for brief and transitory labour he will grant you rest and life and length of days for ever and ever. Amen.
