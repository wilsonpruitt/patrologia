## *95* PROEM.

[1103A]

*How,* says the Psalmist, *shall we sing the canticle of the Lord in a strange land? [n: (Psal. CXXXVI.)]* But what is *the canticle of the Lord?* First, to speak simply: *The canticle of the Lord* is whatever we compose, write and say according to the manner of the Church's doctrine or ordinance, in a concordant modulation of senses and words. *The canticle of the Lord* is the whole page of the Old and New Testament, which we sing well when we announce it with right and full faith, setting forth to the praise of the Lord now the series of the things done, now the signification of the things themselves. But a *Canticle* of this kind anyone can sing by way of knowledge, not only in his own land but also *in a strange land;* because a bodily place does not take away [1103B] the power of singing, though it may take away the will and the pleasure, because of some passion of sadness: as with that people set in the grief of captivity, who, when it was said to them, *Sing us a hymn from the canticles of Sion,* answered: *How shall we sing the canticle of the Lord in a strange land?* For, to take it in figure, their not being able to sing *the canticle of the Lord in a strange land* was our not being able to praise the Lord perfectly in the place of our pilgrimage; which is to us as it were a strange land; *Because we have not here a lasting city, but we seek one to come [n: (Hebr. XIII)]*, which is our fatherland and the land of our eternal inheritance. For to tell the works of God, to declare his commandments, to write and expound them, is, as we have said, to sing a certain canticle of the Lord [1103C]: this too is possible in this place of our pilgrimage, since the Psalmist says: *Thy justifications were the subject of my song in the place of my pilgrimage [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*.

But that we may consider something higher, there is also another canticle of the Lord, which it is not possible to sing in a strange land. And which is that? Surely the one which the Holy Spirit modulates by his sevenfold grace, as it were through the distinctions of seven voices; because that voice of the inner man sounds in one way through the spirit of wisdom, in another through the spirit of understanding, in another through the spirit of counsel, and in another through the spirit of fortitude; but in another manner through the spirit of knowledge, and in another through the spirit of piety, and in another again [1103D] through the spirit of the Lord [n: (Isa. XI)].

For by these distributions of graces, as by certain different voices, is constituted the whole modulation, of the canticle *96* of the Lord, of that most sweet canticle, which a life honest, holy, pleasing to God and perfect sings on the strings of the virtues.

For there is no dissonance there between flesh and spirit, because the flesh sings in accord with the spirit so concordantly that they build up the perfect harmony of the spiritual [1104A] canticle, which is, in the place of our pilgrimage, the best kind of divine canticle. But this *canticle* cannot be sung in a strange land, that is, in a body subject to sins; which is then a strange land when, being by sinning in the power of the devil, it is unworthy of the dwelling of Christ: or it is then estranged from us when, doing ill, it struggles against the spirit and the reason of the mind. Strange is the land. Man by always loving earthly things, and on this account estranged from God, and therefore powerless to sing the canticle of the Lord, because *the sensual man perceiveth not the things that are of the Spirit of God [n: (I Cor. II)]*.

But to what purpose are these things? Plainly to this, that I may show you, most holy sisters, that this office which you have enjoined on me is beyond my strength, because while I am not at all [1104B] or but little fit to sing *the Canticle of the Lord,* far less fit shall I be to sing *the Canticle of the Lord of canticles.* For matter of this kind ought to belong to orators declaiming eloquently and wisely, whose office is to speak aptly for persuading, and whose end is to persuade by speech; but since there is in me neither wisdom nor eloquence, in one who lacks the office of speaking aptly, still less assuredly is the end of persuading by speech present. For I should then be writing to you profitably if I were saying the things I say or am about to say so aptly that I could persuade you to honesty of life and profit of soul. For my land is a strange land, resisting the spirit, serving the Lord of sin, and therefore I am not fit to sing in it *the Canticle of the Lord,* how much less [1104C] *the Canticles of canticles.* But as a certain one was, let it be permitted to one who fears to hope, so that I, who for the smallness of my understanding fear to pursue so sublime a matter, may yet hope through your prayers to be helped, out of regard for the Divinity, in carrying through the things I have begun.
