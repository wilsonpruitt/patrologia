## CHAPTER II FOLLOWS.

VERSE 7.-- *I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, that you stir not up nor make the beloved to awake, till she herself please.*

The beloved, therefore, delighted by the sleep of his beloved, under whose head, that is, her mind, he had placed his left hand, that is, temporal profit, but whom with his right, that is, with the love of eternal life, he had embraced, addresses the daughters of Jerusalem, that is, faithful souls, [1104D] in this manner, saying: *I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, that you stir not up nor make the beloved to awake, till she herself please.*

What is this sleep, so sweet and so wholesome, that for its sake such an adjuration of the beloved is made! Is it not that in which the beloved, resting in sweet and desirable quiet, had said above, *his left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me?*

*97* For she had set all temporal things behind her, [1105A] but with that contemplative eye of the desiring soul she was intent on the eternal and unfailing goods, and in this desirable vision of the mind she was delightfully as it were sleeping. For a sleep of this kind cannot come about lightly in a mind already disengaged from all doings and laid down for itself in great quiet in the love of God. Whence the Lord, admonishing through the Psalmist: *Be free,* he says, *and see that I am God [n: (Psal. XLV)]*. These two words are inseparable in this purpose, because no one at all will be able with the eye of inmost contemplation to see the essence of the Godhead unless he has first been careful to be free from every exterior hindrance: having set *be free* first, then, he adds *see,* so as to intimate that one must first be free in mind from all thought of worldly things, so that thence [1105B] it may become possible, the eye being as it were closed upon earthly things, to sleep in the love of the Divinity with a keener and subtler vision.

But since the grace of this sleep does not come and remain equally, as it is established that one is raised up by a greater, another by a lesser power of contemplation (for Mary advanced further in the divine vision than Peter or John, but these further than any other saint): it is certain that all the saints have the holiness of this sleep in diverse manner, manifold because of the disparity of quiet, so that each in his own place, according to the grace of quiet conferred on him, has his special sleep in the contemplative life. Whence the prophet, in the person of every faithful one who sleeps in this manner, says: *I will take my rest, and consider [1105C] in my place [n: (Isa. XVIII)]*: as if he said openly: Because this is the order of sleep, that it is not permitted to sleep before having quiet, I will take my rest, that is, I will take away from me the causes of all earthly care, and then at last, sleeping in mind from these things, I will cleave to divine contemplation in the place of the grace specially conferred on me. While the beloved, then, sleeps in whatever manner or place, the daughters of Jerusalem are adjured by the beloved that they stir her not up nor make her awake, till she herself please.

But what are *the daughters of Jerusalem* but souls tending towards the vision of heavenly peace, yet, the infirmity of the mind still hindering [sic: *praepudiente*], less able to arrive?

And what is it of the beloved to help, but to bind by the decretal writ of his [1105D] precepts? Further, what are the precepts themselves, but, like roes and harts of the fields, lifting up to heavenly things and making eager and strong in good work, the doctrines of the Holy Scriptures.

Or by *the roes and the harts* we can understand the Fathers of the Old and New Testament, whose talents, virtues and pursuits are denoted by natural behaviour. For it is said that roes have four notable marks of nature: that when she has been wounded she is healed by tasting *98* dittany; that she discerns wholesome herbs by the keenness of her sight; that she ruminates; that she feeds in mountainous places and always seeks the higher. And this is the life and doctrine of the saints: to heal their excesses by humble [1106A] confession, to discern the better sentences of the Holy Scriptures by the keenness of their understanding, to take the food of the divine commandments by reading and to take it up again by committing it to a retentive memory, to rise up from virtues to virtues by daily progress.

In the hart also three commendable marks of nature are observed: namely, that swallowing a serpent, it drinks from a spring and puts off all the venom together with the hairs of its whole body; that, chased by dogs in the densest woods, it opens a way of flight for itself with its horns; that in changing pastures, forming a column, one setting its head on the back of another, they bear one another up because of the weight of their horns.

These things too it befits the faithful to do and to teach: that he who has been infected by some venom of sin should run back to Christ, the fountain [1106B] of mercy, by the infusion of whose kindness and grace he may be free both of that sin and of the other vices of the soul. If in his mind he is chased by malignant spirits, as by certain dogs, in the woods of worldly affairs, which are full of sins and vices, let him make himself a way of escaping them by a strong repulse of the spirit, or, having renounced all occupations of this kind, let him be careful to be free for God alone in the love of the contemplative life, which is also to open for oneself with the horns a way of salvation. For the horn, though it is born of the flesh, goes beyond the flesh; and he who cleaves to the contemplative life, though he be in the flesh, is yet raised up in mind outside the flesh. Let them be careful to bear one another's infirmities of manners or of bodies with equanimity, and to serve one another with dutiful charity; as [1106C] the Apostle says: *Bear one another's burdens and so you shall fulfil the law of Christ [n: (Gal. VI)]*.

*The daughters of Jerusalem,* then, are adjured by the Bridegroom, that is, souls desiring to follow peace are bound, *by the roes and the harts of the fields,* that is, by the precepts and teachings of the holy Fathers, *lest* they stir up *or make to awake* the beloved, that is, lest they disquiet her from the purpose of this inmost quiet and sleep; *till she herself please,* that is, till that inmost indweller of hers inspires her, to whom she is bound by love, so that she may be willing at times to condescend to the infirmity of her neighbours and to provide for their necessary profits. For this willing lies in the disposition of him who provides all things, who by a hidden inspiration makes known to her [1106D] when it is useful and honourable to cleave to contemplation and when to provide for the necessities of neighbours *99* according to place and time.

Finally, the force of this admiration is such, by which the sons of obedience are constrained, that they show their prelates the honour of reverence, because it is written: *Obey those set over you and be subject to them, for they watch as those who will render an account for your souls [n: (Hebr. XIII)]*. Hence it is that they do not dare to rouse them, as vicars of Christ in office, but as spouses [sic: *sponsus*] in the zeal of holy conversation, lest they disturb them, if they compel them by some shamelessness or importunity of their own to be torn away from the secret of inner quiet and more often to be occupied with outward things. The subjects themselves also ought to receive the word of this admiration among one another [1107A], lest they disquiet the pious pursuits of the brethren, whether in reading, or in prayer, or in some spiritual office, so long as the inspiration of the Divinity holds them in it.

But the beloved, ever burning for the quiet of this contemplative sleep, may rejoice that she is confirmed and defended by such an adjuration of the beloved and as it were by right ought not to be disquieted, made more secure she proclaims: *The voice of my beloved.*

VERSE 8.-- *The voice of my beloved, behold he cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping over the hills.*

*The voice,* she says, of the aforesaid admiration is not the voice of some prophet, not of a lawgiver, not of a teacher, as of one announcing or teaching something of himself; but rather of the *beloved* himself, both through himself, and [1107B] through them establishing the decrees of his mysteries and commandments; because all Scripture is *the voice of my beloved,* uttered by the spirit of his mouth and written in our hearts by the finger of his divinity. And therefore you ought not to hold this voice of small account, daughters of Jerusalem, so as to rouse me for the sake of outward things and compel me to wake more often.

For this too is a certain kind of the voice of the beloved, a voice adjuring and as it were juridical: *Seek first the kingdom of God and his justice and all these things shall be added unto you [n: (Matth. VI)]*, so that this ought to be fair, the beloved himself dictating it, that for those who cleave to God the necessary use of the creature should consequently serve. The beloved himself plainly taught and confirmed it by his own testimony, when he extolled the partner of this my pursuit [1107C] with such praise, *Mary,* he said, *hath chosen the best part, which shall not be taken away from her [n: (Luc. X)]*. This surely is also *the voice of the beloved,* so that I ought rightly to set this pursuit of my sleep before the watchings of all affairs. But what is it that I heard and learned from this voice of my beloved, that is, from Holy Scripture, when through the quiet of the mind I slept in his contemplation? This, assuredly, which I will now tell you, O daughters of Jerusalem, of the power of my beloved himself. *Behold he cometh leaping upon* *100* *the mountains, skipping over the hills. Behold,* she says, *he cometh.* His coming is spoken of not according to the divinity but according to the humanity. For he who according to the divinity is uncircumscribed and not in a place [1107D], how should he be said to come, who, existing immovable and unchangeable, as he remains ever one and the same in himself, so that he is neither changed in place nor borne into diverse states by any variation. But as often as he shall be said to come into the hearts of the saints, this is not according to any movableness of his, but according to their aptitude, by which they apply themselves less or more to his love, that he will be alleged to come through his inspiration. According to the humanity, then, he came, when, made passible for us, he willed to suffer the local translation of human custom: for that he might become visible he came from heaven and assumed human nature, in which he suffered and died and rose again; and he ascended into heaven, all of which he did, to be moved and as it were to come from one thing to another. In this manner of coming, then, [1108A] *the beloved cometh, leaping upon the mountains,* when in the time of the dispensation he shone forth with many and magnificent leaps upon the mountains of the sublime virtues.

For certain mountains are the chief and eminent virtues, upon which this beloved made his leaps when he made certain progressions from virtue to virtue: for God, high above the heavens, came to earth humble by being born; from which mountain of humility he ascended to another mountain, of obedience, *for he was made obedient to God the Father unto death, but the death of the cross [n: (Philip. II)]*; by taking which upon him he made a leap onto the mountain of patience; from which at last he climbed onto the mountain of glory, where by rising again and ascending into heaven he set up the triumph of his victorious death.

[1108B] *This beloved, then, cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping over the hills;* because whatever of wisdom and understanding, whatever of counsel and fortitude, whatever of knowledge and piety, whatever of divine fear, whatever finally of any honesty, justice and holiness ever could be or can be, shone forth more sublimely and more brightly in this beloved, as in the head, before all his members, that is, the saints, who are called hills. Anyone of understanding can see this in part in the books of the Gospels: how this beloved leaped upon the mountains, that is, upon the virtues, by word and example, skipping over the examples and teachings of all the saints by his singular and incomparable pre-eminence of working and doctrine; *he hath rejoiced [1108C] as a giant to run the way, because from the highest heaven is his going out, and his circuit even to the highest of it [n: (Psal. XVIII)]*.
