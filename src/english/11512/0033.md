## CHAPTER II FOLLOWS. (cont.)

But why do I so greatly invite thee [sic: *se*] to these things? Because the cold of unbelief and the storm of blindness and ignorance, which had overcast the whole face of the world, have gone back; and *the flowers have appeared,* that is, the beginnings of good works have now begun to spring up, and *the time of pruning is come,* that is, the counsel and rule of cutting away vices and sins are now had in the time of grace. *The voice* also *of the turtle is heard,* that is, the sound of preaching, from which resound the counsels, precepts and examples of chastity, of solitary conversation, of heavenly speculation and of penance, all of which the turtle expresses, chaste and moaning, a lover of solitude and [1113A] nesting on high. For in the very beginning of the Gospel voice the turtle cries out by words and by manners, whether the Lord Jesus Christ himself, the son of virginity, or his mother herself, the blessed Virgin Mary, or his forerunner, blessed John the Baptist. Finally, the Lord himself, born of the Virgin, is the herald of chastity; led into the desert, he consecrates solitude; praying on the mountain, he admonishes that contemplation be pursued; he exhorts to penance, proclaiming as with a moaning voice: *106* *Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand [n: (Matth. X)]*. The blessed Mother of God also is a singular mirror of chastity, who, solitary [sic: *solidaria*] and set in divine contemplation, is greeted by the angel and taught beforehand that she is to be the mother of Christ, and soon announces the sentence of divine punishment changed by penance [1113B], when she proclaims that *his mercy in generation and generations* is to be [n: (Luc. II)]. But Saint John the Baptist, the son of chastity, a virgin in mind and body, the more he was removed from men, the nearer he stood to God through contemplation; *for he was in [sic: iu] the desert preaching the baptism of penance [n: (Matth. III)]*, and crying with the moaning voice of the turtle: *Bring forth fruits worthy of penance [n: (ibid.)]*.

VERSE 13.-- *The fig tree hath put forth her green figs, the flowering vines have given their fragrance. Arise, make haste, my love, my fair one, and come.* [var: Vulg. Ct 2:13 *Surge, amica mea*, without *propera*, "make haste"]

*The fig tree,* he says, *hath put forth her green figs,* that is, Judaea, hitherto barren, carnal, has begun to understand the ceremonies [1113C] and, by believing *with the heart unto justice, but* by confessing *with the mouth unto salvation [n: (Rom. X)]*, what it savoured carnally, this it now savours spiritually and longs to show by faith and by work.

*The flowering vines also have given their fragrance,* because the gentiles, having heard the word of faith, now begin to spread everywhere the fragrance of good work.

Not without reason, then, is the order of this speech attended to, in which these three, faith, hope and charity; or the other three, intention, working, contemplation, are considered, since these are the consequences of those: that he who believes should hope and love; and no one can hope and love except he who has believed; no one also works unless he first intends what, and how, and when, and why he works; but the subtlety of contemplating [1113D] no one can attain unless he has advanced to it by the sublimity of good works.

But it will not be, as we believe, unfitting to note the sevenfold number of the cause, so that we may show that the effect of the cause itself was always the condescension and good pleasure of the sevenfold Spirit: For the passing of the winter, that is, of unbelief; the passing of the rain, that is, of persecution; the appearing of the flowers, that is, of good works; the cutting away of vices; the proclaiming of the turtle, that is, of the Gospel; the putting forth of the green figs of the fig tree, that is, the revealing of the legal observances into the truth of the mysteries; the sweetly fragrant working of the vines, that is, of the gentiles converted to the faith: what are they but the good pleasure and condescension of the sevenfold Spirit?

For causes of this kind, and what was effected from them, [1114A] or is effected, causes, that is, to believe, to hope, to love, the Spirit of God worked from the beginning and works still daily; because all removal of unbelief and malice, and the bestowal of faith and salvation, stand by the gift of him who provides, composes and orders every *107* state of equity, honesty and profit in holy Church. Whence also the Bridegroom, when he speaks to the bride: *The voice of the turtle is heard,* adds, *in our land,* namely so as to intimate that there is no effect of the sacraments, no hope of salvation, outside the Catholic Church: but that every voice of the Gospel preaching, in the observance of the precepts, in the order and reverence of the sacraments, is to be held within holy Church, in which the Holy Spirit himself is given and received. For *in the beginning,* as it is written, [1114B] *the earth was void and empty,* because before the human mind was made fruitful by the divine spirit, it was composed with no flower or bud of the virtues; but after *the spirit of God moved over the waters [n: (Gen. I)]*, that is, over the flowing and unstable hearts of men, while at length the light of truth and the fruit of justice is poured out through the whole world, so that that earth, at first void and empty, became the Church of God, in which he himself might dwell through his grace, and might make it his land, now flowering and fruitful, and showing forth a certain living and rational pattern of honesty and discipline, as it were of a man, through the indwelling grace.

Since, therefore, the beloved has made the beloved solicitous for her own salvation, he admonishes her not to be negligent [1114C] of the salvation of her neighbours too, so as to intimate this charity, which provides for neighbours as for itself with the zeal of kindness, so that she may deservedly be honoured with the address of the grace that calls and justifies, *love, dove, beautiful one.*

For it follows, when the beloved, the former number of the address being repeated, [says] *arise, make haste, my love, my fair one, and come.* Because, he says, thou hast advanced by my grace to this, that thou art able to take care of thyself, by despising the world and by cleaving to my love through the pursuit of the contemplative life, thou oughtest also at times to profit thy neighbours by word and example through the labour of the active life; because, since I have made thee such that thou hast the grace of teaching and of working, not without peril to thyself wilt thou render account for the buried talent. But arise at some time to this labour [1114D] from that most sweet sleep of thy quiet, for the sake of a brother's need, and make haste by teaching and by drawing back thy neighbours from the love of this world, that thou mayest lead them too with thee into that solitary dwelling of thy quiet. And this thou shalt do as my love through faith, my fair one through working, my dove through the infusion of the Holy Spirit.

VERSE 14.-- *My dove in the clefts of the rock, in the hollow of the stone wall, show me thy face; let thy voice sound in my ears, for thy voice is sweet and thy face comely.*

Because I have compared thee to a dove *108.* But the *dove* has her refuges *in the clefts of the rock,* in *the hollow of the stone wall;* what also will be thy refuges [1115A], in which thou mayest set all the hope of thy salvation, so as to flee from the manifold temptation of this world? surely *in the clefts of the rock,* of which it is written: *And the rock was Christ [n: (I Cor. X)]*; for these clefts are the wounds of my passion, which are to every Christian as it were certain refuges in all adversities. For my five wounds are the safe refuges of thy five senses, so that when thou hast held out the sign of my passion in this number, thou mayest be able to persist secure in the integrity of all thy senses. For this integrity of the senses will be necessary to thee as thou teachest and workest, because if thou bring forth the things thou teachest and workest in my name with honesty and profit, reasonably, orderly and moderately, thou wilt be able the more to draw the minds of those who hear or look on [1115B]. For generally an honourable kind of case, if it is brought forth unworthily, gives offence, just as a lowly kind of case too, if it is at times brought forth reasonably, will obtain a share of honour.

In my passion, then, thou hast been made love, fair one, dove, because to this end I laid down my life for thee, I shed my blood, *that* I might present thee to myself a bride *glorious, not having spot, nor wrinkle [n: (Ephes. V)]*. For when, foul of it through thyself, thou didst not suffice for thyself, nor could the whole creation suffice for thee, my passion, to which the whole world is not equal, made satisfaction to God for thee, so that thence, reconciled and joined to me, thou mayest be called *love, fair one, dove.*

[1115C]Or the clefts of the rock are the four Gospels, in which my deeds and words are looked into; or else the pursuits of the virtues, namely of humility, patience, obedience, prayers, vigils, holy readings, and the rest, which are as it were certain approaches for coming to me and for entering the kingdoms of heaven, of all of which thou oughtest to preach to thy neighbours and to show them, because without these they cannot attain salvation.

*My dove in the clefts of the rock, in the hollow of the stone wall.* Christ the rock [n: (I Cor. X)], Christ also the stone wall. The stone wall indeed before the passion, because he was still frail and passible from human infirmity; but the rock after the resurrection, when, all corruption laid aside, he was made impassible from the firmness of his glorified [1115D] body. But the beloved ought to be a *dove in the clefts of the rock, in the hollow of the stone wall,* because while she considers the many marks of powers, of works and of virtues which were in Christ from the strength of the divinity, yet she ought specially to consider that mark of charity which he showed in his flesh, which he delivered to death for her salvation: for he showed the judgment of this great love towards man *109* when for his redeeming he was able to offer nothing other than himself.

The hollow of the stone wall, then, is the charity of Christ dying in the flesh, in which charity, of the Church indeed generally, but specially any holy soul is a dove, because through charity she finds a place of abiding in Christ, where spiritually she may nourish the young of good [1116A] works and escape the snares of the ancient serpent: for it is written that *God is charity, and he who abideth in charity abideth in God, and God in him [n: (I Joan. IV)]*.

The *dove* is also *in the clefts of the rock, in the hollow of the stone wall.* But the dove has a moaning instead of a song; and the faithful soul ought to moan when she recalls the passion of the Redeemer, weighing what God has done for her, when he *who did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth [n: (I Petr. II)]*, willed to die for sinners, and what man owed, God made man paid. Let her moan also for her excesses, *in the clefts of the rock, in the hollow of the stone wall,* because she has *an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the just: and he is the propitiation for* her *sins* [1116B] [n: (I Joan. II)], who daily confers salvation on every penitent by the sacrament of his passion and the singular example of this charity. For daily our Redeemer renews his clefts for us, that is, the signs of his passion, in the immolation of his body and blood; and he shows the hollow in which we may be protected from the snares of the enemies in the assigning of so great a charity.

The stone wall can also be taken as the doctrine of the apostles, or of apostolic men, because as a stone wall is built of many stones, so the doctrine of the saints is constituted of very many teachings of the virtues. In it there is for every faithful soul a hollow, that is, a keeping of life, because from their sayings and writings she learns how, by fleeing sins and by nourishing the young of good works [1116C], she may escape the snares of malignant spirits.

The beloved, then, is admonished at times to leave the quiet of contemplation and to go out to the labour of the active life for the sake of her neighbours, so that what she herself learned through quiet from the divine precepts and the examples of the saints, this she may bring into the minds of her neighbours by preaching; and as she herself by the sweetness of these was affected as to the assiduity of contemplation, so also she may affect the minds of her neighbours with desire of them through the power of preaching.
