## CHAPTER II FOLLOWS. (cont.)

*Till the day breathe, and the shadows decline,* that is, until, that true and eternal day of future beatitude growing bright, and the night of this worldly misery and tribulation withdrawing *114.*

[1120C]When *God shall be all in all [n: (I Cor. XV)]* he will give me the full and perfect and unchangeable reward of his vision, where he in me with the beauty of his gifts and I in him with the eternal incorruption of body and soul shall be fed.

But because this mutual love which we have set down always befits any father and son, this is proper to those things which are said relatively, that the very thing that they are, they are said [sic: *dicautur*] to be of others, or in whatever way something to something else. For whatever is recognized in the nature of relation, it is necessary that it be considered together with another: for when father is said, it is necessary that there be a son whose father he is said to be, and in the same way, by conversion, it must be said of the son. If, then, the saints are sons of God, [1120D] through love and the observance of his commandments, it is necessary that God himself be called their father by grace, by creation and by governance, and conversely, if God is father, they are sons. Whence here too it is not absurd to say *my beloved to me* in respect of the father, *and I to him* in respect of the son or daughter; for God himself says in a certain place of his own: I will be to them for a father, and they shall be to me for sons and daughters [n: (II Reg. VII)].

Or let us say it in another way; it seems to have almost the like sense of relation, so that when it is said *my beloved to me,* there is understood: is a helper and protector; *and I to him,* there is understood: am obedient. These too are brought together with each other, so that because he is a helper and protector to me, therefore I am obedient to him, and because I am obedient to him, he bestows his help upon me.

[1121A] *Who feedeth among the lilies,* because he delights in the whiteness of virtues and the cleanness of thoughts and of works, as he says in a certain place: *And I am present to clean thoughts [n: (Prov. VIII)]*; and as it is written: *Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God [n: (Matth. V)]*, so long does he work the effect of his grace in me, until the day of understanding and of spiritual knowledge shine forth, the darkness of ignorance, or of carnal understanding, having been put to flight. For the more the mind is joined to God in advances of the virtues, the higher it is lifted up in the subtlety of divine contemplation.

Or *until the day is,* that is, the light and splendour of good works which has shone forth in my mind through the indwelling of divine grace, may shine again upon the hearts of others by word and example, and remove from them every shadow or [1121B] night of depravity and malice: for the fruit of the light is in all goodness and justice and truth.

*Return: be thou like, my beloved, to a roe, and to a young hart upon the mountains of Bethel.* [var: Vulg. Ct 2:17 *super montes Bether*, "upon the mountains of Bether"]

While the beloved was feeding among the lilies of the soul, shining white and fragrant with virtues, he so refreshes that soul with the sweetness of his charity that, despising earthly things, she was wholly suspended in heavenly things, and yet she could not comprehend the uncircumscribed divinity *115* as it is. Whence, when with the whole effort of her mind she pursues the beloved, who exceeds all sense and as it were in a certain manner flees, because he is incomprehensible and ineffable, she is beaten back by the radiance of so immense a [1121C] brightness, and does not judge that he is to be pursued further, whom she considers can in no way be comprehended. For to this it is written of the holy living creatures that, when a voice came above the firmament, they stood and let down their wings [n: (Ezech. I)]. By the name of firmament, indeed, the supernal powers are designated, which, while other companies of angels were falling from heaven, themselves stood firm in the love of God. But the voice in the mind is a certain sound of understanding; but one voice comes to be beneath the firmament, another from the firmament, and another above the firmament. For as holy minds lift themselves up from earthly things to things above, a voice comes to be beneath the firmament when they understand subtly some things of the nature, order, quality of visible things; but a voice comes to be from the firmament when they weigh the ineffable and eternal joys of those supernal [1121D] spirits; but a voice comes to be above the firmament when, in contemplating, they think something of the loftiness of the Divinity, of which, because they can comprehend nothing fully as it is, standing and not able to advance further, they humbly lay down the wings of contemplation, and they are astounded at him whom they understand to be incomprehensible; being astounded, they venerate and are afraid; being afraid, they love; loving, they at some time behold him as he is.

Because, therefore, in the contemplation of the Divinity the beloved fails, and cannot long follow the beloved as though already flying away, she calls him back and implores that at least he deign in some manner to reveal the sacraments of his humanity, [1122A] saying: *Return, my beloved, be thou like to a roe or to a young hart upon the mountains of Bethel;* as if she said in other words: Since I am not able perfectly to grasp the sublimity of thy divinity, *for we see now through a glass in a dark manner [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*, at least grant me to search out and to understand the truth of thy humanity, the reason of the truth, the utility of the reason; the truth in faith, the reason in the cause, the utility in the end of the cause. For we believe that thou wast made man for this reason, that thou mightest save the human race, whose end is eternal life.

But in the action of a cause of this kind thou wast like to a roe or to a young hart, because, born of the stock of the patriarchs, like a young hart of servants thou didst swiftly dispense the order of human salvation; for [1122B] after thou didst begin to preach, in three years and a half thou didst accomplish the universal good of so celebrated and wonderful a business, and having suffered thou didst rise quickly after three days; and after forty days thou didst ascend the heavens, and such an outrunning and return was as of a roe or of a young hart leaping on the mountains.

*116* But this outrunning or return of thine was upon the mountains of Bethel, that is, of the house of God, which is the Church, whose advance and institution was being carried on in this outrunning and return; upon whose mountains, that is, the apostles and the other holy preachers, like a swift young hart thou didst run together by the ready gift of thy grace, when thou didst teach them by the sudden coming of the Holy Spirit, so that what others can scarcely grasp through long spaces of time, these attained in one moment. [1122C] For by what could thy leap upon these mountains be swifter than to give in one hour the knowledge of all tongues.

Grant, then, that I may understand the sacraments of all these things, that understanding I may be able to meditate on them, that I may not draw back from them in faith and work, in faith by believing, in work by imitating according to my power. For thou art like to a roe or to a young hart in my heart when I turn over these sacraments of thy dispensation, namely how, to institute these things, thou didst run as by certain leaps from heaven into the womb of the virgin, from the womb onto the cross, from the cross into the sepulchre, from the sepulchre again into heaven. But this likeness appears to me *upon the mountains of Bethel,* that is, in the Scriptures which holy Church holds [1122D] and venerates, which Scriptures, namely, are mountains on account of the loftiness of the senses which are contained in them, from the utilities of the divine precepts or from the sacraments and the realities of the sacraments.

But because the whole force of this love between him who is beloved and her who is beloved hangs on faith in the sacraments and the observance of the divine commandments, having let go that intention of seeking by which she wished to comprehend God in his nature, but could by no means, she began to attend to how she might comprehend God through faith and justice. For if, according to the voice of John, *God is charity [n: (I Joan. IV)]*, and to have faith and justice is to love God, assuredly to have this charity is to have God.

[1123A]But what do we call this charity, except all that good which can be conceived in the mind, believed in the heart, confessed with the mouth, exercised in work? truly no one attains all this good except him who perfectly loves God, and no one loves God except him who attains this good, as was set down a little above, *my beloved to me and I to him.*

For this good is a certain way, which leads to better things to be comprehended, of which life the term and close is eternal life.
