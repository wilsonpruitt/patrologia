## CHAPTER THREE. (cont.)

Behold the roes and harts of the divine *122* fields, which show that it is more useful to sleep in the chamber of contemplation than to keep watch for exterior business; not that these things ought to be omitted altogether, when the profit or necessity of neighbours demands it, but that the part of Mary is better than that of Martha.

[1127B]With these, then, and other like sentences, as with certain incantations, the beloved adjures the daughters of Jerusalem, lest lightly and without cause they compel their neighbours to come down from inmost quiet and to pass over to exterior actions. Wherefore you, O daughters of Jerusalem, who see the vision of peace, yet so, *if you walk in the Spirit, and have mortified the deeds of the flesh [n: (Rom. VIII)]*, *for the flesh lusteth against the spirit, but the spirit against the flesh [n: (Gal. V)]*, to be free of whose wars is to see the vision of peace, always love the secret of holy contemplation, always be zealous to bring this beloved, that is, the Lord Jesus Christ, into the chamber [1128A] of your mind, so that when the Spirit of divine charity has taken you up, and has taught you to despise all earthly things, you may hear with the prophet *behind* you *the voice of a great commotion: Blessed be the glory of the Lord from his place [n: (Ezech. III)]*.

For you are the place of the glory of God when you receive him in your hearts through the love of holiness, and if you sprinkle forth from yourselves anything of virtue and grace through humility, patience, obedience and chastity, and the other works of piety, it is the glory of God, from whom and through whom you have it, and the same himself is blessed, to whose praise and glory profits all that is seen in you of honesty and discipline.

For this is the voice of a great commotion, because those who are lifted up through the spirit are moved with so great a grace [1128B] of compunction that they afflict themselves with grievous lamentations, either because they are still bound by their sins, or because they are put off so long from the joys of the heavenly life.

You hear also behind you the voice of a great commotion when you attend to the tumults and fleeting joys of this world, but out of love of the eternal joys set them after in your mind, and, forgetting the things that are behind, and stretching yourselves forth to what is to come, you follow on to the prize of the supernal vocation, so that the hearing of this voice may be no small consolation to you, while it is said: *Blessed be the glory of the Lord from his place.*
