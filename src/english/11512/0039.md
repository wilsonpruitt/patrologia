## CHAP. III.-- *Continuation.* (cont.)

Holy Church, therefore, which has been gathered from two peoples coming from the foreskin and from the circumcision, ascends through the desert of faithlessness when, the unbelievers of both peoples remaining in faithlessness, she herself among them grew up by faith, hope, charity to wonderful summits of virtues. And the more grievously she was crushed and as it were broken small by their persecuting, the higher did she send up from the fire of the Holy Spirit the smoke of holiness, by the straightness and rigour of life and of doctrine. Whence it is added: *As a little rod of smoke from the aromatics of myrrh [1132B] and of frankincense and of the whole powder of the perfumer.*

She was as a little rod, because she remained straight and rigid in end and in operation: but she was a little rod of smoke, because that very straightness and strength smoked from the fire of divine love. But what was the material in which this divine fire grew hot? assuredly faith, hope and charity, which are signified by the kinds of aromatics, of myrrh and of frankincense and of the whole powder of the perfumer. Now myrrh has a bitter juice, and with it also the bodies of the dead are embalmed lest they putrefy; but frankincense is offered in sacrifice to God; further, from the whole powder of the perfumer a most precious ointment is made. Nine made, when for the preservation of the orthodox integrity of the faith they suffered from the unbelievers and heretics, [1132C] the corrupters of the faith itself, persecutions and deaths many and various, sent up the myrrh of smoking faith as it were into heaven; for through the whole world this smoke of faith and of sufferings ascended, which blinded and put to flight the unbelieving and the persecutors by the bitterness of miracles, but enlightened and confirmed believers with how sweet an odour. But when, fervent in the worship of God and in religion put into deed, they were continually instant in prayers for the state of the Church and for attaining the eternal beatitude which they hoped for, did they not also bring forth the frankincense of smoking and fragrant hope? for unless they had a firm hope of future goods, they would by no means assiduously supplicate God to attain them. But when, strong in faith and hope, they could say with the Apostle: *I am sure that neither life, nor death, nor angels, [1132D] nor principalities, nor powers, nor things present, nor things to come, nor might, nor height, nor depth, nor any creature shall be able to separate us from the charity of God [n: (Rom. VIII)]*. Did they not spread on high the odour of the whole powder of the perfumer? For what is charity but the whole powder of the perfumer? For charity is a universal virtue, because all virtues come forth from its root, and therefore wherever there is any virtue, there is charity.

But who is this perfumer, if not the Holy Spirit, because by his grace he compounds this universal powder? *For the charity of God is poured forth in our hearts by the Holy Spirit who is given to us [n: (Rom. V)]*.

*128* Let us see to what great subtlety the Apostle reduced charity as it were into powder, when, [1133A] enumerating its parts: *Charity,* he says, *is patient, is kind, envieth not, dealeth not perversely, is not puffed up, is not ambitious, seeketh not her own, is not provoked to anger, thinketh no evil, rejoiceth not in iniquity, but rejoiceth with the truth, beareth all things, believeth all things, hopeth all things, endureth all things [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*. Behold into how many parts he divides charity, so that through these determinate parts he may intimate that whatever virtue shall have been the last, and the most fortified [cj: *munitissima*; read *minutissima*, "the smallest"] of the whole body of good, this is the atom of charity. But where all is said, nothing is excepted; all good therefore is charity; therefore, as the whole powder of the perfumer is made from all precious spices, so charity is composed of all the virtues of the soul, so that not unfittingly it is marked [1133B] by the signification of the whole powder of the perfumer; for where any virtue is lacking, charity is not perfect.

Every soul too that is beloved of God ascends through the desert of this world when, having spurned and set after all its cares and business and delights, she always tends to heavenly things by advances of the virtues, and although she be detained in the world by the body, yet she dwells in heavenly things by the mind. Through the desert also she makes ascents of the soul, because the more the soul [cj: *quanto animus*; read *quanto minus animus*, "the less the soul"] is occupied about exterior things, and the more it is deserted by the frequency of useless thoughts, the more is it borne up by the love of divinity and the delight of good acts. And this *as a little rod of smoke from the aromatics of myrrh and of frankincense and of the whole powder of the perfumer,* so that, according to the sense above, she [1133C] too may have the rod of equity, by faith, hope and charity, through the grace of the Holy Spirit. In this vale of tears also let the myrrh of labour and patience smoke in the sight of God, as she weighs: *that the sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come that shall be revealed in us [n: (Rom. VIII)]*. Let her kindle also the most pure frankincense by the instancy of prayers, delighted by whose sweet odour God may confer on her the grace of his blessing, through which she may grow and be multiplied with an offspring of virtues. And let her be fragrant with the whole powder of the perfumer, so that with a spirit of humility and a contrite mind she may render to God every office of holiness, *for a sacrifice to God is an afflicted spirit [n: (Psal. L)]*: for one is sifted as it were into the finest powder [1133D] when, through the breaking of a heart greatly humbled, one set in the highest virtues is made thin before the eyes of God. For with the finest powder of this breaking and humility, kindled by the fire of the Holy Spirit, David was smoking when he said: *129* *I am a worm and no man [n: (Psal. XXI)]*, and when in another place he called himself a dead dog and a flea [n: (I Reg. XXIV, XXVI)], and in the Psalm Moses is said to have stood *in the breaking in the sight of God, to turn away his wrath [n: (Psal. CV)]*. For with how great contrition of spirit and virtue of compassion was he crushed when, praying for the sin of the people, he said to God: *Either forgive them this trespass, or if thou do not, strike me out of thy book that thou hast written? [n: (Exod. XXXII.)]* Paul also, broken small by the abasement of humility, names himself the least of the apostles [1134A] [n: (II Cor. XV)], but, crushed by the breaking of piety and compassion, wishes to become anathema for his brethren [n: (Rom. IX)].

But this ascent is said to have been *as a little rod of smoke*; for smoke, when it ascends, so fails little by little in ascending that it cannot be seen at all, because so great is the subtlety of the contemplative life in the saints, so great the sublimity of holy conversation through the desert of inmost solitude, that it escapes the minds of those who marvel rather than it is held by consideration to imitate it, so that, while it does not prevail to comprehend by following, it fails in the very consideration of it.

VERSE 7.-- *Behold, threescore valiant ones of the most valiant of Israel surround the bed of Solomon.*

It is not repugnant to reason, as I think, if we take this *bed of Solomon* [1134B] in three ways, either as the universal Church, or the soul of each saint, or the holy Scripture of both testaments; not that he who is uncircumscribed is comprehended by places, but that the granting and preservation of graces and of saving effects is his repose in some one of the aforesaid, saving, guarding and governing.

For the universal Church is the bed of this Solomon, that is, of the peaceable man, our Lord Jesus Christ, because he rests in her through the saving observance of his commandments and the life-giving concorporation of his sacraments, which he displays to her under the species of sacramental things, pacifying those things which are in heaven and on earth, so that there may be as it were one commonwealth of this magnificent emperor, namely one [1134C] Church, both that which rejoices in the fatherland and that which is still a pilgrim on earth. *For the head of the Church is Christ, but she the body of Christ [n: (I Cor. XI)]*; and as the body without the head does not live, nor can do anything; and as the head is not vital without the body; so also Christ confers life on no one without the unity of the body of the Church. Christ and the Church, then, are one, who, since the head is undivided from its body, whoever does not have the peace and unity of the Church does not have Christ either: whence also in the very body of the sacraments, namely in the body and blood of Christ, to the head the body, that is, *130* to Christ the Church, is made consacramental and concorporal, by her own and by his mystery, when the mystery of Christ taken in the truth of his body [1134D] profits no one unless he has taken up the mystery of the Church, namely the ecclesiastical fellowship, in the same sacrament; because, since, as we said, Christ is undivided from his body, that is, the Church, in his sacrament the same Christ is not said to be truly taken unless he be taken whole, concorporated to that same Church by his own and the Church's mystery; for he himself says: *He that eateth my flesh and drinketh my blood, abideth in me and I in him [n: (Joan. VI)]*.

Where, then, does Christ rest more than in the Church, which daily in partaking is concorporated to his body and blood and is made as it were one with him? Whence those who cut themselves off from the unity of the Church by living evilly or by teaching perversely, because they do not abide in the Church, since Christ and the Church are one, do not abide [1135A] in Christ, and therefore, when they partake of his body, they take him indeed in the truth of the substance, but they do not take him to the effect of their salvation. For this is not truly to take him, because he is not taken whole, since indeed, since at the altar the Church, as has been said, is concorporal and consacramental in Christ, he does not take the whole body of Christ, namely the head with the members, who, since he is outside the Church, does not unite himself to Christ and to the Church in the sacrament of both. He takes indeed the sacrament itself according to its essence, which turns out to some for life, to some for their end; but the virtue and the reality of the sacrament he does not take, because, since this is the virtue and the reality of the sacrament, that by loving one another whoever [1135B] partake of that which profits no one to his end, but profits every believer to salvation, be of the unity of the body of the Church, whoever, Christ conforming and uniting them, shall not have been true partakers of so great a fellowship, do not truly take Christ, while they are proved to take him not to their salvation but to judgment: for since Christ himself commands, saying: *If thou offer thy gift at the altar, and there thou remember that thy brother hath anything against thee, leave there thy gift before the altar, and go first to be reconciled to thy brother, and then coming thou shalt offer thy gift [n: (Matth. V)]*; he shows that the gift is not to be accepted where the soul of the offerer has not had the bond of peace and charity. But if this unity, not kept individually among fellow members, repels [1135C] the gifts of those who offer, how much more will he who deserts the unity of the whole body of the Church be able neither to offer a gift nor to partake of what is offered?

But this bed is Solomon's, that is, the *peaceable one's*, who said: *My peace I give unto you, peace I leave unto you [n: (Joan. XIV)]*, who confederates the things which are in heaven and on earth, because, since the Church, the body of Christ, is one, which partly reigns in heaven with Christ *131*, partly is still a pilgrim in the body, whoever withdraws from the unity of this pilgrim part does not merit the fellowship of that supernal part, since indeed, since they are one according to the virtue and reality of the sacrament, that is, of ecclesiastical unity, although they are not yet associated in one place, yet they are so joined in spirit that whoever does not have the one, [1135D] does not have the other. Whence in this bed of this peaceable one, that is, in the Church, in which Christ still rests through the veils of the sacraments, whoever through penance has recovered the lost unity merits to be associated also to that which is on high, when he comes and incorporates in reality who reconciled us to God through his blood.
