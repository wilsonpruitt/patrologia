## CHAP. III.-- *Continuation.* (cont.)

Let us say, by likeness, that in the thigh is also the seed-bed of offspring, from which, conception having been given, man is formed, who, his members being distinguished one by one in the womb, is at length brought forth by being born, and little by little, through the stages of the ages, is led on into the perfect man. And what is the suggestion of the delighting flesh in each man, but a certain conception of a vice suddenly rushing in? Which, if it is not at once pressed down by the sword of the mind stoutly resisting, takes form in the mind through delight, then through consent is as it were born, [1139D] and so, growing step by step through action, it is made through habit a damnable and deadly vice; for in the propagating of each vice these are the parts of its progression, namely suggestion, delight [sic: *delectasio*], consent, action, habit, just as in man too these are the parts of the process of birth, that is, conception, formation, nativity, the drawing-out of the body and the fulness of age. Every man's sword, therefore, ought to be upon his thigh, so that, the head of evil suggestion being cut off [sic: *praecito*] and the seed-bed, as it were, of malice to be conceived being weakened, the whole body of the vice that would be born may be driven off; and why this? *Because of fears,* he says, *in the night.* For what do those who guard a city, or a house, fear by night, but sudden enemies or thieves, but malignant spirits, [1140A] who try to plunder or steal the treasures of our soul? These indeed, through the night of depraved suggestion or delight, with which they wrap the mind of each man so that it cannot discern the peril of the evil itself, rush in suddenly and creep in, and wound the mind with such darts of temptations that at times they cast it down from the state of its rightness. But this they can do only by night, when, the warmth of pious zeal withdrawing, the cold of torpor and negligence has taken hold of the mind; when the sun of justice has set in it on account of sloth and has not shone upon it with the light of his grace; because then they make ready their arrows that they may shoot at the poor man in secret [n: (Psal. X)].

Because of these fears in the night, therefore, that is, the hidden ambushes of temptations, each one ought [1140B] to stand girded with the arms of justice, and to watch all the entrances *136* of his inner house so vigilantly that no place of entering may lie open to the enemy. Zeal of this kind shows who are the valiant or most valiant, who guard, or are able to guard, the bed of the true Solomon by surrounding it, and who are most expert in the girding of a holy purpose, always holding the swords of providence and of ready vigilance, because of the sudden fears in the night of creeping vice.

But where and whence these most valiant ones receive the knowledge and daring of this spiritual warfare, what follows suggests [sic: *insinuaut*]; for it is added: *King Solomon made himself a litter,* etc.

VERSE 9.-- *Solomon made himself a litter of the wood of Libanus.*

[1140C]For whence do they receive it to be valiant, if not from the doctors of holy Church, whose deeds and words they desire to imitate, or from the testimonies and authorities of the holy canonical Scriptures.

Now the litter of this true king Solomon is holy Church, or the whole canonical Scripture of the Church itself, or, in the individual, the soul of any just man; for since "ferculum", a litter, is so called from *ferendo*, bearing, each one of these is surely not absurdly named a litter equivocally: the Church, indeed, on account of the bearing of the divine sacraments which are contained in it; holy Scripture on account of its taking up of the divine utterances, or precepts, whose mysteries and utility it contains; but the soul of the just man [1140D] on account of its perceiving of divine knowledge, through which it is made capable of honourableness and discipline; all which are the delights of spiritual banquets.

And how the several members can adhere to each head of those we have set forth, we shall take care to set out; that is, we shall assign how the silver pillars, the golden reclining-place, the purple ascent, and the midst of these strewed with charity, fit either the Church or canonical Scripture or the soul of the just man.

First, however, it must be considered in what way each of these litters is said to be built of the wood of Libanus: Libanus is interpreted *whitening*. And what is understood by the wood of Libanus, which is recorded to be by nature incorruptible, but the saints and elect, [1141A] who through strength of spirit and the whiteness of worldly life have kept, and keep, an incorrupt holiness in thought, speech and work?

Of the wood of Libanus, therefore, king Solomon established a litter for himself, because Christ, out of the venerable and incorrupt deeds and teachings of his saints, both most becomingly ennobled his Church, and composed his holy Scripture, and instructed the soul of each just man, so that *131* each one of these should be a litter of heavenly banquets and a refreshment of faithful souls.

VERSE 10.-- *The pillars thereof he made of silver, the reclining-place of gold and the ascent of purple: the midst he strewed with charity for the daughters of Jerusalem.*

The silver and the gold are fittingly [1141B] understood in each one on account of the brightness and purity of the utterances, and the majesty of the senses which shine in those same utterances. For if thou refer it to the Church, its pillars are of silver, because the holy patriarchs and prophets, and the apostles too, or apostolic men, in their preaching, or in the exposition of each testament by which they lift up and sustain the Church, set forth what they say or write in faithful and lucid speech.

In which speech the reclining-place is of gold, because their very preaching, or exposition, glows with the precious brightness of its senses; in which anyone who before laboured in not knowing and in seeking, now understanding, may as it were by resting redeem himself, and leans the mind wearied as it were by ignorance upon the banquets of inner refreshment.

[1141C]The ascent of this litter too is of purple, because the highest profession in the peace of the Church is that which, by contempt of the world, mortification of the flesh and the casting away of one's own will, arranges an ascent made purple by the martyrdom of the mind, when the mind offers itself *to God* *a living victim for an odour of sweetness [n: (Ephes. V)]*. For no one can ascend by contemplation to the highest step of divine charity unless he is free from all the cares and desires of the world; because, although the steps of the saints ascending by virtues are many and diverse, as it is written of them: *They shall go from virtue to virtue [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)]*; yet the highest ascent, as we learn, is where the mind lifts itself above itself by contemplative charity. For in this purple [1141D] ascent there are diverse steps, because one is more eminent than another; for it is more to pray, fast and keep vigil than only to meditate and read; it is more to bear insults and reproaches or persecutions of whatever kind for justice than only to abstain from injustice: yet in all ascents of this kind the labour of body and mind, and the continence of life, is as it were a certain precious purple of the mind.

But *the midst* of this litter king Solomon *strewed with charity for the daughters of Jerusalem,* because in the midst of these so sublime merits in the Church, many minds, as though strewn low by frailty and infirmity, are laid down; because, though they are not able to leave the world perfectly, yet they love and venerate those who despise the world; though they cannot labour for [1142A] God by fasting, keeping vigil and praying, or by mortifying the flesh, yet while they love these things in those who do them, they make them as it were their own, and what they themselves *132* do not have in themselves through infirmity, this they have in others through charity. Such souls certainly belong to this litter, and are refreshed in it in their own manner, because what the saints and the perfect attain in deed and virtue, these attain by charity alone.

Further, if thou look upon the whole body of canonical Scripture, *its pillars of silver, the reclining-place* also *of gold,* are the sentences and authorities of the divine utterances, clear in the brightness of their words, and shining in the singular excellence of their senses, the reading of which is the sweetest refreshment of the soul, since there faith sustains, hope lifts up, charity through contemplation [1142B] makes to recline. Whence also the reclining-place is of gold, because contemplative charity is greater than faith and hope, as gold is more precious than silver. For when any of the faithful has learned from a litter of this kind, by reading or hearing, what he ought to believe, what to hope, then, all transitory things being put behind, in the love of God alone, as in a certain golden reclining-place of contemplation, he is fed and nourished through quiet of mind unto eternal life.

In this litter the ascent is of purple, because therein are recited the passion of the Redeemer, and the glorious triumphs of the martyrs dying for the love of that same Redeemer, in which passions there were several ascents: for as we can see in the very head of the martyrs, first scourged, then smeared with spittle, struck with blows of the hand, [1142C] crowned with thorns, lastly fastened to the cross, he made as it were a certain purple ascent in his passion; following whose footsteps the martyrs had the ascents of manifold labour, because *having had trial of mockeries and stripes, moreover of bands and prisons, they were stoned, they were cut asunder, they were tempted,* at the last *they died in the slaughter of the sword [n: (Hebr. XI)]*, so that through these ascents of passions they might reach the highest step of eternal beatitude. But when pious minds read and hear of this ascent, they busy themselves to imitate it, so that, as of the temporal passions, so also of the eternal consolations they may be made partakers.

The midst also of this litter is strewed with charity, because in the whole divine page charity holds as it were the middle [1142D] place, while all the sentences of the holy Scriptures look to it as to a certain best and sure becoming, as the Apostle says, that *the end of the precept is charity from a pure heart and a good conscience and a faith unfeigned [n: (I Tim. I)]*. And when the Lord showed the two precepts of charity, namely of God and of neighbour, *on these,* he says, *two precepts the whole law hangeth and the prophets [n: (Matth. XXII)]*; but that from which all other things hang seems in a manner to hold the highest and principal place. *The midst* therefore of this litter king Solomon *strewed with charity,* because in the midst of the holy Scriptures he established the primacy of charity.

And why this? *For the daughters of Jerusalem,* that is, for the souls belonging to the heavenly Jerusalem, that is, the vision [1143A] of peace, that they may understand that neither the silver pillars, nor the golden reclining-place, nor *133* the purple ascent, has anything of virtue without the virtue of charity, but that all things, and by charity, ought to proceed as many branches from the one root of a tree. For the Apostle says that *if I speak with the tongues of men and of angels,* which is to have silver pillars, *and have not charity, I am become as sounding brass or a tinkling cymbal; and if I should have prophecy and should know all mysteries and all knowledge,* which is to be a golden reclining-place, *and if I should deliver my body so that I burn,* which is to have a purple ascent, *and have not charity, it profiteth me nothing [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*.

[1143B]Behold how our king Solomon *strewed the midst with charity* in this his litter, when through the whole body of holy Scripture he established charity as in a certain centre, so that all things revolve round it and into it. And as the sun is highest when it is fixed in the centre and goes no further, so holy Scripture, disputing of the virtues, is highest in the proclaiming of charity, and does not go beyond it, to the prejudice of any virtue.
