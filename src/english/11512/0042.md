## CHAP. III.-- *Continuation.* (cont.)

But in the third place, if thou consider the soul of any just man, its pillars of silver are the four cardinal virtues, of which all the others are appendages, namely prudence, justice, fortitude, temperance. But how of silver? By the purity of proven faith and by the true preaching of evangelical doctrine, drained of all [1143C] dross of heretical falsity, of which it is written: *The words of the Lord are pure words, silver tried by the fire [n: (Ps. XI)]*. Whence the Lord says of heretics through the prophet: I gave them silver and gold, but they made Baal out of the silver and gold [n: (Ose. II)]; now gold pertains to the mind, silver to utterance: for God grants gold and silver to a holy soul when he grants it to have both a divine sense and wisdom, and to bring forth with beauty of utterance that which the mind conceives. And because heretics deprave this sense and, teaching it depraved, give it utterance, they as it were fabricate idols of falsity out of gold and silver.

He, then, who wishes that his virtues, if he has any, should be pillars [1143D] which sustain the structure of the soul, ought to take pains that they be of silver, because, unless they shine with the purity of faith and doctrine, it is greatly to be feared lest, not being able to bear the structure itself, they sink down through the weakness of their crookedness. But of whom the pillars are of silver, his reclining-place too is of gold, because *out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh [n: (Matth. XII)]*; since where wisdom abounds in the sense, there praiseworthy eloquence flows forth outwardly. It is written: *Wisdom resteth in the heart of the just, and prudence in the speech of his mouth [n: (Prov. XIX)]*. For when the mind shines with the brightness of wisdom, it follows that it utters prudently that by which it understands anything. But why is the mind of the just man called a reclining-place? Surely because the Godhead, in a mind which pleases it by the beauty of humility, patience, chastity and [1144A] obedience, is said to recline itself through the gold, the gift of its indwelling grace; *134* whereas, on the contrary, it is said of the minds of the reprobate that *the Son of man hath not where to lay his head [n: (Matth. VIII)]*.

And what is the purple ascent of this litter? The faith and memory of the Lord's passion; to believe which, and to set in it all the hope of one's salvation, and after its form to crucify oneself also to the world, to vices and concupiscences, so that one can say with the Apostle: *The world is crucified to me, and I to the world [n: (Gal. VI)]*, is without doubt to ascend by the purple ascent of the mind.

And how this litter's *midst with charity* is strewed *for the daughters of Jerusalem,* the Apostle [1144B] shows, who says: *I became weak to the weak, that I might gain the weak [n: (I Cor. IX)]*; whence is this but from charity? For since love is twofold, namely of God and of neighbour, and the one hangs from the other, whoever is endowed with the highest virtues ought to have this midst: that, when brotherly necessity or utility has required it, he should come down a little from that height of his rigour, and should provide for the infirmity of his neighbours by condescending to it.

For in this way, unless he love his neighbour, he is by no means proved to love God, since the one love is established from the other: *For he that loveth not his neighbour whom he seeth, how can he love God whom he seeth not? [n: (I Joan. IV.)]* [var: Vulg. *fratrem suum*, "his brother"]

Whether, then, the Church, or holy Scripture, or [1144C] the soul of any just man, be called the litter of this true Solomon, what follows does not resist the members from adhering, not unfittingly, to each head. But if the gaze of the mind be applied diligently, it will not be, as I think, tedious to consider how beautifully the last member of this litter is sealed with the conclusion of charity. For what is there of religion in the Church, what of sacramental reality, what of knowledge or eloquence in the whole divine page, what of holiness and justice in the soul of a saint, which does not set its whole beginning or end in regard to charity? And further, what are the sacraments of the Church, what the utterances of the whole of holy Scripture, or what is it that the soul of the just man ought to believe, to hope and to love, [1144D] but the incarnation, passion, resurrection and ascension of Christ, and the showing forth of all the things he did for human salvation? And what are the holy proclamations of all these, but signs of divine charity? *for on account of his exceeding charity wherewith God loved us, he sent his Son in the likeness of flesh, made a curse for us, that he might redeem us from the curse [n: (Ephes. II)]*. Whether, then, this litter be called the Church, or holy Scripture, or the soul of the just man, everywhere this charity holds the middle place, because from it all things proceed, and to it all things look. For the whole sum of this charity is *God made man,* and this is the whole that the saints teach in the Church through Holy Scripture, namely that we should attend with all our might to the [1145A] *135* greatness of this divine charity and mercy, because through it we have been freed from death and restored to the eternal life which we had lost by sinning.

Whence now, in what follows, the daughters of Sion are admonished to attend diligently to the grace of the divine goodness, not only in the thing but also in the working of the thing itself, and to take notice of the wonderful ordering of their salvation.

VERSE 11.-- *Go forth and see, O daughters of Sion, king Solomon in the diadem wherewith his mother crowned him, in the day of his espousals and in the day of the joy of his heart.*

Sion is said to be *watching, and the daughters* of *Sion,* that is, *of watching,* who are they but the souls of believers, who through the grace of contemplation keenly watch into the [1145B] secrets of the heavenly mysteries? To them it is said that they should go forth, because they ought by no means to be occupied in carnal things who desire to be held by the eye of the mind in spiritual things. *Go forth,* he says, *and see,* for you cannot see unless first you go forth: *Go forth* therefore, that is, go out from the consideration of corporeal and visible things, and fix the gaze of the mind on the glory of the divine magnificence, and on the very cause of the universal redemption, and on the whole incorporeality, altogether, of human salvation. Why? Namely for this, that you may see *king Solomon in the diadem wherewith his mother crowned him:* but with what eye ought you to see? Surely with the inner rather than the outer, because by the faith of corporality you ought to go out to corporality; [1145C] it behoves you to understand spiritually, not carnally, the things which you see or hear: for so it is said *see,* as if it were said, understand.

You, therefore, *daughters of Sion,* who are called not sons, on account of strength, but daughters, on account of infirmity; because you have not yet advanced into the robustness of spiritual understanding, since king Solomon has made a litter for you, at whose banquets you sit daily by looking into the sacraments of the Church, by reading and hearing canonical scripture, by searching out also the sentences of the holy doctors, *go forth* with a higher sense *and see* that same *king Solomon in the diadem wherewith his mother crowned him.*

What or of what kind is this diadem of king Solomon *wherewith his mother crowned him?* We can indeed [1145D] say, taken singly, the one sign of the passion, namely the crown of thorns with which his mother the Synagogue, who begot him according to the flesh, crowned him in mockery; but it seems better that we take this diadem as the whole sum of the twofold passion, which, as a diadem is composed of many kinds of precious stones, so is composed of many signs of diverse sufferings; which *136* after he had borne it in his body, he was crowned with glory and honour as with a certain diadem, as the Apostle said: *We saw Jesus for the suffering of death crowned with glory and honour, that through the grace of God he might taste death for all [n: (Hebr. II)]*. Whence you, O *daughters of Sion,* do not consider only the outward and contemptible signs of these sufferings, that is, the scourges, the spittings, the blows, the [1146A] crown of thorns, the cross, the burial; but from this consideration *go forth,* and behold with the eyes of the mind what of glory and honour, of remedy and health, has sprung forth thence for the whole world. For this is the diadem which you ought to admire and venerate, and this is the primacy of charity which he established in the midst of his litter; and with such a diadem the excellence of his mercy towards you shines. Was he not crowned with glory and honour, rising again from this corruption of a passible body into impassibility and incorruption, and did he not set the crown of this glory and honour upon his body, which is the Church, when he confirmed to it, with himself, a participation in future incorruption for ever?

Or if we call this diadem, generally, the whole order of the human dispensation, *wherewith his [1146B] mother crowned him,* namely the blessed Virgin Mary, from whose womb he took up that same humanity, it does not seem to be repugnant to reason; for in the composition of this diadem with which Christ was crowned as man there are, as it were, certain ornaments of distinguished gems: the nativity, the circumcision, the baptism, the fast, the temptation, the passion, the resurrection, the ascension; all which are enclosed in the gold of his mercy. For gold is more precious than all metals, *and the tender mercies of God are over all his works [n: (Psal. CXLIV)]*. Out of the most shining gold, therefore, of the infinite mercy of God, and out of the works and signs of that mercy, which we have recounted above, as out of certain gems, is fashioned this diadem of king Solomon, namely of the true peaceable one, [1146C] the Lord Jesus Christ: *Who, reconciling us to God, set at peace all things that are in heaven and on earth [n: (Colos. I)]*. Whence the daughters of Sion, who have the grace of divine contemplation, are admonished that, looking into all these things with the inner sense, they should understand how great a salvation has flowed forth to them thence.

And when was he crowned with such a diadem? *In the day,* he says, *of his espousals, in the day of the joy of his heart.* What was the day of the espousals of Christ, but the time of the incarnation, when he came forth from the Virgin's womb *as a bridegroom coming out of his bride chamber [n: (Psal. XVIII)]*, to couple to himself the Church, for whose redemption he deigned to become incarnate? Or rather the day of espousals was the time of his passion, when, redeemed with the price of his blood, he presented *to himself the Church [1146D] a glorious bride, not having spot, nor wrinkle [n: (Ephes. V)]*. For with this price of inestimable weight he betrothed her; for her he caused the eternal kingdom of his Father to be written down as a dowry; from her he himself also exacts espousal gifts, *137* [d: exacts] namely the merits of faith and the virtues, without which no soul can be coupled to God; some parts of which he enumerates a little later in what follows.

What, then, was the day of the joy of his heart? The time, assuredly, of the resurrection, when, all the sadness of passion and corruption being overcome, he showed himself festive and crowned with glory and honour, with the trophy of a completed redemption. But this affection of heart did not so befall him as that beforehand, doubtful, he did not know what was to come, and then, as if [1147A] with doubt removed, conceived joy in his mind (for no time is ascribed to the Godhead); but a manner of speaking of this kind befits only his humanity, by which he is said to have had a day of joy on account of the glory of the resurrection and the fulfilled purpose and order of redemption; although this too could rather be referred to the Church, which is his body, according to that manner of speaking by which, frequently in holy Scripture, what is said of the head is referred to the body, as, conversely, from the body to the head, on account of the concorporation and unity by which Christ and the Church are made one; for the passion, resurrection and ascension of Christ is the greatest and special joy of the Church, when she remembers that by this wonderful help she has passed from captivity to liberty, from death to life, from corruption to [1147B] incorruption.
