## *138* CHAPTER IV. (cont.)

*147* But the seventh proclamation of the beauty proclaimed is this: *Thy two breasts are as two fawns of a roe, twins, which feed among the lilies till the day break and the shadows retire.* As was shown above, [1155B] by the two breasts can be understood the diligence of the masters who instruct the lesser, the twin teaching of piety and of knowledge: of piety, when they form them in the honourableness of the active and the contemplative life, or in the love of God and of neighbour; of knowledge, when they instruct them in the reading of the Old and the New Testament, or in the historical and allegorical exposition of that same Scripture. Which *two breasts are as two fawns of a roe, twins,* because these instructors of the lesser in the Church are [sic: *pavi*] little in their own eyes, acting and teaching according to the doctrine of their elders, and presuming nothing of their own knowledge, following the holy fathers in life, conduct and doctrine as sons follow mothers, and so tending to the things above, and in these things advancing daily in wit and understanding, and leading with them those whom they teach to the understanding [1155C] and desiring of higher things. Who are well compared not simply to fawns but to twins, because of those same twin teachings of piety and knowledge cleaving inseparably to one another, so that, as though begotten in one moment, neither exists prior in any part of itself. For the Church of Christ has always had nurslings of both lives, that is, of the active and the contemplative; she has always flourished in the love both of God and of neighbour; she has always been bright with the knowledge of both Testaments. And the two testaments are so inseparably joined to one another that the one is significative or expositive of the other; for the Old signifies the New, the New expounds the Old. So too the love of God without the love of neighbour, nor that of neighbour without the love of God, can in any wise exist, [1155D] because the one is probative of the other. For he who loves his neighbour is proved to love God, because he is invited by the love of God to love his neighbour. Neither can he be a lover of the contemplative life who is ignorant of the active life, nor does he hold the active who does not desire the contemplative.

The two, then, doing at one time one and the same thing, because they are convertible by the consequence of subsisting; for it follows that he who understands the Old Testament understands the New, since they are one, this in figure, that in truth; and that he who loves his neighbour loves God, and conversely, which must always be together; but he cannot contemplate God who does not know how to act well. These two, then, existing on both sides in an undivided mode, are called twins, as it were under one generation, [1156A] and are proclaimed in the two breasts of the Church in the seventh and last place of beauty.

Further, the historical and the allegorical exposition are not always so inseparably joined, because very often the pure history alone is to be held, sometimes the allegory alone, when many things can stand according to the letter *148* which can by no means stand according to the spirit, and certain things are understood according to the spirit which in no way are taken according to the letter.

But where they fittingly agree together, the aforesaid likeness of twins is not unfittingly attended to, so that in the two breasts of the Church they are proclaimed undividedly as it were two fawns of a roe, twins. But because every pursuit of piety looks to a certain end, for nothing of good, except for the attaining of eternal life, [1156B] is taught and learned, he adds:

*Which feed among the lilies till the day break and the shadows retire;* for the qualities of all the kinds set down above, which were proclaimed in the beauty of the Church's breasts, feed among the lilies, that is, are nourished in the whiteness and fragrance of the virtues, because no one makes progress in the honourableness of the active and contemplative life, or in the love of God and neighbour, without the page of each testament, except through cleanness of mind and the exercise of good action.

And how long? *till the day break and the shadows retire,* that is, until the day of eternal felicity dawn and the night of this mortality fail, and then that be seen perfectly in truth which here was seen through [1156C] a glass in a dark manner, and what here was held in hope be held there in reality. There those two breasts, which we distinguished above in fourfold wise, will be seen in that eternal day, not in labour but in reward, which the breaking of the day and the retiring of the shadow signify; for the shadow signifies labour, the day reward.

But this word *donec,* "till", is set not for a limit but for the everlasting, that we may understand that we must labour so long in this life by working well, until we lay hold of eternal life by resting happily.

VERSE 6.-- *I will go to the mountain of myrrh, and to the hill of frankincense.*

After the beloved, marked out by an excellent variety, had enumerated the kinds of beauty in the body of the beloved, that is, [1156D] of the Church, he adds the general comeliness of all the members which they ought to have who wish to belong to her body. But this comeliness in a twofold way, that is, by the mortification of the flesh and by the pleading of a devout spirit: for he who walks according to the flesh, and does not by the spirit mortify the deeds of the flesh, is not of that fair body of the Church which has not spot nor wrinkle; *for they that are Christ's have crucified their flesh, with the vices and concupiscences (Gal V)*; the pleading of a devout spirit, too, is born of the most clement breathing of the divine Spirit, because *the Spirit himself asketh for us with unspeakable groanings [n: (Rom. VIII)]*. For "asketh" is said for "maketh to ask", because whose mind [1157A] he fills, he moves *149* to the grace of compunction, so that either from the consideration of his sins, or from the delay of the kingdom that shall profit him, he may groan, groaning may entreat, entreating may at some time receive either pardon of sins or the crown of desired beatitude.

The mortification of the flesh, then, is expressed by myrrh, because of the bitterness of labour and suffering; and the pleading of a devout spirit is understood by frankincense, because of the grief of slight compunction or penance, smoking as it were, or the ardour of charity, from which the pure prayer of the supplicating mind is directed to God. But though both virtues, namely to mortify the flesh and to pray often, are of the highest honourableness and utility in the body [1157B] of holy Church, yet that one is proved the more excellent which is acquired with the greater labour of mind and body.

Wherefore, that their merit may be distinguished, the myrrh is proclaimed by the name of a mountain, the frankincense by that of a hill: he says, then, *I will go to the mountain of myrrh, and to the hill of frankincense,* as if he said more openly in other words: to the minds of those who labour with this intention, that they may mortify themselves to vices and sins, and who study to obtain this by assiduous prayers, I will go through co-operating and conferring grace, that I may prove those minds worthy of my indwelling which the beauty of these virtues delights, since I am ready both to succour those who labour and to assent to those who supplicate.

[1157C]VERSE 7.-- *Thou art all fair, love, and there is not a spot in thee.*

Since, he says, I have above extolled thee with sevenfold proclamations of beauties, and then have graciously regarded in thee the mountain of myrrh and the hill of frankincense, for the subjection of the mortified flesh is indeed a great eminence of mind, whence is born the contemplative pleading of the free spirit towards God, but by the mortification of the flesh vices and sins are so lulled to sleep that there is nothing which resists the spirit, but so great both the purity and the evenness of the mind, that thou canst admit no defilements in thy body which might avail to blot out the grace of thy comeliness.

*Thou art all fair then, my love, and there is not a spot in thee;* a spot, I say, is not in thee, that is, it has no [1157D] being, because even if some one has come upon thee for a little while and by human frailty, it is quickly repelled by compunction of heart and humble confession and daily prayer.

VERSE 8.-- *Come from Libanus, my bride, come from Libanus, come: thou shalt be crowned from the head of Amana, from the top of Sanir and Hermon, from the dens of lions, from the mountains [sic: montitibus] of leopards?*

Wherefore, *come from Libanus, my bride, come from Libanus,* that is, because, every concupiscence of the flesh being now conquered and mortified, thou art made white by the cleanness of heart and body; *come from Libanus,* that is, from this thy whitening, for Libanus is interpreted *whitening [sic: canditatio]*; and come not once, but again, because not *150* only must thou please in thought, [1158A] but in deed also; come, I say, from this thy whitening; for thou canst not come unless thou be white; and as my Bride, who art now through this whitening worthy of my embraces, pass over also into the fellowship of my kingdom, that thou mayest henceforth hold the unchangeable state and habit of a whitened soul. Come also a third time, because it profits not to please only in thought and deed, unless thou please also by perseverance in the good work itself, which is the representative certainty of future felicity.

Or else, that thou mayest proclaim the faith of the holy Trinity: come preaching the Father, come preaching the Son, come preaching the Holy Spirit, and, distinguishing the one God in these three persons, establish the holy sacraments of religion, and confirm the whole world in the [1158B] unchangeable truth of this faith.

Or else: come by faith, come by hope, come by charity, that is, all the perfection of good work, take up by faith, lay up by hope, embrace by charity. Or else, the sacrament of my body and blood and the reality of the sacrament: come by faithfully believing, come by reverently handling, come by healthfully receiving, that, incorporated into me through this my remedy of the soul, thou mayest truly be called my bride and love.

But to an incorporation of this kind thou canst not come, except from Libanus, that is, either white by baptism; or, if for fault of thine thou hast been cut off from my body, receiving back thy former whiteness by the fountain of penance.

And all these instructions of truth and faith, [1158C] scatter them through the regions of the whole world, that my praise may be announced through thee even to the ends of the earth; let those who seek, seek my sacraments, recognize them and receive them, and let their conversion become thy reward. For this is what follows, *thou shalt be crowned from the head of Amana, from the top of Sanir and Hermon, from the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards.*

By these three names of mountains are designated three kinds of worldly heights, by whose destruction holy Church has attained perfect peace, and by whose humbling she has merited a perpetual crown.

The first height was in the worshippers of idols, who, when they had known the good of the reason implanted in them and the beauty of visible things, [1158D] the Creator of them all, *glorified him not as God, or gave thanks, but became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened, because they changed the truth of God into a lie, and served the creature rather than the Creator [n: (Rom. I)]*: and thus to despise God was a certain height of pride.

The second height was the persecution of the reprobate, who, gainsaying God by perverse conduct, pursued the heralds of truth and justice with great injuries, afflicting them with many punishments and driving them even to death; and thus to resist God himself in his elect was also a certain height of pride.

*151* Further, the third height was the perverse [1159A] speech or writing of the heretics, by which they tried to destroy the true faith through human reason, and laboured to corrupt the simplicity of Holy Scripture with a certain haughtiness of dialectical discourse; who, while they sought to seem wise, were made fools, desiring through a certain pride of mind only to show off their own knowledge.
