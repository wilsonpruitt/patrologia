## *138* CHAPTER IV. (cont.)

Now Amana is interpreted *night bird,* Sanir *tooth of watches,* Hermon *anathema:* which significations of the names can be fitted first indeed to the very head of all the wicked, of whom it is written: *For he is king over all the children of pride [n: (Job XLI)]*, so that he may be called night bird, and tooth of watches, and anathema. For *a night* indeed *bird,* which by another name is called the owl or the light-shunner, the devil is rightly named, [1159B] who hates the light, that is, good works, and loves sins, which are the works of darkness; and, as it is written: *He sleepeth in the shade, in the secret of the chamber [n: (Job XL)]* [var: Vulg. Job 40:16 *in secreto calami*, "in the covert of the reed"], because the minds round which he flies in tempting, while he subjugates them through consent, he prepares for himself a resting-place as it were in the shade of sin. He also is called *tooth of watches,* because it is written of him that *as a roaring lion he goeth about seeking whom he may devour [n: (I Petr. V)]*, whose watching is never to rest from evil will. He is also not unfittingly called *Anathema,* because he is sequestered and as it were excommunicated from the fellowship of the good spirits; and he hastens to drag others too with him into destruction, that, as he himself, so they also may become anathema from God.

What befits the head, then, can not unfittingly [1159C] befit the members also: and we said a little above that by these three names *of mountains* three kinds of worldly heights can be signified, which we placed in three qualities of perverse men, namely in the worshippers of idols, and the persecutors of the saints, and the heretics.

But it must be seen how the interpretations of these names befit such men: the worshippers of idols, namely, can not absurdly be called a *night bird,* because of blindness and lightness of mind: blindness, because, as this bird flees the day and wanders rather by night, so they, unwilling to receive the light of truth and faith, embraced rather the darkness of unbelief, so long as they were not freed from this unbelief by the grace of God. Further, because of lightness they are compared to a bird, [1159D] because they wandered in such uncertainty of error that they could grasp nothing true, nothing stable, in their worship. But the persecutors of the saints can be called *tooth of watches* because of the savagery and monstrousness of the persecutions, so that the tooth *152* may be the savagery itself, and the watches the persecutions that have no rest: for in Jeremiah also it is written that, when the Lord had said: *What seest thou, Jeremiah?* he answered: *I see a watching rod; and the Lord: Well,* he says, *hast thou said, for I will watch over my word, to perform it [n: (Jer. I)]* [var: Vulg. Jr 1:12 *Bene vidisti*, "thou hast seen well"]. As, then, in this place the watching rod is taken for correction, not resting because of the sins of the people until it be fulfilled, so the savagery of the persecutions, watching in the punishment and slaughter of the saints, can be called *tooth of watches.*

[1160A]But the heretics are called *anathema,* that is, separated from God, because, while they are separated from the communion of the Church for the perversity of their faith by the steel of cutting off, that is, of excommunication, assuredly they are separated from the head, that is, from Christ. And the Apostle says: *If any man preach to you other than what you have received from us, let him be anathema [n: (Gal. I)]*; but the heretics preached other than what had been preached by the apostles: therefore they were anathema, and so, anathematized and excommunicated, they went out from the Church.

Therefore the bridegroom cries *Come* to the bride three times, to show that she is to be crowned three times from the head or top of these; *thou shalt be crowned,* he says, *from the head of Amana, from the top of Sanir and Hermon.* And how shalt thou be crowned? [1160B] First *from the head of Amana,* that is, from the worshippers of idols, who, converted at thy preaching, have grown up in faith and action like good trees, whose conversion and progress was assuredly the adornment of thy head. Then also *thou shalt be crowned from the top of Sanir,* that is, from the persecutors of the saints, who, at thy exhortations and at the terrors of miracles, laying aside their savagery, submitted themselves to the yoke of Christ, whose conversion and humbling also is the amplification of thy crown in return for thy labour.

Thirdly *thou shalt be crowned from the top of Hermon,* that is, the proud eloquence of the heretics, who, after they had been changed from the depravity of their false knowledge by thy eloquence of sound doctrine, reasonable and joined with wisdom, [1160C] defended with the strongest assertion of truth the faith which they formerly attacked, whose reformation to the right faith is thy crown and the reward of thy labour.

And since, on the contrary, as *the soul of the just is the seat of God [n: (Sap. VII)]*, so the soul of the perverse is the dwelling of the unclean Spirit, there follows: *From the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards:* those whom we distinguished under the names of the three mountains could rightly be called dens of lions, because, while the malignant spirits subjected them to themselves through malice, nourishing that malice in them by their own suggestion and the consent of those men, they rested in them as it were in certain dens. For the lions are the malignant spirits, which roar round about to deceive *153* the human race, thirsting for the blood of the soul, that is, desiring to take away [1160D] from the soul the nourishments of the virtues. For as in the blood of a man is his life, so in the nourishments of the virtues the life of the soul is [sic: *es*]

But the mountains of leopards are, over pride, the same hearts of the reprobate, and the leopards are the same malignant spirits, because of their fierceness and the manifold kinds, as it were the varied colours, of their thousandfold frauds. *From the dens,* then, *of lions and the mountains of leopards* the Church is crowned, because she by her preaching makes the minds of those who, brooding over earthly and perverse doings, had been dens of demons, seats of divine grace; and the hearts of those also who, holding forth a proud and as it were singular life or knowledge, brought forth the varied colours of sects and heresies, she makes worthy of the divine indwelling in the spirit of humility; out of [1161A] their conversion and progress, surely, she is heaped with the greater fruit of her holy labour.

VERSE 9.-- *Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, bride, thou hast wounded my heart with one of thy eyes, and with one hair of thy neck.*

Still further the bridegroom, that is, Christ, approves and admires the zeal and progress of the bride, that is, of the Church, or of each faithful soul, and the comeliness and merit of that progress; so that, whereas above he first enumerated seven parts of her beauty, as it were in her outward habit, then set forth the twofold quality of the inward habit, and thirdly offered a threefold crown also for the progress of others, in all which he showed the great good pleasure of his condescension, now he brings in a more eminent, and as it were [1161B] above the rest the greatest, part of beauty, a twofold one, in which the sister and bride, by the greatness of her very dignity, has not only moved the heart of the bridegroom in that same good pleasure of his, but has even wounded it: sister through faith, bride through holy and true love. But what is this twofold part: *One of the eyes and one hair of the neck?* If in each one's sense we consider twin eyes as in one body, we shall place one on the right side, the other on the left. Now the eye of the left side is the wondering at all visible things, wonderfully and orderly disposed; from the consideration of which, though through a glass and in a dark manner, it gathers some magnitude, beauty and power of the Creator, and in [1161C] their administration and government his wisdom and goodness. Further, the eye of the right side is led away above, outside these visible things, when it is loosed from every contagion of this worldly desire, and is fixed upon the very face of the Creator by the gaze of contemplative charity. By this one of the bride's eyes, then, *154* the heart of the bridegroom is wounded, because, while the holy mind so couples to itself the supernal grace through the force of divine charity that it receives the name of Seraphim, that is, of burning, out of its very ardour it brings as it were a certain wound of love to the divinity united to it.

*In one,* however, *hair of the neck* we understand the subtlety of preaching: but since there are many subtleties of preaching, which yet is its one and singular subtlety, as it were one hair of the neck? According to the parts [1161D] of the faith, both in the deity of the Trinity and in the incarnation of Christ. And what is the one hair in the deity of the Trinity? One God in three persons, whose incomprehensible reason of Divinity we are bidden to believe, are not permitted to discuss; and what is the one hair of the neck in the incarnation of Christ? *God made man.* For who can in any wise investigate how the Word is embodied, how the supreme and life-giving Spirit is animated within the womb of a mother, how he who has no beginning comes to be and is conceived?

But in this one hair of the neck, on both sides, the heart of the beloved is wounded, because, while each one believes this, which is not gathered by human reason, he is strongly bound together in the love of almighty God through the merit of faith; *but faith has no merit, to which human reason affords proof.* [1162A] For faith is not against reason, but above reason; and therefore he who will believe nothing except what he comprehends by reason, as the philosophers, has no merit by his faith; but he who believes that which is not contrary to reason, and yet is above reason: whence the Apostle says: *The word is nigh in thy mouth, and in thy heart, this is the word of faith. [n: (Rom. X.)]* Christ too is not against reason, but above reason; *in one,* then, *of the eyes* love, *in one hair of the neck* faith is expressed. But because faith working through love is the foundation of all good things, while without love there is no virtue in faith itself, *for the devils believe and tremble [n: (Jac. II)]*, and for this reason these two cannot be distinguished where they join a mind of perfect virtue to God: by one [1162B] eye, then, of speculative love, and by one hair, that is, by the subtlety of faith, which consists chiefly in two things, namely the mystery of the divinity and the sacrament of the incarnation, the heart of the beloved is wounded, because by the force of a mind abounding in faith and love it is in some manner pierced toward knowing the more; whence not once but again, repeating, he says: *Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, bride, thou hast wounded my heart.* For very commendable before the eyes of almighty God is both faith working through love and love advancing through faith: faith, namely, the foundation of all good. love, building upon it, the grace and adornment of that foundation.
