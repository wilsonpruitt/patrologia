## *172* CHAPTER V. (cont.)

For he who through the integrity of faith hastens to the inheritance of the supreme Father surely understands prudently what, or of what kind, is that which he believes: but he who, God revealing, knows the mysteries of the divine precepts and sacraments, [1184D] while he bravely endures adversities for God, while he also bravely despises the flattering joys of the world, surely shows what he hopes; but he who, full of the grace of the Holy Spirit, embraces the good of simplicity and innocence, because he is zealous to be just, surely his very justice speaks that he loves God. Further, he who has so ordered for himself his manner of living, that in all his acts he knows how to hold the governance of holy discretion, while he both tempers himself from the pleasures of the world through the pursuits of the virtues, and does not handle the good of justice indiscreetly, shows how clean and right he is in all his working.

*181* Well, therefore, does the bridegroom call the bride, whom he rouses to the office of preaching, *sister, love, [1185A] dove and undefiled,* that he may intimate to her with how squared a mind she ought to stand in all her offices and acts. But she answers him knocking, and refuses to rise from that deliberate leisure of inner quiet, saying: *To be stripped [sic: Exspoliari] of my tunic,* etc.

VERSE 3.-- *I have stripped myself of my tunic, how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them?*

The hindrances, she says, of the world, and the cares of the active life, in which I was till now entangled, I have cast from my mind, that I may be able more freely to be at leisure in divine contemplation; how now dost thou exhort me to rise again from this quiet through the office of preaching, and to serve the pursuits of the active life? *I have washed my feet,* that is, the negligences of my works, which I contracted from the dust [1185B] of thoughts and of worldly things, I have washed thoroughly with the water of compunction rising from divine charity; how shall I contract them again from the filth of earthly din? For it is very difficult to converse among the men of this world and to be stained by no contagion of human frailty. For matter applied outwardly to the bodily senses easily infects the beauty of the soul within.

But while I was turning these things over with myself, and did not wholly purpose to depart from my resolve, I will tell, O daughters of Jerusalem, what my beloved did.

VERSE 4.-- *My beloved put his hand through the opening, and my belly trembled at his touch.*

For what is his *hand,* what the *opening,* what his *belly*? Surely you ought to understand the hand of the beloved [1185C] as the power of his omnipotence and of his hidden inspiration. When, however, the mind of man, given to the pleasures of the world, thinks nothing of God, nothing of the salvation of the soul, being as it were shut round and beset by a certain mute, and a wall of perversity, it has now no access or opening through which the light of divine grace may deign to enter.

But because divine power sometimes prevents such men by inspiring, so that they are pricked with compunction for their sins, the wall of the pre-damned mind being as it were broken, an opening and a certain rent is opened, whence may flow the tears of compunction, stirred up by the grief of a penitent and as it were rent heart. Of such a rending the prophet warns: *Rend,* he says, *your hearts and not your [1185D] garments [n: (Joel. II)]*.

And since there are two kinds of compunction, *182* because the mind turned to God is pricked first by fear, afterwards by love, when anyone, dreading the torments of hell, having renounced the desires of the world, bewails the sins which he had committed, it is as if divine grace made an opening and a rent in his heart. But in the progress of good conversation, after he has conceived some security from the divine mercy, he burns with so great love toward God, that he who before wept lest he should be led to punishment, afterwards begins to weep most bitterly because he is delayed from the kingdom.

Then, therefore, through the opening which grief has made in the penitent's heart, the most loving Lord puts in the hand of soothing grace and mercy, and with so very sweet a [1186A] touch refreshes the mind, that, wholly kindled in the love of God, it seeks and desires nothing earthly, sets even all visible things at little, and strives with all its mind to attain only heavenly and eternal things.

At the sweetness of whose touch the belly trembles, because the mind, drinking the sweetness of the contemplative life, is moved by the power of inspiring grace, so that it understands that the love of God cannot be without the love of neighbour, but that this is the fullness of perfect love, that he who loves God for his own sake should also love his neighbour for God's sake. In this manner, therefore, O daughters of Jerusalem, *my beloved put his hand,* that is, the help of his power and grace, *through the opening,* of my pricked heart, intimating and admonishing that, [1186B] because I have made entry for him in my heart by cleaving to his love, I should not hesitate also to prepare an entrance for him into the hearts of my neighbours through the office of preaching, since he himself is always at hand through helping grace, and is wont both to bestow on me what, or how, I should teach, and to open the ears of the minds of the hearers.

Whence also this same sentence which I have referred to myself I can, not absurdly, refer also to my hearers, so that the beloved may be said to put his hand through the opening as often as, by sounding through the voice of preaching at the outer ears of the hearers, by putting the hand of helping grace into the inner ears, he enters. At whose touch my belly trembles, because all the weaker ones of mine, what [1186C] they before did not understand for weakness of wit, understand, and when, hearing the terror of divine judgment, they tremble, they learn to be moved also to the fruit of good work.

VERSE 5.-- *I arose therefore to open to my beloved: my hands dropped myrrh, and my fingers full of the most approved myrrh.*

For since he promised me his help, by which I might obtain the *183* fruit of my labour, it was fitting that I should not flee the office of preaching, but that, as I had opened my heart to him by his grace, so also I should open the hearts of my neighbours to him by the same grace through preaching

*My hands,* however, *distilled myrrh,* because, [1186D] according to the voice of the Doctor of the Gentiles, I chastised my body and subjected it to servitude, lest perhaps while I preached to others I myself should become reprobate [n: (I Cor. IX)]. For my hands are my works, which ought always to distil myrrh, because they ought always to be fragrant of the mortification of the flesh, of abstinence from the vices and of the affluence of the virtues.

*My fingers,* moreover, *full of the most approved myrrh,* because those very works of mine, of the virtues, if there are any, ought indeed to have great governance of discretion, but that discretion itself ought to overflow with charity divine and fraternal. For what is the most approved myrrh, but charity, the most choice of all the virtues? But why myrrh, unless because *love is strong as death, and jealousy hard as hell [sic: inferrus]? [n: (Cant. VIII.)] For no one [1187A] hath greater charity than that a man lay down his life for his friends [n: (Joan. XV)]*.

*Fingers,* then, *mine,* that is, the acts distinguished in one mind, as in one body, by the ten precepts, *are full of myrrh* of charity, because when I love my beloved strongly, I do not refuse to labour even unto death for keeping and preaching his laws.

For the salvation also, or the need, of the brethren I am zealous to have this discretion, that while I desire rather to be suspended in divine contemplation, fraternal charity nevertheless at times compels me to descend, and to gather the myrrh of labour for their utility.

When therefore at the exhortation of my beloved I had now risen, that by preaching the word of life to my neighbours I might open to him their shut hearts also, with my hands [1187B] distilling myrrh, my fingers also full of the most approved myrrh, I girded myself for the office of preaching.

VERSE 6.-- *I opened the bolt of my door to my beloved: but he had turned aside and had gone by. My soul melted when my beloved spoke: I sought and found him not; I called and he answered me not.*

I loosened, I say, for a time the rigour of my purpose, or the enclosure of my silence, and going forth into public, *I opened my mouth and drew in the spirit [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*. Having cast from myself also every obstacle of the mind, I began more diligently to search out the wonderful testimonies of the beloved, which I might both show in work and declare in speech. But when I wished that he would enter wholly to me, and said to him with Moses: *Lord, show me [1187C] thy face [n: (Exod. XXXIII)]*, *he had now turned aside and had gone by* and had left only his back parts to be seen in some measure.

*184* For what was his turning aside and going by? Surely his turning aside was to lower himself from that sublimity of the heavens to our infirm things by being born and by dying, and because he did it with time, this was his going by: for all those things were in passing, *for Christ rising again now dieth no more, death shall no more have dominion over him [n: (Rom. VI)]*. When therefore I strove to comprehend him as he is, in some measure, and to show him in whatever way to others desiring to hear, he withdrew, turned aside, and went by, because he is incomprehensible, and in this life no one can perfectly grasp that divine [1187D] substance by sense. And when he had gone by, I saw his back parts only, because I could understand something of his humanity, and preach it.

And when I thus preached the sayings and deeds of my beloved, and provoked the minds of the hearers to keep his precepts and to follow his footsteps, a greater force of compunction dissolved me, so that I wholly flowed out in his love, because he who spoke from me outwardly to others through the voice of preaching was speaking also to me inwardly through the grace of inspiration. For as often as we preach him, he himself speaks in us and through us, as he also says in the Gospel: *For it is not you that speak, but the Spirit of your Father that speaketh in you [n: (Matth. X)]*. But where the Spirit speaks to the Father, [1188A] the Son also speaks, because those whose substance is one, one also is their operation. And I was taken with such sweetness of his speech, that my soul, hearing him within, not only softened from all hardness, but even melted, and, growing strong by deliberate counsel for the daily exercises of good works, said with the Psalmist: *I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me [n: (Psal. LXXXIV)]*. For he well attends to God speaking in himself, who obeys with ready will the grace that inspires and goes before free will: he also hears God speaking in himself, whoever understands, by its own revealing, what he reads in Holy Scripture. And when by meditating more frequently in it he seeks God, the more subtly he searches him out, the higher is God exalted in his heart; whence the Psalmist: *Man shall come [1188B] to a high heart, and God shall be exalted [n: (Psal. LXIII)]*; and in Ezekiel it is written that when the living creatures were lifted up from the earth, the wheels also were lifted up together beside them [n: (Ezech. I)]: for the more attentively Holy Scripture is attended to, the more sublimely is it raised up into the spiritual sense for him who subtly considers it.
