## *172* CHAPTER V. (cont.)

When, therefore, at such speech of the beloved my soul was melting through the fire of charity, I sought him the more vehemently by reading, by meditating, by preaching, and I desired to know what manner of one he was; I sought him by praying, by fasting, by keeping vigil, by macerating the flesh, but in this life I could not find *185* him as he is. *I sought* therefore *him, and found him not,* because I myself could neither know him perfectly by reading. nor show him to others by preaching; *for we see through a [1188C] glass in a dark manner [n: (II Cor. XIII)]*. *I called, and he answered me not:* when I thought him now as it were present to me, although placed in some manner a little far off, and as though I were soon to lay hold of him, and called with some voice of subtle understanding, he answered me not, because, as I went back over the things which I thought I had understood perfectly, he did not open them to the full through the answering voice of his grace. For it is written in the vision of Ezekiel the prophet that *when a voice came above the firmament, the living creatures stood, and let down their wings [n: (Ezech. I.)]* For they had already spread the wings of inmost contemplation, already they had conceived, by flying with the mind, some knowledge, however great, of the Godhead, though yet in a dark manner; but when the voice came not beneath the firmament [1188D] nor from the firmament, but above the firmament, they stood still, not able to go further, and, the wings of the contemplation they had begun being let down, they perceived that to comprehend the incomprehensible was above them. For, deeming him so present that they could comprehend him, they called, but he did not answer them, because what they thought they had understood of God, he did not show by the voice of inner address to be so. *I called* therefore *and he answered me not,* because, while I wished to attempt something above me, I recognized how far beneath it I was, since I could attain no perfect knowledge of the incomprehensible substance.

VERSE 7.-- *The watchmen that go about the city found me: they struck me and wounded me; the watchmen of the walls took away my cloak from me.*

[1189A]What then? I laid down the wings which I had spread in vain beyond my power, and, turned to the writings or sayings of the holy Fathers, I heard: *Advance not the bounds which thy fathers have set [n: (Prov. XXII)]* [var: Vulg. *Ne transgrediaris terminos antiquos*, "pass not beyond the ancient bounds"], who indeed *spoke inspired by the Holy Spirit [n: (II Petr. I)]* and showed the way and manner of believing, or of disputing about God. They, assuredly, are the watchmen of the city, that is, of holy Church, of which it is written: *Glorious things are said of thee, city of God [n: (Psal. LXXXVI)]*, because they guard her from the onset of evil spirits and men by words and examples and by the defences of their prayers.

These, then, *found me,* because they proved me drawing back from my former intention and willing to be [1189B] *186* content with their sayings and writings: *they found,* I say, because they so drew me by the sweetness of their discourses that, every other intention set aside, I would attend to the sole final and supernal cause, namely life eternal, which is God himself; the vision of him is the full and perfect rest and consummation of all our labour. They, then, while I read or heard their writings, *struck me* with the dart of divine love, and not only struck, but also *wounded,* because they so pierced my mind with the compunction of heavenly desire that a fountain of tears flowed from it, as it were a kind of blood of the soul.

*The watchmen of the walls* also *took away my cloak.* Now by the walls in this place the Holy Scriptures can not unfittingly be [1189C] understood, because, just as a wall is built of many and diverse stones, so Holy Scripture is made up of many and diverse sentences and teachings of the holy Fathers. And as walls fortify a city, so the Holy Scriptures guard holy Church, because all her fortification or adornment is the knowledge and observance of the Holy Scriptures. Further, the *watchmen* of these *walls* can be understood as all the simple in the Church, of whom it is written: *The simplicity of the just shall direct them [n: (Prov. XI)]*, who, although they have either little or no knowledge of the Scriptures, yet, while they preach by their works the precepts of those same Scriptures that are to be observed, edify their neighbours [1189D] too in no mean degree. These *watchmen,* then, of the spiritual *walls took away my cloak,* because by the example of good works they took from me the hindrances of superfluous thought, which veiled my mind with the enfolding of undiscerning inquiry, and they persuaded me not to be wise in high things, but to consent to the humble [n: (Rom. XII)], and to serve God in simplicity.

VERSE 8.-- *I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, if you find my beloved, that you tell him that I languish with love*

Wherefore *I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, if you find my beloved, that you announce to him that I languish with love.* But what is my adjuring? Surely to bind by the sacrament of faith and of working; otherwise [1190A] you are not *daughters of Jerusalem,* because you do not belong to the vision of supernal peace, unless you please God by faith and work. *If,* therefore, *you find the beloved,* that is, if you have so far profited by my preaching that by believing rightly and working well you have him, then *I adjure you,* that is, as I have said, I bind you by the sacrament of faith and of working, which to violate is to lose salvation; *that you announce to him,* namely, by showing forth the signs and as it were a certain stamp of my zeal and labour, with which in preaching *187* I have toiled for the sake of your salvation, as the Apostle says to his disciples: *But the signs of my apostleship are you among the gentiles [n: (II Cor. XII)]*.

And what ought you to announce to him? Namely, *that I languish with love,* that is, I am so held by his charity and love that toward every pleasure [1190B] and beauty of the world I remain weak and languid and as it were already without feeling. Do you, therefore, announce by your life and manners how true, right and profitable my preaching has been, so that your progress may become the fruit of my labour also.

At this voice of the beloved obeying the beloved, and rising up to preach, and setting forth the zeal and profit of that same preaching, the *daughters of Jerusalem,* that is, holy souls belonging to the vision of supernal peace, inquiring the causes of so great an adjuration, address the beloved also in this manner: *What manner of one is thy beloved of the beloved,* etc.

VERSE 9.-- *What manner of one is thy beloved of the beloved, O most beautiful among women! what manner of one is thy beloved of the beloved, that thou hast so adjured us?*

[1190C]We attend, they say, to no small force in this adjuration, in which we have learned that the whole sum of our salvation hangs, and therefore we desire to know the power and dignity of this beloved, for whose sake thou hast so adjured us.

*What manner of one is,* then, *the beloved of the beloved,* that is, the Son of the Father, God of God, light of light? For it is a great thing to know him whom thou art at such pains to commend, and of the highest prudence to understand him for love of whom thou languishest so greatly. But since *the beloved of the beloved,* that is, the Son of the substance of the Father, of one essence, of one power, of one will, and of one operation, cannot be perfectly comprehended, *for we know in part and we prophesy in part* [1190D] [n: (I Cor. XIII)], yet whatever that is which can be thought, understood, or said of God, thou, *O most beautiful among women,* that is, among souls that bring forth spiritual sons to God, canst teach, because this thy special prerogative of beauty has merited, that since thou art called the beloved of this beloved, thou canst draw nearer to him with the eye of the mind. For all thy glory and adornment is from within [n: (Psal. XLIV)], and the pure God, to whose purity nothing can be compared, is not discerned except by pure hearts, because *blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God [n: (Matth. V)]*.

Beautiful indeed are the other women, that is, the faithful souls which in their own manner and place and time beget spiritual sons to God, but thou art the most beautiful of all, the Church universal, who daily [1191A] beget sons in the spirit through the sacrament of baptism and the teaching of holy preaching. Do thou, therefore, make known to us *what manner of one is the beloved of the beloved,* that *188* is, what is to be understood and believed concerning his divinity, according to human possibility. Or again, *what manner of one is the beloved of the beloved,* that is, how he who was once made beloved to thee by being born and dying for thee is now beloved, by pouring into thee the abundance of his grace and drawing thee into the divine sweetness of his love. Or, how he who formerly, in an earlier time, was beloved in the ancient fathers by showing the majesty of his divinity through figures and dark sayings with very many signs of miracles, is now beloved in the last time by assuming flesh and working the salvation of the whole world. And that [1191B] the same words are repeated is the eager inquiry of a mind striving to know.

Further, the beloved, that she may kindle the souls of those inquiring [sic: *pereunctantium*] to greater love and reverence for her beloved, sets forth his quality tropologically, while she sings the singable beauty of his divinity and humanity with ten proclamations, as it were on a psaltery of ten strings [n: (Psal. XCI)]. For the number ten is perfect, and is contained in the sacrament either of the Decalogue or of the four Gospels; which, beginning from one, so arrives at four as to complete the number ten; for if upon unity you add the three following numbers, that is, two, three and four, you will have ten, and so through four [1191C] you will complete the number ten.

Now the psaltery is a musical instrument formed after the fashion of the letter Delta, which in Greek expresses four. Beautifully, then, does the beloved sing the dignity and beauty of the beloved as it were on a psaltery of ten strings, by the testimony of ten proclamations, to show that every sacrament flowing to her from the grace of the beloved sounds forth the Old and the New Testament, the one singing beforehand in figure that it was to be shown, the other now singing that it has been shown in truth. She says, therefore: *My beloved is white and ruddy.*

VERSE 10.-- *My beloved is white and ruddy, chosen out of thousands.*

If we draw near diligently with the ear of the heart, we shall be able to perceive with the inner hearing that the chief matter of such a harmony [1191D] as the beloved intends to measure out on this ten-stringed instrument is the grace, worthy to be preached, of a twin beauty, namely of chastity and of martyrdom, which, first considered in the head itself, is afterwards diffused through his members, now alternately, now with a sweetness intermingled.

*My beloved,* she says, *is white and ruddy:* this twin colour, *189* namely whiteness and ruddiness, appears chiefly indeed and singularly and fully in the head of the Church, that is, Christ, because to the whiteness of his most chaste and most holy flesh, which he took from the most chaste Virgin by the ineffable operation of the Holy Spirit, no whiteness can be compared. And of the wisdom of God the Father, which he himself is, it is written: *Above all the beauty of the stars, [1192A] being compared with the light, she is found before it; for she is the brightness of eternal light, and the unspotted mirror of the majesty of God [n: (Sap. VII)]* [var: Vulg. Sap 7:29 *super omnem dispositionem stellarum*, "above all the order of the stars"].

He is *white,* then, in divinity, because of the unspeakable splendour of the divine nature, by which he *enlighteneth every man that cometh into this world [n: (Joan. I)]*: and *white* also from humanity, because of the assumption of flesh, immaculate, as we have said, and free of all sin. Further, he was *ruddy* specially from the passion, because no charity can be equalled to his charity, since he so loved his own handiwork that he chose to die for it, not disdaining to be made the sin of the flesh and the curse of the cross [n: (Rom. VIII; Gal. III)] Of which singular passion of his he himself speaks in the prophet: *I have trodden the winepress alone, and of the gentiles there is not a man with me [n: (Isa. LXIII)]*.

[1192B]For no passion is compared with this passion, which alone has wrought the salvation of the whole world, alone has led the captive servant out of the servitude of sin into the true *liberty of the glory of the sons of God [n: (Rom. VIII)]*.
