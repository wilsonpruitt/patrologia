## *172* CHAPTER V. (cont.)

Whence, when she had first said, *my beloved is white and ruddy,* she added, *chosen out of thousands:* for the number a thousand, because it is set for perfection, signifies universality. *Chosen,* therefore, *out of thousands* he is said to be, because he alone is set before the whole company of the faithful by the singularity of his virtues and graces: for of him it is written that God *anointed* him *with the oil of exultation above his* partakers [n: (Psal. XLIV)]. And whereas all the elect receive a divided grace, *one indeed after this manner, and another after that [n: (I Cor. VII)]*, to the Only-begotten alone *God giveth not the Holy Spirit by measure (Joan. [1192C] III)*, *and of his fulness all* receive [n: (Joan. I)], *in whom dwelleth all the fulness of the Godhead bodily (Coloss II)*, because *he is the head above all principality, and power, and virtue, and dominion [n: (Ephes. I)]*.

This *beloved,* then, the Lord Jesus Christ, is singularly *white and ruddy* in the personal matter of his own divinity and humanity; *chosen* he is *out of thousands:* but in those thousands themselves, that is, in the whole company of the elect, the whiteness of holiness and the ruddiness of the passion do not shine in one manner nor in equal measure; because in some both, in some not both, but now to this one the whiteness of purity and the ruddiness of martyrdom together, now to that one either whiteness alone or ruddiness alone is allotted, so that in the *190* [1192D] sight of God this one is white and ruddy, that one either wholly white or wholly ruddy; the one goes before with the greater, the other with the lesser, sometimes with an equal share of those virtues. In his body, therefore, that is, the Church, Christ also is white and ruddy, according to the measure which it pleases his kindness and grace to allot to each of the elect.

VERSE 11.-- *His head is as the finest gold. His locks are as the high branches of palm trees, black as a raven.* [var: Vulg. *Caput ejus aurum optimum*, without *sicut*: "his head is the finest gold"]

Whence presently, in what follows, the gifts of his twin beauty are enumerated part by part, now together, now alone, shining forth in the same body of Christ, so that after the singular dignity above all the saints first set forth in the head, there may be added the colours of the diverse [1193A] dignities in his body, advancing in their own manner and place through the grace that unites them and incorporates them to himself. For she says: *His head is as the finest gold.* What is understood by the gold but his divinity? For as gold is more precious than all metals, so the divinity of Christ surpasses all creatures; and whatever glory of its dignity there can be in those creatures is nothing in comparison with the divinity. The *head,* then, *of the beloved,* that is, his divinity, is *as gold,* and not gold absolutely, but the *finest* gold, because the substance of the divinity is good, and not only good, but also the supreme and most general good, and, so to speak, becomes the good of goods, because he himself is the fountain and origin of all good, from whom all good things proceed. Or [1193B] because in Holy Scripture the principal part of the mind is called the head, and Christ is the power and wisdom of God the Father [n: (I Cor. I)], and the Word begotten from the heart of the Father, by which Word and by which wisdom all things were made, and without which nothing is made. The *gold,* then, that is *finest* is that power and wisdom itself, which is *the brightness of the glory of God and the figure of his substance [n: (Hebr. I)]*.

And whatever of beauty and comeliness is diffused into the body of this head flows forth from that divinity and wisdom itself, so that the grace of the locks, of the eyes, of the cheeks, and of the other members, which will presently be numbered in what follows, may worthily be proclaimed of this body.

The first of which is, *his locks are as the high branches of palm trees, [1193C] black as a raven:* the locks of this head can be understood in two ways, namely as the holy prophets and apostles, or as the Holy Scriptures which flowed forth through them by the inspiration of God. For they clung to God as to their head by the sweetness of contemplative charity, and therefore they received from that very power and wisdom of God great subtlety of mind in beholding that divine Word.

*191* Whence they are called *As the high branches of palm trees,* because, since their conversation was in heaven [n: (Philip. III)], they were also lifted up in mind by contemplation into heaven, every trouble of earthly delight being overcome and trodden down; the glory of which spiritual triumph is signified by the palm, which is the sign [1193D] of victory. But because for the gaining of this victory they were afflicted with no small evils, whether by bearing adversities from wicked men, or by crucifying in themselves their flesh with its vices and concupiscences [n: (Gal. V)], they are rightly called *black as a raven.* For the despisers of worldly glory were reputed by the lovers of the world as base and obscure, but they were fitted as fine locks to the glory and comeliness of the divine head all the more, the more they were despised by the worldly for the love of the Godhead.

Further, the Holy Scriptures which were brought forth through them can for this reason be called the locks of the divine head, because, as locks spring from the head, so the Holy Scriptures are produced from the divine wisdom; and as those adorn the head, so the Holy Scriptures adorn the [1194A] eminence and principality of the Church of God. And they are said to be *as palms lifted high,* because [sic: *qui a*] the minds of the faithful are lifted up by their admonition or consolation from earthly things to the loving and seeking of heavenly things, and [sic: *ub*] they proclaim the spiritual armour against vices and sins, and promise also the palm of eternal blessedness to those who strive well unto the end. But these *locks* are said to be *black as a raven,* because the Holy Scriptures are wrapped in many obscurities, *for dark is the water in the clouds of the air [n: (Psal. XVII)]*, because the knowledge in the Holy Scriptures is deep. But because this blackness signifies affliction, whether voluntary or compelled, therefore, while some suffering of mind or body is attended to in it, the beloved in this part is proclaimed wine-coloured, that is, ruddy [1194B] only, in his body.

VERSE 12.-- *His eyes are as doves upon the brooks of waters, which are washed with milk, and sit beside the plentiful streams.*

What is understood by the eyes of the beloved but the prelates or angels of the Churches, who are set as it were as watchmen over the house of God, that is, holy Church: whence God says to one of them: *I have made thee a watchman to the house of Israel, and thou shalt hear the word out of my mouth, and shalt tell it them from me [n: (Ezech. III)]*. For the watchman looks out from on high, so that he may announce the coming of enemies the better to be guarded against, the further off it is; whence the Lord says through the prophet to a watchman of this kind: *Let there be a trumpet in thy throat, as an [1194C] eagle upon the house of God [n: (Ose. VIII)]*. For the watchman has a trumpet in his throat when he preaches the word of God openly and publicly, far and wide, and announces with lofty voice and mind what end *192* there is for virtues and vices, who is as an eagle upon the house of God, because, attending to the Godhead with the keenness of subtle understanding, he calls forth those committed to him to the subtlety of the contemplative life, spreading over them the wings of his protection, with which, by zeal for doctrine and by the support of prayers, he may keep them from the snares of the malignant spirits.

But these *eyes* of the beloved are *as doves, which are washed with milk,* because the holy watchmen, attending over the house of God, if they are truly the eyes of God, have works of simplicity and innocence, with chastity of heart and [1194D] of body, by which they nourish both themselves and those subject to them unto life eternal. And whence this? Because they dwell *upon the brooks of waters, and sit beside the plentiful streams.* For this is the natural custom of doves, that, sitting upon the streams of waters, they guard against the coming of hawks: because, then, these watchmen abide *upon the brooks of waters,* that is, the sentences of the Scriptures, therefore they know how to provide both for themselves and for their subjects, by composing or correcting manners, and by guarding against the snares of the ancient enemy. They *sit* also *beside the plentiful streams,* because daily they have before their eyes the teachings of both Testaments, which are in truth not only full but most full of the knowledge of all sacraments, which in the one are set forth through figures and dark sayings, [1195A] and in the other are preached as shown in truth. And in this third member one of the twin colour in the body of the beloved proclaimed above is to be considered, namely whiteness, so that souls washed with the milky colour may bear the ensign of chastity and purity.

VERSE 13.-- *His cheeks are as beds of aromatical spices set by the perfumers. His lips are as lilies dropping choice myrrh.*

By the cheeks of the beloved the continent can not unfittingly be understood, adorning their purpose by a plurality of diverse virtues, as by a multitude of hairs showing forth manliness of soul, who, while for God they abstain from the pleasure of the eyes or of the ears and of the other senses, of foods also and garments, and of the other delightful things of this world, [1195B] build as many beds of spices as the scents of continence they spread from themselves.

For, for example, the reverent restraint of the eyes, or their turning away, is a certain beautiful bed, in which as many merits of continence shine forth, so many fragrances do the spices breathe. The ear *193* also, and the mouth and the nostrils and the hands can be called certain beds, which because they are perceptive of good or evil things, while certain things, though good in their own nature, very often become evil by the abuse of those who partake of them. As often as the irrational appetites for those things are broken by the love of virtue, those beds breathe with the kinds of spiritual spices with which they are sown, so that each spice breathes according to that which the quality of continence begets, because [1195C] the more it delights by it, the more does the continence of that thing commend it to God.

But because these continences ought to have a fitting state by the virtue of discretion, so that neither natural necessity be turned into vice, nor the rigour of virtue exceed the honest measure, rightly are these *beds* said, *by the perfumers,* to be *set,* because not at random, not without discretion and as it were confusedly, but in order and composedly and with reason are continences of this kind set in their beds, by those, namely, who are proved to have had the knowledge of this setting. For these perfumers are the holy apostles and holy doctors, who in their sayings and writings show forth the kinds of virtues as certain perfumes, out of which [1195D] they compound against vices and sins, as against diseases of the soul, a certain most wholesome antidote. The *cheeks,* then, *of the beloved are as beds of spices,* which are *set* *by the perfumers,* because assuredly those who give out from themselves the diverse odours of continence gather from the sayings and deeds of the holy Fathers whatever of diligence and comeliness they fashion in their manners.

And it is to be considered that in this member too the twin colour of the beauty foretasted above, namely ruddiness and whiteness, shines forth, so that by the outward bearing the inward is denoted, because, while these cheeks are ruddy and white, they give testimony both to the modesty of chastity and to the labour of continence, where it cannot easily be judged which colour is the more eminent, whether ruddiness in martyrdom or whiteness in virginity, because both virginity [1196A] is found in martyrs and itself makes martyrs. For in these cheeks two things are regarded in one, because in the virtue of each one both the merit of continence and chastity shines forth, and for the labour the reward of martyrdom is hoped for.
