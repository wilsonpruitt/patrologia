## *172* CHAPTER V. (cont.)

But in this place the beloved who above, as we have often said, is brought in coloured in two ways, namely white and ruddy, by the beauty of the body alone [1203D] shines in the face of his body, that is, of the Church, which he chose for himself *not having spot nor wrinkle [n: (Ephes. V)]*, which the singing of that part concerning the form of Libanus proves, which is interpreted *whitening.*

VERSE 16.-- *His throat is most sweet, and he is all lovely: such is my beloved, and he is my friend, daughters of Jerusalem.*

In the throat is the voice, in the voice is sound, in sound, if the voice is articulate, the sure expression of some thing. What is understood by the *throat* but the sound of Holy Scripture? And we know that the whole of Holy Scripture was established either by the voice of the Lord himself, or by that of the saints, whom God himself used as certain instruments. For what do the Holy Scriptures sound but God speaking either through himself or through his own? And speaking what? [1204A] Assuredly the quality of the promise to come, according to the purpose of him who predestines, calls, justifies and glorifies his elect, the sweetness and suavity of which glorification he grants them to foretaste now through hope that lifts up, and pants after it with all desire, and as it were already in some manner takes a certain trial of that same suavity.

That very trial, namely, can also be called the *throat* of this beloved, because in the mind of every holy soul that foretastes the sweetness of the divine goodness there savours delightfully that suavity which it understands from the preaching of Holy Scripture, of which it is said that *neither hath eye seen, nor ear heard, neither hath it entered into the heart of man, what things God hath prepared [1204B] for them that love him [n: (Isa. LIV; I Cor. II)]*. Whence also the Psalmist says to those who profit by the trial of that suavity: *O taste and see that the Lord is sweet! blessed is the man that hopeth in him [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]*. The throat, then, of the beloved is either the voice of Holy Scripture telling forth the suavity of the divine loving-kindness, or the trial of that same loving-kindness sweetly refreshing the soul of each of the faithful. *203* It is a *throat most sweet,* because it utters the memory of so great an abundance of the divine holiness, that to the attentive helper nothing seems to sound sweeter in the ear of the heart than not only to hope for, but also to hold with firm certainty, the everlasting goods of the beatitude to come, and therefore to desire them with the whole soul.

Whence, whereas above the several parts are compared each to some [1204C] thing, as the head to gold, the locks to the high branches of palm trees, the eyes to doves, the cheeks to beds of spices, and the rest, as has been set out above, to things befitting them, this last alone is compared to nothing, because that supreme and most general good, as it is unspeakable, so is it incomparable.

And the proclamations of this beloved having been enumerated part by part, whether in himself or in his body, as though each string of this kind of ten-stringed instrument had been played out, she sets down in some manner the final note of the praise that has been sung, saying: *And he is all lovely:* as if she said: Why is it that I proclaim his beauty and dignity part by part? There is nothing in him that I can except: for *he is all lovely,* whether you attend to the loftiness of his divinity, or regard the lowliness of his humanity [1204D]. For this is the sum of the whole praise, which is sung at the end and expresses in itself a certain colour of the whole song.

For what can be said more briefly and more definitely of Christ, God and man, for the sum of praise, than that it be said, *all lovely!* For if all that is in him, or can be said of him, is lovely, is blessed on every side, assuredly it is concluded that to call him all lovely is the supreme and final measure of praise.

*Such is my beloved, and he is my friend, daughters of Jerusalem.* You have asked of me, she says, what manner of one my beloved was, for whom I so greatly adjured you; behold, I have shown you what manner of one my beloved is, whose bearing I have expressed through each of his members. But what [1205A] is to be said of this bearing? Has this beloved obtained this bearing through disposition or affection, and is he susceptible of intensification and remission? In his own nature, by no means, who is essentially good, wise, fair, holy and just; but in his body, that is, the Church, he passes through disposition or affection into this bearing, receiving more and less, according as it pleases his grace to dispose in each, his member. This quality or this bearing is his nature, because he himself is goodness, wisdom, beauty, holiness and justice.

And he is worthy of my seeking, not that I am necessary to him, but that he is very necessary to me. Why? Because *he is my* *204* *friend, O daughters of Jerusalem;* for the bearing of my inner man [1205B] is formed from him, as from image into image, through participation in the grace dwelling in my heart, by whose illumination are opened to me those secrets of invisible things which are contained in the sublimity and signification of the divine precepts and sacraments, according as understanding and reason instruct and lead me, which are the greatest beauty of that inner bearing of mine. *This my friend* guards me into knowledge of this kind through the grace of humility: for he himself says to the Father: *I confess to thee, Father, because thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones [n: (Matth. XI)]*: for humility is the guardian of the virtues. And he is called *amicus,* friend, as it were *animi custos,* guardian of the mind; for that I may [1205C] not lose this friend, I ought to have him through humility as the guard of my mind. For if the mind, affected or disposed toward divine knowledge, at some time through co-operating grace receives a bearing, that is, knowledge of this kind abiding in itself, the mind itself becomes as it were one with God by participation in him, so that it too, from fellowship with God, is called God. Whence a certain wise man says to his son: *If God is the mind, as the songs tell us,*
*Let him be chiefly worshipped by thee with a pure mind.*
As if he openly said: Since the mind is God to us, that is, the true and spiritual life, glorify this chiefly by living well. For by the ears, through the power of reason and understanding implanted in it, by discerning rightly and leading its life well, retaining God in itself, it is God [1205D], not indeed essentially, but by appellation, as the Prophet says of the saints: *I have said: You are gods, and all of you the sons of the Most High [n: (Psal. LXXXI)]*, and the power of such an utterance is the covenant of a great friendship; but, attending to the good of this friendship, not the sons but the *daughters of Jerusalem,* that is, souls still weak and not yet able to attain the perfection of the virtues, and this, in the beloved, to have established the great beauty of spirit of this beloved, they too being kindled with the love of holy desire, desire also to enjoy the good of this friendship, by which they may be able to attain [sic: *ad ipisci*] that same beauty of spirit. For they say: *Whither is thy beloved gone,* etc.

VERSE 17.-- *Whither is thy beloved gone, O most beautiful among women! whither is thy beloved turned aside? and we will seek him with thee.*

[1206A]What is it for God, who is not contained by place, and with whom there is no change nor shadow of alteration, to *go away,* or to *turn aside*? [n: (Jac. II.)] God is said in two ways to *go away* or to *turn aside,* namely both by the approach and by the withdrawal of his grace, according to the perfection or the defect of any mind, either drawing near to God, *205* or withdrawing from him. But in this place the going away of this beloved is to be moved toward someone by the mercy of his goodness, and his turning aside also is to glide into the mind of anyone by the gift of his grace.

Tell us, they say, *O most beautiful among women,* whither *thy beloved,* by going away and turning aside through his mercy and grace, has set forth to us an example of living well, or of striving more fully in any virtue, [1206B] in any of his faithful. *Tell,* that is, show, either by the reading in which thou daily recitest their life, or by the action by which thou daily numberest them into thy fellowship, and we too, led on by their example, *will seek with thee* this beloved, by praying, fasting, and calling him by other good works, that he may deign to turn aside to us also, visiting us with the gift of his grace. For he himself says: *If you shall seek me with all your heart, I shall be found by you [n: (Jerem. XXIX)]*: for to seek him is not by prayer alone, but also by good action, as also elsewhere he says of those who seek him: *For they seek me from day to day, and desire to know my ways [n: (Isa. XXXVIII)]*. For in saying that they wish to know his ways, he assuredly shows [1206C] that they seek him by good action.

*We will seek,* then, *with thee* the beloved in this manner, *O most beautiful among women.* But of what women? Assuredly either of those who in themselves bring forth to God the fruits of good work, as sons of holiness, by faith and love, or of those who beget others, as sons of light, by word and example unto the day of eternal life. Because, then, we marvel at so great a beauty in thee, *O most beautiful among women,* which thou hast drawn to thyself by seeking and finding thy beloved, therefore we too, desiring to attain some trace of thy so great and so noble beauty, desire to know to whom thy beloved is moved, or to whom he turns aside, through his mercy and grace, so that, seeking him too by thy example, after we have found him, even if not most beautiful, we may yet in some measure [1206D] be accounted beautiful. To these things the Church, or the soul beloved of God, showing the place of the moving or turning aside of this beloved, suggests at the same time also the act in that turning aside of his, saying: *My beloved is gone down into his garden,* etc.
