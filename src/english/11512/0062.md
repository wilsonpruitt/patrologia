## CHAPTER VI. (cont.)

*One is,* he says, *my dove, my perfect one:* for otherwise she is not my dove unless, bearing in her mouth the sign of peace and mercy, she keep the unity of the spirit in her heart and exercise the virtue of mercy in work: *For mercy and truth have met each other, justice and peace have kissed [n: (Psal. LXXXIV)]*, for she ought always to sing mercy and judgment [n: (Psal. C)], in the power of binding and loosing granted her by me. Nor otherwise will she be *my perfect one* unless she have full knowledge of discretion, that she may know who is justly and worthily to be bound or loosed. *One is* moreover *the dove and one my perfect one,* because outside her there is given no remission of sins, nor distribution of gifts, and therefore those are shut out who do not confess one, [1214C] holy, catholic and universal Church. For in one house the lamb is eaten and its flesh is not carried out, because there is no efficacy of the sacraments except in the one catholic Church.

*She is one to her mother,* that is, to the Jerusalem above, because she who here on earth is one in faith and in the dignity of the sacraments, there in heaven will be one in the beatitude of eternal life: for one is the beatitude of the saints, although they do not enjoy it in one manner, because of the qualities of diverse merits. *The chosen of her that bore her,* that is, of the Church of the ancient Fathers, namely of the patriarchs and prophets, whose faith she imitated and inherited the promise: [1214D] for she is chosen, to whom should be shown that promise of the purpose of God which, made to the Fathers in the first days, was fulfilled in the last days, the same faith justifying her concerning things past and future which justified the ancient Fathers concerning things future only. For faith is as much concerning things present and future as concerning things past; although faith concerning things present is said improperly and not properly: for when the Apostle, defining faith, says: *Faith and the substance of things to be hoped for, the evidence of things not appearing [n: (Hebr. XI)]* [var: Vulg. *Est autem fides sperandarum substantia rerum*, "now faith is the substance of things to be hoped for"], it is surely clear that faith is the evidence of those things which cannot appear, for things which are apparent have not faith, but acknowledgment.

But since we are treating of the Canticles of canticles, does [1215A] any musical speculation of the two numbers set above, namely sixty and eighty, pertain to them? It does not seem at all beside the matter, if, where canticles are treated, we sometimes speculate on the modes of singing. For since the whole frame of our soul and body is joined by musical fitting together, it is plain, and proved by examples, on the authority of divine and human Scripture, that the force of musical harmony has had great power for composing, *216* or for stirring, or for pacifying souls. Ought not some speculation of musical consonance to have power also in this *Canticle of canticles,* which wholly aims at kindling souls to the love of the heavenly bridegroom, for the composing or stirring of the soul? Surely in this place too it will be able to lend a little [1215B] support to the imperfection *of the concubines and young maidens,* so that they may be incited in some way to attaining the perfection which it is agreed they do not yet have.

But since every well-ordered disposition is assigned to the proportions of numbers, it must be seen what consonance consists in the proportionality of these two numbers, sixty and eighty. The relation of eighty to sixty, then, is ἐπιτριτα or sesquitertian, because eighty has within itself the whole of sixty, and its third part, that is, twenty, in which proportion διατέσσερον sounds together in music; it is called διατέσσερον from four, because it resounds with four voices, consisting of two tones and a semitone.

[1215C]If it is asked what is to the point in this place, by the proportion of these numbers, the consideration of this symphony? we answer that there is a reason sufficiently fitting in a consideration of this kind, because the whole universal Church spread through the four parts of the world, which, as has been said, is distinguished by three qualities of those living in her, that is, of the perfect, the less perfect, the imperfect, attains the whole sum of her salvation through the faith and sacraments of the New Testament, which was instituted by the perfection of the four Gospels, so that as it were a certain sweet concord of the διατέσσερον symphony, the consonance of the evangelists, consisting in four books as in four voices, by which the soul of all Christians [sic: *Christianonorum*] is stirred to the worship of holy religion and [1215D] is composed.

*The daughters of Sion saw her and declared her most blessed, the queens and concubines praised her.* Such a Church, he says, as has been said, differing in the quality of merits, but alike in the unity of faith and sacraments, *the daughters of Sion saw,* that is, souls attending by the speculation of the mind, not by carnal vision, and weighing the beauty and honour of the whole Church, in the excellence of spiritual dignities, and the grace of the virtues, and also the necessary profit of that beauty, since there consist in her both the more generous and more wholesome granting of temporal things, as the incomparable and never-ending bestowal of eternal things: accordingly *they declared her most blessed,* because nothing is more blessed than always to have God, [1216A] here temporally by using him in his gifts, there eternally by enjoying him.

*217* *The queens also and concubines praised her,* because to holy souls who know how to rule their inner and outer senses by reason this honour of holy Church and the profit of that honour is very pleasing, and they praise much, that is, they approve, the spiritual insignia of her workings and the pious supports of sacramental things.

VERSE 9.-- *Who is she that cometh forth as the dawn rising, fair as the moon, chosen as the sun, terrible as an army of a camp set in array?*

Whence also these daughters of Sion, or the beloved himself, proclaiming this beauty of hers, not interrogatively as those who do not know, but affirmatively as those who marvel, [1216B] whom indeed they know well, with such a proclamation, as it were with the eighth voice, raise and say:

*Who is she that cometh forth as the dawn rising, fair as the moon, chosen as the sun, terrible as an army of a camp set in array?* To those looking here not in passing but diligently there is offered, in the measure of praise of this kind, a modulation not to be despised of the symphony tasted beforehand, which we have called διατέσσερον, by which is hinted the confirmation of faith, hope, charity, and the sacraments, and the preaching of the Gospel through the four parts of the world, and also the useful and necessary attaining of the four principal virtues. For why should there not be attended to here, as it were, the distinction of four voices, the comparison of four diverse things, namely the coming forth of the dawn, the beauty of the moon, the choosing of the sun, [1216C] the strength of the army set in array. For what more is there in the comeliness of the whole Church? In short, if faith, hope, charity and the sacraments be lacking, if the preaching of the Gospel is not received, if the foundations of the four virtues be not present, from which the buildings of all the other virtues rise, what will be the beauty of the Church, or of each holy soul?

But it must be seen what these likenesses of things intimate, how they fit the things said above, namely faith, hope, charity and the sacraments. The *dawn* is the beginning of the day, further faith is the beginning of holiness and religion as of a certain day, because by the name of day and light holiness is understood, as also by the name of night and darkness sin. The Church, [1216D] then, or any holy soul *cometh forth as the dawn,* because, divine grace calling, she comes first to faith and through the same grace daily takes the increases of that same faith. The *moon,* moreover, always receives light from the sun, and, set in the other part of the hemisphere over against the sun, pours the full light of her orb upon the lands; but when she begins to wane, she returns to the sun, that she may be kindled again by it, and at length with the *218* fullness of her light may aid the world. And whence does holy Church, or any holy soul, receive the light of knowledge, the light also of holiness and justice, and of holy hope, but from that true sun of justice, the Lord Jesus Christ, *who enlighteneth every man that cometh into this world? [n: (Joan. I.)]* But because her beauty differs greatly from the [1217A] beauty of him from whom she receives that she be fair, she is called not absolutely fair, but by comparison *fair* is she called *as the moon:* for as the moon compared to the sun, or shining with the sun, does not shine: yet whatever of light she has she has from the sun, so also the saints compared to the Holy of holies are not, yet whatever of holiness they have, they have from him. And what is the Church but the gathering of the saints? and when, often withdrawing from divine contemplation for the sake of fraternal love, or by the obstacle of their own frailty, they have waned a little from the light of subtle understanding, gathering themselves again to themselves at some time, they run back with their whole mind to him of whom it is written: *Come ye to him and be enlightened [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]*, that, looking back upon him as through a certain opposition of the mind, [1217B] they may receive their fullness, such as it is, from him of whom it is said: *And of his fullness we all have received [n: (Joan. I)]*.

What then do these, coming forth by faith and the works of faith, conceive but hope? For who would not hope for good things to come, whose works are full of light; so that deservedly the Church is called in them *fair as the moon?*

But since faith is *as the dawn coming forth,* that is, pouring forth the first light of religion and of its holiness, through faith certainly one approaches hope, because what we believe we hope that we shall at some time obtain. Whence the Apostle [n: (Rom. V)]: *Access,* he says, *we have through faith into this grace in which we stand, and glory in the hope of the glory of the sons of God;* [var: Vulg. *et gloriamur*, "and we glory"] as if he said in other words: Through faith, which is the beginning and [1217C] as it were a certain entrance of every good work, as the dawn of the day, we approach and advance into the grace of full light, that is, of holiness, according to the measure which grace itself shall have granted us, in the grace of which light we stand, that is, we persevere, because we have hope of being joined through this to the future glory of the sons of God: and at once he adds: *And not only so,* it is understood, we glory in this hope, *but we glory also in tribulations, knowing that tribulation worketh patience, and patience trial, and trial hope, and hope confoundeth not.* And to show the efficient cause of this hope, its certainty, he adds, *because charity is poured forth in our hearts by the holy one who is given to us.* [var: Vulg. *caritas Dei … per Spiritum Sanctum*, "the charity of God … by the Holy Spirit"] For this is the anchor [1217D] of all good intention and working, because, as the same Apostle says: *Charity shall never fall away [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*.

*219* And this is what follows, *chosen as the sun,* namely because of charity, because as the sun is brighter than all the stars, so charity is more excellent than all the virtues, although according to the Godhead it is altogether incomparable. And holy Church, in those who burn with perfect charity and shine in working, is called *chosen as the sun,* because, since star differeth from star in brightness [n: (I Cor. XV)], that part of the saints is compared to the sun which is found brighter than the rest in holiness, namely in those who are still weak and tender in faith and work, *she cometh forth as the dawn;* but in those who by the progress of their manner of life have already somewhat [1218A] advanced, she is called *fair as the moon:* further, in those who are perfect, and through the charity which, being perfect, casteth out fear, in of the highest spirits, which are named seraphim, that is, burning, but passing over are joined more nearly to the sun, she is rightly spoken of as *chosen as the sun,* or *chosen as the sun,* that is, predestined unto adoption, that she herself may be this by grace, which he is by nature, the true sun, who *gave power to become sons of God to those who believe in his name [n: (Joan. I)]*.
