## CHAPTER VI. (cont.)

The name indeed is equivocal, since though in both cases it is called a *chariot*, yet in definition it is different: for to fall away from God through the suggestion of the devil and the delight of the flesh, the consent also of the spirit, and the habit of evil working, is to be the chariot of the devil; and conversely, to advance toward God through the suggestion of God, and the delight of the soul, the consent also of the spirit, and the habit of good working, is to be the chariot of God: and conversely the chariot of God is also the mind made four-square by the four virtues which we set down above, that is, by prudence, justice, temperance, fortitude; to which is opposed [1226B] the chariot of the devil, namely the mind unnerved by the four vices contrary to those very virtues, that is, by folly, iniquity, lust, fear.

The Church therefore cries, or the bridegroom himself through the Church, to the Sunamite: *Return, return, return, return,* so that she who had transgressed in these four ways, by returning to God may offer four fruits of penance also, mortifying these things in herself through the grace of God and the strength of the mind. *Return,* he says, through penance and satisfaction for past evils, *return* through contempt of the world, *return* through mortification of the flesh, *return* through constancy in the virtues. *Return,* moreover, *that we may behold thee,* that is, that after thou hast done penance we may have mercy on thee. For to see is, in God, to have mercy: whence the Psalmist to [1226C] God: *Look,* he says, *upon me and have mercy on me [n: (Psal. LXXXV)]*; and on the contrary the turning away of his face is his wrath, as the same Psalmist says: *Thou turnedst away thy face from me, and I became troubled [n: (Psal. XXIX)]*.
