## *229* CHAPTER VII. (cont.)

*[1230D] 234* VERSE 4.-- *Thy neck is as a tower of ivory: thy eyes like the fishpools in Hesebon, which are in the gate of the daughter of the multitude. Thy nose is as the tower of Libanus, which looketh toward Damascus.*

Because the voice goes out through the pipe of the neck, and food is ministered to the belly, rightly are the holy preachers designated by the *neck,* who through the office of preaching minister the victuals of doctrine to their fellow servants. But it is to be considered why it is called a *tower,* and why *of ivory. A tower,* first on this account, because the preaching of the saints is lofty on account of the sublimity of its sense; next because from the lofty contemplation of the mind, as from a certain tower, the coming of the enemy is foreseen, so that their snares may be guarded against, and this contemplation of the mind is taken from the teaching of the holy preachers: *of ivory* on account of the whiteness of the wisdom shining within; but the chaste utterance of the eloquence sounding without, as is sung in the Psalm, that *the words [1231A] of the Lord are chaste and the commandment of the Lord is lightsome, enlightening the eyes [n: (Psal. XVIII)]*.

The *neck* therefore of holy Church, or of each holy soul, is *as a tower of ivory,* because its preaching is lofty, and firm, and beautiful, and while it raises the mind to God, it defends it from the snares of enemies, and suffers it to be corrupted by no stain of heretical depravity; on the other hand the Synagogue is called through the prophet *a cloudy tower [n: (Mich. IV)]*, because the carnal knowledge of the Jews is dark and involved, which is indeed a *tower,* on account of the containing of the sublime sacraments which are hidden in it, but *cloudy* on account of the carnal understanding of the letter alone. *Thy eyes like the fishpools in Hesebon, which are in the gate of the daughter of the multitude.*

The *eyes* of holy Church, or rather of each faithful [1231B] soul, may not unfittingly be understood as two considerations in the mind, as it were in the head, directed to right and to left, namely to the smallness and misery of the temporal life, and to the eternity and felicity of the perpetual and unchangeable life.

Which are rightly called *as fishpools in Hesebon,* which is interpreted *the girdle of mourning,* because assuredly from mourning and sorrow for sins, or from desire of the eternal goods, fountains of tears flow forth like fishpools through compunction. These fishpools indeed flow forth from a twofold compunction, which springs from the consideration of these two eyes, because while the mind weighs the punishments to come, and remembers its own evils, it is pricked to penance, as it were watered from one of the two fishpools.

[1231C]But these *fishpools* are *in Hesebon,* because by this twofold compunction it is girded on the one side with the mourning of penance, on the other with that of love, so that it both may not slip back to its former vices, from the remembrance of the evils to come, and may bind itself strongly to the *235* pursuits of the virtues from desire of the goods to come, fulfilling the Lord's precept: *Let your loins be girt [n: (Luc. XII)]*.

*The fishpools* moreover are said to be *in the gate of the daughter of the multitude.* But what is this gate? surely the going out of this life and the entering in of the life to come; whence it is said in the Psalm of the blessed man: *He shall not be confounded when he shall speak to his enemies in the gate [n: (Psal. CXXVI)]*: for there shall be no confusion to the holy man in the going out of this life, [1231D] when the adverse party would withstand and harm him, because in his life he served him in whom the prince of this world found nothing of his own.

But who is the daughter of the multitude, whose that gate is said to be, but the holy soul of each faithful man, the daughter namely of that multitude of which *there was one heart and one soul [n: (Act. IV)]*? Which then indeed on earth was the rude and primitive Church, but it prefigured the unity of that which now in heaven, true and perfect concord, reigns with Christ through ineffable and unchangeable charity.

The daughter of this multitude surely has those fishpools of twofold compunction in her gate, because through penance for past evils, and desire of the goods to come, going out of this [1232A] life confidently and happily she enters the kingdoms of heaven. Beautifully therefore the *eyes* of the Church or of the holy soul are said to be as *fishpools in Hesebon which are in the gate of the daughter of the multitude,* because while from the remembrance of sins, or from love of eternal life, they are moist with the fountain of tears through twofold compunction, they open to themselves the entrance of the heavenly kingdom in the going out of life.

Otherwise the *eyes* of the Church may not unfittingly be called its overseers and teachers, to whom also this likeness can be fitted, that as the *fishpools in Hesebon* gird and fortify the city, so these gird and fortify the Church with the *girdle of mourning,* which Hesebon is interpreted, while by their teaching and preaching, as by a certain saving fountain, they call forth to compunction, to confession, to satisfaction, [1232B] saying that word of the Gospel: *Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven will draw near [n: (Matth. III)]*. For penance can rightly be called the girdle of mourning, because the life of the penitent ought so to be girded and fortified on every side that he both mourns what he has committed, and does not further commit what is to be mourned. For that is true penance by which past sins are so looked upon that future ones are feared, and it has mourning from the past, fear from the future. But because this fear hath punishment, while from the remembrance of the past and fear of the future it draws on mourning of mind, rightly does the penitent bind his life with the girdle of mourning.

But these *fishpools* *in Hesebon* are *in* the *236* *gate of the daughter of the multitude:* but who is the daughter of the multitude, or what is this multitude? Did not the apostles, and their successors, apostolic men, [1232C] beget holy Church? Beholding whose dignity the Psalmist says: *Exceedingly,* he says, *are thy friends honoured, O God, exceedingly is their principality strengthened [n: (Psal. CXXXVIII)]*; considering that they were not few but many, *I will number,* he says, *them, and they shall be multiplied above the sand [n: (ibid.)]* The *daughter of this multitude* therefore is holy Church, whom, as we have said, they begot by their teaching, who has a *gate,* namely the door of discretion, through which she discerns whom or what sort she should repel from her body. For since tares are always mixed with the wheat, the good indeed she incorporates into herself as sweet foods, the bad she tolerates in her body like bad humours, but heretics she utterly estranges from herself as deadly [1232D] poison.

In this gate of discretion, therefore, the overseers or teachers of holy Church stand daily; as certain eyes in the head they know how to discern through the spirit of wisdom who belong, or do not belong, to the body of the Church, which fishes are to be chosen or which rejected on the shore of the last station: in fine, to the faithful they show the doors of life open, for sinners who do penance they open them when closed, but for the impious and those who do not do penance they close them eternally.

Or, *the gate of the daughter of the multitude* is called faith, which is the beginning and as it were a certain entrance to the way of salvation, without which it is impossible to please God [n: (Hebr. XI)], which the holy teachers set forth in the forefront to those seeking the way of life, and desiring to come [1233A] to the heavenly fatherland; because each ought first to believe rightly, and then, believing rightly, to work well.

But because we have said that this *gate* *of the daughter of the multitude* is the grace of discretion, by which the holy overseers and teachers of the Church know how either to admit the penitent or to exclude those who do not do penance, what sort that discretion is or ought to be is added, when it is said: *Thy nose is as the tower of Libanus,* etc.

For by the *nose,* by which we discern odours and stenches, is designated the discretion of the saints, through which they know how to discern the odours of the virtues and the stenches of the vices, that they may understand what they ought to choose and what to reject. Which surely is said to be a *nose as the tower of Libanus,* [1233B] because, namely, the discretion of those set over others ought both always to be fortified by circumspection, and always to stand in the height of life, that is, not to lie in the valley of infirm working. For as a tower is set on a mountain for a watch-post, that enemies who come may be seen from farther off, so the life of the preacher ought always to remain fixed on high, that after the manner of the nostrils it may discern the stenches *237* of the vices, and the odours of the virtues, may foresee from afar the onsets of the malignant spirits, and make the souls committed to it wary through its own prudence. And because discretion itself is said to be the mother of the virtues, it is rightly compared to the tower of Libanus, which sounds *whitening,* inasmuch as through it the beauty of all the virtues is understood to be guarded.

But this tower is said to look *toward Damascus,* [1233C] because the life of the saints is fortified with a strong guard against the temptations of the vices through the lofty consideration of discretion. For Damascus is interpreted *drinking blood,* by which this world is rightly designated, which is daily made drunk with vices and sins; for the prophet shows that sins are designated by blood, saying: *And blood hath touched blood [n: (Ose. IV)]*, that is, sin has come to sin; and the Psalmist: *Deliver me,* he says, *from bloods, O God, God of my salvation [n: (Psal. L)]*.

This tower therefore is directed *toward Damascus,* because the life of the saints, lofty and fortified with virtues, is fortified beforehand against the pleasures of this world by strong and discreet consideration.

VERSE 5.-- *Thy head is like Carmelus: and the locks of thy head as the purple of the king joined to the channels.* [var: Vulg. *vincta canalibus*, "bound in the channels"]

[1233D]By the *head* the principal part of the mind is designated. But Carmelus is interpreted *knowing circumcision* or *soft river.*

Rightly therefore is the holy mind compared to Carmelus, because it is both lofty in divine contemplation, and knows how to cut off from itself the carnality of manners or of thoughts by spiritual circumcision, and, the grace of the divine Spirit pouring it in, is zealous to water the hearts of the hearers with sweet teaching as with a soft river. *And the locks of thy head as the purple of the king, joined to the channels.*

*The locks of the head* may be understood as the thoughts of the mind, which are said to be *as the purple of the king joined to the channels,* because the more they are joined to and intent upon the Scriptures as upon certain channels, the more they are kindled to the imitation of the passion of Christ by mortifying [1234A] their flesh with its vices and concupiscences, or by offering the flesh itself, if need be, to persecutions for Christ, sacrificing to God the precious purple of heart and body in martyrdom.

We can also in another way fit this little portion of the speech to the head itself of the Church, that is, to the Lord Jesus Christ, namely so that in his own person he brings himself in speaking to that same Church, his bride: *Thy head is like Carmelus, and the locks of thy head as the purple of the king joined to the channels.* I, he says, O bride, who am called and am thy head, can be compared to Mount Carmel on account of its height and the interpretation of its name; because since I am the most general good, there is nothing that could be more excellent than my nature according to the divinity, and further according to the humanity [1234B] *238* nothing holier than my life and conversation, and in my teaching true and perfect circumcision of heart is learned, and all my preaching runs down truly as a soft river, that it may refresh the minds of the hearers by its wholesomeness, and form them to the pursuits of good work. *The locks of this thy head* are my apostles, my other saints also, who cleave to me by charity as locks to the head, to whom *purple* well agrees, because as the purple which is nearer the channels is better dyed, so their mind which is made nearer to my words and institutions or those of my prophets is the more kindled by power to the love of me, and in my tabernacle, that is, in my Church, as precious and royal *purple,* namely scarlet twice dyed [n: (Exod. XXVIII)], to shine forth with the love of God and of the neighbour it [1234C] strives.
