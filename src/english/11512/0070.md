## *247* CHAPTER VIII.

VERSE 1.-- *Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother, that I may find thee without, and kiss, and now no one may despise me.*

If we look into the allegorical sense, this voice can be referred to those saints who before the coming of the Lord burned with great desire to see what they knew had long been foretold by the patriarchs and prophets, and what they themselves foresaw in the spirit as to come, namely [1241C] the incarnation of Christ, through which they did not doubt that the universal salvation of the world would come about. Let that ancient people of the saints, then, say in the desiderative mode of speech: *Who shall give thee to me for my brother sucking the breasts of my mother: my brother,* I say, *my* on account of the taking up of human flesh, or the kinship of race, *sucking the breasts of my mother,* that is, born and nourished in the synagogue of the Jews. And why with this desire? *that I may find thee,* she says, *without and kiss,* that is, that I may see thee openly in the flesh and embrace thee, whom in the secret of the Godhead I cannot see: *and now no one may despise me,* nay rather may venerate and love me, on account of the special nobility of so noble an offspring born of my flesh; *I will take hold* moreover [1241D] *and bring into the house of my mother,* because this pleased thy condescension, that in the nation of the Jews alone thou shouldst show the presence of thy flesh and demonstrate the power of thy miracles. *There* then *thou shalt teach me* both by words and by deeds what I ought to understand in figure, what in truth, and that thou thyself art *the end of the precept unto justice to every one that believeth [n: (Rom. X)]* [var: Vulg. Rom 10:4 *Finis enim legis, Christus*, "For the end of the law is Christ"; *finis praecepti* is 1 Tim 1:5]. But how much shall I profit by the sweetness of thy teaching! *I will give thee a cup of spiced wine,* because thou shalt, as it were, be given to drink with the delight of my faith and working, which I will offer thee in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and in the virtue of faith, hope and charity. For as spiced wine consists chiefly of three things, that is, of wine, honey and spices, so my mind is seasoned from the teaching of thy words with the faith of the Holy Trinity, or, as [1242A] has been said, with the virtue of faith, hope *248* and charity. *I will give* also *new wine of my pomegranates,* because thy preaching or working will bring forth in me the newness and sweetness of a soul purged from vices, from the fervour of charity and the zeal of manifold virtues.

Further, if we consider the moral sense, we shall wish this voice for every faithful soul which, intent on sound Scripture, builds up itself with knowledge and others with the word of exhortation: for the soul that desires to have the knowledge of Holy Scripture cries to God with all the eagerness of the mind in these words: *Who shall give thee to me for my brother sucking the breasts of my mother?* Why I call thee *brother*, assuredly both on account of the sharing of humanity and on account of the grace of adoption, by which thou namest thyself *brother*, saying: *Go, tell my brethren [1242B] [n: (Matth. XXVIII)]*, and: *Whosoever shall do the will of my Father, he is my brother, sister and mother [n: (Matth. XII)]*. *Who* then *shall give thee to me for my brother,* that thou mayest be with me, and labour with me through co-operating grace, *thee* I say *sucking,* that is, making to suck, *the breasts of my mother.* For this is said in that mode of speech which Scripture also uses in another place: *For he himself asketh for us with unspeakable groanings [n: (Rom. VIII)]*: saying *asketh* for what is making to ask.

But what are *the breasts of my mother,* and of what mother of mine? Assuredly *the breasts of my mother* are the doctrines of holy Church, which from the pages of both Testaments nourish the infancy of my mind and bring it forth into the strength of manly understanding. But why do I so greatly [1242C] desire thee to be with me? *that I may find thee without and kiss:* what is this? For where dost thou lie hidden when thou art not without? Assuredly in the cloud of the day, that is, in the obscurity and subtlety of spiritual understanding; and when I have received it by the illumination of thy fire, that is, of the Holy Spirit, assuredly I find without thee who didst lie hidden within, and when I have found thee, *I kiss,* that is, I embrace thee with all the arms of my mind, because I so delight in the gift of understanding received that it pleases me to say with the Prophet: *How sweet are thy words to my palate, more than honey and the honeycomb to my mouth! [n: (Psal. CXVIII.)]* [var: Vulg. Ps 118:103 *super mel ori meo*, "more than honey to my mouth", without *et favum*]

*Now* indeed, as I thus cleave to thy embraces and wisely speak out the mysteries of Holy Scripture, [1242D] *no one shall despise me,* because, as it is written: *The wise man shall be filled with blessings, and the living shall praise him [n: (Eccli. XXXVII)]* [var: Vulg. Sir 37:27 *videntes*, "they that see him"], and: *The wise man shall inherit honour among the people, and his name shall be living for ever [n: (Ibid.)]*; and again: *The mouth of the prudent is sought in the Church, and they shall think upon his words in their hearts [n: (Eccli. XXI)]*. *No one* therefore *shall despise me,* because they hear that the anointing of thy Spirit teaches me concerning all things [n: (I Joan. II)], nay, falling upon the earth they have adored the Lord, declaring that God is truly in us [n: (I Cor. XIV)].

VERSE 2.-- *I will take hold of thee and bring into the house of my mother. There thou shalt teach me, and I will give thee a cup of spiced wine, and new wine of my pomegranates.*

[1243A] *249* So much, moreover, does this sweetness please the palate of my heart that, having found thee in this way, I would not hold thee alone for my own profit, but rather, *taking hold, I will bring thee into the house of my mother,* namely the household of holy Church, so that what I understand from thy grace I may share with goodwill with my brethren who desire to hear and to learn.

*There* then *thou shalt teach me,* because the more I teach others, the more I learn with them: for conference brings more than reading. *And I will give thee a cup of spiced wine,* because by thus teaching, or conferring, I shall set forth the figure of the law with the truth and grace of the Gospel, or the historical sense seasoned with allegory and morality, and this *cup* I will give thee, because I shall bring all this knowledge to thy honour and grace.

[1243B] *I will give thee* also *new wine of my pomegranates,* because while I preach the patience of the saints and the manifold labour of the virtues, I myself also receive the example of their labour and patience; all of which, however, I ascribe neither to their power nor to mine, but to thy loving-kindness and grace.

Therefore, by so great a presence of my beloved and my brother, and by the sweetness and grace of him abiding with me, snatched away as it were from all the pleasures and cares of this world, I turn to you, O daughters of Jerusalem, and I declare what I hold in me as my purpose, and show with how great charity I cleave to him. What, they say, is this? *His left hand,* she says, *under my head.*

[1243C]VERSE 3.-- *His left hand under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me.*

For all the temporal goods granted me by him I put after, for love of the eternal goods, but the eternal goods themselves I desire with my whole mind, and I purpose to attain to them with all the strength of my soul. For although *in his left hand* are *riches and glory [n: (Prov. III)]*, as regards the usefulness and beauty of the creature by which man is led to the knowledge of his Creator, so that he may not be inexcusable [sic: *inexusabilis*], yet *length* of *days is in his right hand [n: (ibid.)]*, not such as is ended, but such as abides unchangeably in its own blessedness.

*His left hand* then *under my head* will be, but his *right hand* *shall embrace me,* because the memory of temporal things will hold me, yet not for delight in them and [1244A] one perpetual, but the desire of eternal things will surround me wholly, so much that it turns all my senses to the pursuit of them, and compels me to forget the things that are behind, and to stretch my whole self forth into those that are before. [n: (Phil. III)].

*250* But because the bridegroom does these things, who rejoices in the pious pursuits and progress of the soul, and is delighted by the sweetness of her devotion and pure intention, *I adjure you,* he says, *daughters of Jerusalem,* etc.

VERSE 4.-- *I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir not up nor make the beloved to awake, till she herself please.*

Do not, he says, daughters of Jerusalem, lead the beloved away from so profitable and honourable a purpose of hers by any care of passing things, importunately suggesting to her [1244B] your necessity or her own concerning outward things, which do not allow the mind to be free to seek and perfectly to keep the leisure of holy contemplation; but rather leave this to her judgment, who is taught by the anointing of my Spirit [n: (I Joan. II)]; hold this discretion, that she may know when it is profitable now to apply herself to divine things, now to condescend to your infirmity. Or else, the *beloved* that is to be, in no way *stir up, nor make to awake,* by compelling her with threats or promises, because God does not approve forced services; rather leave her to her own will, because it is written: *Voluntarily will I sacrifice to thee [n: (Psal. LIII)]*, and, *They that offer themselves of their own accord to the Lord, are blessed by the Lord [n: (Lev. XXII)]*: *But no one cometh [1244C] to me, unless the Father who hath sent me draw him [n: (Joan. VI)]* [var: Vulg. Jo 6:44 *nemo potest venire ad me*, "no man can come to me"]. For if he is drawn he comes, if he does not come he is not drawn: therefore do not compel her *till she herself please:* for then will the sacrifice of a good work be acceptable to God, if it is supported by the seasoning of a good will.

But you, most beloved sisters, whom this part of the Canticle has held with its melody somewhat long and at some length, attend to this voice, as it were the last, in its laying down, considering how acceptable to God and how sweet it is for the soul to rest in the purpose of holy conversation and to sleep in the divine pursuit of contemplation; and on the contrary how dangerous, and how contrary to God, it is to lead you away from this purpose on any occasion, and to twist you aside to the care of temporal things.
