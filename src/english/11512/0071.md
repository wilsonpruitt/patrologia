## BOOK FOUR.

[1243]

[1243D] *251* If the zeal of my littleness in this special kind of Canticle had so far profited with your love that, stirred up in your whole spirit, with the cares of temporal things put behind you, you could say with the Apostle: *I will pray with the spirit, I will pray also with the power; I will sing with the spirit, I will sing also with the power [n: (I Cor. XIV)]* [var: Vulg. 1Cor 14:15 *mente*, "with the understanding", both times], my heart would assuredly exult greatly in the Lord, because it would be no small rejoicing of mine to say with the Apostle: *The joy and my crown are you in the Lord [n: (Philip. V)]*. For if [1244D] after the manner of my singing you now already put forth leaves somewhat in the Lord, you rightly praise the canticle, because this assuredly is to praise the canticle: to experience the canticle and its measure. But what is the force of this canticle? According to its name, by which it is called *Canticle of Canticles,* to bear the mind more excellently and more sublimely to the love of its Maker, and perfectly to lay aside from it whatever from the things of the world can stand in the way of contemplation of this kind. If then you have [1245A] experienced it somewhat, let the concord and harmony of flesh and spirit tell you, for which not to be opposed to one another is to sing beautifully and to make melody rightly. If the handmaid serves the free woman, that is, if the flesh consents to reason, there is no better nor sweeter harmony which may please the supreme Godhead; this indeed is to sing *a new canticle* and of this kind is the *praise* that is *in the Church of the saints [n: (Psal. CXLIX)]*. For *the praise of God* is the pious life and conversation of the saints, because they have this such living of theirs from the grace and kindness of God: therefore God is to be praised, not man himself, because this is not the work of man, but the work of God who justifies man himself.

For what is *a new canticle,* but the venerable commandment of the New Testament? What is it to sing *a new canticle* [1245B] but to put off the old man and put on the new? *Put off,* says the Apostle, *the old man, and put on the new, who according to God is created in justice and holiness [n: (Ephes. IV)]*: therefore to be just and holy is assuredly to sing a new canticle, for all perfection of holiness is recognized from the instrument of the evangelical instruction. For whereas the law says: *Thou shalt not kill,* the Gospel teaches that one must not be angry without cause; and whereas the law *252* says that one must not commit adultery, the Gospel teaches that one must not sin even in concupiscence [n: (Exod. XX; Matth. V)]. Such perfection of holiness is surely and assuredly the melody of the new canticle. This new canticle the Church of the saints sings, and by frequent singing of this kind attains to the singing of [1245C] *the Canticle of Canticles.* For what indeed is it to sing the Canticle of Canticles, but to love the Lord with the whole mind, the whole soul, the whole strength? For as it is called the Canticle of Canticles because it surpasses all other canticles in its dignity, so charity can be called the virtue of virtues, because it surpasses all virtues by its excellence.

If then you, O beloved sisters, truly and sincerely love the Lord, assuredly you sing the Canticle of Canticles; and because *praise is in the Church of the saints,* in you certainly will be the praise of God, who, as we believe, are the Church of the saints. Bless therefore the Lord in his works and sing to him *in the canticles of your lips, and with harps [n: (Eccli. XXXIX)]*. For whatever marks of religion and holiness are in you, they are [1245D] assuredly his works, in which, when you bless, when you ascribe all these things not to your own industry but to his grace.

Hence you ought to sing to him *in the canticles of lips and harps,* lest it be said of you: *This people honoureth me with the lips, but their heart is far from me [n: (Isa. XXIX)]*. For what is it to sing *in the canticles of lips and harps?* Is it not when voice and life praise the Lord? for then lips and harp sound well together. Now in the harp are stretched strings made from the entrails of dead animals, which are fitted in the wood in so diverse a manner that they give a diverse sound according to the tension and the diversity of the measures. What then can be designated by the harp, but the mind of the just man? in which strings made from the entrails of dead animals are stretched, because in [1246A] it are presented the mortifications of vices, which it has learned from the sayings and writings, or the deeds, of the holy Fathers withdrawn from this life, by which it so chastises its senses inwardly and outwardly that in the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth it presses down with sweet melody the concord of flesh and Spirit. And a certain diversity of sounds is there in some manner denoted, as long as the mortification of pride is carried out in one way, that of lust in another, of wrath in another, and of avarice in another; rightly, with a certain most beautiful harmony, the mind is seen to sound forth delightfully to the Lord its God *a new canticle,* nay *the Canticle of Canticles:* you sing *in the canticles* then *of lips and harps,* when both by voice and by work you pay to the Lord your God the service of your holiness. If then at the voice of this canticle your mind has been for some time stirred up, as we believe, [1246B] to the love of the heavenly bridegroom, and if you have the ear of the heart ready for a harmony of this kind, your devotion will the more animate us to the things that remain. For no one willingly recounts to an unwilling hearer.

I shall begin, then, to touch the last part, as it were the final string of this Canticle, the Holy Spirit guiding the fingers of the mind, and in it, the Lord helping, I shall lay down the whole *253* harmony of the promised tetrachord.

VERSE 5.-- *Who is she that goeth up from the desert, flowing with delights, leaning upon her beloved? Under the apple tree I raised thee up; there thy mother was corrupted, there she that bore thee was violated.*

If I ascribe this voice to the Church wondering at the conversion [1246C] of Judaea, it will not be absurd; because it was assuredly to be wondered at that a people of hard neck and untamable heart, which after the passion of the Lord would not believe him, though many signs attested his Godhead, but rather, after many injuries inflicted upon him, fastened him to the cross, suddenly, when the apostles preached, seized the way of salvation and took up the doctrine of the new law.

And she wonders, and not without cause, because she greatly attends to this, that *she goeth up from the desert,* that *flowing with delights,* that *leaning upon the beloved* she goes up. For the Synagogue was a kind of desert, abandoned by God on account of unbelief, as he threatens concerning her through the prophet, *And there shall be left,* he says, [1246D] *the daughter of Sion as a covert in a vineyard, and as a lodge in a garden of cucumbers, as a city that is laid waste [n: (Baruch VI)]*. And the Lord in the Gospel: *There shall be left,* he says, *to you your house deserted [n: (Matth. XXIII)]*. From this *desert,* then, Judaea, when she believed, *went up* through faith, because from that low-lying and weak understanding of carnal observance she passed over to the spiritual and sublime form of the evangelical instruction. But she could not go up except *flowing with delights,* because she could not attain to that sublimity of new understanding and grace except through the affluence of the virtues and the abundance of holy charity. But she could not flow with these delights of the virtues unless she leaned *upon the beloved* of holy Church, that is, Christ, because without his co-operating grace no [1247A] proclamations of holiness and faith could come to her.

Further, in another way, if thou look to each holy soul, attend carefully to what is above, *where the beloved adjures the daughters of Jerusalem that they stir not up nor make the beloved to awake till she herself please.* For what dost thou think the beloved attained in this her sleep, that the beloved should so earnestly forbid her now to be stirred up and awakened? What, I say, so beautiful, so delightful, and so pleasing to himself did almighty God attend to in this her sleep. Assuredly, if thou considerest rightly, this sweetest beloved regards with the placable countenance of his condescension something great and honourable and worthy of admiration. For he wonders at her ascending, at the place also of the ascent, [1247B] at the bearing of the one ascending and at the support of her ascending. What is this ascent as of one sleeping and resting, but a contemplative lifting up from earthly things to heavenly, from the world to the Lord? But because solitude of mind and body helps this ascent much, for the one is helped by the other, since that of the mind is defended by the solitude of the body, and solitude of the body profits little if the mind is busied about the din *254* of the world the almighty Father with a serene gaze of loving-kindness wonders at the soul going up from the desert, saying: *Who she that goeth up from the desert? Who is she?* he says, « *who hath disposed ascents in her heart, in the vale of tears [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)]*, » *going up from the desert,* that is, from the solitude of a mind [1247C] despising the din of this world and sleeping in the quiet of the Spirit and choosing only the observance of my commandments? For from this it follows that she conceives such a bearing that she seems *to flow with delights,* because this is what the observance of my commandments confers, that she flows with the delights of the virtues, nor is it anything else to flow with virtues than to keep my precepts. For whatever can be or be called virtue, this is all contained in the catalogue of my precepts. But whence has she this power, namely *to go up, to flow with delights?* Is it from herself? By no means, because every soul is infirm and weak without co-operating grace. But who is this beloved? My Son, I say, of whom I said to you: *This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased (Matth. [1247D] III)*. Because then she was *leaning upon him,* because she followed his footsteps, keeping and, as far as she could, imitating what he showed and taught through the flesh by doing and speaking, therefore she was able to go up in mind, and to flow with the delights of the virtues.

It will not be absurd to understand it in another way also, namely that we take the *desert* as the present life, and these *delights* with which *the beloved flowing goes up* as Holy Scripture. For why should Holy Scripture not be called *delights,* in which so great diversities of understanding for the profit of the soul are found, like diverse dishes of food at one table: for as among diverse dishes one food is more delicate than another, so in Holy Scripture among diverse understandings one sense is more subtle than another: for there, now [1248A] the bare history feeds those who read like a coarser food, now under the texture of the letter the moral allegory refreshes them to the marrow like a more subtle food, now contemplation, lifting them up to higher things, sweetly affects the mind like a singular and choice food. For whoever *flows with delights,* is loosened in a certain relaxation of himself, and is released from the pursuit of labour as if from weariness: because assuredly when the soul has begun to abound inwardly in delights, it by no means any longer consents to brood upon earthly desires, but, taken by the love of its Maker, and free in a sweet captivity, it sighs by fainting to contemplate his beauty, and as it were by growing weary grows strong, because while it can no longer carry sordid works, it hastens through quiet to him whom it loves inwardly. *From the desert,* then, of the present life of the world *the soul flowing with [1248B] delights goes up,* because while it is fed with the mystical understandings of the Holy *255* Scriptures, it is lifted up daily to contemplate the things above.

Yet she is described as *leaning upon the beloved,* because unless she herself cleaves to Holy Scripture by studiously searching it, she by no means lifts the step of her ascent to grasp those sublime things of Spiritual understanding: for the mind intent on Holy Scripture *leans* upon her beloved, because while God is found and recognized in Scripture itself, the mind of the one recognizing and loving leans as it were upon him.

This voice, then, can be that of the Church exulting at the progress of each holy soul and saying: *Who is she that goeth up from the desert, flowing with delights, leaning upon her beloved?* As if she said in other words: [1248C] Who is she that, intent on the meditation of the Holy Scriptures, cleaves with such zeal to my beloved, the Lord Jesus Christ, that by their mystical understandings, as it were flowing with certain delights, she is able to go up through contemplation to the love of eternal life.
