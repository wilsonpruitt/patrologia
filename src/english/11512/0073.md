## BOOK FOUR. (cont.)

For why should the holy soul not despise all its substance for the love of God, when it despises its very self [1252B] so far that for the same charity of God it does not fear death? For if it despises for the sake of his love all those things which are not God, what is there that it could set before that same love? And since there is nothing in heaven which it sets before charity, which is God, assuredly it is not a little but nothing if it despises all its substance for its sake. It follows therefore that charity is the highest good, if no good can be set before it.

VERSE 8.-- *Our sister is a little one and hath no breasts; what shall we do to our sister in the day in which she is to be spoken to?*

Above the beloved had commended the eminence of charity, and had shown of what kind or how great a good charity itself is; how it can be reached [1252C] he now purposes to demonstrate: because, since it is of so great excellence, so great dignity, the way is not easy to lay hold of it, and to hold it in the peace of perpetual security. Necessary therefore for obtaining it are constancy or fortitude of mind, knowledge of the Scriptures, and perseverance in good work. Wherefore the beloved says of the beloved, that is, the Church, or any holy soul whatever, which he calls sister on account of faith and good working, that *she is a little one and hath no breasts;* and he gives at once counsel as to what is to be done for this imperfection.

*Our sister,* he says, *is a little one:* whence is she a little one? Because she is still tender in faith, in sense, in word and reason, in strength of mind and in the [1252D] action of a good life. *And hath no breasts.* How *hath she no breasts?* She does not yet have the understanding of Holy Scripture, which is contained in the two Testaments, because although she reads and hears what *260* the letter shows and sounds, she has not yet received the knowledge of spiritual understanding, in which all the force and method of speaking and disputing rightly consists, without which the power neither of teaching nor of preaching can be obtained. Wherefore she ought first *to put away the things that are of a little one [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*, and to tend to perfection by believing and working, that after she has had breasts through the entire knowledge of the two Testaments, then at length she may fittingly take up the office of teaching and preaching.

*What then shall we do,* he says, *to our sister, in the day [1253A] when she is to be spoken to?* Since our sister is still a little one, and therefore cannot be spoken to, yet let her grow daily and desire to attain to perfection; when the time of speaking to her shall have come, so that it may be said to her: *Arise, make haste, my love, show me thy face, let thy voice sound in my ears [n: (Cant. II)]*, what counsel shall we find in the day of discretion now possessed, of understanding also and knowledge, and of an honourable life?

VERSE 9.-- *If she is a wall, let us be built upon it bulwarks of silver: if she is a door, let us join it together with boards of cedar.* [var: Vulg. Ct 8:9 *aedificemus*, "let us build"]

There are two qualities of prelates: one of those who, intent on the office of public preaching, against heretics and all reprobates *themselves as a wall for the house [1253B] of Israel,* that is, the Church, *do oppose [n: (Ezech. XIII)]*. The other of those who, bearing only the office of teacher and cleaving to the contemplative life, kindle their subjects to the pursuit of good action by word and example, to whom they are as it were a door, when they show them the entrance of the heavenly kingdom by teaching rightly and working well.

*What then shall we do to our sister, in the day* such *when she is to be spoken to,* and admonished that she now begin to rise to preaching or teaching? She must have two things necessary for this office, namely a *wall* and a *door,* that she may be constant and learned, resisting heretics and instructing the people of God: for she will have the wall for defence, the door for leading in.

[1253C] *If* then *she is a wall,* what shall we do? *Let us build,* he says, *upon it bulwarks of silver:* for bulwarks are necessary against the ambushes of enemies; but why *of silver?* because *the words of the Lord are silver tried by the fire [n: (Psal. XI)]*. Bulwarks of silver therefore are the fortifications of the divine words, which are to be built in the mind of the preacher through the knowledge of the Holy Scriptures. *If,* he says, *she is a wall, let us build upon this wall bulwarks of silver;* if, he says, she has amid adversity and prosperity a spirit of fortitude *261* and of such constancy that she stands like a wall for the defence of justice, let us give her also the knowledge of the Holy Scriptures, from whose sentences, as from certain bulwarks, [1253D] she may be able to fight against the depravities of heretics or of perverse men and to defend the reason of truth.

Further, *if she is a door,* and studiously keeps watch to this end, that she may instruct her hearers with the word of life, and lead them into a good manner of living, what shall we do? *Let us join,* he says, *that door together with boards of cedar;* for a painting is in place of reading to those who do not know letters. What then is the matter of this painting which is to be placed on this door, but the life and conversation of the saints? For when any prelate, for the sake of edification, sets before his subjects the examples of the saints, assuredly he paints as it were in their minds what they ought to love, what to fear, or how they ought to order their life in imitation of them.

[1254A]Whence also the painting itself is said [sic: *dieitur*] to be composed *with boards of cedar;* for the cedar is of an incorruptible nature, and therefore *boards of cedar* are rightly understood as the saints, because they are corrupted by no rottenness of vices, nay they fill the Church with a good odour, because *the good odour of Christ* they are *in every place [n: (II Cor. II)]*.

*With boards of cedar then the door is joined together* when any one of the preachers confirms his preaching by the virtues of the saints whom he brings in for example, so that he may open the gate of eternal life to those who imitate, or shut it to those who despise.

Therefore holy Church, or any holy soul whatever, which tends to the love of her bridegroom, while she is still a little one and tender in faith and knowledge, in morals and discipline, if she [1254B] desires to attain that perfection which may bring forth in her the full charity of God, it will be necessary for her to have, with constancy of mind, the bulwarks of the divine words, and to stand with happy perseverance in doctrine also and a good life. For when she has had the breasts of preaching and teaching, so that she can also nourish others and lead them to perfection, she seems as it were bound by debt to share also with her neighbours this which she has from the grace of God, as the Apostle says: *To the Greeks and to the barbarians, to the wise and to the unwise I am a debtor, so, as much as is in me, I am ready to preach the gospel to you also that are at Rome [n: (Rom. I)]*. Whence, at once after the grace of knowledge has been obtained, presenting herself of her own accord at the door of preaching she constantly answers her bridegroom: *I am a wall and my breasts are as a tower.*

*[1254C] 262* VERSE 10.-- *I am a wall, and my breasts are as a tower since I became before him as one finding peace.*

For she shows the cause of the grace conferred upon her, namely the finding of peace, through which she strives to tend constantly by preaching and working to her own profit and that of others; for since *the life of man upon earth* is *a warfare [n: (Job VII)]*, when one is in the fight and suffers the force of adversaries, how should he stand for the defence of others, who is still labouring for his own? It is necessary therefore first to feel peace and quiet in oneself, to obtain inmost security for him, and then first to be a wall for the defence of others; first to know how to instruct, rule and teach oneself, then to extend the breasts of one's doctrine to the profit of others, [1254D] which also is a sign of a certain peace, because when the mind labours on account of ignorance, and struggles with itself to obtain knowledge, once knowledge has been obtained, after it long fought with itself, it is assuredly proved to have gained the fruit of great peace and quiet.

What peace then does she say she has found, and where? Assuredly that peace *which surpasseth all understanding [n: (Philip. IV)]*, which the bridegroom himself left to his lovers, saying: *Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you [n: (Joan. XIV)]*; for what peace can be preferred to this peace, which here indeed begins, but there abides eternally? For Christ himself is peace, of whom the Apostle says: *For he is our peace, who [1255A] hath made both one [n: (Ephes. II)]*; whence also she says she found this before him. *Since,* she says, *I became before him as one finding peace:* for there she found peace, where she recognized as present him who is true peace. But how did she find him present and recognize him? and how, he being present, did she find peace? Assuredly in such a way as he himself taught, who says to his own: *Have salt in you, and have peace among you [n: (Marc. IX)]*. For being about to teach concerning having peace, not without cause did he put first, *have salt in you;* for in no way can anyone attain this peace unless he has had a mind seasoned with the salt of wisdom; for as a certain one says. Wisdom is to have been free of folly, because as salt takes away rottenness from flesh, so wisdom takes away folly from the heart. And by the seasoning of wisdom, [1255B] folly having been taken away from the heart, which begets the rottenness of vices, one is quickly drawn together to this peace, by which every fight between flesh and spirit is lulled to sleep, and the flesh desires nothing that is repugnant to the spirit; and so, this peace having been found in this land, one will at some time come to that which is to be taken away by no end. But how is this fight taken away, so that *263* peace may be granted, except before him, except in the presence of him who, consoling his own who are thus to fight, says: *Have confidence, I have overcome the world [n: (Joan. XVI)]*; as if he said: Because I have overcome, through me you also shall overcome?

Rightly then the bride says: *Since I became before him as one finding peace, I am a wall, and my breasts are as a tower;* because since by his grace I have overcome carnal affections, and have been strengthened with spiritual peace, I can [1255C] stand for the salvation of others also, and constantly speak forth the words of exhortation. *My breasts* indeed *are as a tower,* because I have grown so much from the divine grace in the word of exhortation that I can, according to the prophet, ascend a high mountain and lift up my voice with strength that I may preach the gospel to Jerusalem [n: (Isa. XL.)] Indeed *I am a wall,* for defending and keeping the people of God, *and my breasts are as a tower,* that I may be able to look forth from on high, and to announce from afar things to come.

VERSE 11.-- *The peaceable had a vineyard in that which hath peoples: he let it out to keepers, a man bringeth for the fruit thereof a thousand pieces of silver.*

It is greatly to be considered who this peaceable one is, [1255D] or what is the vineyard of the peaceable one, and who the peoples, or who the keepers, or what the fruit of this vineyard.

This peaceable one is the Lord Jesus Christ: for the causes of the peace noted above he is called peaceable, *whose place is made in peace [n: (Psal. LXXV)]*, and who called us in peace, *dissolving the enmities in his flesh, making void the law of commandments in decrees, that he might make the two in himself, into one new man making peace, that he might reconcile both in one body to God, by the cross killing the enmities in himself, and coming he preached peace to them that were afar off and peace to them that were near [n: (Ephes. II)]* [var: Vulg. Eph 2:17 *pacem vobis, qui longe fuistis*, "peace to you that were afar off"].
