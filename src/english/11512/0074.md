## BOOK FOUR. (cont.)

With the eye of the inner man illuminated it is to be carefully looked into how fittingly, how truly, and how uniquely this one is peaceable, who such and so great and so long-lasting [1256A] enmities dissolved in his flesh; the wonderful and adorable majesty of the reconciliation that was made is then the more recognized, when the quality and the quantity of the enmity is weighed; for how great an enmity did the first man contract toward God through the evil of disobedience, that he not only lost that felicity of paradise in which he had been placed, but also took upon himself death, the avenger of transgression? But the weight of this enmity grew through the succession of the human offspring by the increase of malice and wickedness, so that the Maker himself, as Scripture says, *touched inwardly with sorrow of heart*, said: *It repenteth me to have made man [n: (Gen. VI)]*, and therefore, sentence of judgment having been given, save Noe, who alone was then saved with his wife and sons, the whole world was consumed by the avenging water.

[1256B]But who would dissolve the weight of so great an enmity? Would *264* an angel? But an angel, equally with man a creature of God, needed the grace of God, and a creature could not free a creature from a necessity of this kind. There came therefore the Maker of the creature himself, needing nothing, almighty, and in every way sufficient to destroy the wall of this enmity, and to establish without any obstacle the eternal covenant of reconciliation and of true peace. And because he could not be seen through the Godhead, he came through the humanity that he might become visible; and at his birth the whole world met him with a sign of temporal peace, announcing and proclaiming the token of that highest and eternal peace which was to be established through him. Whence also at his nativity the angels cried: *Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men [1256C] of good will [n: (Luc. II)]*.

Who then is more and truly peaceable than this so great eternal maker of so great a peace? Solomon indeed, who was called from his name *peaceable*, for Solomon is interpreted *peaceable*, had temporal peace, a temporal king, by God's granting, so that he might build a house to God with no one hindering; but by his name, and his work, and his time he went before this true peaceable one, as night goes before day, the shadow before the truth; who, that he might found for himself a house, namely holy Church, laid as it were in the foundation the sign of this wonderful peace, which is to be kept, *Peace,* saying, *I leave with you, my peace I give unto you [n: (Joan. XIV)]*.

To this *peaceable one there was a vineyard,* namely holy Church, which is therefore not unfittingly called a *vineyard,* because, [1256D] if the cultivation or the nature of the vine is attended to, she seems to draw to herself no small profit for her own progress from its likeness. For what indeed is the manner of cultivation that is applied to the vine? In the time of pruning, the useless shoots being cut back, it is as it were cleansed; then, dug about, it is raised up and tied to stakes: further, if thou attend to the quality of its nature, thou wilt see that first it fixes a living root, then, because it is pliant and apt to fall, with its tendrils, as with certain arms, it binds whatever it lays hold of, and by these it raises and lifts itself up. What then is the pruning in the Church or in any soul whatever, but the cutting away of vices from the hearts? and what the digging about, but the bringing back to the mind [1257A] of sins through compunction and confession? For as with a kind of hoe of compunction the mass of earthly works is brought back to memory, so that the mind, unburdened through confession and satisfaction, may be able to bear fruit better in good acts. And what does the minister of this office do who receives the penitent, and imposes on him as he confesses the measure of penance? He raises him with the hand of sure consolation and with the hope of the divine mercy, and sets before him, through the examples of the saints, what *265* purpose of life he should hold henceforth, by which he ought to support himself, as if tied to stakes, lest he fall.

Further, according to the nature of the vine the progress of the Church or of any soul whatever is considered, because after it has been planted and fixed in the root of faith, the layering of humility being pressed down, lest it could be bent back or drawn away by any storms or tempests [1257B] of the world, it embraces all its neighbours with the embraces of charity as with tendrils and coils, and rests in their compunction.

Such, then, *was the vineyard* to the true *peaceable one,* our Lord Jesus Christ; not that it is not so now, or ought not to be in the future, but we ought to take *was* as said in his foreknowledge and predestination, as it is written elsewhere of every creature: *What was made in him was life [n: (Joan. I)]*, because in his foreknowledge all things already were and lived, just as in the mind of some craftsman there is the form or pattern of the thing to be made, whose likeness the hands of the worker imitate, so that he may bring the thing forth into the open.

The *vineyard,* moreover, *was in that* (understood: peace) *which [1257C] hath peoples,* because the Church has been gathered not from the one nation of the Jews, but from the peoples of all nations, into the unity of faith, love and peace, as it is written: *They had one heart and one soul [n: (Act. IV)]*. Further, it is to be noted that he does not say, *in that* peace which the peoples have, but *which hath peoples,* so that the grace of him who calls, justifies, glorifies may be the more attended to than the labour and zeal of him who wills, runs, strives to lay hold, because, as the Apostle says: *It is not of him that willeth nor of him that runneth, but of God that sheweth mercy [n: (Rom. IX)]*. *For he is our peace who hath made both one [n: (Ephes. II)]*, and from it is *the peace which surpasseth all understanding [n: (Phil. IV)]*. When therefore this peace *hath peoples,* no schism, or any dissension, can break into the unity of their faith, but they cleave so firmly [1257D] in that peace that they become a most choice *vineyard* to the true *peaceable one,* our Lord Jesus Christ: for if they themselves had peace without the peace that has them, they would not be held by a firm root, because they would not be planted together in that peace in which in the selfsame they might sleep and rest [n: (Psal. IV)]. Which sleep and rest is given after this life in the selfsame, *because we shall see him as he is [n: (I Joan. III)]*, and *there is no* change there, *nor shadow [sic: obrumbratio] of alteration [n: (Jac. I)]*, but the peace which we shall once have received we shall keep for ever without any change.

But these *peoples* whom this peace *hath,* although in the first place we ought to take them as the universal Church gathered from Jews and Gentiles, yet in the second [1258A] place we can not unfittingly take them as the particular Church established within the universal itself through the diverse kinds *266* of professions, which also can be called the *vineyard* of the Lord, and ought to be bound together by the same peace and unanimity. Further, if we descend to individuals, some faithful soul serving God can not absurdly be called the *vineyard* of the Lord, whose cultivator is the Holy Spirit through grace, since according to the form set down above, which we said befits the whole, it seems not unfittingly to suit the single one also, namely that this be its zeal: to cut away vices and sins like useless shoots with the hand of pious work through co-operating grace, to bring earthly works back to the mind through compunction and confession, as through a digging about, [1258B] and so, by doing penance and doing good works, to offer fruit acceptable to God.

*He let it out to keepers:* the keepers of this vineyard, that is, of holy Church, were the holy apostles or their successors, apostolic men, whose zeal and diligence it was to instruct her in holiness and justice, and to defend her by word and example from the plague of evil works and of depraved doctrines; not that whatever progress they attained in this keeping was of their own work, but because they were ministers and officers of the divine operation and efficacy, as it is said by the Lord in Numbers to Aaron: *Put ye my name upon the children of Israel, I the Lord will bless them [n: (Num. VI)]* [var: Vulg. Nm 6:27 *Invocabuntque nomen meum super filios Israel, et ego benedicam eis*, "And they shall invoke my name upon the children of Israel, and I will bless them"] : as if he said in other words: Do you, through the ministry delivered to you, pour my grace [1258C] into men by word and work; it will be mine to show the good of that grace in them and to bring it to effect.

Now the word of delivering which is uttered in this pronouncement is to be attended to, not without fear, by all the prelates of the Churches and by their subjects, because it forewarns both those to the virtue of prudence and these to the virtue of obedience, to be wary and careful. For the Apostle speaks to prelates: *Take heed to yourselves and to the whole flock, wherein the Holy Spirit hath placed you bishops, to rule the Church of God, which he hath purchased with his own blood [n: (Act. XX)]*; further, to subjects: *Obey,* he says, *your prelates and be subject to them. For they watch as being to render an account for your souls, that they may do this with joy and not with grief, [1258D] for this is not expedient for you [n: (Hebr. XIII)]*. Therefore it ought to make you, that is, the prelates, attentive with solicitude, providence, and these, that is, the subjects, with humility, patience, on account of the word of this delivering, in which it is said: *He let it out to keepers.*

If then God has delivered his Church to keepers, it is greatly to be feared lest, if the keepers neglect the keeping, and those to be kept despise the keepers, or resist and disparage the keeping, they gravely offend him who placed the keepers and established the keeping. These keepers indeed God calls watchmen, as he himself speaks by the prophet: *I have made thee a watchman to the house of Israel; the things which thou seest, thou shalt announce to them from me* [1259A] [n: (Ezech. III)]; *267* *the things which thou seest,* he says, *thou shalt announce to them from me,* that is, what thou understandest with the mental eye, or beholdest with the bodily eye, as needing to be corrected or amended, *thou shalt announce to them,* by exhortation, counsel and precept, *from me,* that is, according to the reason and manner which I shall have shown thee beforehand by inspiring. And in the delivering of this keeping God set before the keepers themselves, or watchmen, the danger, and the manner of escaping the danger, saying: *If, when I say to the wicked: Thou shalt die the death, and thou declare it not to him, he indeed shall die in his iniquity, but his blood I will require at thy hand,* behold the danger. But the manner of escaping this danger follows in what he says: *But if thou declare it to the wicked, that he may be converted from his iniquity, and he hear thee not, he indeed shall die in [1259B] his iniquity, but thou hast delivered thy soul (ibid)*. To the keepers therefore, that is, to the prelates in the Church, danger is set before them for negligence, security for vigilance, whence greater care and solicitude ought to hold them toward the office entrusted to them.

Further, the danger of private persons or subjects is not less, nay it is graver, for contempt and disobedience; because while prelates mostly sin from infirmity and negligence, these offend from pride and rashness, and their contempt and irreverence is the contempt or irreverence of God. So he himself says: *He that heareth you heareth me, and he that despiseth you despiseth me [n: (Luc. X)]*; and to the prophet, whom he says he gave as a watchman to the house [1259C] of Israel: *Therefore* he says, *they do not hear thee, because it is an exasperating house [n: (Ezech. III)]*. An evil and fearful appellation, that the house of the undisciplined and disobedient should be named *an exasperating house*, and in another place they are called *of stiff neck and uncircumcised heart*, because they resist the Holy Spirit [n: (Act. VII)]. For they who resist the word of discipline and honourableness resist the word of God. *The words,* moreover, *of God are spirit and life [n: (Joan. VI)]*, and therefore they resist the Holy Spirit. Whence the Prophet, because he saw that to resist the word of God is damnable, asked to be taught goodness and discipline by God, because he had believed his commandments and been obedient [n: (Psal. CXVIII)].
