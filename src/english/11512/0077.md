## BOOK FOUR. (cont.)

[1266D]On the contrary, it designates the widowhood and at the same time the corruption of that one, that her price neither remained in unity nor came to two, when it is said: *I bought her to me for fifteen pieces of silver and a core of barley and half a core of barley.* If thou attend to the pieces of silver, there are neither ten pieces of silver, which would be the tenfold unity, nor twenty, which would be the tenfold two, but there are *fifteen,* in which number, namely, it is more than unity, less than two: likewise there is neither one core of barley only, nor are there two cores, but a core and a half of barley, more than the monad, less than two. And so this too is most unhappy for her, that she is neither a virgin nor has a husband. Therefore *a thousand are thy peaceable* pieces of silver, *276* that is, all the discourses harmonizing in one faith; for this number is a thousandfold [1267A] unity, and they are thine, making or acquiring peace, that is, the remission of sins; since to have one Lord, one faith, one baptism is to be in the catholic Church, where the remission of sins is given through peace, that is, Christ, *who is our peace, who hath made both one [n: (Ephes. II)]*. For Holy Scripture, which sounds forth and teaches these three things, contains in itself the whole sum of this peace, because whatever there is of law, of God, of faith, of the religion of Christianity, and all the sacraments of the Church are constituted from these, and he who has them as he ought to have them attains that peace which feels the wars of the vices; and when by this peace he has advanced to an abundance of virtues, the remission of sins having been given him, he will reach that perpetual and never-to-be-ended peace, where there will be no labour, no pain, [1267B] nothing at all which could oppose him.

These three, therefore, are the instruments of this peace, since every soul tending toward it must always consider in whom it believes, what it believes, how it believes. For if thou ask whom, or in whom, thou oughtest to believe, God is set before thee; but if thou askest what to believe, the sacraments of the Church are shown thee; further, if thou ask how thou oughtest to believe, the religion of Christianity is prescribed to thee. *For faith without works* is idle, nay, *is dead [n: (Jac. II)]*, and: *Without faith it is impossible to please God [n: (Hebr. XI)]*. Accordingly, as we have said, on account of the unity of these three things, which constitute and acquire the true and eternal peace, there may rightly indeed be said to be for this vineyard a *thousand* who are *peaceable.*

[1267C]But *two hundred for them that keep the fruits thereof,* why are they said to be? First indeed it is to be seen who are these *keepers of the fruit,* then what this number, the hundredfold two, given to them, means, of which we spoke a little above. As keepers of this *fruit* we can name in the first place those who preside in the Church of God by word and work, and who, lest this fruit be snatched from the minds of the faithful by heretics and other perverse men, strive to withstand with tongue and hand, that is, with doctrine and working, never to be barren, but always to beget an offspring of virtues in themselves and in others. But in the second place all Christians generally ought to be keepers of this *fruit*, because the Christian religion requires this of them, that they lose not eternal life by living evilly, but, being always one Spirit with God, [1267D] be heaped from this spiritual conjunction with the fruitfulness of good works, and so at length in a virginal wedlock, in the heavenly nuptials which the hundredfold two here signifies, may enjoy God for ever. The dowry of which wedlock will be *277* the double stole, namely the beatitude of body and soul, which the hundredfold two can also signify, because the hundred, too, on the right hand, expresses the crown of the same beatitude.

VERSE 13. *Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the friends hearken: make me hear thy voice.*

After many and diverse colloquies of the beloved and his beloved, beautifully and laudably uttered, the same [1268A] beloved, laying down as it were the sum and final voice of this epithalamium, as though he now instructs [cj: *erudit*; read *eruditae*, "to one now instructed"], and proclaims to her fully taught out of the fellowship of his discourse, and announces that she should come forth as an example to others in her form and beauty which she has taken on from this instruction. *Thou that dwellest,* he says, *in the gardens.* What are the gardens, if not the pleasantness of the Scriptures or the delight of the virtues? for nothing flowers more delightfully or smells more sweetly than the region about the streams and flowing waters or shoots of the Holy Scriptures, and the beautiful, delightful and wholesome variety of the virtues. For on both sides the discipline is laudable and heavenly, here in speaking, there in doing. But what is it to dwell in these *gardens*, if not to rest in frequent meditation of the holy law and exercise of the virtues? For he whose will is in the law of the Lord, and who meditates on his law day and night, dwells simply at home with Jacob [n: (Gen. XXV)], that [1268B] is, abides in the tranquillity of his mind with secure stability, admitting nothing varied, nothing changeable into the sameness of faith or morals. But on the contrary he does not dwell in these gardens who, intent with Esau on the hunting of worldly things [n: (Gen. XXV)], wanders about the din of the world through the woods of thoughts, keeping neither the unity of faith nor the integrity of morals.

Therefore, O beloved, *thou that dwellest in* these *gardens* simply and wholesomely, *make me hear thy voice.* What voice? The voice of pious discourse, the voice of virtue, the voice of one teaching well and rightly, the voice of one living justly and piously. For as true and right preaching is a great voice, so also a pious and holy way of life is a great voice. Humility is a great voice, obedience is a great voice, chastity [1268C] is a great voice, patience is a great voice, charity is a great and resounding voice; and with as many virtues as the holy soul shines, with so many great voices, as it were, it cries to God.

And why do I wish *to hear thy voice*? Namely that I may hear thee crying out by word and work, as it is written: *The children that shall be born and shall rise up, let them hear and tell it to their children, that they may put their hope in God and not forget the works of God, and may seek his commandments [n: (Psal. LXXVII)]* [var: Vulg. *et narrabunt filiis suis*, "and shall declare to their children", without *audiant*]. And what is my *hearing*? Surely to approve, to accept, and to judge worthy of my help, *278* for when I hear I approve, when I hearken I help. But do thou *make me hear thy voice.* How wilt thou do it? By living honourably and piously, by teaching true and pious things. For reason and understanding, [1268D] memory and wit I have granted thee; that thou mayest be inexcusable, and not say that thou neither knowest nor art able, I have taken away from thee every necessity of impossibility, so that thou mayest have and do as is due: but I wish to be a debtor to thee, so that when thou hast done what thou oughtest by right to do, I may be overcome when I am judged [n: (Psal. L)]; by thy judgment, I say, I wish to be overcome, because I am greatly delighted if thou usest my gift well, and therefore it is thine to bring it about that I wish *to hear thy voice:* and yet thy doing is not thine, but mine, because that thou art willing and able is the gift of my grace.

*Make me,* he says, *hear thy voice.* Why? Because *the friends hearken:* whose friends? Both mine and thine: mine through charity, because they love me by keeping [1269A] my word; thine, because they love thee by wishing thee salvation. For there are friends whom thou seest not with bodily eyes, who rejoice over thy progress and salvation, or over thy conversion, of whom it is written that: *There is joy to the angels of God upon one sinner doing penance [n: (Luc. XV)]*. *These indeed are ministering spirits in all the earth for them who receive the inheritance of salvation [n: (Hebr. I)]* [var: Vulg. *in ministerium missi*, "sent to minister", for *in omnem terram*]: for these are solicitous and act for the salvation of men, as it is said by one of these invisible friends to one of the visible friends, that *at that time shall Michael rise up, who standeth for the children of thy people [n: (Dan. XII)]*, and presently, *and none is my helper in all these things but Michael your prince [n: (Dan. X)]*.

Therefore these *friends hearken,* because they are greatly delighted [1269B] *to hear thy voice* crying to me, whether by praying, or by psalmody, or by preaching, or by wishing. There are also other friends, whom thou hast before thine eyes, who are likewise delighted to hear such a fourfold symphony of concords, like a beautiful διατέσσαρον, and therefore they give ear and hearken, and long to hear thee singing and saying: *But I, like an olive tree, have borne fruit in the house of the Lord, I have hoped in the mercy of my God, and I will wait for thy name before the face of thy saints [n: (Psal. LI)]* [var: Vulg. *sicut oliva fructifera in domo Dei*, "like a fruitful olive tree in the house of God"]. These, I say, holy and such friends, hearken, in the optative and desiderative mood, that thou mayest become such as they themselves are, as one of them wished to become *anathema* for his brethren [n: (Rom. IX)], whose salvation he desired; and because [1269C] he himself was already dwelling in the gardens of the virtues, of wisdom also and of knowledge, he was solicitous so to temper his voice in my ears that he might please both God and men, and might show by life and doctrine what befitted the salvation of men. Whence this voice of his is sounding from the gardens of wisdom and virtue, and audible to me: *I chastise my body and bring it into servitude,* I subject it, *lest perhaps, while I have preached to others, I myself become a castaway [n: (I Cor. IX)]*. In this *279* way, while the flesh is so subjected to the spirit that it desires nothing below itself, seeks only what is above itself, this is the voice which sounds delightfully in my ears; nay, this is the *Canticle of canticles*, than which mortal and passible man can sing nothing sweeter in this mortal life; by which voice and by which canticle he is ever caught up [1269D] to take hold of that ineffable sweetness which *eye hath not seen, nor ear heard [n: (I Cor. II)]*, the attaining of which will make man immortal and impassible. While the angels and all my saints hearken to this voice and this canticle and desire to hear it, they, the ones (that is, the angels) having attained, and the others (that is, men) holy through faith, hope and charity not doubting that they will attain, the same glory, long greatly that thou also, who hast not yet attained, mayest attain it with them, and for this they unceasingly make supplication to me. What, at length, does the beloved answer to this voice? *Flee away, O my beloved,* etc.

VERSE 14.-- *Flee away, O my beloved, and be like to the roe, and to the young hart upon the mountains of spices.*

If we look attentively, great is this voice of one humble [1270A] of heart, whether we refer it to the whole Church, or to any soul devoted to God: for why should not holy Church, or any holy soul, use this voice toward God in the present life? For one of the saints says: *We know in part and we prophesy in part; but when that which is perfect is come, that which is in part shall be done away [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*. What did he perceive, who pronounced the imperfection of knowing and prophesying in this present life? for that which is in part is not perfect; but he says this imperfection is to be done away when in the future life that which is perfect shall come; and such imperfection, if thou consider rightly, is surely the flight of this beloved.
