## BOOK FOUR. (cont.)

Further, whatever is done in the sacraments of the Church [1270B] contains great and profound mysteries, all of which proclaim the power, virtue and grace of this beloved; but who is there who is able to dispute of these to the full? whence the beloved, astonished before such greatness, knows in part and prophesies in part, but because she is not able to comprehend to the full, she fails, and cries out to the beloved to flee, not from neglect but from failing: for "flee", in the discussion of the subtle matters of the sacraments, is the flight of this beloved. For when we cannot fully set forth or discuss their force and efficacy, this beloved flees us: likewise when we direct our gaze on the face of sacred Scripture, but cannot see it fully through and enter the secrets of its treasures, assuredly [1270C] this beloved flees us.

*280* But let us not rashly hold him back as he flees; rather, whatever we cannot comprehend in a sacrament or in the subtlety of the holy Scriptures, let us burn with fire, that is, reserve to the Holy Spirit, and lest by discussing too much we fall into a heretical sense, let us suffer the beloved to flee. For according to the example of blessed Jacob, who wrestled with the angel, we indeed are admonished to wrestle with the word of God, but after, the blessing having been given, we are lamed by the touch of the sinew, let us permit the word of God to flee. For what is it to wrestle with the word of God, if not untiringly to search out the sense of sacred Scripture? What, then, is it for the victor to ask and receive a blessing from the vanquished, if not to seek and [1270D] find the understanding of that sense? But the victor, the blessing received from the vanquished, limps with his thigh withering, because he who by God's inspiring has understood sacred Scripture well, every pleasure of the flesh being quenched, advances in the active and contemplative life, and conquers with great gain indeed to the spirit, but with great loss to his flesh. Lastly, he limps who conquered, because he who was conquered touched the sinew of his thigh, and it withered; how does he limp? Namely, resting one foot on the earth, holding the other up from the earth, that is, not wholly abandoning the active life, for the care of those subject to him, and cleaving with all his strength to the contemplative for its sweetness.

This lame man leaps or ascends like a hart, and scarcely deigns to touch the earth, for *his conversation is in heaven [n: (Philip. III)]*: yet *in part,* for [1271A] with one foot only is he now meanwhile suspended there, until *that which is perfect* shall come. And therefore, because now he cannot comprehend perfectly, he rightly says: *Flee away, O my beloved.* When therefore, speaking with the Lord on the mountain, we perceive with the mind the sense of the word of God, let us attend to that saying of the Apostle: *Be not highminded, but fear [n: (Rom. XI)]*, lest while we incautiously pursue the fleeing beloved we slip into the pit of perverse dogma. Again, we say in this manner that the beloved flees, because he is always incomprehensible; for this is to be granted to his majesty, that he cannot be comprehended, and that he is always desired and desirable, and that it is hidden from us, as long as we are in this body, *how great* is *the multitude of his sweetness, which he hath hidden for them that fear him [n: (Psal. XXX)]*.

[1271B]Let us therefore say to him: *Flee, flee,* being cautious and provident for ourselves, lest ever while we live we think that we have comprehended, following that we may comprehend. Or let any faithful and wise soul say in this manner to God with a humble and devoted mind: *Lord, I am not* worthy *that thou shouldst enter under my roof [n: (Matth. VIII)]*, I am not worthy that thou shouldst show in me some frequent miracle of thy presence or visitation. *Flee away,* she says, *O my beloved,* I seek not glory in the present life, but I desire grace; I seek not the brightness of miracles, but I long for the remission of sins. *Flee away,* I say, *O my beloved,* and *281* make such leaps like the leaps *of the roe* or *of the young hart,* not upon me, but *upon the mountains of spices,* upon the lofty merits of the saints and of the perfect. It is not mine to treat or dispute of [1271C] the secret of thy divinity, of the highest teachings of thy precepts, of the deep mysteries of thy sacraments, but rather of those who can be called lofty mountains for the eminence of their wisdom and virtues, who, touched by thy Holy Spirit, send forth as smoke thy good odour, so that they can say with the Apostle: *We are the good odour of Christ [1272A] unto God in every place [n: (II Cor. II)]* [var: Vulg. *Deo in iis qui salvi fiunt*, "unto God in them that are saved"], and therefore they are rightly called *mountains of spices.*

And what wonder if I, conscious of my infirmity, say to thee to flee, when the great and learned mountains themselves are astonished in the consideration of thy very works, or precepts, or even sacraments, and sometimes suffer thee to flee? For one of these mountains, who had been stretched up even to the third heaven, and had heard *things which it is not granted to man to utter*, [n: (II Cor. XII)], fearing to pursue thee, the beloved, as thou fleest, *O the depth,* he says, *of the riches of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God! how incomprehensible are his judgments, and how unsearchable his ways! For who hath known the mind of the Lord, or who hath been his counsellor? or who hath first given to him, and recompense shall be made him? for of him and [1272B] by him and in him are all things; to him* be honour and *glory for ever and ever [n: (Rom. XI)]*.

Therefore, *O my beloved, flee away,* not that thou mayest forsake me wholly, but that what I cannot attain here, since thou art incomprehensible, thou mayest grant me in the life to come, in true and eternal beatitude without end. Be like *to the roe, and to the young hart upon the mountains of spices.* That is, transcend even the understanding of the perfect saints by the incomprehensible knowledge of thy divinity; or make such leaps in their hearts that they may advance *from virtue to virtue* [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)], because as often as the elect advance from good things to better, from virtues to virtue, God makes as it were certain leaps in their minds, so that by these steps or leaps little by little they may come to that highest and perfect [1272C] state *in which the God of gods is seen in Sion [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)]*, where they may unceasingly say and sing in the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth, not simply a canticle, but the Canticle of canticles, that is, to know and to perceive that highest and best good for ever and ever. Amen.
