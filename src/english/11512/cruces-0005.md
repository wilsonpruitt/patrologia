## Standing renderings (for merge-time reconciliation with T01/T03)

- *ubera* = breasts · *osculum* = kiss (*redemptorium / reconciliatorium / contemplatorium* = redeeming / reconciling / contemplative) · *charitas* = charity · *dilectio* = love · *dilectus/dilecta* = beloved · *Sponsus/sponsa* = Bridegroom/bride.
- *adolescentulae* = young maidens. At 1028B the gloss turns on the diminutive (*non adolescens, sed per diminutionem adolescentula*), so both words stand in italic Latin there with a short English gloss.
- Ct 1:3 *in odorem* = "to the odour"; Wolbero's *in odore* (1030C) = "in the odour", kept distinct because his gloss turns on it.
- *incrementum naturale / affectuale* = natural / affectual growth · *vita theoretica*, *vir theoricus* = theoretic life, theoric man.
- Aristotle's four species of quality (1033C–1034C): *habitus/dispositio* = habit/disposition; *potentia/impotentia* = power/impotence; *passibilis qualitas/passio* = passible quality/passion; *figura/forma* = figure/form.
- The Cologne page numbers (`* 2*`, `*3,*` … `*20*`) are carried verbatim at their places.
- Thou/you by number (Pattern 17). The bride's address to the Bridegroom is *tu*, so it takes thou. *putatis* (1026A) takes you.

## Conjectures glossed with `[cj:]` (6)

In each case the printed word is real and the faithful English says something the author argues against.

- **1023B** *ex uno istorum verberum tuorum profluat remissio peccatorum*. *verberum* means "lashes", while the whole passage contrasts the Bridegroom's inviting *ubera* with the law's wine that *terret, avertit et exasperat*. Glossed with *uberum*, "breasts".
- **1026D** *magis fovens dementia quam feriens sententia*. *dementia* means "madness" and is plate-clear. The sentence praises the Bridegroom's *lenitas* over *asperitas*. Glossed with *clementia*.
- **1029A** *imitando etiam malam voluntatem sibi … jungit*: God "imitating" the evil will. The sense is changing it (compare *dum de malo mutat in bonum, voluntatem transferat* at 1029C). Glossed with *immutando*.
- **1029A** *foris per opertum opus nostrum internus opifex innotescit*: "outwardly through our *covered* work" is the opposite of the *foris … innotescit* the sentence builds. Glossed with *apertum*.
- **1033C** *dispositio est habitus facile nobilis*. The contrast with *habitus … diutissime permanens* needs *mobilis* ("easily changed", the Aristotelian definition). Glossed.
- **1033C** *quod non possit in aliud quodlibet leviter non flecti*. The printed double negative asserts the opposite of the definition of *impotentia* (Pattern 18: negation class, the 8195 @1250B precedent). Glossed on *non flecti*, read *flecti*.

- **1017D** *sibipo rrigi*: the plate splits the word, and our TEI reads *sibi porrigi*. TEI patch filed. - **1018** Our twin prints `*3,* Unde`, and the plate prints `3. Unde`. Patch filed. The Cologne marker is carried as our twin has it.
- **1020D** *debriata*: *debriare* is attested medieval Latin (= *inebriare*). Pattern 9, rendered "inebriated".
- **1021A** *et natura Dei est omnibus superferri liquoribus* (plate confirmed): "it is the nature of God to be borne above all liquids". Wolbero's oil lore (compare 1025D *Oleum quippe omnibus superfertur liquoribus*, and 1032A) suggests *olei*. But the ἔλεος/*misericordia* play makes *Dei* intelligible, so there is no marker. Also 1021A *exivit* has no expressed subject, and the English supplies "[man]".
- **1023A** *osculo dilectionis illi jugiter adhaereant, donec … conjungar*: the plate has the 3rd plural inside a 1st-singular sentence. Rendered "they may cleave".
- **1023B** *invitant, animant et confortantur*: a passive among actives. Rendered "and are strengthened".
- **1023B–C** *magnitudo pietatis suae* ("his", in a passage addressed to "thee") and *id quod posterius est … et quod prius est … praecurrere facit*. As printed, both the later and the earlier "run before". Probably *quam quod prius est* was meant. Rendered as printed.
- **1023D** *Nonne et hoc fuit impleri Spiritu sancto.*: the question closes with a period on the plate, and the period is kept (Pattern 8).
- **1024B** *pro sui fecunditata*: the plate's last letter is smudged at the column edge and may be *e*. - **1025A** *austeritate regalium caeremoniarum* (plate confirmed): "royal ceremonies", where the context (*legalis sententiae* 1019B) suggests *legalium*. There is no false assertion, so no marker.
- **1026B** *et propter nimiam charitatem suam qua dilexit nos Deus (Ephes. II).* is a verbless member closed by a full stop, then *Te Filium suum misit …* This is kept as printed.
- **1027D** *Cujus hominis partes constitutivas intra enumerans* has *intra*, perhaps for *infra*. Rendered literally ("within").
- **1027D** *(ibid.):* the plate ends the paragraph with a colon, and our twin has a period. Patch filed; the English follows the plate.
- **1028A** *ventilans adversarios*: the paragraph has no closing stop on the plate, and none is supplied. The same holds for **1033D** *applicandi*.
- **1030C** *munus est, quae … promisisti*: a plural relative after *munus*. Rendered "the gift, the things which thou hast promised".
- **1031A** *semel divini muneris mercede privat* has no object; the English supplies "[himself]". On the plate a stray raised sort follows *(Matth. XXIV; Luc. XVII).*, which is ignored and not in our twin.
- **1031D** Augustine (In Ep. Jo. tr. 8) is printed *sed quae radix est intra radicata est cupiditas*. Rendered word for word without supplying punctuation: "but which root is within rooted is cupidity". Augustine's text is usually given as *quae radix est in terra. Radicata est cupiditas?* That is from recollection and unverified here, so it is a note only.
- **1032D** *quorum pedes, pedes recti duabus pennis (Ezech. I) geminae charitatis … et sublevantur et corpora sua, id est opera minuunt* is plate-confirmed and barely construes. *minuunt* ("diminish") perhaps stands for *muniunt* or *tegunt* (Ez 1:11 *duae tegebant corpora eorum*). Rendered literally.
- **1033C** *passiva vero qualitas*, beside *passibilis* elsewhere: "passive quality", kept distinct.
- **1034A** *cui gratiae assentire et allaborare non habet non posse* is a deliberate double negative that construes: no one lacks the ability. Rendered "does not have the inability to assent", with no marker.
