## Column notes

- **1089B** — *duo extremi referuntur ad spiritum timoris Domini*: "the two last" (initial and filial), not "the two extremes". As printed.
- **1089C** — our Latin **Mundamus** *est cessare a bono*; plate p. 551 clearly **Mundanus** (paired with *servilis* in the sentence before). English "Worldly fear" follows the plate. TEI patch.
- **1089C** — *ubi timor et spiritus se invicem comitantur, sicut legitur, timor est sine spe*: plate confirms *spiritus* and *sine spe*. Rendered literally ("fear and the spirit accompany one another, as it is read, fear is without hope"). The sense wants something like *timor et spes … timor non est sine spe*; not repaired, no marker (the words construe; the logic is the difficulty).
- **1090A** — *utile, honestum judicans*: asyndeton as printed, carried.
- **1090A** — *Tu, inquit, o dilecte*: the vocative *o* is blacked out on scan A; scan B p. 549 prints it, italic, as our Latin. The speaker is the bride, so *inquit* = "she says" here (the Bridegroom speaks at 1093C, *o dilecta*, "he says").
- **1090C** — *dicendum quomodo et caput et ad capitis membra appetetur*: plate confirms **appetetur** (future passive of *appeto*, "will be sought"). Rendered literally. Probably *aptetur* ("is applied"): the same idea is expressed by *aptatur* at 1091B. Real word, no marker.
- **1090D** — *quia et ad caput et ad pietatis membra diximus referendum*: plate confirms **pietatis** where the argument (and 1090C twice) has *capitis*. Rendered "the members of piety". Crux only.
- **1091B** — *tanto majorem habet requiem in divinitatis contemplatione quasi in uno lectulo sunt. Deus per gratiam …*: the full stop and the plural *sunt* are on the plate. Carried; the following verbless series (*Deus … inspirans … homo … perficiens*) is rendered verbless.
- **1091C** — *participabilis et participans hoc est caput membrum*: no *et* between *caput* and *membrum* on the plate; English "head, member" supplies none.
- **1091D** — *inhabitantis proportionis gratiae tuae donationes*: plate confirms *proportionis*. Rendered "the gifts of the proportion of thy grace dwelling in the mind". Crux only.
- **1092A** — VERS. 16 prints *Tigna domorum nostrarum cedrina laquearia nostra cypressina.* with **no mark** between *cedrina* and *laquearia* (plate p. 552 and our Latin). The English carries none (Pattern 8), so it reads "are of cedar our panelled ceilings of cypress". The re-quotation below (1092A) has the comma.
- **1093A** — our Latin *ut manu artificis* **runc natum** *decorum praestet aspectum*; plate p. 553 prints **runc'natum**: between *runc* and *natum* stands the upper stroke of a broken *i* sort, at the width of one letter, not a word space. Scan B p. 551 shows the identical fragment (same printing). The word is *runcinatum*, "planed" (*runcina*, a plane). English "planed" follows the plate. TEI patch (our space for Migne's damaged *i*). No `[sic:]`: the letter is on the plate.
- **1093B** — *quorum omnia vita, eruditione, institutione, auctoritate … confirmatur*: plate confirms *omnia*. Taken as an accusative of respect ("in all things") and so rendered; no marker.
- **1093C** — *hunc gloriosum … statum, ad illius *84* constitere nutum … fatetur*: plate confirms **constitere** (a perfect 3rd plural in form, where the construction after *fatetur* wants an infinitive, *constitisse*). Rendered "that this glorious state … has stood at the nod". Crux only.
- **1094A** — *Alter quoque, o dilecta, ego sum flos campi, ille nimirum de quo Isaias dicit*: plate confirms **Alter**. It construes as "another (flower) too … that one of whom Isaiah says" and is rendered so ("Another too, O beloved, *I am the flower of the field,* that one, namely, of whom Isaiah says"). *Aliter* is possible; no marker.
- **1094A** — *multi alii procedunt flores, id est gratia spiritualis quos*: singular *gratia spiritualis* between plural *flores* and *quos*, as printed. Rendered "that is, spiritual grace, which".
- **1094B** — *quia non scabrosa, neque tortuosa,* **vicus**, *sed plana et aequalis*: plate p. 553 confirms *vicus* ("a village") between commas. It does not construe; rendered literally ("because it is not rough, nor tortuous, a village, but level and even"). Not repaired; perhaps a corruption of *situs* or of a third adjective.
- **1094C** — *nam gloriantes … se continentes* **profectum** *non habent lilium candoris virginei, et interioris* **munditia** *signum*: plate confirms both. Rendered literally: "have not progress, the lily of virginal whiteness, and, by inward purity, the sign". Probably *profecto non habent lilium, candoris virginei et interioris munditiae signum* ("they certainly have not the lily, the sign of virginal whiteness and inward purity"). Crux only.
- **1094D** — *ita tu, o Ecclesia mea, sponsa mea, sive quaevis me diligens anima esse* **debetis** *…, ut cum me hoc modo fueris imitata, vere mea proberis amica*: the plural *debetis* after the singular *tu*, then singular *fueris … proberis*. Pattern 17 renders the number as printed: "so thou … you ought to be … when thou hast imitated me … thou mayest be proved".

As at 0023, Wolbero's gloss turns *amica* into *amicitia* (*verae amicitiae probatio*, 1095A *Dei amicitia*); the English keeps "friendship" for *amicitia* and "a friend to me" for the predicative *mihi amica* (1095A), which is not the lemma.

- **1095B** — *ut dicit B. Gregorius, quisquis malos non tolerat, ipse* **tibi** *per intolerantiam testis est*: plate confirms *tibi*. Gregory (*Hom. in Ezech.* I.9) has *ipse sibi … testis est*, "is witness to himself". Rendered "to thee" as printed; crux only (not a negation or correlative).

- **1096C** — *gratia vero talem aestum umbra misericordiae et remedio poenitentiae* **movit**: plate confirms *movit*. Rendered "moved". Probably *removit* or *amovit* ("removed"); crux only.

- **1097A** — plate p. 555 prints *perfecta corporis et* **ani mae** *meae refectio*, *anima* split with a full word space. Scan B p. 553 is identical. Our Latin agrees. Split run carried whole, `[sic: *ani mae*]`, sense "soul" rendered beside it.
- **1097B** — *Sub cujus umbra* **seu** *Ecclesia, quia … illius rami per totam mundi latitudinem* **diffusis**, *mirabiliter protegitur*: plate confirms *seu* ("or") and the case clash *rami … diffusis* (nominative noun, ablative participle). Rendered literally ("*Under whose shade* or the Church, because, the branches of that tree … being spread, she is wonderfully protected"). *seu* is probably *sedet* ("sits"), which Ct 2:3 *sedi* and 1096C–D would support; no marker (the sentence is broken, not false).

- **1098A** — *sicut vinum … suo naturali* **colore** *omnem noxium excoquit humorem*: plate confirms *colore* ("colour"), where *excoquit* ("boils out") wants *calore* ("heat"). Rendered "by its natural colour". Crux only.
- **1098B** — *Haec est ergo cella vinaria perfectio, et angustam viam libere intrantibus, suaviter sapiens et ob hoc perfectibilis divini praecepti intelligentiae de charitatis excellentia.* Plate confirms every word. It does not construe (*intelligentiae* genitive or dative with no governor; *sapiens … perfectibilis* with no noun). Rendered as closely as the words allow, not repaired.
- **1099C** — *ut primo toto corde, deinde tota anima, postremo tota virtute Deus diligatur credendo, tota anima confitendo, tota virtute operando*: *toto corde* is not repeated before *credendo*. The English does not supply it.

- **1100C** — second quotation of Ct 2:5, plate p. 556 *Fulcite me* **loribus**: the first sort of the word is a damaged, hooked glyph, not an *f* (compare *floribus* at 1100A, clean). Scan B p. 554 shows the same damaged sort (same printing). Our Latin reads *loribus*. `[sic: loribus]` (inside the italic lemma), sense "flowers" rendered beside it. It may be a broken *fl* ligature; a wrong sort cannot be excluded.
- **1100D** — *Cupio disso'vi*: plate prints *dissolvi* with the *l* broken (fragment visible); our Latin *dissolvi*. No marker.
- **1100D** — ⚑ *Quorum profectum videre pro magna interim reputat consolatione, quando Dei faciem quam tantopere desiderat videre, perfecte* **praevalet** *comprehendere.* Plate p. 556 confirms: **no *non***. The literal English says that Paul "is perfectly able to comprehend the face of God", which is what the whole passage denies: 1100B *videre sicuti est* **non praevalet**, and the *dilatio* of the reward (1100D, 1101A). Carried literally, with `[cj: *praevalet*; read *non praevalet*, "is not able"]` (Pattern 18: the English asserts what the author argues against; the dropped word is a negation, the 7561 @0648 form of the gloss).
- **1101A** — *Unde quibusdam suis discipulis consolatoria mittit, verba dicens*: the comma after *mittit* is on the plate. Rendered "he sends consolatory things …, saying words", keeping the mark.
- **1101C** — ⚑ *non profectu et utilitate proximorum consolatur, verum etiam re praesentium bonorum et spe futurorum sublevatur*: plate p. 557 confirms **non … verum etiam** with no *solum*. The literal English says the bride is **not** consoled by her neighbours' progress, which contradicts the whole exposition of VERS. 5 just ended (*magna certe consolatio … quando videt proximos proficere*, three lines above). Carried literally with `[cj: *non*; read *non solum*, "not only"]`: a correlative whose answering member (*verum etiam*) is printed and whose own member is not (Pattern 18, the 7561 @0648 and 11535 @1231B class).
- **1101D** — *Laeva Christi, id est sinistra*: a gloss of one Latin word for "left hand" by another; English has no pair. *sinistra* stands in italic Latin ("The left hand of Christ, that is, the *sinistra*"), per the Song rule's case 2 (a gloss depends on the word).
- **1102A** — *Haec est enim dextera ejus quae* **eum** *amplexetur, … quae* **eum** *adepta fuerit, … requiescere possit*: plate confirms *eum* twice (masculine, where the bride/soul wants *eam*). English "him" as printed; the construction of the second *quae* is loose and is rendered "which, when she shall have obtained him, she may be able to rest".
- **1102B–C** — *nec oculus videre, nec auris audire, nec in cor hominis possit ascendere (I Cor. II)*: 1 Cor 2:9 adapted into an infinitive clause; only *nec oculus*, *nec auris*, *nec in cor hominis* are italic on the plate, and the English keeps those three spans.
- **1102D** — the end of Book I (*Amen*). The *LIBER SECUNDUS* head that closes chunk 0029 stands at the top of p. 558 (cols 1103/1104), above *95 PROOEMIUM*; confirmed there.
