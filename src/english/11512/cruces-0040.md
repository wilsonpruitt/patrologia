## Column notes

- **1136B** — *Nam quibus omnia cum illo donavit, quando omnipotentem* **non** *habent, nihil est quod in ipso et per ipsum non possint.* Plate p. 574 confirms *non habent* (zoomed). Rendered literally: "when they do not have the Almighty, there is nothing which … they cannot do". The argument wants "since they have the Almighty"; *quando* may be interrogative ("when do they not have the Almighty?"), which construes, but the plate prints a comma, not a question mark. Carried, crux only (the negation is printed and is in the English).
- **1137B** — *numerositas sexagenario numero determinatur* **:** *non enim nihil rationis habet*: plate p. 575 prints a colon after *determinatur*; our Latin has no mark. English follows the plate ("fixed at the number sixty: for"). TEI patch (punctuation).
- **1137D** — *Solent autem* **mihi** *qui Arithmeticae artis scientiam habent … dicere*: plate confirms *mihi*. Rendered "are wont to say to me". *superfluum / superflui / superfluitatem* kept on one root ("superfluous … superfluity") because the gloss turns on it.
- **1138A** — plate prints *sexagenari i* at a line end with a wide space before the final *i*; read as justification spacing at the end of the line, not a split sort. Our Latin *sexagenarii*. No marker.
- **1139B** — *aliud* **avaritiae contra liberalitatem**: the list otherwise runs virtue *contra* vice; here the order is reversed. Plate confirms. Rendered literally ("another of avarice against liberality"). Crux only.
- **1139D** — *suggestio,* **delectasio**, *consensus*: plate p. 576 confirms *delectasio* (zoomed; the *s* is clear). Non-word for *delectatio*. `[sic: *delectasio*]` with the sense "delight" beside it.
- **1139D** — *ut* **praecito** *capite malae suggestionis*: plate confirms *praecito* (zoomed). Non-word for *praeciso*. `[sic: *praecito*]`, sense "cut off" beside it.

- **1140C** — *ferculum a* **ferendo** *dicatur*: the gloss is an etymology. The English keeps *ferendo* in italic (as printed) and names the Latin noun in straight quotes ("\"ferculum\", a litter, is so called from *ferendo*, bearing"). Elsewhere *ferculum* = "litter". Note that Wolbero also treats the *ferculum* as a course of a banquet (*coelestium ferculum epularum*, 1141A; *ad cujus epulas*, 1145C); "a litter of heavenly banquets" renders the words as printed.

## Standing renderings (for merge-time reconciliation)

- Following the peers: *sponsus/sponsa* = Bridegroom/bride · *amica mea* = my love · *lectulus* = bed · *pacificus* = peaceable one · *charitas* = charity, *dilectio* = love · *Salomon* = Solomon · thou/you by number (Pattern 17; the *filiae Sion* are "you") · VERSE n.-- · Cologne numbers verbatim.
- Introduced here: *fortes / fortissimi* = valiant ones / most valiant · *ferculum* = litter (etymology at 1140C handled as in the note above) · *reclinatorium* = reclining-place (keeps the root the gloss uses: *reclinat*, *se reclinare*, *caput reclinet*) · *ascensus purpureus* = purple ascent (in the lemma "the ascent of purple") · *media charitate constravit* = "the midst he strewed with charity" (Wolbero plays *constravit* against *constratae … mentes*, "strewn low", 1141D) · *columnae argenteae* = pillars of silver · *filiae Sion* = daughters of Sion · *speculatio* = watching · *acervus testimonii* = heap of testimony · *Libanus … candidatio* = whitening · *praelati / praepositi* = prelates / those set over · *timores nocturni* = fears in the night.
- **Heads:** *CAP. III.-- *Continuatio.** rendered `CHAP. III.-- *Continuation.*`; *CAPUT IV.* = `CHAPTER IV.` (the peers render *CAPUT SECUNDUM* as CHAPTER TWO). The stints on 0030–0039 meet the same *Continuatio* head; one form to be chosen at merge.
