## Migne's foot notes

- **One note in the whole range: p. 638, `(4) Locus obscurus.`**, set under col. 1263. It is keyed in the text at **1263B**: *…et conferat perfecte aeternam impassibilitatem, immutabilitatem, immortalitatem (4).* The key stands before the full stop, at the end of the paragraph (the last sentence of chunk 0075). It was placed in the Latin as `[cn: 4 | Locus obscurus.]`, with nothing in the English. TSV: `data/briefs/11512-NOTES-0075.tsv`.
- **The number was read, not inferred.** On both copies the digit is ink-filled, and in this face 1 and 4 are close (the OCR layer's unreliability here is known). I read it at 600 dpi on scan A (p. 638: the key in the text and the note at the foot) and on scan B (p. 636: the note at the foot). I then compared it pixel-enlarged against **`141` in the index on p. 645**, the same printing and the same small face. There the **4 has a crossbar projecting to the left at lower mid-height and a closed counter; the 1s have only a top flag and a foot.** The foot digit on A shows the left-projecting bar and the counter hole. The text key shows the diagonal into the stem and the counter. **Printed: (4)**, on both key and note. Scan B's glyph is the same ink-filled shape and does not settle it independently.
- **Numbering and gaps:** p. 638 carries only (4). There is no (1), (2) or (3) anywhere on the page, or anywhere else in pp. 636–645. **Every other page in the range (636, 637, 639–645) has no foot note**, and each foot was read full width. So the sequence has a gap of (1)–(3) on this page, like p. 613's lone (3). This has to be documented page by page, as the brief asks; I did not guess at it.
- **Crux (Migne's own *Locus obscurus*).** The note is keyed to the end of the sentence at 1263B: *Haec ergo aliquo modo esse potest mensura sive dimensio, quam potest habere fructus iste **participans**, non secundum se ut dictum est, sed secundum participans, ut quandoque a participante sicuti est adeptus, omnem deinceps adimat dimensionis in crescendo passibilitatem sive mutabilitatem, et conferat perfecte aeternam impassibilitatem, immutabilitatem, immortalitatem.* The obscurity is plausibly the subject. *fructus iste participans* makes the fruit (God) the *participans*, whereas the whole argument from 1260D calls God the *participabile* and the soul the *participans*. The *ut*-clause then has the fruit, "attained by the participant", take away passibility, but from whom it does not say. Rendered literally ("which this fruit, participating, can have"). No marker, since Migne's note is the witness and stands on the Latin side (Pattern 19).
