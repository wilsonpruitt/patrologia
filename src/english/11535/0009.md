## CHAPTER XIV. *That the office of true devotion is threefold.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is certainly to be noted that the office of true and perfect devotion is threefold. ⟦s2⟧ The first is the confession of the crime, the second the request for mercy, the third the glorification of him who shows mercy. ⟦s3⟧ The first is the accusing of the fault, the second the supplication for pardon, the third the magnifying of the divine munificence. ⟦s4⟧ All these things this Daniel of ours performs magnificently, as is known from his words. ⟦s5⟧ It is the confession and the accusing of fault, where in another place he speaks [1251B] to the Lord in prayer: *We have sinned, we have done iniquity, we have acted impiously, and we have departed, and we have turned aside from thy commandments and thy judgments [n: (Dan. IX)]*. ⟦s6⟧ It is the supplication and the imploring of pardon, where thou readest, the same man saying: *Hear, O Lord; be appeased, O Lord; attend, and do [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s7⟧ It is without doubt the glorification and magnifying of his Creator, and of him who shows mercy to him, that thou readest in this place: *Blessed be the name of the Lord from everlasting and even unto everlasting, for wisdom, and fortitude are his, and he changeth times and ages, transferreth kingdoms, and establisheth them [n: (Dan. II)]*. ⟦s8⟧ Wisdom and fortitude are indeed goods of the one who receives them, but without doubt they are the goods of God who bestows them, since whoever have wisdom and fortitude, [1251C] whoever have been able to act wisely and strongly, have received them from his gift. ⟦s9⟧ *For what hast thou,* says the Apostle, *which thou hast not received? [n: (I Cor. XIV)]*. ⟦s10⟧ He, he says, changeth times and ages. ⟦s11⟧ We know that all things have their time, and that all things under heaven pass in their own spaces. ⟦s12⟧ A time to plant, and a time to pluck up that which is planted. ⟦s13⟧ After this manner it can be considered in other things also. ⟦s14⟧ And so from the occasion of the time we receive an opportunity, that we may be able to do now these things, now those, more profitably or more readily. ⟦s15⟧ And so he changes the times for our uses, when now he accommodates opportunity to the exercises of these virtues, of those. ⟦s16⟧ But what, I ask, does it profit to have received the opportunity of the things to be practiced, unless from the gift of that same bounty we receive also the faculty of performing them? ⟦s17⟧ But we know [1251D] that different ages are more apt for different effects. ⟦s18⟧ Certainly the things we learn in boyhood we retain more deeply and bring forth more readily. ⟦s19⟧ In the age of youth the strength of the body is more robust, and the spirit bolder; and therefore that age is more effective for the practice of exercise. ⟦s20⟧ Up to a more advanced age both manifold teaching and manifold experience of things increase knowledge by daily advances; and therefore that age, beyond all the rest, is vigorous in counsels and strong in prudence. ⟦s21⟧ And so the first of the ages spoken of prevails for learning, the second for practicing, but the third for discerning. ⟦s22⟧ After the likeness of years we consider, so to speak, the ages of merits also; and according to the difference of the various advances we know that we have the efficacy of various virtues. [1252A] And so differing efficacies and diverse faculties are in a manner the ages of years. ⟦s23⟧ That best governor of merits therefore alternates the measures of times and of ages, who by the dispensation of his goodness administers both the faculty and the opportunity, now of these virtues, now of those. ⟦s24⟧ He transferreth kingdoms and establisheth them, when, the tyranny of vices being overthrown, he makes the virtues to reign. ⟦s25⟧ Of these peoples of vices or of virtues, and of the destruction of the one as well as the exaltation of the other, it is mystically written, when it is said through the Prophet: *Thou hast brought a vineyard out of Egypt; thou hast cast out the nations, and planted it [n: (Psal. LXXIX)]*. ⟦s26⟧ The vineyard is planted when a good purpose is fixed in a good intention. ⟦s27⟧ The nations are cast out when, by the edict of confession and the vow of profession, sins are threatened and vices are renounced. [1252B] To the same purport runs that which the same Prophet [sic: *alios*] took heed of concerning the extirpation of vices, or the planting of virtues: *Thou hast cast out the nations, and planted them; thou hast afflicted the peoples, and driven them out [n: (ibid.)]* [var: Vulg. Ps. 43:3 opens *Manus tua gentes disperdidit*, "Thy hand destroyed the nations"; the opening clause as printed here is that of Ps. 79:9 just quoted, whose object is the vine, *eam*]. ⟦s28⟧ That hateful people of vices is driven out by the greatness of affliction, when at length the soul, long and much oppressed, is set free from the dominion of carnal desires as much by contrition of body as by compunction of heart. ⟦s29⟧ And so in this manner the author and protector of our salvation, while he destroys the dominion of vices in us and restores the virtues in their place, transferreth kingdoms and establisheth them. ⟦s30⟧ And rightly indeed, for the kingdom is the Lord's, and he shall rule over the nations: *He giveth,* he says, *wisdom to the wise, and knowledge to them that understand instruction. ⟦s31⟧ He revealeth [1252C] deep and hidden things, and knoweth what is set in darkness, and light is with him.* ⟦s32⟧ Let no one, then, presume upon his own genius; let no one ascribe the knowledge of inward or of outward things to his own studies or merits. ⟦s33⟧ For it is he who gives wisdom to the wise, he equally who gives knowledge to them that understand instruction. ⟦s34⟧ To the same purport without doubt goes he who says: *All wisdom is from the Lord God, and hath been with him always, and is before all time [n: (Eccle. I)]*. ⟦s35⟧ With both agrees he who says: *Every best gift, and every perfect gift, is from above, coming down from the Father of lights [n: (Jac. I)]*. ⟦s36⟧ He revealeth deep and hidden things, and knoweth what is set in darkness. ⟦s37⟧ For those things which are truly deep, and exceed the measure of human understanding, the Apostle cries out [1252D] when he says: *O the depth of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God, how incomprehensible are his judgments, and his ways past finding out! [n: (Rom. XI.)]* [var: Vulg. *O altitudo divitiarum sapientiae et scientiae Dei*, "O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and of the knowledge of God"] Yet of these very sublime and deep things he does reveal, through his Spirit, as much as it behooves us to know, or is expedient for us. ⟦s38⟧ He reveals therefore the deep things and the secrets of his own hiddenness; nor these only, but also the hidden things of another's counsel, which an alien malice conceals from us, that it may the more easily deceive us. ⟦s39⟧ Thou hast heard what we ought to desire or to hope for concerning his revelation; hear further what we should fear concerning his knowledge. ⟦s40⟧ He knoweth what is set in darkness; he knows indeed our inmost things and whatever is hidden of our heart, and not only those things which we alone know of ourselves and wish to be hidden from others, but also those which we ourselves are not able to penetrate in the hiding places of our conscience. ⟦s41⟧ He reveals therefore his own things, he reveals [1253A] another's, he knows ours. ⟦s42⟧ He reveals his own secrets, he reveals another's frauds, he knows our hidden things. ⟦s43⟧ He reveals the deep things of his own dispensation, he reveals the hidden things of the devil's fraud, he knows the dark things of the human conscience. ⟦s44⟧ He reveals his own deep things, that we may know what we ought to believe of him, what to hope from him. ⟦s45⟧ He reveals another's hidden things, that we may know what we ought to fear, what to beware of. ⟦s46⟧ He knows our dark things, lest we believe that anything can lie hidden from him, and deceive ourselves with a vain hope. ⟦s47⟧ He revealeth deep and hidden things, and knoweth what is set in darkness: *The uncertain and hidden things of thy wisdom thou hast made manifest to me [n: (Psal. L)]*. ⟦s48⟧ Behold the revelation of deep things; and elsewhere he prays to the same effect: *Unveil mine eyes, and I will consider the wondrous things of thy law [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*. ⟦s49⟧ He feared another's hidden things who said: *Thou wilt bring [1253B] me out of this snare which they have hidden for me [n: (Psal. XXX)]*. ⟦s50⟧ To the same thing looks what he sings elsewhere: *With their tongues they acted deceitfully; the venom of asps is under their lips [n: (Psal. XIII)]*. ⟦s51⟧ Did they not wish to hide the venom which they carried under their tongue? ⟦s52⟧ He believed that our lurking places could not lie hidden from him, who said: *But all things are naked and open to the eyes of God [n: (Hebr. IV)]* [var: Vulg. Heb. 4:13 *oculis ejus*, "to his eyes"]. ⟦s53⟧ It is Solomon's judgment that he alone knows all things, or knows all the hearts of the sons of men [n: (Sap. IX)]. ⟦s54⟧ With whose judgment he agrees who says: *God searcheth the hearts and the reins [n: (Psal. XII)]*. ⟦s55⟧ But what did he mean by saying, and light is with him. ⟦s56⟧ Without doubt God is light, and in him there are no darknesses at all. ⟦s57⟧ Why then does he not say what he could truly have said, that God is light, but says, and with him is light? ⟦s58⟧ Or did he perhaps wish to teach us this, [1253C] that where he himself has abided through indwelling grace, the light of necessary knowledge cannot be lacking? ⟦s59⟧ For the light indeed which he himself is, as it is in itself, we certainly cannot meanwhile see in this life; but the light which is with him we have been accustomed to receive, not only for solace, but also for support. ⟦s60⟧ But of what sort do we say this light to be, through which we recognize what we ought to do, what to avoid, what to love, what to hold in hatred, what to believe or to hope? ⟦s61⟧ Of what sort, I ask, is that light which knows deep and hidden things, he indeed revealing, he finally inspiring, who gives wisdom to the wise, and knowledge to them that understand instruction? ⟦s62⟧ Thus far Daniel has glorified God for the common good; but now for the special benefit specially bestowed [1253D] upon himself and upon his companions. ⟦s63+64⟧ *To thee,* he says, *God of my fathers, I confess, and thee I praise, because thou hast given me wisdom and fortitude, and now thou hast shown me the things we asked of thee, because thou hast opened to us the word of the king.* [var: Vulg. Dan. 2:23 *Deus patrum nostrorum*, "God of our fathers"] Above he said, he giveth wisdom to the wise. ⟦s65⟧ But here he says: Because thou hast given me wisdom and fortitude; and again: Because thou hast opened to us the word of the king. ⟦s66⟧ The chief order of charity, to prefer common goods to private ones. ⟦s67⟧ For charity seeketh not the things that are her own, but the things of others. ⟦s68⟧ He therefore who by the instinct of charity set common goods before his own, what wonder if he first offered the sacrifice of praise for those things which he loved more?

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But it is to be noted that those three things which we assigned above to Daniel's office we can equally [1254A] mark in this one verse. ⟦s2+3⟧ Mark the voice of confession and of glorification in that which thou readest: To thee, God of my fathers, I confess, and thee I praise; and he shows that he offered the prayers of supplication in that which he says: And now thou hast shown to us the things we asked of thee. [var: Vulg. Dan. 2:23 has "hast shown ME" here, and the lemma printed above has it so too; this re-quotation gives "to us"] But in these three, unless I am mistaken, the office of true devotion is contained or fulfilled.
