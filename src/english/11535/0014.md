## CHAPTER XIX. *That we recover a lost grace of contemplation better by devotion than by investigation, and that we obtain freely that also which we acquire by the merit of devotion.*

But behold, Daniel now comes to the narration, or the exposition, of the vision, when he adds: *Thy dream, and the visions of thy head upon thy bed, are of this kind [n: (Dan. II)]*. Because by the dream the bodily sense is lulled, we rightly understand by it the excess of mind, and by the dream those things which we see through the excess of mind. The head is the highest and chief member of the body, and the mind is the principal part of the inner [1261C] man. The visions of the head, therefore, are the contemplations of the mind. Now in a bed we are wont to rest, and therefore in it we rightly understand the leisure of rest. In this bed our inner man is laid as it were to rest, when the spirit is composed to the highest tranquillity. In this bed a dream is then in a manner seen, when amid the leisures of its inmost rest and highest tranquillity the mind is lifted up to the contemplation of sublime things. Thy dream, he says, and the visions of thy head are of this kind. It is nothing else for our Daniel to say these things, and to go over again the lost dream, and to teach the king, than that the devout mind, often after the grace of contemplation has been lost, calls back to memory what that grace once was in it, and by much insistence of compunction [1261D] repairs it again, and at last possesses it more firmly and more perfectly. For the king came to know his vision more firmly and more perfectly through Daniel's office than he had before received it by himself. For to this end such grace is often given to some in their beginnings, and withdrawn, that they may recognize by experience what they ought to seek after, and with how great avidity. To this end again it is granted to be recovered (yet not without great labor), that from it may better become known how strictly it must be guarded. But that we may pass on to what follows: behold, before Daniel explicates the vision, he is zealous to set down two things first, namely how the king had first obtained it, and how Daniel himself might afterwards recover it. And of the first: *Thou, O king,* he says, *didst begin to think [1262A] upon thy bed what should be after thee. And he that reveals mysteries showed thee what things are to come.* [var: Vulg. *post haec*, "after these things"] As if he said openly: Because thou didst diligently seek concerning things to come, thou hast deserved to receive a revelation of things to come. Of the second also he adds: *To me* moreover *too, not in the wisdom which is in me above all living, has this sacrament been revealed, but that the interpretation might be made manifest to the king, and that thou mightest know the thoughts of thy mind.* As if he said to the king more openly: To me also it has been revealed, not through my own sense, not on account of my merit, but for thy advancement. And I think this very thing is plain to all, how much it pleases according to the history, that he in no way ascribes to his own effort or to his merits that other thing which he had obtained by prayers. Thou, O king, he says, didst begin. And do thou consider, and [1262B] consider well, if thou art able, how much the thought of things to come avails with God, which is wont to be divinely rewarded with a revelation of things to come. Hear a man anxious concerning things to come, and vehemently occupied in thoughts of this kind: *Will God then cast off for ever, and will he not add that he be more favorable yet? Or will he cut off his mercy unto the end, from generation to generation? [n: (Psal. LXXVI.)]* Hear the same man taught concerning such things, and deeply enlightened as to those things which he had sought: *The Lord is compassionate and merciful, long-suffering and very merciful; he will not be angry for ever, nor will he threaten unto eternity [n: (Psal. CII)]*. Perhaps he had then lost the revelation of things to come, and grieved to have lost it, when he said: *There is now no prophet, [1262C] and he will know us no more [n: (Psal. LXXIII)]*. And likewise: *And the light of the eyes, even that is not with me [n: (Psal. XXXVII)]* [var: Vulg. *lumen oculorum meorum*, "the light of my eyes"]. He was aspiring, unless I am mistaken, to the former grace, who said: *Who will grant me that I may be according to the former days? [n: (Job. XXIX.)]* [var: Vulg. *Quis mihi tribuat, ut sim juxta menses pristinos*, "the former months"] He was seeking again the accustomed revelation of mysteries, who asked of the Lord: *Unveil mine eyes, and I will consider the wondrous things of thy law [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*. Thou too who hearest or readest these things, if thou feelest that thou hast fallen from such grace, attend diligently to what thou dost silently within thyself; consider with what thoughts thou art vehemently occupied. If thou meditatest on empty things alone, on pressing things alone, what wonder if thou art cheated of the revelation of deep things, of things to come? In the beginning of thy conversion, when thou didst think on eternal things alone, on spiritual and future things alone, when thou didst sigh after sublime things alone, [1262D] what wonder if thou didst deserve to be consoled according to thy desire? The Lord has heard the desire of the poor. Thou hast now ceased to sit and rest at the Lord's feet, after the likeness of Mary. Thou art now troubled about many things, after the likeness of Martha. Remember therefore whence thou hast fallen, and do penance, and do the former works. If thou desirest to restore that former grace, if thou seekest to recover the visions of thy head upon thy bed, run back to Daniel, renew thy devotion. For the renewal of the heart, surely, for the restoration of a lost grace, thou advancest better and art promoted more swiftly by inward devotion than by profound investigation, unless perhaps thy investigation be about thy own declines, and the remedies of those declines. That saying of Daniel agrees with these words. To me also, not in the wisdom [1263A] which is in me above all living, has this sacrament been revealed. Assuredly the king, inquiring after every word of wisdom and of understanding, found in Daniel tenfold above all the soothsayers and magi. Nevertheless a man of so great wisdom obtained what he sought not so much by investigation as by prayer. Let no one, therefore, seek through human wisdom those things which are above human understanding. It calls us perhaps to a deeper understanding, that Daniel does not ascribe to himself even that which he had obtained by the merit of devotion. But that the interpretation, he says, might be made manifest to the king. Perhaps that also is obtained freely which is acquired by the merit of devotion. For devotion itself indeed we have not from ourselves, but we receive it from the gift of the bestower: *For what hast thou,* says the Apostle, *which thou hast not [1263B] received? [n: (I Cor. IV)]*. Therefore both by devotion, and indeed by grace: *Freely,* he says, *you have received, freely give [n: (Matth. X)]*. If therefore by devotion we receive freely, that too is gratuitous which we obtain by its merit. When grace is bestowed for grace, then, just as the first, so also the second gift is obtained freely. We do not therefore receive the gifts of God because we have deserved them, but that from them we may be able to advance, or to profit others. And the understanding of truth indeed we receive for our own advancement, but the teaching of truth for the advancement of others. Now by both is free will helped toward the good, and both are divinely bestowed upon it for a cooperation in the good. But by the king, as we have said, we understand the liberty of the will. To Daniel, therefore, the interpretation [1263C] of perplexed things, the account of the divine judgments, is given as it were for this end, that it may be made manifest to the king, when devotion is for this end illuminated unto truth, that free will may have and recognize wherein it ought to exercise itself, what to beware of, what to choose.
