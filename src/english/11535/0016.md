## CHAPTER XXI. *How everyone who desires one day to remain fixed in true stability must subside from the height of his own presumption.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thou, O king, wast seeing, and behold, as it were one great statue that statue, the great one, and lofty in stature, stood over against thee, and its look was terrible. ⟦s2⟧ The head of this statue was of the finest gold. ⟦s3⟧ But the breast and the arms of silver. ⟦s4⟧ Further, the belly and the thighs of bronze, but the shins of iron. [1265B] A certain part of the feet was of iron, a certain of earthenware. ⟦s5⟧ Thou wast seeing thus, until a stone was cut out without hands, and struck the statue upon its feet of iron and of earthenware, and crushed them. ⟦s6⟧ Then were crushed together the iron, the clay, the bronze, the silver and the gold, and were reduced as it were into the cinders of a summer threshing-floor, which were carried off by the wind, and no place was found for them. ⟦s7⟧ But the stone which struck the statue became a great mountain, and filled the whole earth. ⟦s8+9⟧ This is the dream [n: (Dan. II)]*. [var: Vulg. *abscissus est lapis de monte sine manibus*, "was cut out of a mountain without hands"] Behold, as thou canst see, that image which is discerned through the vision is by manifold change always led into worse things; and that which in the dream begins from the most precious material, running down through manifold declines, at last ends in the vilest material. ⟦s10⟧ For what is begun from gold is at [1265C] the last consummated in mire. ⟦s11⟧ That I may therefore briefly touch upon what I think concerning these things, according to a certain general consideration: I judge that everyone must, according to the form set forth of the vision shown, decrease within himself from his own estimation, and fail more and more in confidence in himself, and temper his presumption, if he desires the kingdom of heaven to be built within him, and that kingdom indeed which can never be ended. ⟦s12⟧ Every man naturally loves and approves himself, and assuredly, in so far as he loves, so far also does he esteem himself great. ⟦s13⟧ But always the measure of esteem hangs upon the greatness of love; because the mode of love is without mode, the greatness of his own esteem is without measure. ⟦s14⟧ O how rare it is that a man can be found [1265D] who does not think himself, against the Apostle's sentence, to be something, when he is nothing! ⟦s15⟧ But is he at least something small? ⟦s16⟧ Nay, truly great, and exceedingly great beyond measure, and, that I may speak figuratively, almost more than golden, while in truth he is vile mire. ⟦s16⟧ Why art thou proud, earth and ashes? ⟦s17+18⟧ *If thou be exalted as an eagle, and if thou set thy nest among the stars of heaven, thence will I draw thee down, saith the Lord [n: (Abd. IV)]*. [var: Vulg. *et si inter sidera posueris nidum tuum*, with no *coeli*, "among the stars"] It behooves him therefore who still swells immeasurably within himself to subside from that enormous height of his own esteem, and to descend by steps to the depths of true humiliation, and at length to reckon himself as a nothing and a void: to trample himself in contempt as vile mire, and to look upon himself every hour as cinder and ashes, if he desires that kingdom of eternal stability to rise up within himself. [1266A] He accords with this sentence to whom there was none like upon the earth: *I am compared to mire, and am made like to cinders and ashes [n: (Job XXX)]*. ⟦s19⟧ But do you wish to hear yet more plainly how utterly he was cast down from all confidence in himself, who appeared great among all the men of the East? ⟦s20⟧ *I have despaired,* he says, *by no means shall I live any longer now [n: (Job VII)]*. ⟦s21⟧ He says that he has despaired, but perhaps thou doubtest of what: of the Lord, or of himself? ⟦s22⟧ Hear therefore what he says elsewhere, and know how strongly he remained fixed in confidence in the Lord: *Even if he slay me, I will hope in him [n: (Job XIII)]*. ⟦s23⟧ He hoped, therefore, but of the Lord. ⟦s24⟧ He despaired none the less, but of himself. ⟦s25⟧ O how great a perversity, to presume of oneself and to despair of the Lord's mercy! ⟦s26⟧ If thou trustest much in thine own virtue, there thou raisest thyself on high, and [1266B] swellest up after the likeness of a mountain, where thou oughtest to have lowered thyself and made thyself as a valley. ⟦s27⟧ It is good therefore to say to this mountain: be lifted up, and be cast into the sea. ⟦s28⟧ Because of such mountains the earth shall be troubled, and mountains shall be carried into the heart of the sea. ⟦s29⟧ On the contrary, if thou distrustest the Lord's protection, there beyond doubt thou makest, wickedly and perversely enough, a valley where thou oughtest to have had a hill, nay a mountain, and a mountain exceeding high. ⟦s30⟧ That therefore the crooked things may be made straight, and the rough ways plain, every such valley shall be filled, every mountain and hill shall be brought low, and at that time the mountain of the house of the Lord shall be raised above the top of the mountains. ⟦s31⟧ O how great a height of mind! o how great a fortitude or firmness, to trust in the Lord's mercy alone, and to remain fixed in such [1266C] confidence! ⟦s32+33⟧ This is that mountain which, after the casting down of the statue and the attenuation or extermination of confidence in oneself, grows from small to immense, so that it cannot be moved for ever: *He that trusteth,* he says, *in the Lord, as mount Sion, shall not be moved for ever, he that dwelleth in Jerusalem [n: (Psal. CXXIV)]*. [var: Vulg. *Qui confidunt in Domino*, plural, "they that trust in the Lord"] This is the mountain which must fill the whole surface of the earth, so that a man may dare nothing of his own effort, may presume nothing at all save of the Lord's virtue. ⟦s34⟧ I think that a city set upon this mountain cannot be hid. ⟦s35⟧ If thou believest me, thou wilt build for thyself on this mountain that structure which is built as a city, whose participation is in itself, that there may be a firmament in the earth on the tops of the mountains. ⟦s36⟧ On this eminence assuredly the kingdom of charity ought to have its metropolis and the seat [1266D] of its empire, and of its kingdom there shall be no end. ⟦s37⟧ For the kingdom of charity, if I mistake not, is that kingdom which, according to Daniel's sentence, shall not be destroyed for ever. ⟦s38⟧ Behold what the raising up, or the casting down, of this statue sets before thee; behold what things, and how great, it represents to thee by way of likeness.
