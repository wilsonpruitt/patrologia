## CHAPTER XXXI. *On the various steps of corruption, and the properties of those who descend from good into evil.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But let us ask what this means, that to these things there is subjoined: *Thou art therefore,* he says, *the head of gold, and after thee shall rise up another kingdom less than thee [n: (Dan. II)]*. ⟦s2⟧ Give heed, I pray, to what he says, to what he sets out to teach us by his interpretation: Thou art, he says, the head of gold. [1282C] For, to say nothing of the rest, this alone, I think, is the thing in which we have need of an interpreter; and would that we might understand the one interpreting, and know this at least through an interpreter. ⟦s3⟧ Does not one statue represent the person of one man, and does it not, as we have expounded, signify the transgression of anyone falling from the highest things into the lowest? ⟦s4⟧ If he who is of this sort is one, having a golden head, a silver breast, but the other members of bronze or of iron, why, I pray, if he to whom this is said is himself the golden head, why is he not himself also the silver breast, and the rest in this manner? ⟦s5⟧ Or art thou perhaps thyself in that part in which thou art golden, and not thyself in that part in which thou art not golden? ⟦s6⟧ Whosoever therefore thou art, when after the golden head thou hast now begun to have a silver breast, see, [1282D] I pray, and diligently give heed, lest perhaps thou thyself be now not thyself. ⟦s7⟧ If, after much charity and a great devotion of spirit, thou hast ceased to be devout and left off being golden, wilt thou be the same as thou wast before? ⟦s8⟧ *Man,* he says, *when he was in honor did not understand; he hath been compared to senseless beasts, and is become like to them [n: (Psal. XLVIII)]*. ⟦s9⟧ To be like to God, a wonderful glory; to be like to cattle, a miserable disgrace. ⟦s10⟧ Therefore it is one man and another, he that is like to God and he that is like to cattle. ⟦s11⟧ Man was made good, and was created unto goodness: *This only I have found,* says Ecclesiastes, *that God made man upright, and that he himself hath entangled himself with infinite questions [n: (Eccli. VII)]*. ⟦s12⟧ If, then, thou glowest with charity and abidest in the love of the good, thou hast a golden head, and thou art thyself, because thou art that which, and unto which, thou wast made and [1283A] oughtest to be. ⟦s13⟧ But when charity grows cold in thee, when thou hast now begun to fail from thy former fervor, wilt thou still be thyself, and not rather another less than thee? ⟦s14⟧ Thou art, he says, the head of gold, and after thee shall rise up another kingdom less than thee. ⟦s15⟧ Art thou not now less than thyself, when thou hast begun to do out of counsel alone what before thou wast doing out of great counsel and much desire? ⟦s16⟧ And after thee, he says, shall rise up another kingdom less than thee. ⟦s17⟧ Is it not another kingdom when the mind is ruled by counsel alone, than when it is driven also by the desire of the good? ⟦s18⟧ Is not counsel alone, without desire, a lesser kingdom than a fervent desire with prudent counsel? ⟦s19⟧ Is it not less to do good out of deliberation alone than also out of great love or delight. ⟦s20⟧ Rightly, then, is it said: And after thee shall rise up another kingdom [1283B] less than thee. ⟦s21⟧ And afterward concerning the third kingdom it is inferred, when it is said: And another third kingdom of bronze, which shall rule over the whole earth. ⟦s22⟧ As what is done out of love belongs to the gold, and as what is done out of deliberation alone belongs to the silver, so the good that is done out of simulation belongs to the bronze. ⟦s23⟧ In the first charity reigns, in the second truth, in the third reigns pretense. ⟦s24⟧ See then, I pray, how rightly it seems to fit this third kingdom that it is described as ruling over the whole earth. ⟦s25⟧ For the emperor of this kingdom has no lot in the kingdom of heaven, who exercises his body in good work but neglects to nourish his heart in good will: *The kingdom of heaven,* he says, *is within you [n: (Luc. XVII)]* [var: Vulg. Luc. 17:21 *regnum Dei*, "the kingdom of God"]. ⟦s26⟧ Within indeed, within in the heart charity reigns, within truth reigns; yet not content with these [1283C] bounds they break forth outward through good work. ⟦s27⟧ See then whether perhaps those two aforesaid kingdoms belong to the kingdom of heaven, if the kingdom of heaven is altogether within us, in that either of them can be found within us. ⟦s28⟧ But that king and emperor of the third kingdom, having no part in the kingdom of heaven, driven down from the height and cast forth outside, because he cannot in heaven, claims a kingdom for himself on earth. ⟦s29⟧ For he hath cast forth his inmost parts in his own life, and cannot say, our conversation is in heaven. ⟦s30⟧ How much more, then, can his dominion not be in heaven? ⟦s31⟧ Yet he rules over the whole earth, exercising the flesh in good work, leaving the mind empty of good intention. ⟦s32⟧ *And the fourth kingdom,* he says, *shall be as iron. ⟦s33⟧ As [1283D] iron breaketh in pieces and tameth all things, so shall it break in pieces and crush all these things.* ⟦s34⟧ What is it that he says, all these things, unless that this kingdom crushes the other aforesaid kingdoms. ⟦s35⟧ How is iron wont to break in pieces the other metals, namely those, the gold and the silver? ⟦s36⟧ As in the gold charity, in the silver truth, in the bronze pretense, so, as has been said above, in the iron cruelty is signified. ⟦s37⟧ But who could fail to know how the rigor of cruelty is wont to scatter and to destroy holy desire, which belongs to the gold; true counsel, which belongs to the silver; the pious but simulated service, which belongs to the bronze work? ⟦s38⟧ Well, therefore, has it been said: and the fourth kingdom shall be as iron.
