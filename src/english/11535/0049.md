## CHAPTER XLVI. *How he glorifies God alone, who was so shamelessly magnifying himself.*

And I blessed, he says, the Most High. Behold, he who above so shamelessly magnified himself now glorifies him alone who, according as the Apostle says, is over all things blessed forever: *And I praised and glorified him that liveth for ever [n: (Rom. IX)]*. Him that liveth for ever: that life, surely, which is himself, for whom to be is nothing other than to live, nor to live [1343B] than to be. For his power is an everlasting power, because what he can do once, he who is almighty can always do. And his kingdom in generation and unto generation, because to him nothing accrues from time, nothing departs, with whom there is no change, nor shadow of alteration. And all the inhabitants of the earth are reputed as nothing before him. By the inhabitants of the earth understand the lovers of earthly things, who know not how to seek the things that are above, nor can say with the Apostle: *But our conversation is in heaven [n: (Philipp. III)]*. But they are truly reputed as nothing who have lost true being, for whom assuredly it were better not to be at all than to be thus, unless someone think it better to be always wretched than not to be at all. For he does according to his own will as much among the powers of heaven as among [1343C] the inhabitants of the earth, and there is none that can resist his hand and say to him: Why hast thou done it? To the same sentence agrees what the Psalmist says: *Whatsoever he would he hath done, in heaven and in earth, in the sea, and in all the deeps [n: (Psal. CXXXIV)]*. Since in what follows the same Nabuchodonosor says in the praise of God that all his works are true and his ways judgments: what is it that here he says that he does all things according to his own will? Surely if all his works are true, all things are done in truth; surely if all his ways are judgments, all things are done in equity: how then according to will? Or is it perhaps that to be done according to equity is to be done according to will, and by the very fact that it is done in equity, by that very fact, I say, it is done according to his will? But if it is [1343D] altogether the same to be done according to equity and to be done according to his will, then his will is the same as equity, and equity the same as will. And there is none that can resist his hand and say, Why hast thou done it? Who should resist him? who gainsay him? since he does all things as powerfully as wisely, since there is no one who could either restrain omnipotence or convict the highest wisdom.

## CHAPTER XLVII. *What proud glorying does, or what devout glorification effects.*

*At that very time my sense returned to me, and I came unto the honor of my kingdom and unto my comeliness, and my figure returned to me [n: (Dan. IV)]*. At that very, he says, time. At that very time in which I blessed God, in that very one in which I [1344A] praised and glorified him. Surely, if we well recall the things said above, he fell there, when he was magnifying himself proudly enough. Behold, this same man rises again when he devoutly glorifies God, and his Lord. Surely thou seest what proud glorying does, surely thou seest what devout glorification effects. Proud glorying deserves a casting down, devout glorification an exaltation. Behold, when he now glories not in himself but in the Lord, his sense returns to himself. What above he called his sense restored to him, he now calls his sense returned to himself; or perhaps a man's sense returns to itself when the eye of discretion is called back to the consideration of itself. The sense returns to itself when it is divinely [1344B] enlightened unto the consideration and knowledge of its own merits. While therefore the human spirit glorifies its God, its sense returns to itself; because while it opens its eyes to that light of the Godhead, and strains vehemently toward it, it more fully acknowledges the darkness of its own weakness: *Come ye to him,* he says, *and be enlightened [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]*. What wonder if by drawing near to the light we are enlightened? what wonder if from light a light is kindled? *And in thy light,* he says, *we shall see light [n: (Psal. XXXV)]*. Light is seen in light when, from the knowledge and working of the divine brightness, the spirit is more fully enlightened unto the knowledge of human weakness. Yet this twofold knowledge is wont to minister alternate increases to itself, and each assuredly to grow out of the other. For from the knowledge of God the spirit advances to the thought of itself, and in the same way [1344C] from much knowledge of itself it grows to the knowledge of God. But behold, while the sense which had been lost returns, he received back his kingdom which he had lost. Without doubt, unless the grace of discretion be repaired in him, the spirit is never brought through to the full integrity of virtue.

## CHAPTER XLVIII. *How through the discretion of judgment, or through circumspection of oneself, the discipline of morals is wont to be reformed.*

I came, he says, unto the honor of my kingdom and unto my comeliness. It is assuredly plain that man's free will receives and holds a kingdom from God, when it can now restrain the evil motions of the heart from evil. But this kingdom is then made honorable when, by [1344D] God's gift, it can now exercise the good motions of the heart in good. And that same kingdom is overlaid with a worthy comeliness when whatever good is done in it is ordered with the highest moderation and with great discretion. And so he receives and obtains a kingdom who bravely tames and manfully restrains the rebellious motions of the spirit. And at that time he who is such shall judge among the nations, shall fill up ruins, shall crush the heads in the land of many. And he has then an honorable kingdom too, when he is now made Lord of hosts in so great a multitude of virtues so innumerable, and, having soldiers under him, says to this one, Go, and he goes, and to another, Come, and he comes, and to his servant, Do this, and he does it. This kingdom is made comely thereby, and most of all when, bearing himself modestly, he moderates all things with meekness [1345A] and tranquillity: *Son,* he says, *do thy works in meekness, and thou shalt be loved above the glory of men [n: (Eccle. XIII)]* [var: Vulg. *in mansuetudine opera tua perfice*, "perfect thy works in meekness"]. And so the honor of the kingdom is to act powerfully, the comeliness of the kingdom to act wisely, so that in that his kingdom he who is the possessor of the kingdom may reach from end to end mightily and order all things sweetly. And my figure returned to me. Figure is an outward quality, namely the exterior form of the body. But bodily discipline belongs to the outward quality, as the composure of the mind to the inward quality. In the figure, therefore, which consists in bodily lineaments, there is rightly understood the discipline of the body, the form of religion, the habit and composure of the outer man. And so the figure returns when, according to the former custom, the form of religion lost for a time [1345B] is reformed. The sense having returned, the figure returns, because through the discretion of judgment and through circumspection of oneself the discipline of morals is either newly established or the old renewed.
