## CHAPTER XI. *On the vice of fraudulence.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Then without doubt fraud grows into fraudulence, when out of much malice it now befalls the mind that it is altogether willing to deceive when it can, and after [1358D] much use and practice there is nonetheless at hand to it, through much cunning now, that it can easily deceive when it wills. ⟦s2⟧ For what else is fraudulence than a cunning malice, as prone to every fraud as it is prompt? ⟦s3⟧ Rightly is the fraudulence of hypocrites figured in the leopard, which is sprinkled over its whole body with certain spots. ⟦s4⟧ For hypocrites indeed make a show of holiness in their work, but what they love is perverse; and what lives within in the affection must needs at some time break forth and appear in the work. ⟦s5⟧ And so they that are of this sort do good things openly and evil things in secret. ⟦s6⟧ They therefore who break forth stealthily into works of dissoluteness, or as often as they can do it excusably, what else do they do than sprinkle the body of their own action with certain spots? ⟦s7⟧ Let us see, then, [1359A] of what sort this beast is said to be, which is described in the third place. ⟦s8+9⟧ *After these things,* he says, *I was beholding, and lo, another as it were a leopard, and it had wings as of a bird, four upon itself, and four heads were upon the beast, and power was given to it [n: (Dan. VII)]*. [var: Vulg. Dan. 7:6 reads *et ecce alia quasi pardus* and *quatuor capita erant in bestia*, "in the beast"; Migne prints *altera* for *alia*, and *super bestiam*, "upon the beast"] Give heed how rightly those that are designated in this beast are said to have wings. ⟦s10⟧ Certainly when birds fly, they lift themselves above themselves. ⟦s11⟧ Certainly when they will to fly, they suspend themselves through the void. ⟦s12⟧ Do not minds of this sort lift themselves above themselves, and suspend themselves through the void, when for the sake of ambition and empty glory they stretch out the observances of a hard strictness almost beyond the measure of human possibility? ⟦s13⟧ For to exercise the zeals of virtue, and not to care for or to seek the merits or the rewards of the virtues, what else is it than to suspend oneself on high through [1359B] the void? ⟦s14⟧ And what else is it to have many heads, than to do nothing simply? ⟦s15⟧ For as in the wings we understand occupation and exercise, so in the head we understand intention. ⟦s16⟧ For the head rules the whole body, and all our action is subservient to the intention.

## CHAPTER XII. *On the evil of fraudulence, how multiform it is in work.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Do you wish to know yet more fully what kind of wings fraudulence is wont to have? ⟦s2⟧ That I may therefore briefly express what I think: let one be called the wing of simulation, another of dissimulation, the third of ostentation, the fourth of excuse. ⟦s3⟧ For simulating minds, and those that are slaves to hypocrisy, are wont; they are wont, I say, not only to boast of the goods they have, but even to simulate that they have [1359C] goods which they have not at all. ⟦s4⟧ On the contrary they are wont craftily to excuse manifest evils, but hidden evils (as if they had them not at all) cunningly to dissimulate. ⟦s5⟧ Simulation and dissimulation are concerning hidden things. ⟦s6⟧ But simulation concerning hidden goods, dissimulation concerning hidden evils. ⟦s7⟧ On the other side, however, ostentation and excuse are wont to be concerning manifest things; but the former concerning manifest goods, the latter concerning manifest evils. ⟦s8⟧ For in those goods which are manifest and cannot be denied, ostentation has its place, just as in manifest evils alone excuse is sought. ⟦s9⟧ Our left hand is brought forth into the midst more rarely than the right, and by that very fact is more rarely seen. ⟦s10⟧ Rightly therefore by the left we understand the more hidden things, and rightly by the right the more manifest. ⟦s11⟧ And so the wings [1359D] of simulation and of dissimulation seem to belong to the left, even as the wings of ostentation and of excuse to the right. ⟦s12⟧ Thou hast therefore in ostentation and simulation the first pair of wings. ⟦s13⟧ And thou hast likewise in excuse and dissimulation the second pair of wings. ⟦s14⟧ For ostentation and simulation are concerning goods alone. ⟦s15+16⟧ But the former concerning true and manifest ones, as it were a wing on the right; the latter concerning feigned ones, as it were a wing on the left. ⟦s17⟧ On these two wings they fly, and commending themselves to their neighbours and publishing abroad their own reputation, they spread their glory round about. ⟦s18+19⟧ But excuse and dissimulation are concerning evils alone: the former concerning manifest ones, as it were one wing on the right; the latter concerning hidden ones, as it were the other wing on the left. ⟦s20⟧ As on the two former, as has been said, they fly, so on these latter they veil their own [1360A] body. ⟦s21⟧ For beyond doubt simulators and hypocrites are wont, on this side and on that, under the covering of excuse and of dissimulation, to veil their hinder and more shameful parts.

## CHAPTER XIII. *On the evil of fraudulence, how manifold it is in intention.*

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Behold, let us see concerning the number of the wings and concerning the plurality of the heads. ⟦s2⟧ Now we know that the fraudulence of hypocrites, in all that it keeps doing, pants after the advancement of its own honour. ⟦s3⟧ For all that it does, or intends in doing, is a slave to ambition. ⟦s4⟧ But ambition is nothing else than the coveting of honour. ⟦s5⟧ Now the first species of this evil is the coveting of liberty, the second the coveting of dignity, the third the coveting of authority, the fourth the coveting of power. ⟦s6⟧ Behold how ambition [1360B] divides itself into four heads, to which, as has been said, the whole action of hypocrites is subservient. ⟦s7⟧ The coveting of liberty is when one now shrinks from being under others. ⟦s8⟧ The coveting of dignity is, by courting higher things, always to pant from step to step. ⟦s9⟧ The coveting of authority is when one now courts to appear to all a man of great counsel and of holiness, and that whatever is to be established or defined should hang upon his counsel or his judgment. ⟦s10⟧ The coveting of power is when one now courts to be set over others. ⟦s11⟧ We often see even those who have professed a voluntary obedience, how gladly, how eagerly they take upon themselves occupations troublesome enough and burdensome, that from the occasion of their own administration they may obtain a somewhat greater liberty of going out, of speaking, of acting. ⟦s12⟧ What does this, I ask, unless the ambition of liberty? [1360C] We see others striving always from step to step, and those who are of a lower grade aspiring from the subdiaconate, subdeacons to the diaconate, deacons to the presbyterate; and though from it they have perhaps acquired nothing of greater liberty or of power, yet they judge it no small thing to hold at least a higher place than the rest. ⟦s13⟧ But whence is this in them, unless from the ambition of dignity? ⟦s14⟧ That man of that Achitopel, speaking, teaches us what the ambition of authority can do in certain men; the traitor and parricide Absalon teaches us what the ambition of power avails.

## CHAPTER XIV. *How from the vice of fraudulence the mind falls into the vice of shamelessness.*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1360D] But it is to be noted what is said of this beast: That power was given to it; and beyond doubt, by the wonderful and hidden judgment of God, the things which hypocrites can do are almost greater, and they do them and keep the observances of a stricter life, than they who afflict themselves for God's sake; and although it is established that all flesh is hay, and all the glory thereof as the flower of hay [var: Vulg. Isa. 40:6 *flos agri*, "the flower of the field"], yet the followers of vanity often labour more for a vain and transitory glory than true emulators for the true and eternal glory. ⟦s2⟧ But it is very difficult, nay almost impossible, that men of this sort should be able long to lie hid. ⟦s3⟧ Hear what Solomon thinks here, when he says: *He that walketh simply, walketh confidently, but he that perverteth his ways shall be made manifest [n: (Prov. X)]*. ⟦s4⟧ It behoves therefore that those who walk doubly and fraudulently, and the perverters of their own ways, [1361A] be made manifest, and that that prophetic word be fulfilled in them: *Uncover thy shame, lay bare thy shoulder, make bare thy legs, pass over the rivers, thy ignominy shall be revealed, and thy reproach shall be seen. [n: (Isa. XLVII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ Men of this sort, when they have been caught in this state of fraudulence, often rush yet into worse things: for when they despair of being able any longer to lie hid in their own depravities, when they lose that one joy of their boasting without any hope of recovering it, straightway they break forth into open shamelessness. ⟦s6⟧ Behold how through fraudulence a man is cast headlong into shamelessness.
