## A. Markers placed

### `[var: …]` — Migne's scripture against the received text (Pattern 14)

**1. 1276A–B · Dan. 2:34 · *Videbas, ita, donec abscissus est lapis sine manibus…***
Migne prints the lemma **without *de monte***; the Clementine Vulgate reads *abscissus est
lapis **de monte** sine manibus*. Rendered as printed; `[var: Vulg. adds *de monte* after
*lapis*: the stone is "cut out of a mountain"]`. **Material**, because Richard's whole
argument in 0022 turns on the stone *becoming* a mountain (*lapis non crescit in rupem sed
mutatur in montem*); the Vulgate's *de monte* would have the mountain there from the start
and would blunt the point he is making. Chunk 0016, which prints the whole dream, likewise
lacks *de monte*, so this is the work's text, not a one-off setting.

**2. 1276C · Jer. · *Percussi, filios vestros, et non doluerunt, renuerunt recipere
disciplinam*** `[n: (Jer. V)]`.
Migne's note points to Jer. 5, whose Vulgate reads *Percussisti eos, et non doluerunt:
attrivisti eos, et renuerunt **accipere** disciplinam* — second person, and no sons. The
opening words (*percussi filios vestros*) are Jer. 2:30. Rendered as printed;
`[var:]` names both. **Material**: Migne's first person makes God the striker in his own
voice, which is exactly the point Richard is pressing (*melius operatur inspiratione quam
percussione*). *recipere* for *accipere* noted inside the same marker; on its own it would
not have earned one.

**3. 1276D · Ps. 50:19 · *cor contritum et humiliatum Deus non despiciet*** (unitalicized,
but a proof-text and therefore inside Pattern 14's scope).
Vulgate: *Cor contritum et humiliatum, **Deus, non despicies*** — a vocative and a second
person. Migne's third-person *despiciet* turns an address to God into a statement about
him. Rendered as printed ("God will not despise"); `[var:]` gives the Vulgate. **Material**
under the runbook's mechanism 5 (agency mis-bound) — this is the same class, one step
milder.

**4. 1278C · Dan. 2:35 · *nullusque locus inventus est **in** eis***
Vulgate (and this work's own chunk 0016): *nullusque locus inventus est **eis***, dative,
"no place was found **for** them." Migne's *in eis* is locative. Rendered as printed;
`[var:]` gives both and names 0016 as the internal control. **Material and internally
attested**: Richard's own gloss at 1279D reads *Raptis autem vento nullus locus **eis**
invenitur* — he glosses the dative, so the lemma at 1278C disagrees with its own exposition
nine lines later.

**5. 1279A · Eccl. 1:13 · *Proposui, in corde meo quaerere…***
Vulgate: *proposui **in animo meo** quaerere*. Rendered "in my heart"; `[var: Vulg. *in
animo meo*, "in my mind"]`. **Material in this work specifically**, because *cor* /
*animus* / *mens* are the load-bearing seam of the fixed vocabulary and Richard is
elsewhere precise about them.

**6. 1279A · Phil. 3:8 · *Omnia arbitratus sum ut stercora, ut Christum lucrifacerem***
Vulgate: *arbitror ut stercora, ut Christum **lucrifaciam*** — present throughout. Migne's
perfect/imperfect subjunctive is rendered as printed; `[var:]` gives the Vulgate tense.
Richard's frame (*cum dicebat*) is past, so the tense shift is doing work on the page.

**7. 1279D–1280A · I John 4:18 · *perfecta charitas mittit timorem*** (unitalicized
proof-text).
Vulgate: *perfecta caritas **foras** mittit timorem*, "casteth fear **out**." Without
*foras* the sentence says perfect charity **sends** fear, which is the opposite of what
Richard immediately draws from it (*Nescit enim perfectio charitatis augere formidinem*).
Rendered as printed; `[var:]` supplies the Vulgate. **This is the most consequential
divergence in the range** — a dropped adverb inverts the proof-text.

**8. 1281B · Dan. 2:37 · *Tu, rex regum es, et **Dominus** coeli…***
Vulgate: *et **Deus** caeli regnum et fortitudinem…* Rendered "the Lord of heaven";
`[var:]`. ⚠ **The three-way comparison the brief asked for:** the verse is printed **three
times** in this stretch — 1281A (*Tu, **Rex** regum es*, capitalized, no continuation),
1281B (*rex regum … **Dominus** coeli*), and chunk **0025** at 1284A (*rex regum … **Deus**
coeli*, the Vulgate reading, with the Dan. 2:38 continuation attached). So Migne prints the
Vulgate's *Deus* once and *Dominus* once, in the same work, seven columns apart. Only the
1281B occurrence is marked, since 0025's agrees with the received text. **Recommendation for
whoever holds 0025: do not "harmonize" the two; the disagreement is the datum.** The
capitalized *Rex* at 1281A is a typographic variant only, and takes no marker — but note that
Richard glosses it as a title (*Rex itaque regum est rationalis consensus*), which is very
likely why the compositor capitalized it.

**9. 1283B–C · Luke 17:21 · *Regnum coelorum, intra vos est***
Vulgate: *ecce enim **regnum Dei** intra vos est*. Rendered "the kingdom of heaven";
`[var: Vulg. *regnum Dei*]`. **Material and load-bearing**: the sentence it proves is
*Neque enim habet hujus regni imperator sortem **in regno coelorum***, and the following
paragraph runs *duo illa praedicta regna pertineant ad **regnum coelorum**, si omnino
**regnum coelorum** intra nos est*. Richard needs *coelorum*, and Migne's lemma gives it to
him where the Vulgate does not. This is the clearest case in the range of a lemma that has
been conformed **to the argument** rather than to the Vulgate.

### `[sic: …]` — carried type (Pattern 12)

**10. 1279B · *reatus nostri formido omnis atque **suscipio** absorbetur***
*suscipio* is a first-person verb standing in a nominative noun slot coordinated with
*formido*; the intended word is plainly *suspicio*, "suspicion." Carried as
`[sic: *suscipio*]`. **Corpus control run:** `atque suspicio` occurs in **11 PL files**;
`atque suscipio` occurs in **3**, and I read all three — 9285 (*confiteor, et adoro, atque
suscipio sacras… imagines*) and 7530 (*veneratione complector atque suscipio*) are both

stands in a noun slot.** 7a⁗ check: the clause keeps its verb (*absorbetur*, "is swallowed
up") and its first subject ("all dread of our guilt"); no negation, interrogative or
conjunction is inside the quarantine.

**11. 1280C (head) · *et **quatem** potestatem ex gratia accipimus***
*quatem* is not a Latin word; *qualem* is, and the head's own first clause prints *Qualem
libertatem*. Carried as `[sic: quatem]` inside the head's italic summary (no nested
asterisks — Pattern 10's rule for a carried run inside an italic span). **Corpus control:**
`qualem potestatem` occurs **6×**, `quatem potestatem` **once**, in this file. 7a⁗ check:
the quarantine removes an interrogative adjective, but the parallel *Qualem* three words
earlier is intact and governs the series, so the reader sees the gap in the right place and
the head still reads as a list of indirect questions.

**12. 1282D · *Simile esse Deo, **mira ile** decus; similem esse pecoribus, miserabile
dedecus.***
A word-division defect (Pattern 10, split direction) for *mirabile*. Carried whole:
`[sic: *mira ile*]`. **Corpus control:** `mira ile` occurs in **one** file (this one);
`mirabile decus` occurs in **zero**, so the control here is negative and I say so rather
than overstating it — the conjecture rests on the antithesis, not on a parallel. 7a⁗ check
(and this is the 7a⁗-b shape, so it was checked hard): the clause is **verbless in the
Latin too** (*decus* / *dedecus* in apposition), so no predicate is lost; no negation, no
conjunction. What the quarantine *does* cost is half the *mirabile* / *miserabile*
jingle, which is why it is recorded here in full.

## B. The pulls I resisted — lemmata where Migne agrees and my instinct did not

These are positive evidence, per the brief.

1. **1277A · Ps. 49:21 · *Arguam te, et statuam contra faciem tuam.*** Douay reads "and
   **set before** thy face," and that phrasing arrived before the Latin was read. Migne
   prints *contra*, the Vulgate prints *contra*, and **Richard's gloss hangs on it**: the
   very next clause is *per ejusmodi percussionem seipsum **contra** seipsum erexerat*.
   Rendered **"will set against thy face."** No marker: nothing diverges.
   ⚑ Note also the pun Richard is working, which an English reader can only be told about:
   ***statuam*** is both "I will set" and "statue," the object of the whole exposition.
   Logged, not marked; there is no marker for a pun.
2. **1277A · Ps. 50:5 · *peccatum meum **contra me** est semper.*** Douay's "always
   **before** me" is the received English. Migne and the Vulgate both print *contra me*, and
   the same *contra … contra* hook as above depends on it. Rendered **"always against me."**
3. **1280A · Ps. 124:1 · *Qui **confidunt** in Domino sicut mons Sion.*** Migne here prints
   the Vulgate's **plural**. ⚠ Chunk **0016** of this same work prints the **singular** *Qui
   **confidit** in Domino* with a `[n: (Psal. CXXIV)]`. My chunk agrees with the Vulgate and
   takes no marker; **whoever re-reads 0016 should consider one there.** Flagged because a
   later reader who meets both will assume one of them is ours.
4. **1279C · Ps. 30:2 · *In te speravi, non confundar in aeternum.*** Vulgate has *In te,
   **Domine**, speravi*. A pure omission that changes nothing; rendered as printed, no
   marker.
5. **1279A · Rom. 6:21 · *Quem ergo fructum habuistis in quibus nunc erubescitis?***
   Vulgate: *habuistis **tunc in illis**, in quibus…* Omission only; no marker. (Also
   Pattern 17: *habuistis / erubescitis* are plural, so **"you," not "thou."**)
6. **1276C · Isa. 55:11 · *non **redibit** ad me vacuum … et **prosperabit** in his.***
   Vulgate: *non **revertetur** … et **prosperabitur***. `[var:]` **declined**, deliberately.
   *redire* / *reverti* are synonyms, and the active/passive shift in *prosperabit* has **no
   English exponent** ("shall prosper" renders both), so a marker would announce a
   divergence the reader could not see. Recorded here instead. Named because it is precisely
   the sort of thing a divergence-hunting agent over-marks.
7. **1279C · Ps. 102:2–3 · *Benedic, anima mea, Domino…*** — exact, including the elision of
   nothing between v. 2 and v. 3. No marker.
8. **1281C · II Cor. 1:12 and John 15:5** — both exact (the former drops only *Nam*).
9. **1282D · Ps. 48:13 · *Homo, cum in honore esset non intellexit…*** — exact.
10. **1280A, 1280B · Dan. 2:35 · *Lapis autem, qui **percussit** statuam***. Vulgate:
    *qui **percusserat***. Pluperfect → perfect; no sense change; chunk 0016 also prints
    *percussit*. No marker.
11. **1282D–1283A · Eccl. 7:30 · *quia fecerit Deus hominem rectum, et ipse **immiscuerit
    se infinitis quaestionibus***.** Vulgate: *quod fecerit … et ipse **se infinitis
    miscuerit quaestionibus***. Compound verb + word order only. No marker. Migne's note
    reads `(Eccli. VII)` for Ecclesiastes; **`[n:]` contents are verbatim and were not
    touched.**
12. **1276A · Matt. 3:9 · *potens est enim **Dominus** de lapidibus istis…*** Vulgate:
    *potens est **Deus***. Declined: same referent, no argument rests on it. (Compare item 8

13. **1283D · Dan. 2:40** — Migne prints *Et **quartum regnum*** and *sic comminuet **haec

## C. Passages that would not construe, and conjectures **declined**

Every one of these is a census candidate. The `[cj:]` bar (a negation or a correlative) is
not met by any of them, so none was glossed in the text; all are rendered as printed.

1. **1275C · *quia quo quisque proximum suum minus amat, eo utique **minus** infirma
   molestius tolerat.*** Two comparatives (*minus*, *molestius*) in one *eo* clause; the
   sense the argument needs is "the more troublesomely he bears weak things," and *minus*
   after *eo utique* contradicts it. Rendered as printed ("by so much the less, assuredly,
   does he bear weak things the more troublesomely"), which is as close as the words allow.
   Declined: not a negation, not a correlative — but note it is *adjacent* to the correlative
   class (*quo … eo*), and it is the one site here I would re-put to Wilson if the bar is
   ever widened.
2. **1275D · *dummodo in alios furit, modo in semetipso torpescit.*** The neighbouring
   *modo* makes *modo … modo* ("now … now") the evident intent; *dummodo* is a real
   conjunction and renders ("so long as"), so nothing is marked. Declined.
3. **1277C · *usque ad digni vel debiti finis **talum** non perducitur.*** *talum* =
   "ankle, heel." Rendered literally, "even to the heel of a worthy or due end." **Corpus
   control:** `finis talum` occurs in exactly one file, this one. Declined nonetheless, and
   deliberately: in a work whose entire subject is a statue read from head to feet, "the
   heel of an end" may well be Richard's own figure rather than a defect. Naming it here so
   that nobody silently emends it to *terminum*.
4. **1278A · *Unde utique est, quod jactantiae argentum quam aurum arrogantiae, quo facilius
   eo et citius atteritur.*** The *quam* clause has no comparative to attach to. Rendered as
   closely as the words allow. Declined.
5. **1279C · *Nonne pro favillarum eventilatione Domino **supplicabit**, cum Domino in
   oratione **proclamaret**…*** Future indicative against imperfect subjunctive, where the
   two flanking sentences both use past tenses (*cum Propheta **dicebat***, *cum …
   **erumperet***). *supplicabat* is the evident reading. Declined — a tense/person anomaly,
   explicitly outside the bar.
6. **1279C · *Vide ergo quia corruptionis **opibus** in favillam redigitur.*** "By the
   riches of corruption" — rendered literally, with no expressed subject.
   **Corpus control:** `corruptionis opus` occurs 2×, `corruptionis opibus` only here.
   Declined.
7. **1280C · *sed non **aequa** accepit qualitatem personae agnoscere.*** Rendered "but he
   did not receive equal things, to recognize…" *aeque* would construe. Declined.
8. **1282B · *Volucribus coeli potenter imperat, **cui** cogitationes suas … occupat.***
   The two parallel sentences on either side both use *cum* + indicative; *cui* leaves the
   relative unattached. Rendered literally ("to whom he occupies his own thoughts").
   Declined — not a negation or correlative. **This is the least satisfying English in the
   range and I would rather it were seen than smoothed.**
9. **1276A · *nec propter testeam fragilitatem de suo **corpore** redargui.*** The series
   is *tumore … errore … rigore*, three ablatives in *-ore* naming a fault; *torpore* would
   complete it and *corpore* breaks it. Rendered "reproved concerning their own body."
   Declined.
10. **1275C · *Et quidem saepe **quaedam** … praesumpserunt … vivunt … possunt.***
    Feminine/neuter plural pronoun governing masculine plural participles and verbs.
    **Pattern 9: grammatical gender has no English exponent** — rendered "certain ones,"
    no marker, no conjecture, flagged so a later sweep does not churn it.
11. **1277B · *Primo itaque in ferri **contritionem** damnamus mala.*** Accusative where the
    parallel next sentence has ablative *In testae … contritione*. No English exponent
    (Pattern 9 family); rendered "in the crushing of the iron." Logged, not marked.

## D. Punctuation carried against the English ear (Pattern 8)

Each of these is a mark the plate prints that an English editor would remove or replace, and
each is preserved. They are listed so that a later reader does not "fix" them.

1. **1274D · *per aurum potest intelligi arrogantia**.** per argentum jactantia, per aes
   pertinacia…*** — a **full stop in the middle of a five-item list**, followed by a
   lower-case continuation. Carried exactly: "…by gold arrogance can be understood. by
   silver boastfulness, by bronze obstinacy…"
2. **1279B, 1279C, 1279C · three consecutive *Nonne …* sentences closed with a PERIOD, not a
   question mark** (*…cooperuit me.* · *…non confundar in aeternum.* · *…propitiatur
   omnibus iniquitatibus tuis.*). All three end with a period in the English. This is the
   single largest punctuation-fidelity decision in the range; the ear wants three question
   marks and the plate gives none.
3. **1281C · *Quid enim habes quod non accepisti**,** nihil enim aliud est…*** — I Cor. 4:7
   printed with a **comma**, not a question mark. Carried: "For what hast thou that thou
   hast not received, for to have such power…"
4. **1282A · *Quid autem cum et illa suppetunt quae sufficiunt ad perfectionem**.***
   — period. Carried. The **next** sentence (*…et sub ditione tua universa constituit**?***)
   *does* print a question mark and keeps it.
5. **1283A · *Nonne minus est bonum facere ex sola deliberatione, quam etiam ex magna
   dilectione seu delectatione**.*** — period after a *Nonne*. Carried.
6. **1277C · *In argenti igitur contritione boni operis imperfectio**.*** — a verbless
   sentence closed with a full stop; the verb (*arguitur*) arrives only in the next
   sentence. Rendered verbless and stopped where the plate stops.
7. **1279A · *…de omnibus quae fiunt sub sole [n: (Eccle. I.)]* Ecce area…*** — **no
   terminal stop at all** after the Ecclesiastes lemma. None supplied.
8. **1279B · *sed, flante vento, favillae, tolluntur*** — a stray comma between subject and
   verb. ⚠ **This one I did NOT carry.** Latin and English comma conventions are not
   1:1 anywhere in this corpus (our English re-commas subordinate clauses throughout), so a
   preserved subject–verb comma would read as *our* typo rather than Migne's, which is the
   opposite of what Pattern 8 exists to achieve. Rendered "the ashes are taken away."
   Recorded here so the decision is visible; if the project wants internal commas carried
   1:1, this is the site to start from.

## E. Vocabulary — the fixed table, and three calls I want ratified

**Followed against my own judgement, per the brief:**

- ***animus* = spirit / *mens* = mind**, held visibly apart everywhere. The site the brief
  named — **1281A**, *in uno eodemque **animo** quasi unus alium consulit, quando illud quod
  per unum **mentis** statum non potuit* — reads "**in one and the same spirit** the one, as
  it were, consults the other, when what he could not attain **through one state of mind**
  he learns through another." The other *animus* in the range is **1282D**, *magnamque
  **animi** devotionem* → "a great devotion of spirit." *anima* is "soul" throughout
  (1274C, 1279C, 1279D); *spiritus* is "spirit" (1278C–1279A, and *afflictio spiritus*).
  **No *animus* / *spiritus* collision occurs in this range** — the brief's two flagged
  sentences are in 0005 and 0034, neither of which I hold.
- ***detrimentum* = decline** at 1279D (*contra similium corruptionum detrimenta*), against
  a strong pull toward "damage" or "loss" in that sentence.
- ***gradus* = step** throughout, including the two chapter heads that use it, where "degree"
  read more naturally.
- ***testa* = clay** and ***aes* = bronze** throughout, including *plantam testeam* →
  "the clay sole."
- ***industria* = effort** at 1276B (*quaelibet humana industria*).
- ***praesumptio* = presumption**: at 1279C, *per veniae **praesumptionem*** is rendered
  "through the presuming upon pardon" rather than "presumption of pardon," because here the
  word is positive (confident reliance) and the English noun alone would have made Richard
  say the opposite of his sentence. The root word is preserved; flagging the wording in case
  a peer prefers strict "presumption."

**Three calls I want ratified for the whole work:**

1. ⭐ ***simulatio* vs *simultas*.** Both occur in this range and they must not collapse.
   1274A/1283B print ***simulatio*** ("the good that is done *ex simulatione*"); 1283B and
   1283D print ***simultas*** in the rhyming triad ***charitas · veritas · simultas***,
   where the sense demanded is plainly the same as *simulatio*, not the classical "grudge,
   enmity." I have rendered **simulatio = "dissimulation"**, **simultas = "pretense"**, and
   **ficta / simulatum = "feigned" / "simulated"**. Rendering *simultas* as "enmity" would
   have been classically correct and would have broken the argument (bronze = *pertinacia*
   and feigned piety everywhere else in the work). **Please ratify or overrule before other
   agents meet *simultas*.**
2. ***intelligentia* = "understanding"** at 1278C (*spiritalis intelligentia* → "spiritual
   understanding"). ⚠ The fixed table assigns **understanding** to ***intellectus***.
   *intellectus* does **not** occur anywhere in 0020–0024, so no collision arises in my
   files, but it will elsewhere. If the work needs them distinguished, *intelligentia* →
   "intelligence" is the natural fallback and this is the site to change.
3. ***theoria*.** 1282B prints *bonis meditationibus **spiritualibusque theoriis***.
   "Theories" is actively misleading in modern English and "contemplations" is already spoken
   for by *contemplatio*, which occurs three times in the same chunk (1280B, 1280D). Rendered
   as **italic Latin, *theoriae***, under the register rule for technical words with no clean
   English equivalent. Ratify or replace.

## F. One structural note

**1281C · the split lemma with the *inquit* inside it.** Migne prints *Gloria,* inquit,
*nostra haec est…* — the catchword, the speech verb, then the rest. English word order
cannot put "Glory" alone in the first span and still construe, so the first span carries
"Our glory" and the second "is this, the testimony of our conscience." The same one-word
shift was needed at 1279A (*Quem ergo,* ait Apostolus, *fructum habuistis…* → "*What fruit
therefore,* says the Apostle, *had you…*"). In both cases **the number of spans, the
position of the *inquit* inside the quotation, and the `[n:]` position are unchanged**
(Pattern 5). Logged as a class, since every chunk of this work will meet it.
