## A. Markers fired

### A1. `[var:]` — Migne's scripture against the received text

**1286A / 1288A (0026) · Dan. 2:43 · *commiscebuntur quidem humano semini***
Vulgate: *commiscebuntur quidem humano **semine*** (ablative). Migne prints the
**dative** *semini*. Rendered "shall be mingled **to** human seed"; `[var: Vulg.
*humano semine*, ablative, "mingled with human seed"; Migne's dative governs the gloss
that follows]` placed after the lemma.
**Why it is material and not cosmetic:** Richard's gloss immediately takes up the
dative — *Ecce quomodo **adhaereat humano semini*** (1288A) — i.e. carnal prudence
*cleaves to* human seed rather than being *mixed with* it. The exposition rests on the
case ending. Rendering the Vulgate ablative here would have detached the gloss from its
own lemma: 7a″ exactly.
Marker placed after the closing italic rather than inside it, because nested italics are
not expressible inside a marker (Pattern 10 note).

**1288C (0027) · Rom. 5:20 / Matt. 24:12 · *ubi abundavit iniquitas, superabundavit et gratia*, `[n: (Matth. XXIV)]`**
Vulgate Rom. 5:20: *ubi autem abundavit **delictum**, superabundavit gratia*. Migne
prints *iniquitas* — the noun of **Matt. 24:12** (*abundavit iniquitas*) — and his own
note points at Matthew, so the plate is quoting a conflation and citing the wrong half
of it. Rendered as printed ("where iniquity abounded"); `[var: Vulg. Rom. 5:20 reads
*delictum*, "offence", where Migne's noun is that of Matt. 24:12, to which his own note
points]`.
**Material** because *iniquitas* is the chapter's hook word: the head is *de profundo
**iniquitatis** ad summum fastigium charitatis*, and the sentence four lines later
repeats *de profundo iniquitatis provehit*. "Offence" would have cut the proof-text
loose from the argument it proves.
Note reproduced verbatim per the `[n:]` rule; the mis-citation is Migne's and is not
corrected.

**1288D (0027) · Dan. 2:44 · *et regnum ejus populo alteri non dabitur***
Vulgate: *et regnum ejus alteri populo non **tradetur***. Migne prints *dabitur* (and
inverts *alteri populo*). `[var: Vulg. *non tradetur*, "shall not be delivered over"]`.
Marked rather than merely logged **because the [cj:] at 1289D hangs on this verb** — the
reader who meets the conjecture there needs to know that the received text has a
different verb in the lemma. Also dropped in Migne: *autem* twice (2:44), unmarked.

**1290B (0027) · Dan. 2:45 · *ostendit regi quae futura sunt postea***
Vulgate: *quae **ventura** sunt postea*. `[var: Vulg. *ventura*, "what shall come to
pass"]`. **Material** because the gloss picks the printed participle straight up:
*ut cum adhuc **futura** est, dum adhuc praecaveri potest, sua homini ruina reveletur*.
The whole of cap. XXXV is about foresight of what *is to be*, not of what *comes*.

**1292C (0028) · Isa. 42:8 · *nec laudem meam sculptili***
Vulgate: *et laudem meam **sculptilibus*** (plural). Migne prints *nec* for *et* and a
**singular** *sculptili*. Rendered "nor my praise to a graven thing"; `[var: Vulg. *et
laudem meam sculptilibus*, "and my praise to graven images", plural]`. The number is
worth marking here because cap. XXXVIII, three sentences on, argues from **plural**
*simulacra gentium* and *simulacra corde gestare*: the plate's singular is the odd man
in its own context.

**1295B (0029) · Isa. 40:6 · *omnis gloria ejus quasi flos feni***
Vulgate: *quasi **flos agri***. `[var: Vulg. *flos agri*, "the flower of the field"]`.
⭐ **The strongest divergence in the range.** Richard adduces Isa. 40:6 solely to
license the equation *fenum* (Ps. 105:20's calf-fodder) = carnal glory: *Quid aliud erit
vitulus **fenum** comedens, nisi humani favoris appetitus, carnalem et temporalem
gloriam ambiens?* With the Vulgate's *flos agri* the second *fenum* is simply not in
the verse and the syllogism has no middle term. The printed divergence is what makes
the argument work; conforming it would have quietly destroyed it.

### A2. `[cj:]` — printed word real, faithful English false (Pattern 18, negation/correlative bar only)

**1289D (0027) · *O felix regnum quod nunquam dissipabitur, quando populo alteri in aeternum dabitur!***
The exclamation praises the kingdom for the very thing the lemma denies. Migne's own
lemma at 1288D reads *regnum ejus populo alteri **non** dabitur*, and his gloss at 1289A
reads it as exclusivity (*fons ille de quo **non** communicat alienus*). The `non` has
fallen out of the exclamation. Rendered as printed, with
`[cj: *dabitur*; read *non dabitur*, "shall not be given"]`.
Inside Wilson's bar: the wrongly-printed word is a **negation** (here a dropped one),
which the 7561 @0648 precedent explicitly admits ("a dropped word is glossable under
this pattern too, because the gloss opens on the word Migne *did* print"). The guard is
satisfied — *dabitur* stands verbatim in the twin.
Alternative construal considered and rejected: that *quando* is the corruption and the
clause is causal. Either way the negation is the thing missing, so the gloss is placed
on the negation.

**1294B (0029) · *sit postmodum in carnibus commodis... videant***
The sentence is a *Quemadmodum enim ... sic* correlative: *Quemadmodum enim in
spiritualibus ... prius vident quae salubriter facere possint, et tamen facere nolint;
**sit** postmodum ... videant quae utiliter facienda sint*. With *sic* the period
construes exactly; with *sit* the apodosis has no correlative and a stranded
subjunctive of *sum*. Rendered "may it be", with `[cj: *sit*; read *sic*, "so"]`.
Inside the bar: **correlative**, the 11064 @1142D *si*/*sic* class precisely.

### A3. `[sic:]` — carried type, with corpus controls

**1286D (0026) · *somno et otio torpore***
The clause is a list of infinitives — *vestiri velle, madere, **torpore**, irasci,
indignari*. *torpore* is an ablative noun stranded among infinitives and has no slot;
this is 11632's *sternis*-for-*sterilis* shape, not `fuges`/`munde`.
`to grow numb [sic: *torpore*] with sleep and idleness`.
**7a⁗ discharged:** the marker took the limb's only predicate, so the sense ("to grow
numb") stands in plain words beside it. Conjecture: *torpere*.
Control: *torpore* is common (327 files) — the frequency control does **not** support
this marker, and I say so: it rests entirely on the syntactic-slot test, not on rarity.
This is the weakest of my four `[sic:]`.

**1287A (0026) · *in agnoque labore***
`acquire in [sic: *agnoque*] labour`. Control: **1 file in the whole PL TEI, and it is
ours** (`grep -rlw agnoque sources/pl/tei/` → `11535.xml`). A hapax at this site is the
plate's defect. Conjecture uncertain — *magnoque* is the nearest shape and gives
"in great labour too", parallel to *multo tempore*; *angore quoque* is possible. I do
not claim either.
7a⁗ check: the quarantine takes a modifier only; the clause keeps its verb, has no
negation, no conjunction. Safe.

**1294B (0029) · *temporalibis lucris***
`in temporal [sic: *temporalibis*] gains`. Control: **1 file in the PL TEI, ours.**
Non-word for *temporalibus*. 7a⁗ discharged — the adjective's sense stands in plain
words beside the marker so the phrase is not left headless.

**1294D (0029) · *et tamem idololatriae simile***
`and yet [sic: *tamem*] what he practised in deed was like to idolatry`. Non-word for
*tamen*. **Control is weaker and I flag it as such:** *tamem* occurs in 38 PL TEI files,
so it is a recurring compositor/transcription slip across the corpus rather than a
hapax. I still marked it, because Pattern 7's non-word class turns on the form not
being a word at all, and *tamem* is attested nowhere as a Latin word; but the
corpus-control argument that settled *agnoque* is not available here.
**7a⁗ discharged:** the marker swallowed the sentence's adversative, so "yet" stands in
plain words beside it — without that, the page would have said Richard thinks soundly
*and* his deed resembles idolatry, with no contrast.

## B. Conjectures DECLINED — census candidates, named with reasons

These are all real printed words that make a false or unconstruable claim, and all fall
**outside** Wilson's 2026-08-15 bar (negation or correlative only). I have not widened
the bar. Each is a Pattern 18 census candidate.

1. ⭐ **1295A (0029) · *inde minus desideriorum turba corrumpatur*** — **the most
   consequential declined site in the range.** The clause as printed says the crowd of
   desires is *less* corrupted by the source that illuminates the understanding. **Migne's
   own chapter head, two lines above, says the opposite**: *CAPUT XL. Quod saepe
   desiderium **corrumpitur** unde intelligentia illuminatur.* The likely reading is
   ***imis***, answering *in summis* in the same sentence (*unde **in summis**
   intelligentia ... illuminatur, inde **in imis** desideriorum turba corrumpatur*) — a
   clean *summis/imis* antithesis that the printed *minus* destroys. Declined because
   *minus* is a comparative adverb, not a negation or a correlative; but it is the
   founding 11081 *munde* shape exactly (real adverb, fluent English, false assertion),
   and if the bar is ever widened this site should be the first one revisited.
   Rendered as printed: "the crowd of desires is the less corrupted."

2. **1293D (0029) · *Quartum quod quasi varium sit*** — the seven-fold scheme is
   os · oculi · aures · **nares** · manus · pedes · guttur (Richard sets it out in that
   order at 1293A), and the fourth item's content is *prudenter discernere*, which he
   has just assigned to the nostrils (*ad nares discretio*). ***narium*** for *varium*
   is near-certain. Declined: not a negation or correlative. Rendered "which is as it
   were a various thing."

3. **1287A (0026) · *Quot timores, quot honores, quot algores hostis, noctis et
   frigoris*** — the three genitives pair off *timores*/hostis, ?/noctis,
   *algores*/frigoris, so *honores* is almost certainly ***horrores***. "Honours of the
   night" is fluent and false. Declined on the bar; rendered "how many honours".

4. **1284D (0025) · *imperium, quia negligentius urgere*** — in a series of
   *quia* + object + *potes* clauses (*bonos disponere*, *malis resistere*, *rebelles
   revincere*), the fourth has a comparative **adverb** where the pattern wants an
   accusative object; *negligentiores* ("the negligent") would restore it. Rendered
   literally, "because thou canst urge the more remissly". Declined.

5. **1289D (0027) · *nemo uritur!*** — the exclamation runs through the 1 Cor. 13 list
   (*nemo aemulatur · non agitur perperam · nemo inflatur · nemo ambitiosus quaerit quae
   sua sunt*) and then prints *nemo uritur*, "no one is burned", where the list's next
   item is *non irritatur*. ***irritatur*** is the obvious reading. Declined; rendered
   "no one is burned!" The negation itself is intact, so 7a is not at issue.

6. **1294B (0029) · *in carnibus commodis*** — the parallel is *in spiritualibus et
   animae lucris* ‖ *in [carnalibus] commodis et temporalibus lucris*. *carnibus*
   (fem.) cannot agree with *commodis* as a noun, but *commodus* is also an adjective,
   so the phrase does construe — absurdly — as "in advantageous fleshes", and that is
   how I rendered it. **No `[sic:]`**, because it renders in place and the absurdity
   leaves the defect visible (Pattern 12's own test). Conjecture: *carnalibus commodis*.

7. **1289D (0027) · *ad debiti finis talum non perducitur*** — *ad calcem perducere* is
   the idiom, and *calcem* would be expected. **But I decline even the conjecture**: this
   is a treatise on a statue read from its golden head down to its feet of iron and clay,
   and *talus* ("ankle-bone", "heel") may well be deliberate. Rendered "to the heel of
   its due end". Recorded here so a later reader does not "fix" it.

## C. Passages that will not construe — rendered as closely as the words allow

1. **1289C (0027) · *Si aut in argento illud opus intelligitur quod fit ex sola
   deliberatione, non autem ex dilectione, non fit tam ex dilectione quam ex veritate,
   quid tam consentaneum charitati quae congaudet veritati?*** The protasis contains two
   clauses that contradict each other (*non autem ex dilectione* … *non fit tam ex
   dilectione quam ex veritate*), and *Si aut* is itself odd (*Si autem*?). **Both
   negations carried** per 7a, with the result that the English is as self-contradictory
   as the Latin. No marker fired: nothing here is a non-word, and the defect is not
   localizable to one word, so no `[cj:]` guard could be satisfied. Escalated rather
   than repaired.

2. **1294D (0029) · *Ecce quam sane, et de divinis sentit, et tamem idololatriae simile
   quod per opus exercuit.*** The second limb has no finite verb (*est* must be
   understood) and *tamem* is carried under `[sic:]` (A3). Rendered with the ellipsis
   supplied as plain text, no brackets. The sentence remains loose in English because it
   is loose in Migne.

3. **1286A (0025) · *Caetera autem quae sequuntur utramque lectionem in eamdem
   sententiam concurrunt*** — *utramque lectionem* is a bare accusative with no governing
   verb (*concurrunt* is intransitive). Rendered as an accusative of respect, "run
   together, for both readings, into the same sense." No marker; Pattern 9 territory at
   worst.

## G. Fixed vocabulary — where I followed the table, and one tension

- ***mens*** = mind · ***animus*** = spirit · ***anima*** = soul, kept visibly distinct
  throughout. They collide inside single clauses twice in my range and both were handled
  consciously:
  - **1287C (0026)** *cum praevaricatrix **mens** primo ad saecularia desideria
    revertitur, ad tolerandos mundi labores **animus** obduratur* → "when the
    transgressing **mind** first turns back … the **spirit** is hardened".
  - **1288B (0026)** *dum interna **animi** anxietate … desaeviunt* → "by the inward
    anxiety of the **spirit**".
  Neither of the two *animus*/*spiritus* collision sentences named in the brief (0005,
  0034) falls in my range; *spiritus* occurs here only as **Spiritus sanctus** (1289B),
  so no collision arose.
- ***aes*** = **bronze** throughout, including *simulationis aes* (1289C) and *aeream
  pertinaciam* (1289C). ***testa*** = **clay**; ***lutum*** rendered **mire** to keep the
  pair distinct in *ferrum mistum testae ex luto* ("iron mingled with clay of mire") and
  in *Quasi enim lutum in testam duratur* ("mire is hardened into clay"). Flagging this
  for the merge as a **work-level call I would like ratified**: the table fixes *testa*
  but is silent on *lutum*, and the two words are glossed against each other here.
- ***gradus*** = **step** (1284A head *corruptionis gradibus* → "steps of corruption";
  1288C *quibusdam profectuum gradibus* → "certain steps of advance").
- ***virtus*** = virtue · ***gratia*** = grace · ***devotio*** = devotion ·
  ***discretio*** = discretion · ***industria*** = effort (1292C *in aliqua industria* →
  "in any effort") · ***praesumere*** = to presume · ***obduratio/obdurare*** =
  hardening / to harden · ***superbia*** = pride.
- **Tension logged, not decided:** the four-fold vice scheme of cap. XXXII needs four
  fixed English words and the table names none of them. I used **arrogance**
  (*arrogantia*) · **boasting** (*jactantia*) · **obstinacy** (*pertinacia*) ·
  **savagery** (*saevitia*), and held them through cap. XXXIV where all four recur
  against the four metals. These four recur across the whole of Book I; **if a peer
  agent has fixed different words, mine should yield to whichever came first** — but the
  set must be uniform, because Richard's argument at 1289C is a point-by-point matching
  of the four vices to the four clauses of 1 Cor. 13.
- ***detrimentum*** does not occur in my range.

  *Primum*–*Sextum*. The seventh is the unnumbered clause that follows — *ad omnia haec
  divinum auxilium implorare debemus* — matching *ad guttur supplicatio* in the list of
  seven offices at 1293A. Not a lacuna; nothing supplied.

  expressed antecedent (*animas* understood). Pattern 9: gender has no English exponent,
  no marker, no churn.
- Chapter numerals follow the plate exactly (XXXII–XL, arabic nowhere). No head printed
  without its closing period in this range.
