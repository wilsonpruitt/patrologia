### A1. `[var:]` — Migne's scripture against the received text (11 sites)

**1296D (0030) · *Quis novit sensum Domini, aut quis consiliarius ejus fuit?* `[n: (Isai. XL.)]`**
Rendered as printed ("Who hath known the sense of the Lord, or who hath been his
counsellor?"). `[var: Vulg. Isa. XL:13 opens *Quis adjuvit spiritum Domini*, "Who hath
helped the spirit of the Lord"; the wording printed here is that of Rom. XI:34, *Quis

**Material**: the plate cites Isaiah and prints Paul. Isa. 40:13 in the Vulgate asks who

*sensus* — exists only in Rom. 11:34. The note is Migne's and is reproduced unchanged.
⚠ **Paired site: 1305A (0032) prints the same verse as *Quis cognovit sensum Domini*,
again under `[n: (Isai. XL)]`.** The two printings differ from each other (*novit* /
*cognovit*) and **both** differ from Isaiah, so both are marked, each var naming the
other. This is the "Migne re-quotes his own lemma against himself" habit the brief names,
here compounded by a mis-citation that is constant across both printings.

**1302B (0032) · Job 29:16 · *Causam quam nesciebam, diligenter investigabam***
Vulgate: *causam quam nesciebam **diligentissime** investigabam*. `[var: Vulg.
*diligentissime*, "most diligently"]`.
**Material** because the proof-text is adduced for the superlative: the sentence that
introduces it is *Ecce quam diligenter, ecce quam instanter quaerit quod nescit*. Migne's
positive flattens exactly the degree the argument is proving.

**1303C (0032) · Ps. 118:18 · *Illumina oculos meos, et considerabo mirabilia de lege tua***
`[var: Vulg. Ps. CXVIII:18 *Revela oculos meos*, "Uncover mine eyes"; this work prints
*Revela* at 1253A and at 1262C]`.
**The work is its own control here, and it settles the case.** The same verse, with the
same `[n: (Psal. CXVIII)]`, is printed **three times** in this work: *Revela* at 1253A
(0009) and 1262C (0014), *Illumina* here. Two printings agree with the Vulgate and one
does not, so the divergence is local to this setting — *Illumina oculos meos* is the
opening of **Ps. 12:4**, i.e. a contamination from a neighbouring verse, not a variant
copy-text running through the work. Rendered "Enlighten mine eyes."
⚑ **Merge note for the reconciliation step:** the two earlier printings were Englished
"Unveil mine eyes" (0009) and "Reveal mine eyes" (0014). Those two render the *same*
Latin two ways and should be reconciled to one form; mine is deliberately different
because the Latin is different, and must not be swept into agreement with them.

**1304A (0032) · Ps. 115 · *Vota mea reddam in conspectu omnis populi ejus***
`[var: Vulg. Ps. CXV:14 *Vota mea Domino reddam coram omni populo ejus*, "I will pay my
vows to the Lord before all his people"]`.
Migne drops *Domino* and prints *in conspectu omnis populi* for *coram omni populo* —
the wording of Ps. 21:26 (*vota mea reddam in conspectu timentium eum*) bleeding into
Ps. 115. Rendered as printed. Material because the addressee (*Domino*) is the thing the
sentence is about: Richard adduces it as the *exsecutio* of the vow sworn to God.

**1305A (0032) · Isa. XL / Rom. XI:34** — see the paired entry at 1296D above.

**1305A–B (0032) · 2 Pet. 1:20 · *quia prophetia Scripturae propria interpretatione non fit***
`[var: Vulg. *quod omnis prophetia Scripturae*, "that no prophecy of Scripture"]`.
Migne drops ***omnis***. Rendered as printed: "that prophecy of Scripture is not made by
private interpretation." **Material and it is a scope change, not a stylistic one**: with
*omnis … non* the Vulgate says *no* prophecy is so made — a universal negative, which is
exactly the universal restraint Richard is citing it for (*quomodo pariter **omnes** ab
ejusmodi praesumptione compescat*). Without *omnis* the printed sentence is a weaker,
unquantified claim. Per 7a the negation printed (*non*) is carried and nothing is
supplied; the quantifier's absence is recorded in the marker, not repaired in the text.

**1305B–C (0033) · Dan. 4:5 · *qui habet spiritum sanctorum in semetipso***
`[var: Vulg. Dan. IV:5 has *deorum* before *sanctorum*, "the spirit of the holy gods";
Richard restores the word in both of his own re-quotations below]`.
**The strongest site in the range, and it is a within-plate diff.** The lemma as printed
drops *deorum*; then the very next sentence glosses it as *Ecce **spiritum deorum
sanctorum** habere dicitur* (1305C), and the re-quotation at 1305D–1306A prints *Qui

Richard's argument is that *dii sancti* excludes *omnes dii gentium, daemonia*, and that
the *sancti* here are men called gods (*Ego dixi: Dii estis*). Rendered as printed ("the
spirit of the holy ones") with the divergence marked; the two re-quotations are rendered
with "gods", because that is what they print. Only the lemma is marked, per the brief's
rule that the divergent printing takes the marker and not both.

**1305D (0033) · Rom. 8:26 · *qui interpellat pro nobis gemitibus inenarrabilibus* `[n: (Rom. VIII)]`**
`[var: Vulg. Rom. VIII:26 reads *postulat*, "asketh"; *interpellat*, "maketh

A conflation of Rom. 8:26 and 8:34 under a chapter-level note that is correct for both.
Marked although the quotation is **not** in italics — Pattern 14 has no formatting

**1308D (0034) · 1 John 3:2 · *Fratres, filii Dei sumus, et nondum apparet quid erimus***

**1309A (0034) · 1 Cor. 6:17 · *Qui adhaeret Deo, unus spiritus est***

Dei** intuitus est … et sic **aperientur** oculi ejus* → `[var: Vulg. Num. XXIV:4 has no

*aperientur*]`.

perfect, "hath beheld", where this second printing prints the present *videt*, "seeth";
the printing at 1309C keeps the perfect]`.

the second printing and against the first printing's future *aperientur*. Both are
rendered exactly as printed, the first with "shall be opened" and the second with "are
opened", so the reader can see the plate disagreeing with itself across five lines.

### A2. `[sic:]` — carried type (3 sites), each checked against the whole PL TEI

**1298C (0030) · *quia perfores aditus ad interiora patet***
Carried: `through the doors [sic: *perfores*] the entrance to the inner parts lies open`.
**Control run:** `perfores` occurs in exactly **two** files in the 87M-word PL TEI —
11535 (here) and 7132. It is a real form (2 sg. subj. of *perforare*, "thou mayest bore
through") and therefore not a non-word, but it has **no grammatical slot**: the clause
needs *per fores*, "through the doors", which is what the whole passage is about (*Daniel
… in regis foribus observat*, *Danielem regis foribus praesidere*, *civitatis nostrae
foribus praesidentem* — *fores* four times in fifteen lines). This is Pattern 10's fused
direction, and Pattern 12's "renders only by hiding the defect" test.
**7a⁗ compliance:** the marker swallows a preposition and its object, so the sense is
supplied in plain words beside it ("through the doors"). Without that the clause would
have lost both.

**1303A (0032) · *multam ibi moram invectere***
Carried: `weaves in [sic: *invectere*] much delay there`.
**Control run:** `invectere` occurs in **one** file in the whole PL TEI — this one. A
form unique to this plate at this site is the plate's defect (the standard this work has
been using). Conjecture: ***innectere***, "to weave in / entangle" — *moram innectere* is
idiomatic and gives exactly the required sense of protracted, unnoticed delay.
**7a⁗ compliance:** the marked run is the **only infinitive** of the clause (*antequam
advertat quid intendat, multam ibi moram invectere*), so the English supplies the verb
in plain words beside the marker. The conjecture stays here, not in the text.

**1303B (0032) · *quasi per somnium cornere***
Carried: `to discern [sic: *cornere*] the divine judgments`.
**Control run:** `cornere` occurs in **one** file in the whole PL TEI — this one.
Conjecture ***cernere***, near-certain: the antithesis is *aliud est … cornere, et aliud
… perpendere … dijudicare*, and the parallel passage the sentence points back to
(*sicut in alterius visionis expositione jam diximus*) uses seeing-verbs throughout.
**7a⁗ compliance:** again the marked run is the limb's only verb, so "to discern" stands
in plain words beside the marker.

### A3. `[cj:]` — one site, and it is offered for ratification, not asserted

**1302B (0032) · *quaerit quod nescit, nisi prius desistit, donec somnii sui solutionem inveniat***
Rendered: `he seeks what he knows not, unless [cj: *nisi*; read *nec*, "nor does he first
desist"] he first desists, until he finds the solution of his dream which he seeks`.
The printed *nisi* is a real word and renders in place, so Pattern 12 has nothing to
wrap; and the English it yields **asserts the opposite of the sentence's own point**,
which is that Nabuchodonosor did *not* leave off (*Ecce quam diligenter, ecce quam
instanter quaerit*, and at 1302A *videamus quam instanter Nabuchodonosor quaesivit quod
nescivit*). The idiom wanted is *nec prius desistit donec … inveniat*.
**Why I fired it rather than declining it:** the word that went wrong is a **negation**
(*nec*), which is the class Wilson's 2026-08-15 ruling explicitly brought inside the bar
even where the sentence is incoherent rather than merely false — the 7561 @0648 *solum*
site is the same shape (a printed word that survives, a negation that fell out). The
verbatim-in-the-twin guard is satisfied by *nisi*.
⚠ **If the bar is read narrowly — that *nisi* is itself in 7a's negation list and so is a
substitution rather than a loss — this site should be demoted to a declined census
candidate.** It is the only `[cj:]` in the range and nothing else depends on it.

## C. Vocabulary: the collisions this range was flagged for

### C1. *animus* / *spiritus* at 1309A (0034) — the second of the work's two collisions

Printed: *quanta sit virtus verae devotionis … quae **animum humanum Deo conjungit, et cum
eo quasi unum spiritum efficit**. *Qui* enim *adhaeret Deo, **unus spiritus** est*.* The
same collision stands in the chapter head (*quae animum humanum Deo conjungit*).

**What I did: rendered both "spirit"** — "which joins the **human spirit** to God, and
makes it with him as it were **one spirit**." The chapter head follows suit.

**Why, and why my site differs from 0005's.** The 0005 agent met *ex animi impetu* beside
*ex spiritus sui impetu* and ruled both "spirit" because Richard's *eodem/eamdem* there
**assert the two are the same impulse**, so differentiating would silently deny an
identity the text states. Here the logic runs the other way: the two words name two
*different* things — a human *animus* and the *spiritus* that results — which are
**joined into one**. So the argument for one English word is not identity but the
**conclusion**: the sentence's whole force is that what is joined becomes *unus spiritus*,
and it is 1 Cor. 6:17 that licenses the claim. If *animus* were rendered "mind" here
(as 11536 would have), the proof-text would be about spirit and the claim about mind, and
the *unum spiritum efficit* would have nothing to attach to. The union is the point, and
one English word is what carries it.
⚠ Note that the same sentence is where the plate has assimilated the proof-text to the
claim (*Deo* for *Domino*, §A1 above). The two observations reinforce each other: this is
a sentence in which quotation and gloss have been drawn together, and both moves are
recorded rather than smoothed.

### C2. *animus* / *mens* — kept visibly distinct at all 14 sites in the range

The range holds chunk 0030's chapter head, the sentence the brief names as forcing the
ruling: *Quod quidquid **animum** per cogitationem pulsat, **mentis** devotionem excitare
debeat* → "That whatever strikes the **spirit** through thought ought to arouse the
devotion of the **mind**." The body repeats the collision verbatim at 1298C (*omne quod
animum per cogitationem pulsat … mentis devotionem excitat*) and again at 1298D (*devotio
mentis*), and 0031 has *quando eam **animus** jam ad manum habet* immediately after
*quae **mentis** arbitrio subjacet* (1301D). **Every one is rendered spirit / mind.** No
site in the range resisted the distinction; the head and its own body sentence are the
proof that the plate is using the two words deliberately, three lines apart, of the same
act.

### C3. *sacramentum* — I followed the ratified table against a shipped neighbour

Dan. 2:47's *quia potuisti aperire **sacramentum** hoc* falls twice in 0030 (1296B,
1296D) and is rendered **"this sacrament"**, per ratified item 5 (*sacramentum* =
sacrament, kept apart from *mysterium* = mystery).
⚠ **This disagrees with the immediately preceding chunk.** 0029 renders the same phrase
"this **secret**". The rest of the work is with me: 0008 twice ("concerning this
sacrament", "calls that matter a sacrament"), 0011 ("the divine sacraments and
mysteries"), and 0014 twice — where **the very same Dan. 2 phrase** *sacramentum hoc* is
already Englished "this sacrament". So 0029 is a single drift against five sites plus the
ruling, and it is a **merge-step fix in 0029, not in my chunks**. Flagged here rather
than acted on because 0029 is committed and outside my range.

### C4. Other fixed-vocabulary calls followed, one of them against my ear

- ***sensus* = sense** throughout, including inside the quotation *Quis novit **sensum
  Domini*** (1296D, 1305A), where every English Bible says "the **mind** of the Lord".
  **Followed the table against my own judgment**, and it is the right call here for a
  reason beyond consistency: Richard immediately glosses the verse with *nemo novit quae
  sunt Dei nisi Spiritus Dei*, and the range's other *sensus* sites — *ex intimo animae
  sensu* (1295D–1296A), *intellectualis sensus* (the head of Book II cap. II, and 1300D,
  1309C), *sensu exteriori* / *interiori sensu* (1302C) — are one continuous thread about
  the faculty of sense. Rendering the lemma "mind" would have imported *mens* into a
  sentence that does not contain it, in a work whose whole seam is keeping those apart.
- *scientia* = learning (1305B *ad divinorum judiciorum scientiam*), *cognitio* =
  knowledge (1301C *ad plenam sui cognitionem*) — they stand four lines apart in 0031.
- *enodatio* = unravelling (heads of capp. V and VI) vs *solutio* = solution (the Dan. 4
  lemma throughout 0032) — they stand in the same sentence at 1304C.
- *magisterium* = mastery, at 1297B (*magisterii arcem*), 1298B twice, and 1305B (*sine
  ejus magisterio, qui eas inspiravit*) — the last is the head-word of cap. VI.
- *mentis excessus* = excess of mind (1300A, 1300C — the only two in the range).
- *intelligentia* = understanding (1297A, 1302C, 1303C, 1307C, 1307D); *intellectualis*
  = intellectual (1300D, 1309C). No *intellectus* in the range.
- *conjectura*/*conjicere* = conjecture (1296B), kept apart from *interpretatio*.
- *arbitrium* alone = "the will", after 0023's shipped *arbitrii potestas* / *arbitrii
  libertas* → "the power of the will" / "the liberty of the will"; *liberum arbitrium* =
  free will (1301D).
- **Proper names in Migne's Latin form**: *Nabuchodonosor*, *Balthassar*, *Sidrac* /
  *Sidrach*, *Misach*, *Abdenago*, *Balaam*, *Urias*. *Balthassar* matters twice over in
  0034, where the whole chapter is the etymology *capillus capitis*; the Latin name is
  kept beside the translated gloss ("Balthassar is interpreted *hair of the head*") so
  the gloss still explains something.
- *Babylon … **confusio*** (1297D) — etymology gloss, translated ("interpreted
  *confusion*") with the name it explains standing beside it.

## D. Passages that would not construe, and other plate observations

**1305C (0033) · *Sed profecto qui hoc loco designantur. Spiritum sanctum habere
creduntur;*** — Migne's full stop falls **inside** the sentence, stranding a relative
clause with no main verb and leaving *creduntur* without its subject. Rendered exactly so:
"But assuredly they who are designated in this place. They are believed to have the Holy
Spirit;". **Pattern 8: the mark is not deleted and nothing is supplied.** The sense the
two fragments carry between them is plain and is not in doubt; only the punctuation is.

**1309A (0034) · *vide quam recte Balthassar per effectum dicitur. Per quem quilibet in
filiorum libertatem adoptatur.*** — the same shape: a full stop breaking a relative
clause off from its antecedent. Kept, and the English breaks in the same place.

**1300A (0031) · *quia per somnium corporis sensus corporeus sopitur*** — *somnium*
("dream") stands where the sense wants *somnum* ("sleep"): it is sleep that lulls the
bodily sense, and the very next clause distinguishes them (*Prius dormimus quam somnium
videamus*), while 1300C of this same chunk prints *post excussum **somnum***.
**Rendered "dream" per the fixed table and Pattern 7** — "Because through the dream the
bodily sense of the body is lulled" — awkward, and deliberately so.
⭐ **The work's own control settles it as authorial usage, not a defect, and I record the
control because I nearly called it one.** 0014 argues the identical point in the identical
words — *Quia per **somnium** sopitur sensus corporeus, recte per illum mentis excessum
intelligimus* — so Richard uses *somnium* of sleep in both places, and the shipped 0014
English renders it "by the dream" exactly as mine does. **No marker, no conjecture, and
NOT a census candidate.** Two agents independently reaching the same rendering on the same
construction is the outcome the fixed table exists to produce.

**1302C (0032) · *quae sensu exteriori percepi possunt*** — *percepi* for *percipi*.
**Control run: `percepi possunt` occurs elsewhere in the PL TEI**, and *percepi* itself in
five-plus files, so the e/i interchange in the passive infinitive is an attested medieval
orthography, not this plate's defect. **Pattern 9: rendered normally ("can be perceived"),
no italics, no marker.**

**1302C (0032) · *Illa autem velut intus sunt qua: nisi interiori sensu…*** — the token
`qua:` is *quae*: it is the ligature **quæ** misread by the digitization, and `qua:`
occurs in **16 files** across the PL TEI, which makes it an OCR artifact class of our
source rather than anything Migne set. **No `[sic:]`** — a defect in our files is never
attributed to his plate. Rendered "which". Recorded here so a later pass can decide
whether it belongs in `data/tei-patches/`.

**1298B (0030) · *vel communi infirmitati deseruimus*** — with a dative and in a present-
tense series (*consulimus … deseruimus*), this is *deservimus*, "we serve", with **u for
v** — the ordinary orthography, Pattern 9. Rendered "serve". *Deseruimus* read as the
perfect of *desero* cannot govern a dative and would reverse the sense of the clause; it
is recorded here so that nobody later "restores" a desertion Richard is not describing.

**1309B (0034) · *qui frequentibus relevationibus divinitus relevari laetificarique
meruerunt*** — *relevatio*/*relevare* are real words ("relief", "to relieve") and render
in place, so they are rendered ("relieved and gladdened by frequent relievings"); but the
whole paragraph is about **revelation** (*divinae revelationis desiderio*, *ex
multitudine revelationum suarum*, *lumen revelantis gratiae*), and *revelationibus …
revelari* is almost certainly what stood. **Conjecture declined** (not a negation, not a
correlative; the printed reading asserts nothing false, only something tautologous).
**Named as a census candidate.**

**1307D (0034) · *quid, inquit, per effectum istum David scelestius*** — *effectum* where
Gregory's sense wants *affectum*; a real word, renders in place ("through this effect"),
declined, named.

**1300B (0031) · *cum divino lumine tactus mentis intelligentia perspiciat*** — masculine
*tactus* against feminine *intelligentia*. **Pattern 9: Latin grammatical gender has no
English exponent.** Nothing to render either way, no marker, entry marked so a later
sweep does not churn it.

## E. Structural notes

1. **`## LIBER SECUNDUS.` closes 0030 with no text after it**, and the two blank lines the

   head conventions.
2. **`## CAPUT PRIMUM.` recurs here** (0031) as Book Two's first chapter, rendered
   `## CHAPTER ONE.` — the second of the work's three. Not a defect; the plate restarts.
3. **`## CAPUT X` (0034) prints with no period after the numeral**, unlike every other head
   in the range. **Preserved**: `## CHAPTER X *That most of all…*`, no period supplied
   (Pattern 8 applied to heads).
4. **Standalone column-anchor lines** — `[1298C]`, `[1299A]`, `[1301B]`, `[1307C]`,

   inline in the English. Line structure is 1:1 with the twin in all five chunks.
5. **Interpolated particle inside a lemma** — `*Nescit* namque *homo finem suum…*` (1301A)

   *omnia suffert*` → "*Charity* indeed *beareth all things*"): "*Man knoweth not* indeed

   match the twin exactly in all five chunks.

   lemmata; the two supplied pairs in 0030 ("your" and "our", 1296A) mark words being
