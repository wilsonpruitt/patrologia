## A. Markers fired — `[var: …]` (Pattern 14). Eleven in all.

### A1. 1310B (0035) · Dan. 4:6 · ***quem** ego scio quod spiritum … habeas*
Vulg. *Baltassar princeps ariolorum, **quoniam** ego scio quod spiritum sanctorum deorum
habeas in te*. Migne prints the relative *quem*, which leaves "whom I know that thou hast
the spirit of the holy gods in thee" without a governing verb. Rendered as printed;
`[var: Vulg. *quoniam ego scio*, …]`.
**Material** because the whole chapter hangs on the king's *reason* for demanding the
interpretation of Daniel rather than of the soothsayers — Richard re-quotes the clause at
1311A precisely as a causal premise (*si veraciter constat quod per Nabuchodonosor dicitur:
Scio quod spiritum … habeas in te*). The causal *quoniam* is the hook; Migne's relative is not.

### A2. 1311B (0035) · Mark 11:24 · *Amen dico vobis, quidquid orantes petieritis … et fiet vobis*
Vulg. *Propterea dico vobis, omnia quaecumque orantes petitis, credite quia accipietis, et
**evenient** vobis*. Four divergences in one clause (opening formula, *quidquid* for *omnia
quaecumque*, future perfect *petieritis* for present *petitis*, *fiet* for *evenient*).
One `[var:]` covering the run.
⚠ **Cross-agent note:** the launch brief records that **chunk 0044 prints *petitis*** for the
same verse in the same work. So Migne's plate quotes Mark 11:24 twice with different verb
forms; the 0044 agent should diff against this entry rather than treat it as fresh.
Migne's note reads `[n: (Marc. VI)]` for a verse in Mark **XI**; the note is Migne's citation
and is reproduced verbatim, uncorrected.

### A3. 1311B (0035) · Dan. 4:6 re-quoted · *solutiones eorum* for the lemma's *solutionem eorum*
Migne re-quotes his own lemma four columns later with the plural. Vulg. and the lemma at
1310B both have the singular. Marked on the **divergent printing only**, per the brief's rule.

### A4. 1312D (0036) · Dan. 4:7 re-quoted · *ecce arbor **magna** in medio terrae*
The lemma at 1311D (and Vulg. 4:7) read *ecce arbor in medio terrae*; *magna* belongs to
v. 8. Migne's re-quote pulls it forward, and the gloss that follows immediately
(*Quid intelligemus per magnitudinem arboris?*) is built on it. Marked; rendered as printed.

### A5. 1313B (0036) · Phil. 4:12–13 · *Ubique et in omnibus **instructus** sum. Scio humiliari…*
Vulg. *Scio **et** humiliari, scio et abundare (ubique et in omnibus **institutus** sum) et
satiari, et esurire, et abundare, et penuriam pati.* Two divergences: **instructus** for
*institutus*, and the parenthetical clause hoisted to the **front** of the quotation.

### A6. 1314C (0036) · Phil. 4:12 re-quoted · *Scio humiliari, scio et abundare **ubique**, et in omnibus instructus sum*
**⚠ The diff the brief asked for.** The two printings are NOT the same text:

| | 1313B | 1314C |
|---|---|---|
| order | *ubique…instructus sum* **first**, then *Scio humiliari* | *Scio humiliari* **first**, then *ubique…instructus sum* |
| extent | continues *et penuriam pati* + v. 13 *Omnia possum…* | stops at *instructus sum* |
| *ubique* | with *in omnibus* | pulled back to sit with *abundare* |
| note | `[n: (Philipp. IV)]` | `[n: (Philipp. VI)]` |

Both keep *instructus* and both drop the Vulgate's *et* after *Scio*, so the shared
divergence is the plate's habit, not a one-off. Both marked, each on its own difference.
Migne's second note cites **Philippians VI**, a chapter the epistle does not have;
reproduced verbatim, uncorrected.

### A7. 1313B–C (0036) · Eccl. 1:16 · *mens mea contemplata est multa sapienter et **didicit***
Vulg. *et **didici*** — first person. Migne's third person makes *mens mea* the subject of
the learning. Rendered "and hath learned"; marked. **Material**: Richard adduces the verse
for the tree's *proceritas*, i.e. for what the mind reaches, and the third person keeps the
mind rather than the man as the agent of the whole clause. Word order *praecessi sapientia
omnes* (Vulg. *praecessi omnes sapientia*) is cosmetic and took no marker.

### A8. 1314D (0037) · Prov. 15:2 · *Os enim sapientis ornat scientiam*
Vulg. *Lingua sapientium ornat scientiam.* Migne prints **mouth** for *tongue* and the
**singular** wise man for the plural. Marked. Material for the gloss, which is running on
*verba* / *folia* and on the single tree ("in the leaves, words").

### A9. 1317C (0038) · Sir. 34:11 · *Qui non est tentatus, **quid** scit?*
Vulg. *Qui tentatus non est, **qualia** scit?* Rendered "what doth he know?"; marked.
Migne prints the `?` and it is kept (Pattern 8).

### A10. 1317C–D (0038) · Sir. 34:9 · *Vir in multis expertus **cogitavit** multa*
Vulg. *cogitabit*, future. Perfect for future; marked. Printed roman, not italic —
Pattern 14 has no formatting precondition, and the clause is adduced as a proof-text.

### A11. 1318D (0038) · Dan. 4:11 · *dispergite **fructum** ejus*
Vulg. *dispergite **fructus** ejus*, plural. Marked once, at the lemma. The whole exposition
of cap. XXII (1321C–D) is built on the singular — *fructusque dispergitur*, *Fructus
dispergitur* twice — so the singular is the reading the gloss needs, and the re-quote at
1322A repeats it. **Deliberately not marked a second time in 0039**: it is one divergence,
not two.

### A12. 1319A (0038) · Prov. 28:14 + 27:1 · *Nescit enim quid futura pariat dies*
Prov. 28:14 ends at *Beatus homo qui semper est pavidus*; the second clause is Prov. 27:1,
and there it reads *ignoras enim quid **superventura** pariat dies* — second person, and a
different participle. Migne conflates the two verses, alters both words, and cites only
Prov. XXVIII. Rendered as printed; marked.

### A13. 1319B (0039) · Dan. 4:10 re-quoted · *Ecce, inquit, **vigil sanctus** de coelo descendit*
The lemma at 1318D and Vulg. print *vigil, **et** sanctus* — two subjects, "a watcher, and a
holy one". Migne's re-quote drops the *et*, yielding one subject, "a holy watcher". Marked;
rendered as printed ("a holy watcher"). **Material and immediately so**: the next two
sentences argue from *sanctus* and *de coelo* as two separate warrants (*cum sanctum legas…*;
*ex eo quod sanctus et de coelo descendisse perhibetur*), and the third printing of the same
clause, eight lines later at 1319C, restores the *et*. The plate is inconsistent with itself
inside one column band.

### A14. 1319D (0039) · Dan. 4:11 re-quoted · ***Praecidite** arborem*
The lemma at 1318D and Vulg. print *Succidite arborem, et praecidite ramos ejus* — *succidere*
of the trunk, *praecidere* of the branches. At 1319D Migne re-quotes the command as
*Praecidite arborem*, transferring the branch-verb to the trunk; at 1320C, seven lines later,
he prints *Succidite arborem* again. Marked on the divergent printing only. **Material**:
cap. XXII's whole scheme depends on the two verbs staying apart (*Arbor succiditur et ramus
praeciditur, quando virtus in occulto, et virtutis opus deficit in aperto*). Rendered as
printed ("Cut off the tree") both at 1319D and in its echo two lines later, since Migne
prints *praecidere* there as well.

### A15. 1319D (0039) · Heb. 1:14 · *administratorii spiritus sunt … qui haereditatem **capiunt** salutis*
Vulg. *Nonne omnes **sunt** administratorii spiritus … qui haereditatem **capient** salutis?*
Present for future, plus the word order of the opening. Marked. Material: Richard is arguing
that the angels' guardianship is *now* and *perpetual* (*perpetuam sollicitudinem impendunt*,
*nec nocte nec die*), which the present tense serves and the future does not.

### A16. 1321A (0039) · 1 Cor. 10:13 · *Fidelis est **Dominus**, qui **non sinit** vos tentari*
Vulg. *Fidelis autem **Deus** est, qui non **patietur** vos tentari.* Two divergences, one of
them the plate's known *Dominus*/*Deus* habit (the brief records *Dominus coeli* → *Deus
coeli* across 1281B/1284A in round 1 — this is the same substitution running the other way).
Rendered "the Lord"; marked.

4. **1317B · Ps. 127:2 · *Labores manuum tuarum manducabis*** — Vulg. interposes *quia*

6. **1313C · 1 Cor. 2:6–7 · two omissions** — Migne drops *Deus* from *quam praedestinavit

8. **1317C · Ps. 77:72 · note reads `[n: (Psal. LXVII)]`** for Ps. LXXVII. Text matches

   timebit cor meum* under `[n: (Psal. LV)]` (Ps. 55:12 supplies only the first clause,

10. **1316C · Ps. 36:35 · *elevatum **super** cedros Libani*** for Vulg. *sicut cedros

11. **1318A · Ps. 1:2 · *sit in lege Domini voluntas ejus … meditetur*** for Vulg. *Sed in
    lege Domini voluntas ejus … meditabitur*. Richard has bent the verse into a jussive to

12. **1313D · Prov. 25:27**, **1315C · Sir. 3:22 (*Altiora igitur te…*)**, **1316D · Ps.

## C. ⭐ The pulls I resisted — lemmata where Migne agrees and the ear wanted otherwise

Positive evidence, per 7a″.

1. **1311D · THE TREE LEMMA, Dan. 4:7–9 · it agrees with the Vulgate word for word.**
   I checked all nine clauses individually against the received text: *Visio capitis mei in
   cubili meo* · *videbam et ecce arbor in medio terrae et altitudo ejus nimia* · *Magna
   arbor, et fortis, et proceritas ejus contingens coelum* · *aspectus illius erat usque ad
   terminos universae terrae* · *Folia ejus pulcherrima, et fructus ejus nimius* · *et esca
   universorum in ea* · *Subter eam habitabant animalia et bestiae* · *et in ramis ejus
   conversabantur volucres coeli* · *et ex ea vescebatur omnis caro*. **Zero divergences.**
   The pulls, all resisted:
   - *altitudo ejus **nimia*** — Douay's "exceeding great" was the arriving phrase. Rejected:
     Richard's whole cap. XIV turns *nimia* into a **fault** (*vide … ne forte sit et nimia*;
     *hoc fortassis succisionis causa exstitit quod ejus altitudo nimia fuit*), and cap. XVI
     does the same for *fructus nimius* against *multus* and *copiosus* explicitly. Rendered
     **"excessive"** at every occurrence, in lemma and gloss alike. "Exceeding great" would
     have made the tree's fault a virtue.
   - *aspectus illius* — the eye wanted *ejus* to match the surrounding clauses. Migne prints
     *illius* and so does the Vulgate; kept.
   - *esca universorum in ea* is verbless in both. English needs a copula, so "and in it the
     food of all" supplies one as **plain text with no brackets** (the brief's rule); nothing
     else is added.
   - *animalia et bestiae* — "cattle and beasts" (Douay) was the arriving pair and is wrong
     here: Richard argues from the pair that *bestiae* are the wild animals and therefore
     *animalia* must be the **tame** ones (*relinquitur ut nomine animalium sola hic mansueta
     intelligere debeamus*). "Cattle" pre-empts an inference he draws in the next sentence.
     Rendered **"the animals and the beasts"** throughout, lemma and gloss.
2. **1314D–1315A · Wisd. 7:15–21 · agrees.** The long catalogue matches clause for clause;
   Migne elides v. 16 and runs *ipse enim* straight on, which is quotation-compression, not a
   variant. The pull was *virtutes elementorum* → "powers of the elements": rejected, because
   *virtus* is fixed as **virtue** for this work and the tree's whole allegory is *virtus*.
3. **1314D · Job 29:21–22 · agrees**, including *stillabat eloquium meum*.
4. **1315A · Rom. 1:14 · agrees.** 5. **1313D · Rom. 11:20 and Rom. 1:21 · agree** (the *non*
   of *non sicut Deum glorificaverunt* is carried). 6. **1314A–B · 2 Cor. 2:15–16 · agrees.**
   7. **1317B · Ps. 118:104 · agrees.** 8. **1317D · 1 Cor. 9:27 · agrees.**
   9. **1318A · Col. 3:2 · agrees.** 10. **1318B · Eccl. 1:5 · agrees.**
   11. **1320A · John 13:27 · agrees.** 12. **1321A–B · Ps. 29:7–8 · agrees** but for the
   dropped *autem* of *Ego autem dixi*.
13. **⭐ 1321B · Ps. 37:11 · *Dereliquit me virtus mea, et lumen oculorum **meorum** et ipsum
    non est mecum* — agrees with the Vulgate, and therefore diverges from Migne's OWN earlier
    printing.** The brief asked for this diff and it comes out the other way round from what
    one expects: **chunk 0014 at 1262C prints the clause WITHOUT *meorum*** and carries a
    `[var:]` for it (cruces-0010 §1262C). Here the plate prints the full Vulgate clause.
    **No marker here** — the divergent printing is 0014's and it is already marked. Rendered
    "the light of my eyes" here and "the light of the eyes" there, which is exactly right:
    the two printings must not be harmonized.

## D. Passages that would not construe, and conjectures DECLINED

Named, per the brief; census candidates.

1. **1319D (0039) · *Quoniam **erga** rectitudinis statum subvertere malum esse cognovit*.**
   *erga* is a real word in a real form, so Pattern 12 has nothing to wrap; but with it the
   clause has no object for *subvertere* and reads "since he knew that to overthrow, as
   regards the state of rectitude, was an evil". The near-certain reading is ***ergo***
   ("since therefore he knew that to overthrow the state of rectitude was an evil"), which is
   what the sentence's *nevertheless…* second half requires. **`[cj:]` DECLINED**: Wilson's
   bar reaches a negation or a correlative, and *ergo* is neither. Rendered as literally as
   the printed words allow, with *erga* given its own English ("as regards"); the conjecture
   lives here. **This is the strongest Pattern 18 census candidate in the range.**
2. **1319D (0039) · *et secundum quod in summis divinitus discunt, et imis humana disponere*.**
   *et imis* for *in imis* — the preposition is not repeated and the ablative stands bare.
   Renderable ("in the lowest") without any repair to the sense; no marker, no conjecture.
3. **1314A (0036) · *auctoritatesque ejus tam a bonis diligitur, quam a malis timetur*.**
   Plural subject, two singular verbs. The intended reading is almost certainly
   *auctoritasque ejus*. **No marker.** English concord is obligatory, so the disagreement
   has no English exponent that is not simply ungrammatical English of our own making; a
   `[sic:]` on *auctoritatesque* would swallow the *-que* (7a⁗ question 3) and the noun
   itself, leaving a lexeme-sized hole in a clause whose whole point is what authority does.
   Rendered "and his authorities are alike loved by the good and feared by the evil". Flagged
   here for the merge in case the work's other agents meet the same shape.
4. **1313C (0036) · *Proceritas ejus coelum tangebat, **qui** veraciter dicere poterat*.**
   Masculine relative for feminine *proceritas* — Richard has slid from the tree to the man.
   Pattern 9: grammatical gender has no English exponent, the English relative is "who"
   either way, nothing to emend. Marked here so a later sweep does not churn it.
5. **1319C–D (0039) · *Alia itaque sunt quae boni angeli per seipsos faciunt, alia quae fieri
   permittunt nulla omnino sine eorum dispositione uspiam fiunt.*** Two clauses fused with no
   connective and no punctuation between them. Rendered as printed, with a comma where Migne
   has one; the negation *nulla omnino* is carried in full. No marker: Pattern 8 forbids
   supplying the stop the sentence wants.

## F. Fixed vocabulary followed against my own judgment, and tensions logged

1. **⚠ *intellectus* = "intellect" (ratification 3) produces two hard sentences in 0038**, and
   the brief predicted both.
   - **1317C · Ps. 77:72 · *In intellectibus manuum suarum deduxit eos*** → "In the intellects
     of his hands he led them." The sense of the verse is manual **skill**; "intellects of his
     hands" is opaque English. **Followed the ratification**, because Richard's gloss
     immediately makes the phrase mean cognitive advance won through works, and the pairing
     with *A mandatis tuis intellexi* two lines earlier is the point of the whole paragraph.
   - **1317D · *enarrabit intellectum*** → "shall declare intellect". Same call, same reason.
   - The seam holds where it matters: *intelligentia* is "understanding" throughout
     (*ad majorem intelligentiam proficit* 1317C, *intelligentiae sol* 1318B, *sensum
     spiritalis intelligentiae* 1311C), and the verb *intellexi/intelligit* is "understood /
     understands", which the ratification does not govern and which keeps the paragraph
     readable. **No tension in the noun/verb split; the tension is only in the two Psalm
     phrases above.**
2. ***fortitudo* = fortitude** (ratification 4) forces ***robur* = "strength"** here, since
   the tree's *robur* is the trunk-word: *immensum robur virtutis* (1312D), *robur
   fortitudinis* (1317B, rendered "the strength of fortitude"), *arboris illius robur
   succisum* (1321B). The lemma's adjective *fortis* is "strong" and its abstract *fortitudo*
   is "fortitude"; the pair is visible on the page and nothing collides with *virtus
   corporis*, which does not occur in the range.
3. ***scientia* = learning / *cognitio* = knowledge** (ratification 5) carries two chapter
   heads here (XVI twice) and the whole of 0037; *notitia* = "cognizance" at 1314B
   (*notitiam virtutis*). Followed without difficulty.
4. ***studium*.** The fixed table gives "zeal", and the shipped chunks use **both** "zeal"
   (the abstract: *summo studio*, *studia devotionis*) and "studies" (the concrete plural:
   *a nostris studiis*, *secular studies*). I followed the shipped split rather than
   flattening it — "spiritual studies" in the cap. XI head and body, "zeal" for the abstract
   (*studium meditationis* → "the zeal of meditation", *studii genus* → "kind of zeal").
   **Naming it for the merge**: if the work is to have one English word, "zeal" everywhere
   makes the cap. XI head read "subservient to spiritual zeals", which I judged worse.
5. ***mens* / *animus*** kept visibly apart everywhere, as required: *mens* "mind" (*mens mea
   contemplata est*, *circumspectiones mentis*, *mens humana*), *animus* "spirit"
   (*animus humiliatur* 1310C, *virtute animi deficiente* 1321C, *animi arbitrium* 1311B →
   "the judgment of the spirit"). They collide inside single clauses twice in 0035 alone
   (1310C–D) and the distinction is carried in both.
6. ***sacramentum* = sacrament** (ratification 5), never "secret", in Dan. 4:6 and its three
   glosses — including where Douay's "no secret is impossible to thee" is the arriving
   phrase. The gloss depends on it: *Fit itaque summae devotioni nullum sacramentum
   impossibile*, and *mysterium* stands separately at 1313C (*Dei sapientiam in mysterio*)
   and 1311C (*de divinis mysteriis*).
7. ***solutio* = solution** (ratification 5) in the lemma and both re-quotes; *enodatio* does
   not occur in the range.
8. ***eruditio* = instruction** (*plena eruditio omnium* 1314D, *multitudinem eruditionis*
   1319A) and *erudire* "to instruct" (*hominem ad discretionis … perfectionem erudiunt*,
   *ad discretionem erudimur*), against the chastening sense the sentences carry by themselves.

## H. For ratification, if the work wants it

- ***nimius* = "excessive"**, in lemma and gloss alike, throughout Book II. It is not a
  preference: cap. XIV and cap. XVI both argue that the word is a **fault**, and cap. XVI
  contrasts it with *multus* and *copiosus* in one sentence. Chunks 0040+ re-quote *altitudo
  ejus nimia*; they should not print "exceeding great".
- ***animalia* = "animals" / *bestiae* = "beasts"**, never "cattle", for the same reason
  (1316A's argument from *mansueta* vs *fera*). Re-quoted at 1316B, 1316D, 1317A and again in
  the later Daniel chapters.
- ***vigil* = "watcher"**, *vigilare* "to be watchful", *vigilia* "watch" — Dan. 4:10's word,
  and cap. XX turns it into an angelology (*utrique recte satis vigiles dici queant*).
- ***proceritas* = "tallness"** kept distinct from *altitudo* "height" and *sublimitas*
  "sublimity". Cap. XIV runs all three in one sentence (*Proceritas itaque ejus alta fuit, et
  altitudo ejus procera fuit*), which collapses into nonsense if two of them share a word.
