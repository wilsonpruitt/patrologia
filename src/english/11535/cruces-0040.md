# Cruces — 11535, chunks 0040–0044 (PL 196, 1322B–1333A)

Richard of St Victor, *De eruditione hominis interioris*, Book II, capp. XXIII–XXXIV.
The range runs from the flight of the beasts and birds (Dan. 4:11) through the whole of the
second half of the tree-lemma — *germen radicum … alligetur vinculo ferreo et aereo … et septem
tempora mutentur super eum … In sententia vigilum* — to the king's demand for the interpretation
(Dan. 4:15). No book division falls in this range.

Each entry: column · printed reading · what I did · why.

## A. Markers fired

### A1. `[var:]` — Migne's scripture against the received text

**1322C (0040) · Ps. 24:21 · *Quia innocentes, et recti adhaeserunt mihi?***
Vulgate begins the verse *Innocentes et recti adhaeserunt mihi, quia sustinui te*. Migne's
italic run opens with a *quia* that is Richard's own subordinator after *protestetur* ("protests
… **that** the innocent…"), while the Vulgate's *quia* stands later in the verse and governs a
clause Migne does not print. Rendered as printed ("*that* the innocent and the upright have
cleaved to me?"), with `[var: Vulg. Ps. 24:21 begins *Innocentes et recti adhaeserunt mihi*; the
*quia* Migne prints inside the quotation is the conjunction binding it to *protestetur*]`.
Marked rather than merely logged because the italic boundary makes the conjunction look like part
of the verse, which it is not.
**The `?` is Migne's** and belongs to the framing *Nonne conversabantur…*; carried per Pattern 8.

**1322C (0040) · Ps. 87:9 · *posuerant me abominationem sibi***
Vulgate *posuerunt* (perfect). Migne prints the **pluperfect** *posuerant*. Rendered "they had
made me an abomination to themselves"; `[var: Vulg. *posuerunt*, perfect, "they have made"]`.
Material here because the tense is doing work in Richard's frame: he is asking whether the birds
*had already* flown from the branches at that time (*Nonne et tunc temporis … aufugerant*), and
the pluperfect of the proof-text is what answers the pluperfect of the question.

**1323D (0040) · Ps. 101:9 (Migne: Psal. CII) · *Non in aeternum irascetur, neque in aeternum comminabitur?***
Two divergences at one site, and this is the range's most valuable entry.
1. Vulgate: *Non **in perpetuum** irascetur, neque in aeternum comminabitur.* Migne prints
   *in aeternum* **twice**, flattening the verse's own variation of phrase.
2. Migne closes it with a **question mark** on a clause the received text states flatly.
**The work's own control settles that both are this printing's, not the verse's:** chunk 0014
prints the same verse at 1262B as *… non in perpetuum irascetur, neque in aeternum comminabitur*
— with *in perpetuum*, and with a full stop. The committed English there reads "he will not be
angry for ever, nor will he threaten unto eternity."
Rendered here as printed and with both negations intact: "He will not be angry unto eternity,
neither will he threaten unto eternity?" — i.e. **the `?` is carried and the `Non … neque` is
carried**, even though the two together make the English read as a question expecting the answer
Richard does not want. `[var:]` records the Vulgate reading and names 0014's printing.
⚠ Note for the merge: **do not "reconcile" 0040 with 0014.** The plate disagrees with itself and
that disagreement is the finding.

**1323D (0040) · Hab. 3:2 · *Cum enim iratus fuerit, misericordiae recordabitur***
Vulgate: *cum iratus **fueris**, misericordiae **recordaberis*** — second person, addressed to
God. Migne prints the **third** person throughout. Rendered "For when he shall have been angry,
he will remember mercy"; `[var:]` gives the second-person Vulgate reading.
Material: the third person is what lets the clause serve as the object of *audeat, et dicat* in
Richard's sentence, i.e. as something the penitent says *about* God rather than *to* him.

**1324C (0041) · 2 Cor. 4:18 · *quae autem non videntur aeterna***
Vulgate closes *quae autem non videntur, aeterna **sunt***. Migne drops the second copula.
`[var:]` marked; rendered with the ellipsis carried ("but the things which are not seen,
eternal"). Small, but it sits inside the chunk's four-term antithesis
(invisibilia/visibilia · aeterna/temporalia) and the elision is visible in the English.

**1326D (0041) · Prov. 12:10 · *Viscera avari crudelia***
Vulgate: *viscera autem **impiorum** crudelia*, "the bowels of **the wicked**". Migne prints
***avari***, "of the covetous man". Rendered as printed; `[var:]` gives the Vulgate noun.
**Material, and the reason it is marked rather than logged:** the sentence it proves is
*ex avaritiae vitio … obduratur* — Richard adduces the verse **for avarice specifically**, and
the received *impiorum* would leave his proof-text proving something more general than the vice
he named. Whether the substitution is Migne's compositor, his copy-text, or Richard quoting from
memory is not adjudicated here (Pattern 14 forbids speculating).
Note the printed **full stop** after *secundum illud.* before the quotation: carried, Pattern 8.

**1328B (0042) · Dan. 4:14 · *quod dominatur Excelsus … constituet super eo***
Vulgate: *donec cognoscant viventes quod **dominetur** Excelsus in regno hominum … et humillimum
hominem constituet super **eum***. Migne prints the **indicative** *dominatur* and the **ablative**
*super eo*. `[var:]` on the lemma; the re-quote eleven lines later at 1328C takes a short second
`[var:]` per the round-1 rule that each occurrence is marked.
**Self-diff, and it runs the other way for once:** chunk 0043's opening line (1329A, non-italic)
prints ***dominetur*** — agreeing with the Vulgate — and then at 1329D the same chunk prints
***dominatur*** again. So within four columns the plate gives subjunctive, indicative, indicative.
0043's *dominetur* takes no marker (it agrees with the received text) and is logged here instead.
The gloss is indifferent to the mood, so nothing in the exposition adjudicates it.

**1328C (0042) · Phil. 2:13 · *qui operatur in nobis velle et perficere pro bona voluntate***
Vulgate: *qui operatur **in vobis et** velle et perficere*. Migne prints ***in nobis*** and drops
the first *et*. `[var:]` marked. This is the plate's known habit — round 1 recorded *intra nos*
for *intra vos* in Luke 17:21 at 1240A — so it is reported as another instance, not as a
discovery.

**1330A and 1330B (0043) · Ps. 85:16 · *Da imperium puero tuo***
Vulgate: *Da imperium **tuum** puero tuo*. Migne drops *tuum* at **both** printings; both marked,
per the Luke 17:21 precedent.
⚠ **Vocabulary hook:** *imperium* is rendered **"rule"**, not the Douay "command", because
Richard's gloss immediately equates it with a kingdom — *Pete ergo et tu tale imperium, vel
regnum* — and glosses the whole passage as the interior kingdom over one's own vices. "Command"
would leave *tale imperium, vel regnum* explaining nothing.

**1330B (0043) · Rom. 9:16 · *Non est enim volentis, neque currentis, sed miserentis Dei***
Vulgate: *Igitur non volentis, neque currentis, sed miserentis **est** Dei*. Migne prints *enim*
for *igitur* and moves the copula forward, leaving *miserentis Dei* without it.
**Diffed against the plate's own earlier printing, as the brief requires:** 0040 @1323D prints
*Non enim volentis neque currentis, sed miserentis est Dei* — i.e. the *enim* substitution is
common to both printings, and only the copula moves. **I marked 0043 (the divergent printing) and
left 0040 unmarked**, per the round-1 rule "mark the divergent one, not both". The 0040 site's
*enim*-for-*igitur* is logged here and is deliberately not marked: it is a connective swap of the
kind a citation formula makes, and it changes no sense.

**1331A (0044) · Dan. 4:15 · *Hoc somnium vidi ego rex Nabuchodonosor … nequeunt***
Vulgate: *Hoc somnium vidi ego **Nabuchodonosor rex** … omnes sapientes regni mei **non queunt**
solutionem edicere mihi*. Migne inverts title and name, and prints *nequeunt*. `[var:]` records
both; the English follows the printed order ("I, king Nabuchodonosor"). *nequeunt* is the same
verb written solid and would not on its own be worth a marker; it rides along in the same note
rather than taking a second.
**Self-diff:** the re-quote at 1331D drops *rex* altogether and adds *ejus* (*interpretationem
ejus narra festinus*). Both printings rendered as printed; the re-quote takes no marker, since
truncation and a resumptive pronoun are Richard's citation practice, not a divergent text.

**1332C (0044) · Mark 11:24 · *Amen dico vobis, quidquid orantes petitis … et fiet vobis***
Vulgate: *Propterea dico vobis, **omnia quaecumque** orantes petitis, credite quia accipietis, et
**evenient** vobis*. Migne prints *Amen dico vobis*, *quidquid*, and *fiet vobis*. `[var:]`
marked.
**The diff the launch message asked for:** chunk 0035 @1311B prints this same verse with
***petieritis*** (future perfect) where 0044 prints ***petitis*** — and it is **0044 that agrees
with the Vulgate here**, while 0035 diverges. 0035 also carries a different note, `(Marc. VI)`
against 0044's `(Marc. XI)`; both notes are reproduced verbatim and neither is corrected.
So the verse's *frame* (*Amen … quidquid … fiet*) is divergent at both sites and its *verb* is
divergent at only one, which is the opposite of what the re-quoting habit usually produces.

**1325A (0041) · *ab assuetis concupiscentiis a vertitur***
Split type for *avertitur* (Pattern 10). **Control:** word-bounded `a vertitur` occurs in **2 of

## B. Passages that would not construe — rendered as printed, no marker

**1325B (0041) · *quasi contradictionem suarum portas rebellionum vectibus munire***
The accusative *contradictionem* and the feminine genitive plural *suarum* cannot both be
governed here; the sense the sentence needs is *contradictionis suae portas*, "the gates of their
contradiction", which is exactly what the next sentence spells out (*porta aerea, nisi
contradictio pertinax … vectis ferreus, nisi rebellio audax*). Rendered by the sense the printed
nouns supply, "to fortify the gates of their contradiction with the bars of rebellions", and
logged. **No `[cj:]`:** the case anomaly is neither a negation nor a correlative, and Wilson's bar
stands. **Census candidate, declined and named.**

**1326C (0041) · *cum crudelitatis ministris, tabulisque feris partem habere***
⭐ **The range's one genuinely unresolved reading.** *tabulisque* is a perfectly good ablative
plural of *tabula*, so Pattern 12 has nothing to wrap and Pattern 7 governs: rendered literally,
"with the ministers of cruelty, and with wild planks". It is nonsense in place.
**Control:** the string *tabulisque feris* occurs **once in the whole PL TEI — here**; every other
`tabulisque` in the corpus sits with carpentry or records (*cratibus tabulisque tecti*,
*documentis tabulisque populi*). **Conjecture: *talibusque feris*, "and with such wild beasts"** —
and the sentence's own answering clause is the evidence, *nisi cum **talibus** sociis*, five words
later, with *feris* already established as the chapter's word for the cruel (*Per feras … recte
satis crudeles quique intelligi possunt*).
**Declined as a `[cj:]`**: *talis* is a correlative pronoun, which brushes the bar, but the
printed *tabulis* is not a wrongly-printed **negation or correlative pair** in the sense Wilson
ruled — the correlative is in the *answering* clause and is printed correctly. **Named here as a
census candidate; I have not widened the bar.** If the census reopens it, this is the strongest
site in the range.

**1329D (0043) · *justum quaelibet et coelestem vitam ducentem***
*justum* (masc. acc. sg.) and *quaelibet* (neut. nom./acc. pl.) cannot agree, and neither agrees
with *vitam*. Read as *justam quamlibet … vitam*, "a just life of whatsoever sort"; rendered that
way and logged. **No `[cj:]`** — number/gender anomaly only, and Pattern 9 notes that English has
no exponent for the gender. Census candidate, declined.

**1330D (0043) · *talem hominem, forte qualis designatur in arbore***
*forte* read as the adverb "perhaps" (with the *talem … qualis* correlative), not as *fortem*
agreeing with *hominem* — which the chapter's own vocabulary would also support (*vide quam
fortis et potens … invenitur*, three lines above). Rendered "such a man, perhaps such as is
designated in the tree" and flagged here as genuinely ambiguous: **the alternative reading is
"such a man, strong, such as is designated in the tree."** Nothing in the sentence decides it.

## E. Fixed vocabulary — followed, including where it cost something

- ***animus* = spirit / *mens* = mind** held apart everywhere, and they collide in this range:
  1327D–1328A (*Vitiorum … dominatio **animum** paulatim emollit*) sits in a chapter whose whole
  subject is the *mens*, and 1331D–1332A runs *animo … animus … animus* through a passage about
  what the *mens* cannot reach. Rendered "spirit" for *animus* throughout. ***spiritus* and
  *animus* never stand in the same clause anywhere in this range**, so the brief's two-site
  collision is not touched here: *spiritus* appears only as the malignant spirits at 1326A, the
  ministering and unclean spirits at 1328B–1328A, and in 0044 as the Holy Spirit and the Dan. 4
  *spiritus deorum sanctorum* — every one of them unambiguous in context, and none beside an
  *animus*.
- ***aes* = bronze**, uniformly, against the ear's "brass" for *portas aereas* (Ps. 106:16) — the
  ratified five-materials rule wins over the familiar psalm cadence, and it has to, because the
  chapter's allegory runs *ferrum → audacia* / *aes → pertinacia* across four columns and back
  into the Dan. 2 statue.
- ***pertinacia* = obstinacy** (the four-vice scheme) and ***saevitia* = savagery** (1327B, *primus
  gradus saevitiae*) followed exactly; *audacia*, which is not in the scheme, is rendered
  **audacity** throughout so the pair *pertinaciter / audaciter* stays visible.
- ***detrimentum* = decline** at 1327A (*intellige detrimenta bonorum*) — "losses" read better
  against *incrementa malorum* and was refused.
- ***industria* = effort** at 1327B–C, twice, where "industry" was the pull.
- ***simulatio* = dissimulation** at 1329C (*absque simulatione*) — and note this is *simulatio*,
  not the ratified *simultas*; the two do **not** collide anywhere in this range.
- ***notitia* = cognizance** in CAPUT XXXIV's own head; ***solutio* = solution** and
  ***interpretatio* = interpretation** kept apart through 0044, where Nabuchodonosor asks for both.
- ***eruditio*/*erudire* = instruct** at 1329D (*ad circumspectionis cautelam erudiuntur*), the
  title word, where the sense is plainly chastening — carried by the sentence, per the table.

⚠ **One tension logged, per the brief's instruction to follow and report:**
**1324A (0040) · *se a suis voluptatibus non posse cohibere cum sacramento asseverant*.** The
ratified table fixes *sacramentum* = **sacrament**, kept apart from *mysterium* — a ruling made
for the Dan. 2 material, where *sacramentum* is the mystery Daniel unfolds. Here the word plainly
means an **oath**: it stands in parallel with *etiam cum juramento affirmant* in the preceding
clause, and no sacrament is in view. I rendered *cum juramento* "with an oath" and *cum
sacramento* "under solemn oath", so the parallel survives and neither word is flattened onto the
other. **I did not follow the table into nonsense, and I am not deciding the work's usage: this is
a request for ratification** — either the table gains an exception for the oath sense, or it is
narrowed to the Daniel material where it was made.

## G. Note on the Job citation printed *inside* the italics

**1322C (0040)** prints its locator as bare text inside the lemma — *… et vita ipsa putabantur
indigni (Job XXX)* — rather than as a trailing `[n: …]` marker, which is what every other citation
in this range does. Reproduced exactly as printed, inside the italic run and untranslated, on the
same principle that governs `[n:]` contents. It is not a defect and takes no marker; noted only so
that an indexer knows this locator will not be found by scanning `[n:]` alone.
