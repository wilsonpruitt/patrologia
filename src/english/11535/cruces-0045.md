## A. Markers placed

### `[sic: …]` — carried type (Patterns 10 + 12)

Each was controlled against the whole PL TEI (5,276 files, 87M words) before it was set.

**1. 1335B · Dan. 4:17 · *Arborem … quam vidisti* `sub limem` *atque robustam***
Migne sets *sublimem* as two words. `sub limem` occurs **once in the entire PL TEI — this
file, this site**; *sublimem* occurs in 310 files. A word-division defect, Pattern 10:
the whole run is carried, spacing included, and marked `[sic: sub limem]`. **No second
italic marker**, since the run sits inside an already-italic lemma (Pattern 10's nesting
rule). 7a⁗ check: the quarantined run is an attributive adjective; the clause keeps its
verb (*vidisti*), carries no negation and no conjunction, so nothing is supplied beside
the marker. Conjecture: *sublimem*, "lofty" — near-certain, and the parallel at 1335B's
own *cujus altitudo pertingit ad coelum* carries the sense anyway.

**2. 1337C · *divinae* `pietatatis` *viscera***
A dittographic syllable; not a word. Corpus control: `pietatatis` in **2 files** against
2,148 for *pietatis*. Decisive against the plate. Carried and marked. 7a⁗: the marker
takes a genitive noun only; the governing phrase ("the bowels of the divine …") and the
sentence's verb survive outside it. The work's own parallel at **1342A** prints *Quis
divinae **pietatis** viscera digne pensare sufficiat?* — same sentence, correct form,
five columns later. Conjecture: *pietatis*, certain.

**3. 1337C · *quod* `contemplum` *suum non statim vindicat***
`contemplum` is not a Latin word. Corpus control: 3 occurrences in 87M words (11296,
21385, and this file), i.e. plate-level rarity everywhere it appears. Carried and marked.
⚠ 7a⁗ was checked at this site with care, because the marker sits in a negative clause:
the English retains **both** the verb (*vindicat*, "avenge") and the negation (*non*) —
"he does **not** at once avenge his own [sic: *contemplum*]". Only the object is
quarantined. Conjecture: *contemptum*, "the contempt shown him", which is what the
antithesis (*sed correptionis nostrae satisfactionem exspectat*) requires.

**4. 1338A · *quem de* `fieta` *humilitate sua conscientia accusando fatigat***
`fieta` for *ficta*. **Unique to this file in the whole PL TEI.** Carried and marked.
Conjecture: *ficta* — certain, because the same sentence's neighbours print *ficta
humilitas* three times in the preceding six lines (1338A, twice).

**5. 1341A · *et* `vetut` *unguibus debacchantur***
`vetut` for *velut*. Corpus control: 3 bare occurrences of `vetut` in the whole TEI, plus
4 of `vetutissim-` (itself defective for *vetustissim-*) — i.e. a known compositor's
confusion of *l*/*t*, not a word. Carried and marked, **untranslated** (so "as it were" is
*not* also rendered; that would double-count the run). 7a⁗: *velut* is a comparative
particle, not a negation, conjunction or predicate; the clause keeps *debacchantur* and
*unguibus*. Conjecture: *velut* — certain, and the same phrase prints correctly as *et
velut unguibus desaeviunt* eleven lines later at 1341B.

**6. 1334D · Dan. 4:16 · *somnium, et* **interpretationes** *ejus non conturbent te***
Vulgate: *interpretatio eius*, singular. Corpus control: `interpretationes ejus non
conturbent te` occurs **nowhere else in the PL TEI**; the singular is attested (7197).
**Material and internally decided**: Migne's own next lemma, eight lines below, prints
*et **interpretatio** ejus hostibus tuis sit* — singular. Rendered as printed
("the interpretations thereof"); `[var:]` names the singular and points at the next lemma.

**7. 1334D–1335A · Dan. 4:16 · *somnium his* **quae** *te oderunt***
Vulgate: *his qui te oderunt*, masculine. Corpus control: `somnium his quae te oderunt`
occurs **nowhere else**; `somnium his qui te oderunt` in two other works (11412, 7197).
**Decided inside the chunk**: Richard re-quotes the same clause three lines later as
*Somnium his **qui** te oderunt* — so the plate contradicts itself and the second printing
holds the received form. Rendered as printed with English *which* (the relative that can
carry a non-personal antecedent), which is not an accident of style: Richard's gloss
immediately reads *Vitia veri animae hostes sunt, **quae** eam ad aeternum interitum
impellunt* — vices, neuter. The printed *quae* is the reading his gloss picks up. Also
noted inside the marker: Migne adds *sit*, which the Vulgate lacks.

**8. 1336C · Dan. 4:24 · *forsitan ignoscet Deus* **dilectis** *tuis*** ⭐
**The most consequential divergence in the range**, and the one the launch message asked
to be adjudicated between Patterns 14 and 18. Migne prints *dilectis*, "thy beloved ones",
where the sense requires *delictis*, "thy sins" — the whole verse is about redeeming sins
with alms.

*What settled it.* Corpus control: `forsitan ignoscet Deus dilectis tuis` occurs **once in
87M words — here**; `forsitan ignoscet delictis tuis` occurs in **four** other PL works
(11412, 10900, 7197, 10885). And the work is again its own control: at **1337B**, inside my
own chunk, Richard re-quotes the clause as *Forsitan, inquit, ignoscet **delictis** tuis*
and then expounds it as pardon for offences. So *delictis* is both the received reading and
this author's reading; *dilectis* is this setting of this column.

*Pattern 14, not Pattern 18, and why.* Pattern 18's own boundary is explicit and is about
**where** the divergence sits, not how it reads: "`[var:]` inside a quoted scripture,
`[cj:]` in the author's own prose" — the worked case being 7561 @0664's *modio* for *medio*
inside Deut. 13:1, which has exactly this shape. This word stands inside a continuous,
italicised, `[n:]`-cited lemma of Dan. 4. It is therefore `[var:]`.
Independently, Wilson's `[cj:]` bar would decline it anyway: *dilectis* is neither a
negation nor a correlative. Both routes give the same answer, which is why I am confident
in it.
Note the divergence from *ignoscat* (Vulgate subjunctive) and the added *Deus* were **not**
marked: the four corpus witnesses all print *ignoscet*, so the indicative is the circulating
reading of the verse, not this plate's. The marker names *delictis* only.

**9. 1340C · Dan. 4:30 · *et* **ungues quasi ungues avium***

(*et ungues **ejus** quasi ungues avium*) and abbreviates again at **1341D** (*ungues ut
avium accepit*) — three forms of the clause inside one chunk.

**10. 1342D · Rom. 14:5 · *Alius judicat* **inter diem et diem***
Vulgate: *alius iudicat diem plus inter diem*, "one esteemeth one day above another".
Rendered as printed. **Material**: Richard's exposition two lines above turns on the

⚠ Honest weakness of this one, stated so a later sweep can reverse it: the corpus control

**11. 1345A · Eccli. 3:19 · *in mansuetudine* **fac** *opera tua***

## B. ⚠ Markers I placed and then WITHDREW, and the corpus control that killed them

These are recorded in full because the withdrawals are evidence, and because three of them
would have been **false markers** — a `[var:]` asserting a divergence that is not there is
worse than a missed one, since nothing downstream can catch it.

**W1. 1335D · Dan. 4:20 · *rore coeli* **conspergatur** *… donec septem tempora*
**commutentur*** — WITHDRAWN.
I first marked *conspergatur* against the Vulgate's *tinguatur* and *commutentur* against
*mutentur*, on the assumption (which the launch message shared) that this run is a fourth
printing of the passage that 0038, 0040 and 0041 print, and could be diffed against them.
**It is not.** Dan. 4:20 is the *interpretation*, and it differs from the *vision* of Dan.
4:11–13 in the received text itself: *dissipate illam* for *praecidite ramos ejus*,
*dimittite* for *sinite*, *vinciatur ferro et aere* for *alligetur vinculo ferreo et
aereo*, *sit pabulum ejus* for *pars ejus in herba terrae*. Migne reproduces **every one**
of those Vulgate-internal differences correctly.
Corpus control, run afterwards and decisive: `rore coeli conspergatur` is printed by
**four** independent PL works (11412, 7197, 21346, 11535); `donec septem tempora
commutentur` by **three** (11412, 21346, 11535) against one for *mutentur* (7197). Migne
is with the majority at both sites. **No divergence. No marker.** The coordinator's
correction arrived independently and agrees.

**W2. 1335A · Dan. 4:22 · *cum bestiis* **ferisque** *erit habitatio tua*** — WITHDRAWN.
I marked this against a Vulgate *cum bestiis feris* ("with the wild beasts", one class),
arguing it was material because the exposition at 1339C expounds **two** classes
(*bestialiter viventibus … feraliter agentibus*). Corpus control is a 2–2 split:
`cum bestiis ferisque erit habitatio` in 10877 and here; `cum bestiis feris erit habitatio`
in 7197 and 8585. With the witnesses evenly divided and no plate at hand, I cannot
establish which is the received reading, so the marker comes off. **Recorded here as an
open question, and it is a real one**, because the plate prints the clause three ways
inside my two chunks: *ferisque* at 1335A and 1335D, *atque feris* at 1339C, *et feris* at
1339C's own gloss twelve lines later.

**W3. 1333B/1333D · Ps. 64:9 · *Exitus matutini, et vespere* **delectabis*** — WITHDRAWN.
This looked like the strongest find in 0045: Migne's **active** *delectabis* against the
Gallican *delectaberis*, with Richard's gloss hanging on exactly that (*Tu, inquit,
delectabis, non ipsa hora* — "**Thou** shalt delight, not the hour itself"). Corpus
control killed it: `matutini … delectabis` is printed by **twelve** PL works against
**two** for *delectaberis*. The active is the dominant PL reading of the verse, not this
plate's. Rendered active (which the gloss requires), **no marker**. ⭐ This is the single
best argument in the range for running the corpus control before setting a marker rather
than after: every literary signal pointed at a divergence and there is none.

**W4. 1333D · Jer. 17:9 · *Pravum siquidem est cor* **hominis**, *et inscrutabile*** —
WITHDRAWN. Vulgate: *cor omnium*. Corpus control: `Pravum est cor hominis` in **eleven**
works, `Pravum est cor omnium` in **two**. Dominant PL reading. Rendered as printed, no
marker.

## C. Conjectures and markers DECLINED — census candidates, named

Wilson's `[cj:]` bar (a conjecture is glossed only for a **negation or a correlative**)
declines every one of these. They are named so the census does not have to find them again.

**C1. 1333B · *Istud est tempus ridendi.*** Printed with no antithetic clause. The
argument requires the pair *tempus flendi / tempus ridendi* — the next two sentences ask
*Cur non sit tempus ridendi in matutino … Cur non sit tempus flendi in vespertino* — and
*Istud*, following *considerandi infirma nostra*, attaches "laughing" to the wrong member.
Either a clause has dropped out or *ridendi* stands for *flendi*. Rendered as printed
("That is the time of laughing"), no marker. **Declined**: the missing element is a
correlative *member*, not a correlative *particle*, and supplying it would be inventing a
clause, not glossing a word.

**C2. 1333B · *Vespertinum tempus est quando ante contemplantis oculos lumen revelantis
gratiae emergit…*** The sentence describes the **morning** in this chapter's own scheme
(*mane … ab interioribus ad exteriora exire, et vespere ab exterioribus ad interiora
redire*; *Delectant … exitus matutini, contristant autem introitus vespertini*). *Matutinum*
is what the argument needs. Rendered as printed, no marker. **Declined**: a real,
correctly-formed word in its slot, and neither a negation nor a correlative — the Pattern
18 bar as written. The nearest ruled analogue is 11064 @1154C, likewise declined.

**C3. 1333C · *Quis autem digne* **miremur** *quam sit dulce…*** Interrogative singular
*Quis* with a first-person **plural** verb. The sentence will not construe in either
language. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow ("But who may we worthily wonder
how sweet it is…"), which is awkward English on purpose: I would not silently drop either
*quis* or the plural, since a mid-run person switch is a named invisible-defect class.
**Declined** — an explicit person/number anomaly, the class the 2026-08-15 ruling scoped
*out*.

**C4. 1333C · *saepe extra semetipsum per mentis* **excelsum** *rapit*** against **1333C**'s
own *humanae mentis **excessus*** eight words later, and against the work's fixed term
*mentis excessus* = "excess of mind" (ratified item 5). *excelsum* is a real noun ("a
height") and renders in place ("through the height of the mind"). Rendered as printed, no
marker. **Declined**: not a negation or correlative. Flagged for the merge pass, because a
reader will meet "the height of the mind" and "the excesses of the human mind" in
consecutive clauses and they are the same technical phrase in the plate's intention.

**C5. 1337C · *Post finem, inquit, mensium duodecim* … *Respondit rex, et ait***
against the Vulgate's *Responditque rex et ait* — and against **1338B**, where Richard
re-quotes it as *Responditque rex, et ait*, with the *-que*. A genuine self-diff, logged;
**no `[var:]`**, because a lost connective particle does not change the sense and Pattern
14's bar is materiality. *mensium* for *mensuum* is orthographic and takes nothing.

**C6. 1339A · Matt. 5:16 · *ut videant* **opera vestra bona*** for the Vulgate's *vestra
bona opera*. Word order only, no gloss turns on it. **Declined** — but recorded, because
the 7914 @1079B precedent shows a reversed order *can* be material when the gloss picks up
the sequence, and here it does not.

**C7. 1339B–C · Dan. 4:29 · *et cum bestiis* **atque** *feris erit habitatio tua*** for the
Vulgate's *et feris*; and at 1339C Richard's own gloss then prints *et cum bestiis et feris
erit habitatio tua*, the Vulgate form. Connective only. **Declined**, logged with W2 above
as part of the same open question.

**C8. 1339D · Isa. 40:6 / 1 Pet. 1:24 · *omnis caro fenum, et omnis gloria ejus quasi flos*
**feni*** — Isaiah reads *flos agri*, 1 Peter *flos faeni*. Migne's form is 1 Peter's, and
it is woven into Richard's own sentence with no italics and no `[n:]`. **Declined**: this
is a conflated allusion, not a quotation adduced as proof, so it sits outside Pattern 14's
scope. Same treatment for *ante ruinam exaltabitur cor* at 1334C, which conflates Prov.
16:18 and 18:12.

**C9. 1342A · Dan. 4:31 · *et regnum ejus* **in generatione et generationem*** for the
Vulgate's *in generationem et generationem*. **Declined on the corpus control, decisively**:
`in generatione et generationem` is printed by **42** PL works, `in generationem et
generationem` by **40**. Both forms circulate massively; this is Pattern 9 territory, not a
divergence. The same form recurs at **1343B** in 0049 and takes nothing there either.

**C10. 1342C–D · Isa. 5:20 · *ponentes amarum dulce et dulce amarum*** where the Vulgate
(and chunk 0041 at 1325D) prints *ponens amarum **in** dulce et dulce **in** amarum*.
Woven, unitalicised, uncited. **Declined**; logged because it is another instance of the
plate re-quoting itself with a difference.

**C11. 1340B · Dan. 4:30 · *sermo completus est super Nabuchodonosor,* **ex** *hominibus
abjectus est*** — the Vulgate has *et ex hominibus*. Dropped connective, no marker.

## D. The resisted pulls — lemmata where Migne agrees and my instinct did not

Positive evidence, per the brief. Each of these I checked word by word and left alone.

**R1. ⭐ 1336A vs 1339D–1340A · Dan. 4:22 *super regnum hominum* / Dan. 4:29 *in regno
hominum*.** This is the range's headline non-finding, and it is exactly the shape the brief
warned would be a divergence. At **1336A** Richard quotes *Donec scias quod dominetur
Excelsus **super regnum** hominum*; at **1339D–1340A**, three columns later, the same
formula prints *quod dominetur Excelsus **in regno** hominum*. The plate appears to
contradict itself on the word the argument rests on, and CAPUT XLII's whole point is that
*una eademque sententia* is repeated three times.
**Both printings are correct.** The received text distinguishes them: Dan. 4:22 (the
threat, *super regnum*) from Dan. 4:29 (the fulfilment, *in regno*) — Douay preserves the
distinction as "ruleth **over** the kingdom of men" against "ruleth **in** the kingdom of
men", and Dan. 4:14, quoted at 1328B–1329D in the peer range, is *in regno* again. Migne
has quoted the right verse in the right place both times. **No marker at either site**, and
a later reader who "fixes" one of them will be wrong.

**R2. 1339C · Dan. 4:29 · *Fenum quasi bos comedes*** against **1335D**'s Dan. 4:22 *fenum

## E. Fixed vocabulary — followed, and one place it strained

Followed without incident: *mens* mind · *animus* spirit · *anima* soul · *cor* heart ·
*intelligentia* understanding · *intellectus* intellect (only *intellectualiter* occurs
here, 1339C) · **fortitudo fortitude** (load-bearing in this range: *in robore fortitudinis
meae* recurs five times at 1337C–1339A and is glossed *ecce arrogantia*, so "in the
strength of my fortitude" keeps *robur* and *fortitudo* visibly apart) · *cognitio*
knowledge · *scientia* learning (does not occur here) · *discretio* discretion ·
*magisterium* mastery (1338D) · *arrogantia* arrogance, the four-vice scheme intact ·
*eruditio*/*erudire* instruction/instruct (1334A *divinitus eruditur*) · *praesumere*
presume · *liberum arbitrium* free will (1344C) · *mentis excessus* excess of mind (1333C)
· proper names in Migne's Latin form (*Nabuchodonosor* throughout; *Balthasar* at 1334D
and 1335A — note the plate spells it with **one** *s* in 0046 where 0035 at 1310B prints
*Balthassar*; both kept as printed in their own chunks).

**Strain, logged as the brief requires: *pietas*.** Held to **piety** everywhere, against
my own judgment at two sites. At 1337C *divinae pietatatis viscera* and 1342A *divinae
pietatis viscera* the sense is God's tenderness, for which "the bowels of the divine piety"
is stiff; and at 1337D *tam justa quam pia* pairs *pius* with *justus*. "Compassion" was
the obvious alternative and is unavailable, because *compassio*/*compati* occurs in the
same range (1337A–B, *alienae afflictioni compati*) and would collide. *misericordia* holds
mercy, *miseratio* compassion (1338D, 1342A–B). Piety stands; flagged for ratification
rather than decided here.

## F. Vocabulary calls I am proposing for the whole work

These are new to this stretch of Daniel and will recur in 0050–0057 and in the peer ranges
0035–0044. They need to be one word across the work or the tree allegory loses its hooks.

1. **bestia = beast · fera = wild beast · animal = animal.** Non-negotiable in this range:
   1339C glosses *bestialiter degens et feraliter agens* as two distinct vices (carnality
   and cruelty), and 1340D–1341A turns on *ferae* preying on *animalia* while *aves* prey
   on *aves*. Collapsing any two is the *testa*/*fictilis* error in another key.
2. **ungues = claws** (not "nails"). Carries both the birds of Dan. 4:30 and 1341D's *dente
   et ungue in omnes saevit*, "with tooth and claw", where "nails" would be unreadable.
3. **fenum = hay · herba = grass.** Both are in the Dan. 4 lemma at once (*fenum ut bos
   comedes* beside *in herbis foris* / *in herba terrae*), so they must not share a word.
   "Hay" also carries *omnis caro fenum … quasi flos feni* at 1339D.
4. **germen radicum = the shoot of its roots.** Used at 1335D and 1336B; the peer range
   0040 (1323B–1324A) expounds *germen* at length as *propositum correctionis*, so whatever
   that agent chose must win if it differs — flagged for the merge pass.
5. **decor = comeliness · decorare = to make comely.** Dan. 4:33's *ad honorem regni mei
   decoremque perveni* is expounded at 1344C–1345A as a matched pair with *honor*, so the
   two English words must stay distinct and stable.
6. **aula = hall · palatium = palace.** Richard distinguishes them explicitly at 1337D–1338A
   (*Forte palatium … humilitas vera* against *aula Babylonica humilitas ficta*).
7. **gloriatio = glorying · glorificatio = glorification.** CAPUT XLVII's head is built on
   the opposition and it recurs through 1344A.
