## A. Markers fired

### `[var: …]` — Pattern 14 (Migne's scripture against the received text)

Policy applied, stated so it can be checked: **a `[var:]` fires where the English on the
page would assert something different** — a different word, tense, number, direction, or a
dropped word that carries sense. Pure abridgement of a longer verse (clauses elided, no
wording altered) and connective-level substitutions that leave the English unchanged are
logged in §B instead, not marked. Twelve fired.

1. **1345D · Job 4:6** — `*Ubi est timor tuus, et fortitudo tua, patientia tua, et perfectio
   viarum?*` Vulg. *Ubi est timor tuus, fortitudo tua, patientia tua, et perfectio viarum
   **tuarum**?* Migne adds *et* after *timor tuus* and drops *tuarum*: the English loses "of
   **thy** ways" and the verse stops indicting Eliphaz's hearer. Marked; rendered as printed.
2. **1345D · Apoc. 2:4–5** — `*Habeo adversum te aliquid quod primam charitatem reliquisti.
   Memento unde excideris…*` Vulg. *Sed habeo adversum te, quod caritatem tuam primam
   reliquisti. Memor esto itaque unde excideris.* Three divergences at once: *Sed* dropped,
   *aliquid* intruded, *primam charitatem* for *caritatem tuam primam* (the possessive gone),
   *Memento* for *Memor esto itaque*. **Diffed against the work's own other printing at
   1271C (chunk 0019), which reads *Sed habeo adversum te, quod charitatem tuam primam
   dereliquisti* — i.e. much closer to the Vulgate, and with *dereliquisti* for *reliquisti*.**
   The two printings of one verse disagree in four places. Marked here (the more divergent).
3. **1346D · Dan. 4:34** — `*Nunc ego Nabuchodonosor…*` Vulg. *Nunc **igitur** ego*. The
   inferential connective is the hinge of the chapter (Nabuchodonosor praises *because* he
   has been restored), and it is gone. Marked. ⚠ Migne also prints a comma inside the lemma,
   *et viae ejus, judicia*, which Richard's own exposition four lines later does not have
   (*viae ejus judicia*). Punctuation, so carried under Pattern 8, not marked.
4. **1348B · Prov. 16:18** — `Scimus quia ante ruinam exaltabitur **cor**.` Vulg. *ante
   ruinam exaltatur **spiritus***. Both noun and tense differ; unitalicized but carried by a
   `[n:]` and functioning as a proof-text, so Pattern 14's "no formatting precondition"
   applies. Marked.
5. **1347C · Dan. 7:4** — `et cor **ejus** datum est ei.` Vulg. *et cor **hominis** datum
   est ei.* ⭐ **The most consequential divergence in the range.** Marked. The plate's reading
   is not a slip that can be shrugged off, because **Richard expounds it as printed**: at
   1355D he asks *Cujus ejus? Aut cor hominis, aut cor leaenae* — "Whose, its? either a man's
   heart or the lioness's" — a question the Vulgate's *cor hominis* makes impossible. His
   copy-text read *ejus*. Rendered "its heart was given to it", and the beast is "it"
   throughout the range so that the ambiguity Richard exploits survives into English.
6. **1353D · Dan. 7:4 (re-quotation)** — same *cor ejus*, marked again at its second
   printing, per the Luke 17:21 precedent in the brief (mark each occurrence).
7. **1348D · Ps. 75:12** — `*Vovete… qui in circuitu ejus affertis munera*`. Vulg. *omnes
   qui in circuitu ejus*. The dropped *omnes* narrows the address. Marked. (The note reads
   `(Psal. CLXXV)`; the Psalter stops at 150. Note kept verbatim — see §D.)
8. **1348D · Eccl. 5:4** — `*Alioquin melius est non vovere, quam vota non reddere.*` Vulg.
   *Multoque melius est non vovere, quam post votum promissa non reddere.* Marked.
9. **1349A · Ecclus. 2:1** — `*Fili, accedens ad servitutem Dei sta in timore…*` Vulg. *sta
   in **justitia et** timore*. A whole member of the precept is gone. Marked.
10. **1350D · Ps. 13:5 + Ps. 73:19** — ⭐ **the double printing named in the launch brief, and
    it is worse than a doublet.** At 1350C Migne prints Ps. 73:19 as *Ne tradas **bestiis**
    animas confitentes tibi* (= Vulgate, unmarked); eight lines later, at 1350D, he prints it
    again as ***Non** tradas… **bestiae** animas confitentes tibi*, joined under one `[n:]`
    with Ps. 13:5 *Illic **trepidabant** timore* (Vulg. *trepidaverunt*). So the second
    printing diverges twice over — negative particle and the number/case of the beasts — and
    the note conflates two psalms. **The plate adjudicates itself three lines later**: Richard
    paraphrases *sed **ne** tradas **bestiis** animas confitentes tibi*, i.e. back to the first
    printing. Marked on the second printing only; rendered "Do not deliver … unto the beast"
    against the first printing's "Deliver not up unto the beasts", so the difference is
    visible on the page and not only in this file.
11. **1351C · Ps. 130:1** — `qui ambulant in magnis et mirabilibus **sub se**.` Vulg. *neque
    ambulavi in magnis, neque in mirabilibus **super me***. Marked. ⚠ **The plate contradicts
    itself within two columns**: at 1353A the same tag is quoted *ambulare in magnis et
    mirabilibus **supra** se*, which is what the argument needs (presumption reaching above
    itself). *sub se* at 1351C makes the first class of bestial men walk in great things
    *beneath* themselves, which is the opposite of the vice being described. Rendered as
    printed at both sites; marked at 1351C.
12. **1354A · Abd. 4** — ⭐ **the third printing of this verse in the work, and the three
    disagree.** 1265D (0016): *Si exaltatus fueris ut aquila, et **si** inter sidera **coeli**
    posueris nidum tuum, inde **detraham** te.* 1272B (0019): *Sed si exaltatus fueris ut
    aquila, et inter sidera posueris nidum tuum: inde **detraham** te.* 1354A (mine): *Si
    exaltatus fueris, ut aquila, et inter sidera **coeli** posueris nidum tuum, inde
    **traham** te.* Vulgate: *…et **si** inter sidera posueris… inde **detraham** te.* So
    *coeli* is the plate's own addition (twice of three), the second *si* survives once of
    three, and **only this printing loses the prefix of *detraham***. That loss is
    directional: *detrahere* is to drag **down** from the stars, *trahere* merely to draw —
    and the whole chapter is about wings being plucked off and the beast falling. Marked, and
    rendered "thence will I draw thee" as printed.
13. **1357C · Ps. 43:16** — `…cooperuit me **a facie** exprobrantis et obloquentis.` Vulg.
    *a **voce** exprobrantis et obloquentis.* Marked. Note that the divergence runs against
    Richard's own use: the chapter is about the *tongue* that speaks against, so *a voce* is
    the reading his argument wants and the plate gives him *a facie*. **Diffed against 1258B
    (chunk 0012), which prints the same verse without the *a facie/voce* tail at all** — so
    the work quotes Ps. 43:16 twice, at two different lengths, and the longer printing is the
    divergent one.
14. **1357C · Ps. 93:21** — `*Captabant… condemnabant*` (imperfects). Vulg. *Captabunt…
    condemnabunt* (futures). Marked. **Diffed against 1341B (chunk 0048), which prints the
    same verse with the Vulgate's futures** — the work disagrees with itself, and this is the
    aberrant printing.

*(Numbered 1–14 for reference; twelve distinct `[var:]` markers, two of them the second
occurrence of an already-marked divergence.)*

### `[sic: …]` — Pattern 12 (carried type)

Two fired, both settled by corpus control before firing.

- **1346B · *cruditur*** — `ex ipso lapsu suo, homo saepe ad veram humilitatem **cruditur**`.
  There is no verb *crudire*; the sense required is *eruditur*, **and it is the work's own
  title word** (*De **eruditione** hominis interioris*, and the ratified table's *erudire* =
  "to instruct"). Marked `[sic: *cruditur*]` and, per 7a⁗ question 1, **the sense is supplied
  in plain words beside the marker** — "is often instructed [sic: *cruditur*] unto true
  humility" — because the marked run was the clause's only predicate.
  ⚠ **Control run, and it is honest rather than flattering:** `grep -rlw cruditur
  sources/pl/tei/` returns **four** files, not one — 9324 (*ipsorum locorum testimoniis
  cruditur*), 11703 (*secundo cruditur ignorantia*), 8486 (*Israel ergo, reservatis gentibus
  cruditur*) and this one. Every one of the four is a context that requires *erudi-*. So this
  is not a hapax defect but **a recurring c-for-e accident in Migne's plates**, and the marker
  is correspondingly weaker than the 11550-style hapax cases. It still stands, because the
  form corresponds to no lexical entry and therefore cannot be rendered at all (Pattern 7's
  non-word class); Pattern 9 exempts *attested* medieval spellings, and this is not one.
- **1351D · *erudelitas*** — `in tertio calliditas, in quarto **erudelitas**`. Control:
  **hapax in the whole PL TEI** (`grep -rl erudelitas` returns only 11535). The plate
  corrects itself four lines later — *In quarto regnat **crudelitas*** — and the vice-list
  is fixed by ratified item 6's neighbourhood. Marked; no plain-word supply needed, since the
  clause's verb (*regnat*) is carried from the previous member and no negation is involved.

## B. Divergences and oddities logged, deliberately **not** marked

Recorded because the brief asks for the whole divergence list, and because a later sweep
should not have to re-derive why each was declined.

- **1348C · Eccl. 5:3** — *displicet enim **Deo** infidelis, et stulta promissio* for Vulg.
  *displicet enim **ei***. An explicitation; the English is identical either way.
- **1349B · Jas. 1:14** — *Unusquisque **enim** tentatur concupiscentia sua* for Vulg.
  *Unusquisque **vero** tentatur **a** concupiscentia sua*. Connective swap; the ablative
  without *a* renders the same.
- **1349C · 1 Mach. 7:50** — *Siluit… terra Juda **diebus paucis*** for Vulg. *Et siluit
  terra Juda **dies paucos***. Ablative for accusative of duration; no sense at stake.
- **1350D · Deut. 32:24** — *Dentes bestiarum immittam **in eis*** for Vulg. *in **eos***.
  Same. (The note reads `(Deut. XXXVIII)`; Deuteronomy has 34 chapters — see §D.)
- **1352A · Eccl. 4:10** — *non habet sublevantem* for Vulg. *non habet sublevantem **se***.
- **1352A · Ecclus. 2:14** — *Vae duplici corde, et terram ingredienti duabus viis* against
  the Vulgate's fuller *Vae duplici corde, et labiis scelestis, et manibus malefacientibus,
  et **peccatori** terram ingredienti duabus viis.* **Elision of clauses, not variation of
  wording** — declined by the stated policy, though *peccatori* is a real loss.
- **1352B · 1 Cor. 13:8** — *Nam charitas est, quae nunquam excidit* recasts *Charitas
  nunquam excidit* as a relative clause to fit Richard's sentence.
- **1351B/C, 1351C · Ps. 9:29(10:8), Luke 18:2, Matt. 24:12** — all pluralized or recast into
  Richard's syntax (*qui sedent… ut interficiant*; *qui nec Deum timent nec homines
  verentur*; *abundante iniquitate* for *quoniam abundavit iniquitas*). Author's weaving, not
  the plate's divergence.
- **1354D · 1 John 2:19** — *Exierunt a nobis* for Vulg. *Ex nobis prodierunt*. Different
  verb, identical English ("they went out from us"); declined.
- **1357D · Ps. 26:2** — *Appropiant super me nocentes* drops the Vulgate's opening *Dum*,
  turning a subordinate into a main clause. No assertion changes.
- **1358A · Ps. 5:11 + Ps. 13:3** — the two half-verses are conflated under one note
  `(Psal. V)`. **Diffed against 1253B (chunk 0009), which prints the second half alone**
  (*Linguis suis dolose agebant, venenum aspidum sub labiis eorum*) under `(Psal. XIII)`, and
  against 1272-ish in 0019, which quotes the same half unitalicized. The conflation is the
  plate's, the wording of each half is the Vulgate's; nothing marked.
- **1355C** — *Deus superbis resistat, humilibus autem det gratiam* is noted `(Luc. IV)`; the
  text is Jas. 4:6 / 1 Pet. 5:5. Note kept verbatim (§D).

## C. Passages that would not construe, rendered as printed

- **1345C · *qualis ejusmodi fuerit*** — `Debet autem anima aliquando diligenter quaerere,
  qualis ejusmodi fuerit`. *qualis* and *ejusmodi* stack two "of what sort" expressions on
  one clause. Control: `"ejusmodi fuerit"` returns **zero** hits in the PL TEI outside this
  site. Rendered flatly — "what sort of thing of this kind it was". No conjecture proposed;
  *quondam* would give good sense but I have no evidence for it.
- **1346B · *Amplificatio magnitudinis, ex virtute humilitatis adjuncta, divinitus gratia
  confirmationis.*** — verbless. Rendered with the copula supplied as plain text (no
  brackets, per the brief), reading *adjuncta* with *virtute*: "The amplification of
  greatness, from the virtue of humility adjoined, is divinely the grace of confirmation."
- **1348A · *quod tertius in seipso proprio experimento probavit*** — "which he, a **third**,
  proved in his own self by his own trial". *tertius* has no antecedent: the subject is God,
  the experiencer Nabuchodonosor. Rendered literally. Likeliest conjecture *ipse*; offered
  here with low confidence and **not** in the text.
- **1348A · *Ante forte illud… voluit… imprimere… ?*** — the sentence opens with *Ante* and
  closes with a question mark that only an interrogative particle would license; *An forte*
  would give exactly the printed punctuation. **Declined as a `[cj:]` — see §E.** Rendered
  "Beforehand, perhaps, he wished…?", question mark carried per Pattern 8.
- **1349B–C · *animus ad semetipsum redit atque redarguit acriterque reprehendit*** — the two
  latter verbs have no expressed object. Rendered without supplying one ("returns unto
  itself, and confutes, and sharply rebukes"); the next clause (*Sibi irascitur, sibi
  indignatur*) supplies the sense for the reader without our supplying a word.
- **1354C · *Sic utique, sic saepe quod ex cordis levitate praesumitur, post in multam mentis
  fluctuationem in amaritudine.*** — no main verb, and *in amaritudine* is ablative where the
  motion-construction wants accusative. Rendered as the printed words allow, verbless.
- **1354C · *de coelo amissis aliis dejecta*** — "cast down from heaven, the **others** having
  been lost". *alis* (wings) is almost certainly meant: the sentence is entirely about the
  beast's *alae*, which were plucked off eight lines earlier. A single-letter intrusion, a
  real word, not a negation or correlative — **declined, §E**. Rendered literally.
- **1356D · *animus nondum et apertam malitiam effrenatur*** — *et* where *ad* is wanted
  (*effrenari ad* takes the goal). Rendered "not yet unbridled even to open malice", carrying
  the negation intact. Declined as a conjecture (§E).
- **1358B · *Qui enim loquitur, inquam, non potest latere.*** — the argument needs "he who
  **detracts**"; *loquitur* renders in place and is left alone.

## E. Conjectures **declined** — census candidates, named as the brief requires

Wilson's bar (a `[cj:]` is glossed only where the wrongly-printed real word is a **negation
or a correlative**) is applied and not widened. Five sites:

1. **1348A · *Ante* for *An*** (0050). The nearest to the line in this range: an
   interrogative particle governs the mood of the whole sentence, which is 7a⁗'s own question
   3, and the printed `?` is otherwise unexplained. Declined because *Ante* is neither a
   negation nor a correlative, and because 7a⁗ governs markers, not bare Pattern 7 renderings.
   **If the bar is ever widened to interrogative particles, this is a candidate.**
2. **1354C · *aliis* for *alis*** (0053). Noun; declined.
3. **1356D · *et* for *ad*** (0054). Preposition/conjunction; declined. (Note it is *not* a
   swallowed conjunction in the 7a⁗ sense — nothing is quarantined here, the negation *nondum*
   survives intact in the English.)
4. **1348A · *tertius*** (0050). No antecedent; declined, and no confident conjecture.
5. ⭐ **1357A · *redere* for *rodere*** (0054) — **the one I most nearly marked, and the
   control is what stopped me.** The clause reads *proprie proprium sit invidiae alienam
   vitam **redere** et morsibus obtrectationum lacerare*, and nine lines later the same idea
   is *qui alienam vitam libenter **rodunt*** — so *rodere*, "to gnaw", is certain from the
   plate itself. I was about to fire a `[sic:]` on the non-word class. **The corpus control
   forbids it:** `grep -rlw redere sources/pl/tei/` returns four files, and the other three
   (8611 *redere et confirmare*, 8744 *necesse est redere rationem*, 11578 *debent redere
   coronas*) all use it as an **attested medieval single-*d* spelling of *reddere***. So
   *redere* is a real word (Pattern 9 territory), merely the wrong one (Pattern 12's
   "fuges/munde" class), and it renders in place. Rendered "to **render** another's life",
   which is odd but not false, and logged here. **This is a Pattern 18 census candidate on
   the noun-slip side of the bar.**

Also declined and worth naming for the same census: **1356A · *ei non agnus sed **ut sus**
succedit*** (0053). Read *ursus*: the very next chapter opens *bestia alia, **similis urso***
and closes *Sic pardus **urso** succedit* — the identical `X <dative> succedit` frame. So
"not a lamb but as it were a **sow**" almost certainly conceals "not a lamb but a **bear**",
i.e. Pattern 10 split type (*ur sus* → *ut sus*), the same shape as the corpus's known *ur
sam* for *ursam*. **Declined as a marker** because both fragments are real words forming a
grammatical phrase that renders in place — Pattern 12's stated test — and declined as a
`[cj:]` because a noun is neither a negation nor a correlative. Rendered "there succeeds it
not a lamb, but as it were a sow". ⚠ This is the site in the range where the faithful English
most misleads, and it is here rather than in the text by the rules as they stand; if the
`[cj:]` bar is ever widened, **read this one first**.

## G. Vocabulary — calls made, and one I would like ratified for the whole work

Following the ratified table without exception; *mens* mind / *animus* spirit / *anima* soul
are kept visibly distinct throughout (they stand in adjacent clauses repeatedly in 0051 and
0053, never in one clause), *fortitudo* is "fortitude" at 1345D, *scientia* is "learning",
*cognitio* "knowledge", *intelligentia* "understanding", *discretio* "discretion",
*detrimentum* "decline", *gradus* "step", *praesumptio* "presumption", *pertinacior* "more
obstinate", *saevum* "savage", *simulatio* "dissimulation" and *simulare* "to dissemble",
*impudentia* "shamelessness".

**Requested for ratification — Book Three's own vice-chain**, which is a second four-term
scheme running alongside the ratified *arrogantia / jactantia / pertinacia / saevitia*, and
which will recur in every remaining chunk of Book Three (one beast per vice, and 0054 already
hands the leopard to the next agent):

| Latin | English chosen | why |
|---|---|---|
| *insolentia* | **insolence** | and *insolitum* / *non solitum* = "unwonted" / "not customary" in the 1352D etymology |
| *invidentia* | **enviousness** | ⚠ must stay distinct from *invidia* = **envy**: 1356A opens *Plus aliquid sonare videtur invidentia quam invidia*, so collapsing them destroys the chapter's first sentence |
| *fraudulentia* | **fraudulence** | |
| *impudentia* | shamelessness | already ratified; listed to show the chain closes on it |

And the three degrees of the bear's teeth at 1357A–B, which must also stay distinct because
Richard defines each against the others: *oblocutio* = **speaking-against** · *derogatio* =
**derogation** · *detractio* = **detraction**. (*oblocutio* has no one-word English; the
hyphenated noun was chosen over "reproach", which is already *exprobratio* in the Ps. 43
lemma printed in the same paragraph.)

Two further pairs held apart deliberately: *infrenata* = **uncurbed** vs *effrenata* =
**unbridled** malice (1351D, printed four words apart in one list), and *praecipitatio* =
**precipitancy** vs *praesumptio* = presumption (the two eagle-wings, 1353B).

⚠ **One tension logged against my own judgement, as the brief requires.** At 1351A the
Latin is *zelum rectitudinis habeant, sed secundum scientiam non habent* — an unmarked echo
of Rom. 10:2, where every English ear wants "not according to **knowledge**". The ratified
table gives *scientia* = **learning** and *cognitio* = knowledge, and the quotation is
neither italicized nor noted, so the scripture-echo licence does not reach it. **Followed the
table**: "not according to learning". Flagging it in case the table wants an exception for
Rom. 10:2 specifically.

## H. Italics added where the plate has none — declared

Three sites where Richard glosses a Latin word **as a word** and the plate prints it in roman:
1352D (*insolitum* / *non solitum* / *insolentia*), 1356A (*invidens* / *invidentia*), 1357B
(*rogare* / *derogare*). In each the Latin word is kept in the English and set in italics, on
the mention-vs-use principle; without the word the gloss explains nothing, and without the
italics it reads as a typesetting error. This is the only place in the range where italic
structure is not 1:1 with the Latin twin. **Declared here rather than done silently**; if a
verifier enforces strict italic parity, these are the three.
