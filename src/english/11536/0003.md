## CHAPTER XV. *How the spirit is to be advanced step by step to contempt of self.*

[1082C]

The first thing, then, for each man is to restrain love of self to a fixed measure, that afterward he may deserve to see that great and astounding miracle also: the Jordan, namely, first indeed fixing its course, then swelling, at last going backward and ascending to the higher places. And that first thing indeed — namely, to check excess — although great, is nevertheless not wondrous; but to bend the course back toward the mountains is a wonderful spectacle and a spectacular miracle. Altogether and beyond doubt it is one thing to temper love of self, and another to hold it back and turn it aside. Moderated love of self is one thing, and great contempt of self another. I think it was not without cause that such a miracle was celebrated nowhere but in the Jordan, [1082D] which is interpreted *their descent.* But what is that descent but the humbling of the heart? Or of whom is it "their," unless of those very ones who descend and humble themselves willingly? For those whose descent is not voluntary — it is indeed in them, but in a certain way it is not theirs, because it does not profit them. It must therefore be of their own accord, that it may profit them and may truly be called theirs. Are they [sic: *ereduntur*] to have descended of their own accord, or to have profited anything in this, of whom it is read: *Thou hast cast them down while they were lifted up? [n: (Psal. LXXXVIII.)]* He therefore who wishes to see that great miracle must descend from every elation of mind and be humbled by a willing will — not like those who were cast down unwillingly, to the praise of God indeed, though to no profit of their own. So then, only in the Jordan, in the descent [1083A] of them, in the descent of those who descend, in the humbling of those who humble themselves, is so renowned a deed and heavenly miracle wrought. The greater thou art, therefore, humble thyself in all things, and thou keepest the banks of the Jordan: worthy that the Jordan give way to thee, that the Lord show thee miracles.

## CHAPTER XVI. *How, when vain love has been drained away, the spirit is enlarged in the love of God.*

Wouldst thou see the waters of the Jordan fix their course and stand in one place? *But for me,* he says, *it is good to cleave to God; to put my hope in the Lord God [n: (Psal. LXXXII)]*. Wouldst thou see the lower waters run down and fail? *My soul refused to be comforted [n: (Psal. LXXXVI)]*. Wouldst thou see the upper waters swell and run upward? *As the hart longeth after the fountains [1083B] of waters, so longeth my soul after thee, O God [n: (Psal. XLI)]*. In short, do thou also not go after thy concupiscences, and thou hast fixed the waters. Love not the world, nor the things that are in the world: and the lower waters, which run downward, have failed. In short, delight thou in the Lord, and thou wilt see them stand fixed in one place. Love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart, and with thy whole soul, and with thy whole mind, and thou wilt see the waters swell vehemently and rise up — nor is it a wonder — into a great mass. I think thou canst find nothing whereby to spread them more widely than to pour them out with the whole heart, and to stretch them through the whole soul, and out of them to fill the whole mind. O how vehemently this saving wave had swelled in the breast of him who said: *I desire to be dissolved, [1083C] and to be with Christ! [n: (Philipp. VIII.)]* To this same sense runs that which the Prophet, in singing, declared of himself: *My soul hath fainted after thy salvation [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*. But what manner of inundation of waters, I ask, was that, where a father is compelled to cry out for a parricide son? *Absalom my son, my son Absalom: who would grant me that I might die for thee, Absalom my son? [n: (II Reg. XXIX.)]* I think no place could be found whither they might spread themselves more widely. For no one has greater charity than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends. So too that inundation swelled not a little, concerning which we have the Lord's own testimony: *Many sins are forgiven her, because she hath loved much [n: (Luc. VII)]*. What wonder, I ask, if those things appeared from afar which had swelled much? Do you wish to know how far off appears [1083D] the inundation of that love which surged so greatly, which bowed itself even to the kissing and the anointing of the feet: *Amen I say to you, wheresoever this Gospel shall be preached in the whole world, that also which she hath done shall be told in memory of her [n: (Matth. XXVI)]*. But do you wish to know yet more openly how far off they can appear, if only they have swelled after the fashion of a mountain? *In this shall all men know that you are my disciples, if you have love one toward another [n: (Joan. XIII)]*. How far off, I beseech thee, does that appear which all men must know? But it is necessary, as we have already said, that the streams of true charity swell after the fashion of a mountain, if they would appear so far off. But perhaps thou art still seeking the mountain, after whose likeness thou readest that the waters swelled. See whether perhaps it be that curdled mountain, [1084A] the fat mountain, the mountain of the house of the Lord prepared on the top of the mountains — inasmuch as the Lord has exalted him and given him a name which is above every name, that in the name of Jesus every thing may bend the knee, of things in heaven, on earth, and under the earth. After the fashion of this mountain, after the likeness of that great and wonderful love by which he laid down his life for his sheep, let him know that the waters must swell who desires them to appear from afar and to fulfil the Lord's precept: *This is my precept, that you love one another, as I have loved you [n: (ibid.)]*. Hear the likeness, and apply imitation. As I have loved you, he says. The likeness of love is after the fashion of a mountain. How much did he love us, who died for our sake? Can it be that they did not hold the likeness of this mountain in their love [1084B] — they who for Christ were stoned, were cut asunder, were tempted, died in the slaying of the sword? For this reason, as I judge, their sound went out into all the earth and their words unto the ends of the world: that they might appear from afar, that they might be seen by all, and that all might imitate them. But why, I ask, do we wonder if those waters appeared from afar which, as it is written: *swelled even unto Sartan? [n: (Josue III.)]* Sartan is interpreted *their tribulation.* But what else is their tribulation, according to what we have already said concerning the Jordan, but the tribulation of the tribulated? And a voluntary tribulation is each man's own. For what each man takes up patiently, or even gladly, he makes his own, because he compels it to serve his own profit. As therefore the descent is the descent of those who willingly [1084C] humble themselves, so also the tribulation is the tribulation of those who for the Lord's sake love tribulation. And so that tribulation is the tribulation of the tribulated which is reckoned to the tribulated for merit. See, then, those waters which were the upper ones — how much they had grown, how much they had swelled, which had stretched themselves even unto Sartan. O love, strong as death, thou who dost stretch thyself even unto Sartan, and dost spread thy bosom even unto voluntary tribulation? What manner of enlargement of love, I ask, is this; what manner of inundation of waters — to stretch and enlarge one's own heart even unto voluntary tribulation, unto willing suffering, unto a death that is wished for? Doubt not, then, that the upper waters have stretched themselves to that place, as often as thou seest men rejoicing in tribulation [1084D] and loving their own tribulation for the Lord's sake, as the apostles went rejoicing from the presence of the council, because they were held worthy to suffer reproach for the name of Jesus.
