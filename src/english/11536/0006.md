## CHAPTER V. *How, after the full correction of self, the spirit is brought in to the contemplation of eternal things.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No one, then, ought to neglect this very thing which can be comprehended from the consideration of his own corruption, nor to look upon it negligently and as it were in passing; [1091A] but straightway, at the entering in of the ark and the dipping of the feet, to fix his step and to weave delays, until the upper water grow and the lower fail, and the whole people of the Hebrews pass over. ⟦s2⟧ For as soon as we have begun to review and to examine our life, and to dip the feet of our searching in the water, as soon as we have felt our spirit affected and, at the consideration of itself, marvelling and growing indignant, we ought gladly to linger in this spectacle of our own wonder, until the love of inward things swell, but the love of lower things wholly fail, and the whole throng of our desires pass over, and touch and hold the land of promise by the certitude of its own hope. ⟦s3⟧ Then at last, as I judge, it will be time that the ark itself of our intelligence may enter the land of promise, [1091B] when it has sent on before it the people of all its vows and wills. ⟦s4⟧ For it is necessary first to believe, to hope for, to love, and greatly to desire the good things of the Lord in the land of the living, before it is granted us to know and to understand them, to see them face to face. ⟦s5⟧ *Unless,* he says, *you believe, you shall not understand [n: (Isa. VII)]*. ⟦s6⟧ *He that loveth me,* he says, *shall be loved by my Father, and I will manifest myself to him [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. ⟦s7⟧ Love, then, precedes manifestation, that manifestation may follow love. ⟦s8⟧ Let the throng of affections go before the ark, that the ark of contemplation may follow after the throng. ⟦s9⟧ The land of promise must be held first by desire rather than by understanding. ⟦s10⟧ I think that the Apostle had brought over the whole multitude of his thoughts and of his affections when he said: *I desire to be dissolved, and to be with Christ* [1091C] [n: (Philipp. I)]. ⟦s11⟧ For what had he left himself of the desires of this life, who did not even love life itself, by reason of his exceeding desire for Christ? ⟦s12⟧ Hence it is, as I judge, that he prevailed to bring the ark of his contemplation into the land flowing with milk and honey, that he might see the good things of Jerusalem all the days of his life, and might learn from the Lord's revelation that which eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, nor hath it ascended into the heart of man. ⟦s13⟧ I lie, if he himself does not glory that a man of this kind was caught up even to the third heaven, and there heard secret words which it is not lawful for a man to speak.

## CHAPTER VI. *That in the life to come, after the contemplation of eternal things, the spirit is loosed to every good pleasure of its own desire.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1091D]I think that we ourselves also, after the passage of the inward household and of so great a multitude, when we have begun to bring our ark of contemplation in thither, and to see no longer through a glass and in a riddle, but face to face — when we shall hold in act what meanwhile we hold in affection — it will then no longer be necessary for the Jordan to hold back the streams of its own channel and to check by resisting the rush of its own course. ⟦s2⟧ Far be it, far be it — when this corruptible shall have put on incorruption, and this mortal shall have put on immortality — far be it, I say, far be it, that it should still be necessary to chastise the body and to bring it into servitude, and even then to be mortified all the day long: *When the just,* Christ himself also bearing witness, *shall shine as the sun in the kingdom of their Father [n: (Matth. XIII)]*, and the wicked man shall be taken away, that he may not see the glory of God. ⟦s3⟧ It will not [1092A] be said to us any more at all: Love not the world, nor the things that are in the world — when the Lord shall have made a new heaven and a new earth, according as the creature itself shall be delivered from [sic: *liberabitura*] the servitude of corruption into the liberty of the sons of God. ⟦s4⟧ And although the comeliness in creatures shall then be incomparably greater and more beautiful than now, and sweeter in bodily gratifications, more abundant and more delightful, there will be nothing that can lessen the concupiscences of the spirit in all these things which the bodily sense shall touch. ⟦s5⟧ The concupiscence of the flesh shall not know how, the concupiscence of the eyes shall not know how, to extinguish the concupiscences of the spirit, but to kindle them; not to cool them, but to inflame them more keenly. ⟦s6⟧ Whatever shall meet it without, whatever within, will minister nourishment to spiritual love, to divine love. ⟦s7⟧ Then beyond doubt [1092B] the spirit will be delighted within, and will be delighted without. ⟦s8⟧ It will be delighted in spiritual things, it will be delighted in bodily things, and it will go in and go out, and will find pastures. ⟦s9⟧ Then the waters of the Jordan will return into their own channel, and will flow as they had been wont before. ⟦s10⟧ The channel of the Jordan is the appetite for what is advantageous. ⟦s11⟧ In a channel of this kind the waters of Jordan are borne with a rush, because the desires of the heart are drawn off hither and thither according to the appetite for their own advantage. ⟦s12⟧ Whither dost thou think a running down of waters of this kind tends, if not into good pleasure? ⟦s13⟧ What, then, is it for the waters to run as they had been wont before, but to follow good pleasure and to have laid hold of it? ⟦s14⟧ Naturally indeed every appetite is drawn to what is advantageous, and no one is forbidden there to follow the leading of this channel in his own desire. ⟦s15⟧ Many things please here which nevertheless are not seemly, [1092C] and therefore ought not to be done. ⟦s16⟧ It is necessary, therefore, often in this life to check the rush of the waters, and manfully to break the violence of the concupiscences. ⟦s17⟧ Nothing there will please but what will also be lawful. ⟦s18⟧ It will therefore do no harm that the streams of desires flow out at will, and be drawn off to every good pleasure. ⟦s19⟧ The license of this liberty, as I judge, the Prophet was hoping for and sighing after when in singing he proclaimed: *Make the free offerings of my mouth well-pleasing, O Lord [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*. ⟦s20⟧ Often, and beyond measure, the free offerings of the mouth and the good pleasures of the heart are other things. ⟦s21⟧ For we covet one thing with the mind, and other are the things which we approve with the mouth. ⟦s22⟧ Very often indeed those things are other which please by sentence, and other those which please by concupiscence. ⟦s23⟧ The former please from judgment, [1092D] the latter from desire. ⟦s24⟧ The former from definition, the latter from delight. ⟦s25⟧ The Prophet asks, therefore, that the very same things which are vowed may also be well-pleasing, that they may come together into one and always meet one another: the vow and the good pleasure, the sentence and the concupiscence, the judgment and the desire, the definition and the delight. ⟦s26⟧ Make the free offerings of my mouth well-pleasing, O Lord: that whatever pleases from discretion may please from delight, and that those things alone may please from delight which are lawful from deliberation. ⟦s27⟧ Let it pass over, then; let the people of the Hebrews pass over; let the ark also pass over after them into that land flowing with milk and honey, into that supernal and everlasting delightfulness, that at length it may one day see face to face what it had before sought so earnestly with much desire. ⟦s28⟧ Let that great troop of so many desires pass over; [1093A] let that theoric ark of intelligence pass over also into those inward and eternal joys, that then at least the Jordan may receive a free course, and every human affection may roll itself licitly and freely into every good pleasure, and the human spirit (as one cleaving firmly and immovably to the highest and spiritual goods) may find nothing of contradiction in things outward and lower. ⟦s29⟧ Unless I am mistaken, that wave which had swelled above will run back into its own channel with a greater rush than before, and will spread more widely, and will exceed the measure of its former limitation — inasmuch as the love of the Word and of creatures finds most abundant nourishment for a greater burning, and for this reason rushes toward all things on every side with a more fervent desire.
