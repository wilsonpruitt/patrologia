## CHAPTER VII. *How some, even in this life, are lifted up to the contemplation of eternal things.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1093B]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But that which is read concerning the passage of the ark seems to be able to be fulfilled in some manner even in this life. ⟦s2⟧ That apostolic testimony proves this, which we brought forward above also; and we do not doubt that he who still says such things was living in this corruptible life. ⟦s3⟧ *I know,* he says, *a man in Christ — whether in the body, or out of the body, I know not, God knoweth — such a one caught up even to the third heaven: and I know such a man, that he was caught up into paradise, and heard secret words which it is not lawful for a man to speak [n: (II Cor. XII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Behold where he had brought in the ark of his contemplation, who had fixed the eye of intelligence even upon the secrets of the third heaven. ⟦s5⟧ But I think that not even [1093C] Paul himself would have lifted the ark as far as that place, had he not first sent thither the whole household of his desires, so far that he counted all things as dung, that he might gain Christ. ⟦s6⟧ Many, as I judge, possess by merit this land of promise of which we speak, who cannot bring their ark in thither after the likeness of Paul, inasmuch as they still keep back by desire, outside this land, some of that inward household of their affections. ⟦s7⟧ But when all this throng of the Hebrews shall have crossed the Jordan, when the ark of the covenant itself shall have been brought in after them into the land of promise, what harm, I ask, will it now do to resume its course and, after its custom, to flow back into its own channel — provided only it do not overpass the banks of fitting usefulness and of just [1093D] necessity, provided only that, following the channel of equity and of modesty, it do not flow out into the field of superfluous liberty? ⟦s8⟧ For what is it for the throng of the Hebrews to have crossed the channel of the Jordan, but that heavenly desires, tending toward the glory to come, have so far subdued the appetite of the flesh that it can no longer ensnare or hinder them? ⟦s9⟧ And indeed, before the people of the Lord pass over with the Lord's ark, lest the wave of the Jordan hinder their passage, it is necessary to restrain its running down, and to break the rush of the flesh, and often to turn the spirit away even from a necessary concupiscence. ⟦s10⟧ For never is human affection perfectly inflamed to the desire of eternity, never is human understanding fully sharpened to the contemplation of eternal things, unless the care of the flesh [1094A] be frequently and strongly repelled even in things lawful and very necessary. ⟦s11⟧ So far, then, must the body be chastised and brought into servitude, until it can no longer in any way hinder the progress of merits. ⟦s12⟧ But after it has let pass the throng going by and the ark accompanying it, in so far as it will not be able to stand in the way of spiritual exercises, it will be lawful for each man to follow the channel of his own advantage. ⟦s13⟧ Hence it is that we have often now seen many afflict the body first with wondrous abstinence, and fall into grave, nay incurable, diseases; and these same men, after concupiscence had been subdued, after grace received from God, loosen the reins of their former rigor, and indulge their body in food and drink, in [sic: *sommo*] and clothing, far more than the keeping of health would have required. ⟦s14+15⟧ For this often serves spiritual [1094B] pursuits, that a man full of grace guards or repairs the strength of the body, so far as he usefully can. Yet he who now indulges himself according to this license ought to beware lest he loose the waters too much, lest they forsake the channel of his own advantage and overpass the banks of modesty; let them flow at least as they had been wont before — those waters of which it is written that they had filled the banks of their channel. ⟦s16⟧ This, as I judge, they alone know how to do well who now savor the things that are above, who have now tasted the sweetness of heavenly things, who loathe earthly things, who have now brought their ark into that land of promise. ⟦s17⟧ No one can, as I judge, attain to the perfect summit of outward goods who has not yet deserved to feel by tasting the sweetness of inward goods. ⟦s18⟧ I think that unless the Prophet had first [1094C] tasted how sweet the Lord is, and how great is the multitude of the sweetness of his house — I think that unless he had learned this by experience, he could not at all have said that from his own sentence: *My soul refused to be comforted [n: (Psal. LXXVI)]*. ⟦s19⟧ That it may not therefore always be necessary to check the course of the Jordan, we must carry our ark over into the parts beyond it; but neither is that permitted, unless we can send on before all that throng of our vows.

## CHAPTER VIII. *That we ought always, through the zeal of contemplation, to foresee whither we should tend by desire.*

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Assuredly this ark ought neither to follow everywhere, nor always to be sent on before. ⟦s2⟧ In the place of horror and of vast solitude let it go before the throng; let it follow after the throng [1094D] as it enters into the land of promise. ⟦s3⟧ Let it go before toward our own things, let it follow after toward divine things. ⟦s4⟧ On this side of the Jordan, the things that pertain to us; beyond the Jordan, the things that are above us. ⟦s5⟧ Here we are bidden to examine and to approve and afterward to hold: *Prove all things,* says the Apostle, *hold that which is good [n: (I Thess. V)]*. ⟦s6⟧ There we are commanded to believe, that one day we may deserve also to understand: *Unless you believe,* he says, *you shall not understand [n: (Isa. VII)]*. ⟦s7⟧ We ought therefore, so long as we are still in the desert, so long as we make our journey through the wilderness, to send our ark on before us, and subtly to foresee whither the household of the virtues ought to tend, following after by zeal or by desire: *That you may be able,* says the Lord, *to see and to know by what way you enter, because you have not walked [1095A] by it before [n: (Josue III)]*. ⟦s8⟧ Hence that of the Wise Man's sentence chimes in with this, when he says: *And let thine eyelids go before thy steps [n: (Prov. IV)]*. ⟦s9⟧ But toward those things which are beyond the Jordan, which exceed our sense, we strive better by desire than by zeal, by affection than by understanding — inasmuch as we are bidden to send on before the throng, not the ark, and it is better in this part to desire much than to examine much, that we may deserve to be brought in thither where we cannot break in by our own strength. ⟦s10⟧ For he who is a searcher of majesty shall be overwhelmed by glory [n: (Prov. XXV)]. ⟦s11⟧ There, then, let it go before, that it may show the way.
