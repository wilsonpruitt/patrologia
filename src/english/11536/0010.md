## CHAPTER XII. *On the twofold premeditation, namely of rewards and of merits.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But it is one thing to seek of what sort, or how great, or of what kind are the goods we ought to hope for from the recompense of merits, and another to investigate zealously by what pursuits [1099B] or exercises we shall be able manfully to obtain them. ⟦s2⟧ The former is first, the latter second. ⟦s3⟧ The first is to investigate the goods to be hoped for, the second the goods to be done. ⟦s4⟧ For who would gird himself bravely for the contests of labors, unless he knew beforehand the greatness of the wage from the divine recompense? ⟦s5⟧ But what will it profit to believe and to desire the eternal recompense, unless with equal zeal a man seek to know by what pursuits of merits he may acquire it? ⟦s6⟧ It is necessary, therefore, to send messengers ahead after messengers, and to dispatch other scouts after the first. ⟦s7⟧ The first messengers are the scouts of rewards, the second messengers are the scouts of merits. ⟦s8⟧ The former are scouts of recompenses, the latter are scouts of works. ⟦s9⟧ So indeed the scouts who are sent by Moses are one set, and those scouts [1099C] who are sent by Joshua another. ⟦s10⟧ Some are they whom the prophet of the Lord sends ahead, others they whom the athlete of the Lord sends ahead. ⟦s11⟧ The office of the prophet is one thing, the office of the athlete another. ⟦s12⟧ The office of the prophet is to foretell things to come, but the office of the athlete is to preordain the things to be done. ⟦s13⟧ To the one it belongs to know beforehand the things firmly to be believed; to the other it belongs to premeditate the things bravely to be done. ⟦s14⟧ The one is of foreknowledge, the other is of providence. ⟦s15⟧ It is the part of the former to foresee, of the latter to provide. ⟦s16⟧ For what is foresight, but the scouting of things to come? ⟦s17⟧ And what is providence, but the scouting of things to be done? ⟦s18⟧ We have, therefore, two kinds of scouts; each busy, each necessary. ⟦s19⟧ The first seems to belong to Moses, the second to [1099D] Joshua. ⟦s20⟧ According to this likeness, therefore, some of our thoughts ought to be foreseers of things to come, others providers of things to be done, both indeed scouts of hidden things and searchers-out of things unknown. ⟦s21⟧ Do you wish to hear a man premeditating concerning things to come, after the likeness of Moses, sending scouts ahead before his face? ⟦s22⟧ *I meditated,* he says, *in the night with my heart. ⟦s23⟧ Will God cast off for ever, and will he add no more, that he should be yet more well-pleased? or will he cut off his mercy unto the end, from generation to generation? or will God forget to show mercy? or will he in his anger shut up his mercies? [n: (Psal. LXXVI.)]* Do you wish to hear a man providing concerning things to be done, and after the likeness of Joshua appointing scouts before himself? ⟦s24⟧ *What,* he says, *shall I render to the Lord for all the things [1100A] that he has rendered to me? [n: (Psal. CXV.)]* Do you wish to hear one of the subtle scouts — what he found by scouting, or what he answered out of his own judgment to that first question of the Prophet? ⟦s25⟧ *God,* he says, *shall wipe away every tear from the eyes of the saints, and now there shall be no more mourning, nor crying, nor any sorrow at all, for the former things are passed away [n: (Apoc. XXI)]*. ⟦s26⟧ Behold how favorable a God this man foresaw in the time to come — he concerning whom David asks whether he will at some time be more well-pleased. ⟦s27⟧ But it looks, as I judge, to the second scouting, what he found elsewhere, whence also he says: *It is good to give praise to the Lord, and to sing to thy name, O Most High [n: (Psal. XCI)]*. ⟦s28⟧ To the first scouts, therefore, it belongs to persuade negligent minds of what is just; but to the second, to instruct minds that are ready and prepared for all things for the things [1100B] to be done. ⟦s29⟧ Consider therefore what sort of tidings good scouts are wont to bring back from those regions, or how much these very things which they bring back avail to raise up minds; and strive to seek out other things and to report them, that you too may be able to raise up the minds of your hearers into longings for the like, it is the voice of a scout: *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality [n: (I Cor. XV)]*. ⟦s30⟧ It is the voice of a scout: *The just shall shine, and shall run to and fro like sparks among the reeds; they shall judge nations, and shall reign for ever [n: (Sap. III)]*. ⟦s31⟧ It is the voice of a scout: *How great is the multitude of thy sweetness, O Lord, which thou hast hidden for them that fear thee! [n: (Psal. XXX.)]* Is that excellent scout to be thought not to have entered that land flowing with milk and honey, and not diligently [1100C] to have surveyed it once seen, when he dares so boldly to promise to those who are to enter it? ⟦s32⟧ *They shall be inebriated with the plenty of thy house, and thou shalt give them to drink of the torrent of thy pleasure [n: (Psal. XXXV)]*. ⟦s33⟧ Does he not seem in these words to run together with that saying of the scouts, where it is said: *We entered,* they say, *into the land to which thou didst send us, which truly flows with milk and honey, as can be known from these fruits [n: (Num. XIII)]*.

## CHAPTER XIII. *How one must press more vehemently upon the speculation of rewards.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ One must, as I judge, apply oneself to this scouting more vehemently than to the other, and press upon the first meditation more frequently than upon the second, because it is easier to discern what is just than to persuade to what is laborious. [1100D] Hence it is, I judge, that the children of Israel send more men on the first mission, but on the second scouting fewer are sent. ⟦s2⟧ And rightly indeed. ⟦s3⟧ For in the first searching out, as has been said, there is made the scouting of rewards; but in the second scouting there is made the searching out of merits. ⟦s4⟧ But the rewards of the blessedness to come are incomparably greater than any merits of ours whatsoever can be. ⟦s5⟧ For the sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come that shall be revealed in us. ⟦s6⟧ Many, therefore, are they who are sent the first time, but few they who are sent the second. ⟦s7⟧ Rightly are many sent where many things are sought, and for few things to be sought few are dispatched. ⟦s8⟧ There we find twelve, here two. ⟦s9⟧ There the number six, here the number one is doubled [1101A]. ⟦s10⟧ Unity, from which the number six takes its beginning, rightly designates inception, just as the number six, by reason of its own perfection, is agreed to designate perfection. ⟦s11⟧ What therefore is sought there pertains to perfection, not to inception; but what is found here seems to pertain to inception, and not to perfection. ⟦s12⟧ There is the perfection of our glorification; but here there is only a certain inception of our justification. ⟦s13⟧ In that glorification there will be the emptying out of all evils and the fullness of all goods. ⟦s14⟧ But in this our justification there is neither a full extermination of vices nor a perfect consummation of virtues. ⟦s15⟧ There, full security, perfect gladness. ⟦s16⟧ Here, neither full purity nor perfect integrity. ⟦s17⟧ There is full security, where no evil is feared. ⟦s18⟧ There is perfect gladness, where every good is possessed. [1101B] Here there is no full purity, where vice struggles against us; here no perfect integrity, where infirmity holds sway. ⟦s19⟧ There, therefore, out of the twofold perfection of security and gladness one blessedness is consummated, so that the number six is doubled and the number twelve is completed; here, out of the twofold imperfection of purity and integrity, our justification takes its beginning, so that from the doubling of unity each number six may take the beginning of its own perfection unto the consummation of the number twelve. ⟦s20⟧ Rightly, therefore, are twelve scouts sent on that mission, where the blessedness of the twofold perfection is sought by investigation. ⟦s21⟧ Rightly are two dispatched on that delegation, where scarcely certain beginnings of a perfect justification are found. ⟦s22⟧ Finally there are twelve, if I remember well, who are sent on the first scouting, and there are twelve [1101C] things which they are bidden to scout out. ⟦s23⟧ But there are also two who are sent on the second scouting, and two things which they are commanded to search out. ⟦s24⟧ There are many things that could be said concerning the first scouting; but these, that they may be worthily set forth, require a *syntasma* of their own.
