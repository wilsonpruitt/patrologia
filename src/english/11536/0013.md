## CHAPTER VII. *On the solidity of fear.*

[1107D]How useful, I ask, do we suppose that stone to be which we set in the first place, namely the solidity of fear, through which the mind is founded upon the solid ground of humility. And how much it avails, that saying of the Lord proves which says: *Upon whom shall my Spirit rest, save upon the humble, and the quiet, and him that trembleth at my words? [n: (Isa. LVI.)]* This is the stone of stumbling and the rock of scandal, unless perchance you doubt that this is the stone which the builders rejected, and which is become the head of the corner. For proud minds, and such as wish to establish their own righteousness, are wont to suffer nothing more grievously, to be indignant at nothing more, than to appear despised and to be seen as worthless.

## CHAPTER VIII. *On the weight of compunction.*

[1108A]

But the second stone also, which has been called the weight of compunction, I think can be found in Jeremiah, who sought waters for his head that he might bewail the slain of his people [n: (Jer. V)]; and I do not doubt that it was given him by him who turned the rock into pools of waters, and the crags into fountains of waters. This same rock I think can be found in Peter, in that, touched inwardly with grief of heart, he went forth outside and wept bitterly, striking the flint twice; and most abundant waters came forth, and there was made a watering above and a watering below.

## CHAPTER IX. *On the longsuffering of hope.*

[1108B]The third rock, which is the longsuffering of hope, I do not doubt can be found in Job, from which he poured out for himself rivers of oil, wherewith also he soothed the pains of his body. For he hoped until the morning amid so many losses and wounds of griefs, never drained of the oil of his hope, nowhere diffident of the anointing of the divine mercy: *Although he should kill me,* saying, *I will hope in him [n: (Job XIII)]*.

## CHAPTER X. *On the integrity of charity.*

The fourth rock likewise I find in Peter, to whom we do not doubt it was said by the Lord: *Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church [n: (Matth. XVI)]*, in that he loved the Christ of the Lord with his [1108C] whole heart, and with his whole soul, and with his whole mind, truly acknowledging that he was the Christ, the Son of the living God. Where, I beseech you, is the integrity of charity to be found, if you do not find it in Peter, who before the searcher of hearts dares to profess and to say: *Lord, thou knowest that I love thee? [n: (Joan. XXI.)]*

## CHAPTER XI. *On the maturity of gladness.*

But who would dare to doubt that the fifth rock is in that people whom the Prophet of the Lord, among the other teachings of his prophecy, calls blessed? *Blessed,* he says, *is the people that knoweth jubilation [n: (Psal. LXXXVIII)]*. Who indeed would deny that they are blessed whom the Lord fed with the fat of wheat, and filled them with honey out of the rock: for he laid up gladness and exultation in store [1108D] upon them. You perceive, as I judge, what sort of thing it is to possess this stone also among the rest, which flows with the honey of gladness, and satisfies with inward sweetness him that enters into the joy of his Lord. What will it be, I ask, as often as occasion serves, to be filled with honey out of the rock, and on the way to drink of the torrent of pleasure and be inebriated? This is that fifth stone, as has been said, which has been called the maturity of gladness. Let him therefore have nothing of levity, let nothing perishable, nothing carnal have savor for him; but let him compose his mind to the great weight of maturity, who hungers and thirsts for this alone which flows from this stone.

## CHAPTER XII. *On the harshness of severity.*

But that man, as I judge, was fighting in the sixth stone, [1109A] to whom it was said by the Lord: *Behold, I have made thy face stronger than their faces, and thy forehead harder than their foreheads; as adamant and as flint have I made thy face [n: (Ezech. III)]*. Jeremiah therefore was grinding down the provoking house of Israel with adamant and flint, in that it was of a worn-down forehead and a hard heart; and standing in the gate of the house of the Lord he announced to them their crimes, fearing neither their words nor their faces. This stone that king holds in his hands who is strong and mighty in battle, in whom the strong man armed was cast down, and the warlike man from his youth. For David prevailed against the Philistine with a sling and a stone in the name of the Lord. You surely see with what arms that stripling, both despised and small of stature, cast down Goliath, that most monstrous giant, in that [1109B] he had been taught by the Lord to have [sic: *manns*] skilled for the fight and his fingers for war. He had indeed arms not carnal, but mighty through God unto the destruction of fortifications; and he was in them fighting and destroying every height that lifted itself up against the knowledge of God, and bringing into captivity every understanding unto the obedience of Christ. For he understood above all that taught him, and therefore he confidently announced the justice of the Lord in the great Church, as he himself both bore witness of himself concerning the past and promises concerning the future: *I have declared,* he says, *thy justice in the great Church; lo, I will not restrain my lips: O Lord, thou hast known it [n: (Psal. XXXIX)]*. And so you have ready to hand to avenge all disobedience: he rebuked the haughty, he corrected the unquiet, and in the stone of most harsh rebuke and in the harshness [1109C] of correction he cast down the adversaries of truth. And though he was a man most gentle among his people and most meek — *Remember,* saying, *O Lord, David, and all his meekness [n: (Psal. CXXXI)]* — though he was, I say, of so great meekness, nonetheless he slew in the morning all the sinners of the earth, that he might destroy out of the city of the Lord all that work iniquity.

## CHAPTER XIII. *On the austerity of abstinence.*

We have said that the seventh stone is the austerity of abstinence. Upon this rock the father of Samson offered a holocaust to the Lord, and obtained to beget from a barren woman the strongest of men, to the ignominy of the nations [1109D] and to the glory of his own people Israel [n: (Judic. XIII)]. He was ashamed indeed to have fed for some while at last a barren woman, and one that beareth not; and thereafter he would not — as neither ought he — be a debtor to the flesh, to live according to the flesh; but chastising his body and bringing it into servitude, and immolating the flesh of kids upon the rock of abstinence, and consuming the broth of the flesh together with the flesh itself by fire received from heaven, and restraining the flowing things of the body, he willed thenceforth no more to go after his own lusts. And so, the assault of the flesh being tamed, he strengthened the assault of the spirit, which is made stronger by the infirmity of the flesh.
