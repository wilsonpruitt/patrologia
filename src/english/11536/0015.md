## CHAPTER XVII. *On the certainty of discretion.*

We have said that the certainty of discretion is the eleventh stone; and if we seek it diligently, we find it soonest in Paul. For he so trusts in the gift of discretion, he so presumes upon the certainty of his own counsel, that he confidently gives counsel where he has no precept of the Lord: *Concerning virgins,* he says, *I have no precept of the Lord, but I give counsel (I [1112D] Cor. VII)*. Hence it is that he sets himself before others as an example of manner of life, and shows his own life to his followers as a form of living, when he says: *Be ye imitators of me, as I also am of Christ [n: (I Cor. XL)]*. And rightly indeed, in that the Lord set his feet upon a rock and directed his steps. With stones of this kind, as I judge, was the way of those travellers paved concerning whom the Prophet cries out in wonder: *How beautiful are the feet of them that preach the gospel of peace, of them that bring glad tidings of good things! [n: (Rom. X.)]* With these stones, unless I am deceived, that great patriarch Joseph, whom the prince of peoples set as lord of his house and prince of all his possession, that he might instruct his princes as his own self, and teach his elders prudence. With these stones, I say, he is rightly believed to have paved the streets of Egypt [1113A], so much so that the children of Israel had no need to serve in clay and brick all the days of their life. The Prophet was still enduring a scarcity of these stones, and was laboring under a great want of discretion, while he was yet fixed in the mire of the deep, because the certainty of a settled counsel was not yet established for him — so much so that he cried out to the Lord and said: *How long shall I take counsels in my soul? [n: (Psal. XVIII.)]* But he would never have fully emerged from this deep of mire, had he not at last at some time found this rock of which we speak: *Upon a rock,* he says, *he hath exalted, me, and now he hath lifted up my head above my enemies [n: (Psal. XXVI)]*. He who does not pave a way for himself out of this kind of stones never walks cleanly, never plants steady steps. He therefore who wishes [1113B] to walk firmly and honorably ought to do nothing at all unadvisedly; and it is plain that he has paved a way for himself with such a stone as he ought, and he can walk securely. Do all things, says the Wise Man, with counsel, and thou shalt never repent [n: (Eccle. XXXII)].
