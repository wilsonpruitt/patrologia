## Work-wide conventions (reconciled at merge)

- ***turba* → "throng"** throughout. The noun occurs ONLY in chunks 0006–0008 and
  0009+; the first batch had rendered it "crowd" there, the second "throng", and
  the 13 first-batch instances were converted at merge. Load-bearing: Richard's
  scheme repeatedly opposes the *turba* to the *arca* (*turbam non arcam
  praemittere*, *turbam praecedat / turbae succedat*).
  **Note for future sweeps: every `turb-` string in chunks 0000–0005 is the VERB
  *turbare* / noun *turbatio* ("to trouble", "disturbance"), a different lemma.**
  A substring grep for "turba" reports 31 hits across those chunks and every one
  is a false positive. Match `turb(a|am|ae|is|as)`, not `turba`.
- ***dilectio* → "love"**, not "charity" (*charitas* owns "charity"). Fixed after
  1088B prints `in signum dilectionis et pignus charitatis` in one phrase. The
  word does not occur at all in chunks 0009–0016, so no conflict arose.
- *exterminatio* → extermination · *promotio* → advancement · *provectio* →
  furtherance · *profectus* → progress (all four kept distinct) · *arca* → ark ·
  *crimen/peccatum/culpa/scelus* → crime/sin/fault/wickedness ·
  *intelligentia* → intelligence vs *intellectus* → understanding ·
  *excessus mentis* → excess of mind · *eremus/desertum/solitudo* →
  wilderness/desert/solitude. Full tables below.

## The PL 196 negation defect — this work adds two more

At **1106D** a dropped `non` makes Richard say he *does* want to expound the twelve
tribes here, immediately after saying he did so elsewhere and will not. At
**1097D** `noluit` stands for `voluit`, making the Psalmist unwilling to ascribe
the change to God — the reverse of the argument. With 11534 @1054A, 11537 @1069D,
11531 @1000D and 11551 @0230A, that is **six instances across five works in this
one volume, running in BOTH directions** (negation dropped and negation added).
Each is rendered literally per Pattern 7. The clustering is itself the finding:
it looks like a property of how PL 196 was set, and it may deserve a targeted
sweep rather than being caught one work at a time.

### Dropped/garbled negatives and connectives — the class flagged in the brief

**1079C — `Moeror pro nobis aeternis, mare est a dextris`** (chunk 0001).
Printed *nobis* where the argument requires *bonis*: the whole sentence is the pair
"sorrow for eternal **goods** on the right hand / sorrow for eternal **evils** on the left,"
and the chapter head is *De gemina cordis compunctione*. *nobis* is a real word, so per
Pattern 7 / Pattern 12 it is rendered as ordinary English with no `[sic:]` marker:
**"Sorrow for us eternal ones is the sea on the right hand."** Near-certain compositor's
error for *bonis*. **Worth a human's eye** — it makes Richard say something the next clause
immediately contradicts.

**1095B — `Sit, inquit, inter arcam spatium cubitorum duorum millium`** (chunk 0008).
The correlative term of *inter* has dropped out. Josh. 3:4 reads *sitque inter vos et arcam
spatium cubitorum duum milium*. Rendered as printed: "Let there be, he says, between the
ark a space of two thousand cubits." **Worth a human's eye** — a genuine omission in the
printed text, of the same family as the dropped *non*/*nisi* cases at 11534 @1054A,
11537 @1069D, 11531 @1000D, 11551 @0230A.

**1095C — `Sed usque ab duo millia cubitorum ulterius progreditur`** (chunk 0008).
*ab* for *ad*: the sense demands "as far as two thousand cubits." Real word, so rendered
literally, no marker: "But he advances further, even from two thousand cubits."

**1095A — `Illic ergo praecedat, ut viam ostendat`** (chunk 0007, closing sentence).
Printed *Illic* ("there," i.e. beyond Jordan), but the immediately preceding sentence has
just argued the opposite — *jubemur turbam non arcam praemittere* — so the argument requires
*Hic*. Rendered as printed. **Worth a human's eye**: the chapter's conclusion currently
reverses its own thesis.

## Cruces

### A. Dropped or wrong negative — flagged loudly

**1. 1106D — `Horum autem rationem hoc loco ostendere jam volumus`**
Printed: *Horum autem rationem hoc loco ostendere jam volumus, de quibus quod nobis
rectius videbatur alias jam diximus.*
Rendered literally: "But the reason of these we now wish to set forth in this place,
concerning which we have already said elsewhere what seemed to us more correct."
**The sentence asserts the opposite of what it goes on to say.** The relative clause
("we have already said elsewhere what seemed to us more correct") is a refusal to
treat the matter here, and Richard has just written, four sentences earlier, *alio
loco jam diximus*. Reading required: **`non volumus`** (or `nolumus`). This is the
same defect class as 11534 @1054A, 11537 @1069D, 11531 @1000D and 11551 @0230A — a
negative dropped in this printing of PL 196. Rendered as printed per Pattern 7; a
reader of the English will meet a contradiction that is Migne's, not ours.

**2. 1097D — `Ipsum cui noluit hoc opus ascribere, maluit designare ex descriptione.`**
Rendered: "The very one to whom he was unwilling to ascribe this work, he preferred
to designate by a description."
The argument is that the Psalmist *refused to ascribe the work to himself* and
therefore named God — *"haec mutatio dexterae Excelsi"* — as elaborately as he
could. `noluit` makes him unwilling to ascribe the work **to God**, which is the
reverse. Reading required: **`voluit`**, or `cui` for `sibi`. Same class as (1);
rendered as printed.

### B. Non-word type carried through — `[sic: …]` in the English

**3. 1109B — `manns` for `manus`** (0013). *…doctas habere manns ad praelium et
digitos suos ad bellum* (Ps 143:1). Not a word; carried untranslated in italics and
marked `[sic: *manns*]`. Conjecture *manus* is certain (the verse is quoted

**4. 1106C head — `duodceim` for `duodecim`** (0012, CAPUT VI). *De duodceim
principalibus virtutibus…* Carried as `[sic: duodceim]` **without** italic

content. The chapter itself then prints *duodecim* correctly three times.

**5. 1113B–C — `lapis is tranquillitas contemplationis`** (0016). *Dictus est, ut
superius est dictum, lapis is tranquillitas contemplationis.* The run is
unconstruable — `lapis` is nominative and `tranquillitas` is nominative with no
copula between them, and `is` does nothing. Carried as `[sic: *lapis is*]`.
Conjecture: **`lapis iste`** (i.e. "that stone is called… the tranquillity of
contemplation"), the terminal *-te* dropped; the chapter's own head reads *De
quiete contemplationis*, so *tranquillitas* here is Richard's, not an intruder.

### C. Real words that are merely wrong — rendered as ordinary English, no marker

**6. 1111C — `per invisibilem formam, invisibiliumque figuram`** (0014).
Rendered: "through invisible form and the figure of invisible things."
The clause is the classic Victorine ascent formula and the very next sentence gives
it correctly: *Felix cui **visibilium** scientia fit scala ad **invisibilia**
cognoscenda*. The first `invisibilem` must be **`visibilem`**. Rendered as printed
per Pattern 7, which produces "through invisible form … into the understanding of
intelligible things" — visibly circular, which is the evidence.

**7. 1114A — `omnis inferior vis animae proprio interim induitur officio`** (0016).
Rendered: "every lower power of the soul is meanwhile clothed with its own office."
The sentence has just said that in this burial the body lies senseless, *nihil
sensualitas, nihil agit imaginatio* — the lower powers **cease** to operate.
`induitur` ("is clothed with") states the opposite; **`exuitur`** ("is stripped of")
is required. Rendered as printed.

**8. 1105C — `teipsum in humilitate custodis`** (0012). Indicative in a run of
imperatives (*memento statim…*). Rendered "you keep yourself in humility."
Reading: **`custodi`**.

**9. 1109C — `In promptu itaque habes ulcisci omnem inobedientiam, arguebat
elatos…`** (0013). Second-person present `habes` inside a sequence of third-person
imperfects about David. Rendered "And so you have ready to hand to avenge all
disobedience: he rebuked the haughty…" Reading: **`habebat`**, or `habens`.
(The phrase is Paul's, 2 Cor. 10:6, which may explain the intrusion of the
second person.)

**10. 1106B — `Debemus itaque et nos promptum et familiare haberi`** (0012). Passive
`haberi` where the construction wants active `habere` ("we ought to have ready and
familiar…"). Rendered with the passive as printed: "we too ought to be held ready
and familiar at every time of temptation." Reading: **`habere`**.

**11. 1110A — `Miraris quilibet audis`** (0014). Rendered "You wonder, whoever you
are that hear…" `quilibet` will not construe with the second-person `audis`.
Reading: **`quisquis audis`** or `quilibet audit`.

**12. 1105D — `ab hoc exsecrabili superbiae typo`** (0012). Rendered literally "from
this execrable type of pride." Sense wants the Greek τῦφος, "swelling, conceit" —
`typus` is used for the puffed-up pride of Jer. 17:5–6 just quoted, not for a
figure or foreshadowing. Kept as "type"; the reading is Migne's word and is
translatable, so no `[sic:]`.

**13. 1097C — `Vix eadem aliquando, inquit, coepi, quod qui incoepisse me credidi,
vix sero coepi fieri bonus de malo…`** (0009). The sentence will not fully
construe: `quod qui` is doubled, and `eadem` has no antecedent. Rendered as closely
as the printed words allow: "Scarcely at some time, he says, did I begin these same
things, in that I who believed myself to have begun scarcely and late began to
become good out of evil…" Probably `quia ego qui` for `quod qui`; not emended.

**14. 1097D — `Vix cum quaedam boni exordia tandem apprehendisse se memorat, ut …
ostendat.`** (0009). The `cum` has no verb of its own and `ut` no clear governing
clause. Rendered "Scarcely, when he records that he has at last laid hold of certain
beginnings of good, does he show himself to be following after his ark…" Minor;
listed for completeness.

**15. 1097D — `committere`** (0009). *Erat sane in oculis ejus admirabile talem de
malo ad bonum committere.* Rendered "to commit such a man from evil to good."
`committere` does not take *de … ad …* in this sense; **`commutare`** is the likely
reading (and is what the surrounding argument, *fieri bonus de malo*, wants).
Real word, translatable, so no `[sic:]`.

**16. 1109D — `fluxasque corporis restringens`** (0013). `fluxas` is feminine
accusative plural with **no noun** — a substantive appears to have dropped (e.g.
*voluptates*, *illecebras*). Rendered "restraining the flowing things of the body."

### D. Punctuation carried per Pattern 8 — preserved, not repaired

**17. 1102C — a full stop in mid-sentence** (0011): *…quem exploratorum sagacitas
instruit. et arcae ordinata antecessio in suo itinere dirigit.* The relative clause
has two coordinate verbs and the stop falls between them, before a lowercase *et*.
Preserved exactly in the English ("…instructs. and the ordered precedence of the
ark directs…").

**18. 1105D — a comma where a full stop belongs** (0012): *…et non saturabitur cum
venerit aestas, quam utilis ergo, putamus, erit…* The Jeremiah quotation runs
straight into Richard's own sentence over a comma, with *quam* lowercase.
Preserved.

**19. 1107D — a statement closed with a full stop that is plainly a question**
(0013): *Quam utilem, quaeso, putamus illum lapidem quem primo loco posuimus,
soliditatem videlicet timoris per quem mens fundatur in solido humilitatis.*
Preserved with the full stop.

**20. 1100C — an interrogative closed with a full stop** (0010): *Nonne in his
verbis in illam exploratorum sententiam concurrere videtur, ubi dicitur: …* ends
with `.` Preserved.

**21. 1114D — a `?` closing a statement** (0016): *…et cum gloria suscepisti me?*
The Psalm verse is declarative; the question mark belongs to Richard's framing
*Quid, quaeso, scala indigeat…* several clauses back. Preserved.

**22. 1113A — `In petra, inquit, exaltavit, me`** (0015). A comma between verb and
its object pronoun. Preserved ("he hath exalted, me").

**23. 1112D–1113A — anacoluthon at the Joseph sentence** (0015): *His, ni fallor,
lapidibus magnus ille patriarcha Joseph, quem princeps populorum constituit … et
senes ejus prudentiam doceret.* The sentence has no main verb; the following
sentence (*His, inquam, lapidibus…*) restarts and supplies it. Migne's full stop
between them is kept, so the English carries the same broken construction.
