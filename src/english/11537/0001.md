## A SERMON ON PALM SUNDAY. (cont.)

I think that by now it will be needful to seek an ass, and, amid such emblems of the virtues, to claim for oneself the protection of humility. So too Christ Jesus, when they drew near to Jerusalem, caused an ass to be brought to him, and sat upon it. Now we all know that the ass is a filthy and cheap animal, most gentle indeed, [1063A] but almost more contemptible than all the rest; and so it bears, as I judge, the figure of humility, by which the soul becomes cheap to itself and comes into contempt. And indeed without such an animal our procession may perhaps be worth the looking at; yet without it, beyond doubt, it will not be, nor could it be, so wholly admirable. Who, I beseech you, would not rightly marvel that an ass should be present among the choirs of those going before and singing psalms on this side and on that, and that so filthy an animal should appear amid such glorious and solemn array? Certainly, so far as concerns me, it appears in my own eyes more wonderful that a man clothed in pontifical vestments should sit upon an ass, and amid such insignia should not blush at such a seat, than that he should carry in his hands that wood which is sweet to almost all, or should wear a mitre upon his head [1063B] or a pallium upon his shoulders. It is surely neither new nor wonderful to glory in the virtues; but this is a great thing and to be wondered at: amid all the highest ornaments of the virtues not to disdain the ass, to cleave firmly to the good of humility, that one and the same man should appear wonderful to all others and contemptible to himself alone. In this order, surely, we make our procession wonderful, if amid the summits of obedience and mercy we do not forsake the modesty of humility. By this art, surely, we shall be able to win over our equals: if with the utmost diligence we are zealous always to hold the last place; to prefer them before ourselves in the office of veneration and in place of honor, without dissimulation; nay, truly to believe that they are to be preferred, and to persuade oneself of it, [1063C] and to wish others to be persuaded of it. You surely see that in this business we have need — a thing that almost slipped from us — of the she-ass and of her colt. There are two things, humility and humiliation: humility is designated in the she-ass, humiliation in the colt. The colt is born of the she-ass, and true humiliation arises out of humility. Humility is within, humiliation without; humility in the heart, humiliation in action; humility belongs to the affection, humiliation to the act. But that humiliation alone must needs be true which comes down from humility. He who truly acknowledges his own weakness, and becomes cheap to himself in his own eyes, has already, as it were, brought the she-ass into his presence. But if he blushes to humble himself before the eyes of men, or to appear contemptible, he has the mother present indeed, but has thus far lacked the colt's [1063D] presence. If you humble yourself outwardly, but esteem yourself something great, you have the colt, but you do not have the mother. Let us therefore bring the she-ass and the colt with her, that we may be truly humble in mind and may exhibit true humiliation in action. Otherwise, if you humble yourself feignedly and walk deceitfully, then, according to the prophet's word — *when thou shalt be compelled to pass over the rivers, to uncover the thighs, without doubt thy shame shall be revealed [n: (Isa. XLVII)]* — since nothing is covered that shall not be revealed.

Let us therefore be zealous to be truly humble, and truly to appear humble; and in this order, as I judge, we shall be able to win over our equals. For it is agreed that among equals envy is especially wont to be born, and from envy discord? But if we sit upon the ass, as has been said, [1064A] and hold among them the lowest place, a cheap one and an abject, we cut away the tinder of envy. Without doubt true humiliation not only restores peace among equals, but also confirms it with superiors and with inferiors. See therefore what it is to seek such a seat, and, if there be need, to fetch it even from afar. For by some it is easily taken up from near at hand; but by others it must be fetched from afar. For those who still live outrageously, and commit things to blush at, find ready to hand enough for which to blush and to humble themselves. Such was that publican who blushed, and did not dare so much as to lift his eyes to heaven. But those who find nothing in their life to blush at — such as I believe those to be who come from Bethany and pass by [1064B] Olivet, who keep the flowering Pasch and strew their way with the flowers of the virtues — must, as I judge, fetch the ass from afar, and take the matter of their humiliation either from the lapse of their former life or from the state of the life to come. So Paul too, because he had not ready to hand anything from which he might take an ass from near by — since he was conscious to himself of nothing — sent the scouts of his consideration to the road of his old manner of life, and thence summoned to himself matter for humbling himself. Hear whence he fetches an ass for himself, and what a lowly place of abasement he chooses for himself, when he says: *I am the least of the apostles, who am not worthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God [n: (I Cor. XV)]*. But what shall he do [1064C] who can say with blessed Job: *For neither doth my heart reprove me in all my life (Job XXVII)*? Assuredly he who is such has nothing from which to fetch an ass out of those things which he has already left behind his back, since he finds nothing in his past manner of life whereby he might become cheap to himself. But behold, he has what he may do: let him set before his own face the measure of that highest perfection which he has not yet laid hold of, and let him look toward it. Let him consider what that perfection of angelic purity is, what that consummation of angelic integrity; and without doubt, in the village that is over against him, he can gather by comparison the measure of his own imperfection. Let him consider what that purity of the life to come is, and of what sort its integrity; how pure it is from every evil, how whole in every good, pure from all corruption, [1064D] whole from all imperfection; and I think that in this twofold ministry he is able to fetch an ass for himself, and to find whence he ought truly to humble himself. Assuredly it will not be safe, without such a humble seat, to meet the children of the Hebrews as they cry out on every side, Well done! well done! — since they are children, and therefore cannot discern whom, or when, or how they ought to praise. Such a seat, as I judge, the Apostle wished us to seek, when he admonished us not to be high-minded concerning ourselves: *Not minding high things,* he says, *but consenting to the humble [n: (Rom. XII)]*. This is the manner of procession which Christ held, and which surely he willed us also to hold. Do you wish to know more plainly that he came from Bethany, and that in everything he did he held to obedience? *I came not,* he says, [1065A] *to do my own will, but the will of the Father who sent me [n: (Joan. VI)]*. And the Apostle testifies: *That he was made obedient unto death, even the death of the cross [n: (Philipp. II)]*. He surely passed also by the Mount of Olives, and by his own death, which he endured for us, he showed how much he loved us. For greater charity hath no man than that a man lay down his life for his friends. Do you wish to know how he sat upon the ass, or how humbly he came? Whereas he was in the form of God, he thought it not robbery to be equal with God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made into the likeness of men, and in habit found as a man. But that by every such way he makes for Jerusalem, who is ignorant — unless perhaps it can lie hidden from someone that he himself is [1065B] our peace, who hath made both one, breaking down the middle wall of partition, the enmities in his own flesh, making void the law of commandments by decrees? Behold how Christ passed over; behold with what flowers he strewed his way, that he might keep a flowering Pasch and pass through the lovely places of the virtues. Behold what Christ's procession was. Behold whence and whither; behold by what way and how — which I recall having set forth above. Out of obedience, through piety, with humility, unto peace. I know not whether what a longer discourse above set out more copiously could have been summed up more briefly. Remember that you too must strew your way with such flowers, if you desire to celebrate the flowering Pasch. Good flowers are obedience and mercy; good flowers are humility and peace. Behold, you say, [1065C] enough of the flowers; and where are the fruits. Do you wish to know in Christ what the fruits are of obedience kept and of mercy bestowed — in that he was made obedient to the Father for us unto death? Recall that word of the Apostle: *That Christ rising again from the dead dieth now no more, death shall no more have dominion over him; for in that he liveth, he liveth unto God [n: (Rom. VI)]*. Take this also: what the fruit is of peace sought and of humility taken up. *For which cause,* he says, *God also hath exalted him, and hath given him a name which is above every name, that in the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven, on earth, and in hell [n: (Philipp. II)]*. Behold what a passage, behold how fruitful. From death to life, from tribulation to glory: behold the second Pasch, behold the fruit-bearing Pasch. O what a fruit are life and glory! O sweet [1065D] fruit, O lofty fruit: everlasting life with the highest glory, and the highest glory with everlasting life. But Christ was unwilling to eat these fruits alone; nay rather, he distributed, he gave to the poor. And now: *As in Adam all die, so also in Christ all shall be made alive. But every one in his own order. The firstfruits, Christ; then they that are his [n: (I Cor. XV)]*. And in the place where it was said: *You are not my people, there it is now said to them, sons of the living God [n: (Ose. I)]*. *For as by the disobedience of one man many were made sinners, so also by the obedience of one many are made just. For if by one man's offense death reigned through one, much more they who receive abundance of grace, and of the gift, and of justice, shall reign in life through one, Jesus Christ (Rom. [1066A] V)*. Knowing therefore what the fruit of obedience is — true immortality and the incorruption of eternal life — let us all run with great haste to Bethany, subjecting ourselves to every obedience; distrusting nothing of its fruit, but holding without any hesitation that this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality.
