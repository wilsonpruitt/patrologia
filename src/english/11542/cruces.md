# Cruces — 11542, Richard of St Victor, *De sacrificio David prophetae* (PL 196)

Garbled or irregular printed readings in Migne's plate, rendered per the intended
reading (house convention) and logged here, plus citation anomalies passed through
verbatim under the marker rule.

## Chunk 0000

- **Col 1034A** — `Introducamus cum interim ad coenam nostram`. Printed `cum` for
  *eum*; the sentence needs the object ("let us bring **him** in to our supper"),
  which the answering clause *ut … introduci mereamur ad coenam suam* confirms.
  Rendered per the intended reading.
- **Col 1033D** — `si ex designatis animalibus tria ei fercula opponimus`.
  `opponimus` where the parallel a line earlier (*quales epulas ei apponamus*) and
  the sense want *apponimus*. Rendered "set … before him"; not emended in substance.
- **Col 1034B–C** — `*Ignem,* inquit, *veni mittere in terram; et quid volo, nisi ut
  ardeat? (Luc. [1034C] XII.)*` — this scripture locator is printed **inline**, not
  as an `[n: …]` note, and a column anchor falls inside it. It has deliberately NOT
  been wrapped in a `[f: …]` tag (pattern 4): it is scripture, and `[f: …]` feeds the
  *fontes* pool, not the scripture index; a tag could not contain the anchor in any
  case. Kept verbatim, unanglicized, anchor in place. Same treatment for
  `(Psal. [1036B] XXXII)` in chunk 0001. **A human should decide** whether such
  inline scripture locators want their own marker.

## Chunk 0001

- **Col 1034D** — `Credo videre bona Domini interra viventium`. `interra` is
  *in terra* run together by the compositor. Rendered "in the land of the living."
- **Col 1035A** — `in spei suae cercitudine solidatur`. `cercitudine` for
  *certitudine*. Rendered "in the certainty of his hope."
- **Col 1035B** — `Sed coelum illud quod intra non est, cur non habeat sui generis
  terram…`. The plate reads `intra non est`; *nos* has dropped out, leaving a
  spurious negative that reverses the argument (the next sentence is *Si intra nos
  est regnum coelorum, cur non et intra nos sit terra viventium?*). Rendered per the
  intended reading, "that heaven which is within us."
- **Col 1036D** — `In melle accipe quidquid glorificae jucunditatis in corporeis
  sensibus incorporea substantia distillat`. `in corporeis` for *incorporeis*: the
  antithesis with the immediately preceding clause (*corporeis sensibus corporea
  substantia*) requires the incorporeal member on both sides. Rendered "into the
  incorporeal senses."
- **Col 1036D** — the same sentence ends **without terminal punctuation** in the
  plate (…*distillat* then the paragraph break). Reproduced as printed; no full stop
  supplied.
- **Col 1036D–1037A** — `hujusmodi promissionnes`, with doubled **n**. Typographic
  only; rendered "promises."
- **Col 1038A–B** — `Quid enim est aliud eum vitulum offerre, quam male jam et
  desiderare subesse potius quam praeesse…`. `male` for *malle* ("to prefer"), which
  the doublet *malle … et desiderare* and the following *subesse potius quam
  praeesse* both require. Rendered "than now to prefer and to desire to be under
  rather than over." Note also that the sentence is interrogative in sense but
  printed with a full stop; reproduced as printed.

## Chunk 0002

- **Col 1039D** — `quoniam digni, habiti sunt pro nomine Jesu contumeliam pati`.
  Stray comma between `digni` and `habiti`. Ignored in the English as the
  compositor's slip; no other consequence.
- **Col 1041C** — `cum cor mansuetum et pium sibi extorquet pro Domino zelata, et
  increpationis cornibus delinquentes ferire`. `zelata` cannot stand: the parallel
  infinitive *ferire* in the same clause requires *zelare*. Rendered "wrings from
  itself to be zealous for the Lord, and to strike offenders…".

## Chunk 0003

- **Col 1041D** — `Sed merito et hoc in loco, juxta superiorem modum. quaeritur cur
  ab Abraham…`. A full stop is printed mid-sentence after `modum`, before the
  lower-case `quaeritur`. Rendered as one sentence; the stop is a plate defect.
- **Col 1042B** — `qui non solum in potentiores vel aeque potentes, sed etiam
  potentiores … impugnat`. `potentiores` is printed **twice**; the first member must
  be the weaker one (*minus potentes* / *inferiores*) for the climax *sed etiam
  potentiores* to mean anything. Rendered "not only those less powerful or equally
  powerful, but even the more powerful."

## Citation anomalies — passed through verbatim, flagged for a human

`[n: …]` contents are reproduced unchanged per the marker rule. These are Migne's
numbers, not ours:

- **Col 1031C `[n: (Psal. LXX)]`** vs **col 1041D `[n: (Psal. LXV)]`** — the *same*
  verse (*Holocausta medullata offeram tibi cum incenso arietum*, Ps. 65:15 Vulg.) is
  cited two different ways in the one work. LXV is right; LXX at the incipit is
  wrong. Both stand as printed.
- **Col 1033D `[n: (Apoc. IV)]`** for *Ecce ego pulso ad ostium…* = Apoc. 3:20, not
  ch. 4.
- **Col 1033B `[n: (Prov. XI)]`** for *Vir obediens loquitur victorias* = Prov.
  21:28, not ch. 11.
- **Col 1035D `[n: (Prov. XVIII)]`** for *Impius cum venerit in profundum malorum
  contemnit* = Prov. 18:3, correct.
- **Col 1040A `[n: (ibid.)]`** (Rebecca's counsel) resolves to Gen. XXVII, the
  immediately preceding non-*ibid.* locator; left as printed for the indexer to
  resolve.
