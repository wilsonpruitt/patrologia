# Cruces — 11542, Richard of St Victor, *De sacrificio David prophetae* (PL 196)

Garbled or irregular printed readings in Migne's plate, logged here, plus
citation anomalies passed through verbatim under the marker rule. The English
renders **what the plate prints**, per `translation-style.md` **Pattern 7**: a
printed reading that is construable is rendered literally even where it is odd or
says the opposite of what the argument requires, and type that is not a word at
all is carried into the English untranslated, in italics. The conjectured reading
is recorded here and only here; it is never applied to the running text. (This
work was first translated under the withdrawn `src/english/10103/cruces.md`
convention of rendering "the intended reading"; those places were re-rendered
from the plate on 2026-07-28.)

## Chunk 0000

- **Col 1034A** — `Introducamus cum interim ad coenam nostram`. As printed the
  sentence does not construe: *cum* is a real word but has no object as a
  preposition and no clause as a conjunction, and *introducamus* is left with
  nothing to bring in. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow, *cum*
  standing where the plate puts it: "Let us meanwhile bring in with to our
  supper." The intended reading is *eum* ("let us bring **him** in"), which the
  answering clause *ut … introduci mereamur ad coenam suam* confirms.
- **Col 1033D** — `si ex designatis animalibus tria ei fercula opponimus`.
  **Not an emendation**: *oppono* means "set before / set over against," so "set
  three dishes before him" is the literal rendering of what is printed. Logged
  only because the parallel a line earlier (*quales epulas ei apponamus*) and the
  supper imagery suggest the compositor meant *apponimus*.
- **Col 1034B–C** — `*Ignem,* inquit, *veni mittere in terram; et quid volo, nisi ut
  ardeat? (Luc. [1034C] XII.)*` — this scripture locator is printed **inline**, not
  as an `[n: …]` note, and a column anchor falls inside it. It has deliberately NOT
  been wrapped in a `[f: …]` tag (pattern 4): it is scripture, and `[f: …]` feeds the
  *fontes* pool, not the scripture index; a tag could not contain the anchor in any
  case. Kept verbatim, unanglicized, anchor in place. Same treatment for
  `(Psal. [1036B] XXXII)` in chunk 0001. **A human should decide** whether such
  inline scripture locators want their own marker.

## Chunk 0001

- **Col 1034D** — `Credo videre bona Domini interra viventium` (Ps. 26:13).
  `interra` is not a word — *in terra* run together by the compositor. Carried
  through untranslated per Pattern 7: "the good things of the Lord interra of the
  living." (It sits inside an italicized scripture quotation, so the carried form
  takes no further italic marker.)
- **Col 1035A** — `in spei suae cercitudine solidatur`. `cercitudine` is not a
  word; the intended reading is *certitudine* ("certainty"), which the same work
  prints correctly at 1036D–1037A (*ad spei nostrae certitudinem*). Carried
  through untranslated per Pattern 7: "he is made firm in the *cercitudine* of his
  hope."
- **Col 1035B** — `Sed coelum illud quod intra non est, cur non habeat sui generis
  terram…`. Rendered as printed, negative and all: "But that heaven which is not
  within — why should it not have a land of its own kind…" — which reverses the
  argument. The intended reading is *quod intra nos est* ("which is within us"):
  *nos* has dropped out and *non* stands in its place. The evidence is the
  sentence immediately preceding (*De regno coelorum catholica fides certissime
  agnovit quod intra nos sit*) and the one immediately following (*Si intra nos
  est regnum coelorum, cur non et intra nos sit terra viventium?*).
- **Col 1036D** — `In melle accipe quidquid glorificae jucunditatis in corporeis
  sensibus incorporea substantia distillat`. Both printed words are Latin, so the
  phrase is rendered as printed: "an incorporeal substance distils into the
  corporeal senses," which collapses the antithesis. The intended reading is
  *incorporeis*, wrongly divided: the immediately preceding clause is *corporeis
  sensibus corporea substantia*, and the argument needs the incorporeal member on
  both sides of the second.
- **Col 1036D** — the same sentence ends **without terminal punctuation** in the
  plate (…*distillat* then the paragraph break). Reproduced as printed; no full stop
  supplied.
- **Col 1036D–1037A** — `hujusmodi promissionnes`, with doubled **n**.
  Typographic only, but *promissionnes* is not a word, so it is carried through
  untranslated per Pattern 7: "to inherit *promissionnes* of this kind together
  with faithful Abraham." The intended reading is *promissiones*, printed
  correctly two sentences earlier (*dictae sunt promissiones*).
- **Col 1038A–B** — `Quid enim est aliud eum vitulum offerre, quam male jam et
  desiderare subesse potius quam praeesse…`. *male* is a real word ("badly, ill")
  and is rendered as printed: "than now ill, and to desire to be under rather than
  over," which leaves the *et* joining an adverb to an infinitive. The intended
  reading is *malle* ("to prefer"), which the doublet *malle … et desiderare* and
  the following *subesse potius quam praeesse* both require. Note also that the
  sentence is interrogative in sense but printed with a full stop; reproduced as
  printed.

## Chunk 0002

- **Col 1039D** — `quoniam digni, habiti sunt pro nomine Jesu contumeliam pati`.
  Stray comma between `digni` and `habiti`. Ignored in the English as the
  compositor's slip; no other consequence.
- **Col 1041C** — `cum cor mansuetum et pium sibi extorquet pro Domino zelata, et
  increpationis cornibus delinquentes ferire`. `zelata` is a real form (neuter
  plural participle of *zelo*, "things zealously pursued") and is rendered as
  printed: "wrings from itself things zealously pursued for the Lord, and to
  strike offenders with the horns of rebuke" — which leaves *extorquet* governing
  a noun and an infinitive at once. The intended reading is *zelare*, which the
  parallel infinitive *ferire* in the same clause requires.

## Chunk 0003

- **Col 1041D** — `Sed merito et hoc in loco, juxta superiorem modum. quaeritur cur
  ab Abraham…`. A full stop is printed mid-sentence after `modum`, before the
  lower-case `quaeritur`. Rendered as printed per Pattern 8: the stop stands and
  the continuation stays lower-case. The stop is a plate defect; the intended
  reading is a comma (or no mark), with `quaeritur` continuing the sentence.
- **Col 1042B** — `qui non solum in potentiores vel aeque potentes, sed etiam
  potentiores … impugnat`. Rendered as printed, with `potentiores` **twice**:
  "not only those more powerful or equally powerful, but even the more powerful,"
  which destroys the climax. The first member must be the weaker one (*minus
  potentes* / *inferiores*) for *sed etiam potentiores* to mean anything; that is
  the intended reading. Per Pattern 11, the repeated occurrence is now marked:
  "but even [d: the more powerful]."

## Citation anomalies — passed through verbatim, flagged for a human

`[n: …]` contents are reproduced unchanged per the marker rule. These are Migne's
numbers, not ours:

- **Col 1031C `[n: (Psal. LXX)]`** vs **col 1041D `[n: (Psal. LXV)]`** — the *same*
  verse (*Holocausta medullata offeram tibi cum incenso arietum*, Ps. 65:15 Vulg.) is
  cited two different ways in the one work. LXV is right; LXX at the incipit is
  wrong. Both stand as printed.
- **Col 1033D `[n: (Apoc. IV)]`** for *Ecce ego pulso ad ostium…* = Apoc. 3:20, not
  ch. 4.
- **Col 1033B `[n: (Prov. XI)]`** for *Vir obediens loquitur victorias* = Prov.
  21:28, not ch. 11.
- **Col 1035D `[n: (Prov. XVIII)]`** for *Impius cum venerit in profundum malorum
  contemnit* = Prov. 18:3, correct.
- **Col 1040A `[n: (ibid.)]`** (Rebecca's counsel) resolves to Gen. XXVII, the
  immediately preceding non-*ibid.* locator; left as printed for the indexer to
  resolve.
