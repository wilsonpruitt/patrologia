## On the Spirit of Blasphemy (cont.)

According to the evangelist Matthew you hear the Lord saying: *Every sin and blasphemy shall be forgiven to men, but the blasphemy of the Spirit shall not be forgiven; and whoever shall have said a word against the Son of man, it shall be forgiven him; but he who shall have said it against the Holy Spirit, it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, nor in the world to come [n: (Matth. XXI)]*. First he sets down the general sentence, then he excepts the things to be excepted. He sets down the things remissible, [1189C]he excepts the things irremissible. The blasphemy, he says, of the Spirit is not forgiven, because this evil, having in itself nothing of excuse, deserves to receive nothing of excusatory remission. What he said of this evil, the same he adds concerning any word uttered against the Holy Spirit: Whoever, he says, shall have said a word against the Holy Spirit, it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, nor in the world to come. What here are they to say who consign every man that blasphemes even once against the Spirit to eternal damnation! Are they to say that everyone who shall have said any word whatever against the Holy Spirit shall perish for ever? But if they shrink from saying this, let them expound to us how one ought to understand what is said of a word uttered against the Holy Spirit, that it shall not be forgiven in [1189D]this world, or in the world to come. For if they are to say that this evil is expiable, yet in some respect irremissible, let them see whether the same may be said of the spirit of blasphemy. For it is one thing for a sin to be inexpiable, and another, in the sense here taken, to be irremissible. The sin of the devil is inexpiable, which can be purged by no remedy; but that sin of man may not incongruously be called irremissible for which there is no relaxation of the penalty such as comes to pass in sins that are actually similar. Behold, one and the same blasphemy one man commits from compulsion, another from some deception, a third from malignity alone. Behold, in the three the fault, as to the action, is in every way alike. In two of them, nevertheless, it receives something of remission, because it has something of excuse. In the third [1190A]it receives no such remission, because it will have no excuse. The first of these sins against the Father, the second against the Son, the third (he to whom it is not forgiven) sins against the Holy Spirit; this is what the Lord says according to the evangelist Mark: *He who shall have blasphemed,* he says, *against the Holy Spirit has no forgiveness for ever [n: (Marc. III)]*. That remission is said to be given or not given according to the mutual regard of those receiving or not receiving it, ought to be understood of the same deed. For, concerning the most strict expiation, something is remitted for the same blasphemy to those who blaspheme against the Father and the Son, but nothing to those who blaspheme against the Holy Spirit. Well, then, is it said of him who shall have blasphemed against the Holy Spirit that he has no forgiveness for ever. For if he repent, nothing [1190B]is remitted to him of the fullness of the most strict expiation; if he repent not, nothing is remitted to him of the fullness of the most strict damnation. That this rightly comes to pass is consequently added, when he says: But he shall be guilty of an everlasting sin. What he says is as much as if he had said more openly: Therefore he has no forgiveness, nor even ought to have it, because he is not guilty of the kind of offense to which something ought to be remitted. But he is guilty of the worst offense of all, since he is guilty of an everlasting sin, and such that no determinate relaxation of remission ought to be made for him, as we have taught above — that is, to blaspheme against the Holy Spirit, to utter a word of blasphemy from malignity alone. But such blasphemy can never be an offense without a detestable and execrable hatred of God; and beyond doubt the hatred of God is an everlasting offense. [1190C]An everlasting offense is called that which never falls away, which is bound to be for ever. Assuredly, as the love of God never falls away, so the hatred of God never falls away: *Charity,* says the Apostle, *never falls away [n: (I Cor. XXIII)]*. What was said by the Apostle of the love of God can be said also of the hatred of God. And, as the love of God can rightly be called an everlasting merit, so the hatred of God is rightly called an everlasting offense. What is said of charity, that it never falls away, is not to be understood as though, once had, it cannot be lost, but that, contrary to the property of faith and hope, it does not cease to be for ever. So there are certain evils that have place only in this life, such as adulteries and thefts, and certain ones both in this life at once [1190D]and in the world to come. For he who departs from this life with hatred of God wastes away under the same disease for ever, and he who hates for ever offends for ever, and by offending without ceasing deserves to suffer without ceasing. He, therefore, who is guilty of hatred of God is guilty of an everlasting offense. But, as was shown above, the blasphemy which is against the Holy Spirit is never apart from hatred of God. Rightly, then, is it said of a blasphemer of this kind that he is guilty of an everlasting offense. But certain men add to what the Lord says something of their own which the Lord does not say. The Lord says that he is guilty of an everlasting offense; these men say that he is eternally guilty of an everlasting offense. For he who says that a man of this kind can by no reason merit pardon, does he not say that he is eternally guilty of an everlasting offense? [1191A]But perhaps these men gather this from that which they hear from the Lord's voice: He who shall have blasphemed against the Holy Spirit has no forgiveness for ever. But is not the same thing said by the same Lord of a word spoken against the Holy Spirit? He who shall have said, he says, a word against the Holy Spirit, it shall not be forgiven him, neither in this world, nor in the world to come. How many men, I ask, will be found who would dare to say that they have never said any word against the Holy Spirit? Are all the rest to be despaired of who do not presume to say this? Ought they to despair of themselves who dare not say this? But if by teaching of this kind an ox or an ass fall into the pit of despair, I fear lest the Lord of animals require the price of the animal-men from the hand of the [1191B]teacher — nay, of the caster-down.

But there are certain men temporally guilty of a temporal and transitory offense, and there are certain men eternally guilty of a temporal and transitory offense. Likewise there are others eternally guilty of an everlasting offense. Why should there not also be others temporally guilty of an everlasting offense? Adultery is actually committed in time and passes away, and constitutes the adulterer guilty, but temporally guilty if he shall have truly repented, but eternally so if he refuse to repent. He who passes from this life with hatred of God will be eternally guilty of an everlasting offense. So too the offense which, in the sense we have said, can be called everlasting, constitutes the one who truly repents guilty only for a time. Since indeed there can be men eternally guilty of a temporal offense according to divine justice, why should not some be believed [1191C]to be guilty for a time of an everlasting offense according to divine mercy? His tender mercies are over all his works. For nothing is more wonderful than his tender mercies, and for that very reason they are over all his works. Let us consider still further what Mark adds of his own to those things which the Lord had said: *Because they said,* he says, *He has an unclean spirit [n: (Marc. III)]*. In these words the evangelist seems to render the reason whence it came about that the Lord discoursed these things concerning the worst kind of blaspheming. [1192A]The evangelist shows, then, that the Lord taught them concerning the blasphemy which has no remission for this reason: because they said that he had an unclean spirit. Without any ambiguity that insult to Christ was a detestable blasphemy. But that word of blasphemy, I ask, was it against the very Son of man, or against the Holy Spirit? But if it is said to be against the Son of man, it will be a remissible word; if against the Holy Spirit, it will be irremissible. But if it is called a remissible word, it has nothing to do with that blasphemy which has no forgiveness for ever, and, according to what the evangelist subjoins, it answers nothing to the Lord's words which he set before. The coherence of the sayings therefore compels us to believe that the calumniators of Christ blasphemed against the Holy Spirit. What [1192B]then shall we say? Did they sin against him whom they did not revile, and not blaspheme against him whom they profaned by their words? Who is fit for these things? By authority, therefore, and at the same time from reason, we can plainly gather that the profaners of Christ blasphemed so wickedly not through ignorance, but through malice alone. They understood, without any ambiguity, that no one could work such signs unless God were with him. Since, therefore, they blasphemed through malice alone, they sinned against him to whom goodness is wont specifically to be attributed, and for that very reason, as to that kind of sin, they were unworthy of the alleviation and remission of the most bitter penalty. For, had they sinned through ignorance and offended against him who is the wisdom of God, in punishing the [1192C]sin it would have been dealt with them more leniently, and in it they would have received something of remission. From these words of the Lord it is manifestly gathered that certain things which are common to all the persons are appropriated to certain persons not without great mystery. Many things concerning these words of the Lord are still worthy of diligent investigation. But this we leave, better and more advisedly, to profounder talents. Concerning that which you asked of me I have said what I could. If I have become foolish, you have compelled me.
