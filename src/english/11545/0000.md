## On the Surpassingly Excellent Baptism of Christ

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1011D]To one kindred in blood, a lord in sanctity, to his beloved N., his own R., such as he is: to will one and the same thing, with one accord, in the One. ⟦s2⟧ Led by repentance for my promise, I almost preferred to have promised falsely than to write empty things, for this reason: that concerning those matters which you asked to be explained to you by me, I judged that I perceived nothing at all worthy even to be spoken, still less to be written; but chiefly for this reason, that in those things which I feebly perceive I labor under a poverty of words. ⟦s3⟧ But since the little child Jesus did not blush to lie hidden in swaddling-clothes [n: (Luc. II)], I was not at all ashamed to convey the poor sense of my slenderness under [1012D] the swaddling-clothes of words. ⟦s4⟧ As often as Mary wraps her little one in swaddling-clothes, so often does a chaste mind expound in simple words the truth which it perceives, even if feebly. ⟦s5⟧ And perhaps, if I mistake not, it will be more satisfactory to run into empty speech than into false speech. ⟦s6⟧ Attend, therefore, I beseech you, rather to who speaks, and to whom, than to what is said, or by what means. ⟦s7⟧ And so I ask for a prayer in return for a prayer, not so eloquent as devout, not distinguished by variety of words, but filled with a shower of tears. ⟦s8⟧ If, however, in those things which [1013A] you require of me you yourself perceive anything better, this I demand of you by the right of recompense. ⟦s9⟧ But now I turn my pen to the words of the prophet, full of novelty, worthy of wonder, which you desire to be unlocked for you. ⟦s10⟧ Enlighten your face, etc. [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]. ⟦s11⟧ But God gives the increase by enlightening and sanctifying within. ⟦s12⟧ Behold how great was John, how great was Paul. ⟦s13⟧ Yet they perceived not great but humble things of themselves, because they were enlightened so as to know themselves. ⟦s14⟧ We are nothing, and yet we esteem great things of ourselves, because we are blind, having a white film upon our eyes: *He who sent me to baptize in water, he said to me: Upon whom you shall see,* etc. [n: (Joan. I)] The descent of the Holy Spirit, as also the mission of the Son himself, certain simpler folk understand, soundly enough, but too little. ⟦s15⟧ For when they hear from Truth itself: *I [1013B] went forth from the Father, and came into the world [n: (Joan. XVI)]*, they so understand it as though he were first in the Father, and not in the world. ⟦s16⟧ And that when he went forth from the Father he forsook the Father, and withdrew from the Father. ⟦s17⟧ And that when he came into the world, he came thither where before he was not. ⟦s18⟧ This understanding is depraved, and to be banished from every faithful heart. ⟦s19⟧ For these do not consider the immensity of God. ⟦s20⟧ For God, since he is immense, is in every place, filling every place. ⟦s21⟧ And therefore he cannot pass from place to place, as though forsaking one place and occupying another. ⟦s22⟧ Nor is he only in every place because he is immense, but he is also everywhere whole, because he is simple. ⟦s23⟧ For this is the nature of simplicity: that wherever it is, there it is whole. ⟦s24⟧ Since, then, God is immense and simple, it follows that he is everywhere whole. ⟦s25⟧ Nor, when he is said [1013C] to be everywhere whole, is he predicated to have parts; nay, in this very thing he is denied to have parts; because, if he had parts, he would not be everywhere whole. ⟦s26⟧ Nor, when he is said to be whole everywhere, is it to be understood as though he infuses himself into every creature according to fullness. ⟦s27⟧ But he is everywhere whole in himself, that is, wherever he is, he has himself whole. ⟦s28⟧ This is to be understood of the Father, this of the Son, this of the Holy Spirit. ⟦s29⟧ What, then, is the mission of the Son but the assumption of the servile form? ⟦s30⟧ And what the descent of the Holy Spirit, but a certain symbolic presentation and mystical signification of him? ⟦s31⟧ For he, descending in the dove, is not thus in himself moved, who ever remains in himself unmoved. ⟦s32⟧ Nor did he in any way begin to be in the Lord in a manner in which before he was not in him. ⟦s33⟧ To us not [1013D] having it is given, and to those having it, that it may be had more fully. ⟦s34⟧ To the Lord, as not having it, it was not given, nor to him as having it, that he might have it more fully — to whom in his own conception it was given according to fullness. ⟦s35⟧ For as the Father was with the Son by majesty and, in the man assumed, by the infusion of grace, so also the Holy Spirit. ⟦s36⟧ But the Son alone was in him by personal union. ⟦s37⟧ Whence he alone — not the Father, not the Holy Spirit — is called Emmanuel, that is, God with us. ⟦s38⟧ The descent of the Holy Spirit, then, is nothing but a certain mystical signification of him. ⟦s39⟧ For in this, that he manifested himself visibly in such a form over the Lord when baptized, he intimated what is invisibly done in us when we are reborn in Christ; just as the Son was baptized, not for his own sake as though needing any remedy, but that he might furnish us [1014A] an example of humility and confer a remedy of salvation. ⟦s40⟧ And the Father himself so thundered: *This is my beloved Son,* etc. [n: (Matth. III)], so demonstrating that we are made his sons through the power of so great a sacrament.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Consider how great is the dignity of this sacrament, that the Son himself so commended it by receiving it, and the Holy Spirit by presenting himself, and the Father by so thundering; through its efficacy we are made members of Christ, temple of the Holy Spirit, and sons of God. ⟦s2⟧ Moreover the heavens, before closed through fault, are opened to us; not without great mystery did the Holy Spirit descend, not in a raven, or a kite, or in another form, but in the form of a dove alone. ⟦s3⟧ Of the dove it is written in the Song of Songs: *One is my dove, [1014B] one to her mother [n: (Cant. VI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ By the dove is understood the Church. ⟦s5⟧ The Church is the body of Christ; the Holy Spirit is the spirit of Christ. ⟦s6⟧ Fittingly, then, did the Holy Spirit appear in a dove, and not in another form, because the spirit of Christ is only in the body of Christ. ⟦s7⟧ And only the body of Christ lives also by the spirit of Christ, just as my body alone lives by my spirit, and your body lives by your spirit. ⟦s8⟧ In the body of Christ nothing dead, outside it nothing living, is found. ⟦s9⟧ To the unity of which body the Apostle invites us, saying: *Be careful to keep the truth of the spirit in the bond of peace [n: (Ephes. IV)]*. ⟦s10⟧ He calls the unity "of the spirit," that unity which the spirit makes in the body of Christ, which is the Church, of which unity the bond is peace; to which unity he [1014C] invites us, saying: *One body, and one spirit [n: (ibid.)]*, as though he should say: Therefore we ought to keep the unity of the spirit because we are one body, and one spirit. ⟦s11⟧ One body with our neighbor, and one spirit with God; one body by ministering to one another, one spirit by willing the same thing. ⟦s12⟧ We are one body by the uniting of many members, and one spirit dwells in us. ⟦s13⟧ Therefore we also ought to keep the unity of the spirit, because to us there is one Lord, and one faith, and one baptism. ⟦s14⟧ It behooves us not to dissent through diversity of lords, because to us there is one Lord — the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit. ⟦s15⟧ For those who have diverse lords sometimes are at discord because of their discord. [1014D] There are also those who have the same lord, but he does not remain one for long, but is changed almost at every moment, now glad, now sad, now just, now unjust. ⟦s16⟧ But our Lord, as he is one, so also remains one and the same for ever. ⟦s17⟧ And one faith: we are all bidden to believe the same, as also to say the same. ⟦s18⟧ It does not become us, the faithful, to dissent in the confession of faith. ⟦s19⟧ Let there be, then, a pure and sincere integrity of faith in the heart, let there be a pure and sincere confession of faith in the mouth, because with the heart one believes unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation. ⟦s20⟧ If we cannot fully understand all the things to be believed, let us at least hold that usage and form of speaking which the holy Fathers handed down to us in the Scriptures, especially when speech is made of the incarnation of the Word, or of his eternal generation. ⟦s21⟧ Just as [1015A] children are first trained to know how to form sound words, and then are to be taught to understand the things that they say. ⟦s22⟧ Behold, daily I hear in the Church that Christ is a perfect man, subsisting of a rational soul and human flesh.
