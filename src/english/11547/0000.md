## *What animals are called clean and unclean.*

[0255A]

You ask why, since in the first creation all things are said to be good, of those to be brought into the ark some are called clean, but others unclean — since these, by the very fact that they are unclean, do not seem to be good. But in the consideration of things there is one judgment according to the first condition, another according to human custom, and yet another according to the divine law. According to the first condition all are judged good or clean; according to human custom some appear clean, others unclean. But from among the customarily clean, a certain divine sanction has made exception and decrees them unclean. The first judgment is according to the definition of reason, the second according to the experience of sensuality, the third according to the decree of the divine sanction. [0255B] Reason judges according to the nature and property [0256A] implanted in things. Sensuality [judges] according to its own affection, and to the difference of its pleasure or its horror. The divine sanction [judges] according to some likeness, for the sake of signification. According to the judgment of reason, nothing is unclean but sin. Sensuality reckons unclean whatever the sense of the flesh shrinks from or is nauseated by. When the divine sanction decrees unclean something that is naturally clean, it does so for the grace of signification, and thereby intends something truly unclean to be blotted out from the morals of men. Many things, therefore, which according to custom or some institution are reckoned unclean, according to the reason of their own property are judged clean and good.

## *Who is said to be a soul, and what it is to be purged of the old leaven.*

[0256B]You ask how those are called *azymi* by the Apostle who [0257A] nevertheless are commanded by the same [Apostle] to purge out the old leaven. For what *azyma* [is] among the Greeks, that same thing our word "leaven" (*fermentum*) expresses among us; but *azyma* [means] the same as "without leaven." If therefore they are not without leaven, how can they be called *azymi*? Or if they lack all leaven, how are they able to purge out what they do not have at all? *Purge out,* he says, *the old leaven, that ye may be a new lump, even as ye are azymi [n: (I Cor. V)]*. In these words of the Apostle, if you consider well, you will find — so it seems to me — not one question only, but several. But while you propose one alone, in that very act you signify that you have no doubt about the others. Gladly, therefore, will I try to resolve your doubt, provided that afterward you too resolve my doubt — and not one only, but a twofold one — so that I may receive the talent entrusted [0257B] back with interest. Behold, we confess that the same [persons], in the same respect, cannot both be *azymi* and have leaven; yet the same [persons], in one respect and another, can both be *azymi* and not lack leaven. There are those who think nothing wickedly of God, and are purged from every leaven of wickedness of that kind, but by living badly have not yet deserved to be purged of the leaven of malice. According to the rule of goodness they think true things of God, but according to the rule of goodness they will not live rightly. Such men lack the leaven of wickedness, but do not yet lack the leaven of malice: *Think,* says the Scripture, *of the Lord in goodness [n: (Sap. I)]*. See the Jews, how wickedly they think of Christ. See the gentiles, that they cannot think true things of God: both, therefore, are full of the leaven of wickedness. See [0257C] any heretic: in respect of something he thinks well of God, in respect of something he is now unable to think well of God. From the leaven of wickedness, therefore, he has not yet deserved to be fully purged. It is wickedness when one is now unable to perceive what he ought. It is wickedness when malice so inebriates the mind, and so far blinds it, that it can no longer perceive concerning God or neighbor according to what the rule of truth dictates. Attend now to Christians, and you will see how many there are who in all things believe truly, but in all things do not live sincerely. They have the *azyma* of truth, but they have not the *azyma* of sincerity. They are *azymi* according to faith, but they are not *azymi* according to work. Rightly, therefore, is it said to those who are of this kind: *Purge out the old leaven, that [0257D] ye may be a new lump, even as ye are azymi,* so that they who are now *azymi* by faith may with all zeal purge out whatever of impurity they have in work. Behold, now you have heard what you yourself asked. Hear now yourself what I desire to be expounded to me. Surely a new lump, unless it be first baked, cannot be eaten; but bread without leaven can be eaten. Why then are they not invited by the Apostle to that which is proved to be better and more perfect? Why are they not invited to be bread without leaven rather than a new lump? Behold my doubt; hear also another. What is unleavened bread (*panis azymus*) but food? If therefore they were *azymi*, are they not also to be taken as food? But they are also commanded to feast upon *azyma*. Are the same [persons] both to be feasted upon and about to feast? Are the same [persons] both the banquet [0258A] and the guests, the feasters and the feast? If they themselves are *azymi*, if they are about to feast upon *azyma*, are they not to be devoured by their very selves? These two little questions I require for the debt owed at interest; a third, gratis. For according to the abundance of your generosity you ought to answer even one of them gratis. Since true faith is wont always to be — or ought to be — prior to sincere works, why are they commanded to feast first upon the *azyma* of sincerity, not upon the *azyma* of truth?
