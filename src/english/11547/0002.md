## *Purge out the old leaven, that a man may be made a new lump.* (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ We know indeed that it was commanded in the Law that, when they sacrificed the lamb and celebrated the pasch, they should eat no bread at all but unleavened. ⟦s2⟧ If, then, the Apostle had said that they were indeed not *azymi* but had *azyma*, and had invited them to have it yet more abundantly, it would seem consequent enough that he should add: [0261D] *For our pascha hath been sacrificed, even Christ; therefore let us keep the feast.* ⟦s3+4⟧ What we understand historically of the one, we ought to understand spiritually of the other; for whatever is said of the one seems said for the sake of the other. ⟦s5⟧ What is there said of bodily leaven is here understood of spiritual leaven. ⟦s6⟧ Those, then, who are said not to have *azyma* but to be [*azyma*] — it is a marvel by what reason they are invited to eat *azyma* or the lamb. ⟦s7⟧ But whoever would more fully understand the force of the apostolic sentence, it will behove him to search these things out more deeply. ⟦s8⟧ A twofold pasch, assuredly, it behoves us to celebrate; a twofold passage, without doubt, to make. ⟦s9⟧ For *pascha,* as we have often heard, is interpreted "a passing-over." ⟦s10⟧ And we must pass first from injustice to justice, secondly [0262A] from misery to glory. ⟦s11⟧ A good passage, to pass from iniquity to goodness; a good passage none the less, to pass from misery to blessedness. ⟦s12⟧ Both we ought to do solemnly; of both to rejoice inwardly. ⟦s13⟧ But as to the first, in the thing; as to the second, in hope. ⟦s14⟧ The first solemnity, then, we ought ourselves to make with his help; the second to await from his gift alone. ⟦s15⟧ In the one it behoves us to take the lamb for eating; in the other to show ourselves fit as food for him. ⟦s16⟧ In the one, then, it behoves us to have *azyma,* to eat *azyma*; in the other, it behoves us to be *azyma,* and to yield to the eating of the Lamb. ⟦s17⟧ Nor marvel that you hear it behoves us to be fed upon the Lamb, and to become the Lamb's food, since you likewise read (if you recall): *That we have put on Christ, who nevertheless are Christ's garment [0262B] [n: (Rom. XIII)]*. ⟦s18⟧ And of both we have testimonies of the Scriptures. ⟦s19⟧ It behoves us, then, meanwhile to become *azymi,* and to be purged from every leaven of oldness, if we wish to be found worthy and acceptable in that future solemnity. ⟦s20⟧ But if we wish to be *azymi,* it behoves us to eat *azyma.* ⟦s21⟧ But, you say, if we eat unleavened bread, shall we therefore be bread? ⟦s22⟧ We know assuredly that bread is wont to be incorporated into him who eats, but not to incorporate the eater into itself. ⟦s23⟧ We know bread that is wont to nourish the flesh, to fatten the flesh, and to render it more robust. ⟦s24⟧ What is this bread which makes that which is of the flesh fail of itself, and makes the eater pass over into its own likeness? ⟦s25⟧ But, that you marvel not the more at this, hear what manner of bread, what manner of *azyma,* we set before you to eat: [0262C] *I am the living bread which came down from heaven [n: (Joan. VI)]*. ⟦s26⟧ Beyond doubt, if you eat this bread, not it in you, but you shall be changed into it [n: (lib. VII Confessionum D. August., cap. 10)]. ⟦s27⟧ If you will not believe me, hear him whom you dare not contradict: I am the food of the grown; grow, and thou shalt eat me. ⟦s28⟧ Nor shalt thou change me into thee, as the food of thy flesh, but thou shalt be changed into me. ⟦s29⟧ But perhaps you still ask that it be shown you more evidently in what way this can be done. ⟦s30⟧ If, then, you enjoy truth and are delighted with such food, do you suppose that, by hungering for truth and enjoying it, you can change truth into falsity? ⟦s31⟧ If you take Christ as your food, it is better for you that he make you truthful out of a liar, than that you make him a liar out of the truthful. ⟦s32⟧ Beyond doubt Christ is the highest truth; beyond doubt Christ is the highest purity [0262D]; the *azyma* of truth makes the truthful out of the liar; the *azyma* of sincerity makes the clean of heart out of the unclean. ⟦s33⟧ When you have been confirmed in truth and sincerity, beyond doubt you will be *azymi,* and worthy to be taken as food for the Lamb. ⟦s34⟧ But when he yields into your food, and when he takes you into his food, in both cases not he into you, but you are changed into him. ⟦s35⟧ In the first change he makes you like himself in justice; in the second he makes you like himself in glory. ⟦s36⟧ In the first, as has been said, he makes the truthful out of the liar, and the clean of heart out of the unclean; in the second, the immortal out of the mortal, and the incorruptible out of the corruptible, when he shall configure the body of our lowliness to the body of his brightness: *For this corruptible,* the Apostle witnessing, *must [0263A] put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality [n: (II Cor. XV)]*. ⟦s37⟧ If, then, we wish to be like him in glory, it behoves us to be *azymi*; and if we wish to become *azymi,* it behoves us to eat the *azyma* of sincerity and of truth.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But it does not suffice to have *azyma* of this kind, and to grasp by understanding and hold in memory what sincerity is or what stands true, unless we add that we also eat [it], and, working — as it were, chewing — study to transfix it into our affection, and to fill our inmost parts with sweetness and satiety of this kind. ⟦s2⟧ You see assuredly that it is one thing to have *azyma,* and another to eat *azyma,* and yet another to be *azyma.* ⟦s3⟧ If, therefore, we cannot be *azymi* unless we eat the appointed *azyma,* what marvel if he [0263B] wished those who already were in part *azymi,* and in part were not, [to become], through the mediating new lump, wholly *azymi* — what marvel, I say, if he invited them to eat *azyma*? ⟦s4⟧ But principally he invites them to the eating of the Lamb, without whose grace they could not persevere in that which they already were, [namely] *azymi,* nor in that which they yet were, [namely] *azyma,* nor [could there be] a new lump, nor from it could *azymi* be made. ⟦s5⟧ Now attend, I beseech you, to this — how consequent it is, and how fitting, that he who of himself made your food for you, you should of yourself make his food for him. ⟦s6⟧ You have sat at the table of a rich man; attend to what is set before you, and see that it behoves you to prepare such things. ⟦s7⟧ The Apostle wishes *us to be made a new lump [n: (I Cor. V)]*, and from the lump bread without leaven — and deservedly. ⟦s8⟧ For our pascha hath been sacrificed, [even] [0263C] Christ. ⟦s9⟧ Let us, then, be made a new lump, that Christ may find in us whereof a feast may be made for him. ⟦s10⟧ For our pascha hath been sacrificed, even Christ, that we may find in him wherewith we may feast. ⟦s11⟧ And from this, that Christ hath been sacrificed, both are effected: that we both ought deservedly to do what the Apostle admonishes, and are able also to accomplish it. ⟦s12⟧ For from the immolation and eating of the Lamb (if only we eat him in the *azyma* of sincerity and of truth) we receive grace, that we may be able to fulfill the admonitions of the Apostle. ⟦s13⟧ Let him, then, eat the Lamb with *azyma* who [would be] a new lump, who desires to be pure of all leaven, who prepares himself apt and fit for the eating of Christ. ⟦s14⟧ Behold, now you have received why they who are called *azyma,* why they who are admonished to be made a new lump, are enjoined [0263D] to feast on the Lamb with *azyma.* ⟦s15⟧ But it yet remains to inquire that which we set in the last place. ⟦s16⟧ For since Solomon says: *Let thine eyelids go before thy steps [n: (Prov. IV)]*, and since it is prior to look forward than to proceed, to recognize than to do — why is the *azyma* of truth set after by the Apostle in admonishing, if it must be prior in eating? ⟦s17⟧ But that we may come the more conveniently to this point of the question, let us first diligently inspect what goes before. ⟦s18⟧ The Apostle, then, teaches how it is lawful to eat the Lamb, and how it is by no means lawful to eat him. ⟦s19+20⟧ It is not lawful to eat him in the leaven of malice and of wickedness; but, as you consequently hear, in the *azyma* of sincerity and of truth.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Hence it is permitted to observe what there is between the pasch [0264A] of the Jews and the pasch of Christians. ⟦s2⟧ There, assuredly, bodily leaven is interdicted; here, however, spiritual leaven. ⟦s3⟧ There unleavened bread is enjoined; here, however, the *azyma* of sincerity and of truth. ⟦s4⟧ And deservedly indeed: for there is sacrificed the lamb of the Law, but a beast; here, however, the lamb spiritual and supercelestial. ⟦s5⟧ There a brute animal; here man, God, the Word of God. ⟦s6⟧ The immolation of each gives to pass over from servitude to liberty — but of the one, from bodily [servitude] to bodily [liberty]; of the other, from spiritual to spiritual. ⟦s7⟧ The one immolation gives to pass over from Egypt into the desert; the other gives to return from the world into paradise. ⟦s8⟧ The one, in fine, gives to pass over from the servitude of Pharaoh into an earthly kingdom; the other transmits from the servitude of the devil into the kingdom of heaven. ⟦s9⟧ From these things it is given to understand how incomparably greater, how [0264B] inestimably worthier this solemnity is than that; our pasch, in fine, than the Jews': *For our pascha hath been sacrificed, even Christ. ⟦s10⟧ Therefore,* he says, *let us keep the feast [n: (I Cor. V)]*. ⟦s11⟧ You have heard what is the fruit of the immolation. ⟦s12⟧ Do you wish yet to hear what is the fruit of the feasting? ⟦s13⟧ *Unless ye eat the flesh of the Son of man and drink his blood, ye shall not have life in you [n: (Joan. VI)]*. ⟦s14⟧ But, lest it be thought said of any life whatever, let us hear yet what is said upon the same: *He that eateth my flesh and drinketh my blood hath life everlasting [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s15⟧ Behold what fruit he finds from the eating of him — not every one, indeed, but he that approaches worthily: *Otherwise, he that eateth and drinketh unworthily, eateth and drinketh judgment to himself [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s16⟧ Let us keep the feast, then, but not unworthily. ⟦s17⟧ This is what [0264C] the Apostle wished to teach you when he subjoins: Not in the leaven of malice and of wickedness, but in the *azyma* of sincerity and of truth. ⟦s18⟧ To eat in the leaven of malice and of wickedness is, beyond doubt, to eat unworthily. ⟦s19⟧ But that you may be made worthy, eat in the *azyma* of sincerity and of truth. ⟦s20⟧ Behold, two things are forbidden, two are enjoined. ⟦s21⟧ Behold, you have two against two: the *azyma* of sincerity against the leaven of malice, the *azyma* of truth against the leaven of wickedness. ⟦s22⟧ Now every spiritual corruption in men seems to arise either from concupiscence of the flesh or from ignorance of the mind, and every kind of spiritual leaven [seems] to be comprehended under these two. ⟦s23+24⟧ But by the name of malice and of wickedness the Apostle is not said to have comprehended every kind of leaven; for not all concupiscence pertains to [0264D] malice, just as not all ignorance is imputed to wickedness. ⟦s25⟧ It is one thing to feel concupiscence, and another to consent to concupiscence. ⟦s26⟧ It is imputed to infirmity that we feel concupiscence against the will; to malice, that we consent to concupiscence out of deliberation. ⟦s27⟧ For malice is to consent, out of purpose and deliberation, with a ready mind, to a known evil. ⟦s28⟧ What else, then, is malice than unbridled concupiscence? ⟦s29+30⟧ For concupiscence is either bridled or unbridled: the former venial, the latter damnable. ⟦s31⟧ Yet each infects, each corrupts, and each by that very thing shows itself to pertain to leaven. ⟦s32⟧ So likewise ignorance is either venial or damnable. ⟦s33⟧ Venial, indeed, [0265A] that which descends from infirmity; damnable, that which rises from malice. ⟦s34⟧ The one beclouds the mind, for the other blinds it. ⟦s35⟧ For malice is wont so often to blind a man that it permits him not to think of his neighbor in simplicity and goodness, and sometimes not even of God. ⟦s36⟧ Malice, then, begets and nourishes the vice which the Apostle in this place names wickedness (*nequitia*), in that in its own judgment it can no longer (*nequeat*) keep the rule of truth and of goodness.
