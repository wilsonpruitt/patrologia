# Cruces — 11547 (Richard of St Victor, *Declarationes nonnullarum difficultatum Scripturae*)

Garbled / anomalous printed readings in Migne, PL 196, cols 0255A–0266C. Rendered
literally in the translation; logged here rather than silently emended.

- **col 0255/0256 (chunk 0, head 2)** — Migne prints the running head as
  *"Qui dicatur **anima**, et quid purgari a veteri fermento."* "anima" (soul)
  makes no sense against the chapter's content, which concerns who are called
  *azymi* (unleavened) yet told to purge the old leaven. Almost certainly a
  misprint for *azymus/azymi*. Head translated literally as printed ("Who is
  said to be a soul…").

- **col 0256B (chunk 0)** — Migne prints *"quomodo ab Apostolo **axymi**
  dicantur"* — "axymi" for *azymi* (metathesis/typo). The technical term is
  kept in italic Latin throughout this work (the quaestio turns on the
  untranslatable *azyma/azymi/azymus* pun); this one instance printed with the
  corrupt spelling. Rendered as *azymi* in the flow, anomaly logged here.

## Translation-convention note (not a crux)

The Greek-loan technical term *azyma / azymi / azymus* (unleavened bread / the
unleavened / unleavened one) is kept in **italic Latin** throughout, because the
entire question is a lexical argument built on its triple reference (the bread,
the persons, the feast) that no single English word carries. *pascha* is
likewise kept where Richard etymologizes it (*pascha = transitus*, "a
passing-over"). *fermentum* → "leaven," *conspersio* → "lump," *massa* → "mass."
Note contents (e.g. *[n: (II Cor. XV)]* for what is 1 Cor 15:53) are reproduced
verbatim per the sacred-marker rule, uncorrected.
