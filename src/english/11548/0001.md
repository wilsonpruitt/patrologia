## LETTER IX. OF M. TO RICHARD.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ To the lord R., by the grace of God prior of the Church of Saint Victor, his own M., greeting — to which he rather longs cheerfully to ascend.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Among the things which I deposited in your house are two linen cloths and two hand-towels, of the use of which I now have need. ⟦s2⟧ Wherefore let your kindness bring it about that both be distinguished from one another, and that one of the linen cloths, which lacks embroidery, together with whichever [1229B] of the hand-towels is the newer, be sent to me by this our friend, brother Walter. ⟦s3⟧ But let the rest, placed together, be kept in your custody as before, until I come to you. ⟦s4⟧ Give thanks to your venerable friend the prior of Saint Evurtius, and to this bearer of these presents, brother Walter, who out of veneration for you are ready to show me more things than I would wish to receive from them for nothing. ⟦s5⟧ Farewell forever in Christ Jesus our Lord. ⟦s6⟧ Wherever on earth I may be, I am yours, desiring to comply with your will and to serve your generosity. ⟦s7⟧ Which the matter itself, God granting, will unfold, if ever the time to come shall afford us occasion of serving you.

## LETTER X. OF THE SAME TO THE SAME.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1229C]

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ To the lord R., by the grace of God prior of Saint Victor, his own M., greeting — to which the way of truth leads.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Whoever leans upon the grace of those whose friendships he has earned discloses the tokens of his own success. ⟦s2⟧ For whosesoever affections each has won for himself by merits, by the right of those same [1230A] he discharges their offices, so far, however, as accords with honesty. ⟦s3⟧ And so I, brought into your friendship not by this track — but by that whereby the kindness implanted in you showed me beforehand the approach — entered freely into your grace. ⟦s4⟧ For while I call to mind the condition of your goodness, it becomes clearly known to me that your generosity, as is fitting and expedient, lies open to all. ⟦s5⟧ Wherefore, that I may comply with your excellent custom, I approach the magnificence of your charity, that you would grant some place of gentle affection and pious desire to lie open to me with you. ⟦s6⟧ For the friendship of the lord Walter, prior of Saint Evurtius, I render you manifold thanks. ⟦s7⟧ If anything shall please you, you will be able to send word by this my messenger to your friend the prior of Saint Alban. ⟦s8⟧ Farewell. ⟦s9⟧ If [1230B] any of your friends remain in the places through which this boy will have his journey, I pray that your love send him on to them, lest they suffer injuries to be done to him on the way. ⟦s10⟧ Farewell forever.

## LETTER XI. OF G., PRIOR OF ST ALBAN, TO RICHARD.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ To his venerable friend, and deservedly honorable, Richard, prior of Saint Victor, G., prior of the church of Saint Alban, greeting and the affection of due love.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Because you have taken pains diligently to carry through my petitions, long since entrusted to your care, I confidently attach the present one to the rest. ⟦s2⟧ Bend, therefore, to my prayers, as your custom permits, and let your love receive the bearer of these presents, my brother M., [1230C] commended for my sake, and in whatever he shall have need let your counsel support him, that I may have more manifold thanksgivings to render you. ⟦s3⟧ Let your love graciously receive the little gift which I have sent to be presented to you on my behalf, and regard rather the affection of the sender than the smallness of the thing sent. ⟦s4⟧ Farewell.
