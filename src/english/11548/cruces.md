# Cruces — 11548 (Richard of St Victor, Epistolae et miscellanea)

## Col 1225B (chunk 0000) — obscure/possibly corrupt term
- **Reading (as printed):** `quas in schola et frequenti verborum commercio consuevistis canigenas appellare`
- **Note:** `canigenas` (acc. pl.) is not a standard Latin word and yields no clear sense as an epithet for *divitiae* ("riches") in the schoolroom banter Ernisius recalls; it may be a corruption or a coined slang term. Left untranslated in italic (*canigenae*) rather than emended or glossed, and rendered: "riches which in the school and in the frequent traffic of words you used to call *canigenae*."

## Col 1225C (chunk 0000) — printed lacuna in the Horace quotation
- **Reading (as printed):** `Nunquid tunc fungebamini vice cotis, . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . *acutum* / *Reddere quae ferrum valet, exsors ipsa secandi?*` [n: (HORAT. Ars. poet. V, 304.)]
- **Note:** Migne prints a row of spaced periods marking an ellipsis in the middle of the Horace couplet (*Ars poetica* 304–305, "fungar vice cotis, acutum / reddere quae ferrum valet exsors ipsa secandi"). The dots are the edition's own lacuna mark, not garbled type. Reproduced verbatim in place; the two italicized fragments of the couplet are kept as two lines and translated ("*which can render iron* / *sharp, though itself having no share in cutting?*").

## Col 1228D (chunk 0000) — minor typographical slip
- **Reading (as printed):** `latori praesentium corum quae penes nos habentur notulas compendiosas tradidimus`
- **Note:** `corum` is a plain misprint for *eorum* ("of those") — the genitive governing *quae penes nos habentur* ("those things which are in our possession"). Rendered per sense ("brief notes of those things which are in our possession"); the Latin itself is not emended. Noted for completeness.
