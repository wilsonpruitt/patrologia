## PROLOGUE.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0405A]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ After the human race was cast out from the joys of paradise, coming into this pilgrimage of the present life, it has a heart blind to spiritual understanding. ⟦s2⟧ To which blind heart, if it were said with a human voice: Follow God, or: Love God, as was said to it in the law, once sent outside and cold through the torpor of unbelief, it would not grasp what it heard. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore the divine word speaks to the torpid and cold soul through certain riddles, and out of the things she knows it secretly instils into her the love she does not know. ⟦s4⟧ For allegory makes, as it were, a kind of engine for the soul set far from God, that by it she may be lifted up to God: for with riddles interposed, while she recognizes in the words something that is her own, she understands in the sense of the words [0405B] what is not her own, and through earthly words she is separated from earth. ⟦s5⟧ For by this, that she does not shrink from the known, she understands something unknown. ⟦s6⟧ For by things known to us, out of which allegories are put together, the divine meanings are put together, and while we come to know the outward words, we arrive at the inward understanding. ⟦s7⟧ For hence it is that in this book which is written as the Song of Songs, words of a love as it were of the body are set down, so that the soul, chafed back to warmth from her own body by discourses of her accustomed manner, may grow warm again, and through words of the love that is below may be roused to the love that is above. ⟦s8⟧ For in this book kisses are named, breasts are named, cheeks are named, thighs are named. ⟦s9⟧ In which words Holy Scripture is not to be derided, [0405C] but the ampler mercy of God is to be considered; because, while it names the members of the body, it so calls to love.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is to be noted that he is wondrously with us, and works mercifully. ⟦s2⟧ Who, that he might kindle our heart to the incitement of holy love, came down even to the words of our base love. ⟦s3⟧ But whence he humbles himself in speaking, thence he exalts us in understanding, because from the discourses of this love we learn by what power of this love we may glow in the love of the Godhead. ⟦s4⟧ Yet this must be watchfully looked to by us, lest, when we hear the words of outward love, we remain at the sensing of outward things, and the engine which is set there that it may lift us should rather press us down so that we be not lifted. ⟦s5⟧ We ought therefore in these bodily words, in these outward words, to seek whatever is inward, and, speaking of the body, as it were [0405D] to become outside the body. ⟦s6⟧ We ought to come to these nuptials of the Bridegroom and the bride with the understanding of inmost charity, that is, [0406A] with the wedding garment, that is, with a worthy understanding of charity: with which if we are not clothed, let us drive away [cj: *repellamus*; read *repellemur*, "we shall be driven out"] from this nuptial banquet into the outer darkness, that is, the blindness of ignorance. ⟦s7⟧ We ought through these words of passion to pass over to the virtue of impassibility. ⟦s8⟧ For Holy Scripture is in words and senses just as a painting is in colors and things. ⟦s9⟧ And he is exceedingly foolish who so clings to the colors of a painting that he does not know the things which are painted. ⟦s10⟧ For if we embrace the words which are outwardly spoken, and do not know the senses, we hold only the colors, as though not knowing the things which are depicted: *The letter killeth,* as it is written, *but the spirit quickeneth [n: (II Cor. III)]*; for the letter covers over the spirit as chaff covers the wheat; but chaff is for beasts to feed on, wheat for men. ⟦s11⟧ He therefore who [0406B] uses human reason, let him cast away the chaff of beasts, and hasten to eat the wheat of the spirit. ⟦s12⟧ For to this end it is useful, that the mysteries be covered, wrapped up in the letter. ⟦s13⟧ For hence it is written: *The wise shall hide understanding [n: (Prov. X)]* [var: Vulg. *Sapiens abscondet scientiam*, "a wise man hideth knowledge"], because assuredly under the covering of the letter the spiritual understanding is covered over. ⟦s14⟧ Hence again in the same book Scripture says: *It is the glory of God to conceal the word [n: (Prov. XXV)]*. ⟦s15⟧ For to the mind seeking God, God appears the more gloriously the more subtly and inwardly he is searched out that he may appear. ⟦s16⟧ But are we not to inquire into those things which God conceals in mysteries? ⟦s17⟧ We ought indeed.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For there follows: *And it is the glory of kings to search out the discourse.* ⟦s2⟧ For they who have already learned to rule and to search out their own bodies or the motions of the flesh, [0406C] are kings. ⟦s3⟧ It is therefore the glory of kings to search out the discourse, because it is the praise of those who live well to scrutinize the secrets of God's commandments. ⟦s4⟧ Hearing therefore the words of human converse, we ought to be as it were men out of the earth, lest, if we hear in a human way the things which are said, we be able to perceive nothing of divinity concerning those things which we ought to hear. ⟦s5⟧ Paul desired his disciples to be as it were no longer men, to whom he said: *For whereas there is among you zeal and contention, are you not men? [n: (I Cor. III.)]* The Lord esteemed his disciples as it were no longer men, when he said: *Whom do men say that the Son of man is [n: (Matth. XVI)]*. ⟦s6⟧ And when they had answered him with the words of men, he at once added: *But whom do you say that I am?* ⟦s7⟧ For when above he says *men,* and then [0406D] subjoins, *but you,* he made a certain distance between men and his disciples, because plainly, [0407A] by instilling divine things into them, he made them above men, as the Apostle says: *If therefore there be any new creature in Christ, the old things are passed away [n: (II Cor. V)]*. ⟦s8⟧ And we know that in our resurrection the body is so joined to the spirit that all that had been of passibility is taken up into the power of the spirit. ⟦s9⟧ He therefore who follows God ought daily to imitate his own reason, so that, as then, he may have nothing passible in his heart, so that according to the inward man he may already be a new creature. ⟦s10⟧ Let him now tread down whatever has been old, and in old words seek the force of newness alone. ⟦s11⟧ For Holy Scripture is a certain mountain, from which the Lord comes into our hearts to be understood; of which mountain it is said through the prophet: *God will come from Lebanon, and the Holy One from the shady and thick mountain [n: (Habac. III)]* [var: Vulg. *Deus ab austro veniet, et Sanctus de monte Pharan*, "God will come from the south, and the Holy One from mount Pharan"]. ⟦s12⟧ That mountain is thick [0407B] by its meanings, and shady by its allegories. ⟦s13⟧ But it must be known that, when the voice of the Lord sounds on the mountain, we are bidden to wash our garments, and to be cleansed from every defilement of the flesh, if we hasten to approach the mountain. ⟦s14⟧ For it is written: *That if a beast touch the mountain, it shall be stoned [n: (Hebr. XII)]*. ⟦s15⟧ For a beast touches the mountain when men, given over to irrational motions, draw near to the loftiness of Holy Scripture, and do not understand it according as they ought, but irrationally bend it to the understanding of their own pleasure. ⟦s16⟧ For everyone absurd, or slothful in mind, if he be seen about this mountain, let him be slain by most fierce sentences as it were by stones. ⟦s17⟧ For this mountain burns, because Holy Scripture, which spiritually fills with the fire of love, sets alight. ⟦s18⟧ Whence it is written: *Thy word is exceedingly fiery [0407C] [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*. ⟦s19⟧ Whence, when certain men walking in the way heard the words of God, they said: *Was not our heart burning within us, while he opened to us the Scriptures? [n: (Luc. XXIV.)]* Whence it is said through Moses: *In his right hand a fiery law [n: (Deut. XXXIII)]*. ⟦s20⟧ By the left hand of God the wicked are understood, who do not pass over into the right-hand part. ⟦s21⟧ The right hand of God are the elect, who are separated from those on the left. ⟦s22⟧ In the right hand of God, therefore, the law is fiery, because in the hearts of the elect (who are to be set at the right hand) the divine precepts are fragrant, and they are set alight with the ardor of charity. ⟦s23⟧ Let this fire, therefore, burn away whatever of rust and of oldness is outwardly in us, and offer our mind as a holocaust in the contemplation of God.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0407D]Nor is it to be attended to idly that this book is called not a canticle, but the Song of Songs. ⟦s2⟧ For as in the Old Testament some things are holy, others the Holy of Holies; some Sabbaths, others the Sabbaths of Sabbaths; so in Holy Scripture some things are canticles, others the Song of Songs. ⟦s3⟧ Holy were the things which were done outwardly in the tabernacle; Sabbaths were those which were celebrated in each single week; but the Holy of Holies were received with a certain more secret veneration; and the Sabbaths of Sabbaths were kept only on the highest festivals. ⟦s4⟧ This Song of Songs is a secret, and a certain inward solemnity, which secret is penetrated in the hidden places of one who understands. ⟦s5⟧ For if it is attended to in outward words, it is not a secret. ⟦s6⟧ It is also to be known that in Holy Scripture some are [0408A] canticles of victory, others canticles of exhortation and protestation, others canticles of exultation, others canticles of help, others canticles of conjunction with God. ⟦s7⟧ The canticle of victory is that which Moses sang when the Red Sea had been crossed, saying: *Let us sing to the Lord, for he is gloriously honored: the horse and the rider he hath cast forth into the sea [n: (Exod. XV)]* [var: Vulg. *magnificatus est … dejecit*, "is gloriously magnified … he hath thrown down"]. ⟦s8⟧ The canticle of exhortation and protestation is that which Moses said to the Israelites as they drew near to the land of promise: *Attend, O heaven, to the things which I shall speak; let the earth hear the words out of my mouth [n: (Deut. XXXII)]* [var: Vulg. *Audite caeli quae loquor, audiat terra verba oris mei*, "Hear, O ye heavens, the things which I speak"]. ⟦s9⟧ The canticle of exultation is that which Anna sang, the fruitfulness of the Church having been foreseen in herself, saying: *My heart hath exulted in the Lord [n: (I Reg. II)]*, where by herself she expressed the figured fruitfulness of the Church's offspring, when she said: *The barren hath borne [0408B] very many, and she that had very many is become weak [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s10⟧ The canticle of help David sang after the battle, saying: *I will love thee, O Lord, my strength [n: (Psal. XVII)]*. ⟦s11⟧ But the canticle of conjunction with God is this, which is sung at the nuptials of the Bridegroom and the bride, that is, the Song of Songs, which is by so much loftier than all canticles as it is also offered at nuptials of a loftier solemnity. ⟦s12⟧ For by those canticles vices are avoided, but by this one each and all are enriched with virtues. ⟦s13⟧ For by those the enemy is guarded against, but by this the Lord embraces with familiar love.
