## PROLOGUE. (cont.)

And it is to be noted that sometimes the Lord in Holy Scripture calls himself Lord, sometimes father, sometimes bridegroom. For when he wishes to be feared, he names himself Lord; when he wishes to be honored, father; [0408C] when he wishes to be loved, bridegroom. For he himself says through the prophet: *If I am Lord, where is my fear? If I am father, where is my honor? [n: (Malac. I.)]* [var: Vulg. Mal. 1:6 has the father first: *Si ergo pater ego sum, ubi est honor meus? et si Dominus ego sum, ubi est timor meus?*] And again he says: *I have espoused thee to myself in justice and in faith [n: (Ose. II)]* [var: Vulg. Osee 2:20 *sponsabo te mihi in fide*, "I will espouse thee to me in faith"]. Or assuredly: I have remembered the day of thy espousal in the desert. But because he first wishes to be feared that he may be honored, and first to be honored that men may come to him, he both names himself Lord for the sake of fear, and father for the sake of honor, and bridegroom for the sake of love; through fear one comes to honor, but through honor of him one arrives at love. For by how much honor is worthier than fear, by so much the more does God rejoice to be called father than Lord. And by how much love is dearer than honor, by so much the more does God rejoice to be called bridegroom than father. In this book, therefore, the Lord [0408D] and the Church are named not Lord and handmaid, but Bridegroom and bride, so that not only fear, not only reverence, but love also may be served, and in these outward words the inward affection be stirred up. When he names himself Lord, he indicates that we have been created; when he calls himself father, he indicates that we have been adopted; when he names himself bridegroom, he indicates that we have been conjoined. But it is more to be conjoined to God than created and adopted. In this book, therefore, where he is called Bridegroom, something loftier is instilled, since in it the covenant of conjunction is shown. Which names, in the New Testament (because the conjunction of the Word and the flesh, of Christ and the Church, is now accomplished and celebrated) are recalled with frequent repetition. Whence John [0409A] says while the Lord was living: *He that hath the bride is the bridegroom [n: (Joan. III)]*. Whence the same Lord: *The children of the bridegroom shall not fast so long as the bridegroom is with them [n: (Matth. IX)]* [var: Vulg. Matt. 9:15 *Numquid possunt filii sponsi lugere*, "Can the children of the bridegroom mourn"; the "shall not fast" form is that of Mark 2:19 and Luke 5:34]. Whence also it is said to the Church: *I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you a chaste virgin to Christ [n: (I Cor. XI)]*. And again: *That he might present to himself a glorious Church, not having spot or wrinkle [n: (Ephes. V)]*. And again in the Apocalypse of John: *Blessed are they that are called to the supper of the Lamb of the marriage [n: (Apoc. XIX)]* [var: Vulg. Apoc. 19:9 *ad coenam nuptiarum Agni*, "to the marriage supper of the Lamb"]. And again in the same place: *And I saw the bride as a new spouse coming down from heaven [n: (Apoc. XXI)]* [var: Vulg. Apoc. 21:2 *Et vidi sanctam civitatem Jerusalem novam descendentem de caelo a Deo, paratam sicut sponsam ornatam viro suo*, "And I saw the holy city, the new Jerusalem, coming down out of heaven from God, prepared as a bride adorned for her husband"]. Nor does it depart from a great mystery, that this book of Solomon is set third among his works: for the ancients said that there are three orders of life, the moral, the natural, and the contemplative. Which lives the Greeks named ethic, physic, and [0409B] theoric. In the Proverbs, too, the moral life is expressed, where it is said: *Hear, my son, my wisdom, and incline thy ear to my prudence [n: (Prov. II)]* [var: Vulg. Prov. 5:1 *Fili mi, attende ad sapientiam meam*, "My son, attend to my wisdom"]. But in Ecclesiastes, the natural. For there it is considered that all things tend to an end, when it is said: *Vanity of vanities, and all is vanity [n: (Eccle. I)]*. In the Song of Songs the contemplative life is expressed, while in it the coming and the sight of the Lord himself is desired, when it is said in the voice of the bride: *Come from Lebanon, come [n: (Cant. IV)]*, or else he signified the orders and the lives of the three patriarchs, namely of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Abraham indeed held morality through obedience, while Isaac figured the natural life by digging wells. To dig wells in the depth is to search out by inquiry, through natural consideration, all the things which are below. [0409C] But Jacob held the contemplative life, who saw the angels ascending and descending. But because natural consideration does not lead to perfection unless morality be first held, rightly is Ecclesiastes set after the Proverbs; and because the supernal contemplation is not beheld unless these things which slip away below be first despised, rightly is the Song of Songs set after Ecclesiastes. For first it is to compose one's manners, afterwards to consider all things which are present as though they were not present. But in the third place, with the cleansed edge of the heart, he beholds the inward and supernal things. And so by these steps of books he made as it were a ladder to contemplation, so that while first things are well conducted in the world, afterwards even the honorable things [0409D] of the world be despised, and at the last the inmost things of God be beheld. Now thus, in general, the coming of the Lord in this time is awaited from the voice of the Church, so that also, in particular, every single soul may behold the entrance of God into her own heart, as it were the approach of the Bridegroom into the bridal chamber.

And it is to be known that in this book four speaking persons are introduced, the Bridegroom and the bride, the young maidens with the bride, and the troops of companions with the Bridegroom. For the bride assuredly is the Church herself, the Bridegroom the Lord; but the young maidens with the bride are souls beginning, and coming to their youth through new zeal; while the companions of the Bridegroom are either angels, who often, living from him, have appeared to men, or else all perfect men in the Church, [0410A] who have known how to announce the truth to men. But those who singly are young maidens or companions are all together the bride, because all together they are the Church, although according to each one all these three names may be taken. For he who loves God perfectly is the bride; he who preaches the Bridegroom is a companion. He who, as yet new, follows the way of good men is a young maiden. Let us strive, therefore, that we may be the bride: if we do not yet prevail in this, let us be companions: if we have attained neither of these, let us at least come together to this bridal chamber as young maidens. Since therefore we have spoken of the Bridegroom and the bride, the Lord and the Church, let us, as young maidens or companions, hear the words of the Bridegroom, hear the words of the bride, and from their discourses learn the fervor of love. And so let holy Church, [0410B] long awaiting the coming of the Lord, long thirsting for the fountain of life, say how it behoves her to see the presence of her Bridegroom, how she desires him.
