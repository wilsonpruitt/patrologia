## CHAPTER ONE. *How God is sought in stillness, and on the greater desire for grace.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In my little bed by night I sought him whom my soul loveth [n: (Cant. III)]* [var: Vulg. Cant. 3:1 *per noctes*, "by nights," plural; the exposition below turns on the single night]. ⟦s2⟧ The soul which seeks God, and desires to attain more fully to the love and the knowledge of God, ought to seek him in the little bed, that is, in the stillness of the mind. ⟦s3⟧ For she ought to be at leisure, and to be still from the occupation and disquiet of earthly things. ⟦s4⟧ For unless she has been still from the care and occupation and [0410C] love of outward things, she cannot seek God, nor does she deserve to find him. ⟦s5⟧ In the little bed therefore, that is, in the stillness of the mind, God is to be sought. ⟦s6⟧ And by night, that is, through the adversities of temptations and of labors, and in the battle of the virtues and the vices. ⟦s7⟧ For the soul must labor in many adversities, and be held back by many obstacles, and be wearied by heavy wrestling, before she perfectly corrects her manners, and obtains cleanness of heart, and deserves to see God. ⟦s8⟧ Whence deservedly she is said to seek God by night, namely through adversities and labors. ⟦s9⟧ And not in the bed, but in the little bed, because the stillness had from such things is scant, and narrow, since vices assuredly assail the soul, and former habit, and the memory and the delight of past sins. ⟦s10⟧ For which cause she also calls it her own little bed, [0410D] and not ours, because she cannot have this in common with the Bridegroom, nor perfectly enjoy him in so scant a stillness, because his place is made in peace, and not in the battle and disturbance of vices. ⟦s11⟧ In this manner God is sought in the little bed by night by those beginning and not yet perfect. ⟦s12⟧ After this he is sought in another manner in the little bed and by night, namely when the soul, her vices more fully mortified and her temptations lulled, and her conscience purged, shall have obtained a fuller stillness, the grace of the Holy Spirit coming into the mind and quieting the vices and the temptations. ⟦s13⟧ According to that which is written: *The bow he shall break in pieces and shatter, arms and shields he shall burn with fire [n: (Psal. XLV)]*. ⟦s14⟧ Then the soul will be able to be at leisure, and to see that the Lord is sweet. ⟦s15⟧ Thus [0411A] the peace and tranquillity of the mind is the little bed in which the bride rests. ⟦s16⟧ In this little bed she seeks the beloved by night, namely when the desires of the flesh are lulled, and after the battle and the labors victory has followed, and stillness has been given. ⟦s17⟧ For in the day there is laboring, and in the night there is a ceasing from labor, and in the night there is stillness. ⟦s18⟧ When therefore, after the burden of the day and the heats, temptation namely, the soul comes from labor to the little bed of stillness, and to the night of resting from weariness and disquiet, then, now unencumbered and at leisure, she will be able better to seek the beloved. ⟦s19⟧ In this little bed and in this night she sleeps, but her heart keeps watch. ⟦s20⟧ For she sleeps outwardly, but inwardly keeps watch. ⟦s21⟧ And as much as she is still and at leisure outwardly, so much is she occupied and unquiet inwardly in seeking the beloved. ⟦s22⟧ But she seeks him in the little bed and not in the bed; because [0411B] she does not possess full stillness, but narrow, so long as she does not enjoy the beloved to her wish, nor has him with her. ⟦s23⟧ Whence also she calls the little bed itself her own, as being one which she alone has, and in which she does not yet rest with the Bridegroom. ⟦s24⟧ And therefore it is well said that she seeks by night (namely of blindness) in which she is held, so that she does not fully enjoy him, nor feel his presence. ⟦s25⟧ For he is indeed present by essence, present by desire, and he is already had who is desired, because one not had could not be desired; but he is not at hand by the revelation of his presence. ⟦s26⟧ For he has not yet put her blindness to flight, nor illumined her to feel his presence. ⟦s27⟧ Of this night, therefore, she complains, and pleads that through it she seeks the beloved, and does not find him. ⟦s28⟧ She desires [0411C] therefore to be illumined and to have the eyes of her heart opened, that she may find him, and by the grace drawing near may understand his presence, and may feel both that she is seen by him and that she sees him. ⟦s29⟧ For she seeks the illumination of an ampler grace, the understanding of his presence, and the experience of spiritual sweetness: *I sought,* she says, *him whom my soul loveth.* ⟦s30⟧ In the love of the soul we can note a spiritual and also an affectionate love. ⟦s31⟧ But when we love someone affectionately, we say that we love him as our own soul, as if she should say: Whom I love affectionately and spiritually, that is, unto the salvation of the soul, not unto temporal or carnal advantage. ⟦s32⟧ But the Bridegroom does not always at once offer himself to her who seeks him, nor show himself to her who desires him, but often defers to fulfil her wishes that he may increase them, and afterwards reward them increased and more perfectly [0411D] fulfil them. ⟦s33⟧ He also sometimes defers these things, that he may prove the constancy of her who seeks, and grant the things desired the more profitably the later. ⟦s34⟧ For what is acquired with labor is held more closely and guarded more warily. ⟦s35⟧ And sometimes desires more quickly fulfilled fail, and deferred ones grow the more. ⟦s36⟧ Whence the bride, feeling the delay, and not knowing the dispensation, and impatient of love, complains, saying: *I sought, and I found him not [n: (Cant. V)]*. ⟦s37⟧ For she does not yet perceive the grace sought for, she has not yet felt the presence of the Bridegroom.
