## CHAPTER IV. *How the soul knows the presence of the angels, and lays open her desires to them.*

[0416B]

*The watchmen who keep the city found me [n: (Cant. III)]*. The soul thus desiring, thus seeking, is found by the watchmen who keep the city, namely the holy angels, who keep the city, that is, the Church or the faithful, and by whose keeping the mercy of Christ fortifies its own. And they are rightly called watchmen, because they watch and are solicitous about the elect, that they may be defended from temptation, that they may make progress in good, and that they may be saved. For they are ministering spirits, sent forth in ministry for the sake of those who receive the inheritance of salvation. By their ministry and vigilance God protects his faithful; he that keepeth Israel does not slumber, neither does he sleep. Our adversary, [0416C] however, goes about like a lion, seeking whom he may devour. Some he devours as a lion, whom he casts down by open battle, and others he seduces by hidden fraud; for these, as is read in Job, he hides a snare in the earth, and sets a trap upon the path [n: (Job I)]; because he both deceives them through earthly desires, and sets there the nooses of his deception where he sees the path of their heart to be, and sees them prone to sinning. Thus the enemy goes about to deceive. But the holy angels run to and fro to defend. This discord is carried on between the angels of peace and the angels of discord. Thus is the contest waged for man, and he does not watch for himself, but grows torpid. For it behooves him to see, to watch and to pray, both to see and to consider his own peril, to watch over his own salvation, [0416D] and to pray and implore help against temptation. For how many does that lion devour, how many does the destroying angel put to death, of those who do not have the sign of the living God on their foreheads, who do not have both their doorposts and the lintel of the house anointed with the blood of the Lamb of God; who do not have faith working through love, and do not deserve to be fortified and defended by the banner of the cross of Christ or by the power of his passion. Nor does Leviathan wonder if he swallow these down: for he shall swallow up a river, and shall not wonder, the evil, that is, and the carnal, who flow away after the manner of water and run down from sin to sin, and by the slipperiness of vices descend to the depths of torments. Even, alas! he has confidence that the Jordan may flow into his mouth, that is, the elect, over whom he trusts to prevail. O how great [0417A] a multitude of the swallowed, and how small a number of the rescued. But the merciful Lord hides his own in his tabernacle (that is, in the Church) in the day of evils, and protects them in the hidden place of his tabernacle (that is, in Christ), who is called the hidden place of the Church, because he dwells secretly and invisibly in his elect through charity; inasmuch indeed as it is joined to them, and they are protected through faith and through the power of his passion from the tempter, the devil. For because Christ by his passion conquered the enemy, by the power of that same passion he fortifies his own, that they may not fear the foe, since they are made members of him who triumphed over him. God is indeed faithful, who does not suffer his own to be tempted above that which they can bear, but with the temptation makes also an issue. So ordering and tempering temptations, [0417B] that from the very thing by which the enemy seeks to cast them down they may be tried, and being tried may the more be crowned. It happens besides that, in assailing them (so far as to separate them from God), he rather joins them to God, because, while they are tempted, they are more humbly made subject to God that they may be delivered from temptation. Whence blessed Job says that the beasts of the earth (namely, the demons) are made peace-making toward the good [n: (Job V)]. He does not say *pacatae*, because they do not have peace, but *pacificatae*, because they make peace for them while by assailing them they afflict them. God therefore turns to a crown for his own that which the enemy works for their ruin. But he does not so reckon it, but his heart is set on crushing, and on the destruction of not a few nations. Thus God hides the elect in the Church, in the day of evils he protects them in himself, he fortifies them by the keeping of angels. These he shows forth [0417C] to his own as ministers and messengers, that they may minister to them for salvation, and announce their necessities, and carry their prayers. And although he himself sees and knows the causes of every one, he yet wills that this be announced to him through his angels, so that he may commend his charity and his condescension toward man, and for the sake of messengers so worthy and so beloved may bestow more fully.

Nor is it a wonder that he shows forth his angels as ministers to the elect, and himself as well. For he too is himself the angel of great counsel, namely of our redemption and salvation, which he was sent to work in the midst of the earth. He ministers to us indeed by his manner of life and his humility, offering in himself an example of living, and setting himself as a little one in the midst of the disciples (that is, as humble), that we may be made little ones as he is. He ministered [0417D] to us also by his death, in which he labored for us that we might not labor, and bore a temporal death that we might not bear an eternal one. Therefore the Lord ministered to us in this life, and passing over out of this life also he will minister to us in that refreshment of twofold sweetness, because he will feed us with the milk of his humanity and the honey of his divinity. The ministry of the angels too is not only temporal to us, but also everlasting, because through their present help we shall receive the inheritance and eternal salvation, and shall enjoy their joy with them without end. But how do we suppose they love our salvation, and how greatly do they desire to have us as partners with them? Who could estimate with how great charity and care they watch over those committed to them? how they rouse the torpid, and kindle the more the solicitous and [0418A] fervent? How on this side they excuse evils, on that present good things to the divine gaze; how they defend, or obtain grace for these? And when they have looked upon a soul fervent with great desire, and sighing to God with pure intention, how do we suppose they love her, and rejoice together with her, and turn aside to her? How do they run to and fro between her and God? Since indeed they are the friends of the Bridegroom, they listen to her voice, which they make the Bridegroom hear; her voices are her desires: these cry out vehemently in the ear of the Bridegroom, these the friends listen to, namely the angels; in these they delight, these they announce, and they invite her to come, they console her, and admonish her to seek and to knock, for the reason that he who seeks finds, and to him who knocks it is opened. [0418B] And so meanwhile they frequent and visit the soul thus fervent, until the Bridegroom comes, and by the increase of a fuller grace they prepare her more fully for the coming of the Bridegroom himself. They draw her thought to understand their presence and to learn their familiarity, so far that from this acquaintance she may grow and advance to the divine. The soul therefore, seeking God, is found by these, and after the circuit of the city, after her search, she merits the coming of the blessed angels, she perceives their meeting, by these she is received; for these come before the Bridegroom, they show forth their presence, they reveal themselves: for they are angels of light, they come with light; and being suffused with it, she is at once illumined and touched, that she may understand their coming and feel their presence. And while she perceives this grace, she confesses that she has been found [0418C] by the watchmen, although she complains that she has not found the beloved. And so for the soul to have been found by the watchmen is for her to have found grace to feel their presence, she who before, suffering the darkness of her own blindness, felt nothing but herself. Then she directs her desire and her thought upon them, and forms a familiar colloquy as it were with acquaintances, she insinuates her vows, announces her love, asks about the Bridegroom. Have you seen him whom my soul loves? She seeks them as witnesses of her love, and equally of the longed-for presence of the Bridegroom. For certainly she knows that the angels of God enjoy his vision, and are not ignorant of her love and her desires, and she wonders and reckons it as it were undue, that they should see God and leave her destitute of that vision, she who would say: Have you not already long since seen him whom you have the desire [0418D] to enjoy, and in him my desires? The beloved declares that he stands at the door of the soul and knocks; and if any one shall open to him, he will come in to him, and will sup with him. I have already openly opened my door through desire of him, and he does not enter. Moreover, I have entered in to him: and I knock at his door, which he promises will be opened to him who knocks. And forgetful of both promises, he neither enters into mine nor opens his to me. I stand, and he does not look upon me; I knock, and he does not open; I cry out, and he does not answer; I seek him, and I do not find him. Whither then has he gone? Have you not seen him, you who are angels always seeing him, since you always see the face, and he and the Father are one, and he who sees the one sees also the other? Therefore him who does not enter my door, nor open his own, and [0419A] accordingly is found neither with me nor with himself, I shall seek with you, of you I shall inquire, to you I shall pass over, that with you I may find him whom my soul loves, the Bridegroom of the Church, etc.
