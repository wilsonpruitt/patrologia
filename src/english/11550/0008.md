## CHAPTER VII. *How in secret the mind enjoys God, and is instructed by him concerning hidden things.*

[0423D]

But it does not suffice the bride to have brought the beloved into the house of the mind, unless she bring him also into her chamber, where she may enjoy him more familiarly and more secretly, and be alone with him alone, and converse with him, and, all others being removed, be present at his counsels, know his secrets, learn his hidden purposes, and where God may make manifest to her the uncertain and hidden things of his wisdom. There she desires to hear the hidden word, that is, the inward and hidden inspiration, which in Job is called a hidden word [n: (Job IV)], because it is granted to few, or because it is enjoined silently to the ear of the heart. And this word she is declared to hear in the night, when deep sleep is wont to seize upon men, that is, when, the desires of the flesh being trodden down, [0424A] the stillness of the mind is possessed. In the silence of the night, therefore, when God is secretly with her in her chamber, when all things are pacified and still, when peace is had within from the disturbance of vices, and without from every unquietness and occupation, when the vices are silent, and the memory of all sins, and the outward senses too are lulled to sleep, and the memory of worldly things. And so, when there has been so great stillness, and all things are already silent, then at last the voice of God can be heard, which does not only speak, but whispers, that is, thinly and secretly makes something manifest to the human mind. And not only the whisper of God, but also the veins of his whisper does the soul then receive by stealth, because she understands the subtlety of the divine inspiration, and the origins of the causes by which grace comes to the mind. For in diverse ways [0424B] God visits, because now he pricks with the mind by the desire of divine love, now by the hope and joy of future recompense, now by the delight of some virtue, now by the knowledge of her own imperfection, now by the fear of punishment, and by the recollection of sins, or by the manifestation of some peril or negligence, now by the memory of a good thing, now by the example and grace of some perfect man. In these and other ways grace visits the mind, and not always in the same way, but now in this, now in that. For as in diverse persons there are diverse gifts, and distributions of the Spirit, so that this one receives this grace, that one that: so in one and the same man there are diverse visitations of the Spirit. The soul therefore with whom God whispers in silence knows and discerns these things, and according to the manner in which she is visited [0424C] she conforms and directs herself. For she knows the discretion of grace and its use, she understands too its presence or absence, its coming also and its departure, the causes also of its coming, the motions and thoughts, the forerunners and the heralds of grace. And that humility above all things merits it she has learned by frequent experience, and she understands the manifold and hidden manners, or motions, of humility. Nor is it doubted that she offers a sacrifice pleasing to God, and heaps up grace for herself, as often as she abases and humbles herself for God's sake. In order therefore that she may obtain it more abundantly, she follows the footsteps of humility as much in thought as in work. In mind she abases herself by her own judgment, and outwardly for God's sake she subjects [0424D] herself to men and puts herself after them, she chooses abjection, she makes herself contemptible, that she may be precious with God, she humbles herself that she may be exalted, she rejoices in reproaches; for she knows that for contumely she will receive grace from God, for reviling a blessing. David understood this, who, when Semei cursed him, chose rather to suffer him than to be avenged through his soldier, foreseeing that for that cursing good would be rendered him by the Lord. Thus God gives grace to the humble, but resists the proud. But who could know an old and ingrained pride, who its hidden roots and its subtle thoughts, by which for the most part it creeps in latently, and steals upon those who do not even know it, save when God whispers (that is, secretly reveals it)? Which, the more it is spread abroad in the mind, so much the less is it known, because through it the mind is the more [0425A] blinded. So also in words and in action it lies hid in manifold ways, nor is it known, unless one be instructed by grace to know it. And so pride lies hid in the soul, and the soul herself does not know it; but God sees this, because he beholds hidden things, and therefore he withdraws his grace, or does not grant it. Not only therefore for manifest, but also for hidden, and not for greater only, but also for lesser pride is the mind dried up of grace: *I will make desert,* says the Lord, *the mountains and the hills, and I will dry up all their shoot [var: Vulg. (Isa. 42:15) *gramen*, "grass"] [n: (Psal. XLII)]*; designating by the mountains the greater, the proud, and by the hills the lesser. These mountains are dried up and grow parched, but the valleys, that is, the humble, are watered with the water of grace, and abound with spiritual grain. To understand latently these mountains, and the origins of visiting grace [0425B] and the causes of meriting or demeriting it, is to receive by stealth the veins of the divine whisper. These veins creep silently and with a light murmur, and are not heard by an unquiet mind, and one in which vices, or any disturbances, make an uproar and cry out. And so the understanding of these things is perceived and learned in the chamber (that is, in the secret place of the mind), where all things are shut out but God, where the soul alone whispers with him alone; this is the solitude into which God leads the soul, that he may speak to her heart. There she can hear what the Lord God speaks in her. There he opens to her the inward ears only: for the ears of the heart lie open; the tumult of secular business closes them, and the stillness of secret consideration opens them. Then [0425C] the mind pierces the words of God more keenly, when it refuses to admit to itself the tumult of secular cares. This chamber is a school, where she may learn in silence, where the secrets of the precepts may be taught, where she may be instructed in the knowledge of the divine will, in the knowledge of manners, in the discretion of virtues, in the contemplation of spiritual things. To this chamber the soul ought to withdraw, as Moses to the tabernacle of the covenant, to consult the Lord concerning doubtful matters, concerning the ordering of the house of her mind, concerning the things to be done in it, concerning her own state, concerning the spiritual and bodily necessities of others, concerning the love of God, concerning the love or the defense of justice, concerning the discretion of zeal to be exercised, concerning the time for overlooking excesses, concerning compassion for offenders, concerning strictness. Here she daily examines and searches out [0425D] her inward and outward things, and what her meditation is, or about what it especially is or ought to be; what her speech is, moderate or dissolute, humble or proud; and likewise what her working is, strenuous or slack; finally what the disposition of the whole house is, and what things in it are to be corrected, what to be borne with. But in so far as she comes to purity of conscience, in however great a measure by mortifying her own will she has joined it to the divine, and made herself one with God, in so far does she know spiritual things and understand the will of God. In order therefore that she may perceive this grace, she rightly desires to bring the beloved into the chamber, that is, the secret place of stillness, of meditation, of prayer, where she may be still from outward things, may learn by inward and mystical meditation, and may obtain by prayer.
