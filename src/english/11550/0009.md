## CHAPTER VIII. *On the merits and the usefulness of the contemplative life, and when one ought to come to rest.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0426A]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, until she herself will [n: (Cant. III)]*. ⟦s2⟧ With much labor, insistence and prayer the bride found the beloved, and at last brought him into her chamber. ⟦s3⟧ Whence it is fitting that after her weariness she should at last rest, and enjoy the beloved whom she has laid hold of with so many sweats and runnings to and fro, and not easily be torn away from love of him. ⟦s4⟧ God therefore pleads on her behalf and admonishes that she be not roused from her sweet sleep (that is, from the stillness of divine love), nor drawn away from the secret of the chamber to the care of governing. ⟦s5⟧ For in this chamber she not only enjoys the embraces of the beloved and [0426B] carries through her own affairs, but also attends to the necessities of others. ⟦s6⟧ And in diverse ways she here enjoys God, namely now as Bridegroom, now as teacher, now as father; now she entreats him for herself, now by prayers she bends and appeases him for others, now she sleeps in his love, now she contemplates, now she orders and arranges certain things with him, now she hears from him and is taught, now she enjoys his embraces. ⟦s7⟧ For she has with him embraces and kisses, she has offspring and breasts, but all these things not material or carnal, but chaste and wholesome. ⟦s8⟧ In these embraces God clasps and is clasped, because he first brings in and insinuates his will to the soul with a certain sweetness: and when the soul has been aroused by this charity, while she joins herself to him who calls and draws her, she as it were clasps him who clasps [0426C] her. ⟦s9⟧ For in these embraces God clasps first, and thus in turn the soul clasps, because it belongs to the worthier or the more ample lover to clasp first; whence he also first loved us, that we might be able to love him, and he showed forth besides that charity than which no one can show forth a greater or an equal. ⟦s10⟧ And so God embraces the soul when he draws her to himself through grace and joins her to himself. ⟦s11⟧ He kisses her when he refreshes her with the sweetness of his grace; he makes her fruitful when he increases charity. ⟦s12⟧ Her breasts grow fat with milk when she is filled with the sweetness of compassion. ⟦s13⟧ There is there a spiritual wedlock, namely a concord and a conjunction of wills. ⟦s14⟧ There it is not a carnal coupling that makes the womb fruitful, but the Holy Spirit that impregnates the mind with twin offspring (that is, with a twofold charity), namely the love of God and of the neighbor. [0426D] Hence the soul becomes a wife; she has received the breasts of piety which before she did not have, when she was still a little one, but now she has learned to have compassion on sinners, yet to be indignant in a youthful manner. ⟦s15⟧ Here she sleeps with Christ, while she closes her eyes to outward things and knows inward things through contemplation. ⟦s16⟧ In which sleep, with Jacob, she sees the angels ascending and descending [n: (Gen. XXVIII)], ascending to the contemplation of God, descending to the visitation of man. ⟦s17⟧ She contemplates in the angel this alternation of the vision of God and of the visitation of man, how they both cleave to God and condescend to us, and carry through our cause with God. ⟦s18⟧ She contemplates and considers also the souls now reigning with Christ, namely what affection they have toward us, and how they desire to have us as partners [0427A] with them, and bestow upon us their help and intercession, and bear solicitude for us. ⟦s19⟧ For through these are the daughters of Jerusalem adjured, that they stir not up the beloved; because, just as they cleave to the love of the Creator and yet bear and carry through the care of man's salvation, so the perfect soul both rests in the love of God and attends to the salvation of her neighbors. ⟦s20⟧ She is also adjured through these; because with these and among these she runs to and fro for those to be saved, and prays not only to God, but also to these, and implores their help. ⟦s21⟧ To these she announces man's necessity, with these she labors in this business. ⟦s22⟧ Well therefore is she adjured through these, inasmuch as they are privy to and witnesses of her charity, and of her industry, and of her necessary occupation about her neighbors. ⟦s23⟧ For these are the roes and the harts of the fields (namely the angelic creation, and men) [0427B] whose souls have already passed over to the kingdom of heaven. ⟦s24⟧ These roes both seek the heights and look out from afar; because, being lifted up by lofty contemplation, while they behold God, they know in him whatever is done lower down in his creation. ⟦s25⟧ The harts too pass through thorn-brakes, and overcome venom. ⟦s26⟧ So the souls of the saints, leaping over the thorns of sins, and overcoming the venom of the enemy's suggestions and the malice of the world, have already leapt over to the kingdom. ⟦s27⟧ And the field of the roes indeed is the happiness of that life. ⟦s28⟧ But the field of the harts is the spaciousness of this world. ⟦s29⟧ Yet the roes pass over to this field by the condescension of charity, and the harts have leapt over to that one by desire and by faith. ⟦s30⟧ These harts, still dwelling in the body, have leapt over thither, because their feet, that is their affections, were standing in [0427C] the courts of Jerusalem. ⟦s31⟧ But now in soul they have passed over thither, and afterward they are to come thither in body also. ⟦s32⟧ To these the bride too leaps over by contemplation, with these she has carried over with herself the care of her neighbors, with these she pleads their cause inwardly before God. ⟦s33⟧ Rightly therefore does the Bridegroom admonish that she be not stirred up, for the reason that she is well occupied about the salvation of her neighbors.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And the carnal solicitude and trouble is indeed good, but the spiritual occupation and care about many is better and more sublime, inasmuch as it is familiar both to God and to the citizens of heaven, and abiding, and more secure. ⟦s2⟧ The outward occupation indeed, that is servitude, because bodily labor too is performed in it. ⟦s3⟧ Inward solicitude and prayer is a more sublime and worthier office. ⟦s4⟧ The former [0427D] serves, while it renders a ministry; the latter obtains with God by merits and by familiarity. ⟦s4⟧ The Lord says that the harvest is great, and admonishes that he be entreated to send laborers into it. ⟦s5⟧ Which the perfect soul is most of all able to accomplish, who cleaves to God by the familiarity of devotion; she now beseeches God to send laborers into the harvest, now that he grant to this harvest the grace to work usefully. ⟦s5⟧ The active man occupies a part of this harvest by outward ministry, but the contemplative occupies the whole harvest by the help of prayer. ⟦s6⟧ For there are two lives, the active namely and the contemplative, which are designated by Martha and Mary; Martha labors without, Mary prays within. ⟦s7⟧ Tell her, says Martha, that she help me, [n: (Luc. X)]; well does she help her who implores the divine help for her, because in vain would she spend labor, [0428A] plant and water, unless God gave the increase. ⟦s8⟧ Magdalene therefore chose the best part, who exercises the grace of contemplation, and yet is occupied in her sister's part, so that she may be crowned also with the reward of the active life, in which by an equal charity she merits an equal reward. ⟦s9⟧ Martha labors in one place with the body about certain things, Mary in many places by charity about many things. ⟦s10⟧ For in the contemplation and love of God she sees all things, is enlarged to all things, comprehends and embraces all things, so that in comparison with her Martha may be called solicitous about few things. ⟦s11⟧ Greatly therefore is this one thing necessary, and deservedly to be preferred, in which the soul both cleaves to God through love and shows charity and solicitude to all. ⟦s12⟧ Well therefore does the bride watch in mind for her neighbors, although she sleep, that is [0428B] be still, from outward things in the body; nor ought she to be stirred up that she may awake, that is, watch outwardly, who thus watches inwardly by charity and prayer. ⟦s12⟧ And so the bride is well occupied, although she work not outwardly, because she strives at least to obtain for many, nay for all. ⟦s13⟧ The Bridegroom therefore forbids the daughters of Jerusalem (namely, the souls desiring to come to the vision of eternal peace) to stir her up and to draw her away to outward occupation. ⟦s14⟧ And she is for this reason also called beloved by him, because she rests in his love and carries through the salvation of many; but it is forbidden that she be stirred up until she herself will, for the reason that she is so joined to God and so thoroughly taught in the knowledge of his will, that for going out and returning at the divine judgment, a just and necessary cause urging it, she is prepared, and her charity is prudent and ordered, so that [0428C] she obeys the divine will, so that, according to the saying of the wise man, she has mercy on herself, pleasing God, nay, according to Wisdom itself, she first weeps over her own self. ⟦s15⟧ Nor only does she not entangle herself in secular business that she may please God, to whom she has proved herself, but she also withdraws herself from the necessities of her neighbors, by which she knows that she is weakened or entangled away from perfecting; yet she does this not out of unkindness, but out of prudence. ⟦s16⟧ Rightly, so long as the imperfect man has but a little of the oil of charity, whereby he himself only may be anointed, and has not yet deserved to be made solid through the inward gifts of grace, he does not wish to be entangled outwardly, nor let him seethe with worldly cares, nor let it befall her, by the wind of human converse and of mutability, to be dried up from that little which he has received. ⟦s17⟧ For outward occupation quickly changes and casts down the imperfect, because dying flies, [0428D] that is, worldly thoughts, spoil the sweet ointment of grace less firmly grounded in the soul [n: (Eccle. X)]. ⟦s18⟧ He desires therefore to abound first for himself, that so he may be able to pour into others; with this oil he wishes rather to be anointed than to anoint, to pour out of a full vessel rather than to pour away, to flow rather than to flow off. ⟦s19⟧ For he who desires without discretion to profit others, and pours out before the time, remains always poor and empty of grace, and hinders his own progress, and profits others less. ⟦s20⟧ And so the prudent soul does not wish to be called away from the stillness and silence which cultivates her in justice, because the cultivation of justice requires silence, until that cultivation shall have come to fruit and maturity, namely until she shall have perfectly advanced; and until she be clothed with strength from on high she studies to abide in the city, [0429A] that is, within the cloisters of conscience. ⟦s21⟧ For some men affect governing before the time, namely when they are still imperfect, and are drawn rather by carnal appetite than by the instinct of usefulness. ⟦s22⟧ But for the soul to feel this appetite and this will is not for her to will it, because that will is of the flesh, not of the spirit, and not her own but another's, if she herself does not love or follow it, but hates and detests it. ⟦s23⟧ She does not therefore will this, when carnality wills it. ⟦s24⟧ But when, carnality being mortified, she shall have willed only spiritually to profit her neighbors, then at last she is judged to will this; for this will is her own, because she consents to it. ⟦s25⟧ But the carnal will is not her own; because, while she strives against it, it is not she herself that works this, but the sin that dwells in her. ⟦s26⟧ Until therefore she shall have come to that state, that she will this spiritually [0429B] and not carnally, she ought not to be stirred up, nor taken up for the governing of others. ⟦s27⟧ But although the beloved shall have come to this spiritual will, Christ nevertheless admonishes that she be not stirred up until she will, because he knows her to be such that she will will it when it shall be opportune.
