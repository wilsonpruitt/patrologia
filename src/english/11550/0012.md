## CHAPTER X. *On the rest above, and how the perfect desire it.*

*Behold the little bed of Solomon: threescore valiant men go about it, of the most valiant of Israel [n: (Cant. III)]*. Solomon is interpreted *the peaceable one*. And he signifies Christ, who made peace with the things that are in heaven and on earth, who reconciled sinners to the Father, and reformed concord between angels and men, and joined converted sinners to the just. And those whom he here joins in concord, he also joins to himself in the kingdom [0434C] in perpetual charity and peace, and leads them to perpetual rest. Which rest is called a little bed, because in it there is a pausing from all the unquiet and labor of this life; in that rest they pause who here have labored for God: *For there,* as is read in Job, *they that were wearied in strength rest, and they that were once bound, without any trouble [n: (Job III)]* [var: Vulg. has the perfect *requieverunt*, "have rested," and *pariter sine molestia*, "alike without trouble"]. There the eternal rest refreshes and rewards those whom here the labor of the commandments and the advance of the virtues wearied; and those who here were bound with the bonds of human infirmity, there, the bonds being burst, sacrifice to God the victim of praise. These bonds are hunger, thirst, sleep, weariness, and the rest of this kind; all of which will cease there, because they shall not hunger, neither shall they thirst any more, nor will there be labor there, nor sorrow, nor shall this material sun fall upon them to burn them, [0434D] nor yet the heat of temptation: for then already the Spirit says to them that they may rest from their labors and from their temptations. The Spirit, who strengthened them to obey the commandments, then says that they may rest from labor. For those who have entered into that rest have rested from their works and from their labors, as God also from his own, wherein he labored for man and redeemed him. From these labors he rested in himself; for these labors his members rest in him. None ever labored as he did, because there was no sorrow like his sorrow. And according to the multitude of his sorrows the eternal consolations have gladdened his members, because his great sorrows conferred great joys upon his members, his great labor perpetual rest, and his bitter death the sweetness of everlasting life. For these things [0435A] they love him incomparably, and rejoice in his love: for they have him whom they love, they know him and see him, all of which is eternal life. For it is the same thing to rest with him, and to rejoice with him, and to have him; likewise to love is to know, because love is knowledge, and to know is to see. And this is eternal life, to know the Father, the true God, and Jesus Christ whom he sent: this is that beatitude, and these are the dowries of the soul, the knowledge of God, love, and enjoyment. Who could suffice to think upon that mutual love of the Savior and of the saved, and how he loves those whom he redeemed at so precious a price, how he rejoices together with them at their salvation, how they themselves love him, and rest in his love, and rejoice in him through whom they know and always see that they have been redeemed and carried over to rest? And although [0435B] they love him with an eternal charity for their redemption, they are loved by him incomparably more. So greatly did he love his own in the passion, and so great was the fervor of charity in him for redeeming them, that he could have taken away the bitterness of his passion altogether, but he would not, and rather took upon himself a more bitter pain of martyrdom, and would suffer more than no man ever suffered or could suffer, that he might show forth an incomparable charity. Which charity toward their salvation was so great that, when he had redeemed them at the price of his own death, he calls them to mind not so much as redeemed by himself as given to him by the Father, saying in a certain place: *Father, those whom thou hast given me, I will that where I am, there also they be with me [n: (Joan. XVII)]* [var: Vulg. *illi sint mecum*, "they may be with me"]. So greatly does he exult over their redemption that he would not reckon great that which he bore for them, [0435C] but he seems to have those whom he received as given to him rather in the way of a gift than as bought at a price or by recompense. He, therefore, who so loved them in exile, will love them incomparably also in the kingdom; he had also a greater charity toward these than toward his own body, in dying for them; and there also he will have a greater charity toward them than they can have toward him, and he will rejoice more over their salvation than they themselves can rejoice in it. And so he will rejoice over their salvation, and he himself will be their joy; him they will taste, him they will have in memory, him in desire; in these three the soul consists, and these three the Son of God will perfectly fill, who also is the Wisdom of God. For these are the streets of Jerusalem, that is, the broad ways [0435D] of the citizens of Jerusalem, who see the peace of God; these streets are reason, memory, will; and they are broad, because many things flow into these streets, and enter in and go out. For how many things does reason comprehend, how many memory, how many the will? But these streets shall be paved, that is, made level, that is, perfectly filled with pure gold, that is, with divine wisdom and with the enjoyment of God. For God will occupy each of them, will gladden, beatify and perfectly satisfy them. For whatever reason will be able to taste, whatever memory to recall, whatever the will to desire, God will be, and he will fill each of these, because he will be all things in all; and there will be sung to him a canticle of gladness for the happiness of eternal beatitude. There, while the just without end behold the torments of the damned, they grow in the praises of God; and because they discern both in themselves [0436A] the good with which they have been rewarded, and in those others the punishment which they have escaped, there will be thanksgiving and the voice of praise. They will give thanks to God their deliverer, and will love him with perpetual charity, in whom also they will unceasingly look upon the signs of their deliverance, namely the scars of his wounds. And the more they shall remember the perils and the misery which they escaped, the more, exulting, will they say: Our soul hath passed through the torrent; perhaps our soul had passed through the water intolerable. But, praising God, they will add: Blessed be the Lord, who hath not given us to be a prey to their teeth, namely the demons', from whose snare, the snare of hunters, their souls have been snatched away; and the snare is broken, because the temptation has ceased; and they themselves are set free, but the enemies are bound fast with the chains of eternal damnation. [0436B] Whom also they will always see in hell, so that, their torments being beheld, they may always rejoice, and love their deliverer, and give thanks to him: whence the Lord speaks to Job, that the elect receive the devil as an everlasting reward [n: (Job XL)]; and because here he is occupied with them while by tempting he afflicts them, and there while they shall rejoice at the sight of his punishment which they escaped, for all these things there will be thanksgiving, and there will be the voice of praise, because him whom they will love they will praise perpetually. For Jerusalem will praise, that is, *the vision of peace,* that is, all who see that peace will praise the Lord, who is their peace and exultation and eternal beatitude; and in his own peace and concord, and in charity, he so unites and joins his own in the kingdom, that whatever of happiness and of joy one of them shall have, this becomes also, through charity, [0436C] another's, while this one wishes for that one what he himself has, and that one loves this one's good in him as his own in himself. As many, then, as are the elect who will reign with Christ, so many will the rewards be there, and of so many will be the joys of each one who shall be saved. If any man could reckon the number of the citizens above, he would also be able to reckon the joys of each one, because so many are the joys of the elect.
