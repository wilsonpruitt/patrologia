## CHAPTER X. *On the rest above, and how the perfect desire it.* (cont.)

It must now be seen how poured out they are, and how long-lasting. Let us compare the joy of this life with the joy of our homeland. If we double it, if we treble it, however much number can be multiplied by number, we shall not reach the equality of the joy of the blessed; and because here there is a little fire, there we sing [cj: *canimus*; read *caminus*, "a furnace"], as the authority says: The Lord liveth, whose fire is in Sion and his furnace [0436D] in Jerusalem [var: Vulg. *dicit Dominus*, "saith the Lord"]. But neither can we reach its term, because it is infinite, in immensity as in eternity, so far as concerns the comprehension of the present life; so that by no comparison, by no reckoning can we attain to the greatness of this joy or to its long duration. It has been said how manifold this joy is, how great, how lasting; it must now be seen what it is, or in what it consists. Whence it must be known that it is in God and is God: for neither could it be called true and perfect if it were not God, or if it were outside God. And because nothing is true, nothing perfect outside God, nor could anything besides God suffice for the creature made to the likeness of God, or perfectly fill her, this is that full joy which in the Gospel the Lord admonishes us to ask and to seek [0437A] [n: (Joan. XVI)], and which he declares that those who ask shall receive. And this is that which eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither hath it entered into the heart of man, which God hath prepared for his own; the small and the great are there, and the servant and the free man from his master [var: Vulg. Job 3:19, *servus liber a domino suo*, "the servant free from his master"]. Those who here served for God's sake have passed from labor to rest, from servitude to liberty, now free from the fear of any punishment or separation, and they possess the fear of reverence and of love. For they enjoy the liberty of the sons of God, with which liberty the Son of God set them free, that they might be truly free. Christ served that servants might be given over to liberty; he took the form of a servant that those might be reformed who had been deformed through sin, and deprived of the likeness of God. He took flesh from us, he gave us his Spirit, and thus, while he took upon himself the servitude of our infirmity [0437B] and conferred the liberty of his own Spirit, he restored us to liberty: for where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty; in this Spirit we call and receive his Father as our Father also; in the soul, then, he has reformed us to his own likeness, in the body he will reform us to the brightness of his own body. This is the liberty of the elect, this their joy; of this liberty they glory, of this glory they rejoice, namely that they enjoy the glory of the sons of God, that they are the sons of God, and therefore heirs, and that they have God as Father and Christ as brother; that they are heirs of God and coheirs of Christ, and that they possess the inheritance of the heavenly Father together with Christ their brother, in which they have him, the firstborn, as partner and fellow. He is also [0437C] the firstborn among many brethren; these Christ has as brethren and coheirs in his own liberty and glory, these he has with him as companions at table. For they shall eat and drink at his table in his kingdom; and the rest of this table, as is read in Job, is full of fatness [n: (Job LVI)]. The rest of this table is the refreshment of that glory which is full of fatness, because it perfectly satisfies, as the Prophet says: *I shall be satisfied when thy glory shall be made manifest [n: (Psal. XVI)]* [var: Vulg. *apparuerit*, "shall appear"]. With that glory we shall be satisfied, and at the same time we shall desire it, and desire will have no failing, nor satiety any loathing, because satiety will accompany desire, and desire satiety: for its immensity will supply the satiety, and the taste of its sweetness the desire. Always, then, will it satisfy, and always will it delight: this is that refreshment, this is [0437D] the rest of that table, full of fatness. For we shall be filled with the fat of the wheat that fell into the earth, that is, with the divinity of our Savior, who took upon himself the failing of our mortality, that he might refresh us with his divinity. Of the torrent, that is, of his transitory and troubled passion, he drank in the way of this world, that he might give us to drink of the torrent of his pleasure in our homeland. He drank the cup of the passion, and prepared for us the fountain of the water of eternal life: these are the delights, that refreshment, that abundance of Jerusalem, that rest and the fatness of that table. That is the torrent of pleasure with which the elect are made drunk, so that they forget, and are without share in all misery, and rejoice without end: this is the beatitude, the rest and the little bed of our peaceable one, where he feeds his own with these delights, and where he lies down at midday. Here the bride has a little bed with [0438A] the Bridegroom in the night, that is, in the blindness of this life; but there at midday, that is, in full charity and knowledge. Here she knows only in part, there she will know as it is and perfectly; there she sleeps and rests in peace without any disturbance, and in the selfsame, that is, in his peace, she is still, who is peace and joy which is the selfsame. And because he is always the same, and is in the same manner, and is never changed, nor is brought to an end, because he has neither change nor shadow of alteration, that fullness and those delights the devout and the valiant, who manfully sweat in the fulfilling of the commandments, circumscribe by thinking and go about by considering. These are designated by the number sixty, which is completed out of ten and six; for by ten the Decalogue, that is, the commandments, is signified; by six the labor which is expended in [0438B] fulfilling those same commandments. These valiant men, then, go about Solomon's little bed with desire; they do not climb into it, or sleep in it: for they can only think something concerning that rest and search it out, but not comprehend it perfectly and as it is. However much they circumscribe of it by thinking, or conjecture concerning its equality and happiness, this is to go around, not to enter into perfect knowledge. Whence, because what they can know of that rest is very little, it is expressed by a diminutive, and it is called not so much a bed as a little bed. They are not able in like manner to go about this bed who still groan for past sins, and who have need to wash with the tears of penitence the bed of sadness and of a sick conscience: for these [0438C] have not a still bed but a troubled one, while conscience troubles them within, and the sad memory of their sins; nor can they so much go about and desire the rewards above as still fear the torments. But when through true penitence they have been cleansed from the filth of their sins, and after a long struggle set free from the passions of the vices, and strengthened through grace, and have passed over from fear and hope to perfect charity, then with enlightened eyes they are able to ascend and to contemplate the things above. Accordingly the valiant, nay rather those who shall be of the most valiant of Israel, that is, the more devout and spiritual, have it to go about Solomon's bed, and are able to accomplish in work whatever they understand in Scripture. But those who fulfill the commandments sluggishly, and live softly and dissolutely, are not [0438D] able to climb to the consideration of this rest, in whom carnal and worldly desires are still strong, namely the appetite of gluttony, the fury of anger, the heat of avarice, the burning of lust, the spite of impatience or of envy. For these are the weaker in God, the less perfectly these things have failed in them. But when these things have been weakened in them by long zeal and labor, and strengthened through spiritual desires, and then, the more they have pressed these down beneath themselves, so much the more are they strong and powerful above, and can go about this little bed. The most valiant of Israel, then, that is, of those who contemplate and seek God with the mind, and whose spiritual desires shall have been the more fervent, go about this little bed. For, kindled with vehement desires, they go round about on every side and seek an entrance, [0439A] that they may both taste this rest and in some measure enter into it. This, then, is their going about, and the course of desire around this bed.
