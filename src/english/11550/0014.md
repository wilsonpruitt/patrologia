## CHAPTER XI. *On caution in fighting against the enemy, and on the remedy against blindness of heart.*

*All holding swords, and most skilled in wars [n: (Cant. III)]*. The valiant men who go about Solomon's bed now consider the things above and stretch toward them, now below wage war against the adversary of the human race. That they may be able to fight against him, they first take up, according to the Apostle, the armor of God, that is, the virtues; and since they are armed with these and have them all, they take care not to be negligent in any one of them. For they know that if they offend in one, they are made guilty of all, [0439B] and will have none at all, and so will appear to the enemy not armed but naked. For he who lacks any virtue has the vice contrary to it, and so is outside charity, because virtue and vice cannot exist together. And whoever is outside charity will not be able to have strength against the adversary. Then they fortify themselves with justice, and arm themselves as with a breastplate, covered about with which they remain impenetrable against the darts of the enemy. For he who renders to God and to his neighbor what he owes is fortified on every side, and the enemy finds nothing in him to strike. They have also faith in the things that are not seen, as a shield, which is set first against the enemy, although they attack it first; and the enemy strike and assail this the more, the more spiritually they storm them; for while perfect men believe and [0439C] love things future and eternal, they judge and despise things earthly and transitory. Whence, when there is temptation concerning those things which they do not love, they easily conquer the tempting adversary; for in vain is the net cast before the eyes of them that have wings. For they have wings, and are lifted up by two wings, that their manner of life may be in the heavens, and that they may seek those things and rise above earthly ones, namely by meditation and by contemplation. They take up also the hope of salvation as a helmet; for in this they rejoice so much that they not only bear labors willingly, but are delighted in them, and reckon themselves blessed through them, as it is written: *For thou shalt eat the labors of thy hands [n: (Psal. CXXVII)]*, thou art blessed. For truly blessed is he who both believes and loves things to come, and expends works for which he may look [0439D] and hope for them, and is delighted and rejoices in the labors which he performs for their sake. Fortified with these arms, the soldiers of Christ can fight securely and manfully. For while, enlightened through grace, they believe things to come, and perform works for which they may hope for them, they have faith and hope; they have also the virtues and justice. Thus armed, they take up the word of God as a sword, with which both to protect themselves and to strike the enemy: for the word of God is living and effectual, and like a penetrating sword, discerning the thoughts of the evil one. The valiant men of our Solomon, then, all hold swords, because they embrace Holy Scripture and retain it in memory, and cleave to it with the mind, in which they have divers instructions whereby they may know and overcome the ambushes of the crafty enemy. For the reading of Scripture and meditation, [0440A] and the devout chanting of psalmody, strengthen the mind, render the enemy weak, and empty out his strength. And so, while they hold it in memory and fulfill it in work, and through it become most skilled for contending with the enemy, because through the understanding of Scripture they are instructed, and through working they are enlightened, as the Psalmist says: *In the understandings of his hands,* that is, through working, *he brought them back [n: (Psal. LXXVII)]* [var: Vulg. *in intellectu manuum suarum deduxit eos*, "in the understanding of his hands he led them"]. Their first caution is this, that, being about to fight with the enemy, they take up this humility, and do not trust that they can contend by their own strength, but, reckoning themselves weak, commit themselves wholly to the divine protection. For they become strong in this conflict only so far as, for contending with the enemy, they trust not in themselves but in God. For with the enemy of the human race no man [0440B] could contend on equal terms, their strengths being compared the one with the other; whose fortitude is so great that no human strength can be compared to him, because of the many arts of harming which he has, which no man suffices to know.

With divine strength, therefore, must we engage with these, which strength the knowledge of one's own infirmity and humility properly deserves; and humility alone, as is read in the Lives of the Fathers, can escape these snares. So Jonathan, hemmed in by a multitude of enemies, when he knew that he could not resist, fled to divine help and came forth victor [n: (I Reg. XIV)]. So Josaphat, set in a like peril, when he professed himself weak before himself, turned himself wholly to the calling upon God, and prevailed against his enemies [n: (II Par. XVII)]. Each of these triumphed more by humility and prayer than by strength; but with humility [0440C] and prayer the Lord's passion is to be remembered and to be sounded forth with faith. In it the soldiers of Christ receive strength against the enemy; for there the fortitude of God was made weak, that we might be strengthened. There the enemy came forth strong, that he might be made weak; there he prevailed, that he might be cast down. So much, then, are the members of Christ strong in this battle, as they have been able to place their faith in it. In this firmness of faith, on this stone of help, Moses stood, when, his people fighting with the Amalekites, he prayed to the Lord [n: (Exod. XVII)]. And though his hands were heavy before, so great a fortitude thereafter came forth in them that they were not slackened until the enemies were overcome. But his hands in praying were held up by Aaron, who is interpreted *the mountain of fortitude,* [0440D] and by Hur, who is called *fire:* for prayer must be pressed manfully and with fervor, which fervor is understood by this fire. Prayer lifted up by these two, namely by constancy and by devotion, will triumph over the enemy, and will obtain what it asks. Sometimes these soldiers of our peaceable one fight thus with Moses, the throng of good thoughts engaging with the throng of evil or vain ones; sometimes, consolation being withdrawn and they being as it were forsaken, they receive and recover help with Jonathan by praying, and conquer the enemy. For he, forsaken by his companions, with his garments rent, put earth upon his head and prayed; he received back his companions, he slew the enemy. So the soldiers of Christ, in the anguish of temptation, when good thoughts withdraw and are troubled, rend their garments and put earth [0441A] upon their head, while they reckon the virtues in themselves rent and imperfect. And because they are earth and ashes, they turn this over in mind, and by praying and by humbling themselves they recover their spiritual thoughts, those they had before, and overcome the enemy. Of Judas Maccabeus too we read that, having once engaged with the enemy, he was of such constancy and confidence that he crossed over to them before he would permit them to cross over to him [n: (I Machab. III)]. Which, as in him, so in the servants of Christ is a sign of victory: for his enemies held this for a sign, that if Judas should first cross the torrent that lay midway between them, he would come forth victor, and they would not be able to resist him: which was done, and he came forth victor. So it prefigures that the soldiers of Christ will be victors, if, undaunted at obstacles, they are prepared to bear persecutors or adversities [0441B] and constantly pass through them. This Judas, when war pressed on, was strenuous; but in time of peace provident: in war he acted manfully, and when it ceased, or rather was interrupted, he did not grow torpid in idleness, but made arrangement concerning the things to be done; the polluted places (opportunity being taken) he himself cleansed, and renewed the holy things, and rebuilt what had been destroyed. So the soldiers of Christ are taught now to fight bravely with the enemy, and now to cleanse the polluted places of their own mind (which is the house of God), and to build up the virtues, and to renew holy pursuits and pious works. These swords (namely these instructions and examples of Scripture) they hold, and through them they become most skilled for spiritual wars: *And every man's blade upon his thigh, because of fears in the night [n: (Cant. III)]*. In the thigh carnal propagation is noted, [0441C] since by the thigh can be understood carnal motions and the wills of the heart. For from each of these a great propagation is drawn; because as from the flesh divers thoughts are generated, so from the heart many wills proceed.
