## CHAPTER XI. *On wariness in fighting against the enemy, and on the remedy against blindness of heart.* (cont.)

As much as anyone follows these delights or these wills, so much is he blinded in mind, because the things that are sweet to the flesh are hurtful to the spirit, and the things that give carnal delight make the soul bitter. Earthly delight is that sour grape, and whoever eats it, his teeth are set on edge (that is, spiritual understanding is blunted), and according to Jeremiah his teeth are broken to the number; because according to the number of carnal delights the knowledge of spiritual things is broken and scattered. And as much as a man delights in these things, so much is he blinded [0441D] within, and the night of blindness is begotten in the mind. Which night is the more to be feared, in that even without knowing it we sin in many things, and take delight; for human nature is prone to evil, and offends in many things which it does not know; because, as is read in Job, we dwell in houses of clay, and have an earthly foundation, and are consumed as by a moth, which secretly and without sound consumes a garment [n: (Job IV)]: hence it is that we are the more often dipped in the filth of sins and are made abominable, that is, such garments of ours, that is, our bodies, make us abominable. Again, we often sin, and take delight in what we too little know, and we sin without knowing it; and these delights and sins the Lord knows, although we ourselves are ignorant of them; and therefore he permits us to be blinded. Again, he knows [0442A] the works of individuals, and therefore he brings on night, as is read in Job, that is, he permits blindness to come to pass *(Job XXXIV)* [var: Vulg. Job 34:25 *Novit enim opera eorum, et idcirco inducet noctem*, "For he knoweth their works, and therefore he shall bring night"]. For by sin we are blinded, and being blinded we sin the more, because we do not see in how many things we offend: whence these nights are to be feared, and against these fears of the night the sword of the word of God is to be held upon the thigh of delights and pleasures, not to be laid aside or set far off, so that through it sins may be known and cut away: for the word of God illumines, and gives understanding of them. Which things, if [men] guard against them too little, will also be the less known. The mind being little by little blinded, they are strengthened even by habit against a man, so that he cannot easily avoid them. There is also another step, effectual and necessary for knowing and repressing these delights, [0442B] namely the sword of the love of God, and that the soul be wounded with charity for him who was wounded through them, and grieve together with him who bore sorrows for her: for he himself was wounded for our offences, and himself bore our sorrows. For these things, O man, let the sword of love wound thee, and let the sword of sorrow pass through thy soul. This sword is twice sharpened: it wounds with love, and thus opens the understanding and illumines the mind. For love begets knowledge and cuts away sins, that thou mayest beware of offending him whom thou lovest. And so upon the thigh of delights, or of carnal pleasures, the soldiers of Christ have the sword of love because of the fears of the night, that is, because of hidden lapses into sin; because if they do not love, then by reason of blindness and ignorance they offend in many things. And again, [0442C] while they are fervent with love, they turn aside the peril of lukewarmness; because, as love illumines, so lukewarmness blinds. For in a slack mind earthly delights grow, and self-wills grow into a wood; because, according to Solomon, briars and thorns fill the field of the slothful man [n: (Prov. XXIV)] [var: Vulg. Prov. 24:31 *urticae*, "nettles"], which, as much as it has borne fruit, so much do they beget a shady grove of these thickets and darken the mind, so that the slack man understands the less in how many things he offends, attends the less to himself, takes the less hold of himself. This night is to be feared, in which a man daily becomes worse, and yet more secure about himself. But those who have had the sword of divine love will shake off this fear, and this fear of thorns and briars will not come upon them; and for the having of it [0442D] the memory of God's benefits avails most, that thou mayest call to mind and think over again that he has made thee and remade thee, that he has done well to thee. Think upon his goodness, his bounty and his liberality: how good he is in himself, how bountiful toward thee, spending himself for thee, how liberal to thee, heaping his gifts upon thee. Thinking on these things thou wilt love him whom thou shalt consider to be such toward thee. And this sword of love will cut off the evils that sprout up, and will render thee secure from this fear of the night.
