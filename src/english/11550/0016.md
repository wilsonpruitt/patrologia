## CHAPTER XII. *On the virtues and perfection of the soul, on cleanness, desire and discretion.*

*King Solomon made himself a ferculum of the wood of Lebanon [n: (Cant. III)]*. The *ferculum* is the holy soul, who feeds and refreshes. For she refreshes Christ, and refreshes his members [0443A] also. She refreshes Christ, while he takes delight in her good works and zeal and desires, as it is written: My people has sated me with voices, has filled me with sweet odours. For she refreshes his members by example, while by her holy life and manners [each] is edified and amended. She refreshes by prayer, while she obtains for them from God the grace by which they are consoled and edified. She feeds unto salvation by teaching, while she strengthens them by preaching, and instructs them to savour things eternal and wholesome. She feeds by counsel, while she exhorts to good, and by sweet admonition raises up and restores the sorrowful, and kindles and strengthens the good yet more; she feeds by converse, while she converses of what is good, and, so far as she prevails, takes away occasion for useless speech. She brings forward words of edification into the midst, and furnishes charity [0443B] with matter for good discourse and gives her labour to peace; she lends no ear to a detracting tongue, but scatters it with a sad countenance. Thus the holy soul refreshes both Christ and his members. Whence Christ is said to have made this *ferculum* for himself; and the holy soul is also called a *ferculum*, a *ferculum* from bearing, because she is borne from place to place, wherever the will of him who governs shall have directed her, namely of God who disposes all things, that she may refresh many and instruct them unto salvation. This *ferculum* Christ made of the wood of Lebanon. These are the cedars which grow on Mount Lebanon, and they signify the elect, who grow and bear fruit through Christ and in Christ. For Lebanon, which is called *candidatio*, signifies Christ, who came forth of a clean virgin without any stain [0443C] of sin. These woods are fair, tall, incorruptible, sweet-smelling, unharmed by worms, and they signify the saints, who are fair through holy manner of life and through honourable morals; tall, because they stretch upward by desire, and seek the things above; incorruptible, because their charity and holiness so begins here that it is not ended unto eternity, but is perfected in the world to come; sweet-smelling, because they send forth from themselves the odour of good repute, and by holy esteem draw others to good; unharmed by worms they are, because they do not suffer the worms, that is, the demons, to feed upon their inward parts, nor, they themselves being unaware, [do they suffer] strangers to eat up the strength of their mind, because, taught by many trials, they know their hidden suggestions; of these woods Christ made a *ferculum*, that is, a soul filled with all the fulness of grace, [0443D] so much so that she is abundant in herself, and is able to refresh others and to profit them; he makes, I say, such a soul of the wood of Lebanon, that is, of the example of the saints, when in their life and manner of living he shows her a form which she may imitate; when in the same spirit in which he enlightened them he restores her, and leads her through to the step of perfection; when he builds in her the virtues with which they were adorned; when at last, according to their form and likeness, he shapes and composes her.

The pillars of this soul are the four principal virtues, namely prudence, fortitude, temperance, justice: well is that soul supported which is propped by these, so that she be prudent to know the things that pertain to salvation; strong to accomplish what she has known; temperate, that she may beware of evils, and may do good things [0444A] discreetly, in place and in time. And when she has been such, she will be just, and will hold justice in the fourth place. These pillars are of silver, because they make the soul that is sustained by these virtues splendid both within before God and without before men, and set forth from her a fair appearance. He made the reclining-place of gold. The reclining-place is a pure conscience, in which Christ, the wisdom of God, reclines himself and rests; which is golden, because it is baked out by the fire of tribulation, and purged as gold and proved. For she who is pleasing through innocence of life is made more pleasing and cleaner through the bearing of adversities, and worthy to be God's golden reclining-place. The Jews put wood into his bread, who is the bread of angels and of men; they set wood for him to be racked upon, they did not put wood under him [0444B] to recline upon. This bread was baked out by the passion, and made firm. Suffer with him, holy soul, so that thou mayest be purged and made firm by the fire of tribulation; be not consumed as lead, but be made cleaner as gold. If, scorched by tribulation, thou shalt not have failed, but shalt have profited through patience, thou shalt be gold and God's reclining-place. And he will recline in thee, who had no reclining-place on the cross, and will make a golden one in thee, who himself had none of wood. For this cause he endured the passion, and had no reclining-place: that by his example and love we may suffer, and that through the bearing of adversities we may be so cleansed that he himself may recline and rest in us in this world, and we may rest with him in the world to come. Think upon this charity of his, soul, and bend thyself wholly to his love and good pleasure, that thou mayest spend [0444C] thyself wholly upon him, and bear steadfastly and with a devout mind whatever things shall have been brought upon thee for his sake, that thou mayest in some measure answer to his benefits and become his reclining-place. Therefore, that thou mayest be a golden reclining-place, study to have also the gold of spiritual wisdom; let not thy wisdom be muddy, but golden; not earthly, sensual, devilish, but spiritual and divine. Of this spiritual wisdom the deep of human wisdom says: It is not in me; and the sea, that is, the bitterness of vices, says: It is not with me. This carnal wisdom therefore is to be cast away, and to be exchanged for this spiritual one; and so gold is to be bought, clean, fire-tried, proved, that we may be made rich, and may possess this gold. After spiritual wisdom and the amendment [0444D] of morals, one must come to this virtue of patience, that we may willingly, nay cheerfully, bear whatever adversities, and rejoice in them for Christ's sake. Thus by degrees and little by little Christ advances and cleanses the soul, as much by his own grace as by the example of his gifts, that she may become gold, nay his own golden reclining-place. He makes in her also a purple ascent. He makes an ascent in the soul, when by his grace he makes her desire the things above. But this ascent is purple and [sic: *rebeus*]: for such a soul suffers at times revilings and detractions from the wicked, who envy her devotion, for the reason that they themselves neither love the good nor will to do it. And when she hears the revilings of the wicked, she receives from them a certain disturbance of mind and a redness of confusion. Now purple is so red, [0445A] that yet that redness appears scattered and varied: so in the holy and devout soul that redness is varied; because whence she reddens from the hurling of revilings, thence she reddens with charity, wherewith she also burns toward those who revile. Again she reddens from the desire wherewith she is kindled toward things above and eternal: these rednesses are various, and all precious, so that they deservedly adorn the devout soul, and ought to be compared to purple. The middle parts of this soul are paved with charity. These middle parts are works of the class of good things, namely common works, which can be done with both a good and a bad mind, such as are watchings, fastings, prayers and almsgivings, and the rest. These ought to be paved with charity, namely that they be done in charity and with a good intention. And again, that they be so done with brotherly charity that the weak be not [0445B] scandalized or weakened in them, but that these be level (that is, made with such evenness and discretion) that the soul may walk in all of them without complaint, and may know how to practise them opportunely and again to leave them off: well will these middle parts be paved with charity, if they shall have been so ordered. And so Solomon now makes himself a *ferculum* of the wood of Lebanon, and pillars of silver, and a reclining-place of gold, a purple ascent, and paves the middle parts with charity, when he enriches the soul with the fulness of grace and with spiritual banquets, and shapes her from the examples and teaching of the perfect, that she may be able to edify others. When he adorns her with the beauty of virtues and strengthens her with perfection, and when he makes her so clean that he may rest in her and have a reclining-place, and so lofty and precious that through desire of the things above [0445C] and charity toward her neighbours and the bearing of evils she may be compared to purple, and so discreet and companionable in her outward manner of life that she may please the divine gaze, and not offend the sight of her neighbours.
