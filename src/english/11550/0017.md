## CHAPTER XIII. *On the manner of making progress, and on the example of Christ, and on the reward.*

*Go forth, and see, daughters of Sion, king Solomon in the diadem wherewith his mother crowned him, in the day of solemnity and of gladness [n: (Cant. III)]* [var: Vulg. Cant. 3:11 closes *desponsationis illius, et laetitiae cordis ejus*, "of his espousals, and of the gladness of his heart"]. It is an exhortation to faithful and devout souls, that they consider and contemplate in mind the beauty and glory of Jesus Christ, which he possesses in heaven [0445D] through the punishment of his martyrdom. These souls are the daughters of Sion, that is, of that city above, who behold God, which is our mother: these have been spiritually born again and regenerated in Christ, to this end, that they may be daughters and heirs of that vision and beatitude: to these it is said that by faith and desire they go forth from earthly things to consider the things above, and to contemplate the diadem of our Solomon, that is, of Christ, who redeemed us and made peace for us. O daughters of Sion, go forth from carnal concupiscence, and from vicious habit, and from blindness of heart, and see, that is, contemplate spiritually, the glory of our king, the felicity of that gladness above, that you may be eager in entering, drawn by that sweetness and delight, so that you may so walk as to go forth from your own selves. For some walk, [0446A] but do not go forth; because now they go forward, now they go backward, now they walk slackly, now crookedly. For sometimes some press on in good works, and also commit evils. For through a certain sloth and blindness of mind they beware of evils too little, while they are zealous to do well, not attending to the fact that good things mingled with evils do not please God. Again, some are too little strong for anything laborious, and from faintheartedness of spirit as well as of body they endure a burden; others think nothing harder to bear than that they can resist their own pleasures, and they take delight in temptations or in concupiscences, resisting their own passions and motions too little; who, as much as they suffer themselves to be overcome in the least things, so much do they become weak against sins and vices or temptations, and insufficient for walking [0446B] and for going forward. Others go forward the less, because they do not walk rightly and discreetly. In certain good works only, and those less useful, they exercise themselves the more, and in others more fitting for their own advancement they neglect themselves; they press on in labors of the body, and neglect the virtues of the soul and godliness, whereas bodily exercise avails to little, and godliness is more necessary, because it avails to all things. Again, because they have not keeping of the heart and circumspection, through this negligence they offend in many things. And again, others are very solicitous about certain small and light matters, and the greater ones they neither beware of nor know, who, swallowing a camel, strain out a gnat. But all these, although they walk, yet remain within themselves; because they do not leave their former manners and their ingrained [0446C] imperfection and torpor, so as to go forth, with all things amended and the eyes of the heart cleansed, to see God, and Solomon crowned with the diadem. First of all, therefore, it is necessary that we so do good that we turn aside from evil; because he does not go forward well who now walks, now falls down. Again, it behooves us to strengthen the weak knees, and to beware of excessive faintheartedness and infirmity; otherwise we shall not go forth from our own selves so as to attain to this vision. Again, we ought to make straight steps for our feet, that we may know the things by which we can most make progress, and may direct thither the steps of the mind; that is, that we press on to correcting ourselves in all things, to cleansing the heart, to increasing charity. If [0446D] we so walk, we shall assuredly go forth from our own selves, and shall be able to see king Solomon, the eyes of the heart being cleansed from former affections and from blindness. For then we shall be able to see him, that is, to contemplate him, through loftiness of mind, shining in the diadem, that is, the aureole, wherewith in this life his mother Judaea crowned him by inflicting martyrdom upon him, through which she fashioned for him such a crown and glory. For because she crowned him with martyrdom in the time of his passion, he himself was crowned with such a diadem in the day of solemnity and of gladness of his glorious Ascension: for indeed a great solemnity it was, when the citizens above ran to meet their king, and the Lord, the Redeemer of men, coming with triumph; a great gladness indeed, both of the Redeemer himself and of those same citizens, for the redeemed: whence [0447A] it is said of him that he caused his citizens to be called together, that they might congratulate him and rejoice together with him over the man found and redeemed, whom he had lost: this is the day of solemnity and of gladness, or of the espousals and of the gladness of his heart. For then he espoused to himself the bride alone, namely the Church of the angels above; then he entered upon those nuptials, and joined himself to her, from which he will return to those nuptials of men at the end of the world, when, namely, he will join this bride to that bride: *In the day of his espousals,* he says, *and of the gladness of his heart.* How great a gladness of heart the Son of God received in that espousal, that is, the joy of his humanity, namely according as he is man, as much as pertains to the glory of the Redeemer, speech does not express, understanding does not grasp. For as many as his redeemed shall be, of so many [0447B] will his gladness be. [sic: *Ex*] As much as each one will rejoice over his own salvation, so much and a greater joy will he have over each one of them. O daughters of Sion, consider this glory and gladness of our king, set this example before you! When you suffer adversities, go forth from your imperfection and impatience, that you may lightly bear the evils brought upon you; see your king, who suffered for you, and through his passion was crowned with glory and honor. And when you suffer anything for godliness' sake, think over again what manner of persecution and contradiction he endured from the ungodly for you. For if God suffered for man, how much more ought man to suffer for himself? Scanty and brief is that which you can suffer, immense that which you await through it. The momentary and light of our tribulation works in us also an eternal [0447C] weight of glory. Think upon the eternal, and you bear the momentary lightly; think upon the weight, that is, the greatness of the glory, and light becomes the tribulation which such a recompense accompanies; while you suffer, gaze upon the crown: for then there is made for you the diadem wherewith you may be crowned in the day of solemnity, which will be concerning you when you go forth out of the body, or of that espousal, when from the nuptials of the Church militant you shall pass over to the nuptials of the Church triumphant. Therefore, O daughters of Sion, prudent virgins, trim your lamps, prepare for yourselves the oil of good works and the light of charity, and so go out to meet the Bridegroom, and the bride! Then you shall be a crown of glory in the hand of the Lord, and a diadem of the kingdom in the hand of your God.
