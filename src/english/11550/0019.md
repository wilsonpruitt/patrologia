## CHAPTER XV. *How the soul despises earthly things, and knows the things within.*

*Thine eyes are of doves, besides that which lies hid within [n: (Cant. IV)]*. Such a soul has the eyes of doves, because she looks simply upon these earthly things, nor is she snatched toward them by the desire of covetousness, but rather is content with little, does not court what belongs to others, is not carried away by the desire of ambition or of riches, or of any carnal [0450A] delight. The inward sufficiency of the mind makes the outward look pleasing, so that concerning earthly things she has the eyes of doves, just as in those covetous of earthly things the appetite makes the eyes of kites. The care of things does not afflict her, love does not vex her, business does not weary her; she enjoys great stillness and security, while she does not seek these things, does not care for them, does not covet them. For as is read in Job: *Man is born to labor, but the bird to flying [n: (Job V)]* [var: Vulg. Job 5:7 *et avis ad volatum*, "and the bird to fly"]. Because those who are men, that is, human, carnal, that is, taken up with the desire of worldly things, have labor; but the good, while they do not love or desire these things, escape and fly away from their affliction. For the evil this labor is born of covetousness, which is the root of all evils; but the good are born to this flight [0450B] and liberty by the renewing and lifting up of the Holy Spirit. These are they who fly as clouds, and as doves to their windows [n: (Isa. LX)], because they are lifted up to eternal desires, and are carried by no covetousness to the lowest things. Which flight and dove-like look the Holy Spirit works in the soul, who is designated by the dove. This Spirit makes the outward eyes simple, and opens the inward; because he illumines the sense of a man and advances it to understand and comprehend spiritual things and the things above. The eyes therefore of the devout soul are of doves, because her senses have been illumined through the Holy Spirit, and taught, savouring spiritual things. Seeking those things, intent upon them, treading down and despising earthly things. For they savor according to the spirit those things which are wisdom, which is life and peace: this wisdom is life, because it quickens [0450C] the soul; it is peace, because it makes her tranquil, composes, moderates, feeds, gladdens, advances her, makes her blessed. This wisdom is not singular, but also set apart [cj: *sed et segregata*; read *nec segregata*, "nor set apart"], but common; not seeking her own things, but those of others; not crafty, not malevolent, but pure, sweet, lovable, pleasing; not obstinate, but easy to be persuaded; consenting to the good, agreeing with the well-disposed; she lacks the gall of the disturbances and bitternesses which the love of earthly things has in itself. She savors God, while she knows, she is from God, and in God. Whence her eyes, that is, her senses, are called doves', that is, the Holy Spirit, since through him she sees and understands. These doves' eyes, that is, the spiritual senses, this sublimity, this tranquillity of mind is in thee, O soul, besides those things which lie hid [0450D] within. For those things which thou turnest over secretly with Christ in the chamber of the heart, who could set forth outwardly? Who outwardly could understand or express in words that mutual talking together, the boldness of speaking to him, the consolation received from him or the revelation, the unity of minds, the concord of pleasures [cj: *voluptatum*; read *voluntatum*, "of wills"], the mutual love, the zeal for justice and for souls, the desire of the vision and of the enjoyment of God, the weariness of pilgrimage, the longing to be dissolved? Now the sense of such a soul is opened, that she may understand the Scriptures; now she is instructed concerning hidden, or doubtful things, now she is so united to God that she is made one spirit with him; now she is so absorbed by the spirit that she does not recall that she is dwelling in the flesh; now she knows certain secrets of the divine inbreathing which before she did not recognize; but now [0451A] she is so joined to God, and feels God joined to her, and so transfers her whole will into God, that she either understands God's will or bends it to her own. For out of that purity of heart wherewith she agrees with God's will in all things she also understands it, and is worthy to bend it to her own pleasure. Now she so joins God to herself through love and draws him, that she believes she has him as it were alone; now she is so enlarged into him and passes over into him, that in him she embraces every man through charity, and sees all men in the light wherewith she is enlightened, and toward all (because with one and a like charity) she has compassion and condescends. So she directs her intention upon him, that through him she may see and comprehend many as well as few. Such a soul obtains certain things she has asked for on account of the greatness of her faith, certain ones [0451B] on account of her profound humility, certain ones through importunity. Sometimes by devotion, and by the fervor of an immense desire, she constrains God; sometimes she bends him by the composition of words, and by coaxing or careful speech; sometimes, a just cause for mercy having been shown, and by reason, she receives consolation concerning some of her petitions, concerning others a revelation, and now a certain one, now a doubtful one, sometimes she understands that she has been heard, but does not know the manner. So Tobias knew that he had been heard, but concerning the manner it was not revealed to him. For he believed this to be the manner, that he should die, whereas rather it was that he should be enlightened. In certain things she hopes that God will be propitious, in that she feels herself forestalled with consolation in praying, and helped. In certain things to be obtained [0451C] she feels that God must be bent, and beaten upon with long-continued prayers. In certain things she learns by experience that [sic: *cum*] he seeks and desires a man who may set the hope of prayer against his vengeance. But in some things she recognizes that his wrath cannot be resisted, even if it should happen that Moses and Samuel stand in his sight. These and suchlike familiar and secret things of prayer, of meditation and of knowledge lie hid within in the perfect soul in the present. But what things concerning things to come lie hid, they are hidden from the eyes of all living. For it does not yet appear what shall then be; because, when in that life God shall have appeared, she will be like to him, because she will see him as he is. Then she will be perfectly made like to God, to whom now she is made like in part; because, when that which is perfect shall have come, that which is in part shall be done away, and [0451D] then she shall know even as she has been known. Then she shall see how the Father is in the Son, and the Son in the Father, and the Holy Spirit in both: then at last she shall see the holy Trinity in its own glory.
