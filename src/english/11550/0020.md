## CHAPTER XVI. *How holy meditations are obtained from Christ through prayer.*

*Thy hair is as flocks of goats, which have come up from mount Galaad [n: (Cant. III)]*. The hair of the bride is holy meditations, which, as hair grows upon the head, so arise in the mind. And this hair is compared to goats on account of certain likenesses. For goats, in searching out pastures, are wont to seek the heights of mountains and of trees, and they also ruminate. So holy [0452A] meditations always stretch toward the heights, and labor to search out and to know spiritual and heavenly things. And so far as they prevail to grasp them, they are eager to ruminate, that is, to go over them again and to think them over afresh, and to call them back up from the belly of the mind to the memory, and to grind and break these small for savor and for edification. Those trees are good men, growing in grace and lofty. While therefore the devout soul attends to these [sic: *hos a tendit*] and meditates upon their holiness, and strives to raise herself up to their imitation, she climbs these trees by meditation; and while she loves and considers the vigor of their mind, the vigor of their daily advance and of their spiritual renewal, and passes it over alike into memory and into imitation, she plucks from them, as it were, food whereby she may be nourished. For he who attends to those who do well, and is delighted by their virtues, [0452B] by their devotion and their manner of life, is himself through them stirred up and nourished to the good and the perfect. These goats climb mountains also, while the meditations of the soul look upon the lofty life and manners of the perfect, and desire to ascend to these, and join themselves to good men, and seek out their fellowship, their consolation, their converse, their prayer. So these goats climb trees or mountains, so they always seek the heights, and despising the lowest things they seek and search out the things above, and are delighted by the sight of these, are refreshed by feeding upon them, are renewed by desire, make progress, and stretch upward by their example. They always make progress, because they always attend to good men; they are always fresh, always eager. For a man becomes of such a manner of life [0452C] as those he looks upon, as those he attends to, and as those to whom he joins himself more familiarly. These goats, namely holy meditations, come up from mount Galaad. Mount Galaad signifies Christ: for Galaad is interpreted *a heap of testimony,* and the saints are heaped together in Christ, and are gathered, and have the testimony of Jesus, and of faith in him and of his works, living stones indeed, and belonging to the heavenly building. Mount Galaad is read to have had most abundant pastures, and Christ feeds most abundantly, who not only refreshes by example, but also pours in grace. For none of the saints builds up by example as Christ does, because in none was there such humility, patience and obedience, meekness, charity, and perfection of all the virtues. In him also was all the fullness of grace, and that to such a degree that out of this [0452D] fullness all receive, as many as have grace; this is the mountain of God, a fat mountain, and not only fat in itself, but also making others fat out of itself, nourishing some with the milk of grace, refreshing some through the more abundant sweetness of wisdom more abundant therefore are the pastures on this mountain.

But it must be seen how these goats, namely devout meditations, come up from God. The soul lies down, and is in the depths, when she thinks of earthly and carnal things. But she rises, and ascends on high, when she meditates on spiritual things and turns them over in mind. In carnal thoughts, therefore, we are pressed down and cast down by our very selves; to spiritual things we are lifted up by God, because good meditations are from God and rise in the soul, while carnal and evil ones are from us. For there is not in us, [0453A] that is in our flesh, any good, because the flesh is barren, nor does it bring forth thoughts of this kind, but germinates for us thorns and thistles. Out of the heart also come forth evil thoughts, and suggestions are made besides through evil angels. When therefore the soul is pricked with compunction for past sins, or purposes to correct and to keep herself as to present ones; when she is kindled with delight in some virtue or with zeal for making progress, is moved to compassion for her neighbor, to zeal for justice, to the love or the desire of eternal life; when she is affected with some devotion or spiritual sweetness, when she is illumined, instructed, or roused to some good: such meditations and affections are from Christ, who speaks in the soul a thought of peace, and whose Spirit suggests all these things in secret. When [0453B] therefore things of this kind are felt in the mind, then assuredly the goats come up from mount Galaad, that is, holy meditations arise and rise up from Christ. For he who abides in Christ, and Christ in him, this man bears much fruit of these thoughts; and in whom these meditations are gathered and heaped together, he has testimony that Christ abides in him, and from him he has the assent of these meditations. From Christ therefore they are, and from him they come up, who nourishes them in our heart, and makes them rise up together and ascend. Who now suggests them of himself, and now is to be prayed to that he may bestow them: for Christ sleeps at times, so that, his Spirit not speaking but a contrary wind blowing, the little ship of the mind is covered with the waves of temptations [0453C] or of evil thoughts. Whence he must be roused, that he may command these winds and the sea of these disturbances, and make a calm in the mind. And so, when the soul prays and rouses Christ by the insistence of her prayers, spiritual and longed-for thoughts are received. For they come up from Christ roused, and made favorable through prayer. And thou mayest see in what manner, while the soul is insistent in prayers, one after another rises and comes up; and as many motions of praying as there are, so many meditations rise and come up; and when the soul is insistent and multiplies her prayers, these thoughts are multiplied; and at length they are so thickened in the soul that now she is walled about by an immense host of them, she who before seemed alone, and as it were forsaken. Because therefore these meditations are from Christ, and come up in the mind, let the soul come up into this mountain, [0453D] let her abide in Christ, let her call upon Christ. Let her take up the sublime office of praying. Let her go up into the mountain to pray alone, all occupations, that is, being shut out and put away; let the words of Christ abide in her, let her hear his law with the ear of the heart, and keep his commandments, that whatever she shall have willed to ask may be done for her. For he who hears the law, his prayer shall be acceptable; and he who fulfills the commandments shall be heard, and from the commandments fulfilled in work he shall be instructed, because to him who keeps justice God will give wisdom and the acquaintance with spiritual things. And, as Solomon says [n: (Prov. VI)], and according to the Psalmist, from the commandments of God he understands [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]. So from this fat and abundant mountain, the devout soul praying, these goats come up, that is, holy meditations; but by the malignant spirit useless thoughts are suggested and rise up, [0454A] drying up the fatness of devotion, and extinguishing the meditations of good things.

These are that people of the Amalekites. Amalec is interpreted *a people licking:* for they lick and destroy the sweetness of the ointment, they consume the unction of the Holy Spirit. Against these one must fight by prayer with Moses, and the hands must be lifted up together with the heart, that we may pray, and likewise keep the commandments in work; if we so lift up both to God, and are not wearied, we shall overcome [n: (Exod. XVII)]. If we cease, or act more slothfully, Amalec shall overcome, as we read was done there. Sometimes we possess holy meditations in stillness, and without the assault of Amalec; sometimes, when he engages with us, and now prevails, now is overcome, prayer profits, and the hands in praying [0454B] shall have been lifted up more slackly, so that the soul experiences these vicissitudes, so that now she sees herself the higher, now is affected with spiritual delight, now is occupied and pressed by evil thoughts: yet if she does not cease in praying, nor grow weary, there is no doubt but that Amalec is overcome and put to flight. When through our own negligence and the dissolution of the heart we slip away from good thoughts to vain and hurtful ones, and sometimes when the adversary tempts and suggests, it happens at times that, dried up for a long while and destitute of grace and of spiritual thoughts, she does not experience that sweet sweetness; for which she must pray so much the more humbly and insistently, inasmuch as we are ignorant whether we are in any way the cause of this failure. For a man is deprived of grace on account of some hidden sin, or [0454C] on account of ingratitude, namely by working unworthily through it, or by not rendering thanks to the giver, or because he does not seek it as he ought, or because he admits carnal thoughts. When therefore this dryness has come about, and the soul has been made as earth without water, the hands must be spread out to God in prayer; and with Elias, who after the dryness of three years asked for rain [n: (III Reg. XVIII)], one must sit down to prayer, and ask for the rain of grace. He sent the boy again and again, to prove whether his prayers had been heard, and it was not done before the seventh time; so, when the mind has been dried up for a long while from the grace of devotion, one must pray and press on many times over, nor cease from prayer, until the stillness of the mind, which [0454D] is understood through the seven-fold number, and the grace which is sevenfold, may work. For without great insistence and laborious prayer, a mind dried up from grace does not deserve to receive it. But she is not able to obtain that rest by devout prayer, since she is destitute of devotion and of those sweet affections; but it is necessary that she make recompense by labor and importunity. And so, if not for friendship's sake, yet for importunity's sake she shall receive what she asks. But that her prayers may be heard, it is necessary that she cut off and circumcise what is superfluous of speech, of thought, and also of work, lest she be dissolute in thought, speaking vain, superfluous, or hurtful things. Nor let her be without keeping of the heart, admitting indifferently whatever thoughts, and neither discerning concerning them nor being wary for herself; nor let her even be soft [0455A] and dissolute in work, handling and doing not what is expedient, but what delights. For that mount Carmel, upon which Elias poured forth this prayer and received what he asked, notes this keeping and that circumcision. For Carmel is interpreted *the knowledge of circumcision.* Those who have not known how to circumcise and cut off these things in themselves are not worthy to be heard. They are indeed the mountains of Gelboe, upon which neither dew nor rain of grace descends, because upon it was cast away the shield of the mighty, that is, manly keeping and circumcision. For without keeping neither is any virtue granted, nor is fuller grace acquired. So therefore let the circumspect soul see, and cut off what is superfluous and hurtful; let her press on with prayers again and again, and not cease when at first the little cloud of grace comes up, until at length there break forth and follow [0455B] the immensity of the saving rain, and it fully water and pour into the mind which had long been parched with hurtful dryness and with the failure of grace. Hence therefore let her ask for grace and await it, hence let her prepare herself for it, and consider with what caution she may return to holy meditations: which will come about best in this way, that she run back first of all to those most lately had. For these are received the more easily, the more nearly they have been had; but to the more remote, and to those had further back, the running back is more difficult. In this manner the end of prayer is better than the beginning. So from Christ, who is the beginning and the end, and through Christ, the diligent and solicitous soul will obtain all things, and will make in herself the assent of good meditations.
