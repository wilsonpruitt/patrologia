## CHAPTER XXIII. *On the tenderness of the good, and especially of the blessed Virgin.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thy two breasts are as two fawns of a roe, twins, which feed among the lilies, until the day breathe, and the [0473D] shadows decline [n: (Cant. IV)]*. ⟦s2⟧ These are the breasts whom above he had called the neck, teachers and also perfect men, converting others to good by admonition. ⟦s3⟧ These breasts are filled with the milk of charity, with which they refresh the younger and more tender, and as mothers they suckle and nourish them. ⟦s4⟧ The soul as yet carnal does not have these breasts, until she grow in grace, and turn the blood of carnality into the milk of devotion. ⟦s5⟧ But when she shall have grown in grace, and conceived tenderness, then she becomes a mother, she suckles the tender, she consoles the desolate; struck with insults or afflicted with injuries she pours out the milk of patience and of compassion, not the blood of carnal agitation. ⟦s6⟧ For because she is filled with the milk of tenderness, she shows forth the sweetness which she received, she has compassion on the miseries of the one hurting her more than she is moved by the injury. ⟦s7⟧ In him who does the injury there is wickedness [0474A] of heart, and there is misery of soul. ⟦s8⟧ He also who endures has in himself that whereby he may be moved either to anger or to clemency. ⟦s9⟧ If he is indignant rather than compassionate, he is carnal, and himself also liable to the misery of sin; because as the one sins through wickedness, so does this one through anger and impatience. ⟦s10⟧ Blood touches blood, that is, sinner touches sinner. ⟦s11⟧ The touch proves what each one is. ⟦s12⟧ If the one touched, that is, struck by injury, had breasts, he would pour out milk. ⟦s13⟧ But when he suffers injury and does not suffer with it; he grieves and does not grieve with another, because he does not pour forth the milk of tenderness, he proves that he does not have breasts, nor is a mother, but a sister still little and not having breasts. ⟦s14⟧ Because therefore the perfect soul receives from above the grace which she pours back upon others, and thus she herself also is suckled, she is deservedly compared to fawns, which run to the milk [0474B] and seek the heights. ⟦s15⟧ For she rises from that whereby she herself may grow and may refresh others. ⟦s16⟧ The two fawns are the active and the contemplative, who assuredly do not cease to run to the milk of grace, this they desire, and they always tend toward the perfect, and seek the things above. ⟦s17⟧ They always reckon themselves new, always beginners and insufficient, because they stretch themselves to the things before, until they meet in the perfect man, until indeed they arrive at perfection; never do they judge that they have laid hold, but with themselves they say that they have only begun, with themselves they are always little ones, that is, humble, and therefore they seek the milk of grace, to this they tend, this they suck: whence, because they run unceasingly to this milk, they receive it, and by it are refreshed, and are so filled with its sweetness that its abundance suffices to supply the want of others. ⟦s18⟧ These are [0474C] the fawns of the roe, that is, of the Church, which is accustomed to seek the heights, and they are twins, because both are refreshed by the same doctrine, as twins are nourished by the same milk. ⟦s19⟧ For Holy Scripture instructs the contemplative just as it does the active, and therefore both find that whereby they may be spiritually nourished. ⟦s20⟧ But these feed among the lilies. ⟦s21⟧ By the lilies, which shine and have fragrance [sic: *odotem*], we understand the cleanness of those who live well and the fragrance of the virtues. ⟦s22⟧ Among which these twins feed, because they are delighted in the cleanness of the good and in the good fragrance of those who so live. ⟦s23⟧ In their salvation, and equally in the fragrance of good example, they rejoice, and in what they afford to others they are glad, among these things they are delightfully refreshed. ⟦s24⟧ For at the destruction of the evil they are tormented, but at the progress and prosperity of the good they rejoice together, as long as they dwell in the [0474D] exile and blindness of this world. ⟦s25⟧ And this is what follows, until the day breathe, and the shadows decline. ⟦s26⟧ Until the ignorance and misery of the present life fail, and the day of eternity and of divine knowledge shine. ⟦s27⟧ But these breasts can be transferred to the blessed Virgin. ⟦s28⟧ For she has two breasts pouring out the milk of a twofold love, because she obtains pardon for the guilty and grace for the just. ⟦s29⟧ Which breasts are as two fawns of a roe; these fawns we can understand to be the angels and the souls of men already translated to the kingdom. ⟦s30⟧ Who are well called fawns, because they have climbed the mountain of the heavenly habitation, and are refreshed with the sweetness of divine contemplation, as fawns are accustomed both to seek the heights and to be nourished with milk. ⟦s31⟧ These twin fawns are of the roe, that is, of Christ, from whom alike they take and suck the milk of a twofold [0475A] sweetness, namely of divinity and of humanity. ⟦s32⟧ For the roe is Christ, because he leaped upon the mountains and sprang over the hills. ⟦s33⟧ And thus he gave a leap into the womb of the blessed Virgin. ⟦s34⟧ And from this into the world, and from the world into heaven, where he feeds those fawns with heavenly milk. ⟦s35⟧ And so, since both the angels and the holy souls are solicitous for sinners, and come to their help both by merits and by intercession, it is to be believed that the blessed Virgin can do as much in this as both these creatures; nay, she is judged mightier than both, because both are repaired through her, and the ruin of the angels has been restored through her, and human nature reconciled. ⟦s36⟧ But what wonder if the blessed Virgin pours out the milk both of reconciliation for sinners and of grace for the pious, she who reconciled the world, and bore grace itself, nay [0475B] the very fountain of grace, which is spread abroad through the world and stretched out even to the world's end? ⟦s37⟧ For the blessed Virgin is the paradise out of which that fountain goes forth, which is then divided into four heads, because from the body of Christ which proceeded from her there flowed forth a fountain of blood which sacramentally flowed thence into the four parts of the world, alike to wash away sins and to pour in grace upon his own. ⟦s38⟧ Deservedly therefore is her mercy compared to the running of fawns, it runs through the world, it waters the world and pours itself in. ⟦s39⟧ It is compared to the swiftness of fawns, because her tenderness runs to meet more swiftly than she is invoked, and anticipates the causes of the wretched; but she is compared to fawns, because the sweetness of goodness which she bestows she herself sucks from above. ⟦s40⟧ But these are thy breasts, O blessed one, that is, thy tenderness, with which [0475C] thou sucklest the wretched, while thou obtainest mercy for them; thou sucklest the wretched and art thyself suckled by that same mercy, from her thou receivest what thou pourest back. ⟦s41⟧ Thou hast merited the prerogative of a singular grace, because thou art privileged above women, so that thou hast nourished, not with the milk of women, but with virginal milk, and not a mere man, but God. ⟦s42⟧ Whence how singular is this, because she was seen to have neither a first like her nor one to follow. ⟦s43⟧ Deservedly therefore art thou judged able to do as much as the holy angels and the souls of the saints, and art compared to both. ⟦s44⟧ To thee therefore, mother of mercy, mother of the wretched, the exiled sons of Eve cry out, the very miseries cry out. ⟦s45⟧ For misery has a cry, and this valley has tears: for it is a valley of tears, so much so that, if the wretched themselves cry not out, these things sound not in [0475D] thine ears [cj: *non insonent*; read *insonent*, "they do sound"]. ⟦s46⟧ These things cannot be silent before thee, nor lie hidden from thy hearing, in that thou hast ears for hearing miseries, and for thee to know them is to hear them. ⟦s47⟧ For as wherever the body shall be, there the eagles also are gathered, so wherever misery shall be, thy mercy both runs and runs to help. ⟦s48⟧ By God thy breasts are filled with tenderness, so that, touched by the knowledge of any misery, they may pour out the milk of mercy, nor canst thou know miseries and not come to their help. ⟦s49⟧ And what wonder if thou dost abound in mercy, thou who didst bear mercy itself? ⟦s50⟧ Christ sucked carnal breasts in thee, that through thee spiritual ones might flow for us. ⟦s51⟧ For when thou didst suckle mercy, from that same mercy thou didst receive breasts. ⟦s52⟧ And just as he took our flesh in thee, he also gave us his Holy Spirit. ⟦s53⟧ When God sucked material milk [0476A] from thee, spiritual milk likewise began to flow through thee to the sons of God. ⟦s54⟧ From that time thou wast made mother of the wretched, and didst begin to nourish the wretched with the milk of mercy. ⟦s55⟧ And although the mercy of God is from eternity, it began to be more ample from a point of time. ⟦s56⟧ And since its beginning began from thee, its bounty also took increase through thee. ⟦s57⟧ In thee therefore the milk of divine mercy grew together, and from thee it flowed forth to us. ⟦s58⟧ Thou thyself wast first filled, and from thee this abundance came down to us. ⟦s59⟧ Whence, because thou art made drunk with this milk, and pourest out that milk for us, and thy tenderness runs through the world, and runs to meet the wretched and comes to their help, deservedly art thou compared to fawns, and art made like to suckling kids, and to those that run alike. ⟦s60⟧ For they suck that milk in the divine fruition, and run to meet us; because they are both satisfied with the contemplation of God they run to meet us in charity, and come to our help [0476B] by merits and by intercession. ⟦s61⟧ But since both the angels and the holy souls run about with solicitude concerning us, and succor us, thou art compared to both, because thou alone canst do and dost accomplish as much as both, and above both. ⟦s62⟧ These fawns too feed among the lilies, because they desire the salvation of those still dwelling in exile, and are refreshed by a clean life and by the fragrance and repute of a good manner of life, which is to feed among the lilies. ⟦s63⟧ Which assuredly they do until the day of eternal beatitude dawn, and the shadows be ended, namely the miseries of the present life.
