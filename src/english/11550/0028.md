## CHAPTER XXIV. *On mortification, and on prayer, and on the perfect beauty of the soul.*

*I go to the mountain of myrrh, and the hill of frankincense [n: (Cant. IV)]*. [var: Vulg. *vadam*, "I will go"] The mountain of myrrh is the soul which mortifies [0476C] her members that are upon the earth, fornication, uncleanness, lust, evil concupiscence, and avarice, etc. She is lofty, and deservedly to be compared to a mountain, because she treads upon and passes over earthly and lowest things, and raises herself to desire and to know the things above, seeking the things that are above, where Christ is, and relishing these, not the things that are upon the earth. She judges earthly things, and condemns all that are in this mortal life, knowing that nothing in this earthly life of dying men subsists or is lasting. She has myrrh while she mortifies in herself carnal affections, and her own will, or whatever lower thing can delight. Mountains are wont to be high and dry, but lower places moist. The devout therefore are mountains, [0476D] because they are high through the love of the things above, and dry while they extinguish in themselves and dry up the moisture of carnal desires. These mountains the Lord visits, and he does not come to moist places, because God does not remain in damp places, but the devil. Whence every devout man strives in all ways to dry up this moisture, and to be flooded from above with spiritual water. For this he spreads out his hands to God, and his soul as earth without water, namely of carnal moisture, the soul thirsting for this grace as earth which is without moisture is wont to desire water; whence he deserves to be heard swiftly, because his spirit fails, that is, his will and appetite in earthly and carnal things. Dry therefore is the land of the good, and not having the moisture of earthly love. It is not as the land [0477A] of Egypt, that is, of the world, where, the seed being cast, waters of irrigation are led in the manner of gardens. The land of Egypt is the heart, human and darksome, where he casts his seed who sows in the flesh, and plants his root in worldly things, where he easily leads the waters of carnal delight through the garden of the mind. This irrigation is ready at hand, but as much as it is at hand, so quickly does it fail and dry up. But the mountain is the devout mind, dry from the lowest delights, nay that mountainous land which the Lord God gives to his own, because it raises the good from earthly things to desire the things above, as often as it ascends to a high heart. This land awaits from above the rain of grace, and God is wont to send it from the beginning of the year even to the end thereof, from the beginning, that is, from the time of birth even to the end of life, and to pour it in with a heavenly [0477B] irrigation. For which waters the hearts of the good pant, because, as is read in Job, their root is opened beside the waters [n: (Job XXIX)] [var: Vulg. *radix mea*, "my root"], that is, their hidden thought and the intention of the mind is expended to receive the streams of grace and the gifts of the Spirit. These waters they thirst for, and that they may be made worthy to receive them, they dry up from the water of carnal delight, and are empty. For the obtaining of which they ascend also, by prayer and contemplation, to the waters that are above the heavens, namely to all the heavenly spirits, who praise God and are made drunk with the torrent of his pleasure. But how should the soul not draw this fountain, and receive the waters of grace, she who is opened to all the heavenly spirits, and spreads herself out in desire, and knocks upon them all with prayers? Who if at length she so enlarge and spread herself out, that as she asks grace for herself from all [sic: *ad omnibus*], [0477C] so also she desire and demand it for every man. Shall she not deserve to be flooded with it the more largely, the more amply she is enlarged in charity and toward many? Again, when she is stretched out to all those spirits, does she not embrace each one to whom the guardianship of a man has been committed? And so when she spreads out the bosom of her heart to all the spirits that she may take spiritual gifts and draw grace, and is stretched out in turn by desire to each one, and whatever she shall have been able to obtain or to desire is granted to those same and is poured back for the salvation of those committed to them, he is deservedly heard who thus helps his own intercessors and advocates, even as he begs their help and patronage. For they themselves wish mercy for those committed to them, [0477D] and rejoice with those who labor together with them concerning their salvation. Deservedly does such a soul have her advocates favorable in prayer, she who desires as much to bestow as to receive. Deservedly is that soul blessed and pleasing to God which so sows upon all waters, and casts the seed of prayer, that she does not withdraw this seed even from the land of her enemies, but intercedes for them also out of charity. Much is her root opened, which is spread out to all these spirits, and is alike enlarged to all men, which ascends to the upper waters of the spirits, and below comprehends the necessities of all men: much is it opened, when an equal intention is directed to all the spirits for all men, and she strives to obtain grace for all men equally as for herself. When therefore the root [0478A] of the soul is thus opened, and is so manifoldly spread out, namely to the waters of the Holy Spirit, to the waters of the holy spirits, to the waters of the peoples (for the waters are peoples), deservedly does she please God much, much is she flooded with the water of grace, much is she enlarged in charity. She is a lofty mountain by desiring the things above, having myrrh and frankincense alike, while she mortifies in herself all worldly and vicious affections, and devoutly prays for all. Whence Christ deservedly turns aside to her, to her he comes, as being beautiful in his eyes, and pleasing and worthy of his coming. Whence he commends her, saying! *Thou art all beautiful, my love, and there is no spot in thee.* A woman is less beautiful if indeed she is graced with beauty of body, but is pale in countenance. But when she shall have both elegance of body [0478B] and ruddiness of cheeks, then she is judged more perfectly comely. So the soul, when through worthy penance she has been cleansed from sins and has destroyed the body of sin, is beautiful as it were in body. And when also through daily confession she has been cleansed from daily sins, and suffers shame for them, she is at the same time adorned as it were with the ruddiness of cheeks, so that she is deservedly called all beautiful: And there is no spot in thee of mortal sin, nor does venial sin persevere either, because she does not cease daily to wash and cleanse herself by penance and contrition. Nay, she is cleansed from venial sins the more fully, the more she is affected with great charity toward her neighbors, and strives by praying to obtain salvation for them. Again, she is all beautiful who is adorned by good works and virtues, and by humility and by the [0478C] confession of her sins (with which she had been stained) is beautified. For by judging her sins, and humbling herself for them, she is not only cleansed from sins, but also acquires the adornment of humility whereby she may also be beautified. And there is no spot in her, namely of elation. For from this she is free, while through desire she is always kindled to do greater things, and reckons small whatever she has done, while, weighing the benefits and labors of the Savior, she knows that she cannot worthily answer to them. She is therefore all beautiful and without spot, while the memory of her evils even adorns [sic: *decora*] her, and no elation over the good things she has done stains her.
