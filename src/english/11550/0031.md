## CHAPTER XXVI. *On the whole and perfect beauty of the blessed Virgin Mary.*

*Thou art all fair, my love, and there is no spot in thee [n: (Cant. IV)]*. The blessed Virgin Mary was all fair, because she was sanctified in the womb. And once gone forth from the womb she never committed either a mortal or a venial sin. And before the conception of the Son of God, indeed, she was first kept from sins by grace; but after it she was so confirmed by the power of the Most High, overshadowed [0482D] and strengthened, that she could not commit sin at all. From the time she was made the temple of God, she was so privileged that she could in no wise be disfigured by any spot. She was likewise all fair, because full of grace, so that there was not in her any venial sin, not even the least, to prevent grace from being able to have place in her. For when one commits some venial sin, then he does not merit, nor is grace increased in him. All fair therefore was she whom grace possessed wholly, because sin had no place in her. She had also the virtues without disturbance or the passions of the vices, and she possessed them, delighted with love of them and helped by grace. Therefore in her the virtues were perfect, and poured over into continual use, so that she savored, willed, or felt nothing other than good. Such [0483A] was she in the virtues; so great also was her charity that it was extended not only to her own nation (namely the Jews), but these unknown and known, to all men at last: to all these her charity was widened, for these she was solicitous, for these she prayed unceasingly, for these she was heard in praying, the angel Gabriel saying to her: *Thou hast found grace with God [n: (Luc. I)]*, namely, that which thou wast seeking. She desired, sought and obtained the salvation of all; nay, the salvation of all was made through her, whence she is also called the salvation of the world. All fair therefore was she, because cleansed in the womb, and thereafter confirmed in holiness and cleanness, the tinder of sin being first lulled asleep in her, so that she did not sin, and at last extinguished, so that she could not sin thereafter; she attained at last angelic purity on earth, [0483B] and the likeness of God in the virtues and in holiness and in perfection of manners: just, tender, prudent, modest, merciful, holy in mind and body. Rightly therefore is she proclaimed fair, who attained on earth both the divine and the angelic likeness. And because humility is the place of God's grace, so much so that one is capable of grace in the measure that he has been humble, as she possessed this virtue perfectly and wholly, so grace filled her wholly, and adorned her wholly. This then is the inward beauty of the mind. The outward beauty also was the comeliness of chastity, the whiteness of virginity. She was also wholly bright in work, having nothing dark or sinister, but all bright and perfect, so that she lay open to the gaze of others and shone by example, clothed in the gilded raiment of the virtues and surrounded with the variety of merits. [0483C] Such a one did the heavenly King desire; into one so comely, so clothed, the sun of justice poured himself, and clothed himself in flesh in her. Nor is it a wonder if she was bright whom the splendor of glory filled, if she was fair who received into herself the splendor of light. Nor is it to be doubted that the fire of love and the inward whiteness shone in her outwardly also, so that she who had angelic purity had an angelic countenance also. Rightly therefore is she called all fair, because she was fair in face, fair in mind and body. She was terrible even to the princes of darkness themselves, so that they did not presume to approach her, or to tempt her. For the flame of charity frightened them off; her prayers and the fervor of her devotion burned them; they stood amazed at her, free from sins. For no one [0483D] is so holy as not to have had a spot and a defect, except her. For the moon, which shines by night, and the stars are not clean in comparison with her; that is, those who shine by holiness of life as the moon or the stars are darkened, in the night of this life, in comparison with her. For the stars, that is the saints, are darkened by the mist of human fault; but the blessed Virgin was all fair, whom the sun of justice wholly enlightened and flooded, so that she had neither spot nor the darkness of fault.

Above these things she was adorned with martyrdom. For a sword pierced through her own soul, not a material one, but one of sorrow. By which martyrdom she suffered more grievously than by iron. For as incomparably as she loved, so much the more vehemently did she grieve. Whence, as there was no love like her love, so neither was there sorrow like her sorrow. In the martyrs [0484A] the greatness of love softened the sorrow of the passion; but the blessed Virgin, the more she loved, the more she grieved, and by so much was her martyrdom the more grievous. Whence, because she loved more than all, and the force of her sorrow was according to the greatness of her love, she suffered more grievously in mind than the martyrs in flesh. Fair therefore was the blessed Virgin in the comeliness of virginity, fair in the praise of confession, and fairer than all the rest who have lived holily, fair in the palm of martyrdom, and without any spot. Because therefore she is so fair, and in so manifold a way comely, rightly is she called from Lebanon, that is, from *candidatio*. She is also said to be about to be crowned from the head of Amana, Sanir and Hermon, that is, from the prince of this world, who bears princedom over the lovers of the world, and over those separated from God through the stench of cleanness [cj: *munditiae*; read *immunditiae*, "of uncleanness"], or through other sins. This prince of the world [0484B] and head of the wicked the blessed Virgin overcame, trod down, and crushed his strength, while she kept perfect cleanness in body and humility in heart, so that he could have no place of creeping either in her breast or in her mind: this strong woman, whose price is far off and from the uttermost ends, bearing him who should overcome the strong armed man that possessed his own court (that is, the world) in peace. Which blessed one, while by her merits and by her intercession she freed from their hearts those whom he had either cast down by openly raging, or, covered over, had deceived by secret and various fraud, is declared to be crowned from the dens or mountains of lions and leopards.
