## CHAPTER XXVII. *On spiritual sight, and how the care of one's own salvation, and of another's, is pleasing to God.*

[0484C]

*Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, my bride; thou hast wounded my heart with one of thine eyes, and with one hair of thy neck [n: (Cant. IV)]*. There are two sights or eyes: one by which we contemplate the things above, another by which the transitory and earthly things. That one by which the things above are seen the Savior came to open, as he himself says: *Into this world I came, that they who see not may see, and they who see may become blind [n: (Joan. IX)]* [var: Vulg. *In judicium ego in hunc mundum veni*, "For judgment I am come into this world"]. For the enlightening of this he came to us; he endured the opening of wounds and death, that he might open this light to us, and quicken us unto the knowledge of salvation. Whence he expresses in these words how much he loves this eye, namely the contemplation and the desire of our salvation, saying: thou hast wounded [0484D] my heart. As if he should say: O soul, thou who hast deserved to be called my sister, from the time when, by taking flesh, I made myself thy brother, and whom I chose as bride when I joined thee to myself in love and in unity of will: in that thou seest and seekest the eternal rather than the earthly with the eyes of the heart, thou dost so gladden me and become pleasing to me, that thy love wounds my heart and pierces it by its own excess. My love toward thee was strong as death. Whence I willed to die for thee, and to take upon me the hard wounds of the body for thee, that I might receive from thee the sweet things of love. That thought therefore, and fixing the eye of the heart upon the opening of them, thou openest this one alike to love and to knowledge. For love opens acquaintance, so that while thou lovest thy Savior, thou mayest understand also the salvation which he purchased with such charity and at the price of his very self, to be immense, and worthily [0485A] to be sought after. Loving me therefore because I bore these things for thee, thou beginnest also to love the joy which I purchased [sic: *emavi*] for thee and prepared. And thus through the visible things of my humanity and of my labor thou comest to the seeing and comprehending of the invisible things of things eternal; from me thou transferest thy contemplation to the seeing of me; from me having no beauty or comeliness, to my glory and majesty in the heights, where I am such that I am delightful to look upon even to the angels. Thy spiritual sight therefore, and thy salvation, is the contemplation of my love and of the enjoyment of me. For I am thy Savior, and thy salvation; and I who was thy price shall be thy reward, and the twofold vision of me is eternal beatitude. This spiritual sight, and the desire of thy salvation, I love vehemently in thee; with this one eye alone, that is, with the one and single [0485B] contemplation of thy salvation and of my love and enjoyment, thou woundest my heart, and with one hair of thy neck. The neck joins the head and the members. Thou art therefore a neck to me, while thou art zealous to convert souls and to join them to me. One hair of the neck is the single thought and the chief intention with which thou laborest about this. In this twofold solicitude and care, both of thy own salvation and of another's, thou claimest my love specially for thyself, and piercest to the inmost parts of my heart. There pleases me also, and singularly touches me, the simple eye of intention with which thou seekest nothing against me, nothing besides me. For through this thou art bright in thy whole body, that is, by good and sincere work, so that thou art worthily praised, and hast learned to wound my heart with thy love.
