## CHAPTER XXX. *On the fountain of the soul and the living waters of grace which flow from Christ.*

*Fountain [sic: nortorum] of gardens, well of living waters, which flow with a rush from Lebanon [n: (Cant. IV)]*. The fountain is the devout soul which wells up and flows, and always rises new, because she is always renewed in God. For she meditates, or works good, or is borne by desire toward the things ahead. This fountain does not cease to boil up, and to break forth into the [0491B] love of God, to [sic: *exerescere*] toward its own, and to widen out into the love of the neighbor. For she does not grow old through sluggishness, but always grows most perfectly and to the full, and out of the fullness in which she abounds she visits and waters those lower and weaker than herself. These are gardens, and they are enclosed so long as they keep themselves from sins, and cultivate and exercise themselves in virtues and good works. Toward these, set as it were nearby and joined by spiritual kinship, she flows out; and those who go astray, these she pours herself into, these she corrects, these she makes fat, these she consoles. And those who for the most part tremble with faint-heartedness while they perceive less of the advance of grace, while they are less raised up by the help of preaching or of consolation: such the devout and perfect soul visits, because she lifts them now by consolation, now by the help of prayer, now by teaching [0491C] or admonition. For she is a well of living waters, having attained, that is, the deep grace and knowledge of the Holy Spirit, because she both abounds in herself so that she can pour out again upon others. Not so that well from which the Samaritan woman drew water, of which he who drinks thirsts again. The water of that well is the delight of sin, which, when a man drinks it, does not so much satisfy him as provoke in him a thirst for sinning. In short, while he desires to settle his thirst, another thirst grows. But this well holds living water, which may quench the thirst of sin and leap up into life everlasting, because this satisfies spiritually and leads through to an eternal satiety. That well is deep, because things desired are obtained with difficulty; but the depth of this one makes no difficulty of drawing, but [0491D] ease. For the more deeply and the more fully she receives grace, the more she has it at hand. For depth is a founding, and a perfection by which she has the good even in her affection in such a way that she loves it for the sake of the good itself, and takes delight in the good itself, and does good not only for the sake of reward, but confesses to God because he is good. This is Isaac's third well, which he called Breadth, because the Lord had spread him wide upon the earth; for a man is spread wide upon the earth when he has come to the perfection of grace and, servile and initial fear being passed, has climbed up to filial love. A man digs the first well when he casts earth (that is, earthly sins) out of himself through fear of punishment. The second, when not only by fear but also by love of the good he guards against sins. The third, [0492A] when out of love alone he both forsakes evil and does good. And in the digging of the two wells he endures the slanders and the enmities of the shepherds of Gerar, that is, of the demons who see our sojourning. For Gerar is interpreted *incolatus visio,* the seeing of a sojourning. These shepherds feed and nourish the carnal motions in us, seeing us to be sojourners and newcomers upon the earth, and striving to deprive us of our fatherland. But when we have come to perfect charity and spiritual fervor, and are led to every good out of love, then strife ceases in us, and the assault of the demons, because they have no strength to contend with us, but [sic: *ipse*] they are weakened in the fight and unnerved by spiritual fervor and by love of the good. Then the soul is so spread wide in charity and grace, and is so enriched with its fullness, that out of herself [0492B] she pours into others also. And as it is written: *Out of his belly shall flow living waters [n: (Joan. VII)]*. This grace of the Holy Spirit is compared to living waters, because, while it fills, it here quickens the soul and leads it through to the life to come. But these waters flow with a rush from Lebanon. Lebanon, which is interpreted *candidatio,* signifies Christ, who is white, because he is without all sin, and who shines in the heavens with such whiteness and comeliness that the contemplation of him is the joy of the angels as much as of the saints. For he is the brightness of eternal light and the unspotted mirror of the majesty of God. He is white also, and is the light that enlightens every man coming into this world. And so copiously is that light poured abroad that it rises even upon the ungrateful, and shines in darknesses which by no means comprehend it. It flows around the evil, however, [0492C] but does not flow into them, because it does not reach through to their hearts. For they have shut themselves up and have condemned their own entrance with the bars of sins, lest the light of the divine gifts penetrate to them; but the good it illumines and flows into, because, fortifying the lodging of the heart against the defilements of sins and being zealous for cleanness, they prepare themselves to receive grace. In these it dwells, these it fills, refreshes and makes drunk; these holy ones, the dwelling-place and city of God, the rush of this river gladdens. Again, there are waters which flow with a rush from Lebanon, that is, from Christ, into the devout and the humble. Lebanon is a mountain from which flow the waters of Jordan, which is interpreted *descensus eorum,* their descent. That mountain is Christ, from whom grace descends to the hearts of those who are valleys, that [0492D] is, the humble, who love abjection and who take and seek grace with a single and simple desire. Such men lie open in no way to carnal or worldly desires, nor do they strain after human favors: therefore they receive these streams of grace as copiously as they zealously shut themselves up against all outward allurements. And so with a rush the waters of grace flow from Christ to cleanness of heart, to burning desire, to the valley of humility. From Lebanon, that is from Christ, living waters flowed down when the Holy Spirit flowed into the disciples like a torrent and made them drunk with the sweet abundance of his gifts. And again, when the same Spirit filled the round world. Whence these waters are deservedly said to flow from Christ, because from him the time of grace began; from Lebanon these waters flowed with a rush [0493A] when, Christ at the supper blessing the bread, the sacrament of the Eucharist began to be celebrated, and, his one passible presence being taken away, he remained with us in many places until the consummation of the age. Offered also once upon the cross, he is offered for us many times upon the altar. And of him who now dies no more, and over whom death shall no further have dominion, the mystery of his death and its effect unto our salvation is daily repeated; for there the death of the Lord is proclaimed. These living waters flow with a great rush. For this grace runs through the borders of Christendom, and waters the faithful and flows into them. For the faithful receive it not only when present, but they take it in remote places also, if they draw near with desire and touch with faith. By faith the woman touched him who came up behind, and [0493B] did not press upon him bodily. But to those who touch the form of the sacrament by faith this fruit comes through. This power goes out from him, namely, ampler grace, remission of sins, strength against sins, defense against temptations. So the faithful, in whatever places they may be, are cleansed as much as they draw near by faith, and receive this grace. These are the living waters which are drawn from the fountain of the Savior, so that they are deservedly said to flow with a rush, which fill so many and in so many ways, and are known to flow so far and so widely. Be spread wide, then, my soul, toward this fountain that flows so widely; go about all its places, that thou mayest draw much water of grace from this fountain. Nowhere dost thou approach this fountain idly, never dost thou return from [0493C] it without fruit. So much wilt thou draw from this fountain as thy desire and thy faith shall have been. He required a spiritual touch of Mary and forbade the bodily one. For to touch and to see spiritually is more healthful and more meritorious, the Lord saying: *Blessed are they that have not seen, and have believed [n: (Joan. XX)]*. Grant me therefore, Lord Jesus, fountain of goodness, who flowest with so great a rush and art spread wide in so many places, that I may run to thee by faith so far and wide, and may touch thee, so that I may fully draw thee and deeply possess thee; and thus may become a well of living waters, and may be able to pour out again upon others thee who flowest into me with a rush.
