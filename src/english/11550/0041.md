## CHAPTER XXXV. *How God is desired, and withdraws himself, and how he is present unto salvation, and hears.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I opened the bolt of my door to my beloved. ⟦s2⟧ But he had turned aside, and had passed by [n: (Cant. V)]*. ⟦s3⟧ The bride relates again how, that she might enjoy the beloved, she guarded against the least negligences, which could provoke his offense. [0505C] Namely in useless and superfluous speech, or thought, in bodily comfort, or in wandering freedom, which things can be understood by the bolt, that is, the bar. ⟦s4⟧ For the bar, though it be small and narrow, yet closes the breadth of the door, so that unless it be unbarred the door cannot stand open. ⟦s5⟧ So these least things, unless they be cut away, the beloved does not deign so to come to the soul as to enter perfectly into her, and to fill her with perfect grace. ⟦s6⟧ Small negligences darken the soul, and make an impediment to ampler grace. ⟦s7⟧ Knowing this, the bride keeps the eyes of her heart from the dust of these things, that she may be able to see the beloved through contemplation, and to receive him more fully. ⟦s8⟧ And so she presses the more upon the cleansing of her heart, that the beloved may enter into her more perfectly, and as through the [0505D] door, by whom she had known herself before to have been visited less fully, and touched as through an opening. ⟦s9⟧ But he turns aside, and passes by, not withdrawing from her, but going apart to one side. ⟦s10⟧ For he is at her right hand, lest she be moved from the good begun, but not appearing to her in the face. ⟦s11⟧ For he is near to all that call upon him in truth [n: (Psal. CXLIV)]. ⟦s12⟧ She desires indeed to contemplate him, and to speak with him face to face, as a man is wont with his friend. ⟦s13⟧ But he hides his face, remaining with her and in her, although he is felt to be less present. ⟦s14⟧ In this way, although he turns aside to her, he turns away from her and passes by, lest she be lifted up, and ascribe it to her own merits if she enjoys him according to her wish. ⟦s15⟧ When therefore she has shown herself more worthy of receiving him, he does not offer himself to her as she desires, but turns aside from her, that he may preserve her [0506A] in humility, and may stir up her desire the more and fulfill it more perfectly. ⟦s16⟧ For when from his turning aside she has been humbled, and made more fervent, from this her merit also grows, and she becomes worthy to receive him more fully. ⟦s17⟧ But he is said to turn aside then, when he does not grant that his presence be felt, though he is present to her. ⟦s18⟧ The soul then does laboriously what before she did delightfully. ⟦s19⟧ In which labor she is spiritually strengthened, and while she acts manfully her heart is comforted, that she may bear strongly and patiently wait for the Lord, when he either afflicts by adversities or withdraws consolation. ⟦s20⟧ For the exercise of labor strengthens the mind by use, that from it she may take strength; and it shows rather that she does good things by the strength of labor than by the sweetness of consolation. ⟦s21+22⟧ And so in the time of contemplation [0506B] fervor toward God is stirred up, and when this is past the work is proved and virtue exercised; or she complains that the beloved turns aside from her, because, comprehended in part, he is understood to be the more incomprehensible, as the Psalmist says: *Man shall ascend to a deep heart, and God shall be exalted [n: (Psal. LX)]*. [var: Vulg. *Accedet homo*, "man shall come"] And Solomon: *I sought wisdom, and it withdrew farther from me [n: (Eccle. VII)]*. [var: Vulg. *Dixi: Sapiens efficiar*, "I said, I shall be made wise"] For by as much as a man ascends to God, by so much does he find him lofty and incomprehensible. ⟦s23⟧ Whence Job: *He shall not be searched out when his voice shall have been heard (Job XXXVII)*. ⟦s24+25⟧ She pleads too that he has turned aside, because the mind hungers for and desires what it has tasted, and by as much as it perceives his sweetness, by so much the less does what it grasps of him suffice it. ⟦s26⟧ The knowledge of his goodness shows that what she received is scanty, stirs up complaint, kindles her [0506C] to love more, and humbles her the more after she is kindled. ⟦s27⟧ And because she does not have him according to her wish, she pleads that he has turned aside and passed by. ⟦s28⟧ For she melts wholly in the love of him whose inspiration she has received. ⟦s29⟧ And this is what follows:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *My soul melted, when the beloved spoke.* ⟦s2⟧ When the beloved speaks in the mind, hard things are softened [sic: *emoliescunt*], and the things which before seemed hard because of earthly pleasures, when the soul is melted and so warmed, grow soft and become delightful, according to that which is written: *The things which before my soul would not touch, now, for anguish, are my food [n: (Job VI)]*; bitter things then become sweet, because she is so melted by love that she little feels whatever tortures the body. ⟦s3⟧ She is dissolved into tears [0506D] for the greatness of love, and her tears become her bread day and night, so long as it is not granted her to contemplate her God. ⟦s4⟧ Whence again she complains, saying: *I sought, and I found him not; I called, and he did not answer me.* ⟦s5⟧ She does not find him whom she sought according to her wish, nor does he answer her as to all the things which she asks or desires, so as to hear as he is asked, or to reveal to her that he has heard; but he does not hear him according to his will, yet sometimes he hears better, unto profit. ⟦s6⟧ And when he is thought not to have heard, he hears better, because he grants what he knows to be more expedient. ⟦s7⟧ Paul, asking that the sting of his flesh be taken from him, God heard better when he did not take it away [n: (I Cor. XII)], because if he had taken it away, he would not have been saved. ⟦s8⟧ And so, when he is thought not to answer, he answers better; nay rather, [0507A] he himself forms in the soul the words and desires to which he may answer. ⟦s9⟧ For he himself asks, that is, he makes her ask, and he answers, while he grants the things that are expedient. ⟦s10⟧ Sometimes she desires that it be answered her how many iniquities and sins she has, that her crimes and offenses be shown to her. ⟦s11⟧ In which he answers her well, when to her contrite he remits all the things which she neither recalls nor ever specially set forth. ⟦s12⟧ For hidden dangers also, or necessities, she passes over into him by faith and love, and asks him who sees all things that he himself may see these things for her, and come to her aid. ⟦s13⟧ For she knows that her necessities are many, which she herself does not see nor know, and that many dangers lie hid from her, which she fears the less the less she knows them; and therefore it comes about that she is more specially committed to him, that by coming to her aid he may answer her where he himself [0507B] knows her necessities. ⟦s14⟧ Which answer she merits, bountiful according to the measure of her faith and love. ⟦s15⟧ In this also he answers her, that no voice of her prayer or desire is without merit, because it is made out of charity. ⟦s16⟧ If also she should ask something that is not expedient, in this he answers her, that he does not grant it, and that the pious intention itself of the petition will not lack its reward. ⟦s17⟧ And so, when she finds that the beloved does not answer her according to her wish, she seeks consolation and instruction in Scripture, and finds it. ⟦s18⟧ Whence follows:

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *The watchmen who went about the city found me.* [var: Vulg. *custodes qui circumeunt*, "the keepers who go about"] While the bride is thus made anxious by the love of the beloved and by the intent of seeking, the keepers found her, who went about the city, that is, the preachers, who run about preaching to this end, that they may fortify and defend the Church from false doctrine [0507C] and from sins. ⟦s3⟧ While she seeks their doctrine, and questions them concerning her salvation and advancement, they meet her, and find her, and instruct and kindle her to love God more. ⟦s4⟧ And this is what follows:

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They struck me, and wounded me.* ⟦s2⟧ For they strike her who loves with the sharp arrows of the word of God, and wound her with a greater love. ⟦s3⟧ For these are the arrows of the Lord, which he wrought for those who burn, that is, made effectual for those who love. ⟦s4⟧ For they pierce those who love, but rebound from the sluggish. ⟦s5⟧ Which preachers are also called keepers of the walls, because they busy themselves to instruct those also who govern the Church. ⟦s6⟧ These also take away the cloak of the bride, because whatever they detect to be still in her from her old manner of life, [0507D] they withdraw through the office of preaching, and by their instruction they uncover the danger or sin which lay hid from her: *Ask thy father,* says Scripture, *and he will declare to thee thy elders, and they will tell thee [n: (Deut. XXXII)]*. ⟦s7⟧ Which, because some neglect to do, they wander in the danger of mortal sin, clothed with whose cloak they will not have the nuptial garment of charity.
