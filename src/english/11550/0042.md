## CHAPTER XXXVI. *On the love of the beloved, and its quality.*

*I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, if you find my beloved, that you announce to him that I languish with love [n: (Cant. V)]*. The bride sought the beloved by herself, and received also the instruction of the preachers; now she seeks the prayers of devout souls. For these are the daughters [0508A] of Jerusalem, born of God, and belonging to the vision of the future peace. These she adjures, that when they have found the beloved, that is, have had him present through the grace of devotion, they may announce to him her love and desire, and offer prayers for her. The heavenly spirits indeed, and the souls of the saints reigning with Christ, she has often entreated, and through the angels in whose presence she was accustomed to pray and to sing psalms she has announced her prayers to them. For these also are daughters of Jerusalem, who unceasingly contemplate that peace. But now she asks these daughters of Jerusalem (that is, devout souls dwelling on earth). For this is the manner of the good and of those advancing, that first they seek God by themselves, and press upon the cleansing of the heart. Then that concerning their own state and hidden dangers they consult the skilled, and [0508B] that they seek the prayers of the good, and love their familiarity. For when they commit themselves to the prayers of the good, and join themselves familiarly to them in charity and humility, it is wont to come about that from the desire which they detect in them they too are kindled. For those whom they understand to seek God so ardently, they trust to know him already in great part. Whence, seeking out of affection, they say: *What manner of one is thy beloved of the beloved, O most beautiful of women, what manner of one is thy beloved of the beloved, that thou hast so adjured us.* The beloved of the beloved is the Son of God of the Father, and he who loves the Son loves the Father also, as he who sees the Son sees the Father also. But out of admiration of him and desire, the devout souls, thus inflamed, repeat and name the beloved, desiring also themselves to be taught [0508C] by her, and to be refreshed by that sweetness of the beloved received from her. Whom they also call most beautiful, because they most surely judge that she has the beauty of the mind and is adorned with virtues. This humility too is wont to be among the good, that they think better of others than of themselves, and hear more delightfully the things which are said out of knowledge (that is, are brought forth from inward experience). Those who love, too, gladly speak of him whom they desire. For the devout, when they come together to speak of God, are wont both to be the more kindled toward God by mutual conference, and, out of the confidence which they have, to bring forth to one another what they feel or understand concerning God. God too is wont to be in the midst of them, and to heap up grace in those who for his sake are heaped together, are gathered, [0508D] and speak of him. Whence the bride, the more inflamed at the question concerning the beloved, begins to express his beauty and to tell the benefits conferred on her by him, saying: *My beloved is white and ruddy, chosen out of thousands.* He is white, because he is the brightness of eternal light, and Light of light. White, because he is free from all sin, because he did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth. And ruddy, because in his passion he was drenched with his own blood, in which there was neither beauty nor comeliness, that he might make me comely, and that his blood might adorn the cheeks of my soul, and might reform me who was deformed. Therefore is he my beloved to me, because he suffers these things for me, and does these things also for me. Who is also chosen out of thousands, because out of the whole human race none is without [0509A] sin, except him. For none is like him among the lawgivers, nor among the thousands of thousands, and the ten times a hundred thousand who minister to the Lord of majesty, or stand by him. For gold or glass shall not be made equal to him, that is, the society of the supernal citizens, whose hearts both shine with brightness and are translucent with purity to one another. And this, because he is not only man, but God. Whence follows:

*His head is the best gold.* His head is his divinity, because the head of Christ is God. Which head is the best gold, because the goodness of the divinity excels all things that were made by it; this is the gold of the land of the living, and the gold of that land is the best. For shining, so that it cannot be seen by human eyes, and by those living in the flesh. For man shall not see God, [0509B] and live. Therefore then is he my beloved, because this is his head, this his chief part, this, the sublime which cannot be seen. I shall see when that life shall have appeared, because then I shall be like him, and I shall see him as he is. Who will grant me this twin happiness, because he will configure the body of my lowliness to the body of his brightness, and will conform my soul to the likeness of his divinity.
