## CHAPTER XXXIX. *A description of certain members of the beloved, and the praises of the Virgin Mary.*

*His legs are columns of marble, which are founded upon bases of gold [n: (Cant. V)]*. These are they who support religious men out of their own goods, who impart temporal things to them, that they may receive spiritual things from them. For he who receives a prophet in the name of a prophet shall receive [0515A] the reward of a prophet. He who supports a just man for the serving of God shall not only be a sharer of his reward, but shall have his reward together with him. These are the legs of the Bridegroom, because these are they who go about visiting the sick, and coming to those who are joined to them in the flesh; of these, not only does the door lie open to the traveler, as with Job, but they also go with Tobias to the natives, and divide to each one out of their means as they are able. Who, like columns of marble, are upright in knowledge and in work, and unbreakable by vices and sins. They are wise rightly and soundly, supplanted by no false doctrine or religion. For some teach false things under the appearance of religion. [0515B] Demons also suggest in the heart a false justice, and a false understanding in place of the light of truth. Certain men indeed bend the bow of Scripture, and prepare their arrows in their quiver, that with their arrows they may shoot in the dark at the upright of heart; and the things which the Lord has perfected, that is, has perfectly made, and has set forth above the teachers, they destroy and bend to their own error. Demons also work certain subtle things and certain revelations in some men. And likewise certain new and unwonted things, which the curious and the simple marvel at and venerate. The understanding of the good, therefore, is right and sound; their works are formed as the life proceeds out of the heart. They make straight steps with their feet, because they do all things with counsel, and they lay open in confession [0515C] not only their sins, but also the hidden suggestions of the heart. For he cannot err who lives with counsel, nor be deceived by the enemy who uncovers his suggestions. Again, the good are upright and unbroken, who are not bent that demons may pass over them, as it is written of the evil one: *Bow down, that we may pass over [n: (Isa. LI)]*. Nor are they broken by injustice, so as to consent to the temptations of demons and to fall into sin. They are therefore upright and erect, because they both keep justice in themselves, and raise themselves up for the desiring of justice and set themselves against injustice. Whose firmness and constancy is compared not to any stones whatever, but to marble, because it is not cheap and common, but precious and rare. Nor is the strength of stones their strength, because they have not grown hard through insensibility, but through the rightness of zeal. [0515D] These columns are founded upon bases of gold, that is, upon the faith of the prophets and of the apostles. For indeed the same faith was in the prophets and in the apostles. What those believed would come, these believed had come; what those hoped for and awaited, these beheld and possessed. Whence one of them speaks, saying: *My days have been swifter than a runner; they have fled away, and have not seen good [n: (Job IX)]*. These bases were golden, because they shone through works. For without works faith is idle, but through works it is precious and to be compared to gold, because it is informed by charity. Upon these bases are founded these legs and columns, because the merciful and the upright are built up upon the foundation of the prophets and of the apostles, Christ Jesus himself being the chief corner stone, [0516A] who joined two walls in himself, namely the Jews and the gentiles, the just and converted sinners, and has made men fellows of the angels in heaven. There follows:

*His beauty is as of Lebanon.* These are the virgins, having the comeliness and the likeness of Christ. For the soul which has charity has the likeness of Christ, and, advancing more fully in the correction of manners, more fully receives this likeness. When anyone has repented, straightway in the very contrition of the heart he receives the likeness on God's part, which little by little he will possess more fully, and in the future perfectly. By this likeness all the good are conformed to Christ, but more especially the virgins, who together with this likeness have the whiteness and the cleanness of life; whence it is compared to Lebanon, which signifies Christ, who is interpreted *white*; therefore cleanness is nearest [0516B] to the author of cleanness. Whence, because the virgins obtain this special comeliness, they are well called beauty. And because they are without the stain of pollution, they are more familiarly joined to the Lamb without stain. They also follow the Lamb whithersoever he shall go. They follow specially him to whom they have specially made themselves like. To live in the flesh beyond the flesh is a heavenly life, not an earthly one. Whence, when virgins tame the sin of the flesh, which above all prevails over the human race and clings to nature, in a certain manner they are outside the flesh and are made like to God, who is Spirit. They are therefore the beauty of Christ as the beauty of Lebanon. For Lebanon is Christ, nor is he only white, but also a whitening. For he makes the elect white and comely. Lebanon also is fruitful in noble trees; so Christ brings forth noble ones, [0516C] namely those whom he had regenerated through his Spirit into liberty, that they may be sons of God and truly free, that they may not commit sin, that they may not be servants of sin. Among these trees the loftier and nobler was the blessed Virgin, who was exalted like a cedar in Lebanon, that is, in Christ, because she is not lofty through herself, but through Christ, and was made lofty and chosen by him, so that she might thus grow up and herself give increase to her own Creator. A lofty and noble tree, which brought forth this lofty fruit, namely the Son of God. Finally, she is herself the cedar which grew in Lebanon, that is, in Christ; and while she herself begot Christ, the cedar as it were produced Lebanon, and the mountain came forth out of the tree when God came forth out of man. This is the new and unheard-of thing which [0516D] God did upon the earth, that a cedar should bring forth Lebanon, a tree a mountain, that a woman should compass a man, that is, God, and that him whom the world could not contain the Virgin should enclose in her womb. And so the virgins are the beauty of Christ, but the blessed Virgin is his special beauty, who never stained her beauty by any mortal or venial sin. Whence the heavenly King desired her beauty, and, taking flesh in her, adorned this beauty yet more, because he filled her with an ampler grace. And so, when the world was wrapped in darkness and deformed, he brought forth the Sun of justice, beautiful in form above the sons of men, and thus restored light for blindness, and a beautiful beauty for deformity. She therefore, singularly the beauty of Christ, beautiful above all, and most like to Christ, not only [0517A] having herself the beauty of Christ, but also restoring it in others. And so Mary is above men, in that she never committed sin, as men do, among whom none has been found who does good and does not sin. She is also above the angels, because she surpasses them in purity, while she contemplates the divinity more clearly than they. And she not only did not commit sin, but also blotted out the sins of others; nor was she only bright in herself, but also sent forth rays of light from herself, because through her the world received the light of grace. And so Mary is the middle and the mediatrix between things divine and things human, so that she surpasses human things, while she works things so lofty through her Son. O wonderful dignity, that from a woman there should proceed a sun enlightening the world, that light should shine out from earth, that through a woman the darkness of the world [0517B] should brighten into light! Through her that sun shone out of her upon the world, and gave a brighter light, because it more fully illumined hearts. Thus the eternal light rose new out of the temporal, and spread its rays more widely. Nor was the light of grace given through her on earth only, but also the vision of God to souls in heaven. This blessed one is that great sign in heaven, a woman clothed with the sun, because the sun of divinity shines in upon her, and shines round about her, and occupies her wholly, and after her, who is most of all enlightened, gives light to the citizens of heaven. She first, and above all, received the splendor of this sun. She is clothed with this sun, and is perfectly and pre-eminently flooded with the enjoyment of the divinity. And she as it were put it on, when she received so great a fullness of it. But she is clothed with this [0517C] sun in heaven for this reason, because on earth she had perfectly put on Christ, as the Apostle says: and she was *full of grace* [n: (Luc. I)]. But the moon is under her feet, that is, the Church triumphant, because it is lower, and, enlightened by this sun after her, the moon suffers a waning; and therefore, because every man except her has had some waning, he can be enlightened by this sun, but not equally clothed. This blessed one has been exalted like a cedar, has been made lofty like Christ, and if as much [cj: *tantum*; read *non tantum*, "not so much"] as Christ: Christ was the least in the kingdom of heaven, that is, in the present Church; Mary was the humblest of all who have been upon earth. Therefore she was made lofty unto this, that she might conceive the highest of all, and after him become the highest of all. Well had she humbled herself with Christ, who was to be made lofty with Christ. As [0517D] Christ is at the right hand of the Father, so is she at the right hand of the Son. Christ is above all the works of his hands; Mary is above those same works after Christ, because she has been exalted not only above the souls of the saints, but also above the choirs of the angels. In the most humble Mary, therefore, Christ humbled himself unto the taking of flesh, and thus exalted her humbled with himself. Lofty therefore is Mary, but loftier is Christ. Whence of him there follows:

*Chosen as the cedars.* To the beauty, the strength, the loftiness and the fragrance of the cedars all the glory of the woods yields; so neither the highest men on earth nor the angels in heaven can be made equal to Christ. I wish therefore to consider how lofty and pre-eminent he is in himself, and how greatly he humbled himself for my sake: whence, when [0518A] I fear the judge, I wish to consider the brother, and the partner of my nature. For he will judge in the humanity which he mercifully took for us. Let him bear the sentence of humanity and of mercy, of a brother, and not of a judge or of justice. If I shall wish to justify myself, let him be judge, and let him condemn me; if I confess myself wretched, let him bestow mercy. I myself wish to be judge in the judge's place, that he himself may not be judge, but a brother in the judge's place; and I wish to judge myself, lest he himself judge. If I come so to the judgment, and have with me in my cause the mother of mercy, who shall deny that the judge is favorable? Mary was made the Mother of God because of mercy; and this too I believe that she exercises unceasingly for the human race before the Father and the Son, and most of all will exercise this judgment in the judgment for those to be examined. [0518B] She is the gate in which Christ, the husband of the Church, will be noble, when he shall sit to judge with the senators of the earth. Then the loftiness of this cedar will appear, and how greatly he stands out above men, and how exalted he was reckoned, whose breath was in his nostrils, that is, breathed in the mouth of men. Let us therefore seek Mary, the mother of mercy and the gate of heaven, and let us hope for mercy in the gate of judgment, that through the gate of judgment we may enter unto eternal life.
