## CHAPTER XL. *On Christ and his merits.*

*His throat is most sweet, and he is wholly desirable [n: (Cant. V)]*. The throat of the Bridegroom is his inward savor, and spiritual consolation, which sweetly affect those who taste and have experience of them. [0518C] For if by thinking thou comprehend the goodness of God, and, reaching unto the savor of him, thou wilt proclaim with the bride the sweetness of this throat. The taste also of his love is so sweet that it not only refreshes the mind, but even makes one forget the food of the body. If thou also contemplate spiritual and mystical things, whatever thou hast been able to comprehend of him thou wilt approve as sweet and delightful, so much so that thou wilt reckon all things except him but little sweet, nay none at all, and wilt judge that he alone is good, that he alone is to be embraced and loved. But if thou pass over in mind to the considering of things above, it will be sweet to meditate what it is to see God, and to be satisfied with the vision of him, that he himself is in us, and we in him, and that we are one with him, and to lack all misery, and to rejoice with him without end: the inward savor of these things [0518D] is the throat of the Bridegroom. But the heart which shall perceive this sweetness must be cleansed of vicious affections, because unless the vessel of the heart be pure, it less perceives, or retains, this sweetness of the spirit, because it has thus far drawn near out of the dregs of carnality. Let it therefore refuse to be consoled save in God; let it be mindful of God, and be delighted in him, and be exercised in this delight, so that his spirit may fail, that is, his own will, and that he may despise what delights outside God. As much as he shall have despised outward delights, so much will he be able to be delighted in God. Let him cast away the delight of worldly things; let him leave the world, that is, worldly things; let him approach the solitude of the mind; let him sit solitary, and let him lift himself above himself, let him lift the body above the body, that he may transcend bodily things, and comprehend the things above and the spiritual. All that is [0519A] in the world is concupiscence of the flesh, or concupiscence of the eyes, or pride of life. He who shall have despised these things, by vanquishing carnal concupiscence, by calling himself back from outward things to inward and invisible things, and by humbling himself with pride trodden underfoot, assuredly leaves the world and comes to solitude, so that he may be able to lay hold of the acquaintance and the savor of spiritual things. This is the Egypt out of which the people of God goes forth, and this is the solitude to which it ought to strive. Out of this solitude messengers are sent, and they bring back of the fruits of the land of promise. These messengers are spiritual meditations and holy desires. These are sent by the devout soul to consider this land. These, by searching out this land, enter it; they bring word of its abundance and sweetness; they carry of the fruit with them. This fruit is [0519B] the taste of that sweetness, the foretaste of that sweetness, a fruit sweet to the throat of the devout soul that tastes this fruit and experiences its sweetness. He who tastes this sees, that is, knows, how sweet is this most sweet throat of the Bridegroom, which not only excels all sweetness, but even expels it. The more the sweetness of this is felt, the more it is desired. It has hunger in this life, not satiety; refreshment, not fullness. For it is so sweet that it is always desired, so immense that it is never comprehended to the full; and he who is so affected as to call himself satisfied, and wholly filled, I know not whether he is refreshed from the throat of the Bridegroom, or from adultery and a false messenger: *They that eat me shall yet hunger,* says the Wisdom of God; *and they that drink me shall yet thirst [n: (Eccli. XXIV)]*. Here the goodness of God is a slender taste, [0519C] in the future satiety, wherewith we shall be satisfied when his glory shall be made manifest, and we shall be filled with the good things of the house of the Lord. The true savor is thus discerned from the false, because it is not vehement or impetuous; it does not titillate the flesh, but restrains and mitigates the motions of the flesh. It is modest, tranquil, light, sweet, peaceable; it strengthens the spirit, weakens the flesh, kindles and enlightens the mind; it does not lift up, but humbles. For its sweetness stirs up desire, and while it kindles ampler desires it always renders a man humble concerning his own advance. For since what he has is little, as he always reckons himself needy, so he always thinks humbly of himself. An imperfect or false savor shows a man rich to himself, because it does not penetrate unto the knowing of the riches of the goodness of God. But that savor is [0519D] imperfect which the new and the carnal as yet receive, and those who have not yet attained the perfect cleanness of the heart, but have the eyes of the heart bleared, so that they cannot see clearly, and comprehend spiritual things. Thence therefore they think themselves rich, whence they see little, and have not the true knowledge. If such men, being more enlightened, knew more, and laid hold of the immensity of this sweetness, they would rather be humbled than lifted up, because they would understand how scant is what they were able to perceive of it. False is the savor which the enemy works, and which sweetly affects and gladdens the mind; but it lifts up, it does not humble; it counterfeits fullness, but has it not: for how empty of true grace those are who perceive this will be plain if they be tried by adversities. Whence they may be compared to a bridal chamber [0520A] which is shining without, but empty within; such men shine and glitter outwardly, because they have a certain appearance of devotion, but within they are empty of virtues and have no fervor. In the secret place of this bridal chamber the enemy sleeps and has stillness, because holy desires and fiery prayers do not rouse or put him to flight, but the lukewarmness of the mind and the false brightness of religion endures him and cherishes him. Their glory is not from within, nor is it so much in conscience as in outward repute; and because they are poured out outwardly, therefore they do not know or perceive inward things. Their savor, even if it be sweet, yet is not most sweet, because it is not from the Bridegroom, but from the adversary. Most sweet is that which the good perceive, who through cleanness of heart have come to the true acquaintance of his goodness. These can proclaim his most sweet [0520B] throat, because, experience teaching them, they have tasted his sweetness. If we also consider his humanity, most sweet was his throat, through which the words of the divinity came forth, because they were spirit and life, as he himself says. Sweet therefore was the preaching, and those words with which he spoke of the kingdom of God. Most sweet therefore was his throat, and he wholly desirable: desirable was the vision of his humanity on earth, but much more desirable his glorified humanity in heaven, which is desirable even to the angels, who desire to look into it. For he is desirable in countenance, whose countenance the whole company of the citizens above desires; but most desired of all is the contemplation of his divinity, to which all that is desired cannot be compared. He is therefore [0520C] wholly desirable, who is also desirable in his members, alike in those still sojourning on earth and in those reigning in heaven, because their grace delights as much as their glory considered. For here they have the brightness of holiness, and there of glorification. Their holiness is beloved for imitating, and their joy for rejoicing with them and enjoying together with them. Sweet is the contemplation of their joy, which we hope will be ours also, and which we await. This awaiting has gladness, so that we may be rejoicing in hope of this, and patient in tribulations, or in the labors which we bear for this. Christ is therefore desirable even in his own, because out of his own glory and comeliness he made them comely and desirable, so that he may be desired not only for his own sake, but [0520D] for the sake of the elect, whom we shall have as companions and partners with him in glory, and in whose glory we shall rejoice. He is therefore wholly desirable, because in himself and in his members. And this is my beloved, O daughters of Jerusalem; this is he by whom I am loved and whom I love, not in word, nor with a human tongue, but in work and in truth, because I hear his commandments and keep them. Consider the beauty of him, desire his love, O daughters of Jerusalem, that is, souls desiring to come to the vision of the peace above: for through his love, and the fulfilling of his commandments, one comes through to the vision of his beauty, to the happiness and peace above, to which may Jesus Christ, the Bridegroom of the Church, deign to lead us, who lives and reigns world without end. Amen.
