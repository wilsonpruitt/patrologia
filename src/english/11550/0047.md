## CHAPTER XLI. *The Bridegroom invites the bride to receive her inheritance.*

[0521A]

*Come into my garden, my sister, my bride [n: (Cant. V)]*. Thou hast been made a garden for me, O soul, by working in thee for me the diverse perfumes of the virtues and of good work. Now also it is time that thou come into my garden, to receive the delights of my garden, because thou art a sister, thou art an heir; come to the inheritance, because thou art a bride; come to the embraces, come to sleep that thou mayest receive the inheritance; because, when I shall have given sleep to my beloved, this is their inheritance. Thou hast slept outwardly, but thou hast watched with thy heart and hast thought upon the inheritance; come to the joy for which thou hast labored, rest after labor, contemplate by sight what thou hast contemplated by faith. [0521B] Hope has refreshed thee; let vision now refresh thee. In the left hand thou hast been still; let the right hand embrace thee, and let earthly things not constrain thee and encompass thee; come into my garden. My garden is a garden of delights. In my garden the roses of the martyrs are red with wondrous comeliness; with wondrous whiteness the lilies of the confessors and of the virgins shine and give forth their fragrance. In my garden there are kinds of diverse fruits, and as many delights as there are merits of the blessed who reign in the kingdom. In my garden is every planting of the ancient Fathers, or of the new grace. Whatever was ever planted, whatever of good has been done from the beginning of the world, hither the fruit of all has ascended. Hither it has finally come; all these things have profited thee, they await thee, they will receive thee. This is the fruit of my elect, which always remains, and is not only the fruit of those who do it, but also of those who love it in [0521C] others; by loving it thou hast made it thine, for by loving me thou hast loved all that is mine; by loving thou hast made thine things mine, and the merits of my own are common to my elect. For single things are not only of single men, but all things are of all. Come therefore hither, to receive all things at once, because all things have been prepared for thee: new things with old have I kept for thee, namely the ancient and the recent merits of my own, that thou mayest be delighted with all, mayest possess all, mayest be satisfied out of all. I have reaped my myrrh with my spices: I reaped myrrh in the martyrs, spices in the confessors and in those who lived well. The myrrh of the martyrs has come to the harvest: for their bitterness passes away, and they have come through to the fruit; for they have received the rewards for their labor. The confessors, from the confession of their own [0521D] sins and of my praise, have passed over to confessing to my name unto praise without labor, unto the praise of peace and of the vision of me. The confessors were spices: for by the example of a good life they refreshed others and were fragrant with a good odor. My myrrh also have I reaped: for I consummated the bitterness and the punishment of my death, but through this I obtained for my members the sweet joys of salvation. The tastes of whose passion are savory to me through love, but that which is confected out of it, with the sweet spice of the redemption of man, refreshes me; neither the myrrh of the martyrs nor the spices of the confessors would have perceived these joys, if the myrrh of my death had not perceived them. My blood extinguished the flaming sword set before paradise; and this being extinguished, the cherubim also, whom it barred along with itself, does not deny to man [0522A] the entrance, since he knows him to have been redeemed by the blood of his own Creator. Nor does he receive him only into his own fellowship, but also unto the likeness of knowledge; for the cherubim is called *the fullness of knowledge*, and man receives the likeness of knowledge, that he may know perfectly, and even as he has been known, who here knew by experience. I have eaten the honeycomb with my honey, I have drunk my wine with my milk. In the passion I bore my bitter things, but now I perceive the sweet things of the resurrection. Then roasted as a fish, now I eat the honey of glorified humanity. And I who was as one despised and the last of men am now crowned with glory and honor and set above all the things of the Father, and not only above all the things of men, but also of angels; I eat the honeycomb with the honey, because in glorified humanity I enjoy in [0522B] myself the glory of the divinity, and in my members I perceive the same. This is my sweetness, these my delights with the sons of men, these the feasts, this the banquet. This is food and drink to me, that I eat these things more sweetly than honey, and drink them more pleasantly and delightfully than wine and milk. In these delights I take my joy, and with the same I feed and give drink to my members: for the honeycomb of honey were to me those sweet in manners and in works, the wine and the milk the wise and the simple; to which banquet come, O sister and bride, after thy labors.

## CHAPTER XLII. *On the assumption of Mary, and her praises.*

*Who is this that ascends, and crossing the deserts? [n: (Cant. III)]* [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:5 *de deserto*, "out of the desert"; Cant. 3:6, which the note cites, *per desertum*, "through the desert"]. Whither does she tend, or what does she portend, covered with flowers? *Papae,* filled with riches, she does not run upon the thief, the singular virgin. O robber, now art thou robbed, made the prey [0522C] of a virgin! This is the enemy of Holofernes, the Hebrew woman, why dost thou spurn the Jews, Aman, Esther is a Jewess, thou spreadest a cross for Mardochai, thou shalt be punished with it. The singular wild beast; the king is Assuerus, Esther of great name. This is that rod of Jesse, which Isaias reports to have been born of the root, whose flower is the Messiah, the harvest of whose passion has strewn the ways of the world with fruit a hundredfold, with sixtyfold and thirtyfold. This is that Sunamite who served David. This Jahel, so tiny, crushed Sisara. This is the queen of Saba, who came to the realms of king Solomon, whose wisdom she heard in the sanctuary; she saves her male child out of the hand of Pharaoh; this is the fleece of Gedeon, enriching the little vessel with dew; this is the singular *ferculum* of the true Solomon, and the ivory tower, [0522D] golden, shining from the whole, wholly delightful; this is she in whom the king reclines himself, the reclining-place of wayfarers while he turns aside; this is the inn, the King of eternal glory inclines his head upon her, having no hut. O of how sweet a memory is a mother so praiseworthy! This is the tree set aflame, but that knows not how to be burned up; the star begotten of Jacob, the herald of the true sun; the gate always barred, passable to the king alone; but also the ark of the covenant, borne upon the shoulders of David, carried over with great jubilation. She, holding the moon beneath her sole, stands compassed with the sun; such does she stand forth, and so holy, having so many charisms, so many figures, signs so great, bearing so many riddles, as there are stars of heaven. Consider how great she is, thou whoever thou art in jubilation: propped up with these little flowers, hedged about with these apples, she stands plucked out of perils, laurelled in the harbor, and, blessed, joined to the Trinity, she now enjoys the longed-for [0523A] kisses! O let the reborn now applaud! O the jubilation wherewith she jubilates, the mother made one with her son! O how brightly she glows red, clothed in double garments; the translator does not so winnow it, faith so alleges, that she now reigns with God! Hail, Mary, full of grace; may God be with thee. Of our corruption, which the Jew dreams. Let the seducer Sadducee waste away in his deceit: [0524A] for thou hast truly risen. O sweet mother of Christ, have mercy on the guilty! Hail, Mary, mother of grace, queen of the world, mother of mercy, save us from ruin, fountain of life, vein of pardon; hasten then to come when we draw near to death, that with thee we may be able to rejoice perpetually. Amen.
