## ⚠ The *lectus* / *lectulus* pair — how this range renders it (0002, 0003)

Fixed per the work brief and held visibly distinct through all five chunks:
**\*lectulus\* = "little bed" · \*lectus\* = "bed"**, with no exceptions and no elegant
variation. The exposition at **0410C** is what forces it: *Et non in lecto, sed in
lectulo, quia quies a talibus habita exigua est, et angusta* → "And not in the bed, but
in the little bed, because the stillness had from such things is scant, and narrow."
The argument is restated at **0411B** (*Quaerit autem hunc in lectulo et non in lecto*)
and is unintelligible unless both English terms are already in place, so the diminutive
is carried even where Richard is not arguing from it (0410B *in lectulo hunc quaerere
debet*, 0411A *lectulus est in quo sponsa quiescit*, 0411A *ad lectulum quietis*).

**Where *lectus* appears on its own, it stays plain "bed" — and 0003 is where this
matters.** At 0412A *Multo labore lectum sibi paraverat* the bride prepares a **bed**
(not a little bed), and at 0413D Job's *stratus* and the bride's *lectus suae quietis*
/ *lecto turbato* are all "bed": Richard is now comparing her *lectus* with Job's
overturned couch, and using "little bed" there would silently import the CAPUT PRIMUM
argument into a chapter that does not make it. *Stratus* (0413D) is rendered "bed" as
well, distinguished only by context; there is no third English diminutive in play.

Nine other agents inherit this: **"little bed" is never "couch", "small bed" or
"pallet", and "bed" is never "little bed".**

## Carried type — `[sic: …]` (Pattern 12)

- **0000 @0406C** — Migne prints *nonne **homine** estis?* in I Cor. 3:3 (Vulg. and sense

## Real printed words whose faithful English misleads — `[cj: …]` (Pattern 18)

- **0000 @0406A** — *ab hoc nuptiali convivio in exteriores tenebras, id est, ignorantiae
  caecitatem **repellamus***. *repellamus* is a perfectly good active subjunctive, so the
  faithful English is "let us drive away … from this nuptial banquet into the outer
  darkness" — i.e. an exhortation to expel, where the sense the sentence requires (and the
  Matt. 22:13 allusion it is built on) is that **we** are expelled: *repellemur*. Rendered
  as printed, with `[cj: *repellamus*; read *repellemur*, "we shall be driven out"]`.
- **0004 @0414C** — *Ad quos dum accedit tepidus incalescit, et fervens amplius
  **ignoscit***. A real verb ("pardons"), and the clause is a two-member climax about
  **fire**: the lukewarm grows warm, the fervent — *ignescit*, catches fire. The printed
  word makes Richard assert something he is not arguing (that fervour issues in pardon in
  a sentence about the fire Christ sent on earth). Rendered "pardons the more", with
  `[cj: *ignoscit*; read *ignescit*, "catches fire the more"]`.

## Passages that will not construe, rendered as closely as the printed words allow

- **0000 @0405A** — *caecum cor a spirituali **intellectum** habet*: accusative after *a*,
  where *intellectu* is required. A case slip has **no English exponent** (Pattern 9
  family), so the English reads "it has a heart blind to spiritual understanding" and
  nothing is marked. Conjecture: *a spirituali intellectu*.
- ⭐ **0000 @0406C — the range's chief unresolved crux.** *Humanae ergo conversationis
  verba audientes **quasi ex terra homines esse debemus**, ne si humanitus quae dicuntur
  audimus, nihil divinitatis … sentire possimus.* Rendered literally, "we ought to be as
  it were men out of the earth", which is the **opposite** of the argument: the next two
  sentences are *Quasi jam non homines desiderabat esse Paulus discipulos suos* and the
  Lord making *quamdam distantiam* between *homines* and his disciples. Two rival repairs
  fit the printed page and I can decide between them from neither the sense nor the type:
  (a) *quasi **extra terram** homines esse debemus*, exactly parallel to this chunk's own
  *quasi extra corpus fieri* eleven lines earlier — my preferred conjecture; (b) a dropped
  *non* (*quasi ex terra homines esse **non** debemus*). **Because there are two candidates,
  no `[cj: …]` was fired** — Pattern 18 requires the conjecture, not merely the defect, to
  be secure. Left literal and escalated here. Whoever settles it should look at the plate,
  not at our TEI.
- **0002 @0410D** — *Arcum conteret et confringet, arma et scuta comburet igni* (Ps. 45:10).
  Migne's comma sits after *confringet*, where the Vulgate punctuates *confringet arma, et

- **0002 @0411A** — *post pondus diei, et **aestus tentationem** scilicet a labore ad

## Fixed vocabulary — tensions worth recording

- *quies / quiescere* = **stillness / to be still** throughout, per the brief's table, even
  where "rest" would read better (0412A *a quaerendo minime quiescit* → "in no wise is
  still from seeking"; 0414C *a quaerendo ampliorem quiescit* → "is she still from seeking
  an ampler grace"). "Rest" is used only for *pausandi* / *requies*-type senses; where
  *quiesco* governs *ab* + ablative the English is "be still from", which is stiff but
  keeps the word joined to the *quies mentis* of CAPUT PRIMUM. Flagged rather than
  decided for the whole work by me.
- *adolescentulae* = **young maidens** (0001) — the diminutive is kept, on the same
  reasoning as *lectulus*; *sodales* = **companions**, *greges sodalium* = "troops of
  companions". Later agents inherit these.
- *Canticum canticorum* = **the Song of Songs**; a plain *canticum* = **canticle**. 0000
  @0407D turns on the pair (*non canticum, sed Canticum canticorum*), as does the fivefold
  list at 0408A (canticles of victory, exhortation and protestation, exultation, help,
  conjunction with God).
- *sacra Scriptura* → **Holy Scripture** (Pattern 3), six times in 0000–0001; *Scripturas*
  at 0407C is "the Scriptures".
- *sponsus* is capitalized **Bridegroom** wherever it denotes Christ, but 0001 @0408B–C
  keeps lowercase "bridegroom" in the sequence *Dominum … patrem … sponsum* where Richard
  is listing the three **names God calls himself**, alongside lowercase "lord/father"; it
  reverts to Bridegroom from *sponsus nominatur, et sponsa* (0408D) onward, where the
  person is meant rather than the title. Scripture lemmata quoting *sponsus* generically
  (John 3:29, Matt. 9:15) stay lowercase.
