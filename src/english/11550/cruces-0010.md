## 1. Lemma words checked against the received text (Pattern 14 / 7a″)

Every Song lemma and every scriptural quotation in the range was read word by word against
the Vulgate before the English was written. Result below; the four markers fired are itemized
in §2.

### Song of Songs lemmata — all three agree with the Vulgate

- **0429C (Cant. 3:6)** *Quae est ista quae ascendit per desertum sicut virgula fumi ex
  aromatibus myrrhae et thuris, et universi pulveris pigmentarii?* — **identical to the
  Clementine text**, word for word. No marker.
- **0434B (Cant. 3:7)** *En lectulum Salomonis sexaginta fortes ambiunt ex fortissimis
  Israel* — **identical**. No marker.
- **0439A (Cant. 3:8a)** *Omnes tenentes gladios, et ad bella doctissimi* — **identical**.
  No marker.
- **0441B (Cant. 3:8b)** *Uniuscujusque **etiam** ensis super femur suum, propter timores
  nocturnos* — Migne inserts *etiam*, which the Vulgate does not print. Rendered ("And every
  man's blade…"). **No `[var:]`**: an added connective at a re-quotation seam does not change
  the sense, and Pattern 14 fires only on sense-bearing divergence. Recorded here so the
  divergence is on the record.

## 2. Markers fired

### `[sic: …]` — carried type (Pattern 12; sense supplied in plain words per 7a⁗)

1. **0429C · *ad eos mente aceedit*** → "to them she draws near [sic: *aceedit*] in mind".
   *aceedit* is not a word (*accedit*, near-certain: a broken **c**). It is the clause's only
   verb, so 7a⁗ Q1 applies and the sense stands in plain English beside the marker.
2. **0431C · *Pigmen enim est quaelibet virtus*** → "For a pigment [sic: *Pigmen*] is any
   virtue whatsoever". ⚠ **Flagged as the least secure marker in the range.** *pigmentum*
   is the form used everywhere else in this passage (*universi pigmenti*, *primum
   pigmentum*, *pigmentarius*), so *Pigmen* looks like a truncated setting — Pattern 10
   family. But I could not confirm that *pigmen* is unattested in medieval lexica, and if it
   is attested, Pattern 9 governs and the marker should be dropped and the word simply
   rendered "pigment" (which is exactly what stands beside the marker, so the fix is a
   deletion, not a re-translation). **A reviewer with Du Cange should settle it.**
3. **0441A · *mente rectractant*** → "they turn this over [sic: *rectractant*] in mind".
   *rectractant* is not a word (*retractant*). Again the clause's verb, so the sense is
   supplied beside the marker per 7a⁗ Q1.

### `[cj: …]` — real word, faithful English misleads (Pattern 18)

4. **0430B · *non tam gaudere dicendus est quam gaudium discere*** → "not so much to rejoice
   as to learn joy [cj: *discere*; read *deserere*, "to forsake"]". *discere* is a real verb,
   so nothing is defective and `[sic:]` has nothing to wrap — and the English then asserts
   the opposite of the argument, which is that transitory joy is *lost*: the same sentence
   ends *gaudium ejus simul transit et deperit cum tempore*, and the **next** sentence is
   *Est itaque desertum mundus, quia gaudia sua **deserit** et amittit*. The whole chapter is
   built on *deserere*/*desertum* wordplay. Gloss is additive; Migne's word keeps its English.
5. **0436C · *et quia hic igniculus, ibi canimus, sicut dicit auctoritas: Vivit Dominus cujus
   ignis in Sion et **caminus** in Hierusalem*** → "there we sing [cj: *canimus*; read
   *caminus*, "a furnace"]". *canimus* is a real verb, and the page says the blessed *sing*
   where the sentence is contrasting a little fire here with a furnace there — and the proof
   text Richard immediately quotes prints *caminus* nine words later. This is the clearest
   Pattern 18 site in the range.

## 3. Passages that will not construe — rendered as closely as the words allow

- **0436B · *et quia hic se cum eis dum eos tentando affligit*** — accusative *se* with no
  verb at all. Rendered "and because here he [is occupied] with them while by tempting he
  afflicts them", with the supplied verb in square brackets (the corpus convention for our
  own supplied English). No conjecture is defensible: *exercet*, *jungit* and *habet* all fit
  the slot and none is indicated.
- **0432C · *Si extollitur et insolescit contra justitiam, vel qui in faciem nos caedit
  convicio, ita quod causa simus in nostra culpa vel vitio.*** — a sentence with no main
  verb, apparently a broken continuation of the preceding 2 Cor. 11:20 list. Rendered as an
  English fragment, punctuation as printed (Pattern 8).
- **0433B · *tantum … quantum ad abstrahere ab hac familiaritate … potuerit*** — *ad* +
  infinitive after *potuerit*. Rendered as a plain infinitive ("as he shall have been able
  … to withdraw"); the printed *ad* has no English exponent here.
- **0433C · *quia acedia laboratur*** — impersonal passive with an ablative. Rendered
  "because there is a laboring under sloth" rather than converting it to an active ("because
  he labors under sloth"), which would have quietly repaired the construction.
- **0435C · *sed videatur eos quos accepit datos sibi potius in munere quam emptos pretio*** —
  *videatur* with an accusative and no governing infinitive. Rendered "but he seems to have
  those whom he received as given to him rather in the way of a gift…"; the supplied "to have"
  is the minimum the English needs and is noted here rather than bracketed.
- **0438D · *Cum vero haec in eis debilitata fuerint … et per spiritualia desideria roborata,
  et tunc quanto haec sub se presserint…*** — *roborata* is neuter plural and so agrees with
  *haec* (the vices), where the sense wants *roborati* (the men); and the *et tunc* leaves the
  *cum* clause without an apodosis. Both rendered as printed. The gender/number agreement is
  Pattern 9 territory (no English exponent for the agreement itself), so no marker.
- **0440B · *Cum inimico enim … nullus ex aequo decertare posset … **quorum** tanta est
  fortitudo, ut nulla virtus humana **ei** comparari possit*** — plural relative for a
  singular antecedent, then singular *ei*. Rendered as printed ("their strengths … compared
  to him"). Pattern 9, no marker.
- **0440B · *coram se profiteretur*** (Josaphat) — "professed himself weak **before
  himself**"; sense wants *coram eo* (before God). Rendered as printed. Not a Pattern 18
  site: it is a pronoun-reference slip, not a negation or correlative, and Pattern 18's
  ruling is expressly scoped away from that class.
- **0429C · *dum factis eis dicunt*** — dative plural where the addressee of the Job
  quotation is God (*ei*). Rendered "they say to them". Same class as the previous entry, no
  marker.
- **0435B · *pati voluerit plus quam **nullus** hominum unquam passus sit*** — *nullus* for
  *ullus* after a comparative is **attested medieval usage**, so Pattern 9 applies: rendered
  normally ("more than no man ever suffered"), no italics, no marker. Negation carried, per
  the negation-fidelity rule.
- **0430D · *ut perficere et ampliorem gratiam mereatur accipere*** — *perficere* transitive
  with no object; *proficere* ("to advance") would give the expected sense and is a one-letter
  variant. Rendered as printed ("that she may deserve to perfect and to receive a fuller
  grace"). Left in the crux, not the text: the reading is uncertain, not misleading, which is
  Pattern 18's own boundary.
- **0436D · *Videndum est nunc quam **infusa** et quam diuturna sunt*** — the subject is the
  *gaudia* of the previous chunk, and *infusa* ("poured in") may be *diffusa*. Rendered "how
  poured out they are"; no marker, uncertainty only.

## 5. Fixed-vocabulary decisions, and the one place the table is under strain

- ***lectulus* = "little bed" · *lectus* = "bed" — held everywhere, and the range is where it
  pays off.** Chunk 0013 argues the diminutive explicitly at 0438B: *quia minimum est quod de
  illa quiete cognoscere possunt, per diminutivum exprimitur, nec tam **lectus** quam
  **lectulus** nominatur* — "it is called not so much a bed as a little bed." Richard then
  uses the two words *differentially* within a single paragraph (0438B–C *hunc **lectum**
  ambire* of the penitents who cannot manage it, 0438D *hunc **lectulum** ambire possunt* of
  the strong), so any smoothing to "bed" would erase an argument he has just spelled out. The
  lemma at 0434B accordingly reads "the little bed of Solomon", odd as it sounds.
- ⚠ ***quies* — I did NOT render "stillness" throughout, and this is the one place I departed
  from the table; flagging it for merge-time reconciliation rather than deciding for the
  work.** The table gives *quies* = "stillness (of the mind's rest)". In CAPUT X *quies* and
  *requies* are used interchangeably for **eschatological rest** (*ad quietem perpetuam
  perducit*, *Quae quies lectulus dicitur*, *in illa quiete pausant*, *hujus quietis
  consideratio*), where "stillness" would misname the doctrine and would also split what
  Richard treats as one word-pair. I therefore rendered:
  - *quies/requies* = **"rest"** wherever it denotes the eternal repose of the blessed
    (0434B–0439A throughout), reading the table's parenthesis "(of the mind's rest)" as
    scoping the entry to the contemplative sense;
  - **"still/stillness"** where it is the mind's quiet in this life — 0433C *quietam vitam
    ducentibus* → "those who lead even a still life"; 0433C *propter quietem exterior proximi
    cura* → "for the sake of stillness"; 0438C *non quietum, sed turbatum habent lectum* →
    "not a still bed but a troubled one"; 0438A *in idipsum … quiescit* → "she is still".
  If the work's merge decides otherwise, the change is mechanical in these chunks.
- ***gladius* vs *ensis* (0439A–0441B): "sword" and "blade" respectively.** Two distinct
  English words were kept because the exposition glosses *gladius* and builds on it —
  *sumunt verbum Dei quasi **gladium***, *vivum est enim verbum Dei … quasi **gladius***,
  *Hos **gladios** … tenent* — so "sword" has to be free to carry that whole chain, while
  *ensis* occurs once, in the lemma, unglossed. "Blade" is the deliberately quieter word.
- ***virgula* = "little rod"** (not the Douay "pillar of smoke"). The gloss requires it: 0433D
  *sicut virgula quae recta est, et in altum crescit … virgulae enim quae crescendo
  grossantur, non nimis in altum ducuntur; sed virgulae quae velocius et sursum crescunt,
  subtiliores et minores sunt*. A pillar cannot thicken as it grows or be slender, and the
  diminutive is doing the same work as *lectulus*. §Song case 2.
- ***decantatio* = "chanting"** (register rule L2), *meditatio*/*contemplatio*/*compunctio*/
  *affectus* per the table, *sponsa*/*sponsus* = "the bride"/"the Bridegroom".
- **Pattern 3:** *sacram Scripturam* at 0439D → "Holy Scripture" (both capitals);
  *Scripturae* elsewhere in the same passage → "Scripture".
- **Pattern 17:** the only second person in the range is 0433B *Vigiliae et orationes sunt
  **tibi** inutiles, si sic ista **exerceas*** — grammatically singular, so **thou** ("are
  useless to thee if thou so practice them"), addressed to a single reader.

## 6. Chapter-head reading I am least sure of

**0429C / 0432B heads · *De spirituali **assensu** animae*** — rendered "On the spiritual
**assent** of the soul", twice (0010 and its 0011 continuation), because *assensus* is a real
word and Pattern 7 renders what is printed. But the chapter expounds *quae **ascendit** per
desertum* and the word *ascendere* recurs perhaps twenty times in it, so *ascensu* ("ascent")
is very likely what was meant. **No `[cj:]` fired**, for two reasons: the marker would sit
inside a `## ` head, where marker parity with the Latin head is fragile, and a head is a
structural label rather than an assertion, so it does not meet Pattern 18's "asserts something
the author is arguing against" bar. If the work's editor wants it glossed, both heads change
together, and 0012–0013's CAPUT X heads are unaffected.
