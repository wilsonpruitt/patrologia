## A. Markers fired

### `[var: …]` — Migne's scripture against the received text (Pattern 14)

1. **0442A (chunk 0015)** · *Item novit opera **singulorum**, et ideo **inducit** noctem, ut in Job
   legitur* · **rendered as printed** ("the works of individuals… he brings on night"), with
   `[var: Vulg. Job 34:25 *Novit enim opera eorum, et idcirco inducet noctem*…]` · Vulg. has
   *opera eorum* (of *them*, the wicked of the preceding verses) and the future *inducet*. Richard's
   exposition runs on the universal present — *et ideo nos excaecari permittit*, *permittit evenire
   caecitatem* — so both differences are the ones the gloss uses. Note also that the citation here
   is printed as inline italic *(Job XXXIV)*, not as an `[n:]` note; it is reproduced verbatim in
   that form.

2. **0442C (chunk 0015)** · *agrum hominis pigri replent **vepres** et spinae* [n: (Prov. XXIV)] ·
   rendered "briars and thorns", with `[var: Vulg. Prov. 24:31 *urticae*, "nettles"]` ·
   Vulg. Prov. 24:31 has *urticae* … *spinae* (nettles … thorns). **Load-bearing:** the gloss
   twelve lines later re-quotes the divergent word — *non veniet super eos timor iste spinarum et
   **veprium*** — so the English lemma had to keep *vepres*/briars or the gloss would refer to a
   word not on the page.

3. **0445D (chunk 0017)** · lemma *…in diademate quo coronavit eum mater sua, **in die
   solemnitatis et laetitiae*** [n: (Cant. III)] · rendered "in the day of solemnity and of
   gladness", with `[var: Vulg. Cant. 3:11 closes *desponsationis illius, et laetitiae cordis
   ejus*…]` · **the strongest divergence in the range.** Vulg. Cant. 3:11 closes *in die
   desponsationis illius, et laetitiae cordis ejus*. Migne's lemma prints *solemnitatis* for
   *desponsationis* and drops *cordis ejus*. Richard then expounds **both** readings in turn —
   first the *dies solemnitatis* as the Ascension (*in die solemnitatis et laetitiae gloriosae
   Ascensionis suae*), then, at 0447A, he quotes the Vulgate form outright,
   *In die desponsationis illius, ait, et laetitiae cordis ejus*, and glosses the espousals of the
   heavenly Church. Conforming the lemma would have collapsed a two-part exposition into one.
   (The `ait` inside that italic run is kept in place per Pattern 5's principle; the run is
   italic, not guilleted, in the plate.)

4. **0447D (chunk 0018)** · lemma *Ecce tu pulchra es, amica mea, ecce tu pulchra* [n: (Cant. IV)]
   · rendered "Behold thou art fair, my love, behold thou art fair", with `[var: Vulg. Cant. 4:1
   *Quam pulchra es, amica mea, quam pulchra es*…; the wording printed here is that of Cant. 1:15,
   whose second clause also closes with *es*]` · two facts, no conjecture as to cause: (a) the note
   cites Cant. IV, but Cant. 4:1 reads *Quam pulchra es… quam pulchra es*; the words printed are
   those of Cant. 1:15 (*Ecce tu pulchra es… ecce tu pulchra es*); (b) the second clause here has
   no copula. The note `[n: (Cant. IV)]` is left exactly as printed — the chapter Richard is
   expounding *is* Cant. 4 (the next lemma, 0450A, is 4:1b), so this is not a citation error to be
   corrected but the divergence itself.
   ⚠ **Copula:** the English supplies "art" in the second clause. Latin elides the copula freely
   and English has no exponent for that elision in *ecce tu pulchra* (Pattern 9 reasoning); the
   `[var:]` states that Vulg. 1:15 prints *es*, so nothing is concealed.

5. **0450A (chunk 0019)** · *Homo nascitur ad laborem, **avis vero ad volandum*** [n: (Job V)] ·
   rendered "but the bird to flying", with `[var: Vulg. Job 5:7 *et avis ad volatum*, "and the bird
   to fly"]` · Vulg. has *et avis ad volatum*. Fired although the sense is unchanged, because the
   exposition immediately reverts to the **Vulgate's** noun — *ad hunc **volatum** et libertatem
   nascuntur*, *Quem **volatum**…* — so the plate's lemma and the plate's gloss do not use the same
   word, and that is exactly the evidence a reader cannot reconstruct from a conformed lemma.

### `[sic: …]` — carried type (Pattern 12), with the 7a⁗ check applied to each

6. **0444D (chunk 0016)** · *Sed ascensus iste purpureus est et **rebeus*** · carried as
   `[sic: *rebeus*]` · not a Latin word; near-certain *rubeus*, "red." 7a⁗ check: the clause keeps
   its verb (*est*), carries no negation, no interrogative, no conjunction — the quarantine takes a
   predicate adjective only, and the next four sentences (*ruborem confusionis*, *purpura ita
   rubea*, *rubor ille dispersus*, *unde rubet*) supply the sense in the author's own words. No
   plain-words supplement needed.

7. **0447B (chunk 0017)** · ***Ex** quantum unusquisque de propria salute gaudebit, tantum et majus
   gaudium de singulis eorum habebit.* · carried as `[sic: *Ex*]`, the correlative rendered
   ("As much as… so much and a greater joy…") · *Ex* + the adverbial accusative *quantum* has no
   grammatical slot (Pattern 12 test 1); conjecture *Et*, matching the *Item*/*Item* chain of the
   surrounding sentences. 7a⁗ check: the marker must **not** swallow the *quantum … tantum*
   correlative, so only the intrusive *Ex* is inside it and the correlative is stated in plain
   English beside it. Only the one word is marked, not a run: this is a wrong/intruded word, not a
   word-division defect, so Pattern 10 does not apply.

8. **0451C (chunk 0019)** · *In quibusdam experitur **cum** quaerere, et desiderare virum…* ·
   carried as `[sic: *cum*]`, with the accusative-and-infinitive supplied in plain words ("she
   learns by experience **that** … he seeks and desires") · *cum* cannot govern an infinitive;
   conjecture *eum* (God), the subject of *quaerere*/*desiderare*, which the following relative
   *qui … opponat* requires. 7a⁗ check: the marked run was the clause's only subject-accusative, so
   the English states the indirect-statement force outside the quarantine.
   **Second, unmarked defect in the same clause:** *qui **spem** orationis vindictae ejus opponat*
   almost certainly stands for *sepem* — Ezek. 22:30, *virum qui interponeret **sepem***, which the
   sentence is quoting, and Jer. 15:1 (*Moyses et Samuel*) follows immediately. **Declined as a
   `[cj:]`:** *spem orationis* renders in place and states nothing Richard argues against ("the
   hope of prayer" set against God's vengeance is his own subject-matter here), so it fails the
   Pattern 18 bar. Rendered as printed; conjecture recorded here.

### `[cj: …]` — real word, faithful English misleads (Pattern 18)

9. **0450C (chunk 0019)** · *Non est haec singularis, **sed et segregata**, sed communis; non sua,
   sed aliorum quaerens…* · rendered "not singular, but also set apart
   `[cj: *sed et segregata*; read *nec segregata*, "nor set apart"]`, but common" · the printed
   words are all real Latin, so Pattern 12 has nothing to wrap, and the faithful English asserts
   that this wisdom **is** set apart in a sentence whose whole point (*sed communis; non sua, sed
   aliorum quaerens*) is that she is not. The defect is in a **correlative/negation** —
   *sed et* where the parallelism needs *nec* — which is the class Wilson's 2026-08-15 extension of
   Pattern 18 explicitly brought in, so incoherence is not a defence here. The gloss opens on the
   printed words, which stand verbatim in the Latin twin.

10. **0450D (chunk 0019)** · *unitatem animorum, **concordiam voluptatum**, amorem mutuum…* ·
    rendered "the concord of pleasures `[cj: *voluptatum*; read *voluntatum*, "of wills"]`" ·
    *voluptatum* is a real word and renders in place, and that is the problem: in a catalogue of the
    soul's secret intimacies with Christ, "concord of pleasures" asserts what Richard spends
    chunk 0015 arming the soul against (*gladius … super femur delectationum et **voluptatum***;
    *In remissa enim mente crescunt terrenae delectationes, et silvescunt propriae voluntates*).
    *unitatem animorum, concordiam **voluntatum*** is the pairing the list wants.
    ⚠ **Borderline, flagged for the merge read:** *voluptas* can be innocent, and a reviewer who
    thinks the printed word tolerable should strike the marker rather than alter the English —
    nothing in the rendering depends on it.

## E. Fixed-vocabulary decisions, including the one hook word

24. ⭐ ***ferculum* (0443A–0445B, chunk 0016) — KEPT IN ITALIC LATIN.** Migne's Cant. 3:9 agrees
    with the Vulgate word for word (*Ferculum fecit sibi rex Salomon de lignis Libani*), so there is
    no textual divergence; the whole question is English. **Richard hangs two incompatible glosses
    on the one word:**
    - *Ferculum est sancta anima, **quae pascit et reficit*** — the word as a **dish**, the thing
      carried in at a meal, which feeds; the entire first half of the chapter (*reficit… pascit
      doctrina… pascit consilio… pascit colloquio*, and later *spiritualibus epulis ditat*) hangs
      on that.
    - *ferculum **a ferendo**, quia **fertur** de loco ad locum* — the word as a **litter**, from
      *ferre*, because it is carried.
    No single English word carries both hooks: "litter" (the Douay-Rheims rendering of the verse)
    kills *pascit et reficit*; "dish"/"tray" kills the palanquin the verse actually describes and
    makes "King Solomon made himself a tray of the wood of Lebanon" absurd. Under
    `translation-style.md` §The Song of Songs case 2 and the register's italic-Latin provision, the
    Latin therefore stands, in italics, at every occurrence (inside the italic lemma it takes no
    second italic marker, per Pattern 10's nested-italics rule), and the etymology renders as
    "a *ferculum* from bearing, because she is borne from place to place" — so an English reader
    meets both the feeding and the carrying, in Richard's own words, with no gloss of ours added.
    **Explicitly NOT harmonized with any other Song commentary in the corpus.** 10804's treatment
    of the same lemma word was not consulted as an authority and is not being followed *as* an
    authority: this work's printed Latin and this work's double gloss decided it, and they would
    have decided it the same way had no other commentary existed.

25. ***reclinatorium* → "reclining-place"** (0444A–0445B). Cant. 3:10; the gloss is
    *in qua **reclinat** se et requiescit Christus*, and the argument runs on the verb for a full
    column (*reclinabit in te, qui reclinatorium non habuit in cruce*; *ut ipse reclinet et
    requiescat in nobis*). Douay's "seat" and the common "back" both sever that hook. Hyphenated
    coinage kept identical at all eight occurrences.

26. ***ascensum purpureum* → "purple ascent"; *media charitate constrata* → "the middle parts…
    paved with charity."** Both glosses are etymological in the same way — *Ascensum in anima
    facit, cum… superna desiderare hanc facit*, and *sed ista **strata** sint (id est, tanta
    **aequalitate** et discretione facta)*, where "paved/level" is what *aequalitas* explains.

27. ***candidatio* (0443B) kept in italic Latin**, untranslated: *Libanus enim qui candidatio
    dicitur*. It is a name-etymology of Lebanon, and the whiteness it depends on is carried into
    the English by Richard's own next clause (*ex **munda** virgine… sine omni peccati labe*).
    Supplying "a whitening" in quotation marks would have put our gloss on the page beside his.

28. **Fixed table followed throughout, without tension:** *anima* = soul, *mens* = mind,
    *quies* = stillness (0450A, *magna **quiete** et securitate fruitur*), *sponsa* = the bride,
    *sponsus* = the Bridegroom (0447A, 0447C), *amica mea* = **my love** (0447D lemma — the
    11536/10804 drift word, and there is exactly one occurrence in this range), *dilecta* of the
    soul = "beloved" (0449D). *lectulus*/*lectus* do not occur in this range.
    *voluntates* rendered "wills" / "self-wills" (0441C, 0442C) to keep it distinct from
    *delectationes* = "delights" and *voluptates* = "pleasures", all three of which Richard
    contrasts in one sentence at 0442B.

29. **Pattern 17 (thou = singular, you = plural) — the range is the pattern's own worked case.**
    Chunk 0017 addresses the *filiae Sion* in the grammatical plural from end to end (*egredimini,
    videte, considerate, vestras lampades, exite*) and takes **you/your** throughout; chunks 0015,
    0016, 0018 and 0019 address one hearer — *o homo* (0442B), *anima sancta* (0444B), *o anima*
    (0450D) — and take **thou/thee/thy**. Both are on the page within a few columns of each other
    and the distinction is Migne's morphology, not our reverence.
