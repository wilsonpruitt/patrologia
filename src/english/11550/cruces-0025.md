## 1. Scripture divergences from the received text (Pattern 14 / 7a″)

### Marked `[var: …]`

**0467A/B (0025) · *Sicut turris David collum tuum, quae aedificatur cum propugnaculis* (Cant. 4:4).**
The Vulgate reads *quae aedificata est cum propugnaculis*. Migne prints the present passive
*aedificatur*, "is built." Rendered as printed ("which is built"), marked
`[var: Vulg. *aedificata est*, "has been built"]`. Material, and Richard's own exposition
depends on it: the tower is a *constructio … et cumulatio bonorum operum, augmentum gratiae*,
and *in quo omnis aedificatio constructa crescit et proficit* — an ongoing building, not a
finished one. The perfect would flatten the gloss.

**0476B/C (0028) · *Vado ad montem myrrhae, et collem thuris* (Cant. 4:6).**
The Vulgate reads *vadam ad montem myrrhae, et ad collem thuris*. Migne prints the present
*Vado*, "I go." Rendered as printed, marked `[var: Vulg. *vadam*, "I will go"]`. The lemma is
Christ's announcement of his coming to the soul, and the whole chapter glosses it as an arrival
already under way (*ad hanc Christus merito divertit, ad hanc venit*), so the tense is not
indifferent.

**0477B (0028) · *radix eorum aperta est secus aquas* [n: (Job XXIX)]** (Job 29:19).
The Vulgate has *Radix mea aperta est secus aquas* — Job speaking of himself. Migne prints
*eorum*, third-person plural, agreeing with Richard's subject *corda bonorum*. Rendered as
printed ("their root"), marked `[var: Vulg. *radix mea*, "my root"]`. Whether this is Richard
adapting the quotation to his sentence or a plate reading is exactly the question Pattern 14
forbids us to adjudicate in the text; both readings are on the page now.

**0481A (0029) · *Omne quod non fit ex fide … peccatum est* [n: (Rom. XIV)]** (Rom 14:23).
The Vulgate reads *omne autem quod non est ex fide, peccatum est*. Migne prints *non fit ex
fide*, "is not **done** of faith." Rendered as printed, marked
`[var: Vulg. *non est ex fide*, "is not of faith"]`. Material here specifically, because
Richard's gloss is about **acts** done against conscience (*qui hoc contra talem conscientiam
faceret*), and the printed *fit* is the word his gloss hangs on.

### Divergences checked and deliberately NOT marked

Each of these is a compression, a truncation, or a woven allusion rather than a change of sense,
so `[var:]` would be noise (Pattern 14 fires on sense).

- **0470D–0471A (0026) · *Mille clypei pendent ex ea*** (Cant. 4:4b) — **verbatim Vulgate.** Named
  in the launch prompt; checked word by word; no divergence. Likewise *omnis armatura fortium*
  where it re-enters the exposition at 0472A and 0473B.
- **0473C/D (0027) · *Duo ubera tua sicut duo hinnuli capreae gemelli qui pascuntur in liliis,
  donec aspiret dies, et inclinentur umbrae*** (Cant. 4:5 + 4:6a) — **verbatim Vulgate**, except
  that Migne runs 4:5 into the first half of 4:6 as one lemma. A lemma boundary, not a wording
  divergence. *donec aspiret dies* is the Vulgate's own word: rendered literally, "until the day
  breathe," not "till the day break."
- **0478D (0029) · *Veni de Libano, sponsa mea; veni de Libano, veni*** and
  ***Coronaberis de capite Amana, de vertice Sanir et Hermon*** (Cant. 4:8) — **verbatim
  Vulgate** as far as they go. The second lemma stops before the Vulgate's *de cubilibus leonum,
  de montibus pardorum*; Richard nonetheless glosses those words (*in quibus leones et pardi
  habitant*), so the truncation is Migne's typography, not a variant.
- **0478A (0028) · *Tota pulchra es, amica mea, et macula non est in te*** (Cant. 4:7) — verbatim
  Vulgate.
- 0467B *Non ego … sed gratia Dei mecum* (1 Cor 15:10) drops the Vulgate's *autem*; 0469B *Panis
  ejus in utero vertetur* (Job 20:14) drops *illius*; 0470C *Erit Omnipotens* (Job 22:25) for
  *Eritque*; 0473B *Cum lapidibus regionum pactum tuum* (Job 5:23) drops the opening *Sed*;
  0467D *Elegit suspendium anima mea* (Job 7:15) drops *quam*; 0471A *Glorietur humilis in
  exaltatione sua* (Jas 1:9) drops *autem frater*. All compressions that leave the sense intact.
- 0473A *inter lapides commoratur* [n: (Job VIII)] — the Vulgate has the future *commorabitur*
  (Job 8:17). Woven into Richard's own sentence (*sicut in Job legitur*) rather than set off as
  a quotation; tense only; no marker.
- 0476C *quae sursum sunt, ubi Christus est, et haec sapiens, non quae supra terram* (Col 3:1–2)
  prints *supra terram* for the Vulgate's *super terram*; 0477A *in morem hortorum* for Deut
  11:10's *in hortorum morem*. Unnoted, woven allusions; word order and preposition only.

⚠ **Lemma re-quotation drift inside 0027, logged and NOT normalized.** The lemma prints *qui
pascuntur **in** liliis*, and the exposition re-quotes it three ways: *Hi autem pascuntur **inter**
lilia* · *Inter quae pascuntur hi gemelli* · *Hi quoque hinnuli pascuntur **in** liliis* (0476B).
The English follows each occurrence as printed ("among the lilies" throughout, since English
*inter*/*in* + ablative of place both give "among" here), so the printed variation is visible on
the Latin side and no gloss depends on the difference. No marker: Pattern 14 is for scripture
quoted **as** scripture, and two of the three are Richard's own prose.

⚠ **`[n: (Job XXX)]` at 0468D** is attached to *pugnare ex pharetra … hastam vibrare*, which is
Job 39:23 (*super ipsum sonabit pharetra, vibrabit hasta*), not Job 30. Note contents are
verbatim per the sacred-marker rule; recorded here only so the indexer's `refDisplay`/`refKey`
split has the observation. No correction, no marker.

## 2. Defective type carried through — `[sic: …]` (Patterns 7, 10, 12) with the 7a⁗ check

For each, the English clause was read with the marker's contents deleted, and the verb /
negation / conjunction supplied in plain words beside the marker where the quarantine took it.

**0467B (0025) · *augmentum gratiae et perfecta dilectis Dei et proximi*.**
*dilectis* (dat./abl. pl. of *dilectus*) has no slot: the series needs a nominative singular
governed by *perfecta*. Conjecture *dilectio*, "love," near-certain. Carried as
`perfect love [sic: *dilectis*] of God and of neighbor` — the noun's sense supplied beside the
marker per 7a⁗ q1, since deleting the run would leave the series without its third member.

**0471B (0026) · *tantum erga cum potentiora sunt*.**
*erga* cannot govern *cum*; neither fragment stands alone, so the whole run is carried per
Pattern 10. Conjecture *erga eum*, "toward him." Rendered
`so much the more powerful are they [sic: *erga cum*], and the vices have strength to assail
him` — predicate and referent both survive outside the quarantine (*ad impugnandum eum*
follows in the same sentence), so nothing needed supplying.

**0472B (0026) · *quae exhibent arma inquitatis peccato*.**
*inquitatis* is not a word (for *iniquitatis*; cf. Rom 6:13). Carried, with the sense supplied
beside it: `which they yield as arms of iniquity [sic: *inquitatis*] unto sin`.

**0474C (0027) · *Per lilia quae nitent et odotem habent*.**
*odotem* is not a word (for *odorem*). Carried, sense supplied:
`which shine and have fragrance [sic: *odotem*]`. Note that *odorem* recurs correctly twice in
the same sentence (*odorem virtutum*) and again at 0474C (*bono odore*), which is what makes the
conjecture certain.

**0477C (0028) · *ut sicut sibi ad omnibus gratiam postulat*.**
*ad* + ablative *omnibus*; conjecture *ab omnibus*, "from all." The whole run is carried, and
because a preposition is a force-carrier (7a⁗-b) the sense is supplied beside it:
`as she asks grace for herself from all [sic: *ad omnibus*]`.

**0478C (0028) · *dum etiam hanc malorum memoria decora, et … nulla elatio maculat*.**
*decora* is a real adjective but has no slot: *hanc* is accusative and the parallel clause has a
finite *maculat*. Conjecture *decorat*. The marked run was the clause's only predicate, so the
verb is supplied beside it per 7a⁗ q1:
`while the memory of her evils even adorns [sic: *decora*] her`.

**0480C (0029) · *Sed si contritio interveveniat, veniale est*.**
*interveveniat* is not a word (dittographic *-ve-*; for *interveniat*). The marked run was the
protasis's only verb, so it is supplied beside the marker:
`But if contrition intervene [sic: *interveveniat*], it is venial`.

**0480D (0029) · *quia pot isset se ad faciendum sollicitum exhibui se*.**
⚠ **Two word-division defects in one clause — the 7a⁗-b shape, and the most dangerous site in
this range.** `pot isset` is *potuisset* split across a space; `exhibui se` is *exhibuisse* split
so that the ending detaches as the real word *se* (which also duplicates the genuine *se*
earlier in the clause). Both runs are carried whole per Pattern 10, spacing included. Quarantining
them takes out **the clause's only finite verb and its only infinitive at once**, leaving "because
he … solicitous for the doing" with no predicate whatever; so both senses are supplied in plain
words beside the markers:
`because he could have [sic: *pot isset*] shown himself [sic: *exhibui se*] solicitous for the
doing, and so, at the time of doing, not have omitted it.`
The argument this closes (culpable omission is imputable because the man *could have* been
diligent) is destroyed outright if either verb is left inside the quarantine.

## 3. Dittography — `[d: …]` (Pattern 11)

**0470D–0471A (0026) · *Si enim fundatae fuerint virtutes in anima, perfecte se despicit, et vere
humilis est, minus laeditur vel gravatur, robur non habent contra hanc vitia, seu tentamenta, et
qui perfecte se despicit, et vere humilis est, minus laeditur vel gravatur, si contemptus
habeatur.***

Migne sets the run *perfecte se despicit, et vere humilis est, minus laeditur vel gravatur*
**twice**, across the 0471A column break — a compositor's eye-skip of a whole clause, not a
doubled word. Both occurrences are rendered (Pattern 7); the marker annotates provenance only.

⚠ **The marker is on the FIRST occurrence, not the second, and that is deliberate.** Pattern 11
wraps "the repeated occurrence … not the original," and here the copy that belongs to the
sentence is the **second**: it carries its own subject (*et qui*…) and its own conditional tail
(*si contemptus habeatur*), while the first copy interrupts the apodosis *robur non habent contra
hanc vitia*. Marking the second would have annotated the grammatically integrated clause and left
the relative *qui* with no predicate on the page — the 7a⁗ hazard applied to a `[d:]`. Nothing is
removed either way; the choice is only about which copy the tooltip attributes to the plate. Flag
for the merge step in case the work settles a different convention for a clause-length doubling.

## 4. `[cj: …]` — the printed word is real and the faithful English misleads (Pattern 18)

**0471C (0026) · *ut tentatus sive a carnis concupiscentia, sive gula, sive voluntate propria,
sed propinquorum affectu, vel carnali aliquo motu*.**
*sed* is a perfectly good conjunction, so Pattern 12 has nothing to wrap and Pattern 7 renders
it — and the English then reads "or by his own will, **but** by affection for kinsfolk," which
breaks a *sive … sive … sive* series in the middle and makes the fourth member contrast with the
first three instead of joining them. This is the correlative class the 2026-08-15 extension of
Pattern 18 rules in. Additive gloss, Migne's word first:
`but [cj: *sed*; read *sive*, "or"] by affection for kinsfolk`.

**0475C/D (0027) · *adeo ut si ipsi miseri non clament, ista auribus tuis non insonent.***
Rendered literally, the page says that if the wretched do not cry out **their miseries do not
sound in the Virgin's ears** — and Richard's very next sentence denies it: *Non possunt haec ante
te silere, nec auditum tuum latere*, followed by *nec possis miserias scire, et non subvenire*.
The whole passage argues that misery reaches her whether or not it is voiced (*Habet enim miseria
clamorem, et vallis haec lacrymas*). The printed word that went wrong is a **negation**, which is
precisely where incoherence is not a defence, so the gloss is additive and the negation is still
carried in the English:
`these things sound not in thine ears [cj: *non insonent*; read *insonent*, "they do sound"]`.
Per 7a, no word was translated as its opposite and nothing was smoothed; the contradiction stands
on the page with the conjecture beside it.

## 5. Passages rendered as printed that will not fully construe (Pattern 7, no marker)

**0467D (0025) · *ut carnaliter esse fortes desinant et spiritualiter convalescant ita dum de se
desperant …*** — no punctuation between *convalescant* and *ita*. Nothing supplied (Pattern 8);
the English runs on identically.

**0467D (0025) · *Qui enim fortes fuerat in quaerendis terrenis, fortes fiant …*** — singular
*fuerat* under a plural *Qui … fortes*, then plural *fiant*. English **does** have an exponent
here, so the mismatch is preserved visibly rather than smoothed: "For he who had been strong in
seeking earthly things, let them become strong…". No marker: the words are real, they occupy
their slots, and the defect is visible in our English as it is in the Latin.

**0470A (0025) · *super has roboratur et firma consistit anima in miraculis non solidatur vel
fundatur*** — Migne prints no mark between *anima* and *in miraculis*, so two clauses run
together. Neither supplied nor deleted (Pattern 8); the English reads "…stands firm in miracles
she is not made solid or grounded, but…". Conjecture: a full stop or semicolon after *anima*.

**0470D (0025) · *Quoties ergo tentationibus inimici resistimus cumque confutamus*** — *cumque*
is a real word and can be read "and when," which is how it is rendered ("and when we confute
them," *tentationibus* supplying the object). Conjecture *eumque*, "and him," which the following
*retorquetur in eum* supports. Not marked: the word occupies a slot and renders in place, so
Pattern 12's own boundary keeps it out (a real word merely wrong is rendered as ordinary English).

**0471A (0026) · *humilitatem exaltationis intelligit*** — rendered literally, "understands the
humility of exaltation." Conjecture *humilitatem exaltationem*, "understands humility to be
exaltation," which is what the antithesis (*de contemptu vel abjectione gloriatur* + Jas 1:9)
wants. Left as printed; no marker (both words real, both in slot).

**0472A (0026) · *In qua turri omnis armatura fortium esse adjungitur*** — the construction has
no clean English; rendered word-for-word, "In which tower all the armor of the strong is added to
be," i.e. the verse adds the clause *omnis armatura fortium* to the tower. Flagged for the merge
in case a later hand finds a better literal.

**0472C (0026) · *quorum laudibus dum afflictus fuerit aliquis*** — *afflictus* ("afflicted") in a
sentence about being **flattered** into losing self-knowledge; conjecture *afflatus*, "puffed up."
Rendered as printed. Declined for Pattern 18: the word is neither a negation nor a correlative,
and "afflicted by their praises" is odd rather than a false assertion (the 8195 asyndeton
boundary).

**0476D–0477A (0028) · *Pro hac expandit manus suas ad Deum, et animam suam sicut terram sine
aqua, carnalis videlicet humoris hanc gratiam anima sitire sicut terra quae est sine humore solet
aquam desiderare, unde velociter exaudiri meretur*** — anacoluthon: the Ps 142:6 allusion loses
its verb, and the infinitive *sitire* hangs with no governing verb. Rendered as closely as the
printed words allow (*sine aqua … carnalis videlicet humoris* taken as "without water, namely of
carnal moisture"), with nothing supplied. Every word is real, so no marker.

**0477C (0028) · *Quae si se denique sic dilatet et expandat, ut … desideret, et poscat.*** — a
protasis with no apodosis; the sentence simply stops. Rendered as printed, no terminal repair
(Pattern 8). The following *Nonne tanto largius …?* is probably the intended apodosis, put as a
question.

**0479B (0029) · *Ubi enim a carnalibus cordis vasa vacua non fuerit*** — singular *fuerit* with
plural *vasa*. Rendered with a normal English plural ("the vessels of the heart have not been
empty"), because forcing the disagreement ("the vessels … hath not been") would read as our own
error on a page where the Latin faces it. Logged as a number mismatch; ⚠ **marked so a later
sweep does not churn it** — this is the boundary case between Pattern 9 (normalize where English
has no exponent) and the 11632 @0304A ruling (mark where rendering hides the defect), and it was
decided for Pattern 9 because the disagreement is between a subject and its copula and the Latin
twin shows it plainly.

**0476A/B (0027) · *quia et Dei contemplatione satiantur nobis occurrunt charitate*** — no mark
between *satiantur* and *nobis*. Nothing supplied; the English runs together identically.

## 6. Fixed vocabulary — followed, with the tensions logged

- ***fortis*/*fortitudo* = "strong"/"strength" throughout**, including the lemma's *omnis
  armatura **fortium*** at 0472A/0473B ("all the armor of the strong"), not "of valiant men."
  Chapters XXI–XXII are one continuous argument about *fortitudo* — *fortes in gratia* ·
  *manu fortis* · *in robur transire virile* · *firmos ac fortes* — and Douay's "valiant"
  would break the hook the whole exposition hangs on at exactly the point Richard is glossing.
- ***propugnacula* = "bulwarks"** (0025, four occurrences). Richard's gloss is strictly defensive
  and offensive at once — *quibus se contra hostes defendat, et ex quibus hostes impugnet et
  feriat* — so the word had to be a fortification a defender fights **from**, not a mere
  battlement. "Bulwarks" carries both; "battlements" and "parapets" carry only the first.
- ***clypei* = "shields"** (0026, and 0468D in 0025 where the enemy *clypeum ei opponit*). Fixed
  across both chunks deliberately: the same noun is the bride's ornament in the lemma and the
  devil's weapon in the exposition, and Richard is playing on that. "Bucklers" (Douay) would have
  broken the pair, and the neighbouring *jaculo non perforatur* / *in hostem jacula mittamus*
  ("dart") needed to stay distinct from it.
- ***hinnuli capreae* = "fawns of a roe"**, mother and young kept distinct, because the whole of
  chapter XXIII turns on the distinction: *hinnuli capreae, id est Ecclesiae* and *gemelli sunt
  capreae, id est Christi a quo pariter capiunt et sugunt lac* — the *caprea* is the one suckled
  **from**, the *hinnuli* are the ones who *ad lac currunt*. A modern Bible's "two young roes"
  collapses them into one animal and the gloss stops working. *gemelli* is kept as a separable
  apposition ("twins") for the same reason: Richard glosses it on its own (*quia eadem doctrina
  reficiuntur utrique*).
- ***lectus* = "bed"** at 0472A (*nunquam lecti suavitate oblectati*). This range contains no
  *lectulus*, so the load-bearing diminutive pair is not exercised here; recorded so the merge
  can see the range was checked for it.
- ***sponsa* = "the bride"** (0478D, *sponsa mea* → "my bride"); ***amica mea* = "my love"**
  (0478A). Both per the table.
- ***quies* = "stillness"** at 0479B (*non praestat quietem*; *super humilem et quietum
  requiescit* → "the humble and the still"), per the table, even though "rest"/"quiet" would read
  more naturally in a sentence about satiety. Logged as a place the table was followed against the
  ear.
- ⚠ ***pietas* = "tenderness" — a choice this range had to make, and the merge should ratify or
  overrule it.** Chapter XXIII (0027) uses *pietas* ~10 times of the Virgin's disposition, in
  explicit distinction from *misericordia* ("mercy," ~12 times), *compassio* ("compassion"), and
  *piis* ("the pious," 0475B). Rendering *pietas* as "mercy" or "piety" would either collide with
  *misericordia* or import the wrong sense; "tenderness" keeps all four separable
  (*pietatis lac* → "the milk of tenderness" beside *misericordiae lacte* → "the milk of mercy").
  The chapter head *De pietate bonorum* is translated to match ("On the tenderness of the good").
  **This is the one term in the range whose choice reaches beyond it** — if *pietas* appears
  elsewhere in 11550 rendered otherwise, this is the drift to reconcile at merge.
