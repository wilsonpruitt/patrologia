## 1. Scripture divergences from the received text (Pattern 14 / 7a″)

Criterion applied, stated so it can be checked: `[var: …]` fires where the quotation is marked as
a quotation (italic and/or an `[n: …]` locator) **and** the divergence changes the sense. A woven
allusion in Richard's own running prose, with neither italics nor a locator, is logged below and
left unmarked — the calibration round 1 used at cruces-0025.

### Marked `[var: …]`

**0481A/B (0030) · *sol non occidit super iracundiam ejus* [n: (Ephes. IV)]** (Eph. 4:26).
The Vulgate reads *sol non occidat super iracundiam vestram* — a jussive addressed to the hearers,
"let not the sun go down upon your wrath." Migne prints the indicative *occidit* and the singular
*ejus*, turning a command into a statement of fact. Rendered as printed ("the sun does not go down
upon his wrath"), marked `[var: Vulg. *sol non occidat super iracundiam vestram*, "let not the sun
go down upon your wrath"]`. **Material, and the gloss depends on it:** Richard's *id est Christus*
identifies the sun with Christ, which only works of a statement, not of a precept — the precept has
no third party to be identified. Both readings are now on the page.

**0484C (0032) · *In mundum hunc ego veni, ut qui non vident videant, et qui vident caeci fiant*
[n: (Joan. IX)]** (John 9:39). The Vulgate reads *In judicium ego in hunc mundum veni*. Migne drops
*In judicium* entirely and reorders the rest. Rendered as printed, marked
`[var: Vulg. *In judicium ego in hunc mundum veni*, "For judgment I am come into this world"]`.
An omitted phrase, not a compression: the whole point of the verse in John is the judgment, and
Richard's gloss substitutes a different purpose for it (*Pro hoc illuminando ad nos venit*).

**0488D/0489A (0034) · *Radix mea aperta est super aquas* [n: (Job XXIX)]** (Job 29:19).
The Vulgate reads *secus aquas*, "beside the waters." Migne prints *super*. Rendered as printed
("above the waters"), marked `[var: Vulg. *secus aquas*, "beside the waters"]`.
⭐ **This is the second divergence in this work at the same verse and they are different ones:**
0028 @0477B prints *radix eorum aperta est secus aquas* (marked `[var:]` there for *eorum*), while
this site keeps *mea* and changes the preposition. The two sites together are evidence about
Migne's copy-text that neither carries alone, so both markers are wanted.

## 2. `[cj: …]` fired (Pattern 18, within the ratified negation/correlative bar)

**2.1 · 0481A/B (0030) · *Ira enim non est mortalis quando aliquis movetur circa proximum
voluntate et consensu nocendi ei…*** — a negation has fallen out and the sentence now asserts
what Richard spends two chapters denying: that anger **with** the will and consent to harm the
neighbor is not mortal. His own next sentence supplies the missing word: *Simile est de omnibus
aliis motibus **citra voluntatem, et consensum** faciendi contra dilectionem Dei et proximi*; and
0482A settles it outright, *cum eis ita consentit aliquis … tunc primum mortaliter peccat*. The
printed clause's own reason — *quia tantum primos motus iracundiae incurrit*, "because he incurs
only the first motions of wrath" — describes the case **without** consent. Rendered as printed,
glossed:
`with the will and consent [cj: *voluntate et consensu*; read *sine voluntate et consensu*, "without the will and consent"]`.
Fires under ratified item 7 (the wrong word is a negation) and under the 7561 precedent that a
**dropped** word is glossable, because the gloss opens on words Migne did print. A second
conjecture is possible — deleting *non* from *non est mortalis* — and is weaker: *citra
voluntatem* in the following sentence points at the ablative phrase, not the verb.

**2.2 · 0484A (0031) · *per munditiae fetorem*** — "through the stench of **cleanness**," in a
list of the reasons men are *a Deo separatis*. Richard's own gloss of the same mountain, twelve
lines earlier in 0030 at 0482B, reads *Sanir, id est fetentes per **immundam** vitam*, and 0029 at
0479D has *those who stink through uncleanness*. Rendered as printed, glossed:
`through the stench of cleanness [cj: *munditiae*; read *immunditiae*, "of uncleanness"]`.
⚠ **The judgment call, named so it can be overruled:** I treated a lost privative *im-* as a
negation, which is what brings it inside the ratified bar. The near-misses that bar declines
(*spem* for *sepem*, *effectum* for *affectum*) are lookalike substitutions that do not invert a
claim; this one inverts it, and the founding case of Pattern 18 is itself *munde*/*mundo*. If the
merge reads the bar more narrowly, the marker comes out and this entry stays as a census
candidate.

## 3. `[sic: …]` fired (Pattern 12) — all four confirmed against the corpus

Method per ratified item 6: grepped all 5,276 PL TEI files (87M words) with word boundaries.
**Each of these four forms occurs exactly once in the entire corpus — this work, this site.**
The corpus is its own control, and a hapax is not an attested medieval spelling (so Pattern 9's
exemption does not reach them).

**3.1 · 0485A (0032) · *gaudium quod tibi emavi atque praeparavi*** — *emavi* is not a Latin form
(*emo* → *emi*). Carried untranslated with the sense supplied beside it per 7a⁗: `the joy which I
purchased [sic: *emavi*] for thee and prepared`. Conjecture: *emi*, "I bought" — Christ has just
said *pretium tuum fui*, and 0485A closes *praemium ero*, so the commercial metaphor is explicit.
7a⁗ check: the clause keeps a second predicate (*praeparavi*), no negation, no conjunction inside
the marker.

**3.2 · 0486D (0033) · *Hujus ordor floris est sicut odor agri pleni*** — *ordor* for *odor*, four
words before a correct *odor* in the same clause. Carried with the sense beside it: `The odor
[sic: *ordor*] of this flower is as the odor of a full field`. The chapter's whole second half is
an argument about odor (*odor unguentorum*, *odor thuris*, *boni odoris famam*), so leaving the
English without the word was not an option.

**3.3 · 0489A (0034) · *Hunc ergo hortum sic conclusum fons signarus irrigat*** — *signarus* for
*signatus*, and the very next clause prints *fontem itaque signatum* correctly. Carried with the
sense beside it: `a sealed [sic: *signarus*] fountain waters`. ⚠ This one sits **inside the
work's central image**: it is the second member of the Cant. 4:12 lemma, so a bare quarantine
would have taken the seal out of the sentence that introduces the seal.

**3.4 · 0490C/D (0034) · *junguntur, quis dum carnem a lascivia restringimus*** — *quis* for
*quia*. A real word with no grammatical slot (a nominative interrogative stranded before a
*dum* clause and a *consequens est* main clause), so it keeps the marker under the 11632 ruling,
test 1. The conjunction it swallowed governs the whole sentence, so 7a⁗ question 3 requires the
force in plain words: `because [sic: *quis*] while we restrain the flesh from wantonness…`.

**3.5 · 0490D (0034) · *Primi parentes terestrem habebant paradisum*** — *terestrem* for
*terrestrem*, a hapax in the corpus. Carried untranslated: `The first parents had a
[sic: *terestrem*] paradise`. No sense supplied beside it, because the same sentence's *post hanc
**coelestem** habituri* carries the contrast; the reader loses nothing but a word he can see is
broken.

## 4. Declined `[cj:]` sites — census candidates, per ratified item 7

Each is a real printed word whose faithful English is awkward or wrong-footed, and none is a
negation or a correlative, so none is glossed in the text.

1. **0487D (0034) · *virtutum plantaria, et spiritualium studiorum gemina*** — read *germina*,
   "shoots," which is exactly what 0490D prints in the parallel phrase (*virtutes scilicet et
   spiritualium **germina** studiorum*) and what 0488C prints at *germina virtutum suffocat*.
   *gemina* is a real word and this chapter is saturated with it (*De gemina conclusione*, *gemina
   concussione*, *gemina sollicitudine*, *gemina dulcedo*), so the alternative reading is that
   *gemina* is adjectival, "twofold," modifying *plantaria* across the comma. Rendered on the
   adjectival reading with a supplied noun in brackets — "the seedlings of the virtues, and the
   twin [seedlings] of spiritual studies" — because the twin-things-of-studies reading yields
   nothing at all. Declined: not a negation, and two conjectures compete.
2. **0483C (0031) · *si pulchra fuerit **quam** splendorem lucis in se suscepit*** — two
   accusatives, no relative subject; read *quae*. Rendered "if she was fair who received into
   herself the splendor of light." A case slip, not a negation.
3. **0484D (0032) · *Illa igitur cogitatio, et in illorum apertura oculum cordis defigens aperis
   hunc…*** — a nominative *cogitatio* with a second-person verb *aperis*; read *cogitatione*.
   Rendered as closely as the printed words allow ("That thought therefore, and fixing the eye of
   the heart upon the opening of them, thou openest this one…").
4. **0486B (0033) · *quantum **ipse** novit perfecte compati et subvenire*** — masculine *ipse*
   where the subject through the whole sentence is the *sponsa*; read *ipsa*. Rendered "he himself"
   as printed, which makes God the measure of her advance. Note this is **not** the Pattern 9
   gender exemption: English *has* an exponent here (he/she), so choosing "she" would have been a
   silent repair, not a normalization.
5. **0490D (0034) · *Hanc paradisus arborum profert et emittit*** — *Hanc* accusative with
   *paradisus* nominative in the same phrase; read *paradisum* (Migne treats *paradisus* as
   feminine three sentences later, *Haec est paradisus in qua*, and that is attested medieval usage
   — Pattern 9, no marker). Rendered "This paradise of trees she brings forth and sends forth."
6. **0483A (0031) · *sed has ignotas et notas, ad omnes denique homines, ad hos omnes charitas ejus
   dilatata fuit*** — *has* has no governing preposition or verb (read *ad has*). Rendered as
   printed, anacoluthon included.
7. **0488A (0034) · *Per silentium enim excolit in profectu et justitia*** — *excolit* transitive
   with no object; read the passive *excolitur*, "is cultivated," which the next clause's *cultus
   justitiae* supports. Rendered actively as printed.
8. **0486C (0033) · *judicio praecellere et boni odoris famam meretur anteferre*** — active
   *anteferre* where the sense wants the passive *anteferri*. Rendered actively.

## 5. Passages that will not construe — rendered as closely as the printed words allow

**5.1 · 0486B/C (0033), the longest one · *hujus suavitatis fragrantiam, et unctionis spiritualis
quam tam benigne afficitur ad omnes, condolet, gemit, orat et subveniri cupit in naribus superni
sponsi, suavis omnium virtutum, operibus redolet, et placere cognoscitur.*** Two accusatives
(*fragrantiam*, *unctionis* … ) with no governing verb; *quam* where the sense wants the ablative
*qua*; *suavis omnium virtutum* left without a noun to agree with (an *odor* is probably lost).
Rendered clause by clause with the accusatives promoted to the subject of *est … in naribus*, which
is the least invention the printed words permit. **No word was added and none dropped.** Flagged
because a later reader will suspect our English before he suspects the plate, and it is the plate.

**5.2 · 0482B/C (0030) · *Horum leonum, seu pardorum habitatio sunt iniqui, quorum perversos mores
et peccata dum boni non imitantur, sed fugiunt, et interdum tales exemplo et doctrina ad bonum
convertunt. De his montibus et cubilibus … coronantur*** — the *dum* clause never reaches its main
verb, because Migne closes the sentence with a full stop after *convertunt* and starts a new one
whose subject silently shifts to *boni*. Preserved exactly (Pattern 8: never delete a printed
stop): the English hangs where the Latin hangs. The negation *non imitantur* is carried.

**5.3 · 0484B (0031) · *Quae beata, dum ab eorum cordibus quos … deceperat, meritis et
intercessione sua liberavit, de cubilibus seu montibus … coronari perhibetur*** — *ab eorum
cordibus* with an accusative object *quos* gives "she freed from their hearts those whom he had
deceived," which is what is printed; the antecedent of *eorum* is the lions of the preceding
sentence. Rendered literally, with *tectus* ("covered over") kept as the printed nominative
describing the deceiver.

**5.4 · 0486D (0033) · *ita dulcia et suavia erant quae proferebant, vel docebant ad hujusmodi
flores pervolat*** — two clauses fused, with Migne's comma the only mark available. The comma is
kept and no colon or stop supplied (Pattern 8), so the English reads as the anacoluthon it is.

**5.5 · 0484B (0031) · *nullum locum rependi*** — **not a defect, and it looks like one.**
*rependi* here is the gerund of *repere*, "to creep" (rep- + -endi), not a form of *rependere*, "to
repay." Rendered "no place of creeping," of the devil in the Virgin's breast. Logged so that a
later sweep does not "fix" it into a repayment. The whole phrase is a corpus hapax, which is what
made it worth checking rather than assuming.

## 7. Two smaller notes

**7.1 · 0481C (0030) · *praepropere, laute, nimis ardenter, studiose*** — Gregory's five modes of
gluttony (*Moralia* XXX), which the tradition lists as *praepropere, laute, nimis, ardenter,
studiose*. Migne's setting yields four members, with *nimis* absorbed into *ardenter*. Rendered as
four, "over-hastily, sumptuously, too ardently, with studied care." No marker: nothing is broken
type and nothing asserts a falsehood; a dropped comma is not a defect class we mark. Logged
because the count is a real datum about this plate.

**7.2 · 0481D (0030) · *sicut dicit Augustinus, nullum peccatum adeo peccatum veniale est…*** —
the Augustine quotation is introduced with a comma and Migne prints no quotation marks of any
kind. No guillemets supplied (Pattern 6: « » is his, and he has none here) and no straight quotes
either, since the boundary of the quotation is not determinable from the page. The comma stands.
