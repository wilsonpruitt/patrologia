## 1. `[sic:]` — carried type (4)

### 1.1 ⛔ **0035 @0491A (lemma), `Fons nortorum` for Cant. 4:15 *Fons hortorum*** — the range's principal defect

Migne's lemma opens the chapter with **`Fons nortorum`**. *nortorum* is not a Latin word;
the defect sits on the lemma's own governing genitive, so this is Pattern 12 with the
7a⁗-b care (a word-division-class hole exactly the size of a lexeme, and the lexeme is
the one that makes the fountain a fountain *of gardens*).

**What I did.** `*Fountain [sic: nortorum] of gardens, well of living waters, which flow
with a rush from Lebanon [n: (Cant. IV)]*`. The carried run takes **no second italic
marker** because it sits inside the already-italic lemma (Pattern 10's nested-italics
clause), and **"of gardens" stands outside the quarantine in plain English** so the
garden is not lost to the reader — without it the chapter's fountain has nothing to
water and CAPUT XXX loses its connection to the *hortus conclusus* of CAPUT XXIX.

**The corroboration is dense and internal, and does not need a plate read.**
- *hortus* is set **correctly 14 times in chunk 0034 alone** (7 × *hortus* incl. *hortus
  conclusus*, 4 × *hortum*, 2 × *horto*, 1 × *horti*).
- 0035 itself prints *Isti sunt **horti** et sunt conclusi* nine lines below the defective
  lemma.
- 0036's own lemma prints *perfla **hortum** meum* correctly; 0037 prints *in **hortum**
  suum*, *in **hortum** meum*, *hortum meum conclusum*, *hortulanus*.
- **Corpus control (the *Pigmen* method, ratified item 6):** across the full PL TEI
  (5,276 files) `Fons hortorum` occurs in **61 files**, and `nortorum` occurs in
  **one file — this one, this site.** The corpus is its own control.

Conjecture: *hortorum*, certain.

### 2.1 0036 @0493D — Cant. 4:16 `fluent` for *fluant* · **FIRED**

> *Surge, aquilo, et veni, auster; perfla hortum meum, et **fluent** aromata illius.*

One letter, and it is the mood: a **jussive subjunctive** becomes a **future indicative**.
Corpus: *fluant aromata illius* 40 ×, *fluent* 31 × — so the reading circulates widely
and is not a one-off of this plate; that is a fact about transmission, not a reason to
suppress the divergence from the received text (Pattern 14 forbids speculating about
*why* two readings differ).

**Fired, and Richard's own gloss is why.** He reads the clause as consequence, not wish:
*Tunc… **fluunt** aquae gratiae per hortum animae* (0494A), *et cum minus **fluerent**
haec aromata, non minus habebantur* (0494B), ***Fluunt** tunc aromata virtutum tanta
utique ubertate* (0494C), ***Fluunt** tunc ita virtutum aromata, ut…* (0494D). The English
must therefore read "shall flow", and a reader who knows "let the spices flow" has to be
told the plate does not say it.

⭐ **This is the same clause, the same divergence, and the same ruling as 11632 @0401D**
(Philip of Harveng, cruces-0090 §2f) — an independent range in a different Song
commentary fired here too, on the same reasoning from the commentator's own glosses.
Two commentaries reaching the same call from their own plates is the strongest evidence
this range produced that the threshold is set right. Per the §Song ruling the two
Englishes are **not** harmonized; they happen to agree because both plates agree.

### 2.2 0038 @0497D — Cant. 5:1 `Comedite, amici, et inebriamini` · **FIRED**

Migne drops **the middle member** of a three-part invitation: received *Comedite, amici,
et **bibite**, et inebriamini, carissimi*. Corpus: the *bibite* forms dominate heavily;
the bare two-member form is rare.

**Fired, because a dependent gloss hangs on the missing member and then reinstates it.**
Richard's exposition is built as eating-for-friends / drinking-for-dearest: *Amicos
igitur **cibo**… Deus pascit. Charissimos vero plenius reficit et **potat**, imo
abundantiore gratia etiam **inebriat**… De **inebriatione** vero loqui formido* (0500A–B).
So the plate has lost the very verb whose gloss occupies the whole second half of the
chapter, and a reader collating the two would think Richard had invented *potat*.
(*charissimi*/*carissimi* is Pattern 9 spelling — no marker.)

### 2.3 0038 @0498A — Isa. 55:2 `argumentum` for *argentum* · **FIRED**

> *Appendunt enim **argumentum** hujus non in panibus et laborem suum non in saturitate.*

The corpus prints the verse seven times and every one reads *appenditis **argentum** …
non in panibus, et laborem **vestrum** non in saturitate*. **Two changes, weighed
separately:**
- *appendunt / suum* for *appenditis / vestrum* is **Richard's own weave** into third
  person (his subject is *mali*), which the Register's scripture rule protects and no
  marker touches.
- *argumentum* for *argentum* is **material**: "silver" becomes "argument", and the page
  then says the wicked weigh out *the argument of Scripture* not in bread — fluent, and
  not what Isaiah says.

⚠ **Pattern 18 was considered and is wrong here**, on the ruled boundary: the divergence
sits inside a quoted scripture (the `[n: (Isai. LV)]` is on the clause), which is
Pattern 14's territory. **7561 @0664 is the governing precedent** — *modio* for *medio*
in Deut. 13:1, the identical shape (one letter, real word, fluent and false inside a
quotation), ruled into Pattern 14 on 2026-08-15.

### 2.4 0038 @0498C — Job 29:24 `non credebant mihi` · **FIRED (the weakest of the four)**

Corpus: *ridebam ad eos, non credebant* 9 ×, with the added dative *mihi* in **two
files only, this one and 21392**. Fired because a gloss depends on the object: Richard's
frame both before and after is about believing **God himself** — *Si vero rideat ad eos,
**non ei credunt*** … *Non itaque cito, vel temere **ridenti Deo credunt** boni*. Without
*mihi* the verse says only that they did not believe; with it, it says they did not
believe *him*, which is the reading the gloss needs.

⚠ **If the merge trims one marker from this range, trim this one.** It is a pronoun-class
addition and the ratified threshold is suspicious of pronoun-equivalence. I fired it on
the dependent-gloss half of the test, not the sense half. The English rendering ("did not
believe me") is what is printed and stands either way.

## 7. Fixed vocabulary followed against my own judgment

1. ***ferculum* kept in italic Latin at all four occurrences in 0038** (@0498A *tot
   fercula*; @0499D *Ferculum enim est quaelibet meditatio* and *cum hoc ferculo refectus
   es*; @0500A *quando fercula divinitas infert*), per ratified item 3. **The tension,
   stated:** here the word is not Solomon's litter of Cant. 3:9 but a *dish* of food, and
   plain "dish" or "course" would render it cleanly. I followed the ruling anyway, and I
   think it is right on its own merits and not only by fiat: Richard's sentence
   *Ferculum enim est quaelibet meditatio, **quia pascit mentem*** is verbatim the
   feeding gloss (*quae pascit et reficit*) that the ratification says the Latin has to
   keep, so the Latin word is what carries chapter XXXIII back to chapter XXI. An English
   "dish" here would silently cut that thread.
2. ***quies* / *quiesco* — the ratified split applied once, and it went to "stillness".**
   0038 @0501B closes the chapter *Ad hunc modum orandi sive meditandi conor, et suaviter
   **quiesco**, cum adhuc pertingo.* This is the mind's contemplative quiet in a sentence
   about a method of prayer, not the rest of the blessed, so: **"and I am sweetly still"**
   (not "I rest"). Site logged as ratified item 2 requires. No eschatological *requies*
   occurs in my range.
3. **Etymological glosses stay in Latin, with the sense beside them** — the *candidatio*
   precedent, extended to the three name-interpretations in 0035: *Gerare enim
   interpretatur **incolatus visio***, *Libanus qui interpretatur **candidatio***, *aquae
   Jordanis, qui interpretatur **descensus eorum***, plus 0036's *unde aquilo **aquas
   ligans** dicitur*. In each the Latin stands italic and an English sense follows in the
   clear ("*incolatus visio,* the seeing of a sojourning"; "*aquas ligans,* the binder of
   waters"). The etymologies are the argument; an English-only rendering would make
   Richard's derivations explain nothing, and a Latin-only one would leave the reader out.
4. ***pietas* = tenderness** (0036 @0494D *pietatis visceribus*, @0495B *viscera
   pietatis*; 0037 @0495D *pietati divinae*), never "piety" or "mercy", per ratified
   item 5. *misericordia* → mercy and *miserationes* → mercies stay separate (0039
   @0502D, 0038 @0501A).
