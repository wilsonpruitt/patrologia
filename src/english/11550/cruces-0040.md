## 0. The *descriptio sponsi* — the member-by-member vocabulary fixed at the head, per the brief

This range is the catalogue of the Bridegroom's members (Cant. 5:3–14), so each concrete noun was
fixed deliberately, with the hook Richard's gloss hangs on named. **These are the choices a merge
must ratify, because chunks outside the range re-quote several of them.**

| Latin | English | the hook it has to keep |
|---|---|---|
| *comae* (5:11) | **locks** | glossed as *eos qui illi fideli famulatu adhaerent* — persons, so a collective ("hair") would not take *nigrae … dicuntur*, *Qui … se efferunt*. |
| *elatae palmarum* | **the upraised boughs of palms** | ⚠ the gloss is etymological: *quia electi dum semper ad alta virtutum **se efferunt*** (0510A) and *rami productiores semper ad excelsa tendentes*. "Branches" (Douay) loses *efferre* entirely; "upraised boughs" keeps it. |
| *spatulae* | **left in italic Latin** *spatulae* | Richard defines one technical palm-word by another (*Elatae palmarum sunt spatulae, id est rami productiores*). Englishing it would make the definition define nothing — the *ferculum*/*reclinatorium* precedent (ratified item 3). |
| *oculi* (5:12) | **eyes** | glossed *contemplativi … cordis oculis*; also *oculum simplicem* / *fenestras oculorum*, all one word. |
| *rivos aquarum* | **the streams of waters** | *rivi* recurs three times in the gloss (*Solent columbae super rivos … Solent quoque rivi oculos … clariores reddere*). See §1 for the declined `[var:]` against Vulg. *rivulos*. |
| *fluenta plenissima* | **the fullest floods** | needs to stay distinct from *rivos*: the doves sit *super rivos* but *juxta fluenta*, and the whole *quies* argument (§5) hangs on the difference of the two prepositions. |
| *genae* (5:13) | **cheeks** | *quasi genae in facie sponsi rubent* — the martyrs' blush; "jaws" (some Vulg. glossators) would kill it. |
| *areolae aromatum* | **garden-beds of spices** | must be a **planted** bed (*consitae*, and *Ab his pigmentariis consitae sunt hae areolae*). ⚠ deliberately NOT "little beds": *lectulus* owns "little bed" in this work and the collision would be invisible to a reader. |
| *pigmentarii* / *pigmentum* | **perfumers** / **pigment** | follows the already-shipped 0010–0011 renderings (*pigmentarius* = perfumer, *pigmentum* = pigment), which is what keeps Richard's *Pigmentarii sunt praedicatores … pretiosum pigmentum … composuerunt* coherent with chunk 0010's *Pigmen* passage. |
| *labia* (5:13) | **lips** | glossed *praedicatores, quia verba ejus loquuntur*. |
| *myrrha prima* / *myrrha probatissima* | **the first myrrh** / **the most approved myrrh** | ⚠ *probatissima* had to keep the *probare* root: the gloss at 0505B is *quia pura **approbat**, impura **reprobat*** and at 0504B *pura intentione*. Douay's "choice myrrh" breaks that; "most approved" holds it. *prima* stays literal because Richard argues from it twice (*in primis seipsos mortificant*; *Myrrha prima est optima*). |
| *manus tornatiles aureae* (5:14) | **hands turned on the lathe, golden** | the gloss is *Sicut **tornatura** recte et polite volvitur* — the lathe has to be visible in the English or the comparison has no antecedent. Douay's "as of gold" drops *tornatiles* altogether. |
| *hyacinthis* | **hyacinths** | glossed as the STONE (*lapis est aerei coloris et viridis*), not the flower; kept as the mineral name. |
| *venter* | **belly** | one word for the lemma at 0503C (*venter meus intumuit*, glossed *ipsa mens in qua cogitationes excoquuntur, sicut cibi in ventre*) and at 0514B (*Venter ejus eburneus*). "Womb" would break the food image. |
| *pessulum* / *sera* / *reserata* | **bolt** / **bar** / **unbarred** | 0505C's gloss defines *pessulum* by *sera* and then turns on *nisi illa **reserata***; bar/unbarred keeps the pair audible. |
| *foramen* | **opening** | one English word across 0503C (grace as through a chink, *rima* = chink) and 0513D (*unum foramen inimico … relinquunt*). |
| *vigiles* (lemma 5:7) vs *custodes* (gloss) | **watchmen** vs **keepers** | deliberately different, so the divergence marked in §1 is visible in the English too. |

## 1. Scripture divergences from the received text (Pattern 14 / 7a″)

Every named lemma word in the launch prompt was collated word by word against the Vulgate, and, where
memory was not enough, against the full PL TEI corpus as its own control (the method ratified in brief
item 6: file counts across 5,276 files).

### Marked `[var: …]`

**0503C (0040) · *et venter meus **intumuit** ad tactum ejus* (Cant. 5:4).**
Vulgate: *intremuit*, "trembled." Migne prints *intumuit*, "swelled." Corpus control: *venter meus
intremuit* occurs in **31** files, *venter meus intumuit* in **one — this one.** Rendered as printed
("my belly swelled"), marked `[var: Vulg. *intremuit*, "trembled"]`. **Material, and the gloss is on
the other side of it:** Richard's own exposition of this very lemma ends *quo tangatur et accendatur ad
amorem, et **contremiscat**, et metuat* (0503D) — he is expounding a trembling that the printed lemma
no longer contains.

**0506B (0041) · *Ascendet homo ad cor altum, et exaltabitur Deus* [n: (Psal. LX)]** (Ps 63:7).
Vulgate: *Accedet homo ad cor altum*. Corpus control: *Accedet/accedet homo ad cor altum* 46 files,
*Ascendet* 3. Rendered as printed ("Man shall ascend"), marked `[var: Vulg. *Accedet homo*, "man shall
come"]`. **Dependent gloss, immediately:** *Quantum enim homo ad Deum **ascendit**, tantum eum sublimem
… invenit.* The whole point is ascent, and the Vulgate's *accedet* would not supply it.

**0506B (0041) · *Quaesivi sapientiam, et ipsa longius recessit a me* [n: (Eccle. VII)]** (Eccl 7:24).
Vulgate: *Dixi: Sapiens efficiar, et ipsa longius recessit a me*. Corpus control: *Quaesivi sapientiam,
et ipsa longius* occurs in **no other file**; *Sapiens efficiar* in 15. Rendered as printed, marked
`[var: Vulg. *Dixi: Sapiens efficiar*, "I said, I shall be made wise"]`. Material: the chapter is about
**seeking** (*Quaesivit sponsa per se dilectum*), and the printed substitution puts the argument's own
verb into the proof-text.

**0507B (0041) · *Invenerunt me **vigiles** qui **circumierunt** civitatem* (Cant. 5:7).**
Vulgate: *custodes qui circumeunt civitatem*. Corpus control: *custodes qui circum-* 11 files,
*vigiles qui circum-* 2. Rendered as printed ("The watchmen who went about the city"), marked
`[var: Vulg. *custodes qui circumeunt*, "the keepers who go about"]`. ⚠ **The tell is in Richard's own
next sentence**, which re-quotes the lemma as *invenerunt eam **custodes**, qui circumierunt civitatem*
— the gloss word and the lemma word differ on the same page. The English keeps them different
(watchmen / keepers) so the reader can see it without the apparatus; *custodes murorum* at 0507C is
"keepers of the walls" to stay with the gloss.

**0511C (0043) · *sitivit anima mea ad Deum **fontem** vivum* [n: (Psal. XLI.)]** (Ps 41:3).
Vulgate: *ad Deum **fortem** vivum*, "the strong living God." Rendered as printed ("after God, the
living fountain"), marked `[var: Vulg. *fortem*, "the strong living God"]`. **Dependent gloss:** the
sentence introducing it is *dulce poculum de illo **fonte vivo** hauriunt, et ipsum **fontem** sitiunt*
— Richard's image is drawn out of the divergent word itself. ⚠ Recorded for the record: *fontem* is
the **dominant** PL transmission of this verse (40 files against 7 for *fortem*), so the marker names
the Vulgate as witness and asserts nothing about how the reading got here (Pattern 14's "never
speculate").

## 2. Defective type carried through — `[sic: …]` (Patterns 7, 10, 12) with the 7a⁗ check

Two sites in the whole range. For each, the English clause was read with the marker's contents deleted
and the verb / negation / governing word confirmed to survive outside the quarantine.

**0506C (0041) · *emoliescunt fortia*.**
*emoliescunt* is not a word (for *emollescunt*, "grow soft"). Corpus control: *emoliescunt* occurs in
**one file — this one**; *emollescunt* in 2. Carried in italics, and because the marked run was the
clause's **only predicate** (7a⁗ q1) the sense stands in plain English beside it:
`hard things are softened [sic: *emoliescunt*]`. The conjecture is near-certain from the same sentence's
*mollescunt* eight words later.

**0510C (0043) · *ne nos scandalizet, et in errorem subtilitate **nox a** perducat*.**
⚠ **A word-division defect — the 7a⁗-b shape.** `nox a` is *noxia* split across a space (agreeing with
*subtilitate*: "by harmful subtlety"). The whole broken run is carried per Pattern 10, spacing included,
including the fragment `a` which is itself a real Latin word. Quarantining it takes out an **adjective**,
not a force-carrier: the clause keeps its *ne*, its subjunctive *perducat* and its object *in errorem*
outside the marker, so the negative-purpose force is intact — but the adjective's sense is supplied
beside the marker anyway, because "by subtlety" alone would make the right eye's own subtlety the
danger:
`lest it scandalize us and lead us into error by harmful [sic: *nox a*] subtlety`.
*noxia/noxium* occurs correctly four times elsewhere in this work (0015, 0020, 0023, 0034) and once more
in this very chunk (*noxium reputandum*, 0510D), which is what makes the reading certain.

**Checked and NOT marked — the *pigment-* family, per the brief's warning.** Every occurrence in the
range was read character by character: 0044 prints *pigmentariis* ×4 (once in the lemma, three times in
the gloss), *Pigmentarii* ×1, *pigmentum* ×1. **All six are whole words.** No truncation of the 0431C
`Pigmen` kind anywhere in 0040–0044. Likewise *quiquid* (twice at 0510D) takes no marker: it stands in
**40** PL files, so it is an attested spelling and Pattern 9 governs.

## 3. `[cj: …]` — the printed word is real and the faithful English misleads (Pattern 18)

**0503D and 0504A (0040) · *amplius **ignoscit** ad dilectionem Dei et proximi* · *magis ad hoc
**ignoscit***.**
*ignoscit* ("pardons") is a real verb, so `[sic:]` has nothing to wrap; but *ignoscere* cannot govern
*ad* + accusative, and the sense the passage requires is *ignescit*, "catches fire" — the chapter is
about being kindled (*accendatur ad amorem* in the same sentence, *Accenditur etiam ad mortificandum*
just below, and the chapter head is *Quomodo gratia … accendit*). Corpus control: *ignescit* stands in
60 PL files, *ignoscit ad amorem* in none.
`she pardons the more [cj: *ignoscit*; read *ignescit*, "catches fire"] unto the love of God and of
neighbor` (and identically at 0504A).
⚠ **This follows an already-shipped precedent inside the work, and that is the reason it was applied
rather than declined.** Chunk 0004 (0414C, *fervens amplius ignoscit*) is live with exactly this gloss.
The site is not obviously inside the ratified `[cj:]` bar (item 7: negations and correlatives), so if
the merge decides to withdraw it, **all three sites must be withdrawn together** — 0004, 0503D, 0504A.
Two Englishes for one defect in one work is the drift the within-work reconciliation step exists to
prevent.

### Named `[cj: …]` census candidates — declined, per ratified item 7 (do not widen the bar)

- ⭐ **0511D (0043) · *Unde juxta fluenta haec **resident**, quia hanc quietem mentis nondum habent.***
  **The one site in this range I would put to Wilson.** Two sentences earlier Richard has equated the
  sitting with the stillness — *sessio haec juxta fluenta requiem eorum significat, **sedere enim
  quiescentis est*** — so of the imperfect, who by his own statement have no stillness of mind, the page
  now says they DO sit. Conjecture: a dropped *non* (*non resident*), which is glossable under Pattern
  18's 7561 @0648 clause (the gloss opens on the word Migne did print). It is the **negation** class,
  where item 7 says incoherence is no defence — **and it was still declined**, because the alternative
  reading is live: *juxta* (beside, not upon) may itself be the concession, the imperfect being at the
  margin of the floods without being washed in the streams. Where a reading is merely uncertain it stays
  in the crux. **Rendered exactly as printed, with no repair anywhere in the English.**
- **0505B (0040) · *Digiti vero **sponsi** pleni sunt myrrha probatissima*.** The fingers under
  discussion are the **bride's** throughout the lemma (*digiti mei*) and throughout the gloss (*discrete
  … agit omnia*, *cor suum*), so *sponsae* is expected. Rendered as printed ("the fingers of the
  Bridegroom"); declined — a genitive slip is neither a negation nor a correlative.
- **0512C (0044) · *in triumphante rubent speciali decore per **areolam***.** Conjecture *aureolam*, the
  martyr's **aureole** — which is the standard reward-word for martyrs in the triumphant Church, and
  which is what "a special comeliness" is waiting for. *areolam* is a real word and the lemma's own
  word (*areolae aromatum*), so the page reads "blush … through the garden-bed," which is odd rather
  than false. Rendered as printed; declined. ⚠ Recorded prominently because a reader will trip here and
  the conjecture is strong.
- **0504B (0040) · *et melior **acta** plus sibi vilescit*.** Conjecture *facta*, "made better."
  *acta* is a real participle and occupies its slot; rendered "being made better." Declined.
- **0511A (0043) · *et **munditia** terrena et carnalis*.** In a list of what spiritual consolation makes
  bitter and cheap, "earthly and carnal **cleanness**" is the opposite of the argument; conjecture
  *immunditia*, "uncleanness." Rendered as printed. Declined: not a negation in Migne's type but a
  missing prefix on a real word, and item 7's near-miss list (*spem* for *sepem*, *effectum* for
  *affectum*) is exactly this shape.
- **0511D (0043) · *ut cum spiritu coeperint carne **consumment***** (Gal 3:3, woven). Active
  *consumment* for the Vulgate's passive *consummemini/consummentur*. Rendered "they consummate in the
  flesh." Declined (voice, not negation — item 7's explicit scope note).
- **0510A (0043) · *dum **innumeris tribulationis** angustatur*.** *tribulationis* (genitive singular)
  where *tribulationibus* is wanted by *innumeris*. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow
  ("straitened by innumerable tribulation"), nothing supplied. Declined — a case ending, no marker
  class fits.

- **0505C (0041) · *Ita haec minima, nisi rescindantur, non dignatur dilectus ita venire ad animam…***

## 5. Fixed vocabulary — followed, with the tensions logged

- ***quies*/*requies* — the ratified split was exercised hard in 0043, and the seam is real here.**
  Eschatological → **"rest"**: *videant **requiem illam** quia bona* (0511B), *futuram **requiem**
  desiderant* (0511B), *Quam **requiem** nequaquam per contemplationem viderent* (0511B). Contemplative
  → **"stillness"**: *si non **requiem mentis** haberent* (0511B), *sessio haec … **requiem** eorum
  significat, sedere enim **quiescentis** est* (0511C), *quietem mentis et puritatem cordis* (0511C),
  *hanc **quietem mentis** nondum habent* (0511D), *requies mentis acquiritur* (0512A). ⚠ Note that
  **one sentence carries both** (*Quam requiem … si non requiem mentis haberent*), which is the clearest
  evidence yet that the distinction is in the Latin and not our drift.
- ***vacare* = "to be at leisure"** at 0514C (*ut suae libidini **vacent*** → "that they are at leisure
  for their own lust"), per the table, even though "give themselves over to" reads better. Logged as a
  place the table was followed against the ear.
- ***meditari* = "meditate"** (0514D, *superna **meditantur** et sapiunt*); ***compunctio* =
  "compunction"** (0503B); ***affectus* = "affection"** throughout 0503B and 0511D–0512A (*dulcis
