## CHAPTER VIII. *On the manner and measure of the boards.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ There follows: *Thou shalt make also the standing boards of the tabernacle of setim wood, which severally shall have ten cubits in length and in breadth one apiece, and a half [n: (Exod. XXVI)]*. ⟦s2⟧ So much for the measure of the boards; and at once something is attached concerning their mortising. ⟦s3⟧ Now there are two kinds of mortising. ⟦s4⟧ The first, where one side of the board is hollowed and the other sharpened, and thus one board is fixed into another. ⟦s5⟧ But mortising of this kind makes the surface flat on neither [0217C] part. ⟦s6⟧ There is also another kind of mortising, which is on both sides. ⟦s7⟧ For on this hand a certain cutting-away of the side is made as far as the middle of the board on the inner part; on that hand likewise, but from the outer part; and the cutting of the boards is set into the cutting, so that the whole wall may seem one board, and the surface on either hand be rendered wholly flat. ⟦s8⟧ Of mortising of this kind is understood what follows: *In the sides of the board let there be made two mortisings, whereby board may be connected to board.* ⟦s9⟧ Now there were twenty boards with forty bases on the southern side, and as many, and of the same measure, on the northern side. ⟦s10⟧ But on the western part there were eight boards with sixteen bases. ⟦s11⟧ For each board had two silver bases [0217D] set under it. ⟦s12⟧ There are those who hand down that the corner boards on either hand have only half a cubit. ⟦s12⟧ But whence this may be proved they cannot show. ⟦s13⟧ But they attend too little, as it seems to me, to the general statement which they read at the beginning of the description: *Thou shalt make,* he says, *also the standing boards for the tabernacles of setim woods, which severally shall have ten cubits in length, and in breadth one apiece and a half.* ⟦s14⟧ If each has a cubit and a half in breadth, then the corner boards also. ⟦s15⟧ Or is it perhaps that, because they are treated of separately, they are rightly said to have a measure of their own? ⟦s16⟧ Nay, so much the more is it to be denied; since when he treats of them apart, in nothing does he except their measure from the general rule. ⟦s17⟧ To whom that which we have said of the boards is not enough, let him hear what the author [0218A] says far afterward of the bases. ⟦s18⟧ There were besides a hundred talents of silver, out of which were cast the bases of the sanctuary, and of the entrance where the veil hangs. ⟦s19⟧ A hundred bases were made of the hundred talents, one talent being reckoned to each base. ⟦s20⟧ If the corner boards had only a third part of the breadth, how did they receive bases equal with the rest? ⟦s21⟧ But they say that the tabernacle holds only ten cubits of breadth, and thence they gather what has already been said of the breadth of the corner boards. ⟦s22⟧ That the tabernacle has thirty cubits of length, whence it may be proved I do find. ⟦s23⟧ But that it holds only ten cubits of breadth, whence it may be proved I do not find. ⟦s24⟧ Yet I will say what is wont to strike the mind on that head, whence we can conjecture this rather than prove it. [0218B] We believe that that temple of Solomon was made, after its own measure, after the likeness of this tabernacle. ⟦s25⟧ According to the reasoning of the likeness, therefore, the length of the tabernacle will be triple to the breadth. ⟦s26⟧ According to the reasoning of the likeness the length of the inner house will be equal to its breadth, but half of the length of the outer house. ⟦s27⟧ Yet we can hold unshaken both that which we conjecture of the breadth of the tabernacle and that which we read generally of the measure of the boards, if we say that the corner boards fill up, with half a cubit on either hand, the tenth cubit which is left over above the six boards, and exceed by one cubit on either side. ⟦s28⟧ For thence, beyond doubt, the tabernacle stood the more sturdily, and bore the driving of the winds the more stoutly. ⟦s29⟧ Such an arrangement, I believe, he wished to designate who said: [0218C] *But toward the western quarter of the tabernacle thou shalt make six boards; and again, two others, which shall be set up at the corners behind the tabernacle;* and again, of the same he made six boards, and two others at each corner of the tabernacle behind. ⟦s30⟧ For in respect of the walls on either hand they are said to be behind, or after the tabernacle. ⟦s31⟧ For if they are joined to the walls only from the side, there will be nothing in respect of which they may be called behind, or at the back. ⟦s32⟧ According to what has been said, therefore, the western front of the tabernacle will be of ten cubits of breadth within, and of twelve cubits of breadth without. ⟦s33⟧ See whether perhaps this be what Ezekiel wished to be understood, when in the description of the temple he attaches, among the rest (and he measured the fronts, six cubits of breadth on this hand, and [0218D] six cubits of breadth on that), the breadth of the tabernacle [n: (Ezech. IV)]. ⟦s34⟧ This we have expounded more fully in its own place. ⟦s35⟧ But here only this is to be attended to, that the breadth of the tabernacle is called a doubled six.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And it is to be noted that the eastern part of the temple had as much within, about the gate, as the whole breadth of the western part of the tabernacle without. ⟦s2⟧ But it is to be held beyond doubt, according to the reading of Exodus, that there were five bars through all three walls of the tabernacle: one about the top of the boards, another about the bottom, a third about the middle, a fourth between the middle and the top, a fifth between the middle and the bottom. ⟦s3⟧ For that opinion of certain men about bars of five cubits seems so frivolous, and so contrary to authority, that it does not even seem to need refuting.

## CHAPTER IX. *On the erection of the tabernacle.*

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0219A]

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It remains still to say those things about the tabernacle which are to be added concerning the erection of it. ⟦s2⟧ First, then, the boards were raised upon the bases; afterward the bars of the walls were drawn in through the circles. ⟦s3⟧ Then was inserted that bar which, for the holding together of the walls, went through the middle of the boards, according to the book, from corner to corner, where at the entrance of the tabernacle the veil hung down. ⟦s4⟧ After these, cords thrown over were stretched strongly across the tabernacle, and were firmly fastened to pegs fixed at a distance from the tabernacle. ⟦s5⟧ And so it came about that they both held the tabernacle itself firm and unshaken against the violence of the winds, and bore up its covering, lest in the upper parts it should make a belly [0219B] and gather the waters of overflowing rains; lest in turn, by the increase of the gathering, the hollow itself, and by the increase of that hollow, the gathering of the waters should grow, until at last the excess of the weight should altogether burst the covering, or, tearing it away, drag it inward and downward to itself. ⟦s6⟧ But on the western quarter the cords, hooked from the inner part, came down slantwise over the top of the walls to the lower parts, and, being firmly fastened to pegs, held the tabernacle on that side. ⟦s7⟧ Likewise on the eastern quarter we ought to understand that cords come down from the said bar and from the ends of the walls, and, stretched out from the pegs, hold the tabernacle on that side. ⟦s8⟧ Those who wrap the tabernacle as it were in the swaddling-clothes of infancy cannot find to what uses it has pegs, [0219C] although about these they read in this manner, if they will: *All the vessels of the tabernacle for all uses and ceremonies; both its pegs and those of the court thou shalt make of brass [n: (Exod. XXVII)]*.
