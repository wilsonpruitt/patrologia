## CHAPTER XI. *Description of the altar of holocaust.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ There follows concerning the altar of holocaust, which is described in this manner: *Thou shalt make also an altar of setim woods, [0220D] which shall have five cubits in length, and as many in breadth, that is, foursquare, and three cubits in height. ⟦s2⟧ And the horns at the four corners shall be of the same [n: (Exod. XXVII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ This altar had only four walls. ⟦s4⟧ For above it was open; and, as is read in what follows, it was not solid, but empty and hollow within. ⟦s5⟧ All these things are plain enough. ⟦s6⟧ But the things that follow about the grate are very intricate, and are for that reason to be examined the more diligently. ⟦s7⟧ I say therefore first what seems to me; and thereafter I shall try to prove that same thing by the authority of the letter itself. ⟦s8⟧ First, then, let two rods of brass, of such thickness as to be able to bear up the holocausts and the wood laid upon them, be carried from the one side of the altar to the other, so that their heads may appear outside near [0221A] the corners, and may have four brazen rings set into them. ⟦s9⟧ And they ought to be set in so that the one half of the altar be above and the other below. ⟦s10+11⟧ And from these there ought to rise inward four brazen posts, as it were four feet of the grate: two, namely, on this hand and beside the one side; two on that, beside the opposite side. ⟦s12⟧ Upon the top of these, certainly, the grate ought to be made, of brass, in the manner of a net, according to the book. ⟦s13⟧ And let it be made at such a height that it and the altar answer one another to one equality. ⟦s14⟧ Those rods first spoken of were therefore as it were the bases of the grate, and the four posts as it were its feet. ⟦s15⟧ And all these ought to be so set into one another and joined together that they make as it were one body, and can be torn away neither from one another nor from the altar. ⟦s16⟧ And there were [0221B] two little doors in the hinder wall, namely in the upper half of the altar. ⟦s17⟧ The one hung from the one side, and the other from the other, and when shut they touched each other in the midst. ⟦s18⟧ But the hearth of the altar was nothing else than a certain brazen plate, somewhat thick. ⟦s19⟧ Nor was the hearth brought in through the approach of the said doors, and laid upon the bases of the grate and upon the threshold of the door; yet first that whole lower half of the altar was filled with earth up to the place of the grate. ⟦s20⟧ Through this approach the wood was brought in, whereby the fire was fed; through the same approach the ashes were drawn out. ⟦s21⟧ And the altar itself was filled with earth up to the middle for this reason: that it might firmly stand [sic: *acret*] during the sacrificing, and that this very thing might accord [sic: *stacederet*] with that command of the Lord: *You shall make me an altar of earth [n: (Exod. XX)]*. ⟦s22⟧ But the grate [0221C] was to the altar itself in place of an upper surface, where the holocaust was wont to be laid on and, fire being set beneath, to be burned. ⟦s23⟧ With an empty suspicion, therefore, do they labor who think that sacrifice could not be made upon the altar without steps or some raising up. ⟦s24⟧ For it had only three cubits of height. ⟦s25⟧ But now we ought to consider whether Scripture itself agrees with those things which we have already said of the grate. ⟦s26⟧ Of the grate it is read thus: *And a grate of brass in the manner of a net,* where "thou shalt make" is understood, *through whose four corners there shall be four brazen rings, which thou shalt place beneath the hearth of the altar.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Note that the rings were at the corners of the grate, and beneath the hearth of the altar. ⟦s2⟧ And it is added: *And the grate shall be as far as the middle of the altar.* ⟦s3⟧ If as far as the middle of the altar, [0221D] then either from the bottom upward, or from the top downward, as far as its middle. ⟦s4⟧ Let us compare with this place what is read far afterward of the same grate, when it is said: *And its grate he made of brass in the manner of a net; and beneath it, in the middle of the altar, the hearth, four rings being cast at as many tops of the network [n: (Exod. XXXVIII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ If the hearth was beneath the grate and in the middle of the altar, then the grate held the upper half of the altar; otherwise, it was itself beneath the hearth.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is to be noted that in one respect the bases were of the grate, and in another respect were not of the grate. ⟦s2⟧ In one respect they seem not to be of the grate, because grates are not wont to have bases. ⟦s3⟧ Yet in another respect they were of it, because it sat upon them. ⟦s4⟧ Rightly [0222A] therefore is it said of the hearth that it was beneath the grate, although it lay upon the bases: *Four rings being cast,* he says, *at as many tops of the network.* ⟦s5⟧ Sometimes "top" means the same as "extremity." ⟦s6⟧ They were therefore either at the upper extremities or at the lower. ⟦s7⟧ But this very thing is determined from what is said in the first description: *Through whose four corners there shall be four brazen rings, which thou shalt place beneath the hearth of the altar.* ⟦s8⟧ If the hearth was beneath the grate and the rings beneath the hearth, then they were at the lower tops. ⟦s9⟧ For if they were at the upper tops, then, in order that the hearth might have the rings beneath it, it would have to have the top of the grate also, and so the whole grate, beneath it. ⟦s10⟧ And note that if the rings were beneath the grate and at its corners, then [0222B] something of the grate was beneath the hearth, where the corners could be, and whence the rings could hang down, so that they might be under it. ⟦s11⟧ They were, therefore, where the bases lying beneath the grate and the posts rising on high made corners, because they hung from the extremities of the bases. ⟦s12⟧ The rings, therefore, were beneath the hearth — not because it lay upon them, but because by position it held the higher place. ⟦s13⟧ It remains now only to ask this: whether the same rings are both the altar's and the grate's. ⟦s14⟧ Let us hear, then, what is added to the things already said in the first description: *Thou shalt make also two bars for the altar of setim woods, which thou shalt cover with plates of brass, and shalt draw in through the circles.* ⟦s15⟧ They shall be on either side of the altar, for carrying it. ⟦s16⟧ If the bars are on either side of the altar, then the circles also, through which the bars too are to be drawn. ⟦s17⟧ The grate, therefore, [0222C] has rings, and the altar circles. ⟦s18⟧ But are the circles of the one and the rings of the other different, each from each? ⟦s19⟧ With these things, then, which are said in this description, let us compare, if it please, those which are set out about the same in the recapitulation: *Four rings being cast,* he says, *at as many tops of the network, for putting in the bars for carrying it: which bars he also made of setim woods, and covered with plates of brass, and drew them [sic: incirculos] which stood out on the sides of the altar [n: (ibid.)]*. ⟦s20⟧ Certainly, as you hear, he made bars for putting into the rings of the grate, which same, he says, he also drew into the circles of the altar. ⟦s21⟧ Certainly the grate was within the altar, beneath which, in the middle of the altar, was the hearth. ⟦s22⟧ But were the rings that hung from the tops of the grate likewise within [0222D] the altar? ⟦s23⟧ How, then, could those same bars be put into the rings that lay hidden within, and be drawn into the circles that stood out without on the sides of the altar? ⟦s24⟧ But who would say this? ⟦s25⟧ It must be, therefore, that the rings of the grate and the circles of the altar are the very same, and that in them are the very same bars, and not different ones. ⟦s26⟧ It must be, therefore, that the tops of the grate itself also appear outwardly through the middle of the walls, from which hung the circles that stood out on the sides of the altar. ⟦s27⟧ See: what we said above of the altar, or of its grate, we have proved by the authority of the letter itself. ⟦s28⟧ But what has been said of the approach of the little doors can be gathered not so much by manifest authority as by manifest reason, as has already been in part shown above.
