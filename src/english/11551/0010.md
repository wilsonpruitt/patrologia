## SECOND TREATISE. On the temple of Solomon, according to the letter. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is to be noted that nothing is said concerning the measure of the bases; but from what is read in this book, or in Paralipomenon, how great it is, is easily discovered. ⟦s2⟧ For according to this book the *stylus* itself had eighteen cubits, and the two capitals held eight cubits. ⟦s3⟧ But according to Paralipomenon the whole column had thirty-five cubits, whence it is also established that its base had nine cubits. ⟦s4⟧ At the beginning of this description it is read that the temple had thirty cubits in height. ⟦s5⟧ If therefore the height of the porch answered to the height of the temple, how could columns of thirty-five cubits in height [0240D] be contained in it? ⟦s6⟧ From the mutual comparison of these statements, therefore, it is plainly gathered that one went up into the porch by steps, and that in that place where the columns stood, the ground was five cubits lower than the pavement of the porch itself. ⟦s7⟧ Now let those things be brought into the midst which are read at the end of Jeremiah concerning the said nets, or pomegranates, lest perchance they seem to someone to be at odds with what has been said above: *And nets,* he says, *and the pomegranates were surrounded by a hundred nets. ⟦s8⟧ And nets, and ninety and six pomegranates hanging. ⟦s9⟧ And all the pomegranates were surrounded by a hundred nets [n: (Jer. LII)]*. ⟦s10⟧ As has been said above, in one net there were a hundred pomegranates set between in the middle of it, and a hundred hanging in the one. ⟦s11⟧ But from what occasion it came about that there were not [0241A] more than ninety-six hanging there in the time of Jeremiah, I confess I do not know. ⟦s12⟧ According to what he has here given to be understood, one net was composed of a hundred nets; he means one order of chainings weaving round one order of openings to be understood as one net. ⟦s13⟧ If you count from the top downward, you will find fourteen openings in one order. ⟦s14⟧ If you count round about, you will find a hundred openings in one order. ⟦s15⟧ There were therefore fourteen nets there according to one consideration, and a hundred according to another consideration. ⟦s16⟧ There were nets of seven [0242A] rows as far as the pomegranates set between in the middle. ⟦s17⟧ Whence he also says: *And nets, and the pomegranates were surrounded by a hundred nets.* ⟦s18⟧ And there were other nets of seven rows as far as the pomegranates hanging at the lowest parts. ⟦s19⟧ Whence he also adds: *And nets, and ninety-six pomegranates hanging.* ⟦s20⟧ But whether the lower pomegranates hung in the outermost openings, or beneath them, could be doubted; but it is determined by his subjoining the general statement, when he says: *And all the pomegranates were surrounded by a hundred nets.*
