## THIRD TREATISE. On the harmony of the times of the kings reigning together over Juda and over Israel. To the blessed Bernard, abbot of Clairvaux.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0241]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0241B]In those things which are said in the book of Kings concerning the length, [sic: *vevaria*] succession of those who reign, much contrariety, as you say, seems to be present; whence also the reckoning of the numbers is thought to be corrupted by the fault of the scribes. ⟦s2⟧ Whence also, before I should write anything on these matters according to your request, I consulted the writings of the Jews by means of the Jews, and I learned that both their writings and ours run together into one meaning. ⟦s3⟧ It is established with me, then, that there is no contrariety in these things, although the truth itself has hitherto lain hidden even from them. ⟦s4⟧ Far be it from me to believe that in the divine Scriptures the truth has anywhere so gone astray that it cannot be recovered at all! ⟦s5⟧ First of all, then, it is to be known that when it is read that some king reigned so many or so many years, it must not always be so understood as though [0241C] each one so completed the last year of his reign that no shortfall remained to its completion. ⟦s6⟧ But if that year which was divided between the successor and the predecessor were ascribed to neither of them, a great part would be taken away from the truth of the sum of the reckoning. ⟦s7⟧ Quite fittingly, then, and compendiously, to avoid trifling fractions, Scripture attributes a year common to two to him who obtained the greater part of that year. ⟦s8⟧ Likewise, if we attend diligently, we shall find for certain that what we often see done in our own times was frequent in those times. ⟦s9⟧ We often see that kings crown their sons while they are still alive, in order to forestall the contradiction and rebellion of their subjects. ⟦s10⟧ This was frequent among the kings of Israel, since they feared lest through some rebellion their kingdom [0241D] should return to the house of David. ⟦s11⟧ Concerning this, indeed, although it is not everywhere openly said in [sic: *Sciptura*], yet there is found in it that from which it may easily be proved in its own places.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And it is to be noted that the times of those gathering together are sometimes attributed to the father alone, sometimes to the son alone, sometimes to both son and father. ⟦s2⟧ For there are those who, although they have already crowned their sons, in no way wish them to exercise royal power while they themselves live. ⟦s3⟧ Deservedly therefore is such a time of reigning attributed [0242B] to the father alone. ⟦s4⟧ There are those who, after the crowning of their sons, now because of an infirmity of body, now of mind, give themselves over to leisure, are slaves to luxury, and commit the affairs of the kingdom in all things to their sons. ⟦s5⟧ Are not these times of reigning more rightly ascribed to the son than to the father? ⟦s6⟧ But when they handled the affairs of the kingdom in common, and defended the kingdom against rebels now together, now separately (as the Roman emperors also are read to have done in their times), are not these times of reigning deservedly ascribed to both? ⟦s7⟧ See: Hieroboam, king of Israel, is said to have reigned twenty-two years, and his son Nadab two years. ⟦s8⟧ But if these two are said to have filled out twenty-six years in reigning, what is said about the comparison of the two kingdoms will in no way be able to stand. ⟦s9⟧ For if Asa, king of Juda, reigned [0242C] in the twentieth year of king Hieroboam, how could Nadab reign, as is read, in the second year of the reign of Asa, and Baasa in the third? ⟦s10⟧ But if we understand that Asa began his reign at the end of the twentieth year, so that the year which is Hieroboam's twenty-first is the first for king Asa, and that which is Hieroboam's twenty-second is also the second for king Asa, and the same is the beginning of Nadab's reigning and his first year, so that he reigns one year with his father but the greater part of the following year without his father — if, I say, you are willing so to take it, you will see that in all this there is nothing of contrariety.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Likewise it is said that Baasa, who slew Nadab, beginning to reign in the third year of the reign of Asa, reigned twenty-four years. ⟦s2⟧ It is understood therefore that Baasa lasted until the twenty-seventh year of king Asa, [0242D] although he completed it only in its greater part. ⟦s3⟧ How then is Hela, son of Baasa, said to have begun to reign in the twenty-sixth year of the reign of king Asa, and to have reigned two years, unless because he reigned the twenty-sixth year of king Asa and the first part of the twenty-seventh year with his father, and the latter part of the same year without his father. ⟦s4⟧ For at the end of that twenty-seventh year Zamri slew him, and reigned in his stead seven days. ⟦s5⟧ But it is read that, when [0243A] the army had heard that Zamri had rebelled and had slain the king, all Israel made Zamri king over itself. ⟦s6⟧ It is established therefore that the same year was Zamri's first of reigning which was king Asa's twenty-eighth. ⟦s7⟧ What then is it that is read of him, that he reigned in the thirty-first year of the reign of Asa? ⟦s8⟧ But we know that, when Thebni rebelled, half the people followed the one and half the other. ⟦s9⟧ What therefore is said, that he reigned in the thirty-first year of Asa, is understood to be said of that time when, Thebni being now dead, he began to reign over the whole. ⟦s10⟧ But Scripture says that he reigned twelve years. ⟦s11⟧ If therefore Asa's twenty-eighth year was Zamri's first of reigning, it is established that Asa's thirty-ninth year was this man's twelfth in the kingdom. ⟦s12⟧ How then is it established that Achab, [0243B] the son of that same Zamri, reigned, as is read, in the thirty-eighth year of Asa, unless because without doubt Achab reigned two years with his father? ⟦s13⟧ And there accords with this judgment the fact that Josaphat, son of king Asa, is said to have reigned in the fourth year of the reign of Achab. ⟦s14⟧ For the same year which was Asa's forty-first proved to be Achab's fourth in the kingdom. ⟦s15⟧ But Scripture relates that king Asa died in the forty-first year of his reign, and that Josaphat his son reigned in his stead. ⟦s16⟧ But Achab reigned twenty-two years, whence also the same year was Josaphat's eighteenth in the kingdom which proved to be Achab's twenty-second and last. ⟦s17⟧ But Ochozias, son of Achab, reigned, as is read, in the seventeenth [0243C] year of the reign of Josaphat, and Ochozias' brother Joram in the eighteenth year of the same — the former, that is, in the next-to-last year of his father, and the other in that same father's last year. ⟦s18⟧ How then was it true that Ochozias reigned two years, or how did he reign in his father's stead, if his brother began to reign in the last year of their father? ⟦s19⟧ But from this it is given to understand that he passed one year in the kingdom with his father, and the other with his brother. ⟦s20⟧ It is understood that this same Ochozias received the kingdom from his father at the end of his father's next-to-last year, and passed the greater part of the following year with his father. ⟦s21⟧ But the same year, as we have said, which was Achab's last, was the eighteenth of Josaphat, king of Juda. ⟦s22⟧ It is weighed therefore that at the end of that same eighteenth year, after his father's death, Ochozias, broken by a fall and, at Elias' prophesying, [0243D] despaired of life, took his brother into the kingdom, and passed another year with him in sickness. ⟦s23⟧ But if Joram, son of Achab and brother of Ochozias, reigned, as is read of him, in the nineteenth year of Josaphat king of Juda, what is it that a little after he is said to have reigned in the second year of Joram, son of the same Josaphat king of Juda? ⟦s24⟧ Or was perhaps the same year the son's second which was the father's eighteenth. ⟦s25⟧ And indeed it could have been that, on account of some infirmity or some other cause urging it, the father at that time had crowned his son, although he did not at all permit him for some seasons to exercise royal power.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For it is to be noted that, when Joram son of Achab is said to have reigned in the second year of the other Joram, he is not called king Joram, nor is it said in the second year of his reign; [0244A] Scripture says nothing of the kind, as it was accustomed to say above in the case of the rest, but, making mention of the death of Ochozias, it added. ⟦s2⟧ And Joram his brother reigned in his stead in the second year of Joram, son of Josaphat king of Juda. ⟦s3⟧ If the son of Achab reigned in the second year of the son of Josaphat, how is that true which is said conversely, that the son of Josaphat reigned in the fifth year of Joram son of Achab, unless it is given to understand that from that time, his father granting and appointing it, he used the royal power in part, and handled the affairs of the kingdom together with his father? ⟦s4⟧ Hence it is that the times of those reigning together are ascribed from that same fifth year both to the son and to the father; for the times passed before are attributed to the father alone. ⟦s5⟧ But that year which was the fifth of the son of Achab was, of Josaphat — who reigned twenty-five years — [0244B] the twenty-third. ⟦s6⟧ But do you wish to know more openly from Scripture itself that that year was common to three? ⟦s7⟧ In the fifth year, he says, of Joram son of Achab king of Israel, and of Josaphat king of Juda, reigned Joram son of Josaphat king of Juda. ⟦s8⟧ See, the same year is said to be of king Josaphat which was his son's first and Achab's son's fifth — not because it was king Josaphat's fifth, but because it was common to him with the rest. ⟦s9⟧ For three years, then, father and son reigned together. ⟦s10⟧ There seems to run together into the same judgment the fact that the son of Josaphat is said to have reigned eight years. ⟦s11⟧ For after his father's death he could not reign so many years, since indeed in the fifth year after the death of Josaphat, Jehu slew both Ahazias, the son of Josaphat's son, and Joram [0244C] son of Achab. ⟦s12⟧ But in the kingdom of Juda this son of Josaphat is found to be the first to have reigned together with his father.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is to be noted that the same year was the eighth of Joram king of Juda which was the twelfth of Joram king of Israel. ⟦s2⟧ How then is it said that Ahazias, son of Joram king of Juda, reigned in his father's stead, and for one year, if the same year was the last for the father and for the son and for the king of Israel? ⟦s3⟧ For at the same time, as has already been said, Ahazias and the king of Israel were slain by Jehu. ⟦s4⟧ But the son could have reigned for the greater part of the year with his father, and for the remainder without his father. ⟦s5⟧ And since he survived his father in the kingdom, he is said to have reigned in his stead. ⟦s6⟧ From these things, then, which have been said it will be manifest, as I believe, how false is the opinion (not to say the assertion) of certain men who, a false question having been raised, say for the sake of a solution that the son of Josaphat reigned twenty years. ⟦s7⟧ For the father could not have reigned twenty [0244D] years, if his son who reigned in his stead was slain by Jehu in the eighth year of his father, or a little after. ⟦s8⟧ It is established that Jehu, as has been said, slew Joram son of Achab, who reigned twelve years; and afterwards it is said of the same Jehu that in the eleventh year of Joram son of Achab, and of king Ahazias over Juda, he came into Jezrael and slew Jezabel. ⟦s9⟧ What then is it that what Jehu did first, he is said to have done in the twelfth year of Joram king of Israel, and what he did afterwards, he is said to have done in the eleventh year of the same king. ⟦s10⟧ But by a wonderful reasoning he intimates that in different respects one and the same year was for him the eleventh and the twelfth. ⟦s11⟧ For eleven years he reigned [0245A] by himself alone, but one he passed in the kingdom with his brother.
