## THIRD TREATISE. On the harmony of the times of the kings reigning together over Juda and over Israel. To the blessed Bernard, abbot of Clairvaux. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But it is to be noted that we find in this place a construction like that which we noted above. ⟦s2⟧ There it is read: In the fifth year of Joram son of Achab king of Israel, and of Josaphat king of Juda, reigned Joram son of Josaphat king of Juda. ⟦s3⟧ But here it is said: In the eleventh year of Joram son of Achab, and of king Ahazias over Juda, Jehu came into Jezrael. ⟦s4⟧ There the fifth year of Joram son of Achab is said to be of Josaphat as much as of himself, although it was the fifth in the kingdom for Joram alone. ⟦s5⟧ Here the eleventh year of the same Joram is said to be of Ahazias as much as of himself, although it was the eleventh in the kingdom for Joram alone. ⟦s6⟧ Jehu reigned over Israel ten and eight years, in whose seventh year Joas son of Ahazias began to reign over Juda, and reigned forty years. [0245B] But the same year was Joas' twenty-second which was also Jehu's last. ⟦s7⟧ But Joas, son of Jehu, began to reign in the twenty-third year of Joas, and reigned ten and seven years. ⟦s8⟧ The same year therefore was Joas' last which was also the thirty-ninth of Joas king of Juda. ⟦s9⟧ How then is it written that Joas son of Joachas reigned in the thirty-seventh year of Joas king of Juda, unless because Joachas made his son king three years before his own death? ⟦s10⟧ But when Joachas was dead, his son Joas reigned alone in the fortieth and last year of Joas king of Juda; whence also for Amasias, son of that same king of Juda, that is found to be the first year of reigning which is said to be the second for Joas the son. ⟦s11⟧ In the second year, says Scripture, of Joas son of Joachas king of Israel, reigned Amasias son of Joas king of Juda.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0245C]It is to be noted that the times of reigning together are attributed to the father alone, whence it is also gathered that the son by no means exercised royal power before his father's death. ⟦s2⟧ Joas king of Israel is said to have reigned sixteen years, in whose second year Amasias king of Juda reigned. ⟦s3⟧ The year therefore which was Joas' sixteenth and last was Amasias' fifteenth. ⟦s4⟧ How then did Jeroboam, son of Joas king of Israel, reign in the fifteenth year of Amasias, as Scripture says, unless because in his father's last year he reigned together with his father? ⟦s5⟧ The same year, as we have said, was for king Joas the sixteenth and last, which was also for Amasias king of Juda the fifteenth. ⟦s6⟧ For fourteen years therefore after the death of Joas, Amasias reigned, of whom it is read that he reigned twenty-nine years. [0245D] What then is it that Amasias is said to have lived twenty-five years after the death of Joas, who nevertheless is not found, according to Scripture, to have reigned after that man's death except for fourteen years? ⟦s7⟧ But it is given to understand that after the twenty-ninth year of his reign, whether because of infirmity or for some other cause, he was at leisure for himself, and committed the affairs of the kingdom to his crowned son. ⟦s8⟧ Hence it is that Azarias, who is Zozias, son of Amasias, is said to have reigned in the twenty-seventh year of [sic: *Jerobam*] king of Israel, as is read in what follows: In the twentieth year, he says, of Jeroboam king of Israel, reigned Azarias son of Amasias king of Juda, as we have already said above, the same year was the first in the kingdom for king Jeroboam which for his own father was the last in the kingdom and [0246A] in life. ⟦s9⟧ But between the first and the twenty-seventh year you will find twenty-five years, in which Amasias is said to have survived king Joas. ⟦s10⟧ From that time therefore Azarias son of Amasias began to exercise royal power, not by his father's permission alone, but by hereditary right. ⟦s11⟧ As has been said above, in the fifteenth year of Amasias, king of Juda, Jeroboam began to reign over Israel. ⟦s12⟧ The same year therefore was Jeroboam's fifteenth in the kingdom which proved to be Amasias' twenty-ninth. ⟦s13⟧ And if Azarias son of Amasias received the kingdom from his father immediately after his father's twenty-ninth year, assuredly the same year was both Azarias' twenty-sixth and Jeroboam's forty-first and last in the kingdom. ⟦s14⟧ For the same Jeroboam reigned forty and one, and Azarias fifty [0246B] and two years. ⟦s15⟧ What then is it that Zacharias son of Jeroboam began to reign, as is read, in the thirty-eighth year of Azarias? ⟦s16⟧ Did the kingdom of Israel remain without a king from the twenty-seventh year of Azarias until his thirty-eighth year? ⟦s17⟧ It can indeed come into doubt whether on account of age, or on account of rebellion, or for some other cause, he was unable until then to prevail in his father's kingdom. ⟦s18⟧ That in the thirty-eighth year of Azarias king of Juda Zacharias son of Jeroboam is said to have reigned must be so understood: that in the thirty-eighth year indeed he began to reign, and reigned six months, not so much of that year as of the following one; and after him Sellum, who slew him, for one month. ⟦s19⟧ After these, Manahen, beginning to reign in that same thirty-ninth year, passed the lesser [0246C] part of that same year in the kingdom. ⟦s20⟧ For if he had passed the greater part of that year, Scripture would, after its manner, have ascribed to him not ten years only, but eleven. ⟦s21⟧ The things which are said of Phaceia, or of Phacee, seem plain enough. ⟦s22⟧ Joathan son of Ozias is described as having reigned sixteenth ( *sic* ) years, and yet Osee king of Israel is declared to reign in his twentieth year: whence it is given to understand that he lived twenty years in the kingdom, but that from the sixteenth year he made his son king, and that for whatever cause he so deserved to be exalted in the kingdom that the times of reigning together were ascribed rather to the son than to the father. ⟦s23⟧ In the second year of Phacee, who reigned twenty years, Joathan king of Juda began to reign. ⟦s24⟧ The same year therefore was Joathan's sixteenth, [0246D] which was also Phacee's seventeenth. ⟦s25⟧ But in the seventeenth year of Phacee, Achab son of Joathan began to reign, but, as it is given to understand, at the end of that same year: for if he had reigned for the whole time of that year, or for the greater part of that same year, I do not see why that year would not be ascribed to Achab, as are the rest that follow. ⟦s26⟧ The same year therefore was Achab's third which was the twentieth of Phacee, whom Osee slew. ⟦s27⟧ The same was nonetheless Achab's fourth, which is said to be his father's twentieth. ⟦s28⟧ Osee therefore reigned in the fourth year of Achab. ⟦s29⟧ What then is it that in what follows he is said to reign in the twelfth year of that same Achab? ⟦s30⟧ Or was it perhaps, as befell Zamri, that from the time when, Phacee being slain, he snatched the kingdom for himself, until the twelfth [0247A] year of king Achab he could not prevail, many rebelling against him? ⟦s31⟧ Whence it seems to have come about that the times in the kingdom which followed, but not those which preceded, were ascribed to him; [0248A] otherwise he would be said to have reigned eighteenth ( *sic* ) years rather than nine.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[0247]A summary declaration of the two following tables, for the recognizing of the contradiction and the harmony of the times of the kings reigning together over Juda and Israel.*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0247A]In what follows I have added two tables, in which are contained the things I have set out above. ⟦s2⟧ In the first are contained those things which seem to raise a question, or to bring in a contrariety. ⟦s3⟧ In the second table these same things are arranged in such an order that each of the things which raised a question may seem able to be understood from it without exposition. ⟦s4⟧ In the first table, and in its first order, you have arranged from the top downward the names of the kings reigning in Juda. ⟦s5⟧ And in the second order, [0247B] how many years each of them reigned. ⟦s6⟧ In the third order you have the kings of the Israelite kingdom. ⟦s7⟧ And in the fourth, the times of their reigning. ⟦s8⟧ In the numbers which are written beneath certain kings, it is noted in what year of the reign of him [0248A] who is written above, those began to reign who are written opposite on the other side. ⟦s9⟧ In the second table, at its first front, is set in order the series of the times from the beginning of the schism until the times of the first captivity. ⟦s10⟧ In the second order is contained the series of the times of the kings of Juda succeeding one another in order. ⟦s11⟧ In the third order it is open to see when it befell that sons reigned together with their fathers. ⟦s12⟧ What the second and third orders teach concerning the kings of Juda and their sons, this the fourth and fifth orders teach [0248B] concerning the kings of Israel. ⟦s13⟧ In which this too can be weighed, that certain times of reigning together are attributed to the sons alone, certain to the fathers alone, and certain to both sons and fathers.
