## PROLOGUE. *A literal explanation of the living creatures and of the wheels.* (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *This was the vision running about in the midst of the living creatures. ⟦s2⟧ A brightness of fire, and out of the fire lightning going forth.* ⟦s3⟧ These things are plain enough. ⟦s4⟧ *And the living creatures went and returned after the likeness of flashing lightning.* ⟦s5⟧ Behold what we have already said above, that the living creatures went and went back. ⟦s6⟧ But in what manner, do you suppose? ⟦s7⟧ After the likeness of flashing lightning. ⟦s8⟧ A swiftness astonishing, and to be attributed to heavenly living creatures alone. ⟦s9⟧ *And as I beheld the living creatures, there appeared one wheel upon the earth beside the living creatures.* ⟦s10⟧ Not one only beside them all, but single wheels beside single creatures, as what follows declares. ⟦s11⟧ And so one wheel appeared beside each single living creature. ⟦s12⟧ But of what sort? ⟦s13⟧ *Having,* he says, *four faces.* ⟦s14⟧ But how it had four [0531B] faces will better appear from what follows: *And the appearance of the wheels, and their work, was as the vision of the sea.* ⟦s15⟧ They are said to have the likeness of the sea because of their incredible magnitude. ⟦s16⟧ *And the four of them had one likeness, and their appearance and their works were as if a wheel were in the midst of a wheel.* ⟦s17⟧ Let us imagine that there were, as it were, two wheels inserted one into the other, and joined crosswise into a single fabric of one work; and then we shall be able to understand how each one is said to have four faces. ⟦s18⟧ *Going toward their four sides, they went, and they did not turn back as they walked.* ⟦s19⟧ We say this of the wheels in the same manner in which we determined it above of the living creatures. ⟦s20⟧ *There was also stature in the wheels, and height, and a terrible appearance.* ⟦s21+22⟧ So far as it seems to me, as often as they stood they did it uniformly, nor [0531C] did they raise themselves up to stand indiscriminately toward every side, as we see in our own wheels; but where that fabric designated above, as of two wheels, was inserted crosswise on this side and on that, and coupled one to the other — when they wished to stand, they always fixed one of those parts in the earth and raised the other on high. ⟦s23⟧ It is easy, therefore, to see how ready it was for them, after standing still, to roll themselves in like manner toward every side, without any turning of the body — they that, as has been said, had four faces. ⟦s24⟧ And we may refer the word "terrible" both to the stature and to the height, so that it may be understood to be of such magnitude that it was a horror to look upon; whence also it was said above of the wheels that their appearance was as [0531D] the vision of the sea. ⟦s25⟧ *And the whole body was full of eyes round about the four of them. ⟦s26⟧ And when the living creatures walked, the wheels also walked alongside them; and when the living creatures were lifted up from the earth, the wheels also were lifted up together with them.* ⟦s27⟧ All these things are plain, nor do they need any exposition of ours. ⟦s28⟧ *Whithersoever the spirit went, the spirit going thither, the wheels also were lifted up together, following it. ⟦s29⟧ For the spirit of life was in the wheels.* ⟦s30⟧ The spirit which the wheels followed seems to be one, and the spirit which is said to be in them another. ⟦s31⟧ *And the likeness of a firmament above the head of the living creatures, as the appearance of terrible crystal, and stretched out above their heads from above.* ⟦s32⟧ What is said, "as of terrible crystal," I think was said for the enormity of its magnitude: *And under the firmament their wings were straight, the one toward the other. ⟦s33⟧ Each one covered [0532A] his body with two wings, and the other was likewise covered.* ⟦s34⟧ When any one of them covered its own body with those two wings which were on the right and on the left of the human face, any one of the rest did likewise. ⟦s35⟧ And when any one of them was covered by the other two wings, the same manner of covering the body was observed at the same time by the others. ⟦s36⟧ In whatever manner, therefore, any one covered its own body, in like manner any other one was covered. ⟦s37⟧ He calls the sound as it were the sound of the most high God, for the reason that it was of such magnitude that it ought by right to have been called divine — just as a song may worthily be called royal, or the king's, which befits a royal audience.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Again, concerning these wheels, or the living creatures, we read in another place: *One wheel beside one cherub, and another wheel beside one cherub. ⟦s2⟧ And the appearance of the wheels was as [0532B] the vision of the chrysolite stone, and the aspect of them was one likeness of the four, as if a wheel were in the midst of a wheel. ⟦s3⟧ And when they walked, they went toward the four sides. ⟦s4⟧ They did not turn back as they walked, but to the place whither the first, which was going, inclined to go, the rest also followed, and they were not turned about [n: (Ezech. X)]*. ⟦s5⟧ All these things are already plain from what has been said above. ⟦s6⟧ There follows:

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And their whole body and their necks, and their hands and their wings, and the circles, were full of eyes round about the four wheels, and their whole body.* ⟦s2⟧ As for his saying *earum,* it is to be understood that the reference is made not to the wheels only, but also to the living creatures; for the statement had been set forth concerning both. ⟦s3⟧ For although he says *earum,* he nevertheless adds certain things which belong to the living creatures as much as to the wheels, certain things indeed which belong to the wheels alone, and certain things [0532C] which are fitting to the living creatures alone — unless perhaps someone should think that the wheels have necks, feet, and hands. ⟦s4⟧ But he would speak more customarily if he had put *eorum,* not *earum.* ⟦s5⟧ But because he had spoken of the wheels in the last place, he made the reference according to the gender which he had put in the nearest place. ⟦s6⟧ Such is that in the psalm: *They shed innocent blood, the blood of their sons and of their daughters, whom they sacrificed to the graven things of Chanaan [n: (Psal. CV)]*. ⟦s7⟧ He said *quas,* not *quos,* although the reference was not made to the daughters alone — unless perhaps someone should wish to say that the daughters alone were sacrificed to the graven things. ⟦s8⟧ But who would say this? ⟦s9⟧ What is here said, "their whole body," we understand of the wheels as much as of the living creatures. ⟦s10⟧ What is added, "and the necks and [0532D] the hands and the wings," of the living creatures alone. ⟦s11⟧ But what is said afterward, "and the circles," must be understood of the wheels alone; and what is set down last, "round about the four wheels," ought to be referred to the circles only. ⟦s12⟧ He says, therefore, that they were full of eyes: their whole body, and the necks and the hands and the wings, and the circles round about the four wheels. ⟦s13⟧ He adds still certain things concerning the wheels, and certain things which belong to the living creatures alone: *And these wheels he called whirling, in my hearing.* ⟦s14⟧ Behold, concerning the wheels. ⟦s15⟧ And he adds what belongs to the living creatures alone: *And one had four faces.* ⟦s16⟧ If he was speaking of the wheels alone, what is it, or whence is it, that he says *unum*? ⟦s17⟧ I think that from this very thing it will be manifest that he was not speaking of the wheels alone. ⟦s18⟧ There follows:

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0533A] *One face was the face of a cherub, and the second face the face of a man, and in the third the face of a lion, and in the fourth the face of an eagle.* ⟦s2⟧ But where "the face of a cherub" is read, in certain codices "the face of a calf" is found. ⟦s3⟧ But although the letter is different, yet in both places the sense is one. ⟦s4⟧ From the foregoing we have recognized that the body of the said living creatures ended in a calf; and therefore, if we weigh it rightly, the face of a calf agreed better with the body — so much so that by a certain right of propriety this face may, or ought to, be called specially that of the creature described. ⟦s5⟧ But perhaps someone may answer to this and say: Just as it approached more nearly to the nature of a calf in that it had calves' soles, so from the fact that it bore hands, or wings, it displayed in the one the property of a man, from the other that of a bird. ⟦s6⟧ But we know that it is proper [0533B] to man to have hands. ⟦s7⟧ It could not, therefore, have calves' hands in the way it had calves' soles. ⟦s8⟧ The same is to be understood of the wings. ⟦s9⟧ But to have feet is common to man as much as to the ox, to the eagle as much as to the lion. ⟦s10⟧ What then does it mean that the living creature which had four faces received calves' feet, save that the calf-property claimed for itself in its body something more than the rest of its own right, seeing that it impressed the likeness of its own quality upon those very members which are equally common to others also? ⟦s11⟧ Because of this prerogative of propriety, therefore, we rightly understand the face of a calf where we read the face [0534A] of a cherub. ⟦s12⟧ And it is not a different thing that above we find the face of an ox, and here the face of a calf. ⟦s13⟧ For every calf is an ox, but it is not convertible with the name of ox, by which the species of the animal is designated.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But it is to be noted that the same order of position is found in this place which is expressed above. ⟦s2⟧ But there the face of a man is numbered first, for the reason that it is more principal according to dignity. ⟦s3⟧ Here, however, the face of a calf, for the reason that it is more principal according to propriety. ⟦s4⟧ But because the calf's face is not more worthy than the rest, he would not say "the face of a cherub," or "the face of a calf," is "the first face," but "one face": *One face,* he says, *was the face of a cherub, and the second face the face of a man.* ⟦s5⟧ The face of a man, therefore, was the second face, that is, [0534B] in the second place from that which was set first; and in the third place the face of a lion, and in the fourth the face of an eagle.
