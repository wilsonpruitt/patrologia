## CHAPTER XI. *On the tables where the holocaust was sacrificed, and on the chambers of the singers.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And in the porch of the gate two tables on this side, and two [0562C] tables on that side, that upon them may be sacrificed the holocaust, and that for sin, and that for trespass.* ⟦s2⟧ What the holocaust for sin and for trespass may be, and many other things after this manner which pertain to the exposition of the letter, I deliberately pass over, because for the present I am wholly intent upon that alone which I have not found elsewhere: how this so subtle description of the prophet may be able to stand according to the letter, or ought to be understood. ⟦s3⟧ But if this, as is here said, was the office of the tables, that upon them the holocaust ought to be sacrificed, let each man choose what it is rather fitting to think: whether that victims ought to be sacrificed at this gate alone, or that the other gates also have like tables for that office. ⟦s4⟧ If sacrifice was made at this one alone, what will become of the victim of the prince, who entered in and went out by the [0562D] eastern gate alone? ⟦s5⟧ If at the eastern gate alone the victims are washed on days that are not feast days, what will become of the victim of the people, when the eastern gate was shut, which was opened to the prince alone? ⟦s6⟧ By allegations of this kind we can gather that the divine Scripture silently intimates many things out of a certain deliberateness. ⟦s7⟧ He has already said that there are four tables, two on this side and two on that, within the porch. ⟦s8⟧ He says further that there are also four others outside the porch, two likewise at the one side, and two at the other. ⟦s9⟧ *And at the outer side,* he says, *which the door of the gate that goes toward the north ascends, two tables, and at the other side before the porch of the gate two tables. ⟦s10⟧ Four tables on this side, and four tables on that side. ⟦s11⟧ Along the sides of the gate there were eight tables, upon which they sacrificed.* ⟦s12⟧ Since, [0563A] as was said above, the temple occupied the summit of the mountain, and on every side the mountain itself raised itself little by little to greater heights as far as that place, this gate (and no wonder) had one side higher, the other lower. ⟦s13⟧ And so from the one side one went up to the gate, from the other one went down to the gate. ⟦s14⟧ For because this was the northern gate, as Scripture itself determines, it had the temple beyond doubt on the western side, which side indeed rose higher than the eastern side. ⟦s15⟧ The eastern side is determined when it is said: *And at the outer side which ascends to the door of the gate which goes toward the north, two tables.* ⟦s16⟧ For what follows is said of the other side: *And at the other side before the porch of the gate two tables.* ⟦s17⟧ And so there were, as is subjoined, four tables on this side, and four tables on that side. ⟦s18⟧ For there were four [0563B] at the one side, two within the porch and two outside the porch. ⟦s19⟧ Likewise two within and two without at the other side. ⟦s20⟧ Therefore along the sides of the gate there were eight tables upon which they sacrificed. ⟦s21⟧ The things which follow are sufficiently plain of themselves and need no exposition. ⟦s22⟧ *And four tables for the holocaust built of squared stones, of the length of one cubit and a [sic: *demidii*], and of the breadth of one cubit and a half, and of the height of one cubit, upon which they may lay the vessels in which the holocaust and the victim is sacrificed; and their lips of one palm, turned back inward round about. ⟦s23⟧ And upon the tables the flesh of the oblation.* ⟦s24⟧ Thus far concerning the tables. ⟦s25⟧ But there follows concerning certain chambers, of which nothing has been said in the foregoing.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0563C] *And outside the inner gate the chambers of the singers in the inner court, which was at the side of the gate that looks toward the north, and their faces over against the southern way, one from the side of the eastern gate which looked toward the way of the north.* ⟦s2⟧ It is a marvel that it is said: In the inner court which was at the side of the gate that looks toward the north. ⟦s3⟧ For the inner court was not at the side of this gate, but rather was situated before it, because it lay in the middle between the three inner gates. ⟦s4⟧ But when "inner court" or "outer court" is said, we ought not always to take this uniformly. ⟦s5⟧ That has been called the inner court which, of a hundred cubits every way, is placed in the middle; and that the outer court by which the inner court was girded on every side. ⟦s6⟧ But in the inner [0563D] court there was nothing else save one altar; but all the rest — porticoes, chambers, the temple, the houses — were in the outer court. ⟦s7⟧ Yet according to another consideration the place in which the temple was, and those houses which lay alongside the temple on its three sides, is called the inner court, as is read in what follows. ⟦s8⟧ For there were the chambers of the sanctuary in which the priests laid up and ate the flesh of the oblation, which nevertheless it was not lawful to carry out into the outer court, Scripture forbidding it, which says: *This is the place where the priests shall boil what is for sin and for trespass, where they shall boil the sacrifice, that they carry it not out into the outer court, and the people be sanctified [n: (Ezech. XLVI)]*. ⟦s9⟧ Yet these flesh-portions, which were forbidden to be carried out into the outer court, they laid up in the aforesaid chambers, [0564A] and they were eaten, as is read concerning them: *These are the holy chambers in which the priests eat who draw near into the Holy of Holies unto the Lord. ⟦s10⟧ There shall they lay the Holy of Holies and the oblations for sin and for trespass [n: (Ezech. XLI)]*. ⟦s11⟧ And so for one reason that is called the inner court which was before the temple, because, as has been said, it lay in the middle. ⟦s12⟧ But for another reason that by which the temple was girded on three sides, because it was entered into even through that which was properly called the inner court. ⟦s13⟧ So an inner little chamber is wont to be called a hall, not because it is within the hall, but because it holds a more secret place, and because through the hall the approach to it lies open. ⟦s14⟧ So indeed [d: so] no place was more secret, and none more remote from the entrance of the gates, than that in which the temple was built. [0564B] And so according to this consideration, in comparison with this place, even that which Scripture is wont by custom to call the inner court can be called the outer court. ⟦s15⟧ For one reason therefore the place in which the temple was is called the inner court, and for another that which held the middle and had the altar of holocaust. ⟦s16⟧ Nothing therefore forbids us to understand that place in which the temple, or the adjacent houses, were, in that which is said: and over against the inner gate the chambers of the singers in the inner court, which was at the side of the gate that looks toward the north. ⟦s17⟧ In this court, which lay alongside the aforesaid gate on the western side, was that chamber of the singers which is now being described, and it adhered laterally to the same gate or portico. ⟦s18⟧ Concerning this chamber it is treated more fully in what follows, and it is, [0564C] unless I am mistaken [sic: *in fallor*], described in these words: *And he brought me into the chamber which was over against the separate building, and over against the house looking toward the north,* and the rest, of which we shall speak more fully in its place. ⟦s19⟧ But for the present this seems to be said concerning this chamber, that its breadth began from this gate and spread itself toward the west. ⟦s20⟧ But its length extended itself from the north into the south. ⟦s21⟧ Whence is that which is subsequently added concerning the same chambers, when it is said: And their faces over against the southern way. ⟦s22⟧ And so the gate had chambers of the singers of this kind, as has been said, on the western side. ⟦s23⟧ It had others also on the eastern side, which assuredly were situated over against both the eastern and the northern [0564D] gate. ⟦s24⟧ If therefore, over against the eastern gate, they had their front toward the gate of the north, whence is that which is subjoined to the same: one from the side of the eastern gate which looked toward the way of the north? ⟦s25⟧ He says therefore that one face of chambers of this kind was from the side of the eastern gate, which face looked toward the way of the north. ⟦s26⟧ But the letter would be plainer if it had been said, "the face of certain of them," not "a certain face," or "one face of them was from the side of the eastern gate." ⟦s27⟧ But as it is, not only is "one" there set down for "the face of certain of them," but also "their faces" is understood from the foregoing. ⟦s28⟧ But these chambers, which were toward the way on the east of the northern gate, had their face not over against the way of the north, because it went crosswise before them and not over against them, as those which were on the west had the southern way [0565A] going over against them [sic: *contracuntem*]. ⟦s29⟧ But we ought to understand the same of both sides of the eastern gate, and likewise also of one side of the southern gate. ⟦s30⟧ But Scripture is wont deliberately to pass over the things which it knows can easily be understood of themselves. ⟦s31⟧ There follows:

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And he said to me: This is the chamber which looks toward the southern way, of the priests who keep watch in the wards of the temple. ⟦s2⟧ But the chamber which looks toward the way of the north shall be of the priests who keep watch for the ministry of the altar.* ⟦s3⟧ The chamber which looked over against the southern way was nearer to the temple, and therefore it justly ought to belong to those priests who kept watch in the wards of the temple: for in dignity they excelled those who served the altar. ⟦s4⟧ But common [0565B] to both sets of priests is that which follows, when it is said: *These are the sons of Sadoc, who of the sons of Levi come near to the Lord that they may minister to him.*
