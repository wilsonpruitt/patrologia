## CHAPTER XII. *On the kitchens which were built beneath the porticoes, and on the inner court.*

In this place it must be considered that toward the eastern way, on this side and on that, about the porticoes both of the outer and of the inner court, a certain space still remains empty between them and the chambers, as is plain, set before the eyes, from the figures placed above; in which places those kitchens could be, of which Scripture makes mention in what follows, when it says: *And kitchens [0565C] were built beneath the porticoes round about [n: (Ezech. XLVI)]*. But toward the way of the north the outer portico had only one space of the same size, which adhered to it on the eastern side. For on the western side it had chambers adjoining it. But it is agreed that the southern portico of the outer court also has a like space on the eastern side for the same office, and that chambers adhere to it on the other side. From like things therefore we judge like things, and therefore to the two porticoes of the inner court, on this side and on that, to either on both sides, we leave a like space for the office of the kitchens. After these we range the chambers of the singers, of fifty cubits both in length and in breadth, so that according to their length [0565D] so also may their breadth be, as we read concerning certain chambers. For concerning the length of all the chambers of the outer court one sentence is given, when far afterward in what follows it is said: Because the length of the chambers of the outer court was fifty cubits. After these chambers of the singers, a certain way of ten cubits in breadth being interposed, fifteen chambers are ranged on this side and on that in the eastern way, that there may be, as it is written, thirty round about the pavement. But there were likewise fifteen on one side only, that is, on the eastern part, both in the northern way and in the southern way. But there are from the fronts of the inner gates, as will afterward be taught, as far as the place of the common people, a hundred and forty cubits. Out of these, if we give five cubits to the wall, the rest [0566A] being equally divided among fifteen, we shall find that each single chamber holds nine cubits in breadth. There follows:

*And he measured the court, in length a hundred cubits, and in breadth a hundred cubits foursquare, and the altar before the face of the temple.* From this place it is sufficiently plain that the inner court, according to breadth, held that whole space of a hundred cubits which was the middle in those five hundred cubits which that wall of five hundred cubits enclosed from the north into the south. But because the eastern porticoes had measures like the other porticoes in all things, and enclosed a court of the same length, and rose by [sic: *totitem*] steps, it is agreed beyond doubt that their measure extends as far as the middle hundred, [0566B] and that from its beginning the inner court takes its beginning. But how many of those hundred cubits toward the west it took in within itself — I speak according to the consideration of the plane — I do not yet think can be proved from the aforesaid. For although toward that part also it had a hundred cubits so far as the surface goes, yet what or how much of that middle hundred, from the [sic: *Occiente*], the swelling of the mountain took away according to the reckoning of the plane — the mountain which there rose up toward that part — is not yet plain. For one line of a hundred cubits lying flat, and another raising itself obliquely, emerging from the same point, do not meet in the same cathetus, nor do they enclose an equal space according to the plane; but how these things stand, from what follows [0566C] it will be plain. And the altar, he says, before the face of the temple. This he therefore annexes here, because the altar was in this court which lay before the temple. Concerning its measure nothing is here said, but far below it is subtly explained. In this court we make that water, which went forth from the temple, to run down and to descend from the right side of the altar. But about the lower parts of the court we cut it into four, and lead it through the midst of the kitchens, so that it passes before the chambers, that there may be a place where the holocaust can be washed, according as it is written: *And through the several chambers a door in the fronts of the gates, and there they washed the holocaust.* Of all these things which we have said we have more fully expressed the form and pattern in the last and great figure, as can there be seen.
