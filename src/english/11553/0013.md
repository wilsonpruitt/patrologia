## CHAPTER XIII. *On the size or of the buildings which lay adjacent to the temple.*

[0566D]

Since therefore we have for the present completed the things which seemed to need saying concerning the front part of this building, we now turn our pen to describing its western part, and whatever of the aforesaid shall have to be supplied to the aforesaid we shall supply in its own place. It pleases me here, as also above in the description of the porticoes, to gather into one the testimonies of [sic: *Scriturae*] from this side and from that, and from their comparison to establish the position, manner, and order of the buildings to be described, and to show what is to be thought concerning them, authority proving it on the one hand, reason on the other. As we taught above, the temple with its portico [0567A] rose beyond the inner court on its western side, and on the other three sides the same temple had three houses with their porticoes, a certain court nevertheless being interposed, each one of which held a hundred cubits in length. For concerning that building which was at the back of the temple on the west, which Scripture is also wont to call "separate," from the fact that it adhered to none of the rest, it is read: *And he measured the length of the house, a hundred cubits, and that which was the separate building, and its walls, of a hundred cubits in length.* But concerning the other buildings which were on either side it is subjoined, when it is said: *And he measured the length of the building over against the face of that which was separate at the back, the ethecas on either part, of a hundred cubits.* For while [0567B] a building of a hundred cubits in length is said to be measured on either part, it is proved that there was a building of the same length on both sides. Behold, concerning the three houses; let us now see concerning their porticoes. That there was a portico at the northern building, Scripture makes manifest, when it says: *Where the chambers were lower in the upper parts, because they supported the porticoes which rose out of them from the lower and from the middle parts of the building.* But that this was said of the northern building and of its portico, the surrounding context of the letter plainly shows, as will afterward be manifest in the exposition of the letter. That this portico of which we have spoken before was of fifty cubits we can indeed prove from this, that its enclosure, that is, the wall which girded its chambers [0567C] which supported it, is read to have been of fifty cubits: *And the enclosure,* he says, *the outer one, along the chambers which were in the way of the outer court before the chambers, its length fifty cubits, because the length of the chambers of the outer court was fifty cubits.* The breadth of this portico is afterward expressed in that which is written in another place: *According to their length, so also their breadth.* This indeed follows from the description of this portico and of its chambers, which can also with fuller reasoning be proved likewise from other places: namely, that the northern building had chambers in its lower parts, upper chambers in its upper parts. For in vain would its portico rise fifty [0567D] cubits from the ground, unless it had also an upper chamber behind it which answered to it in a fitting height. For what does Scripture say concerning the porticoes of this building? *Therefore,* he says, *they rose out of the lower and out of the middle parts of the building by fifty cubits of the earth.* [var: Vulg. *a terra cubitis quinquaginta*, "from the ground, fifty cubits"] Finally, by comparison with these chambers which were in the lower parts of the building, the wall which embraces the chambers of the portico is named the outer enclosure, in that it is joined more nearly to the outer court, nay even immediately. But this portico was joined to the northern portico of the inner court, whence also it is written: *Where there was a portico joined to the triple portico.* But if, from that portico of the inner court, we add to that measure which it had within the walls — of which alone we have spoken above — five cubits as it were for [0568A] the thickness of the wall, beyond doubt we find two hundred and fifteen cubits as far as the place of the common people. And so from that place the aforesaid house with its portico extended itself toward the west into a hundred and forty-five cubits, beyond which, as far as the same place of the common people, seventy cubits still remained over. For in that part, as will afterward be taught, in which they were joined, they had a common wall, and therefore they held not a hundred and fifty, but only a hundred and forty-five cubits. Between this building and the temple there was a certain space of a hundred cubits in length and of fifty cubits in breadth. Whose measure is determined when it is said: *In the face of the length a hundred cubits of the door of the north, and of the breadth fifty cubits.* [0568B] But whatever we have said concerning the manner and measure of the northern building, the whole of it we ought to think concerning the southern building according to the same likeness, which indeed we suppose to be sufficiently plain from those things which have already been said above. We have now how far these buildings with their porticoes extended, but it is not yet agreed how much of an interval they enclosed. But this indeed, as I judge, will be manifest when it shall have been established how that building which is called separate was ranged between these two. It had indeed a hundred cubits in length and eighty cubits in breadth. For within the walls it had ninety cubits in length and seventy in breadth, and the wall itself was everywhere round about of five cubits, as Scripture shows: *And that which was the separate building, [0568C] and turned toward the way looking to the sea, of seventy cubits in breadth. But the wall of the building was of five cubits in breadth round about, and its length ninety cubits;* whence also it is inferred: *And he measured the length of the house, a hundred cubits, and that which was the separate building and its walls, a hundred cubits.* And so it was situated at the back of the temple, near the wall which divided the place of the common people, and it extended its length without any doubt into the north and the south. But it spread its breadth from the west into the east. Since therefore beyond the aforesaid buildings lying on either side there was a space of only seventy cubits, it is necessary that the breadth of the building, which was of eighty [0568D] cubits, occupied with a great part of itself the interval of the aforesaid buildings. The length therefore of the separate building is extended between those aforesaid houses, and there is still added each space of twenty cubits, on account of that which is read concerning their chambers: *And between the chambers a breadth of twenty cubits round about the house on every side.*

It is agreed therefore that between the two buildings which lay adjacent to the temple on this side and on that a space of a hundred and forty cubits was assuredly enclosed according to the plane. But the northern house, as also the southern, had its portico not over against the middle, but over against its outer, or lower, part; and on the north both began from one and the same line. For where the chambers which supported the portico of this house [0569A] are described, there is at once annexed concerning their entrance: *And beneath these chambers was the entry of those going into them from the east from the outer court, in the breadth of the enclosure of the court which was over against the eastern way, in the face of the separate building.* This court, of which he makes mention in this place, the northern house assuredly had on the north; and from that house as far as the place of the common people it extends its breadth into the north. Therefore the said chambers would have their entry not over against the breadth of this court, nay rather over against the house, unless the portico with its house began from one place on the northern quarter. But from the northern side of the same portico as far as the inner court there were a hundred cubits, of which its breadth held fifty. Then, a certain [0569B] walk of ten cubits in breadth being interposed, that aforesaid chamber of the singers began, and extended itself as far as the twentieth cubit of the inner court. Whence it is agreed that its length was of fifty cubits. This chamber is described in what follows, when it is said: *And he led me into the chamber which was over against the separate building, and over against the house looking toward the north, in the face of the length a hundred cubits of the door of the north, and of the breadth fifty cubits, over against the twenty cubits of the inner court, and over against the pavement laid with stone where there was a portico joined to the triple portico.* I think it is plain how this chamber was over against the separate building, or how it was in the face of the interval of a hundred cubits in length and [0569C] of fifty cubits in breadth. Which lay between the temple and the northern building, if this chamber extended itself as far as the twentieth cubit of the inner court. No less plain is it how it was over against the house looking toward the north, if it began from the tenth cubit after the portico of that house. But now it behooves us to consider that it is said to be over against the pavement of the outer court; and lest we be able to understand any other than the northern pavement of the outer court, we are constrained by the addition of that clause, in that which we read: *Where there was a portico joined to the triple portico.* For in the eastern region we nowhere find a portico joined to a portico. But the northern portico of the inner court, as is plain from the foregoing, [0569D] had on the east the chambers of the singers, on the south the inner court, on the north this pavement of which we now speak. It is plain therefore that the portico which had been joined to this portico beyond doubt adhered to it on the west, and, touching it in part and leaving it with the greater part of itself, extended itself toward the north and bounded this pavement on the west. In order therefore that the aforesaid chamber may be able to have some part of itself over against this pavement, it behooves it to adhere to the triple portico, nay rather to have a common wall with it in the very place of their junction. After which wall, if the portico of the northern house began, and had [sic: *darietemque*] after the wall, assuredly the chamber was set over against the said pavement [0570A] by just so much as the thickness of its wall had. But that that walk of which we have spoken was before this chamber, Scripture subsequently shows, when it subjoins: *And before the chambers a walk of ten cubits in breadth.* Nor ought it to move us that the same building is called now "chambers," now "chamber," just as the same portico is named now "porches," now "porch." For let the one be referred to the distinctions, the other to the whole.

It must indeed be noted that in that place where the northern house and its portico made an angle before the aforesaid chamber and this place of the walk, there was a certain place of thirty cubits in length and of twenty cubits in breadth, concerning which what shall seem good [0570B] will be said in its own place. For according to what is plain from the foregoing, since from the eastern side of that chamber as far as the northern building there were only fifty cubits, that chamber indeed could not have more than thirty cubits in breadth, where it behooved twenty cubits to remain over, that what Scripture says concerning these chambers which lay about the temple might stand: *And between the chambers a breadth of twenty cubits round about the house on every side.* But it is agreed that from the same eastern side of the same chamber as far as the boundary of the sanctified space of five hundred cubits there were two hundred and twenty cubits, of which thirty, as has been said, the breadth of the aforesaid chamber held. Then the space or court which extended itself between it and the separate building [0570C] alongside the length of the temple occupied a hundred cubits according to the aforesaid authority of Scripture. But the breadth of the separate building had eighty cubits, from which place a breadth of ten cubits remained over. Of which five, as will afterward be shown, the breadth of the wall held: for the other five a certain way had, which lay in the middle between that wall and the building. But if these numbers come together into one, beyond doubt they rise to two hundred and twenty. Now it is easy to see how far the temple extended itself, which with its porch, according as will be taught in later places, was of a hundred cubits in length. But because the porch of the temple without doubt adhered to the inner court, [0570D] it and the aforesaid chamber began from the same line. But because the chamber, as has been said, and the court set before it extended themselves by a hundred and thirty cubits as far as the separate building, and the temple with its porch held only a hundred cubits, it is agreed that between the temple and the separate building the interval was of thirty cubits.
