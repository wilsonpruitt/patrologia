## CHAPTER XIV. *On the site or the plan of the remaining buildings which were toward the western quarter.*

But that we may pass on to the other things which still remain to be described: beyond the portico of the oft-mentioned northern building there were, on the north, ten chambers [0571A] over against that same portico, set in order toward the north as far as the outer wall, alongside the side of the pavement, of a length, according to the length of the portico, of fifty cubits. These are those chambers of the outer court from which Scripture draws a likeness, where it describes the length of that portico, or of the chambers which were beneath it, when it says: *And the outer enclosure, according to the chambers which were in the way of the outer court before the chambers, its length was of fifty cubits, because the length of the chambers of the outer court was of fifty cubits.* "According to the chambers," he says, "which were before the chambers." Before what chambers, I ask, save before those of which he was then treating, and of which he had already said many things — those which were beneath the porticoes, and which supported, as [0571B] was there said, the porticoes of the northern building? They are therefore described as being before the aforesaid portico, and those same chambers are called chambers of the outer court, that where they were may be openly determined. Yet between these chambers and the adjacent portico a certain way was interposed, of a breadth, as I judge, of five cubits, where was the entrance to the chambers which were before the separate building. For when the chambers which supported the porticoes have been described, concerning this entrance it is straightway subjoined, when it is said: *And beneath these chambers was an entrance from the east for those entering into them from the outer court, in the breadth of the enclosure of the court which was over against the eastern way, in the face of the separate building.* This assuredly is that entrance of which we have already said something above. From this [0571C] way as far as the wall of the outer court there were ninety cubits. And if these be divided among the X chambers, each one of them is found to be of nine cubits in breadth. But what has been said of the northern quarter is to be understood in all things, according to the same likeness, of the southern quarter. There were, therefore, ten chambers on the one side and ten on the other. These are those twenty chambers, as we believe, of which we read in what follows. Behold, in these chambers we find the same measure as in those above, which we extended to nine cubits, so that all are of one breadth, as they are, and of one length. It remains now to speak of that portico or porch of the separate building [0571D] which, between the northern house and the outer wall, adhered to it on the north. But which [way] the porch had, that Scripture determines. "Which way was before the separate porch." This porch, therefore, or portico, was of fifty cubits both in length and in breadth, and had chambers beneath itself, and rose above those of the lower and of the middle buildings, and on its upper side, that is, on the east, had a walk, or way, of ten cubits in breadth. All of which Scripture seems to determine in few words indeed, where it is read: *And before the building there were chambers, and a way before their face, according to the likeness of the chambers which were in the way of the north.* If these chambers, and the way set before them, had in all things the likeness [0572A] of those which were at the way of the north, then these, like those also, adhered to the house joined to them beneath the portico, and in like manner; and whatever we read of those, of themselves, in site, in quality and in quantity, we ought to accommodate to these according to the proposed little rule of the likeness.

And note that, although there were other chambers at the way of the north, the circumstance of the letter constrains us to understand those of which he had treated in the last place, and not those of which he said nothing. But by "the way of the north" he names, for his own love, that way which from the north went from gate through gate toward the inner parts — which way indeed passed before the site of the aforesaid chambers. But we have taught above that beyond the separate building ten cubits are left over, [0572B] which above we assigned partly to the wall, partly to the interposed way. Its portico, therefore, will in the same manner have ten cubits beyond itself on the west, if it was situated, after the likeness of the northern building, over against the outer parts of the house. And because beyond the northern house there were seventy cubits to the west, between it certainly and the separate porch there will be ten cubits, so that, after the likeness of the former portico, this portico also may hold a space of fifty cubits, and may have on its upper or inner part a walk of ten cubits. But on its lower or outer part we ought to understand, according to the former manner, that there was a way of five cubits, so that this porch may have the entrance of its chambers beneath itself, that is, on its lower and outer [0572C] side, after the likeness of the portico aforesaid. But also, that in this too one likeness may run together on both sides, attend where the separate building makes an angle with this its porch, and you will find here, just as there, a certain space of thirty cubits in length and of twenty cubits in breadth. This is that place of which, far afterward, we read in what follows: *And there was there a place turning toward the west, and he said to me: This is the place where the priests shall cook for trespass and for sin, where they shall cook the sacrifice, that they may not carry it out into the outer court, and the people be sanctified.* But that this place of cooking was in that court which had the chambers of the sanctuary, Scripture, if it be diligently questioned, assuredly does not suffer to lie hidden. For attend [0572D] to what is premised there where this place is described: *And he brought me in,* he says, *by the entrance which was at the side of the gate, into the chambers of the sanctuary belonging to the priests, which looked toward the north.* And it is added: *And there was there a place turning toward the west.* But of these chambers we read elsewhere: *The chambers of the north, and the chambers of the south, which are before the separate building — these are the holy chambers in which the priests are vested who draw near to the Lord into the Holy of holies. There they shall lay the Holy of holies, and the oblation for sin and for trespass.* Assuredly, if we diligently discuss these or other places of Scripture, we shall be able to find no other place for the aforesaid kitchen save that which we have designated before.

[0573A]It must indeed be noted what is said of this place, that it was turning toward the west. From which words it is manifestly gathered that the mountain indeed sloped downward behind the back of the temple, where it bore the separate building. We have already said that that designated place had thirty cubits of length and twenty cubits of breadth. The half of these, of thirty cubits in length and of ten cubits in breadth, which adhered to the separate building, had the kitchens; the remaining half — that, namely, of ten cubits in breadth — the walk held, so that that way passed before the face of the kitchens and bent itself back, in that part, into the court of the sanctuary. According to this likeness we believe in all things that that space designated above, of the same magnitude, [0573B] also had kitchens, and that that walk of ten cubits bent itself back, before their face, into the court of the sanctuary and looked toward the temple.

But whatever we have said of the northern side, all this we ought to understand of the southern side, in the site and manner of the buildings, according to the same congruence of likeness, in keeping with what we read of it: *And every entrance of them, and their likenesses, and their doors, according to the doors of the chambers which were in the way looking toward the south. A door at the head of the way, which way was before the separate porch, for those entering by the eastern way.* The southern quarter, therefore, had a like porch before the separate building, a like house, a like portico, a like [0573C] chamber alongside the inner court. There was, therefore, one extremity of the aforesaid chamber over against the last twenty cubits of the inner court, as was said above, on the one side; and in the same manner there was another over against the last twenty cubits of the inner court on the other side; and between those two an interval of sixty cubits. In this interval the porch of the temple was situated, which on the east adhered to the inner court, and on the west, as has been said, had the temple adhering to it.
