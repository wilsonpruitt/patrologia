## CHAPTER XIX. *On the description of the altar of holocaust.*

It still remains to speak of the altar and to explain its description. *But these,* he says, *are the measures of the altar in the truest cubit, which had a cubit and a palm. [n: (Ezech. XLIII)]*. Concerning this cubit we have already taught above, and we ought not to repeat the same. In its bosom there was a cubit, and a cubit in breadth, and the definition [0597D] up to its lip round about, of one palm. That I may briefly run over the measure of the whole altar: it was of five cubits in height, and up to the end of the fourth cubit it had twelve cubits as much of length as of breadth. But from the fourth cubit upward it had a certain projection of one cubit on every side, so that in its topmost parts, over against the fifth cubit, the altar was of fourteen cubits in length and fourteen in breadth. That projection therefore had one cubit of height and one cubit of breadth on every side beyond the measure of the altar, so that of it may be understood what he says, "In its bosom there was a cubit, and a cubit in breadth." But the upper half of this topmost cubit the crown of the altar occupied; the remaining half [0598A] was beneath the crown. And the crown itself had one palm of thickness. Whence is that: *And the definition up to its lip round about, of one palm.* In this place "definition" is the same as "termination." And so the topmost extremity of the [sic: *altars*], or of the crown, where the altar was terminated or finished, was namely of one palm's thickness. But there was still, about that same extremity of the crown, another certain small projection hanging round on every side, which he also calls the lip. Whence also he says: *And the definition up to its lip round about, of one palm.* Or perhaps more rightly a certain opening, which was in the middle of the altar from the top down to the lowest parts, is described in that which is said: *In its bosom there was a cubit and a cubit in breadth.* But let the other things which follow be expounded according to the former [0598B] sense. There was also beneath the altar itself a certain ditch of two cubits in depth, which received the ashes that flowed down through that opening. Whence also he adds: *This too was the ditch of the altar; and from the bosom of the earth up to the last brink, two cubits, and the breadth of one cubit.* He describes the depth and the breadth, and is silent about the length; yet he hints at it in this, that he shows the two cubits to be terminated at the last brink, that is, at the base of the altar. And so its length seems to me to begin from the front part beneath the base of the altar, and to extend itself even behind the back of the altar, so that it might be able to receive the blood which was poured out at the base, and behind the back of the altar might lie open, as it were, for drawing out the ashes that flowed in. There follows:

[0598C] *And from the greater brink up to the lesser brink, four cubits, and the breadth of one cubit.* By "brink" we may mean the abrupt breaking-off, or the cutting-off, of some flat or surface. He calls the lesser brink, therefore, the base of the altar, which was of twelve cubits; but the greater brink he calls the base of the aforesaid projection, which was of fourteen cubits. And so from the one brink to the other there were four cubits, according to the height of that which was called Ariel, and the breadth of one cubit according to the breadth of the projection. But well did he set down the one cubit separately and with a determination. For the placing of the four cubits rose on high, but the situation of the one cubit bent itself to the side and spread out in breadth. But beginning from the greater brink, [0598D] why did he not first set down the one cubit, which according to this order comes first, unless because he wished, after the manner of the Latins, to set the greater number first? But he would seem to have set it down more orderly if he had wished to say thus: "and from the lesser brink up to the greater brink, four cubits, and the breadth of one cubit." This order the Seventy followed, who set it thus: *From the lesser propitiatory up to the greater propitiatory,* designating by propitiatory and propitiatory the same places which we understand in the brinks. And indeed rightly enough could these two places be called propitiatories, because in these two places the blood was wont to be placed or poured out for expiation and propitiation: *But the Ariel itself of four [0599A] cubits.* By "Ariel" he means the body itself of the altar, which was of four cubits in height, as has been said, above which the projection spoken of above rose. Which perhaps he named Ariel, that is, *strong lion*, for this reason, that after the manner of a lion it devoured and swallowed up the victims of the people through that opening already spoken of. And from the Ariel upward, four horns, namely at the four corners of the altar. *And the Ariel of twelve cubits in length by twelve cubits of breadth, four-square with equal sides.* This is said of the body of the altar, as we have already taught above. *And the brink of fourteen [0600A] cubits in length by fourteen cubits in breadth at its four corners.* And of this likewise we have said above, that a measure of this kind was about the upper parts of the altar. And a crown round about it of half a cubit, namely of height. For concerning the thickness it was said above: *And its bosom of one cubit round about.* Recall the two senses which we set down above: according to the first, what is now said will be a superfluous repetition; according to the second, it will be a necessary addition for completing the height of the altar.

## CHAPTER XX. *A description or division of the land of promise, and what portion is owed to the priests, and what to the Levites.*

[0599B]

*Concerning this sanctified place he at once adds a few things, that it is for the priests, and far afterward in what follows. as if he should say, there will be in all the aforesaid sanctified place another sanctified place, in which will be the sanctuary, and the temple, so that it may itself rightly be called the sanctified place of the sanctified place, just as [0599C] it is wont to be said, the holy of holies.*

*That he says "from this measure" is not to be referred only to what is nearest, but to the whole which is comprehended in the verses above. What is now bidden to be measured is that which a little after is said ought to be the Levites'.*

[0600B] *It is plain from what comes after that the temple was in the lot of the priests. That therefore he says "in it" is to be referred to that measured thing concerning which it is premised, "and from this measure." For in the middle of that measure was the temple. But the interposition subjoined, and the reference made in the neuter, makes the letter obscure.*

*By these two following verses he determines whose those two aforesaid measures ought to be, and in the first indeed concerning the first. The sanctified place,* he says, *of the land shall be for the priests; as if he should say: That which above I named the sanctified place — that, I say, shall be for the priests.*

[0600C] *This is that measure which he set down in the second place, where he says: Thou shalt measure a length of twenty-five thousand, and a breadth of ten thousand.*

The sanctified place of the land, a length of twenty-five thousand, and a breadth of ten thousand. It shall be sanctified in all its border round about. And on every side there shall be a sanctified place, five hundred by five hundred, four-square round about, and fifty cubits for its suburbs [0599C] round about.

And from this measure thou shalt measure a length of twenty-five thousand, and a breadth of ten thousand.

[0600B]And in it shall be the temple, and the Holy of Holies.

The sanctified place of the land shall be for the priests, the ministers of the sanctuary, who draw near to the ministry.

[0600C]And there shall be for them a place for houses, and a sanctuary of holiness; but twenty-five thousand of length and ten thousand of breadth shall be for the Levites, who minister to the house.
