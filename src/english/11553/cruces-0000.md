# Cruces — 11553 (Richard of St Victor, *In visionem Ezechielis*), chunks 0000–0004

Migne, PL 196, cols 0527A–0545A (Prologue and Capp. I–IV). Printed readings are
rendered literally in the translation per `translation-style.md` Pattern 7;
conjectures live here only.

## Carried type (Pattern 10 + 12 — `[sic: …]` in the English)

- **col 0535C (chunk 0002)** — *"centum cubitos longitudinis, et centum cubitos
  **latitudiuis**"*. `latitudiuis` is not a word; near-certainly *latitudinis*
  (n/u inversion, a standard Migne compositor slip). Carried untranslated in
  italics inside `[sic: *latitudiuis*]`.

- **col 0536A (chunk 0002)** — *"Eminebat itaque templum cum vestibulo suo a
  parte **occident eratali**, situmque in atrio exteriori"*. A split/interpolated
  run: the sense requires *a parte occidentali* ("on the western side"), and the
  intruding letters `erat` appear to be a displaced verb driven into the middle
  of *occidentali*. Both fragments are carried as one run per Pattern 10
  (`[sic: *occident eratali*]`); rendering only *occidentali* would repair the
  plate silently.

- **col 0544C (chunk 0004)** — *"**qninquaginta** cubitorum longitudinem ejus,
  et latitudinem viginti quinque cubitorum"*. `qninquaginta` for *quinquaginta*
  (u/n inversion). Carried as `[sic: *qninquaginta*]`. The **arithmetic
  confirms the intended figure**: the three porches are 25 + 10 + 25, and
  Richard's own summary two sentences later says they *"implebant cubitos
  sexaginta"* — so the porch is 50 long × 25 wide, exactly as the parallel
  southern-gate lemma (col 0544D) prints *quinquaginta* correctly. The defect
  is orthographic only; no measurement is in doubt.

## Readings rendered literally though they will not construe

- **col 0527B (chunk 0000)** — *"**Supervacuum** enim et **inane** quis possit
  solidum aliquid fundare, vel firmiter statuere?"* The two accusatives have no
  governing preposition; the argument requires something like *in supervacuo et
  inani* ("upon the superfluous and the empty"). Rendered "For upon the
  superfluous and the empty, who could found anything solid…", which is as close
  as the printed words allow; the supplied "upon" is the minimum needed to put
  the accusatives into English at all and is flagged here rather than concealed.

- **col 0527B (chunk 0000)** — *"ubi ipsa sibi **expugnat**, vel nihil nisi
  frivolum resonat"*. *expugnare* ("to storm, take by assault") does not take a
  dative and does not construe with *sibi*; the expected verb is *repugnat*
  ("fights against itself"), which the whole clause requires. **Rendered as
  printed** ("where it assails itself"), not repaired: *expugnat* is a real word
  and takes no `[sic: …]` per Pattern 12's boundary.

- **col 0537B (chunk 0003)** — *"Adductus est autem in terram promissionis,
  **haud dubium ante quod** de terra Babylonis…"*. *ante* will not construe here;
  the expected reading is *haud dubium **autem** quod* ("but there is no doubt
  that"). **The printed `haud` is carried** ("there is no doubt") and *ante* is
  rendered "before", producing the awkward "there is no doubt before, that it was
  from the land of Babylon". This is deliberate: smoothing *ante* away would be
  exactly the silent repair Pattern 7a forbids.

- **col 0540B (chunk 0003)** — *"Sed dicitur: ***Ei** ascendit per gradus
  ejus.*"* Richard has quoted this same lemma nine lines earlier as *"et ascendit
  per gradus ejus"* (col 0540A), and the Vulgate (Ezek. 40:6) reads *et*. `Ei` is
  therefore a compositor's slip for *Et*. Because *ei* IS a Latin word, Pattern
  12 forbids carrying it untranslated, so it is **rendered literally** — "To him
  he went up by its steps" — which is the closest English the printed words
  allow. The reader should know the sentence is Migne's, not the translator's.
  **This is the one place in chunks 0000–0004 where the literal rendering is
  actively misleading**, and it is the class Pattern 12 flags as still open
  (cf. 11081 col. 0030B).

- **col 0535A (chunk 0002)** — *"Sane si consulas tam rationem quam Scripturam,
  **inventes** unumquemque trium horum murorum tres portas habuisse."* `inventes`
  for *invenies*. Treated as a Pattern 9 orthographic variant (e/i interchange is
  routine in medieval Latin) and translated normally, "you will find"; logged so a
  later sweep does not read it as a silent repair. If the volume editor prefers to
  class it as broken type, it would take `[sic: *inventes*]`.

## Scripture divergences from the received text

- **col 0533C (chunk 0002)** — Ezek. 42:20 is printed *"Per quatuor ventos mensus
  est **illud** murum ejus undique per circuitum"*. The Vulgate has no *illud*
  (*mensus est murum eius*). Rendered as printed ("he measured it, its wall on
  every side round about"). No `[var: …]` — the intrusive pronoun does not change
  the sense.

- **col 0541D (chunk 0004)** — Isa. 62:6 is printed *"Super muros tuos,
  Hierusalem, constitui custodes, tota die et nocte non tacebunt **laudare nomen
  Domini**"*. The Vulgate reads *tota die et tota nocte perpetuo non tacebunt*
  and has no *laudare nomen Domini*; Migne's clause supplies an object the
  received text lacks. Rendered as printed. Borderline for `[var: …]`; logged
  here instead, since the added phrase specifies rather than reverses the sense.

- **col 0530B (chunk 0001)** — Ezek. 1:11 is printed *"Duae pennae singulorum
  **jungebant**"* (active) where the Vulgate has *iungebantur* (passive).
  Rendered as printed, "The two wings of each joined." Richard's argument does
  not turn on the voice.

## Pattern 9 items (no English exponent — logged, English unchanged)

- **col 0527C (chunk 0000)** — *"Ex his namque **quosdam, quae** Scripturam
  sanctam recipiebant"*: masculine *quosdam* with relative *quae*. English "who"
  carries no gender; nothing to render either way.
- **col 0532C (chunk 0001)** — *"nisi forte **qui** putet"* for *quis putet*.
- **col 0541A (chunk 0003)** — *"hic nomen"* for *hoc nomen*.
- **col 0542C (chunk 0004)** — *"Post hanc portam orientalem **de quo** modo
  loquitur"*, referring to *portam* (feminine).
- **col 0531A (chunk 0000)** — *"facies igneas habebant, **qui** calorem …
  fundebant"*: masculine relative for feminine *facies*.

## Punctuation carried per Pattern 8

- **col 0529C (chunk 0000)** — *"quia in eam etiam partem faciem habebant Nos cum
  ad posteriora redire volumus…"*: the plate prints no stop between *habebant*
  and *Nos*, though the capital shows a new sentence was intended. No stop is
  supplied in the English.
- **col 0540B (chunk 0003)** — *"Cur, quaeso, primo loco ejus mensuras non
  caperet … si hoc de alia aliqua atrii exterioris porta intelligi voluisset."*
  A question closed with a full stop. Reproduced as printed.

## Negation fidelity

Every printed `non` / `nec` / `neque` / `nisi` / `nunquam` / `nullus` / `nihil` /
`haud` in cols 0527A–0545A is carried into the English, and none is added. Two
paragraphs are negation-dense and were read against the Latin word by word:
**col 0529B** (*"Revertentes ergo non revertebantur … non retro vertebantur, quia
non convertebantur"* — four negations, all present) and **col 0541A–B**
(*"neminem esse tam improbum … quem … contraire non pudeat, qui … reprehendere
non erubescat … si … non subsannaverit"* — a coherent double-negative period, not
a defect). **No confirmed dropped- or intrusive-negative site was found in chunks
0000–0004.** The nearest approach is the `haud dubium ante quod` of col 0537B
above, where the negation itself is intact and the corruption lies in the
adjacent word.

## Numerals — all checked, all internally consistent

Logged as verified rather than as defects, since this volume has a documented
history of corrupt measurements:

- **col 0533 / 0534C** — walls of 600 / 500 / least; and 50 cubits of *suburbana*
  between the middle and outermost wall. 500 + 50 + 50 = 600. Consistent.
- **col 0535C** — thirty chambers, fifteen south + fifteen north. Consistent.
- **col 0535B vs 0543A** — seven steps to the outer court, eight to the inner,
  stated twice in the same terms. Consistent.
- **col 0536C** — house (100) + portico (50) − shared wall (5) = 145. Consistent.
- **col 0539B** — 500 cubits divided 200 (outer court) + 100 (inner court) +
  100 (temple) + 100 and more behind. Consistent, and matches the 200 cubits
  restated at col 0544A.
- **col 0544B / 0544C / 0545A** — porch 25 + gate-way 10 + porch 25 = 60, matching
  *"fecit frontes per sexaginta cubitos"* (col 0543C) and *"implebant cubitos
  sexaginta"* (col 0545A). Consistent.

## Translation-convention notes (not cruces)

- The work-wide architectural vocabulary fixed for 11553 is followed without
  departure: *porticus* → portico, *vestibulum* → porch, *atrium* → court,
  *gazophylacium* → chamber, *thalamus* → little chamber, *limen* → threshold,
  *calamus* → reed, *mensa* → table, *platea* → pavement, *arula* → little
  hearth, *aedificium* → building, *medietas* → half.
- Latin words Richard quotes **as words**, when his argument turns on their form
  and English has no exponent, are kept in italic Latin: *earum* / *eorum*,
  *quas* / *quos*, *unum* (col 0532B–D). Greek/Latin technical terms likewise:
  *exedrae*, *ethecae* (col 0536D–0537A).
- Where Richard re-quotes a lemma **as a phrase** inside his own prose and Migne
  prints it unitalicized, the English marks it with straight quotes `" "` per
  Pattern 6 (marks we supply), never guillemets and never added italics. Guillemet
  count in these five chunks is 0, matching the Latin twin.
- *CAPUT PRIMUM* is rendered "CHAPTER THE FIRST" (the 11539 precedent), keeping
  the plate's numbering style rather than normalizing to "CHAPTER I."
- `CAPUT III` prints without a following period at cols 0537B and 0541D; the
  English head reproduces that.
