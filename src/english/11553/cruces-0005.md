## 0546A (chunk 0005) — *utrumque* for *utrimque* in the Ezekiel lemma

Migne prints, quoting Ezek. 40:12: *Et mensus est marginem ante thalamos cubiti unius,
et cubitus unus finis **utrumque**.* The Vulgate has *utrimque*, "on both sides," and
Richard's own argument requires it (he immediately glosses the verse as meaning the
one-cubit margin lay "not only in front, but also on either side"). Decisive internal
evidence: **at 0553B the same lemma is re-quoted and there Migne prints *utrinque***.
So the plate contradicts itself between two printings of one verse, twelve columns apart.

Rendered as printed ("and one cubit the boundary, both"), with a `[var: Vulg. *utrimque*,
"on both sides"]` beside it. No `[sic: …]` — *utrumque* is a real Latin word, merely the
wrong one (Pattern 12's boundary), so what stands on the page is our English, not his type.

## 0549A (chunk 0006) — *liber* where the syntax requires *libet*

*…et quae de thalamis seu liminibus hoc loco dicenda videbantur ordine prosecuti sumus,
**liber** hoc loco ad majorem dictorum dicendorumque evidentiam … colligere, ut…* The
infinitive *colligere* has no governing verb; *libet* ("it pleases [me] … to gather")
supplies one exactly and is a one-letter difference. Rendered with the printed word taken
as the adjective it is — "I am free in this place … to gather" — which construes with the
infinitive and keeps *liber* visible. No marker: the word is real and translatable.

## 0553A–B (chunk 0007) — *ut in superioribus* where the argument demands *non ut*

**This is the negation site.** Migne prints:

> *Ecce limen hoc ut superiora portae limen dicitur, et tamen hic, **ut in superioribus**,
> nihil determinationis adjungitur, et ex eo ipso innuitur quod vere et proprie portae
> dicatur.*

Richard has spent the two preceding columns proving the opposite: at 0551B and again at
0551D–0552A he insists that in the *earlier* cases a determination (*juxta vestibulum
portae intrinsecus*) **was** appended, and that its presence is precisely what shows those
thresholds were not properly the gate's. Here he argues that *nothing* is appended, and
that the absence is what proves this threshold IS properly the gate's. The clause as printed
therefore asserts the reverse of the sentence it sits inside, and of the chapter around it.
Conjecture: *non ut in superioribus* (or *ut non in superioribus*) — a dropped `non`, the
PL 196 defect class.

**Rendered exactly as printed** ("and yet here, as in the foregoing, nothing of determination
is added"), so the English contradicts itself where the plate does. No repair, no marker —
there is nothing carried, only a printed word doing the wrong work.

## 0553D (chunk 0007) — *vestibularum* for *vestibulorum*

*Nullum ergo hujusmodi **vestibularum** limen X cubitorum capere potuit.* Feminine genitive
plural of a neuter noun. Pattern 9: a declension/gender mismatch has no English exponent —
there is no emendation to make or undo. Translated normally ("of porches of this kind");
no marker, no churn on a later sweep.

## 0556D (chunk 0008) — sentence ends with no terminal stop

*…Nam interior porticus convertebat faciem suam ad exteriora, et exterior nihilominus
convertebat suam ad interiora* — the plate simply stops; the next paragraph opens
*Notandum sane…*. Under Pattern 8 no mark is supplied: the English paragraph ends unpunctuated
as the Latin does.

## 0559A (chunk 0009) — *quadraginta cubitos* where the work elsewhere prints *sexaginta*

**The measurement defect.** Migne prints, in Richard's back-reference to the first
description: *de longitudine, **et fecit frontes per quadraginta cubitos***. Every other
occurrence of that lemma gives sixty — 0554C (*Et fecit frontes per sexaginta cubitos*),
0555C (*et fecit frontes per LX cubitos*), and Richard's own arithmetic at 0554A–B
(XXV + XXV + X = LX). Forty also breaks the very inference he draws from it three lines
later: two porches of twenty-five plus a third of ten fill sixty, not forty.

Rendered as printed ("and he made fronts of forty cubits"), so the figure and the arithmetic
disagree on the page, which is the evidence. Conjecture: *sexaginta*, effectively certain.

## 0559B (chunk 0009) — mid-sentence interpunct

*…quod quidem legitur et apud secundam portam **·** Et vestibulum, inquit…* Migne sets a
raised point where a full stop or comma would be expected. Reproduced verbatim (Pattern 8);
not normalized.

## 0559C (chunk 0009) — *longitudo* where the sense requires *latitudo*

*Notandum quod porticum per portam intelligit, et quod pavimenti solam **longitudinem**
describit: nam ejus **longitudo** ex alio melius patebit.* The second noun must be the
dimension NOT yet described; having just said that only the length is given, "for its length
will appear better from elsewhere" is self-cancelling. The parallel passage at 0558B–C
settles it: *Notandum quod solam longitudinem centum cubitorum describit. Nam **latitudo**
pavimenti quanta fuerit … melius ex sequentibus patebit.* Conjecture: *latitudo*.

Rendered as printed ("only the length … for its length will appear better"). No marker.

## Conventions applied, beyond the work-wide list

- *coenaculum* → **upper chamber**, *solarium* → **loft**, *tabulatum* → **planking** —
  needed because *thalamus* was already spoken for by "little chamber"; these three carry the
  storey above the gate at 0554D–0555A and had to stay distinct from it.
- *margo* → **margin** (the one-cubit ledge round each little chamber), *ejectura muri* →
  **projection of the wall**.
- *frons* → **front** throughout (never "façade"), including the plural *frontes* of the
  sixty-cubit measure, since Richard's whole argument at 0554A turns on identifying them.
- *determinatio* / *determinare* → **determination** / **determine** in the technical
  scholastic sense (a qualifying phrase that pins a term down); this is load-bearing at
  0551B–0552A and 0553A–B.
- Roman numerals are kept Roman where Migne sets them (X, XIII, XXIV, XXXIV, XLII, LX, XXV,
  VII, V) and spelled out where he spells them out, including the mixed run at 0556B
  (*XV gazophylacia … et decem et quinque*).
- *lxx* at 0551B is left in Migne's lower case, unexpanded.

## Blind polarity read, 2026-07-30 (unit 11553-u2) — one item left UNRESOLVED

**0551A — *Quam autem vir ille … metitur … Scriptura innuit quod …*: our clause
binding may invert Richard's inference. NOT changed; recorded for a ruling.**

Migne prints *Quam autem vir ille qui portam ingreditur hoc limen primo loco, et
ante thalamos metitur, patenter, ut arbitror, Scriptura innuit quod in primo
praedictorum VII vestibulorum invenitur.*

Our English makes the **measuring-order** the thing Scripture hints, and the
**location** the reason for it ("…Scripture hints that that man … measures this
threshold in the first place, and before the little chambers, *in that it is
found in the first* of the aforesaid VII porches").

The reading the argument seems to want is the reverse: *because* he measures it
first and before the chambers, Scripture plainly hints **that it is found in the
first porch** — i.e. the `quod` clause is the content of *innuit*, not a causal
tail. Two things support that: the location is what this stretch is out to
establish, and Richard states it as settled at 0551C (*Primo de limine dicit
quod erat in primo vestibulo*).

**Left as printed, deliberately.** *Quam* is unaccounted for on either parse,
and `cruces-0014.md` records a comparable unresolvable *Quam autem …* at 0571D
where the English supplies a bracketed word — so this is likely the same
defective construction rather than a mis-parse of a sound one. Re-binding the
clause would assert a reading of a sentence that does not fully construe, which
is the emendation Pattern 7 forbids. **No claim is reversed to its opposite** —
both propositions are Richard's — so this is not a polarity defect; it inverts
the direction of an inference. Flagged for Wilson.

**0536D — *oportet totum hoc et australem plagam habere vestibulum*** (chunk
0002, found by unit 11553-u1). Our English makes *totum hoc* a co-subject rather
than the object of *habere*. The resulting claim is the same (the south side has
all of this too), so it is not a polarity defect and was not changed — recorded
because it is a genuine mis-parse and was in no cruces file before.
