Richard of St Victor, PL 196, cols 0571A–0585. Rendered under `translation-style.md`
Pattern 7 / 7a: the printed reading stands in the English, the conjecture lives here.

## Carried type (Patterns 10 + 12 — `[sic: …]` in the English)

- **col 0574D (chunk 0015)** — *"Hoc tamen inter ista et orientale et pavimentum fuit,
  quod **iliud** secundum unam dimensionem octoginta cubitos … habuerit."* `iliud` is
  not a word; near-certainly *illud* (l/i confusion, a standard Migne compositor slip),
  and the following verbs (*habuerit … possedit*) require a neuter singular subject.
  Carried untranslated in italics inside `[sic: *iliud*]`.

  The **same sentence carries a second defect** which is not marked, because it is a
  real word doing the wrong work: *"inter ista **et orientale et** pavimentum"* will
  not construe. The sense demands *inter ista et orientale pavimentum* ("between these
  and the eastern pavement"), and the whole paragraph is a comparison of the two lateral
  pavements with the eastern one. The intruding second *et* is rendered as printed
  ("between these and the eastern and the pavement"), which is unconstruable English
  because the Latin is unconstruable Latin.

- **col 0585A (chunk 0017)** — *"ostium unum ad viam **Aquilenis**"* in the Ezekiel
  lemma (Ezek. 41:11). `Aquilenis` is not a word; *Aquilonis* is certain — the very
  next clause pairs it with *ad viam australem*, and the work prints *Aquilonis* dozens
  of times elsewhere. Carried untranslated inside `[sic: Aquilenis]`. Per Pattern 10 the
  run takes **no inner italic asterisks**, because it sits inside an already-italic
  scripture lemma and nested italics are not expressible.

## Scripture divergence from the received text (`[var: …]`)

- **col 0584A–B (chunk 0017)** — **the sharpest site in this batch.** Migne's lemma at
  Ezek. 41:7 prints *"Idcirco **satius** erat templum in superioribus"*, and then, eight
  lines later, Richard re-quotes his own lemma as *"Quod autem ait, idcirco **latius**
  erat templum in superioribus"* and builds the entire following argument on *latius*
  ("wider"): the sides do not reach the upper storeys, *"et idcirco templi amplitudo ibi
  **latior** erat"*. The Vulgate reads *latius*. So the plate contradicts itself between
  the lemma and its own gloss, half a column apart.

  **Both are rendered as printed** — "the temple was preferable in the upper parts" in
  the lemma, "the temple was wider in the upper parts" in the gloss — with
  `[var: Vulg. *latius*, "wider"]` beside the lemma. No `[sic: …]`: *satius* is a real
  Latin word, merely the wrong one (Pattern 12's boundary). Conjecture: *latius*,
  effectively certain, on the strength of Richard's own re-quotation.

- **col 0581C (chunk 0016)** — Ezek. 41:1 is printed *"mensus est frontes sex cubitos
  latitudinis hinc, et sex cubitos **altitudinis** inde"*; the Vulgate has *latitudinis*
  in both members. Marked `[var: Vulg. reads "of breadth" in the second member also, not
  "of height"]` and rendered as printed. **This one is emphatically not to be repaired**:
  Richard reads the divergence deliberately and expounds it at length (*"Secundum metitur
  non quid latitudinis ex alio latere exstiterit … sed quid altitudinis a superioribus
  portae usque ad primum coenaculum fuerit"*), and the six-cubit height is the figure
  from which he later derives the height of both gates and of the upper chamber (0583B–C).
  His text is the divergent one; a conventional Ezekiel would destroy the argument.

- **col 0572C (chunk 0014)** — Ezek. 46:20 is printed *"ubi **coquent** sacerdotes pro
  **delicto et pro peccato**"*; the Vulgate has *coquant* (subjunctive) and the order
  *pro peccato et pro delicto*. Rendered as printed. No `[var: …]`: neither the mood nor
  the order changes the sense, and Richard's argument turns only on *locus* and
  *vergens ad Occidentem*.

## Readings rendered literally though they will not construe

- **col 0571D (chunk 0014)** — *"**Quam autem vestibulum habebat**, Scriptura illa
  determinat. Quae via erat ante vestibulum separatum."* The relative *Quam* has no
  expressed antecedent; the scriptural clause that follows supplies *via*, so the
  intended reading is almost certainly *Quam autem **viam** vestibulum habebat*. Rendered
  "But which [way] the porch had, that Scripture determines", with the supplied noun in
  brackets — the minimum needed to put the feminine accusative into English at all, and
  flagged here rather than concealed.

- **col 0572A (chunk 0014)** — *"pro amore suo nominat viam quae ab Aquilone…"*
  *pro amore suo* ("for his own love") is odd of a naming-practice; the expected phrase
  is *pro more suo*, "after his custom", which is exactly what the sentence is about
  (Richard is explaining Ezekiel's habitual way of naming that road). One letter.
  **Rendered as printed** — "he names, for his own love, that way…" — because *amor* is
  a real word and can be rendered; the conjecture stays here.

- **col 0573D (chunk 0015)** — *"quod superficietenus in omnem partem absque omni ambiguo
  centum cubitorum erat, **sed illud**: *Et mensus est atrium…*"* *sed illud* introduces a
  proof-text with no verb and no construction; *secundum illud* ("according to that
  word") is the standard formula and is what the sense requires. Rendered as printed
  ("but that: *And he measured the court…*").

- **col 0575D–0576A (chunk 0015)** — *"Hujus autem trianguli basim … **ejus quae**
  hypotemisam, id est, lineam inclinatam si quantumlibet protraxeris…"* *ejus quae* will
  not construe with the accusative *hypotemisam*; the expected reading is *ejusque
  hypotemisam* ("and its hypotenuse"), which the parallel accusatives (*basim …
  hypotemisam … cathetum*) require. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow
  ("and of it which the hypotenuse"), which is deliberately awkward English for
  deliberately awkward Latin.

- **col 0582B (chunk 0016)** — *"Quod igitur dicit portam sex cubitorum, ipsum hiatum
  inter ejecturas **intelligere**."* A bare infinitive with no governing verb — an
  anacoluthon; the parallel sentence immediately following supplies *oportet referre*,
  so *oportet intelligere* is what is wanted. Rendered with the supplied copula in
  brackets: "In that therefore he says the gate of six cubits, [is] to understand the
  opening itself between the projections."

- **col 0576D (chunk 0015)** — *"et de tertio et intimo thalamo **dicitur esse**:"* followed
  by the lemma. The construction is loose (*dicitur* with a bare *esse* and no predicate).
  Rendered literally, "and of the third and innermost little chamber it is said to be:".

## Numerals — the arithmetic, checked figure by figure

This volume has a documented history of corrupt measurements (cf. 0559A in chunk 0009),
so every figure in these four chunks was checked against Richard's own reckoning.
**All of them agree**, and one of them agrees remarkably:

- **0571A–C** — outer-court chambers: 90 cubits ÷ X chambers = 9 each; ten on each side
  = the twenty of the later lemma. Consistent.
- **0572B–0573B** — 70 cubits beyond the northern house = 10 (interval) + 50 (portico) +
  10 (walk); the corner space 30 × 20 halves into 30 × 10 kitchens + 30 × 10 walk.
  Consistent.
- **0573C** — 20 + 60 + 20 = 100 across the inner court. Consistent.
- **0574A** — 200 + 100 + 200 = 500 from extremity to extremity. Consistent.
- **0575C–D, 0575D–0576A** — the geometrical demonstration is exact: five sections of 20
  give A F = 100, A E = 80, E K = 60; 80–60–100 is a true right triangle, and Richard's
  ratios *sexquiquarta* (hypotenuse : base = 100 : 80 = 5 : 4) and *sexquitertia*
  (base : cathetus = 80 : 60 = 4 : 3) are both correct. **The rise of the mountain follows
  from it and also checks**: 0.75 × (60 + 80 + 60 + 80) = 210, the figure printed at
  0576A; and 0.75 × 60 = 45, the figure printed three lines later. Nothing here is corrupt.
- **0576B** — 5 cubits = 7½ feet, one step per foot, seven or eight steps. Consistent.
- **0576C–D** — 13 + 2 = 15; 15 + 25 = 40; 40 + 5 = 45; 45 + 45 = 90. Consistent
  throughout, and the 45 matches the 45 derived geometrically above.
- **0580, 0581C–D** — porch 5 + 10 + 5 = 20; temple 20 wide, opening 8 + 1 + 1 = 10 leaves,
  leaving 6 on each side. Consistent.
- **0582B–C** — opening 6 + ½ + ½ = 7 leaves; (20 − 6) ÷ 2 = 7 on either side. Consistent.
- **0583A–C** — height 20 (double the tabernacle) gives gates of 14 and 13 (20 − 6, 20 − 7);
  height 30 (equal to Solomon's) would give 24. Both branches compute correctly.
- **0583D** — three sides of 6 cubits fit beneath a 20-cubit storey; 3 within + 30 without
  = the 33 of *bis triginta tria*. Consistent.
- **0585A–0586A** — breadth 20 + 4 + 4 + 6 + 6 = 40; + 4 + 4 + 5 + 5 = 58; + 1 + 1 = 60.
  Length 40 + 20 + 11 + 24 + 5 = 100. Both totals are exactly the figures Richard names.

### The one numerical tension worth recording

- **col 0574A vs 0574C (chunk 0015)** — at 0574A the inner porticoes *"sexaginta cubitorum
  mensuram **intra parietes** tenebant"*; at 0574C, deriving the wall thickness, *"constat
  unamquamque **quinquaginta** cubitos tantum **intra parietes** habere"*. Read of the same
  dimension the two are incompatible; read together they yield the wall thickness Richard
  wants (60 outer − 5 − 5 = 50 inner), which is precisely the inference he draws. Most
  probably the first *intra parietes* is loose for the sixty-cubit *frons* measured
  overall. **Both are rendered as printed**; no figure is altered. Recorded because it is
  the only place in these four chunks where two printed measurements of one object differ.

## Negation fidelity (Pattern 7a)

Every printed `non` / `nec` / `nisi` / `nihil` / `nullus` / `nunquam` / `ne` in cols
0571A–0585 is carried into the English, and none is added. The negation-dense passages
were read against the Latin word by word:

- **0572A** *"et **non** illa de quibus **nihil** dixit"* — both carried.
- **0574C** *"**nihil** ejus mensurae confert, vel detrahit"* — carried as "contributes
  nothing to its measure, nor takes anything away"; the second verb's negation is
  inherited from *nihil* in the Latin and is made explicit by "nor", not supplied.
- **0574D** *"**nonnisi** octoginta cubitos"* — rendered "no more than eighty cubits", which
  keeps a negation on the page; "only eighty" was rejected because it erases it.
- **0581C–D** *"Secundum metitur **non** quid latitudinis … Alioquin **non** possumus"* — both
  carried.
- **0582C–D** *"aperte **non** dixit … omnino **non** dicit … **nisi** quod debeamus"* — all three
  carried.
- **0583D** *"**non** plus quam tria latera … **non** habebant"*, **0584A** *"**non**
  usquequaque … et **non** attingerent"* — all carried.
- **0584B** *"**non** usquequaque pertingebant, vel … omnino **non** erant … obstacula …
  **nulla** habebat"* — three in one sentence, all carried.
- **0584D** *"qui templum ingredi vel **non** possent, vel **non** auderent"*, **0585A**
  *"**nec** locum orationis dixerat, **nec** quod in circuitu"* — all carried; the second
  pair required a supplied "[said]" to put the second *nec* into English, bracketed.

**No confirmed dropped- or intrusive-negative site was found in chunks 0014–0017.** The
0553A–B site recorded in `cruces-0005.md` has no counterpart here; the two places where
the printed text contradicts itself in this batch (0574D *et orientale et pavimentum*,
0584A–B *satius* / *latius*) are lexical, not polarity, defects.

## Conventions decided here, beyond the work-wide list

- ***peribolus* → "enclosure."** Richard glosses the word himself twice — *"ejus peribolus,
  **id est, murus** qui ejus gazophylacia … cingebat"* (0567C, chunk 0013) and *"sed
  peribolum ejus exteriorem, **id est parietem illum** quem ad exteriora habebat"* (0594B,
  chunk 0019) — so an English word is available and the argument survives it. "Enclosure"
  keeps it distinct from *murus* / *paries*, both of which are "wall". **Chunks 0013 and
  0019 carry the other four occurrences; if their agents chose otherwise, theirs is the
  larger share and this should follow them.**
- ***hiatus* → "opening"** and ***ambitus* → "compass"** — load-bearing at 0580–0582D, where
  Richard's whole distinction of the three senses of *porta* (door / opening / surrounding
  ornamented wall) depends on keeping the three words apart. *ejectura* → "projection"
  (matching *ejectura muri* → "projection of the wall" in `cruces-0005.md`), *valva* →
  "leaf", *copula valvarum* → "coupling of the leaves".
- ***deambulatio* → "walk"** (the ten-cubit way) and ***deambulatorium* → "walk"** at 0583C;
  *tabulatum* → "planking" per the peer convention.
- ***sexquiquarta* / *sexquitertia* (0576A) are kept in italic Latin**, unexpanded and with
  Migne's *sex-* spelling. They are technical ratio-names with no single-word English
  exponent, and the register rule keeps such terms in Latin; the values are verified above.
  No `[sic: …]` — *sesqui-* / *sexqui-* is an attested medieval orthography (Pattern 9).
- ***hypotemisa* → "hypotenuse", *cathetus* → "cathetus"** — the first is the standard
  medieval form of *hypotenusa* and is translated normally under Pattern 9; the second has
  no ordinary English equivalent and is kept.
- ***arduitas montis* → "the steepness of the mountain"** (chapter head of Cap. XV).
- **`CAPUT XV` prints with no following period** at col 0573D. The English head reproduces
  that, as `CAPUT III` was reproduced without its period in `cruces-0000.md`.
- **Guillemet count is 0 in all four chunks, matching the Latin twin.** Lemmata that Migne
  sets in italics keep the italics; lemmata Richard re-quotes inside his own prose, which
  Migne sets unitalicized, take supplied straight quotes per Pattern 6 (0571A, 0580–0582B,
  0584B, 0584D).
- ***sanctum sanctorum* follows the plate's own casing** — lower case in Richard's prose
  (0582A, 0583A, 0586A), capitalized *Sancta sanctorum* where Migne capitalizes it inside
  the lemma at 0572D.
- ***Notus* (0573B lemma) → "the south"**, matching the work-wide *australis* → southern
  rather than keeping the wind-name.
