# Cruces — 11553 *In visionem Ezechielis*, chunks 0018–0021 (Capp. XVII–XX)

Richard of St Victor, PL 196, cols 0587A–0600C — the closing four chunks of the work.
Rendered under `translation-style.md` Pattern 7 / 7a: the printed reading stands in the
English, the conjecture lives here.

---

## Carried type (Patterns 7 / 10 / 12 — `[sic: …]` in the English)

### 0588B (chunk 0018) — *Ante solaria* for *antesolaria*

*__Ante solaria__ quaedam intelligimus esse istas ethecas, quas, ut Hieronymus in his
verbis testatur, Romae vocant solaria de coenaculorum parietibus eminentia…*

Read as printed, *ante solaria* is "before the lofts," which strands the neuter plural
*quaedam* with no noun to modify (*ethecas* is feminine). The word required is
*antesolaria*, and **Migne himself prints it as one word twelve lines later**, at 0588D:
*per limina solariorum situs __antesolariorum__ exprimuntur*. So the plate divides at
0588B a compound it sets whole at 0588D. Split type per Pattern 10; the whole run is
carried, including the real word *Ante*, and marked `[sic: *Ante solaria*]`.

Conjecture: *antesolaria*, certain on the plate's own evidence.

### 0589D (chunk 0018) — *qnod* for *quod*

*Angulos itaque vocat, eo __qnod__ angulos tenerent ligna illa…* u/n inversion, the
standard Migne compositor slip already logged twice in this work (0535C *latitudiuis*,
0544C *qninquaginta*). Not a word; carried untranslated in italics, `[sic: *qnod*]`.
Conjecture: *quod*, certain.

### 0590D (chunk 0018) — *sta* for *ista*

*…quae ab eo loco incipiebat ubi __sta__ porticus porticui aquilonaris domus juncta erat…*
A dropped initial letter; *sta* is not a Latin word in this position. Carried untranslated
in italics, `[sic: *sta*]`. Conjecture: *ista* ("that portico"), near-certain — Richard uses
*ista porticus* of this same triple portico repeatedly in Capp. XIII–XVII.

### 0598A (chunk 0021) — *altars* for *altaris*

*Erat itaque suprema extremitas __altars__, vel coronae, ubi altare terminabatur…*
A dropped *i*; not a word. Carried untranslated in italics, `[sic: *altars*]`.
Conjecture: *altaris*, certain — the genitive is required by *extremitas*, and the plate
prints *altaris* correctly in the same sentence's neighbours (0597D, 0598B, 0598C).

## Readings rendered literally though they will not construe

### 0587B (chunk 0018) — *hunc inde* for *hinc inde*

*…unde constat inter templum et utramque domum __hunc inde__ quadraginta cubitos fuisse…*
The phrase Richard uses throughout the work for "on this side and on that" is *hinc inde*
(0588B in this very chunk, 0590D, and passim). *hunc* is a real Latin word (masculine
accusative of *hic*) and therefore takes no `[sic: …]` under Pattern 12's boundary, but it
governs nothing here and leaves *inde* stranded. **Rendered as closely as the printed words
allow** — "there were, this one thence, forty cubits" — rather than smoothed to the idiom.
Conjecture: *hinc inde*.

### 0591B (chunk 0018) — *Nondum sane* where the argument requires *Notandum sane*

*Ostium unum ad Aquilonem. __Nondum sane__, quia quod prius quasi singulariter posuit,
postea pluraliter designavit.*

*Nondum* ("not yet") answers nothing in the preceding sentence and leaves the *quia* clause
without a main verb. *Notandum sane* — "it must indeed be noted" — is Richard's standing
formula and supplies exactly what is wanted; he uses it at 0588A in this chunk, at 0594B in
chunk 0019, and at 0558B, 0558C, 0558D, 0559C earlier in the work. The corruption is
*t*→*n*, *a*→*u*, *d* dropped.

**This is a negation site and the printed negation is carried**: the English reads "Not yet
indeed, because what he first set down as if in the singular, he afterward marked out in the
plural," which does not follow, exactly as the Latin does not follow. No repair, no marker.

### 0591D–0592A (chunk 0019) — *de interioribus aedificii* where the lemma reads *de inferioribus*

*__Inferior__ porticus de __interioribus__ aedificii eminebat, quia supra gazophylacia sita
erat, quae __inferioribus__ aedificiis respondebant. __Superior__ porticus de __mediis__
aedificii eminebat…*

The sentence pair is built as a strict parallel on the scripture Richard is glossing, which
he quotes twice in these two chunks: *Eminebant de __inferioribus__, et de __mediis__
aedificii* (0592C, and again at 0595D). The lower portico must therefore project *de
inferioribus*, not *de interioribus* — and the same sentence's own relative clause
(*quae inferioribus aedificiis respondebant*) prints *inferioribus* correctly.

**Rendered as printed** ("The lower portico projected from the inner parts of the building"),
which breaks the parallel on the page as the plate breaks it. *interioribus* is a real word,
so no `[sic: …]`. Conjecture: *inferioribus*, effectively certain.

### 0597C (chunk 0020) — *ut inimicitias vitaret*

*Forte ergo ut __inimicitias__ vitaret, imo absque dubio certi causa mysterii, ut plenum
mysticumque numerum exprimeret, minores hoc loco calamos posuit…*

"Enmities" has no antecedent: the whole passage is about why the prophet reckoned in the
lesser reed rather than the greater, and nothing hostile is in view. Something in the class
of *inaequalitates* / *ambiguitates* is what the argument wants. The word is real and
translatable, so it is **rendered as printed** ("that he might avoid enmities") and no
conjecture is offered here with any confidence — the reading is corrupt past secure
restoration, and is marked rather than adjudicated (the 9604 boundary).

## Migne's abbreviations in the Cap. XX glosses (0599B–0599C) — decision recorded

The italic gloss paragraphs opening Cap. XX are set very tight and Migne abbreviates inside
them: **`sanctific.`** twice, and **`sanctuar,`** (with a comma where the period belongs).
These are **translated, not carried**. Reasoning: an abbreviation is not broken type — it is
legible, unambiguous (the unabbreviated *sanctificatum* / *sanctuarium* stand in the same
sentence and in the scripture blocks immediately below), and it has a full English exponent.
Marking them `[sic: …]` would blame Migne for a defect where he merely compressed, and would
bury the four genuine carried runs above in noise, which is the argument Pattern 9 makes.
The stray comma after *sanctuar* is reproduced as a comma, per Pattern 8.

## Numerals — checked, and internally consistent throughout

Logged as verified results, since this volume has a documented history of corrupt
measurements and this work has already produced one (0559A).

- **0587A–B (chunk 0018)** — separate building 100 cubits between the northern and southern
  house, plus 20 cubits of space on either side = **140** across, as printed; and of that
  140, "the remaining sixty" is the temple's breadth, leaving 40 on each side.
  40 + 60 + 40 = 140. **Consistent.**
- **0587B–C (chunk 0018)** — the slope argument: the court is 40 by the level and 50 on the
  surface, and Richard says 100 : 80 :: 50 : 40. Both are 5 : 4. **Consistent**, and it
  matches the 100/80 pair established for the other courts earlier in the work.
- **0588A (chunk 0018)** — the whole court 140 long (S→N) × 100 broad (toward the East); a
  single part beside the temple 100 long × 50 broad. **Consistent** with the 50-on-the-surface
  figure just above.
- **0590A–B (chunk 0018)** — wall of six cubits' thickness holding two doors, inner and
  outer; matches the six-cubit reed of 0597A. **Consistent.**
- **0592A–D (chunk 0019)** — earth higher by **60** on the temple side; the first upper
  chamber projects **50**; difference **10**, which is exactly the figure printed.
  Total height 50 + 50 + 50 = **150**, as printed. **Consistent.**
- **0592B (chunk 0019)** — chambers 50 long and "not more than nine cubits" broad; six
  porticoes divided into seven portions. No arithmetic to check, no conflict with the rest.
- **0597A–C (chunk 0020)** — **the reed identity, and it checks exactly.** The wall is
  500 reeds and also 500 cubits; the reed is six cubits; Richard resolves it by a lesser
  reed of one cubit and a palm, and states *"octoginta et tres calami cum triente de
  majoribus complent quingentos de minoribus."* 83⅓ × 6 = **500**. **Exact.** This is the
  most load-bearing computation in the four chunks and the plate has it right.
- **0597D–0600A (chunk 0021)** — the altar. Height **5**; 12 × 12 up to the end of the
  fourth cubit; a projection of **1** cubit each way from the fourth cubit up, giving
  **14 × 14** at the fifth. The lemmas then print *Ariel duodecim cubitorum* (12) and
  *crepido quatuordecim cubitorum* (14), and the Ariel's height is **4**, which is exactly
  the distance from the greater brink (14, the projection's base) to the lesser (12, the
  altar's base). The crown takes the upper **half** of the topmost cubit, the remaining
  half lying below it. Every figure closes. **Consistent throughout.**
- **0599B–0600C (chunk 0021)** — the land: 25,000 × 10,000 twice over (priests' portion and
  Levites'), with the sanctified square 500 × 500 four-square and 50 cubits of suburbs.
  Agrees with Ezek. 45 as printed and with the 500-cubit wall of 0596D above.

## Negation fidelity (Pattern 7a)

Every printed `non` / `nec` / `neque` / `nisi` / `nunquam` / `nullus` / `nihil` / `nondum` in
cols 0587A–0600C is carried into the English, and none is added. The negation-dense passages
were read against the Latin clause by clause:

- **0588C–D (chunk 0018)** — *videtur determinare velle interius sanctuarium ejusmodi
  caelaturas __non__ habere* … *__Ne__ enim altiores vel demissiores ipsis coenaculis
  putarentur*. Both carried.
- **0589A (chunk 0018)** — *__non solum__ interius, sed etiam extrinsecus*. Carried.
- **0590B (chunk 0018)** — *__ne__ muri latitudinem excederet dum aperiretur*. Carried.
- **0591B (chunk 0018)** — *ut __nesciamus__ utrum aliquid (__nisi__ forte pro litterae
  expositione) ad praedicta addere debeamus*. Both carried.
- **0592A (chunk 0019)** — *Sed quis __nesciat__…?* … *__Ne__ ergo putaremus terram ab una
  parte sexaginta cubitis cavatam fuisse*. Both carried; the second is the load-bearing one,
  since dropping it would reverse Richard's whole account of the terracing.
- **0592C (chunk 0019)** — *in his vero __nullae__ erant*, and the lemma *columnas __non__
  habebant*. Both carried.
- **0592B (chunk 0019)** — *quae tamen __non plus quam__ novem cubitorum latitudinis* …
  *__non tam__ videntur parietibus interseptae, quam columnis distinctae*. Both carried,
  including the comparative *non plus quam*, which is the class flagged at 11088 @0896C.
- **0592D (chunk 0019)** — *Inferior autem porticus __non ubique__ a terra quinquaginta
  cubitis eminebat*. Carried; this one matters, because without it the sentence asserts the
  opposite of the ten-cubit correction that follows.
- **0595B (chunk 0020)** — *longitudinem ante templum centum cubitos, secundum planum habere
  __non__ posse* … *quod secundum plani considerationem omnino habere __non__ potuit*. Both
  carried, and the pair is coherent as printed.
- **0595D (chunk 0020)** — *et eadem replicare __non__ debemus*. Carried.
- **0596B (chunk 0020)** — *__ne__ duo habendo aliarum in hoc aedium formam excederet*.
  Carried.
- **0597A (chunk 0020)** — *Sed diversitatem calamorum intelligere __nil__ obstat* …
  *ad sui testimonium __minus__ sufficere videtur*. Both carried; *minus* is rendered "less,"
  not inverted.
- **0598D (chunk 0021)** — *cur cubitum unum prius __non__ posuit … __nisi__ quod majorem
  numerum Latinorum more prius ponere voluit?* Both carried, and the question mark stands
  as printed.
- **0597D (chunk 0021)** — *et eadem replicare __non__ debemus*. Carried.

**No confirmed dropped- or intrusive-negative site was found in chunks 0018–0021.** The two
sites where the plate defeats its own argument (0591B *Nondum sane*, 0591D *interioribus*)
are corruptions of adjacent words, not of a negation, and both are rendered as printed.

## Conventions decided here, beyond the work-wide list

- ***ethecae*** — kept in italic Latin throughout (the 0000–0004 precedent for *exedrae* /
  *ethecae*), because Richard glosses the word from Jerome rather than translating it, and
  the gloss is the point of the passage at 0588B.
- ***antesolarium*** → **fore-loft** (0588D). Coined against the settled *solarium* → "loft",
  and required to keep it distinct from both *solarium* and *coenaculum* → "upper chamber" in
  a sentence that uses all three.
- ***peribolus*** — kept in italic Latin (0593B, 0594B, 0595B). Richard's argument at 0594B
  turns on distinguishing the *peribolus exterior* from the portico itself, and he supplies
  his own English-equivalent gloss in the next clause (*id est parietem illum quem ad
  exteriora habebat*); translating the head-word would pre-empt his gloss. Where it falls
  inside an italic scripture lemma it takes no second marker, nested italics not being
  expressible.
- ***mansio*** → **storey** (0591C), in *in triplicem mansionem distinguebatur* — the sense is
  demonstrably a tier, not a dwelling: the same sentence resolves it into chambers below, one
  upper chamber in the middle, and another at the top.
- ***crepido*** → **brink** (0598B–0600A), following Richard's own gloss *praeruptionem, vel
  praecisionem alicujus planitiei, vel superficiei*. "Ledge" was considered and rejected as
  too flat for *praeruptio*.
- ***definitio*** → **definition** (0597D–0598A), kept rather than idiomatized, because
  Richard immediately glosses it: *Hoc loco definitio idem est quod terminatio.*
- ***Notum*** (0596A, Ezek. 42:12) → **the south wind**, not "the south". The compass rule
  fixes *australis* → southern, and Migne here prints the wind's proper name in a passage
  that names the other three as winds (*ventum orientalem*, *aquilonarem*, *australem*,
  *occidentalem*, 0596C–D); rendering *Notum* as a bare compass point would erase that.
- ***Ariel*** — kept untranslated as the proper name Migne prints, with his own gloss
  *leo fortis* → "strong lion" left in the italics he sets.
- **`propitiatorium et propitiatorium` (0598D) is NOT dittography** and takes no `[d: …]`.
  Richard has just quoted the LXX as reading *a propitiatorio minori usque ad propitiatorium
  majus*, and the following clause says these two places are the two *crepidines*: the
  repetition names the lesser and the greater propitiatory in turn. Rendered plainly,
  "designating by propitiatory and propitiatory the same places."
- **Re-quoted lemmata** inside Richard's own prose take supplied straight quotes `" "` per
  Pattern 6, never guillemets and never italics the plate lacks. The one guillemet pair in
  these four chunks is the III Reg. VI, 34 quotation at 0590B, which Migne prints — count
  1 : 1 with the Latin twin, as verified.
- **Latin word-forms the argument turns on** stay in italic Latin: *earum* / *eorum* at
  0591B, where Richard is explicitly weighing which gender is read.
- **Roman numerals**: Migne spells out every numeral in these four chunks; none is set in
  Roman, and none has been converted.

## A divergence from chunks 0005–0013 that needs a sweep, not a silent choice

These four chunks translate the chapter head-word: **`CAPUT XVII.` → `CHAPTER XVII.`**, per
the settled `CAPUT PRIMUM` → "CHAPTER THE FIRST." convention recorded in `cruces-0000.md`
and per the standing rule that `## ` head text is translated.

**The work is currently inconsistent about this.** Chunks 0000–0004 translate it (CHAPTER THE
FIRST, CHAPTER II, CHAPTER III, CHAPTER IV); chunks 0005–0013 leave it as `CAPUT V.` …
`CAPUT XII.` This is flagged rather than resolved unilaterally: the fix is a one-line sweep of
the English heads in 0005–0013 (and of 0014–0017 if the same choice was made there), not an
edit any single agent should make to a peer's files.
