## Sermon on the Mission of the Holy Spirit (cont.)

It is still to be noted in these words how fittingly and how distinctly both the plurality of persons and [1020C] the unity of substance are commended. For when it had been set down first, "The Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world," there is at once subjoined, "and that which containeth all things," not "he who containeth all things" — although in this way too it could indeed have been rightly said. But by a wondrous reasoning Holy Scripture willed, as has been said, to show under so great a brevity of words both the plurality of persons and the unity of substance. The plurality of persons is shown in this, that this spirit is called the Spirit of the Lord. For he himself is one, and another is he whose Spirit he is said to be; but the identity of substance is shown while the same Spirit is said to be that by which all things are contained. This Spirit therefore is that same which he is from whom are all things, that same which he is through whom [1020D] are all things, that same which he is who in the beginning created heaven and earth, that which he is through whom all things were made, and without whom was made nothing. This Spirit therefore will be the same as the Father, the same as the Son, because the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one God, one substance, one nature, one divinity, one majesty. Rightly therefore is it written of this Spirit: "The Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world, and that which containeth all things hath knowledge of the voice." But if this Spirit contains all things and likewise fills them, what is it, I ask, that the Lord's disciples received on this present day, if they were full of that Spirit who fills all things even before this day? What is it that in this festival of Pentecost we call to mind and honor the coming of the Holy Spirit, whom it is established to be everywhere [1021A] and to fill all things? Whence, or whither, should he come, if he is everywhere and can nowhere be absent; or what more would they take in, if even before they were full of this spirit? But it must be known that this Spirit, just as the whole Trinity also, is present in one way through the everywhere-present power of his majesty, and present in another way through inbreathing grace. By the presence of his majesty he is present to all creatures, corporeal and incorporeal, inanimate as well as animate. For the Spirit of wisdom reacheth everywhere by reason of his purity; through illuminating wisdom the same Spirit is present to rational creatures only, yet to all of them, whether good or evil, to the blessed alike and to the wretched. For he enlighteneth every man coming into this world; if every man, then the just and the unjust, those to be blessed [1021B] and those to be damned. Through inspiring grace he is present to all the good, to the perfect and the imperfect, to the blessed and to those to be blessed. For it is he who worketh all things in all, dividing to every one according as he will. He is therefore within all creatures by the diffusion of his substance; he is present to all rational beings by the participation of his wisdom. But to all the good he is present by the co-operation of his grace. By the presence of his majesty therefore he is at hand to every creature whatsoever; he is always present everywhere, so that never at all, just as never, can he be absent. In this manner therefore, he has nowhere to withdraw, nowhere to return, but everywhere at every time to remain unmovingly. But by the participation of his wisdom, just as he is unmovingly absent from certain of his own creatures, [1021C] so also to certain others he is always present; to the former, according to this manner, he can never be present; to the latter, according to the same, he can never be wholly absent. For he cannot be wholly absent from rational creatures, to whom the participation of his wisdom is always communicable. Otherwise how, I ask, would those be rational which could not at all be capable of reason and partakers of wisdom? But the irrational creature, because it is not capable of reason, cannot receive this Spirit according to the participation of wisdom. Otherwise it would not itself be irrational, if it had been capable of reason and wisdom. But according to the infusion of grace he can, to these same ones, both be present after a long absence and again, after a long presence, be absent, [1021D] and be absent yet further. According to this manner therefore this Spirit goes and comes, withdraws and returns. For *the Spirit* indeed *breatheth where he will, and thou hearest his voice, but thou knowest not whence he cometh or whither he goeth [n: (Joan. III)]*. For this Spirit, as has been said, now withdraws the infusion of his grace, now bestows it, and in this manner often goes and comes. But, that you may wonder the more, it often happens that at one and the same time this Spirit in some respect remains and in some respect withdraws. For when this Spirit of manifold grace withdraws one *Some grace from a man out of* those previously had, but permits him at the same time to have another, does he not, to the same man at the same time, as has been distinguished, in some respect go and in some respect come? You see then how differently the Spirit is wont to bear himself toward these [1022A] according to the threefold manner of his infusion, namely, of substance, of wisdom, or of grace. Indeed according to the diffusion of his substance he is believed to be everywhere; but according to the infusion of grace it is established that on this present day he descends into that land, and fills that land with the illumination of his grace, of which it is read: *I am come to send fire on the earth; and what will I, but that it be kindled? [n: (Luc. XII.)]* What is this land but the primitive Church? What, I say, is this land into which that heavenly fire descended, but the Church of Christ, into which today the Holy Spirit descended in fire and in tongues? This is that land which standeth for ever, in that it is founded upon a firm rock, and therefore neither shall the gates of hell be able to prevail against it. To this land [1022B] belongs whoever remains fixed in the faith and charity of Christ. For he who wavers in faith, or still tosses about through diverse desires, is like a wave of the sea which is moved and carried about by the wind. But he who is of this sort cannot belong to that land or whole world of which it is written: *The earth is the Lord's and the fulness thereof, the whole world and all they that dwell therein [n: (Psal. XXIV)]*. Nay rather he seems to belong to those seas above which that whole world, by the Lord's working, is founder, of which he also says? *For he hath founded it upon the seas, and hath prepared it upon the rivers [n: (ibid.)]*. But whoever hath not the Spirit of Christ belongs not to this whole world. For he who hath not the Spirit of Christ; [1022C] he is none of his. If therefore truly and beyond doubt the earth is the Lord's and the fulness thereof, the whole world and all they that dwell therein, then he must have the Spirit of the Lord who desires to belong to this whole world. Rightly therefore is that understood of this world which on this present day is sung so festally: *The Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world.* Was not the land of the Lord steeped, nay rather filled, with this Spirit, when the primitive Church, as has been said, received in fiery tongues that Paraclete who proceedeth from the Father? Does it not seem to you that at that time was fulfilled what is sung in the psalm: *Thou hast visited the earth, and hast inebriated it [n: (Psal. LXIV)]*. For the inhabitants of this land were so inebriated with that spiritual new wine that any one of those who at that time could hear them [1022D] must justly have wondered how they were drunken, seeing that it was already the third hour of the day. For they were all filled with the Holy Spirit, and began to speak with divers tongues, according as the Holy Spirit gave them to speak [n: (Act. II)], this assuredly is what we ought to call to mind yearly, this indeed is what we do call to mind yearly, when we sing: *The Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world.*

Do you wish to know this also, how in that land he contained all things in which he had filled all things. Consider therefore, after the filling of this Spirit, how steadfastly he bore himself who before was terrified at a maidservant's voice and denied Christ. So too the rest, who before hid themselves for fear of the Jews, now openly confess Christ before the chief priests. Whence was this to them, save that they had been clothed with power from on high [1023A] in that they had received the Spirit of the Lord: *For all their power is by the spirit of his mouth [n: (Psal. XXXII)]*. Whatever of steadfastness, whatever of patience was in them, was from that Spirit who had filled their hearts. In short, whatever of virtue, whatever of fortitude they had, they had received from that same Spirit who contained all things. Let no one therefore attribute to himself that he stands in the faith or perseveres in good. For no man can say, Lord Jesus, but in the Holy Spirit. If he cannot say anything good without this Spirit, when will he be able to do anything good without him? For it is he who worketh all things in all, dividing to every one according as he will. In him therefore stands everyone who perseveres in good; and let him that standeth take heed lest he fall. But how, you say, can anyone fall — nay, how can anything of good even slip away from anyone, [1023B] if this Spirit contains all things? Or is it perhaps that those things are said to be which this Spirit deigns to contain? For who would deny that he loses true being whom this Spirit has let go? In short, in no way can those things pertain to true being which are not from this Spirit, whom it is established to contain all things. For how are those to be said even to be, which have lost true being? But again these same ones return, as it were, to being, when they receive this Spirit of the Lord, he himself assuredly doing this who calleth unto those things that are not as those that are [n: (Rom. IV)]. For he shall send forth his spirit and they shall be created, and he shall renew the face of the earth [n: (Psal. CIII)]. Every state of rightness, therefore, as it is obtained through him, so none the less is it contained by him. And because that which is deserted by him in a manner ceases to be, [1023C] this Spirit is rightly said to contain all things.
