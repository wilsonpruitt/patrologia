## Sermon on the Mission of the Holy Spirit (cont.)

But I ask likewise how the same Spirit had knowledge of the voice. Or perhaps in this, that he made others to have it, as it was also said to Abraham [n: (Gen. XXII)]. Now I know that thou fearest God in that what would be learned I have made known. But that that knowledge of the voice which that Spirit gave was a thing to be wondered at and astonished at, and how greatly it filled the hearts of the disciples, those Jews bear witness, religious men out of every nation which is under heaven: *Behold, are not all these,* they say, *who speak Galileans. And how have we heard every man our own tongue wherein we were born [n: (Act. II)]*. But, lest it seem to you a small thing that they speak with the tongues of all nations, ask those same Jews further what they speak in those same tongues: [1023D] *We have heard them,* they say, *speaking in our tongues the wonderful works of God [n: (ibid.)]*. O how great a thing it is to speak the wonderful works of God even in one tongue. But although great was that which they sounded forth outwardly by word, yet far greater is believed to be that which they themselves heard within. Great indeed it was to hear that voice by which the disciples of Christ taught the wonderful works of God, but far greater was it to understand that voice of inward inbreathing by which they themselves learned, and yet they had knowledge of both voices, both of that by which they had learned and of that by which they taught. Otherwise they would neither themselves have known the wonderful works of God, nor have made them known to others. That voice which they formed outwardly was a bodily voice, that voice which they heard inwardly was a spiritual voice. The one sounded outwardly with noise, and expressed to the hearers one thing after another in whatever way [1024A] it could and as far as it availed; the other breathed inwardly without any noise, and in a moment, and in the twinkling of an eye, impressed upon minds in what manner it willed and as much as it pleased. O how great a distance there is between voice and voice, between the bodily voice and that spiritual voice! Yet of both he has knowledge, and of both that Spirit who contains all things makes men to have knowledge. For indeed, as you read, that which containeth all things hath knowledge of the voice, let it be a small thing, I beseech you, to you who hear or read these things, to have heard or known that voice alone, and to have knowledge of that which is wont to resound outwardly. Be zealous and take pains to hear and to understand that voice which alone suffices to lead into all truth, and which alone without the noise of words teacheth man knowledge, this [1024B] voice of inward inbreathing the prophet sought after, when he said: *I will hear what the Lord God will speak in me [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*. Yet what wonder if he panted after that voice, if he sighed toward that whose power, whose sweetness he had been so well taught and had so often experienced? You have heard above of its power; do you wish still to hear of its sweetness? You have heard, I say, how effectual those inward utterances are for teaching; attend now also how sweet they are for hearing. Yet let him say this whom experience had taught: *How sweet,* he says, *are thy words to my jaws, above honey to my mouth [n: (Psal. XCIII)]*. If it is so great a thing, and so delightful, to speak of what I have experienced, to hear spiritual men and to have teachers of truth so learned, how much greater and how much more delightful [1024C] to learn daily from God himself, to have learned daily. Blessed is the man whom thou shalt instruct, O Lord, and shalt teach him out of thy law. This blessedness indeed, and the losing of his Lord's teaching, the prophet feared, when he said in prayer: *O Lord, who shall be like to thee; hold not thy peace, neither be thou still, O God [n: (Psal. LXXXII)]*. It is assuredly a small thing to me if I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, unless I merit at some time to hear and to understand what the Lord God speaketh in me. For what does it profit to have knowledge of the human or the angelic voice, and not to know the voice of him who has knowledge, and, when he wills, makes men to have knowledge, of every voice? O truly blessed apostles, who had such a master, who received the Spirit of truth, who [1024D] had knowledge of all things, who could give knowledge of all things. O truly blessed, whose hearts this Spirit filled with his grace, whose souls he strengthened unto every virtue, whose minds he enlightened unto all knowledge! Rightly in remembrance of this deed do we sing what we have now so often repeated and do not cease to repeat: *The Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world; and that which containeth all things hath knowledge of the voice.*

Behold, we have now said in what manner the disciples of Christ could receive the Spirit of the Lord, who nevertheless in some respect could never be lacking to them, whom it is established to be everywhere and to fill all things. For he who according to the presence of his majesty is everywhere, according to the bestowal of his grace, as [1025A] we have already said, can be present and absent. In this therefore did this Spirit show them the abundance of his presence, that he poured into them the abundance of his grace. Truly therefore and beyond doubt they received the Holy Spirit on this present day, who awaited his coming with faith and with desire according to Christ's promise. But again I ask, were they without the Holy Spirit before that coming of the Holy Spirit? Did they not have the grace of the Holy Spirit before that day of Pentecost? If no man can say "Lord Jesus" but in the Holy Spirit, was Peter at that time without him, when so securely as truly he dared to confess before Christ concerning Christ, *that thou art the Christ, the Son of the living God? [n: (Matth. XVI.)]* Were the disciples of Christ without the Holy [1025B] Spirit when they cast the unclean spirit out of possessed bodies, and did many other signs in this manner? [n: (Luc. X.)] Certainly we know it was done before that day, nor ought we to doubt of that which we read in the Gospel, that Jesus breathed upon them, saying: *Receive ye the Holy Spirit; whose sins ye shall forgive, they are forgiven them [n: (Joan. XX)]*. But, to be silent about the rest, without the grace of the Holy Spirit they could not so much as believe in Christ. What then did they receive on this present day, if even before this day they had been steeped in the grace of the Holy Spirit? But it is indeed established that droppings of grace of this kind are given, now unto infusion alone, now unto diffusion, and sometimes even unto effusion. [1025C] It is infusion even if less flows in than the vessel could hold. It is diffusion when it is poured out so far that no part of it is left empty. Infusion now passes into effusion when what has been poured out overflows. According to the form of this likeness many receive the grace of the Holy Spirit, some unto infusion alone, some unto diffusion, and some unto effusion. He receives unto infusion alone who indeed perceives grace, yet less than could suffice for him. He receives unto diffusion who perceives as much grace as could suffice for him. He receives unto effusion to whom so much is supplied as could suffice both for himself and for certain others. Infusion is for sustaining, diffusion for satiety, effusion for disbursement. Infusion for refreshment, diffusion for filling, [1025D] effusion for dispensing. Infusion for the beginning of good, diffusion and effusion for the consummation of good. Infusion for the building up of oneself, effusion for the building up of one's neighbor. From this, therefore, a man receives that he may be of profit; from those, that he may make progress. We can therefore both have grace and yet lack its fulness. For it is one thing to have present grace, and another to have sufficient grace. Certainly even before this day the disciples of the Lord were not without grace, but on this day they received the fulness of grace. In short, there was one of them whom Scripture affirms to be full of grace, when it says: *Stephen, full of grace and fortitude, did many signs and wonders among the people [n: (Act. VI)]*.

The apostles therefore at this time received the infusion of the Holy Spirit unto diffusion, because [1026A] they were all filled with the Holy Spirit; and not unto diffusion alone, but even unto effusion, because they began to speak the wonderful works of God with divers tongues. Those therefore who were filled with the Holy Spirit received the fulness of grace. And those who before had present grace now began to have sufficient grace. What the first grace was, the fall of Peter bears witness; what the second was, the steadfastness of Stephen declares. Certainly Peter would not fall if he had the fulness of grace, nor would Stephen stand on so great a summit of virtues if he lacked that fulness. But when we say that they received the fulness of grace, we would indeed have it understood thus, that they received sufficient grace, not that they possessed all grace after that infusion. For to have been able to possess [1026B] or to take in all grace belongs assuredly to him alone of whose fulness we all have received. We therefore say nothing else in saying that any one whatsoever receives the fulness of grace, than that according to the state of the present life and the capacity of each he has sufficient grace. For that fulness of grace which man could have, or rather did have, before sin was indeed one thing; and another is that which he now has or is able to have according to the state of the present misery. For before sin man had sufficient grace both for avoiding every evil and for paying every debt; otherwise he could not have avoided sin. But this fulness of grace, just as no one has it in this life, so beyond doubt neither is anyone able to have it. For to which [1026C] of men was it ever given either to have fulfilled every debt or to have avoided everything unlawful? This is rightly believed of him alone of whom it is truly written: *Who did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth [n: (I Petr. II)]*. As far therefore as concerns the state of the present life, in saying that some man is full of the Holy Spirit, or has the fulness of grace, we say nothing else than that he has received from the Lord grace sufficient for all those things which the Lord wills to exact of him strictly, though not for all those things which he purposes mercifully to forgive him. Yet many and great things there are, nay, more arduous and more difficult than all the rest: which God exacts from man after he has sinned, which assuredly he would never exact if man had never sinned. For through many tribulations [1026D] must we enter into the kingdom of God. Call to mind, I beseech you, how great things they suffered for Christ who heard from him: *He that will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me [n: (Matth. XXI; Luc. IX)]*. You know well enough by what way Christ entered into life eternal. For it behoved Christ to suffer and to rise again from the dead, and so to enter into his glory [n: (Luc. XXIV)]. In like manner the saints also, having had trial of mockeries and stripes, moreover of bonds and prisons, were stoned, were cut asunder, were tempted, died in the occasion of the sword [n: (Hebr. XI)], that they might run after Christ, and, holding the way of Christ, might enter with Christ into life.
