## Sermon on the Mission of the Holy Spirit (cont.)

But would God exact from man the endurance of tribulations and labors, of injuries and reproaches, of suffering and [1027A] death, if man had not sinned. By no means would he exact it. But behold, he now exacts all things of this kind from man, and for all things of this kind he mercifully bestows his grace upon many, and bountifully lays it out. It seems therefore that man can in some respect receive a greater grace in the state of this our mortality, although he cannot fully recover the integrity of that first perfection. And in a wondrous manner indeed, he who is not sufficient for that first way of perfect purity now girds himself for greater contests of the virtues. As therefore we call a little vessel full when it holds as much as it can hold, so we assert a man to be full of grace when he receives such and so great a grace as the capacity of his condition [1027B] is fit for. One therefore receives greater than another, and one less than another; and according as there are divisions of graces, to every man is given the manifestation of the Spirit unto profit. To one indeed by the Spirit is given the word of wisdom, to another the word of knowledge according to the same Spirit; to another faith in the same Spirit, to another the grace of healings in the one Spirit. And in this manner one and the same Spirit worketh many other things, dividing to every one according as he will. Behold, brethren, what manner of Paraclete, behold what manner of comforter they received who merited to hear from the Lord: *I will ask my Father, and he will give you another Paraclete [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. O what a thing it will be to have as comforter him who is able to work all things in all, and to divide to every one [1027C] according as he will! If therefore we wish to have such a comforter, let us be zealous to be such as he may deign to descend upon, and with his grace: *Upon whom,* he says, *doth my Spirit rest, but upon the humble and quiet, and him that feareth my words? [n: (Isa. LXVI.)]* Behold what manner of man he ought to be who awaits the coming of this Spirit. O how willingly, how gladly ought we to cast ourselves down to every subjection, to bend to every humility, that we may merit to receive this Spirit and to have such a comforter! Consider, I beseech you, how sweet it is, and how delightful, to be cherished by his consolation, to be filled with his sweetness, whose pleasantness overcomes all pleasantness, whose sweetness exceeds all sweetness. Hear what he thinks of him from whom this sweetness flows forth: *My spirit,* he says, [1027D] *above me is sweet [n: (Eccle. XXIV)]*. O happy heart, that art filled with this honey! O blessed soul, that art refreshed with this pleasantness! Thou hast found honey, eat what is enough. Taste and see, I beseech you, who are of this sort; taste, I say, and see how pleasant is that Spirit, sweet above honey. I marvel if you will not cry out in the voice of exultation and of confession: O Lord, how pleasant is thy Spirit in us! Blessed are they that hunger and thirst after this inward pleasantness, for they shall be filled when they have tasted it. I boldly affirm that one single drop of this honey can do what the whole world cannot do. The heart which the abundance of the whole world does not satisfy, one drop of this sweetness fully inebriates. Certainly that great Alexander, who was able to subjugate the whole world, [1028A] was not able to satisfy his own desire. For, the world having been subjugated to him, if there were yet another world which he trusted could be subjected to him, with no less greed would he aspire to subject that one to himself. And if, that one likewise subjugated, others and yet others remained over, they could have provoked rather than satisfied the gluttony of that heart. Consider therefore how great is the breadth of that world which is within, which this outward world, however far extended or however often multiplied, cannot satisfy. Do not the philosophers seem to have erred who called man a microcosm, that is, a lesser world? Is not, I beseech you, that human mind deservedly to be called the greater world, which is assuredly greater than the world, which the whole world by no extension of itself, by no [1028B] multiplication of itself, can satisfy? For if this world be compared to the breadth of one heart, what else, in comparison with that heart's incapacity or greed, does it seem to be than a certain little morsel, [sic: *quiae*], cast into that mouth of the inner man, avails not at all to fill it, nay, scarcely appears at all? But if thou gapest after that inward and supernal sweetness, if thou sighest after true and eternal pleasantness, hear what is divinely enjoined upon thee, and attend to what is at the same time promised thee: *Open wide thy mouth, and I will fill it [n: (Psal. LXXX)]*. Certainly, if thou hungerest after earthly delights, if thou thirstest after carnal pleasures, it is better for thee to draw thy mouth together than to open it wide, to restrain thy desire than to extend it, since nowhere in any place [1028C] wilt thou find whence thou mayest be able to satisfy it and to give it content. What then is that mouth of the inner man but the desire of the heart? For to fill that mouth of the outer man is neither a great thing for God to promise, nor a great thing for man to receive, since one morsel of bread avails and is wont to fill it. But that mouth of the inner man (the desire of the heart, that is) is never satisfied, is never filled save inwardly with spiritual delights, and blessed is the man who has filled his desire from them. Certainly, whoever thou art that hast spiritual delights at hand or canst have them, thou hast wherewith to fill thy mouth, thou hast wherewith to fill thy belly, thou hast also wherewith to fill thy tabernacle. Enlarge therefore the place of thy tent, lengthen thy cords, stretch forth, spare not. The delights of the Spirit suffice [1028D] abundantly and superabound to fill thy mouth, thy belly, thy tabernacle, to fill, in short, that whole world of whose breadth we have already said many things, and yet could not worthily express it.
