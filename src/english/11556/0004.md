## Sermon on the Mission of the Holy Spirit (cont.)

When therefore the Spirit of the Lord shall in this manner have filled the whole world with that inward sweetness, and shall have filled thy desire with good things, thou wilt begin to wonder how that land which is not satisfied with water can be sated with that sweetness. Or can that perhaps be sated with honey which cannot be sated with water? Or can that which cannot be satisfied with water even be inebriated with spiritual wine: *Thou hast visited the earth and hast inebriated it [n: (Psal. LXIV)]*. With such wine assuredly were they given drink at whom the Jews wondered how they were drunken, seeing that it was scarcely [1029A] the third hour of the day. You have heard of those inebriated with wine; hear also of those sated with honey: *He fed them with the fat of wheat, and filled them with honey out of the rock. [n: (Psal. LXXX)]*. He knows not at all, he knows not what true and full refreshment is: whom that supercelestial and more than honeyed sweetness does not satisfy: and he who is of this sort can sing to the Lord: *For my soul is as earth without water unto thee [n: (Psal. CXLII)]*. But on the contrary, as often as that spiritual sweetness has satisfied the empty soul, and has satisfied the hungry soul with good things, he ought to sing and to say: *For the Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world [n: (Sap. I)]*, in that it is he who has filled his every desire with good things. But as we have already indicated above, not all receive this instilling of inward sweetness in like fashion: for some unto infusion alone, [1029B] some unto diffusion, and some receive it even unto effusion. If thou art still content with infusion alone, it will deservedly be said to thee: *For honey and milk are under thy tongue [n: (Cant. IV)]*. But when thou shalt have filled with a more abundant plenty of such infusion not only the bosom of the mind but also that of the mouth, and shalt have received this instilling of spiritual sweetness unto diffusion, thou wilt think it said to thee: *Grace is poured abroad in thy lips [n: (Psal. XLIV)]*. But when that inundation of spiritual grace shall have begun to swell higher, and to flow forth abundantly through the mouth, and the infusion of sweetness to pass over into effusion, deservedly, as I judge, such an effusion having now been made, it will be said to thee, whoever thou art, a soul steeped with such an effusion: *For thy lips are a dropping honeycomb [n: (Cant. IV)]*. This abundance of inward sweetness [1029C] he had surely experienced who said: *How great is the multitude of thy sweetness, which thou hast hidden for them that fear thee! [n: (Psal. XXX.)]* This beyond doubt he had experienced who refused all other consolation as often as he grieved over the withdrawal of this, whence also he said: *My soul refused to be comforted [n: (Psal. LXXVI)]*. For this alone has fashioned the mind in its inmost parts, and does not suffer it to run through alien desires and to wander abroad. He therefore to whom it is granted to experience these things must marvel how that human mind, a wanderer and a fugitive upon the earth, which fleeth as a shadow and never continueth in the same state, cleaves wholly to one desire of inward pleasantness, and how all that crowd of so many desires binds itself to one wish, or [1029D] how so great a household of such innumerable thoughts remains fixed in one and the same regard of its longing. But whence is this, I ask, save that the Spirit of the Lord who fills all things at the same time also contains all things? For in that very thing wherein he fills, in that same he also contains. For whence he affects our inward parts with his sweetness, thence he turns all the motions of the heart to himself, and fixes them in the desire of that pleasantness which has been experienced. For that running to and fro of wavering desires and of tumultuous thoughts is never checked unless it be sated with inward refreshment. For as long as the mind cannot attain to true and full refreshment, it has always somewhere to hasten by desire; and so it is one and the same spiritual sweetness which alone suffices to satisfy our inmost parts, and to compose them into one [1030A] peace, so that the same Spirit is rightly said both to fill all things and to contain all things. But this spiritual satiety poverty of spirit better claims and more easily procures than abundance of wealth. Truly blessed are the poor in spirit, according to the judgment of the Lord Jesus [n: (Matth. V)]. For the poor shall eat and shall be filled, and shall praise the Lord. On the contrary the rich have wanted and have suffered hunger. Deservedly therefore shall the poor give praise, who have merited to be filled. O how delightful is the praise that follows this satiety! O how inward is the exultation that accompanies this pleasantness! *The memory,* he says, *of the abundance of thy pleasantness they shall utter forth, and shall exult in thy justice [n: (Psal. CXLIV)]*. To this praise assuredly, to such exultation, the Prophet was inviting the guests of that inward pleasantness, [1030B] when he said: *Bless the Lord, O my soul, and all that is within me, his holy name [n: (Psal. CII)]*. And the cause is subjoined, when it is said: *Who filleth thy desire with good things [n: (ibid.)]*. How great, I ask, is the acclamation; how great, I beseech you, the outcry that rises on high and reaches the ears of the Lord of Sabaoth, when that whole inward household acclaims the divine praise together, and resounds with equal concord in the voice of exultation and of confession; when the whole substance of the inner man is dissolved into dancing, and the mind of man, loosened to the very marrow, is turned wholly into jubilation? This is that voice of salvation which is heard in the tabernacles of the just. Truly it is the voice of salvation, the voice of jubilation. For blessed is the people that knoweth jubilation [n: (Psal. CXVII)]. Who therefore could worthily unfold the dignity of this voice, [1030C] who could even worthily estimate it? That Spirit of the Lord alone therefore knows it, and suffices to weigh it worthily, who by his sweetness dissolves the hardness of the heart into dancing of this kind. What other voice, I ask, is more rightly understood in that which is said: *The Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world; and that which containeth all things hath knowledge of the voice [n: (Sap. I)]*. What, I say, is this voice which the Spirit of the Lord is said to know, of which above all others he deigns to have knowledge? You see how "voice" is here set down indeterminately, so that whatever that voice may be which is worthier and more excellent than all the rest may rightly be understood by the excellence of its greatness and by the prerogative of its dignity. So assuredly, when "the Prophet" or "the Apostle" is set down without determination, none other than the most distinguished of the prophets and the most eminent of the apostles, David [1030D] and Paul, is consequently understood. What then is this voice of voices, but that which alone avails and is wont to make a people blessed? Blessed is the people that knoweth jubilation. Blessed is he who finds in himself, who feels in his inmost parts, whence he may sing for himself, and as it were out of his own judgment: The Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world; and that which containeth all things hath knowledge of the voice. Blessed is that man whose desire shall have been so satisfied and filled with a certain spiritual sweetness that, out of the breadth or the greed of his own heart, he dares truly to confess that the Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world. Blessed is that soul whose whole inward heart shall have been so fixed from the inmost part in the desire of divine sweetness alone, and so estranged from every fall of concupiscence, [1031A] that by the testimony of conscience, for its inmost parts, it dares to profess; that the Spirit of the Lord is that which containeth all things. Blessed is that soul which attends within itself to an outcry of such rejoicing and of such giving of thanks, and which worthily ascribes the knowledge of it to him alone who in that soul fills all things and contains all things, so that with confidence it may say, and out of experience may sing: The Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world; and that, [1032A] which containeth all things, hath knowledge of the voice. Truly and beyond doubt he seems to me to experience the power of this solemnity whose tongue, by the testimony of conscience, is loosed into this profession, when out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh, so that he truly professes for himself that the Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world; and that which containeth all things hath knowledge of the voice.
