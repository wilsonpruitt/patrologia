## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

This soul, therefore, receiving the admonitions of salvation not now drawn off [1152D] from afar, but from the fountain itself, that is, from the highest Solomon; taught already in the Proverbs in what manner she may possess worldly things, and in Ecclesiastes by what reasoning she may now forsake and despise in her mind the things she has possessed; hears now at last in the Songs how she may pass over from things visible to things invisible, and cleave to the author of all with the whole strength of her mind and the fullness of her affection. Thus, from the moral discipline by which she holily shares what she possesses, through the rational, by which she rightly judges of worldly things, having entered upon that holy and highest *theoria*, having now utterly forgotten the things that are behind, climbing that holy mountain and the summit of her own mind, she follows through the midst of the cloud and the darkness that holy Moses to the heights of contemplation; [1153A] where, abiding in a secret dwelling [n: (Exod. XIX, 20, et XX, 21)], she seeks not lofty panelled ceilings, nor the painted flanks of attendants, nor acres of fields stretched out beyond measure, but, that she may abound plentifully in all things, seeks him alone who is all things, who is the light and the rest of souls, the fount of good things and the origin of virtues, whom to apprehend is the highest thing, and whom to enjoy the most delightful: and being driven within by desire of him, she utters what she feels, and no longer restraining herself within the cloisters of silence, speaks, after the manner of a lover, either to herself or to some heavenly Virtue akin to her, saying, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » Love indeed errs rather often in judgment, and while it is borne headlong by its own desire, whithersoever it has turned itself it seems to itself to behold the thing it desires, and that any other person too [1153B] experiences in himself what it feels: whence, as though it were plain to others who it is that love draws and as it were absorbs into itself, when she utters outwardly what she does within, she designates no one by name, but with a certain discreet indiscretion so says, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » This indeed is the secret of a perfect mind, and the soliloquy of a soul now cleansed with the heavenly Bridegroom. Come then, turn aside the edge of thy mind a little, and for a short while come down from the highest things to the lower; consider a soul still bound fast by unbelief, one who has not yet been enlightened to receive the rays of faith, has not yet touched the thresholds of the Church, and knew nothing at all of the Bridegroom: wilt thou judge that the Song rightly belongs to her? that she should speak to the Bridegroom of holy souls, saying, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth? » Would not [1153C] this Parable be unseemly in her mouth? But if thou consider a soul enlightened indeed by faith, yet dead in works, grievously oppressed and overwhelmed by sins, making for herself a long rope of sin, and not running back to the good by that remedy of penance: can the Song rightly be fitted to her, that, misshapen of mouth, and of horrid face, and foul in all her body, she should nevertheless say to the Father of all, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » But a soul fortified by faith and imbued with the sacraments, now raising herself by the law of the mind to the good of virtue, now, in the lapse into sin, taking upon her the law that fights back in her members; subject to this alternation, mourning, and in her mourning crying aloud: « O who shall deliver me from the body of this death? » [1153D] [n: (Rom. VII, 24.)] Will she fitly sing the Song, that, overcast with the tears of sadness and drawing the sobs of penance, she should say, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. »

But if, advancing a little, thou consider a soul upon a certain higher step, governing herself by a kind of even moderation, so that she neither now admits the sins for which damnation is due, nor yet climbs to the heights of virtue, dwelling in a manner with Lot in Segor, not yet reaching the mountains: she assuredly, in proportion as she does not yet touch the summits of virtue, is the more delighted by outward things, and burns less strongly in the desire of the heavenly kiss; so that neither to her does that rightly belong which was said, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his [1154A] mouth. » Enter now at last the secret place of the spiritual soul; consider a soul now established in grace, whom the spirit has drawn out into the mountains, who has now determined to dwell on the heights, who, the world being now overcome, has trodden sin underfoot, who, running after the Lord with Peter, has now said, « Behold, we have left all things, and have followed thee [n: (Matth. XIX, 27)]; » she, though she be far from the darkness of unbelief, from the reign of sin and of death, from the assault of temptation, from the torpor of the mind, does not yet presume so far as to dare, where she awaits the kiss of the highest King, which perhaps she has not yet tasted, saying, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth: » but either she sits content, with Mary, with the kiss of the holy feet, or, according to John, « she stands with the friends of the Bridegroom, and rejoiceth with joy at his voice [n: (Joan. III, 29)], » [1154B] which voice, most full of sweetness, she has now been accounted worthy to hear within, and in a certain appearance and promise of things to come alike to be delighted and to rest. From hence ascend, if now thou canst, to the sixth step of perfection, by which a love now consecrated enters the heights of contemplation; where the Lord is transfigured upon the mountain for her, and that most holy Moses, hidden from human eyes, now privy to the heavenly secrets, rests most sweetly in the very hidden place of the Godhead: here the most blessed soul, contemplating in her heart the King whom she loves, and stretched out toward God as far as in this life is lawful, is refreshed by the sweetness of the eternal vision; and, turning back the eye of contemplation upon all things that have been created, while she wonders not so much at them as at [1154C] the wisdom of the highest Maker in them, and at his unspeakable power, disdaining all that is created, gathers herself wholly into that wisdom, and is bound fast to it by the bond of charity and by the light of contemplation; and while she sees that all that is comely in the creature is not in the least degree comparable to his comeliness, she takes fire and burns toward that invisible fire, and, experiencing the wound of charity inflicted upon her mind by the sweet sting of the Godhead, and demanding that there be foretasted for her certain pledges, as it were, both of the blessedness to come and of that most sacred conjunction of the created spirit with the uncreated, which shall be without end in an indissoluble bond of charity, with an inward; and crying out with a sound of the mind audible to the holy and supernal powers alone, she says, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » For love kindled toward God is charity; and [1154D] the beholding, with intent mind, of him who is loved is contemplation: and these so accompany one another that contemplation stirs up love, and love always advances contemplation toward the things before; so that whither contemplation penetrates by the ray of the supernal light, thither it draws love after it; so that to the soul thus advancing the Lord is both illumination through contemplation, and true salvation through charity; as it is said, « The Lord is my illumination, and my salvation; whom shall I fear? » [n: (Psal. XXVI, 1.)] The soul, therefore, turned away from the world and converted to Christ with her whole mind, forgetting the things that are behind [n: (Phil. III, 13)], and stretching herself mightily toward the things before, beholding the world now far down beneath her, snatching herself wholly into eternity, [1155A] beholding by contemplation the highest and eternal things of the Lord Jesus, and by urgent love desiring them, because she wholly succumbs to love and languishes with spiritual desire; yet not presuming upon the full embraces of the bridegroom, that her love may be satisfied at least in something, asks a kiss, and, speaking in mind either to herself or perhaps to a familiar angel, imparting spiritual things to the spiritual, opens her love, and, loving, so begins from love, saying, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » And since these things have been said in the manner of a little preface, we shall expound the rest in the following tome, the Lord breathing upon us, who lives and reigns for all ages of ages. Amen.

*Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.*

By unremitting labour, by continual exercise of virtue, this faithful [1155B] soul has been brought so far that she aspires to the kiss of the highest King, saying, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » Hence it is that we by no means run on with a gliding pen, but stand as though clinging upon the heights, desiring earnestly to search out and to imitate the things at once hidden and perfect of this holy soul. In which matter this first occurs, that she seems most confidently to glory in herself together with the Apostle, so as to say, « Our glory is this, the testimony of our conscience [n: (II Cor. I, 12)]. » For she who seeks the kiss of the highest Godhead knows beyond doubt that she has a clean heart, knows that she has clean lips, an honourable face, and one not unworthy of the kiss of so great a King: so that there seems rightly to belong to her what was said, « For I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you a chaste virgin [1155C] to a chaste one [n: (II Cor. XI, 2)]: » [var: Vulg. *Christo*, "to Christ"] and again elsewhere, « That he might present to himself a glorious Church, not having spot, nor wrinkle [n: (Ephes. V, 27)]: » for this soul has no « spot » from uncleanness, no « wrinkle » from duplicity, no depraved conscience from hatred of a neighbour; there proceed from her heart no murders, no adulteries, none of the other things that are wont to weigh down the conscience and are wont to defile a man: for with all watchfulness she keeps her own heart, hearing attentively through Solomon, « for from it life proceedeth. [n: (Prov. IV, 23)]. » At the doors of this heart stands the fear of God, provident and solicitous, lest on every side a way of approach to its secret places lie open to the vices: at the bulwarks of this heart stand the stern battalions of the virtues, which do not now, as of late, fight against sins; [1155D] but glory in the Lord Jesus, the victory over them being even now obtained: and by how much the bitterer the battle they long experienced, by so much the more joyful is the glory in which they now triumph. Above stands chastity girt up, which rejoices that she has crushed the uncleanness of the flesh upon the rock of abstinence. With placid countenance stands humility, the companion of chastity, minding nothing lofty; but repressing by the Lord's power the elation of mind that ever seeks high things, and bravely and prudently dashing the foul madness of its head against the rock, which is Christ. Thus faith despises unbelief, hope despairing misery, charity envy, simplicity guile, innocence malice, truth falsehood, and each several virtue despises the vices opposed to it, as [1156A] monstrous corpses now mightily dashed against the rock of truth. Here thou mayest see that ancient concupiscence of the flesh lamenting at the funerals of her extinguished offspring; carrying round in the golden cup of Babylon her various allurements, and all that can be contrived by the most exquisite art of temptation, plotting this by force, this by all her craft, to see whether the things grown cold may be warmed, and the things extinguished may be raised up to their ancient spirit of life. That sacred choir of the virtues mocks her as she casts her darts, knowing that the Egyptians, smitten by the hand of the Lord, are not to be called back to life any more: whence, exulting spiritually, it takes up psalm and timbrel, singing out that song of Moses with full gladness to the Lord, « Let us sing to the Lord; for he is gloriously magnified: the horse and the rider he hath cast into [1156B] the sea [n: (Exod. XV, 1)]. » The evil rider is every malignant spirit; the evil horses are the worst vices; and with what violence the evil Angels who sit upon them are borne along is manifest, when they drag down souls wretchedly tempted into the depth of hell, and subject them to eternal torments, the divine justice bringing it about.
