## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

This soul glories that these are extinguished for her, and, sitting in the midst of the virtues, now entrusts herself wholly to them, and commits herself utterly to their services; and to her every virtue does service most humbly, each furnishing what is its own. Who could now run through with the pen, who could set forth in speech, what comeliness each several virtue confers upon this perfect soul, what admirable grace each several virtue supplies? Who could express her comeliness to the full? whom [1156C] continence renders chaste, and innocence simple, meekness gentle, mercy devout, circumspection provident, temperance sober, prudence discreet, patience brave; and whom charity, now perfected, directs with much reverence along the sacred paths of justice? For from the very beginning of holy fear, straightway planting her steps in humility, thence passing through the camps of unchastity, and so ascending through the remaining virtues as they are disposed in the mind by the Spirit of God, looking no longer upon vanities and false madnesses, she now entrusts herself wholly to love in a certain higher place, and commits herself to its most excellent fullness. Here, dwelling apart, she cannot but meditate upon what she loves; she cannot but contemplate [1156D] what she desires: with a thirst sent upon her from above, she takes fire toward that living fountain, and is daily refreshed with the sweet bread of tears; while she grieves that her desires are drawn out to such length, and, suffused with tears, makes her complaint. Here she frequently repeats and turns over with herself that saying, « To me to live is Christ, and to die is gain: I desire to be dissolved, and to be with Christ [n: (Phil. I, 21, 23)]. » And that other, « Woe is me! that my sojourning is prolonged [n: (Psal. CXIX, 5)]. » She is indeed forced to feel, and compelled to experience in herself, what she lacks of the highest, and how far off she is from eternity. A spirit released from the flesh has seen manifestly him whom it loves, enjoys the good it desires, is not tormented by the deferring of its desire, is touched by no grief at all; there love is wholly in the light, wholly rests in joy: [1157A] but the weariness of love in the present life is beautifully described, when it is said, « Laughter shall be mingled with sorrow, and mourning taketh hold of the ends of joy [n: (Prov. XIV, 13)]. »

In this life indeed the laughter of a faithful soul is this: that with a clean conscience, with a perfect heart, with a most full integrity of love, she knows that she follows the Lord Jesus into the heights, and that she reaches out the kiss of her mind toward his human things and his divine alike: yet this laughter is still mingled with sorrow, and mourning takes hold of this joy; since she knows that she has not yet apprehended him whom she follows with her heart, and considers that what she desires above all things is deferred from her. Hence the sorrow of the loving soul, hence the tears with which she washes herself through the nights, and waters the bed of her mind; that, being already cleansed, [1157B] she may be cleansed yet more, and that she who had been fair may shine forth far fairer. The soul, trusting in these virtues, conscious of this her comeliness, after vices long since stormed, after the gifts of the virtues obtained, after desires long drawn out, languishing with love, raising herself upward by contemplation, passing through heaven in her mind, asks a kiss for the solace of her grief, for a certain foretaste of the gladness she longs for, and thus begins the Song which is called of Songs, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » Thou mayest perhaps see an Angel indignant, and saying: What has been done? what is this so great and so new a change of things? why have the lowest been mingled with the highest? has heaven been stretched out above in vain? has a seat in the dunghill been appointed for man in vain? does the human mind penetrate [1157C] heaven? and does it, by no means content now with its own bounds, run through not only the regions of heaven, but the inmost parts of the heavens? But if in any way this has been permitted her, what is this, that the human mind should exalt itself to such a height, and should seek the kiss of the creating power, of the wisdom that works all things by a word, of a goodness subject to no constraint, that is, of the highest and inaccessible Godhead, saying, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth? » Has any Angel, or Archangel, or any spirit of the number of the Virtues, of the Cherubim or the Seraphim, ever so spoken with himself, silently or in mind, as either ever to ask or to hope for the kiss [1157D] of him beside whom we stand trembling, and whose embassies we have served by obeying for so many ages? When the Word was made flesh, and the Lord Jesus was passing through his human affairs upon earth, we called it a great thing, and accounted it a marvel in our eyes, that a poor woman was admitted to his feet, to fix a kiss upon those feet which she had nevertheless first wiped with her hairs and washed with the flood of her tears [n: (Luc. VII, 38)]. When the Lord transfigured himself upon the mountain, and revealed to human eyes his divine things, which he had always had, did that loving Peter, or the most beloved John, presume to seek the kiss of his majesty, who, not bearing the voice that came down from the magnificent glory, fell prone to the earth [1158A] [n: (Matth. XVII, 2)]. The Apostle too, caught up hence [n: (II Cor. XII, 4)], to whom the secrets of heaven were entrusted, and those holy words of silence revealed, did he, minding lofty things among us, so set his mouth in heaven that, not measuring himself, not considering his own human estate, he aspired to the kiss of the highest Godhead?

Moses too, going up into the mountain [n: (Exod. XIX)], hiding within the cloud, trembling at the sound of the trumpet, does he present himself to the delights of this kiss, who begs that that merely carnal law, soon to be abolished, be engraved not now upon the hearts of men, but only upon tablets of stone? Since, according to the Truth, « no one among those born of women is greater than John the Baptist [n: (Matth. XI, 11)], » and since, according to the same, « he that is least in the kingdom of heaven is far greater than he [1158B] [n: (ibid.)]; » it could have been enough for the human soul if even the least angel had received her with a kiss. This one's zeal perhaps, as proceeding not from anger or from malice, some supernal virtue tempers, answering him as he judges severely, that mortals are not to be resented if they desire to escape the snares of death, and strive with the whole effort of the mind to apprehend the liberty of the glory of the sons of God. For the condition of mortals is very wretched: set in the midst of the world, they behold hell beneath and heaven above; and for man not only is it easy to descend, but it is most difficult not to fall: to this the flesh is prone by its own weight, temptations press, malignant spirits drag him down with the ropes of sins, ever envying human salvation, as they have long envied our glory also. But as the descent [1158C] is easy, so is the ascent to the things above difficult: in this lies the life of men, in this the battle of souls, in this glow the endeavours of those who live holily in the world: that, escaping the snares of sins, they may pass over into our liberty, and conform themselves to us by purity of life, and join themselves with us in the love of the highest Godhead. For souls that have come forth out of the pit of misery and out of the mire of dregs, forsaking the vain things of the world, nay treading them underfoot in mind: with what devotion are they to be received, with how great a charity are they to be cherished by us, lest they ever look back and degenerate, but may unceasingly kindle their love toward the Father of lights, until they receive the gifts promised to the blessed? A soul, therefore, set in holy desires, spurning earthly things, not yet [1158D] apprehending the heavenly, let her be permitted to use the figures of her own words as a kind of incentive to her love; especially since, when she names a kiss, or an embrace, or a couch, or anything of the sort, she thinks nothing carnal, presumes nothing unworthy, meditates nothing at all save what is holy: [nt: ( For all that a wretched woman sins in a kiss, and carnal affection, is as far from her mind as she herself, purified by virtue, has withdrawn far from vice. )] For there is also a holy kiss, as the Apostle mentions, saying: « Salute one another with a holy kiss [n: (Rom. XVI, 16)]. » And, according to the Truth, the Father running to meet his returning son, « being moved with mercy, and running up, fell upon [1159A] his neck and kissed him [n: (Luc. XV, 20)]. » These things, therefore, which zeal had stirred up among the Supernal Ones, being lulled to rest by the working of modesty, we shall defer to the treatise following the things that are to be said, the Lord inspiring us.

*Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.*

As has already been said in the tome above, there is a certain kiss of foul pleasure, which a mind now lifted up to the things above disdains even to remember: But there is a holy kiss, which the Apostle mentions, saying, « Salute one another with a holy kiss, » and the truth in the Gospel, « the Father, being moved with mercy, fell upon the neck of his son, and kissed him. » A kiss, therefore, is of one sort a kiss of reconciliation, of another a kiss of congratulation, of another a kiss of gladness and of a certain permitted and sanctified pleasure. It is a kiss of reconciliation [1159B] when minds before at discord are called back, through some mediator, into peace and the favour of friendship; and the kiss that is shown outwardly is the sign of that confederation and conjunction of minds which is made within. But it is a kiss of congratulation when we receive with congratulation kinsmen long absent, and friends returned from pilgrimage or from elsewhere, and show the certainty of our inward joy by the very kiss that we give outwardly. But the kiss of the Bridegroom to the Bride is a kiss of a certain sanctified pleasure. There is therefore a threefold kiss, for which the church sighs, and which every faithful soul desires to be given her: for the holy church longs for reconciliation, longs for congratulation, [1159C] and longs also for that holy gladness which shall be without end. For while the Word was not yet incarnate, there was no peace of the church with God, which, after this life had slipped away, was drawn wholly into hell.

For man made himself wholly liable to eternal death, that is, in his stock and in his offspring, when he scorned to obey his author, from whom he had lately received so many and so great things, him (I say) who not only willed and commanded, but even held out threats, in a matter most trifling. For although it be no great proof of love if a man obey in a small thing, yet it is a great sign of an irreverent mind, and a token of the utmost contempt, if a man scorn to obey in a small matter: for according to the Truth, « He that is not faithful in a little, how [1159D] shall he be faithful in a great matter? » [n: (Luc. XVI, 10.)] By how much the less, therefore, was the thing in which man scorned to obey, by so much the greater is the sin that he incurred, and the greater the punishment that he deserved. For he who offends against an eternal Judge, without doubt what he deserved was unto eternity; for that judicial power which lacks an end inflicts a punishment without end: and as man deserved good without end at the hand of the Lord, when he departed from the truth. For as the good angel was first light, and afterwards a firmament, when he did not consent to him who desired to set his seat toward the North [n: (Isa. XIV, 13)]: so man too, if he had resisted the temptation, would by that very fact have deserved to be confirmed against temptation and sin, so that thereafter he could neither have sinned nor have been tempted; but, the number of days appointed him being here innocently [1160A] fulfilled, and the tree of life tasted, he would have come to an equality with the angels, he who had created him working mightily in this also. As, therefore, the reward of obedience would have been eternal, so, by the even scale of justice, an eternal punishment was owed to disobedience; and to it man, after his sin, being wholly liable, fell wholly into hell.
