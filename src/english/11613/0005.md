## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

[1163D]We observe that it was not any one just man from Adam down to Christ, but all men throughout the world far and wide, into whom the grace of the Holy Spirit had poured itself, who understood that from the seed of Adam one would at some time be born who, out of the trespass of one, would at some time destroy that same thing on behalf of all his members, and, the kingdom being handed over to the Father, would make men equal to the Angels. This faith, then, and this revelation in the spirit, kindled the desires of the Saints and drew their minds forward into the ages to come, so that they reckoned the delay of this life long, by which so great an expectation was deferred, and short as well, as though it were to be ended before they should behold this with bodily eyes. All these, therefore, whom one faith governed and hope urged on, that is, the whole multitude of those who believe devoutly, Solomon (in [1164A] whom wisdom had established a seat for herself), regarding them by that very light with which he was illumined, brings in, as a Bride, set in longings for the Bridegroom long since promised her, uttering the words of her desire in this manner: « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » By the threefold repetition, in which it is said, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth, » he marks a threefold kiss which she shows that she desires: the first of reconciliation, as has been said; the second of congratulation; the third, that of everlasting gladness. Reconciliation the Church desired, she who had learned that she had wholly sinned in Adam; and this, because she knew that it could be brought about by no other than by the Only-begotten of the Father himself, that is, by her Bridegroom. She desires that the Father kiss her, which is, that he remit his anger to her by the kiss of his mouth, that is, by the incarnation [1164B] of his Only-begotten, who is both the mouth and the Word of the Father. Whence it is also expressly said that the Father kisses, but with the kiss of the Son; because when the Father saves, he saves those whom he saves not indeed by his own incarnation, but by that of his Son. Yet since the Father also truly wrought that incarnation (seeing that the working of the whole Trinity is inseparable), therefore rightly, since it is a kiss of the mouth, the Father is thereupon said to « kiss », that is, to work in the Son that very assumption of flesh which he does not have in himself.

These things, then, having been said concerning the kiss of reconciliation, let us come, with the Lord's help, to the searching out of that which is called the kiss of congratulation. For usage has it so, and Scripture often calls it to mind. For Isaac says [1164C] to his son, « Come near to me, and give me a kiss, my son; and he came near, and kissed him [n: (Gen. XXVII, 26, 27)]. » It is read also of Jacob that, when he had seen Leah and had known that she was his cousin, « he removed the stone with which the well was closed, and, the flock being watered, he kissed her, and lifting up his voice he wept [n: (Gen. XXIX, 10, 11)]. » Laban also, « when he had heard that Jacob had come, ran to meet him, and embracing him and rushing into kisses, led him into his house [n: (ibid., 13)]. » Of Jacob too, when he had returned from Mesopotamia of Syria after that long sojourning, it is read that « Esau, running to meet his brother, embraced him, and clasping his neck and kissing him, wept [n: (Gen. XXXIII, 4)], » Joseph likewise, « when in his embrace he had fallen back upon the neck [1164D] of Benjamin his brother, wept, and kissed all his brethren, and lamented over each one [n: (Gen. XLV, 14, 15)]. » The prodigal son too, « when he was yet far off, his father saw him, and was moved with mercy, and running to him fell upon his neck, and kissed him [n: (Luc. XV, 20)]. » By all which, without doubt, is understood the kiss of congratulation, by which the parents and kinsfolk of their own congratulated them upon their successes and applauded them inwardly, showing outwardly by the kiss the joy which they cherished within. And this kiss of congratulation the Church hopes and expects to be given to her: which will be on that last day, when the merits of life shall be set forth, and the Lord Jesus shall render to each according as he has done in life, whether good or evil. For when the lost sheep has then been carried back to the folds on the shoulders [1165A] of the good shepherd, his friends and neighbours will be called together, the holy angels, to be sure, who keep his will with them in unbroken steadfastness, and for this reason are friends; and who enjoy his vision without ceasing, and for this reason are rightly named neighbours; to whom he will say, « Congratulate me, because I have found my sheep which was lost [n: (Luc. XV, 6)]. » which he is to say in like manner of the lost drachma as well.

This will be the highest congratulation, when not only shall that heavenly Church of blessed spirits applaud the good shepherd, but she also who shall have been carried back upon the shoulders of his humanity will offer her congratulation to the Bridegroom, who rejoices over her restoration, and to the multitude of the supernal virtues, which rejoices likewise. There will be fulfilled on that very day [1165B] what is contained in the history: for Joseph, making himself known to his brethren, « commanded that all should go forth out of doors, and that no stranger should be present at their mutual recognition. And he lifted up his voice with weeping, and said to his brethren, I am Joseph; and he kissed them [n: (Gen. XLV, 1)]. » Strangers will they be on that day to whom the Lord is to say, « Depart from me, ye cursed, into the everlasting fire, which is prepared for the devil and his Angels [n: (Matth. XXV, 41)]. » When these have been separated on that day, the Lord Jesus will make himself known to his brethren, saying, « I am Joseph; » as if he should plainly say: Call back to memory how, being sold and set in the prison of death, I came forth thence by rising again; and now behold openly that I both possess the sceptres of the kingdom and, [1165C] being thus endowed by the Father, hold the summit of judiciary power over every creature. Here the Bridegroom will congratulate the Bride, and the Bride the Bridegroom; and the indivisible joining of their minds will be made solid in a perpetual covenant. The Bride will congratulate the Bridegroom, because after the infirmities of the flesh, after the prison, after the scourges, after the buffets, after the spittle, after the nails, after death, after the sepulchre, after the great stone rolled to it, he has been so far exalted that in the flesh he took upon him he judges all flesh, and bends to his glory every knee of things heavenly, earthly, and infernal. But the Bridegroom's congratulation toward the Bride will be, that she has not run at uncertainty, that she has by no means fought in vain; that, death being triumphed over and Satan trodden down beneath his feet, he has gained the Church for himself the more gloriously by how much the more laboriously, [1165D] and has already endowed her, so gained, with unspeakable glory: of which indeed the good one will on that day applaud both all and each. For what is that giving of thanks by the Lord, in which he is to say on that day, « I was hungry, and ye gave me to eat [n: (Matth. XXV, 35)], » and the rest in this manner, if not a certain congratulation of the Judge himself and Bridegroom upon those inward goods of his Bride? For it is divided into six clauses, the very perfection of the number designating the whole fullness of all goods; by which in the present life every faithful soul pleases the Lord; by which also the Church wholly conforms herself to her Bridegroom. For the works are mercy: that the poor man be fed, and given drink, and the naked clothed, and the guest received under a roof, the sick visited, and [1166A] the imprisoned. But is God well pleased in these alone? Is not every several virtue pleasing to him? For what of that holy virginity? what of honourable chastity? what of the frugality of the Saints? what of silence? what of holy preaching? what of justice, fortitude, temperance, and prudence? what of the virtues beyond number, whose praise is in the Holy Scriptures, in the acquiring of which the continual and unremitting labour of the Saints sweats? But if the Lord is well pleased in these, what is it that in the judgment, when he shall congratulate the Church upon her goods, the Lord will call to mind these alone which were bodily performed? They are indeed goods, but not the only ones.
