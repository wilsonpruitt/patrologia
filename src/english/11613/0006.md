## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

It stands, therefore, that in the clause of words already spoken all the endowments of the soul, all the goods of the Church generally, [1166B] are comprehended. For the Lord hungers and thirsts: yet in saying this we subject the holy Godhead to no suffering; for the life of that supreme Godhead itself is incapable of suffering; and by as much as temporal life is subject and liable to suffering, by so much is that eternal and uncreated life utterly far from and a stranger to all suffering. Yet after the manner of the Scriptures he is said to hunger and to thirst; and not in one way only, but in several. For the Lord hungers in his members, and he hungers in himself. In his members he hungers, when any one of his own is brought to the necessity of bodily or spiritual food: which suffering charity relieves, to be sure, with the various works of mercy, refreshing with food and drink him who is wasted, and with spiritual things instructing [1166C] and building up him who desires the spiritual: for as it is a devout thing to relieve bodily hunger and thirst, so it is most devout to relieve the want of spiritual food as well: for as by the judgment of truth the soul excels the body, so it is far better, far holier, to relieve her than the body. Yet both are good, both are well pleasing to the Lord, even unto thanksgiving. Like is the consideration concerning nakedness also. The Lord is cold in body, and he is cold in spirit: in body, when the harshness of the cold is driven off by no covering of clothing; in spirit, when a man is clothed within by no garment of virtue. This cold is from the North wind, by which the hearts of many are not only chilled but hardened [n: (Job XXXVII, 9; Eccli. XLIII, 22)]; of which the prophet says, « Before the face of his cold who shall stand? » *(Psal. CXLVII, [1166D] 17.)* This nakedness of Christ in his members he covers, who forms and instructs by the examples of the virtues the man destitute of virtue. To whom the Lord, congratulating him, is to say at the end, « I was naked, and ye covered me. » But in what manner the Lord is a stranger and a guest in his members is plain; while in himself he is a stranger and a guest among those who have not yet received the faith which is in him: to such as these he himself also says, « The foxes have holes, and the birds of the air nests: but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head [n: (Matth. VIII, 30)]; » because unclean and deceitful spirits, possessing them, dwell in the darkened hearts of sinners; « but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head. » For the head of the Church is Christ; and the head of Christ is God; and with those [1167A] the Son of man lays his head who confess him equal to the Father, coeternal, and consubstantial: to whom the Son himself is neither a recent God nor an alien one (for to those he is recent who protest that he once began, and was not begotten of the Father from eternity; and to those he is alien who attempt to affirm that he is not the same in substance with the Father). Among these he is a guest and a stranger; to all of whom it is said by the prophet, « Let the deceitful lips be made dumb, which speak iniquity against the just, in pride and in abuse [n: (Psal. XXX, 19)]. » Among these he is a guest and a stranger, knocking by the prophets, by the apostles, by all the Scriptures, that they may open to him; to whom the Lord is to say on that day, « I was a guest, and ye took me not in *(Matth. XXV, [1167B] 43)*, » because the faith which is in me, which I preach, unto which I was sent, ye did not admit. Whence it is given us to understand who they are that receive the Saviour into their lodging: those, without doubt, who receive the teaching of sound faith with worthy veneration, utterly execrating heresy, and, simple as doves, gathering only the clean grains of faith; for whom indeed it is not enough to receive the Saviour under their roof by faith only, unless they receive him also with charity and virtue of every kind For he himself says, « Behold, I stand at the door knocking; if any man shall hear my voice, and open the gate, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me [n: (Apoc. III, 20)]. » The Lord enters in to us when we ourselves open the gate of the heart to believing: we are refreshed by a common supper when, love having been conceived from above, we are stretched out toward [1167C] every good work by the gift of spiritual grace. But to visit the sick, both brotherly charity and divine authority admonish us. For what is more fitting to humanity than that man should relieve man, brother brother, the strong the weak, both with the solace of a word and with the help of a work, with devout affection and full compassion of mind? And this is to be shown not only to him who is sick in body, but to him who is ailing spiritually. For in many the languor of the spirit so gathers strength that in them there is now neither the voice of confession nor the feeling of one repenting. Of these the Apostle says, « Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is scandalized, and I am not burned? » [n: (II Cor. XI, 29.)] These are to be visited often, and admonished with the word of life, [1167D] until, gaping seven times, they may open their mouth unto confession by the gift of the sevenfold Spirit, and may be conformed to the very teachers of life, not only by growing warm within, but also by the full ordering of their outward man. For hence it is that when Elias raised up the dead man [n: (III Reg. XVII, 21)], he spread himself over him; and Elisha, when he raised up the dead man, « applies his own mouth to his mouth, and his eyes to his eyes, and his hands to his hands [n: (IV Reg. IV, 34)]. » So that, as the Apostle says, those who are raised from death, by imitating the holy teachers, may now compel all the members of their body which before had been « vessels of iniquity unto iniquity [var: Vulg. *arma iniquitatis*, "weapons of iniquity" (Rom. VI, 13)], » to serve virtue Of these the Truth itself says, [1168A] « The healthy have no need of a physician, but they that are ill [n: (Matth. IX, 12)]. » Some of these indeed are wearied by lighter sicknesses, others by heavier and long-lasting ones. And so these, whom the oldness of sin oppresses, a certain prison of souls, as it were, shuts in; whose person the prophet takes upon himself when he says, « Bring my soul out of prison [n: (Psal. CXLI, 8)]; » and that other word, « I am stuck fast in the mire of the deep [n: (Psal. LXVIII, 13)]; » and again, « The cords of sinners have wrapped me about [n: (Psal. CXVIII, 61)]: » and to visit these in a spirit of gentleness, to prick them with the word of life, and, drawing them away from vices, to restore them to virtue, is a far greater mercy than to bring out those shut up in body by devoutly loosing their chains.

Since therefore there are two commandments, one pertaining to the love [1168B] of God, the other to the love of one's neighbour, on which the whole law hangs and the prophets, and in which the perfection of human life consists, the clause spoken above embraces in itself the whole by which either commandment is fulfilled. For by those things which are shown to our neighbours with a regard to the Godhead are to be understood all things by which we relieve any necessity whatever of theirs, spiritual or bodily: for among sufferings there is nothing more bitter than hunger, thirst, nakedness, want of shelter, sickness, chains: whence by these things, which press and afflict any brother more vehemently, are to be understood absolutely all things by which, at the working of mercy, with a regard to the Godhead, we relieve any bodily or spiritual necessity that demands it. For in his members, in two ways, as [1168C] has been said, the Lord hungers and thirsts and is cold, desiring now a bodily, now a spiritual remedy; but in himself in one way only: for he does not require this alone of us, that we bestow on our brethren what is needful; he wills that our faith and charity be directed principally upon himself, and be derived from him secondarily upon our neighbour. Those things, then, which have regard to our neighbour, these the Lord hungers and thirsts for in his members, and demands; but those which are not toward our neighbour, but are kept for reverence of Christ, these are they which the Lord is said to hunger and thirst for in himself, since with him this is the same thing as to will. « And the Lord, coming to the fig tree, hungered [n: (Matth. XXI, 18)]; » and hanging on the cross he said, « I thirst [n: (Joan. XIX, 28)]. » What [1168D] did he hunger or thirst for? Our salvation, beyond doubt, our faith, and the charity which is toward him, long-suffering, and continence, and the other virtues which, although they do not reach toward our neighbour, are kept for God's sake: for the virtues which are toward our neighbour he hungers for both in himself and in his members; but these which are toward himself, in himself alone.

It stands, therefore, that in the clause of words already spoken all the endowments of the soul, all the goods of the Church generally, are comprehended. And this is the kiss of congratulation for which the Church sighs, which every soul asks in the Spirit, saying, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » Thus she speaks to the Father, his mouth, according to this [1169A] sense. Naming the Son, and understanding by the kiss both of the Father himself and of the Son the Holy Spirit. For she says, « Let him kiss me, » that is, let the Father, he who created me, make me worthy of the kiss; and this, « With the kiss of his mouth, » that is, through the Holy Spirit of his Son, who is his mouth. For that Spirit is himself the kiss which the Father gives, the kiss which the Son gives; because he is a vapour, and a sweetness proceeding from both: who, as he proceeds from both, so both the Father and the Son pour him forth into the hearts of the blessed. For of him the Lord says, « I will send you the Spirit [n: (Joan. XV, 26)]: » and elsewhere, « The Spirit whom the Father sends in my name [n: (Joan. XIV, 26)]. » She asks therefore of the Father that the Spirit be given her through the Son, so that, receiving from him the gifts of graces, she may pass over into a certain new comeliness, [1169B] that on that day she may hear from the Father through the Son the voice of congratulation, and receive the kiss of congratulation. For it is the voice of congratulation by which the Lord is to say on that day, « Well done, good servant [n: (Matth. XV, 6)]; » and that word to the heavenly Virtues, « Congratulate me over the lost sheep. Congratulate me over the drachma that is found [n: (Luc. XV, 6)]; » as if he should say: Consider what the comeliness of the Church is, and how great; understand with how many gifts of graces, with how great a glory of virtues she shines; rejoice together with me that I have laboured for one such and so great. What (I ask) will be the peace of the whole Church at this voice? what will be the tranquillity of holy souls in mind? when at this congratulation of the Bridegroom, now secure concerning her own glory, [1169C] there will be nothing more for her to fear, nothing at which for the rest she should shudder. And this peace indeed is that security for which the Church sighs, that on that day, on which the angels shall tremble and the archangels be greatly afraid, she may have her Bridegroom appeased toward her, and, sanctified by his Spirit, may be pleasing to herself in the comeliness of the virtues; and through the kiss of the Son, which is the Holy Spirit, may receive from the Bridegroom peace, congratulation, and the gifts promised: of which may the Lord Jesus make us partakers, who is unto the ages. Amen.

*Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.*
