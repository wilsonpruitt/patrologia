## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

The kiss of reconciliation, therefore, and the kiss of congratulation having been set forth, it remains that we pursue, with the Lord's help, the kiss of pleasure. And lest this be perhaps troublesome to delicate ears, [1169D] if in treating of spiritual things we call to mind a certain bodily pleasure, let them have this before their eyes: that it is an epithalamium of the Bridegroom and the Bride that is being enacted, that it is a nuptial [sic: *carnem*] that is being handled: nor is it a wonder if in a marriage, in the Sacrament of spiritual things, mention be made of this kiss; since the carnal commingling itself of Bridegroom and Bride unfolds in the Holy Scriptures no small Sacrament among things spiritual. For that carnal commingling is a Sacrament of that joining which is in the Lord Jesus, namely of flesh and of the Word: so also that kiss of the Bridegroom and the Bride is a certain manifest showing forth of that gladness which, after that dreadful judgment and [1170A] the heavenly Bridegroom's congratulation upon the goods of the Bride, is to be forever. For pleasure is of one sort foul, and of another most holy: and that indeed is holy of which the Prophet makes mention, saying, « They shall be inebriated with the plenty of thy house; and thou shalt give them to drink of the torrent of thy pleasure [n: (Psal. XXXV, 9)]: » which pleasure indeed is called a « torrent », not because it passes quickly away, but because it pours itself in abundantly. For of this also « Isaias says, The eye hath not seen, besides thee, O God, what things thou hast prepared for them that love thee [var: Vulg. *expectantibus te*, "them that wait for thee"] [n: (Isa. LIV, 4)]. » According to which « Paul » says, « Neither hath the eye seen, nor hath it ascended into the heart of man, what things God hath prepared for them that love him [n: (I Cor. II, 9)]. » Whence the Prophet also fittingly says, « They shall be inebriated with the plenty of thy house: » [1170B] for drunkenness changes the state of the mind and in a manner estranges it from itself; so in that glory there will be no small change of the mind. In this life a certain affection of the flesh draws us toward parents and kinsfolk, acquaintances and well-wishers; in that life all this will be swallowed up; the whole of love will be stretched out toward God, the whole mind will gather itself into him; she will not know a father, she will be ignorant of a mother also; she will be touched with affection toward no one save him whom she has known to be conformed to Christ, whom she has perceived to be joined and united to his body: « for the Lord Jesus shall deliver up the kingdom to God and the Father, » and he himself shall be « all things in all [n: (I Cor. XV, 24, 28)]. » In this life indeed we are carried down by a certain propagation of the flesh through father and mother; and thus many of us are joined to one another by a certain kinship of blood and affection among ourselves: [1170C] but in the regeneration nothing is owed to flesh or to blood. For what father shall raise thee up out of the dust? What mother shall lead thee through from death to life? The order of things shall be led back to its own beginning. The first man was created out of the earth, and the woman formed out of his bone; and by a certain flowing forth of blood all men were propagated from them.

The first creation, therefore, of men was out of the earth; the second, out of a man; the third, out of a man and a woman; the fourth, in the Lord Jesus, not out of a man, but out of a woman: but in the regeneration that alone will be which went before in Adam. There will now be nothing in it which we shall owe either to a woman or to a man. He who formed Adam out of the earth will on that day raise up all men out of the dust; so that [1170D] we may now owe the whole to our creation, nothing to the flesh. And as Adam was debtor and bound to the Lord alone for the whole of his affection and love, and in every good, bodily as well as spiritual, so altogether on that day let us owe anything to none but the Lord. He will be to us father and mother and brother and sister, and in the truth of the flesh raised again the vigour of the spirit shall possess all things; there will now be nowhere a father according to the flesh, or a mother, nor will there be able to be any other degrees whatever of kinship according to the flesh. All these things having now been exchanged in a happy commerce, so that in place of those which we had in the flesh we may now have God as father even according to the flesh; that he himself may be for us [1171A] a sufficient pleasure, and « all things in all. » For if thou turn the mind's eye toward spiritual things, thou wilt find the same more abundantly. For here man is delighted with gold, he is delighted with silver and with precious stone, with the affection of his children, with the abundance of crops and fruits, with the subjection of birds and beasts and fishes, and with this, that the round world lies open and subject to him far and wide: this is a great pleasure, but to senses imperfect and as yet little enlightened. The glitter of gold dazzles the eye, but the eye not yet enlightened. If thou hold out gold to a glorified man, thou dost not move or delight him. The glorified eye gazes inwardly: in gazing upon the creature it beholds and contemplates him who presides over the creature. To me as I gaze, the body which is without is set in my way, and what is within is hidden: [1171B] such is not the vision of a glorified man: for him the creature does not hide the Creator, but opens the soul through the body; in the soul it opens the mind, in the mind the memory, the understanding, and the reason, and all the virtues also which grace the mother forms within: nor does he behold only the effect of the work, but he also gazes upon that hand which wondrously forms all things. He beholds that supernal wisdom working wondrously in bodies, and far more wondrously in spirits; and while he wonders at the work, he wonders far more at the workman, who mightily holds all things together. He wonders at that highest and superessential Spirit poured into every creature, immeasurable and simple; granting to some to be, not to live; to others life, but without sense; to others sense, but devoid of reason; to others [1171C] reason, but hindered by the admixture of the body; to others a substance wrapped in no commingling of body. And considering in all these a venerable loveliness and an unspeakable comeliness, he wonders nevertheless at the art itself more in the artificer than at the whole of this which he observes in the order of the creature: fixing the mind's eye upon whom, he flies away as into the deep of the sea, contemplating in the Godhead itself such things as neither the tongue can tell nor even a holy mind can conceive in the present life. The heaven is moved, the stars are borne in their circuit, the planets are moved in their circles, the air is moved by the blowing of the spirits appointed to it, the sea, binding the earth about, renders it unmoved: in these the living creatures walk about in varied forms and figures, the kinds of metals [1171D] gleam, the pleasing sweetness of fruits smiles upon us, and the colour, smell, and taste of things beyond number strike the senses of mortals with their great variety, and in all these there is a most beautiful order, praiseworthy by the merit of that wisdom which so disposes all things. But the glorified spirit, running through in mind all the forms in matter and the whole comeliness of the creature, enters that holy secret place of the highest Godhead; where it looks upon the causes of all things, and at last upon the whole creation, as it is in the mind and ever lives, far more fully than it would look upon it while it slipped away under time. And so, entering into the treasures of wisdom and knowledge, it finds in overflowing measure all that can adorn and beatify the mind; so that nothing is now lacking to it in any grace, but it says confidently [1172A] now to the Lord Jesus, « Thy grace is sufficient for me [var: Vulg. *sufficit tibi gratia mea*, "my grace is sufficient for thee"] [n: (II Cor. XII, 9)]. » For man, gathered within the wave of the Godhead, his mortal part having been swallowed up by that magnificent glory, overflows with peace and tranquillity and with an abundance of all things desirable. So, that superessential and uncreated Spirit pouring himself into each of the elect, that in all he himself may be all things, in that he is in himself and from himself the unfailing sufficiency of all things that can be desired, both for each and for all. This is the hope of the saints, this their expectation, this that end at which, when any soul has arrived, it will rejoice that it has now most blessedly completed its course. This is the kiss of pleasure which, out of the place of pleasure which the Lord had planted from the beginning, the holy Church desires to be given her, which every faithful soul desires to be given her, [1172B] saying, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » And so in a wondrous manner, at the very beginning of the work she makes mention at once of the end, saying, « Let him kiss me: » so that she may bear witness that he toward whom she burns with desire, who is the beginning and the end of all things, is beginning and end for her also; to whom may the Lord Jesus lead us, to whom with the Father and the Holy Spirit be glory, honour, and dominion unto the ages of ages. Amen.

*Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.*

These are the words of the Bride, with which she addressed the Bridegroom above: the words which follow are the Bridegroom's, which he joins on to those at once, saying:

*For thy breasts are better than wine. 2. Fragrant with the best ointments.*
