## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

[1178B]The Bride therefore, marking the glory of the young maidens, in which they were received through love; according to which they who had been young maidens are now accounted queens; and considering that these could not have been raised into this glory unless they were drawn by the Father, opens her bosom, enlarges her prayer, praying for herself, that she in her least members may be drawn to the Father; wishing that we may all together meet with her unto a perfect man, and the measure of the age of the fulness of Christ [n: (Ephes. IV, 13)]; whence also she subjoins that which follows, « Draw me after thee. » For it must be known that all of us, as many as run, are drawn in two directions: the one which is called the right, the other which is called the left. He is drawn to the left who, always entangled in evil works, [1178C] consumes his life in them. But he is drawn to the right over whom a good angel presides, showing him the good things that he should do, and to the finishing of these let him always be intent: whence, speaking to God, she says, « After thee, » that is, in those things which are pleasing to thee, in which I may have thee as my leader before me, in those things keep me, and suffer me neither to stray nor to be turned aside elsewhere: for so it is written, « No man comes to me, unless the Father, who sent me, draw him [n: (Joan. VI, 44)]. » [var: Vulg. *Nemo potest venire ad me*, "No man can come to me"] That we may therefore approach to God, there is need of the Father drawing, that he himself may go before us, may so present himself as leader, and, when he has led, may himself receive us. Likewise also we read it written, « And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all things to myself [n: (Joan. XII, 32)]. » Christ indeed was lifted up from the earth when he was raised from the earth upon the cross: [1178D] likewise he was lifted up when, forsaking the earth, he was set in heaven at the right hand of the Father: and by the consideration of himself thus, whether set upon the cross or raised to the right hand of the Father, he draws every chosen soul to himself, while they both crucify themselves to their vices and, caring not for earthly things, hang themselves upon him who sits above on high. For he who is drawn seeks the heights, and wholly intends to lift himself above himself; he beholds before him the mountain of the virtues, and while he strives to lay hold of its steep places, he beholds himself at times in the depth, and wonders. And so he cries out, saying, « Draw me after thee, » because out of this valley I do not ascend save by thee. But if thou shalt have felt the hand of God stretched out to thee, and shalt have recognized thyself drawn out of the valley of miseries and out of [1179A] the mire of dregs, lay hold of the hand, hold it, kiss it, and say, « I will not let thee go, unless thou bless me [n: (Gen. XXXII, 26)]: » have thanks to him who will truly lead thee forth, that thou mayest run in the odor of his ointments: which thou wilt recognize by a like saying. For dogs run by the keenness of their nostrils, that they may take wild beasts, which, while they flee, infect the thickness of the air with their scent, and so show to those that follow the way by which they may be taken.

That comparison indeed is a figure of the demons: for they who are tempted flee, and there are malignant spirits, or evil men, that follow, in order to drag the fleeing into evils, and to slay them, and to glory in their deaths, and to sate themselves. So is that hateful hunt accomplished in the world, in which the spirit that is the author of malice [1179B] sounds the horn, admonishes with his voice, and, in order to suggest what he wills, admits evil men and perverse spirits, and slays innumerable men who do not resist temptation. But the Spirit of God works far otherwise: he gives the good will, gives to carry through in effect that which is good; gives to take delight in the things which are above, and to be sprinkled unceasingly with the odor of the things which are above. Wonderful indeed is the anointing of the divine Spirit, who renders the skin which he anoints supple and fit for the stitching, and makes it apt for the adornment of the supreme King. With the odor of this ointment is he drenched who assiduously meditates in the law of the Lord, fitting himself so that he may resist whatever things are hurtful, may give himself over to no sins, may apply himself to the manners of the saints, may sanctify himself by imitating them, and, drenched with the odor of spiritual [1179C] ointments, may always rejoice in them, may securely take his delight, and rest. He entreats therefore in this manner, « Draw me after thee, » that is, Thou, to whom none comes save by thee, knit for me the rope of thy love, whereby I may be drawn to thee, whereby it may not suffer me to turn aside elsewhere. For Solomon says that « a threefold cord is not easily broken [n: (Eccle. IV, 12)]; » and if thou shalt have suffered this to be knit to my members by the hand of the malignant one, so that by thinking evil things it drag my heart to itself, by speaking these things inflame my whole tongue out of itself, and by doing these things bend my whole body and turn it aside to itself; what is there for me, save that I say that word of the apostle? « Unhappy man that I am: who shall deliver me from the body of this death? » [n: (Rom. VII, 24.)] Do thou, O Lord, [1179D] answer me, « The grace of God by Jesus Christ our Lord [n: (ibid., 25)]. » Let this thy grace, O Lord, deliver me, because thou art strong and mighty: in this battle do thou, being the stronger, snatch his rope out of his mighty hand; do thou break it asunder; do thou knit thy whole threefold cord to my members; that I may inwardly savor [sic: *saptiam*] thee in my heart, may proclaim thee and thy things with my tongue, and may devoutly fulfil in work those things only which are pleasing to thee. Do thou mightily snatch me, willing to follow thee, from the malignant one; make me to walk after thee, that when I have walked after thee I may not begin to run at uncertainty, but may lay hold of the crown which thou hast promised to them that love thee, being drawn hither [1180A] by thee. This indeed is that which follows:

*We will run in the odor of thy ointments.* [var: Vulg. *in odorem*, "to the odor"]

That is, being snatched from the malignant one by thee, stirred to the good by thee, brought to the highest by thy drawing, « we will run, » that is, we shall go forward our whole life long; « In the odor of thy ointments, » that is, in the fragrance of the spiritual virtues, wherewith thou touchest and anointest thine own. And so, wishing gifts to the good, and desiring that they be drawn to better things, she says, « Draw me after thee; » that thou mayest help me, who am willing, and running, and desiring thee with my whole heart, in this, that at length by a blessed end I may lay hold of thee, and, the good course being finished, may now rest secure and be delighted in thee. But because certain men neither run nor will to take up the course after thee, [1180B] let thy visitation deign to visit these, that thou mayest open to them the light of truth, and draw them, being enlightened, to thee, and, a rope of charity being given them, bring them through to thee. These that great Paul signifies, who before his calling ran wholly after Satan; but afterwards, being struck by the Lord, he was laid low and enlightened, and made a wonderful preacher, and, bound by this threefold cord, running after God, was consummated by the sword. Whether therefore thou drawest after thee one willing and able to run, that he may run the more, and may arrive there whither he could not arrive save by thee; or convertest one unwilling and unable to run, and dost so enlighten him by the grace given, that he too may lay hold of thee. In the good odor « of thy ointments, » that is, of the virtues breathing forth from thee, we will run, rendering thanks to thee who [1180C] hast laid hold of us, and rendering praises in the glory to which thou hast brought us by thy gift. There follows;

*The King brought me into his cellars: we will exult, and be glad in thee.*

A prayer has been set forth above, in which it was said, « Draw me after thee; we will run in the odor of thy ointments. Lest it should seem useless and uttered without fruit, there is consequently added, « The King brought me into his cellars. » For behold, she is now drawn, drawn indeed, and brought into the very cellars of the King. But who is more worthily called King than he who, having the rights of all kingdoms, distributes them to whom he wills, and when he wills, and as long as he wills; and, considering in these the manners of men, either crowns them according to their merits, [1180D] or, marking their insolence, afflicts them worthily for the things they have committed. This truly is God, who rules all things by one command, who contains the things which are above and the things which are below, giving to each of his creatures its own being, that they may be, and be well, and himself God ruling over all things. This is he whom this Bride desires, that is, the soul, or the Church, wholly purged of sins, whose kiss she has this long while demanded, seeking to lay hold of him whose desire she already feels within her. Which indeed, because it is near to the highest blessedness, is still deferred, nor is the kiss given, until the soul, or the Church, may understand from the creatures whether she has chosen one who is worthy. Hence it is that [1181A] either by an angel or by some virtue this Bride is brought into the cellars of the King, that she may either know his riches, and from his riches recognize who he is, and of what sort and how great is he whom she desires. Which this Bride observing, and giving thanks, says, « The King brought me into his cellars: we will exult, and be glad in thee. » Behold, she who is in the cellars exults in the cellars: and the cellars indeed are some part of the divine palace, containing within themselves the riches of the king, and his secret things. But the palace of the King is that of which the prophet makes mention, saying, « O Israel, how great is the house of God, and vast! great is the place of his possession, and it has no consummation, high and immeasurable [n: (Bar. III, 24, 25)]. » [var: Vulg. *finem*, "end"] Hence let us consider his palace, which is assuredly as great as [1181B] the creation reaches. Its length, its breadth and its height measure out the whole dimension of the creature. But if thou shouldst go further in mind, hear the Scripture; for it says, « It has no consummation, high and immeasurable: » for God is indeed in every creature. But is God consummated together with the creature, so that where the creature receives its end, there God also should obtain his end? Far be this, and let it be driven far from human understanding. For God, as he is immeasurable, so is he infinite and eternal: and if he is infinite, there is assuredly nothing whereby he should have an end; but where he appoints the boundary of the creature, there he receives himself in his own simplicity and purity: according to which the Truth testifies in the Gospel, saying, « Glorify me, Father, with thyself, with the glory which I had before the world [1181C] was made [n: (Joan. XVII, 5)]. » For then was the Father in the Son and the Holy Spirit, the Son was in the Father and the Holy Spirit, the Holy Spirit also was in the Father and the Son, filling up the blessed Trinity, and sanctifying one unity in substance.
