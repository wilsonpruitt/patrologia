## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

This therefore is the palace of the king; and its cellars are certain heavenly mansions, of which the Lord makes mention, saying, « In my Father's house there are many mansions [n: (Joan. XIV, 2)]. » Which mansions indeed are the diverse habitations of the heavenly virtues, and of diverse glory, which he disposes and orders in that secret manner of his own which the Lord knows, so that in them he may establish the place of his glory and of his holy habitation. Who discerns and separates these? [1181D] who divides and gives judgment upon their fellowships? High indeed, and unknown to our understanding, is that incomprehensible indwelling of all the virtues and angels, who, invisible to us, were found standing and stable in temptation; and, when those fell headlong who, lifted up in mind against the Lord, fell away, they were made stable by the merit of their own constancy, and were joined to God by perpetual charity. Whose names, as they are known to us, so are their powers unknown, holding together heaven and earth, disposing all the things that are done in them, moderating the life of the just, and not ignorant of the punishments which are due to the wicked: whose number God the Creator alone knows, who created them from the beginning, and enjoys and takes delight in their unceasing obedience: their nature the holy Trinity knows, their number the second person [1182A] in the Trinity knows, our Lord Jesus Christ, who, when he was being led to his passion, said, « Turn thy sword into its place. Dost thou think that I cannot ask my Father, and he will present me more than twelve legions of angels [n: (Matth. XXVI, 52, 53)]. » Which twelve legions are found to be seventy-nine thousand, and nine hundred, and ninety-two, that is, eighty thousand less eight. But the number of the others is unknown to us, who sound forth praises beyond estimation to God, and, being occupied in those praises, are not wearied, nor do they fail, but rather it is the more a delight to be delighted by the frequenting of these praises; so that either they may always go forward in the love and the knowledge of that majesty which they behold, or, being brought to perfection, may [1182B] persevere without end, stable in that which they have long since attained.

These mansions therefore are the cellars, into which this Bride says that she has been brought, showing that by an angel, or by some virtue, she is formed by their examples, so that, contemplating God, she may love him with all her strength, and may cherish his praise without intermission, and may show that she takes her delight not in the affluence of temporal and passing things, but rather in the continual frequenting of these; saying, « The king brought me into his cellars: we will exult, and be glad in thee. » For to exult is to rejoice in mind, and to be glad is to show by an outward sign the joy which is within. Therefore, that she may attribute wholly to God the joy which she announces that she has conceived, she adds, [1182C] « In thee: » for vain alike is exultation, and vain is gladness, save that which is founded in the Lord: whence the Psalmist says, « Be glad, ye just, in the Lord, and glory, all ye upright of heart [n: (Psal. XXXI, 11)]. » And likewise he begins a Psalm, saying: « Exult, ye just, in the Lord; praise becomes the upright [n: (Psal. XXXII, 1)]. » Behold what gladness and exultation, which praise assuredly and glorying follow after, is wholly from God, when God is gathered up with the whole heart, the whole soul, the whole mind, when thy heart is opened to loving thy neighbor, even as thyself: and the recompense of all these things is not sought in this life, but is most patiently awaited in the life to come: whence also there is subjoined;

*Mindful of thy breasts above wine.*

Here truly it must be attended to, that this exultation and [1182D] gladness proceeds wholly from the memory of the breasts, which the Bride confesses to be the breasts of the Bridegroom himself: and by what reason this is true must be considered. For the Bridegroom, in the first answer of his, answered the Bride above, Thy prayer is not to be repelled, because « thy breasts are better than wine: » whence, since here the Bridegroom says that the breasts are the Bride's, and in what follows the Bride professes that the breasts are the Bridegroom's, either these must of necessity be the same breasts, or diverse ones must indeed be assigned: but lest hindrances make delay, we testify that the breasts will be the same, both the Bridegroom's and the Bride's. For these breasts we testify to be the divine oracles; which indeed are God's, divinely inspired by him: and they are the Church's, granted to her out of the grace of God for the nourishing and instructing of the peoples. This [1183A] Bride therefore says: « We will exult, and be glad in thee; mindful of thy breasts, » that is, of the divine oracles, which have been inspired by thee, and granted to me by thy giving. « Above wine, » that is, above the understanding of all those oracles which, as wine turns men's minds away, so turn the soul away from thee, and detain it occupied in their hands. There follows:

*The upright love thee.*

Which addition indeed is the commendation of the things said above. For no one loves save he who is upright, nor can any man be upright and not love. For he is upright who raises his head to that for which he was created, who is stretched out with his whole body thither whence it has been granted him to be, to live, to be wise, and to understand. Whence is this to any man, save from God? For the Psalm says, [1183B] « Know ye that the Lord himself is God; he made us, and not we ourselves [n: (Psal. XCIX, 3)]. » He therefore who raises himself to God is upright, because without doubt he tends thither whence he acknowledges these things to be present to him: but he who is bent downward, who is inclined to the world, fixing his head downward and raising his feet upward, is likened to a monster, and is crooked; of whom the prophet says, « I am made wretched, and I am bowed down even to the end [n: (Psal. XXXVII, 7)], » bewailing without doubt the wretchedness of him who, thus departing from the good, ends his life in death. But the Lord, not willing that we should be bent down in this manner, healed and raised up the woman in the Gospel who for eighteen years was bowed down and unable to look upward, making her whole from being sick, and upright from being crooked; showing that it is he himself [1183C] whose love heals and raises up, makes upright and receives. There follows:

4. *I am black, but beautiful, O ye daughters of Jerusalem.*

Hitherto the Bride has addressed her Bridegroom, she who is either the Church of God, or any perfected soul, now loathing the world and contemplating God with a pure heart: but she, seeking the kiss, says, « Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. » Which, because it is not yet given, but is deferred to that time in which, after the resurrection of all, the souls, united together with the very bodies in which they rendered a service pleasing to God, shall be gladdened with a perpetual eternity, and shall together be endowed with immortality. But it was answered her by the Bridegroom: The kiss which thou askest I do not indeed [1183D] give now, but when God shall be all in all, and the number of your brethren shall be filled up, then I promise that I will give it: and for the filling up of that number of the brethren I judge thee indeed apt; and this for this reason, because « thy breasts are better than wine. » Cheered by which answer, she addresses him again, saying, « Thy name is oil poured out: » and likewise that other, « Draw me after thee, » that, helped by the strength of thy breasts, with such a multitude as befits thee, I may at length come through to thee. But now, being taught what is to be done, and that the peoples must be nourished by her breasts, so that, being enlarged unto salvation, she may run together with them, having before her eyes that word, « Obedience is better than victims, and to hearken, rather than to offer the fat of rams [n: (I Reg. XV, 22)]; » she obeys, and, [1184A] being humbled, turns herself to the peoples, saying, « I am black, O ye daughters of Jerusalem. » But who does not know Jerusalem to be that city which the Lord chose before all others, that in it he might teach, and in it bring forth the words of life, and in it and in its borders work the miracles at which the world should be astounded, and might wonderfully fulfil what had been begun by an angel in Nazareth, brought forth afterwards in Bethlehem, and in that city at the last by his passion, and by the glory of his resurrection, by his ascension also, and by the sending of the Holy Spirit. But addressing this city, she says, « I am black and beautiful, O ye daughters of Jerusalem. » [var: Vulg. *sed*, "but"] But she calls its daughters all the souls converted to him by the preaching of the Lord Jesus; which deserved to hear either him bodily, or his preaching, beforehand through the prophets and other just men, or afterwards through the apostles, and those [1184B] who had faith in them: these she names daughters, as being suppliant, as being obedient, doing nothing against the command of the Father, keeping the faith as it was committed to them, and, in the keeping of it even to the term of life, most constantly persevering, and despising death of whatever kind.
