## 5.

*Do not consider me, that I am dark, because the sun has discolored me.*

As if she should say, I am indeed black, discolored by innumerable sufferings, afflicted with horrible torments: but you who will to be daughters of Jerusalem, do not attend to this, do not set my sufferings before your eyes, and do not reckon them as intolerable. What I suffered at Rome in Peter by the cross, what through Paul by the sword, what through John in the cauldron, what through Andrew in Achaia, what through the several apostles in the diverse places of the world, then through Stephen, through James in Judea, what through Polycarp, what through Dionysius, what through Laurence, what through Vincent, what through the Theban Legion, what [1187C] through all the martyrs far and wide through the world, how in these things I was discolored, do not attend; but for what cause I endured these things; what fruit I may obtain through that cause; with what blessed glory I hold by most certain hope that I shall be endowed. Hither attend, hither look, that the Sun of justice [sic: *justistitiae*] himself wrought this discoloring in me. These things I suffered for him who is the Sun of justice, who shall reform our body, configured to the body of his brightness, that I may live with him in perpetual glory, and enjoy everlasting blessedness. There follows:

*The sons of my mother have fought against me, they have set me a keeper in the vineyards.*

The Church says that she was black; but whence this blackness first befell her she consequently shows, [1187D] saying, « The sons of my mother have fought against me. » These she calls the sons of her mother, begotten of the stock of Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob; who, when all the sons of men in the whole round of the earth turned aside to the left part, to adore idols and to sacrifice to them, refused to do this, but rather remained stable and immovable in the faith of the one God, who had created all things, through whom all things subsist. These she testifies to have been in this the sons of her mother, that is, of divine grace; whom, when the world was wrongly turning aside, she called back from its errors, and kept stable to herself in this faith, as sons: to whom, when the time of grace had been manifoldly revealed through the prophets, and afterwards, the Lord being born from the heavens among them, [1188A] had evidently appeared by signs and mighty works, yet they refused to receive him, to believe him, to give faith to his preaching, though it was declared by innumerable mighty works: but decreeing to be stable in the law given through Moses, they willed, after the signs, and the mighty works, and the miracles, to accuse him before the judge, betrayed by the hand of his own disciple, though he was innocent, and to receive him when he was given to them, and, though he bore all things innocently, to fasten him to the cross. Resigning this life, they were given over into a reprobate sense, and they who received it received power to be the sons of God: according to that which is said in the prophet, « I will call her that is not my people, my people, and her that is not my beloved, beloved; and her that has not obtained mercy, one that has obtained mercy. And it shall be in this place, where it was said [1188B] to them, You are not my people, there they shall be called the sons of the living God [n: (Ose. I, 10, et Rom. IX, 25, 26)]. » But this Bride says that these, long since the sons of the mother, grace, while that state still remained, while the prophecies were as yet in suspense, when the signs were not yet, the mighty works not yet declared; but now by no means sons, but most bitter enemies and unclean murderers, assailed her; that, the head being broken, the members also might be broken, and, Christ being taken away, as they hoped, out of the midst, his Church might be utterly taken away.

This is plainly shown in the Acts of the Apostles, where « the Prince of the Priests rising up, and all who were with him (which is the heresy of the Sadducees) were filled with zeal; and they laid hands upon the Apostles, [1188C] and put them in the public ward [n: (Act. V, 17, 18)]; » and, when these had been mightily delivered by an angel, they afterwards gave charge to the blind, that they should by no means speak in the name of the Lord Jesus. But these, long since the sons of divine grace, now wholly reprobate as toward the grace that offered itself, made war against me. But they of the new people, receiving the faith, and confessing the Word of God, true God and made man, subjecting themselves to him with the heart, set me, who from the first Abel have stood forth, who with Henoch deserved to have God, and who have taken up into myself all the just to whom this grace was revealed, set me a keeper for themselves in the vineyards, which they established throughout the whole world far and wide. These vineyards the Church calls holy, which they who had received the faith of Christ planted in their own blood, and consecrated unto his glory. [1188D] Set over these, this Bride says that she was set a keeper in the vineyards: over which, although she applied as much keeping as she could, yet she confesses that she did not keep them. Which we understand to be said with regard to the opinion of men: for they who were consummated by the sword, who were consumed by fire, who were destroyed by such punishments as the world could devise, who shall understand these to have been worthily kept? According to this opinion, this Bride says:

*I have not kept my vineyard.*

That is, I did not forbid them from the taking up of torments for Christ: whom nevertheless I have already sent before into eternal life, where there are no perils, but only eternal joys. To this there is consequently annexed:
