## 6.

*Show me, thou whom my soul loves, where thou feedest, where thou liest down in the midday, lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions.*

For this Bride has confessed to her Bridegroom that, although set as keeper in the vineyards, she « did not keep » them, or her, for the sake of the one love of faith and of charity; that is, she did not restrain them from the fervor of undergoing suffering; but, by exhorting and encouraging them, she sent them over with most certain hope to the joys of the heavens promised to her. Hence, that she may be made the more certain, and that no one may be held doubtful in himself concerning that hope, she sends forth a prayer, saying, « Show me where thou feedest, where thou liest down; » having nevertheless set this before, « Whom my soul loves. » For it is wont to be the custom with suppliants, that when they have besought that anything be given or done for them, they hold out [1189B] something, or offer something, whereby they may understand that the face of him to whom supplication is made will be at peace toward them: whence, because among the heavenly gifts nothing is found more eminent than charity, nothing that can be given dearer than love, she holds out that love of hers which she asks for, saying, « Show me, thou whom my soul loves; » that what she asks may not be denied to one who asks and at the same time loves. For what is dearer to God? what is found more acceptable than the love of our soul? Run through all the things which are in the world: nothing comparable to this, nothing so acceptable to God that it might be offered, will meet thee. For God created the world, and all the things which are of the world; but for what cause? that it might indeed be a habitation for man: but for what cause did he here establish man? that he might [1189C] obey him, and out of the love of his soul be obedient to him. The end therefore and the cause of the creation of things is this, that we may love God with the whole soul, may please him in all things, may cleave to him indivisibly: according to that which is said, « He who is joined to the Lord is one spirit [n: (I Cor. VI, 17)]. » [var: Vulg. *adhaeret*, "cleaves"]

This Bride therefore, joined to God, having all souls, both those which are and those which shall be, joined to God in that unity which Paul the Apostle sets forth to us, what can she ask of the Bridegroom which he should deny? concerning what can she intercede which he should not hear? She says therefore, « Thou whom my soul loves, show me where thou feedest. » Which short word, how widely it may be extended we understand, if we rightly acknowledge the Lord to be the shepherd of all the things which [1189D] are fed far and wide. For he himself says, « I am the good Shepherd; the Shepherd [n: (Joan. X, 11)], » I say, « good, » through whom not even the bird hungers, through whom not even all the things which are sustained by another's food hunger. But what is it to us to inquire concerning the feeding of those who, though they are fed by his giving, neither render thanks to the giver, nor, so as to know how to render them, have received the thanksgivings of the giver? Men therefore are they whom God feeds; to whom he subjects all the things which are in the world, sheep and oxen, all the beasts of the field also, the birds of the air, and the fishes of the sea. Of whom certain men receive these gifts of God with a worthy giving of thanks; but others, not attending to what they are, or whence they are, or for what cause they were given, grasp all these things [1190A] as by a chance occupation; nor do they by any reasoning know who is the author of them, or who the giver. Since therefore God feeds all these, it lies with him which of them he may worthily reward according to their giving of thanks, or which, being unworthy, he may cast away from his table by eternal damnation. But what is it to us to handle these matters, since these are bodily things, and this Bride of God spiritual? let her intend to inquire further, saying, « Show me, thou whom my soul loves, where thou feedest. »

A spiritual pasture therefore must be sought, wherewith it is worthy that the Bridegroom should feed, and being fed with which they may be made spiritual. For there is a certain bodily bread, wherewith the body is nourished; and there is a bread wherewith the spirit is fed. The bread wherewith the body is nourished is procured by the plow and the hoe: the bread wherewith the spirit is fed is ministered from above [1190B] out of heaven. For there is a bread of life, Holy Scripture, divinely inspired by the Father of lights; and there is a bread of life, every perfected virtue: there is a bread of life, the body of Christ, granted to us for a sacrament and a sanctification. These are the breads of which the prophet makes mention, saying to the daughters of the Bride, « Blessing I will bless her widow, I will satisfy her poor with breads [n: (Psal. CXXXI, 15)]. » For Sion, « a watchtower, » is holy Church, from the lofty watchtower of the virtues assiduously contemplating the Lord Jesus, and fearfully and anxiously awaiting his coming. A certain part of her, already glorified with the Lord, rejoices in the heavens; a certain part, still sustaining exile, is a pilgrim from the Lord upon the earth: according to [1190C] this part she is a widow, as though separated from her husband, the Lord, namely, Jesus, who went away into a far country when he pierced the heavens with the truth of the body he had assumed, and is to return in the day of the full moon, that is, of the glorification to come. This widow the Lord blessing blesses, namely, with a twofold blessing; giving her grace in the way, that she may not fail; giving her at length glory in the fatherland, that she may rest from her labors. But he refreshes with breads those poor of his who are poor in spirit; who, although for the solace of their pilgrimage they possess certain temporal things, yet do not incline their mind to these, but raise up their love and their affection to the giver of all things. These he refreshes with the breads already named, namely, with the bread of the scriptures, [1190D] the bread of the virtues, the bread of the sacraments, the bread, namely, most white, which is the bread of angels; of which it is said: « Man ate the bread of angels [n: (Psal. LXXVII, 25)]. » These are the breads of which the Gospel discourse makes mention, saying, « Which of you shall have a friend, and shall go to him at midnight, saying, Friend, lend me three loaves; because a friend of mine has come to me, and I have not what to set before him [n: (Luc. XI, 4, 5)]. »

Well does he receive a friend, who intends to refresh and to honor him with this threefold bread. The bread of the scriptures therefore is the first among these breads, for this reason, that in spiritual advancement the first thing is to know in whom thou believest and whom thou lovest; the second, to be conformed [1191A] to him whom thou lovest; the third, to be confirmed in this very thing, that thou look not back, that thou go not back. Through the scriptures therefore we come to know, through virtue we are conformed, but through the sacrament we are confirmed. By the bread of the scriptures we are enlightened unto faith, that afterwards we may be confirmed unto virtue; whence it is written, « The declaration of thy words gives light, and gives understanding to little ones [n: (Psal. CXVIII, 130)]. » For there is a word which is heard when uttered, and there is a word which abides as born: but from the declaration of the word which is heard uttered, there is conceived the understanding and knowledge of the Word which always abides born; this is Christ Jesus our Lord; whose faith is surely conceived when the word of God, Holy Scripture, is soberly and faithfully opened and expounded. For it is written, that [1191B] « they knew him in the breaking of bread [n: (Luc. XXIV, 35)]. » The Gospel history lies open: the Lord was walking with the disciples, and was not indeed known, who was received by them into their lodging, and at length was known in the breaking of bread. O blessed breaking, whereby Christ is known! whereby Christ is conceived in the heart of his own! The bread of the scriptures therefore is the first; which the Lord also reveals, when, working miracles, he deigned to refresh five thousand men with five loaves; but four thousand with seven loaves. For it is written, « When, having taken five loaves, looking up to heaven, he blessed, and broke, and gave the loaves to his disciples, but the disciples to the multitudes [n: (Matth. XIV, 19)]. » O wonderful blessing! which, while it breaks the loaves, does not diminish them, and increases them when it distributes them; [1191C] multiplies them when it consumes them in the refreshing of many; and what is against the nature of bodily bread, this it brings back into nature. He handed over the Pentateuch to the apostles, commanding that they break it, and distribute it to all, that all may be satisfied from it, and that many fragments of the expositors may remain: so also, blessing the Heptateuch, he commanded it to be broken for the peoples, and from this that very many baskets of the expositors be filled. For this bread has such a nature, that when it is broken, and distributed, and communicated in the correction of many, it is always increased; and whence it seems to be diminished, thence rather it receives enlargement and increase. But the Pentateuch, that is, the five books of Moses, and the Heptateuch, which are the four Gospels, the epistles of the apostles, and their Acts, [1191D] which Luke writes, « Paul also the apostle. » These things the Lord sets forth to his own, that they may be as seeds, and that many fruits may arise from them, whereby the whole multitude cleaving to God may be fed, and fully refreshed. But it is the bread of the virtues, when from the word we have heard we conceive virtue in the mind, and remain firm and stable in it; when, driving off the disgrace of the vices, we mortify them in work, turn aside from them in speech, and dread to touch them in thought. Thus is place made for virtue in the mind; so that what ambition widely embraces, that is despised for Christ; what incontinence suggests, « let it slay in chastity; » what is exalted through pride, that is broken by humility; [1192A] and every vice that is spread abroad is driven far off by the mighty command of charity. Of this the Psalmist makes mention, saying, « My tears have been bread to me day and night [n: (Psal. XLI, 4)]. » For when he said that the love of God was to be preferred to the world, it was answered him that this should be laid open by a manifest miracle; for they said, « Where is thy God? » [n: (Ibid.)] Which hardness of heart being heard, the prophet turns to tears, praying for them that they may be softened, that at length they may be converted; having these tears indeed for breads, and reckoning them his refreshment. He makes mention of the same, saying, « I am smitten [sic: *suum*] as hay, and my heart is withered; because I forgot to eat my bread [n: (Psal. CI, 5)]. » For unless this bread of the virtues be assiduously eaten, so that a man be strengthened by it, he is smitten by the sickle of sin, and his [1192B] greenness falls away, and straightway his flower withers. For the same prophet repeats, saying, « All the day my enemies reproached me [n: (ibid. 9-10)]; » and why? « Because I ate ashes as bread, » that is, I was delighted with sin as though with virtue. These are the breads which Habacuc had crumbled in the little trough [n: (Dan. XIV, 32)], that he might carry them to the reapers, whom the angel of the Lord carried by his hair, and in the vehemence of his spirit set him in Babylon, that he might carry them to Daniel. For that Daniel had crushed these breads of the virtues in the trough of his heart; and there he made trial what things, and of what sort, and how great, virtue could do. This bread is that which was set at the head of Elias fleeing from the face of Jezabel, in which it was said to him, « Arise, eat; for a great [1192C] way remains. He arose therefore, and ate, and walked in the strength of that food forty days, and forty nights, unto the mount of God, Horeb [n: (III Reg. XIX, 7, 8)]. » This therefore is that bread which we demand daily, saying, « Give us this day our daily bread [n: (Luc. XI, 3)]; » because without this bread of virtue and of grace it is unjust for any man to approach to, or to attain to, any bread of the sacrament. But this third is that bread of the sacrament, which the Lord makes of himself in a wonderful manner, which he grants to his Church to be communicated unto life, and with which if any man shall not have communicated by believing and equally by eating, there is no doubt that he will by no means have eternal life.
