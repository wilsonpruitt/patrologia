## 6. (cont.)

Let these things, then, that have been said concerning the pasturing of the Bridegroom suffice; and if we so understand him [1192D] that he either furnishes pasture or himself receives pasture, it makes no difference; when he distributes these loaves to them by preaching, or they receive the same loaves by believing and loving; let him be one and the same who is pastured and pastures, and in them, as in his faithful members, is delighted and takes his rest. For hence it is added, « Where thou pasturest, and where thou liest down, » in that he has the couch of his own rest in those whom he either feeds with these loaves, or who are refreshed by the same loaves, a faithful teacher ministering the word. For this too is joined on, « At midday. » For midday is called the middle of the day, in which the sun shines more fervently, and the day dawns brighter than is its wont. Each one, therefore, has his own midday, when, [1193A] forgetful of temporal things, he now runs over spiritual things in his mind; and unmindful of bodily things, sets the things that are of the spirit before his eyes in his mind. Hence came forth the dogmas of the Stoics, of the Epicureans, of the Peripatetics, hence the many fictions of studious men, who, the way not yet laid open to them, laying down the highest good one in this fashion and another in that, and not having Christ for their leader, each erred in his own midday. But to Abraham, sitting in the valley of Mambre [n: (Gen. XVIII, 1)], in the door of his tabernacle, in the heat of the day, as it is written, the Lord appeared; and, plainly showing him the unity of substance, in which he truly is, and the trinity in persons, he made him a professor of the true faith. Of whom the Lord in the Gospel: « Abraham your father rejoiced that he might see my day: he saw it and [1193B] was glad [n: (Joan. VIII, 56)]. » For then he manifestly beheld the day of his trinity, when he understood him in the trinity to be the third person, and one God in that same trinity. For thus it is written, that « the Lord appeared to one sitting in the tabernacle [n: (Gen. XVIII, 1)], » in which the unity of substance is shown: and when he had opened his eyes, « three men appeared » [n: (ibid., 2)]; behold the mystery of the Trinity. And since it was afterward said to them, « Lord, if I have found grace in thy eyes, pass not by thy servant [n: (ibid., 3, etc.)]; » behold the mystery of Unity. « But I will fetch a little water, and wash ye your feet; and I will set a morsel of bread, and strengthen ye your heart: afterward ye shall pass on: for therefore have ye turned aside to your servant. Who said, Do as thou hast spoken; » behold the mystery [1193C] of the Trinity. And after these things, all things are comprehended under the mystery of the Trinity, as far as that word, « Returning I will come to thee at this time, life accompanying [n: (ibid., 10)]. » This is that noble midday in which the Lord lies down, in which he rests: as also that of Moses [n: (Exod., 12)], in which he understands that the people of God is to be led out of Egypt in the blood of the lamb: as also this most recent one, in which the Lord, prefiguring himself to the apostles, showed his face after the manner of the sun, and his garments in the whiteness of snow; and a voice also sounded from heaven, « This is my beloved son, in whom I am well pleased [n: (Matth. XVII, 5)]. » Of this the Bride says, « Show me, thou whom my soul loves, where thou liest down in the midday, » that is, what that midday may be, « Where thou liest down, » that is, where thou restest, and preparest for thyself a place of rest and of inmost repose; [1193D] « Lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions. » He is said to wander who is not held by one judgment of his own mind, now approving one thing, now thinking another about the same matter, and settling nothing certain for himself in which he may abide and establish for himself the foundations of his belief. That this may not befall her, she requires to be made certain by the Bridegroom what meditation may please him, what things she ought to think concerning him, or to preach to others, so that persevering in these she may not run about after the flocks of his companions, nor increase the number of those who set themselves up as the Lord's companions by thinking certain good things of him, while in other things they contradict him by lying most wickedly.
