## 7.

*If thou know not thyself, O most beautiful among women, go forth.*

Taught by the Spirit of wisdom, this Bride prayed to the Lord, that he would teach her by his Spirit where he pastured, where he chose himself a couch at midday. But afterward, being for a little while lifted up in spirit, she subjoins what has been said, « Lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions. » Whence she seems to hold out threats, that unless what she asks be done, she will mingle herself with the flock of strangers. Marking this, the Lord is carried a little way, and for a little while, into anger against her who was lifted up, according to what is said, « With the holy thou wilt be holy, and with the innocent man thou wilt be innocent; and with the elect thou wilt be elect, and with the perverse thou wilt be perverted [n: (Psal. XVII, 26, 27)]. » For the Lord, as he is found holy to the holy, innocent [1194B] to the innocent, elect to the elect, so most justly is he found perverse to the perverse. Whence to her saying, « Lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions. » The Lord answers, « If thou know not thyself, O beautiful one among women [var: Vulg. *pulcherrima*, "most beautiful"], go forth. » But it is to be marked what is said, « O beautiful one among women! » For the Lord chose her for himself as Bride, that she might be with him, might abide with him, might bear herself in his company in good faith, and might never depart from him through any man's perversion: beautiful therefore is she who has been so lifted up, so beloved, that while the rest remain in the estate of women, she herself has been chosen unto the throne of a Queen, and appointed to hold princedom over them all. And these women, among whom she has been chosen (in comparison with whom she seems comely, nay to be wondered at [1194C]), are all the souls of the Heathen, of Publicans, of Jews not following the law and not confessing Christ, and of Christians also, who confess in words that they know God, but deny it by their deeds. That this therefore may be brought back to her memory, it is said, « O beautiful one among women, if thou know not thyself. »

It is indeed a great peril for a perfect soul, or for a chosen bride, to be ignorant of herself: to be ignorant, namely, of this, that she was created out of nothing by the highest Father, the God of all, and yet formed to his image and likeness. She is indeed « Image » (as we said above) by reason, knowing what is just, what is unjust, what is to be done, what to be shunned, and that what she does not wish to be done to herself is to be denied to her neighbor, [1194D] and many things of this kind: all which things the soul observing, fully expresses and keeps the image of him by whom it was created. But she is « Likeness » when, faith having been received, she proceeds further, choosing for herself virtues which she may love, which she may cherish, by which she may rightly please him by whom she was created, and, all these being gathered in mind and in deed and perfected by charity, may give and offer them. There is moreover this, that she is said to be created to the image and likeness of God, because she was so created that she never ceases to be, but abides and stands fast unto perpetual eternity, forever retaining the good which by virtue she has merited from the Lord's hand, or else the evil, if her deserts have required it. There is another thing also in which it does not become the Bride to be ignorant of herself, but it is just to keep perpetual [1195A] knowledge of it: that he who before the ages was ever God and not man, for the sake of redeeming her and leading her back and fitting her to himself in the dignity of a Bride, was for her sake made man, mortal from immortal, passible from impassible, corruptible from incorruptible, and, having suffered those things which the long series of the Gospels unfolds, bore all, that he might lead her back to the Father, and glorify her with himself in glory. Who shall tell out this glory? which neither eye hath seen, nor ear heard, nor hath it ascended into the heart of man, which God hath prepared for them that love him. If the soul or the beloved Bride is ignorant of these things, what remains but that it be said to her, « Go forth, » as if to say, withdraw from my company, « And go after the steps of the flocks, » the steps of those whom thou callest thy flocks, that is, [1195B] preach their sayings and their opinions, and proclaim them on every side: and feed thy kids? Behold what fruit will come to her, if she shall have gone out from the company of the good, if she shall have listened to those who walk ill, if she shall have proclaimed what they themselves preach and teach. What fruit is this? that she will feed « her kids, » that is, carnal motions, wanton and fetid; these she will feed by loosening the reins to luxury and to all the vices [n: (Matth. XXV, 41)], so that they may grow into those kids whom, set on the left hand, the Lord on the last day will send into eternal punishment. There follows:

*Beside the tabernacles of the shepherds.*

Some shepherds are good, who train their sheep unto life, and, if necessity require it, do not cease to die for them: the best exemplar of these is Jesus [1195C] Christ our Lord. There are others also, who by teaching perversely drag the flocks committed to them into death: and for this they fix tabernacles for themselves, as it were certain lecture-halls in which they may solemnly be heard. Being moved, therefore, the Bridegroom gives counsel [sic: *eonsilium*] to the Bride who was lifted up; that, if wearied of good counsel she tries to flee, she may try to pitch her tent for herself beside the tabernacles of these men, and to teach, so that those whom an equal guilt binds in fault an equal torment may also rack in punishment. There follows:

8. *To my company of horse in the chariots of Pharaoh have I likened thee, my friend.*

The Bridegroom does here what belongs to a lover. Her whom he had chosen for himself as Bride he commended with words, soothed with attentions, enlarged with promises. And she, receiving these promises, [1195D] while she is borne along by headlong desire, went a little too far in a word, saying, « Lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions. » A word indeed which she ought not even to have thought, and which, even if it had been presumed in thought, it was yet not lawful to open by speaking. The Bridegroom, moved for a little while at this word, marking that whence thanks ought to have been returned, thence threats were held out against him, answered with moderation, speaking a word of anger, « If thou know not thyself, O beautiful one, go forth, » and go, following the steps of those whom thou callest to thyself shepherds; and since I have no communion with them, thou wilt have to withdraw from my company. But the Bride, [1196A] understanding that her word was heavily taken by the Bridegroom, groans and grieves; and because she had offended by a word, she trembles to utter anything at all by a word. And when this became known to the Bridegroom, whether by an Angel intervening, or in that way by which he knows all things, forgetting the husband he put on the Bridegroom, he grieved with her that grieved, and to her that repented he at once forgave what she had committed, mindful of that which is said, « I will confess against myself my injustice to the Lord, and thou hast forgiven the impiety of my sin [n: (Psal. XXXI, 5)]. » Therefore, hatreds being wiped away and anger removed, that the things now said might be taken away from memory and this Bride recalled to her former state, he accosts her with a word of friendship, a word which she had not yet deserved to hear, saying, « My friend; » thus signifying that all that had been diminished from grace is now pleasing to him in thee. [1196B] He says therefore, « My friend, I have likened thee to my company of horse. » But here what company of horse of the Bridegroom is understood, save those who fight bravely everywhere for the Bridegroom, and spread his victories abroad in heaven and in air, by land and by sea? This is that multitude of Angels which, when battles were first waged, when the proud cohort said, « I will set my seat toward the North, and I will be like the Most High [var: Vulg. *exaltabo solium meum … sedebo in monte testamenti, in lateribus aquilonis*] [n: (Isa. XIV, 14)], » clung with one accord to its own prince, giving honor and glory to him who had created it, and resisting the disturber, unto all his confusion and ignominy. Him therefore, cast down from heaven, irrevocably estranged from that glory which he was lately able to hope for, he casts down into hell, and commits to him places of horror and of vast solitude to be inhabited, so that no going forth lies open to them save such as the divine [1196C] dispensation grants; so that hence a going forth, they may either prove the elect, or hurl headlong those foreknown unto damnation by a temptation fitted to them. Hence the Lord Jesus, and his chief members, Peter and Paul and all the apostles, and all the thousands throughout the world, at his instigation and by his sending it into the hearts of the wicked, were given over to the cross and to the sword and to other passions innumerable, that they might be proved by the temptations of these, and, not overcome even by death itself, might be received into eternal glory. For John says, « When the devil had now put it into the heart, that Judas the son of Simon Iscariot should betray him [n: (Joan. XIII, 2)]: » and the Lord in Job, « Behold he is in thy hand, but yet save his life [n: (Job II, 6)]. » Behold, that power was given him in the head, and in his best members [1196D]. For the power which was given him in Job, that same power was granted him in Peter, and in Paul, and in all the other elect throughout the world; that he might either tempt them with death, or press them down in the flesh, yet himself keep their souls unharmed. So the company of horse of God, obtaining victory in these, exercised the same also upon men, first drowning in the waters of the flood those defiled by their deceit, afterward destroying with brimstone and with fire the cities given over to sin; but later, when the Lord was plaguing with hardest strokes the Pharaoh raging against the sons of Abraham, and he still did not come to his senses, upon him, who had entered the sea with chariots and horsemen, that he might seize the sons of Israel walking through the dry ground in [1197A] the midst of the sea, the heavenly company of horse being sent in, it executed vengeance for the Lord, and swallowed up both the king himself and the princes, the chariots and the charioteers and the horsemen, the waters being brought back to their former place by their ministry; and so it freed his people by a glorious victory.
