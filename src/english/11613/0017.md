## 7. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ This therefore is what the Bridegroom says, « My friend, I have likened thee to my company of horse, in the chariots of Pharaoh, » that is, while the angelic company was occupied in overthrowing the chariots of Pharaoh, I appointed thee like to that company of mine, so acting for thee, so fighting for thee, so rescuing thee, as one willing always to have thee a partaker of my company of horse. ⟦s2⟧ According to what was said by the Lord to the Jews, « You err, not knowing the scriptures, nor the power of God. ⟦s3⟧ For in the resurrection [1197B] they shall neither marry nor be given in marriage, but they are as the angels of God in heaven [var: Vulg. *erunt*, "shall be"] [n: (Matth. XXII, 29, 30)]. » But how great a thing it is to be likened to those whose virtue is that virtue, whose power is that power, which was shown at Lachish, in the army of Sennacherib [n: (IV Reg. XIX, 39)], when, as he held out threats against Jerusalem, it came to pass that in that night the Angel of the Lord came and struck of the camp of the Assyrians a hundred and eighty-five thousand men, for the delivering of Jerusalem. ⟦s4⟧ But if by one Angel striking so many thousands of men were laid low, what would happen if the whole company of horse of God rose up against the earth? it would not come about that even one of the reprobate should be left. ⟦s5⟧ For the Lord says, when the profane nation was seizing him, and Peter, to defend him, [1197C] struck with the sword the servant of the Prince: « Put up thy sword into the scabbard [var: Vulg. at this place *converte gladium tuum in locum suum*; the words printed are those of Joan. XVIII, 11]. ⟦s6⟧ Dost thou think that I cannot ask my Father, and he will give me more than twelve legions of angels? [n: (Matth. XXVI, 52, 53.)] » Which number indeed contains eighty thousand, eight less: and for the defence of Eliseus also many thousands indeed were sent, which, when Eliseus prayed that the Lord would open the eyes of his servant, were shown to the servant himself: for thus it was said, « Behold the mountain full of horses and of fiery chariots round about Eliseus [n: (IV Reg. VI, 17)]. » This therefore is the company of horse of God, in whom he fights, by whom he works his mysteries, to whom he likens the Bride whom he chose out of men, and conforms her to himself in comeliness, in power, and in virtue. ⟦s7⟧ He says therefore, « My friend, I have likened thee, » that is, in my predestination [1197D] I have already made thee like « to the company of horse » of mine, that is, to my Angels, whom I send forth to execute my vengeances, to my wars, to those things which seem to men exceedingly grievous, as was lately shown in the chariots of Pharaoh. ⟦s8⟧ There follows:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ 9. ⟦s2⟧ *Beautiful are thy cheeks, as the turtledove's: thy neck as necklaces.*

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The Bridegroom says to the Bride, that he may soothe her and render her at peace with him in all things, and may not allow her in any degree to spring back to the feeding of her kids, « I have likened thee, my friend, to my company of horse, » that is, in predestination I have appointed thee like in all things to my company. ⟦s2⟧ But because what has been predestined [1198A] must needs be fulfilled in act, in order that thou mayest be made worthy of the heavenly company, it behoves that they who hold the place of cheeks in thy body should be as it were the cheeks of a turtledove, and should present in themselves as it were the likeness of that bird. ⟦s3⟧ For the nature of this bird is this: it is joined to one mate, and when he is slain it is not coupled to another: it sits upon the branches of trees now dead, lacking leaves and fruit: there it moans, and sends forth a moan instead of a song. ⟦s4⟧ Those therefore whom this Bride is understood to have in her body as cheeks, these she is bidden to have like to the cheeks of the turtledove. ⟦s5⟧ For the cheeks are those parts which chew the food, and champ it and lessen it, and make it fit to be passed into the body. ⟦s6⟧ These are the holy writers, who, taught by the word of God, conceive Holy Scripture in their mind, and refresh themselves with the same, [1198B] and by imparting it to the uses of holy Church render her fit for the nuptials, and dear to God, and worthy of the eternal wedlock. ⟦s7+8⟧ These understand themselves to be joined as to a mate to Jesus Christ our Lord, « who, being in the form of God, thought it not robbery to be equal with God: but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men, and in habit found as a man [n: (Phil. II, 6, 7)]. » To him as to a mate they join themselves, to him they cleave, him they love; and since he has now been taken up from them into heaven, they affirm and maintain « that there is no other name under heaven in which men must be saved [n: (Act. IV, 12)]. » These, sitting upon dead branches, because those whom they consider dead to the world but living to God, [1198C] in them they take delight and rest, those whom neither the leaves of worldly wisdom clothe, nor worldly fruits delight, which quickly wither and perish. ⟦s9⟧ But these send forth a moan instead of a song, moaning that they lack that which they long for, and do not lay hold of the Lord Jesus whom they desire. ⟦s10⟧ Of these gathered into one it is said, « Blessed is he whose will is in the law of the Lord, and in his law he shall meditate day and night. ⟦s11⟧ And he shall be like the tree which is planted beside the running waters, which shall bring forth its fruit in its season. ⟦s12⟧ And its leaf shall not fall off, and all things whatsoever he shall do shall prosper [n: (Psal. I, 1, 2, 3)]. » These things are assigned to the writers of the divine law; as those which follow are attributed to its teachers and preachers.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1198D] *Thy neck as necklaces.*

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For *collum*, the neck, is so called from *colligando*, binding together, in that it binds the body to the head, and turns these two into one. ⟦s2⟧ By which name preachers and holy teachers are aptly named, who, receiving those things which are given by the Holy Spirit and expounding them by his gift, set them before the whole Church, and make her cleave to her head; who assuredly ought to be as necklaces fixed upon the breast, admonishing of the good those to whom they are attached. ⟦s3⟧ For a necklace fixed upon the breast speaks thus, says thus: Whom I have undertaken to guard, be thou chaste, be modest, be a lover of all honesty; let no adulterous hand know thee, nor let anything touch [1199A] thee whereby the faith owed to thy Bridegroom may be injured, or love in any point diminished. ⟦s4⟧ Which voice assuredly well befits the preacher and the teacher, that he be chaste and honest, and, like a round necklace, perfect on every side by his roundness, and circumspect. ⟦s5⟧ Let him keep these things in himself, let him teach everywhere that they are to be observed. ⟦s6⟧ Whence that golden necklace rings out well, saying: The loins of the faithful are girded, when evil concupiscence is repressed, when wicked thought is restrained, when the mind resists lust, when effect is denied to wantonness, when luxury is trodden down, when avarice and rapine are contemned, when injury of a neighbor is avoided, when pride is conquered, when envy is overcome. ⟦s7⟧ And that these necklaces may fitly hang from the neck, and remain firmly fixed upon the breast, the Bridegroom says:
