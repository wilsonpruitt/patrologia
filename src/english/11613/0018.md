## 10.

*We will make thee golden murenulae.*

The *murena* is a fish, comely in its length, and sprinkled over with a variety of color. Whence chains wrought of gold are called *murenulae*, which, set about the neck, are stretched out at length, and, joined to the necklace, bind it immovably to the breast-band. These therefore he sets forth that he will make for the Church his Bride; because as the understanding of those who give the law, or who give the first principles of letters, out of which either the Old or the New Testament arises, is from the divine wisdom, so also the understanding, the talents, and the whole invention of those who are the expositors of the same Scriptures are from him: and the things by which they enlarge, or magnify, or strengthen the Church proceed from the overflowing of the divine gift. The Bridegroom speaks of them [1199C] because he himself will make them, namely those sentences, but « vermiculated with silver, » that is, adorned with the divine eloquence. Whence he signifies that he gives them not only the understanding which the expositors put forth, but that he also ministers the fragrance of eloquence, whereby they please, whereby they are received into the fellowship of their hearers. He says therefore, « We will make thee golden *murenulae*, » that is, we will confer upon thee divine sentences, « vermiculated, » that is, adorned after the manner of worms, and set off « with silver, » that is, with the divine eloquence, which rings more sweetly than all metals. But the very change of speech is to be marked, that whereas hitherto the Bridegroom has used the singular in his speech, now, framing his words to the same Bride, he speaks in the plural number, « We will make golden *murenulae* » for thee; thus [1199D] showing the secret of the Trinity, which established the whole of both laws, and administered to the expositors of both the grace whereby they might do this and be pleasing. Whence the restoring of the highest salvation is now to be hoped for by her, whom, lest she ever give heed to the feeding of her kids, he both names above « friend, » and here, promising his own gifts, now reveals to her the mystery of the highest and adorable Trinity.

11. *While the King was in his repose, my spikenard gave forth its odor.*

Now when the Bride had heard those things which were set before her by the Bridegroom, namely, that she had been likened to the heavenly company of horse; and that they who in her body hold the place of cheeks [1200A] ought to have the likeness of the turtledove; and that they who hold the place of the neck should be likened to necklaces; having heard nonetheless the promise, that *murenulae* made by the hand of the highest Trinity are to be given her; the Bride opens her mouth and draws in the spirit, having yet nothing that she may offer, nothing that she may worthily render in return, save only that charity with which she was endowed from the beginning, before the incarnation of Christ, and that with which, after his advent and his ascension into the heavens, she was drawn to him by a marvellous gift. Hoping this to be precious, reckoning this unworthy of repulse, she makes mention of it, and promises that it will abide with her in eternal stability, saying, « When the King was in his repose [var: the lemma above and Vulg. *Dum*, "While"]. » And by the King's repose she names that purpose of the divine mind by which the Father disposed to send his Son into the lands, [1200B] to take flesh of the kindly virgin Mary, and that he, born of her, should teach the world, and that they who did not bear his holy doctrine should affix him to the cross, and that he should die, and after death rise again by his own power, and afterward ascend mightily into heaven, charity marvellously working these things, and the Son obeying herein, and the Holy Spirit herein consenting. And although this was disposed by eternal law, yet it had delay, and from Eve unto Adam, and on account of Adam's offence, it was deferred unto this fullness of time. But now through Christ grace and truth has been given [var: Vulg. *gratia et veritas per Jesum Christum facta est*, "grace and truth was made by Jesus Christ"] [n: (Joan. I, 17)]; and what had been disposed by the Father was completed by the working of grace.

He says therefore, « When he was in his repose, » that is, while he whom king above all things by judgment, the Son, [1200C] namely, who with the Father and the Holy Spirit rules all things; while he « was in his repose, » that is, was resting without labor in this purpose of his mind, « My spikenard gave forth its odor. » The spikenard is a lowly and odorous tree, by which the Bride designates those to whom, before his advent, God himself conferred knowledge of the mystery and of the Sacrament which he was to work; who preached and loved him that was to come, and who burned with the inmost desire of the mind for this, that as they saw him in the spirit, so they might be made worthy to see him with bodily sight. This burning is charity, and the odor of holy desire, by which the holy Fathers kindled one another toward him. It is that odor which the Bride declares that the spikenard gave, while the king was in his repose. [1200D] This odor went forth first from the Father into the world and into the hearts of mortals, who, being indignant at the serpent because he had deceived the woman, said: « Because thou hast done these things, thou art cursed among all living things and beasts of the earth. Upon thy breast shalt thou go, and earth shalt thou eat all the days of thy life [n: (Gen. III, 14)]. » Which, as it was said, was so fulfilled in him by the divine sentence. For what goes upon its breast is never lifted from the earth: which is true of him, because the hearts of those whom he inhabits, while he lords it in them, he never permits of his own will to be lifted from earthly pleasures and thoughts, never to rise up into the light of the virtues which is from heaven; but « thou shalt eat » it all the days [1201A], that is, he compels them to be delighted with earthly pleasures, to sleep and rest in them, by a persuasion as false as he himself is false. But at length mercy looked down from heaven; for God says, « I will put enmities between thee and the woman, and thy seed and her seed [n: (Gen. III, 15)]; » as if he should say: This woman by believing thee has slipped away, I will create one woman who shall crush thy whole head, who by the opposing of virtue shall wear away the beginnings of all thy suggestions which thou fixest in wretched mortals, and will in no wise permit them to reach the effect thou urgest.

This was that blessed Mary, who was sanctified from the womb, who was straightway so walled about by the Holy Spirit that no place was left to the serpent of tempting her, or of suggesting anything sinister: if [cj: *si*; read *sic*, "so"] all that [1201B] is wont to move the hearts of mortals, offered to her, presented to her, was straightway crushed by the heel of virtue. Whence between her and the serpent enmities have been set and made firm, so that what pleases the one displeases the other, what the one party urges the other utterly abhors, nor can wills wholly contrary agree upon one point. She possesses the glory of heaven, and rejoices to have with her perpetually all who commit themselves to her devotion; and those who tarry here, and have their heart toward her, she guards with her prayers, nor are they to be turned aside by his suggestions, or indeed to be wholly made subject. But the serpent goes about the lurking-places of hell, and there disposes places for his own; here, for those who stray from the faith, who do not fear to fulfil in act the things which he suggests in a thousand ways, he prepares punishments beyond reckoning, [1201C] and deadly griefs. These then are the enmities set between this woman and the serpent, which are also firmly established between the seed of the woman and the seed of the serpent. For the seed of this woman is Jesus Christ himself; the seed of the serpent, all who are not Christ's. The first seed of her was Abel, who confirmed his testimony to the Son of the Virgin by faith, by sacrifice, and by death. Her seed was Henoch, who bore testimony to the same son by true faith and by his ascension. Her seed was Noe, who, after the pattern of the same son, saved the Church in the flood of many waters in the wood. Her seed were Sem and Japheth, who did not look upon their father's shame, but covered it with faces turned away, veiling their own faces. For here God the Father, after the pattern of Noe, is said to have fallen asleep, when he consented that his son should be seized, [1201D] and, shamefully affixed to the cross, should be exposed to reproach; whom Cham saw and mocked, the accursed people of the Jews: but the two brothers, with faces turned away, did not see these things, nor mock them; two peoples, namely, the one of Jews only, penitent and converted, the other of the Gentiles. These, venerating the things done upon the cross, reckon nothing base, but covering them with the cloak of the scriptures, they venerate and adore them all. And to this seed Abraham is worthily ascribed, to whom, sitting in the door of his tabernacle in the heat of the day, the Lord is read to have appeared, and to have opened at once the signal mystery of the highest Trinity and of the Unity: to whom afterward, in his son Isaac, the mystery of the Lord's [1202A] passion was revealed, when, Isaac being bound for the passion, the father's sword, the angel forbidding, is drawn back from his neck, and the ram caught by the horns is offered. In which the will is truly to be held for the deed, which the angel alone hindered, and to which the father consented, the sword being now brandished, the neck stretched out, the son. With these two patriarchs that third, Jacob, is worthily numbered, whom we know as sleeping upon the stone, which is Christ, and beholding the ladder raised into heaven, by which certain angels were descending to visit the elect, while others were carrying up to the heights those already elect and now long well-deserving; and whom, worn down by the long squalor of toil, we know to have earned the two daughters of Laban, that is, the two Churches, the one given to action, the other to contemplation, [1202B] and, his father Laban being unwilling, to have returned with them to his own place; and, as he was at his last and prophesying by a prophetic spirit true things concerning Christ in his son Judah, we do not doubt that he said these words. « Judah, thee shall thy brethren praise [n: (Gen. XLIX, 8)]. » In which, when he had foretold many things concerning his passion and resurrection, he adds this also, « The sceptre shall not be taken away from Judah, nor a ruler from his thigh, until he come that is to be sent, and he shall be the expectation of the nations [n: (ibid., 10)]. » Which indeed is true of Christ; for from David their first King they had Kings in succession from the tribe of Judah, or at least Leaders, until, seizing Christ, they said, « We have no king but Caesar » [n: (Joan. XIX, 15)]. « If thou release this man, thou art not Caesar's friend [1202C] [n: (ibid., 12)]: » and adding they said also, « His blood be upon us, and upon our children [n: (Matth. XXVII, 25)]. » By which blood they were consequently overwhelmed; and when the Roman army came they were so destroyed by sword, by famine, and by pestilence, that countless thousands of that people died; and all the remnants, given over to exile and scattered under divers kings, serve foreign rulers, and guard their life by performing what they are commanded.
