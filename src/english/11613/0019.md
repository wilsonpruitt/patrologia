## 10. (cont.)

This they obtained from the death of Christ, of whom it is said, « and he shall be the expectation of the Gentiles: who his robe [n: (Gen. XLIX, 11)], » that is, his inner garment, « shall wash with wine, » white, namely, and pure and clarified, most clear, that is, he shall purify it by a certain interior and spiritual [1202D] understanding: « and his cloak, » namely the outer garment, this he shall cleanse « with the blood of the grape; » that is, with the passion of Christ. For those who serve Christ inwardly, discussing his hidden things in mind, and searching out more closely the several things which are said of his Trinity and Unity, of his divinity and his assumed humanity, and of the several sacraments of the Church, must needs excel by a certain higher and supereminent understanding. But for the rest it is enough if they confess and believe his death and blood and their own deliverance; concerning whom this was set down before, « Tying his cloak [var: Vulg. *pullum suum*, "his colt"] to the vineyard, and his she-ass, O my son, to the vine [n: (ibid.)]. » For Christ is the vine to which the she-ass is tied, [1203A] that is, by whose preaching the primitive Church of the Jews, which established both laws, was converted. But « to the vineyard » of his, that is, to the apostles, the Church of the Jews and of the Gentiles has been tied, when, acquiescing in their words and admonitions, it believed and adored Christ, the power and wisdom of God, bearing our infirmities, redeeming by his own death the death to which it was liable. But who would doubt that Moses belonged to this seed? who first wrote, « I will put enmities between thee and the woman, and thy seed and her seed. » And who could recount David also, and Job, Isaiah, and all the prophets? whose testimonies concerning the advent of Christ, if any man should undertake to write them, would lay upon himself the care of making a great volume. All these indeed are recorded to be the seed of the blessed virgin; [1203B] whom she bore, testifying by word, desiring in heart, and earnestly ministering the good odor and the desire of him to one another and to others. And these indeed are the things which this holy Bride recounts to her Beloved: that these holy fathers believed Christ before he came into flesh, foretold him, and desired him with a marvellous expectation. Now, proceeding further, she shows (after he has now come into what is ours, and, the mystery for which he had come being fulfilled in our flesh, has ascended into the heavens) how the Church, in whose number she reckons herself, follows him thither, saying:

12. *A little bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me, he shall abide between my breasts.*

[1203C]For this is as if she should say: My Beloved has been removed far from me in body, but has not departed from me in spirit: for he will make a stay with me; but what stay? that of which the Lord says, « If any man love me, he will keep my word, and my Father loveth him [var: Vulg. *diliget*, "will love"], and we will come to him, and will make our abode with him [n: (Joan. XIV, 23)]. » Behold that this stay is the abiding of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, with the Bride: in which she knows the Father. For she has the power of treading upon serpents and scorpions, and of resisting every spirit that is not good, drawing her into evils, nay urging the worst things; she has also wisdom, whereby she has the savor to do what she can; she has also in the Holy Spirit the will always to do that which is good: so that, while she can always, is always wise, [1203D] always wills that which is of honor, that which is of virtue, thou mayest hence acknowledge in her the abode of the highest Trinity. In the Apocalypse also John says, « Behold I stand at the door and knock: if any man shall hear my voice, and open the gate, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me [n: (Apoc. III, 20)]. » Who would doubt that this Bride heard the voice of her beloved? who would question that she opened the door to him? who would not understand that she is made glad with him at the supper? who would not judge that at this supper the Father and the Holy Spirit sit together with the Son? A rich supper, at which it is not doubtful that the heavenly Spirits minister; over which he presides who is their Lord. What banquets, what dishes, [1204A] what gifts are set out at it, understand from this which the apostle says, « comparing spiritual things with spiritual [n: (I Cor. II, 13)]: » for spiritual are the things which the Church sets before her Bridegroom with those assessors.

Here is set first the humility that is pleasing to God, with those virtues which accompany it, that is, the fear of God, penitence, confession pleasing to God, and the holy satisfaction of work. In a certain second order are set ordered chastity, moderate sobriety, commendable abstinence, and the things which are about these. The third place is held by peace, patience, brotherly love, and that which transcends all, charity toward God, and holy contemplation. This the Lord in John names a « supper, » because as no other meal follows supper, so he desires that the end of our life be consummated [1204B] in these things; that we may lay hold of him by fearing, by repenting, by making satisfaction, by living chastely, by having love toward all, and by loving God above all things, and may enjoy him without end. The Bride says therefore, « My Beloved to me, » that is, with me, « shall abide; » as if she should say, he will make a stay. And that she may signify the place where he will make his stay, she adds, « Between my breasts. » The breasts contain within themselves the chest, the principal part of our body, in which the heart is contained, and the liver, and the other parts whence very many powers arise, by which the human body is in great part ruled and quickened. That therefore she may designate by name the place where the Beloved may abide with her, she says, « Between my breasts, » that is, In the midst of my breast, that [1204C] he himself may hold my heart, and kindle it with love of himself, and that whatever shall seek to turn me to another thing, this he may weaken and turn away. For with the heart we love God, with the heart we hold charity; and if we retain charity, we assuredly have justice and prudence, fortitude and temperance; for charity retains in itself the effect of all these. For justice renders to each what is his own: it renders to God what is his, which is all honor, all glory, and the humble and holy devotion of every creature: it renders to a neighbor this, that one love him as much as oneself, that one watch over his advantages as much as over one's own, and be saddened and grieve at his disadvantages as much as at one's own: and this, resisting lust and the other impulses of the mind which are not right, what does it bear but the act of fortitude, [1204D] and strive to fulfil that act in itself? But temperance fulfils it, while in all these things it considers and provides that nothing be too much, that nothing be less than right. Let us mark what Paul asserts concerning it, saying, « Charity is patient, is kind: Charity envieth not, dealeth not perversely, is not puffed up, is not ambitious, seeketh not her own, etc. [n: (I Cor. XIII, 4, 5)]. »

And if any man should diligently seek to pursue these things, he will find this the more excellent way, that is, the way to which no effect of virtue is lacking, from which it is established that nothing is taken away whereby one might be able to be brought near to God. Whence the apostle says, « You are bought with a great price. Glorify and bear God in your body [n: (I Cor. VI 20)]. » The Bride says [1205A] therefore that her Beloved will abide with her, and will be to her as a bundle of myrrh. For myrrh is an ointment which flows from a tree, which is named by the same name, myrrh; for they are equal in name because they are equal also in quality: for myrrh is a tree, and also the bitter ointment which runs down from it. Whence the bodies of the dead are sprinkled with this very ointment, lest either rottenness corrupt them, or a worm come to destroy them: so the Lord's body is read to have been anointed with myrrh and aloes. This Bride says therefore, « My Beloved to me shall abide between my breasts [n: (Joan. XIX, 39)], » and he will be to me as a bundle of myrrh. And if thou refer this to the ointment, it is as if it were said, He will be to me as some little store of myrrh, which [1205B] may defend me both from rottenness, lest I be corrupted, and from the biting of worms, lest I be destroyed: if to the tree, it is plain that a bundle is made from a tree, and that she has this laid up within her breast for the same defence. For this « bundle of myrrh is the great memory of the passion of Christ; by which each man sees himself bound unceasingly to every neighbor, and understands how great a debtor he is to God, who suffered such things for him: and if thou hold it, if thou behold God, whithersoever thou turn thyself, suffering upon the cross, thou wilt free thyself by this very contemplation from the rottenness of vices and from the biting of worms, that is, from the temptation of unclean spirits. There follows:

13. *A cluster of Cyprus is my Beloved to me.*

Because this Bride, speaking of the Bridegroom, says, « Between [1205C] my breasts my beloved shall abide with me: and he will be to me as a bundle of myrrh, » which prepares bitterness only, it could be seen from this that the passion of Christ furnished cause for grief alone, but conferred no gladness upon the mind: whence, the bitterness being set first which is owed to Christ truly suffering, she adds the delight which from his death is shown to all who venerate it. For as it is just that grief and compassion be shown to one who grieves, so to him who frees and redeems the world joy and exultation are truly owed. Whence this Bride says, « A cluster of Cyprus is my beloved to me. » For Cyprus is an island abounding in vines, having clusters incomparable to the rest in their greatness, giving a wine which gladdens those who taste it [1205D] by the sweetness inborn in it, and soothes and mitigates grief, if there be any from suffering. For Christ is the bread which strengthens, he himself the wine which gladdens, and he himself is the oil which maketh glad the face of man, This Bride says therefore that her Beloved has been made a cluster of Cyprus; because as a cluster is nothing else than a gathering of many like grains firmly joined together into one, yielding wine abundantly when it is pressed, and gladdening on every side those who taste it: so has Christ been made, binding many to himself by his death, and reconciling them to himself in the faith of his passion; who, when they are pressed out, that is, when they are questioned with tortures and with other means most wickedly devised, send forth the wine of holy confession, by confessing and glorifying the Lord Jesus, and even, if need be, by undergoing death; [1206A] they confess and acknowledge that he alone is the living and true God: and if they survive, they exult with joy as confessors; if they are consumed, glad in death they rejoice as victors with Christ conquering.

Paul expresses both, saying, « Christ was made obedient for us unto death, even the death of the cross [var: Vulg. *humiliavit semetipsum, factus obediens usque ad mortem*, without *pro nobis*] [n: (Phil. II, 8)]. » Behold the myrrh, to which we owe myrrh, and bitterness, and compassion of every kind. « For which cause God also hath exalted him, and hath given him a name which is above every name: That in the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven, on earth, and under the earth; and that every tongue should confess that the Lord Jesus Christ is in the glory of God the Father [n: (ibid., 9, 10, 11)]. » Exulting indeed in this joy, this Bride says, « A cluster [1206B] of Cyprus is my Beloved to me. » For cleaving to the wood, he moved the earth, he darkened heaven; crucified, he crucified the world. So he drew all things to himself, and as victor he gladdened me, who suffer with his death, and overflowed me with joy. There follows:

*In the vineyards of Engaddi.*
