## CHAPTER II.

1. *I am the flower of the field, and the lily of the valleys.*

The Bridegroom, called to rest, answers thus: Thou callest me to rest, my friend, my Bride; and that thou mayest allure me the more, thou addest that « our little bed is flowery: » but this answer I give thee, that « I am the flower of the field, and the lily of the valleys. » In which this is to be observed, that there are two fields, one heavenly, the other earthly. The heavenly field extends as far as heaven reaches, because, [1212B] having no ending, it is great and measureless. This field has flowers: angels, archangels, virtues, dominations, thrones, principalities, and powers, cherubim, and seraphim: of each of these orders there are many thousands: with these flowers heaven is filled. All the saints desire to be among these flowers; with groans and sighs they pant to be in their company. Of all whom indeed « I am the flower: » from me indeed they receive what they are; from me they have this of fragrance, that they are good; all these stand unceasingly before my majesty; they sing together a hymn of glory to me, saying, « Holy, holy, holy, the Lord God almighty » [n: (Apoc. IV, 8)]. Me therefore, who refresh all these with good fragrance, and supply fragrance sufficient for their feeding, [1212C] what is it that thou demandest that I be present at thy flowery little bed, and that thou redoublest thy voice, as though about to add something to my glory? Which indeed was said thus, not because the Bridegroom disdains to be present at the good beginnings of the faithful; but that the Bride, hearing these things, may be the more inflamed with her own desire, and may the more choose by her own prayer that he be present, who is the flower of the whole world, and its comeliness, and its illumination. Which the Bridegroom too indeed intimates, saying, « Of the field, » the heavenly one, « I am the flower, » yet I am « also the lily of the valleys. » The earthly field, therefore, is this world, which that higher flower, visiting it, showed himself to the faithful as a lily, and gave and showed himself humble to the humble. In the lily indeed there is a flower most sweet in scent, most pleasing to the sight, and there is also [1212D] a stem thick with leaves; feeding not indeed by scent, nor having sweetness of taste, yet having great efficacy in medicines. These things indeed befit Christ; who, sent by the Father, truly appeared on earth as a lily of most sweet scent; of whom Paul says: « But thanks be to God, who always leads us in triumph in Christ Jesus, and manifests the odour of his knowledge by us in every place: For we are a good odour to Christ unto God in them that are saved, and in them that perish [n: (II Cor. II, 14, 15)]. » [var: Vulg. *Christi bonus odor*, "the good odour of Christ"] But he was most pleasing to the sight of those who received him. Whence Peter says: « Lord, to whom shall we go? thou hast the words of eternal life. And we have believed, and have known, that thou art the Christ the Son of the living God » [n: (Joan. VI, 71, 72)]. But the stem thick with leaves is his humanity, [1213A] which indeed, in the words by which he reproved the world, displeased many, to whom he took care to offer the help of a saving medicine. But he is « the lily of the valleys, » because « God resists the proud, but to the humble he gives grace [n: (Jac. IV, 6)]. » From above too it was said, « Let the proud be confounded, because they have done iniquity unjustly against me [n: (Psal. CXVIII, 78)]. » But of the humble it was said, « that the valleys shall abound with corn: they shall cry out, yea they shall sing a hymn [n: (Psal. LXIV, 14)]. »

2. *As the lily among thorns, so is my friend among the daughters.*

The flower of the world has spoken of himself, saying, « I am the flower of the field. » who also declared to the world that he, true God and man, would profit the humble, saying, [1213B] « And the lily of the valleys. » But now he goes on concerning the Bride, who is joined to him by the love of the heart, saying: As I, a lily among thorns, have lived in the world; so my friend shall dwell among the same in the world. But he names as « thorns » the detractions, upbraidings, and mockeries by which the world despised him with wicked derision: he names as « thorns » also their deeds and handlings, by which, seizing him with swords and clubs, they led him as a robber and a murderer to the chief men, Annas and Caiaphas: and thereafter, before the Judge, spat upon, dishonoured with blows of the fist and with buffets, and crowned with thorns, they demanded that he be condemned and fastened through with nails. To these thorns, to this thorny bed, he calls the bride, who was preparing for herself a flowery bed [1213C] beneath beams of cedar and panelled ceilings of cypress, who, humbled, did not refuse this bed, but endured one like it in Peter, and in Paul too, and the rest of the apostles, in Stephen, and in the other many thousands of martyrs throughout the world, and received it gladly. But from whom she received it he adds, saying, « Among the daughters, » that is, generations depraved and perverse, to whom the Lord showed himself. Yet these did not know him; for so it is written, « He came unto his own, and his own received him not [n: (Joan. I, 11)]: whom he names « his own, » not by affection, but by creation alone; since indeed, laying hands upon his own, they afflicted them with torments and tortures: he now names the transgressing daughters by the first creation, and now [1213D] signifies that they have a filial affection along with his own. There follows:

3. *As the apple tree among the trees of the woods, so is my beloved among the sons.*

The Bridegroom and the Bride answer one another with reciprocal praise, and, refreshing themselves with mutual praises, the more they praise, the more they desire to praise. For this Bride says, « As the apple tree among the trees of the woods, so is my beloved among the sons. » For the apple tree is a tree that puts forth branches widely, brings forth leaves, swells with blossom, and out of the blossoms brings forth the fruit of apples even to ripeness: which fruit, whether it be called *pomum* or *malum*, is of surpassing colour, of pleasant taste, and of a fragrance excellent beyond what can be or could ever be told. Laden with this, the tree itself, which is called « the apple tree, » not only takes on comeliness in itself, [1214A] but also clothes the surrounding woods with comeliness, and renders the whole place about it most pleasant, and most worthy of a holy habitation. This Bride says therefore, « As the apple tree among the trees of the woods, so is my beloved among the sons; » wishing by the tree of surpassing fruit that the only-begotten Son of God, beloved of her, be understood, who so clothed and arrayed with glory and honour the flesh which he assumed, that deservedly, before all the saints, it is held most worthy of honour and reverence, and is most excellently venerated. Whence it was said, « The Lord hath reigned, he is clothed with comeliness, the Lord is clothed with strength, and hath girded himself [n: (Psal. XCII, 1)]. » He adds also this which is said, « Among the trees of the woods; » for as Christ is a heavenly tree, so all the saints are heavenly trees, who, living in the flesh, [1214B] brought forth fruits pleasing to Christ. Among them the apostles, and the holy martyrs, and all the Saints together are numbered, who, knowing nothing save Christ, following him by a glorious death, bearing witness to the truth, have long since received glorious seats in heaven. Of these it is said in the Psalms, « Then shall all the trees of the woods rejoice before the face of the Lord, because he cometh; for he cometh to judge the earth [n: (Psal. XCV, 13)]. » For the Lord is to judge this world; who in a body once condemned and now wondrously glorified will show himself visible to all the saints in their own raised bodies; and there will then be rejoicing for the trees of the heavenly woods, when by a sure apprehension [1214C] and glorification of themselves, not only of the spirit but of the body also, they have known that they did not run at uncertainty, nor labour in vain. But the trees of that wood through which the beasts and the whelps of lions pass to snatch food for themselves will then be assigned to their own night, and to everlasting darkness. This therefore is what the Church says: « as the apple tree, » laden with its own fruits, shines out « among the trees of the woods » with a certain wondrous beauty; « so my beloved, » the only-begotten Son of God, upon whom my love has bestowed itself wholly, stands far more eminent with a certain unspeakable glory among all the saints, whom he has adopted to himself as sons not by creation alone, but by grace also. There follows:

*Under the shadow of him whom I had desired, I sat.*
