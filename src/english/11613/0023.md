## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

Here it must be observed whence a shadow comes. [1214D] A shadow comes to these bodies from a light-bearing body, and from a shadow-casting body, and from the place upon which the shadow is thrown: if any of these be lacking, there is no shadow. This tree of the apple therefore, since it stands among the trees of the woods, since it is spread wide with branches and rich with leaves, must needs cast a shadow against the sun: beneath which it follows that this Bride sits and rests. The « shadow, » therefore, is the frequent meditation upon the divine incarnation and passion, upon the resurrection also and the ascension, and upon his acts and words; into which she protests that she panted with the whole desire of her heart, that there might be fulfilled in deed what had been promised by the Father, and foretold by the prophetic promise of the holy Fathers. Whence Isaias, « Let thy ancient counsel, O Lord, be made true [n: (Isai. XXV, 1)]. » [var: Vulg. *cogitationes antiquas fideles*, "ancient faithful designs"] Jeremias [1215A] too, desiring this in the Lamentations, says, « The breath of our mouth, Christ the Lord, is taken in our sins: to whom we said, Under thy shadow we shall live among the nations [n: (Thren. IV, 20)]. » And there stand the other oracles of the holy Fathers, which a diligent reader will easily find for himself. This therefore is the shadow, in which this present Bride bears witness that she has sat. For whereas the postures of bodies are four, to stand, to sit, to recline, and to lie, sitting is that which is more fit for ordering and disposing affairs, and more acceptable at once for giving counsels and for forming judgments. Whence Holy Scripture too often makes mention of it; for David says, « The Lord shall sit as king for ever [n: (Psal. XXVIII, 10)]. » And that other, « Thy throne, O Lord, is for ever and ever [n: (Psal. XLIV, 7)]. » [var: Vulg. *Sedes tua, Deus*, "Thy throne, O God"] And that which follows; « The Lord said [1215B] to my Lord: Sit thou at my right hand [n: (Psal. CIX, 1)]. » In the book of Kings too it was said by the prophet Micheas: « I saw the Lord sitting upon a high throne, and all the host of heaven standing by him on the right hand and on the left [n: (III Reg. XXII, 19)]. » [var: Vulg. *super solium suum*, "upon his throne"] Isaias too says: « I saw the Lord sitting upon a throne high and lifted up: and the things that were beneath him filled the temple [n: (Isai. VI, 1)]. » And after all these the Lord himself says, « When the son of man shall sit upon the seat of his majesty, then shall all nations be gathered together before him [n: (Matth. XXV, 31, 32)]. » Girt about with these authorities also, the Church says of herself, « Under the shadow of him whom I had desired, I sat, » that he might come, or, which shadow I had desired, that it might be fulfilled in affection, I sat deliberating and desiring, [1215C] wholly bent in this affection, that he might fulfil to me, being his Bride, the things promised, and might not put off my prayers to an immeasurable while. In this his shadow « I sat, » because I applied my whole mind to this, that I might devoutly receive him, whether about to be incarnate or perhaps already incarnate, might faithfully adore him born, might behold him teaching the fullness of faith, might not despair of him dying, but might rise up with him rising again in great certainty of faith, and might follow him ascending thither whence he had come, with the whole intent of my mind, and might cleave to those who render him service. There follows:

*And his fruit is sweet to my throat.*

As a shadow stands apart from its own body; so the fruit of that apple stands apart from its own image. For this apple tree is a heavenly [1215D] tree, the heavenly wisdom indeed, in the heavens for this reason, that it is rooted in the heart of the Father; which not in the heavens only, but, when it pleased the Father, made a root in the earth: in the earth indeed, when the whole flood of divinity betook itself into the heart of the immaculate Virgin, and the heavenly shower with a gentle gliding-down utterly drenched the virgin fleece: which, since it is rooted in heaven and in earth, brings forth indeed a fruit that ministers sweetness to those who are in the heavens, and to those who are on earth. But what is this fruit? The Flesh indeed which from clean bread upon the holy altar, by the office of the minister, by the power of the word, has been made, and granted in a most sacred tasting for the feeding of the Church, lest perchance it faint by the way. Of which the Church says, to whom this too has been given, « And his fruit is sweet to my throat. » [1216A] O blessed throat, to which this sweetness has been granted! that, abiding in man, it may snatch itself above man, and being on earth, joining itself to him who presides in heaven, may mingle itself, from this sweetness, with that which is in life eternal. And because this has been granted only to the good and the holy, the Church says in her elect, « And his fruit is sweet to my throat. » For nothing in this life is sweeter than to pass from this tasting into that life which lacks end and all malice, and to come from this image of the fruit to that fruit which may always satisfy us, and make us joyful in eternity. There follows:

4. *The king hath brought me into the wine cellar; he hath set charity in order in me.* [var: Vulg. *Introduxit me in cellam vinariam*, without the king]

Between the Bridegroom and the Bride mutual discourses have now long been held: [1216B] she interposed, he answered, she commended him with many praises, and was herself much commended with praise by him. Whence, because words have been joined to words, and sayings like to sayings set down in return, lest likeness beget weariness, the Bride refrains from this manner of speaking, no longer speaking to the Bridegroom, but directing her words to her friends, and reporting these things, « The king hath brought me into the wine cellar. » But it must be observed in this place that some wine in Holy Scripture is commendable, and some reprobate. Reprobate, as is that of which Moses makes mention, saying: « Their vine is of the vineyard of Sodom, and of the suburbs of Gomorrha: their grape is a grape of gall, and their clusters most bitter. Their wine is the gall of dragons, and the incurable venom of asps [n: (Deut. XXXII, 32, 33)]. » [1216C] For the wine which is pressed out from this vineyard, or from those clusters, or from those grapes, is that most bitter wine. For this is the sense; of the reprobate, who, understanding the Holy Scriptures perversely, draw them into an alien meaning, and, fixing their eyes upon the sun without discretion, are themselves darkened by the light, and drag others with them into condemned error. There are also those who, though they do not raise their friends up to the Holy Scriptures, yet by their daily deeds and words corrupt the simpler sort, and they themselves compel them, bound with a threefold knot, to have foremost dwellings in hell. Of these the Prophet: « There is none that doeth good, there is not so much as one. Their throat is an open sepulchre, with their tongues they have dealt deceitfully; judge thereby, O God: the venom [1216D] of asps is under their lips [n: (Psal. V, 11, et Psal. XIII, 3)]. » [var: Vulg. *judica illos*, "judge them"] Commendable is that of which Solomon says, « Wisdom hath built herself a house, she hath hewn out seven pillars. She hath slain her victims, she hath mingled her wine, and set forth her table. She hath sent her handmaids to call to the tower, and to the walls of the city [n: (Prov. IX, 1, 2, 3)]. » For the heavenly wisdom, for herself, « constructed a house, » when God wholly appeared in man: « she hewed out » in it « seven pillars, » because the sevenfold spirit is in it. She administered grace to the world: « she slew her victims, » because by her own passion and by that of her own she prepared that same house: « she mingled wine, » because to those not grasping the secrets of the divinity she laid open the sacraments of the assumed humanity: « She set forth her table, » because she prepared for us the pastures of Scripture [1217A] by opening them: « She sent her handmaids, to call to the tower, and to the walls of the city because, as the Apostle says, Not many are wise according to the flesh, not many mighty, not many noble: but the foolish things of the world hath God chosen, that he may confound the wise: and the weak things of the world hath God chosen, that he may confound the strong: and the base things of the world, and the things that are contemptible, hath God chosen, and the things that are not, that he might bring to nought the things that are. » [n: (I Cor. I, 26, 27, 28.)]
