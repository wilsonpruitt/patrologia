## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

These indeed are handmaids on account of their humility and lowliness, through whom the Lord admonished us to possess « the tower, » that is, the eminence of eternal life, and the walls of the heavenly Jerusalem. This is the wine which was pressed out from that vine and its branches, who says, « I am the vine, you the branches *(Joan. XV, [1217B] 5)*. » These the heavenly Father presses out, and they yield the best wine; that wine of which David says, « They shall be inebriated with the plenty of thy house [n: (Psal. XXXV, 9)]. » With this wine were Peter and Paul inebriated, when the one by the cross, the other by the sword, bore witness to God at Rome. With this wine all the apostles and martyrs were made drunk, when, despising all things that are of the world, and reckoning them as dung, they cast them away, and following Christ with a pure confession, they resolved rather to choose death for themselves with him and for him, than to lead a life together with those things. The Church says therefore, « The king hath brought me into the wine cellar, » that is, into a certain storehouse of the divine Scriptures, by which he drew me away from the world, by which he now permits me to have my dwelling among the things that are above: whence there follows, [1217C] « he hath set charity in order in me. » Behold that God sets charity in order in the Church, without which there is no ascending into heaven, without which there is no coming to God by any reasoning. For « God » is « charity [n: (Joan. I, 4, 8)], loving beyond doubt every creature; which unless he loved, it would not be at all, nor would there appear any cause or manner of its existence among creatures. He endowed with heavenly glory the good angels, and all the heavenly powers whatsoever, persisting in the goodness in which they were created, and to the spirit of deceit (who says, « I will set my seat toward the north, and I will be like the Most High) [n: (Isai. XIV, 14)] » in no way consenting, but firmly cleaving to the most high goodness by which they were created: so that they who, though they could sin, did not sin, [1217D] may now lack all possibility of sinning, and, being truly called « the firmament, » may cleave to him firmly and indissolubly, and love him, and obey him. Having charity toward these, he is assuredly loved by them; and this is found to be the first progression of charity. He also loves even the malignant spirits, not approving their malignity, by which they are turned away from him; but approving what is from himself, that they are knowing, that they are trembling, that they are strong, that they are unceasingly in possession of their own existence, and stand unceasingly obedient to the command of the highest King: this he approves, and it is acceptable to him, and he retains what is his own for himself, and confirms these same in all that he himself wills. Hence it is that Job is not tempted [n: (Job II, 8)] save by license granted from the highest Father: and when, sitting on the dunghill, he scraped the corrupt matter with a potsherd, [1218A] it was forbidden to Satan that he should tempt him in his soul: and Satan, fulfilling his commands, having fully tempted him in the body, did not strike him in the soul. So too, when Ahab was to be tempted, the Lord is read to have said to the adversary, « Who shall deceive Ahab king of Israel, that he may go up and fall at Ramoth Galaad. And a spirit went forth, and stood before the Lord, and said, I will go forth, and I will be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets. And the Lord said, Thou shalt deceive, and thou shalt prevail [n: (III Reg. XXII, 20, 21, 22)]: » and thus, license being received, Ahab, tempted by the malignant one, perished. So too God loves even evil men, wholly reproving indeed their malice, but that they are, that they are men, that they are capable of discipline, that they are capable of reason: these things indeed [1218B] he approves and loves, because they too are from him: but that they use all these things for evil, that they degenerate from that for which they were made, these things he neither approves nor loves, because they are not from him. Hence there is no discrepancy of the Scriptures; hence it is said to God, « Thou hast hated none of the things which thou hast made, O Lord [n: (Sap. XI, 25)]. » Hence also the Lord says, « I will be an enemy to thy enemies, and I will be adverse to thy adversaries [n: (Exod. XXIII, 22)]. » [var: Vulg. *affligam affligentes te*, "I will afflict them that afflict thee"]

Thus in the Scriptures thou wilt find many things that seem contrary to one another: but when this is understood, that in the same creature God loves certain things, while there are in that same creature certain things which he hates, thou wilt find nothing contrary. For Judas the traitor [n: (Joan. XIII, 2)], when the devil had already put it into his heart that he should betray him, did God [1218C] pursue him with hatred? [n: (ibid., V, 27.)] For he handed him the dipped bread, saying, « What thou dost, do quickly; » and very many other words besides, by which he might have been rebuked and corrected, had he not willingly given himself wholly over to Satan. So Saul [n: (Act. IX, 1)], when, breathing out threatenings and slaughter against the disciples of the Lord, he was going to Damascus with letters received, that he might persecute Jesus with the utmost hatred, the Lord indeed looked upon, when a light from heaven shone round about him, and falling to the earth he could see none of those things which he had been wont to see until then; to whom counsel was given from heaven, that he should enter the city, and be baptized, and consent to those things which should be said to him by Ananias; to which he indeed consented, and being baptized, he preached Jesus [1218D] the more loftily in proportion as he was made, not earthly from the earth, but wholly heavenly, being taught from heaven: and if it had befallen him to die before he was called, God would have found out for him a cause in which he might justly be saved.

God therefore loves all, both good and evil; the good, because they persist in goodness and advance; the evil, because, falling away from good, they present themselves as a matter for the justice of God, and, unwilling as they are to be tormented for ever, making themselves worthy of it, they prove and demonstrate that God concludes all things with a just end. Thus he himself has fulfilled what he commanded to be done: for he says, « Love your enemies; do good to them that hate you [n: (Matth. V, 44)]. » For how great are the good things which God shows to his enemies, and to those who hate him, there is no one who does not easily comprehend. Thus therefore God [1219A] sets charity in order in himself, teaching the Church how she ought to have charity set in order in herself [n: (Joan. I, 4, 19)]. For since God first loved us, this is the order, that we love him first and chiefly. And « let us love » him « with the whole heart, and the whole soul, and the whole mind [n: (Matth. XXII, 37)]: » we love with the whole heart, if we covet nothing in our heart, revolve nothing in our mind, which we believe displeases him: we love « with the whole soul, » if we apply the soul, which moves the body, to the fulfilling in act of nothing that displeases him: we love with the whole mind, when we do anything good, and there exists nothing of our devotion which draws us to anything other than to God. This is the first order of charity [n: (ibid., V, 39)]: the second, that a man love his neighbour as himself. But whom [1219B] do we judge to be our neighbour? This question is ancient, and was put to the Lord himself, and was resolved by the parable put forth concerning him who fell among robbers. For the Lord says: « It happened that a certain priest went up by the same way, and having seen him passed by. In like manner also a Levite, when he was near the place, and saw him, passed on. But a certain Samaritan, being on his journey, came near him, and seeing him, was moved with mercy. And drawing near he bound up his wounds, pouring in oil and wine. And setting him upon his own beast, he brought him to an inn, and took care of him. And the next day he took out two pence, and gave them to the host, and said, Take care of him, and whatsoever thou shalt spend over and above, I, when I return, will repay thee [n: (Luc. X, 31-36)]. » [var: Vulg. *descenderet*, "went down"]

This good Samaritan is said to have been his neighbour, [1219C] who seeing him was moved with mercy, and took care of him. O how many priests are we! O how many Levites! who passing by see the afflicted, see the wretched, and do not bind up their wounds, pouring in the oil of mercy and the wine of justice! O how many are we who, having not two pence only, but many weights of silver and gold, do not bestow even one mite upon the host, that he may take charge of him; and that the Lord himself may answer for us when he shall return! Whom therefore we see moved with mercy toward one afflicted, whom thou seest bearing care of him, and grieving with and suffering with his pain as with his own, him do thou, who hast entrusted thyself wholly to God with heart and soul and mind, reckon thy neighbour, and love as [1219D] thyself; which is, in this, in which « love also thyself. » For with this Scripture, in which it is said, « Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart, » that agrees which the Lord says in the Gospel: « Blessed are the clean of heart, for they shall see God [n: (Matth. V, 8)]; » and likewise: « From the heart come forth evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, fornications, thefts, false testimonies, blasphemies: these are the things that defile a man [n: (Matth. XV, 19, 20)]. » Whence Solomon too says, « With all watchfulness keep thy heart, because life proceeds from it [n: (Prov. IV, 23)]. » The same also subjoins in the Proverbs: « There are six things which God hates, and the seventh his soul detests: [1220A] Haughty eyes, a lying tongue, hands that shed innocent blood, a heart that devises most wicked thoughts, feet swift to run into evil, a deceitful witness that uttereth lies, and him that soweth discords among brethren [n: (Prov. VI, 16-20)]. » Whom therefore dost thou love as thyself? him for whom thou desirest that to befall which thou desirest for thyself, to whom thou standest as a helper in this, whom thou callest back from the evil which is everlasting, and from this, so far as thou canst, thou turnest and separatest him. What shall I say to thee of a father, or of a mother? who indeed are to be loved in that in which they are loved by God; but in the rest to be spurned. The Lord loved the Egyptians, imparting to them the good things which he made common to good and evil alike, showing signs and wonders by which they might have been converted, if they had had minds capable of being turned [1220B] to good. And profiting nothing in them, he gave them over to destruction, that they might go into the punishment which they had themselves deserved. God therefore teaches in what a father and a mother ought to be loved, or kinsfolk, and the rest of one's relations; namely, in that in which each is loved by God also: but in certain other things, in which one is turned away from God, he is indeed not to be loved, yet nevertheless to be honoured; according to that saying, « Honour thy father and thy mother, that thou mayest be long-lived upon the earth [n: (Exod. XX, 12)]. » For honour is paid to many, and far from them love is entwined with something else. There follows:

5. *Stay me up with flowers, compass me about with apples, for I languish with love.*
