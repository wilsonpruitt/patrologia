## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

This Bride of Christ, that is, the Church, or any perfected soul, having been brought into the wine-cellar of the King, [1220C] and given the Lord's wine to drink, shows charity set in order within her, saying to the Angels, or to the heavenly Virtues, or to the very ordainers of the Church, « Stay me up with flowers, compass me about with apples; for I languish with love. » This languor is good, this languor is to be desired; it seeks that no medicine be applied to it save that which is of flowers, or assuredly of apples. This languor is from the ordering of charity, when a man loves God with his whole heart, with his whole soul, with all his strength, and grasps him with his whole mind [n: (I Joan. II, 15, 16)]. Here the concupiscence of the flesh, here the concupiscence of the eyes, is not; here there is not the pride of life, which is of the world. She, for this charity, is truly dead, and in her lives the spirit which truly quickens, nor does the flesh bear rule, which [1220D] truly profits nothing: she seeks to be succoured, so that she may be stayed up with flowers, and compassed about with apples. What flowers shall we give? what apples are to be sought, that we may succour her that languishes? Fragrant flowers indeed, refreshing by their odour, and feeding souls, and strengthening them, that she may abide in that very thing wherein she is, and advance, and not grow lukewarm until she lay hold of it. Here, if thou bring together the flower of the violet, the whiteness of the lily, the reddening roses, and the unwithered flowers of every herb, and the flowers of all trees that are fruitful, thou shalt be welcome, and shalt work together, on the other part, toward the staying up of her, lest perchance she slip. These are the words of the saints, wherewith, being questioned concerning the Lord, [1221A] they answered most readily; those in which thou shalt be able to find faith, humility, patience, constancy, and fortitude, peace and hope, and that charity of which we treat. These, if thou begin from Abel, and rightly run through the line of the just as far as Abraham; if from his sacrifice thou come as far as Moses; if from Moses, the Spirit leading, thou reach unto Christ Jesus God; if from the Lord Jesus thou rightly fix the mind's edge upon those who were sent by him into the world: thou shalt find nothing in the words of these save what may teach faith, strengthen hope, and sow charity far and wide. But because fruits come forth from the flower, it is rightly subjoined, « Compass me about with apples, » that is, strengthen me with their fruits. For their fruits are their works, by which they lent credence to their words, by which [1221B] they in no wise blushed to give their sufferings to Christ, nay rather consecrated by a most valiant death the truth which they preached. Here thou shalt rightly give the cross to Peter, the blessed prince of the apostles, the sword to Paul, the cauldron of boiling oil to John, the stake to Andrew; to the one James thou shalt assign the sword of Herod, to the other the fuller's pole. And running through the rest of the apostles, and showing all the holy martyrs crowned for Christ by a most valiant death, thou shalt most strongly compass her about with these fruits as she abides in her languor, that she waver not, that she doubt not, but may come from this life which must needs be ended, to the life which is always and without end. There follows:

6. *His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me.*

[1221C]This Bride, having addressed her friends, embraced this in her prayer, that through them she might be stayed up with flowers, and compassed about with apples, that her intent might never be turned away from the love of the Godhead, wherewith she had already begun to languish. And so she gladdens them, as men heard on her behalf, saying: « His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me: » as if she should say, From this I understand that your prayers for me have been heard, in that his left hand is now under my head, and now assuredly I conceive that his right hand shall embrace me. But here it is to be noted that the Lord has alike a left hand and a right; according to what Solomon says of wisdom in the Proverbs: « Length of life is in her right hand, and on the left riches and glory [n: (Prov. III, 16)]. » [var: Vulg. *longitudo dierum*, "length of days"] But that Jesus Christ is [1221D] the head of the Church no one now doubts. For as all the members depend from the head, and that member is without office which does not serve it: so indeed all the faithful have from Christ that they are, that they are faithful, that they are working well in faith, and in good work conceiving hope of eternal life, to which they are already joined by charity. These indeed are the gifts of God: temporal blessedness, and that which is already the angels', and shall be eternal for us: whence the Lord says: « All power is given to me in heaven and in earth. Going therefore, teach ye all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost [n: (Matth. XXVIII, 18, 19)]. »

The senses of the body also are strong chiefly from the head, by which all the members of the body are governed, namely [1222A] sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch: which indeed are acknowledged to be strong in Christ beyond all mortals. For he sees every work that we do, he hears what we speak; what we think, he assuredly smells, the good which we conceive in the mind; and this he tastes which we fulfil in act. But what shall I recount of touch? which indeed is wonderful; for whom he touches, him he straightway changes into himself, changes into another man, so that he himself becomes a stranger to himself. He therefore understands the left hand to be under the head, who knows that riches and all this temporal glory are from him [n: (I Reg. 2, 7)]. For the Lord makes poor and makes rich, he humbles and lifts up, that a man may sit with princes, and hold the throne of glory. For of himself the Lord says: « All power is given to me in heaven and in earth. Going therefore [1222B] teach ye all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost. Amen. » But if not only in heaven, nay rather in earth also, all power has been given to him, it is certain indeed that all riches, and all glory and temporal blessedness, which is known to have an end, is from him; and that also which in heaven has long since been granted to the angels, and is promised to be granted without end to all his own. Whence rightly is the left hand of God declared to be under the head of the Bride, in that it is certain that none obtains this temporal prosperity and glory save by his gift. For in his figure it is said, « By me kings reign, and the founders of laws decree just things: by me princes rule, and the mighty decree justice [n: (Prov. VIII, 15, 16)]. »

[1222C]Because therefore the kings of the world are now in great part subject to Christ (since to him is bowed every knee of kings, of prelates, of the mighty, and of princes), it is rightly said by the Church that the left hand of God rests under « her » head, that is, under the head of the Church; and it is certain that he dispenses all these things to his members as he wills. Yet these things he gives to some who receive the benefits ungratefully, who exalt themselves in what is bestowed, who, having no thanks to God, heap up for themselves wrath in the things that are given, against the day of eternal retribution. But God confers these things on his own faithful, that they may dispense all good things to the good, that they fail not on the way, being shamefully in want; that they may show themselves sons of charity, and may reckon that this grace is not given them for merit; but may rather seek that [1222D] which is to be shown hereafter to his own. Whence also it is subjoined: « And his right hand shall embrace me. » For what is the right hand of God reckoned to be, save the very eternity of God? which indeed embraces the whole Church, glorifying her first of all in the patriarchs and the prophets, crowning her in the apostles and the martyrs, and worthily rewarding also her other members; so that there is no part at all of the Church which may complain that it is untouched by his grace. There follows:

7. *I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes, and the harts of the fields, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, until she herself will.*

Thus far the Bride has drawn out very many words, speaking indeed of the Bridegroom, yet not to him, which is a sign of exceeding love. For love so stirs the mind [1223A] that, if at any time either shame or fear does not permit her to speak to the Bridegroom, yet, kindled by the goads of love, she does not omit to make words concerning him, to herself or to any other whatsoever. Which the beloved himself considering, shows a like token of love, no longer speaking to her, but making words to others concerning her, thus: « I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, that you stir her not up. » He sees her, now in great part of herself languid with love, desiring to rest in his bosom away from the world's disturbances, loathing even necessary conversations, glad in the delight of the Bridegroom alone; and considering also that other part of herself which knows nothing of this languor, bound in great part of itself to the world, which, reckoning her as one dead, desires her to condescend to its own necessity [1223B] and to take up a part of its labour also: pitying her that languishes, he thus says, « I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir her not up, » that is, that you call her not away from her peaceful rest.

But the adjuration must be attended to, wherein he says, « by the roes and the harts of the fields. » For this adjuration is not without cause. These indeed are beasts estranged from the world, set apart from the converse of men, refreshing themselves with herbs, clean animals indeed, which chew the cud and divide the hoof. All these things suit the saints, who, set apart in mind from carnal things, are fed with green herb, that is, with the unfading doctrine of the saints, which they chew, that is, once it is understood they often call it back to memory; and they divide the hoof, because, these things being understood, they separate eternal things from temporal [1223C]. Separately also it belongs to roebucks that they seek the heights, and, when wounded, touch dittany: which is worthily fitted to the saints, namely to seek heavenly things, and, if there be any hurt, to heal it with Christ as physician. This too is the nature of the hart: when he is a hundred years old, or perchance of greater number, and now inclines to old age, he marks for himself in whatever place the serpent's dwelling, and, as nature teaches, straightway draws it forth with his breath, eats it, and consumes it; and having drunk down this supper with a draught from a spring of purer water, he straightway lays aside his horns, and changes his hide and his hair, and recovers his sight, and, reforming his whole self, made young again and now to live long, he passes over into another life. And this is fitted to the saints in this [1223D] way: for the saints live, and, their virtue long kept and now ripened, when they already incline to old age, they mark for themselves in whatever place the dread serpent's dwelling, namely where he reigns, where he dwells, where by his great craft he scatters the hearts of those that dwell there, and severs them from charity: hoping therefore that the consummation of their virtues will be good if they wear down these commotions, if they restore peace, and teach men to keep it when restored; they approach this place of the serpent, and, taught by the breath of the Holy Spirit, they draw forth this ancient serpent, they scatter what he has sown, and, grinding him as it were with their teeth, they eat and consume him, [1224A] and bring his deeds to nothing: and afterwards, by the draught of a clear spring, that is, taught by the graces and the doctrines of our Lord Jesus Christ, they pass over into eternal life: in which, changed into the new man, they live a blessed life, to which nothing is lacking, or nothing can be lacking, that it desires. But such is this adjuration: « I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, that you stir not up, » that is, so may it be granted you to attain the virtues which we signify by the roes and the harts, that you stir not up the beloved, until she herself will.
