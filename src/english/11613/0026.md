## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

Here the times of action and of contemplation are fixed. The times of contemplation indeed are pleasing and acceptable, so that they to whom this grace is given can scarcely be torn away from it. For they love, and [1224B] by loving they understand how many things they lack which they behold in contemplating; these they strive to attain; and they reckon those troublesome to them through whom it comes about that this calling is hindered. But it is the voice of the beloved that is added, « Until she herself will. » Whence it is given to understand that there are certain times in which the Bride may wish to be torn away from this calling, and to be conformed to lower things: as for instance when faith is weakened, when heresy is sown abroad, when it already clings after the manner of a canker, and, unless remedies be applied, it already takes hold of the whole body, and hopes now to possess the whole for itself. Whence Arius says, « There was, when the Son was not, » reckoning that the whole Trinity was not co-eternal with itself: but supposing that divine generation (of which it is said, « Who shall declare his generation?) [n: (Isai. LIII, 8)], » to be like to this [1224C] human generation of ours; in which he who is father goes before, and from him consequently is he who is son. Which heresy assuredly ranged over the whole world, so that scarcely four bishops could be found in the Church who thought rightly concerning the faith of the eternal Trinity. For, to be silent of the other parts of the world, I will disclose how great evils came of this in Africa alone. Huneric the son of Genseric, who on his father's death had succeeded to the kingdom, moved by the Arian perfidy, thrust three hundred and thirty-four catholic bishops into exile, closed their churches, and afflicted the people with divers punishments. And Thrasamund, succeeding him in the kingdom, when the churches of the Catholics were closed, banished two hundred and twenty bishops into exile in Sardinia [1224D]; to whom Pope Symmachus ministered daily supplies. In other parts of the world also this heresy so ranged abroad that it was needful to call together the whole Church, and to come together into one, and to entreat God with poured-out prayers, that, this pest being removed, he would declare the truth of the matter that was in question: and he, inclined by the devout prayers of his beloved and of the other faithful, compelled Arius, when he had come to Constantinople to defend the parts of his heresy and had of necessity turned aside to the public privies, to undergo death, his inmost vitals being poured out; nor did any defender of his heresy appear [1225A] thereafter. So too, when contemplation is penetrating the secret things of heaven and busying itself in them so far as it can, if, teaching having now ceased, either heresy or perchance other sins reign among those in subjection, or if perchance it is desired that succour be brought to the need of neighbours in their distresses: it is the time in which contemplatives should wish to come down, and to succour those who are awaiting help from them. The order of the reading, therefore, is this: This is the voice of the beloved [sic: *moi*], « I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, until she will. » There follows:

8. *Behold, that one cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping over the hills.* [var: Vulg. *iste*, "this one"]

This is the Bride's assertion. For she had said that that voice by which it was said, « I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, [1225B] etc. » was the voice of her beloved: this she now asserts. Seeing her beloved coming, and not gainsaying those things whereof the Bride testified that he was the author, she thus proclaims his coming beforehand, « Behold, that one cometh, leaping upon the mountains, skipping over the hills. » But here a broad reading is opened up, where in Holy Scripture there is treatment of mountains and of hills. For this Scripture reckons some mountains evil, and affirms some worthy of praise: it reckons those evil, as the mountains of Gelboe, where they fell who seemed strong in Israel; which David cursed, saying: « Gelboe, let neither dew nor rain descend upon you [n: (II Reg. I, 21)]. » [var: Vulg. *veniant*, "let there come"] For Gelboe is interpreted « descent, » signifying the mountains of pride and of boasting, whereon, the more thou ascendest, so much the [1225C] more thou fallest and descendest. Whence it is said, « Every mountain and hill shall be brought low [n: (Luc. III, 5)]. » On this indeed there fought the children of Israel, already reprobate before God, Saul and Jonathan, who, being lifted up and reprobated by God, led forth against the enemy the people that followed them, and fell together with those that fell. Whence David says, « Mountains of Gelboe, » that is, of vices, on which the more one ascends, the more one descends; « let neither dew nor rain, » that is, heavenly doctrine, « descend upon you, » where they fell who, hoping themselves strong in Israel, fell conscious to themselves of their weakness. These are evil mountains; David himself recalls that they are to be turned away from, saying, « The mountains, like wax, melted at the presence of the Lord [n: (Psal. XCXVI, 5)]. » [1225D] For they who, being powerful and exalted in the world, prefer themselves to others, and look down on them as though unequal to themselves, these do not behold the Lord coming in wrath and in fire; but as wax before the face of fire, so they melt away and fail. But there is a good mountain of which the same David says, « Selmon, the mountain of God, a fat mountain [n: (Psal. LXVII, 15)]. » For Selmon is interpreted « shadow, » signifying the Lord Jesus Christ, whom the Godhead wholly overshadowed; whence it is subjoined: « The mountain of God, a fat mountain; a curdled mountain, a fat mountain. » For there is none among the sons of men on whom the flood of the Godhead has so wholly bestowed itself, whom the whole fulness of the Godhead has inhabited bodily: whence also it is subjoined, « Why suspect ye curdled mountains? » [n: (Psal. V, 17.)] For there is none among the sons of men, [1226A] in whom the holy Godhead is so curdled together with the humanity that one and the same person is both true God and true man: for this is the mountain in which it hath pleased God to dwell therein: for in it the Lord shall dwell unto the end, which is given to be understood as without end. For is the Godhead to be separated from the humanity? on whom is bestowed all power in heaven and in earth of judging, of taking up, and of taking away. He, as « King of kings, and, Lord of lords [n: (Apoc. XIX, 16)]; » so indeed may be called the mountain of mountains: whence also Isaias says, « In the last days the mountain of the house of the Lord shall be prepared [n: (Isai. II, 2)]. » For this house of the Lord, which God inhabits bodily, is the Lord Jesus Christ; and the mountain of this house is the very loftiness of the Godhead; into which the Lord Jesus himself [1226B] is lifted up above the top of all mountains, to whom all nations shall flow together, and all peoples; whence all the saints receive it that they are called mountains. Whence is that saying, « I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains [n: (Psal. CXX, 1)]: » and elsewhere, « Thou enlightening wonderfully from the everlasting mountains [n: (Psal. LXXV, 5)]. » He indeed by his own fulness enlightens the mountains, and, establishing them in eternity, grants them to others throughout the world, that these may be illumined by their light: seeing therefore her Bridegroom coming, this Bride says, « Behold, he cometh, leaping upon the mountains. » From what mountain does he leap? from the heart of the Father. Into what mountain does he leap? into the heart of the holy Virgin (in whom he put on humanity, fitting to himself a body and a soul, and taking to himself this mortal life), from which he makes a leap onto the cross, whereby he redeemed us: [1226C] his leap from thence is next into the sepulchre; wherein tarrying forty hours, and rising thence, he makes a leap into immortal life; into which, tarrying forty days with the apostles and the rest of his own, he ascended to the place whence he came forth, that he might teach us to ascend thither by the virtues, and to reckon this the end of our course.

The Lord also, in coming, is said to leap upon the mountains. For the mountains are the doctors of the Church, illumined from the everlasting mountains, who by reason of the eminence of their knowledge and understanding are rightly named mountains; upon whom he makes his leaps, when he lights them up with the fire of his flashing. For he made a leap into the apostle Peter, when, the Lord asking, « But whom [1226D] do you say that I am? » [n: (Matth. XVI, 15.)] and the others being silent, he answered, « Thou art Christ, the Son of the living God. » Whence he received the keys of the Church, and the power also of binding and of loosing, and obtained the primacy of the whole Church after Christ. But that he made leaps into him and into the others, when he assured them of his resurrection, when on the day of Pentecost he made them bright with the Holy Ghost, when he made them steadfast and strong under every questioning of them, when at the beautiful gate Peter and John healed by a word alone the man lame from his mother's womb, when by his preaching Peter converted and baptized unto the Lord Jesus now three thousand, and at another time five thousand, is left in doubt to none; since it is certain that these works came about thus not by the power of man alone, but by the true [1227A] presence of God. Since therefore it is certain that he knew the fraud of Ananias and Sapphira, none but God revealing it, and that they also expired at his rebuke; who would not understand that God leapt forth into him, and that he accomplished these things at the command of him who willed them? And when at Joppa in the upper room at the sixth hour Peter was praying, and there fell upon him an ecstasy of mind, so that with heaven opened he saw let down to him from heaven a great vessel, after the manner of a linen sheet, by four corners, in which were all four-footed beasts, and creeping things of the earth, and fowls of the air, and he heard a voice saying to him, « Peter, kill and eat [n: (Act. XI, 6)]; and he said, « Far be it from me, Lord, because I have never eaten anything common and unclean! » and the Lord said to him, « That which God hath purified, call not thou [1227B] common. » Who would not say that the Lord leapt into him, when, the messengers of Cornelius being heard, he came to Caesarea, and finding that God is not a respecter of persons, baptized him and all his house, upon which the Holy Ghost had descended.
