## 10.

*Behold my beloved speaketh to me.*

This Bride has admonished all to beware of her beloved, who cometh leaping upon the mountains, skipping over the hills, who cometh like to a roe and to a young hart, who standeth behind the wall, beholding through the windows and the lattices what is done within, what pleases him, or what displeases him. But now she intimates that the Bridegroom speaks to her, saying, « Behold my beloved speaketh to me. Arise, make haste, my friend, my dove, my beautiful one. » He had seen her sitting in the house as it were on high at a window, cherishing her love toward the Bridegroom by holy contemplation, and pondering the ways in which her love might burn unceasingly toward the Beloved: and that he may stir her up, as one languid and by that very languor as it were dead to the world [1231B], and, once raised up, may kindle her anew with the fires of a new love, he says:

*Arise, make haste, my friend, my dove, my beautiful one, and come.*

Whom he exhorts with three words, saying: « Arise, Make haste, Come; » and whom he designates with three names, saying, « My friend, My dove, My beautiful one; » that the friendly setting of the names may lighten all that the threefold command of the words urges to be done. For he says, « Friend; » for from this I knew thee loving me, that thou hast left father and mother, brothers and sisters, house and fields, and hast followed me. Thee also have I heard saying in Paul, « Who shall separate us from the charity of God, which is in Christ Jesus? For I know that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, [1231C] nor powers, nor any other creature shall be able to separate us from the charity of God [n: (Rom. VIII, 38, 39)]. » Thee also have I heard protesting in Peter, « Lord, thou knowest all things; thou knowest that I love thee [n: (Joan. XXI, 17)]. » He adds also, « My dove, » as a most innocent bird, intent upon the propagating of its young, fed on the cleanness of grains, having a moan instead of a song, flying in flocks, reckoning nothing good save what is in common, tearing at a bird unlike itself neither with beak nor with claw: thee indeed, conforming thyself as far as thou canst to the likeness of that dove which bore witness to Christ in his baptism, and pleasing me in such a form; thou whom by these reasons I justly approve as beautiful, Arise. But in saying, « Arise, » [1231D] he did not utter it in that manner in which it is said to a sinner, Arise. For to a sinner it is said, « Arise, thou that sleepest, and rise up from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten thee [n: (Ephes. V, 14)]. » Which indeed is said to a man wrapped in grievous sins, in which he is said to sleep, that is, to rest in a grievous delight; to whom it is said with a holy voice that he arise, that is, that he rest from sins; and that he rise up from the dead, that is, that he set not for himself a place of abiding in hell, among the dead: and when thou shalt have ceased to sin, and hast begun now to abhor these things which pleased thee, then « Christ shall enlighten thee, » that is, he will show the way by which he wills thee to go. Nor indeed is it in this [1232A] way said here to the Bride that she arise, who long since had left father and mother, house and brothers, and the rest of the things that are had in the world, and, having followed Christ, had said, « Draw me after thee; we will run in the odour of thy ointments. » [var: Vulg. *Trahe me: post te curremus*, "Draw me: we will run after thee"]

What then is it to say to the Bride that she arise? He had known that she was wholly given to this, that she meditated assiduously upon his love, which had drawn him from the Father into the holy Virgin; and that him whom, born of her, it was certain to have run through the evils of the world, and, crucified, to have risen again with death despised, and thereafter to have ascended into the heavens, and to have promised heavenly goods to those that follow him, she embraced with her whole desire, longed for with a vow, and delighted in with soliloquy: to her therefore the Beloved says, « Arise, » which is, Rouse thyself from this [1232B] rest of thine, from this peace of thine: I indeed am he who, adjuring the daughters of Jerusalem, said, « That you stir not up, nor make the Beloved to awake, until she herself will. » Now thou art one spirit with me; I do not doubt that thou willest what I will. Beg not to die, while thou canst profit many, lest this delay draw peril to itself; make haste, that by hastening thou mayest come to me. I am he whom thou lovest; already thou hast without the one whom thou desirest; wherefore I say to thee, that thou come, that thou mayest see with me, and understand thy works, and, if they are good, rejoice together in them.

11. *For now the winter is past.*

Winter is that part of the year in which the light is shorter for us, but the darkness of the night deeper; in which all things are bound fast with frost, in which all things are stiff with wintry ice, and moisture has dominion over bodies: all these things [1232C] mark the inclemency of the season, and he testifies that it has now passed away, and that therefore the time of his coming is opportune. Which indeed was present then, when unbelief had oppressed the world on every side, when the light of faith shone upon the hearts of few, when evil had come forth from the north wind, which held in the hearts of all, chilled with the frost of malice; and hardened impiety permitted the ice of hearts to be melted by no warmth of the Holy Spirit. Moisture had dominion in bodies, in which no love of chastity or of honour restrained the reins of luxury: but luxury, and the importunity that accompanies luxury, commanded that all things be mingled in foul intercourse. This the disobedience of Adam the first father bears witness to; the malice of Cain also bears witness; and Lamech « the murderer » [1232D] bears witness; this also the impurity of the ages bears witness to, blotted out first by water, afterwards by fire, eight excepted. There follows:

*The rain is gone and departed.*

Not every rain is called *imber*, but only the wintry; joining almost day and night together, which not even roofs keep out; by which rivers are swollen, by which bridges fall down, and the rising fords deny the way to travellers, and, snatching all things away by their onrush, bear them down into the deep. Which indeed agrees with the time of unbelief: for where it happened that faith was lacking, there it came about that all spiritual goods were lacking [1233A]: there was no hope there, no charity, no fear of God, no obedience to God, no reverence, and almost all that mankind owed to God, it paid to Satan. This rain was unceasing: the unspeakable teaching of the masters, magnifying Jupiter, offering him sacrifices, venerating Apollo, Mercury, and Bacchus, adoring Juno, Diana, Venus, and Pallas, and the rest, which shame blushes even to have named in a sacred treatise, and modesty forbids: but the necessity alone of expounding the sacred reading sets them forth. For this rain overthrew the foundation of faith, the walls of hope, and the roofs of charity: for when the rain descended upon this house, the floods came also together with it; for this unbelief being received, the flooded minds of men swelled beyond the channels appointed for them; [1233B] and they to whom the faith, worship, and reverence of the one God was given by God the Father, and by the fathers who gave him credence, attributing this to many gods, and confessing that there are many Gods, went forth from the channel and the banks appointed them, and drew with them into the deep of hell countless thousands of men. For the winds blew, because the blasts of the malignant spirit were not wanting to them. The bridges were overthrown, because Holy Scripture, which makes for us a bridge to God, became cheap. No passage lay open to the traveller, because for him going forth from this life there was no access to God. For there was made, as the Lord says, « a great fall of that house; » and truly great, inasmuch as for it there is no return to life, no communion with the good to come for ever. Thee therefore, whom I perceive to be my friend, because thou willest [1233C] what I will; thee, whom I understand to excel in dove-like manners, whom I judge conformed to me, and therefore beautiful, thee I call forth, saying to thee, « Arise, » that is, Come forth from this contemplation of peace and quiet wherein thou art held; make haste, that the compliance of thy obedience may follow at a word upon the command of my precept, « and come, » that, made a partaker of one spirit with me, thou mayest rejoice together with me, who helped thee by my spirit that thou shouldest do these things, that thou shouldest remove these scandals of the world: for the winter being removed, and the rain removed which had troubled all things, a sweeter breath has now blown over the face of the earth, and the breeze of the Holy Spirit, sent forth from above, has adorned it, when faith was received, with the flower of its own comeliness: this is what is said:

12. *The flowers have appeared in our land.* [1233D]

Where unbelief commanded, there faith now reigns; where there was despairing misery, there good hope hopes for itself, and rejoices to attain eternal things; where the mind was cold and grew numb, kindled by no fire of charity, there charity now orders all things by its own command, raising upward the things that were below, and by good works presenting all things to the good creator. He added also:

*The time of pruning is come.*

Which pruning assuredly shows forth the time of holy confession. For trees are pruned, and from them the useless shoots are taken away, which bear no fruits, and which, if they grow out, hinder the useful branches from bearing: so vines, [1234A] so other trees are cut free of useless branches, that those which are useful may become more useful, and may bring forth richer fruits for the husbandman. Which indeed is rightly fitted to the time of fulness and of grace. For some trees are dry and dead, bringing forth neither leaves nor fruits; some indeed have leaves, but not fruits; others indeed fruits, but bad ones: and others there are to which the leaves lend beauty, and the best fruits their worth. In these the whole race of men is comprehended: of these indeed the axe and the fire await three kinds. But those whom neither the leaves of words nor the fruits of works clothe, or who, if they bring forth any fruits, yet bring forth bad ones, these the judicial axe of the Lord, discerning well, cuts off, and by a just judgment [1234B] fits them to the fire that is appointed for them. But those to which the leaves lend beauty, and the best fruits their worth, are the trees of Paradise, in which God dwells, among which he walks, unto which he calls forth his Bride, and which he cleanses with the pruning-hook, that they may bring forth fruits; which fruits may remain for them unto life eternal. Which pruning-hook indeed is holy confession, wherein a man considers himself, calls to mind all that he has done in his life, opens and condemns himself to himself; and, confessing this very thing with his mouth to him to whom he is appointed, repenting with his heart, washing and cleansing it with tears, taking up and rendering a worthy burden of penance, he is made a fruitful tree, which is planted beside the running waters, and, bringing forth the fruit of grace, is most joyfully received by the Lord. The time of pruning also is most pleasing to the Lord, [1234C] wherein he sees the glory of the apostles, the constancy of all the martyrs throughout the world, who indeed were cut off by the heavy pruning-hook of martyrdom, and cast down as useless, and, received gloriously by him in heaven as members of the most high body of Christ, do now judge the nations, and reign for ever. There follows:

*The voice of the turtle has been heard in our land.*
