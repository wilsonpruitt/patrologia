## 10. (cont.)

This voice is the voice of the Bride, who, being called forth, has now come to the Bridegroom; with whom the Lord mingles conversations, mingles (that I may so speak) soliloquies; for to the Father alone, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, are these things known which are spoken, which are revealed to this Bride: whence she herself also says, « My Beloved to me » as though he speaks to me; « and I to him » as though I answer words, but to you I reveal not what these secrets are. God keeps each of his secrets, [1241D] lest we attempt to enter in, nor let us presume to open what he reveals to the Bride alone; these things we pass over; to him alone to whom they have been committed we commit them, since he has not willed that they be opened to us. There follows:

*Who feedeth among the lilies. 17. Until the day breathe, and the shadows decline.*

These things being passed over, these being committed to silence, which God speaks to the Bride alone, the Bride adds what the Bridegroom does in the heights, wherein he is gladdened, wherein he is delighted, wherein he is refreshed, what pasture is held more pleasing to him, saying, « Who feedeth among the lilies. » But these lilies, sprung up in the heavenly gardens and courts, are so much the mightier in comeliness, in odour, and in sweetness, as he by whom they were planted is the more delighted to be present among them [1242A] and to be refreshed by the sight and the odour of them. These indeed are the heavenly virtues; their comeliness and heavenly fragrance is wonderful; so that he is said to be fed by this, who alone creates, alone orders, alone feeds all things which he creates, and sustains them. The Beloved is said to be fed among these, that they may study to be conformed to them who, living on earth, already desire heavenly things, that by the keeping of integrity they may bear up the palm of chastity even unto the converse of angels; and that the rest also may stand forth strangers to vices, claiming nothing of this world for themselves, that lust may not inflame them, that avarice may not goad them, that wantonness may not unman them, that ambition may not lay them low, that envy may not waste them, that the secular care of business may not make them anxious, « Until the day breathe, and the shadows decline. » It has been said that the Beloved is fed [1242B] among the lilies; but now it must be said how long he will abide among the same, others not being joined to him for the feeding; for this is what is said, « He is fed among the lilies; » and among these same, others not being joined to him, he shall be fed until that day of judgment breathe, in which God shall manifestly shew himself, rendering to every one according to his works; to the good, good things without end, to the evil, evil things indeed that shall abide without end. Two days indeed of the Lord are rightly named: one, in which he was judged, and of him it was said, « Not this man, but Barabbas [n: (Joan. XVIII, 40)]: » and likewise of him it was said, that « he be crucified; his blood be upon us, and upon our children [n: (Matth. XXVII, 23, 25)]. » The other, in which he shall judge the whole world raised up from death, « And he shall judge all men in equity, and the peoples [1242C] in his truth; » the day in which « the light of the moon shall be as the light of the sun, and the light of the sun shall be sevenfold, as the light of seven days [n: (Isa. XXX, 26)]. » This also is rightly called the day of the Lord, in which he is to shew himself the true Lord, and to render to them all things which they have deserved. When therefore this day shall have breathed, that is, shall have begun to appear, then shall he join the lilies gathered from the earth to those which have sprung up in the heavenly gardens; then shall there be made one Church of men and of angels, when men shall now not marry, nor give in marriage [var: Vulg. *neque nubentur*, "nor be given in marriage," passive], but shall be as the angels of God [n: (Matth. XXII, 30)]: then shall he be fed at once among the heavenly lilies, and among those which have been taken from the earth, and made equal to the heavenly, and, by virtue working through grace, made [1242D] conformable to their glory: then shall the shadows decline. The whole time which we here pass we run through under a certain alternation, now measuring the day and the hours of the day, now reckoning up the night and its hours as many: but this night the upraised shadows of the earth, and of the bodies which are in it, bring to pass: for when the sun moves below, the upper parts of the world are darkened, and the earth; and as the multitude of bodies which are in it casts its shadow upward, they are drawn over with the cloak of the night of the earth. But when this day shall have breathed, the sun abiding perpetually in it shall illuminate it, and the saints shall shine with clear light in the sight of the Most High, nor shall they feel this alternation of the oncoming night; but all those shadows which make this night, declining into the lower parts of the world, shall descend, and [1243A] shall make that dark night, now to abide perpetually, meet indeed for the damned and wretched. There follows:

*Return: be like, my beloved, to a roe, and to a young hart upon the mountains of Bether.*

The Bride, long since called forth, stood by the Bridegroom who called her, saw him feeding among the heavenly lilies, and, longing for that very thing for herself and for hers, deserved to hear that she should wait, « until the day breathe and the shadows decline: » then shall the lilies sprung from the earth be gathered together, and, joined and made like to the lilies above, shall be endowed with this gift, that the Bridegroom may be fed among them, and be delighted for ever, and that they themselves may sing that word of the apostle, « I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course, I have kept the faith [n: (II Tim. IV, 7)]; there is laid up for us [1243B] this crown of justice, which behold the just judge shall render to us. Whence she suppliantly prays that the Bridegroom may return, and visit, and cherish the Church which labours on the earth; that he himself may take up her labours, himself advance them, and himself lead them through even unto perfection, saying: « Return, my beloved. » There follows, « Be like to a roe, » that is, thy incarnation, whereby thou art like to a roe, that is, seeing all things clearly; and whereby thou art likened to a young hart, that is, art reckoned to have been brought forth to the world wondrously from the ancient fathers as a young hart: this present to her, this call back into her memory, that she may praise thee, adore thee, glorify thee above all the mountains of Bether, reckoning none equal to thee in the Church, but understanding thee to be far more sublime and far more holy than all the mountains which are in Bether, which is, « The house of God, » or, « The house [1243C] rising up, » or, « The house of the watch, » all which is the Church.
