## CHAPTER III.

1. *In my little bed through the nights I sought him whom my soul loves; I sought him, and found him not.*

This Bride, or any faithful soul whatsoever, has entreated. Her Beloved, that, not deserting heaven, he may nevertheless look upon the Church labouring on the earth, and, taking pity, condescend to her labour; now already, that mercy may the more easily move him, and make him to be inclined to the things which are asked, she adds the words of the same wherewith she supplicates: « In my little bed through the nights I sought him whom [1243D] my soul loves. » But it is to be noted in this place, that Solomon, a festive king, and opulent in all things, given also to delights (as is the custom with most kings), makes known this his opulence to the world, and writing says: « There are sixty queens, and eighty concubines, and of young maidens there is no number [n: (cap. VI, 7)]. » But where there are so many queens, so many royal concubines, and the number of the young maidens who are nourished for the Bridegroom is not known, there [sic: *perfecto*] luxury abounds, wherewith one may gladden a taste that eats daintily and disdains common things; there are stipends, whereby their adornment may not displease the King at his coming, and all other things which may draw and allure him at his coming [1244A] to love. Which things, whosoever thou art that shalt hear them, it is not good that thou be broken in mind, that thou relax thy mind to soft things, nor that thou take delight in base things, nor that thou entangle thy mind in these things which are said, but rather that through these things which are said bodily thou understand spiritual things, as is the custom of the Scriptures, and that thou rather raise up and kindle thy love in those things which are spiritual. For this festive king Solomon is that great King, who is the Father, who is the Son, who is also the Holy Ghost, who, while the ages run their course, joins to himself one Church in three orders; of which he designates that part, « the first and chief, » by the name of queens, the second by that of concubines, the third by that of young maidens. And indeed they are designated by the name of queens who have long since run together, who [1244B] have ascended after him that draws, and have deserved to lay hold of him that draws; so that with the Apostle they may say: « For we know that neither death, nor life, nor any other creature shall be able to separate us from the charity of God, which is in Christ Jesus, our Lord [n: (Rom. VIII, 38)]. » [var: Vulg. *Certus sum enim*, "For I am sure"] Consider in this order Elias, consider Eliseus also; of whom the one was taken up to God in a fiery chariot and with fiery horses; the other, going up from Jericho to Bethel, having received the mockery of the boys of that place saying [n: (III Reg. II, 11)], « Go up, thou bald head, go up, thou bald head, cursed them in the name of the Lord. And there came forth immediately two bears out of the forest, and tore of them two and forty. But he went up from thence to mount Carmel [n: (IV Reg. II, 23, 24)]. » These two therefore, and very many others of the Old Testament, [1244C] thou wilt worthily attribute to this order, who, knowing nothing but God, and foreknowing that he should be born of a virgin, truly ran after him, and assuredly laid hold of him, and, being made heirs of his kingdom, glory with him for ever. But what is to be thought of those whom the page of the Gospel commemorates, who saw God present in the flesh, knew him from his words and deeds, and, following him alone, said: « Behold we have left all things, and have followed thee: what therefore shall we have? » [n: (Matth. XIX, 27.)] What of Peter? who, when the Lord said, « But whom do you say that I am? » [n: (Matth. XVI, 15, 16)] confidently answered: « Thou art Christ, the Son of the living God; » whom indeed the Lord declares blessed, because he answered that this was revealed to him not by [1244D] flesh and blood, but by his Father who is in heaven; whom he distinguishes as blessed in six clauses, saying: « Thou art Peter [n: (ibid., 18)], » that is, in the faith of me firm as a rock; and so named by me Peter, as it were a rock. « And upon this rock, » which I understand to be myself, and appoint to be in thee, « I will build my Church, » that is, I am about to give strength to the Church, and I will build so firmly that « the gates of hell shall not prevail against it, » that is, the malignant spirits, and the evil rich men who follow them, shall not hurt it. « And I will give to thee the keys of the kingdom of heaven [n: (ibid., 19)], that is, that none may enter into the kingdom of heaven, save through the faith and the sacraments [1245A] which have been committed to thee from me. And he shall be accursed, that is, the faith or the sacraments received shall not profit him; whom thou shalt have cursed; as those things shall be safe for him whom thou shalt have blessed.

What is to be said of John the evangelist? who says, « The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us: and we saw his glory, the glory as it were of the Only-begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth? » [n: (Joan. I, 14.)] It is indeed to be said, that both these, and all the apostles, and all who are made like to these not indeed in dignity, but in charity and in works, these are they whose souls, received into heaven and honoured, are of such dignity that they deserve to be called queens by the King himself; in whom he so glories that he says, « There are sixty queens; » whom assuredly, how many soever [1245B] they be, he comprehends under the number of sixty, calling them sixty. The number sixty indeed consists of two perfect numbers multiplied into each other; for whether thou say six times ten, or ten times six, thou wilt make one number, namely sixty, and thou wilt assign these two numbers on either side, the ten of the law, the six of the Gospel. These therefore who are perfect in both, by fulfilling the law and the Gospel, are rightly applied to the number sixty.

These souls therefore are rightly called queens, who, joined to Christ by the right law of matrimony, have clung to him with the inmost affection of charity, and have brought forth sons by their example, justly heirs of the eternal kingdom; of whom it is said, « A chosen generation, a holy nation, a purchased people [n: (I Petr. II, 9)]. » Truly blessed are they who [1245C] have deserved to be made partakers of the heavenly virtues, and to enjoy that glory of charity which knows no failing. But in the second place are numbered those souls which are called concubines; for, as the Apostle says, « God indeed hath set some in the Church, first apostles, secondly prophets, thirdly doctors, after that miracles, then the graces of healings, helps, governments, kinds of tongues, interpretations of speeches [n: (I Cor. XII, 28)]. » In these perhaps thou wilt find a place for the concubines, which indeed are those souls which, though they be joined to God by some fellowship, have not cleaved to him with the whole heart, not with the whole mind, not with all their strength, nor have been made equal to the apostles; for, as the Apostle says, not all are appointed apostles, not all prophets. For to some [1245D] perfect grace is given, and the Lord knows who they are; to some imperfect, or perfect in one thing, not perfect in others; and these indeed beget sons from themselves, not as though begotten in matrimony, or heirs of the kingdom; but such as the Lord succours through his mercy, that they may be received in his kingdom and nourished, instructed, and saved. « But of young maidens there is no number; » for the young maidens are souls lately converted to the faith, or at least brought to conversion after very many sins committed: concerning whom it is not known what their consummation shall be; whom assuredly he regards, and, as they are the gifts of his mercy, in the manner which he determines, draws to himself. These therefore are the distinctions of the Church, which [1246A] Solomon distinguishes in what follows, saying, « There are sixty queens, » etc., which we have touched upon for the present for this cause, that we might teach that this Bride, called forth by the Bridegroom, and addressed with heavenly words, and taught by soliloquies, is that one who was endowed by Solomon with the name of queen, when it was said, « There are sixty queens: » but that she who remains on the earth, and is designated by the name both of concubines and of young maidens, is indeed that one for whom the Church above prays, saying, « Return, my beloved [n: (cap. II, 17)]. » To whose prayer, that the majesty of the beloved may the more easily be inclined, and may hear her voice, the words which she pours forth weeping are joined: « In my little bed through the nights I sought him whom my soul loves. » Our little bed indeed is our conscience, [1246B] in which we rest, if we present good things to ourselves; if evil things and the worst meet us, we are indeed straitened and afflicted. For him whose own heart reproves him, and who confesses that he has sinned grievously against the Lord, and has borne himself ill toward his neighbours, for him there is no rest, but labour, and affliction of spirit, and a broad confusion of grief: whence the Prophet says, « I have laboured in my groaning, I will wash my bed through each several night [n: (Psal. VI, 7)]. » He understands indeed the labour of conscience, which he shews both to groan, and to labour, and to wash the bed through each several night; which indeed then washes the bed, when in repenting it grieves and groans that it has offended God: and it washes it through each several night, while through each several sin it bewails that it has incurred the wrath of God: poured out in tears, when [1246C] it redoubles that it has watered with tears its own body, whereon it laid itself low by sinning, saying, « I have watered my couch with my tears; » because the eye of my mind is troubled with fury, that is, with the manifest wrath of the Lord, by the presence of mine enemies, that is, of my sins, among which, from the very first beginning of sinning, I have grown inveterate and been made old, and have been well-nigh reprobated from the very grace of the Lord; for Isaias says, « The bed is straitened, so that one must fall out; and a short cloak cannot cover both [n: (Isa. XXVIII, 20)]. » For the human mind is narrow and strait, so that if an evil spirit occupy it, the Spirit of God forsakes it; and when the Spirit of God has occupied it, this working, and free will judging better, [1246D] that malignant spirit shall justly and utterly depart, because the « cloak, » that is, the measure and brevity of our creation, « both, » the Spirit of God and the malignant one, is not able to « cover, » that is, to comprehend. Since therefore she seeks God through each several night, and it has been said to her by the Lord, « Ask, and you shall receive [var: Vulg. *et dabitur vobis*, "and it shall be given to you"]; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened to you [n: (Matth. VII, 7)]; » what is it that she says, « I sought, and found him not? » This indeed is what we have said, that she has not yet ascended to the higher things, has not yet attained that dignity wherewith the queens were endowed, to whose petition nothing is denied, to whose seeking nothing is closed, to whose knocking it is opened the more readily: as yet she acts among the lowest things; whence she says:

2. *I will rise, and will go about the city: by the streets and the broad ways [1247A] I will seek him whom my soul loves: I sought him, and found him not.*
