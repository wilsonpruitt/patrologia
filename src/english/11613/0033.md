## CHAPTER III. (cont.)

« I will rise, » that is, from this burden of my sin, and at the same time I will shake off my filth; « And I will go about the city, » this great Church indeed, which now is common to the good and to the evil, that if I shall see in it any counsel, and any help that may profit, I may follow it out. And that she may shew more closely what she is about to do, she adds, « By the streets and the broad ways, » that is, by those bound to an order, and the married, or the free who remain without a spouse: through these « I will seek him whom my soul loveth. » But although she is querulous, and zealous in seeking, yet she adds, « I sought him, and found him not. » What then?

3. *The watchmen who keep the city found me.*

[1247B]Who are these watchmen? Angels indeed, the guardians of the Church, to whom this guardianship has been committed, that they may know and understand what good, what evil any one has done, and what he has deserved to have rendered to him: of whom the Lord says in the Gospel, « See that you despise not one of these little ones; for I say to you, that their angels in heaven always see the face of my Father who is in heaven [n: (Matth. X, 18)]; » whom she addresses, saying:

*Have you seen him whom my soul loveth?*

These indeed she addresses, from whom she does not openly receive an answer. For when she has twice surveyed the whole Church, and in both places has said that she has not found her Beloved, what the cause may be why she has not found him, [1247C] she is to hear secretly from the watchmen. For she said, « In my little bed through the nights I sought him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, and found him not; » which is that she has brought back to memory in her conscience the « nights, » that is, the dark deeds wherewith she had offended God, and has said nothing further, nor done anything: which indeed is not enough with God for the remission of sin, to turn over in conscience alone that very thing which thou hast done, unless thou study humbly to amend by heart, by mouth and by work that which thou hast done. Thou amendest by heart, while thou repentest; by mouth, while thou confessest to him to whom thou art assigned; but by work, while by fasting, discipline, prayer, alms, watchings and teaching thou desirest to restore what was ill done. For blessed James says, « Confess therefore your sins one to another, and [1247D] pray one for another, that you may be saved [n: (Jac. V, 16)]. » This law of confession the blessed watchmen teach her, whom she both obeys, and she receives the said law of confession, repenting in heart, confessing with mouth, desiring to fulfil in work what is enjoined. There follows:

4. *When I had a little passed by them, I found him whom my soul loveth; I held him, and I will not let him go, till I bring him into my mother's house, and into the chamber of her that bore me*

Behold how much this threefold confession profits: for she, having confessed her excesses by heart, and by mouth, and by work, when she had now passed by the « watchmen, » that is, the angels who guard the Church, in the Spirit, found him whom her soul loveth, God, namely, whom she acknowledged [1248A] as her propitiator, by whom she understands that her sins have been remitted to her, and all things cast out from the eternal memory. Whence she says, « I held him, » that is, I clave to him with the firm root of charity, not about to let him go, « till I lead him into my mother's house, and into the chamber of her that bore me; » that is, till I convert to him the Synagogue, which is my mother, and the gentile world, which is she that bore me. For this holy Church, gathered from the whole world, had Judaea for her mother in faith, from whom first she had the worship of the one God, rejected idols, offered to him alone the victims of sacrifice and of holocaust, and obtained very many precepts of the law: but she had the gentile world for her that bore her unto the faith of Christ, since Judaea rejected it, and by her teaching she received fully his faith and all his sacraments. Him therefore she will not [1248B] let go, because she will not be silent, she will not cease from preaching, not from the testifying of the things which Christ taught, until both the gentile world and all Judaea receive him; and, both she, and that one who has been called forth on high and honoured as much by the name as by the dignity of a queen, bringing this to pass, the truth of Christ may be fully made known to the world. There follows.

5. *I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, until she herself will.*

Where that which was set down is repeated, there is rightly a calling to mind of that which was said. The Bridegroom said above, « His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me [n: (cap. II, 6)]. » Which indeed stood forth pleasing to the Bridegroom; for it proves the faith whereby [1248C] she subjects all temporal goods to the power of the Lord Jesus Christ: it proves also the hope whereby she sets forth that she is to be embraced in eternity by the right hand of God. Here also, that it is said that she is not to let him go, until by the preaching of both he be perfectly brought into the world, is gratefully received by him; and therefore it is enjoined to the daughters of Jerusalem, under the same adjuration, that they presume not to hinder it: « I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, until she herself will. » Of which, since what the Lord has given has been said, we pass on to the rest.

6. *Who is she that goeth up by the desert?*

This Bride, magnified by the presence of God, binds herself by promises that she will not rest, that is, that she will not desist from the preaching of Christ, until the whole [1248D] world acknowledge him, and be made subject to him. But the daughters of Sion, discerning her fixed in the fulfilling of this promise, and converting now these, now others to the faith, and ascending with them to things above, marvelling at her providence and her perseverance, say, « Who is she? » that is, of what sort and how great is she! and how most worthy of wonder, « who thus goeth up by the desert? » The desert is a place which all judge worthy to be deserted, which by this judgment none inhabit; the briars and thorns which it produces they abhor and fear; the venomous things which it brings forth, these they vehemently flee. By which name indeed this world is understood, which brings forth the thorns of sins, receives the venoms of unclean spirits, and sends those who receive [1249A] these things to the caverns of hell. « Who » therefore « is she! » that is, of what sort, and how great, and how most worthy of wonder, who through this world, subject to infinite temptations, and subjecting to itself with innumerable temptations those who give it their labour, and turning them back, sets for herself the steps of her ascent, and seeks to ascend from thence whence we know that so many thousands have hitherto descended; and seeks to acquire rewards for herself from thence, whence we have seen so many thousands of men incur for themselves the punishments of hell. « Who » therefore « is this she, » who treads down the glory of kings, and dreads not the threats of judges, fears not reproaches, despises flatteries, laughs at torments if they be applied, joyfully receives the penalty of death, and joyfully, accompanied by the virtues, transcends this world? Great indeed is this she, [1249B] who, if she is pressed by adverse things, is not broken; if she receives joyful things, is by no means lifted up; but shewing herself always the same in either case, purposes through this desert to flee, to ascend, and to draw near to God, whence also it is subjoined:

*As a little rod of smoke of the aromatical spices of myrrh, and of frankincense, and of all the powder of the perfumer.*

Smoke at the same time partly rises, and partly, that which had risen before, is wont to disappear in the higher regions: so the Church in certain of her members is ever begotten anew, in certain of them, who had been born before, is ever gathered to heavenly things. For smoke born of fire, mounting on high, withdraws itself little by little from human sight: so the Church, kindled by fire, namely of the Holy Spirit, unto the love of God, [1249C] ceases not to ascend, until, drawn away from earthly things, she be caught up to the invisible things of heaven: who is rightly called not a rod, but a « little rod, » because this is the manner of the ascent, that the ardour of compunction is sometimes so subtle that neither does he himself comprehend it who has deserved to have it, nor does he himself discern it to whom it has been given. Whence here not a rod, but a « little rod, » is rightly said, because there is understood a compunction of heart straight and attenuated, and nimble also, inasmuch as it is known to have arisen from the aromatical spices of myrrh and frankincense. Myrrh indeed defends bodies from putrefaction; but frankincense, when kindled, gives forth fragrance: whence by myrrh is understood the mortification of the flesh; by frankincense, the cleanness of prayer. The Church indeed mortifies the flesh, while she denies pleasures, and while she sends forth to God the fervour of prayer, she draws out as it were the smoke [1249D] of spices from the censer, that is, from her own heart, that she may smell sweetly to God, and kindle her neighbour. And lest she should present mortification only, and prayer, and draw near destitute of the other virtues, it is added, « And of all the powder of the perfumer, » which is: She herself is the little rod of smoke, arisen not only from myrrh and frankincense, but rather one which takes its substance from the powder which is pounded and mingled by the perfumer for the making of good perfumes, and is presented to God for glory. But in commemorating these spices, she sets first myrrh, then frankincense, afterwards all the powder of the perfumer, because it is first to mortify the concupiscence of the flesh, afterwards to offer to God the acceptable vows of the heart, [1250A] and so with humility to grow in every kind of virtue, whereby she may be able to please God. There follows:

7. *Behold sixty valiant ones of the most valiant of Israel surround the little bed of Solomon.*

The daughters of Jerusalem have already declared this Church to be wonderful in comeliness and in the marvellous exaltation of her work, whom they now proceed to set forth, shewing and magnifying the beatitude to which she tends, to which she ascends, whither she is drawn, whither, reduced into the powder of the perfumer, she shall now presently be seen worthy to be set, to be raised on high, and to be exalted: which beatitude assuredly he shews, designating the little bed of Solomon, and his litter, and the diadem of glory, wherewith on the day of his espousal he was held worthy to be exalted above all [1250B]. And of the little bed indeed it is thus said, « Behold sixty valiant ones surround the little bed of Solomon. »
