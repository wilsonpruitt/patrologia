## CHAPTER III. (cont.)

But it is certain that by Solomon, who is interpreted *peaceable*, Christ is signified, who pacified all things by his own blood, and brought back into one commonwealth the things which are in heaven, and those which he redeemed from the earth. But his little bed is the beatitude in which the King rests with the saints, to which the Church daily ascends through the desert; with how great adornments it is spread, with how great ornament it shines, flowing with how great abundance of delights, and overflowing with how great abundance of charity, they know whom that same little bed already receives, whom the Lord has already endowed with that same beatitude. This indeed « sixty valiant ones surround; » for that the chastity of this little bed may be kept inviolate, [1250C] sixty valiant ones « surround » it, that is, guard it. But in the number sixty are contained the preachers of the word, who, taught by the law and by the Gospel, instruct the good also with knowledge, and lead them through even unto perfection. For the number six is attributed to the Gospel; for by this number the Lord measures out the good things of the good, and the evil things of the evil, saying: « I was hungry, and you gave me to eat: I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink: I was a stranger, and you took me in: naked, and you covered me; sick, and you visited me; I was in prison, and you came to me [n: (Matth. XXV, 35, 36)]. » This therefore is rightly attributed to the Gospel, whereby, if ten be multiplied six times, sixty will be produced. These guardians of the little bed are therefore rightly reckoned sixty, who, [1250D] whithersoever they be carried in word, pass not beyond the Gospel, and depart not from the law. But these are named « valiant, » for fortitude is that virtue which alone defends the virtues, keeps judgments, unconquered in labours, valiant in battles, neglects money, flees the avarice which unmans virtue (for nothing is so contrary to fortitude as to be overcome by gain), is not tempted by covetousness, is not broken by fear, because virtue is consistent with itself, so that it pursues vices as the poisons of virtue; which, that he may not express it in a middling way, he subjoins, « Of the most valiant of Israel; » for they are most valiant who without weakness of desire contemplate eternal things, conquer the flesh, gainsay pleasures, bear the hard things of the world, despise blandishments for the sake of eternal things, overcome adversities, look upon their end, [1251A] labour without, that they may rejoice within. There follows:

8. *All holding swords.*

It has been said who they are that guard; but now it must be said in what manner, being fortified, they guard this little bed, « holding » indeed « swords. » Here the armour of the doctors is expressed, which is more fully shewn by the Apostle: for blessed Paul says: « Stand therefore, having your loins girt about with truth, and having on the breastplate of justice, and your feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace: in all things taking the shield of faith . . . and take unto you the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God [n: (Ephes. VI, 14-17)]. This indeed is the armour of the doctors, which is designated in this place by the sword, which is the principal part [1251B] of the same armour. For the sword cutting on both sides is the word of God, dividing bodily things and spiritual, shaking the shadow away from the truth, « For the word of God is living, and more piercing than any two-edged sword, reaching unto the division of the soul and the spirit, of the joints also and the marrow, a discerner of the thoughts and intents of the heart. » This the valiant hold in their hands, practising in work that which they see in meditation. For they reckon it not enough to know and to understand, unless they fulfil in work that which they have understood: for we discern many who admonish well and live ill, who fear not to do that which they detest in word, and are understood to practise with their hands that which they « weave together » in word. So did the Scribes and Pharisees teach, [1251C] to whose words the Lord bids us give assent, but to turn aside from their works, and he charges his own to teach otherwise, saying, « So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven [n: (Matth. V, 16)]. » By these therefore are swords held in their hands, who confirm their doctrine by pious works, and confidently teach what they themselves fulfil in work. There follows:

*And most learned in wars.*

For he is learned in wars who resists sin by work; more learned in wars, who resists it both by work and by the word of teaching; but most learned in wars, who resists it by word and by work, and by the zeal of discipline applied. For he delivers the innocent to destruction who frees from punishment him in whom the desire of robbing still breathes; and [1251D] he plots the destruction of many who spares the evil by granting pardon; for easiness of pardon is an incitement to fault. There follows:

*Every man's sword upon his thigh.*

The very impossibility of warring restrains many from warring, as if they have a sword, and hold it in their hands, and cannot draw it, or can do so only with difficulty: the enemy therefore is not slain, and he escapes unpunished from him at whose hands he has deserved punishment. Therefore it is subjoined, « Every man's sword upon his thigh, » that both the ease of drawing it and the opportunity of quickly striking the enemy may help him. For the sword is upon the thigh, when by preaching one's own flesh is tamed, lest, when he has preached to others, he himself be made a castaway. There follows:

[1252A] *Because of fears in the night.*

The life which is lived here is passed through days and nights: of the works which are done here, some are attributed to the day, some to the night; to the day, those which are of the light, to the night, those which are of the darkness. But the life which we await shall be wholly in the day, wholly in the light: there shall then be no participation of justice with iniquity, no fellowship of light with darkness, no agreement of Christ with Belial. Belial with his own shall wholly bristle with darkness, and thence no going forth, in whatsoever manner, to tempt the faithful shall now lie open to him by any reason; who, because he is not yet bound by this strictness of judgment, now goes forth; now, while there is time, he busies himself and strives to bring in by all means the work of the night and of the darkness. But these valiant ones drive him back, lest perchance the snares of the tempter lay these low, [1252B] secure and unarmed, and evilly snatch away anything from the guardianship committed to them. There follows:

9. *King Solomon made himself a litter of the wood of Lebanon. 10. He made the pillars thereof of silver, the reclining-place of gold, the going up of purple: the middle parts he paved with charity because of the daughters of Jerusalem.*

After the description of the little bed, the description of the litter is rightly subjoined. For the King, rising from the bed, takes himself to the litter, that he may be borne from place to place, according as he shall see the business of each to require. He says therefore, « King Solomon made a litter of the wood of Lebanon. » But Lebanon is interpreted *a whitening*; and the woods of Lebanon are cedars and cypresses; which indeed are straight and upright, putting forth their foliage on high, unfading, [1252C] incorruptible, sending forth also from themselves a most excellent odour: of these the King made himself a litter, wherein he might be carried about, whereto business should require him to be carried about. These woods indeed designate the ranks of the saints, which are straight, tending straight to God in meditation and in action; and incorruptible, because no corruption touches these when they are received into eternity, no suffering of death, or of grief, or of any trouble whatsoever affects them. Among these number the blessed apostles, Peter and Paul, and the rest raised on high with apostolic dignity, who bear the Lord through the world, who testify and announce to the world his deeds, his words and his promises. The same also he reckons pillars of silver, whom God gives as preachers to the Church, strengthened with the rectitude of justice, that they may sustain the Church by their examples, [1252D] and adorn her with the brightness of eloquence, as with the splendour of silver. He made also in this a reclining-place of gold, which indeed are the illustrious minds of the faithful hearers, in whom, while he shewed beforehand the heavenly joy, he composed as it were a reclining-place out of the gold of wisdom; for Wisdom is « more precious than all riches, and all the things that are desired are not to be compared with her [n: (Prov. III, 15)]. » But because to this one comes by much tribulation, and ascends by the shedding of blood, it is added: « He made the going up of purple; » for thither each one is borne up by martyrdom, which is designated by the purple; or assuredly by the passion of Christ, which is more precious than purple, through which there is an entrance into the Church and into heaven; the entry [1253A] to glory is opened by the sacrament of the passion. The litter being demonstrated, the pillars shewn, and the reclining-place, and the going up of purple also, it remains that the floor of the litter also be demonstrated, which he shews thus: « The middle parts, » that is, the floor which is in the middle of the litter; this he « paved with charity: » well does the prerogative of charity shine forth; for if thou art an apostle, thou belongest to the litter; if thou art a martyr, thou belongest to the same; if thou art a virgin, thou shinest forth in it by the merit of virginity: but if thou art not equalled to these merits, and yet thou lovest, thou art rightly received in this litter; for the middle parts have been paved with charity, that whosoever believes and loves may sit in the midst of it. Which indeed was added « because of the daughters of Jerusalem, » that is, because of those who are younger in time, and stand forth inferior in merit; who, if they are not [1253B] equalled to the highest, yet are not strangers to this litter, being kept in charity. There follows:

*Go forth, and see, ye daughters of Sion, king Solomon.*
