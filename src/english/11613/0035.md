## CHAPTER III. (cont.)

It has been said concerning the little bed, and concerning the keeping of it; and it has been said concerning the litter, and the adorning of it; it remains that it be said concerning the kingdom, and the glory thereof; which he pursues, saying: « Go forth, and see, ye daughters of Sion, king Solomon in the diadem wherewith his mother crowned him. » But by king Solomon, Christ (as has already been said) is understood, who bore the cross upon his shoulders, and through it has now subjected to himself all the kingdoms of the earth. To see him, therefore, the present Scripture stirs up all, as many as desire to be « daughters of Sion, » that is, daughters of the heavenly [1253C] watch-tower, who with the whole devotion of the heart long that a place be prepared for them in the heavens, that they may go forth, and after their going forth may see king Solomon in the diadem wherewith he was crowned [n: (Gen. IV, 8)]. But it does not exhort them to go forth as Cain went forth abroad, and rising up against his brother plotted against him. O wretched Cain, and to be pitied by none, who having overstepped the bound of innocence, who having forgotten brotherly affection, opened to the world the broad way of parricide, and slaying the innocent, declared himself guilty to the world [n: (Gen. XXXIV, 1)]; nor indeed as Dina went forth from her father's tent, that she might see the women of that region; whom, as she was busying herself curiously, and lifting up her eyes to those things which it was not lawful to imitate, Sichem the son of [1253D] Hemor saw, and loved her, and slept with her, oppressing the virgin by force; and the vengeance of this the very death of Sichem, and of his father Hemor, and of all the males who were found in the city, and the laying waste of the city itself, bears witness. For these goings forth are evil; nor are they written to this end, that it should be lawful to imitate them, but that, being written, they may be guarded against, and shunned alike in thought and in deed. But good goings forth are very many; as that of which the Lord said to Abraham, « Go forth out of thy land, and from thy kindred, and out of thy father's house, and come into the land which I shall shew thee: and I will make of thee a great nation, and I will bless thee, and I will magnify thy name, and thou shalt be blessed. I will bless [1254A] them that bless thee, and I will curse them that curse thee; and in thee shall all the kindreds of the earth be blessed [n: (Gen. XII, 1, 2, 3)]. » Which indeed was fulfilled by Abraham in obeying, and even now by the Lord, in truly fulfilling the things that were promised. Of Jacob also it is said, that going forth from Bersabe he was journeying to Haram [n: (Gen. XXVIII, 10)], and there, putting a great stone under his head, he saw a vision, and heard the Lord saying to him: « And in thee and in thy seed shall all the tribes of the earth be blessed: » and that this going forth was doubtless a good one, the very issue of the work that followed shews.

Of blessed Peter also it is read, that when he had gone forth from the hall in which Christ was being judged, the cock crew, and he wept bitterly: which going forth [1254B] doubtless signifies that he was even now estranged from the denial by which he had thrice denied the Lord, and that the tears poured forth from the bitterness of his heart, pardon being granted from above, were now received in heaven. Since therefore there is a certain going forth that is wretched, and departs far from God, and a certain one that is happy, and draws near to God, so far as may be, with the steps of penitence and of love; by what « Aaron » is it counselled to the daughters of Sion to go forth, that they may be able to contemplate and behold the King of glory in his diadem? Not surely by that whereby Cain went forth, whereby he slew his brother, and deserved to be slain himself: nor by that whereby Dina, following curiosity, wished to see those of another race, and grieved that she herself had been mingled with one of another race, and had incurred the loss of her virginity: nor by that whereby [1254C] the spirit going forth from the face of the Lord said: « I will go forth, and I will be a lying spirit in the mouth of all his prophets [n: (III Reg. XXII, 22)], » and I will slay Achab: but by that which Abraham and Jacob, Peter and Paul followed, and were made familiars of God, and his friends. We must go forth therefore from the camp of unbelief and of ignominy, from the camp of vain fear and of impenitence, from the camp of envy and of malice, from the camp of unchastity and of all uncleanness, that having gone forth from these, we may enter the camp of all the virtues, in which we may deserve to see the highest King, and to be pleasing to him. There follows:

*In the diadem wherewith his mother crowned him in the day of his espousals, and in the day of the joy of his heart.*

The holy mother of this highest King is that blessed virgin [1254D] Mary, whom the angel saluted, whom the Lord chose before all, whom the Holy Ghost overshadowed, whose praise surpasses all speech, of whom, whatsoever be said, that which is said is less than her praise; to her all utterances succumb, they ask pardon, and, however worthily the praises be uttered, they confess that nothing can be said whereby her merits may be increased or equalled. She crowned this King with a diadem, because this King took flesh in her and of her, in which he gloriously triumphed over the devil, and over the death by which the world was pressed down. For it is her flesh wherewith the woman compassed him, whom, born of her, the virgin most holily wrapped in swaddling bands, and laid in the manger. [1255A] This is he whom the star from heaven shewed forth, which drew kings from the East to adore him and to honour him with gifts; this is he who advances in wisdom, and age, and grace with God and men; this is he who in baptism purged our crimes; this is he who shone forth to the world by miracles; this is he whom the holy cross bore in its arms; this is he who rose from the sepulchre, and on the fortieth day, while the peoples looked on, ascended the heavens, and on the last day shall openly judge all; to see him therefore they who desire to be daughters of Sion are invited, that, having gone forth from the camp of the vices and entered the camp of the virtues, they may say with the apostle Thomas, « My Lord, my God [n: (Joan. XX, 28)]; » [var: Vulg. couples the two titles, "my Lord and my God"] and may see him and attend upon him in the last day, because, « this is life everlasting, that they may see thee the true God, and [1255B] Jesus Christ whom thou hast sent [n: (Joan. XVII, 3)]. » [var: Vulg. reads that they may know thee the only true God] With this diadem indeed his mother crowned him in the day of his espousals: for to this God, Father, and Son, and Holy Ghost, the blessed Virgin was truly espoused, when, acknowledging her the Mother of the Son of God, they joined her to themselves in true charity, and espoused her to themselves with the whole fulness of love. Hence indeed joy enters into the heart of the Virgin, hence she declared herself blessed, saying: « My soul doth magnify the Lord, and my spirit hath rejoiced in God my Saviour [n: (Luc. I, 46, 47)]. » This Bride, being summoned to the end of life, lest she be entangled and ensnared in these passing things, and admonished not to hope that what is spoken of is even now about to come, so that the Bridegroom should now be seen, should be bodily handled, the Bridegroom repeats what [1255C] was said above, saying:
