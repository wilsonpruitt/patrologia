## CHAPTER IV.

1. *How beautiful art thou, my friend, how beautiful art thou! thine eyes are those of doves.*

Concerning which, because by the Lord's gift we have already said what came to hand, we are now silent, wishing to attempt that which has been added:

*Besides that which within.*

He has indeed called his beloved like in her eyes to a dove; and lest this be understood too perversely, he added, « Besides that which lieth hid within; » for the dove, if she feeds on clean things, if she is without gall, if she tears not with beak or claw, if she has groaning instead of song, if she flies in flocks, is led to these things by a certain natural motion; to these things she is bound by no fear of God, [1255D] but is confined within them by a certain inborn law of nature: but the soul beloved of God, if in these things she likens herself to the dove, has within her in the mind that whereby she is found unlike her. For the dove, if she feeds on clean things, seeks food, not God: the soul, if she feeds on clean things, seeks God, not food. If the dove lacks gall, that is of nature; if the soul governs herself without anger, that is of grace. If she has groaning instead of song, she does not indeed grieve, but modulates her song as it has been given her: if the soul groans, she prays that the very presence of God may be with her, and that she may glory in it. « For great » (as blessed Gregory testifies) « is the glory of the open mouth; but far incomparable is the glory of the hidden recompense: » beautiful is this which is discerned without; but far and incomparably [1256A] more beautiful is that to which one comes invisibly: beautiful is the outward converse; but more beautiful is the unspotted desire of the heart, whereby blessedness is sought, and the conscience is cleansed. This therefore lies hid in the soul beloved of God, and within the dove is utterly unknown. There follows:

*Thy hairs are as flocks of goats.*

But it is to be noted that this commendation is comprised in the number seven, that thou mayest understand that the things which are said of her are said spiritually, and ought to be spiritually understood: for as it has been said of the eyes, so also it is to be understood of the hairs. For the hairs are of the body, and afford adornment to the face; sometimes they are cut off, and the very cutting confers adornment: if it should happen that they are wanting, a deformity of the face will appear. These [1256B] therefore are the faithful peoples, by whose throng the Church is adorned; who, by recalling to memory the heavenly things which they have heard, ruminate upon them, thus seeking their pasture on high, and contemplating things eternal in the cleanness of faith: who doubtless are cut off, when they confess that they have in any way sinned either in action or in contemplation; and this cutting affords adornment to the face, because true confession is the restoring and reforming of the spirit. Or if the subtlety of meditations be understood in the hairs, this is rightly compared to roes, which both tends to lofty things, and, if it is bound by temporal necessity, yet does not withdraw from the intention of things eternal. There follows:

*Which came up from mount Galaad.*

[1256C]Galaad is interpreted *a heap of testimony*, that is, the multitude of martyrs, who, having died for the faith of Christ, are witnesses of the truth. Or, « Galaad » is Christ himself, in whom the multitude of martyrs is one. From this mount the roes have come up, because the saints, imitating his death, have ascended to things on high There follows:

2. *Thy teeth as flocks of shorn sheep, which came up from the washing, all with twin young, and there is not one barren among them.*

« The teeth » are the doctors and expounders of the divine word: these grind the food for those whom they teach, and incorporate it, who, faithfully understanding and teaching the sayings of the Lord, pass these over into the substance of their souls, and keep [1256D] and break small the life already received by faith. These, compared to a flock of shorn sheep, are said to come up from the washing, who in baptism, the fleece of sin being laid aside, seize upon the way of virtue, draw the peoples after them, and out of Gentiles and Jews constitute one Christian people: or, rendering them perfect on this side by faith, on that by work, they enrich and adorn them with twin fruits. Whence none among them is found barren, by whom the preaching of the word received is diligently fulfilled in work. There follows:

3. *As a scarlet fillet, thy lips; and thy speech is sweet.*

The lips cover the teeth, they utter words, and the things which are formed within by the tongue and the teeth, being brought forth without, they render audible. They therefore who are the teeth are also the lips, which bring forth into the light without the things they form within; [1257A] and, that all the things they have said may be heard more attentively, they colour these with the Lord's passion, and labour together, with the sweetness of honey-flowing humility, that they may render them savoury to the taste of spirits. Whence here also there follows, « Thy speech is sweet. » There follows:

*As a piece of a pomegranate, so are thy cheeks, besides that which lieth hid within.*

The pomegranate is the Church, which holds as many grains under one rind as it contains faithful. The cheeks of this are the divers Churches established in divers places by the devotion of the faithful. For to whom his holy humanity is pleasing, by these a church is consecrated to it: to others the holy Trinity is at heart, to whom the place of a most devout man is sanctified. These therefore are the cheeks, running together into one head, namely the Lord Jesus Christ, [1257B] who is God threefold in persons, and one in humanity, the head and beginning of all things, which are, and which have been, and which are to be. Yet these cheeks are thus. « as a piece of a pomegranate: » for as in the pieces of a pomegranate there are grains, differing from the grains of the other part; so also in these places of the faithful there are divers devotions, the one there adoring and following the majesty of God, the other here most devoutly pursuing his humanity and humility, yet none of them fully comprehending the hidden things of God which as yet remain unknown. Whence here also it is subjoined, « Besides that which lieth hid within; » for that which neither eye hath seen, nor ear hath heard, nor hath ascended into the heart of man, this God keeps with himself, about to reveal it when he will.
