## 4.

*Thy neck is as the tower of David, which is built with bulwarks: a thousand bucklers hang upon it, all the armour of valiant men.*

These parts having been shewn, the eyes, and the hairs, the teeth, the lips, and the cheeks, there follows the construction of the neck, whose framing is continuous with those things which have been mentioned. He says therefore that this neck is as the tower of David: for a tower was built by David in Jerusalem, repelling the darts of foes, fearing no engines of enemies; this is the preaching of the doctors, lofty and impregnable; which, while it preaches true things, is repelled by no falsehood of them that oppose it; while it flashes with miracles, it is proved to be true by the very demonstration of the thing. These therefore are the bulwarks [1257D] with which this tower is built, the holy Scriptures by which it truly builds up its own truth: and « a thousand bucklers, » that is, a thousand protections, by which it repels and represses the depravity of those who argue against it: these protections indeed hang from the holy preaching, because that which is holy in the patriarchs, that which is noble in the prophets, that which is notable in the miracles, all bears witness to the Gospel, and approves its truth, together with that armour of valiant men which remains. But who could express this armour of valiant men? Who could worthily comprehend in a word that which transcends all speech? [1258A] For who would suffice worthily to utter the constancy of Peter, who the fortitude of Paul, who the love of John, who the virtue of all the saints? by whom this armour goes forth, and affirms that these things which are preached are true. There follows:

5. *Thy two breasts are as two young roes that are twins, which feed among the lilies. 6. Till the day breathe, and the shadows decline.*

These are the breasts which we suck, from which we receive our increase, that we may please Christ the King, the two Testaments, of which the one is preparatory, the other consummating. That is preparatory which was given by Moses, in which it promises concerning Christ those things which we now see fulfilled concerning him in the New Testament; for this was the first prophecy, wherein the Lord [1258B] says to the serpent: « I will put enmities between thee and the woman, and thy seed and her seed: she shall crush thy head, and thou shalt lie in wait for her heel [n: (Gen. III, 15)]. » Setting forth therefore the blessed virgin, at whose heel, that is, at her outermost part, whereby the serpent lying in wait « saw » the earth, the serpent, that he might not prevail in his bite, was crushed by that very heel of hers. Her first seed, by Adam's teaching, appeared as Abel the just, who, offering a lamb, foreshewed the passion of Christ, and confessing it, fell therein innocent and a martyr. In this seed Seth « the numerable » and Henoch, Noe also saving the world by wood, and Abraham not sparing his own son, and Moses himself saying thus: « A prophet [1258C] shall God raise up to you of the midst of your brethren, like unto me, and he shall put his words in his mouth, and he shall speak to them all that he shall command him. But he that will not hear his words, which he shall speak in the name of God, he himself shall be the avenger [n: (Deut. XXVIII, 18, 19)]. » [var: Vulg. has God himself speaking in the first person throughout, "I will raise them up a prophet … I will be the avenger," and "like to thee"] And Isaias, bearing witness to this, says, « Behold a virgin shall conceive, and bear a Son [n: (Isa. VII, 14)]. » Jeremias says thereto, « The Lord shall make a new thing upon the earth, and a woman shall compass a man [n: (Jer. XXXI, 22)]. » [var: Vulg. puts the verb in the perfect, "the Lord hath created a new thing upon the earth"] Ezechiel affirming this says, « This gate shall be shut, and it shall not be opened, and a man shall not pass through it, and it shall be shut to the Prince [n: (Ezech. XLIV, 2)]. » And Daniel says, « when the Holy of holies shall have come, the anointing shall cease among you [n: (Dan. IX, 24)]. » [var: Vulg. at this place says rather that the Holy of holies may be anointed, and knows nothing of an anointing that ceases] Thus running through the whole Old Testament, thou wilt be able to find in it, in its promises, in its holocausts, [1258D] in its sacrifices, a certain preparation for the New Testament. But the New Testament is that which we hear, which we read, which we attest with Christ as its author, and, this now having its standing, the old perished, save that it bore faithful witness to the truth of this one. These are the breasts, « as two young roes, » which, when thou wouldst grasp them, will straightway flee from thee, and thou seest them climb up on high: for the understanding of man does not take in the lofty mysteries of the just, nor has faith any merit, for which human reason affords proof. Faith therefore is to be followed in these things, and let human reason wholly hold its peace in the confession [1259A] of the Trinity and in the truth of the Unity, in the mystery of the Incarnation and the sacrament of the Passion, in that gift of the altar which we offer, and let it venerate and love that which faith preaches (for these « feed among the lilies, » that is, they receive faith from those assemblies of the saints who, like lilies, stand before God, and receive from him the splendour of everlasting glory) until we stand with them, when the day of judgment shall have breathed, and these shadows of *igorantiae*, which for the present hinder us, shall have been wholly done away. There follows:

*I will go to the mountain of myrrh, to the hill of frankincense.*

The present seven having been run through in praise of the Church, lest he make delay in pursuing this through the remaining members, he does that which belongs to a lover: he desires to see [1259B] what he loves, and to be present with her whom he magnifies with so great praise. Whence he says, « I will go to the mountain of myrrh, and to the hill of frankincense, » that is, I myself will visit the Church in person, which is the mountain of myrrh and the hill of frankincense. But the mountain of myrrh is the height of mortification; of which the Apostle says, « Mortify your members which are upon the earth: fornication, uncleanness, lust, evil concupiscence, and covetousness, which is the service of idols [n: (Col. III, 5)]. » And the Church mortifies these things by considering the things that are above, where there is nothing, nor can be, of those things which have been named. But the hill of frankincense is the humility of prayer, whereby the Church raises herself on high, and, as from the censer of the heart, ascends like the smoke of frankincense. For by these [1259C] virtues the Church, or every faithful soul, is cleansed in the mortification of vice, striving in prayer, washing herself with tears, that, her filth being washed away, she may appear beautiful to the Bridegroom; whose endeavour he himself assists, and, praising his own work in her, he says:

7. *Thou art all beautiful, my friend, and there is not a spot in thee.*

And this indeed is the cause why I should go, because thou art « all beautiful, and there is not a spot in thee, » whose fulness he fully describes, setting last that which is first: for unto this spiritual beauty the first thing is that in the washing of baptism, or in the shedding of blood, the spots be blotted out: then that the spiritual virtues which please God be taken on, so that the Bridegroom, piously desiring her adorned in her head and in her members, [1259D] may come to her, and that a holy dwelling in her may please him. Whence he says:

8. *Come from Libanus, my bride, come from Libanus, come: thou shalt be crowned from the head of Amana, from the top of Sanir and Hermon, from the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards.*

For Libanus is interpreted « a whitening; » whence she also, flooded with the light of the virtues, is called forth to come to God, inasmuch as to her it is thrice said, « Come, » that, perfected in faith and gladdened in hope, she may draw near to him, and, refreshed with the eternal fulness of brightness, may rejoice together with him: which joy indeed is noted, when it is said, Thou shalt be crowned from the head of Amana; » which [1260A] crown indeed is proved to be made from the summit of Amana, from the top of Sanir and Hermon. For « Amana » is a mountain of Cilicia, which is called « Restless, » or, « Turbulent, » designating the men of the world, who, out of avarice and desire of passing things, are always found restless and troubled. « Sanir » is a mountain in Judea, which is interpreted « Stench, » signifying those whose bodies are stirred by the burnings of luxuries, and are defiled. But « Hermon » is a mountain of Judea, which is also interpreted « Anathema, » and signifies those who, lifted up through pride, are so carried away that they have no communion with the Lord, who says, « God resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble [n: (Jac. IV, 6)]. » What then is it, that the crown of his beloved is made out of these summits, unless that they who [1260B] had been troubled and overthrown by the filthy desire of things are fully converted to Christ; and that they whose bodies innumerable luxuries were tearing asunder embrace and love Christ alone; that they also who preferred themselves to all others are humbled even unto death for Christ, and say, « For me to live is Christ, and to die is gain [n: (Phil. I, 21)]. » By these therefore the Church is crowned and exalted; and embracing them in one word, he subjoins and says: « From the dens of lions, and from the mountains of leopards. » For these mountains are dens, that is, places in which lions take their rest. For malignant spirits are called « lions, » which lie down in those in whom the aforesaid vices now reign. But the « mountains » of leopards are those [1260C] in which virtue is mingled with vice, and that which is called hypocrisy rejoices to be honoured by all, the vice being covered with the cloak of virtue. And so, these who had been mountains of leopards and dens of lions being converted to God with a pure mind, the leopards, that is, embracing purity, and the lions loving simplicity and humility, a crown is prepared for the bride, which is now shewn to be for her a sign of victory, in that she has overcome the world. There follows:

9. *Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, my bride, thou hast wounded my heart.*
