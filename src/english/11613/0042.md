## 5.

*I arose up to open to my beloved: my hands dropped with myrrh.*

« I arose up, » that is, I shook off from me the burden of sins wherewith I was weighed down. Whence it is said, « Rise, thou that sleepest, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten thee [n: (Ephes. V, 14)]. » Rising, I also admonished others, that they should rise: to these I showed in myself the pattern of a good work, and, opening the door whereby they might enter in to the beloved, I dropped upon them with my hands the first myrrh, and with my fingers the most approved myrrh. But myrrh, wherewith the bodies of the dead are anointed, and are guarded from rottenness and corruption, signifies the mortification of the flesh, wherewith the spirit is well anointed, while by that saving continence which is wrought [1272D] through fastings, through abstinence, through watchings, and other things of this kind, the flesh is called back from the allurements of luxury, and is adorned with the blessed end of continence: she declares that she has dropped this with her own hands, that is, has shown it forth in all her works, saying, « My hands dropped with myrrh. » And therefore she adds « the first, » because that which flows first from the tree, this the more effectually keeps bodies from corruption. But she shows that she has dropped this, that she may show that she has watched very greatly in the pursuits of frugality. There follows:

*And my fingers were full of the most approved myrrh.*

In which place she opens the virtue of « tearing apart » [cj: *discerptionis*; read *discretionis*, "of discretion"]: for by the fingers discretion is shown: [1273A] because after it is touched by the touch of the finger, that is understood by a certain sure reasoning of the mind. Whence, lest in this guardianship of chastity the measure be exceeded, it is added, « My fingers full of the most approved myrrh. » Myrrh indeed is approved with a wife, more approved with a continent widow, most approved with a holy virgin abiding so. There follows:

6. *I opened the bolt of my door to my beloved.*

Lest the approach to the beloved should be shown to lie in the continence of chastity alone, this is added: « Of my door I opened the bolt, » that is, I removed the silence of the mouth, wherewith until now I was held back, and I opened my mouth, and drew in the spirit, and because I desired thy commandments, those things which thou hast commanded me to keep, and which thou hast willed to be commanded and kept by all thine, these [1273B] I opened, these I admonished men to keep, that so they might approach the Bridegroom, and be pleasing to my beloved. There follows:

*But he had turned aside, and was gone.*

It has been said that she arose that she might open, and fitted her hands, that is, her works, that she might preach; that she admonished all to continence, that so they might approach the Bridegroom, and thus removed the *possulum* of the door, that all might cleave to Christ, and so possess him. « But he had turned aside, » lest, being laid hold on, he should be held; « and was gone, » that is, far surpassing what can be said or promised of him, he had withdrawn himself into that delightfulness which neither eye hath seen, nor ear heard, nor hath it entered into the heart of man, which he himself prepares for them that love him. Which indeed is so done, that [1273C] neither may a sluggish soul cease to seek him, nor may one that labours reckon that she has fully comprehended him. There follows:

*My soul melted when he spoke.*

This beloved had gone; but behold, after his manner, he now returns. He had knocked, he was asking that it be opened to him, and when the Bride rose up to open to him, he himself passed by, and did not suffer himself to be found even by her that sought him. This is the act of the inward man; for he sometimes seeks God, and does not find him. He is found also when the slothful man has now ceased to seek him. For behold this Bride has now risen, she has now held the bolt that she might open, and, not finding him who was knocking, she stood amazed within herself; but God, [1273D] beholding her amazed and marvelling, lest his absence long afflict her, returns to console her, that she may be gladdened by his speech. These therefore are the signs of his coming. As wax melts at the fire, and white snow at the sun, so at the coming of her Bridegroom this Bride was melted, and was changed from that sloth and hardness which she had already gathered in herself. For thus she says, « My soul melted, » that is, from hard and impenitent, penitent; from impatient, compassionate; from unmerciful, merciful; passing from every state of the vices, which I had already begun to feel in myself, into the contrary state of virtue, I melt, and, being now joined to God, I rejoice together with him. Thus indeed, when she bewails her own sins, when [1274A] she laments the hurts of others, or their misdeeds, when she bewails the state of this misery, to which she is subjected of necessity, thus when she bewails the delay of the glory to come, into which she is not received according to her desire, she is indeed melted, saying, « My soul melted. » These indeed are the four rivers of Paradise, wherein the faithful souls wash themselves, wherein they cleanse themselves, wherein they wash away sins, nourish virtues, and stand in the sight of the divine majesty, without spot and wrinkle, worthily and purely. But by her soul she means all who from Adam even unto the end shall have believed that Christ, the Son of God, was to be born, or was born, of the Virgin, and would redeem the world by his death; all of whom, for the unity of faith, naming them as it were one, she says, « My soul melted. » But she subjoins, « When [1274B] he spoke. »

That this may be the more diligently considered, that saying of blessed Paul is to be weighed; for he says, « In manifold wise and in many manners God of old speaking to the Fathers in the prophets. Last of all in these days he hath spoken to us in the Son [n: (Hebr. I, 1, 2)]. » Here indeed he spoke « in manifold wise » in the prophets, that is, through many words; for the great manifoldness of the sentences which the Lord spoke through them could not be unfolded in few: for a contracted brevity begets obscurity, and that obscurity indeed does not admit of understanding. « And in many manners, » he spoke through them; now indeed historically, now allegorically, now tropologically, now anagogically, mingling his words. « But last of all he hath spoken to us in the Son *(Matth. [1274C] IV, 17)*, » whom, sent forth from the bosom of the Father, brought forth through the womb of his mother, the world heard teaching and saying: « Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven is at hand [n: (Matth. V, 3)]. » And likewise that, « Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. » Blessed are the meek, blessed are they that mourn, blessed are they that hunger and thirst after justice, blessed are the merciful, blessed are the clean of heart, blessed are the peacemakers, blessed are they that suffer persecution for justice, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. They saw also works wonderful to tell: a leper coming to him, to whom Jesus said: « Be thou made clean [n: (Matth. VIII, 3)], » and forthwith his leprosy was cleansed [n: (ibid., 13)]: saying also to the centurion: « Go, and as thou hast believed, so be it done to thee [1274D] [n: (ibid., 14)]: » and his servant was healed in that hour; touching Simon's mother-in-law sick of a fever, who was straightway healed. They saw him also casting out all malignant spirits by his word, and healing all the blind, the dumb, the deaf, and the lame, and all that were ill at ease; and speaking thus, and confirming his words with signs following, and, that he might be believed the Lord of death and of life, raising the dead, and raising himself, who had been given over to death and laid in the sepulchre, and promising this resurrection to his own in himself in life eternal: what could they do but marvel, be astonished, and believe, and adore? And hearing from him that on the last day he will bring all things to judgment, [1275A] and will say to the good, « Come, ye blessed of my Father, possess the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world [n: (Matth. XXV, 34)]. » For I was hungry, and I thirsted, I was naked, and a stranger, sick, and in prison, and in all these things you helped me. What could they do but be melted, so that they sold their own goods, and bestowed all upon the poor, and gave their very selves to them, and made themselves subject, that so they might fulfil the Gospel. Rightly therefore has every such soul melted, when the Beloved spoke. For there is nothing that the Father loves more than the Son, to whom he bears witness by his own voice at his Baptism, saying, « This is my beloved Son [n: (Matth. III, 17)], » whom above all that are in the world I love, in that I find him in every thought, word, and work, just in all things. [1275B] There follows:

*I sought him, and found him not.*

This soul, or the Bride of God, has testified that she was melted at the voice of the Beloved; whence it might be understood that she had comprehended him, and had from this friendly speech found an end to her labours. Lest anyone conceive this in his heart, she adds, saying, « I sought him, and found him not. » For all the elect souls seek him; they desire, being raised again in their bodies, to stand by his presence; and because this is not yet, but is most certainly promised as to come, she says, « I sought, » that is, I asked the raising again of them all, and I desired that faith [cj: *fidem*; read *finem*, "an end"] be set to all the labours wherein we seek God, and pant after him. Him indeed I longed for, yet I did not find him, [1275C] because until the day appointed by the Father there shall be the waiting for him, and the certain reward of all. Yet it is written, « Every one that asketh, receiveth: and he that seeketh, findeth: and to him that knocketh, it shall be opened [n: (Matth. VII, 8)]. » Which indeed is thus: All the time wherein we live, wherein we run, is present to God; for eternity is the present state of all those things which have been, which are, or which are to come, since therefore the Lord, sitting in the midst of the circle, beholds with one glance the things which have been, which are, and which are to come, all these are judged present to him, by whom in one glance they are clearly beheld. He says therefore, « Every one that asketh, receiveth, » that is, every one who in truth asks that eternal life be given him, and who seeks that the incorruption [1275D] of the Saints be given him, and who knocks for this with multiplied prayers, that it may be granted him to be received into that glory of the saints; he indeed, when that day shall be at hand, is to receive this, to find this, and to be truly brought into that glory, of which it is said, « I will, Father, that where I am, there also may my minister be [n: (Joan. XVII, 24)]. » [var: Vulg. *ut ubi sum ego, et illi sint mecum*, "that they also may be with me"] There follows:

*I called, and he did not answer me.*

Assuredly every elect soul cries to God, saying, « O God, come to my assistance [n: (Psal. LXIX, 2)], » that if at any time he that is malignant tempts, your grace may withstand him, lest he bring to the effect of sin that which was conceived at his instigation. He adds [1276A] also, « O Lord, make haste to help me [n: (ibid.)], » as if he should say, Thou, O God, who protectest us by helping while we are tempted, make haste to bring us to that state wherein neither his suggestion may be able to move us, nor any temptation of his to bend us to evil: which indeed shall then come to pass, when all the multitude of the elect shall be joined to the angels of God, and they shall neither marry, nor be given in marriage, but shall be as the angels of God. There follows:

7. *The keepers that go about the city found me; they struck me, and wounded me, the keepers of the walls took away my cloak from me. 8. I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, if you find my Beloved, that you tell him that I languish with love.*
