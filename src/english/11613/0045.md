## CHAPTER VI.

1. *My Beloved is gone down into his garden, to the bed of aromatical spices, to feed in the gardens, and to gather lilies.*

[1282B]This garden of the Beloved is the blessed multitude of the faithful, opening to him the whole bosom of its mind, loving and contemplating him with its whole strength, embracing him with a fuller affection of the heart, so that it accounts it gain to die even for him, and does not scorn to endure torments for his sake. In this number thou shalt reckon Abel, who first received the Lord by his own death, being alike virgin and martyr, confessing in himself the priest and the victim, himself alike priest and victim, alike virgin and martyr, he fell, victim and priest. Thou shalt number also Enoch, whom God took from the world; and because he walked with him, he deserved to have life in himself. Noah also, whom, the world being destroyed, God saved in the wood. Abraham also, and his noble offspring. Moses himself also, who, manifestly [1282C] confessing the Lord, says, « I will raise up to you a prophet from the midst of your brethren, like unto thee; and I will put my words in his mouth, and he shall speak to them all the words which I shall command him. But he that will not hear his words, which he shall speak in my name, I will be the avenger [n: (Deut. XVIII, 18, 19)]. » [var: Vulg. *suscitabo eis de medio fratrum suorum*, "unto them, from the midst of their brethren"] In the midst of these thou shalt behold the blessed and highest of virgins, the hope of the ages, Mary, in the bed of aromatical spices, trusting with John her holy guardian, having on the right of this aromatic bed, standing by her, Peter and Paul, Andrew and James, and all the rest, who, bearing witness to him by their own blood, gave forth to the world a most sweet odour, and by their own death brought back the dead to life. This [1282D] indeed is the bed of aromatical spices, which thou understandest with the mind more than thou couldst be taught by word, which by its own blood, and by pains that cannot be told out, deserved to confess Christ. Here the balsams grow fragrant, the pigments give forth their scent, and all the incenses sprinkle abroad a most sweet odour. Hither at times the Beloved turns aside, that he may feed in these gardens, that is, may be refreshed by the glorious testimony of all these, and may gather these true lilies, truly worthy of his fellowship, that is, may establish them in the highest peace and blessedness. Which Church assuredly the Lord is said sometimes to visit apart, that the palm of martyrdom in men of either sex may be understood to have deserved of him something far beyond [1283A] the rest; but that the remainder, the universal Church, scattered far and wide through the world, may be observed to be far greater in number, and less in merit. There follows:

2. *I to my Beloved, and my Beloved to me, who feedeth among the lilies.*

This is the voice of the Bride, who says that she and her Beloved exchange soliloquies, she with him and he with her; which, as they are hidden, so we let them go, nor do we in any manner communicate them to others. There follows:

3. *Thou art beautiful, my friend, sweet and comely as Jerusalem: terrible as an ordered line of battle of the camp.*

These things indeed have already been said in part: for what occurs concerning the beauty of this Bride, that has been recorded. [1283B] It remains that we say also of her sweetness that which the Lord shall teach. That sweetness is not bodily, delighting and alluring some by touch, but rather of the spirit; whence it was said to the Lord also, « Thou, O Lord, art sweet and mild [n: (Psal. LXXXV, 5)]. » So also is she sweet, who, being hurt, murmurs not again, and patiently bears what is brought upon her; whence she obtains the comeliness of patience, like that heavenly Jerusalem, to whom on earth no peace was given, and who, being now taken up into heaven, rests in a great fulness of peace; because « she is terrible as an ordered line of battle of the camp. » For an ordered line of battle of the camp is when they who are about to fight, their whole strength being gathered, stand armed in a fitting place, and have on this side and on that, on the right and on the left, the massed lines of fighters, [1283C] which withstand those that come, and by the strength of arms break their onrushing assaults before it comes to the battle; which indeed strikes terror into the enemy, and therefore rests in the highest peace. There follows:

4. *Turn away thy eyes from me, for they have made me fly away.*

Behold, the Bridegroom has recorded that his Bride is beautiful, sweet and comely, as one who is pleasing to the Bridegroom; her also terrible, so that she fears no encounter of an opposing enemy, so that she stands secure; her therefore, contemplating him in great part, desiring now and now to enter into the secret places of the Godhead, and hoping now to have the fulness of glory which the Lord promises to his own, he beautifully rebukes, [1283D] saying, « Turn away thy eyes from me, » that is, seek not to lay hold of the beauty of my sublime glory; « for they have made me fly away, » that is, to ascend beyond the sense of men, and to show myself as yet incomprehensible to man, because neither hath eye seen, nor ear heard, nor hath it ascended into the heart of man, to will [cj: *velle*; read *nolle*, "not to will"] to show it to any one before the common resurrection of men, and their conjoining with their own spirits. There follows:

*Thy hair is as a flock of goats, which have appeared from Galaad. 5. Thy teeth as a flock of sheep which have come up from the washing, all with twin fruits, and there is none barren among them. 6. As the rind of a pomegranate, so are thy cheeks, besides that which lieth hid within thee.*

These same things have been said above, and expounded; which [1284A] to say again is superfluous and burdensome. There follows:

*There are sixty queens, and eighty concubines, and of young maidens there is no number.*

This also, because, the Lord teaching us, we have explained above, we forbear to explain again, and turn our attention to those things which follow. There follows:

8. *One is my dove, my perfect one, one is she of her mother, the chosen of her that bore her.*

Which indeed is a reciprocal praise. For the Bridegroom commends with worthy praise the Bride who praises him, saying, « One is my dove. » In what manner she is called a dove, by what reason she is understood to be perfect, thou shalt observe from those things which have been said above. But she is here called one, because, as out of many grains one heap, out of many berries one bunch, [1284B] out of a multitude of cattle one flock is fitly called one; so also this one Church, one Bride of God, is understood out of the multitude of all the souls which have been called, or shall be called, to God in the unity of faith, which are strengthened in the unity of hope, which are already established, or shall be established, unto God in charity not feigned. And though she be one, yet is she « one » « of her mother, » that is, one after the likeness of her mother, namely the heavenly Jerusalem, which the Bridegroom asserts to be the mother of this one; and as that consists of a multitude of heavenly spirits, and, joined to God in one charity, is one, so he understands this one to be gathered out of an infinite multitude of souls and to be one, and in this to be like and conformed to her mother, in that she in no wise separates herself from God, [1284C] but binds herself with firm charity to him whom she loves. Whence there follows, « The chosen of her that bore her, » that is, chosen after the manner of her that bore her. But this bearer is the same as is also her mother: yet by the diversity of the words he denotes diverse things; naming her mother, because she consists of a diversity of spirits, and is one, and this one, gathered out of a diversity of souls, likewise stands fast in unity: but her that bore her, because God, the reprobate part of the spirits being condemned, established and confirmed her chosen unto himself; and in like manner the Church, the reprobate multitude of men being condemned, he chose unto himself, and appointed her to be a partaker of his own glory with him. There follows:

*The daughters saw her, and declared her most blessed: the queens and the concubines, and they praised her.*

[1284D]It has been said that the heavenly Jerusalem, the Church, namely, which consists of blessed spirits, is the mother of the Church which consists of souls, and is from the earth; and that this one has been chosen by God unto glory after the likeness of that one. Whence, if it be asked by what testimony this is true, or by what reason it is proved, this answer is added, Because the daughters of Sion saw her, that heavenly Jerusalem, namely, which is the mother of this Church which is from the earth, and which has been taken up by God, and chosen unto glory; the queens saw her, the concubines also saw her, and declared her most blessed, contemplating her with the eyes of the heart, those most blessed spirits, some indeed standing by the majesty and privy to its secrets, others most dutifully ministering, others laboriously running through the regions of the world, [1285A] and announcing the will of God in all things and through all things, and fulfilling it in effect. Her the « queens, » which is discerned to be the highest order in the Church, and the « concubines, » which is next after them, beholding together with the daughters of Sion, truly declared most blessed, and commended as worthy of all praise. And this testimony of the whole Church which is on earth, there is none who denies it to be true, none who rightly contradicts it. There follows:

9. *Who is she that goeth forth as the dawn rising, beautiful as the moon, chosen as the sun, terrible as an ordered line of battle of the camp?*

By whatever love a man is drawn, to that he is quickly turned, to that he is called back in mind. The Bridegroom therefore, conscious [1285B] of his own love, and not ignorant how great admiration the Bride whom he has chosen is worthy of, that he may show her admirable to all, turning to her, says, « Who is she that goeth forth [sic: *progeditur*] as the dawn rising? » It is called *aurora*, as it were *hora aurea*, the golden hour, which, going before the Sun, announces that the sun is about to come, and, glowing red after the manner of gold, shows beforehand that an end is about to come to the darkness: and who knows not that this existed in the primitive Church; who, scripture revealing it, is not ignorant that those first fathers went before the rising of Christ, who is the true Sun of justice, and went before him by their own testimony? For who would doubt that just Abel fell by his brother's sword, and by his own passion showed forth the passion of Christ; that Enoch was translated by the Lord; that Noah, when the deluge was washing the world away, was saved by wood; that Abraham also [1285C] prefigured the Lord in many things? These assuredly, and all the Fathers of that testament, stood forth as the dawn, in whom the rising Church showed beforehand that the true Sun, who is Christ, was to be born, and that she was to be more fully illumined by him. But when the Lord was born, that came to pass which is written, « He came unto his own, and his own received him not. But as many as received him, he gave them power to be made the sons of God [n: (Joan. I, 11, 12)]. » Who, being made sons of God, and illumined by his light, shone indeed as the moon, not with their own light, but with a light not their own: whence it is added, « Beautiful as the moon. » For the moon receives from the sun that whence it shines, and floods the world with a fuller light than the dawn: so the rising Church, [1285D] first as the dawn, then as the moon, but thirdly as the sun, being chosen, ministered full light to the world.
