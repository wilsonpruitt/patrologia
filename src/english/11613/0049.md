## CHAPTER VII. (cont.)

He is blessed whose vineyard is visited in the morning by the Lord. For the vineyard of each one is this present life; and the morning is for each one the beginning of the present life. But there are certain ones whom in the morning, that is, from the very [1295D] outset of life, God visits, directs, and magnifies, and whom he shews even by manifestation to be such that from them great fruits of miracles are to come; « Let us see if the vineyard has flourished. » To these the Bride invites the Beloved, that he may abide with them, and make these to flourish, and himself keep the flowers that bring forth fruits. These indeed are our advances in God: for as a vineyard produces first leaves, then the flower, thence the fruit; so in the things that are God's, for those who are solicitous to see and to have him, a certain initiation is the turning away from evil; for a great advance is a happy conversion to the things that are God's. But the highest thing in these is the love, pleasing to God, of the things that are God's, and the blessed completion of them in work. Whence also it is subjoined:

*If the pomegranates shall have flourished.*

[1296A]In pomegranates indeed the love of martyrdom is signified. Greater love indeed hath no man, than that one lay down his soul for his friends. Him indeed, though she admonishes all her own to see, yet these especially, who burn with the love of martyrdom, who desire to lay down their life for Christ and through death to ascend upward, she admonishes by a certain special visitation to give heed. Whence also she says:

*There will I give thee my breasts.*

What are these breasts which the Bride is about to give to the Bridegroom when he shall so have come, and shall so have visited the Church? The breasts indeed are that part of the body wherewith the Church nourishes her sons, wherewith she draws out their infancy, and brings them forth into the strength of youth; and when they have grown up into this strength, she leaves them to themselves, and to the martyrdom which they desire, and [1296B] to him for whose sake they desire it. She says therefore, « There will I give thee my breasts, » that is, I will open all the folds of my mind, that I may understand from thee what is to be said to each order, in what manner each order is to be admonished, that it may come to thee and possess thee for ever. There follows:

*The mandrakes have given a smell. In our gates are all apples: the new and the old, my Beloved, have I kept for thee.*

Having called her Beloved to come to the Church, to visit her, and she who desires to see him, she adds this, saying: For that indeed which I long for, I doubt not that I shall find thee difficult [cj: *difficilem*; read *non difficilem*, "not difficult"], because I understand that the mandrakes in our gates have already given thy odour. The mandrakes indeed she names the Prophets, which keep the form of a human body, and which heal whatever member [1296C] of the human body a disease occupies, from a like member, by powder scraped for the purpose. This also makes the barren woman fruitful, and profits those who suffer by much art of medicine: whence this expresses the Prophets, who express the humanity of Christ, and the things which he wrought in man; and this in our gates, that is, in the Scriptures which have now been made ours, they cease not to cry out and to prophesy. These indeed are all the apples, which the new and recent prophets foretold; « and the old, » the ancient, namely, which have been spoken from Adam until now, have I kept for thee, because they are to be fulfilled in thee, and to be shewn by the very accomplishment of the work.
