## CHAPTER VIII.

1. *Who shall give me thee for my brother, sucking the breasts [1296D] of my mother, that I may find thee without, and kiss thee, and that now no man despise me. 2. I will take hold of thee, and lead thee into my mother's house: there thou shalt teach me, and I will give thee a cup of spiced wine, and new wine of my pomegranates.*

This Bride, solicitous concerning the coming of her Bridegroom, has long since in prayer greatly desired it, saying, « Come, my Beloved; Let us go forth into the field; » and what she prayed for she foretold would assuredly be at hand, because the mandrakes have spoken this beforehand, this all the prophets have foretold. Therefore concerning this which shall certainly come to pass, she now asks, saying: « Who shall give me thee for my brother, » that is, [who] will be so worthy as to shew thee to me visibly as my brother, to demonstrate thee to me in bodily form as [1297A] my brother. For it is ancient, and known by hearing, that God the Father of all created me among the rest, and stood forth as a father to me among the rest, and that I among the creatures have always called upon him as a Father: but that he himself should be a brother to me is new and unheard of from the ages, and yet is to be spread abroad to the world. For the angel Gabriel was sent to Mary, saying, « Hail, full of grace; the Lord is with thee. The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow thee. And therefore that holy thing which shall be born of thee shall be called the Son of God [n: (Luc. I, 28, 35)]. » Behold that the Son of God, born of the holy Virgin, born again by holy baptism, was made a son of the true Church, and began to be a brother to us all, who before all things stood forth to us as Father. Whence after the resurrection [1297B] he says, « Go, tell my brethren that they go into Galilee; there they shall see me [n: (Matth. XXVIII, 10)]. » Whence it is that him whom hitherto I had called God the Father, I now name my brother; and therefore brother, because sucking the breasts of my mother, namely of Mary; who, from this that she was made his mother, is worthily the mother of all who call upon him, and implore his help for themselves in whatsoever necessity. Through her indeed the Son of God was found without, that is, in this bodily form, which he newly took, and in it appeared to the world, and in this he received to himself from him (as has been said) the kiss of reconciliation; whence she says, « And now let no man despise me. » For before God had held out to man the kiss of reconciliation, [1297C] man was held despicable both to the angels and to the demons, and even to his own self, who was being dragged wholly into Hell to be possessed. But after he was reconciled by the death of the Only-begotten, forsaking Hell, he began to possess the region of Paradise and of heaven, and from thenceforth he was held in contempt neither by the possessors of heaven, nor by those of Hell. For she herself says, « I will take hold of thee, » that is, I will hold thee with a most firm embrace: not by threats, not by stripes, not by every kind of death shall I be removed or torn away from thee: and thus will I bring thee « into my mother's house, » namely of the heavenly Jerusalem, which instructs me, and shews me the example of that love whereby I may always cleave to thee, and may not be able to be removed from thee; and I will bring thee into « the chamber of her that bore me, » that is, that ancient dwelling-place of love, wherein [1297D] she that bore me has ever held thee, that is, that most ancient gathering sprung from the patriarchs and the prophets and the other faithful, and that holy congregation which could never be torn away from thee. « There thou shalt teach me, » because then shall we all be taught of God. And what shall we give thee? « A cup of spiced wine, » namely everlasting love; which is formed in so many ways, as wines are wont to be formed from divers kinds; « And new wine of my pomegranates, » that is, the passion of my martyrs, who are so joined to one another in the sweetness of love, as apples are joined to one another among apples, and by no evils could they be turned away from thee. There follows:

3. *His left hand is under my head; and his right hand shall embrace me. 4. I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir not up, [1298A] nor make the Beloved to awake, until she herself will.*

Because these things have already been said, we do not repeat them, but where they were said, we commit them to the studious to be read. There follows:

5. *Who is she that goeth up from the desert, flowing with delights, leaning upon her Beloved?*

One that loves easily looks back upon one that loves him, and the words which love forms it easily pours over to itself. Whence, because from the things already said the affection of the Bride that loves him is easily marked, the Bridegroom straightway affords her a response of love, asking and inquiring who she is: not that he is ignorant of her, but that he may permit no one to be ignorant. Of what sort is she, and how great, and how most worthy of wonder, who thus goeth up from the desert? Naming this world [1298B] a desert, which very many strive to inhabit as a fatherland, and, cheated of their endeavour, descend from hence to the places below, and thus come to know what fatherland is destined for them. This desert is that place which the Lord deserted by ascending, when, in the sight of many, he was lifted up and ascended into heaven. Wherefore it follows, that this same one is « mighty in delights, » flowing with which, and understanding that they please the Lord in the highest degree, she seeks to stand by him with right faith, strong hope, fervent charity, and to present to him these things given to her by himself. But this is what he says: Who, and of how great merit, is she who, this world being deserted, namely which God deserted by ascending from hence, which for others has been wont to be a place of descent, making for herself a place of ascent, leaps forth on high, presenting to God the delights [1298C] of the virtues, which she knows to have been conferred upon her by him? But blessed Gregory, looking more deeply upon these things, says: The Church, flowing with the delights of the words of God, ascends from the present desert to things above. To flow with delights is, in the love of God, to be filled with the banquets of the Scriptures. Flowing therefore with delights she ascends, because daily she lifts us up, fed with mystical understandings, to things on high; in whose words there are as many delights as there are diversities of spiritual understanding. Now history feeds us, moral allegory refreshes us, sometimes contemplation suspends us toward higher things. The soul flowing with these delights is refreshed with mystical understanding, in which the present obscurity is illuminated by the brightness of the day that follows, so that even in this corruption it may break forth into the understanding of that [1298D] true light, and, fed with the delights of the words, may learn by foretasting what it hungers for of the food of truth. There follows:

*Under the apple tree I raised thee up: there thy mother was corrupted: there she that bore thee was violated.*

It has been said that she, flowing with delights, is leaning upon her Beloved: now it is consequently shewn that she could and ought to lean upon him. For he says, « Under the apple tree I raised thee up, » that is, I called thee back from death; thee set indeed under the apple tree, that is, liable to the sin which was contracted by the apple tree. For they say that that forbidden tree, from which man in Paradise was bidden to abstain, was an apple tree, and that that wood, which was good for food, and beautiful to the eyes, and delightful to behold, [1299A] came to be a concupiscence to the woman; who took of it, and held it out to the man, and made herself, and her posterity, guilty of disobedience. From this evil of disobedience indeed this Bridegroom, bearing the cross, snatched all; and therefore he confesses that he raised up and redeemed her, placed under the apple, that is, under the sin which was contracted from the apple. And that this be not esteemed a small thing, he adds, « There thy mother was corrupted; there she that bore thee was violated; » calling mother the whole generation which was drawn out from Adam even to Abraham: but naming her that bore thee that generation which, from Abraham even to Christ, was bound by a certain stricter law of commandments, teaching by these that all begotten by human generation are liable to this offence; unless either the Spirit sanctified them before [1299B] they were born, as the holy Virgin who was made the dwelling-place of God, John the Baptist, and Jeremiah, whom we know to have been sanctified from the womb, or God succoured them by his sacrament. But if anyone cares to look more deeply, let him read again the treatise which we discoursed above concerning the kiss of reconciliation, and wherein we set forth the things which were granted to us, the Lord dictating. There follows.

6. *Set me as a seal upon thy heart, as a seal upon thy arm.*

Because I freed thee from the death which thou didst deserve, through the death of my cross; therefore I consequently enjoin upon thee, that me, that is, my love, whereby I was made man [1299C] for thee, whereby I was crucified for thee, whereby I humbled myself that I might rescue thee from the death which thou hadst deserved, thou set always as a seal, that is, as a certain memorial, upon thy heart, that is, in thy heart; that in everything which thou shalt have purposed to do thou attend always to this love, that thou presume not to fulfil anything in act, unless thou see it to cleave to this love. The same also he subjoins concerning the arm, « As a seal upon thy arm. » For if these are things cleaving together, the heart deliberating, and the arm executing that which is deliberated by the heart. And if both of these hold the memorial of this love, they will restrain themselves from every work which suits not this love. Thus fortifying the heart and the arms, he urges that which has been said elsewhere: for elsewhere it is said, « Six things there are which [1299D] the Lord hateth, and the seventh his soul detesteth: Haughty eyes, a lying tongue, hands that shed innocent blood, a heart that deviseth most wicked thoughts, feet that are swift to run into evil, a deceitful witness that uttereth lies, and him that soweth discord among brethren [n: (Prov. VI, 16, 17, 18, 19)]. » All which things indeed, if thou hast the love of God, if thou fix it in thy heart and in thy arm as a memorial, shall be made far from thee, nor is there anything wherein they may hinder thee in the least.

*For love is strong as death*

Death indeed is strong, because it destroys kings, casts down tyrants, despoils the rich, and finally snatches to itself everything [1300A] that lives in the world, ending all things by one common end, and shutting up the universe: so also the true love of Christ is indeed strong, rejecting whatever things are adverse to Christ, not shuddering at the sword, not fearing the prison, not dreading chains, willingly taking upon itself every torment that can be devised, reckoning these things not as torments, but venerating and adoring them as helps to its own salvation. The world devised wild beasts, devised burning plates, devised wheels and screws; yet charity, abiding in the hearts of those whom malignity was assailing, overcame and conquered all these things. Whence this love is not strong as death, but is stronger than death itself, which remains whole while death rushes upon it, and through it deserves to obtain eternal rewards. There follows:

[1300B] *And emulation is hard as hell.*

As love is commended, so emulation is disapproved; for emulation is said to be an envious imitation of charity. For certain men, seeing the martyrs honoured, and the holy confessors venerated by all, envying those honours, and desiring the same, put themselves forward as though followers of the martyrs [sic: *marytrum*] and imitators of them, and thus esteemed themselves martyrs, or as it were martyrs: whose zeal the Bridegroom himself thus disapproves, saying, « Emulation is hard as hell. » For as Hell drags those whom it snatches, nor permits any of them to be drawn out from it; so hard is the condition of these men, which is thus made one with hell, feigning good for itself only with evil guile.
