## 10.

*I am a wall: and my breasts are as a tower; since I am become in his presence as one finding peace.*

The answer of the Beloved being heard, wherein he admonishes the Bride both to set forth bulwarks of silver for the converted synagogue, and to send fit defenders for the defence of the Church, she gratefully says, « I am a wall, » I too will set myself against the blows; and my breasts, namely the prelates of the Churches, whom I name my breasts for this cause, that I nourish them with my milk, these shall be with me the firm and unassailable defence of the Church, and with me the incontestable acquiring of eternal life; in that, my sister who had perished being now restored to me, « I am become in his presence as one finding peace. » For then shall peace truly be found, when none of those who were foreknown unto life shall any longer be lacking, [1302B] but the Church, now holding all her own within herself, shall rejoice in the highest prosperity. There follows:

11. *There was a vineyard to the peaceable one in that which hath peoples: he delivered it to keepers.*

The praise of this canticle being now consummated, and the fulness of this Bride being consummated, when the synagogue, which she names her sister, shall have been converted to the faith for her, and the fulness of the Gentiles shall have entered into the faith, the Bride now speaks of herself entire, saying, « There was a vineyard to the peaceable one. » I indeed, now called with the fulness of the Gentiles, with the synagogue now converted to the faith, have stood forth a vineyard to the peaceable one, that is, to Jesus Christ our Lord, who has made peace with all things that are in earth, and that are in heaven. « In that which hath peoples, » that is, in the multitude of the Gentiles, which was wholly created by God, but [1302C] not wholly foreordained unto life; « For many are called, but few chosen [n: (Matth. XX, 16)]. » But I, with the multitude of all the elect, of Jews and of Gentiles, was a vineyard to the peaceable one, making for him chosen fruits, worthy to be received by him, worthy to be presented to God the Father. Which vineyard indeed she says the peaceable one delivered to keepers: for that part of the Church which stood forth from Adam even to Christ was committed to the Patriarchs, and the Prophets, and the Doctors of the holy Law, to be kept. But that which shall be from Christ even to the end of the world was granted to the holy apostles, and to the martyrs, and to others excelling in honour, to be instructed. There follows:

[1302D] *A man bringeth for the fruit thereof a thousand pieces of silver. 12. My vineyard is before me. A thousand are thine, O peaceable one, and two hundred for them that keep the fruits thereof.*

This vineyard indeed the peaceable one tended, planted, and pruned, and brought through to flower and to fruit; which was not turned into bitterness, nor brought forth wild grapes, but choice grapes, and most worthy to be presented to God the Father. Whence here it is said, « A man bringeth for the fruit thereof a thousand pieces of silver. » Who is this man? That good man whom David calls to mind, saying, « The man who hath not walked in the counsel of the ungodly, and hath not stood in the way of sinners, and hath not sat in the chair of pestilence. But his will is in the law of the Lord, and on his law he shall meditate day and night [n: (Psal. I, 1, 2)]. » [var: Vulg. opens the verse with a beatitude, "Blessed is the man who hath not walked…"] He alone, most worthy to be called a man, receives the fruits of the Church, [1303A] and offers them to God the Father, from whom, for the rewarding of the Church, he receives a thousand pieces of silver, which he brings, that the Church may be enriched, and may rejoice together that she has borne fruit well. But by the thousand pieces of silver he denotes eternal life, and its infinite beatitude, wherewith the Lord shall reward his own, when he shall have presented them to the Father, and shall have set seats for them in the heavenly places. But he signifies that this beatitude is comprehended by the number of a thousand, which is the fourth unity: for the first unity is unity itself; the second, the ten; the third, the hundred; the fourth, the thousand; after this the number is extended to infinity, having always the thousand for its root and unity. Well therefore by this is infinity shewn, and by the silver, the excellence of beatitude; for concerning this it is said, « The words [1303B] of the Lord are chaste words: silver tried by the fire, proved of the earth, purified seven times [n: (Psal. XI, 7)]. » Because the words which preach the Lord contain chastity in themselves, admitting in themselves no heresy, no incontinence at all, but are in themselves as silver tried by the fire of the Holy Spirit, than which no utterance rings sweeter, nothing is more savoury to the soul; than which nothing is found more proved from the earth, nothing more purified by the fire of the sevenfold Spirit: concerning which she receives this answer of the Father, « My vineyard is before me, » that is, All this multitude of the elect is pleasing to me, it is with me in every thought and work, which thou signifiest to me by the thousand peaceable ones, and by the two hundred, who keep the fruits thereof. For these are those perfect ones who from Adam even to [1303C] Christ instructed this whole body with words, kept it with examples, and adorned it with merits. But the hundred that remain are those who from Christ even to the end of the world presided over that elect multitude: these are presented to God the Father, and are crowned in eternal beatitude with those over whom they had presided. There follows:

13. *Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the friends hearken: make me hear thy voice.*

This is the last admonition of the Bridegroom, wherewith he admonishes the Bride concerning his praise, saying, « Thou that dwellest in the gardens, make me hear thy voice. » She indeed, now holding the primacy in the gardens of Judaea, now holding her converted in the faith, instructing her with the precepts of life, [1303D] fully possessing also the multitude of the Gentiles, plainly residing in the gardens of the virtues, containing in herself all the virtues which smell sweetly to the Lord, is well said to dwell in the gardens of the Churches, and, that praise may be brought forth by her to the Lord, is entreated with heavenly admonitions, when it is said, Thou that dwellest in the gardens of the Churches, [1304A] make me hear thy voice, that my praise may resound from all the Churches, and that from every one my praise may ring back again with thanksgiving. For « the friends » « hearken; » for the Angels, and all the angelic powers with them, joined to Christ together with these from the beginning by the virtue of faithful friendship, await and hearken to the glory of my rewarding, which they already hope for in the glory of thy praise. There follows:

14. *Flee my Beloved, and be thou like to the roe and to the fawn of the harts upon the mountains of spices.*

This is the last answer of the Church, wherewith the Bride answers her Beloved, saying, « Flee, my Beloved. » The Bridegroom admonishes her concerning praise, and exhorts her to sound forth praise, not that he wills that she should now begin the canticles of praise, but the common resurrection of all being awaited, [1304B] when the Bridegroom shall have come in a cloud, when an angel with a clear voice shall have sounded forth his coming, saying, « Behold the Bridegroom cometh, Go ye forth to meet him [n: (Matth. XXV, 6)]; » when the mournful trumpet shall have sounded for all to rise again, and the Judge, thronged about with Angels, shall have sat on the seat of his majesty, and the world, none excepted, shall have run to meet him, and he shall have borne a dreadful sentence upon the reprobate, and shall have given to all his elect the sentence which he is to speak, « Come, ye blessed of my Father, possess the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. » Then indeed shall the praise and thanksgiving of the Church with all the saints sound forth, such as has not been heard from the beginning of the world, and all that partake of her praise shall be blessed by the Lord unto the ages. And because this was shewn beforehand to the Bride that loves him, therefore, not [1304C] with indignation, but bearing this delay patiently, she thus answers, « Flee, my Beloved, » which is: withdraw thee for the present from our sight; in this standing like to the roe and to the fawn of the harts, which are indeed clean animals, and keep themselves from the sight of men upon the ridges of the mountains. Which mountains indeed, where this fawn of the harts dwells, that he may distinguish them from the other mountains, he adds, « of spices. » For the mountains of spices are the holy hosts of the angels, and the dwellings of all the heavenly powers, from which breathe forth all the aromatic graces, which it is certain are pleasing to the Lord. Upon these the Lord pours forth from himself the balsam of life, that, anointed therewith, they may now live for ever: of which anointing assuredly the Church, desiring to be [1304D] made partaker, and always sighing after it in the things which have been said, has sought that the kiss of most holy delight be given to her; and now, made certain by the most sure promise of her Bridegroom that she shall receive it after a little while with the whole of her fulness, she rests.
