# Cruces — 11613 (Gilbert Foliot, *Expositio in Cantica canticorum*), chunks 0000–0004

PL 202, cols 1147A–1163C. Covers the EPISTOLA NUNCUPATORIA (Patrick Young's 1638
dedication to William Juxon), the GILBERTI PRAEFATIO, and the opening of CAPUT PRIMUM
on the lemma *Osculetur me osculo oris sui.*

## The named lemma — 7a″

**1. *Osculetur me osculo oris sui.*** (0001 @1149C, and re-quoted twenty-one further
times across 0001–0004.)

- **Printed reading agrees with the Vulgate exactly** (Cant. I, 1). No `[var:]`.
- Fixed English: **"Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth."** Held identical in
  every re-quotation; only the terminal mark varies, and it varies *because Migne varies
  it*, so it is carried each time:
  - `? ` — 0001 @1149C; 0002 @1153B (both inside guillemets, both preserved).
  - `: ` — 0001 @1149D; 0002 @1154A; 0004 @1163A.
  - `; ` — 0004 @1162D (in the *acsi dicat* frame).
  - `. ` — all others.
- ⚠ **Three of Migne's *nunquid* questions close with a full stop, not a `?`** (0002
  @1153C *dicat tamen ad Patrem omnium*; 0002 @1153D *singultus poenitentiae trahens*;
  0003 @1158A *in terram proni corruerunt*). Rendered with the printed stop under
  Pattern 8. **Not defects of ours; do not "restore" the marks.**
- The lemma is fragmented twice into its own words and re-quoted piecemeal — 0004 @1163A
  « *Osculetur* » alone, and @1163C « *Osculum* » alone. Rendered « Let him kiss » and
  « A kiss », matching the fixed rendering word for word.

## Divergences from the Vulgate examined and NOT marked

Pattern 14's gate is *changes the sense*. Each of these is a real divergence, is recorded
here as data, and takes no marker because the sense Foliot draws is unaffected.

- 0000 @1149A, Exod. I, 1: *ingressi sunt **in** Aegyptum* (Vulg. *ingressi sunt Aegyptum*).
- 0000 @1148A, Cant. VIII, 6: *Fortis est ut mors dilectio* — Vulg. has *quia* before it.
  Simple abridgement.
- 0001 @1151A, I Cor. XV, 46: *Prius namque quod animale est, deinde quod spirituale.* The
  Vulgate states this by **negation** (*sed non prius quod spiritale est*), and Migne's form
  drops the negative. **Checked under 7a and declined**: nothing is asserted here that the
  Vulgate denies — the abridgement converts the Vulgate's own negative construction into the
  affirmative it entails, and Foliot's gloss (first the animal, then the spiritual) is what
  both forms say. Recorded because a reader comparing the two will notice the missing *non*.
- 0001 @1151D, Gen. I, 28: *universis quae moventur* (Vulg. *universis **animantibus** quae
  moventur*).
- 0001 @1152A, Eccle. XII, 1: *Memento **Domini** Creatoris tui* (Vulg. *memento creatoris tui*).
- 0001 @1152A, Eccle. XII, 13: *hoc est omnis homo* (Vulg. *hoc est **enim** omnis homo*).
  Foliot glosses the clause three separate ways at @1152C, and none of the three turns on *enim*.
- 0001 @1151B / 0004 @1163A, Heb. XI, 6: the same verse printed two ways within one work —
  *sine fide placere Deo est impossibile* and *sine fide Deo placere impossibile est*. Word
  order only. **This is Migne's inconsistency, not ours**; both rendered as printed, and the
  English differs in nothing but the same word order.
- 0001 @1152C: the lemma *mandata ejus **observa*** is re-quoted by Foliot as « mandata
  **servare** ». Both rendered "keep the commandments" — the verb is synonymous and the gloss
  turns on *timere*/*amando*, not on the verb of keeping.
- 0002 @1153D, Rom. VII, 24: ***O** quis me liberabit **a** corpore* (Vulg. *quis me liberabit
  **de** corpore*).
- 0002 @1154A, Joan. III, 29: recast from the Vulgate's *amicus autem sponsi qui stat et audit
  eum* into *cum amicis Sponsi stat, et gaudio gaudet ad vocem ejus*. Note that **amicus** here
  is the Song's third love-word in its Johannine use ("friends of the Bridegroom") and is kept
  distinct from *sponsa*.
- 0002 @1155C, Ephes. V, 27: *maculam, **neque** rugam* (Vulg. *aut rugam*). The two nouns are
  then glossed separately, in guillemets, so both are held fixed as "spot" and "wrinkle".
- 0002 @1155C, Prov. IV, 23: *quoniam* for Vulg. *quia*. Note also that Migne prints the full
  stop **inside** the quotation before the note and again after it (*procedit. [n]. »*); both
  stops are carried.
- 0003 @1156D, Phil. I, 21+23: *Dissolvi **cupio*** for Vulg. *desiderium habens dissolvi*.
- 0003 @1157A, Prov. XIV, 13: ***novissima** gaudii* for Vulg. *extrema gaudii*.
- 0003 @1158A–B, Matth. XI, 11: both halves recast into indirect/comparative form
  (*Nemo … major sit*; *ipso **longe** sit major*). *longe* is Foliot's, not the Vulgate's.
- 0003 @1159D, Luc. XVI, 10: *Qui modico fidelis non est, quomodo erit fidelis in magno?* —
  a considerable recasting of the Vulgate's *qui fidelis est in minimo et in maiori fidelis est*
  into a rhetorical question. Migne prints the `?`; it is preserved. Declined a `[var:]`
  because the entailment Foliot draws (contempt in a small matter is contempt) is the verse's
  own, and because naming a single "received reading" would mean choosing between Luc. XVI, 10
  and XVI, 11 on our own authority.
- 0004 @1160C, Col. I, 26: Migne prints **sacramentum** where the Clementine Vulgate reads
  *mysterium*. Not marked: the words are synonyms in this register, Vulgate witnesses vary at
  this verse, and Foliot uses *sacramentum* as his own term throughout (*Incarnationis hoc
  sacramentum*, *fidei Sacramentis*), so an English that split them would misrepresent him.
  Rendered "sacrament" wherever *sacramentum* stands.
- 0004 @1161A, Psal. CX, 2 and XVIII, 10: word order only (*opera Domini magna sunt*;
  *ipsius vera sunt judicia … in semetipsis*), and *ipsius* for the Vulgate's *Domini*.
- 0004 @1161A–B, Gen. III, 6: abridged, with *tulit **mulier** de fructu illius* where the
  Vulgate's subject stands earlier in the verse.
- 0004 @1161D, Col. II, 14: Migne prints inside guillemets a paraphrase in the present tense
  (*cruci Christi potenter affigitur, et deletur*) of a verse the Vulgate gives as participles
  round *delens … adfigens illud cruci*. Rendered as printed. **Declined a `[var:]`**: it is
  Foliot's own syntax, not a divergent reading of the verse, and Pattern 14 exists for the
  latter.
- 0004 @1163A, Jac. I, 6: *Postulet autem **unusquisque** in fide* (Vulg. *postulet autem in fide*).

## ⚠ Declined `[var:]` — the unidentified prophet, 0004 @1160C

> *Hoc consilium illud antequam est, de quo propheta ait, « Consilium tuum, Domine, antiquum
> verum fiat. »*

Migne prints **no note** here, and the wording matches no Vulgate verse. It is close to the
Old-Latin form of Isa. XXV, 1, whose Vulgate reads *cogitationes antiquas fideles amen*.
**No marker taken, and the reason matters**: identifying the verse is *our* inference, not
Migne's, and a `[var:]` whose "received reading" rests on our own identification of an
unlabelled quotation asserts more than we know. Rendered as printed. If a later pass confirms
the referent from the Old-Latin witnesses, the marker can be added then.

## `[cj: …]` — real word, misleading English (1)

**0004 @1160C.** *Hoc consilium illud **antequam** est.* *antequam* is a perfectly good Latin
conjunction, so nothing is broken and `[sic:]` has nothing to wrap; but the sentence will not
construe, and the required word is the adjective *antiquum* — which Migne prints **in the very
quotation that follows**, and which Foliot's own phrase *a seculis absconditum* then glosses.
Under Pattern 18 as extended 2026-08-15 (incoherence is no defence where the failed word is a
connective), the gloss is additive: "This counsel is that before [cj: *antequam*; read
*antiquum*, "ancient"]".

## `[nt: …]` — a note that is prose (1)

**0003 @1158D.** Migne's note *(Nam totum quod in osculo misera peccat, et carnalis affectio, ab

## Pattern 7 cruces — rendered literally, not repaired

1. **0003 @1159D — a sentence with no apodosis.** *et ut homo de manu Domini bonum sine fine
   meruit, cum a veritate recessit.* The *ut* clause has no *sic* clause: the argument requires
   "as man deserved good without end …, so he deserved evil without end when he departed from
   the truth", and the second limb is simply not on the plate. Rendered as printed, ending where
   Migne ends. **No marker**: nothing is broken and nothing is a wrong word — text is missing,
   and `[ed:]` is reserved for what our *digitization* lost, not for what the plate never had.
   ⚠ This is the most substantive hole in the range; a blind read will stop here.
2. **0002 @1154C — a stranded adjective.** *quasdam sibi praelibari velut arrhas expostulans,
   **interno;** solumque … audibili sono mentis exclamans.* *interno* agrees with *sono*, four
   words later, and Migne's semicolon severs them. Under Pattern 8 the mark rides as printed, so
   the English strands the adjective too ("with an inward; and crying out with a sound of the
   mind…"). It reads as broken because it **is** broken.
3. **0000 @1149A — an unstated subject that inverts the sense.** *haec, quos irae filios,
   dictante justitia, perfecerat, in libertatem gratiae … reformat.* The subject of both verbs
   is grammatically *haec* (= *dilectio*), which makes **love** the maker of the sons of wrath;
   the sense wants *justitia*, which the ablative absolute has already used up. Rendered with the
   neutral "it", preserving the ambiguity rather than choosing.
4. **0004 @1163A — an active verb where a passive is wanted.** *quae sine fide est oratio,
   **exaudire** non potest.* Printed as active ("cannot hear"); the sense is *exaudiri* ("cannot
   be heard"). Rendered literally with God as the implied subject ("and the prayer that is without
   faith he cannot hear"), which is the closest English the printed word allows. Declined `[cj:]`
   because the printed reading, so construed, is not false — only strained.
5. **0004 @1163B — lowercase after a full stop.** *…nominatur. osculans, ipse Pater est.*
   English has no exponent for a Latin lowercase mid-sentence-start, exactly as it has none for a
   gender mismatch (Pattern 9); capitalized in the English, and the Latin column preserves it.
   Logged so a later sweep does not churn it.
6. **0002 @1153B — an imperfect subjunctive among perfects.** *Sponsum prorsus **nesciret*** in a
   chain of *perceperit … contigerit*. Rendered as a past ("knew nothing at all of the
   Bridegroom"); no marker, since the tense is expressible and nothing is asserted falsely.
7. **0002 @1154C — an ambiguous *in ipsam*.** *dedignans totum quod creatum est, in ipsam tota
   colligit, ipsi nexu charitatis … adstringitur.* *ipsam* is feminine and has two candidates,
   *sapientiam* (four words back) and the soul herself. Read as *sapientiam*, since *ipsi* in the
   next clause must be the same referent and the soul cannot be bound to herself by charity.
8. **0003 @1156B, Pattern 9 item — no defect.** *ad petram, **qui** Christus est.* Masculine
   relative on a feminine antecedent, from I Cor. X, 4. Latin gender has no English exponent;
   translated normally, marked here so no sweep churns it.

## Fixed vocabulary in this range

The antithesis pairs the brief names mostly have not yet collided at this point in the work —
the Bride has not spoken to the daughters of Jerusalem, and *nigra/formosa*, *laeva/dextera*,
*flores/mala* are all still ahead. What **has** collided, and is held fixed:

- **sponsa / sponsus** — Bride / Bridegroom. Both occur at 0003 @1159B (*Sponsi vero ad sponsam*),
  the one place in the range where they meet in a single clause. Migne's own capitalization is
  followed: *Sponso*/*Sponsum* capitalized of Christ (0002 @1153B, @1153C), lowercase *sponsum*
  at 0000 @1150B and *sponsi* at 0002 @1155A, and the English follows the plate rather than
  regularizing.
- **amica** — does **not** occur in 0000–0004. *amicus* does, twice, and both are the ordinary
  masculine: *amico suo charissimo* (0000 @1147A, Foliot to Robert) and *cum amicis Sponsi*
  (0002 @1154A, John III, 29). Neither is the Song's *amica mea*, and neither is rendered "bride".
- **dilectus** — does not occur as the Bridegroom-noun in this range. *dilectio* occurs
  (0000 @1148A, 0001 @1152C) and is the ordinary noun "love".
- **amor / dilectio / charitas.** All three are in play at 0001 @1152B–C and 0002 @1154C, where
  Foliot varies them for style within two sentences (*ad amorem malus angelus non assurgit* …
  *in quo timor dilectioni concurrat*). **charitas is always "charity"** — the distinction is
  load-bearing at 0002 @1154C (*Amor namque in Deum accensus, charitas est*, "love kindled toward
  God **is** charity", which is only a statement if the two words differ). *amor* and *dilectio*
  are both "love": they are synonyms here, and inventing an English distinction Foliot does not
  make would be the mirror of harmonizing a lemma.
- **caro / spiritus** — flesh / spirit, fixed; they meet head-on at 0004 @1161C (*non delectatur
  hic caro; sensus ipse rationalis in spiritu carnem trahit post se*).
- **Ecclesia / anima** — Church / soul, fixed; they meet at 0000 @1150B (*Animam igitur, vel
  Ecclesiam*), which is the sentence that sets the whole work's allegory. Note Migne prints
  *ecclesia* **lowercase** throughout 0003 @1159B–C and capitalized elsewhere; followed.
- **Parabolae / Proverbia — a real distinction, not a synonym.** Solomon's first book is
  « Parabolarum » at 0000 @1150A and *Parabolas / Parabolarum volumine* throughout 0001, and the
  lemma at 0001 @1150D is *Parabolae Salomonis* — all rendered **"Parables"**, because the gloss
  is about the book's title. But at 0002 @1152D Migne writes *jam edocta **in Proverbiis***, and
  that is rendered **"in the Proverbs"**. Foliot uses both names for one book; the English keeps
  both, because collapsing them would conceal that he does.
- **tomus / tractatus** — the work's own division-words, and they are two. *tomo* (0002 @1155A
  *sequenti tomo*, 0003 @1159A *tomo superiore*) = **tome**; *tractatum* (0003 @1159A, 0004
  @1163C *in tractatum sequentem*) = **treatise**. Both appear within four lines of each other at
  0003 @1159A, which is what forces the distinction.
- ***mala*, the homograph.** No apple anywhere in 0000–0004. Every occurrence in this range is
  *malus, -a, -um* = **evil**: *malum [angelum]* (0001 @1152B), *mali angeli* (0001 @1152B, 0002
  @1156B), *Ascensor malus* and *equi mali* (0002 @1156B), *ex ira vel malitia* (0003 @1158B),
  *malitiam innocentia* (0002 @1155D), *hoste maligno* (0004 @1162A). Recorded so the next agent
  knows the count starts clean.
- ***theoria*** (0002 @1152D) kept in italic Latin. Foliot has *contemplatio* twelve times in the
  same chunk and builds an argument on it (*amorem contemplatio suscitet*); rendering *theoria*
  "contemplation" too would fuse two words he keeps apart at the very moment he is defining one
  of them.
- ***Acoluthus*** (0000, epistle) kept in italic Latin. Young means the companion piece printed
  with the *Expositio* in 1638 ("for to go forth into public alone, without a companion, was
  neither seemly"); "acolyte" would carry a liturgical sense the sentence does not have.
- ***Sirasirim*** (0000 @1150A, twice) — Foliot's transliteration of the Hebrew title. Left as
  printed; he glosses it himself in the next breath (*quod « Canticum canticorum » appellatur*).

## Pattern 17 — thou/you in this range

The range contains the corpus's cleanest worked case of the number rule, and both halves are
inside **one sentence** of Young's dedication (0000): *Amplissima Dominatio **Vestra*** →
"**Your** most Ample Lordship", grammatically plural; *Amplitudini **Tuae*** → "**Thy**
Amplitude", grammatically singular. Young switches; the English switches with him. Elsewhere:
Foliot to Robert is *tu* throughout (thou), and the soul's *Seorsum **vos**, abstractus a
**vobis*** at 0001 @1149D is plural (you) — the one plural in Gilbert's own prose, addressed to
the world's goods she is dismissing.
