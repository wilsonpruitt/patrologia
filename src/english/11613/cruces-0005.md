## Scripture divergences outside the Song (markers fired)

- **0006 @1167D, Rom. VI, 13** — Migne prints « vasa iniquitatis ad iniquitatem » where the
  Vulgate has *arma iniquitatis* (and *ad iniquitatem* is from VI, 19). The image changes
  (vessels for weapons) and the gloss that follows turns on the members serving. `[var:]`
  fired.
- **0007 @1170A, Isa. LXIV, 4** (Migne's note prints *Isa. LIV, 4*, kept verbatim; the verse
  is LXIV, 4) — Migne prints *quae praeparasti **diligentibus** te* against the Vulgate's
  *expectantibus te*. **This is material and it is the whole point of the sentence:** Foliot
  immediately adduces 1 Cor. II, 9 with *Juxta quod Paulus ait*, and the two texts agree only
  because Isaias has been conformed to Paul's *diligentibus se*. `[var:]` fired.
- **0007 @1172A, II Cor. XII, 9** — Migne prints *Sufficit mihi gratia **tua***, the persons
  inverted from the Vulgate's *sufficit tibi gratia mea*, because the frame makes the soul
  say it *Domino Jesu*. Rendered as printed; `[var:]` fired to record the received reading.
- **0008 @1174A, Matt. XXV, 41** — Migne prints *Ite, maledicti, in ignem aeternum, qui
  **praeparatus** est* where the Vulgate (and **this same work at 0005 @1165B**) prints
  *Discedite a me maledicti … qui **paratus** est*. A work quoting one verse two ways is
  exactly the datum this edition exists to show. `[var:]` fired. Migne's note reads
  *(Matth. XV, 41)* for XXV, 41 — kept verbatim.

## Scripture divergences recorded, marker DECLINED (and why)

Each of these was read word by word against the Vulgate; the marker was declined because the
divergence does not change what the quotation asserts, which is Pattern 14's bar. They are
logged so that a blind read does not have to re-derive them.

- **0005 @1164C–D** — the six kiss proof-texts are quoted loosely: Gen. XXVII, 26–27 (*et
  accessit* for *accessit*); Gen. XXIX, 13 (*complexus* without *eum*); Gen. XXXIII, 4
  (*stringensque collum **ejus***); Gen. XLV, 14–15 (compressed: *cum amplexatus recidisset
  in collum Benjamin … flevit*, the Vulgate's mutual weeping dropped); Luc. XV, 20 (*vidit
  pater ipsius*, without *illum*). Sense unaffected in every case.
- **0005 @1165B, Gen. XLV, 1** — Migne's quotation runs the verse into *et osculatus est eos*,
  which is Gen. XLV, 15. A conflation of the same scene; the gloss depends on the kiss, which
  is genuinely in the chapter.
- **0005 @1165D, Matt. XXV, 35** — *Esurivi, et dedistis* without the Vulgate's *enim*.
- **0006 @1166D, Matt. XXV, 36 / 43** — *Nudus **fui**, et operuistis me* and *Hospes **fui**,
  et non collegistis me* for the Vulgate's *nudus … et*, *hospes **eram***. Foliot's parallel
  phrasing across the six clauses; no sense at stake.
- **0006 @1166D, Matt. VIII, 20** — *ubi caput **suum** reclinet*; the note prints *(Matth.
  VIII, 30)* for VIII, 20, kept verbatim.
- **0006 @1167B, Apoc. III, 20** — *Ecce, ego sto ad ostium **pulsans*** for the Vulgate's
  *ecce sto ad ostium et pulso*. Participle for finite verb; assertion identical.
- **0006 @1167D, IV Reg. IV, 34** — condensed to *Os suum ori ejus … applicat* (present tense,
  the Vulgate's three *posuit … super* clauses collapsed). Recognizably the same act.
- **0006 @1168A, Matt. IX, 12** — *Non est opus **sanis medicus*** for *valentibus medico*.
  Case and lexis both differ; the proposition is the same.
- **0006 @1168C, Matt. XXI, 18** — *Veniens autem Dominus ad ficulneam, esurivit*, Foliot's own
  weave of vv. 18–19.
- **0006 @1169B** — « Congratulamini mihi de ove perdita. Congratulamini de drachma inventa »
  compresses Luc. XV, 6 and 9, where **the same work at 0005 @1165A quotes XV, 6 in full and
  in Vulgate form**. Recorded as an internal inconsistency of the plate's quoting practice.
  Migne's note at 1169B reads *(Matth. XV, 6)* for the *Euge serve bone* of Matt. XXV, 21 —
  kept verbatim.
- **0006 @1169A, Joan. XIV, 26** — *Spiritum quem **mittit** Pater* for the Vulgate's future
  *mittet*. Nothing in the gloss turns on the tense; declined deliberately rather than
  overlooked.
- **0006 @1169A, Joan. XV, 26** — *Mittam vobis Spiritum*, an abridgement.
- **0007 @1170A, I Cor. II, 9** — the clause *nec auris audivit* is absent and *his qui
  diligunt eum* becomes *diligentibus se*. Abridgement; and see the Isaias entry above, which
  is where the divergence does work.
- **0007 @1170B, I Cor. XV, 24, 28** — *Tradet enim Dominus Jesus regnum Deo et Patri* supplies
  a subject the Vulgate's *cum tradiderit* leaves in the verb, and *ipse erit omnia in omnibus*
  for *ut sit Deus omnia in omnibus*.
- **0008 @1173B, Exod. XXI, 23–25** — *Reddat quis animam pro anima* for the Vulgate's *reddet
  animam pro anima*: jussive plus indefinite subject where the Vulgate has a plain future.
  Rendered "Let a man render…"; the precept is unchanged.
- **0008 @1173D, Matt. XXIII, 13** — *hypocritae* absent.
- **0008 @1175A, Eccle. VII, 30** — *Deus **creavit** hominem rectum* for *fecit*. A synonym;
  declined.
- **0009 @1175C, Isa. LV, 2** — *argentum **vestrum*** added.
- **0009 @1176C, Luc. VI, 19** — *Virtus **enim** de illo exibat, et omnes sanabat* (word order).
- **0009 @1176D, Joan. XXI, 25** — *Sunt et multa alia* for *sunt autem et alia multa*.
- **0009 @1177A, Heb. XI, 6** — *Sine fide placere Deo impossibile est*; the Vulgate's *autem*
  gone and the order rearranged.
- **0009 @1177D, Act. IX, 5** — *Ego sum Jesus **Nazarenus***, from the parallel at Act. XXII, 8.
  Nothing in the gloss turns on it. Migne's note reads *(Act. IX, 45)* for IX, 5 — verbatim.
- **0009 @1177C, Luc. IX, 35** — this one DID take a `[var:]` (see above): *dilectus … qui mihi
  per omnia complacet … hunc audite* is a Matthean form standing under a Lucan citation, and
  *electus* vs *dilectus* is the reading at issue in that verse's whole textual history.

## Type and readings

- **0007 @1169D — `[sic: *carnem*]`.** Migne prints *nuptiale **carnem** est quod tractatur*.
  *carnem* is a real word but has no slot here: a feminine accusative under the neuter
  *nuptiale* in a nominative predicate. Read *carmen*, "song" — the sentence is glossing the
  book as an epithalamium two clauses earlier, and *nuptiale carmen* is the standard phrase.
  It is carried rather than rendered because English nouns show no case, so "it is a nuptial
  flesh that is treated" would be fluent English that silently repairs the disagreement
  (Pattern 12, test 2).
- **0008 @1172D — `[sic: *praecontis*]`.** Not a Latin word. Read *praeconiis*, "by
  proclamations, by heralding" — *ab ipso quasi praeconiis extollitur*, and Foliot uses
  *praeconio* of exactly this act eight columns later (0009 @1176B). Carried under Pattern 7;
  it swallows no verb, negation or conjunction.
- **0009 @1176A — `[sic: *soquentes*]`.** Not a Latin word. Read *sequentes*, "those following."
  The sense ("those following it") is supplied in plain English beside the marker per 7a⁗,
  since the carried run is the object of *potet et inebriet*.
- **0009 @1176B — `[sic: *invensionibus*]`.** Not a Latin word. Read *inventionibus*,
  "inventions" — the same word is spelled correctly at 0008 @1174C (*adinvenit*, *invenire*),
  and the sentence needs the noun the gloss has been building for a column ("all the
  inventions … against it"). Sense supplied beside the marker.
- **0009 @1176D — `[sic: *saliari*]`.** Not a Latin word. Read *satiari*, "to be satisfied" —
  the clause is about the spirit's food. This one is the infinitive complement of *praevaleat*,
  so the English supplies "satisfied" beside the marker (7a⁗, question 1: the main predicate
  survives, but the marked run carried the only content verb).
- **0009 @1178A — `[cj: *Si*; read *Sic*, "So"]`.** *Si factus adolescentulus, audivit ab Anania
  verba quibus excrevit* — *Si* is a perfectly good word, so nothing is defective for
  `[sic:]` to wrap, and the faithful English ("If, made a little youth, he heard…") turns
  Paul's actual instruction by Ananias into a hypothesis, in a sentence whose second half
  answers it with the correlative *sic infinitus credentium numerus*. This is the 11064
  @1142D case exactly (*si* for *sic*, a correlative weakened into a condition), which
  Pattern 18 already admits.

## Readings rendered as printed, no marker (Pattern 7, conjecture kept out of the text)

- **0005 @1163D** — *qui de admisso unius pro omnibus membris suis **ipsam** quandoque
  destrueret*: the feminine *ipsam* has no printed antecedent (*admisso* is neuter). Probably
  *mortem* or *culpam* from the sense. Rendered "would … destroy that same thing"; nothing is
  supplied.
- **0005 @1165D** — *cujus quidem **bonus** illa die et universis applaudet, et singulis*.
  Read *bonis* ("will applaud her goods, both all and each"), which is what the next sentence
  goes on to enumerate — the six clauses of Matt. XXV. Declined for `[cj:]`: the printed
  reading is obscure, not false, and Pattern 18's bar is a sentence that says something the
  author is arguing against.
- **0005 @1166A** — *Misericordia quippe sunt opera*: *misericordia* cannot agree with the
  neuter plural *opera*; read *misericordiae* (genitive, "works of mercy"). Rendered "For the
  works are mercy". A case slip, no English exponent worth marking.
- **0006 @1167B and @1167D** — Migne prints **no full stop** at *…omnimoda charitate et
  virtute Ipse namque ait* and at *…virtuti jam servire compellant De iis ipsa Veritas ait*.
  Pattern 8: the English does not supply the marks. Both read abruptly, and that is the plate.
- **0006 @1169A** — *Sic ad Patrem loquens, os ejus, juxta hunc sensum. Filium nominans et …
  Spiritum sanctum intelligens.* Migne's full stop falls inside a participial construction,
  leaving the second sentence with no finite verb. Carried as printed (Pattern 8).
- **0008 @1172D** — *Num ad ubera divertamus…?* where the run of the argument wants *Nunc*
  ("Now let us turn aside"). *Num* renders in place as an interrogative particle and the
  sentence is a question either way, so no marker; the conjecture is here.
- **0008 @1174C** — *ut quae Dei sunt negligat, et ea quae Dei sunt, haec inquirere et
  investigare contemnat*: *quae Dei sunt* twice, in two different clauses. **Not `[d:]`** —
  the phrase is not a compositor's immediate doubling but a resumptive repetition with its
  own syntax in each clause. Rendered twice, unmarked.
- **0009 @1176D** — *Hoc panes **duodecim** ad **novem millia** hominum satianda ampliavit.*
  ⚠ **The most conspicuous factual oddity in the range.** The Gospels have five loaves and five
  thousand (Matt. XIV) or seven and four thousand (Matt. XV); *twelve* is the number of
  baskets of fragments and *nine thousand* is in no account. Rendered exactly as printed.
  Declined for `[cj:]` because this is Foliot's own prose, not a quoted scripture (so Pattern
  14 does not reach it), and because no single printed word can be glossed as the error — both
  numerals would have to go, which is emendation, not annotation.
- **0009 @1178A** — *signa et mirabilia … quibus nec resistere, nec contradicere **poterunt***:
  future where the narrative is past (*poterant*). Renders in place; both negations carried.

## Vocabulary held fixed across the range

*sponsa*/*sponsus* = Bride/Bridegroom throughout (Migne's own capitalization varies; the English
does not). *dilectus* as the Song's Beloved does not occur in this range — *dilecta mea* at
1172C is the ordinary participle and is rendered "my beloved". *laeva*/*dextera* and
*sinistra*/*dextra* both occur in 0008's argument about the two breasts and the two Testaments;
English has one pair of words for two Latin pairs, and the alternation is recorded here rather
than invented in the translation (Pattern 9 family). *voluptas* = "pleasure" throughout 0007
(including *torrens voluptatis*, *locus voluptatis*), *jucunditas* = "gladness". *mala* in this
range is **always the adjective "evil"** — *sive bona, sive mala* (0005 @1164D), *malitiose
peccantes* (0008 @1173B); the Song's apples do not appear before 0009 ends. Two etymological
glosses keep their Latin beside the English because the gloss IS the etymology: *ubera* from
*ubertas* (0008 @1173A) and *serum* gathered *sero* (0008 @1173C).
