# Cruces — 11613 (Gilbert Foliot, *Expositio in Cantica canticorum*), chunks 0010–0014

PL 202, cols. 1178B–1192C. Column + printed reading + note. Markers taken are named;
declines are named too, with the reason, because a flagged passage is not a checked passage.

## Fixed vocabulary held across the range

`sponsa` Bride · `sponsus` Bridegroom · `dilectio`/`diligit` love, loves (no *dilectus* the
Beloved occurs in this range) · `laeva`/`sinistra` left, `dextera`/`dextra` right ·
**`nigra` black · `formosa` beautiful** · `nigredo` blackness · `fusca` dark ·
`decoloravit` discolored · `forma` form · `decor` comeliness · `formositas` beauty ·
`pulcher` fair · `caro` flesh · `spiritus` spirit · `Ecclesia` Church · `anima` soul ·
`tenebrae` darkness / `lux`, `lumen` light · `cellaria` cellars · `justitia` justice.

⛔ **`mala` audit.** Every instance in 0010–0014 is *malus, -a, -um* = **evil**, never the
Song's apple: 1178B *malis semper operibus*, 1179A/1179B *mali homines*, 1181D *quae
debentur malis supplicia*, 1185C *multa malitia … intenta ad malum*, 1179C *cogitando
mala*. No apple lemma falls in this range (the Song's *mala* begin later). Checked
individually from the syntax.

`amica` does not occur in this range. `amicus` at 1190D–1191A is the Gospel's **friend**
(Luke 11) and is rendered "friend", never "bride".

## 1. THE NAMED LEMMA — 1183C, and its re-quotations

**1183C, lemma 4:** `4. *Nigra sum, sed formosa, filiae Jerusalem.*` — **agrees with the
Vulgate exactly** (Cant. 1:4). No marker. English fixed at "I am black, but beautiful, O ye
daughters of Jerusalem."

⚠ **1184A — THE HEADLINE DIVERGENCE. Migne re-quotes his own lemma with *et* for *sed*:**
`« Nigra sum et formosa, filiae Jerusalem. »` — twenty lines below the lemma that prints
*sed*. Marked `[var: Vulg. *sed*, "but"]`. This is material and not cosmetic: the
concessive *sed* is the whole hinge of the chapter (Foliot's exposition at 1184C–1186A
rests on blackness being conceded and beauty asserted *against* it — *Attendendum itaque
nigredinem hanc plus habere formae in se*, and 1186A's two-column diptych *nigra sum* →
*sicut tabernacula Cedar*, *sed formosa* → *sicut pelles Salomonis*). A copulative *et*
flattens that into a pair of coordinate attributes. Rendered as printed: "I am black and
beautiful."

**Every other occurrence of the pair prints *sed* and is rendered "but":** 1184A (the bare
fragment `« Nigra sum, filiae Jerusalem. »`, which drops *sed formosa* entirely — a
fragment, not a variant, so no marker), 1184C, 1185C, 1186A, and the diptych halves at
1186A. Nine re-quotations of *nigra* and five of *formosa* in 0011–0012 were checked one by
one against the lemma; the words never drift.

**1187A — `« Formosus sum sicut pellis Salomonis; »`** — the Bride speaks of herself with a
**masculine** *Formosus*. Pattern 9: Latin grammatical gender has no English exponent, so
there is nothing to render either way and nothing to mark. Logged so a later sweep does not
churn it. Note also that this is Foliot's own construction, not a Song lemma.

Logged, **no marker taken** — divergence real but the sense does not turn on it, and no
gloss depends on the word:

- **1178B** *in virum perfectum, **et** mensuram aetatis* (Ephes. IV, 13) — Vulg. *in

- **1179C** *« funiculus triplex **non facile** rumpitur »* (Eccle. IV, 12) — Vulg.
  *difficile rumpitur*. Same sense by a different construction; Foliot glosses the cord, not

- **1181B/C** *« … claritate quam habui **antequam mundus fieret** »* (Joan. XVII, 5) —
  Vulg. *priusquam mundus **esset**, apud te*, and Migne drops the opening *Et nunc … tu*

- **1182A** *An putas quia non possum rogare Patrem meum, et exhibebit mihi plus quam

- **1183D** *« Melior est obedientia quam victimae »* (I Reg. XV, 22) — Vulg. *Melior est
  **enim** obedientia*.
- **1185A** *« … quia **emisit** Dominus angelum suum »* (Act. XII, 11) — Vulg. *misit*.

- **1185A** *« … in plagis **super** modum »* (II Cor. XI, 23) — Vulg. *supra modum*.
- **1185B** *« eam separare queant a charitate Dei »* and **1186C** *« Scio quod nulla

  sentence (*eam* = the Bride; *me* for the Vulgate's *nos*). Declined: the reference of the
  pronoun is manifestly the commentator's, not a witness to a divergent text, and Pattern 14
  forbids speculating about why readings differ. Rendered exactly as printed.
- **1184C** *Quia « oportet **me** ire Jerosolymam … »* (Matth. XVI, 21) — Vulg. *oportet
  **eum** ire*, and Migne drops the closing *et tertia die resurgere*. **Declined

- **1185C** *« Videns autem Deus … cuncta cogitatio cordis esset intenta ad malum »* (Gen.

- **1187D–1188B** *« Vocabo non plebem meam … et non dilectam **meam**, dilectam »* (Ose. I,

- **1188B/C** *« Exurgens Princeps Sacerdotum … »* (Act. V, 17, 18) — Vulg. *Exsurgens

## 3. Broken type carried through (Pattern 10 / 12)

- **1179D** — *ut te intus corde **saptiam*** for *sapiam*. A non-word: carried untranslated
  in italics and marked, `may inwardly savor [sic: *saptiam*] thee in my heart`. Per 7a⁗
  question 1 the marked run was the clause's only predicate, so the sense ("savor") stands
  in plain words **beside** the marker rather than inside it. Conjecture: *sapiam*,
  near-certain — the parallel members that follow are *te et tua lingua denuntiem* and
  *opere devotus adimpleam*, giving heart / tongue / work.
- **1187C** — *ipse Sol **justistitiae** fecit in me* for *justitiae*. Dittographic syllable;
  carried and marked, `the Sun of justice [sic: *justistitiae*] himself`. Sense supplied
  beside the marker per 7a⁗, and the conjecture is settled by Migne's own next sentence,
  *qui Sol est justitiae*, six words later.
- **1192A** — *« **Percussus suum** ut foenum, et aruit cor meum »* (Psal. CI, 5) for
  *Percussus **sum***. ⚠ **The most dangerous site in this range.** *suum* is a perfectly
  good Latin word, but it has **no grammatical slot** — the clause is left with no finite
  verb at all — so Pattern 12's first test applies and it is carried: `I am smitten [sic:
  *suum*] as hay`. Per 7a⁗ the copula is supplied in plain words outside the marker;
  quarantining *suum* silently would have left "smitten as hay" with no subject and no verb.
  Conjecture: Vulg. *sum*, "I am (smitten)". A second `[var:]` beside the `[sic:]` was
  considered and declined as telling the reader the same thing twice.

## 4. Punctuation carried as printed (Pattern 8)

- **1186D** — *Summus enim decor … huic affuit; **quem. nullo** carere quo vita hominum
  adornari queat, quisquis bene sanus intelligit.* A full stop breaks the relative clause
  after *quem*. **Preserved in the same position**: "…was present to him; whom. whosoever is
  well in his wits understands to lack nothing…". It reads as a defect because it is one;
  never deleted.
- **1182A** — *An putas quia non possum rogare Patrem meum, et exhibebit mihi plus quam
  duodecim legiones angelorum.* Migne closes an interrogative with a **full stop** where the
  Vulgate prints `?`. The mark rides as printed and the English ends with a period. This is
  Pattern 8's "never supply", and it is **not** the 8b indirect-question case: the Latin is a
  direct question and it is *Migne's* mark that is odd, not English grammar refusing one.
- **1179C / 1185A** — the same verse (Rom. VII, 24) is punctuated two ways on two plates:
  *« Infelix ego homo**:** quis me liberabit… »* at 1179C and *« Infelix ego homo**!** Quis
  me liberabit… »* at 1185A. Both carried exactly as printed; the divergence is Migne's.
- Question-mark and exclamation parity was counted against the Latin twin for all five
  chunks and matches exactly (0010 3/1 · 0011 3/0 · 0012 3/9 · 0013 1/0 · 0014 10/3).

## 6. Declines — passages examined and deliberately left alone

- **1188C — *caecis postmodum denuntiaverunt, ne omnino loquerentur in nomine Domini Jesu.***
  The dative *caecis* has no plausible referent (the apostles were not blind; the subject is
  the Sanhedrin). Rendered literally, "they afterwards gave charge to the blind, that they
  should by no means speak…". **Pattern 18 `[cj:]` was considered and declined**: the bar is
  that the printed word makes the sentence assert something the author is *arguing against*,
  and this produces an odd referent rather than a false claim; it is not a negation and not a
  correlative, so it falls in the declined class with 11064's *ipsos* and *suscepit*. A
  reader who wants the conjecture — *caeci*, nominative, of the accusers themselves — has it
  here. Flagging without acting is deliberate, not an oversight.
- **1191D — *ut sint haec quasi semina, et multa ex his **fruges oriatur***.** Plural
  *fruges* against singular *oriatur*. English "may arise" is number-neutral, which is
  Pattern 12's second test (a rendering that hides the defect), **but the marker must wrap
  the defective run and it is not determinable which token is defective** — *fruges* for
  *frux*, or *oriatur* for *oriantur*, or *multa* pulling either way. Marking an arbitrary
  guess would misattribute the compositor's error. Rendered "that many fruits may arise from
  them" and logged instead. Named so a later reader can rule it.
- **1182A–B — *sed harum frequentatione laudum potius delectari **magis** est*.** Pleonastic
  *potius … magis*, and *est* with a bare infinitive where a gerundive is wanted. Rendered
  as closely as the printed words allow ("but rather it is the more a delight to be delighted
  by the frequenting of these praises"), which is clumsy in English because it is clumsy on
  the plate. No emendation, no marker: the words are all real and all occupy their slots.
- **1184C — *mundumque sibi, et se mundo contemptibilem parat*.** The subject of *parat* is
  ambiguous between the Holy Spirit and the soul, and *sibi* between the two. Rendered
  without resolving it ("prepares the world to be contemptible to itself, and itself to be
  contemptible to the world"); the ambiguity is Migne's syntax, not a defect.
- **1186B — *sic et **hae**, ab amore Dei … averti*.** Feminine plural *hae* after a
  discussion of the masculine *tabernacula Cedar* and their inhabitant; the antecedent must
  be the perfected souls / churches. Rendered "so neither could these women be turned away";
  gender is Pattern 9 and there is nothing to mark.
- **1180D — *ea quibus vult, et quando vult, et quamdiu vult, distribuit*.** Reads as though
  a word has dropped (*ea quibus [vult] … distribuit*), but it construes as printed
  ("distributes them to whom he wills"). No action.

## 7. Second person (Pattern 17)

Number, not reverence, decides throughout. God addressed as *tu* takes **thou** (1178C–1180B
passim, the long prayer *Trahe me post te … Tu volentem me sequi te*); the reader or single
soul addressed as *tu* takes **thou** (1179A *si porrectam tibi manum Dei senseris … manum
apprehende*; 1185B *Tu quoque quae filia Jerusalem dici desideras*; 1189B *Percurras omnia*;
1190D–1191A *nosse in quem credas, et quem diligas*). The daughters of Jerusalem, addressed
as *vos*, take **you** (1185B *attendite*; 1187B *vos quae filiae vultis esse Jerusalem,
nolite hoc attendere*, and the Ose./Rom. quotation's *non plebs mea **vos***). Matt. 26:52's
*Converte gladium **tuum*** is singular to Peter, so **thy**.

## 8. Head convention

`## CAPUT PRIMUM. (cont.)` → `## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)`, following the sibling Song commentary
10804 and the corpus majority. `(cont.)` is our chunk division and is carried untranslated.
The numbered heads `## 5.` and `## 6.` (0013, 0014) are Migne's section numbers and pass

## 9. ⚠ Cross-chunk conflict for the merge step (runbook step 4)

The lemma at **1189A/B** (`*Indica mihi, quem diligit anima mea, ubi pascas, ubi cubes in
meridie…*`) sits in **0014** and is re-quoted and glossed in **0015**, which a different
agent holds. The two renderings differ on three words and must be reconciled at assembly —
one voice within a work:

| | 0014 (this range, lemma-bearing) | 0015 |
|---|---|---|
| *quem diligit anima mea* | whom my soul **loves** | whom my soul **loveth** |
| *ubi pascas* | where thou **feedest** | where thou **pasturest** |
| *in meridie* | **in the** midday | **at** midday |

Recommendation, offered not imposed: take the lemma-bearing chunk's wording. *feedest* is the
natural English of *pascas* and 0014's own gloss runs on feeding (*pastorem*, *pascit Deus*,
*Spirituale pabulum*) while still having "pasture" available for *pabulum*; *in the midday*
is the literal rendering of *in meridie*; and *loves* is the ordinary present that Pattern 17
does not touch (there is no second person in that clause, so *loveth* buys no distinction).
Whichever is chosen, it must be applied in both chunks.
