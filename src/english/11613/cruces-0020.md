are eight lines apart at 1217B–C and the distinction is deliberate.

## 2. *pulchra* / *pulcher* — the gender the English cannot carry (1206D–1210C)

The two lemmata stand consecutively and are grammatically opposed:

- 14 @1206D — *Ecce tu pulchra es, amica mea, ecce tu pulchra.* (feminine, the Bride)
- 15 @1209B — *Ecce tu pulcher es, Dilecte mi, et decorus.* (masculine, the Bridegroom)

English adjectives carry no gender, so the referent is carried **by the vocative
Migne prints and by nothing else**: "my love" (*amica mea*) can only be the
Bride, "my Beloved" (*Dilecte mi*) can only be the Bridegroom. No word was
invented to disambiguate; Pattern 9's ruling on grammatical gender applies (no
English exponent, no emendation to make). Foliot's own gloss reinforces it
independently — 1209C opens *Sic amans amanti pari respondet elogio*.

**"beautiful", not "fair".** *pulchritudo* is glossed continuously for four
columns (1207A–1210C: what beauty is, whose beauty, the beauty of the Trinity),
so a lemma reading "fair" would leave the gloss explaining a word not in it.
This is case 2 of the Song ruling (the gloss depends on the word). The familiar
Douay cadence is exactly the 7a″ pressure that had to be resisted here.

## 3. *mala* — the homograph, decided from the syntax at every site

**Apples / apple tree** (all in 0022–0024):
1213D–1214C *Sicut malus inter ligna sylvarum* and its five re-quotations —
the apple TREE (feminine *malus*, *Malus enim arbor est*, *Malus enim ista …
arbor est* @1215D); *fructum malorum* @1213D — apples; *istius mali fructus*
@1215C — of that apple; lemma 5 @1220B *stipate me malis* — apples.

**Evil** (adj./noun): 1209B *malis quibusque praecinctos*; 1213B *male
deridendo*; 1216A *omni malitia*; 1216C *prave*; 1217C, 1218A–B *spiritus
malignos, maligno, malos homines, in malum utentes, bonis malisque*; 1219D
*cogitationes malae*, *pedes veloces ad currendum in malum*; 1219D *a malo quod
semper est*.

The two classes come within four lines of each other at 1215C–D (*istius mali
fructus* … *omni malitia* @1216A) and again at 1220A–B (*in malum* … lemma
*stipate me malis*). Each was decided from case, gender and governing verb, not
from context alone.

**Lemma 5 @1220B is left un-glossed by the plate** — Foliot's exposition of
*flores* (the saints' words) versus *mala* (their works) begins in chunk 0025.
The lemma is rendered "flowers … apples" and the two must stay distinct there.

**@1213D — *sive pomum dicatur, sive malum*.** Foliot distinguishes two Latin
names for the same fruit; English has one word for both, so an English rendering
would make the sentence say the fruit is called an apple or an apple. Both words
are kept in italic Latin (Song ruling, case 2 — the gloss IS the naming). No
marker: this is an editorial retention, not carried defective type.

## 5. Scripture divergences — LOGGED, NO MARKER, and why

Declined deliberately. A blind read will look at each of these.

- **1207B, Isa. II, 12 + 16** — *Dies Domini exercituum super omnem superbum et
  excelsum, et super omne quod visu pulchrum est.* A conflation of v. 12 and
  v. 16. **Migne's own note says `(Isa. II, 12, 16)`** — the divergence is
  already adjudicated on the page (the Nicetas precedent).
- **1207B, Isa. III, 18–19** — clause order rearranged, *et lunulas* → *lunulas*.
  Word order and a dropped connective; nothing asserted differently.
- **1207C, Psal. XLIV, 14** — *circumamicta **varietate*** for Vulg.
  *varietatibus*. Number only, and the gloss (*virtutum omnium varia
  diversitate*) works either way.
- **1207D, Luc. XV, 21** — *Pater, non sum dignus … fac me sicut unum ex
  mercenariis tuis.* Conflates v. 21 with v. 19 and reads *ex* for *de*. A
  composite of one speech, in a passage adduced for the shame of the prodigal;
  no clause asserts anything the Vulgate does not.
- **1209A–B, Gen. III, 6–7** — word order transposed and *qui comedit* dropped
  after *deditque viro suo*. **Nearest to the line in this list**: the plate's
  ellipsis has the eyes of both open without the man's eating being stated.
  Declined because the omission reads as citational abridgment (Foliot's
  argument is about Eve's eye, not Adam's eating) rather than a divergent
  reading; flagged here so a blind read can reverse the call.
- **1210B, I Pet. I, 12** — *in **quam** desiderant et angeli prospicere*, case
  and connective adapted to Foliot's own relative clause (*haec pulchritudo …
  in quam*). Weaving, not divergence.
- **1211D and 1217C, Isa. XIV, 14** — *Ponam sedem meam ad aquilonem, et ero
  similis Altissimo*, twice, against Vulg. *sedebo in monte testamenti, in
  lateribus aquilonis … ero similis Altissimo* (vv. 13–14). Declined: this is the
  standing patristic form of the boast, the asserted content is identical, and no
  part of Foliot's gloss rests on *ponam sedem*. Recorded because it recurs and
  because a marker on one occurrence and not the other would be incoherent.
- **1213A, Joan. VI, 71–72** — *Filius Dei **vivi***, an added word (harmonizing
  to Matt. XVI, 16) and a well-attested Vulgate variant.
- **1213A, Jac. IV, 6** — *humilibus **vero*** for *autem*.
- **1213A, Psal. LXIV, 14** — the guillemet opens **before** *quia*, so Foliot's
  own connective sits inside the quotation. Rendered inside it ("that the valleys
  shall abound…"), per Pattern 6.
- **1215B, Matth. XXV, 31–32** — condensed from two verses to one clause.
- **1216B, Deut. XXXII, 32–33** — *botri amarissimi* (pl.) for *botrus
  amarissimus*.
- **1218A, III Reg. XXII, 20–22** — the Lord's *In quo?* and the spirit's *Ego
  decipiam illum* are dropped; **and Migne closes *cadat in Ramoth Galaad*
  with a period where the Vulgate has `?`.** Pattern 8: the mark is NOT supplied.
- **1218B, Sap. XI, 25** — word order, and *Domine* added.
- **1219A, Matth. XXII, 37** — recast by Foliot into the first plural
  (*Diligamus*) inside guillemets; his adaptation, rendered as printed.
- **1219D, Prov. VI, 16** — *quae odit **Deus*** for *Dominus*.
- **1220B, Exod. XX, 12** — truncated at *super terram*; *matrem* without *tuam*.
- **1216D–1217A, Prov. IX, 1** — the quotation reads *Sapientia **aedificavit**
  sibi domum*, and Foliot's own gloss six lines later re-quotes it as *sibi
  « domum **construxit**, »*. Two different verbs for one lemma **in Migne**;
  both rendered as printed ("hath built" / "constructed"). No marker: the second
  is Foliot's paraphrase inside his own sentence, not a scripture citation.

## 6. Plate defects, carried type, and hard readings

- **@1209A — `[sic: *animi*]`** (0020). Migne prints *Sitivit **animi** mea ad
  Deum*. *animi* is a real form but has no grammatical slot: *mea* is feminine
  singular and *sitivit* singular, so nothing in the clause can govern it
  (Pattern 12, test 1). Almost certainly *anima*. Carried in a marker; **7a⁗
  checked — the verb (*sitivit*) survives outside the quarantine and the clause
  still predicates**, so no sense had to be supplied beside it. The clause reads
  "My [sic: *animi*] hath thirsted for God…".
- **@1207A — a full stop mid-sentence.** *…triplici jam magnificavit elogio.
  dicens,* — a period followed by a lowercase participle. **Preserved**
  (Pattern 8, never delete). The English reads "…with a threefold commendation.
  saying,". Same shape at **1213B** (*« Ego flos campi. » qui etiam mundo…*) and
  **1208C** (*…quae poterit esse vexatio? qui nec praesens aderit*), both
  preserved. Almost certainly a comma in the compositor's copy in each case.
- **@1208D — a clause with no verb.** *quae irrationabiliter non irascitur, nec
  dicto, nec facto quemquam sed quantumcunque offensa…* — *quemquam* is an
  accusative with nothing to govern it; a verb of harming (*laedit*, matching
  *nec rostro laedit* eight words earlier) has dropped out. **Rendered as
  printed**, gap and all: "…who is not angered unreasonably, nor anyone by word,
  nor by deed, but however greatly offended…". No verb supplied (Pattern 7);
  no `[sic:]`, because nothing is broken — something is absent, and there is no
  run to carry. Not `[cj:]` either: Pattern 18 opens on a word Migne DID print,
  and here he printed none.
- **@1211D — an ambiguous purpose clause.** *…malignos spiritus enecat, ne
  bonorum corda perturbent, ne a bonis initiis ad majora consurgant.* The
  grammatical subject of the second *ne* clause is still *spiritus*, which makes
  the devils the ones rising from good beginnings; the sense wants the good to
  be the ones prevented from rising. **Rendered literally**, keeping the
  ambiguity and both negatives ("lest they disturb the hearts of the good, lest
  they rise from good beginnings to greater things"). No negative was added or
  removed to resolve it.
- **@1211D — the lemma re-quoted short.** Lemma 16 prints *laquearia **nostra**
  cupressina*; the re-quotation eleven lines later prints *laquearia
  cupressina*, without *nostra*. **Both rendered as printed** ("our panelled
  ceilings of cypress" / "the panelled ceilings of cypress"). This is the exact
  shape 7a″ warns about, and the difference is Migne's, not ours.
- **@1215C — *ut mihi Sponsa promissa compleret*.** *Sponsa* is nominative (or
  ablative) where the sense wants a dative agreeing with *mihi*. Rendered "that
  he might fulfil to me, being his Bride, the things promised", reading *Sponsa*
  as a loose nominative in apposition to the speaking Church. No marker: a case
  slip has no English exponent (Pattern 9), and the reading is recoverable.
- **@1216C — a dangling genitive.** *Hic enim sensus est; **Reproborum**, qui
  sanctas Scripturas prave intelligendo…* — *Reproborum* depends on a noun
  (*vinum*) that is not printed. **Rendered with the gap visible** ("For this is
  the sense; of the reprobate, who…"). The word was not supplied.
- **@1216C — *praeduces*.** *…in inferno praeduces ipsi mansiones habere
  compellunt.* An unusual form; taken as *praedux* used adjectivally with
  *mansiones*, "foremost dwellings". Rendered so. Not marked: it construes.
- **@1218A — *non horum approbans malignitate*.** *malignitate* (ablative) where
  *approbans* wants the accusative *malignitatem*; the parallel *quod ab ipso
  est approbans* follows immediately. **No marker, no repair**: English nouns
  have no case exponent, so "their malignity" is the only possible rendering
  either way — Pattern 9's class, logged so a later sweep does not churn it.
- **@1208A — *Sic sic pulchram nominat*.** NOT a Pattern 11 dittography.
  Emphatic *sic sic* is idiomatic Latin, and the doubling is rhetorical, matching
  the doubled lemma it glosses (*ecce tu pulchra … ecce tu pulchra*). Rendered
  "So, so does he name her beautiful". Declined deliberately.
- **@1212B — *seraphin*** for *seraphim*, three columns after *Seraphim*
  @1210A. Attested medieval spelling (Pattern 9): normalized to "seraphim" in
  the English, preserved in the facing Latin. No marker.
- **@1213C — *sponsam* lowercase** where Migne capitalizes *Sponsa* everywhere
  else in the range. Rendered lowercase ("the bride"), mirroring the plate, on
  the same principle as *Dilectus*/*dilectus* in §1.

## 7. Guillemets — two deliberate imbalances, both Migne's

`verify-english` parity is met chunk by chunk, including where the plate itself
does not balance (Pattern 5 corollary: unclosed quotations stay unclosed).

- **0022 @1213C — 29 open / 28 close.** *« In propria venit, et sui eum non
  receperunt [n: (Joan. I, 11)]: quos « suos, » non affectu…* — the Joan. I, 11
  quotation is opened and never closed; the inner *« suos, »* closes normally.
  Mirrored exactly. Do not supply the missing `»`.
- **0024 @1217C — 25 open / 24 close.** *« Deus » enim « charitas est [n: (Joan.
  I, 4, 8)], omnem procul dubio diligens creaturam…* — the second quotation
  opens and runs on unclosed into Foliot's own clause. Mirrored exactly.
- **0024 @1217C** also prints the closing `»` **after** the note and after a
  closing parenthesis that opened outside the quotation: *…et ero similis
  Altissimo) [n: (Isai. XIV, 14)] »*. Reproduced in that order.
- **0023 @1217A** — *« Misit ancillas suas, ut vocarent ad arcem, et ad moenia
  civitatis quia, ut ait apostolus, Non multi sapientes…* — the Prov. IX, 3
  quotation is not closed before Foliot's gloss and the I Cor. I citation begins;
  the whole run closes once, at *destrueret. »*. Reproduced; the English
  therefore also carries a gloss inside the quotation marks.

## 8. Inline citation at 1217B — not a Pattern 4 `[f: …]`

0024 prints *« Ego sum vitis, vos palmites `*(Joan. XV, [1217B] 5)*`. »* — a
citation set inline in italics inside the quotation, with a column anchor
falling inside it, rather than as an `[n: …]` note. Reproduced **verbatim,
untranslated, anchor in place**, on the `[n: …]` principle. **No `[f: …]` tag**:
this is a lemma-and-gloss commentary, not a florilegium (runbook false positive
4). It is the only inline citation in the range.
