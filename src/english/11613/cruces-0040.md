# Cruces — 11613 (Gilbert Foliot, *Expositio in Cantica canticorum*), chunks 0040–0043

Columns 1268A–1279D (Cant. V, 1–14). Column + printed reading + note. Divergences from the
Vulgate are listed whether or not a `[var: …]` was taken; where a call was declined the reason
is given, because a flagged passage is not a checked passage.

Conventions inherited from wave 1 (`11613-MERGE-NOTES.md`) and **not re-decided here**:
`amica` = **friend** · `sponsa` = Bride / `sponsus` = Bridegroom, mirroring Migne's own
capitalization · `dilectus` = beloved, capital `Dilectus` = the Beloved, again mirrored ·
`pulcher`/`pulchra` = **beautiful**, never "fair", `pulcherrima` = "most beautiful" ·
`nigra` = **black** (the *Nigra sum, sed formosa* word — see §3 below).

## 1. The named Song lemmata and their divergences (7a″ deliverable)

### 0040 @1268A — 1. *Veni in hortum meum, soror mea sponsa; messui myrrham meam cum aromatibus meis.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 1):** identical. **No divergence, no `[var:]`.**
- *messui* rendered **"I have reaped"** rather than "I have gathered" because the gloss is built
  on the harvest image: *Quadam falce mortis praescidi* — "with a certain sickle of death I have
  cut them off." "Gathered" (the familiar English) would leave the sickle explaining nothing.
- **Re-quotations, all held to the lemma:** « Veni in hortum meum. » · « Soror mea sponsa, »
  (twice) · « Messui myrrham meam, … » · « Cum aromatibus meis, ».
- ⚠ **Guillemet imbalance carried, not closed:** « Messui myrrham meam, opens at 1268B and Migne
  never closes it before the next opening « Cum aromatibus meis, ». Mirrored as printed
  (31 open / 29 close across the chunk).

### 0040 @1268C — *Comedi favum cum melle meo; bibi vinum meum cum lacte meo.* / *Comedite, amici, et bibite, et inebriamini charissimi.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 1):** identical apart from *charissimi* for *carissimi* (orthography, not a
  reading) and Migne's **dropped comma before *charissimi***. Punctuation rides as printed
  (Pattern 8), so the English reads "and be inebriated most dear ones" with no comma; the gloss's
  re-quotation « inebriamini charissimi, » is punctuated the same way. **No `[var:]`.**
- *amici* → "friends", *charos* → "dear", *charissimi/charissimos* → "most dear", so that the
  gloss's own three-step ladder (*amicos* … *charos* … *charissimos*) survives in English.

### 0040 @1268D — 2. *Ego dormio, et cor meum vigilat.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 2):** identical. **No `[var:]`.**
- **Re-quotations:** « Ego dormio. » (four times, all "I sleep") and the split pair at 1270B,
  « Cor » tamen « meum, » … ipsa « vigilat, ». ⚠ **The split is Migne's and is preserved as two
  guillemet units, not merged into one**: English "« The heart » however « that is mine, » …
  this same « watcheth »". Merging them would have read better and would have destroyed a
  guillemet pair.

### 0040 @1270B — *Vox dilecti mei pulsantis, aperi, soror mea, amica, columba mea, immaculata mea.* — ⭐ **`[var:]` TAKEN**
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 2):** *Vox dilecti mei pulsantis: **Aperi mihi**, soror mea, amica **mea**,
  columba mea, immaculata mea.*
- Migne's lemma drops **both** the dative *mihi* and the possessive *mea* after *amica*.
- **`[var: Vulg. *Aperi mihi, soror mea, amica mea*, "Open to me, my sister, my friend"]` taken.**
  Ground: the missing *mihi* is not cosmetic. Foliot's whole gloss is that the Bridegroom asks to
  be admitted **to himself** — *sibi postulat aperiri* … « Aperi mihi, soror mea; » … *habitaculum
  quaerenti in te* — and he restores *mihi* in his very first re-quotation, four lines below the
  lemma he printed without it. The reader of the page needs to see that the lemma and the gloss
  do not agree.
- ⚑ **This is a second sighting of the merge-notes' 0009 shape** (a work quoting itself and
  diverging from itself), but here it took the marker cleanly, because the received reading
  *Aperi mihi, soror mea, amica mea* is **not** printed verbatim in the Latin twin — Migne's own
  re-quotation stops at *soror mea*. The mirror test therefore did not fire. Worth noting when
  Wilson rules on the 0009 gap: the blocker is the *length* of the overlap, not the fact of
  self-quotation.
- **Re-quotation order also differs from the lemma** and is preserved: the lemma runs *soror,
  amica, columba, immaculata*; the gloss at 1270C runs « soror mea, amica mea, immaculata mea,
  columba mea es. » — dove and undefiled transposed. Internal re-quotation variance, **no
  second `[var:]`**, per the *Adjuro vos* precedent established at 0025–0029.

### 0040 @1270C — *Quia caput meum plenum est rore, et cincinni mei guttis noctium.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 2):** identical. **No `[var:]`.**
- ⚠ Second unclosed guillemet of the chunk: « Quia caput meum plenum est rore. opens at 1271A and
  runs to the end of the sentence without a closing mark. Mirrored.

### 0040 @1271B — 3. *Exspoliavi me tunica mea, quomodo induar illa? lavi pedes meos, quomodo inquinabo illos?*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 3):** *Ex(s)poliavi me tunica mea**:** quomodo induar illa? lavi pedes
  meos**:** quomodo inquinabo illos?*
- Two differences, neither a reading: *Exspoliavi* for *Expoliavi* (orthography), and Migne's
  **commas where the Vulgate sets colons**. Carried as printed (Pattern 8). **No `[var:]`.**
- **Both question marks are Migne's and both ride.** The gloss then repeats the pair verbatim
  and adds four more direct questions of its own (*illam quonam modo jam induam?* · *in quonam
  modo complectar quod nesciam?* · *Has quonammodo inquinabo?* · *qualiter … inclinabo?*), so the
  chunk carries **11 question marks against the Latin's 11**. Verified by count, not by eye.

### 0041 @1271D — 4. *Dilectus meus misit manum suam per foramen, et venter meus intremuit ad tactum ejus.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 4):** identical. **No `[var:]`.**
- *venter* rendered **"belly"**, not the familiar "bowels", because the gloss is anatomical and
  literal: *Venter namque osse caret, firmitate nulla participat, quod intromittitur hoc recipit,
  et decoctum reddit* — a thing with no bone that receives food and renders it digested. "Bowels"
  would make Foliot's physiology unreadable. *foramen* = "the hole", for the same reason: Ambrose
  glosses it as *oculus mentis*, and a "key hole" (Douay) imports a lock the gloss does not have.
- ⚠ **Third unclosed guillemet in the range:** the final « Venter meus intremuit ad tactum ejus.
  at 1272B is never closed. Mirrored (8 open / 7 close).

### 0042 @1272C — 5. *Surrexi, ut aperirem dilecto meo: manus meae stillaverunt myrrham.* / *Et digiti mei pleni myrrha probatissima.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 5):** identical (Migne splits the verse into two lemmata; that is
  segmentation, not a variant — the *curremus* precedent relayed at 0009/0010).
- *probata / probatior / probatissima* rendered **"approved / more approved / most approved"**
  rather than Douay's "choicest", because the gloss builds an explicit three-step comparison
  (*cum uxore … cum vidua continente … cum virgine sancta permanente*) that a superlative-only
  English cannot carry.

### 0042 @1273A — 6. *Pessulum ostii mei aperui dilecto meo.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 6):** identical. **No `[var:]`.**
- **The re-quotation inverts the word order** — « Ostii mei aperui pessulum, » for the lemma's
  *Pessulum ostii mei aperui* — and the inversion is preserved in English ("Of my door I opened
  the bolt"). Not harmonized: Migne prints two orders and the reader should see both.

### 0042 @1275D–1276A — 7. *Invenerunt me custodes qui circumeunt civitatem; percusserunt me, et vulneraverunt me, tulerunt pallium meum mihi custodes murorum.* 8. *Adjuro vos, filiae Jerusalem, si inveneritis Dilectum meum, ut nuntietis ei quia amore langueo.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 7–8):** identical, including the capital *Dilectum* being Migne's own
  (the Vulgate prints lowercase). Capitalization mirrored, per the wave-1 convention. **No `[var:]`.**

### ⛔ 0042 vs 0033 — *custodes qui circumeunt* against *vigiles qui custodiunt*: THE PAIR IS REAL, AND IT IS NOT A DEFECT
The launch brief flagged that a peer rendered « Invenerunt me **vigiles qui custodiunt**
civitatem » at 0033 while my 0042 prints « Invenerunt me **custodes qui circumeunt** civitatem ».
Checked, and the answer is clean:

**These are two different verses of the Song, and Migne's Latin agrees with the Vulgate in both.**
- **Cant. III, 3** (chunk 0033) is *Invenerunt me **vigiles** qui **custodiunt** civitatem.*
- **Cant. V, 7** (chunk 0042) is *Invenerunt me **custodes** qui **circumeunt** civitatem.*

So neither chunk has a variant, and **the two must stay visibly distinct in English** — not
because we are preserving a defect, but because the Song itself says two different things two
chapters apart, and Foliot glosses them differently (at 0033 the *vigiles* are angels who teach
the law of confession; at 0043 the *custodes civitatis* are the Church's Prelates and the
*custodes murorum* are the angels).

**Renderings taken here, offered to the merge as the reconciliation:**
- *custodes* → **"keepers"** (0042 lemma, and every one of the ~10 occurrences in 0043's gloss,
  including *custodes Ecclesiae*, *custodes murorum*, *custodes Ecclesiarum*).
- *circumeunt* → **"go about"** (and *circumeuntes* in the 1277A gloss → "going about", so the
  gloss visibly picks the lemma's verb back up).
- *vigiles* → **"watchmen"** and *custodiunt* → **"keep"** are what 0033 needs to stay distinct.
⚑ **Merge action:** confirm 0033 did not also use "keepers" for *vigiles*. If it did, 0033 should
move to "watchmen", not 0042 to something else — because 0043's gloss expounds *custodes* for
two whole columns and needs the noun on the page.

### 0043 @1277B — 9. *Qualis est Dilectus tuus ex Dilecto, o pulcherrima mulierum? Qualis est Dilectus tuus ex Dilecto, quia sic adjurasti nos?* 10. *Dilectus meus candidus et rubicundus, electus ex millibus.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 9–10):** identical except that Migne **capitalizes all four *Dilectus/
  Dilecto*** where the Vulgate prints them lowercase. Mirrored exactly ("thy Beloved from the
  Beloved"), per the wave-1 convention. **No `[var:]`** — capitalization is Migne's editorial
  hand, and the parallel Latin column preserves it, so nothing is concealed.
- ⚠ ***Dilectus tuus ex Dilecto* was deliberately NOT smoothed.** "What manner of one is thy
  Beloved from the Beloved" is opaque in English and is opaque in Latin; every modern version
  paraphrases it ("more than another beloved"). Foliot treats the doubling as the passage's
  difficulty and answers it with the two natures (*geminam in Christo … naturam*), so a smoothed
  English would remove the thing he is explaining. Rendered literally, twice, identically.
- **Re-quotations, all held:** « O pulcherrima mulierum! » (twice, with Migne's exclamation marks
  both times) · « O pulcherrima mulierum! Qualis est Dilectus tuus? » (an **abridged** re-quotation
  stopping before *ex Dilecto* — carried as printed, no marker) · « Qualis est Dilectus tuus ex
  Dilecto, quia sic adjurasti nos? Dilectus meus candidus et rubicundus. » · « Dilectus meus
  candidus et rubicundus; » · « Candidus est: » · « Et rubicundus. » · « Dilectus meus candidus,
  et rubicundus, » (with a comma the lemma lacks) · « Electus ex millibus. »
- **Migne prints a full stop, not a question mark, on the direct question at 1277D** — *Quo signo
  nobis elucidas … quem et tu diligis.* Carried as printed (Pattern 8, and the wave-1 note that
  *nunquid* questions in this work close with full stops). Do not "fix" it.
- ⚠ *duodenario … numero* — Foliot says the description is comprehended "under the number
  twelve" and then names two things. Rendered as printed; he is counting the twelve members of
  the whole *descriptio* that runs from V, 10 to V, 16, not the two natures.

### 0043 @1278C — 11. *Caput ejus aurum optimum. Comae ejus sicut elatae palmarum, nigrae quasi corvus.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 11):** identical (Vulgate sets a semicolon where Migne sets a full stop
  after *optimum*; carried as printed). **No `[var:]`.**
- ⭐ ***nigrae* → "black", the same word as 0011's *Nigra sum, sed formosa***, per the launch
  brief. Here it is hair and not the Bride, and Foliot's own gloss makes the echo the point:
  *quia ii etsi virtute proficiant, **nigros** se ut corvos fore protestantur* — the faithful
  confess themselves black even as they advance. A different English word here (swarthy, dark)
  would hide the connection from exactly the reader who would notice it.
- *elatae palmarum* → **"the topmost boughs of palm trees"**, not Douay's "branches", because
  Foliot glosses the word: *Elatae palmarum partes in palma superiores dicuntur* — "the higher
  parts in the palm." "Branches" would leave that sentence saying nothing.

### 0043 @1279A — 12. *Oculi ejus sicut columbae super rivulos aquarum, quae lacte sunt lotae, et resident juxta fluenta plenissima.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 12):** identical. **No `[var:]`.**
- *fluenta plenissima* → **"the fullest streams"** rather than "plentiful streams", because the
  gloss puns the superlative straight across: *juxta antiquorum Patrum **scripta plenissima***.
  Keeping one English word in both places is what makes the gloss legible.

### 0043 @1279B — 13. *Genae illius sicut areolae aromatum consitae a pigmentariis.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 13):** identical apart from Migne dropping the Vulgate's comma before
  *consitae*. **No `[var:]`.**
- The gloss quotes the lemma **in three separate guillemet fragments out of order** — « Areolae »
  … « a pigmentariis, » … « consitae sunt, » — with the verb turned from participle to finite.
  All three preserved as three units; the English word order follows Migne's, not English's.

### 0043 @1279C — *Labia ejus lilia distillantia myrrham primam.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 13):** identical. **No `[var:]`.**
- The gloss re-quotes with *stillantia* for the lemma's *distillantia*; both render "dropping"
  in English (there is no English exponent for the prefix here). Recorded, no marker.

### 0043 @1279D — 14. *Manus illius tornatiles aureae, plenae hyacinthis.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. V, 14):** identical apart from the Vulgate's comma after *tornatiles*.
  **No `[var:]`.** The gloss falls in chunk 0044, outside this range — flagged for whoever has it,
  since the lemma rendering "turned and golden" must be held there.

## 2. Non-Song scripture — divergences, taken and declined

### 0041 @1272A — Psal. XLI, 3, *Sitivit anima mea ad Deum vivum* — **`[var:]` DECLINED**
- **Vulgate:** *Sitivit anima mea ad Deum **fortem** vivum.*
- Migne drops *fortem*. **Declined**, and the reason matters for consistency: Pattern 14's bar is
  a divergence "in a way that changes the sense," and a dropped divine epithet in a proof-text
  whose whole point is the verb *sitivit* does not reach it. **Recorded here so the merge can
  overrule if it prefers the lower bar** — but note that taking it here would require taking the
  next item too, and the two should stand or fall together.

### 0041 @1272A — Psal. CXVIII, 119, *Mirabilia testimonia tua, **Domine**, ideo scrutata est ea anima mea* — **`[var:]` DECLINED, and a wrong citation kept**
- **Vulgate (Ps. CXVIII, 129):** *Mirabilia testimonia tua: ideo scrutata est ea anima mea.*
  Migne inserts a vocative *Domine* the psalm does not have. Same class as the item above,

- ⚠ **The citation is also wrong: Migne prints 119, and the verse is 129.** Ps. CXVIII, 119 is

  the most plausible reading, but "most plausible" is not evidence, and no *Ibid.* depends on it
  either way (the next note is a fresh *Psal. XCII, 5*), so nothing downstream is at risk from

### 0042 @1275D — Joan. XVII, 24, *Volo, Pater, ut ubi ego sum, illic sit et minister meus* — ⭐ **`[var:]` TAKEN**
- **Vulgate (Joan. XVII, 24):** *Pater, quos dedisti mihi, volo ut ubi sum ego, et illi sint
  mecum.*
- Migne's quotation ends with *illic sit et minister meus*, which is the close of **Joan. XII, 26**

- **`[var: Vulg. *ut ubi sum ego, et illi sint mecum*, "that they also may be with me"]` taken.**
  This one does change the sense and does change the argument: Foliot is proving that the elect

  may be with me"), while the printed conflation narrows it to the single *minister*. **No claim
  is made in the marker about *why* the two texts have fused** (Pattern 14 forbids it); the
  XII, 26 identification is recorded here in the crux, where it belongs.

## 3. Defects in the plate — carried type, conjectures, and what was declined

### 0042 @1273A — ⭐ `[cj: *discerptionis*; read *discretionis*, "of discretion"]` — **TAKEN**
> *Quo in loco virtutem « discerptionis » aperit: per digitos enim, **discretio** demonstratur.*

**Pattern 18 exactly.** *discerptio* is a real Latin word ("a tearing to pieces"), so Pattern 7
renders it and Pattern 12 has nothing to wrap — and the English then says Foliot opened "the
virtue of tearing apart," which is not a virtue and is not what the next six words of his own
sentence say. *discretio* stands in the very next clause, and the whole paragraph is about
measure and discernment (*ne … modus excedatur*). The gloss is **additive**: Migne's word keeps
its English and the conjecture stands beside it.

### 0042 @1273B — *possulum* carried, `[sic:]` not used
> *sicque **possulum** ostii amovisse*

For *pessulum*, which Migne prints correctly in the lemma one paragraph above and again at 1273C
(*pessulum ut aperiret*). **Not a word**, so it is carried in italics untranslated under
Pattern 7's non-word ruling, in a sentence that keeps its verb and its sense on either side of it
(7a⁗ question 1 checked: the run is a bare object, it governs nothing, and *ostii amovisse*
survives). No `[sic:]` because the type is carried, not glossed — the italic *possulum* IS the
marking.

### 0043 @1279A — *colombae* carried inside guillemets
> *sunt « sicut **colombae** quae lacte sunt lotae. »*

For *columbae*, printed correctly in the lemma four lines above. Non-word, carried in italics.
⚠ **It sits inside a guillemet quotation and takes no second italic marker** — nested italics are
not expressible, and this is the case Pattern 10 names. Sense is unaffected: the doves are named
in the lemma immediately above and the gloss's subject (*viri spirituales*) is intact.

### 0043 @1279D — *expreverunt* carried
> *et illis peccantibus … **id expreverunt**, et se Deo … devoventes*

Not a Latin word. Almost certainly *spreverunt* ("they spurned it"), which is what the sentence
requires — the good angels rejected the rebels' course. **Carried in italics, and the conjecture
is deliberately NOT put in the text**: unlike the *discerptionis* case, this is a non-word, so
Pattern 10 governs and `[cj:]` is unavailable (it requires the printed word to be real).
⚠ **7a⁗ check run, and it passes narrowly:** the quarantined run is the clause's only verb, but
the clause's force survives in the two participial limbs on either side (*peccantibus … volentibus*
before, *devoventes … calcaverunt* after), so the English does not assert anything false. Flagged
rather than repaired.

### 0042 @1276A — *ei **vestra** obstet gratia* — **`[cj:]` DECLINED, and here is why**
The sentence is third-person about God throughout (*Clamat … ad Deum omnis anima electa*), and
then a second-person **plural** possessive appears: "that **your** grace may withstand him."
*nostra* is the obvious conjecture. **Declined**, on the ground that the printed word is real, is
grammatical, and has an intelligible reading as it stands: the sentence has just quoted a direct
address to God (« Deus, in adjutorium meum intende ») and slides into continuing that address,
which is a thing this author does elsewhere. Pattern 18 requires the English to assert something
**false**; a pronoun shift mid-sentence is odd, not false. Rendered as printed ("your grace"),
Pattern 17 respected (the Latin is plural, so the English is plural). **Named here because a
blind reader will stop at it**, and if Wilson prefers the lower bar the fix is one `[cj:]`.

### 0040 @1269D–1270A — the sentence with no main clause
> *Cui cum in corde vigilavit ad intelligentiam, qui sibi septem annorum futuram copiam; et septem
> annorum inediam declaravit.*

Two relatives (*Cui … qui*) and no principal verb: the limb that should say "Joseph was the one
who watched in heart for him" is simply absent from the plate. **Rendered ending where Migne
ends, deliberately unmarked** — `[ed:]` covers only what OUR digitization lost, and nothing here
is lost on our side. Same class as the 1159D finding already in the wave-1 notes; **worth
Wilson's eye alongside it, since that now makes two apodosis-less sentences in one work.**

### 0040 @1269C — *anima **qua** cordis sedes est*
*qua* where *quae* is wanted ("the soul which is the seat of the heart"). Rendered as an ablative
of place, "the soul wherein is the seat of the heart," which is what the printed case allows.
Compare 1270B, where Foliot writes the construction the other way round and correctly:
*Dei sapientia, **cui** cor meum sedes est*. Recorded, unmarked — a case ending is a gender/
morphology matter with a defensible literal rendering, and Pattern 9's spirit applies.

### 0042 @1273A — *quia post digiti tactu tangitur, id certa quadam ratione mentis intelligitur*
Does not construe: *post* is stranded and the antecedent of *id* is unclear. Rendered as closely
as the printed words allow ("because after it is touched by the touch of the finger, that is
understood by a certain sure reasoning of the mind"). No conjecture offered — more than one
repair is available (*quod* for *quia*, a lost *quod* after *post*), and guessing between them
would be the silent-smoothing Pattern 7 forbids.

### 0040 @1268B — *abcessisse* for *abscessisse*

## 5. The homograph watch — *mala*

⛔ Checked deliberately, per the brief. **The apple sense does not occur in 0040–0043 at all**;
the Song's *mala* belong to chapters II and VIII. The *malus, -a, -um* forms that do occur
