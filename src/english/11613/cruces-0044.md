# Cruces — 11613 (Gilbert Foliot, *Expositio in Cantica canticorum*), chunks 0044–0048

Columns 1280A–1295C (Cant. V, 14 – VII, 11). Column + printed reading + note. Divergences from
the Vulgate are listed whether or not a `[var: …]` was taken; where a call was declined the
reason is given, because a flagged passage is not a checked passage.

Conventions inherited from waves 1–2 (`11613-MERGE-NOTES.md`) and **not re-decided here**:
`amica` = **friend** · `sponsa` = Bride / `sponsus` = Bridegroom · `dilectus` = beloved, capital
`Dilectus` = the Beloved, **Migne's own varying capitalization mirrored, never normalized** ·
`pulcher`/`pulchra` = **beautiful**, never "fair"; `pulcherrima` = "most beautiful" ·
`caprea` = **roe** (0047 lemma 4) · `mammae` and `ubera` both = **breasts** (the declared
flattening) · numbers spelled plainly ("sixty", "eighty", 0045 @1284A).

## 1. The named Song lemmata and their divergences (7a″ deliverable)

### 0044 @1280C — *Venter ejus eburneus, distinctus sapphiris.*
- **Vulg. Cant. V, 14b:** identical. No `[var:]`.
- Re-quotations held: « eburneus, » @1280C · *Quae quidem Sapphiris distincta est* @1280D
  ("set with sapphires" both times).
- ⚠ Gender: *Quae quidem … distincta est … adornatum* @1280D–1281A — feminine relative and
  participle for masculine *venter*, then a neuter participle in the same sentence. **Pattern 9:
  no English exponent, English untouched, logged so a later sweep does not churn it.**

### 0044 @1281A — 15. *Crura illius columnae marmoreae, quae fundatae sunt super bases aureas.*
- **Vulg. Cant. V, 15:** identical. No `[var:]`.
- Re-quotation held: *basibus aureis* → "bases of gold" in the gloss, matching the lemma.

### 0044 @1281A — *Species ejus ut Libani, electus ut cedri.*
- **Vulg. Cant. V, 15b:** identical. No `[var:]`.
- **`species` = "fairness"**, following **0021 @1210B**, which renders the triad *Haec forma, haec
  species, haec pulchritudo* "This is the form, this the fairness, this the beauty." That fixes
  the family for the whole range: *species* = fairness, *speciosus* = fair (see §4).
- **`Libanus` kept, not "Lebanon"** — 0037 and 0039 already print Libanus, and the gloss is the
  etymology (*est enim Libanus, « Candidatio »* → « a whitening »), which a modern place-name
  would leave explaining nothing.
- Re-quotation held: « electus ut cedri. » @1281B = "chosen as the cedars", and `electus` stays
  **"chosen"** through 0045's *electa genitrici suae* and *electa ut sol*.

### 0044 @1281C — 16. *Guttur illius suavissimum, et totus desiderabilis: talis est Dilectus meus, et ipse est amicus meus, filiae Jerusalem.*
- **Vulg. Cant. V, 16:** identical; Migne capitalizes *Dilectus*, mirrored.
- ⭐ **This is the masculine twin of the `amica` ruling, and both words stand in one line:**
  *talis est **Dilectus** meus, et ipse est **amicus** meus*. Rendered "such is my **Beloved**,
  and he is my **friend**", so *amicus* takes the same English as the ratified *amica* = friend
  and the two Song terms stay visibly distinct in the one sentence where they meet.
- Re-quotation held verbatim @1281D: « Talis est Dilectus meus, et ipse est amicus meus, filiae
  Jerusalem. » — capital *Dilectus* again, mirrored.
- **`guttur` = "throat"** throughout, which also carries 0048's *Guttur tuum sicut vinum optimum*.

### 0044 @1281D — 17. *Quo abiit Dilectus tuus, o pulcherrima mulierum? Quo declinavit Dilectus tuus, et quaeremus eum tecum?*
- **Vulg. Cant. VI, 1:** identical. No `[var:]`.
- *pulcherrima* = **"most beautiful"** per the inherited rule; "of women" (genitive) keeps it
  distinct from 0016's *in mulieribus* "among women".
- Re-quotations held: « Quo abiit Dilectus tuus? » · « abire, » · « Quo declinavit Dilectus
  tuus, » — all three with the capital *Dilectus* Migne prints.
- ⚠ **@1282A *qui haec pulcherrima mulierum est*** — masculine *qui* with a feminine referent
  (*quae* expected). Rendered "since she is this most beautiful of women." **Pattern 9: Latin
  gender has no English exponent; no `[cj:]`, nothing to emend in English.**

### 0045 @1282A — 1. *Dilectus meus descendit in hortum suum ad areolam aromatum, ut pascatur in hortis, et lilia colligat.*
- **Vulg. Cant. VI, 1(2):** identical. No `[var:]`.
- *areola aromatum* = **"bed of aromatical spices"**, following 0043 @1279B (*areolae aromatum*
  → "beds of aromatical spices"). The diminutive is **not** argued by Foliot here, so it is not
  forced into "little bed"; contrast *lectulus* / *virgula*, which he does argue.
- Re-quotations held: *in areola aromatum* · *hujus aromaticae areolae* · *aromatum areola* ·
  *in his pascatur hortis* · *haec vere lilia … colligat*.

### 0045 @1283A — 2. *Ego Dilecto meo, et Dilectus meus mihi, qui pascitur inter lilia.*
- **Vulg. Cant. VI, 2(3):** identical. No `[var:]`. Both *Dilectus* capitalized here; contrast

### 0045 @1283A — 3. *Pulchra es, amica mea, suavis et decora sicut Jerusalem: terribilis ut castrorum acies ordinata.*
- **Vulg. Cant. VI, 3(4):** identical. No `[var:]`.
- ⭐ **The open merge collision resolved as instructed: `decora` = "comely."** It is Douay's word,
  0016 @1194C's precedent inside this work for Foliot's own *decora*, and 0030's ruling; and the
  gloss here depends on it (*unde patientiae **decorem** obtinet* @1283B → "the comeliness of
  patience"), as it does again at 0048 @1292D. **Recommendation for the merge, unchanged from
  0030's: *speciosa* at 0029 yields.** A word is free for it — see §4, where this range fixes
  *species* = "fairness" / *speciosus* = "fair" on 0021's precedent, so **0029's *speciosa mea*
  can become "my fair one"** with no further collision anywhere in the work.
- **`castrorum acies ordinata` = "an ordered line of battle of the camp."** Douay's "an army set
  in array" was rejected because Foliot *defines the phrase* @1283B–C — *Acies enim ordinata
  castrorum est, cum qui pugnaturi sunt … in castris* — and again glosses *castra* at 0047
  @1288D (*choros castrorum* → "the companies of the camps"). An English that drops the camp
  leaves two columns of gloss expounding a word not on the page.
- Re-quotation @1283B: « **terribilis est** ut castrorum acies ordinata. » — Migne inserts *est*,
  which his own lemma four lines above does not print. **Migne against himself: logged here, no
  `[var:]`**, per the standing rule that the witness list has no internal term.

### 0045 @1283C — 4. *Averte oculos tuos a me; quia ipsi me avolare fecerunt.*
- **Vulg. Cant. VI, 4(5):** identical. No `[var:]`.
- Re-quotations held: « Averte oculos tuos a me, » · « Quia ipsi me avolare fecerunt, ».
- ⛔ **@1283D — the range's one `[cj: …]` on a lemma gloss, and it is a lost negation.** Migne
  prints *… nec in cor hominis ascendit, ante communem hominum resurrectionem, et cum suis
  spiritibus coadunationem, **velle** cuiquam ostendere.* Rendered literally the clause says the
  Bridegroom **wills** to show his glory to someone before the general resurrection — which is
  the opposite of the whole paragraph, whose point is that the eyes made him *fly away* and that
  he remains *incomprehensibilis adhuc homini*. *velle* is a real word, so Pattern 12 has nothing
  to wrap; Pattern 18 covers exactly this, and its 2026-08-15 clause is decisive: **incoherence
  is not a defence where the word that went wrong is a negation.** Taken as
  `to will [cj: *velle*; read *nolle*, "not to will"]`. The alternative reading — that a negative
  is carried over by ellipsis from the three *nec*s — is possible and is why the gloss is
  additive rather than a substitution. **A plate read would settle it; a vote would not.**

### 0045 @1284A — 8. *Una est columba mea, perfecta mea, una est matris suae, electa genitrici suae.*
- **Vulg. Cant. VI, 8:** identical. No `[var:]`.
- ⭐ **`mater` and `genitrix` must stay two words, because Foliot argues the distinction**
  @1284C: *Haec vero genitrix, eadem quae et ejus mater est: diversitate tamen verborum, diversa
  denotat.* Fixed as **`mater` = "mother"**, **`genitrix` = "her that bore her"** (Douay's
  phrase), so the sentence that says the two words denote different things is readable.
- **`racemus` vs `botrus` @1284A** — *ex multis granis acervus unus, ex multis **racemis botrus**
  unus*. Fixed as **`racemus` = "berry"**, **`botrus` = "cluster"**, giving "out of many berries
  one cluster" — which is what the argument needs (many make one) and keeps `botrus` free for
  0048's *ubera tua botris* → "thy breasts to clusters," Douay's word.
- Re-quotations held: « Una est columba mea. » · « una » « matris suae, » · « Electa genitrici
  suae, » — the last with *ad modum genitricis suae* glossed "after the manner of her that bore
  her."

### 0045 @1284C — *Viderunt eam filiae, et beatissimam praedicaverunt: reginae et concubinae, et laudaverunt eam.*

### 0045 @1285A — 9. *Quae est ista quae progreditur quasi aurora consurgens, pulchra ut luna, electa ut sol, terribilis ut castrorum acies ordinata?*
- **Vulg. Cant. VI, 9:** identical. No `[var:]`.
- ⛔ **@1285B — `[sic: *progeditur*]`.** Migne's own re-quotation eleven lines below prints
  « Quae est ista quae ***progeditur*** quasi aurora consurgens? » for the lemma's *progreditur*.
  *progeditur* is **not a Latin word** (there is no *progedior*), so Pattern 7's non-word class
  applies and Pattern 12 marks it. ⚠ **7a⁗ applied:** the quarantined run is the clause's only
  verb, so the English supplies the sense beside the marker —
  "Who is she that **goeth forth** [sic: *progeditur*] as the dawn rising?" — rather than leaving
  a hole the size of the predicate.
- **`aurora`: the etymology is the gloss, so the Latin stays visible.** @1285B *Aurora dicitur,
  quasi hora aurea* is rendered "It is called *aurora*, as it were *hora aurea*, the golden
  hour," and *aurora* is "the dawn" everywhere else. Without the two Latin words on the page the
  pun — reinforced three words later by *auri more rutilans* — explains nothing.
- **Capitalization mirrored inside one sentence @1285B:** *quae **Solem** praeveniens **solem**
  affuturum denunciat* → "which, going before the **Sun**, announces that the **sun** is about to
  come." Migne's own inconsistency, reproduced, not normalized.
- Re-quotation held: « Pulchra ut luna. » @1285C.

## 2. ⛔ The *mala* homograph — every instance in the range, tabulated

The brief called this range the heaviest *mala* load in the work, and it is: **apples,
pomegranates and evils all occur, and 0046 puts the first two inside one lemma.** Decided from
case, gender and governing noun at every site. The pomegranate/apple contrast is **Foliot's own**
— a peer established it at 1265B (pomegranates = martyrs, apples = confessors) — and 0046
@1287B reproduces it exactly: nuts = the hearts of the saints, *poma* = the confessors who are
**not** made equal to the martyrs, *mala punica* = those who bud into suffering for Christ.

|---|---|---|---|
| 0046 @1286C | *germinassent **mala punica*** (lemma 10) | **pomegranates** | *punica* agrees; the fruit-name |
| 0046 @1287C | *de **malis** … **punicis*** | **pomegranates** | *punicis* agrees; same fruit re-quoted |
| 0046 @1286C | ***poma** convallium* (lemma 10) | **apples** | follows 0039's *poma* = "apples" |

## 3. Divergences from the Vulgate, and the `[var: …]` calls

**Taken (7).** Each opens by naming an external witness and is phrase-length, per the two
hard-won rules.

1. **0044 @1281C, Exod. XXI, 24** — Migne prints *Reddet **homo** animam pro anima*, supplying a
   subject the Vulgate does not have. `[var: Vulg. *reddet animam pro anima*, without a subject
   "man"]`. Kept because the added noun changes who is bound by the lex talionis, and Foliot's
   whole antithesis is Old-Testament *homo* against New-Testament *si quis*.
2. **0044 @1281C, Matth. V, 39** — Migne prints *Si quis percusserit te in **maxilla una**, praebe
   ei **maxillam alteram***, where the Vulgate specifies the **right** cheek.
   `[var: Vulg. *in dexteram maxillam tuam, praebe illi et alteram*, "on thy right cheek"]`.
   ⚑ Material in **this** work specifically: Foliot builds a sustained argument on *dextera*
   against *laeva*/*sinistra*, and the plate has removed a *dextera* from a proof-text.
3. **0045 @1282C, Deut. XVIII, 18** — *Prophetam suscitabo **vobis** de medio fratrum **vestrorum***
   for the Vulgate's *eis … suorum*. `[var: Vulg. *suscitabo eis de medio fratrum suorum*, "unto

## 4. Vocabulary fixed in this range, for the merge diff

- **`species` = "fairness" · `speciosus` = "fair"**, on **0021 @1210B**'s precedent (*Haec forma,
  haec species, haec pulchritudo* → "This is the form, this the fairness, this the beauty").
  Governs 0044's lemma *Species ejus ut Libani*, its gloss *Haec Deo species attribuitur*, the
  *habitationes speciosae* two lines above, and Ps. XLIV, 3 *Speciosus forma prae filiis hominum*
  → "Fair in form above the sons of men." ⭐ **This is what frees "comely" for *decora* without
  stranding *speciosa*:** with *speciosus* = "fair," **0029's *speciosa mea* can read "my fair
  one"** and all three adjectives — *pulchra* beautiful, *decora* comely, *speciosa* fair — stay
  distinct across the whole work.
- **`suavis` = "sweet"** (*Guttur illius **suavissimum*** → "most sweet"; *suavis et decora* →
  "sweet and comely"; *odorem suavissimum* → "a most sweet odour"), matching 0030's `dulcis` =
  "sweet". ⚠ **The two collide once, at 0044 @1281C–D**, where Migne stacks *Quid hoc **dulcius**?
  quid **suavius** esse potest?* — two comparatives of the same idea in one breath. Rendered
  "What is **sweeter** than this? what can be more **delightful**?" Declared here rather than
  left silent: English has no second word at the same register, and flattening both to "sweeter"
  would make Foliot's doublet look like a stammer.
- **`electus` = "chosen"** throughout (*electus ut cedri* 0044 · *electa genitrici suae*,
  *electa ut sol* 0045 · *quos eligit*, *electa in gloriam* in the glosses), never Douay's
  "excellent"/"bright", because Foliot glosses the election itself at 0045 @1284C.
- **`tornatilis` = "turned"** (0044 *manus … tornatiles* · 0047 *crater tornatilis*), with the
  *tornus* / lathe gloss in both chunks.
- **`caprea` = "roe"** and **`ubera` = "breasts"** — inherited, applied at 0047 @1290D.
- **`monilia`, `femina/femora`, `ager`/`agi`, `aurora`/`hora aurea`, `Libanus`** kept in italic
  Latin where Foliot's gloss is the etymology (0018's *murenulae* precedent).
- **`Sulamitis` = "Sulamitess"** across the 0046/0047 seam.
- **`castra`** = "camp(s)" in both *acies ordinata castrorum* (0045, 0046) and *chori castrorum*
  (0047), so Foliot's one image reads as one image.
- ⚠ **`synagoga` capitalization is Migne's and varies**: capital *Synagoga* through 0046 and at
  0047 @1288C, lowercase *synagogae* from 0047 @1290C through 0048 @1292D. **Mirrored, not
  normalized** — the same discipline the merge notes impose on *Dilectus*.

## 5. Plate defects and conjectures

- ⛔ **0045 @1285B — `[sic: *progeditur*]`**, a non-word for *progreditur*, quarantined under
  Patterns 10/12 with the verb's force supplied outside the marker per 7a⁗. See §1.
- ⛔ **0045 @1283D — `[cj: *velle*; read *nolle*, "not to will"]`**, a lost negation in a real
  word. See §1 for the full argument and the competing reading.
- ⛔ **0046 @1286B — `[cj: *lux carnis*; read *lex carnis*, "the law of the flesh"]`.** Migne
  prints *et quia **lux carnis** legem mentis percutit*, one clause after *Cum ergo **lex peccati**
  cum **lege mentis** contendit* — the Romans VII antithesis, stated correctly, then restated with
  *lex* become *lux*. Rendered literally the page says the flesh has a **light** that strikes the
  law of the mind, in a paragraph whose entire scheme is light = faith against darkness = flesh
  (*fides lux est, nox infidelitas*, eight lines below). Pattern 18 exactly: the type is not
  broken, so `[sic:]` cannot reach it, and the faithful English asserts something Foliot did not.
  Single competing conjecture, so the call was taken rather than declined.
- **Declined, 0046 @1286D — *venti **fiant** et irruunt in domum ejus*.** The Matt. VII, 25 echo
  has *flaverunt venti*, so *fiant* for *flant* is a plausible one-letter slip. **Declined**
  because *fiant* renders perfectly well ("the winds arise") and asserts nothing false — Pattern
  18's ❌ case. Logged so the blind read does not treat it as unseen.
- **Declined, 0048 @1292C — *Canales quidem sunt emissiones gratiarum Dei, **quas** modis pluribus
  in suos quos eligit, ipsosque martyrii gratia dignos efficit ipse et suscipit.*** The relative
  *quas* has **no verb**; *efficit* and *suscipit* both take *ipsos*, not *quas*. Rendered with a
  bracketed supply — "which in many manners he [sends] into his own whom he chooses" — using the
  corpus's existing convention for supplied English words rather than inventing a marker. No
  `[ed:]`: nothing is missing from **our** digitization, and no `[cj:]`: the missing item is a
  whole verb, not a wrong word, so there is nothing to gloss additively.
- **Declined, 0045 @1282B — Abel's clause.** *virgo pariter et martyr existens, sacerdotem et
  hostiam in se sacerdos pariter et hostia, confitens virgo pariter et martyr, hostia et sacerdos
  occubuit* repeats *virgo/martyr* and *sacerdos/hostia* twice each in twenty words, with the
  participle *confitens* stranded between them. Rendered clause for clause as printed, in Migne's
  order. **No emendation, no marker** — nothing here is a non-word or a false assertion; it is
  just rhetoric the plate may have doubled. Flagged so the blind read looks.
- **0048 @1292A — *pluviam **septem annis** clausam*.** Foliot's own prose (III Reg. XVIII), where
  the drought is three years and six months. **No `[var:]`: this is not a scripture quotation and
  Migne prints no locator**, so a marker would rest on our identification rather than on his —
  the principle already set at 0004 @1160C. Logged as a factual oddity for Wilson's eye.
