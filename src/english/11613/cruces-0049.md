# Cruces — 11613 (Gilbert Foliot, *Expositio in Cantica canticorum*), chunks 0049–0052

Columns 1295D–1304D (Cant. VII, 12 – VIII, 14). **These are the last four chunks of the work.**
Column + printed reading + note. Divergences from the Vulgate are listed whether or not a
`[var: …]` was taken; where a call was declined the reason is given, because a flagged passage
is not a checked passage.

Conventions inherited from waves 1–2 (`11613-MERGE-NOTES.md`) and **not re-decided here**:
`amica` = **friend** (and `amicus` = friend, which is what 0052's lemma 13 needs) · `sponsa` =
Bride / `sponsus` = Bridegroom · `dilectus` = beloved, capital `Dilectus`/`Dilecta` = the
Beloved, **mirroring Migne's own varying capitalization** · `pulcher`/`pulchra` = **beautiful**,
never "fair" · `laeva` = **left hand** / `dextera` = **right hand** · `caprea` = **roe** (never
goat) · `mammae` and `ubera` both = **breasts** (the declared flattening) · `custodes` =
**keepers** (0042's ruling, and 0052 expounds the word for a column).

### 0050 @1296D — 1–2. *Quis mihi det te fratrem meum sugentem ubera matris meae… et mustum malorum granatorum meorum.*

  nemo despiciat. » · « Apprehendam te, » · « in domum matris meae, » · « Poculum ex vino

### 0050 @1297D — 3–4. *Laeva ejus sub capite meo; et dextera illius amplexabitur me. 4. Adjuro vos, filiae Jerusalem, ne suscitetis, neque evigilare faciatis Dilectam, donec ipsa velit.*
⭐ **This is the reprise chunk. Both verses were englished by peers and were checked against them
before rendering.** Foliot's own gloss is one sentence long (*Haec quia jam dicta sunt, non
repetimus*), so the lemma carries the whole weight.

- **Cant. II, 6 / VIII, 3 — the *laeva* verse.** 0025 @1221C prints *Laeva ejus sub capite meo,
  et dextera **ejus** amplexabitur me* and englishes it "His left hand is under my head, and his
  right hand shall embrace me." **Migne's printing HERE differs twice:**
  1. **`;` for `,`** after *capite meo*. **The English differs**: the semicolon is carried.
  2. ***dextera ILLIUS*** for 0025's *dextera **ejus***. **The English does NOT differ, and this
     is a decision, not an oversight.** *ejus* and *illius* are both simply "his" in English;
     there is no exponent for the distinction between the two demonstratives, and manufacturing
     one ("that one's right hand") would invent a distinction of sense the Latin does not make.
     This follows two standing precedents: **Pattern 9's** "Latin *X* has no English exponent —
     log it as a crux, leave the English alone," and **the merge notes' own ruling on the
     *Adjuro vos* conjunctions**, where *quoad* / *quoadusque* / *quousque* took no marker
     because English has one word for all three. **No `[var:]`** — and note that a `[var:]` was
     impossible here regardless: the divergence is Migne against Migne, and a `[var:]` must open
     by naming an external witness (0018's rejection). Against the **Vulgate**, both printings
     agree (*dextera illius* is in fact the Vulgate's own reading at Cant. VIII, 3 — so it is
     0025's *ejus*, not this chunk's *illius*, that is the odd one out).
- **Cant. VIII, 4 — the *Adjuro vos* verse. This chunk gives a SIXTH printing.** Tabulated
  against what the merge notes already record for 0025–0029, 0033 and 0042:

  | site | conjunction | *eam* / object | case of *dilectam* | adjuration clause |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | 0025 @1222D | *donec* | *Dilectam* absent from one re-quote | lower-case | *per capreas cervosque camporum* present |
  | 0033 @1248B | *donec* | present | lower-case | present |
  | 0033 (re-quote) | *donec* | present | lower-case | **absent** |
  | 0042 @1276A | — | (a different verse, Cant. V, 8) | — | — |
  | **0050 @1297D** | ***donec*** | **present** | **CAPITAL *Dilectam*** | **absent** |

  - **The capital IS mirrored**: "nor make **the Beloved** to awake", against 0025's and 0033's
    "the beloved". This is the one difference in the verse that English can carry, and per the
    standing convention Migne's capitalization is mirrored and never normalized.
  - Everything else is identical to the peers' English, deliberately: "I adjure you, daughters of
    Jerusalem, that you stir not up, nor make … to awake, until she herself will."
  - **No `[var:]`, following the merge notes' explicit instruction.** Against the Vulgate this

    make … to awake". Two printed negations, two in the English, none supplied.

### 0050 @1298A — 5. *Quae est ista, quae ascendit de deserto, deliciis affluens, innixa super Dilectum suum?*
- **Vulgate (Cant. VIII, 5):** identical. **No `[var:]`.**
- ⚠ **This is a DIFFERENT verse from 0033's** *Quae est ista, quae ascendit **per** desertum?*
  (Cant. III, 6), which 0033 englishes "Who is she that goeth up **by** the desert?". The two must
  not be harmonized: *per desertum* = "by the desert", *de deserto* = "**from** the desert".
  The frame ("Who is she that goeth up") is deliberately identical, because Migne's Latin frame
  is identical; the preposition differs because his does.
- ⚠ **1298B: « potens in deliciis, »** — Foliot re-quotes his own lemma's *deliciis affluens* as
  *potens in deliciis*, and no verse of the Song reads so (Cant. VII, 6 has *carissima in
  deliciis*). Migne against himself again, and again no `[var:]` is possible. Rendered as printed,
  "mighty in delights"; the gloss then reverts to *affluens* and stays with it for fifteen lines.
- The Gregory excerpt at 1298C–D is unattributed by any `[n:]` and is carried as printed.

### 0050 @1298D — *Sub arbore malo suscitavi te: ibi corrupta est mater tua: ibi violata est genitrix tua.*
- **Vulgate (Cant. VIII, 5b):** identical apart from Migne's **colons** where the Vulgate
  punctuates `;` and `,`. Carried as printed; the re-quotation at 1299A punctuates the same
  clause with **semicolons**, and that too is carried. **No `[var:]`.**
- ⛔ **THE HARDEST *mala* SITE IN THE RANGE, and it is a genuine ambiguity in Foliot, not a
  defect.** *arbor* is feminine, so *arbore malo* cannot be adjectival ("evil tree") — it is
  *malus, -i* f., the **apple tree**, and Foliot says so explicitly: *Dicunt enim vetitam illam
  arborem … malum fuisse*. He then slides across the homograph inside four lines:
  - *sub arbore malo* (lemma, and 1298D re-quote) = **apple tree**.
  - *quod arbore malo contractum est* = **apple tree** ("contracted by the apple tree").
  - *vetitam illam arborem … malum fuisse* = **apple tree**.
  - *Ab hoc quidem inobedientiae **malo*** = **evil** — "from this evil of disobedience", with
    *inobedientiae* fixing it as the neuter noun.
  - *seipsam sub **malo**, id est, sub peccato quod ex **malo** contractum est* = **apple**, twice.
    Rendered "under the apple … contracted from the apple", **not** "under the evil": Foliot's
    whole point in the sentence is that the tree of the lemma is the sin's origin, and the
    preceding clause has just identified the tree as an apple. ⚠ **This is the one place in the
    range where the two senses could each be defended; the reading is recorded here rather than
    smoothed, and a reader who disagrees can see exactly which word was decided which way.**
- *pulchrum oculis* → "**beautiful** to the eyes", per the standing ruling; not "fair".
- *mater* / *genitrix* kept visibly distinct, because 1299A **argues** the distinction (*matrem
  vocans generationem omnem … genitricem vero illam nominans*): **mother** vs. **she that bore
  thee**. Collapsing them would leave that sentence expounding a difference that is not on the
  page.

## 2. Textual cruces

### 2.1 — 0050 @1297D: *ut sibi mala junguntur in malis* — the apple/evil call, made and stated
Migne prints, of the martyrs, *qui sibi sic in amoris dulcedine juncti sunt, **ut sibi mala
junguntur in malis**, et nullis a te potuerunt malis averti.* The clause immediately follows
« Et mustum **malorum granatorum** meorum, » and glosses it.
- **Rendered "as apples are joined to one another among apples."** The referent is plainly the
  **pomegranates** of the lemma (the grains packed inside the fruit), and "as the grains are
  joined in the pomegranates" is what Foliot means. **It is not what he wrote.** He wrote bare
  *mala*, and this work's own system — fixed at 0039 and 0046 and unbroken — is *poma* / *mala*
  = apples, *mala punica* / *mala granata* = pomegranates. Importing *granata* into the English
  would be a silent emendation of exactly the class Pattern 7 forbids, and it would conceal an
  elision that is itself evidence about how Foliot handled the word.
- **The third *malis* in the same clause is "evils"** (*nullis … malis*, ablative with *nullis*),
  and it is rendered so. The clause therefore reads apples–apples–evils in eleven words. **If a
  later reader overturns the first two, the third must not move with them.**

### 2.2 — 0049 @1296B: *difficilem te invenire non ambigo* — `[cj:]` TAKEN
Migne prints *Ad hoc quidem quod expeto, **difficilem** te invenire **non** ambigo, quia
mandragoras in portis nostris odorem tui jam dedisse intelligo.*
- Rendered literally, the Bride says she does not doubt that she will find him **difficult** —
  in a sentence whose *quia*-clause gives the reason he is **near** (the mandrakes have already
  given his odour), and in a paragraph whose whole burden is that his coming is assured.
- Every word is real Latin and correctly set, so `[sic:]` has nothing to wrap; the English
  asserts something the passage denies, which is Pattern 18's founding shape, and the merge
  notes' own 7561 @0648 precedent extends `[cj:]` to a **dropped negation** as well as to a
  wrong word. Marked additively: **`difficult [cj: *difficilem*; read *non difficilem*, "not
  difficult"]`**. Migne's word keeps its English; nothing is removed.
- ⚠ The competing construal — that *ambigo* + accusative-and-infinitive here means "I hesitate
  to find thee difficult" — was considered and does not rescue the sentence either, so the call
  is not between two good readings and Pattern 18's decline-and-log clause does not apply.

### 2.3 — 0050 @1299C: **A THIRD SENTENCE WITH NO APODOSIS.** ⭐ This makes it a class
*Haec enim **si** cohaerentia sunt, cor deliberans, et brachium id quod corde deliberatur
exsequens.* The protasis is complete, the two participial limbs are complete, and **there is no
main clause**; the next sentence starts a fresh conditional (*Quae si ambo memoriale … tenent …
cohibebunt*) which does have its apodosis.
- **Rendered ending where Migne ends, and deliberately unmarked** — `[ed:]` covers only what OUR
  digitization lost, and nothing was lost: the TEI, the Latin chunk and the plate all stop there.
- ⭐ **This pairs with 1159D and 1269D, already logged by two other agents.** Three apodosis-less
  sentences in one work, in three ranges, found by three readers who could not see each other.
  **The merge notes call two of them "a class, not an accident"; three settles it.** Worth
  Wilson's eye on whether this work's plate drops limbs systematically — and note that all three
  were found by translating, not by any check.

### 2.4 — 0050 @1300B: *marytrum* — `[sic:]` taken, sense kept outside the quarantine
*se quasi sequaces **marytrum** et ipsorum imitatores praeferebant.* A non-word (metathesis for
*martyrum*), carried under Pattern 7/10. Rendered **"followers of the martyrs [sic: *marytrum*]"**
— per 7a⁗ the marker isolates the defective type and not the force it carries, and *marytrum* is
the clause's only object noun; deleting it would leave "followers of the — and imitators of them."
The same sentence prints *martyres* correctly twice, so the type is certainly a slip.

### 2.5 — 0050 @1297D: *quot* set as *quod*
*amorem … qui tot modis formatus est, **quod** modis vina formari solent.* The correlative to
*tot* is *quot*; Migne prints *quod*. Rendered "formed in so many ways, **as** wines are wont to
be formed", which is as close as the printed words allow. **No `[cj:]`**: the English asserts
nothing false, only slightly loose, and Pattern 18's ✅-cases require a false assertion or a lost
negation/correlative that changes the claim. ⚠ Borderline — it *is* a correlative, and a stricter
reading of the 2026-08-15 extension would mark it. Recorded so the decision is visible.

### 2.6 — 0051 @1301B: *Hanc … sanctum apostolum … convertendam*
*Hanc itaque **sanctum apostolum** in novissimis convertendam, et Eliam, et Henoch ad hos
convertendos a Christo reservatos praesciens…* The gerundive *convertendam* agrees with *Hanc*,
but *sanctum apostolum* is a bare accusative with no preposition and no verb to govern it; the
sense requires an agent (*per sanctum apostolum*, or a nominative *sanctus apostolus*).
- Rendered **"to be converted in the last times [by] the holy apostle"**, with the supplied
  preposition in square brackets per the corpus convention for supplied English words, so the
  reader can see it is ours. **No `[cj:]`** — the defect is a missing function word, not a real
  word asserting something false, and bracketing already exposes it.

### 2.7 — 0051 @1300D: *illam* in the lemma, *illum* in the re-quotation
The lemma prints *nec flumina obruent **illam*** (feminine, agreeing with *charitatem*); eight
lines below Migne re-quotes « flumina non obruent **illum**, » in the masculine, of the same
charity. English has "it" for both and **no exponent for the gender**, so the English cannot show
it (Pattern 9). Recorded here. Note also that the lemma's *nec* becomes *non* in the
re-quotation — both are negations, both carried, the English reads "neither shall … overwhelm"
and "shall **not** overwhelm" respectively.

### 2.8 — 0051 @1301A: the lemma's `.` against the re-quotation's `?`
The lemma prints *Quid faciemus sorori nostrae in die quando alloquenda est.* with a **full stop**
(the Vulgate has `?`); the gloss re-quotes « Quid faciemus sorori nostrae, in die quando
alloquenda est? » with a **question mark** and an added comma. **Both carried exactly as printed**
under Pattern 8 — the English lemma ends on a period and the English re-quotation on a question
mark. This is the whole of the chunk's `?`-parity story: 4 in the Latin, 4 in the English.
**No `[var:]`** on the Vulgate's mark: a mark is not a reading.

### 2.9 — 0052 @1302D: Psal. I, 1 without *Beatus* — ⭐ `[var:]` TAKEN
Migne introduces the verse *Vir ille bonus, cujus meminit David, dicens, « **Vir** qui non abiit
in consilio impiorum… »* under the note `[n: (Psal. I, 1, 2)]`. The Psalm opens ***Beatus** vir
qui non abiit*, and the dropped word converts a beatitude into a bare definition of "the man."
- **This is material and the argument runs on it**: Foliot has just asked *Quis iste vir est?*
  and answers by identifying the *vir* of Cant. VIII, 11 with the *vir* of Psalm 1. With
  *Beatus* present the proof-text is a blessing; without it, it is a description of the man the
  question asked after. The reader who knows the Psalm will supply "Blessed" silently and never
  see what Migne's page actually offers as the proof.
- Marked `[var: Vulg. opens the verse with a beatitude, "Blessed is the man who hath not
  walked…"]`. Content written as a **phrase in English**, per the merge notes' finding that a
  short Latin content trips the Pattern 14 mirror test; the reading is unchanged by the wording.
- The rest of the citation is verbatim Vulgate, including the three *non*s, all carried.

**Chunk 0052 is the last chunk of 11613, and there is no colophon, no closing formula, no
