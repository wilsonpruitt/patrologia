## *101* HERE BEGINS THE PROLOGUE OF THE SAME.

[0181A]You demand a work heavy and arduous, and unequal to my strength; not sufficiently attending to what you so insistently, so anxiously ask for, and from whom, since — to confess the truth — I in no way know or feel within myself an understanding so subtle that I might be able to bring to effect what you desire. And indeed the poet is found to have admonished his hearers rather carefully — among whom I sometimes recall that I sat, a boy — that those who write should not be headlong, rashly presuming upon any great matters whatever, but rather circumspect, taking up a matter equal to their own strength: *Take up,* he says, *you who write, a matter equal to your*
*Strength; and turn over long what your shoulders refuse to bear,*
*What they are able to carry.*
[0181B] But if the weighing of one's strength is a narratorial thing in the songs of the heathen, much more is it so in the nuptial and spiritual Song of Songs, which surpasses not only the dirges of the Muses and the empty fables of the poets, but even the hymns of our own men and the songs of the prophets. Furthermore, so many treaters of this work, as many modern as ancient, are to be found, that I do not know whether those who come later could add anything beyond; and since there are very many commentaries of theirs upon this Song, anyone coming later [0182A] would seem; not so much to strike out new things as rather to steal what was found before. Nevertheless, whatever I object does not appear to satisfy you, but you wish me to attempt what is not to be had in my knowledge or in my eloquence. And assuredly a spiritual matter weighs down and overweighs heavier shoulders, since spiritual things, according to Paul: « The animal man does not perceive [n: (I Cor. II)]. » And so, like a man prudent and aware, I put my hand into the fire, or commit myself in a fragile skiff through the deep perils of the sea, trusting in him alone who wills that his own be worn down by labour not in vain, who, being good and merciful, says to those who speak of him and labour for him: « I am here. » With him, I beg, do you also commend me by your prayers, and obtain for me his grace, that I may be able to do what I wish — which, as one just and [0182B] truthful, he will not grant to my merits, and, as one compassionate and merciful, he will not deny to your prayers. *102* You men of old know only one moon;
In our times another and greater is at hand.
The sun, long alone, is amazed at a greater newness,
For a new moon brings forth a new sun.
If you wish to know better which, or who, I shall unfold it:
Christ the sun rises, the moon Mary bears him.
