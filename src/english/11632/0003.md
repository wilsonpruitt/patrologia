## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

[CANT. I.] *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.* Here first there occurs a question to be asked: since in this drama four persons, as it were, are brought on addressing one another — the Bridegroom and the companions of the Bridegroom, the Bride and the maidens who accompany her — why at the beginning the Bride more hastily leaps forth to speak, when that office would seem to be granted to the worthier Bridegroom. But by this it is hinted that the virgin is not darkened by ignorance, but with a learned spirit searches out the words of the preceding prophets; [0188D] and just as it is agreed that Solomon as well as the rest foreknew things to come, so did she know the things that had been foretold of her. She knew therefore that in many parts and in many ways God had of old spoken beforehand in the prophets [n: (Hebr. I)], and *106* had followed out whatever was necessary for the making of the marriage — he who from the days of Moses and far beyond had foresignified by many figures of things and riddles of words that this was to be, namely, that for the grace of the marriage he would come into the bosom of a virgin. But the virgin wills that all things that foresignify should [0189A] now be silent, that figures and riddles should not have place as before, that he who is promised should at last show forth his own presence, and should not any longer hold back the desire of her who awaits him from the sight of him. Because, she says, things promissory do not suffice for a tender love, but the languor grows heavy if things fulfilling are deferred longer than is right; since for obtaining what I love I neither ought nor am able to bring to bear things coercive, I judge it worthy simply to offer words of entreaty. *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.* I have learned, she says, from the report of the Scriptures, that it was once said to Abraham that the nations would rejoice that they were blessed in his seed [n: (Gen. XII)]; and that this is not held worthy of belief in Isaac his son [n: (Gen. XXI, XXII)], but is more effectually fulfilled at the fitting time in mine. Isaac too, begotten of a father now aged, ready, obedient, prepared [0189B] for the victim at a boyish age — what did he designate but this joy of mine, which my God assigned to me before I came to be? But as the world grows old I grieve that he whom I desire does not draw nearer, that he who is to be sent does not more quickly obey him who is to send him, that to me who love and long my joy is not conferred, which I desire the more in that it is not taken away from him who possesses it. I have learned that Jacob, about to sleep, did not put a pillow under his head, but with a stone set beneath slept far sweet [n: (Gen. XXVIII)], in which I have no doubt that he is figuratively foresignified who, when the kiss has been given me, shall be cut from the mountain without hands. In Judah the son of Leah, whom the aged father wills to be praised by his brothers [n: (Gen. XLIX)], from whom it is certain enough that the Jews are derived both in race and in name, no other seems to me [0189C] more fittingly signified by him than he who confesses God the Father to be Lord of heaven and earth [n: (Luc. X)]. I have learned of Joseph, whose death is found more pleasing to his brothers than his life [n: (Gen. XXXVII)], from the time that, having become a dreamer, he was clothed with a coat of many colours [n: (Gen. XXXIX)], whose chastity is read to have been assailed by the threats or the blandishments of a shameless mistress, but the young man leapt over the snares set before him with an unencumbered mind. He it is, as I see, who, when the kiss has been given me, shall be clothed with a new garment varied with virtues, and, proceeding as Bridegroom from the bridal chamber, shall suffer many envious men; who shall flee the malice of the present life as an unchaste harlot, having the cleanness of the highest chastity and of chaste goodness as his handmaid. He it is to whom, as Lord of things, the rest shall bow with bended knee, whom the stars, the sun and the moon, whom kings and [0189D] princes shall adore; who shall sustain with wondrous prudence Egypt labouring more with death than with famine [n: (Gen. XLI)], and shall minister the nourishment of life to all who worthily ask. Why should I unfold Moses, Aaron, Joshua, why the order of the judges, why should I name David, why the other kings, why each of the prophets, in whose catalogue not one at all is set down who does not by word or by deed speak beforehand of him whom I desire? But because he sent all these before as certain heralds and timbrel-players, in whose preludes he did not yet give his presence but promised it, now, impatient of delay, I do not accept their heraldings; I weigh all at a low price, since he is not received in presence. My understanding has indeed [0190A] now perceived that he is to come, since they promise it, but so great a love of him who is to come has intercepted the desire of me who wait, that whatever promissory thing now creeps up to the understanding or to the memory — as trumpets and flutes, sistra and psalteries, and whatever clatters outside at the door — I now, as it were, loathe, while I wish him alone to be led into the midst, and to be brought into my bridal chamber more sweetly and more laboriously. Let him therefore who is to come not stretch out the delay longer, let him not hold the desire of me who wait in suspense by an intolerable postponement, but let him who was promised of old at last descend as dew upon the fleece, and let him not salute from afar, but show himself nearer to her who longs [n: (Judic. VI)]. For I wish that he hide himself no longer in the bosom of the Father, that he not from thence dull my ears with a wordy frequency of messengers, but that he flood me — who have long borne it and can bear it [0190B] no longer — with an ampler grace, and pour himself wholly into my lap by a full inflowing. For to what does it profit that a soul held in suspense be soothed by so great promises, and that what is promised be experienced with no effect at all, since languor rather leads to death if health be always absent, and the beloved be not at last presented to her who loves by a welcome commerce? *Let him therefore kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.* He, moved by the heat and the desire of her who asks, although he does not at once fulfil the impatient soul of her who waits, still goes on to send to the virgin a more pleasing messenger, who in private speech should tell that the Lord is nearer. And whereas in many of those who went before there was much holiness, in this one much fortitude was found, in fact as well as in name, who, telling that the most mighty defender of Israel was at hand, [0190C] worthily deserved to be reckoned specially by the name of Gabriel. He, sent to the virgin, not forgetful of the excellence of him who sent him, does not stand outside at the door as one hindered by servile chains; but with greater confidence he entered a certain vestibule of the inner bridal chamber, having already professed both by word and by countenance who he is and why he comes. « Hail, he says, full of grace [n: (Luc. I)]. » The angel marvels that the virgin's secret place is unapproachable even to himself, carefully perceiving that it is not granted save to her Lord alone; and he rejoices to find the place of the bridal chamber so serene that, lying open to God alone, it rejects every stranger. *107* Hail, he says, virgin worthily glad beyond all virgins, now filled with the worthy gifts of all grace: to whom my Lord and the Lord of all has sent me before; nay, I see that he who sent me [0190D] is already with thee. « Hail, he says, full of grace, the Lord is with thee. » I thought that I came as a forerunner by the command of him who sends, but the commander has seemed to me to come before his messenger: perhaps because he willed first to find the virgin alone, lest I should seem to approach her, untaught and timid, without him. I judge that the angel, whom the Lord who sent him had already come before, did not find the aforesaid virgin in the forum or in the street, in tumult or in dance; nor did he find her held at last by spindle, needle, comb, wool, flax, nor by servile work, nor at the loom, but at leisure, or rather at a spiritual business, clinging to the Scriptures with an understanding or an affection wholly holy, not carnal. The angel himself therefore reveres her, found thus, and thus intent upon a divine [0191A] study, all witnesses removed; and marvelling at a youth robust not in years but in advances, he gives with much reverence the greeting entrusted for the virgin. « Hail, he says, full of grace, the Lord is with thee, blessed art thou among women [n: (Luc. I)]. » Thou, he says, so given over to a leisure or a business so honourable, so modest, oughtest worthily to be declared blessed among all women, or beyond all women; and so great a blessing shall attend thee, full of grace, that neither a first like thee nor a second following shall be made equal to thee. « And when she had heard, he says, she was troubled at his word, and thought what manner of salutation this should be [n: (ibid.)]. » The virgin, seeing an unwonted appearance of a man, is greatly afraid; and hearing something new, troubled none the less, she shudders, both because a man was not accustomed to be present at her spiritual leisure, and because she had not yet [0191B] perceived that she was worthy of so great an excellence. She turns therefore and turns over again what a salutation of this kind portends, what the angel standing by, or the Lord who sends him in, intends, since she does not yet manifestly comprehend the secret of the mystery, and it seems worthy that the virgin fear in all things, lest she offend. And the angel said: « Fear not, Mary, for thou hast found grace with God [n: (ibid.)]. » Let not a sudden fear, he says, trouble thy girlish mind, since with God thou hast found a saving grace; refuse not with a fearful heart his holy purpose and his good pleasure in thee, since that Lord gives thee the greetings which I, a messenger, bring. And he adds: « Behold, thou shalt conceive in thy womb, and shalt bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name Jesus [n: (ibid.)]. » In thy womb, he says, the Son of God shall celebrate a wondrous marriage, and [0191C] thy very womb shall beget [sic: *eumdum*] Bridegroom as man, whom thou shalt call Jesus, by the desert of his own goodness; for he shall save his people from their sins. And Mary said: « How shall this be done, since I know not a man? » [n: (ibid.)] Since I, she says, have decreed that I shall not marry in a carnal wedlock, and thou truly assertest that I shall shortly bring forth a son, by thy leave let me say it, I wish without doubt to know the manner whereby this, as being unfitting, can fittingly come together. I had indeed read, Isaiah writing it, that this marvel was to be; but neither he nor another had said that this was to be in Mary; and because this shoe seems wrapped about with a tangled latchet, thou, to whom perhaps it has been given, I wish that thou make it loosed for me. And the angel [0191D] answering said to her: « The Holy Spirit shall come upon thee, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow thee [n: (ibid.)]. » Know, he says, that thou shalt experience no commerce of a manly coupling, but shalt conceive, by the working of the Holy Spirit, possibly indeed, yet ineffably; because he shall utterly remove from thee whatever unseemly thing is either said or felt, and shall forbid every heat of foulness by interposing himself as a kind of shade. And since the Spirit of God works this begetting holily and wondrously, without doubt that which shall be born shall be holy; and of God there shall arise no other than the Son of God, since in thee God and man are personally made one. And Mary said: « Behold the handmaid of the Lord, be it done to me according to thy word [n: (ibid.)]. » Having heard the manner in which a marvellous begetting may come to pass, in which God may be born of a [0192A] virgin who shall know no commerce with a man, the humble virgin is ready to obey the Lord, and now hopes and loves that what she understands to be holy should come to pass the sooner. Because, she says, it is the Lord whom thou preachest, and I know myself the handmaid of the Lord, I now ask that thy word be fulfilled, and that it be done to me according to it: rejoicing that he, good and merciful, has not despised me, undeserving, but, clement and most high, has looked upon me, cast down and humble. And so, the ministry which thou hast faithfully fulfilled being accomplished, and the mystery which thou hast spoken being understood with a faith sufficiently certain, this thy forannouncing has left me rather in suspense and, as it were, unquiet, nor is anything enough for me if I do not feel him present who sent thee. Let him therefore now send no other in his own stead, and let no messenger promise things to come to a hastening mind, [0192B] but rather let him now at a fitting time present himself to me, let him, present, presently kiss me who am present. *Let him kiss me,* I say, *with the kiss of his mouth.* Let this joining, I say, be made in me not by lips, but by two natures coming together, so that both natures may wondrously be of one person; and those whom until now both fault and natural diversity had set far from one another, let an undivided unity of person now league together in firm peace. For I understand that the fulness of the Godhead shall come into my womb, keeping unharmed my seal of virginal chastity; and that in this bridal chamber the Son of God himself, by the sweet kiss of the marriage, shall join in the unity of person two unlike things of natures. God shall indeed become man, the excellence of his own nature *108* not contaminating, not consuming, but powerfully purifying [0192C] and assuming the nature of man; and the assumer shall give to the assumed a marvellous and incomparable gift, namely, that the assumed and the assumer may become one by a wondrous conjunction. One, I say, not either God only, or only man, but one God-man; one Christ. Not indeed so that the substance of God be confounded, but so that humanity be assumed into God. This assumption, this joining, whereby God willed to unite man to himself personally, and to strike or to unite a covenant of peace between nature and nature in the womb of the virgin, is in the present passage called by an honourable word a kiss, because peace returned among those at discord is confirmed by a kiss. The virgin therefore wills that in her womb the concord of God and of man be repaired, and that the repaired concord be strengthened by an unshaken covenant, so that he who was God and Son of none but God the Father [0192D] may in a new and wondrous manner become the bridegroom and the son of a mother. Let him become the bridegroom, joining the virgin to himself by a certain conjugal covenant, resting in her bridal chamber, peace having been made by the nuptial kiss; in her, or through her, begetting spiritual sons by a spiritual efficacy, so that both he and she may rejoice in the fruit and offspring of sons. Let him become the son, taking true flesh in her womb, yet keeping her wholly entire, a virgin and unstained; and in her, as God, consecrating all the saints as sons and heirs of God, and, born of her as man, making them his co-heirs. At this benefit, ancient indeed in its promises, modern in fact, the virgin rejoices and exults, and asks that it remain for ever; and considering that by that good an evil both following and long-standing can be destroyed, [0193A] she wills that, when the kiss has been given her, he strike an everlasting pact with her. For she sees that he judges it wholly unfitting and unworthy that peace should not be unshaken when someone has given the sign of peace; and for this reason she wills him to be wrapped in that binding, as it were, which, once confirmed, ought not and cannot be further loosed.
