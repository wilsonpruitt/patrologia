## CHAPTER II.

*Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.* The mouth of the Lord — so is every holy man or prophet called, through whom he unfolds to men his own thoughts and secrets; through whom he both reveals to us things past and foretells things to come, more sweetly commands good things, more severely forbids evil ones. It is agreed that Moses was of old the mouth of the Lord, to whom God seems to have granted so great a grace that he was held privy to this secret long [0193B] beforehand, and that as a faithful messenger he should speak it as a thing that was to come after. « A prophet, he says, shall God raise up to you from among your brethren, like unto me; him shall you hear [n: (Deut. XVIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Deut. 18:15 reads *Prophetam de gente tua et de fratribus tuis sicut me suscitabit tibi Dominus Deus tuus: ipsum audies* — singular "thy brethren," "thou shalt hear"; the printed plural form is that of Act. 3:22] That David was the mouth of the Lord is doubted by none of the faithful — he who venerates this mystery foreknown by a prophetic spirit; and lest perchance he seem to begrudge to others what he foresees, taking up the psaltery, he modulates praises and Songs to him. « He himself, he says, as a Bridegroom coming forth from his bridal chamber, hath rejoiced as a giant [n: (Psal. XVIII)]. » That Isaiah's word may be commended with worthy reverence, and that the speech may not be judged contemptible on account of the speaker, in speaking to a people of hard neck, which is not easily moved to faith, in order to allure them or perhaps to terrify them, he sometimes declares himself the mouth of the Lord. « The mouth, he says, of the Lord [0193C] hath spoken these things [n: (Isa. I)]; » [var: Vulg. Isa. 1:20 *quia os Domini locutum est*, without *haec*, "these things"] and as Isaiah declares himself to be the mouth of the Lord, so too Jeremiah ought to be believed, and can none the less be said, to be so — men who, at the Spirit's teaching, spoke beforehand of the virgin's childbearing, and followed out the mysteries to come with a foreannouncing word. Isaiah: « Behold, he says, a virgin shall conceive and shall bring forth a son, and you shall call his name Emmanuel [n: (Isa. VII)]. » [var: Vulg. *et vocabitur nomen ejus Emmanuel*, "and his name shall be called"; Migne prints the second person plural *vocabitis*] Jeremiah: « The Lord, he says, shall make a new thing upon the earth, and a woman shall compass a man [n: (Jer. XXXI)]. » [var: Vulg. Jer. 31:22 *creavit Dominus novum super terram*, "the Lord hath created," a past tense] Finally the angel Gabriel himself is found to be the mouth of the Lord, by whom the virgin, now grown up and marriageable, is approached in person concerning so great a mystery; and lest even a slight darkness of not knowing leave her in suspense, he unfolds both that she is to bring forth and the manner of the bringing forth. Whence the virgin, sufficiently certain, wills now no longer to hear the mouth of the Lord, but, burning [0193D] with love, wills to feel the kiss of the mouth — namely, that she may be cherished by the gracious coming of the Bridegroom, by a firm peace, by an unremoved presence, according to what is announced by the prophet or by the angel, as by the mouth of the Lord. « Be it done to me, she says, according to thy word [n: (Luc. I)]. » Be it done, that the fulness of grace may render me blessed among women; that the royal beauty of the bridegroom coming may adorn my bridal chamber; that the holiness of the Spirit alone may make my womb fruitful; that he may be mine, the Son of God, salvation, justice, wisdom, fortitude. By this kiss let that bridegroom, well-disposed, incline himself to me; by this let him lay me back — bride, mother, Virgin — in a peaceful bosom, in a gracious embrace; let this soothe me, refresh me, make me serene; let this, claiming me for himself, estrange me from all who come against me. But [0194A] let this be done according to thy word, which thou hast brought forth not with thy own mouth but rather with the mouth of the Lord, since thou hast been not thy own mouth but rather the mouth of the Lord; because thou art not reckoned thine own angel, thine own herald, thine own messenger, but his who speaks to me through thee, who makes thee wholly his own, that thou mayest speak. Wherefore I do not say to thee: Kiss me with the kiss of thy mouth, but rather of him: *Let him kiss me,* I say, *with the kiss of his mouth,* that he may at last deign to bless me with his own kiss, which to thee, his mouth, it was granted to proclaim with an announcing word. *109*

## CHAPTER III.

*Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.* The Virgin, hearing that, betrothed to the Son of God, she should bring forth God and man, and that in conceiving she should not at all feel a man, her purpose being kept, but that the Holy Spirit, [0194B] overshadowing her, should come upon her with an effectual power, with a certain faith and a robust hope, fervent in Spirit or in love, rejoices indeed in the hearing, but wills to rejoice more fully in a more pleasing effect. That spirit therefore of so great grace, of so great peace, so sweet, so kindly, of a power so wondrously effectual; who, warding off far away the injuries of the flesh, strengthens the purpose of chastity, who gives fruitfulness while saving the seal of virginal honour — that Spirit, I say, the virgin longs to be given to her more fully, and by him, as by a kiss, that the gifts promised her be confirmed. Let the voice of the angel cease, she says, who answers me with such great benefits, and let the Son of God himself come nearer, who betroths me, a virgin, to himself alone: let him touch me with his mouth, whose touch shall flood me with much grace; let him deign me with his kiss, let him make me fruitful with his spirit. In a kiss [0194C] of the mouth there is not only an outward joining of lips, but a certain breathing forth of what is within is also perceived and felt; and if these be clean and filled with a sweetened savour, the one who kisses pours as it were a sweet spirit into the one kissed. Since therefore the Spirit is belched forth, as it were, from the heart and mouth of the Son (for as the Spirit is preached to proceed from the Father, so also from the Son) — than which secret place none is truly esteemed cleaner and sweeter — rightly does the virgin ask to be kissed by the Bridegroom, that is, by the Son of God, so that she may taste the inward good, the saving Spirit being drawn in and perceived. I long, she says, that he come and stand nearer, in whom I have believed, whom I have loved, that that bridegroom, familiarly admitted into my chamber, may purify me, magnify me, gladden me with a sweet kiss: not that there be, as [0194D] is wont, an outward conjunction as of lips, but a certain welcome and sweet inpouring into me of the Spirit of his mouth. Finally, after God had first formed man from the slime of the earth, he breathed, as we read, the breath of life into his face [n: (Gen. II)]; and just as he is understood to have been made or fashioned as though by the hands of God, so was he animated to live in time by the breath of his mouth. Far below, Elisha, when he wished to raise a dead man, bending himself over him as he lay, willed to apply not only hands to hands but also mouth to mouth, so that, touched by the breath of the living, he might receive a living soul, and that, as the other lay upon him, the dead man might receive not a loss but rather a gain [n: (IV Reg. IV)]. But far more loftily and much more sacredly does the virgin will the Son of God to be at hand to her; much more sweetly, far [0195A] more laboriously does she will him to be present in a kiss — namely, that he impart a kiss, that is, that he make the Spirit of his mouth to be felt, that he touch her with a spirit of fuller grace and efficacy. Thou, she says, my Bridegroom, my God, deign to impart thy presence to me; give the kiss to her who asks, that is, give me to feel thy Spirit; let thy breathing upon me be so useful, so fruitful, and so pleasant, that I may both remain a virgin according to my vow, and yet, conceiving, be fruitful. Breathe upon me, I say, not only that with the rest I may be able to shape labour into a precept, but kiss the virgin, that by a kiss thou mayest give to the virgin, beyond all others, a conception: let thy kiss work wondrous things in me, and work them wondrously, so that both fruitfulness may be preserved to the Bride and virginity to the Mother by a singular privilege. You therefore in this audience, all [0195B] and each of you who stand by, if you think anything worthily and reverently of the word you have heard: attend to and love both the kiss which the Son of God the Father holds out, and the singular merit and solemn miracle of so great a mother. She, knowing and feeling the sacraments of the nuptial kiss, seeking nothing else, nor content with any other whatever, is bent as it were principally upon obtaining it; and for this reason at the beginning of her speech she asks especially that it be imparted to her, loving to experience its sweetness and its efficacy.
