## CHAPTER IV.

*Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.* Let him, she says, whom I love, whom I choose before all, be wondrously joined to me; let an inward sweetness be poured out upon me by his kiss, by his Spirit; by whose help and working let the integrity of my [0195C] flesh be fortified, and let the Word be personally united in me to flesh, God to man. See the miracle of the Mother of the Lord. The virgin receives the Bridegroom's kiss, perceives the Spirit of him who kisses; and in the kiss she now conceives as a mother, while by the working of the Spirit she receives what is conceived. And this indeed the angel Gabriel had also preached beforehand, when he announced that the overshadowing Spirit would come upon her [n: (Luc. I)]; the evangelist Matthew [n: (cap. I)] afterwards confirmed it, when, recalling the deed, he commended the virgin mother. « She was found, he says, having in her womb of the Holy Spirit. » And below: « For that which is born in her is of the Holy Spirit [n: (ibid.)]. » This grace, this efficacy the virgin desires to be given her, when she so pointedly asks to be kissed by the bridegroom: longing in the [0195D] kiss not to feel the outward kisses of the Bridegroom, but that his inward Spirit pour forth an effectual grace upon her. In which type, as you know, when we are presented at the altar, and, consecrating the sacred mysteries, are ourselves also consecrated to the Lord — because by the desert of so great *110* an office we are now filled with the Holy Spirit — religion judges it worthy that we who stand by should kiss one another alike. Let us kiss, I say, not that we may mingle lips with lips, but that we may pour back upon others the grace of the Spirit we have received; and let those who stand by that same sacred mystery, humble and intent, become partakers of the sacrament by the working of the Spirit and the designation of the kiss. The virgin therefore wills that the earnest of the nuptial kiss be given her by the Bridegroom; she wills to receive by the Spirit of his mouth a certain [0196A] commerce of spiritual efficacy, namely, that both the integrity of her modesty and of her special purpose may remain unviolated, and that her virginal womb may conceive in a new manner and bring forth what it has conceived. This gift, this benefit, this grace that virgin alone deserved to find: that, when she was to conceive, not the knowledge of a man but the inpouring of the Spirit was found to come before her; that by a kiss alone, a virgin alone, alone might bring forth God and man, and that in her childbearing things so diverse, so discordant, should come together in a sounding miracle. But that Bridegroom, feeling the devout impatience of her who burns, does not turn away his ear from the desire and the prayers of her who calls upon him; and that mercy, which is his own, and his ancient counsel he does not hold back with himself any longer, but drawing nearer he presents himself more sweetly to her who loves. He presents himself, I say, and gives the vehemently [0196B] longed-for kiss to her who asks; and, the kiss being given, he gives a greater confidence to her who speaks, namely, that she should not at once withdraw as though the business were finished, but that, having once begun to speak, she should now go on confidently. So indeed you also, brethren, know that it sometimes happens when two are seen to come together alike from opposite sides: if the lesser earns to find the favour of the greater, the greater at the beginning deigns to impart a kiss to the lesser. And having received it, the lesser is strengthened with a longer confidence, so that thereafter he may speak out boldly whatever he judges worthy; and he who has felt the other's goodwill in holding out a kiss is rendered now more confident and more ready to speak. And so the virgin, having received the Bridegroom's presence, comforted by the nuptial kiss, knowing that she was to be a mother [0196C] by the inpouring of the Spirit in a virginal womb, somewhat more confident, turns herself as it were away from prayers, and turns herself with worthy praises to the Bridegroom now present.
