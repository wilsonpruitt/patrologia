## CHAPTER VIII. (cont.)

*116* The third ointment, however, calms the sea, tempers [0206C] wrath, stills the winds by its majesty, with its hand makes serene the turbulent motions of the soul; it levels the road, holds the right hand, steadies the step, and, as though the harbour were already found, makes a man secure and confirms him. For within himself the strong traveller, now in a manner serene, happily estranged from the assault of vices and from any degenerate consent, united by patience, now presumes upon a sure thing, as it were, trustfully and humbly. And though whatever adversities there be murmur behind his back and press upon him, yet they do not withstand him to the face with effectual boldness, while, forgetting the things that are behind, he covets and faints for the courts of God alone [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)], and turned to him with his whole affection, longs, as it were, to join face to face. « My face hath sought thee out, he says; thy face, O Lord, will I seek [n: (Psal. XXVI)]. » [0206D] Touched by the odour of this ointment, he either does not feel adversities of any kind, or reckons them empty, counts them for nothing, does not consent to them, and burns with so great a longing for the things ahead which he already feels, that with a deaf ear he passes by whatever is discordant and dissenting. Let all things then that can hurt for a time roar and clatter with threats or with blows, let the things that can profit flatter with fraud, with lying, with entreaty, with reward: what will this man do but hold his sure road gladly and bravely, and, as from the brow of a mountain, answer those barking below with that word of the Apostle: « Who shall separate us from the charity of Christ? Tribulation, or distress, or famine, or nakedness, or peril, or the sword? I am sure that neither death nor life [n: (Rom. VIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Rom. 8:35 has *an persecutio*, "or persecution", before "or the sword"] Since therefore so great a certitude of perseverance confirms him that he cares not what any adverse [0207A] strength may clamour: he now seems to obtain the reward laid up for him, which stands promised to those who persevere and abides unfailing. « I have finished, he says, my course, there is laid up for me a crown of justice [n: (II Tim. IV)]. » And again: « By hope we were saved [n: (Rom. VIII)]. » Whoever has experienced the odour-bearing sweetness of this ointment becomes not uncertain of things to come as though they were present, and so bravely and so sweetly goes forward from virtue to virtue, that he hopes, and holds the hoped-for salvation already as though given. The first ointment, therefore, purged out the wickedness of the old leaven; the second, by the new gift of a sprinkling, prepared the unleavened of merits; the third confirmed those feasting with the sure privilege of health restored. The first, with a heavy scouring-cloth and a shrewd draught-vessel, turned the house about, and overturned the bilge as well; the second [0207B] poured a strengthening medicine into the sick mind; the third rendered hope more certain, that it should no longer fear fall and ruin. Accordingly the first may be called purgative, the second strengthening, and the third confirming, because by the first every sick man is cleansed of the dregs of his vices; by the second, with a welcome file and labour, the health he wished for is repaired; by the third, the mind is confirmed in its purpose both by a certain humble gladness at present gifts and by a sure confidence of things to come. With these ointments the holy Virgin had felt the Bridegroom's breasts to abound, and had felt that she was not to keep their grace with herself, after the manner of the envious; with the vent closed; but to offer out of doors a most sweet odour to the devout and the humble, and by its benefit to fatten with an abundance of merits minds that were dry before. But because [0207C] the benefit of so great an odour is not reached by human powers, if he that comes is not forestalled by the divine hand and help; the Virgin shows by what gift it may without doubt be reached, so that, this being shown, he who hears and does not love may be held inexcusable.
