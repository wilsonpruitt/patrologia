## CHAPTER IX.

*Thy name is oil poured out.* The Bridegroom's name is Jesus, which was named by the Angel before he was conceived in the womb [n: (Luc. I)]. And who either understood this name more truly, or loved it more sincerely, or preached it more fittingly, than that virgin mother herself, to whom the Angel first announced it: « Behold, he says, thou shalt conceive in thy womb and shalt bring forth a son, and thou shalt call his name Jesus [0207D] [n: (ibid.)]. » Jesus, in the Latin tongue, is called *Saving*, or *Saviour.* According to his work is his name also, and he has his name from the thing itself, « for he shall save his people [n: (Matth. I)]. » Rightly therefore is he called Jesus, who shall be the salvation of the people, whose « name whosoever shall call upon, he shall be saved [n: (Joel. II)]; because both to him who calls devoutly the right hand of the Lord gives strength, and to him who confesses worthily he is become a hearer unto salvation [n: (Psal. CXVII)]. This name the Bride calls oil, and *eleos* in Greek is, in Latin, *mercy*, because that this Jesus or Saviour willed to be ours, our own justice did not give it to be so, but his mercy; for while our own merit cast us far from his face, he, calling us back, by that mercy which is his own made us safe. Whence the Apostle: Not by the works of justice [0208A] which we have done, he says, but according to his own mercy he saved us [n: (Tit. III)]. » When God appoints to save the undeserving, after wrath he remembers this mercy; through this name, or for the sake of it — this oil — he has pity on the unworthy and the wretched; by the strength of this name he drives our enemies far off, and, freeing us from vices, restores us to himself as friends. Which he who had long stood wandering in the way of sinners now hopes for, now promises himself, rejoicing at the visitation of grace, knowing that the gifts of God are not of his own repentance, that the calling is not conferred by it, but that the name and the oil of mercy is borne above all the liquors *117* of merits. « For he gave him a name which is above every name, that in the name of Jesus every knee should bow; and every tongue should confess that Jesus is Lord [n: (Philip. II)]. » [var: Vulg. *donavit illi nomen*, "he bestowed on him a name", and the confession runs *quia Dominus Iesus Christus in gloria est Dei Patris*, "that the Lord Jesus Christ is in the glory of God the Father"] [0208B] Therefore that penitent says: « For thy name's sake, he says, thou wilt quicken me [n: (Psal. CXLII)]. » And he adds: « In thy mercy thou wilt destroy my enemies [n: (ibid.)]. » And in another psalm: « For thy name's sake, O Lord, thou wilt be merciful to my sin [n: (Psal. XXIV)]. » To which hope, to which assertion, not even the Lord himself gives the lie, but through Isaias, taught by the Spirit, he mercifully foretells it, so that the penitent, hearing this, may presume to be strengthened with a fuller confidence, and may know in what he ought to glory as healthfully as he does truly. « For all they that love thy name shall glory in thee, O Lord [n: (Psal. V)]. » Of which name, through Isaias: « For my name's sake, he says, I will remove my fury far off [n: (Isa. XLVIII)]. » For the sake of the name, or of the oil which pours out the dew of mercy, he removes far from those who had deserved it [0208C] his judgment and his fury; and if he were not held to be Saviour, if not pitying and merciful, what would every wretched man be, but justly pressed down by his intolerable justice? But what is it that the oil is called poured out? So long as oil is held in a little vessel, either it is not known, or how great its grace and usefulness may be is not felt, because, though the physician who knows all things knows it himself, yet the sick do not, to whose wounds still to be healed he has not applied it. For a time this oil was held as in a sealed vessel, when the Son of God was accounted good indeed and merciful with himself, having in very truth the purpose of pitying in his affection, but not yet fully gladdening the wretched in the effect. In a vessel, as it seems, he willed this oil [0208D] to be contained, when by a hidden judgment he would not have mercy on the angels who grew proud, when he neither turned them from their fall by grace going before, nor after their lapse converted them by mercy coming to their aid. But to those who stood he gave to feel this oil, whom he mercifully deigned to keep, lest they should fall along with the others, increasing for them and laying on so effectual a soothing of this oil that from it they received a sure and unswerving firmness for standing. Yet since this is above us, we should perhaps say that it pertained nothing to us, if we too did not feel the benefit of that same oil; nay rather, if he did not more largely and more lavishly incline the open vessel toward us, and, the oil being wholly poured out, incline as it were his whole mercy toward us. For both our necessity and his goodness seemed to require this, that [0209A] to a fuller misery a fuller mercy should be conferred; and because there is no distinction but that manifold sin gathers and confounds both Jews and Gentiles [n: (Rom. X)], the whole face of this oil should pour itself out on both, that the yoke might rot. Which the Prophet foreseeing in the Spirit and praying for: « May God, he says, have mercy on us, may he make his countenance shine upon us, and may he have mercy on us [n: (Psal. LXVI)]. » [var: Vulg. adds *et benedicat nobis*, "and bless us", between the two clauses] What does he mean by "have mercy," and "have mercy"? He adds: « That we may know thy way upon earth, thy salvation in all nations [n: (ibid.)]. » Because, he says, we have sinned, both Gentiles and Jews, and there is no distinction, it is needful that this pouring out of the oil should reach to both, that thou mayest graciously call us Jews back to him who is the way, and that we may alike know thy salvation in all nations. [0209B] Oil shut in a little vessel, or in a closet, keeps the holy angels safe; but oil poured out heaps together the scattered handfuls of elect men: and mercy, which to protect the healthy hid itself within the sanctuary of heaven, when the vessel is inclined pours itself out to heal sick men. « O Lord, he says, bow down thy heavens and descend; touch the mountains and they shall smoke [n: (Psal. CXLIII)]. » He who offers the full vessel of mercy to the angels who stand, inclines the vessel to heal sick men upon earth; and what seemed to be held in it he now pours out with a liberal, even a prodigal hand, and upon those who must be greatly pitied he pours out much mercy. At whose healing touch the harmful swellings go down, the stings are pressed back, the pains of wounds grow gentle; like smoke the vanity of proud minds [0209C] vanishes away, and, the disease being driven off, health revives and grows strong.
