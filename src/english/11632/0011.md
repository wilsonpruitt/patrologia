## CHAPTER X.

*Thy name is oil poured out.* The Bridegroom's name is Christ; and Christ is called *anointed*, because God anointed him with the oil of gladness above his fellows [n: (Psal. XLIV)]. Not only anointed, but he may also be called oil, in that by his own power he wipes us clean of the harmful phlegm, and with a sweet grace anoints us who are withered and dry. For the name of Christ, defined with a fitting understanding, teaches that God and man are united in one person, and that one Christ is effected by the wonderful and incomprehensible force of the joining, although the natures of God and of man are far unlike. For as a rational soul and corruptible flesh make one [0209D] man, so God assuming and the man assumed render Christ none the less one: and while the unconfused diversity of both natures is held, yet in Christ himself the unity of person is preached. And if the faith of this name be poured out over us by the sprinkler of him who has mercy, if the concretion of the natures, the unconfused unity of the person, be loved by us: because he flows and abounds with much chrism and wonderful charism, he makes us glad outright, while he makes us fruitful with the merits of the virtues. But this oil, or this name, is now no longer shut within the narrow *118* bounds of Judaea, but is poured out among the nations even to the ends of the world, nor does anyone among them rightly think himself to come to salvation [0210A] who does not first go forward in strength by the faith of this name. « There is not, he says, any other name under heaven whereby we must be saved [n: (Act. IV)]. » [var: Vulg. *nec enim aliud nomen est sub caelo datum hominibus*, "for there is no other name under heaven given to men"] There is none, therefore, who can hide himself from the true light of this oil, since its ray pours itself out mightily unto the ends of the earth; and it enlightens every man coming into this world [n: (Joan. I)], who has not by an obstinate malice made himself unclean. For everyone who has happily obtained by the gift of grace ears to hear knows, and everyone prudently instructed in the faith of this name knows, that Christ is the corner-stone, the coupler of Jews and Gentiles, himself the head of the Church, the one mediator of God and men; our bone and our flesh by a nuptial commerce and a natural fellowship, himself anointed and anointing those near him with spiritual power and grace. [0210B] Which oil, or name, he so poured out far and wide both among Jews and among Gentiles, when the love of him spread itself abroad in their hearts: that first among the Jews the apostles truly confessed Christ, and consequently among the Gentiles many were anointed with the same chrism. For when the apostles were asked whom men said the Son of man to be, they answered that the word or judgment of all was not one, and when at the last their own mind about this was more diligently asked, Peter, as much for himself as for the rest, reported in a learned discourse: « Thou art Christ. And he: Blessed art thou, he says, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood hath not revealed it to thee, but my Father who is in heaven [n: (Matth. XVI)]. » But after this oil, or name, had flowed mercifully in Judaea, Idumaea also received [0210C] the pouring out of the same: and into it Christ at his coming so stretched out his shoe, that he made them friends whom he found strangers by the desert of error. And when they had received so great a name with a fixed faith and a hope beyond doubt not empty, they were called generally by that same Christ Christians, and whoever was worthy to be contained in the catalogue of the faithful, as he willed to be a Christian, so he willed to be reckoned by the name. Rightly therefore is this name called not poured in but rather poured out, in that it is assigned not to the Jew only, not to the Greek in particular; but though those first glory in the faith of this name, yet neither are those further off held to be shut out from that same glory. For into all the earth the sound of this name has gone [n: (Psal. XVIII)], and its pouring out has passed to the ends of the earth, imparting to those far off as to those near [0210D] the benefit of salvation — to those, that is, who have loved to adorn their faith with works. For whoever of the faithful are spread abroad from the rising to the setting, from the north even to the south, are steeped in the Christian name or in the oil of Christ, nor is it shut off from any by land, by sea, or by any space whatever, but it is poured out upon all on whom God has willed to have mercy.

## CHAPTER XI.

*Thy name is oil poured out.* The Bridegroom's name is God. A name of this sort the Father is believed to have poured into the Son, when he is said to have begotten him coequal, coeternal with himself, when the unbegotten gave to the begotten to be naturally God, and willed that none but God should be party to this giving. [0211A] For as whatever God is, so whatever has been substantially drawn from him, is more fully poured into this one; by no beginning, no end, no place, no time is any one shut out; and from him from whom he has his being, from him it is essentially granted to him — that which to every creature is naturally inaccessible. But he himself who is God poured out his name, in a manner, like oil, when he did not confound the newly-made angels with a cloud of ignorance, but by the bounty of him who had made them they perceived from whom they had been made, and received that the Father and the Son together are inseparably one God. Nor did this pouring out instruct the angels in heavenly places only, but the same instruction passed over to men on earth as well, and the certainty of divine knowledge marked out Judaea, and in Israel there was [0211B] received the greatness of this so illustrious a name [n: (Psal. LXXV)]. « Adore him, he says, all ye his angels; Sion heard and was glad [n: (Psal. XCVI)]. » Whom the angels in heaven venerate as God and Lord, his fellow-citizens of Sion on earth also, hearing the name, give thanks; and among that people the name of God is celebrated, the oil is strong when poured, though not yet poured out among the nations with a fuller grace. But when the long-desired fullness of time came, in which the height of the divine counsel was made known to us, the Son, sent from the Father, willed to be made man in the Virgin and of the Virgin, and thus that the name which is God should be more fully published abroad both among the Gentiles and among the Jews. « Let the nations know, he says, that thy name is God [n: (Psal. LXXXII)]. » [var: Vulg. Ps. 82:19 *Et cognoscant quia nomen tibi Dominus*, "And let them know that thy name is Lord"] « And all kings shall adore [0211C] him, he says; all nations shall serve him [n: (Psal. LXXI)]. » [var: Vulg. *omnes reges terrae*, "all the kings of the earth"] Whom him? He adds: « His name continueth before the sun [n: (ibid.)]. » What profit is there in this name? « And in him shall all the tribes of the earth be blessed [n: (ibid.)]. » What is that name? « Blessed be the Lord God of Israel [n: (Luc. I)]; and blessed be the name of his majesty. The name of his humility is Man; but the name of his majesty is God [n: (ibid.)]. » Of which the Apostle: « Who is over all things, God blessed for ever [n: (Rom. IX)]. » This God, abiding in the bosom of the Father and dwelling in light inaccessible, having from him, or with him, an equality ungranted to angels as to men, willed that before the *119* Virgin's bringing forth his name should be poured among a few, but after the bringing forth should now be poured out with a fuller rush [0211D] upon all everywhere. For him whom the nations of the Gentiles knew not, being hidden, and whom few of the Jews, taught by the Spirit, in some fashion perceived, after he had been poured into the womb of the Virgin at the end of the times, he was poured out through the streets and squares after the likeness of water. « I am poured out, he says, like water; and all my bones are scattered [n: (Psal. XXI)]. » After the manner of water he poured himself out to the common knowledge of all, when his charity spread itself abroad in our hearts, when he scattered abroad the gifts of his graces, gave to the poor, when he gave to men him who strengthens, the bread of angels. Whom therefore Isaias far above calls hidden, we now see, as with the oil poured out, set forth to all: set forth indeed that he may pour the healing oil over the sick, hidden that he may hide us who are healed in the hiding-place [0212A] of his face. « Truly, he says, thou art a hidden God [n: (Isa. XLV)]. The God of Israel, the Saviour. He is hidden, when in the bosom of the Father he hides himself far from human gaze; but set forth, when out of his mother's womb he pours himself out for the salvation of Israel. Hidden, when in God, as God, he veils his face from us; set forth, when he reveals himself as having come to save his people. Upon the hidden the angels ever burn to look; the poured out and set forth men now love to cherish, and those whom, weighed down by the burden of a corruptible body, the desirable hiding does not yet hold, this pouring out of the oil now renders fervent in spirit. Wherefore the Virgin, when she had said: *Thy name is oil poured out,* subjoined: *Therefore,* and the rest.
