## CHAPTER XII.

[0212B] *Therefore the maidens have loved thee.* Because, he says, thou hast poured out thy name like oil, when, coming forth from the bridal chamber, thou didst truly teach that thou art not man only but God also: this teaching they that are taught of God by no means neglected, but with a spirit made new they loved thee whom before they had not known. And these he named maidens, not by error but prudently, and therein designated the affectionate tenderness of those that love, because this age both loves more tenderly, and adorns itself more curiously, and bestirs itself more often — roving in its eyes, restless in its runnings to and fro, not knowing how to stand still, while its eagerness is unfulfilled. Those therefore whom the mould of hurtful eld does not disfigure, whose inward face the wrinkle of former depravity does not furrow, whose eye grows not dim, whose tooth falls not, whose querulous voice does not [0212C] grow hoarse, whose trembling step does not give way, whose spine is not bent with age, but in whom, such things being laid aside, warmth is strong and the vigour of the mind grows young again — these assuredly kindle more vehemently to loving, bear delay more heavily, faint more sweetly, languish more graciously. And indeed maidens, while they desire to obtain the end of their vow, study more curiously to please those whom they love; and that they may render themselves more pleasing to their sight, they strive with utmost care and utmost zeal to be seen more beautiful. Giving therefore their labour either to the keeping or to the getting of beauty, with a mirror set before them they consider the state of their face, and rejoicing in the good things which they weigh to be theirs by nature, what nature has conferred less of, they supply by watchful care. And the more beautiful they [0212D] have offered themselves for pleasing, the more pleasing are they rendered for bearing children, because they who in the eyes of their bridegrooms are delighted with an ampler beauty are more graciously rewarded by those same with a kiss, and made fruitful by an embrace.

One of such women, taking the mirror anxiously in her hand and discerning in it more diligently what she is, brought forth; because the body which is corrupted discolours the beauty of the soul, and the earthly habitation labours to weigh down a mind given over to many things [n: (Sap. IX)]. And this mind, pressed but perhaps not oppressed, that she may restore it both to righteousness and to salvation, she chastises her body and worthily subjects it to servitude [n: (I Cor. IX)]; with a view to love she shrinks not from enduring peril, nor death; she wipes away whatever spotted thing she judges to displease the Bridegroom; she wills her forehead to be white, her tender cheeks to flush faintly, her lips to breathe shamefastness, [0213A] her words moderation: her whole face, in short, to shine again with a new grace, that she may worthily please him to whom she wills to show herself approved. But when with cleansed brow, with a face so glad, she has shown herself approved and lovable, by the merit of this labour and this love she has not remained unfruitful: but, the Spirit making her fruitful, conceiving in a generative womb, she has borne sons to the Bridegroom by spiritual word and work. To whom she also says: « My little children, of whom I travail in birth again until Christ be formed in you [n: (Gal. IV)]. » And that: « In Christ Jesus, by the Gospel, I have begotten you [n: (I Cor. IV)]. » A certain other maiden, when she was fervent, and in that very fervour was pledging herself to remain fixed and steadfast, being not attentive with a judgment worthy enough to her own infirmity, still raw and insufficient, was found a little over-confident: « Lord, [0213B] says Peter, I am ready to go with thee both into prison and into death [n: (Luc. XXII)]. » And he, whose part it was, as with all else, so also to know Peter himself the better, knowing that he willed indeed what he had said, but was not able: « I tell thee, he says, Peter, the cock shall not crow this day, until thou thrice deny that thou knowest me [n: (ibid.)]. » And since the denial of the diffident would have come utterly to failure, if the mighty grace of him that has mercy had not set a hand beneath; willing that Peter should not only spring back from his rash lapse, but, being afterwards confirmed, should travail with spiritual sons: « I, he says, have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not: and thou *120* being once converted, confirm thy brethren [n: (ibid.)]. » Visited afterward indeed by the regard of divine mercy, and strengthened by the ampler gift of the Spirit breathed upon him, that same Peter grew white-hot to love [0213C] more strongly, to persist more firmly; and by the merit of a stronger love he deserved to hear from the Bridegroom himself: « Simon son of John, lovest thou me more than these? Feed my sheep [n: (Joan. XXI)]. » [var: Vulg. John 21:15 reads *pasce agnos meos*, "feed my lambs"; *pasce oves meas* belongs to v. 17 — Migne joins the question of v. 15 to the command of v. 17] The apostles therefore, and apostolic men, are not undeservedly named maidens, who in this drama are declared to accompany the Virgin Bride; and what renders them maidens is not fewness of days, not of age, but a more fervent faith, an impatient love, the grace of the newness granted them. For when that error had been driven out which had been widely scattered abroad in Judaea, the Pharisees preaching that Galilee would not bring forth Christ, and that, according to them, it was in no wise worthy to be believed that the carpenter's son, the brother of James, should be Christ [n: (Matth. XIII; Marc. VI)] — one who rather among them earned stones [0213D] not for a good work but for blasphemy [n: (Joan X)], and because, being man, he confessed himself God by usurpation; when, I say, this error had been driven out, the newness of faith was conferred upon the maidens, and what the Bride had earlier drawn in secret was carried over to the apostles. To me, says the Bride, thy name, O God, thy oil poured itself in, when thy dew poured over my fleece, when thy divinity poured over me, when I beheld within me God entire, the Word of the Father. The Son of God, and I myself, wholly full, loved thee with a plenary affection. But when I was filled, this oil was poured back upon those that are near, and, that it might be able to be received, was poured out upon the maidens; and what was poured into me, since they could not receive it, they neglected; poured out upon themselves, with fervent spirit they loved it. For none followed that same Virgin more familiarly, [0214A] or by her mediation more fully attained the knowledge of the Bridegroom, than the apostles, who, both before and after Christ's passion having long conversed with her, heard from her more frequently the mystery of the Word incarnate. These « all, he says, were persevering with one accord in prayer with Mary the mother of Jesus [n: (Act. I)]. » [var: Vulg. Acts 1:14 *Hi omnes erant perseverantes unanimiter in oratione cum mulieribus, et Maria matre Iesu, et fratribus eius* — Migne drops "with the women" and "and his brethren"] One of whom beyond the rest took the care of that same Virgin, and at Christ's command diligently reduced himself to her service; and imparted to her, as a son to a mother, a compliant diligence, and, having received her into his own, served her with the ministry of a provider. But she, having conversed so long a time with John and with the rest, who shall weigh with what affection she embraced them, with what care she strove to teach them the things she had known earlier and more perfectly, and to recount more frequently and more insistently [0214B] the wondrous order both of her conceiving and of her bringing forth? For the things which she saw through, fulfilled in herself by a high and ineffable counsel, she grudged not to those that stood by her, nor to maidens of this sort; but she rejoiced to call them to mind often in their ears, willing that they equally should obtain a share of the grace granted her. Accordingly, when she had said: *Therefore the maidens have loved thee,* she added: *Draw me,* etc.
