## CHAPTER XIV.

*The king hath brought me into his storerooms.* She who a little before had asked that she be drawn by him whom she loves, now confesses herself drawn and brought into his storerooms; and the grace she has found she does not hide out of envy, nor [0217B] dishonour by obscure silence, but to the praise of the Bridegroom and to his glory she makes it manifest to the maidens. For because, burning with an ampler desire, she shone again with ampler merits, the Bridegroom brought her as it were in a snatching into his own secret chamber; and to her, thus brought in, he showed the wondrous abundance of spiritual things, the presence of the ointments, the truth of the gifts promised. Having entered thereupon and beholding the incomparable storehouses of Solomon, she was astounded, and as it were grew stiff at the grace of the new vision; and having no more spirit in her, nor able to contain herself for joy, she ran back outside, announcing the benefit she had deserved to obtain. You, she says, to whom divine grace has already granted that you should run, see that you incur not a blameworthy failure through delay or through toil, but constantly and bravely run to riches that are not uncertain, unknown [0217C] perhaps to you, but to me not wholly untried; and he who brought me into his fullness, and has not yet led you to what you have desired — by that counsel which he himself knows — wills indeed that you should rejoice at my present goods, and by my example have a surer confidence of your own. And him the Bride here willed to name not bridegroom, not beloved, but King, willing, as I judge, to designate the royal power of the Bridegroom: to commend his wealth, his riches, the fullness of all things, whereof the storerooms of kings beyond all others are found to overflow. Moreover, that he is powerful no rightly wise man can doubt, since truthful Scripture is found to affirm it by many testimonies: for both he casteth not away the mighty, since he himself also is mighty [0217D] [n: (Job XXXVI)], and: « Whatsoever he would, he hath done [n: (Psal. CXIII)], » and power is at hand to him when he shall will [n: (Sap. XII)]; and that he is rich, the Apostle says: « Who is rich, etc. [n: (Rom. X)], » and the Gospel: « There was a certain rich man who had a steward [n: (Luc. XVI)]; » without doubt signifying God. Finally, who is richer than the Lord, whose « is the earth and the fullness thereof, the world, and they that dwell therein? [n: (Psal. XXIII.)] » [var: Vulg. Ps 23:1 *et universi qui habitant in eo*, "and all they that dwell therein"] The Bridegroom of the Virgin therefore is not unfittingly called King, by which name both his power and his abundance are commended; who moreover is recorded to have not a storeroom but storerooms, that his store may be taught to be far greater — since it is certain that more things are contained in storerooms than in a storeroom, and that many things are filled up with many, and diverse with diverse abundance. Into [0218A] many, then, rather than into one, does the Virgin declare herself brought in, signifying that she has attained the hidden treasures of wisdom and of knowledge. In which she deserves to receive not the spirit of this world, but the Spirit which is of God [n: (I Cor. II)], that she may know the things that have been given her by God; and knowing, may venerate, so that when she sees in herself the Word of God, and loves him made flesh, yet having the mind of Christ, she may know him no more according to the flesh [n: (II Cor. V)]. Moreover the hidden things of the divine wisdom, uncertain to all others, are made certain to the Virgin by an incomparable gift, in an unspeakable manner; and she rejoices to be led into that sanctuary of secrets, whereto the other virgins rejoice to be brought, not with equal step alongside her, but after her. She first, she more perfectly, both received the announcing angel and, according to his word, felt [0218B] the overshadowing Spirit: she knew that in the conceiving she bore no fault, no coition, and that in the bringing forth she uttered no groan according to Eve's law. She knew that he whom she cherishes, whom she embraces according to the natural need of little ones, is the life and the vital nourishment of angels; that he whom she holds in her lap, whom she suckles at a mother's breast, is God from eternity in the hidden bosom of God the Father: she knew herself, by a wondrous newness, the mother of God and man, and herself the nurse of her own nourisher and maker, and of all things. These, perhaps, are the storerooms into which the Bridegroom brought the Virgin Mother, which none but the wise Architect built with his own hand, and which in building he filled with so great a store of spices that the odour, as of the sweetest ointments, may rouse to running all that are slow. There is as it were one storeroom [0218C] whereby the divinity came down into man unconsumed, another storeroom whereby humanity was taken up into God; which two, namely the Word and the flesh, are so joined to each other by a certain wondrous work that in Christ they are both naturally diverse and personally not disjoined. And whosoever seems to understand and take up either of them without the other, if he confesses Christ either man without God or God without man, is seen to have entered one storeroom, as it were, of the King, *123* but to have sprung back from the other either by ignorance or by contumacy. But Mary the Virgin entered not one only, but both alike, who professed him whom she bore to be at once God and man; upon whom she leaned the more devoutly as the more surely, holding in memory, and sweetly calling to mind, [0218D] what she brought forth, and whom, and in what manner. For she brought forth not man only, but God also, whose wondrous humanity and humanized divinity was indeed uncovered to the Virgin brought within, but for some time was covered to the maidens themselves — who, running in the odour after that Mother and Virgin, shall be brought forward, and into those same storerooms, when the time and their merits shall have agreed, shall be led in. And with these to be led in the Virgin declares that she will rejoice, signifying that through them she will receive an increase of joy; since she who is already secure concerning the gift received by herself, when they too shall have received it, will enjoy an ampler joy for their sakes. This is what she adds: *We will exult,* etc.
