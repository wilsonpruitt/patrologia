## CHAPTER XXII.

*They have set me a keeper in the vineyards, my own vineyard I have not kept.* Any soul whatever may rightly be called a vineyard, of which the one seems to be compared to the other by a certain likeness; because as a vineyard or vine is found to consist of many things — of the tendril, the cluster, the shoot — so also the soul is marked out with many powers or virtues. Let not the sons of my mother glory, says the Virgin, that [0234D] they have fulfilled their wickedness upon me, that raging against me they have filled my soul with heavy bitterness, that my vigilance did not keep it against their violences, but that their sword passed through it. For whence they the more violently took away my own soul, thence, not knowing what they did, they transferred me to the keeping of many; and when they brought the graver loss upon my vineyard, they conferred a useful benefit upon you, daughters of Jerusalem. The abandonment or failure of my vineyard is without doubt your advancement in spiritual things; and in that they hurt me for a time, as they reckon, they set for you, to whom I pass over, a necessary keeper. And not unfittingly may there be signified by the name of vineyards not only souls one by one, but by flocks the assemblies [0235A] of souls; because as many shoots obtain the name and the yield of the vine, so many souls together make a flock or an assembly, to which the Lord, speaking: « Fear not, he says, little flock [n: (Luc. XII)]. » And the Prophet: « Thou hast brought a vineyard out of Egypt [n: (Psal. LXXIX)]. » And elsewhere: « The vineyard of the Lord of Sabaoth is the house of Israel [n: (Isa. V)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa 5:7 *vinea enim Domini exercituum*, "of the Lord of hosts"; Migne prints the Hebraism *Sabaoth*] The sons therefore of the mother, who brought the aforesaid annoyances upon the Virgin, transferred her, driven from her own vineyard, to many others; because she who was wickedly despised by her brethren and fellow tribesmen was received by many vineyards or assemblies of the Church. But the Virgin did not fortify her own vineyard by her keeping, because that wicked people obeyed neither her warnings nor her examples, but, rejoicing to be left to its own will by a judgment hidden from it, with a hardened neck [0235B] ran into that curse of Isaiah: « Woe to the sinful nation, to a people heavy with iniquity, to a wicked seed, to wicked sons! » [n: (Isa. I.)] Whom perchance both the prophet and the Virgin therefore named sons, and not daughters, because by the masculine name she designated their perverse strength — men mighty and strong against the Virgin's soul, drunk with the wine of the damned, in whose merits they rejoice, and whose penalties also they shall bear: « Woe to you that are mighty, he says, to drink wine, and strong to mingle drunkenness [n: (Isa. V)]. » And that other: « The strong have sought after my soul [n: (Psal. LIII)]. » These strong and mighty men, when they drove the Virgin back from the keeping of their own selves, compelled her to pass over to the keeping of our vineyards; and what was worthily taken from them that were nearer, according to their merits, was conferred upon us [0235C] that are further off by the grace of a divine gift. How many assemblies throughout the whole world rejoice today to be fortified by this Virgin's patronage, and their churches to be guarded by her welcome solicitude, who, though they execrate, as is right, the malice of the lost sons, yet give thanks that it was useful and necessary to themselves? But to say nothing of others, brethren, is not this assembly of yours gathered in honour of this Virgin, and are not this place and church dedicated in her name; and do you not rejoice in her keeping and her special patronage, embracing your keeper with a certain affection and spiritual tenderness? Which of you, when he calls her memory to mind, or even when he hears her name, does not rejoice with devotion, does not applaud with devout [0235D] obedience, hoping to find her Son placable and propitious if he can come before the mother with due reverence? Happy fault, useful battle, fruitful perversity, whereby a hateful brotherhood rages against our Virgin, whereby, the ragers being left behind, a gracious blessing passes over to us, whereby the Virgin disowns her brothers, reproves her own household, hates her own; sets our vines in order, hedges them with a wall, prunes, digs, and (that I may conclude briefly) keeps us with all diligence. Nevertheless, because in these vineyards too there may sometimes *133* be found men who presume by their evil manners to pull down those same vineyards, the virgin, turned toward the Bridegroom, wills to be more fully taught [sic: *a beo*] who are his, that she may beware of those whom she has known not to be reckoned in the number of his own. And so she says: *Show me,* etc.
