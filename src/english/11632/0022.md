## CHAPTER XXIII.

[0236A]

*Show me, O thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest in the midday, lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions.* Thou, she says, whom I love not in word, not in tongue, not in the mere noise of the lips, but with heart and mind and with all the strength of the inward parts: show where thou feedest, where a welcome refreshment is prepared for thee, where thou findest the food wherewith thy hunger may be filled. « For I was hungry [n: (Matth. XXV)], » he says. I know that a prudent shepherd does not tarry in all places, not in all pastures, but prudently searches out which they are, and in which a profit is to be had; and shrinking from and passing by those which he has known not to have the sap of life, he feeds and refreshes in those which he has perceived not to shrink from life. And beautifully she inquires so anxiously not about the food but about the place, [0236B] because perchance she knows well enough with what fodder he is delighted, holding it certain that his food is cleanness, justice, truth, and other such things as effectually work true holiness. But who knows where this holiness is truly and firmly to be had, unless he be taught by him who teacheth man knowledge, since man knows not what is in man, nor is judgment worthily made concerning another [n: (I Cor. II)] — whose face, though it appear, yet whose conscience is not seen? The Virgin therefore wills not to be seduced by the outward face of men, but to be led into the very chamber, as it were, of truth; and to come to the place where the Bridegroom rejoices to find pasture and rest, not by a material foot but by a spiritual eye. Finally Joseph too is sent by his father's solicitude to visit again his brothers in the pastures, [0236C] and coming into Sichem, a man being found, most diligently asks where they feed — the boy not understanding that he would be a burden to his elders, and that he would not rejoice, as he had hoped, in the finding he desired. « I seek my brethren, he says; show me where they feed. And the man said to him: They are departed from this place; and I heard them saying: Let us go into Dothaim. Joseph therefore went forward after his brethren, and found them in Dothaim [n: (Gen. XXXVII)]. » Rightly is Joseph read to have found his envious brothers in Dothaim, to which place they are reported to have come not by chance but of set purpose, knowing perchance that they would there not so much find a life-giving pasture as fulfil a wicked will and a decree of crime. Dothaim is interpreted *their green [0236D] fodder,* or *sufficient failing.* Their green fodder, that is, the envious men's, is envy itself, which they taste, which they chew over, which grows strong in their minds, which with a pestilent savour grows sweet inwardly in their throats; which is not trodden down by a censor's foot, does not wither by a swift repentance, but with untiring rancour grows green even to the height of crime. For nothing is more welcome to the faithless than to envy their betters, to disparage the absent, sometimes to fawn upon the present; with a feigning countenance (when the power of hurting is not at hand) to smile, and, when the time for hurting has shone out, to hurl the poisoned dart. As therefore in Osee Ephraim feeds on wind, when he vainly desires what he hopes for to come to pass *(Osee XII)*, so every envious man is not [0237A] unfittingly recorded to have come into Dothaim, when he is delighted with the poison of spite as with a green fodder. But because Dothaim is also interpreted *sufficient failing,* whence that wicked man tends the more vehemently toward the effecting of his malice, thence, guilty of the crime, he falls into an ampler failing of conscience; and that damage alone which he loves to inflict upon another plainly suffices for a torment to the man who labours under envy. Which a certain heathen beautifully hints: *Than envy,* he says, *the Sicilian tyrants have found no*
*greater torment.*
But whoever is agreed to bear in his mind that venomous spite, than which the Sicilians found no heavier torment, he assuredly is convicted of failing from the quiet state of the mind, and a failing of this kind suffices for the death of him that fails. For he who envies is found to hurt himself more [0237B] than his neighbour, and what he attempts to inflict upon another he is found to suffer first; and his own envy, which for the most part is not felt by the other, pulls down the man from whom it proceeds, as the moth a garment. Joseph's brothers therefore, who, as you have read, were found in Dothaim, that is, already forestalled by a certain inward failing and by envy, seeing Joseph and speaking beforehand one with another, run to meet him as he comes, offering to him who knew nothing not a greeting, not a kiss, but hatred. « Behold, they say, the dreamer cometh; come, let us kill him [n: (Gen. XXXVII)]. » A wicked place, a place execrable and unspeakable, a place to be detested in the judgment of all (of those, at least, who are soundly wise); a place worthily to be named not refreshment but failing, where charity perishes, brotherhood fails, and an accursed spite prevails. [0237C] Because therefore many are known to feed in Dothaim and in other places of this kind, and whoever have followed them are struck with some penalty or other, the Virgin, anxious not about the general breadth of the many, asks in what *134* place and in what special solitude the Bridegroom feeds. For she knows that his place is not possessed by many, she knows that in his pastures there are not many lords; she knows likewise that not every pasture is the Bridegroom's, and therefore she desires the more diligently to know which is his.
