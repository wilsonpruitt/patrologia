## CHAPTER XXIV.

*Show me where thou feedest, where thou liest.* I know, she says, that thou feedest not in the covert of the reed, that thy cleanness does not rest in moist places, wherein that Behemoth lies hid in a rest that is welcome to him [n: (Job XL)]; wherein he cherishes [0237D] and promotes his wicked depravities, and by a pestilent feeding brings evil wills into effect. In these indeed thy soul takes no pleasure, thou hast no will in them, as by Isaiah's oracle thou truly assertest concerning the Jews, who, not being subject to thy will, to thy good pleasure, are cast forth from thy refectory, far from thy face. For how dost thou feed where thou dost not yet choose to dwell, where, straitness compelling, thou lovest not to walk more freely, where the appetite of feeding is beaten back by a certain bitterness set against it, when a wicked seed, when a provoking house is found? I am sure that mount Gelboe is not held pleasing to thy pasture, wherein Saul, David's adversary [n: (I Reg. XXXI)], fears not to enter [0238A] battle, wherein the strong fall and death adjudges Saul and Jonathan to its own laws, upon which no dew, no rain comes, as David complaining curses it. I know likewise that the plain of Sennaar is not offered to thy pasture [n: (Gen. XI)], wherein, pride reigning, no reverence is paid to thee, wherein the presumption of an unskilled multitude is struck by the diversity of tongues, and, they being divided one from another, a tower of infinite greatness is not completed. The plain of Mageddo also I judge does not please thee for feeding [n: (II Paral. III)], wherein the archers struck the just Josiah unto death: from which that justice which was already glad in the hope of victory, the fortune of the battle being turned, is carried back utterly dead. But hast thou perchance chosen the valley of Tophet as suited to thy pasture [n: (IV Reg. XXIII)], which thou hast known to be most delightful in situation, [0238B] foul in sacrilege; wherein the children of Israel paid honour to God, not to thee, but to Moloch, in a wretched superstition, and by a blind rite carried over to him their sons and daughters through the fire? Not thine is the pleasantness of this valley, which pleases not Josiah either, whose pestilent rite that same king's sagacity took away, when those whom the very face of the place and its most delightful beauty invited to sacrilege, the scattered bones of the dead and the heaped foulness of corpses drove back. Because therefore thou feedest not in mountains, plains and valleys of this kind, I ask that thou teach and more diligently show where thou dost feed, if perchance thou art foretold to have other mountains, plains and valleys, in whose lap thou art delighted, feeding or lying down.

The Virgin indeed knows that other mountains are commended in the Scriptures, in which no one is read to perish [0238C] but to be saved, which the Bridegroom himself, loving them and visiting them with a friendly affection, makes worthy of himself and fit for a special pasture. Joel the prophet: « In mount Sion, he says, shall be salvation [n: (Joel. II)]. » And David: « He chose mount Sion, which he loved [n: (Psal. LXXVII)]; » and again: « Mount Sion, the city of the great King [n: (Psal. XLVII)]; » and that other: « The Lord loveth the gates of Sion [n: (Psal. LXXXVI)]. » And what wonder if that mountain, which the Lord embraces with a benevolent affection, is asserted to offer welcome fodder to him that embraces it, so that the lover is not denied to feed in that which he loves — to whom there is no refreshment at all save where his love is? Therefore perchance John: « I saw, he says, upon mount Sion a Lamb standing [n: (Apoc. XIV)]. » [var: Vulg. Apoc 14:1 *et ecce Agnus stabat supra montem Sion*, a finite verb, "the Lamb was standing"; the exposition immediately following turns on the participle *stantem*] But standing to what end, unless [0238D] for feeding? For the lamb stands while he feeds; but while he rests, he lies down. But the Virgin, anxious not about one only but about both, asks where the Bridegroom feeds and where he lies down, as though one place were for feeding and another none the less for resting. For the Bridegroom is the Lamb. For of him of whom John elsewhere says: « He that hath the bride is the bridegroom [n: (Joan. III)], » of the same he said: « Behold the Lamb of God [n: (Joan. I)]. » The Bridegroom therefore, or the Lamb, feeds upon a mountain — not upon that which Isaiah names dark [n: (Cap. XIII)], nor upon that which Zacharias asserts to be great and to be brought down into a plain before Zorobabel [n: (Cap. IV)]; but upon mount Sion, which is commended by the merit of seeing, which, having its name from the thing, [0239A] past the objects set close at hand, descries things more remotely placed. Of which mountain Isaiah prophesying: « Send forth, he says, the Lamb, O Lord, from the rock of the desert to the mount of the daughter of Sion [n: (Isa. XVI)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa 16:1 *Emitte agnum, Domine, dominatorem terrae, de petra deserti*; the title "the ruler of the earth" is not printed here] Finally through Ezechiel the Lord promises: « Behold I, he says, will feed my sheep [n: (Ezech. XXXIV)], » and, determining where he will feed them: « In the mountains, he says, of Israel will I feed them [n: (ibid.)]. » Israel is interpreted *seeing God.* Good therefore and rich would I call those mountains to which it is granted to behold God, whose pasture the good Shepherd is found to promise to his sheep; because the saints who are pre-eminent in virtues are lifted up by their merits, who, dwelling in the heavens, are flooded with the light of contemplation: they assuredly refresh God's good pleasure, God's desire, God's will; they offer to his commandment the richness of a manifold [0239B] spiritual obedience. « To the saints, he says, who are in his land, he hath made wonderful all my wills in them [n: (Psal. XV)]. » He hath made wonderful, he says, that is, he fed, cherished, and wonderfully brought through to effect, that I might do in them all things whatsoever I willed, and that their works might be great and wonderful, sought out unto all my wills [n: (Psal. CX)]. Finally *135* in the Gospel the disciples: « Whither wilt thou, they say, that we go and prepare for thee that thou mayest eat the pasch? » [n: (Marc. XIV.)] And he: « The master, he says, of the house will show you a large upper room furnished; there make ready [n: (ibid.)]. » In that he says an upper room, it is high and eminent; in that it is large, it is broad and sufficient; in that it is furnished, it is fair and alluring. The upper room in which the Bridegroom's refreshment is, is commended by the height of its dignity, [0239C] because the heaven of heaven is assigned to the Lord, and the earth to the sons of men [n: (Psal. CXIII)]; and, that it may suffice the more, it is enlarged with a great space, because he is not gladdened by a scanty seat and a niggardly tripod; and, that it may ever allure him, it is spread and fitted with wrought carpets and sweet-smelling flowers, because he that is beautiful above the sons of men spurns things unclean, unseemly and foul. The Bridegroom feeds also in a plain — in that plain, namely, which is levelled by the tearing out of masses of rubble, which, rooting up thorns and briers, is beautified with a pleasant surface; which, the barren frost being wiped away, is made fruitful by a spring-like mildness, laughs with green herbs, and is varied with the manifold colour of flowers. Of which the first and chief says: « I am the flower of the field [n: (Cant. II)]. » Rich is that plain, nor is it to be negligently passed over in silence; upon whose [0239D] face Ezechiel sees many bones [n: (Ezech. XXXVII)], and exceedingly dry, which by an ineffable hearing perceived the word of the Lord, and received sinews, flesh and skin by a wonderful joining, and at last, the spirit coming from the four winds, lived again. Finally in the Gospel [n: (Luc. VI)] the Bridegroom, coming down from the mountain, stood in a plain place, wherein, because the power of healing and the fullness of grace shone forth, a copious multitude of peoples flowed together to him from every side; and the good Shepherd, marking that it was eager to hear and to be healed, refreshes it with a generous hand with the word of life, with saving fodder. The Bridegroom feeds also in the valleys, which David asserts to abound with corn [n: (Psal. LXIV)], and in the valley of Josaphat, which seems to glitter with the grace of humility, while the greater each man is, the more he makes himself less in all things, and he whom his neighbour worthily [0240A] commends and lifts up, discolours himself by his own judgment. « When you have done, he says, all good things; say that we are unprofitable servants [n: (Luc. XVII)]. » [var: Vulg. Luke 17:10 *cum feceritis omnia quae praecepta sunt vobis*, "all things that are commanded you"] And that other: « If we judged ourselves, we should not be judged [n: (I Cor. II)]. » In mountains, plains and valleys of this kind the Bridegroom feeds, because loftiness of life, evenness of manners, and humility of judgment is the Bridegroom's welcome refreshment; which as a prudent craftsman he himself works in his own faithful ones, while he rejoices to bring his wills through to effect in them. « My meat is, he says, to do the will of him that sent me [n: (Joan. IV)]. »
