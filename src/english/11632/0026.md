## CHAPTER THE FIRST.

[0245D]

*If thou know not thyself, O fairest among women, go forth, and go thy way after the footsteps of the flocks.* After the Virgin's many words, wherein she showed the heats of her own mind, wherein she asked that the longed-for kiss be given her and the Bridegroom's chamber be shown her: not hardness, not contempt, nor at length any further bar of silence holds the Bridegroom, but, having diligently heard her vows and prayers, he gives an answer. [0246D] And assuredly the Virgin, who so anxiously wills to be taught where the Bridegroom lies down, he admonishes first that she neglect not to gaze upon herself the more studiously, that, turning to her own things, she be not ignorant of her own beauty, lest perchance, being unknown, it be discoloured by mould or by negligence. For not warily enough does he kindle himself to acquire the rest, who does not attend with the eye of worthy consideration to the things first acquired, and who heaps up with rash labour the riches he has coveted, while he [0247A] does not watch over with diligent care the things laid up long since. As the Poet describes, the hunter errs not a little and offends, who chases what flees and, spurning what is taken, leaves it behind; and the prophet Aggeus showed that he also is none the less to be reproved who put the wages he had gathered into a bag with holes [n: (Agg. I)]. Accordingly the Bridegroom sends the Virgin first to consider her own beauty, and does not permit her to give the things past over to oblivion: putting her in mind of this very thing, which he counsels her to remember, that she may the more easily be able to incline her ear to his admonitions. *O fair one,* he says, *among women.* That that Virgin whom the Son of God chose beforehand for himself from eternity for a mother, in whom, taking flesh at the fitting time, he gathered himself into narrower limbs, of whom he willed to be born, the hedge of her flesh not being broken [0247B] by a new manner of birth, whose son he was made, being before the birth and after the birth utterly uncorrupted in chastity: that that Virgin, I say, was beautiful among the rest, that is, before the rest of women — this I judge worthy to be believed, nor is a faith of this sort faulty. For neither is it credible that God, the Son of God, should have chosen a mother swarthy, one-eyed, marked with a scrofulous swelling, or hunchbacked: and not rather one beautiful with a snowy countenance, a rosy colour, pleasing eyes and a full body — so that she who was chosen before the rest of women for a worthier office should not only be uninfected with any foulness, but also, in bodily appearance, fairer than all and perfect. Although the fact that she is called fair among women befits not so much the grace of her countenance and the comeliness of her body, as her perfect manners and that admirable [0247C] holiness of hers, by which, full of grace by the angel's witness — which is nowhere attested of any other woman — she alone deserved to be made the mother of Christ alone. Regarding a beauty of this sort, the angel says: « Blessed art thou among women [n: (Luc. I)]. » For she herself is blessed among women, that is, in the midst of women, upon whom by Eve's desert a harsher curse is inflicted, to whom, as to her that first offended, they are by a wretched law conformed, and, being liable to sins, are deformed by their motherly likeness to her. Among whom, wretched and deformed, an ampler beauty was granted to this Virgin, who, having overpassed the old curse and the hereditary law, the Spirit overshadowing her, put far from herself every heat of malice and every stain of sin, and by the merits of innocence and by the beauty of her mind [0247D] set herself before all women. And to the increase of her beauty, rejoicing in the wholeness of her body, she is made fruitful in a new kind untried by all; and by an unspeakable birth the virgin brought forth such a son that no other bore, either before or afterward, one mightier or one equal. Accordingly so great and so incomparable a loftiness — what is it but the wondrous and eminent beauty of this Virgin, wherein she was not only not forestalled by the rest of women, but was herself found far fairer and more excellent among them all? To the considering of which the Bridegroom calls her back *140* and leads her again, and does not yet more fully bring her into the longed-for chamber, that she may first know with how great a grace she is adorned, and whence and whither she has come may be recalled by a mindful humility. Thou, he says, who hast now obtained a greater grace than all women, [0248A] and, remaining a virgin, art wondrously made the mother of God, while thou findest not what remains and dost not yet come to the thing longed for, attend more diligently to what has already been conferred upon thee by my gift. For since, greedy of things to come, thou dost not yet prevail to obtain them, if thou take care humbly to remember the grace already received, the perception of things present will more solidly confirm thy faith, and faith confirmed will await things to come both the more sweetly and the more securely. But if thou set aside and know not the things which thou already provest as though present, and, while thou graspest at things untried, art found neglectful of the things proved: *Go forth* — which indeed I say to thee neither counselling nor commanding, but striking fear with threats, and, foreknowing what is to come, foretelling these things. Go forth, he says, that is, thou shalt go forth, and shalt deserve to be flung back from the secret place, thou shalt be made far [0248B] from the sanctuary into which thou askest to enter, thou shalt be driven from that very welcome chamber which thou willest to be shown thee, and thou shalt seem to go forth when thou art not permitted to go in. So indeed they are said to be blotted out of the book of the living who deserve not to be written with the just in the eternal volume; and by common speech we say that these things are taken away from us which, being hoped or longed for, we know are at last not conferred upon us. And so the Virgin also will complain that she goes forth outside, when by the desert of her ignorance she shall suffer repulse from the entrance, when, forgetful of her own beauty, she shall appear foul and discoloured, when out of the remembrance of things present she shall not have a sure confidence of things to come. But if, O Virgin, thou shalt mindfully consider that thou hast the pledge and earnest of the Spirit, which none of the elect deserved to obtain in that manner at all; thou shalt assuredly know that whatsoever [0248C] thou worthily askest is not taken away, nor is the just desire of her that is aflame defrauded, but deferred. For thou who hast received so many and so great things above all women, who without a father didst conceive a Son by the working of the Holy Spirit, who, according to Gabriel, didst find a wondrous and unspeakable grace with God, while thou didst bear him whom thou hadst conceived without coupling and without groaning — what dost thou henceforth by right reckon to be denied thee, if only the time shall come when it shall be worthy to be given? He therefore who has conferred so much, from him await what remains; and if, as for the most part appears, he shall make a delay to thy longing mind, await it again; and lest perchance the delay weigh upon thee, remember what thou hast already received, and by the memory of the things received strengthen thyself unto faith and patience. For in that which thou already holdest thou hast surpassed all the beauty of women, [0248D] and with the fair face of thy mind thou hast gone before all the daughters of Eve; and if thou shalt know this, and shalt render worthy thanks to the giver, then, sighing after what remains, thou wilt not give an undue murmur to him that defers. For to this end he defers, that the desire deferred may groan more fervently and more constantly, not that, faith being shaken, it may faint away through impatience; that in the time of delay thou mayest cultivate and adorn thy fair face the more amply, and not more negligently discolour it with the dust of forgetfulness and with a degenerate ignorance. *If therefore thou know not thyself,* go forth from my company, from my bosom, from me, from the very face at last of him whom thou hast loved: *And go thy way after the footsteps of the flocks,* which a little before thou didst dread. *Go forth,* he says, *and go thy way.* For whoever has gone forth from the sanctuary which he ought to have entered, going on to worse, is now not permitted [0249A] to stand, but to go away; and because he feared not to forget himself and the counsel of the one admonishing him, he is compelled to journey far from himself and farther from the one admonishing. And he goes away after the footsteps of the flocks who follows their depravity going before him, who, being lovers of errors and of sects, know not the unity of the faith; or who, living after the manner of beasts, entice and deceive those who follow them, and grievously persecute with their own depraved manners those whom they go before.
