## CHAPTER II.

*And feed thy kids beside the tents of the shepherds.* When anyone has gone forth and gone away, having his understanding already darkened, he is said to feed kids, while he brings depraved affections through to their effect, while he cherishes and furthers inconstancy of mind, wantonness of manners, bodily uncleanness, and loves to lead an animal [0249B] life. He perhaps feeds lambs who, strengthened by the spirit of pitying grace, has harmless acts, clean and simple thoughts, whose fruit and whose whiteness that highest Shepherd approves and loves, and to the feeding of them he admonishes his lover: « If thou lovest me, he says, feed my lambs [n: (Joan. XXI)]. » [var: Vulg. John 21:15 *Simon Ioannis, diligis me plus his? … Pasce agnos meos* — a question in the received text, printed here as a condition; Migne gives the interrogative form of the same verse at 0254B] But he feeds kids who, with corrupted mind, with seduced spirit, with lost will, does evil things, rejoices in malice, is weighed down by equity; and follows after those things which the just judge judges to be set on the left hand [n: (Matth. XXV)], that upon them the avenging angel with his ministers may harshly rage. Kids of this sort he is said to feed, not in the tents of the shepherds, from whose fellowship he is barred by his own urgent malice, whom their life does not entice, whose works do not delight but offend him, nor [0249C] does he love to imitate what they set forth in doctrine and in manners. And because he is not found to dwell in the tents of the shepherds, in which the voice of exultation and of salvation alike is heard, he at least remains near, nor are pastures found farther off, while *141* he holds the name of Christ, no other baptism, the sacraments in common. And so, shrinking from virtue, he glories in the show of piety, and, not holding the reality, is visibly overshadowed by the sacrament of faith; and on this account he is recorded to feed, not within, but beside the tents of the shepherds, because, although he be in some sort without, yet the appearance of the thing is neighbour to it. Shepherds are all the perfect, who follow out the right faith not so much in name as in works, who, washed once in the laver of regeneration, [0249D] are often washed again with devout tears, who devoutly celebrate the sacraments of the Church unto their own salvation, since they obtain not so much the outward form of them as the power. In which tents they correct their own depravities into straightness, they subject their own wills to the divine good pleasure; and with discreet governance they rule their affections as they do their works, and day by day pass on to ampler advances. But in these men do not dwell, though they draw somewhat near to them, they who destroy the faith by their works, while they do not forsake the sacraments of the faith, who, not having the power, are adorned with its outward title; and they imitate as shepherds those whom they shall have seen to be good, not in the reality, but in name. But perhaps to some it will seem hard and austere that the Bridegroom addresses the Virgin Mother thus, that to her who has spoken beforehand of sweet things and holds him lovingly he thus threatens at the very beginning [0250A] of his answer; since it is certain that after the aforesaid kiss she does not thus go forth from him or go away, and accordingly that things so heavy and so superfluous do not befit one that kisses or loves. For although it can more fittingly be assigned to any soul, or even to the Church, to whom, when she offends, Christ judges it useful now to promise and now to threaten: yet the beauty of this one is believed to be especially the more constant, in whom God is made man, and the fulness of the Godhead dwells bodily. I judge it worthy therefore that we should see a little more attentively how the Bridegroom loves to answer like for like to the Virgin who has spoken beforehand in the language of love; and how, having already perceived beforehand her prayers, her vows, her untiring desire, he, returning praises for praises, has not dissented from her desire.
