## CHAPTER IV.

*And feed thy kids beside the tents of the shepherds.* He calls the apostles kids, who, although it is not denied that by the Virgin's word and [0254A] example they advance to a great holiness, yet, while they live a mortal life and are held in the prison-house of the flesh, according to the Bridegroom's counsel confess themselves unprofitable servants, and, being ignorant of their own justice, confess their sins with a humble voice [n: (Luc. XVII)]. One of whom: « If we shall say, he says, that we have no sin, we seduce ourselves, and the truth is not in us [n: (I Joan. I)]. » And another: « In many things, he says, we all offend [n: (Jac. III)]. » Those therefore whom the whiteness of conscience, the beauty of innocence, the simplicity of manners, the eminent holiness of merits render lambs, the same the confession of sin with fear makes kids, while as yet a man knows not whether he be worthy of hatred or of love. Accordingly, when any just men are called lambs, and innocence merits their praises, [0254B] which are not held worthy to be commended save by Christ's gift: he says « my », as he pointed out long afterward to Peter, when to him that loved he assigned the care of them that love. « Simon son of John, lovest thou me? feed my lambs [n: (Joan. XXI)]. » But when he wills them, by the desert of sin, to be reckoned by the name of kids — whom James and John assert are not held to be without sin — he says « thy », that he may lay upon the Virgin an ampler care of them, whereby she may keep watch over the healing of them, and the Virgin may carry them forward to a better state. As it was said to Moses: « Go down, thy people hath sinned [n: (Exod. XXXII)]. » Or because, as the lambs by the Gospel's testimony obtain the right hand, so the kids are known to be set at the Bridegroom's left [n: (Matth. XXV)]: by the kids he wills, not unfittingly, all the Virgin's acts to be signified, that is, whatever [0254C] in this life the Virgin is bidden to work in time. For the chastising of herself, care for others, preaching or doctrine, sweats and colds, watchings, fasting, discipline are indeed Christ's gifts, but they seem to pertain to his left hand, because, not fixed with a steadfast step, they quickly deserve to obtain an end. Yet if they be fed, they also make an increase; not as it is in Osee: « Foolish Ephraim feedeth the wind [n: (Ose. XII)], » [var: Vulg. Hos 12:1 *Ephraim pascit ventum*, with no *stultus*, "foolish"] but let the Bride take care to feed beside the tents of the shepherds, that is, that she conform herself to the precepts of the Scriptures and to their authors. The shepherds are Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, Moses and David, the patriarchs and the prophets, of whom some are found to have laboured, in the letter, in the feeding of flocks, and all of them to have kept watch far more anxiously in ruling and correcting [0254D] manners. And *144* perhaps such shepherds therefore choose to dwell in tents, because they teach and love to hasten from virtue to virtue; and nothing present suffices them as they stretch toward a greater advance, until, that which is in part being done away, they come at last to the perfect. Beside whose tents, and according to whose doctrine and example, the Virgin is bidden to rule and direct her own acts, that she may follow the rule of the Scriptures, which the long series of the prophets long ago set forth as a thing to be known and held. For although the Virgin Mother by a certain special grace already goes before those same authors, yet in time their authority goes before her, nor is it an injury if she be invited to their form and commendable doctrine, that [sic: *vidilicet*] she may follow by [0255A] example those whom she now seems to excel in merits. But whence the Virgin has this, that she may prevail to do what she is admonished, that, not knowing herself, she may go forth, and, going away, may rejoice to feed the kids — this the Bridegroom shows her, that she may glory in the Lord alone, and, knowing not her own face, may marvel at his face alone. Therefore he adds: *To my cavalry,* etc.

## CHAPTER V.

*To my cavalry in the chariots of Pharaoh have I likened thee, my friend.* He calls her friend, whose mind accords utterly with what it hears, whose converse now disagrees from the commandments not even a little, who is so zealous to do and to accomplish all things that are commanded, that, loving him who commanded, she may deserve to be loved by him. As it is written in the Gospel: « You are my friends, if you do [0255B] the things that I command you [n: (Joan. XV)]. » Further, that she might become a friend, he likened her to his own cavalry in the chariots of Pharaoh. He alludes to a thing done, he touches the history, which describes that the children of Israel passed over the depths of the sea with a dry footprint, but that the chariots of Pharaoh and his whole army utterly perished. But in what respect the Virgin Mother is held like to this people, it is fitting to see; for a likeness of this sort seems not to have a fitness worthy enough, especially since in that passage the people of Israel is not found to have had horses, and there was no cavalry, seeing that it is agreed that horses and horsemen were lacking to it. For of Pharaoh it is written: « The horse and the rider he hath cast into the sea [n: (Exod. XV)], » [var: Vulg. Exod 15:1 *equum et ascensorem deiecit in mare*, "he hath thrown down"; the verb printed here is that of Exod 15:4, quoted two lines below] and below: « Pharaoh's chariots and his army [0255C] he hath cast into the sea [n: (ibid.)]. » But when the children of Israel passed over, no mention is made of horses or of chariots, nor do they escape Pharaoh's threats by such a defence, lest anyone should reckon that what the hand of God alone willed wondrously to work was worthily to be assigned to horses or to chariots. Finally the sea, though perhaps by a ship, yet is not crossed by a horse or by a rolling wheel; and in that passage the people experienced neither a ship nor a vehicle, but, the waves being divided and standing on this side and on that after the manner of a wall, they pass over with dry foot, not about to feel the onset of the Egyptians any more. And so for no other cause is it rightly called a cavalry, save that God sat upon it spiritually, and as ruler mercifully presided over the subject people; and that people was the chariot, or the four-horse car, of him that presided, and he willed to be the leader and charioteer of the car [0255D] that bore him beneath. To whom Moses says: « Thou hast been a leader, he says, in thy mercy to the people which thou hast redeemed [n: (Exod. XV)]. » And Habacuc: « Thou who ridest upon thy horses, and thy chariots are salvation [n: (Habac. III)]. » Such a cavalry, he says, as I made my own in the chariots of Pharaoh, that is, above all the chariots of Pharaoh, and made over to my own service, when I set it free, being led out of Egypt, from among the hands of its pursuers: so by a deep mystery I bound thee to a certain wondrous ministry, when I blessed thee, my chosen mother and Bride, among women, that is, above all women. For all the motions, all the onsets which the flesh suggests, which the enervated mind heaps up, all the crowds of vices which Egypt, ruinously depraved, sends forth and sends in, I drowned and quenched with the flood of the water that bore them down, and I wiped away thy stains by the benefit of the grace [0256A] that washed them clean. And, the enemies being quenched, thee, assigned the more freely to my service, made over with a willing neck to a light and sweet yoke, I, a mighty horseman, a skilled charioteer, lead through the midst of the sea, and, the waves being removed on this side and on that, I bring thee through to the solid land of the living. There stands on the right hand and on the left the overhanging height of the waters, which an invisible strength holds fixed after the manner of a wall; they are astounded and marvel that they are held by a new and unwonted kind of thing, that a way is given to the four-horse car, and that the slipping waves are not feared. For so, turning and dwelling in the midst of sinners, attending on this side to the foolishness of the Gentiles, on that to the scandals of the Jews [n: (I Cor. I)], thou drawest upon thyself from their malice no fault, no sin; thou art not broken by reproaches, thou dost not forsake the way shown thee beforehand. And not only does the heap of the overhanging wave [0256B] not press or oppress thy head, but neither does a passing stream so much as touch the feet of thee that walkest; nay rather, with dry foot, with clean footprint, with unspotted life, thou passest by the way of this world, unassailed by any touch of the vices. But endowed with so great a gift, how art thou not a friend — thou whose converse in a world so unclean is so chaste, who wast made the keeper of my mind, the sharer of a spiritual secret, a true mother, knowing nothing of the marriage bed? But that in conceiving she knew not the marriage bed, nay rather that from that time and thenceforward no touch of Venus *145* hurt her virgin mind, the Bridegroom seems to intimate in the words that follow, whereby he willed the more evidently to commend her chastity.
