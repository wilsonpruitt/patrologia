## CHAPTER X.

[0269A]

*A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me; he shall abide between my breasts.* Myrrh is a bitter spice, according to the judgment of physicians, wherewith, if a man diligently anoint the bodies of the dead, its bitterness gives to the corpses so effectual a remedy that for length of days they feel no corruption of worm or of rottenness. By the name of myrrh therefore the virgin's Bridegroom is rightly figured, with whose bitter bundle her conscience is not weighed down; but what is reckoned heavy and most bitter by another that loves not, is judged by her that is greedy of salvation to be by so much the sweetest as it is the more saving. The bitterness of myrrh is the Bridegroom's word or commandment, whose first taste, as it were, some are found so to abhor, and its first face, its very hearing, so to displease them, [0269B] that with hardened heart they not only choose not to consent, but hasten to spring back further from it as from a thing hurtful and contrary to themselves. You know that when Christ in the Gospel was speaking of perfection to a certain young man, and was exhorting him to the contempt of riches and to a voluntary poverty; he spat out the savour offered him as sad and bitter, and held not what he heard, proclaiming himself covetous more by deed than by word: « When the young man, he says, had heard the word, he went away sad; for he had great possessions [n: (Matth. XIX)]. » Elsewhere, when Jesus had said: « He that eateth my flesh and drinketh my blood, abideth in me and I in him: many, he says, of his disciples, hearing it, said: This saying is hard, who can hear it? From that time many of his disciples went, he says, [0269C] back: and walked no more with him [n: (Joan. VI)]. » You see that these give not faith to what they hear, that they bear not a hard word as it were the bitterness of myrrh; that they have no profitable consent with such an adversary, but with a lost mind abhor that saving commandment. « Be, he says, at agreement with thy adversary, whilst thou art in the way with him [n: (Matth. V)]. » But the Virgin whom we have in hand spat not out such a commandment, but bore the bitterness of myrrh with a willing mind; and what the ill-delicate reckon the sharper with a hurt palate, she with a better counsel and a concordant Spirit loves to hold the more pleasing. Wherefore also she named of that same myrrh not a bundle but a little bundle, wherein she signified the sweet, if I may so say, [0269D] bitterness of the divine commandment; which presses not, weighs not down the neck or the shoulders of her that bears it, but increases love in her, and love makes light the yoke of him that lays it on. For whatsoever she judges necessary for herself in this life, she rejoices to accomplish and to carry through not as Simon under compulsion, but for the bridegroom's love; and with that little bundle she embalms her own flesh, and takes pains to make it firm with an abiding estate, while whatever she bears in the flesh she loves to commend wholly to her beloved. Our labour indeed, the labour of us wretched ones, or our wretched action, rots away as often as Christ's love grows cold in us; and if perchance the ready Spirit burns with love of him, myrrh is a bitter bundle to us, when either the flesh grows weak or waxes insolent against the Spirit. For neither can we utterly break our own wills, nor wholly banish [0270A] the pleasures of the world or of the body; and we unhappy ones bear such a burden with much labour, while by turns fear is overcome by hope, and hope again by fear. But the virgin embraces her beloved so tenderly, so sweetly, and her conscience is so delighted with the love of him that is sweet, that whatever of bitterness she is compelled to bear in the present, she counts it light and very little for the love of him whom she already tastes, and shall taste more fully hereafter. Therefore she says: *To me the beloved,* she says, *my own to me;* though others, she says, seem *153* to bear a hard bundle, who have not yet deserved to obtain a concord of the flesh and of the Spirit: to me nevertheless my beloved has willed already to show himself a little bundle, to me who, the wrestling of the flesh being lulled to sleep, have been granted to cleave to his continual embraces. For the Virgin, considering that he whom she loves [0270B] is a King, and therefore that he is powerful, rich, noble; cares not if at any time hard things clamour without, since within, in his embrace, she rejoices already secure. For him whom a little before she calls a King: « While he was, she says, King in his repose [n: (Cant. I)], » she now loves to name her beloved, that she may show that she loves one not weak, not poor, not base-born; upon whose embrace she hangs herself with fervent affection, and whatever is beneath or behind she little esteems with a worthy disdain. Wherefore, when she had said: *A bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me,* she adds: *Between my breasts,* etc.

## CHAPTER XI.

*He shall abide between my breasts.* Good is this Bundle, and to be desired not moderately by her that loves, which [0270C] bows not the back or the shoulder, nor brings disgust upon her that bears it; but with a pleasing sweetness abides between her breasts, where we discern that what is loved is not so much borne up as held and pressed close. Not so is he of whom it is written: « He turned his back away from the burdens [n: (Psal. LXXX)]. » And you have read that the shepherd carries the lost and found sheep back to the flock upon his shoulders [n: (Luc. XV)]. Of which the same, complaining, says: « I have laboured with bearing [n: (Isa. I)]. » But the Virgin, bearing the aforesaid little bundle of myrrh, thinks not that she labours, nor says that she carries that little bundle upon her back or her shoulder, but, by a new manner of speaking, that he gladly abides between her breasts, where the seat of the heart is said to be and love to hold princedom. And the Virgin's breasts seem [0270D] to figure her twofold love, whereby, namely, the Virgin loves to love the Bridegroom as God and man: God as Creator and Lord of the whole creation, man as her son by the grace of a wonderful and unspeakable begetting. To whom that rightly applies which was said by the Prophet: « The God of my heart, and God is my portion [n: (Psal. LXXII)]. » Finally there is none who knows not that a mother embraces her son between her breasts, whom, from the very fact that she is a mother, she is proved by a certain law of nature to love: and the Virgin, for love's sake, compared her Son to a little bundle, whom the prophetic word, by the merit of his humility, named a boy or a little one. « A little one, he says, is born to us [n: (Isa. IX)]. » That little one sweetly abides between his mother's breasts, whom, not by nature but by a prevailing grace, [0271A] the mother strongly embraces: and with unremitted love she holds the more firmly, on this side God, on that man, nay her son wholly and at once, and lets him not go; she loves, and by no impulse of bitterness leaves off loving. Of whom she says below: « I held him, and I will not let him go [n: (Cant. III)]. » Nor does the mother embrace only her son, but the Bride embraces the Bridegroom the more tenderly, and as she, so he rejoices in a mutual embrace; who, when he kisses her, as was said in the beginning of the drama, assuredly abides the more sweetly between her breasts. For in a kiss (if indeed it be consummated the more greedily and the more sincerely), as mouth is said to be joined to mouth, so breast to breast; nor is the love of them that kiss proved to advance to a nicety, if the kiss given with the mouth be not made firm with a pleasing embrace. Rightly therefore does the Virgin assert that the Bridegroom abides between her breasts, [0271B] that is, that a tender love is confirmed with the glue of an embrace; which, lest after the manner of others it be reckoned brief and perfunctory, the Bridegroom is said never to be disgusted with, but to make a tarrying therein. *Between my breasts,* she says, *he shall abide.* Whereof she subjoins: *A cluster,* etc.
