## CHAPTER XII.

*A cluster of Cyprus is my Beloved to me, in the vineyards of Engaddi.* Wonderful and much to be loved is this beloved, whose excellence my understanding, or the understanding of one like me, does not grasp; yet, that it may in some way grasp him, I hear him named now myrrh, now a cluster, knowing nevertheless that he is none of these things, but that he is figured by these names. A cluster is a fruit which, with the process of time and ripeness obtained, is fit for eating, and afterwards, being pressed in the press, strains out the liquor of wine for drinking. By which [0271C] name the Virgin's Bridegroom is not undeservedly called, whose knowledge is ripened in the heart of them that rightly believe by a fitting advance; and, being ripened, he himself is eaten with the greedy affection of them that love him, and by that spiritual food the life of them that eat is spiritually quickened. Asserting which in the Gospel: « He that eateth me, he says, he also shall live by me [n: (Joan. VI)]. » Happy is he who both wills and is able to purchase this food, his substance being sold, and to prepare the greedy jaws of his heart for such a viand: to repair with its so sweet taste the withered strength within, and, the care of the flesh being cast off, to strengthen the Spirit with diligent zeal. Happy, I say, is he in whose conscience the knowledge of this cluster grows ripe, whose appetite burns for it, in whose jaws it grows sweet, who, loving to anoint his palate [0271D] with a pleasing savour, feels its vital juice *154* pass through to his inward vitals. Which having passed through, the flesh withers, the spirit is made fat, the outward man faints away, the inward is renewed: the sense of the flesh subsides, the spirit receives increase, and when the one is as it were dead the other stretches the more hastily toward its issue. For being no longer content to eat the cluster with famished jaws, after the vital food he desires to drink off a fruitful cup; nor does the eating he has desired seem sufficient enough for him, if it be not seasoned with a sweeter wine and a more pleasing draught. And so with the roasting press of devotion and of love he presses strongly the cluster now ripe, throws out the grape-stones, and strains the wine that shall please the more graciously: and he who until then had as it were eaten with some labour, drinks the more sweetly and grows the more fully glad, while he feels nothing contrary in love. For food seems in a manner to be changed into [0272A] drink, and the cluster into wine, while a man is found so sweetly to be made drunken with divine love that, forgetting things present, he is held by the desire of things to come alone, and, knowing no sadness, seems already to enjoy that joy of his Lord.

Whose sweetness the most blessed Virgin foretasted more fully than all, and with whose wine or love she burned far more vehemently; especially when, after the Son's death (which, being seen, the Mother could not but grieve at), his flesh was found to flower again by the benefit of the resurrection. For she who, while he was dying, tasted the sad bitterness of a certain myrrh, when the same rose again exulted the more sweetly, as one that had drunk wine; and that her joy may be clearly known to be more excellent than the rest. The cluster whereof the discourse is, is not unfittingly commended from its place: *A cluster,* she says, *of Cyprus [0272B] is my beloved to me.* Cyprus is an island, before the rest that lie about it, rather first than second, as they say. who have seen it, so fruitful of the very best wine that not only is its fruitfulness held pleasing and sufficient for itself, but from thence also wine is carried over into other regions by the merit of its own. Whose cluster is found fairer to look upon, more pleasing to eat, sweeter to drink, when at the ripe season it makes trial of the winepress of them that tread; and as it seems to smile upon the sight, so also upon the taste, and the mind of him that loves it rejoices to answer it as it plays before him. Rightly therefore did the virgin will to call her beloved a cluster of Cyprus, who vouchsafed to present so great a joy to her that loved him, that, the mourning being wiped away which the passion of him that died had brought, she was rendered as it were drunken with the most sweet taste of him that rose again. Who, although to many others he was a cluster [0272C] that they might rejoice, and that by the grace of him that rose again they might have hope of rising again in flesh and in spirit, yet the virgin asserts that to her he was nothing but a cluster of Cyprus, to whom the Bridegroom is believed to have afforded an ampler and a more perfect joy. For the virgin had known both that the Son's death would profit for the blotting out of vices, and that his resurrection would bestow the merit of justification; and in both, while others variously distrusted, she distrusted not, and by how much the more firmly she believed and the more robustly she loved, by so much the more amply did she rejoice. Worthily therefore did she understand him in whom she believed, whom she loved, to be in a manner comparable to a cluster of Cyprus, whose love grows ripe with her by an incomparable advance, and, being tasted the more fully and the more sweetly, makes her grow glad the more strongly and the more joyfully. But that this cluster may be commended with a yet ampler grace, mention of the vineyards of Engaddi [0272D] is not kept silent; and although Cyprus once named seemed sufficient and enough, Engaddus is added, that nothing may be able to be lacking to the commendation.

*A cluster,* she says, *of Cyprus is my beloved to me, in the vineyards of Engaddi.* As it seems, she says that a cluster of Cyprus is in the vineyards of Engaddi, which saying the truth of the matter contradicts according to the letter; because Engaddus is known to be far more remote from Cyprus, and neither custom nor necessity is found for clusters to be carried over from vineyards into vineyards. For if a man should labour to carry clusters from vineyards into vineyards, he is accused of a certain ineptitude in the judgment of the prudent; as if, his senses being lost, he should carry wood into the wood, or pour waters into the fullest sea, when that ought rather to be carried thither where most certainly it does not abound. Finally [0273A] Engaddus is said not to have vineyards, but certain little trees, which it is agreed are better than vines; which, if they be pricked with a learned hand at the opportune season, are known to sweat oil, nay the liquor of balsam. And because their owners till such little trees with much diligence, that like vines they may be made readier to produce liquor; [sic: *idco*] perhaps the divine word called them vineyards, whereby it signified them both as subject to tillage and as rich in a precious liquor. Because therefore a cluster of Cyprus is not to be found in these vineyards or little trees, nor can what has been said fit according to the letter, it is necessary that in this word a spiritual understanding be sought, which, as you have already received, holds the greater princedom in this drama. In the tree or the little tree the virgin's Bridegroom is not unfittingly figured, who, if [0273B] he be pricked by the touch and love of them that worthily believe, sweats oil, gives the liquor of a precious balsam, brings forth much grace to her that loves, and pours back a more pleasing spirit. « Therefore, he says, I love them that love me [n: (Prov. VIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Prov 8:17 *Ego diligentes me diligo* — the emphatic pronoun "I", not the connective "therefore"] And in the Gospel: « Somebody, he says, hath touched me: for I have felt power going forth from me [n: (Luc. VIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Luke 8:46 *nam ego novi virtutem de me exiisse*, "for I know that virtue is gone out from me"] This Bridegroom, when he is touched with the pleasing affection of one that loves, pours back the sacred balsam, the power of an overflowing spirit; he brings forth that which stills diseases and by its presence keeps off all hurt, he brings forth that unction which, error as well as ignorance being driven out, teaches concerning all things. And this benefit seems to be intimated by the very name of the place, if any man shall have turned it over or been able to search it out more expressly, because Engaddus is interpreted *the fount of the kid,* or *to the eyes of temptation,* whose [0273C] *155* interpretation serves the mystery of so great a matter. The kid is reprobated in the law, which is sent out into the desert by the ministers [n: (Levit. XVI)], and in the Gospel the same is set on the left hand by the merit of sin [n: (Matth. XXV)]; and for this cause, by him, sin or the sinner is not unfittingly figured, who by his own merit is reprobated by him who loves nothing but justices. But the fount of the kid is the Spirit himself, whose infusion washes sin, cleanses the sinner, renders the flesh of Naaman the leper purer than a boy's [n: (IV Reg. V)], changes the kid into a lamb, giving the merit of an innocent life [n: (Joan. IV)], and becomes in him that has drunk a rich fount, a fount of water springing up. The same Spirit is also not undeservedly called the eye of temptation, by whose light and grace temptation is foreseen [0273D] far off; who, teaching man knowledge, forming wisdom, directing the understanding, the darkness of night being wiped away, leads him on to the perfect day. The cluster of Cyprus in the vineyards of Engaddi therefore is this our virgin's beloved, because the love and joy wherewith her mind is affected is made solid in Christ himself, worthily in his own Spirit; and loving him in him, she rejoices of him and not of another. To her indeed he is a fruit-bearing tree, a medicinal balsam, a vital fount, a laver of injustice, the light of knowledge, a spiritual eye; and whatever is judged necessary either for the driving off of evils or for the obtaining of goods, is truly proclaimed to be had in him and from him. And lest we be judged wholly strangers to that benefit, although we are in no wise made equal to so great a merit of this virgin, with the sacrament of holy [0274A] baptism we have received certain visible things in token of things invisible. For we are anointed with oil in figure of the heavenly medicine, whereby, torpor being driven far off, we are prepared for an agile discipline, that our spirit, anointed with the divine spirit, may be held the stronger in the wrestling, and may the more readily slip out of the adversary's hand or snare. Afterwards we are washed throughout with the clean fount of visible water, that we may be spiritually cleansed from the filth not so much of the flesh as of the soul; and that we may exchange the rough motions of the kid for all meekness, and reserve in ourselves nothing on the left hand, nothing worthy of judgment. Thirdly a light is kindled for us, that our eye may not grow dim, that when it has foreseen whatever things are hurtful it may either forbid or chastise them; that, not being ignorant of Satan's cunning devices — what, namely, they feign, what they intend — it may walk honestly as in the day, [0274B] and darkness may not overtake it. So his own baptizer instructs the strenuous athlete, when he is newly girded for the contest, more by significations than by speech; that when he has received a lively understanding of the things which have been said, he may with the utmost effort, with grace, take pains to come through to their effect. In all which the virgin is believed to be equal to none, not second, but far before the rest she enjoys, rejoicing, the Bridegroom's love; and to her by a special privilege that saying worthily belongs which we know to be as yet forbidden to us, our merits requiring it. *A cluster,* she says, *of Cyprus is my beloved to me, in the vineyards of Engaddi.* After the virgin had spoken these things of her beloved to the maidens, and had mystically set forth what of patience, what of joy she has in him; he, as it were turning himself back to her, delays not to answer the more graciously, [0274C] praising and commending what he understands, and loves, to be preeminent in her.
