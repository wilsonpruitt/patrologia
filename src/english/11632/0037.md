## CHAPTER XV.

*Behold thou art fair, my beloved.* Nor does the wonderful beauty of thy Godhead alone please me, which thy loftiness, coequal with the Father and the Spirit, obtains; but thou hast also kindled me not slightly with that comeliness wherewith, abounding in grace, thou, the lofty one, didst descend to lowly things. [0277C] For no small comeliness is that wherewith, girding thyself, thou art strongly clothed, when by the Father thou wast set over Sion his holy mount [n: (Psal. II)]; when, taking flesh in me and personally made one with it, thou didst come forth as a Bridegroom out of the chamber [n: (Psal. XVIII)], marked with humility in power. In which flesh, above the law of the flesh, thou didst not feel the vices of the flesh, but, conceived of the Spirit, didst flower with an inborn vigour; and, suffering the penalty of the flesh, thou didst fail in the sight of them that persecuted thee, but in the sight of the faithful thou didst perfect the saving mystery for the world. For it was needful that, living in the flesh under penalty, thou shouldest teach us, shouldest show thyself a master of living as much by thy manners as by thy words; that with anvil and hammer thou shouldest forge the rule of the Gospel, and, suffering death in [0277D] the flesh, shouldest triumph over the devil, the author of death. But being given over to death, thou didst rise on the third day, and, made immortal, strengthening faith, didst give hope: to the heavens whence thou hadst come thou didst carry up the substance of our flesh, and, the Spirit being given to thine own, thou didst raise their minds thither. Who, worthily considering that thou wast able so to be born and to live, to die and to rise again, and that thou didst will by a deep counsel, as man, to do these things for men, would not be amazed, would not wonder, would not love this comeliness, would not greedily embrace so mighty, so kindly a giver? Which, although it be hidden from others, from whom, their guilt requiring it, grace of this kind is either taken away, or, being to be given hereafter, is now deferred by a hidden judgment; yet to me in the present the order of this matter is declared, to me it is open, to me it is pleasing, what thy Godhead [0278A] works in man. *Behold,* I say, *thou art fair, my beloved, and comely.* Behold, that is, in evidence to me is the beauty of thy majesty; to the opened eye of faith the excellence of the Godhead presents itself, which by its bounteous gift floods the understanding of my mind, and, the understanding being made clear, gives the effect of an indissoluble love. Behold again, the saving dispensation of thy humanity is not hidden from me: the conception, the nativity, the converse of a singular merit; the power, the cleanness, the patience, the praise of the doctrine, the flashing of miracles, the integrity of manners, the perfection of discipline. In all which thou, a man, art adorned above men, and with a singular comeliness art compared neither to a prophet nor to an apostle: and therefore I love thee before all, thee alone I choose, my beloved, although as yet, while life is lived here, imperfectly (as I seem to see). But [0278B] that imperfection may not trouble me more than is right, I desire that thy presence may manifest itself to me more nearly; and lest I should seem to invite the fair and comely one to foul things, I have taken pains to prepare a place fitting for a lover.

## CHAPTER XVI.

*Our little bed is flowering; the beams of our houses are of cedar, our panelled ceilings of cypress.* Because the noble Virgin has found a fair and comely lover, she does not after a rustic fashion call him forth to labour, for delicate love knows not to be worn down by degenerate toil or torment, but loves quiet and leisure, and rejoices in a kiss or in a joining. As yet unversed in this, that *158* lover, a rude one, seems to have stood forth, nor did his love afford him rest worthy enough: who asks that she whom he loves be given him for this, that they may be able to labour together, [0278C] but not yet rejoice to keep holiday. « Her have I loved, and have sought her out, he says, and have sought to take her to me for a spouse; and I became a lover of her beauty [n: (Sap. VIII)]. » And below: « Send her out of the seat of thy greatness, that she may be with me and may labour with me; and that I may know what is acceptable with thee, for she knoweth all things and understandeth [n: (Sap. IX)]. » This man saw that it was as yet fitting for him to labour, and that Wisdom, whom he loves, should labour together with him, that by the common toil the lover, as yet less wise, might be taught, and, when he had been taught, might be carried forward to the rest of the little bed. Finally that woman whose praiseworthy fortitude is commended is recorded to put her hand to the neck, and to take hold of the spindle with her fingers; and she seems to be made gracious to her husband, in that she has not eaten [0278D] her bread idle [n: (Prov. XXXI)]. But our Virgin, far surpassing both this woman and that lover, obtains now quiet, not tumult; leisure now, not labour; and offers not a wrestling-school to a contender, but a little bed to a lover, which she has taken pains to adorn and to make ready with a more cultivated ornament. The Virgin's little bed is the secret place of her heart, which she tills and cleanses with a diligent hand and care, lest anything unseemly and unbecoming should appear to the Bridegroom at his coming; and, that she may afford a sweet repose to his so great dignity, she props it and fills it with the pleasing softness of a careful workmanship. And because, besides the softness laid beneath, a noble lover rejoices also in comeliness, the Virgin, not unskilled in the matter, adorns the little bed with a pleasing flower; and, the flower of a wondrous chastity being scattered with much diligence, she renders that same little bed as it were a little garden of delight. [0279A] For lest any man, hearing of the Virgin's little bed and taking it ill, should doubt of the profit, and should stray from the path of holiness by a gross and carnal understanding, the Virgin declares it adorned with gathered flowers; and the flowers are chastity, and the repute of that same chastity. For virginal chastity adorns her secret knowledge after the manner of a flower, and renders the little bed pleasing, pouring out the grace of comeliness and of odour; because it is that which is preeminent by an excellent virtue and merit, and which, that it may offer itself as a pattern, shines out with an eminent fame or name. Wherein nothing bodily, nothing bestial is understood, nothing given over to sin, but the whole utterly spiritual; because the drama, or the song, or the nuptial epithalamium flowers with mystical words, having nothing of hay or of flesh. But although that thing thus flowers and avails which love has more diligently prepared, [0279B] yet the Virgin named it not a bed, but rather a little bed; by which name she fulfilled that law well enough known to her, whereby she was wont to hold to lowly things as much in word as in deed. Wherefore also, she says, I call that same little bed not properly mine, but in common ours, and (which is worthy of praise) I do not presumptuously assign it to myself; because, although I was found obediently to consent to him that called and helped me, yet both his preventing grace and his following mercy willed to impart the efficacy. Not mine therefore, but ours ought the little bed rightly to be called, which it is agreed is prepared by the work and diligence of both of us; which thou with the hand of grace, and I with that of labour and industry, have made serene — thou by benefit, I by exercise; thou with the flowers of virtues, I with the flowers of manners, have adorned it. Whereof I willed to make [0279C] so marked a mention to this end, that, called forth by its beauty, thou mayest vouchsafe to come to its dwelling; and that, far from the tumult wherein I know thou art not often present, I may rejoice — than which is nothing better — to be with thee in the flowering secret place. For although for the sake of the brethren and of neighbours it is needful to speak and to go out, yet, that I may behold thee, it is much more pleasing to enter into silence, and in the leisure of quiet to be soothed by thy presence, to enjoy a mutual embrace, to experience the fruit of love, the joy of the Spirit. To the deserving whereof our houses also seem to emulate my zeal, and with however slow a foot to follow me as I rest in the little bed; which, although they are not yet able to be advanced to this, that they should become a little bed, yet are already found to deserve no slight grace. And in this I rejoice, yea [0279D] and I shall rejoice, because thou pleasest not only when thy presence is now held in the little bed, but when, according to the Prophet [n: (Psal. XLVII)], it shall be known to be in the houses; to which, namely, in beams of cedar thou givest an incorruptible firmness, in panelled ceilings of cypress a lasting ornament nonetheless.
