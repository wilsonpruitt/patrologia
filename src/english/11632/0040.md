## CHAPTER XX.

*As the lily among thorns, so is my friend among the daughters.* Although, as you have heard, I said that I am the lily of the valleys, [0283C] yet know further that I am among more grievous thorns; by which I suffer myself to be touched the more often, to be pricked the more troublesomely; but, touched and pricked, I lose not my due state or whiteness. After this pattern my friend has her converse among the evils of this world, and by a wonderful power the light is proved to shine in the darkness; and the darkness poured about her does not darken her flowering light, nor do threatening thistles thresh out her unbroken beauty. She rises like a lily among thorns, and grows; among dark things she shines, among rough things she grows meek; driven by the blowing wind she seems to feel some workings, but she sustains no hurts of whiteness or of odour. I deem the grievous thorns to be the cares and anxieties of this life, wherewith the minds of the just (while life is lived here) are harassed; they groan and [0283D] long that, being more fully freed from them, they may at last with Christ rejoice in the fruit of peace obtained. Thorns are nonetheless the Jews and the Gentiles alike, who, aged in malice, childish in lost sense, execrating the faith, wrapped in grosser darkness, persecuted Christ and his Mother with a grievous hatred. Among whom the Son and the Mother flowered after the likeness of a lily, and alike kept their whiteness unhurt and their own odour, while, contracting to themselves nothing of guilt from their malice, with unconquered patience they spurn the detracting tongues. Nay, by how much the more the life of the malignant grows dark with a foul soot, by so much the more does the holiness of these, worthily considered, shine forth; nor has the undefiled beauty of the lily less of comeliness and grace, if beside it be seen the rough foulness [0284A] of the thorns. The lily among thorns therefore has an ampler title of praise, since, while those grow dark, this retains a more pleasing form; because while both Gentiles and Jews rage with serpentine tongue against the Lord and against his Christ [n: (Psal. II)], Christ, the flower and the lily, grows meek in manners, and shines with miracles and doctrine. To which flower and lily the virgin takes pains the more diligently to be made like, loving and desiring to glory in so great a likeness; and as among thorns the lily is made beautiful with a splendid comeliness, so the Bride among the daughters is adorned by the merit of virtues. By daughters he means those who, lacking manly rigour and a masculine spirit, are torpid with a womanish softness, sleep with a feminine drowsiness; feeble for honest work, effectual for what is soft and hurtful, crafty in flattering, treacherous [0284B] or shameless in detracting. Who, seeing or hearing of the Mother rejoicing in her son, clothed with a lost sense deny her to be either a virgin or fruitful; and, endowed as she is with a singular gift, they measure her carnally according to the common law of all, while they lie that she brought forth in no other way than the manner made trial of by other women. But him whom she bore they deem the son of Joseph, and testify that he has very many brethren (though he is an only one); they know not that he is man begotten of a human being without a Father, shrinking from what they hear, that he is God, the Son of God, without a mother. Among these and their blasphemies the virgin is not broken, does not wither, but her mind is strengthened by faith, and, flowering, grows young with the joy of the Spirit; she embraces that tender and unfading flower of the root, and imitates its comeliness with a zeal not so much of steps as of advances [0284C]. Finally, according to Solomon: « A joyful mind maketh a flowering age [n: (Prov. XVII)]. » As therefore the son, a flower and a lily rising among thorns, yet feels not the thorns, since he consents not to their malice by a like affection; so the mother and friend, having her converse among the daughters, knows not the darkness of the daughters, but, a daughter of light, shines, not having the works of darkness. Nay, that more may be said, not only do the thorns bring no harm to the lily, but the lily confers very much ornament upon the thorns; and the singular whiteness which in the lily, by a natural benefit, spreads itself abroad, pours over no small grace upon the nearer thorns. Did not the lily pour out an incomparable benefit on every side, when Christ poured forth devout prayers for them that persecuted him? when with the hand of mercy he scraped away the foulness [0284D] of the thorns, and turned the buckthorn into a flower, and the prickles into fruit? « Father, he says, pardon them, for they know not what they do [n: (Luc. XXIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Luke 23:34 *Pater, dimitte illis: non enim sciunt quid faciunt*, "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do"] Drenched as with dew by this prayer, the roughness of the thorns grows meek, the inward dryness yields, benevolence grows green; after a new manner the little branch buds and puts forth leaves and flowers, and the little flower grows glad to imitate the lily, if but with a slight whiteness. Who knows not that thorns once dry sometimes flower out by the gift of the summer season, and that in them not so much the old prickles as fresh little flowers stand out; which, standing by the lily, though far unequal in greatness or comeliness, yet rejoice in a whiteness in some sort like, and in a nearer neighbourhood? To us therefore drawing near to Christ the beauty of the lily [0285A] contracts to itself nothing of foulness, but our foulness acquires from it no small grace for itself; and while the pleasantness of the lily thus admits us, *162* as the fulness of a planted garden, our solitude, once dry, now at last flowering, rejoices exceedingly. Foreseeing which, Isaias: « The desert and the pathless land shall be glad, he says, and the solitude shall exult; and it shall bud as the lily [n: (Isa. XXXV)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. 35:1 *florebit quasi lilium*, "shall blossom as the lily"] Our mind is not undeservedly called a desert and a pathless land, when, forsaken by God, it is not inhabited by citizens of virtues or of works, when the kindly author walks not in it, loves not to behold it, marking that solitude to be beset by the prickles of vices as by thorns. But when divine grace as a spring mildness shall visit it, and shall call the white lily within the garden's plantings, assuredly it shall be glad, it shall exult with the gift of a new bounty [0285B], because, joined to the lily, it shall bud as a lily. For shrinking from the buckthorn, which the roughness of thorns was to bring forth — because a punishment fiercely raging awaits thorny manners — it shall bring forth sweet little flowers rivalling the lily in whiteness, and shall exult in the hope of fruit, having the first-fruits budding. Among thorns of this kind that white lily — nay, the brightness of eternal light — works wondrously, and rejoices to turn rough things into a plain, and the prickle into a flower; being about, by that goodness which is his own, one day to bring the matter to this, that the flower may bring forth fruit, and that he may rejoice in the fruit he shall enjoy. So also the friend is rightly believed to have her converse among the daughters, to bear care of them, to work their salvation the more anxiously; and those who were once wandering in mind, lifted up in eyes, shameless in tongue, barren of works, vouchsafing them at length her fellowship, she renders agile and fruitful. [0285C] For those whom she admits within the noble council-chamber of her hearing, she suffers not to remain barren as they stand by her; but instructing them with a word, fortifying them beforehand with a pattern, helping them with her intercession, she invites them to love, urges them to work, and gladdens them concerning the outcome. And thus those who once by the merit of their life had been daughters of Babylon, having entered into Jerusalem she renders daughters of vision; among whom, rejoicing, she sees the Bridegroom, loves to embrace him, and, as he the Bride, so she loves to venerate him with worthy praises.
