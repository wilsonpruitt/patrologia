## CHAPTER XXII.

*Under the shadow of him whom I had desired, I sat; and his fruit was sweet to my throat.* If, she says, I have wished to commend my beloved, as you have heard, if I have wished to compare him with a fruitful apple tree among barren trees, do not think me uncertain of the things I set before you, and abounding in words but untried in things worthy of praise: by experiment I have learned that this apple tree is useful, that it is good; I have felt how great a refuge is in it, how great a refreshment, how great a salvation; because under its shadow it has been granted me not [0289D] only to be, but to sit, to flee the harmful heats, to obtain the grace of quiet. A good quiet, which the hand not of wrath pursuing but of grace loving has fashioned; over which not dark death, but the spreading apple tree has cast the shade of its boughs and leaves; in which the sinner is not buried in the tomb of despair or of ignorance, but the just mind is soothed with a sweet refreshment by the benefit of advancement. Wretched is that people which long willed to sit in darkness and in the shadow of death, forgetful of heavenly things, to cleave carnally to earthly; which, not knowing the living life, grew dark with the dim death of crimes, of which shadow the body is the death without end that is to follow. For sin, whereby the soul is pierced through as by a sword, and is found to be made further off from him who is life, is the first death of the soul, sadder in the time to come, [0290A] pleasant now; but the eternal damnation which justly follows is the second death. And that indeed is a death, and the shadow going before of the death that follows; but this is both a death and the truth of the shadow that went before. Although it may befall us to be in the first of them, it is not of counsel that we should sit, that is, that we should obtain a lost quiet with no regard to repenting. For he who sits in the shadow, and does not fear to forestall the death that is to come, when she coming hastens to find him *165* torpid, and whom she has found in the dark and as it were in her own right, she binds, and holds him, cast into prison, by the unloosable force of her binding. Yet nevertheless the vehement spirit, having pity on some, terrifies them and shakes them with a sudden sound; and the Dayspring, visiting from on high, floods them with the morning light; and when they have been led forth out of sleep and out of the shadow, harsh and nightlong, of a hard peril, a glad day receives them, [0290B] and a shadow sweeter and of the daytime. Now the day is faith enlightening: the virtue of hope is the sweeter shadow, in the foreknowing of the vision; in which, though it be good to be, and better to live, it is best to sit, until at last it be granted to obtain the full light more fully. He is in the shadow of the daytime who knows that not only present things but also things that follow have being; who sees through a glass that which it is agreed is not yet visibly at hand; who with undoubting faith understands that the invisible God is; who believes him to be a rewarder of them that hope in him. Paul: « It behoveth, he says, him that cometh to God to believe that he is; and that to them that seek him he be a rewarder [n: (Hebr. XI)]. » Without this faith no one shall please God, nor is that conscience worthy whose knowledge shall not hold God; because he also that is ignorant, as you have read, is read to be ignored, and in truth [0290C] he who is not known cannot be accepted by one who does not know him [n: (I Cor. XIV)]. Therefore the Prophet: « He believed, for which cause he also spoke [n: (Psal. CXV)]. » [var: Vulg. Ps. 115:1 *Credidi propter quod locutus sum*, first person — "I have believed, therefore have I spoken"] Thou art thyself my God. Without faith, therefore, no one can be just; nay, that I may say more, he who has not believed ought not even to be said to be; and he who is not, consequently is not in the shadow either. Of such it is written: « Deliver not thy kingdom to them that are not [n: (Esther. XIV)]. » [var: Vulg. *sceptrum tuum*, "thy sceptre," and with the vocative *Domine*] He is rightly said not to be, who knows not that God is, to whom, though he has been made, he by whom he was made is not yet known; who with blinded mind is driven far from the desert of existing, not having yet attained that faith which is the shadow, or the beginning, of subsisting. Of which Paul: « If only, he says, we hold the beginning of his substance firm unto the end [0290D] [n: (Hebr. III)]. » He therefore who has faith, holding firmly that God is, both is, and is in the shadow; but it is not enough to be there. Nay, he must labour, who is already agreed to be by the merit of faith, that living in it he may deserve also to be just. For to be in faith is not yet to be just; but to live by faith, or to live in faith, is reckoned for justice, since a dead faith, even though it does not love, does work. For love is the life of faith, and therefore not he who believes, but he who loving believes, is proved to live; and living in faith he is worthily praised, and is proclaimed commendable for justice. Whence by Habacuc: « My just man, he says, liveth by his own faith [n: (Habac. II)]. » [var: Vulg. Hab. 2:4 *iustus autem in fide sua vivet*, "the just man shall live in his faith," without *meus*; the possessive with *ex* is the wording of Heb. 10:38] Even one that is not just will be in faith, but none save the just shall live in faith; because he too is in faith who says: « Lord, Lord [n: (Matth. VII)], » and yet it is not [0291A] opened to him; and he lives in faith who, doing the will of the Lord, is heard. And because faith is not the presence of the thing wished for, but the substance of the thing to be hoped for and, as was said before, the shadow of the vision [n: (Hebr. II)], the just, whom their own merits do not yet admit to that same vision, promise themselves meanwhile not so much to be as to live in its shadow. « In his shadow, they say, we shall live among the Gentiles [n: (Thren. IV)]. » [var: Vulg. *umbra tua*, "in thy shadow," second person] In the shadow the just shall live, when in faith they shall love to work, when justly, piously, holily they shall give diligence to their converse; when it shall be given them with a living faith to rejoice among the Gentiles, that is, among men, to whom it is not yet given to glory in the vision found among the angels. They shall live indeed in the shadow, when him whom it is agreed was made flesh in the womb of the Virgin they shall hold with firm faith to have been born God before the ages; [0291B] when him who was found a man to suffer hard things for men they shall love as being God, coeternal with and altogether like to the majesty of the Father. Lastly they shall live, when, as though the things which that man willed to endure unworthily among men were put behind them, they shall understand and shall love God blessed unto the ages to be pre-eminent above all things; when, leaving behind what any animal men can see and hold, they shall have before their eyes chiefly that which none but the spiritual deserve to have [n: (ibid.)]. Therefore they set this first: The Spirit before our face, Christ the Lord. Christ the Lord is held to be a spirit before our face then, when our faith beholds him now not according to the flesh but according to the spirit; when, understanding that his flesh alone [0291C] profits nothing, it knows him who quickeneth to be God, a spirit [n: (Joan. VI)]. For the very many things that are preached of him according to the flesh, they who still savour the flesh embrace too childishly; and the hinder parts of Christ they do not put last, but with faith injured, not knowing the order, they put them first. When Peter heard the nearness of his Passion preached, he was found to embrace the flesh a little more tenderly than the spirit; and before his face he had Christ indeed, but a man about to die, not a spirit raising up, not the God of life about to give the dead back again. « Far be it, he says, from thee [n: (Matth. XVI)], » this shall not be, thou shalt not die. And he: « Go, he says, behind; thou savourest not the things that are of God [n: (ibid.)]. » [var: Vulg. Matt. 16:23 *Vade post me, Satana*, and the name is not omitted; *retro* is the word of Mark 8:33] Therefore Paul, removing his hearers from the things that are man's, wishes them to be made readier for the things that are [0291D] God's, understanding that the things of the flesh are by right judgment to be set behind, and that God and the things that are his are to be set before the face. « That you may walk, he says, worthily of God [n: (I Thess. II)]. » And elsewhere: « And if we have known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know him so no longer [n: (II Cor. V)]. » In this shadow, or faith, in which it is granted even to the unjust to be, but to live, as was said before, to none save the just, there is indeed much labour, while it is lived in with fear; for wretched man knows not whether he be worthy of hatred or of love [n: (Eccle. IX)]. But the virgin Mother, who before all found grace with God, keeps sabbath in it, obtains a happy jubilee; and not only lives (which is common to all that labour) but, as one secure, *166* sits, which she obtains as her own by resting. For she it [0292A] is upon whom that Holy Spirit came, in whom the power of the Most High found out for itself a pleasing rest; and setting her far from fault or from fear by the veiling of the shadow, he conferred upon her, in love alone, a peaceful sitting. *Under the shadow,* she says, *of him whom I had desired, I sat.* With desire she had desired that God the Son of God should come unto her, that a grace untried by all should come before her childbearing as it came before the conception; that kissing her, whom he had kissed, he should fortify her with the seal of chastity, and should keep, as her mind, so also her flesh, with the virgin flower unhurt. Which being received, the Virgin, now knowing neither fault nor fear, sits as it were upon a throne under the shadow of a more gracious refreshing; and while the rest labour below, whom the want of merits still cramps, she in the great furnished upper room already tastes an unspeakable sweetness. *And his fruit,* she says, *[0292B] was sweet to my throat.* The fruit of the shadow, or of the overshadowing Spirit, is the eternal sweetness, which the Virgin's palate already feels and her inward mind more sweetly tastes; but that savour is still held as it were in the throat, while the belly gapes for it, and its greed is not filled. In this life assuredly whatever of the sweetness from above is foretasted, whatever of the divine and incorporeal the mind beset with flesh experiences, is still as it were in the tasting, whereby the desire of the taster is soothed; it has not yet come to the belly, where perfect satiety is found. Therefore rightly the Virgin, to show that she has attained no little sweetness, and that so long as we live here every sweetness is unfinished, *his fruit,* she says, *was sweet to my throat;* not yet [0292C] to my belly, because both for the present she rejoices to be delighted with a most sweet tasting, and looks to be satisfied unto perfection, when his glory shall have appeared. And whoever tries the office of eating a little more attentively knows that food in the throat is indeed not yet swallowed, but is being swallowed; and that the swallowing is so near that it cannot, or scarcely can, be called back outward to the mouth, so that the eater reckons as eaten what he has felt to be already turning in his throat. So too the Virgin, while she is still engaged in this life under the law of mortals, is kindled with a love so sweet beyond other mortals, that the fruit of love, or of the beloved, already grows sweet in her throat, and, confidence prevailing, no threatening fear grows bitter there. For she has already so experienced that highest thing, that desirable good of the world to come, that, reaching out to what is perfect, [0292D] she is not uncertain of the remainder of that same good; and she already holds in her throat what we, still far unequal, hunger after; what we, still needy, scarcely foretaste on the tip of the tongue or on the lips. For neither we nor the like of us have yet been brought so far as to rejoice more fully, being led into that sanctuary of God; whereof the virgin, still living in the flesh, takes to herself no small confidence, and what is to be more fully hereafter she already confidently presumes as though it were done.
