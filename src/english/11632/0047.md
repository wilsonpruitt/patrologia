## BOOK THREE.



## CHAPTER THE FIRST.

[0303A]

*The voice of my beloved.* Rightly did the Bridegroom will to warn the daughters of Hierusalem under an oath, that they should not presume to remove the beloved from her sleep or her leisure until she willed it, knowing that she, when the fitting time should come, would more suitably wake up and betake herself to those whom charity compels her to remember. And she, having tasted the slumber wherein she deserved to fall asleep in his left hand, does not wish to pass over in silence the savour she drank there; but for the sake of her neighbours, breaking off a little what she had begun, she is roused, she wakes, she reports to us what she has perceived. But first she shows that she has heard the Bridegroom speaking, and has caught the voice of the speaker as one well known; who, careful for her sleep, not only puts his left hand under the head of her that sleeps, but also with a terrible oath [0303B] sets himself against the troubles that come.

## CHAPTER II.

*The voice of my beloved.* This voice, I say, which in leisure and sweet silence makes me serene; which keeps outward troubles away from the calm chamber of her that rests; which thunders and adjures, and drives far off importunate assaults; which satisfies in part, though not wholly, the desire of her that sleeps: this voice, I say, is beyond doubt his whom I have loved, whom in cherishing me, in defending me, I have already often known to be gracious to me. I recognize him whom I love, I feel his voice now present, I perceive by the hearing that he loves and does not neglect one who loves; his voice sounds for me, speaks of me, and favouring me grants me a defence, and to you who are set beneath grants a teaching neither useless nor contemptible. His voice declares [0303C] me his beloved, plainly hints that my will pleases him, teaches you discipline, teaches also knowledge, teaches goodness too [n: (Psal. CXII)]; it admits me into the chamber, or rather into a kind of porch, of a welcome peace, and suffers me to sleep a little; to you who wait it *173* is not silent as to what it inwardly judges to be expedient. I therefore, the friend of the Bridegroom, stand and rejoice because of the voice of the Bridegroom [n: (Joan. III)], and shall rejoice the more when he shall have appeared who is heard; and would that, until I see him, I might not see death. But when will he come? Why does he weave delays? Thinkest thou, shall I see him? Would that he might rend the heavens and come [n: (Isa. LXIV)]. I wish indeed that the herald should no longer speak before him, that the forerunner should no longer go before; but that he himself who speaks should come nearer, that my heart may see [0303D] and rejoice to be filled with the word of that prophecy which with a truthful heralding he uttered by the mouth of Isaias: « I myself, he says, that spoke, behold I am here [n: (Isa. LII)]. » Yet nevertheless, so far as I seem to myself to forebode, nay so far as I seem already to feel, behold he comes; he has found out the way of prudence, the manner of journeying, the fitting time; and he whom I desire, for whom I sigh, whom I embrace with unceasing vows, behold he comes with a rush, and is already nearer, at the doors.
