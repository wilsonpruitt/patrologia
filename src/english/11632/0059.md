## CHAPTER XV.

*For our vineyard hath flourished.* He who a little before named (vineyards) consequently names a single (vineyard), though rather in counting the passage is wont to be made from the singular number to the plural; hinting by this that the souls to which the Bridegroom willed to give a saving life he gathers, by the union of faith and of consent, into one people's assembly. « We, say they, are his people, and the sheep of his pasture [n: (Psal. XCIX)]. » And they call themselves (a people), that the singleness of faith may be commended, and they call themselves (sheep), that the diversity of the many may be shown; because a timely unity gathers many either into a bundle or into a handful, [0333C] and one Lord, one faith, makes them one people. And that a unity of this kind may be seen to be the more expressly commended, in those apostles whom Christ willed to be gathered into one [n: (Act. IV)] the love of possessing all things together so put far off whatever stood importunately in the way, that there was one soul to them all and one heart to them all [n: (Act. II)]. This vineyard perhaps is said in a special manner to have flowered, into which the Bridegroom is read to have poured his Spirit on the fiftieth day; which, rich with a new gift and burning with the love of a new holiness, put forth new and unheard-of blossoms of a voluntary poverty. Whereof it was foretold: « The vineyard of the Lord of Sabaoth is the house of Israel [n: (Isa. V)]. » Rightly is the vineyard of the Lord of Sabaoth, putting forth wondrous flowers and about to put forth fruits equally wondrous, called the house of Israel — a house beyond doubt not darkened [0333D] by blindness; a house seeing God in hope in the present and about to see him again hereafter; rejoicing much in that faith, and about to rejoice far more in the sight. Within the enclosures of this vineyard it is fitting that little foxes be not admitted, or, if perchance they have been admitted, that they be not suffered to remain longer; but that, when they have destroyed by a hidden avarice Ananias and Sapphira [n: (Act. V)], as yet ill and tender, they be caught the sooner, lest they go on and fulfil the coveted malice of their fraud. For the Bridegroom's companions, faithfully standing by Peter and showing the hidden bite of the little fox in the false brethren, suffer not her venomous snares to be spread everywhere among the rest; and the seducing foxes grieve that their purpose is broken and their frauds detected. For those over whose salvation [0334A] the angels have perceived themselves to be set, and of whom they have learned that the Bridegroom truly has care, these they will not, nor do they suffer them, to be given over to the earth and to be the portions of foxes [n: (Psal. LXII)], but they forewarn them and help them not to lie under their frauds. At this [sic: *tempere*] therefore the foxes are not undeservedly called little, while they are easily kept from ravening by the ministry of the angels; which foxes, when the end of the times shall have come, will grow great more maliciously, when, namely, with their strength unbridled, the more briefly they have to rage the more freely they shall grow hot for it [n: (Apoc. XX)]. Then the angelic solicitude, as though not sufficing, will call them to a greater audience, and our own Samson, setting fire beneath, will bind the tails of them all; and the loosing of the foxes will hasten to rage into the harvests of the aliens, that the works both of these and of those may deserve to perish in one [0334B] burning. For then the wicked spirits shall perish by a fire that destroys but does not consume, and evil men also shall none the less wretchedly perish by an immortal death; those, their hinder parts indissolubly bound together, shall not return to the liberty of wickedness; and of these, as of the aliens, the harvests — both their works and the hope of their works — shall perish. And because the Bridegroom in his benevolence wills this to be kept far from the vineyards, and wills the angels to be employed in keeping it off while the time is fitting, the Bride rejoices, and loves to answer her loving Bridegroom in like manner the more, the more she sees him to be the readier toward her maidens. Whereof she adds: *The beloved,* etc.
