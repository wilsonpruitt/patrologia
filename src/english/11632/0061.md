## CHAPTER XVII.

*Who feedeth among the lilies, until the day breathe, and the shadows be bowed down.* For when, the night being bowed down, nothing dark, nothing hurtful in us shall any longer run in upon us, and at the morning's rising a gentler breeze shall breathe and an unfailing light shall meet us; the Bridegroom perhaps will then no longer run about among the lilies for the sake of feeding, since, being once seen by us, he will not run back again to his own to be hidden. For from that time, and thenceforward, he shall not be fed between the one lily and the other, nor shall he be shown either forsaking the one or referring himself to the other; but he shall so be in the one that he is continually presented to the other — nay, so in both that [0337D] he is absent from neither even for a little. « I, he says, in the Father, and the Father in me [n: (Joan. XIV)]; » and below, of the Spirit: « He shall abide, he says, with you, and shall be in you [n: (ibid.)]. » Therefore God in God, God abiding in himself, containing all things, shall be for ever; and none the less shall God be all things in all; and he who now breathes when he wills, in what manner he wills, and where he wills, then breathes upon all, flooding them with a light of the day, or rather of long continuance. For to whom, while it is lived here, that holy spirit worthily breathes; into whom by his kindly bounty he breathes himself not only unto life but unto a living life — upon him a brighter day then breathes with an unspeakable increase, against which the ancient night, the importunity of the shadows, thenceforward [0338A] conspires not; for God is a day, a day that a setting *193* knows not, one day, whose breathing now, and whose breathing upon us then still more, is no moderately seasonable thing. A day at whose fuller breathing the shadows are bowed down, the darknesses of the night give place, wicked spirits, or spiritual wickednesses, are driven out from among those above and pressed down even to those below. Unhappy indeed are those shadows, which, when the day breathes, are compelled to be bowed down, and, their neck being shattered, to be weighed down with infernal punishments: their power of raging lost, but not the straitness of suffering, they execrate their own heavy substance, or their eternal wretchedness of existing in evil. Far happier were they, if they might deserve to be brought to nothing, if, as of a wicked power, so also they might utterly be deprived of substance; because for a wicked man, or a wicked angel, it will be better now not to be, [0338B] than always to be in so great a misery — not of doing hurt, but of suffering. But shadows of this kind, the confusion of darkness, the obscure voice, the power of malignant spirits which shall at last more fully receive what it wickedly wrought, impatient of the day that breathes, is heavily bowed down under a hard burden, and is irremediably assigned to the avenging fire and the living worm. But meanwhile, while the consummation of the world, the last end of things, is deferred, or while the term of her days is not yet at hand for the aforesaid Virgin, the bridegroom is found to go and to come again, and not to abide unmoved; and the beloved grants not to her that loves him a fuller abundance of himself. Now, drawn far nearer to heaven as it were, he wills to be sought by the Virgin; now, drawing near to earth, he suffers himself to be felt hastily and for a little: [0338C] there he seems more present, and hence to be farther off; but thence to return, when he wills to be at hand nearest to the Virgin. Therefore he is not unfittingly said to converse among the lilies, and to delight in his running out and his running back after the manner of things unstable: to be fed between two shores, and to be turned to neither of them with a fixed footprint: now absent, now present, to go away the more quickly, and straightway to return. To whom the Virgin subjoins: *Return,* etc.
