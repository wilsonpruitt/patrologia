## CHAPTER XX.

*I will arise, and will go about the city: through the lanes and the streets I will seek him whom my soul loveth.* Because, straitened in my little bed, I cannot find him whom I love, I will arise; [0343B] and I will set about to go round the breadth of the city; I will arise from the leisure and diligence of contemplating, and will gird myself to the zeal and the business of working. Nor will I imitate Dinah [n: (Gen. XXXIV)]; I will not go forth to see the women abroad; I will not buy a farm, nor go into the country for secular business; but I will go round the fortified city within its circuit, and will enter in unto the heart of Jerusalem for the sake of calling away and of strengthening. I will not assign my nights to fault, nor to the body, nor to leisure, but for the speaking of good I will lift up my hands in the nights [n: (Psal. CXXXIII)]; and him whom I love I will seek the more diligently through the lanes and the streets — him whom I cannot find in my little bed — if perchance I may be strong enough to find him among them. For sometimes the profit which we feel not in leisure we find more bountifully in the business of speaking; [0343C] and when we impart the due care to our brethren and our neighbours, he is the more present at hand whom by this office we obey. I judge those to be called lanes whom the straightness of faith and the certainty of a long-suffering hope directs; but streets, those whom the sweeter breadth of an advanced charity receives; through whom the Virgin asserts that she will seek the Bridegroom, because, imparting care to them, she reckons that she shall please him. For because the Bridegroom had above invited her to arise, « arise, he says, make haste, my friend, » that is, that thou mayest visit thy neighbours again for teaching: the Virgin, obeying his warnings, breaks off for a little the leisure she had taken up, and promises both to arise and to seek the Bridegroom through the lanes and the streets. For the Virgin loves with a worthy care to visit again and to go round those who are contained within the religious circuit of the fortified city, who within the enclosures of the Church confess themselves fellow-citizens of the saints and [0343D] of the household of God — removing by her intervention whatever is hurtful, and bestowing by her example the things that shall profit. And she walks through the lanes and the streets in that same city when she helps and instructs those that are upright in faith, long-suffering in hope, diffused in charity, and busies herself to promote them to better things, that she may deserve more amply to please him to whom she has approved herself. For thus is he both sought by them that love him and wills to be sought, and wills that they who seek should earn the grace of him that is sought. Thus, through those that are narrow and subtle in understanding, and enlarged in knowledge, the anxiety of them that seek is exercised; and though they profit not yet enough for the finding, they are at least promoted to a greater merit. Thus, I say, through the men of the Church, as through the lanes and the streets of the city, he wills his own face to be sought, but that face, being invisible, [0344A] not yet to be found through visible men; because, although by giving the grace of seeking he wills a greater merit to be acquired, yet he wills not that they who seek should as yet prove the wished-for fulness of the thing sought. When therefore Isaiah cries: « Seek ye the Lord while he may be found [n: (Isa. LV)], » he seems not, as I judge, to mean this, that they who seek can find here to the full him whom they sought; but rather that they who study to bring a watchful diligence to the seeking can at the present time obtain, not the reward of merit, but the merit of the reward. They therefore who have as yet to live by faith, in hope of what is to come, seek the Lord God, being about to find him not at once but afterwards; through the nights they seek the day, being about to enjoy his presence not at once, nor to come to full joy until the day shall have shone forth. For through the nights, although a worthy [0344B] zeal is bestowed upon the seeking, yet not the light, but the merit of finding the light is found; and in the presence of the nights it is a fitting time for seeking, and, the merit being found, there remains the reward to be obtained. For who would reckon to find the day through the nights, since, unless the nights give place, the more welcome day refuses to come; and since the presence of the nights can be called nothing else than the absence of the sun, and since, when the sun rises, it is agreed that flight is straightway proclaimed to the nights? While the night is indeed present, the sun sends not forth his shining orb, and, the darkness remaining, he suffers not his own face to be seen; but, hastening to his rising, he sends the day-star before as a herald, by which token he promises himself not present, but coming the sooner. But that day-star (by which messenger the coming sun is preceded) [0344C] is found by them that seek while the nights are still at hand; and when it is found, the very darknesses of the nights already grow thin, but they wholly vanish not save when the sun is risen. While therefore through the nights the absent sun is sought, sometimes the day-star shows itself, which intends not to show the sun while the nights are still present, but instructs them that seek whither they should tend, whither they should direct the eye that is to find, namely from what quarter they should hope for the presence of the sun that is to come. For so long as a deep night holds and oppresses the world, and an unclean dregs presses hard upon the consciences of us wretched men, who knows (the day-star not yet seen) whence the sun has to rise, since, with no forerunner, not even the way to him can be found? But when he that seeks the sun beholds the day-star going before, taught by that foreteller he is now held by a certain assurance that [0344D] the presence of the sun is not far from him that foretells it, but that, because the night comes between, his face is not yet seen. And so he that seeks a light through the nights finds a light, but a light of the night, being about by this light to find a light afterwards, but a light of the day; and this near day, this sun that is to come, who wills his own presence to be deferred, *197* wills the day-star to be borne before, that men may come to it more surely. This does he that asks receive, this does he that seeks find, this is the door that is unbarred to him that knocks: to him that seeks the sun for a reward, the way is first shown as a day-star; first, I say, of grace is merit given to him that labours, and so, as of merit, the sun is given, nay shall be given, for a reward to him that waits. For the bridegroom does not give the reward at once, as he gives the merit, even though perchance every man set the good wine before that which is worse [0345A] [n: (Joan. II)] — but he is the true sun, who alone above all things deserves to be loved; and he wills indeed to be seen, and, unless the day-star be sent before, he is not seen. Therefore perhaps, when the Gospel had said: « Every one that asketh receiveth, and he that seeketh findeth [n: (Matth. VII)], » marking that the benefit of merit is given in the present, it showed that another thing remained to be given in what follows: « And to him that knocketh, it says, it shall be opened [n: (ibid.)]. » He who by asking, it says, and by seeking rejoices that the merit going before is imparted to him, shall by knocking — that is, by pressing on — rejoice that the door is afterwards opened to him; because he who through the nights is advanced by grace together with merit, as by a day-star, shall, the sun's palace being opened, enjoy to the full the glory of the full light. A little above, when it said, ask, seek, knock, subjoining words answering to each, it bestowed nothing in the present, but brought forth (using the future tense) [0345B] words of promise: leaving us in suspense whether they should be fulfilled within the nights, that is in this time, or should pass over all at once to the wished-for presence of the eternal sun. « Ask, it says, and you shall receive; seek, and you shall find; knock, and it shall be opened to you [n: (ibid.)]. » [var: Vulg. Matt. 7:7 *dabitur vobis*, "it shall be given you"; the reading printed here is the wording of John 16:24] But lest it be long doubtful that nothing is not bestowed in the nights upon the desire of us that ask, and that nothing likewise is not deferred until the day dawn and the sun rise rather than the day-star, it straightway added: « For every one that asketh receiveth, and he that seeketh findeth [n: (ibid.)]. » This is of the present time. For it says not, he shall receive and he shall find, but « He beginneth and findeth. And to him that knocketh, it says, it shall be opened. » [var: Vulg. Matt. 7:8 *accipit*, "receiveth"] This is of the sun that is to come. For it says not, « to him that knocketh » it is opened, but « it shall be opened, » that it may distinguish between that which is granted us to obtain in the present, and that which [0345C] is promised to be granted much better in what follows. So to Jacob, who asked and sought not Lia for a wife but Rachel [n: (Gen. XXIX)], the father, keeping the custom of the country, gave Lia before Rachel; in the marriage he set the elder-born first — not indeed of equal beauty, but fruitful — promising that entrance to the second should be given the young man afterwards. In like manner the Virgin also, seeking through the nights, through the lanes and the streets, him whom she had known to be beautiful above all, first receives, first finds the fruitful gift of merit; but because she now pants not so much after merit as after the reward, not so much after labour as after rest, she is believed to have found nothing so long as he reveals not his face. Therefore she repeats: *I sought,* etc.
