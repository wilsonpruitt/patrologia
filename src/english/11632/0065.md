## CHAPTER XXI.

[0345D]

*I sought him, and found him not.* Behold, now for the third time the Virgin asserts that she has sought him whom she loves, and complains that she has not yet found the sought one according to her liking; and, he being not found, she is not broken by labour, she despairs not of her purpose, but perseveres in the hope of finding and in the zeal of seeking. It belongs to one that loves to seek once, and again, and many times over, what is loved; if delay come between, not to desist from what is hoped for; and if she be hardly used while that which is sought comes not the sooner, yet let her not faint: to a desire too long deferred hope comes to the rescue. The Virgin therefore seeks to behold the face of him whom she loves, and nothing suffices her that seeks so long as she perceives herself hindered from her desire; and, not content with her little bed (in which a girl's modesty might worthily seem able [0346A] to be kept), she goes about the city, walks through the lanes, and now shrinks not from being found in the midst of the streets. So great a love urges her, an unrestrained burning so kindles her — a burning that is a despiser of persons and heeds not what befits whom; which, when once it has blazed up, cares no longer for the judgment of reason, and conforms to its own unrestraint those whom it has made to burn. The virgin, blazing with that unrestraint the more delightfully the more strongly, called back by no fear of the night, dreading no public place that grudges virgins their way, seeks him whom she loves; and, not content to seek once, while she repeats it and hastens hither and thither, she is found by them that meet her.

## CHAPTER XXII.

*The watchmen who keep the city found me.* While she goes about wandering and is carried anxiously round, [0346B] while she runs to and fro and keeps seeking, and the presence of the sought one is not offered; they to whom the care or the keeping of the city is committed, who keep watch upon the walls with the vigilant anxiety of charity, come and find that she grows not sluggish with a degenerate sleep — and, loving, rejoice at her watchings and at the Virgin's anxious love. For to this end the companions of the Bridegroom, lofty in angelic dignity, upheld by a wondrous grace and stable in their own holiness, are sent into ministry, and watch over our city, that they may both see the progress of them that love and may cherish it with a ready will. These therefore find her, and rejoice that the Virgin is found; *198* they consider her that loves, and marvel at the greatness of her love; and they who had known very many lovers to be commended in a long line behind, see, and grudge not, [0346C] that the bridegroom is now more loved by the Virgin. For reckoning over in order, upwards, the citizens and household of that city, to whom it was given to have loved the bestower of grace, the author of truth, they weigh that in all of them the grace of loving was less, and they rejoice that the Virgin has found none among mortals her equal in loving. And expressly the watchmen were not found by the Virgin, but the Virgin by the watchmen: because the anxiety of the watchmen is bent upon the city to this end, that it may seek out the more diligently who receives a falling away and who a progress in holiness, and that the good will of an affectionate charity may grieve with the one and rejoice with the other. But the Virgin, as she sought not men, so neither did she seek angels; but with the highest desire she busied herself to find the Lord of the angels; and therefore, if she perceives any perchance to come to meet her, [0346D] she declares rather that she is found by them than that she finds them. And when her finders would perhaps have accosted her, being found, and asked whence and whither she was going, or what she meant to find, she, forgetful of the female sex, hastens to forestall them that were about to speak, and, impatient of love, to open her desire to them

## CHAPTER XXIII.

*Have you seen him whom my soul loveth?* The Virgin should more modestly have waited in silence while those spoke first whom she knew to hold an ampler degree of dignity, until the angels that met her should set about to admonish the woman beforehand, and, they speaking first, she thus admonished should answer. But love, being more immoderate, knows not how to use measure or governance; it fears not sex, not order, not to overpass the bounds; [0347A] it seems with a certain haughtiness of mind to forget the rights of all laws, thinking it just to remember unceasingly him alone whom it loves: « Lord, she says, I will be mindful of thy justice only [n: (Psal. LXX)]. » Holding him therefore alone in an unswerving memory — him by whose grace he loves the undeserving, and by whose justice the deserving — when she feels the angels to be at hand for the keeping of the city, to admonish the imperfect and not to fail the more perfect, she reckons their presence not enough for her, until she shall have found him whom she believes to be far above the angels. For this cause, not enduring to be accosted by those same who watch zealously over the care of the citizens, she rather accosts them inquisitively; and love, stronger than fire, not knowing how to be contained under the embers of ash, breaks into flame more evidently and wills to be seen by them that stand about. And since she is held slower to be silent and readier [0347B] to speak, she fans him alone upon her lips, and is wordy about no other — him, I say, whom she loves, whom, being beloved, she seeks the more ardently to find; and since she finds him not yet, she wills at least to hear news of him the more inquisitively. You, she says, to whom it is given always to behold the face of the father, whom it is agreed are hindered from beholding neither by fault, nor by flesh, nor by any trouble whatever: him whom I love, whom I desire to find, you have not seen — have you seen in God the Father the Son who is equally God? Has that happiness been bestowed upon you his companions which to me, his betrothed, is thus far denied by a heavy law — namely, that with a keen eye you may behold my Bridegroom in his glory, who to mine, the eye of his mother and his Bride, endures not yet to be seen? Him, I beseech you, with whom you have found so happy a grace — if it please you, if it seem fitting, [0347C] say whether you have seen him; and to me that burn either show him upon whom you desire to look, or at least declare with a comforting word your own vision of him. For it will be no small comfort to me if you declare that you have seen him, the grace of seeing whom I have not yet deserved to find; because I am not one to grieve that any others, happy men, have come before, whither I, far slower, rejoice not yet to have come. Say, I say, whether you have seen him — nay, since you have seen him, declare it laudably; commend with a saving doctrine what you have more fully perceived of him; because I judge it worthy that an ear be given to you that teach, who have deserved to contemplate his face (while we are still in the dark). You indeed, with jaws that labour not with hunger, but blessed, unwearyingly desiring, [0347D] yet — loathing being driven far off — satisfied, eat in his kingdom the living bread, the incorruptible nourishment; refreshed with the fatness whereof you know no trouble at all [n: (Matth. XVIII)]. And though you are now at hand to us that labour on the earth, for the sake of comforting, yet you are always present at his table and his banquet; and while through the importunate nights a fasting hunger presses us, in the courts of that Lord there is given you a full refreshing, one day.

The angels are not brought in to answer the Virgin's questions, perhaps because the Virgin would not endure the delay of answering, whose burning mind every deferring now troubles; and for this cause she makes plain what, impatient of delay, she did. *199*
