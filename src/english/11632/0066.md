## CHAPTER XXIV.

[0348A]

*When I had a little passed by them, I found him whom my soul loveth.* She perceives that he whom she loves is not any one of the angels, nor can be shown by an angel, since he abides far above the excellence of the angels; holding it for certain that the Bridegroom and son so veils his own face, which is God's, that none sees it save he to whom the same son shall have willed to reveal it. Unwilling therefore to linger any longer among these, nor to await their answers with an anxious hearing, the Virgin hastens to pass by the more quickly to the seeking of the Bridegroom, whom she could not find save by passing by. Or perhaps, as has been said before, the watchmen, questioned concerning the Bridegroom — not torpid with ignorance, but the readier and the freer for answering — are not silent that they had seen him in his own glory, [0348B] lest they should seem to have grudged the seeker her affections or her advances. They teach the Virgin, therefore, that he whom she loves is not to be seen with them, but is to be sought far beyond with a loving vigilance, and that a passing-by must be made by her that seeks (that she may be able to see); which done, the beloved is to be shown to her that loves according to the good pleasure of him that shows. O Virgin, they say, we both rejoice in seeing him whom thou lovest, and what we have seen and heard, this we testify; and (if thou truly desirest that the sight of him should likewise strengthen thee) it behoves thee not to stand, not to cleave, but rather to pass by. Discerning that the nature of every creature is mutable — having indeed a beginning, though not likewise about to have an end — pass thou by, and thou shalt find him of another and unlike nature, which knows no change nor shadow of a vicissitude to come. [0348C] These things, or the like of them, the watchmen perhaps answered the Virgin, and promised her the finding of him whom she so loves; but the Virgin, in more haste, did not rehearse their words in order, but set forth more compendiously what she herself had done: *When I had passed by,* she says, *them, I found him whom my soul loveth.* By the words of comfort of those, she says, by whom I was found while I sought my beloved, I was for a while detained; but burning insatiably, and not content with such solace, I was stretched out with looser reins toward that which lies beyond. For seeing that the angels did not supply me my [sic: *desideriam*], and that the keepers of the city had not the effectual grace of supplying it, I passed on, considering whether the cherubim and seraphim, and the spirits of a worthier excellence, might confer [0348D] upon me in my burning the gift of a fuller sufficiency. For as there are angels who, sent for the ministry of salvation, minister to the heirs, so perhaps there are others more worthy, who, since they are not sent by the Lord, stand by him; and by however much they are set over these lower and ministering ones, by so much are they laid beneath him to whom they rejoice unceasingly to stand by. I passed the angels, therefore, when I ascended to those worthier spirits. I passed quite by when I marked that neither sort could become the fulfillers of my desire; when I understood that among the angels not the order only but the nature is unavailing, and that no creature at all suffices for my desire. I passed by when I marked that the watchmen, or the angels, had been made. I passed quite by in considering that they had been made not by themselves but by another; and that he who had made them is so the author of these and of all things that he himself has, for his own part, no creator at all — [0349A] neither himself nor any other whatsoever. I passed by when it was truly given me to know this: that the angels, being made, can do nothing at all without him who made them. I passed quite by in considering that he who is the maker can do all things through himself, who alone suffices me for all things in giving himself. But thus to pass the watchmen, or rather to pass them quite by — what is it but to find him whom my soul loveth? who cannot be more fully found unless the creature be passed over, which none but one passing quite by is worthily able to prove. So then, passing quite by the watchmen, I thus found him whom I love, and thus came to the excellence of him who is not made but maker; I found that the Creator far surpasses every creature, and that therefore nothing besides him ought to be loved. But when the Virgin says [0349B] that she has found him whom she loves, what is it that she premises — that she had a little passed by the watchmen — when she seems to have passed not a little but very much, indeed all things, she who rejoices to have found him who is far above all things? But because, while she is held by the law of mortals, by the bond of a heavier flesh, however far she advance in the ardour of a purer desire, she yet finds not the Bridegroom save only by the eye of faith or of love, she rightly says "a little," in respect of a fuller grace. For though by zeal and grace she cleanses and advances her understanding, and by a marvellous advance, going beyond the angels, comes to her beloved: yet because this coming, while life is lived here, has something imperfect in it, the eye of faith thinks itself carried over by a small passage, as it were. Great indeed might this passage rightly be called, if she now weighed [0349C] that she was gainsaid by no burden of the flesh; if she exulted greatly not in faith but in fact, not in hope but in sight; if she enjoyed the Bridegroom's face, and no disturbance called her back from that enjoying. Great would the passage be, if she saw herself despoiled of her inborn mortality, and, clothed upon with glory, worthily exalted above the angels; sitting on the Bridegroom's throne by a royal right and no wrong, and, Bride and mother, cleaving to the son by a certain singular privilege. But meanwhile, while *200* no such fact or sight is granted, she embraces in faith and hope him whom she loves; and him for the finding of whom she says that she had a little passed by the watchmen, she declares that, once found, she held with a most firm bond.
