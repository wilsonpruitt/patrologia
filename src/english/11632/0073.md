## CHAPTER VI.

[0363D] *Go forth, ye daughters of Sion, and see king Solomon in the diadem wherewith his mother crowned him, in the day of his espousal, and in the day of the gladness of his heart.* Because the companions had foretold that the Virgin was now the little bed, now the *ferculum* of Solomon, and had described the one as adorned with much strength and the other with much beauty, lest in this they should seem to have bestowed more upon the bride than upon the bridegroom, and, in commending the Virgin, to have paid Solomon less than was due, they invite the daughters of Sion to the study of a larger understanding, that they may attentively consider what of glory or of honour the things aforesaid have brought to Solomon. Wherefore they take pains expressly to forewarn them to go forth, namely lest they should be willing to remain either in their own hovels of vices or in the darknesses of ignorance; because they are not fit [0364A] for seeing whose life is entangled in vices, or whose understanding is hindered by a mist poured over it. You therefore, they say, whom we invite the more diligently to see, know that in the things which are behind you must not remain any longer; and for this cause we will that you go out from the dark, from the wicked wrapping, and that you free the feet of your going out from the spots that hinder them. Go out therefore from the hovel of a life that darkens the understanding, go out from the midst of a dim-eyed ignorance; go out from the gross and rustic understanding of dim-sighted peoples, advance into daughters, have a solicitude concerning your advance. Go forth, and by going forth be worthily made daughters that gaze; with an anxious hand cleanse your eyes that labour with bleariness: with a mirror, as it were, set before you, wipe away the spots of your face, and after the manner of girls bestow care upon the beauty that is to be obtained. [0364B] By this going forth and advance out of the inward negligence of a blameworthy workshop, by this zeal of gazing and by the mirror of discipline, you shall obtain that vision which is necessary for you, whereby it shall be given you to see Solomon in his diadem. Solomon, I say, the king, whom none can see in his own comeliness save the saints, with face indeed washed and with a cleaner eye — they who burn with a girl's solicitude to obtain beauty, who with a cleansed eye are able to gaze upon things inaccessible to the dim-sighted. Solomon's diadem is the passibility of the flesh, wherewith the undefiled virginity of his mother crowned him, when the Lord did a new thing, and a woman compassed a man, according to the prophecy of Jeremias [n: (Jer. XXXI)] — a Virgin nevertheless conceiving and bearing, by the testimony none the less of Isaias [n: (Isa. VII)]. Which flesh, or [0364C] passibility of the flesh, is not undeservedly honoured with the name of a diadem; because, although in the eyes of the dim-sighted it is reckoned a dishonour or an ignominy, yet whosoever rightly understands that base things of any kind are unworthy of Solomon, and, understanding, loves to know what end there is in a passion of this sort — how great a contest there is in the flesh, how great the profit in the contest — assuredly attributes to it a name not of ignominy but of honour, not of dishonour but of a crown. « We see, saith Paul, Jesus, for the passion of death crowned with glory and honour [n: (Hebr. II)]. » To this crown the Virgin mother ministered the material in her womb; the Spirit, the craftsman, wrought with a learned hand the material so ministered, and wove it into a round with a work so perfect that it incomparably overtops gold, stone, or amber. The Jew, beholding this, esteems it nothing but thorns and toil; [0364D] the unbeliever, considering it, thinks it to have no beauty nor comeliness: the cleansed eye beholds him beautiful in form above the sons of men, not only because he is God, but because, being man, he is not held by the debts of men. *209* To him indeed the material of flesh is naturally common with men, but that same community is free of carnal vices; and by the power of this mystery he admits us personally into the unity of himself, and, when once we are joined to him, suffers us not to be divided from him. Which the round perfection of the diadem beautifully signifies, whose wholeness no division at all breaks asunder; whence also the diadem, taking division away, is said to signify, because whosoever is contained within the compass of this crown knows not to be divided. In this crown therefore, that is, in [0365A] his flesh, he couples and joins us to himself, and, as partakers of the contest, disjoins us not from honour and glory; which glory the mother is said in a manner to have imparted to Solomon, when the Virgin is believed to have conceived God-man in her womb. Who should not render praises to the Virgin, who should not make worthy mention of her — who crowned Solomon not with gold, not with stone, but with her own flesh; who conferred upon the king that whereof he might will and be able to glory, wherein he might conquer and be worthy to be crowned by the merit of victory? Which assuredly they record to have been done in the day of his espousal, and by that deed they intimate that night or mist is far away, because the espousal there present brought no darkness into the bridal chamber, whereon a hallowed day and a hallowing light shone. For when the Bridegroom had entered, an incomparable light arose, [0365B] and no other is admitted, and the gate lies open to none without; but with equal consent they close every approach against all that come upon them, and the companions or angels dance and clap festively before the doors. In a bright day therefore the mystical espousal is celebrated, the chaste Virgin is coupled to one man by a spiritual commerce; the woman compasses the man, Solomon is crowned with a diadem, and to God, by nature immortal, the nature of mortals is personally united. But in this day, wherein the Virgin's Creator was made her Bridegroom, who shall tell what or how great was the gladness of her heart, when in her womb our humanity was taken up into God, and, the Spirit overshadowing, was not consumed by fellowship with him that took it up? How great was the gladness of her heart is not ours [0365C] to utter or to feel, who have not deserved to enter into a sanctuary so sacred and so secret; she herself, to whom God willed to open it more fully and more perfectly, could scarce blurt out even a little of the joy conceived in her heart. « My spirit hath rejoiced, she says, in God my Saviour [n: (Luc. I)]. » Whatsoever therefore is done in the Virgin without doubt knows no night; upon her, as she crowns the king, day is poured in, light grows bright, and with a splendour diurnal, or rather diuturnal, the mother's spirit is made glad — for the worthy taking whereof our eye, the eye of the weak, grows not clear. For neither is this that Bersabee whom David, Urias not knowing it, defiled by adultery, but Mary, the flower of Virgins, whom, singular as she was, the holy Spirit took upon him to make fruitful; nor is this that Solomon who is reported to have reigned from Dan even to Bersabee forty [0365D] years, but Christ, of whose kingdom the end, of whose empire the term, is not had. Whose glory of flesh, whose diadem, no Jew, no Gentile, no accursed man whatsoever can worthily weigh — who, though a Christian in name he whitens himself with the appearance of piety, yet, denying the power thereof, grows dark with a poverty of merits. Therefore the Companions or angels, to whom by the merit of felicity it had been given already to see, lest they should seem to grudge to others the benefit they had received, think it not to be hidden under a bushel, not to be covered with silence, but they invite the daughters of Sion to prepare themselves with fervour and zeal for seeing. For in this praise is rendered not only to Solomon but to the Virgin also, [0366A] and the glory conferred upon the crowned is not taken away from her that crowns: nay, whatsoever the taking of flesh seems to work in the son that is worthy of glory is poured back upon Mary, of whom the same son willed to be incarnate. For which cause the Bridegroom turns not his presence long away from her, but after the words of the companions turns himself again to her praises, that he may show himself to be so crowned with a diadem, so beautiful in glory, that it may stand plain that the beauty of the crown is not wanting to her that crowns him either.
