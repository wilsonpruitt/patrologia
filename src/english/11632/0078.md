## CHAPTER XIII.

*A thousand shields hang upon it, all the armour of the valiant.* The same Scripture which he seems to figure in the bulwarks he seems to figure also in the shields, and to commend her under various names because of her various effects; for since her efficacy in helping is found manifold, it is no wonder if she is marked with manifold words. From the tower, then, the Bridegroom says that shields hang down, and, lest we deem them few, he adds that he keeps not silence as to the number; so that when [0374B] the enemies do not assail, we may not only run back to the hanging shields, but may trust in the number of the shields with a hope that does not deceive. The thousand, which is held highest in the order of numbers, because it is made up of perfect ones, is called the most perfect of perfect ones; because, as the ten is filled up out of ten units, the hundred out of ten tens, so the thousand is filled up out of as many hundreds, than which no other single number is held greater among numbers. By which number is commended the strong defence of the shields, their perfect usefulness, the useful hanging up of things perfect, which are set in order not only for the adorning or the defence of David's tower, but are also set forth with ample defence for us that flee to the tower. The Virgin indeed, whose neck *215* is compared, as has already been said, to a tower, is prepared with manifold valour for [0374C] the defence of us all, who makes clear and expounds to us the sequence of the old Scripture, showing accomplished in herself in very deed what that Scripture sets down as to come in mystery. For what Jeremias long before foretold of a woman that should compass a man [n: (cap. XXXI)], what Isaias foretold of a Virgin that should conceive and bear a son [n: (cap. VII)], what Ezechiel prophesied in a riddle dark enough of the gate not opened [n: (cap. XLIV)], what Moses marvellously prefigured of the rod of Aaron [n: (Num. XVII)], what prophecy of old declared in many places and in many ways and did not exhibit — they that give heed weigh this, that it hangs for us more evidently from Mary. It hangs, I say, from her, nor is it shut up in her with bolts or bars, but by a manifest benefit it is spread abroad even to [0374D] our knowledge, so that, running back to the Virgin, we may rejoice to find more manifestly what a grudging antiquity willed to cover from us with a mystical cloud. Whose mysteries, which we say hang for us from Mary — that is, shine forth from her and through her upon us that look upward — the bridegroom in this epithalamium rightly designated by the name of shields, and, setting them forth to us that are lower, assigned them more nearly and more specially to the tower. For the Virgin herself knew first and more perfectly the mysteries of Holy Scripture, which were both said and foretold of her in the obscure soothsaying of the prophets, and, being more subtly and more excellently instructed in their riddles, she brought them forth to us and offered them to lovers as bucklers. The hollow surface of a buckler protects and hides the citizen, and holds back or beats back from over against him the malice of the assailant; [0375A] so too the deep breadth of Holy Scripture, impassable to the enemy, becomes by the ample help of the Virgin our valour who are citizens. Assuredly through her a sanctified day has now shone upon us that were once in darkness; through her our sun of understanding has shone back in the shield of Holy Scripture; through her we now see, now hold Christ, of old promised, the figures and riddles being wrapped away, the promises of Holy Scripture being now discharged. Through her the lucid perfection of Holy Scripture has condescended to us that are far lower, and with open bosom has shown itself to all that advance in zeal and understanding, in which one may see the sequence of perfect commandments that shall profit them that love, the manner and order of living, and in fine all the armour of the valiant. For whosoever wills to be armed with worthy valour finds in Holy Scripture whence he may rightly [0375B] glory, in which a life-giving building lies hid under the shadow of the letter, which grows clear, by her benefit, to them that worthily seek the Virgin. But they that have no care to seek diligently and to advance valiantly are not able to find useful armour in Holy Scripture because to them that know not the Virgin the face of Holy Scripture is dark; nor is the armour of them that are ignorant, but of them that are valiant in faith. Hence it is that the gentiles, whom a foolish unbelief harshly buffets, and the Jews, whom the Virgin's childbearing scandalizes with no less ruin; find not in Holy Scripture Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God, the knowledge whereof hangs for us, not obscure, from the Virgin. For because they draw not near to the Virgin with worthy reverence, they believe not worthily the marvellous conception, nor the childbearing so unspeakable; it remains that they either see not, or, seeing, [0375C] abhor the hanging shields, and languish unarmed, and perish of their languishing, not using the armour of the valiant. But they whose left hand is not fortified with a hanging sword, whose present conversation is not wholesomely instructed by the understanding of Holy Scripture: their right hand glories not in salvation, nor in the blessedness to come; nay, their reckoning reaches not to the right hand, but is cheated. The neck of the Virgin therefore is not unfittingly likened to a tower, through which so manifold a benefit of Holy Scripture is published to us: through which the mystery of the incarnate Word is spread abroad to us, through which the word of the Father, passing through, is presented to our ears, nay, and to our eyes. For that good word which the very heart of God the Father unspeakably uttered forth, when he ordained to come to men and to be seen by men: taking as it were [0375D] the vehicle of a voice, made for itself a path of this Virgin's neck, through which, having passage, it set itself before them that sought not and that asked not. By the passing of which word that path of his is so magnificently adorned, the Virgin's neck is strengthened with so great valour, that it is held not insufficient to bear the burdens of us that are infirm, and after the manner of a strong tower guards us that are weak. Lastly *turris*, a tower, is named from *tuendo*, guarding (as some assert), because it guards both itself and whatever lies adjacent, and by its guardianship the hope of them that run back to it is made secure, especially if there hangs down a thousandfold row of shields, and all the armour of the valiant. And because the Virgin seems more abundantly to excel in these, the Bridegroom goes on still to add praises to praises; and, loving her that is lovable, [0376A] he does what is proper to a friend to do, who esteems alike whatsoever commendable thing can be said of his friend. He says therefore: *Two breasts,* etc.
