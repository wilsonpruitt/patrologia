## CHAPTER II.

*I am come into my garden, my sister, my spouse; I have gathered my myrrh with my aromatical spices.* Even as the Lord aforesaid is said, when he sent the angel [0405C] to the Virgin, to have come before him, and the Virgin is said to have found grace with him: so here too, being asked to come, he promiseth not his coming to her that asketh, but confesseth that he hath come and hath done what had seemed to him fitting. Thou, saith he, dost carefully ask that I would come, but I have come already: I have come before the time as it were set by thee and the hope of the gift asked for; and beyond what thou shouldest beg or ask, being well-willing I have hastened to come, and I have visited my garden not now by one that walketh before me, but by mine own presence. For it is not meet that a garden which with mine own hand I set in order when I planted it, which when planted and watered I called forth to a fitting increase, I should give up naked of my presence into the hand of them that deceive, and should weaken the former gifts [0405D] of advancement by later and worse ones. The garden therefore which I made, by grafting in it divers virtues, that same I made my own, by conferring a useful keeping; that which I enclosed and sealed, by forbidding all hurtful things, I cherished and advanced as mine own, by affording it my unbroken presence. For how should I defraud of my solicitude, of my presence, her whom as a sister the one only Father offered me to be loved with a kinsman's affection, her whom as a spouse the mysterial oneness urged me to embrace by a severe *234* and sincere commerce — how, I say, should I defraud her, and should not rather crown good beginnings with a better ending? Of which manner of names, to wit of Sister and of Spouse, because the meaning hath in some sort been set out above, I forbear to go over it again, lest so often repeated it should breed weariness; and the prudent [0406A] reader will by no means cleave perchance to the present things alone, but will more carefully turn back his mind and his eye to the things above. But what he hath wrought in the garden by his condescension he showeth, who had been asked with so great desire to come, lest he should be thought to have done nothing there through sloth and negligence, although he had not yet accomplished all things according to the Virgin's pleasure. *I have gathered,* saith he, *my myrrh with my aromatical spices.* Strict chastisement, the strong austerity of abstinence, virginal continence, integrity as well of body as of mind: these with a wondrous anointing keep the virgin's flesh from eternal hurt, and above the right of nature, above the nature of wrath, heap up a mass of merits. Such myrrh the bridegroom coming into his garden hath now gathered, because the mother's continence so perfectly pleased the Son, that when he was incarnate, [0406B] though nature marvelled at an unspeakable begetting, yet the newness of the thing brought in no rot of pride, no little worm, no hurt. For a fruitful integrity, ungranted to all virgins before and after, the bridegroom carried away from the floor as already reaped into the barn, when by his own good pleasure and keeping he willed so to fortify her, that neither the beast of the field nor the bird that is wise for high things could come in unto her. For the pleasure of the flesh, which is wont beastlike to come before fruitfulness, marvelleth to find nothing of its own in the Virgin's conceiving; and marvelleth that in an earthly field a harvest so ripe should grow, and, being taken away as it were by stealth, should suffer nothing of the spoiler. The fowls of the air, which sow nothing honest or profitable to salvation, but for the most part make away with the sowings of others by a devouring greediness: [0406C] indiscreet presumption and empty pride, which are wont to be born of the advancements of the good, and by an inborn depravity rejoice to be fed upon their failings: these grieve that the aforesaid harvest of myrrh is so withdrawn out of the midst, that neither by violence nor by fraud can they be brought in unto it even for a little. The little birds complain that they were drowsy longer than they ought, and were forestalled by the reaper's love and watchful zeal; but that now they have hands watchful and stretched out for the plunder, and are held back by the closure of the barn and by a bar that cannot be unbarred. In such a barn the bridegroom shutteth up his aromatical spices also, and, they being sealed with the key of David, he heareth not the voices of them that bark against him, because he snatcheth away the virgin's modesty as it were by a certain reaping, lest in a field exposed to the wild beasts it be hurt [0406D] by an envious whisperer. The enemy man, who for the most part soweth cockle above the wheat, who, not prevailing to hurt the life, at the least stains the good name of the good, he driveth forth from the Virgin's touch with a hand that meeteth him, and banisheth far off the enemy of chastity from the cloister of modesty. For he willeth not that her fame, more fragrant than spices or ointments, should be able to be defiled by the stronger arguments of any sophister, but that a sure faith should hold her both Virgin and fruitful, and should preach it as a true thing, though to others it be untried. Thus withdrawing the myrrh with the aromatical spices from a plucking rival's sickle, he reapeth it and delivereth it to a barn that keepeth it more faithfully, while he heapeth together her chaste purpose and the most sweet odour of her chastity, and preserveth it by the strong patronage of truth.
