## CHAPTER III. (cont.)

*I have eaten [sic: *fovum*] with my honey: I have drunk my wine with my milk.* Rich by the year's increase in the reaping of myrrh or of spices, he addeth not that he was steeped in an unction according to the literal sequence, but he eateth, though myrrh seem not to be fit for eating; and he drinketh, though no man would say that he thirsteth after spices. But because the Virgin's converse is exposed to pressings and shearings, and the perfection of her merits was gotten not by soft sleep but by watchful studies; and because, in fine, whatsoever the Virgin heapeth up for herself in the present by labour bringeth in to the Bridegroom that giveth and loveth no bitterness but sweetness: therefore, the harvest being made, he now declareth that he hath eaten, showing therein that he had hungered for the Virgin's advancements. Lastly in the Gospel: *Soul,* saith that man, *thou hast much goods laid up [0407B] for many years: take thy rest, eat, drink, make good cheer [n: (Luc. XII)]*. This rich man therefore is not he whose soul must be required in the night for the things he hath committed, but rich with his barn shut upon the harvests brought in; he stretcheth out no false hope unto many days or unto a year, he applieth to those same harvests neither threshing nor winnowing-fan. For by a wondrous manner of speaking, that which he had called myrrh or spices in the reaping he now findeth to be a honeycomb, now declareth to be honey in the eating; because the taste of him that eateth findeth nothing sweeter than honey, and no integrity is found more pleasing to Christ than his mother's. Ye know that a honeycomb is called a kind of storing case, which the more sagacious bee is found to work, marked out with waxen little cells; which bee, cunning in working, hideth herself under a certain covering, [0407C] wherein she layeth up the comb, and in the comb, with wondrous diligence, the honey. Such a honeycomb he himself, whose protection loveth to cover it and with an ever-watchful care to guard it round about — he himself, *235* I say, suffereth not to be hurt, permitteth not to dry up, but with the honey set within it bringeth it in to his own table, to his own good pleasure. A tenderer comb indeed is grievously hurt and drieth up, and, the honey being denied it, is lost through dryness and becometh empty: as when the integrity of the flesh groweth not sweet by the savoury love of Christ, and to him that hateth to sin virtue groweth not bright by its own worth. For frail flesh is rightly said to be wax, which a more studious and austere converse reduceth into a comb; into which honey is put, and an incomparable sweetness lieth hid shut up, when the love of Christ, that is sweeter, is the cause of the chastity taken up. The comb therefore which an inward emptiness drieth and consumeth, [0407D] Christ taketh not for his own food, the Bridegroom taketh not into his own grace; and worthily reprobate to him is that chastisement of the flesh, or that integrity of a maiden, which is presumed to be brought forth and offered dry, without honey. But that one is confidently presented to his sight, to his eating, which is more fully fattened with copious honey, with a gracious love; with whose fat sweetness, or sweet fatness, the eater is made glad, and the more he feasteth, the richer doth the food grow strong. But because drinking is fitly joined to eating, the Bridegroom accordingly signifieth not only that he hath drunk, but also what he hath drunk, that he may show the Virgin who serveth the cup to be both learned and rich, and the draught commendable, which refresheth and fatteneth him that drinketh. *I have drunk,* saith he, *my wine with my milk.* The prudent Virgin, studious to please by a diligent ministry, [0408A] mingled wine in the bowl for her beloved when he had worthily dined, that when he was refreshed with food and likewise made glad with wine, he might be the more bountiful to give whatsoever should be asked of him. And rightly, being given over to a service not corruptible but divine, she giveth the Bridegroom to drink not with turbid waters, but better and more advisedly with wine; showing that in the embrace and ministry of the nuptials she is not cold with any lack, but burneth with a spiritual affection. For it is not that incurable wine, comparable to the venom of asps or of dragons [n: (Deut. XXXII)], wherewith the lost error of the nations rageth and goeth astray; wine, I say, signifieth a love ungranted to rustic and ragged poor men, whereby they that are rich in grace, noble in mind, are caught up into ecstasy. Lastly wisdom, calling us forth to divine love, mingleth not insipid waters but, by a prudent mystery, wine, [0408B] that our converse, which is of little ones or of babes, may grow young, and, the wine being drunk, may ripen with worthy advancements. *Drink,* saith she, *the wine which I have mingled for you: forsake childishness, and live [n: (Prov. IX)]*. You, saith she, whom a childishness holdeth that is not of years but a weakness of manners, not of age; you who labour not by a failing of strength, but after the manner of carnal men with a languid affection: drink the wine which I have mingled for you — not Falernian, but the spiritual love whereof they that are drunken deserve to live for ever. Forsake childishness, which by no means sufficeth to aspire to greater things, which loveth not, or rather knoweth not how to love save carnally; which by an ill-advised love hindereth and darkeneth them that love, and by a hurtful dullness maketh them, being darkened, conformable to him that darkeneth. Such lovers [0408C] their own love advanceth not unto the light but removeth from the light; delight maketh not serene, but rather a hard clamour estrangeth them from sweet rest; the deceitful show of love shaketh their hearts and striketh blindness into them, and, oppressive as it is, by a most evil advancement, at the last step, bringeth in death. To such a certain heathen: *What hath undone you,* saith he, *I will tell: ye know not how to love.*
These men, made drunk not with wine, or perchance with the wine of dragons and of asps, are far removed from the saving cup of wisdom; which cup she reacheth forth to her hearers with no niggard hand: them that have drunk she refresheth, and them that are refreshed she inebriateth with a right excellent sweetness. This wine of wisdom, the cup of grace and of virtue, whereby he that hath drunk obtaineth the efficacy of life and of salvation, the Virgin who beareth God drinketh more fully; and drinking, is more sweetly gladdened, who in an unvaluable manner, remaining a Virgin, [0408D] is made fruitful. She holdeth in a chaste bosom the son begotten of God and of herself, whom she conceived in a Virginal womb, whose Bridegroom's Bride she is by a deep counsel, by a nuptial mystery; she gathereth him to her with a spiritual affection, and as she loveth the son with a Bridal love, so also the Bridegroom with a filial. In such nuptials the waterpot is not drained, the royal cup knoweth no water, the prince in the banqueting-room standeth not amazed as ignorant of the mystery; no man complaineth for the fear or the presence of poverty, the Bridegroom is not charged with an unlearned or a preposterous bounty. For he who goeth forth from the height of heaven, from the hidden bosom of the Father, who entereth the nuptial bridal chamber shut with the sign of soundness unbroken, and thence, the mystery of the nuptials being celebrated, goeth forth as Bridegroom: so great he [sic: *attunt*] with himself; so great a fullness he willed to be present at the nuptials, that [0409A] it is agreed that nothing honest and nothing necessary is wanting to them. Because therefore nuptials ought not to be celebrated without wine, and chiefly such as are made not with regard to the flesh alone but to the divine, in these nuptials wine faileth not but aboundeth, which not only refresheth but maketh fruitful the very celebrators of the nuptials. Spiritual love indeed, which it is agreed doth seethe more strongly than wine, is found with so great a fervour of spirit to make the Virgin drunken, that, forgetting the waters by whose flood the cleanness of chastity is in danger of being worn away, with an unwearied desire she passeth wholly into the love of truth. But Truth is Christ; and Christ is God and man. The Virgin therefore loveth God Christ with a love beyond doubt not carnal; but, because God is a Spirit, she loveth him with a spiritual love: God, I say, a spirit, she loveth with a vehement spirit, that strongly and effectually weareth away whatsoever the [0409B] flesh savoureth amiss. For God, a Spirit who willeth not to be visibly seen, rejoiceth in likeness *236* and willeth to be held by an invisible glue; and he is chiefly delighted with that love, as with an ancestral unmixed wine, wherewith he that loveth him embraceth him spiritually. Such a love he conferred of old upon the angelic spirits; and if he who willed to become an enemy out of a friend had abidden therein, man would not have been deceived, nor would he grieve to have incurred the desert of death, which by that one's envy is read to have entered into the whole world [n: (Rom. V)]. But because that love was found to be lessened in the angels, God willeth it to be restored, not in them, but in men; and both before the law and under the law he requireth to be spiritually loved by the patriarchs and the prophets; and, the days of grace being risen, he acknowledgeth this love more expressly in the Virgin [0409C] mother. Perceiving that it aboundeth more fully in her by the grace of his own giving, he willed to accept it after the manner of wine, the more joyfully as it was the sweeter; and with an eager taste he drank the draught offered by the Virgin, and in the hiding-place of his good pleasure he shut it up as firmly as irrevocably. Rightly therefore doth the Bridegroom affirm that he hath drunk his wine, that is, that he hath received with a grateful draught the divine love which he conferred upon the Virgin; the love wherewith she willed to embrace God Christ with her whole heart, her whole soul, all her strength — and the same Christ is believed to accept it, and, having accepted it, to be delighted therein. But because the Word disposed to be born flesh, God man, of the Virgin, the Virgin loving God set herself as Mother to love him as man also; knowing that the commandment of the law is not profitable enough, not fruitful, [0409D] if to the first and greatest there be not added the like or the second. She loveth therefore her neighbour, satisfying the law or Holy Scripture; she loveth Christ as man, mindful of the unspeakable begetting; she persecuteth not her own flesh in her son with a stepmother's hate, but embraceth it with a mother's affection, and loveth it by a natural right. For neither is her hardness the hardness of stones, nor can her mind be called brazen [n: (Job VI)]; but the Mother is found so given over to a tender love, that, marking Christ made man out of herself, she loveth him as herself, or, if it may be said, more than herself. But this love, this affection or care of a mother, is figured under the name of milk, which the mother by nature bestoweth upon him she hath borne; because a mother holdeth or hideth her son in her bosom or her lap, and with tender love and solicitous affection poureth in the milk. Whose [0410A] milky love, flourishing with a natural sweetness, derogateth not from the divine, prejudiceth not the wine, is not always an adversary to the spiritual; but deserveth to find no mean grace with God, if it love not to bear the first rule alone, but to serve under in the coupling. Lastly the bringer of the evangelical law, saying: *He that loveth son or daughter more than me, is not worthy of me [n: (Matth. X)]*, condemned not the privilege of nature, but prudently ordered the coupling of the two affections, that the love of God, being set before, should lack manner or measure, but that natural love should hold the second place in the joining. For although some men, who are not yet fit either to understand or to love the spiritual, take in the natural the sooner as they take it the more easily, yet it is agreed that the love which is God's is the first and greatest, which willeth the second [0410B] to be present next to itself as in a joining. And the second indeed is sometimes found in some without the first, in whom it is forestalled either by ignorance or by a degenerate negligence; but the first, which flourishing in perfection knoweth not to envy, bringing in the second, willeth ever to see it with itself in the coupling. In that therefore Paul first insinuateth as it were the second, when he is constant in preaching to the Corinthians none but Christ crucified, he spareth their weakness, he consulteth their advancement, he furthereth faith; that they who could not yet take in Christ's Godhead might at the least both understand and love his charity toward themselves; and that they might, by loving at least, render a return to that so great goodness of him whom they knew to suffer freely for themselves, nay for all. *I judged not,* saith he, *myself to know anything among you, but Jesus Christ, and him crucified* [0410C] [n: (I Cor. II)]. And a little below: *As unto little ones,* saith he, *I gave you milk to drink, not meat [n: (I Cor. III)]*. But he who thus cherisheth and advanceth the imperfect with a milky feeding, knowing what he bestoweth upon whom, speaketh wisdom unto the perfect; and preacheth Christ the Son of God to be eternal God, born before the ages, begotten and coequal to him that begetteth; and so giveth milk to the little ones and wine to the greater to drink, wherein he noteth divers advancements, or degrees of advancement; having to teach both those weaker ones by whom natural love is the more easily taken in, and those greater ones to whom the spiritual is now worthily fitting. But the Virgin, from the time she was made a mother after a new kind, was borne up by the grace of the divine gift to so great a height of perfection, that she loveth Christ as truly God with the highest [0410D] desire and by a just right, and to the same as man imparteth likewise the due right of nature. She loveth in the Son the excellence of the Godhead with a pure mind; she loveth also her own flesh tenderly and sweetly by nature; to the loving of the one she is caught up and borne aloft with a stronger heat, to the other she as it were striveth back and is brought down again with a softer step. To the one she raiseth herself up more vehemently, drunken; to the other she boweth herself more gently, sober: to the one she serveth the wine of fortitude, to the other the milk of tenderness; a twofold love flourisheth in the Virgin, and the one disparageth not the other, and Christ taketh up and accepteth the concordant coupling of both. *Come,* saith Isaias, *buy without money: and without any exchange, wine and milk [n: (Isa. LV)]*. The prophet, knowing that Christ is not appeased with a solitary love, but that the giant of a twin substance is delighted with a twinned gift, willed by a prudent counsel to instruct us to buy [0411A] wine and milk, signifying that wine befitteth God, milk man, nay rather that wine and milk befit Christ, God and man. But since this wine and milk ought to be bought according to the prophet's teaching, *237* what meaneth it that to this buying no exchange is admitted, no money, save that that twofold love, wherewith Christ, God and man, willeth to be loved alike, can be purchased neither with gold nor with silver nor with any earthly price? For not by a heap of moneys, but by poverty of spirit, is that kingdom bought and gotten, in which everyone who soundly loveth Christ abideth with him for ever; whose love also in the present nonetheless knoweth no cheap price, nor willeth to be held by him that swelleth with an abundance of temporal things. Rightly therefore doth Isaias call them forth to this [0411B] buying, who in buying put forward no mention of money, but who rather offer a slender spirit, the purity of an inmost mind, continence of the flesh, and nakedness, the soft linen being left behind. Fortified with this price I believe the Virgin entered into the nuptial market, and mingled alike for the Bridegroom the wine and milk that she had found and bought, that she might serve Christ, God and man, with a twin cup, knowing that he would not thirst for the one without the other. Having drunk thereof and been refreshed, he turneth himself again to his companions, and carefully inviteth them to the nuptial feast, that they who serve as attendants in the bridal chamber that is privy to his secrets may be accounted guests also and partakers of the rich table.
