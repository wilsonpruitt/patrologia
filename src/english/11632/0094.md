## CHAPTER IV.

[0411C] *Eat, O friends, drink and be inebriated, most dearly beloved.* He calleth the angels friends, who stood fast in his friendship when Lucifer and many that agreed with him ceased to love: who stood by him as he celebrated the nuptials, they that loved not being driven far off, and who with a worthy ministry pressed the more familiarly to know the hidden mysteries. These, that stand by and press on, that they may take the fitting advancements of knowledge, the Bridegroom suffereth not to be needy, not to be fasting; but with the storeroom opened and the bounty of his gifts displayed, he calleth them as it were to a table *of fat things full of marrow, and of wine purified from the lees [n: (Isa. XXV)]*. *Eat,* saith he, *O friends.* You, saith he, whom I willed to be present as companions and friends at the nuptials, and, they that love not being removed, to be at hand for the kisses of them that love: at my table with me [0411D] eat the things that are set before you — that is, understand the things which, being understood, shall deserve to please. Understand what of grace or of savour the solemnity of the nuptials, what the depth of the mysteries hath; what of glory or of honour the Virgin's fruitfulness, what the mother's integrity hath: understand what of sacrament is contained in *The Word was made flesh [n: (Joan. I)]*, what of help things divine bring to things human, things heavenly to things earthly. But this understanding is rightly named an eating; and when an angel, or any other of this sort, is invited to eat, the divine word purposeth not to drive away a bodily want, but signifieth that a spiritual understanding is to be sought. Lastly, when the hand of the Lord was sent to Ezechiel in a vision, and in it a book was brought sealed with a seal: *Son,* saith he, *of man, eat this volume, and going [0412A] speak to the children of Israel [n: (Ezech. III)]*. Because it profiteth nothing, or little, that the hand sent brought a sealed book, if it lie hid from the prophet what that same book containeth written out, he is of necessity admonished to eat the volume, that is, that not with the tooth but with the mind he may take what is contained in the volume. For he that eateth, not content with the mere show or comeliness of the food, burneth the more and desireth to be refreshed with the taste or the savour; and whosoever is rightly wise is fed, not with the tinkling sound of Holy Scripture, nor with a feeling, but rather with an understanding worthy unto profit. And because he cannot be fed therewith by the powers of his own will alone, but by being endowed with grace and by the benefit of the divine bounty, the prophet, after he had heard the voice or the commandment of the Lord, sufficeth not yet to himself, but declareth that he was fed by him. *And I opened,* saith he, *my mouth, and he fed me with that [0412B] volume [n: (ibid.)]*. To him that called me forth to eat I strove to open my mouth; to him that admonished me to understand I applied a zeal to obey; and he, having already come before me with the admonitions he offered, fed me, intent as I was, with the volume spread open — that is, endowed me with a large knowledge and a lettered understanding. *He spread it open,* saith he, *before me: and said, eat this volume [n: (ibid.)]*. The book indeed which the hand sent brought me rolled up, by no zeal of mine, by no powers of mine did I render unrolled; but she that brought it took away the veil that hung over it or was spread upon it, and made that which was shut up in the volume evident and spread open. For although it be mine to obey him that admonisheth, to give as it were consenting hands unto advancement, yet it is of his grace to give to me, or to any man, a nimble understanding: it is mine to open my mouth, that I be [0412C] not altogether worthy to be driven from the feeding; it is his to fill it, that is, to give it to be prudently understood. *Open wide,* saith he, *thy mouth, and I will fill it up [n: (Psal. LXXX)]*. And elsewhere: *Give me understanding, that I may learn thy commandments [n: (Psal. CXVIII.)]* And he: *Understanding,* saith he, *I will give thee, and I will instruct thee [n: (ibid.)]*. Speaking therefore to the companions whom the Bridegroom willed to be present at his mysterial secrets, he willed not that they should be as rustics fasting among the nuptials, but, a table being set forth of a deep greatness and an exquisite fullness, he set his friends far from the hunger of the unskilled multitude. *Eat,* saith he, *O friends* — that is, understand the marvellous mystery, the unspeakable sacrament, which to the rest, that are not friends, openeth the veil of ignorance: but to you *238* that minister and [0412D] stand by me I have given to know those things whereto nature attaineth not, and whereunto my grace alone supplieth the power. For neither, as some say, did the angels from the beginning naturally obtain to know the sacraments which remained more fully hidden in God; which, when the fullness of time came, that God the Son of God willed to celebrate the nuptials, he willed to reveal to them first and more familiarly. Whence the Apostle: *What is,* saith he, *the dispensation of the sacrament hidden from ages in God, that the manifold wisdom of God may be made known to the princes and powers in the heavenly places by the Church [n: (Ephes. III)]*. [var: Vulg. Eph. 3:10 *principatibus et potestatibus*, "to the Principalities and the Powers" — the angelic order; Migne prints *principibus*, "to the princes"] The wisdom of God, remaining uniform in itself but working outwardly many and divers things, deserved to be called manifold, which is made known as it were more manifoldly to the princes and to the angelic powers, when the secret mystery of the Word to be incarnate shineth out more clearly [0413A] upon them. But because to neither angel nor man doth understanding suffice enough unto merit, if a sweeter affection of loving apply not itself to the understanding, rightly after the eating are the friends also freely admonished, that both a learned making and a perfect refreshing may work a vital sufficiency. *Drink,* saith he, *and be inebriated, most dearly beloved.* If, by me, saith he. giving, ye take in the hidden sacraments by understanding, being not yet content therewith, be ye careful for advancement; and that ye may seem both to profit enough and as it were to perfect what ye have begun, eating, drink — that is, love what ye have perceived by understanding. For it profiteth nothing, or little, to understand what things have been great, what perfect, if we love not with a mightier grace the things understood: and as the people that understandeth not, by the testimony of Osee [n: (Osee. IV)], shall be beaten, so [0413B] the wicked servant, not loving the things understood, shall condemn himself as guilty of negligence. Rightly therefore are the companions or friends admonished not only to understand but also to love, that when they have joyfully perceived deep things they may marvel at a most sweet sweetness; and that they who a while since had as it were grieved over the wretched fall of the sinner may now for joy stand amazed at so marvellous a counsel of the Redeemer. Whose height, when by understanding and by loving they lay hold of it as it were with hands, being made drunken with amazement and joy they suspend themselves into so great an ecstasy as it were, that, forgetting the wounds which the serpent's fraud once brought upon men, they now think of nothing but the physician or the medicine at hand. They eat, when they understand that a life-giving mystery, that saving nuptials are celebrated; they drink, when they love that man is reformed unto his former, nay unto an ampler [0413C] grace; they are inebriated, when they stand amazed and cleave fast, and for joy suffice not to marvel that manhood, taken up into God, deserveth to be exalted above the angels. Them that were privy to this secret the bridegroom in speaking named friends, but them that were drunken with love and joy he heaped with the name of most dearly beloved: signifying both that they are pleasing to him who apply a spiritual understanding to the knowing of subtle things, and that the same are most pleasing when they come and attain unto the advancement of loving. These being refreshed with food and drunken with a more gracious draught, the bride, reclining her head in a sweet conscience or love, forgetting things visible, suspendeth herself among things invisible; and what she tasteth, what she perceiveth, she grudgeth not to us, but showeth.
