## CHAPTER V.

[0413D] *I sleep, and my heart watcheth.* When the companions and friends that stand by a work so divine give heed and marvel, made drunken with the amazement of a deep greatness and not with wine: the bride reclineth herself within the chamber in a softer reclining, to whom the bridegroom now serveth the desirable cup of a marvellous savour. The friends watching, and striving as it were to lay hold, and standing amazed at the manner or the measure of a thing so deep: the Virgin, judging whatsoever applaudeth not in deep silence to be a market and a thing of the market-place, resteth in the nuptial bridal chamber and closeth her eyes even for a little with [sic: *sommo*]. For the business, or the solicitude of a clamorous business, being driven away, the noise being shut out abroad, the door of a pure mind being closed as it were: amid the bridegroom's embraces she falleth asleep the more eagerly as it is the sweeter, and, failing into [0414A] him, and by failing advancing, she desireth things insensible alone. Abiding in the body, a stranger to the things that are of the body, forgetting her cares, her serene mind is caught up into ecstasy: borne up by a calm rapture, she rejoiceth to forget whatsoever is beneath, sleeping and resting in the things which she chiefly loveth to obtain. But lest her sleeping should seem to strike in negligence or torpor, and to afford confidence to the ambushes of the enemy, to open a looser entrance: the virgin setteth a watchman who may keep the watches unwearied, who may foresee from afar whatsoever maketh an unlawful noise. For while she sleepeth, and is loosened into the tender embraces of the bridegroom, and hath forgotten the things behind and is loosed from the cares of temporal things, her own heart watcheth, driving far from sleep the clouds of phantasms, the empty phantasies, the heavy noises of the world, [0414B] the seducing melodies of sirens. Her own heart watcheth, acting with a circumspect solicitude lest a mass of forensic acts cast upon her should trouble the sweetness of sleep that she hath found; lest any molestation should enter, even secretly, the secret of her chamber, which, being entered, might rage and violently shake off her sleep. Lastly her own heart watcheth, that when with a careful hand it hath driven away every adversary, it may hearken and hear the bridegroom whispering with a slender murmur: and, the door being as it were shut against whosoever maketh a harder noise, it may lend an ear to the bridegroom that more sweetly and more subtly *239* speaketh. For the more closely she loveth to be joined to him, the more eagerly to cleave to him, so much the more gratefully doth she will to hear, and for the bridegroom's voice to rejoice with joy: and giving her ear to the hearing, she counteth herself the more grievously hurt if for his voice she refuse not whatsoever else hath sounded. For he, [0414C] cherishing her sleep and favouring the desire of her that sleepeth, when he shall so have given her milk, speaketh to the heart of her that loveth: and to her, as to her leisure so to her business, he setteth what bound he will, as he is read of old to have promised by the prophet: *I will give her milk,* saith he, *and I will speak to her heart [n: (Osee. II)]*. I will give her milk, saith he, when she shall lay herself down in my bosom with her head bowed, and shall put nothing between with mutual embraces; when, greedy of silence, she shall be delighted to fall asleep under my shadow, and, thirsting, to draw in the sweetness she hath found, and, drawing it in, to thirst. But I will speak to her heart, when I shall admonish her to awake, when I shall restrain her from her own profits out of regard for her neighbours, that, the draught of the foretasted sweetness being laid aside for a little, she may go forth to work abroad, watching [0414D] with brotherly charity. The bridegroom therefore giveth the Virgin milk, when he leadeth her in to the secrets of his breast: he speaketh to her heart, when he calleth and bringeth the same back watchful unto us; when, the sweetness of sleep being tasted, wherein she loveth to forget temporal things, he biddeth her awake unto the love and the care of her neighbours. And she, while she sleepeth and tasteth what sweetness there is in resting, and her heart watcheth with a solicitude of serving more diligently: lo, of a sudden a certain interruption of the sweetness now perceived, a hand as it were knocketh at the door, the voice of her beloved shaketh her ear. By which shaking the Virgin, perceiving herself challenged (for she knoweth the voice again, because she hath now heard it often), seemeth to answer to herself alone, or to the maidens standing by her, and to recount with a mindful word both whose and what voice she had heard.

## CHAPTER VI.

[0415A]

*The voice of my beloved knocking.* When in my chosen reclining, saith she, I am held by the gift of sleep, and with a heart watchful enough am nonetheless intent upon the Bridegroom's good pleasure: out of a hidden place a certain voice, as I perceive, breaketh forth as it were into the open, which the more carefully interrupteth my advancement as I sleep, lest it come, as I will, unto perfection. Whose importunity beateth the more harshly upon my ears, and driveth me back from that which I was burning more firmly to lay hold of: and a repulse so strong, that I may confess the truth, I should bear ill, if I did not worthily set his good pleasure before my own will. For I know that it pleaseth his will not a little, that the door of a holy mind be found not to stand open to strangers; that whosoever shall have passed by through the by-ways of worldly [0415B] pandering may find it shut, and not lying open after the fashion of a brothel. The bridegroom himself also, when he willeth to come to that same door, loveth to find it shut rather than open, and to prove its seal so tenacious, so indissoluble, that it should altogether never know how to be opened save when he himself hath knocked. For a holy mind, restraining itself within a spiritual workshop, ought so to embrace the zeal of contemplating, so the discipline of leisure or of silence, that it open not its cloister to itself, that it break not forth to the office of preaching, unless it have been knocked upon and have drunk in the voice of him that commandeth. Therefore the Virgin sleeping, and soothed with a delightful sleep, rejoicing to be shut in by her cloister as one entangled in softer meshes: hasteneth to open not only not to any man, but not even to the bridegroom, until at last, being knocked upon, she is commanded [0415C] and by the commandment is compelled to obey. Who indeed requireth not the commander to command, but the more quickly knoweth again the voice, even a slender voice, of him that knocketh: and as she heareth not, nor giveth ear, to open at any admonitions of a deceiver, so she stoppeth not her ear when the voice of the commander soundeth. And it soundeth and knocketh at the door, or the leisure, of her that resteth, when by the benefit of one that teacheth he willeth to counsel them that are without; when, pitying the blindness and the neediness of the orphan that is ignorant, he willeth it to be wiped away by a virginal labour, and the merit of her that laboureth to be increased. But what that voice thundereth above, or what it saith, what it commandeth or showeth, soothing the bride as she sleepeth, the bridegroom himself willed not only that the bride should know it more secretly or more sacredly, but willed also that it should come, even slenderly, unto them that are without.
