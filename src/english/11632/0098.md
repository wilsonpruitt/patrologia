## CHAPTER XI.

*My hands dropped down myrrh, and my fingers are full of the most approved myrrh.* When the Virgin was rising, and [0421D] as it were going on to open, hoping that Christ would enter in the more fully when the gate was opened: in the going she felt that the gate itself would not easily lie open, unless she took care to apply strong hands to the lifting of the bolt. For the gate was not opened straightway, as she declareth herself to have risen, nor doth she assert that it lay open straightway when she rose; because, although she confesseth beyond doubt that she rose in order to open, yet the efficacy of opening is not straightway afforded. For she saith not: I arose and opened, but she saith: *I arose to open.* But because a certain difficulty perchance set itself against her as she rose, the Virgin advisedly applied her hands to the removing of it — hands, I say, not torpid with leisure, not dry, not deceitful, but strong with a nimble virtue, effectual to fulfil worthily what was purposed. *Hands,* saith she, *mine dropped down myrrh.* [0422A] Hands are actions; but by myrrh she seemeth to name the strictness of judgment, because whatsoever the Virgin did she willed to judge the more strictly, knowing that he deservedly feareth not the judgment to come who by a humble will hath chosen to bear the present one. The hands therefore of the virgin dropped the bitterness of myrrh, when all her acts held the rule of discipline, when whatsoever she took care to work in this laborious flesh she restrained the more diligently, lest it should presume to wander the more negligently. For myrrh is said to strengthen soft and flowing flesh, and to preserve it lest by a natural law it lie subject to rottenness; and the works of our hands, lest perchance they come to a natural failing, a regular strictness maketh firm and leadeth unto perfection. And because it sufficeth not for the virgin if by living strictly [0422B] she advance much in virtue, unless she both instruct the rest and draw them along with her unto salvation: she saith that her hands do not hold or have myrrh, but rather drop it down, that she may show the advance of her works to be derived unto them that are without. Ah, for to this she riseth, that her instruction stick not fast nor waver anywhere, and that whatsoever she knoweth profitable for the rest she hold not shut up but drop down, that she may profit them that stand by [sic: *assitentibus*] her not by the desire of her will alone, but by a form of justice shown forth and by the example of a strict holiness. To the opening therefore the anxious virgin stretcheth forth her hands; to the instructing of her neighbours she showeth beforehand a dropping of myrrh, holding it settled that then she instructeth the more effectually, when the strictness of manners hath hastened to go before the instruction of words. But because a severe strictness of living is not held sufficient to the full [0422C] if in it a discreet moderation, or a moderate discretion, be not held: after the hands dropping down the unction of a commendable myrrh, she consequently applieth herself to make mention of fingers. *Fingers,* saith she, *mine are full of the most approved myrrh.* Fingers, although beneath they cleave to one another as by one root or joining, yet come forth outwardly discrete with an unequal measure or proportion; and their discretion is fashioned by so prudent a craft of nature that, serving the hand, it is held as profitable as it is fitting. By the fingers therefore is rightly understood the discretive virtue, which, if it be joined undivided as a companion to our works, then whatsoever in them she affecteth not the more strictly, paineth the more severely or straiteneth, *244* that same discretion by her fellowship rendereth the more perfect and adorneth. Which doubtless, [0422D] although now more briefly, now more profusely she extendeth herself, according as she seeth through and attendeth what is expedient for the business set before her, yet, as she striveth not to pass beyond the due bounds, so she findeth it indiscreet and, injurious to stand short of them. Whence she herself, who is not denied to be called one and the same discretion, is applied so manifoldly to many, and to divers businesses, that it is not out of tune if she be figured not so much by the name of a finger as of fingers, in that she worketh very many things, dividing to each as she will. But willing to commend fingers of this sort, the most prudent Virgin asserteth that they abound with the most approved myrrh; because as strictness, being indiscreet, profiteth nothing or little, so discretion shineth not forth unless it be accustomed to hard burdens. But discretion shineth and is adorned with a rosy comeliness when the strictness joined to it is not straitened [0423A] by a niggard boundary, when myrrh is poured into the fingers not from an empty horn but rather from a full one, and the fulness thereof is worthily approvable, not confounded. But that may fittingly be called approvable or approved whereto the conversation of us that labour striveth so to be given over, that whatsoever miry thing the interruption of the present workshop draweth to itself, the severity of discipline brought over it may diligently boil away. But the Virgin named the myrrh wherewith she intimated her fingers to be full not approved, but rather, most approved: intimating perchance that the myrrh which is approved may be given with grace to the rest that labour, but that the most approved is truly, is specially, assigned to herself. Approved is that which chastiseth the restless body and subjecteth the chastised body to servitude, lest the law engrafted in the members withstand life and salvation utterly; which represseth the disordered motions [0423B] and the enkindling passions of the body as they sprout, and suffereth them not to be made oppressive. But most approved is that which utterly denieth entrance to all vices, which so banisheth the craftiness of enemies with the door of the mind barred, that not only doth she not permit a conceived wickedness to be led unto effect, but by a stronger advance doth not now even loosen the gate unto its conception. It is most approved when not only the hand committeth not an unlawful work, but the mind, far purer, admitteth nothing reprehensible; when not only the stronger spirit consenteth not to the law of the flesh, but both flesh and spirit have now felt nothing worthy of pardon. Which sort of myrrh, although it seem to be far from our borders, yet it is certain that it is not wanting to this so great virgin, but that by an excellent gift it is so at hand to her fingers that [0423C] she asserteth them to be not needy but rather full. For she it is whose discretion the more fully embraceth the bitterness of strictness, whose strictness is governed by a discreet moderation; who, being mighty in both, both rejoiceth with the doors shut to find the pleasing sweetness of silence, and utterly refuseth not, when she is touched by the hand of the spouse, to open. Whence she subjoineth: *The bolt of the door,* etc.
