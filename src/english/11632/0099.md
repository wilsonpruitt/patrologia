## CHAPTER XII.

*The bolt of my door I opened to my beloved: but he had turned aside, and was gone by.* After the Virgin had shown her hands and her fingers commendable with myrrh, and had prepared herself for the opening by a discreet exercise, she unbarred the door that was shut before, the bolt being removed, and what she had as it were hidden within she [0423D] declared at the command of him that knocked. For she who before rejoiced in a sweet silence now, the spouse knocking at the gate, judgeth it worthy that she speak, feeling that she pleaseth him none the less in speaking, whose grace she had rejoiced to obtain in silence. To him therefore whom she loveth she consenteth to open the door — that is, she looseth her silence and setteth about to instruct her neighbours: in the new and the raw laying a foundation of faith, in the advanced and the great heaping up a firmament of a loftier justice. Not for herself indeed but for her beloved doth she open the door and unbar the fastening; not for herself but for him that knocketh doth she loose the doors and set forth an opening, because when she layeth out the Lord's talent to the rest for usury, she taketh care not for her own glory or name but for his. For the charity wherewith the mother embraceth the spouse and the Virgin the son seeketh not the things that are her own, nor [0424A] catcheth after anything of her own, but whether she seem to give her pains to the leisure of contemplating or to the business of preaching, she serveth with a devout and whole spirit him alone whom she loveth. Who, although she affecteth leisure more than business, yet, cleaving to that, she utterly refuseth not this; but in business, when she now applieth herself to it, it more evidently becometh known by what cause she is held when she the more diligently cleaveth to leisure. For leisure indeed is not held worthy of the greatest praise if the sweetness of it be not held by a commendable cause; nor doth that highest judge will that leisure be held out to him as a merit, but that a commendable cause be shown beforehand in the face of it. Which the holy Virgin understanding, confesseth that she opened not the door but the bolt of the door, that not the bolt may be commended by the door but the door by the bolt; because that invisible [0424B] and most keen searcher of things invisible is a lover not so much of the study that proceedeth as of the cause that goeth before. *The bolt,* saith she, *of my door I opened to my beloved.* According to the letter, when any man is found knocking at the gate, it is not the bolt, as you know, but rather the door that is opened; and he indeed thrusteth back the bolt *245* who willeth to afford entrance to him that knocketh, which he had thrust in when he willed to keep the door shut. But here not the door but the door's bolt is said to be opened, wherein not the letter but a spiritual understanding willeth to be sought; because as the bridal chamber is not to be built of wood or of stone, so neither door nor bolt admitteth the grossness of a literal understanding. By the name of the door therefore silence is not unfitly figured, under the covering whereof the Virgin lieth hid and rejoiceth in her hiding-place; [0424C] but the bolt which is set to the door lest it be turned is the love of the Bridegroom, for whose sake the Virgin embraceth silence. Outward things indeed being put behind her, when the Virgin lieth hid shut up under silence, the love of the Bridegroom is in the cause, stronger than any bar; and, the cause and the effect running together in her, the Virgin, the home-born handmaid of quiet, rejoiceth unspeakably in her advancement. But if the beloved knock and ask the Bride to open, assuredly he willeth to find his own love in that very silence; and he proveth that love to be the cause of the silence she hath taken up, if for his sake for whom she rejoiceth in silence the Virgin endureth to be present at outward things also. Otherwise, if, the beloved knocking, the Virgin withstand with a stubborn mind, there is no doubt that she is instant upon silence with a desire that is not truthful; because she that truly loveth, not knowing how not to hear him whom [0424D] she loveth, rejoiceth to open her love to him whom she loveth, that she may be loved the more. The Bridegroom therefore knocking, the Bride taketh not away but bringeth forth the bolt evidently — that is, she diligently openeth the cause wherefore she rejoiceth in leisure — while for the Bridegroom's love she layeth aside and banisheth leisure, and for him for whose sake she was at leisure she now refuseth not to be instant upon business also. For whose sake, accustomed to the titles of victory, she willed to be shut in within; for his sake, now going forth without, she becometh an athlete again, showing in business by what cause she was held in leisure, singing that prophetic word which is briefly contained in the verse of the Psalm: *If I have remembered thee upon my bed, in the mornings I will meditate on thee [n: (Psal. LXII)]*. He calleth the bed that quiet wherein she is delighted in the chamber, when, the door being as it were shut, she is sequestered from the tumult of outward things; [0425A] but those morning things which return after the quiet silence of the night may rightly enough be called the action and the doctrine of temporal things. He therefore who for Christ's sake truly lieth hid under a pleasing silence, for the same Christ's sake acquiesceth also to give his pains to outward things; and he showeth what memory or what love of him he holdeth upon his bed when he shall have been called in the morning to labour. So also the Virgin, when, morning being as it were made, she receiveth commandment to speak, openeth to the Bridegroom the bolt wherewith her door was made fast, and showeth with what love, with what spirit she was held under silence, while she refuseth not to obey when she is asked to go forth into public. She openeth, I say, that to him who had knocked and had first moved her to open she may make satisfaction by answering, and may rejoice to find him without; that him whom in silence she could not yet sufficiently overcome, being called into [0425B] public, she may avail to ensnare by the merits of obeying. *But he had turned aside and was gone by.* The beloved knocketh at the Bride's door and asketh that it be opened to him, and, the bolt being opened, he cannot be found; and in a wondrous manner he inclineth himself to her presence, and when he is now thought to be as it were held, fleeing back to his own he straightway turneth aside. For the excellence of his nature is so incomprehensible that, when he hath poured himself with a large benefit into a mortal creature, he as it were offereth himself wholly and conferreth upon her that loveth a hope of holding him; and, her desire being stretched out, his immensity cannot be comprehended. He kindleth and stretcheth out the sweet desire of her that burneth; he provoketh her that burneth, he promiseth the presence of him that provoketh; and when he is now thought present amid the veins of her that more tenderly embraceth him, he fleeth her greedy hands and is withdrawn from the throat that gapeth for him. He fleeth, I say, [0425C] the Virgin, pressed not by a blameworthy offence but by corruptible flesh; and he turneth away from her eyes a face not granted her, and, not taking the thing away but rather deferring it, he rendereth her hope untired, maketh firm her faith, dwelling in light inaccessible. Who, turning away from his mother's gaze the face she most desireth, passeth to the bosom of the Father and to the light revealed to no mortal; whose immortality, although it will to be loved by mortals, yet willeth to be presented to none of them unto sufficiency. For although he will to be at hand for the helping of some and others abundantly, and more abundantly, yet he willeth always that something be wanting to all, so long as either guilt tendeth toward its remedy, or the weaker disease toward its medicine, or this corruptible thing busieth itself to lay aside its natural corruption. For not only [0425D] guilt but corruption also darkeneth us and conformeth us to the world wherein we live; and lest we fly forth from it more freely, it bindeth us with a knot not easily to be unravelled, by which thong our eye is not indeed utterly overwhelmed, but is dimmed. Seeing therefore his beloved still subject to this nature, and held by the wrapping of a flesh that is not of vice but of nature, he presenteth himself to her the more frequently in what manner or order he will, but his immortal excellence quickly absenteth itself from a mortal. For the immortal, although he promise that he may in some sort be known and felt, yet admitteth not a mortal eye to see him more fully; but when he shall have come and shall have asked the Virgin to open, he straightway turneth aside and hasteneth to pass over to things invisible. He cometh indeed, when he poureth himself the more gratefully and sweetly into a pure mind; he passeth by, when [0426A] he hideth himself from the same lest he be seen face to face: he cometh, when he leadeth her into a true and sincere hope of the best vision; he passeth by, when he withdraweth himself from a desire that still hopeth and hasteneth. He cometh, I say, when he giveth the Virgin to thirst for the clear fountain of good; he passeth by, when to her that thirsteth he giveth not to find more fully *246* that which she seeketh. He cometh, when he granteth the Virgin's faith to attain to the highest merit; he passeth by, when he suffereth not yet that faith pass over into the thing, and hope into sight. He cometh, lastly, when by commanding or by knocking he carrieth the Virgin over unto the advancement of obedience; he passeth by, when she, not arriving, is as it were dissolved into a certain failing: he cometh, when the Virgin stretcheth herself forth with a prosperous course unto the things before her; he passeth by, when she, running, hath not yet laid hold of the prize. From whom withdrawing her hand [0426B] rather than her mind, she addeth: *My soul,* etc.
