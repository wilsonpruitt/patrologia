## CHAPTER XIII.

*My soul melted as the beloved spoke. I sought him, and found him not; I called, and he answered me not.* The Virgin marvelleth, and beyond what can be told is astonished, and within herself pineth away with an exceeding stupor, while she the more diligently acquiesceth in the commands or the admonitions of him whom she tenderly loveth, and yet as yet groweth strong by no powers, by no merits, unto the obtaining of him. He indeed speaketh and admonisheth the Virgin to open; she riseth and hasteneth the more diligently to obey him that speaketh; nay, the word being heard, she is so brought down into an affectionate tenderness that she seemeth to herself melted as by a mighty burning. That Spirit being present who vehemently loveth to stand by, [0426C] the word of the Bridegroom, mightily glowing as fire in a furnace, parcheth the whole Virgin, and putteth every dross far from her, and melteth her into his own love as a fusible material. Being melted, she seemeth to retain nothing of her own, nothing proper to herself, but to offer herself apt and tractable unto every form whatsoever: so that whether she be suffered to lie hid within and to be present at spiritual things, or be bidden to go forth without, to speak to her neighbours and to profit them, her heart be not troubled, nor bring forth a complaint about the precept, but rejoice to stand by him that commandeth and to persist in what she hath begun. Since therefore she rejoiceth to be intent upon none but spiritual things, whether in leisures or in businesses, and, the things that are behind being put after her, to be stretched out only unto the things before; she as it were complaineth that he whom she seeketh with so great [0426D] study, whom she seeketh with an eye so purged, is not yet found, nor doth she come to him with unveiled face. Him, saith she, I love with my whole heart, him with my whole soul, with all my powers; I cease not to call, I join prayers to prayers, vows to vows: and being sought he straightway slippeth away, and doth not yet more fully give what he promiseth; being called, although he heareth, yet he answereth not sufficiently to my desire. What is it therefore that, when he speaketh — that is, when his sweetness becometh known to me — my soul, tasting it, now melteth wholly with a fiery love; and when I seek the more greedily, he, as though envious, absenteth himself, and answereth not her that calleth — that is, presenteth not the face that is sought? Who nevertheless, considering me set in so great a desire, suffereth me not to be destitute of all solace whatsoever; but lest, that which I asked being deferred, I should seem to myself too much circumvented, [0427A] while I seek, I am sought; while I find not, I am found.

## CHAPTER XIV.

*The watchmen who go about the city found me: they struck me, and wounded me; the keepers of the walls took away my cloak.* [var: Vulg. Cant 5:7 *Invenerunt me **custodes** qui circumeunt civitatem… tulerunt pallium meum **mihi** custodes murorum*. Migne's *vigiles* is the word the Vulgate uses at Cant 3:3 instead, and it makes the verse's two parties two different words; the exposition below turns on the distinction.] Far back the same Virgin is said to have been found by the watchmen [n: (sup. l. III, c. 22)], and the Virgin to have the more diligently accosted them that found her; who study the more anxiously to go about the city of the faithful, and, the reprobate being neglected, to impart their due care to the elect. Whereon I remember that I said in that place the things which occurred at that time — I know not whether they deserved to please or to suffice the reader; and lest perchance a repeated exposition of the same things be a burden, I pass over to expounding those things only which have lately been heard. *They struck me,* saith she, *and [0427B] wounded me.* The companions of the Bridegroom, to whom the care of the faithful is committed, who watch over their flock with an unremitted anxiety, go about Jerusalem, praising the name of the Lord upon the walls, and they cherish and promote those whom they shall have seen about to come to him. Nor doth it suffice them to visit once, but they are not grieved to visit again, and they rejoice to inspect and to perfect the affections of them that go forward; and those whom they shall have known to pass over from virtue to virtue more strongly and more freely, these they love to find, being sought the more frequently, in their purpose. They are therefore reported to find again the Virgin found before, and they follow up her fervid love with a due favour; and they whose care it is to arouse and to kindle them that love, that he may be loved the more, lift up and commend him that is loved. But while commending [0427C] the unspeakable magnificence of the Creator, they bring in upon the Virgin's mind a fuller force of love; who, while they desire to relate, and yet suffice not, how great is the excellence of him that is loved, the Virgin, strongly struck, is wounded with a sharper dart. For the praise of the beloved is a certain dart or arrow, wherewith when the mind of this Virgin *247* hath been worthily sought after, she rejoiceth not so much to be grievously pricked as to be sweetly pricked to compunction; nor is she accounted so much pierced through with a dart as soothed with a healing draught. By which dart or draught being the more sweetly dissolved into love, she seemeth to herself the more gratefully destitute of the things that are of the body; and, the sense of bodily things being unnerved wherein she was the more hardly wrapped, she loveth the more tenderly by how much the more freely she is set in mind among the heavenly things. So doubtless [0427D] the Virgin rejoiceth that her cloak is taken from her, when with a mind as it were naked she feeleth herself lifted up among the heavenly things; when she feeleth the exceeding praises of the Bridegroom preached by the watchmen, and feeleth herself stripped by the kindling of those praises from the care of bodily things. For she who is set among bodily things, either by the law of mortals or by the love of carnal men, is covered the more laboriously with a certain cloak as it were; whence it cometh to pass that if she be drawn out of it, either utterly in effect or in the affection of a devout spirit, she rejoiceth to open to the Bridegroom, and to cleave to him the more openly with an uncovered face. Whereunto when the watchmen exhort the Virgin with admonitions or with praises, they are said to wound her when she is struck and to take away her cloak; and while her mind is intent upon their presence or their converse, forgetful of her own flesh she stretcheth herself wholly into the love of the spirit. For to this end are the ministering spirits sent to men, to this end do they strive with much anxiety [0428A] when they have been sent, that they may [sic: *informen t*] any lesser ones whatsoever unto lawful things, lest they transgress, but may urge the greater ones to forsake even lawful things with a stronger desire. Who, when they find the Virgin glowing with the torches of love, lay wound upon wound and study to inflame her the more, and take from her the cloak wherewith she is naturally clad — that is, the care wherewith all mortality is found to care for itself. For so do they cry out that he who is loved is admirable, so do they preach him unapproachable, that they may snatch the virgin mind wholly into an ecstasy; that, forgetful of the body with whose heavy presence she for the most part grieveth to be wrapped about, she may reckon herself for a little while loosed from the burden of it. Nevertheless the watchmen show not themselves for long to the Virgin either, but are quickly withdrawn from her to whom they sometimes condescend; not [0428B] utterly deferring to her will, but rather with a subtle prudence to her sanctity, and betaking themselves back to the care of the rest, to the guarding of the walls. But by the name of the walls I judge the apostles to be figured, by whose anxiety the Church rejoiceth to be defended; who, raised up and carried up not, as is wont, with stone or with mortar, but with a heap of virtues and with the indissoluble firmament of charity, go about the city, strongly equipped for its guarding — if yet they have been worthily guarded by the watchmen. Whom the most devout Virgin, considering them to be carried up unto advancement, because she cannot yet fully attain unto her beloved, turneth herself to them, willing to use a brotherly solace, not envying but rejoicing together with their grace or their salvation.
