## CHAPTER XVII.

*My beloved is white and ruddy, chosen out of thousands.* My beloved, saith she, whose acquaintance you have not yet (as it seemeth) acquired, but which by a zeal of acquiring you have already carefully sought out, is conspicuous not with one splendour only, but with a twofold [0431B] splendour, whose inestimable whiteness joineth to itself a pleasing redness. Now whiteness is a light unfailing, unapproachable to them that are dim of sight, a day that knoweth no evening, not pressed down by any darkness that followeth after: a light shining white eternally, and none the less the whiteness of eternal light, enlightening angels as well as men, present alike to the abiding and to the perishing. It is, I say, the whiteness of light, a light present to all yet not shown to all, shining indeed in the darkness, and not comprehended by the darkness [n: (Joan. I)]; a whiteness brighter than the sun, a light that hath gone beyond whiteness, snow, taking on a red garment, yet suffering no stain upon the garment. For God, the Word of God, the Only-begotten of the Father, the image of the begetter, putting on a servile body, the tunic of misery [0431C] and of toil, and conversing as man among men, liveth a life so modest that to them which look worthily he offereth as it were a ruddy countenance. For taking true flesh of others, he was so made like unto men that in the eyes of the dim-sighted there remaineth to him neither comeliness nor look: and being needy and weak, and, according to Zacharias [n: (cap. III)], wearing filthy garments, he sickeneth with foulness, he waxeth pale with a miry face. But I, the mother, whose mind is not darkened, whose understanding is not dull, to whom above all others my own child-bearing hath been made known and my conceiving made clear — I know my son's flesh to be utterly alien from every vice, and that he taketh nature, and of guilt taketh upon him nothing save the penalty. He therefore, as it were a fugitive, blusheth at every vice whatsoever, and sustaining its penalty alone, he is beyond doubt not befouled but reddened: [0431D] nor is it the hurt of guilt, but rather a flight from hurt, when his cheeks are suffused with the blood-red ruddiness of the passion. Nor the cheeks only, but the whole garment of the body is red; the passion maketh the whole comely, and empurpleth it with a penal adornment: and that which to any sinner is ignominy and an intolerable loss, is to him nothing but grace and a commendable increase of glory. Lastly, fire also is red and is found of one colour with blood, whose heat guilt endureth not, if it be not prevented by mercy; and he whose wood, hay, stubble maketh trial not of light but of burning — that fire, saving gold, silver, stone, measureth back to each the like [n: (I Cor. III)]. My beloved therefore is a fire burning up [0432A] the chaff, parching the mire, bringing the reprobate to nothing, mercifully chastening the dissolute: a fire shut up in an earthen vessel, red with a judicial power, shaking round about in judgment with a red power. For God, whom as Son he willed to be substantially united to himself before the ages, him in the end of times he willed to be found in the habit of a man, whose becoming man cannot easily be known by any, save by him to whom he hath willed to impart his grace thereunto. Which grace assuredly he willeth to be sought by the desire of the studious and the study of them that desire, and to you, as I understand, who worthily seek it, he willeth to grant it; and I envy you not, since I myself seem to be prevented by that same grace, but I teach them that seek, and I rejoice that you are effectually instructed. He is therefore the brightness of glory, the figure of the substance, the image of the paternal Godhead, the whiteness of eternal [0432B] light, the mirror of the divine majesty [n: (Hebr. I)]; he is white in the white Father, sprinkled with no stain, ever clean; and enduring to have a mother, he willed to become ruddy. Shining white, I say, as the light of light, he sought withal to be red; but being red that he might take counsel for us, he ceaseth not to shine white in himself eternally: among the choirs of angels and the crowds or assemblies of men, no giant is mightier, none is found like unto him. Wherefore out of thousands the Father, the more solicitous for us, chose this one, whom, sent by his own will, no hard law or necessity constrained; but being made priest and victim, he offered himself in sacrifice by his will, laying down the soul he had taken, and by a like power taking again the soul he had laid down. And it is excellently defined by the aforesaid [0432C] number, when this finite number is put for every infinite, that out of all, angels as well as men, who can be comprehended in no number, he alone should be the chosen for the ministry of offering. For no other, whether man or angel, was able so to offer, and no man's oblation could effectually confer this; and therefore out of thousands, that is, out of the infinite number of them all, he is singularly chosen, *250* who more perfectly effecteth that for which the imperfect sufficeth not. And that he is worthy to be chosen through whom this benefit is conferred, a worthy sequence of praises is subjoined in the testimony that followeth.
