## CHAPTER VII.

*There are threescore queens and fourscore concubines, and of maidens there is no number.* The Bridegroom attendeth unto those who by the gift of grace attain unto the Catholic faith, and follow after the firmness of the same faith either in deed or in show: whose divers deserts, willing to designate them figuratively, he seemeth mystically [0450B] to assign unto them divers names. By the name of queens are they not unfitly figured who, hedged about with a kingly defence, are overcome neither by the fraud of the adversary nor by his violence; who, while they keep their ways, that they offend not in word and in work, draw nigh unto the bed of the eternal King by the profiting of spiritual things. Where both they are filled with a lofty knowledge and are gladdened with a lowly conscience, and, gladly resting in the embrace of the Bridegroom, are made fruitful by his benefit; and with the fruitful seed-plot of doctrine they beget unto him spiritual sons, by whose fertile begetting the nuptial chambers are enriched and made glad. Who are rightly said to be comprehended in the number of sixty, because it is certain that in the ten and in the six the perfection of the law and of works is commended, the one of which being multiplied by the other, the number sixty cometh [0450C] to be completed. For as six times ten, *261* so ten times six, make sixty. He therefore who rejoiceth to fulfil in works the ten precepts of the law, or who ordereth according to the form of the law whatsoever he worketh in time. he assuredly is worthily contained in the number sixty, whose fruitful dignity is said to please the highest King. It is also possible to find in this number the five and the twelve, whose multiplication seemeth to come unto sixty; because if twelve be taken five times, or five twelve times, it is doubtful to none but that they are brought unto the sum of the number sixty. And he is rightly said to take five twelve times who governeth the five senses of the body in that manner or order which the form or the institution of the apostles hath prudently ordained, whose perfection [0450D] the number twelve hath commended. *And fourscore,* saith he, *concubines.* As they who chastely love the Bridegroom and beget sons unto him are queens, so also they who love not sincerely and yet preach are concubines, whose knowledge and preaching doth indeed beget spiritual sons, but, their conscience being wounded, they themselves that beget remain animal. Who are not unfitly reported to be fourscore, because they are known to hold the ten of the law in word and in knowledge, but, lacking the twofold love, they slip back into the eight, while they embrace in life and in manners the things that are of the world. For four are the seasons of the year, four the elements of the world, four the climes of the globe, four in fine the affections wherewith any man is carnally affected and travaileth, and, if he know aught rightly, he discoloureth it by a lost will. Which [0451A] affections that prudent philosopher biddeth and exhorteth to be driven back, knowing that he erreth over whom a carnal passion beareth rule; which passion, naturally implanted, although he pluck it not up by the root, yet he erreth if he drive not the same back with a fitting chastisement. *Joys,* saith he, *drive away,*
*Drive away fear,*
*And put hope to flight,*
*Nor let grief be present.*


Whosoever, being lost in sense, is found to double the aforesaid four, that is, to strengthen the carnal affections with an affection added over and above, against the philosopher's prohibition: he assuredly, filling up the eight, is not yet granted to come unto the ten, because in the evils which he accomplisheth it is not possible to find the twofold form of love. *And [0451B] of maidens there is no number.* When queens and concubines preach and sow the spiritual word, although the desire of the former be just and of the latter carnal: a new offspring is assuredly begotten by their zeal or labour, which receiveth the Bridegroom by faith and followeth him with a readier affection. For whether they that preach the word be held just or unjust, by their word many are begotten in faith and in knowledge: and as from the good, so from the evil, a copious offspring is born, numerous indeed unto no man, but rather unto God alone. Of whom he saith: *I will number them, and they shall be multiplied above the sand [n: (Psal. CXXXVIII)]*. They who yield assent unto the teachers that preach the faith are fully known unto him who numbereth the multitude of the stars; but unto us that attend unto them gathered together of every kind of fishes in a net, [0451C] their multitude is utterly innumerable as the sand. Who, because they have already received the rudiments of the faith they have heard, but have not yet profited so far as to announce unto others the instructions of the same faith: by reason of their newness or of their imperfection are named [sic: *a dolescentulae*], and are unknown of the man whose knowledge is yet dull, whose eye is dim. Not whether they be good, who are afterward chosen into vessels, or evil, who, being sent out, are spewed forth without, so long as they are contained in the net as it were in a confused order, are they held numerable by any man.
