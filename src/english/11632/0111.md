## CHAPTER VIII.

*One is my dove, my perfect one; one is she unto her mother, the chosen of her that bore her.* [var: Vulg. *matris suae*, genitive — "she is the one of her mother"; the dative Migne prints here is repeated three times in the exposition below] It is certain that many cleave unto me with a lawful [0451D] love, that many, it being renounced, obtain only the appearance of truth; but by the word and doctrine of them that preach so many are everywhere begotten in the faith, that unto any man attending solicitously they seem utterly innumerable. But the Virgin above the rest, pleasing unto me [n: (II Tim. II)], useful, seasonable, knoweth not the plural number, but is one: burning with the desire of me alone, she is held by a religious unity, one is joined unto one, with a singular or a unique dignity. She is one, I say, forasmuch as she is not carried away into divers and perverse desires; forasmuch as she is never, not even for a little, taken away from him whom, being the one, she uniquely loveth; forasmuch as she is not straitened by the allurements of earthly things or by the bonds of many affections from beholding the Bridegroom [0452A] more fully, and is retarded by nothing save only the burden of the flesh. Rejoicing in the hope of things to come, groaning amid the transitory things that are present, the present things being loathed, she groaneth as a dove for the eternal things that are still absent; while she is held by the flesh, she seemeth to herself harshly to wander; nothing sufficeth her, until, rejoicing, she see the Bridegroom more fully. She is one, while, cleaving unto him, she loveth nothing beside him; and if perchance she seem to love aught, she loveth it not save for his sake; and so, if she do aught, she referreth it unto nothing other than unto him, one, watching unto him alone, until at last she sleep in peace in the selfsame. And because she is thus gathered by a watchful effect into the love of the One, by the desert of this unity she hath already deserved to be called my perfect one, seeing that unto her no holiness is lacking, so far as the present life suffereth, *262* in whom, I dwelling therein, dwelleth the [0452B] fulness of the whole Godhead. For neither is there another unto whom I would promise so great a gift of grace, so long as she yet complaineth that she is held in the prison-house of the flesh; who, to wit, if she should apply care to behold herself, might dare, while she liveth mortally, to confess herself perfect. *Not that I have already received,* saith a certain one, *or am perfect [n: (Philip. III)]*. And another: *My imperfect being,* saith he, *thine eyes did see [n: (Psal. CXXXVIII)]*. But if Paul and David acknowledge themselves truly imperfect, though in thine eyes, O God, deservedly not despised, unto whom, whether of the new or of the old, is so great, or so singular, a praise rendered, save perchance unto the Virgin, who is rightly set before all the rest? She therefore is one unto her mother, and in this excellence singular, at whose singularity the remaining throng and the common converse are astonished; every man marvelleth at a Virgin so [0452C] uplifted, so perfect, and her mother rejoiceth for a daughter such as this, so unique, so singular. But the mother who willed to nourish such a daughter for herself, who cherished and advanced her, and gave her to come unto so great an excellence — who is so worthily, who is so rightly proclaimed as grace herself, of which the angel Gabriel testifieth that the Virgin is full by gift? *Hail,* saith he, *full of grace [n: (Luc. I)]*. For grace herself, who by the goodness which is her own hath abolished the stains of our faults, so filled the aforesaid Virgin with an excellent benefit, that, one bearing one, she yet remained an unbroken Virgin, and, remaining a Virgin, answered the fructifying grace with a new and singular offspring. She is therefore one unto her mother, because grace her mother is seen to have neither a first like unto her [0452D] nor a second, who in a like form should show herself alike a Virgin and fruitful: no other did she honour with so great titles of virtues, this one and only one she adorned with a singular privilege. But lest any think that she deserved a privilege of this kind, and that she was not first chosen, but rather that she chose, as she is one unto her mother, so also is she reported the chosen of her that bore her, that not only her perfection, but also the election that went before it, may together be commended. Her assuredly did grace choose, and as it were beget her when she was not; the same received and nourished her, advanced and helped her, when now she was: in choosing or in begetting holding the part of her that beareth, in nourishing and advancing discharging the office of a mother or of a nurse. For she [0453A] who by a provident knowledge chose the Virgin beforehand, the same mercifully received and gathered her with a motherly affection: there as one that beareth, when in secret she foresaw that she should come; here a mother, or a nurse, when more evidently she advanced her unto an incomparable profiting. For she advanced her, and formed her in her own workshop; formed her by desire, by manners, by discipline; formed her also by letters, by ripe counsel, by the prudence of the serpent; and when she was formed, the simplicity and perfection of the dove completed her. Therefore perchance is she alike named *my dove* and *my perfect one*, that the perfection of the dove may be known to be at hand in her, that those seven virtues, as it were, which are naturally bestowed upon doves, may be known in her to be spiritually completed in a like number. As they say who have more diligently searched out the natures of birds, [0453B] setting their mind not upon a wandering report, but rather upon a truth known beforehand: the dove is naturally marked with a certain seven of virtues, wherein the perfection of our Virgin is found to be spiritually figured. Whereof, because I am found to have deferred speaking long above [n: (l. II, c. 13)], when in the second book this epithalamium seemeth first to have brought doves into the midst, that which I there had promised I will after a sort follow out at present, since now no mention of doves is set down in what followeth.

The dove lacketh gall. Our Virgin also, from the time when the angel Gabriel saluted her, from the time when the Holy Spirit coming upon her overshadowed her, hath given unto God to drink no bitterness either of acts or of affections, but [0453C] hath dropped down the honeyed sweetness of her manners more sweetly than the honeycomb.

The dove teareth not with her beak. And the Virgin repayeth not the deserved requital unto any that do her wrong, but in every grievance so showeth an unbending patience, that even when she saw her only Son betrayed, and, being betrayed, crucified, she had rather that the wicked should be converted than that they should be so afflicted according to their desert.

The dove nourisheth another's young The Virgin indeed keepeth in the surge of this life a mind so serene, that she thinketh it not foreign to succour clemently even them that are without and unworthy: nay unto all that are in need, as to the Jews, so to the Gentiles, she both giveth by her discourses a profitable doctrine, and with an equal benevolence showeth by her manners a discipline.

[0453D]The dove gladly dwelleth beside the streams. So also the Virgin diligently leaneth upon the streams of the Scriptures: she embraceth the books, she liveth according to the admonitions of the prophets, she is assiduously intent upon divine readings, she is at leisure for spiritual study, not detained by outward cares.

The dove chooseth the better grains. The Virgin nevertheless, while she applieth care or study in the Scriptures, doth not only set whatsoever things are profitable before all that shall be hurtful, but with a clear-sighted eye chooseth among good things the better, embracing the more eagerly whatsoever she hath known to be more pleasing unto the Bridegroom.

The dove gladly buildeth her nest in the rock. The Virgin also worketh in a firm faith whatsoever she is able, whose working is made solid by the rocky defence of faith; a faith that overtoppeth all is not troubled by the flood of waves, groweth not weary by a windy rage, is not shaken by the hammer of fear.

[0454A]The dove groaneth instead of singing. The Virgin, although she modestly rejoiceth in the aforesaid six of virtues, yet, because him whom she loveth she contemplateth not yet with a revealed *263* face, although in these as it were six days she busieth herself to work all things very good, yet, because it is not yet granted her to keep holy day more fully in the seventh, still, as one in need and expressing the nature of the dove, she groaneth, while she prevaileth not in that which she desireth, while she is held, and longeth to be dismissed more quickly. By which groaning the Virgin detracteth nothing from her deserts, nothing from her holiness; nothing from it, from that unity which above we commended as we were able, doth the groaning of the dove take away, but that for the place and the time she is perfect; nay, if for the present she groaned not, she would the more rightly be called, and would be, imperfect. Of whom it is added: *They saw her,* etc.
