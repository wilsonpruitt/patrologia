## CHAPTER IX.

[0454B]

*The daughters of Sion saw her, and proclaimed her most blessed: the queens and the concubines, and they praised her.* [var: Vulg. *Viderunt eam filiae*, with no place-name; Migne prints *illam* for *eam* and adds *Sion*, the word Philip's next sentence expounds] The daughters of Sion are above called the daughters of Jerusalem, who, as hath been said before, accompany the Virgin in this epithalamium, who truly behold with faith the nuptials that are present, and, while the rest are dim of sight, are not held by a wandering error or by ignorance. Who, attending unto the Virgin marked with so great virtues, so perfectly, so incomparably polished by labour together with grace, are astonished, they marvel, and they proclaim her not blessed (which they see to be common to very many), but rather most blessed, that is, the most excellent of all the rest. For whereas many others may not undeservedly be called blessed, both the Angel and Elizabeth affirm that this one is blessed among all, that is, above [0454C] all women, who against the custom of the flesh, against the interdict of mighty nature, alone remaining a Virgin brought forth the blessed fruit of a fruitful womb. Which when they see, and are not able sufficiently enough to open so great a mystery, nor can they find any like thing among women, the daughters of Sion extol her, and confidently proclaim her most blessed, and with a speech that envieth not they evidently announce her excellence. For the apostles, seeing through with a lynx-like understanding that Christ was born of a Virgin, and envying that same benefit neither to the Jews nor to the Gentiles, affirmed not only the Son but also the mother to be most blessed; by which kind of sight they also, according to their own little measure, deserved blessedness. [0454D] *Blessed,* saith he, *are the eyes that see the things which you see [n: (Luc. X)]*. Not only the apostles, but other teachers also, their successors in place and in time, are made in the Church the praisers of this so great Virgin; of whom some, by the fruitful desert of a chaste desire, are queens, others, not loving but feigning love, are more rightly called concubines. For in these days wherein the faith, bringing forth fruit in the whole world, increaseth, but among not a few, malice abounding, charity waxeth cold, both many praise the Virgin, provoked by a most devout love, and many, denying virtue, are coloured with the show of piety. For the honour of the Virgin hath so grown unto this present time, and her fame or knowledge hath so filled the whole world, that not only true but also false believers seem to exult in her praises, and whom devotion doth not, them [0455A] at the least occasion maketh to praise her withal. Therefore may this Virgin be called wondrous, and enough to be wondered at, whom the Angel and the prophet affirm to be blessed above all; whom, being blessed, they that love to exalt with praises rightly rejoice, and they that neglect to love her yet dare not now to cease from her praises. In whose praise it is added: *Who is she,* etc.

## CHAPTER X.

*Who is she that cometh forth as the morning rising, fair as the moon, chosen as the sun, terrible as an army of camps set in array?* The Bridegroom provoketh his devout hearers unto wonder, that they may deservedly be rendered the readier unto the praise of the Virgin; and that, when they shall have heard the question asked: *Who is she that cometh forth as the morning,* being admonished [0455B] of her going forward, they may be the more astonished at the things that went before. The morning rising, the darkness is affrighted, the night groweth thin, the obscurity is scattered, the grosser shadow vanisheth away: a new light springeth up, a certain face of the world shineth out, colour is restored unto things, the surface of the earth groweth glad. Our Virgin therefore is like unto this morning, who visiteth us that are oppressed with a deeper darkness and a denser shadow; upon which, while with the majesty of her hand she striketh as it were a sudden terror, they disappearing, she bringeth in unto us the morning radiance. But the moon, as they say, shineth not with her own light, but rather with another's, and what she receiveth from the sun she poureth back upon the rest with a full horn: assigned indeed especially to give light unto the night, less than the sun alone in such wise that she is set before the remaining stars. So also the Virgin knoweth that whatsoever of light she holdeth, whatsoever [0455C] of grace or of honour, is from Christ, who is of a mightier excellence; less than whom alone, she herself hath the primacy over the other saints, putteth our darkness to flight, and rejoiceth to render light. Fair therefore is she unto us, nay fairer than *264* the moon, who refresheth at last with a seasonable light them that are pressed by a long night, who with a torch going before bringeth us back unto the way when we wander and grope, and with a watchful care leadeth us in unto the longed-for presence of the sun. She is also chosen as the sun. The sun was made that it might appear a greater luminary than both the stars and the planets, and, whithersoever it shall have turned itself, might be seen to flash overtopping them all: that it should know not the shadow of night, should not grow dim at the onset of the mighty, should go about the world with a firm course, should not chastise its falls with penitence. But from the time when the Virgin entered the sanctuary of a fuller grace, having gone beyond not only any saints whatsoever, [0455D] but even the apostles that went before, burning with an exceeding love, glowing red with an unwearied holiness, she taketh the care of us all, having suffered nothing destructive in herself. She rejoiceth to go about with a watchful care the world that is pressed with darkness, to repair the cooling ruins with a most fervent affection, to drive away the clouds of our faults by a shining intervention, to make fruitful the earth of our minds by a most kindly regard. In this she abideth and remaineth, unto this she is divinely chosen, that in affection and in effect she may be perfect among the remaining saints: after the manner of the sun, marked with a steadfast splendour by the rays of virtues, never negligent of her own, not forgetful of us that are set far beneath. Who, because, bearing the part of the morning, she is found [0456A] to shine out unto us that are dim of sight, and knoweth that she hath the fairness of the moon from none other than another, and is seen, like the sun, to remain with a steadfast step in that which she hath begun; even as she is profitable and welcome unto them that rightly behold her, so is she terrible unto her enemies as an army of camps set in array. Which array or terror, because you can remember that it was set down and expounded above, it is not necessary that the exposition of the foregoing be repeated.
