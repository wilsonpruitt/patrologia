## CHAPTER XXVIII.

*Come, my beloved, let us go forth into the field, let us abide in the villages.* Thou saith she, whom I love, who above didst not refuse, being called, to come; whom coming I have known to impart to me a more bountiful grace — come yet again, if thou lovest to find me prompt and prepared, that I may both will and be able to consent to thy commandment. Above indeed I had as it were feared that my washed feet should be defiled, and that, rejoicing in a pleasing little bed, I should again be called forth to outward things; but because I now understand that this is expedient for the needs of many, I refuse not whatsoever I shall see to accord with thy good pleasure. Come therefore, my Beloved, and let us hasten to go forth together into the field, that we may announce thy name to the world and thy [0470B] grace; that the things which thou hast long since hidden and now disposest no longer to lie hid may become known to all who shall nevertheless be worthy to obtain this benefit. Not only in Judea, which is called as it were our city, where our fellow-citizens are and the generation of our flesh is propagated, but in the villages also, that is, without, among the Gentiles, let us cause the knowledge of us to be spread abroad; and that it may be spread the more effectually, let it not irk us to abide there the longer. And the beloved and the beloved one are then seen to go forth into the field, and are not held in the secret of the bridechamber with the door shut, when the world loveth to embrace by faith the Word made flesh, and venerateth the mother of whom the Word willed to be incarnate. But to those the mother and the son are not yet gone forth who, by a rustic dulness not having professed the catholic faith, adore not God [0470C] and man together, being struck with scandal, and abhorring the fruitful Virgin honour her not with a worthy reverence. Therefore the Virgin, seeing that this ought no longer to be deferred, willeth the knowledge of hidden things at length to be brought forth into the midst, and a sure faith to be carried over in the apostles to the Jews, and that the Idumeans be not made outcasts from the grace of the same gift. For into Idumea Christ refused not to stretch forth his shoe, and changed enemies into friends and strangers into household folk, when by teaching and grace he made known to the distant nations that his immortality had shod itself with our mortality. In which nations the Virgin admonisheth him to make a stay, and he, being admonished, refuseth not; wherein she secretly reproveth and accuseth the hardness of the Jews, who, deserting the faith of Christ all too soon after the apostles, [0470D] so remain, deferring their conversion even to the end of the world. But into the villages, that is, the nations which were foolish far from the citizens, which, rude and untilled, had not yet known the prophetic teaching, the mother and the son come, and make a lingering dwelling among them, while they so cleave to the son that they hold also a grateful mention of the mother; at whose advancement the Virgin, loving to rejoice, and understanding that this same thing pleaseth the son no less, yet urgeth and taketh it upon her confidently to admonish him that he persist in promoting unto better what hath been well begun.

## CHAPTER XXIX.

*Let us get up early to the vineyards, let us see if the vineyard hath flourished, if the flowers bring forth fruits, if the pomegranates [0471A] have flourished. There will I give thee my breasts.* Because all the faithful have found our benevolence profitable to themselves, and are not uncertain of their own advancement if grace shall have continued, let us get up early, when night is now compelled to be turned into light, and let us render them watchful with a labour and a zeal not slothful. In the morning indeed the solicitous husbandman willeth to go out to the vineyards, to provide them what is useful, to free them from what is useless, to cut away superfluities, to apply diligence to necessary things, rejoicing if he shall have weighed that they answer him with a fitting increase. And so let us also get up early, that is, let us hasten to take away the evening horror, to give the dawn to the vineyards, to pour in the morning sun: let our visitation drive far off and beat back the darkness of ignorance, and pour over the Churches of the Gentiles the new light of grace. Let the nations know that the Lord is thy name; [0471B] let them know and go forth and begin their work; and let those which before, barren and dry, were weighed down with a nightly dulness, advance and flourish in the morning, and be held as fertile vineyards. Let our visitation, our vision, confer increase upon them, and let it not permit them to be afflicted more than is just, but with the tribulation make also an issue: let us press upon the work, thou going before mightily and mercifully, and I diligently and faithfully interceding for the Churches. Let us see if it hath flourished — that is, let us hold it pleasing that the vineyard seemeth to have flourished; already *274* it sheweth beforehand that it hath received our grace; already it hath grown green, already it hath flowered, already it rejoiceth to have given hope to the solicitous husbandman, and with a flowering appearance to have given forth sure signs of advancement. And because the flower sufficeth not, if it be not granted to come to fruit, let us see, let us approve [0471C] that the flowers seem to bring forth fruits, because the nations which are taught to come together into the hearing of faith delay not to mark that faith itself with worthy works. Let us see also if the pomegranates have flourished; let us weigh that in them many have been strong with so great a fortitude that, fervent with love, they expose themselves to the enduring of martyrdom, even though the executioners set no material sword against them without. But that the syllabic conjunction, that is *If,* is so often repeated, is no doubting, but the speech is confirmed; because what she foretelleth is by no means doubtful, but certain to the virgin, nor doth the outcome of the matter contradict such a saying of hers. For many among the Gentiles, upon whom the mother and the son conferred their benevolence, after the manner of a vineyard have by labour and zeal brought forth a flowering [0471D] appearance, and by the advance of grace have led their flowering affections into fruitful effects, and by their form as well as their teaching have brought others to fruit. But some who burn with the torches of a greater desire flower as pomegranates, they redden with tribulations, they endure in their purpose so many and so great troubles in the contest that they differ from the fruit of martyrdom only by the suffering of death. For the Spirit which resteth not from envy and from lying oftentimes so burneth against certain men with unbridled malice that with the utmost zeal it busieth itself either to weaken their life, or at least to defile their good name with sinister rumours. It goeth forth therefore, and infecteth those whom it findeth more prone to malice; it armeth with shamelessness elders that are such not by mastership but by presbytery, who, when they are not able [0472A] to fulfil their concupiscence at their pleasure, determine to turn back the whole weight of the crime upon Susanna. A sinister report flieth, and shaketh the whole city; they are amazed and wonder at the unseemly novelty; and the innocent woman would have utterly perished by a false judgment, had not the divine mercy at length come to her aid, Daniel being sent. Who is there that would not praise her, that would not praise Marina and Eugenia and others of that kind — who would presume to defraud them of the reward from martyrdom, whom the guile of accusers and a shameless falsehood assailed, and whose souls the sword, not of iron but of trouble, pierced through? Finally God, sometimes suffering some to be deceived and to perish, permitteth the spirit of lying to rage in their ears; and the prophets, who deserve not to see true and useful things, prophesy by their own spirit whatsoever they know to please the king. [0472B] Being seduced therefore, they seduce Achab, itching in his ears, and with false soothsayings assert an imminent victory; but Micheas, who alone with a free voice testifieth the truth, first smitten with a buffet, is straightway made over to prison. There are some who, loving to speak vain things and to divine lies, in no wise will that any truthful man meet them; and both the prophet and he to whom it is prophesied smoothly and wickedly, not foreseeing his own case well enough, rejoiceth over a brother's trouble. A disease or pestilence of which kind creepeth widely even to-day and rangeth further; this stain occupieth many and is spread over a broad space; and very many are found who, bearing before them the appearance of love in countenance and habit, maliciously detract, and curiously hear them that detract. For being darkened with malice they deem that more is conferred upon their own praises if [0472C] they shall have weighed that the honour due is not paid to their brethren; if they have known the fame of others to be weakened by any reproach whatsoever, they think their own either to stand more firmly or to be more copiously heaped up. And so, hating Micheas, they follow not his truthful soothsaying, and lying to the king they comply with his desires; and while both king and ministers are seduced by a flattering lie, those are not deserted whom they unjustly and wickedly persecute. For the Virgin is at hand, and vouchsafeth them her own vision, whom she prudently understandeth to be pressed down by an unjust wounding; and lest, being wounded, they fail, but rather that, being tempted, they may rejoice in the issue, delivering them from present things she promoteth them to better by her intervention.
