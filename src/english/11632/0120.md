## CHAPTER XXXIII.

[0474D]

*[CAP. VIII.] Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother, that I may find thee without, and thoroughly kiss thee, and now no man shall despise me?* [var: Vulg. *et iam me nemo despiciat*, "and now let no man despise me" — a present subjunctive, and with the pronoun before *nemo*; Migne prints the future indicative *despiciet*, which is the tense Philip's own gloss below carries throughout (*deferetur… tolletur… erit*).] The Virgin desireth that the things which, touching the Gentiles and the Jews, she already foreknoweth while they are yet absent, may become known without to a world that knoweth nothing save what is evident; and that the knowledge of the Bridegroom, which already lieth open to her as it were within the bridal chamber, may go forth abroad into the field, where the sun dawneth more largely. Who shall give thee to me, saith she — that is, who shall bless me with so great a gift of his own, who shall so enlarge my desire, pressed down with straits, that I may be able to find thee without for my brother, and to set forth to the rest, who know them not, the things that are known to me? Thee indeed the begetting of flesh maketh my brother, nay, the brother of all; thee a mother feedeth in time, and that not of necessity, but of nature: [0475A] thou suckest her breasts, thou refusest not to take from her the nourishment of little ones, when thou usest food, drink, raiment, sleep, whereby thou excusest not thyself from being man. Him whom my mother proclaimeth to be such a one, so very little, who shall give me to find without in the street — to find, I say, not man only, which is not denied to the eyes of unbelievers, but God made man, which is imparted to none save the faithful? I will, saith she, that the Gentiles and the Jews, who are wont to be knowers of nothing but things visible, may see thee, understand thee, love thee to be God; and that thou, who art proclaimed a hidden God by prophetic foretelling, mayest be seen by these with a sure faith, the eye of the heart being unveiled. And if it be granted me to find thee there, I shall rejoice, and shall thankfully kiss him whom I have found among men; that is, I shall be delighted with the love and the honour of thy name, [0475B] namely when I shall behold thee loved and praised of men. Yet the Virgin willeth not to kiss him when found, but rather to kiss him thoroughly, and the composition of the word betokeneth nothing less, but an increase; and we are affirmed thoroughly to kiss, as it hath seemed to some, in that we kiss through very many places, expressing thereby a greater desire. The Virgin therefore willeth, if it may be, to find him among men, that she may thoroughly kiss him whole, and be compelled to pass over nothing without joy; that she may see him honoured by them as God and man, and, thoroughly kissing him who is marked with a twofold honour, may rejoice to be delighted. And when such honour shall be worthily paid to the Son, the reverence due to the Virgin herself also shall not be taken away, and she shall not be despised in the eyes of them that rightly seek, who before all women was chosen to bring forth such a one. For whosoever shall rejoice that he understandeth and loveth him as at once God and [0475C] man will not hold her, of whom he was born, contemptible, nor shall he be smitten with the scandal of the Jews nor with the folly of the Gentiles, when by the just heralding of the truth the wondrous integrity of the mother shall be established. Whosoever, I say, willeth to pay honour to the Son according to his good pleasure, setteth himself also not to take away from the mother the honour due to her, because a good son accepteth the homage of no man who seeketh not consequently to pay honour to the Virgin Mother.

## CHAPTER XXXIV.

*I will take hold of thee, and lead thee into my mother's house, and into the chamber of her that bore me: there thou shalt teach me.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:2 has no *et in cubiculum genitricis meae*, "and into the chamber of her that bore me"; the phrase is Cant. 3:4's, printed as the lemma at 0349D. LXX and Vet. Lat. carry it at 8:2 (*καὶ εἰς ταμιεῖον τῆς συλλαβούσης με*), and Philip expounds the added chamber through this chapter and the four that follow.] When I shall perceive thee prudently understood and lately loved, favouring them that love, I will take hold of my beloved as it were with hands; [0475D] and holding him whom I have taken, I will not suffer thee to be led back from the purpose, but will cause thee to be brought in more largely into my mother's house. For far be it that I should fail them that love, that I, who am not despised, should despise; but I will cherish the imperfect more diligently, even unto the perfect. I will love them that love, and will advance them that they may love more; and thee, O Beloved, I will admonish with unwearied prayers, that thou help them. Thus will I take care to lead or to bring thee into my mother's house, when with dutiful solicitude I shall make her ready for thine entering in, when thou shalt solicitously obey me as a son his mother, and shalt follow not their ancient deserts, but my desire. The Virgin's mother, as was said before, is the very nature of mortals, which bringeth us all forth into this light by a not unlike begetting; which, that it may have for itself a house wherein it shall abide unto the [0476A] end of the world, bringeth forth men for itself by a succession that shall at no time cease. Into this house, that is, into men, the Virgin promiseth to lead the Bridegroom, that in them he may mercifully work their own salvation, that he may walk about in them, and rejoice to come to the chamber, where he may lie down, where he may rest, and may be able to find the reclining of quiet. Into the house *277* of her mother, and into the chamber, the solicitous virgin leadeth the bridegroom, when, dutifully pitying us, she leadeth him with devout prayers unto this: that he dwell in us, that he walk about in us for his goodness' sake, that he tarry in us, and be not called away thence by the desert of our depravity. *There,* saith she, *thou shalt teach me.* When it shall have come to this, that I rejoice thou art brought in to all the elect, and art not led back from the accomplishing of their salvation by the desert of their faults, then thou shalt teach me, [0476B] and shalt make me certain of hidden things; then shall I know both that they are elect, and that I am solicitous for the elect. Thou shalt teach me, I say, when the hope which before thou hadst given me by promise thou shalt with a larger grace translate into a thing fulfilled; when of that glory, whereof thou givest the longing to them that wait, the promissory term ceasing, thou givest the effect by a better.

## CHAPTER XXXV.

*And I will give thee a cup of spiced wine, and the new wine of my pomegranates.* When thou shalt make me taught, the effect of a fuller grace being known, I also will give thee the gifts of a mightier abundance: I will offer thee a cup most sweet in taste and in savour, wine indeed spiced, a love compounded out of labour. For the deserts [0476C] of them whom I shall have known to please thee — their loves, that is, and their labours — I will heap together as it were in a mixing-bowl, and will pledge them to thy sight, or rather to thy drinking; and, that thou mayest receive them the more thankfully, I will commend them with devoted benevolence. For if aromatic kinds bestow a seasoning upon wine, a skilled hand being applied it is turned into a spiced draught; and if to love there be added tribulation and the straitness of suffering, that love, being offered to the Bridegroom, obtaineth the place of a welcome draught. Which urging home, she saith: *And the new wine of pomegranates.* New wine boileth and bubbleth up, raging with tumult and with turbulence, casting all filth from itself with a stronger onset, and so betokeneth that love which careth not to be soothed with the quiet of this life, but, running to martyrdom, burneth to be polished by pressures and by beatings. But by pomegranates he willed those to be figured in whom, like [0476D] seeds, very many virtues are gathered together, whom the inward collecting of virtues maketh precious, and the ruddy face of their sufferings maketh beautiful in the eyes of them that rightly see. But "my own" the Virgin saith perchance for this cause: that whosoever, loving Christ, giveth himself over to the Passion and to martyrdom, she rejoiceth to bestow help and care upon him, and whatsoever he profiteth she reckoneth all her own. The new wine therefore of pomegranates is the unrestrained fervour, or the fervid love, of them that show themselves ready to suffer patiently all things which the world either horribly threateneth or rejoiceth violently to inflict. The cup of this wine or new wine the virgin loveth to pledge, when she rejoiceth that men fervent in spirit flash with virtues, and, taken away out of the midst, pass over at last to the table or the unfailing banquet of the Bridegroom, [0477A] and soothe the taste of him that feasteth with the sweet excellence of their deserts. And lest she be reckoned to ascribe this to herself more than is just, nay rather, that she may testify that whatsoever she rejoiceth over in them is of the divine gift, turning to the maidens that stand by she saith: *His left hand,* etc.
