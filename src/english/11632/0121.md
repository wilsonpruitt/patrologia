## CHAPTER XXXVI.

*His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me.* That I lead the bridegroom, saith she, into my mother's house and into all the elect, that I serve the cup and heap gifts upon gifts, is his benefit, his left hand, his temporal consolation, which receiveth me as I lean back far more softly than the feathery tenderness of a pillow. For with this solace he cherisheth my insufficiency, and sustaineth it in the present, who with a more bountiful right hand shall embrace me in that which followeth; and he who now [0477B] so ruleth me that he forsaketh me not at all, shall one day so rule me that he shall set all sufficiency round about. In the fruit of that thing, and in the shadow of this hope, I now sweetly lie hid; much from things present, more largely from things to come, do I [sic: *nilaresco*]; which assuredly for this cause I make known to you that stand by, that you also may love him by whose so great gifts I am now enriched. Now this little verse is found set down and expounded before, and there another goeth before it which betokeneth the virgin's languor, because, when the virgin languishing asketh to be compassed about with apples and stayed up with flowers, straightway mention is made of the left hand, which is understood to sustain her as she lieth down. But here, where salvation is in the doorway, and no mention is made of languor, where the age is more advanced, and the advancement is of a stronger desert, she, [0477C] now secure of her own things, is solicitous for the profitings of others, and, forgetting as it were herself, rejoiceth in the perfected deserts of the elect. She seeth that they are to be compared, by the sweetness of love, to wine, to new wine, to the fervour of the spirit, and that they are the more worthily to be offered to the royal table and to the divine taste; whose oblation she desireth so ardently to be fulfilled, that in regard of it she patiently beareth her own to be deferred. For considering with what fruit her conceiving and the integrity of her childbearing become known to the hearts of the faithful, and to what end many in the process of time grow hot to love the bridegroom, she cannot but rejoice over such things, as well to come as present; and, their *278* glory being foreseen, she seemeth more gladly and more quietly to await her own. Beholding which, the bridegroom saith: *I adjure you,* etc.

## CHAPTER XXXVII.

[0477D]

*I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, till she please.* You, saith he, ye apostles, you doctors of the Church, go, teach all nations; and as touching them whom the Virgin foreseeth by a prophetic understanding to be saved, see that your negligence draw not back from advancement him that was foreseen; lest, if perchance you watch too little over the keeping of them, you stir up the desire of her from the quiet she hath already found. Now this adjuration, whereby the vigilance of teachers is so solicitously called upon, is found none the less in the second and third book of this work, where it hath been worn down enough by expounding — if at least it please the reader — nor, being repeated in this last one, hath it now need to be expounded. [0478A] Yet this may be said: that in the first place, taking care of her own self, she rejoiceth in spiritual leisure, from which she is forbidden to be called away by temporal business or unquietness, lest, while she loveth to be at leisure and contemplateth spiritual things, she be compelled to return, before she will, to the solicitude of her neighbours. But in the second place she taketh care lest Judaea, her heart being hardened, be seduced, but that the knowledge of the bridegroom be brought into her house or chamber; nor doth it suffice her enough that she be made thankfully an attendant to the bridegroom or to the son, if she should know that her own mother is utterly forsaken of grace. From this also the bridegroom forbiddeth her to be called away, as well in affection as in effect: namely, lest the apostolic teachers be found by their negligence to thwart it, but let them take care and be zealous to employ a watchful diligence, that the Virgin cease not to rejoice at the advancement of her own nation. [0478B] But in the third place she taketh care either only for the Gentiles, or alike for Jews and Gentiles, naming her mother the infirmity or the nature of men, into whose house — that is, into all flesh — she willeth the knowledge of the bridegroom to be brought in; and the bridegroom willeth that she be not drawn away from this desire by any trouble whatsoever. That that may be fulfilled which is written: *And all flesh shall see the Salvation of God [n: (Luc. III)]*

## CHAPTER XXXVIII.

*Who is this that cometh up from the desert, flowing with delights, leaning upon her Beloved?* The angelic spirits, standing by the bridegroom and the bride as they talk together, privy to the secrets of the espousals, beholding the deep mysteries, are held fast, nor are they able enough to be astonished [0478C] or to marvel what, or how great, the Virgin is, whose nuptials they see celebrated. They weigh indeed that she, endowed with a wondrous understanding, rejoiceth at the present advancement of others and is solicitous for that to come; that she burneth on this side with desire of the bridegroom, on that with solicitude for her neighbours; marked with virtues, comely with the eminence of her deserts. They consider also that the Bridegroom so rejoiceth in her leisure or quiet that he utterly keepeth off every approach of any indiscreet person, that he admitteth no importunate one within the cloisters of quiet, and suffereth her not, as she resteth, to be stirred up until she herself shall will it. But when they perceive the virgin to excel in these and other such things, when they scarce comprehend so great a grace of hers, so great deserts of hers, they speak one to another and inquire in mutual discourse what or how great is she [0478D] whose eminence they contemplate.

## CHAPTER XXXIX.

*Who is this that cometh up from the desert,* etc. The earthly habitation was already, through a long series of times gone by, a desert, and, sins requiring it, untried in heavenly goods; which Astraea forsook, being fuller of nettles, of thorns, of briers, and, bearing herself back to the stars, held a more pleasing place there. In which solitude the nature of mortals lay down oppressed with languor, while, the vigour of the spirit being corrupted, it leaned upon the allurements of carnal things; and, sunk under a heavy flood, it knew no way of coming up, it was hardly weighed down with mire, nor was there any possibility of wiping it away. Thence, by a new kind, by a newness [0479A] incomparably to be admired, by a portentous miracle as it were, by a thing to be compared with none in all the ages gone by, a virgin riseth and standeth forth, infected now with no filth of vices, and by a wondrous ascent is carried up to the excellence of deserts. What therefore, or how great, is she? Who worthily weigheth her eminence, who so ascendeth out of the ancient horror, out of the vast solitude, that, abiding there in body, in mind she holdeth a higher degree, and now floweth not away with the vices of manners, but, against custom, floweth with the delights of deserts? And in very truth we see her flowing with exceeding delights, and leaning, after the manner of delicate women, upon him whom she loveth; who, walking upon the earth, scarce attempteth to touch the earth, whose delights the beloved beareth up on high and sustaineth. Who shall worthily consider her, who shall not [0479B] marvel at her height, seeing that neither before nor afterward is she compared to any *279* of women, who, leaning and specially trusting in him whom she loveth, needeth nothing of ours, not distrustful of the advancement of perseverance? But that it is so solicitously asked by the angels who she is — if you will call it to mind, you shall find it thrice repeated; and whosoever be brought in to make such a question, the question itself maketh mention of the Virgin's advancement. First it is asked: Who is this that, made slender after the manner of a little rod by manifold disciplines, and compared to the smoke of aromatical spices and to all the powder of the perfumer, while yet living in the flesh so stretcheth herself on high by desire that whosoever more diligently attendeth to this is delighted by her odour or her example? Secondly it is asked: Who is this that, going forward, is like the dawn, while, the things behind being left [0479C] as it were a thicker shadow of the nights, she obtaineth the beauty of the moon, nay, and the likeness of the sun or of the day, and, striking terror into her enemies, is comparable to an army set in array? Thirdly it is asked: Who is this that, delicate after the manner of noble matrons, forgetful of all things, delighted in him alone whom she loveth, so faileth from her own things that she willeth not to stand in them even a little, and so profiteth in him that she loveth to lean her whole self upon him? And that she may know and call to mind whence and whither she hath come, she heareth the Bridegroom saying: *Under the apple tree,* etc.
