## CHAPTER XLIII.

*If a man shall give all the substance of his house for love, he shall despise it as nothing.* Whosoever rejoiceth not in the portion of things senseless or irrational alone, but, obtaining a human shape, useth a prevailing *281* reason — if he shall have given whatsoever he hath in regard of the charity that is to be gotten, he shall seem to have given nothing, if he be made master of this will. Substance is the name of whatsoever our necessity or our cupidity heapeth up in the present, and of whatsoever management either spendeth or keepeth for temporal uses; substance, I say, not that whereby we essentially subsist for living, but that which is said to stand under us or to subsist [0482C] for us that live, for our using. Which for this cause is not unfitly called the substance of a man's house, because his necessity is sustained by such supports, and with them every solicitous man enricheth and honoureth his house — he, that is, who laboureth either, being provident, to keep off poverty, or, being covetous, to increase riches. Such substance he more largely squandereth and scattereth who plungeth himself into a deep desire of getting love; who, when he shall have given the one and shall have perceived that he hath received the other, comparing what was given with what was received, shall seem to himself, as he looketh the more diligently, to have given nothing. For with so great eagerness he loveth and embraceth this that he hath received, that, accounting for nothing whatsoever he had given, thus despised, there seemeth to be no comparison [0482D] of that which passeth away with that which perisheth not; but that is held as nothing and falling, and this as abiding, as somewhat great and chief. Nor is it beside the purpose if a man's body be in this place named substance, since man is proved to subsist in it or out of it; and for this cause Scripture addeth to make mention *of his house*, because there the treasure of the soul hath its dwelling as in a fruitful vessel. If therefore this body, wherewith it is plain that the soul is so tenaciously mingled that it cannot easily be restrained from affection toward it (for there is none to be found who would be despoiled of this house or body, since Paul also chooseth rather to be clothed upon than to be stripped; and love drew not Peter, who loved thrice, utterly back from that law of the flesh, but, Christ foretelling it, another led him whither he would not) — if, I say, a man of heart shall have given this body, to which [0483A] the soul is bound with clamps so tenacious, it is fitly judged that he hath given nothing [n: (Rom. VIII)]. For the sufferings of this time are not worthy. Which love the Bridegroom for this cause so manifoldly extolleth and commendeth, that the loving Virgin may greatly esteem what is so greatly commended, and that, when she shall have weighed that by the Bridegroom's gift she hath attained no small height of that which is commended, she may understand that she is not despised in his eyes. Concerning whom the companions subjoin: *Our sister,* etc.

## CHAPTER XLIV.

*Our sister is a little one, and she hath no breasts. What shall we do to our sister in the day when she is to be spoken to?* We see, say they, that the Virgin hath advanced not slightly in the grace of love, yet that she hath not yet [0483B] more fully attained to that which she desireth; because, although in regard of love she is already come to a contempt of the aforesaid substance, yet she hath not yet found him whom she so loveth in his own comeliness. And because she is our sister by most lofty deserts indeed, and not by nature, being afterward to receive with us the inheritance of the one God the Father, let us, speaking one to another, confer a little concerning her; and if perchance she needeth aught of ours, let us bring it, not now singly, but all alike. So far as it hath seemed to us, this our sister, who, as was said above, hath already deserved to be brought into the chamber, doth not yet rejoice that she is brought in from chamber to chamber; and this indeed is the cause: that she is not yet sufficient enough, nor grown up, but labouring under littleness, and not brought on to years as it were [0483C] marriageable. A dishonest love, which intemperance or lust forerunneth, which a Venus or a Cupid ill-persuasive usurpeth and defileth — such a love, I say, is wont to snatch away a Virgin at an unripe age, obeying pleasure rather than making ready things that shall profit the Virgin. But since this love which we have in hand, though it be more vehement, yet loveth to be more prudent, and the lover willeth this love to profit not so much himself as the Virgin, the same Bridegroom waiteth, and waiteth again, and hath patience in love, so long as the Virgin advanceth in deserts and in an ampler grace. Still therefore she is little, and we see that she hath no breasts, although it was shown above that the same lacketh not breasts; but because they seem not yet to have obtained a full sufficiency, we are said not to have those things which are willing indeed to appear to the bridegroom, [0483D] but are not yet strong enough to appear. Not fit for nuptials are virgins, while they are yet in childhood, while the age is little, while the breasts do not yet swell — which, though they stand out a little and put forth a token of the age that shall be, yet still keep the old tenderness of one still unripe. Since therefore she is thus little, and it is thus plain that she hath no breasts, what shall we do to her? what further shall we now be able to employ? what that belongeth to us can now further be bestowed, that she may at last rejoice that the day of the nuptials is not deferred? *282* She is indeed to be spoken to, and to be brought into a more secret bridal chamber, but first she must be led forth from things present and not yet sufficient; and that she may be led forth hence, what more is to be done by us, that may avail, namely, toward obtaining a day so desirable? In that day, that [0484A] is, in respect and in furtherance of that day, what can we bestow upon this sister? with what zeal or love of ours shall we prepare her more amply, that she may come to the day for which she is now held as it were impatient and so solicitous, when the noble lover, the law of the espousals being consummated, shall speak to her? For although above he hath spoken with her, or of her, in many parts and in very many ways, yet he hath followed not a completing gift, but a meritorious zeal; now it remaineth that she follow him within the chamber of the chamber, and that he, in that manner which he knoweth, ineffably speak to her when she hath followed. Under the word "to speak to" a spiritual mystery is covered, because this nuptial epithalamium familiarly useth figures; and this speaking-to, whereby we desire and foretell that the bridegroom shall come together with the bride, is itself that [0484B] conjunction whereby the face of the seeker is granted to find the face of him that is sought. It is a speaking-to when he, being present to her that is presented, shall hide nothing fitting to be said, but shall reveal his face to her that lacketh corruption; and, with none mediating, the one shall be joined to the other in secret, and the Virgin, seeing the Word, shall be satisfied with a love now fulfilled.
