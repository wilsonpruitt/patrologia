## CHAPTER XLV.

*If she be a wall, let us build upon it bulwarks of silver. If she be a door, let us join it together with boards of cedar.* When the companions ask what they may do that shall be fitting for that day whereon the Virgin is to see the long-desired glory of the divine beauty, they themselves delay not to answer as it were with an answering speech, and they determine to provide for the Virgin [0484C] with a solicitous benevolence. *If she be a wall,* say they, compacted of stones and mortar — nay rather, of virtues coupled by the binding of a charity set between — it remaineth that we fashion upon it bulwarks of silver, and that we confirm the strength of the wall by a defence added above. Jerusalem is the city of the Holy One, the numerous gathering of the faithful, given over in the present to fruitful labours, glorious with a hope that cannot be confounded, which one Lord, one faith coupleth and bindeth into one sheaf as it were; and the same the holy Virgin girdeth about with example and doctrine, with deserts and intercessions. If therefore she be a wall — nay rather, because she is, rejoicing to be set about the citizens — it is expedient that some defence be set above her that is thus set about, that namely our solicitude, our exhortation may watch and be intent from above, lest [0484D] the enemy stretch himself even a little to pierce it. For although we cannot yet carry the labouring Virgin hence, as she desireth, this at least we owe and will now bestow upon her that laboureth: that our defence may render her fortified against whatsoever cometh upon her, and our frequent speaking-to may render her instructed to bear it patiently. Thus we prepare for the wall bulwarks not of stone, but of silver, when we strengthen the virgin with solicitous defences and with speakings-to, when we strongly forbid the malice of assaulting enemies from approach, and cry out to them that the virgin is secure of the issue. But lest this city seem so shut in by the wall set about her that through it she be shut out from those benefits which she hath not from herself, but as it were from without, she who is the wall is none the less [0485A] called a door, through which whatsoever is necessary for the city is ministered. For through the Virgin the Bridegroom loveth to enter in to the gathering of the faithful, and through her also to succour all that trust in her; she, closed to every man, is unbarred to God alone by the little key of faith, that through her the Word made flesh may be visibly brought in to men. If therefore she be a door, let us join it together with boards of cedar, that is, let us add to this aforesaid grace an unconquered strength, that an odoriferous breadth may meet whosoever detracteth, and an unfailing strength withstand him that presseth on perchance more hardly. Meanwhile, while the noble Virgin laboureth and sweateth in the contest, let us prepare and strengthen her patience with the hope of the crown; and in very truth that which she hopeth and asketh to be given her is not far off: as we perceive, she shall now see and rejoice that the Bridegroom is revealed with unveiled [0485B] face.

## CHAPTER XLVI.

*I am a wall, and my breasts are as a tower, since I am become in his presence as one finding peace.* The Virgin, hearing that the angels are not held negligent and slothful toward her, but, being solicitous for her, promise their patronage or their ministry, showeth in a short discourse what she rejoiceth to be already bestowed upon her, hoping that the things which remain be not deferred by a longer delay. I, saith she, grace giving it and the industry of the hand working it, am now a wall not about to fall, charity binding in the midst the heap of living stones, *283* over which my breasts rise up, as a tower, affording honour to the wall, striking terror into the enemies that shall approach. As the wall betokeneth the heap of virtues and the bond of inward charity, so the breasts betoken the form of life [0485C] and the doctrine of truth, which come forth and hasten to appear without unto all, that they may instruct them that love, and compel adversaries to shrink away. In which, although she is not yet perfected for the completing of the present nuptials, yet she hath obtained them very many years since, namely since she deserved to find as it were peace in his presence, whose other peace she desireth and looketh to find right soon. For since the Holy Spirit overshadowed her for conceiving, the Son that was to be born brought her not peace, but as it were peace: peace, that is, from vices, peace in grace, from the din of a carnal begetting — not the peace of vision, not the peace of a dwelling that shall abide in eternal glory. Neither doth she show that she rejoiceth in her own advancement alone, but she showeth that the rest also obtain no slight advancement, who [0485D] through her, grace giving it, have so far advanced that they have deserved to become a vineyard rich not in vine-shoots, but in peoples. Whereof she subjoineth: *There was a vineyard,* etc.

## CHAPTER XLVII.

*There was a vineyard to the peaceable, in that which hath peoples. He delivered it to keepers: a man bringeth for the fruit thereof a thousand pieces of silver.* Since, the Spirit working, I found as it were peace in his presence, the vineyard of the Lord of Sabaoth hath had an effectual advancement; and that which before was held within the narrow bounds of Judaea is now found more broadly and more fruitfully in that which hath peoples. That which hath peoples may be called faith, or unity, or love, to which it is plain that divers peoples are so given over with one mind that, the apostles preaching, not only did many [0486A] thousands of the Jews believe, but their sound also went forth into all the earth, and their preaching was heard. Such a vineyard he committed to the apostles and to apostolic men to be kept, whose preaching propagated it not only in Judaea, but among the nations also; and Paul, when he beheld Judaea hardening and being perverted, from that time and thenceforward declareth that he turneth himself to the Gentiles. Which vineyard, grace prospering it, so grew up in a little time, so did away the newness of infancy and its tender ineffectualness, that a man — that is, one mighty not so much in strength as in virtue — should give for the fruit thereof a thousand pieces of silver, that is, all his own things for salvation. The flower of the vineyard is the conversion of life; the fruit is the perfection that shall follow, for whose hundredfold whosoever desireth to be a man casteth away things transitory; the fruit, lastly, is the very repaying of the spiritual reward, [0486B] which a man procureth for himself by the contempt of a temporal price. For he who is found to act not womanishly, but manfully, measureth not what he can give for the fruit of that vineyard, but, greedy of the spiritual reward, that he may receive it with usury, prodigal of the temporal price, bringeth it without weight, number or measure. For the thousand pieces of silver — that is, whatsoever he possessed either infinite in number or great in price, which deceitfully smiled upon his eyes and favourably rang in his ears — he bringeth to the apostles' feet and casteth down, and holdeth in contempt in the stead of dung, that he may receive the eternal fruit by the intervention of temporal things despised. But when the Virgin hath related both her own advancements and those of others, and the vineyard hath been commended which flourisheth more in peoples than in a multitude of vine-branches, she heareth not now [0486C] the angels answering, but with ears erect the bridegroom, who both favoureth and applaudeth the praises of the Virgin and of the vineyard.

## CHAPTER XLVIII.

*My vineyard is before me: thy thousand are the peaceable, and two hundred they that keep the fruits thereof.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:12 *et ducenti **his** qui custodiunt fructus eius*, "and two hundred for them that keep the fruits thereof." Migne's text drops *his*, so that the two hundred are the keepers themselves rather than a payment made to them — which is the construction Philip's exposition follows.] The Bridegroom showeth that the vineyard which followeth him, and giveth temporal things for his sake, walketh not in darkness, but is in the light before him; to which, favouring and approving that, its own things being given, it now abideth in him, he repayeth not like for like, but double, or a hundredfold, together with himself. He, saith he, who for my sake holdeth temporal substance in contempt, that he may be the more comforted, let him know that he is in my sight; and let it be made known to whatsoever inexperienced man readeth or heareth, that, loving him that loveth, I turn not away thence [0486D] my gracious face. For those thousand pieces of silver, which, according to thy word, a man loveth to bring — he who after thy pattern loveth to set things invisible before things visible — shall be peaceable, that is, they shall earn the rewards of peace: he shall bestow peace upon them that lavish their own for things eternal. Nor shall they be able to be called peaceable only, but two hundred, that is, they shall confer a double peace upon him that lavisheth; because the peace or perfection which the distribution of goods earneth is both great in the present, and shall follow greatest in the time to come. As perfection is understood in a thousand, so is it understood also in a hundred, which are assuredly doubled when the vineyard is brought to so effectual an increase that, superfluous things being cast away, he may deserve to find a double *284* peace, whosoever loveth to keep the fruits thereof with watchful care. For he who willeth to preside by his form and to profit by a commendable doctrine, [0487A] and loveth to be a solicitous keeper of the aforesaid fruit, he by the thousand pieces of silver bringeth in the first peace, but not the chief; and, changing the hundred into two hundred, he afterward bringeth over the second and better. But having briefly shown what belongeth to all the perfect by a general grace, he straightway returneth to the virgin with a special love and solicitude; and having care of her above all the branches of the aforesaid vineyard, he willeth her to pass over more quickly from a transitory peace to one that shall abide.
