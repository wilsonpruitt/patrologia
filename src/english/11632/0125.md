## CHAPTER XLIX.

*Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the friends hearken: make me hear thy voice.* Although, saith he, I still defer both them that bring and them that defer to bring the thousand pieces of silver for the two hundred, and suffer them not to be led on to the desired [0487B] reward that followeth, yet thee, whom I love before all, I will not restrain any longer from thy desire; but, the choice being given, I will that thou shortly have whatsoever thou shalt have chosen. Thou therefore, whom I led forth out of the dwelling-place of darkness, out of the shade of hard death, and who by an excellent grace dwellest not in a dry place, but in the gardens — choose a pleasing word, put forth thy voice, speak thou thyself: the companions stand by, the friends hearken, they desire to hear from thee thyself. I myself, who know better whatsoever thou art now purposing to say, will to hear thy voice, and the good pleasure of thine inward choice, that the manner of thy saying may become known, not to me, but to the rest, from whom thy choice lieth hid, if it shine not forth in words. With thee the love of things vainly passing away and the fear of things coming on do not wither, but faith, hope, love, like [0487C] gardens, grow bright with a flowering dew; and so wondrous a grace of these things so delighteth thy desire that no shaking bendeth thee toward the things that are beneath. Thou desirest indeed to go forth, or to pass over straightway to things above; loathing whatsoever is of time, thou wilt the more quickly lay hold on things eternal; whatsoever I have bestowed for the present, thou wilt exchange for the desire of things to come, and, the cloak of the flesh being cast off, to greet in presence my face, the face of thy beloved. And she: *Flee,* etc,

## CHAPTER L.

*Flee, my beloved, be like to the roe and to the young hart upon the mountains of spices.* In many places or times above the loving Virgin hath invited her beloved, and hath asked in many ways that he come; and in the very head of the Canticle she more diligently longed for his kiss, [0487D] and through this whole epithalamium hath shown that she willeth and seeketh this. What then meaneth it that she now driveth him away, with desire of whom she above crieth out that she languisheth? what cause shall we say there is that she biddeth him flee whom she so loveth, unless perchance this, that lovers have their angers in hope of a more pleasing concord, because, as a certain man declareth: *The angers of lovers are the renewal of love?*

But, as I seem to see, the Virgin's love knoweth no angers, but burneth to see the more quickly him whom she so loveth; and because she knoweth that he is not seen in this place or body, she desireth to go thither with him, where she may be able to behold him. *Flee,* saith she, *my beloved,* to the secret sanctuary of the Father; [0488A] return quickly, call me thither with thee, where I may be hurt by no troubles any more; strip me the more quickly of the corruption of this body, and where it is lawful, as much as is lawful, reveal thyself visible to me. And because above thou wast wont to be likened to the roe and to the young hart, be now also in this business swift, nimble and discerning; let thy consideration worthily discern what beseemeth a bride and a mother, and let it set me apart, by a more excellent privilege, from all that are purely men. The mountains of spices are the supernal and angelic powers, which bring to us that labour divers and necessary healings, by whose ministry useful spices are procured for us — he nevertheless disposing it by whose perfumer's art they are tempered. Below these angelic mountains I will not that thou speak with me any further, but flee above them, that thou mayest be held far more excellent; [0488B] hold thy seat at the Father's right hand, unapproachable to angels as to men, and already granted, as I see, to me with thee, to the mother with the son. Hold therefore my right hand, and lead me forth in thy will: I will follow the Lamb whithersoever he goeth [n: (Apoc. XIV)], halting with no slowness. Flee quickly, that I may flee, because I also am ready to flee; fleeing, exalted above the angels, I shall see thee going before. But after that the virgin Bride, the mother, hath been more gloriously received by the Son, and hath obtained a glorious seat with the Bridegroom above the angels — with what honour she rejoiceth, with what joy she seeth herself honoured, I judge that silence is rather to be kept than that I should dare to be wordy of such things. For that which my understanding, or the understanding of one like me, taketh not in — what manner or effect of uttering can there be, [0488C] since even that which the understanding somehow reckoneth that it contemplateth cannot easily be unfolded in fitting words? This surely may be said: that where the Son is, who set the flesh he had taken at the Father's right hand (whatsoever that truly is which Scripture hath called the right hand), there is his mother, who ministered to him her own flesh, who out of her womb, her integrity safe, brought forth God as man. *285* There, I say, she is, and is crowned with a glorious honour or with an honourable glory, and of him, and in him, and with him whom she bore, having her desire, she glorieth; and to him as Bridegroom the Bride so cleaveth with true and sincere embraces that, love being satisfied, an unremitted desire of love endureth. The mother therefore is with the Son not only in spirit, whereof not even a slight doubt is held, [0488D] but in body also, which assuredly seemeth not incredible; because although canonical Scripture crieth not this out with evident proofs, yet dutiful faith is led to it by likely arguments — which, though one hasty in word and in knowledge prove it not sufficiently, yet that Augustine of ours affirmeth it worthy to be believed. Nor is it grievous to the angels if they see the Bride with the Bridegroom, the mother with the Son, obtain the royal throne, and hold now upon the heavenly seat him whom before she held in her lap; if they see human flesh gloriously honoured above themselves in the mother and in the Son, and the nuptials happily begun among us there more happily consummated.

Of which things, because I have spoken [0489A] as I was able, though not as I would, and with a foot slow enough have reached the end of speaking: I beseech thee, O holy Virgin, that, since thou now standest by thy Son in the heavens, thou cease not from care and solicitude for him who is thine. Thine indeed am I, if not by a sufficiently worthy desert, yet at least by an affection a little readier; thine, serving thee, though as yet not knowing whether I be worthy of hatred or of love [n: (Eccle. IX)]; thine, desiring to please thee with services and with gifts, if only thou shalt grant me effectually to prevail in my purpose. Do thou therefore, O holy Virgin, receive from my hand a little gift. It is not gold, not silver, not a stone fit for the offerings of kings, but an unpolished libation of words, brought forth with a grosser understanding, an offering small enough, yet full of watchings and of toil. After I betook myself [0489B] to the exercise of it as to a rough training-school, I was smitten with many troubles, and by the onset of the malignant was heavily beaten back as it were in the face; and the work begun I many times laid aside under harder pressures, [0490A] yet, thy more gracious remembrance calling me back and helping me, I let it not go. Do thou therefore, by whose help I failed not in the furnace of pressures under a heavy hammer, but, breathing again as best I might, have now finished in thy name the work begun — do thou now receive it offered, correct mercifully him that offereth it and amend him, love him amended, and commend me, being loved, to thy Son. For I know that, if I shall have been commended to him by thy benevolence, either the venomous serpent shall not bite me at all, or by his biting he shall not hurt me; and that Son of thine shall either call back the tongues of detractors to a modest silence, or shall change their poisonous rumours into a health-bearing antidote. So assuredly I hope, by thine intervention and by thy grace, to be delivered from all things hurtful, and, being delivered, to be heaped through grace with mightier gifts; and to me thus heaped thou wilt now [0490B] grant to rejoice through thee in the Son, and — that I may set an end to the book — thou wilt grant me, freed at last from evils, to glory with you in the heavens.

*Joannes GALLEMART, Doctor of Sacred Theology, and professor of catechesis in the University of Douai, this 7 Feb. 1618.*
