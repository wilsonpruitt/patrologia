# 11632 — blind polarity read (runbook step 4a)

Eleven readers at ~12 chunks each, plus marker auditors, all **fenced off** from
the 21 `cruces-*.md` files, `data/briefs/11632.md`, `next-session-resume.md` and
git history. The fence is the method: on 11638 every agent given the cruces file
returned "all already logged" and found nothing, while blind readers on the same
text found four live defects. Reconciliation against those files happens here,
**after** findings are formed.

Findings were verified against the plate before any edit was applied. This file
records what was applied, and — more importantly — **what was deliberately not
applied and why**, so a later session does not have to re-derive it.

## NOT applied — open for the merge

1. ⚠ **0024 @0240C — *ille mundus*: "that world" or "that Clean One"?**
   Reader argues *mundus* is the **adjective**, in asyndetic apposition with
   *ille Sanctus*, with *ad quem nihil **immundum** attinet* making the jingle
   audible — and that earlier in the same chunk the world *revels* in this heat
   (*mundus in maligno positus aestuabat*), so it cannot be what fails to bear
   it. **Both readings are grammatical.** The supplied dashes were fixed because
   that is a plain Pattern 23 violation; the reading itself is a genuine crux and
   **is not being flipped on one reader's argument**. Merge decides.
2. **0020 @0232C — *manantibus*.** English shipped "them that **laid hands upon**
   her", which is neither the plate's word (*mano*, to flow) nor the likely
   emendation (*minantibus*, "them that threaten", parallel to *impugnantibus
   repugnare*). A third reading supplied by association with *manus*. **Should be
   rendered literally with *minantibus* logged as the conjecture** — needs a
   translator's hand, not a mechanical swap.
3. **0012 @0213D** — Migne prints a full stop after *Verbum Patris*; the English
   keeps the stop but **moves *introspexi* across it**, leaving Migne's second
   sentence without the verb it prints. Both English sentences read smoothly
   where the Latin's first does not construe. Honest rendering leaves the verb
   where printed and lets the fragment stand.
4. **0017 @0226B** — *pravitatis, quae… vexatur et purgatur* (singular); English
   pluralized both verbs to attach the clause to "penalties", which also yields
   the odd claim that penalties are purged.
5. **0033 @0268A** — *lege non facto corporis* rendered as an ablative absolute
   ("the law of the body **not being observed**"); "observed" is not in the
   Latin and the construction is not an absolute.

## The negation sweep — a measured null, not a silence

`necdum`/`nondum` was grepped first in every range, ahead of *non*/*nec*/*nisi*,
because that particle had already been caught flattening to "not" during
translation.

- **0000–0011:** 5 sites, all carried as "not yet".
- **0012–0023:** 4 sites, all carried; 2 further English "not yet"s correspond to
  *adhuc… non*, legitimately.
- **0024–0035:** 9 sites, all carried; *nequaquam* → "in no wise", *haud dubium*
  → "there is no doubt but that".

**No flattened "not yet" anywhere in 0000–0035**, and no English negation without
a Latin source. ⚑ Note what this means: the negation-fidelity failures found in
these 36 chunks were **all lexical and syntactic, not polarity**. The prose held
where it was being watched; it gave way where nobody was looking.

## Readers 4–6 · chunks 0036–0071 · 35 ours-side sites, 26 applied

### The largest single repair in the read — 0046 @0301A, *Capreae*

*Capreae* is **dative singular** ("to the roe this is implanted by nature"), and
every verb in the sentence is singular feminine: *contenta non sit*, *appetit*,
*inventura*, *praeterit*, *adit*, *diligit*. Our English read it as a nominative
plural and **pluralized eight verbs to agree with it.** Two controls, both
inside the work: Migne's own parallel two sentences later keeps the singular
(*Cervus… devorat*, correctly "the hart… devours" in our English), and the
relative that picks the roe back up, *per **quam***, is singular feminine — our
"Whereby" was concealing that it had lost its antecedent. Repaired throughout.

### 7a″ lemma conformation — the Douay arriving over the plate

- **0063 @0341D + @0342C — *per noctes* shipped as "by night".** The exposition
  between the two lemmata is *entirely about the plural*: *recte virgo non tam
  **noctis** facit quam **noctium** mentionem*, then four *Nox est…* definitions,
  then *Per has noctes* — which our own English renders "**Through these
  nights**." The commentary was explaining a word no longer on the page. Both
  lemmata repaired.
- **0068 @0353A — *dilectam* shipped as "**my** beloved", *donec ipsa velit* as
  "till she please".** Migne prints no possessive. ⭐ Checkable inside the work:
  the same verse ships "nor make **the** beloved to awake, till **she herself**
  will" at 0046 and "nor make **the** beloved to awake" at 0121. **Three
  occurrences; only the one in this range was conformed.** Brought into line.

### NOT applied — readings, trade-offs, and one marker question

1. **0044 @0297A — *«Stay* therefore *me,* she says, *up with flowers»*.** Reads
   as split gibberish in English. But the Latin prints ***Fulcite* ergo *me,*
   ait, *floribus…*** — three italic runs — and our English mirrors them **1:1**.
   The proposed repair reads better and **moves sacred marker boundaries**. That
   is a trade-off for the merge, not a defect. **Not applied.**
2. **0052 @0314D — *non vere facies coloratur*:** "not **truly** coloured" or
   "not coloured **with spring**"? *vere* is equally the ablative of *ver*, and
   the paragraph is built on *verna suavitas* / *vernat viror*. Both grammatical;
   possibly Philip's pun. Crux, not edit.
3. **0061 @0338B — *factus a Virgine*:** agent ("sought **by** the Virgin") or
   separation ("become **far from** the Virgin")? Reader argues separation from
   *longe* and from the next clause's *longius… abesse*. Argued, not asserted.
4. **0067 @0351B / 0062 @0339A — printed *cum* for *eum*, handled two
   incompatible ways** (silently read as *eum* in one place; read as the
   conjunction with a bracketed pronoun supplied in the other). One must move;
   both want a crux.

### Migne-side, controlled by corpus counts rather than asserted

The readers counted rather than guessed, which is what makes these usable:
- ***angusti-* occurs 30+ times across the 126 chunks; *augusti-* exactly twice**
  — and one of those two prints *se **angustant*** and ***augustius** inhaerent*
  in a single sentence. The plate confuses au/an. (0050 @0312C.)
- ***fiducialiter* occurs 14 times; *fiducialibus* once** (0055 @0325A) — where
  our English silently supplies the adverb. Least-bad rendering, but it needs a
  crux, because nothing on the page tells a reader the printed form is not one.
- *boni* for *bovis* (0049 @0308A) — the ass/ox doublet is named outright three
  lines later, *pigritiam **bovis***.
- *vox* for *nox* (0063 @0342B) — the next clause is *non tam **noctis** facit
  quam **noctium** mentionem*.
- Also logged: *munditia* for *immunditia*, *vitae* for *petrae foramina*,
  *plaudi* for *claudi*, *collum* for *colum* (Prov. 31:19), *suum* for *sum*
  in a re-quotation the plate corrupts against itself.
