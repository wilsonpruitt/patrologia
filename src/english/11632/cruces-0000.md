## §1 · THE QUOTATION COLLATION — all 43 listed spans, itemized

Every span in `data/quotations/11632/0000-0005.md` was collated word by word
against the Vulgate. **Spans recorded as AGREES were collated and found to agree;
the absence of a marker on them is a finding, not a silence.**

### Chunk 0000 — 6 spans

1–4. **0181A, four italic spans** (*Sumite,* / *materiam vestris, qui scribitis,
aequam* / *Viribus; et versate diu, quid ferre recusent,* / *Quid valeant
humeri.*) — **not scripture.** Horace, *Ars poetica* 38–40, quoted as verse and
set by Migne on three lines; the line structure is preserved in the English.
Migne prints *humeri* where Horace has *umeri* — an attested medieval
orthography, Pattern 9, deliberately unmarked.

5. **0182A « Homo non percipit animalis [n: (I Cor. II)]. »** — 1 Cor 2:14.
Vulg. *Animalis autem homo non percipit ea quae sunt Spiritus Dei.* Migne's span

(*spiritalia*) sits **outside** the guillemets in Philip's own clause. Every word
printed is a Vulgate word and the sense is unchanged. **Collated; no `[var:]`** —
a quotation cut short to fit a host sentence is citation practice, not a variant
reading. Rendered « The animal man does not perceive », with *spiritalia*
outside, exactly as printed.

6. **0182A « Adsum. »** — one word, no locator, no `[n:]`. Nearest received text
is Isa. 58:9 *Ecce adsum*. Not a lemma; rendered « I am here. » **No marker.**

## §2 · THE TYPOGRAPHY QUESTION — 11632 does NOT open in an italics-only regime

⭐ **Brief §7 asks where 11632's seams fall. In this range there is no seam,
because both marks are in use from the first columns, and the division between
them is FUNCTIONAL, not sequential.** Counted across 0000–0005: 21 guillemeted
spans, 22 italicised — and they never compete for the same job.

- **« » marks scripture quoted inside the running prose.** All 21 guillemeted
  spans in this range are scripture; not one is anything else.
- **Italics mark four other things:** (a) the running Song lemma being expounded
  (*Osculetur me osculo oris sui*, 7×); (b) book titles quoted as titles
  (*Cantica canticorum Salomonis*, *Parabolae Salomonis…*, *Verba
  Ecclesiastae…*); (c) words discussed as words (*videntes*, *pax*,
  *adversarius*, *pacificus*); (d) classical verse (the Horace) and Migne's
  marginal source-edition numbers.

**This is a materially different opening from 11638**, whose Books I–II italicise
scripture and switch to guillemets only at *INCIPIT TERTIUS TOMUS*. Anyone
reconciling the two works should not expect 11632 to repeat that history, and
**an italic run in a later 11632 chunk is not by itself evidence of a pre-seam
regime** — it may simply be a lemma. Under Pattern 6 nothing was tidied in either
direction; guillemets are 1:1 with the Latin twin in all six chunks (verified
mechanically: 0/0, 2/2, 2/2, 9/9, 6/6, 2/2 pairs).

**No guillemet asymmetry occurs in this range** — every « has its », and none is
stray or unopened.

**Migne's marginal source-edition numbers *101*–*110*** run through the range
(*101* in the 0000 head, then *102* 0182B, *103* 0183D, *104* 0185B, *105* 0187A,
*106* 0188D, *107* 0190C, *108* 0192B, *109* 0194A, *110* 0195D). Kept italic,
verbatim, **at the exact word they interrupt** — *108* in particular falls between
*excellentiam* and *non contaminans*, and the English clause was built around it
rather than moving it. Same class as 11638's *303*–*313*.

## §3 · PATTERN 7 — printed readings rendered as printed, conjectures kept here

**Three `[sic:]` fired. Each is a non-word, and none of the three is also
translated in the running text** (brief §8's named failure — a `[sic:]` on a word
that was ALSO Englished passes every check and reports nothing).

1. **0185B `[sic: *molodia*]`** — *non vocis **molodia**, sed morum consonantia*.
   Read *melodia*, near-certain: the phrase is a melody/concord antithesis and
   *molodia* is not a word. Carried untranslated in italics.
2. **0186C `[sic: *tilulo*]`** — *insuper et in **tilulo** regem regis filium se
   appellat*. Read *titulo*, certain: the same sentence-run has *in ipso titulo*
   at 0185C and *in hujus quoque libri **titulo*** at 0187A. Carried.
3. **0191C `[sic: *eumdum*]`** — *ipse tuus uterus **eumdum** Sponsum hominem
   generabit*. Read *eumdem*, certain. ⚠ The English deliberately reads "thy very
   womb shall beget [sic: *eumdum*] Bridegroom as man" and does **not** also say
   "that same": supplying the English demonstrative alongside the marker would
   have translated the defect and quarantined it at the same time.

**Rendered as printed, no marker (real words, merely wrong or merely odd):**

- **0181B *narratoria*** — *Quod si virium consideratio **narratoria** est in
  carminibus aethnicorum*. Rendered "is a narratorial thing". The word is
  formable from *narrator/narratio* and construes as a predicate adjective, so
  Pattern 12's boundary puts it on the English side; but the sense wanted is
  plainly "necessary/required" and *necessaria* is the obvious conjecture. **The
  English is ours and reads strangely on purpose.** ⚑ Second opinion welcome at
  merge; do not silently smooth it to "necessary".
- **0182A, the misplaced semicolon.** Migne prints *serius quisque [0182A]
  videatur**;** non tam nova cudere, quam furari potius praeinventa* — a
  semicolon cutting *videatur* from its own infinitives. Pattern 8: **preserved**,
  so the English reads "…would seem; not so much to strike out new things…".
  It is meant to look wrong.
- **0189B *longe suavis obdormisse*** — the construction requires the comparative
  adverb ***suavius*** (*longe suavius*, "far more sweetly"), and *suavis* is the
  nominative adjective, which cannot govern *obdormisse*. Under 7a the printed
  word may **not** be read as its comparative to rescue the clause, so the English
  reads "**slept far sweet**". ⚠ **This is the sentence in the range most likely
  to be "fixed" by a later reader.** The conjecture is *suavius* and it lives
  here, not in the text.
- **0195A *laborem fingere valeam in praeceptum*** — rendered literally, "may be
  able to shape labour into a precept". Construable but strained; no emendation
  offered and none made.

**Negation audit (brief §9).** Every printed `non`/`nec`/`neque`/`nisi`/`nullus`/
`nihil`/`nunquam` in the six chunks was carried, including the ones that make the
English clumsy: *nec debeam, nec praevaleam* (0189A), *nullus penitus adnotatur,
qui **non** … praeloquatur* (0189D), *nisi suo soli Domino **non** concessum*
(0190C, rendered "not granted save to her Lord alone" — the Latin's redundant
negative is preserved in the "not … save"), *non defectum, profectum vero*
(0194D), and the double *non praestabit … non negabit* at 0182B. **No negation
was supplied that the Latin does not print.**

## §4 · CONVENTIONS SET BY THIS RANGE — ⚑ for the merge to ratify

Brief §5's ruled vocabulary was applied as given. These are the **new** decisions
the opening forced, which §5 does not cover.

1. ⚑ **`liber` = "book", `caput` = "chapter", `volumen` = "volume" — and 11632
   has no *tomi*.** The work divides into *libri* (`## LIBER PRIMUS.` → **BOOK
   ONE.**) and *capita* (`## CAPUT PRIMUM.` → **CHAPTER ONE.**). Note that 11638's
   *tomus*/*liber* problem does **not** arise here in the same shape: at 0186C–0188B
   Philip uses *liber* and *volumen* interchangeably for Solomon's three books
   (*In tertio **volumine*** … *liber ille primus*), and both are rendered by their
   own English word, never merged.
2. ⚑ **Migne's own numbering inconsistency in the heads stands.** He prints
   `CAPUT PRIMUM` in words, then `CAPUT II`, `CAPUT III`, `CAPUT IV` in numerals.
   The English mirrors it: **CHAPTER ONE.** then **CHAPTER II.** etc. Pattern 9
   normalizes a section label only where English has *no* exponent for the defect;
   word-vs-numeral has one, so nothing was normalized. **Do not tidy this to a
   uniform series.**
3. **`INCIPIT` = "HERE BEGINS"** (brief §5), applied at the 0000 head.
4. ⚑ **`PROOEMIUM IN CANTICA.` → "PROEM ON THE SONGS."** — plural, because
   *Cantica* is the plural Philip argues for at 0185B–C. The number of *Cantica*
   / *Canticum* / *Cantici* is tracked per occurrence throughout, never
   harmonized; see §1 items 8–9.
5. **`(cont.)` on the repeated `CAPUT PRIMUM` head is untranslated** and carried
   verbatim (brief §10.7).
6. **`[CANT. I.]`** at the head of 0003 is Migne's own bracketed marginal
   locator. Reproduced verbatim, untranslated, in place.
7. ⚑ **Migne's mixed capitalization of *Sponsus*/*sponsus*, *Sponsa*/*sponsa*,
   *Virgo*/*virgo* is MIRRORED, not regularized.** He runs both within single
   sentences (0193D *advenientis **sponsi**…* beside 0193C *adventu **Sponsi***;
   0194A *sponsam matrem **Virginem***). The English capitalizes exactly where he
   does. **This is the most likely drift point across 21 agents** and is the one
   convention here that a mechanical sweep could check.
8. **Pattern 17 applied throughout, and it does real work in this range.** The
   Annunciation dialogue is singular on both sides — *thou/thee/thy* for Gabriel
   to Mary and Mary to Gabriel, including *Osculare me osculo oris **tui*** at
   0194A — while Philip's own frame is plural: *postulatis*/*deposcatis*/
   *vestris precibus* (0182A–B), *vos veteres nostis* (0182B), *Vos igitur in hoc
   auditorio … qui astatis* (0195A–B), *sicut nostis* and *vos, fratres, nostis*
   (0195D, 0196B) all take **you**. The payoff case is item 31 above: Deut. 18:15
   printed in the plural, rendered "him shall **you** hear", visibly different
   from the singular the Vulgate would have given.
9. **Pattern 3.** *Scripturis* meaning the canon occurs twice in the range
   (0184A *vera legerent in Scripturis*; 0190D *scripturis inhaerentem*) and is
   rendered **the Scriptures** both times. ⚑ Neither instance uses *divina* or
   *sacra* — which is precisely the form 11638's sweep pattern missed — so a
   later sweep of 11632 must match the bare *Scriptura(e)* too.
10. **Register vocabulary held to brief §5 where it appears:** *clemens* =
    "clement" (0192A) kept distinct from *misericors* = "merciful" in the same
    sentence; *adolescentulae* = **maidens** (0188B, 0188C).

## §7 · RATIO NOTE

EN/LA by chunk (verifier's own figures): 0000 1.55 · 0001 1.55 · 0002 1.52 ·
0003 1.56 · **0004 1.64** · **0005 1.68**. The verifier warns on the last two. The cause is genre, not
padding: Philip writes rhymed, heavily parallel *prosa rimata*, and the last two
chunks are its densest stretch (*purificet, magnificet, laetificet*; *me deliniat,
me reficiat, me serenet*; *utilis, fertilis, et jucunda*), where a Latin
participle or a syncopated ablative absolute needs a finite English clause each
time. 11638 ran 1.35–1.63 on the same author. No clause was expanded that the
Latin does not carry, and nothing was summarized.
