## §2 · THE THREE FINDINGS THE MERGE SHOULD CARRY

⭐⭐ **Ps. 82:19 — *nomen tibi DEUS*, not *Dominus* (0011 @0211B).** This is the
proof-text of *Caput* XI, whose whole thesis is announced in its first line:
*Nomen Sponsi, **Deus** est.* The Vulgate reads *Et cognoscant quia nomen tibi
**Dominus***. Conforming the lemma would leave the chapter arguing that the
Bridegroom's name is God from a verse that says it is Lord — the exact 7a″
failure. **If another range meets Ps. 82:19, expect *Deus* and do not conform
it.**

⭐ **Isa. 53:4 — *infirmitates nostras*, not *dolores nostros* (0008 @0204B).**
Migne's reading is the Matt. 8:17 form. It is adduced to prove *why the Bride
says ointment**s**, plural* — *languores multos, multas **infirmitates** nostra
sibi miseria comparavit* — and the next 200 words run on *quae **infirmitas**,
quo unguento indigeat*. The Vulgate's *dolores* would leave the plural argument
without its keyword.

⭐ **The head lemma of *Caput* V prints *ubera SUA* (0006, col. 0196C).** The
Vulgate, the frontmatter `incipit`, and every other printing of the lemma in
this range (0007 ×3, 0008, and the re-quotation inside *Caput* V itself) read
*ubera tua*. The commentary that follows the defective printing expounds the
second person outright — *longe esse meliora **ubera tua** vino* — so the
divergence is confined to the lemma line. Rendered as printed ("his breasts")
with a `[var:]`. **Worth watching across the other twenty ranges: if the
running lemma is printed *sua* again elsewhere it is Philip's text; if this is
the only instance it is the compositor's, and this range holds the datum.**

## §3 · TYPOGRAPHY — the seam question, answered for this range

**11632 does not divide its two quotation marks sequentially the way 11638
does. It divides them by FUNCTION, and does so from Tome I onward.** Across
cols 0196D–0212A both marks are in continuous simultaneous use:

- **Guillemets « »** carry every quotation Philip adduces as a witness —
  scripture, and only scripture, in all 45 runs in this range.
- ***Italics*** carry (a) the running Song lemma at the head of every *Caput*
  and at its recapitulations, (b) classical verse (0198A, 0201A), (c) words
  displayed as words (0207D, 0209C), (d) printed locators — *(Job II)* at
  0197B and *(Psal. LXXXIV)* at 0203A, the latter **inside** a guillemet run,
  and (e) Migne's marginal source-edition page numbers *111*–*119*.

So **an italic run in this work is not evidence of a pre-seam book**, and a
range that meets both marks on the same column has not crossed a seam. I
crossed no *Explicit/Incipit* — chunks 0006–0011 are continuous inside Tome I,
*Capp.* V–XI — and I report no seam. Whether a seam exists later is still open;
this range shows only that the *starting* practice already uses both marks.

**Guillemet asymmetries, mirrored and not balanced (Pattern 5 corollary):**

1. **Unopened » at 0197C.** Philip's paraphrase of Job 3 (*Quare, inquit Job,
   non in vulva miser ego mortuus exspiravi…*) is closed by a » that was never
   opened: `…uberibus sum lactatus [n: (Job III)]? »`. Reproduced as printed.
   The chunk's counts are therefore 8 « against 9 » and this is correct.
2. **Unclosed « at 0211D–0212A.** `« Vere, inquit, tu es Deus absconditus
   [n: (Isa. XLV)]. Deus Israel Salvator.` opens and never closes; the run
   swallows the whole of Philip's closing exposition to the end of the chunk.
   Reproduced as printed — 11 « against 10 ». **This is also why the extractor
   listed only 14 spans for 0011: the Isa. 45 quotation is invisible to it.**

**Two guillemet runs swallow Philip's own commentary** (the 11638 @0569A
phenomenon, twice in this range). At **0207D–0208A** a single « … » encloses
Joel 2:32, Ps. 117, the *eleos*/*misericordia* etymology, the whole argument
that our salvation is of mercy and not of justice, and Titus 3:5 — 110 words of
which perhaps 25 are quoted. At **0211C** a run opening at Luke 1:68 encloses
*et benedictum nomen majestatis ejus* (which is Ps. 71:19, under a *(Luc. I)*
note) and then Philip's own gloss *Nomen humilitatis ejus, Homo est; nomen vero
majestatis, Deus est*. Both reproduced 1:1. Likewise **0210B**, where *At ille*
sits inside the Matt. 16 quotation.

## §5 · RANGE CONVENTIONS (offered to the merge, not settled unilaterally)

**Words displayed as words: the Latin is translated, the italics are kept.**
Four fall in this range, all in the same etymological construction that §5 of
the brief ruled on for *candidatio*:

- 0207D *Jesus, Latina lingua* **Salutaris** *dicitur, vel* **Salvator** →
  "*Saving*, or *Saviour*". The argument runs Jesus → Saviour → *ipse enim
  salvum faciet populum suum*, and Latin left standing would break it.
- 0209C *Christus autem* **unctus** *dicitur* → "*anointed*". The whole of
  *Caput* X runs anointed → oil → *ipse unctus et ungens*.
- 0207D ***eleos** autem Graece, Latine **misericordia** est* → the **Greek**
  word is kept (*eleos*; it is named as Greek and is the foreign term), the
  Latin gloss is Englished: "*eleos* in Greek is, in Latin, *mercy*". Two
  italic runs in, two out.

This is the same call 11638 §2.3 made and I have made it the same way; it is
worth a line in the merge because 11638's own §1 lists *Eleos* and
*misericordia* among its surviving italics without saying whether they were
Englished.

**Pattern 17.** The range is almost entirely third person. Every second-person
form is inside a quotation and every one is singular in the Latin (*ubera
**tua***, *tu erudieris*, *faciem **tuam***, *nomen **tuum***, *inclina coelos
**tuos***, *tu es Deus absconditus*) — so *thou* throughout. The two plurals are
Philip addressing his readers (*Nunquid non **vobis**… videbatur* 0198B; *De quo
**legistis*** 0197C; *ut **legistis*** 0199D) and the Matt. 23 *Vae **vobis***,
all rendered *you*. No mixed-number sentence occurs in this range.

**Ruled vocabulary held throughout** (brief §5): *misericordia* = mercy
(~40 instances, never "pity" where the noun is used) kept distinct from
*clementia* (does not occur here); *charitas* = charity (0206D *a charitate
Christi*; 0211D *ejus charitas se diffudit*) — never "love", which is reserved
for *amor*/*dilectio*, both of which also occur (0205C *inducit… amorem*,
0210B *illius dilectio*); *Sponsus* = Bridegroom, *Sponsa* = Bride;
*unguentum* = ointment (never "perfume"), *species* = spices, *confectio* =
confection/compounding.

## §6 · PATTERN 7 — printed readings rendered as printed, conjectures kept here

**1. 0202C, *Pecte igitur virgo ubera sponsi vino dicit meliora*.** `[sic:
*Pecte*]` — carried untranslated in italics. *Pecte* is a real Latin word
("comb!", imperative of *pecto*) but cannot fill the adverb slot the sentence
gives it, and the conjecture is certain: **`Recte`**, which is exactly how the
parallel sentence opens the same argument at 0196C (*Recte, inquit, adventum
tuum… praeoptavi*) and at 0199C (*Recte ergo Jesus dicitur*, 0207D). I judged
this over the line Pattern 12 draws between "a real word merely wrong" (render
it) and carried type: rendering "Comb" would put a considered-looking reading
on the page that misrepresents the plate more than the carried run does. This
is the boundary Pattern 12 itself flags as worth watching (the 11081 @0030B
case); **if the merge prefers the other side of it, the fix is one marker.**

**2. 0204D–0205A, *coepit tuam statim turpitudinem abhorrere*.** Rendered as
printed — "began at once to abhor **thy** foulness" — inside a paragraph that
is otherwise wholly third person and reflexive: the next clause is *suae merito
foeditatis **ipsa sibi** vehementius displicere*, and the one after *suo
**ipsius** odio*. The conjecture is ***suam***. Rendered literally, not
repaired; a real word wrongly inflected takes no marker under Pattern 12.

**3. 0203D, *quae Deus, honorum semper amans, legitur odoratus*.** Rendered
"ever loving honours". The conjecture is ***bonorum*** ("ever loving good
things"), which the context — God smelling the odour of sacrifices — plainly
wants. A real word, so rendered, not carried.

**4. 0206C, *unitus patientia, certam velut fiducialiter et humiliter jam
praesumit*.** *certam* is a feminine accusative with no noun anywhere in the
sentence to govern it. Rendered as closely as the words allow ("presumes upon a
sure thing, as it were"); the likely loss is *certam **victoriam*** or *certam
**salutem*** (the latter is what the parallel at 0207A supplies —
*quasi datam teneat jam salutem*). Not supplied in the text.

**5. 0206B, *ejus labores et agones fingit gratia misericorditer in
praecepto*.** *fingit* rendered "fashions"; *figit* is possible and would give
"fixes… in a precept". Both construe; no repair made, no marker.

**6. 0207B, *non apud se, ad modum invidorum; clauso spiraculo retentare*.**
Migne's semicolon falls inside the phrase, between *invidorum* and its
participial clause. Preserved (Pattern 8), which leaves the English punctuated
oddly at the same point.

**7. 0202A, Gal. 2:14 ends with a full stop where the sense is a question**
(*quomodo gentes cogis judaizare.*) — 0006 @0199A. The Vulgate prints `?`.
Preserved as a statement per Pattern 8; not supplied.

**Negation audit.** Every *non / nec / neque / nisi / nunquam / nullus / nihil*
in the six Latin chunks was matched against the English on a second pass, with
particular attention to the dense clusters at 0196D (*non sponsorum… non
lactari*), 0198D–0199A (*Nolebat… non solum… quod fas non est*), 0208D (*noluit
misereri… non avertit… non convertit*), 0209D (*non principio, non fine, non
loco, non tempore*) and 0210A–D (*non prius… non reddiderit se immundum… non
Judaeo tantum, non Graeco… nec est ab aliquibus… interclusum*). No negation was
dropped and none was supplied. The one place where carrying a negation makes
the English read strangely is 0210C — *remotiores quoque ab eadem exclusi
gloria **non** habentur* — and it is carried.

## §7 · WHAT A LATER BLIND READ SHOULD LOOK AT FIRST

1. **0207D's 110-word guillemet run.** If any repair has hidden anywhere in
   this range it is there: Migne's quotation marks enclose Philip's own prose,
   which means the reader cannot tell quotation from commentary and neither
   could I. Read it against the Latin as one unit.
2. **The `[var:]` at 0199C–D** (the Ps. 18/Ps. 44 fusion) is the marker in this
   range making the largest claim, and it asserts a *conflation* rather than a
   variant. If the merge decides a fusion under one note is not a Pattern 14
   case, that marker is the one to withdraw.
3. **`[sic: *Pecte*]` at 0202C** — §6.1, the one place I chose the less
   conventional side of a Pattern 12 boundary.
