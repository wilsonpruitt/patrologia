## §1 · TYPOGRAPHY — this range crosses NO seam, and the reason matters

⭐ **11632 does not switch marks the way 11638 does. It prints both, side by side,
throughout, and the division is by FUNCTION.** Every chunk in my range carries
both, and a count across the whole work confirms the pattern holds from chunk
0000 onward (guillemets and italics appear together in 0000, 0002, 0003 … 0029);
there is no run of italic-only chunks followed by a run of guillemet-only ones.

The functional division, consistent in all six of my chunks:

- ***Italics*** — the **Song lemma under exposition**, both when quoted as the
  chapter's head-text (*Ideo adolescentulae dilexerunt te.*, *Trahe me; post te
  curremus…*, *Introduxit me rex in cellaria sua.*, *Nigra sum, sed formosa…*)
  and when re-quoted piecemeal in the gloss (*Curremus,* … *Trahe me.* …
  *Memores,* … *in te;*). Also Migne's marginal source-edition page numbers
  (*120*–*128* run through this range) and one printed locator (§4 below).
- **« »** — **every other scripture quotation**, without exception in this range:
  53 guillemeted spans, all of them non-Song scripture (or, at 0220A, the Song
  quoted from *elsewhere in the book* — *Mel et lac sub lingua tua*, Cant 4:11,
  which is not the lemma under exposition and therefore takes guillemets).

**That last case is the proof.** At 0220A Philip quotes the Song itself and
Migne guillemets it, because it is a proof-text, not the lemma. The mark tracks
the quotation's *role*, not its book. Do not "tidy" 11632 toward 11638's
book-boundary model — the two works are marked on different principles, and a
sweep that assumes a seam in 11632 will manufacture one.

**Guillemet asymmetries: none in this range.** All six chunks balance (7/7, 6/6,
4/4, 13/13, 13/13, 10/10) and every pair is reproduced 1:1 in the English.
Pattern 5 *inquit*-interruptions are frequent here — Philip's habit is to drop
*inquit* / *ait* / *inquit Petrus* / *ait Isaias* inside the quotation — and all
of them stay inside the guillemets, untouched.

## §3 · TYPE DEFECTS

**⭐ 0015 @0220A — `Desalutari` (Luke 1:47). The one `[sic:]` in the range.**
Migne prints « Exsultavit Spiritus meus in ***Desalutari*** meo ». *Deo salutari*
has been run together and lost its *o*: `Desalutari` is not a Latin word at all,
so Pattern 10 governs (run-together division is non-word type) and it is carried
into the English untranslated in italics inside `[sic: …]`, with the surrounding
*in … meo* rendered around it. Intended reading, near-certain: **Deo salutari**,
"in God my saviour", the Magnificat's own words. The English therefore reads
"« My spirit hath exulted in my [sic: *Desalutari*] »", which is ugly and is meant
to be: the alternative is silently repairing a compositor's fusion in the one
verse in this chapter that every reader knows by heart. ⚠ Note that the verse is
otherwise **in full agreement** with the Vulgate — this is a plate defect, not a
divergent reading, and it takes no `[var:]`.

**⭐ 0016 @0222D — *percincti* against Migne's own *praecincti* at 0223B.**
Luke 12:35 is quoted twice in one chunk, eight lines apart, and Migne spells the
participle two different ways: *percincti* at 0222D, *praecincti* at 0223B. The
Vulgate has *praecincti*. **No `[sic:]`:** *percingo* is an attested (if rare)
verb, so this is Pattern 12's "real word that is merely wrong" class — rendered
as ordinary English ("girt about" against "girded" at the second occurrence) and
marked `[var:]` against the Vulgate, not `[sic:]` against the plate. The
self-correction inside a single chunk is what makes it evidential: whatever
Philip wrote, Migne's compositor knew the right form and set it eight lines
later.

**0012 @0213D — a misplaced full stop, preserved (Pattern 8, and 11638 §3).**
Migne prints *cum in me totum Deum, Verbum Patris. Dei Filium introspexi* — a
stop dropped into the middle of an appositional series, breaking *Verbum Patris*
from *Dei Filium*. The English carries the stop where the plate sets it, and the
resulting fragment stands. Intended reading, obvious: *…Verbum Patris, Dei
Filium introspexi*, "the Word of the Father, the Son of God, I beheld within".
**Not normalized** — English has an exponent for a stop in the wrong place, so
Pattern 9's normalization licence does not reach it, and 11638 ruled explicitly
that this construction stands.

## §5 · THREE THINGS THE MERGE SHOULD KNOW

**1. The mechanical quotation list does not see bare `[n:]`-noted quotations,
and one of my markers is on a span it never listed.** Rom 8:10 at 0226B
(*spiritus autem vivit propter **justitiam*** for the Vulgate's *propter
iustificationem*) is set with neither guillemets nor italics — it sits naked in
Philip's sentence with only its note — so it does not appear among the 80 spans
I was handed. It is nevertheless a genuine, expounded divergence: Philip repeats
*vivit propter justitiam* eight lines later as the phrase he is glossing, so the
Vulgate's *iustificationem* would take the keyword out of the argument (the 7a″
mechanism exactly). **Other ranges should read their chunks for `[n:]`-noted
spans the list could not catch.** I found this one only by reading the notes
against the prose after finishing the listed collation; there may be more of the
class in this work, and no script will surface them.

**2. Migne's citation sigla are unusually unreliable in this stretch — five
misnamed references in six chunks — and two of them are the SAME error twice.**
*(II Cor. IX)* is printed for 1 Cor 9:26 at 0216C and again for 1 Cor 9:24 seven
lines later. A repeated wrong siglum on one column is evidence about the
copy-text or the compositor's exemplar, not a slip, and it is worth the merge
counting the class across all 21 ranges before anyone proposes a
`citation-corrections.json` entry. **Nothing was corrected here.**

**3. 11632's marking practice is functional, not sequential — the brief's open
question, answered for this range.** Italics = the Song lemma under exposition
(plus marginal page numbers and printed locators); guillemets = every other
quotation, *including the Song itself when quoted as a proof-text from elsewhere
in the book* (Cant 4:11 at 0220A, Cant 5:11 at 0226A, Cant 2:14 at 0226C — all
guilleted, while the lemma five lines away is italic). If the whole work holds to
this, **11632 has no seam to find**, and the 419/635 corpus counts in the brief
reflect a two-channel system running in parallel from column one, not a switch.
The merge should test this against the ranges at the *Explicit/Incipit*
boundaries before concluding either way.
