## §1 · TYPOGRAPHY — no seam here either; §7 of the brief holds throughout

Both marks run side by side in every chunk of this range, divided by function
exactly as wave 1 described. The proof case recurs and is worth naming, because
it is the cleanest instance yet:

⭐ **0239C — Cant. 2:1 is GUILLEMETED while the running Song lemma two columns
earlier is italic.** *Quorum primus et praecipuus ait: « Ego flos campi
[n: (Cant. II)]. »* The chapter's own lemma, *Indica mihi ubi pascas, ubi cubes*,
is italic at 0237C, and the Song quoted as a proof-text from elsewhere in the
book takes « ». Same chunk, same column band, both marks. **11632 is not marked
by book, it is marked by role**, and no seam should be hunted here.

**Two printed locators sit inside guillemet runs with no `[n:]` wrapper**, the
0016 @0222B phenomenon, and both are reproduced exactly:

- **0232D** « Non sunt, ait, recordati foederis fratrum *(Amos I)*. » — italic
  locator, inside the quotation, not counted among the chunk's four notes
  (verified: III Reg. III, Ephes. III, Joan. X, Psal. LIV). Here the locator is
  **correct** (Amos 1:9), unlike the 0222C case.
- **0236D** *(Osee XII)* — an italic locator inside Philip's own prose (Hos 12:1,
  *Ephraim pascit ventum*), again with no `[n:]`.

**Guillemet asymmetries: none.** All six chunks balance (6/6, 2/2, 3/3, 6/6, 3/3,
17/17). Pattern 5 *inquit*/*ait*/*inquiunt* interruptions are frequent and every
one stays inside the marks.

**The `[n:]` does not always sit inside the closing ».** At 0235B
(*…filiis sceleratis! » [n: (Isa. I.)]*) and 0239B (*…manduces pascha? »
[n: (Marc. XIV.)]*) Migne prints the note **after** the closing guillemet, where
elsewhere he prints it before. Mirrored in both places; not tidied.

**Marginal source-edition page numbers** *129*–*135* run through the range, one
or two to a chunk, kept italic and in place.

**Capitalization mirrored, per brief §7a.** *Virgo*/*virgo* alternates within
single paragraphs (0229D–0230A has *virginem*, *virgo*, *virginis* lower case in
the middle of a chapter that is otherwise *Virgo*; 0231B *virgo prudens*; 0235D
*conversa virgo*) and the English follows word by word — "the Virgin" / "the
virgin", "the Bridegroom" / "the bridegroom" (0229D *sponsi thalamus*). ⭐ The
sharpest case is **0238D**, where John 3:29 is guilleted with **lower-case**
*sponsam sponsus* inside a sentence whose surrounding prose has capital
*Sponsus* three times. Rendered "he that hath the bride is the bridegroom" inside
the marks, "the Bridegroom" outside.

## §3 · PATTERN 7 / PATTERN 8 — printed readings rendered as printed

**1. 0233D — a comma inside its own phrase, preserved.** Migne prints *non de
bono ut aiunt, opere, sed de blasphemia persequuntur*: the parenthetical *ut
aiunt* has been dropped between the adjective and its noun, and the commas set
around the wrong words. The English carries the interruption at exactly that
point — "not for a good, as they say, work, but for blasphemy" — and reads as
oddly as the Latin does. Pattern 8; not normalized, because English has an
exponent for a comma in the wrong place.

**2. 0238A — *divisisque ab invicem turris, non consummatur magnitudinis
infinitae*.** The ablative absolute *divisisque ab invicem* has **no noun
anywhere in the sentence** to agree with (the sense requires the builders, or the
tongues, neither of which is in the nominative or ablative here), and *turris*
stands against a genitive *magnitudinis infinitae*. Rendered as closely as the
printed words allow — "and, they being divided one from another, a tower of
infinite greatness is not completed" — with "they" supplied only as English
grammar's minimum. Conjecture: an ablative such as *linguis* or *artificibus*
lost from the phrase. **Not repaired, not marked** — a real construction with a
missing referent is not carried type.

**3. 0238B — *in qua filii Israel Deo non tibi, sed Moloch superstitione misera
deferebant*.** As printed, the children of Israel paid honour **to God**, and
then "not to thee, but to Moloch" — which contradicts itself inside six words,
since the *tu* addressed throughout the chapter is God. Rendered literally,
contradiction included: "wherein the children of Israel paid honour to God, not
to thee, but to Moloch." Conjecture, near-certain: ***non Deo tibi, sed Moloch***
— "not to thee, their God, but to Moloch" — i.e. *non* has migrated one word to
the right. **Not repaired in the text** (brief §9: never translate a word as
something other than what it means to rescue a sentence), and no marker, because
every word printed is a real Latin word correctly inflected.

**4. 0235D — `[sic: *a beo*]`, the one `[sic:]` in the range.** Migne prints
*conversa virgo ad Sponsum vult **a beo** qui sint ejus plenius edoceri*. *A beo*
is not Latin: the space has migrated one letter to the right in *ab eo*, which is
Pattern 10's split-type class (the defect is the division, so the whole run is
carried, *a* included). Carried untranslated in italics inside `[sic: …]` with
the sentence built around it — "wills to be more fully taught [sic: *a beo*] who
are his." Intended reading, certain: ***ab eo***, "by him", the *Sponsum* named
four words earlier. Rendering "by him" and saying so in a crux was considered and
rejected: it puts a considered-looking reading on the page where the plate has
none.

**Negation audit.** Every *non / nec / neque / nisi / ne / nunquam / nullus /
nihil / nondum / necdum* in the six Latin chunks was matched against the English
on a second pass, with particular attention to the dense clusters at 0232D–0233A
(*non verentur… in nullo miserentur… noluerunt… non sorori exhibent, non denique
homini*), 0233B–C (*nec dilexit… nec sensu mollitur… nec Scripturae testimoniis,
nec naturae imperiis… nec Deum timent, nec hominem reverentur… nec virginem in
conceptu, nec post partum virginem*), 0236A–B (*non verbo, non lingua, non
solo strepitu… non in omnibus locis, non in cunctis pascuis… non habere… non
senserit abhorrere*) and the whole of 0237D–0238B, which is a chain of five
successive *non pascis* denials. **No negation was dropped and none supplied.**
The two places where carrying one makes the English read strangely are §3.3 above
and 0236A *quae vitalem succum noverit non habere* / *quae a vita non senserit
abhorrere* — a double negative Philip means positively; both are carried.

## §5 · THE THREE THINGS THE MERGE SHOULD CARRY

**1. ⭐⭐ Cant. 1:5 prints *nigra sum* for the Vulgate's *fusca sim* (0230D), and
this is the range's most important datum.** Brief §5 rules *nigra* = black and
*fusca* = swarthy, strictly distinct, *fusca* never "black" — which means the
Vulgate's Cant. 1:5 is **not** a blackness verse and Migne's is. Philip's CAPUT
XIX–XX are four columns of *nigredo* built on it, and the whole *nigra sed
formosa* architecture of Capp. XVII–XXI depends on the colour word being the same
one as in v. 4. **If another range meets Cant. 1:5, expect *nigra sum* and do not
conform it** — and if any range meets *fusca*, that is a fact worth reporting,
because it would mean Migne is inconsistent with himself across the work.

**2. All nineteen `[n:]`-only spans in this range were Philip's own prose, not
quotations — and the class is still worth every minute it costs.** Wave 1 found
Rom 8:10 in this class and it was a genuine expounded divergence; I found none.
That is the useful result to report, not a null one: it tells the merge that the
class's hit rate is low but that the reading pass is cheap, and it means the
range's six `[var:]` markers all sit on spans Migne himself marked. **Two of the
nineteen are nevertheless worth the index's attention** because they are
citation-practice data rather than variants: the Ps 40:10 + Ps 54:14 fusion at
0234B under a single *(Psal. LIV)* note, and the Ps 110:2 gloss at 0239B where
Philip writes *voluntates **meas*** for the psalm's *eius* while glossing a
different psalm.

**3. 11632's two-channel typography holds through CAPUT XXIV, and 0239C is the
cleanest proof in the work so far.** Cant. 2:1 (*Ego flos campi*) is guilleted as
a proof-text in the same chunk whose own Song lemma stands in italics — same
chapter, same speaker, same book, different marks, because the *roles* differ.
Three wave-1 agents converged on this and my six chunks add nothing against it.
**Two subsidiary facts for the merge's typographic sweep**, both new here: Migne
prints the `[n:]` **outside** the closing » at 0235B and 0239B (elsewhere always
inside), and he prints two locators **inside** guillemet runs with no `[n:]`
wrapper at all (*(Amos I)* 0232D, and *(Osee XII)* 0236D in bare prose). Neither
is an asymmetry and neither was tidied, but a script counting notes by their
position relative to guillemets will mis-bin them.
