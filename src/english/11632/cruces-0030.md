## §1 · THE TERM DECISION THE MERGE MUST RATIFY — *muraenula* stays Latin

⭐⭐ **This is the most consequential thing in the range and it is work-wide, not
range-local.** *Caput* VIII (chunk 0032) expounds Cant. 1:10 *Muraenulas aureas
faciemus tibi, vermiculatas argento*, and **its entire argument is that the word
names a fish**:

> *Muraenula piscis est, qui nutritus in aquis… Cujusmodi piscem forte Sponsus
> promittere Virgini videtur… quod intellectum ad litteram nobis penitus
> interdicit, cum eumdem piscem aureum vel argenteum esse dicit.*

Then the fish's shape carries the whole chapter — tail to mouth, a ring, meshes
of a net, a chain, Moses' head-and-tail offering, *ut integretur macula, fiat
annulus, muraenula retundatur*. **Rendering the lemma "chains of gold" (the
10379 solution) makes seventeen paragraphs explain nothing**: a chain that "is a
fish" is not an argument, it is a non-sequitur.

So this range follows **10804's precedent, which `translation-style.md` names as
correct for this very word**: ***muraenula* / *muraenulae* stays in italic
Latin**, in the lemma and in the prose alike (17 instances in 0032). This is the
Song ruling's case 2 — *the gloss IS the etymology*, the *stylus*/*epistylium*
and *murenulae*/*ferculum* class — and not a Pattern 12 carry-through, so **no
`[sic:]` and no crux-per-instance**.

⚑ **The lemma recurs later in the work and every later agent must render it the
same way.** Within a work: one voice (the style file's own boundary clause). If
the merge prefers "little lampreys" or "chains", the change is mechanical, but it
must be made *everywhere* or *Caput* VIII becomes incoherent on its own page.

**The companion decision, and it went the other way:** *vermiculatas argento* is
**translated**, as "vermiculated with silver". The gloss chain here runs
*vermiculat* → *vermiculatio* → *vermilio* → *vermiculus* → *vermis* (Ps. 21:7
*Ego autem sum vermis*), and English happens to carry the whole chain visibly —
vermiculate / vermiculation / vermilion / little worm / worm. Nothing is lost,
so nothing is left in Latin. Brief §5's *candidatio* rule governs: italic Latin
is only for words with no clean English equivalent, and this one has one.

## §3 · THE FIVE `[var:]`, IN FULL

**1. ⭐⭐ Ps. 11:7 at 0264C — *eloquium* SINGULAR, and it is `[n:]`-only.**
Migne prints *Habetis quia **eloquium** Domini **eloquium castum**, argentum igne
examinatum, probatum terrae, purgatum septuplum* `[n: (Psal. XI)]`. The Vulgate
reads the **plural** *Eloquia Domini, **eloquia casta***. **The singular is the
word Philip goes on to expound**, twice, within eight lines: *Petri **eloquium**
ignis non sonorum… Joannis vero **eloquium** non satis lucidum invenitur.* His
whole point is that one man's speech and another man's speech are each assayed
like silver, and the plural would dissolve the comparison into a general
predication about scripture.

⛔ **This span is set in NEITHER guillemets NOR italics** — it is the class the
brief warns about at §8 and that cruces-0012 §5.1 found Rom. 8:10 in. The
extractor listed it only because the note is attached; it could not know where it
began, and the opening is *eloquium*, not *Habetis*.

**2. Isa. 43:2 at 0264C — two words, and Philip's next clause uses both.**
Migne: « **Si transieris**, ait, **per ignem**, non combureris; et flamma **non
nocebit** in te ». Vulg.: *cum **ambulaveris in igne**, non combureris, et flamma
non **ardebit** in te.* (*transieris* has migrated from the preceding water
clause, *cum transieris per aquas*.) The marker matters because the sentence
Philip builds on it reads *illaesum **transiens***, "passing through **unharmed**"
— which picks up *transieris* and *nocebit* precisely, and neither of which is in
the Vulgate's wording of the verse.

**3. Lam. 3:65 at 0268A — tense, plus an added vocative.** Migne: « **Domine**,
ait, **dedisti** eis scutum cordis, laborem tuum ». Vulg.: ***Dabis** eis scutum
cordis, laborem tuum* — future, no vocative. The tense is material in the place
Philip puts it: the verse is the third of three proof-texts (*utquid dereliquisti
me* · *Laboravi sustinens* · this) adduced to show that the incarnate life was
*non quietis haec habitatio, sed laboris* — an accomplished labour, not a promised
one. Same class as the 0193C Jer. 31:22 marker in wave 1.

**4. Prov. 8:17 at 0273B — *Ergo* for *Ego*.** Migne: « **Ergo**, inquit,
diligentes me diligo ». Vulg.: ***Ego** diligentes me diligo.* Under Pattern 5
the interrupting *inquit* sits inside the quotation, so *Ergo* is the quotation's
first word, not Philip's connective outside it. Rendered "Therefore" as printed;
the marker records the emphatic pronoun the received text has and this printing
does not. (This is the one marker in the range where the divergence is most
likely a compositor's transposition rather than a copy-text reading — but
Pattern 14 forbids speculating about *why*, so the note states both readings and
nothing more.)

**5. Luke 8:46 at 0273B — *sensi* for *novi*.** Migne: « Tetigit me, inquit,
aliquis: ego **enim sensi** virtutem de me exisse ». Vulg.: *nam ego **novi**
virtutem de me exiisse* — "I **know**", not "I **felt**". Material here because
the verse is adduced for a *touch*, and the paragraph around it is built on
sensation: *cum **tangitur** affectu placito diligentis… qui si **tactu** et amore
digne credentium **compungatur***. The Vulgate's *novi* would put knowledge where
Philip's argument needs feeling.

## §4 · PATTERN 7 AND PATTERN 10 — four `[sic:]`, and what was NOT marked

**The four carried runs. None of the four is ALSO translated in the running
text** (brief §8's named failure).

1. **0030 @0256C `[sic: *a vida*]`** — *et alter alterum non fastidit, sed fauce
   semper **a vida** satiatur.* **Pattern 10, split type**: *a vida* is not a
   Latin phrase (*vida* is not a word), and the run is *avida*, agreeing with
   *fauce* — "with an ever-greedy throat". The whole broken run is carried,
   spacing included; the English reads "with a throat ever [sic: *a vida*] is
   satisfied" and does **not** also supply "greedy".
2. **0031 @0260B `[sic: *munos*]`** — *Ipsa prior, ipsa felicior accipit grande
   **munos***. Read ***munus***, certain (*grande munus*). Carried untranslated;
   the English does not also say "gift".
3. **0032 @0263A `[sic: *cam*]`** — *nulla adversitas a proposito **cam**
   praevalet removere*. Read ***eam***, certain. The English reads "no adversity
   prevails to remove [sic: *cam*] from her purpose" and supplies no pronoun.
4. **0035 @0273A `[sic: *idco*]`** — ***idco** forte divinus sermo vineas
   appellavit*. Read ***ideo***, certain. The English does not also say
   "therefore".

**Rendered as printed, no marker (real words, merely wrong or merely odd) —
conjectures live here and NOT in the text:**

- **0263C, *fornax exstitit et incudo*.** The slot wants the nominative *incus*;
  *incudo* is a real word (1 sg. of *incudo*, "I forge on an anvil") and cannot
  fill it. Migne himself sets the correct form two columns later (*tanquam in
  **incude** macula tunditur*, 0265A). Rendered "anvil" as an inflectional error
  under Pattern 12's "real word merely wrong" clause; ⚑ **if the merge judges
  *incudo* a non-word in this slot, the fix is one `[sic:]`** and I flag it
  rather than defend it.
- **⚠ 0265A, *non viandi completio, non gaudium ei acceptio, sed promissio dat
  coronae*.** **This clause does not construe as printed** and it is the passage
  in the range most likely to be quietly repaired. *gaudium* is accusative in a
  list of nominatives, and *coronae* cannot be the object of *dat*. Rendered as
  closely as the printed words allow — "not the completing of the wayfaring, not
  joy, the receiving of it for her, but the promise gives of the crown" — which
  is meant to read wrong. Conjecture: *non gaudii ei acceptio… sed promissio dat
  coronam*. **No emendation made, none smuggled into the English.**
- **0262C, *Inquieta undarum profunditas… non sufficiunt admirari, quod festinant
  adversus Virginem… congregari*.** Rendered actively ("cannot wonder enough
  that they hasten…"), the waves personified — which is what *obstupescunt
  attendentes* eleven words later requires. A passive reading ("are not enough to
  be wondered at") is possible; logged, not adjudicated in the text.
- **0266B, *nec de utero altero concepisse*.** Rendered "nor had conceived of
  another womb". Odd as printed (*de viro altero* is the obvious sense); a real
  word, so rendered, not carried.
- **0273B, *temptationis oculis*** — see §2, 0035 table.
- **0274A, *in luctu fortior habeatur*.** Rendered "the stronger **in the
  wrestling**": *luctus* for *lucta* is attested medieval usage (Pattern 9, no
  marker), and the paragraph is unambiguous — *athletam strenuum, cum recens
  accingitur **in agone***, with the wrestler's oil three lines above; the same
  chunk-neighbour uses *lucta* correctly (0270A, *sopita **lucta** carnis*).
- **0273B, *volverit aut valuerit perscrutari*.** Rendered "shall have turned it
  over or been able to search it out" — *volvere* "to turn over in the mind" is
  good Latin and construes with *ipsum*. The rhyme partner makes ***voluerit***
  the likely intention; not read that way, logged here.
- **0272B, Migne's misplaced full stop, PRESERVED with its lower case.** He
  prints *potius quam secunda, ut aiunt**. qui** viderunt, vini peroptimi tam
  fecunda*. The English reads "…rather first than second, as they say. who have
  seen it, so fruitful of the very best wine…". Brief §5's ruling is explicit —
  **lowercase after a misplaced stop STANDS**, because English has an exponent for
  it — and 11638 §3 and cruces-0012 §3 both settled the construction. ⚠ **Do not
  churn this.**
- **0272B, the second stop, likewise preserved**: *ut ejus gaudium excellentius
  caeteris liquido cognoscatur**.** Botrus, de quo sermo est…* — a stop cutting a
  purpose clause from the main clause it depends on. Two sentences in the English,
  as printed.

## §5 · TYPOGRAPHY AND CAPITALIZATION — this range crosses no seam either

**Brief §7 holds without exception across cols 0256C–0274C, and this range adds
two more instances of the decisive proof.** Guillemets carry every non-lemma
scripture quotation (42 runs); italics carry the running Song lemma, printed
locators and Migne's marginal page numbers *146*–*155*. **The Song quoted as a
proof-text takes guillemets** — « Cum esset, inquiens, Rex in accubitu suo
`[n: (Cant. I)]` » at 0270B and « Tenui eum, nec dimittam `[n: (Cant. III)]` » at
0271A, both guilleted while the italic lemma sits a few lines away in the same
column. That is now Cant. 4:11 @0220A, 5:11 @0226A, 2:14 @0226C, **1:12 @0270B
and 3:4 @0271A** — five instances, three ranges, one rule. **11632 has no seam
and no one should go looking for one.**

**Guillemet asymmetries: none in this range.** All six chunks balance and every
pair is 1:1 with the Latin twin. Pattern 5 *inquit / ait / inquiens / ait sacerdos*
interruptions are very dense in this stretch — Philip drops one into roughly
every second quotation — and **every one stays inside the marks**, in the italic
lemma runs (*Cum esset,* inquit, *Rex in accubitu suo*) exactly as in the
guillemeted ones.

**Two printed locators inside guillemet runs, neither wrapped in `[n:]`**:
*(ibid)* at 0260D and *(Matth. [0267C] XVI)* at 0267B — the latter with a column
anchor inside it. Both reproduced exactly, italics and all; neither counts toward
`noteCount` (verified against the body).

⚑ **§7a mirroring — the drift point — held word by word.** The range is full of
mixed case inside single sentences and none of it was regularized:
*Virgini… **virgo** diligens hilarescit* (0258A) · *cum ea **sponsus** est*
(0258B, lower case in a chunk that capitalizes *Sponsus* eleven times) · *in utero
**virginis** Deus homo* (0259C) · *Patrem cum **filio** et **sanctum** aeque
**Spiritum*** (0265B) · *Sponsae **virgini** loquens* (0265B) · *nardus
**virginis*** (0268A) beside *Virginis* throughout 0266 · *Dilectum vero suum
**virgo*** (0270A) · *amore **sponsi*** (0269D) · *Bonus iste **Fasciculus***
(0270B, capital) · *nostra **virgo*** (0268D) and *hujus **virginis*** (0273D,
0274A) in a chapter that capitalizes *Virgo* nowhere after 0272A. The Cant. 1:13
lemma prints *Dilectus* capitalized once and lower case three times. **All
mirrored.** This is mechanically checkable against the Latin twin and I would
check it first in any review.

**Pattern 17.** Philip's own frame is plural throughout and takes *you*
(*accepistis* 0259C, *vobis videtur* 0259D and 0263C, *nostis* 0261A and 0264A,
*Videtis* 0258A, 0263C, 0269C, *Habetis* 0264C, *Nostis* 0269A, *legistis* 0270C,
*accepistis* 0273A). Every second-person singular is inside a quotation and every
one is singular in the Latin — *vincula colli **tui**, captiva filia Sion*; *si
**transieris**… non nocebit in **te***; *dereliquisti me*; *dedisti eis… laborem
**tuum***; *consentiens adversario **tuo**, dum **es** in via*; *faciemus **tibi***
— so *thou* throughout. The one plural inside a quotation is Matt. 11:29
*Tollite jugum meum super **vos**, et **discite***, rendered *you*, sitting four
lines from the singular Isa. 52:2 — the number distinction is visible on the page
exactly as Pattern 17 intends.

**Ruled vocabulary held** (brief §5): *charitas* = **charity** throughout 0032,
including the doublet *dilectio sive charitas* (0263B) which is split "love, or
charity"; *misericordia* = **mercy** (0266D, 0273C); *torcular* = **winepress**
(0263D, 0272B); *species* = **spice** (0269A, of myrrh); *adolescentulae* =
**maidens** (0265C, 0274B); *recti*, *cellaria*, *clementia*, *nigra/fusca/formosa*
do not occur. *Sponsus* = Bridegroom, *Sponsa* = Bride, *unguentum* = ointment,
*nardus* = nard (and *nardus pistica* = spikenard at 0268C, where Migne's own
Latin distinguishes them).

**One new fixed equivalence this range had to set**, offered to the merge:
***accubitus* = "repose"**, with *accubuit* / *accumbens* / *accubitui* rendered
"reposed" / "reposing" / "his repose" throughout *Caput* IX. The chapter turns on
the single root — *in accubitu suo* → *in meo utero Rex ille gratanter accubuit*
→ *cujusmodi accubitus accumbenti complacuit* → *Sponso accumbente in dextera
divinae majestatis* — so a Douay-style "at his repose" for the lemma and
"reclined" for the verbs would have severed it. *recumbenti* (John 12, of Christ
at table, 0268C) is kept distinct as **"reclining"**, because Migne's Latin
distinguishes it.

## §7 · WHAT A LATER BLIND READ SHOULD LOOK AT FIRST

1. **0265A, *non viandi completio, non gaudium ei acceptio, sed promissio dat
   coronae*** (§4). The English is deliberately unconstruable because the Latin
   is. This is the single most likely place in the range for a well-meaning
   repair.
2. **0264C, the Ps. 11:7 `[var:]`** (§3.1). It is on an `[n:]`-only span, which
   means nothing on the page marked it as a quotation and nothing mechanical
   would ever have found it. If it is wrong, it is wrong invisibly — check the
   left edge of the quotation, which I judged to begin at *eloquium* and not at
   *Habetis*.
3. **The *Cum* / *Dum* lemma of Cant. 1:12** (§2, 0033). Four printings, no
   marker fired. Either the merge rules it Philip's Bible (brief §6 shape) or it
   is a compositor's habit; **the decision needs the other twenty ranges'
   evidence, not mine.**
4. **§1's *muraenula* decision.** It is the only term choice in this range that
   would be expensive to reverse late.
