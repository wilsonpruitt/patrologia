### 2.1 ⭐⭐ 0277B — Wisd. 7:26 + Heb. 1:3 fused, with *imago veritatis* for *imago bonitatis*

Migne prints, inside Philip's own *quia*-clause:

> *Pulcher, inquam, es, quia **candor lucis aeternae, speculum sine macula divinae
> majestatis**, paternae splendor gloriae, figura substantiae, **imago veritatis***
> `[n: (Hebr. I)]`

Three things at once:

1. **The note names Hebrews only**, but the first two members are **Wisdom 7:26**
   (*candor est enim lucis aeternae, et speculum sine macula Dei maiestatis, et
   imago bonitatis illius*), and only *splendor gloriae, figura substantiae* is
   Heb. 1:3. A two-book fusion under a one-book siglum.
2. **Wisdom's *imago bonitatis* is printed *imago veritatis*.** The change is
   real and is not a truncation: *bonitas* and *veritas* are different
   predications of the Son.
3. **Wisdom's *Dei maiestatis* is printed *divinae majestatis*.**

**Not marked**, and the reason is exactly brief §8a's: the run is set in neither
guillemets nor italics, it is grammatically a string of appositives inside
Philip's own sentence (the finite verb is his *es*), and the extractor's left
edge — *Pulcher, inquam, es* — is his prose, not the quotation's. **If the merge
rules the class markable, this is the site in this range that takes the marker,**
and *imago veritatis* is the word to put in it. Nothing in the surrounding
exposition picks the word up, which is why I do not think it rises to §3's level
even if the class were opened.

### 2.2 ⭐ 0278D — Prov. 31:19, *manum ad collum* where the sense requires *colum*

Migne prints:

> *illa mulier cujus fortitudo praedicabilis commendatur, **manum ad collum
> mittere**, et **fusum digitis apprehendere** memoratur* `[n: (Prov. XXXI)]`

The Vulgate of Prov. 31:19 reads *Manum suam misit ad **fortia**, et digiti eius
apprehenderunt fusum*. Two separate observations:

- ***ad collum*, "to the neck", cannot be right beside *fusum*, "the spindle".**

  doubled *l* makes it the neck.

  fortia* here.

**Rendered as printed** — "to put her hand to the neck, and to take hold of the

## §3 · THE FOUR `[var:]`, IN FULL

**1. ⭐ Luke 23:34 at 0284D — *ignosce* for *dimitte*, and *quia* for *non enim*.**
Migne: « Pater, inquit, ***ignosce*** illis, ***quia nesciunt*** quid faciunt ».
Vulg.: *Pater, **dimitte** illis: **non enim sciunt** quid faciunt.* The main verb
is a different word — *ignoscere*, "to pardon", against *dimittere*, "to remit" —
and it is the Old Latin form of the most famous sentence in the Passion, which is
precisely the 7a″ conformation hazard: the remembered English arrives before the
printed Latin is read. The marker matters here because the sentence Philip builds
round it reads *cum **manu misericordiae** spinarum turpitudinem abradebat* — a
hand of *pardon* scraping the thorns, not a debt remitted. Rendered "pardon them"
so that the divergence is visible in the English, per Pattern 7a's rule that a
`[var:]` may not elide what it exists to report. The negation is intact on both
sides (*non enim sciunt* / *nesciunt*) and is carried.

**2. ⭐⭐ Isa. 35:1 at 0285A — *germinabit* for *florebit*, and Philip expounds the
divergent verb twice.** Migne: « Laetabitur, inquit, deserta et invia, et
exsultabit solitudo; et ***germinabit*** quasi lilium ». Vulg.: *et **florebit**
quasi lilium.* **This is the range's clearest marker.** Within nine lines Philip
writes *quia juncta lilio quasi lilium **germinabit***, and then *spe fructus
exsultabit habens primitias **germinantes*** — the whole close of *Caput* XX runs
on budding-into-fruit, not on blossoming, and *florebit* would put the argument's
keyword back in a verse that no longer contains it. Everything else in the verse
agrees word for word (*deserta et invia*, *exsultabit solitudo*), which is what
makes the one substitution legible as a reading rather than a paraphrase.

**3. Acts 10:26 at 0286D — an added clause, *sicut et tu*.** Migne: « …Petrus
autem levavit eum dicens: Surge, et ego ipse homo sum, ***sicut et tu*** ». The
Vulgate ends the verse at *et ego ipse homo sum*; the comparative clause is in no
edition. It is material in its place: Philip's whole point is that Peter refused
adoration **by asserting parity with Cornelius**, and *sicut et tu* states the
parity the argument needs. (Also in the same span: Migne supplies the subject
*Cornelius*, which the Vulgate carries in the preceding verse, and prints
*levavit* / *autem* for *elevavit* / *vero* — neither marked.) ⚑ Note the marker's
content deliberately quotes the *Vulgate's* wording, not Migne's, so that it
cannot collide with Pattern 14's mirror test against the Latin twin.

**4. ⭐ Eph. 2:3 at 0288D — *Sumus* for *eramus*, present for imperfect.** Migne:
« ***Sumus***, inquit, natura filii irae ». Vulg.: *et **eramus** natura filii
irae, sicut et ceteri* — "we **were**". The tense is the point in the place
Philip puts it: the sentence he is proving reads *Nos autem miseros multo minores
angelis **culpa fecit**… **eo quod versum est vitium in naturam***, an argument
about our standing condition, not about a state Paul has left behind. The
Vulgate's past tense would make the proof-text say the opposite of the sentence
it is adduced for. ⚑ Cross-reference for the merge: **cruces-0024 recorded Eph.
2:3 as an unmarked weave at 0030** (*irae filius ex natura*). This is the same
verse returning as a guilleted proof-text with the tense changed — a second
attestation, and worth counting.

## §5 · PATTERN 7 / 10 / 12 — one `[sic:]`, and what was NOT marked

**1. 0283A — `[sic: *ooopertus*]`.** Migne prints *Deus est in homine mundae
carnis trabea **ooopertus***. Three *o*'s: not a word in any Latin, and therefore
Pattern 7's non-word class. Read ***coopertus***, "covered", certain from the
slot (*trabea* ablative of means) and from *opertorium* / *occultavit* five lines
away. **Carried untranslated in italics inside the marker, and the English does
NOT also supply "covered"** — the failure brief §8 names ("a `[sic:]` on a word
that was ALSO translated passes"). The clause reads "is God in man, [sic:
*ooopertus*] with the robe of clean flesh."

**Rendered as printed, no marker (real words, merely wrong or merely odd) —
conjectures live here and NOT in the text:**

- **⚠ 0287C, « Conservus tuus *suum*, et fratrum tuorum… ».** The Apoc. 19:10
  re-quotation has lost its only finite verb: ***suum*** stands where ***sum***
  belongs, and **Migne himself prints the correct *conservus enim tuus sum* nine
  lines above at 0287A**, in the full quotation of the same verse. So the
  conjecture is certain and the plate refutes itself on the same page. Even so,
  *suum* is a **real word**, which puts it in Pattern 12's explicit exclusion (the
  *fuges* / *munde* class: "rendered as ordinary English under Pattern 7, because
  they *can* be rendered"), so **no `[sic:]`.** The English reads « Thy
  fellow-servant his own, and of thy brethren that have the testimony of Jesus,
  adore God. » — verbless and wrong, deliberately, with Migne's commas preserved.
  ⛔ **This is the single most likely place in the range for a well-meaning
  repair.** ⚑ If the merge judges *suum* a non-word *in this slot* (the
  *incudo* question cruces-0030 §4 left open), the fix is one `[sic:]` and I flag
  it rather than defend it — the two cases should be decided together.
- **0283C, *nonnullas videtur sentire functiones*.** *functio* has no sense here
  that construes cleanly against its rhyme-partner *laesiones*; medieval usage
  (*onus, munus*) would give "burdens". **Rendered neutrally and literally as
  "workings"**, from *fungi*; conjectures *concussiones* / *fluctuationes* are
  possible and not supplied. Logged rather than adjudicated in the text.
- **0275A, *ut humilem spiritu reddat virginitatis flosculus clariorem, flore
  claram virgineo, spiritu humilis pulchriorem*.** A chiasmus with only one
  finite verb: *virginitatis flosculus* is the first subject, and the second is
  ***spiritu humilis*** — a nominative adjective, where *spiritus humilis* would

- **0275B, *nec videtur diligenti **lectio** haec justo amplius requisita*.**

  Horatian tag that follows (*juxta poetam decies repetita* — *A.P.* 365, of a

  Logged, no emendation.
- **0279B, *quem tu manu gratiae, ego laboris et industriae **serenavi***.** The
  verb is first-person singular and governs a second-person subject as well

  adorned" carries both persons; the Latin zeugma cannot be reproduced exactly.
  Logged so the smoothing is on the record.
- **0288D, *ut sancta confirmatio aeque vestigio permaneret*.** *vestigio* has no

## §7 · RULED VOCABULARY (brief §5) — held, plus three new fixed equivalences

**Held:** *amica mea* = **"my friend"** (0274C, 0283C, 0285C — the Cant. 2:2
occurrence the brief predicts) · *misericordia* = **mercy** (0279B, 0282B ×2,
0284C) kept distinct from *clementia* (does not occur) · *charitas* (does not
occur) · Pattern 3 as above · *Sponsus* = Bridegroom, *Sponsa* = Bride, *Virgo* =
Virgin, *Deitas* / *deitas* = Godhead. *muraenula*, *vermiculatas*, *nigra /
fusca / formosa*, *cellaria*, *recti*, *adolescentulae*, *torcular*,
*candidatio*, *tomus* do not occur in this range. **Cant. 1:12 does not recur
here, so this range contributes no evidence to brief §8c's *Cum* / *Dum* count.**

**Three new fixed equivalences this range had to set**, offered to the merge:

1. ⭐ ***lectulus* = "little bed", *lectus* = "bed", never interchanged.** Forced
   by 0279B: *non tamen Virgo **lectum**, sed magis **lectulum** nominavit; quo
   nomine legem illam… adimplevit, qua tam verbo quam opere humilia tenere
   consuevit.* The diminutive **is** the humility argument. Fourteen instances
   across 0037–0038. **The lemma is Cant. 1:15 and will recur.**
2. ***laquearia* = "panelled ceilings"** (not "rafters", Douay's word), because
   *Caput* XVII glosses them as laid *beneath* the beams and stretched wall to
   wall as a binding — a rafter is the *tignum*, which is the other half of the
   same lemma and must stay "beam".
3. ***malus* = "the apple tree"** throughout *Caput* XXI, including where Migne
   writes neuter *malum* (0287B) — the chapter's three-term proof (*umbra,
   fructus, odor*) is about the tree, not the fruit, and *poma* is separately
   "apples" at 0286A.

Also fixed within the range for consistency: *accubitum* = **"repose"** (0278C),
following cruces-0030's ruling · *solitudo* = **"solitude"** throughout 0285A–B,
because Isa. 35:1's *solitudo* and Philip's *nostra… solitudo* are the same word
and the gloss depends on it · *scrupulus* / *scrupulosa* = **"scruple" /
"scrupulous"** (0280D), keeping the pun between the cypress's knotless wood and
scrupulous obedience · *convalles* = **"deep valleys"** against *valles* =
"valleys", which Migne's own *Valles, imo convalles* distinguishes.
