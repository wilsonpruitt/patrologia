## §2 · THE TWO `[var:]`, IN FULL

**1. ⭐ Eph. 2:3 at 0307C — *sumus* for *eramus*, and the tense is the argument.**
Migne prints « Natura, » inquiens, « filii ***sumus*** irae ». The Vulgate reads
*et **eramus** natura filii irae, sicut et ceteri* — **imperfect, not present.**
The marker is fired because the tense is precisely what Philip is arguing:

> *Quod enim primo parenti primitus delinquenti solo accidit **vitio non
> natura**, nobis posterioribus longe miserioribus inest, **tam vitio quam
> natura***… *et Paulus nimis verax est.*

His whole point is that what was accidental in Adam is **still, now**,
constitutive in us — and the proof-text is adduced as a present-tense
predication about the speaker and his hearers. The Vulgate's *eramus* places it
in the pre-baptismal past, which is the opposite of the claim; and Philip's very
next sentence apostrophizes Paul as though the Apostle had just said something
he wishes were untrue (*Utinam esses, Paule, vir non habens spiritum*), which
only works if Paul has spoken in the present. Rendered "we **are** children of
wrath"; `[var:]` records the received reading.

**2. ⭐⭐ Isa. 42:2 at 0318D — *in plateis*, which is MATTHEW's word, in a
chapter built on the street.** Migne prints « Non audietur **in plateis** vox
ejus » under `[n: (Isa. XLII)]`. Isaiah 42:2 reads *non clamabit… nec audietur
vox eius **foris*** — "abroad", "outside". The phrase *in plateis* is
Matt. 12:19's rendering of the same prophecy (*neque audiet aliquis **in
plateis** vocem eius*).

**This is fired, and the reason is 7a″ exactly: *platea* is the keyword of the
whole chapter.** *Caput* IX turns on the turtledove's voice being secret rather
than public, and Philip states it with that noun three times around the
quotation:

> *nuntiabat, **non turbis in platea**, sed apostolis in secreto* (0318B) ·
> *quem prorsus turbis absconditum foro gaudentibus vel **platea*** (0318C) ·
> *vox secreta, quam non capit turba fremens, **fori** litibus et tumultibus
> assueta* (0318C)

— and then adduces the prophecy as proof, *De qua voce scriptum est*. Conforming
the lemma to Isaiah's *foris* would leave three sentences arguing about the
**street** from a proof-text that no longer mentions one.

The marker states both readings and names the second witness; **it does not
speculate about why they differ** (Pattern 14). It is worth the merge's notice
that this is the *third* attested instance in 11632 of the class cruces-0024
named at 0240B — **a note citing one book while the words printed are another
book's** (Luke 9 cited / Matthew 8 worded; Deut. 32 cited / Ps. 91 worded; now
Isa. 42 cited / Matt. 12 worded). Here, unlike the other two, the span is
**guilleted**, so a marker is available and was used.

## §6 · TYPOGRAPHY — brief §7 holds, with one counter-instance worth counting

**Guillemets carry the adduced authority; italics carry the lemma, printed
locators and marginal numbers, across all six chunks.** 37 guilleted runs, and
every one is scripture adduced as proof. Italics carry: the running Cant. 2:8–13
lemma and its piecemeal re-quotations, the counterfactual lemma at 0311A
(*En ipse stat intra parietem*), the printed locator *(Luc. [0317D] XXIV.)* at
0317C, and the marginal source-edition numbers *174*–*182*, continuous with
cruces-0030's *146*–*155*.

⚑ **The counter-instance, and it cuts against the functional rule rather than
for it:** at 0314A Philip adduces **Sallust** (*Cat.* 20.4) explicitly as an
external authority — *non solum praesens veritas Canticorum, sed et **verax
sententia** digne concinit **ethnicorum*** — and Migne prints *Idem, inquit,
velle et idem nolle, ea demum firma amicitia est* in **neither guillemets nor
italics**. Under the widened rule cruces-0024 §1a proposed ("« » = a quotation
adduced as an authority, whatever its source") this should have been guilleted,
as the Delphic oracle and Virgil are at 0251D–0253A. **It is not.** Nothing was
supplied. The merge should weigh this beside cruces-0030's Ovid case (also
unmarked): the widened rule may need to be stated as *permissive* rather than
predictive — Migne guillemets non-scriptural authorities **sometimes**, and
never italicizes them in prose.

⭐ **A guillemet pair Migne himself splits.** 0307C prints « Natura, » inquiens,
« filii sumus irae ». This is the construction Pattern 5 forbids *us* to
introduce, and here the plate has it. Reproduced 1:1 — two pairs for one

## §8 · WHAT A LATER BLIND READ SHOULD LOOK AT FIRST

1. **0306B, *virgini auferre voluit et conferre*** (§3). The English is
   deliberately self-contradictory because the Latin is. One letter (*afferre*)
   makes it smooth, which is precisely why it must not be changed.
2. **0316C, *multa eum munditia suffocavit*** (§3). Second most repairable
   sentence in the range; the conjecture *immunditia* is in the crux and must
   stay there.
3. **0318D, the Isa. 42:2 `[var:]`** (§2.2). If it is wrong it is wrong on the
   most consequential span in the range. The check is whether *in plateis* really
   is absent from every edition of Isa. 42:2 and present in Matt. 12:19 — it is,
   but it is the claim to test.
4. **0304A, `[sic: *signa*]`** (§3.1). The only marker in the range whose *class*
   is arguable (Pattern 10 vs Pattern 12). I have stated the case both ways.
5. **§4.2, the *cancelli* = "lattice" decision.** It is the one term choice here
   that would be expensive to reverse late, and it is the only place in the range
   where a natural English word (*chancel*) was declined on purpose.
