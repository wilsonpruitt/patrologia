## §1 · ⭐⭐ THE FOUR FINDINGS THE MERGE SHOULD CARRY

### 1.1 · ⭐⭐ Cant. 2:13 at 0323C — the lemma prints ***Sponsa mea*** for the Vulgate's ***speciosa mea***, and Philip's next sentence proves it is his copy-text

The head lemma of *Caput* XI is printed:

> *Surge,* inquit, *amica mea, **Sponsa mea**, et veni, columba mea, in
> foraminibus petrae, in cavernis maceriae.*

The Vulgate reads *surge amica mea **speciosa mea** et veni columba mea*. Migne's
*Sponsa mea* is not a slip, and **the proof is internal and needs nothing outside
the work** — the whole chapter exists to explain the substituted word, and Philip
states the substitution himself, in the second sentence, as an observation about
the text in front of him:

> *Quam igitur **amicam, columbam, formosam**, cum primo* Surge *diceret,
> nominavit, iterum* surge *dicens, **amicam et columbam** nihilominus nominavit;
> **Sponsae nomen interserens, quod nusquam eum supra recolo nominasse***
> (0323D)

He is collating his own two printings of the invitation: at Cant. 2:10 the three
epithets were *amica mea, columba mea, formosa mea*; here, he says, two of the
three return and **the name of Bride is inserted in the third slot** — the slot
the Vulgate fills with *speciosa*. He then notes that the name is new to the book
(*nusquam eum supra recolo nominasse*), and the remaining four columns are an
exposition of *why the Bridegroom reaches for the bride-name at the moment he
asks her to leave contemplation for public work*:

> *hoc illi nomen objicit quo eam novit ad obedientiam plus teneri* (0323D) ·
> *Cum igitur audit Virgo **sponsam** diligentius se vocari… intelligit non se
> Sponso debere vel in modico refragari* (0324A) · *Sponsam, inquit, volo
> nonnunquam adolescentulis demonstrari* (0324B)

Conform the lemma to *speciosa mea* and *Caput* XI has no subject. **`[var:]`
fired**, naming the Vulgate reading only; it does not quote Migne's own words,
per Pattern 14's mirror test.

⚑ This is §6-shaped evidence (Zech. 4:14, Gen. 3:19): a divergence the author
knows is there and builds on. It is the strongest such instance so far in a
*lemma* rather than a proof-text.

### 1.2 · ⭐ Cant. 2:14 at 0323C — *in cavernis maceriae*, PLURAL, and the plural is what is expounded

The same lemma prints *in **cavernis** maceriae* against the Vulgate's *in
**caverna** maceriae*, singular. The plural is not incidental: every one of
Philip's six later handlings is plural, and the argument turns on there being
**many** hollows, one per angelic office —

> *Cujus **cavernae** et internae velut mansiunculae vel **recessus**, quos
> patentes invenit quisquis…* (0324D) · *Digne ergo eidem Virgini **cavernae**
> maceriae non clauduntur, sed recessu patulo ad capiendum eam latius
> diffunduntur* (0325C) · *ab antiquo in hac maceria **cavernae** patuerunt, ex
> quo inde angeli lapsu perdito corruerunt* (0325D) · *omnesque suos aditus,
> omnes illi exerit aperturas* (0325D)

The last of these is the decisive one: the hollows are the **gaps left by the
fallen angels**, which are necessarily many, and which men enter one by one.
A singular *caverna* makes the whole angelology unstatable. **`[var:]` fired.**

### 1.3 · ⭐ Isa. 56:10 at 0328C printed under `[n: (Isa. LI)]` — and the same siglum is CORRECT eleven columns later

Migne prints « canes muti `[n: (Isa. LI)]`, » — the words are **Isa. 56:10**
(*speculatores eius caeci omnes… **canes muti non valentes latrare***), and the
sentence continues, outside the guillemets, *dormientes et amantes somnia*, which
is the same verse (*videntes vana, dormientes et amantes somnia*).

⭐ **And `[n: (Isa. LI)]` is used again at 0332D–0333A, where it is right**:
« animae meae: Incurvare, ut transeamus » is Isa. **51**:23. So within one range,
one siglum, one chapter number: **wrong once, right once** — the exact shape the
brief's §8b names for *(Psal. XCII)* at 0275B/0281A. Reproduced verbatim in both
places, corrected in neither.

⚠ The guillemets here wrap **only** *canes muti*, though the rest of the verse
runs on unmarked immediately after. Reproduced exactly; no marks supplied.

### 1.4 · ⭐ Migne prints the Vulgate reading in his OWN apparatus at 0321B — and that is why no `[var:]` fired there

2 Pet. 1:19 is printed:

> « Habemus firmiorem propheticum sermonem, cui benefacitis attendentes quasi
> lucernae lucenti in ***obscuro* [ *alias* caliginoso] loco** `[n: (II Petr. I)]`. »

The Vulgate reads *in **caliginoso** loco*. Migne's text has *obscuro*, and his
own bracketed variant supplies *caliginoso* — i.e. **the divergence is already
adjudicated on the page**, by the editor, in the printed apparatus. A `[var:]`
would tell the reader a second time and blur whose observation it is; this is the
`( sic )` reasoning of brief §10.5 and the Nicetas note-(c) precedent applied to
a variant bracket. **Logged, no marker.** The `[ *alias* caliginoso]` is
reproduced verbatim and untranslated, per the register rule on `[ *al.,* …]`.

⚑ Worth the merge's eye as a *class*: this is the first place in 11632 where
Migne's own apparatus, rather than our collation, is the witness to a Vulgate
divergence. If more `[ *alias* …]` brackets turn up in later ranges the merge
should rule the class once — my reading is that they never take a `[var:]`.

### 4.1 · The Sir. 15:9 weave at 0327C, and why the substituted word is worth the merge's minute

*quia et in ore peccatoris non est laus, sicut legitur, **pretiosa***. The
Vulgate reads *Non est **speciosa** laus in ore peccatoris*.

⚑ **The substituted word is the one the chapter cannot spare.** The clause
immediately before it is *si non antecesserit vocem **facies speciosa*** — and
*speciosa* is the chapter's own predicate of the Virgin's face (*decoris sui
merito concupitam*, *amator legitur **speciei***, *tantae placet merito
**speciei***). Had *speciosa* stayed in the quotation, Philip would have used one
word for the sinner's praise and the Virgin's face in adjacent clauses; *pretiosa*
keeps them apart.

Whether that is Philip's deliberate choice, his copy-text, or a compositor is
exactly the research question Pattern 14 forbids a marker to answer. **Logged,
no marker** — and the frame *sicut legitur* is his own, so §8a's left-edge
argument applies as well.
