## §1 · ⭐⭐ THE THREE FINDINGS THE MERGE SHOULD CARRY

### 1.1 · ⭐⭐⭐ Cant. 2:17 at 0338D — **Migne prints *Bethel* where the Vulgate has *Bether*, and the whole back half of the chapter is an etymology of the divergent word**

The head lemma of *Caput* XVIII is printed:

> *Revertere, dilecte mi: similis esto capreae hinnuloque cervorum, super montes
> **Bethel**.*

The Vulgate reads *revertere; similis esto, dilecte mi, capreae hinnuloque
cervorum super montes **Bether***. Two divergences, and they are of completely
different weight:

1. **The vocative is transposed** — *Revertere, dilecte mi: similis esto* for
   Vulg. *revertere: similis esto, dilecte mi*. Sense-neutral, and the
   re-quotation at 0340A (« Similis, inquit, esto capreae, hinnuloque cervorum »)
   drops *dilecte mi* altogether while Philip's own re-quotations at 0339A and
   0339C print *Revertere, dilecte mi* as a unit. Migne's own text is internally
   consistent; the Vulgate's is not being followed.
2. ⭐⭐ ***Bethel* for *Bether*** — and **this is the range's principal find**,
   because Philip does not merely quote the word, he *construes* it, at length,
   as the name of the Virgin's conscience:

> *Ut aiunt qui notitiam Hebraeorum nominum aestimant se habere… Beth, domus; el
> vero Deus interpretatur; et **Bethel domus Dei** Latino eloquio nominatur.
> **Domus autem Dei** non incongrue interna Virginis conscientia judicatur,
> thalamus scilicet spiritalis, qui Deo Dei Filio praeparatur…* (0341A–B)

and the identification then governs the remaining twenty lines of the chapter:
*Quae nimirum **domus** vel inhabitatio, jure dicitur montuosa* · *qui super
montes Virginis*. **Conform the lemma to *Bether* and the entire etymology, and
with it the chapter's conclusion, ceases to have anything to stand on.** Hebrew
*Bether* ("division", "cleft") yields nothing remotely like *domus Dei*.

`[var:]` fired at the head lemma, stating both readings and naming the witness;
it does not speculate about whether this is Philip's copy-text, an Old Latin
reading, or a compositor's letter (Pattern 14). ⚠ **The re-quotation at 0341A
also prints *Bethel*** (*Super montes, inquit, Bethel*), so the reading is
attested **twice** in the chunk, which puts it in the evidential shape of brief
§6's Zech. 4:14 and Gen. 3:19 — a copy-text reading, not a slip. **This is the
strongest §6-class candidate the work has produced since those two, and the merge
should treat it as one.**

### 1.2 · ⭐⭐ Matt. 7:8 at 0345B — Migne prints « **Incipit** et invenit » where the argument requires *accipit*, and the correct form stands twice within twenty words

*Caput* XX's whole close is an argument about the **tenses and the verbs Matthew
prints**. Philip has just quoted Matt. 7:7 (future throughout) and Matt. 7:8
(present, then future), and he says so explicitly:

> *Hoc de praesenti tempore. **Non enim dicit, accipiet et inveniet, sed «
> Incipit et invenit. »*** … *Non enim ait, « pulsanti » aperitur, sed «
> aperietur, »*

*Incipit* means "he begins". The antithesis Philip is drawing is
***accipiet*** (future, which the text does **not** say) against ***accipit***
(present, which it does), and the whole point collapses on the word printed.

⚑ **The decisive detail is internal.** Migne prints the correct *accipit* twice
inside the same paragraph — « Omnis qui petit **accipit**, et qui quaerit invenit
» at 0345A and « Omnis enim qui petit **accipit**, et qui quaerit invenit » at
0345B, the latter **five words before** the defective printing — and *accipiet*
three words before it. So within one column the plate has *accipit*, *accipiet*
and *Incipit*, and only the third is impossible.

**Rendered as printed** ("He beginneth and findeth"), which is meant to read
wrong, with a `[var:]` naming the received reading. **Not a `[sic:]`:** *incipit*
is a real word and Pattern 12's "real word merely wrong" clause keeps it on the
English side — the same call cruces-0042 made for *Adjuvo*/*Adjuro* at 0299D,
and this is the same shape one column later in the work.
⚠ **This is the sentence in the range most likely to be silently repaired**, and
the repair would be invisible: one letter restores a perfectly cogent argument.

### 1.3 · ⭐ Matt. 7:7 at 0345B — *et accipietis* for the Vulgate's *et dabitur vobis*, in the one place where the verb is the argument

> « Petite, ait, **et accipietis**, quaerite et invenietis; pulsate et aperietur
> vobis `[n: (ibid.)]`. »

Vulg. Matt. 7:7 (and Luke 11:9) read *Petite, **et dabitur vobis***. *Et
accipietis* is the wording of **John 16:24** (*petite et accipietis*).

Ordinarily this would be logged and left unmarked — both readings are future,
and nothing is asserted that the Vulgate does not assert. **It is marked because
of what Philip does with it four lines later**: *Non enim dicit, **accipiet** et
inveniet, sed « **Incipit** [i.e. *accipit*] et invenit »*. His antithesis is
between the **future *accipietis* of v. 7** and the **present *accipit* of v. 8**
— one verb in two tenses. With the Vulgate's *dabitur vobis* the pairing is
between two *different* verbs and the argument is materially weaker. This is
7a″'s test exactly: the exposition rests on a word, so the word is marked where
it diverges.

⚠ **I record this as a judgment rather than defend it.** If the merge reads
*accipietis*/*dabitur* as below Pattern 14's threshold, the fix is to delete one
`[var:]`; §1.2's marker is independent of it and stands either way.

### 3a · ⭐ A third counter-instance to brief §7's functional rule — **Ambrose, adduced by name, printed bare**

At 0337B Philip closes his *excursus/recursus* argument by citing Ambrose

text.

Father, not a *gentilis***: the earlier two could be explained away as Migne
declining to guillemet pagan matter. That explanation is now dead. **Brief §7's
"permissive, not predictive" formulation is confirmed a third time, and the merge
can treat the question as closed in the same way §7 closed the book-boundary
question.**

## §5 · CONVENTIONS SET OR HELD IN THIS RANGE

1. ⭐ **`lectulus` = "little bed" — HELD, and *Caput* XIX is where brief §5's
   ruling pays.** Cant. 3:1's *In lectulo meo* opens the chapter, and Philip's
   entire gloss is the **diminutive against a non-diminutive**: *cubilis
   amplitudinem in **brevem lectulum** non redegit, nec intra fines arctos
   **breviato lectulo** se coegit* · *Dilatavit enim **cubile** suum* · *in
   **cubili dilatato** cum illicitis amatoribus est polluta* · *unus cum illo
   spiritus **brevi tanquam lectulo** se angustat*. **A third bed-word is
   therefore needed and is fixed here: *cubile* = "couch"**, kept distinct from
   *lectulus* = "little bed" and *lectus* = "bed" (which does not occur in this
   range). Isa. 57:8's *dilatasti **cubile** tuum* accordingly reads "thou hast
   enlarged thy **couch**", and the antithesis short-little-bed / enlarged-couch
   survives intact into the English. **Offered to the merge as the third term of
   brief §5's pair.** ⚠ A fourth, *cubiculum* (0338C, *in secreto mentis
   **cubiculo***), is rendered "chamber".
2. ⭐ **`vicus` = "lane" · `platea` = "street" — matched to chunk 0051, NOT to
   Douay.** Cant. 3:2 prints *per vicos et plateas*, and Philip glosses the pair
   as narrow-against-broad: *Vicos arbitror illos dici, quos **rectitudo** fidei…
   dirigit certitudo, plateas vero quos provectae charitatis suavior excipit
   **latitudo***. Douay renders the lemma "the streets and the broad ways", which
   would have been the natural choice — **but chunk 0051 (cruces-0048's range)
   already renders Luke 14:21's *in plateas et vicos* as "into the streets and
   lanes"**, and within a work there is one voice. So *platea* = **street**
   throughout, consistent with 0051 and with 0318D's Isa. 42:2 « Non audietur
   **in plateis** » = "in the streets", and *vicus* = **lane**. ⚑ **This is the
   one cross-chunk reconciliation the merge must not undo**; reversing it would

3. ⭐ **`aspirare` = "to breathe (upon)" and `inclinari` = "to be bowed down" in
   the Cant. 2:17 lemma — a deliberate departure from Douay's "till the day break,
   and the shadows retire", and *Caput* XVII is the reason.** The chapter is one
   sustained play on the breath-root and the bowing-root, and both are load-bearing:
   - *spirare* / *aspirare* / *inspirare* / *conspirare* run **eleven times in
     one column** (0338A: *modo **spirat**… tunc **aspirat**… digne **spirat**…

     et nunc **spiratio**, et tunc magis **aspiratio**… Dies qua plenius
     **aspirante***). Four English verbs are fixed on the four Latin ones:
     **breathe / breathe upon / breathe into / conspire.** "Break" for *aspirare*
     would silently delete the argument.
   - *inclinari* is expounded as the shadows' **posture in punishment**, not
     their withdrawal: *quae die aspirante coguntur **inclinari**, **cervice
     conquassata**, poenis infernalibus aggravari* · *fasce duro graviter
     **inclinatur***. "Retire" makes the neck-shattering a non sequitur; **"be
     bowed down"** carries it.

   here for the first time), so no earlier chunk constrains it; but chunks
   0054–0059 print Cant. 2:16 and may print the *donec* clause as a cue.
   **Flagged for the merge as the one lemma word in this range where an earlier
   range could collide.**
4. **`caprea` = "roe" · `hinnulus (cervorum)` = "the fawn (of harts)" ·

## §7 · WHAT A LATER BLIND READ SHOULD LOOK AT FIRST

1. ⛔ **0345B, « Incipit et invenit »** (§1.2). The English is deliberately
   incoherent because the Latin is, and the correct *accipit* stands five words
   away on the plate — which is exactly what makes the repair *feel* authorized.
   One letter restores a cogent argument. **Do not.**
2. ⛔ **0342B, *Nec vox una*** (§4). Second most repairable; the conjecture *nox*
   is in the crux and must stay there.
3. ⛔ **0345B, the *nihil… non conferri, nihil… non differri* double negative**
   (§4). Under brief §9 the printed count is exactly what stands: two *nihil*,
   two *non*, and no others.
4. ⭐ **0338D/0341A, the Cant. 2:17 `[var:]` on *Bethel*** (§1.1). If it is wrong
   it is wrong on the most consequential span in the range. The claim to test is
   whether *Bether* is the reading of every edition of Cant. 2:17 — it is — and
   whether *Bethel* could be a compositor's letter rather than copy-text: **two
   printings three columns apart, plus four columns of etymology built on it, say
   no.**
5. **The 0345B Matt. 7:7 `[var:]` on *et accipietis*** (§1.3) is the one marker in
   the range whose *threshold* is arguable. I have stated the case both ways and
   would not defend it against a merge that reads it as sub-threshold.
6. **§5.2, the *vicus*/*platea* assignment.** It was decided by consistency with
   chunk 0051 rather than by Douay, and it is the one term choice here that is
   expensive to reverse late because it touches two ranges.
7. **§5.3, *aspirare* = "breathe" against Douay's "break".** If chunks 0054–0059
   have printed the *donec* clause as a lemma cue with "break", the two must be
   reconciled — **and the reconciliation must go my way, because *Caput* XVII is
   eleven breath-words long and Douay's verb makes them all inexplicable.**
