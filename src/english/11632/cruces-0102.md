### 1.2 · ⭐⭐ Cant. 5:12 at 0434D — the lemma has LOST the word its own chapter expounds, and Philip's gloss contradicts the lemma four times

Migne prints the running lemma of *Caput* XIX as:

> *Oculi ejus sicut columbae super **rivos** aquarum, quae **factae** sunt lotae, et resident juxta fluenta plenissima.* (0434D)

Vulg. Cant. 5:12: *Oculi eius sicut columbae super **rivulos** aquarum, quae
**lacte** sunt lotae, et resident iuxta fluenta plenissima.*

Two divergences, and the second is the finding. ***lacte* has become *factae***,
which as printed barely construes ("which have been *made* washed") and drops the
milk — **and the milk is the whole second half of the chapter.** Philip glosses it
back in, four separate times, inside three columns:

> *Quod autem columbae perhibentur, non aquis, sed potius **lacte** lotae, innuit quod longius a columbis materialibus sunt remotae* (0435D)
>
> *non aquis lotae, sed **lacte** potius referuntur, quia **lacti nivalis candor** naturaliter assignatur, in quo gratiae columbinae munda simplicitas designatur* (0436B)
>
> *secundoque profectu velut **candore lacteo** nos emundat* (0436D)
>
> *vel **lavacro uti lacteo** memorantur* (0437A)

**The gloss quotes the lemma's own word four times and the lemma does not contain
it.** The argument — dove-simplicity, snowy whiteness, milk as the mark that these
are not material doves — has nothing to stand on in the verse as printed.

⭐ **The companion divergence supplies an internal control on the same line.** The
lemma prints *rivos*; twelve lines later Philip writes *non solum super aquas, sed
super **rivulos** sunt aquarum* (0436A) and then builds on *instar **rivuli**
non constare* (0436B). So on both halves of the lemma the plate's exposition
carries the Vulgate reading and the plate's lemma does not.

**`[var:]` FIRED**, immediately after the lemma, naming the witness and giving both
Vulgate words with a gloss (Pattern 14). ⚠ **The marker is available here and was
not at the §8a cases**, because Migne italicises this run: it is marked as a
quotation, so its boundary is printed and a `[var:]` asserts nothing the plate
does not. That is the same distinction brief §8b drew for Isa. 42 at 0318D.
The English renders what is printed — "which have been made washed" — and is meant
to read wrong.

⚑ **On *rivos*/*rivulos* alone I would not have fired**: a diminutive lost is
sub-threshold under Pattern 14, and Philip himself writes *rivos* once in his own
prose at 0435D. It rides inside the marker because it sits in the same six words
and eliding it would have been the §8-warned "a `[var:]` must not elide the
divergence it exists to report" in reverse. The marker's **assertion** is *lacte*.

⛔ **This is the most repairable place in the range.** One letter restores the
Vulgate, the sentence improves, and four columns of exposition quietly lose their
subject.

**No negation was dropped and none was supplied.** ⚠ **Two places were caught by
this pass and would otherwise have shipped weakened:**

1. **0434C, *nec nisi per alterum caret fine*.** The draft had rendered the pair
   idiomatically as "and lacketh an end **only** through another", which is
   defensible English for *non nisi* but leaves neither particle visible. Rewritten
   to "and lacketh **not** an end **save** through another" — the standard
   *nisi* → "save" mapping the corpus already uses, with the *nec* carried. This is
   the shape of mechanism 3 (connective smoothed), which the audit note in Pattern
   7a says leaves **no lexical trace at all**; here it left one only because the
   Latin was in front of me.
2. **0444C, *quis talem **non** quaerat studiosius, **non** teneat avidius… et
   alios ad quaerendum **non** studeat erudire?*** The draft had let a single "would
   not" distribute across all three verbs — grammatical in English, but two of the
   three printed *non*s were then invisible. Rewritten so each verb carries its own:
   "would not seek… **would not** hold… **would not** be zealous to instruct."

⭐ **And one place where the double negation was deliberately kept, because it is
the range's best sentence:** 0445A *ab illo diligi qui **nescit non diligere**
diligentem* → "to be loved by him who **knoweth not how not to love** him that
loveth." An English ear wants "who cannot but love"; that would drop one of two
printed negations and flatten the figure Philip is closing his book with.

## §5 · CONVENTIONS SET OR HELD IN THIS RANGE

1. ⭐⭐ **`elatae palmarum` (Cant. 5:11) IS translated — "the raised boughs of palm
   trees" — against Douay's "branches of palm trees", and *Caput* XVIII depends on
   it.** This is the *vermiculatas argento* call, not the *muraenula* call: **English
   has the exponent, so italic Latin is not available.** Philip's whole gloss is the
   participle. *Elatae vero illi palmarum esse ramusculi perhibentur, qui **in altum**
   prae caeteris procera rectitudine promoventur* (0433C) · *Sic virtutes coelorum
   **in altum** prae caeteris sunt **elatae*** (0433C) · *ad intuendam cujus faciem
   semper **erigere se** festinant* (0433D) · *quasi triumphantes **elatam palmae**
   gestare gratulantur* (0433D) · *palmarum, ut jam dictum est, similes sunt
   **elatis*** (0434B) · *elatis palmarum **proceris**… similes* (0434A). **Six
   occurrences of the root across one chapter, and every one is the argument**:
   raised bough → raised on high → the angels lift themselves → they bear the raised
   bough in triumph. "Branches of palm trees" is botanical and severs the chain
   exactly as "thy plants" severed *emissio*. ⛔ **This is the one cross-chunk
   decision in my range the merge must not undo**, and the lemma does not recur after
   0434C, so it is a closed set.
2. ⭐ **`distinctus`/`distinguitur`/`distinctio` (Cant. 5:14) = "distinguished", NOT
   Douay's "set with".** Same argument, smaller: *Venter ejus eburneus, **distinctus**
   sapphiris* (0440D) → *sapphiris… est **distinctus*** (0441D) → *venter eburneus…
   **distinguitur** sapphiris* (0442B) → *sed operandi seriem **distinctionis** gratia
   decorari* (0442B). Philip's closing move in *Caput* XXIII is that God's works are
   *distinguished* — set apart from one another in a series — and "set with sapphires"
   makes the last sentence explain nothing. **The lemma does not recur after 0442B.**
3. ⭐ **`electus` = "chosen" everywhere, at both lemmata, against Douay's "excellent"
   at Cant. 5:15.** *electus ex millibus* (Cant. 5:10, 0431A) and *electus ut cedri*
   (Cant. 5:15, 0443A) are the same word and Philip glosses both with the verb:
   *solus ille sit **electus** ad ministerium offerendi… iste singulariter est
   **electus*** (0432C) and *Quem nimirum Deus Pater **elegit**… Propter quod
   **electus est** ut in aedificio collocetur* (0443B–C). One English word, both
   places. **Douay's "excellent as the cedars" would have broken the second
   chapter's whole argument from election.**
4. **The taste-words split, and the split is forced by one chiasmus.** *suavis* =
   **sweet** · *dulcis* = **delightful / delightfulness**. Set at 0443D–0444A, where
   Migne prints ***dulcedo suavior, suavitas dulcior** non habetur* — a chiasmus that
   collapses into "no sweetness is sweeter, no sweetness more sweet" under any single
   equivalent. "Sweet" is kept for the lemma word (*suavissimum*, *suavior*,
   *suavitas*, *suavius* — five occurrences) and *dulcis* takes the second slot.
   **Logged as a judgment call, reversible, and flagged as the one I am least sure
   of** — a merge that prefers "sweet" for *dulcis* must change *suavis* everywhere,
   including the printed lemma.
5. **`species` takes a THIRD sense here, extending cruces-0084 §5.3.** That range
   split it "spice" (aromatic catalogue) / "kind" (garden). This range adds
   **"fairness"** for the Cant. 5:15 lemma *Species ejus ut Libani* (0443A) and for
   *laudari digna est facies merito **speciei*** (0437A) — the face's beauty, which
   is *speciosus* = "fair" territory (cruces-0084 §5.2) and not spice or kind. ⚠ **And
   "spice" also occurs in this range**, four times in 0105's perfumer passage
   (*multimodis divites **speciebus*** 0437B · *herbas sive **species** non contrivit*
   0437D · *salutis **species** collegerunt* 0438A · *aromaticas **species**
   attulerunt* 0438B), so both senses stand within six columns. **The word has three
   English exponents in this work and no single one; logged so a merge sweep does not
   flatten them.**
6. **`charitas` = charity — held, and the range is thin on the pressure point.** Two
   instances only: 0437C *ardet praesentia **charitatis*** → "the presence of charity
   burneth" and 0442D *inniti visus est illi **geminae charitati*** → "that twin
   charity", which is the same phrase cruces-0084 §5.6 recorded at 0400B and is
   rendered identically. **No *dilectio* occurs in the range**, so the doublet is not
   tested here.
7. ⭐ **`amicus meus` (Cant. 5:16) = "my friend" — the masculine counterpart of brief
   §5's ruled `amica mea`, and it is confirmed by the same mechanism.** Migne prints
   *ipse est **amicus meus**, filiae Hierusalem* at 0444C and Philip glosses it in
   his own voice at 0445A: *quem fieri et **amicum** vere diligentibus intellexi*.
   §5's ruling was proved from John 15:14's *amici mei*; this lemma is the word in
   the masculine, in the same book, glossed the same way. **"My beloved" is already
   taken by *dilectus meus* in the same verse** — *Talis est **dilectus meus**, et
   ipse est **amicus meus*** — so the two must differ in English, which is the
   §5 argument in miniature. **Offered to the merge as the completion of that rule,
   not a new one.**
8. **Beauty-words: cruces-0084 §5.2's set held unchanged.** *formosus* = comely ·
   *speciosus* = fair · *pulcher* = beautiful · *decor* = comeliness. All four are
   live in this range (0431C *non decor illi maneat* · 0437A *pulchritudo elucet
   faciei* · 0437B *forma prae filiis hominum speciosus* · 0434A *comarum
   pulchritudo*), and *formosus* does not occur. Nothing to reconcile.
9. **The colour triple held: *niger* = black throughout 0103, *candor* = whiteness,
   *rubor* = redness. *fuscus* occurs nowhere in the range**, so brief §5's "*fusca*
   is never black" rule is untested here. ⚑ *candidus* at 0431A/0432B is rendered
   **"white"** (the Cant. 5:10 lemma *candidus et rubicundus*), and *candidatio* does
   not occur — but brief §5's ruling that *candidatio* is **translated** governs the
   same field and is consistent with this.
10. **Bed- and chamber-words: none occur in this range**, so brief §5's *cubile* =
    "couch" ruling and cruces-0060's *lectulus*/*lectus* pair are untouched. *thalamus*
    does not occur either, so cruces-0084 §5.5's open question is not advanced.
