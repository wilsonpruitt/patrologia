## §1 · ⭐⭐ THE FINDINGS THE MERGE SHOULD CARRY

### 1.1 · ⭐⭐⭐ Cant. 6:8b at 0454B — Migne **ADDS *Sion*** to the lemma, and Philip's very next clause expounds the added word

> *Viderunt illam, **filiae Sion**, et beatissimam praedicaverunt: reginae et
> concubinae, et laudaverunt eam.* (0454B, head lemma of *Caput* IX)

Vulg. Cant. 6:8: *Viderunt **eam filiae**, et beatissimam praedicaverunt;
reginae et concubinae, et laudaverunt eam.* The Vulgate's *filiae* is **bare** —
no place-name anywhere in the verse.

⭐⭐ **And the addition is load-bearing in the first six words of the exposition.**
Philip's opening sentence is:

> ***Filiae Sion** supra **filiae Hierusalem** appellantur*, *quae Virginem, ut
> ante dictum est, in hoc epithalamio comitantur…* (0454B)

— "The daughters of **Sion** are above called the daughters of **Jerusalem**."
His whole first paragraph is an **identification of two named groups**, and it
identifies a name the Vulgate's lemma does not contain. On the Vulgate text the
sentence has nothing to identify: *filiae* and *filiae Hierusalem* are not two
names. He then keeps the added name in play twice more (*hanc attollunt **filiae
Sion**, et beatissimam praedicant confidenter*, 0454C).

**`[var:]` FIRED** at the lemma, naming both readings. The marker is available
because Migne italicises the run — the quotation's boundary is printed, so the
marker asserts nothing the plate does not (the same distinction cruces-0102 §1.2
drew, and the reason §8a's unmarked class takes no marker).

⚑ **The companion divergence rides inside the same marker and is not elided**:
Migne prints ***illam*** for the Vulgate's *eam* in the first clause while
keeping *eam* in the last (*laudaverunt **eam***), so the verse uses two pronouns
where the Vulgate uses one. Sense untouched; it is inside the marker only because
it sits in the same six words.

⚑ **Copy-text shape, and the evidence is in the plate.** *Viderunt **illam*** is
printed **three** times — the chunk's `incipit`, the lemma at 0454B, and the
forward citation *De qua subditur:* Viderunt illam, *etc.* at 0454A (chunk 0111)
— and never *Viderunt eam*. And ***filiae Sion*** is a phrase this work prints
elsewhere in its own right: at 0073 it is the head lemma of Cant. 3:11
(*Egredimini, filiae Sion*) and at 0023 in Philip's prose. So the Song supplies
the name twenty-eight columns earlier, and the natural mechanism is conformation
to Cant. 3:11 — **exactly the shape of §6's Cant. 5:7 *vigiles*/*custodes* case,
where Migne's 5:7 was conformed to 3:3.** Two members of that shape are now
attested in Book V–VI alone.

### 1.2 · ⭐⭐ Cant. 6:8a at 0451D — ***matri suae*** (dative) for the Vulgate's ***matris suae*** (genitive), printed four times, and the whole chapter is built on the dative

> *Una est columba mea, perfecta mea; una est **matri suae**, electa genitrici
> suae.* (0451D)

Vulg. Cant. 6:8: *…una est **matris** suae, electa genetrici suae.*

One letter, and it changes the construction: the Vulgate's genitive says *she is
the one **of** her mother* (the mother's only child, the Hebrew sense); Migne's
dative says *she is one **unto** her mother*. **Philip reads the dative and builds
on it.** The phrase is printed **four times in the chunk, always dative**:

| col | printed |
|---|---|
| 0451D | *una est **matri suae*** (head lemma) |
| 0452B | *Haec igitur **matri suae** una est, et in hac excellentia singularis* |
| 0452D | *Una est igitur **matri suae**, quia mater gratia, nec primam visa est habere similem nec secundam* |
| 0452D | *sicut una **matri suae**, sic et genitrici electa perhibetur* |

⭐ **And the mother he means is *gratia*, not Anne** — *quia **mater gratia***
(0452D), *quae tam digne, quae tam recte, quam ipsa gratia praedicatur* (0452C).
That reading needs the dative: grace is the one **to whom** the Virgin is one and
for whom she is unique, and the four columns of *Caput* VIII run *una uni
jungitur* → *in amorem unius… collecta* → *unitatis hujus merito… perfecta*. The
genitive would make the sentence a statement about parentage, which is not what
he expounds.

⚑ **The work knows the genitive.** *matris suae* is printed correctly at 0020 and
0120 — in Philip's own prose, on other verses. So the form is available to the
compositor and does not appear at this verse.

**`[var:]` FIRED** at the lemma (italicised, boundary printed). ⚠ **This is the
marker in my range a merge is likeliest to weigh again**, and I say so plainly:
one letter, and the English "one unto her mother" / "the one of her mother" is a
small shift. I fired it because it is a **change of case, not of orthography**,
because it is printed four times, and because §7a mechanism 5 (reference or
agency mis-bound) is precisely the class no negation-aware check can see.

### 1.3 · ⭐⭐ Cant. 6:11 at 0457D — ***conturbata est*** (passive) for the Vulgate's ***conturbavit me*** (active), and Philip's gloss carries BOTH readings

> *Nescivi: anima mea **conturbata est** propter quadrigas Aminadab.* (0457D)

Vulg. Cant. 6:11: *Nescivi: anima mea **conturbavit me** propter quadrigas
Aminadab.* The Vulgate makes the soul the **agent** troubling the speaker;
Migne's passive makes the soul the **patient**.

**The gloss is the interesting part, and it runs both ways inside one column:**

- passive, twice — *sum **turbata**, expavi, obstupui* (0458A) · *Virgo prius
  nescia **conturbatur*** (0458D, the chapter's closing sentence)
- **active, once, and it is the Vulgate's own construction** — *labore
  salutifero deficiens, vel magis, proficiens **me turbavit*** (0458B)

⭐ So at 0458B Philip writes *me turbavit* for a soul that troubles **her**,
which is the reading his own lemma does not print. **`[var:]` FIRED** (the lemma
is italicised), and the crux records the internal control that runs *against*
firing, exactly as cruces-0096 §2b did for *distillaverunt*: the Vulgate reading
survives in the exposition. I fired anyway because unlike *distillaverunt*
(compound for simple, sense untouched) this is a **change of voice and of
agency**, which is Pattern 14's own threshold.

### 1.4 · ⭐⭐ Exod. 3 at 0456B — the note says Exodus and the **words are Acts 7:34's**, verbatim

> *Videns vidi,* inquit, *afflictionem populi mei, et descendi liberare eos*
> `[n: (Exod. III)]`. (0456B)

- Vulg. **Exod. 3:7–8**: ***Vidi** afflictionem populi mei **in Aegypto**… et

### 4a · Carried type — `[sic: …]` fired three times

All three contents were machine-checked as verbatim substrings of their Latin
twins. **Conjectures live here and NOT in the text.**

1. ⭐ **0451C, `[sic: *a dolescentulae*]`** — Pattern 10 **split type**, the
   clearest in the range: *novitatis vel imperfectionis merito, **a
   dolescentulae** nominantur.* The word is *adolescentulae*, and it is the

   spacing included, per Pattern 10's "carry the WHOLE broken run" rule**, even

   ⚑ Pattern 7a′ observed: the run governs an accusative-and-infinitive, and the

   mechanical: *Videns* is printed correctly at 0457D**, eight lines above, in the

  emissi forinsecus respuuntur… cuivis hominum numerabiles non habentur.***

### 4c · ⭐ One editorial judgment I want the merge to see: *castra* left in italic Latin at 0449A

> *quia et angelos caro vel carnis copula nunquam laedit, et remotis a complexu
> domestico **castris**, **castitas** nomen dedit.* (0449A)

This is an **etymology**: *castra* ("camp") from *castitas* ("chastity"), the
standard patristic derivation, and Philip makes it the hinge on which the lemma's
*terribilis ut **castrorum** acies* becomes a statement about the Virgin's
virginity. **English shares no root.** Rendered "chastity gave its name unto the
*castra*, the camps that are removed from the household embrace" — the Latin
noun shown once, italic, with its English gloss beside it, so that the inference
is available to a reader who cannot see the Latin column.

⚑ **Why this and not the *muraenula* treatment.** *castra* is translated "camps"
at all **nine** of its other occurrences in the range, including the lemma
itself, because there the word is doing no etymological work. Leaving it Latin
throughout would make the lemma unreadable; leaving it English throughout makes
one sentence explain nothing. This follows `translation-style.md`'s Song ruling
case 2 (*stylus*/*epistylium*, *murenulae*/*ferculum*) rather than brief §5's
blanket-italic list. **It is the reason 0109's asterisk count runs +2 against its
twin**, and it is reversible in one edit if the merge prefers uniform English.
