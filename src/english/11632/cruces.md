## §1 · WORK-WIDE CONVENTIONS — RULED AND APPLIED. DO NOT RE-DECIDE.

These are settled and swept. They are set down here with their reasons so that a
later sweep does not churn them, and so that a reader can tell a considered
choice from an accident.

**Divisions.** *tomus* = "Tome" / *liber* = "book" is inherited from 11638 —
but **this work has no *tomi*.** Its divisions are *liber* = **book** (six of
them), *caput* = **chapter**, *volumen* = **volume** (135 instances, none of
them a division of the work). The *tomus*/*liber* collapse that cost 11638
sixteen sweep sites cannot arise here in that shape; the 11638 rule stands only
in case a *tomus* ever turns up. `INCIPIT` = "HERE BEGINS", `EXPLICIT` =
"Here ends".

**`CAPUT PRIMUM` = "CHAPTER THE FIRST."** — ruled and swept 2026-08-08. Seven
sites: chunks 0002, 0003 (`(cont.)`), 0026, 0047, 0069, 0091, 0108. The ranges
had split 3–3 against "CHAPTER ONE."; the three were swept. **"Chapter the
First" is the right side of that split** because *PRIMUM* is an ordinal spelled
out, and it is the spelled-out-ness that carries Migne's own contrast with the
179 numeral chapters that follow. "Chapter One" is a cardinal and flattens
exactly the distinction the plate makes. ✅ Verified: 7 `## CAPUT PRIMUM` in the
Latin, 7 `## CHAPTER THE FIRST` in the English, 0 `## CHAPTER ONE`.

**⭐ *amica mea* (Cant. 1:8) = "my friend", NOT "my love."** Ruled from *Caput* V,
where Philip glosses the word and proves it from John 15:14's *amici mei*. The
lemma recurs at Cant. 2:10, 2:13, 4:1, 4:7, 5:2, 6:3 — nearly every range met it.
✅ 20 Latin *amica mea*, 17 English "my friend"; the three shortfalls are
re-quotations absorbed into Philip's own syntax, not drift. The one English
"my love" in the whole work (0122 @0480D) renders ***mea dilectio***, not *amica
mea*, and is correct — checked, no edit.

**⭐ *muraenula* stays italic Latin.** *Caput* VIII's whole argument is that the
word names a **fish** (*Muraenula piscis est…*) and the chapter runs
tail-to-mouth → ring → net-mesh → chain → Moses' head-and-tail offering.
"Chains of gold" makes seventeen paragraphs explain nothing. ✅ 22 Latin
instances (0032 ×21, 0033 ×1); the five English instances not carrying their own
asterisks are either the frontmatter `incipit` (verbatim Latin) or nested inside
a wider italic run — nesting, not drift.

**⭐ *vermiculatas argento* IS translated** ("vermiculated") — the companion call,
decided the other way **on purpose**, because English carries the whole gloss
chain: vermiculate → vermilion → little worm → *Ego autem sum vermis*. Italic
Latin is reserved for words with no English exponent; this one has one. ✅ 6/6.

**⭐ The bed triple, and it is a triple on purpose.** *lectus* = **"bed"** ·
*lectulus* = **"little bed"** (40 Latin / 41 English) · *cubile* = **"couch."**
0279B's whole humility argument turns on the diminutive (*non tamen Virgo
lectum, sed magis lectulum*), and **"bed" is not available for *cubile*** because
a third word collapsing into the pair would void that argument. See §4.5 for the
two sites where this had not in fact been swept.

**Other ruled equivalences, all held work-wide:** *cancelli* = **"lattice"**
(10/10; 0312D identifies the lemma with the architectural sense and "chancel"
would sever the argument) · *emissio* = **"emission"**, not Douay's "plants" ·
*vicus* = **"lane"** (9/10) and *platea* = **"street"** (18/19), kept distinct
against Douay, load-bearing at 0318D · *ferculum* stays **italic Latin**, on the
*muraenula* precedent · *maceria* = "dry-stone wall", distinct from *murus* ·
*adolescentulae* = "maidens" · *charitas* = **charity** (62 Latin / 65 English),
never "love" · *candidatio* is translated, not carried.

**⭐ The colour triple, kept strictly distinct:** *nigra* = **black** ·
*fusca* = **swarthy** · *formosa* = **comely**. *fusca* is never "black" — and in
this work that matters more than in 11638, because **Cant. 1:5 prints *nigra sum*
where the Vulgate has *fusca sim*** (0230D): the colour word *and* the mood both
changed, and Philip builds four columns of *nigredo* exposition and the whole
*nigra sed formosa* architecture on it. Marked `[var:]`, never conformed.

**Pattern 3, "Holy Scripture"** — both words capitalized, for *divina*, *sacra*,
*divinae Scriptura(e)* **and the bare form alike**. 22 instances in the English;
**0 instances of "divine Scripture" or "sacred Scripture"**, which is the defect
11638 shipped. The bare form is the one 11638's sweep missed and this work's
brief warned about in advance: it fires on a bare *in Scripturis* (0260A), a bare
genitive *Scripturae* (0307D), a bare nominative *Scriptura* (0331B), and
sixteen times in chunks 0078–0083 alone, fourteen of them bare.

**Pattern 17: `thou` = singular, `you` = plural.** A number marker, not a
reverence marker. Anchor case: 0454D, Luke 10:23 *vos videtis* → "**you** see".

**⭐ Migne's mixed capitalization is MIRRORED PER INSTANCE, never regularized.**
*Sponsus*/*sponsus*, *Sponsa*/*sponsa*, *Virgo*/*virgo*, *Dilecto*/*dilecto*,
*Spiritus sanctus*/*sancti Spiritus* — he runs both cases inside single
sentences. This was named in advance as the likeliest drift point across
twenty-one agents, and **it did not drift**: every range checked its own plate
word by word and reported so. The worked example that closed the question is
0305B `Spiritus sanctus` against 0356C `sancti Spiritus` — two ranges appeared to
disagree, both were right, and there was nothing to reconcile. **Before reporting
a capitalization disagreement in this work, check whether Migne simply prints
both. He usually does.**

**Guillemet asymmetries are Migne's and are mirrored, never balanced.** Five
chunks are asymmetric — 0006 (8 «, 9 »), 0011 (11/10), 0044 (4/3), 0083 (5/4),
0089 (10/11) — and the English reproduces each 1:1 (422 « and 421 » on both
sides). At 0307C Migne himself breaks one quotation into two guillemet pairs
around *inquiens*, doing what Pattern 5 forbids *us* to do; reproduced.

## §2 · THE TYPOGRAPHY — three findings, none of which one range could have made

### 2.1 · The marks divide BY FUNCTION, not by book

11638 changes how it marks scripture at a **book boundary** — italics in Books
I–II, guillemets « » from Book III. **11632 is not built that way.** It runs both
marks in parallel from chunk 0000, divided by function: **guillemets carry a
quotation adduced as an authority** (scripture is the commonest case, not the
rule — the Delphic oracle, Virgil, and two bare pronouns displayed as words are
all guilleted at 0251D–0254B), and **italics carry the running Song lemma** plus
book titles, words discussed as words, printed locators, and Migne's marginal
source-edition page numbers.

**The book boundaries were checked and carry no seam** — `LIBER SECUNDUS.` at
0246C (cruces-0024), `LIBER TERTIUS.` at 0303A (cruces-0042), `LIBER QUARTUS.` at
0354C (cruces-0066), `LIBER QUINTUS.` (cruces-0090). Both marks run identically on
either side of each. **The question is closed: later book heads are ordinary
text.**

**The proof that the division is functional is that the Song ITSELF takes
guillemets when quoted as a proof-text rather than as the lemma** — Cant. 4:11
@0220A, Cant. 5:11 @0226A, Cant. 2:14 @0226C, Cant. 1:12 @0270B, Cant. 5:11
again at 0432C, each with the italic lemma a few lines away. Five instances,
four ranges.

### 2.2 · ⭐⭐⭐ BUT THE GUILLEMETS STOP — and the seam is nowhere structural

Found independently by the 0096–0101 and 0102–0107 agents, then verified against
all 126 Latin chunks.

| chunks | opening « |
|---|---|
| 0000–0089 | **420** |

**From chunk 0097 to the end of the work there is not one guillemet** — 29
chunks, ~73 columns, ~50 notes, none. The last « in the book stands at col.
0416D. And Philip does not stop quoting: adduced proof-texts continue at full
density, each with its *inquit*/*ait* and a correct note, **printed in italics**.

⭐ **Internal control, exact and on one verse: Isa. 53:7 is guilleted at 0399C and
italic at 0440A–B** — same verse, same siglum, forty-one columns apart.

⚑ **The seam falls mid-*Caput* VII of Book V, 29 columns from either book head.**
So this does not overturn §2.1's no-seam-at-a-book-boundary result; it
**strengthens** it. Migne's practice changes where nothing structural happens,
which is why nobody predicted it and why it had to be measured across the whole
work rather than reasoned from one range.

⛔ **The practical consequence, which was pushed into the briefs of every range
from 0090 on:** "italic run = probably just a lemma" is true to 0089 and false
after it. In the last 29 chunks italics carry the lemma *and* every quotation,
and they are told apart by function, not by mark. A range applying the earlier
rule would have mis-classed every quotation it met and collated none of them.

### 2.3 · ⭐⭐⭐ THE DISPLAY RULE — it dissolves the "counter-instances"

Waves 3–5 kept logging classical and patristic authorities printed **bare** —
Ovid at 0259, Sallust at 0314A (adduced explicitly, *verax sententia…
ethnicorum*), Ambrose **by name** at 0337B, Virgil at 0385A — and read them as
counter-instances to §2.1, killing one explanation after another, including the
tempting "Migne declines to guillemet pagans", which a Father printed unmarked
disposed of. **They were never counter-instances. The discriminator is
typographic DISPLAY.**

- A quotation Migne sets **displayed**, on its own line or lines, is
  **italicised**.
- A quotation Migne **runs into the paragraph** is **bare**.

⭐ **The control is one line of Horace printed twice.** At 0022 the *Siculi* tag
is displayed on its own line and italicised (*Invidia,* inquit, *Siculi non
invenere tyranni* / *Tormentum majus.*); at 0447D the same line is run into
Philip's sentence — *majus quo nec Siculi, juxta quemdam ethnicum, tormentum
invenere* — and printed bare. Same poet, same line, two treatments, one variable.
✅ Checked against all 126 chunks: every displayed block in the work is
italicised, and every bare authority is run-in.

⚑ **The rule is PERMISSIVE, not predictive**, and that has not changed:
reproduce what is printed, and never add a mark because the function seems to
call for one. What it changes is what counts as a finding. A bare adduced
authority is **expected**; only a *displayed* quotation printed bare, or a
*run-in* one italicised, would be real.

⚑ **Migne's `[n:]` is usually inside the closing » but not always** — outside at
0235B and 0239B; a bare locator inside a run with no `[n:]` wrapper at 0232D and
0236D; a printed locator standing *inside* a guillemet run at 0203A and 0222B–C.
Reproduced as printed in every case.

## §3 · PHILIP'S BIBLE — the copy-text readings

A divergence attested in **more than one range** is evidence about Philip's copy
of the Bible, not a local slip. These are the merge's own results; no single
agent could have made any of them.

**⭐⭐⭐ Cant. 2:17 — *montes BETHEL* for the Vulgate's *Bether*** (0338D, again at
0341A). Philip spends four columns on the etymology of the divergent word:
*Beth, domus; el vero Deus interpretatur; et Bethel domus Dei… Domus autem Dei
non incongrue interna Virginis conscientia judicatur.* Conform the lemma and the
back half of *Caput* XVIII has nothing to stand on. **Printed twice, which is the
copy-text shape** — the shape that made Zech. 4:14 and Gen. 3:19 findings rather
than slips on 11638.

**⭐⭐⭐ Eccles. 9:1 — the pair REVERSED in six of its seven printings.** *utrum
**odio** dignus sit an **amore*** against the Vulgate's *utrum amore an odio*. ✅
Verified against all 126 Latin chunks: reversed at 0029, 0042, 0062, 0070, 0074
and 0125; **only 0049** carries the Vulgate order. So the copy-text shape comes
with its own internal control — Migne contradicts himself exactly once. And the
seventh printing (0489A, the last in the work) is **recast**: different wording,
the reversal surviving three re-castings. Statistically the strongest member of
the class. Marked nowhere it is unmarkable: at 0489A the span carries neither
guillemets nor italics nor a note, and under §8a's rule it is logged, not marked.

**⭐⭐⭐ Cant. 5:9 — and it is the only member of the class with an AUTHORIAL
witness.** At 0429B Migne prints *ex dilecto* **once** where the Vulgate prints it
twice. At 0430B–C **Philip discusses the omission himself**: he reports that most
of the codices he could find carry the phrase twice, and declines to say whether
the repetition is the author's or a scribe's. This is not our inference from his
exposition — it is his own collation note, printed. `[var:]` fires; Migne stands.

**⭐⭐⭐ Cant. 7:13 — A NEW SHAPE: Migne DIVIDES the verse differently.** At 0473B
he prints *Mandragorae dederunt odorem **in portis nostris***, where the Vulgate
gives the gates to the apples (*In portis nostris omnia poma*). **Every word is
present — only the break moved.** All of *Caput* XXXI expounds the gates as the
mandrakes' (*necdum in aedibus, necdum in thalamo… sed in portis*), and *Caput*
XXXII opens its lemma at *Omnia poma* with the gates already spent.
⚠ **This shape is not greppable the way a divergent word is.** Nobody was looking
for it for 113 chunks. It is found only by reading the lemma against the
Vulgate's own division — which is now a standing instruction for lemma-and-gloss
commentaries.

**⭐⭐ Cant. 5:7 — *vigiles* for *custodes*** (0427A), which is Cant. 3:3's word,
conformed onto 5:7. **The proof it is copy-text and not a compositor's reach is
Migne's own cross-reference**: `(sup. l. III, c. 22)` resolves exactly to the
Cant. 3:3 lemma in chunk 0065. Philip then builds two columns on the resulting
distinction (*custodes murorum* kept *a vigilibus*), which the Vulgate's single
word makes unwritable.

**⭐⭐ Cant. 8:2 at 0475C** — the lemma carries a **whole clause the Vulgate does
not have** (*et in cubiculum genitricis meae*), the LXX/Old Latin reading, and
five chapters of exposition stand on it. **Cant. 8:12 at 0486C** is its opposite
number: a **deletion** (*his* dropped from *et ducenti his qui custodiunt*) which
changes who the two hundred are, and Philip's exposition follows the printed
reading.

**Others attested and marked:** *Sponsa mea* for *speciosa mea* (Cant. 2:13,
0323C, with Philip's next sentence proving it is copy-text) · *in cavernis*
plural (Cant. 2:14) · *grex* for *greges* (Cant. 4:1–2, and the chapter is an
argument about the singular) · *pellis Salomonis* singular (Cant. 1:4, and
*Caput* XVIII expounds one skin flayed from one animal) · *Sunamitis* for
*Sulamitis* (seven printings; *Sulamit-* occurs nowhere in the work) · *matri
suae* dative for *matris suae* genitive (Cant. 6:8a, printed four times, the
whole chapter built on the dative — and **the work knows the genitive**, printing
it correctly at 0020) · *Sion* **added** to Cant. 6:8b at 0454B, with Philip
expounding the addition in his next clause · *conturbata est* for *conturbavit
me* (Cant. 6:11, voice reversed, gloss carrying both).

**⭐ Cant. 1:12 — *Cum* for the Vulgate's *Dum*, ruled here.** Migne prints *Cum*
four times: three in chunk 0033 and again at 0270B where the verse returns as a
guilleted proof-text with Philip's own gloss picking the word up. **No `[var:]`
fires, and that is right** — the sense is untouched, which is Pattern 14's
threshold. But four independent printings is copy-text evidence, so it is
recorded here as an attested reading rather than a slip. ⚠ The fifth occurrence,
at 0397C, prints **only the verse's second half** and supplies no evidence either
way; a range reporting "met, agrees" would have been counted as an attestation of
something it never witnessed. **Name the words, not the verse.**

**⭐ An expounded quotation Migne leaves unmarked is LOGGED, never marked** —
ruled once for all twenty-one ranges. The class: Ps. 90:5–6 fused at 0245A, with
the next sentence glossing the fusion; Lam. 4:20 at 0291B printed with the Old
Latin *ante faciem nostram* against the Vulgate's *oris nostri*, expounded three
times in two sentences, and carrying **no guillemets, no italics and no `[n:]`**;
1 Cor. 2:9 at 0372D; Deut. 32:33 at 0469A; the Chalcedonian formula at 0444A.
**And one member is a DELETION, which is worse:** John 19:39 at 0399D prints
*ferens misturam myrrhae et aloes **libras centum*** for the Vulgate's ***quasi**
libras centum*, unmarked in every channel, twelve lines before Philip argues *in
pondere et numero… certo pondere, perfecto numero consummatum*. His whole
four-sentence argument needs the hundred to be **exact**, and what has gone is
the Vulgate's hedge. Every other member substitutes; a substitution at least
leaves a strange word on the page. **A deletion leaves nothing at all**, and no
mechanical channel can reach it.
**The ruling stands: log the candidate with its evidence, place no `[var:]`.** A
`[var:]` asserts a divergence *inside a quotation*, and where Migne marks the
words as neither quotation nor lemma we cannot establish the quotation's
boundary. Marking it would have us assert a boundary the plate does not print.

### 4.1 · ⭐ *aromata* = "spices". "Aromatical spices" comes out. — 12 edits

*aromata* is **one Latin word**; "aromatical spices" is **two English words**,
and it is Douay's phrase at Cant. 3:6 and 5:1. Fourteen chunks render the root;
nine of them say **"spices"** and five say **"aromatical spices"**. This is the
work's characteristic defect class — the Douay arriving over the plate — showing
up in vocabulary rather than in a verse.

**⭐ The ruling is forced from inside chunk 0105, which contains both words and
contradicts itself in three lines.** Migne prints *areolae **aromatum*** (noun) in
the lemma and *arboribus **aromaticis*** (adjective) in the gloss. Our English
gave the lemma "beds of **aromatical spices**" and then, two sentences later,
glossed it "the Virgin compared him to **beds of spices**" — the same phrase, two
ways, on one page. If *aromata* takes two words, the genuine adjective *aromaticus*
has nothing left to be.

**RULE: *aromata* (the noun, any case) = "spices". *aromaticus* (the adjective)
= "aromatic". A bare *aromata* never takes "aromatical".**

### 4.2 · ⭐ *suavis* = "sweet". The 0107 mapping wins. — 6 edits

- **0097–0099:** *dulcedo* = "sweetness", *suavitas* = "**pleasantness**".

⭐ **0107 is right, and the reason is that its mapping is fixed by the plate while
0097's is free.** Chapter XXVI's lemma is Cant. 5:16 ***Guttur illius
suavissimum*** — "His throat is most sweet" — and the whole chapter is an
exposition of that superlative (*non tam suavior quam suavissimus*). The chapter
then locks the two words into a chiasmus that **forces** distinct English:
*dulcedo suavior, suavitas dulcior non habetur*. If *suavitas* is "pleasantness"
there, the lemma the chapter expounds has changed word underneath it.

exponent almost everywhere; the two collide only where Philip puts them in one
period. **RULE: *suavis/suavitas* = sweet/sweetness. Where *dulcedo* stands in the
same period as a *suav-* word, *dulcedo* = delight/delightfulness. Elsewhere
"sweetness" for *dulcedo* is left standing** — a full 88-site sweep buys nothing

### 4.6 · The Gallemart colophon at 0490B — a proposed house ruling

Migne closes the work with *Joannes GALLEMART S. Theol. Doctor, et catecheseos
professor in Universitate Duacensi, hac 7 Febr. 1618.* — the subscription of a
Douai censor, printed italic. It is not Philip's text and not scripture; it is a
**documentary signature**, and our English currently reads "**John GALLEMART**,
Doctor of Sacred Theology, and professor of catechesis in the University of
Douai, this 7 Feb. 1618." — half-Englished, and half in Migne's capitals.

**Proposed ruling: in a documentary subscription, the NAME stands as printed and
the OFFICE is Englished.**

| file | col | current | proposed |
|---|---|---|---|
| `0125.md` | @0490B | `*John GALLEMART, Doctor of Sacred Theology, and professor of catechesis in the University of Douai, this 7 Feb. 1618.*` | `*Joannes GALLEMART, Doctor of Sacred Theology, and professor of catechesis in the University of Douai, this 7 Feb. 1618.*` |

**Reasoning.** A signature identifies a historical person in a record, and
Englishing half of one manufactures a person — "John Gallemart" signed nothing.
The capitals are Migne's typography and fall under the same mirroring rule as
*Sponsus*/*sponsus* (§1). The office, by contrast, is ordinary descriptive Latin
with an ordinary English exponent, and leaving it in Latin would tell an English
reader nothing. **This is a house ruling, not a work ruling** — it will govern
every censor's *approbatio*, *imprimatur* and editor's subscription in the
corpus, which is why it is worth deciding once here rather than per work.

## §12 · RULED 2026-08-09 — the four `[sic:]` on real Latin words STAY

The marker audit proposed stripping `[sic:]` from four sites because Pattern 12
reserves the marker for carried **non-word** type and these are all real Latin
words. **Ruled the other way: all four keep the marker**, and Pattern 12 has been
amended to record the test the ruling turns on.

**The test is "does it render IN PLACE?", not "is it a Latin word?"**

| site | printed | conjecture | why no English carries it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0007 @0202C | *Pecte* | *Recte* | imperative "comb!" stranded in a declarative clause governed by *dicit* |
| 0067 @0350A | *ama* | *amat* | imperative among third-person finite verbs (*deprehendit… intendit*) |
| 0075 @0369A | *sternis* | *sterilis* | 2nd-person verb where the clause needs a predicate nominative |
| 0048 @0304A | *signa* | an adverb | renders **only by hiding the defect** — see below |

The first three have no grammatical slot at all. Pattern 12's own examples,
*fuges* for *sugas* and *munde* for *mundo*, are not like them: those occupy
their slot and merely mean the wrong thing.

⭐ **0048 is the instructive one and it goes the same way for a different
reason.** *ut **signa** prius ardeat* sets a plural noun against singular
*ardeat*. "That signs may burn" is perfectly good English — and that is exactly
the problem. English *may burn* is number-neutral, so rendering *signa* "signs"
silently repairs the disagreement and leaves the reader nothing to see. **Where
English has no exponent for the defect, rendering it IS the repair**, and the
marker is the only treatment that does not quietly fix Migne.

⚑ This runs opposite to Pattern 9, which normalizes precisely where English has
no exponent — and the difference is worth stating, because a later sweep will
meet both. Pattern 9's cases (grammatical gender, section labels) are systemic
features of *Latin* and carry no information about this plate. A number
disagreement in one clause is a fact about *this printing*. **Normalize the
language; mark the page.**

Consequence for the split/join asymmetry recorded above: it is now decidable on
the same test rather than by counting precedents. *aforis* (0077) renders in
place as "from without" and takes no marker; *persequenter* (0046 @0300C) and the
lemma-level *myrrhoe* (0069 @0353D) are non-words and **should be marked** — both
are currently normalized silently, and both remain owed.

### The five withdrawals, and why two of them matter beyond their own column

| col | our TEI | the plate | what it cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0202C** | *Pecte* | **Recte** | a `[sic:]`, and one of §8.1's four disputed cases |
| 0256C | *a vida* | **avida** (one word) | a `[sic:]`, and one of §8.4's six carried splits |
| 0260B | *munos* | **munus** | a `[sic:]` |
| **0299D** | *Adjuvo* | **Adjuro** | a whole `[var:]` |
| 0300D | *persequenter* | **persequentur** | a `[sic:]` |

⭐ **0202C settles the first of §8.1's four.** That section argued the four real-word
`[sic:]` should stand *because none of them construes in place* — *Pecte* leaves its
sentence without a subject. **It leaves nothing without a subject: Migne prints
*Recte*, and the sentence is ordinary.** The word that would not construe was never on
the plate. ⚠ This does not decide the other three by itself, and one of them has now
been read the other way: **0304A *signa* IS Migne's own** (read this session, zoomed;
the plate prints *ut signa prius ardeat*). So §8.1's boundary question survives — it is
still Wilson's — but its evidence base has changed: the class is not homogeneous, and
each member has to be read.

⭐⭐ **0299D is the class the gate exists to catch: a `[var:]` MANUFACTURED by the
digitization.** The marker asserted that Migne prints *Adjuvo* ("I help") where the
Vulgate has *adiuro*, and built the observation that Migne prints the adjuring form
both at 0299C and at 0302A into the case. He prints it at 0299D too. The divergence
was our own transcription's, and the English lemma now reads "I adjure you." **Nothing
in our files could have caught this** — the marker's own internal control pointed at
the right answer and was read as evidence for the wrong one.

⚑ **0256C and 0235D go opposite ways on the same defect class, 21 columns apart.**
0256C's *a vida* is CC's inserted space (the a/v gap on the plate is a kerning gap,
markedly narrower than the flanking word spaces at 8×); 0235D's *a beo* is **Migne's
own displaced word-space** for *ab eo* (a full word space, plainly). §8.4's split/join
asymmetry cannot be resolved by policy: **every split has to be read.**

### One marker kept, with its lemma corrected

**0241C is *lchari*, not *lehari*.** The plate's second letter is an open **c** against
the crossbarred *e* of *stulte* on the same line, at 8× on the native page. Migne's own
misprint (for *Icari* — the Daedalus sentence two lines above demands Icarus) stands as
a `[sic:]`; only what we quote it as changes, in the TEI and the English alike.
