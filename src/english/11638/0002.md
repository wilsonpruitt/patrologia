## THE SECOND ALLEGORY CONCERNING JACOB.

With abounding memory, in the delight of the Holy Spirit, we ought to call to mind that in the earlier part of this book we spoke of the journey of Jacob the patriarch to the Eastern land, and (in so far as the Holy Spirit at that time supplied us with strength) the poverty of our wit discussed that same Jacob and the Eastern land by interpreting them in an allegorical sense. It remains, [0496B] therefore, that we go over it again in like manner, and — not as is worthy, but as the thinness of our understanding can in some fashion take it in — seek out the sweetly fragrant flowers of a second allegory. For the divine page is a certain field overflowing with flowers, in which the faithful soul ought to take delight, gathering the various flowers of allegory, that is, meditating upon the spiritual senses. Whence David the prophet says: *Delight thou in the Lord, and he will give thee the petitions of thy heart [n: (Psal. XXXVI.)]* The Eastern land, therefore, is justly called the heavenly Jerusalem, namely the dwelling of the citizens on high, of which the Psalmist says: *My portion in the land of the living [n: (Psal. CXLI.)]* To which land whoever shall be able to come by working well shall not taste death any more; because then every holy man is born again in God by an eternal birth, when his temporal life is ended by a [0496C] conditional death. For this cause an elegant custom has grown up in holy Church, that we proclaim the birthdays of the saints then, when we learn that they have passed out of this light. Who then is that Jacob, who is interpreted *wrestler*, save our spirit *290*, upon whom a wrestling against spiritual wickednesses is enjoined without interval? Let our Jacob, therefore, journey to the land of the East, that is, let our spirit hasten with the steps of good working to the land of promise: to which land of promise Paul was sighing, when he said: *Let us hasten to enter into that rest [n: (Hebr. IV)]*. But as we hasten toward the heavenly Jerusalem, a well which is closed with a great stone meets us, because, as David the prophet says: *The judgments of God are a great [0496D] deep [n: (Psal. XXXV)]*. [var: Vulg. Psal. XXXV, 7 *judicia tua*, "thy judgments"] With whom Solomon agrees: *All things,* he says, *are reserved uncertain for man after death [n: (Eccle. IX)]*. [var: Vulg. Eccle. IX, 2 *sed omnia in futurum servantur incerta*, "but all things are kept uncertain for the time to come"] But what do we say that this well is, which is closed with a great stone, save the hidden and terrible counsel of God, which is in no wise made manifest until the day of judgment? At which the prophet, in dread, says: *Come and see the works of God, terrible in his counsels over the sons of men [n: (Psal. LXV)]*. And what do the shepherds there with their flocks signify, who lie down about the well, save the faithful souls both of prelates and of subjects, who await the judgment of God? Whence it is well said that, when the shepherds had been gathered together with their flocks, then the stone was removed from the mouth of the well, and so the sheep were watered: because, when all men come to the judgment of God, his terrible counsel, [0497A] which is now hidden, shall be made manifest. Then at last shall the sheep be watered with the water of the unsealed well, when every man shall be damned or saved according to the just judgment of the hidden counsel of God: *Go, ye cursed, into everlasting fire which is prepared [n: (Matt. XXV)]*; [var: Vulg. Matth. XXV, 41 *Discedite a me maledicti in ignem aeternum, qui paratus est diabolo*, "Depart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, which was prepared for the devil"] but to those who shall be on the right hand: *Come, ye blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom [n: (ibid.)]* [var: Vulg. Matth. XXV, 34 *possidete paratum vobis regnum*, "possess the kingdom prepared for you"] And so in a wonderful and unspeakable manner shall Jacob know again the face of his kinswoman Rachel, when our spirit shall look upon the contemplative life in the heavens, for which it long sighed while yet set upon the earth. And thence it is that Rachel is interpreted *a beginning seen,* because then not *in a riddle* nor *through a glass,* but *face to face [n: (I Cor. XIII)]* shall our spirit contemplate God himself. But the kiss which Jacob gave to Rachel [0497B] we can interpret under that saying of the Lord in which it is said: *Father, I will that, as I and thou are one, so they also may be one in us [n: (Joan. XVIII)]*. [var: Vulg. Joan. XVII, 21 *ut omnes unum sint, sicut tu Pater in me, et ego in te, ut et ipsi in nobis unum sint*, "that they all may be one, as thou, Father, in me, and I in thee"] Clearer than light, therefore, it stands, that when one gives to another the kiss of peace, without the setting of any thing between, he joins mouth to mouth with a charitable affection. Hence it is that the blessed John says: *We are the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be; but we know that, when he shall appear, we shall be like to him, because we shall see him as he is [n: (Joan. III)]*. [var: Vulg. I Joan. III, 2 *nondum apparuit*, "it hath not yet appeared"] There shall not be, therefore, between the Creator and the creature any opposition; otherwise it would not be true and perfect blessedness.

## RECAPITULATION FROM WHAT WENT BEFORE ACCORDING TO ETHICS, *That is, the moral understanding.*

[0497C]Coming to morality, and desiring to discourse of morality, we must be men of good manners; for neither can we say anything elegantly of the manners of men, unless in some part we have been instructed or adorned with manners. We said above (unless perchance it has slipped from our mind) that this book takes its beginning from perfection, that is, from the letter O, which signifies perfection. Which letter, according to the Latins, as the philosophers say, intimates in itself the form of the whole world; and, formed according to the Greeks, represents to us, after its little measure, the holy Trinity. Thou, therefore, who readest or hearest this book, be zealous for perfection, be busy to be perfect. Hear not me, but the Lord saying in the Gospel: *Be [0497D] ye perfect, as your Father also is perfect who is in the heavens [n: (Matth. V.)]* [var: Vulg. Matth. V, 48 *Estote ergo vos perfecti, sicut et Pater vester caelestis perfectus est*, "Be you therefore perfect, as also your heavenly Father is perfect"] Nevertheless thou shalt in no wise be able to be perfect, unless, iniquity being put away, thou hast perfectly cleaved to that highest perfection which is God. Which perfection assuredly David desired, who said: *But for me it is good to cleave to God [n: (Psal. LXXII.)]* Perfectly, therefore, canst thou be perfect, if thou believest and holdest in thy heart the faith of the holy Trinity, and fulfillest that same faith with good works. Perfectly, I say, shalt thou present thyself perfect to God in the day of judgment, if in this world, so far as thou art able, thou keepest thyself free from all sin. But since, as the blessed Gregory says, a good work avails nothing without chastity, as neither does chastity avail anything without a good work, therefore rightly indeed in our recapitulation let the lilies follow, they signify virginity [0498A] and chastity and cleanness. There are, moreover, certain men who are virgins and chaste, but are not clean, because they are insolently proud of the keeping of their virginity or chastity. For Eve, our first parent, was indeed in body a virgin and chaste, but she stood forth unclean in mind, because she obeyed the devil promising falsehoods rather than God threatening truly. Thence the Holy Scripture says: He that exalteth his heart is clean before God; nor does it profit anything to be a virgin and chaste in body, if one has been unchaste in mind. [var: Vulg. Prov. XVI, 5 *abominatio Domini est omnis arrogans*; the maxim as commonly cited runs *immundus est coram Deo omnis qui exaltat cor suum*, "unclean before God"] Let there flower again in us, therefore, the spiritual lilies, namely the marks of virginity and of chastity and of cleanness of heart, so that, made chaste in body and soul, we may deserve to be marked with that evangelical blessing by which those are ennobled to whom it is said by the Lord: *Blessed [0498B] are the clean of heart, for they themselves* *291* *shall see God [n: (Matth. V.)]* Whence deservedly the violets follow in the order of the recapitulation, which shine again with an airy brightness, because those keep chastity well who through the observance of it seek to come to the glory of the heavenly kingdom. But the other flowers which in this exposition are not expressed by a special name fittingly intimate the remaining virtues, by which the aforesaid virtues are adorned. For without humility, obedience, charity, patience, long-suffering, perseverance, prudence, and discretion, which is the mother of all the virtues, no man can hold fast perfection, nor perfectly possess peace or concord (which are betokened by the kiss of the Bridegroom and the Bride).
