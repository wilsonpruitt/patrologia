## *292* HERE BEGINS THE SECOND MORALITY, INTERMINGLED WITH FLOWERS OF ALLEGORY.

Concerning Isaac the patriarch the text of the history has told to our ears how of old he commanded his servants to dig a well. And they, digging deeper, found water abundantly, and therefore called that well Abundance. For what did that well [0500A] which the servants of Isaac dug set forth, save the Old Testament, which, before it was expounded by the doctors, remained shut up by a barrier of earth? Let us therefore dig this well with a spiritual hoe, and without doubt we shall find abounding waters, that is, the spiritual senses. In the preceding treatise we spoke above of Jacob allegorically and morally; but now let us return by a spiritual road to the same Jacob, and let us seek out in him a second morality with a loftier understanding. Which morality is here and there intermingled with certain allegorical flowers, lest it be judged by prudent and vigorous men to be uncultivated. Behold thyself, O man, and, when thou art considered, thou shalt find in thyself, by a figure, the whole world, and — that I may speak more boldly — if thou hast lived well, thy very Creator himself. If, [0500B] therefore, man is of so great dignity that he holds in himself, in a certain manner, the likeness not only of the whole world, but even of God himself — for man was made to the image and likeness of God [n: (Gen. I)] — it ought not to seem absurd to any if we wish to interpret our spirit or soul, by alternating turns, now as Jacob, now as the Eastern land.

Let us say that our spirit is itself the Eastern land, and meanwhile the spiritual Jacob. For as the historical Jacob wrestled with the Angel and prevailed against him; and as the allegorical Jacob, that is, Christ, triumphing over death and the devil, overcame them on the wood of his cross; so the moral Jacob, that is, our spirit, ought to crucify to itself this world, and to fight against itself, so that, ruling over its own mind, [0500C] stronger as the stormer of cities. Crucify to himself the world, I say, must the spiritual man, that he may be able to say with the apostle Paul: *The world is crucified to me, and I to the world [n: (Galat. VI)]*. He must, I say, labour beyond his strength, the inward man, that he may overcome [sic: *vineat*] himself and the world, quickened by the example of the Lord Jesus, who says: *Have confidence, for I have overcome the world [n: (Joan. XVI)]*. Nor let him strive with less than the utmost care, that he may be able to be made the Eastern land, out of which the vices are ceaselessly rooted up, and in which the spiritual virtues arise. And the way of the spiritual Jacob, that is, of our spirit, ought rightly to be understood as Christ, who says: *I am the way [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. The way, I say, which leads straight to God, is simple obedience itself. Whence also, according to the history, Jacob was obeying his mother Rebecca [0500D] when he was going upon that way of which we speak. There is, therefore, no straighter way than obedience; and there is no true and straight obedience save in him who said: *I am the truth and the life [n: (ibid.)]*. That place to which Jacob is reported to have come at his first lodging, that is, the house of God, or Luz, is interpreted *the nut of Beth-el* or *the almond*. Let our spirit, therefore, intent upon assiduous prayers, be made the house of God, because the Lord says thus: *My house shall be called the house of prayer [n: (Matth. XXI)]*. Nevertheless, let him who desires to be made the house of God not refuse to become a nut or an almond. For neither can our spirit be the dwelling-place of God without fortitude and patience, long-suffering and prudence; which the hardest and almost unconquerable shell of these fruits signifies. And that Jacob [0501A] willed to rest after the setting of the sun signifies that our spirit then perfectly rests in God, when it mortifies and kills in itself everything that belongs to sin. And the stones which he gathered and put under his head betoken the examples of the saints, which, laying them up in our head, that is, in our mind, and living according to them, we perfectly despise the world. And that the saints are called stones, the apostle Peter testifies, who says: *And you, as living stones, be built up into a dwelling-place of the Holy Spirit [n: (I Petr. II)]*. [var: Vulg. I Petr. II, 5 *superaedificamini domus spiritualis*, "be built up a spiritual house"] Nor is it any wonder, indeed, since the Lord Jesus himself is called in David [n: (Psal. CXVII)] the corner stone, and in Daniel: *A stone cut out of the mountain without hands [n: (Dan. II)]*. Jacob's falling asleep sets forth that we ought perfectly to renounce [0501B] the world and to cleave to Christ; or it signifies the contemplation of the highest Godhead and the forgetting of all worldly vanity. The ladder standing upon two pieces of wood, which Jacob saw in the heights, whose top pierced the secret places of heaven, is the two Testaments, which teach and instruct us, and lead us through to the knowledge of the divine majesty. Whence the history says well, that Jacob saw the Lord leaning upon the top of the ladder. For by no reasoning can we come to the Lord himself, unless, after our little measure, we have spiritually fulfilled the commandments of the two Testaments. O how great is the clemency of our Redeemer What then did God's leaning upon the top of the ladder prefigure, save his own coming down for the redemption of the human race, which even then from that [0501C] time he was already in some manner preparing? That stone which Jacob, when his sleep was done, anointed before the other stones, signified Christ, filled before all the saints with the charisms of the Holy Spirit.

*293* Of which spiritual anointing and incomprehensible pouring-in of the Holy Spirit, David the prophet speaks to the Son of God: *Because thou hast loved justice and hast held iniquity in hatred, therefore God thy Father hath anointed thee with the oil of jubilation above all thy saints [n: (Psal. XLIV)]*. [var: Vulg. Psal. XLIV, 8 *unxit te Deus, Deus tuus, oleo laetitiae prae consortibus tuis*, "God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows"] And the stone which Jacob anointed and set up for a title signifies that Christ, through the mystery of the Incarnation, was to obtain in holy Church the place of the pontifical dignity, and to possess throughout the whole world the sceptres of royal authority. Whence deservedly [0501D] Jacob says: *That truly the Lord is in this place [n: (Gen. XXVIII)]*, because outside the catholic Church no one can be saved, nor please God. Moreover, if we wish to interpret this ladder according to morality, not unfittingly do we understand by its two pieces of wood faith and hope, which as it were take us up upon the earth and, lifting us up, bring us in even to the secret places of heaven. For in this world faith and hope are necessary to us, which in the heavens shall have no work [n: (Rom. VIII)]; because, as the Scripture says, God, in whom we believe and hope, and whom we await through patience, in the world to come *as he is we shall see [n: (I Joan. III)]*, and therefore we have no further need of faith and hope. The rungs which are set into the two pieces of wood of this ladder can be understood as the remaining virtues, without which faith and [0502A] hope stand utterly useless. For as our body dies without the soul, so faith without works, James bearing witness, is called dead [n: (Jacob. II)]. And therefore rightly indeed after faith and hope charity follows in the order, which is divided into two, that is, into the love of God and of neighbour. For he who only believes in God or hopes in him, and yet neither loves God himself, nor loves his neighbour for God's sake, and moreover refuses to work that which is good, it plainly appears that he stands like the demons. Of whom the apostle James says: *The demons believe and tremble [n: (ibid.)]*. Let us set, therefore, as the first rung of this ladder which leads to heaven, humility, the love of God and of neighbour, good thoughts, better speech, best works, chastity of body and of soul, cleanness [0502B] of heart, brotherly peace, concord, confession, penance, bodily discipline, fasts, vigils, prayers, exercises of the body for God's sake, and spiritual meditations in the law of God, contempt of the world, disdain of our body, spiritual desires; and at the top of this ladder let us set obedience, because it is she who leads us to the contemplation of the majesty on high. For thus the Holy Scripture says of obedience: Through obedience they come to the knowledge of the hidden things of God [n: (II Cor. II)]. Let our spirit ascend, therefore, by this ladder: *From virtue to virtue,* until it see God, *downward in Sion,* reigning [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)]. [var: Vulg. Psal. LXXXIII, 8 *videbitur Deus deorum in Sion*, "the God of gods shall be seen in Sion"] For this cause, finally, the Lord also seems to be leaning upon obedience, which is set at the top of this ladder, because through obedience [0502C] he was disposing to reconcile the human race to himself, and, according to the apostle Paul [n: (Coloss. I)], to make peace by a spiritual concord between the things that are in heaven and the things that are on earth; whence the same Paul says: *Christ was made obedient to the Father unto death [n: (Philipp. II)]*. [var: Vulg. Philipp. II, 8 *factus obediens usque ad mortem*, without *Patri*] And in another place: *From those things which he suffered, though he was the Son of God, he learned obedience [n: (Hebr. V)]*. And that Jacob, awaking, says: *Truly the Lord is in this place [n: (Gen. XXVIII)]*, plainly declares that God then truly dwells in our heart, if our spirit shall have perfectly possessed the aforesaid ladder in itself, and our mind, the vanities of the world being spurned, shall have fallen asleep in the love of God with a sweet slumber. Indeed we can morally be the anointed stone, if we have received the anointing from the Holy Spirit, as those received it to whom John [0502D] the apostle was saying: *And you have received the anointing from the Holy Spirit, and you have no need that any man teach you, because his anointing teacheth you concerning all things [n: (II Joan. II)]*. [var: Vulg. I Joan. II, 27 *Et vos unctionem, quam accepistis ab eo, maneat in vobis*, "and as for you, let the unction which you have received from him abide in you"] And the field in which the well is found by Jacob is not unfittingly understood as our spirit or soul, if only it shall have been filled with the fragrant sweetness of spiritual flowers, as was he who said: *We are the good odour of Christ [n: (II Cor. II)]*. But the well in this place ought to be understood as the Holy Spirit, of whom in the Gospel the Lord Jesus was saying: *If any man thirst, let him come to me and drink, and out of his belly shall flow living waters. Now this he said of the Holy Spirit, whom they that believed in him were to receive [n: (Joan. VII)]*. [var: Vulg. Joan. VII, 38 *Qui credit in me, sicut dicit Scriptura, flumina de ventre eius fluent aquae vivae*, "He that believeth in me, as the Scripture saith, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water"]
