## HERE BEGINS THE SECOND TOME Upon that which is said: *Thy breasts are better than wine, fragrant with the best ointments.*

[0503B]But that those things which follow in this book may shine out to us more clearly, it is needful that what precedes in this book should not depart from our memory. For thus says [0503C] blessed Jerome: For the divine discourse cleaves to itself in turn, and what follows hangs upon what goes before. For just as the manna which once came down from heaven to earth was one thing and signified another, so without doubt the divine utterances show one thing outwardly and hide another within. For that manna which God rained upon the children of Israel was bodily food, yet spiritually it signified the flesh of Christ, who says in the Gospel: *My flesh is meat indeed, and my blood is drink indeed [n: (Joan. VI)]*. Of which blood indeed the dew held the figure, which came down from heaven together with the manna. As therefore the Israelites, gathering the manna [n: (Num. XI)], ground it in a mill, and having bread therefrom, prepared for themselves every delight and every sweetness; [0503D] so the sons of spiritual grace and the enemies of the killing letter ought to break open and to ruminate Holy Scripture with spiritual senses, and thence to serve out spiritual food to their hearers. Let us not therefore be of the servants of Abraham, who, remaining at the foot of the mountain, keep the ass; but let us go up with Abraham and Isaac into the high mountain, that we may be able to see the Angel, and the ram clinging by its horns among the briers [n: (Gen. XXII)]. Who then are they who, remaining at the foot of the mountain, keep the ass, but they who in the sacred utterance do not seek the spiritual and higher sense? For it is as it were the part of a brute beast to keep watch beside the mountain — to be unwilling to seek the allegorical and moral sense in the divine letters. But the Angel who appeared to Abraham on the mountain after he had left the ass signifies the allegory which makes itself known more fully to those whom the historical [0504B] understanding does not hold back from the ascent of spiritual intelligence. And well is the same Angel, who is set for a figure of allegory, reported to have cried out from heaven, because spiritual allegory always [0504C] preaches the mysteries of the Church and the heavenly sacraments. But the ram which clung by its horns among the briers signified, not unfittingly, morality, which hangs upon the roughness of the letter. Whence well is the same ram said not to have been fixed fast, nor bound, among the briers, because morality is either rarely or never fully found by experience within the sequence of the letter, but is as a ram clinging by its horns among briers; and so morality itself is prudently drawn off, by prudent and lettered men, from the history itself to which it subtly cleaves. Let us consider therefore how reasonably the Bride makes an apostrophe to the Bridegroom, that is, how holy Church turns to Christ and says: For this cause have I desired thy coming. that is, thy kiss, because *thy breasts are better than wine.* We can in this place, with sound understanding, [0504D] understand the two breasts of Christ to be his mercy and his clemency, in such wise at least that mercy be attributed to his humanity, and clemency to his divinity. Whence the prophet Jonas says [n: (cap. IV)] to the Lord: *I know, O Lord, that thou art clement and merciful* [var: Vulg. Jon. 4:2 *Scio enim quia tu Deus clemens et misericors es*, "for I know that thou art a God clement and merciful"]; as if he should say: I know for certain that thou, who now art made known to all as God only, it shall come to pass that, our humanity being taken up, thou shalt be made man for us. Moreover, by the wine, which is known to be of a harsher savour, the severe justice of the Mosaic law is not undeservedly understood. Let the Bride therefore say to the Bridegroom, that is, the Church to Christ: For this cause have I desired thy coming, *because thy breasts are better than wine,* which is to say: Better is thy clemency and mercy than the severe and unmerciful justice of the ancient law. Finally, if spiritually by the two breasts of the Bridegroom which are in the divine page we understand allegory and [0505A] morality, and by the wine, the history, we in no way stray from the true sense. Let us also say with the Bride to the Bridegroom: *Thy breasts are better than wine,* that is, sweeter is morality and allegory than the for the most part hard and unreasonable understanding of the history. Otherwise: *Thy breasts are better than wine.* For the two breasts of God were the law and the prophets, which slew those they suckled. Whence Paul says: *The law worketh wrath,* (that is, death) *and the law brought nothing to perfection [n: (Rom. IV)]* [var: Vulg. Rom. 4:15 *lex enim iram operatur*; the second clause, *nihil ad perfectum adduxit lex*, is Hebr. 7:19]. Of these breasts Isaias says: *Whom shall he teach* *295* *knowledge, and whom shall he make to understand the hearing? them that are weaned from the milk, torn away from the breasts [n: (Isa. XXIX)]*; as if he should say: Christ will teach that spiritual knowledge and the hearing of his Gospel to him who shall utterly remove from himself the carnal and tasteless senses of the law and the prophets. [0505B]

Of these breasts David says: *From the breasts of my mother I was cast upon thee [n: (Psal. XXI)]* [var: Vulg. Ps. 21:10–11 divides these words: *spes mea ab uberibus matris meae. In te projectus sum ex utero*]; which is to say: I forsook the killing letter and the Jewish understanding of the law, and came forthwith to thee, who art the highest truth of the law. The law therefore and the prophets are in this place the breasts of God, but the wine is the Gospel, of which Christ says: *New wine into new bottles [n: (Matth. IX)]*, that is, into the apostles, must be put. Christ himself is the cluster, the wine is the Gospel. As from the cluster wine proceeds, so Christ founded the Gospel. Which is well prefigured in the history which relates that once two men carried upon their shoulders, on a staff, a cluster with its branch out of the land of promise [n: (Num. XIII)]. Those two men bearing on the staff the cluster with the branch [0505C] are the two peoples who slew Christ upon the cross: the Jews crying, *Crucify him [n: (Luc. XXIII; Joan. XIX)]*, and the Gentiles who set him upon the cross. Let the Bride therefore say to the Bridegroom, the Church to Christ: *Thy breasts are better than wine,* that is, the utterances of the law and the prophets, tempered and bedewed with the grace of evangelical preaching, are greatly bettered, that is, made sweeter and better. For the law said: *If any man shall have committed this or that sin, let him die [n: (Exod. XXI)]*. But the Gospel cries: If any man shall have sinned, let him confess, let him do penance, and he shall not die. Greatly therefore are thy breasts bettered, that is, made sweeter. *Fragrant with the best ointments.* Ointments are compounded of divers spices sweetly smelling. Let us seek therefore [0505D] these ointments. John the apostle says: *You have an unction from the Holy Spirit, and his unction teacheth you of all things [n: (I Joan. II)]* [var: Vulg. I Joan. 2:20 *vos unctionem habetis a Sancto*, "from the Holy One"]. Let us see finally the ointment which was set upon the head of Aaron, and ran down upon his beard. Aaron signified Christ, and his beard the apostles, who, as a beard, clung more familiarly than the other saints to their head, Christ. The ointment therefore set upon the head of Aaron and running down upon his beard are the divers gifts of the Holy Spirit coming down from Christ upon his apostles.

Filled with these ointments Paul said: *We are the good odour of Christ in every place [n: (II Cor. II)]* [var: Vulg. II Cor. 2:15 *Christi bonus odor sumus Deo*, "unto God"; *in omni loco* belongs to v. 14]. And elsewhere: *We give thanks to God, who hath manifested the odour of his knowledge by us in every place [n: (ibid.)]* [var: Vulg. II Cor. 2:14 *Deo autem gratias... et odorem notitiae suae manifestat per nos in omni loco*, present *manifestat*]. By these ointments therefore, that is, by the gifts of the Holy Spirit and by the apostolic teachings, [0506A] the breasts of the Bridegroom, anointed and smoothed — that is, the prophets and the law, tempered with the new wine of the Gospel — are made far more fragrant, that is, more odorous, than they were before. Strive therefore, O hearer, thou who hearest and readest what the breasts of the Bridegroom are; strive, I say, thou also to have breasts of thine own, such as Peter and Paul had, of whom the one said: *I gave you milk to drink [n: (I Cor. III)]*. But the other said: *As newborn babes, desire ye milk [n: (I Petr. II)]*. If therefore thou askest what these breasts are, I answer: They are the two precepts of charity, the love of God and of neighbour, that thou love God above all things, and thy neighbour as thyself. But these breasts they have not who love the world, because the more closely they are joined to it, the further they withdraw from God. Let these therefore hear the apostle James crying terribly: [0506B] *Whosoever shall will to be a friend of this world, shall be made an enemy of God [n: (Jac. IV)]*.
