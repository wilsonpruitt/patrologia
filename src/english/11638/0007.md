## RECAPITULATION FROM WHAT PRECEDES.

Above, by the inbreathing grace of the Holy Spirit, we said beforehand, and through the ears of the body poured into the minds of our hearers, that that manna which God once rained from heaven upon the children of Israel truly held the figure and image of the body of Christ. But perhaps there are now some who will say — either such as this opinion will not please, or such as are filled with a spiritual and higher intelligence: This opinion will breed loathing, in that we have strayed exceedingly from the divine understanding, we who have made a corruptible thing, namely the manna, equal to an incorruptible thing, namely the body of Christ. Assuredly, if they disdain to give credit to our words, let them see and consider [0506C] what in Exodus the text of the history utters to us even when we are silent: *It appeared,* he says, *in the wilderness small, and as it were beaten with a pestle, like unto the hoar frost upon the earth. And when the children of Israel had seen it, they said one to another: Manhu, which signifieth. What is this? For they knew not what it was. And Moses said to them: This is the bread which the Lord hath given you to eat [n: (Exod. XVI)]*. Let us see therefore whether perchance we may anywhere find Christ called bread. The Lord himself says in the Gospel: *I am the living bread which came down from heaven; if any man eat of this bread, he shall live for ever; and the bread which I will give is my flesh for the life of the world [n: (Joan. VI)]*. Behold how fitly the Old and the New Testament agree together. [0506D] What therefore Moses calls manna and heavenly bread, this the Lord in the Gospel calls bread coming down from heaven, and his own flesh. Nevertheless, the tumult of secular thoughts being for a little while removed from us, let us consider why the Holy Spirit says through them to Moses the prophet that it appeared small. Then, why the same manna is described to have been beaten with a pestle; and for what cause also it is reported to have lain upon the earth like unto the hoar frost. For if, according to the nature of the grammarian's *296* art, we look subtly upon this part, namely *minutum*, we find without doubt that *minutum* is said from the verb which is *minuo, minuis.* Not therefore by chance, but by a sure sacrament of reason, is the manna described to have been *minutum*, because it bore the figure of the body of Christ, of whom the prophet David speaks to God [0507A] the Father, saying: *Thou hast made him a little less than the angels, thou hast crowned him with glory and honour, and hast set him over the works of thy hands [n: (Psal. VIII)]*. Not undeservedly therefore is it related that the manna, appearing from above, is found small upon the earth, because that supernal Majesty of the Only-begotten Son made itself exceedingly small when it came down to bear the injuries of our mortality, and, taking the form of a servant, according to the word of Paul the apostle emptied itself [n: (Philip. II)]. Much, I say, was that manna fortified, when the Creator of heaven and earth and of all things lay hidden among men without the esteem of any dignity. Exceedingly, I say, did that manna appear fortified, when from his own creatures the Creator of the human race endured so many reproaches. Whence in the book of Exodus [0507B] it follows that the same heavenly manna is seen as it were beaten with a pestle. Which indeed most plainly agrees with the humanity of Christ, which, as we have already said before, bore so many dishonourings and so many revilings from [sic: *perficis*] men. Much indeed was this spiritual manna beaten with a pestle when the body of Christ was smeared with spittle, grievously handled with scourges, dishonoured with a crown of mocking honour, and moreover held by all for a laughing-stock. With the pestle, I say, of the dread passion was the spiritual manna beaten through, when the soldier's lance pierced the side of Christ, and the hardness of the nails bored through his hands and feet. It must therefore be confessed, all ambiguity being removed, that Christ was true God and true man, whose image or figure that ancient manna held. Without doubt he was man [0507C] who, bearing the pain of the passion, died, and commended his spirit into the hand of God the Father, saying: *Father, into thy hands I commend my spirit [n: (Luc. XXIII)]*. For he was true God, and the Son of God not adoptive but consubstantial and coeternal, who said in the Gospel: *I have power to lay down my soul, and to take it up again [n: (Joan. X)]*. But that the same manna had the likeness of hoar frost in no wise drives us back from the divine and mystical understanding, but rather subtly leads us in to understand a truth of such depth. It is clear to almost all men who use human reason that hoar frost is nothing else but water bound fast by frost, which appears chiefly at that season when the sun grows somewhat cool from the fervour of its own [0507D] burning, and the wintry air congeals with the roughness of its cold the water which it finds upon the earth. For this cause therefore, as we judge, did the manna appear upon the earth like unto hoar frost, that it might intimate to us the hardness or the unbelief of the Jewish people. Whence in the Gospel it is written: *It was the feast of the dedication at Jerusalem, and it was winter (ibid)*. The Jews therefore surrounded Christ, saying: *How long dost thou take away our soul: If thou be the Christ, tell us plainly [n: (ibid.)]*. It stands therefore clearer than light that the cold and torpor of unbelief held their hearts, who busied themselves to entrap Christ with snares, and refused to believe in him who had come for their own salvation, as though they knew not who he was. Indeed, to intimate the hardness of the hearts of the Jews [0508A] the evangelist says that it was winter when the Jews said to the Lord Jesus: *If thou be the Christ, tell us plainly.* Whence well does it follow in Exodus that when the children of Israel had seen the manna they said: *Manhu, which signifieth. What is this? For they knew not what it was.* For behold, the fathers in the wilderness, seeing the manna, say: *What is this?* But their sons, seeing him whose figure that manna held, say: *If thou be the Christ, tell us plainly.* Who therefore, filled with so great wonder, would not deservedly be astounded, when the ancient law and the new preaching of the Gospel cry one and the same thing? But concerning the Jews under the old law the text of the history relates that they were utterly ignorant what the manna was. And concerning their sons Paul the apostle, that excellent preacher of the new law, says: *For if they had known Jesus [0508B] the Lord, they would never have crucified the King of glory [n: (I Cor. II)]* [var: Vulg. I Cor. 2:8 *si enim cognovissent, nunquam Dominum gloriae crucifixissent*, "the Lord of glory"]. There remains therefore even to this day among the faithless Jews the ignorance of Manhu, but there remains among faithful Christians an unstained faith and knowledge of the Lord Jesus. Let the Jews at last ask Moses what Manhu is; let Moses answer them: This is the bread which the Lord hath given you from heaven to eat.

But let us, our own questioning laid to rest, receive with the intent ear of the heart what the Lord said in the Gospel to the Jews: *Your fathers did eat manna in the desert, and are dead [n: (Joan. VI)]*. But why are they dead? Because of that manna they believed nothing else than what they saw with bodily eyes. And in like manner their sons also, stricken with their fathers' unbelief and blindness, [0508C] judged Christ to be man only, and disdained to believe him true God and true Son of God.

It stands therefore for certain that both the fathers and the sons were condemned by a like death of unbelief. To whom well does the Lord say in the Gospel: *I said to you that you shall die in your sin [n: (Joan. VIII)]*. Which indeed the prophet Ezechiel had well prophesied long before, who had said: *And why will you die, ye children of Israel? because* *297* *I will not the death of the sinner, saith the Lord, but that he be converted and live [n: (Ezech. XVIII)]* [var: Vulg. Ezech. 18:31–32 *quare moriemini domus Israel... quia nolo mortem morientis*, "house of Israel," "of him that dieth"; *sed ut convertatur et vivat* answers rather to Ezech. 33:11]. He therefore who prophesies not of the present but of the future, saying: *You shall die,* manifestly declares the perfidy of the Jews, and that in him who was to be born for their sakes they would not [0508D] believe.
