## RECAPITULATION FROM WHAT PRECEDES, ON THE BREASTS OF THE BRIDEGROOM.

In the earlier treatise of this book, when according to our small measure we were disputing of the breasts of the Bridegroom, we interpreted those breasts of his, according to the spiritual intelligence, as clemency and mercy; in such wise at least that clemency be referred to his divinity, and mercy be attributed to his humanity. Of these spiritual breasts of the Bridegroom therefore, which are clemency and mercy, the figure is found in very many places throughout the sacred utterances. Whence is that passage in the third book of Kings [n: (cap. VI)]: *Solomon made,* he says, *in the entrance of the oracle two doors of olive wood, and graved upon them a [sic: victuram] of cherubim, and likenesses of palm trees, [0509A] and anaglypta standing out very far; and he covered with gold both the cherubim and the palms and the rest.* Of these same spiritual breasts of the Bridegroom, I say, namely of clemency and mercy, a figure is found in the same book of Kings [n: (cap. X)] under the name of hands, as it is written: *King Solomon made,* he says, *a great throne of ivory, and clothed it with the finest gold, which had six steps; and the top of the throne was round in the hinder part, and two hands on this side and on that holding the seat, and two lions stood beside each several hand, and twelve little lions standing upon the six steps on this side and on that.* But afterwards the history of Kings adds that *there was no such work made in any kingdoms [n: (ibid.)]*. These, I say, are the two spiritual breasts of the Bridegroom, which the prophet Zacharias [0509B] in his book [n: (cap. IV)] calls two olive trees. Even as it is written there: *I saw, and behold a candlestick all of gold, and its lamp upon the top of it, and its seven lights upon it, and seven funnels for the lights that were upon the top thereof, and two olive trees over it, one upon the right of the lamp, and one upon the left thereof,* and in what follows, when the same prophet asked of the Angel who was speaking to him what the two olive trees were, or the two branches of the olive trees which are beside the two golden beaks, in which are the funnels of gold, it was answered him by the holy Angel: *These are two sons of the oil of brightness, who stand before the Ruler of the whole earth [n: (ibid.)]* [var: Vulg. Zach. 4:14 *isti sunt duo filii olei*, without *splendoris*]. If now at length we return to the order of our narration, and show how the two doors of olive wood set in the entrance [0509C] of the oracle signify the two breasts of the Bridegroom, clemency and mercy — nevertheless, the veil of the killing letter being removed, it must first be said to the friends of spiritual grace that he who in the Canticle of Canticles is called the Bridegroom, he himself is called the oracle of the temple and the ivory throne in the book of Kings. Him finally the prophet Zacharias also named, in the Holy Spirit, a golden candlestick. As therefore in the human body the breasts are bodily set, so in Christ these two virtues are naturally implanted, namely clemency and mercy. Whence they are deservedly called his breasts, because by the sweet affluence of these virtues the world is filled, nourished, and sustained. Of which affluence indeed the prophet David [0509D] says: *The earth is full of the mercy of the Lord [n: (Psal. XXXII)]*. For how could this world subsist, which is weighed down with so many iniquities, or how would the race of mortals obtain salvation, if it were judged with the clemency and mercy of God removed? It is manifest that neither the world nor man could subsist for a single hour of the day, if God were willing to look upon the evils which are done by men in this world, and not upon his own clemency. Wherefore the prophet David also says: *O Lord, do not unto us according to our sins, neither reward us according to our iniquities [n: (Psal. CII)]* [var: Vulg. Ps. 102:10 is indicative: *non secundum peccata nostra fecit nobis, neque secundum iniquitates nostras retribuit nobis*, "he hath not dealt with us according to our sins"]. And so well are the breasts of the Bridegroom taken by the two doors of the oracle, because as through those doors they entered who were to enter the oracle, so they who are to enter the kingdom of heaven shall enter not by their own merits [0510A] or works, but by the clemency and mercy of God. Nevertheless, what do we judge it to be, that upon the doors of the oracle cherubim and palms, and *anaglyta* also, are said to have been graved? It is manifestly plain that cherubim is interpreted *a multitude of knowledge* or *of wisdom*, and it is known to many that in ancient times the hands of the victorious were adorned with palms for a token of praise and of victory. But an *anaglyptum*, so far as it is given us to understand, is said to be a certain kind of sculpture whose hinder part lies hidden within, but whose front part, shaped by the sculptor's work of human skill, stands out beyond measure. Not undeservedly therefore are cherubim found to have been graven upon the doors of the oracle, which are interpreted *a multitude of knowledge* or *of wisdom*, because indeed I reckon it just that they who hope in the clemency [0510B] and mercy of God should in no wise depart from true knowledge or wisdom. For there is no greater wisdom than that a man should withdraw utterly from evil, and without interval do the good whereby he may please God. Which wisdom the Psalmist took care to comprise in few words, saying: *Turn away from evil, and do good [n: (Psal. XXXVI)]*. But the likenesses of palm trees follow in right order *298* after the cherubim, because their hands are justly adorned with the palm of victory who sweat at good works with intent devotion, and, so far as is lawful to mortals, guard themselves from evils with anxious purpose. Whence well has ecclesiastical authority long since obtained, by excellent usage, that the right hands of the martyrs of Christ and of virgins ought to be marked with palms worthy of praise, who by despising [0510C] overcame the present world, and by keeping their virginity and chastity did not hesitate to give over their bodies to martyrdom for love of the heavenly kingdom. It is indeed a mighty victory to keep the integrity of mind and body in this world, and to despise oneself along with the present world by overcoming it even unto death. Wherefore the very Creator of the world, who by despising had trodden down the wicked world, said to his soldiers with a voice of exhortation: *Be of good heart, for I have overcome the world [n: (Joan. XVI)]*. And finally we can in no way better overcome the world than by renouncing utterly both it and those things which are of its right. But he who already restrains himself from evil and works well, who keeps virginity or chastity, and is seen to have perfectly renounced [0510D] the world, it is expedient for him that he draw not back the reins of his course from the appetite of the other virtues; of which virtues, namely, the *anaglypta* which are found graven upon the doors of the oracle are known spiritually to hold the figure. For *anaglypta*, as we said above, are sculptures which are hidden within in their hinder part, but whose front part is clearly manifest to all beholders.

He therefore who within abounds in the bowels of charity, and without bestows the works of that same charity bountifully upon his neighbours; who keeps obedience in mind, and shows forth the works of that same obedience at times in operation; who is humble and meek in heart, and has the tokens of that same humility and meekness in his outward showing; who expresses in himself the remaining traces of the virtues [0511A] — from his mind and body, beyond all doubt, there come forth spiritually *anaglypta* standing out very far. And so at last must we think concerning the remaining virtues. But if any man loves chastity within, if he mortifies the flesh without, if he fasts, watches, labours and prays, he, all doubtfulness removed, shows that *anaglypta* have been carved in his own flesh. Since therefore without faith it is impossible to be saved, therefore does the history deservedly add that Solomon covered with gold the cherubim and the palms and the *anaglypta*. For he who shall have abounded in the aforesaid virtues, which are signified by the cherubim and the palms and the *anaglypta*, if nevertheless he shall have lacked the Catholic faith, will in no wise be able to be saved. But that by gold in Holy Scripture the Catholic faith is understood, blessed Peter teaches, who says: *That your faith may be much more precious [0511B] than gold which is tried by the fire [n: (I Petr. I)]* [var: Vulg. I Petr. 1:7 *ut probatio fidei vestrae multo pretiosior auro quod per ignem probatur*, "the trial of your faith"]. Behold, we have shown that by the two doors of the oracle the two breasts of the Bridegroom, namely the clemency and the mercy of God, ought spiritually to be understood.
