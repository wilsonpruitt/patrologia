## *301* RECAPITULATION FROM WHAT PRECEDES. ON THE GOLDEN AND IVORY THRONE WHICH SOLOMON MADE, HOW IT OUGHT TO BE REFERRED TO THE PERSON OF HOLY CHURCH.

Almost all the sacramental words which are found overshadowed in the divinely inspired Scriptures are sometimes [0515D] indeed referred to our head, who is Christ; sometimes, however, they ought to be assigned universally to holy Church, which is the body of that same head of ours; sometimes they can not unfittingly be fitted to each several one of the faithful, who are the members of the aforesaid head and body.

That this is so, let us know for certain, that as the ivory throne, of which we have already often spoken, befits the Lord Jesus Christ, so consequently it agrees with holy Church, which is his body. But since already, so far as the faculty of our wit sufficed, we have allegorically assigned the aforesaid throne to our head, Christ, it remains now that we ascribe the same, in an allegorical sense, to our body, holy Church. [0516A] *Solomon made,* he says, *a great throne of ivory [n: (III Reg. X)]*. For because Solomon is interpreted *peaceable,* not undeservedly is the Lord Jesus Christ taken by Solomon. Of whom indeed Paul the apostle says: *He is our peace, who hath made both one [n: (Ephes. II.)]* This our peaceable king therefore made an ivory throne, because, as the divine page testifies: *Wisdom hath built herself a house [n: (Prov. IX)]*; and because holy Church in all her teachings preaches that chastity is chiefly to be kept, it is elegantly established also that that throne, which prefigured holy Church, was built of ivory. For ivory is the bone of the elephant, which beast is said to be of so great coldness that either rarely, or once in the year, as we judge, it scarcely comes to pay the dues of natural coupling. [0516B] Which indeed becomingly alludes to the sacraments of holy Church, which utterly forbids conjugal works to not a few of her sons, but to some grants conjugal acts by indulgence, for the repairing of the succession of human offspring. There is besides another sacrament which is wont to be told of the elephant and of the dragon, which without doubt ought specially to be assigned to holy Church: for the [sic: *draeo*], as blessed Augustine says, is a serpent or beast of twelve cubits in length, which pursues the elephant for this cause, that its blood is a drink very delightful to him. Wherefore, when he wills to kill him, that he may delightfully drink up the blood which he desires, with the length of his body and tail he ensnares the feet of the elephant, that the elephant may not be able anywhere to turn aside [0516C] or to go forward. And so it comes about in a wondrous manner that, while the elephant, his feet ensnared, is overthrown and pressed down upon the dragon, he crushes and slays with the mass of his own body that very dragon which was contriving to bring death upon him. What then does this elephant signify, but holy Church? And what that dragon, but the devil, who with assiduous machinations persecutes that same holy Church? Whence also in his Apocalypse John the apostle says: *And the dragon persecuted the woman, and the woman fled into the wilderness. And the dragon sent a river after the woman, that he might cause her to be carried away by the river [n: (Apoc. XII)]* [var: Vulg. Apoc. 12:15 *et misit serpens ex ore suo post mulierem aquam tamquam flumen*, "the serpent... water as it were a river"]. And this is that woman, namely holy Church, of whom it is said through Solomon: *Who shall find a valiant woman? [n: (Prov. XXXI.)]* It is besides to be known that holy Church, which is called the Bride and the dove and the [0516D] beloved in the Canticle of Canticles [n: (cap. II)], is herself called the ivory throne in the book of Kings. And her whom Solomon calls a valiant woman, John in the Apocalypse names a woman fleeing into the wilderness. She is finally the house which wisdom built, standing upon seven pillars. But as to what is said, that that throne which Solomon made of ivory was great, this is in no wise contrary to holy Church. For she it is who in her head, namely Christ, has penetrated the height of heaven, and, borne up by her feet, namely the apostles, has come even to the utmost bounds of the whole world. Which indeed is well prefigured in the prophet Daniel, when to that most impious king Nabuchodonosor holy Church is shown by a vision under the appearance of a certain great [0517A] tree. As it is written there: *I saw, and behold a certain great tree was in the midst of the earth, whose height reached unto heaven, and its branches were spread out unto the ends of the earth [n: (Dan. IV.)]* [var: Vulg. Dan. 4:8 *aspectus illius erat usque ad terminos universae terrae*, "the sight thereof was unto the ends of the whole earth"; the branches enter only at v. 9] Beneath it dwelt the beasts of the field, and in its branches abode the birds of the air, and the food of all was in that tree, and of it all flesh did eat. The tree therefore in the midst of the earth is holy Church, of which it is said that *God hath wrought salvation in the midst of the earth [n: (Psal. LXXIII)]*. Its height, which reaches unto heaven, is the head of holy Church, Christ, of whom Paul says: *We have a great high priest, Christ Jesus, who hath penetrated the heavens [n: (Hebr. IV)]* [var: Vulg. Hebr. 4:14 *pontificem magnum qui penetravit caelos, Iesum Filium Dei*, "Jesus the Son of God"]. But its branches are the Apostles, to whom the Lord said: *I am the vine, you [0517B] are the branches [n: (Joan. XV)]*. Whence well is it said that its branches were spread out unto the ends of the earth, because *into all the earth hath gone forth the sound* of the apostles, *and their words unto the ends of the world [n: (Psal. XVIII)]*. But as to what the Prophet subjoined, that under the earth dwell the beasts of the field, and in its branches abode *302* the birds of the air: by the beasts, bestial men can be taken, and by the birds, spiritual men ought to be understood. For the former, as being animal, obtain the lowest place in holy Church the latter however. as being spiritual, bear themselves up to lofty things on the wings of the virtues. But the food of all which is related to have been in that tree is the evangelical preaching, namely the refreshment of faithful souls, which is preached in the Church. And since indeed at the end of this vision it is added that [0517C] all flesh did eat of that tree, it is manifestly given to be understood that all flesh which is to be saved in the day of judgment must needs live according to the Gospel of Christ, and in no wise depart from holy Church. But as to what the history of Kings brought in besides, that Solomon clothed with radiant gold the throne which he had made, this in no wise drives us back from the ecclesiastical sacraments: for holy Church is clothed with the brightest gold, because she is adorned with every ornament of wisdom. For by gold wisdom is designated, as it is said through a certain wise man: *Wisdom that is hid, and treasure that is not seen, what profit is there in them both? [n: (Eccli. XL.)]* [var: Vulg. Eccli. 41:17 *sapientia absconsa et thesaurus invisus*] Moreover, if by gold we wish to understand the ornaments of the spiritual virtues, let us hear with devout mind how the Psalmist [0517D] meets our understanding. *All the glory,* he says, *of the king's daughter is within, in golden borders, clothed round about with varieties [n: (Psal. XXXXIV)]*. But the six steps which were in the aforesaid throne signify the six ecclesiastical degrees which are in holy Church, namely readers, doorkeepers, acolytes, subdeacons, levites, priests. For as by those six steps one ascended unto the seat of the throne, so indeed by these six ecclesiastical degrees one comes to the most holy altar. But as to what the book of Kings relates, that that throne was round, this signifies that holy Church, ascending from virtue to virtue, ever tends toward perfection. Whence in the Canticle of Canticles it is said: *Thy belly is like a turned bowl [n: (cap. VII)]* [var: Vulg. Cant. 7:2 *umbilicus tuus crater tornatilis... venter tuus sicut acervus tritici*, "thy navel is a turned bowl... thy belly as a heap of wheat"]. For whatever [0518A] is wrought upon the lathe, it plainly stands that it is presented more perfectly to human sight. But the two hands which hold the seat. are the divinity and the humanity of Christ, which in holy Church build for us the most holy Altar. For unless God were made man, his flesh would in no wise be daily offered upon the holy altar. And unless he himself, God, became man true God, he would in no wise be adored with the Father and the Holy Spirit in equal honour. The four lions which stood there beside the hands signify the four virtues, namely justice, fortitude, prudence, temperance, which in holy Church obtain the triumphal token. The twelve little lions which stood upon the six steps on this side and on that are the twelve apostles, namely the preachers of holy Church. For because lions are strong [0518B] animals and bear the type of royal dignity and authority, not undeservedly are the Apostles understood by the lions, whose royal dignity and kingly authority is preached through the four quarters of the world. But in the conclusion of this sentence, the history of Kings subjoined that such a work was not made in any kingdoms. Truly indeed does the history of Kings say it, since so wonderful a work has nowhere been made as is holy Church, which [sic: *quodidie*] lays open to her sons, through faith and working, the heavenly kingdoms.
