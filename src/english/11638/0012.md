## RECAPITULATION FROM WHAT IS ABOVE, CONCERNING THE IVORY THRONE: HOW IT OUGHT TO BE REFERRED TO THE MORAL SENSE.

He who wishes to instruct men in morals ought [0518C] himself to be instructed in his own morals. But because it is found hard that anyone should be adorned on every side with good morals, we ought not on that account to cease altogether from the building up of morals, since we hold this on the authority of the divine sayings: that our mind ought always to be intent upon good morals and works, and our tongue to publish abroad to all the praises of its Maker with untiring devotion. Whosoever thou art that readest or hearest that scripture which tells how Solomon made a great throne of ivory, attend not to that material throne only, but look to thy body and to thy soul, since indeed what was done in that throne materially, this thou canst find in thyself spiritually [0518D] and morally. For a throne of gold and of ivory thou shalt be able to be, spiritually and morally, if thou givest thy pains to the true wisdom which is signified by the gold, and if in mind and body thou strivest perfectly after the chastity which is shown by the ivory. There are, in fine, in thee those six steps by which one ascended to the perfect seat, namely the six works of mercy, which, because we reckon that we have already recounted them above, we here pass over in silence, that the reader be not wearied. And since the throne is said to have been round, it plainly shows that he desires to be the throne of God, let him strive after every perfection, let him keep mind and tongue and body from all sin. But the two hands which hold the seat are two things, namely the will of God and the present life, which keep the soul in this body. [0519A] But that the soul of every faithful man may be called a seat, this the divine page cries out even while we are silent, saying: *The soul of the just man is the seat of wisdom [n: (Sap. VII)]*. And the four lions which are beside the aforesaid hands can rightly be understood as four virtues, *303* namely fear and love, contempt and esteem. For we ought both to love and to fear the will of God, and the present life, as is expedient, both to esteem and to despise. But the twelve little lions which stand upon the six steps on this side and on that are the twelve sacraments of holy Church, namely the threefold renunciation of the devil, and the threefold profession of the catholic faith. the receiving of holy baptism, the communion of the body and blood of Christ. confession, penance, the forgiveness of sins in the Holy Spirit, [0519B] and perseverance in good works unto death. These, therefore, are the spiritual lions, which drive far from the souls of the faithful that lion who goes about seeking whom he may devour; and lest he presume to dwell in them any longer, they repel him by their own power and sanctification. But afterward the historian closed this saying with such an ending, saying: *There was not such work made in all kingdoms [n: (III Reg. X)]*. He who is well-mannered, whose morals breathe far and wide a most sweet odour, who unremittingly presses upon good morals and cleaves to them, who offers himself to God as a daily sacrifice, whose morals God approves and praises, who by the correcting of his morals is made spiritual out of animal, he beyond doubt can say of himself that *there was not such work made in all kingdoms.*
