## RECAPITULATION FROM THE FOREGOING, CONCERNING THE THRONE OF IVORY AND GOLD, IN WHAT MANNER, UNDER AN ALLEGORICAL INTERPRETATION, IT OUGHT TO BE REFERRED TO THE PERSON OF THE BLESSED AND GLORIOUS AND PERPETUAL EVER-VIRGIN MARY.

[0519C]

*Solomon made,* he says, *a great throne of ivory, and clothed it with gold exceedingly bright [n: (ibid.)]* [var: Vulg. III Reg. X, 18 *fecit etiam rex Salomon thronum de ebore grandem, et vestivit eum auro fulvo nimis*, "with exceeding tawny gold"]. I beseech thee, my most bright Lady, if I speak anything less worthy than thy dignity deserves, let this not turn out to me for the loss of my soul, but let it fall out for the increase of merit and of heavenly glory. For I know, I know, that if all my members were turned into tongues, I should not be strong enough to utter as is worthy thy praises, which the sublime glory of thy singular virginity deserves. I know, I say, my Lady, that even if [0519D] all that is mine could speak, yet they would not find in heaven or on earth anyone like to thee. In heaven, moreover, there is none like to thee, because if we treat of God, God is greater than thou, and therefore he is not thy like. But if our tongue wishes to say anything of the saints, or of the angels, or of any creatures, though created by God, it is manifest that thy dignity excels and surpasses all creatures, for as it is written. *Thou art exalted above the choirs of angels to the heavenly kingdoms.* Again I beseech thee, my Lady a thousand times more than most dear: thy little servants who gladly honour thee, and devoutly serve thee, and becomingly speak of thee what is worthy — help them all by thy prayers and merits, and sustain them, comfort them, keep them from all sin, and lead them through to the immortal joys of thy Son. [0520A] Thou art therefore that throne — not the one which Solomon the mortal king made, the son, that is, of David the king, but rather that one which God chose from eternity and fore-chose for himself to dwell in, namely the Son of God, the immortal King. Thou art that throne of which the Psalmist says: *Thy throne, O God, is for ever and ever; the rod of direction is the rod of thy kingdom [n: (Psal. XLIV)]* [var: Vulg. Psal. XLIV, 7 *sedes tua, Deus, in saeculum saeculi*, "thy seat"]. Thou art, I say, my Lady, dearer than all dear ones, a throne framed of heavenly gold and of spiritual ivory. By the gold is understood the divinity of Christ thy Son; but by the ivory, which is of a cold nature, thy most holy virginity. Of the gold, therefore, of divine majesty, and of the ivory of perpetual virginity art thou framed, since both the divinity of thy Son was ineffably overshadowed in thee, the Holy Spirit working with him, and thy virginity remains and has remained [0520B] unviolated to thee. Moreover, what the investigators of natures are wont to say about the nature of ivory I judge in no wise to be passed over in silence. For it agrees with blessed Mary, with her dignity, and with the sublime power of her help. For they say that if ivory be very often applied to an aching head, by its natural power it soothes and drives out all pain of the head. Those, therefore, who ache in the head — that is, in the mind — being wounded by the darts of vices, it is needful without doubt that they implore the help and aid of holy Mary the Mother of God, and say from the innermost affection of the heart: « We flee to thy protection, where the weak have received strength; and for this cause, O Virgin Mother of God, we render to thy dignity the giving of thanks. » Thou art, I say, Lady, the house which Wisdom, that is the Son, built for himself [n: (Prov. IX)], and [0520C] in which he set seven spiritual pillars, for comeliness and glory, with eternal stability: namely virginity, chastity, continence, humility, faith, hope, charity. Truly in blessed Mary there shone forth the singular privilege of incomparable virginity and the fragrance of odour-bearing chastity, since unless she had been a virgin and chaste, the Son of God would in no wise have chosen her to dwell in. For it was fitting that she should be a virgin who was redeeming the world, since that first parent was a virgin who had ruined the whole world. Since therefore blessed Mary the Mother of God was a virgin, she was consequently chaste and continent. But concerning her unspeakable humility, what can we bring forward worthy of her praises? For indeed when this most holy [0520D] and a thousand times *304* most blessed Virgin was chosen by the Angel to be mother of the Son of God, she confesses herself with humble devotion the handmaid of that same Son of hers, saying: *Behold the handmaid of the Lord; be it done unto me according to thy word [n: (Luc. I)]*. But the Mother of God had faith undefiled, since she believed, at the Angel's announcing, that she could become the Mother of God. And hope had set its lofty citadel in that most blessed Virgin, for although what she heard was unwonted and unheard-of in ages and generations gone by; yet she hoped with most certain faith that it must so come to pass as it was proclaimed to her by the Angel. How the Mother of God overflowed in the bowels of charity, no one can hold unknown who has been in any degree instructed in the knowledge of the divine law. For as her Son incurred the curse of the law for the redemption of the human race. [0521A] For cursed, says Moses [n: (Deut. XXI)], is he who shall hang upon a tree [var: Vulg. Deut. XXI, 23 *maledictus a Deo est qui pendet in ligno*, "cursed of God is he that hangeth on a tree"], so she too doubtless chose rather to lie under the curse of the law with the keeping of her virginity, than, virginity lost, to leave behind carnal sons in Israel. So, in fine, the Mosaic law said: cursed is he, or cursed is she, who has not left seed in Israel. Thou art therefore brighter and more splendid than all the brightness and splendour of the sun, far and incomparably more precious than every precious stone; thou art found more shining than every gleam of the stars, being as thou art the star of the sea; for thou art the brightness of eternal light, and the mirror without blemish of the divine majesty. Thou art the inheritance of the Lord; in thee the [sic: *Flius*] of God tarried. Thou canst say: *The Creator of all things, who created me, rested in my tabernacle [n: (Eccli XXIV)]*. Thou art the tabernacle set in the sun, [0521B] from which tabernacle the Son of God came forth *as a bridegroom out of his chamber [n: (Psal. XVIII)]*. Thy womb is the silver bed in which is that golden apple, of which Solomon says: *Golden apples on beds of silver — a word* brought forth *in its own time [n: (Prov. XXV)]* [var: Vulg. Prov. XXV, 11 *mala aurea in lectis argenteis qui loquitur verbum in tempore suo*, "he that speaketh a word in due season"]. For the special nativity of thine only-begotten Son could not be declared by a worthier or brighter comparison. For the apple brought forth on beds of silver is the Word made flesh in the womb of the Virgin. But the six steps which are described as having been in the ivory throne signify especially the six degrees of humility which blessed Mary had in her heart. Whence the Holy Spirit well speaks in the Song of Songs: *When the king was at his repose, my spikenard gave forth its odour [n: (Cant. I)]*. For spikenard is said to be an aromatic tree of low stature, [0521C] by which the humility of blessed Mary is fitly understood. Which virginal humility doubtless pleased the king resting at his repose, when that same king — the Son, that is, of the eternal King — descending from heaven took most holy flesh in the womb of the blessed Virgin Mary. But now let us see what the six degrees of humility are. For the first degree of humility is that a man confess himself created from earth, and acknowledge that for the desert of his own depravity he will return into ashes. But the second degree is that a man never grow proud over any grace received. The third, that for the benefits conferred upon him by the Lord he render thanks unceasingly to his Creator himself. The fourth, that a man esteem himself inferior to all in merits. The fifth, that [0521D] he judge himself equal to none. The sixth, that he in no wise reckon himself better than or superior to anyone. These are the six spiritual degrees of humility, by which God descends to the heart of him who humbles himself, and by which man ascends to the knowledge of God descending to him. But after these things the narrative intimated to us that that throne was round on its outer part. And in an earlier treatment I remember to have said that whatever is round seems to be perfect; what then does the roundness and perfection of the golden throne suggest to us, unless that blessed Mary was a virgin before childbirth, and after childbirth remained an unviolated virgin? And the hands indeed which held the seat of the ivory throne signify the two Testaments, which everywhere preached the humanity of Christ which he took in blessed Mary.

[0522A]The four lions therefore which stand beside the aforesaid hands are the four Gospels, which cry out the same thing as the two Testaments. The twelve little lions which are said to have been upon the six steps on this side and on that are the twelve virtues which it is agreed that blessed Mary assuredly possessed: namely spiritual life, joy in the Holy Spirit, long-suffering, patience, goodness, kindness, meekness, faith, incomparable modesty, virginity, compassion, perseverance in a holy purpose. For blessed Mary led a spiritual life, since unless her life had shone full of virtues, it would in no wise have pleased God. She doubtless had joy in the Holy Spirit, since she herself, filled with spiritual jubilation, said in her hymn: *My spirit hath rejoiced in [0522B] God my saviour [n: (Luc I)]*. Long-suffering indeed was not lacking to her, for the things which she heard from the Angel, though they were unheard-of and unwonted, she yet awaited with long-suffering that they might be fulfilled in her. She was fortified in no small measure with the virtue of patience, since the insults which she saw inflicted upon her Son — nay, even upon herself — by the faithless Jews, she bore with a patient mind. Who would deny that she had goodness and kindness, whom beyond doubt we confess to have flourished in all abundance? But meekness held the chief place in her, since unless she had been meek, the Holy Spirit would in no wise have chosen her to dwell in. But incomparable faith, as we have already recalled above, ranged through the inmost parts of her heart, since although it was against the laws of human nature that a Virgin was being chosen Mother of God, yet [0522C] she believed that it could so come to pass at the nod of God, as it was proclaimed to her *305* by the Angel. Her modesty, that is her religion, with what proclamations can we exalt as is worthy, she whom we know for certain to have been found by the Angel at leisure for prayer and silence. But the doors of her Virginity and the tokens of her most entire chastity, with what praises shall we proclaim them as is fitting, she who had so far unlearned to enjoy the converse of men that she was terrified even at the angel's entrance and address? Whence the Angel: *Fear not,* he says, *Mary, thou hast found grace with the Lord [n: (ibid.)]* [var: Vulg. Luc. I, 30 *invenisti enim gratiam apud Deum*, "with God"]. For when the Angel Gabriel proclaimed to her that glorious and ineffable Hail sent from God, because he saw her afraid, he straightway warned her not to fear anything. [0522D] But the compassion and grief which Blessed Mary endured at the death of her Son, no one is able to pour out clearly into human hearing. For she saw God and the Son of God, whom as a Virgin Mother she had conceived of the Holy Spirit, crucified by the most impious Jews, and — as though he were guilty of some crime — condemned beforehand along with thieves. But how could the inward parts of the Blessed Virgin Mary remain without the experience of grief, when she beheld her own flesh, which the Son of God had taken in her womb for the salvation of the human race, hanging upon the cross? The perseverance in a holy purpose and in good works which Blessed Mary had we can show by an easy judgment, since it is agreed, all ambiguity removed, that from her birth to the end of this mortal life she pleased God [0523A] in all things. But after these things the book of Kings closed the foregoing saying with such an ending, saying: *There was not such work made in all kingdoms [n: (III Reg. X)]*. O how admirable is that conclusion! Truly, such work was not made in all kingdoms as is that which a Virgin conceived of the Holy Spirit, a Virgin bore the Son of God, a Virgin remained unviolated after childbirth. [0524A] There was not, I say, from the beginning of the world such work made, that a Virgin Mother should suckle the Son of God with virginal breasts. Therefore, since to this second throne — that is, to this volume — the breasts of the Bridegroom gave the beginning, it is fitting that the breasts of the Bride, the Virgin and Mother, should grant to that same throne a suitable end.

[0523] *Here ends the second Tome on the Song of Songs.*
