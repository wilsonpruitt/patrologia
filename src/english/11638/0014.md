## HERE BEGINS THE THIRD TOME Upon that which is said: *Thy name is oil poured out; therefore the maidens have loved thee exceedingly.*

[0523B]He who is pleased to enter some garden planted with trees of divers kinds and adorned with blossoming flowers is gladly refreshed by the taste of the fruits, and is wonderfully delighted by the sweet fragrance of the blossoming flowers. What then do we say this garden is, but the most delightful text of the Holy Scriptures? And what the trees and flowers? Assuredly the spiritual meanings of the Scriptures. Let us therefore enter this garden in mind and spirit, and see what it is that the Bride says to the Bridegroom: *Thy name is oil poured out.* As those say who discourse of the natures of things, oil is reported to have in itself seven powers or natures, which seem most aptly to agree with the name of Christ, since according to Isaiah the prophet [n: (cap. XI)] the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit [0523C] are said to have rested upon him; namely, « the spirit of wisdom and understanding, the spirit of counsel and fortitude, the spirit of knowledge and piety, the spirit of the fear of the Lord. » But now let us come to the discussing of the seven natures of oil, and fit them to the Lord Jesus. Oil is said to float above all liquids into which it is put. Which well agrees with the Lord Jesus, since as Paul says: « God gave him a name which is above every name [n: (Philip. II)]. » And God himself « is over all things blessed for ever. Amen [n: (Rom. IX)]. » Oil receives no external liquid, since no stain or contagion of sin infected Christ, as Peter says: « Who did no sin [n: (I Petr. II)] » it refreshes men, and Christ gives us his blood [0523D] and his flesh to drink and satisfies us. It heals wounds, since Christ, as Isaiah says: « He healed our wounds [n: (Isa. LIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. LIII, 5 *livore ejus sanati sumus*, "by his bruises we are healed"] It is such within as it is without, since our nature, taken up by the divinity of Christ, by participation in that same divinity was wholly without fault. Applied to fire, it gives light to those sitting in darkness. and Christ enlightens every man coming into this world [n: (Joan. I)]. If it is poured out in one place, it is seen to occupy the ground around it: but Christ was born in Bethlehem, yet by a star shining from heaven he was announced to the Magi of the East. Compare therefore, if it please thee, the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit with the seven natures of oil, and thou shalt consider wondrous things in the law of God. There is no *306* greater wisdom than that a man should know his Creator, « who is over [0524B] all things God blessed for ever. Amen. » No one can have a worthier understanding than that he should know the good which he understands, and within the secret place of his heart in no wise receive the work of the devil. But then our body and soul use wise and perfect counsel, when, confession and penance having gone before, it is refreshed by the body of Christ and the blood of Christ. And our mind then attains a stronger health, when it imitates Christ according to its possibility. Assuredly we cannot come to fellowship with Christ, unless through true knowledge we strive to possess chastity of body and cleanness of heart. We then shine with a great blaze of charity, when with a sincere mind we bear bowels of piety toward all men. We confess that we truly have the holy fear of the Lord [0524C] then, when, doing well in one place, we let a sweetly-smelling example flow forth from us far and wide. Let the Bride therefore say to the Bridegroom: *Thy name is oil poured out,* as if she said: As oil which is poured out in one place is seen to occupy the ground around it, so thou indeed wast crucified for me in Jerusalem, but wast preached through the whole world by me; and therefore the maidens, that is the faithful souls, have loved thee. Since indeed thou didst die on the cross for them, therefore those maidens have loved thee so far that the martyrs themselves died for thee. Let us strive therefore, as Paul says, to suffer with Christ and to die with him [n: (Rom. VIII)], that we may be able to rise again to the blessed life [0524D] with him, since if we have been companions of his sufferings, we shall likewise be companions of his resurrection and joy [n: (II Cor. I)].
