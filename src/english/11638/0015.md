## RECAPITULATION FROM WHAT IS ABOVE.

Goldsmiths, who are wont to work in gold and silver or in precious stones, when they fashion some precious vessel between their hands — there are perhaps some men who praise and marvel at that vessel which they behold, and are delighted with the wondrous variety of the craftsman's work; but the craftsmen nevertheless still set beneath the hammers the vessel which they have in their hands, thin it with files, adorn it with gems; and although to the men standing by that vessel already seems on every side to be perfect, to the craftsmen it seems to be less polished and perfect. And so in that manner, when we treated above that saying [0525A] — namely, "Thy name is oil poured out" — although what we have said may perhaps please some, yet still, by the helping grace of the Holy Spirit, we can add something to the earlier treatment which may not displease the hearing of readers and hearers alike. "Thy name is oil poured out; therefore the maidens have loved thee [n: (Cant. I)]." This is the voice of holy Church, which praises the clemency of her Bridegroom, and his ineffable charity, in this two-membered utterance. Nor is it any wonder if the Bride praises the clemency of her Bridegroom, since the Bridegroom himself also does not keep silent about the affection and tenderness which he has had toward his Bride. For thus he himself says through Jeremiah the prophet: « With an everlasting charity have I loved thee, therefore have I drawn thee, taking pity [n: (cap. XXXI)]. Concerning this ineffable charity [0525B] of Christ, indeed, wherewith he himself loved holy Church, John the apostle says: Herein is the charity of God perfected in us, not because we have loved him, but because he first loved us [n: (I Joan. IV)]. » [var: Vulg. I Joan. IV, 10 *non quasi nos dilexerimus Deum*, "not as though we had loved God"] Moreover it is pleasing to consider whether in the Old Testament or in the prophets anything said about oil can be found which agrees with the name of the Bridegroom and can becomingly be fitted to the mysteries of the Bride, that is, of holy Church. For in Exodus the Lord says to Moses: « Command the children of Israel that they bring thee the purest oil from the olive trees, and pounded with a pestle, that a lamp may burn always before me in the tabernacle of the testimony *(cap XXVII)*. » Isaiah the prophet also, making mention of oil, says: « A vineyard was made for my beloved [0525C] on a horn, the son of oil; and he hedged it in, and picked the stones out of it, and planted it a choice vine, and built a tower in the midst of it, set up a winepress in it, and looked that it should bring forth grapes, and it brought forth wild grapes [n: (cap. V)]. » But in Zechariah the prophet it is thus written of oil: « These are the two sons of the oil of splendour, who stand before the ruler of the whole earth [n: (cap. IV)]. » [var: Vulg. Zach. IV, 14 *isti sunt duo filii olei qui assistunt Dominatori universae terrae* — without *splendoris*, "of splendour"] For since in the earlier treatment, aided by the help of the divinity of Christ and somewhat made fruitful by the dew of spiritual understanding, we discoursed of the seven natures of oil — not indeed as is worthy, but according to the small measure of our wit — and assigned those same natures to the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit, which are in Christ in their fullness; it now remains that we speak something further about oil under the allegorical sense, which may agree both with Christ and with holy Church, [0525D] and may morally suit each and every one of the faithful. Let us see, therefore, what it is that Isaiah the excellent prophet says: « A vineyard, he says, was made for my beloved on a horn, the son of oil. » O thou, Lord Jesus Christ, who art the son of the highest oil, unveil the spiritual eyes of my heart, that I may be able to consider the wondrous mysteries which are hidden from eternity in thy holy and most blessed law. *Eleos* indeed in Greek is called in Latin *misericordia*, mercy. For the Lord says in the Gospel: « Be merciful, as your Father also is merciful [n: (Luc. VI)]. » David the prophet also, refreshed by the superabundant grace of the Holy Spirit, speaks thus of God: « My God, my mercy [n: (Psal. LVIII)]. » God therefore almighty, ineffably loving and *307* especially clement, who in Isaiah is called oil, [0526A] is himself doubtless called merciful in the Gospel and in his Only-begotten Son, and is proclaimed mercy by David the prophet. To the most beloved Son, therefore, of this ineffable and incomparable oil a certain most dear vineyard was made on a most excellent horn, that is, in the humanity which he took from our sinful mass; which humanity so surpassed the remaining mass, from which it was taken, by the power of the divinity that took it, as a horn is wont to surpass that flesh out of which, in rising, it is seen to proceed. But concerning this horn, that is, concerning the incarnation of the Lord Jesus, Zacharias, filled with the grace of the Holy Spirit, said, « Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, because he hath visited and wrought the redemption of his people [n: (Luc. I)]. » [var: Vulg. Luc. I, 68 *Benedictus Deus Israel*, without *Dominus*] And as though someone were asking of him what mercy the God of Israel [0526B] had done to his people, he straightway added, saying: « He hath raised up a horn of salvation in the house of David his servant [n: (ibid.)]. » [var: Vulg. Luc. I, 69 *erexit cornu salutis nobis*, "a horn of salvation for us"] If we should wish to interpret that horn otherwise than the truth of the matter holds, truth itself, propped upon authority, and authority strengthened by faithful truth, would contradict us. For it is manifest that a royal Virgin of David's stock was chosen, from whom and in whom that horn — that is, our humanity — was taken up by the Word of the Father; and if this had not been done, the human race, which had perished, could in no wise be saved. And this is the horn of salvation which has been raised up for us, namely the Word made flesh for our salvation, which dwelt among us [n: (Joan. I)]. On this horn, therefore, is the vineyard most dear to the beloved Son of oil; that is, through the humanity which [0526C] he took, the Son of almighty God acquired holy Church for himself. But that the Son himself is called beloved, his Bride bears witness in the Song of Songs: « My beloved, she says, is white and ruddy [n: (Cant. V)]. » Ruddy, because of the shedding of blood on the cross; white, because of the prerogative of virginity and chastity. Again in the same little book his most dear Bride speaks thus of the same Bridegroom of hers: « My beloved, she says, shall abide between my breasts [n: (Cant. I)]. » Holy Church therefore, which in Isaiah is called a vineyard, is herself called Bride in the Song of Songs. This vineyard indeed was made most dear in delights to the beloved Son of oil, from the time when he took flesh from her, since as Paul says: « No man ever hated his own flesh [n: (Ephes. V)]. » And in another [0526D] place the same Paul more plainly intimates what love the Bridegroom has toward his Bride: « Husbands, he says, love your wives, as Christ loved holy Church and delivered himself up for her [n: (Col. III)]. » [var: Vulg. Ephes. V, 25 *sicut et Christus dilexit ecclesiam, et se ipsum tradidit pro ea*] Whence the same Bridegroom in the Song of Songs says of his Bride: « How beautiful art thou, my love, how beautiful art thou; thy eyes are those of doves! [n: (Cant. I.)] » [var: Vulg. Cant. I, 14 *ecce tu pulchra es, amica mea, ecce tu pulchra, oculi tui columbarum*] The Bridegroom doubles the beauty of his Bride by repetition for this reason: because he foreknew her a virgin and chaste in soul and in body. And he compared her eyes to doves, because he foresaw that she had that simplicity of faith which heretics lack. But nevertheless, what do we judge this to be that the prophet says, that God hedged in his vineyard, that is, holy Church? holy Church is indeed called a vineyard, [0527A] not only in Isaiah, but in the book of the Gospels and in the Song of Songs: in the Gospel, where the householder hired workmen into his vineyard [n: (Matth. XX)]; but in the Song of Songs, where it is written: « There was a vineyard for the Peaceable One, » and, « Our vineyard hath flourished [n: (Cant. II)]. » This vineyard, therefore, the Lord surrounded with a hedge, when he fortified it with the sayings of the prophets, and, lest it could be trodden down and torn asunder by heretics, instructed it with apostolic teachings as with a strong garrison. Whence it well indeed follows in the same prophet, that « he picked the stones out of it [n: (Isa. V)]. » For stones are picked out of a vineyard by men for this reason, that they may be cast out of that vineyard as harmful things. And so the heretics, who in the Gospel are called cockle [n: (Matth. XIII)], are themselves called stones in Isaiah the prophet, and [0527B] in the Song of Songs are described in elegant speech under the name of foxes, as it is written: « Catch us the little foxes, which destroy our vineyards [n: (Cant. II)]. » But what the Prophet says: [And he planted it a choice vine], this doubtless is what the Lord says in the Gospel: « Therefore I have chosen you out of the world, that you may go and bring forth fruit, and that your fruit may remain [n: (Joan. XV)]. » [var: Vulg. Joan. XV, 16 *ego elegi vos et posui vos, ut eatis*; *de mundo* stands at verse 19] And Paul the apostle in his Epistles: « I, he says, have planted, » and, of God: « you are the husbandry [n: (I Cor. XIII)]. » But of the apostles it is said: « These, living in the flesh, planted the Church with their own blood. » The tower too, which the prophet Isaiah testifies to have been built in the midst of the vineyard, is itself the name of the Lord, of which it is said through Solomon: [0527C] « The name of the Lord is a most strong tower; to it the just man shall flee, and shall be saved [n: (Prov. XVIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Prov. XVIII, 10 *ad ipsum currit justus et exaltabitur*, "the just runneth to it and shall be exalted"] For men flee to a tower for this reason, that they may be freed from the harassing of enemies. And so the name of the Lord is well compared to a tower in which men are saved, since as Peter the apostle says: « For there is no other name given under heaven wherein we must be saved, save in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ [n: (Act. IV)]. » [var: Vulg. Act. IV, 12 *nec enim aliud nomen est sub caelo datum hominibus, in quo oporteat nos salvos fieri*, without the closing clause] But the winepress which was built in the aforesaid vineyard represented to us the figure and image of the most holy altar, since as in a winepress or press the grape is squeezed, and being squeezed is strained into most sweet wine, so gloriously and wondrously each day the body and blood of Christ is consecrated and offered upon the altar. But that which follows in Isaiah, that [0527D] the Lord looked for his vineyard to bring forth grapes, and behold, it brought forth wild grapes — let us understand it as said of heretics and false Christians. For the heretics, who do *308* not consent to the ecclesiastical sacraments, nay rather withstand and fight against them with perverse doctrines, and the false Christians who profess that they know God but by their deeds deny him — these beyond doubt beget and bring to the Lord thorns and wild grapes instead of grapes, that is, useless and harmful fruits. But nevertheless what ought to be done with such men on the day of judgment the prophet makes plain in what follows in his narrative, saying: « Now therefore, men of Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem, judge between me and my vineyard, that is, concerning my vineyard [n: (Isa. V)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. V, 3 *habitatores Jerusalem et vir Juda, judicate inter me et inter vineam meam*] [0528A] And so by the men of Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem we ought to understand especially the apostles and the other saints, whom without doubt we know to be citizens and inhabitants of that heavenly Jerusalem which is above, our mother. And so to them, all ambiguity removed, the Lord promises in his Gospel, saying: « You who have followed me shall sit upon twelve seats. judging the twelve tribes of Israel [n: (Matth. XIX)]. » And Paul the apostle: « Know you not, he says, that the saints shall judge the world? [n: (I Cor. VI.)] » And in another place it is written of all the saints: « The saints shall judge the nations, and shall rule over the peoples [n: (Sap. III)]. » Of these apostles, namely, and of the other saints, already exalted before the majesty of God in eternal predestination, Zechariah the prophet, contemplating their glory, [0528B] prophesied, saying: « These are the two sons of the oil of splendour who stand before the Ruler of the whole earth [n: (Zachar. IV)]. » For by the number two the prophet signified the apostles and the other saints for this reason: because there are two precepts of charity, the observance and fulfilling of which makes men saints and friends of God. But who he is that is signified by the oil we have already touched upon above, when that saying from Isaiah was expounded by us. It is to be known, moreover, that in Zechariah the prophet something more is said than in Isaiah. For God almighty, because he himself is both truly merciful and true mercy, is in Isaiah simply called oil, but in Zechariah, with an addition, is named the oil of splendour. And not undeservedly is God the Father [0528C] almighty said to be the oil of splendour, since his Only-begotten Son is called by the Apostle the splendour and figure of his substance [n: (Hebr. I)]. Splendour therefore is the Father, splendour is the Son, splendour none the less is the Holy Spirit; yet they are not three splendours, but one splendour. For indeed in like manner in the catholic faith we believe and declare: God is the Father, God is the Son, God is the Holy Spirit; and yet they are not three gods, but one God. Since therefore, as we said above, *eleos* in Greek is called in Latin *misericors* or *misericordia*, merciful or mercy, consequently by the oil is understood he who is merciful, and God the Father of mercies. If therefore by the oil we take God the Father, it is given us to understand that we should interpret his Son as the splendour of the oil. The oil of splendour, therefore, [0528D] and the splendour of the oil, is the unbegotten begetter, and the only-begotten of God the Begetter.
