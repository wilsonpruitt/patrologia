## RECAPITULATION FROM THE FOREGOING. IN WHAT MANNER THIS SAYING OF THE VINEYARD OUGHT TO BE REFERRED TO THE SYNAGOGUE.

« A vineyard, he says, was made for my beloved on a horn, the son of oil [n: (Isa V)]. » But what the vineyard is the same prophet declares more plainly when he says: « The vineyard of the Lord of hosts is the house of Israel, and the men of Judah his delightful plant [n: (ibid.)]. » Of this vineyard David the prophet also speaks: « Thou hast brought a vineyard, saying, out of Egypt; thou hast cast out the nations, and planted, it [n: (Psal. LXXIX)]; » and the rest which follows in [0529A] that psalm concerning the same vineyard, which can easily be understood without any interpretation. This, I say, is that vineyard, namely the Jewish people, of which the Saviour says in the Gospel: « A certain man, a householder, planted a vineyard, and let it out to husbandmen. And it came to pass, when the time of the vintage drew near, that he sent his servants to the husbandmen, that they might receive the fruit of the vineyard from the husbandmen. But the husbandmen, seeing the servants of the householder, laid hold on them, and, having afflicted them with many insults, killed them. But the householder, hearing what had happened, said: I will send my only-begotten, most dear son to them; perhaps they will reverence him. But they, seeing the son of their Lord, said one to another: Behold, here is the heir: let him therefore be killed, and so the inheritance will be transferred to us [n: (Luc. XX)]. » [var: Vulg. Luc. XX, 9 *homo plantavit vineam et locavit eam colonis*; and XX, 14 *hic est heres, occidamus illum, ut nostra fiat hereditas*] Who [0529B] then can more rightly be understood as this householder than God the Father almighty of our Lord Jesus Christ? For he is the true householder, who governs and rules the whole world with clement tenderness, and according to his irreproachable wisdom disposes Christians, Jews, pagans, heretics. For these are the households, of which that householder is said to be father, and whose providence he does not disdain to have. For that same most loving householder provides for the Christians, that is, for his own sons, on this condition, that if they will obey his precepts they may be able to escape the punishments of hell, and may be able to find the unfailing joys of paradise. And that same householder shows that he has care of the Jews, since he waits with clement goodness that they may return to [0529C] himself. Pagans and heretics he in no wise drives from his providence, since although, like bad servants, they are ungrateful for his benefits, yet the Lord still waits with patient long-suffering that, leaving their heresies and their idols, they may come back to him. This householder therefore planted a vineyard when he led the Israelite *309* people out of Egypt with a mighty hand and an outstretched arm, and made them dwell in the land of promise, the foreign nations having been driven out. And he let it out to husbandmen when he charged the high priests and the scribes and the Pharisees to instruct that same people according to the law of Moses, and to restrain them from the appetite of all evil, so far as is lawful to mortals. And that same householder sent his servants to the aforesaid [0529D] vineyard when he appointed the prophets to instruct that same Israelite nation. But the husbandmen, recognizing the servants of their Lord, afflicted several of them with insults, and upon some inflicted an undeserved death. For if we are willing to be more diligently intent upon the pages of Holy Scripture, we shall be able to find very few indeed of the elect who have not either been afflicted with insults by the impious, or unjustly delivered over to a most cruel death. And it is manifest that the most impious Jews condemned Isaiah and Jeremiah to a cruel death, and dishonoured almost all the remaining prophets with many insults and revilings. But the son of the householder who is killed by the husbandmen is the Lord Jesus Christ, who is crucified by the high priests and the scribes and the Pharisees. But let us now return to the foregoing [0530A] saying, of which we had purposed to speak under the figure of the Synagogue. « A vineyard, says Isaiah, was made for my beloved, on a horn, the son of oil. Since indeed we have said somewhat, though less than sufficiently, about the vineyard, it now remains that we set forth where the Son of God is called beloved. For in the Gospel it is written that the Holy Spirit came down upon the Lord Jesus in the likeness of a dove, and the voice of the Father was heard, saying: This is my beloved son, in whom I am well pleased; hear ye him [n: (Luc. IX)]. » [var: Vulg. Luc. IX, 35 *hic est Filius meus electus, ipsum audite*, "my chosen son"] For of this beloved, this most dear one, the Bride speaks in the very book of which we are speaking: « My beloved, she says, to me, and I to him, who feedeth among the lilies, until the day break, and the shadows decline [n: (Cant. II)]. » As if she said: My beloved shall be joined to me by faith, and therefore [0530B] I shall be joined to him by faith and by work, who feedeth among the lilies, that is, is delighted with chaste and loving and just works, until the day break, and the shadows decline — that is, until the day of judgment appear, and the images of sins be brought to nothing. To this beloved, therefore, the vineyard on the horn showed itself contrary, that is, the Synagogue exceedingly contradicted the humanity which Christ took; so far that it would never believe in him whom it saw lying open to human sight as a man only, and not as God. This vineyard, I say, was made exceedingly bitter to this beloved, when the Synagogue gave the Lord Jesus gall and vinegar to drink in the humanity wherewith he was clothed. Thou who hearest these things oughtest to remember that I said above that by the horn is to be understood the humanity of the Son of God, [0530C] and that from the authority of sacred speech I showed a sufficient reason. But why the Son of God is called by Isaiah the son of oil we ought to repeat in a brief reason, since we have already taught this above sufficiently and fitly. For *Oleos* in Greek is called *misericordia*, mercy, in Latin. Since therefore the Father is merciful and is mercy, it stands for certain that Christ is the Son of the God of oil, since he is the Son of the merciful God. This beloved son of oil, therefore, that is, Christ the Son of God, hedged in his vineyard, that is, the Synagogue, when he taught it with the examples and institutions of the patriarchs and fortified it with the books of the prophets, lest, namely, it should admit any other Lord beside himself, and lest that singular wild beast of which the Psalmist complains [n: (Psal. LXXIX)], namely the devil, should lay it waste. [0530D] For a vineyard is hedged in for this reason, that its fruit may be kept unharmed for the lord of that vineyard, and that its barriers may not be easily unbarred to enemies and to beasts. But as to what is said: « The Lord picked the stones out of it [n: (Isa. V)]; » it stands that after the election of the apostles that Synagogue was driven out by God and cast off. And of this casting-off it is well found written in Isaiah, where the Lord speaks of Sobna the scribe and provost of the temple: « Behold, says Isaiah, O thou Sobna; behold, he says, the Lord will drive thee out of thy station, and will cause thee to be carried away as a cock is carried away [n: (Isa. XXII)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. XXII, 17 carries no expulsion clause here; the words about the station stand at verse 19 as *et expellam te de statione tua*] In like manner also it is written of the casting-off of the Synagogue under the person of king Jechonias: « If Jechonias king of Judah were as a ring of gold on my hand, saith the Lord, thence would I pluck him away and cast him off [0531A] [n: (Jer. XXII)]. » [var: Vulg. Jer. XXII, 24 *Jechonias filius Joachim rex Juda anulus in manu dextera mea, inde avellam eum*] For she was as it were a Bride driven out of her station, when the Synagogue was cast down from that glory of her former state for the desert of her iniquity; and that same Synagogue was carried away as a cock, when, because of her unfruitfulness and imperfection, she was removed from outward understanding and from Jewish superstition. For we know that a cock is a beast castrated and unfruitful, to which the ancient law is well compared, which, as blessed Paul says, brought nothing to perfection; whence it deservedly follows in Isaiah the prophet concerning the same Sobna: « What dost thou here, or as it were who here? [n: (Isa. XXII)]. » For without doubt we are wont to say "what doest thou here" to him whose work we by no means approve. And "as it were who," not that he truly holds some place as a perfect man, [0531B] but yet does not carry out work worthy of the merits of his office or of his place. We can indeed refer that election of the stones, of which we spoke above, to the kings and prophets whom we do not doubt to have been chosen by God in the Synagogue. But as to what the prophet Isaiah says, that the Lord planted that vineyard a choice vine, it is plainly given us to understand that indeed the kings and prophets and the whole Israelite nation drew their origin from the stock of Abraham, *310* who was chosen by God. But indeed that planting much degenerated from that glorious and fatherly life, from whose root it seemed to draw its shoot. Which degeneration the Lord in the Gospel reproached to the Jews with a most kindly upbraiding, saying: « If you are the seed of Abraham, [0531C] do the works of Abraham; and why do you wish to kill me? this Abraham did not do [n: (Joan. VIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Joan. VIII, 39–40 *si filii Abrahae estis*, "if you are the sons of Abraham"; and *nunc autem quaeritis me interficere*, "but now you seek to kill me"] For the Jews had greatly degenerated from that glory of the blood of Abraham the patriarch, their father. For Abraham loved, honoured and adored God; but the Israelite nation hated God, dishonoured him, and condemned him on a most cruel cross. But the tower which is said by the prophet to have been built by the Lord in the midst of the vineyard signifies the temple, most famous in ancient times for its dignity, which no one is ignorant was built in the midst of the Jewish nation by the precept of God. But the winepress which stood in the aforementioned vineyard intimates to us the altar which the Israelite nation held in great veneration in the midst of itself. Which altar indeed is well compared to a winepress, [0531D] since as in a winepress many grapes are squeezed and reduced into the liquor of wine, so the divers and many victims which, being placed on the altar, were burned, were referred by their signification to one sacrifice, that is, to Christ. But as to what the prophet says, that the Lord looked for his vineyard to bring forth grapes, and it brought forth wild grapes: he announces by manifest tokens that this saying belongs above all to the Jews. Did not the Jews themselves bring wild grapes instead of grapes, when they denied their Creator before the face of Pilate? [n: (Joan. XIX.)] Which indeed blessed Peter upbraids to them, saying: « But you asked that a murderer should be given to you, and delivered over to death the author [0532A] of life, whom God raised from the dead [n: (Act. III)]. » [var: Vulg. Act. III, 15 *auctorem vero vitae interfecistis*, "but you killed the author of life"] Did not the most wicked Jews make wild grapes for the Lord instead of grapes, when they said to Pilate: « Crucify him; » And: « If thou release this man, thou art no friend of Caesar [n: (Joan. XIX)]. » Therefore that which Isaiah says in what [sic: *subseqnentibus*]: « Now, men of Judah and inhabitants of Jerusalem, judge between me and my vineyard [n: (Isa. V)], » no one is ignorant belongs to the general resurrection, in which the holy apostles shall judge and condemn that vineyard. This, in fine, the Lord in the Gospel testifies to the Jews themselves as they disparaged him, when he says: « If I, as you say, cast out devils by the prince of devils, by whom do your sons cast them out? [n: (Matth. XII.)] » [var: Vulg. Matth. XII, 27 *si ego in Beelzebub ejicio daemones*] Therefore they themselves shall be your judges. Which this same prophet Isaiah also declares, [0532B] when he prophesied of the coming of the Lord and of the holy apostles to the day of judgment, saying: « The Lord will come to judgment with the elders of his people [n: (Isa. III)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. III, 14 *Dominus ad judicium veniet cum senibus populi sui et principibus ejus*] Solomon too took care to comprise in a brief speech Christ, and holy Church, and the holy apostles, and the day of judgment, when he says: « Noble in the gates is the man of holy Church, when he shall sit with the senators of his people [n: (Prov. XXXI)]. » [var: Vulg. Prov. XXXI, 23 *nobilis in portis vir ejus, quando sederit cum senatoribus terrae*, "her husband… with the senators of the land"] And so the apostles, who in the Gospel are called twelve judges and sons of the Jews according to the flesh, are themselves doubtless called in Isaiah the men of Judah and the inhabitants of Jerusalem and the elders of the people, and are named by Solomon the senators of the people. These indeed are the two sons of the oil of splendour whom Zechariah the prophet [n: (Cap. IV)] says in the spirit [0532C] of prophecy to stand before the Ruler of the whole earth. Which he described by the number two for this reason, that he foresaw in the prophetic spirit that they would preach the divinity and the humanity of Christ. Further, that which the same prophet says: « These are they who stand before the Ruler of the whole earth, » let us understand to have been said not only of the apostles, but also of all the saints. Whence the [sic: *Domiuus*] himself in his Gospel says: « Father, I will that where I am, there also may my minister be [n: (Joan. XII)]. » [var: Vulg. Joan. XII, 26 *ubi sum ego, illic et minister meus erit*, "where I am, there also shall my minister be"] Which blessed Augustine also, the excellent doctor and most faithful defender of the catholic faith, testifies when he says: « Let us know for certain that after the ascension of the Lord to the heavens all the souls of the saints are with Christ, and going out from the body they go to Christ, as it is written: *The souls of the just [0532D] are in the hand of God [n: (Sap. III)]*, awaiting the resurrection of their body, that they may enjoy together with Christ an entire and perpetual beatitude. In like manner also the souls of sinners are set in hell, awaiting under fear the resurrection of their body, that with the devil they may be turned to eternal punishment.
