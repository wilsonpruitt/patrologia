## RECAPITULATION FROM THE FOREGOING ACCORDING TO THE MORAL SENSE. (cont.)

[0537C]These religious, then — if indeed they are to be called religious who bear themselves as we have just now depicted, or even, which is a shame to say, still worse — if at any time they are corrected by their masters for their own salvation, or if they are constrained to sit in their own cloister and to keep their own order, straightway, the fear of God being cast off and the purpose of their religion and of their order forsaken, leaping out of their cloister unruly under the pretext of a stricter religion, they range as gyrovagues through the diverse parts of the round world, or — alas the grief! — what is more infamous, they return to the world. To whom assuredly that fittingly belongs which the blessed apostle Peter says in his epistle: « The dog returned to his own vomit, and the sow that was washed to her wallowing in the mire [n: (II Petr. II)]. » And: [0537D] « It were better for them not to have known the way of truth, than after the knowledge of it to go backward [n: (ibid.)]. » [var: Vulg. II Petr. 2:21 *viam justitiae*, "the way of justice"] These converts therefore, so long as they abode in the holy purpose, so long did they stand as it were a vine of Sorech, that is, a chosen vine, in the vineyard of God, rooted and founded in charity. But now they are estranged backward, turned into a reprobate sense, and, according to the prophet Ezechiel, made utterly useless and degenerate, they avail for nothing further, save that they be cast into the fire and burn. Farther, therefore, from the order of our narration have we digressed, while we disputed of the nature of the vine and of the vineyard; nevertheless it is now a fitting time that we return to that which we had begun. « A vineyard, says Isaias [n: (cap. V)], was made most dear to my beloved, on a horn, the son of oil. » [var: Vulg. Isa. 5:1 *Vinea facta est dilecto meo in cornu filio olei*, without *charissima*] [0538A] The beloved, therefore, is he to whom the bride in the Song of Songs sends her legates, saying: « Tell my beloved that I languish for love of him [n: (Cant. V)]. » O how happy, and how blessed is the soul that languishes with the love of God! In it, without doubt, the love of the deceiver of this world has now utterly perished. And the deceitful glory of the flowing age has been brought to nothing. To this her beloved bridegroom that same most dear bride still speaks amid delights in the same little book. « Come, my Beloved, let us go forth into the field, let us see if the vineyard hath flourished, if the flowers bring forth the fruits of brightness and of good working [n: (Cant. VII)]. » But surely, he says, this vineyard was made most dear, that is, our soul, to this beloved son of oil, namely of almighty God, on a horn, to wit. It is plain to all, or at least to such as are illumined with a rare sense [0538B] and understanding, that every horn exceeds the flesh out of which it issues; and therefore not undeservedly is every spiritual virtue taken by the horn. Whence also Moses the prophet, for the excellence of his merits and the singular prerogative of the spiritual virtues wherein he wholly flourished, alone among mortals appeared horned from the fellowship of speaking with God; as it is written: When Moses had come down from Mount Sinai, he knew not that it was horned [n: (Exod. XXXIV)], that is, that his face was glorious and admirable, from the familiarity of converse with God. But the Lord Jesus also, who before Moses — nay, before the world was made — existed as God, yet after Moses in the last times appeared to this world as mortal man through the mystery of the Incarnation, is declared by the prophet Habacuc *(Habac. [0538C] III)* to have horns in his hands, in which assuredly his greatest strength is discerned to have been. For thus it is written in the Canticle of the same prophet: « Horns are in his hands [n: (ibid.)], » namely Christ's, and in what follows: « There, he says, is his strength hidden, there shall death fall down in his sight [n: (ibid.)]. » [var: Vulg. Hab. 3:5 *ante faciem ejus ibit mors*, "before his face shall go death"] Moreover, if we were minded now to bring into the midst whatever the divine eloquences are wont to utter concerning these spiritual horns, the day would more easily fail *314* us than the abundance of matter for speaking. But neither will we be silent altogether. « The horns of the just, says the Psalmist, shall be exalted [n: (Psal. LXXIV)]. » Who is so dull and so stupid as to dare say that a just man has horns? But the virtues of the just man are his horns, wherewith [0538D] he himself fights against the devil, and whereby he is lifted up and exalted before God. Let Isaias then say that the vineyard was made most dear to the beloved son of oil, that is, that our soul, on account of the virtues which it has, is loved and held in great honour by the Only-begotten Son of God; and this is what is said through the prophet: « If thou shalt separate the precious from the vile, thou shalt be as my mouth [n: (Jer. XV)]. » For to separate the precious from the vile is to discern virtues from vices. But who is the son of oil? Since it has now been said very often, to repeat the same thing so many times is exceedingly tedious. But as to what the Prophet says, that the Lord fenced the vineyard, it befits us to understand that fencing assuredly of our own soul, which is the spiritual vineyard of God. O how glorious and how illustrious in virtues is that soul, of which God has so great [0539A] care, that it is fenced by him with loving diligence! For thus the Lord himself says through the prophet: « I will hedge up thy ways, and thou shalt go after thy lovers, and shalt not overtake them, and then thou shalt say: I will return to my former husband, because it was better with me than it now is [n: (Osee II)]. » [var: Vulg. Osee 2:6–7 runs in the third person, *sepiam viam tuam spinis … et sequetur amatores suos et non apprehendet eos … et dicet: vadam et revertar ad virum meum priorem*] Moreover, tarrying a little upon this matter, it is pleasing to consider in what manner they that enclose gardens make their fences. For a most blessed soul is called by God, morally, an enclosed garden. It is indeed known to all that fences are made of stakes and of rods, in such wise namely that the stakes, fixed below, cleave to the earth on the one part, but on the other part look upward into heaven with heads erect. Afterwards, moreover, rods are interwoven crosswise, and in such fashion the fence guarding God's garden is completed. Nevertheless, [0539B] what can we more rightly call the stakes of this spiritual fence but the saints of God, who, although they still tarry on earth for the infirmity of the flesh, are yet assiduously with Christ in heaven, and cleave to him with spiritual desires? whence one of such spiritual stakes, dwelling on earth, said: « But our conversation is in the heavens [n: (Philipp. III)]. » And what are the rods, but their examples and good works, and their catholic doctrines? With these stakes therefore and with rods of this sort God fences his vineyard, because by the examples and teachings of his saints he daily tills, teaches, instructs and admonishes the souls of his faithful. Hence it is that David said: « The Lord shall send an Angel round about them that fear him, and shall keep them [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Ps. 33:8 *immittet angelus Domini in circuitu timentium eum et eripiet eos*, "the angel of the Lord shall encamp round about them that fear him, and shall deliver them"] Another prophet likewise no less testifies in [0539C] the person of God, saying: « He that toucheth you, toucheth the apple of my eye [n: (Zach. II)], » it plainly stands that among all the members which a man has, the apple of the eye appears the more tender and the more delicate. If therefore we are of such authority that we are made equal to the apple of God's eye, we ought not to be ungrateful for his benefit, but it is needful that we so live that we may deserve from him to be kept and fenced like a good vineyard. That also which the prophet Isaias so greatly expresses, so as to assert that God chose out stones from his vineyard. Let us not doubt that it was said of our soul, if indeed, as we have already proved above, we shall have been the vineyard of God by living spiritually. For the stones that are in God's vineyard can be understood as vices and concupiscences and unclean desires also, which, as [0539D] stones make a vineyard barren, so all these make our soul barren. But now God is to be entreated, that he himself may deign to choose out and cast forth the stones from that vineyard, that is, to root out of our soul all the vices that displease him; that so our soul, as a good branch abiding in him who is the true vine, may hear with the apostles: « Without me ye can do nothing, and as the branch is not able to bring forth fruit unless it abide in the vine, so neither can ye unless ye abide in me [n: (Joan. XV)]. » But as to what Isaias says in the sequel of his narration, that « God planted his chosen vineyard, » let us know that these things pertain morally to our body and to our soul. « For man was made to the image of God, and to [0540A] his likeness [n: (Gen. I)]. » That planting was assuredly choice enough, which was created to the likeness and image of God. Moreover in the book of Wisdom it is found written: « God made man simple and upright, but he himself mingled himself with infinite questions [n: (Eccle. VII)]. After these things, says Isaias, God built a tower in the midst of his vineyard. » [var: Vulg. Eccle. 7:30 *quod fecerit Deus hominem rectum*, without *simplicem*] If therefore our soul desires to be God's vineyard, it behoves it without doubt to have a tower built by God in the midst of itself. For a tower, when it is constructed, is raised up from the bottom in quadrangular form. Build therefore, O Lord God, in my vineyard a quadrangular tower, that is, found and cause to be rooted in our soul those four virtues, namely prudence, fortitude, justice, temperance, [0540B] and in our body no less abstinence, sobriety, chastity, true penitence. But let the remaining virtues be the stones of this spiritual building. And let the cement which binds and embraces the stones be charity itself, without which no work is said to be good, and in which every good work is made solid and founded; for thus says Paul: « Be ye rooted and founded in charity [n: (Ephes. III)]. » « A winepress, says Isaias, the Lord built in his vineyard. » Known to all is the work that is done in a winepress. For in the winepress the grapes are pressed out, and being pressed out they melt, that they may be turned into most sweet wine. Although therefore the Lord says of his passion through the prophet: « I have trodden the winepress alone, and of the Gentiles there is not a man with me *(Isa. [0540C] LXIII)*, » yet there is still another spiritual winepress, *315* which would that the Lord Jesus might deign to build in the vineyard of our body. For holy compunction, and the pouring out of blessed tears for the love of Christ, ought not unfittingly to be called the winepress of the soul. This spiritual winepress that most dear bride amid delights had built within her, who in the Song of Songs, sighing of her Bridegroom, said: « My soul melted, as my Beloved spoke to me [n: (Cant. V)]. » There is shown therefore in this sentence the inestimable and incomparable affection of the Bride toward her bridegroom, who loved him with so sincere a love that she was even wholly melted at his word. Nevertheless with a far ampler affection and [0540D] a loftier charity does that same bridegroom love that same bride of his, who for love of her not only came down from heaven a little lower than the angels, but even came as far as the punishment of an ignominious death. Which indeed the prophet Jeremias attests in his lamentations, saying: « The Lord hath trodden the winepress for the virgin daughter of Juda [n: (Thren. I)]. » For that most blessed soul, which in the Song of Songs is called the Bride, is in Jeremias named the virgin daughter of Juda. Now the soul wedded to God is called a virgin because of the integrity of faith, and the chastity of the body wherewith she is clothed. But daughter of Juda, for the humble confession of sins and the glorious perseverance in the praises of God. For Juda is interpreted *confession*, which is referred to the confession of offences and to the praise of God. [0541A] Although many things still remain that might be said of the winepress, yet I judge that these can suffice for modern hearers, who, listening willingly, are studious of brevity, and lest the volume, drawn out at greater length, weigh down the minds of tedious hearers. Truly, ought we so far to fear to offend the slothful and the tedious, that we should leave undone that which can help and instruct and edify strenuous men, exercised in the meditation of God's law? By no means. Let us see then what it is that follows in the prophet Isaias. And the Lord, says Isaias, looked for his vineyard to bring forth grapes, and behold, alas the grief! it brought forth wild grapes. Which sentence assuredly is rather to be feared than expounded. « For behold, says the apostle James, the husbandman waiteth for the precious fruit [0541B] of the earth, patiently bearing till he receive the early and the latter. Be ye also patient, and strengthen your hearts in the service of God until the Lord himself come [n: (Jacob. V)]. » [var: Vulg. Jac. 5:8 *confirmate corda vestra quoniam adventus Domini appropinquavit*, "for the coming of the Lord is at hand"] Moreover, that we may not leave wholly undiscussed what was said above, that the Lord looked for his vineyard to bring forth grapes, and it brought forth wild grapes, it must be said in few words that woe indeed to those who are waited for by their Creator throughout their whole life that they may bear fruit, and at last, the course of the present life being finished, appear fruitless. For generally all Christians who have received the faith, and are imbued with the works of faith, if they live ill, if they close their last day without the working of faith, beyond doubt bring forth wild grapes instead of grapes. But [0541C] thou also, whosoever art called a convert, though thou seem for a time to be within the compass of a monastery or of a cloister, if before thy last day comes upon thee thou withdrawest from [0542A] holy conversion and from the service of God, without doubt (if it can be said) thou makest — more than, alas the grief! — wild grapes for the Lord thy God instead of grapes. Nevertheless those are blessed and happy who, by an opposite turn, bring precious grapes instead of wild grapes to God who awaits them unto salvation. These are assuredly they who once clove to this deceitful world with excessive love, but afterwards, perceiving that they had erred, were converted with the whole affection of the heart to holy religion, and, the vanity of the world despised, delivered themselves over with all their strength to the service of God. Of whom the prophet Zacharias seems to me to say: « These are the sons of the oil of brightness who stand before the Ruler of the whole earth [n: (Zach. IV)]. But since we have very often already premised above that oil signifies mercy, and mercy designates him of whom [0542B] the Psalmist says: My God, my mercy [n: (Psal. LVIII)], » this we avoid repeating again, lest we beget loathing in our hearers. [var: Vulg. Zach. 4:14 *isti sunt duo filii olei*, "these are the two sons of oil", without *splendoris*] But this stands for certain, that if God is called oil, they who are the sons of oil can equally be called also the sons of God. To which sentence the blessed apostle John accords with a consonant voice, saying: « Brethren, we are the sons of God, and it doth not yet appear what we shall be. We know that when he shall appear we shall be like to him, because we shall see him as he is [n: (Joan. III)]. » [var: Vulg. I Joan. 3:2 *carissimi*, "dearly beloved", and the perfect *nondum apparuit*, "it hath not yet appeared"] Let us consider then who he is that speaks. He was a convert indeed, he was an apostle and an Evangelist, he had renounced the world, and therefore he said boldly: « We are the sons of God. » It is manifest therefore that he who more perfectly [0542C] renounces the world, and more familiarly, after the manner of the apostles, cleaves to God, is a son of God, and can truly be so called.
