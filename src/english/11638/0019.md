## RECAPITULATION FROM THE FOREGOING ACCORDING TO THE MORAL SENSE. (cont.)

[0541] *Here ends the third Tome.*

## HERE BEGINS THE FOURTH TOME Upon that which is said: *Draw me after thee, we will run in the odour of thy ointments.*

[0541C]As we approach the exposition of the fourth Tome of this book, it befits us to invoke with pure mind and utmost intention *316* him who on the fourth day, by making the lights of heaven, gave light to the world, that he may deign to bring in upon us the veil of the letter of the Old Testament, so that according to his will we may be able to find the spiritual sense in letters covered with a darker robe. For indeed a great veil set over that sentence, namely: « *Draw me after thee.* As in the eightfold work of God in the book of Genesis, and in the eight sentences of Amos the prophet, where it is said: For three crimes of Damascus, and for four, I will not turn [n: (Amos I)], » and in the eightfold vision of Zacharias the prophet. Nevertheless, these sentences being a little tasted beforehand, let us direct our stylus to those things we premised above, and let us search out by spiritual investigation whose is this voice that says: *Draw me after thee,* or to whom it speaks. That this voice is holy Church's, which says: *Draw me after thee,* no wise man doubts: but that her bridegroom is [0542C] Christ the Son of God, to whom she herself speaks, all the authority of the New as well as of the Old Testament proclaims with a consonant voice. But it is greatly to be wondered at, for what reason holy Church asks to be drawn by the Son of God, that is, by her Bridegroom, since indeed that spiritual attraction belongs chiefly to the Father by the authority of his Son. For thus the same Son of God says of the same most dear Father of his in the Gospel: « No man cometh to me, unless my Father hath drawn him [n: (Joan. VI)]. » [var: Vulg. Joan. 6:44 *nemo potest venire ad me nisi Pater qui misit me traxerit eum*, "no man can come to me, except the Father, who hath sent me, draw him"] Finally that same most loving Father says of the same illustrious and fore-chosen Bride of his Son, that is, of holy Church, through Jeremias the prophet: « I have loved thee with a perpetual charity, therefore have I drawn thee, having mercy on thee [n: (Jere. XXXI)]. » It must be confessed therefore that God the Father, by the abundance of his sweetness, in the invisible love of inward charity, has drawn the mind of his Son's Bride to this, that she herself, that same most dear Bride, should ask to be drawn by that same Bridegroom of hers, namely the Son of God. But surely, O thou hearer, do not believe that drawing to be violent which the Bride longs to have done to her by her Bridegroom. [0543A] For it is not violent, but is in all ways charitable and voluntary. Hence it is indeed that the Psalmist, inflamed with spiritual desire, that he may be able worthily to utter the praises of God, draws in the grace of the Holy Spirit, as it is written: « I opened my mouth, and drew in the Spirit [n: (Psal. CXVIII)], » — the holy is understood; and for what he had drawn him in he manifestly subjoined, saying: « because I desired thy commandments [n: (ibid.)]. » It is given us therefore to understand that the grace of the Holy Spirit is drawn in by the desire of God's commandments. There is besides another drawing, greatly to be praised and approved, when someone is known to be set in some anguish, and his friend, having compassion and grieving with him, bears up that same anguish with him by grieving together, that he may be able to free him from that same [0543B] anguish. In such wise also it comes about that he who by the help and compassion of his friend is freed from that anguish is constrained to love that same friend of his more than he was wont, and is drawn by a wondrous and inestimable affection of charity to the love of him. In this most loving manner therefore holy Church, kindled with the divine love of charity, having considered the compassion of unspeakable tenderness which Christ had of her when he underwent death for her, says: *Draw me after thee;* as if she said: As thou hast died for me, so grant me that I may be able to die for thee. Or else *draw me after thee,* that is, make me to come to the knowledge of thy majesty, so that no longer through a glass and in a dark manner, but face to face [n: (I Cor. XIII)] I may be able to see how thou art in the Father, and the Father in thee, and how [0543C] the Holy Spirit proceeds from thee and from the Father. *We will run in the odour of thy ointments,* this voice seems to be that of the maidens, that is, of the faithful souls, speaking to the Bridegroom. Our mother holy Church, confident in the excellence of her merits in her stronger members, that is, the apostles and martyrs, beseeches thee that she may deserve to come to the knowledge of thy majesty; but we maidens, that is, the weaker and feebler yet faithful souls, have not yet laboured so much for thee that we dare sigh after the inaccessible light in which thou dwellest, but *we will run in the odour of thy ointments,* that is, by living well in this world we shall follow the examples of thy saints, that after death we may be able to come to their fellowship. Or else *we will run in the odour [0543D] of thy ointments,* that is, we shall walk the way of thy commandments, that, enlarged by thy charity, we may be able to receive within the [sic: *armaliolum*] of our heart the free gifts of the Holy Spirit. Let us run therefore, most dear brethren, in the odour of God's ointments, that it may be said of us: « Blessed are the undefiled in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]. » Let us run, I say, with the apostle Paul [n: (Philip. III)] so that we may lay hold with him, namely, of everlasting life; for he runs well who, living piously and justly, closes the last day of his life with a religious end. Lastly, he who runs forsakes the place in which he was, and stretches toward the place in which he was not. So therefore let us forsake the glory of this world, let us flee the concupiscences and pleasures [0544A] of this age, let us with Paul be emulous [n: (I Cor. XII)] of the better free gifts, which are the ointments giving forth odour, that is, the gifts of the Holy Spirit, that, drawn out of the pit of misery and out of the mire of dregs, we may be able to come to the perpetual joys of paradise.
