## HERE BEGINS THE FIFTH TOME Upon that which is said: *The king hath brought me into his storerooms, we will be glad and rejoice in thee, mindful of thy breasts: above wine the upright love thee [n: (Cant. I)]*.

[0547B] *319* As the sacred history of the Old Testament, when it was read, intimated to the minds of the hearers with spiritual modulation, Isaac the holy patriarch, son of that most noble and excellent patriarch Abraham, had once, when the day was already drawing on toward evening, gone out to meditate in the field [n: (Gen. XXIV)]: and it came to pass, while he walked meditating, that he lifted up his eyes and saw Rebecca his bride coming, and ran to meet her, and led her into the tent of Sara his mother, and loved her so greatly that he even tempered, for the greatest part, the grief which had befallen him from the death of that same mother of his. For because Isaac is interpreted *laughter* or *joy,* not undeservedly do we spiritually understand by him our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the joy and gladness, the jubilation and exultation, and the unfailing delight [0547C] of his saints. But that the Lord Jesus himself is the son of Abraham according to the flesh, Matthew the evangelist attests when he says: « The book of the generation of Jesus Christ, the son of David, the son of Abraham [n: (Matth. I)]. » But that saint Isaac, the day now drawing on toward evening, had gone out to meditate in the field, signifies that the Lord Jesus Christ, as the end of this world drew near, came into this world for this, that he might fulfil the mystery of his most holy incarnation. For the walking of holy Isaac into the field insinuates to us the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ into this world. Whence also his most dear Bride says to the same Lord Jesus in the Song of Songs: « Come, my beloved, let us abide in the woods, let us go forth into the field [n: (Cant. VII)], » although the blessed [0547D] Ambrose interprets that field as a stronghold. [var: Vulg. Cant. 7:11 *egrediamur in agrum, commoremur in villis*, "let us go forth into the field, let us abide in the villages" — and in that order] Moreover, that Isaac brought Rebecca into his mother's tent, and for love of her tempered the grief of his mother's death, signifies that the Lord Jesus Christ joined holy Church to himself by spiritual nuptials and epithalamic Songs. Whom assuredly he so loved with spiritual love, that he even [0548B] handed over the Synagogue, namely his carnal mother, to oblivion. Lastly, Rebecca herself is interpreted *patience,* whom Isaac brought into his tent, which surely alludes elegantly to the mysteries of holy Church; whom our Lord Jesus Christ, the contest of this world being finished, shall bring in to the eternal mansions of heavenly glory — to the founders of which Church, namely the holy apostles, he himself says: « In your patience you shall possess your souls [n: (Luc. XXI)]. » Considering therefore in this little book this most blessed and most happy bringing-in, and as though that were already done which is still to come after the day of judgment, the Bride of Christ, exulting with unspeakable joy and incomprehensible jubilation, thus addresses the maidens, saying: *The king hath brought me into his storerooms* [0548C] [n: (Cant. I)]. Truly, who is this king by whom this Bride says she has been brought into the storerooms? He assuredly of whom the prophet David says: « O God, give thy judgment to the king, and thy justice to the king's son [n: (Psal. LXXI)]. » Which indeed the same king, son of the highest king, makes plainer in the Gospel when he says: « The Father judgeth no man, but hath given all judgment to the Son [n: (Joan. V)]. » But what are these storerooms, if not those tabernacles or courts of which David the prophet sang: « How lovely are thy tabernacles, O Lord of hosts, my soul coveteth and desireth to be in the courts of the Lord? » [n: (Psal. LXXXIII.)] [var: Vulg. Ps. 83:3 *concupiscit et deficit anima mea in atria Domini*, "my soul coveteth and fainteth for the courts of the Lord"] These tabernacles indeed, or courts, the Lord Jesus in the Gospel calls mansions, where he says: « In my Father's house there are many mansions [n: (Joan. XIV)]. » Holy Church therefore, [0548D] the Bride of Christ, considering spiritually those mansions or tabernacles or courts, and panting after them with the whole desire of her mind, the love of the world set behind her, as though she already possessed in present those things which she doubts not she shall possess when the day of judgment is done, says: *The king hath brought me into his storerooms.* Truly, says holy Church, *the king hath brought me into his storerooms,* because « what [0549A] eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, neither hath it ascended into the heart of man [n: (I Cor. II)], » as he had promised me, he has faithfully bestowed. Whence Isaias the prophet says: « The eye hath not seen, O God, besides thee, what things thou hast prepared for them that love thee [n: (Isa. LXIV)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. 64:4 *exspectantibus te*, "for them that wait for thee"] By the charity therefore of this unspeakable joy, by the affection of incomprehensible jubilation, by the contemplation of the highest and unutterable Trinity, the maidens, that is, the faithful souls, being kindled, say: *We will be glad and rejoice in thee.* For then, when the sacrament of the resurrection and of the general judgment is completed, when the Son of God shall say to his faithful: « Come, ye blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the origin of the world [n: (Matth. XXV)], » [var: Vulg. Matth. 25:34 *possidete paratum vobis regnum a constitutione mundi*, "possess the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world"] and when the body which is corrupted shall no longer weigh down the soul, nor the earthly habitation press down [0549B] the sense that thinketh many things, but the soul, seeing God as he is, face to face [n: (I Cor. XIII)], by participation of the vision of the highest and incomprehensible Trinity, shall make its flesh wholly spiritual; then, I say, *320* shall these maidens, that is, the faithful souls, more truly say, *we will be glad and rejoice in thee.* For to us dwelling on earth and faithfully obeying thy precepts thou hast promised great things indeed, but in heaven thou hast rendered greater. Therefore *we will be glad and rejoice in thee. Mindful of thy breasts,* that is, of thy precepts, by the keeping of which and by the grace of the Holy Spirit we come to the contemplating of the brightness of thy majesty; or else *we will be glad and rejoice in thee, mindful of thy breasts above wine,* that is, of thy eloquences, which eloquences have been made sweet and honey-flowing [0549C] to us above wine, that is, more than the doctrine of the Mosaic law, which is deservedly compared to wine for the austerity of its precepts: which wine is said to kill little ones, because, as Paul says, « The law worketh wrath (that is, death) [n: (Rom. IV)]. » Therefore, King Christ, because thou hast conferred so many benefits on thy Bride, therefore the sons of that same Bride, that is, *the upright love thee.* Upright in thought, upright in speech, upright in work, they love thee. They are deservedly called upright, who after confession and penitence return not to sin. We may indeed refer that sentence to the person of Christ, so that Christ says: *The king hath brought me into his storerooms,* which is to say: My Father, who is the King of kings, commanded me to take flesh within the bowels of the blessed Mary, ever [0549D] a virgin. Which bowels of the most holy Virgin are deservedly called the storerooms of God the Father, because by him they were predestined from eternity, and by the same prepared and consecrated in the end of the ages for the nativity of his Son. Which nativity the maidens knowing, that is, the angelic and heavenly powers, sing glory to God in the highest and announce peace on earth to men of good will [n: (Luc. II)], as if they said to the Father: Deservedly *we will be glad and rejoice in thee,* because thou dost dispose to repair, through the incarnation of thy Son, our number which had been diminished by the devil's fall. *Mindful of thy breasts above wine,* as if it said: We remember thy mercy and thy admirable tenderness, whereby thou now temperest the austere sentence [0550A] of just damnation, which thou didst once bring upon the first man, when, his iniquity requiring it, thou didst shut him out from the joys of paradise and drive him forth into this valley of tears and of miseries. Considering therefore these benefits mercifully bestowed upon her by thee, *the upright love thee,* because neither in heaven nor on earth do they find anything dearer or worthier than thee. For thou didst create the whole world out of nothing when it was not, and didst fashion man of the slime of the earth with admirable power, and the same man whom the devil by his fraud seduced and destroyed, thy most kindly tenderness led back to the joys of paradise and saved; and therefore the upright love thee, because they find nothing which they may love better and more justly than thee. Let us also therefore, brethren, bring Christ into our storerooms. Now our storerooms are our body [0550B] and our soul, into which the Lord promises that he will enter, if, all evils being shut out, he shall find them prepared and clean for him, as he himself says: « Behold, I stand at the door and knock; if any man shall open to me the gate, I will come in to him, and will sup with him, and he with me [n: (Apoc. III)]. » But what do we think — what of joy, what of jubilation, what of gladness or of exultation — will the maidens, that is, all the virtues of our soul, then possess, when they shall see Christ present in his storerooms? Then moreover shall the Bridegroom say to these maidens: « Your joy no man shall take from you [n: (Joan. XVI)] »
