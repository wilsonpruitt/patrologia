## HERE BEGINS THE SIXTH TOME From that which is said: *I am black, but comely, O daughters of Jerusalem,* as far as *my vineyard I have not kept [n: (Cant. I)]*.

[0561C]They who seek gold, and linger in the golden mountains, for this reason, when the upper soil has been dug away, search through the lower and deeper parts of the earth, that with labor and sweat they may deserve to come to the long-desired weight of gold. But when by long digging they perhaps find gold, although they behold it covered over with rust drawn across it, and blackened with the filth of the earth, they nevertheless in no way despise it. For they know that if it be cast into the fire, and come between the hands of the hammerers, it will at once lay aside its accidental blackness together with the rust and the filth, and without delay will receive the natural brightness of gold with a flaming splendor. Who then are they who seek gold, if not they who love the wisdom and the spiritual understanding of the Holy Scriptures? And what are the golden mountains, if not [0561D] the holy doctors, that is, the prophets and the apostles? Of whom it is written: « I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains, whence help shall come to me [n: (Psal. CXX)]. » What then does the mass of gold covered with rust and filth signify, if not the writing of the prophets, which is exceedingly obscure, and wrapped up in divers enigmas and figures? Which it is needful that we should boil down in the fire of the Holy Spirit so long, until, the dross of the killing letter being cast away, we may come to wisdom and to spiritual understanding. As therefore gold, although outwardly discolored with rust and filth, is by no means scorned, [0562C] because its inward gleam and brightness is considered, so the writing of the prophets, although it be hedged about with the hard rind of the outward letter, is nevertheless in no way to be scorned, because within there lies hidden a precious and spiritual understanding. That therefore what we say may now be more clearly made plain, let us weigh what it is that the Bride, that is, holy Church, says to the maidens: *I am black,* she says, *but comely, O daughters of Jerusalem* — black indeed outwardly, but comely inwardly; as if she should say: I am gold darkened by the blackness of an accidental rust, but if fire be applied and I be burned through, I shall appear the brightest gold, that is, refined gold. For thus says the Scripture: « As gold is tried by fire, so tribulation of temptations tries the just [n: (Prov. XVII)]. » [var: Vulg. Prov. 17:3 *Sicut igne probatur argentum et aurum camino, ita corda probat Dominus*, "as silver is tried by fire and gold in the furnace, so the Lord trieth the hearts"] And Jerusalem is interpreted *the vision of peace,* and he calls the daughters of [0562D] Jerusalem the faithful souls, whom above he had called maidens. Whose study and highest intent it is that they may be able to please God alone, who according to the apostle Paul [n: (Ephes. II)] is our peace, and hath made both one. Let holy Church then say: O you, daughters of Jerusalem, that is, O you, faithful souls, who continually desire to see God, who is our peace, be not troubled, do not wonder, that I am black in the passion of my martyrs, but rejoice and be glad, that I am comely in the receiving of the eternal rewards. Black indeed I am, [0563A] and I mourn on account of the present persecutions which heretics and evil Christians inflict upon me, but I am comely and I rejoice, knowing that through many tribulations I must enter into the kingdom of God. Now she subjoins a comparison of her blackness and of her comeliness, saying: *As the tents of Cedar I am black, and as the skin of Solomon I am comely.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 1:4 *sicut pelles Salomonis*, plural, "the skins (curtains) of Solomon"; the singular is what the whole exposition below rests upon] Cedar was a son of Ismael the son of Abraham, and Cedar is interpreted *darkness.* And so it is related of him that he endured the greatest persecution from his neighbors, and, ever shifting his tents, fled from place to place. Which assuredly agrees well with holy Church, which, set amid the darkness of straits and the persecutions of adversaries, says: « We have not here a lasting city, but we seek one to come [n: (Hebr. XIII)]. » Of these [0563B] adversaries indeed the same Church complains, saying in the Psalms that « they have hated me without cause [n: (Psal. XXIV; Joan. XV)]. » But concerning the skin of Solomon I do not remember that I have found anything written, save in this place only, neither in the New nor in the Old Testament. For this reason a great question is raised for us. But it is to be known that not without a mighty mystery is that found in the divine letters which with so great difficulty and obscurity is scarcely understood. But unless we shall have understood the whole from the part, we shall assuredly grasp no spiritual understanding in this place. For it is the custom of Holy Scripture sometimes to take the whole from the part, sometimes the part from the whole. Which assuredly in this place it is necessary to do. For we read in the book of Kings [0563C] *328* that in his own time the most mighty king Solomon, when all Israel had been gathered together, set out with great array to Gabaon, because there was the greater high place [n: (III Reg. III)], and the tabernacle which the Lord had commanded to be made by the hand of Moses his servant, when the children of Israel went out of the land of Egypt. Which tabernacle was made after the pattern and likeness of a certain spiritual and heavenly tabernacle, which was shown in the mount to Moses the servant of God. Moreover thus says the Lord to Moses: « See, he says, that thou make the tabernacle according to the form of that tabernacle which thou sawest in the mount [n: (Exod. XXV)]. » [var: Vulg. Exod. 25:40 *inspice et fac secundum exemplar quod tibi in monte monstratum est*, "look and make it according to the pattern that was shown thee in the mount"] Therefore the earthly tabernacle was made after the pattern of the heavenly tabernacle. And that the tabernacle which was set up by Moses was of skins, [0563D] David testifies, who said to Nathan the prophet: « Seest thou that I dwell in a house of cedar, and the ark of God is in the midst of skins [n: (I Par. XVII)], » that is, in a tabernacle set up of skins. [var: Vulg. I Par. 17:1 *arca foederis Domini sub pellibus est*, "the ark of the covenant of the Lord is under skins"] Whom then did Solomon, who is interpreted *the peaceable one,* designate other than Christ, of whom Paul says that through his own blood he made peace as to the things that are in heaven and the things that are on earth. And what does it signify that the same Solomon went with all Israel to Gabaon, if not this, that Christ ascended into heaven with those whom he drew out from the lower regions. Whence David says: « Christ ascending on high, led captivity captive [n: (Psal. LXVII)]. » [var: Vulg. Psal. 67:19 *ascendisti in altum, cepisti captivitatem*, "thou hast ascended on high, thou hast taken captivity"; the wording here follows Ephes. 4:8, which does not name Christ] And what is that greater high place, if not the knowledge of the highest and incomprehensible Trinity? For we cannot climb a greater high place than that we should [0564A] know, as it is from eternity, that the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one and true God. And of what thing did that tabernacle bear the figure, which was built of the skins of dead animals, if not of the present Church, which was founded and established by our holy Fathers, that is, the prophets and the apostles? Therefore by the skin of Solomon we ought spiritually to take the whole tabernacle which was built of skins, that is, the whole from the part. Otherwise in this sentence we shall find no spiritual sense at all. Let the Bride then say, that is, holy Church: *I am black as the tents of Cedar,* that is, just as Cedar once suffered persecution from his neighbors, so I daily suffer harassment from heretics and false Christians, [0564B] but *I am comely as the skin of Solomon,* that is, as the tabernacle of Solomon built of skins, which, on account of the manifold ornaments of gold and of silver which it contained within, shone with an inestimable manner and an incomparable brightness. *Do not consider me that I am swarthy,* that is, do not despise me on account of the innumerable oppressions which I endure from adversaries, because for those very things I look for heavenly glory from the eternal judge in the day of judgment. *Because the sun hath discolored me,* for that reason, she says, you ought not to hold me in contempt if amid tribulations I appear swarthy, that is, black, because the sun hath discolored me, that is, because for the sake of Christ, who is the sun of justice, I gladly embrace and suffer these things; knowing most certainly that the humbler and the more abject I shall have been in this world for love of him, [0564C] the more glorious and the more acceptable I shall be in eternal happiness before his majesty. *The sons of my mother have fought against me:* where a sick man suffers the greater pain, there he is the more often compelled to lay his hand. Which holy Church seems to do in these things, who complains of heretics and false Christians by repeating, saying: *The sons of my mother have fought against me.* The mother of holy Church is the grace of the Holy Spirit, by which heretics and false Christians were regenerated in holy baptism, who fight against that same Church with words and with deeds and with depraved teachings. Of whom John in his Epistles says: « They went out from us, but they were not of us. For if they were of us, they would surely have remained with us [n: (I Joan. II)]. » Hence [0564D] Paul says: « I know that after my departure grievous wolves will enter in among you, who will not spare you [n: (Act. XX)]. » [var: Vulg. Act. 20:29 *lupi rapaces … non parcentes gregi*, "ravening wolves … not sparing the flock"] *They have set me keeper in the vineyards.* Holy Church here speaks in the person of the holy apostles, of whom it stands clearer than light that they were made planters and keepers of the vineyards, that is, of the Churches gathered from the Gentiles. *My vineyard I have not kept,* that is, the Synagogue of which we were born we forsook, on account of her unbelief and the blindness of her heart, and we made our passage over to the Gentiles, who received the word of God with joy. Whence Paul says to the Jews: « To you it behooved to speak the word of God first, but because you have thrust it away, and have judged yourselves unworthy of eternal life, behold we turn to the Gentiles [n: (Act. XIII)]. » Therefore the apostles were set as it were keepers in [0565A] the vineyards of the Gentiles, when it was said to them: « Go, teach all nations [n: (Matth. XXVIII)]. But then they did not keep their own vineyard, when the faithless Jews drove them out of the Synagogue, threatening them that they should speak to no one in the name of Jesus [n: (Act. IV)].
