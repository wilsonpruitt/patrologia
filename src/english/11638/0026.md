## RECAPITULATION FROM WHAT WENT BEFORE, HOW THIS SENTENCE, NAMELY, « I AM BLACK, BUT COMELY, » IS REFERRED TO THE PERSON OF CHRIST.

This sentence, namely *I am black, but comely,* can reasonably, by a good understanding, be referred to the flesh of Christ hanging upon the cross *329*, and as it were saying: *O you, daughters of Jerusalem,* that is, O you, faithful souls, black indeed I am on account of the pain of the imminent passion and the most rigorous [0565B] death, but comely and beautiful shall I be a little afterward, on account of the glorious resurrection and the wonderful ascension. Whence Paul says: « We see Jesus, on account of the suffering of death, crowned with glory and honor [n: (Hebr. II)]. » *As the tents of Cedar,* that is, just as once his neighbors persecuted Cedar, so also the Jews, my friends and my kinsmen, stood against me, and, crying out: « Crucify him [n: (Luc. XXIII; Joan. XIX)], » lifted up their voice against me. Which also the prophet Jeremias had prophesied long before in the person of Christ, saying thus: « My inheritance is become to me as a lion in the forest, it hath given out its voice against me [n: (Jer. XII)]. » Of the lion it is related that he goes round with roaring the forest [0565C] in which he abides, and this he does for this reason, that no means of going out may lie open to the animals remaining within the forest, so that afterwards he may the more easily bring death upon whichever animal he will. Which can fittingly and suitably enough be applied to the Jewish people, who, like a lion going round the whole forest of this world, says: « You see that we prevail nothing, behold the whole world goes after him. If we let him alone thus, all will believe in him [n: (Joan. XII)]. » [var: Vulg. Joan. 12:19 *ecce mundus totus post eum abiit*, "behold the whole world is gone after him"; the second sentence stands at Joan. 11:48] Which Jewish people also gave out its voice against Christ, when it cried to Pilate: « If thou release this man, thou art not Caesar's friend [n: (Joan. XIX)], » and why it said this it at once subjoined: « We have a law, and according to the law he ought to die. [n: (ibid.)]. » By Cedar therefore, who is interpreted *darkness,* [we ought to understand] the darkness of death; but by the skin of Solomon we ought [0565D] to understand the glory of the resurrection. Let the flesh of Christ hanging on the cross then say: *I am black as the tents of Cedar, but I am comely as the skin of Solomon,* that is, they are able to seem to bring upon me the darkness of death, but the glory of the resurrection they are not able to take away. *Do not consider me that I am swarthy, because the sun hath discolored me,* that is: Do not look upon me in that calamity and misery in which I now hang for you upon the cross, but look upon me as already made bright by the resurrection, and coming to judgment in my majesty. *Because the sun hath discolored me,* that is, because the sun of justice, who dwells within me, both willed me thus to be discolored through death, and after the resurrection will make me bright with incomparable glory. Whence the same Son says in the Gospel: « Glorify me, Father, with thyself, with the brightness which [0566A] I had before the world was made [n: (Joan. XVII)]. » [var: Vulg. Joan. 17:5 *claritatem quam habui priusquam mundus esset apud te*, accusative, "the glory which I had, before the world was, with thee"] *The sons of my mother have fought against me.* The mother of Christ, according to the flesh, was the Synagogue, because it too drew its origin through the patriarchs and the kings, and through blessed Mary. But in what manner the Jews, who are the sons of his mother, that is, of the Synagogue, fought against him in the passion, the Psalmist testifies, saying: « The sons of men, their teeth are weapons and arrows, and their tongue a sharp sword [n: (Psal. LVI)]. » *They have set me keeper in the vineyards, my vineyard I have not kept.* It is manifest that the faithless Jews persecuted Christ both living and dead, so far that with vain solicitude they even kept watch over his sepulchre; and the more they cast him out from themselves through their [sic: *incrudelitatem*], the more they sent him over to the Gentiles, who were to believe [0566B]. « Now the vineyard of the Lord of hosts, as Isaias says, is the house of Israel [n: (Isa. V)]. » Therefore the Jews set Christ as keeper in the vineyards of the Gentiles, when, being unwilling to believe in him, they drove him out from their hearts. And then Christ did not keep his own vineyard, when he forsook them, their own faithlessness requiring it. Whence he himself also says in the Gospel: « Behold your house shall be left to you desolate [n: (Luc. XIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Luc. 13:35 *relinquitur*, present, "is left"]
