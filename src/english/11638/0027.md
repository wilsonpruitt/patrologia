## RECAPITULATION FROM WHAT WENT BEFORE, HOW THIS SENTENCE, NAMELY, « I AM BLACK, BUT COMELY, » OUGHT TO BE REFERRED ALLEGORICALLY TO OUR NATURE.

I think therefore that we do no wrong, if we refer this sentence: *I am black, but comely,* to our nature [0566C] allegorically. For the spiritual man understands all things spiritually. Let our nature then say — not existing in that state in which it was founded by the highest Creator, but rather in that in which it remained, seduced by the devil: I am black on account of the disobedience and the sin of the first Adam my father, but I shall be comely on account of the obedience and the justification of the second Adam, that is, of Christ my redeemer. Whence Paul says: « As by the disobedience of one man many were made sinners, so by the obedience of one just man many shall be constituted [n: (Rom. V)]. » [var: Vulg. Rom. 5:19 *per unius oboeditionem iusti constituentur multi*, "by the obedience of one, many shall be made just" — there *iusti* is the predicate of *multi*, not a genitive with *unius*] *As the tents of Cedar,* that is, as Cedar was once made unstable on account of the persecution which he suffered from his neighbors, so from this time on I shall be unstable and shall never remain in the same state, on account of [0566D] the proud hope of empty glory which the devil falsely promised me, when he persuaded me to believe him, saying: « If you eat of the forbidden fruit, your eyes shall be opened, and you shall be as gods [n: (Gen. III)]. » [var: Vulg. Gen. 3:5 *in quocumque die comederitis ex eo*, "in what day soever you shall eat thereof"; the Vulgate has nothing answering to *de fructu vetito*, "of the forbidden fruit"] *As the skin of Solomon.* The Catholic Church holds and believes for certain that blessed Mary, who ever remained an inviolate virgin, drew her origin from our corrupted nature and sinful lump. Which blessed Virgin was made not only the skin, but even the tabernacle of Solomon, that is, of the true peaceable one, Christ, when the same Christ deigned for *330* the salvation of the human race to be born of her. Let our nature then say; Through the first Adam, a sinner, I have been made black, but through the second Adam, my redeemer, I shall be [0567A] comely. I am black, because I believed the devil rather than God, but I shall be comely, because the Son of God will take flesh of me, and will lead me back to the contemplating of the glory of the divine majesty — to which, from that state in which I had been created, I should have passed over without the pain of death, had I not, seduced by the devil with an empty hope, miserably fallen. *Do not consider me that I am swarthy,* that is, do not contemplate me such as I was made at the persuasion of the ancient enemy, but rather contemplate me such as I was created by the highest Creator. *Because the sun hath discolored me.* If by the sun we take temptation, we have a true and most approved sense. For the Lord says in the Gospel concerning the seeds sown upon the rock, that is, concerning unbelieving Christians and perverse heretics, that: [0567B] « When the sun was risen they withered away [n: (Luc. VIII)], » that is, when temptation had arisen they failed in faith and in work. [var: Vulg. Luc. 8:6 *natum aruit*, "having sprung up it withered"; *sole orto* belongs to Matth. 13:6, where the verb is *aestuaverunt*, "they were scorched"] Finally in the book of Psalms it is found written: « By day the sun shall not burn thee [n: (Psal. CXX)], » that is, in prosperity no temptation shall deceive thee. Let our nature then say: because the sun hath discolored me, that is, the temptation brought in by the devil took from me the color of immortality and of innocence, and discolored me with the color of death and with the blackness of sin. *The sons of my mother have fought against me.* The mother of our nature and of the angelic nature is the most clement grace of God the Creator, by which men and angels were wonderfully created, as is said in Genesis. « In the beginning God created heaven and earth [n: (Gen. I)], » that is, in himself, who is the beginning of all creatures, God created the angelic and the earthly [0567C] nature. From which angelic nature, namely, those angels, withdrawing through pride, apostatized, who afterwards, made demons, fought against our nature in paradise by the mediation of the serpent. Of whom in this place our same nature rightly complains, saying: *The sons of my mother have fought against me,* as if she should say: They who together with me were mercifully created by the highest Creator, they themselves cruelly drove me out of paradise, in which I had been set, through the suggestion of the serpent. *They have set me keeper in the vineyards; my vineyard I have not kept.* We can interpret this sentence according to that word of which it is written in Genesis: « Now the Lord had planted a paradise of pleasure from the beginning, in which he placed the man whom [0567D] he had formed, that he might work and keep it [n: (Gen. II)]. » For our nature was set as it were a keeper in the vineyards, when God sent the first man into paradise, planted with trees of divers kinds, that namely, by thinking and by speaking what should be pleasing to God, it might work and might keep itself, lest it be deceived by the devil. And our nature was as it were set over the vineyards of paradise, when it heard from the Lord: « Of every tree of paradise eat thou, but of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil do not eat [n: (ibid.)], » yet nevertheless, because our nature by no means kept this obedience but laid itself under the devil through disobedience, therefore it both lost the vineyards of paradise over which it had been set, and kept ill its own self, that is, its own vineyard.
