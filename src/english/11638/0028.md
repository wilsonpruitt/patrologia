## ACCORDING TO THE MORALITY. (cont.)

Since therefore, God helping, we have set forth the threefold allegory of this sentence, it now remains that, with the same one aiding us, we bring to light the morality which lies hidden in it, and reasonably fit it to our very selves, that is, to our soul. Let our soul also then say — wearied with divers and innumerable temptations, walled about with the thorns and the stings, the calamities and the miseries of its flesh, weighed down with the rottenness and the corruption of its flesh or body: *I am black,* because « the flesh lusteth against the spirit [n: (Galat. V)] » and unceasingly my body drags me to sin [n: (Rom. VI)]; *I am black,* she says, because I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind and [0568B] leading me captive into the law of sin. And now she has subjoined in what manner she is black, namely, *as the tents of Cedar.* That is, I am sluggish and unstable toward the good, but prone and slippery toward the evil. But of this Cedar the Psalmist complains when he says: « Woe is me! that I have dwelt with the inhabitants of Cedar [n: (Psal. CXL)]. » [var: Vulg. Psal. CXIX, 5 *habitationibus Cedar*, "the dwellings of Cedar"] that is, with those who love darkness, and are unstable in mind and in body. Whence the Lord speaks to Ezechiel, saying: « Son of man, thou dwellest among scorpions [n: (Ezech. II)]. » But the Lord called his prophet "son of man" for this reason, lest the same prophet should be made proud thereat, and slip into vain glory, because his God spoke to him. Now the scorpion has a bland and mild countenance, but behind it is wont to strike with its tail in an arched wound. Who then are these [0568C] scorpions, if not seducing men, and demons, the vices of the flesh, our concupiscences, and pleasures? For all these things, like scorpions, show us a bland and most sweet countenance, when by delighting us they draw us to sin; but afterwards from behind they strike as with a tail, and they kill, because, for that we gave them our assent unto sinning, on the last day of judgment we shall with all these irremediably endure the torments of hell. And this doubtless is for scorpions to strike with the tail and to kill. Those therefore whom Ezechiel calls scorpions, those David in the Psalms, *331* and Solomon in the Canticles calls Cedar. But thou also, whoever thou art that standing in the Church of God dost pray or sing psalms, as often as thou wanderest in mind and in spirit through the whole [0568D] world, and art laid waste within by unclean and foul and shameful thoughts, know thou that thou dwellest with the inhabitants of Cedar. But this also which follows in the same verse is greatly to be feared, namely: « My soul hath long been a sojourner [n: (Psal. CXL)], » that is, my soul has taken delight very long with the inhabitants of Cedar, that is, with vices and concupiscences, with pleasures and unclean desires, all which are Cedar, that is, darkness, because they make a man dark. Whence Paul says: « You were once darkness, » that is, sinners; « but now light, » that is, just, in the Lord [n: (Ephes. V)]. » *But I am comely as the skin of Solomon.* A little above she gave a likeness, namely the tents of Cedar, to which she likened herself when she says: *I am black as [0569A] the tents of Cedar.* But now she renders a comparison, namely, the skin of Solomon, to which she compared herself through comeliness, saying: *But I am comely as the skin of Solomon.* By which skin of Solomon we said above that the whole tabernacle is designated, which was built of skins. But it is necessary that we say somewhat of it according to the morality: « The tabernacle was built of skins dyed red, of hyacinth-colored skins, and of haircloths [n: (Exod. XXVI)]. By the skins dyed red we understand the martyrs, who poured out their blood for God; and by the hyacinth-colored skins we take the confessors, who for the love of the heavenly country labored through the whole course of their life. And by the haircloths we take sinners. If therefore our soul wishes to be the tabernacle of God, let it have in itself the charity [0569B] of martyrs, the faith of confessors, the hope of sinners. For it must needs possess, still amid adversity and prosperity, the fortitude and the patience of the martyrs. In good works, the perseverance and the longsuffering of the confessors; the penance and the humble confession of sinners. Moreover it is good for our soul that it be spiritually the ark of the Testament, which was in the tabernacle of God. For the ark was set up four-square of woods that cannot rot, gilded with the best and precious gold, made steady upon two bars by which it was carried [n: (Exod. XXV)]. If therefore our soul shall have imitated the examples of the saints (who are the woods that cannot rot), and shall have retained by memory the four Gospels (which are signified by the squareness of the ark), and shall have given labor to the spiritual wisdom which is designated by the gold, [0569C] and shall have understood spiritually both the Old and the New Testament, which [are set forth] figuratively by the two bars; then our soul will be a spiritual ark in the tabernacle of God. In the tabernacle, finally, there was an urn containing the heavenly manna, and the two tables of the testament, and the rod of Aaron which budded [n: (III Reg. VIII; Hebr. IX)]. » All these things therefore it behooves us to possess spiritually, that namely we may have the urn containing the heavenly manna, that is, that we may believe Christ to be both true God and true man. For by the urn his humanity, and by the heavenly manna his divinity, is understood. But none the less are the two tables of the Testament necessary to us, that after we shall have perfectly believed concerning Christ that he is true God and true man, we may hold a sound and Catholic mind concerning the Father [0569D] and the Holy Spirit, who are designated by the two tables, believing that the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit is one God. When therefore we shall have confessed the holy Trinity, that is, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, to be one God, I think it necessary that we hold a Catholic judgment concerning the humanity of Christ, which he took in blessed Mary, who is shown forth by the rod of Aaron. That namely we may believe without doubt that, as the same rod without the tillage of human help flowered and made fruit, so the blessed Virgin Mary without the touch of man conceived and bore the Son of God of the Holy Spirit. If therefore our soul shall have been the spiritual tabernacle of God, if within itself it shall have spiritually contained the ark, the urn, the tables, the rod of Aaron; then truly will it be able to say: [0570A] *I am comely as the skin of Solomon. Do not consider me that I am swarthy, because the sun hath discolored me.* When for the love of God and for the intent of the heavenly country we preach the Gospel to our hearers, if perchance out of that very speaking of ours we incur vain glory, either because we speak well, or because we are gladly heard by the people, then our soul, made swarthy by the contagion of vain glory, ought to say, *do not consider me that I am swarthy,* that is, do not despise me because I have sinned, since for the love of Christ I have incurred the lapse of empty glory: not that it pleases him, if I have offended in his own work, but because, unless for love of him I were to undertake the office of preaching, I should in no way have come to this stain of sin. Or assuredly, *do not consider me that I am swarthy,* that is, do not judge me [0570B] to be a sinner in my nature, because by the most high God I was created without the stain of sin: and that I now appear a sinner in the present, this I do not possess naturally out of myself, but because this pleased the sun of justice, that is, God, who according to his just will sent me over to this sinful flesh, from the indwelling of which my nature contracted sin, which before had been created without the blemish of sin by the good Creator. For as good wine, when it is put into a corrupt little vessel, is at once corrupted — not that from its own nature it has this, that it should be corrupted, but by the participation of the corrupt vessel into which it has been put it is corrupted — so our soul is not naturally *332* a sinner and corrupt, but by the participation of the corrupt body in which it has been placed, although it is naturally incorrupt, [0570C] it is made accidentally corrupt. Truly how this comes to pass no one knows, but because it so pleases God, and pleased him from the beginning, who in his holy disposition from eternity ordained all things blamelessly. And this is what follows, because the sun hath discolored me. *The sons of my mother have fought against me.* The sons of the mother of our soul may allegorically be called the five senses of the flesh, namely sight, hearing, smell, taste and touch, by which the human flesh is ruled and governed, as matter is sustained and helped by its own sons. Which sons of the flesh, in what manner they war in daily conflict against the soul, we can show by an experience convincing enough. Does not sight fight against the soul when the eye sees a woman so as [0570D] to lust after her? Whence Jeremias says: « My eyes have plundered my soul [n: (Thren. III)]. » [var: Vulg. Thren. 3:51 *oculus meus depraedatus est animam meam*, singular, "my eye hath wasted my soul"] For truly the unhappy eyes plunder and assault the wretched soul, when the unlawful things which they see without they announce to her within, and strive to take from her the simple chastity by which our soul is familiarly joined to God. But the battle which our hearing unceasingly wages against the soul, anyone can easily show. For whatever we hear that is sin, and that disagrees from the good of true religion, all this without doubt wounds our soul. What then shall I say of the smell, which sends to the soul all the things that by an outward breath it draws to itself within? And while it infects him with outward delights, it weakens and kills her as with wounds inflicted. But how our mouth [0571A] by lying slays the soul, let Solomon, being asked, answer, for thus he himself says: « The mouth that lieth killeth the soul [n: (Sap. I)]. » Which David assuredly testifies, saying in the book of Psalms: « Thou wilt destroy all that speak a lie [n: (Psal. V)]. » And the Lord in the Gospel: « Out of thy mouth, he says, thou shalt be justified, and out of thy mouth thou shalt be condemned [n: (Matth. XII)]. » [var: Vulg. Matth. 12:37 *ex verbis tuis*, "by thy words"; the exposition that follows turns on the mouth] Therefore according to the sentence of the Lord, our mouth, by speaking and by eating unlawful things, condemns us. But if we eat the things that are necessary for us, and speak the things that are pleasing to God, then our mouth justifies us. But with what darts touch assaults the soul, the soldiers of Christ know, who daily fight the spiritual battles of the Lord. Whence Holy Scripture also testifies: « As he who touches pitch [0571B] is defiled by it, so he who touches his neighbor's wife is made utterly unclean [n: (Eccli. XIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Eccli. 13:1 *qui communicaverit superbo induet superbiam*, "he that hath fellowship with the proud shall put on pride"; the clause on the neighbor's wife stands at Prov. 6:29] Let our soul then say: *The sons of my mother have fought against me,* that is, the five senses which reign in the flesh assail me with unceasing war, and because, while I am in the flesh, I must use their solace, I am in no way able to pass over even a single hour of the day without their battle. *They have set me keeper in the vineyards, the vineyard* [sic: *neam*] *I have not kept.* It is manifest, and no one living religiously doubts, that the more diligently each one keeps watch over the guarding of another's actions, the less carefully does he sometimes observe his own actions. And that our actions are called vineyards, blessed Gregory testifies in his writings. [0571C] Let our soul then say: *They have set me keeper in the vineyards, my vineyard I have not kept.* Which is to say: While I desire to keep and to amend the actions of my neighbors, I attend less studiously to my own proper actions. For indeed they also who keep the temporal vineyards of this world many times incur the perils of blowing winds and of rushing rains, nor do they fear, for the watching of those same vineyards, to endure the loss of their own body, while they love the temporal rewards which they judge that they can receive from the lords of the vineyards. For so, even so, do the prelates of holy Church. For when through preaching they serve for the gathering in of souls, they sometimes bear less care of their own selves than would be needful, and not seldom for that very preaching of theirs they gladly take on the death of the body from persecutors, [0571D] because they set before their eyes the eternal rewards which they await from God — which assuredly the apostles and the martyrs did.
