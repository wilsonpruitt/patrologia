## HOW THIS SENTENCE, « I AM BLACK, BUT COMELY, » IS ASCRIBED TO BLESSED MARY.

Since therefore, after we have with God's aid brought to light the threefold allegory and the simple morality of this sentence, it is fitting that we direct our pen to the person of the blessed Virgin Mary, because we do not doubt that this whole book was put forth by the Holy Spirit through the mouth of Solomon unto her praise and unto the honor of holy Church. Let blessed Mary herself then also say: *I am black, but comely, O daughters of Jerusalem,* that is: I, a daughter of the heavenly Jerusalem, which is above, our mother [0572A] [n: (Gal. IV)]; I am black in the present world, that is, contemptible and despised and worn out with mourning, while I see my son crucified by the envy of the Jews, drenched with his own blood; black, I say, am I by reason of present sadness, because the pain of my son's anguish reaches even to the pain of my heart. Whence Simeon also said to blessed Mary: « And thy own soul a sword shall pierce [n: (Luc. II)]; » as if he should say: The pain of the passion and of the death of Christ thy Son shall reach and shall wound *333* the inmost recesses of thy heart; and so thou shalt prove thyself to be his true mother, when with the inmost affection of thy heart thou shalt suffer together with him dying upon the cross. Let blessed Mary then say: *I am black* by reason of grief and sadness, because I see my son dying upon the cross; *but I am comely,* that is, I rejoice and exult, because [0572B] I behold him already rising again from death. *As the tents of Cedar I am black,* but *as the skin of Solomon I am comely.* By the tents, which by carrying are shifted from place to place, is understood the transitory and changeable sadness of the present life; but by the skin, that is, by the tabernacle of Solomon, is designated the heavenly glory which God inhabits. Let blessed Mary then say: I indeed am now afflicted with a transitory and temporal sadness for the death of my son, but a little afterward I shall rejoice with him rising again, and ascending to the heavens, and reigning in the heavenly glory. *Do not consider me that I am swarthy, because the sun hath discolored me,* that is, do not hold me in contempt, nor despise me, because you discern me afflicted in the present tribulation, since indeed, O you Jews, it is on account of my son, who is the sun of justice, [0572C] that I am troubled and afflicted — whom you have slain. Or assuredly, do not contemplate me blackened with calamity and misery for the death of my son, but consider me as already placed at his right hand, and reigning with him in eternal brightness. *The sons of my mother have fought against me.* But spiritually the sons of the mother of the blessed and perpetual Virgin Mary were the wicked Jews, who stood forth as the sons of the Synagogue, and without mercy fought against her, when they slew her son upon the cross. For from the pain and the passion which the Son endured on the cross she was in no way exempt. Moreover it is to be believed that the anguish of the death of Christ dying on the cross reached even to the inward parts of his most loving Mother, nor could she look upon him hanging on the cross [0572D] save with great pain, whom she knew both to be true God, and to have truly taken flesh of her own flesh. *They have set me keeper in the vineyards, my vineyard I have not kept.* Rejoice and praise, O habitation of Sion [n: (Isa. XII)], » that is, the Church gathered from the Gentiles, thou who art kept and governed by so great a keeper. Be glad, I say, because the Queen of the heavens and the Lady of the angels has been set as thy keeper. For thou art the spiritual vineyard of the Lord Sabaoth, that is, of hosts: over thee is set as keeper the empress of the world, who has been exalted above the choirs of the angels. Of whom it is deservedly said that she did not keep her own vineyard, and that on account of the unbelief and the pride of the Jews she forsook the Synagogue. Which assuredly she seemed to have been bound to keep with a carnal affection, and to cherish with the help of her prayers, because she is known [0573A] to have drawn her carnal origin from it. Nevertheless, since that same Synagogue, that is, the faithless Jews, spurned her as though she were their sister, and would not honor her, by the just judgment of God, both in this world and in the day of judgment, they shall lack her intercession and her help. « Thou therefore, Jerusalem, praise the Lord, and praise thy God, O Sion [n: (Psal. CXLVII)], » that is, O Church of the Gentiles, thou who art therefore spiritually called Jerusalem and Sion, because thou art proved to cleave by faith and by work to God, who is the highest peace; and standing upon the watchtower, that is, upon the height of the spiritual life, thou beholdest thine enemies afar off. Praise, I say, O Church, the God [0574A] who has given thee so great an advocate, namely his own glorious mother, whom the Jews for this reason despise, because they say that she was their sister according to the flesh. Let us therefore, the faithlessness and the impiety of the Jews having been driven from our hearts, honor and magnify with due praise the Mother of our Redeemer. Knowing this for certain, that the honor and the praise, and the glory, and the magnificence which we show to his mother on earth will make him well-disposed toward us in the heavens also, and, coming to judgment in his majesty with angels and archangels, placable.

[0573] *Here ends the sixth Tome.*
