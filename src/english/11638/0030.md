## HERE BEGINS THE SEVENTH TOME Upon that which is said: *Show me, thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest down at midday, lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions [n: (Cant. I)]*.

[0573B]Among the twelve precious stones, whoever desires to find the topaz, more precious than the other stones, must needs know by nature the sizes and the colors of all the stones, lest perchance, led by error, he take the one for the other without distinction. For among the twelve there are two stones in some part like the topaz, namely the chrysolite and the chrysoprase, which seem to be of a golden color, as the topaz is. Yet in this the topaz differs from the other stones, that it is rarer in the finding, and, as is said, shines back with every color. That same stone, moreover, is found in Ethiopia and in the region of the Thebaid, whence also in the same province *334* an island is reported to be, which is called Topazion, where that same stone, the topaz, is found. And that the topaz comes from Ethiopia, the Lord, speaking to blessed Job, makes plain. [0573C] For when the Lord was speaking to that same Job concerning wisdom: « The topaz of Ethiopia, he says, shall not be made equal to it [n: (Job XXVI)]. » Who then are the twelve precious stones, if not the twelve apostles founded upon the foundation which is Christ? And what other does the topaz signify, rarer and more precious than the other stones, if not our Lord Jesus Christ, who is « beautiful in form above the sons of men [n: (Psal. XLIV)], » and is humbler than all men? Hence also he himself is reported in the Gospel to have said to his disciples: « Learn of me, for I am meek and humble of heart [n: (Matth. XI)]. » Whence it is well said that the topaz shines back with every color, because « in him dwelleth all the fullness of the Godhead [n: (Coloss. II)], » [var: Vulg. *in ipso inhabitat omnis plenitudo divinitatis corporaliter*, adding "bodily"] and he himself is adorned [0573D] with the adornment of every spiritual virtue, because according to John, « We have seen him as the only-begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth [n: (Joan. I)]. » [var: Vulg. *et vidimus gloriam ejus, gloriam quasi unigeniti a Patre*, "and we saw his glory, the glory as of the only-begotten of the Father"] Moreover, that the same stone, the topaz, has given from itself its name to the island in which it is found — namely, that from the topaz the island is called Topazion — is believed to have come about by no means without a mighty mystery. For as by the topaz we understand Christ, so also by this island we understand holy Church. And just as this [0574B] island obtained its name from the topaz stone, so as to be called Topazion from it, so also holy Church is named Christian from Christ the corner stone, and all the faithful Christians take delight in being marked with the name of Christ. Assuredly, in that we have compared holy Church to an island, we have spoken according to Isaiah the prophet, who by the spirit of prophecy calls all the Churches islands, saying: « Hear, ye islands, and hearken, ye peoples from afar [n: (Isa. XLIX)]. » Rightly therefore are the Churches of the gentiles compared to islands, because as islands are lashed by the blasts of the winds and are often shaken by the tumults of the rushing waves, so the Churches spread abroad through the various parts of the world are pressed down by great persecutions and are driven about by innumerable temptations. But this also, that the topaz, according to the sentence [0574C] of the Lord speaking, is said to be found in Ethiopia, and that the same topaz is by no means made equal to wisdom, may fitly enough be fitted to the Lord Jesus. For by wisdom Christ's divinity, and by the topaz his humanity, is worthily understood: which topaz is taken up out of the Ethiopian land, because his humanity is assumed out of our sinful and darkened nature; and we can in no wise compare his humanity to his divinity, because he is equal to the Father according to divinity, but less than the Father according to humanity. This, lastly, the same Son makes plain in the Gospel, saying: « I and the Father are one [n: (Joan. X)], » which pertains to his divinity; and in another place: « The Father is greater than I [n: (Joan. XIV)], » [0574D] which pertains to his humanity. Whence also David the prophet bears witness in the Psalms: « Thou hast lessened him a little less than the angels [n: (Psal. VIII)]. » By a very little less than the angels was the Son of God lessened, because, whereas he was God immortal and invisible, for us he was made man, mortal and visible. And since for the salvation of the human race, at the bidding of his Father, he so far humbled himself, therefore he received from that same Father of his the name which is above every name, that at his name [0575A] every knee should bow, and that by every creature of heaven and of earth [n: (Philip. II)] he should be adored as one God with the Father and the Holy Spirit. And this is what follows in that verse: « With glory and honor thou hast crowned him, O Lord, and hast set him over the works of thy hands [n: (Psal. VIII)]. » [var: Vulg. has no *Domine* in Psal. VIII, 6]
