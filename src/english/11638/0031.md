## HERE BEGINS THE SEVENTH TOME Upon that which is said: *Show me, thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest down at midday, lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions *. (cont.)

Therefore, after this little distinction which we have made, let us now return to that from which we have strayed rather far, and with the intent ear of the heart let us hear what it is that the Bride says to the Bridegroom, the Church to Christ: *Show me, thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest down at midday,* etc. As if she should say: Thou art the topaz, thou art the precious stone; thee I seek, thee I desire, thee above all things I love. Show me thine own self, because among so many precious stones I cannot find thee; and this is what [0575B] follows: *Lest perchance I begin to wander through the flocks of thy companions,* [var: Vulg. and the lemma above, *ne vagari incipiam post greges sodalium tuorum*, "lest I begin to wander after the flocks"] seeking thee thus, as once those men wandered and erred of whom in the Gospel the Lord was saying: « Whom do men say the Son of man to be? The disciples answered: Some Elias, some Jeremias, or one of the prophets [n: (Matth. XVI)]. » [var: Vulg. *At illi dixerunt: Alii Joannem Baptistam, alii autem Eliam, alii vero Jeremiam*, naming John the Baptist first] All these then were beginning to wander after the flock of the companions, that is, they were erring among the precious stones, seeking the topaz, and they wished to take for the topaz the chrysolite or the chrysoprase, which in some part are like it, because they are, as it is, of a golden color. For they had heard that the prophets had wrought miracles as Christ did, which is to be of a golden color as the topaz is. But Peter, the Holy Spirit revealing it, the other stones [0575C] being left aside, acknowledged the true topaz when he said: « Thou art Christ the Son of the living God [n: (ibid.)]. » As if he should say: The prophets indeed stood forth among men of great authority in their time, that is, like precious stones they shone with the glory of miracles; but nevertheless, in the manner in which thou art the Son of God, they in no wise were. Let us hear then, the tumult of worldly affairs put away, the prayer by which holy Church beseeches Christ that he would deign to manifest himself to her by sure tokens, lest perchance she should adore and worship some one of the saints (which God forbid!) in his stead, and, seduced by the error of her own simplicity, should fall into idolatry. Let us consider lastly what Christ answers to holy Church, and how *335* he removes the worship of the divinity alone from all the saints, and attributes it to God alone, to whom by right it is owed. For the saints [0575D] we ought to honor, not to adore. But God alone is to be adored and to be honored; the saints indeed, for love of him, are only to be honored, not to be adored. Let Christ therefore say to holy Church: Thou beseechest me that, although with all ambiguity removed I should assure thee where — that is, in what place — I feed or lie down, and I will answer thee that at midday I am fed and lie down, that is, in my Father I rest and abide, in whom I have abode equal to him in all things from eternity. And that the Son abides in the Father and the Father in the Son ineffably from eternity, every authority of the New as well as of the Old Testament proclaims with concordant voice. Whence is that which the Lord speaks to the Father concerning his disciples: « Father, I will that as thou art in me, and I in thee, so they also may be one in [0576A] us [n: (Joan. XVII)]. » [var: Vulg. Joan. XVII, 21, *ut omnes unum sint, sicut tu, Pater, in me, et ego in te, ut et ipsi in nobis unum sint*, without *volo*] And in another place the Lord says to Philip: « Philip, he that seeth me seeth my Father also; thou believest not that I am in the Father and the Father is in me [n: (Joan. XIV)]. » [var: Vulg. Joan. XIV, 9–10, *videt et Patrem* (without *meum*), and *Non creditis quia ego in Patre, et Pater in me est?* — plural, and a question] Otherwise believe for the very works' sake, since therefore we have sufficiently shown by the authority of the Holy Scriptures how the Son abides in the Father, that is, how Christ lies down and feeds at midday: it now remains that it be shown by us where in the divine oracles God the Father is called midday, because this is rarely, nay most rarely, found, and the more rarely, by so much the more preciously. God almighty, « the Father of mercies, and the God of all consolation, who comforteth us in all our tribulation [n: (II Cor. I)], » to whom the evils displease which we do, and to whom the goods please which we perform, himself speaks in Isaiah the prophet, [0576B] saying: « I will rest in my place, and I will consider as the midday light [n: (Isa. XVIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. XVIII, 4, *Quiescam, et considerabo in loco meo, sicut meridiana lux clara est*] What then is it that he says, In my place I will rest? When did he himself labor, that he should now seek rest? And why does he compare himself to the midday light rather than to the breeze or to another hour of the day? And what is it that he says, I will consider, since to consider belongs to him who either knows not a thing or understands it not? For he himself knows and understands all things before they come to be. What then is the rest of God, if not peace in the heart of a faithful and religious Christian? For thus it is written: « In peace in the selfsame I will sleep and I will rest [n: (Psal. IV)]. » And in Isaiah we find the Lord saying: « This is my rest, refresh thou the fallen [n: (Isa. XXVIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. XXVIII, 12, *reficite lassum*, "refresh ye the weary" — plural imperative, and *lassum* "weary" for *lapsum* "fallen"] Great rest therefore do we give to our God, when we exhort the fallen sinner to be turned from his [0576C] sin. And elsewhere Scripture says: « Upon whom shall I rest, if not upon the humble and the quiet, and him that trembleth at my words? » [n: (Isa. LXVI.)] [var: Vulg. Isa. LXVI, 2, *ad quem autem respiciam, nisi ad pauperculum et contritum spiritu, et trementem sermones meos*, "to whom shall I have regard, but to the poor little one and contrite of spirit"] And what is the place of God, if not peace and justice, piety and mercy, and obedience the mother of all virtues? Hence David the prophet says: « In peace is his place made, and his habitation in Sion [n: (Psal. LXXV)]. » For Sion is interpreted *specula.* Now a watchtower is a lofty thing, upon which, if a man be set, he will be able to look very far away from himself. By Sion therefore, which is interpreted *specula,* is understood obedience, the mother of all virtues. In which, if any man deserve to persevere unto the last day of his life, without doubt he will be able to come even to the contemplating [0576D] of the brightness of the divine majesty. For thus Holy Scripture speaks concerning obedience: « Through obedience one comes even to the knowledge of the hidden things of God. » And what is it that God the Father considers, if not this, that he himself, according to Job the prophet [n: (cap. XXXI)], numbers all our steps, that is, our works, so that, namely, after death he may render to each one of us according to what he has done. For thus says Paul the apostle: « We must all be made manifest before the tribunal of Christ, that every one may receive according as he hath done, whether it be good or evil [n: (II Cor. V)], » [var: Vulg. II Cor. V, 10, *ut referat unusquisque propria corporis, prout gessit*, "that every one may render the proper things of the body"] Hence also blessed Augustine says: "As we now stand in the Church, and behold one another present, so on the day of judgment we are to stand before the tribunal of God. For this soul which we now [0577A] have, then we shall have, and in this very body wherewith we are now clothed we are to be raised up. For we were not made like horses and oxen, that we should perish by eternal death, but after we have lived here, we shall be judged elsewhere, that either for good works we may obtain eternal rewards, or for our sins we may obtain perpetual punishments." Whence also the Prophet says: « God hath spoken once [n: (Psal. LXI)], » that is, from eternity he unchangeably established and predestined it. What? « These two things which I have heard [n: (ibid.)]. » What are they? All « the power of God [n: (ibid.)] » is the almighty Father's. Behold, thou hast the one. And to thee, O Lord Jesus Christ, mercy. Behold, thou hast the other. For thou wilt render in the day of judgment to every man according to his works [n: (Apoc. XXII)]. And that judgment pertains to the Son, and not to the Father, [0577B] hear him saying in the Gospel: « The Father judgeth not any man, but hath given all judgment to the Son [n: (Joan. V)]. » God the almighty Father therefore considers in his place, as the midday light, all the things which we do in this world, that through his Son he may render to us in the day of judgment according to what we have deserved. Whence it is written: « God most high is a patient renderer [n: (Eccle. V)]. » And rightly is God called Most High, because no thing is compared to his loftiness. And Holy Scripture calls God a patient renderer for this reason, that he who patiently awaits sinners, that they may be converted, at length the more harshly condemns those not converted. But now let us behold why God almighty compared himself to the midday brightness, and why [0577C] he who is the Creator of all creatures made himself equal to his own creature, that is, to the brightness of the sun. For neither among all creatures could he find, so far as concerns us, any creature brighter than the sun. As therefore the sun, above all the elements and luminaries *336* of this world, stands eminent and shines back by reason of its dignity and its brightness, so God almighty appears more worthy and more eminent than all the things which he created unto the praise and glory of his name. And as the sun, in the burning midday season, with the rays of its wondrous brightness poured out everywhere, illumines the whole world, so every creature, his omnipotence and mercy being considered, trembles before God almighty and adores him. Of which mercy the Psalmist says: « The earth is full of the mercy of the Lord [n: (Psal. XXXII)]; » and again: [0577D] « The mercies of the Lord I will sing for ever [n: (Psal. LXXXVIII)]. » Truly the earth is full of the Lord's mercy, because every creature which lives in it has received this — that it may subsist — not from its own merit, but from the mercy of God. And well does the same Psalmist say that he will sing the mercies of the Lord for ever, because both this, that we work well here, and that after death we are brought into the joys of paradise, all this proceeds from the mercy of God, who founded us when we were not, and by his own tenderness saved us when we had been deceived and lost by the devil's persuasion. Who also, according to Paul the apostle [n: (I Cor. II)], wills that no man perish, but wills that all come to the acknowledgment of himself. But if this stands firm, that God wills no man to perish, but [0578A] wills all to attain to the knowledge of his divinity, why do they perish who perish? Assuredly they perish for this, that they scorn to obey the commandments of God. Behold, by God's bounty we have shown, as much as we could, from the words of the Gospel, that the Son abides from eternity in the Father. And from the oracles of Isaiah the prophet we have brought forth a sentence in which God the Father calls himself, by comparison, the midday light.
