## CONCLUSION OF THE SENTENCE.

Therefore it stands for certain, and no man doubts thereof, that if the Father is the midday, and if the Son abides in the Father, without doubt he abides in the midday. Let Christ therefore answer — it is just — to holy Church, and say: Thou seekest of me that I show thee where I feed and lie down, and I will answer thee that at midday I feed [0578B] and lie down, that is, in my Father, who is the true midday, I abide as I have abode from eternity.

## RECAPITULATION FROM THE FOREGOING, IN WHAT MANNER THIS SENTENCE OUGHT TO BE REFERRED TO THE PERSON OF THE FATHER, NAMELY, « SHOW ME, » ETC.

He who watereth the eternal mountains from his upper places, who satisfieth and maketh drunk the earth lying beneath from the fruit of his works [n: (Psal. X)], he himself is able to give us the spirit of spiritual wisdom, filled with which we may be strong to understand those hidden mysteries in the Scriptures of the divine law which we desire. For as once Moses, that excellent prophet [n: (Exod. XIX)], first ascended the mountain, and afterwards had familiar converse with the Lord concerning secret things, so it is needful that holy Church do now. For she climbs as it were a great and [0578C] lofty mountain when, despising all transitory things and transcending her very self, she came to the colloquy of her Bridegroom, that is, of the Son of God, and sought of him, saying: *Show me, thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest down,* etc. The highest mountain therefore being ascended — that is, after she has fully enjoyed the most sweet address of the Son of God the Father — it is needful that she come humbly to God the Father himself, and seek of him with suppliant devotion where he feeds, where he lies down. Let us return therefore to the beginning of our discourse, and let us subtly inquire what the eternal mountains are, or what the bedewings from above are, or what is the earth lying beneath, or the satiety and drunkenness of the fruits of God. Those, I judge, ought to be understood as the eternal mountains of which the Psalmist says to the Lord: [0578D] « Thou enlightening wondrously from the eternal mountains [n: (Psal. LXXV)]. » The eternal mountains therefore are the patriarchs and the prophets and the apostles, by whom we have been wondrously enlightened, because through the examples of the patriarchs, through the utterances of the prophets, through the doctrine of the apostles we came to know the faith of the Christian religion — we who before, removed far from the knowledge of God, were sitting in darkness and in the shadow of death; whence Isaiah the prophet says: « The people of the gentiles that walked in darkness hath seen a great light; to them that dwelt in the region of the shadow of death, a light is risen [n: (Isai. IX)]. » [var: Vulg. Isa. IX, 2, *Populus qui ambulabat in tenebris*, without *gentium*] Truly a great and wondrous light has risen upon the peoples of the gentiles, when the faith of Christ was planted and rooted in their hearts through the preaching of the apostles, and as they began to have Christ as an indweller within them, who before were receiving into the [0579A] lodging of the heart the devil, that most wicked seducer and most cruel slayer of their own souls. Whence also Paul says: « That Christ dwell by faith in our hearts [n: (Ephes. III)]. » [var: Vulg. Ephes. III, 17, *in cordibus vestris*, "in your hearts"] And lastly blessed Augustine: « Faith, he says, in thy heart, Christ is in thy heart. » It stands therefore clearer than light that, unless the light of faith shine back in our hearts, we shall sit in the darkness of unbelief, and shall in no wise have Christ as an indweller. Of these mountains the Psalmist says: « The high mountains for the harts, the rock a refuge for the hedgehogs [n: (Psal. CIII)]. » That the rock signifies Christ, Paul the apostle teaches, saying: « And the rock was Christ [n: (I Cor. X)]. » And the high mountains are those same who above *337* were called the holy mountains, namely the prophets and [0579B] apostles. The custom of harts is said to be such, that they flee to the mountains when they are put to flight by hunters. It is plain to all that hedgehogs are full of prickles and thorns. By these two animals are designated two kinds of sinful men, which are contained in the bosom of holy Church. One kind is that which does penance and weeps for its sins, and, distrusting its own acts, hopes to come to the mercy of God through the suffrages and prayers of the saints. Which kind of men is rightly compared to the harts, which flee to the mountains that they may be delivered from death. But the other kind of men is that which lies under hidden and public sins, and the more, despairing, it subjects itself to divers crimes, by so much the further does it withdraw from God; and while, the fear of God being cast off, it dreads not to sin before [0579C] men, it shows itself to be, after the manner of the hedgehog as it were, full of thorns and prickles. Yet sometimes this kind of men is pricked with compunction in the article of death, and, the mass of its sins being considered, is greatly tormented in its inner man; and while it doubts not that it is going to the pains of eternal torment, touched by a divine inbreathing it hastens to return to Christ, who is the true rock — who, opening to mortals the bosom of clemency and the fountain of tenderness and mercy, cries through his prophet: « I will not the death of the sinner, but that he be converted and live [n: (Ezech. XVIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Ezech. XXXIII, 11 *nolo mortem impii*, "the death of the wicked"; Ezech. XVIII, 23, which Migne cites, reads *numquid voluntatis meae est mors impii*] The high mountains therefore are a refuge for the harts, and the rock is a refuge for the hedgehogs, because as penitent sinners who work well are saved through suffrages [0579D] and prayers, so the wretched, the despairing, and those hedged about with the thorns of their own sins are received by Christ, if only at the last hour of their life they be turned to him. For thus Holy Scripture says: « In whatsoever hour the sinner shall have groaned, he shall be saved [n: (ibid.)]. » [var: Vulg. Ezech. has no such sentence; cf. Ezech. XXXIII, 12, *impietas impii non nocebit ei, in quacumque die conversus fuerit ab impietate sua*] Behold, God helping, we have shown as much as we could what the eternal mountains are; now it remains that we demonstrate what the waterings from above are. For Moses says: « Let my doctrine gather as the rain, and let my speech flow as the dew [n: (Deut. XXXII)]. Isaiah also says to the Lord: « For thy dew is the dew of light [n: (Isa. XXVI)]. » It is manifest therefore that the Holy Scriptures are called the waterings from above, [0580A] by which the prophets and apostles were watered, and as it were made fruitful by a heavenly mouth. Of this watering Paul the apostle speaks, saying: « The earth, drinking in the rain that cometh from above, and bringing forth what is seasonable, receiveth blessing from those by whom it is tilled [n: (Heb. VI)]. » [var: Vulg. Heb. VI, 7, *Terra enim saepe venientem super se bibens imbrem, et generans herbam opportunam illis a quibus colitur, accipit benedictionem a Deo*] Of this heavenly bedewing again David speaks, saying: « The stream of the river maketh the city of God joyful [n: (Psal. XLV)]. » And the city of God is the body or the soul of a just man, which is then more fully made joyful, when it is delightfully bedewed by the streams of the divine utterances. Hence again David says: « They shall be inebriated with the plenty of thy house, and thou shalt give them to drink of the torrent of thy pleasure [n: (Psal. XXXV)]. » For the house of God is holy Church, the plenty of that same house is Holy Scripture, by which the faithful soul is soberly and religiously made drunk, [0580B] when, lulled by a spiritual excess, it forgets all the things that are of this world, and is joined to God alone by purity of mind. And the torrent of God's pleasure is the renunciation of this world, because as a torrent overwhelms and hurls down all things set before it, so he who perfectly renounces the world despises things prosperous and adverse, gladly crucifies his flesh with its vices and concupiscences; there is nothing that can hold back the purpose of his mind, until he may come to him whom he loves above all things and above his very self. Since therefore it stands firm that the renunciation of this world is a great pleasure and delight of God, it is manifest that they who renounce neither their own selves nor the desires or concupiscences of the world prepare in their hearts no acceptable delight at all for God. [0580C] Let the impious therefore go and abound in the riches of this world, and let them know beyond doubt that the more they serve this world and their own flesh by living wickedly, by so much the further do they withdraw from God, and join themselves to the devil, that cruel lord. For thus says John the apostle: « Little children, love not the world, nor the things that are in the world; for if any man shall have loved the world, the charity of the Father is not in him [n: (I Joan. II)]. » [var: Vulg. I Joan. II, 15, *Nolite diligere mundum, neque ea quae in mundo sunt. Si quis diligit mundum…* — without *Filioli*, and with the present *diligit*] From which words it clearly appears that the love of this world removes the charity of God from human hearts; and since God is said to be charity, charity being removed from our heart, without doubt God also is removed.
