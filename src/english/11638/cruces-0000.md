## C. Vulgate divergences — the deliverable list

Every printed quotation in the range was read word by word against the Vulgate.
**Twenty-three material divergences fired `[var: …]`** (2 · 4 · 8 · 2 · 6 · 1
across chunks 0000–0005); the rest are logged here
without a marker. Migne's reading is what the English renders in every case.

### Marked `[var: …]`

1. **0000 @0490C, Ps 30:20** — Migne `Magna, inquit, multitudo dulcedinis tuae`;
   Vulg. `Quam magna multitudo…`. The loss of *Quam* turns an exclamation into a
   statement, and Philip's gloss (`Vere magna et admirabilis est multitudo`)
   follows Migne's declarative, not the Vulgate's exclamatory. Note that the
   `[n:]` falls **inside** the guillemets on the plate and is reproduced there.
2. **0000 @0491D, 1 Cor 10:11** — Migne `Quoniam quidem omnia quae contigerunt
   patribus nostris, in figura contigerunt illis`; Vulg. `Haec autem omnia in
   figura contingebant illis`. A full re-cast, and *patribus nostris* is
   Philip's own hinge for the next sentence (`Si ergo apud illos figura fuit…`).
   Not italicized on the plate; rendered as running text, as printed.
3. **0001 @0494B, John 17:3** — Migne drops `solum Deum verum`. **Material and
   load-bearing:** Philip immediately supplies the missing predicate himself
   (`subauditur: et Spiritum sanctum, unum et verum Deum esse`), which only makes
   sense against a text that lacks it. A conformed lemma here would have made
   Philip's gloss redundant.
4. **0001 @0494B, Luke 2:14** — Migne `Gloria in excelsis Deo`; Vulg. `in
   altissimis`. Familiar-liturgical form against the Vulgate's.
5. ⛔ **0001 @0495C, Luke 12:35 (cited as Luc. XXII)** — Migne prints `Sint lumbi
   **nostri** praecincti`. The Vulgate has `lumbi **vestri**`. **This is the
   range's sharpest 7a″ trap:** the remembered "let your loins be girded" arrives
   before the printed word is read, and it also silently repairs Migne's chapter
   number. Rendered "Let **our** loins be girded." The reference is also wrong
   (Luke 12, not 22) — printed as-is per `refDisplay` policy, not corrected.
6. **0001 @0496A, Ps 93:19** — Migne `Secundum multitudinem **tentationum**
   mearum`; Vulg. `**dolorum** meorum`. **The whole paragraph is about
   temptation** (`ab omnigena tentationum infestatione vulneratis`), so the
   divergent word is the one the argument runs on. Unmarked as a quotation on the
   plate apart from the italics; no `[n:]`.
7. **0002 @0496D, Ps 35:7** — Migne `Judicia **Dei** abyssus multa`; Vulg.
   `judicia **tua**`. Third person for second; Philip's frame requires the third.
8. **0002 @0496D, Eccl 9:2** — Migne `Omnia reservantur homini post mortem
   incerta`; Vulg. `sed omnia in futurum servantur incerta`. *post mortem* is not
   in the Vulgate at all and is exactly what Philip's argument (the sealed well
   of God's counsel until judgment) needs.
9. **0002 @0497A, Matt 25:41** — Migne `Ite, maledicti…`; Vulg. `Discedite a me
   maledicti… qui paratus est diabolo`. *praeparatus* for *paratus* is an
   orthographic variant only (Pattern 9, no marker of its own).
10. **0002 @0497A, Matt 25:34** — Migne `percipite regnum`; Vulg. `possidete
    paratum vobis regnum`.
11. **0002 @0497B, John 17:21–22 (cited as Joan. XVIII)** — Migne `Pater, volo ut
    sicut ego et tu unum sumus, ita et ipsi unum sint in nobis`; the Vulgate's
    clause order and wording are wholly different. Reference likewise off by one
    chapter; printed as-is.
12. **0002 @0497B, 1 John 3:2 (cited as Joan. III)** — Migne `nondum **apparet**`
    (present); Vulg. `nondum **apparuit**` (perfect). The 7914 @1071D class
    (tense quietly restored to the Vulgate). Rendered "it doth not yet appear."
13. **0002 @0497D, Matt 5:48** — Migne `Perfecti estote, sicut et Pater vester
    perfectus est qui in coelis est`; Vulg. `Estote ergo vos perfecti, sicut et
    Pater vester caelestis perfectus est`.
14. ⛔ **0002 @0498A, Prov 16:5 — A DROPPED NEGATIVE, and it inverts the
    argument.** Migne prints `Qui exaltat cor suum, **mundus** est coram Deo`.
    The maxim as commonly cited in the schools is `**immundus** est coram Deo
    omnis qui exaltat cor suum` (a loose form of Prov 16:5, `abominatio Domini
    est omnis arrogans`). Philip's whole paragraph argues that a virgin who is
    proud of her virginity is **unclean** — Eve was `corpore virgo et casta, sed
    mente immunda` — and the very next clause says `nec prodest quidquam virginem
    et castum esse corpore, si fuerit incestus in mente`. As printed, the
    Scripture proof asserts the exact contrary of the sentence it is adduced for.
    **Rendered literally ("He that exalteth his heart is clean before God") per
    Pattern 7a; not repaired.** The lost `im-` is a plate defect of exactly the
    11536 @1085D / 8625 @0940B class. This is the single most important entry in
    the file.
15. **0003 @0499A, Ezek 36:26–27 (cited as cap. XI et XXXVI)** — Migne `Tollam a
    vobis cor lapideum`; Vulg. `auferam cor lapideum **de carne vestra**`.
16. **0003 @0499D, 1 Cor 6:17** — Migne `Qui adhaeret **Deo**, unus spiritus
    **efficitur cum ipso**`; Vulg. `Qui autem adhaeret **Domino**, unus spiritus
    **est**`. Philip's own chunk 0000 close (`unus spiritus effici merear cum
    ipso`, @0493A) is built on Migne's expanded form, not the Vulgate's.
17. **0004 @0501A, 1 Pet 2:5** — Migne `superaedificamini **in habitaculo
    Spiritus sancti**`; Vulg. `superaedificamini **domus spiritualis**`. Migne's
    reading is what the surrounding argument (the spirit as *habitaculum Dei*)
    requires.
18. **0004 @0501C, Ps 44:8** — Migne `unxit te **Deus Pater tuus** oleo
    **jubilationis** prae **omnibus sanctis** tuis`; Vulg. `unxit te Deus, Deus
    tuus, oleo **laetitiae** prae **consortibus** tuis`. Three divergences in one
    verse, and the third is load-bearing: the sentence immediately before reads
    `significavit Christum **prae omnibus sanctis** impletum Spiritus sancti
    charismatibus` — the exposition quotes Migne's phrase, not the Vulgate's.
19. ⛔ **0004 @0502B, Ps 83:8** — Migne prints `deorsum in Sion` where the Vulgate
    has `Deus **deorum** in Sion`. *deorum* → *deorsum* is a one-letter plate
    corruption that turns "the God of gods in Sion" into "downward in Sion," and
    Philip has already supplied `Deum` outside the quotation (`donec videat Deum
    *deorsum in Sion* regnantem`), which is what makes the corrupt reading
    construe at all. Rendered "until it see God, *downward in Sion*, reigning."
    Not repaired.
20. **0004 @0502C, Phil 2:8** — Migne adds `Patri` (`factus est obediens **Patri**
    usque ad mortem`), which the Vulgate lacks; the addition is Philip's point
    (obedience reconciling the race to the Father).
21. **0004 @0502D, 1 John 2:27 (cited as II Joan. II)** — Migne `Et vos unctionem
    a Spiritu sancto accepistis, et, ut aliquis doceat vos, necesse non habetis`;
    the Vulgate's clause is a jussive (`maneat in vobis`) and names no Spirit.
22. **0004 @0502D, John 7:37–38** — Migne compresses, dropping `Qui credit in me,
    sicut dicit Scriptura` and reading `de ventre ejus fluent **aquae vivae**`
    for `flumina… aquae vivae`; also `dicebat de Spiritu **sancto**` for `dixit
    de Spiritu`.
23. **0005 @0504A, Isa 59:1–2** — Migne `Non est invalida manus Domini ad
    salvandum, sed **peccata nostra fecerunt murum inter vos et Deum**`; Vulg.
    `Ecce non est abbreviata manus Domini ut salvare nequeat… sed iniquitates
    **vestrae** diviserunt inter vos et Deum **vestrum**`. Note the internal
    number clash Migne prints — *peccata **nostra*** but *inter **vos*** —
    carried as printed (§B).

## D. Text cruces — printed readings rendered literally, not repaired

1. ⛔ **0001 @0494D — the sentence breaks off mid-word.** Our TEI ends the
   paragraph `…in unam personam Dei et hominis aeternaliter **constit**`. The
   verb is almost certainly *constituat*, but the truncation is at the level of
   our **digitized source**, not demonstrably Migne's plate, so it takes neither
   `[sic:]` (which would blame the plate) nor a silent repair. The fragment is
   carried in italics and an **`[ed: …]`** marks the hole (Pattern 13, clause 3):
   a reader-visible break that would otherwise read as our own bug.
   **Recovery: read PL 203 col. 494D on the plate; if the plate carries more,
   the fix belongs in `data/tei-patches/11638.json`, and the `[ed:]` comes out in
   the same commit.**
2. ⛔ **0004 @0500B–C — a clause with no verb.** Migne prints `…et pugnare contra
   se ipsum, ut dominans animo suo, **fortior ut expugnatore urbium**.` The
   allusion is Prov 16:32 (`qui dominatur animo suo, expugnatore urbium`), where
   the missing word is *melior est* / *sit*. The second `ut` stands where a
   finite verb belongs and cannot be construed with the ablative *expugnatore*.
   **Nothing is supplied.** Rendered "so that, ruling over its own mind, stronger
   as the stormer of cities." — the English stops where the Latin stops. Every
   `ut` Migne prints is on the page (Pattern 7a: the printed word is carried even
   when the sentence will not construe).
3. **0004 @0500C — `ut *vineat* seipsum et mundum`.** *vineat* is not a Latin
   word; *vincat* is certain from the context (`ut vincat seipsum et mundum`,
   answering `ego vici mundum` two lines later). Non-word class, Pattern 7 as
   ruled 2026-07-28: **carried untranslated in italics inside `[sic: *vineat*]`,
   not read as *vincat*.**
4. **0004 @0501B — a missing terminal stop.** `O quam magna est clementia nostri
   Redemptoris Quid enim innixio Dei…` — the plate prints no punctuation between
   the exclamation and the following question. **Not supplied** (Pattern 8); the
   English runs on identically.
5. **0003 @0499B — a full stop inside a relative clause.** `fides, spes,
   charitas sunt. quae nostras simplices enutriunt cogitationes.` The stop
   before the lowercase *quae* is the plate's. **Not deleted** (Pattern 8, the
   11542 @1041D class): the English reads "…are faith, hope, charity. which
   nourish our simple thoughts."
6. **0000 @0493A — `David *prophetae* dicit`.** Dative where the parallel at
   0004 @0501C reads `David *propheta* loquitur ad Filium Dei`. Rendered
   literally as a dative ("David says to the prophet"); this is a real word
   merely mis-inflected, so under Pattern 12 it takes no `[sic:]` — the English
   word on the page is ours. Conjecture: *propheta*, near-certain.
7. **0000 @0493A — `Junge ergo, si *tali* placet, lilium violae vel rosae`.**
   Bare dative *tali* with no antecedent; rendered "if it please such a one."
   Conjecture: *si talia placent* or *si ita placet*. Nothing supplied.
8. **0001 @0494C — `Duo ejusdem e diverso *dentes* accedunt`,** in a sentence
   whose next clause is `simul conjunctis **labiis**`. *dentes* (teeth) where
   *labia* (lips) is what a kiss requires and what Philip himself writes three
   words later. Rendered "Two teeth of the same one draw near from opposite
   sides"; a real word, so Pattern 7 not Pattern 12 — no marker. Conjecture:
   *labia*, but the *dentes*/*labia* pair may be Philip's own (two teeth = the
   two natures meeting), so this is flagged, not resolved.
9. **0002 @0497D–0498A — two finite verbs, no relative.** `ideo bene quidem in
   nostra recapitulatione **sequantur** lilia virginitatem, castitatemque, et
   munditiam **significant**.` Conjecture: *lilia, quae virginitatem… significant*.
   Rendered as two clauses exactly as printed: "…let the lilies follow, they
   signify virginity and chastity and cleanness."
10. **0000 @0491B — a promise of seven, a list of two.** `omnes et singulae
    sententiae… septem in se ad minus continere videntur, scilicet…` followed by
    only the divinity/humanity and the Christ/Church conjunction before the
    sentence turns to rhetoric and the arts. Rendered as printed; the count is
    made good at 0000 @0493B (`In ista sententia invenies septem sacramenta…`),
    which lists seven. No defect asserted.
11. **0001 @0494A — `per omnen textum sui`** for *omnem*: orthographic, Pattern 9,
    translated normally.
12. **0003 @0499B — `ne nos *dimersos* obruant`** for *demersos*: attested
    medieval spelling, Pattern 9, no marker.
13. **0004 @0500A — `terra obice *obstrusum* manebat`:** *obstrusus* is attested
    (from *obtrudo*); Pattern 9, translated normally ("shut up by a barrier of
    earth").
