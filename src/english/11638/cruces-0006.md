## Terminology decisions (for the merge — these are the ones a peer agent could differ on)

The work's whole argument is a **fixed pair of virtues**, and elegant variation on it
would be a doctrinal error of the same class as 7a″'s lemma-conformation. Fixed here:

| Latin | English | why fixed |
|---|---|---|
| *clementia* | **clemency** | never "kindness"/"mercy" — Philip assigns *clementia* to the divinity and *misericordia* to the humanity and the distinction is load-bearing throughout 0006–0011 |
| *misericordia* | **mercy** | as above |
| *Sponsus / Sponsa* | **Bridegroom / Bride** | |
| *ubera* | **breasts** | the lemma word; never "bosom" |
| *moralitas* | **morality** (the moral sense) | opposed to *allegoria* and *historia* in his threefold scheme |
| *historia* | **the history** (the literal sense), and **the history of Kings** where it names the book | |
| *oraculum* | **oracle** (the inner sanctuary, Douay usage at III Reg. VI) | |
| *anaglypta / anaglyptum / anaglyta* | kept **italic Latin, in the form printed each time** | Philip *defines* the word ("a kind of sculpture whose hinder part lies hidden within…"); an English equivalent would make the definition explain nothing. The spelling varies on the plate (0509A *anaglypta*, 0510A *anaglyta*, 0510A *Anaglyptum*) and each form is kept as printed. |
| *sacramentum* | **sacrament** everywhere; *mysterium* = **mystery** | forced by 0504C, where the plate prints both in one phrase (*ecclesiastica mysteria et sacramenta coelestia*). This gives medieval-broad "sacrament" at 0506D (*certo rationis sacramento*) and 0516B (*aliud sacramentum … de elephante*); that breadth is Philip's, not ours. |
| *occidens littera* | **the killing letter** | |
| *sacra / divina Scriptura* | **Holy Scripture** | Pattern 3 |
| *candidatio* | kept **italic Latin** | Song-of-Songs ruling, case 2: the gloss IS the etymology (Libanus → whiteness → virginity), and no English word carries it |
| *pacificus* (gloss on *Salomon*) | **peaceable** (translated) | contrast with *candidatio*: here the etymology survives into English, because "peaceable" and "peace" (*pax nostra*, Eph 2:14, quoted two lines later) share a root |
| *minuo / minutum* | kept **Latin** in the grammatical excursus at 0506D–0507A | the argument is an explicit etymology (*a verbo quod est* minuo, minuis, minutum *dictum reperimus*) tied to Ps 8:6 *Minuisti eum*; Englishing it would destroy the derivation. Italics supplied on the mention-forms Migne prints roman (register rule for technical Latin); Migne's own italics on *minuo, minuis,* are kept. |

**Second person (Pattern 17).** Nothing in this range addresses a plural except quoted
scripture. Philip's own singular addressee — *o auditor qui audis et legis … stude et tu*
(0506A), *Sic enim habes in Evangelio scriptum* (0514D), *si … adjunxeris … adimplebis*
(0515B) — takes **thou** throughout. Quoted plurals take **you**: *Lac vobis potum dedi*,
*Vos unctionem habetis … docet vos*, *Patres vestri … moriemini*, *Ut fides vestra*,
*Confidite*, *Poenitentiam agite*, *vos palmites estis*. Quoted singulars take **thou**:
*ubera tua*, *Scio, Domine … es tu*, *in te projectus sum*, *in manus tuas*, *Si tu es
Christus*, *Consilium tuum*, *Semen tuum*, *In semine tuo*, *Venter tuus*. The range
therefore shifts number inside one paragraph at 0506A (thou → *Lac vobis*) and that is
the plate's own shift, not drift.

**Quotation typography.** Migne marks **every** quotation in 0006–0011 with italics; the
plate prints **no guillemets at all** in this range (verified: 0 « in all six Latin
chunks). Per Pattern 6 none were supplied, and no `" "` were supplied either — the
italics already do the work, and adding straight quotes on top would double-mark. Guillemet
parity with the Latin twin is therefore 0 = 0 in every chunk. **This is the italic-dominant
end of the work; the switch to « » begins at 0014, outside this range.**

**Inline edition apparatus.** The plate carries italic page-numbers of an earlier edition
inside the running text — *295* (0505A), *296* (0506D), *297* (0508C), *298* (0510B),
*299* (0512A), *300* (0513D), *301* (0011 head), *302* (0517B). All are reproduced
verbatim, untranslated, in place, per the Joel precedent. Three of them fall **inside** an
italic scripture lemma or a head; they are kept there.

## A. Vulgate-divergence list — the `[var: …]` decisions

Every printed quotation in the range was read word by word against the Vulgate. Twenty
markers fired. The ones that fired are listed first; the ones examined and **deliberately
not** marked follow, because that list is the evidence that the marked ones were chosen,
not merely noticed.

### Fired

1. **0504D · Jon. 4:2** — Migne: *Scio, Domine, quia clemens et misericors es tu.*
   Vulg.: *scio enim quia tu Deus clemens et misericors es.* The plate replaces the
   predicate **Deus** with a vocative **Domine**. Material, and Philip's gloss hangs on
   exactly this word: *Scio pro certo quia **tu, qui nunc Deus solummodo omnibus
   innotescis**…* — he is expounding a *tu* that the Vulgate makes explicitly *tu Deus*.
   `[var: Vulg. Jon. 4:2 …]`.

2. **0505A · Rom. 4:15 + Hebr. 7:19** — Migne prints *Lex iram, (id est mortem) operatur,
   et nihil ad perfectum adduxit lex* under a single `[n: (Rom. IV)]`. Only the first
   clause is Romans; *nihil ad perfectum adduxit lex* is Hebr. 7:19. Migne's own note is
   reproduced verbatim and uncorrected (a reference is never emended here); the `[var:]`
   states the two loci so a reader is not left thinking Rom. IV contains both.

3. **0505B · Ps. 21:10–11** — Migne: *Ab uberibus matris meae in te projectus sum.* The
   words are Vulgate words but drawn from **either side of a verse division**: v. 10 ends
   *spes mea ab uberibus matris meae*, v. 11 begins *In te projectus sum ex utero*. Marked
   because the stitched clause is what Philip glosses (*Occidentem litteram … deserui*),
   i.e. the argument rests on the join.

4. **0505D · I Joan. 2:20** — Migne: *Vos unctionem habetis **a Spiritus sancto***. Vulg.:
   *vos unctionem habetis **a Sancto*** ("from the Holy One"). Material: the whole
   paragraph is about the gifts of the Holy Spirit descending from Christ, and the plate
   has already supplied the identification the Vulgate leaves open. (For the grammatical
   defect in *a Spiritus sancto* see § B.1.)

5. **0506A · II Cor. 2:15 (+14)** — Migne: *Bonus odor sumus Christi **in omni loco***.
   Vulg. v. 15: *Christi bonus odor sumus **Deo***. *In omni loco* belongs to v. 14. The
   plate has swapped the addressee-dative for the locative phrase of the previous verse.

6. **0506A · II Cor. 2:14** — Migne: *Gratias agimus Deo qui odorem notitiae suae per nos
   **manifestavit** in omni loco.* Vulg.: *Deo autem gratias qui … odorem notitiae suae
   **manifestat** per nos in omni loco* — present, not perfect, and the opening is
   restructured. Marked chiefly for the tense: Philip's point is a standing apostolic
   fragrance, and the perfect closes it into a past act.

7. **0508B · I Cor. 2:8** — Migne: *Si enim Jesum Dominum cognovissent, nunquam **Regem
   gloriae** crucifixissent.* Vulg.: *si enim cognovissent, nunquam **Dominum gloriae**
   crucifixissent.* **King** for **Lord** of glory, plus the added object *Jesum Dominum*.
   The commonest sort of 7a″ trap in this work: the familiar English ("the Lord of glory")
   is exactly what the plate does not say.

8. **0508C · Ezech. 18:31–32 / 33:11** — Migne: *Et quare moriemini **filii Israel**? quia
   nolo mortem **peccatoris**, dicit Dominus, sed ut convertatur et vivat.* Vulg. 18:31–32:
   *quare moriemini **domus Israel** … quia nolo mortem **morientis** dicit Dominus Deus,
   revertimini et vivite*; the tail *sed ut convertatur et vivat* answers to 33:11. Two
   substantive word-changes plus a conflated close.

9. **0509B · Zach. 4:14** — Migne: *Isti sunt duo filii olei **splendoris***. Vulg.: *isti
   sunt duo filii olei*. The added genitive is not in the received text.

10. **0509D · Ps. 102:10** — Migne: *Domine, non secundum peccata nostra **facias** nobis,
    neque secundum iniquitates nostras **retribuas** nobis.* Vulg. is **indicative**:
    *non secundum peccata nostra **fecit** nobis, neque … **retribuit** nobis.* The plate
    gives the liturgical petition-form. Material: a statement about what God has done
    becomes a prayer for what he may do, and Philip's frame (*si Deus ad mala … vellet
    respicere*) reads either way, which is precisely why the substitution is invisible.

11. **0511B · I Petr. 1:7** — Migne: *Ut **fides vestra** multo pretiosior sit auro quod
    per ignem probatur.* Vulg.: *ut **probatio fidei vestrae** multo pretiosior auro…* The
    Vulgate prices the *trial* of the faith; the plate prices the faith. Philip's argument
    (gold = the Catholic faith) is built on the plate's shorter form.

12. **0512C · Isa. 25:1** — Migne: *Consilium tuum, Domine, antiquum verum fiat.* Vulg.:
    *quoniam fecisti mirabilia, cogitationes antiquas fideles, amen.* An entirely different
    Latin of the verse. Marked with the Vulgate reading only; **no conjecture is offered
    about its origin** (Pattern 14 forbids speculating why witnesses differ).

13. **0512D · Ezech. 17:3–4 (+22–23)** — Migne's eagle sentence is a free conflation:
    Vulg. 17:3–4 has *aquila grandis magnarum alarum, longo membrorum ductu, plena plumis
    et varietate, venit ad Libanum et tulit medullam cedri*, and the transport ends *in
    terram Chanaan, in urbe negotiatorum*; the placing *in monte sancto et excelso* belongs
    to 17:22–23. The whole Marian exposition that follows depends on the mountain, so the
    join is load-bearing.

14. **0513D · Gen. 3:15** — Migne: ***Semen tuum** conteret **caput serpentis***. Vulg.:
    ***ipsa** conteret **caput tuum***. The plate's reading is the *semen*-reading, not the
    *ipsa*-reading, and Philip's point (the promise of the **coming** of the Son) requires
    the seed. This is the single most consequential divergence in the range.

15. **0515B · Isa. 11:2** — Migne omits ***spiritus Domini*** before *spiritus sapientiae
    et intellectus*. Marked because Philip is counting: he needs **seven** gifts to add to
    five virtues and reach twelve, and the plate's shorter list gives exactly seven.

16. **0516C · Apoc. 12:15** — Migne: *Et misit **draco flumen** post mulierem.* Vulg.: *et
    misit **serpens ex ore suo** post mulierem **aquam tamquam flumen***. The whole
    surrounding *exemplum* is elephant-and-**dragon**, so the substituted subject is the
    one the argument needs.

17. **0517A · Dan. 4:8** — Migne: *cujus proceritas pertingebat usque ad coelum, et **rami
    illius dilatabantur** usque ad terminos terrae.* Vulg. 4:8: *proceritas eius contingens
    caelum, **aspectus illius** erat usque ad terminos universae terrae* — the *branches*
    enter only at v. 9. Philip then identifies the branches with the Apostles and quotes
    Ps. 18 on their sound going out to the ends of the earth: **the exposition rests
    entirely on a word the Vulgate does not have at this verse.** Textbook 7a″.

18. **0517A · Hebr. 4:14** — Migne: *Habemus pontificem magnum **Christum Jesum** qui
    penetravit coelos.* Vulg.: *habentes ergo pontificem magnum qui penetravit caelos,
    **Iesum Filium Dei***. The christological title is replaced.

19. **0517C · Eccli. 41:17** (Migne's note reads *Eccli. XL.*, kept verbatim) — Migne:
    *Sapientia **occulta** et thesaurus **absconditus***. Vulg.: *sapientia **absconsa** et
    thesaurus **invisus***. Both nouns carry a different adjective.

20. **0517D · Cant. 7:2** — Migne: ***Venter tuus** sicut crater tornatilis.* Vulg.:
    ***umbilicus tuus** crater tornatilis, numquam indigens poculis; **venter tuus sicut
    acervus tritici***. The plate has moved the turned bowl from the navel to the belly.
    Philip adduces it for **roundness** (*thronus ille rotundus … de virtute in virtutem*),
    which the Vulgate's *acervus tritici* would not give him. He is quoting his own base
    text, the Song — which makes this the most surprising divergence in the range.

## B. Carried type, defects and unconstruable readings

### Marked `[sic: …]` — carried untranslated (Patterns 7, 10, 12)

1. **0507B · *perficis*** — *tot convicia a **perficis** hominibus pertulit*. Not a word;
   conjecture ***perfidis*** ("faithless"), near-certain: the whole chapter is about the
   *perfidia Judaeorum* and 0508B prints *apud perfidos Judaeos* correctly. Carried.
2. **0509A · *victuram*** — *sculpsit in eis **victuram** cherubim* (III Reg. 6:32).
   Conjecture ***picturam***, the Vulgate reading. Carried; sits inside an italic lemma, so
   it takes **no second italic marker** (Pattern 10) — `[sic: victuram]`, asterisks
   deliberately omitted.
3. **0512A · *vestitvit*** — *Et **vestitvit** eum auro fulvo nimis.* Conjecture
   ***vestivit***, which the *same lemma* prints correctly nine columns earlier at 0509A.
   Inside an italic lemma; no second italic marker.
4. **0513C · *abur*** — *si pannus lineus super ipsum **abur** mittatur.* Conjecture
   ***ebur***, printed correctly three words later (*sua virtute ebur pannum … custodit*).
   In roman prose, so italics are kept inside the marker. ⚠ **The trap here was ours, not
   Migne's:** the first draft of this chunk rendered *abur* as "the ivory" — a silent
   repair of exactly the class Pattern 7a describes, invisible afterwards because the
   sentence is about ivory. It was caught on the marker sweep and reverted.
5. **0516B · *draeo*** — ***draeo** quippe, ut ait beatus Augustinus, serpens vel animal
   est.* Conjecture ***draco***, printed correctly in the next clause and throughout.
6. **0518B · *quodidie*** — *quae filiis suis … **quodidie** patefacit regna coelestia.*
   Conjecture ***quotidie***; the work prints *quotidiano* at 0513A and *quotidie* at
   0518A, so this is a one-off and not an attested variant (Pattern 9 does not cover it).

### Real words that are merely wrong — rendered as ordinary English, **no** marker
Pattern 12's boundary: these *can* be rendered, so the English word on the page is ours
even though the reading is Migne's.

7. **0507A (×2) · *munitum* for *minutum*** — *Multum, inquam, manna istud **munitum**
   erat* and *Valde, inquam, apparuit **munitum** istud manna*. Rendered **"fortified"**
   both times. The argument requires *minutum*: the sentence before it reads *manna fuisse
   **minutum** describitur*, the sentence after glosses *se **minoravit***, and the entire
   excursus is the etymology *minuo → minutum*. **The literal rendering therefore says
   the opposite of the argument, and it stands.** Translating it "small" would have been
   the 11547/8625 silent repair with no trace left on the page.
8. **0506D · *per eos* for *per os*** — *quare Spiritus sanctus **per eos** Moysi prophetae
   dicat apparuisse minutum.* Rendered literally, "says **through them** to Moses the
   prophet". Conjecture ***per os Moysi prophetae***, "through the mouth of Moses the
   prophet" — the very phrase the work prints at 0512A (*per os historiographi*). The
   printed genitive *Moysi prophetae* is left dangling in the English as it dangles in the
   Latin; no connective was supplied to make it construe.
9. **0511D · *pius* for *prius*** — *Verumtamen **pius** considerandum nobis est.* A
   nominative adjective where an adverb is required. Rendered **"piously"**; conjecture
   *prius*, "first" (which the paragraph's *Superest igitur … Tunc demum … Verumtamen*
   sequence wants).
10. **0513C · *Qui* for *Cui*** — *Qui ergo manifeste non clarescit vel tenuiter scienti…*
    Nominative against the dative *scienti*. Rendered literally ("Who therefore … does not
    manifestly grow clear, even to one who knows but slightly"); conjecture *Cui*. This is
    Pattern 7a mechanism 5 (reference mis-bound), and the negation *non* is carried.
11. **0517B · *sub terra* for *subter eam*** — *quia **sub terra** habitant bestiae agri*,
    where the quotation five lines above reads *Subter eam habitabant bestiae agri*.
    Rendered "under the earth". The plate contradicts its own quotation of Dan. 4:9 within
    one paragraph, and the English shows it.
12. **0504D · *efficieris*** — *futurum est, ut … pro nobis homo **efficieris***: future
    indicative where *futurum est ut* requires the subjunctive. Rendered "thou shalt be
    made"; no English exponent for the mood (Pattern 9 class).
13. **0512A / 0514B · nominative-for-genitive slips with no English exponent** —
    *divinitatis hominem illum quem assumpsit … provexit* (for *divinitas*), and
    *summitatis throni rotunda sit* (for *summitas*). Rendered by sense; there is no
    emendation to make in English and none to undo (Pattern 9).

### Unconstruable — rendered as closely as the printed words allow

14. **0518A** — *Et nisi ipse Deus homo fieret verus Deus, nullatenus cum Patre et Spiritu
    sancto pari honore adoraretur.* The sentence does not construe: *fieret* wants one
    predicate and has two (*homo*, *verus Deus*), and the preceding sentence has already
    used *Nisi enim Deus homo fieret* in the other direction. Rendered word-for-word —
    "And unless he himself, God, became man true God, he would in no wise be adored…" — with
    **no connective supplied** to smooth it. Probable intent: *nisi ipse Deus homo **esset**
    verus Deus*, "unless this same God-made-man were true God." Escalated rather than fixed.
15. **0514A–B · *floruit*** — *In quinta autem aetate quae erat a David usque ad
    nativitatem beatae Mariae **floruit**, quia…* Two finite verbs (*erat*, *floruit*) in
    one relative clause. Rendered by taking both with *aetate* and supplying the connective
    **in brackets** — "which was from David unto the nativity of blessed Mary **[and]**
    flourished" — so that the supplement is visible as ours.

### Punctuation carried against the sense (Pattern 8 — preserved, never repaired)

16. **0504C** — *Ideo optavi adventum tuum. id est osculum tuum* — full stop mid-clause,
    lowercase continuation. Kept: "…thy coming. that is, thy kiss".
17. **0506C, 0508A** — *Manhu, quod significat. Quid est hoc?* The Vulgate's *quod
    significat quid est hoc* is one clause ("which means, What is this?"); Migne's full stop
    cuts *significat* off from its object. It is printed **twice** at 0506C and 0508A, both
    times the same way, so it is the plate's settled punctuation, not a slip in one line.
    Kept both times: "Manhu, which signifieth. What is this?"
18. **0517B** — *infimum in sancta Ecclesia obtinent locum isti vero. veluti spirituales…*
    — no mark where the clause turns, a full stop three words later. Kept exactly: "…obtain
    the lowest place in holy Church the latter however. as being spiritual…"
19. **0518A** — *Duae autem manus quae tenent sedile. divinitas et humanitas Christi sunt* —
    full stop for a comma. Kept.
20. **0512C** — *(si dici fas est!)* — exclamation mark inside the parenthesis, kept.
21. **0507D** — the citation *(ibid)* is printed **without a closing period and inside the
    italic run**, not as an `[n:]`. Reproduced exactly as printed matter, untranslated.

### Chunk-boundary defect

22. **0511B, end of chunk 0008** — the file ends
    *…et ipse est propitiatio pro peccatis nostris (I Joan. II.* ` ,`
    The citation's parenthesis is **never closed**, the italic run swallows it, and a bare
    comma stands after the closing asterisk. Reproduced verbatim, including the orphan
    comma; **no `»`-style supply and no `[ed:]`**, because nothing here is demonstrably lost
    from the digitization — the text simply breaks at our chunk division and chunk 0009
    opens a new head. Per the standing rule on `(cont.)` boundaries this is ours to merge,
    not to mend. **Flagged for the merge:** whoever assembles the work should confirm
    against 0009 that no words fall between them.
