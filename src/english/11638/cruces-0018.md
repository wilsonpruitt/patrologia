## 1. Terminology decisions made in this range — read these first at merge time

These are the choices a neighbouring stint most likely made differently. Where a peer
chose otherwise, reconcile to ONE form across the work; my reasons are given so the
merge can adjudicate rather than coin-flip.

| Latin | My English | Why |
|---|---|---|
| *cellaria* (Cant. 1:3, the whole lemma of Tomes V–VI) | **storerooms** | The author glosses *cellaria* four different ways — the books of the divine law (0022), the virtues (0023), the mansions of the Father's house (0021), the Virgin's womb (0021). A word that can hold books, virtues, mansions and a womb has to be neutral; "cellars" imports wine, "chambers" imports rooms only. Douay's "storerooms" is neutral and already English. |
| *cella vinaria* (Cant. 2:4, 0022) | **wine cellar** | Kept distinct from *cellaria* precisely because Migne prints a different word; collapsing both to "cellar" would erase the distinction the paragraph turns on. |
| *recti diligunt te* | **the upright love thee** | "Righteous" was used in an early draft of the head and corrected: *rectus* is glossed in 0021 by *recti cogitatione, recti locutione, recti operatione* — a straightness word, not a justice word (the work uses *justus* separately, e.g. *cornua justi*, 0018). |
| *ubera* (Cant. 1:3) | **breasts** | Glossed by the author as *praecepta tua* and again as *eloquia tua*; the gloss only works if the literal word stands. |
| *adolescentulae* | **maidens** | Fixed at 0019 and held through 0021, 0023. |
| *trahe me post te* | **draw me after thee** | Held identical in all seven occurrences in 0019 and the one in 0020 (the work re-quotes this lemma constantly and the exposition turns on *post te*). |
| *tomus* (as division head) | **Tome** | *Explicit tertius Tomus* / *INCIPIT QUARTUS TOMUS* / *Explicit quartus tomus*. Rendered, not carried, per Pattern 9 (structural labels normalize in English while the Latin column keeps the printed form). |
| *torcular* | **winepress** | Uniform in 0018 and 0023 (the *torcular* of Isa. 5, of Isa. 63, of Thren. 1, and the "winepress of the soul"); the whole allegory collapses if the same word varies. |
| *filius olei* / *filii olei* | **son of oil / sons of oil** | Kept literal because the argument in 0018 runs *oleum → misericordia → Deus → filii Dei*; "children of the anointing" would break every link. |
| *inquilinus / inquiline* (of Moses, 0020 and 0023) | **lodger** | Same English in both, though the case differs (nom. 0020, voc. 0023). |
| *pigmentarii* (0022) | **perfumers** | Not "apothecaries": the whole simile is odour, grinding and decanting. |
| *charitas* | **charity** | Never "love", to keep *dilectio* / *amor* separately renderable — 0023 prints *Fortis est ut mors charitas, sive dilectio*, where the author himself doubles the two words, and the doublet is unrenderable if both are "love". *dilectio* = **love**, *amor* = **love** where no contrast is in play but **love** with the Latin visible in the doublet. |
| *cornu* (Isa. 5:1 and Hab. 3:4) | **horn** | Literal throughout; the entire exposition of 0018 is that a horn exceeds the flesh it grows from. |
| *labruscae* | **wild grapes** | Standard; kept against *uvae* = **grapes**. |
| *anagoge* (head, 0023) | **anagoge** | Left as the technical term, unitalicized, matching the sense-label vocabulary of the other heads (*moralitas* → morality, *allegoria* → allegory). |

## 4. Readings that will not construe — rendered as printed, conjecture here only

Pattern 7a: none of these is repaired in the running text.

- **0019 @0541C `inferre`** — *quatenus velamen litterae Veteris Testamenti dignetur
  nobis **inferre**, ut … sensum spiritualem … valeamus reperire.* Printed *inferre*
  ("bring in upon") makes the prayer ask God to **impose** the veil so that the
  spiritual sense may be found — self-contradictory. Conjecture ***auferre*** ("take
  away"), which is what the purpose clause requires and what the same author asks in
  the parallel at 0555B. **Rendered "bring in upon us" as printed.** This is a real
  word wrongly used, not broken type, so no `[sic:]` (Pattern 12's boundary).
- **0019 @0541C, no main verb** — *Et enim velamen magnum super istam sententiam
  positum, scilicet: «…»* has no finite verb (*est* wanting). Rendered as the verbless
  clause it is.
- **0020 @0545A–B, the *esse* passage** — *sed quod ab eo creatum est, ut per ipsum
  consistat qui creavit, **necesse est per se esse, et sine alio esse, unum dignoscitur
  esse**, quod ad Deum proprie pertinet.* As printed this asserts that the **creature**
  must be *per se* — the exact opposite of the sentence that follows (*Per alium esse
  et sine alio esse non posse, creaturae proprium comprobatur esse*). Something has
  dropped, most likely a *non* or a *per ipsum* for *per se*; the corruption is not
  localizable to a single word, so no marker fires. **Rendered as printed, including
  the self-contradiction.** ⚠ This is the passage in the range most likely to be
  "fixed" by a later reader; it should not be.
- **0023 @0560B `abs re`** — *tunc profecto nunc **abs re** factum esse creditur,
  quoniam quidem quadraginta duae virtutes in serie hujus catalogi positae inveniuntur.*
  The idiom is ***non* abs re** ("not without purpose"), and the whole following
  paragraph exists to show the forty-two *was* purposeful (42 mansions, 42 generations).
  The printed text therefore says the opposite of what the author immediately proves.
  A dropped negation of exactly the Pattern 7a class. **Rendered "beside the purpose"
  as printed; the *non* is NOT supplied.**
- **0023 @0559C–D `Eam majorem`** — *Eam majorem quippe charitatem nemo habet, quam ut
  pro persecutoribus suis exoret…* Expected *Ea major* / *majorem hac* (Joan. 15:13).
  *Eam* is a real word in the wrong case; rendered literally ("no man has that greater
  charity, than that…"). No `[sic:]` per Pattern 12's boundary.
- **0022 @0553D `debuisses`** — *audita morte ejusdem inimici sui Saulis de cujus utique
  interitu **debuisses** gaudere* — a second-person singular pluperfect subjunctive in a
  clause whose subject elsewhere is David (*debuisset* expected). Rendered as printed
  with **thou** (Pattern 17: the morphology decides, not the sense).
- **0022 @0554C, number shift mid-sentence** — *o tu amator vel auditor divini verbi …
  **te oportet** … considerare et diligenti intuitu … **perpendite**.* Singular address
  then a plural imperative in one sentence. Both kept: "it behoves **thee** to consider
  … and do **ye** weigh". Not smoothed to one number.
- **0023 @0555D `fratribus vestris`** — *et cum **fratribus vestris** nullatenus
  charitativi apparemus* — "your brethren" governed by a first-person plural verb;
  *nostris* expected. Rendered as printed.
- **0022 @0554A `in montem Gelboe`** — accusative after *in* where the locative ablative
  is required (*in monte*, as the same chunk prints at 0553C). Rendered "on Mount
  Gelboe"; English has no exponent for the case error, so Pattern 9 applies — logged,
  not marked.
- **0023 @0560A `fortitudo in adversariis`** — "fortitude among adversaries" where the
  catalogue's parallel member is *temperantia in prosperis* and *in adversis* is
  expected. Construable as printed; rendered literally.
- **0023, `Ramasse` / `Ramesse`** — the same station is spelled two ways nine lines
  apart (@0561A *Ramasse*, @0561B *Ramesse*, the latter inside the Exod. 12:37
  quotation). Both kept as printed.
- **0020 @0546B `Sathaelis`** — the fallen angel named *Sathael*, not *Satanas*.
  Rendered "Sathael", not normalized.

## 7. Pattern 17 (second person) — how it was applied here

Singular addressees, all rendered **thou/thee/thy**: the *auditor* (0019 @0542C, 0020
@0544D, 0022 @0551D and @0554C, 0023 @0558B), Moses apostrophized (0020 @0544C, 0023
@0558C), the individual *conversus* (0018 @0541C), God/Christ addressed by the Bride
and the maidens throughout 0021, David's interlocutors in 0022, and every scriptural
*tu* (Osee 2, Jer. 15, Gen. 3:19, Ps. 80:11, I Reg. 24 and 26, Exod. 32, II Reg. 24,
I Reg. 16, Luc. 19:42).

Plural addressees, rendered **you/your**: Zach. 2:8 *Qui tangit **vos***, Joan. 15:4
*nisi in me **manseritis***, Jac. 5:8 *Patientes et **vos** estote*, Luc. 21:19 *In
patientia **vestra***, Matth. 25:34 *quod **vobis** paratum est*, Joan. 16:22 *Gaudium
**vestrum***, Matth. 5:44 *Diligite inimicos **vestros*** (both occurrences), II Reg.
2:5–6 *Benedicti **vos***, II Reg. 1:24 *Filiae Israel … cultui **vestro***, I Reg.
12:23 *orare pro **vobis***, Matth. 10:20 *Spiritus Patris **vestri***, and 0023
@0555D's *cum fratribus **vestris***.

The two are adjacent within single sentences more than once (0022 @0554C, above);
in every such case both were carried rather than levelled.
