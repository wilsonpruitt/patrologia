# Cruces — 11638 Philip of Harvengt, *Moralitates in Cantica canticorum*, chunks 0024–0029

PL 203, coll. 0561–0574. Agent 5 of 6. Range: the close of Book V (0024) and the
whole of Book VI (0025–0029), which expounds Cant. 1:4–5 (*Nigra sum, sed formosa*)
four times over — allegorically of the Church, of the flesh of Christ, of our nature,
then *secundum moralitatem* of the soul, and finally of the blessed Virgin.

---

## 1. Terminology decisions (for the merge — reconcile against the other five agents)

The lemma of Cant. 1:4–5 recurs some forty times in this range, in fragments. One
English form was fixed for each fragment at first occurrence and never varied:

| Migne | English |
|---|---|
| *Nigra sum, sed formosa* | "I am black, but comely" |
| *filiae Jerusalem* / *filiae Hierusalem* | "O daughters of Jerusalem" |
| *sicut tabernacula Cedar* | "as the tents of Cedar" |
| *sicut pellis Salomonis* | "as the skin of Solomon" (**singular** — see §2.1) |
| *Nolite me considerare quod fusca sum* | "Do not consider me that I am swarthy" |
| *quia decoloravit me sol* | "because the sun hath discolored me" |
| *Filii matris meae pugnaverunt contra me* | "The sons of my mother have fought against me" |
| *Posuerunt me custodem in vineis, vineam meam non custodivi* | "They have set me keeper in the vineyards, my vineyard I have not kept" |

Other fixed equivalents in this range:

- ***tomus*** (the work's own division: *Explicit quintus tomus*, *INCIPIT SEXTUS
  TOMUS*, *Explicit sextus tomus*) → **"book"**. Not "volume" and not "tome": Philip
  is numbering the parts of one work.
- ***moralitas*** → **"the morality"**, in the heads (*SECUNDUM MORALITATEM* →
  "ACCORDING TO THE MORALITY") and in the prose (*moralitatem quae in ea latet* →
  "the morality which lies hidden in it"). Chosen over "the moral sense" because it
  is the work's own title-word (*Moralitates*) and because Philip pairs it with
  *allegoria*, which is not glossed either. **Flag for merge:** if another agent has
  "moral sense", one of us must move.
- ***anagoge*** → "the anagoge" (0024 head).
- ***recapitulatio*** → "recapitulation"; *a superioribus* → "from what went before".
- ***Sponsa*** → "the Bride"; ***adolescentulae*** → "the maidens".
- ***perfidus / perfidia*** of the Jews → "faithless / faithlessness" throughout
  (never "perfidious"), matching *infidelitas* → "unbelief" as a distinct word.
- ***sol justitiae*** → "the sun of justice"; ***formositas*** → "comeliness", to keep
  it visibly cognate with *formosa*.
- ***fusca*** → "swarthy", kept distinct from *nigra* "black", because Philip glosses
  one by the other (*fusca, id est nigra appareo*, 0564B) and the gloss dies if the
  two words collapse.
- ***clarificare*** → "to make bright" / *claritas* → "brightness", kept cognate
  through the resurrection passage at 0565D–0566A, where the same root carries the
  argument from *decoloravit* to *clarificabit*.
- *Jerusalem* (0025) and *Hierusalem* (0029) are the same name in two spellings.
  English has one exponent; both render "Jerusalem". Pattern 9, no marker.

## 2. Vulgate divergences — the deliverable list

Every quotation printed in the range was read word by word against the Vulgate.
**Fifteen divergences were judged material and carry a `[var: …]`;** the rest are
recorded here without a marker.

### 2.1 Marked `[var: …]`

1. **0563A — Cant. 1:4, *sicut pellis Salomonis*** (Vulg. *sicut **pelles**
   Salomonis*, plural). **The most load-bearing divergence in the range**, and the
   reason this chunk exists: from 0563B to 0564A Philip builds an argument *out of*
   the singular. He says he can find *pellis Salomonis* written nowhere else in
   either Testament, raises it as a *quaestio magna*, and resolves it by the figure
   *a parte totum* — one **skin** standing for the whole skin-built tabernacle at
   Gabaon. A plural "curtains/skins" would leave nothing to explain. The English is
   singular everywhere the Latin is singular, including in the recapitulations at
   0565D, 0569A, 0570A, 0572B.
2. **0562C — Prov. XVII**, « Sicut igne probatur aurum, sic justos probat tribulatio
   tentationum. » Not the Vulgate's Prov. 17:3 (*quomodo igne probatur argentum et
   aurum camino, ita corda probat Dominus*) at any point: the subject, the object
   and the verb all differ. Cited as Prov. XVII notwithstanding; the reference is
   left as printed.
3. **0563C — Exod. XXV**, « Vide, inquit, ut tu facias tabernaculum secundum formam
   illius tabernaculi quod in monte vidisti. » Vulg. Exod. 25:40 is *inspice et fac
   secundum exemplar quod tibi in monte monstratum est*; Migne's wording is nearer
   Hebr. 8:5 but is not that either (*tabernaculum* is supplied twice).
4. **0563D — I Par. XVII**, *arca Dei est **in medio pellium***. Vulg. 1 Par. 17:1
   has *arca foederis Domini **sub pellibus** est*. Material because the whole
   inference — that Moses' tabernacle was made of skins — is drawn from this clause.
5. **0563D — Psal. LXVII**, « **Ascendens Christus** in altum, captivam duxit
   captivitatem. » Attributed to David, but Ps. 67:19 reads *ascendisti in altum,
   cepisti captivitatem*; the printed wording is Ephes. 4:8, with the name *Christus*
   added, which stands in neither.
6. **0564D — Act. XX**, *lupi **graves**, qui **non parcent vobis***. Vulg. Act.
   20:29: *lupi **rapaces** … non parcentes **gregi***. Two changes, and the second
   moves the object of the sparing from the flock to the hearers.
7. **0566B — Rom. V**, *per obedientiam unius **justi** constituentur multi*. Vulg.
   Rom. 5:19: *per unius oboeditionem **iusti** constituentur multi*, where *iusti*
   is the predicate of *multi* ("many shall be made **just**"). In Migne the word has
   migrated into the genitive phrase and become "of one **just man**", leaving *multi*
   with no predicate at all. Rendered as printed ("many shall be constituted"), which
   is awkward English and is meant to be: the printed clause is incomplete. **This is
   the range's purest 7a″ trap** — the familiar English ("many shall be made just")
   arrives instantly and would silently restore a word the plate does not carry.
8. **0566D — Gen. III**, « Si **de fructu vetito** comederitis… » The Vulgate's
   serpent says *in quocumque die comederitis ex eo*; "of the forbidden fruit" is not
   in Gen. 3:5 in any form.
9. **0567B — Luc. VIII**, « Sole orto aruerunt. » Luc. 8:6 reads *natum aruit*; *sole
   orto* belongs to Matt. 13:6, where the verb is *aestuaverunt*. Migne's clause is a
   fusion of the two and stands as such in neither.

Also marked, in the same class:

10. **0566A — Joan. XVII**, *claritat**e** quam habui antequam mundus fieret*. Vulg.
    17:5 has the accusative *claritatem … priusquam mundus esset **apud te***. The
    ablative changes the construction ("with the brightness") and *apud te* is gone.
11. **0565C — Joan. XII**: « Videtis quia nihil proficimus, ecce totus mundus post
    ipsum vadit. Si dimittimus eum sic, omnes credent in eum. » Sentence 1 is Joan.
    12:19 but with *totus mundus … vadit* for *mundus totus … abiit*; sentence 2 is
    Joan. 11:48, under the single citation (Joan. XII).
12. **0566B — Luc. XIII**, *relinq**e**tur* (future) for the Vulgate's present
    *relinquitur*.
13. **0570D — Thren. III**, plural *Oculi mei depraedati sunt* for the Vulgate's
    singular *oculus meus depraedatus est*. Marked because the exposition immediately
    following is plural (*infelices oculi … depraedantur et oppugnant*) and so rests
    on the printed number.
14. **0571A — Matth. XII**, ***Ex ore tuo*** *justificaberis, et ex ore tuo
    condemnaberis*. Vulg. 12:37: ***ex verbis tuis***. Material and marked because
    the entire paragraph is about the **mouth** (*os nostrum … os nostrum nos
    justificat*), and the Vulgate's "words" would leave the proof-text arguing
    something adjacent to the point.
15. **0571B — Eccli. XIII**, « Sicut qui tangit picem ab ipsa inquinatur, sic qui
    tangit uxorem proximi sui, immundus prorsus efficitur. » Only the first limb is
    Sir. 13:1; the Vulgate's second limb is *qui communicaverit superbo induet
    superbiam*. The clause about the neighbour's wife is Prov. 6:29 in substance and
    stands nowhere in Ecclesiasticus.

*(Count as filed: 0025 ×6, 0026 ×3, 0027 ×3, 0028 ×3 = 15 markers.)*

### 2.2 Divergences logged but NOT marked (minor: no change of sense)

- 0563A **Hebr. XIII** *Non habemus hic* — Vulg. *non **enim** habemus hic*.
- 0563B **Psal. XXIV; Joan. XV** *odio habuerunt me gratis* — agrees with Joan. 15:25;
  the Psalm reference (Ps. 24 for the more usual 34:19 / 68:5) is Migne's and is left
  as printed per the standing rule on references.
- 0564C **I Joan. II** *Ex nobis **exierunt*** … *Si **enim essent** ex nobis* — Vulg.

- 0565B **Luc. XXIII; Joan. XIX** *Crucifige **illum*** — Vulg. *crucifige crucifige
  eum* / *crucifige crucifige*. Pronoun and repetition only.
- 0565B **Jer. XII** *dedit contra me vocem **suam*** — *suam* is not in Jer. 12:8.
  Not marked: it is the word the exposition needs and the sense is unchanged.
- 0567D **Gen. II** *Plantaverat autem **Dominus*** — Vulg. *Dominus **Deus***.
- 0567D **Gen. II** *noli comedere* — Vulg. *ne comedas*.
- 0568B **Ezech. II** *inter scorpiones habitas* — Vulg. *cum scorpionibus habitas*.
- 0568D **Ephes. V** *Fuistis aliquando tenebrae* — Vulg. *eratis enim aliquando*.
- 0572A **Luc. II** *pertransi**bi**t gladius* — Vulg. *pertransiet*.
- 0573A **Psal. CXLVII** *Tu ergo Hierusalem, lauda Dominum* — Vulg. Ps. 147:12 *lauda

  the verse is untouched, and Philip's *id est Ecclesia gentium* attaches to the
  vocative either way.
- Throughout: the lemma prints *quod fusca **sum*** (indicative) where the Vulgate has
  *quod fusca **sim*** (subjunctive). No English exponent; not marked.
- 0567D **Gen. II** *custodiret **illud*** for the Vulgate's *illum* (*paradisum* is
  masculine). Pattern 9 gender mismatch — English "it" either way, no marker.

## 3. Defect markers fired

- **`[sic: *incrudelitatem*]` — 0566A** (`quanto magis illum a se per
  **incrudelitatem** ejecerunt`). *incrudelitas* is a hapax in this corpus (grepped:
  one occurrence, this one) and is not an attested medieval form as far as can be
  shown here. Its prefix is privative, so any rendering of it as "cruelty" would be
  the silent repair Pattern 7a forbids — translating a word as something other than
  what it means in order to rescue the sentence. **Conjecture: *crudelitatem***,
  which the parallelism (*quanto magis … tanto magis*) and the sense both want, and
  which is one minim-cluster away. Carried untranslated in italics per Pattern 7's
  non-word class.
- **`[sic: *neam*]` — 0571B** (`vineam **neam** non custodivi`). Plainly for *meam*.
  The printed lemma appears **twice within eleven lines**, correctly as *vineam meam*
  at 0571C, which is itself the proof that the first is compositor's type and not the
  author's. Only the broken word is inside the marker; *vineam* is a real word and is
  translated ("the vineyard"), so the English reads *…in the vineyards, the vineyard*
  [sic: *neam*] *I have not kept.* — deliberately odd, because the plate is.

No `[d: …]` (no dittography found) and no `[ed: …]` (nothing lost in the
digitization) in this range.

## 4. Guillemet defects in the plate — reproduced, not repaired (Pattern 6 / Pattern 5 corollary)

Three unbalanced runs. All are Migne's, all are mirrored exactly, and each is why
this range's guillemet counts are odd in the verifier's output.

1. **0565A (chunk 0025) — an opening « never closed.** *« Ite, docete omnes gentes
   [n: (Matth. XXVIII)]. Sed tunc vineam suam non custodierunt…* runs on into
   Philip's own narrative and to the end of the chunk without a closing mark. Latin
   «11 »10; English «11 »10. Per the Pattern 5 corollary the missing » is **not**
   supplied.
2. **0568D (chunk 0028) — a closing » with nothing to close.** *« Fuistis aliquando
   tenebrae, » id est peccatores; « nunc autem lux, » id est justi, in Domino [n:
   (Ephes. V)]. **»*** — two openings, three closings. The stray third mark is
   reproduced where it stands. Latin «12 »13; English «12 »13.
3. **0572D (chunk 0029) — a closing » with no opening.** *Exsulta et lauda, habitatio
   Sion [n: (Isa. XII)], **»** id est Ecclesia de gentibus congregata…* The Isaias
   quotation is printed unopened. Latin «3 »4; English «3 »4.

Also worth recording, because it looks like an error and is not:

- **0569A–0569C (chunk 0028): a guillemet run that swallows the commentary.** Migne
  opens at *« Tabernaculum fuit constructum de pellibus rubricatis…* and does not
  close until *…virga Aaron quae fronduit [n: (III Reg. VIII; Hebr. IX)]. »* — some
  270 words later. Everything between (the exposition of the red skins as martyrs,
  the hyacinth skins as confessors, the haircloths as sinners, the ark, the four
  Gospels, the two bars) is Philip's own prose, inside Migne's quotation marks. The
  run is reproduced 1:1; nothing is re-punctuated.

**Pattern 5 instances** (an attribution verb inside the quotation, translated in place
and left inside): 0563C *« Vide, **inquit**, ut tu facias… »*; 0566B *« Vinea autem
Domini exercituum, **sicut dicit Isaias**, domus Israel est. »*; 0571A *« Ex ore tuo,
**inquit**, justificaberis… »*. None was broken around an English speech tag.

## 5. Other cruces

- **0566A, *quia et ipsa … originem duxit*.** *ipsa* is feminine, and in the sentence
  *Mater Christi, secundum carnem, Synagoga fuit, quia et ipsa per patriarchas et
  reges, et per beatam Mariam originem duxit* the natural antecedent by sense is
  Christ (masculine) — but the whole recapitulation is spoken by the ***caro
  Christi***, which is feminine and is the speaker of every verb around it. Rendered
  "because **it** too drew its origin", carrying the feminine referent as the flesh.
  No emendation; flagged here because a reader may expect "he".
- **0570C, *sicut **materia** filiis suis sustentatur et adjuvatur*.** Rendered
  literally, "as **matter** is sustained and helped by its own sons." **Conjecture:
  *mater*** — the comparison is of the flesh governed by its five senses to a mother
  sustained by her sons, and *materia* makes the simile say nothing. *materia* is a
  real word and construes grammatically, so under the Pattern 12 boundary it takes no
  `[sic: …]`: the English word on the page is ours, and the conjecture lives here.
- **0570D, *Dumque **eum** exterioribus delectationibus inficit … **illam** debilitat
  et occidit*.** The masculine *eum* has no clean antecedent (the nearest nouns are
  *odoratus*, masculine, which is the subject, and *animam*, feminine, which is
  *illam*). Rendered as printed — "while it infects **him** … it weakens and kills
  **her**" — with the shift of gender left visible. Possibly *eam* in the plate's
  copy-text; not emended.
- **0562C, *aurum clarissimum, id est **obrysum***.** *obrysum* is an attested
  spelling of *obryzum* (refined gold; the corpus has *obryzo* twice at 11703).
  Pattern 9: translated normally, "the brightest gold, that is, refined gold." No
  italics, no marker.
- **0563C, *excelsum majus*.** Rendered "the greater high place", kept identical at
  its second occurrence (*Quid vero est illud excelsum majus*) because the second
  passage glosses the first.
- **0567A, *Dicat ergo natura nostra**;*** — the plate prints a semicolon where a
  colon is expected, before direct speech. Preserved (Pattern 8); the English reads
  "Let our nature then say; Through the first Adam…".
- **0565D (chunk 0026), *Per Cedar ergo qui tenebrae interpretatur, tenebras mortis***
  — the first limb has no finite verb; it borrows *debemus intelligere* from the
  second limb. Supplied in brackets, "[we ought to understand]", per the corpus's
  existing convention for supplied English words.
- **0569C (chunk 0028), *quae per duos vectes figuraliter, spiritualiter
  intellexerit*** — two adverbs and one verb, and *figuraliter* has no verb of its
  own. Rendered "which [are set forth] figuratively by the two bars", with the
  supplied verb bracketed.
- **Column sequence at the end of 0029: [0573A] → [0574A] → [0573].** Reproduced
  verbatim and in order. The jump to 0574A and the bare [0573] on the *Explicit* line
  are what the source carries; per the standing false-positive rule on column-band
  arithmetic, no correction is proposed.
- **0567 (chunk 0027) ends with two heads and no text**: *RECAPITULATIO A
  SUPERIORIBUS* followed by *SECUNDUM MORALITATEM.*, then the bare anchor [0568A].
  This is the chunk boundary — the body of the section is chunk 0028, whose head
  repeats as *SECUNDUM MORALITATEM. (cont.)*. Both heads are translated and both
  blank lines kept; not a defect.

## 6. Second person (Pattern 17)

Singular addressees take *thou/thee/thy*, plural *you/your*, decided by the Latin
morphology alone:

- **thou** — Exod. 25:40 to Moses (*ut **tu** facias … vidisti*, 0563C); David to
  Nathan (*Videsne*, 0563D); Pilate (*Si hunc dimittis, non es amicus Caesaris*,
  0565C); Ps. 120:6 (*sol non uret **te***, 0567B) and Philip's gloss on it (*tentatio
  nulla decipiet te*); the reader addressed directly at 0568C–D (*Sed et **tu**
  quicunque … scito **te** habitare*); Matt. 12:37 (*Ex ore **tuo***, 0571A); Simeon
  to Mary (*tuam ipsius animam*, 0572A) and the *quasi diceret* gloss expanding it;
  the Church of the Gentiles addressed from 0572D to 0574A (*custodiris … Tu es enim
  … tibi … hostes tuos*), including Ps. 147:12 inside it.
- **you** — the daughters of Jerusalem throughout (*Nolite me considerare*, *O vos
  filiae Jerusalem*); the serpent to Adam and Eve (Gen. 3:5, *comederitis … oculi
  vestri … eritis*, 0566D); Paul to the Ephesians (0568D) and to the Jews at Antioch
  (Act. 13:46, 0564D–0565A); Act. 20:29 (*in **vos** … parcent **vobis***); the Jews
  addressed by Mary at 0572C (*o vos Judaei … quem vos interfecistis*); Luc. 13:35
  (*relinquetur **vobis** domus **vestra***).

No case in the range required a judgment call: every second person is morphologically
unambiguous.
