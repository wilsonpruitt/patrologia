## 3. ⛔ The Vulgate divergence list — the deliverable

Every printed quotation in 0030–0034 was read word by word against the Vulgate. **17 `[var:]`
markers fired**; the divergences that were logged but left unmarked are listed after.

### Marked `[var:]` — material divergences

**0030 (2)**
1. **0573C, Coloss. II, 9** — Migne: *in ipso omnis plenitudo divinitatis inhabitat*. Vulg.: *in ipso inhabitat omnis plenitudo divinitatis **corporaliter***. The word order is inverted and *corporaliter* is absent. Marked because the missing word is the one Col 2:9 is normally adduced for.
2. **0573D, Joan. I, 14** — Migne: *Vidimus **eum** quasi **unigenitum** a Patre*. Vulg.: *et vidimus **gloriam ejus, gloriam** quasi **unigeniti** a Patre*. Migne's object is Christ himself, not his glory, and *unigenitum* is accusative to match. Philip's argument (the topaz "shines with every colour," i.e. Christ is adorned with every virtue) rests on seeing **him**, so the divergence is load-bearing.
3. **0575A, Psal. VIII, 6** — Migne adds a vocative *Domine* the Vulgate does not print. Marked because the added vocative turns a description into an address.

**0031 (6)**
4. **0575A–B, the lemma itself** — the chapter head and the first re-quotation print Cant. 1:6, *ne vagari incipiam **post** greges sodalium tuorum*; the **second** re-quotation, twenty words later, prints ***Ne forte** vagari incipiam **per** greges sodalium tuorum*. ⚠ This is the range's most important reading: the author re-quotes his own lemma with a different preposition and an added *forte*, and then expounds *vagari*. Both forms are rendered as printed and neither has been conformed to the other.
5. **0575B, Matth. XVI, 13–14** — Migne: *Quem dicunt homines esse **hominis Filium**? Responderunt discipuli: **Alii Eliam**, alii Jeremiam…*. Vulg.: *…esse Filium hominis? At illi dixerunt: Alii **Joannem Baptistam**, alii autem Eliam, alii vero Jeremiam…*. John the Baptist is dropped from the list of mistaken identifications — and the list is precisely what the topaz/chrysolite exposition is counting.
6. **0576A, Joan. XVII, 21** — Migne: *Pater, **volo ut** sicut tu es in me…*. Vulg.: *ut omnes unum sint, sicut tu, Pater, in me, et ego in te…*. Recast as a first-person volition.
7. **0576A, Joan. XIV, 9–10** — Migne: *videt et Patrem **meum**, **non credis** quia ego in Patre et Pater est in me* **[full stop, not a question]**. Vulg.: *videt et Patrem… Non **creditis** quia ego in Patre, et Pater in me est?* Three divergences at once: *meum* added, plural → singular, question → statement. Punctuation follows the plate (Pattern 8): the English is a statement.
8. **0576B, Isa. XVIII, 4** — Migne: *Quiescam **in loco meo**, et considerabo quasi meridiana lux*. Vulg.: *Quiescam, et considerabo **in loco meo**, sicut meridiana lux **clara est***. Migne's word order attaches *in loco meo* to *quiescam*, and the next four sentences of commentary interrogate exactly that phrase (*Quid est ergo quod dicit in loco meo quiescam?*). Conforming to the Vulgate here would have destroyed the exposition.
9. **0576B, Isa. XXVIII, 12** — Migne: *Haec est requies mea, **refice lapsum***. Vulg.: *Haec est requies mea… **reficite lassum***. Singular imperative for plural, and *lapsum* "the fallen" for *lassum* "the weary." The very next sentence glosses *peccatorem **lapsum** … exhortamur* — the commentary is built on *lapsum*, and the familiar "refresh the weary" would have left it unsupported. This is the range's clearest 7a″ trap.
10. **0576C, Isa. LXVI, 2** — Migne: *Super quem **requiescam**, nisi super **humilem et quietum**, et trementem **verba mea**?* Vulg.: *ad quem autem **respiciam**, nisi ad **pauperculum et contritum spiritu**, et trementem **sermones meos**?* An Old-Latin/LXX-shaped form (LXX ἐπὶ τὸν ταπεινὸν καὶ ἡσύχιον). It is adduced under the heading *Quae est ergo requies Dei* — the divergent verb *requiescam* is the whole reason the verse is here.
11. **0576D–0577A, II Cor. V, 10** — Migne: *ut **recipiat** unusquisque prout gessit*. Vulg.: *ut **referat** unusquisque **propria corporis**, prout gessit*.

**0032 (5)**
12. **0578D, Isa. IX, 2** — Migne: *Populus **gentium** qui ambulabat in tenebris*. Vulg. has no *gentium*; the commentary immediately reads it as *populis gentium*.
13. **0579A, Ephes. III, 17** — Migne: *in cordibus **nostris***. Vulg.: *in cordibus **vestris***. A person change, and the gloss that follows continues in the first person plural.
14. **0579C, Ezech. XVIII, 23** — Migne: *Nolo mortem **peccatoris***. Vulg.: *nolo mortem **impii***; and the whole passage is about the *peccator*.
15. **0579D, "Ezech." (*ibid.*)** — *Peccator quacunque hora ingemuerit, salvus erit* **is not in the Vulgate at all**, at Ezekiel or elsewhere; it is the medieval adage drawn from Ezech. XXXIII, 12. Marked, because a reader who checks the citation will find nothing there.
16. **0580A, Heb. VI, 7** — Migne: *Terra bibens desuper venientem imbrem, et fructificans **opportunam**, ab his, a quibus colitur, accipit benedictionem*. Vulg.: *Terra enim saepe venientem super se bibens imbrem, et generans **herbam** opportunam illis a quibus colitur, accipit benedictionem **a Deo***. Note *opportunam* is left with no noun to agree with — rendered "what is seasonable," not silently supplied with "herb."
17. **0580C, I Joan. II, 15** — Migne prefixes *Filioli* (from I Joan. II, 12/18) and prints *si quis **dilexerit** mundum* for Vulg. *si quis **diligit** mundum*.

**0033 (5)**
18. **0580D, Gen. III, 19** — Migne: *« **Terra** es, et in **terram** ibis. »* Vulg.: *pulvis es, et in pulverem reverteris*. ⚠ **The single most important divergence in the range.** The entire second half of the seventh book is an exposition of *terra* — "we ourselves are earth," the earth given to the serpent for food, the earth that yields her fruit. On the received Vulgate text the argument has no proof-text at all. A conformed "dust thou art" would have been smooth, correctly cited, verifier-clean, and would have destroyed the chapter.
19. **0581A, Eccle. VII, 30** — Migne: *Deus fecit hominem **simplicem et** rectum*. Vulg.: *quod fecerit Deus hominem rectum*. The gloss immediately splits the two words (*simplicem, ut malum nunquam faceret, rectum autem, ut…*), so *simplicem* cannot be dropped.
20. **0581C, Joan. XVII, 3** — Migne: *ut cognoscant te, et quem misisti **Filium tuum unum et verum Deum esse***. Vulg.: *ut cognoscant te **solum Deum verum**, et quem misisti **Jesum Christum***. A doctrinally loaded recasting: Migne's form predicates "the one and true God" of the Son. Rendered as printed.
21. **0582A–B, Psal. CI, 26** — Migne: *Et tu **in principio**, Domine, terram fundasti*. Vulg.: *Initio tu, Domine, terram fundasti*. The next sentence turns on it: *Filium autem Dei Propheta hic **principium** appellat*.
22. **0582C, Isa. XXVIII, 21** — Migne: *Opus ejus alienum fuit ab eo, ut faceret opus suum, prius fecit opus alienum*. Vulg.: *ut faciat opus suum, alienum opus ejus: ut operetur opus suum, peregrinum est opus ejus ab eo*. Philip himself calls the words *valde obscura* and says they cannot easily be found in the other prophets — which is a fair description of a text that is not the Vulgate's.

**0034 (1)**
23. **0584B–C, Psal. XLIV, 11** — Migne: ***Intellige**, filia, et vide, et inclina aurem tuam*. Vulg.: *Audi, filia*. Woven, unguilleted, into the Father's speech; rendered "Understand, O daughter."

## 4. Textual cruces — printed readings rendered literally

1. **0575D (0031), *quamvis*.** *Tu me deprecaris **quamvis** remota omni ambiguitate te certificem ubi…* — *quamvis* ("although") will not construe with the *ut*-sense the sentence needs; *ut* is the obvious conjecture. Rendered "that, although with all ambiguity removed I should assure thee where…", i.e. as printed. No emendation.
2. **0575D (0031), *pascor* for *pascam*.** Christ's answer prints the **passive** *in meridie **pascor** et accubo* where the lemma, the question, and the parallel answer in 0032 (*in meridie **pasco** et accubo*, col. 0578A–B) are all active. Rendered "at midday I am fed and lie down" in 0031 and "at midday I feed and lie down" in 0032 — the two are **deliberately different** because the plate is. Do not harmonize them at merge.
3. **0576B (0031), *aurae*.** *quare se comparat meridianae luci plus quam **aurae** vel alteri horae diei?* — in a list of hours of the day, *aurorae* ("dawn") is the near-certain intention; *aura* ("breeze") is a real word and is rendered as printed ("the breeze"), per Pattern 7 and the *requiescere faciam* boundary. No marker (a real word merely wrong is not `[sic:]` matter).
4. **0578A (0032), *Respondeat ergo justum est Christus*.** An unconstruable run: an impersonal *justum est* dropped inside the jussive. Rendered "Let Christ therefore answer — it is just — to holy Church"; conjecture *Respondeat ergo (justum est) Christus* or *Respondeat ergo ut justum est*.
5. **0578D–0579A (0032), *et sicut coeperunt*.** *…in cordibus eorum est plantata et radicata, **et sicut** coeperunt in se Christum habere habitatorem* — *sic* is expected. Rendered "and as they began to have Christ as an indweller."
6. **0575C (0031), *eam* for *cultum*.** *cultum solius divinitatis ab omnibus sanctis removeat, et **eam** soli Deo … attribuat* — feminine pronoun for a masculine antecedent (*adorationem* understood). Pattern 9: gender has no English exponent, "attributes it"; no marker, no churn at merge.
7. **0581A (0033), *postquam* for *per quam*.** *rectitudinem mentis quam a Deo acceperat, **et postquam** ipsi soli placere debuerat … intorsit* — *per quam* is the sense required. Rendered "and after which he ought to have pleased him alone," as printed. The whole period is anacoluthic (the *quia* clause never reaches its verb before *juxta Dei dispensationem actum est* takes over) and is rendered as printed, including the shift to *possit* at the end.
8. **0583C (0033), *insensibile spiraculum vitae*.** Rendered "the insensible breath of life." Odd of Gen. 2:7; possibly *insensibiliter* or *insensibili*. Rendered as printed, no marker.
9. **0583D–0584A (0033), *redemit* without an object.** *Filius ejus Dominus Jesus Christus sanguine suo redemit* — the object is supplied in square brackets, "redeemed [thee]," on the ~95-instance corpus precedent for supplied English words.
10. **0584D (0034), *refertur*.** *Daniel propheta … memoria abundantiae suavitatis Spiritus sancti **refertur** … Christum montem interpretatur* — *refertur* cannot be "is reported" here, because the sentence's finite verb is *interpretatur*; read as *refertus (est)* / *refercitur*, "is filled." Rendered "is filled with the memory of the abundance of the sweetness of the Holy Spirit." Conjecture logged, no emendation and no marker (a real word merely wrong).

## 5. ⚠ The unclosed guillemet at 0579D (0032) — mirrored, not repaired

Migne prints:

> *Moyses namque dicit:* **«** *Concrescat ut pluvia doctrina mea, et fluat ut ros eloquium meum* [n: (Deut. XXXII)]. *Isaias quoque ait ad Dominum:* **«** *Quia ros lucis, ros tuus* [n: (Isa. XXVI)]. **»**

The Moses quotation is **opened and never closed**, and the sentence attributing the next
quotation to Isaiah is swallowed inside it. Per Pattern 5's corollary the missing `»` is
**not supplied**: the English opens where Migne opens and closes where Migne closes, so
chunk 0032 carries 15 `«` against 14 `»` in both twins, exactly as the plate does.
`verify-english.mjs` check 7 passes on this because it counts against the Latin twin, not
against balance. **Do not "fix" this at merge.**
