## §1 · THE STRUCTURE OF THE BOOK, AND THE TYPOGRAPHY SEAM

⭐ **This work changes how it marks scripture partway through, and the change is
a BOOK BOUNDARY, not a printer's whim.** Books I–II mark quotations with
*italics*; Book III onward marks them with guillemets « ». The switch falls
between chunks 0013 and 0014, at cols 0523/0523B — exactly at
*Explicit secundus tomus* / *INCIPIT TERTIUS TOMUS*. Corpus-wide counts: 316
italic runs, 286 guillemet runs.

**Do not normalize the seam in either direction.** Under Pattern 6 a « » is a
mark Migne PRINTS and is reproduced 1:1; a `" "` is ours. Two things look like
exceptions and are not:
- 0013's single guillemet pair is the ***Sub tuam protectionem* antiphon** — a
  liturgical class Migne guillemets even inside the italic books.
- 0017's single italic run is a printed **locator** (*Exod. [0533D] XXVII*), not
  scripture.

After the seam italics survive **by function**: words discussed as words
(*Eleos*, *Oleos*, *misericordia*), Migne's marginal source-edition page numbers
(*303*–*313*), and a lemma nested inside a guillemet quotation.

**Guillemet asymmetries are Migne's and are mirrored, never balanced** (Pattern 5
corollary): unclosed « at 0532C–D, 0565A, 0579D; stray » at 0568D; unopened » at
0572D and 0557C. A 270-word run at 0569A–C swallows Philip's own commentary
inside Migne's quotation marks; reproduced as printed.

## §2 · WHAT THE MERGE CHANGED

**1. `tomus` = "Tome"; `liber` = "book". Ruled here, swept 16 places.**
The seven divisions of the work are *tomi*; the Song itself is the *liber*.
Five agents collapsed both to "book"; one used "Tome"; that one was right, and
was itself inconsistent (`Tome` at 0019, `tome` at 0020).
⚑ **The decisive text is 0019 @0541C: *Ad quartum tomum exponendum **hujus
libri** accedentes*.** Rendered with one English word that becomes "the fourth
book of this book". Migne's own gloss at 0005 settles the sense —
*Explicit primus **tomus**, id est primum **volumen*** — a division of a work,
not the work. Capitalized as a locator per Pattern 2.
Also normalized in the same sweep: *INCIPIT* = "HERE BEGINS", *EXPLICIT* = "Here
ends" (one agent had "BEGINNETH"/"endeth").

**2. Pattern 3, "Holy Scripture" — 11 instances swept across FOUR agents' ranges.**
The English carried 6 × "divine Scripture", 3 × "divine Scriptures", 2 × "holy
Scripture" against 17 Latin instances of *divina/sacrae/divinae Scriptura(e)*.
Pattern 3 fixes the standing name as **Holy Scripture**, both words capitalized,
and says explicitly that a capital S alone is not enough. Chunks touched: 0000,
0001, 0002, 0003, 0004, 0014, 0023, 0025, 0028.

**3. `candidatio` — translated, not carried.** *Libanus candidatio interpretatur*
occurs at 0005 and 0009 in the same etymological construction; one agent gave
"*a whitening*", the other kept "*candidatio*". **Translated**, because both
passages argue from whiteness → purity → virginity, and the Latin left standing
makes the inference unavailable to an English reader. The register rule reserves
italic Latin for technical words with no clean English equivalent; *candidatio*
is an ordinary noun and has one.

## §3 · WHAT THE MERGE RATIFIED — no edit needed, do not churn

**Migne's lower-case after a misplaced full stop STANDS.** Eight instances
(0003, 0006, 0011 ×2, 0012 ×2, 0014, 0015), and **all three agents who met the
construction independently preserved it** — there was no drift, only a request
to have it ruled.
Reasoning, since a later sweep will be tempted: Pattern 9 normalizes only where
English has **no exponent** for the Latin defect (grammatical gender, a typo'd
section label). Lowercase-after-a-stop *is* expressible in English and expresses
exactly what the plate shows, so Pattern 8 governs and both the mark and its
consequence stand. Capitalizing would make the passage read as a deliberate
sentence fragment — a worse misrepresentation than the lowercase.

**Terminology checked across all 35 chunks and found already consistent:**
- The Song's colour triple, spanning two agents and 60 instances:
  *nigra* = **black** (28/28) · *fusca* = **swarthy** (9/9) · *formosa* =
  **comely** (23/24). Kept strictly distinct; *fusca* is never "black".
- *charitas* = **charity**, never "love" — verified: no chunk under-renders it.
  0023 prints the doublet *charitas, sive dilectio*, which forces the split.
- *clementia* = **clemency** kept distinct from *misericordia* = **mercy**
  (28/25 and ~58/54).
- *moralitas* = **the morality** · *cellaria* = **storerooms** (vs *cella
  vinaria* = wine cellar) · *recti* = **the upright** · *adolescentulae* =
  **maidens** · *torcular* = **winepress** · *anaglypta* and *scalio* kept
  italic Latin.

**Pattern 17** (thou = singular, you = plural; a number marker, not a reverence
marker) was ruled the same day as this translation and carried in every agent's
brief. 0504A is the worked case — one sentence carrying *peccata **nostra*** and
*inter **vos*** together.

## §4 · FINDINGS NO SINGLE AGENT COULD HAVE MADE

These are the merge's own results: a divergence attested in **more than one
agent's range** is evidence about Philip's copy of the Bible, not a local slip.

⭐ **Zech. 4:14 — *duo filii olei SPLENDORIS* — four chunks, three agents.**
0008, 0015 (×2), 0016, 0018. The Vulgate reads *duo filii olei*; Migne adds
*splendoris* every single time. **And Philip knows it**: at 0015 @0528C he says
so outright — *in Isaia simpliciter oleum appellatur, in Zacharia vero cum
additamento, splendoris oleum nominatur* — and builds a Trinitarian exposition on
the addition (Father as oil, Son as splendour, *non tamen tres splendores sunt,
sed splendor unus*). A lemma conformed to the Vulgate here would void a whole
chapter, and the consistency across three agents shows the reading is his text,
not a compositor's slip on one page.

⭐ **Gen. 3:19 — *Terra es et in terram ibis* — two chunks, two agents.**
0020 and 0033, guillemeted both times, against the Vulgate's *pulvis es et in
pulverem reverteris*. The second half of the work expounds *terra* (we are earth;
the earth given the serpent for food; the earth that yields her fruit). On the
Vulgate text those chapters would have no proof-text at all.

**Cant. 1:4 — *sicut pellis Salomonis*, singular** (Vulg. *pelles*). Philip
raises the singular himself as a *quaestio magna*, says it stands nowhere else in
either Testament, and resolves it *a parte totum*. Kept singular in all five
recapitulations.

**The running lemma is re-quoted against itself and both readings stand**: the
head of Tome VII prints *ne vagari incipiam **post** greges*, and 0575B re-quotes
it as ***per** greges*. Neither was conformed to the other — the within-work
merge rule reconciles *our* vocabulary, never Migne's printed text.

## §5 · POLARITY — rendered as printed, NOT repaired

The class this edition exists to expose. Each was rendered literally and its
conjecture kept out of the running text.

- **0498A, Prov. 16:5** — Migne prints *Qui exaltat cor suum, **mundus** est
  coram Deo*; the received text has *immundus*. Philip's paragraph argues the
  proud virgin is **unclean** (Eve *corpore virgo… sed mente immunda*). Rendered
  "is clean before God". The 11536/8625 class exactly.
- **0560B, `abs re`** — the idiom is *non abs re*; the printed text asserts the
  opposite of what the next paragraph proves. Negation **not** supplied.
- **0507A, `munitum` for `minutum`** (×2) — rendered "fortified", which
  contradicts the argument it sits in. That contradiction is the plate's.
- **0566B, Rom. 5:19** — *per obedientiam unius **justi** constituentur multi*
  moves the Vulgate's predicate into the genitive and leaves *multi* with nothing
  to be made. Rendered awkwardly and deliberately.
- **0545A–B** — the *esse* passage asserts the creature must be *per se* and the
  next sentence denies it. Corruption not localizable to one word, so no marker
  fired. ⚠ **Flagged by its agent as the passage in the work most likely to be
  wrongly "fixed" later.**

⭐ **One silent repair was caught in draft and reverted**, which is what the rule
is for: at 0513C the non-word *abur* had first been rendered "the ivory" —
invisible, because the sentence is about ivory. It is now carried as
`[sic: *abur*]`.

## §7 · METHOD DATUM — naming the words worked

Every agent was handed a verbatim list of **every quotation printed in its own
range**, extracted mechanically from the Latin (602 in all), with the instruction
that the divergence list was the most valuable thing it would produce. This is
the 8930 finding applied deliberately rather than hoped for.

Result: **139 `[var:]` markers across 35 chunks**, plus roughly 120 further
quotations collated and *explicitly logged as unmarked* in the range files — so
the absence of a marker is recorded evidence rather than silence. Compare 8930,
where the range given a strong but generic "read every lemma carefully" produced
1 marker and a later blind read found seven defects in it.

The blind read will test that. Until it runs, this number is an input, not a
result.
