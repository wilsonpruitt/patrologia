## Annals



## 96.

[0987B]The passion of Saint Dionysius. Saint Sanctinus, making his way to Rome with Antoninus after the martyrdom of blessed Dionysius, by that same man's command, was instructed by the visitation of an angel and turned aside to the city of the Clavi, which is called [0987C] Verdun; preaching the word of God to the citizens, he converted many, and, building and consecrating a church in honor of blessed Peter, he was chosen first bishop by those very Christians whom he had converted.

## 360.

In these times, after the devastation by the Huns, the churches flourished again, and Pulchronius, a disciple of Lupus bishop of Troyes, is set over the church of Verdun as its 5th bishop.

## 503.

The passing of Saint Firminus, bishop of Verdun. In whose place blessed Vitonus is appointed by King Clovis, and is enthroned in the pontifical see.

## 518.

The annunciation of Saint Agericus.

## 528.

The passing of blessed Vitonus, bishop of Verdun.

## 554.

The ordination of Saint Agericus to the episcopate.

## 602.

Saint Agericus died; Caramerus succeeds. [0987D]

## 688.

Pepin, son of Ansegisus, began to reign. From whose times blessed Madelveus continued down to the times of Charlemagne.

This Pepin, duke and illustrious prince of the Franks, together with his wife, the illustrious matron Plectrude, gave to the church of Saint Vitonus the estate of Parida with its appurtenances, together with the spacious wood which is called the Nauca of Saint Vitonus, in the seventh year of King Childebert.

## 758.

[0988B]Saint Madelveus, bishop of Verdun, died.

## 771.

Peter is installed as bishop of Verdun.

## 795.

Peter, bishop of Verdun, died; [0988C] Austrannus succeeds.

## 800.

Austrannus, bishop of Verdun, died; Herilandus succeeds.

## 847.

Hildinus, bishop of Verdun, died; Hatto succeeds.

## 915.

The church of Saint Vitonus of Verdun was burned, [sic: *liba*]: the memorials of the saints.

## 920.

Dado, bishop of Verdun, died; Bernoynus succeeds.

## 941.

Bernoinus, bishop of Verdun, died; Berengar succeeds, a kinsman of the emperor Otto.

## 952.

The clerics are driven out of the monastery of Saint Vitonus, and monks are put in their place under Abbot Hubert. [0988D]

## 958.

Berengar, bishop of Verdun, died; Wicfrid succeeds, who founded the church of Saint Paul of Verdun.

## 986.

Wicfrid, bishop of Verdun, died; Adalbero succeeds.

## 990.

Adalbero, bishop of Verdun, died; Heymo succeeds, who founded the monastery of the nuns of Saint Maurus. [0989A]

## 1005.

Fingenius, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died: Richard succeeds.

## 1025.

Heymo, bishop of Verdun, died; Raymbert succeeds, who founded the monastery of Saint Agericus.

## 1039.

Raymbert, bishop of Verdun, died; Richard succeeds.

## 1046.

Richard, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; Waleran succeeds. Richard, bishop of Verdun, died; Theoderic succeeds.

## 1048.

The city of Verdun is taken by Duke Godfrey and by Baldwin, is plundered, and is burned together with the monastery of Saint Mary.

## 1049.

Blessed Odilo, abbot of Cluny, died. The church of blessed Mary Magdalene was dedicated by [0989B] Pope Leo IX.

## 1056.

The emperor Henry died; Henry the boy succeeds.

## 1060.

Abbot Waleran died; Grimold succeeds.

## 1064.

The chapel of Saint Bartholomew, with its forecourt, is dedicated. And the star which is called a comet appeared; in which year also Harold, king of the English, is slain, William succeeding him.

## 1069.

Duke Godfrey, son of Duke Gozelo, died; his son Godfrey, called the Hunchback, succeeds.

## 1071.

Ermenfrid died, canon and builder of the church of blessed Mary Magdalene.

## 1075.

Duke Rudolf invaded Saxony. [0989C]

## 1076.

Godfrey the younger, duke, died; Godfrey who is called of Bouillon succeeds.

## 1077.

Rudolf succeeds Abbot Grimold. A hard frost, continuous for 4 months.

## 1078.

Abbot Grimold died.

## 1079.

A battle in Saxony, where Duke Rudolf is slain by Henry the younger.

## 1080.

The body of the lord bishop Richard was translated, and was buried before the choir in the new basilica of Saint Mary.

## 1082.

A great strife between the kingdom and the priesthood. On account of which the monks were expelled from the monastery of Saint Vitonus for seven years, namely until after the death of Theoderic, bishop of Verdun

## 1086.

A battle at Sathanacum. [0989D]

## 1087.

William, duke of the Normans and king of the English, died. Pope Victor. The translation of Saint Nicholas to Bari.

## 1088.

Theoderic, bishop of Verdun, died; Richer succeeds, and Pope Urban.

## 1091.

Herimann, bishop of Metz, died.

## 1095.

The consecration of Bishop Richer. A council of the bishops of almost the whole world, except the Lotharingians, the Germans, and the Bavarians, was assembled at Clermont in Auvergne.

## 1097.

The star which is called a comet appeared.

## 1098.

Pope Urban died; Pope Paschal succeeds. [0990A]

## 1099.

Abbot Rudolf died; Laurence succeeds. Jerusalem is taken by Duke Godfrey of Bouillon and by the Christians.

## 1106.

The emperor Henry the father having died, Henry succeeds.

## 1107.

Bishop Richer died; whom Richard, the bishop-elect, succeeds.

## 1111.

The monastery of Saint Vitonus deserted, the monks having been expelled by Bishop Richard. Henry, son of Henry the said king, coming to Rome as though to give satisfaction to Pope Paschal, seized him with the cardinals within the church of blessed Peter by treachery, and thrust him into a filthy place.

## 1114.

The monks of Saint Vitonus return to the monastery after the death of Bishop Richard. The [0990B] bishopric was vacant for three and a half years.

## 1116.

Henry succeeds as bishop of Verdun.

## 1117.

A battle before Verdun of the counts Raynald and William. Pope Paschal died; Gelasius succeeded.

## 1118.

Baldwin, king of Jerusalem, died.

## 1119.

Pope Gelasius died; Calixtus the Second succeeds.

## 1120.

The city of Verdun is taken by Count Raynald, and the greatest part of the city, with the monasteries of Saint Saviour, of Saint Peter, and of Saint Agericus, is burned and despoiled.

## 1122.

The citizens of Verdun handed over this city by treachery to Count Henry of Grandpré; and he, having held it for some time, committed many [0990C] evils there.

## 1124.

Concord was made between the kingdom and the priesthood.

## 1125.

Pope Calixtus died; Honorius succeeds. Henry the Fifth, emperor, died; Lothar, duke of Saxony, succeeds.

## 1129.

Henry gave back the bishopric. Ursio, abbot of Saint Denis of Reims, is elected.

## 1130.

Pope Honorius died; Innocent succeeds.

## 1131.

Ursio renounced the bishopric, and Adalbero the archdeacon is elected and is consecrated at Paris on holy Easter day by Pope Innocent.

## 1132.

The tower of Verdun is taken and overthrown.

## 1138.

The emperor Lothar died; whom [0990D] Conrad succeeds.

## 1139.

Laurence, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; Segard succeeded.

## 1142.

Abbot Segard died; whom Cono succeeded.

## 1143.

Pope Innocent died; Celestine succeeds.

## 1144.

Pope Celestine died; Lucius succeeded.

## 1145.

Pope Lucius died; Eugenius the Third succeeds.

## 1147.

Pope Eugenius, on the day of his feast, transferred the body of the most blessed Father Vitonus into a new bier, [0991A] and on the day of the feast of Saint Martin dedicated the church of blessed Mary.

## 1150.

Pope Eugenius died; Anastasius succeeds, and afterward Adrian. The monastery of Saint Mary Magdalene and the greatest part of the city were burned.

## 1152.

Conrad, king of the Romans, died; Frederick, duke of Alsace, succeeds.

## 1153.

A battle before Mouzon, where the men of Metz perished. Ascalon taken by the Christians. Frederick, king of the Lotharingians, set out for Rome to enlarge the empire.

## 1156.

The ordination of Bishop Albert, two years before the death of Adalbero.

## 1158.

Adalbero, bishop of Verdun, died.

## 1161.

Pope Adrian died; Alexander [0991B] III succeeds.

## 1163.

Albert, bishop of Verdun and monk of Saint Vitonus, died; Richard called the Child succeeds.

## 1171.

Richard, bishop-elect of Verdun, died; Arnold succeeds. Saint Thomas, bishop of Canterbury, suffered.

## 1178.

Cono, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died, who ordered the stone towers and the place called the Treasury to be made, and the bier of Saint Vitonus to be completed. Whom Richer succeeds.

## 1179.

Richer is brought to an end by his own free will; Allestannus the younger succeeds, and in that same year he died; whom Allestannus the elder succeeded. [0991C]

## 1181.

Arnold, bishop of Verdun, died, he struck by an arrow in the head before the castle of Sainte-Menehould; Henry succeeds.

## 1182.

Pope Alexander died; Pope Lucius succeeds.

## 1183.

Allestannus the elder renounced the abbacy; Thomas, abbot of Mureau, is put in his place.

## 1186.

Pope Lucius died; Urban succeeds. Henry renounced the bishopric, and Albert is elected.

## 1187.

Abbot Thomas renounced; Hugh succeeds. Pope Urban died, and Gregory succeeded: who died in the same year; Clement succeeds.

## 1189.

The emperor Frederick died; his son [0991D] Henry succeeds.

## 1190.

Pope Clement died; Celestine succeeds.

## 1194.

A famine so severe that grain was sold at the price of 4 pounds.

## 1197.

*Pope Celestine died*; *Innocent the Third* succeeds. Abbot Hugh gave up the prelacy; whom Stephen succeeds; who, struck with paralysis, renounced the abbacy in the same year. Ludwig is chosen abbot, [0992A] the brother of Albert, bishop of Verdun.-- *The emperor Henry died*; to whom *succeeds* Philip.

## 1200.

Constantinople was taken by the Franks and by the Venetians, and they appoint Baldwin, count of Flanders, emperor.

## 1208.

*Philip*, king, not yet emperor, *was slain* by the palatine . . . . . . . ; Otto the *count* succeeds . . . . . i; what after he *was consecrated*, ruling by the man*dates of Pope* Innocent, excommunicated . . . . . . dis, he *was deposed* from the roy*al* dignity, and Frede*rick, duke of Ap*ulia, *was raised* to the empire. A great sedition arose at Verdun, the citizens rising against the clerics, the clerics against the citizens, and perpetrating many evils; at the last Bishop Albert died, slain by a lance; whom Robert the precentor succeeds. [0992B]

## 1216.

A general *council was convoked* at Rome, and there the emperor Frederick *was signed* with the cross. Pope Innocent died; Honorius succeeds.

## 1217.

Robert, bishop of Verdun, died; John of Aspremont succeeds, who founded the monastery of Saint Nicholas in the Meadow. A fire of this city of Verdun took place, from the steps of Saint Mary and from the gate of Ancelin's quarter as far as the bridge of Brachiolum, generally from wall to wall.

## 1222.

Saint Dominic died, the founder of the Order of Preachers.
