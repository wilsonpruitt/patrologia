## 1224.

John, bishop of Verdun, renounced the bishopric, passing over to the bishopric of Metz; whom Rudolf of Torte succeeds, cantor of [0992C] Lyons. Ludwig, abbot of Saint Vitonus, ordered a silver table to be made before the high altar. Pope Gregory admonished the emperor Frederick that he should set out for Jerusalem with his army, which had long since been signed with the cross, to destroy the enemies of the Christian faith.

## 1226.

*Honorius IX, pope, died*; *Gregory succeeds.*

1227 . . . . . a fight between Rudolf, *bishop* of Verdun, and the ci*tizens* . . . . . ii.

## 1232.

Saint Dominic, founder of the Friars Preachers, canonized.

## 1237.

On the 8th of the Ides of March, Abbot Ludwig, worn out with old age, resigned the abbacy — he who had begun the costly work of the monastery [0992D] — and died in the following year. Whom William, abbot of Saint-Mansuy, succeeds by election, formerly a monk of Saint Vitonus; who in that same costly work, in the buildings of the houses and of the chapel, in the shrines of the saints, in bells of the best and largest sort, and in the acquisition of lands, was found most faithful, just as his predecessor also was.

## 1241.

Pope Gregory died; Celestine succeeds, who sat 18 days; the see is vacant for three years. [0993A]

## 1243.

Innocent succeeds in the papacy.

## 1245.

Rudolf, bishop of Verdun, died; Guy of Trainel succeeded; who died in the same year; whom Guy of Mello succeeds. A great strife arose between him and the citizens of Verdun; peace having been made, in the same year he passed over to the bishopric of Auxerre; whom John of Aix succeeds.

## 1253.

Pope Innocent died; Alexander succeeds. John of Aix, bishop-elect of Verdun, a deacon, died; whom, by the provision of the lord pope, James, archdeacon of Laon, succeeds, a Frenchman by birth, from the city of Troyes.

## 1256.

James, bishop of Verdun, was translated by the pope's command to the patriarchate of Jerusalem; whom Robert of Milan succeeds. [0993B]

## 1259.

Abbot William died. The see was vacant for eight years, while a suit lasted between two men elected, namely Dominic the provost and Rudolf, prior of Mont-Saint-Martin, who nevertheless prevailed; and on this account the costly work of the monastery ceased, many attacks and damages having been done to this church by him. Pope Alexander died; the aforesaid James, bishop of Verdun and patriarch of Jerusalem, succeeded, called Pope Urban IV, who instituted the solemnity of the most holy Sacrament.

## 1264.

Pope Urban died; Clement succeeds.

## 1265.

Charles, count of Anjou, brother of Louis king of France, leader of the army of the Christians, overcame Manfred, the enemy of all Christendom. [0993C]

## 1267.

Clement died; Gregory succeeds.

## 1270.

Saint Louis, king of the Franks, died.

## 1271.

Robert II, bishop of Verdun, died; Ulric of Sathanacum succeeded, provost of blessed Mary Magdalene.

## 1272.

Pope Gregory [died]; Innocent succeeds.

## 1273.

Ulric, bishop of Verdun, died; after him the see was vacant for three years.

## 1276.

Pope Innocent [died]; Pope Adrian [succeeds], and then John. And in the bishopric there succeeds Gerard of Grandson.

## 1277.

Pope John died; Nicholas succeeds.

## 1278.

Gerard of Grandson, bishop of Verdun, died; his brother Henry of [0993D] Grandson succeeds.

## 1281.

Abbot Rudolf died; John succeeds.

## 1286.

Abbot John died; Thierry succeeds. Henry of Grandson, bishop of Verdun, died; it was vacant for 4 years.

## 1290.

James of Revigny, most general of clerics, succeeds in the bishopric of Verdun. [0994A]

## 1292.

Abbot Thierry died; Gobert the almoner is elected; but Philip Dorne, prior of Corbigny.

## 1296.

James II, bishop, died in the Roman curia, who had gone thither on account of the discord which he had with the citizens of Verdun. There succeeds John of Richericourt, called of Aspremont Ill u s .

## 1297.

Abbot Philip died; Hugh of Reims succeeds.

## 1302.

John of Richericourt, bishop of Verdun, died; Thomas of Aubemont succeeds.

## 1303.

Hugh, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; Baudoc of Failly succeeds, prior of Munau.

## 1305.

Abbot Baudoc died; Nicholas [0994B] Maisel the cellarer succeeds. Thomas, bishop of Verdun, died; Nicholas of Neuville-sur-Orne succeeded.

## 1312.

Nicholas, bishop of Verdun, resigned the burden of his bishopric; whom Henry of Aspremont succeeds.

## 1314.

Henry of Luxembourg, *imperarator*, died, who at the receiving of the Eucharist and in the administration of the blood of Christ was poisoned in Italy, poison having been put into the chalice.

## 1316.

Nicholas, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; whom Simon of Nancy succeeds, prior of Flavigny. A great famine and a very great mortality of men.

## 1318.

Simon of Nancy, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; whom Theobald of Bazeilles succeeds, prior of [0994C] Munau.

## 1319.

Theobald, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; his kinsman Erard of Bazeilles succeeds; who wiped out the debts and pledgings of his predecessors.

## 1325.

The archdeacon of Trier, the king of Bohemia, the count of Bar, and the duke of Lorraine with their following were three times in the year before Metz. On this account Patries was wholly burned

## 1332.

There were thunderings and lightnings on the morrow of the Lord's Nativity. The greater men of the city of Verdun were banished by the community of the city.

## 1336.

The citizens of Verdun burned the vill of Buxey, and as they were returning, at the bridge of Way many were slain and wounded by the men of Bar, [0994D] and the bishopric of Verdun by treachery.

## 1340.

Erard, abbot of Saint Vitonus, ordered the two larger middle bells to be made.

## 1346.

The king of the English near Crécy overcame the king of the Franks, and there fell the king of Bohemia, the duke of Lorraine, and many other princes.

## 1349.

Henry, bishop of Verdun, died; [0995A] Henry of Germiny is elected. He was not confirmed. Erard, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; Bastiavus, prior of Chaudefontaine, is elected; but Raymond Dachic obtained it by favor of the apostolic see.

## 1353.

Gerard of Vaudenay succeeds Abbot Raymond by favor of the apostolic see. Who squandered the goods of the monastery of Mary, encumbered its rents and revenues, pawned letters and charters of the utmost necessity, impoverished its men and subjects and despoiled them of their goods, diminished its revenue by a third part, and damaged it in other things to the amount of 26 thousand francs and more, and reduced the monastery itself to such poverty that scarcely twelve times in the year were matins sung in it. Who all things, may God spare him and forgive. [0995B]

## 1381.

Gerard of Vaudenay, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; whom John of Tric succeeds, prior of Flavigny.

## 1382.

John of Tric, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died; Henry of Passavant, called le Champenoy, succeeds, prior of Chaudefontaine, who greatly reduced the pledgings and debts of his predecessor.

## 1391.

Henry, a good governor, died as abbot; Reginald Pallardelli succeeds by favor of the apostolic see, a monk of Saint Michael.

## 1403.

Liébaud of Cusance, bishop of Verdun, died; John of Sarrebonne, son of the lord John of Commercy, knight, succeeds; who suffered many troubles from robbers and from some of the vassals of the bishopric of Verdun, inflicted upon him wickedly [0995C] and without cause.

## 1413.

A fire, on the vigil of the feast of Saint Vitonus, left unattended in the southern bell tower, melted the two heavier bells; which Abbot Reginald restored in the following year.

## 1417.

Abbot Reginald died; his nephew succeeds. Stephen of Bourges, almoner of Saint Vitonus, a most faithful ruler, [died].

## 1420.

John of Sarrebonne was translated to the bishopric of Châlons by an exchange made between him and Louis of Bar, cardinal, bishop *Portunensem*, duke of Bar, marquis of Pont, lord of Cassel, and administrator of the bishopric of Verdun. [0995D]

## 1430.

Abbot Stephen, the lesser bell of Abbot Reginald having been broken up, made a greater one. Louis of Bar, cardinal and bishop of Verdun, died; Louis of Haraucourt, cantor of Verdun, succeeds.

## 1431.

Stephen, abbot of Saint Vitonus, out of that magnificent [0996A] and costly old work of the church of Saint Vitonus, begun by the abbots Ludwig and William and brought to utter ruin, lacking a roof, taking the better stones, began another new one, and had it completed as far as the southern portal of the common entrance on either side, with its *verevis*, its *volturis*, and a fitting covering.

## 1436.

The body of Abbot Ludwig was translated from the old work into the new, under the altar of Saint Mary at the head.

## 1437.

The 4 first altars of the new work were dedicated on the 6th of the Kalends of June. Louis, bishop of Verdun, obtaining the bishopric of Toul, William, abbot of Saint-Thierry near Reims, succeeds by apostolic authority in the bishopric of Verdun [0996B]

## 1438.

A severe famine.

## 1448.

Abbot Stephen bought 4 copper columns.

## 1449.

By an exchange William, bishop of Verdun, passed over to the bishopric of Toul. And Louis, bishop of Toul, returned to Verdun, which he ruled well.

## 1451.

A mortality of the epidemic at Verdun, of 2500 men.

## 1452.

Stephen, abbot of Saint Vitonus, died. John of Arency the chamberlain is elected; but, 15 months having passed, he is cast out by the apostolic see at the instance of the lord William Hugh, cardinal, to whom the administration is granted; after nine months Anthony of Serrières, prior of Flavigny, extracted it from him, giving an annual pension of 400 gold ducats. [0996C]

## 1454.

The work of the new church of Saint Vitonus ceased.

## 1455.

The same lord William, cardinal, died, and his pension with him.

## 1456.

Louis, bishop of Verdun, died; William of Haraucourt, his kinsman, provost of Mont-faucon, succeeds.

## 1461.

Charles, king of the Franks, died; his son Louis succeeds. Abbot Anthony, the greater bell of Abbot Stephen having been broken up, restored it. And he bought two silken copes woven with gold and silver. John of Arency died, once elected of Saint Vitonus, chamberlain and prior of Chaudefontaine.
