# Cruces — 11648, *Annales* (PL 204, cols 987–996)

Verdun annals (Saint-Vanne). Corrupt or lacunose printed readings rendered
literally per `translation-style.md`; nothing emended in the text.

## 0988C (s.a. 915)

**Printed:** `Combusta est ecclesia Sancti Vitoni Virdunensis liba: memorie
sanctorum.`
*liba* is not construable here. Probably a broken *libri* / *cum libris* (books
burned with the church) or a truncated *libra*; Migne prints it with a colon
before *memorie sanctorum*. Left as *liba* in italics, untranslated, with the
rest rendered literally. Human check wanted against another witness.

## 0990A (s.a. 1099)

**Printed:** `succedit Laurentius Jherusalem a duce Godefrido Bulloniensi et a
christianis capitur.` — no punctuation between the two annal items. Rendered as
two sentences ("Laurence succeeds. Jerusalem is taken…"); the sentence boundary
is supplied by sense, not by the plate.

## 0991B–C (s.a. 1179)

**Printed:** `Richerus spontanea voluntate finitur.` *finitur* is ambiguous
between "dies" and "his term is ended" (i.e. resigns); the following clause
("Allestannus the younger succeeds, and in that same year *he* died") suggests
resignation. Rendered literally, "is brought to an end by his own free will."

## 0991C (s.a. 1181)

**Printed:** `Obiit Arnoldus episcopus Virdunensis is sagitta in capite
percussus`. The bare *is* is corrupt — most likely *ictu* (sagittae ictu) or a
dittography of the preceding syllable. Rendered per the intended reading
("struck by an arrow in the head") without the stray word.

## 0991D–0992A (s.a. 1197) — italic fragments

Migne sets fragmentary/restored readings in italic, and the italic runs break
**mid-word**: `succedit Inno *centius tertius.*`, `cui *succedit* Philippus`.
English cannot reproduce a mid-word break at the same point; the italics are
carried to the nearest corresponding sense unit (*Innocent the Third*,
*succeeds*). Same policy applied at 1208 and 1216 below.

## 0992A (s.a. 1208) — lacunose entry

**Printed:** `*Philippus* rex nondum imperator *est int* erfectus a palatino
. . . . . . . ; succedit Otto co *mes*. . . . . i; quid postquam conse *cratus
est man* datis Innocencii *pape im* perans excommunicatus . . . . . . dis
depositus *est reg* ia dignitate, et *sublimatus* est imperio Frede *ricus dux
Ap* ulie.`
The plate is damaged: dot-lacunae kept verbatim with the same dot counts, and
the orphan letter-strings (`i`, `dis`) kept as printed rather than restored.
*quid* is corrupt for *qui*, rendered "and … he". The mid-word italic breaks
(`est int|erfectus`, `co|mes`, `conse|cratus`, `man|datis`, `pape im|perans`,
`est reg|ia`, `Frede|ricus dux Ap|ulie`) are moved to sense-unit boundaries per
the 1197 note.

## 0992B (s.a. 1216)

Same lacunose typography: `*Convocatu* m est … *concilium,* … cruce si *gnatus
est*`. Italics carried to the English sense units (*council was convoked*,
*was signed*).

## 0992C–D (s.a. 1226)

`*Obiit Honorius* IX, papa` — the numeral is wrong in the source: the pope who
died in 1227 and was succeeded by Gregory IX is Honorius **III**. Left verbatim
(the annalist's error, not a typographic one). The following unnumbered 1227
line is itself lacunose (`. . . . . pugna`, `ci *ves* . . . . . ii`) and is kept
as printed inside the 1226 section — it is not a `## ` head in the source.

## 0993D–0994A (s.a. 1290)

`Jacobus de Revigneyo clericorum generalissimus` — *generalissimus* is odd for a
bishop-elect (Jacques de Revigny was a celebrated jurist; one expects
*legum doctor* or *generalis*). Rendered literally, "most general of clerics."

## 0994A (s.a. 1292)

`Gobertus elimosinarius eligitur; sed Philippus Dorne prior de Corbigney.` The
*sed* clause has no verb — cf. 1349, where the parallel construction supplies
*obtinuit*. Rendered literally, verb unsupplied.

## 0994A (s.a. 1296)

`Johannes de Richeri-curte dictus de Aspero-monte Ill u s .` — the spaced
`Ill u s .` is broken type (probably *Illus.* = *illustris*, or a stray catch-
word). Reproduced verbatim.

## 0994C–D (s.a. 1325, 1336)

- 1325 `Propter hoc Patries tota fuit combusta` — no final punctuation in the
  source; *Patries* is an unidentified place-name, left as printed.
- 1336 `et Virdunensis episcopatus per tradicionem.` — verbless fragment
  (something like *captus est* is wanting). Rendered literally, "and the
  bishopric of Verdun by treachery."

## 0994D (s.a. 1314, 1316)

`imperarator` for *imperator*; `maximo mortalitas` for *maxima mortalitas*.
Obvious compositor's slips, translated per the intended reading, not emended in
any apparatus.

## 0995A (s.a. 1353)

`Qui bona monasterii Marie dissipavit` — *Marie* is unexpected (the house is
Saint-Vanne; possibly the Sainte-Marie church of the same annal, or a stray).
Left as "the monastery of Mary." Closing sentence `Qui omnia parcat sibi Deus et
indulgeat` is corrupt for *Quae omnia* — rendered per the intended reading,
"All which may God spare him and forgive."

## 0996A (s.a. 1431)

`cum suis verevis volturis et coopertura decenti` — *verevis* is not a Latin
word; likely *vitreis* (glass windows) or a Romance form for window-frames.
Left untranslated in italics; *volturis* taken as *volturae*, vaults.

## 0995C (s.a. 1420)

`episcopum Portunensem` — for *Portuensem* (Porto). Translated per the intended
reading.

## Chronology to check (not typographic)

- s.a. 1064: Harold's death and William's succession (in fact 1066) are entered
  under 1064, in the same entry as the comet (1066). Left as dated.
- s.a. 1150: the death of Eugenius III (1153) is entered under 1150.
- s.a. 1200: the capture of Constantinople (1204) is entered under 1200.
- s.a. 1272 / 1276: papal successions are compressed and partly wrong
  (Gregory X d. 1276; Innocent V, Adrian V, John XXI all 1276).
