## PRELIMINARY NOTICE.

[0639A]

That the blessed Alcuin wrote a brief commentary on the Song of Songs, the very writer of his Life attests in express words: « On the Proverbs of Solomon, he says, and on Ecclesiastes, and on the Song of Songs (he wrote) with lucid brevity. » The same thing several manuscript codices confirm, in which certain diligent librarians give assurance that a similar commentary is contained under the name of Alcuin. Thus in the catalogue of the manuscripts of the Harleian collection, vol. I, is noted codex 212, on parchment, in quarto, containing as no. 2 a Commentary of Alcuin on the Song of Songs, which at folio 100 b takes its beginning from these words: Here begins the book of the Song of Songs, that is, the better things of those who make better, as the Holy of Holies. Similarly in the catalogue of the manuscripts of England and Ireland, edited at Oxford in 1679 in folio, among the manuscript codices of the Jacobean library, no. 8676 is listed, Albinus's Commentary on the Song of Songs. In the San-Lorenzo library at Liège of the Order of St. Benedict there exists: Alcuin or Albinus on Ecclesiastes and the Song of Songs. [0639B] The most distinguished and likewise most celebrated Montfaucon, in volumes I and II of the *Bibliotheca bibliothecarum*, lists with the utmost fidelity several codices containing the same commentary; in vol. I, p. 628, he writes: « In the library of the king of England: Alcuin on the Canticle. » Vol. II, p. 1202: In the library of Jumièges: Alcuin's Exposition on the Song of Songs. » P. 1222: « In the library of Saint-Aubin of Angers: Alcuin's Exposition on the Song of Songs. » P. 1239: « In the library of the monastery of Saint-Ouen of Rouen: Alcuin on the Song of Songs. » P. 1253: « In the library of the monastery of the Blessed Virgin of Bec: A Brief Exposition on the Song of Songs, under the name of the Church and the Synagogue. It is by Alcuin the abbot, as is there noted. » P. 1284: « In the library of Saint-Bénigne of Dijon: Albinus's Exposition of the Song [0640A] of Songs. » P. 1342: « In the library of the abbey of Savigny of the Cistercian Order: Alcuin on the Song of Songs. » P. 1361: « In the library of the monastery of Saint-Michel in-peril-of-the-sea: Alcuin's Exposition on Genesis, on the Song of Songs. » Finally, in the Oxford-Cambridge *Ecloga*, or catalogue of the manuscripts of England, there is attributed to Alcuin a Compendium on the Song of Songs, which is perhaps the same as the Compendium edited by the distinguished Patrick Young, which we here present.

To this trustworthiness of the manuscript codices the most celebrated writers add weight: Helinand, in Vincent of Beauvais, Book XXIII of the *Speculum Historiale*, chapter 173; Trithemius, in his book *On Ecclesiastical Writers* and in Book II of *On the Illustrious Men of the Order of St. Benedict*, chapter 26; John Bale, Century II of the *Writers of Britain*; Conrad Lycosthenes in the *Epitome* of the *Bibliotheca* of Conrad Gesner; Possevino in the *Apparatus sacer*, vol. I.

It is astonishing that the learned writers of the *Histoire littéraire de la France* disregarded these testimonies which we have set forth, and denied that any complete commentary on the Song of Songs flowed from Alcuin's pen, and [claimed] that [0640B] all his labor upon the aforesaid book consisted in the exposition of a single little verse — the 6th of chapter VI — contained in the letter to Daphnis his disciple, to be presented at the end of this little work. For the testimonies adduced by no means admit this interpretation — testimonies which speak not of the exposition of only a single portion or verse of the Song of Songs, but of a commentary on the Song of Songs, that is, on this entire book. These same men, elsewhere most learned, are still more mistaken when they very confidently assert, in the place cited, p. 336, that that Compendium on the Song of Songs, which in the year 1638 was edited at London by Patrick Young together with the commentaries of Gilbert Foliot, is nothing other than the same letter to Daphnis. For this headlong judgment is refuted merely [0641A] by the sight of that edition, of which indeed the aforesaid men — otherwise truly excellent — had no copy at hand.

It ought therefore to be certain, at least by historical faith, that our Alcuin expounded not merely some portion, but the entire book of the Song of Songs in a brief style — or, as the writer of his Life puts it, with lucid brevity. It now remains to be inquired whether this Compendium, which we present from the London edition prepared by Patrick Young, is that very labor which Alcuin bestowed in expounding that book. In Alcuin's favor fights the authority of the distinguished Patrick Young, the royal librarian and a diligent searcher of ancient monuments, who first brought that Compendium forth into public, under the name of Alcuin, without hesitation, from the royal library; which he would rightly be presumed not to have done had he not been supported by the trustworthiness of a certain *392* manuscript codex, free of all suspicion of novelty or corruption. To this authority the manuscript codex 69 of the royal Vatican library adds strength — in octavo, written in the 10th century — a copy of which was furnished me by [0641B] the eminent lord Cardinal Passionei, then prefect of that library, one to be celebrated with the highest praises in the republic of letters even after his death: in which codex the same Compendium — with a few changes, which we note — is included among the letters of Alcuin, though indeed without a name prefixed. Finally, the style in which this commentary is written is by no means at variance with the style of Alcuin, although in his commentaries — where for the most part, after the manner of that age, the opinions of the earlier Fathers are culled — he does not keep a constant character, as he does in the letters written out of his own genius.

And these foundations will seem to no one not sufficiently firm, so that this Compendium of the exposition of the Song of Songs may be attributed to Alcuin, as its true author, without danger of delusion. I confess indeed that a certain doubt about the true author of this commentary was cast upon me when, in perusing the *Stromata* on the Song of Songs of Angelomus, abbot of Luxeuil — in vol. XV of the *Bibliotheca of the Holy Fathers*, Lyons edition, p. 415 and [0641C] following — I found that this our Exposition was there contained for the greatest part, as to its opinions and even its very words. But observing that Angelomus borrowed almost everything he commented upon, whether on the books of Kings or on the Song of Songs, from the sayings of other teachers and expositors, and learned it from a most eloquent master (perhaps Alcuin himself) by lending an attentive ear, just as he himself professes in the apologetic preface to the books of Kings; considering besides that Angelomus took also the beginning of his commentary on the Song of Songs, from those words: The Scriptures most manifestly teach that Solomon was called by three names, etc., from the beginning of Alcuin's commentary on Ecclesiastes, I easily shook off the doubt previously conceived; so that I now by no means doubt that those things which in Angelomus's commentary agree with this Compendium were likewise borrowed from the same source; and nothing hinders me from judging Alcuin the true author of this Compendium on the Song of Songs; [0641D] relying, that is, upon the testimony of the writers cited above, and upon the trustworthiness of several manuscript codices — above all the Vatican one, which I here use, and the English one, which the most celebrated lord Patrick Young used.

## A POEM Prefixed in the Vatican Codex to the following little commentary.

This book Solomon sang with wondrous sweetness,
[0642A]which holds the excellent songs of Bridegroom and Bride,
singing on this side and that the praises of the Church and of Christ;
and it recalls the faithful companions, men and women, of the bridal chamber.
These things, I pray, young man, remember to commit to your mind:
these songs are far better than false Maro's.
These sing to you the true precepts of everlasting life;
that other will sound ill in your ears, things frivolous and witty [ F., falsa].
