## CHAPTER ONE.

VERSE 1.-- Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. The Synagogue longs for God to be made incarnate, and with devoted love runs to meet him at his coming. Her voice sounds first in the Song of love; and when the holy prophets frequently showed her the way of living, and pointed out his coming — he who, like a Bridegroom going forth from the bridal chamber, would enrich [0642B] the world with a new blessing — then, the voices of the heralds being past, she began to long for the presence of the King and Savior himself, saying: Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth; which is to say: let him not always appoint angels to instruct me, nor prophets; let him at last come himself, who has been promised so long; let him enlighten me with the light of his presence, and, as if bestowing a kiss, addressing me with his own mouth, let him strengthen me — that is, let him not scorn to instruct me when I ask about the way of salvation. And this is read to have been fulfilled in the Gospel: While Jesus sat on the mountain, his disciples came to him. And opening his mouth he taught them, saying: Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven [n: (Matth. V, 1)] . So she says: Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth. That is, let him touch me with the sweetness of his presence, which I have often heard promised by the prophets.

[0642C] *393* For thy breasts are better than wine. The sweetness of gospel teaching is better than the austerity of the law. By wine he means the ardor of legal knowledge; by milk he means the rudiments of gospel faith. Whence Paul: I gave you milk to drink [n: (I Cor. III, 2)] , that is, food. The breasts of the Bridegroom are better than wine, because whomever the rudiments of faith regenerate from water and spirit, they lead forth with the entrance to the heavenly life laid open — which the long observance of the law was not able to do, as the Apostle witnesses, since he says: The law brought nothing to perfection [n: (Hebr. VII, 19)] . By the breasts are understood the teachers, who give us the milk of knowledge to drink.

VERSE 2.-- Fragrant with the best ointments. The Holy Spirit is compared to ointments for this reason: because as ointment heals a wound, so the Holy Spirit drives out [0642D] vices, cherishes and heals souls. Good were the ointments with which the prophets and priests were anointed under the law, but better are those with which the apostles and their successors have been anointed invisibly; of whom Paul says: And it is God who anointed us, and God who sealed us [n: (II Cor. I, 21)] . And John: Let the anointing which you have received from him remain in you [n: (I Joan. II, 27)] . Thy name is oil poured [0643A] out. It is the giving of a reason; for it is no wonder if his members are fragrant with ointments, since he himself took his name from ointment. Christ [is named] from chrism, that is, the Anointed from anointing; and this name, with the grace of the Holy Spirit, is poured out in baptism upon all the faithful. Whence Peter: How God anointed him with the Holy Spirit and with power [n: (Act. X, 38)] . And in the Psalms: God has anointed thee with the oil of gladness above thy fellows [n: (Psal. XLV, 7)] . He says oil: not dripped, but poured out; for God does not give him the Spirit by measure, since he himself is full of the Holy Spirit. Therefore the young maidens have loved thee. That is, the souls of the elect, renewed by the grace of baptism.

VERSE 3.-- Draw me after thee. The Synagogue at first begged that God would come and offer her the kiss of peace; [0643B] now the Church, knowing that he has already come and already returned to the heavens, longs to be able to follow him. And because she cannot accomplish this by herself, she implores the leading of him to whom she desires to come. Draw me after thee. That is: because without thee we can do nothing, we pray that thou uphold with the right hand of thy protection us who run to thee. For we cannot run, or finish the course, unless we run with thee as leader and helper. Whence Paul: I have labored more than all of them, yet not I, but the grace of Christ [n: (I Cor. XV, 10)] . We will run together in the fragrance of thy ointments. That is, by the increase of the gifts of the Holy Spirit, working well together with thee, let us hasten to the fellowship of the heavenly Jerusalem, that we may follow thee ascending into heaven. The King has brought me into his storerooms. That is, into the eternal [0643C] joys of the heavenly homeland. The storerooms of the eternal King are the joys of the heavenly homeland, into which the Church is now brought by faith, to be brought more fully by the reality. We will exult and rejoice in thee, mindful of thy breasts above wine. In thee, not in ourselves, mindful in all things of the grace of faith above the doctrine of the law; which is to say: in no way do we exalt ourselves for what we have received, but in all that we live well we will exult in thy mercy, mindful with how great tenderness thou hast deigned to renew us, and how thou hast deigned to soften the austerity of the law by the grace of gospel faith. The upright love thee. No one loves thee but the upright; and no one is upright but he who loves thee.

VERSE 4.-- I am black, but comely, O daughters of Jerusalem. [0643D] I am black in the pressures of persecutions, but comely in the beauty of virtues. That is: I appear black indeed in the eyes of my persecutors, but before God I shine, adorned with the beauty of virtues. As the tents of Kedar [Cod. Vat., Cedar], as the skin of Solomon. Kedar was the son of Ishmael, which *394* is interpreted [0644A] "darkness." The holy Church says she is black like Kedar, because by the afflictions of unbelievers she has been so darkened, as though she had become the enemy of the whole world. Solomon was peaceable both in name and in life, who made himself tents from the skins of dead animals; so the Lord creates for himself the Church out of those animals which mortify in themselves the desires of the flesh; therefore the Church is like the skin of Solomon.

VERSE 5.-- Do not regard me, that I am dark, because the sun has discolored me. That is: do not wonder if I am despised by men, because the heat of excessive persecution, or the love of Christ, has discolored me. The sons of my mother fought against me: they set me as keeper in the vineyards; my own vineyard I have not kept. The bitterness of the persecution of the sons of the Synagogue made me not keep [0644B] my vineyard at Jerusalem, but [to be] the keeper of many vineyards, that is, of Churches, throughout the world. The mother of the primitive Church is the Synagogue, to whom it is said through the prophet: Zion, thou shalt be called a city, and the mother of cities [n: (Zach. VIII, 3)] .

VERSE 6.-- Show me, O thou whom my soul loves, where thou feedest, where thou liest at noon, lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions. Show me my shepherd, whom I love with my whole soul, and in what places he has his pastures [ Cod. Vat., pascuam] and rest, lest, troubled by the heat of temptations, I begin to seek him after the little assemblies of his companions, that is, of the heretics.

VERSE 7.-- If thou knowest not thyself, O fairest [Cod. Vat., pulchra] among women, go forth and go away after [0644C] the tracks of the flocks. If thou knowest not that thou art betrothed to me under the dominion [ F., conditione] of such a temptation, go forth from my fellowship, and follow the various deeds of those who go astray. And feed my kids beside the tents of the shepherds. That is, nourish the lost hearers who, having followed the teachings of foolish masters, will stand at the left hand.

VERSE 8.-- To my cavalry among the chariots of Pharaoh have I likened thee, my love. As I freed the former people from the fear of Egypt, so thee, my bride!, if thou trust in me, I will free from the hands of thy persecutors.

VERSE 9.-- Thy cheeks are beautiful as a turtledove's [Cod. Vat., turris ]. I have adorned thee with such great virtue of saving modesty, that thou mayest not corrupt, by any seduction of wicked teachers, [0644D] the chastity of the faith promised to me. Thy neck is as jewels. By the neck are signified the holy preachers, who show by works what they teach by word: by the jewels is expressed the perfection of their work.

VERSE 10.-- We will make thee little chains of gold, inlaid [0645A] with silver. By the little chains Holy Scripture is shown, which shines inwardly with the gold of spiritual meanings, and gleams with the silver of heavenly eloquence.

VERSE 11.-- While the King was on his couch, my spikenard gave forth its odor. While Christ the King rests in the blessedness of the heavenly secret [ Cod. Vat., Regi . . . quiescenti], the virtue of the saints in the Church ministers to us the grace of a great sweetness.

VERSE 12.-- A little bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me; he shall abide between my breasts. The death of my beloved, which he underwent for my salvation, shall always abide in my memory.

VERSE 13.-- A cluster of cypress is my beloved to me, in the vineyards of Engaddi. He who [ Edit., quid] was a little bundle of myrrh in the bitterness of the passion is himself a cluster of cypress in [0645B] the sweetness of the resurrection. Myrrh saddens, wine gladdens. In the vineyards of Engaddi, because of the divine graces which after the resurrection he bestowed in baptism upon those who share in him. Engaddi, "the fountain of the kid," signifies baptism.

VERSE 14.-- Behold, thou art fair, my love, behold, thou art fair. Thou art fair in clean works, in which thou dost live soberly in this world. Thou art fair in simplicity of heart, because for the sole aim of eternity thou dost persist in good deeds. Thine eyes are *395* doves'. The eyes of thy heart are clean and simple, and free from all duplicity of deceiving; or, thy senses are endowed with spiritual understanding.

VERSE 15.-- Behold, thou art fair, my beloved [Cod. Vat., mi], and comely. The holy Church, hearing that a twofold [0645C] beauty has been attributed to her by the Lord — namely of work and of intention, with the simplicity of a pure heart — answers with devoted voice: Whatever of beauty, grace, simplicity, hope I have, I have received by thy bounty; but thou art truly and beyond comparison fair, and comely by the perpetuity of thy divine nature, and by the wondrous dignity of the humanity thou hast assumed. Our little bed is flowering. The bride rests with the bridegroom in the little bed, when in a flowering time of peace the peace of the holy Church both flourishes with holy virtues and is multiplied with spiritual offspring.

VERSE 16.-- The beams of our houses are of cedar, our paneled ceilings of cypress. The beams and paneled ceilings signify the teachers of the holy Church, for their protection [ Cod. Vat., munimentum] and adornment; of cedar and of cypress [0645D] for their outstanding virtues and the fragrance of a good life. By whose word and example the Church is sustained, lest she be shaken apart by heretical depravity. The odor of cedar puts serpents to flight, which suits those who by the power of the heavenly word are wont to root out the poisoned dogmas of the heretics. The cypress, which lays down the beauty of its foliage at no impulse of the winds, expresses the constancy of those who adorn the holy Church, like paneled ceilings, with the higher ornaments of virtues.
