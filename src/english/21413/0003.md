## CHAPTER III.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 1.-- In my little bed by night [Cod. ⟦s2⟧ Vat., noctem] I sought him whom my soul loves; I sought him and found him not [Cod. ⟦s3⟧ Vat., quaesivi illum et non inveni ]. ⟦s4⟧ Long ago, she says, with much zeal I sought the Lord, but because [ Cod. ⟦s5⟧ Vat., quia] I was still subject to the enticements [0648D] of my flesh, and blinded by the darkness of deep ignorance, I found not the light of truth, that is, the Lord.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 2.-- I will rise and go about the city; through the streets and squares I will seek him whom my soul loves. ⟦s2⟧ I sought him, and found him not. ⟦s3⟧ I resolved in my mind to rise from the little bed of carnal pleasures, to go about lands and seas, and to approach the teachings [ Cod. ⟦s4⟧ Vat., ministeria] of the philosophers; but not even thus did I find him.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 3.-- The keepers [Cod. ⟦s2⟧ Vat., vigiles] who keep the city found me. ⟦s3⟧ Have you seen him whom my soul [0649A] loves? ⟦s4⟧ The watchmen who keep the Church are the apostles and teachers, who found the gentile world anxious ( Cod. ⟦s5⟧ Vat., sollicitantem] in the search for truth.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 4.-- When I had passed a little beyond them, I found him whom my soul loves. ⟦s2⟧ When I gave myself over to their teaching, soon I found the light of truth, which I had long sought [ Cod. ⟦s3⟧ Vat., quod quaesivi]. ⟦s4⟧ I held him, and will not let him go, until I bring him into my mother's house, and into the chamber of her that bore me. ⟦s5⟧ I have held [ Cod. ⟦s6⟧ Vat., teneo donec . . . ] him with firm faith, and will hold him, until at the end of the age, through the office of preaching, I bring him into the house and bed of the Synagogue, who bore me in the Lord, and there shall be one fold and one shepherd.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0649B]VERSE 5.-- I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, that you stir not up, nor make to awake, my beloved, until she herself please. ⟦s2⟧ In the foregoing we did not find that the bride had come to the rest of sleep; but rather that, leaping from her little bed, she had hastened with the greatest labor to the finding of her beloved; and how the beloved adjures the daughters of Jerusalem, that they should not disturb her. ⟦s3⟧ But perhaps that soul, lulled in a happy slumber, which knows how to rest in divine love, closes the eye of the heart on outward things, and opens it on inward things. ⟦s4⟧ Rightly therefore are the daughters of Jerusalem adjured not to stir up the beloved, that is, not to hinder by an importunate irruption a mind devoted to God from its aim of heavenly desire. ⟦s5⟧ This passage is repeated in the Song of love, that the Lord may show that he takes no lesser care [0649C] for the Church gathered from the gentiles than for the Church gathered from the Jews. ⟦s6⟧ For that he adjures the daughters not to stir up the beloved can be so understood, that he commands those who from among the Jews came first into the faith not to trouble them, nor to gainsay the rest of those who from the error of gentile-hood have come to the faith.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *398* VERSE 6.-- Who is this that comes up through the desert? ⟦s2⟧ The Synagogue marvels how the gentile people, cleansed by no ministry of circumcision, instructed by no admonition of the prophets, had suddenly ascended from the lowest pleasures, through the desert of gentile-hood and idolatry, to the high summits of the virtues and the embraces of the Bridegroom. ⟦s3⟧ Whose faith is of the greater wonder, in that it invincibly keeps what it received from above, [0649D] as John witnesses, who says: I know where thou dwellest, where the seat of Satan is; and thou holdest my name, and hast not denied my faith [n: (Apoc. II, 13)] . ⟦s4⟧ By the desert can also be signified that manner of life which, separated from the enticements of the world, is given only to the searching of God's law and to the observance of the heavenly precepts. ⟦s5⟧ As a little column of smoke from aromatic spices. ⟦s6⟧ Kindled with the fire of love, she strives toward heavenly things with every effort of the virtues. ⟦s7⟧ Smoke at the same time is wont partly to rise, partly, being drawn on high, to vanish. ⟦s8⟧ So also the Church, in certain of her members, is newly born through [0650A] the grace of the Holy Spirit; in certain others, as in those born before, she is gathered to heavenly things. ⟦s9⟧ She is likened not to smoke, but to a little column of smoke, that the unity of the Church's faith may be signified. ⟦s10⟧ Of myrrh and frankincense, and of all the powder of the perfumer. ⟦s11⟧ From the mortification of carnal pleasures, and the purity of prayers, and the odor of all the virtues.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 7.-- Behold, sixty valiant men surround the bed of Solomon, of the most valiant of Israel. ⟦s2⟧ The bed of Solomon is the quiet manner of life of the saints. ⟦s3⟧ The sixty valiant men are the holy preachers and most valiant warriors, who with a clean heart are worthy to see God.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 8.-- All holding swords, and most skilled in wars. ⟦s2⟧ For they hold the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God — they who fulfill in deed what [ Cod. ⟦s3⟧ Vat., quidquid] they teach with the mouth. ⟦s4⟧ Each one's sword upon [0650B] his thigh, because of nightly fears. ⟦s5⟧ The nightly fears are the snares of hidden temptation. ⟦s6⟧ But the sword is the watchful guard, pressing down the concupiscences of the flesh, lest the uncleanness of life stain the word of preaching.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 9.-- King Solomon made himself a litter of the wood of Lebanon. ⟦s2⟧ The litter of Solomon is the holy Church, which raises believers to the banquets of eternal blessedness, which is built of those strong in soul, as of incorruptible timbers.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 10.-- Its pillars he made of silver. ⟦s2⟧ The silver pillars are the teachers shining with the light of eloquence. ⟦s3⟧ The golden headrest. ⟦s4⟧ The golden headrest is the hope of perpetual rest promised to the faithful [ Cod. ⟦s5⟧ Vat., perpetua . . . promissa]. ⟦s6⟧ The purple ascent. ⟦s7⟧ What else is the purple ascent but the blood of the martyrs [0650C] and the passion of our Redeemer? ⟦s8⟧ For one does not ascend to the banquets of life except through the mystery of the passion of Christ. ⟦s9⟧ The midst he paved with charity, for the daughters of Jerusalem. ⟦s10⟧ For everyone who has charity of God and neighbor will come joyful to this rest, to these banquets. ⟦s11⟧ All these ornaments Christ bestowed on the Church out of the exceeding charity with which he loved us and gave himself for us.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 11.-- Go forth and see, O daughters of Zion, King Solomon. ⟦s2⟧ Go forth in mind and deed from the turbulent conduct of the world, that you may be able to see the King of peace. ⟦s3⟧ In the diadem with which his mother crowned him. ⟦s4⟧ See the Lord Christ in the humanity which, taken from his virgin mother, he placed at the right hand of the paternal majesty. [0650D] In the day of his dispensation [Cod. ⟦s5⟧ Vat., disponsationis]. ⟦s6⟧ In the time of his incarnation, in which he came forth from the virginal womb to join to himself the Church as his bride. ⟦s7⟧ And in the day of the gladness of his heart. ⟦s8⟧ That is, of the redemption of the human race, which was a day of gladness to Christ.
