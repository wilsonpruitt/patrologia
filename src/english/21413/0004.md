## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 1.-- How fair thou art, my love, how fair thou art! ⟦s2⟧ He calls the Church fair; and he repeats "fair," because he saw her to be praiseworthy both in action and in preaching: [0651A] in action, namely, because like a little column of smoke she rises from aromatic spices; and in preaching, because she strives to make even her neighbors come into her fellowship, saying: Go forth and see. ⟦s3⟧ Thine eyes are doves'. ⟦s4⟧ Thy senses are excellent in the contemplation of spiritual things *399* and modest [ Al., venerandi], by which thou hast merited to see and to know my gifts, which thou hast lately set forth, and my diadem, which thou hast lately preached. ⟦s5⟧ Besides that which lies hidden within. ⟦s6⟧ For great indeed is the glory of open work, but far more incomparable is the reward of eternal recompense, which, while we make our pilgrimage on earth, cannot be seen [ Cod. ⟦s7⟧ Vat., quae necdum videri potest]. ⟦s8⟧ Thy hair is as flocks of goats. ⟦s9⟧ By the hair can be understood the faithful peoples, who by their number furnish the greatest adornment to the Church. ⟦s10⟧ Which [0651B] have come up from Mount Gilead. ⟦s11⟧ Gilead is interpreted "heap of testimony," which well suits the united multitude of the saints.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 2.-- Thy teeth are as flocks of shorn sheep. ⟦s2⟧ By the hair are signified the more fragile, but by the teeth the more perfect ones, apt for governing the Church. ⟦s3⟧ Which have come up from the washing. ⟦s4⟧ That is, from the font of holy baptism, they who are both shorn and washed, that is, stripped by renouncing [ Cod. ⟦s5⟧ Vat., renuntiantes] the world, and cleansed by the washing of life. ⟦s6⟧ All with twin offspring. ⟦s7⟧ That is, fruitful with a twofold charity. ⟦s8⟧ And there is not a barren one among them. ⟦s9⟧ There is none who does not produce the offspring of good work.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 3.-- Thy lips are as a scarlet ribbon [Cod. ⟦s2⟧ Vat., cocunea], and thy speech is sweet. ⟦s3⟧ By the scarlet ribbon [0651C] is understood the doctrine of truth. ⟦s4⟧ The lips of the bride are likened to scarlet, because the Church does not cease to preach the price of the Lord's blood, by which she was redeemed; or because holy preaching blazes with the ardor of charity. ⟦s5⟧ As a piece of pomegranate, so are thy cheeks, besides that which lies hidden within. ⟦s6⟧ By the cheeks is expressed modesty, by the pomegranate the passion of Christ. ⟦s7⟧ The bride therefore has the redness of the pomegranate in her cheeks, when she confesses the sacrament of the Lord's passion in words and proves it by deeds.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 4.-- Thy neck is as the tower of David. ⟦s2⟧ The tower of David is the Church, the neck is the preachers, by whose faith and constancy in that same city she is firm, and on every side unassailable. ⟦s3⟧ Which is built with bulwarks. ⟦s4⟧ But the bulwarks of that same city are the defenses of Holy Scripture, or the examples of the preceding [0651D] Fathers. ⟦s5⟧ A thousand shields hang from it. ⟦s6⟧ Because as many as are the precepts in the divine books, so many are the defenses of our breast, by which we are defended against all snares. ⟦s7⟧ All the armor of the valiant. ⟦s8⟧ It is the whole equipment, whether of action or of heavenly doctrine, by which we not only escape, but also overcome.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 5.-- Thy two breasts are two twin fawns of a she-goat [Cod. ⟦s2⟧ Vat., capreae], which feed among the lilies.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 6.-- Until the day breathe and the shadows decline. ⟦s2⟧ The two breasts are [d: are] the two peoples coming from the circumcision and from the gentile world, who through humility indeed understand themselves to be small and sinners; but running in charity [0652A] they pass over all the obstacles of the world. ⟦s3⟧ Which feed [ Cod. ⟦s4+5⟧ Vat., pascunt] among the lilies, that is, in the most shining examples of the holy Fathers, until, the eternal day breathing, we pass beyond the shadows of the present [ Cod. ⟦s4+5⟧ Vat., praesentes] mortality. ⟦s6⟧ I will go to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of frankincense. ⟦s7⟧ By myrrh is expressed the mortification of the flesh; by frankincense the devotion of prayer. ⟦s8⟧ As if the Bridegroom would say: I will frequent them, and, gracious, will glorify with devout illumination those whom I perceive to be exalted in the virtue of passion or of prayer.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 7.-- Thou art wholly fair, my love, and there is no spot in thee. ⟦s2⟧ In all, namely, who fear God, small and great.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 8.-- Come from Lebanon, my bride, come from Lebanon, come. ⟦s2⟧ Lebanon is interpreted "whiteness," that is, [0652B] the soul made white by good deeds, which the Bridegroom exhorts three times to come: first, living [ Cod. ⟦s3⟧ Vat., viventes] in the flesh through good works. ⟦s4⟧ Secondly, freed from the flesh, to receive the blessed life. ⟦s5⟧ Thirdly, when the body has been received back, to enjoy after the resurrection the perfect joys. ⟦s6⟧ Thou shalt be crowned from the head of Aman [Cod. ⟦s7⟧ Vat., Amana], from the top of [Sanir] and Hermon, from the dens of lions, from the mountains of leopards. ⟦s8⟧ The lions, for their pride, and the leopards, for their cruelty, are the evil spirits. ⟦s9⟧ But by the name of the mountains are signified the proud hearts of unbelievers, where the unclean spirits have their seat. ⟦s10⟧ When the holy preachers convert such men to the way of salvation, they are crowned from the head and top of the mountains, that is, from the princes of the wicked, because from the labor of the [0652C] contest grows the crown of glory.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 9.-- Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, my bride, thou hast wounded my heart. ⟦s2⟧ In the wounding of the heart is understood the greatness of Christ's love toward the Church. ⟦s3⟧ In one *400* of thine eyes, and in one hair of thy neck. ⟦s4⟧ In the holy unity of the teachers, that is, of the eyes; and in the devout unity of the subject people, that is, of the hairs.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 10.-- How beautiful are thy breasts, my sister, my bride. ⟦s2⟧ Again, by the name of breasts are expressed the holy teachers, because of the consolation of the weak and the sustaining of little ones, which is done with milk. ⟦s3⟧ Thy breasts are more beautiful than wine. ⟦s4⟧ The sweetness of grace is more beautiful than the austerity of the law. ⟦s5⟧ And the odor of thine ointments above all aromatic spices. ⟦s6⟧ The most delightful fame of the faith, spread [ Cod. ⟦s7⟧ Vat., [0652D] diffusae] through the whole world, is broader than that of the old law and of the Fathers [in it], which is confined within Judea alone.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 11.-- Thy lips, O bride, are a dripping honeycomb. ⟦s2⟧ A honeycomb is honey in wax: but honey in wax is the spiritual meaning of the divine oracles within the letter. ⟦s3⟧ Dripping honey, because almost every single sentence has manifold meanings. ⟦s4⟧ The lips of the bride are the teachers, who make plain the manifold meanings contained in the sacred letters. ⟦s5⟧ Honey and milk are under thy tongue. ⟦s6⟧ By milk is signified the instruction of little ones, by honey the stronger doctrine of the perfect. ⟦s6⟧ Under the tongue, that is, in the meditation of the heart. ⟦s7⟧ And the odor of thy garments [0653A] is as the odor of frankincense. ⟦s8⟧ The garments of the Church are her works, which are compared to the odor of frankincense, because all the holy things the Church does for the Lord render through them the office of prayers.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 12.-- A garden enclosed is my sister, my bride, a garden enclosed, a fountain sealed. ⟦s2⟧ The enclosed garden is the Church, which brings forth the manifold shoots of spiritual works. ⟦s3⟧ She is a fountain, because she overflows with saving doctrine; enclosed, because fortified by the Lord's protection she endures; sealed, by the word of faith.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 13.-- Thy shoots are a paradise of pomegranates with the fruits of apples. ⟦s2⟧ Through the watering of holy baptism the holy Church sends forth from herself a paradise of pomegranates, that is, of the holy martyrs. ⟦s3⟧ With the fruits of apples, that is, with the fruit of the holy [0653B] virtues.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 14.-- Cypress with spikenard, spikenard and saffron. ⟦s2⟧ Cypress is an aromatic tree, signifying the blessing of heavenly grace. ⟦s3⟧ Spikenard expresses the figure of the Lord's passion, saffron the fervor of charity. ⟦s4⟧ Cypress is joined to spikenard, when divine grace strengthens us to suffer for Christ. ⟦s5⟧ Again, spikenard is joined to saffron, when out of charity we willingly undergo death for Christ. ⟦s6⟧ Sweet cane and cinnamon. ⟦s7⟧ Sweet cane, which is also cassia [ Cod. ⟦s8⟧ Vat., casia], is an aromatic tree, but a small one, and therefore signifies the humble in spirit. ⟦s9⟧ Likewise cinnamon signifies those who despise themselves, which is itself also a short tree, but fragrant and sweet; for humility has great praise and sweetness with God. ⟦s10⟧ With [0653C] all the trees of Lebanon. ⟦s11⟧ As sweet cane and cinnamon show the humble thoughts of the saints, so the trees of Lebanon show their lofty actions. ⟦s12⟧ Myrrh and aloe are aromatic trees, which express the continence of the flesh. ⟦s13⟧ With all the chief ointments. ⟦s14⟧ That is, with the more excellent graces of the virtues. ⟦s15⟧ And beautiful is the conjunction of these trees with the ointments, because while we restrain the flesh from wantonness, it follows that we receive the greater gifts of the spirit.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 15.-- A fountain of gardens, a well of living waters, which flow with force. ⟦s2⟧ The Church is both — a fountain of gardens, because she brings forth spiritual fruits; and a well of living waters, because of the hidden mysteries, which are opened to the saints through the revelation of the Holy Spirit [alone]. ⟦s3⟧ Of living waters, because of the divine oracles, [0653D] which proceed from the invisible treasures of divine grace. ⟦s4⟧ From Lebanon. ⟦s5⟧ He speaks of the Church herself, who is both white through the cleanness of faith, and high through the glory of virtues.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERSE 16.-- Arise, O north wind, and come, O south wind, and blow through my garden. ⟦s2+3⟧ By the north wind are signified the adversities of the world, by the south wind its allurements, because by a twofold assault the Church is tested. "Arise" is the voice of one permitting, not of one commanding. ⟦s4⟧ And let its aromatic spices flow. ⟦s5⟧ That is, let the constancy of the virtues scatter wondrous odors. ⟦s6⟧ Let my beloved come into his garden. ⟦s7⟧ Let the Lord come into his Church, and himself keep her everywhere unspotted, and make her fruitful with the harvest of faith [ Cod. ⟦s8⟧ Vat., fecundam]. ⟦s9⟧ And let him eat the fruit of his apples. ⟦s10⟧ And let him gladly [0654A] look upon and thankfully receive the works of his saints. ⟦s10⟧ *401*
