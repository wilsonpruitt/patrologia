## CHAPTER V.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- Come into my garden, my sister, my spouse. ⟦s2⟧ Come, he says, most often into my Church; and I come to correct, to help, to strengthen. ⟦s3⟧ I have gathered my myrrh with my spices. ⟦s4⟧ By myrrh, suffering or mortification; by the spices, all the virtues are expressed. ⟦s5⟧ He reaps the myrrh with the spices when he leads the martyrs, together with the rest of the elect, to the ripeness of their rewards. ⟦s6⟧ I have eaten the honeycomb with my honey, I have drunk my wine with my milk. ⟦s7⟧ By the honeycomb and the wine the preachers are understood; by the honey and the milk, the hearers; and the inward Judge approves and rewards both. ⟦s8⟧ Eat, O friends, drink and be inebriated, most dear ones. ⟦s9⟧ Friends, by doing [0654B] what I command; most dear, by embracing me with entire charity. ⟦s10⟧ I beseech you, thus fill your hearts with the deeds of the saints, as with a choice banquet.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- I sleep. ⟦s2⟧ By the gift of God's grace, in the peace of the present life [ Al., Ecclesiae] I worship him. ⟦s3⟧ And my heart waketh. ⟦s4⟧ The more tranquilly I am free from outward assaults, the more deeply I see with inward sight [ Cod. ⟦s5⟧ Vat., eo altius intus video] how good God is. ⟦s6⟧ The voice of my beloved knocking. ⟦s7⟧ He knocks at the door of the heart when the Lord rouses us to advance in virtues. ⟦s8⟧ Open to me, my sister, my love, my dove, my undefiled. ⟦s9⟧ That is, open your heart to me. ⟦s10⟧ My sister, because my co-heir. ⟦s11⟧ My love, because privy to my secrets [ Cod. ⟦s12⟧ Vat., arcanis meis]. ⟦s13⟧ My dove, because illumined by the gift of my Spirit. ⟦s14⟧ My undefiled, because alone worthy [0654C] of my regard. ⟦s15⟧ For my head is full of dew, and my locks of the drops of the nights. ⟦s16⟧ The head of Christ is God; the locks are the gatherings of the faithful. ⟦s17⟧ In the dew and the drops of the nights is shown the charity growing cold in many, which they ought to have had toward God and neighbor. ⟦s18⟧ Therefore the Lord necessarily rouses each of the faithful to the zeal [ Al., officium] of preaching. ⟦s19⟧ To whom, being summoned to the labor of teaching, the Church replies:

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- I have put off my coat, how shall I put it on? ⟦s2⟧ As if she said openly: I have forsaken worldly affairs for your sake, how shall I take them up again? ⟦s3⟧ I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them? ⟦s4⟧ By the tears of secret compunction I have already washed away earthly thoughts; how shall I again be polluted with the filth of the world [ Al., immundis]? — for the office of preaching can scarcely exist without worldly occupation [ Al., praeoccupatione] [0654D].

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- My beloved put his hand through the opening. ⟦s2⟧ For the beloved put his hand through the opening when the Lord invisibly kindles us to the work of virtues by hidden compunction, and calls back to our memory how, and how great things, descending from the bosom of the Father, he suffered for us. ⟦s3⟧ And my belly trembled at his touch. ⟦s4⟧ The spouse, recalling these things in her inmost conscience, wholly trembles, accusing the sloth of repose, hastening to the labor of preaching.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- I rose up to open to my beloved. ⟦s2⟧ That is, to preach the word of the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ My hands dropped with myrrh; my fingers were full of the [choicest ] myrrh. ⟦s4⟧ For by the hands works, by the fingers discretion [0655A] is signified; by the myrrh, continence and sufferings; which is then most approved when, solely for the charity of God and neighbor, either continence [is practiced], or sufferings are undergone.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- I opened the bolt of my door to my beloved. ⟦s2⟧ He opens the bolt to the Bridegroom who [opens the temple of his breast at the coming of divine inspiration, that is], makes it worthy of his visitation and indwelling. ⟦s3⟧ But he had turned aside and passed on. ⟦s4⟧ Because to no one in this life is granted the full vision of God, as in the life to come, therefore the beloved is said to pass on — that is, he shows himself to be seen and enjoyed more fully in the future. ⟦s5⟧ My soul melted when he spoke. ⟦s6⟧ The more sweetly, she says, I received the voice and nearness of my beloved, the more loftily whatever was cold in me grew warm with charity [0655B] [and was melted ( Cod. ⟦s7⟧ Vat. et quidquid rigidum liquefuit)]. ⟦s8⟧ I sought him, and found him not; I called, and he did not answer me. ⟦s9⟧ Because the gift of compunction and of inmost sweetness is not in the will of him who wills, but in the mercy of him who gives; therefore they do not always possess it equally, since God does not offer himself to those laboring in exile in the same way as to those reigning in the fatherland.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- The keepers that went about the city found me, they struck me, and they wounded me; the keepers of the walls took away my cloak from me. ⟦s2⟧ The keepers of the city, that is of the Church, [are] the holy teachers, who with diligent preaching go about *402* the hearts of individuals, and wound them with the darts of heavenly love, and, that they may burn the more, set them aflame, and withdraw from them the covering of their old [0655C] manner of life.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, if you find my beloved [Cod. ⟦s2⟧ Vat. om. meum], that you tell him that [Cod. ⟦s3⟧ Vat. quia] I languish with love. ⟦s4⟧ Deservedly the spouse languishes, when, struck by the sword of the word of God, she puts off the earthly garment; and she adjures the daughters of Jerusalem, that is, the souls worthy of God, to refer the greatness of their love to God, and to ask for themselves the heavenly help for beholding his glory.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- What manner of one is thy beloved of a beloved, O fairest among women? what manner of one is thy beloved of a beloved, that thou hast so adjured us? ⟦s2⟧ I beseech thee, since thou hast so adjured me, that with the love by which thou sayest thou languishest, thou make me too burn through the word of preaching; [0655D] and show me what manner of one thy beloved is on that side by which he may be loved, not feared — because perfect charity casteth out fear [n: (I Joan. IV, 18)] .

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- My beloved is white and ruddy, chosen out of thousands. ⟦s2⟧ White, because without sin; ruddy with the blood of his passion; chosen out of thousands, because he alone is the mediator of God and men.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- His head is the finest gold. ⟦s2⟧ The head of Christ is God, who alone is good and best. ⟦s3⟧ His hair is as the high branches of palms, black as a raven. ⟦s4⟧ The hair is the throngs of the saints, who cleave to God in faithful service. ⟦s5⟧ Raised on high as palms, because of their eminence [ Cod. ⟦s6⟧ Vat. om. eminentiam] before God. ⟦s7⟧ Black, because of [0656A] their being despised among men. ⟦s8⟧ Or: raised on high because of victory; black because of afflictions.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- His eyes are as doves upon the streams of waters — they are the holy teachers, through whom the Church sees what is right; who are well [compared to] doves, because of their innocence [ Cod. ⟦s2⟧ Vat. om. innocentiam] and simplicity; and are compared to streams of waters because of the divine graces. ⟦s3⟧ Which are washed with milk, and sit beside the fullest streams. ⟦s4⟧ He calls them washed with milk, that is, cleansed by the sweetest grace. ⟦s5⟧ And they sit beside the fullest streams, that is, the abundance of all spiritual gifts. ⟦s6⟧ In the streams may be signified the learning of the old law, and in the fullest streams the perfection of the gospel teaching, because the preachers bring forth out of their treasures things old and new [n: (Matth. XIII, 52)] .

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0656B]VERS. 13.-- His cheeks are as little beds of spices. ⟦s2⟧ In the cheeks of our Savior are expressed at once his modesty [ Cod. ⟦s3⟧ Vat., modesta pietas] and his severity. ⟦s4⟧ In the little beds of spices are signified the virtues, and the sweetness, and the fame of his glory. ⟦s5⟧ Planted by the perfumers: his lips are lilies dropping the first myrrh. ⟦s6+7⟧ By the prophets and the apostles; these [narrating] the secrets of his coming incarnation [ Cod. ⟦s6+7⟧ Vat., futura], those, narrating its accomplished deeds. ⟦s8⟧ His lips are the words of his teaching; lilies, because they promise the charity of the heavenly kingdom; dropping the first myrrh, because they preach that one must come to it through the contempt of present pleasures.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- His hands are turned and golden. ⟦s2⟧ The hands, that is, his works; because what he taught in words, he fulfilled in deeds. ⟦s3⟧ Turned on a lathe, because they hold in themselves the whole rule of justice [0656C], as it is said: It behooveth me to fulfil all justice [n: (Matth. IV, 15)] . ⟦s4⟧ And the hands are golden, because all that he did as man, he accomplished by the glory of his divinity. ⟦s5⟧ Full of hyacinths. ⟦s6⟧ Which rouse us to the hope and love of heavenly things; because the hyacinth [ Cod. ⟦s7⟧ Vat., in hyacintis] is a gem of an airy color. ⟦s8⟧ His belly is of ivory, set with sapphires. ⟦s9⟧ The belly of the beloved therefore signifies the frailty of his humanity; the ivory, the beauty of chastity; the sapphire, the loftiness of heavenly virtues. ⟦s10⟧ Set with sapphires, because partly his human frailty is understood in hunger, temptation, weariness, death; partly his divine loftiness in miracles, in the resurrection and the glory of the ascension.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- His legs are pillars of marble. ⟦s2⟧ By the word "legs" are intimated the journeys [ Al., munera] of Christ's incarnation [0656D], which are compared to pillars of marble on account of their firmness and uprightness. ⟦s3⟧ Which are set upon bases of gold. ⟦s4⟧ Because whatever was done through him or concerning him, all was ordained by the counsel of divine foreknowledge [ Cod. ⟦s5⟧ Vat., provisionis; al., promissionis] before the ages of the world. ⟦s5⟧ His appearance is as of Lebanon; chosen as the cedars. ⟦s6⟧ As Lebanon by the loftiness and beauty of its trees surpasses other mountains, so the Lord Christ surpasses all the saints by the loftiness of his merits and the grace of his virtues. ⟦s7⟧ Chosen as the cedars. ⟦s8⟧ As it surpasses in its dignity the other trees that grow in Lebanon, so Christ transcends in his glory all who are born to life in the Church.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0657A]VERS. 16.-- His throat is most sweet. ⟦s2⟧ In the throat is recalled the inward sweetness of his words, which he who tastes hungers not. ⟦s3⟧ And wholly to be desired. ⟦s4⟧ What more *403* do you seek? ⟦s5⟧ He is wholly to be desired, because wholly God and wholly man, on whom the angels also desire to look. ⟦s6⟧ God in the majesty of the Father; man in the virginity of the mother; in the one the Creator, in the other the Savior. ⟦s7⟧ Such is my beloved, and such is my friend, O daughters of Jerusalem. ⟦s8⟧ The more devotedly each one loves God, the more familiarly he has God as friend; and it is necessary that you understand him to be such, if you wish to have him as a friend.
