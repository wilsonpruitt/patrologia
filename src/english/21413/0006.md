## CHAPTER VI.

VERS. 1.-- Whither is thy beloved gone, O fairest among women! For the sake of the beauty of the song [ Cod. Vat., carnis] [0657B] the persons speaking are varied; yet nevertheless they signify the Church of Christ, which is expressed by the word "spouse." Fairest among women. Fair are the individual churches of the saints, but fairest is the whole company of the entire holy Church throughout all the world. Whither hath thy beloved turned aside, and we will seek him. He who once dwelt on earth in bodily form — tell whither he has turned aside, that we may follow [ Al., quaeramus] him with thee.

VERS. 2.-- My beloved is gone down into his garden, to the little bed of spice. As if she had said, he has ascended thither whence he descended. Into the garden, that is, into his Church, that he might water it with the fountain of his grace, as a little bed of spices, that it might bud with the flowers of virtues. That he may feed in the gardens, and gather the lilies. He feeds in the gardens while he delights in the pious labors of the saints; and he gathers the lily [0657C] while he leads those who attain to the perfect [ Cod. Vat., perfectorum] whiteness of merits into the heavenly kingdoms.

VERS. 3.-- I to my beloved, and my beloved to me, who feedeth among the lilies. I prepare for my beloved a place of dwelling in myself; and he for me with himself, who ever feeds among the holy desires of chaste minds. Thus far it is the voice of the holy Church, praising and seeking the Bridegroom; now what he, being sought, replies, is added below.

VERS. 4.-- Thou art fair, my [love], sweet and comely as Jerusalem. The holy Church is called sweet and comely as Jerusalem, because the desire of her life is now likened to the vision of inmost peace. Terrible as an army set in array. That is, united [ Al., [0657D] juncta] and knit together in charity, so that no place be opened to the enemy through the evil of discord, because only in the unity of peace are we terrible to the enemy.

VERS. 5.-- Turn away thy eyes from me, for they have made me flee away. That is, the eyes of thy mind. Do not seek to know me perfectly on the journey of thy pilgrimage, which cannot be; because the more intently thou seekest to know me, the more surely wilt thou understand me to be incomprehensible; and do not seek on the way the reward which is reserved for thee in the fatherland. Thy hair is as a flock of goats, which have appeared from Gilead.

VERS. 6.-- Thy teeth as flocks of sheep, which came up from the washing, all with twin young, and there is none barren among them. These little verses were expounded before, but repetition is a sign of firmness [0658A]. By the hair, the peoples; by the teeth, the teachers; by the twin young, the two precepts of charity are understood.

VERS. 7.-- Thy cheeks are as the rind of a pomegranate, apart from thy eyes. The cheeks of the holy Church are the spiritual Fathers, who are wonderful in virtues, and venerable in character, and not ashamed to glory in the cross of Christ. And these things that are seen in her are very great, but far greater are those that are not seen and are reserved for the future.

VERS. 8.-- There are sixty queens, and eighty concubines, and of young maidens there is no number. The queens are those who, out of love for the Bridegroom and with an eye to the heavenly reward, beget for the eternal King a [spiritual] [0658B] offspring, through the preaching of truth and faith and the font of holy baptism. The concubines are those who teach Christ, by preaching or baptizing, for carnal ends only [ Cod. Vat., quae carnali solummodo commodo]. And therefore the former are signified by the perfection of the number six, multiplied by the decalogue [ Al., denarium]; the latter, by the imperfection of the number eight, not sincerely keeping the decalogue. The young maidens are the souls lately reborn by the grace of Christ, whose sum, because of the multitude [ Cod. Vat., prae multitudine] of the heavenly citizens, transcends number.

*404* VERS. 9.-- One art [Cod. Vat., est] thou, my dove, my perfect one. Namely the holy and universal Church throughout all the world. She is one to her mother, the chosen one of her that bore her [Cod. Vat., matris . . . genitricis]. That is, the heavenly Jerusalem, which is the mother of us all, whence [0658C] the grace of the Holy Spirit descends to us, through which we are reborn to God. The daughters of Zion saw her and proclaimed her most blessed: the queens and the concubines praised her. Those whom he before called young maidens, he now names daughters. The queens and concubines are the same as before, who all praise the catholic Church.

VERS. 10.-- Who is she that goeth forth as the rising dawn? The Church goes forth as the dawn, because now the sun of true light, having risen in her, is shown to the world after the darkness of long ignorance. Fair as the moon; because, illumined by the sun of justice, she lights up the night of the world. [ Chosen as the sun ], because she bears the image of her Maker in all justice, holiness, and piety [ Cod. Vat., veritate]. Terrible as an army set in array. [0658D] Terrible to the powers of the air [ Cod. Vat., aereis] in the unity of charity, faith, and hope.

VERS. 11.-- I went down into the garden of nuts, to see the fruits of the valley. For the garden of nuts is the Church of the present age [ Cod. Vat., Ecclesia praesens], where we do not at all see one another's consciences, but when the shell of the body is broken by temptation, the taste of inward sweetness will appear. The fruits of the valley are the fruit of humility. The holy Church goes down through the holy teachers, who make progress toward good fruits, or who still need the watering of teaching. That I might look whether the vineyard had flourished. That I might look whether the pursuits of virtues had flourished. And whether the pomegranates had budded. Whether any were prepared, after the example of the Lord's passion, to shed their own blood.

[0659A]VERS. 12.-- I knew not: my soul troubled me because of the chariots of Aminadab. I knew not that the gifts of spiritual grace were in thee, O spouse, but my soul troubled me because of the bringing in [ Cod. Vat., inductionem] of the New Testament, and of the gospel chariot, by which [ Cod. Vat., quia] Aminadab (which is interpreted, "the willing one of my people") — that is, Christ — is borne throughout the whole world; to whom the Church, consoling, presently replies.

VERS. 13.-- Return, return, O Shulammite; return, return, that we may look upon thee. Be not troubled, but return to the acknowledgment of thy Redeemer, who was so often promised to thee in the prophets and the law. Return by purity of faith, by perfection of works. O Shulammite, that is, captive — now that the bond of unbelief is utterly scraped away [0659B] [ Verba penitus abraso omissa in Cod. Vat. ] — return to thy Redeemer, that thou mayest be saved.
