## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6+s7+s8+s9⟧ VERS. 1.-- Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother? This is the voice of the ancient just, desiring the coming of Christ in the flesh. Sucking the breasts of my mother, that is, to be born and nourished in the synagogue, according to the nature of the human condition. That I may find thee outside. The beloved was within, while in the beginning was the Word; outside, when the Word was made flesh. And kiss thee. That is, that I may see face to face, and speak mouth to mouth. And now let no one despise me. Before the coming of Christ, the Church was only within the narrow bounds of Judaea; after the ascension, it was spread abroad and venerable in all the world.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0661D]VERS. 2.-- I will take hold of thee. ⟦s2⟧ I will receive thee, as thou comest, with ready and faithful devotion. ⟦s3⟧ And I will lead thee to the house of my mother. ⟦s4⟧ When the dispensation of the flesh is accomplished, I will lead thee, returning, with joyful eyes into the house of the mother, where the heavenly Jerusalem is our mother. ⟦s5⟧ There thou shalt teach me. ⟦s6⟧ Thou shalt make me hope for better gifts than I had had in the law. ⟦s7⟧ And I will give thee a cup of spiced wine. ⟦s8⟧ That is, fervent love, adorned with the various spices of virtues. ⟦s9⟧ And the new wine [Cod. ⟦s10⟧ Vat., mistum] of my pomegranates. ⟦s11⟧ That is, the glorious triumph of the holy martyrs, who with most fervent charity do not hesitate to pass over to thee through sword and flames.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0662A]VERS. 3.-- His left hand under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me. ⟦s2⟧ The left hand signifies the gifts of Christ's incarnation; and the right hand expresses the future joys of the saints with Christ.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4, 5.-- I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir not up, nor make [the beloved] to awake, until she herself will. ⟦s2⟧ Who is this that cometh up from the desert, flowing with delights? ⟦s3⟧ The voice of the Synagogue, wondering how the Church had come up from the desert of heathendom into the sudden embraces of the Bridegroom. ⟦s4⟧ Flowing with delights, that is, with the beauty of all gifts. ⟦s5⟧ Leaning upon her beloved. ⟦s6⟧ Referring all that she has to the grace of the beloved, leaning upon him alone. ⟦s7⟧ Under the apple tree I raised thee up. ⟦s8⟧ The Bridegroom himself answered the Synagogue on behalf of the spouse. ⟦s9⟧ Under the apple tree I roused thee, that is, under the tree of the cross I called thee back [0662B] [ Cod. ⟦s10⟧ Vat., renovavi] from perpetual death, as the apostles and the rest of the elect from Judaea. ⟦s11⟧ There thy mother was corrupted; there she that bore thee was violated. ⟦s12⟧ That is, the greater part of the people, by denying Christ and choosing Barabbas, was reprobated unto the cross.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- Set me as a seal upon thy heart, as a seal upon thy arm. ⟦s2⟧ We bind a seal upon the arm or the finger for the remembrance of any matter. ⟦s3⟧ By the heart, thought; and by the arm, work is signified. ⟦s4⟧ If thou wouldst have me as Bridegroom, let there be within charity in unfeigned faith, and without devout working. ⟦s5⟧ For love is strong as death, jealousy hard as hell. ⟦s6⟧ My love toward thee is strong even unto death, O Synagogue! but thy jealousy against me was hard as hell. ⟦s7⟧ But [0662C] turn thy jealousy into love, and thou shalt be to me spouse and sister and friend. ⟦s8⟧ Its lamps are lamps of fire and of flames. ⟦s9⟧ The lamps of love are the hearts of the faithful. ⟦s10⟧ Of fire, because of the fervor of the heart; of flames, because of the efficacy of working [ Cod. ⟦s11⟧ Vat., orationis].

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- Many waters could not quench charity, neither shall the floods overwhelm it. ⟦s2⟧ By the many waters and the floods he means the assaults of temptations, which visibly or invisibly do not cease to attack the souls of the faithful, to which charity does not yield. ⟦s3⟧ If a man should give all the substance of his house for charity [Cod. ⟦s4⟧ Vat., dilectione], he shall despise it as nothing. ⟦s5⟧ But the saints, forsaking the substance of the whole world for the greatness of love, unless they also cast away their very selves, [0662D] profit nothing.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- Our sister is little, and hath no breasts; what shall we do to our sister in the day when she is to be spoken to? ⟦s2+3⟧ It signifies [ Cod. ⟦s2+3⟧ Vat. et al., designat] the first times of the Church being born from the gentiles, when as yet it was both small in number and less fit to preach the word *407* — as if the Bridegroom had said to the Synagogue concerning her: What shall we do to our sister in the day when she is to be spoken to? as if he said openly, small indeed in number is the Church of the gentiles, and not yet [ Al. add ad] is she sufficient to undertake the mystery [ Cod. ⟦s4⟧ Vat., ministerium] of the word; what then seems to thee, O Synagogue, is to be done concerning our sister in the day when she is to be spoken to, that is, to be led into the faith through the ministry [0663A] of the word? ⟦s5⟧ To the silent Synagogue the Bridegroom himself answered what ought to be done:

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- If she is a wall, let us build upon it bulwarks of silver. ⟦s2⟧ As if he said, if she has in herself any strong in faith, or bright in genius, or instructed in philosophy, we will add to them bulwarks of silver, that is, the knowledge of the divine Scriptures, that they may the more easily protect the weak and the unlearned. ⟦s3⟧ If she is a door, let us join it together with boards of cedar. ⟦s4⟧ If they be simple, yet eager with zeal for teaching, let us set before them the examples of the just of former times, that they may the more surely and effectively fulfill the work of teaching. ⟦s5⟧ The cedars signify the virtues of the saints, and the boards the breadth of charity. ⟦s6⟧ To these things the Church herself replies.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- I am a wall. ⟦s2⟧ I am framed together [0663B] of living stones, and united by the glue of charity, and built upon the immovable foundation. ⟦s3⟧ And my breasts as a tower, since I was made in his presence, that is, having most mighty teachers like a tower, who both know how to nourish the little ones and to drive off all the darts of the evil one. ⟦s4⟧ And this befell me from that time when Christ reconciled me, [and through him I found peace]. ⟦s5⟧ As one finding peace. ⟦s6⟧ Because he himself is the true peace, making both one.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- There was a vineyard for the peaceable one in her, who hath peoples. ⟦s2⟧ The vineyard, that is, the catholic Church abounding in the fruit of faith, was for the peaceable one, that is, for Christ, who pacified all things in heaven and on earth, in that congregation which possessed many peoples out of the whole world, not in Judaea only. ⟦s3⟧ He handed it over to keepers, to the prophets and [0663C] the apostles, or to the angelic dignities. ⟦s4⟧ A man brings for its fruit a thousand pieces of silver. ⟦s5⟧ That is, for the eternal reward of this vineyard, he brings a thousand pieces of silver; that is, for the acquisition of the heavenly kingdom, which is the fruit of the vineyard, he left all that is of the world. ⟦s6⟧ The number one thousand is put for perfection, that is [ F., illius], of him who [0664A] left all things. ⟦s7⟧ The pieces of silver are taken for all money.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- My vineyard is before me. ⟦s2⟧ And this is the voice of the Bridegroom. ⟦s3⟧ But even though I entrust thee to other keepers, yet I ever have thee in my presence, seeing and rewarding the devotion of thy labor in all things. ⟦s4⟧ Thy thousand are for the peaceable one. ⟦s5⟧ Thy thousand — understand, pieces of silver; as if he had said, those who for my love give up all their own, have them reserved in peace, signifying the simpler ones in the Church, who lose all their substance, designated by the number one thousand, for the charity of God. ⟦s6⟧ And two hundred to those who keep its fruits. ⟦s7⟧ That is, pieces of silver to those who keep its fruits; who are the holy teachers, who give up all things of the world, and cease not to labor in the word of preaching. ⟦s8⟧ These are gifted, with me, with a double reward [0664B], which is signified by the number two hundred.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the friends hearken. ⟦s2⟧ Because our discourse must be ended, hear from me this last farewell. ⟦s3⟧ Ever dwell [ Cod. ⟦s4⟧ Vat., habita] in the gardens of virtues, and know that the friends, that is, the angelic spirits and the souls of the saints, ever regard thee and rejoice in thy progress. ⟦s5⟧ Make me to hear thy voice. ⟦s6⟧ That is, let the voice of preaching, as much as thou art able, ever be heard by me. ⟦s7⟧ To these things the spouse replied:

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- Flee away, my beloved, and be like to a roe and to a young hart upon the mountains of spices. ⟦s2⟧ As if she said openly: since, appearing in the flesh, thou hast brought me the precepts and gifts of life, now, these being accomplished, return into [0664C] the bosom of the Father [ O my beloved, be like to a roe and to a young hart upon the mountains of spices ]. ⟦s3⟧ And let this be a comfort to me, that although I cannot behold thee with continual vision [ Cod. ⟦s4⟧ Vat., nequeo], thou mayest at least deign to console me with frequent visitation [ Cod. ⟦s5⟧ Vat., saltim crebra visione me consolari memento].
