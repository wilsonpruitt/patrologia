## A Book on How the Advent of the Lord Is to Be Celebrated

[1079B] Here begins the letter of Berno, abbot of the monastery of Reichenau, to Aribo, archbishop of Mainz, on how the Advent of the Lord ought to be celebrated in this year, when the Nativity of the Lord has fallen on the second weekday.

To ARIBO, archbishop, raised on high to the summit of the highest dignity, through whom in the Lord's threshing-floor the good measure of wheat is faithfully dispensed to his fellow servants: BERNO, though a worthless bondman of the Mother of God, wishes him for a preface that "Well done" of the good servant.

As often as we are less than agreed in the rite of the ecclesiastical order, we must have recourse to the counsel of the masters, through whom, as Scripture testifies, divine Wisdom searched out the depths of the rivers and brought hidden things into the light — the lawgiver also enjoining this: Ask thy father, and he will declare unto thee; thy elders, [1079C] and they will tell thee. For this reason we have judged that you above all, most devoted Father, are to be consulted about those matters which have come into doubt concerning the celebration of the Lord's Advent — you to whom the providence from above has granted such a fullness of knowledge that it has led you through the water of the divine Scriptures not only as far as the ankles or to the knees, or even as far as the loins, but has brought you all the way to that very torrent which you cannot cross over: For, he says, the deep waters of the torrent had swollen, which cannot be waded through. And though it cannot be crossed, yet one can come to it with the help of that very Wisdom of God which is signified by the torrent. Therefore touch in some measure the water of this torrent, which no mortal can cross, and offer some taste of understanding to our thirsting [1079D] breasts.

## CHAPTER ONE. The argument of the present question is set forth, and various opinions are brought forward, and it is established that no more than four weeks are to be given to the celebration of Advent.

[1080B]

That you may recognize the more plainly what we are asking about, we make known what nevertheless we by no means believe to have escaped you this long while: that as often as the Lord's Nativity has fallen on the second weekday, as in the present year, a great error pours itself through the churches of God — some saying that no more than four Lord's days ought to be celebrated together with the most sacred vigil of the Lord's Nativity itself; but others contending on the opposite side that the Lord's Advent ought rather to have four full weeks, dedicating the fifth Lord's day especially to the vigil of the Lord, having, so far as we have been able to investigate, no other argument or reason for their position, except that those four offices [1080C] which are found in the night Antiphonaries, and likewise the lessons of the holy Gospels and of the Apostle, may be more fully carried through.

Against these, others bring forward the fuller opinion of the ancients, resting on the reason of the truth — namely that of blessed Pope Gregory and of Jerome the priest — of whom the one, in the book of the Sacraments, that is, the Missal, gave these titles: The first Sunday of the Advent of the Lord; the second Sunday; the third Sunday; the fourth Sunday, which is nearest before the Nativity of the Lord. But the other, in the Lectionary, the book which he called the Comes, thus: The fourth Sunday before the Nativity of the Lord (which Amalarius the priest named the preparation of the Lord's Advent), then the fourth Sunday before the Nativity of the Lord; the third Sunday before the Nativity of the Lord; the second Sunday [1080D] before the Nativity of the Lord; the first Sunday before the Nativity of the Lord — so that, by an inverted manner, the one which in Jerome is called the first or nearest before the Nativity of the Lord is in Gregory called the fourth Sunday of the Advent of the Lord; [1081A] not unfittingly, as I think, on account of the five ages of this world in which the human race was held captive under the yoke of the devil, until by being born he should appear in the flesh through the womb of the untouched Virgin — he who by the victim of his own body should abolish the sins of the flesh and sanctify the beginning of the sixth age that followed. Or else because our inner man is corrupted through the five senses of the body, so that death works in us, as the prophet says: Death is come in through your windows; so that in these five weeks a lodging of the mind may be prepared for that king of ours, whom the prophetic lesson in that very fifth week announces as about to come, in these words: Behold, the days come, saith the Lord, and I will raise up to David a just branch.

Likewise, in the day Antiphonary, after three offices there [1081B] immediately follows the fast, and then the Sunday is vacant, so that from this it may be clearly proved how greatly they also fail in this matter who in the present year place after the vacant Sunday the fast which ought to have preceded it, beginning the Advent of the Lord on VI Kal. Decemb.

But if, guarding against this, they fast earlier, they nonetheless act against the institutions of the Fathers, who laid down that the fast of the tenth month is always to be celebrated on the Saturday nearest before the Nativity of the Lord. But the number four, which, as we noted above, St. Gregory set in the days of the fourth Sunday of the Advent of the Lord, celebrating it by such a mystery: that man, who consists of four elements, should through four weeks await the Advent of him whom of old he promised through the three orders of the books, namely of the law, of the prophets, of the psalms, [1081C] and through the fourth, that is, the beginning of the Gospel — the angel telling to holy Mary ever virgin the order of his conception and birth — and whom we know most certainly to have been presented to this world for the salvation of the human race. And lest so great an attestation of my littleness, which I have brought forward out of the opinion of others rather than my own, should seem to be lightly esteemed, let the sayings of our elders be set forth in the midst, so that from these too it may be clearly proved whose usage is held to be the more correct.
