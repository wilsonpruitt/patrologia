# Cruces — 21425, Berno of Reichenau, *Liber qualiter adventus Domini celebretur* (PL 150, cols 1079–1086)

## 1080C — `Ama larius`

Migne prints the name broken across a line as `Ama larius presbyter`. Read as
*Amalarius* (the same author cited by name at 1081C and quoted at length in
ch. II); rendered "Amalarius the priest." Broken type, not a variant.

## 1080C — the Jerome/*Comes* list

**Printed:** `Dominica quarta ante Natale Domini (quam Ama larius presbyter
praeparationem Dominici Adventus nominavit), deinde Dominica quarta ante Natale
Domini; Dominica tertia …`

*Dominica quarta* stands twice in succession. The first ought on the argument to
be *Dominica quinta* (the fifth Sunday = Amalarius' "preparation of the Lord's
Advent"; Berno's whole point is the five-Sunday reckoning of the *Comes* against
Gregory's four). Rendered **as printed**, with the repetition intact — the
correction is not made in the text. This is the single reading a human
read-through should check against another witness, since the numeral carries the
argument.

## Dating notation kept verbatim

`VI Kal. Decemb.` (1081B), `V Kal. Decemb.` / `III Nonas` (1083A), `XI Kal.
April.` / `XV Kal. Febr.` (1084C) are left in Migne's abbreviated Roman form,
unexpanded and unconverted, because the computistical argument is about the
notation itself. Likewise the ordinals of the Sundays (fourth/fifth) and the
arithmetic of 4 + 8 + 8 + 23 + 3 = 46 at 1084A are reproduced as reckoned, not
recomputed.

## 1084B–C — chant incipits

*Memento*, *Omnis terra*, *Adorate*, *In excelso throno*, *Domine, ne in ira*
are introit/responsory incipits, kept in Latin and italicized (they are titles of
chants, not quoted sentences). Migne does not italicize them; the italics are
editorial in the English only, consistent with the anchor's handling of titles.

## Unmarked scripture (no `[n:]` in this work)

Berno quotes without any note apparatus. All quotations are translated from the
Latin as printed, not from a conventional English version — notably
Deut. 32:7 (`Interroga patrem tuum`), the Ezekiel 47 waters (ankles / knees /
loins / uncrossable torrent), Jer. 9:21 (`Introivit mors per fenestras
vestras`), Jer. 23:5 (`Ecce dies veniunt … germen justum`), Rom. 13:11–14,
Ps. 24:1 (`Ad te levavi animam meam`), Isa. 32:20 (`Beati, qui seminatis super
omnes aquas`), Cant. 6:1–2, and Luke 24:42 (`assi piscis parte`) in Aribo's
reply.

## 1086A–B — Aribo's rescript

`sancti examinis sub vestro regimine mellificantis` — *examen* here is the
bee-swarm sense (the community "making honey"), continuing the honeycomb figure
of the next clause; rendered "the holy company that makes honey." Not corrupt,
but easy to misread as *examen* = examination.
