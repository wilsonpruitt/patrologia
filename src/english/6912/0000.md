## NOTICE.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *The printing being already finished, there were communicated to me some annotations and observations, by the zeal and kindness of that distinguished man the lord abbot Terribilini, which I judged should be added to the work in this place, lest I should defraud readers of the benefit of them.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *That learned man holds, then, in the first place, that Damasus was born at Rome, and he uses to confirm this opinion the same reasons which I have touched on. ⟦s2⟧ He observes also that there are many ancient writers, mentioned by Sarazani and [0417C] by Coustant, who treat of that pontiff and praise him: but that among them there is not so much as one who called him a Spaniard before the Catalogue of the Roman Pontiffs, which they say was compiled under Felix IV, or Anastasius, by whom they think that Catalogue was copied out — but he judges that the authority of neither of these writers is great. ⟦s3⟧ He then brings forward more recent authors from that renowned nation, and says that they rest on no solid foundation, and moreover fight among themselves about the place; lastly, from the Breviary of Évora printed in the year 1548, he copies out the collect and the lessons appointed for the Feast of St. ⟦s3⟧ Damasus. ⟦s4⟧ Here, then, in this place, is the whole of it gathered together.*

## From the Breviary of the Church of Évora in Lusitania, printed in type in the year 1548, p. 837.ON THE FEAST OF SAINT DAMASUS, POPE AND CONFESSOR.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0417D]

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Collect.* --Thy mercy, O Lord, we beseech thee, mercifully bestow upon us, by the intercession of thy blessed confessor and pontiff Damasus: and be favorable to our offenses by his prayers. ⟦s2⟧ P.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Lesson I.* --Blessed Damasus, the first of this name, a Spaniard by nation, of Guimarães by native place, from the province of Braga, his father being Antonius, succeeded in the pontificate — according to Jerome in the Ecclesiastical History — Liberius, but according to others Felix II, in the year of the Lord 369, under the emperors Valentinian and Valens. ⟦s3⟧ He sat eighteen years, two months, ten days. ⟦s4⟧ T.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Lesson II.* --But Ursicinus, or, as others read, [0418B] Ursinus, a deacon of the Roman Church, not bearing that Damasus was preferred before him, broke out into such madness that, having persuaded a certain unskilled and rustic bishop, and having gathered a band of turbulent and seditious men, he extorted for himself to be made bishop in the basilica which is called Sicininus, the laws, the order, and the tradition being overthrown. ⟦s3⟧ And so, since the matter was carried on not only by votes but also by force and arms, many fell on both sides. ⟦s4+5⟧ Ammianus Marcellinus, a historian of that time, relates that the bodies of one hundred and thirty-seven men slain in one day were found in the basilica of Sicininus. [0418C] At length, by the consent of the priests and the people, Damasus was confirmed; and Ursicinus was cast out of the City and transferred to the Church of Naples. ⟦s6⟧ T.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Lesson III.* --But Damasus, accused of adultery through envy, cleared himself in a synod held before forty-four bishops; by whom he was acquitted as innocent, and his accusers, the deacons Concordius and Callixtus, were condemned and cast out of the Church. ⟦s3⟧ Certainly blessed Jerome, in the letter to Pammachius, calls him not only an excellent man and learned in the Scriptures, but a virgin also, and the teacher of a virgin Church. ⟦s4⟧ T.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Lesson IV.* --At last, the Church being at peace, Damasus, delighting in literary leisure, wrote the Lives of all the Pontiffs [0418D] who had been before him, and sent them to Jerome; and when, searching out many bodies of the saints, he had found them, he adorned their tombs with verses. ⟦s3⟧ For he had an elegant talent in composing verses. ⟦s4⟧ He composed many short works in heroic verse, and likewise many Letters. ⟦s5⟧ T.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Lesson V.* --He greatly increased the temples and the divine worship. ⟦s3⟧ For he built two basilicas, and dedicated the one beside the theater, the other on the Ardeatine Way, at the catacombs; and he adorned with verses, for the memory of posterity, the Platonia, where the bodies of the apostles Peter and Paul had once lain, and the bodies of the saints buried in that place. ⟦s4⟧ T.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Lesson VI.* --And the basilica which he had founded in honor of Saint Lawrence not far from the theater of Pompey, [0419A] he adorned with the greatest gifts: a silver dish of twenty pounds' weight, a silver bucket of fifteen pounds, an embossed silver cup of ten pounds; five silver chalices, five silver crowns. ⟦s3⟧ He gave also as a gift the houses set about the basilica, and likewise the Papyrian estate in the territory of Ferentinum, and the Antonian estate, and the baths set not far from the temple. ⟦s4⟧ T.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Lesson VII.* --He, a council having been held at Rome, condemned for heresy Apollinaris of Laodicea and Timothy his disciple: as we read in the Letter sent by him to the bishops of the East. ⟦s1+2⟧ We read also his other synodal letters: as to the bishops throughout Illyricum, and to Paulinus bishop of Thessalonica, against various heresies. ⟦s3⟧ He appointed that the psalms should be sung in the Church by turns day and night, [0419B] the choir being divided into two parts, and that at the end of the psalms there should be sung, Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Spirit. ⟦s4⟧ He commanded also that at the beginning of the celebration of Masses the confession should be said. ⟦s5⟧ T.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Lesson VIII.* --At his instance also Jerome corrected the psalter according to the seventy translators, and divided it by vigils: to whose writings Damasus first gave authority. ⟦s3⟧ For then the Bibles of Jerome began to be read, whereas before the writings of the seventy translators alone were held in esteem. ⟦s4⟧ Tu.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Lesson IX.* --He held ordinations five times, in which he created thirty-one priests, eleven deacons, and bishops in various places to the number of sixty-two. ⟦s3⟧ He died at the age of eighty, on the third of the Ides of [0419C] December, in the second year of Arcadius Augustus. ⟦s4⟧ He was buried in his own basilica on the Ardeatine Way, beside his mother and his sister, and the pontificate was vacant thirty-one days. ⟦s5⟧ Tu.

## *At the end of this Breviary these words are read:* TO THE PRAISE OF ALMIGHTY GOD AND OF THE EVER UNDEFILED VIRGIN MARY, HERE ENDS

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The Breviary of the divine offices, according to the rite of the holy Church of Évora, once more emended, corrected, altered, and now made far more orderly and elegant, by the command and authority of the most reverend Father in Christ and most illustrious prince and lord, the lord Henry, cardinal of the Holy Roman Church of the title of the Four Crowned Saints, Infante of Portugal, [0419D] and first archbishop of that same Church.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ AT LISBON, *at the shop of* LUÍS RODRIGUES, *bookseller, royal printer, in the year from the birth of Christ one thousand five hundred and forty-eight, in the month of April.*
