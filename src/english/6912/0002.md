## AN ANALYSIS OF CERTAIN OPINIONS OF THE ABBOT TERRIBILINI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0421B]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6+s7+s8+s9⟧ On poem IX, *On the Catacombs of the Holy Apostles*, he does not doubt that the blessed Apostles at one time *dwelt* in them, as Damasus says — that is, were placed there; but he conjectures that they were carried thither immediately from the martyrdom itself, and were laid there, otherwise than others hold, who write that blessed Peter was buried at the foot of the Vatican, and blessed Paul on the Ostian Way. But he thinks they were translated from the Catacombs to the place of their passion long before the pontificate of Cornelius and Xystus, because Caius, a Roman priest, before the second century had yet run out, writes that the *trophies of the Apostles met* the religious devotion of the Christians *on the Vatican and on the Ostian Way*. For my own part I shall not touch this question, which men far more learned have scarcely disentangled to this day. I will add only this: that I can perceive no reason why that Damasian *little verse*, of which I gave warning in the *notes*, ought not to express that marvelous deed [0421C] which Gregory the Great relates. If, therefore, there is anyone who does not judge so, let him assign to the same passage, I ask, some other meaning equally apt. Let him declare also to what good purpose Damasus warned us thus in another, earlier little verse: The East sent the disciples — which we willingly grant,
⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Or could this perhaps ever have been unknown? ⟦s2⟧ But if the men of the East did not take pains to seize for themselves and lay claim to the *trophies* of the blessed Apostles, let him bring forward some reason why Rome rather deserved to *defend* her own citizens;
⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ and let him show also what other force this same word *defend* has. ⟦s2⟧ I know that Pearson, at the year 258, no. 2, rashly wrote that *the History which Gregory relates is worthy of no credit.* ⟦s3⟧ But let that be permitted to him alone, and to such as he.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ On poem X. The poem which I set out in that place from the Palatine codex of Gruter, and judged could be fitted to St. ⟦s1+2⟧ Stephen, Pope and Martyr, the learned man also judges to be the offspring of Damasus; but he conjectures that in it Xystus, Pope and Martyr, is praised, [0421D] whom we know from Cyprian to have been *put to death in the cemetery*. ⟦s3⟧ But the poem itself is against this conjecture, since from it it is plain that Stephen alone then fell a martyr, all the rest, taken with him in the cemetery, being let go unharmed. ⟦s4⟧ On the contrary, with Xystus *four deacons* were slain, and perhaps others, which the learned man himself observes on Poem XXXIII. ⟦s5+6⟧ This poem, therefore, with which we are dealing, suits the former most holy martyr and pontiff rather than the other.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ So the same Damasus, poem XX: By your blood you changed your fatherland, etc.
⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Now a dweller of the Lord.*


⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ A likely conjecture, and reading; and if it be kept, the *Saturninus* with whom this poem deals will be that same man who had been held at Rome under Decius, praised in Cyprian, epp. 20, 21, and afterward a martyr. ⟦s2⟧ He observes besides that in the Martyrology of Jerome, and in the Gelasian Sacramentary, at III kal. ⟦s3⟧ December, one reads *At Rome, of Thraso* — that is, in the cemetery — *Saturninus, Chrysanthus and Daria, Maurus, and* LXXII *others*. ⟦s4⟧ Since, then, Damasus had praised all of them as blessed martyrs, would he have passed over those LXXII unpraised? ⟦s5⟧ But because a slip is easy in [0422C] copying out numbers, the distinguished man rightly conjectures that to them belong the *little verses* which I reported at *no.* 2 in the *Appendix*, and that Damasus himself is their author.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For the same reason he thinks that poem XXII, *On an uncertain Greek martyr*, pertains *to St. ⟦s1⟧ Hermes*, who is known to have been laid *in the cemetery of Bassilla*, called also that of Hermes, and, as it seems, in nearly the same *place as Protus and Hyacinthus*. ⟦s2⟧ For thus the Anonymous of Mabillon: *on the Pincian way, Pamphilus, Bassilla, Protus, Hyacinthus, Hermes*; and the Gelasian Sacramentary at V kal. ⟦s3⟧ September.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ On Poem XXXIII, *On his own Sepulchre*: Cyprian had written, *epistle 82 of the Verona edition*, that Xystus was put to death in the cemetery, *and with him four deacons*. ⟦s3⟧ So Baluze and Vignoli, from manuscript codices. ⟦s4⟧ Against this Fell reads, and with him *Quartus*; and he sets his mark upon the Martyrologies for joining several companions to the same Sixtus, namely *Felix*, or *Felicissimus, Agapitus, Donatianus, Faustinus, Praetextatus*, and others: and that reading pleased Ruinart and Castellanus also. ⟦s5⟧ The exact man therefore observes [0422D] that the former reading is confirmed by the testimony of Damasus, who attributes several *companions* to Sixtus, which argument the Bollandists also, after Baronius, used at the 6th day of August. ⟦s6⟧ To this opinion indeed a still greater force accrues from this, that Florentinius and Sollerius observed that there was no mention of this *Quartus* before Ado and Usuard. ⟦s7⟧ Whatever, then, is the case concerning him, what can be asserted for certain is this: that the blessed pontiff cannot, without injury, be stripped of the long-standing possession of his companions, whom, as the Leonine Sacramentary says in his Mass, *he was urging on, as the sons of his teaching, to earn the same glory*; although, together with that same most distinguished man, I would exclude from their number *Praetextatus*, who, when the other Martyrologies and Sacramentaries are inspected, [0423A] is mentioned in the Martyrology of Florentinius not so as to denote some martyr, but the famous cemetery called after him, in which the memory of those Martyrs was kept. ⟦s8⟧ But who, and how many, were Sixtus' companions in martyrdom, the Bollandist fellows diligently inquire; and whoever consults them will have done a thing worth the labor.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ names? ⟦s1⟧ It is hard that I should be able to unfold them:
⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ so great peoples of the just has impious fury swallowed up!
