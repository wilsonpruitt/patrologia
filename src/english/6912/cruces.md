# Cruces — 6912, *Addenda* (PL 13, cols 417–424)

Migne's addenda to the Damasus volume: (1) a *Monitum* reporting the abbot
Terribilini's annotations, (2) the Évora Breviary office for St. Damasus (1548),
(3) a dossier of testimonies on Damasus' birthplace, (4) an analysis of
Terribilini's opinions.

## 0417C — head run-on

The section head is printed with no space at the sentence break:
`… pag. 837.IN FESTO SANCTI DAMASI PAPAE ET CONFESSORIS.` Reproduced with the
same run-on in English (`p. 837.ON THE FEAST OF SAINT DAMASUS …`) so the head
matches the Latin twin 1:1.

## 0417D–0419C — liturgical cues

The `P.` at the end of the collect and the `T.` / `Tu.` at the end of each lesson
are the Breviary's cues (*Per…*, *Tu autem Domine…*). Kept verbatim,
unexpanded.

## 0418B — `basilica, quae Sicinini appellatur`

Kept as printed (the Sicininian basilica, Ammianus' *Sicinini*), not normalized
to *Liberii*.

## 0419C — `tertio idus Decembris: anno secundo Arcadii Augusti`

The dating is left in the printed Roman form ("on the third of the Ides of
December"), unconverted. The Breviary's chronology is internally inconsistent
(accession 369 + 18 years 2 months 10 days ≠ the second year of Arcadius); left
as the Breviary reckons it.

## 0419D — imprint

`OLISIPONE, apud LUDOVICUM ROTORIGIUM bibliopolam` — the Latinized printer's
name is given in its Portuguese form ("at Lisbon … Luís Rodrigues"), which is
the identification, not an emendation; the capitals and the italic run of the
imprint are kept as Migne sets them.

## 0419D–0420A — `de rebus Hisqaniae`

The incipit and body print `Hisqaniae`, which is not a word: broken type for
*Hispaniae* (Marineus Siculus' *De rebus Hispaniae* is a known title, and the
dossier's argument is about Spanish birthplaces throughout). Per Pattern 7 the
non-word is carried into the English untranslated, inside the italic title:
*On the Affairs of Hisqaniae*. An earlier draft translated the conjecture
(*On the Affairs of Spain*); that has been withdrawn.

## 0420A — `turres … octo et vigniti supra centum`

`vigniti` is not a word: transposed type for *viginti* ("twenty"), giving
Marineus' count of 128 towers. Per Pattern 7 the non-word is carried into the
English untranslated and in italics, the rest of the additive formula rendered
in the printed order: "we counted towers, eight and *vigniti* above a hundred."

## 0420A–0421A — vernacular quotations

Two quoted passages are **not Latin**: Beuter's chronicle (0420D, Castilian) and
Flórez's *España Sagrada* (0421A, Castilian). They are translated into English
like the rest, and the small-capital emphasis Migne gives them
(`PAPA DAMASO, NATURAL DE TARRAGONA`; `ESPAÑOL`) is carried over. A reader
wanting the Castilian must go to the Latin twin — flagged here because the
project's policy statements assume a Latin base text.

## 0420B–0420C — place-names in the birthplace dossier

*Vimaranes* (Guimarães), *Igaeditanum* / *Egitania* (Idanha-a-Velha), *Bracara
Augusta* (Braga), *Mantua Carpetanorum* (Madrid) are left in the Latin forms the
witnesses use, because the whole dossier is an argument *about* which name
belongs to Damasus; only unambiguous modern equivalents (Évora, Braga, Madrid,
Tarragona) are given in English where the source itself is already unambiguous.

## 0422A — Terribilini's supplement to Damasus, poem XIII

Migne sets the reconstruction with two overlaid typographic signals: *italics*
for Terribilini's supplied words, and initial **capitals** for the words/letters
surviving on the stone (`Insons Vita fuit Marci`, `Justitiae AE custos`,
`Ornav Et Damasus`). The italics are carried into the English; the
capital-marking of surviving stone letters **cannot** survive translation, since
it operates at the level of Latin letters. The stray `AE` at 0422A and the
broken `Ornav|Et` are kept as printed. Anyone using this passage
epigraphically must read the Latin twin.

## 0422A–0422B — the Saturninus inscription (poem XX)

The damaged lines are left **verbatim, untranslated**, because they are
letter-level evidence, not prose:

- `OLA NUNC FUERAT CRARTA . . . . . CINIS AN . .` (Palatine codex, Gruter
  p. MCLXXI) — dot-lacunae reproduced with the same counts; `CRARTA` is as
  printed.
- `NUNC XPI FUERAT CHARTAGINIS ANUS` (same codex, p. MCLXXVI).
- Terribilini's restored couplet `Horum martyrum cultor Damasus / IncOLA nunc
  XPI FUERAS Carthaginis, ante.` — the mixed case is the argument (which letters
  are on the stone, which are supplied), so the couplet stands in Latin.

The framing prose around them is translated normally.

## 0422C — `LXXII`

The number of martyrs (`et alios LXXII`) is kept in Roman numerals throughout,
because Terribilini's argument turns on how easily numerals are miscopied.

## 0422C–D — `sigillatque Martyrologia`

*sigillat* is odd; probably *signat* ("notes, marks") or *sigillat* in the sense
of setting a mark against. Rendered "sets his mark upon the Martyrologies."
Worth a check against Terribilini's own text.

## 0423A → 0424A — column jump

Migne's text runs 0423A directly to 0424A; cols 0423B–D carry no text of this
piece. Both anchors reproduced as printed; the gap is not an omission in the
translation.

## Titles and locators

Poem-titles (*de SS. AA. Catacumbis*, *de Sepulcro suo*, *Epitaph. Projectae*)
are translated and title-cased per style pattern 2, since they open the units
they name. Bare bibliographic locators inside the prose (`epistol. 82 Ver.
edit.`, `hymn. XI, de Coron.`, `p. MCLXXIII, n. 9`, `III kal. Decembris`,
`V kal. Septembris`) are left in Migne's abbreviated Latin form.
