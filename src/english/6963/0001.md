## 5.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ She does not seek one kiss, but many kisses, that she may be able to fulfil her desire; for she who loves is [1855B] not content with the scantness of a single kiss, but demands very many, claims more, and has been wont thus to commend herself the more to her beloved. ⟦s2+3⟧ And so she in the Gospel was thus approved: *Because she has not ceased,* says the Lord, *to kiss my feet; and therefore many sins are forgiven her, because she has loved much [n: (Luc. VII, 46, 47)]*. [var: Vulg. *remittuntur ei peccata multa quoniam dilexit multum*, a present "are forgiven"] So this soul also desires many kisses of the Word, that she may be enlightened with the light of divine knowledge; for this is the kiss of the Word, namely the light of sacred knowledge. ⟦s4+5⟧ For God the Word kisses us when the Spirit of divine knowledge illumines our heart and the very governing part of man; and the soul, endowed with this pledge of nuptial charity, glad and exulting, says: *I opened my mouth and drew forth the spirit [n: (Psal. CXVIII, 131)]*. [var: Vulg. *adtraxi spiritum*, "drew in the spirit"] For a kiss is that whereby lovers cleave to one another [1855C] and, as it were, take possession of the sweetness of an inward grace. ⟦s6⟧ Through this kiss the soul cleaves to the Word of God, in that the spirit of the one kissing is poured over into her: just as they also who kiss one another are not content with a foretaste of the lips, but seem to pour their own spirit into one another. ⟦s7⟧ *1549*

## 6.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth.* ⟦s2⟧ Wilt thou fit this to Christ? ⟦s3⟧ Nothing is more welcome. ⟦s4⟧ Wilt thou fit it to thy own soul? ⟦s5⟧ Nothing is more delightful. ⟦s6⟧ *Let him kiss me*: he sees thee to be clean from every sin, since offenses are of the earth; therefore he judges thee worthy of the heavenly sacraments, and therefore invites thee to the heavenly banquet, that *he may kiss thee with the kisses of his mouth.* ⟦s7⟧ Yet, because of what follows, thy soul, or the human condition, or the Church, has seen herself cleansed from all sins, worthy to be able to approach [1855D] the altar of God. ⟦s8⟧ For what is the altar but the form of the body of Christ? ⟦s9⟧ She has seen the wondrous sacraments, and says: *Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth;* that is, let Christ fix a kiss upon me. ⟦s10⟧ Why? ⟦s10⟧ Because:

## 7.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thy breasts are better above wine,* [var: Vulg. *meliora sunt ubera tua vino*, "better than wine"; the plate has printed the same lemma with *bona* and with *optima* above] that is, thy senses are better, thy sacraments are better above wine — above that wine which, though it has sweetness, [1856A] though it has gladness, though it has grace, yet in it the gladness is worldly, while in thee the delight is spiritual. ⟦s2⟧ Even then, therefore, Solomon was introducing the marriage either of Christ and the Church, or of spirit and flesh and soul. ⟦s3⟧ And as though despising all her own delights and pleasures, desiring [sic: *cupieos*] to cleave to the heavenly commandments, she says: since the precepts of thy testaments are best above every appetite of the flesh and every pleasure of the world; for she remembered that she had thus fallen before, in Eve, while she set the pleasure of the body before the heavenly commandments. ⟦s4⟧ And so, showing that she loves not only the appearance of the Word and a certain countenance, but all his inward parts, she adds to the grace of the kisses: *Because thy breasts are good,* she says, *above wine, and the odor of thine ointments above all spices.* ⟦s5⟧ She asked for a kiss; God the Word poured himself wholly into her, and bared his breasts to her, that is, his doctrines and the disciplines of his inward wisdom, and was fragrant with the sweet odor of his ointments: [1856B] and, taken captive by these, she says that the delight of divine knowledge is more abundant than the gladness of every bodily pleasure.

## 8.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thy name is an ointment emptied out, or oil poured forth;* that is: this world was wholly stinking with the unclean impurities of divers crimes; now everywhere the sweetness of modesty breathes, the ointment of faith, the flower of integrity. ⟦s2⟧ There breathes also in the Word the odor of grace and the remission of sins, which, being poured out over the whole world, has filled all things as with an ointment emptied out, because through all men the heavy filth of vices has been wiped away. ⟦s3⟧ This ointment was emptied out above the Jews, and was gathered up for those acting: emptied out in Judaea, and it gave forth its scent in all lands. ⟦s4⟧ With this ointment Mary was anointed, and the Virgin conceived, the Virgin bore the good odor, the Son of God: this ointment was poured out upon [1856C] the waters, and sanctified the waters: with this ointment the three young men were anointed, and the flame bedewed them with moisture; with this ointment Daniel was anointed, and he softened the mouths of the lions and stroked down their wildness. ⟦s5⟧ This ointment flows daily, and never fails. ⟦s6⟧ Take thy vessel, O virgin, and draw near, that thou mayest be able to be filled with this ointment: take the ointment valued at three hundred pence, but given freely, not sold, that all might have it freely. ⟦s7⟧ O virgin, be anointed; be not made sorrowful as Judas was, because this ointment is poured out, but bury Christ together with thee: shut thy vessel indeed, lest the ointment flow away: shut it with the key of integrity, with modesty in speaking, with abstinence from boasting; she who has this ointment receives Christ: *Thy name is an ointment emptied out.* [1856D]

## 9.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (Vers. 2.)] Therefore the maidens have loved thee.* ⟦s2⟧ Who are these maidens, but the souls of individual men, who have laid aside the old age of this body, being renewed through the Holy Spirit? ⟦s3⟧ *Thy name is an ointment poured forth,* than the power of which utterance nothing can be more excellent. ⟦s4⟧ For just as ointment shut up in some vessel holds in its own odor — which odor, so long as it is confined within the narrows of that vessel, keeps its force, even though it [1857A] cannot reach many — but when the ointment has been poured out of the vessel in which it was shut, it is said to be spread far and wide: so also the name of Christ, before his coming, was shut up in the people of Israel, as it were in some vessel of the minds of the Jews: for *God is known in Judaea, in Israel his name is great. [n: (Psal. LXXV, I)]*. ⟦s5⟧ This name, therefore, which the vessels of the Jews, confined within their own narrows, contained. ⟦s6⟧ A great name indeed even then, when it clung within the narrows of the weak and the few, but it had not yet poured out its greatness through the hearts of the nations and unto the ends of the whole world: *1550* but after he shone forth by his coming through the whole world, he stretched out that divine name of his through the whole creation, not filled up by any addition — for fulness knows no increase — but filling the empty places, that his name might be wonderful in [1857B] all the earth. ⟦s7⟧ The pouring forth of this name, therefore, signifies a certain abounding overflow of graces and a plenty of heavenly goods; for whatever is poured out overflows out of abundance. ⟦s8⟧ *Therefore the maidens have loved thee.* ⟦s9⟧ Good, she says, is prudence, but sweet is mercy; for the first few attain, while the second reaches to all: for the sake of this thy indulgence, she says, the souls renewed in spirit love thee.

## 10.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *[n: (Vers. 3.)] Draw us after thee; we will run in the odor of thine ointments.* [var: Vulg. *trahe me post te*, "draw me," singular; the exposition that follows turns on the plural "us"] The holy soul, having now kissed the Word of God, takes no measure and is not satisfied, saying: Sweet art thou, O Lord, and in thy delightfulness teach me thy justifications [n: (Psal. CXVIII, 12)]. ⟦s3⟧ Having kissed the Word of God, she desires him above all beauty, loves him above all gladness, delights in him above all [1857C] spices, longs to see him often, to gaze on him frequently, longs to be drawn that she may be able to follow: *Thy name,* she says, *is an ointment emptied out; therefore we maidens love thee;* therefore we strive, but we cannot lay hold: draw us, that we may be able to run; that from the odor of thine ointments we may receive strength to follow. ⟦s4⟧ She hastens also to see the inward mysteries, the very rest of the Word, the very dwelling-place of that highest good, and his light and brightness in that fatherly bosom and retreat; she hastens to hear his discourses, and when she has heard she receives him above all sweetness.

## 11.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ Let the prophet teach thee who has tasted, and says: *How sweet are thy words to my throat, above honey and the honeycomb to my mouth [n: (Psal. CXVIII, 103)]*! [var: Vulg. *quam dulcia faucibus meis eloquia tua, super mel ori meo*, without "and the honeycomb"] For what else does the soul desire [1857D] that has once tasted the sweetness of the Word, that has once seen his brightness? ⟦s3⟧ Moses, set on the mountain for forty days receiving the Law, sought no food for the body: Elijah, hastening to that rest, prayed that his soul might be taken from him: Peter, himself also beholding on the mountain the glory of the Lord's resurrection, was unwilling to come down, saying: *Lord, it is good for us to be here [n: (Matth. XVII, 4)]*. ⟦s4⟧ How great, then, is that glory of the divine substance, how great the gifts of the Word, upon which the angels also desire to look!
