## 12.

The soul, therefore, that sees that, does not seek this body, and understands that there ought to be [1858A] the least possible familiarity with it; she renounces the world, leads herself away from the bonds of the flesh, and strips herself of all the entanglements of these pleasures. And so Stephen was seeing Jesus, and did not dread to be stoned; nay, when he was being stoned, he prayed not for himself but for those by whom he was being destroyed. Paul too, caught up as far as the third heaven, did not know whether he was with the body or without the body; caught up, I say, into paradise, he no longer felt the use of his own body, and, hearing the words of God, blushed at how he should come down to the infirmities of the body. And so, knowing what he had seen in paradise, or what he had heard, he cried out, saying: *Why do you still, as though living out of this world, make decrees? Touch not, handle not, taste not, which things are all unto corruption by the very use [n: (Coloss. II, 20 et 21)]*. [var: Vulg. *ne tetigeritis neque gustaveritis neque contrectaveritis, quae sunt omnia in interitu ipso usu*; the three prohibitions stand in a different order, and "unto destruction"] For he wished us to be in the figure of this world, not in possession and use, [1858B] so that we might so use this world as though we used it not, as those passing by, not as those who settle; walking as in the image of the age, not in covetousness, that by a most swift reckoning we may pass beyond the very image of this world. And so he himself, walking by faith, not by sight, was a pilgrim away from the body and present with God: and when he was upon the earth, he had his conversation not in earthly but in heavenly things. Therefore let our soul, which wishes to draw near to God, lift herself up from the body, let her always cleave to that highest good, to that good which is divine, which is always, and which was from the beginning, and which was with God [n: (I Joan. I)], that is, the Word of God: this is that divine thing, *in which we live, and are, and are moved [n: (Act. XVII, 28)]*. [var: Vulg. *in ipso enim vivimus et movemur et sumus*, "live and are moved and are," the last two members in the other order]

## 13.

*We will run in the odor of thine ointments.* This [1858C] odor is breathed by the soul that begins to open to Christ, that she may first receive the odor of the Lord's burial, and may believe that his flesh saw no corruption, nor withered with any odor of death, but rose again, embalmed with the odor of that eternal and ever-flourishing flower. For how could he wither even in the flesh, whose name *is* *1551* *an ointment emptied out?* He emptied himself that he might breathe upon thee; this ointment always was, but it was with the Father, it was in the Father, and gave forth its scent only to angels and archangels, as within the vessel of heaven. The Father opened his mouth, saying: *Behold, I have set thee for a covenant of my race, for a light of the nations, that thou mayest be for salvation unto the utmost part of the earth [n: (Esai. XLIX, 6)]*. [var: Vulg. *dedi te in lucem gentium, ut sis salus mea usque ad extremum terrae*, without the covenant clause and with "my salvation"] The Son came down, all things were filled with the new odor of the Word, the heart of the Father uttered forth a good Word, the Son was fragrant, the Holy [1858D] Spirit breathed out, and poured himself abroad through the hearts of all: *for the charity of God has been poured abroad in our hearts by the Holy Spirit [n: (Rom. V, 5)]*. The Son of God himself at first held in his own odor within the body as within a vessel, awaiting the time, as he says: *The Lord gives me the tongue of learning, that I may know when it behoves me to speak a word.* [var: Vulg. Isa. 50:4 *Dominus dedit mihi linguam eruditam, ut sciam sustentare eum qui lassus est verbo*, "to sustain him that is weary with a word"] The hour came, he opened his mouth, the ointment went forth, when virtue was going out from him. And therefore she hastens to the Word, and asks to be drawn, lest perchance she be left behind; because the Word of God runs, and is not bound. And so *he exults as a giant to run [1859A] the way [n: (Psal. XVIII, 6)]*. [var: Vulg. *exultavit ut gigans ad currendam viam*, a past "he exulted"] And because *his going forth is from the highest heaven, and his meeting even unto the highest thereof [n: (Ibid., 7)]*, seeing herself unequal to so great a swiftness, she says: *Draw us.*

## 14.

Good is the soul that asks not for herself alone, but for all: *Draw,* I say, *us;* for we have the desire of following, which the grace of thine ointments breathes into us: but because we cannot match thy courses, *Draw us;* that, propped by thy help, we may be able to press upon thy footsteps. For if thou shalt draw, we too shall run, and shall catch the spiritual breezes of swiftness. For the burden is laid down for those to whom thy hand is a prop: and thine oil is poured in, by which he was healed who had been wounded by robbers.

## 15.

But lest what she says seem to thee shameless — *Draw us* — hear him who says: *Come to me, all you that [1859B] labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you [n: (Matth. XI, 28)]*. Thou seest that he draws us willingly, lest we be left behind as we follow: but let him who wishes to be drawn run that he may lay hold, and let him run forgetting the things behind and reaching after the things that are before; for thus will he be able to lay hold of Christ; whence the Apostle also says: *So run that you may lay hold.* She too wishes to come to the prize, who desires to lay hold. Prudently, therefore, she asks that she may be drawn [sic: *attrabatur*], because not all are able to follow. And so, when Peter said: *Whither goest thou?* the Word of God answered: *Thou canst not follow me now, but thou shalt follow hereafter [n: (Joan. XIII, 36; Matth. XIV, 30)]*. He had committed to him the keys of the kingdom of heaven, and yet judged him unequal to himself in following. This soul, however, he did not put off, because she was not presuming, but asking. ## 16.

*[n: (Vers. 3.)] He has brought me into his chamber.* [var: Vulg. *introduxit me rex in cellaria sua*, "into his storerooms," plural] Blessed is the soul that enters the inner chambers of the Word; for she, rising up out of the body, becomes more remote from all things, and searches and seeks after that which is above her very self, that divine thing, whether by any means she may attain it: and when she has been able to grasp it, having passed beyond the things that are intelligible, she is confirmed in it and is fed upon it. Such was Paul, who knew that he had been caught up into paradise, but did not know whether outside the body or in the body. For his soul had risen up out of the body, and had led and lifted itself away from the inward parts and the bonds of the flesh; and, made a stranger to himself, he held within himself the unspeakable words which he heard and could not publish abroad, having marked that it is not lawful for a man to utter them. The good soul, therefore, despises the visible [1859D] and the sensible, and does not settle in them, nor tarry and sit down in the beholding of them: but she ascends to those things that are eternal and invisible and full of wonders, raising herself with the pure perception of a devout mind. For indeed, being zealous for perfection, she reaches toward that good of the Godhead alone, and thinks that nothing else is to be sought, because she holds what is highest.

## 17.

*The king has brought me into his chamber.* The kiss is simple, but the secret of the chamber is full of business; for in this place she testifies that she wishes to know the mysteries more fully, that she may enter the inner shrines of the heavenly mysteries, [1860A] and that the treasures of wisdom and knowledge hidden in Christ may be opened to her: and that she may be able to do this, perhaps she says that which was said above: *Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth.* This signifies the grace of the Holy Spirit coming upon her, as the Angel said to Mary: *The Holy Spirit shall come upon thee, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow thee [n: (Luc. I, 35)]*. But when the king brought her into his chamber, the time of the passion declares it, the piercing of the side, the shedding of blood, the ointment of the burial, the mystery *1552* of the resurrection; so that she received the kiss as a betrothed, but into the chamber of Christ the Church was brought in, no longer as merely betrothed, but also as wedded: nor did she only enter the bridal chamber, but also obtained the keys of the lawful union. And therefore, set as it were in the bridal chamber, she says: *A binding of the drop is my brother to me; between [1860B] the midst of my breasts he rests [n: (Vers. 12)]*. [var: Vulg. *fasciculus murrae dilectus meus mihi, inter ubera mea commorabitur*, "a bundle of myrrh is my beloved to me… he shall abide"]

## 18.

But if we seek the chamber, he himself teaches us who says: but thou, when thou shalt pray, *enter into thy chamber, and, the door being shut, pray to thy Father in secret [n: (Matth. VI, 6)]*. The chamber of the Church is the body of Christ. The king brought her into all the inward mysteries, gave her the keys that she might open for herself the treasures of knowledge, might spread open the doors of the sacraments that were closed before, might know the grace of him who rested, the sleep of him who died, the power of him who rose again. In that chamber the bride finds the justices of the Lord Jesus. What are those justices? Surely the sacraments of baptism, as we read: for when John said to him as he came to baptism, *I ought to be baptized by thee, and comest thou to me?* Jesus answered: *Suffer it now, for so it becomes us to fulfil all [1860C] justice [n: (Matth. III, 15)]*. In that chamber she learns the justifications of the Lord, she has come to know the counsel of God, than which nothing can be more sublime; for this is the divine thing, in which remission of sins is made.
