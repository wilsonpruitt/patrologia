## 19.

She seeks to learn from the Lord those things which men could not teach unless God had taught them first: and after the apostles had learned them from Christ, the Church says: *The king has brought me into his chamber,* that is, into that secret place in which are all the treasures of his knowledge and understanding. And therefore it is said to thee: *When thou prayest, enter into thy chamber [n: (Matth. VI, 6)]*. [var: Vulg. *cum orabis*, a future "when thou shalt pray"] which signifies the secret place of mind and soul. Into this chamber of justices the bride asked to be led, when she had run out after the odor of the ointment, which flows always and never fails. [1860D]

## 20.

*The king has brought me into his chamber.* The Greek has "into his storehouse" and "into his cellar": where are the good libations, where are the good odors, sweet honeys, where are the divers fruits, where are the varied dishes, that thy meal may be seasoned with very many dishes. And so, having been brought into that secret place of the divine bridegroom:

## 21.

*Let us exult,* she says, *and be glad in thee; let us love thy breasts above wine;* [var: Vulg. *exultabimus et laetabimur in te, memores uberum tuorum super vinum*, a future "we will exult" and "mindful of thy breasts"] for she exults not in riches and in treasures of gold and silver, not in the fruits of possessions, not in powers, not in banquets, but in God alone, saying to him: *Let us love [1861A] thy breasts above wine.* Yet she so drank that she was not swallowed up by the wine, but drew the gladness of the heart from his grace, and did not stagger with the drunkenness of the body. And so, drinking this wine, she opened her eyes and saw the straight way, and left the crooked ways, saying: *Equity has loved thee,* [var: Vulg. *recti diligunt te*, "the upright love thee"] that is: winding journeys do not follow thee, but the path of justice alone can come to thee; for he who loves justice does not turn himself away from Christ. For how should an innocent conscience fear the judge of justice and the rewarder of merits? Yet the same soul, knowing herself darkened by the fellowship of the body, says to other souls, or to other heavenly powers set beside the sacred mystery: *Do not look upon me, since I have been darkened, because the sun has not looked upon me.*

## 22.

*[n: (Vers. 4.)] I am dusky and comely, O daughters of Jerusalem.* [1861B] [var: Vulg. *nigra sum sed formosa*, "I am black but beautiful," and *decoloravit me sol*, "the sun has discolored me," where the plate prints the negative "the sun has not looked upon me"] Set before thee the Lord Jesus reclining at the banquet, and John leaning himself upon his breast, and the others marvelling that a servant should lean upon his Lord, that that sinful flesh should recline upon the temple of the Word, that that soul, bound in by the chains of the flesh, should search out the court of the divine fulness. To these others, therefore, as they marvelled, the soul of John answered: *I am dusky and comely, O daughters of Jerusalem;* dusky through fault, comely through grace. The flesh says it too, *I am dusky and comely;* dusky with the dust of the world, which she gathered in her striving, comely with the spiritual eye, wherewith she wiped away the dust and squalor of this world: dusky through vice, but comely now through the washing, which cleanses away every offense: I am dusky because I have sinned, comely because Christ now loves me; for her whom he had banished in Eve, he takes back in [1861C] the virgin, he receives from Mary.

## 23.

The Synagogue says it too, whose mysteries very many think to be expressed in this place; who, when she saw herself cast off on account of the impiety of the whole people, yet consoled herself, *1553* because she saw Christ himself out of her own people, and not him only, but Peter, John, and James cleaving to Christ, and because she had the oracles entrusted to her. And therefore she said: *I am dusky and comely, O daughters of Jerusalem:* dusky through unbelief, comely through the law: dusky through the fall, comely because the sun has loved me, and I was made the first congregation of God. *Do not flee from me because I am dusky;* I am dusky for this reason, that the sun of justice has forsaken me, who before was wont to give me light. I have lost the color of my countenance, the keenness of my eyes has been dulled, wherewith before I looked upon the sun: [1861D] I walk in darkness, I who know not the day of Christ. Yet do not despise me, since he who forsook me can look upon me again. He is wont to have mercy again, to gather the scattered, to seek out the forsaken, to collect the destitute. All ye tribes of Israel, come together, look upon me as I was comely before my offenses; *do not look upon me, since I have been darkened;* that is, do not look at this alone, that I have been darkened: the sun has not looked upon me, and therefore I have been darkened. But the sun shines upon the just and the unjust, upon the just for grace, upon the unjust for [1862A] mercy; giving to the one the reward of their merits, forgiving the others their sins. And upon the nations he did not shine before; now he shines, now he rises upon them who once rose upon me; he who has forgiven them will forgive me also. Do not judge, because I have been darkened, that the sun has utterly forsaken me and no longer looks upon me, nor seeks me out in my sickness. He has hidden himself from me because I have not kept his commandments: he will be reconciled when he shall have seen the repentance of my offenses. The sun has not seen me, because I did not receive him when he came, I did not open the windows that the light of life might come in: when I shall have opened them, he will enlighten my eyes, who came that he might enlighten the whole world, and that even those who do not see may see.

## 24.

Or the Church of the nations says: *Do not look upon me, since I am dusky, because the sun has not regarded me;* in that the sun of justice long judged the congregation of the nations, bound as it were with the winter's frosts and the ice [1862B] of gentile error, unworthy to be surveyed by the serene light of his countenance. Did not the harshness of a wintry season seem to thee to be there, when God was known in Judaea only? But now the fulness of the summer light shines out, when Christ is all things and in all. Is not *the earth the Lord's, and the fulness thereof [n: (Psalm. XXIII, 1)]*? And truly the whole round world is in the Church, because *there is not Jew only or Greek, not Barbarian or Scythian, not slave or free: but we are all one in Christ [n: (Coloss. III, 11)]*. [var: Vulg. *sed omnia et in omnibus Christus*, "but Christ is all and in all"] The sun shines upon all, the day gives light to all. *I am black, and comely.* She has put "black" first, that she might increase the comeliness: she did not say, I am black comely, lest what is black should be thought comely: but she says, I am black by the former sin, but comely [1862C] by the confession of sin, and by zeal for amendment, and by love of virtue. Although therefore *And* is a syllable which, as the grammarians call it, is conjunctive, yet it has a distinguishing force, whereby confusion is distinguished and separated; as, if thou shouldst say that Ambrose Bassus is within, one man is thought of; but if thou assert that Ambrose *and* Bassus are within, two are certainly understood.

## 25.

*As the tabernacles of Cedar, as the skins of Solomon;* for tents are made from the hides of dead animals. Therefore she who said this was dead to sin and living to God: and let us too, then, die to sin, that we may live to God. Filled with the spirit, with the delight and sweetness of gladness, we shall be spiritual hides, lacking bodily infirmity, and keeping in the whole bosom of the mind the grace of the divine mysteries poured into us. [1862D] But that the tabernacles of Cedar, or the skins of Solomon, mean this body is clear from many passages: yet understand it also from this, that when Adam and Eve laid aside the image of the heavenly man which they bore before, and put on the image of the earthly man, they are said to have been clothed with tunics of skin; for the fault they had committed had made them bodily out of spiritual.

## 26.

Nor is that absurd to understand, that the bride says she has been made like skins scorched by the burnings of the sun; in that, though she was set in the harshness of tribulations, [1863A] she yet mortified her body, that it might not be able to feel that harshness: for as the skin of a tabernacle does not feel the burning of the sun, in that it is the skin of a dead animal, so she, being dead to sin, could not feel the burning of sin: either because she was not broken by necessities, or because we are a body *1554* of affliction, and ought always to carry about the death of the Lord Jesus in our body.

## 27.

*[n: (Vers. 5.)] The sons of my mother have fought against me;* that is, the passions of the body have assailed me, the enticements of the flesh have discolored me: therefore the sun of justice has not shone again upon me, because, widowed of my defense, I could not keep my devotion and my full observance; for this is what is meant by *I have not kept my own vineyard,* because I brought forth thorns and not the grape, that is, doing sins instead of fruits. And when she speaks of the Word, [1863B] the splendor of the Word shining upon her, she turns to him and says:

## 28.

*[n: (Vers. 6.)] Where dost thou feed, where dost thou abide at midday?* [var: Vulg. *ubi pascas, ubi cubes in meridie*, subjunctive and "where thou liest down"] Rightly she says, *where dost thou feed,* because the Word of God is royal: *where dost thou abide,* because he is moral: *at midday,* because he is mystical: since indeed at midday Joseph, set at the banquet with his brothers, was revealing the mysteries of the times to come. But David also says: *Reveal thy life to the Lord and hope in him, and he himself will do it, and will lead forth thy justice as the light, and thy judgment as the noonday [n: (Psalm. XXXVI, 5)]*. [var: Vulg. *revela Domino viam tuam*, "reveal thy way to the Lord"] And Paul himself asserted that a light shone about him at midday, when he was turned from persecution to grace.
