## 29.

She complains, therefore, that she has been forsaken, that she has been left destitute, poor out of rich; for she abounded in the gift [1863C] of graces, but she began to be in want when the abundance of the divine presence was denied her. And therefore she asks even to be held as a hireling, she who before claimed for herself the grace of a more precious union.

## 30.

*They have set me as a keeper in the vineyards; my own vineyard I have not kept [n: (Vers. 5)]*. Some think that this is said of those who bind the Synagogue to the precepts of the law, that she may keep the law and guard her own vineyard, which she could not keep. And so she was keeping it that she might make grapes, but she made thorns. What, then, the vineyard is, David comes to testify to us, saying: *Thou hast brought a vineyard out of Egypt, and hast cast out the nations, and hast planted it [n: (Psalm. LXXIX, 9)]*. The apostles too may be the sons of her mother, who truly assailed the Synagogue, saying: *Behold, we turn to the nations:* and they began to sow the word [1863D] of God among the peoples. The prophets too may be understood, who, by warning and denouncing that the Synagogue should keep her own vineyard, accomplished nothing; for she made the thorns of wickedness, who ought to have brought forth the rich fruits of virtue: and therefore she confesses that she could not keep her own people, and deservedly seeks too late to hold him whom, while she held him, she lost: far unlike her who says: *I have found him whom my soul loves; I held him, and I will not let him go [n: (Cant. III, 4)]*. [var: Vulg. *tenui eum, nec dimittam*, "I held him, nor will I let him go"] And so that one invites the bridegroom into her garden; this one, asking where he feeds, says to him: [1864A]

## 31.

*Tell me, whom my soul has loved.* [var: Vulg. *indica mihi quem diligit anima mea*, "show me, whom my soul loves," in the present; the gloss that follows turns on the past tense] Why dost thou say *whom my soul has loved,* and not "whom she loves"? Why hast thou let go him whom thou didst hold? Thou didst love him by the faith of the fathers, thou hast lost him by unbelief: thou didst hold him by the bonds of charity, thou hast lost him by the long rope of faithlessness. Therefore thou knowest not where he feeds, where he abides; for if thou didst know, thou wouldst not seek. For thou sayest: *Where dost thou feed, where dost thou abide at midday?* Thou knowest that the midday is the Church which holds Christ, and thou seekest him in the nights. Say to me, she says, O Christ, answer me at least for this cause, that thou wast dear to me before and beloved; therefore, though I have lost the privilege of so great a charity, yet, as one who art merciful, answer me, *Where dost thou feed, where dost thou abide?* Thou hast forsaken me, thou hast gone away to the nations, thou hast withdrawn far from me, and hast been made nearer to those from whom thou wast far. But thou hast been made near, because they have believed in thy blood. Thou hast withdrawn from me, because I did not receive the cross [1864B] as the redemption of the world, but as the [sic: *damnanationem*] of a guilty man. But they who received it as the author of salvation are at midday; for them thou shinest, for them thou blazest out, for them thy grace is warm as the noonday. To me thou wast the morning, when as yet I believed, but I did not believe fully, because I have not been found at midday, like Joseph with his brothers, who held the midday: for them thou hast been made noonday, who are fed in thy riches and hope in thee. Therefore, as David said: *Thou shalt lead forth their justice as the light, and their judgment as the noonday [n: (Psalm. XXXVI, 6)]*. [var: Vulg. *educet quasi lumen iustitiam tuam, et iudicium tuum tamquam meridiem*, "he will lead forth thy justice… and thy judgment"]

## 32.

Thou seekest, then, as a stranger, thou who wast a neighbor; as a poor woman, thou who wast rich: thou wishest to follow, thou who didst go before; and would that thou mightest at least follow those whom thou oughtest to have gone before! Thou desirest to be a hireling, thou who [1864C] before didst gather hirelings; and so this is the voice of a hireling, saying: *Lest* *1555* *perchance I become one wrapped about, beside the flocks of thy companions.* [var: Vulg. *ne vagari incipiam post greges sodalium tuorum*, "lest I begin to wander after the flocks"] Thou who before didst receive proselytes out of the nations, now thou thyself among the peoples wishest to be received in the place of proselytes, and to be gathered as a stranger. The Word of God answered the longing soul:

## 33.

*[n: (Vers. 7.)] Unless thou know thyself, O comely one among women.* [var: Vulg. *si ignoras te, o pulcherrima inter mulieres, egredere*, "if thou know not thyself, O fairest among women, go forth"] Know thyself, O man: it is said to thy soul, unless thou hast known thyself comely among women. Know thyself, O soul, that thou art not of earth, not of clay, because God breathed into thee and made thee into a living soul. Thou art a magnificent work, formed by the inbreathing of God: *Attend to thyself,* as the Law says, that is, to thy soul: let not the things of the world and of the age hold thee, let not earthly things [1864D] delay thee. Hasten with thy whole intention to him by whose inbreathing thou standest: *A great thing,* he says, *is man, and precious is a merciful man: but to find a faithful man is a labor [n: (Prov. XX, 6)]*. [var: Vulg. *multi homines misericordes vocantur; virum autem fidelem quis inveniet?*, "many men are called merciful; but who shall find a faithful man?"] Learn, O man, wherein thou art great and precious. The earth shows thee to be vile, but virtue makes thee glorious, faith makes thee rare, the image makes thee precious. For where is anything so precious as the image of God? which ought first to pour faith into thee, so that some likeness of its author may shine again in thy heart; lest he who questions thy mind should not recognize the author. Or is anything so precious as humility; [1865A] that, looking upon the nature of body and of soul, by the one thou shouldst subject thyself, by the other recognize thyself. The enticement of the flesh, prone to vice, urges mercy; because what thou hast bestowed upon another, thou payest to thyself: whatever proceeds from thee runs back into thee, and whatever thou shalt do, thou payest to thyself: and whatever profit thou shalt bring, it profits thee. Let the vigor of the living soul, the perception of reason and the mind capable of understanding and of judgment, so that it may be seen a worthy house for so great a dweller, not lose the privilege of its own nature. *Unless thou know thyself, O comely one among women.* Thou who complainest that thou hast been forsaken: unless thou know thyself, unless thou repent of thy fall, unless thou approve the intention of thy devotion, unless thy faith and sincerity be increased, thy complaint will profit nothing. Or thus: unless thou know thyself, that thou art comely; unless thou keep the beauty of thy nature, and the enticements of the body do not sink thee down, [1865B] nor hindrances hold thee back, the nobility of a better creature will avail thee nothing.

## 34.

Know thyself, therefore, and the comeliness of thy nature; and go forth as one stripped of the bonds of the feet, and thrust out with bare footprint, that thou mayest not feel the coverings of the flesh: let not bodily chains entangle the footstep of thy mind, that thy foot may appear shapely. For such are they who are chosen by the Lord to announce the kingdom of heaven, of whom it is said: *How shapely are the feet of them that preach the gospel of peace [n: (Esai. LII, 7)]*. Such was Moses, to whom it is said: *Loose the shoe of thy feet [n: (Exod. III, 5)]*; [var: Vulg. Exod. 3:5 *solve calciamentum de pedibus tuis*, "from thy feet"] so that he who was to go forth to call the people to the kingdom of God should first cast off the hides of the flesh and walk with bare spirit and with the footstep of the mind; this, then, is what he says: *Go thou forth in the sandals of the flocks, and feed [1865C] thy kids in the tabernacles of the shepherds.* [var: Vulg. *egredere et abi post vestigia gregum, et pasce hedos tuos iuxta tabernacula pastorum*, "go after the footsteps of the flocks… beside the tabernacles"]

## 35.

By the flocks we understand a kingdom, in that it belongs to power to preside over flocks. And every man presides over himself by a certain royal power, if he restrains in himself the excesses of the body and reduces his flesh to servitude; and therefore it is said: *The kingdom of God is within you.* Whence beautifully he says to the soul: *Go forth,* that is, go forth from servitude, go forth from the command and dominion of the flesh, and go forth not in the flesh but in the spirit, go forth to the governance of power. And therefore he added: *Feed thy kids,* that is, rule those things that are on thy left hand; for if they are not ruled, they easily slip away. Restrain the wantonness, the playfulness of thy body and its unreasoning excess, tame its light motions: feed them not in bodily tabernacles, but in the tabernacles of the shepherds who know how to rule a flock; for the tabernacles of Israel are lovely, like groves overshadowing a river, in which the soul, set as it were [1865D] in battle array, exercises a good soldiery and searches out the assaults of the enemy.

## 36.

*Unless thou know thyself, O comely one among women.* What is it to know oneself, but that each man should know that he was made to the image and likeness of God, capable of reason, who ought to till his own earth like a good husbandman with a certain plough and sickle of wisdom, so that either hard things may be split or luxuriant things cut back, and who ought to govern the inward portion of himself by the command of the mind? [1866A] Whence also it is written in the Law: *Attend to thyself, lest a hidden word be made in thy heart [n: (Deut. XV, 9)]*. [var: Vulg. Deut. 15:9 *cave ne forte subrepat tibi impia cogitatio*, "beware lest perchance an impious thought creep upon thee"] Attend to thyself, he says: he certainly does not say *1556* to thy money, not to thy possessions, not to the strength of the body, but to thy spirit and to thy mind, whence all counsels, deeds, and thoughts flow. Attend to thyself, therefore, there, where thou knowest thyself to be the stronger. Know thyself: which the men of the nations assign to Pythian Apollo, as though he were the author of this maxim; whereas they carry over to their own what has been usurped from ours, and Moses, who wrote the book of Deuteronomy, was far earlier than the philosophers who invented these things.
