## 37.

Whence Solomon also, following the divine oracle, wrote in the Song of Songs: *Unless thou know thyself, thou comely one among women* [var: Vulg. *Si ignoras te, o pulcherrima inter mulieres*, "If thou know not thyself, O fairest among women"]; that is, unless thou know thyself to be mortal [1866B] and rational, and quickly confess thy sins, quickly tell thy iniquities that thou mayest be justified — unless thou be converted, and be the first to accuse thy own faults, the day of death comes and there is now no remedy of conversion; thou art forestalled while thou art thinking of it. Kindle thy torch before the bridegroom's door be shut against thee, for he is not wont to wait long for the negligent. *Unless thou know thyself,* he says, *thou comely one among women;* and say thou: *I am swarthy and comely* [var: Vulg. Cant. 1:4 *Nigra sum, sed formosa*, "I am black, but beautiful"]*: I am swarthy,* because I have sinned, *comely,* because I am loved, because I am of the stock of Abraham, a chosen stock, beloved of God. The grace of the fathers will profit thee nothing: *For God is able of these stones to raise up sons to Abraham [n: (Matth. III, 9)]*. And elsewhere in the Gospel thou hast read that the devil bound a daughter of Abraham with the chains of his own wickedness, whom the Lord loosed on the sabbath day. Unless therefore thou know thyself, it will profit thee nothing [1866C]. Even if thou say, I am a daughter of Abraham — thou who believest not, who correctest not thine error: Abraham indeed is saved, but nobility of stock will not help thee, unless faith have saved thee. Let not the promise given to the fathers deceive thee; I accept not the person of a man, I accept not the prerogative of stock, unless I have seen a nobility of manners answering to the stock, so that there be an election of a just stock.

## 38.

But if thou know thyself, and know that thou art liable to sin, thou must go out among the heels of the flocks. Go out therefore with bare foot, and feed thy kids in the tents of the shepherds. He who is without sin feeds lambs, feeds sheep; but she who is liable to sin, let her feed the kids which are on the left; for she cannot be at the right hand of the good shepherd. Let her rather follow him who says to Peter: *Come behind me, Satan [n: (Marc. VIII, 33)]* [var: Vulg. *Vade retro me, Satana*, "Go behind me"]. By following, Peter deserved to be set at the right hand; and therefore [1866D] it is said to him: *Feed my little lambs [n: (Joan. XXI, 16)]* [var: Vulg. *agnos*, "lambs" — without the diminutive]. But hear whither she is to go out: *To the tents,* he says, *of the shepherds,* that is, to the nations, to the dispersion. And therefore he said: *I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered [n: (Zach. XIII, 7)]* [var: Vulg. Zach. *Percute pastorem, et dispergentur oves*, "Strike thou the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered"]; that the whole round of the earth might be filled with the flock of Christ — signifying that the Synagogue would then be safe, when she too shall have joined herself to this dispersion which has filled this world; for the tents of the shepherds are the kingdoms of the earth. [1867A]

## 39.

*[n: (Vers. 8.)] To my mare among the chariots of Pharaoh have I likened thee, my neighbor* [var: Vulg. *Equitatui meo in curribus Pharaonis assimilavi te, amica mea*, "To my cavalry… my friend"]. In that the likeness of the Church seems here to be compared to the running of swift horses, the richness of her grace is reckoned. Do not therefore understand one mare of the rich king, but a word put absolutely for the cavalry, as we have often said — of what sort that sheep of his is, put for the flock of sheep. Pharaoh therefore, as a most powerful and most wealthy king, had powerful horses; whence Scripture also says: *The chariots of Pharaoh [n: (Exod. XIV, 9)]*, as being a most powerful kind for the use of wars and a support in fighting: and indeed with these chariots Pharaoh easily overtook the fleeing Hebrews. As therefore horses yoked together easily draw the chariot, and patiently take up the yoke, and bear it with grace, and by the taking up of that yoke grow gentle: so also the congregation of the nations, [1867B] untamed in its heathen manners, once vaunted itself. But when it took up the yoke of him who says: *Take my yoke upon you, for it is light; and my burden, for it is sweet [n: (Matth. XI, 29 et 30)]* [var: Vulg. *jugum enim meum suave est, et onus meum leve*, "my yoke is sweet and my burden light" — the two epithets exchanged]; and the bride of Christ began to be exalted by the concord and gentleness of the peoples, and to be carried about the whole world, then, like a chariot caught up above the world by swift horses, she went up to the bridegroom.

## 40.

For Christ has his own horses, of whom the prophet says: *Thou hast sent thy horses into the sea, troubling many waters [n: (Habac. III)]* [var: Vulg. Hab. 3:15 *Viam fecisti in mari equis tuis, in luto aquarum multarum*, "Thou madest a way in the sea for thy horses"]; in that the apostles, by preaching the Gospel, stirred up the peoples of the nations, who are moved like many waters and are roused into billows of waters, so that, rising up from the earthly ceremonies of idols, they might believe in Christ. And above he says: *Thou hast mounted [1867C] upon thy horses, thy cavalry is health* [var: Vulg. Hab. 3:8 *quadrigae tuae salvatio*, "thy chariots are salvation"]. O marvellous [sic: *duo decajugum*] of good horses, for whom the bits are of peace, *1557* the reins are of charity: bound fast among themselves by the bonds of concord, and subjected to the yoke of faith, carrying in upon four wheels the mystery of the Gospel to the bounds of the whole world, bearing the good charioteer, the Word of God: by whose whip the enticements of the world were put to flight, the prince of this world driven out, the course of the just fulfilled. O great contest of rational horses! O mystery to be wondered at! A wheel ran within a wheel, and was not hindered: the New Testament within the Old Testament, it ran within that through which it was announced. Into four parts the wheels went, and they turned not backward; because the spirit of life was in them, which ran into the four parts [1867D] of the whole world: and it ran without stumbling, because the good life of the horses squared with it. The horses therefore ran, because he who had mounted the horses was not sleeping.

## 41.

The charioteer of our souls, therefore, is Jesus; who wills that we too should mount our own horses, that is, our bodies, and always be watchful, lest it be said of us: *They have slumbered who mounted horses [n: (Psalm. LXXV, 7)]*. This sea must be crossed briskly; it is scarcely crossed even by the watchful. But if any man shall have slept, he will not be able to cross, but is drowned, like the Egyptian, whose soul and body alike perished. For *the horse* and *the rider he cast [1868A] into the sea [n: (Exod. XV, 1)]* [var: Vulg. *dejecit*, "cast down"], who did not follow the law, but [sic: *per sequebantur*].

## 42.

*To my mare among the chariots of Pharaoh have I likened thee.* The labour of virtue seeks victory, so that she cannot be compared to that mare which is Solomon's, swift for running, apt for bearing young; since the fruitfulness of the soul is desired and sought. This mare therefore is precious, and the chariots of Pharaoh are swift. But in this place we have undertaken to speak of the soul, of this mare who is reckoned like to this soul, that is, of prophetic or apostolic virtue: that she be numbered in the flock of those who by the fruitfulness of their preaching have filled the spaces of the whole round of the earth. And although set in the body, yet they felt no losses of their spiritual course. And therefore she is praised, that, the heavenly precept enlightening her, [1868B] she is now shapely, now beautiful, who bears in her countenance the grace of chastity, and lifts up the necklaces of her neck, in which are the marks of patience and humility. And on this account, praising his bride, he said:

## 43.

*[n: (Vers. 9.)] How shapely are thy cheeks become, as a turtle-dove's, thy neck as necklaces* [var: Vulg. *Pulchrae sunt genae tuae sicut turturis; collum tuum sicut monilia*, "Beautiful are thy cheeks… thy neck as jewels"]. The countenance is freer where there is the consciousness of charity; and to bear the yoke of Christ is sweet, if thou reckon them to be the ornaments of thy neck, not its burdens. Lift up therefore thine eyes always to thy Lord, and seek God, that thou mayest find him. Raise up thy neck: thou wearest necklaces, not chains: even dumb beasts rejoice in trappings, and seem to themselves rather to be adorned than to be tied. *Cheeks as a turtle-dove's:* let them bear the marks of modesty; *the necklaces of the neck:* let them lift up the confidence of freedom; for the yoke of Christ is light, [1868C] and therefore the neck is not pressed down by it, but lifted up.

## 44.

*[n: (Vers. 10, 11.)] Likenesses,* he says, *of gold will we make for thee, out of distinctions of silver, until the king shall be in his reclining* [var: Vulg. *Murenulas aureas faciemus tibi, vermiculatas argento. Dum esset rex in accubitu suo*, "Golden chains will we make thee, inlaid with silver. While the king was at his couch"]. For out of those who are of the Law and of the prophets, they had before believed but moderately in the glory of the Lord Jesus; and his inheritance, spread abroad through the peoples, the more often it was tried, so much the more was it approved. For the frequent persecutions of the Church have brought forth for us the titles of the just, the victories of martyrdom. And so, like good gold: when the Church makes use of wrath, it feels no losses, but rather its brightness is increased, until Christ come into his kingdom, and lay back his head upon the faith of the Church. He, when he had come to the lost sheep of the house of Israel, had not where to lay back his head: but now faith is already fragrant, and therefore [1868D] the Church says: *My spikenard has given its odour;* and she says it, awaiting her recompense with confidence. The ointment of grace breathes forth, in which the Virgin brought forth, and the Lord Jesus took up the sacrament of the incarnation.
