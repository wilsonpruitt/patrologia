## 14.

In this shadow rested the faith of the Fathers, the holy devotion of the prophets. Say therefore, O religious congregation or holy soul: *In his shadow I have desired, and sat down.* A shadow is the jubilee year: for who sees its mystical things, so as to know face to face those things which were prescribed by the Law to be fulfilled in the five hundredth year? Shadows are the feast days according to the Law, from the first month, from the tenth day of the month unto the fourteenth, [1874C] and thence unto the twenty-first of the month. What shadow is that celebration between the first and the seventh month, at that nameless season? A shadow is the sanctified liberty on the sabbath. A shadow is the feast day of the new moon of the seventh month, and the memorial of the trumpets, the tenth day of the seventh month, the day of propitiation. Thou seest that the monad and the decad are two feast days. Likewise the fifteenth day, the day of the Tabernacles through eight days: in shadow are all these things. In shadow is the first day so called, and the eighth day so called is in shadow, which things are prescribed for the feasts of tabernacles according to the Law.

## 15.

Set before me some of those who believe out of the circumcision, learned in the Law, men of careful observance, and now enlightened by the acknowledgment of the Gospel, fattened with spiritual grace: in these the Church speaks, which sees Christ, [1874D] which receives the bridegroom, which is fed with his food: *In his shadow I have desired, and sat down, and his fruit is sweet in my jaws.* What is his sweet fruit, if not the preaching of the Lord's passion? as he himself says: *Behold the inheritance of the Lord, sons; the reward, the fruit of the womb [n: (Psal. CXXVI, 3)]*. For what fruit can be sweeter in our jaws than the remission of sins?

## 16.

*And his fruit is sweet to my [sic: *gutluri*].* There is a spiritual manna, that is, a rain of spiritual wisdom, which is poured from heaven upon the ingenious and upon those who seek, [1875A] and bedews the minds of the pious, and sweetens their jaws: he therefore who has understood the infusion of divine wisdom is delighted, and seeks no other food, nor lives on bread alone, but on every word of God. He who is more curious asks what that is which is sweeter than honey: the minister of God answers him: This is the bread which the Lord has given you to eat. What this bread is, hear: *The Word,* he says, *which God ordained.* *1562* This ordinance of God therefore, this nourishment, feeds the soul of the wise man, and enlightens and sweetens it, shining with the flash of truth, and soothing as with a certain honeycomb, so by the sweetness of divers virtues and by the word of wisdom. For *the honeycombs of honey are good words,* as it is written in the Proverbs [var: Vulg. Prov. 16:24 *Favus mellis composita verba*, "Well-ordered words are a honeycomb"]. Therefore, when it has tasted the fruit of sweetness, impatient, it hastens on to more perfect things, saying: [1875B]

## 17.

*[n: (Vers. 4-7.)] Bring me into the house of wine, set love in me, strengthen me with ointments, hedge me about with apples; for I am of wounded love. His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me. I have adjured you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the powers and strengths of the field, if ye shall rouse and rouse up again love, until she will* [var: Vulg. Cant. 2:4–7 *Introduxit me in cellam vinariam, ordinavit in me caritatem. Fulcite me floribus, stipate me malis, quia amore langueo… Adjuro vos, filiae Jerusalem, per capreas cervosque camporum, ne suscitetis, neque evigilare faciatis dilectam, quoadusque ipsa velit*, "He brought me into the wine cellar… Prop me up with flowers… because I languish with love… I adjure you… by the roes and harts of the fields, that ye stir not up, nor awake the beloved, till she please"]. Rightly does she seek him, rightly does she desire him; because our commander has well disposed all things in this course and journey. First of all he judged that this tent must be strengthened upon the foundation of faith. Then, if any lodging of ours is rough and dry and steep, yet that Commander of ours parts asunder the troubled things, waters the dry, makes fruitful the deserts. If there is anything of bitterness, anything of temptation, anything of weakness, our leader both tempers the bitter things, [1875C] and mitigates the anxious; he loosens hard things and strengthens them into strong.

## 18.

Wherever the king of the earth wills his army to rest, he appoints no ignoble village, none lacking in supplies, no sandy and bare places of growth: but a city noble in buildings, restored and rich in stores, or a pleasant field, or one green with pastures, or wooded and level places convenient for a standing camp. If therefore the kings of the earth know how to provide advantages for those who follow them, how much more does God, who is good, know how to dispose things profitable for those who love him? And first, if an unknown road must be taken, guides of the ways are chosen, who run before the column; but these commanders reckon this an injury to themselves: God, however, went before, when the Hebrews made their journey; and indeed he spoke to them in the pillar of cloud. And that thou mayest know [1875D] that he went before, *God,* he says, *went before them: by day indeed in a pillar of cloud, showing them the way; but by night in a pillar of fire, and the pillar of cloud did not fail by day [n: (Exod. XIII, 21 et 22)]* [var: Vulg. Exod. 13:21–22 *Dominus autem praecedebat eos ad ostendendam viam… Numquam defuit columna nubis per diem*, "And the Lord went before them… Never did the pillar of cloud fail by day"]. Following this pillar going before us, which does not fail lest she herself should fail, the Church was refreshed by its shadow; and therefore she says: *In his shadow I have desired, and sat down, and his fruit is sweet in my jaws [n: (Sup., v. 3)]*; because she was fed by the Lord, and led into the place of pastures and the water of refreshment. [1876A]

## 19.

*Bring me into the house of wine.* Out of the supplies made ready, she seeks to go forward to another lodging, in which she may take the grace of the mysteries and the sweetness of gladness. Hence too, advancing on the true way, she says: *Order love in me.* Good standing camps, where there is the fullness of charity. Charity cannot be without faith; for there are as it were three sureties of the Church: hope, faith, charity; when hope has gone before, when faith has been founded, charity is set in order, the Church is joined.

## 20.

*Strengthen me,* she says, *with ointments, hedge me about with apples.* Thou hast other lodgings, to which the Church succeeds with delight. These lodgings are of the cross of Christ and of the burial, in which the Church is wounded, but with the wound of charity; for it is a wound that Christ received, but it is ointment that he poured out. [1876B] It is an apple that hung; this apple the Church has tasted, and says: *And his fruit is sweet in my jaws.* And that thou mayest know that the Lord is an apple, thou hast read above: *As an apple among the trees of the wood, so is my cousin.* We too confess a wound, when we preach Christ crucified, but we are a good odour to God; because the cross of Christ is to the Jews a scandal, to the Greeks foolishness, but to us the power of God and wisdom. With this wound the Church is wounded, when she preaches the death of her Saviour, but this wound is of charity. And indeed he who believes not, denies: he who loves, confesses. The Manichean denies, the Christian confesses; and therefore it is written: *More useful are the wounds of a friend than the willing kisses of an enemy [n: (Prov. XXVII, 6)]* [var: Vulg. *Meliora sunt vulnera diligentis quam fraudulenta oscula odientis*, "Better are the wounds of one who loves than the deceitful kisses of one who hates"]. Beautifully therefore the Church says: *For wounded with charity [1876C] am I.*

## 21.

Let us bare our members to the good wound, let us bare them to the chosen arrow: this arrow is Christ, who says: *He has set me as a chosen arrow [n: (Esai. XLIX, 2)]*; it is good therefore to be wounded with this arrow. This advance is not of a middling lodging; not all can say that they are wounded with love. *1563* The apostles said it, when they were stoned for Christ and preached Christ: Paul said it, when he was beaten thrice with rods, and disputed day and night that Christ was to be adored by the nations: the martyrs say this, who are wounded for Christ; and because they have deserved to be wounded for his name, they love the more.
