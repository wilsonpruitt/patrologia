## 22.

*His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me.* And these lodgings belong to the Church as she walks upon the royal way; *For there are many mansions with my Father [n: (Joan. XIV, 2)]*. The Lord Jesus bears witness: *And behold I come, and summon you [n: (Ibid.)]*. Good is the lodging of wisdom, and whether in her left hand or in her right, it is a good lodging; because it is the royal way. And therefore [1877A] the wise messengers say: *By the royal way will we go; we will not turn aside to the right hand, nor to the left, until we pass thy borders [n: (Num. XX, 17)]*. This the messengers sent by Moses said to the king of Edom, that is, to the earthly king; because all earthly things, whether on the right or on the left, are evil. An evil lodging is folly, an evil lodging is intemperance; and therefore the Hebrew passes through these lodgings: he does not turn aside to them, but passes through, that he may come to the left hand of wisdom and to her right, and abide in these, where are the riches of simplicity, where glory, where length of life is. *For length of life is in her right hand,* as Solomon says: *and in her left hand riches and glory [n: (Prov. III, 16)]* [var: Vulg. *Longitudo dierum in dextera ejus*, "Length of days is in her right hand"]: in the right hand is life, in the left rest. Would that I might rest upon her left hand, and not seek pillows! *For woe to them who [1877B] sew pillows,* Ezekiel says [n: (Ezech. XIII, 18)] [var: Vulg. *Vae quae consuunt pulvillos sub omni cubito manus*, "Woe to them that sew cushions under every elbow"]. These lodgings the good leader prepares for his Church, and by the ways of wisdom he directs her journey. And indeed the wise man praises her ways: *Her ways are good ways, and all her paths are in peace [n: (Prov. III, 17)]* [var: Vulg. *Viae ejus viae pulchrae, et omnes semitae illius pacificae*, "Her ways are beautiful ways, and all her paths peaceable"].

## 23.

*His left hand under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me.* These things the bride speaks concerning Christ, the soul concerning the Word of God: but Christ is the same as the Word of God and wisdom. Blessed therefore is the soul whom wisdom embraces. Great is the hand of wisdom, great her right hand; she embraces the whole soul; for she is wholly fortified who is betrothed to the Word of God. For the fullness of wisdom is to fear God: the soul therefore which fears God fortifies itself with a full defence. Wisdom sends her left hand under her neck, [1877C] and her right hand into her embrace. Both her arms indeed are stretched out unto useful things; yet the several hands of wisdom have their own proper gifts: *In the right hand is length of life,* *in the left hand riches and glory.* With good gifts assuredly is her hand endowed on both sides; yet they have herein a variety of their own office, that they comprehend both present and future times; so that the left hand is the rewarder of present things, the right of things to come.

## 24.

We can know this also from the prophecy of the holy patriarch Israel; for when he had set his right hand upon Ephraim, and his left upon Manasseh, and Joseph had wished to change them out of consideration of their age, so that he might place the father's right hand upon the head of Manasseh the elder son, he would not, and said: *I know, son, and this one also shall be for a people, and this one shall be exalted, [1877D] but the younger brother shall be greater than he [n: (Genes. XLVIII, 19)]* [var: Vulg. *Scio, fili mi, scio: et iste quidem erit in populos, et multiplicabitur; sed frater ejus minor major erit illo*, "I know, my son, I know: and this one indeed shall be for peoples, and shall be multiplied"]. In what greater? In this surely, that he was preferred in the blessing, since those who came after said of Ephraim: *God make you as he made Ephraim and Manasseh [n: (Ibid., 20)]* [var: Vulg. *Faciat tibi Deus sicut Ephraim et sicut Manasse*, "God make thee as Ephraim and as Manasses" — singular]: or because his seed is a multitude of nations, which in this world chose labour, believing in the Lord Jesus, that it might have the consolation of things to come. But the seed of Manasseh is the people of forgetfulness, which, having forgotten its own author, has been exalted for a time in this world, and shall pay heavy penalties for the rest; because it denied its own God and Lord. [1878A]

## 25.

We have said this in order to prove that those things are preferable which are to come. And indeed the left hand of wisdom is under the head of the bride, but the right hand is higher, which embraces the whole bride. And so that hand is as it were the prop of present quiet, upon which the soul, leaning, rests. She has where to lay back her head, because the left hand of wisdom is riches and glory. For that season these things soothe, and are in part a solace; and therefore: *The Son of man had not where to lay back his* *1564* *head [n: (Matth. VIII, 20)]*; *because, when he was rich, he was made poor [n: (II Cor. VIII, 9)]* [var: Vulg. *quoniam propter vos egenus factus est, cum esset dives*, "because for your sakes he became needy, whereas he was rich"]. Nor did he seek any glory of this world; because he had come, not to succour a portion, but the whole human race, saying: *Ye are angry with me because I made a whole man sound on the sabbath [n: (Joan. VII, 23)]*. that is, wholly — not that he might enrich him [1878B] by the bestowal of substance, not with the marks of honours, not that he might heap him up with an increase of worldly glory; for these things have not the fullness of blessedness and of grace: but wholly, that is, so as to embrace the length of eternal life. For the life which is in the right hand of wisdom is not like the common life, but is length of life, so that he who receives life from wisdom may obtain not scantiness of life, but perpetuity and the length of eternity.

## 26.

A good garment is the eternity of life. And so the bride, upheld by the riches of pomegranates, that is, of divers and innumerable fruits, and chiefly by the odours of faith, of grace, of wisdom and of glory and of eternal life, which are about the left hand and the right hand of the bridegroom, rouses Christ through holy souls by the applause of the congregations that praise him, [1878C] saying:

## 27.

*I have adjured you, daughters of Jerusalem, by the powers* from *the strengths of the field, if ye shall rouse and rouse up again love, until she will.* That field which the Lord blessed is not this earthly one, either bristling with woods, or broken with torrents, or useless for vines, or stony with barren gravel, or gaping and dry with drought, or wet with blood, or uncultivated with brambles and thorns; but that field of which the Lord says: *And the beauty of the field is with me [n: (Psalm. XLIX, 19)]*. In this field is found that grape which, being pressed, poured out blood and washed the world: in this field is that fig tree under which the saints shall rest, refreshed by the sweetness of spiritual grace: in this field is that fruitful olive tree, flowing with the ointment of the Lord's peace: in this field flower [1878D] the pomegranates, which cover very many fruits under one defence of faith, and cherish them with a certain embrace of charity.

## 28.

Since therefore wisdom and honourable brightness are so far perfect, if they have charity (for the fullness of the law [n: (Rom. XIII, 10)] is charity), she wills charity to be roused and roused up again: to be roused in the Old Testament, to be roused up again in the New. Charity is God, as we read; charity is Christ [n: (I Joan. IV, 8)] [var: Vulg. *Deus caritas est*, "God is charity"]. He is roused *as a lion, and a lion's whelp; that he may go up out of the seed of Judah [n: (Gen. XLIX, 9)]* [var: Vulg. *Catulus leonis Juda: ad praedam, fili mi, ascendisti*, "Juda is a lion's whelp: to the prey, my son, thou art gone up"]: he is roused up again as [1879A] one sleeping, as one reclining; because he was raised from the dead not by a human majesty, but by his own and his Father's. Whence Scripture says: *Who shall rouse him up?* For neither an angel nor an alien Power could rouse him, since he himself was rousing others. Therefore when he here says: *If ye shall rouse and rouse up again love, until she will;* he speaks of those who can fittingly preach his resurrection, so as to kindle in their hearers the ardour of faith and of devotion: or Christ is roused in those who first come to him, and roused up again in those who, after they have come, have slept. Christ therefore sleeps for the negligent, and is roused in the saints.

## 29.

This charity therefore she rouses and rouses up again, until she receive his voice and summon his presence: because, being sought, he not only comes, but even [1879B] comes leaping. But by now the bridegroom was at hand, so as to prove that which, while we are yet speaking, he says: *I am here.* He is present, because he is roused by the daughters of Judah, as it were roused up again by the daughters of Jerusalem. The Church hears the sound of his voice, and says:

## 30.

*[n: (Vers. 8.)] The voice of my cousin: behold, here he comes, leaping over the mountains, bounding over the hills* [var: Vulg. *Vox dilecti mei; ecce iste venit saliens in montibus, transiliens colles*, "The voice of my beloved; behold he cometh leaping upon the mountains"]. This book is like a maniple, having many noble persons, as it were a triumphal maniple, and signifying many acts, which we can rather understand than hold expressed. For, as though she had heard the bridegroom coming and [sic: *collonquentem*] with certain who were journeying along with him, the bride says: *The voice of my cousin.* While she speaks with the daughters of Jerusalem, and asks that they stir up and rouse up again the bridegroom, suddenly, perceiving as it were the sound of a voice sent [1879C] from afar, she says: *The voice of my cousin,* announcing him whom before she was seeking to have announced to her. Him whom she desired to be roused by others, believing him now, roused up again by her own prayer, to be coming from the Father, she gladly says: *The voice of my cousin.* Well did she add, *my cousin;* that no other women, but she alone, his cousin, might claim his coming for herself. *1565*
