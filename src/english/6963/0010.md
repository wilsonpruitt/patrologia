## 31.

*Behold*, she says, *he is come* [var: Vulg. Cant 2:8 *ecce iste venit*, "behold, he cometh"]: still am I seeking him, and he has already come; still am I catching at intercessions that he may come, and he is already near at hand; I long for charity to be roused up in me, I deem myself wounded of charity, and charity herself hastens to me the more. I said, Come; he leaps and leaps over. I ask him to come with grace; he works increases of graces; and while he comes, he carries increases of grace with him, [1879D] and by coming he acquires, because he too is eager to please his own beloved. He leaps upon the high places, that he may ascend to the bride; for the bride's marriage-chamber is the tribunal of Christ. He leaps upon Adam, he leaps over the Synagogue; he leaps upon the Gentiles, he leaps over the Jews.

## 32.

Let us see him leaping He leaps from heaven into the Virgin, from the womb into the manger, from the Jordan into the cross, from the cross into the tomb, into heaven from the sepulchre. Prove to me, David, that he leaps; prove that he runs. For thou hast said: *He hath rejoiced as a giant to run the way. His going forth is from the height of heaven, and his meeting even to the height thereof; and there is none that can hide himself from his heat [n: (Psal. XVIII, 6 et 7)]*. And now he leaps, and now he runs, from [1880A] the heart of the Father upon his saints, from the East upon the West, from the North upon the South. This is he who ascends above the sunset, he himself above the heavens of heavens toward the East, he himself ascends above the mountains, he himself above the hills.

## 33.

Would that I, wretched man, might say it, and that my soul might say: *Behold, he is come*, and he is come not upon earthly things, not upon the valleys, but he is come *Leaping upon the mountains* [var: Vulg. Cant 2:8 *saliens in montibus*, "leaping in the mountains"]; for God is the God of mountains, not of valleys: where therefore he leaps, he leaps upon mountains. If thou be a mountain, he leaps upon thee; he leaps upon Isaiah, he leaps upon Jeremiah, he leaps upon Peter, John, James. *The mountains are round about him [n: (Psalm. CXXIV, 2)]*: if one cannot be a mountain and thou dost not prevail, be at least a hill, that Christ may ascend upon thee; and if he leaps over, that his passing may guard thee. [1880B]

## 34.

We have spoken of Christ and the Church; let us speak of the soul and the Word. The soul of the just man is the bride of the Word. If she should desire, if she should long, if she should pray and pray unceasingly, and pray without any disputing, and stretch herself wholly toward the Word: suddenly she seems to hear the voice of him whom she does not see, and in the fear of her senses she recognizes the odor of his divinity, which those who believe well for the most part experience. Suddenly the nostrils of the soul are filled with spiritual grace, and she feels the breath of his presence breathe upon her, of him whom she seeks, and she says: Behold, this is the very one whom I seek, the very one whom I desire.

## 35.

Is it not so, that when we ponder something out of the Scriptures and cannot find the explanation of it, while we doubt, while we seek, suddenly he seems to us to ascend, as it were, upon the mountains, that is, upon the loftiest doctrines. [1880C] Then, appearing to us as it were upon the hills, he enlightens the mind, that he may pour into our senses what seemed difficult to be found: and so the Word becomes, as it were from absent, present in our hearts. And again, when something is somewhat obscure to us, the Word is as it were withdrawn, and we long for the coming of him as though he were absent; and again he appears, he shows himself to us as though he were present with us in the knowing of those things which we seek.

## 36.

He leaps, then, often in the heart of each one; he leaps over and goes out and returns: if thou shouldst follow, if thou shouldst seek, if by the welcome disputation of faithful teachers thou shouldst beg that the Word which had gone out and had passed over be raised up again for thee. Like her who sought and found, who said: *My brother has passed by, my soul went out at his word [n: (Cant. V, 6)]* [var: Vulg. *at ille declinaverat atque transierat; anima mea liquefacta est, ut locutus est*, "but he had turned aside and passed on; my soul melted when he spoke"]. Therefore even if he leaps upon the mountains, follow; even if [1880D] upon the hills, follow. For there are found on the mountains and on the hills the hunters of the Lord, who track out those who are taken for life; for thus God said through Jeremiah: *Behold, I send many fishers and many hunters, and they shall hunt them upon every mountain and upon every hill [n: (Jerem. XVI, 16)]* [var: Vulg. *de omni monte et de omni colle*, "from every mountain and from every hill"]. There, then, let the people of God be sought and found in the doctrine, the grace, the discipline of Peter and of Paul; that they be not in the deep valley, where there is weeping, but on the mountains from which Christ enlightens each man. And when we read Peter, Christ enlightens; and when we read Paul, Christ enlightens. Paul healed, Christ enlightened; for when the name of the Lord Jesus had been invoked, he rose again, by whose gift he was made [1881A] whole. Peter raised up a dead woman, Christ enlightened; and therefore he says: *Thou dost enlighten wonderfully from the everlasting mountains [n: (Psal. LXXV, 5)]*.

## 37.

We therefore who cannot be mountains, let us stand *1566* on the mountains or on the hills; so that when the Lord shall have sent his fishers and hunters to hunt those who are upon every mountain, or upon every hill, that is, in the precepts of the law and of the prophets, and who have all their conversation in the knowledge of the New and the Old Testament, they may find us ready and may gather us like good ears of corn, reapers sent at the opportune season; because if anyone shall have been found outside the mountain or the hill, he will not be able to be gathered as a good ear by those who, that we may make mention of another comparison, shall be sent to separate the wheat [1881B] from the chaff. The Lord therefore has skilled ministers of very many operations: the same are fishers, the same also are hunters, and reapers. If in the time of harvest, at which these are directed to reap, thou shouldst wait ripe, thou wilt be able to behold him leaping upon the mountains, and thou wilt see the Lord Jesus like a roe or a young hart upon the mountains of Bethel. For he leaps upon the Church, which is the house of bread, in that he strengthens the hearts of the faithful; whence the bride also says of him:

## 38.

*[n: (Vers. 9.)] My cousin is like a roe or a young hart, upon the mountains of Bethel;* [var: Vulg. Cant 2:9 *similis est dilectus meus capreae hinnuloque cervorum*, "my beloved is like a roe and a young hart," with no mountains of Bethel] for he leaps upon the Church, which is the house of God, in that he strengthens the hearts of the faithful. Rightly, like a little roe, because the goat is pastured in high places. She is called *dorcas* from seeing, [1881C] for the *dorcadion* is of keener sight. What is fitter to Christ than this, who saw the Father whom no one has seen, or, if anyone has seen him in Christ, the Son himself revealed him? Rightly *like a young hart*: the fawn is as it were a son, in whom the force of the paternal nature has grown, so that hidden things do not lie concealed from him, serpents flee, poisons do not harm. Accordingly the serpent, led forth out of his lurking-places, who was being led forth out of the man, said: *Why hast thou come before the time to torment us [n: (Matth. VIII, 29)]*. Let us therefore gaze upon this leaping fawn, that we may not be able to fear the serpent.

## 39.

*Behold, he himself stands behind our wall, looking through the windows, peering through the nets.* [var: Vulg. Cant 2:9 *prospiciens per cancellos*, "looking through the lattices"] He comes, then, and first is behind the wall, that he may loose the enmities of soul and body, the wall being taken away which seemed to bring a hindrance [1881D] to concord. Then he peers through the windows: what the windows are, hear the Prophet saying: *The windows are opened from heaven [n: (Esai. XXIV, 18)]* [var: Vulg. Isa 24:18 *cataractae de excelsis apertae sunt*, "the floodgates from on high are opened"]. He signifies therefore the prophets, through whom the Lord looked upon the human race, before he himself came down to the earth. And today too, if any soul seeks him much, she will merit much of mercy; since to him who seeks much, much is owed. If therefore any soul should seek him more earnestly, she hears his voice from afar: and although she seeks him from others, she hears his voice before those very ones from whom she seeks. She sees him coming to her with a leap, that is, hastening and running [1882A] and leaping over those who with feeble heart cannot take in his power; then peering through the riddles of the prophets, by reading them and holding fast their words; for she sees him peering, but as it were through a window, not as though he were yet present. She sees him raised above the nets. What is this? Unless perhaps because those nets are nets to us, they are not nets to him, in that that soul is still within things sensible and worldly, which are wont to catch the mind of man and to wrap it in a certain fold of their own.
