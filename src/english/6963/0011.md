## 40.

*Behold, he himself stands behind our wall, looking through the windows, peering through the nets.* [var: Vulg. Cant 2:9 *prospiciens per cancellos*, "looking through the lattices"] For the Church, or the soul of the just man, had seen him leaping like a fawn upon the mountains, and, first catching sight of him suddenly behind the wall of her house, peering through the windows, [1882B] raised above the nets, she exults and rejoices, because she too is loved by the Bridegroom. And he, as though he himself also were wounded by the beauty of charity, when at first he was away from his beloved, was present at her asking for kisses (for he is asked when it is said [n: (Cant. I, 1)]: *Let him kiss me with the kisses of his mouth* ); then did not spurn the prayers and blandishments of the bride as she offered him her breasts; and kindly led her into the inner parts of the house; then, playing as with a wanton love, because he wished to try the senses of her who loved him, going out often that he might be sought by the Bride, returning often that he might be invited to kisses, stood behind the wall, peered through the windows, raised above the nets; that he might neither be wholly absent, nor as it were wholly enter in, and that he himself might call the bride to himself; that the exchanges of coming to one another might become the more welcome, and that they might kindle love [1882C] with mutual words. *1567*

## 41.

*[n: (Vers. 10.)] Rise up,* he says, *come, my neighbor, my well-formed one, my dove;* [var: Vulg. Cant 2:10 *surge, propera, amica mea, columba mea, formosa mea, et veni*, "arise, make haste, my love, my dove, my beautiful one, and come"] and thou too, if thou hast a wall well founded, not that middle wall which divides the members of one house, but one built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, so that its framed structure may grow into a temple, and may not sever its inner parts but fortify them. If therefore thou hast in thee the building of God, and thy windows stand ever open toward the East, the Word comes, *He stands behind thy wall [n: (Cant. II, 9)]*; for *the eyes of the Lord are upon the just [n: (Psal. XXXIII, 16)]*; *he peers through thy windows [n: (Cant. II, 9)]*.

## 42.

What are these windows? We read of the windows of which Jeremiah says: *Death has entered through the windows* [1882D] [n: (Jerem. IX, 21)] [var: Vulg. *quia ascendit mors per fenestras nostras*, "for death is come up through our windows"], through which avarice entered, lust entered. Thine eye is a window. If thou hast looked upon a woman to lust after her, death has entered through thine eye: if thou hast looked upon a widow's estate, or the treasures of a minor, and hast stirred up thy covetings, *Death has entered through the window.* As therefore death enters through these, life enters too: if, looking upon the comeliness of a consecrated girl, thou reverencest the Lord Jesus, in that the age of old age has come into her tender years, a life unspotted, and thou thyself offerest thy daughter, that she may be consecrated with the holy veil; if thou lookest upon a minor's estate not like an anxious invader, but guardest it like a diligent parent with religious [1883A] affection: through these windows Christ peers, that he may call the bride.

## 43.

*Raised above the nets:* rightly *raised;* because he alone is the one whom the nets of sins have not enwrapped. We were all within the nets, nay rather we are still within the nets; because no one is without sin save Jesus alone, whom, knowing no sin, the Father made sin for us; for indeed he delivered him to the snares, delivered him to the nets: sending him not in the sin in which all men were, but in the likeness of sinful flesh, that of sin he might condemn sin in the flesh. The flesh was sin, according to that saying, because it had been condemned by a hereditary curse; sin was the enticement and the handmaid of sin. The Lord Jesus came, and in flesh liable to sin he practiced the warfare of virtue: our members [1883B] have been made now no longer weapons of lust, but weapons of virtue; for where the incitements of lust were, there now are the dwellings of chastity. Here therefore he came to the snares, but willingly: he came to the nets, but secure. All things were full of nets, crammed with snares; hear him who says: *In this way wherein I walked, the proud have hidden a snare for me [n: (Psal. CXLI, 4)]* [var: Vulg. Ps 141:4 *in via hac qua ambulabam absconderunt laqueum mihi*, without *superbi*, "the proud"]. And in the book of the wisdom of Sirach thou art admonished to know that thou walkest in the midst of snares [n: (Eccli. IX, 20)]. As many vices, so many nets: as many sins, so many snares.

## 44.

Already the hereditary bonds held thee; Jesus came to the snares, that he might loose Adam: he came to set free what had perished. We were all held by the nets, no one could rescue another, since he could not rescue himself. Such a one therefore was needful, whom the chains of human generation, liable to offenses, [1883C] would not hold, whom avarice had not taken, whom guile had not bound. That one alone was Jesus, who, though he had surrounded himself with the bonds of this flesh, was not captured, was not fettered: but bursting them and dissolving them, he called the Church the more to himself, *Peering through the snares, raised above the nets;* that she too might learn not to be held by chains. In fact he was so far from being far from the chains, that for us he underwent even death, yet not made a slave of death, but free among the dead; for he was free who had the power of loosing death. In fine, let him himself teach thee who says: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up again [n: (Joan. II, 19)]* [var: Vulg. John 2:19 *et in tribus diebus excitabo illud*, "and in three days I will raise it up"]. Let us hear, then, what he says:

## 45.

[n: (Vers. 11 - 13)]. *Rise up, come, my neighbor;* that is, rise from the dead, rise up from the chains by which thou wast held [1883D] encompassed. Rise up, for I have risen again for thee: loose the bonds of iniquity, for I have already loosed them for thee: come, for already the nets are loosed, the Virgin has borne, a child is born of a virgin, he owes nothing to the inheritance of woman, as the son of a woman he is not held: behold the middle wall of the enclosure now loosed, which divided the concord of the inward affections, and stirred up in diverse directions the dissensions of bodily passions. Come therefore secure; I long to see thy face and to hear thy voice: come, that thou mayest no longer see me through nets, but, beloved, mayest enjoy face to face the countenance of thy lover.

## 46.

And let these things be run through thus far in the moral sense. But those [1884A] mystical things, if we can, let us behold at least in their outermost line. The Bride was sitting within the house, devoted to the Lord, within the wall of the Law and *1568* of the prophets, founded upon a building of spiritual stones, which shut in and walled about the royal house, full of delight and gladness. She marveled at the royal treasures, and gazed upon them anxiously, longing to take to herself the wisdom which might show her these riches. She was in secret, but she was seeking the interpreter of that very secret. The Lord Jesus came, leaping upon the mountains. To us he seemed to come late, but he was hastening. He was leaping, in fine, and leaping over, that he might leap over the bodily and stony doctrines of the Jews. He stood behind the wall of the house which was in the Old Testament, peering through the window of the Law and the caverns of the prophets. [1884B]

## 47.

Not yet were the vestibules of that house open, not yet had the keys of wisdom laid open the barriers of the gates by which the inner parts of the Law were shut; yet from the higher part, that is, the spiritual part, looking forth he calls the Church, that she, rising sublime through the Law and the prophets to the summit of the Gospel, may tread with fearless foot upon the nets and the knots of Jewish interpretation. And therefore she is called *Neighbor*, that she may cleave to Christ and not seek worldly things: therefore *Well-formed*, that she may bear the shapely feet of those who preach the Gospel: therefore *Dove*, that she may seek spiritual things and leave earthly things behind.

## 48.

*Behold,* he says, *the winter is past, the rain is gone, it has departed to itself, flowers have been seen in the earth, the time of cutting is come.* [var: Vulg. Cant 2:11-12 *jam enim hiems transiit; imber abiit, et recessit. Flores apparuerunt in terra nostra; tempus putationis advenit*, "for now the winter is past; the rain is gone and departed. The flowers have appeared in our land; the time of pruning is come"] Before the coming of Christ it was winter: Christ came, he made summer. Then all things were destitute of flowers, [1884C] bare of virtues: Christ suffered, and all things began to be made fruitful with the buds of a new grace. The rain of overflowing wantonness is gone, and the clouds risen from foul disgraces are now dispersed in the summer serenity of a pure conscience. Therefore they do not escape whose flight is made in winter; because they do not follow the passion of the Lord, they do not take up their cross, and they follow Christ.

## 49.

Rain hinders flowers: but now flowers are seen in the earth. Good flowers are the apostles, who poured out the fragrance of their diverse writings and works. *The time of cutting is come,* when ripe grain is stored in the barns; and he who reaps receives a reward. *The voice of the turtledove has been heard,* because she has found a nest for herself; for the Church is the house of chastity. *The fig tree,* which for its unfruitfulness was ordered to be cut down, *has now begun to bear fruit.* [1884D] The vine carried over out of Egypt, destroyed by the casting down of its walls, is now not assailed by beasts; nor is it unshapely with brambles, but now fragrant with flowers.
