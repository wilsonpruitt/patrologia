## 50.

*Rise up,* he says, *come, my neighbor, my well-formed one, my dove, my perfect one; neighbor* indeed by the desire of faith, *well-formed* by the comeliness of virtue, *dove* by spiritual grace; for *the wings of the dove covered with silver* signify that eternal power, and the flight of the dove declared the presence of the Holy Spirit. Christ therefore calls her to himself that she may come, since she was already coming endowed with spiritual dowries. *For behold,* he says, *the winter is past, the rain is gone, it has departed [1885A] to itself, flowers seen in the earth.* See in what manner the holy Church is invited. *The winter,* he says, *has departed to itself,* lest, being naked, she dread the winter, a winter not of the season but of infirmity, which strips the fruitful field of the soul of every flower. For there is a winter not of the earthly sun, there is a winter of the mind, when cold glides into the soul, when the warmth of the spirit fades away, when the vigor of sense is loosened, when excessive humour overflows and weighs down the mind, when the inward sight grows dark. And therefore the Lord says: *See that your flight be not in winter or on the sabbath [n: (Matth. XXIV, 20)]* [var: Vulg. Matt 24:20 *orate autem ut non fiat fuga vestra in hieme vel sabbato*, "but pray that your flight be not in winter or on the sabbath"]; for it is good that the day of judgment or of death should come then, when the kindly temperateness of the soul is in vigor, when the heavenly mystery shines back with serene light, when our heart burns within us. For then Christ is present, as Ammaon and Cleophas testify [1885B] in the Gospel, saying: *Was not our heart burning within us in the way, when he had opened the Scriptures in us [n: (Luc. XXIV, 32)]*. And the soul is in vigor when the flower too is seen in the earth. Who is this flower of good fragrance, save he who said: *I am the flower of the field and the lily of the valleys [n: (Supra, vers. 1)]*: of whom also it is written in Isaiah: *A rod shall go forth from the root of Jesse, and a rod shall ascend from the root, and a flower shall go forth from the rod [n: (Esai. XI, 1)]* [var: Vulg. Isa 11:1 *et egredietur virga de radice Jesse, et flos de radice ejus ascendet*, "and there shall come forth a rod out of the root of Jesse, and a flower shall rise up out of his root," with no middle member]. The root indeed is the family of the Jews, the rod is Mary, the flower of Mary is Christ: and when he shines forth in our earth, and is fragrant in the field of the soul, or blooms in his own Church, we can neither fear cold nor dread rain, but may await the day of judgment. And therefore the Church, that she might see this flower, was hastening with all zeal, as she herself testifies *1569* [1885C] in what follows, saying: *In the bed by nights I sought him whom my soul loves [n: (Cant. III, 1)]* [var: Vulg. Cant 3:1 *in lectulo meo per noctes quaesivi quem diligit anima mea*, "in my little bed by night I sought him whom my soul loveth"].

## 50.

*The fig tree has put forth its green figs.* The comparison of that tree is apt for the Synagogue, because just as that tree, overflowing with flowing leaves, disappoints the hope of its owner by an empty expectation of the yields that were hoped for: so also in the Synagogue, while its teachers, unfruitful in works, glory only in words as in overflowing leaves, the empty shade of the Law prevails; and the hope of a false yield, once awaited, mocks the prayers of the believing people.

## 51.

There is also in the nature of the tree that whereby thou mayest the more believe that the likeness of the Synagogue is expressed by comparison with it. For indeed, if thou inquire diligently, thou wilt find the habit of this tree's nature distinct from the practice of the rest; [1885D] for others bear the flower before the fruit, and mark out the apples that are to come by the flower that heralds them: this one alone from the beginning buds forth apples in place of flowers; in the others the flower is shaken off and the apples are born, in this one the apples fall off, that apples may succeed them. Therefore those earlier ones, void of fruit, come forth in the place of the flower: thus, by a certain premature habit of being born, they that know not the order of nature cannot keep the benefit of nature. For indeed where the bud is wont to thrust itself out from the middle of the bark, there each of the tiniest apples of this kind breaks forth: concerning which we read in the Song [1886A] of Songs: *The fig tree has put forth its green figs.* And so, while the other shoots whiten in the first spring, the fig alone knows not how to grow white with a flower of its own; perhaps because there is for these no riper use in their apples. For when others succeed, which are spat out as though degenerate, and the weak root being dry, they are stripped away, those being renewed whose sap is more serviceable. Yet some very few remain, and do not fall, upon which this yield has smiled, so that they push forth from the middle of two twigs by a short tendril breaking out, whereby, covered by twin defenses, as though in the lap of nature their parent, they grow up by the fostering of a fuller sap. These, called forth by the mildness of a gentler air, and grown to maturity through longer seasons, when they have put off the wild temper of their former sap, are preferred to the rest in appearance and in the grace of ripeness. [1886B]

## 52.

Look now upon the observances and the minds of the Jews, who, as it were the first fruits of the ill-bearing Synagogue, fell after the likeness of what is slipping away, that apples of our kind, destined to abide beyond the age, might succeed them. For indeed the first people of the Synagogue, weak as it were with a dry root of works, could not draw in the richness of natural wisdom. And therefore it fell like useless fruit, that as it were from the same tendrils of a fruitful tree, out of the richness of the ancient religion, the new people of the Church might come forth. Therefore he who was ceased to be, that this one might begin who was not: yet the first out of Israel, whom the branch of a stronger nature had lifted up, under the shadow of the Law and of the cross, colored in the bosom of each by a twin sap, after the pattern of the ripening green fig, surpassed the rest in the grace of most beautiful fruits, to whom [1886C] it is said: *You shall sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel [n: (Matth. XIX, 28)]* [var: Vulg. Matt 19:28 *sedebitis et vos super sedes duodecim*, "you also shall sit upon twelve seats"].

## 53.

*The vines in flower have given their fragrance [n: (Sup., v. 5)]*. Well is the Church designated by the vine; for as the vine is wedded to trees, so the body is joined to the soul, and the soul also to the body: as the vine, while it is bound, is raised up, and when cut back is not diminished but increased; so the holy people, while it is bound, is stripped: while it is humbled, it is lifted up: while it is cut back, it is crowned. Nay more, as a tender shoot, cut off from the old tree, is grafted into the offspring of another root: so this holy people, the scars of the old shoot being smoothed away, grows up cherished in that new sign of the cross as in the lap of a devoted parent; and the Holy Spirit, poured into this prison-like body, as though let down into deep [1886D] trenches of the earth, washes away by the watering of the water of salvation whatever is foul, and raises the bearing of our members into heavenly discipline.

## 54.

This vine the diligent husbandman is wont to dig, to water, to strive over; and, the mounds of earthy mass being cleared away, he is accustomed now to scorch the works of our body with the sun, now to drench them with rain, and to hem the field close, lest the bud be hurt by brambles, lest the shade of the leaves run riot, and lest the unfruitful boasting of words, by overshadowing the virtues, hinder the ripeness of the natural disposition. [1887A]

## 55.

*[n: (Vers. 14.)] And come,* he says, *my dove, in the covering of the rock, beside the outwork;* [var: Vulg. Cant 2:14 *columba mea in foraminibus petrae, in caverna maceriae*, "my dove in the clefts of the rock, in the hollow of the wall"] that is, come beside the Gospel: the bulwarks of thy faith are the deeds of Christ; the supports of thy wall *1570* are the words of the Lord; the passion of the Lord's body is thy strength.

## 56.

*Show me thy face, and make thy voice known; for thy voice is sweet, and thy face comely.* [var: Vulg. Cant 2:14 *sonet vox tua in auribus meis: vox enim tua dulcis*, "let thy voice sound in mine ears: for thy voice is sweet"] Sweet is thy voice; *for with the mouth confession is made unto salvation:* and comely the face; because she who is not ashamed of her author is not confounded at her Redeemer. She shows therefore her face, bearing before her the seal of the cross; and she makes her voice known, taking up the authority of preaching. For by the covering of the body of Christ, whereby he was redeemed from sin, he finds the defense of spiritual grace, so that he may both perceive and speak the things that are for his salvation. Sweet [1887B] therefore is the voice, which spoke in the divine testimonies: *Comely the face,* which was not confounded in the sight of kings.

## 57.

*Rise up,* he says, *come, my neighbor;* that is, rise up from the delights of the world, rise up from earthly things, and come to me, thou who still labourest and art laden, thou who art anxious for the things that are of the world: come above the world, come to me, for I have overcome the world: come near to me, now beautiful with the comeliness of eternal life, now a dove, that is, gentle and meek, now wholly full of spiritual grace. Rightly therefore she ought no longer to fear the nets, since he calls the soul to himself who could not be caught by the temptations and the nets of the world. For since we men walk in the midst of snares, through the appetite for food we are liable at once to nets and to snares. [1887C] He, set in a body, did not fear the nets, but was raised above the nets, that is, above the temptations of the world and the passions of the body; nay, he made others also to be raised above them.

## 58.

Rise up, come, my neighbor, fear not the nets, already the winter is past. The passover is come, indulgence is come, the remission of sins is come, temptation has ceased, the rain is gone, the storm and the buffeting are gone. Before the coming of Christ it was winter; after the coming of Christ there are flowers, whence he says: *Flowers have been seen in the earth.* Where before there were thorns, there now are flowers. *The time,* he says, *of cutting is come.* Where before there was desert, there is harvest. *The voice of the turtledove has been heard in our land:* well did the prophet add *our*, as though marveling, that where before there was shamelessness, there is chastity. *The fig tree has put forth its green figs.* [1887D] She who as unfruitful was ordered to be cut down has now begun to bring forth fruit. But why dost thou doubt, because he said green figs? He shakes off the earlier ones, that he may bring forth later and better ones: as the fruit of the Synagogue is cast away, but that of the Church is renewed.
