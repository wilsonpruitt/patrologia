## 59.

And although there is full tranquillity, and the mysteries have grown to their strength, yet again he says: *Rise up secure in the covering of the rock,* that is, safe by the protection of my passion and by the defense of faith. For they sucked honey out of the rock and oil out of the firm rock. Clothed with this covering, the souls of the pious are no longer naked, and this is their [1888A] outwork. And therefore to this soul also he says: *And come thou, my dove, in the covering of the rock beside the outwork: show me thy face, and make thy voice known.* He exhorts her to confidence, that she be not ashamed of the cross of Christ, nor of its seal. He exhorts her to confession; he wills that all snares be removed, that the good fragrance of faith may breathe, that the day may shine, that the shadow of the hostile night may not harm; since he who is beside Christ says: *The night is passed, and the day has drawn near [n: (Rom. XXIII, 12)]*. There is also a shadow of worldly things which passes away, and the day of heavenly things is Christ, who shines for his saints.

## 60.

*[n: (Vers. 15.)] Take for us the little foxes that destroy the vines, that our vines may flower.* [var: Vulg. Cant 2:15 *capite nobis vulpes parvulas quae demoliuntur vineas: nam vinea nostra floruit*, "catch us the little foxes that destroy the vines: for our vineyard hath flourished"] Scripture calls him a man who is after the image and likeness [1888B] of God; but the sinner it is wont to call not a man, but either a serpent, or a horse neighing after females, or a little fox, or a beast of burden. Of one of these the Lord says: *Go, tell that fox [n: (Luc. XIII, 32)]*, that is, of Herod. And elsewhere, when he perceived that he was being questioned deceitfully, he said: *The foxes have dens, and the birds of heaven nests, where they may rest [n: (Matth. VIII, 20)]* [var: Vulg. Matt 8:20 *vulpes foveas habent, et volucres caeli nidos; Filius autem hominis non habet ubi caput reclinet*, "the foxes have holes, and the birds of the air nests; but the Son of man hath not where to lay his head"]; in that they linger deceitfully in certain lurking-places of earthly passions.

## 61.

Whence Samson too bound foxes to himself two by two, to whose tails he tied torches, and sent them out through the harvests of the *Allophyli* [n: (Judic. XV)]: signifying by this figure that wicked and deceitful men, and most of all heretics, have a tongue free for barking, but that their outcomes are hindered, or their beginnings quarrelsome, [1888C] while the end of their fraud is assigned to burning. And therefore he sent out three hundred foxes also, in that the faithless do indeed desire to commend themselves by the preaching of the cross, but cannot hold its mystery: they who, by this *1571* preaching made up, and false and feigned, attempt rather to burn up the fruits of others, whereas assuredly the true cross of the Lord does not burn up the merits of others, but makes them fruitful.

## 62.

And deservedly is it written: *Take for us the little foxes that destroy the vines, that our vines may flower.* By which it is shown that either the Lord Jesus or the Church commands that the wiles of the deceitful be driven out from his vines, lest they harm the tiny vines, since they cannot harm vines already grown up. For a heretic can tempt the imperfect; he cannot supplant [1888D] the perfect.

## 63.

*[n: (Vers. 16.)] My brother is mine, and I am his.* [var: Vulg. Cant 2:16 *dilectus meus mihi, et ego illi*, "my beloved to me, and I to him"] This is the voice of a virgin; for to keep the body unspotted from mingling with a man, and, in a body of clay, to bear up the palm of chastity by the guardianship of integrity even to the converse of angels, is a private benefit and a public praise. How rare on earth is he who can say this, how estranged from vices, how set apart from every stain of sin, who has nothing in common with the world, saying: *My beloved is mine and I am his, he shall abide in the midst of my breasts.* [var: Vulg. Cant 1:12 *inter ubera mea commorabitur*, "he shall abide between my breasts" — the second member of the printed lemma follows this verse rather than 2:16] That is to say, let him claim nothing [1889A] of this world for himself, one who has no possession of bodily desires, whom lust does not inflame, avarice does not goad, wantonness does not unman, excess does not discolor, ambition does not lay low, envy does not waste, no care of secular business makes anxious: a true minister of the altar, born for God, not for himself; for Levi, as the interpretation has it, signifies: He himself taken up for me; it signifies: And he himself is mine; it signifies also merely: Taken up; it signifies: And taken up for me.

## 64.

The very same man is Levi both for me and for God. How the same is a priest both for me and for God. And he is both my advocate and the Lord's intercessor: offering sacrifice for me, and the same man offering himself to the Lord. In fine, elsewhere too by the interpretation of Levi it is said, *For me;* for indeed if Levi be named by me, Levi is for me: if he offer for me, [1889B] he is for me: if he intercede for me, he is for me: but if he be called by the Lord, he is said to be mine: that is, tributary to no other, tithe-paying to no other, bountiful to no other out of a possession. He himself is mine, that is, as one abounding in place of all his own goods. From him I seek no tithes, no fruits, no gifts, no offerings; he himself is to me in place of an offering, he himself in place of tribute. Not bountiful to me in his own possession, but he himself is my possession, he himself my fruit, he himself my understanding. Taken up by me, or taken up for me: this cannot be without divine grace. For as a possession cannot be mine unless I have bought it, so he cannot be Levi unless he has been taken up by the Lord; for when he has been taken up, rightly is it said, he himself is mine. In fine, when it had been said to Moses that he should divide the dwelling-places among the people of the Jews tribe by tribe, [1889C] and should distribute a portion to each several tribe, God excepted the Levitical tribe, saying to the sons of Levi: *He shall have no portion, nor lot in the midst of his brethren; because the Lord God is their part [n: (Deut. X, 9)]* [var: Vulg. Deut 10:9 *non habuit Levi partem neque possessionem cum fratribus suis, quia ipse Dominus possessio ejus est*]: and elsewhere: *I the Lord am their portion [n: (Num. XVIII, 20)]* [var: Vulg. Num 18:20 *ego pars et hereditas tua in medio filiorum Israel*, "I am thy part and inheritance in the midst of the children of Israel"].

## 65.

An earthly division is denied to these, that they themselves, while they claim no secular portion for themselves, may become the possession of the heavenly one; or that they may know how to possess this only, that is, the obedience of faith and of devotion, being far richer than those who spread out the ample expanses of their possessions. For however far they extend their boundaries, both the land fails and the sea shuts in their mad desires, and they pay tributes greater than their fruits are. But this man, possessing nothing, serves as a soldier to none but God; his portion is above the earth, [1889D] it does not fail with the earth, it is not shut in by the sea. He whose portion is God is possessor of the whole of nature. In place of fields, therefore, he is enough for himself, having a good fruit which can never perish. In place of houses it is enough for him to be the dwelling of the Lord and the temple of God, than which nothing can be more precious. For what is more precious than God? Or what is lacking to this man who can say: *Let it not befall me to glory save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world is crucified to me, and I to the world [n: (Galat. VI, 4)]* [var: Vulg. Gal 6:14 *mihi autem absit gloriari nisi in cruce*, "but God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross"] ?

## 66.

The prince of this world cannot claim [1890A] fellowship for himself in this man, in whom he finds nothing of his own. And therefore the Lord, who came to teach that God should become our portion, says: *For the prince of this world comes, and in me he finds nothing [n: (Joan. XIV, 30)]* [var: Vulg. John 14:30 *et in me non habet quidquam*, "and in me he hath not any thing"]. And deservedly, willing them to be imitators of himself, he says: *Do not possess gold, nor silver, nor money [n: (Matth. X, 9)]*. Whence Peter, showing that his portion was in God, not in the world, says: *Silver and gold I have not, but what I have,* *1572* *this I give thee: in the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth rise up and walk [n: (Act. III, 6)]* [var: Vulg. Acts 3:6 *argentum et aurum non est mihi*, "silver and gold I have none"]; that is, this is my portion, my portion is Christ: *In the name therefore of Christ Jesus rise up and walk (Ibid)*. That is, in my portion I am rich, in my portion I am powerful. Deservedly do I look for such fruits of that portion, that health and life may be given; because this is the patrimony of that portion which I have chosen for myself: *My beloved is mine, [1890B] and I am his.*

## 67.

*Who is pastured among the lilies.* The lily signifies chastity. The good shepherd therefore knows where he may pasture his flock, what fodder is fit for his sheep. What are our pastures, that is, the pastures of the faithful, save Christ? In whose pastures the Prophet rejoiced that he was set, saying: *In a place of pasture, there he has set me [n: (Psal. XXII, 2)]*; for there he pastures and refreshes us. Good pastures are the divine sacraments. There thou pluckest the new flower, which has given the good fragrance of the resurrection: thou pluckest the lily, in which is the splendor of eternity: thou pluckest the rose, that is, the blood of the Lord's body. Good pastures too are the books of the heavenly Scriptures, in which we are fed by daily reading, in which we are restored and refreshed; when we taste the things that are written, [1890C] or more often chew over what we have sipped with the edge of the lips. By these pastures the flock of the Lord is fattened. Good pastures of Christ too are the mountains of the valleys; for in these also Christ pastures like a roe or a young hart, that is, in the splendors of the saints.
