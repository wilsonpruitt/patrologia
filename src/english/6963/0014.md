## 68.

*[n: (Vers. 17.)] Until the day breathe, and the shadows decline.* Zacchaeus climbed up into a sycamore tree that he might see Christ: for us too Jesus spread out his hands, that he might overshadow the whole world. How are we not in the shadow, we who are protected by the veil of his cross? How are we not in the shadow, whom the crucified defends from the malice of the world and from the burning of the body? Or do we not know that the Word of God, coming into this world, did not come as the Word. Such as it was in the beginning, such as it was with God: *but he emptied [1890D] himself, taking the form of a servant [n: (Philip. II, 7)]*. He came in a light cloud, and since he was the power of the Most High, he overshadowed Mary; that he might transfigure the body of our lowliness, to be made conformable to the body of his glory. As he therefore changed his form when he was born of the Virgin, so to us also the words of God seem to be transfigured when they are read in the Gospel, when their appearance is seen in the Scriptures as through a mirror; because the whole truth cannot be grasped here, but when that which is perfect shall have come, they shine forth no longer through examination, not through appearance as transfigured, but in whole and expressed truth. [1891A]

## 69.

Let him therefore bend the soul with the yoke of the Word, and rule it with his reins, that by the will of God it may be led away from vices and take in the fragrance of eternal life; for this life is not the perfect life, but this life is in shadow. Therefore, that Christ Jesus might be born a man of the Virgin, the power of the Most High overshadowed her who was to be his mother; because he came down in shadow, beginning from shadow to work the salvation of man, and about to complete it in the brightness of the eternal sun. This life, then, is a shadow. Hasten to the sun, that he may defend thee from the cold of this shadow, and pour over thee the warmth of summer. And therefore he urges that prayer must be made that our flight be not in winter or on the sabbath, not signifying a season and a day, but forbidding us to be cold in merits and to be fasting from good works.

## CAPUT TERTIUM.



## 1.

[1891B] *[n: (Vers. 1.)] In my bed, in the night I sought him whom my soul loved: I sought him and I found him not. I will rise up therefore, and will seek in the city, in the forum, in the streets, him whom my soul loved.* [var: Vulg. Cant 3:1-2 *in lectulo meo per noctes quaesivi quem diligit anima mea… surgam, et circuibo civitatem: per vicos et plateas quaeram*, "in my bed by night I sought him whom my soul loveth… I will rise, and will go about the city: in the streets and the broad ways I will seek," with no forum] This soul had already received the good pledges of charity; but because we ought always to be anxious, always intent, and because the Word of God leaps forth like a little roe, or like a young hart, let the soul be always watchful, and hold out before her him whom she seeks and whom she longs to hold. Therefore, as though lamenting that he had slipped away from her, *In my bed,* she says, *by nights I sought him whom my soul loved.* Let him who seeks well seek in the bed, seek by night: let there be neither a day of rest nor nights of it; let no time be empty of the duty of piety; and even if he has not found at the first, let him persevere in seeking. ## 2.

*[n: (Vers. 2.)] I will rise up* therefore, *and will seek in the city, in the forum, in the streets.* And perhaps she has not yet found him for this reason, that she sought in the forum, where lawsuits are; in the streets, where the markets of things *1573* for sale are; for Christ is not purchased with money. Let holy devotion suffer no time of preaching to pass by empty. If those who study secular teachings indulge very little in sleep, how much more ought those who long to know God not to be hindered by the sleep of the body, save so far as is enough for nature? David washed his bed with tears through the several [sic: *nocctes*], and rose also in the middle of the night that he might confess to the Lord: and dost thou think that the whole night is to be assigned to slumber? Then the more must the Lord be prayed to by thee, then must protection be sought and fault be guarded against, when it seems to have secrecy. [1891D] Then most of all, when darkness is round about me and walls cover me, must it be considered that God beholds all hidden things. Say not therefore, wrapped about with darkness: Who sees me? And whom do I fear, hedged in and shut up by walls? Because *The face of the Lord is upon them that do evil [n: (Psalm. XXXIII, 17)]*. In fine, if thou seest no witness, dost thou not see thyself? Dost thou not fear the testimony of thine own conscience? Dost thou not know that that gloom of night is not a covering but an incitement of sin? When bodies grow hot with sleep and food, then too the vigor of the mind is loosened by slumber, is dissolved by sleep. Then [1892A] the lust of impure embrace creeps in, then the heart is troubled with the squalor of uncleanness, chastity is not seen, purity is not considered, the glory of modesty is not reckoned.

## 3.

It was night when Judas betrayed, when Peter denied. At that time therefore especially must the justifications of God be woven over again in the mind, and the exhorting commandments be read again: let those precepts concerning chastity not be absent, so that the mind, occupied with these, may quench the blaze of lust, the burning of the flesh. Hold that in mind: *I will wash my bed every night [n: (Psal. VI, 7)]*. For who, given over to debauchery, wrapped up in disgraces, washes his bed every night? He knows not how to weep who commits what must be wept for; and though he himself is a thing to be wept over, he has no tears for his own punishment. But he who chastises his body, and is an anxious governor of himself, and, groaning and [1892B] grieving over the offense of a former fall, seeks how he may wash it away with the weeping of penitence, this man washes his bed every night. Let us not therefore sleep whole nights through, but let us assign the greatest part of them to reading and to prayers. Hear the voice of the Church seeking Christ even by night: *In my bed,* she says, *by nights I sought him whom my soul loved.*

## 4.

Take it according to the letter, that she sought by night by praying, by beseeching, and also by lamenting with tears. She sought by night; because *He made darkness his hiding-place [n: (Psal. XVII, 12)]*, that we might learn to seek him more anxiously. The Church gathered out of the nations therefore sought him in the prophets, and therefore believed. In fine, the Evangelists and Paul set down testimonies of the Lord's coming out of the prophets, whence it is written [1892C]: *For night unto night showeth knowledge [n: (Psal. XVIII, 3)]*. She sought by night, in persecutions and adversities, in tribulations and hard sorrows. It is night for all for whom there is no perfect security; whence the Lord says: *The night will come when no man can work. While I am in this world, I am the light of this world [n: (Joan. IX, 4)]* [var: Vulg. John 9:4-5 *venit nox, quando nemo potest operari. Quamdiu in mundo sum, lux sum mundi*, "the night cometh, when no man can work. As long as I am in the world, I am the light of the world"]. Let us not therefore work in darkness; for if our works shine, we do not work in darkness, but in light.

## 5.

It is day for those to whom Christ is present, it is night for those to whom he denies himself. It is no great thing for thee if thou give thanks to the Lord then, when thou art in prosperity and in favorable things: but if thou cleave to Christ then, when a persecutor harries thee, when some storm troubles thee. Hast thou lost a son? In that grief, in that night, in that destitution, remember the Lord thy God; lest, as one not heard, [1892D] thou be ungrateful and transgress in thine affliction. Hast thou been driven into exile? Remember the Lord thy God, lest thou prefer the love of a fatherland forbidden thee before God. Crushed by the power of some rich man, hast thou lost thine own means, dost thou lack the wherewithal to live? Remember the Lord thy God, lest the night of want lead thee away from the affection of devotion. For this is the commandment of the Law, that thou seek the more by night, when thou art the more heard by the Lord, and mayest be able to say: *In tribulation I called upon the Lord, and the Lord heard me in a broad place [n: (Psal. CXVII, 5)]*.

## 6.

But neither does it suffice for grace to seek perfunctorily, [1893A] but that thou press on and bend thyself to the task. In fine, this Church or soul, who sought in the bed, sought by night, did not find at the first; because perhaps she sought in the bed. *1574* But after she rose and went away into the city (see whether it be into that city in which the Lord, about to celebrate the passover, sent his disciples, saying [n: (Matth. XXVI, 18)]: *Go into the city to a certain man* ), after she sought, as I have said, in the forum, where oil is sold, which those awaiting the Bridegroom are wont to buy; where right is, where the laws are. For if the law is spiritual, the forum too is assuredly spiritual, where those skilled in the eternal law dispute. Which forum is not tumultuous with lawsuits, but glorious with the tribunals of Christ. Where did she seek, I say? In the forum, and afterward she sought in the streets, out of which were gathered those who were to come together to the banquet of the householder of [1893B] the Gospel: who, being called, did not think that they should excuse themselves from such a feast. After she sought, I say, in the forum and in the streets, she fell in with those who go about the city, and then at last she was able to find what she was seeking; perhaps because, set in tribulation and fear, she the more finds grace.
