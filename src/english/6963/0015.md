## 7.

*[n: (Vers. 3.)] They found me who go about the city; they took away my cloak.* [var: Vulg. Cant. III, 3 *Invenerunt me vigiles qui custodiunt civitatem*; the taking of the cloak stands at Cant. V, 7] She had a struggle, then: but by what reasoning they did not take away her cloak here, I am at a loss to find out; unless perhaps because there she said that she had asked: *Have you seen him whom my soul has loved?* She who was speaking Christ, [sic: *nex*] was not stripped of her cloak, and she found him whom she was seeking. Learn in what manner Christ is sought: by those, surely, who do not seek him perfunctorily, but hold him fast [1893C] by force, as she does who says: *I held him and I will not let him go,* who found him with faith and bound him fast with meditation.

## 8.

If we take them for good guards, they are certainly angels. But he who passed beyond the angels. Found the Word; and therefore there was not much of an interval, when he passed beyond these and found Jesus. How then did they take away her cloak below, unless perhaps because, by the advance of an offense against faith, the Church, while she is stripped bare, is the more loved by the Lord, putting off from herself the old man that she may put on the new, whom sins do not cover but the hidden things of the mind make bright; or because, being about to come to Christ, she laid aside the mantle of worldly wisdom? And he was not stripped of his cloak, after he had begun to be more perfect.

## 9.

Let us therefore be mindful of the heavenly ordinances of justification; that while we sing them with the hidden voice of the mind, we may be mindful [1893D] in the night of the name of the Lord, and may say as it is written: *This was made mine, because I sought out thy justifications [n: (Psal. CXVIII, 56)]*; that is, this remembrance was made mine, that I might be mindful even in the night of thy name, not lulled asleep by drunkenness, not loosed into slumber by feasting, not taken up with worldly cares, lest forgetfulness of the reverence owed to thee should creep upon me: but chastening my members by daily meditation, and exercising the intention of the mind; so that by constancy this course may become a solemn one for us, so that we may reverence the Lord Jesus with our whole affection even in the nights.

## 10.

*In my chamber, in the nights I sought him whom [1894A] my soul has loved.* [var: Vulg. *In lectulo meo per noctes quaesivi quem diligit anima mea*] We can understand it in this way as well. He seeks Christ in his bed who seeks him with tranquillity, with peace. He seeks in the nights, since he was speaking in parables; for *He hath set* indeed *darkness for his covert, and night sheweth knowledge unto night [n: (Psal. XVII, 12)]*. Then, because the things which we say in our heart ought to prick us in our beds.

## 11.

But even so she did not find him, and therefore she says: *I will rise up,* that is, I will raise and lift up my intention, that I may seek untiringly, may seek anxiously: I will go into the city. There is also a soul which says: *I am a fortified city, I am a besieged city [n: (Esai. XXVII, 3)]*. [var: Vulg. Isa. XXVII, 3 *Ego Dominus qui servo eam*] There is a city fortified through Christ; there is that city Jerusalem in heaven, in which the interpreters of the divine law abound, and men skilled in the disciplines, through whom the Word of God is sought. [1894B] I will seek, she says, in the forum of that city, in that forum where those learned in the law handle rights, where the oil is sold which the Gospel virgins buy, that their torches may always shine, and that the smoke of iniquity may not put them out. I will seek, she says, in the streets in which the waters overflow, bursting out from those fountains of which Solomon says that one must drink.

## 12.

*They found me,* she says, *the guards who go about the city.* While therefore she seeks Christ, she finds the guards who are in ministry; from these she asks: *Have you seen him whom my soul loves?* But the soul which seeks God passes even the guards by. For there are mysteries which even the angels long to see, whence Peter says: *They were announced,* he says, *to you by those to whom they preached the gospel, the Holy* *1575* *Spirit having been sent from heaven, [1894C] upon whom the angels long to look [n: (I Pet. I, 12)]*. [var: Vulg. *per eos qui evangelizaverunt vobis* … *in quem desiderant angeli prospicere*]

## 13.

*[n: (Vers. 4.)] How little a while after I had passed beyond them, I found him, I held him, and I did not let him go.* [var: Vulg. Cant. III, 4 *Paululum cum pertransissem eos, inveni quem diligit anima mea*] Therefore he who has passed beyond the guards has found the Word. John passed beyond them, who found the Word with the Father. There are also many others who seek Christ in leisure and do not find him; and they seek him in persecutions, and they find him. And therefore, as if after temptations — because he is present in the perils of his faithful ones — *How little a while,* he says, *after I had passed beyond them, I found him, and I held him, and I did not let him go.* For everyone who seeks, finds; and he who has found ought to cleave to him, lest he be able to lose him.

## 14.

And since through the Gospel we see the heavenly mysteries figured upon earth, let us come to Magdalene, [1894D] let us come to the other Mary. Let us consider in what manner they sought Christ in the nights, in the chamber of his body in which he lay dead, when the angel said to them: *You seek Jesus who was crucified: he is not here, for he is risen: why then do you seek the living with the dead [n: (Matth. XXVIII, 5 et seq.)]*? [var: Vulg. Matth. XXVIII, 6 ends *surrexit enim, sicut dixit*; *Quid quaeritis viventem cum mortuis* stands at Luc. XXIV, 5] Why do you seek in the sepulchre him who is already in heaven? Why do you seek in the bonds of burial him who looses the bonds of all men? Not the sepulchre but heaven is his seat; therefore one of them said: *I sought him, and I did not find him [n: (Cant. III, 1)]*. Yet while they go to bring word to the apostles, Jesus, having pity on those who sought him, met them, saying: [1895A] *Hail.* But they came near, and held his feet, and adored him.

## 15.

Jesus therefore is held, and is delighted to be held so, because he is held by faith. In short, that woman also delighted him who touched him and was healed of the flow of blood, of whom he said: *Someone has touched me; for I feel that power has gone out from me [n: (Luc. VIII, 46)]*. [var: Vulg. *nam ego novi virtutem de me exiisse*, "for I know"] Touch him therefore, and hold him by faith, and clasp his feet faithfully; that power may go out from him to heal thy soul. And if he say: *Do not touch me;* do thou hold him. *For I have not yet ascended to my Father,* he said [n: (Joan. XX, 17)]. Once he said: *Do not touch me,* when he had risen: or perhaps he said it to her who thought that he had been carried off by theft and not raised up again by his own power. In short, in another book thou hast it that to those holding his feet and adoring him he said: *Do not [1895B] be afraid [n: (Matth. XXVIII, 10)]*.

## 16.

Hold him therefore, thou also, O soul, as Mary too was holding him, and say: *I held him, and I will not let him go:* to whom both of them were saying, we hold thee. Go to the Father, but do not forsake me, lest I slip again; and with Mary, who does not stray but holds the tree of life, snatch up with thee her who clings to thy feet. I will ascend with thee, do not let me go, lest again the serpent pour out his venom, lest again he be able to bite the woman's heel, that he may supplant Adam. Let thy soul therefore say: *I hold thee, and I will bring thee into the house of my mother, and into the secret place of her who conceived me [n: (Cant. III, 4)]*; that I may know thy mysteries, that I may drink in thy sacraments.

## 17.

*I held him, nor will I leave him, until I bring him into the house of my mother, and into the chamber of her [1895C] who conceived me.* [var: Vulg. Cant. III, 4 *in cubiculum genitricis meae*, "into the chamber of her that bore me"] What is the house of my mother and her chamber, but the inward and hidden part of thy nature? Guard this house, cleanse the inner rooms of this house, so that when the house has been unstained, and defiled by the filth of no adulterous conscience, a spiritual house may rise up, framed together upon the corner stone into a holy priesthood, and the Holy Spirit may dwell in it. She who seeks Christ thus, who thus entreats Christ, is not left by him: nay rather, she is even visited again and again.

## 18.

Or the house of the mother is perhaps the house in which the discipline of morals shines out: while that secret place is the one in which are the higher mysteries, in which the honey of divine grace gives out its fragrance. Or is not the remission of sins sweeter than all honey? Is not the resurrection of the dead [sic: *floregratior*] than every one? Receive then Eve, no [1895D] longer covered with the leaves of the fig tree, but clothed with the Holy Spirit, and glorious with new grace; because now she is not hidden away as one stripped bare, but comes to meet us as one surrounded with the splendour of a shining garment, because grace clothes her: yet neither was Adam naked at first, when innocence clothed him.
