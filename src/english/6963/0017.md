## 28.

There is a just mercy: but there is also an unjust [1898D] mercy. In short, it is written in the Law concerning a certain man: *Thou shalt not have mercy upon him [n: (Deut. XIX, 13)]*. And in the books of Kingdoms thou readest that Saul incurred offense for this reason, that he had mercy on Agag the king of the enemy, whom the divine sentence had forbidden to be kept alive [n: (I Reg. XV, 9)]. So that if anyone, moved by the pleading of a robber's children and bent by the tears of his wife, should think that he ought to be acquitted, while the desire of robbing still pants within him: will he not hand over the innocent to destruction, who sets free a man who is plotting the destruction of many? Assuredly, if he holds back the sword, looses the chains, [1899A] relaxes the exile; [sic: *car*] why does he not take away, by the gentler road that lies open to him, the power of robbing, he who could not wrest away the will? Then, between two men, that is, an accuser and a defendant contending at equal peril of life — the one if he did not prove his case, the other if he were not convicted by the accuser — let not the judge follow that *1578* which belongs to justice; but while he has mercy on the defendant, let him condemn the man who proves the charge: or while he favours the accuser who cannot prove it, let him cast off the guiltless. This therefore cannot be called a just mercy.

## 29.

In the Church itself, where it is most fitting to show mercy, the form of justice ought to be kept as strictly as possible, lest anyone who has been kept back from the fellowship of communion should, by a brief little tear made ready for the moment, or even by more abundant weeping, wring out through the easiness of the priest [1899B] the communion which he ought to ask for over a very long time. Does he not, when he indulges one unworthy man, cause very many to be drawn on to the contagion of falling? For easiness of pardon supplies an incentive to offending. This has been said for this reason, that we may know that mercy is to be dispensed to debtors according to the word of God, according to reason: but otherwise let the sword carry out what belongs to it.

## 30.

*[n: (Vers. 9, 10.)] King Solomon made himself a bed out of the woods of Lebanon; he made its pillars of silver, its headrest of gold, its ascent of purple, its back strewn with jewels.* [var: Vulg. Cant. III, 9–10 *Ferculum fecit sibi rex Salomon*, "a litter"; and Vulg. has nothing answering to *dorsum ejus gemmis stratum*] What is this bed, but the appearance of our body? For in jewels the air is displayed under the appearance of brightness, in gold fire, water in silver, earth by wood; of which four elements the human body consists, in which our soul reclines, [1899C] if it be not without rest upon the roughness of the mountains or upon the dry ground; but, raised on high above the vices, may rest propped upon the wood. Whence David also says: *May the Lord bring help to him upon the bed of his sorrow [n: (Psal. XL, 4)]*; for how can there be a bed of sorrow, when that which has no feeling cannot feel sorrow? Now a body of sorrow is like a body of death: *Unhappy man that I am! who will free me from the body of that death [n: (Rom. VII, 24)]*? [var: Vulg. Rom. VII, 24 *de corpore mortis hujus*, "of this death"]

## 31.

And since we have brought in a verse in which we have made mention of the Lord's body, lest perhaps it disturb any reader that the Lord took upon himself a body of sorrow, let him remember that he both sorrowed over the death of Lazarus and wept, and in the passion was wounded, and from the wound blood and water came out, and he breathed out his spirit [n: (Joan. XI, 35)]. Water for the washing, blood for [1899D] drink, spirit for the resurrection. For Christ is to us one hope, faith, charity: hope in the resurrection, faith in the washing, charity in the sacrament. Yet as he took upon himself a body of sorrow, so also he turned the couch in infirmity; because he turned it to the advantage of human flesh: for by the passion infirmity was loosed, death by the resurrection. And nevertheless you ought to mourn for the world and to rejoice in the Lord: sorrowful unto penitence, eager unto grace — although the Doctor of the Gentiles has laid it down by a saving precept that we must both weep with them that weep and rejoice with them that rejoice [n: (Rom. XII, 15)]. [1900A]

## 32.

*He strewed the midst with charity, for the daughters of Jerusalem.* Who are these daughters? It ought not to be hidden that those seem superior who are [sic: *generali*] beforehand, and weaker those who come afterwards. For the birth-throes of this world are compared to the womb of a woman; since those are stronger who were born in the strength of youth, weaker those born in the time of old age. For this world has failed in the multitude of its begetting, like the womb of one giving birth, and like an aging creature it lays down the strength of its youth, as though the vigour of its powers were now withering. These, then, are the daughters of Jerusalem, that is, the more tender and weaker souls: to these he shows himself, that his image may shine in their confession, may shine in their love, may shine in their works; and, if it can be done, that his whole form may be expressed in them.

## 33.

[1900B]These daughters of Jerusalem, therefore, seeing the bride ascending and not resisting, and delighted by the odours of her merits, recognizing also that she is the bride of that peaceable Solomon, follow after her with sedulous escort as far as the bed of Solomon: because true rest in Christ is due to her. For the bed of the saints is Christ, in which the hearts of all men, wearied by the battles of the world, find rest. In this bed Isaac rested, and blessed his younger son, saying: *The greater shall serve the lesser:* reclining in this bed Jacob blessed the twelve patriarchs: reclining in this bed the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue rose from death: lying in this bed the widow's only son, who had died, loosed the bonds of death when called by the voice of Christ. The bride, therefore, having been brought as far as the rest of the bridegroom, they sing the marriage [1900C] song, saying to the daughters of Jerusalem:

## 34.

*[n: (Vers. 11.)] Go forth and see upon king Solomon the crown with which his mother crowned him, in the day of his espousals.* [var: Vulg. Cant. III, 11 *in diademate quo coronavit illum mater sua in die desponsationis illius*] They sing the epithalamium, and call the other powers of the heavens, or *1579* the souls, to see the charity which Christ has toward the daughters of Jerusalem. Whence he also deserved to be crowned by his mother as the son of charity, as Paul shows, saying: *Because God has rescued us from the power of darkness, and has carried us over into the kingdom of the son of his charity [n: (Coloss. I, 13)]*. [var: Vulg. Col. I, 13 *in regnum filii dilectionis suae*, "of the son of his love"] And so the son of charity is himself also charity: not having charity from accidents, but having it by his own substance, like the kingdom of which he says: *For this was I born [n: (Joan. XVIII, 17)]*.

## 35.

And therefore he says, *Go forth,* that is, go out from the [1900D] anxieties and thoughts of the world: go out from bodily straits: go out from the vanities of the world, and see what charity the peaceable king has in the day of his espousals: how glorious he is, because he gave resurrection to bodies, and joined souls to himself. This is the crown of a great contest, this the illustrious gift of Christ's espousals, his blood and his passion. For who could give more, he who did not even spare himself, and laid down his soul unto death for us?

## 36.

But what is the crown of glory, but the Church which [1901A] crowns Christ her head? What is the delight of the whole earth, but the house of the Christian people, the court of the saints, of whom it is written: *Their sound has gone out into all the earth [n: (Psal. XVIII, 5)]*? Therefore, because the Church is the crown of glory, it is said: *Go forth, daughters of Sion, and see king Solomon, in the crown with which his mother crowned him, in the day of his espousals, and in the day of the gladness of his heart.*

## 37.

It is said to souls that they should go out from the hovels and prisons of the body, that they should go out beyond the thoughts of the body, that they should depart from covetous desires, and from cares, and from the other passions of this flesh and of slippery affection: that they should climb above the world, go out from this world, meet Christ, be present and ready, shining with burning torches. As though the angels of Christ were speaking, they say these things: You [1901B] cannot see his brightness, his glory, unless you go out from the cares of human frailty, O daughters of Jerusalem. As if they said: *Why do you seek the living among the dead [n: (Luc. XXIV, 5)]*? [var: Vulg. Luc. XXIV, 5 *Quid quaeritis viventem cum mortuis?*] Christ is not sought within this world, who willed his disciples to be above the world.

## 38.

What is the crown with which Christ is crowned, but the crown of glory? Joseph had the crown of chastity, Paul of justice, Peter of faith. There are crowns of the several virtues. Christ alone has the crown of glory, with which the Church crowned him. In this crown are all the crowns; because glory is not the portion of one crown, but the reward of all the crowns.

## CHAPTER FOUR.



## 1.

[1901C] [n: (Vers. 1.)] But now the Bridegroom, turned to the praise of the bride, says: *How beautiful thou art, my love, how beautiful thou art! thine eyes are as doves, apart from silence.* [var: Vulg. Cant. IV, 1 *Oculi tui columbarum, absque eo quod intrinsecus latet*] Beautiful in preaching, beautiful in her manner of life. *Her eyes also,* he says, *are as doves.* They are the eyes of a man, plainly adorned with spiritual senses, which are keen for seeing the mysteries, and ready for penetrating the secret things of divine Scripture, shining with reasonable milk, in which there is no spotted confusion of guile, but the pure and unstained simplicity of a single-hearted affection. Therefore he afterwards mentioned these doves as washed in milk, in an abundance of waters.
