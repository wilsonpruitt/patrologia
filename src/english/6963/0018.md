## 2.

Now as for the rest — the teeth and the cheeks, and the lips of the bride like a scarlet cord — we understand them to be the virtues of the soul and the diversities of teachers: who either supply spiritual [1901D] nourishment to the mind by sedulous dispensation, or, by the preaching of the Lord's cross, bind the hearer as with a certain cord of the Word: or who are modest in their bashfulness and most pleasing in the flower of youth. And though they be called back from the touch by shame, yet let the odour of Christ be fragrant in them: and just as the ointment runs down upon the cheeks, as from the priestly head, so also let the beauty of doctrine shine out in them.

## 3.

*Thine eyes are as doves apart from thy silence;* which both sees spiritually, and knows that there is a time for keeping silence and a time for speaking; so that it may bring forth its word [1902A] in season, lest by unseasonableness of speaking it be able to incur sin.

## 4.

*Thine eyes are as doves.* Here the simplicity and grace of the dove, that is, of the Church, is revealed. See then the simplicity of the eye: for he who sees a just man and rejoices, himself also wishes to be just: for it is a beautiful thing that a man should be delighted in others by that *1580* which he wishes to keep in himself, if he can: for it is inborn in good men that the modest man should love the chaste, the wise man the prudent, with holy affection, the merciful man the generous, and that they should love their own virtues in others. For very many, indeed, the sight of a just man is an admonition to correction, but for the more perfect it is a joy. For if there is such power in natural things that a living creature, when seen, profits the jaundiced — so that even the body of that creature when dead is said to profit, if it be shown to those who have fallen into [1902B] this kind of ailment — can we doubt that the sight of a just man heals, whose eyes are as doves, simple and modest? Therefore a cheap and irrational animal has such power that it can heal a man in the tiny moment in which it is seen: will a just man, if only he be looked upon with faith by one who desires to receive benefit from him, confer nothing? How beautiful a thing it is, then, that thou shouldst be seen and do good!

## 5.

A just man, then, is a good thing. This, in short, is why the apostle Paul went up to Jerusalem, that he might see just men: and he stayed with Peter fifteen days, that he might gain something from living with him [n: (Gal. I, 18)]. For this reason Paul himself and Barnabas, when they were entering Jerusalem, were magnificently received by [1902C] the Church, and by the apostles, and by the elders: but when they wished to depart, they were begged not to go away, and, as we read afterwards concerning Paul, he was escorted on his way with tears. Do not the very rays of the eyes seem to pour a certain power into those who faithfully desire to see him?

## 6.

But just as a just man gladdens the heart of the innocent when he is seen, so the wicked are tormented by the knowledge of the just, because they are reproved even by the silent conduct of the saints. Chastity torments the incontinent, generosity avarice, faith impiety. Of this too let us take a like example from a cheap living creature. For as we have said that a dumb animal profits when it is seen, so we perceive that the wolf does harm, if he has got ahead of anyone in the seeing; for those whom the wolf [1902D] has seen first are reported to lose their voice. The basilisk also (this is a harmful serpent), if it should see any animal first, is reported to kill it: and it is alleged that a man is slain at once who has been able to be seen beforehand by a serpent of this kind. The serpent itself also is said to die, if it has been forestalled by the sight of a man. Therefore, if there is such power either in the eyes of a serpent or in the eyes of a man, that if the one sees the other first he can kill him: is there no power in the eyes of a just man, who is filled with the grace of virtue — especially since faith works so much that even she who touched the Lord's fringe was healed, and he upon whom the Lord Jesus looked drew at once [1903A] from his eyes the grace of health [n: (Matth. IX, 22)]?

## 7.

But he who sees a just man ought to know what he sees. He does not see him in the body, not in his clothing, not in his patrimony, not in his face: but he sees within. He does not, I say, see him, unless he has seen his mind, unless he has attended to his speech, unless he has been able to grasp his understanding, has been able to take up wisdom from his discourse. Then therefore will he rejoice, when he has looked upon these things, when he has come to know them. So then let us also, whenever we hear of a just man, hasten to see him, like that woman who heard that the Lord Jesus was reclining at table in the house of the Pharisee, and went in, and poured ointment over his feet [n: (Luc. VII, 37 et 38)]. Let us be imitators of her, for who would doubt that in that woman there is a figure of the Church? [1903B] Wherever, then, a just man may sit, wherever he may recline at table, let us hasten to see him. It is a precious thing to see a just man, that thou mayest see him according to the image of God. What is outside profits nothing: what is within heals. Certainly in him who is without we often behold him who is within: so that if we see a poor man, let us honour in the poor man him to whose likeness he was made, of whom he says: *You gave me to eat, because what you gave to one of these, you gave to me [n: (Matth. XXV, 35 et 40)]*.

## 8.

*Thy hair is as a flock of shorn ones, which have been revealed from mount Galaad.* [var: Vulg. Cant. IV, 1 *Capilli tui sicut greges caprarum quae ascenderunt de monte Galaad*] Therefore hair, because the power of all the senses is in the head: for *The eyes of the wise man are in his head [n: (Eccl. II)]*. Deep, then, is the prudence of teachers, which can reveal the things that are obscure, [1903C] and open up the heights of the senses.

## 9.

*[n: (Vers. 2.)] Thy teeth are as a flock of shorn ones, which have come up from the washing, all of which bring forth twins, and there is not a barren one among them.* [var: Vulg. Cant. IV, 2 *omnes gemellis fetibus, et sterilis non est inter eas*] What is said in the literal sense of she-goats is to be understood mystically of the flock of the Church. And let these animals not seem cheap to thee; thou seest that this flock feeds on the heights, bold upon the mountain. And so where for others there are precipices, there for the she-goats is no danger: where for others is danger, there is the food of that flock, there the sweeter *1581* fare, there the choicer fruit. They are watched by their shepherds as they hang from the bushy rock, where the inroads of wolves cannot reach; there fruitful trees supply whole fruit. One may see them swollen with abundant milk, anxious with a mother's love over their tender offspring. [1903D] Therefore the Holy Spirit chose them, to which he might compare the coming together of the venerable Church.

## 10.

And that thou mayest hear it mystically: the hair of the Word is the height, and a certain eminence, of just [sic: *animasum*]; since the understanding of the wise man is in his head. For it is certain that wisdom lies in the height of human thought. And just as she-goats are shorn, that they may lay aside what is superfluous: so also holy Church has a flock of shorn souls, that is, the virtues of many souls, in which flock thou couldst find nothing without feeling, nothing superfluous; since faith has made them wise, [1904A] and spiritual grace has cleansed them from every stain of superfluous things.

## 11.

Rightly, therefore, have the souls of the just been revealed, and revealed from mount Galaad, that is, from the passing over of testimony, in that the heavenly testimony passed over from the Synagogue to the Church. And so on this mountain grow the incense-gum, the resin, and the other perfumes which those Ishmaelite traders were carrying, as thou hast it in the first book of the Old Testament [n: (Gen. XXXVII, 25)]. And so, just as she-goats refreshed with good food and blooming in the warmth of the sun are washed in the river, and rise up out of the river exulting and clean: so the souls of the just come up from the spiritual washing.

## 12.

These are they who truly bring forth twins, in whom there is no barrenness of virtues, no sterility [1904B] of merits. Well do they bring forth twins, because they double their understandings; whence thou hast it written in Proverbs: *And do thou write these things for thyself in threefold fashion, unto counsel and unto knowledge [n: (Prov. XXII, 20)]*. [var: Vulg. Prov. XXII, 20 *Ecce descripsi eam tibi tripliciter, in cogitationibus et scientia*] He set a threefold writing before, and added two things, counsel and knowledge. But knowledge is twofold: one of incorporeal things, the other of corporeal; whence also in what follows thou hast: *Thy two breasts are as two twin fawns of a roe, which feed among the lilies, until the day break and the shadows retire [n: (Vers. 5)]*.

## 13.

We have spoken of fruitfulness; let us speak of the teeth. For very many who sail, and those hastening on a journey by land, when they have seen some beautiful place, linger there for the sake of the delight, they feed their eyes and lift up [1904C] their mind. Nor is any of this reckoned a delay in their travelling, but a favour. So it is our heart's desire also to consider the most beautiful teeth of just souls; for Scripture has taught that the teeth of the just are most beautiful, speaking indeed according to the letter of the patriarch Judah, but spiritually of Christ: *His eyes are merry from wine, and his teeth are as milk [n: (Genes. XLIX, 12)]*. [var: Vulg. Gen. XLIX, 12 *Pulchriores sunt oculi ejus vino, et dentes ejus lacte candidiores*] In which it proclaimed not the services of human flesh, but the gifts of divine grace. The example teaches us, therefore, that the teeth are not to be passed over, where we have discoursed of the eyes.
