## 14.

What then are the teeth of just souls, but those which, receiving food shapeless and hard, or often cold, or hot beyond measure, now break it small, now warm it, now temper it, according as the quality of the nourishment has been? They break the hard things small, [1904D] lest the harshness of the letter in the Old Testament and the rigidity of a worldly understanding, if it be not dissolved by the spiritual tooth, should choke the vital parts themselves — the passages of the books of salvation being shut off — and choke a certain gullet of the soul, by a careless voracity. It is fitting, therefore, that thou first divide it, if the food which is taken seems solid to thee, and that thou distinguish it; and that, having softened it, thou pour it without any harm to the soul into all its members by a natural division; so that its whole body may feast upon the vital juice, and that thou take nothing corpse-like, nothing dead, into thy mouth, lest it be said: *Their throat is an open sepulchre [n: (Psalm. XIII, 3)]*. But mayest thou drink in the living word, [1905A] that it may be able to work in the inward parts of thy mind. These teeth are whiter than milk; because they are the teeth of the just.

## 15.

In short, although all our fathers were baptized in Moses in the cloud and in the sea, it is nevertheless not written idly: *That all ate the same spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink [n: (I Cor. X, 3 et 4)]*; so that a certain greater brightness might come to these teeth of the saints, whom, after the passage of the Red Sea, we shall recognize to have been cleansed by the bitterness of the fountain of myrrh tempered through the grace of the wood *(Exodi XV, 25)*; then by the drink of the twelve fountains, and last by the watering of the rock that poured out a spiritual wave; *For the rock was Christ [n: (I Cor. X, 3 et 4)]*. *1582* Therefore they also ate manna [n: (Num. XX, 11)], that so often washed they might eat the bread, as it is written, [1905B] of angels [n: (Psalm. LXXVII, 25)]. Now too in the mysteries of the Gospel thou recognizest that the baptized, though washed in the whole body, are nevertheless afterwards cleansed by spiritual food and drink; for that is the true brightness of the teeth, where the tuneful confession of a mind well aware of itself resounds. That eye is clean which no beam of grave sins has weighed down, which no mote of light defilements has troubled.

## 16.

*Thy teeth are as a flock of shorn ones, which have come up from the washing, all of which bring forth twins, and there is not a barren one among them.* This is no ordinary praise: first, by the fitting comparison with shorn ones; for we know that she-goats both feed on the heights without danger, and take their food in safety upon sheer crags, and then, when they are shorn, are unburdened of what is superfluous. To their flock the Church is compared, having in herself many virtues of souls, which through the washing lay aside [1905C] their superfluous sins, which bring to Christ mystical faith and moral grace, which speak of the cross of the Lord Jesus. In these the Church is well-formed, whence God the Word says to her: *Behold, thou art well-formed, my neighbor: behold, thou art well-formed; thine eyes are as doves. Thou art wholly well-formed, my neighbor, and there is no reproof in thee;* [var: Vulg. Cant. IV, 1 and 7 *amica mea* … *macula non est in te*] because guilt has been drowned. *Come hither from Lebanon, O bride, come hither from Lebanon, and thou shalt pass through from the beginning of faith;* [var: Vulg. Cant. IV, 8 *veni de Libano … coronaberis de capite Amana*] in that, renouncing the world, she has passed beyond the age, and has passed over to Christ.

## 17.

*[n: (Vers. 3.)] Thy lips are as a scarlet cord.* For in scarlet the appearance of fire, and the blood of the Lord's cross, glows red. The lips of the Lord are scarlet, which spoke his own passion. In short, in Exodus scarlet is set in the place of fire [n: (Exod. XXV, 4)]. For the world does not consist [1905D] of scarlet, but of four elements. But in scarlet the figure of fire is expressed, and unless its heat penetrated heaven and the air, the seas and the lands, all things would be dissolved as though their powers were spent. By the cord, therefore, we recognize the bond of persuasion; by the scarlet, either the ardour of desire which sparkles in the minds of the hearers, or the token of the passion.

## 18.

*[n: (Vers. 4.)] Thy neck is as the tower of David, which is built in Thalphioth; a thousand shields hang upon it, all the darts of the mighty.* For the neck is upright toward God, and fit for the yoke of Christ, and is bent by no bending down to the earth [1906A] of enticements; as the royal tower of Christ is, upon which Nebuchadnezzar cannot lay his yoke. For David, that man mighty of hand, founded that tower, and built it above the heights of the walls; that it might be both for defence and for adornment: for defence, because it foresees the enemy and drives him off: for adornment, because it stands out not only among lowly things, but even among lofty ones. Yet it is so for defence or for adornment only if it has in itself the teachings of the word as certain necklaces of trappings: if it has also the darts of the mighty prophets, which are aimed by certain arms of faith against every height that exalts itself.

## 19.

Do not, therefore, bring down into the dust of death thy soul, to which the Lord has granted also a natural height and vigour, by which it may rouse itself and rise up. [1906B] Whence also what is said of this conjunction of soul and body is opportunely taken, in that in the natural mystery of our life the soul, bound to the body, creeps as it were upon the ground and clings to the pavement, partly on account of this earthly dwelling, partly because this body is of earth. Both the lodging of the region, therefore, and the very matter of our body agree with this judgment. And therefore the Apostle beautifully desires to be freed from the body of this death; because we are shut in a certain prison, and, set in a certain hovel of wantonness, we are wrapped in the darkness of our offenses.

## 20.

Let us therefore walk according to the will of God, that we may be said to cleave to God. For he who lives according to the appetite of the body is flesh: he who lives according to the precepts of God is spirit. Let not our soul then become flesh, that is, so that we should be called flesh; like those also who perished in [1906C] the flood, of whom it was said: *That they are flesh [n: (Genes. VI, 3)]*. But rather let our flesh, obeying the governance of the soul, become soul, and deserve to be called by this name; as the household of the patriarch Jacob was called, and the holy posterity of his generation; for so it is written: *These are the sons of Bala, whom Laban gave to Rachel his daughter: she bore these to Jacob, seven souls.* And in the same place: *And all the souls which entered with Jacob into [sic: Aeyyptum], which came out of his thighs, besides the wives of his sons, sixty-six [n: (Genes. XLVI, 25 et 26)]*. And at the beginning of Exodus we read: *And all the souls from Jacob were seventy-five [n: (Exod. I, 5)]*. [var: Vulg. Exod. I, 5 *septuaginta*, seventy] *1583*

## 21.

Therefore those who dwelt with Joseph and came out of Egypt are souls: but they who, [1906D] though they were angels of God — that is, imitating by the uprightness of their life the grace of the angels ( *For they who [n: (Matth. XXII, 30)] do not take wives, and they who do not marry, shall be as the angels of God in heaven* ) [var: Vulg. Matth. XXII, 30 *neque nubent neque nubentur, sed erunt sicut angeli Dei in caelo*] — they, then, who, though they seemed to be angels, were captivated by womanly beauty, these are flesh, as the Lord God said: *My spirit shall not remain in men for ever, because they are flesh [n: (Genes. VI, 3)]*. [var: Vulg. Gen. VI, 3 *in homine … quia caro est*, in the singular] And rightly are those who have no part in lust compared to angels, for they are not flesh, because they are not in the flesh, but in the spirit: such were those who followed the Apostle as their teacher, to whom he says: *But you [1907A] are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit [n: (Rom. VIII, 9)]*. But those who are captivated by the bodily lust of women are flesh: and would that they were only flesh, and not neighing horses as well! *For,* he says, *they neighed after the wife of his neighbor [n: (Jerem. V, 8)]*.

## 22.

*[n: (Vers. 7.)] Thou art wholly well-formed, my neighbor, and there is no reproof in thee.* [var: Vulg. Cant. IV, 7 *Tota pulchra es, amica mea, et macula non est in te*] But who can reckon any beauty greater than the comeliness of her who is loved by the king, approved by the judge, dedicated to the Lord, consecrated to God. Always a bride, always unwed; so that neither may love have an end, nor modesty a loss. This assuredly is the true beauty, to which nothing is lacking, which alone deserves to hear from the Lord: *Thou art wholly well-formed, my neighbor, and there is no reproof in thee.*

## 23.

*[n: (Vers. 8.)] Come hither from Lebanon, come hither from Lebanon: [1907B] thou shalt pass over and pass through from the beginning of faith, from the head of Sanir and Hermon, from the lairs of lions, from the mountains of leopards.* By these tokens it is shown that the perfect and irreproachable beauty of the virgin soul, consecrated at the divine altars, not bent by mortal things amid the encounters and the lairs of spiritual beasts, but intent upon the mysteries of God, has deserved her beloved, whose breasts are full of gladness; for wine gladdens the heart of man.

## 24.

*And the odour,* he says, *of thy garments is as the odour of Lebanon. Come from Lebanon, O bride, come from Lebanon: thou shalt pass over and pass through from the beginning of faith:* thou shalt pass over into the world to do battle, thou shalt pass through to Christ to triumph over the world. Thou hast heard that he has separated thee from the assaults of lions and leopards, that is, of spiritual wickednesses: [1907C] thou hast heard that the beauty of thy virtues pleases him.
