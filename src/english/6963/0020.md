## 25.

*Come hither from Lebanon, bride, come hither from Lebanon: thou shalt pass over, and thou shalt pass through, from the beginning of faith, from the head of Sanir, and Hermoniim, from the caves of lions, from the mountains of leopards;* [var: Vulg. *Veni de Libano… coronaberis de capite Amana, de vertice Sanir et Hermon, de cubilibus leonum*, "come from Lebanon… thou shalt be crowned from the head of Amana"] that is, go forth out of the body, and [sic: *exne*] thyself wholly. For thou canst not be present to me unless thou first be a pilgrim away from the body; since they who are in the flesh are pilgrims away from the kingdom of God. *Come hither,* he says, *come hither.* Well did he repeat it, because whether present or absent thou oughtest to be present to and to please the Lord thy God: be present when present, be present when absent, even if thou art still in the body; for all are present to me whose faith is with me. He is present to me who has gone out from the world: he is present to me who thinks of me, who gazes on me, who hopes from me, to whom I am the portion: he is present to me who [1907D] has been absent from himself: he is with me who is not within himself; since he who is in the flesh is not in the spirit: he is with me who goes forth out of his own self: he is beside me who has been outside himself: he is whole to me who for my sake has lost his own soul; and therefore, *Come hither, come hither, bride; thou shalt pass over and pass through from the beginning of faith.* She passes over and passes beyond the rest who has come through to Christ: she passes over by the merit of faith and by the brightness of works, she who shines like Sanir and Hermon, that is, the way of the lamp: she passes over when the temptations of the world are conquered and the spiritual [1908A] wickednesses overcome, seeking the crown of a lawful contest.

## 26.

*[n: (Vers. 9.)] Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, bride, thou hast wounded my heart with one of thine eyes, and with one hair of thy neck.* For these eyes are the eyes of the mind, namely of the inner man, not these eyes which discharge the service of looking. For there is an eye both of the mind and of the flesh, but that eye is blind which does not see the things that are divine, which is puffed up in vain by the mind of the flesh. There is also another eye, the sense of Christ, by which the Church sees Christ, as he himself says to the Bride: *Thou hast taken us in heart by one of thine eyes.* [var: Vulg. *Vulnerasti cor meum in uno oculorum tuorum*, "thou hast wounded my heart with one of thine eyes"] Rightly is Christ seen with one eye, because he is not seen with the eye of the flesh, or because the Church, having two eyes, the moral and the mystical, sees Christ more with the eye of faith; [1908B] for the mystical eye is the keener, the moral the sweeter.

## 27.

And perhaps these are the eyes with which Paul saw eternal things, when he began not to see temporal things. In short, he who did not see Christ before he lost his eyes, saw *1584* him after he had lost the sight of his eyes; for he saw who said: *Who art thou, Lord [n: (Act. IX, 5)]*? Certainly he saw Christ, whom he also confessed as Lord. And below, *Lord,* he says, *what wilt thou have me to do?* Did he not then see him whose command he was awaiting? With what eyes therefore did Paul begin to see more, save with those which he himself showed us, saying: *I will pray with the spirit, I will pray also with the mind [n: (I Cor. XIV, 15)]*? In short, that thou mayest know that he saw while praying: *It came to pass,* he says, *when I was returning to Jerusalem, while I was praying in the [1908C] temple, I had a dread, I saw him saying to me: Make haste, go out more quickly from Jerusalem; for they will not receive thy testimony concerning me [n: (Act. XXII, 17 et 18)]*. [var: Vulg. *fieri me in stupore… exi velociter ex Jerusalem: quoniam non recipient*, "that I was in a trance… go out speedily from Jerusalem"]

## 28.

These eyes therefore fail for the word of God, and say: *When wilt thou comfort me?* With these eyes the prophets were called seers, because through revelation they discerned with the mind the things that were hidden. But because the eye of the mind and the eye of the flesh are one eye, and man is then upheld by consolation when the flesh and the mind do not desire diverse things, but desire one thing and seek one thing; therefore they are intent upon him who says: *I and the Father are one [n: (Joan. X, 30)]*. These eyes too confess that they are one; because they discharge one and the same desire and office.

## 29.

*[n: (Vers. 10.)] Thy breasts are more beautiful than wine, [1908D] and the odor of thine ointments above all spices.* And below: *The odor of thy garments is as of Lebanon.* [var: Vulg. *sicut odor thuris*, "as the odor of frankincense"] See what advance thou grantest us, virgin! For thy first odor is above all the spices which were sent to the Saviour's burial, and it breathes out that the motions of the body are dead and that the delights of the members have perished. Thy second odor, as the odor of Lebanon, breathes forth the integrity of the Lord's body, the flower of virginal chastity. Let thy works therefore compose a honeycomb of honey. The odor of the garments also is the odor of our acts. [1909A] Bring thy hands therefore to thy nostrils, and with unwearied and ever-watchful eagerness of mind search out the odor of thine acts. The odor of thy right hand will soothe thee, and thy members will breathe out the fragrance of the resurrection, thy fingers will sweat myrrh, that is, thy spiritual deeds will breathe out the grace of true faith. Thou takest therefore, virgin, delight from thine inner body, and thou art sweet to thyself, thou art pleasant to thyself, and thou dost not begin to be displeasing to thyself (which often happens to those who sin).

## 30.

*[n: (Vers. 11.)] A dropping honeycomb, thy lips, bride.* Let thy works therefore compose a honeycomb of honey; for virginity is worthy to be compared to bees, so laborious, so chaste, so continent: the bee feeds on dew, knows no coupling, composes honey. Dew too, for the virgin, is the divine word; because the words of God come down like dew. [1909B] The virgin's modesty is an unviolated nature, the virgin's offspring is the fruit of the lips, without bitterness, fertile in sweetness: the labor is for the common good, the fruit is common. How I would wish thee, daughter, to be an imitator of this little bee, whose food is the flower, whose offspring is gathered with the mouth, composed with the mouth. Do thou imitate her, daughter; let thy words hold out no veil of guile, let them have no wrapping of fraud; that they may both have purity and be full of gravity. Let an eternal posterity of thy merits also be brought forth for thee by thy mouth, and gather not for thyself alone, but for many also for what dost thou know when thy soul shall be required of thee; lest, leaving behind the receptacles of thy granaries heaped up with grain, which will serve neither the use of thy life nor thy merits, thou be snatched away thither, whither thou canst not carry thy treasure. Be rich therefore, [1909C] but for the poor man, that they who share thy nature may share thy means also.

## 31.

*A dropping honeycomb, thy lips, bride.* What the honeycomb is, teach us, Solomon. For thou hast said: *Good words are a honeycomb of honey [n: (Prov. XVI, 24)]*. [var: Vulg. *Favus mellis composita verba*, "well-ordered words are a honeycomb of honey"] And truly a good honeycomb, which the Church eats, redolent of honey gathered by the spiritual richness of many prophets as of bees. This is the honey of which he says: *I have eaten my bread with my honey [n: (Cant. V, 1)]*. [var: Vulg. *comedi favum cum melle meo*, "I have eaten the honeycomb with my honey"] The lips of the preacher drop honey, when the limbs of a fallen soul, bruised by hard mischances or downfalls, are revived. *Thy lips drop a honeycomb, bride, honey and milk are under thy tongue;* [var: Vulg. *Favus distillans labia tua*, "thy lips are a dropping honeycomb"] for the mouth of the just distils wisdom: from the mouth of the just proceed the honeys of sweetness and of mercy: in the mouth of the just there is no guile, no deceit, no bitterness [1909D] of sin. The Church hears the words of the just, the people of God hears the words of the wise man, is delighted by the sweetness of the address, is soothed by the pleasantness of moral discourse, saying: *How sweet are thy words to my throat, above honey and the honeycomb to my mouth [n: (Psalm. CXVIII, 103)]*! [var: Vulg. *Quam dulcia faucibus meis eloquia tua, super mel ori meo*, "how sweet are thy words to my throat, above honey to my mouth" — without *et favum*] For the honey of bees delights for an hour, but its savor quickly *1585* vanishes, and for the most part corrupt bowels are hurt: but the honeys of moral words, though they sting, do not hurt.

## 32.

Know however to whom those things are entrusted; for it is written: *Say nothing into the ear of a fool; lest at any time [1910A] he mock thy prudent words [n: (Prov. XXIII, 9)]*. [var: Vulg. *In auribus insipientium ne loquaris, quia despicient doctrinam eloquii tui*, "speak not in the ears of fools, for they will despise the instruction of thy speech"] For the fool will vomit out and cast back thy word, who cannot perceive its sweetness. How can the words of God be sweet in thy throat, in which is the bitterness of wickedness? How is there *honey and milk under thy tongue,* when thy tongue frames guile; so that thou conceivest one thing in thy breast, and puttest forward another in public speech, that thou mayest deceive the unwary? Since Peter the apostle says to thee that thou shouldest lay aside all malice, and cast off the paint of dissimulation, saying: *As infants born a while ago, reasonable and without guile, desire ye milk [n: (I Pet. II, 2)]*. [var: Vulg. *sicut modo geniti infantes, rationabile, sine dolo lac concupiscite*, "as newborn infants desire the reasonable milk without guile"] For showing us what it is to have honey and milk under the tongue, he teaches that we should know not to curse those who curse, but to render blessing: let us not know how to hate save the way of iniquity, that with a resisting [1910B] affection we may beware of doing what we execrate.
