## 5.

Hence therefore those Platonic feasters, hence that nectar out of the prophetic wine and honey, hence that sleep was transferred, hence that perpetual life which he said his gods feast upon, because Christ is life. And therefore with the seeds of such words the belly of his soul was filled, and she herself went out at the Word: and the soul which goes out from that servitude, and lifts herself up from the body, follows the Word. Otherwise, that we may go back somewhat to what is above [n: (Sup., cap. 4)]: *A garden enclosed, my sister, bride, a garden enclosed, a fountain sealed.* By which he signifies that the mystery ought to remain sealed with thee, lest it be violated by the works of an evil life, and by the adultery of chastity: lest it be divulged to those to whom it is not fitting, lest it be scattered among the faithless by garrulous talkativeness.

## 6.

[1918D] Good therefore ought the guard of thy faith to be, that the undefiled integrity of life and of silence may persevere. Whence the Church also, keeping the loftiness of the heavenly mysteries, casts from herself the heavier storms of the wind, and invites the sweetness of springlike grace. And knowing that her garden cannot displease Christ, she calls the bridegroom himself, saying: *Arise, north wind, and come, south wind: blow through my garden, and let my ointments flow [n: (Cant. IV, 10)]*: *1591* *let my brother come down into his garden, and eat the fruit of his fruit-trees [n: (Cant. V, 1)]*; for she has good and fruitful trees, and her roots [1919A] which she had dipped in the watering of the sacred fountain, and they have sprouted into good fruits with the bud of a new fecundity; so that they may no longer be cut down by the prophetic axe, but be made fruitful with evangelical richness. In short the Lord, delighted by their fertility, answers:

## 7.

*I have entered into my garden, my sister, bride: I have gathered myrrh with my ointments, I have eaten my bread with my honey: I have drunk my drink with my milk.* Why I have said bread and wine, the faithful understand. But this is not doubtful, that he himself eats and drinks in us, as thou hast read in our case, that he said he was in prison [n: (Matth. XXV, 36)]. Whence the Church also, seeing so great a grace, exhorts her sons, exhorts her neighbors, to run together to the sacraments, saying: *Eat, my neighbors, and drink, and be drunken, my [1919B] brethren.* What we should eat, what we should drink, the Holy Spirit has expressed to thee elsewhere through the prophet, saying: *Taste, and see that the Lord is sweet; blessed is the man that hopeth in him [n: (Psalm. XXXIII, 9)]*. In that sacrament is Christ, because it is the body of Christ: it is therefore not bodily food, but spiritual. Whence the Apostle also says concerning its type: *That our fathers ate spiritual food, and drank spiritual drink [n: (I Cor. X, 3)]*. For the body of God is a spiritual body, the body of Christ is the body of the divine Spirit; because Christ is spirit, as we read: *The Spirit before our face, Christ the Lord [n: (Thren. IV, 20)]*. [var: Vulg. *Spiritus oris nostri, christus Dominus*, "the breath of our mouth, Christ the Lord"] And in Peter's epistle we have: *Christ died for us [n: (I Pet. II, 21)]*. [var: Vulg. *Christus passus est pro nobis*, "Christ suffered for us"] In short this food strengthens our heart, and this drink gladdens the heart of man, as the prophet recorded [1919C] [n: (Psal. CIII, 25)].

## 8.

*A garden enclosed, my sister, bride, a garden enclosed, a fountain sealed;* let her not easily open her mouth, nor unseal it with common speech; for it is not fitting that thou answer even concerning divine things themselves, unless questioned by the word of God. What hast thou to do with the rest? Speak to Christ alone, converse with Christ alone. For if it is written that women should keep silence in the Church [n: (I Cor. XIV, 34)]: how much more is it unfitting that a virgin's door lie open, unfitting that a widow's doors lie open! Quickly the plotter against modesty creeps in, quickly he catches up a word which thou wouldst wish to call back. If Eve's door had been closed, neither would Adam have been deceived, nor would she, being questioned, have answered the serpent. Death entered through the window, that is, through Eve's door. Death enters through thy door, if thou speak falsehood, if basely, if [1919D] shamelessly, lastly if thou speak where it is not fitting. Let the doors of thy lips therefore be closed, and let the vestibule of thy voice remain barred, then perhaps to be unbarred, when thou shalt have heard the voice of God, when thou shalt have heard the word of God. Then myrrh shall sweat for thee, then the grace of baptism shall breathe upon thee, that thou mayest die together with Christ from the elements of the world, and rise again with Christ. *Why yet,* he says, *as though living, do ye decree concerning this world? Touch not, handle not, taste not, the things which are unto corruption by the very use [n: (Coloss. II, 20 et seq.)]*. [var: Vulg. *Ne tetigeritis, neque gustaveritis, neque contrectaveritis: quae sunt omnia in interitum ipso usu*, "touch not, taste not, handle not: which are all unto destruction by the very use"]

## 9.

*Let my brother come down into his garden, that he may eat [1920A] the fruit of his fruit-trees.* What are these fruit-trees? Thou wast made a dry tree in Adam, but now through the grace of Christ thou hast sprouted as a fruit-bearing tree. Gladly did the Lord Jesus receive it, and with heavenly condescension answered his Church: *I have come down,* he says, *into my garden, I have gathered myrrh with my ointments, I have eaten my bread with my honey, and I have drunk my wine with my milk.*

## 10.

Eat, he says, my brethren, and be drunken: *I have gathered myrrh with my ointments [n: (Psalm. LXXIX, 9)]*. What is this vintage? Know the vineyard, and ye shall recognize the vintage. *A vineyard,* he says, *thou hast brought out of Egypt,* that is, the people of God. Ye are the vineyard, ye are the vintage: as a vineyard ye were planted, as a vintage ye have given fruit. *I have gathered myrrh with [1920B] my ointments,* that is, unto the odor which ye have received.

## 11.

I have eaten my bread with my honey. Thou seest that in this bread there is no bitterness, but that all is sweetness. *I have drunk my wine with my milk,* dost thou see that such is the gladness, which is polluted by the filth of no sin? For as often as thou drinkest, thou receivest remission of sins, and art made drunken with the spirit. Whence the Apostle also says: *Be not drunk with wine . . . . . but be filled with the Holy Spirit [n: (Ephes. V, 18)]*. For he who is made drunken with wine staggers and totters: he who is made drunken with the spirit is rooted in Christ, and therefore that drunkenness is excellent which works soberness of mind.

*1592.* 12. *Eat,* he says, *my friends, and drink, and be drunken.* A good drunkenness, which makes a certain ecstasy of the mind toward better and pleasant [1920C] things, so that our mind, forgetful of its anxieties, may be gladdened with the wine of pleasantness. Good is the drunkenness of the spiritual table; in short, *how excellent is the inebriating cup [n: (Psalm. XXII, 5)]*! [var: Vulg. *calix meus inebrians quam praeclarus est*, "how excellent is my inebriating chalice"] And elsewhere thou hast: *Make drunk her streams, multiply her generations [n: (Psalm. LXIV, 11)]*; [var: Vulg. *multiplica genimina ejus*, "multiply her fruits"] in that the drunkenness of the earth, when it has been poured in by a heavenly shower, is wont to raise up seeds and to multiply fruits. And so, when the Word, which came down from heaven like rain, has made drunken with divine preaching the veins of our earth, that is, of the soul and of the mind, the pursuits of divers virtues are aroused, and the fruit of faith and of chaste devotion grows up, and rightly is it said to him: *Thou hast visited the earth, and hast made it drunken [n: (Psalm. LXIV, 10)]*. For by the assumption of a body he visited, that he might heal the sick: he made drunken with spiritual grace, that he might soothe the anxious [1920D] with pleasantness.

## 13.

*I sleep, and my heart waketh.* There is also a working sleep of the saints, according to what is written: *I sleep, and my heart waketh.* And according as holy Jacob, sleeping, saw the divine mysteries which waking he had not seen; from heaven to the lands the air going before the saints, the Lord looking back, and promising the possession of that land. And so, sleeping in a short sleep, he obtained what afterwards he acquired with great labor, for his inheritance is the sleep of the saints, keeping holiday from all pleasures of the body. [1921A] from every disturbance of the mind, bringing tranquillity to the mind, placidity to the soul; that, as though loosed from the bond of the body, it may lift itself up and cleave to Christ. This is the sleep of the life of the saints.

## 14.

*The voice of my beloved knocking, open to me, my sister.* Even if thou sleep, if only Christ has known the devotion of thy soul, he comes, and knocks at her door, and says: *Open to me, my sister.* Well *sister,* because the marriage of the Word and of the soul is spiritual; for souls know not the covenants of wedlock, and the practice of bodily union, but are as the angels of God in heaven. *Open,* he says, *to me,* but close to strangers. Close to the age, close to the world. Neither go thou forth outside to those material things, nor, leaving thine own light, seek another's; because material light pours in a dark [1921B] gloom, so that the light of true glory is not seen. *Open therefore to me,* be not straitened, but enlarge thyself, and I will fill thee. And because, having traversed the world, I have found more of troubles and of offense, and have not easily had where I might rest; therefore do thou open, that in thee the son of man may lay his head, for whom there is no rest save upon the humble and the meek.
