## 15.

*Open to me, my sister; arise, my neighbor, my dove, my perfect one.* [var: Vulg. has *amica mea* and *immaculata mea*, "my friend… my spotless one," and prints no *surge*] Neighbor by charity, a dove by simplicity, perfect by virtue. *For my head is full of dew;* for as the dew of heaven removes the drynesses of the night, so the dew of our Lord Jesus Christ has distilled the moisture of eternal life upon the nightly and worldly darkness. This is the head which knew not how to grow parched by the heat of the world, whence he says: *For if they do these things in the moist, [1921C] what shall they do in the dry [n: (Luc. XXIII, 31)]*? [var: Vulg. *in viridi ligno… quid fiet*, "in the green wood… what shall be done"] This head therefore bedews others, and for itself abounds. And well does the head of Christ abound; because thy head is Christ, who is always full, and is not drained dry by his own liberalities, nor fails through long-continued largess. Upon this head no iron comes up, which is the instrument of war, the badge of discord. Now look, and see of what sort that dew is, namely, not of common moisture.

## 16.

*But my locks are of the drops of the night.* [var: Vulg. *cincinni mei guttis noctium*, ablative and with "nights" plural] Do not, my neighbor, take these locks to be locks of bodily hairs: those are not ornaments, but crimes; the pandering of beauty, not the precepts of virtue. The Nazirite has other locks, upon which no iron comes up, which no one cuts away: locks not arranged with the curling-iron or ordered by art, but which shone forth curling with the manifold grace of gleaming virtues. [1921D] Learn in the history what manner of locks the Nazirite has, which, so long as Samson kept them untouched, he could never be overcome. He lost his locks, and lost the merit of his virtue [n: (Jud. XVI, 17)].

## 17.

[n: (Vers. 3.)] Therefore, when the voice of the Word has been heard, seek not how thou mayest put on the tunic which thou hadst put off by night; for it is shown, and frequently offered through spiritual wickedness, how — how, I say, thou mayest put it on: forget it and know it not; as though the Lord were already standing by, do thou, free of bodily bonds, rise up troubled, [1922A] and prepare the inward mind by prayers while thou risest; that from lowly things thou mayest strive toward the things above, and be zealous to open the doors of thy heart. While *1593* thou stretchest out thy hands to Christ, thy deeds shall breathe a faithful odor.

## 18.

*Open to me, my sister.* When thou desirest to meet one of the foremost men, dost thou not first draw near to his house: afterwards thou seekest to be informed and instructed, that thou mayest learn the mind of the master of the house: then, that thou mayest enter his house, thou entreatest that no one cast thee off and shut thee out. Knock thou therefore also at that heavenly palace; knock not with the hand of the body, but with a certain right hand of thy prayer. Not the hand of the body alone knocks; the voice knocks also, for it is written: *The voice of my brother knocks at the door.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 5:2 *Vox dilecti mei pulsantis*, "the voice of my beloved knocking"] We knock also with the finger. In short, [1922B] Thomas too deserved with his finger to open the door of the resurrection: and Jesus says to thee: *Put thy finger hither, and put it into my hands and my side, and be not unbelieving, but faithful [n: (Joan. XX, 27)]*. [var: Vulg. *et vide manus meas, et affer manum tuam, et mitte in latus meum*, "and see my hands, and bring thy hand, and put it into my side"] Knock therefore with thy finger, if thou canst not with the whole hand; knock at the door. Christ is the door, who says: *By me if any man shall enter in, he shall be saved [n: (Joan. X, 9)]*.

## 19.

When therefore thou hast knocked at this door, see how thou enterest; lest perchance, even having entered, thou be outside the sight of the king. Many enter palaces, and do not straightway see that king of the earth, but often keep watch, that at some time they may deserve to see him. Nor do they presume upon the opportunity of seeing him, but are presented at his bidding, and pour out a petition; that they may be received with good will, being careful of the first approaches of their speech; [1922C] that nothing stumble, that nothing give offense: how much more must God be entreated, that our prayer may enter the door of his mercy!

## 20.

*The voice of my brother knocks at the door.* Learn thou also, O bride of Christ, to bar thy door in the times of night; lest anyone easily find it lying open. The Bridegroom himself wills it to be shut when he knocks. Our door is our mouth; it ought to be opened to Christ almost alone, nor let it be opened unless the Word of God has first knocked. *The voice of my brother knocks at the door.* Hear him knocking, hear him desiring to come in.

## 21.

*[n: (Vers. 2.)] Open to me, my sister, my bride, my dove, my perfect one; for my head is full of dew, and my hair of the drops of the night.* Consider when God the Word most of all knocks at thy door: when [1922D] his head has been filled with the nightly dew. For he deigns to visit those set in tribulation and temptations; lest anyone perchance succumb, overcome by hardships. His head therefore is filled with dew or with drops, when his body labors. Then therefore must we keep watch, lest, when the Bridegroom has come, he depart shut out. For if thou sleep, and thy heart do not keep watch, he departs before he knocks: if thy heart keep watch, he knocks, and demands that the door be opened to him.

## 22.

We have therefore a door of our soul; we have also gates, of which it is said: *Lift up your gates, ye princes, [1923A] and be ye lifted up, ye everlasting gates, and the King of glory shall enter in [n: (Psal. XXIII, 7)]*. There is therefore also a heaven in those in whom are the everlasting gates. If thou wilt raise up these gates of thy faith, the King of glory shall enter in to thee, bearing the triumph of his own passion. For justice has gates, for of these too we read it written, the Lord Jesus saying through his prophet: *Open to me the gates of justice [n: (Psal. CXVII, 19)]*. And below the prophet David says: *Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem; praise thy God, O Sion; for he hath strengthened the bolts of thy gates [n: (Psal. CXLVII, 12)]*.

## 23.

There is therefore a soul which has a door, there is one which has gates. To this door Christ comes, and knocks: he knocks also at the gates. Open therefore to him; he wills to enter, he wills to find the Bride keeping watch. Do not therefore [1923B] make delays for a good lover: he quickly withdraws; and thou by the sleep of thy body shalt be seen to have shut out him who knocks. For thou dost shut him out when thou art slothful, when lazy, when drowsy. By these bars Christ is shut out: if thou be chaste and sober, beware lest thou be negligent. He does Christ the greater injury who repels him at his coming.

## 24.

*Open to me, my sister, my neighbor:* he knocks at the door even when thou sleepest: if only, being roused, thou awake, or, being called, thou open the door of thy breast, he will enter in. But if thou flee the prophetic reading, if thou read not at home, if thou be unwilling to hear in the Church — art thou not like him who winks with averted gaze, that he may not see what he could see, and shuts his eyes lest he look, though the power of seeing is his: or as many in a rage have cast their hands upon their own eyes; so thou too at first turnest away [1923C] rather by a winking dissimulation than by an abrupt refusal. For when thou comest to the Church and assertest that thou art *1594* a Christian, thou seemest whole, thou openest the eyes with which thou couldst see: but while thou dissemblest to hear the things that are read, thou closest them lest thou see for thyself, even if to others thou seem to see. Thou castest also certain hands of faithlessness and intemperance upon the eyes of thy soul, and bringest upon thy heart a blindness (which is the graver thing) that is voluntary; so that seeing thou seest not, hearing thou hearest not.

## 25.

Dost thou think that thou art alone when thou committest fornication, and dost not remember that the eyes of the Lord behold the round earth? Dost thou not hear him who says: *Behold, the hour comes that ye be scattered every man to his own, and leave me alone: but I am not alone, for the Father is with me [n: (Joan. XVI, 32)]*. The Father therefore is present, the Son of God also is present, the ministers are present, [1923D] the Cherubim and Seraphim are present, who say. *Holy, holy, holy, the earth is full of thy majesty [n: (Esai. VI)]*. [var: Vulg. *plena est omnis terra gloria ejus*, "the whole earth is full of his glory"] For the world is full of virtues, because it is full of wickednesses: the round earth is full of remedies, because it is full of snares.

## 26.

*[n: (Vers. 3.)] I have put off my tunic, how shall I put it on?* [var: Vulg. *Expoliavi me tunica mea: quomodo induar illa?*, passive, "how shall I be clothed with it?"] For indeed in this night of the world the garment of bodily life must first be put off by thee; for the Lord put off flesh from himself, that for thee he might triumph over the dominations and powers of this world. *How shall I put it on?* See, O soul devoted to God, what she says. [1924A] So has she put off the acts of the body and earthly manners, that she knows not how, even if she should wish it, she could put them on again. *How shall I put it on?* That is: with what shame, with what modesty, with what memory at the last? For the habit of good has lost the use of the old depravity.

## 27.

The soul, hearing *Open to me,* having a head full of dew — that is, of worldly temptations — suddenly troubled, and as one about to rise who was bidden to rise, says, while she gave off the scent of aloes and myrrh, the badge of burial: *I have put off my tunic, how shall I put it on: I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them?* For she fears lest she rise again into temptations, lest she return again into fault and sin, and begin to defile her goings-out and the advances of her virtues with earthly footprints.
