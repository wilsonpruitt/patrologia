## 28.

Assuredly in this way also she declares the perfection of her virtue, in that she has deserved so great a charity of Christ; that he comes to her, and knocks at her door, and comes with the Father, and sups with that same soul, and she with him, as John the apostle said in the Apocalypse [n: (Apoc. III, 20)]. For indeed, when she had heard in the passages above: *Be here from Lebanon;* [var: Vulg. *Veni de Libano, sponsa, veni de Libano*, "Come from Lebanon, my bride, come from Lebanon"] and when she perceived that she could not be present to Christ in the flesh, but was then present if she were present in the spirit, conforming herself to his will, that she too might be conformed to the image of Christ; now she does not feel the spoils of the flesh, now, as it were a spirit stripping itself of the conjunction of the body, now as one forgetful, and one who, if she wished, could not remember that coupling, she says: *I have put off my tunic, how shall I put it on?* For I have put off that garment of skins which Adam and Eve received after [1924C] their fault, the tunic of corruption, the tunic of the passions; *How shall I put it on?* She does not ask to put it on: but so signifies it cast away, that now it could not be a garment for her.

## 29.

*[n: (Vers. 3.)] I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them?* That is: I have washed my footsteps, while I was going out, and lifting myself from the fellowship of the body, from that connection and familiarity of the carnal embrace: how shall I defile them, so as to return into the enclosure of the body and that dark prison of its passions? So do we bury the passions, if we do not loose the cross of this body; if we do not write again, upon the cross of Christ, the bond of sin which has been blotted out; if we do not put on the garment of the old man which we have put off. For it is written in the Canticles: *I have put off my tunic, [1924D] how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them?* The members of the body therefore have been mortified: why do its vices sprout up? Therefore the law did not prevail, because it did not mortify the flesh: therefore it passed by like a shadow, because it did not discolor: and therefore it overshadowed us from the sun of justice, because it heaped up crimes.

## 30.

Sometimes to be naked is a sign of virtue, because it is written: *I have put off my tunic, how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them?* What tunic therefore she has put off, and how she has not found in what manner [1925A] to put it on, let us disclose from the Scriptures if we can. For there is a certain bodily tunic, there are certain woven veils of desires; and therefore it is sometimes better to be naked in body than veiled in heart. Whence Paul too admonishes us that it is *1595* much better to be stripped than to be clothed over, saying elsewhere: *Stripping yourselves of the old man with his acts, put on the new, who is renewed unto knowledge, according to the image of him who created him.* [var: Vulg. *et induentes novum*, a participle, "and putting on the new"] Therefore she who has been stripped, and has her feet washed, knows not how she may not be defiled; for by grace she forgets what she had drawn in by nature.

## 31.

*I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them?* Thou hast learned in the Gospel that to wash the feet is a mystery of faith, a badge of humility, according to what is written: [1925B] *If I, the Lord and master, have washed your feet, how much more ought you to wash one another's feet [n: (Joan. XIII, 14)]*! [var: Vulg. *et vos debetis alter alterius lavare pedes*, "you also ought to wash one another's feet"] But this pertains to humility. As to the mystery, however, he ought to wash his feet who wishes to have part with Christ: *For if I shall not wash,* he says, *thy feet, thou shalt not have part with me [n: (Ibid. 8)]*. [var: Vulg. *Si non lavero te*, "If I wash thee not"] When this is said to Peter, what is judged of us? He therefore who has washed his feet has no need to wash again; and therefore let him beware lest he defile them.

## 32.

And well does holy Church say: *I have washed my feet;* she does not say, how shall I wash them again, but *how shall I defile them again?* As one forgetful of the old stain, forgetful of the contagion, she admonishes how in a bodily ministry we ought to wash away the spiritual trace of our acts. And so, when once thou hast washed thy feet [1925C] with the watering of the eternal fountain, and hast cleansed them by the sacrament of the mystery, beware again of the filth of bodily desire, lest thy miry acts be defiled with earthly squalor. These are the feet which David washed in the spirit, who teaches thee how thou canst not defile them, saying: *Our feet were standing in thy courts, O Jerusalem [n: (Psal. CXXI, 2)]*. Assuredly understand here the feet not of the body but of the mind; for how should an earthly man have the feet of the body in heaven? Jerusalem, as Paul has taught thee, is in heaven: and the same has taught thee how thou canst stand in heaven, when he says: *But our conversation is in heaven [n: (Philip. III, 20)]*. The conversation of manners, the conversation of deeds, the conversation of faith.

## 33.

*I have done judgment and justice, deliver me not [1925D] to them that slander me.* Where justice is, there is mercy. Mercy frees from sin. How therefore am I delivered to sinners? Like to this is that in the Canticles: *I have put off my tunic, how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them?* I have put off the tunic of the sinner, and the veil of the earthly man; why am I adjudged as though a sinner and an earthly man? I have washed my feet, that no lot of sin might cleave to my footstep; why dost thou give power over me to them that offend?

## 34.

Jeremiah in his Lamentations says: *Thy iniquity hath failed, [1926A] O daughter of Sion; he shall no more add to drive thee out: he hath visited thine iniquities, O daughter of Edom, he hath revealed upon thy sins [n: (Thren. IV, 21)]*. [var: Vulg. *Completa est iniquitas tua… discooperuit peccata tua*, "Thy iniquity is accomplished… he hath uncovered thy sins"] Dost thou observe that iniquity could not fail without God's visitation, nor the correction be full, save through the grace of the Lord and Savior? But how iniquity fails, hear the Church saying: *I have put off my tunic, how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them?* The garment of the old man, therefore, woven through with the vices of error, laid down in the regeneration of the laver, she knows not how she could put on; for by the zeal of correction a forgetfulness of sins had grown up in her. So great is the force of a completed amendment, that she returns to a certain age of spiritual childhood, which knows not the ways of error; even if she wished, she could not admit a crime, because use has grown unaccustomed [1926B] to knowing how to sin.

## 35.

*[n: (Vers. 4.)] My brother put his hand in from the lookout, and my belly was troubled over him. I myself rose up to open to my brother. My hands dropped myrrh, my fingers were full of myrrh.* [var: Vulg. *Dilectus meus misit manum suam per foramen, et venter meus intremuit ad tactum ejus*, "My beloved put his hand through the opening, and my belly trembled at his touch"] First therefore he puts in his hand as though from a lookout, when God is estimated from his works, whence he says: *If ye believe not me, believe the works [n: (Joan. X, 38)]*. [var: Vulg. *et si mihi non vultis credere*, "and if you will not believe me"] Then love is increased, and, conceived in the innermost bowels, grows up. Thereupon, his seeds having been poured into the intelligible womb in which is the receptacle of the Word, our soul desires to drink in his fullness dwelling bodily; for the womb of women is not troubled, save the wombs of those who have been heavy in the belly. The soul therefore rises to open to the Word of God, but while she spreads herself out and [1926C] opens, that she may mortify the works of this world by the reception of the Word, like him who says: *We carry about the mortification of the Lord Jesus in our body [n: (II Cor. IV, 19)]*. Therefore, while she opens, the Bridegroom passes by; for he wills always to be sought, and more frequently to be found. And if he shall have found the door shut, he knocks; and if through delay *1596* he has been shut out, he withdraws; but quickly he returns, to him he knocks again, that at least afterwards he may find his Bride prepared.

## 36.

It can indeed be taken thus also, *My brother passed by:* as we read that he penetrated the inmost marrow of the beloved, in the manner in which it was said to Mary: *And a sword shall pierce through thy own soul, that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed [n: (Luc. II, 35)]*. [var: Vulg. *ex multis cordibus*, "out of many hearts"] In short, the Bride added that her soul had gone out at his word, which happens when the soul is a pilgrim from [1926D] the body and is present to God. Hearing these things the Bridegroom sent his operation, the Word, as through a cave, not yet face to face: he sent it as it were a hand, *And my belly,* she says, *was troubled over him.* Whosoever so lives can say:

## 37.

*My brother put his hand through the lookout, and my belly was troubled at him: I rose to open to my brother.* It is good that at the coming of the Lord the inward parts be troubled. If Mary was troubled at the coming of the angel, how much more are we troubled at the coming [1927A] of Christ! For when the things of God flow in, bodily affection goes into exile, and that habit of the outward man grows out of use. Do thou also be troubled, do thou make haste. Those men are bidden to eat the lamb in haste. Rise, open: Christ is at the door, he knocks at the porches of thy house: if thou shalt open, he will enter in, and will enter in with the Father. Nor only when he has entered does he leave a reward: but even before he enters he sends the reward on ahead. Still the soul is troubled, still she gropes along the walls of her house, still she seeks the door where Christ is, still she looses the bond of the flesh and the enclosure of the body, still Christ knocks outside.
