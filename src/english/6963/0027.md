## 38.

What is our window, but that through which we see the works of Christ, namely the eye of the soul and the gaze of the mind? And therefore, O virgin, let Christ come in through thy window, [1927B] let Christ put in his hand: let the love of the Word come to thee through the window, not the love of the body. If therefore the Word of God puts in his hand through thy window, see how thou preparest thy windows, see how thou oughtest to wipe them clean from every dust of offenses. Let the windows of a virgin have nothing foul, nothing adulterate. Far off be the antimony and the other trifles of an affected comeliness, far off the enticements of an adulterate love. Like to this is the closing of the ears, on which no burdens are to be hung, no wounds to be fixed, but there is one adornment: to hear what profits.

## 39.

*[n: (Vers. 6.)] I opened,* she says, *to my brother; my brother passed by.* How did he pass by? That is, he penetrated the inward parts of the mind, as it was said to Mary: *And a sword shall pierce through thy own soul.* For the living Word of God, [1927C] like a sharp sword, penetrating the bars of bodily thoughts, searches out the inward parts of the heart. Set therefore in thy chamber and in the time of night, meditate always upon Christ, and hope for his coming at every moment. If he seems to thee to delay, rise up. He seems to delay when thou sleepest long: he seems to delay when thou art idle from prayer: he seems to delay when thou dost not lift up thy voice in psalms. Dedicate to Christ the firstfruits of thy watchings, offer to Christ the firstfruits of thy acts.

## 40.

*[n: (Vers. 5.)] I myself rose up to open to my brother; my hands dropped myrrh, my fingers are full of myrrh upon the hands of the fastening.* [var: Vulg. *digiti mei pleni myrrha probatissima*, "my fingers full of the choicest myrrh"] Let us consider what this signifies. First, as I have said, God the Word is seen in his own works as through a cave, not fully [1927D] and perfectly: then love is increased and the conception grows, and out of his seeds, which the soul has received in a certain intelligible womb, she desires to see the whole fullness of his divinity dwelling in him bodily, as we read. She rose that she might see that Word of God more closely, and in this very thing her advance is signified, that she rose by vigor and strength; for the presence of the Word drank in the strength of the soul, as the presence of Mary, when she was heavy with the womb, instructed John set within the womb: so far that he leapt out of the womb and exulted, recognizing the presence of the Lord. [1928A]

## 41.

She rose to open, and her works and deeds were mortified to the world. For such ought the soul to be which is to receive the Word: that she die to the world, and be buried together in Christ; for thus is Christ found, and such a lodging does he seek for himself. Then the very ministries of her operations, that is, the hands and the fingers by which Christ is grasped, are mortified: which fingers we may reckon as the eminent works *1597* of our deeds. And so, as it were out of his own embrace, when she was already stretching out her intelligible hands and fingers to grasp the Word, the devout soul says that it passed by her, yet not that it had as yet passed through. And this advance it has, when the Word of God passes by and passes through the soul, because it is written: *And a sword shall pierce through thy own soul, that the thoughts of many [1928B] hearts may be revealed [n: (Luc. II, 35)]*. Hence there is as yet a passing by, not a passing through; as in Mary, in the later things, there is perhaps a passing through, where the Lord Jesus is set as a seal in the midst of her.

## 42.

In short, straightway there is another advance of the Word passing through, in that the soul went out at his Word; that is, following his Word she went out of the body, lifting herself out of its habitation, and making herself a pilgrim to it, that she might be present to God, and be a fellow-citizen of the saints. For we cannot at once be of the household both of the flesh and of God. Therefore in this place the soul is signified to go out, as I have said, when she withdraws herself from the pleasures of the body. In short, it is written: *Go out of Babylon, fleeing from the Chaldeans [n: (Esai. XLVIII, 20)]*. [var: Vulg. *Egredimini de Babylone, fugite a Chaldaeis*, both plural imperatives] Not indeed that the Hebrew should flee the regions of Babylon, but its manners, as he is admonished by the prophetic [1928C] address: since indeed there are those who are Hebrews in Babylon, and teach by their manners that they have gone out of Babylon. For those too of whom the prophet says that *they sat upon the rivers of Babylon [n: (Ps. CXXXVI, 1)]* sat indeed in the region of Babylon, but were not in its vices and confusion. For how were they in that confusion of vices, who wept, doing penance, because they had fallen away from the ark of devotion and faith, and from the merits of their fathers' virtue? But the soul which goes out at the word seeks the Word; whence she also, while she was seeking, fell in with the guards who go about the city.

*[n: (Vers. 7.)] The guards who go about the city found me, they struck me, and wounded me, and the keepers of the walls took away my cloak from me.* Well indeed did she come as a bride with a cloak, with which she might veil [1928D] her head when the bridegroom should meet her; as Rebecca, who, having learned that Isaac was coming to meet her, came down from the camel and covered herself with a cloak: so this soul too sent before her the badges of the nuptial garment, lest perchance she be cast out as not having a wedding garment; or that she might veil her head because of the angels. But they struck her, that she might be the more tested; for souls are exercised by temptations. They took her cloak from her, seeking whether she brought the true beauty of naked virtue, or because one ought to enter that heavenly city without a covering, bringing with him no coverings [1929A] of paint. There are also those who search out whether any soul carries with her the spoils of carnal enticement and bodily concupiscence. She is stripped of her cloak when her conscience is made manifest. But there is one who is well stripped, to whom it is permitted to imitate him who says: *For the prince of this world comes, and in me he finds nothing [n: (Joan. XIV, 30)]*; [var: Vulg. *et in me non habet quidquam*, "and in me he hath not anything"] for assuredly he found nothing in him alone who did no sin.

## 44.

Blessed also is she in whom he finds nothing grievous or manifold, but finds in her the garment of faith and the discipline of wisdom. And so without loss to herself (because even if one should wish it, he cannot take away true wisdom), though the adversary clamor; yet where the true integrity of a harmless conversation shines out, she has passed the guards, and, mingled with the daughters of that heavenly city, seeks the Word; [1929B] and by seeking she stirs up his charity within herself, and learns where she may seek the Word. It is not only an injury not to have found him whom thou seekest, but very often to have sought where one ought not is itself a wound: to have sought in the houses of men who falsely take to themselves the name of teachers, to have sought more boldly than modestly. Therefore let us beware, by her example, lest the guards who go about the city find us.

## 45.

*They found me,* she says, *the guards who go about the city: they struck and wounded me, and the keepers of the walls took away my cloak from me.* Not in herself, O daughters, but in us is the Church wounded. Let us therefore beware lest our fall become the Church's wound, lest anyone take from us the cloak, that is, the garment of prudence, the badge of patience, by which the ambition of a softer [1929C] garment is put off: for *They who are clothed in softer garments are in the houses of kings [n: (Matth. XI, 8)]*. [var: Vulg. *qui mollibus vestiuntur*, "they who are clothed in soft things"] But to us Christ has given the cloak with which he himself clothed his apostles and his own body, which in short he bids thee give, if any man asks thy tunic, that thou leave him the cloak also [n: (Matth. V, 40)]; that is, that thou hand over the badge of thy philosophy, and clothe him about, as with the garment of thy prudence, *1598* who before was naked.

## 46.

There therefore, O daughters, let us seek Christ where the Church seeks him, on the mountains of good odor, which by the sublimity of their lofty deeds breathe out the sweet odor of life according to the summits of their merits. She who seeks Christ ought not to be common, ought not to be in the market or in the streets, querulous of voice, slippery of step, easy of approach, cheap of aspect. The Apostle denies thee earthly fellowship, and teaches that beyond the bounds, almost, of nature [1929D] we must fly up to heaven on spiritual wings. *Mind the things that are above,* he says, *not the things that are upon the earth [n: (Colos. III, 2)]*. But because this was impossible for those shut up in this enclosure, as it were, of the body, and because when bodies are dead the soul is said to fly back to the things above, being bound by a certain law of our nature while we live, therefore he added: *For you are dead, and your life is hidden with Christ in God [n: (Ibid., 3)]*. If it is hidden with Christ in God, let it not appear to the world, for Christ is dead to the world, and lives to God. See now how Christ loves to be desired, and loves not [1930A] idle talk. That virgin opened her doors to the Word of God: but *he passed by,* she says, *and my soul went out at his word [n: (Vers. 6)]*. [var: Vulg. *Anima mea liquefacta est, ut locutus est*, "My soul melted when he spoke"] She went out from the world, she went out from the age, she remained in Christ. *I sought him,* she says, *and I found him not.* For Christ loves to be sought long.
