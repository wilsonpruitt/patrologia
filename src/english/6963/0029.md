## 55.

Vain indeed was the effect of his petition, but the affection of the servant was worthy of approval; because, having advanced by devotion beyond his own nature, he estimated from the Lord's angels [1932D] how much more he might desire of the Lord's countenance, whereby he longed to fill up his desire. He knew that in man himself there would be after death one glory and another brightness: *For as star differs from star in brightness, so also is the resurrection of the dead; which, even if it is sown in corruption, yet shall rise in incorruption, shall rise in glory, shall rise in power, shall rise a spiritual body [n: (I Cor. XV, 41-44)].* He therefore who could know these things rightly presumed to desire to see the countenance of the Lord, since he would see it when the death of the body had been undergone. Such a form assuredly had he taken on, and such did the Lord already approve, that the heavenly minister and executor of the oracle stood no distance from the angels. And therefore, [1933A] since he knew *That the angels of the little ones daily see the face of the Father who is in heaven [n: (Matth. XVIII, 10)]*, [var: Vulg. *angeli eorum in caelis semper vident faciem Patris mei*, "their angels in heaven always see the face of my Father"] he thought that he could and ought already to see it, as one forgetful of the body and having laid the flesh aside; like him who, being still set in the body, when he was caught up into paradise, said that he knew no further whether he had been caught up to the third heaven in the body or outside the body [n: (II Cor. XII, 3)]. For the saints, even when they bear a body, are nevertheless not in the flesh but in the spirit; since they who are in carnal desire and conversation cannot please God. But the saints are not in the flesh by their conversation, but in heaven.

## 56.

From these things therefore let us comprehend the affection of the languishing Bride. She desired to see the countenance of God as one innocent, as it were with a certain countenance of conscience, and she longed to open [1933B] to him the face of her inward mind, and to be more fully known. Impatient love, knocking day and night at harlots' doors, if the desires of possessing are put off longer, itself faints in the waiting while it hopes: in which assuredly there is not an end of love, but an increase. And whatever is desirable, if it does not fall to him who desires it, he faints toward it, and he who desires lays down, as it were, his very soul; yet if a nearer hope rises up, the hope close at hand gives strength; but if there be an absence of the beloved, by that very thing whereby she desires him absent, she who has longed suffers a fainting of her own soul. And so, the further off that is which is desired, the more does he faint who desires it. This therefore is to faint: that every man passes over with all his zeal into that which he loves; he thinks it, he cleaves to it, he sounds it forth, that which he has received to be loved; into it he is poured over by a certain [1933C] fainting of the soul. As if a mother should await the presence of a son, in the way that the wife of Tobias awaited her travelling son; fainting from desire, and set in straits, she seemed as one undone [n: (Tob. X, 3 et seq.)]. For what else do her words signify but a certain fainting? But the more the affection is wearied, the more abundantly does love grow; and the longer he who desires is absent, the more do the desires of her who waits take fire with a certain greater force of love. The flesh faints, but the desire is nourished and increased.

## 57.

And so the holy soul knows not how to desire anything other than the Bridegroom, who is Christ Jesus: him she longs for, him she desires, toward him she strains with all her powers, him she cherishes in the bosom of her mind, to him she opens and pours out herself, and this alone she fears, that she might lose him. [1933D] And so, the greater the desire with which the soul has been roused, longing to cleave to God her Savior, so much the more does she faint. Therefore this fainting works indeed a diminution of frailty, but a taking-on of strength. She longed, she desired, she fainted, she was dissolved in her whole affection, that she might become wholly his whom she desired, as David himself also says: *I pour out my prayer in his sight, and I declare my tribulation before him, in the failing of my spirit [1934A] from me [n: (Ps. CXLI, 3 et 4)]*. For his spirit failed; nay rather, the spirit failed from him who denies himself, that he may cleave to Christ. And so, as one impatient of love, she ran about, and sought everywhere the Word of God, because she was wounded, because she was naked; and she sought the support of someone by whom Christ might be asked to come. See her burning, see her desiring: *I adjure you,* she says, *O daughters of Jerusalem,* etc.; then, mingled with the daughters of that heavenly city, she seeks the Word, and by seeking and by contemplating his beauty, she stirs up his charity within herself. *1601*

## 58.

*[n: (Vers. 10.)] My brother,* she says, *is white and red, chosen out of thousands.* [var: Vulg. *Dilectus meus candidus et rubicundus*, "My beloved is white and ruddy"] For it is fitting, O virgin, that thou shouldst fully know whom thou lovest, and shouldst acknowledge in him every mystery both of his unbegotten divinity and of the incorporation [1934B] he assumed. He is therefore white by right, because he is the splendor of the Father: red, because he is the offspring of a virgin. The color of each nature shines and glows red in him. Yet remember that the badges of divinity in him are more ancient than the mysteries of the body; because he did not begin from the Virgin, but he who was came into the Virgin.

## 59.

*My brother is white and red.* White with eternal brightness, red with the appearance of human color, which he took on by the mystery of the incarnation. And rightly does that very thing which is red smell sweet; because the flesh of Christ is without sin, which the faithless, handling it, defiled their own hands: while the saints, venerating it, burned with the odor of piety.

## 60.

*Chosen out of thousands. Ten thousands,* says Jacob, *of Ephraim and thousands of Manasseh;* that is, let him have dominion over both the Jews [1934C] and the Gentiles, and out of both peoples acquire for himself the fullness of the Church. And therefore holy Jacob laid his right hand upon Ephraim; because thou readest, the Church saying in the Song of Songs: *My brother is white and red, chosen out of ten thousands.* [var: Vet. Lat. — Migne prints this same lemma as *ex millibus*, "out of thousands," at §§58–59 above, and *ex decem millibus* here; the plate is inconsistent with itself and is not harmonized] In short, they proclaimed even David — the author of the young Mary, from whose succession Christ was born by the Virgin's childbearing — in ten thousands: but Saul in thousands, though they ought rather to have set the favor of reverence toward the king first.

## 61.

*[n: (Vers. 11.)] His head is gold Cephas.* [var: Vulg. *aurum optimum*, "the finest gold"] Which signifies a steadfast and eminent wisdom. The body of Christ is the Church, and the head of this is as gold, because the wisdom of the saints is precious, that is, men just and prudent. [1934D]

## 62.

*His hairs are as fir trees, black as the raven.* [var: Vulg. *comae ejus sicut elatae palmarum, nigrae quasi corvus*, "his locks as the branches of palm trees, black as a crow"] Of this he said elsewhere, *Thy head of hair is as a flock of shorn ones.* Therefore a head of hair, because the power of all the senses is in the head: for *The eyes of the wise man are in his head [n: (Eccle. II, 14)]*. The deep prudence, therefore, of the teachers, which can reveal the things that are obscure and open the heights of the senses. And disputants of this kind are the hairs of the Church, like the young of the ravens to whom the Lord gives food: as he gave to holy Jacob, and fed him [1935A] from his youth. These others, and deep ones, the Lord feeds with heavenly mysteries in the richness of doctrine.

## 63.

*[n: (Vers. 12.)] His eyes* also *are as doves.* The eyes are men, that is to say, adorned with spiritual senses, who are keen for the seeing of mysteries, and ready for penetrating the secrets of divine Scripture, shining with rational milk, in whom there is no spotted confusion of guile, but the unspotted sincerity of a simple affection. Therefore he made mention of these doves as washed in milk amid an abundance of waters, saying:

## 64.

*His eyes are as doves upon the abundance of waters, washed in milk, of one sitting upon fullness,* [var: Vulg. *super rivulos aquarum, quae lacte sunt lotae, et resident juxta fluenta plenissima*, "upon the little brooks of waters, which are washed with milk, and sit beside the plentiful streams"] The Lord baptizes in milk, that is, in sincerity. And these are they who are truly baptized in milk, who believe without guile, and bring a pure faith, that they may put on an unspotted [1935B] grace. Therefore the Bride ascends white to Christ, because she has been baptized in milk; therefore the powers marvel at her, saying: *Who is this that comes up made white [n: (Cant. VIII, 7)]*? [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:5 *Quae est ista quae ascendit de deserto, deliciis affluens*, "Who is this that comes up from the desert, flowing with delights"] A little while before she was saying, *I am black [n: (Cant. I, )]*: now she is seen made white, and ascends to heaven, and, leaning upon the Word of God, now penetrates the heights.
