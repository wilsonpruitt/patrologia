## 65.

And not undeservedly is there an abundance of waters there where Christ is, from whom the human mind desires to be filled. These are the waters the stag thirsts for, which, when he has drunk them, he cannot thirst. These waters the Prophet thirsted for when he said: *My soul hath thirsted for thee [n: (Psalm. XLI, 3)]*. [var: Vulg. Ps 41:3 *Sitivit anima mea ad Deum fontem vivum*, "My soul hath thirsted after God, the living fountain"; the printed wording is that of Ps 62:2] Christ therefore sits upon the abundance of waters, and upon the fullness, and therefore he who baptizes in water says: *And we all have received of his fullness [n: (Joan I, 16)]*. Whence the eye of punishments also is not alien to the Church; because [1935C] even if John was baptizing in Aenon, he was baptizing near Salim, where there was an abundance of waters, and twelve fountains, and seventy palm trees. These fountains the Church has, that is, in the Old Testament the twelve patriarchs, in the New the twelve apostles; and therefore it was said: *In the churches bless ye the Lord God from the fountains of Israel [n: (Psalm. LXVII, 27)]*. With these fountains is he first washed over, whosoever attains the most sacred mysteries; for these fountains, flowing out from the eternal fountain, have streamed over the whole world. Where these fountains are, there is the ascent of souls. In short, they have interpreted Salim as "the very one ascending"; for he truly ascends who lays down his own sins. By this word, therefore, *1602* the use of a purifying sanctification is expressed.

## 66.

*[n: (Vers. 13.)] His cheeks are as phials of spice, [1935D] bringing forth perfumeries.* [var: Vulg. *Genae illius sicut areolae aromatum, consitae a pigmentariis*, "his cheeks are as beds of spices set by the perfumers"] Now we understand the cheeks and the rest to be the virtues of the soul, and the diversities of teachers: who either administer spiritual nourishment to the mind by a diligent stewardship, or bind the hearer, by the preaching of the Lord's cross, as it were with a certain line of the Word, or are most pleasing by the modesty of a bashful nature and by the bloom of youth. And even if they are called back from touch by shame, yet let the odor of Christ be fragrant in them: and as upon the cheeks the ointment comes down as it were from the priestly head, so also let the beauty of doctrine shine out in them.

## 67.

*His lips distilling full myrrh.* [var: Vulg. *Labia ejus lilia distillantia myrrham primam*, "his lips are lilies distilling the first myrrh"] For by [1936A] myrrh is declared the ointment of the passion and the grace of the resurrection, which pours the revived odor of life into the raised-up inward parts of the dead. Let the doors of thy lips therefore be shut, and let the porch of thy voice remain barred, then perhaps to be unbolted when thou hast heard the Word of God. Then shall myrrh sweat forth for thee, then shall the grace of baptism breathe upon thee; that thou mayest die together with the elements of the world, and rise again with Christ.

## 68.

*His lips distilling full myrrh.* But we think ourselves idle if we seem to devote ourselves to the word only: and we reckon of more account those who work than those who exercise the pursuit of knowing the truth; for very many say: Behold the man and his works — as though he who devotes himself to the word does not work; whereas [1936B] this is a work rather than the others. For if justice is a work, if temperance is a work, if fortitude is a work, then assuredly wisdom also is a work; for these four are held to be the principal virtues. For if Christ works according to that which is justice, assuredly he works according to that which is the Word. And he was working when he was in the beginning with the Father. In short, through him all things were made, that thou mayest know that he is the worker of all things, and that our work is Christ Jesus. For indeed, according to that which he is the Word, the Word to those who inquire, the Word is a great work. Whence, when Martha was hastening about the service, but Mary was hearing the word of the Lord, she who was hearing deserved to be set before her who was serving; for when Martha said: *Lord, hast thou no care that my sister hath left me to serve alone? Bid her therefore [1936C] that she help me,* the Lord answered and said: *Martha, Martha, Mary hath chosen a good part, which shall not be taken away from her [n: (Luc. VII, 40-42)]*. [var: Vulg. Luke 10:42 *Maria optimam partem elegit, quae non auferetur ab ea*, "Mary hath chosen the best part"] Thus it is determined by the authority of a divine sentence that to know the Word is a greater work than to serve.

## 69.

But perhaps someone may say that it has been said by the Apostle: *That the kingdom of God is not in speech, but in power [n: (I Cor. IV, 20)]*. I do not deny that it is written, but I recognize in what speech; namely, that which a puffed-up man has poured out, a speech which cannot profit the hearers, which is without the showing of the Spirit and of power. This speech Paul does not deign to recognize; for he wishes rather to commend the power of speech. In short, the Apostle wished his own speech to be such as came in weakness, that it might make others stronger: in fear [1936D] and trembling, that those who feared might dread nothing but the Lord Jesus; that, trembling, they might keep his peace and tranquillity. Hear then what manner of speech the Apostle had: *And my speech,* he says, *and my preaching were not in the persuasion of the words of human wisdom, but in the showing of the Spirit and of power [n: (II Cor. II, 4)]*; [var: Vulg. 1 Cor 2:4 *non in persuasibilibus humanae sapientiae verbis*, "not in the persuasive words of human wisdom"] because faith is established not in the forensic speech of wisdom, but in the showing of the Spirit, but in the power of God.

## 70.

Therefore in the speech of the saints there is power, but in that forensic and philosophical speech the vanity of the world. And that there is power in the speech of the saints, [1937A] let this prophet also teach thee, who says: *The Lord will give the word to them that preach the gospel with much power [n: (Psal. LXVII, 12)]*; that is, that they may be able to preach the Gospel with much power. It is proved therefore that in the preaching of the Gospel there is power, and the preaching of the Gospel is the speech of the saints; so there remains no doubt that in holy speech there is power. And this is the myrrh distilling from the lips of the Bridegroom, namely that by which vices are mortified.

## 71.

*[n: (Vers. 14.)] His hands are turned on the lathe, golden, full of hyacinths.* See what hands made man, and what a man they made! Him assuredly *Whom we have put on according to Christ, despoiling the old man with his acts, and putting on the new who is renewed unto the knowledge according to the image of him who created him: where there is no bondman and free, but all things and [1937B] Christ in all [n: (Colloss., III, 9-11)]*. We have put on Christ therefore, as it is said also elsewhere: *Ye have put on Christ [n: (Galat. III, 27)]*. We have received the Holy Spirit, who not only forgives our sins; *1603* but even makes us his priests, to forgive sins to others. Therefore the Prophet says: *Thou hast fashioned me and hast laid thy hand upon me [n: (Psal. CXXXVIII, 5)]*: [var: Vulg. *Tu formasti me*, "Thou hast formed me"] he fashioned by the clay, he laid on his hand by spiritual grace; although very many take this psalm to be spoken in the person of the Savior. Hear that the hand of the Lord is also called Spirit: Job cries out, *The divine Spirit who made me [n: (Job. XXXIII, 4)]*. [var: Vulg. *Spiritus Dei fecit me*, "The Spirit of God made me"] These therefore are the hands which prepared man, Christ and the Spirit.

## 72.

Therefore the Lord Jesus is also the author of our body, who first made man unto the image; [1937C] and afterwards fashioned him from clay, and willed to save what he had made, to save what he had molded, that he might make the whole man safe. These are the hands of God turned on the lathe, that is, perfect: golden on account of the wisdom which is Christ, full of hyacinths on account of the Holy Spirit, and the fullness of his graces.

## 73.

*[n: (Vers. 14.)] His belly is of ivory, set apart with sapphires.* The Apostle says, *And if he was crucified out of weakness* — behold the belly, that is, the weakness of the flesh — *yet he lives out of the power of God [n: (II Cor. XIII, 4)]*; behold the distinction of the sapphire. For the sapphire, in that it bears the appearance of a serene sky, signifies the works which are of the divinity. But [sic: *Onir*], on account of chastity and brightness and perpetuity, signifies that flesh of the Savior, of which the same Apostle says: *That although we have known Christ according to [1937D] the flesh, yet now we know him no more [n: (II Cor. V, 16)]*.

## 74.

*[n: (Vers. 15.)] His legs are pillars of marble, founded upon golden bases.* They signify that the Apostles are the pillars of the Church, who were founded in holy fear. For as Peter, James, John and Barnabas seemed to be pillars of the Church, so also whosoever has overcome this world becomes a pillar of God, whom he strengthens who says: *I have strengthened its pillars [n: (Psal. LXXIV, 4)]*. So too the golden base is fear full of discipline, [1938A] because it is the beginning of wisdom. In the fear therefore of the wise, apostolic preaching is made firm as a pillar of Christ upon a golden base. Accordingly, to speech and to the apostolic word the tribunal is the fear of the just man, and the golden base full of prudence: but the good image, as a likeness of the truth, is the speech of the saints.

## 75.

And see that the fear of the saints is as it were a golden base. Read Isaiah: see how many things he set fear under, that he might make fear blameless and good: *The Spirit,* he says, *of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and of power, the Spirit of knowledge and of piety, the Spirit of the fear of the Lord [n: (Esai. XI, 2)]*; [var: Vulg. *spiritus consilii et fortitudinis, spiritus scientiae et pietatis*, "the spirit of counsel and of fortitude, the spirit of science and of godliness"] how many things did he set fear under, that it might have what it could follow! It is formed by wisdom, it is instructed by understanding, it is directed by counsel, it is made firm by power, it is ruled by knowledge, [1938B] it is adorned by piety. Take these things away from the fear of the Lord, and it is an irrational and unwise fear, one of those: *Without, fightings; within, fears [n: (II Cor. VII, 5)]*; with which Paul too would have been afflicted, had he not had the Lord consoling him.
