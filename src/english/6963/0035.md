## 11.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And well is it said, *Her nostrils are as the tower of Lebanon;* [var: Vulg. Cant. 7:4 *nasus tuus sicut turris Libani*, "thy nose is as the tower of Lebanon"] because in the sacrifices there is the sweet odor of the Church, in which sacrifices the remission of sins is a victim of good odor. ⟦s2⟧ Take therefore these nostrils, O man, that thou mayest sunder the flowery things from the rank-smelling, and then the Lord will quicken thee. ⟦s3⟧ For when he shall have marked what thou askest of him, that he may turn the eyes of thy mind away from vanity, he works together with thy soul, so that, if it is caught by some appearance, or is not bent either by hardness or by weakness, he may bend it with the yoke of the Word, and govern it with his own reins, that by the will of God it may be drawn away from vices, and may take the odor of everlasting [1949B] life.

## 12.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *[n: (Vers. 8.)] I will go up into the palm tree, and I will lay hold of its height.* [var: Vulg. *Dixi: Ascendam in palmam, et apprehendam fructus ejus*, "I said: I will go up into the palm tree, and will take hold of the fruit thereof"] Whence know thou that he has gone up even to this soul, and has led her through to the place of the palm, when he also says to her: *How beautiful and sweet art thou become, O charity, in thy delights! ⟦s3+4⟧ Thy stature is become like to a palm tree [n: (Cant. VII, 6)]*. [var: Vulg. Cant. 7:6–7 *Quam pulchra es, et quam decora, carissima, in deliciis… Statura tua assimilata est palmae*, "How beautiful art thou, and how comely, my dearest, in delights"] And she herself says: *I said, I will go up to the palm tree.* ⟦s5⟧ But charity itself also is a palm, for it is itself the fulness of victory; for the fulness of the Law is charity. ⟦s6⟧ Let us run therefore that we may lay hold, let us run that we may conquer: he who has conquered goes up into the palm, and eats the fruits thereof; he who has conquered runs no longer, but sits, as *1611* it is written: *He that shall overcome, I will give to him to sit with me in my seat, as I also have overcome, and have sat with my Father in his seat [n: (Apoc. III, 21)]*. ⟦s7⟧ Hence [1949C] the philosophers set forth in their books those race-course contests of souls, nor yet were they able to come through to the palm; because their souls knew not the sweetness and the height of the Word. ⟦s8⟧ *I will go up,* she says, *into the palm* by conquering the world; that I may hold the height of the Word, which this soul knew, in whom was the turning of the Word; for thus she says:

## 13.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *[n: (Vers. 10.)] I am my brother's, and his turning is upon me.* [var: Vulg. *Ego dilecto meo, et ad me conversio ejus*, "I to my beloved, and his turning is toward me"] This sense she has repeated a third time, and diversely, in the Song of Songs. ⟦s3+4⟧ At the beginning she says: *My brother is mine, and I am his: who feeds among the lilies until the day breathe, and the shadows incline [n: (Cant. II, 16)]*. [var: Vulg. Cant. 2:16–17 *Dilectus meus mihi, et ego illi, qui pascitur inter lilia, donec aspiret dies*, "My beloved to me, and I to him, who feedeth among the lilies, till the day break"] Then she says: *I am my brother's, and my brother is mine, who feeds among the lilies [n: (Cant. VI, 2)]*. ⟦s5⟧ At the end she says: *I am my brother's, and his turning is upon me.* ⟦s6⟧ The first belongs to the instruction [1949D] of the soul; and therefore she also set first: *My brother is mine;* for by his showing himself she took also the affection of cleaving to God. ⟦s7⟧ That which follows is according to progress: the third according to perfection? ⟦s8⟧ In the first, as in instruction, the soul still sees shadows, not yet stirred by the revelation of the Word drawing near; and therefore the day of the Gospel did not yet shine upon her: in the second, without the confusion of shadows, she gathers holy odors: in the third, being now perfect, she ministers rest to the Word within herself; that he may be turned upon her, and lay back his head and rest; and she invites him to her field, saying: [1950A]

## 14.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *[n: (Vers. 11.)] Come, my brother, let us go out into the field, let us rest in the strongholds.* [var: Vulg. *Veni, dilecte mi, egrediamur in agrum, commoremur in villis*, "Come, my beloved, let us go forth into the field, let us abide in the villages"] Above she was inviting him to the garden; here to a field having not only the grace of flowers, but also wheat and barley, that is, the firm foundation of solid virtues, that he may look upon its fruit. ⟦s3⟧ *Let us rest,* she says, *in the strongholds,* to which Adam, when he had been cast out of paradise, had been banished: in these he rested, but he tilled the earth.

## 15.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But for what reason she would have him go out into the field, the understanding of it is plain: that as a good shepherd he may feed his flock, may lift up the weary, may call back the wandering. ⟦s2⟧ For although this soul has kept both new things and old, yet there are still, as it were, lambs who must be nourished with the juice of milk. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore, as one perfect, she intercedes not for herself, but [1950B] for others; that he may go out from the bosom of the Father, that he may proceed forth as a bridegroom out of his chamber; that going out he may run his way, that he may win the weak; that he may not linger in that hidden throne of the Father, and in that light, whereto weak horses are not able to climb; but that he may be taken up and brought into the house and the secret place of the Bride. ⟦s4⟧ Let him be without for himself, that he may be within for us: let him be in our midst, even though he be not seen by us. ⟦s5⟧ Whence she says: *Who shall give thee to me, my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother? ⟦s6+7⟧ Finding thee without, I will kiss thee [n: (Cant. VIII, 1)]*. [var: Vulg. *Quis mihi det te fratrem meum sugentem ubera matris meae, ut inveniam te foris, et deosculer te*, "Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother, that I may find thee without, and kiss thee"] Good is the soul which is without, that the Word may be within: she outside the body, that the Word may dwell in us.

## 16.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *I will take thee up,* she says, *and I will bring thee in [n: (Ibid., 2)]*. [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:2 *Apprehendam te, et ducam in domum matris meae*, "I will take hold of thee, and bring thee into my mother's house"] Rightly is the Word of God taken up and brought in; because he knocks at the soul, that the door may be opened to him: and unless [1950C] he shall have found the door opened to him, he does not enter. ⟦s3⟧ But if any man shall have opened the gate, he enters and sups. ⟦s4⟧ Thus the Bride takes up the Word, that by taking him up she may be taught. ⟦s5⟧ Whence not undeservedly he goes up to her, to the higher dwellings, and she ever receives an advance of virtue.

## 17.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Come, my brother, let us go out into the field, let us rest in the strongholds, let us rise at daybreak in the vineyards, let us see whether the vine has flowered.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 7:12 *Mane surgamus ad vineas; videamus si floruit vinea*, "Let us get up early to the vineyards; let us see if the vineyard flourish"] Many fields have fruits; but that one is better which abounds both with fruits and with flowers. ⟦s3⟧ The field of the Church, then, is fruitful with diverse plenties. ⟦s4⟧ Here thou mayest discern the buds of virginity blooming with flower; there, as woods in the plains, widowhood strong in gravity; elsewhere, as the rich produce of the Church, the harvest of wedlock filling the barns of the world, and, as it were with the offspring of a wedded vine, the winepresses of the Lord Jesus overflowing, [1950D] in which the fruit of faithful wedlock abounds.

## 18.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The Bride, having obtained the plenty of her desire, speaks to the Bridegroom: *Come, my brother, let us go out into the field, let us rest in the strongholds, let us rise at daybreak to the vineyards.* ⟦s2⟧ Thou seest how the Bride invites the Word of God, that he may come into the lands and take away the sins of the world. ⟦s3⟧ This field was before a desert, squalid with the briars of our offences, bristling with thorns. ⟦s4⟧ There was a stronghold in which Adam, bound fast, held the heirs of his posterity in perpetual exile. ⟦s5⟧ Thither, then, the Church leads Christ, [1951A] that he may set Adam free. ⟦s6⟧ Then, the exiles being loosed, the field of this world began to have fit tillers: and that which was before hungry was made fruitful by the planting *1612* of the eternal vine. ⟦s7⟧ Nor yet does she so range abroad, glad with spiritual shoots, but she calls Christ to these vineyards, where there are those who sing psalms and those who pray, where by nights and by days a harmless fruit endures.

## 19.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *[n: (Vers. 12.)] There,* she says, *will I give my breasts, there the mandrakes have given their odor.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 7:12–13 *ibi dabo tibi ubera mea. Mandragorae dederunt odorem*, "there will I give thee my breasts. The mandrakes have given a smell"] Very many distinguish a certain sex among mandrakes, so that they think there are both males and females, but that the females are of heavy odor. ⟦s3⟧ It signifies therefore that the nations, which before stank, since they were the weaker by a certain unmanned feebleness of unbelief, began to bear fruits of good odor after they believed unto the coming of the Lord. ⟦s4⟧ We read also that [1951B] holy Rachel received mandrakes from her sister Lia, that she might grant her to sleep that night with holy Jacob. ⟦s5⟧ Ruben the firstborn had brought the mandrakes to his mother Lia, who, with bleared eyes, took on the figure of the Synagogue; because she could not see the grace of Christ, the gaze of her feeble mind being dulled. ⟦s6⟧ By which it is declared that the fruits which the Synagogue had before received from the firstborn Son of God, she yielded up to the Church. ⟦s7⟧ But because, the lying-together of that night having been sought, Lia conceived and bore an heir of her posterity, she fulfilled the mystery; since, when the apostles believed, the remnant of the Jews were made safe through the election of grace, whence the Church says:

## 20.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (Vers. 13.)] New things and old, my brother, have I kept for thee. ⟦s2⟧ Who shall give thee to me for a brother, sucking the breasts of my mother? ⟦s3⟧ Finding thee without, I will kiss thee, and indeed they shall not [1951C] despise me. ⟦s4+5⟧ I will take thee up and bring thee into my mother's house, and into the secret place of her that conceived me: there thou shalt teach me.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 7:13–8:2 *omnia poma nova et vetera, dilecte mi, servavi tibi… et jam me nemo despiciat. Apprehendam te, et ducam in domum matris meae: ibi me docebis*, "all fruits new and old, my beloved, I have kept for thee… and now no man may despise me. I will take hold of thee, and bring thee into my mother's house: there thou shalt teach me"] Having therefore the shaping of the new and the old Scriptures, and not feeling herself contemptible, she holds the Word not only by prayers in the secret of the heart, but also with the voices of a psalm-singing choir she kisses him, as with certain kisses of grace.

## 21.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *New things and old, my brother, have I kept for thee;* this is to say, I hold all the commandments of the New and the Old Testament. ⟦s2⟧ The Church alone can say this: no other congregation says it, the Synagogue does not say it, holding neither the new things according to the letter, nor the old things according to the spirit. ⟦s3⟧ The Manichaean heresy does not say, *The old things have I kept for thee,* seeing that it does not receive the prophets. ⟦s4⟧ Deservedly is she called made white, who shines with the grace of either Testament. [1951D]

## 22.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *I have adjured you, O daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir up and stir up again love, until such time as it shall will.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:4 *Adjuro vos, filiae Jerusalem, ne suscitetis, neque evigilare faciatis dilectam, donec ipsa velit*, "I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, till she please"] Still she seeks one that stirs up, and demands that it be stirred up for her by the daughters of Jerusalem, for whose sake, that is, for the sake of faithful souls, she desires that the Bridegroom be provoked to a more abundant love of her. ⟦s3⟧ And so those powers speak, which guard the gates of heaven.
