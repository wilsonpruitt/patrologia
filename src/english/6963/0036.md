## 1.

*[n: (Vers. 5.)] Who is she who this one comes up white, leaning upon her brother?* [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:5 *Quae est ista quae ascendit de deserto, deliciis affluens, innixa super dilectum suum?*, "Who is this that cometh up from the desert, flowing with delights, leaning upon her beloved?"] In what manner she who has set [1952A] her head upon the left hand of wisdom, that she might open her hand to the poor for the relieving of the needy; that she might use riches divided or left to her, not riches seized and plundered — riches which, desirous of glory, she acquired even by good works, and did not court by the empty boasting of worldly dignity: for this it is to set one's own head, and a certain ruling part of the senses, above the hand of wisdom. That soul, I say, ascends by whitening merits, out of the desert of this life, as very many hold it to be, unto that ever-flowering place of eternal gladness.

## 2.

These are the powers which also in the book of Isaiah say: *Who is this that comes from Edom, the redness of garments out of Bosor, so shapely in his robe [n: (Esai. LXIII, 1)]*? [var: Vulg. *Quis est iste, qui venit de Edom, tinctis vestibus de Bosra? iste formosus in stola sua*, "Who is this that cometh from Edom, with dyed garments from Bosra? this well-formed one in his robe"] These, I say, are they that marvel that out of this broken and rocky desert any soul is able to ascend [1952B] without the ruin of great vices; and therefore they give thanks that she has been found, who has not defiled the garments of natural innocence with the ink of worldly folly, but rather has cleansed them with the whiteness of spiritual wisdom and of grace. The daughters of Jerusalem marvel, the holy souls of the patriarchs and of the prophets and of the just of old, or the heavenly powers, saying: *Who is this that comes up made white, leaning upon her brother?* That is, with such solemnities the Church shines, and she who before was dark through the day now is splendid in the nights, and gives back her light. The Lord himself also, delighted with so great an offering of them that sing psalms, says:

## 3.

*[n: (Vers. 6.)] Set me as a seal upon thy heart, as a signet upon thy arm; because charity is strong as death, hard* *1613* *as hell is thy zeal. [1952C] Its wings are wings of fire, and of flames. Much water shall not be able to shut out charity, and the floods shall not overwhelm it.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:6–7 *quia fortis est ut mors dilectio, dura sicut infernus aemulatio; lampades ejus lampades ignis atque flammarum. Aquae multae non potuerunt exstinguere caritatem, nec flumina obruent illam*, "because love is strong as death, jealousy hard as hell; the lamps thereof are lamps of fire and of flames. Many waters could not quench charity, neither shall the floods drown it"] Because the Church has seen so great a devotion, she thinks the peoples now fit, that we may be able to bear his seal in our heart and on our arm. For him the Father, God, has sealed, and he who has received his testimony has set his seal that God is true; and therefore they that work the food which endures unto life everlasting are sealed unto the image and likeness of Christ, who is the image of the invisible God. As therefore God is true, do thou also seal truth in thy thought and in thy work; that thy mouth speak not a lie, that thy hands work not the works of men, which are of this deceitful world, but those things which are of God: that they may give bountifully to the poor, may raise up the weak, may honor the dead with the mounds of burial. By these works [1952D] charity is sought, that no one may be able to be torn away from Christ even by the peril of death; whence he says: *Who shall separate us from Christ? Tribulation, or distress, or persecution [n: (Rom. VIII, 35)]*? [var: Vulg. *Quis ergo nos separabit a caritate Christi?*, "Who then shall separate us from the charity of Christ?"] And below: *For I am confident that neither death, nor life, nor angels [n: (Ibid.)]*. [var: Vulg. Rom. 8:38 *Certus sum enim*, "For I am sure"]

## 4.

In that same fashion also the zeal of charity is hard, and its wings are wings of fire. It has wings as a dove, for the feathers of the dove are silvered, wherewith he who loves flies forth, saying: *Behold, I have gone far off in flight, and I abode in the wilderness [n: (Psal. LIV, 8)]*. But the wings of charity are wings of fire, wherewith it kindles the ardor of love. [1953A] With this heat the Lord made his angels fervent spirits, and his ministers a fire that burns, but does not burn up. We read also of a wing of the temple, upon which the devil, tempting, made the Savior to go up, which was on the pinnacle of the temple [n: (Matth. IV, 3)]. There are therefore heights of piety, there are pinnacles of charity, which have been wont to kindle the vapor of grace in human breasts; so that much water is not able to quench nor to shut out charity, and no floods of worldly tempests shut it in. We have said that charity shuts out fear; we have not said that it shuts out every disturbance; for indeed to him who loves God there is a deep tranquillity of a mind confirmed.

## 5.

*[n: (Vers. 7.)] Many waters,* he says, *shall not be able [1953B] to shut out charity, and the floods shall not overflow it.* Much water is of diverse passions, and the floods of worldly desires stirred up by the motions of the body, which nevertheless cannot overthrow the wall of charity. And therefore one grounded in charity says: *Our soul has passed through the torrent [n: (Psal. CXXIII, 5)]*. Could the water of the sea shut out the charity of Moses? And that the series of the psalms may support thee according to the letter: assuredly he who loved God believed the way through the seas to be safe for him; but they who loved not God, being sunk in the waves, endured an end worthy of their sacrilege.

## 6.

*[n: (Vers. 5.)] Who is this that comes up, leaning upon her brother?* Above they said, *Who then is this that looks forth as the daybreak, shapely [1953C] as the moon, choice as the sun?* [var: Vulg. Cant. 6:9 *Quae est ista quae progreditur quasi aurora consurgens, pulchra ut luna, electa ut sol?*, "Who is she that cometh forth as the morning rising, beautiful as the moon, bright as the sun?"] Here something more was found to be added, in that she had ascended leaning upon the Word of God; for the more perfect recline upon Christ, as John also was reclining on the breast of Christ. So therefore this one also either was leaning upon Christ, or was laying herself back upon him: or assuredly, since we are speaking of a marriage, being now as it were delivered over into the right hand of Christ, she was being led into the chamber for the Bridegroom. And because there is now a bond of charity, the bridegroom caresses her, and says:

## 7.

*Under the apple tree I raised thee up; there thy mother travailed with thee, there she travailed with thee that bore thee.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:5 *Sub arbore malo suscitavi te; ibi corrupta est mater tua, ibi violata est genitrix tua*, "Under the apple tree I raised thee up: there thy mother was corrupted, there she was defloured that bore thee"] Good is the soul which rests under a fruitful tree, and one especially of good odor. For if Nathanael was good, in whom there was no guile, and was seen under a fig tree, surely good is the soul which under the apple tree was raised up by [1953D] her Bridegroom. For it is more to be raised up than to be seen: and more still to be raised up by the bridegroom. For although Nathanael was seen under the tree, yet his soul was not a bride; because he came secretly to Christ, because he feared the Jews. She was not shapely as the moon, choice as the sun, who was in the shadow; because a bride who weds by day confesses publicly. Therefore this one is under the apple tree, that one under the fig; because this one spread abroad more widely the odor of her confession, while that one had the sweetness of the purity of innocence, and had not the burning of the spirit.

## 8.

*There,* he says, *thy mother travailed with thee, there she travailed with thee that bore thee;* for there we are born, there we are born again. [1954A] But those are travailed with in whom the image of Christ is formed, whence *1614* Paul also says: *Little children, whom I travail with, until Christ be formed in you [n: (Galat. IV, 19)]*. [var: Vulg. *Filioli mei, quos iterum parturio*, "My little children, of whom I am in labour again"] For he has travailed who receives in the womb the spirit of salvation, and pours it into others. Whence, since Christ was now formed in her, he says:

## 9.

*[n: (Vers. 6.)] Set me as a seal in thy heart, as a signet on thy arm.* The seal of Christ is on the forehead, the seal is in the heart: on the forehead, that we may always confess; in the heart, that we may always love; the seal on the arm, that we may always work. Let his image therefore shine in our confession, let it shine in our reading, let it shine in our works and deeds; that, if it can be brought to pass, his whole appearance may be expressed in us. Let him be our head, because the head of the man is Christ; let him be [1954B] our eye, that through him we may see the Father; let him be our voice, whereby we may speak to the Father; let him be the right hand, whereby we may offer our sacrifice to God the Father. He also is our seal, which is the badge of charity and of perfection; because the Father, loving, sealed the Son, as we read: *Whom the Father, God, hath sealed [n: (Joan. VI, 27)]*. [var: Vulg. *Hunc enim Pater signavit Deus*, "For him hath God the Father sealed"] And so Christ is our charity. Good is the charity, when he offered himself to death for those he loved: good is the charity which forgave sin.

## 10.

And therefore let our soul put on charity, and charity of such a kind as is strong as death; because as death is the end of sins, so also is charity; since he who loves the Lord ceases to sin: for *charity thinks no evil, neither rejoices over [1954C] iniquity, but bears all things [n: (I Cor. XIII, 5)]*. For he who seeks not the things that are his own, how shall he seek the things of another? There is also that strong death through the laver, by which every sin is buried and the fault is remitted. Such was the charity which that woman of the Gospel bore, of whom the Lord says: *Her many sins are forgiven, because she has loved much [n: (Luc. VII, 47)]*. [var: Vulg. *Remittuntur ei peccata multa*, "Many sins are forgiven her"] There is also that strong death of the holy martyrs, which does away former guilt; and therefore is it strong, and charity is not unequal to it, charity which is made equal to the passion of the martyrs, that it may take away the desert of offences.
