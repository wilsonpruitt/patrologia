## 11.

Zeal also is as hell, since he who has the zeal of God spares not even his own for Christ. And so charity has death, and charity has zeal, and charity has wings of fire. In fine, Christ, loving [1954D] Moses, appeared to him in fire. And Jeremiah, having in himself the gift of divine charity, said: *And there was a fire flaming in my bones, and I am undone on every side, and I cannot bear it [n: (Jerem. XX, 9)]*. [var: Vulg. *et factus est in corde meo quasi ignis exaestuans, claususque in ossibus meis: et defeci, ferre non sustinens*, "and it became in my heart as a burning fire, shut up in my bones: and I fainted, not able to bear it"] Good therefore is charity, having the wings of burning fire, which flits through the breasts and the hearts of the saints, and burns up whatever is material and earthly; but whatever is unmixed it proves, and what it has touched it makes better with its own fire.

## 12.

This fire the Lord Jesus sent upon the earth, and faith shone out, devotion was kindled, charity was lit up, justice shone again. With this fire he inflamed the hearts of his apostles, as Cleophas testifies, [1955A] saying: *Was not our heart burning within us, when he opened the Scriptures [n: (Luc. XXIV, 32)]*? The wings of fire in the flame, therefore, are the divine Scriptures. In fine, he was opening the Scriptures, and fire was going forth, and was piercing the hearts of them that heard. And they are true wings of fire, because: *The words of the Lord are chaste words, silver tried by the fire [n: (Psalm. XI, 7)]*. When Paul also was taken up by Christ, he saw a light shine round about upon him, and upon those who were with him; he fell from terror, and rose up the more approved. In fine he was made an apostle, who had come as a [sic: *presecutor*]. The Holy Spirit also came down, and filled the whole house in which very many were sitting, and there appeared parted tongues as it were of fire. Good wings, wings of charity, truly wings which flitted through the mouths of the apostles: and wings of fire, because they spoke a purified [1955B] speech.

## 13.

With these wings Enoch flew forth, caught up to heaven; with these wings Elias flew forth, carried over to the things above by a fiery chariot and by fiery horses. With these wings the Lord God led the people of the fathers by a pillar of fire. These wings the Seraphim had, when it took coals of fire from the altar, and touched the mouth of the prophet, and took away his iniquities, and purged his sins. By the fire of these wings the sons of Levi were purged, and the peoples of the nations were baptized, as John testifies, saying of the Lord Jesus: *He shall baptize you in the spirit and in fire [n: (Matth. III, 11)]*. [var: Vulg. *in Spiritu Sancto et igni*, "in the Holy Spirit and fire"] Deservedly did David wish his reins and his heart to be burned, because he knew that the fiery wings of charity are not to be feared. Deservedly did the Hebrew children in the burning furnace not feel the burnings of the fire, because the flame of charity [1955C] cooled them. And that we may know more fully *1615* that perfect charity has wings, thou hast heard him saying a while ago: *How often would I have gathered thy children together, as a hen gathers her chickens under her wings [n: (Matth. XXIII, 27)]*.

## 14.

Let us take therefore these wings, which as flames direct us to the things above. Let every man strip his soul of its filthier wrappings, and, as gold by fire, let him approve it wiped clean of the mire. For thus the soul is purged, as the best gold. But the beauty of the soul is unmixed virtue and comeliness, a truer knowledge of the things above; that it may see that good upon which all things hang, while itself hangs upon none. By it, therefore, it lives, and receives understanding. For that highest good is the fountain of life, the desire of which is kindled within us, to draw near [1955D] to which and to be mingled with it is a delight: which to him who does not see it is a matter of desire, and to him who sees it is life; and therefore he despises all other things, and by this one alone is soothed and delighted. This is that which supplies all substance; but itself, remaining in itself, gives to others, and takes nothing into itself from others; of which the Prophet says: *I said to the Lord: Thou art my God, for thou hast no need of my goods [n: (Psal. XV, 2)]*.

## 15.

*Its wings are wings of fire.* All must be proved by fire, whosoever desire to return to paradise; for not idly is it written that, when [1956A] Adam and Eve had been cast out of the seat of paradise, God set at the outgoing of paradise a fiery sword that turned about. All must pass through the flames, whether it be John the evangelist, whom the Lord so loved that he said of him to Peter: *If I will that he remain, what is that to thee? Follow thou me [n: (Joan. XXI, 22)]*. [var: Vulg. *Sic eum volo manere donec veniam, quid ad te?*, "So I will have him to remain till I come, what is it to thee?"] Some have doubted of his death; of his passing through fire we cannot doubt, because he is in paradise, nor is he separated from Christ. Whether it be Peter, who received the keys of the kingdom of heaven, who walked upon the sea, he must say: *We have passed through fire and water, and thou hast brought us out into a refreshment [n: (Psalm. LXV, 12)]*. But for John the fiery sword will quickly turn aside, because there is not found in him iniquity, whom equity loved. If there was in him anything of human vice, divine charity boiled it away: *For its wings [1956B] are as wings of fire.* He who here shall have had the fire of charity, there will not be able to fear the fire of the sword. To Peter himself, who so often offered his own death for Christ, he says: Pass over, recline. But he says: *He has tried us by fire, as he himself has tried silver.*

## 16.

*Its wings are wings of fire.* Since therefore the power of flying has been given to us, let every man stir up in himself the grace of God, and, forgetting the things behind, seeking after the things before, let him press on toward what is appointed. Far from the honors of the soldiery, far from the heats of the world; lest, as the fables relate, the wax loosed by the heat of the sun, the feather slipping back, fail the [sic: *lcareos*] flights. For although the weightiness of their sayings be against it, yet with poetic salt they meant to declare that flights through heaven are safe for the maturity of the prudent; but that youthful levity, liable to the desires of the world, its feathers flowing back, [1956C] and the framework of its merits loosened through the forgettings of truth, slips back to the earth with the greater ruin.

## 17.

Flight is by no means easy for all; the course of human life is difficult also for the living creatures that are at discord upon the earth. But if the order of our action agree with thee, the prophet will see even in us that one wheel upon the earth, joined to the four living creatures. Ezechiel therefore will see again — for he still sees, and is in vigor, and will be in vigor [n: (Ezech. I, 15 et 16)] — he will see, I say, a wheel in the midst of a wheel, gliding upon the earth without stumbling. For the wheel upon the earth is the life of the body, fitted to the virtue of the soul, and formed with a coherent course to the precept of the Gospel. But the wheel in the midst of the wheel is as it were a life within a life, in that the life of the saints [1956D] is not out of tune with itself; but such as it was in the earlier age, such let it be also in that which follows: or that in this life of the body the practice of eternal life be turned round. When these things shall have agreed together, then let the divine voice sound back, then above the likeness of the throne a likeness as the appearance of a man will appear. This man is the Word, because *the Word was made flesh [n: (Joan. I, 14)]*: this man is the driver of our living creatures, the ruler of our manners, who, according to the measure of our merits, mounts now for the most part a chariot, now a mountain, now a ship.

## 18.

*Set me as a seal in thy heart, as a signet [1957A] on thy arm, thou who hast kept new things and old for me.* Thou art my seal, thou art unto my image and likeness. In thee shines the image of justice, the image of wisdom, the image of virtue. And because the image of God is in thy heart, let it be also in thy works: let the likeness of the Gospel be in thy deeds, that in thy manners thou mayest keep my precepts. The likeness of the Gospel will be in thee, *1616* if to him that strikes thy cheek thou offer the other, if thou love thine enemy, if thou take up thy cross and follow me. Therefore have I carried the cross for thee, lest thou shouldst hesitate to carry it for my sake. The daughters of Jerusalem heard this, that the Lord God was now joining the Church to himself: and because, considering the greatness of the Word, they esteemed themselves unequal to so great a marriage, lest perchance they might not be able to bear the weight of so great a union, they excuse themselves, saying: [1957B]

## 19.

*[n: (Vers. 8.)] Our sister is a little one, and she has no breasts.* [var: Vulg. *Soror nostra parva*, "Our sister is little" — without the diminutive] For thus they who wish to put off a marriage are wont to excuse it: that they allege the weakness of unripe age, and assert that she has no breasts, which signify the time of marriageable age. This token is wont to be common to all virgins about to wed: that when the breasts have begun to stand out, then they are judged fit for union.
