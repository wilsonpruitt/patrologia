## 20.

*What shall we do to our sister, in the day in which he shall speak in her,* or, as Symmachus has it, *in which he shall speak to her?* [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:8 *Quid faciemus sorori nostrae in die quando alloquenda est?*, "What shall we do to our sister in the day when she is to be spoken to?"] That is: in the solemnity of a betrothal a conference is wont to take place, and a confirmation of the marriage. What then shall we do, they say, being troubled, because the spiritual union presses upon us? They cannot excuse themselves from so great a marriage; for there is no one [1957C] who does not think the union either of the soul and the spirit, or of Christ and the Church, a blessed thing. But because the fulness of the Word and of the Holy Spirit quivers and flashes, and there is nothing that can be made equal to them, therefore they desire to defer it, that by that postponement either the soul or the Church may be able to be more perfect. They say therefore:

## 21.

*[n: (Vers. 9.)] If she is a wall, let us build upon her chambers of silver: and if she is a gate, let us carve upon her boards of cedar.* [var: Vulg. *Si murus est, aedificemus super eum propugnacula argentea; si ostium est, compingamus illud tabulis cedrinis*, "If she be a wall, let us build upon it bulwarks of silver: if she be a door, let us join it together with boards of cedar"] The soul of a holy man is a wall. The Church too has her walls, who, being now more perfect, says: *I am a fortified city [n: (Esai. XXVII, 3)]*. [var: Vulg. Isa. 27:3 *Ego Dominus qui servo eam*, "I am the Lord that keep it"; the reading here follows the Greek] This is the wall which has twelve apostolic gates, through which entrance into the Church lies open to the people of the nations. But a wall, although it enclose the circuit of the whole city, is then [1957D] the better fortified when it has had chambers made ready, in which the defenders of the city may have a safe resource for watching and for guarding. But because this city is rational, and all its hope is in the Word of God, not iron but silver bulwarks are required for it, accustomed to beat back the assaults of the enemy by heavenly sayings rather than by bodily pleasures.

## 22.

Propped by that defence, shining with that splendor, she is judged the fitter for the union with Christ. And because Christ is the gate, who says: *If any man enter in by me, he shall be saved (Joan. [1958A] X, 9)*; the church too is called a gate, because through her the approach to salvation lies open to the peoples. But lest she be corrupted by the moths or worms of the heretics, the daughters of Jerusalem, or the angels, or the souls of the just, say: *Let us build upon her boards of cedar,* that is, the good odor of a sublime faith. For the odor of this timber is sweet, and neither worm nor moth corrupts it. Therefore the use of this timber is chosen for raising the peaks of roofs, and for shaping the elements of letters, with which the age of boyhood is steeped in the study of liberal learning. This timber, then, is sublime for grace, light for burden, sweet for odor, useful as an instrument of knowledge, apt for the ministry of eternal knowledge. But even as Christ, loving his Bride, was urging her toward the solemnity of the spiritual union, so also the Church, now taken with the comeliness of the Word, was hastening [1958B] to the marriage. And so, impatient of the delays and of the postponement which the daughters of Jerusalem were eager to weave in, she says:

## 23.

*[n: (Vers. 10.)] I am a wall, and my breasts are towers.* [var: Vulg. *Ego murus, et ubera mea sicut turris*, "I am a wall, and my breasts are as a tower"] That is: do not doubt whether I am a wall; for they had said: *If she is a wall;* I, she says, am a wall, and I have not small breasts, but my breasts are as towers. How do you say that I have no breasts? I have senses as towers of wisdom, in which there is abundance, as it is written: *And abundance in thy towers [n: (Psalm. CXXI, 7)]*. By these breasts, that is, by her senses, she esteemed herself fit for so great a marriage. But the daughters of Jerusalem could not yet so esteem her, because they did not see the abundance of her senses. And she added: [1958C]

## 24.

*[n: (Vers. 11.)] I was in his eyes as one finding peace;* [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:10 *ex quo facta sum coram eo, quasi pacem reperiens*, "since I am become in his presence as one finding peace"] that is: my senses being deliberated upon, since I have found the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, and keeps hearts and senses in Christ Jesus. Such, she says, was I in the eyes of the bridegroom, as one who has peace; for it is written: *They who rightly seek peace shall rejoice.* *1617*

## 25.

*[n: (Vers 12.)] I,* she says, *am a fortified city, I am a besieged city [n: (Esai. XXVII, 3)]*. By these walls she is fortified, by these, when besieged, she is defended. And truly the soul is a wall, which is stretched out before the camp. Whence she herself also says in the Song: *I am a wall, and my breasts are as towers [n: (Vers. 10)]*. A good wall is that which the Lord has painted, as he himself says: *In my hands I have painted thy walls, and thou art always in my sight [n: (Esai. XLIX, 16)]*. [var: Vulg. *Ecce in manibus meis descripsi te; muri tui coram oculis meis semper*, "Behold, I have graven thee in my hands: thy walls are always before my eyes"] Good is the soul which has [1958D] God as its watchman, and is in his hands; as the prophetic soul, which is commended into the hands of God as a spirit [n: (Psal. XXX, 6)], and which is in the sight of God: *For the eyes of the Lord are upon the just;* as he himself also says: *I was in his eyes as one finding peace.* She has good towers, who has both a word concerning things intelligible and a discipline concerning things moral. The beloved and the beloved one therefore hastening, the spiritual union was celebrated, sought by mutual consent. And so, singing as it were a marriage song, the Spirit exulted in the prophet, saying: [1959A]

## 26.

*[n: (Vers. 11.)] A vineyard was made for Solomon in Beelamon; he gave his vineyard to those who keep it.* [var: Vulg. *Vinea fuit pacifico in ea quae habet populos: tradidit eam custodibus*, "The peaceable had a vineyard in that which hath people: he let out the same to keepers"] The Spirit therefore cries out: A congregation of peoples has been planted, but founded upon the root of the eternal vine; and it has subjected spiritual hearts, with a meek heart, under the yoke of the Word. And it was planted in a multitude of nations. For that *Beelamon* is to be understood thus, Symmachus, Aquila, and the other translations have taught in the Greek tongue. The old union, which could not bring forth fruit, was repudiated; the vineyard was given to new and faithful tillers, who could not only make fruit, but also keep it. One sheep therefore went astray, but being called back it filled the spaces of the whole world. Error had led away one sheep, but the grace of the Lord gathered together a multitude of peoples. Man went astray, but in the Church he is now a wall, [1959B] and a strong wall. Adam went astray; David is a wall, who did not forget the commandments of God. This vineyard, therefore, being guarded and walled about with a spiritual fortification, gives a thousand fruits to Christ, but two hundred fruits to the keepers; and so the Church says:

## 27.

*[n: (Vers. 12.)] My vine is in my sight: a thousand for Solomon, and two hundred for those that keep the fruit.* [var: Vulg. *Vinea mea coram me est. Mille tui pacifici, et ducenti his qui custodiunt fructus ejus*, "My vineyard is before me. A thousand are for thee, the peaceable, and two hundred for them that keep the fruit thereof"] The perfection and fulness of Christ is the portion of little ones. Thou hast this mystery in Genesis, where Joseph assigned five portions to Benjamin the younger brother, and single portions to the remaining brethren [n: (Gen. XLIII, 34)]. To the Lord therefore is offered the portion and the prerogative of the five senses, which he himself whom he loves assuredly assigns to him, as he loved Paul also, to whom he gave the chief place of wisdom for the calling forth of the nations. Let fear depart here from the holy virgins, to whom the Church first assigns so great [1959C] defences, she who, anxious for the increase of her tender offspring, herself as a wall grows up with breasts abounding after the manner of towers: until, the assault of the hostile siege being broken up, she may win peace for a strong youth by the defence of a mother's strength. Whence the Prophet also says: *Let peace be in thy strength, and abundance in thy towers [n: (Psalm. CXXI, 7)]*.

## 28.

*[n: (Vers. 13.)] Thou that sittest in the gardens, the friends listening to thy voice, make thy voice known to me.* [var: Vulg. *Quae habitas in hortis, amici auscultant; fac me audire vocem tuam*, "Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the friends hearken; make me hear thy voice" — the Vulgate's addressee is feminine, Migne's masculine] And so this soul, having the grace of her own breasts, enters into the gardens, and finding there the Bridegroom sitting and disputing with his friends, says: *Thou that sittest in the gardens, make thy voice known to me:* to me, she says, not to the friends.

## 29.

*Thou that sittest in the gardens, the friends listening to thy voice, make thy voice known to me.* For she was delighted [1959D] that Christ was sitting in the gardens, and that the friends set in the gardens were listening to his voice: but because those friends were of the heavenly ones, Archangels or Dominations and Thrones (for men had been driven out of paradise on account of disobedience to the heavenly commandments, and therefore the Church could not yet hear his voice, which she desired to hear), therefore she says: *Make thy voice known to me.* Whence we also, if we wish him to sit within us, let us be gardens enclosed and fortified, let us bear the flowers of the virtues, the sweetness of grace, that we may be able to hear the Lord Jesus disputing [1960A] with the angels. But because it was to come to pass that, when the Church had come through to fulness, she would be tried by various persecutions, therefore, while she was being delighted with the grace of the Word, suddenly she discerns the ambushes of the persecutors: and since she feared more for the Bridegroom than for herself, or because Christ is the more sought after by persecutors in us, therefore she says:
