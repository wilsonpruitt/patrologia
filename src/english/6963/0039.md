## 30.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *[n: (Vers. 14.)] Flee, my brother, and be thou like to a little roe or to the fawn of the harts, upon the mountains of spices.* [var: Vulg. Cant. 8:14 *Fuge, dilecte mi, et assimilare capreae hinnuloque cervorum super montes aromatum*, "Flee away, O my beloved, and be like to the roe and to the young hart upon the mountains of spices"] He flees on account of the weak, who could not bear the heavier trials. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore it is written that we should flee from cities to cities: and if they shall have persecuted us in this city, let us flee into another. ⟦s4⟧ On account of the weak, therefore, as we have said, let him flee those who pursue: or let him flee from the lowest things, and pass over to the mountains of spices, [1960B] he who for martyrdom may be able to bring the odor *1618* of the blessed resurrection. ⟦s5⟧ The mountains of spices are the saints. ⟦s6⟧ To them Christ flees for refuge; because *His foundations are in the holy mountains [n: (Psal. LXXXVI, 1)]*. ⟦s7⟧ To them therefore he flees for refuge, who are his stable foundations. ⟦s8⟧ In us he flees; in them he abides in a faithful station. ⟦s9⟧ A mountain of spices, therefore, is Paul, who can say: *We are the good odor of Christ unto God [n: (II Cor. II, 15)]*. ⟦s10⟧ A mountain of spices is David, the odor of whose prayer went up to the Lord, and therefore he said: *Let my prayer be directed as incense in thy sight [n: (Psal. CXL, 2)]*.

## 31.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Symmachus, however, and Aquila have rendered it as that Christ says to the Church: *Thou that sittest in the gardens,* that is, thou already sittest in the gardens, thou art worthy of the paradise above; and therefore *Make thy voice known to me, to which the friends are listening:* [1960C] I too desire to hear it. ⟦s2⟧ The Church began to be in the gardens, after Christ was taken in the gardens.

## 32.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Flee, my brother, and be made like to a hart or to the fawn of a hart, unto the mountains of spices.* ⟦s2⟧ For, hated by the serpent snakes, and fleeing of dogs, and an enemy to the serpents that creep upon the ground, he knows not how to dwell save in the sublimity of virtues, knows not how to abide save with such daughters of the Church as can say: *For we are the good odor of Christ unto God [n: (II Cor. II, 15)]*. ⟦s3⟧ But to some it is the odor of death unto death, in them that perish; to others the odor of life unto life, in those assuredly who with a living faith hope for the odor of the Lord's resurrection. ⟦s4+5⟧ These are the mountains of spices, who received the body of the Lord Jesus, and bound it in linen cloths with spices. [1960D] For whosoever have believed that Jesus died, and was buried, and rose again, these have lifted up the lofty summit of true faith with the peaks of the virtues. ⟦s6⟧ Where then is Christ sought, namely in the breast of a prudent priest?

## 33.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Flee, my brother.* ⟦s2⟧ She exhorts the Bridegroom to flee, because now she herself, earthly as she is, will be able to follow him as he flees. ⟦s3⟧ She says that he should be like a little doe which escapes out of the nets; for she herself also wishes to flee, and to fly out above the world. ⟦s4⟧ Let us flee therefore into the truest fatherland. ⟦s5⟧ There is our fatherland, [1961A] and the Father by whom we were created, where is Jerusalem the city which is the mother of all. ⟦s6⟧ But what is that flight? ⟦s7⟧ Assuredly not of the feet, which are of the body; for these, wherever they run, run upon the earth, and pass from soil to soil. ⟦s8⟧ Nor let us flee by ships or by chariots, or by horses which are bound fast and fall; but let us flee with the mind and with the eyes, or with the inward feet let us accustom our eyes to see the things that are bright and clear, to gaze upon the countenance of continence and of temperance, and upon all the virtues, in which there is nothing rough, nothing dark and crooked: and let a man look upon himself and upon his own conscience, and cleanse that eye, that it may have nothing filthy. ⟦s9⟧ For that which is seen ought not to be out of tune with him who sees; since God willed us to be conformed to the image of his Son. [1961B]

## 34.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ That good, therefore, is known to us, nor is it far from every one of us: *For in him we live, and are present, and are moved; for we are also his offspring [n: (Act. XVII, 28)]*, [var: Vulg. *In ipso enim vivimus, et movemur, et sumus*, "For in him we live, and move, and are"] as the Apostle wished to admonish the Gentiles. ⟦s2⟧ For it is itself the good which we seek, and the only good; for no one is good, but God alone. ⟦s3⟧ This is the eye which beholds that great and true comeliness. ⟦s4⟧ None but a sound and strong eye looks upon the sun, nor can any but a good soul [1962A] see the good: let him therefore become good who wishes to see the Lord, and that which is good. ⟦s5⟧ Let us be like to this good, and according to it let us work the things that are good. ⟦s6⟧ This is the good which is above all working, above all mind and understanding; it is that which always remains, giving to all to live and to be; because Christ is the fountain of the life of all, of whom the prophet says: *In his shadow we shall live.* ⟦s7⟧ For now *our life is hidden in Christ: but when Christ our life shall have appeared, then we also shall appear with him in glory.* [var: Vulg. Col. 3:3–4 *vita vestra est abscondita cum Christo in Deo. Cum Christus apparuerit, vita vestra, tunc et vos apparebitis cum ipso in gloria*, "your life is hid with Christ in God"]

## 35.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Therefore let us not fear death; since it is the rest of the body, but of the soul either liberty or release. ⟦s2⟧ Nor let us dread him who can kill the flesh, but cannot kill the soul; because we do not [1962B] fear him who can take away a garment, we do not fear him who can steal what is ours, but cannot steal us. ⟦s3⟧ Let us therefore be souls, if we wish to be Hebrews of those who are the companions of Jacob, that is, his imitators. ⟦s4⟧ Let us be souls; but our members are garments: garments indeed are to be kept, that they be not torn, that they grow not old: but far more ought he who uses them to keep and guard himself.
