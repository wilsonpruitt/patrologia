## 3.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But if the man given over to religious labor suffers these things frequently and importunately, the idle man assuredly will never be free of them. ⟦s2⟧ Now, since we have set out the members of our pursuits, [0073D] let us heap up the increases of character. ⟦s3⟧ And I do not think it outside our admonition that food should be plain and clothing cheap, since these were devised for the sake of covering the body, not adorning it; and the former for sustaining the soul rather than burying it. ⟦s4⟧ Bound over, therefore, to those acts which we showed above, let a man lay aside all hatred of an enemy. ⟦s5⟧ And not content with this end alone, let him exchange enmities for love, let him temper anger with the bridle of patience, let him conquer avarice with the strong command of abstinence, and above all let him fence in his tongue with the guard of silence: lest it grow hot, contentious, into quarreling; lest it run, insolent, into cursing; lest it creep, slanderous, into disparagement; lest it slip, too easy, into swearing; lest it feign, counterfeit, into lying; lest it grow crafty, cunning, in circumvention; lest [0074A] it be tossed, proud, in self-exaltation; but observing a time for keeping silence and a time for speaking, let him know that every idle word is liable to a reckoning. ⟦s6⟧ For this reason the most blessed David also begs a guard for his mouth. ⟦s7⟧ And the sacred page of blessed Paul appoints that speech be seasoned with salt. ⟦s8⟧ And the precepts of James, being testified, have marked that no member is so untamable as the tongue. ⟦s9⟧ Let him therefore who climbs a way of this kind be silent, mild, kindly. ⟦s10⟧ And since the affection of charity is held to be indissoluble, let him in equal zeal always judge another the better man. ⟦s11⟧ For by this bridle he binds fast envy, which, being rather often stirred up by the judgment of the self-exaltation neighboring upon it, while it seeks that honor be rendered back to itself, runs into the mark of wretched spite. ⟦s12⟧ Nor indeed does a man attain the first place if he requires it of men: *for God resists the proud, [0074B] but gives grace to the humble [n: (I Petr. V, 5)]*. ⟦s13⟧ And he, when he was training his disciples in a zeal of this kind, declares him the greatest who wishes to be the least of all. ⟦s14⟧ Suppose your brother is honored by some more readily: neither hold those men as less your friends, nor regard him as a rival in the veneration preferred to you. ⟦s15⟧ Rather judge that the increase of his honor grows to your own account, knowing it written: *For if one member glories, all the members rejoice together. ⟦s16⟧ And you are the body of Christ, and members [n: (I Cor. XII, 26)]*. ⟦s17⟧ For whoever loves any one of you ascribes every dignity to God, in whom you come together. ⟦s18⟧ In this man mild silence is pleasing; in this man winning affability is loved. ⟦s19⟧ This one his raw and inborn simplicity commends, this one his learned knowledge. ⟦s20⟧ This one's patience in the continuance of fasting, [0074C] this one's contempt in the virtue of abstinence, this one's diligence in the assiduity of reading, is praised according to the diversity of the divine gifts. ⟦s21⟧ And these things, as it is written: *One and the same Spirit works, dividing to each as he wills. ⟦s22⟧ For as the body is one and has many members, and all the members of the body, though they are many, are yet one body, so also is Christ. ⟦s23⟧ For we have all been baptized in one Spirit into one body [n: (Ibid., 11)]*. ⟦s24⟧ Each of you can take beforehand the sum of all good things, if no one begins to exalt himself in that in which another excels. ⟦s25⟧ But when you shall bind one another close in the knot of tight-drawn charity, then do not condemn, by the austerity of a more attentive life, those who are outside and are still bound by worldly chains in secular activity, knowing it written: *I would not have you judge, lest you be judged (Luc. [0074D] VI, 37)*. ⟦s26⟧ And again: *Who art thou that judgest another's servant [n: (Rom. XIV, 4)]*? ⟦s27⟧ To his own Lord he stands or falls. ⟦s28⟧ For God is able to make him stand. ⟦s29⟧ Rather, the way of truth being shown by the exhortations of kindness and by an enticing gentleness, open up the cloud of error, so that each man may begin not to be dragged, but to follow the one going before.

## 4.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Harder things must always be bent gradually, lest the curve, bowed by force, give way and strike a fragment off before it comes round into a circle. ⟦s2⟧ For here rather often the scandals of the common crowd are sharply begotten, while a censor's command is arrogated to the yoke of religion. ⟦s3⟧ Let our life command, let our tongue persuade, because example carries more authority, and the gentle shaping of character brings on a desire of itself. ⟦s4⟧ In every act of yours, therefore, shine as in a mirror [0075A] reflecting the light. ⟦s5⟧ Circumspect everywhere, everywhere careful, that spite find nothing, that false rumor inflict nothing. ⟦s6⟧ Honor upright women, given over to a true zeal for religion, with a measure of veneration, so that there be neither an inhuman austerity nor a slack attentiveness. ⟦s7⟧ Let your going to one another not be thrust upon you, but native — such as a cause has made, not such as idleness has let loose. ⟦s8⟧ Hear several along with several; or if ever a number of the other sex be lacking, let either the tried gravity of someone's years, or the frequented character of the place, or the reckoning of the hour make it seemly, because for you especially the time of life, the hour, and solitude are to be avoided. ⟦s9⟧ Not that it is right for me to judge otherwise concerning those who have truly once dedicated devoted hearts to God, but that out of abundance we should so guard against everything that can be feigned, as though it could also be believed, lest [0075B] a suspicion born from the occasion creep into any wound of our reputation. ⟦s10⟧ Let the very seeds of rumor perish before they are nourished by tongues. ⟦s11⟧ Yet not the credit of the deed only, but even the possibility of a lie must be bound up by us. ⟦s12⟧ For blessed and glorious is that life concerning which nothing is permitted to falsehood. ⟦s13⟧ Nor indeed have I seized upon this judgment of mine as a severe censor, but have taken up a heavenly command as a gentle admonisher: for so also says the Apostle: *Being jealous over you with the jealousy of God, I have espoused you to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ [n: (II Cor. XI, 2)]*, knowing it to be written: *Put not an offense or a stumbling block before a brother [n: (Rom. XIV, 13)]*; and again: *Be without offense to Jews and to Gentiles, and to the Church of God [n: (I Cor. X, 32)]*; and again: *Let not therefore our good be blasphemed [n: (Rom. XIV, 16)]*; and again: *For why is our liberty judged by another's [0075C] conscience [n: (I Cor. X, 29)]*; and again: *But let all your things be done honorably [n: (I Cor. XIV, 40)]*; and again: *Commending ourselves to every conscience of men before God [n: (II Cor. IV, 2)]*; and again: *Let us provide good things not only before God, but also before men [n: (Rom. XII, 17)]*; and again: *That he who is on the opposing side may be confounded, having nothing evil to say [n: (Tit. II, 8)]*. ⟦s14⟧ Nor does it escape me that the envy of worldly spite always burns into the disparagement of religion, and fixes the biting tooth of sharp speech, pressed in by malignant rumors. ⟦s15⟧ Let the world use its own nature, so long as it find nothing in our discipline. ⟦s16⟧ Let falsehood be flung about after its custom, so long as what is said cannot even be plausible. ⟦s17⟧ No one on that account ought to despise the wound of ill fame, because rumor has always been accustomed to counterfeit; but so much the more attentively [0075D] should he take precaution, because it is wont even to compose fictions. ⟦s18⟧ No one is rendered secure from the fear of disparagement by the commonness of disparaging. ⟦s19⟧ Nor is a continued matter of solicitude a cause for negligence. ⟦s20⟧ Let there be, therefore, a safe and circumspect guard of your modesty and reverence. ⟦s21⟧ Let your going out from the house be necessary and rare — such as either the reckoning of religion has called forth, or the cause of practicing your work for the substance of your food has arranged. ⟦s22⟧ Abstaining from all gifts, except those which suffice for daily food and yearly clothing. ⟦s23⟧ Knowing it to be written: *But having food and wherewith to be covered, with these we are content [n: (I Tim. VI, 8)]*; and again: *That you may walk honorably toward those who are outside, and may desire nothing of any man [n: (I Thess. IV, 12)]*. ⟦s24⟧ Nor indeed, for the sake of giving alms, may you take upon yourselves anything from anyone beyond the aforesaid [0076A] sustenance, knowing it written: For if the will is ready, it is accepted according to that which it has, not according to that which it has not. ⟦s25⟧ Thus that divine sentence of the Gospel's discourse enriched with a magnificent gift the poor old woman who cast in only two coins among the lavish offerings of the wealthy. ⟦s26⟧ Because that cannot be little which is the whole. ⟦s27⟧ Nor has anyone given more than he who has reserved nothing for himself.
