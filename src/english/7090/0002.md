## 1.

Although, dearest brother, by reason of the merit and honour of the priesthood in which thou art most powerful, all things of living and teaching [nt: One Colb. codex and another Fossat., with the Roman edition and Quesnel, as in Crabbe's margin: *secundum ecclesiasticas regulas*, against more than twenty other manuscripts. It will be for thy love diligently to bring this book of rules, as a teacher and a monitor, to the notice of the neighbouring peoples and of our fellow priests who in those [0470A] regions preside over their own churches; so that they may be able both to know our ways, and by diligence in teaching to form the conduct of those who flock to the faith. For either they will recognize their own practice from this our consonant reading, or, if anything is still wanting, they will easily be able to supply it by good imitation.

## CHAP. I.



## 3.

First, that no one dare to ordain without the knowledge of the metropolitan bishop [nt: In the Roman edition, and also in the Councils and Quesnel, following the manuscripts of the Spanish and Isidorian collections, *episcopum* is added here, a word which is absent from the Dionysian and Hadrianic collections and other older ones, as also in Siricius. We have already observed that Innocent so borrows the words of Siricius as to adapt them to the discipline to be kept in the Gauls; nor does he likewise enjoin on the Gauls what the former had prescribed for the Italians, *that no one dare to ordain without the knowledge of the apostolic see*.] [n: (Dist. LXIV, c. 5)]; for that judgment is sound which is confirmed by the opinions of many: [nt: Sirmond, Quesnel and some manuscripts: *Ne unus*. Then other editions also: *episcopus ordinare praesumat episcopum*. This last word is absent from most and from the better manuscripts. But here, as just before, it is understood. For this too was established and defined in the synod of Nicaea.

## CHAP. II.



## 4.

Likewise [n: (Dist. LVI, c. 61)], if anyone after the remission of sins [nt: In the Colb. codex written in Lombardic letters there is written in the margin, opposite this decree, *De poenitentibus*. He who set it there, thinking that the discourse here is not of the remission of sins which is received through baptism but of that which is granted when penance has been completed, interpreted this decree as concerning not the baptized but those who have done penance. That it is to be understood otherwise, however, was observed above on Epistle 5 of Siricius, p. 654, note d (in our *Patrologia*, tome XIII, col. 1158, note d). More rightly, therefore, in the Spanish collection this decree is rendered in summary thus: *On those who after baptism have taken up the belt of secular military service.*] has had the belt of secular military service, he ought by no means to be admitted to the clergy.

## CHAP. III.



## 5.

But if any causes or contentions have arisen among clerics [nt: The old editions of the Councils, and only the manuscripts of the Spanish and Isidorian collections, add here *vel inter laicos*. And indeed Pierre de Marca, book I *De Concordia*, ch. 6, n. 10, thinks that this fifth canon and the sixth are explained throughout this passage. Hincmar, Opuscule 47, or the letter to John VIII, ch. 12, p. 773, so supports him as to judge that by this exception, *sine praejudicio tamen Rom. ecclesiae*, etc., the seventh decree of the Council of Sardica is recalled. Paschasius Quesnel, however, in his notes on Leo's Epistle 5, n. 4, judges that the canons of the Council of Sardica alone are regarded here. Yet in those canons nothing is laid down about the causes of clerics of the lower order, except in can. 16, where they are permitted *to appeal to the neighbouring bishops*. Nor is it obscure that the canon recalled here by Innocent is the one which the Africans, to whom the synod of Sardica was unknown, note in their Epistle 3 to Celestine in these words: *The decrees of Nicaea have most plainly committed both the clerics of lower rank and the bishops themselves to their metropolitans.* Wherefore of those three opinions the truer seems the one which Hincmar sets out in the place cited, inasmuch as he teaches that Innocent, *in saying that, according to the synod of Nicaea, judgment on causes that have arisen is to be ended by the bishops of the same province gathered together, explains a chapter* (5) *of the Council of Nicaea: and in forbidding anyone of the ecclesiastical order to fly off to other provinces, explains another chapter* (namely 6) *of the Council of Nicaea, which commands that in the several provinces their privileges be preserved to the Churches.* Certainly those words, *secundum Nicaenam synodum*, which are omitted in the Colb. The same learned man tries to persuade, Dissertation 12, ch. 4, n. 5, that they were added by the editors of the Roman Codex (that is, of the one which he published). But two exemplars of the same Codex, which he calls the Roman, older than the Oxford one, namely the Pithou and the Colbert, in which they are not found, ought to have prevented him from persuading himself of this. They are found, however, in the Colb. Langob. manuscript and in another Colbert, no. 3368, containing an ancient collection of canons, and also in the manuscripts of the Spanish and Isidorian collections, as also in Hincmar, Opuscule 45, ch. 12, and in the book of 55 chapters, ch. 28. And if anyone should perchance presume to do so, let him both be removed from the office of the clergy and be judged by all guilty of wrongs.

## 6.

If greater causes have been brought into the midst, let them be referred to the apostolic see, [nt: So the Roman edition and the old editions of the Councils, with the Oxon. manuscript and others of the Spanish and Isidorian collections, and also Hincmar, Opuscule 45, ch. 12. But in the Dionysian codices and in almost all our other manuscripts, which Sirmond, Labbe and Quesnel followed, *beata consuetudo exigit* is lacking. On the other hand, in the Colb., Langob. manuscript and in another, no. 3368, is found *sicut vetus consuetudo exigit*, and the preceding words, *synodus statuit*, etc., are omitted. This reading is favoured by what Innocent writes, whether in Epistle 29, to the Council of Carthage, n. 1: *Keeping the examples of ancient tradition, and mindful of ecclesiastical discipline, you have strengthened with true reason the vigour of our religion no less now in consulting than before when you pronounced, you who have shown your approval that the judgment should be referred to us*; or in Epistle 30, to the Council of Milevis, n. 2: *Diligently and fittingly do you consult the secret counsels of the apostolic honour . . . concerning anxious matters, what judgment is to be held, following, that is, the ancient rule, which you know has always been kept with me throughout the whole world.* The same is supported both by Cyril to Celestine, Epistle 8, n. 1, speaking thus: *The old custom of the Churches urges that matters of this kind be communicated to thy holiness*; and by Leo, Epistle 5, n. 5, where he asks that the greater causes be referred to him *for the tradition of the ancient institution and the reverence owed to the apostolic see*. Isidore Mercator, in the letter which he forged in the name of Pelagius, copying out this passage of Innocent, alters it thus: *But let the greater and difficult questions, as the holy synod has laid down and blessed custom requires, always be referred to the apostolic see.* Further, by the words *ut sancta synodus statuit* we judge that there are denoted, not the canons 3, 4 or 7 of the Council of Sardica, by which it is permitted to appeal to the Roman bishop, but the letter of the same synod to Julius, in which the synod declares *that it is best and exceedingly most fitting, if the priests of the Lord refer to the see of the apostle Peter from each and every province*. Nor indeed does Innocent in this place reserve to himself the judgment of the greater causes; rather he requires that a report of those causes, judged by an episcopal council, be sent according to custom to the apostolic see. Wherefore that interpretation of Hincmar, tome II, p. 774, does not please us: *that if greater causes, one of which is concerning the appeal of a bishop who has been judged, have been brought into the midst, they be referred to the apostolic see after the episcopal judgment, as the synod, namely that of Sardica, laid down, and old custom requires.* For there it is not a question of the appeal of a bishop who has been judged; but only this is commanded, that what the bishops have judged concerning graver matters be referred to the apostolic see. On this matter, recall what we have set out more fully in the general preface.] as the synod laid down and blessed custom requires, after the episcopal judgment.

## 7.

That a cleric do [nt: The Roman edition, following Dionysius: *ut viduam*. ## CHAP. V.



## 8.

That he who [nt: So Sirmond and Labbe, with the better manuscripts. But others, with the Dionysian, Spanish and Isidorian collections: *viduam*, which word Siricius also uses here.] has taken a woman to wife, though a layman, [0474A] whether before baptism or after baptism, be not admitted to the clergy [n: (Dist. 34, c. 13)]; because he is seen to be excluded by the same fault. For in baptism crimes are remitted; the partnership of a wife taken is not dissolved.

## CHAP. VI.



## 9.

That he who has married a second wife be not made a cleric: because it is written [n: (I Tim. III, 2)], *The husband of one wife.* [nt: In the Langob. manuscript and another Colb.: *Unius uxoris virum. Ac ne ab aliquibus*, what lies between being omitted. A recent controversy on that matter required it. Carterius, namely, a bishop of Spain, *against whom*, says Jerome, Epistle 83, to Oceanus, near the end, *nothing else could be objected by his rivals beyond the conjugal bond, and that before baptism*, had, his first wife being dead, married another after the laver. Oceanus judged that he had been ordained against the precept of the Apostle. Jerome, on the contrary, keenly defends his ordination as lawful in the aforesaid Epistle 83, and that the whole world is full of ordinations of this kind. Certainly Rufinus, while Siricius was still living, cast this opinion in his teeth. Jerome, answering him at the end of book I of the *Apology*, when Siricius had not yet been taken from among the living, so tempers his earlier opinion as no longer to say that the world is full of ordinations of this kind, but that there are some priests of this sort in the Church; and he testifies that he is not bound by this question, but permits each to abound in his own sense. *Questioned by the brethren*, he says, *we answered what seemed good to us, prejudging no one to follow what he wishes, nor overturning another's decree by our opinion.* The first Council of Valence, held in the year 374, can. 1, had already defined *that after this synod, by which help is brought to such unlawful things even though late, no one of the digamists or of the husbands of women married a second time can be ordained a cleric. I, 28)]; and Solomon says [n: (Prov. XIX, 14)], *By God a wife shall be prepared for a man.* That all priests also keep this form, the very practice of the Church shows. For it is quite absurd for anyone to believe that a wife taken before baptism is not counted after baptism; since the blessing which is laid by the priest upon those who marry is taught not to have given matter for sinning, but to have kept the form of the law instituted by God of old. But if [n: (Dist. 34, c. 13)] she is not thought to be counted a wife who was married before baptism, then [0475B] neither will the children who were begotten before baptism be held [nt: So the Royal exemplar of Dionysius. But the old Corb. manuscript with Fossat. and two Colb.: *haberentur*. Quesnel: *habendi sunt*. The rest, printed and written: *et habentur*.] for children.

## CHAP. VII.
