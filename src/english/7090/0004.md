## 2.

*That it is inscribed to the bishops of the synod of Toledo, not of Toulouse.* --In both kinds of exemplars a false inscription is prefixed to it. And in the preliminary index of the Corbie codex, indeed, it is given this title: *Epistola Innocentii papae ad universos episcopos ecclesiae Tolosanae de universis herisebus* [n: (leg. haeresibus)] *vel de ordinationibus episcoporum, et caetera in epistola requirenda:* [0482B] but at the head of the letter itself this superscription is set before it: *Innocentius episcopis universis in Tolosana synodo constitutis.* The same is read also in the one of Saint-Maur-des-Fossés, except that in this one *diversis* has been substituted for *universis*. In most exemplars too, of the Spanish as well as of the Isidorian collection, it is likewise inscribed *universis episcopis in Tolosana synodo constitutis*. Nor did Merlin change this inscription. Indeed Jacques Sirmond also retained the same, and on that account gave this letter a place among the very councils of Gaul. « On which matter, he says in the preliminary *Monitum*, although I understood that it could with good reason be doubted, yet it seemed better that it should stand here entire, even in another's place, especially since it had hitherto been mutilated, than perhaps be missed in its own. » Afterwards, however, he added in the notes that it did not seem doubtful to him « that it was written to the bishops of Spain after the synod of Toledo indeed, but concerning the things done in it, or that followed afterwards. Wherefore, he says, I judge that the ancient inscription of the letter should be restored, [0482C] so that *in Toletana synodo* is read. » Further, he calls it *ancient* because, having already been restored by Crabbe, it was retained by those who reprinted either the councils or the letters of the Roman pontiffs. Before that too it prevailed at least in some manuscripts. We ourselves, among the various codices of Isidore's collection, have come upon one of Navarre written at least 600 years ago, and another of the monastery of St Martin of Champs, which show *in Toletana synodo*. Nor is the soundness of this reading to be doubted, as what we are about to subjoin will confirm. And whence the other crept in may be conjectured from various manuscripts compared with one another.

## 3.

*How it came about that it was inscribed* TO THE SYNOD OF TOULOUSE. --Although in the Noyon exemplar this letter is inscribed *universis episcopis in Tolosana civitate constitutis*, yet in the general index of the same it is headed *ad universos Tolotanos episcopos*. That *Tolotanos* was written there for *Toletanos*, not for *Tolosanos*, is proved by the codex of Saint-Germain, no. 424, [0483A] containing a collection of canons put together 800 years ago, in which, as often as any synod of Toledo is cited, it is constantly called *Tolotana*. Since, therefore, here too the scribes had written *Tolotana* for *Toletana*, the copyists judged the more readily that *Tolosana* ought to be restored, because they both saw a letter of Innocent *ad Exsuperium Tolosanum*, and in the text of this letter, n. 3, read corruptly *Galliarum* or *Galliorum*, in place of *Galliciorum* or *Gallaecorum*. Moreover, since below, n. 6, where from the testimony of the Colbert exemplar we read that Rufinus *in Toletano concilio erroris tui veniam postulasse*, the Corbie one shows *Tolosano* for *Toletano*, no one will fail to agree that the same fault could have crept into the inscription with equal ease.

## 4.

*That it was written to the fathers of the First Council of Toledo.* --What that synod is to which Innocent now writes, and when it was held, is disputed. Baronius, in the earlier edition of the *Annals*, had laid down two things as certain: first, that this letter of Innocent was written to the First Council of Toledo; the other, that this council [0483B] was celebrated in the last consulship of Stilicho, that is, in the year of Christ 405. But in the latest edition he changed his opinion, and, persuaded that the First Council of Toledo is to be dated to the first consulship of Stilicho, and thus to the year of Christ 400, he thought that this letter pertains to some later synod of Toledo. To this opinion many subscribe, and by name Antoine Pagi. And the former opinion, by which the First Council of Toledo was put off to the second consulship of Stilicho, can by no means be defended. But neither does there seem to be any reason why we should distinguish the council, to whose bishops Innocent now writes, from the First of Toledo. That this may become more evident, it will be worth the labour to weigh what has been published under the name of the First Council of Toledo.

## 5.

*The three parts [sic: *patres*] of the First Council of Toledo as published.* --In this council three parts are to be distinguished. The first consists of the preface, 20 canons, and the subscriptions of the bishops. The second contains the rule of faith and [0483C] eighteen chapters against the heresies, with this inscription: *Here begins the rule of the catholic faith against all heresies, and most especially against the Priscillians, which the bishops of Tarragona, Carthagena, Lusitania and Baetica made, and with the precept of Leo, pope of the city of Rome, transmitted to Balconius, bishop of Gallicia.* The last is marked with this title: *Here begin the exemplars of the professions in the council of Toledo against the sect of Priscillian, era* 438. Then follows the preface: « After the council was held on the Kalends of September, on the third day before the Nones of September, after various hearings then held, on the eighth day before the Ides of September there were excerpted from the full acts the professions of the lord Symphosius and the lord Dictinius, bishops of holy memory, and of the lord Comasius of holy memory, then a presbyter, which among the rest they made in the council of Toledo concerning the condemnation of Priscillian or of his sect, in this manner: After some things, and among some things done at the same time, Bishop Dictinius said, » etc., that is, the rest [0483D] of Dictinius's profession, after which, set down, is subjoined the profession of Comasius, with this preface or interlocution placed first: « In the era as above, on the third day before the Ides of September, the professions of the lord Simphosius and the lord Dictinius, bishops of holy memory, and of Comasius of holy memory, then a presbyter. Comasius said. » Finally this profession of Comasius is followed by « the exemplar of the definitive sentence, transferred from the acts. On the day as above the bishops said: Let the sentence be read. And he read, » etc.

## 6.

*They present a council celebrated in the year 447.* --But how those three parts cohere among themselves, or can be referred to one and the same council, no one, as far as I know, has hitherto explained. Only this is agreed among the learned, that the middle part belongs to that council which Leo, writing to Turibius in the year 447, commanded to be gathered; and thus that it is foreign to the First Council of Toledo. Hence, after Baronius, Binius notes that it was inserted among the other two *by the error of the collector*. But neither can the last [0484A] be more fittingly referred to the First Council of Toledo. For the bishops who speak in it indicate that they are holding a synod when Symphosius, Dictinius and Comasius were already dead, who, it is agreed, were still living at the time of the First Council of Toledo, and indeed, present at that very council, condemned the heresy of Priscillian. But those three parts will cohere with one another most aptly if they are referred to the general council of the Spains which was gathered by order of Pope Leo: so that in it first the 20 canons of the First Council of Toledo were read out and confirmed, then the rule of faith was composed, 18 new canons were added, and lastly some excerpts from the full acts of the First Synod of Toledo were recited. What therefore has been published as the First Council of Toledo would more aptly be inscribed: *The council of the Spains which the bishops of Tarragona, Carthagena, Lusitania and Baetica celebrated by the precept of Leo, in the year 447, on the Kalends of September: to which are prefixed the 20 canons of the First Council of Toledo, celebrated in the year 400, and certain excerpts of the same council, copied from [0484B] its full acts, are subjoined.*
