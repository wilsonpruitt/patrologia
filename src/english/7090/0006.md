## 3.

*That some bear with an unjust mind the honours kept for them.* --For I ask why they should have grieved that Symphosius and Dictinius, and the others who condemned the detestable heresy, were then received into the catholic faith? Is it [nt: Sirmond, with the Corc. and Colb. manuscripts: *Numquidnam aliquid*. The codex of Bonneval: *Num quod non aliquid*. By recalling *num* from the first two and *quod non* from the last, the reading is now had entire and clear. The very examples by which Innocent now proves that clerics may lawfully have their honours kept for them after penance, Augustine too employs for the same cause, Epistle 185, n. 45: *For holy David also*, he says, *did penance for deadly crimes, and yet he remained in his honour: and blessed Peter, when he shed most bitter tears, certainly repented of having denied the Lord, and yet he remained an apostle.*] that they did not lose anything of the honours which they held? But if this pricks or goads any, let them read that Peter the apostle was, after his tears, what he had been; let them consider that Thomas, after that doubting of his, lost nothing of his former merits; and finally that David, the eminent prophet, after his open confession, was not deprived of the merits of his prophecy. But if the amendment of conversion both cuts off the errors themselves and retains the dignities; what evil reason [0488B] is it, out of a purpose of obstinacy, to be unwilling to hold to the way of right and the road which leads to salvation?

## 4.

*The force of unity, and the ruin of schism.* --Wherefore your charity must apply itself, and good priests [0489A] must strive, that, with teaching going before [nt: By which, namely, both the perversity of schism or heresy and the rightness of the catholic faith may be proved, *so that the light of truth may drive out the darkness of error*, says Augustine, Epistle 93, n. 5, *and the Church may be seen to receive men corrected rather than perverse*.], all who have been scattered may be gathered into the unity of the catholic faith; and there may begin to be one impregnable body, which, if it be separated into parts, will lie open to every injury of laceration, and will suffer an internal plague from itself, when the frame itself fights against itself. But let all the priests receive these things, written in general concerning the restoration of unity, as though written to each. Thereafter let your charity turn its mind in detail to all the things which shall be proposed.

## CHAP. II.



## 5.

*Rufinus and Minicius usurped ordinations in other men's churches.* --For it could not lie hidden that Rufinus and Minicius, bishops, usurped the ordaining of bishops in other men's churches, against the Nicene canons. That [n: (Nicaen. can. 4, 6 et 16)] no one [0489B] may dare to claim this for himself, provision must now at least be made by us wholesomely: lest the dissimulation by improper [nt: It would be said more clearly: *ne improba usurpatio dissimulatione*.] usurpation grow stronger for the worse, and a rule be made of custom which does not come from the very discipline that has been committed to writing. In which matter let the complaint of Hilary, our brother and fellow priest, be heard first, who asserted that Rufinus had been engaged with every kind of assault against the peace of the churches, and had some while ago asked pardon for his error in the council [nt: So the Colb. manuscript, where the Corb. has *Tholosano*. Here it must be recalled that the acts of the synod of Toledo have by no means come down to us full and entire, but only fragments of them, recited in a later council as then suited the purpose; and that nothing was recited in that council except what concerned the Priscillianists. Rufinus, however, was rather one of those bishops of whom Patruinus complains in the preface of the first council of Toledo, namely those who, having undertaken illegitimate ordinations, stirred up such scandals as went as far as schism, not one of those who had been devoted to Priscillian. The same is to be thought of Minicius also. Whence also that part of the acts of the council of Toledo which concerned them was passed over in the aforesaid council.] of Toledo, and now, although the prerogative of ordaining [nt: Sirmond and Labbe: *sacerdotis*. More correctly the manuscripts: *sacerdotes*. The council of Laodicea, can. 12, the Nicene, can. 4 and 6, and that of Antioch, can. 19, forbid the bishops of each province to be ordained without the consent of the metropolitan. See also Ferrandus, *Breviatio canonum*, c. 4. And the creation of a bishop in out-of-the-way places is contrary to canon 6 of the council of Sardica.] priests was owed to the metropolitan bishop, had ordained a bishop in out-of-the-way places, against the will of the people and the reason of discipline, and had embroiled the churches in scandals. Thereafter the cause of the bishops of Tarragona must be treated, who have complained that Minicius in like manner [0489C] ordained a bishop in the church of Gerona; and sentence must be passed on such usurpation according to the Nicene [nt: That he who has been so ordained ought not to be a bishop, the Nicene synod declares in can. 6. And the council of Antioch, confirming this in can. 13, further decrees the penalty of deposition against the authors of the usurped ordination.] canons. Let there be a full examination also of those bishops who were ordained by Rufinus or by Minicius [0490A] against the rules: so that, since they were made wrongly, they may understand that they cannot any longer hold what they obtained from a faulty beginning.

## CHAP. III.



## 6.

*That John be cut off, if he does not cease to resist the decree on receiving those who return to the Church, to which he had subscribed.* --Concerning JOHN also, the bishop, whose consent was given through legates in the synod of Toledo to the reception of SYMPHOSIUS and DICTINIUS, and to whom that correction seemed approvable, let the things which followed afterwards be examined. And, above all, concerning all those whose communion has come into doubt on account of withdrawal, let a full inquiry be made, so that, according to the decree of the synod of Toledo, they may either be joined in the fellowship of communion for the abolishing of the suspicion of schism, or, [0490B] if any be found who [nt: The Corb. manuscript: *qui abnegant*.] refuse concord and the constitutions of what was agreed, they may be cut off from the communion of the catholic faith by the sentences of your charity: so that what is shunned may now be, not an internal evil, which does not cease [nt: In Labbe and Sirmond: *non desinit serpere*, where the manuscripts have *non desinit semper*, perhaps for *non se sinit semper*. For Innocent, unwilling that anyone be shunned on any suspicion of internal evil, but only where his profession of it is manifest, gives an apt reason: that internal evil does not always allow itself to be kept silent or held within, but is wont at length to break out and betray itself by words or deeds. And in this way he guards against a grave harm into which the Ithacian schismatics had fallen, or those who at their urging had been sent into the Spains by the emperor Maximus to search out the Priscillianists, of whom Sulpicius committed this to writing: *Nor was there any doubt that that storm would have laid waste the greatest part of the saints, with little distinction between the kinds of men. For then judgment was made by the eyes alone, when a man was reckoned a heretic by his pallor or his dress rather than by his faith.*] always silent, but the manifest profession and contagion of the schismatics.

## CHAP. IV.



## 7.

*Certain ordinations against the canons to be tolerated for the sake of peace. But that henceforth they be void, and their authors to be deposed.* --For concerning the ordinations which we learn that the bishops of Spain celebrate through the fault of a depraved custom, something was to have been decreed according to the tradition of the elders, had we not weighed it, lest we should stir up very many disturbances [nt: So the Corb. manuscript, with which the Colb., retaining *quam plurimas*, also adds *ecclesias*. But Sirmond substitutes *quam plurimis ecclesiis*. Augustine, animated by the same Spirit as Innocent, writes in Book III against the Letter of Parmenianus, n. 14: *When the same disease has seized very many, nothing else remains for the good but grief and groaning.* *For then*, he says a little after, *counsels of separation are both vain and pernicious and sacrilegious; because they become both impious and proud, and they disturb the weak good more than they correct the bold bad.* Wherefore both thereafter in n. 16, and in the epistle formerly 64, now 22, n. 5, he explains how circumspectly a multitude is to be rebuked. Which, although said properly of the multitude of the people, is aptly accommodated to every multitude of offenders.] in the churches. Whose deed we so reprehend that, on account of the number of those to be corrected, we do not call into doubt the things which have been done in whatever [0490C] manner, but rather leave them to the judgment of God. For how many of those who, after receiving [nt: The word *baptismi*, which is added here in Sirmond and Labbe, stands indeed in the curtailed exemplars of this letter, but in the entire ones it is passed over in silence. As in the preceding letter, n. 5, *after the remission of sins*, so now *after receiving grace* means the same as *after receiving baptism*. Leo likewise designates baptism by the name of grace, Epistle 18, n. 4, where he writes: *The very quality of the work therefore teaches that the lawful day for celebrating grace generally* (in place of *celebrating baptism*) *is that on which Christ, having been buried, rose again.* Again, below, Innocent in n. 11, as also Cyprian, Epistle 76, to Magnus, and Damasus, Epistle 9, n. 1, call baptism *the grace of God*. It is familiar to others, and indeed to Innocent himself, to call the same sacrament *the grace of Christ* and *the sacrament of Christian grace*.] grace, were engaged in the exercise of the courts [0491A], and took up an obstinacy in winning [nt: Here advocates of causes seem to be noted, who, although they sometimes take up unjust causes, strive nevertheless to win them by the force of eloquence and obstinate zeal.], we find to have been admitted to the priesthood! of whose number Rufinus and Gregory are said to be; how many from some military service, who, while they obeyed the powers, [nt: In some exemplars *severa* is added here; it is absent from the purer ones. For the same reason it is now said *praecepta sunt exsecuti*, as presently *quae sibi sunt imperata fecerunt*. In either place it is signified that those bound to military service and to the curia are wont to be compelled to do things which keep them from the clergy. In Gratian, dist. 51, c. 4, canon 8 of the first council of Toledo is exhibited, interpolated thus: *If anyone after baptism has served as a soldier, and has taken up the cloak or the belt for killing the faithful* (the last three words do not stand in the codices of the council of Toledo), *even if he has not committed graver things, if he has been admitted to the clergy, let him not receive the dignity of deacon.* And the same prejudiced opinion which gave occasion to the interpolation of this canon seems also to have been the cause of the word *severa* being added here.] of necessity carried out commands! how many from the curials, who, while they obey the powers, did what was commanded them! how many who celebrated pleasures and shows [sic: *editiotiones*] [nt: From the preceding letter, n. 15, supply *ludorum vel munerum*.] for the people, have come to the honour of the highest priesthood! of all whom it had behoved none to come even to the fellowship of the order of clerics. If we should order these to be examined one by one, we shall bring upon the provinces of Spain, which we desire to heal, no small commotions and scandals out of zeal for amendment. Therefore we think these things should rather be remitted. But lest like things be committed henceforth, [0491B] the maturity of your charity will have to provide that a necessary end be put at least now to so great a usurpation: this, namely, being established, [nt: In the exemplars of the Isidorian collection there is added here *ut quicumque tales ordinati fuerint, cum ordinatoribus suis deponantur*: which is a mere marginal scholion, or a summary heading of Innocent explaining the text. In the same books, presently, *ordinatoribus* has been substituted for *creatoribus*.] that if any after this shall attempt to come, against the forms of the canons, either to the ecclesiastical order or to the priesthood itself, they be deprived, together with their creators, of the very order and honour in which they have been found.

## CHAP. V.



## 8.

*That the complaint of Gregory of Merida be heard.* --Of GREGORY also, bishop of Merida, who was ordained in the place of PATRUINUS [nt: Those who judged that Patruinus, whose name is read in the preface of the first council of Toledo, and who subscribed first to it, was bishop of Toledo, are corrected at this place. Gregory, for his part, seems to have had as successor Antoninus, who, as Idacius relates in his Chronicle, olymp. 306, n. 21, ruled the church of Merida at the time when Turibius ruled that of Astorga, that is, in the age of Leo.] of venerable memory, let the complaint, if there be any, be heard: and if he has suffered injury against his desert, let him be avenged upon those envious of another's honour: and henceforth let not the spirit of faction rise up against any one of the good.

## CHAP. VI.



## 9.

*That the Nicene rules be kept in ordinations [0492A]. Who are to be kept from orders.* --And although the Nicene rules are known to your charity, dearest brethren, according to which you decree by sentence [nt: Namely, as we observed after Sirmond in the Notice, with Patruinus speaking first, in the preface of the council of Toledo itself.] that ordinations are to be made; yet I have thought fit to insert some part which has been provided concerning ordinations, [nt: The sequence of the discourse here requires *et secundum*. And indeed for copyists *ut* for *et*, and in turn *et* for *ut*, frequently slips out. The slip here is old; wishing to repair it, Isidore Mercator after the word *celebrandas* added *esse sciamus*. From his exemplars the same addition was carried over into the Noyon codex of the Spanish collection, not into the Laon exemplar of the same collection.] that according to this ordinations may be celebrated hereafter, lest free choice to interpret otherwise be left to anyone. And first the things which are forbidden are set out. That no one who has served as a soldier after baptism ought to be admitted to the order of the clergy; nor those who have pleaded causes after receiving baptism; or who after receiving the grace of God have held administration: nor that any of the curials can come to the ecclesiastical order, who after baptism either have been crowned [nt: Namely in those solemnities which flowed from gentile rite, and in which the magistrates were compelled to represent a priesthood dedicated to the worship of demons. To which that of Tertullian looks, in the book *On the Crown*, c. 10: *You have the crowns of all the priestly colleges in Claudius*; whose book *On Crowns*, namely, as is recalled in the same place, chap. 7, was extant, teaching both the origins and the causes and the kinds and the solemnities of them.], or have sustained the priesthood, as it is called, [0492B] and have celebrated public shows. For this also is to be guarded against in the case of curials, lest those same who have been of the curials be at some time demanded back by their curias, which we see frequently happen. All which things, reasonably forbidden, ought to be guarded in every way.

## 10.

*Who are to be chosen. That the baptism of wives is not abolished.* --But what sort of men ought to be chosen into the order of the clergy, a clear form declares: that is, those who have been baptized from their earliest age, and [nt: Labbe: *et electorum officio deputati*: it is corrected from other books. And their reading is confirmed here by Siricius, Epistle 1, n. 13: *Whoever has vowed himself to the services of the Church from his infancy... ought to be JOINED to the office of LECTORS*; and n. 14: *But he who is already advanced in age... at the time when he is baptized, let him at once be JOINED to the number of lectors or of exorcists.*] joined to the office of lectors; or, if they be older, when they have obtained the grace of God, have at once bound themselves to the ecclesiastical orders. And if they have had wives, it must be asked whether they took a virgin wife: because it is written in the old Testament, *Let the priest take a virgin wife [n: (Levit. XXI, 13)]*; [0493A] and elsewhere, *Let my priests marry once.* Nor he who has had two wives [n: (Vide epistolam superiorem. n. 9)], because Paul the apostle says, *The husband of one wife [n: (I Tim. III, 2)]*. Nor that ought to be admitted which some oppose and assert in defence of a depraved error, that a wife taken before baptism ought not to be imputed, because in baptism all things are forgiven; not understanding in this matter that in baptism only sins are forgiven, and the number of wives is not abolished. For if *by God*, as it is written, *a wife is prepared for a man [n: (Prov. XIX, 14)]*, and *what God has joined, let man not put asunder [n: (Matth. XIX, 6; Gen. I, 28)]*; and the very authors of the human race are blessed by the Lord in the beginning: how are these believed to be able to be forgiven among sins? But if what they believe who believe so is true; then [0493B] all righteousness which has been wrought by catechumens before baptism will be taken away through baptism. Let no one therefore think or admit any such thing against the Apostle: but let him faithfully understand that *the husband of one wife* was named whether before baptism or after baptism. For if a wife taken before baptism is not counted in the number, neither will the sons begotten of her be able to be numbered among sons. How absurd and foreign this is, your prudence will judge better. Whence let it be lawful for no one to interpret the divine Scriptures otherwise than as right reason permits: lest, while [nt: In the manuscripts of the Spanish and Isidorian collections *quidam* is added here.] they prepare unjust remedies for themselves as an excuse, they be judged both to have corrupted the law and to have overturned the rules. But those things are to be held which both the course of the divine Scriptures contains and have been instituted by priests with useful reason. [0493C] *And in another hand.* Farewell, dearest brethren.

## THE SAME LETTER.

In the curtailed form in which it stands in the old exemplars of Isidore Mercator, as also in the Noyon, Laon [0494A] and Beauvais exemplars of the Spanish collection, and such as it is in the Roman edition of the pontifical letters and in the old common editions of the councils.
