*On the repression of the Spaniards* ( *for* reprehensione) *who ordain clerics disorderly.* --II. *That pleaders, or curials, or men given to secular military service be not admitted to the clergy.* --III. *That whoever have been so ordained be deposed together with their ordainers.* -- IV. *What sort of men are to be chosen into the order of the clergy.* -- V. *That he who had a wife before baptism, and another after baptism, be not made a cleric.*

INNOCENT to all the bishops assembled in the synod of Toledo [n: ( plures mss. Tolosana)], his most beloved brethren in the Lord, greeting.

Since care has often and exceedingly held me anxious [0494B] over the schism of dissension among the churches, which evil report follows up as advancing more widely day by day through the Spains by degrees of separation; a time has arisen in which so great an amendment cannot be put off, and a fitting medicine ought to be provided. For concerning the ordinations which through the fault of a depraved custom the Spaniards, *and the rest thereafter as in the preceding exemplar, from n. 8 to the end, except that the contents of n. 9 are also passed over here: so that, only the general decrees being retained, the other things which pertain to particular causes or persons have been suppressed.*

## 1.

To thee, dearest brother, who consulted me as to what I thought on each proposed case, I have answered, according to the capacity of my understanding, what things seemed good: what either [nt: The Colb. Langob. codex and another manuscript: *vel lucidior ratio*.] docile reason would persuade to be followed, or the authority of reading would show, or the observed series of the times would demonstrate. To me myself also docility accrues from the conference, while [nt: The older manuscripts: *praescrutatis*; and below: *qui postulatur docere*.] having searched out the reasons I am compelled to answer the things proposed: and thus it comes about that he always learns something who is asked to [0496B] teach. ## CHAP. I.



## 2.

Thou hast proposed what ought to be observed concerning those whom, placed in the ministries of the diaconate or in the office of the presbyterate, sons begotten have revealed to be or to have been incontinent [n: (Dist. 82, c. 2; Ivo p. 6, c. 57; Agathense concil. c. 9)]. Concerning these, both the discipline of the divine laws is manifest, and the admonitions of Siricius, bishop, a man of blessed memory [n: (Epist. 1 c. 7, et epist. 5, n. 3)], have gone forth plainly, that the incontinent placed in such offices be deprived of every ecclesiastical honour, and not be admitted to approach the [nt: In Merlin and Crabbe, as in the manuscripts of the Dionysian, Spanish and Isidorian collections: *tale ministerium*. Better, *tale* is absent from the other books.] ministry which it behoves to be fulfilled by continence alone. For there is a very old authority of the sacred law, kept from the beginning, that [nt: Six ancient manuscripts: *in templis*. Then four: *anno vicissitudinis suae*.] the priests were commanded to dwell in the temple in the year of their [0496C] turn [n: (Luc. I)]; so that, serving the sacred oblations, pure and [0497A] cleansed from every stain, they might claim for themselves the divine [nt: Merlin, Crabbe and most manuscripts: *mysteria*. Siricius indeed, Epistle 1, n. 11, enacts that priests and levites who are incontinent can never handle the divine *mysteria*: but there he speaks of the priests and levites of the new law. And when Gratian, dist. 31, c. 6, cites what follows thus, *that it be lawful for those to be admitted to the sacrifices who do not exercise the carnal office even with a wife*, he is rightly arraigned for adulterating Innocent's sentence. He himself reports it more faithfully at dist. 82, c. 2.] ministries [n: (I Paralip. XXIV)]: and that it be not lawful for those to be admitted to the sacrifices who exercise carnal intercourse even with a wife, because it is written: *Be ye holy, because I also am holy, the Lord your God [n: (Levit. To whom certainly, on account of the succession of offspring, [nt: In the Roman edition, following Merlin and Crabbe, *propterea* is suppressed here, and presently, after the words *ex alia tribu*, there is added *praeter* (Crabbe, in smaller letters, *quam*, which in the Roman edition is expressed without any distinction) *ex semine Aaron*: against the manuscripts.] the use of wives had therefore been relaxed, because no one from another tribe had been commanded to approach the priesthood; how much more ought these priests or levites to keep chastity from the day of their ordination [n: (Confer. concil. For if Paul writes to the Corinthians, saying, *Abstain for a time, that you may give yourselves to prayer (I Corint. ## CHAP. II.



## 5.

And this was asked, what ought to be observed concerning those who, having been given over at every time after baptism to the pleasures of incontinence, at the extreme end of their life ask at once for penance and for the reconciliation of communion [n: (23, q. 4, c. 45)].

## 6.

Concerning these the earlier observance was harsher; the later, mercy intervening, more inclined to mildness. For the earlier custom held that penance be granted, but communion denied. For since in those times persecutions were frequent, lest the facility of communion, once granted, should not recall men, secure of reconciliation, from lapsing, communion was rightly denied; penance was granted, lest all be wholly denied: and the reason of the time made the remission harsher. But after our Lord [0498C] restored peace to his Churches, the terror being now driven off, it was decided that communion be given to those departing [nt: So Labbe, Sirmond and several and better manuscripts. Other printed editions, however: *obeuntibus*; and below, with Dionysius and the Isidorian collection, *subsequi* for *sequi*. That harsher discipline, recalled by Innocent, prevails chiefly in the council of Elvira, can. 6, 7, 8, etc. It had prevailed before in Africa also, but only among certain bishops, who indeed did not think peace should be given to adulterers in such a way, and shut the place of penance against adulteries altogether, and yet did not withdraw from the college of their fellow bishops who thought otherwise and granted them peace, as Cyprian relates, Epistle 52, to Antonianus. There is besides a certain example of this severity in the common book of Ambrose on the Fall of a consecrated virgin, to whom, c. 8, n. 58, this speech is addressed: *Cling to penance until the end of life, and do not presume that pardon is given thee by a human day; for he deceives thee who would promise thee this. For since thou hast sinned properly against the Lord, it befits thee to await the remedy from him alone in the day of judgment.* And although Cyprian, in the letter cited, proves at length that neither penance nor indulgence is to be denied to the lapsed; yet toward its end he decrees this: *Those not doing penance, nor testifying the grief of their offences with their whole heart and with a manifest profession of their lamentation, we have judged are to be wholly kept from the hope of communion and peace, if in sickness and in danger they have begun to beg: because it is not repentance for the offence but the warning of impending death that drives them to ask; nor is he worthy to receive comfort in death who did not think that he was to die.* Which sentence indeed, if with Pamelius it is to be judged somewhat harsher, the same will have to be said also of this canon 22 of the first synod of Arles: *Concerning those who apostatize and never present themselves at the church, nor even seek to do penance, and afterwards, seized by sickness, ask for communion, it has pleased that communion is not to be given them, unless they recover and bring forth worthy fruits of penance.* But this so seemed good to them, not because they greatly distrusted the clemency of God, but the truth of penance of this kind. And indeed Cyprian, toward the end of the book to Demetrianus, teaches that no penance is late for one still remaining in this world, and that no one ought to be deterred from it either by sins or by years; that pardon is given to him who confesses, and that one passes to immortality under death itself. Which, although he says it chiefly of the grace of baptism, there is yet no doubt that he thinks the same of every penance undertaken after baptism, provided it be true. Hence, as is to be seen in his Epistles 13, 14, 15, etc., he always took great care that there should not be lacking one who would impart peace and communion to the lapsed, after the exomologesis had been made, if they had begun to be pressed by some sickness and danger. What Innocent now defines, Celestine afterwards, Epistle 4, to the bishops of the province of Vienne and Narbonne, n. 3, and Leo, Epistle 100, to Theodore, n. 5, expressly confirm, and before them the Roman clergy, in Cyprian, Epistle 3, to the clergy of Carthage, had prescribed in these words: *If those who have fallen into this temptation begin to be seized by sickness, and do penance for their deed, and desire communion, they certainly ought to be helped.* Jerome, above, p. 550, notes that the example of the thief was set before us, lest anyone should at any time think conversion late. But there he asks for conversion and change.], [0499A] both on account of the mercy of the Lord, as a viaticum for those setting out, and lest we seem to follow the harshness and severity of the Novatian heretic who denies pardon. Communion therefore shall be granted, with penance, at the last: so that men of this kind, even at their very end, by the permission of our Saviour, may be delivered from perpetual destruction.

## CHAP. III.



## 7.

It was asked also concerning those who after baptism have held administration, and have either exercised tortures [nt: The Pith. manuscript: *tormenta sua*. More truly the others: *sola*, namely those by which death is not inflicted on the accused. To what end this question is set on foot may be supplied and explained from Epistle 25 of Ambrose, to Studius. For he had likewise asked whether anyone, after a capital sentence had been passed, ought to abstain from the communion of the sacraments. To whom Ambrose, n. 2, answers: *There are some, outside the Church however, who do not call into the communion of the heavenly sacraments those who have judged that a capital sentence should be passed on any.* And straightway he adds: *Many also abstain of their own accord; and they are praised indeed; nor can we ourselves not commend them, we who observe the authority of the Apostle so far as not to dare to deny them communion.* Lastly, what seemed good to the elders on this matter he discloses in n. 9 in these words: *And therefore our elders preferred to be more indulgent toward judges, so that, while their sword is feared, the fury of crimes might be checked, and not provoked: for if communion were denied, the punishment of criminals would seem to be avenged.* Our forebears therefore preferred that it should be in the will of the one abstaining rather than in the necessity of the law.] only, or have also pronounced capital sentence.
