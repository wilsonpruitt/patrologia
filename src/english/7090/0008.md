## 8.

Concerning these we read nothing defined by the elders. For they remembered that these powers had been granted by God, and that the sword had been permitted for the punishment of the guilty, and that the minister of God had been given as an avenger in matters of this kind [0499B] [n: (Rom. XIII, 1, 4)]. How then should they reprehend a deed which they saw had been granted with God as its author? Concerning these, therefore, we hold thus, as has been observed hitherto, lest we should seem either to overturn discipline or to come against the authority of the Lord. But for them, in the rendering of account, all their deeds will be kept.

## CHAP. IV.



## 9.

And this was desired to be known, why husbands who communicate do not come together with adulterous wives, when on the contrary wives are seen to remain in the fellowship of adulterous husbands.

## 10.

Concerning this [n: (32, q. 6, c. 23; Ivo p. 8, c. 214)] the Christian religion condemns adultery in both sexes with equal reason. But women do not easily accuse their husbands of adultery, and hidden [0500A] sins do not have punishment. Husbands, however, have been accustomed more freely to report adulterous wives before the priests: and therefore to the women, their crime being disclosed, communion is denied. But of the husbands, the offence lying hidden, no one is easily kept away on suspicions. Who certainly will be removed, if his shameful deed be detected. Since then the cause is equal, sometimes, when proof is lacking, the reason for punishment is at rest.

## CHAP. V.



## 11.

This also [n: (23, q. 4, c. 46; Ivo p. 10, c. 94)] thou hast wished to inquire [nt: In the margin of the Colb. Langob. manuscript this summary of the chapter is noted: *On advocates, who bring forth sentences according to the crime*. But the question is rather about those who demand capital sentences, or rather who dictate petitions for those demanding them, than about those who bring forth sentences of this kind. And this, as also what was proposed in chap. 3, makes plain how greatly the Church shrinks from blood. And so Innocent does not deny communion to those who after baptism have either held administration or dictated petitions for those demanding a capital sentence, in such a way that he nevertheless teaches, Epistle 37, n. 5, that they are to be kept from the clergy.], whether it be freely granted to those who dictate petitions, namely after the regeneration of baptism, to ask of princes the death of anyone, or blood on account of guilt. A thing which princes never grant without inquiry; but they always send back the very matters committed, or the crimes, to the judges, that, [nt: The early editions of the councils and several manuscripts: *causae cognitae*. The Royal exemplar of the Dionysian collection, with the Colb. Langob.: *causa cognita vindicetur*. According to our reading, which the other books exhibit, before *vindicentur* the word *crimina* is understood. See the Theodosian Code, book II, tit. 18, law 1, and book XIX, tit. 40, law 1.] the cause [0500B] having been examined, they may be punished. And when these have been delegated to the investigator, either acquittal or condemnation is pronounced according to the nature of the business. And while the authority of the laws is exercised against the wicked, the one who dictated will be immune.

## CHAP. VI.



## 12.

Thy charity has also inquired concerning those who, a repudiation intervening, have joined themselves to another in marriage. Whom it is manifest are adulterers on both sides. But those who, while the wife is still living, although the marriage seems to have been dissolved, have hastened to another union, cannot but seem adulterers, so much so that even these persons with whom such are joined seem themselves also to have committed adultery, according to that which we read in the Gospel: *Whoever shall put away his wife, and marry another, [nt: In the old Corb. manuscript and another Colb. *moechatur* does not stand here. The integrity of the sentence would be safe if they had afterwards added *moechantur*.] commits adultery; likewise he also who marries her who is put away commits adultery* [0501A] [n: (Matth. XIX, 9)]. And therefore [nt: In the early editions *tales* is added here: which is absent from the manuscripts. Then six manuscripts of good note subjoin *omnes a communione abstinendos*. After the word *abstinendos*, however, *esse manifestum est*, which Innocent expressed at the beginning of this answer, is understood. Wherefore there is no reason why, with Quesnel, *abstinendi* should be substituted against the faith of the manuscripts.] all are to be kept from the communion of the faithful. But concerning their parents, or their relations, nothing of the kind can be decreed, unless they be discovered to have been instigators of the unlawful union.

## CHAP. VII.



## 13.

But what books are received in the canon, [nt: Dionysius added here *scripturarum*. Before which word the author of the Spanish collection thereupon put *sanctarum*. The Isidorian collection seized on both, but the other manuscripts have neither. Further, the noun *brevis* here means a catalogue. The same sacred books, though not in the same order, are recounted among the canonical Scriptures in canon 47 of the third council of Carthage, in the year 397. And the cited canon of this council is read inserted in the Code of canons of the African church, c. 24, with this addition: *Let this also be made known to our brother and fellow priest Boniface, or to other bishops of those parts, for the confirming of this canon: because we have received from the fathers that these are to be read in church.* Whence there is a conjecture that the decree of the third council of Carthage on the sacred books was recited in another council of Carthage, which was held in the year 419 when Boniface was Roman pontiff, and confirmed with the clause just mentioned.] the list annexed shows. These are the things which thou didst wish to be told in word, as desired: the five books of Moses, that is, of Genesis, of Exodus, of Leviticus, of Numbers, of Deuteronomy, and of Jesus Nave, of Judges one, four books of Kingdoms, together also with Ruth, sixteen books of the Prophets, five books of Solomon, the Psalter. But the rest, which were written either under the name of Matthias or of James the less, or under the name of Peter and John, which were written by a certain Leucius [nt: What is enclosed in brackets Quesnel omits, and it stands only in the exemplars of the Dionysian, Spanish and Isidorian collections. Given on the tenth day before the Kalends of March [nt: February 20, year 405. ## EPISTLE VII. [nt: In the edition of the Councils, 29; the one which was 7 is now 18. It has been copied from Sozomen, who, having found it together with the following Epistle 12 turned from the Latin tongue into Greek, took care to publish both in book VIII, c. 26. From him Nicephorus Callisti afterwards received both, and inserted them in book XIII, c. 32, of his History. By this letter Innocent answered the letters sent to him by the same clergy through the agency of Germanus and Cassian, and so received when the year 405 was already somewhat advanced. For, as Palladius relates, pp. 26 and 27, Germanus and Cassian arrived at Rome after Palladius, and Palladius a month after Cyriacus, bishop of Synnada; who indeed had begun to flee from the East to Rome after the law promulgated at Constantinople in the year 404, on the 18th day of November. In Sozomen and Nicephorus nothing is prefixed to the inscription Ἰννοκέντιος ἐπίσκοπος, etc. Wherefore the title prefixed to it in the editions of the councils, in which the name ἀρχιερέως is attributed to Innocent, being destitute of all authority, is of no weight to prove that the word ἀρχιερέως was then in use. The same is to be said also of Epistle 12, in whose title the editors of the councils of their own accord call both Innocent ἀρχιερέα and John ἀρχιεπίσκοπον.] TO THE CLERGY AND PEOPLE OF CONSTANTINOPLE. *Having consoled them out of their own very judgment, Innocent deplores the unjust substitution of another bishop in the place of John while he lives. Then he declares that the Nicene canons alone are to be received, and that others which oppose them are to be repudiated. Lastly he signifies that it is his care that an ecumenical synod be assembled.*

*[0501B]INNOCENT, bishop, to the presbyters and deacons, and to all the clergy and people of the church of Constantinople, who are under John the bishop, beloved brethren, greeting.*

## 1.

*For what they suffer there is one consolation, that of patience.* From the letters of your charity which you sent by [nt: This Germanus seems to be no other than the one whom Chrysostom mentions above, Epistle 4, n. 3. Nor is there doubt that Cassian is the author of the Conferences, in which Germanus frequently speaks in dialogue as his companion.] Germanus the presbyter and Cassian the deacon, I have learned with anxious solicitude the scene of evils which you have set before our eyes: and by reading them over again and again I have perceived with how great calamities and hardships the faith labours. Which thing indeed the consolation of patience alone can heal. For our God will shortly give an end to such great tribulations, and it will help to have endured these things. [0503A] Truly, this very consolation, necessary as it is, set in the beginning of the letter of your charity, we have recognized, praising your purpose, as confirmed by many testimonies exhorting to patience. For the consolation which we owed you through our letters, you yourselves have anticipated by your letters. For this patience our Lord is wont to supply to those who labour: that the servants of Christ, even in tribulations, may console themselves, reckoning with themselves that the things which they themselves suffer have already before befallen the saints. But we too can borrow consolation for ourselves out of your very letters. For we are not strangers to the fellowship of your grief; since we are afflicted in you.

## 2.

*How unjustly a bishop has been substituted for John while he lives.* [0503B] --For who could bear the things which are committed by those [nt: Namely by Acacius, bishop of Beroea, Theophilus of Alexandria, Antiochus of Ptolemais, and Severian of Gabala, whom the people of Constantinople by their letters signified by name to be the authors of the hardships which they were suffering.] whom it befitted above others to be zealous for tranquillity and peace and concord? But now, in a preposterous manner, innocent priests are driven out of the seats of their own churches. Which indeed our brother and fellow priest John, your bishop, first unjustly suffered, when he had by no reason been heard. No charge is brought, nor is it heard. And what is this abandoned counsel? That there may not be, or be sought, any form of judgment, others are substituted in the place of living priests: as though those who have made their beginning from a crime of this kind could be judged to have or to have done anything right. For we have never found that such things were done by our fathers, but rather [nt: Namely by the council of Sardica, can. 4, where, in favour of a bishop who has appealed to the Roman see, it is enacted: *Let another bishop by no means be ordained in his see, after the appeal of him who seems to have been deposed, unless the cause has been determined in the judgment of the Roman bishop.* But John was cast out even while appealing to a synod, as he himself testifies above, Epistle 4, n. 3. That canon, in somewhat different words, is cited by Ignatius the patriarch in his letter to the pope and the Roman council, in Baronius at the year 861. This very thing, which the synod of Sardica expressly forbids, may be seen to be prohibited also by the Nicene canon 9, by which it is provided *that two bishops be not approved to exist in one city*.] forbidden; since licence has been given [0503C] to no one to ordain another in the place of one living. For a reprobate ordination cannot take away the honour of a priest: since he who is unjustly substituted cannot be a bishop at all.

## 3.

*What canons are to be received or repudiated.* --But as to what pertains to the observance of the canons [nt: Namely, so that the judgment, however unjustly pronounced, might prevail against John, and no place of defence might be given him, Theophilus had sent to Constantinople the canons of the council of Antioch, framed long before chiefly against Athanasius, among which the enemies of John made much above all of the fourth, reported thus by Palladius, cap. 8, p. 78: *If any bishop or presbyter, deposed rightly or wrongly, return of himself without a synod, let such a one have no longer any place of defence, but let him be expelled absolutely.* The very words of the council of Antioch are these: Εἴ τις ἐπίσκοπος ὑπὸ συνόδου καθαιρεθείς, ἢ πρεσβύτερος ἢ διάκονος ὑπὸ τοῦ ἰδίου ἐπισκόπου, τολμήσειέν τι πρᾶξαι τῆς λειτουργίας, etc.; and while Gentian Hervet turns them into Latin thus, *If any bishop deposed by a synod, or presbyter or deacon by his own bishop, shall have dared to celebrate the sacred rite*, he seems not to have rendered accurately enough the words τολμήσειέν τι πρᾶξαι λειτουργίας. For it is not a deacon's part to *celebrate the sacred rite*: and it is plain that a deposed bishop, presbyter and deacon are forbidden by this canon not only to celebrate the sacred rite, but to touch anything of the ministry from which they have been cast down. Wherefore Theodoret, book V of the History, c. 34, relates that the adversaries of John exacted punishment of him no longer for false crimes, but ἀλλὰ τῆς μετὰ τὴν καθαίρεσιν λειτουργίας, *but for the ministry discharged after deposition*. Then a controversy arose about the authority of the canons objected against John, *some*, says Palladius, p. 79, *contending that they were of the orthodox, others showing that they were of the Arians*. On which occasion Elpidius, ibid., p. 80, is said to have said wittily to the emperor: *Let our brothers Acacius and Antiochus subscribe that they are of the same faith as those who put forth the canons which they propose as put forth by orthodox men, and the controversy is solved.* This same controversy, brought before him, Innocent now decides, and teaches that those canons are of heretics, not because orthodox men were altogether lacking in the council of Antioch, but because it is admitted that the Arians, the adversaries of Athanasius, held the first place in it: and a witness of this is the canon cited, which had been put forth on purpose to take away from Athanasius all hope of restoration.], we say that those alone are to be obeyed [0505A] which were defined at Nicaea; which alone [nt: Besides those, says Paschasius Quesnel, Dissertation 12 at the end of the works of Leo, c. 2, *Innocent received the Sardican ones also, but held as Nicene, and adorned and clothed with their name*. And indeed from this, that the pontiff next subjoins about the synod of Sardica condemning the Antiochene canons, it is plain that what was done at Sardica is received by him. But since he praises those things under the proper name of the *Sardican synod*; it is no less manifest that the Sardican canons were received by him as distinct from the Nicene, not as *held for Nicene and clothed with their name*. Wherefore we understand Innocent to proclaim that the Nicene canons alone are to be followed in such a way, not that those should be rejected or spurned which agree with them, but only those which disagree: *Since*, says Leo also, Epistle 96, to Pulcheria, n. 2, *against the statutes of the paternal canons, which were founded by spiritual decrees in the city of Nicaea many ages ago, nothing may ever be dared; so that if anyone wishes to decree something different, he rather diminishes himself than corrupts those*. Certainly the Sardican may be reckoned as it were an appendix of the Nicene synod, inasmuch as Hosius presided over it, who at Nicaea had sat either as chief or among the foremost prelates, and in which the same faith was established and the same heresy overthrown. In the time of pope Damasus, as we observed above, p. 591, n. 3, following Athanasius, lest the Arians, *who devised various names of synods*, should impose upon anyone, various councils were held at Rome and in other places, in which it was enacted *that no synod should henceforth be named in the catholic Church except the one of Nicaea*. How much it concerned Athanasius that the authority of the synod of Sardica should by no means be lightened is unknown to no one.] the catholic Church ought to follow and to acknowledge. But if others are brought forward by certain men, which disagree with the Nicene canons, and are proved to have been composed by heretics, these are rejected by the catholic bishops. For the things which have been devised by heretics, these it is not lawful to join to the catholic rules. For they, by contrary and unlawful decisions, perpetually aim at this, to undermine the judgment of the Nicene fathers. We say, therefore, that these canons are not only not to be followed, but are also to be condemned together with heretical and schismatic dogmas, as they have already been condemned in the synod of Sardica [nt: Not indeed by opposed canons or decrees, but by contrary acts, when they received Athanasius and other bishops into communion, no account being taken of the aforesaid canons, and restored them to their sees. This explanation we do not devise ourselves out of our own, but Palladius supplies it, p. 78, where, having cited canon 4 of the council of Antioch, he adds: *And this canon, as impious and put forth by the impious, was abrogated in the council of Sardica by the Romans, Italians, Illyrians, Macedonians and Greeks, when Liberius or Julius* (Julius was then over the church) *in the principate of Constans admitted into communion Athanasius and Marcellus the Galatian, on whose account this canon also had been made.*] by the bishops who went before us. For it would be better that the things which [nt: It seems that in the Greek κακῶς or οὐ καλῶς should be read: and so here there should be restored *quae male*, or *non recte acta sunt*.] have been rightly done should be condemned, [0505B] than that those things which have been done against the canons should obtain any firmness, dearest brethren.
