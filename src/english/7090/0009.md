## 4.

*That the evils can be healed by one ecumenical synod.* --But what are we to do at present against these things? There is need of the examination of a synod, which we said long since ought to be assembled. For it alone is that which can calm the motions of storms of this kind. And that we may obtain it, it will be useful meanwhile to await the medicine of these evils from the will of the great God and of Christ himself our Lord. All things which have now been thrown into confusion through the envy of the devil, for the proving of the faithful, will be pacified. There is nothing that we ought not to hope for from the Lord through firmness of faith. For we too have long and much been considering by what means an ecumenical synod may be assembled, so that the turbulent motions may at length, by the [0505C] nod of God, come to rest. Let us bear up, then, for a little while; and, fortified with the rampart of patience, let us hope that all things will be restored by the help of our God. Moreover, all the things which you have said you endure, we had already learned before, by careful questioning, from our fellow bishops who fled to Rome, although at different [nt: The different times at which those bishops fled to Rome Palladius notes, pp. 10 and 26: whence also we have assigned above the time of this letter.] times [0507A], that is, from Demetrius, Cyriacus, Eulysius and Palladius, who are with us.

*[0502B] Innocent, bishop, to the presbyters and deacons and all the clergy and the people of the church of Constantinople, who are under John the bishop, beloved brethren, greeting. *

[0502B] From the letters of your charity, which you sent by Germanus the presbyter and Cassian the deacon, I learned with anxious care the scene of evils which you set before our eyes, and by reading them over many times I perceived with how many hardships and labours the faith is afflicted. Which thing the consolation of patience alone heals. For our God will shortly give an end to such great afflictions, and it will be of profit to have borne these things. But indeed this very necessary [0504A] consolation, set at the beginning of the letter of our charity, we have recognized, praising your purpose, as containing many testimonies toward endurance. For the consolation of ours which we owed to send to you, you anticipated by your letters. For our Master is wont to supply this endurance to those who labour: that the servants of Christ, even when they are in afflictions, may comfort themselves, reckoning within themselves that the things which they suffer happened before to the saints also. And we too can bring consolation to ourselves out of your very letters. For we are not strangers to suffering with you, since we too are chastised in you.

## 2.

For who will be able to bear the offences committed by [0504B] those whom it most behoved to be zealous for calm, for peace, and for concord itself? Now, in an inverted manner, guiltless priests are thrust from the presidency of their own churches. Which indeed our brother and fellow minister John, your bishop, was the first to suffer unjustly, having obtained no hearing. No charge is brought, nor is it heard; and what is this forbidden device? That no pretext of judgment may arise, or be sought, others are installed in the places of living priests, as though those who start out from such an offence could be judged by anyone to hold or to have done anything rightly. For we have never known such things to have been dared by our fathers, but rather to have been forbidden, in that authority has been given to no one to ordain another in the place of one living. For an unapproved ordination cannot take away [0504C] the honour of the priest, since neither can he be a bishop who is unjustly put in his place.

## 3.

And concerning the observance of the canons, we write that those ought to be followed which were defined at Nicaea, [0506A] which alone the catholic Church ought to follow, and these to acknowledge. But if others are brought forward by some, which disagree with those of Nicaea, and are proved to have been composed by heretics, these are rejected by the catholic bishops. For the things found out by the heretics, these it is not lawful to attach to the catholic canons. For always through contrary and lawless things they wish to lessen the counsel of those at Nicaea. We say, therefore, not only that these ought not to be followed, but rather that they are to be condemned together with the heretical and schismatic dogmas, as also happened before in the synod of Sardica at the hands of the bishops before us. For it were more fitting that things rightly done be condemned, than that things done directly against the canons should have any firmness, most honoured brethren. [0506B]

## 4.

But what shall we now do at present against such men? There is need of a synodal examination, which we said long ago was to be assembled. For it alone is that which can calm the motions of such tempests. And that we may obtain it, it is useful meanwhile to commit the healing to the will of the great God and of the Christ of them, our Lord. All the things which have now been thrown into confusion through the envy of the devil, for the proving of the faithful, will be made gentle [Lab. παραμυθήσεται]. We ought to despair of nothing from the Lord through the firmness of the faith. For we too are considering much in what way the ecumenical synod may be assembled, so that by the will of God the [0506C] turbulent motions may cease. Let us bear up, then, for a while, and, fortified by the wall of endurance, let us hope that all things will be restored by the help of our God. But against this understanding stands not only the silence of the ancient writers about so profane a rite, but also the fact that they proclaim that woman's image *set up*, *placed* and *dedicated*, not *carried about*. And the natural understanding of these words is that the report of the detractors about this matter was carried about through the provinces and spread through the whole world.] through the provinces and spread through the whole world, I admonished you by other letters, that by repentance of such a deed and by the discontinuance of the purpose the envious rumour might grow old, and the public tongue might not have what to carp at in the morals of the times: although also, concerning the destruction of perishing Illyricum, we have declared among you with pious affection that it is a grief to us, why you [nt: The sequence of the discourse requires *nos*, not *vos*. For Honorius remonstrates with Arcadius that he wished the losses of the commonwealth to be hidden from him, about which he did not deign to write him anything.] have been unwilling to acknowledge these losses of the commonwealth, and they have been announced by other informers rather than by the letters of your piety: yet it is not lawful for us to dissemble with your serenity even this, that report, ever the swift announcer of evils, has not been silent about what has very lately happened in divine matters, not without fear of public danger; and, as the nature of men bears, [0507C] which is ever roused by new chances to detraction, an occasion of carping being offered, it exercises its malice [0508A] against the times with the zeal of savage loquacity. For it has lately been published [n: (Vid. Pallad. pag. 82 et 83)], that at Constantinople, on the most sacred and venerable day of Easter, when religion had gathered almost all the peoples of the neighbouring cities into the same place, the rite being to be celebrated more strictly under the presence of the princes, the catholic churches were suddenly closed, the priests thrust into custody, so that, namely, at the very time at which by princely indulgence [nt: In Sirmond, appendix to the Theodosian Code, pp. 17 and 19, there is a double constitution of Theodosius, by which *for the Paschal festival, except those whom the enormity of the five crimes by no means allows to be released*, he frees the rest from the custody of prison and restores them to their former state. And this clemency he proclaims in the same place, p. 19, *as enjoined and handed down by our forebears*.] the gloomy prisons of the guilty are unbarred, a savage prison should shut in the ministers of the pious law and of peace, and all the mysteries were thrown into confusion in the manner of war, some slain in the very sanctuaries of the church, and such violence raged about the altars that both [0508B] venerable bishops were thrust into exile, and human blood, what is impious to say, drenched the heavenly sacraments. On suddenly learning these things, I confess that we were disturbed. For who, in so bloody a crime, would not fear the offence of almighty God? or how [ *Supply,* quis] would think it to lie outside the highest peril of the Roman world and of all mortals? when God almighty himself, the author of our Empire and the governor of the commonwealth which he has entrusted to us, was believed to be angered by the exceedingly deadly and execrable deeds committed, holy lord brother and nephew, venerable augusti; when, if anything were being treated between the prelates concerning the cause of religion, the judgment ought [nt: Ambrose, Epistle 21, to Valentinian the younger, n. 2, praises the edict of Valentinian I, by which he enacted that *in a cause of faith or of any ecclesiastical order he ought to judge who is neither unequal in office nor unlike in right*; *that is*, says Ambrose, *he willed that priests should judge concerning priests. Indeed, if a bishop were accused on other grounds also, and a cause of morals were to be examined, he willed that this too should pertain to the episcopal judgment.*] to have been episcopal. For to them belongs the interpretation of divine things, to us awaits [ *By the ancient usage for* spectat] the obedience of religion. [0508C] But let it be granted that the princely care presumed something further to itself concerning mystical and catholic questions: [0509A] ought indignation, once roused, to have gone so far as to the exiles of priests, so far as to the slaughter of men; that where chaste prayers, where sincere vows, where unblemished sacrifices are offered, there the sword, not easily to be drawn even against the throat of the guilty, should thrust itself forth?
