## 2.

By the very events, in short, it is taught what the divine majesty has thought about these things. The first judgment [nt: Baronius would prefer *indicium*. Yet *judicium* is to be retained, that is, the punishment and vengeance of God the judge.] of the present commotion indeed was this, and would that it were the only one! for human trepidation, conscious of so great a thing perpetrated, makes us fear, after the proofs of a terrible vengeance, something graver, which may almighty God avert. I hear [n: (Pallad. pag. 91 et 93)] that the most holy church, set out with the wealth of so many emperors [ *Perhaps* expolitam], noble in its precious adornments, most august through the prayer [0509B] of so many supplicating princes, has burned, and that that unique light of the city of Constantinople [ *Baronius adds* ecclesiae] has smoked, fallen into ashes, God not forbidding (for he seems to execrate the defiled mysteries, and to have turned away his eyes from that place which blood had now stained, lest anyone should be able to entreat the heavenly mercy beneath bloody altars); that the sacred buildings also [nt: That is, the most ample house of the senate, which, says Palladius, *although many paces distant, opposite the church to the south, that fire, passing over the multitude of people between as though over a bridge, consumed*. Zosimus bewails its loss. That fire happened on the twelfth day before the Kalends of July, Honorius for the sixth time and Aristaenetus being consuls, as Socrates relates, book VI, chap. 18, and, as the Alexandrian Chronicle adds, on the second weekday, at the sixth hour.], noble with no less splendour, were consumed by the violence of the raging fire as the flame poured itself out more widely, and that the ornaments wrought by our forebears, which adorned the public face, were burned up as though at a kind of funeral of the city.

## 3.

Although I, provoked by frequent injuries, ought to have kept silent about these things, and not so faithfully to admonish a most closely joined brother and the partners of the realm *[Baron. consortem]*, [0509C] yet, putting the tie of blood before the goad of silent grief, I exhort and urge that these things, if it can be done, be corrected by amended morals for the future, and that the divine wrath, so far as by the event it is disclosed to have been roused, be appeased by the diligence of vows. Receive from me the highest proof [nt: In the Roman edition, *judicium*. Better here, with Baronius, *indicium*.] of simplicity. For this reason I thought that this should be intimated to your clemency, lest silence itself should make me suspect with anyone as of a hidden rejoicing, or lest anyone should believe that I give [0510A] consent to such deeds, and that I, who often admonished that they should not happen, did not grieve after they had been committed.

## 4.

For who could be without grief, who remembers that he is a Christian, that so great a disturbance of religion has suddenly been brought in, that the whole state of the catholic faith must needs waver? There was among the bishops a cause which ought to have been resolved by counsel brought together [nt: Baronius: *contractoque concilio*. The preceding word *collato* suggests, and in the Roman edition there rightly follows, *tractatoque consilio*.] and treated. Legates [nt: Here too Baronius substitutes *missis,.... legatis*. Legates were sent to Rome, as Palladius witnesses, pp. 9, 23, etc., from both parties: first, from the party of Theophilus, an Alexandrian lector bearing his letter, then a presbyter of the same Theophilus, Peter by name, with Martyrius, deacon of the church of Constantinople, who delivered other letters of Theophilus together with certain acts to pope Innocent; and lastly Paternus, who called himself a presbyter of the church of Constantinople, bearing letters of the bishops Acacius, Paul, Antiochus, Severian and certain others, in which they slandered John, as though he had set fire to the church of St Sophia. From the party of John, however, to pass over others who afterwards came in great number, there first betook themselves to Rome four bishops, Pansophius, Pappus, Demetrius and Eugenius, and they delivered three letters, one of John, another of 40 bishops concerning communion with him, the third of his clergy. And by name, concerning the letter of John to Innocent, Palladius testifies, p. 22, that it was written also to Venerius of Milan and Chromatius of Aquileia, bishops. Hence Honorius could say that those legates had been sent *to the priests of the eternal City and of Italy*.] were sent from both parties to the priests of the eternal city and of Italy: a sentence was awaited from the authority of all, which was to shape the rule of discipline (for all ought to have been kept intact, and nothing innovated, while a deliberate definition proceeded), when meanwhile a certain wonderful headlong haste blazed up, so that, the letters of the priests who had been consulted by the mutual embassy of the parties not being awaited, the matters not [0510B] examined, the prelates were thrust into exile, delivered over to punishment before they had experienced the sentence of an episcopal judgment. In short, how premature that condemnation was the event has proved. For these [nt: What the emperor here attributes to several, Palladius ascribes to Innocent alone, and says, p. 22: *Upon this the blessed pope Innocent sent letters of communion to both parties, disapproving the judgment which had been made by Theophilus.* And indeed his Epistles 4 and 5 are witnesses of the communion granted by Innocent to both parties. But who would persuade himself that he accomplished a thing of such moment without a synod? especially since we read that Innocent, tenacious of the custom of the elders, prescribed, Epistle 3, n. 1, that the Spanish bishops, who laid something before him, should be heard *in the session of the presbytery*.] whose authority was awaited, having permitted to John the bishop a peaceful communion *[Baron. praemissa]*, judged that concord should be ratified, and thought that no one should be repelled from fellowship before judgment.

## 5.

What now remains but that schisms, dissociated in divers directions, should tear the catholic faith apart, that heresies, ever hostile to communion, should be born from so great a variety of things done, so that it can now scarcely be imputed to the people if perhaps they depart into dissonant parties by the diversity of sects; since from public authority the matter of discords has been sent before, [0510C] and a certain tinder of nourishing sedition has been kindled. That this may not break out afresh into some great ruin of the human race, vows must be made, that God, patient toward human lapses, may prosper and further a thing ill done. For so far as it rests with us, we can fear what has been done; so far as it rests with the mercy of God, ever placable, impunity will be granted not to merit, but to pardon.

*OF THE EMPEROR HONORIUS TO ARCADIUS AUGUSTUS.*

*That he order a synod to be assembled at Thessalonica, and Theophilus to betake himself thither.* [0511]

[0511A]I write now for the third time to thy mildness, asking that the things which have been done by compact against John, bishop of Constantinople, be amended: and, as it seems, it has not yet been accomplished. Wherefore I have written again by these bishops and presbyters, being very solicitous for the peace of the Church, through which our empire also obtains peace; that thou wouldst deign to order the bishops of the East to come together at Thessalonica. For our bishops of the West, having chosen men who know not how to turn aside to evil and falsehood, have sent five bishops, two presbyters, and one deacon of the great Roman church, whom I would have thee deem worthy of every honour; so that, if they are persuaded that John was expelled by right, they may teach me to withdraw from his communion; or, if they convict the bishops of the East [nt: The Greek word ἐθελοχακοῦντας, which is said properly of soldiers who on purpose do not put forth their strength, so that the adversary may win, is rightly applied to those bishops who, when they could have opposed the canons to Theophilus, yielded of their own accord, so that he might be able to oppress John unjustly.] [0511B] of being driven by a depraved will of mind, they may turn thee away from their communion. For what the judgment of the Westerners about John the bishop is [that thou mayest have it ascertained], from all the letters which have been written [nt: What is enclosed in brackets I have judged must be supplied, lest the sentence hang in doubt. It is not more complete in Ambrose of Camaldoli, who here subjoins: *From almost all the letters which they have frequently given me, of these I have subjoined two containing the same thing as the rest.* Palladius, p. 28, relates that Innocent, when he had received various letters, wrote to Honorius, ὑποτάξαντα κατὰ μέρος τῶν γραμμάτων δύναμιν, *and subjoined one by one what those letters contained*, where he intimates that only the force, or the sum, of the several letters was expressed. But from this place it is gathered that not only the sum of the several letters, but also the very letters themselves, or copies of them, were sent to him at the same time.] to me, I have subjoined [nt: Palladius writes, p. 31, that not only the letters of the emperor Honorius, of Innocent, Roman bishop, and of Chromatius of Aquileia, but also those of Venerius of Milan and of others, were delivered to the same legates at the same time. But Honorius was content to mention the writings of Innocent and Chromatius sent to him some while before, not those which they were sending afresh to Arcadius, or which he himself was dispatching in his own name, not those which others had joined to them as occasion offered.] two, containing that which the rest contain, one of the Roman, the other of the bishop of Aquileia. Further, before all things I beseech thy clemency that thou order Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, who is said to be the author of all the evils, to present himself even against his will: so that nothing may hinder the synod of the bishops who shall have come from ratifying a peace befitting our times.

* OF HONORIUS THE EMPEROR TO ARCADIUS THE KING. * [0512A]

For the third time I write to thy gentleness, asking that the things done in the conspiracy against John, bishop of Constantinople, may obtain correction; and, as it seems, it has not been accomplished. Wherefore I have sent again by the bishops and presbyters, being very careful for the peace of the Church, through which our kingdom also is at peace, that thou deign to command the bishops of the East to come together at Thessalonica. For the bishops of our West too, having chosen men not inclined to wickedness and falsehood, have sent five bishops, two presbyters, and one deacon of the greatest church of the Romans, whom deign to care for with every kind of honour, so that, if they are persuaded that John the bishop has been justly cast out, they may teach me to withdraw from their communion; or, having convicted the bishops of the East of wilful baseness, [0512B] they may turn thee away from their communion. For what the Westerners think about John the bishop, out of all the bishoprics which wrote to me I have subjoined two equivalent to them all, the letter of the bishop of Rome and of Aquileia. And this I beseech thy gentleness before all things: deem it right that Theophilus of Alexandria present himself even against his will, through whom above all all the evils are said to have come about; so that the synod of the bishops who have come, hindered in nothing, may award the peace befitting our times.

*[0512C]INNOCENT to AURELIUS and AUGUSTINE, bishops.*

The return of Germanus, our fellow presbyter, most welcome to me, ought not to be empty of our duty. For to greet those most dear through those dear seems to us in a manner natural and fitting. We desire, therefore, that your brotherhood, most loving ones, rejoice in the Lord, [0513A] and we pray that you repay [nt: Reg.: *respondere*.] equal vows to God for us: because, as you well know, we accomplish more by common and mutual prayers than by single or private ones.
