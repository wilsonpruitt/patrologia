## MONITUM ON THE FOLLOWING EPISTLE.



## 1.

In the Corbie exemplar, written about the middle of the sixth century, in the Colbert one written out in Lombardic letters, and also in the old copies of the Code published by Quesnel, and of the collections of Dionysius, Hadrian, the Spanish, Isidore, etc., this letter is presented. Dionysius divided it into seven chapters, which he placed among the decrees of Innocent in such a way that the first of these chapters is called by him Innocent's decree LI, and the last LVIII. In the Corbie codex, however, it is divided into twelve titles, which we have thought fit to set out at the head of the letter itself. A shorter title is prefixed to the same letter in the ancient copies of Quesnel's Code [0521B] in this manner: « *Rescript of Pope Innocent to the Macedonian bishops upon the inquiry concerning three titles.* I. That he who takes a widow to wife be not admitted to the clergy. II. That he who had a wife before baptism, if, she having died, he has married another after baptism, be held a digamist, and not to be admitted to the clergy. III. That those who, departing from the Catholic Church, receive ordination to the priesthood from heretics, be not reckoned until they wish to return. » And indeed it is chiefly of that threefold question that this letter treats. Looking to the first two, Innocent says, num. I: « Of which things I remember having written long since; now the redoubled inquiry of your report compels me to repeat the form with more evident arguments: » because, clearly, of the first he had written both in epist. 2, to Victricius, c. 5, and in epist. 3, to the synod of Toledo, c. 6, and in epist. 6, to Exsuperius, c. 1, and of the second in the cited letters to Victricius and to the synod of Toledo. Nor is it an obstacle that those letters are by no means general. For when [0521C] the Roman pontiffs wrote back to the questions of particular bishops or churches something that could be of use to other churches, they had commonly been accustomed to communicate it to them. But when Innocent comes to the third question, he so confesses that it demands a longer disputation for its difficulty as to indicate plainly enough that it was new and not yet handled by him.

## 2.

That new question arose on the occasion of those whom Bonosus had ordained after his condemnation. For concerning those who had been ordained by the same man before his condemnation there was no difficulty. These, indeed, the synod of Capua had judged were to be received in their honours. Approving that judgment, Innocent had written to Rufus himself and the other Macedonians by name concerning the clerics of Naissus, « who asserted that before the condemnation of Bonosus they had been ordained by him, as presbyters as well as deacons, » « that if, having abandoned and condemned his error, they wished to be joined to the Church, they should be gladly received » [n: (Epist. 17, 1)]. So the dispute was only about those [0521D] clerics whom Bonosus, already condemned, had ordained. When that question had been carried to Innocent, he observes that a distinction must be drawn between those who, baptized in heresy, come to the Church, and those who go from the Catholic Church to heresy, and thence return to the very Catholic Church from which they had gone out. For the former were at once joined to the faithful by the imposition of the hand alone. But the others were admitted to communion only after a long penance. « Whence, he says [n: (Num. 11)], it is established that those who passed over from the Catholic Church to the condemned Bonosus, and suffered or desired to be ordained by him, ought not to have received the dignity of ecclesiastical ordination: » since indeed [0522A] « not even after penance do the canons themselves by their authority permit one to become a cleric. » Wherefore he remonstrates with the Macedonians [n: (Num. 8)] that « those who passed over from the Catholic Church to heresy not only do not do penance among them, but are even heaped with honour ( *of the clerical state* ). » But concerning the others Innocent notes that « there were ancient rules, handed down by the Apostles or apostolic men, which the Roman church guards and commands to be guarded by those who have heard her, » by which it is prescribed [n: (Ibid.)] « to grant to those coming from the heretics, who were baptized there, only lay communion through the imposition of the hand, and not to put any of them in the place of even the least honour of the clerical state. »

## 3.

But he readily confesses that the necessity of the Church sometimes counselled a departure from this last law. For this cause the Nicene synod had judged that the Novatians were to be received in their honours. Such afterwards was the judgment of Anysius and his associates concerning those who had been ordained by Bonosus before his condemnation. To those also who had received ordination from the followers of Priscillian, [0522B] in the year 400, the first council of Toledo granted the same kind of favour. And Innocent indeed not only approves the statutes of the Nicene synod towards the Navatians, or of Anisius and his associates towards those who had adhered to Bonosius, but also vehemently rebukes and refutes those who were displeased by the indulgence of the council of Toledo towards the followers of Priscillian [n: (Epist. 3, c. 1)]. Yet the pontiff, most loving of equity, so admits and approves those exceptions as to contend sharply that, where a necessity of this kind is absent, one must hold to the general rules of the Church. While he strives to persuade of this, he brings forward the Nicene canon 8 in this manner: « Concerning those who name themselves Cathari, that is, the pure, and at any time come to the catholic Church, it pleased the holy and great synod that, having received the imposition of the hand, they remain so in the clergy. » Whence a new question arises about the genuine understanding of this canon, which it will be helpful to explain here.
