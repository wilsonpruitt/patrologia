## 4.

In no other words does the ancient Code of the canons, [0522C] which Paschasius Quesnel will have to have been in the use of the Roman church, report that canon, in cap. 22, in this letter of Innocent, although in chapter 1 of the same Code, where the Nicene canons are presented together, it is expressed thus: « Concerning those who call themselves Cathari, that is, the pure, if any come to the catholic Church, it pleased the holy council that they be ordained, and so remain in the clergy. » Now since this translation is proper to that code, it ought to have been common to those who used that Code, and so to Innocent himself, if, as is alleged, the aforesaid Code was in the use of the Roman Church in his age. Yet in the translation which Innocent follows, while the order of the words is different, the greatest difference certainly lies in this, that where the Code mentioned presents *that they be ordained,* he gives *that, having received the imposition of the hand.* Whence it follows that this pope used a translator of the Nicene council who had read in can. 8 ὅστε χειροθετουμένους, not Quesnel's Code, in which is inserted the translation of a man who, in place of χειροθετουμένους, had come upon χειροτονουμένους. Feeling that this goes no little against his opinion [0522D] of that Code, the man cited, dissert. 12, cap. 7, devised this cause of the aforesaid difference: « that Pope Innocent, since he was answering the objection of the Macedonians, wished to write out or translate word for word the canon which had been objected, from their letters, » not from his own Code. But in answering thus he is convicted of not having weighed Innocent's words sufficiently. For that pope, having first said by way of preface: « First that canon instituted by the fathers must be set down, so that we may be able to observe, etc., » declares plainly enough that he is setting forth this canon first, not copying or translating it from the letters of others. On the other hand [0523A] it is known that in his own copy of the Nicene council he read in can. 8 χειροθετουμένους, not χειροτονουμένους, and that in the same copy the word χειροθετουμένους had been turned into Latin not by the word *of ordination,* but by that *of the imposition of the hand.* For, expressing this Nicene canon summarily in epist. 2, n. 11, after Siricius, epist. 4, c. 8, he prescribes « that those coming from the Novatians or Montenses be received through the imposition of the hand. » Nor does Siricius confirm this only in the place just cited, but also in epist. 1, n. 2, where he answers Himerius, who was consulting whether those coming from the Arian heresy were to be baptized anew: « Whom we, with the *Novatians* and other heretics, as has been established *in the synod,* join to the assembly of catholics by the invocation of the sevenfold Spirit alone, by the episcopal *imposition of the hand.* » For no prudent man will deny that there by the name of synod the Nicene is understood, and its canon 8 is recalled. It is manifest, therefore, that in this canon Siricius and Innocent read *that, having received the imposition of the hand,* not *that they be ordained.* [0523B] Leo also, treading in their footsteps, in epist. 2, answers the inquiry of Rusticus, 18, in like manner: « Through the *imposition of the hand,* the power of the Holy Spirit having been invoked, which they could not receive from the heretics, they are to be joined to the Catholics. » With these agrees the Corbie codex also, written out in the middle of the sixth century, in which the Nicene canon is reported thus: « Concerning those who surname themselves Cathari, that is, the pure, if at any time they come to the catholic Church, it pleased the holy and great council that, *receiving the imposition of the hand,* they remain so in the clergy. » But in what way the imposition of the hand there required is to be understood, it is of no light moment to explain.

## 5.

Here the man cited, in the aforesaid Dissertation 12, c. 7, lays down two things as certain which it would have been worth the trouble to prove. For first he says: « The Macedonians freely confessed that ordination was signified there [n: (Nicaen. can. 8, manus impositione)]. » And then he adds that on this the Macedonians and Innocent were agreed, [0523C] that if the Bonosians were to be permitted to remain in the clergy, this was to be granted them only *by a repeated ordination.* As regards the Macedonians, if from what one man did, although his deed is disapproved by others, one may conjecture what a whole province or region thinks, it must be granted that they were not far from the opinion which the learned man attributes to them. For in the preceding letter we saw that a certain Rusticius, returning from the fellowship of Bonosus to the catholic Church, « had received the presbyterate by a repeated ordination; » and that this was the greatest hindrance to others who desired to return, « while either they grieve that a man of this kind is retained in the Church, or he judges that others ought to be sinned against in the same way as he acknowledges that a sin was committed in his own case. » But who would wish it to be judged, from the singular sin of one man, which is a horror to the rest, what has been received in the usage of a whole province? On the contrary, from the fact that the Macedonians had permitted Germanio, Lupentius and many others to keep their churches without a repeated ordination, [0523D] it is clear that they were not of that opinion, that those ordained by Bonosus might not remain in the clergy except by a repeated ordination. Innocent also, in the words adduced concerning Rusticius, who suffered himself to be ordained again, signifies that he thinks together with the Macedonian clerics on this, namely that he had committed a sin, and indeed a sin of such a kind as does not permit the same man to be retained in the Church or reckoned among the ecclesiastics. Far, therefore, both the same pope and the Macedonian clerics and the bishops of the same province stood from that opinion, that if the Bonosians were permitted to remain in the clergy, this was to be furnished them by a repeated ordination. But let us take the matter up from further back.

## 6.

In the Church that law has always obtained, that those coming from heresy to the Church, whether clerics or laymen, were received through the imposition of the hand. That this custom had prevailed before the Nicene synod, nay, before [0524A] the times of Pope Stephen and of Cyprian, we have proved above, p. 240 and following, by the testimonies both of Stephen himself and of the councils of Africa under Cyprian, and also of Eusebius and others. But it is plain from the same testimonies that the hand was then imposed on them *unto penance,* not that they might be ordained; and anyone who contended that laymen were ordained by this imposition would offer himself to be laughed at by all. Who, therefore, would assent that the Nicene fathers spoke of the imposition of the hand in another sense, where they decree concerning the Novatians: « It pleased the holy and great synod that, having received the imposition of the hand, they remain so in the clergy? » For us, lest we should think they spoke in another sense, the authority is Siricius, who indeed, in the place already cited, epist. 1, to Himer., c. 1, testifies that the Arians and other heretics are joined at Rome to the assembly of catholics by that very imposition of the hand by which the Nicene synod prescribed that the Novatians were to be received, and that this is guarded by the whole East and West together. Whence it follows that what is prescribed in the Nicene canon concerning receiving [0524B] the imposition of the hand, the whole East and West, tradition persuading them, understood to have been commanded concerning all heretics; but that what is added there, *and so let them remain in the clergy,* was granted to the Novatians alone, Innocent himself being the interpreter. And if towards the Arians or other heretics that imposition of the hand was not a repeated ordination, then neither was it towards the Novatians. Besides this, Innocent below [n: (Num. 8)], having professed that he adheres to the law of his church, which willed that the hand be imposed on those coming from heresy, openly teaches that by this imposition ordination is by no means conferred, since he affirms at the same time that only lay communion is granted through it [n: (Num. 9)]. When, therefore, he says, « This was the chief deliberation of Anysius, our brother of former days, and of other fellow priests, that those whom Bonosus had ordained, lest they should remain with him and no mean scandal should arise, should be received *as ordained,* » he does not understand that *ordained* as though they were to be ordained again before they were received, but [0524C] because those whom Bonosus had ordained were to be held as ordained, and received in the grade and order which they had received.
