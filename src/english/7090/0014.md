## 7.

But, as the same pope warns beforehand [n: (Ibid.)], one thing is to be distinguished as the *lawful order,* another as *usurpation.* Nor should we undeservedly call, with him, *guilty of a usurped dignity* those whom Bonosus, when condemned, had ordained [n: (Num. 12)]: because *they thought themselves to be that* which *had been granted them by no regular reckoning.* And although what had been given remained in them, yet because it remained, to speak with Augustine [n: (Lib. II, cont. Epist. Parmen. n. 29)], *to the punishment of the usurper,* both *of him who unlawfully gave* and *of him who unlawfully received;* they are truly to be said to have received nothing, inasmuch as they could not lawfully use the power made for them against the rules. « For the rest, says the same Augustine [n: (Num. 30)], concerning those who are separated from the unity of the Church, there is now no question but that they both have and can give, and that they have perniciously and hand on perniciously (baptism and ordination) [0524D] outside the bond of peace. For each, » he says a little above [n: (Num. 28)], « is a sacrament, and each is given to a man by a certain consecration; the one when he is baptized, the other when he is ordained: and therefore *in the Catholic Church neither may be repeated.* » And a few things being interposed. « But just as they have in baptism what can be given through them, so in ordination the right of giving; each indeed to their own destruction, as long as they have not the charity of unity: but it is one thing not to have, another to have perniciously, another to have salutarily. » And these things are of so much the greater weight for illustrating and proving Innocent's opinion, because, when Augustine set them out about the year 400, and so before Innocent wrote to the Macedonians, he asserts that he is defending not what he himself privately thought, but « what has been discussed and considered, perfected and defined in the very unity of the whole world » [n: (Ibid.)]. Certainly he would not have been going to say « this was defined in [0525A] the very unity of the whole world » if the agreement of the Roman Church in the same doctrine had been lacking. For how scrupulously the Africans, especially at that time and concerning questions of this kind, took care not to dissent from the decrees of this Church, their embassies and letters directed to Siricius, to Anastasius and to Innocent himself are witnesses. But if we weigh where that definition which Augustine cites was published, there will occur first of all what the Africans write, Cod. can. Eccl. Afr., c. 48: « But we suggest this which was commanded us, which is also seen to have been established in the plenary synod of Capua, that rebaptizings and reordinations may not take place. » Nor can anyone deny that in so celebrated a council nothing was done except according to the mind of Siricius, certainly nothing that he himself did not approve. Although, therefore, the learned man already mentioned more than once most recently asserts [n: (Dissert. 12 in Leon. c. 7)]: « Nor was it a question (between the Macedonians [sic: *Macedodonas*] and Innocent) whether the Bonosians ought to be ordained again, so that they might remain in that order of the clergy [0525B] which they had obtained under Bonosus, on that both were agreed; but whether they were to be permitted to remain in the clergy even *by a repeated ordination;* » one may rightly appeal from his opinion to that of Augustine [n: (Lib. II, cont. Epist. Parmen. n. 28)]: « Concerning those who are separated from the unity of the Church, there is now no question but that they both have and can give (baptism and ordination). And therefore in the Catholic Church neither may be repeated. » Whence, he says, when « it seemed necessary that (those coming from the party of Donatus) should perform the same offices which they were performing, they were not ordained again: but as baptism in them, so ordination remained whole. »

## 8.

If we go even to the very source, that is, to the Greek text of the Nicene council, it will become manifest to us that the fathers of this council were unwilling that ordination be repeated except for those whose baptism they judged null, and therefore to be repeated. Certainly, since in can. 8 they grant that the Novatians, whose baptism they admitted, χειροθετουμένους, [0525C] that is, *having received the imposition of the hand,* remain in the clergy; in can. 19 they are unwilling that the clerics of the Paulianists, whose baptism they rejected, be admitted into the clergy, unless « *they had first been baptized and ordained* ( ἀναβαπτισθέντες χειροτονείσθωσαν ) *by a bishop of the catholic Church.* » For not without cause do they not use the same word in those two places: but they admit to the clergy the first χειροθετουμένους, which sounds simply *to receive the imposition of the hand,* the others χειροτονουμένους, which properly sounds *to receive ordination.* Whence it is clear also that the translation of the Nicene canon which Innocent uses in this letter is more apt and more congruous than that which obtains in Quesnel's Code. Perhaps too the craftsman of the latter, since in can. 19, on the Paulianists, he came upon χειροτονείσθωσαν said, was led thence to judge that in can. 8, on the Novatians, one should read in Greek [sic: χειρονουμένους] and in Latin *that they be ordained.* And from a faulty translation of this kind chiefly that opinion seems to have been born which holds that clerics coming from heresy to the Church were received into the clergy only by a repeated ordination. [0525D] But even if there have from time to time been some who doubted whether sacraments were efficaciously conferred by condemned bishops, yet never was the mind of the Church wavering or doubtful in that matter. At the close of the fifth century, when it was the opinion of certain men that, « the judgment having been pronounced by Pope Felix [n: (II adversus Acacium)], he had afterwards acted inefficaciously in the sacraments which Acacius usurped, » Pope Anastasius answered, epist. I, c. 8: [0526A] « Since the condemned man claimed for himself the name of priest, the swelling of pride was inflicted upon his own head; because the people, who in the mysteries thirsted for his gift, was not shut out, but that soul alone which had sinned was, by just judgment, liable for its own fault. » Which is truly said of him who seeks the sacraments with a simple soul, not of him who abuses the occasion of schism to serve his own ambition. In the ninth century a like controversy arose concerning the clerics ordained by Ebbo after he had been cast down from the episcopate. But the third council of Soissons, in the year 866, decreed concerning them that « they should be received in their grades, according to the indulgence of the Nicene synod concerning those whom Meletius, when condemned, ordained, and according to the tradition of the African council » towards the Donatists. See also the second council of Nicaea, Labbe vol. VII, from p. 66 to 78. Thus the Nicene fathers judged that the Meletians and Novatians were to be received in their honours, the Africans certain of the Donatists, the synod of Capua and Innocent those ordained by Bonosus before his condemnation, Anastasius II those [0526B] whom Acacius, when condemned, ordained. Besides, Gregory I, in the epistle formerly 61, in the month of July, Indiction IV, to Quiricus, judged that even the Nestorians likewise were to be received in their honours. Other like judgments and decrees we pass over in silence.

## EPISTLE [nt: In the edition of the Councils, 22. And what was 17 is now 19.] XVII. From Dionysius Exiguus.



## I.

*That if a priest or anyone of the clergy has married a widow, or a woman put away, he lose his office.* -- II. *That if anyone as a catechumen had a wife, and, she having died, has after baptism obtained another, he be not a cleric.* --III. *That those ordained by heretics be not received in the clergy.* --IV. *That in ordinations crimes or vices are not rightly believed to be taken away.* [0526C] --V. *That those who were ordained by the heretic Bonosus were received for this reason, that scandal might not remain for the Church.* --VI. *That in the Church the sin of a people is wont to be passed over unpunished.* --VII. *That the apostolic see had been taken by surprise, and has changed its sentence for the better.*

*From the ancient Corbie codex.*

## I.

*Of those who are alleged to have taken two wives.* --II. *That he who as a catechumen had and lost a wife ought not to be called a digamist.* --III. *In what way crimes are remitted in baptism.* --IV. *Of sons who were born before baptism.* --V. *Of catechumens who have applied themselves to holy virtues.* --VI. *Of heretical clerics, in what way they ought to be reconciled.* --VII. *That the sacrilegious [0526D] and adulterers ought not to be admitted to the clergy.* --VIII. *And in what way heretics are received.* --IX. *Of those who call themselves cathari and pure.* --X. *Of those returning from the Novatians and heretics.* --XI. *Of catholic clerics who pass over to heresy.* --XII. *Of the reception of Fontinus and Eustasius.*
