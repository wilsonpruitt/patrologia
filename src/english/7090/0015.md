## 1.

Great rejoicing took hold of me when, after so many perils of the whole world, so to speak, Vitalis the archdeacon, the bearer of your letters, sent from those parts, arrived all the way to us. When we had seen him, we at once, as was fitting, inquired about your state. But when [nt: The Corbie manuscript with one Colbert manuscript: *reperiremus*.] we found that you were living as we wished, we returned abundant thanks to our God, who protects his servants and those who serve his altars both in [0527B] adversity, and deigns to govern them in prosperity. When he had delivered the letters, I ordered them to be gone through at once: in which I perceived many things set down which might bring [nt: The same manuscripts: *nostris ut inducerent*. The others, with Merlin: *nostri inducerent*. *Injicerent* would not displease.] astonishment upon our minds, and make us doubt not a little whether we were thinking otherwise, or those things were set down just as they sounded. When I had had them read over again several times, I noticed that [nt: From the better manuscripts the word *aliquam* is recalled here, which was formerly wanting. Innocent had already forbidden, in epist. 2, n. 7 and 8, and also in epist. 3, n. 11, that anyone be admitted to the clergy who had married either a widow or a second wife, even if he had taken the first before baptism. It is probable, moreover, that after the example of Siricius he took care that letters and decrees of this kind should travel through the provinces, as he himself speaks in epist. 1, n. 2, lest any of them should plead ignorance. Certainly that about which he testifies that he had written before falls under the two aforesaid heads, not under the other two, which are treated next.] some injury was being done to the apostolic see, to which the report sent was running as to the head of the churches, whose sentence was still being drawn into doubt. Hence, concerning matters on which I remember having written long since, the redoubled inquiry [nt: In the manuscripts of the collections of Dionysius, Hadrian and the Spanish, *relationis vestrae* is wanting. In the Colbert Lombardic codex *relationis* stands, and in the other Colbert and the old Corbie *relegationis*, without *vestrae* added. Then all the other manuscripts subjoin *geminata percontatio vestra*. Merlin printed *gemina percontatio vestra*. The old copies of Quesnel's Code preserve the common reading.] of your report compels me now to repeat the form with more evident arguments.

## CAP. I.



## 2.

Those [n: (Ivo p. 8, c. 503)] who are alleged to have taken widows [0528A] as wives I have learned not only became clerics, but even reached the fillets of the highest priesthood: which no one is unaware is against the precepts of the law. For since Moses the lawgiver cries out: *Let the priest take a virgin to wife [n: (Lev. XXII, 13, 14)]*; and, lest anything in this precept be thought ambiguous, he added, *not a widow, nor one put away.* Against this precept, set under [nt: So the better manuscripts. But other books: *subnixum*. Pithou: *subnexum*.] divine authority, no defence of any other command is opposed except your custom: which, as you yourselves confess, was established from ignorance, to speak more modestly, not from apostolic tradition and sound reason. But let your love know that we, and all the churches through East and West, by no means admit these things; and do not take up such men into even the last place of the ecclesiastical order [nt: Agreeing with the first part of this decree, the first council of Valence, in the year 374, can. 1, decreed that henceforth no one *of the digamists or of the husbands of women married twice could be ordained a cleric*, whether they had fallen into digamy before baptism or after. Yet it does not dare to remove those who had been ordained up to that time. From that severity of discipline the Gallican councils, the first of Orange, can. 25, and the second of Arles, can. 45, later relaxed somewhat, thinking it enough to forbid that men of this kind be promoted beyond the subdiaconate. Nor were the Spaniards, the Moors or the Italians more observant of the apostolic rule in this matter, as are witness the first epistle of Siricius, to Himerius of Tarragona, n. 12, Leo's epistle 1, to the Moors, n. 3 and 5, and his epistle 4, to the bishops of Campania, Picenum and Tuscia, n. 3.] [0528B], and, if they are found, remove them.

## CAP. II.



## 3.

Next it is set down that one ought not [n: (Dist. 26, c. 3; Ivo, p. 1, c. 292, et p. 8, c. 303)] to be called a digamist who as a catechumen had and lost a wife, if after baptism he has obtained another; and that she is to be regarded as the first who was joined to the new man: because that marriage was washed away through the sacrament of baptism together with the other crimes. And since this is said of one, certainly if, while placed in the old man, he had three wives, she who is fourth after baptism will be, for those who interpret thus, the first: [nt: Quesnel: *primae virginis nomen*, against the credit of the manuscripts and the mind of Innocent. In the other editions: *prima virginis aeque nomen*. In several manuscripts: *virgini aeque nomen*.] and she who was married in the fourth place will receive the name of a virgin. Who, I pray, would not see that this is against the precept of the Apostle, who says *(I Tim. III, 2; [0529A] Tit. I, 6) that a man of one wife* ought to be made priest?

## 4.

But it is objected that in baptism everything whatever that was done in the old man is loosed. Tell me, I speak with your leave: are only crimes remitted in baptism, or also those things which are fulfilled according to [nt: So the manuscripts of better note. But Merlin, Crabbe and the Roman edition, with the manuscripts of the collections of Dionysius and the Isidorian: *Domini praecepta*. Our reading is confirmed by that with which number 5 closes: *quod Lex praecepit, et Deus junxit*.] the precepts of the Law and the institutions of God? To take a wife is a crime, or is not a crime. If it is a crime, then, begging pardon beforehand, I would say, the author will be at fault who, when he himself joined them thus in paradise, blessed them to commit crimes. But if it is not a crime, because what God joined [n: (Gen. I, 28)] it is impious to call a crime; and Solomon added: *For a wife is prepared for a man by God [n: (Prov. XIX, 14)]* [var: Vulg. *a Domino autem proprie uxor prudens*, "but a prudent wife is properly from the Lord"]: how is that believed to have been remitted among the crimes which is read to have been accomplished with God as its author? [0529B]

## 5.

What is reckoned of the sons of such men [n: (28, q. I, c. 3)]? Will those not be admitted to the fellowship of the inheritance who were begotten of her who was wife before baptism? And are they to be called either natural or spurious, because there is no lawful marriage, as it seems to you, except that which is taken up [nt: The manuscripts of Dionysius and some others: *assumetur*.] after baptism? The Lord himself, when he was asked by the Jews whether it was lawful to put away a wife, and was explaining that it ought not to be done, added: *What therefore God has joined, let no man put asunder [n: (Matth. XIX, 6)]*. And lest he be believed to have spoken of those who [nt: So the manuscripts, with great agreement. But Crabbe and the Roman edition: *qui*. Further, the verb *sortiuntur* is to be understood passively, just as if *accipiuntur* stood there.] are obtained after baptism, let them remember that this was both asked by Jews and answered to Jews. I ask, and I ask anxiously, if the wife of him who before was a catechumen and afterwards is one of the faithful is one and the same, and he begot sons of her when he was a catechumen, [0529C] and afterwards others when one of the faithful: whether they are to be called brothers, or whether they are afterwards, the father having died, not to have the fellowship of dividing the inheritance, from whom spiritual regeneration is believed to have taken away the name of sons? Since it is absurd to think and judge so, what reason is there (a plague on it) for this to be defended, and to be tossed about by an empty opinion rather than strengthened by any authority, [0530A] since what the Law commanded, and God [nt: Several manuscripts: *jungit*. One of the Colbert: *indulget*; Pithou and Quesnel: *injungit*. It is plain that the words of Christ, *Quod Deus junxit*, etc., are looked to here. But what is subjoined after the words *Deus junxit*, in Merlin *et lex junxit*, in Crabbe *et lex jungit*, rests on no authority.] joined, cannot be reckoned among sins?

## 6.

If a catechumen has applied himself to the virtues, has followed humility, has held to patience, has done almsgiving, has by whatever means rescued those appointed to death, has shuddered at adulteries, has held to chastity; I ask whether, when he has been made one of the faithful, he loses these things, because through baptism all that the old man did is thought to be taken away? Let us look at the gentile man Cornelius, who in his prayers [nt: One of the manuscripts, a Colbert: *orationibus deditum atque eleemosynis per revelationem Petrum evocasse, ipsumque vidisse*. From it Quesnel snatched *orationibus deditum*, and then from Crabbe and the other editions after him subjoined *et eleemosynis vacantem per revelationem Angelum Petrumque*. Merlin had earlier printed *orationibus et eleemosynis per revelationem Angelum Petrumque*. Our reading is that of all the manuscripts, with the one Colbert named above excepted.] and almsgivings saw a revelation, and Peter himself: were these things taken away [nt: Six manuscripts of good note: *abluta*: which can be understood thus, that as stains are wont to vanish when washed away, so the merits of prayers and almsgivings, washed away, are judged to have perished. In which sense we heard above, n. 3, *conjugium per baptismi sacramentum cum caeteris criminibus ABLUTUM*. But if *abluta* is not foreign to Innocent's mind, what we read just before, *quia per baptismum, totum quod vetus homo gesserit putatur AUFERRI*, persuades that the word *ablata* is no more alien to it.] from him through baptism, on account of which baptism is seen to have been granted to him? If it is so believed, believe me, there is no small error: because whatever has been well done, and kept according to the precepts of the law, cannot [nt: The editions after Dionysius and the Isidorian: *non potest facientibus deperire. Nuptiarum ergo copula quia Dei mandato perficitur, non potest*: they are corrected by the authority of the manuscripts, which are wont to be more sincere.] perish for the doer; and that which, [0530B] because it is brought about by God's command, cannot be called sin: and what is not sin ought by no means to be loosed among sins [nt: Crabbe added *credi* here: a word the following editors seized on, although it is absent from the manuscripts.]. And it will be sound to judge that the name of the former wife cannot be abolished, since it was not remitted as a sin, because it was accomplished by the will of God.

## CAP. III.



## 7.

[n: (I, q. I, c. 17; Ivo p. 6, c. 59.)] We have come to the third question, which for its difficulty demands a longer disputation: since we say that those ordained by heretics have a head wounded through that imposition of the hand. [And where a wound has been inflicted, medicine must be applied, that it may be able to receive health. Which health, following after a wound, cannot be without a scar:] and where the remedy of penance is necessary, there the honour of ordination [0530C] cannot have place [nt: The word *decernimus*, which was added here in the Roman edition and the later editions of the councils, we have not met in even one written codex, nor in Merlin, Crabbe, etc. At the opening of this disputation it will be helpful to have observed the difference which lies between ordination and the honour of ordination. And indeed Innocent does not deny that ordination can be conferred by heretics, but contends that *the honour of the ordination* conferred by them *cannot have place*. Nay, he himself asserts at length in the preceding epistle 5 that for an ordination of this kind, where the utility of the Church demands it, its honour can be preserved.] [n: (Num. XIX, 22)]. For if, as is read, what the unclean man has touched shall be unclean: how shall that be granted to him which cleanness and purity have been wont to receive? But on the other hand it is asserted that he who has lost honour cannot give honour: nor has that man received anything, because there was nothing in the giver [nt: Two manuscripts: *in dante remanserat*. In imitation of this place, those who in the Capitulary of the year 803, c. 4, in Baluze p. 381, made definitions against the episcopal functions of the chorepiscopi, expressed their own words: *that no one had anywhere received anything of the episcopal ministry from the chorepiscopi, because there was nothing in the giver which any of them could have received; since what none of them had, he could not give*. But we should not wish Innocent's words to be explained to the sense of this Capitulary. By his argument only this is made out: that by one who lacked the lawful power of ordaining, a lawful ordination could not have been conferred.] which he might receive. We assent, and it is [0531A] true. Certainly, because what he did not have he could not give, he gave at any rate the condemnation which he had, through the depraved imposition of the hand: and [nt: So the old Corbie codex and the other Colbert, with two others favouring, in which *comparticeps factus est* is likewise read, not to mention Pithou, in which *factus est* remains. But other books: *qui particeps effectus est damnato*. Long before, according to Innocent's mind, the council of Laodicea, can. 32, had decreed: *One ought not to receive the blessings of heretics, since these are curses rather than blessings.* Further, he who is made a partaker of condemnation is not without share in reproach; and therefore he is judged by the apostolic sentence unworthy of the honour of ordination.] how he who has been made a fellow partaker of condemnation ought to receive honour, I cannot find.

## CAP. IV.



## 8.

But it is said that the true and just blessing of a lawful priest takes away every fault which had been put in by the faulty one. Then, if it is so, let the sacrilegious, adulterers and those guilty of all crimes be brought forward to ordination, since through the blessing of ordination crimes or faults [nt: In two manuscripts: *possunt auferri*.] are thought to be taken away. Let there be no place for penance, since ordination can furnish what a long satisfaction has been wont to furnish. But [nt: A council of Italy, recalled in the Code of the canons of the African church, cap. 56, had decreed that law, or rather had confirmed it as handed down by the Apostles or apostolic men. There the Africans, although they acknowledge the equity of this law, yet decree an embassy to Anastasius, Innocent's predecessor, to ask that something of its severity be relaxed for them, because of the shortage of clerics with which their churches were labouring. Complying with the same law, Augustine, in the epistle formerly 73, now 245, n. 2, wrote to Possidius: *As to ordaining one who was baptized in the party of Donatus, I cannot be your authority:* although he at once adds: *For it is one thing to do it if you are compelled, another to counsel that you do it.* By all means to be read is the letter of the Africans to John II, epistle viii, in which they ask in what way the priests of the Arians ought to be received into the catholic faith, whether in their honours or in lay communion. And presently they add: *From the silent movements of all our colleagues we have perceived that it pleases no one that they be received in their honours.* And they add also below: *We consult not only about the priests, but also about the very little ones baptized among them, whether they are wont or ought to be brought forward to the clerical state, if they have asked.* The opinion of Cyprian on that matter, in epist. 72, to Stephen, is this: *It is enough that pardon be given to such men returning; but faithlessness ought not to be promoted in the house of faith.*] IT IS THE LAW OF OUR CHURCH, for those coming from the heretics who were nevertheless baptized there, to grant through the imposition of the hand [0531B] only lay communion, and not to put any of them in the place of even the least honour of the clerical state. But those who [nt: The editions, with only the manuscripts of the Spanish collection and the Isidorian: *a catholica fide*. More truly other manuscripts: *a Catholica*; supply *Ecclesia*. So also below, n. 11: *De Catholica ad haeresim transiens*; likewise, *De Catholica ad Bonosum transierunt*. And this manner of speaking is familiar to other writers too, and by name to Augustine.] passed over from the Catholic Church to heresy, whom it is fitting to receive in no other way than through penance, among you not only do not do penance, but are even heaped with honour.

## CAP. V.



## 9.

But [nt: One of the Colbert manuscripts with the old Corbie: *Sed ante duodecim fratres quondam nostri*: corruptly. From the epistle of Siricius, 9, n. 1, we had already learned that the synod of Capua delegated the hearing of the case of Bonosus to Anysius and other bishops of Illyricum, and especially to the Macedonians; now we are taught what their judgment on this case was; and while Innocent approves it, he shows that he is far from thinking the ordination of heretics void and altogether null.] this was the chief deliberation of Anysius, our brother of former days, and of other fellow priests: that those whom Bonosus had ordained, lest they should remain with him and no mean scandal should arise, should be received as ordained. [nt: The same manuscripts: *Vicensimus*: faultily. Nor better another Colbert: *Vicibus, ut opinor, ambiguis*. Further, it was in doubt whether the honour of ordination was always to be kept for those ordained by a heretic, or always to be denied. Innocent resolves and overcomes this doubt, when he answers that it is to be denied them in such a way that, when necessity demands or grave scandal is feared, he judges it is to be kept for them; and he praises the judgment of Anysius and his associates, by which, Bonosus being set apart, they allowed those ordained by him their honours, lest they should remain with him.] We have overcome, as I think, the doubtful points. [0532A] Now therefore [n: (I, q. 7, c. 7; Ivo in prologo)] it is established that what was decreed as a remedy and for the necessity of the time did not exist originally, and that there were ancient rules, handed down by the Apostles or apostolic men, which the Roman church guards and commands to be guarded by those who have been accustomed to hear her. But the necessity of the time greatly demanded that this be done. Therefore [n: (I, q. I, c. 41; Ivo in prologo)] what necessity found as a remedy, when the necessity ceases, what was pressing ought certainly to cease likewise: because the lawful order is one thing, usurpation another, which time drives to be done for the present.
