## 10.

But the canons established at Nicaea permitted this to be done concerning the Novatians. First that canon instituted by the fathers must be set down, so that we may be able to observe either what or in what manner it was meant or commanded by them. [0532B] Concerning those, it says, who name themselves Cathari, that is, the pure, and at any time come to the catholic Church, it pleased the holy and great synod that, having received the imposition of the hand, they remain so in the clergy [n: (Nic. syn. c. 8)]. But I can say that this was commanded of the Novatians alone, and does not pertain to the clerics of other heresies. For if indeed they were defining so concerning all, they would have added that those returning from the Novatians and other heretics ought to be received [nt: The editions: *in suum ordinem*. Next Quesnel: *suscipi*; *quod ita non esse*, the negative particle being added against the credit of the manuscripts and the mind of Innocent: who indeed, having first said by way of preface, *Possum dicere de solis hoc Novatianis esse praeceptum*, then, being about to prove that he can say this in fact, rightly adds, *Quod ita esse*. In place of these words, in the manuscripts of Dionysius or Hadrian, *Quod si ita esset* has been very badly substituted; and from there the author of the Spanish collection, Isidore and Merlin took that reading. Opposite this place, in the Colbert Lombardic manuscript, this marginal note is written: *He speaks of the Novatians: When they have returned to the catholic Church, and been received through the imposition of the hand, let these remain in the clergy. Let the clerics of other heresies, when they have returned, receive only lay communion.*] in their own order. That this is so, that also above all which was said of the Paulianists will be able to confirm, those coming from whom they command even [0533A] to be baptized. When they give orders concerning the Paulianists, will all who return from heretics be to be baptized after this example [n: (Nic. syn. c. 19)]? Since no one would dare to do this, reason itself shows that it was commanded of them only. Finally, Peter and John complete by the imposition of the hand alone those duly baptized by Philip the evangelist. But those whom the apostle Paul found baptized with the baptism of John, and asked whether they had received the Holy Spirit, and who confessed that they had not even heard that name, he ordered to be baptized [n: (Act. VIII, 17; XIX, 2 seqq.)]. You see, therefore, that those duly baptized cannot be repeated with that gift: and that those otherwise washed with water alone are perceived to need to be baptized in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. So too it is read over again, by lucid [0533B] manifestation, that the command was concerning the Novatians only. Manifest reason declares that the distinction was drawn between those two heresies for this cause: because the Paulianists by no means baptize in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit [n: (I; q. I, c. 53)]; and the Novatians baptize in the same tremendous and venerable names, nor was any question ever stirred among them concerning the unity of the divine power, that is, of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit; and therefore [nt: Several manuscripts, with Dionysius: *de omnibus*. It pleases more, with the printed editions and also the Corbie manuscript and two Colbert: *omnibus*; supply *haeresibus*. By the examples of the Novatians, whose baptism is accepted because they sin in nothing in conferring this sacrament, and of the Paulianists, whose baptism is rejected because they sin in the same, the Nicene synod gave us a pattern of those whose baptism is either to be admitted or to be refused.], all being set apart, this one alone was chosen, to which they believed this should be granted, because they sinned in nothing in the sacrament of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.

## 11.

But if anyone, passing over from the Catholic Church to heresy, or one of the faithful [nt: The Isidorian, after the Spanish collection, and from there the Roman edition and the Councils: *ab apostasia revertens et resipiscens*.] turned back to apostasy, coming to his senses, [0534A] wishes to return, will he be able by the same reckoning to be permitted [nt: The Roman edition: *admitti*. Then Dionysius with the older manuscripts: *quorum commissum*. What Innocent says next, that by the canons it is not lawful to become a cleric after penance, rests on rules established before the Nicene synod, which that very synod marks in can. 10 in these words: *Whoever of the lapsed have been promoted to the order of the clergy through ignorance or through the connivance of those ordaining, this does not prejudice the rule of the Church; for once known they are deposed.* Augustine, epist. 185, n. 41, teaches that this rule, which Siricius also confirms, epist. 1, n. 18, was prudently established, both so that, through despair of temporal eminence, the medicine of humility might be greater and truer, and so that the conversion of those returning might be more sincere; since perhaps the fathers had experienced the feigned penances of some through affected powers of honours. A third reason for the same decree, no less firm, Siricius adds in the place cited: namely that *although cleansed, those who were formerly vessels of vices ought not to take up any instruments for administering the sacraments.* A fourth Innocent sets out below. A fifth Simplicius touches on, epist. 18: *lest one who is convicted of having long been perverse should, under the appearance of one coming back, seek not the remedy of salvation, but find the means of propagating depravity.*] to the clergy, whose offence can be abolished only by a long penance? Not even after penance do the canons themselves by their authority permit one to become a cleric. Hence it is established that those who passed over from the Catholic Church to the condemned [nt: That is, after he was condemned in the council of the bishops of Illyricum, to which his case had been carried by the synod of Capua. Hence it is clear that in that whole third question the dispute was about the clerics ordained by Bonosus alone. Nor did the question turn on this, whether those whom that heretic had ordained before the council of Illyricum were to be received. For in this council Anysius with his colleagues, condemning Bonosus, had decreed that those ordained by him, lest they should remain with him, were to be received in their grades; and Innocent approves this decree above, n. 9. But the matter concerned those who had been ordained by him after Bonosus had been condemned by the judgment of Anysius and the Macedonians.] Bonosus, and suffered or desired to be ordained by him, ought not to have taken up the dignity of ecclesiastical ordination, since, deserting the common judgment of all the churches, they judged that their own vanity was to be followed peculiarly in Bonosus.

## 12.

But [n: (1, q. I, c. III)] it is established that many suffered violence, and were dragged unwilling, and were ordained by him while resisting. Let whoever believes this tell me, I wish it: if after the ordination [nt: Quesnel alone: *Bonoso*. Yet the other books, not badly: *Bonosi*, that is, conferred by Bonosus.] of Bonosus he was not present when he performed [0534B] the sacraments; if he was not made a partaker of his communion; if, departing at once from his most wicked conventicle, he returned to the Church: such a man can have some colour of excuse. But those who returned to the Church after either a year [nt: In the printed editions, *aut mensem .... intelligimus eos qui in* (Quesnel: *qui a*) *Catholica*. Quesnel below: *reperire locum*; Merlin and Crabbe: *recipere locum*; where other books keep *invenire locum*. Here Innocent sets out a new reason why he shows himself difficult towards those who come ordained by heretics, so that they be not admitted to the clergy; and he observes that it was the custom of most heretics that any Catholics, however corrupt and depraved in morals, sometimes even expelled or degraded from the Church for crimes, who passed over to them, were not only taken in by them without the satisfaction of penance, but were sometimes even heaped with new honours. For the devil, the parent of the heretical sects, is not at all anxious about purity of morals. The Church, on the other hand, never rejoices in a multitude of hypocrites or of the wicked. Certainly he betrays himself a lover of himself, not of God, who honours those given over to him and does not care that they be good.] or months, it is understood that, because they knew that in the Catholic Church they could not receive ordination on account of their vices, they therefore went to him who made unlawful ordinations everywhere and without any examination, believing that through that appointment they could find in the Catholic Church a place of which they had before despaired. Now I ask what remains: he who returned after a month or more, when he trusted that he had been ordained a presbyter by Bonosus, [0535A] if he did not perform [nt: The copies of the collection of Hadrian: *si non sacra confecit*. Then Crabbe in the margin, and from there the Roman edition and the Councils: *si non populis distribuit*: which is also read in the Pithou exemplar of Quesnel's Code.] the sacraments, if he did not give them to the peoples, if he did not complete Masses according to custom, what you judge of these things, I ask you, set forth more openly. In sum, certainly those who received nothing from Bonosus are guilty of a usurped dignity, who claimed for themselves [nt: In the printed editions, as in the Noyon manuscript, the words *aut non vindicaverunt* are wanting, which are presented in the other manuscripts with great agreement. Hence the sentence of Innocent is had fuller and more whole, pronouncing generally of those who were ordained by Bonosus against right, that whether they claimed for themselves, or did not claim, the authority of performing the sacraments, they are guilty of a usurped dignity, inasmuch as they received nothing of regular and lawful power: and so in vain *they thought themselves to be that which* (badly in the Roman edition and the Councils, *quod de eis*) *had been granted them by no regular reckoning*. Here indeed Quesnel omits the word *regulari*, but it stands in all the books, both printed and written, except that in its place one Colbert faultily has *relaxandi*, and Pithou *relaxari*.] or did not claim the authority of performing the sacraments, and thought themselves to be that which had been granted them by no regular reckoning.

## CAP. VI.



## 13.

Let your love therefore perceive that such things have passed up to now, and take note that what, as you say, necessity certainly commanded, churches already set in peace [nt: Quesnel, with two manuscripts: *non posse praesumere*. If anything were to be added, *debere* would be more apt than *posse*. For Innocent teaches that what necessity commanded to churches when disturbed is by no means to be presumed by churches set in peace. And here, therefore, he not obscurely confesses that he would grant the Bonosians the honours which he now denies, if there were an equal necessity.] do not presume. But [n: (I, q. 7, c. 14)], as often happens, whenever a sin is committed by peoples or by a crowd, because vengeance cannot be taken on all on account of the multitude, [0535B] it is wont [nt: Four manuscripts, with Quesnel: *solere*. Several others, with Merlin and Crabbe: *soleat*. These two editors, as also Quesnel, then subjoin *transire. Priora ergo*. Our reading is that of the more correct manuscripts. Innocent, ever consistent with himself, here confirms what he had already taught in epist. 3, c. 4, concerning offences which are either to be handled more gently or tolerated, or severely restrained and punished. Akin to this is what Augustine writes, book III, against the Epistle of Parmenian, n. 11: *For rebuke by the many cannot be salutary unless he is rebuked who has not a multitude as his associate. In truth,* he says, some things being interposed, *if the contagion of sinning has invaded a multitude, the severe mercy of divine discipline is necessary: for counsels of separation are both empty and pernicious and sacrilegious.* But, as he premises in the same place, n. 13, *when the crime of anyone is so known that he has either no defenders at all, or not such that through them a schism could come about, let not the severity of discipline sleep, in which the correction of depravity is the more efficacious the more diligent the preservation of charity.* So the prudent pastor, moderating discipline according to the different condition of the times, is more indulgent towards some of those ordained by Bonosus, whom he thinks it enough to leave to the judgment of God, and more severe towards others, whom he judges are to be punished according to the prescription of the stricter discipline.] to pass unpunished; I say that the former things are to be left to the judgment of God, and that for the rest one must take precaution with the greatest solicitude.

## CAP. VII.



## 14.

I shall come now to Photinus, as it were a certain very great theme, and, what is to me anxious and most difficult, I shall turn over again the sentences of my elders [n: (36, q. 9, c. 5)]. Concerning him too, by some arrangement, [0536A] as you yourselves also remember, something certainly rather grave had been decreed. But since you show that this was obtained from this see by stealth through a false rumour, as you assert, and drawn out by snares, since the matter has returned to safety; [nt: The particles *et vel*, first suppressed by Dionysius and then passed over in the Spanish collection and the Isidorian, are recalled from six manuscripts of sincerer note. By their help Innocent softens and removes the odium of the word *veniam*, as though he said: *Photinus indeed, whom you testify was not guilty, needs no pardon: but if he had been guilty, I judge it harsher not to ratify even the pardon which you granted him.*] even pardon too, so far as this, at your striving, we substitute after the condemnation [nt: The editions, with several manuscripts: *more*: from a repetition of the last letter of the preceding word, familiar enough to copyists. We prefer, with the old Corbie manuscript and one Colbert, *ore*, that is, pronounced by the apostolic see. And this sentence against Photinus had been pronounced not by Innocent himself, but either by Anastasius or by Siricius. Wherefore he had said beforehand, *Majorum meorum revolvam sententias*: which, although he felt it *anxious* for him, the love of equity and justice nevertheless overcame the feeling of anxiety.] by apostolic mouth; and not to give consent to such great assertions of yours, you being so good, so dear, we judge harder than all hard things. For your approval therefore, dearest brethren, and your sentence and demand, hold it lawful to appoint Photinus bishop, as you pray, and receive our sentence, changed for the better by your labour or testimony, having gained [nt: Crabbe and several manuscripts: *votis*: the letter s wrongly doubled, with which the following word takes its beginning. Next, where some manuscripts present *Eustasium*, others *Eustachium* or *Eustatium* or *Eustathium*, and all *comprobatum*, Quesnel substituted *Eustathius... comprobatus*, I think in order to fit the sentence to the laws of grammar. Here Innocent begins to ask the Macedonians to pardon and remit the sentence which they had pronounced against Eustathius, just as at their request he himself had pardoned and remitted that which the apostolic see had brought forth against Photinus.] your wish.

## 15.

And do not expect that Eustathius, very often approved by me, [0536B] be stripped of the grace of the diaconate. For I gladly hear you when you are anxious for salvation [n: (II Cor. II, 10)]: against the head, even if it must be done, I do not gladly admit it. To whom you stretch out the hand, I stretch it out with you: to whom I stretch it out, stretch it out with me. For this is the apostolic kindness declared to the Corinthians, that the good should always follow without distinction a sentence pronounced [nt: In the printed editions and several manuscripts: *ductam*. In the old Corbie and one Colbert: *ducta ac reducta sententia*. What stands in the other Colbert pleases more: *dictam*. Innocent looks to the words of Paul, both in I Cor. v, 4, where he pronounces sentence against the Corinthian guilty of incest, and in II Cor. II, 6, where he draws back and revokes the sentence pronounced.] and drawn back in one spirit. For, I confess, [0537A] this matter first sits well with me concerning Eustathius the deacon: that he neither spoke anything against the faith, nor admitted one who spoke, nor have I recognized that he committed any crime unto death, either ever bandied about or feigned. And he who is sound in these things, if in some way he does not have amiable manners, is to be held as less loved for the time, not as an enemy [nt: The word *aestimatur* Quesnel passed over. In its place in the other editions stands *aestimatus* or *existimatus*. Hence it is gathered that Eustathius had been not only stripped of the grace of the diaconate but also struck with anathema: which was very harsh against a man of whom no one ever either bandied about or feigned that anything had been said by him against the faith, or any crime unto death committed.] is he judged to be delivered over to the devil for ever. I recognize that amid those rivalries and whirlwinds he thought differently against the zeal of many, I do not say of what kind, and that hence, although the affairs and calamities themselves have been amended, up to [nt: So the manuscripts. But the editions: *adhuc*, *usque* being omitted. Then the Pithou manuscript: *Dyzoniani*. Some others: *Dezoniani* or *Diozeniani*. Next most: *et Quiriaci*.] this point he has not been able, on the commendation of Dizonianus and Cyriacus the subdeacons, to [nt: The Roman edition and the Councils, following the collections of Dionysius, the Spanish and the Isidorian: *emendationem reviviscere. Compescite, quaeso*: they are corrected by the authority of six manuscripts of better note.] revive among you. Extinguish, I ask, from him and from those aforesaid, the [nt: So one Colbert codex and the other, Pithou's, with Quesnel. But others: *animos ac*.] spirited and unjust [0537B] rivalries of whomsoever you see, so that he himself, whole among us, and restored among you together with his own [nt: The editions after Dionysius and the Isidorian: *reparatus mercatur pacem, non fictam pervidens charitatem. Omnibus igitur vobis ac singillatim occurrat charitas, et vinculis quae ..... connexa laetetur*: they are corrected by the help of the better manuscripts, in three of which *charitati* stands in place of *charitatis*. The sentence would be fuller if *charitatisque vinculis* were read.], perceiving a deep peace and an unfeigned charity, may meet you all, and each one singly, and, joined together with you equally for ever by the bonds of charity which are in no way loosed from Christ, may rejoice in the Lord. Given on the Ides of December [nt: In the printed editions *Flavio* is wanting. Next, in three Colbert manuscripts and the Pithou, *Constantino* stands. From the Corbie and Fossés manuscripts, in which *Flaviis Constantio* remains, we conjecture that *et Constante* is to be added. For in this year 414 Constans had been created consul together with Constantius.] [n: (13 Decemb. ann. 414)], Flavius Constantius, a most illustrious man, being consul.

## EPISTLE XVIII [nt: In the edition of the councils, 7. And what was 18 is now 21. It is found not in Mercator's collection only, but also in the Spanish and in that of Dionysius Exiguus. With him it holds the 55th place among the decrees of Innocent. We put it back here, both lest the things which pertain to the affairs of Macedonia be disjoined further, and above all because there is no lack of a conjecture from which we may judge that it was written while the peace of the Antiochene church was being negotiated, and so about the year 414. For Innocent relates below, epist. 22, that this Maximianus, one of the legates from Macedonia, took part in that great business. And this perhaps was the chief cause why this Maximianus and his associate made a longer stay in the City than was hoped.]. *On Bubalius and Taurianus, condemned by the bishops of the province, whose sentence the apostolic see took care to reconsider.*

*[0538A]INNOCENT TO RUFUS, GERONTIUS, SOPHRONIUS, FLAVIANUS, MACEDONIUS, PROSDOCIUS AND [nt: So the better manuscripts. But the Navarre: *Aristeo*; and another, with the printed editions: *Oristeo*. The names of this Aristeas and of Prosdocius do not appear in the inscription of the preceding letter, in which we read the rest named above. From which one may conjecture that in the preceding letter neither the bishops of Macedonia alone nor all of them are named, but in this one both all and only the bishops of this province, their legates being joined in the body of the letter.] ARISTEAS, bishops established throughout Macedonia.*

## 1.

The delay of our fellow bishops Maximianus and [nt: In the letter immediately preceding, as also in Chrysostom's epistle 165, likewise written to the Macedonians, *Eugenius* is joined to *Maximiano*. Hence there is a suspicion that here too *Eumenii* has crept in in place of *Eugenii*.] Eumenius, or rather the importunity of the times, has brought it about that you have repeated your complaint about Bulbalius and [nt: The Royal codex of the pure collection of Dionysius shows *Thariano* here, and *Tariano* below. Others of the collection of Hadrian have *Tariano* here, but *Tauriano* below: which the manuscripts of the Spanish collection and the Isidorian also have in both places.] Taurianus, and that we have risen up again against most abandoned men. But [n: (35, quaest. 9, c. 7)], as I can, I shall measure out [nt: The editions: *imitabor*: they are corrected from the Royal codex cited above.] in fewer words a summary of such great evils, and shall go over again briefly the things I have seen in the volume of your letters. It ought not [nt: In the copies of the collection of Hadrian: *non potuit*. It is to be kept, with the rest, *non oportuit*: as Nicholas I also reads it. Hadrian II imitates the same place in the letter to King Charles given in Indiction v.] [n: (Nicolaus I, epist. 32, n. 12)] to seem grievous to your most pious minds that anyone's judgment be reconsidered: because truth, when more often stirred up, shines out the more in the light: and ruin, recalled into judgment, is condemned more heavily and [0538B] without repentance [nt: That is, of him who judged before, as is also noted in Labbe's margin. From this it is gathered that Bubalius and Taurianus, judged by the Macedonians, appealed to the apostolic see, and that the Macedonians took it ill that their own judgment was being reviewed.]. For it is a divine fruit, dearest brethren, that justice be more often reviewed.

## 2.

But I see that this has moved your minds: that Bubalius, often caught out in many deceits, put forward copies of letters feigned as if from us, although, from the man's habit, nothing that he brought forward seemed by now most worthy of credit. But let all murmuring about such a business now be silenced, and let the very breathings of the convicted devil be suppressed. We have, moreover, subjoined to these the earlier [nt: These letters are not extant.] letters, which we had sent by the bishops mentioned, in which the full [0539A] sentence of our mind was so set out that, when you have read them again, you will seek again nothing doubtful, nothing to be inquired into, in this case for the rest. And send this little page of ours more carefully [nt: In the manuscripts of the collection of Hadrian: *credentibus*: to which word the author of the Spanish collection added *sibi*: and that reading prevailed both in the Isidorian and thereafter in the printed editions. The Royal exemplar of the pure collection of Dionysius supplies the true one. Further, among the churches in which Innocent, epist. 13, n. 2, commits his office to Rufus, Crete also is reckoned.] to the Cretan bishops to be read again: that they may know most fully what has been pronounced concerning Bubalius and Taurianus and the rest; that [nt: It would please more: and *observent*. Next, in place of *administratione*, which we recall from the excellent Royal codex, there stands in the others *admonitione*. Presently the Noyon manuscript: *ne talibus sortiantur*: from which the common reading *ne talibus socientur* was made up. To be kept, with the better manuscripts: *ne talia sortiantur*, that is, that knowing what was judged concerning Bubalius and Taurianus, they may beware lest they do such things, and thence incur an equal penalty. And what that penalty was is understood from the words *qui digni administratione sunt*: for this is said by antiphrasis, since Bubalius and Taurianus had been cast down from the administration of the churches.] those who are worthy of administration may keep to beware of such men, lest they obtain such a lot.
