## 2.

## 4. CAP. III.

The Arians, moreover [n: (I, q. I, c, 73)], [0550A] and [nt: In the manuscripts, *caeterosque*.] the other pests of this kind: because we receive their laity, converted to the Lord, under the image [nt: What that phrase *sub imagine poenitentiae*, repeated again at the close of the letter, may mean is a question. The word *imago* will recur also in the following letter, n. 7, where it is declared that the image of the Lord's day and of the sixth day (on which Christ suffered and rose again) is to be renewed every week. But another passage, of Epistle 29, n. 2, contributes more to illustrating this place, where Pelagius and Coelestius are brought in as men furnished, or rather destroyed, by perverse subtleties of words, *disputing under the image of the catholic faith*. For as there it is called an image of the catholic faith because it bears a certain appearance of the catholic faith, so here too what bears a certain image of penance is named an image of penance. And not without reason did Innocent call the imposition of the hand, by which heretics once corrected were formerly joined at once to the faithful, an *image of penance*: because, of course, that imposition carried a certain profession of penance. Wherefore Augustine, in the book on Baptism against the Donatists, c. 2, says aptly to the mind of this pope: *If the imposition of the hand were not applied to those coming from heresy, they would be judged as though they were outside all fault. But on account of the bond of charity, which is the greatest gift of the Holy Spirit, without which whatever other holy things there may be in a man avail nothing for salvation, the hand is laid on heretics who have been corrected.* But this imposition of the hand did not have the reality of penance, that is, squalor of garments, frequent fasts, and the other labours and apparatus of mournful penance to which those were subjected who, having been separated from the assembly of the faithful, were admitted to it again only after long satisfaction. Of this penance, then, it was merely a shadowing and an image. Further, Siricius, Innocent and Pope Vigilius distinguish two kinds of heretics returning to the Church: one of those who, having been baptized among heretics, say Novatians, Donatists or Arians, betake themselves to the Church; the other of those who, having received baptism in the catholic Church, allowed themselves to be rebaptized by those heretics. And to both they prescribe the imposition of the hand, but one which in the first bears only the image of penance, in the last shows the reality. Of the first Siricius speaks thus, Epistle 1, n. 2: *Whom we, with the Novatians and other heretics, as was established in the synod* (of Nicaea), *join to the assembly of Catholics only by the invocation of the sevenfold Spirit, through the imposition of the bishop's hand*. Of the same he says again, Epistle 4, c. 8: *That those coming from the Novatians or the Montenses be received through the imposition of the hand*. But he notes that others are to be distinguished from these by the exception subjoined: *except those whom they rebaptize*. Our Innocent explains the mind of Siricius in Epistle 2, n. 11, where he likewise prescribes, *That those coming from the Novatians or the Montenses be received through the imposition of the hand*: but he subjoins a more explicit exception in this manner: *except those who, if perhaps passing over from us to them, have been rebaptized: these, if they repent and consider their ruin and prefer to return, are to be admitted under a long satisfaction of penance*. The first, therefore, were received *under the image of penance*; because the hand was laid on them for receiving the Holy Spirit, in such a way that no satisfaction of penance followed. But the others were admitted no longer under the image, but under the very reality of penance; because communion was granted them only *under a long satisfaction of penance*, which followed upon the imposition of the hand. Pope Vigilius, plainly distinguishing that twofold imposition of the hand in Epistle 2, to Profuturus (or, as it is corruptly circulated in the printed editions, to Eutherius), n. 4, illustrates and confirms our explanation, where he likewise decrees concerning those who have been rebaptized: *Whose reconciliation, however, is accomplished not through that imposition of the hand which works through the invocation of the Holy Spirit, but through that by which the fruit of penance is acquired and the restoration of holy communion is completed*.] of penance and with the sanctification of the Holy Spirit through the imposition of the hand, it does not seem that their clerics ought to be received with the dignity of the priesthood or of any ministry: since those for whom we allow [nt: The copies of Hadrian's collection: *promittimus*. More correctly Dionysius, with other codices both manuscript and printed: *permittimus*. What Innocent does not now permit, because no necessity underlies it, he acknowledges above, Epistle 16, n. 9 and 10, can be permitted *for necessity and as a remedy*.] baptism alone to be valid, which is indeed performed in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, we do not judge to have the Holy Spirit from that baptism and those mysteries: since, when their authors fell away from the catholic faith, they lost the perfection of the Spirit which they had received. Nor can they give its fullness, which works above all in ordinations, which they lost through what I should call the perfidy of their impiety rather than their [nt: In the printed editions, *quam per fidem*. More fittingly the particle *per* is absent from the manuscripts. It is noted here that the faith of heretics is rightly called perfidy, because, as Hilary observes in the book against Constantius, n. 24, *whatever is beyond the one faith is perfidy, not faith*.] faith. How can it be that we should judge their profane priests worthy of the honours of Christ [nt: To this points what the Donatists, to whom it was promised that the honour of their clerical rank would be kept, object in Augustine, Epistle 185, n. 44: *If then it is right that we should repent of having been outside the Church and against the Church, how after this penance do we remain among them clerics, or even bishops?* To whom Augustine answers: *This would not be done, since in truth, as must be admitted, it ought not to be done, unless it were healed by the compensation of peace itself.* But it ought not to be done, because those are not worthy of the honours of Christ who are not free from reproach.], [0551A] when we receive their laity, imperfect as I said, for receiving the grace of the Holy Spirit, with the image of penance? Let thy gravity therefore bring these things to the knowledge of our fellow bishops, either through a synod, if it can, or through the reading of this letter: so that what thou hast so necessarily inquired, and we have so carefully answered, may be kept by the common consent and zeal of all.

## 1.
