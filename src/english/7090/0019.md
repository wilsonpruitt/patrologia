## 2.

For who does not know, or does not observe, that what was handed down to the Roman Church by Peter, the prince of the Apostles, and is kept until now, ought to be kept by all; and that nothing ought to be brought in over it or introduced [0552B] which has no authority, or which seems to take its pattern from elsewhere? especially since it is manifest that throughout all Italy, the Gauls, the Spains, Africa and Sicily, and the islands [nt: Supply *terrasque*. Hilary, writing the book on the Synods to those who were joined with the Gauls in the one communion and fellowship of faith, lists together with them the bishops of the first and second Germany and of the Britains. If to these are added the various provinces of Illyricum, which Innocent lists in Epistle 13, n. 2, when committing his vicariate over them to Rufus, a fairly accurate enumeration will be had of the dioceses which belong to the Roman patriarchate. Of the churches named by this pope, however, there are some who think that the church of Lyons should be excepted, which they judge received its first priest neither from Peter nor from his successors. But the author of this opinion confesses elsewhere that it is unknown to him by whom Pothinus, the first prelate of that same church, was either ordained or sent.] lying between, no one founded churches except those whom the venerable apostle Peter or his successors appointed priests. Or let them read whether in these provinces any other of the Apostles is found, or is read to have taught. And if they do not read it, because they find it nowhere, they must follow what the Roman Church keeps, from which it is not doubtful that they received their beginning; lest, while they are eager for foreign assertions, they be seen to abandon the head of their institutions.

## 3.

It is not doubtful that thy love has often come to the City and [0552C] assembled with us in church, and has come to know what custom it keeps, whether in consecrating the mysteries or in performing the other secret rites. That this would suffice [nt: The word *arbitrarer* is added here in the old editions of the Councils, as in the manuscripts.] for the instruction of thy church, or for its reformation if thy predecessors held anything less or otherwise, we should hold as quite certain, hadst thou not thought that we should be consulted about some matters. [0553A] To these we reply, therefore, not because we believe thou art ignorant of anything, but so that with greater authority thou mayest either instruct thine own, or, if any stray from the institutions of the Roman Church, either admonish them or not delay to report them, so that we may be able to know who they are that either bring in novelties or think that the custom of another church than the Roman is to be kept.

## 4.
