## EPISTLE XXVI. OF THE COUNCIL OF CARTHAGE TO INNOCENT. *That the authority of the apostolic see may be added to their sentence, by which they condemned the impieties of Pelagius and Coelestius. And finally, if those men deny that these are theirs, anathema is decreed against any who assert them.*

To the most blessed and most honourable lord, the holy brother, Pope INNOCENT, AURELIUS, NUMIDIUS, RUSTICIANUS, [nt: So the manuscripts, one excepted, in which *Fidentianus*. But the printed editions: *Fidentius*, with the Conference of Carthage favouring it, in which three bishops are read who bore the name of Fidentius.] FIDENTINUS, EVAGRIUS, [nt: In Crabbe, in the margin: *Antonius*: which the manuscripts also have here. But the letter by which Innocent wrote back to him is addressed, in the Corb. and Germ. manuscripts, *Antonio*, not *Antonino*.] ANTONIUS, PALATINUS, ADEODATUS, VINCENTIUS, PUBLIANUS, THEASIUS, TUTUS, [nt: The Roman edition: *Pantonius*, the manuscripts resisting. In the Conference of Carthage, cap. 126, *Pannonius, bishop of the people of Puppita* is read.] PANNONIUS, VICTOR, RESTITUTUS, another RESTITUTUS, RUSTICUS, FORTUNATIANUS, AMPELIUS, [nt: In the Corb. and Germ. manuscripts the name of this bishop is lacking, who in the same manuscripts is called *Ambivius* in Innocent's rescript, and is noted in the Conference of Carthage, c. 133, as *bishop of Pisita*. But in Quesnel, as in the Pith. and Colb. manuscripts and those of the Isidorian collection, he is called *Avivius*, and in Merlin and Crabbe, as in the Roman edition, *Avinius*.] [0564B] AMBIVIUS, FELIX, DONATIANUS, ADEODATUS, OCTAVIUS, SEROTINUS, [nt: In one manuscript, *Majorianus*. Then the Roman edition, after Merlin, *Porturianus*; next, the same edition, *Trispulus*; Merlin and Crabbe, with the manuscripts of the Isidorian collection, *Crispotis*. In the Conference of Carthage, c. 123, *Posthumianus, bishop of the people of Tagora*; and, c. 128, *Crispulus, bishop of the church of Volita* are mentioned.] MAJORINUS, POSTHUMIANUS, CRISPULUS, VICTOR, another VICTOR, [nt: The Navar. manuscript of the Isidorian collection: *Leucianus*. Two Colb. and the Corb., as in Merlin, *Leutius*. Another Colb., *Lucius*, with the Conference of Carthage favouring it; the Vatic., *Leontius*; the Germ., *Eleutius*. Next, the Roman edition *Macinus*, Merlin *Marinus*, Crabbe *Marinianus*. In the Conference of Carthage two bishops are read who were called by the name of *Mariani*.] LEUCIUS, MACIANUS, FRUCTUOSUS, FAUSTINIANUS, QUODVULTDEUS, CANDORIUS, MAXIMUS, [nt: Quesnel and two Colb. manuscripts: *Magerius*; another Colb., *Magarus*; Pith., *Megarius*; the Capuchin codex of the Isidorian collection, *Magorus*; Merlin, Crabbe and the Roman edition, *Magarius*. From the Corb. and Germ. manuscripts, and also from the edition of Augustine, we restore *Megasius*.] MEGASIUS, RUSTICUS, [nt: So the manuscripts, except that in one Colbert, as in Quesnel, *Rufianus*. But Crabbe and the Roman edition: *Sufinianus*.] RUFINIANUS, [nt: None of our manuscripts supports the conjecture of Garnier, by which he thinks *Proculianus* should be read. Next, from the Corb. and Germ. manuscripts and the new edition of Augustine, the name of *Severus* is supplied, which is lacking in the other manuscripts and printed books.] PROCULUS, SEVERUS, THOMAS, JANUARIUS, OCTAVIANUS, PRAETEXTATUS, SIXTUS, QUODVULTDEUS, PENTADIUS, [nt: In one manuscript, as in Quesnel, *item* is set first here: which is absent in five others, as in the edition of Augustine. In the Roman edition, Crabbe and Merlin, *Quodvultdeus* is lacking here. Indeed the same name is omitted before too in the manuscripts of the Isidorian collection.] QUODVULTDEUS, CYPRIANUS, SERVILIUS, [nt: So in Augustine, as in the Corb. and Germ. manuscripts. But in the other books, *Pelagius*.] PELAGIANUS, MARCELLUS, VENANTIUS, DIDYMUS, SATURNINUS, [nt: One Colb. manuscript: *Bazathinus*; another, from the Vatican: *Bizatenus*; two other Colb. and Pith., with the Roman edition and Quesnel: *Bazacenus*; Merlin and Crabbe: *Bazecenus*; the Isidorian collection: *Batecenus*. We follow the edition of Augustine, which agrees with the Corb. and Germ. manuscripts.] BIZACENUS, GERMANUS, GERMANIANUS, [nt: In Augustine, *Inventius*. In the Roman edition, Quesnel, Crabbe and Merlin, as in the Pith. manuscript, *Juventius*. The Isidorian collection, *Viventius*. In other manuscripts, *Invenitus*.] INVENITUS, MAJORINUS, [nt: The printed editions and the manuscripts just mentioned, with which the Isidorian collection now agrees, keep *Inventius* or *Juventius* here too, to which word Quesnel prefixed *item*. Here indeed the Corb. codex shows *Juventinitus*, but in the rescript it keeps the name *Inveniti*, which two Colb. and the Germ. also show here.] INVENITUS, CANDIDUS, CYPRIANUS, AEMILIANUS, ROMANUS, AFRICANUS, and MARCELLINUS, [nt: *et caeteri* was first added here in the edition of Augustine, on the authority of the Corb. and Germ. codices.] and the rest of us who were present at the council of the Church of Carthage.

## 1.

[0564C]When, according to custom [nt: It was their custom to assemble at Carthage about the month of June or about autumn. But they did not constantly assemble in the same months. Therefore that *ex more* refers rather to the place than to the time.], we had solemnly come to the Church of Carthage, and a synod assembled [0565A] from among us was being held for various causes, our fellow presbyter Orosius gave us letters of our holy brethren and fellow priests [nt: In the manuscripts of the Isidorian collection, *Nerotis*, or *Anerotis*. In the editions of the Councils, *Averothis*. Heros was bishop of Arles, and Lazarus of Aix. Both, driven from their sees, when they had come into Palestine, presented a libellus against Pelagius and Coelestius to Eulogius of Caesarea; by whose authority the synod of Diospolis was afterwards assembled in the year 415. But the letters of those bishops mentioned here have perished.] Heros and Lazarus, whose text we have decided should be subjoined to these. These having been read, then, we perceived that Pelagius and Coelestius were [nt: The word *argui*, which was formerly lacking, we supply with the edition of Augustine from the Corb. and Germ. manuscripts. From there too, as also from the Pith. manuscript, we have restored *anathemandi* in place of *anathematizandi*.] charged as the authors of an error altogether wicked and to be anathematized by all of us. Whence it came about that we asked that there be reviewed what had been debated about the name of Coelestius here, at the Church of Carthage, almost five years before. When this had been read out, as thy holiness will be able to observe from the [nt: The Roman edition, *ex subsequentibus*. More fittingly the other books, *ex subditis*. Those episcopal acts, which had been subjoined to this letter, have perished, except the extracts which M. Mercator takes from them at the beginning of the Commonitorium against the Pelagians, and those which Augustine copies out of them, both in book II On Original Sin, c. 3 and 4, and in the book On the Proceedings of Pelagius, cap. 11. Moreover, they are touched upon by the same Doctor in Epistle 157, to Hilary, n. 22.] subjoined documents, although the judgment was manifestly established by which at that time this so great wound seemed to have been cut out of the Church by episcopal judgment; nevertheless we decided this by common deliberation: that the authors of such a persuasion, although [0565B] the same Coelestius is said to have afterwards come to the presbyterate too, unless they most openly anathematize these things, must themselves be anathematized; so that, if their own health cannot be procured, at least that of those who have been deceived by them, or can be deceived, may be procured, once the sentence passed against them is known. This proceeding, therefore, lord brother, we have thought should be made known to thy holy charity, so that to the statutes of our mediocrity the authority of the apostolic see may also be applied, for protecting the salvation of many, and for correcting also the perversity of some.

## 2.

For these men aim at this by their damnable disputations: that, not by defending free will, but rather by exalting it into sacrilegious pride, they leave no room for the grace of God by which we are Christians, by which too the very choice of our will is made truly free, [0565C] while it is freed from the dominion of carnal concupiscences, as the Lord says: *If the Son shall have made you free, then you shall be truly free [n: (Joan. VIII, 36)]*. [nt: This sentence, *Quod auxilium fides impetrat quae est in Christo J. D. N.*, is omitted in the Corb. and Germ. manuscripts.] Which help faith obtains, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord. But these men assert, as we have learned from brethren who have also read their books, that the grace of God is to be reckoned in this: that he established and created such a nature of man as could fulfil the law of God by its own will, whether [0566A] the law written naturally in the heart, or that given in letters. That this same law also belongs to grace, because God gave it to men for their help. But that grace by which, as has been said, we are Christians, of which the Apostle is the preacher when he says, *For I am delighted with the law of God according to the inward man; but I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind, and making me captive* [nt: The Roman edition and that of the Councils, with the Isidorian collection, and also two Colb. manuscripts and Pith.: *in lege*.] *under the law of sin, which is in my members. Unhappy man that I am, who shall deliver me from the body of this death? the grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord [n: (Rom. VII, 22 seqq.)]*, they are altogether unwilling to acknowledge, nor indeed do they dare to attack it openly [n: (I Cor. II, 14)].

## 3.

But what else are they doing, when they do not cease to persuade [0566B] animal men, who do not perceive the things that are of the spirit of God, that for working and perfecting justice, and for fulfilling the commandments of God, human nature alone can suffice them? not attending to what is written, *The Spirit helps our infirmity [n: (Rom. VIII, 26)]*; and, *It is not of him that wills, nor of him that runs, but of God that shows mercy [n: (Rom. IX, 16)]*; and that *we are one body in Christ Jesus, and each of us members one of another, having different gifts according to the grace which is given to us [n: (Rom. XII, V)]*; and, *By the grace of God I am what I am, and his grace in me was not void, but I laboured more than all of them: yet not I, but the grace of God with me [n: (I Cor. XV, 10)]*; and, *Thanks be to God, who has given us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ [n: (Ibid., 57)]*; and, *Not that we are sufficient to think anything as of ourselves, but our sufficiency [0566C] is from God [n: (II Cor. III, 5)]*; and, *We have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellence of the power may be of God, and not of us [n: (Ibid. IV, 7)]*; and innumerable things, for which, if we wished to collect them from all the Scriptures, [nt: In Augustine, as in the Corb. and Germ. manuscripts, *tempus*. With the other books we keep *tomus*; by which word a synodical letter is usually signified among the ancients. And indeed in that sense Athanasius entitled the letter of the synod of Alexandria *the tome to the Antiochenes*: and the council of Constantinople, in epist. 13, to Damasus, n. 4, called the letter of the council of Antioch τόμον. The synodical letter 4 of Damasus himself is designated by the name of *tome* by the same council of Constantinople, can. 5. The use of this word in the same sense is more frequent in the council of Chalcedon, for instance in Labbe's edition, vol. IV, p. 187 d, 264 d, 491 e. Commonly too the celebrated letter of Leo to Flavian is called the *tome*. Sometimes also *tome* signifies the Compendium of a longer discourse, and in that sense it is taken in the preface of the eighth council of Toledo: literally, however, it is the same as *sectio*. As regards the Africans, they seem to mean nothing else here than that they would exceed the measure of an ordinary letter if they were to collect all the testimonies that serve their purpose.] a tome does not suffice. And we fear lest, by recalling before thee these very things [nt: The Roman edition and that of the Councils, after Crabbe: *qui majore*. More fittingly the other books: *quae majore*.] which thou preachest with greater grace from the apostolic see, we should seem to act unfittingly. But we do so because, in proportion as, being weaker, each of us everywhere in preaching the word of God [0567A] is thought the more attentive, the more often and the more boldly we suffer them rising up against us.

## 4.

If, then, Pelagius should have seemed to thy veneration also to have been justly acquitted by the episcopal proceedings ( *of the synod of Diospolis* ) which are said to have been drawn up in the East; yet the error itself and the impiety, which already has many assertors scattered through various places, must be anathematized by the authority of the apostolic see as well. For let thy holiness consider, and suffer with us with pastoral bowels, how pestilent and deadly it is to the sheep of Christ, what follows necessarily from the sacrilegious disputations of these men: that we ought not even to pray that we enter not into temptation, which the Lord both admonished his disciples and placed in the prayer which he taught [n: (Matth. VI, 13)]: or that our faith fail not, which he himself testified that he had asked for the apostle [0567B] Peter [n: (Luc. XXII, 32)]. For if these things are placed in our power by the possibility of nature and the choice of the will, who does not see that they are asked of the Lord in vain, and prayed for deceitfully, when by praying things are sought which are obtained by the sufficient powers of a nature already so created: and that the Lord Jesus ought not to have said, *Watch and pray [n: (Matth. XXIV, 41)]*; but only, Watch, *that you enter not into temptation:* nor to the most blessed Peter, first of the Apostles: *I have prayed for thee,* but, I admonish thee, or I command and enjoin, *that thy faith fail not (Lucae XXII, 32)*? Our blessings too are contradicted by the contention of these men, so that we seem to say over the people in vain whatever we pray the Lord for on their behalf, that by living rightly and piously they may please him; or those things which the Apostle prays for on behalf of the faithful, [0567C] saying: *I bow my knees to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, from whom all paternity in heaven and on earth is named, that he may grant you, according to the riches of his glory, to be strengthened with power through his Spirit [n: (Ephes. III, 14)]*. If then, in blessing, we wish to say over the people: *Grant it, O Lord, to be strengthened with power through thy Spirit:* the disputation of these men contradicts us, affirming that free will is denied if that is asked of God which is in our power. For if we wish, they say, to be strengthened with power, we can be, by that possibility of nature which we do not receive now, but received when we were created.

## 5.

They deny too that little ones are to be baptized for the salvation which is given through Christ the Saviour; and so by this deadly doctrine they slay them for eternity, promising [0568A] that even if they are not baptized they will have eternal life, and that they do not belong to those of whom the Lord says, *For the son of man came to seek and to save that which had perished [n: (Luc. XIX, 10)]*: because these, they say, had not perished, nor is there anything in them to be saved, or to be redeemed at so great a price; because nothing in them is corrupted, nothing is held captive under the power of the devil, nor was [nt: The Pith. manuscript: *fusus esset*; the Isidorian collection and also the Roman edition and that of the Councils: *fusus esse*, omitting next *qui fusus*: they are restored from the manuscripts of better repute. And the blood of Christ, to be shed *for the remission of sins*, is read in Matt. xxvi, 28.] the blood shed for them, which is read to have been shed for the remission of sins. Although Coelestius has already confessed in his libellus, in the church of Carthage, that through the baptism of Christ the redemption of little ones too [nt: In the Roman edition, as in Merlin, *dicunt* is wrongly added here. Augustine, epist. 157, n. 22, speaks of this very thing concerning Coelestius thus: *He was compelled to confess, on account of the little ones to be baptized, that redemption is necessary for them also. Where*, he says, *although he was unwilling to say anything more expressly about original sin, yet by the very name of redemption he committed himself not a little.*] takes place. But many who are said to be or to have been their disciples do not cease to affirm these evils, by which they try to overturn the foundations of the Christian faith, in whatever way they can [nt: The Roman edition and that of the Councils: *possunt astutia*. We remove the word *astutia* on the authority of all the manuscripts.]. [0568B]
