## 6.

Wherefore, even if Pelagius and Coelestius have been corrected, or say that they never held these things, and deny that whatever writings have been brought forward against them are theirs, and there is no way by which they may be convicted of lying: yet in general, whoever dogmatizes and affirms that human nature can suffice for itself for [nt: In the Corb. manuscript, by the first hand, *ad nitenda*, and by a second correction, *ad vitanda*: which also stands in the Germ. and was preferred in the edition of Augustine. We keep, however, *vincenda*, both on the authority of more manuscripts, and because below, epist. 28, n. 13, the five bishops write again that Pelagius defends *the possibility of nature for conquering sins and fulfilling the commandments*. Wherefore in the synod of Diospolis too he had been compelled to confess that *when we fight against temptations and unlawful concupiscences, the* VICTORY *comes not from our own will but from the help of God*.] conquering sins and doing the commandments of God, and in that way is found an adversary of the grace of God, which is declared more evidently by the prayers of the saints: and whoever denies that little ones are freed from perdition through the baptism of Christ and receive everlasting salvation, let him be anathema. As for whatever else is objected to them, we do not doubt that thy veneration, when it has looked through the episcopal proceedings ( *of the synod of Palestine* ) which are said to have been drawn up in the East in the same cause, will so judge [0568C] that we may all rejoice in the mercy of God. Pray for us, most blessed lord pope.

## EPISTLE XXVII. OF THE COUNCIL OF MILEVIS TO POPE INNOCENT.

*That he may restrain as soon as possible by his authority the pernicious heresy of the enemies of the grace of Christ.*

*To the most blessed lord, rightly to be venerated and to be honoured in Christ, Pope INNOCENT, SILVANUS* [nt: In the Roman edition and that of Augustine *senex* is lacking: which word is expressed in all our manuscripts. In the Conference of Carthage, c. 133, *Litorius, bishop of Suava* is named.] *LITTORIUS, FORTUNATUS, DONATUS, [0569A]* [nt: The Pith. manuscript and one Colb.: *Ponticianus*. Two others, with Merlin, Crabbe and Quesnel: *Pontianus*.] *PONTICANUS, SATURNINUS,* [nt: In the Roman edition and that of Augustine: *Christonius*. In the other editions, as in all our manuscripts: *Cresconius*. ## 1.

Since the Lord, by a special gift of his grace, has placed thee in the apostolic see, and has granted thee to our times as such a one that it would rather count against us as a fault of negligence if we kept silent before thy veneration about what must be suggested on behalf of [0569B] the Church, than that thou couldst receive it either disdainfully or negligently; we beseech thee to deign to apply pastoral diligence to the great dangers of the weak members of Christ.

## 2.

For a new and exceedingly pernicious heresy [nt: Garnier recalled from the old editions of Augustine: *perniciosa tempestas inimicorum gratiae coepit*, the other printed editions and all the manuscripts resisting.] is trying to rise up, that of the enemies of the grace of Christ, who by impious disputations are trying to take away from us even the Lord's Prayer. For whereas the Lord taught us to say, *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors [n: (Matth. VI, 12)]*: these men say that a man can in this life, once the precepts of God are known, attain to so great a perfection of justice without the help of the grace of the Saviour, through the sole choice of free will, that it is no longer necessary for him to say, *Forgive us our debts:* and that what [0569C] follows, *Do not bring us into temptation [n: (Ibid., 13)]*, is not to be understood as though we ought to ask for divine help, lest, being tempted, we fall into sin; but that this is placed in our power, and that for fulfilling it the will of man alone suffices: as though the Apostle had said in vain, [0570A] *It is not of him that wills, nor of him that runs, but of God that shows mercy [n: (Rom. IX, 16)]*; and, *God is faithful, who does not permit you to be tempted beyond what you are able, but will make with the temptation also a way out, that you may be able to bear it [n: (I Cor. X, 13)]*. In vain too would the Lord have said to the apostle Peter, *I have prayed for thee, that thy faith fail not (Lucae XXII, 32)*; and to all his own, *Watch and pray, that you enter not into temptation [n: (Matth. XXIV, 41)]*: if this whole thing lies in human power. Little children too, [nt: The Roman edition and that of the Councils: *si nullis*, which the manuscripts support. Next, in Garnier and Augustine, *innoventur*. The reading *etsi nullis* is favoured by that of the preceding letter, n. 5: *promising that* EVEN IF *they are not baptized, they will have eternal life*. From this too it is plain that baptism is here called the *sacraments of Christian grace*.] even if they are imbued with no sacraments of Christian grace, will have eternal life: so they contend, with a presumption [nt: In place of *nequaquam*, Garnier substituted *nequam*: which passed from there into the edition of Augustine. The ambiguity will be removed if you read *praesumptione nequaquam Christiana*.] by no means Christian, making void what the Apostle says, *By one man sin entered into the world, and by sin death, and so it passed into all men, in whom all have sinned [n: (Rom. V, 12)]*; and in another place, *as in [0570B] Adam all die, so in Christ all shall be made alive [n: (I Cor. XV, 22)]*. To omit, then, the other things, very many, which they argue against the holy Scriptures, there are meanwhile these two, by which they try to overturn entirely all that we are as Christians, and which faithful hearts [nt: Garnier: *non sustinent*, adding a negative particle against the faith of the old codices and without necessity.] might endure: that God is not to be asked to be our helper against the evil of sin and for working justice, and that the sacrament of Christian grace does not aid little ones to attain eternal life.

## 3.

Suggesting these things to thy apostolic breast, we have no need to say much, and to magnify so great an impiety with words; since beyond doubt these things move thee [nt: In Quesnel and Augustine: *te ita permoveant*.] so that, as to correcting them, lest they spread more widely and defile many, or rather destroy them, while under the name of Christ they estrange them entirely from the grace of Christ, [0570C] thou canst in no way hold back. The authors of this most pernicious error are said to be Pelagius and Coelestius, whom indeed we would rather have healed in the Church than cut off from the Church with their salvation despaired of, if no necessity compels. Of whom one, that is Coelestius, has come even to the presbyterate in Asia [nt: Mar. Mercator, Commonitorium c. 1, Baluze's edition p. 133, relates the matter thus: Coelestius, *accused on six chapters in the council of Carthage and ordered to condemn them, but in no way acquiescing, was deprived of ecclesiastical communion. From which sentence he thought an appeal should be made to the examination of the bishop of Rome: but soon, neglecting that same appeal, he made for Ephesus, a city of Asia, and there dared by stealth to seek the place of the presbyterate*. Earlier, about the end of the year 411, as Augustine testifies, epist. 157, n. 22, when he was settled at Carthage, *he had already begun to creep up to the honour of the presbyterate in the church of that same city: but through the most faithful freedom of the brethren, on account of these very disputations against the grace of Christ, he was brought as far as episcopal judgment*. There is extant a letter of Nestorius to him in Mar. Mercator, p. 131, inscribed *to the honourable and most religious presbyter Coelestius*.], [0571A] it is said. What was done about him a few years ago, thy holiness is [nt: Namely because the Church, or synod, of Carthage had subjoined to the preceding Epistle 26 the acts of another council held at Carthage about the name of Coelestius almost five years before. Since both this letter of the council of Milevis and that of Carthage, carried by the same bishop Julius, were delivered to Innocent together, there was no need for Garnier to substitute of his own *melius instruetur* for *melius instruitur*.] better instructed from the church of Carthage. But Pelagius, as letters sent by certain of our brethren say, being settled at Jerusalem, is asserted to be deceiving some. Yet many more, who have been able to investigate his meaning more diligently, fight against him for the grace of Christ and the truth of the catholic faith, but especially thy holy son, our brother and fellow presbyter Jerome. But we judge that, with the help of the mercy of the Lord our God, who deigns [nt: One manuscript: *dignabitur*. Into the new edition of Augustine *dignetur* crept, through the sheer carelessness of a copyist.] both to guide thee when thou consultest and to hear thee when thou prayest, those who hold such perverse and pernicious opinions will more easily yield to the authority of thy holiness, [nt: The manuscripts of the Isidorian collection: *de clarum* (through the unskilfulness of a copyist, who had read in his exemplar *de scarum*, that is, *de sanctarum*). The Capuchin codex, by a second correction: *declaratione sacrarum*) *Scripturarum depromptae*. From this, in the Roman edition and that of the Councils, it had become *de claro Scripturarum lumine depromptae*. The better manuscripts supply the sounder reading. Here the fathers of the council of Milevis declare that the authority of the Roman pontiff is supported by the very authority of the Scriptures.] drawn from the authority of the holy Scriptures; so that we may rather rejoice together over their [0571B] correction than be saddened by their destruction, most blessed lord. But whatever they themselves choose, thy venerableness certainly sees that provision must be made urgently and speedily at least for others, very many of whom they can entangle in their snares if they are left unregarded. We have sent these writings to thy holiness from the council of Numidia, imitating [nt: The Roman edition and Garnier omit *Carthaginensem ecclesiam, et*: which words are restored from the other books.] the church of Carthage and our fellow bishops of the province of Carthage, who, we have learned, have written on this cause to the apostolic see, which thou, blessed one, makest illustrious. ( *And in another hand:* ) Mindful of us, mayest thou increase in the grace of God, most blessed lord, rightly venerable and to be honoured in Christ, holy pope.

## EPISTLE XXVIII [nt: This letter is mentioned by Augustine, book On Original Sin, c. 9, and epist. formerly 47, now 215, to Valentinus, n. 12, as written *by five bishops*. Wherefore it was not ill inscribed *of five bishops* in the editions of the Councils. By the same Doctor, epist. formerly 106, now 186, n. 2, it is called *familiar*, to distinguish it from the preceding ones, which are synodical.]. OF FIVE BISHOPS TO POPE INNOCENT. *They uncover and refute the heresy of Pelagius, by which he contended that human nature alone, through free will, suffices for itself for taming desires and overcoming temptations, and also for fulfilling the commandments of God: and they show that wherever he preached grace, he understood by it either free will, or the remission of sins, or the precept of the law, and that something else is understood by the faithful under the proper name of grace. They also explain in what sense his saying that man can be without sin if he wills is said truly, and in what sense, though not without error, it is yet said tolerably.*

*To the most blessed lord, and brother rightly most to be honoured, Pope INNOCENT, AURELIUS, ALYPIUS, AUGUSTINE, EVODIUS, and POSSIDIUS, greeting in the Lord.*

## 1.

From the two councils, of the province of Carthage and of Numidia, we have sent to thy holiness letters subscribed by no small number of bishops, against the enemies of the grace of Christ, who trust in their own strength, and in a certain manner say to our creator: Thou hast made us men, but we have made ourselves just; who call human nature free for this reason, that they may not seek a liberator; and sound for this reason, that they may judge [0572B] a saviour superfluous. For they say it is so strong that, with its powers received once for all in the origin of its creation, it can through free will, with the grace of him who created it giving no further help, tame and extinguish all desires, and overcome temptations. And many of them rise up against us and say to our soul: *There is no salvation for him in his God [n: (Psal. III, 3)]*. The household of Christ, which says, *When I am weak, then* [nt: One Colb. codex: *tunc fortior sum*: which it also seems to have had at first.] *am I strong [n: (II Cor. XII, 10)]*; and to which its Lord says, *I am thy salvation [n: (Psal. XXXIV, 3)]*, awaits with anxious heart, in fear and trembling, the help of the Lord, through the charity of thy veneration also.
