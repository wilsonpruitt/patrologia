## 9.

But it is proved that sufficient answer has been made by your admonitions, so abounding in examples of our law. Nor have we thought that anything remains for us to say, since it is evident that nothing has been passed over by you, nothing suppressed, by which they may be known to be refuted and utterly convicted. And therefore no testimonies are set down by us, because in these things your report is full, and it is sufficiently evident that so many most learned priests have said everything: nor is it fitting to believe that you have passed over anything which could [0588C] profit the cause. *And in another hand:* Farewell, brethren. *And at the side:* Given on the sixth day before the Kalends of February [n: (Jan. 27 ann. 417)] after the consulship [nt: In the Corbie manuscript: *Luni Quarti*. In the Saint-Germain: *p. c. Theodosio augusto VII et Lucio Quarto v. consulibus*: from the ignorance of a copyist, who did not know that by the former note *p. c.* is signified *post consulatum*, and that therefore *Theodosii augusti*, etc., was to be kept, not *Theodosio augusto* substituted; and who did not know that the latter note, *v. c.*, was to be rendered by the words *viri clarissimi*, not by *v consulibus*.] of Theodosius Augustus for the seventh time and of Junius Quartus, V. C.

## EPISTLE [nt: In the edition of the Councils, 25. And what was 30 is now in the appendix.] XXX. OF POPE INNOCENT TO THE COUNCIL OF MILEVIS. *He praises the fathers of the council of Milevis, both because they judged that obstinate heretics were to be separated and the corrected to be received, and because, following the canons received everywhere, they sought the judgment of the apostolic see on anxious matters. Then he proves that the heresy which will have it that man can suffice to himself without the grace of God, and denies that baptism is necessary for little ones for eternal life, fights against the Scriptures. He orders its authors and their followers to be cut off from the Church, but to be received if they condemn their errors.*

*INNOCENT to SILVANUS the elder, VALENTINUS, [nt: It is the more probable that in this inscription Innocent addressed by name the individual bishops who had been named at the head of the letter of the council itself, because the only copies of this rescript that survive are those which, likewise in the preceding epistle, after naming Aurelius, indicated the other bishops under a general formula.] and the rest who were present at the synod of Milevis, most beloved brethren, greeting in the Lord.*

## I.

Among the other cares of the Roman church, and the occupations of the apostolic see, in which we treat the consultations of various persons with faithful and [nt: In the Roman edition and the Councils, from the Isidorian manuscripts: *modica*: a fault which Garnier, trying to correct it on his own, adds *non* before *modica*.] healing deliberation, our brother and fellow bishop Julius unexpectedly thrust upon me the letter of your charity [0589B] which you sent, with a more inclined care for the faith, from the council of Milevis, [nt: Only in copies of the Isidorian collection, and thence in Merlin and Crabbe: *ex Milevitano ex nostra auctoritate concilio*: a gloss worthy of Mercator, and deservedly removed in the Roman edition. As if indeed Innocent would have said that the letter of a council which had been assembled by his own authority had been thrust upon him unexpectedly.] adding also the writings of the synod of Carthage with a like complaint. Truly the Church exults that the shepherds show such solicitude for the flocks committed to them, that not only do they suffer none of them to go astray; but if the grass of a perverse [nt: Merlin: *sive*; Crabbe: *saevae*; the other editions of the Councils, after the Roman, and also Garnier: *obscaenae*; more rightly the other books: *scaerae*: which Labbe too noted in the margin. Next, the Isidorian manuscripts: *dilectionis*.] delight has rather led astray any of the sheep, they are willing either, if these remain in error, to have them wholly separated, or, if they avoid what they had long unlawfully sought, to protect them with the circumspection of their former keeping: taking counsel, that is, on both sides, lest either by receiving such, the others [nt: Quesnel: *seducantur*.] be led by a like example; or, by spurning those who return, these should seem to be thrown to the bites of wolves. Very prudent, and full of the catholic faith, is [nt: Garnier: *consolatio*. The Roman edition and the Councils, with the Isidorian manuscripts: *consultationis voluntas*. In one Colbert manuscript *consultationis* indeed stands, but *voluntas* is absent. More apt, as Quesnel has already observed, though a less Latin word, is *consultio*, which the other Colbert codex presents. By this name, then, is called that by which they take counsel for both sides, or use this counsel, neither to admit the obstinate nor to spurn those returning.] this counsel. For who could either tolerate [0589C] one who errs, or not receive [nt: In the Augustine edition: *se corrigentem*.] one who corrects himself? For as [0590A] I think it hard to show connivance to those who sin; so I judge it impious to refuse the hand to the converted.

## 2.

Diligently therefore and fittingly [nt: Garnier, with the Roman edition: *apostolico consulitis honori, honori*. Quesnel, following one Colbert manuscript: *apostolici consulitis honoris arcanis, honoris*. Nor does the other Colbert codex differ, except that for *arcanis* it has *arcani*. The Isidorian manuscripts: *apostolici consulitis honores arcani, honoris*. One of the Vatican manuscripts: *apostolici consulitis oneris arcana, oneris*. Sounder is the reading which we present here after the edition of the Councils and the Augustine edition. For this is what it means: you who consult the apostolic see, whose it is to disclose secret things. In the same manner of speaking Boniface I, epist. 5, n. 4, says *quae hujus consulis sedis arcanum*. Hence it is clear that *arcam* was wrongly written here in Crabbe's margin in place of the word *arcana*.] do you consult the secret counsels of the apostolic honour (of that honour, I say [sic: *in quam*], upon which, besides the things that are without, the care of all the churches waits) as to what judgment is to be held on anxious matters [n: (II Cor. XI, 28)]: following, that is, the form of the ancient rule, which you know, as I do, has always been kept by the whole world. But these things I let pass: for I do not believe that this is hidden from your prudence. [nt: So our manuscripts. The same sense is also given by what is in Merlin: *Qui id etiam actione firmastis, nisi scientes*. But the Roman edition and the Councils, after Crabbe: *quid id etiam*, with *nisi* then suppressed: which Quesnel likewise omits, preferring before it *quod etiam*. In the Augustine edition: *Qui id enim actione*, etc. In Garnier: *Cur id enim actione*, etc. If to anyone this, that *responses always flow from the apostolic fountain through all the provinces to those who ask*, should seem exaggerated, let him recall what Jerome wrote, epist. 110, now 91: *When I was helping Damasus, bishop of the city of Rome, with ecclesiastical papers, and answering the synodical consultations of the East and of the West.*] Why have you also confirmed this by action, unless knowing that through all the provinces responses always flow from the apostolic fountain to those who ask? Especially [n: (24, q. 1, c. 12)] whenever the reason of the faith is discussed, I think that all our brethren and fellow bishops ought to refer to none but [0590B] Peter, that is, to the author of their name and honour, [nt: Inasmuch as from him the episcopate itself and the whole authority of this name arose, as he has it in the preceding epistle, n. 1; or, as Siricius says, epist. 5, n. 5, *through whom both the apostolate and the episcopate took their beginning in Christ*: which our Innocent too copies out, epist. 2, n. 2.] as your charity has now referred, what may profit all the churches in common throughout the whole world. For they must needs become more cautious, when they see the inventors of evils, on the report of a double synod, by the statutes of our judgment [nt: Quesnel alone: *se ab ecclesiastica communione sejungi*. Next Garnier: *fruetur*, for *fungetur*.] cut off from ecclesiastical communion. Your charity will therefore enjoy a twofold good. For you will both possess the grace of the canons observed, and the whole world will [nt: In the Roman edition and the Councils: *tutabitur*.] make use of your benefit. For who among catholic men would wish any longer to mingle speech with the adversaries of Christ? Who would share even the very light of life in common with them? [nt: The particle *cum* is put before it here in one manuscript.] Let the authors of the new heresy surely be shunned.

## 3.

For what more bitter could they feign against the Lord, [0590C] than when they made void the divine helps, and took away the cause [0591A] of daily prayer? This is to say, What need have I of God? Deservedly might the hymn-singer say against them: *Behold the men who did not make God their helper [var: Vulg. "Ecce homo qui non posuit," in the singular: Behold the man who did not make] [n: (Psalm. LI, 9)]*. Denying therefore the aid of God, they say that man can suffice to himself, and does not need divine grace: deprived of which, he must needs fall, entangled in the snares of the devil, while he strives for fulfilling all the commandments of life by his liberty alone. O perverse doctrine of most depraved minds! [nt: In the old editions of the Councils, as in the Augustine edition: *Advertat id tandem*. In the other printed editions and two manuscripts: *Adverte tandem*: which the epistle 21 of Celestine also favours, c. 7, where this passage is quoted thus: *Adverte tandem o pravissimarum mentium doctrina*. Wherefore, although the order of Innocent's words is changed there, yet on account of so ancient an authority we should incline to keep *adverte*, did not the train of the sentence resist it, in which one reads not *audi*, as Garnier and Quesnel edited against the manuscripts, but *audiat*. It pleases more, therefore, with some manuscripts, *advertens*, or *attendens*.] Noticing at last that liberty itself so deceived the first man that, while he used its reins too indulgently, he fell by presumption into transgression, [nt: Quesnel, with one Colbert manuscript: *praesumptionis*. Merlin and Crabbe, with the Isidorian manuscripts: *in praevaricationis praesumptione*.] and could not be rescued from it unless the coming of Christ the Lord had, by the providence of regeneration, restored [nt: Quesnel alone: *adventu*.] to him the state of his former liberty: let it hear David saying: [0591B] *Our help is in the name of the Lord [n: (Psalm. CXXIII, 8)]*; and, *Be thou my helper, forsake me not, neither despise me, O God my saviour [n: (Psalm. XXVI, 9)]*. Which he would have said in vain, if [nt: Garnier: *si totum*.] what he asked of the Lord with tearful speech had lain so fully in his own will.

## 4.

Since these things are so, and since in all the divine pages we read that nothing but the help of God is to be joined to the free will, and that it can do nothing when destitute of heavenly aids; in what manner do Pelagius and Coelestius, obstinately defending, as you assert, this possibility for it alone, persuade themselves, nay, what is [nt: In Quesnel: *gravius et dignius*. Then for *dolore communi* Garnier substitutes *dolore, communiter*. Then for the particle *jam*, which is suppressed in the Roman edition, *nam* wrongly stands in Merlin and Crabbe.] more worthy of common grief, already very many? We could indeed use manifold examples [nt: Two Colbert manuscripts: *ad instruendum*: which reading Merlin and Crabbe followed.] for destroying such a teaching, did we not know [0591C] that your holiness is fully versed in all the divine scriptures; especially since your report is so filled with so many and such testimonies, that by these alone the present doctrine could be cut down; and that there is no need of recondite ones, since they neither dare nor are able to oppose even those which you set down as readily occurring to you. So [nt: Garnier: *Ergone Dei*.] they try to take away the grace of God, which we must needs seek even when the liberty of our former state has been restored to us; since [nt: Quesnel: *quippe qui nec aliquas*; Garnier: *quippe nefarias*. Our manuscripts support neither.] we cannot avoid the other machinations of the devil either, except [0592A] with the same helping [n: (Augustin. lib. II, ad Bonifac., c. 4, n. 7)].

## 5.

But that which your fraternity asserts that they preach, that little ones can be rewarded with the prizes of eternal life even without the grace of baptism, is utterly foolish. For unless they eat the flesh of the son of man, and drink his blood, they shall not have life in themselves [n: (Joan. VI, 54)]. But those who defend this for them without regeneration seem to me to wish to make void baptism itself, since they preach that these have what is believed to be conferred on them only by baptism. If then they will have it that not to be reborn does no harm: they must needs confess that the sacred streams of regeneration are of no profit either. But, that the depraved doctrine of superfluous men may be ungirded by the swift reason [nt: So Quesnel and two Colbert manuscripts. But Merlin, with another Colbert manuscript: *distingui*: faultily. Nor better the other books: *discindi*. Silius too said *dolos ratione discingere*.] of truth, the Lord proclaims [0592B] this in the Gospel, saying: *Suffer the little ones, and forbid them [nt: Two Colbert manuscripts: *prohibere a me. Pro qua re Pelagium*.] not to come to me: for of such is the kingdom of heaven [var: Vulg. "pueros … regnum Dei," children … the kingdom of God] (Lucae XVIII, 16)*.

## 6.

Wherefore we decree, by the authority of apostolic vigour, that Pelagius and Coelestius, that is, the inventors of new words, which, as the Apostle said, are wont to bring forth nothing of edification, but rather the vainest questions [n: (II Tim. II, 23)], be deprived of ecclesiastical communion, until they come to their senses out of the snares of the devil, by whom [nt: In one manuscript: *capti*.] they are held captive at his will; and that meanwhile they be not received into the Lord's sheepfold, [nt: The Roman edition and the Councils: *quo ad ipsi perversae viae* (Merlin: *secuti tramitem*; others: *quem secuti sunt tramitem*) *deserere voluerint*: they are corrected from the manuscripts.] which they themselves, following the path of a perverse way, have chosen to desert. For they are to be cut off who [nt: In two manuscripts: *nos*.] trouble you, and would pervert the Gospel of Christ [n: (Galat. V, 12)]. And at the same time we command that whoever [0592C] strive with like obstinacy to defend it, a like punishment should bind them; for *not only they that do, but they also that consent to them that do [n: (Rom. I, 32)]* [nt: Supply: *constringere par vindicta debet*. In Quesnel there is added here *digni sunt poena*. I scarcely know whether on any authority. In place of these, in Merlin and the Augustine edition, from two Vatican manuscripts, one finds *digni sunt morte*. Garnier, however, presents these as words of Scripture: *Non solum enim digni sunt morte qui faciunt, sed etiam qui consentiunt facientibus*. Those three readings smack of an interpolator's work all the more because in our manuscripts, as in the Roman edition, Crabbe and others, the pure words of the Apostle stand. And it was enough indeed to have indicated this to bishops versed in the Scriptures.]: because I think there is not much difference between the mind of him who commits and the favour of him who consents. I add yet more: he generally unlearns erring with whom no one agrees. Let this sentence therefore, dearest brethren, remain fixed against the aforesaid: let them be absent from the courts of the Lord, let them be deprived [nt: The particle *duntaxat* is missing in the Augustine edition. Garnier not only passed it over but also changed Innocent's sentence thus: *Caveat custodia pastoralis*. Just as that pope, at the opening of this epistle, praises the shepherds who do not disdain to protect *with the circumspection of their former keeping* the sheep that *avoid what they had long unlawfully sought*, that is, that have turned to a better life; so now he wishes those sheep which obstinately remain in error to lack pastoral keeping, or to be separated from the flock which has been committed to the keeping of the shepherds.] only of pastoral keeping, lest the dire contagion of two sheep [0593A] should perhaps creep through the unwary crowd, and the wolf with ravening heart rejoice within the Lord's sheepfold over such scattered troops of sheep, while the wound of two is overlooked by the keepers. We must take care therefore lest, by admitting wolves, we seem to be hirelings rather than shepherds. We do indeed command (since Christ our Lord has signified by his own [nt: Garnier: *propria voluntate significavit*. The word *significavit* was thence transferred into the Augustine edition.] voice that he does not will the death of the dying [n: (Ezech. XXXIII, 11; II Pet. III, 9)], but only that he turn and live), that if ever they come back to [nt: In one Colbert manuscript *ad sanum* is lacking, in place of which the other presents *sano*, Quesnel *sanum*, Garnier *sani*.] a sound mind, having laid aside the error of their depraved doctrine, and condemn those things by the transgression of which they have condemned themselves, the customary medicine, that is, her own shelter, be not denied them by the Church: lest, while we perhaps forbid them as they return, [nt: In Quesnel, as in one Colbert manuscript: *velut*, for *vere*. In place of this word Garnier substituted *ores*. Then in the Augustine edition, from Vatican manuscripts, as in Labbe's margin: *remanentes et exspectantes*. Then Merlin: *hostis rapidi*. The other editions of the Councils, with the Roman: *hostis rapidis*. But Garnier omits the word *rabidis*, and substitutes none for it.] remaining truly outside the sheepfold, they be swallowed by the raging jaws of the waiting enemy, [0593B] [nt: Garnier: *quas in semetipsas*. Quesnel, with an old Colbert exemplar: *quo auctore semetipsos quibusdam* (the Colbert manuscript: *quosdam*) *spiritalis*.] which they have armed against themselves with the darts of evil disputation. Farewell, brethren. Given on the sixth day before the Kalends of February [n: (27 Jan. ann. 417)], Honorius and [nt: In the Isidorian manuscripts: *Constantino*. In Merlin: *Constantio consulibus*. In the other editions of the Councils and the Roman: *Constantio viris clarissimis* (one Colbert codex: *vv. cc.*) *consulibus*. In Garnier: *Honorio XI et Constantio II coss*. For that consular note to be accurate, it ought to be expressed thus: *Honorio Augusto XI et Constantio v. c.* (that is, *viro clarissimo*, not *vv. cc.*, which is *viris clarissimis*) *consulibus*. But this note is to be reformed from epistles 29 and 31, which have come down to us from manuscripts of better note than this one. For it seems not to be doubted that Innocent signed the three letters, given together, in the same manner, and so *post consulatum Theodosii VII et Junii Quarti*. But because after the consulship of Theodosius VII and Junius Quartus Honorius and Constantius were distinguished with the same dignity, the conjecture is that *Honorio et Constantio cons.* was added in the margin by the work of some copyist, and that this scholion was carried over from the margin into the text.] Constantius, most illustrious men, being consuls.

## EPISTLE [nt: In the edition of the Councils, 26. And what was 59 is now 5.] XXXI. *Innocent writes back to the five bishops that he has already made sufficiently known what he thought of their judgment and what of the perfidy of Pelagius: that the abettors of this heretic, if they are at Rome, lie hidden; but that wherever they live they are to be condemned, and their salvation provided for: that Pelagius's clearing of himself in Palestine is very suspect to him; but that he neither blames nor approves his judges; that there is no need for him to summon him to Rome; that his book is certainly full of blasphemies: that if he himself anathematizes his errors, those whom he has led astray will more easily be recalled; but that these at least must be cared for.*

*INNOCENT [nt: In the Corbie manuscript: *Dilectissimis fratribus Aurelio, Alypio, Augustino, Evodio, Possidonio Innocentius*.], to the bishops AURELIUS, ALYPIUS, AUGUSTINE, EVODIUS, POSSIDIUS, greeting.* [0594A]
