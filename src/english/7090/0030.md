## 4.

Concerning [nt: Labbe, as in the Royal manuscript: *bigamis*. The other books more elegantly: *digamis*.] digamists, however, there ought not even to have been a consultation, since the reading of the Apostle is manifest, that *a man of one wife* ought to be admitted to the priesthood or to the clerical state, and this very one only if he took her a virgin [n: (I Tim. III, 2)]. For she who has had a husband before, although he be dead, yet if she has afterwards been joined to a cleric, the cleric who has taken her cannot be one, because it is provided in the law that a priest cannot have as wife a widow, nor one cast off [n: (Levit. XXI, 13)].

## CHAP. III.



## 5.

But thy religion has consulted concerning laymen, whom the canons forbid to be ordained [n: (Vide concil. Sardic. c. 13)]. It is certain indeed that the ecclesiastical rules contain this: but it has not been so defined as to have been decreed of [0604B] all laymen. For clerics cannot be born and not be made: but [n: (Dist. 51, c. 2; Ivo. p. 6, c. 95)] the kinds have been marked out from which men cannot come to the clerical state, that is, if any of the faithful has served as a soldier, if any of the faithful has pleaded causes, that is, [nt: By this word is designated the advocate, who demands that penalties be decreed by the judges against the accused, just as by the word *administrantis* just after is indicated the judge, whose part it is to decree penalties and torments of this kind, or even to pronounce a capital sentence. About both of these Exsuperius, bishop of Toulouse, once doubted whether they were to be admitted even to communion. Innocent, consulted on this matter, answers him in epist. 6, chap. 3 and 5.] has prosecuted, if any of the faithful has held an administration. Concerning curials, moreover, the reason is manifest; since, even if men of this sort are found who ought to be made clerics, yet since they are rather often reclaimed to the curia, one must beware of them [n: (Vide supra epist. 2, n. 14)] on account of the trouble which often comes to the Church from them.

## CHAP. IV.



## 6.

But laymen who were baptized having wives, and have so ordered themselves that their reputation wavers in nothing, so that they have either been joined to clerics, or have clung to monasteries [0604C] from the time they were baptized, and if they have known not a [nt: So Dionysius, which was not changed even in the exemplars sent by Hadrian to Charlemagne. But the manuscripts of the Spanish and Isidorian collections, as in the printed texts: *concubinam*. It is to be noted that Innocent so permits access to the clergy to those who have taken a wife that he wishes them to have the testimony of a life led innocently from the time they were baptized.] concubine, not a mistress, if they have been watchful in all [nt: The word *omnibus*, first struck out by Crabbe and not restored afterwards, is recalled from Merlin and the manuscripts.] good works, such men are not forbidden to be taken up into the lot of the clerical state. CHAP. V.--Yet so, that the times established by the elders be observed in their case [n: (V. Siric. epist. 1, n. 13, et Zosim. epist. 9, n. 5)]. Nor let anyone quickly become a lector, quickly an acolyte, quickly a deacon, quickly a priest: because if in [nt: The Royal exemplar: *in omnibus officiis*. From this place it is seen how frivolous is what an unskilled writer writes, who, having persuaded himself that the Roman pontiffs were accustomed to ordain only once a year, in the month of December, contends that those who had been ordained had to wait, by no law save that of necessity, until another year, in order to be advanced to a higher grade.] the lesser offices [0605A] they continue long, both their life and their services alike are approved, so that afterwards, having served out the merits of their stipends, they may come to the priesthood, and may not snatch beforehand what a proved life deserves to receive.

## 7.

Since, then, it has been shown by a certain definition who ought to be admitted, or who rejected: out of all those whom thy worthiness sees cannot be rejected thou shalt be bound to choose those whom thou wouldst make clerics. For if we are willing to show NO FAVOUR or benefit to men, we can find such men that, concerning their taking up, we may be able neither to incur scandal nor to blush.

## EPISTLE [nt: In the edition of the Councils, 5. This letter holds a place in the Dionysian collection, whence it was transcribed into the Spanish and Isidorian, and it is entitled title or decree xxviii of Innocent.] XXXVIII. *That those who have begotten sons while in the presbyterate ought to be removed from office.*

*INNOCENT TO MAXIMUS and SEVERUS, bishops among the Bruttii.*

[0605B]The norm of the ecclesiastical canons ought to be unknown to none of the priests: because it is unbecoming enough for these things not to be known by a pontiff, especially since they are both known by religious lay men and held to be binding. Recently indeed [n: (Dist. 81, c. 6)]. Maximilian our son, an *agens in rebus*, [nt: The printed texts, with the manuscripts of the Spanish and Isidorian collections: *in rebus hujusmodi qualem querelam*: they are corrected from the exemplars of Dionysius and Hadrian.] the series of his petition, annexed, declares what sort of complaint he has brought. Who, led by zeal for faith and discipline, does not suffer the Church to be polluted by unworthy presbyters, who, he asserts, have begotten sons while in the presbyterate. That this is not lawful I would set forth, did I not know that your prudence has knowledge of the whole law. And therefore, dearest brethren, having looked into the tenor of the petition which is subjoined, you will order those who are said to have perpetrated such things to be placed in the midst; and the objections which are thrust upon these presbyters having been examined, [0605C] [nt: So the manuscripts of the collections of Dionysius and Hadrian. But others, with the printed texts: *si convicti fuerint*; and just after *tractare* for *tentare*. It does not escape me that the Africans, Code of the Canons of the African Church, c. 4, decreeing *that the bishop, the presbyter and the deacon, or those who HANDLE the sacraments, as guardians of chastity abstain from their wives*, seem to favour the latter reading. But we have thought that we must by no means depart from the manuscripts which we have found more genuine: especially since here the discourse is about presbyters alone, and by the name *sanctorum* are understood not only the sacraments of the body and blood of Christ, but whatever things belong to the ministry of presbyters. Wherefore the word *tentare*, the more general it is, the more apt it is also for expressing this.] if they can be convicted, let them be removed from the priestly office: because those who are not holy cannot handle holy things; and let them be made strangers to the ministry which they have polluted [nt: The Royal manuscript: *polluunt*.] by living unlawfully. We wonder, moreover, that their bishops overlook these things, so that they are judged either to connive or not to know them to be unlawful.

## EPISTLE [nt: In the edition of the Councils, 6. It was inserted into the Spanish collection and Isidore's following Dionysius Exiguus, who placed it under title XXXIV of the decrees of Innocent.] XXXIX. *That after penance no one can be admitted to the clergy.*

[0606A]

*INNOCENT TO AGAPITUS, MACEDONIUS and [nt: In the printed texts, *Mauriano*; the Royal manuscript, *Martino*. The rest, *Mariano*.] MARIANUS, bishops of Apulia.*

That many things are usurped by very many in the province against the ecclesiastical canons and the decrees of the elders, both the reports of divers persons and most faithful suggestions have brought back: which indeed could easily be cut away if bishops were not found the authors in these things, who, while they strive to show favour either to friends or to those who are obsequious, violate religion and corrupt the orders. And so it comes about that all the unworthy take up ecclesiastical honours, and those are admitted to the clergy who would not deserve to have a worthy place even among the laity: just as has been shown in the petition now given to us, [0606B] that a certain Modestus, involved in many crimes, on account of which he is also said to have done penance, has not only been made a cleric, which is not lawful, but also is reaching toward the summit of the episcopate; whereas the canons established at Nicaea exclude penitents even from the lowest offices of the clergy [nt: Not indeed in express words, but because the fathers of this council decree that from which this is understood to follow. Nay, since in can. 10 they profess to follow the ecclesiastical rule on this matter, they set it down as already established before. And indeed that reasoning applies to all who are defiled by any stain of grave sin, by which Cyprian, epistle 72, with the common consent of the brethren, keeps back and removes from the clergy those who were returning to the Church from heresy or schism: *For priests and ministers who serve the altar and the sacrifices ought to be whole and spotless.* To this tend in truth the laws which the Church sanctioned from the beginning, that none should be promoted to the clergy save those who had led from baptism a life harmless and clean from every stain of any crime. But if anyone's fault, at first hidden, was afterwards discovered, the Nicene canons 2 and 10 will that he, when at length convicted, be deposed. See above, epist. 16, n. 11.] [n: (Dist. 50, c. 60; Nic. conc. c. 10)]. And therefore, dearest brethren, having looked into the tenor of the petition, you will order him to be presented: and if it has truly been established that he is such as the petition affirms, let him be removed not only from the ambition, [nt: Crabbe and the other editors after him: *ab episcopatus ambitione*; Merlin and all the manuscripts opposing. Nor indeed is Modestus kept only from the episcopate, but from any grade whatever which he had not yet attained, and from which he was reaching toward the summit of the episcopate.] but also from the office of the clerical state.

## EPISTLE [nt: In the edition of the Councils, 8. It is presented in the collections of Isidore, the Spanish and Dionysius. By the last it is called decree xxxvi of Innocent.] XL. *That it is not lawful to invade another's parish, or to do anything in it without consulting its own bishop.*

*INNOCENT TO FLORENTINUS, [nt: So the old codices of the collections of Dionysius and Hadrian. But others: *Florentio*.] bishop of Tibur.*

[0606C]Not once but several times divine Scripture cries out that the bounds set by the fathers ought not to be moved: because it is wickedness if one should invade what another has always possessed, which our brother and fellow bishop Ursus asserts that thy goodness has perpetrated. For he has complained, not without grief, that thou didst invade the parish of Nomentum, or of Felicia, [nt: In the Royal manuscript: *Faciliensem*. Next, the word *paroecia* signifies not a collection of several churches subject to a bishop, in which sense it is commonly taken in early times, but a single church assigned to one presbyter. In the same sense it is used above too, epist. 25, n. 8, by Innocent himself.] belonging from of old to his [nt: Paschasius Quesnel, on Leo's first letter, note 4, laying it down as certain that the word DIOCESE *was hardly used in those times* (of Leo) *by the bishops of the Romans or of the Eastern parts for a provincial parish subject to some bishop*, raises this place of Innocent against himself, and says that in it *the word diocese seems to him to be taken for administration*, though he admits that in the use in which it is now frequent it was familiar both to the Africans at that time and, after Leo's time, to the Gauls. But since there were then only two dioceses in Italy in the sense in which this word signifies an administration, namely the Roman, over which the Roman prelate presided, and the Italian, which was under the bishop of Milan, in that sense the parish of Nomentum belonged to the diocese of the Roman prelate, not to that of Ursus or Florentinus. But we shall see that the word diocese was used for the episcopal territory by Hilarus, Leo's successor, as also by Gelasius, Vigilius, etc. The same is its use both in Augustine's epistle 209, n. 8, and in these words of the first council of Toledo, can. 20: *It has pleased that from this day no other but the bishop shall make chrism and send it through the* DIOCESE.] [0607A] diocese, and celebrated the divine mysteries there, without consulting him and without his knowledge. If this is true, know that thou hast not lightly [nt: Crabbe added *culpam* here of his own: a word which would require not *non leviter* but *non levem*. Yet it was not corrected afterwards. Further, *incurrere* and *offendere* are synonyms in Tully.] fallen into it. Wherefore, if thou desirest to decline the odium of so great a usurpation, it befits thee, admonished by our letter, to abstain. Certainly, if thou believest that any justice supports thee, all things remaining untouched and in their former state, thou must be present after the venerable days of Easter, so that thou mayest be able to answer the charges of the [nt: The printed texts, after Crabbe: *memoratis*. More fittingly Merlin and the manuscripts: *memorati*, namely of Ursus. Next, *intentionibus*, that is, the expostulation. For *intentio* was familiar to the ancients for a controversy and expostulation, or a suit brought.] aforementioned: and, the parties having been placed in the midst, we may inquire what antiquity or truth holds.

## EPISTLE [nt: In the edition of the Councils, 20. It has come down to us by the help of the three or four collections so often named. In Dionysius it is headed *decree XLIX of Innocent, on the Bonosians, that they are to be compared to the Jews*. This summary, copied thence by other compilers of canons, prevailed both in all the manuscripts and in the early editions of the Councils and the Roman. Nay, from this summary and another of the preceding epistle 16 joined together, Cresconius composed chapter 58 of his breviary in this manner: *On the Bonosian heretics, that they are to be compared to the Jews. And on receiving the clerics whom the same Bonosus ordained before he was condemned. From the decrees of Pope Innocent, tit. XLIX and L.* Hence it came about that Merlin, putting that twofold title before this letter, subjoined to it the preceding epistle 16 as though it were its second chapter. The heresies of Bonosus and Photinus were indeed akin, as we observed in the preliminary Notice to epistle 9 of Siricius, no. 7; yet there is no reason why we should depart from the letter of our epistle, in which mention is made of the followers of Photinus, not of Bonosus. Nay, if this letter was written to the bishop of Senia in Croatia, or, as they used to call it, in Liburnia, not to the bishop of Sena in Etruria, it will be beyond controversy that the partisans of Photinus, not of Bonosus, are noted in it. Moreover no one is unaware that Photinus, when he was bishop at Sirmium in Pannonia, to which Liburnia is neighbouring, infected those provinces with the poison of his heresy. Lastly, Innocent speaks of a heresy which he intimates was spread through almost the whole world, which certainly fits the Photinian rather than the recently arisen Bonosian. And the author of the heresy which Innocent rebukes is said to be Marcus, not because he devised it, but because he showed more zealous effort in propagating it.] XLI. *That Laurentius should take care that heretics following the poisons of Photinus be expelled.*

*[0607B]INNOCENT TO LAURENTIUS, bishop [nt: In some manuscripts: *Siniensi*. In Merlin: *Symensi*.] of Senia.*

We have long wondered, having read the letter of thy love, that heretics following the poisons of Photinus are not only in the territory of thy love, but also publicly prepare conventicles for themselves on the possessions of certain people; whereas nowhere from almost the whole world have so many chosen to dwell as among you. The author of whose nefarious doctrine, Marcus, driven out of the City some time ago, has been led by the audacity of such great rashness as to claim the first place among them for himself. But lest they have any further opportunity of raging, and drag with them into Gehenna, to which they are destined, the souls of the simple and of the rustic, [nt: The Roman edition: *rusticorum*. The other books more correctly: *rusticanorum*: by which word are designated not the inhabitants of the country, but men lacking learning.] action has been taken against them by the defenders [nt: The name and office of the defenders of the Roman church, if I remember well, we now read mentioned for the first time in the letters of the supreme pontiffs. But Innocent does not speak of that office as newly instituted. Mention of them will recur at the end of epistle 9, of Zosimus to Hesychius.] of our Church, by which they may be able to expel them: [0608A] so that those who deny that Christ God was begotten of the substance of the Father before the ages may have a share in the condemnation with the Jews, who denied his deity and deny it even until now. It is thy part, dearest brother, to carry out the things commanded not the more sluggishly: lest thou lose by dissimulation the people entrusted to thee, and begin to render account to God for the lost.

## EPISTLE XLII. OR FRAGMENTS OF THE LETTER OF PELAGIUS TO POPE INNOCENT, WRITTEN AFTER HIS DEATH, BUT WHEN HE DID NOT KNOW HIM TO BE DEAD.



## 1.

*Among these, as Augustine testifies [n: (Lib. de Gratia Christi, c. 30)]*, *Pelagius says* that there are things about which men try to defame him: one, that he denies to little ones the sacrament of baptism, and promises the kingdom of heaven to some without the redemption [0608B] of Christ: the other, that he says that man can so avoid sin as to exclude the help of God, and trusts so much in free will as to repudiate the aid of grace.

## 2.

*Augustine continues [n: (Ibid., c. 31)]*: *When he had mixed in with these invidious complaints about his enemies, he comes to the matter thus:* Behold, let this letter clear me before thy beatitude, in which purely and simply we say that we have whole free will for sinning and for not sinning, which in all good works is always helped by divine aid.

## 3.

*Then, says Augustine [n: (Ibid.,)]*, *having interposed certain things about the condition of man, and about his natural possibility of sinning and of not sinning, he added:* [0609A] Which power of free will we say is in all generally, in Christians, in Jews and in Gentiles. In all free will is equally by nature: but in Christians alone it is helped by grace. In the former the good of their condition is naked and unarmed: but in these who belong to Christ it is fortified by the help of Christ. The former are to be judged and condemned because, since they have free will, through which they could come to faith and merit [nt: On these words Augustine: "Plainly, he says, it is manifest that he says grace is given according to merits, whatever or whatsoever kind of grace he means; which however he does not openly express."] the grace of God, they make ill use of the liberty granted. But these are to be rewarded who, making good use of free will, merit the grace of the Lord and keep his commandments.
