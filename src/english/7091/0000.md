## NOTICE ON THE FOUR FOLLOWING LETTERS.

[0627D]

## 1.

Of those four letters, Nicephorus Callisti, book XIII, chap. 34, has handed down to us the first entire in Greek, and Glycas, *Annals*, part IV, mutilated, with some things cut out here and there; the second and the fourth Glycas alone preserved, in the same place, likewise in Greek: but the third has come down to us only in Latin, copied by the labour of Baronius from a Vatican exemplar. The same Cardinal, [0628D] at the year 407, added to those letters a fifth, of the emperor Honorius to Arcadius, which he had dug out of an old codex of Cardinal Guglielmo Sirleto.

## 2.

Common to all these writings is this mark of forgery, that they make Eudoxia survive John Chrysostom, though it is established by the testimonies of Socrates, Sozomen, Marcellinus, Prosper, Zosimus, and of the Greek acts, that she died in childbirth three years before him. Certainly George of Alexandria is not to be compared with these, on whose authority the emperor Leo, [0629A] surnamed the Philosopher, Cedrenus, Nicephorus and other later Greeks prolonged the life of Eudoxia by three years. Besides, no history reports that the supreme power and favour of this Empress over the mind of Arcadius was diminished in the least, right up to her death: which is nevertheless feigned in the second and fourth letter. Nor is it less foreign to historical faith than to ecclesiastical custom, what is read in the first letter, where Innocent is related, after the death of Chrysostom, to separate Arcadius and Eudoxia from the receiving of the sacraments, to cast Arsacius down from the episcopal rank even after death, and to strike with the same penalty those who communicated with him in his ordination, and to smite Theophilus not only with excommunication but also with anathema, and to remove him from all fellowship of Christians. For in the first place, that the Roman Pontiffs were accustomed to conduct themselves by a far different method with princes, even heretical ones, the letters of Liberius to Constantius, and of Gelasius, Symmachus, Anastasius II and Hormisdas to the emperor Anastasius [0629B] are witnesses. Next, they are not wont to impose so grave a penalty even on any private person, except after admonitions sent before. But where are those admonitions? If Innocent had wished to employ them, he would have learned of the death of Arcadius, who survived Chrysostom by scarcely seven months, before he could have used that extreme remedy. Moreover, it is certain from Innocent's own letters that he reserved the judgment of the whole cause of Chrysostom to an ecumenical synod. Wherefore this pope [n: (Innoc. epist. 5)], having testified that he held communion with Theophilus no otherwise than with John, exhorts him by repeated letters « that if he trusts in the judgment, he present himself at the synod. » Likewise, writing to the people of Constantinople, « We are considering, he says [n: (Epist. 7, n. 4)], how an ecumenical synod may be assembled, so that the turbulent commotions may at last, by the nod of God, be quieted. » And at his request the emperor Honorius wrote a third time to Arcadius [n: (Epist. 9)], threatening him with no threats, but only beseeching « that he order Theophilus, bishop of Alexandria, [0629C] who is said to be the author of all the evils, to present himself even against his will » at the synod. But while Innocent was considering this, the death first of John, and a little after of Arcadius, intervening, brought delays to the assembling of the synod. Yet he did not for that reason cast aside his first counsel, nor even, until the desired synod could be held, did he altogether break off peace and communion with the adversaries of John, but only interrupted and suspended it. Of which matter Palladius, the writer of the Life of Chrysostom, speaks thus toward the end of the dialogue, page 214: « This is the aim of the Roman church: not to communicate with the Eastern bishops, especially [0630A] with Theophilus, until the Lord shall have given place to a universal synod, which may heal the putrefied members of those who perpetrated these things. » Where it is not the judgment of the Roman church that is noted, but its *aim*, or counsel, which it judged it should use until a synod should decide concerning this matter. And indeed Theophilus never ceased to be counted among the bishops of Alexandria even by the Roman prelates. The same is to be said of his other associates, if Arsacius be excepted, whom, as Theodoret witnesses [n: (lib. V, cap. 34)], the bishops of the West « did not deign even to greet. » But with Atticus, put into his place, Innocent himself held it enough to keep communion suspended, being ready to restore it to him when he had satisfied the agreed conditions. Wherefore, to the bishop Maximian, who amicably asked him to send letters of peace to Atticus, he wrote back: « *Suspended* communion is restored to one who shows that the causes by which it had happened are now wiped away, and who professes that the conditions of peace have been fulfilled. » And Theodoret relates that he was in fact, after he had put the name of John back into the sacred tablets, received by the Westerns [0630B].

## 3.

Yet from those things which Theodoret writes in the place cited, he seems to have furnished the matter and occasion for the first letter, and so for the others as well, which so agree with the first that we scarcely doubt at all that there was one fabricator of them all. Nor does it stand in the way that, although Theodoret relates of Arsacius, while he was living, that the bishops of the West so shrank from communion with him that they did not deign even to greet him, the first letter nevertheless removes this same man from his rank *after death,* not before. For from an impostor, who cannot hold himself to the truth even in expressing the things he imitates, the highest fidelity is by no means to be expected. But from that addition, *even after death,* which appears to have been inserted of set purpose, one may fish out the time at which those letters were got up. Certainly, if they had been public at the time when the emperor Justinian was searching everywhere for examples by which it might be [0630C] proved that Theodore of Mopsuestia could lawfully be excommunicated even after death, so notable a case of Arsacius, removed from his rank *even after death*, would not have escaped him. Yet it is nowhere read that he brought it forward: which is no light argument that those letters too did not yet exist anywhere at that time, that is, in the middle of the VI century. And that affected expression, *even after death,* seems to have been inserted for no other reason than to favour those who wished Theodore of Mopsuestia to be excommunicated. Whence one may conjecture that those letters were forged in the sixth century itself, when the aforesaid controversy was not yet laid to rest.

## EPISTLE I. *Sentence is pronounced against Arcadius, Eudoxia, Arsacius and Theophilus.*

[0629]

*OF INNOCENT, ARCHBISHOP OF ROME,* [nt: Nicephorus judged this letter to be common, as it were, to Arcadius, Atticus and Theophilus. He, namely, states first that Innocent, because the legates whom he had sent to Constantinople had been ill-used and unworthily treated, was greatly indignant, and, when a little later he had learned of the death of John, *gave a letter to Arcadius, Atticus and Theophilus, by which he subjected them to excommunication and deposition*. Then he notes that this letter was written in words of this kind: *The voice of the blood of my brother John*, etc. In it, however, there is no word about Atticus. But, as if he wished to meet this observation, having copied out the whole letter he subjoins next: *These things Innocent wrote, fighting for John. Nor yet did he absolve from the charge Atticus either, who after Arsacius* inrasit *into the see of that great man (John)* (ἔξω αἰτίας ἐποίει) *. But how that matter was composed, history will show in its place*, namely in book XIV, chap. 25, where peace is said to have been restored, from the time Atticus ordered mention of John to be made in the prayers.] *TO ARCADIUS THE EMPEROR.*

## 1.

[0629D]The voice of the blood of my brother John cries to God against thee, O emperor, as once that of Abel the just against the fratricide Cain: and he shall be avenged in every way. Nor hast thou committed this only, but even in time of peace thou hast stirred up a great persecution against God and his Church. Thou hast cast out of his [nt: In Nicephorus τῆς ἐπισκοπῆς is omitted here, but below, at n. 3, the same word is kept.] episcopal [0631A] see, the case not judged, the great Teacher of the whole world, and together with him thou hast persecuted Christ. Nor do I so lament for him (for he has obtained his lot and inheritance with the holy Apostles in the kingdom of God and of our saviour Jesus Christ), although that loss is intolerable; but I am afflicted on this account, that first concerning the salvation of your souls, then concerning those who, being deprived of his most wise and spiritual and divine teaching and instruction, are now compelled to suffer famine. For not only did the church of Constantinople suffer the loss of this honeyed tongue, but the whole earth also which is under the sun has been widowed, having lost such a divine man through the persuasion of one woman, who permitted this outrage to be committed. But she will receive both the present punishment here and the everlasting [0631B] torment hereafter, which will come upon her not so very long after. For although blessed John, after he had kept the faith and had strengthened those who were wavering, left this life, yet he has obtained for the eternal ages the inheritance of everlasting delights and of immortal life. But Eudoxia, that new Delilah, who little by little shaved thee with the razor of imposture, has brought upon herself a curse out of the mouth of many, has bound a burden of sins heavy [nt: In Glycas *portatuque difficillimum*. *Quod portari nequeat* pleases more, since it is evident that this place was expressed in imitation of this in Matth. xxiii, 4: *For they bind heavy and unbearable burdens*.] and such as cannot be carried, and has added it over her former sins [n: (Jud. XVI, 19)].

## 2.

Wherefore I, the least and a sinner, as one to whom the see of the great apostle Peter has been entrusted, separate thee and her from the receiving of the immaculate mysteries of Christ our God. And also every bishop or [0631C] cleric of the order of the holy Church of God who shall have dared to administer or to hand them to you from that hour at which [nt: Labbe, from Glycas, in Greek: τὸν πεμφθέντα σύνδεσμον.] the bond with which I bind you shall have become known to you, to have fallen from his dignity [I decree]. But if, as powerful men, you shall have driven anyone to this by force, and shall have transgressed the canons handed down to you by the Saviour through the holy Apostles, you will find on the dread day of judgment, first indeed, that this will be no small sin for you, and next, that no one can then be helped by any dignity of this age: but the hidden things of souls will break forth and be displayed before the sight of all.
