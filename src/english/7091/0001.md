## 3.

But Arsacius, whom you intruded into the episcopal see [nt: This Arsacius, who had promised and sworn to his brother Nectarius, with the Gospels touched, that he would never accept ordination to any episcopate, as Palladius relates, page 94, died when fourteen months had passed after his ordination.] in the place of the great John, we remove from his rank even after death, together with all the bishops who [nt: The Greek word προθέσει is lacking in Glycas. It was added of set purpose: lest those too should seem to be held by this sentence who had communicated with Arsacius unwillingly. Palladius, page 95, relates that almost all communicated not of their own accord with his successor Atticus as well.] by design communicated with him. And let his name not be inscribed in the sacred tablets: for he is unworthy of this [0631D] honour, inasmuch as he polluted the episcopate by a kind of adultery. For every planting which has not been planted by our Father who is in heaven shall be rooted up [n: (Matth. XV, 13)]. But Theophilus, not only cast down from his see, but also punished with excommunication and anathema, we declare altogether estranged from the fellowship of Christians.

* EPISTLE OF INNOCENT, HIGH PRIEST OF ROME, TO ARCADIUS THE EMPEROR. * [0630C]

[0630D]1. The voice of the blood of my brother John cries to God against thee, O king, as once that of Abel the just against the fratricide Cain, and in every way it shall be avenged. Not only hast thou done this too, but even in a time of peace thou hast set on foot a great persecution against the Church of God and his priests. Thou hast thrust the great teacher of the [0632A] inhabited world out of the throne of his episcopate without judgment, persecuting Christ together with him. For neither do I so lament over him (for he has obtained his lot with the holy apostles in the kingdom of God and our saviour Jesus Christ); even if the calamity is unbearable; but I am afflicted, being anxious, first for the salvation of your souls, and for those who have been deprived of his all-wise and spiritual divine teaching and admonition and have hungered for it. For not only has the church of the Constantinopolitans been bereft of his honey-flowing tongue, but the whole world under the sun has also been widowed, having lost so godly a man, through the compliance of one woman who allowed this drama to take place. But she shall receive the punishment from this world and the endless chastisement to come, coming upon [0632B] her not many days after these. For even though blessed John has left this life, having kept the faith and strengthened those who were shaken, yet for unending ages he has inherited the unfailing delight and immortal life. And the new Delilah, Eudoxia, who little by little shaved thee with the razor of deceit, has brought upon herself a curse out of many mouths, having bound up a burden of sins heavy and hard to bear, adding this to her former sins.

## 2.

Wherefore I, the least, and a sinner, as one entrusted with the throne of the great apostle Peter, excommunicate thee and her from the partaking of the immaculate mysteries of Christ our God; and not only so, but also every bishop, [0632C] or cleric being of the order of the holy Church of God, who dare to undertake to give them to you, from the hour at which you read this present bond of mine, to be under deposition. But if you force anyone, as men in authority, and transgress the canons given to you by the saviour through the holy apostles, you shall be knowing that, first, the sin will be no small one for you on the fearful day of judgment, nor will worldly rank be able to help anyone; and the inward parts of those shall be poured out for an example to all.

## 3.

And Arsacius, whom you brought in onto the throne of the episcopate in place of the great John, even after death we depose, together with all the bishops who communicated with him by design. Of whom let not even his name be written in the sacred diptychs; for he is unworthy, as having committed adultery upon [0632D] the episcopate. For every plant not planted by our Father who is in the heavens shall be rooted up. And we add to the deposition of Theophilus excommunication and anathematization and complete estrangement from Christianity.

## EPISTLE II.

*OF ARCADIUS THE EMPEROR TO POPE INNOCENT.* [0633A]

*That he free him and Eudoxia from excommunication.*

All men in possession of their mind know that things committed through ignorance are punished neither by God nor through the laws: but neither does anyone pay penalties in another's name. Wherefore I have written these things, to make thy gentleness certain (for which thing I implore the benign God as witness) that I had knowledge [nt: Concerning the things done with the legates, Palladius, page 32 and following, speaks in such a way as not to betray that the Emperor was privy to them. Nay rather, relating that men were sent who tried to corrupt the legates, he confesses that he does not know whether this was done by the work of the Emperor's courtiers or of Atticus. Whence the falsity of these letters is more and more manifest, by which Arcadius is punished with a most grave penalty on account of the things which the legates had suffered; since not even they could affirm that they had suffered these things with him privy to them. Is the most wise pontiff to be believed to have done with a most powerful emperor that which any judge would be ashamed to do with even the humblest man? since the laws forbid anyone to be punished unless convicted or confessed.] of none of those things which befell our prelates and clerics, and that I am altogether innocent of their condemnation [nt: Nor do these things agree with the narrative of Palladius, from which it is plain that everything was then done by force, with no judgment preceding.]. For since I have used their communion [nt: How could Arcadius be said to have used the communion of those who were not permitted even to greet him, nay, not even to enter the royal city, as Palladius witnesses, p. 32?], there was no cause why I should afflict them. But as regards thy daughter Eudoxia, know that I have exacted from her no mean penalties in proportion to her offence, so that because of this kind of grief and distress she lies seized by a most grave illness. For the rest I beseech thee [0633B] to free us from the separation, and not to punish us manifold. For not even the Lord himself strikes twice for the same offence *(Naum I, 9)*.

* EPISTLE OF ARCADIUS THE EMPEROR TO INNOCENT, HIGH PRIEST OF ROME. * [0634A]

All men who are right-minded know that the things done in ignorance neither God nor laws punish; nor is anyone made to pay the penalty on behalf of another. Hence I have written these things, assuring your gentleness, as the benevolent God bears witness, that I knew nothing of what befell our bishops and clerics, but am guiltless of their judgment. For if I had been a sharer with them, I would not for this have had cause to harm them. Concerning thy daughter Eudoxia also, know that I have requited her too, according to her fault, not moderately, so that from such grief and affliction she lies in a most grievous sickness. I entreat thee, then, to release us from the excommunication, and not to avenge manifoldly. For the Lord will not strike twice for the same thing.

## EPISTLE [nt: In this letter is repeated what had already been written in the preceding one. Wherefore we suspect it to be the work of an idle man who wished to exercise his style.] III. OF ARCADIUS THE EMPEROR TO POPE INNOCENT. *That he is innocent of the things which were committed against his legates or against John; that their authors have been punished by him. And so let him relax the sentence by which he keeps him, with his consort, from the sacred mysteries.*

Altogether, O pontiff of God, not anything [ *Perhaps* nil quidquam] of the things which were boldly perpetrated against the legates [nt: Just as Chrysostom, as soon as he was first cast out of his church, writing to Innocent, Epist. 1, n. 5, testified that all the things which had then been impiously perpetrated had been done contrary to the mind of Arcadius, so also Nicephorus, book xiii, chap. 53, writes that whatever was afterwards done against the legates escaped the notice of *the sluggish Arcadius*.] was done with my knowledge: and this can be [0633C] perceived from the fact that I have severely punished those from whom those things arose. Nor, because that great John was deposed, does any blame for that matter cleave to me, but to the unhappy bishops, because, namely, while they put forward the sacred canons, they turned the judgment of his deposition upon their own profane heads; led on by whose speech, I know not how, I too, to their sentence, which would that I had never done, [0634B] assented. Whence it comes that they themselves are the more in fault. Of whom both those who still remain here, Acacius and Severianus, and those who followed the wicked Theophilus, that with the greatest possible speed, we have ordered to be seized [nt: How shamelessly the architect of these letters lies here is proved by the rescript of Arcadius himself, which Atticus, taking it ill that *none of the Easterners communicated with him*, obtained from this emperor, in which, namely, as Palladius witnesses, page 95, it was enacted: *If any of the bishops does not communicate with Theophilus, Porphyrius and Atticus, let him be cast out of the Church, and driven from his own means*.], and those who are there, that they may pay the penalties due to their daring. But let thy fatherly humanity be appeased toward us: and do not abandon us without share in the uncontaminated mysteries. Then too I punished thy daughter Eudoxia, who had sinned, who also, seized from these things by a most grave illness, lies abed. Do not therefore exact penalties more often, O most reverend [0634C] father: for neither does the Lord exact penalties twice in the same things, as it is written [n: (Naum. I. 9, sec. LXX)]. But where will be the good of penitence [nt: For one truly penitent this was the first thing to be done: that the honour unjustly taken from John should be restored. But scarcely about the year 415, that is, nine years after John's death, did Innocent obtain that the name of this most holy prelate should be put back into the sacred tablets.], unless pardon be granted to those who repent? Therefore, if we were not indeed of the penitent, rightly would the approach to pardon be shut: and if we have accused and condemned ourselves for the things which we did ill; let us not fall away even from the good of penitence, which indeed is pardon.

## EPISTLE [nt: From the genuine letters of Innocent 19, 21, 22 and 23 the falsity of this one, and so of the preceding ones, is plainly proved. For in those this pope is shown to restore peace to those who asked for it, not rashly, but only on prescribed conditions, and those fulfilled. Of those conditions this indeed was the chief, that the name of John should be restored to the sacred diptychs. But now he cares nothing, neither about the name of John, nor about the other bishops attached to him, who were suffering most cruel exiles as if by the authority of Arcadius. So the pontiff, whose wisdom our forebears praise no less in restoring communion to august persons than in taking it away, is feigned to be imprudent.] IV.

[0635]

*OF POPE INNOCENT TO ARCADIUS THE EMPEROR.* [0635A]

[0635] *He absolves Arcadius, and permits him the receiving of the sacred mysteries.*

[0635A]I have approved your zeal, conformable to the will of God, which you have shown on account of his name. Wherefore, loosed from the sacred bonds, approach the Lord's symbols with strengthened minds.

* EPISTLE OF INNOCENT, HIGH PRIEST OF ROME, TO ARCADIUS THE EMPEROR. * [0636A]

I have welcomed your zeal according to God, which you displayed for his name's sake. Hence, released from the sacred bonds, approach the Master's symbols in good strength.
