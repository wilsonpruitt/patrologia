## Exposition of the Canticle Magnificat (continued)

*He has put down the mighty from their seat, and exalted the humble [n: (Luc. I, 52)]*. *The mighty,* because he cast off the sons of the kingdom who seemed to be such, and took up the nations through humble confession into the fellowship of the kingdom and into the adoption of sons, and cast the malignant spirits out of the hearts of men: *and the humble,* that is, humiliated men, *he exalted.* The thought of the heart of God, too, is the living and abiding disposition of eternal and hidden predestination. He himself is the book of life. Therefore the thought is the same as the book. In three ways someone is written in the book: according to foreknowledge, according to cause, and according to operation. As to the first, the predestined are written: they are never blotted out, although for a time one may be permitted to stray from the way of truth as though about to perish. According to cause are written those who, having begun the way of truth, fall away by turning aside to errors. But such are sometimes those who would be worthy of salvation, if they remained such until the end. According to operation are those whose works, according to human judgment, appear such that for them they seem worthy to be written in the book of life: who nevertheless forsake the good things they seemed to do. These, according to foreknowledge, were never in the book of life. *He has put down,* etc. First he cast down the angel from heaven, and man, growing proud, from paradise: and afterward he restored to glory man humbled through penance. But also daily he puts down and humbles all the proud, by withdrawing his grace: and afterward the same, once humbled, he exalts, restoring their former grace. But of whom [1142] is that which follows, *He has filled the hungry with good things, and the rich he has sent empty away [n: (Luc. I, 53)]*? *The hungry* he calls those who know that they lack the true good: by *the rich* he wishes to be understood those who are proud, and think themselves to abound before others in the gifts of graces. Therefore, just as the humble, by thinking little of themselves, deserve to receive greater grace: so the proud, by presuming upon themselves, lose even those things which they had received.

*He has received Israel his servant, being mindful of his mercy [n: (Ibid., 54)]*. *He has received,* as a physician the sick man, *Israel his servant:* that is, he received the humble and innocent, that he might heal the sick, and redeem the captive; that he might justify the ungodly, and save the righteous. *He has received Israel,* not whom he found as Israel, but that he might make him Israel: *being mindful of his mercy,* which he once promised, but had long deferred: *As he spoke to our fathers* (or, *patribus nostris*), *Abraham and his seed for ever [n: (Ibid., 55)]*. Merciful in promising; truthful in fulfilling: because he promised without owing, and fulfilled without deceit. *As he spoke to our fathers, Abraham and his seed for ever.*

Note, upon that phrase, *My spirit has exulted in God my salvation:* some exult neither from God, nor in God. For those who exult in the pleasure of the flesh, or who exult when they have done evil [n: (Prov. II, 14)], these exult neither in God nor from God, and evil is that from which they exult. And because they exult from evil to evil, and set their joy in malignity, therefore they by no means exult in God. There are others who abuse the gifts of grace received, and turn those things which were given for the salvation of the soul to the use of the flesh and the joys of the world: for they rejoice in the gifts received, because they love neither in God nor for God's sake what they have received from God. But those who, having received grace, turn that very thing which they received from him to the love of God, these truly are shown to exult both from God and in God. On the contrary, therefore, that she might show her joy to be solid — that it had neither sprung from vanity, nor been turned to vanity — she affirmed that she loved the gifts of God, and exulted in God, and, for the regard of grace by which she had been prevented, in the Savior, saying: *My spirit has exulted in God my salvation.*
