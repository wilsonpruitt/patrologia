## III.

Exult, O heaven, and be glad, O earth. This day has shone upon you more brightly out of the sepulchre than it has blazed from the sun. Let hell too rejoice, for by the descent of Life to it, it has been loosed from its own impiety. Let it be glad, for it has been visited by the Lord descending into its own seats. Let the man Adam exult, for after long *saecuta* he has seen an unknown light, and, stifled amid the mists of deep night, he has breathed again among the shadows. O lovely light! that flashed forth from the shining summit of heaven, and after the purple streams didst clothe with sudden brightness those sitting [1153D] in darkness and in the shadow of death. Straightway, therefore, the eternal night of the lower regions blazed with light at the descent of Christ. That shrieking of mourners fell silent; the chains of the damned fell away. The minds of the tormentors were struck dumb with astonishment; the whole impious workshop trembled together, when suddenly it saw Christ in its own seats. For who, they say, is this terrible one, flashing with snowy splendor? Never has our Tartarus received such a one; never does the world vomit such a one into our cavern. We see a judge, not a suppliant; he has come to fight, not to yield; to snatch away, not to remain. Where, do you suppose, had our doorkeepers been sleeping, [1154A] when this warrior was battering our bars? If this one were guilty, he would not be so bold. If any offenses darkened him, he would never scatter our darkness with his brightness. But if he is God, what is he doing in a sepulchre? If he is man, why does he loose the captives? Has this one perhaps struck a bargain with our author? Or did he perhaps attack and conquer him too, and so pass over into our realms? Surely he was dead; surely he was conquered. Our warrior has been mocked in the world; he does not know what slaughter he has brought about in hell. O that cross, cheating your joys and bringing our losses to birth! By a tree we were enriched; by a tree we are overthrown. That power perishes, ever dreaded by all peoples. No captive now quivers beneath our slaughter. Behold what must be groaned over — and it exults. Nowhere are the ancient weepings; no wailings [1154B] now resound. The stormy cave has fallen mute. Do you think this one returns without our destruction? No one ever entered to us alive. No one was ever so bold. No one so terrified the executioners. Never in this chasm, blind with black gloom, has a joyous light appeared. Has the sun perhaps departed from the world? But neither heaven nor the stars obey us — and yet hell is bright. What are we doing? Where do we turn? We cannot defend our bloody houses against this one, nor hold the guard-posts of our cavern. He has broken in upon us to our ruin. We cannot hold back so great a light; we have not the strength to crush one endowed with such power. We see our own necks pressed down as well, and now besides we dread our own destruction. Let us be content with the darkness of our night. Let us hide ourselves in our caves. Why are we betrayed by these rays? Why are we thrown into confusion by violence? Why [1154C] are we taken captive in our own seats?

Soon therefore Christ directs his battle line against the cruel ministers of punishments themselves, and cuts down the implacable squadrons with the divine sword. Grim executioners are crushed beneath a tormentor, and the more raging of them waste away with the shrieks forced from them. The very caves of that iron chamber are entered, and the strong are bound with everlasting chains by one stronger. This too the Lord himself had promised, saying: *No one enters into the house of a strong man and will plunder his goods, unless he first bind the strong man, and then he will plunder his goods.* Soon therefore the crowds, sorrowful and mourning in their daylong squalor, and the harried peoples run together, and cast themselves down at the feet of our Redeemer. Behold, the apostolic sayings are proved: *That in the name of the Lord every knee should bow, of things in heaven and things on earth and things beneath the earth.* Soon, then, the captive souls, released from [1154D] their Tartarean prisons, rush forth, and with sorrowful supplication make their lament to the King of the ages: « You have come at last, O most merciful Jesus; help us now and spare the weary. Now, O Christ, quench the savage threats; loose now our pitiable groans. You have redeemed the living by your cross; *cripe* the dead also by your death. The world had perished in the same ruin with us. For your coming every creature sighed. Our torments sighed for you. This hell always grew pale before you. While you are here, absolve the guilty; when you shall have ascended, defend your own. You alone were able to crush the head of the dragon. You willed to break in pieces the gates of bronze and the bars of iron. Let the door lie open, I beg, [1155A] to those who pray: let the kindly light not fail. And if you return to the body, let hell not be deprived of your majesty. »

And so, after the prayers had been heard, after the laws had been set in order, after the torturers had been sunk in the deep abyss [n: ( Leg. voraginis?)], the ditch, our judge today came forth crowned with laurel from the lower regions. Nor was the *canditatum* office lacking; but the whole company of the blessed, in glad array, went forth with its prince. The triumphant one therefore returns to the arena, alive again, so that all the world might know that Christ has come back from the dead. Let believers, then, glory; every nation will clap their hands, because our King has triumphed in the world — who with the Father and the Holy Spirit lives and reigns, God, crucified and raised up again for us, unto the ages of ages. Amen.
