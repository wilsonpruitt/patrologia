## IV. *A sermon of the most illustrious doctor Saint Augustine, dedicated to the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ.*

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1155B]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Hail, O day to be thronged with everlasting assemblies! ⟦s2⟧ At your rising, life rises again; at your shining, the light returns, and on your birthday, the gloom of hell being shut out, the Sun of justice comes to life again. ⟦s3⟧ God indeed does not die, nor is eternity bounded by an end, nor is the light of majesty put out; but when death is sought out by God, help is brought to man through a man. ⟦s4⟧ After the triumphs of the cross, therefore, after the conquering standards, Christ in exultation makes for the lower regions, to war down death as well — he who had taken captive the author of death. ⟦s5⟧ O wondrous battle, brethren, and new manner of victory regained! ⟦s6⟧ Christ conquered by the tree, whereby the devil had before conquered; [1155C] and he who had laid low was laid low by his own arms; and that very thing became his punishment which had before been his victory. ⟦s7⟧ God therefore fought for the world, and, hiding his majesty for a little while so as to give the enemy confidence, he feigned his power to be weak. ⟦s8⟧ For the cause of the world was being pleaded through the wisdom of God. ⟦s9⟧ He was held as one bound, he was heard as one guilty, he was scourged and spat upon as one lowborn and a captive. ⟦s10⟧ But he for this purpose was pressing down his own strength by concealing it, until, having died, he should conquer the one he was preparing to conquer. ⟦s11⟧ He reined in his power so that he might fulfill all justice in the conflict, and a full victory in rising again. ⟦s12⟧ Hence he came to the tree, whence the rights of death had gone forth. ⟦s13⟧ But where he mounted the citadel of wickedness, there he subdued the summit of the enemy's power. ⟦s14⟧ That tree, once the mother and origin of punishments, unwitting, received the just one, transfixes him, afflicts him; there is no guilt, and the punishment rages. ⟦s15⟧ O most cruel [1155D] slayer of the just! ⟦s15⟧ You have lost the supports of your own case. ⟦s16⟧ The cross itself cries out against you; the workshop of death and wounding drives you back. ⟦s17⟧ I do not acknowledge, it says, that he whom I punish is guilty; these are not the fruits of my own stock, nothing here my head; you are deceived, because you graft onto me a fruit not my own. ⟦s18⟧ This one has nothing from me; why does he hang upon me? ⟦s19⟧ I see the likeness of the outward form, but I do not acknowledge in him the poison native to me. ⟦s20⟧ Then he, strong in the battle, just upon the gibbet, innocent under another's torment, ground down the horns of sin with clean hands, and while he fastens his whole self upon the wood, he blunts the sting [1156A] of death and slays the sin which the tree was begetting. ⟦s21⟧ The blood soon poured out from his side washed away whatever crime had stained before, and from the polluted little branches the cross shone forth white. ⟦s22⟧ The guiltless gore ran through the guilty, punishing fibers of the wood, and thereafter, with its healing, they bring forth sacraments of salvation for those who are to live — they that had once offered the nourishments of death. ⟦s23⟧ Yet still the devil, not knowing, glories that he has conquered; and he sees the conquered one crucified, sees him dead, and struck down besides, and, as though glad over a rich slaughter, is troubled no further. ⟦s24⟧ Behold, he says, the one who used to put himself forward as God's offspring, who had won belief for himself by miracles, who had cast out my companions and partners from human bodies and souls, who besides dared to try me in battle. ⟦s25⟧ He was eager to snatch out of my hands the world which I hold captive from its beginning by the law of contest and the right [1156B] of victory. ⟦s26⟧ Now, if he is strong, let him conquer; now, if he prevails, let him rise; now, if he is God, let him come to life again. ⟦s27⟧ O devil, ignorant of your own end! ⟦s27⟧ This victory deceives you; your joys will not be glad but sorrowful. ⟦s28⟧ To your own ruin you armed the tongues of the Jews; you destroyed yourself with those darts. ⟦s29⟧ What he suffered he indeed willed, and he took counsel for us. ⟦s30⟧ For at the moment when you slew the just one, you lost the guilty whom you had held. ⟦s31⟧ But wait a little; do not exult too insolently or too rashly. ⟦s32⟧ For behold, the lower regions tremble at him alive, whom you were rejoicing over as slain. ⟦s33⟧ Already he has broken the ancient prisons, already he has plundered your spoils, and struck down the bloody ministers of your ferocity. ⟦s34⟧ Is this to have conquered — or to have sent life across to the dead? ⟦s35⟧ Set down your reckoning here, most wicked one; let justice the schoolmistress weigh the judgment. [1156C] From the beginning you put to death the world which you yourself had made guilty; a just vengeance followed upon the harm committed. ⟦s36⟧ But this one neither his own nor another's guilt had bound. ⟦s37⟧ Why did you bring on death where you had found no crime? ⟦s38⟧ Why did you inflict punishment where you could find no offense? ⟦s39⟧ Unjustly, therefore, you punished the just for the unjust; justly you lost the unjust for whom you had killed the just. ⟦s40⟧ Let the guilty one now change his condition, since our cause has taken the better part. ⟦s41⟧ Liberty has arisen within servitude; captivity has come about within dominion. ⟦s42⟧ The yoke of the ancient condition has been taken away; now the indulgence of mercy and grace reigns. ⟦s43⟧ For Christ, rising from the dead out of the sepulchre, has shone upon us today; his soul did not linger in hell, nor did his flesh remain in the tomb, as it is written: *For you will not leave my soul [1156D] in hell, nor will you give your holy one to see corruption.* ⟦s44⟧ Behold, Christ has now risen again as soon as he willed. ⟦s45⟧ Where is the death which the enemy brought on? ⟦s46⟧ It is a great sign of mercy, brethren, that Christ by dying thus gave life to the world, in such a way that death itself did not hold him captive. ⟦s47⟧ Therefore he both accomplished our cause with merciful goodness and took nothing away from his own power, since, the moment it pleased him, he also came to life again — who lives and reigns, God, through all the ages of ages. ⟦s48⟧ Amen.
