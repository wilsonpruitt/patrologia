# Cruces — 7508, ps.-Augustine, *Sermones* (PL 47)

Garbled or corrupt printed readings, rendered literally in the English and logged here.

These four Easter sermons are printed by Migne from manuscripts whose orthography is
late-antique/Merovingian throughout (*sollempnitas*, *karissimi*, *querite*, *celestia*,
*Jhesu*, *inlustrissimi*, *dampnatorum*, *nichil*, *justicia*, *condicio*, *letus*,
*cede* for *caede*). These are spelling conventions, not corruptions, and are simply
translated; they are not itemized below.

## Chunk 0 (sermons I–II)

- **1151B** — *remissione omnium peccatorum precepta, per Christi gratiam.* Migne prints
  the manuscript's *precepta* with the correction *[percepta]* in brackets; *precepta*
  ("commanded") is meaningless here and *percepta* ("received") is plainly intended.
  Rendered with the intended reading, the bracketed apparatus kept in place.
- **1151D** — *vita nostra abscondita est cum Christo in Domino* (twice), against the
  Vulgate's *vita vestra … cum Christo in Deo* at Col. 3:3. Migne's *nostra* / *in Domino*
  is what is printed and what the homilist then argues from ("when we shall be dead …
  our life is hidden"), so it is translated as printed. Likewise *quia filii Domini sumus*,
  which is the preacher's gloss, not a Vulgate lemma.
- **1152B** — *ut exaudiat nos vigilantes in sollempnitate sua, quod dormiunt pro salute
  nostra.* Migne himself flags this with *[ sic mss.]*. The clause is broken: *quod* with
  a plural *dormiunt* has no antecedent, and the sense demanded is singular and of Christ
  (*qui dormivit pro salute nostra*, "who slept for our salvation"). Rendered literally
  as printed ("seeing that they sleep for our salvation") with the *sic* marker kept;
  a reader should be aware the intended sense is almost certainly the singular.
- **1153A** — *si alieni sumus ab omni conscriptione peccati.* *conscriptione* is odd;
  the sense is of sin's written record or enlistment. Rendered literally ("every
  enrollment of sin") rather than emended to *conscientia* or *contagione*.

## Chunk 1 (sermon III)

- **1153C** — *quia ignotam lucem post saecuta longa vidit.* *saecuta* is a misprint for
  *saecula*. Rendered with the intended reading ("after long ages").
- **1154A** — *Male intrati sumus.* Passive of an intransitive; the devils mean that
  their house has been broken into to their ruin. Rendered "He has broken in upon us
  to our ruin" rather than emended.
- **1154C** — *Premuntur diri sub tortore carnifices.* Singular *tortore* where the
  parallel wants either Christ or a collective; left literal ("beneath a tormentor").
- **1154D** — *cripe et mortuos morte tua.* *cripe* is a misprint for *eripe*.
  Rendered with the intended reading ("snatch away").
- **1155A** — *post demersos alta voragine fossa tortores.* The printed text will not
  construe; Migne's own note offers *[n: ( Leg. voraginis?)]*, i.e. read *voraginis* with
  *fossa*. Rendered on that reading ("sunk in the deep pit of the abyss"), Migne's note
  kept verbatim.
- **1155A** — *Nec canditatum officium defuit.* *canditatum* is a misprint for
  *candidatum* — the white-robed retinue of the risen Christ. Rendered accordingly.
- **1155A** — *plaudent manibus omnis gens.* Indicative *plaudent* in a run of
  subjunctives (*glorientur*), echoing Ps. 46:2 *plaudite manibus omnes gentes*.
  Translated as a jussive to match the sequence, the singular *omnis gens* kept.

## Chunk 2 (sermon IV)

- **1155D** — *O crudelissime justicida!* A coinage (*justus* + *-cida*), not a standard
  Latin word. Rendered "most cruel slayer of the just" rather than left in Latin.
- **1155D** — *non sunt ista mei germinis poma, nichil hic caput meam.* Corrupt: *caput*
  for *capit* and *meam* for *meum* (a common late-Latin e/u confusion in this witness).
  The intended reading is *nihil hic capit meum*, "he takes nothing of mine here."
  Rendered on that reading; the printed form is recorded in brackets in the text.
- **1155D** — *figit, affligit* — present tenses abruptly interrupting a perfect-tense
  narrative (*suscepit*). Kept as printed (historic present).
- **1156A** — *et medicante pariunt deinceps sacramenta salutis.* *medicante* is an
  ablative absolute with no expressed subject (the blood, or Christ). Rendered "with
  its healing" without supplying a noun the Latin does not print.
