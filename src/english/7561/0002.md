## VII.

Which judgment indeed seems to me to have been divinely promulgated chiefly on account of the fraudulence of those who, since they contrive to concoct a heresy under another's name, generally seize upon the writings of some ancient man, published a little too obscurely, which, by reason of their very obscurity, seem, as it were, to agree with their own doctrine; so that whatever that unknown something is which they put forward, they may seem neither the first nor the only ones to hold it. Their wickedness I judge worthy of a twofold hatred: both because they do not dread to offer to others the poison of heresy, and also because they fan with profane hand the memory of each holy man as though ashes now laid to rest, and defame by a revived opinion the things that ought to have been buried in silence, following altogether the footsteps of their author Ham, who not only neglected to cover the nakedness of the venerable Noah, but also proclaimed it to the rest for mockery. Whence he earned so great an offense of injured piety that even his descendants were bound by the curses of his sin — very far unlike those blessed brothers, who wished neither to violate with their own eyes the very nakedness of their revered father, nor to let it lie open to those of others, but, turned away, as it is written, covered him: which is neither to have approved nor to have betrayed the error of the holy man; and therefore they were endowed with a blessed benediction upon their posterity. But let us return to our purpose. With great [0648] fear, therefore, must the guilt of a changed faith and a violated religion be dreaded by us; from which only the discipline of ecclesiastical ordinance, but also the censure of apostolic authority deters us. For it is known to all how gravely, how severely, how vehemently the blessed Apostle Paul inveighs against certain men who, by a wondrous fickleness, had been too quickly carried over from him who had called them into the grace of Christ, unto another Gospel, which is not another; 2 *Tim.* 4, who had heaped up to themselves masters according to their own desires, turning away their hearing indeed from the truth, but turned unto fables; 1 *Tim.* 5, having damnation because they had made void their first faith; whom those had deceived concerning whom the same Apostle writes to the Roman brethren: *I beseech you, brethren, that you observe those who make dissensions and scandals contrary to the doctrine which you yourselves have learned; and avoid them. For such do not serve Christ the Lord, but their own belly, and by sweet speeches and blessings seduce the hearts of the innocent, Rom. 16. Who make their way into houses, and lead captive silly women laden with sins, who are led away with various desires; ever learning, and never arriving at the knowledge of the truth, 2 Tim. 3. Vain talkers and seducers, who subvert whole houses, teaching things which they ought not, for the sake of filthy lucre, Tit. 1. Men corrupt in mind, reprobate concerning the faith, 2 Tim.* 3, proud, and knowing nothing, but languishing about questions and strifes of words; who are deprived of the truth, esteeming gain to be godliness: and at the same time, being idle, they learn to go about from house to house; and not only idle, but also full of words, and curious, speaking things which they ought not, 1 *Tim.* 5: who, casting away a good conscience, have made shipwreck concerning the faith, 1 *Tim.* 1: whose profane vain babblings advance much unto impiety, and their speech spreads like a canker, 2 *Tim.* 2. And it is well that of them likewise it is written: *But they shall proceed no further. For their folly shall be manifest to all, as also that of those men was. 2 Tim.* 3.

## VIII.

When, therefore, certain such men, going about the provinces and cities, and carrying around their venal errors, had come even to the Galatians, and when the Galatians, having heard these things, seized by a certain nausea of the truth, vomiting up the manna of apostolic and Catholic doctrine, took delight in the filth of heretical novelty, the authority of apostolic power so exerted itself that with the utmost severity [0649] it decreed: *But although we,* he says, *or an angel from heaven preach a gospel to you besides that which we have preached to you, let him be anathema, Gal.* 1. What is it that he says, *But although we?* Why not rather, *But although I?* This is to say: Even if Peter, even if Andrew, even if John, even if finally the whole choir of the Apostles should preach to you besides what we have preached, let him be anathema. A tremendous strictness, that for the sake of asserting tenacity in the first faith he spared neither himself nor his fellow apostles! It is little: *Even if an angel,* he says, *from heaven should preach to you, besides what we have preached, let him be anathema.* It had not sufficed, for the keeping of the faith once handed down, to have mentioned the nature of the human condition, unless he had also comprehended the angelic excellence. *Although we,* he says, *or an angel from heaven.* Not because the holy and heavenly angels can now sin; but this is what he says: If even, he says, that which cannot be done should be done — whoever he be that shall attempt to change the faith once handed down, let him be anathema. But perhaps he spoke these things perfunctorily, and poured them out by human impulse rather than decreed them by divine reason. God forbid. For he goes on, and drives home this very thing with the immense effort of a repeated insinuation: *As we have foretold, and now I say again: If anyone preach a gospel to you besides that which you have received, let him be anathema.* He did not say: If anyone announce to you besides what you have received, let him be blessed, praised, received; but, *let him be anathema,* he says, that is, separated, set apart, excluded, lest the dire contagion of one sheep should, by a poisoned intermixture, contaminate the innocent flock of Christ.

## IX.

But perhaps these precepts were given to the Galatians only. Then those things too were enjoined on the Galatians alone which are mentioned in the following parts of the same Epistle: such as these: *If we live in the Spirit, let us also walk in the Spirit. Let us not be made desirous of vainglory, provoking one another, envying one another, Gal.* 5, and the rest. But if that is absurd, and they were enjoined upon all equally, it remains that, just as these commands concerning morals, so also those which are prescribed concerning the faith comprehend all in like manner; and just as it is lawful for no one to provoke one another or to envy one another, so let it be lawful for no one to receive anything besides that which the Catholic Church everywhere preaches. Or perhaps it was then commanded that if anyone announced besides what had been announced, he should be anathematized; but now it is no longer commanded. Then that too which he likewise says there: *And I say, walk in the Spirit, and you shall not fulfill the desire of the flesh,* was commanded then only, but now is no longer commanded. But if to believe so is at once impious and pernicious, it necessarily follows that, just as these things are to be observed in all ages, so also those things which have been sanctioned concerning the faith not to be changed have been enjoined upon all ages. To announce, therefore, to Catholic Christians anything besides that which they have received was never lawful, is nowhere lawful, will never be lawful; and to anathematize those who announce anything besides what has once been received was at no time not fitting, is nowhere not fitting, will never not be fitting. Since these things are so, is there anyone either of so great audacity as to announce anything besides that which has been announced in the Church, or of so great fickleness as to receive anything besides that which he has received from the Church? He cries out, and by repeating cries out, and to all, and [0650] always, and everywhere through his letters he cries out — he, that vessel of election, that teacher of the nations, that trumpet of the Apostles, that herald of the lands, that one privy to the heavens — that if anyone announce a new doctrine, let him be anathematized. And on the contrary there cry back certain frogs, and gnats, and flies about to die, such as are the Pelagians, and this to Catholics: On our authority, they say, with us as leaders, with us as expounders, condemn what you were holding, hold what you were condemning, reject the ancient faith, the institutions of the fathers, the deposits of the elders; and receive — what, then, at last? I shudder to say: for they are so arrogant that to me they seem able not only not to be affirmed [sic: *afirmari*], but not even to be refuted, without some guilt.
