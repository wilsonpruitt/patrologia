## XXIV.

But let us return to the Apostle. *O Timothy,* he says, *keep the deposit, shunning the profane novelties of words.* Shun it, he says, as a viper, as a scorpion, as a basilisk, lest they strike you not only by touch, but even by sight and by their breath. What is "to shun"? *With such a one not even to take food,* 1 Cor. 5. What is "shun"? *If anyone,* he says, *comes to you, and does not bring this doctrine,* 2 Joann. What doctrine, but the catholic and universal, one and the same through the several successions of the ages, abiding by the incorrupt tradition of the truth, and destined to abide unto the ages without end? What then? *Do not,* he says, *receive him into the house, nor say Ave to him. For he that saith Ave to him communicateth with his malignant works.* *Profane,* he says, *novelties of words.* What is *profane?* Those things which have nothing sacred, nothing religious, wholly foreign to the inner recesses of the Church, which is the temple of God. *Profane,* he says, *novelties of words.* Of words — that is, novelties of doctrines, of things, of opinions — which are contrary to what is old and ancient: which, if they be received, it is of necessity that the faith of the blessed fathers be violated, either wholly, or at least in great part; it is of necessity that all the faithful of all ages, all the saints, all the chaste, the continent, the virgins, all the clergy, the levites and priests, so many thousands of confessors, so many armies of martyrs, so great a throng and multitude of cities and of peoples, so many islands, provinces, kings, tribes, kingdoms, nations, in short by now well-nigh the whole round world, incorporated into Christ the Head through the catholic faith, be pronounced to have been ignorant, through so great a course of the ages, to have erred, to have blasphemed, not to have known what it believed. *Profane,* he says, *novelties of words: shun them* — which to receive and to follow was never the mark of Catholics, but ever of heretics. And indeed, what heresy ever boiled up except under a certain name, in a certain place, at a certain time? Who ever established heresies except one who had first separated himself from the agreement of the universality and antiquity of the catholic Church? That this is so, examples show clearer than light. For who ever, before that profane Pelagius, presumed so great a power of free will that he did not think the grace of God necessary to aid it, in good works, through the several acts? Who, before his monstrous disciple Coelestius, denied that the whole [0671] human race was bound by the guilt of Adam's transgression? Who, before the sacrilegious Arius, dared to sunder the unity of the Trinity; who, before the wicked Sabellius, to confound the Trinity of the unity? Who, before the most cruel Novatian, called God cruel, in that he would rather the death of the dying than that he should return and live? Who, before Simon Magus — smitten by apostolic severity (from whom that old whirlpool of foulnesses has flowed, by a continuous and hidden succession, down to the most recent Priscillian) — dared to call God the Creator the author of evils, that is, of our crimes, impieties, and shameful deeds? For he asserts that such a one creates with his own hands the very nature of men, which, by a certain motion of its own and by the impulse of a certain necessary will, can do nothing else, wills nothing else, than to sin, in that, driven and inflamed by the furies of all vices, it is swept with insatiable desire into all the abysses of foulness. Innumerable are such things, which for the sake of brevity we pass over; by all of which, however, it is shown clearly and plainly enough that this is, as it were, customary and lawful among well-nigh all heresies, [0672] that they ever delight in profane novelties, loathe the decrees of antiquity, and, by the oppositions of knowledge falsely so called, make shipwreck of the faith. But on the contrary, this is truly proper to Catholics: to keep the deposits committed to them by the holy fathers, to condemn profane novelties, and, as the Apostle said and again foretold, *if any man announce anything besides that which has been received, let him be anathema,* Gal. 1.

## XXV.

Here perhaps someone may ask whether heretics too make use of the testimonies of divine Scripture. They use them plainly, and very vigorously indeed. For you may see them flying through each and every volume of the sacred law, through Moses, through the books of Kings; through the Psalms, through the Apostles, through the Gospels, through the Prophets. For whether among their own or among strangers, whether privately or publicly, whether in discourses or in books, whether at banquets or in the streets, they bring forth well-nigh nothing of their own which they do not attempt to shade over with the words of Scripture as well. Read the little works of Paul of Samosata, of Priscillian, of Eunomius, of Jovinian, and of the rest of those plagues; you will discern an endless heap of examples, well-nigh no page omitted which is not painted and colored with the opinions of the New or the Old Testament. But they are the more to be guarded against and dreaded, the more secretly they lurk under the shades of the divine law. For they know that their stenches will be pleasing quickly to well-nigh no one, if they be breathed out naked and plain; and therefore they sprinkle them, as it were, with a certain aroma of heavenly eloquence, so that he who would easily despise a human error may not easily contemn divine oracles. And so they do what those are wont to do who, being about to temper certain bitter draughts for little children, first smear the rims round about with honey, so that the unwary age, when it has felt the sweetness beforehand, may not dread the bitterness. This too is the care of those who color over evil herbs and noxious juices with the names of medicines, so that well-nigh no one, when he has read the remedy written above, may suspect the poison. Hence at last the Savior too cried out: *Beware of false prophets, who come to you in the clothing of sheep, but inwardly are ravening wolves,* Matth. 7. What is the clothing of sheep, but the utterances of the Prophets and Apostles, which the same, with a certain sheeplike sincerity, wove, as it were, into fleeces for that immaculate Lamb which [0673] taketh away the sin of the world? Who are the ravening wolves, but the fierce and raging senses of heretics, who ever infest the folds of the Church and tear the flock of Christ in whatever way they can? But that they may more deceitfully creep upon the unwary sheep, the ferocity of the wolves abiding, they lay aside the wolfish appearance, and wrap themselves in the opinions of the divine law as in fleeces, so that when anyone has felt beforehand the softness of the wool, he may in no wise dread the points of the teeth. But what says the Savior? *By their fruits you shall know them:* that is, when they have begun no longer merely to bring forth those divine words, but even to expound them, and no longer only to vaunt them, but even to interpret them, then that bitterness, then that harshness, then that madness will be understood, then the novel venom will be breathed out, then the profane novelties will be laid open; then first you may see the hedge broken, then the boundaries of the fathers removed, then the catholic faith cut down, then the ecclesiastical dogma mangled. Such were those whom the Apostle Paul strikes in his second Epistle to the Corinthians, saying: *For such,* he says, *false apostles are deceitful workers, transforming themselves into the Apostles of Christ,* 2 Cor. 11. What is *transforming themselves into the Apostles of Christ?* The Apostles brought forth examples of the divine law; these too brought them forth. The Apostles brought forth the authorities of the psalms; these too brought them forth. The Apostles brought forth the opinions of the Prophets; and these no less brought them forth. But when they had begun to interpret in unlike manner the things they had brought forth in like manner, then the simple were distinguished from the crafty, then the uncolored from the painted, then the upright from the perverse, then at last the true Apostles from the false Apostles. *And no wonder,* he says. *For Satan himself transformeth himself into an angel of light.* It is therefore no great thing if his ministers be transformed as the ministers of justice. Therefore, according to the teaching of the Apostle Paul, as often as either false apostles or false prophets or false teachers bring forth the opinions of the divine law, by which, ill interpreted, they endeavor to build up their errors, there is no doubt that they follow the crafty devices of their author — which he assuredly would never have contrived, unless he knew that there is altogether no easier way to deceive than that, where the fraud of nefarious error is insinuated, there the authority of divine words be held forth.
