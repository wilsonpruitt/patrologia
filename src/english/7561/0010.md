## XXVI.

But someone will say: Whence is it proved that the devil is wont to use examples of the sacred law? Let him read the Gospels, in which it is written: *Then the devil took him,* that is, the Lord and Savior, *and set him upon the pinnacle of the temple, and said to him: If thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down. For it is written that he hath given his angels charge over thee, that they keep thee in all thy ways; in their hands they shall bear thee up, lest perhaps thou dash thy foot against a stone,* Matth. 4. What will he do here to poor wretched men, who assailed the very Lord of majesty with the testimonies of the Scriptures? *If,* he says, *thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down.* Why? *For it is written,* he says. Greatly is the teaching of this passage to be heeded and retained by us, that, by so great an example of Gospel authority, when we see any men bring forth apostolic or prophetic words against the catholic faith, we may in no wise doubt that the devil speaks through them. For as then the head spoke to the Head, so now too the members speak to the members [0674] — the members, that is, of the devil to the members of Christ, the perfidious to the faithful, the sacrilegious to the religious, the heretics in short to the Catholics. But what at length do they say? *If,* he says, *thou be the Son of God, cast thyself down;* that is, if thou wilt be the Son of God and receive the inheritance of the heavenly kingdom, cast thyself down — that is, let thyself down from the teaching and tradition of this lofty Church, which is even reckoned the temple of God. And if anyone should ask any one of the heretics who persuades him of such things: Whence dost thou prove, whence dost thou teach, that I ought to forsake the universal and ancient faith of the Catholic Church? — straightway he replies: *For it is written.* And at once he prepares a thousand testimonies, a thousand examples, a thousand authorities from the law, from the Psalms, from the Apostles, from the Prophets, by which, interpreted in a new and evil manner, the unhappy soul may be cast headlong from the catholic citadel into the abyss of heresy. But now, by those promises which follow, the heretics have been wont to deceive the unwary in a marvelous manner. For they dare to promise and to teach that in their Church — that is, in the little conventicle of their communion — there is a certain great and special and plainly personal grace of God, so that, without any labor, without any zeal, without any diligence, even though they neither ask, nor seek, nor knock, whosoever belong to their number are nevertheless so divinely dispensed that, borne up by angelic hands — that is, kept by angelic protection — they can never dash their foot against a stone, that is, never be scandalized.

## XXVII.

But someone will say: If both the devil and his disciples — of whom some are false apostles, others false prophets and false teachers, and all wholly heretics — make use of the divine eloquences, opinions, and promises, what shall Catholic men and the sons of mother Church do? In what manner shall they discern the truth from falsehood in the holy Scriptures? This assuredly they will take great care to do, which at the beginning of this Commonitorium we wrote that holy and learned men had handed down to us: that they should interpret the divine Canon according to the traditions of the universal Church and according to the rules of catholic dogma; and in that same Catholic and apostolic Church they must needs follow universality, antiquity, consent. And if at any time a part should rebel against the whole, novelty against what is old, the dissent of one or of a few in error against the consent of all — or at least of far the greater number — of Catholics, let them prefer the soundness of the whole to the corruption of a part; and within that same universality, let them prefer the religion of antiquity to the profanity of novelty; and likewise, within antiquity itself, let them set, before the rashness of one or of a very few, first of all the general decrees, if there be any, of a universal Council; and then next, if there be none such, let them follow what is nearest — the mutually agreeing opinions of many and great masters. Which being faithfully, soberly, and carefully observed, with the Lord's help, we shall without great difficulty detect the noxious errors of any heretics that arise.
