# Cruces — 7561 (Vincent of Lérins, *Commonitoria*)

Garbled/broken printed Latin rendered literally per policy, with editorial notes. Column-cited.

## Chunk 0001 (cols 0644–0647)

- **col 0645 (ch. V)** — **Printed:** `pro alicujus provincio lae temeraria quadam conspiratione certarent`. Neither *provincio* nor *lae* is a Latin word here: a line-break split/garble of *provinciae* ("of some province"), which is the intended reading, since *pro alicujus* requires a genitive. Per Pattern 7 the non-word is carried into the English untranslated and in italics: "for the sake of some *provincio lae*."

## Chunk 0002 (cols 0648–0650)

- **col 0648 (ch. VII)** — **Printed:** `a quo nos solum constitutionis Ecclesiasticae disciplina, sed etiam censura Apostolicae deterret auctoritatis`. *solum* is a real word and is rendered as printed — "from which **only** the discipline of ecclesiastical ordinance, but also the censure of apostolic authority deters us" — leaving the correlation broken in English as it is in the Latin. The intended reading is *non solum … sed etiam* ("not only … but also"), which the following *sed etiam* requires and which Vincent uses correctly a dozen times elsewhere in the work; the *non* has dropped in Migne's setting. An earlier draft supplied it silently; that has been withdrawn.
- **col 0650 (ch. IX)** — **Printed:** `ut mihi non modo afirmari, sed ne refelli quidem sine aliquo piaculo posse videantur`. *afirmari* (single *f*) is not a word: the intended reading is *affirmari* ("to be affirmed"), the passive infinitive of *affirmo*, which the parallel *refelli* requires. Per Pattern 7 the non-word is carried into the English untranslated and in italics: "they seem able not only not to be *afirmari*, but not even to be refuted."

## Chunk 0004 (cols 0655–0657)

- **col 0655 (ch. XIII)** — **Printed:** `Haec ergo Nestorius, Apollmaris, Photinus ... latrant`. *Apollmaris* is not a word: foul-case *m* for *in* in *Apollinaris*, which is the certain intended reading — the name is set correctly six times in this chapter and the one before it, including twice in the very next sentence. Per Pattern 7 the non-word is carried into the English untranslated and in italics: "do Nestorius, *Apollmaris*, Photinus, rabid dogs, bark."

## Chunk 0006 (cols 0661–0665)

- **col 0664** — `*Si surrexerit in modio tuî propheta, Deut* 13.` Migne prints *modio* ("in your measure/bushel"), almost certainly a misprint for *medio* ("in the midst of you"), which is the wording Deut. 13:1 requires and which Vincent himself prints correctly in the parallel quotation in ch. XVIII (`*Si surrexerit in medio tuî propheta*`, col 0665). Rendered literally as "within your measure" here; the parallel at 0665 rendered "in the midst of you."
- **col 0663** — **Printed:** `...quas ad Philippum Imperatorem... Christiani magisterii auctoritate conscripsi.` *conscripsi* is a real word (1st sg. perfect, "I wrote") and is rendered as printed: "I wrote with the authority of a Christian teacher" — which puts Vincent, absurdly, in the place of Origen. The intended reading is the 3rd person *conscripsit* ("he wrote"), since the subject throughout the passage is Origen and the relative *quas* looks back to *Epistolarum ejus*. An earlier draft rendered the conjecture; that has been withdrawn.

## Chunk 0007 (cols 0666–0667)

- **col ~0666 (ch. XXI)** — **Printed:** `*Scirdentem sepem mordebit eum serpens, Eccles.* 10` (Eccl. 10:8). *Scirdentem* is not a Latin word: a garble of *Scindentem* ("one who breaks/cuts through"), which is the intended reading — it fits the sense and the verse (cf. Vulg. *qui dissipat sepem*). Per Pattern 7 the non-word is carried into the English untranslated, standing inside the italic run Migne already gives the lemma: "*Scirdentem a hedge, a serpent shall bite him.*"

## Chunk 0012 (cols 0681–0686B)

- **col 0684 (ch. XXXII)** — **Printed:** `Ait itaque sanctus Papa Xis tus in Epistola...`. Neither *Xis* nor *tus* is a word: broken type for *Xystus* (= Pope Sixtus III), split by a stray internal space. Per Pattern 7 the non-word is carried into the English untranslated and in italics at this one place: "So then Saint Pope *Xis tus* says." Elsewhere the pope's name is set intact — *Sixti* at col 0683 (ch. XXXII opening), *Xysti* at col 0685 (ch. XXXIII) — and those are rendered "Sixtus" as ordinary name-translation; the Sixtus/Xystus variation is Vincent's/Migne's own and is not a crux.
