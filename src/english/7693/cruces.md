## Grammatical corruptions rendered per the intended sense

- **col 0393B** — printed *Si enim non subjaceat spiritus carnis.* Genitive
  *carnis* for the dative *carni* required by *subjaceat*; the following clause
  (*cedat potius menti caro*) confirms the direction of subjection. Rendered "if
  the spirit be not made subject to the flesh."
- **col 0393C** — printed *incipit impedita lege mentis perire, et superexaltare
  lege corporis.* Ablatives where nominative subjects are expected
  (*lex mentis … lex corporis*), and *superexaltare* active for deponent/reflexive.
  Rendered "the law of the mind, being hindered, begins to perish, and the law of
  the body to exalt itself."
- **col 0394A** — printed *manifestatus in carne, non deseruit permanens in
  nativitate.* *nativitate* is almost certainly a misprint for *divinitate*: the
  antithesis is manifestation in the flesh vs. abiding in the divinity, and
  "remaining in his nativity" is not sense. Rendered literally ("remaining as he
  did in his birth") per the render-as-printed rule — **flagged for emendation.**
- **col 0395A** — printed *Animadvertendum est tamen quod illius se miscuit
  corporis portionem extremi auriculae quae videtur superesse liberaverit.*
  Badly tangled: *portionem* accusative with no clean governing verb before
  *liberaverit*, and *se miscuit* stranded. Rendered on the assumption that
  *portionem … extremi auriculae* is the object of *liberaverit*. Worth a plate
  check.
- **col 0395A** — printed *primam istius auris partem leo devorat, novissimam
  pastor solvat.* Indicative *devorat* against subjunctive *solvat*; read as
  *solvit*. Rendered as parallel indicatives.
- **col 0395B** — printed *Quae multipliciter lectio ipsa per totum a Domino
  revolvente, quae et qualia passi necdum resipiscamus.* No main verb; ablative
  absolute *a Domino revolvente* with *a* + ablative. Rendered "and the reading
  itself unfolds these things in manifold ways throughout, as the Lord recounts…"
- **col 0395C** — printed *Jam tibi non nocet quidquid esset Adam.* *esset* for
  an expected *gessit* ("whatever Adam did"), which the three following relative
  clauses (*quod concupivit … praesumpsit … desideravit*) require. Rendered
  literally ("whatever Adam was").
- **col 0398C** — printed *Maxima peritia est hic impendi fletum … quam illic
  servari.* Comparative *quam* with no comparative term (expected *melius* or
  *potius* … *quam*). Rendered "rather than that it be kept."

### 0395A — sense-reversing-letter (migne, medium confidence)

Printed: *Auris ergo nostra est obedientia nostra: nam cum precamur et resipiscimus, primam istius auris partem leo devorat, novissimam pastor solvat.*

Conjecture: **cum peccamus et resipiscimus ("when we sin and repent") — peccamus corrupted to precamur**

The allegory assigns the devoured first part to sin and the rescued last part to repentance, so praying cannot be what makes the lion devour. The very next sentences say so: 'Prima pars auriculae est *Non concupiscas.* Ista jam deglutita est, quia cogitatio concupivit' — the first part is swallowed because the thought coveted (a sin, not a prayer) — and the rescue of the last part is answered 'Quomodo? Utique per poenitentiam et correctionem.' The printed 'precamur' makes prayer the cause of the lion's bite, the opposite of the sentence's own gloss; and 'resipiscimus' ('come to ourselves again', i.e. repent) presupposes a preceding lapse, not a preceding prayer.
