## CHAPTER ONE.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0963A]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 1. ⟦s2⟧ *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.* ⟦s3⟧ This is the voice of the Church coming to Christ in a kiss; desiring that the offense which men by sinning had contracted before God be undone, and longing to find the peace in which, while we were enemies to God the Father, we are reconciled through his very Son: on account of which the prophet says to God: *Give us thy peace, O Lord, for thou hast rendered all things to us [n: (Isa. XXVI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ In the Gospel too [n: (Luc. XV)] the father kissed the son returning from a far country. ⟦s5⟧ For then in a certain way he handed over a kiss to those coming to him, when he willed his only-begotten to be born in man, through whom also he granted that we should cleave to him by the bond of peace. ⟦s6⟧ Finally the angels too, when Christ was born: *Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will [n: (Luc. VIII)]*, cried out with [0963B] harmonious voice for this reason, because by saying such things to us they plainly taught him who is our peace.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 2. ⟦s2⟧ *For thy breasts are better than wine, fragrant with the finest ointments.* ⟦s3⟧ We not incongruously take the breasts of Christ to be the apostles and evangelists; for through them we are nourished in faith and refreshed with spiritual food. ⟦s4⟧ And they are proved better than wine for this reason, because the evangelical teaching is recognized as more eminent than the law once given. ⟦s5⟧ In short, there the figure, here the truth; there, as with the austerity of wine, it showed only that the neighbor was to be loved and the enemy hated; here the sweetness of grace admonishes that we pray for our enemies and that those who hate us are to be loved. ⟦s6⟧ It also declares those same breasts of Christ, that is, the apostles, to be fragrant with the finest ointments, because they shone forth with such great virtues [0963C] and such great miracles that the odor of their justice, spread far and wide, then delighted those who beheld it, and refreshes even to this day all who hear, as one of them proclaims, saying: *But thanks be to God, who always triumphs over us in Christ Jesus, and manifests the odor of the knowledge of himself in every place through us [n: (I Cor. II)]*.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 3. ⟦s2⟧ *Thy name is oil poured out, therefore the maidens have loved thee.* ⟦s3⟧ He whom God anointed, that is, Christ, *with the oil of gladness above his fellows [n: (Psal. XLIV)]*, of him also the name is as it were oil poured out: for whoever are named Christians from Christ and are anointed with the chrism itself, and, baptized in his name, are filled with the holy Spirit, like the ointment which descends from the head *upon the beard of Aaron [n: (Psal. CXXXII)]*; which declares him to be true, perfect, and most strong; [0963D] that is, the sacred Spirit, who has been poured forth by God in Christ, *descends* also *upon the hem of his garment,* and fills the whole Church even to the end; which, by cleaving to him, is without doubt his very garment. ⟦s4⟧ For in the transfiguration [0964A] *his face shone as the sun,* as we read in the Gospel, *and his garments became white as snow [n: (Mat. XVII; Luc. IX)], such as no fuller upon earth can make them.* ⟦s5⟧ For none of the teachers ever led his disciples to such brightness as he; or thus, washed from their stains, he makes those who believe in him white as snow. ⟦s6⟧ These therefore the maidens, whether the young Churches or uncorrupted souls, have loved; who also, filled with the fragrant ointment, say with the Apostle: *The charity of God is poured forth in our hearts by the holy Spirit, who is given to us [n: (Rom. V)]*.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 4. ⟦s2⟧ *Draw me after thee, we will run in the odor.* ⟦s3⟧ The Lord says in the Gospel: *No man comes to me unless the Father, who sent me, draw him [n: (Joan. VI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ For he is drawn to Christ who believes in Christ; but those run [0964B] after him who imitate him, or who by his gift make progress in following him.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 5. ⟦s2⟧ *The king has brought me into his storerooms.* ⟦s3⟧ Christ brought the Church into his storerooms when he revealed to her the hidden mysteries of the Scriptures, as the Apostle says, *that he might show in the ages to come the abundant riches of his grace, in his bounty toward us in Christ Jesus [n: (Ephes. II)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Or the saints are wholly brought into Christ's storerooms then, when at the end it shall be said to them: *Come, ye blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world [n: (Matth. XXV)]*.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 6. ⟦s2⟧ *We will exult and rejoice in thee, mindful of thy breasts above one.* ⟦s3⟧ The saints exult and rejoice in the Lord when they recall how, through the breasts of Christ, namely the teachings of the apostles, they were nourished with spiritual [0964C] milk. ⟦s4⟧ Which teaching of Christ, while they perceive it to be clearer than the precepts of the ancient law, in it, or from it, beholding Christ, they understand, to whom he himself says in the Gospel: *Blessed are your eyes that see, and your ears that hear; amen I say to you, many prophets and kings desired to see the things that you see, and saw them not; and to hear the things that you hear, and heard them not [n: (Matth. XIII; Luc. X)]*.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 7. ⟦s2⟧ *The upright love thee.* ⟦s3⟧ For the depraved cannot love equity: for those are upright and love God who are not held captive by love of the creature; but, as befits the upright, preferring the precious gift, they have cleaved to God the author of all things with a most strong love. ⟦s4⟧ But those too are approved as loving God rightly who, set not only amid his benefits but amid his scourges, [0964D] likewise give thanks, just as he too was saying: *I will bless the Lord at all times, his praise shall be ever in my mouth [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]*: that is, both when I receive gladness from a gift, and when I receive sorrow (from the same one chastising me). [0965A] Just as another too, tested by a most vehement suffering, rotting in flesh and sincere in heart, spoke: *If we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, why should we not endure evil? ⟦s5⟧ The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away, as it pleased the Lord so is it done; blessed be the name,* etc.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 8. ⟦s2⟧ *I am black, but comely, daughter of Jerusalem, as the tents of Cedar.* ⟦s3⟧ Black by the confession of sins, comely by the grace of the sacrament, daughter of Jerusalem. ⟦s4⟧ For from that [Jerusalem] the Church of the firstborn was born through the Gospel, which first believed in Jerusalem; or certainly, as the Apostle says: *That Jerusalem which is above is free, which is our mother [n: (Galat. IV)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And rightly, because she likens herself to the tents of Cedar; which is interpreted, of darkness; to whose sons the Apostle says: *You were once darkness, but now light in the Lord (Ephes. [0965B] V)*. She can also be called black when she is given over to sufferings, reproaches, insults, and contempt. ⟦s6⟧ Consider her black and comely: in Paul: *In all things we suffer tribulation, but are not distressed; we are perplexed, but not forsaken; we are cast down, but perish not [n: (II Cor. IV)]*. ⟦s7⟧ Just as the tents of Cedar; *we are made as it were the refuse of this world, the offscouring of all;* and like the skin of Solomon, *always bearing about the mortification of Jesus Christ in our flesh, that the life of Jesus may be manifested in our mortal flesh.*

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 9. ⟦s2⟧ *Do not regard me because I am swarthy, for the sun has discolored me.* ⟦s3⟧ That is: do not despise me set in persecution; for I have endured the heat of tribulations, and have as it were lost the beauty of temporal pleasantness: *For through many tribulations must I enter [0965C] into the kingdom of God [n: (Act. XIV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ For I am among those of whom it is written: *Blessed are they that mourn, for they shall be comforted: and you indeed shall have sorrow, but the world shall rejoice, yet your sorrow shall be turned into joy [n: (Matth. V; Joan. XVI)]*.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 10. ⟦s2⟧ *The sons of my mother have fought against me.* ⟦s3⟧ The sons, namely, of the Synagogue, who stoned Stephen the martyr as he taught and reproved: *And there was a great persecution in the Church which was at Jerusalem, and all the brethren were scattered through the regions of Judea and Samaria [n: (Act. VII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Or those also fought against the Church who persecuted Paul, preaching among the gentiles, through the several cities. ⟦s5⟧ Likewise also those who judged that the burdens of the law were to be imposed on those who had believed from among the gentiles, which burdens the blessed James, with the rest of the apostles, [0965D] decreed were salubriously to be removed [n: (Act. V)]. ⟦s6⟧ For those also who had persuaded the Galatians, believing in Christ, to be circumcised, disturbing the evangelical tradition, are taught to have fought against the Church, of whom the apostle Paul says: *For neither* do these who are circumcised keep the law, but *they wish you to be circumcised, that they may glory in your flesh [n: (Gal. V)]*. ⟦s7⟧ Of these same he also said: *Would that they who trouble you were cut off [n: (Ibid.)]*.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 11. ⟦s2⟧ *They have set me a keeper in the vineyards.* ⟦s3+4⟧ These vineyards too are the Synagogues: *Saul had received* power, and *letters from the chief priests, that whatever men and women he found believing in Christ he might deliver into custody to be punished [n: (Act. XI et XXII)]*: so that while he perpetrated these things, he was as it were keeping the vineyard of the Synagogue. [0966A] And there follows, *My own vineyard I have not kept.* ⟦s5⟧ For, made Paul out of Saul, a preacher out of a persecutor, while he preached the word of God to the contradicting Jews and was utterly spurned, he said to those same unbelievers: *To you indeed it behooved to preach the word of God, but since you reject it and judge yourselves unworthy of so great salvation, behold, we turn to the gentiles, for so has the Lord commanded us through the Scripture: I have set thee as a light of the gentiles, that thou mayest be for salvation unto the ends of the earth [n: (Act. XIII; Isa. XLIX)]*. ⟦s6⟧ Or, as he himself wrote to the Galatians, *You have heard of my conduct in Judaism, that I persecuted the Church of God and stormed it, and advanced in Judaism above many of my contemporaries in my nation, being more abundantly a zealot for the traditions of my fathers [n: (Gal. I)]*. ⟦s7⟧ And again: *But [0966B] the things that were gains to me, these I have counted losses [n: (Philip. III)]*, saying as it were under an enigma: *They have set me a keeper in the vineyards, for* my own *vineyard I have not kept.*

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 12. ⟦s2⟧ *Show me whom my soul loves, where thou feedest, where thou liest at noon.* ⟦s3⟧ We read in the Gospel: *If they shall say, Behold, here is Christ, behold, there, go not out [n: (Matth. XXIV; Marc. XIII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ For here and there are known to be parts; but Christ, lying in the Catholic Church as at noon, has flooded the whole world with his splendor, and rests in those who are fervent in spirit, and out of that charity by which he irradiates the whole globe, he daily feeds the peoples who believe in him.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 13. ⟦s2⟧ *Lest I begin to wander among the flocks of thy companions [0966C]:* that is, if in that brightness in which the whole body of thy faithful subsists I have not known thee dwelling, I shall begin to stray among the flocks of the companions, that is, among the congregations of heretics. ⟦s3⟧ For heretics may in a certain part be called companions of Christ, because they profess his nativity, passion, and resurrection; but because concerning them the Apostle says: *Having the form of piety but denying the power thereof [n: (II Tim. V)]*, they are proved to be *no longer* companions but adversaries.
