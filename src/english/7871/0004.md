## CHAP. III.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 56. ⟦s2⟧ *On my bed by night I sought.* ⟦s3⟧ Among those who slumber in the faith, and are blotted out by the darkness of ignorance, the Church sought Christ, whom she loves; but see what follows: *I sought him, and found him not.* ⟦s4⟧ The Lord says to Adam: *In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat thy bread [n: (Gen. III)]*. ⟦s5⟧ For those who are unstrung by some vice or by the drowsiness of sloth do not quickly come to the eating of that bread which descended from heaven, [0974A] but those who have kept watch in holy labors, they find him.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 57. ⟦s2⟧ *I will arise and go about the city.* ⟦s3⟧ I considered all the glory of the present age, I inspected the congregations of the unbelievers and the diverse sects of the philosophers; but not even there did I find him whom my soul loves.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 58. ⟦s2⟧ *The watchmen found me.* ⟦s3⟧ The wise of the world and the worshipers of idols disputed against me, searching out many things in vain, and composing many books by fruitless labors, to whom I said: Why are you troubled to no purpose by superfluous effort? ⟦s4⟧ Why do you wear away your wits in vain with vehemence? have you seen him whom my soul loves? ⟦s5⟧ Since you glory in the false name of wisdom, *God has made foolish* your *wisdom [n: (I Cor. II)]*, because you were in no way able to find Christ there.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0974B]No. 59. ⟦s2⟧ *When I had passed a little beyond.* ⟦s3⟧ When I had passed beyond men of this kind, who in the world are thought famous for their empty knowledge, and had withstood their superstitious inventions, and had judged their wisdom foolish, *she found him whom my soul loves.* ⟦s4⟧ Hence it is that the Apostle says of such: *We speak wisdom among the perfect, yet wisdom not of this world, but we speak the wisdom of God in a mystery, which is hidden, which God predestined before the ages unto our glory [n: (I Cor. I)]*: and because that wisdom which is Christ is not found among such men, there follows: *which none of the princes of this world knew; for if they had known it, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory: as it is written, that eye has not seen, nor ear [0974C] heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man, what things God has prepared for those who love him [n: (Ibid.)]*. ⟦s5⟧ But because the bride finds him whom she loves among the spiritual, hear what follows: *But to us God has revealed them by his Spirit; for the Spirit searches all things, even the deep things of God [n: (Ibid.)]*.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 60. ⟦s2⟧ *I held him, and will not let him go.* ⟦s3⟧ For so will I cleave to Christ, will persevere in his faith, *until,* preaching him in the peoples of the Synagogue as in the house of the mother, *I bring* him *into the chamber of her that bore me:* that the Jews, who until now denied him with the mouth, may receive him with the heart; and, the eyes of their mind enlightened, may at length find *in the chamber,* abiding in the secret place of the holy Scriptures, him who once was revealed to us. ⟦s4⟧ Or Christ is then brought into the house of the mother, when to [0974D] the heavenly Jerusalem the Christian people, which is his body, is joined. ⟦s5⟧ Where is *that which eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor into the heart of man,* etc.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 61. ⟦s2⟧ *I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem.* ⟦s3⟧ This sentence he has already set down above, and, insofar as God has heard us, we remember that we treated it with testimonies adduced. ⟦s4⟧ But therefore I think it is repeated again in this book, lest any of the teachers either solicit those about to believe with gifts, or deter them with threats, but only preach the things that are holy, and voluntarily lead those about to believe, or to be converted, to faith or religion.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 62. ⟦s2⟧ *Who is this that comes up through the desert.* ⟦s3⟧ The Church comes up through the desert, when the people [0975A] of the gentiles, occupying the multitude of believers by faith, advances. ⟦s4⟧ For concerning her it is sung in the Psalms: *He has set the desert into pools of waters, and the land without water into outlets of waters [n: (Psalm. CVI et CXIII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ *Little rods of smoke from aromatic spices,* he means the prayers which from compunction of heart ascend to the Lord from the saints. ⟦s6⟧ For smoke is wont to stir up tears. ⟦s7⟧ Myrrh and frankincense, and the powders of every dust, intimate the marks of mortification, or the diverse gifts of graces which abound in the elect.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 63. ⟦s2⟧ *Or the couch of Solomon.* ⟦s3⟧ The couch of Solomon is the rest of Christ; which rest is fittingly taken of the Church, whom we read in the Psalms to be elect under a number: *For,* he says, *the Lord has chosen Sion, he has chosen her for his dwelling. ⟦s4⟧ This is my rest for ever and ever, here will I dwell, for I have chosen her* [0975B] [n: (Psal. LXXV)]. ⟦s5⟧ And under the number sixty, because sixty consists wholly of its parts, and rises by the number ten, rightly are they described of the mightiest of Israel, who, the Lord being present, are appointed to the guarding of the Church. ⟦s6⟧ To her too it is said by the prophet: *Upon thy walls, O Jerusalem, I have set watchmen; all the day and night forever they shall not be silent. ⟦s7⟧ All holding swords, most skilled for wars. ⟦s8⟧ And the sword of each upon his thigh [n: (Isai. LXII)]*. ⟦s9⟧ For to them the Apostle says: *Stand therefore, having your loins girt in truth, and put on the breastplate of justice, and your feet shod in the preparation of the Gospel of peace, in all things taking the shield of faith, in which you may be able to extinguish all the fiery darts of the most wicked one; and take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God [n: (Ephes. VI)]*. ⟦s10⟧ Those therefore are most skilled for wars, [0975C] who have known how to fight back against the concupiscences of their flesh; and he usefully bears the sword upon his thigh who mortifies the carnal fluxes and lusts with spiritual food. ⟦s11⟧ Whoever therefore of this kind are found in the Church, these are recognized to be strong of the mightiest of Israel, contemplating God.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 64. ⟦s2⟧ *King Solomon made himself a litter.* ⟦s3⟧ Where the setting forth of foods is written, there is the faith of believers, through which Christ is perceived. ⟦s4⟧ For when the Lord was speaking to the Samaritan woman at the well, and she, returning, had announced his majesty to the others remaining in the city, his disciples coming up asked him, saying: *Rabbi, eat. ⟦s5⟧ But he said to them: I have food to eat which you know not.* ⟦s6⟧ And a little after: *from that city many of the Samaritans believed in him* [0975D] [n: (Joan. IV)]. ⟦s7⟧ Their faith he showed would be food for himself, when a little before he foretold to the disciples that he would eat this which they knew not. ⟦s8⟧ But in this place, as the following words teach, the name of litter shows rather the instrument in which something is carried, or, as another edition has, a bearing-chair, than the food itself. ⟦s9⟧ *Of the woods of Lebanon:* of the chief saints in the Church.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 65. ⟦s2⟧ *He made its pillars of silver.* ⟦s3⟧ He prepared the supports of the Church out of his learned men or apostles, as Paul, the vessel of election, wrote to the Galatians: *When,* he says, *James, Cephas, and John, who seemed to be pillars, had recognized the grace of God, they gave to me and Barnabas the right hands of fellowship [n: (Gal. II)]*.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 66. ⟦s1⟧ *A golden resting-place.* ⟦s2⟧ Christ, about to feast well, finds a resting-place in those who, endowed with sincere [0976A] understanding, shine like gold in the holy congregation.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 67. ⟦s2⟧ *A purple ascent.* ⟦s3⟧ The purple ascent teaches the exceptional perfection of the martyrs in the shedding of blood.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 68. ⟦s2⟧ *The middle he built with charity.* ⟦s3⟧ That he might build all these ornaments of the Church, there is no other cause than that *he loved us and delivered himself for us, an oblation and a victim to God for an odor of sweetness [n: (Ephes. V)]*.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ No. 69. ⟦s2⟧ *Go forth and see, daughters of Sion.* ⟦s3⟧ Ever have before your eyes, holy souls, *King Solomon,* our peaceable one, who is Christ, how he endured the passion [n: (Marc., XV; Joan. XIX)], to free the human race from destruction; behold him *in [0976B] the diadem with which his mother crowned him:* in that crown of thorns which the Synagogue set upon his head in the day of his espousal. ⟦s4⟧ For then he espoused to himself the whole Church, when, ascending upon the gibbet of the cross, from that same cross he taught all who believe in him the sign upon their foreheads, as he himself says: *I, when I shall be lifted up from the earth, will draw all things to myself [n: (Joan. XII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ Which day of the passion was also the day of the gladness of his heart: for as Christ bestowed the good things which through his possession, unto generations and many times, upon the faithful.
