## CHAP. VI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 132. ⟦s2⟧ *My beloved has gone down to the garden.* ⟦s3⟧ He went down to his garden when in his coming he visited Israel his people, as he himself says: *I am not sent but to the sheep that are lost of the house of Israel [n: (Matth. X)]*. ⟦s4⟧ But by the little bed of spice we understand the holy Virgin Mary herself: for from her arose for us the order of righteousness, the Lord Jesus Christ.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 133. ⟦s2⟧ *That he may feed in the gardens, and gather lilies.* [0984A] That he may reign among the nations, or take delight in the Churches: that from them he may take up virgins and all the saints.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 134. ⟦s2⟧ *I am my beloved's, and my beloved is mine.* ⟦s3⟧ I am the kingdom of Christ and he is my king; I am his body and he is my head. ⟦s4⟧ *He feeds among the lilies,* when he rests in the heart of virgins, or when there, through the grace which he himself bestows, he daily increases and advances.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 135. ⟦s2⟧ *Thou art beautiful, my beloved, beautiful,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Beautiful through righteousness; sweet and beloved, because always peaceable, comely as Jerusalem. ⟦s4⟧ The Lord says in the Gospel: *In the resurrection they shall neither marry nor be given in marriage, but shall be as the angels in heaven [n: (Matth. XII; Luc. X)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And rightly is the Church described as comely as Jerusalem, to whom for eternity the likeness of the angels is granted by the Lord.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0984B]Num. 136. ⟦s2⟧ *Terrible as an army in battle array.* ⟦s3⟧ How many tyrants and persecutors, together with their author the devil, have tried to break the strength of the Church and in no way have been able to prevail. ⟦s4⟧ For the holy Church has the battle array of a camp set in order, when, Christ being at hand, neither does persecution overcome the martyrs, nor carnal delight the virgins, nor covetousness the merciful, nor the ambition of temporal glory the humble.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 137. ⟦s2⟧ *Turn thine eyes away from me, for.* ⟦s3⟧ Do not require me always to be seen in the body, whom thou discernest better in spirit through faith. ⟦s4⟧ For I ascended into heaven for this reason, that I might not always appear to thee confined to a place — I who so fill all things (by the presence of my divinity) that I am present everywhere, and contain all things, and am contained by no place.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0984C]Num. 138. ⟦s2⟧ *Thy hair is like flocks of goats, thy teeth like a flock of sheep which all [bear] twin offspring, and there is none barren,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ *Like the rind of a pomegranate.* ⟦s4⟧ These four verses, it is agreed, are here written twice, which by God's gift we have already treated in an earlier place: in which, however, he foretold the peoples of the Jews and gentiles to be converted with the fruit of twofold love, and declared the glory of the martyrs with their merits.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 139. ⟦s2⟧ *There are sixty queens, and eighty concubines.* ⟦s3⟧ In the number sixty, which is completed by six ascending through ten, we recognize every perfection of good things which is accomplished from the precepts of the law. ⟦s4⟧ But how the number six consists of the whole of its parts, note briefly: for its sixth part is proved to be one, its third two, and its half three. ⟦s5⟧ But who does not know that one, and two, and three [0984D] are six? which, in another number, with the parts distributed and reduced to a sum, is found to be thus completed with difficulty. ⟦s6+7⟧ And so the *queens* are called the perfect souls of the saints, which are not handmaids of sins: for so it is written, *Where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty [n: (I Cor. III)]*. ⟦s8⟧ But the concubines, who, like Hagar, *bring forth unto bondage [n: (Gal. IV)]*, are rightly reckoned among those who, having received the seed of the word of God, are wise not in spiritual but in carnal things. ⟦s9⟧ But of the *young maidens* of whom *there is no number,* there is the multitude of those who are ready to hear the things that are ever holy, and yet do not strive to fulfill in works what they hear, of whom the Apostle says: *Ever learning, and never [0985A] arriving at the knowledge of the truth [n: (I Tim. III)]*. ⟦s10⟧ And although these are kinds of men who are joined to Christ as it were through his teaching, nevertheless, because in the elect not diversity but unity is commended, hear what follows.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 140. ⟦s2⟧ *One is my dove, my perfect one.* ⟦s3⟧ *Dove,* because she is simple, and is wise in spiritual things; *perfect,* because, leaving the world, she loves me more than temporal things or herself, according to that [word]: *If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all that thou hast and give to the poor, and come and follow me [n: (Matth. XIX)]*.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 141. ⟦s2⟧ *She is the one chosen of her mother.* ⟦s3⟧ Of her mother the heavenly Jerusalem, of which the Apostle says: *But the Jerusalem which is above is our mother [n: (Gal. IV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ She is therefore one to her, because in future blessedness she alone shall be joined to her.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0985B]Num. 142. ⟦s2⟧ *The daughters saw her, and proclaimed her most blessed.* ⟦s3⟧ For what holy souls are there that do not extol the Church with wondrous praises, when not even the concubines, that is the carnal, are silent in her praise, since they admire her unconquered in the martyrs and victorious in the virgins?

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 143. ⟦s2⟧ *Who is this that comes forth as.* ⟦s3⟧ She rises up as the *dawn,* when, the works of darkness despised, she lives chastely and soberly in the married; *beautiful as the moon,* when in the continent she shines with white brightness; *choice as the sun,* when in the virgins she shines forth with the beauty of perfection, like that good earth in the Gospel, which, having received into itself the seed of the word of God, produced fruit thirtyfold and sixtyfold and a hundredfold; *terrible as an army set in order.* ⟦s4⟧ Is she not terrible who overthrew the rites of the nations [0985C] and the worship of idols, and herself, enduring in the faith of Christ, could not be overcome by the nations? ⟦s5⟧ And she is very terrible in this too, when that power is given to her in Peter, where it is said: *Whatever thou shalt bind upon earth, shall be bound also in heaven; and whatever thou shalt loose upon earth, shall be loosed also in heaven [n: (Matth. XVI et XVIII)]*.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 144. ⟦s2⟧ *I went down to the garden of nuts, to see.* ⟦s3⟧ The garden of nuts designates the canon of the Old and New Testament: for the nature of nuts shows one thing in the shell, and retains another in the kernel. ⟦s4⟧ And if anyone does not discern in the holy Scriptures, and does not, as it were, seek the inner things within the shells of the nut, he cannot come to the birth of spiritual understanding. ⟦s5⟧ But as for what he says, *That I might see the fruits of the valley:* that I might see [0985D] the fruit of the most lowly people in the Church, as is sung in the Psalms: *For thou wilt save the humble people, and wilt bring low the eyes of the proud [n: (Psal. XVII)]*.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 145. ⟦s2⟧ *That I might look whether the vineyards had flourished.* ⟦s3⟧ That I might prove whether the Church had so far advanced that there should spring up in her those who would reach the perfection of martyrdom; and that they should be beautiful, ruddy, on account of the shedding of the blood of their Christ.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3+s4+s5+s6+s7+s8+s9+s10+s11+s12⟧ Num. 146. *I knew not, my soul troubled me.* What did Christ not know, except sin? He also knew not those who in the Gospel say: *Lord, did we not prophesy in thy name, and in thy name do many mighty works? And then* (he says) *I will say to them:* Amen I say to you, [0986A] *I know you not [n: (Matth. VII)]*: not that he did not know what manner of men they were, but because he knew that there was nothing good from them. *My soul troubled me because of the chariots of Aminadab.* We find this Aminadab too genealogically in Christ. Whence we recognize that these chariots too are to be taken in a good sense: in which we not unfittingly understand the evangelists. Furthermore, as for what he says, *My soul troubled me,* this troubling is not contrary to tranquillity, but is found to be fitting to solicitude, according to what is said to Martha in the Gospel: *Why art thou solicitous, and troubled [n: (Luc. X)] about many things?* And in the Psalms: *Thou hast moved the earth, and troubled it; heal thou its breaches, for it is shaken [n: (Psal. LIX)]*. But whence do all the narratives of the Gospels narrate except through the Holy [0986B] Spirit, according to that [word] of the prophet Ezekiel: *Wherever the Spirit went, thither also the wheels were borne together [n: (Ezech. III et X)]*?

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 147. ⟦s2⟧ *Return, return, O Shunammite.* ⟦s3⟧ We find this Shunammite twice set in a praiseworthy place in the book of Kings: that is, both her who ministered to king David in his old age, and in his embrace remained indeed a most beautiful virgin [n: (IV Reg. IV)], and also her whose son, born by promise and afterward dead, Elisha raised up [n: (III Reg. I)]. ⟦s4⟧ Wherefore who would doubt that by this name the holy Church is to be understood? ⟦s5⟧ But often *return* is said to her, who once, in that younger son going away into a far country [n: (Luc. XV)], had been turned away; for then she returned, when she was converted to God from the heart. [0986C] Or *return* is said to her so frequently for this reason, that even if, being converted, she should fall, she may not sink into despair, but by the indulgence of hope may recover from the habit of sinning. ⟦s6⟧ Which voice is surely approved as [addressed] to the saints, who desire to behold the beauty of her [now] converted.
