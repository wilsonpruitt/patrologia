## CHAP. VIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 173. ⟦s2⟧ *Who will give thee to me for my brother.* ⟦s3⟧ Christ sucks the breasts of the mother when the Christian people is refreshed by the mysteries of the ancient law, or when the teachers refresh [them] with spiritual sacraments and most devout exposition, as if with true milk.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0989C]Num. 174. ⟦s2⟧ *That I may find thee without and kiss thee.* ⟦s3⟧ That I, he says, may find thee in truth through the Gospel, thee whom the figures of the Old Testament covered. ⟦s4⟧ And he says well: I will find thee without, because to that younger son returning from the far country, the father went back to meet him [n: (Luc. XV)]: *that I may kiss thee,* he says, that I may deserve to be reconciled to thee; *and now no one will despise me;* that is, that it may not be said to Peter, concerning Cornelius whom the grace of the Holy Spirit had gone before: *Why didst thou go in to men having uncircumcision, and eat with them?* ⟦s5⟧ But let Peter himself declare: *In truth I perceive that there is no respect of persons with God, but in every place, and in every nation, he who fears him and works righteousness is acceptable to him [n: (Act. XI)]*.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0989D]Num. 175. ⟦s2⟧ *I will lay hold of thee, and lead thee.* ⟦s3⟧ For when I have laid hold of thee and cleaved to thee through faith, it remains that *I bring thee into the house of my mother:* namely into the congregation of Jerusalem: thither surely I will bring thee, when I myself also, with all the goods which I have obtained through thy grace, shall have deserved to be led there, that is, to the heavenly Jerusalem.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 176. ⟦s2⟧ *There thou wilt teach me, and I will give thee a cup.* ⟦s3⟧ There thou wilt teach me, that I may see now not *in a riddle through a mirror,* but *face to face;* and not *in part* as *now,* but *then I shall know as also I am known [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*, as he says: *And I will give thee a cup of spiced wine, and the new wine of pomegranates:* when I shall have brought forth the best and sweetest fruits of the holy martyrs, [0990A] and the sons of men who hope in the shadow of thy wings *shall be inebriated with the fatness of thy house, and thou wilt give them to drink of the torrent of thy pleasures [n: (Psal. XXXV)]*.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 177. ⟦s2⟧ *His left hand is under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me. ⟦s3⟧ I adjure you, daughters of Jerusalem.* ⟦s4⟧ These things which are written in this place, already foretold above, are found expounded, Christ enlightening [us]. ⟦s5⟧ But I think they are written again in this book for this reason, that they may settle more forcefully in the heart; and, being twice suggested, may be renewed with livelier memory. ⟦s6⟧ These bid that temporal things be despised; and, set beneath us, they teach [them to be] as a left hand placed under the head; and they admonish that the heavenly glory, which embraces us from the higher part as a right hand, is to be awaited; or this, that those about to serve the Lord may perform the things that are holy willingly, not unwillingly, [0990B] according to what Peter the apostle teaches: *Feed the flock of God which is among you, not by constraint but willingly [n: (I Petr. V)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 178. ⟦s2⟧ *Who is this that comes up from the desert?* ⟦s3⟧ Rightly is she described as coming up from the desert, to whom the prophet says: *Because thou wast forsaken and held in hatred, and there was none that passed through thee [n: (Isai. LX)]*, etc. ⟦s4⟧ Of whose sons also it is sung in the Psalms: *They wandered in the wilderness, in a dry place; they found not the way to a city of habitation. ⟦s5⟧ Hungry and thirsty, their soul fainted within them, and they cried to the Lord when they were troubled, and he delivered them out of their distresses; and he led them by the right way, that they might go into a city of habitation [n: (Psal. X)]*. ⟦s6⟧ Therefore it follows.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 179. ⟦s2⟧ *Abounding in delights.* ⟦s3⟧ Whence also the people of Israel, [0990C] going forth from the desert, enters into the land of promise flowing with milk and honey; and that son who, desiring to feed on the husks of the swine, could not be satisfied, is brought in to be refreshed at the feast which the father prepared from the fatted calf that was killed. ⟦s4⟧ These delights the Church, coming from the desert, finds manifold in Christ; and she is written to be *leaning upon her beloved,* because to her the same beloved says through the prophet: *I have made, I will bear, I will carry, and I will save [n: (Isai. XLVI)]*.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 180. ⟦s2⟧ *Under the apple tree I raised you up.* ⟦s3⟧ On the fragrant gibbet of the cross, where I lay down, there I raised thee up from the destruction of sins, when I command that thee, believing, be marked beforehand with the sign of the cross.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 181. ⟦s2⟧ *There thy mother was corrupted.* ⟦s3⟧ Where thou didst deserve to be raised up through my death, there the [0990D] Synagogue was taken through her own impiety, when she cried *Crucify;* and answered, while Pilate was judging: *His blood be upon us and upon our children [n: (Luc. XXIII)]*.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 182. ⟦s2⟧ *Set me as a seal.* ⟦s3⟧ So that thou mayest say with the Psalmist, *The light of thy countenance is sealed upon us, O Lord; thou hast given gladness in my heart [n: (Psal. IV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Since whatever things are sealed cannot easily be violated, so receive my faith, that thou mayest keep it whole and unimpaired, and mayest fear in no way that the treasure of righteousness which thou hast received through the seal of the cross, which I have bestowed upon thee, will be touched by anyone.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 183. ⟦s2⟧ *Set me as a seal.* ⟦s3⟧ Understand that the seal is to be set first upon the heart, then [0991A] upon the arm: that is, that both in thought and in work thou mayest run to my sign; and by this show thyself to be not another's but mine; and, with me protecting thee, mayest persevere whole.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 184. ⟦s2⟧ *For thy love is strong as death.* ⟦s3⟧ I loved thee so much that I willed to die for thee; and this was the strength of love, the bitterness of death. ⟦s4⟧ But *jealousy* is *hard as* hell; so that the saints too, on the other side, do not fear to be slain for Christ, whom they have known without doubt to have undergone death for their salvation.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 185. ⟦s2⟧ *Its lamps are lamps of fire.* ⟦s3⟧ The vessels in which chastity dwells are always splendid and fervent with the fire of the Holy Spirit.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 186. ⟦s2⟧ *Many waters could not quench charity,* nor shall the floods overwhelm it, nor tribulations; no persecutions, however strong, can disturb or conquer the love which is from God, or toward God, [0991B] according to what the Apostle says: *Who shall separate us from the charity of Christ? shall tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or famine, or nakedness, or the sword, or peril? ⟦s2⟧ For I am certain that neither death, nor life, nor angels, nor principalities, nor height, nor depth, nor any other creature shall be able to separate us from the charity of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord [n: (Rom. VIII)]*.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 187. ⟦s2⟧ *If a man should give his whole substance.* ⟦s3⟧ As if he should say: *And if I should distribute all my goods to feed the poor, and if I should deliver my body to be burned, but have not charity, it profits me nothing [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ For nothing is more precious than this, as John [0991C] the apostle says: *For God is charity; and he who abides in charity abides in God.*

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 188. ⟦s2⟧ *Our sister is little, and has no breasts.* ⟦s3⟧ The Church coming from the gentiles, still untrained for believing, and not yet endowed with learned men for teaching, according to what the Apostle says: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual, but as to little ones in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not meat; for you were not yet able, but neither indeed are you now able, for you are still carnal [n: (I Cor. III)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And elsewhere: *If you have secular judgments, set those who are contemptible in the Church to judge them. ⟦s5⟧ I speak to your shame: is there not among you any wise man, who is able to judge between his brother [n: (I Cor. VI)]?* ⟦s6⟧ While he rebukes them in such great faintheartedness, he surely shows a people still small [0991D] in faith, and not yet fit for spiritual nourishments.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 189. ⟦s2⟧ *What shall we do for our sister?* ⟦s3⟧ How shall we order the Church in the time when she is to be called to the faith?

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 190. ⟦s2⟧ *If she is a wall, let us build upon her.* ⟦s3⟧ Those who have begun to grow in faith and to be so established that by their strength they admonish the weak; having received the privilege of honor, let them be appointed to teach the rest, according to what Paul [says] to Timothy: *Commend these things to faithful men who will be fit to teach others also [n: (II Tim. II)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And again to Titus: *For this cause I left thee in Crete, that thou shouldst correct the things that are wanting, and shouldst appoint presbyters in the cities, as I appointed to thee.* ⟦s5⟧ While these things are thus ordered in [0992A] the Church, they are as silver bulwarks added to the walls.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 191. ⟦s2⟧ *If she is a door, let us frame [it].* ⟦s3⟧ The Prophet says in the Psalms: *Set a watch, O Lord, before my mouth, and a door of enclosure round about my lips [n: (Psal. CXL)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And so, if there is anyone fit for speaking, and, like a door which is opened and shut, understands the time of speaking or of keeping silence; let us frame such a door with boards of cedar, so that, while it takes into itself the fragrant wood, it may be able to say with Paul: *God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, by whom the world is crucified to me, and I to the world [n: (Gal. VI)]*.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 192. ⟦s2⟧ *I am a wall, and my breasts are like a tower.* ⟦s3⟧ It is the voice of the Church, advancing both in faith and for faith: *I am a wall;* because he himself has built me, who most mightily [0992B] fights for me. ⟦s4⟧ *And my breasts are like a tower.* ⟦s5⟧ God building [them], they have grown in the loftiness of righteousness, and they nourish in me the little ones who are in Christ; and until they come to the food of perfection, they do not cease to bring them up, until it be said to them by the voice of wisdom: *Leave off infancy, and come, walk by the ways of prudence.*

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 193. ⟦s2⟧ *From the time I was made in his presence.* ⟦s3⟧ From the time I found peace with Christ, from that time I received both the defenses of walls and the bulwarks of towers with comeliness, when by him who founded me I deserved to be a city with them, having fortifications in the saints, and retaining beauty in the elect.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 194. ⟦s2⟧ *The peaceable one had a vineyard.* ⟦s3⟧ Constant cultivation was [0992C] applied by Christ to the Church, which has those peoples of whom it is sung in the Psalms: *Planted in the house of the Lord, they shall flourish in the courts of our God [n: (Psal. XCI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ *He delivered it to keepers.* ⟦s5⟧ He committed it to be ruled by the apostles or their successors the bishops. ⟦s6⟧ To whom, under the name of Jerusalem, the Lord says through the prophet: *Upon thee and upon thy walls I have set thy keepers; all the day and night, unto the end they shall keep silence [n: (Isai. VIII)]*.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Num. 195. ⟦s2⟧ *The man brings its fruits, a thousand pieces of silver.* ⟦s3⟧ Christ, of whom the prophet says: *Behold a man, the Orient is his name [n: (Zach. VI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ He recompenses the kingdom of heaven for holy labors or almsgivings, according to what is said in the Gospel: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world, for I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; [0992D] I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink; I was a stranger, and you took me in; naked, and you covered me; sick and in prison, and you came to me [n: (Matth. XXV)]*. ⟦s5⟧ But in the thousand pieces of silver is shown the fullness of the whole recompense, and the completed reward of the just.
