# Cruces — Justus of Urgell, *Explicatio in Cantica canticorum* (textIdno 7871), chunks 0000–0005

Garbled / uncertain printed readings rendered literally (not emended) in the English, logged here per the crux rule. Column = the Migne column anchor the reading falls under.

## Chunk 0000

- **[0964B/C] col 0964C — No. 6, Canticle lemma:** Migne prints *memores uberum tuorum super **unum*** ("mindful of thy breasts above one"). Standard Vulgate reads *super **vinum*** ("above wine"), which the surrounding gloss ("nourished with spiritual milk … clearer than the precepts of the ancient law") does not depend on. Rendered as printed: "mindful of thy breasts above one." Probable print corruption of *vinum → unum*.
- **[0966B] col 0966B — No. 12, Canticle lemma:** Migne prints *ubi **pascus**, ubi **cubas** in meridie*. *pascus* is a nonstandard form (expected *pascas*, 2nd sing.); *cubas* is indicative for expected subjunctive *cubes*. Both understood as 2nd-person address ("where thou feedest, where thou liest at noon") and rendered so. Logged for the anomalous *pascus*.

## Chunk 0002

- **[0971A] col 0971A — No. 34, Apocalypse citation [n: (Apoc. XX)]:** Migne prints *Beatus qui **habitat** partem in resurrectione prima*. Standard text (Apoc. 20:6) reads *qui **habet** partem* ("who has a part"). *habitat partem* ("who dwells a part") is ungrammatical; rendered literally as printed ("Blessed is he who dwells a part in the first resurrection"). Probable corruption of *habet → habitat*.

## Chunk 0004

- **[0974B] col 0974B — No. 59, Canticle lemma:** Migne prints *…**invenit** quem diligit anima mea* (3rd person, "she/he found whom my soul loves"), where the biblical text (Cant. 3:4) and the first-person narration of the passage expect *inveni* ("I found"). Rendered as printed with the 3rd-person subject supplied by context ("she found him whom my soul loves").
- **[0975A] col 0975A — No. 62, Psalm citation [n: (Psalm. CVI et CXIII)]:** Migne prints *…in **exitus uquarum***. *uquarum* is an obvious typographic corruption of *aquarum* ("of waters"); rendered "outlets of waters."
- **[0976B] col 0976B — No. 69, closing sentence:** Migne prints an anacoluthon / truncated sentence: *sicut enim Christus quae bona per suam possessionem, in progenies, et multa tempora fidelibus condonavit.* No main clause completes the *sicut* comparison; a verb governing *quae bona* is missing. Rendered literally as an incomplete clause ("for as Christ bestowed the good things which through his possession, unto generations and many times, upon the faithful").

# Cruces — Justus of Urgell, *Explicatio in Cantica canticorum* (7871), chunks 0006–0011

Garbled/uncertain printed readings encountered in this batch. Each rendered literally-per-sense in the translation; logged here with column, printed reading, and note. Migne's `[n:]` citations are NOT crux-logged (they pass verbatim by rule even where the numeral looks wrong, e.g. Num. 199 "Joan. XXV," "Matth. XXVIII").

## Chunk 0006

- **col 0979C (Num. 100)** — printed *cruditi* ("Eos qui tenera vel vehementiore doctrina ... cruditi sunt"). Non-word; evidently *eruditi* ("instructed"). Rendered "have been instructed."
- **col 0981A (Num. 115)** — printed *sacrificand locus* ("nec sacrificand locus"), missing genitive/gerund ending; evidently *sacrificandi*. Rendered "no place for sacrificing."
- **col 0981A/B (Num. 115)** — printed *perse utores* ("cum haec omnia perse utores agerent"), a split of *persecutores*. Rendered "the persecutors." (Both may be OCR/digitization artifacts rather than Migne's plate.)

## Chunk 0007

- **col 0983B (Num. 128)** — printed *in corruptionis integritate* ("et in corruptionis integritate perdurant"). Sense requires *incorruptionis*. Rendered "in the integrity of incorruption."

## Chunk 0009

- **col 0988C (Num. 164)** — lemma printed *ad rummandum* ("Labiisque et dentibus illius ad rummandum"); evidently *ad ruminandum* ("to ruminate"), confirmed by the gloss *ruminare* immediately following. Rendered "to ruminate."
- **col 0989A (Num. 169)** — lemma printed *mata punica* ("Si floruerunt mata punica"); evidently *mala punica* ("pomegranates"), confirmed by the gloss *malum punicum* following. Rendered "the pomegranates."

## Chunk 0010

- **col 0990C (Num. 179)** — printed *idem dilectus dicti* ("ad ipsam per prophetam idem dilectus dicti"); evidently *dicit* (or *dixit*). Rendered "the same beloved says."
- **col 0991A (Num. 185)** — printed *castistas* ("Vasa in quibus castistas habitat"); evidently *castitas* ("chastity"). Rendered "chastity."
- **col 0992C (Num. 194)** — printed *contacebunt* ("usque in finem contacebunt [n: (Isai. VIII)]"). The source (Isa 62:6) reads *non tacebunt* ("they shall not be silent"), the opposite sense; *contacebunt* as printed would mean "they shall keep silence / fall silent." Rendered literally per the printed reading, "they shall keep silence." Note also the citation numeral: Migne prints "Isai. VIII" though the quotation is Isa 62:6 — left verbatim per marker rule.
