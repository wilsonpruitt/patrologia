## CHAPTER ONE.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 1. ⟦s2⟧ *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth.* ⟦s3⟧ It is the voice of the Synagogue longing for the coming of Christ. ⟦s4⟧ As though she said: So many times has he promised me his coming through the prophets; let him come therefore now, and *let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth,* that is, let him speak to me through himself.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For thy breasts are better than wine.* ⟦s2⟧ Suddenly turning her words to him for desire of whom she was burning, she adds, *For thy breasts are better than wine.* ⟦s3⟧ By the breasts of Christ the sweetness of the Gospel is understood: because by it, as by milk, the infancy of believers is nourished. ⟦s4⟧ *Wine,* however, signifies the severity of the law: but the breasts of Christ are better than wine, because the sweetness of the Gospel is better than the severity of the law. ⟦s5⟧ For in the law no repentance is reserved, but it is commanded that he who sins be slain; whereas the Gospel says: *I will not the death of the sinner [n: (Ezech. XVIII, 32)]*, and so forth.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 2. ⟦s2⟧ *Fragrant with the best ointments.* ⟦s3⟧ The *ointments* [1057B] are the gifts of the Holy Spirit, or also the workings of the virtues; of which the Apostle says: *We are the good odour of Christ [n: (II Cor. II, 15)]*.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thy name is oil poured out. ⟦s2⟧ Thy name,* O Christ, *is poured out.* ⟦s3⟧ In Greek *chrisma,* in Latin *unctio,* anointing — whence Christ. ⟦s4⟧ The name of Christ is so called from anointing. ⟦s5⟧ For the Holy Spirit is wont to be called by the name of *oil,* according to what the Psalmist says: *God, thy God, hath anointed thee with oil [n: (Psal. XLIV, 8)]*, that is, with the Holy Spirit. ⟦s6⟧ This oil was poured out when that grace which Christ had in a singular way was given to all the elect. ⟦s7⟧ Whence also from Christ they are called Christians, by a sharing in the name of Christ. ⟦s8⟧ And well is it said to be oil not dripped but *poured out,* because this grace was given abundantly to all who cherish the faith of Christ.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Therefore the young maidens have loved thee.* ⟦s2⟧ The *young maidens* are said to be the souls of the elect, which in baptism have left behind the filth of the old man and have been made new in Christ. ⟦s3⟧ And well are they spoken of in the feminine gender: because [1057C] the souls of the saints, the more conscious they are of their own frailty, the more abundantly do they love Christ.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 3. ⟦s2⟧ *Draw me after thee, we will run.* ⟦s3⟧ The voice of the Church. ⟦s4⟧ Here there is a change of person; for hitherto the Synagogue has spoken, here the Church of the Gentiles begins to speak: *Draw me after thee, we will run.* ⟦s5⟧ Because (she says) I know my own weakness, and I see that by my own strength I can do nothing good, by thy grace *draw me* to the imitation of thee; or also, when thou hast *506* ascended into heaven, *Draw me after thee,* that I may deserve to follow thee.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The King hath brought me into his cellars.* ⟦s2⟧ Here the Church speaks concerning Christ her bridegroom to the faithful souls. ⟦s3⟧ The *cellars* of God are eternal blessedness and the joys of the heavenly fatherland, into which the Church *has* already *been brought* by faith and hope, and one day *shall be brought* in the reality.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *We will exult and rejoice in thee.* ⟦s2⟧ Of thy gifts (she says) *we will exult and rejoice.* ⟦s3⟧ But because we know that this is not of our own merit, *in thee,* not in ourselves, *shall we exult.* ⟦s4⟧ This is what the Apostle says: *He that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord [n: (I Cor. I, 31)]*.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1057D] *Mindful of thy breasts above wine.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, *mindful* of thy grace, and of the mercy by which we have been saved. ⟦s3⟧ For the *breasts* are that very grace by which we are saved. ⟦s4⟧ Remembering also that thou hast deigned to temper the severity of the law with the breasts of evangelical teaching.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The upright love thee.* ⟦s2⟧ Those (she says) who are upright of heart love thee; that is, those who attribute nothing to their own merits, but reckon all to thy grace. ⟦s3⟧ For no one loves thee save the upright, and no one is upright save he who loves thee.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 4. ⟦s2⟧ *I am black, but comely, ye daughters of Jerusalem.* ⟦s3⟧ Here again the Church speaks of her afflictions to the holy souls. ⟦s4⟧ *I am black,* because I suffer persecutions; *but* I am *comely* in virtues, O daughters of Jerusalem, that is, ye faithful souls. ⟦s5⟧ *I am black,* that is, misshapen by the persecutions and hardships which I endure; *but* I am *comely* with the beauty of the virtues. ⟦s6⟧ And in what way she is *black,* and in what way *comely,* she shows when she adds:

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As the tents of Cedar, as the skins of Solomon.* ⟦s2⟧ For thus [1058A] it is to be distinguished: I am black as the tents of Cedar, comely as the skins of Solomon. ⟦s3⟧ *Cedar* was a son of Ishmael, which is interpreted darkness. ⟦s4⟧ And the Ishmaelites have always been accustomed to dwell in tents, and have no houses. ⟦s5⟧ The Church says therefore that she is *black as the tents of Cedar,* on account of the persecutions which she suffered from the sons of darkness, which is what Cedar is interpreted. ⟦s6⟧ Hence the Psalmist says: *I have dwelt with the inhabitants of Cedar [n: (Psal. CXIX, 5)]*, that is, I have lived among persecutors and sinners black *with unbelief.* ⟦s7⟧ But she says that she is *comely as the skins,* that is, the tents *of Solomon,* who is interpreted peaceable: since she was worthy of the visitation and consolation of her bridegroom Christ amid her straits. ⟦s8⟧ And as *tents* are made from the skins of dead animals, so the Church, the tabernacle namely of God, is built up out of those who mortify themselves together with their vices and concupiscences [n: (Galat. V, 24)].

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 5. ⟦s2⟧ *Consider me not that I am swarthy, because the sun hath discoloured me.* ⟦s3⟧ O souls faithful and devoted to God, [1058B] *consider me not that I am swarthy,* that is, that I am afflicted with tribulations, that I am oppressed with persecutions; *because the sun hath discoloured me,* that is, the heat of persecution has in a manner darkened my splendour. ⟦s4⟧ As if she said: Think upon my inward beauty, and attend to that, not to those things which I am seen to suffer outwardly. ⟦s5⟧ Such is what the Apostle also says: *Faint not at my tribulations for you, which is your glory. ⟦s6⟧ For the sufferings are not worthy [n: (Ephes. III, 13)]*, and the rest. ⟦s7⟧ Now the sun in the Scriptures has many senses. ⟦s8⟧ Sometimes it signifies the very Mediator of God and men; whence it is written: *To you that fear my name the sun of justice shall arise [n: (Malach. IV, 2)]*, that is, Christ. ⟦s9⟧ It signifies also the Church, as the Lord says: *Then shall the just shine as the sun [n: (Matth. XIII, 43)]*. ⟦s10⟧ Sometimes the heat of persecution, as here and in the Gospel, where it is said of the seed that fell upon the rock, and when the sun was risen it was scorched; which the Lord himself opens up in expounding it: *But when persecution ariseth because of the word, they are scandalized* [1058C] [n: (Matth. XIII, 6, 21)].

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The sons of my mother have fought against me.* ⟦s2⟧ Whence this persecution took its beginning she shows when she adds: *The sons of my mother have fought against me.* ⟦s3⟧ It is the voice of the primitive Church, whose mother is the Synagogue, to whom it is said by the prophet: *And thou shalt be called the faithful city [n: (Isai. I, 26)]*, and mother of cities, daughter of Sion. ⟦s4⟧ Her *sons* are the Jews, who *fought* against the primitive Church when they stirred up persecution against the believers, *and all were scattered through the regions of Samaria [n: (Act. VIII, 1)]*.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They have set me keeper in the vineyards.* ⟦s2⟧ One vineyard was the primitive Church at Jerusalem; but when, because of the Jewish persecution, all the believers were scattered, many vineyards were made, that is, many Churches were built throughout the whole world. ⟦s3⟧ The primitive Church therefore says: *They have set me* (namely the apostles and teachers) *keeper,* that is, that I should be the keeper of the Churches. ⟦s4⟧ Whence also in the Greek it is well said, *they were sown abroad,* where we read, *they were scattered [n: (Act. VIII, 1)]*; for to this end were they divinely scattered [sic: *disperi*], that more Churches might be made throughout the whole [1058D] world.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *My vineyard I have not kept,* that is, that one Church which was at Jerusalem I have not kept. ⟦s2⟧ This is to be referred not to her will, nor to her mind, but to the place. ⟦s3⟧ *I have not kept it* — supply: so that it should be there where it began.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 6. ⟦s2⟧ *Show me, O thou whom my soul loveth, where thou feedest, where thou liest down at midday.* ⟦s3⟧ It is the voice of the Church to her bridegroom Christ. ⟦s4⟧ O bridegroom, whom my soul loveth, that is, whom I love with the whole intent of my mind, *show me where thou feedest,* that is, where thou causest thy sheep to be fed; for he himself is the *good Shepherd,* as he himself says in the Gospel [n: (Joan. X, 14)]. ⟦s5⟧ *Show me where thou feedest and where thou liest down,* that is, where thou restest, and this *at midday,* that is, in the heat of persecution or of temptation. ⟦s6⟧ For the Church, labouring under persecutions, entreats her bridegroom to tell her in whose minds he rests. ⟦s7⟧ For to lie down is, for the bridegroom, to rest, as he himself says: *Upon whom doth my spirit rest (Isai. ⟦s8⟧ LXVI, [1059A] 2)*, and so forth. ⟦s9⟧ For lest the saints wither in the heat of temptation, he himself lies down and rests in their hearts, and protects them from every heat of temptations and persecutions. ⟦s10⟧ Why this?

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Lest I begin to wander after the flocks of thy companions.* ⟦s2⟧ By the flocks of companions he calls the conventicles of heretics, who are companions of Christ, because they too feed their own flocks. ⟦s3⟧ And the sense is: O bridegroom, show me thy saints and elect, lest perchance I stumble and fall in among heretics, thinking that thou canst be found there. ⟦s4+5⟧ This verse the heretics distinguished ill, as though the bride were asking: Where dost thou feed, where dost thou lie down? and he himself answered: At midday I lie down, and in the southern quarter.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 7. ⟦s2⟧ *If thou know not thyself, O beautiful among women, go forth, and go after the steps of the flocks.* ⟦s3⟧ The voice of the bridegroom to his bride the Church. ⟦s4⟧ As if he said: Thou complainest that thou art as it were forsaken by me and blackened by the heats of persecutions; and thou dost not consider how beautiful thou art among women, that is, thou attendest not to the beauty of the spiritual virtues by which thou art adorned above all others, [1059B] although thou art blackened by temptations. ⟦s5⟧ *If* therefore *thou know not thyself,* that is, if thou dost not acknowledge this thy dignity and comeliness, *go forth* from my fellowship, *and go after the steps of the flocks,* that is, follow and imitate the teaching of the erring heretics, *507* who, despising the true Shepherd of the one flock, have heaped up for themselves many flocks.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And feed thy kids,* that is, sinners and erring hearers; and this *beside the tents of the shepherds,* that is, according to the doctrines of the heretics. ⟦s2⟧ For there is one shepherd, Christ, who has one flock, that is, the unity of the catholic Church. ⟦s3⟧ But the shepherds are many, the heretics, who gather to themselves a flock of deceived men, whom they feed for the devil; of whom the Psalmist also says: *They are laid in hell like sheep [n: (Psal. XLVIII, 15)]*. ⟦s4⟧ In this place the wicked are called sheep, not on account of simplicity or innocence, but on account of dullness: because they know not how to resist depraved teaching; but all things that are laid upon them by depraved teachers they endure with no [1059C] good patience.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 8. ⟦s2⟧ *To my company of horsemen amid the chariots of Pharaoh have I likened thee, my friend.* ⟦s3⟧ His company of horsemen he calls the army of the children of Israel, namely the six hundred and six thousand who went out of Egypt and passed through the Red Sea [n: (Num. II, 32; Exod. XIV, 22)]. ⟦s4⟧ And he calls that multitude his *company of horsemen* because, as a charioteer is set over his chariot, so God was set over that people, protecting and defending it, and bringing it into the land of promise. ⟦s5⟧ To this his *company of horsemen he likened* his *friend;* since as that people was saved through the Red Sea, Pharaoh being drowned [n: (Exod. XIV, 27, 28)], so the Church of the Gentiles was freed by baptism from the servitude of the devil, and was brought into the true land of promise and into evangelical liberty; and thus she who was the handmaid of vices was made the friend of Christ, cleansed and washed by baptism from the filth of sins.
