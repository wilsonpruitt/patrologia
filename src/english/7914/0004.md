## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

*Behold he standeth behind our wall.* By the *wall* our mortal condition is named: because, set as it were like some thick wall between us and God, it does not permit us to enjoy the contemplation of him; for the first man was so created that he might enjoy the vision and converse of God [n: (Gen. I, 26)]: but after he had sinned [n: (Gen. III, 6)], a certain wall was set up, namely that mortality by which we are weighed down. For (as the Wise Man says) *The body that is corrupted weigheth down the soul, and the earthly habitation presseth down the mind that museth upon many things [n: (Sap. IX, 15)]*. Her bridegroom, therefore, whom above she compared to roes or to fawns of harts, [1064D] she now says stands behind her wall: because Christ sometimes draws near when he permits himself to be contemplated, as far as the human mind can take him in; but sometimes he is removed afar off, and like a roe or a hart escapes with certain leaps. Since therefore a wall was set between us and him, and we could not see him, he deigned to make in that very wall windows and lattices, through which he might in some manner be perceived, in some manner be discerned. Whence there follows also:

*Looking through the windows, looking forth through the lattices.* For windows and lattices are as it were made in the wall when he by his grace permits himself to be contemplated, although in a glass and in a riddle. This too, which he says, *He standeth behind our wall,* can be referred to his humanity; for he as it were *stood behind our wall* when he set our flesh before himself, in which his divinity lay hid. But that which follows, *Looking through the windows, looking forth through the lattices,* this is referred to his divinity. [1065A] For he who looks through windows or lattices sees all that is done outside, and is himself seen by no one; so God, though he sees all things, cannot himself be searched out and comprehended.

Verse 10. *Behold my beloved speaketh to me,* he exhorts me to preach, saying: *Arise, make haste, my friend, my dove, my comely one, and come.* The voice of the bridegroom calling her forth to the pursuits of public work. *Arise, make haste, my friend,* by faith and love; *my dove,* by innocence and simplicity; *my comely one,* by the beauty of the virtues, hasten and come. As if he said: *Arise* from that most sweet couch of thine, that is, from the quiet in which thou desirest to please me alone in psalms, hymns, and prayers; hasten and come, that is, hasten to the profit of thy neighbours; that them also thou mayest make imitators of thee by the office of preaching and by the example of good works, and mayest lead them with thee to salvation. And it is to be noted that above he adjured the daughters of Jerusalem not to stir up nor make the beloved to awake: but here he himself rouses her and exhorts her to rise [1065B] to labour; because all things have their time, and there is a time for all things under heaven — a time of contemplation, a time also of action. For at one time holy preachers ought to apply themselves to divine contemplation, and to delight their mind with heavenly quiet: but at another time they ought through charity to take care of their neighbours, and to give them examples of good works, and to bestow upon them the teaching of divine contemplation.

Verse 11. *For now the winter is past, the rain is gone and departed.* By the name of winter and of rain is expressed the harshness of unbelief, which held the whole world until the coming of Christ. The bridegroom therefore says: *Arise, my friend, and come,* because *the winter is past,* the Sun of justice having appeared; and the *rain* of unbelief *is departed,* and the serenity of the true faith has shone forth in the world. Such is what the Apostle says: *The night is passed, and the day is at hand [n: (Rom. XIII, 12)]*.

Verse 12. *The flowers have appeared in our land, the time [1065C] of pruning is come.* Winter and rain being ended, the flowers have appeared in the land, namely the grace of the virtues and of chastity; and, the harshness of unbelief passing away, the spring mildness of faith succeeded, *the time of pruning is come,* that is, of the cleansing of the saints. For the pruning of the vineyards signifies the cleansing of the saints, which is in Christ and through Christ; of which he himself says: *I am the vine, you the branches [n: (Joan. XV, 5)]*.

*The voice of the turtle-dove is heard in our land.* That is, the voice of the preaching apostles. For the turtle-dove is a most chaste bird; and since it is wont always to dwell and to nest in lofty trees, it signifies the apostles or the other teachers, who can say: *Our conversation is in heaven [n: (Philip. III, 20)]*. And in that it has a groaning instead of a song, it signifies the weeping of the saints, who exhort their own to tears and lamentation, saying: *Be miserable, and mourn: let your laughter be turned into mourning [n: (Jac. IV, 9)]*. *The voice of the turtle-dove is heard in our land,* that is, the voice of the preaching apostles; *in our land,* that is, in the Church, of which the Psalmist says, *The earth is [1065D] the Lord's, and the fullness thereof [n: (Psal. XXIII, 1)]*.

Verse 13. *The fig tree hath put forth her green figs.* The *fig tree* signifies the Synagogue; and *green figs* is the name given to the first and unripe figs, unfit for eating, which fall easily at the beating of the wind. And at the sounding of the voice of the turtle-dove *the fig tree hath put forth her green figs:* because, while the apostles were preaching, many came forth from the Synagogue of the Jews who both believed in Christ and yet wished to observe the law carnally, and were more delighted with the authority of the law than with the sweetness of the Gospel: wishing to observe circumcision, the sabbath, and the other precepts of the law according to the letter; of whom the Apostle says: *They are zealous for you, not well; but they would have you circumcised, that they may glory in your flesh [n: (Galat. VI, 13)]*.

*The vines in flower have yielded their odour.* After the voice of the turtle-dove was heard, after the fig tree put forth *511* *her green figs, the vines in flower have yielded their odour:* because the multitude of the Churches was spread through the breadth of the world, [1066A] to scatter widely from themselves the flowers of the virtues and the odour of good working.

*Arise, make haste, my friend, my fair one, and come.* Be not willing (he says), my bride, whom I have made my friend by faith and love, to grow slack in idle torpor; but *arise and come,* and show outwardly to thy neighbours the examples of the virtues.

Verse 14. *My dove in the holes of the rock.* If the rock is Christ, according to what the Apostle says: *And the rock was Christ [n: (I Cor. X, 4)]*; the holes of the rock are the wounds which he received on the cross for our salvation, namely the piercings of the nails and the stroke of the lance [n: (Joan. XIX)]. In these holes, therefore, the dove, that is, the Church, abides: because she has set the whole hope of her salvation in the passion of her Redeemer. There she lies hid in safety from the snares of malignant spirits, as from the swoop of the hawk; there she nests, [sic: *il est*] she gathers the brood of good works.

*In the hollow of the stone wall.* Stone *walls* are wont to be made of dry stones for the fortification of vineyards, to drive off [1066B] foxes or the other harmful little beasts. By the stone wall, therefore, is signified the guardianship of the heavenly virtues, namely the angelic protection; as Isaias says of the Lord's vineyard: *And he set a stone wall about it, and dug round about the vineyard [n: (Isai. V, 2)]*. In the hollow of the stone wall, then, she abides, because she is surrounded on every side by the protection of the angels, and is guarded from the temptations of the devil.

*Show me thy face, let thy voice sound in my ears: for thy voice is sweet, and thy face seemly.* Thou (he says), my beloved, who restest most sweetly on the bed of contemplation, come and *show me thy face,* that is, go forth from the secret of quiet to public action, and display the beauty of thy good works to others for an example. *Show me,* he says; for the Church as it were shows her face to God when she displays to her neighbours, for their profit and advancement, through the works of the virtues, how great a beauty she has within. This is what the Lord says in the Gospel: *Let them see your good works, and glorify your Father [1066C] who is in heaven [n: (Matth. V, 16)]*. *Let thy voice sound in my ears.* The voice, namely, of preaching, or of divine praise, by which thou mayest make others to profit. For the voice of the Church sounds *in the ears* of the Lord when God is delighted by the conversion of many through preaching. And well does he say: *Show me thy face,* and *let thy voice sound,* not another's; *show me* thy own *face,* namely, which I gave thee in baptism, when I cleansed thee from every sin. *Let thy voice,* he says, *sound in my ears;* that it may proceed from the sincere intention of love for me; that thou preach for no other cause but for me.

Verse 15. *Catch us the little foxes that destroy the vineyards, for our vineyard hath flourished.* The voice of the bridegroom giving command to his bride and to the daughters of Jerusalem, that is, to the holy teachers, or to the faithful souls. *Catch us* (he says) *the little foxes.* The *fox* is a crafty and deceitful animal, dwelling in caverns and dens, and going not by a straight but by a winding path; and therefore it designates heretics or schismatics, who [1066D] destroy the vineyards, that is, who cease not to tear and to deceive the Church of God, namely the people of the faithful. He commands therefore that they *catch the little foxes,* that is, that they cease not to war down and destroy heretical depravity at its very beginning, lest, being grown strong, it assail more mightily. *For our vineyard hath flourished,* that is, the Church throughout the whole world has put forth the flowers of the virtues. And note that whereas above he said vineyards in the plural, now he says in the singular, *our vineyard hath flourished:* because out of many Churches one Church is chosen.

Verse 16. *My beloved to me, and I to him, who feedeth among the lilies till the day break, and the shadows retire.* The voice of the bride. *My beloved to me* — supply: let him be joined by the bond of charity and love, *and I to him* shall be joined, and shall consent in the interchange of mutual love. Or *My beloved* — supply: let him give words of exhortation and of divine promise; *and I* — supply: will show him my face. Or also, let *my beloved* deign [1067A] to be joined to me by the glue of charity, *and I to him* shall be joined by the bond of worthy obedience, complying with his precepts. *Who feedeth among the lilies,* that is, who is delighted and made glad among the white and sweet-smelling virtues of the saints. And it is to be noted that above the lily is spoken of in the singular; but here, in the plural, *lilies,* because many are the virtues of the saints, but there is only one faith and one charity, in which God is chiefly delighted. Or also *he feedeth among the lilies,* that is, he is most sweetly delighted among the choirs of virgins. For the whiteness of lilies is rightly compared to virginal purity. *Till the day break,* that is, until the clouds of the present life pass away, and the day appear, that is, until the brightness of everlasting blessedness come. And the sense is: *My beloved* affords *me* help, *and I to him* render worthy obedience — he who is delighted with the virtues of the saints — until, the night of the present life being run through, in which he is discerned through a glass and in a riddle, there appear the day of the heavenly brightness, that he may be seen face to face [n: (I Cor. XIII, 12)].
