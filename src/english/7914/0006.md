## CHAPTER III.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 1. ⟦s2⟧ *In my little bed by night I sought him whom my soul loveth; I sought him, and found him not.* ⟦s3⟧ It is the voice [1067D] of the Church gathered from the Gentiles. ⟦s4⟧ *In my little bed* (she says), that is, while I was still resting in carnal desires, and while I was still set in the darkness of unbelief and ignorance, *I sought him whom my soul loveth.* ⟦s5⟧ For many of the philosophers, not knowing God, yet with the highest zeal sought after him, wishing to know the Creator through the creature, as Plato, who in the *Timaeus* disputed much concerning the soul, and as Aristotle, Socrates, and the rest, who spent all the time of their life in studies for the sake of searching out the truth. ⟦s6⟧ *I sought him, and found him not;* for God could not be known through worldly wisdom.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 2. ⟦s2⟧ *I will rise, and will go about the city: through the streets and the broad ways I will seek him whom my soul loveth: I sought him, and found him not.* ⟦s3⟧ I will rise up (she says) from the couch of the body and of carnal delight, and will go about the city of this world, traversing seas and lands. ⟦s4⟧ *Through the streets and the broad ways I will seek him whom my soul loveth,* that [1068A] is, I will look upon those who walk by the broad roads of this world and are given over to their own pleasures, if perchance I may find the traces of my beloved. ⟦s5⟧ Although this may be said, by way of example, of many, yet it was chiefly fulfilled in that Eunuch who came from the ends of the earth to adore the God of heaven at Jerusalem, whom Philip found, and showed him Christ in the prophecy of Isaias [n: (Act. VIII, 27)]. ⟦s6⟧ This also was fulfilled in Cornelius, who, while still a pagan and not knowing God, gave alms and gave himself to prayers, whom Peter baptized with all his house [n: (Ibid. X, 22)]. ⟦s7⟧ Repeat, however, the difficulty of the finding, and he says: *I sought, and found him not.*

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 3. ⟦s2⟧ *The watchmen who keep the city found me.* ⟦s3⟧ The watchmen are the holy apostles and the other teachers of the Church, who keep the city, that is, holy Church, and defend it from the snares of unbelievers and of spiritual enemies; and these she questions.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Have you seen him whom my soul loveth?* ⟦s2⟧ The Church as it were questions the watchmen when with attentive ear she [1068B] receives their preaching.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 4. ⟦s2⟧ *When I had a little passed by them, I found him whom my soul loveth.* ⟦s3⟧ To pass by the watchmen is to search out diligently their sayings and their teaching. ⟦s4⟧ For we are wont to say: I have run through a book in reading it, or I have passed through it. ⟦s5⟧ *When* (she says) *I had passed by them, I found him whom my soul loveth:* because when with careful meditation we seek out the sayings or the writings of the saints, straightway we find the beloved: because we discover God in their teachings. ⟦s6⟧ It can also be understood thus: *When I had passed by them, I found him whom my soul loveth;* that is, when I had understood that Christ surpasses all sublimity and grace, then I found him whom my soul loveth, that is, then I truly understood how far he stands from the merits of the other saints.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I held him, nor will I let him go, till I bring him into my mother's house, and into the chamber of her that bore me.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, I clung to him with most ardent faith and devotion; *nor will I let him go,* but I will persevere in his love and faith, [1068C] *till I bring him into my mother's house and into the chamber of her that bore me;* that is, until I preach him to the Synagogue, who is my mother spiritually, and lead her also to faith in him. ⟦s3⟧ This shall be done at the end of the world, when (as the Apostle says) *the fullness of the Gentiles, shall have come in, then all Israel shall be saved [n: (Rom. XI, 25, 26)]*. ⟦s4⟧ So much, therefore, does the Church promise herself out of the charity of her bridegroom, that she even believes she can convert the Synagogue. ⟦s5⟧ But in that she says, *till I bring him in,* it is not to be thought that she will then leave him, when she has brought him into her mother's chamber; but *till* stands for the everlasting, that is, I will always cleave to him, I will always persevere in his faith and love. ⟦s6⟧ Such is the saying in the psalm: *So are our eyes unto the Lord our God, until he have mercy on us [n: (Psal. CXXII, 3)]*. ⟦s7⟧ Shall it be that after he has had mercy on us, our eyes shall not be unto him? ⟦s8⟧ Assuredly they shall.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 5. ⟦s2⟧ *I adjure you, ye daughters of Jerusalem, by the roes and the harts of the fields, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, until she please.* ⟦s3⟧ Twice is [1068D] this little verse repeated; for it has already been set down above: but above it belongs to the primitive Church gathered from the Jews; here, however, to the Church gathered from the Gentiles and more closely bound to Christ by the bond of love. ⟦s4⟧ For one and the same is God's care for the saints who pleased him either in circumcision, or also in the time of baptism when the grace of the Gospel was shining forth. ⟦s5⟧ But since this little verse has been expounded above, it must now be seen why he brings in the Church as it were sleeping, whence also he adjures the daughters of Jerusalem not to rouse her, since a little above he brings her in not sleeping or resting, but pressing on with the zeal of a most laborious search, by which she was seeking the bridegroom. ⟦s6⟧ If therefore she was seeking the bridegroom, how was she resting? but a most sweet and most pleasant sleep it is to seek Christ. ⟦s7⟧ The Church therefore sleeps, and seeks Christ; she sleeps, namely, to earthly desires and to the deeds of the world: but she watches and seeks the bridegroom, because she cleaves to his [1069A] contemplation, desires him alone, and strives to come to him. ⟦s8⟧ The bridegroom therefore adjures the daughters of Jerusalem, namely the souls that cleave to the desires of the heavenly fatherland, not to rouse her from this sleep of most sweet quiet and contemplation, *until she please.* ⟦s9⟧ And sleep is deservedly called love: because as one sleeping closes his eyes to visible things and contemplates things invisible, so he who applies himself to divine contemplation sleeps as it were a stranger to outward things, and sees only things spiritual and heavenly. ⟦s10⟧ Nor is it a wonder if love be called sleep, since in what follows death is called love: *Love is strong as death.* ⟦s11⟧ But when he says, *until she please,* he shows that the Church of the Gentiles would of her own will prepare herself to bear labours and persecutions for Christ.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 6. ⟦s2⟧ *Who is she that goeth up by the desert as a little rod of smoke of the aromatical spices of myrrh, and of frankincense, and of all the powder of the perfumer?* ⟦s3⟧ The voice of the daughters of Jerusalem, that is, of the saints of the primitive Church, marvelling at the beauty of the Church gathered from the Gentiles. [1069B] *Who is* (they say) *she? ⟦s4⟧ Who,* *513* that is, of what sort. ⟦s5⟧ How worthy of praise and admiration, who, cleansed by circumcision, and not yet sanctified by baptism, already runs to God? ⟦s6⟧ *She goeth up* (he says) *by the desert;* this is said in imitation of the ancient Jewish people, which, freed from the Egyptian servitude, came *through the desert,* the Red Sea being crossed, to the land of promise: so also the Church was going up *through the desert* of the nations, already freed from Egypt, that is, from the confusion of idolatry, and Pharaoh, that is, the devil, being drowned, that she might be fed with the heavenly manna, and might enter the true land of promise. ⟦s7⟧ She goeth up (he says) to the mountains of the virtues, or also to heaven itself, raised on high by the virtues; she who says in the psalm: *I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains [n: (Psal. CXX, 1)]*, and so forth; and elsewhere: *To thee have I lifted up my eyes, who dwellest in the heavens [n: (Psal. CXXII, 1)]*. ⟦s8⟧ These mountains are the patriarchs, prophets, apostles, and the other saints of God, who are deservedly called mountains for their virtues. ⟦s9⟧ For first to the mountains, that is, to the saints, [1069C] does the Church lift up her eyes, or the faithful soul which considers their teaching and their acts, and rejoices to be fortified by their example and intercession. ⟦s10⟧ Afterward, advancing, she dares to direct the gaze of her heart to him also who made the mountains, and says: *To thee have I lifted up my eyes, who dwellest in the heavens;* that is, who restest gladly in the saints. ⟦s11⟧ *As* (he says) *a little rod of smoke of the aromatical spices of myrrh and of frankincense.* ⟦s12⟧ Smoke is born of fire, and straightway seeks the higher regions of the air, until it is withdrawn from the sight of those who look on. ⟦s13⟧ Smoke, therefore, signifies holy desire suffused with the fire of divine love; nor are they compared simply to smoke, but to *a little rod of smoke:* because holy Church, or the soul that loves God, is slender and delicate, made thin by the disciplines of the Holy Spirit, not having the grossness of carnal desires. ⟦s14⟧ Or else they are compared to *a little rod of smoke,* not to scattered smoke, on account of the unity of the Church, which in Christ is one. ⟦s15⟧ And whence that smoke came he shows, when he adds, *Of myrrh and of frankincense and of all the powder of the perfumer.* ⟦s16⟧ With *myrrh* the bodies of the dead are embalmed; but *frankincense* is burnt in the sacrifice of God; and therefore [1069D] by myrrh is designated the mortification of the flesh, by frankincense is expressed the purity of prayers. ⟦s17⟧ To *a little rod of smoke of myrrh and of frankincense,* therefore, is the Church likened: because the saints strive to please God by the mortification of their flesh and by prayers poured forth with a clean and single heart. ⟦s18⟧ *And of* [sic: *unversi*] (he says) *powder of the perfumer,* that is, the heap of all the virtues. ⟦s19⟧ For the spices are said not to have been whole, but reduced to powder: because the actions of the saints are to be weighed with great discretion, and to be winnowed as with the sieve of the most subtle consideration, lest perchance there be in them anything harsh or unfitting: lest at the least through the virtues themselves the thousand-shaped adversary creep in, who, being crafty, is wont to do harm even through good things.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 7. ⟦s2⟧ *Behold threescore valiant ones of the most valiant of Israel surround the little bed of Solomon, all holding swords, and most expert in war.* ⟦s3⟧ Whither this beloved and wonderful one has gone up is shown when there is added, *Behold the little bed of Solomon,* [1070A] and the rest. ⟦s4⟧ Although the *bed of Solomon* may be taken as that heavenly rest of the heavenly blessedness in which God rests with his saints, yet more probably the present Church is understood, in which the saints of God, the tumults of the vices being lulled to sleep, are delighted in the embrace of Solomon, that is, of the peaceable man. ⟦s5⟧ Whence also the Psalmist says of Christ: *His place is made in peace, and his dwelling in Sion [n: (Psal. LXXV, 3)]*. ⟦s6⟧ The *little bed of Solomon,* therefore, that is, the Church, *threescore valiant ones of the most valiant of Israel surround.* ⟦s7⟧ By the threescore valiant ones is understood the order of teachers, who either fortify the Church by preaching, or by contemplation desire to attain to that heavenly blessedness. ⟦s8⟧ And the number sixty consists of the senary and the denary; for six times ten, or ten times six, make sixty. ⟦s9⟧ The senary indeed signifies the perfection of work: because in six days God perfected his works; but the denary, the recompense and reward which will be given to the elect at the end; whence also those who came into the vineyard are read to have received a denarius [n: (Matth. XX, 10)]. ⟦s10⟧ The *threescore valiant ones,* therefore, signify (as has been said) the holy [1070B] teachers, valiant and steadfast in mind. ⟦s11⟧ And there is added *of the most valiant of Israel:* all who believe in Christ and love Christ are called Israel, that is, a man seeing God. ⟦s12⟧ But the *most valiant of Israel* are those who know how to guard the Church by preaching, and to defend her from the assaults of demons or from the attacks of heretics. ⟦s13⟧ For these *surround the little bed of Solomon:* because to them the Church is committed, and by them she must be kept with watchful care. ⟦s14⟧ *All holding swords, and most expert in war.* ⟦s15⟧ For they *hold* the spiritual *sword,* the word of God, of which the Apostle says: *And take unto you* (he says) *the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God [n: (Ephes. VI, 17)]*. ⟦s16⟧ And they *are most expert in war:* because it is needful that they be instructed in the art of spiritual battle, who wage wars *against the principalities and powers of the world, against the rulers of the world of these darknesses, against the spiritual things of wickedness in the heavenly places* [n: (Ibid., 12)]. ⟦s17⟧ For that enemy is most wily, and most expert both by the subtlety of his spiritual nature and also by the long duration of time, [1070C] against whom the saints take up wars.
