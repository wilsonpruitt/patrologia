## CHAPTER III. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 8. ⟦s2⟧ *Every man's sword upon his thigh, because of the fears of the night.* ⟦s3⟧ By the thigh the propagation of offspring is understood; upon the thigh, therefore, they have swords who by the fear of God check and restrain all the motions of carnal pleasure. ⟦s4⟧ And this because of the fears of the night, that is, because of the hidden ambushes of the malignant enemy, who in the night of this world lies in wait chiefly for the saints who stand out in the Church, so that, when they have been deceived, he may be able to defile the couch of Solomon, that is, the rest of the saints. ⟦s5⟧ And it is to be noted that above they are said to hold swords, but here to have *swords upon the thigh*. ⟦s6⟧ For they hold swords that they may withstand adversaries, that they may overcome them, and that they may pierce through heretics and all who contradict the sound faith. ⟦s7⟧ Upon the thigh, therefore, they have swords, that they may take care of themselves, that they may repress the flux of carnal pleasures: lest, preaching to others, they themselves become reprobate [n: (I Cor. IX, 27)].

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 9. ⟦s2⟧ *King Solomon made himself a litter of the wood [1070D] of Lebanon.* ⟦s3⟧ That which above was set forth by the couch of Solomon is now designated by the *litter*, namely the holy Church, in which the saints of God rest as upon a bed, and as upon a bed recline to the banquets of eternal blessedness and satisfaction. ⟦s4⟧ *Of the wood of Lebanon did Solomon make the litter.* ⟦s5⟧ Lebanon is a mountain of Phoenicia, whose trees excel all others in height and in beauty and in durability, and therefore they signify the saints, shining with the appearance of the virtues and hastening toward things eternal. ⟦s6⟧ For *Lebanon* is interpreted "white-robed," or "whitening," or "made white." ⟦s7⟧ The *wood* of *Lebanon*, therefore, is the saints, robed in white, or made white in baptism, and adorned with the beauty of all the virtues.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 10. ⟦s2⟧ *He made the pillars thereof of silver.* ⟦s3⟧ By the pillars are signified the *514* teachers of that same Church, who uphold and strengthen it by word and by example; whence also in the Acts of the Apostles it is said: *James and John and Cephas, who seemed to be pillars (Gal. [1071A] II, 9)*. ⟦s4⟧ These *pillars* are *of silver*, because they shine with the brightness of heavenly speech; for *the words of the Lord are chaste words [n: (Psal. XI, 7)]*, and the rest.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A reclining-place of gold, an ascent of purple.* ⟦s2⟧ By the *golden reclining-place* is understood eternal rest, which is promised to the saints in the Church; to which, nevertheless, the *ascent* is *purple*. ⟦s3⟧ The *purple ascent* is the passion of Christ, because purple imitates the color of blood. ⟦s4⟧ By a purple ascent, therefore, one arrives at the reclining-place, because they come to the reclining at the eternal banquets, and to the repose of the rest above, who in the present labor to venerate and to imitate worthily the passion of the Redeemer. ⟦s5⟧ For unless a man shall have imitated the actions and the life of Christ, he will not be able to arrive at eternal rest.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He paved the midst with charity, because of the daughters of Jerusalem.* ⟦s2⟧ He paved the midst with charity: because Christ commended his charity to all the faithful by suffering for us and by preparing rest for us, according to what the Apostle says: *But God commendeth his charity in us: because when as yet we were sinners, [1071B] he delivered up his Son to death for us [n: (Rom. V, 8)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Otherwise: by the silver pillars are designated the apostles and apostolic men; by the golden reclining-place, the order of the teachers, who, when they promise the unspeakable joys of eternal life, assuredly make us to rest sweetly. ⟦s4⟧ And this reclining-place is well called golden, because unspeakable is that reward which the saints await. ⟦s5⟧ For among perishable things nothing is more precious than gold. ⟦s6⟧ But by the purple ascent is signified the order of the martyrs, who, having imitated the passion of Christ, poured out their blood for him. ⟦s7⟧ But what shall we do, says the blessed Gregory, who are of no merit, who see ourselves to have a place neither among the teachers nor among the martyrs? ⟦s8⟧ There follows, *he paved the midst with charity.* ⟦s9⟧ Let us therefore have charity, with which the midst of this litter is paved, which is also on that account called the broad commandment, because it prepares eternal salvation for all its observers. ⟦s10⟧ Let us hold this effectually, and through it we shall be saved, and this *because of the daughters of Jerusalem*, that [1071C] is, because of the simple souls that are conscious of no virtue in themselves; which, the more conscious they are of their own greater frailty, so much the more do they labor to love their Savior and Redeemer.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 11. ⟦s2⟧ *Go forth, and see, ye daughters of Sion, king Solomon in the diadem wherewith his mother crowned him in the day of his espousals, and in the day of the joy of his heart.* ⟦s3⟧ The voice of the Church inviting the souls of the faithful to behold how wonderful and fair her bridegroom is. ⟦s4⟧ For the *daughters of Sion* are of the Church, who are also daughters of Jerusalem, namely holy souls, citizens of that city above, who both enjoy perpetual peace with the angels, and by contemplation gaze upon the glory of God. ⟦s5⟧ *Go forth*, he says, *ye daughters of Sion*, that is, go out from the turbulent converse of this world; that with a mind unencumbered you may be able to contemplate him whom you love. ⟦s6⟧ *And see*, he says, *king Solomon*, that is, the true peaceable one, Christ, *in the diadem wherewith his mother crowned him.* ⟦s7⟧ As if he should say: Consider Christ clothed in flesh for you, which flesh he took from the flesh of the Virgin [1071D] his mother. ⟦s8⟧ For he calls *diadem* the flesh which Christ took for us, in which by dying he destroyed the empire of death, in which also by rising again he bestowed upon us the hope of rising. ⟦s9⟧ Of this diadem the Apostle says: *We saw Jesus crowned with glory and honor through his passion [n: (Hebr. II, 9)]*. ⟦s10⟧ But *his mother* is said *to have crowned him*, because the Virgin Mary furnished him from her own flesh the matter of his flesh. ⟦s11⟧ *In the day of his espousals*, that is, in the time of his incarnation, when he joined to himself the *Church*, not having spot or wrinkle [n: (Ephes. V, 27)]; or when God was joined to man. ⟦s12⟧ *And in the day of the joy of his heart.* ⟦s13⟧ For the gladness and joy of Christ is the salvation and redemption of the human race; according to what is in the Gospel, when he saw many flowing together to his faith. ⟦s14⟧ *In that hour*, says the evangelist, *he rejoiced and said: I confess to thee, Father of heaven and earth, because thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones (Matth. XI, [1072A] 25)*. ⟦s15⟧ And in the evangelical parable, when the sheep was found, he called together his friends, saying: *Rejoice with me [n: (Luc. XV, 6)]*. ⟦s16⟧ This whole passage can also be referred simply to the passion of Christ according to the letter. ⟦s17⟧ For Solomon, foreseeing in spirit the passion of Christ, long before forewarned the daughter of Sion, that is, the Israelite people: *Go forth*, saying, *and see king Solomon*, that is, Christ, *in the diadem*, in the crown of thorns *wherewith his mother crowned him*, the Synagogue, *in the day of his espousals*, namely when he joined the Church to himself, and *in the day of the joy of his heart*, wherein he rejoiced that the world was redeemed through his passion from the power of the devil. ⟦s18⟧ *Go forth*, therefore, that is, go out from the darkness of unbelief, *and see*, that is, understand with the mind: that he who suffers as a man is true God; or also *go forth* outside the gate of your city, that you may see him crucified on mount Golgotha.
