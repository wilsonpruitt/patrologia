## CHAPTER V. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they settle beside the fullest streams,* that is, beside the abounding knowledge of the Scriptures. ⟦s2⟧ But if we think there is any difference between the brooks and the fullest streams, it must be said that by the brooks are signified the writings of the Old Testament; by the fullest streams is taken the copious teaching of the holy Gospel. ⟦s3⟧ These doves therefore are both upon the brooks of waters, and settle beside the fullest streams: because the holy teachers assiduously search out [sic: *perserutantur*] the mysteries of the Old Testament as well as of the New.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 13. ⟦s2⟧ *His cheeks are as beds of spices planted by the perfumers.* ⟦s3⟧ By the *cheeks* of the bridegroom is taken the modest kindness of Christ, or his severity, or the bearing of his countenance; for in a man's cheeks gladness, or modesty, severity [1086C] also and kindness are discerned. ⟦s4⟧ And therefore in the cheeks of the bridegroom the bearing of our Redeemer's countenance is rightly taken. ⟦s5⟧ For those were his *cheeks* when he rejoiced in spirit, as the Gospel says, or when he wept over the dead Lazarus [n: (Joan. XI, 35)], or when he grieved over Jerusalem that was to perish [n: (Luc. XIX, 41)], and many things of that kind. ⟦s6⟧ These *cheeks* of the bridegroom are compared to beds of spices; for as beds of spices, excellently laid out and set in order, delight the sight of those who look on them, and offer them the grace of their fragrance: so the meekness of Christ delighted the absent by report and those present in very deed, and called them forth to love of him. ⟦s7⟧ These *beds are planted by the perfumers.* ⟦s8⟧ The *perfumers* are the prophets or the apostles, of whom the one sort described all these things as still to come, the other as already done.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *His lips are lilies dropping the first myrrh.* ⟦s2⟧ By the lips of the bridegroom are taken the words of our Lord Jesus Christ, which are compared to lilies: because they announce the rewards of eternal whiteness. ⟦s3⟧ *They drop the first myrrh,* because they teach that these things are to be reached [1086D] through the mortification of the flesh. ⟦s4⟧ Or also the *lips* of the Lord are compared to *lilies,* because they teach the works of chastity and cleanness. ⟦s5⟧ *They drop the first myrrh,* because they suggest that whatever adversities come are to be borne patiently. ⟦s6⟧ The *lips* of the Lord were *lilies* when, teaching on the mountain, he said: *Blessed are the poor in spirit, blessed are the meek, blessed are the merciful, blessed are the clean of heart [n: (Matth. V, 3, 4, 7, 8, 10)]*. ⟦s7⟧ *They drop the first myrrh,* when after these things he added: *Blessed are they that suffer persecution for justice' sake.*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 14. ⟦s2⟧ *His hands are turned and golden.* ⟦s3⟧ For in the hands of the bridegroom the working of the Saviour is worthily taken. ⟦s4⟧ For as a man works easily with the lathe, and needs no rule, so the working of the Saviour was easy; for whatever he willed, he accomplished on the spot: *because he spoke, and they were made [n: (Psal. XXXII, 9)]*; and of whom the evangelist says [n: (Matth. VII, 29)]: He was as one having his word in power. ⟦s5⟧ For they were [sic: *crant*] golden, because his working was carried through by the power of the divinity; for in gold the Divinity [1087A] is figured: because as gold surpasses the other metals and is more precious, so the Divinity excels all its own works in an unspeakable manner.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Full of hyacinths.* ⟦s2⟧ The hyacinth is a stone of the color of air. ⟦s3⟧ What then is taken in the hyacinths, but hope and heavenly desire? ⟦s4⟧ And the hands of the Lord are full of hyacinths, because all his working stirs us up to hope and desire of the things above. ⟦s5⟧ The hyacinth is also an aromatic flower, of purple color and of good scent. ⟦s6⟧ The hands of the Lord are full of hyacinths, because on the cross they were pierced with nails, and sprinkled with the redness of his own blood as with a purple color. ⟦s7⟧ But the former exposition, concerning the hyacinth as a stone, fits better here. ⟦s8⟧ In another translation a stone is found. ⟦s9⟧ For his hands are said to be turned, full of Tharsis. ⟦s10⟧ Tharsis is the stone which we call chalcedony.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *His belly is of ivory.* ⟦s2⟧ Among the members of the human body nothing is frailer than the belly, nothing more tender; and therefore by the belly is taken the frailty of the humanity assumed in Christ. ⟦s3⟧ Now ivory is the bone of the elephant, which animal [1087B] they say is most chaste, and of a most cold nature whence also it is vehemently sought after by the dragon, which is of a hot nature. ⟦s4⟧ The belly therefore of the bridegroom is of ivory, because the assumed humanity of the Redeemer admitted no corruption, no stain; *Sin* indeed *he did not do, neither was any guile found in him [n: (Isai. LIII, 9)]*. ⟦s5⟧ He assumed indeed the frailty of the flesh; and yet he was free of all sin; just as the brazen serpent lifted up by Moses in the desert [n: (Num. XXI, 9)] had indeed the figure of a serpent, but lacked the malice of venom.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Distinguished with sapphires.* ⟦s2⟧ The sapphire is a stone having the color of a clear sky. ⟦s3⟧ Whence in the vision of the Lord it is said: *And they saw the God of Israel, and under his feet as it were a work of sapphire stone, and as the heaven when it is clear [n: (Exod. XXIV, 10)]*. ⟦s4⟧ By the sapphires therefore are understood the works of the divine Majesty, which are accomplished in the flesh. ⟦s5⟧ The belly therefore of the bridegroom was distinguished with sapphires: because the humanity of Christ shone with heavenly powers. ⟦s6⟧ Nor is he said to be full of sapphires, but distinguished with them; so, [1087C] namely, that between the sapphires the whiteness of the ivory might appear: because the Lord so worked those things that were of the divinity, that he none the less accomplished those that were of the humanity, and did not leave off those that were of the divinity. ⟦s7⟧ For the working in Christ, of the divinity and of the humanity, is distinguished. ⟦s8⟧ For that he hungered, that he thirsted, that he wept, that he grew weary, that at the last he could be crucified and die, were works of the humanity; but that he raised the dead, that he came to the aid of all the sick, that he raised himself from the dead, were most evident works of the divinity.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 15. ⟦s2⟧ *His [sic: Crara] are pillars of marble, which are founded upon bases of gold.* ⟦s3⟧ The legs of the bridegroom are the journeys of the Saviour, by which he deigned to become man and to come to us; and these are rightly compared to pillars of marble, because nothing is stronger than marble, nothing straighter than a pillar. ⟦s4⟧ And the legs of the bridegroom are pillars of marble: because all the ways of the Lord are both strong and straight. ⟦s5⟧ Of his strength *524* the Psalmist says: *He rejoiced as a giant to run the way, his going forth is from the top of heaven* [1087D] [n: (Psal. XV II, 6)]. ⟦s6⟧ Of his straightness, elsewhere: *The Lord is just, and hath loved justices; his countenance hath beheld equity [n: (Psal. X, 8)]*; and, *All the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth [n: (Psal. XXIV, 10)]*. ⟦s7⟧ These *legs are founded upon bases of gold,* because all things that were to be done through Christ or in Christ were foreordained and determined beforehand by the counsel of the divinity before the founding of the world. ⟦s8⟧ For gold, which (as has often been said) is more splendid and more precious than the other metals, signifies the most unalloyed secret of the divine counsel. ⟦s9⟧ From the bases of gold therefore the pillars of marble rise, because from the beginning of the world were foreordained the incarnation, the nativity, the passion and the resurrection of the Saviour, and the other mysteries by which the human race was to be redeemed. ⟦s10⟧ Whence also the prophet, praising God, said: *O Lord my God, I will honor thee, I will sing praise to thy name, who doest wonderful things: let thy counsel be made true [n: (Isai. XXV, 1)]*.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *His fairness is as of Lebanon, chosen as the cedars.* ⟦s2⟧ Lebanon [1088A] is a mountain of Phoenicia, the boundary of Judea toward the north, and it is loftier than the other mountains. ⟦s3⟧ The trees also of that mountain excel the timber of all other forests both in height and in fairness and in strength. ⟦s4⟧ As (he says) *Lebanon* is loftier than the other mountains of the land of Judea: so our Redeemer is fairer than all the elect: because *he is fair in form above the sons of men [n: (Psal. XLIV, 3)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And as the *cedar* is more beautiful and taller than the other trees, so he too by the grace of the divinity excels all the saints from the beginning of the world.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 16. ⟦s2⟧ *His throat is most sweet.* ⟦s3⟧ Above, by the lips of the bridegroom his words were understood; now, by the throat, the inward sweetness of those very words is signified, by which our understanding is refreshed; whence also the Psalmist: *Taste and see that the Lord is sweet [n: (Psal. XXXIII, 9.)]* And many indeed can easily understand the words of the Lord; but few are they who are able to penetrate their sweetness, that is, who are eager to fulfil the precepts of God out of love and desire.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And he is wholly desirable.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he were to say: Why do I linger in describing him [1088B] through his single members? ⟦s3⟧ Wholly desirable is my bridegroom. ⟦s4⟧ Wholly (she says), because perfect God, perfect man. ⟦s5⟧ Wholly desirable therefore is he, because he kindles the angels and all the elect to desire of him, according to what the Apostle says: *On whom the angels desire to look [n: (I Petr. I, 12)]*; and the more nearly he is beheld, the more does he provoke those who behold him to gaze upon him with greater desire.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Such is my beloved, and he is my friend, O daughters of Jerusalem. ⟦s2⟧ Beloved,* because through faith and charity I cleave to him; but *friend,* because he has deigned to make me, redeemed from the bond of sin, his friend, and privy to his secrets; and the more anyone loves him, the more worthy of his friendship is he made.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 17. ⟦s2⟧ *Whither has thy beloved gone, O most beautiful of women?* ⟦s3⟧ It is the voice of the daughters of Jerusalem, that is, of the Churches, or also of the holy souls. ⟦s4⟧ For the bride of Christ is the Church, and the daughters of Jerusalem are that same Church, which consists [1088C] of the several souls of the faithful. ⟦s5⟧ They say therefore: *Whither has thy beloved gone, O most beautiful of women?*

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Whither has thy beloved turned aside? and we will seek him with thee.* ⟦s2⟧ For the bride herself had above adjured the daughters of Jerusalem to tell the bridegroom that she languished with his love; those same daughters of Jerusalem had asked what manner of one the bridegroom was, and she accordingly had set forth the form of her bridegroom; now the daughters of Jerusalem themselves ask whither that bridegroom has gone, that they may seek him with her. ⟦s3⟧ This the holy souls do, when they converse with one another concerning eternal salvation: burning with the love of Christ, and desiring the sight of him; or when they diligently search out in whose hearts especially Christ may rest, that they may be built up by their example and manner of life. ⟦s4⟧ And it is to be noted what is said, *Whither has he gone,* or, *Whither has thy beloved turned aside?* ⟦s5⟧ For he as it were turns aside for a time while he is sought, that he may stir up a greater desire to seek him. ⟦s6⟧ For to this end he very often defers the vows and prayers of the saints, that they may begin to desire him more ardently; just as of old [1088D] he did not at once show himself to Mary who sought him, but appearing in the form of a gardener [n: (Joan. XX, 15)], he showed at length that he himself was the one who was sought. ⟦s7⟧ Christ therefore always hears his elect, but he always as it were *turns aside,* when he does not at once grant what is asked. ⟦s8⟧ And well is it said: *we will seek with thee;* for he who seeks Christ without the Church can wander and grow weary, but cannot profit.
