## CHAPTER VI.

Verse 1. *My beloved is gone down into his garden, to the bed of spice, that he may feed in the gardens, and gather lilies.* We have already been taught above what the garden of Christ is, namely the holy Church, of which it was said above: *A garden enclosed is my sister the bride. Christ is gone down* (he says) *into his own garden, to the bed,* which he himself prepared and tilled, which he sowed with the seeds of the virtues, which he lit up with his own grace, which [1089A] he defended by the wall of his own keeping from the onset of the malignant spirits. And well is he said to have *gone down into his own garden;* for in a garden he willed to be buried [n: (Joan. XIX, 41)], and first of all appeared to Mary Magdalen in a garden [n: (Joan. XX, 15)], and there in a manner consecrated the first-fruits of the Church. He is said to have *gone down* when from the higher and heavenly seats he went down into the garden which he himself had planted in a lowly place; but for this he went down, that the garden (that is, the Church) might ascend to him. Whence also in what follows he says of the garden, that is, of his Church: *Who is this that ascends through the desert? To the bed* (he says) *of spice.* By the bed is understood the soul of every faithful man, which is cultivated by heavenly discipline and diligently composed, and squared by the evenness of its sides. *That he may feed* (he says) *in the gardens,* that is, that he may be delighted by the devout labors of the saints. For he himself is fed by our good things, so far that in the poor man he himself is refreshed. *And that he may gather lilies* (he says), that is, that he may gather to himself out of this world the holy souls that have been brought by ripeness of virtue to perfect whiteness, [1089B] and may make them rejoice with him in eternal blessedness. For the *lilies* are holy souls, made white by the pursuits of the virtues and by good works.

Verse 2. *I to my beloved, and my beloved to me, who feeds among the lilies. I to my beloved* — there is understood, I prepare a pleasing pasture; *and my beloved to me* will grant the grace of perfection, or the reward of eternal recompense. Or else, *I to my beloved* prepare a dwelling in my own self; *and my beloved to me,* because he himself dwells in me, and makes me dwell in him; as he himself says in the Gospel: *I in you, and you in me [n: (Joan. XIV, 20)]*.

Verse 3. *Thou art beautiful, my friend, sweet and seemly as Jerusalem.* The Church was seeking her bridegroom as though he were absent; but he, that he might show himself always present, *525* and might make plain that he is always at hand to her who speaks of him and desires him, answers quickly: *Thou art beautiful, my friend, sweet and seemly as Jerusalem. Thou art beautiful,* because thou hast not spot nor wrinkle [1089C] of sin, but art green with every brightness of the virtues. *My friend* — the Church, or every faithful soul. *Sweet and seemly as Jerusalem.* Jerusalem, which is interpreted *vision of peace,* signifies that city above in which is true peace and happiness: because there the saints so rejoice over the gift, that there is nothing over which they should grieve on account of corruption. Therefore the Church, which is still on earth, or every faithful soul, is after the likeness of that Jerusalem sweet and seemly: because so far as it can it imitates that peace. There follows:

*Terrible as a battle-line of the camps set in order.* The Church is *terrible* to the malignant spirits, and she is *set in order as a battle-line of the camps;* when, namely, each of the faithful rightly keeps his own order and his own calling. For there are three orders of the Church: of teachers, of the continent, and of the married; which kinds were shown in those three men, Noe, Daniel and Job. For Noe, who steered the ark amid the waves, signifies the holy teachers; Daniel, unmarried and chaste, signifies the order of the continent; [1089D] Job, who had a wife and sons, expresses the order of the married. Therefore the Church is set in order as a battle-line of the camps, because each order of the Church stands as in a line of battle against the enemy hosts of the malignant spirits, while the holy teachers spend their labor upon preaching and teaching. The continent, despising the things that are of the world, think only upon the things that are of God, and with a mind free from earthly occupations are at leisure for God alone. The married press on with alms and with the other good works of the active life; and so they use the world as not to forsake the things that are of God. This most well-ordered disposition of the Church therefore *is terrible* to the unclean spirits, *as a battle-line of the camps set in order* for war. Or else the Church is beautiful and seemly as Jerusalem in the virtues: but *terrible as a battle-line of the camps set in order,* because she does not cease to carry forth her rule and her power unto the ends of the whole world. Or she is *terrible as a battle-line of the camps set in order,* because she is so knit and joined together by the unity of charity, that by no force of the malignant enemy, [1090A] by no plague of discord can she be pierced; for nothing so terrifies the malignant spirits as charity does.

Verse 4. *Turn away thy eyes from me, for they have made me fly away.* As if he were to say: I indeed gave thee dove-like eyes, by which thou mightest gaze upon me and pierce most keenly into the secrets of the Scriptures; but beware lest thou wish to strain them now to seeing me: because while thou livest in this present life, thou canst not; *For man shall not see me, and live [n: (Exod. XXXIII, 20)]*. But when, loosed from the bonds of the flesh, thou hast come to me, then shalt thou see me with open vision, and there shall be fulfilled what is promised in the Gospel: *But he that loveth me shall be loved by my Father, and I will love him, and will manifest myself to him [n: (Joan. XIV, 21)]*. But as for what is said, *for they have made me fly away,* it is not to be thought that God forsakes those who seek him, and withdraws from them, since he himself commands: *Seek, and you shall find [n: (Luc. XI, 9)]*; but it is rather so to be taken, that the more anyone has wished to search out the divinity of the majesty of God, the [1090B] more does he understand how unsearchable and incomprehensible it is. And this is what the Psalmist says: *Man shall come to a deep heart, and God shall be exalted [n: (Psal. LXIII, 7)]*. As if he were to say: Although a man lift up his heart to seeing God, God shall be exalted, because how great he is cannot be grasped.

*Thy hair is as a flock of goats which have appeared from Galaad.* These little verses have been expounded more than once; but let it not irk us to expound again what it did not irk the Holy Spirit to repeat. For he set down these little verses twice for this reason, that we might hold as most certain and settled what we heard confirmed by repetition. The hairs of the holy Church are the manifold varieties of subtle thoughts. And they are well compared to a flock of goats; for the goat is a figure of the sinner; whence also it is commanded in the law that whoever has sinned through ignorance and has come to know his guilt should offer for his offense a she-goat for a holocaust [n: (Lev. IV, 28)]. The hairs therefore of the Church are compared to a flock of goats, because the saints, who are members of the Church, take pains to blot out all the guilt of their thought by worthy [1090C] penance and by the tears of just compunction. And well are they said *to have appeared from* the mountain *of Galaad.* Galaad is interpreted *a heap of witness;* and therefore by Galaad is signified the mind of the saints, who are a heap of witness, since it attests by the most evident tokens that it disregards all things that are of the world, and desires God alone. By Galaad also can our Lord and Redeemer himself be understood. For Galaad is the name both of a mountain and of a city. *From* the mountain therefore *of Galaad the flock of goats appeared,* because the Church is founded upon Christ, as he himself says: *A city seated upon a mountain cannot be hid [n: (Matth. V, 15)]*. He himself is the heap of witness: because the multitude of the prophets and of the patriarchs bear him witness by their deeds and their writings.

Verse 5. *Thy teeth are as flocks of sheep, which have come up from the washing, all with twin young, and there is not a barren one among them.* By the teeth of the Church is understood the firm steadfastness of her words. And these are well compared to a flock of sheep that have come up from the washing, because the words of the Church sound nothing but innocence and meekness, [1090D] and keep in themselves nothing foul, nothing base or unclean; but they are washed in the washing of sincerity and purity, hearing from the Apostle: *Let not evil speech proceed out of your mouth [n: (Ephes. IV, 29)]*. And well are they said to be *all with twin young,* because the speech of the saints sounds nothing else nor utters anything, save what belongs to the love of God and of the neighbor. For the saints ought to speak those things only which pertain to the everlasting soundness of bodies and of souls.

Verse 6. *As is the rind of the pomegranate, so are thy cheeks, apart from thy hidden things.* We said above that in the cheeks the chaste modesty of the saints is figured; in the *rind of the pomegranate,* the mystery of the passion of Christ, as we hear from Paul the apostle: *Be not ashamed of the cross of Christ, nor of me his prisoner [n: (II Tim. I, 8)]* [var: Vulg. *testimonium Domini nostri*, "the testimony of our Lord," for "the cross of Christ"]. And these things indeed are great, which are in the open: but greatest are those which are within, that is, those which are kept in store for the saints in time to come.

[1091A]Verse 7. *There are sixty queens, and eighty concubines, and of young maidens there is no number.* The queens are the teachers of the holy Church, who by the merit of faith and of knowledge draw near to the couch of the eternal King, and bear spiritual sons to God. The number sixty is completed out of ten and six. For ten multiplied by six *526* rises into sixty. And by ten are signified the divine precepts, on account of the Decalogue of the law; but six is put for perfection, because in six days God finished his works. *The queens* therefore are said to be sixty, because they signify those, as we have said, who are strong in perfection of knowledge and of work, and who preach for the love alone of their Creator and with a view to the recompense above. This number also, that is, sixty, is made up out of twelve taken five times; for five twelves, or twelve fives, make sixty. *The queens* therefore are said to be sixty, because they signify those who, being set in the Church, have known how chastely [1091B] to order the five senses of their body according to the institution of the twelve apostles. The *concubines* signify those who preach Christ not sincerely, but for the sake of temporal gain, or for the sake of popular praise. For these also, like the queens, come to the couch of the King through knowledge, and beget spiritual sons; but they stand estranged from the crown of the eternal kingdom. And well are they comprised under the number eighty; for this number is made up out of ten and eight. But eighty is sometimes taken in a bad sense on account of the number four, by the multiplying of which it consists. For four signifies all things temporal and present, on account of the four regions of the world or the four seasons of the year. Rightly therefore are these *concubines* said to be eighty: because (as has been said) they preach for the sake of temporal and visible things alone, and care less for things spiritual. Of such the Apostle said: *There are some who announce Christ not sincerely,* etc. [n: (Philip. I, 17, 18)]; until he says: *in this I rejoice, yea and I will rejoice. Of young maidens there is no number.* The young maidens are souls already regenerate in Christ, [1091C] who, having put off the oldness of the first man, have been renewed in Christ, and yet are not yet marriageable, not yet fit for the couch of the King; because they have not yet come to the perfection of preaching Christ. And *of these there is no number,* because infinite is the number of souls believing in Christ, nor can it be grasped by any man. For the rest, all the elect are numbered by God; for with him, by whom even the number of the stars is comprehended [n: (Psal. CXLVI, 4)], the number of the elect cannot be unknown.
