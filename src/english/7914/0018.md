## CHAPTER VII. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 5. ⟦s2⟧ *Thy head is as Carmel. ⟦s3⟧ The head*, therefore, of the Church is understood to be the principal part of the mind of the faithful; for as by the head the rest of the members are ruled, so by the mind the thoughts are ordered. ⟦s4⟧ And fittingly is the *head* of the Church compared to *Carmel*. ⟦s5⟧ For Carmelus, which is also called Carmel, is interpreted knowledge or science of circumcision. [1096A] Therefore the head of the Church is likened to Carmel, because it knows that circumcision, once celebrated bodily, must now be observed by it spiritually, according to what the prophet says: *Be ye circumcised to the Lord in your hearts [n: (Jer. IV, 2)]*. ⟦s6⟧ This understanding of circumcision the Jews did not have, and therefore they are rebuked by the blessed Stephen, the protomartyr, *529* who says: *Ye stiffnecked and uncircumcised in hearts and ears, ye have always resisted the Holy Spirit [n: (Act. VII, 51)]*. ⟦s7⟧ In the book of Kings too it is read [n: (III Reg. XVIII, 45)] that Elias went up into mount Carmel, and there by his prayer watered the dry earth with heavenly rains after a long drought of three years and a half. ⟦s8⟧ Which history breathes a beautiful allegory; for Elias, who is interpreted my God the Lord, signifies Christ, who, though he is God and Lord of all and of the whole creation, is in a special way her own, that is, is the proper God and Lord of the Church. ⟦s9+10⟧ He goes up into Carmel when by his grace he climbs the minds of his faithful, and through them bestows upon the parched world the rains of his graces. [1096B] Rightly therefore (as has been said) is the head of the Church compared to mount Carmel: because the mind of the saints is lofty by a certain manner of life, and offers Christ an ascent into itself through the increase of the virtues.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the hair of thy head as the purple of the king bound in the channels.* ⟦s2⟧ If by the head of the Church the mind is understood, by the hair of the head we ought to understand the thoughts of the mind, which continually issue from it. ⟦s3⟧ But the purple colour, which has the appearance of blood, signifies the mystery of the Lord's passion. ⟦s4⟧ According to the letter, what he says, *the purple of the king bound in the channels*, signifies wool already wrought into the purple colour and not yet drawn out into threads, but still set in the channels, which in Greek they call βάτια, in which the blood of the shellfish is wont to run down and to turn the wool into a purple colour. ⟦s5⟧ Mystically these channels signify the humility of the saints, in which the purple of the eternal King is dyed, when all the saints, by imitating the humility of their Redeemer, take pains to be made conformable to his sufferings; so far as they may be turned into [1096C] the dignity of purple, that is, labouring for Christ, they may deserve to be crowned and to reign with Christ. ⟦s6⟧ And this purple is said to be *bound*, because the hearts of the saints are fixed and established in the fear and love of their Redeemer, nor can they ever be separated from his charity.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 6. ⟦s2⟧ *How beautiful art thou, and how seemly, most dear one, in delights! ⟦s3⟧ How beautiful art thou* — namely, in faith — and *seemly* in work, *most dear one, in delights.* ⟦s4⟧ Above it was said of the same Church: *Beautiful as the moon, chosen as the sun, terrible as a battle-line of the camps set in order;* but here it is said, *beautiful and seemly, most dear one, in delights.* ⟦s5⟧ Which things seem to be as it were contrary; for to be set in order like a battle-line of the camps, and to be in delights, are at odds, because delights are wont to unnerve the strength of soldiers. ⟦s6⟧ But the holy Church does both: she is set in order like a battle-line of the camps, because she resists unbelieving heretics, and terrifies the malignant spirits by her holiness and perfection; she is also in delights, because she continually tastes the sweetness of the heavenly pleasantness with the palate of the mind, and amid [1096D] the pressures of this world desires to obtain those delights, and to be satisfied with them without ceasing. ⟦s7⟧ Whence deservedly she who is such is said to be *most dear* to her bridegroom Christ.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 7. ⟦s2⟧ *Thy stature is likened to [sic: pulmae].* ⟦s3⟧ The stature of the Church is her perfect uprightness; for she is not bent down to vices, but stands upright and unmoved. ⟦s4⟧ Whence the Psalmist said in the person of sinners: *I am bowed down and humbled exceedingly [n: (Psal. CXVIII, 107)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And the prophet, under the figure of Jerusalem, says to the sinning soul concerning the malignant spirits: *Because they have humbled thee, and have said to thy soul: Bow down, that we may pass over [n: (Isa. LI, 23)]*. ⟦s6⟧ But on the contrary the holy Church stands upright, and never consents to be bowed down, hearing from the Apostle: *Watch ye, stand fast in the faith, do manfully, and be strengthened, and let all your things be done in charity [n: (I Cor. XVI, 13)]*. ⟦s7⟧ And the Lord speaks to the children of Israel: *I am the Lord your God, who rescued you out of the workhouse of the Egyptians, that you might walk upright [n: (Exod. VI, 8)]*. ⟦s8⟧ The stature [1097A] therefore of the Church are likened to the palm, which in all that she does ever keeps in memory the reward above. ⟦s9⟧ For the palm adorns the victorious hand, and therefore signifies that reward which is to be given in the heavens to the saints who conquer.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And thy breasts to clusters of grapes.* ⟦s2⟧ The breasts of the Church (as was said above) are the holy teachers, who nourish with the milk of simple doctrine those regenerated in Christ. ⟦s3⟧ But these breasts are likened to clusters when the same teachers declare more perfect things to the perfect, as the Apostle said, speaking to the simpler sort: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual, but as to carnal; as to little ones in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not meat [n: (I Cor. III, 1)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Behold breasts full of milk; which breasts were turned into clusters when he said: *We preach Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God, and we speak wisdom among the perfect.*

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 8. ⟦s2⟧ *I said: I will go up into the palm tree, and will take hold of the fruits thereof.* ⟦s3⟧ That it might be shown that all this excellence and greatness of the gifts of the Church comes from the grace of God, [1097B] the same bridegroom is brought in saying: *I will go up into the palm tree;* as if he should say: From that source thou hast breasts for the nourishing of little ones, from which also thou hast clusters, whereby thou mayest hold out a more perfect doctrine to the more perfect: because I by my grace have gone up into thee, and have granted thee this perfection. ⟦s4⟧ *I will go up*, he says, *into the palm tree. ⟦s5⟧ The palm* signifies the Church, or also the soul of any faithful man who is mindful of the Lord his God, who says: *Have confidence, I have overcome the world [n: (Joan. XVI, 33)]*. ⟦s6⟧ She too strives to be the conqueror of all vices and of iniquity. ⟦s7⟧ And fittingly is he said here to have gone up into the palm, whereas above he is read to have gone down into the garden. ⟦s8⟧ For the Church said: *My beloved is gone down into his garden.* ⟦s9⟧ The garden is the same as the palm, namely one and the same Church. ⟦s10⟧ But the Lord goes down into her and goes up to her: he goes down into her by sending her from the heavens the abundance of his grace; he goes up to her by increasing his gifts in her and by lifting her up to loftier things [1097C] by certain advances, as it were by certain steps. ⟦s11⟧ But this too is to be known of the nature of the palm, that in its lower parts it is rough, in its upper parts most beautiful with the grace of its leaves and the beauty of its fruits. ⟦s12⟧ So also the holy Church in her lower parts endures for Christ the roughness of labours and sufferings; but in her upper parts, that is, in the heavens, she awaits the beauty and sweetness of the rewards. ⟦s13⟧ The tree of the Lord's cross too may be expressed by the palm, into which our Redeemer went up for the redemption of the human race, and on which he overcame the enemy of the human race. ⟦s14⟧ He says therefore, *I will go up into the palm tree*, that is: I have spoken through my prophets, and have promised that I would come to the passion and would redeem my Church. ⟦s15⟧ *I will take hold*, he says, *of the fruits thereof*, that is, of the fruits of the good works and labours over which the Church sweats, by rewarding them. ⟦s16⟧ Or also according to my sense, whereby the cross is understood by the palm: *I will take hold of the fruits* *530* *thereof*, that is, the triumph of the passion being fulfilled, I will come to the glory of the resurrection, and will grant spiritual gifts to my apostles.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1097D] *And thy breasts shall be as the clusters of the vine.* ⟦s2⟧ This has already been expounded above; for the breasts of the Church are the teachers, when they nourish little ones with simple doctrine; but they are made clusters of the vine when they announce lofty things to the more perfect. ⟦s3⟧ For the blessed Paul at one and the same time had breasts and clusters, when speaking of Christ he said: *Whose are the fathers, and of whom is Christ [n: (Rom. IX, 5)]*; behold the breasts, that is, the simple preaching concerning Christ: but these breasts were turned into most sweet and most strong wine when he added: *Who is God over all, blessed for ever.*

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 9. ⟦s2⟧ *And the odour of thy mouth as of apples. ⟦s3⟧ Thy throat as the best wine. ⟦s4⟧ The mouth* and the *odour* of the Church is her present preaching, which reaches those who are absent from her by report or by writing; but the *throat* is the very voice of the preacher, which is heard in the present. ⟦s5⟧ The *odour* therefore of the *mouth* of the Church is compared to the odours of apples, because the very report of her preaching is full of sweetness and grace. ⟦s6⟧ But the *throat* is likened to *the best wine*, because the very [1098A] voice of those who preach showed in itself great power to its hearers. ⟦s7⟧ But that the odour of apples is in newness, and that of wine in age, signifies that the preaching of the Church has all sweetness and grace both in its newness and also in its perfection.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Worthy for my beloved to drink, and for his lips and teeth to ruminate upon.* ⟦s2⟧ It is the voice of the Church, who, hearing herself praised by the bridegroom, answers with rejoicing, saying: *Worthy for my beloved to drink;* as if she should say: My bridegroom has compared my throat to the best wine, and that *wine* is *worthy to be drunk* by my very *beloved;* that is, the gospel preaching, which he has placed in my mouth, ought to be announced in the world through him and not through another; for it was not fitting that the mysteries of the kingdom of heaven be preached to the world through any other than through the *Mediator of God and men [n: (I Tim. II, 5)]*. ⟦s3⟧ *And for his lips*, she says, *and teeth to ruminate upon.* ⟦s4⟧ The lips and teeth of the bridegroom, as was said above, are the holy teachers. ⟦s5⟧ That best wine, therefore, is to be ruminated by the lips and teeth of the bridegroom, because the gospel preaching, [1098B] delivered by God, is to be handled and expounded by the teachers. ⟦s6⟧ Nor is it absurd that, although wine is drunk and not ruminated, yet here it is said to be *ruminated by the lips and teeth*, which belongs rather to food than to drink; for the holy teachers ruminate the spiritual wine when they diligently search out the gospel precepts, and investigate them by frequent meditation and confer them one with another.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Verse 10. ⟦s2⟧ *I to my beloved, and his turning is toward me. ⟦s3⟧ I to my beloved* — understand: I bestow the obedience of justice and devotion; to him and to no other do I promise my faith and my service. ⟦s4⟧ *And his turning is toward me*, that in the present he may protect me amid the labours and hardships of this life, and in the future may bring me into the joys of the heavenly life. ⟦s5⟧ This may also be the voice of the Synagogue in the person of the holy Fathers, who longed for the coming of Christ. ⟦s6⟧ As if she should say: I know, she says, that he is always present to me by a spiritual presence, and therefore *I to my beloved* — I desire to serve him with all devotion; but at last *his turning is toward me*, [1098C] because I wish that at last, as he promised, he may appear to me in my own substance. ⟦s7⟧ For the bridegroom had said above: *I will go up into the palm tree*, that is, he had promised that he would come in the flesh, and would be manifested to the world as man in my substance.
