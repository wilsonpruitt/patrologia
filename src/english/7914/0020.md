## CHAPTER VIII.

Verse 1. *Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of my mother?* From these words it is understood that this whole song is divine and spiritual, and has in itself nothing that can be understood according to the letter. For what woman in love ever wished this, that he whom she loves should be made a little child, and her brother; and, turned into an infant, should suck the breasts of her mother? It is therefore the voice of that Church which went before the coming of Christ, which we call the Synagogue, that is, the congregation. *Who*, she says, *shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts of [1100A] my mother?* And the sense is: Thou *who* art now *in the bosom of the Father [n: (Joan. I, 18)]*, who shall grant that thou be made man, and, being partaker and consort of my nature, be called my brother? For he himself deigned to call himself the brother of the apostles, saying through the Psalmist: *I will declare thy name to my brethren [n: (Psal. XXI, 23)]*; and after the resurrection, to the women: *Go*, he says, *tell my brethren [n: (Matth. XXVIII, 10)]*. *Sucking the breasts of my mother*, she says, that is, fulfilling all things that belong to human nature, sin excepted. For the mother of the Synagogue in this place is understood to be human nature itself, from which the Synagogue herself arose. *Sucking*, she says, *the breasts of my mother*, that is, bearing all things that pertain to human frailty.

*That I may find thee without*, that is, that I may see thee made man in the open. For God was as it were within, since he was already hidden in the bosom of the Father's majesty; but he was found without when, made man, he showed himself visible to men, and, being incomprehensible, willed to be comprehended.

*And I may kiss thee. And I may kiss thee*, she says, that is, that I may behold [1100B] thee with a manifest vision, whom now I hold only in the kiss of faith; which she wished at the beginning of this song, saying: *Let him kiss me with the kiss of his mouth [n: (Cant. I, 1)]*, that is, let him now come by himself, that he may appear to me. For the beloved was as it were kissing the Church through the prophets, when he promised his coming through them. She wishes, therefore, that coming by himself he may kiss her, that is, that he may show his presence in his own flesh.

*And now let no man despise me.* For the Synagogue was as it were despised before Christ came: because, given over to the observances of the law, she preached the coming of Christ under type and figure, and not in truth. But after Jesus appeared and enlightened the world by the grace of his coming, *now no man despised* her; nay rather, she was *terrible as a battle-line of the camps set in order* to her enemies: because through the whole world she preached Christ, and subjected the worldly empire to herself. Nor ought it to seem strange if we now call the Church the Synagogue; for the Church is one in the parts that go before and [1100C] in those that follow.

Verse 2. *I will take hold of thee, and will lead thee into my mother's house.* O beloved! by cleaving to thee through faith and charity, *I will lead thee into my mother's house.* The mother of the Church, in this place, is understood to be that heavenly Jerusalem of which Paul says: *But that Jerusalem which is above is free, which is our mother [n: (Gal. IV, 26)]*. *I will lead thee*, she says, *into my mother's house*, that is, I will follow thee returning into heaven, after the act of thy incarnation and the mystery of thy passion. And *I will lead thee*, she says, stands for this: I will escort and follow; because the apostles escorted with their eyes the Lord ascending into the heavens, and followed him with a devout gaze even until the cloud received him, until the angels standing by said: *Ye men of Galilee, why stand you looking up to heaven? This Jesus who is taken up from you into heaven shall so come as you have seen him going into heaven [n: (Act. I, 11)]*.

*There thou shalt teach me.* This refers back to what is above, where it is said, *I may find thee without.* When, she says, I shall have found thee without, [1100D] that is, when I shall have beheld thee in the flesh thou hast assumed, *there thou shalt teach me*: because thou wilt answer to all things that I shall wish to ask. For often too the Lord anticipated the apostles when they wished to ask, and instructed them [n: (Marc. IX)]. It may also be joined to this place, where he says, *I will lead thee into my mother's house, there thou shalt teach me.* When, she says, thou shalt have been received into heaven, and shalt have taken me also up with thee into heaven, *there thou shalt teach me*, and wilt lead me into all truth — which it is impossible for man to be able to attain in the present life. Hence it is that the Lord promises to the apostles, saying: *The hour cometh when I will no more speak to you in proverbs, but will show you plainly of the Father [n: (Joan. XVI, 25)]*. Therefore *there thou shalt teach me*, that is, thou wilt reveal thyself and the Father to me in open vision; that is, thou wilt show me thy divinity face to face.

*And I will give thee a cup of spiced wine.* By the *wine* is understood the fervour of charity and love. *532* *I will give thee wine*, she says, that is, I will show with how most fervent a charity I love thee. And this *wine* will not be plain, [1101A] but *spiced*, namely with good works and virtues. For the love of God, as the blessed Gregory says [n: (S. Gregorius, homil. 30 in Evang.)], is never idle; it works great things if it is there; for if it refuses to work, it is not love. For spiced wine is love together with work. Whence also the Lord in the Gospel: *If any man love me, he will keep my word [n: (Joan. XIV, 23)]*. The proof, therefore, of love is the showing forth of work.

*And the new wine of my pomegranates.* The *malum granatum* is the same as the *malum punicum*. Now it is called *malum granatum* because it is full of a multitude of grains (*grana*); *malum punicum*, because it abounds most plentifully in Africa. For *Punica* is Africa itself. And it has a rind of a blood-red colour, and therefore (as has often been said) it holds the figure of the Lord's passion. For the *new wine of pomegranates* is the love of the holy martyrs, who loved God so greatly that they did not hesitate to die for him. And fittingly is it called new wine, and not any other draught, because new wine [1101B] is most fervent, so that in its exceeding ferment it boils out and purges from itself every impurity. The Church, therefore, will give to the Beloved the new wine of her pomegranates, that is, she will show in the sufferings of the holy martyrs with how great a charity Christ is loved.

Verse 3. *His left hand under my head, and his right hand shall embrace me.* By the *left hand* (as the blessed Gregory says) are understood all the prosperities of the present life [n: (S. Greg. in cap. II Cant. cant.)]; but by the *right hand* is understood the blessed life to come in the heavens. The Church, therefore, has the *left hand* of the bridegroom *under her head*, *and the right hand* embraces her: because whatever prosperities of the present world allure her, she counts for nothing; all things that smile upon her for a time she despises; and while she strives toward the vision of the heavenly blessedness alone, that alone she desires to embrace.

Verse 4. *I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, that you stir not up, nor make the beloved to awake, till she herself will.* The Church has certain persons within her who have been [1101C] born again by the baptism of Christ and are entitled with the name of Christianity, yet by their manners and their manner of life serve the world, and strive to disquiet the Church lulled in a most sweet rest, and to rouse her to the enduring of tumults. These, therefore, on account of the grace of the mysteries of Christ which they have received, are called in this place *daughters of Jerusalem*, although by their manners they do the contrary. And the bridegroom *adjures* them *not to stir up nor to make the beloved to awake*, that is, that they should not by their own restlessness *rouse* her, set as she is in the sleep of a most sweet quiet, that is, in the contemplation of her Creator, and compel her *to awake* unto the anxieties and cares of the world. *Till she herself will*, that is, until, the office of her due service being performed, whereby she serves God in psalms, hymns, and spiritual canticles, she be admonished again by the care of bodily frailty to return to the use of the world.

Verse 5. *Who is this that cometh up from the desert, flowing with delights, leaning upon her beloved?* The voice of the Synagogue marvelling at the Church gathered from the nations. *Who is*, she says, *this*, that is, of what sort is she, of how great merit, *that cometh up from the desert?* For she had been forsaken by God [1101D] through idolatry and ignorance of the truth. She was ascending, therefore, by the steps of the virtues out of the desert, that is, out of the error of unbelief, in which for a time she had been forsaken. *Flowing with delights*, that is, abounding in virtues. *Leaning*, she says, *upon her beloved*, that is, acting with confidence in Christ, and referring all her virtues and all her goods to his grace. She knew that by her own strength she could accomplish nothing of good, and therefore she says with the Apostle: *By the grace of God I am what I am [n: (I Cor. XV, 10)]*. And it is to be noted that she says, *upon her beloved*: for the Synagogue thought that she alone had the knowledge of God, the rest of the nations being left in ignorance. For *God is known*, as the Psalmist says, *in Judea; in Israel his name is great [n: (Psal. LXXV, 2)]*. But to the Synagogue thus marvelling her own bridegroom and beloved answers what follows.

*Under the apple tree I raised thee up. By the apple tree* we ought to understand the Lord's cross, under which tree the Synagogue *was raised up*: because she was redeemed from the transgression of original sin, and from the power of the devil, by the cross of [1102A] Christ: there *was she raised up* who was dead in her sins. And fittingly does he say the apple tree; for above also she compared her beloved Christ to this tree, saying: *As the apple tree among the trees of the woods, so is my beloved among the sons.*

*There was thy mother corrupted, there was she that bore thee violated.* By the mother and her that bore the Synagogue he means the greater and more principal part of the Jewish people, such as were the scribes and Pharisees, who delivered Christ to Pilate [n: (Matth. XXVII, 2)], and who as mother and bearer ought to have ruled and instructed the rest of the people; and such as were those who, at the urging of those chief men, shamelessly cried out: *Crucify him, crucify him [n: (Joan. XIX, 6)]*. This *mother*, therefore, of the Synagogue *was corrupted* and *violated under the apple tree*, when she bound herself and her posterity with a cruel curse, saying: *His blood be upon us and upon our children [n: (Matth. XXVII, 25)]*. For they too were under the cross of Christ — not indeed in faith and devotion, yet by the imprecation of a cruel vengeance upon themselves and upon their posterity.
