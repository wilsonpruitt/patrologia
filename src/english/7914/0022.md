## CHAPTER VIII. (cont.)

*A thousand are thy peaceable ones, and two hundred to them that keep the fruits thereof. A thousand* — understand, pieces of silver, which thou gavest for the fruit of the vineyard — are *thy peaceable ones*; as if he should say: O faithful soul, be not doubtful concerning the eternal reward, for which thou hast given all that is thine: because the *thousand* pieces of silver are *thy peaceable [1105A] ones*, that is, that reward awaits thee assured, and thou shalt obtain true fruit therefrom. And *two hundred* — understand, pieces of silver — are peaceable *to them that keep the fruits thereof.* As by the number of a thousand the fulness of perfection, so also by the number of a hundred the highest perfection is designated: because the hundred is carried over from the left hand to the right. The two hundred pieces of silver, therefore, which is the hundred doubled, signify those who are perfect in doctrine and in holy work, who by living justly and piously keep themselves, and also by preaching convert others. To such as these, therefore, the two hundred pieces of silver are peaceable: because a doubled reward awaits them in the heavens; of whom it is said through Daniel: *And they that instruct many unto justice shall shine as stars unto perpetual eternities [n: (Dan. XII, 3)]*. For these the Apostle bids be doubly honoured, saying: *Let the priests that are among you be accounted worthy of double honour, especially they that rule over you and labour in the word [n: (I Tim. V, 17)]*.

Verse 13. *Thou that dwellest in the gardens, the friends hearken: make me hear thy voice.* The voice of the beloved addressing the bride, that [1105B] is, the Church: O Church, *thou that dwellest in the gardens*, that is, thou that appliest thyself to the cultivating of the shoots of the virtues. For the *garden* of the bridegroom is the Church herself, which (as was said above) he promises to visit often. The Church, therefore, *dwells in the gardens*: because she continually nourishes the nurseries of the virtues; nor does she set up her hut there in passing, as one about to withdraw after a little, but she dwells there in a fixed station. *Make me*, he says, *hear thy voice*, that is, preach the Gospel to those to whom thou art able; announce the precepts of my law, and together with them the promise of the heavenly rewards. For this is pleasant and delightful to me, that I hear thy voice, that is, that I hear thee announcing the words of eternal salvation. But *the friends hearken to thee*, that is, the angelic spirits delight to hear thy voice, whom I made thy friends by the shedding of my blood, and whom I appointed as helpers and protectors against the malignant spirits. Or the *friends*, that is, the spirits of the just, *hearken*, whom I have taken up out of thy company to myself into the heavenly places. For they rejoice together over thy preaching and over the salvation of the brethren. Straightway [1105C] the bride herself answers her beloved.

Verse 14. *Flee away, my beloved, and be thou like to the roe and to the young hart upon the mountains of spices.* A wondrous answer. She is invited to preach, and that the bridegroom may hear her voice; but she exhorts her bridegroom [1106A] to flee. But great is the sequence of the mystery. For because above the nativity of Christ had been foretold, when she said: *Who shall give thee to me for my brother, sucking the breasts:* for his passion had been foretold, where it was said: *Under the apple tree I raised thee up;* now she consequently exhorts the bridegroom to flee, that is, most swiftly to return by the ascension to the Father. And the sense is: thou biddest me preach, thou willest to hear my voice — *flee away, my beloved*, that is, the mystery of thy incarnation and of thy passion being fulfilled, *return* to heaven; that I may begin to think of thee no longer as man, but as God above all things. For then thou wilt hear my voice, because then I shall preach thee more freely to the whole world. And this is what the Lord says in the Gospel: *I tell you the truth, it is expedient for you that I go [n: (Joan. XVI, 7)]*. For it was expedient for the apostles and for the whole Church that Christ should return and should absent his bodily presence: because while he was present in the body they could not love him spiritually, nor could they think of him whom they beheld as man as God invisible and everywhere present. [1106B] *535* But after he himself ascended the heavens, the Church began to love him spiritually, and so now to think of him and to preach him as God. And this is what the Apostle says: *And if we have known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know him no more [n: (II Cor. V, 16)]*. Therefore, *my beloved*, if thou wilt that I so preach thee, *flee away*, that is, withdraw to the heavenly places; and thou who wast comprehensible through thy humanity, be made incomprehensible through thy divinity. Yet nevertheless forsake me not in the present without thy visitation, but deign to seek me out with thy frequent visitation, and to offer thyself to me more often to be seen. And this is what she says: *Be thou like to the roe and to the young hart upon the mountains of spices.* As, she says, roes and the [sic: *hinauli*] of harts, untamed animals, flee from human company, and yet are the more often seen upon the mountains: so thou too after thy ascension, although thou be invisible, yet deign to appear to me more often by the gift of thy grace, and this *upon the mountains of spices. The mountains of spices* are the saints of God, nearest to heaven by the excellence of their holy manner of life, and [1106C] fragrant far and wide with the odour and report of their virtues; hence the Apostle says of himself and of those like him: *Our conversation is in heaven [n: (Philip. III, 20)]*; and elsewhere: *We are the good odour of Christ unto God in every place [n: (II Cor. II, 1)]*.
