## The Rule of Living

[1247B] *341* 1. Act, my son, as you ought; act as is fitting; act as is worthy. Set it before yourself not to sin, not to repeat your faults, not to offend again after a fall; bear good things and bad as they come; endure with a free mind whatever comes; give your effort before all else to patience and gentleness; overcome mockery by despising it; break the arrows of insult upon the shield of patience; make ready the shield of patience against every look; against the sword of the tongue hold out the buckler of endurance — for by keeping silent you will conquer the better [n: (Vide Isidoriana, cap. 82)].

## 2.

Learn modesty from Christ, learn moderation too, [1247C] and give heed to the endurance of Christ, and you will not grieve at injuries. It is the greatest virtue if you do not wound the one by whom you have been wounded, or if, even when wounded, you let it pass. It is the greatest virtue if you spare anyone you had the power to harm; for when you are tormented on this account, your own sins are girding you about; when you are threatened, your own evils are making their way. Whatever adversity comes upon you, it comes upon you because of your sins. Temper your grief, therefore, by considering justice; you will carry the grief better if you attend to the things for which it comes upon you.

## 3.

When, therefore, you are disparaged, pray; when you are cursed, then bless; set a blessing against the curse, and be eager to soothe the angry man by patience; drive off boiling wrath with a gentle word; conquer wickedness by goodness of speech; conquer malice by kindness, appease all your enemies by modesty, overcome the evils of others by your own goodness. Undo with a tranquil mind the insults brought against you; open up your griefs with a quiet heart. A wound in the depth of the heart, however grave it be, evaporates when opened, but eats away the soul terribly when shut in. For the more you cover it, [1247D] the more you increase it; open it, therefore, with a grateful spirit, and it will not torture you.

## 4.

If you have made your brother sad in any matter, make it good to him. If you have sinned against him, do penance with him. If you have offended anyone, win him over by entreaty, spare him swiftly, quickly ask pardon in order to be reconciled for your offense; do not sleep unless you have turned back to peace; do not rest unless you have been reconciled to your brother. Call him back with the swiftest affection of love. Call him back to favor by humility; prostrate yourself before him in the affection of humility, and beg pardon in a suppliant manner; and when you yourself are asked for pardon, grant it freely, and, being appeased, release the one who asks for indulgence.

## 5.

Embrace holy reverence, and receive it at once with kindly charity; do not lightly dismiss the sinner justly, but know the fault, since the judgment is coming upon you; you will not have indulgence unless you have given it. And even if he does not entreat you, and does not humbly ask that it be forgiven him — if by a bad habit his conscience does not [1248B] recognize its own sin — do you nevertheless release it from the heart, and grant pardon of your own will as well; take the brotherly offense, or the taking of offense, away from your heart, and do not be a slave to another's wickedness; for hatred separates a man from God, and is not blotted out even by the shedding of blood.

## 6.

Love peace, cherish peace, have and hold peace with all men, *342* and embrace all in gentleness and charity; make ready to love more than you are loved yourself; do not be faithless in peace, do not be fickle in friendship, hold always to the bond of constancy; invite those who hate you to peace. [1248C]

## 7.

Judge yourself by your own judgment, not another's; and measure yourself not by another's talk, but by your own mind. No one can know better or more fully what you know than you, since you are conscious of yourself. And to whom is it any profit if you are foretold bad, or good? Or what does the praise of men have to do with you, if you are other than you are praised for being? Wherefore shun pretense, shun hypocrisy, do not counterfeit holiness beneath a drabber garment. Be, in your profession and your habit, such as you wish to be reckoned; and let there be simplicity in your walk and purity in your movement; let nothing unseemly, nothing wanton, nothing impudent, nothing insolent appear in your walk; for the bearing of the body is the sign of the mind. Do not offer yourself to others as a spectacle, nor give others occasion to disparage.

## 8.

Do not attach yourself to frivolous persons, nor mingle with the empty; shun the bad, beware of the wicked, flee the malicious, spurn the ignorant, drive from yourself the crowds of men, especially of that age which is prone to vices. Join yourself to the good, and be inseparably allied with the holy. If you do so, you will be a companion [1248D] of their manner of life and of their virtues; for it is a peril to be allied with the worst, and their hatred is better than their fellowship.

## 9.

Close your ears, that you may hear nothing shameless; for empty talk quickly pollutes the mind, and the mind is easily injured by base things and by all evils, and what is heard is gladly done. For no one is believed to hate those by the report of whom he is not injured. Let nothing proceed from your mouth by which you may be made base, or which is itself base; but let those things burst forth from your mouth and from your lips which do not pollute the ears of the hearers; for empty speech is the index of an empty conscience. The tongue lays open a man's character, and as his speech is shown to be, such is his mind proved to be. Restrain your tongue from idle talk, and do not speak empty words; for idle speech will not be without judgment. For each man is to render an account of his words; before the face of every man his own words shall stand. He who does not check evil words passes quickly on to harmful ones; and he who does not reject the least things breaks out utterly into [1249A] the greatest; for guilt in lesser matters begets guilt in greater.

## 10.

Vices grow bit by bit, and while we do not guard against small ones, we slip and fall in great ones; avoid the lesser, therefore, and you will not arrive at the greater. Let the things you say stand out well cultivated by weight and by learning, and let your speech be beyond reproach, useful to the expectation of the hearers; be zealous to speak whatever you speak wholly as you ought; discern what you should say and what you should keep silent. Give heed, and speak, and be skilled in speaking. But deliberate much on what you mean to say, lest you be unable to call back what you have said; flee the slips of the tongue, and let your mouth not lie open to the darts of injury. With a friend always keep silence; watch for the right moment for speaking, and for bringing forth a word, and for weighing it. Let measure remain in your mouth; let there be a balance in your speech; cut away from your tongue also the vice of disparaging, and do not defile your mouth with another's evil; what you disparage in another, this rather fear in yourself.

## 11.

What men say among themselves, never [1249B] desire to know, *343* nor seek out anyone to tell you; leave aside the matter that does not pertain to your own soul, and do not, forgetful of your own character, scrutinize the characters of others; correct your own vices with as much spirit as the zeal with which you have looked into another's.

## 12.

Flee every kind of lie with the utmost care; do not defend anyone's life by the zeal of talkative brethren, nor by any deceit whatever; no lie is just, since it is at variance with truth — and in this matter a double oath is likewise forbidden, for the constant practice of perjury breeds a habit of it. Do the good that you have promised, being not ready with words but slow rather in deeds and works too: never do anything, or presume anything, without consideration; when you will be able to do a thing, do not promise to do it. You will be greatly guilty before God if you do not render what you vow; but in evil promises, break your word. That is no promise which is fulfilled with grief.

## 13.

If you wish to bring your virtues to completion, do not betray them; [1249C] hide your virtues, on account of pride; conceal your good deeds, on account of arrogance; flee to be seen as what you have deserved to be; what by making it known you could lose, keep safe by keeping silent. But reveal the vices of your heart, disclose evil thoughts on the spot — for a sin revealed is quickly cured, while a fault dissembled grows. If the vice lies open, it becomes small from great; if the sin lies hidden, it becomes greatest from least. But it is better that you avoid a vice than that you amend it, lest perchance, when you have run into it, you be unable, through a very bad habit, to call yourself back. The chains of habit are loosed only with difficulty, and things long ingrained are corrected more slowly.

## 14.

Deliberate long on an opinion at its beginning, and what you intend to do at length, search out at length, test, and do; for in acting, nothing is better than delay, and nothing worse than headlong counsel. But in good matters let slowness be put away; for in good things delay does harm, and to put off what is expedient is to hinder it. In good things let slack sluggishness be absent, let negligence be absent; the vices of the flesh quickly seize the idle; for through torpor strength and talent flow away, and nature is corrupted by sloth; dullness puts out the light of knowledge, while skill [1249D] makes it better; talent becomes more ardent when zeal is joined to it, and slower talents are sharpened by studies. Industry rouses the torpor of nature; persistence sharpens the slowness of the spirit. Wakefulness perfects the spirit; by wakefulness more is known — for it often overcomes habit, and often what belongs to nature is the effect of habit; for persistence makes character, and continual practice turns itself into nature, and what you now do with difficulty you will accomplish, through practice, without difficulty.
