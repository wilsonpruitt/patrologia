## 15.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Learn what you do not know, lest you be found a useless teacher; and teach the good you have learned. ⟦s1⟧ When you share wisdom with others, you increase it for yourself; wisdom thereby grows longer; by holding it back it grows less, and while it is bestowed more widely, it abounds. ⟦s2⟧ When your words go before your work — what you utter with the mouth, what you promise with the mouth, fulfill in deed; what you teach in words, display and show in execution. ⟦s3⟧ Be not only a master of the virtues but an imitator of them. ⟦s4⟧ If you teach and also do, then you will be glorious. ⟦s5⟧ For it is not enough to commend what you have said, unless you mingle deeds with your words. [1250A] In the teaching of your own self, hold yourself back from human praise; so instruct others that you may keep guard over yourself; so teach that you may not lose the grace of humility.

## 16.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Beware lest, while you lift others up by teaching them, you be yourself disparaged through an appetite for praise. ⟦s2⟧ *344* And when you instruct them, do not use obscurity, but speak so as to be understood, lest by your speech you displease the simple or offend the prudent; the discourse of teachers will be according to the spirit of the hearers. ⟦s3⟧ Teaching must be begun according to men's characters; the remedies must be applied according to the wound, for different wills call for different discipline. ⟦s4⟧ Each man, therefore, is to be taught according to his own profession. ⟦s5⟧ The profit of the persons must be looked to, and you must handle each one accordingly in instructing him. ⟦s6⟧ Entrust to all the secrets of your profession; speak openly.

## 17.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Announce to all what is hidden from a few; do not blush to speak what you know how to defend; and what you feel to be lacking to your knowledge, seek from others; for by seeking, covered things become clear, and hard things are opened up by comparing. [1250B] Let there be in you no curiosity to know hidden things, nor seek knowledge beyond what shall have been written. ⟦s2⟧ Do not desire what it is not permitted to know. ⟦s3⟧ In debate do not speak against justice; do not strive to make void what is right; in every debate hold to what is right, and love to hear more than to speak. ⟦s4⟧ At the beginning, listen; at the end, speak. ⟦s5⟧ In every matter let the end be attended to, let the last things be sought out — and the last word is better than the first.

## 18.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Reverence all who are better in knowledge and in life, and to each one render the reverence befitting a higher rank according to his own merit of holiness, and do not present yourself as the equal of a holier man. ⟦s2⟧ Render obedience to your elders, and obey their old age with deference. ⟦s3⟧ Comply with all in their precepts in such a way that by complying with men's wishes you do not offend God. ⟦s4⟧ Do not grow accustomed to doing the evil of sight, nor, even if punishments hang over you, do not give consent to anyone who is sinning; for it is better to suffer death than to carry out ruinous evils. ⟦s5⟧ For a man is held liable not only as a doer of deeds, but as one privy to the sin; [1250C] nor is he free from the crime who obeyed in order that evil be done. ⟦s6⟧ He who complies with evil is like the one who does evil; bind the doer and the compliant under an equal penalty.

## 19.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Be zealous to be revered by those under you rather than feared, so that they may cleave to you more by the service of love than by the necessity of opposition. ⟦s2⟧ Let your subjects revere you more than they fear you; for love proceeds from reverence, and trembling brings hatred; fear takes away trust, affection restores it; fear does not preserve a lasting fidelity; where fear grows bold, strength follows it; where there is fear, boldness follows; and where there is dread, despair comes on. ⟦s3⟧ Wherefore temper the sowing of your mouth; and it is fitting to rule your subjects with the utmost goodness; do not be terrible toward those set under you, but so rule that they delight to serve you.

## 20.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Keep measure at every season, so that you do nothing immoderate, neither less nor more than you ought — nor ought you to be anything but moderate even in good things. ⟦s2⟧ For all medicines are useful, and with the right occasion all things become useful; for right things are spoiled by immoderate use, [1250D] and every excess lies in vice; look forward, therefore, to what is fitting for you and for the occasion, and where, when, why, and how long a thing ought to be done; examine the causes and the occasions of things, and carefully distinguish the discriminations proper to each work.

## 21.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Discern all that you do — why you begin a good thing, why you carry it through; in every operation hold to discretion, and be indiscreet in nothing. ⟦s2⟧ *345* When you have well discerned your work, you will find that many things have been corrupted by custom, many presumed upon through bad practice, and many usurped against chaste morals; then keep custom and the law least of all — let practice yield to authority, let law and reason conquer bad practice. ⟦s3⟧ Harm no one by your testimony, nor lend the voice of witness to anyone's peril. ⟦s4⟧ Let your speech obstruct neither anyone's soul nor his goods; defend no one against the truth.

## 22.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ When you judge, do not be turned aside by partiality toward any person, whether from a poor man or from a rich; look to the case, not the person. ⟦s2⟧ Spurn a gift, lest through it justice [1251A] be corrupted; for gifts always play the traitor to truth, and justice is quickly violated by a man. ⟦s3⟧ Do not seek temporal gains from a just judgment; seek no reward for yourself for justice while you are judging; keep your wage for the future, and do not seek to be repaid on earth what is owed you in the world to come. ⟦s4⟧ Shake your hands free of every gift, if you wish to dwell in heaven; in judgment, too, sit without mercy. ⟦s5⟧ Keep the justice of discretion, and do not be more just than you ought. ⟦s6⟧ For everything that is excessive is a vice.

## 23.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is an impious justice not to pardon human frailty; do not, therefore, love to condemn, but rather to amend than to correct. ⟦s2⟧ Keep rigor in the discretion of justice; let mercy follow upon the scrutiny of judgment; let indulgence temper the rigor of discipline; be as merciful in others' offenses as in your own, and judge no one more strictly than you would yourself. ⟦s3⟧ So judge others as you desire to be judged; for while you pardon another's sins, you have mercy on yourself; the judgment you lay upon others you will yourself carry. ⟦s4⟧ For in [1251B] that in which you judge, you are to be judged; in what measure you shall have measured, it will be measured back to you.

## 24.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Determine with justice all the things you have inquired into; never judge by the verdict of suspicion; first prove, then judge; for it is not the one accused, but the accuser, who is guilty; it is a peril to judge anything presumptuously. ⟦s2⟧ In doubtful matters, hold back your sentence in judging. ⟦s3⟧ You who know your own affairs, when you do not know, leave the matter to the divine judgment; do not lean upon a human judgment to condemn what God keeps for his own scrutiny; let us not judge uncertain things, until at the last the Lord shall come, who will bring hidden things into the light. ⟦s4⟧ For although they are not to be believed, yet on that account they will be dismissed — except such as are proved by sure evidences, and not such as are committed to open scrutiny, and not such as are established by the order of judicial process.

## 25.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Let the highest truth be held by you in the highest honor; however lofty the power you hold, press down loftiness in yourself; let honor not exalt you, but let humility preside over the summit of your high station. ⟦s2⟧ Be conspicuous by so much greater humility as you are illustrious with great dignity. [1251C] Fulfill humbly the cares laid upon you, and take up with a submissive mind the ministry handed over to you. ⟦s3⟧ Be obedient to the divine dispensation, and do not dare to go against his will. ⟦s4⟧ Exercise with moderation the rights of the power you have obtained, and set all things in order with a tranquil, not a turbulent, heart. ⟦s5⟧ Beware of honors which you will not be able to hold without guilt. [1252A] For loftiness of honors is a greatness of crimes, and in a higher rank the punishment is without doubt greater.

## 26.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ If you wish to be at rest, desire nothing of the world. ⟦s2⟧ You will always have rest of mind if you cast the care of the world away from you, and you will always enjoy eternal quiet *346* if you withdraw yourself from the din of earthly things. ⟦s3⟧ Cast away from you whatever will be able to hinder a good purpose, and hate with your whole soul the losses which the world loves. ⟦s4⟧ Whatever you have, have it for mercy's sake. ⟦s5⟧ Your virtue lends its support in works. ⟦s6⟧ Do not pick out the one on whom you will have mercy, lest perchance you pass by the one who deserves to receive. ⟦s7⟧ Give to all, lest perchance the one to whom you have not given be Christ himself; and to whomever you give generously, give with cheerfulness; and whatever you bestow, bestow with affection; bestow mercy without murmuring, offer alms without weariness.

## 27.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But let the good will be greater than what is given. ⟦s2⟧ Let the favor be greater than what is laid out; for your work will be such as your intention has been. ⟦s3⟧ For what is dispensed with a good affection, this God receives; but he who [1252B] gives with weariness loses his reward. ⟦s4⟧ He who stretches out his hand with sadness forfeits the fruit of the recompense; there is no mercy where there is no good will. ⟦s5⟧ Minister to the poor out of your own just labors. ⟦s6⟧ Do not take from one in order to bestow on another. ⟦s7⟧ It is of no profit if you refresh one from that whereby you leave another empty. ⟦s8⟧ Such compassion condemns; it does not win favor. ⟦s9⟧ The good that you do, do for mercy's sake, not for boasting; nothing for the sake of temporal reputation, nothing for the sake of fame, but for the sake of eternal life and the charity of God. ⟦s10⟧ Whatever you do, do for the reward to come; let the expectation of eternal recompense hold you fast. ⟦s11⟧ Beyond that, whatever you have done for the sake of glory — this is the whole of your deed.
