# Cruces — 8195, *Norma vivendi* (PL 83)

Garbled or corrupt printed readings, rendered literally in the English and logged here.

The work is a chain of asyndetic imperatives in the manner of Isidore's *Synonyma*
(Migne's single note, at 1247B, points the reader to *Isidoriana*, cap. 82). Many
sentences are elliptical by design rather than corrupt; only genuine breakdowns of
sense or grammar are listed.

## Standing decisions

- The bold arabic numerals *341*, *342*, *343*, *344*, *345*, *346* set into the
  running text are Migne's page/section numeration for the volume, kept italic and in
  place, never bare and never translated.
- Column 1248A and column 1251D are not marked in the printed chunking; the anchors
  run 1247D → 1248B and 1251C → 1252A. This is as printed, not a dropped anchor.
- Second-person singular imperatives are rendered as English imperatives with "you"
  throughout ("act", "learn", "beware"), not as archaic "thou" forms; the addressee is
  the *fili* of the opening sentence.

## Chunk 0

- **1248B, §5** — *peccanti juste non dimittas, sed culpam sciturus, quia in te est
  venturum judicium.* The clause is broken: *sciturus* is a future participle with no
  governing verb, and *juste non dimittas* sits oddly against the whole drift of the
  section, which urges forgiveness. Rendered literally ("do not lightly dismiss the
  sinner justly, but know the fault") without emending *juste* away.
- **1248B, §5** — *aufer a corde fraternam offensam, vel offensionem.* The doublet
  *offensam / offensionem* looks like a variant that has crept into the text; both are
  rendered ("the brotherly offense, or the taking of offense").
- **1248C, §7** — *Quibus vero prodest, si malus, si bonus praediceris.* *praediceris*
  ("you are foretold") for *praedicaris* ("you are proclaimed"); rendered on the
  intended reading.
- **1249C, §13** — *occulta virtutes pro elatione; absconde bona facta pro arrogantia.*
  *pro* + accusative of the vice = "on account of / for fear of" pride. Rendered "on
  account of pride" — the sense is that the virtues are hidden lest pride follow.
- **1249D, §14** — *Expergiscentia perficit spiritum, expergiscentia plus scitur.*
  *expergiscentia* is not a classical noun (formed from *expergiscor*); rendered
  "wakefulness". The second clause is impersonal and elliptical ("by wakefulness more
  is known").

## Chunk 1

- **1250B, §18** — *Malum visus facere, non consuescas, nec adhuc si poenae immineant,
  peccanti cuiquam non aquiescas.* Doubly broken: *visus* for *visum* (or for *visus
  es*), and a double negative *nec … non aquiescas* where a single negation is meant.
  Rendered on the intended reading ("do not grow accustomed to doing an evil that has
  been seen, nor … give consent"), with *aquiescas* (for *acquiescas*) silently
  regularized.
- **1250C, §19** — *ubi timor audet, eum sequitur robur; ubi timor, audentia sequitur.*
  Two versions of the same sentence stand side by side — almost certainly a variant
  admitted into the text. Both are translated as printed rather than one being suppressed.
- **1250C, §19** — *Quapropter tempera oris sementem.* *sementem* ("the sowing") of the
  mouth is unparalleled; possibly for *sermonem*. Rendered literally ("temper the sowing
  of your mouth") and flagged rather than emended.
- **1251A, §22** — *in judicio quoque sine misericordia sedeas.* Directly contradicts
  §23, which condemns judging without pardon; the intended reading is very likely
  *sine avaritia* or *sine acceptione personarum*. Rendered as printed.
- **1251A, §22** — *et cito a viro violatur justitia.* *a viro* is unmotivated; possibly
  for *a munere* (following the preceding clause about gifts). Rendered as printed.
- **1251A, §23** — *sed emendare potius quam corrigere.* *emendare* and *corrigere* are
  near-synonyms, so the antithesis collapses; the sense wanted is "amend rather than
  punish." Rendered on that sense, with the crux noted here.
- **1251B, §24** — *Quamvis enim non credenda sunt, ideo tamen dimittentur, nisi quae
  certis indiciis approbantur, et non quae manifesto examini committuntur, et non quae
  ordine judiciario comprobantur.* The most corrupt sentence in the work: the *nisi*
  clause and the two following *et non* clauses cannot all stand, and the logic inverts
  itself. Rendered word for word as printed, deliberately preserving the incoherence.
- **1252A, §26** — *Suffragatur virtus tua in operibus.* Abrupt and elliptical; rendered
  literally ("Your virtue lends its support in works").
- **1252B, §27** — *Amplius quidquid egeris causa gloriae, hoc est facti tui.* *hoc est
  facti tui* is ungrammatical as it stands (genitive with *hoc est*); the sense is that
  the glory sought is the whole payment of the deed. Rendered "this is the whole of your
  deed."

## Chunk 2

- **1252B, §28** — *nec quod ad aeternae, sed quod ad hujus … gloriam proficit.* Migne
  prints the variant *[ Forte, hujus vitae]*; *aeternae* likewise wants *vitae*.
  Rendered "the glory of eternity … the glory of this", the bracketed variant kept
  verbatim in place.
- **1252B, §28** — *dispositionis qualis esse debeas, descripsi.* The genitive
  *dispositionis* hangs loose from *descripsi*; rendered "what manner of ordering you
  ought to have."
