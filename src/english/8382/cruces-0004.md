## Standing decisions for this range

- **`uxor` = "wife", everywhere.** This Latin uses *uxor* where the Old English behind it has *wif*,
  "woman": *Si uxor aliqua ordinetur communi ordine* (0418B), *laica uxor impura* (0422D),
  *neque aliam uxorem* (0421D), *si uxor aliqua desponsata sit* (0417D, "if any wife has been
  betrothed"). The common brief says not to smooth a Latin Anglicism, so the English keeps "wife"
  and the oddity stays visible. *mulier* = "woman", *femina* = "woman", *puella* = "girl",
  *consors* = "consort", *conjux* = "wife"/"spouse" as context gives.
- **`annus` = "year"** throughout this range. No *hiems* occurs in chunks 0004–0007.
- `servus / serva` = "slave / bondwoman"; `pedisequa` = "handmaid"; `procuratrix` = "stewardess".
- `CAPUT PRIMUM` = "CHAPTER ONE."; `LIBER SECUNDUS / TERTIUS / QUARTUS` = "BOOK TWO / THREE / FOUR." (peer C's "BOOK FOUR." head is matched word for word).
- `secundo semper die` (0426B, 0426D ×2) = "always on the second day", literal. It may mean every other day, or Monday (OE *Monandæg* is not "second day", so that is weak). Left as printed.

## Cruces

- **0416C (head of CAPUT IV) `Do eo quod mulier`**: plate p. 214 prints *Do* for *De*. It is a one-letter slip in a head formula with only one possible reading. It is rendered "On" and logged here. **single-witness**: no `[sic:]` on this copy alone.
- **0415D `infra se` / `volit`**: the TEI reads *infra se* ("beneath herself") and the non-word *volit*. The plate reads *intra se* and *velit*. The English follows the plate ("within her", "shall … wish"). Both are pending patches, not Migne's defects.
- **0418D `uti non lit`**: the TEI has a non-word. The plate reads *uti non fit*, "as it does not happen". The pending patch is filed and the English follows the plate. The sense is the OE hedge "as it should not", rendered literally.
- **0419A `contrabant`**: the TEI has a non-word. The plate reads *contrahant*. The English follows the plate ("contract").
- **0416A `et alios. Dei ministros`**: the TEI has a full stop that the plate does not print. The English follows the plate ("and other ministers of God").
- **0416C `Si quis (hominem) possessionem suam occidat`**: "If anyone kills his (man) possession". The bracketed *(hominem)* is Mansi's/Migne's gloss, carried as printed. The head says *servum suum*, so the "possession" is a slave.
- **0417A `Juvenes et imprudentes omnino suspendendi sunt`**: "are by all means to be hanged". This is printed flat and rendered flat. It follows a canon that gives a man of twenty fifteen years' fast, so the severity is surprising. Still, the Latin says *suspendendi*, and the English says "hanged".
- **0419B `et absque eucharistia, sumatur`** (plate-confirmed, comma and all): "and without the eucharist, let it be received". The subject of *sumatur* is unexpressed and the clause contradicts itself. The intended sense is probably "and the eucharist be received" (*absque* perhaps a misrendering of an OE particle). **Negation fidelity: *absque* is carried**, and nothing is repaired.
- **0419A (head of CAPUT XIX) `crebros coitus`**: "frequent couplings". The canon is about marrying more than twice, so *coitus* stands for "unions". Rendered literally.
- **0419C `noctes diei Solis, et diei Mercurii, et diei Veneris`**: "the nights of Sunday, and of Wednesday, and of Friday", per the vocabulary table.
- **0420A `laicus … quatuor annos; si sit communis conditionis homo, jejunet quinquennium`**: a layman gets four years but "a man of common condition" gets five, the heavier sentence for the lower rank. Printed so and rendered so. Brief §4: the numbers were not normalized.
- **0420D `ut ad nullum jus mundi et pacem redire nolit`** (plate-confirmed): two negatives, *nullum* and *nolit*. The English carries both: "unwilling to return to no law of the world and peace". This is OE negative concord (*nan … nelle*) carried into Latin. Taken strictly, the Latin says the man is willing to come back to the law, and yet he is excommunicated for it. The sense is plainly "will return to no law". Per 7a, both negatives stand and the contradiction is recorded here. **Not a `[cj:]`**: Migne's type is sound and prints what the Latin version says. Pattern 18 is for a wrong printed word, and this is the Latin version's idiom.
- **0421A `ut non offerat plus reddere quam antea mutuo acceperat`**: "that he not offer to return more than he had before received on loan". The prohibition is aimed at the lender, but this clause is framed on the borrower. Rendered as printed.
- **0421D `ut nullius uxoris proximam secum habeant`**: "that they have near them the neighbour of no wife". *proximam* does not construe cleanly. It probably renders OE *neawist*, "nearness, company": no woman's company. Rendered literally, and *nullius* is carried.
- **0421D `neque cognatam suam, neque aliam uxorem`**: both *neque* are carried ("neither their kinswoman, nor any other wife").
- **0422B CAPUT VII**: the canon opens *Si episcopus … dederit, et non recordetur …* and closes the conditional on the quotation without an apodosis. A new sentence then begins with *Si quis contra hoc fecerit*. It is rendered as printed, with a protasis that ends in a colon and the quotation.
- **0422C (head of CAPUT IX) `complacitum sumat cum muliere`**: "makes a compact with a woman". *complacitum* is rare. Rendered by its root sense (agreement).
- **0424B `ita ut non praedicet omnibus hominibus`**: after *ut nullius eorum parcat ex alicujus hominis timore*, the result clause reads "so that he does not preach to all men what they are to do". Taken strictly, this makes the forbidden outcome the purpose. The sense intended is "spare none out of fear, lest he fail to preach". Rendered literally with the *non* carried.
- **0424B `Sacerdos est vox Graeca, et Latine significat *sacrum dans*`**: the etymology is false (*sacerdos* is Latin), but it is printed so. The gloss depends on the Latin words, so they stay in italics with an English gloss beside them in ordinary quotation marks, "giving the holy" (the *stylus* precedent).
- **0425C `quindecim annos, septem in pane et aqua, et septem quolibet anno …`**: fifteen years, but the parts add to 7 + 7. Plate-confirmed. Rendered as printed.
- **0425C/D `exceptis dominicis diebus Solis`**: "excepting the Lord's days, Sundays". Both vocabulary words are kept, and the anchor [0425D] falls between them as printed.
- **0426B `procreat infantem … vel (eum) ad compita et per terram trahit`**: "brings forth an infant … or drags (it) to the crossroads and through the earth". This is a charm (drawing the child through the earth), rendered literally.
- **0427A/B `si laicus, cum permissu ex ebrietate vomat`**: "with permission". The OE is probably *mid gewealde*, "wilfully". Rendered literally.
- **0427B `si per fraudem alium inebrietur`**: *inebrietur* is passive in form with an accusative *alium*. Rendered by the evident sense, "gets another drunk". The passive form has no English exponent that construes.
- **0427B `si vivus sit vendatur aqua` / `vendatur (cibus)`** (plate-confirmed): "let the water be sold" / "let (the food) be sold". This is odd as law. It is probably OE *sylle* ("give", which later means "sell") rendered by the later sense. Rendered literally both times.
- **0427C `Si ea non consecrata ex incuria in terram cadat`**: "If it, not consecrated, falls". The *non* is carried. The canon imposes fifty psalms for an unconsecrated host, which is lighter than the neighbouring tariffs for the consecrated one, and that is consistent.
- **0427D `ut putrefiat ad comedendum`**: "so that it rots for eating". Rendered literally.
- **0428A `Qui comederit antequam ad eucharistiam eat, et postea eucharistiam comedat`**: "eats the eucharist", literal (*comedat*).

## Scripture: every quotation checked (Clementine, `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt`)

The canons introduce these as the Saviour's or scripture's words. All five are paraphrase from an
Old English source, **not** Vulgate quotations, so none takes a `[var:]` (brief §5). The English
renders Migne's words as printed and does not echo the familiar verse where the words differ.

- **0416A–B, CAPUT XV (Jas 5:14–15)**: *Hic notat sanctus Jacobus … et si plenus peccatorum est, illa ipsi remittentur*. This is reported speech in roman type, not quoted. Vulg. *Infirmatur quis in vobis? inducat presbyteros … et si in peccatis sit, remittentur ei*. **Paraphrase.** Address correct.
- **0420C, CAPUT XXVII (Matt 5:23–24)**: *Si munus offeras ad altare Dei, et ibi recorderis quod frater tuus, hoc est: quilibet Christianus inimicitiam foveat tecum, desere ibi munus tuum …* Vulg. *Si ergo offers munus tuum ad altare, et ibi recordatus fueris quia frater tuus habet aliquid adversum te: relinque ibi munus tuum ante altare, et vade prius reconciliari fratri tuo …* **Paraphrase** with a roman gloss (*hoc est: quilibet Christianus*) set inside the italic (plate-confirmed). The address is right ("in his Gospel", the Saviour).
- **0421C, CAPUT IV (Lev 19:2; cf. Lev 11:44, 1 Pet 1:16)**: plate-confirmed *Estote sancti, quia ego Dominus Deus et Dominus vester sum sanctus*. Vulg. Lev 19:2 *Sancti estote, quia ego sanctus sum, Dominus Deus vester*. **Paraphrase.** The inverted *Estote sancti* and the doubled *Deus et Dominus vester* are the Latin version's, and the English renders them: "I the Lord God and your Lord am holy". ⚑ **Address finding:** the canon gives these words to *Salvator* ("the Saviour says"). They are Leviticus, cited in 1 Pet 1:16. ⚑ **The launch brief quotes this line as *… Dominus Deus vester sum sanctus*.** The plate and our TEI both carry *Deus et Dominus vester*.
- **0422B, CAPUT VII (Ps 14:5)**: *Tunc divitiis ejus uti debetis, qui pecuniam suam ad nullum injustum tributum tradit*, given as "the Saviour … through David the psalmist". Vulg. *qui pecuniam suam non dedit ad usuram*. **Paraphrase** (loose). Address correct: a Psalm.
- **0424B–C, CAPUT XVI (Ezek 34:2–5)**: *Vae vobis pastoribus, qui gregis lacte utimini … et cibus facti sunt omnibus bestiis feris*. Vulg. Ezek 34:2 *Vae pastoribus Israel…*, 34:3 *Lac comedebatis, et lanis operiebamini…*, 34:4 *Quod infirmum fuit non consolidastis…*, 34:5 *et factae sunt in devorationem omnium bestiarum agri*. **Paraphrase.** Plate-confirmed word for word, and all six *non/neque* are carried. ⚑ **Address finding:** attributed to *Salvator* ("which the Saviour said concerning the shepherds of the people of Israel"). It is Ezekiel. *Nunc omnis grex meus seditiosus est* has no Vulgate counterpart.
- **Allusions, not quotations (not checked as verses):** 0419A *sanctus Paulus hoc prohibet in doctrina sua* (on remarriage: Rom 7 / 1 Cor 7 permit it, and the canon limits it to twice); 0423A *sanctus Paulus hoc admodum prohibuit* (women at the altar: cf. 1 Cor 14:34); 0424A *uti sanctus Paulus docet* (cf. 2 Tim 4:2); 0418D, 0419B, 0422A, 0422D *sacri libri*. 0423B–0424A, Gregory's answer to Augustine, is the *Libellus responsionum* (Bede, *HE* I.27), paraphrased. It is not scripture.
