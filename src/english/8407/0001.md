## On Reckoning (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ M. What is more fitting than that that letter, which is the smallest of the letters in its character, should signify that number which is the smallest among numbers, that is, one? And no mark among the Latins is multiplied more than four times over. Then after I follows V, which signifies five?

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. Why does V signify five?

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ M. Because it is the fifth vowel in the order of the vowels; and as it is in the order of the vowels, so it is in number. ⟦s2⟧ Hence it is said of the fifth letter, marked with five strokes; of the letter X, ten. ⟦s3⟧ Nor is V doubled, but with a following V, an I is placed in the number. ⟦s4⟧ If one I, there become [0652A] six; if two, VII, seven; if three, VIII, eight; if four, VIIII, nine: it grows no more. ⟦s5⟧ Then follows X, which signifies ten.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. Who shows that X signifies ten?

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ M. Isidore says: The names of the letters among the Greeks both compose words and make numbers. ⟦s2⟧ But the Latins do not compose numbers to letters, except the letter X, which in its figure signifies a cross, and in number denotes ten. ⟦s3⟧ With X following, I is multiplied up to XIIII, which denotes fourteen. ⟦s4⟧ Then follows V after X and there becomes XV. ⟦s5⟧ With V following, an I multiplied four times, thus XVIIII, there becomes nineteen.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. What mark signifies twenty?

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ M. The letter X doubled. ⟦s2⟧ For it has twice ten, thus XX; they are therefore called twenty, because begotten twice ten. ⟦s3⟧ And thus XXX, thirty, begotten thrice ten.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. By what kind of mark is thirty signified?

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ M. By X multiplied thrice. ⟦s2⟧ And X is not multiplied more than three times over.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. Forty — by what kind of mark is it signified?

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0652B]M. By X and L.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. Forty — why is it so called? ⟦s2⟧ M. That is, begotten four times ten. [nt: (For every numeral letter signifying less, if it precedes, takes away from the one following only so much as it has in itself; as I, II, III, IV for IIII, V, VI, VII, VIII, VIIII for IX, X, XX, XXX, XL for XXXX, as some will have it, L, LX, LXX, LXXX, LXL or XC for LXXXX, C, CC, CCC, CD, for CCCC, D for CCCCC, DC, DCC, DCCC, CM for DCCCC, M.)]

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. What mark signifies fifty?

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ M. The letter L.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. Why?

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ M. Because the chief number in the law of the Lord is the fiftieth. ⟦s2⟧ For under that number fell the Jubilee, that is, in the fiftieth year. ⟦s3⟧ And it was fitting that that number which holds the primacy in the old law, that is, the fiftieth, should be signified by that letter L, which is the first in that name which is *lex* (law). ⟦s4+5⟧ But sixty by L and X; seventy, [0652C] by L and two X's; eighty, by L and three X's.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. What mark signifies ninety?

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ M. Two marks signify ninety, either an X between two L's, as LXL, or an X before C, as XC.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. What mark signifies a hundred?

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ M. The letter C.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. Why?

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ M. Because it is the first in that name which is *centum* (a hundred). ⟦s2⟧ For it was often the practice among the ancients to signify names by the first letter.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. Whence is it called *centum*?

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ M. From *cantus* (song), that is, a circle, or from a choir of singers.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ For it was the custom among the ancients to have a hundred men in a choir of singers. ⟦s2⟧ A hundred is therefore called from *cantus* (song) or from a circle. ⟦s3⟧ And C doubled thus, CC, signifies two hundred; tripled, CCC, three hundred; quadrupled, CCCC, four hundred.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. What mark signifies five hundred?

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0652D]M. The letter D itself.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ D. Why?

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ M. It was fitting that, the number of the prior letter, that is, C, being finished, that letter which follows in the order of the letters, namely D, should hold the following number, that is, five hundred. ⟦s2⟧ Then C following D, thus DC, six hundred. ⟦s3⟧ Two C's following D, thus DCC, seven hundred. ⟦s4⟧ C drawn thrice, D following, thus DCCC, becomes eight hundred. ⟦s5⟧ C multiplied four times, D following, thus DCCCC, signifies nine hundred. ⟦s6⟧ But a thousand is marked by the mark M (as we have already said), since it is the first letter in this word *mille* (a thousand). ⟦s7⟧ And a thousand proceeds from a multitude, and reaches up to a hundred hundred-thousands.
