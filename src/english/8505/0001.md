## THE SIXTH AGE.

From the nativity of our Lord Jesus Christ down to Tiberius XIV years are counted. From Tiberius indeed down to the Passion of Christ there are XIX years. And from the Passion of Christ down to Gaius there are V years. From Gaius down to Claudius, IV years. And from Claudius down to Nero [1176B] there are XIV years. From Nero indeed down to Vespasian there are XIV years. And from Vespasian down to Titus, XIV years. From Titus indeed down to Domitian, two years. And from Domitian down to Nerva, XVI years. From Nerva down to Trajan there is I year. From Trajan indeed down to Hadrian, XIIII years. From Hadrian down to Antoninus there are XXI years. And from Antoninus down to Antoninus again, XXIII years. From Antoninus, moreover, down to Commodus there are XIX years. From Commodus in fact down to Helvius Pertinax, XIII years. And from Helvius down to Severus there is I year. From Severus down to Antoninus Caracalla there are XVIII years. From Antoninus Caracalla down to Macrinus, VII years. From Macrinus indeed down to Aurelius there is I year. And from Aurelius down to Alexander, IV years. From Alexander down to Maximinus there are XIII years. From Maximinus likewise down to Gordian, III years. From Gordian down to Philip, VII years. And from Philip down to Decius, VII years. From Decius down to Valerian, III years. From Valerian down to Claudius, XV years. From Claudius [1176C] in fact down to Aurelian, II years. From Aurelian down to Probus there are VI years. From Probus down to Carus, V years. And from Carus down to Diocletian, II years. From Diocletian indeed down to Constantine there are XX years. From Constantine down to Constantius, XXI years. From Constantius down to Julian, XXIV years. And from Julian down to Jovian, II years. From Jovian, then, down to Valentinian there is I year. From Valentinian down to Valens, XI years. And from Valens down to Theodosius, IV years. From Theodosius indeed down to Arcadius, XXVII years. From Arcadius down to Honorius, XIII years. From Honorius down to Theodosius, XV years. From Theodosius down to Marcian, XXVI years. And from Marcian down to Leo, VI years. From Leo indeed down to Leo again, XVII years. Again from Leo down to Zeno there is I year. And from Zeno down to Anastasius there are XVI years. From Anastasius indeed down to Justin there are XXVII years. And from Justin down to Justinian, IX years. Again from Justinian down to Justin [1176D] there are XXXIX years. Again from Justin down to Tiberius, XIII years. From Tiberius down to Maurice there are IV years. From Maurice down to Phocas there are XX years. From Phocas down to Heraclius, VIII years. From Heraclius down to Constantine there are XXX years. From Constantine the father down to Constantine the son there comes one year. Again from Constantine down to Constantine there are XXVII years. And from Constantine down to Justinian, XVII years. From Justinian down to Pippin the Elder there are II years. From Pippin the Elder down to Charles, XXVII years. From Charles down to Pippin and to Carloman, XXVII years. And from Pippin and Carloman down to the time when Pippin was made king, there are X years. From Pippin indeed down to Charles and Carloman, XVII years. And from Charles and Carloman down to Charles there are IV years. And from that point the lord Charles took up the kingdom alone, and by God's help governs it for the needy prosperously down to the present year, which is the XLII year of his reign and the IX of his empire. And the years of the whole sum [1177A] from the origin of the world down to the present year are IV M DCCLXI.

[1178C]DCCLXXIX. King Charles was in Spain at Saragossa.

DCCLXXX. King Charles divided his kingdoms among his sons, and set out for Rome.

DCCLXXXI.

DCCLXXXII. The Saxons, rebelling, killed very many Franks, and Charles, when the Saxons had been gathered together, ordered them to be beheaded; and in this year Bishop Gislebert died on the X kalends of June.

DCCLXXXIII. Queen Hildegard died on the day before the kalends of May, and Queen Bertrada, the mother of Charles, died on the III ides of July at Choisy.

DCCLXXXIV. Charles returned into Saxony three times, and tarried there.

DCCLXXXV. Charles gained Saxony, and Widukind is converted.

DCCLXXXVI. Charles chastised the Thuringians for their offenses.

DCCLXXXVII. Charles set out for Rome, and those [1178D] signs appeared over men.

DCCLXXXVIII. Charles, having captured Tassilo, subdued the Bavarians.

DCCLXXXIX. Charles made war against the Wiltzes in Wendland, and Charles his son received a kingdom beyond the Seine, and that other frost was a very bad one.

DCCXC.

DCCXCI. King Charles was for the first time in the land of the Huns.

DCCXCVI. King Charles sent over his son Pippin into the land of the Huns, and they themselves received him in peace, and he took many treasures, and brought them into Francia.

DCCXCVII. King Charles held a council at the palace of Aachen with the bishops, abbots, and monks, concerning the monastery [1179A] of Saint Paul, how he ought to establish it. And in that same year he advanced into Saxony, and wintered there.

DCCXCVIII. Charles wintered in Saxony, and was there the whole summer, and brought thence a thousand DC chief hostages, and distributed them throughout Francia.

DCCXCIX. The Romans out of envy condemned Pope Leo, and Leo himself came to Charles in Francia, and he restored him to his see.

DCCC. King Charles was at the sea to fish, and Queen Liutgard died, and he set out for Rome, and made peace between the Romans and Pope Leo, and Leo blessed him as emperor.

DCCCI. Charles the emperor, returning from Rome, comes back to the palace of Aachen; and in that same year the Saracens came forth abroad, and were slain by the Franks.

DCCCII. Charles the emperor held a council at the palace of Aachen, that all should generally swear fidelity to him, monks and canons; and so they did.

[1179B]DCCCIII.

DCCCIV. Charles the emperor with his children and the Franks entered Saxony, and ordered some to be killed, [1180A] and others to be scattered throughout his whole kingdom; and Pope Leo came again into Francia.

DCCCV. Charles the emperor sent over his son Charles with an army into Wendland.

DCCCVI. Charles the emperor divided his kingdom among his sons.

DCCCVII. Charles the emperor held an assembly at Confluentes with the Franks, and they gave their gifts, and returned to their own homes.

DCCCVIII. Godfrid, king of the Northmen, came with an army into Wendland, and Charles the emperor sent over his son Charles against him, to withstand him; and he returned into his own land.

DCCCIX. In this year there was such a flooding of waters as had never before been seen in that land, and it was at its highest on the V kalends of January. And in this year the tomb of Saint Amandus the bishop was raised on the XII kalends of October, and restored to its place on the X kalends of November.

[1180B]DCCCX. Charles the emperor advanced with the army of the Franks into Saxony, and there held an assembly at Fereda, and there the Wends came, and he gave them a king.
