# Cruces — 8505, *Chronicon breve* (PL 94)

Rendered as printed; nothing silently emended. Roman numerals kept verbatim throughout.

## Chunk 0000

- **col 1174D (anchor out of sequence).** The anchor sequence runs `[1173D]` → `[1174D]` → `[1175A]`, skipping 1174A–C entirely. Reproduced as printed; may be a Migne plate error or a chunker artifact upstream — flagged, not corrected.
- **col 1174D — Matusala / Mathusalem.** Migne prints *genuit Matusala* and then, in the next sentence, *Mathusalem habens annos CLXXXVII*. Both forms are merely orthographic variants of the same patriarch; kept distinct in English (Mathusala / Mathusalem) so the printed variance survives rather than being normalized to "Methuselah."
- **col 1175A — first-age total.** The generations as printed (CXXX + CV + XC + LXX + LXV + CLXII + LXV + CLXXXVII + CLXXII + D + C) sum to 1656, not the printed *millesimus DCLXVI* (1666). Rendered as printed.
- **col 1175A — awkward ordinal.** *numerus annorum millesimus DCLXVI* mixes an ordinal (*millesimus*) with a cardinal numeral tail; rendered "the number of years, a thousand DCLXVI."
- **col 1175B — Tota / Tola.** *usque ad Tota* in one sentence, *A Tola quippe* in the next; same judge (Tola). Both forms kept.
- **col 1175B — Ebesan / Ebesa.** *usque ad Ebesan* then *De Ebesa autem*; same judge (Ibzan). Both forms kept.
- **col 1175B — third-age total.** The listed intervals do not sum to the printed DCCCCXLII, nor does DCCCCXLII + the printed second-age cumulative give the printed *duo millia DCCCXC*. Rendered as printed.
- **col 1175C — Joam / Joa.** *usque ad Joam* then *A Joa vero*; same king (Joash). Both forms kept.
- **col 1175C — Zesechiam / Zesechia.** Migne's form for Zedekiah, with an initial Z rather than the usual *Sedecias*; kept as printed, and the two inflections kept distinct.
- **col 1175C — Achat.** For Ahaz (usually *Achaz*); kept as printed.
- **col 1175D — fourth-age arithmetic.** The intervals listed exceed the printed total CCCCLXXXIII by a wide margin, and III M CCCLXIII does not follow from the earlier cumulative. Rendered as printed.
- **col 1176A — fifth-age total.** Printed DLXXX and cumulative III M DCCCCLII do not reconcile with the listed intervals. Rendered as printed.

## Chunk 0001

- **col 1179B → 1180A (anchor gap).** After `[1179B]` the next anchor is `[1180A]`; columns 1179C–D are absent from the chunk. Reproduced as printed.
- **annal DCCLVIII printed twice.** The annal for Pippin in Saxony is DCCLVIII, and then after DCCLXVII the death of Waifar and of King Pippin is also printed under **DCCLVIII** — where the sequence requires DCCLXVIII (768, the correct year for both deaths and for the anointing of Charles and Carloman). Printed year kept verbatim; not renumbered.
- **col 1177A — *Deo pro egente*.** **Printed:** *et Deo pro egente gubernat usque in praesentem annum feliciter*. Every word here is a real Latin form — *Deo* (dat./abl. of *Deus*), *pro* + abl., *egente* (abl. of *egens*, "needy") — so all three are rendered as printed: "and to God, for the needy one, governs it prosperously down to the present year." The clause is not construable as it stands: *Deo* is stranded outside the prepositional phrase and *gubernat* has no expressed object. The intended reading is almost certainly the ablative absolute *Deo propitiante* ("God being favorable"), a formula the annalistic register uses freely, with *pro egente* a compositor's misreading of *propitiante*; *pro indigente* is a weaker alternative. An earlier draft blended both conjectures into the running text ("by God's help … for the needy"); that has been withdrawn.
- **col 1176D — *unus fit annus*.** The chronicle's own formula elsewhere is *fit annus I*; here spelled out. Rendered "there comes one year."
- **col 1177B — *Radbodus* / *Radbodus obiit*.** Consistent; no crux, noted only because the neighboring Frankish names shift between *Karolus*, *Karlus*, *Carlus*, and *Carolus* within a few lines. All rendered "Charles"; the Latin variance is preserved in the twin chunk only.
- **col 1178C — *violavit Toringos*.** **Printed:** *Carlus violavit Toringos pro eorum culpis*. *violavit* is rendered by its own sense, "violated the Thuringians," though the annal plainly means a punitive expedition and the chronicle's register would ordinarily use *vastavit* or *edomuit*. An earlier draft rendered it "chastised"; that softening has been withdrawn — the loose usage is the chronicler's and belongs on the page.
- **col 1178D — *ille gelus fuit pessimus alius*.** Awkward word order ("that other frost was a very bad one"), picking up the frost of DCCLXIII; *gelus* for *gelu* is the chronicle's own form. Kept.
- **col 1178D — *Wulzis in Wenedonia*.** The Wiltzes among the Wends; place-name forms kept close to the printed Latin.
- **col 1179A — *hospites capitaneos mille DC*.** *hospites* here means hostages, not guests (cf. DCCLXXVII *dederunt hospites*); *mille DC* kept in the printed mixed form.
- **col 1180A — *ad Confflem*.** Doubled *ff*; the place is Confluentes (Koblenz). Kept in the printed form, transliterated as "Confluentes."
- **col 1180B — *in Fereda*.** Unidentified; left untranslated as printed.
