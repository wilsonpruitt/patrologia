## Treatise (cont.)

Therefore that which is said of the pit, that there is no water in it, pertains not to the sorrow of mind of the elect, but to the outward burning of the fire of the reprobate; in which, namely, since the flame is avenging, there is not even one drop of water at hand for cooling — which, unless he had known that it could be drawn from the streams of the elect, the rich man would by no means have said to Abraham: *Send Lazarus, that he may dip the tip of his finger in water, and cool my tongue, for I am tormented in this flame [n: (Luc. XVI, 24)]*. Thus we plainly say that it is not contrary that the ancient just were bound among the regions below by the fastenings of sorrows. [1383C] But what of it? Whether we have brought forward a certain or a doubtful opinion concerning hell in this respect, this nevertheless we say most truly: that the Son of God, paying for us the death he did not owe, in whatever manner and in whatever persons it was done, having loosed the sorrows of hell, brought back thence only his own; just as he himself long before foretold through Hosea: *O death, I will be thy death; O hell, I will be thy bite [n: (Ose., XIII, 14)]*. In which words it is assuredly to be weighed with care that our Redeemer did indeed utterly slay death, but only in the part which he himself swallowed down; whereas death, in the part that remained, remained. Let it be said, therefore, *O death, I will be thy death,* as though it were said: in the prey which I take, I utterly destroy thee. *O hell, I will be thy bite,* [1383D] that is, I do not swallow thee whole, but biting a part from thee I consume it. For he did not, as the catholic faith has laid down beforehand, bring back thence to the world above any others than his own, by the intervention of his most blessed passion.

But now the order set forth demands that, after the rescue of the ancient just from the regions below, we show whither at this present time the souls of the perfect are received. For the Master of the heavens, who never at any time [1384A] deceived anyone, established a sentence on this from which he said that, from the days of John the Baptist, *the kingdom of heaven suffereth violence, and the violent take it by force [n: (Matth. XI, 12)]*. O you teacher of a new error, do the violent take the kingdom of heaven by force only on the day of judgment? — as the devil has framed it through your mouth — and not rather from the [sic: *dibus*] of John? . . . . . .

*Here one leaf is missing.* . . . if it please you, let these things out of many suffice; but if, on account of the person of my littleness, even these come into doubt, I judge it necessary to approve myself a true witness in these things I have said, by the general opinion of the catholics. For in the *Dogma of the Churchmen,* which is comprised in one volume by distinct sentences, and which is received throughout the whole world by all, defended by [1384B] all, and celebrated with the highest praise, it is thus written under the forty-fifth heading: « Before the passion of the Lord all the souls of the saints were held in hell under the debt of Adam's transgression, until by the authority of the Lord they were freed from their servile condition through his undeserved death. » In the following heading also, that is the forty-sixth, such an authority is contained, as to say: « After the ascension of the Lord to the heavens, all the souls of the saints are with Christ, and going forth from the body they go to Christ, awaiting the resurrection of their body, that they may be transformed to whole and perfect blessedness together with him. As also the souls of sinners, set in hell under fear, await the resurrection of their body, that they may be turned with it to eternal punishment. » [1384C] This is the opinion of the Fathers, both of all who went before and of all who followed after — one, and singular, and general — with which, rightly agreeing, blessed Gregory, pope of Rome, prelate of the apostolic see, the last indeed of the expounders but almost the most outstanding of all, answered thus in the fourth book of the *Dialogues* to the inquiry of his interlocutor, saying: « It is certain that the souls of the perfect just, as soon as they go out from the enclosure of this flesh, are received in the heavenly seats. Which Truth itself also attests by itself, saying: *Wheresoever the body shall be, thither shall the eagles also be gathered together [n: (Luc. XXVII, 37)]*; for where our Redeemer himself is in body, thither are gathered also the souls of the just. And Paul *desires to be dissolved, and to be with Christ.* He, therefore, who does not doubt that Christ is in [1384D] heaven, will not deny that Paul's soul too is in heaven; who also, concerning the dissolution of his body and the habitation of the heavenly fatherland, says: *We know that if our earthly house,* having no inhabitant, falls to ruin, *that we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, but eternal in the heavens.* » [n: (II Cor. V, 1.)]

These things he said of the just and the perfect. But what of [1385A] the wicked? « Since, he says to his aforesaid interlocutor, you have believed, by the satisfaction of the sacred eloquence, that the souls of the saints are in heaven, it behooves that you believe in all respects that the souls of the wicked too are in hell; for by the recompense of the inward justice, from which the just are glorified, it is necessary in all respects that the unjust too be tormented. For as blessedness gladdens the elect, so it must be believed that from the day of their departure fire burns up the reprobate. » And all these things, of course, the same most truthful teacher showed not only by the sentences of words, but also by the examples of things. The same man, assuredly, narrated in the aforesaid book that the soul of Germanus the bishop was borne up into heaven by angels in a sphere of fire. The same, that the soul of blessed Benedict was led to the region above by a way strewn with palls. The same, that the soul of the paralytic little servant of the hospice [1385B] was taken up by the inward praises of the heavens, and carried above their loftiest hinges. The same, that the soul of the holy virgin Scholastica was led to the ethereal [1386A] bridal chamber in the likeness of a dove. The same, that the soul of the venerable Romula was taken up to everlasting life by heavenly obsequies. The same too he said, that the spirit of Gordiana his aunt was handed over to the fires of Gehenna. That the monk Hilliricianus, led down to the places of the regions below, saw the powerful and rich of this world hung in the midst of the flames. It is enough, it is sufficient. What one of the faithful can doubt any further about this matter? This witness too is very fit, against whose opinions to go is a sin. Wherefore I would have you warned, all of you whom that most unskilled man has attempted to lead astray from the heavenly wisdom by the windings of his lies, that, as you press toward the battle-line of faith, you be not turned aside from the journey you have begun by the shattered croaking of a little frog, but that you follow the most clearly resounding blare of the trumpet of the city above. [1386B] This is my prayer to God for you, this also my greeting to you. Amen.
