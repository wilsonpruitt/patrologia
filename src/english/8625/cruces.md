# Cruces — 8625 (*Appendix ad epistolas*, PL 98)

## Col. 0935C (head I) — mismatched bracket `[Ex Mabill. Vet. Analect.)`

The head prints an opening square bracket closed by a round parenthesis:
`RESPONSIO.[Ex Mabill. Vet. Analect.)`. Preserved verbatim in the head text
(and in the copied frontmatter `heads` array). Not emended.

## Col. 0935C–0936C (Odilbert letter) — anacoluthic sentence

The long sentence beginning "Quorum vos meritis et scientia praecellentes…"
(from col. 0935D through the interpolated 0936B/0936C column jumps) never
reaches a finite main verb — it runs on through participial and relative
clauses. This is the source text's own broken syntax (the Ambrosian
compiler's), aggravated by the *vel caetera quae sequuntur* abridgement at
0936B where Migne's editor has cut material. Rendered literally clause by
clause, preserving the incompleteness rather than supplying a verb.

## Col. 0937A — `praesentiae [ *Forte,* prudentiae]`

Migne's own conjectural variant, printed in the text: *dignissimae praesentiae*
with the editorial note that *prudentiae* is perhaps the right reading. Kept
verbatim and untranslated inside the brackets, on the same footing as
`[ *al.,* …]` variants; the main-text word rendered as printed ("presence").

## Col. 0940B (head II & body, CAP. II) — `quo [si] ordine debemus celebramus`

The chapter title is grammatically corrupt: *quo [si] ordine debemus
celebramus* mixes an editorial bracketed *[si]* with the ungrammatical pairing
*debemus celebramus* (indicative where an infinitive *celebrare* is expected).
The bracketed *[si]* is Migne's editor's insertion. Rendered literally,
retaining the `[si]` bracket untranslated in the head, "in what order we ought
[si] to celebrate." Not emended.

## Col. 0940A / 0940B — editorial bracket `[quia]`, `[because]`

At 0940B "quamvis corporaliter acta sint, [quia] aliquid divinitus significant"
the *[quia]* is an editorial supplement; rendered as bracketed "[because]".
Noted for consistency, not an error.

## Col. 0940D — `*Desunt hic plurima.*`

Editorial note in italics closing the chunk ("Many things are wanting here"),
i.e. the text breaks off. Translated as an editorial note, italics preserved —
it is Migne's apparatus, not the author's prose.
