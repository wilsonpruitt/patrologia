## The Life of St. Martin of Tours



## I.

[0657D]After our Lord Jesus Christ ascended in triumph to the heights of the heavens, and as God took his seat in the majesty of the Father, he granted to the ages many lights of teachers, so that, the darkness of ignorance being put to flight, he might turn the nations everywhere to the acknowledging of the true light [ *Al.,* converterent]: that just as the stars shine out in different parts of the sky, so also holy teachers might give light through the various parts of the world, so that the way of salvation might lie open to all, and the light of truth shine out. Whence also he led [ *Al.,* [0658D] directed] the most blessed Martin into the regions of Gaul, that he might shed the light of truth upon peoples numb with foul gloom, and might draw very many nations out of the jaws of the ancient enemy, and graft them into the most sacred body of Christ.

## II.

*The holy man's homeland and military service. He loves to follow the warfare of Christ. As a catechumen he clothes a poor man.* -- The most blessed man, then, was born of pagan parents in the region of Pannonia, in the town of Sabaria; but he was brought up within Ticinum in Italy, and was bound to the belt of military service, according to the nobility of his parents, by the governor [ *Al.,* by the faithless] Julian. [0659A] But the holy man chose rather to serve the heavenly God than to soldier under an earthly emperor; and he was specially chosen to carry the standard of the holy cross into the western parts of the world, and to exchange the oaths of soldiering for the edicts of the Gospel: not to contend with worldly arms for the Roman kingdom, but by particular teachings to enlarge the Christian empire; nor to subdue fierce peoples by the hard law of the Romans, but to lay the light yoke of Christ upon the neck of very many nations. After the mystery of holy baptism he shone in the world with great miracles, and gleamed far and wide over the earth with his teachings; and these are read as his first distinctions. While he was still a catechumen, in the winter cold, he clothed a poor man with half of his own cloak. And with that same part [0659B] of his cloak he beheld the Lord Jesus clothed, amid the angelic hosts, on the following night, being divinely enlightened; so that he might understand as true what the Lord Jesus himself thunders forth in the pages of the Gospel: *As long as you did it to one of these my least ones, you did it to me [n: (Matth., XXV, 40)]*.

## III.

*As a young man, staying at Milan, he withstood the Arians. He withdraws into Gaul and joins St. Hilary.* --This man passed the flower of his youth in the city of Milan, which at that time the Arian faithlessness, with Auxentius favoring it, greatly stained; and holy Martin, like a strong athlete of Christ, manfully withstood it, and undaunted taught the true faith of the holy Trinity. But from there [ *Al.,* thereafter], driven out by the savagery of the Arian princes, he withdrew into the parts of Gaul, and was joined to the venerable [0659C] bishop of the city of Poitiers, Hilary, who at that time stood forth as praiseworthy to all, like a most firm pillar of the faith: and as [ *Forte,* and just as] Lucifer in the sky shines brighter than the other stars, so that holy man in the Church of Christ blazed forth more excellent than all in greatness of renown, in holiness of life, and in the truth of holy teachings. To this man's discipline holy Martin joined himself, after he had left the belt of soldiering, so that, taught by the examples of so great a man, he might go forth the stronger to the Christian battle, armed with the helmet of faith and girded with the breastplate of righteousness, armed with the sword of the word of God, and fortified as an undaunted warrior against all the darts of the evil one.

## IV.

*Visiting his parents, he converts his mother to the faith. [0659D] And likewise a robber.* --This same holy man, admonished also by a divine oracle, converted his parents to the profession of the sacred faith; and his mother, who bore him for the world, he himself regenerated for Christ. On this journey too he suffered the ambushes of robbers; and while he was handed over to one of them to be kept, by evangelical preaching he converted the robber to Christ: and he to whom [ *Al.,* and he to whom] he had been handed over for punishment became for him unto salvation.

*160* V *He returns to the Gauls. He builds a cell. He raises three dead. He heals a mute girl.* --Returning from there he met a malignant spirit, who pledged himself to be a companion to the holy man wherever he should go; to whom he replied undaunted: The Lord is my [0660A] helper, I will not fear thy threats. But enduring many tribulations from the Arians, he made his way back again to the Gauls, and joined to holy Hilary of Poitiers he built himself a little cell near the city, in which he might more freely gather the fruits of contemplation. A certain catechumen clinging to him was carried off by sudden death, the holy man being absent; and the man of God, with prayers poured out, raised him, and baptized him when he had been restored to life; and he lived many years afterward. Another too, hanged by a noose in the field of a certain Lupicinus, he restored to life by holy prayers. A third, in the town of Chartres, the weeping son of a certain woman, that same holy man raised by prayer alone in the sight of the people. Seeing which miracle, very many of the people believed in Christ. Likewise in the time that followed, in the same town, [0660B] he healed by the anointing of blessed oil a girl who had been mute from infancy.

## VI.

*He is chosen bishop of Tours. He shone forth in all virtues.* --But lest the lamp of the Lord should lie hidden covered by a bushel, he was drawn away from his most sweet dwelling, and bound by the prayers of many, he was chosen bishop of the city of Tours, where he performed very many distinguished works of holy virtues, and scattered abroad many teachings of the heavenly life; yet he did not lose the self-restraint of the private life amid the crowds of the peoples. Humble in dress, cheerful in speech, devoted in preaching, truthful in judgment, venerable in his ways, ever watchful in prayers, constant in reading, steady in countenance, loving in affection, honorable in the priestly ministry, an untiring [0660C] sower of the word of God, tireless in multiplying the money he had received from his Lord; rejoicing with those who rejoice, mourning with those who mourn, made all things to all men, that he might gain all for Christ; distinguished in virtues, praiseworthy in goodness, lovable in devotion, shining in his episcopate with many virtues.

## VII.

*Miracles.* --For a crowd of pagans tumultuously carrying out their rite in attendance upon a certain corpse, he bound in one place by a word of prayer; and loosing them again he allowed them to have control of their own steps. A pine tree falling upon him he turned aside by setting against it the sign of the cross, and made the astonished crowd believe in Christ. He set himself against the flames on the roof of a certain house, lest the fire that burned the shrine of idolatry should harm harmless roofs. Another temple too, dedicated to demons by the ancient error, [0660D] since he could not do it by human hand, he overthrew supported by angelic help. And in another place also, while he was overthrowing the rites of the heathen, when he was attacked with a sword by a certain pagan, he set his bare neck against the striker's blow; but the impious man, falling backward and perceiving his own impiety, begged pardon of the saint; and when another poised a blow at the holy head, the blade escaped from his hands. At Trier he restored to her former health, by the sacred pouring of blessed oil, in the presence of the people, a paralytic girl whose limbs were wholly slackened. He freed the servant of Tetradius, a man of proconsular rank, from a demon by the laying on of his hand. Whence that Tetradius too, with his whole household, was converted to the Christian profession. The boy of a certain [0661A] head of a household, seized by a most savage demon, he compelled — putting his fingers into the mouth — to let the malignant spirit go out through the sufferer's private parts. A leper at the gate of the city of Paris he saved from the deformity of his body by a kiss alone; and it is known that the hems of his garment too, and the threads of his hairshirt, healed many diseases. The daughter of Arborius the prefect was freed from most grievous fevers by the laying on of a letter of holy Martin. He healed Paulinus's eye from pain and dimness by laying a sponge upon it.

## VIII.

*He is visited by angels, by the Blessed Virgin, and by other saints. He was renowned for the spirit of prophecy.* --This same holy Martin, falling down the steps and gravely bruised in almost all his limbs, was in the night restored by an angel to entire health: and very often he enjoyed angelic visitations [0661B] and familiar conversation. And on a certain day the man of God was honored and strengthened by a visitation of the most blessed Mother of God and of our Lord Jesus Christ, and of the holy apostles Peter and Paul, and also of the holy virgins Thecla and Agnes. For, fearing nothing of the phantasms of demons and their horrible appearances in various shapes, he could not be mocked by any of their deceits. And in the spirit of the prophets he so shone that he foretold many things to come to many people *161* — as, for instance, he foretold to the emperor Maximus victory in Italy and destruction at Aquileia. And while that same blessed man was celebrating the holy mystery at the altar, a globe of fire suddenly gleamed from the crown of his head. A certain Evantius, being sick, received health before holy Martin entered the house, while the blessed man was hastening to him. In [0661C] that same house too he snatched from the peril of death, by the touch of his holy fingers, a boy struck by a serpent.
