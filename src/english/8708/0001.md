## IX.

*The holy man's patience. He drives out demons,* etc.--For the man of God was of such patience that he did not grieve at insults, nor feel reproaches: nay, he even bore the lashes of certain country folk with a calm mind, and by a word alone he loosed from the ground their beasts that had been fixed to the earth. He flattered no one by fawning; to no prince did he keep silent the words of truth, always running back to the familiar helps of prayer. Whence, if worldly power refused him anything, the divine mercy soon granted it. A certain girl seized by a demoniac spirit was snatched from the enemy's power by the straw where holy Martin was sitting. Freeing not only men but also animals from the power of demons, he commanded them to return, tame, to their own flock. [0661D] He was also of such compassion that he ordered the dogs pursuing a little hare to stand still; and he snatched the wretched little creature from present death. A certain estate in the Sens country, laid waste rather often by hailstorm, was at the prayers of holy Martin set free for many years. A woman was healed of an issue of blood by the touch of his garments.

## X.

*He died.* --With such miracles too the chosen priest of God shone before the world; and, perfect in his whole life and manner of living, [0662A] most illustrious in all devotion and religion, delightful in all goodness and kindness, in the eighty-first year of his age and the twenty-sixth of his episcopate, ripe in years and in merits, he was called to the heavenly kingdoms; so that, along with the manifold wage of his labor, he might receive the everlasting crown from Christ the Lord. But knowing well the day of his death long beforehand, and foretelling it to certain of his brethren, and when the hour drew near, touched by the pain of fevers, joyful in the praise of God, amid the divine words of preaching, amid the angelic voices of heavenly hymns, with his disciples standing by, he rendered his holy soul to God and Christ.

## XI.

*He is buried at Tours.* --His holy body was carried by the clergy of the cities, and by crowds of the peoples, with praises and hymns, to the city of Tours, and there [0662B] he was buried in the public burying-ground, where afterward the blessed bishop Perpetuus built, with praiseworthy work and venerable devotion, a church worthy of the merits of so great a Father: in which even to this day many signs of miracles, very many healing powers, consolations of the sorrowing, and mercies for the glad, are wont to be done, our Lord Jesus Christ granting it, who lives and reigns with God the Father [ and the Holy Spirit in the unity of the divine majesty, through all ages].

## *A SERMON OF THE SAME ALCUIN* ON THE PASSING OF SAINT MARTIN.



## XII.

*Saint Martin's holiness, miracles, and gift of prophecy.* --It behooves us all with one mind to rejoice, dearest brethren, and faithfully to venerate the illustrious day of the death of holy [0662C] Martin; but chiefly those faithful of Christ who are in the western parts of the world, who have been enlightened alike by his teachings and by his examples: because whatever he preached in words he showed forth in works. For in his whole life and in his whole manner of living he was perfect, most illustrious in all devotion and religion, delightful in all goodness and kindness, praiseworthy to all, lovable to all; a virgin among the choirs of virgins, an eminent man among priests, an imitator of the apostles, and a preacher of Christ. Whom the Lord deigned to choose in apostolic grace, as it is written of him: « This is Martin, the chosen bishop of God, upon whom the Lord after the apostles deigned to confer such grace that, in the power of the holy Trinity [0662D] which makes gods, he should be worthy to become the magnificent raiser of three dead men. » After the example of the Lord he raised three dead: and by the spirit of prophecy he foreknew many things also before they came to pass. For he was full of the spirit of prophecy, and therefore by divine inspiration he foreknew his own death, and revealed to certain of the brethren that the dissolution of his body had drawn near. *162*

## XIII.

*He restores peace among clerics at odds. He commands the birds.* -- Now at that time a cause had come upon him for which he found it necessary to go to the village of Candes in his own diocese, [0663A] to restore peace among the clerics of that Church who were at odds with one another. And although he was not ignorant that the end of his days was at hand, yet he did not refuse to set out for a cause of this kind, recalling that word of the Gospel: *Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God:* and wishing to leave peace to all, after the example of the Lord saying to the apostles on the day of his ascension: *Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you. In peace I have sent you away, in peace I shall find you.* And when for this cause he was making the journey with a familiar company of disciples, he saw divers in the river catching a multitude of fish, and said: This is the shape of demons. Then with the mighty power of his words he commanded them to leave that pool and to make for dry and deserted places. And at once, in a wonderful manner, all of them gathered into one flock, [0663B] left the river and made for the mountains and the woods. And those who were present together marveled that such power was in Martin, that by the grace of God he commanded even birds, or demons.

## XIV.

*He falls sick: he refuses to have a softer bed spread for him.* --And when, having stayed a few days in that village to which he had gone, peace having been restored among the clerics, he was already thinking of returning to the monastery, he began suddenly to be forsaken by the strength of his body; and calling together his disciples, he showed them that the time was now at hand for his spirit to be released from the body. Then indeed there was grief and sorrow of all, one voice of those lamenting and saying: Why, Father, dost thou forsake us, or to whom dost thou leave us desolate? For ravening wolves will fall upon thy flock. And who, when thou art gone, will defend us from their ravenings? We know [0663C] indeed that thou desirest Christ, but thy rewards are kept safe for thee, nor will they be lessened by being deferred; have mercy rather upon us. Then he, moved by their weeping, is reported to have wept; and turning to the Lord he said in a suppliant voice: Lord, if I am still needful to thy people, I do not refuse the labor; thy will be done. Doubtless he did not know which he would rather: because he wished neither to forsake these, nor to be separated longer from Christ. For putting nothing in his own choosing, he left all these things to God's decision, saying: Lord, if thou still willest me to remain in the body, I do not refuse the labor: and if thou sparest my age, it is good for me: thy will be done. But do thou thyself guard these for whom I fear. O man beyond words, neither conquered by labor [0664A] nor to be conquered by death; who neither feared to die nor refused to live. But when he was asked by his disciples to have straw put beneath him, as he labored for some days under the force of the fevers, It is not fitting, he said, my sons, it is not fitting that a Christian should die save in ashes and haircloth; for if I have left you any other example, I myself have sinned. And when again he was asked by the priests who had come to his sickness to relieve his poor body by a change of side, Suffer me, he said, suffer me, brethren, to look upon heaven rather than upon earth, that my spirit, now about to go to the Lord, may be set straight upon its journey.

## XV.

*Amid angelic hymns he yields up his soul.* --In the eighty-first year of his age, therefore, and the twenty-sixth of his episcopate, ripe in years and in character, at the village of Candes in his own diocese, as we have said, he happily departed from the world. [0664B] But amid the divine words of holy preaching, with the angelic voices of heavenly hymns, his disciples standing about him, full of grace and holiness, he handed over his holy soul to Jesus Christ. For many at his passing heard the voices of angels praising holy Martin. And how great a multitude came together for his funeral rites cannot be told. For many came with tears from the neighboring cities and monasteries and villages, mourning holy Martin with one voice. O how great was the grief of all! how great above all the laments of the monks and the virgins, saying that it is a holy thing to rejoice for Martin, and a holy thing to weep for Martin. For it was a holy thing that he had lived, and a holy thing that he died; because in his life he had pleased God.

## XVI.

*He is buried at Tours.* -- His body indeed [0664C] was carried by the clergy of the cities, and by crowds of the peoples, with praises and hymns, to the city of Tours, and there was buried with great veneration, a heavenly throng following him with heavenly hymns as far as the place of the tomb. In which place afterward the blessed bishop Perpetuus built a new church with praiseworthy work, worthy of the merits of so great a patron and such a man. In which even to this day many signs of miracles, very many healing powers, consolations of the sorrowing, and mercies for the glad, are wont to be done, our Lord Jesus Christ granting it: who lives and reigns in the unity of majesty, through all ages of ages. Amen.
