## V. VERSES OF ALCUIN

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *These verses are to be written at the front of the codex, where the image of Christ and the images of the four evangelists and of the four prophets are contained.* [1165D]In fourfold order, painted with varied figures,
⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1166A]the hosts of the saints behold great joys.
⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Among them Isaiah, relying on his most lofty teaching,
⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ and Jeremiah alike, chant the miracles of the Lord;
⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Ezekiel describes both the throne of God and these things,
⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ and Daniel tells of the Christ cut from the mountain.
⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [ Matthew describes the human birth of Christ;
⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Mark roars in manner with the voice of a gnashing lion.
⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Luke lows with devout love in the song of Christ.
⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ By writing, John, you pierce heaven with your mind].


⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *These on the other page, where the lamb is painted, and the* XXIIII *elders, and the earth and the sea.* ⟦s2⟧ All that the present earth brings forth to give nourishment,
⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ and this face of the sea surrounds it with an ample border.
⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ O Lamb, they venerate God forever on his lofty throne —
⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ you who by the shedding of your blood wiped away the crimes of the age
⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1166B] *457* upon the cross: may you wipe away the wounds of King Charles.
⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [ The hoary company of the prophets, renowned, and the venerable order,
⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ the apostolic assembly, pressing on with heavenly garlands,
⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ praises, adores, loves, and fears him with a devoted heart.
⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And prince Charles gazes upon him with uncovered face,
⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ praying that he may live with you, long-lived, unto eternity].


⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In another place, where the lamb alone is painted.* ⟦s2⟧ This Lamb Moses showed in the law as one to come,
⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ to bear the wounds of death on behalf of all peoples.


⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Where the right hand of Christ is painted.* ⟦s2⟧ The right hand of the Father, which governs the world by its rule,
⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ and has carried its own Son across into the high heavens.


⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *At the end of John the evangelist.* ⟦s2⟧ And prince Charles, who in holy manner is kindly —
⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1166C]by his help this book here shines forth in its doings.


⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *At the end of the book the scribes speak.* ⟦s2⟧ Thus far, furrowing, we have moved over the holy sea;
⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ our keel has come to the end of the shore.


## VI. TO CANDIDUS SETTING OUT FOR ROME.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ My mind follows you, dearest Candidus, in sorrow;
⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ may your mind, I pray, carry me with it everywhere.
⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Through all the places of God, may the grace of Christ
⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ accompany you as you go, sweet son, from the citadel of heaven.
⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And may it grant you, over lands and woods and rivers and mountains,
⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ a straight journey on a fortunate path,
⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ so that the longed-for day may see you at last in the towers of that
⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ excellent City, in which are the holy thresholds of Peter,
⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ who may bear your prayers to Christ above the steep
⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1166D]summits of heaven — he, the first Father in the world, in his mercy —
⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1167A]prayers which may flow from the mouth of your heart, with tears,
⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ your body prostrate on the ground before the altar of God.
⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Thence, through those airy towers of walls that must one day fall,
⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ coming to the consecrated roof of devout Paul,
⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ stretched out in the sign of the cross, with groaning breast,
⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ there lament the offenses of father and of son together.
⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And straightway, a devout traveler, hasten through the churches
⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1⟧ of the saints, praising God always with your mouth,
⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ who has granted the world to have such patrons,
⟦p48⟧ ⟦s1⟧ standing before the throne of the Father in the citadel of heaven —
⟦p49⟧ ⟦s1⟧ who by their rosy blood conquered kingdoms throughout the earth,
⟦p50⟧ ⟦s1⟧ bearing the cross of Christ upon their devout necks.
⟦p51⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Beg them again and again with a devoted heart
⟦p52⟧ ⟦s1⟧ mercifully to be present always to your prayers.
⟦p53⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Before their devout ashes let the tokens of your tears
⟦p54⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1167B]stand always and everywhere upon the sacred stones.
⟦p55⟧ ⟦s1⟧ You too, throughout the city, with an eager mind, I pray
⟦p56⟧ ⟦s1⟧ to be present among the sacred orders and the feasts.
⟦p57⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Observe what befits each person and each season,
⟦p58⟧ ⟦s1⟧ so that, remembering, you may report it all to the Father,
⟦p59⟧ ⟦s1⟧ and may profit your own people by your counsels and kindly ways,
⟦p60⟧ ⟦s1⟧ so that through you the holy Church of God may flourish.
⟦p61⟧ ⟦s1⟧ If the grace of merciful Christ should go before you
⟦p62⟧ ⟦s1⟧ into the sacred sight of the apostolic Father,
⟦p63⟧ ⟦s1⟧ ask for the ashes of the Fathers, their garments, or perhaps the hair
⟦p64⟧ ⟦s1⟧ of the saints, or the great joys of the holy cross,
⟦p65⟧ ⟦s1⟧ that it may be a support to our life through the ages,
⟦p66⟧ ⟦s1⟧ and to our own likewise, always and everywhere devout.
⟦p67⟧ ⟦s1⟧ When all this has been duly accomplished, Christ having mercy,
⟦p68⟧ ⟦s1⟧ return prosperously to the Father; go quickly.
⟦p69⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1167C]Let Phoebus no longer see you lingering in the Hall,
⟦p70⟧ ⟦s1⟧ but rise with the Morning Star, exulting traveler.
⟦p71⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Let no flame-flying sun, no wind, no frosts or storm-clouds
⟦p72⟧ ⟦s1⟧ now delay the journey of one hastening to the Father.
⟦p73⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Love conquers all things: swifter of foot than the East Wind,
⟦p74⟧ ⟦s1⟧ go in haste, run, fly, hurry on,
⟦p75⟧ ⟦s1⟧ so that Jacob may joyfully see his Joseph with his own eyes
⟦p76⟧ ⟦s1⟧ before the day of dread death, in his own city.
⟦p77⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Why should I sing you more? ⟦s1⟧ Happy, and happily, go now
⟦p78⟧ ⟦s1⟧ along all your ways, my son, God having mercy.
⟦p79⟧ ⟦s1⟧ May the Creator of the world grant you all prosperity.
⟦p80⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Sweet and beloved son, fare well to your Father.


## VII. PRAYERS OF ALBINUS TO GOD AND TO THE APOSTLES.

⟦p81⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1167D] We entreat the mightiest supports of the world's Author,
⟦p82⟧ ⟦s1⟧ who, though he was in glory, took flesh from Mary;
⟦p83⟧ ⟦s1⟧ who, coming down from heaven, the living giver of life,
⟦p84⟧ ⟦s1⟧ having suffered the cross for all, gave gifts to men,
⟦p85⟧ ⟦s1⟧ the most bountiful lawgiver, God and the Son of God;
⟦p86⟧ ⟦s1⟧ who, after his holy triumphs were accomplished, ascending as victor,
⟦p87⟧ ⟦s1⟧ sent twice two and twice five shining Apostles into the world,
⟦p88⟧ ⟦s1⟧ whose measureless glory will shine again through the ages.
⟦p89⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Blessed first of all, the heir, the vicar of Christ,
⟦p90⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Saint Peter the apostle, foremost of the holy flock.
⟦p91⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1168A]Bar-Jona, lofty column, climbing the heights of heaven,
⟦p92⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Peter from the solid rock, following the footsteps of Christ.
⟦p93⟧ ⟦s1⟧ With pure and perfected labor, without deadly grief.
⟦p94⟧ ⟦s1⟧ He rests at Rome in honor, having suffered the cross under Nero.
⟦p95⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The shining miracles of his powers blazed forth.
⟦p96⟧ ⟦s1⟧ To all the nations he shone out, an excellent preacher;
⟦p97⟧ ⟦s1⟧ he shone upon those who did not see, bright as the flash of the sun,
⟦p98⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Saint Paul the apostle, chosen not by men,
⟦p99⟧ ⟦s1⟧ filled with the honorable fruits of the Holy Spirit.
⟦p100⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Caught up to the third heaven, held worthy as teacher of the nations,
⟦p101⟧ ⟦s1⟧ strengthener of the feeble, consoler of those who mourn.
⟦p102⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Proved by manifold fire . . . . . in triumph.
⟦p103⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Whose unutterable wisdom the ages sound aloud.
⟦p104⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Worthy and exceedingly resplendent, a teacher, he taught the nations,
⟦p105⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Saint Andrew, most excellent, apostle of Christ the King.
⟦p106⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1168B]Instrument of the Holy Spirit, adorned with the gifts of grace.
⟦p107⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Brother of Peter the apostle, proclaimer of the law of the Lord.
⟦p108⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Lordly in mind and in countenance, a glory in his manly stature:
⟦p109⟧ ⟦s1⟧ preaching in Achaia, having suffered the cross under Aegeas.
⟦p110⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Now he reigns in glory, an Elijah fit for the kingdom.
⟦p111⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Whom God, out of his labors, has restored to the flowers of life.
⟦p112⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *458* Chosen upon the summit of apostolic honor,
⟦p113⟧ ⟦s1⟧ he shone with the clear light of evangelical teaching:
⟦p114⟧ ⟦s1⟧ James, the mounter of Christ's most exalted glory,
⟦p115⟧ ⟦s1⟧ the consummator of the highest King's excellent victory,
⟦p116⟧ ⟦s1⟧ brother of John the Holy, and of most illustrious memory.
⟦p117⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Beheaded by the sword, crowned with martyrdom


## VIII. EPITAPH OF SAINT RICHARIUS THE ABBOT, OR METRICAL VERSES CARVED IN GOLDEN LETTERS, BY ORDER OF CHARLEMAGNE, ON THAT SAME GOLDEN CASKET IN WHICH THE BODY OF SAINT RICHARIUS WAS LAID.

⟦p118⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1168C]

⟦p119⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *At the front of the tomb.* ⟦s2⟧ A golden urn covers a heavenly treasure,
⟦p120⟧ ⟦s1⟧ a worshiper of the Lord, Richarius by name,
⟦p121⟧ ⟦s1⟧ whom this Centula brought forth of most lofty lineage,
⟦p122⟧ ⟦s1⟧ and who flourished as the excellent pastor of the place.


⟦p123⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *On the right side.* ⟦s2⟧ Having set aside the world, in which he shone with great honor,
⟦p124⟧ ⟦s1⟧ he spurned ample riches for the love of God.
⟦p125⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Here, breaking his own body by hard contest,
⟦p126⟧ ⟦s1⟧ a man devout and great, he is famed forever in the world.


⟦p127⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *On the top of the chest, above.* ⟦s2⟧ Here he restored life to the dead, sight to the blind,
⟦p128⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1168D]and by his fostering care health returns to lepers.
⟦p129⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Full of apostolic powers and apostolic words,
⟦p130⟧ ⟦s1⟧ he held the heavenly banquets always in his mouth.


⟦p131⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *On the left side.* ⟦s2⟧ For him prince Charles, with kindly mind,
⟦p132⟧ ⟦s1⟧ completing a worthy temple, founded it, and a tomb,
⟦p133⟧ ⟦s1⟧ after about a hundred and sixty years,
⟦p134⟧ ⟦s1⟧ while the Lord's servant stands whole even yet.


⟦p135⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *At the front of the feet.* ⟦s2⟧ That by his merits he may take the heavenly kingdoms,
⟦p136⟧ ⟦s1⟧ and may rule the kingdoms of the Franks quiet in peace.
