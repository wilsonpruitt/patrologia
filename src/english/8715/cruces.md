# Cruces — 8715, ps.-Alcuin, *Dubia alia* (PL 101)

Garbled or corrupt printed readings, rendered literally in the English and logged here.

## Standing decisions

- **This is NOT a question-and-answer / doubts collection.** Despite the title *Dubia
  alia* ("further doubtful pieces"), the work is Migne's dossier of nine miscellaneous
  items of **doubtful attribution** to Alcuin — a letter, six poems, a rhythmic prayer,
  and one short prose exposition. There is therefore no *Interrogatio / Responsio*
  house structure to lock. The one recurring structural feature is the **italic rubric**
  introducing a set of inscription verses (*In fronte sepulcri.*, *In dextero latere.*,
  *Ubi dextera Christi est picta.*); these are rendered as translated italic rubrics,
  kept on the same line as the verse that follows, exactly as Migne prints them.
- **Verse is set line for line**, one English line per Latin line, so the column anchors
  that Migne prints flush against a verse line (`[1166A]the hosts of the saints…`) stay
  attached to the same line they mark.
- **Square-bracketed verse blocks** (items V.1 and V.2) are Migne's own bracketing of
  lines whose authenticity he doubts. The brackets and their spacing are reproduced.
- Migne's variant brackets — `[ *Forte,* …]`, `[ *F.,* …]`, `[ *Cod. Sal.,* …]`,
  `[ *Ed.,* …]`, `[ *Edit.,* …; *Mabill.,* …]`, and bare corrections like `[praeteritorum]`,
  `[habemus]`, `[charta]`, `[bonitate sueta]` — pass through verbatim, untranslated,
  placed against the English word they correct.
- The bold arabic numerals *449*, *450*, *456*, *457*, *458* are Migne's volume
  numeration, kept italic and in place.

## Chunk 0

- **1161A, Letter to Leo III** — The whole letter is in rustic Latin whose syntax breaks
  down repeatedly. Two places especially: the opening address has no finite verb until
  *deposcimus*, so the singular *ille vilissimus … servus* is left grammatically stranded
  (rendered with a dash, preserving the anacoluthon); and *quasi coram sanctissimis
  pedibus vestris humano* is unconstruable, Migne suggesting *[ Forte, humi prostratus]*.
  Rendered on the plain sense of the printed *humano* being an error, without importing
  Migne's conjecture into the English.
- **1161B** — *pia oscula ejus ostias salutare*, with Migne's *[ F., pio osculo . . .
  ostia]*. As printed, "to greet his doors with devout kisses" requires reading *ostia*
  for *ostias*; rendered on Migne's suggestion, his bracket kept in place.
- **1161B** — *huc ita, ut dispositum die pro consule habuimus.* Corrupt beyond repair:
  *ita* is stranded and *die pro consule* has no clear sense (possibly a garbled
  *die proposito*). Rendered on the least violent reading ("on the day on which we had
  resolved to attend"), and flagged here as a genuine crux a human should check against
  another witness.
- **1161B** — *veniam delictorum [praeteritorum] indulgentiam rogare.* The doubled
  *veniam … indulgentiam* is either a variant pair or a broken construction; both nouns
  are rendered.
- **1162B, poem II, line 9** — *In traject. mel, compultimque buturque ministrat.* Migne
  prints the abbreviation *In traject.* unresolved (Trajectum = Utrecht); the abbreviation
  is kept as printed rather than silently expanded. *compultim* (for *pultem*) and *butur*
  (for *butyrum*) are rendered on their obvious sense, "porridge" and "butter."
- **1163A ff., poem II** — Personal names are kept in the form Migne prints them where
  they have no settled English form (Hrotberct, Raefgot, Rado, Bassinus, Fulcrad,
  Dorsdrada); where a standard English form exists it is used (Albric, Willibrord,
  Ricwulf, Lull, Cologne, Mainz, Speyer, Moselle).
- **1163A, line 22** — *Remigio postquam spatium sulcaveris amnem.* *spatium* and *amnem*
  cannot both be direct objects; rendered "have furrowed the river's stretch with rowing."
- **1164A, line 61** — *sine tempora serva* with Migne's *[ F., sacra]*. Rendered on
  *sacra* ("consecrated brows"), the bracket kept.
- **1164A, line 64** — *Eja, age, casta [charta] cito navem conscende.* *casta* is a
  misprint for *charta*, which Migne supplies; the addressee of the whole poem is the
  letter/paper itself. Rendered on the corrected reading.
- **1164B, line 77** — *Et cum vere rubrae . . . . . de cortice gemmae.* Migne prints a
  lacuna of five points; the missing word is a verb or adjective. The lacuna is reproduced
  as printed.
- **1165A, poem IV, line 6** — *Quas medici quaerit dextra salutis ope*, with Migne's
  *[ Ed., ore]*. Rendered on the printed *ope*, the variant kept.

## Chunk 1

- **1166A, poem V** — *Ordine quadrato variis depicta figuris / Agmina sanctorum gaudia
  magna vident.* *depicta* agrees with *agmina*, but the sense wants the pictures on the
  page, not the hosts. Rendered as printed.
- **1166B** — *Agne Deum solio semper venerantur in alto.* A vocative *Agne* stands
  inside a sentence whose subject is unexpressed ("they venerate God"). Rendered
  literally with the vocative preserved; the incoherence is in the printed text.
- **1166C** — *Illius hic codex enitet actus ope.* *actus* has no clean construction;
  rendered "this book here shines forth in its doings," by his help.
- **1167D–1168B, poem VII** — This rhythmic, internally rhymed prayer is the most
  degraded item in the work: it consists largely of nominative-phrase apposition with
  almost no finite verbs, and it **breaks off unfinished** at *Decapitatus gladio,
  coronatus martyrio* with no closing punctuation. The English preserves the appositional
  style, the absence of finite verbs, and the abrupt ending. Individually noted:
  - *Binos bisquinos … Apostolos* — "twice two and twice five," i.e. the Twelve counted
    oddly (2 + 10). Rendered literally rather than as "twelve."
  - *Igne probatus vario . . . . . triumpho* — Migne prints a lacuna; reproduced.
  - *Refulsit non videntibus clarus ceu fulgor Phoebus* — *Phoebus* is nominative where
    a genitive or ablative is wanted; rendered "bright as the flash of the sun."
  - *Modo regnat in gloria, regni habilis Helia* — *Helia* is unmotivated (Elijah?) and
    the line may be corrupt; rendered literally.
  - *Frater Joannis Agiae* — *Agiae* is the Greek ἅγιος latinized and misdeclined;
    rendered "John the Holy."
- **1168C, item VIII** — *Stemmate praecelso quem Centula protulit ista.* Centula is the
  monastery of Saint-Riquier; kept in the Latin form as a place-name.
- **1168D** — *Post sexagenos et centum circiter annos / Cum Domini famulus integer
  exstat adhuc.* The *cum* clause hangs without a governing verb; rendered "while the
  Lord's servant stands whole even yet," which is the plain sense of the incorrupt-body
  claim the epitaph is making.

## Chunk 2

- **1169B, item IX** — *et Dominus momordit infernum.* Literally "and the Lord bit hell";
  the image is Hosea 13:14 (*morsus tuus ero, inferne*). Rendered literally ("bit into
  hell") rather than smoothed to "despoiled."
- **1169B** — *Exsurge, Domine, tu et arca sanctificationis tuae* is cited as *Psal.
  CXXXI, 8* (Vulgate numbering); left exactly as Migne cites it.
- **1170B** — *Absit, ut mittam manum meum in unctum Domini.* *manum meum* for *manum
  meam*; rendered on the intended reading. Note also that Migne's *mittam manum in* is
  translated "put my hand upon," matching what the Latin prints.
- **1170B** — *quando Saul in speluncam introivit ventrem purgare.* The episode is
  I Samuel (I Reg.) 24, not 26 as Migne's note gives; the citation is reproduced exactly
  as printed and NOT corrected — flagging it here for the citation-corrections pass.
- **1170B** — *Dele me de libro viventium, ubi me scripsisti.* The trailing *ubi me
  scripsisti* falls outside the italic quotation in Migne, so it is treated as the
  author's gloss and left outside the italics in the English as well.
