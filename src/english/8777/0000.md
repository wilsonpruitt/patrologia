## I.

Because I have learned that among certain persons there are various rumors and suspicions, I have written what is set down below, so that through it you may deign to know what I judge ought to be held to in the exercise of ecclesiastical governance, and so that the murmuring of a few may cease, once my intention has been laid open [0189B] to all. There is one bridegroom, who has a bride; who is to be loved for his own sake, not for the sake of anything else, since he himself is peace, truth, justice, sanctification, and redemption, and every good, and without him no good; who came from heaven, and is above all; who lifts to heaven those whom he snatches up from the earth. The friends of the bridegroom are those who, by seeing and hearing him, are built up into the love and worship of the bridegroom; but the sons of the bridegroom are both those who build up and those who are built up. There is one Lamb, who has a bride, of whom it is said to John the apostle: *Come, I will show thee the bride, the wife of the Lamb;* and there was shown to him the holy city Jerusalem coming down from heaven from God, having the brightness of God.

## II.

Of this bride and city there is sung in heaven *Alleluia, for the Lord our God almighty hath reigned. [0189C] Let us rejoice and exult, and give glory to him, for the marriage of the Lamb is come, and his wife hath prepared herself; and it was granted to her that she should cover herself with fine linen.* Of this wife and city John the evangelist says that she is also the tabernacle of God. For he says: *And I saw the holy city Jerusalem, new, coming down from heaven, prepared by God, as a bride adorned for her husband. And I heard a great voice from the throne saying: Behold the tabernacle of God with men, and he will dwell with them. And they shall be his people, and he, God, shall be with them, their God; and God shall wipe away every tear from their eyes; and death shall be no more, nor mourning, nor crying, nor shall there be sorrow any more.* Of this city and virgin wife the Lamb of God — the Lamb who is God, the almighty Lamb, the Lamb of the Almighty — begets lambs [0189D] and sheep, which he entrusts to the blessed apostle Peter, saying: *Feed my lambs.* And again: *Feed my sheep.* This bridegroom and almighty Lamb says to his bride and wife: *Thy hair is as flocks of goats which have come up from mount Gilead. Thy teeth as a flock of sheep which have come up from the washing, all with twin young, and there is not a barren one among them.* And again: *Thy two breasts are like two twin fawns of a roe, which feed among the lilies.* This wife is also the daughter of her husband, the Lamb and prince, to whom he himself says: *How beautiful are thy steps in sandals, O daughter of the prince!*

## III.

That same prince is the father of his bride, and her brother. For he says to his own wife and sister: *Thou hast wounded my heart, my sister, my bride, thou hast wounded my heart with one of thine eyes, and with one hair of thy neck. How beautiful are thy breasts, my sister, my bride!* Nor is it any wonder [0190B] if the wife is a sister, since her breasts and paps are a tower, and she herself a wall. For thus the father and bridegroom speaks of her: *What shall we do for our sister on the day when she is to be spoken to? If she is a wall, let us build upon it silver bulwarks; if she is a door, let us join it together with boards of cedar.* And she answered: *I am a wall, and my breasts are a tower, since I was made before him as one finding peace.* What wonder if she is a wall and a city, when she is also a vineyard having a husband? *For there was a vineyard,* he says, *belonging to the Peaceable One in that place which has peoples. He handed it over to keepers: the husband brings for its fruit a thousand pieces of silver.* Concerning which that same husband and father at once adds, *My vineyard is before me.* That vineyard is also the virgin's and the spouse, of whom it is sung in the wedding song to the king and bridegroom himself: *The spouse stood at [0190C] thy right hand, in a golden diadem.* And that same bridegroom and father says to the same spouse: *Hear, O daughter, and see.* And the singer of this song says to her: *And, O daughter of the mightiest, the rich among the peoples shall entreat thy countenance with gifts.* These readings follow the Hebrew. But the older translation, in order to show the same spouse to be both queen and the king's daughter, speaks thus to the king himself: *The queen stood at thy right hand.* And a little after: *All the glory of the king's daughter is from within.* This queen is not only a vineyard, but also a garden, and a fountain. *A garden enclosed is my sister, my bride, a garden enclosed, a fountain sealed.* She is called a woman also, as it is written: *Who shall find a valiant woman?* And after some words: *Her husband is noble in the gates, when he shall sit with the senators of the land.* In her person Paul too writes to certain of the faithful: *For I have espoused you [0190D] to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ.* To whom Christ the Lord himself speaks: *Thou art all fair, my love, and there is no spot in thee.* And she of him: *Let my beloved come into his garden, and eat the fruit of his apple trees.*

## IV.

This bride is queen, and wife, and handmaid. But this is no wonder. Yet it is a wonder, and full of all mercy, that her husband and lord is also mistress to her, as the Psalmist sings: *As the eyes of the handmaid are upon the hand of her mistress, so are our eyes unto the Lord our God.* She is also called the woman who has ten drachmas, who, when one was lost, lit [0191A] a lamp, swept the house, sought, and found. She is called a mother too, as he himself speaks through Isaiah, saying: *As if a mother should caress someone, so will I comfort you.* For truly she is both mother and mistress, virtue and wisdom, strength and glory, resurrection and life, way and truth, illumination and salvation, and the rest of this kind.

## V.

This too is no less a wonder: that the handmaid and friend can even be the mother of her Lord, in that manner in which the Lord says: *For whosoever shall do the will of my Father who is in heaven, he is my brother and sister and mother.* Which the Apostle also says of himself: *My little children, of whom I am again in travail until Christ be formed in you.*

## VI.

These manifold names of the bridegroom and the bride, [0191B] of Christ and the Church, have not been taken from any historical deeds (as it is when Scripture speaks of Sarah, Rebecca, Rachel, in whom the joining of Christ and the Church is figured), nor from that kind of speech in which things perceptible to the senses are set down by way of significative foretelling (as is the mountain of the house of the Lord, where the mountain signifies the Lord and the house the Church) and many things of this sort; but from those places where Scripture carries through that ordered narration of deeds, yet indicates Christ and the Church only by various names of this kind, and in which no one is so foolish as to think that anything else is to be sought except Christ and the Church.

## VII.

But the wondrous kindness of the Creator is shown, since with so great a unity the body cleaves to the head, [0191C] and the head presides over the body, as the Prophet sings: *He hath clothed me with the garments of salvation, and hath compassed me about with the robe of justice, as a bridegroom decked with a crown, and as a bride adorned with her jewels.* And after some words: *For the young man shall dwell with the virgin, [0192A] and thy sons shall dwell in thee. And the bridegroom shall rejoice over the bride, and thy God shall rejoice over thee.* And the Apostle plainly shows it, saying: *For as the body is one, and hath many members, and all the members of the body, though they are many, are one body, so also is Christ. For in one Spirit we have all been baptized into one body, whether Jews or Gentiles, whether slaves or free, and we have all been given to drink in one Spirit.* Because, clearly, it is the Spirit of Christ that makes this unity.
