## VIII.

These are the things which furnish an unspeakably delightful diversion to a devout and faithful soul — and such a diversion as, in the present life, can be had from no contemplation of created things; and, if I may say it confidently, among all the works of God, visible [0192B] and invisible, no like of it can be found. Of this diversion the Lord gives command through the Prophet: *Speak to the heart of Israel, and call her away.* Of this too the man with whom God is, in the vision in which his mother taught him, thus gives command in mystical speech: *Give strong drink to those that mourn, and wine to those that are bitter of soul. Let them drink, and forget their want, and remember their sorrow no more.* Of this too the Psalmist, when he called blessed the man who has not looked upon vanity and false madnesses — or, as it is read in the Hebrew, upon the prides and pomps of lying, in which lie the diversions of carnal minds — added: *Thou, O Lord my God, hast done many things, thy wonders; and in thy thoughts there is none like unto thee.*

## IX.

Therefore whoever has undertaken the office of preaching and the place of governance, and neglects, in these highest matters, what is the highest thing of God's mercy — to call away the friends of the faithful — and seeks his consolation and diversion in riches, in ornaments of metal, [0193A] in hunting, in fowling and fishing, and, what is worse, in feastings and drunkenness and the sounds of music, as the Lord detests it through the prophet, saying: *Woe to you that rise up early to pursue drunkenness, and to drink until evening, that you may be inflamed with wine. The harp and the lyre and the timbrel and the pipe and wine are in your banquets; and you regard not the work of the Lord, nor consider the works of his hands* — such a man is no helper of God, as Paul says: *For we are God's helpers. You are God's tillage, you are God's building.* Rather he is a destroyer of God's work, and thereby a helper of Antichrist; [0194A] and although in human eyes he may seem to be a priest, in God's eyes he is not: because when the Lord comes to judgment, he will be cut apart, and his portion will be set with the unbelievers, and of such men it is written: *I saw the ungodly buried: who also, while they were still living, were in the holy place, and were praised in the city as though for works of justice.* Therefore none of these ought to be a leader of God's people, lest he plunge those seeking the heavenly kingdoms into the pit into which he himself has been plunged by God's judgment, as the Lord shows to blessed Job, saying: *Hast thou an arm like God?* And, after some intervening words, *Hide them,* he says — that is, the proud [0195A] and the ungodly — *in the dust together, and plunge their faces into the pit.*

## X.

There are others who, like these, do not appear ungodly to men, but by a feigned righteousness become in men's eyes whited sepulchres; while in God's eyes, that is, within, they are full of dead men's bones and of every filth. The blessed apostle Paul says: *For we are not as very many, adulterating the word of God, but out of sincerity, as from God, before God, we speak in Christ.* And again: *Having this ministration, according as we have obtained mercy, we do not fail, but we renounce the hidden things of dishonor, not walking in craftiness, nor adulterating the word of God, but in the manifestation of the truth.* For just as an adulterer does not seek the fruit of offspring, but only [0195B] to do his own will and fulfil his lust, so everyone who has undertaken the administration of the word, if out of sincerity he does not seek the salvation of his hearers and the building up of the Church, but speaks the word of God for the sake of some other thing which is less than the salvation of his hearers — namely, that he may comply with his own will — is an adulterer.

## XI.

Therefore it is not heretics alone who adulterate the word of God by mixing in their deceits. But so also do all who hasten to arrive at temporal honors and riches through the sacred ministry; and likewise those who cannot be raised to honors and riches, and desire to obtain their daily food or clothing from the sacred ministry (since they do not receive their keep in order to preach, but preach in order to receive; [0195C] nor do they speak out of sincerity, as from God, before God; but they walk in craftiness, nor do they renounce the hidden things of dishonor, but embrace them) — these are adulterers. Therefore everyone who is set over the rest, whether cleric or monk, if he shows himself benevolent and gentle and affable in such a way as to turn the hearts of his subjects to love of himself and to his own praise, is an adulterer, and ought never to undertake the governance of souls. But he who is prudent, gentle, and agreeable in such a way that he turns and kindles those who see and hear him to the love and praise of the bridegroom, is a friend of the bridegroom. He seeks the chastity [0196A] of the bride, and deservedly undertakes the governance of souls. Every cleric or monk to whom has been given some little word of wisdom or word of knowledge, and who, while he speaks, desires to turn and inflame his hearers to love of himself and his own praise — that man preaches himself, not the bridegroom; and for that reason he is to be driven from the inner chamber, because he is an adulterer; he presides outside in the court, not by the bride's command, but by the leave of her forbearance: because whether the bridegroom be announced by occasion or in truth, one must rejoice. For a faithful soul, from whomever it hears the bridegroom's praises, is kindled to love of the bridegroom, not to love of the one who praises him carelessly. For it loves the diligent praiser as a neighbor, not as the bridegroom; it loves him in the bridegroom, not in place of the bridegroom. Walking in the unspotted way, [0196B] this man ministers to the Lord, not to himself; and he can be set before the rest. But one not walking, but lying in sloth, and who ministers thus, ministers to himself, not to the Lord, and ought not to be set over the rest. He who dispenses the keepers of observances to his own pleasure, and not to the profit of the household, dispenses to himself, not to the Lord, and is not the faithful and prudent steward whom the Lord may set over his household. *The labor of fools shall afflict those who know not how to make their way to the city of the bridegroom;* and therefore they ought not to be made leaders of others. The labor of the wise heals them. For they are led by the bridegroom into the right way, that they may go into the city of habitation; and such men ought to become the leaders of those who have here no abiding city, but seek a city to come, among the flock that comes up from the washing. And the fruitful [0196C] are to be loved, and the barren borne with; and the sound are to be guarded, and the sick healed: because the bridegroom came from heaven to seek again what had perished, and to bring back what had been cast away, and to bind up what was broken, and to strengthen what was weak, and to guard what was fat and strong.

## XII.

These things are to be observed thus among the sheep who are subject. But among prelates and shepherds the condition is otherwise. For just as blessed is *the faithful and prudent servant, whom the Lord has set over his household, to give them food in due season,* who imitates the shepherd [0197A] of shepherds in the administration of the pasture; so, on the contrary, it stands with those shepherds who feed themselves, who do not strengthen what is weak, and do not heal what is sick, and do not bind up what is broken, and do not bring back what is cast away, and do not seek again what had perished: from whose mouth may the shepherd of shepherds deliver his sheep, and feed them in the richest pastures — even those which in the present life always endure a wicked shepherd for their own part. And indeed the devout sheep always seeks a good shepherd. But if he is lacking in this mortal life, he is not lacking who laid down his soul for his sheep. On which account, where he cannot be amended, he is to be borne with by the sheep as a hireling sitting in the chair of Moses. For the devout bridegroom, the true shepherd, has commanded that what such men say, the sheep should do; but what they do, [0197B] they should not do.

## XIII.

I have said these things at the last for this reason, lest anyone should think that I hold lost the faithful sheep which prudently bears with a wicked shepherd — admonishing this above all, that every shepherd should nourish and instruct and correct those subject to him in the discipline and fear of the Lord, not for himself but for the Lord: as the highest shepherd also charged blessed Peter, saying: *Feed my lambs,* [0198A] he says, not *thine;* and therefore for me, not for thee. This is the sure sign that thou truly lovest me, if thou carefully feed my lambs, on account of which thou art to receive the crown of justice. So too a certain ancient shepherd signified the care of his solicitude in mystical words, saying: *It is not yet time that the sheep be shut in the folds. Give the sheep drink first, and so lead them back to pasture.* Where this is not done, there comes that which the prophet laments, saying: *Therefore has my people been led away captive, because it had no knowledge; and its nobles have perished with hunger, and its multitude has dried up with thirst.* And another: *The little ones asked for bread, and there was none to break it for them.*
