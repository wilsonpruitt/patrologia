## HERE BEGINS THE CANTICLE OF [sic: *ISALAE*] THE PROPHET.

Isaiah the prophet, after he had disputed in mystical utterances concerning the incarnation of the Lord and concerning the calling together of the Church and the destruction of idolatry — so that first he told of the flower of the rod, ascending from the root of Jesse, upon which [1091C] the grace of the sevenfold Spirit should rest; and afterward proclaimed the concord and the dwelling together of the beasts with the cattle, that is, of the rich with the poor, and the concord in the Church of the fierce and the proud with the meek and the humble; and testified that the sign of the cross calls the nations together from every side to the unity of the faith — at last made manifest that the desolation of the river of Egypt, that is, the error of the Gentiles who dwelt in the darkness of unbelief, was to be dissolved; and so he calls forth the people of the Church, or the faithful soul, to praise God, so far as she may reckon it to his grace that, freed from her enemies and separated from the children of wrath, she has been made a daughter of reconciliation and of mercy. Whence he says:

« I will confess to thee, O Lord, because thou wast angry [1091D] with me. Thy fury is turned away, and thou hast comforted me. » Confession, therefore, as we have often said, is an equivocal word, and is taken according to the diversity of places. For both those confess who bewail their sins — that is, who do worthy penance for them — and those who render thanks to the Lord confess; but the one is in tears, the other in joy; the one is known to be set in affliction, the other in the holy eagerness of the mind. Whence here the faithful people exults with abundant gladness, marking out a certain image of the age to come, where these voices are unceasing and celebrate together the praises of the Lord with devoted mind. For great is the gladness of a soul once sinful, when it remembers that it has been snatched from the bonds of sins and the snares of errors through the grace [1092A] of holy baptism, and translated by the renewal of the Holy Spirit into the lot of the elect, having hope of the life to come and of everlasting joy. But whence this grace came to it he shows, saying:

« Behold, God the Saviour, mind: I will deal confidently, and I will not fear. » For the whole cause of our redemption and salvation consists in the incarnation of Jesus Christ our Lord, since there is no other name given to men upon the earth — Peter the apostle bearing witness — in which it behooves us to be saved; of whom Paul also says: « For by grace we are saved [n: (Ephes. II)]. » And again: « Who, he says, shall deliver me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord [n: (Rom VII)]. » Of whom again he says: « We give thanks to God, who hath made us worthy in the portion of the lot [1092B] of the saints in light, who hath delivered us from the power of darkness, and hath translated us into the kingdom of the Son of his love, in whom we have redemption and the remission of sins; who is the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of every creature, because in him all things were created in the heavens and on the earth [n: (Col. I)]. » In him also everyone deals confidently who rightly believes in the Lord and hopes in him, knowing that he himself is able to deliver and defend those who truly trust in his protection; and on this account he does not fear the adversary, because he cannot harm him unless permitted. Whence the Psalmist says: « The Lord is a helper to me, and I shall look upon my enemies [n: (Psal. CXVII)]. » It is to be understood that they are made weak and powerless; for he wishes spiritual wickednesses to be understood, which he says are to be [1092C] looked upon in that judgment, where the devil, with the rest of his ministers, already destroyed and damned, will appear to human sight.

« For the Lord is my strength and my praise, and he is become salvation to me. » He who is struck cannot fall, to whom the help of the Lord is seen to furnish strength; but lest he whom the Lord's gifts preserved should be thought only to escape dangers, he added, « he is become salvation to me, » because through him they receive eternal life, through whom they also merit to escape the ruins of this age. For he himself is both the strength and the eternal praise of the blessed. Let that most wicked heresy hear that the Lord was made so for those who are saved, and whose Lord he was not before — that we may understand that creation and making in the Scriptures is not always the founding of those things which [1092D] were not, but sometimes grace toward those who have deserved that God become theirs. After these things the Prophet under a figure exhorts those who believe in God, that they should study to learn his doctrine attentively, who have obtained also his faith; and he says:

« You shall draw waters with joy out of the Saviour's fountains. » Whom above he had called Emmanuel, and Hasten to take away the spoils, Make speed to take prey, and by the remaining names, lest he should seem to be another beside him whom Gabriel announced to the Virgin, saying: « Thou shalt call his name Jesus. For he shall save his people [n: (Matth. I)], » him he now calls Saviour, and proclaims that waters are to be drawn from his fountains — by no means from the waters of the river of Egypt, which were smitten, nor from the waters of the river [1093A] of Rasni, but from the fountains of Jesus. For in the tongue of the Hebrews this expresses "Saviour." Whence he himself also cried out in the Gospel: « He that thirsteth, let him come to me and drink. He that believeth in me, as the Scripture saith, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water. Now this, » says the Evangelist, « he said of the Holy Spirit, whom they that believed in him should receive [n: (Joan. VII)]. » And in another Gospel he himself speaks: « He that shall drink of the water that I will give him shall not thirst for ever; but it shall become in him a fountain of water springing up into life everlasting [n: (Joan. IV)]. » Let us understand the fountains of the Saviour to be the evangelical doctrine, of which we read in the seventy-seventh psalm: « In the churches bless ye God, God from the fountains of Israel » — that doctrine, namely, which the apostles poured into the Gentiles; for from thence the fountain of religion flowed forth through the remaining [1093B] nations. Whence there also follows:

« You shall say in that day. Confess ye to the Lord and call upon his name. Make his devices known among the peoples; remember that his name is exalted. Sing ye of the Lord, for he hath done magnificently. Declare this in the whole earth. » For the devices of the Lord are the divine precepts, which have been brought forth solely from the counsel of the highest wisdom, and become altogether salutary to those who are zealous to fulfil them in deeds. These things are enjoined by the apostles and the remaining teachers from Israel upon those who have believed from among the Gentiles: that they should confess to the Lord alone and, with idols forsaken, call upon his name, and proclaim all his works to the unbelieving, that they may know that he alone is exalted, and that « in his name every knee is bent, [1093C] of things in heaven, on earth, and under the earth [n: (Phil. II)], » to whom it must be sung that he hath done magnificently, and in the whole world his mercy is to be proclaimed. With this place also agrees the beginning of the hundred and fourth psalm, where it is thus written: « Confess ye to the Lord and call upon his [sic: *nonem*], declare his works among the nations. » In this, therefore, the order of the sayings consists of three parts. First he says: « Confess ye to the Lord, » that is, praise him and by good deeds acquire for yourselves the grace of the Godhead; because this very thing is to praise the Lord: both to render him honour with the mouth, and to carry out his commands by works. For that confession is here to be understood which does not cease to celebrate the Lord's praises, since the things that follow also declare such a vow and action. Then he admonishes that the Lord be called upon, [1093D] so that after the ministry of most holy praise we may pay him worthy thanks. For he duly calls upon God who cries to God more by deed than by voice. Whence the Saviour says in the Gospel to the proud and to those disobedient to him: « For why do you call me, Lord, Lord, and do not the things which I say? » [n: (Luc. VI.)] And elsewhere: « Not everyone, he says, that saith to me, Lord, Lord, shall enter into the kingdom of heaven; but he that doth the will of my Father, he shall enter into the kingdom of heaven [n: (Matth. VII)]. » Thirdly he says: « Declare his works among the nations, » which can well suit the glorious evangelists and the most holy apostles, and now the priests, who are known to declare his mighty works throughout all the nations.

[1094A] « Rejoice and praise, O habitation of Sion, for great is the Holy One of Israel in the midst of thee. » First it must be said according to the letter: O habitation of Sion, rejoice and praise thy God, because he who before seemed to be thine own God, and was shut within the narrow bounds of the land of Judea, has now filled the whole earth with his knowledge, and rising from death reigns among the nations, and the peoples entreat and adore him — provided at least that he set thy hand a second time to possess what is left of his people, and gathering the fugitives of Israel and the dispersed of Juda, to be collected from the four quarters of the earth; because the seed-plot of the Gospel proceeded, through the apostles who were of the Jews, from the fountains of Israel. But it is better that we interpret Sion, that is, a watchtower placed on high, as the Church, of which [1094B] the fiftieth psalm also sings: « Deal kindly, O Sion, in thy good will, that the walls of Jerusalem may be built up. » So that there may be made acceptable in her to the Lord the sacrifice of justice, oblations and holocausts, and the calf which the most merciful father immolated for the penitent son.
