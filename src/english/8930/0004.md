## HERE BEGINS THE CANTICLE OF ANNA, *Mother of Samuel the prophet.* (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « That he may sit with princes, and hold the throne of glory. » To whom he says: « You shall sit upon twelve seats [n: (Matth. XIX)]; » and giving them the seat of glory as an inheritance. ⟦s2⟧ « For the poles of the earth are the Lord's, and he hath set the world upon them. » Because he himself hath dominion from sea to sea, and from the river unto the ends of the world [1101D] of the earth [n: (Psal. LXXI)]. ⟦s3⟧ « The poles of the earth, he says, are the Lord's: » that is, the four quarters of the world, in which he will enlarge the world of his Church, which from the rising of the sun to its setting praises his name.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « He will keep the feet of his saints. » That is, he directs the works of his elect in the way of justice.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And the wicked shall be silent in darkness. » Who shall be sent into the exterior darkness, where there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth. ⟦s2⟧ Where they shall be silent, that is, they shall cease from the arrogance of their pride.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « For no man shall be strengthened by his own strength. » That is, not by his own strength but by the divine power is a man mighty, that is, the people of believers.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « His adversaries shall fear the Lord. » Because [1102A] the Lord shall make his adversary weak. ⟦s2⟧ It can also be understood, from the ambiguity of the Greek, as "his own adversary." ⟦s3⟧ For when the Lord has begun to possess us, assuredly the adversary who had been ours becomes his, and he will be conquered by us, not by our own powers, because no man is mighty in his own power. ⟦s4⟧ The Lord therefore shall make his own adversary weak. ⟦s5⟧ The Lord is holy, that he may be conquered by the saints, whom the Lord, the holy of holies, makes holy; and through this let not the mighty man glory in his might, and let not the prudent man glory in his prudence, nor the rich man in his riches; but let him that glorieth glory in this, to understand and to know the Lord, and to do judgment and justice in the midst of the earth. ⟦s5⟧ In no small part does he understand and know the Lord who understands and knows also that this is given him by the Lord, that he may understand and [1102B] know the Lord. ⟦s6⟧ But because a just retribution is to come from the Lord upon the sons of Adam in the day of judgment, thence there follows:

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « He shall thunder upon them in the heavens. » He himself shall judge the ends of the earth, because he is just. ⟦s1⟧ This prophetess held throughout the order of the confession of the faithful; for the Lord Christ ascended into heaven, and thence he is to come to judge the living and the dead. ⟦s2⟧ « For who ascended, » as the Apostle says [n: (Ephes. IV)], « but he also who descended into the lower parts of the earth? ⟦s3⟧ He who descended is the very same who ascended above all the heavens, that he might fill all things. » Through the clouds, therefore, he thundered upon his own, which clouds he filled with the Holy Spirit when he had ascended. ⟦s4⟧ Concerning which he threatened the carnal Jerusalem, that is, the ungrateful vineyard, in Isaiah the prophet, that they should not rain [1102C] upon her a shower. ⟦s5⟧ And it was said thus:

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The Lord shall judge the ends of the earth. » As if it were said, The Lord shall judge the last things of a man: since those things will not be judged which are changed in the meantime for the better or for the worse, but the last state in which he shall have been found who is to be judged. ⟦s2⟧ « And he shall give empire to his king, that is, to the Christian people: he gives them the strength by which they may rule the flesh as kings, and may conquer the world in him who for their sake poured out his blood. ⟦s3⟧ There follows:

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And he shall exalt the horn of his Christ. » How shall Christ exalt the horn of his Christ? ⟦s2⟧ Assuredly all those anointed with his chrism we can rightly call christs, yet the whole body together with its head is one Christ. ⟦s3⟧ These things Anna prophesied, the mother of Samuel, [1102D] a holy man and much praised, in whom indeed the change of the old priesthood was then figured, and is now fulfilled, when she was made weak who was many in sons, so that she might have a new priesthood in Christ — the barren woman who bore seven.
