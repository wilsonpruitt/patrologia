## HERE BEGINS THE CANTICLE OF THE BOOK OF DEUTERONOMY. (cont.)

« As an eagle enticing her young to fly, and fluttering over them, she spread her wings and took them up and bore them upon her shoulders. » Another edition: [1138B] « As an eagle covers her nest, and has settled over her young, she spread her wings and received them and took them up upon her shoulder-blades. » The natural philosophers relate that the eagle — which is named from the keenness of its eyes — is of so sharp a gaze that, when it is borne over the seas on motionless wing and lies not open to human sight, it sees from so great a height the little fishes swimming in the water, and, descending like a hurled engine, drags the seized prey by its wings to the shore. For it is said also not to turn aside its gaze against the ray of the sun; whence also it holds up its young suspended by its talon and sets them against the rays of the sun, and those which it has seen keep an unmoving eye it preserves as worthy of the stock; but any which it sees turn their gaze aside it casts away as degenerate. Eagles are also said, when they see their young grow feathered in the nests, to strike them with their wings and to entice them out of the nest [1138C] to fly. And they build their nests in rocks and in trees, in whose nest the stone *echites* is built in, which some have called *gaietes*, useful for many remedies, losing nothing by fire. And these same searchers into nature relate that eagles die not so much of old age or of sickness as of hunger, the upper beak growing to such a length that its hook cannot be opened; but when it has seen that danger threatens it from thence, it blunts its beak upon a very hard rock, until it returns again to its former use. And thence it is written in the psalm in mystic speech, the Prophet saying to the faithful soul: « Thy youth shall be renewed like the eagle's [n: (Psal. CII)]. » By the likeness of the eagle it is shown, according to the letter, that the Lord, by rousing his people, helped them to possess the land of Chanaan [1138D] by wars, and by helping them protected them from their enemies. But according to the mystical sense this eagle signifies the Lord Saviour, who holds the likeness of the eagle in this, that by his own death he snatched away the prey from the malignant enemy, and after his resurrection carried up to the heights of the heavens the flesh which he took for the redemption of the human race, exhorting us by words and by examples that we should follow thither by faith, and reach out by devotion, whither he himself ascended in body; whence also he says in the Gospel: « Wheresoever the body shall be, there shall the eagles be gathered together [n: (Luc. XVII)]. » The other examples also which we have told of the eagle can likewise be figuratively transferred to him and to his sons. For as the eagle is of a sharp gaze, so that, lifted above the seas [1139A] on motionless wing and not appearing to human sight, seeing from so great a height the little fishes swimming in the deep, it seizes them by its preying and drags them to the shore: so our Redeemer, exalted above all the heavens and bodily taken up from human sight, nevertheless sees in the deep of this world, by the gaze of his majesty, those whom he may [sic: *cruat*] by the saving prey, and may draw to the shore of eternal blessedness by the cord of his love. Whence he himself also says in the Gospel: « And I, if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all things to myself [n: (Joan. XII)]. » His young — that is, the sons reborn to him through the grace of baptism — he sets against the rays of the eternal sun through contemplation; and those whom he has seen to have a steadfast eye of mind in gazing, or worthy of his own stock, he preserves perpetually; but any whom he beholds turning back the gaze of the mind to the lowest things and to [1139B] carnal desires, these he casts away as degenerate. And this eagle builds her nest in rocks and in trees, because in those whom she beholds firm in faith and bearing fruit in virtues she brings forth the fruitfulness of a spiritual germination; and there is born that gem and most precious pearl which the sacred text of the Gospel relates that the spiritual merchant procures for himself when all his money has been sold. But that the eagle [sic: *tandit*] its superfluous beak upon a rock, and thus brings back its former measure, signifies that holy men break in pieces whatever they behold in their manner of life to be superfluous and harmful to themselves, dashing it against the rock which is Christ; and, renewing themselves through grace and through good endeavour, they render themselves fit for their former office.

[1139C] « The Lord alone was his leader, and there was not with him a [sic: *alicnus*] god. » Another edition: « The Lord alone was teaching them, and there was not with them a strange god. » It is manifest that one and only God freed Israel out of Egypt, and not the false idols which the Egyptians worshipped. So also in our own deliverance out of the spiritual Egypt there is no other than that same God who once delivered the Israelites — namely the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit, one and threefold, the great and almighty God, who through the incarnation of Jesus Christ our Lord effects the mystery of our salvation. Whence let heretics know how miserably they stray, deviating from the true faith, when they sever themselves through schisms from the Catholic Church — men who are liable to as many idols as they have errors

[1139D] « He set him up over a high land, that he might eat the fruits of the fields. » Another [sic: *edito*]: « He brought them into the strength of the land, he fed them with the produce of the fields. » He calls Palestine a high land, because in comparison with the land of Egypt, which is flat and well watered, it seems mountainous and rough; and it has strength, because it is not barren but most fertile in bringing forth the fruits of the earth unto the fullness of them that dwell in it. Otherwise, the high land is the holy Church, which is exceedingly lofty in virtues and in knowledge, and most fruitful in the germination of good works. There all the faithful, being set up, eat the fruits of the spiritual fields, because there the good seed of the word of God springs up, one bearing thirtyfold, another sixtyfold, another a hundredfold [1140A] fruit, so that each one may find for himself food sufficient according to his capacity.

« That he might suck honey out of the rock, and oil out of the hardest stone. » Another edition: « They sucked honey out of the rock, and oil out of the firm rock. » But nowhere is any such thing read according to the history, if the whole of the Old Testament be reviewed: nowhere did that people suck honey out of a rock, nowhere oil out of a firm rock or out of the hardest stone. But because, according to Paul's word, « the rock was Christ [n: (I Cor. X)]. » They sucked honey out of the rock who saw the deeds and miracles of that same our Redeemer. But they sucked oil out of the firm rock who after his resurrection deserved to be anointed by the outpouring of the Holy Spirit. As it were, therefore, he gave honey in the firm rock, when the Lord, being still mortal, showed to his disciples the sweetness [1140B] of his miracles. But the firm rock poured out oil, because after his Resurrection, being now made impassible, he sent forth the gift of holy unction through the breathing of the Spirit. Of this oil also it is said through the prophet: « The yoke shall rot away at the presence of the oil [n: (Isa. X)], » for they were held under the yoke of demoniac domination; but we have been anointed with the oil of the Holy Spirit, and because the grace of liberty has anointed us, the yoke of demoniac domination has rotted away.

« Butter of the herd and milk of the sheep, with the fat of lambs and of rams of the sons of Basan, and goats with the marrow of wheat, and the blood of the grape. » Another edition: « Butter of oxen and milk of sheep, with the fat of lambs and of rams of the sons of bulls and of goats, with the fat of the kidneys, of wheat, and the blood of the grape. » Although these things according [1140C] to the history hint at the fertility of the land of Chanaan, yet in a loftier sense they show the abundance of the grace of Christ which the holy Church received by the gift of her bestower. They took butter of the herd who learned the fullness of faith from the examples of the patriarchs; and they received milk of the sheep who took simplicity of mind, or easiness of teaching, from the innocent life of the Fathers unto their own edification; and these things, with the fat of lambs and of rams — that is, with the fatness of the charity of the apostles and of those whom they themselves had steeped in the faith — they received unto the refreshment of their mind. For by the name of rams are signified the apostles and men of apostolic dignity, because they themselves are the leaders of the flock of God. Whence it is said in the psalm: « Bring to the Lord, O ye sons of God, bring to the Lord the sons of rams *(Psal. [1140D] XXVIII)*. » But that which is subjoined, « of the sons of Basan, » belongs to the same sense, because Basan is interpreted *fatness*; for they are sons of fatness who have been instructed in the disciplines of the New Testament, wherein consists a multitude of knowledge and a greatness of virtues. That also which is read in the other edition, « of the sons of bulls, » can likewise be referred to the person of the prophets and of the apostles, and also of the other holy doctors, because they are the leaders of the flock of God and preside over the spiritual herds, from whose teachings, namely, these receive the seed of the word, [sic: *undo*] they may be able to bring forth the offspring of good works. Nor would these gifts have sufficed, unless the goats too were added. For goats have the figure of penitents, because in the law they were offered for sin: [1141A] for it is not enough for the faithful to have the gifts of virtues, unless they do penance for their transgressions. Whence he who was at once king and prophet, and to whom it was promised concerning the incarnation of Christ that he should be born of his seed, entreats the Lord for his own offences, saying: « Wash me yet more from my injustice, and cleanse me from my offence; for I acknowledge my iniquity, and my offence is always before me [n: (Psal. L)]. » And that all these benefits which have been named might be heaped up with a more eminent gift, he added the marrow of wheat and the blood of the grape. For what is figured by the marrow of wheat but the body of Christ; what by the blood of the grape but the chalice of the New Testament in the blood of Christ? For among the very many gifts granted out of the mercy of God to the people of the Church, [1141B] this excels among them all: that he has [sic: *ministrasit*] to it, unto eternal salvation, the sacraments of his body and blood in the confecting of bread and wine. There follows:

« He had drunk the most unmixed; the beloved is grown fat and hath kicked. » Another edition: « Jacob drank wine and ate, and was filled, and the beloved kicked. » Assuredly all the gifts of God are conferred, without the dregs of any wickedness or reproach, upon those who believe and think worthily of them. But since, just as the carnal Israelites of old abused the gifts of God and, made worse out of most bountiful benefits, slid down into idolatry, so — alas for the grief! — not a few of the people of the Church, ungrateful for the benefits of God, slip back into vices, and that which was conferred upon them for their salvation [1141C] they turn into their own perdition.
