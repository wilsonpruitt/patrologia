## HERE BEGINS THE CANTICLE OF THE BOOK OF DEUTERONOMY. (cont.)

« Grown gross, made fat and swollen wide, he forsook God his maker, and departed from God his salvation. They provoked him with strange gods, and with their abominations they stirred him to wrath; they sacrificed to demons and not to God, to gods whom they knew not; new and recent ones came to them, whom their fathers worshipped not. » Another edition: « He was made fat and grew gross; he was swollen wide; he forsook God, who made him, and departed from God his salvation. They embittered me with strange things, with their abominations they stirred me up; they sacrificed to demons and not to God, to gods whom they knew not; new, [1141D] recent ones came to them, whom their fathers knew not. » This the ancient Israelites did after the manner of history, as is sufficiently set forth in their books; but the modern Israelites too, when they turn the gifts of God granted them in knowledge and in virtues for the divine service into heresy or hypocrisy, are proved to serve idols and not God — rendering worship, that is to say, to demons and not to God, which our fathers, that is, the patriarchs, the prophets and the apostles and the evangelists, by no means did. Hence it comes to pass for these idolaters that, provoking by their evils the most clement Lord, whom they might have had propitious had they done well, they must suffer him severe and strict on account of their evil works.

« Thou hast forsaken the God that begot thee, and hast forgotten [1142A] the Lord thy creator. » Another edition: « Thou hast forsaken the God that begot thee, and hast forgotten the God that nourisheth thee. » The Lord in a certain manner begot the Israelites through the law and circumcision; he begot also the people of the Church through the grace of baptism, through the sanctification of the Holy Spirit, and he furnished it sufficiently with the nourishment of heavenly food, that is, of evangelical doctrine. But he who fears not to forget these things will justly feel God angry with him through vengeance. Whence it is subjoined:

« The Lord saw, and was stirred to wrath, because his sons and his daughters provoked him; and he said: I will hide my face from them, and I will consider their last end. » Another edition: « The Lord saw, and was jealous, and was embittered because of the anger of his sons and his daughters; and he said: I will turn away my [1142B] face from them, and I will show what shall be to them at the last. » Nothing is heavier among punishments than to be deprived of the presence and the vision of God. Thence the Psalmist entreats the Lord, saying: « Cast me not away from thy face [n: (Psal. L)]. « And likewise: » Turn not away, he says, thy face from me. » Hence Job also speaks to the Lord, saying: « Let me not be hidden from thy face [n: (Job XIII)]. » Since therefore sons and daughters — that is, men of either sex — provoking him for their evils which they do, do not merit his mercy, but, ungrateful, are spurned by him at their last end. This shall be proved when they shall be cast into the eternal punishments of Gehenna; for this is to consider their last end: to make manifest their torments, which he himself bestows upon them justly, not with a troubled mind, but with a tranquil one.

[1142C] « For it is a perverse generation, and unfaithful sons. They have provoked me in that which was not the Lord, and have stirred me with their vanities; and I will provoke them in that which is not a people, and with a foolish nation will I stir them. » Another edition: « For it is a depraved and perverse generation, sons in whom there is no faith in them; they in jealousy have compelled me, and not in God; in anger they have stirred me up with their idols; and I in jealousy will drive them out, and not into a nation; with an unwise nation will I stir them. » Since therefore the Israelites, casting away the worship of their Lord, worshipped idols, and, forsaking the true and living God, served the vanity of images, they were handed over for plunder to the nations, who at that time had not yet deserved to be called by the name of the people of God. Likewise also churchmen, who, the truth being forsaken, [1142D] turned aside to errors, if they shall not have repented of their evils, will be handed over to the malignant spirits — who by growing proud lost their angelic dignity — to be thrust into the prisons of hell. Well, therefore, can the demons be called a foolish nation, because they hate justice and love iniquity.

« A fire is kindled in my fury, and it shall burn even to the lowest parts of hell. » Another edition: « For a fire hath blazed out of my anger, and it shall burn even to the lower regions beneath. » Of those whom the scourges condemn and do not deliver it is written: « Thou hast struck them, and they have not grieved; thou hast bruised them, and they have refused to receive correction [n: (Jer. V)]. » For these, the scourges begin from this life and endure in the eternal striking. Whence also [1143A] the Lord says through Moses: « A fire hath blazed out of my anger, and it shall burn even to the lower regions beneath [n: (Deut. XXXII)]. » For as far as concerns the present striking, it is rightly said: « A fire hath blazed out of my anger. » But as far as concerns eternal damnation, this is aptly subjoined, and it shall burn even to the lower regions beneath — although by some there is wont to be alleged that which is written: « God does not take vengeance twice for the same thing. » These, however, do not attend to that which is said through the Prophet concerning the wicked, and with a double confusion crush them, and that which is written elsewhere. Jesus, saving the people out of the land of Egypt, in the second place destroyed those who did not believe; to whom, nevertheless, if we lend our assent — that any fault whatever cannot be struck twice — this must be reckoned of those struck for their sin and dying in their sin, [1143B] because their striking begins here and is finished there, so that in the uncorrected there is one scourge, which begins temporally but is consummated in eternal punishments; so that for those who altogether refuse to be corrected the striking of present scourges is already the beginning of the torments that follow. There follows:

« It hath devoured the earth and its growth, and shall burn up the foundations of the mountains. I will heap evils upon them, and will spend my arrows among them. They shall be consumed with famine, and birds shall devour them with a most bitter bite; I will send in upon them the teeth of beasts, with the fury of them that drag upon the earth and of serpents. Without, the sword shall lay them waste, and within, dread — the young man together with the virgin, the suckling with the old man. » [1143C] Another edition: « The earth hath eaten the produce of the fields, and shall burn up the foundations of the mountains. I will heap evils upon them, and will consume my arrows among them; wasting away with famine, and they shall be food for birds; and a stretching of the back that cannot be healed. I will send in upon them the teeth of beasts, with the fury of them that drag upon the earth. From without, the sword shall deprive them, leaving them without sons, and in their storehouses, fear — young men with the virgin, the suckling with the settled old man. » It hath devoured, he says, the earth and its growth: then does it devour the earth and its growth, when the flame of eternal vengeance shall burn up the sinful flesh on account of the works of lust. For the growths of the flesh are properly the works of carnal concupiscence: « which are fornications, uncleannesses, immodesties, luxuries, the service of idols, sorceries, enmities, contentions, [1143D] emulations, angers, quarrels, dissensions, sects, envies, murders, drunkennesses, revellings, and such like — they who do these things, » as Paul testifies, » « shall not obtain the kingdom of God [n: (Gal. V)]. » And it shall burn up the foundations of the mountains, when it shall consume with eternal torments the wicked counsels of the proud, by which they believed that they could sin unpunished. He will heap evils upon them, when he shall bring into memory not only their evil works, but also their perverse thoughts and the depraved words for which they are to be punished. He will spend his arrows among them, when he shall show the wrath of his indignation through various torments. They shall be consumed with famine, that is, not of food nor of drink, but with the perpetual barrenness of good works. And birds shall devour them with a most bitter bite: [1144A] namely, the malignant spirits, reproaching them with their past sins, tear them with a most savage mouth. But that which is added after the devouring of the birds, a stretching of the back that cannot be healed, signifies the most bitter wounds which sinners suffer in the torturing of the demons, who afflict them without any pity and force them to pay strong penalties in hell. The beasts and the serpents are these same malignant spirits, who are called beasts on account of their ferocity, serpents on account of their craft. Beasts and serpents drag the reprobate with their teeth upon the earth in fury, when that which they first persuaded carnal men to by flattering, that they might seduce them, they afterwards with a raging mind exact of sinners, that they may torment them. Without, the sword shall lay waste the impious, and within, dread; when in the final vengeance the burning [1144B] of eternal fire tortures the body on every side, and within, the greatness of anguish oppresses the mind; whence it is written: « Their worm shall not die, and the fire shall not be extinguished [n: (Marc. IX)]. » « The young man, he says, together with the virgin, the suckling with the old man. » Having enumerated all the ages, he shows that in the final vengeance no age is to be spared, whom the same worship of idolatry had defiled. But allegorically, in the young man he shows the wanton and the lustful; in the virgin, her that is barren of good works; in the suckling, the dull; in the old man, the slothful and the useless. All these, therefore, whom now admonition and rebuke do not restrain from sins, the just vengeance of God will consume in the final avenging.

« And I said: Where are they? I will make their memory to cease from among men. » Another edition: « And [1144C] he said: I will destroy them; and I will take away their memory from among men. » God's questioning is not ignorance, but rebuke. The fulfilment of this sentence can easily be discerned by him who sees the Jews cast out from the land of promise and dispersed through the whole world. But generally this hangs over all sinners and impenitent men as a thing to come: that they be taken away from the land of the saints, and that their memory be not numbered in the assembly of the elect. Whence through the Psalmist a punishment to come is foretold to the head of all the wicked together with the whole body, when, the several kinds of vices having been reckoned up, it is subjoined: « Therefore God shall destroy thee for ever; he shall pluck thee out and remove thee from his tabernacle, and thy root out of the land of the living [n: (Psal. LI)]. »
