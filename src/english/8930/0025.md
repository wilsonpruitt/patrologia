## HERE BEGINS THE CANTICLE OF MARY *Mother of the Lord.*

The most blessed virgin Mary, after she knew herself to be great with a heavenly birth, in no way lifted herself up over the heavenly gifts [1162A] as though these were from herself; but that she might be more and more fitted for the divine gifts, she fixed the step of her mind in the keeping of humility, mindful of the Scripture which enjoins: « The greater thou art, humble thyself in all things [n: (Eccli. III)], and as soon as the angel who announced to her the divine birth had returned to the heavenly places, she arises, goes up into the hill country, and, bearing God in her womb, she seeks the dwellings of the servants of God and asks for their converse. At her coming Elizabeth, hearing the salutation of the mother of the Lord, at once prophesied concerning her and bore witness that the mother of the Lord had come to her, and pronounced her blessed among women and the fruit of her womb blessed; and moreover she related that at her voice the infant in her own womb had leaped for joy, being filled with the Holy Spirit. Mary too, hearing these things, did not boastfully lift herself up [1162B] with vain glory, but rather through humility, with the whole intention of her mind, rendered thanks to God, saying:

« My soul doth magnify the Lord, and my spirit hath rejoiced in God my saviour. » With so great a gift, she says, and one so unheard of, hath the Lord exalted me, that it cannot be unfolded by any service of the tongue, but can scarcely be grasped by the very inmost affection of the breast; and therefore I offer all the powers of my soul in the praises of thanksgiving; whatever I live, feel, discern, I spend rejoicingly and wholly upon the contemplating of his greatness, of whom there is no end, because my spirit is glad also in the eternal divinity of the same Jesus, that is, of the saviour, whose flesh rejoices me by a temporal conception; to which is like that saying of the Psalmist: « But my soul shall rejoice in the Lord, and shall be delighted over his salvation *(Psal. [1162C] XXXIV)*, » for he too venerated the Father and the Son with an equal love.

« Because he hath regarded the humility of his handmaid; for behold from henceforth all generations shall call me blessed. » She whose humility is regarded rightly rejoices to be named blessed by all, just as, on the contrary, she whose pride is despised is condemned, and, fined with the name of Eve (*Aeva*) — that is, of *vae*, woe, or of calamity — wastes away. For it was fitting that, just as through the pride of our first parent death entered into the world, so through the humility of Mary the entrance of life should be laid open.

« Because he that is mighty hath done great things to me, and holy is his name. » He looks back to the beginning of the song, where it was said, « My soul doth magnify the Lord »: for that soul alone, to which the Lord vouchsafes [1162D] to do great things, can magnify him with worthy proclamations, and can say by way of exhortation to the sharers of the same vow and purpose: « Magnify the Lord with me, and let us exalt his name one with another; » [var: Vulg. *in id ipsum*, "together, unto the same"] for whosoever of us shall have despised, so far as in him lies, to magnify him whom he has known and to hallow his name, shall be called least in the kingdom of heaven. His name is called holy, who by the height of a singular power transcends every creature and is set far apart from all the things he has made — which is better understood by the Greek expression, in which the very word which is said, ἅγιον, signifies as it were to be outside the earth; in imitation of whom we too, according to our measure, are commanded to be set apart from all who are not holy nor dedicated to God, [1163A] the Lord saying: » Be ye holy, because I also am holy [n: (I Pet. I)]. For whosoever shall have consecrated himself will deservedly be seen to be outside the earth and outside the world. For he too can say: walking upon the earth, we have our conversation in the heavens.

« And his mercy from generation unto generations, to them that fear him. » [var: Vulg. *in progenies et progenies*, "unto generations and generations"] Turning himself from the special gifts to the general judgments of God, he describes the state of the whole human race, and sets forth in alternating verses what the proud and what the humble deserve, what the sons of Adam are through free will, what the sons of God are through grace. Not to me alone, then, he says, hath he that is mighty done great things, but in every nation and generation he that feareth him and worketh justice is acceptable to him.

« He hath shewed might in his arm; he hath scattered the proud [1163B] in the conceit of their heart. » By "in his arm" he signifies in the very Son of God — not that God the Father is bounded by the figure of human flesh, and that the Son cleaves to him as a member of the body; but because all things were made through him, therefore he is called the arm of the Lord. For just as thy arm is that through which thou workest, so the Word of God has been called the arm of God, because through the Word he wrought the world. For why does a man stretch out his arm in order to work anything? Because what he has said is not straightway done; but if he prevailed with so great a power that without any motion of his body what he said were done, his arm would be his word. When therefore we have heard that the arm of God the Father is God the Son, let not fleshly custom clamour against us, but, so far as we can by his gift, let us think upon the power and wisdom of God, [1163C] through which all things were made.

« He hath put down the mighty from their seat, and hath exalted the humble; he hath filled the hungry with good things, and the rich he hath sent empty away. » In his arm, then, the Lord throughout all the generations of the world puts down the mighty — that is, the proud — from the seat of their boasting, and exalts the humble, who trust not in their own power but in the goodness of God, « because every one that exalteth himself shall be humbled, and he that humbleth himself shall be exalted [n: (Matth. XXIII)]. » In like manner he fills with eternal goods the hungry who hunger and thirst after justice, and suffers the rich, the greedy and the sparing, to perish empty of good works.

« He hath received Israel his servant, being mindful of his mercy. » Beautifully he calls Israel the servant of the Lord, as one taken up by him to be saved — obedient, [1163D] that is, and humble, according to the saying of Osee: « Because Israel is a child, and I loved him [n: (Ose. XI)]; » for he who scorns to be humbled cannot at all be saved, nor say with the Prophet: « For behold God helpeth me, and the Lord is the protector of my soul [n: (Psal. LIII)]. And whosoever shall humble himself as a little child, he is the greater in the kingdom of heaven [n: (Matth. XVIII)]. »

« As he spoke to our fathers, to Abraham and to his seed for ever. » He signifies the seed of Abraham not carnal but spiritual — that is, not only those begotten of his flesh, but those who, whether in circumcision or in uncircumcision, have followed the footsteps of his faith; for he himself also, being set in uncircumcision, believed, and it was reputed to him unto justice, and he received circumcision as the seal of that same faith, [1164A] that so he might become the father of both peoples through faith, according to what the Apostle most fully set forth to the Romans. The coming, therefore, of the Saviour, promised to Abraham and to his seed for ever, is promised to the sons of the promise, to whom it is said: « And if you be Christ's, then are you the seed of Abraham, heirs according to the promise [n: (Galat. III)]. » Well too do the mothers by prophesying go before the rising either of the Lord or of John, so that, as sin began from women, so also good things should begin from women; and that life, which perished through the deceiving of one, should be given back to the world by two vying with each other in proclaiming it.
