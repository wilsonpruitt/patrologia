## CHAPTER ONE.

[1425A]

If you wish to enter into life through Jesus, who is the way and the door; if you wish to eat of the tree of life and to taste the hidden manna, let it not frighten you off, nor grow cheap in your eyes, that on every side you find the approach hard and mean. He has thorns on his head, nails in his hands and feet, a lance in his side, scourgings on his arms; and he is wholly torn in body, and like a leper foul to look upon and hard to follow. But beware of throwing away the nut because of the bitter shell: for the more bitter it seems on the outside, the sweeter a kernel you will one day find within. Therefore, that you may in some measure be able to comprehend with all the saints what is the length, the breadth, the height, and the depth of the mystery of the holy cross and of the Lord's passion — [1425B] which God hides from the wise and prudent of the world and reveals to little ones — understand the weight of the words that are spoken: because, with God working together with them, they prepare the soul to have devotion in prayer, and consolation in tribulation, and revelation in contemplation; and so you may know not only the things that have been given to us by God, but also him himself who has been given to us.

In this meditation a fire will blaze up, if you meditate on these things even simply according to the letter. Here, therefore, diligently recognize the face of your Creator and Redeemer, and look upon the face of your Christ; and with the whole gaze of heart and body, with the whole understanding and affection, and with a spirit brought very low, so attend to this image as though Christ were dying on the cross in your very presence; and think in your heart whose image and inscription this is: [1425C] because he is God and man.

## CHAPTER II.

Consider first, if you can, and as much as you can, how great is the inward length of brightness in the Godhead, which so lay hidden in him for so many years, reaching from end to end, having neither beginning nor end, in which God the Father chose us in Christ before the foundation of the world. Consider also how great is the outward length of the same in the humanity, in which for so long he endured so many and such great things, in the whole body and for so long a time, from the sole of the foot to the crown of the head, from the incarnation to the resurrection. Consider not only the length, but also the breadth of charity: because he loved us not only with a perpetual, but even with an excessive charity — so that, [1425D] God needing nothing, who made all things for himself, and subjected all things to man, after man had by sinning lost them all, God did not wish to lose him, [1426A] but appointed a lawgiver over him, and instructing him diligently through the prophets, and at last coming in person himself — nay, mercifully showing himself to us, and humbly putting on the meanness of our flesh — after examples of most perfect holiness, after words of saving knowledge, after miracles of singular power, doing good to all, suffering evils for all, that we might not die the eternal death.

So he willed to die with hands spread wide, embracing all; he calls all to himself, he rejects none; with head bowed he offers the kiss of peace to his enemies, altogether taking no notice of the wrong done him — nay, making satisfaction for it; his body pierced through on every side, and his side opened, he bestowed his body and blood, his heart and soul — nay, the whole God and man, and with him life and inspiration [1426B] and all things — most bountifully upon us and upon each one, and repaid so many goods for evils and bore so many evils.

## CHAPTER III.

Attend and see how great was that love, how astounding a love, that for man God willed to become man, and that, in order to exalt men, the Most High even humbled himself. And so, just as man abhors and flees shame, labor, and pain, so God for man's sake chose these things and held to them, when, having set joy aside, he endured the cross, the confusion, and the reproach.

See therefore and consider how lofty is the depth of charity, which the Wisdom of God so united to itself that the charity in him was not only long and broad, but also high and deep — such as it befitted God to display and man [1426C] needed to find. See how great is that sublimity and glorious majesty from which he came, and how deep and mean the humility to which he came down. From highest heaven into the lower parts of the earth; from the throne of glory into the place of misery; from the delights of the angels to all the troubles of men; from the bosom of the Father into the womb of a poor little mother; from the narrowness of the virginal womb into the mean stable of the ass and the ox, and afterward into Egypt for fear of Herod; from there remaining in his own country without honor, humbly bearing so many blasphemies, so many insults, so many wrongs, so many plots. In the midst of his disciples he served like the servant of servants, even to the purging of sins and the washing of feet — even of Judas the betrayer, who could not be appeased by such service, nor by the sweet banquet, nor by the pleasant converse, nor by the kiss of peace, but sold him for a mean [1426D] price to his enemies for death — and the death of the cross. There he so emptied himself that he came down from the gibbet of robbers, his body into the tomb and his spirit [1427A] into hell; at once, in one, rich and poor, living and dead, exalted and humbled, sublime above the heads of the angels, lowly beneath the feet of sinners, reckoned a mean and feeble sinner, a madman, and the last of men.

## CHAPTER IV.

Think how great was the labor of his body, in which he had been so poor from his birth — fasting, keeping watch, going about, preaching, and at the end, from the anguish of impending death, in the agony of long-drawn prayer even to a bloody sweat. Treacherously seized, so unbecomingly bound, and through the night so foully cudgeled from house to house, smeared with spittle and struck with blows before the high priests and the great men, led in the morning to Pilate's house, exposed to worldly justice [1427B] as though guilty of death; then presented to Herod, and by him and his army accounted a fool, and again mocked by Pilate as though a king of fools, and treated like a criminal with [harsh] words and with lashes, so basely accused before so many Jews and gentiles, so grievously scourged, condemned without cause, forced to carry the cross like a robber and a murderer — the just one for the unjust and the wicked, and with the wicked condemned not to exile, not to prison, not to the loss of a foot or a hand, but to death, and the foulest death; and lifted up before friends and enemies, on a high feast day, in a conspicuous place, between the arms of a most mean cross, with hands and feet thus stretched out, to be pierced with iron nails, and so hanging to be mocked by all. There his ears were hearing the blasphemies of his enemies, [1427C] his eyes were seeing men laughing and insulting, his nostrils could smell the stench, and to his thirsting mouth they offered a deadly drink — men who (as Jerome says on Isaiah) had already torn out the hairs of his beard.

## CHAPTER V.

Look upon the letters of the crown, that is, the punctures which the crown pressed into him round about his head, and his whole face hidden and despised, disfigured with spittle and with buffets, and made bloody with the points of the thorns; his whole body full of black and red letters, as many as the blows with which it was scourged; livid and sprinkled with drops of blood: him, in short, the very glory of the angels, and beautiful in form above the sons of men, handled more foully than a leper, more cruelly than any criminal.

Amid these things consider what shame it was to him that the world, [1427D] that so many nations, should be scandalized in him: what shame for so great and such a Lord, the King of kings, to be abandoned by so few servants at so great a moment — a Lord whom all had fled for fear, and were hiding for dread, and were mourning for grief: on so great a solemnity, in his own city, where a little before he had been so magnificently honored; when others were more decently clothed, to be so foully and so often stripped before them all, to be driven about with so many reproaches and mockeries. With how small a garment is the shame of nature covered in him who is the Lord of heaven and earth and the splendor of glory, whose beauty and brightness the sun and moon marvel at — and lest anyone should see so great a disgrace, by a wonderful fellow-suffering the earth is darkened, with shadows scattered everywhere.

[1428A]But to crown the shame, look at the multitude of letters on the surface of the page, as it were the numberless people of Jews and gentiles standing about the cross at such a spectacle: where the centurion with his soldiers and armor-bearers, the high priests and the Pharisees, the elders with the clerks of the law and their ministers, were, as it were in power and authority, beneath the arms of the cross; and the whole people, not only of so great a city but gathered from many regions, stood without compassion before the face of the dying Lord. And the soldiers, making sport, divided his garment among themselves. And the high priests and Pharisees, who ought to have taught others, jesting with one another and with the scribes, mocked as much as they could the excellent miracles he had done and the sweetest words he used to speak; and the robbers hung blaspheming before him.
