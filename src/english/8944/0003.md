## CHAPTER II.

[1135D]

VERS. 1.-- *I am the flower of the field.* Because in the state of the present time labour rather than rest is fitting: rightly does he call the Bride, who is delighted with leisure, to the exercises of labours. As if he should say: Thou indeed, wishing to render thy beauty commendable to me, delightest to flower in quiet with secret virtues: but know thou that I require another manner of beauty in the present, because I am the flower of virtues, from which every fruit of the field goes forth; because I desire that my odour become known to the whole world, which cannot be fulfilled unless, the quiet in which thou delightest being broken off, thou gird thyself to the office of preaching. And *the lily of the valleys* rather than of the mountains, because I reveal the brightness of my manhood, or the splendour [1136A] of eternal light, or of the Godhead, rather to the humble and to such as are ready to suffer. As if: Remember that I, who could be born of a virgin, who am God, appeared humbly in the world, showed myself to be a companion to men, came humbly even unto death. Therefore cease to seek the security of quiet, whence he subjoins, *As the lily.* As a field is adorned with flowers, so the world is adorned with the knowledge of me and with faith.

VERS. 2.-- *As the lily.* Himself being praised, he praises the Bride, thus: *As the lily* cannot be compared to the *thorns* among which it often springs up: in the same way my neighbour is above all the *daughters a lily.* *As the lily.* Thou seekest rest and the praises of the *bed:* but know that thou art made whiter by the stings of tribulations, and that the fruit of preaching is greater than the fruit of quiet. *So is my love.* [n: (GREG.)] [1136B] In the Church neither can the evil be without the good, nor the good without the evil: he was not good who could not bear with the evil. By the dogmas of adverse heresies her wholeness is pricked as with thorns, that watching she may be exercised against her enemies, until, standing out as a lily upon the head of her Bridegroom, she be adorned with that very whiteness.

VERS. 3.-- *As the apple tree,* etc. The Bride, being praised and admonished by the Bridegroom's example to suffer, extols the Bridegroom with a praise in return, in that among the works of the good [singulariter] he likewise stands out as holy, and she promises that she will suffer what he suffers, under the presiding of him who is her refreshment and her refection. *As the apple tree.* The odour of ointments, although it smells sweetly, is nevertheless not sweet to eat. But an apple of the best savour and odour is such that it delights the throat with sweetness, and its smell [1136C] soothes the spirit. *Under the shadow.* Therefore do I set my beloved before all, because in the protection of his tenderness alone, for desire of which I was burning, I see that I have found a refreshment of tribulations, and I feel the most sweet fruit of his gifts, whereby I trust that I shall be refreshed for ever. Now she had desired to breathe again *under the shadow* of her author, when she complained that she was darkened by the sun of persecution, when, imploring the help of God, she cried: *Show me whom my soul loveth: where thou feedest, where thou liest in the midday;* when, not only wearied with the tedium of oppressions, but allured by the remembrance of his beauty and comeliness, she said: *Our bed is flourishing;* and she shows that she has been made possessed of that desire, saying: *Under the shadow.* And it is to be noted that above she was proclaiming *beams of cedar and ceilings of cypress,* yet she was not recording that the protection of these [1136D] sufficed for her; but she was seeking the tree of life alone, in whose shadow she might rest and by whose fruit she might be refreshed, because the saints bring their suffrages by examples, by doctrine and by intercession: yet to the Saviour alone have we to say, *But the children of men shall hope under the protection of thy wings [n: (Psal. XXXV)]*. [var: Vulg. Ps. 35:8 *in tegmine alarum tuarum*, "under the covering of thy wings"] *I sat down, and the fruit,* etc. She says not, I sit, but, *I sat down.* Such are the advances of the Churches, that in the beginning a man desires to sit down in the shadow of the virtues, that his body may come to him, from which the shadow is born: therefore also the nativity of Jesus began from a shadow, and is ended in truth. As it is said: *The Holy Spirit shall come upon thee, and the power of the Most High shall overshadow thee [n: (Luc. I)]*. So afterwards a passage is made to better things.

[1137A] *Sweet.* Because in it the soul understands the cause of life, while it perceives it also bodily.

VERS. 4.-- *He brought me in.* Of how great sweetness, of how great power this refection is, is shown when she subjoins, *He brought me in.* That is, he has so gladdened me with the hope of glory to come that I no longer feel the labours of the world. *He brought me in.* [n: (AMBR.)] As soon as my throat touched the sweetness of his grace, so do I feel myself refreshed in spirit and carried over from the love of earthly things into the things above, as if, brought into the wine cellar, I had been refreshed with the odour and the cup of new wine. Typically by *wine* the grace of the Holy Spirit is marked; the wine *cellar* is the Church, in whose unity alone the Holy Spirit is wont to be given and received; which Church, gathered out of the whole world, God built into one house for himself, and which the Holy [1137B] Spirit consecrated with his own gifts. Which building, because it stands upon the foundation of the greatest charity, by his own working, rightly is it subjoined: *He hath ordered charity in me.* The charity of many is disordered, that which is in the first place they put third or fourth. First, God is to be loved; secondly, parents; then children, afterwards those of the household, who also, if they are good, are to be set before evil children. According to this, in the Gospel he sets down what is proper to the love of each. *Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart, and with thy whole mind, and thy neighbour as thyself [n: (Marc. XII)]*. [var: Vulg. Marc. 12:30 has also *et ex tota anima tua* and *et ex tota virtute tua*, and puts *diliges proximum tuum tamquam teipsum* at 12:31] Enemies, not with thy whole strength, not as thyself, but simply: for it suffices that we love them, and hold them not in hatred. Or, he himself first loved me, and by loving gave me to know how to love him. Or, he ordered, that is, [1137C] charity being set in order, he joined to himself my several members, that is, all the elect; the more eminent he embraces with a larger affection. He shows how greatly charity lifts up the mind, which it has perfectly absorbed, from the love of weak things, when it is subjoined:

VERS. 5.-- *Stay me up.* Being now comforted by the things said above, she so burns into the love of God that, from her exceeding love or from the punishments inflicted upon her, a languor of the flesh is at hand. Or, *stay me up,* that is, be ye others, whether the more perfect or the beginners, a refreshment to me by working well, that I may rest in your good works, I who cannot yet rest by contemplating the face of God. Therefore *stay me up,* that you may have both a present and a future support, as I have in my languor. By how much she kindles herself to the love of the Bridegroom, by so much does she languish away from the love of temporal things: whence she subjoins: [1137D] *I languish.*

VERS. 6.-- *His left hand.* Seek not rest for thy head from any other quarter; for if thou hast his left hand, all things which are mine shall be granted to thee. *His left hand under my head.* When the hearts of the faithful in this life are comforted through the partaking of the sacraments, through the pledge of the Spirit, through the consolations of the Scriptures: which things also God gives for the support of their pilgrimage: *his right hand shall embrace,* when after this life we shall receive the heavenly rewards. The *left hand* (that is, the present life, and the temporal gifts of God), while it strengthens the mind through hope, is given *under the head* for consolation: and the *right hand,* that is, eternal life, *shall embrace,* as one protecting, when through recompense [1138A] he shall glorify us. As if: The temporal benefits of God (whereby I may rest a little from the covetings and disturbances of the world) help in this place of pilgrimage, and the promise of things eternal delights more amply.

VERS. 7.-- *I adjure you.* The primitive Church, turned to the Gentiles, and having spoken thus far as it were in their person, since from inward love she now suffers a failing of the body; the Bridegroom warns the new Church not to disquiet her by sinning. How lovely to God is the repose of this Church and of any faithful soul, is declared from the answer of the Bridegroom. *By the roes.* That is, if you would be comparable to *roes* or to *harts* in the height of virtues and in more precious food, and in the knowledge of what remedy is fitting for your wounds, if you have wounds. Roes and harts, clean animals, [1138B] enemies of poisons, chewing the cud, figure the works of spiritual virtues against the snares of the venomous enemy. Harts, departing from their country in the time of love, sustain themselves with the head of the one laid upon the haunches of the other, going in order, the first bears, the last only is borne, when the first is wearied the last succeeds, when dogs pursue them through the thickets, with their horns laid upon their backs they escape unhurt. When their eyes grow dim they eat a serpent, and having drunk of a fountain they recover their sight, and lay aside what is superfluous. So the saints for the love of God renounce earthly things: they bear one another's burdens, for those less burdened with sins lighten the burdened, by forgiving and by praying for them; they place beneath their mind the strength given through the Holy Spirit, while they are pressed by the wicked, and so [1138C] they pass through unhurt; they eat the devil, that is, they bring him to nothing within themselves, and, the fountain being drunk, that is, Christ, they recover their inward sight, and lay aside the skin, that is, they become free from sins. *That you stir not up.* [n: (GREG.)] If subjects sin in anything for which teachers are compelled to rise from their rest, they incur not only the penalty of that sin, but are also punished for the disquieting of the teachers, because he who presumes to disquiet teachers intent upon heavenly things loses even the virtues which he believed himself to have. At the oath of the Bridegroom the Bride gladly receives it, and hearing that determination added, namely, until she herself will, she perceives that it is secretly commanded her that she should of her own accord be willing to rise, she promises therefore that she will rise, and sets before herself such a Bridegroom, for whose sake she may will it, and a fitting time. [1138D] For after the apostles, set in the primitive Church of the Gentiles, had brought themselves and others into so great perfection that they were now free for contemplation, she understands again that a necessity has been laid upon her of running through the whole world and of building new Churches.

VERS. 8.-- *The voice of my beloved.* This is the voice which I heard adjuring the daughters of Jerusalem that they should not awaken me: I rejoice hearing his consoling voice, though I do not yet see his face; yet, my mind being lifted up to heaven, I taste beforehand some sweetness of the blessedness to come, even in this present life. *Leaping upon the mountains.* He leaps upon the mountains, and as it were sets his feet there, by which sign he is known to have been there, because it imitates him through his passion and through the manner of his life. He made a leap from heaven [1139A] into the womb, from the womb into the manger, from the manger into the cross, from the cross into the sepulchre, from the sepulchre he returned into heaven. He it is who is lifted up above all mountains and hills, that is, above the heights of the saints, both the greater and the lesser. *Skipping over the hills.* On the *hills* the marks of his feet do not appear, but they received the shadow of him flying over: because although not as those greater ones, yet in some measure they imitate him and are visited by him, *Mountains* and *hills* are those who by a singular purity of mind pass beyond the common manner of life of the Church, as it were the flowering plain of the fields, and, withdrawing from the lowest things, contemplate the things above more nearly. In whom he *leaps,* because he illumines the hearts of the lofty with frequent visitation; and he abides not, but *leaps* and *skips over:* because this sweetness of contemplation is short and rare, [1139B] because of the heaviness of the flesh, because the earthly habitation weighs down the mind that thinks upon many things.
