## CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 1.-- *How beautiful art thou.* Since the Bride converted from the Gentiles is now of such perfection that she preaches to the Jews, the case being reversed, and praises the Bridegroom: she too receives from the Bridegroom a worthy [1146B] recompense of praise. This description is extended to the manifoldness of the Church. *Beautiful* in action, because she goes up through the desert as a little rod of smoke, beautiful in preaching, because she draws her neighbours into her own fellowship. *Eyes.* That is, thy senses are excellent and to be revered in the contemplation of spiritual things. He sets about praising her part by part. *Besides that which lieth hid within.* Great is the glory of open work, but far and incomparably greater the eternal reward, which cannot be seen.

*Thy hair.* That all may the more easily grow clear, let us run briefly through the several things. *Hair,* are the voluntary poor. *Cheeks,* the highest prelates. But the *teeth* of the cheeks, are other, lower prelates. *Lips,* the preachers. *Thy hair.* If in the *eyes* the keenness of the spiritual senses is taken, in the *hair* may be noted the multitude of thoughts, [1146C] which all tend to the one end of the heavenly good. Whence they are likened to wild goats, which are clean animals, and seek the steep places of the rocks for the sake of pasture. *Which came up.* He sets down one of the natures of the wild goat, that from it the rest may be understood, and he proclaims that virtue especially of which he sees his hearers to have the greater need. *Which came up.* Of him who is a testimony to them that imitate him, as he himself promised: He who *shall confess me before men,* etc. [n: (Luc. XII.)]

VERS. 2.-- *Thy teeth as flocks of sheep that are shorn.* As in the hair the frailer peoples of the Church are signified, who supply her number: so in the teeth are signified all the stronger, and such as are fit for the ruling of the Church, who prevail by firmness. *From the washing, all.* It belongs to all to be purified by baptism: it belongs to the perfect, and most of all [1146D] to those to whom the care of the sheep has been committed, to renounce all that they possess. *With twin young.* That is, with young altogether like themselves, because they render others such as they themselves are. Or, twofold young, the works of the twin charity, as the faithful converted out of either people. Or, faith and work. *And there is none barren.* Nor are those reckoned among the barren who, once washed, are straightway snatched out of this life. For they had the offspring of faith, which either they themselves for themselves, or others for them, confessed. They had also the vow of good working, which they would have practised in fellowship among the sheep of Christ, had they been waited for, whence it is said: *Being made perfect in a short space, he fulfilled a long time [n: (Sap. IV)]*.

VERS. 3.-- *As a fillet.* Preachers are called *a fillet,* [1147A] because by their preachings they bind in the loose thoughts in the hearts of men, lest they flow abroad and weigh down the eyes of the heart. *Scarlet.* Because she does not cease to preach the price of the Lord's blood, by which the Church was redeemed: or because holy preaching blazes with the ardour of charity. *Thy lips.* The teeth are they that grind: the lips they that lay open what is ground. *Thy speech is sweet.* While they do the things they say, they set their preachings before men as it were savoury food. *As a piece of a pomegranate, so are the cheeks.* In the outwardly *crimson* colour, the will of martyrdom: in the white grains (which are seen when the rind is broken), the whiteness of the virtues: which is increased while the flesh is bruised: whiteness also, when, the passion being fulfilled, they shine with miracles. In the *cheeks* is modesty, in the *punic* apple the passion of Christ. The Church has therefore redness in her cheeks, [1147B] when she confesses in words the sacrament of the Lord's passion, and shows whiteness when, beaten with afflictions, she proves the chastity of a pure heart, and by deeds lays open what of saving grace the cross has. *Besides that which lieth hid within.* Although great are the things that appear without: yet greater are those they keep in secret, which the eyes of God alone behold. *Lieth hid.* All in the Church can hear the confession of the life-giving cross, can see the afflictions of the Church, or even the miracles which have been divinely bestowed upon the Church herself, unbelievers together with the faithful can look upon: but the soul alone knows with how great a love of the invisible life she is held, with how great a love toward God and her neighbour she blazes.

VERS. 4.-- *As the tower of David.* The tower is the stormable [cj: *expugnabilis*; read *inexpugnabilis*, "unstormable"] constancy of the preachers, which, for the defending [1147C] of the buildings of the faith and for the driving back of the darts of the enemy, was built by the strong hand of God and by the desirable king *with bulwarks:* that is, with the knowledge of the divine Scriptures, when he opens the understanding, that they may understand the Scriptures, and by the sayings of them that went before they confirm their own preaching, the *bulwarks* are also the gifts of miracles, by which preaching is built up. For when bodily diseases are healed, men come more easily to the health of the soul. *Thy neck.* We stretch out the neck when we look further off: so the preachers are a neck, because of their watching for the enemy from afar: *a tower,* because of their strength and because of the lofty contemplation of the heavenly joys. Or, *a neck,* because they send food down into the body, or because they join the body to the head: whence, the Lord gives the loaves, but the disciples set them before the crowds. [1147D] For one reason the preachers are called *eyes;* for another, *teeth:* for another, *cheeks:* for another, *neck.* *Bulwarks* are the unbreakable sentences in the divine Scriptures, in which they are safe who defend themselves from afar against the assailants. *A thousand bucklers.* The buckler is that most firm reasoning, relying upon which they go forth of their own accord against the heretics, and fortify themselves while they smite them. *All the armour.* That is, all the equipping, whether of working or of heavenly teaching, by which the saints not only escape, but even overcome the ranks of the malignant: while they draw back some also from error or from vices: all which things depend upon the prelates, because from them we receive the example of resisting and of conquering.

[1148A]VERS. 5.-- *Two breasts.* The same mysteries of the Church are repeated under many figures; but the things repeated always bring something new, and by that very newness delight the minds of the hearers the more. *Two* therefore *breasts,* are the preachers from the two peoples, who are called *eyes* because they make progress in secret. *Teeth,* because they correct the wicked, and pass them over into the body of the Church: *a neck,* because they minister the breath of life, by preaching eternal joys, and the food of doctrine. *Breasts,* because they pour milk into the little ones in Christ, who are *fawns of the roe,* because they discern keenly what things are to be done, and with a swift course of working fly away out of the vale of tears. *Twins,* because they preach the same faith, the same sacraments: who, lest the milk they give out should fail in them, are fed with the flowers of the Scriptures and the examples of the saints, [1148B] by always calling these to mind. *Two fawns* Two peoples, who through humility understand themselves to be little and sinners, but, running in charity, pass over all the obstacles of the world, and by the leaps of contemplation climb to things above. *Which feed.* Because they have their refreshment in those who flourish with virtues and shine, while as yet they cannot see near at hand the very God whom they desire.

VERS. 6.-- *Until that day break.* [var: Vulg. Ct 4:6 *Donec aspiret dies*, without *illa*, "that"] After which there shall be time neither for teaching further nor for learning, but, all the darknesses of this world being ended, all shall shine as the sun. *I will go to the mountain.* And because the Church is such in every part. *I will go,* that is, I will add grace, as well to the lower as to the greater. *I will go.* Because I shall daily gather new peoples. And because [1148C] the discourse is to the Church of the Gentiles, the calling of the Synagogue, which is to be at the end of the world, may not incongruously be seen to be promised: or the calling of whatsoever nations. Whence presently, concerning the most ample beauty of the one and the same Church, which is to be gathered either from the Jews or from whatsoever nations throughout the whole world, it is added, *Thou art all fair.*

VERS. 7.-- *Thou art all fair.* Not only in the more choice members which I have enumerated, but also in those who seem little and frail.

VERS. 8.-- *Come from Libanus.* [n: (GREG.)] And because I go to thee, thou oughtest to come to me, in thought, in speech, in work. Or, the first step is in the flesh for good work: the second in the loosing from the flesh, to receive blessedness in the soul: the third, when the body has been received back, [1148D] when at the resurrection they shall receive the twofold robes. *Come* by faith, *come* by work, *come* by bringing others: or, come three times because of the faith of the Trinity. *Libanus,* a mountain of Phoenicia, is in Hebrew rendered *whitening,* in Greek *frankincense:* whence above, *I will go to the mountain of myrrh and to the hill of frankincense.* While the preachers convert such men to salvation, the crown grows from the princes overcome in their contest. *Come.* As if to say: fear not, lest thou come alone: for I will add many to thee. *From the top of Amana.* Amana is a mountain of Cilicia, Sanir and Hermon mountains of Judaea, in which lions dwell, by whom the demons are noted, who rage against us: and because those are converted whose hearts were the couches of lions, while in their turn by their confession the Lord is believed, [1149A] they are as it were crowned out of the couches of lions, out of which the lion is driven. By Amana we have those whom the devil himself, and through whom he deceives and crushes others, by the watchfulness of great diligence. By Sanir, those to whom the ignorance of such deceiving flies. By Hermon, such as are as it were ready by nature for wickedness, not what the devil designs. By *the den of lions,* those over whom, and through whom over others, a persecuting cruelty holds dominion. By *leopards,* heretics, by whose distinct wiles he deceives, from the chief ones of each of whom God confers a crown of victory upon his own. *From the dens of lions. Lions,* because of pride, or of violence. *Leopards* because of cruelty, or of the variegating of malignant arts. By the name *of the mountains* are designated the proud hearts of unbelievers.

[1149B]VERS. 9.-- *Thou hast wounded the heart.* Therefore oughtest thou to come, and to labour for the gaining of others, because I love thee. Or, therefore will I give thee a crown out of those men, because thou hast prepared for me in thyself a place of love. All thy beauty pleases me, but most of all the catholic unity pleases me, which I see both in the prelates and in the subjects.

VERS. 10.-- *How beautiful are,* etc. Above the Bride praised the breasts in her beloved, here the beloved commends the breasts: in which is noted the unity of Christ and of the Church, because they are two, as the Apostle says, in one flesh: therefore with a like praise the one praises the other. The doctrine is Christ's, because it is given to the Church, because she ministers it. And with reason do I love thee, because thy doctrine, which is contained in the breasts above, is beautiful for the consoling of the weak and the sustaining of little ones. *Breasts,* [1149C] the love of God and of the neighbour: by which the holy mind nourishes its own senses, while it is glued to God by charity, and bestows upon its neighbours what it can. How greatly the Bridegroom loves these breasts he shows in the repetition of the praise, saying: *Thy breasts are more beautiful,* etc. *The odour.* That is, the most sweet report of the faith scattered through the whole world, is broader than the law of the fathers, which was confined within Judaea alone. *Above all aromatical spices.* That is, above all the good works which the wicked do, which, although they sometimes please men, yet never please God, because they do not proceed from charity. Or, above the institutions of the Old Testament, which were a figure of these.
