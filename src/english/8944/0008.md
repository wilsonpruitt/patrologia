## CHAPTER V.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Let the beloved come.* ⟦s2⟧ He who promised that he would be *with me even to the consummation of the world,* [var: Vulg. Mt 28:20 *ego vobiscum sum omnibus diebus, usque ad consummationem saeculi*, "I am with you"] now, when I have the more need of greater grace, let him visit me. ⟦s3⟧ *Let the beloved come.* ⟦s4⟧ The Church, hearing that she is to be proved by temptations, does not gainsay the beloved's provision: but, lest she be overcome, she seeks the help of him who *doth not suffer us to be tempted above that which we are able. [n: (I Cor. X.)]* [var: Vulg. 1Cor 10:13 *non patietur vos tentari supra id quod potestis*, "will not suffer you … above that which you are able"] Or, it can be understood thus, as spoken by the voice of the perfect. ⟦s5⟧ *Let the beloved come.* ⟦s6⟧ As if to say: As we always love him, and take care to serve him also: so let him not in temptations suffer [1152C] us to be shaken even unto failing, but let him now render us the wage of our devout service. ⟦s7⟧ *I am come into the garden.* ⟦s8⟧ The Bridegroom, answering the desire of the Church, testifies that he has already done the very things that were asked: whence strangers may be much kindled to love of him. ⟦s9⟧ *I am come,* by correcting the erring, by lifting up the weak, by strengthening those who do well, by setting the ripe with me in rest. ⟦s10⟧ *I have reaped.* ⟦s11⟧ With a certain sickle of death I cut them off from this life, and led them into the storerooms of blessedness. ⟦s12⟧ *I have eaten the honeycomb.* ⟦s13⟧ By *the honeycomb,* those who in the Scriptures seek the sweetness of spiritual understanding, and make it clear to others; by *honey,* those who gladly taste the feasts of the divine word set before them, and desire to be fed insatiably on them; by *wine,* the strong preachers of heavenly things; by *milk,* hearers as yet weak, who, although they differ in diverse ways of pursuit, [1152D] yet share alike one hope of the reward above, and the judge above approves them. ⟦s14⟧ *Eat, O friends.* ⟦s15⟧ Not only is he himself delighted with the devout acts of the elect: but he also exhorts the faithful to rejoice together over such a banquet, that they may show forth among themselves a greater mutual care of love, and that the lesser may busily imitate, keeping it in memory, what they have seen in the greater. ⟦s16⟧ *Be inebriated, dearly beloved.* [n: (GREG.)] After the draught of joy, by going over again the very virtues of the good, shake off the torpor of the mind, and, kindled to the love of eternal things, feel not temporal punishments; like a drunken man, who forgets all temporal things; he eats, and is not filled: who has learned the feasts of the Scriptures, has known the examples of the just, and yet has not amended his life; he drinks, and is not inebriated, [1153A] who joyfully hears the precepts of life, but remains slothful to fulfil them.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *I sleep.* ⟦s2⟧ Lest the Church of the Gentiles, commended in so many ways, should in her security let go preaching and attend to contemplation alone, the confirming of others is enjoined upon her. ⟦s3⟧ And here he treats of that succession of the Church which is in this present world, namely from that time when the fulness of the Gentiles had entered, even to the end of the world, when the Jews shall enter; and here she seems to be sent not, to unbelievers of every kind, but rather to exhort those who are lukewarm in the Church, and so the Church herself says: *I sleep. ⟦s4⟧ Open to me, my sister,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Rise from the ease and quiet of contemplation, and open their hearts, that, the obstacles of vices being removed, they may clearly let in the light of truth. [1153B] Of my blood, *my sister;* by my approach, *my near one;* [var: Vulg. Ct 5:2 *amica mea*, "my friend"] of my spirit, *my dove;* of my word, which thou hast learned more fully out of thy leisure, *my perfect one. ⟦s6+7⟧ My head.* [var: Vulg. Ct 5:2 *immaculata mea*, "my undefiled"] That is, my faith, and the love of me, has grown cold in their hearts, blinded by the love of earthly things, and has as it were altogether failed. ⟦s8⟧ *My locks.* ⟦s9⟧ That is, the love of my neighbours, who are my hairs clinging to the head: *are full of the drops of the nights,* that is, they suffer the heavy cold of the persecution of the darkened, that is, of those who are both unbelieving toward me and persecute their neighbours; and for the sake of correcting these thou must interrupt (interpolare) the sweetness of contemplation.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *I have stripped myself.* ⟦s2⟧ The Church, not disobedient concerning the office enjoined, but fearing, in taking up the care of the brethren, lest she fall into the sins without which [1153C] this life is not passed through, answers: *I have stripped myself.* ⟦s3⟧ How can it come to pass that, going out from the height of contemplation, I should again be defiled with the filth of the world, since, when the care of temporal helps has been taken up, however excellent a teacher a man be, he hardly avoids sin. [n: (GREG.)] He who girds himself to the care of ruling souls, it remains that he also watch, for those to whom he preaches eternal things, to provide for them the helps of temporal necessities.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *My beloved.* ⟦s2⟧ When the Church fears a fall, because the Lord wills that our love toward him be known in the greatest love of our neighbours, it is fitly added: *My beloved,* as if to say: I do indeed greatly dread converse with the world; but I understand that charity covers a multitude of sins; and I trust in the help of my beloved, by whom their hearts have already been pricked. [1153D] *His hand.* ⟦s3⟧ That is, the compunction that works, or, he has recalled to our memory the work which he wrought in us, because, needing nothing, going forth from the Father's bosom, he willed to dwell on earth for us. ⟦s4⟧ He sends *his hand* when by his power he knocks at the soul through a subtle understanding. ⟦s5⟧ *And the belly.* ⟦s6⟧ By the belly may be understood the softness and frailty of those who take up the office of preaching, because they recognize themselves as less fit, and are afraid. ⟦s7⟧ *The belly.* ⟦s8⟧ That is, the soft and flowing part among us; or, by the name of the belly the heart is designated, which, as food in the belly, so thoughts in the heart are cooked. ⟦s9⟧ The heart trembles if it refuses to do for its neighbours what God did for his enemies. ⟦s10⟧ And the belly trembles, because weakness, rejoicing [1154A] in a heavenly understanding, is troubled in its own exultation, and fears to lose what it knows not how to hold.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *I arose, that I might open.* ⟦s2⟧ I rose from sleeping to labour; because it is necessary that he who purposes to preach the truth should first rise up to do the things he preaches, lest, preaching to others, he himself be found a castaway; to whom the words subjoined belong, *My hands dropped,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ *And my fingers are full of myrrh.* ⟦s4⟧ The fingers, that is, discretion, that they may be shown to work with a view to the reward above alone, not for human advantage. ⟦s5⟧ For as in the hand the fingers are distinct: so in works discretion must be used, otherwise they are of no avail.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *The bolt of the door.* ⟦s2⟧ The cause of sin, that is, unbelief, and any other whatsoever: because, as the bolt holds the [1154B] door, so the cause holds the sin, and when the cause is removed it is removed, and when the sin is taken away the way lies open for Christ. ⟦s3⟧ She had said that her beloved, having put his hand through the hole, had touched her and shaken her with trembling: and being vehemently inflamed by his touch, she now desires to be touched by his hand no longer through the narrowness of the hole, but, the door of the heart being thrown open, to enjoy his most blessed embrace, that is, to be more fully filled with the sweetness of the divine enlightening which she had hastily felt; but because to none of the elect is granted in this life the perfect vision of eternal things, which is reserved for the other life, it is rightly added: *But he had turned aside. ⟦s4⟧ My soul,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Behold now the third step: the first was converted from among the Jews; through which, and after which, came this Gentile Church, of which we have treated hitherto; which Gentile Church, as we have said, established this third one, of which he now [1154C] pursues the account: which shall endure, by the succession of prelates and of subjects, even to the last entering in of the remnants of Israel. ⟦s6⟧ Pass over indeed, but yet he spoke to me, leaving his commandments, that I should patiently wait for him: and after he had spoken, *My soul melted. ⟦s7⟧ I sought and found him not: I called,* etc. ⟦s8⟧ The voice of the same Church, which opened, according to those whom she opened: which before was the belly or the voice of those same men.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *They took the cloak.* [n: (GREG.)] Preachers take away the cloak when, if anything of worldly pomp was left over, they take it away from the soul, or, if any sin was within through ignorance, they uncover it.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *I adjure you, O daughters of Jerusalem, if you find,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The maidens, now advanced to some [1154D] knowledge, come devoutly to the servants of God, who they believe lead an angelic life on earth, and beg them that in the time of their prayer they commend them to the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ You daughters of Jerusalem of the spirits above, who already reign with God. ⟦s4⟧ Or, by the daughters of Jerusalem we can understand also the lower in the Church, whom their superiors likewise admonish to seek together with them: because what is sought by many is sometimes the sooner found, and that everywhere the charity of love may be shown.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *What manner of one is the beloved.* ⟦s2⟧ This Bride, calling to mind the inmost things of her conscience, is greatly afraid, and, accusing her own sloth, hastens out of humility to labour, saying: I beseech thee, since thou hast so adjured us by the fervour of the divine [1155A] charity which thou hast, make me also to burn, and confirm me by a saving admonition in his love. ⟦s3⟧ And singing fitly, with due praise, the proclamations of his virtues and of his power, she is questioned anew in what manner of minds his footprints are wont to be found.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *My beloved is white and ruddy.* ⟦s2⟧ A worthy answer on the Bride's part to those who ask. ⟦s3⟧ *My beloved.* ⟦s4⟧ First, clean and holy, he came into the world, afterward, bloody with his passion, he went out of the world. ⟦s5⟧ By *white,* take the love of the Godhead; by *ruddy,* the flesh that suffered, so that it may denote God and man, and in both *chosen. ⟦s6⟧ Chosen out of thousands.* ⟦s7⟧ Because he alone is the Mediator accepted out of the whole number of angels or of men; whence it is said: *This is my beloved Son,* etc. [n: (Matth. III.)] Because there was not [1155B] *found another, neither in heaven, nor on earth, who should receive the book [n: (Apoc. V)]*. [var: Vulg. Apc 5:3–4 *nemo poterat neque in caelo, neque in terra … aperire librum*; *nemo dignus inventus est aperire librum*, "open the book"]

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *His head is the best gold.* ⟦s2⟧ Another translation: *His hairs are fir trees.* ⟦s3⟧ The fir tree is in Greek called ἐλατη. ⟦s4⟧ Ude, here elate seems not Latin, but Greek. ⟦s5⟧ Or the head, that is, the Godhead, because *the head of Christ is God [n: (Cor. II)]*. ⟦s6⟧ *His locks,* that is, his thoughts, holy and upright, and yet contemptible to fools. ⟦s7⟧ *Black as a raven.* ⟦s8⟧ Contemptible to the world, like that clamorous and shameless bird. ⟦s9⟧ Or, *as a raven,* that they may commit the sacraments of the Church to none until they see them made like to themselves or to their mother, or, because, when they are fair, they understand that they were black by their own nature.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *His eyes as doves.* [n: (GREG.)] Doves are wont to settle beside the streams of waters, that [1155C] they may see in the water the shadow of the birds that fly over, and so avoid their talons: so the saints look out in the Scriptures for the frauds of the demons, and from the deception which they mark, as from a shadow, they know the enemy and flee, which streams are called most full, because concerning whatsoever things counsel is sought in Scripture, through her it is found in full. ⟦s2⟧ By the little rivers may be understood the learning of the old law; by the streams, evangelical perfection: because preachers bring forth both new things and old. ⟦s3⟧ *His eyes.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, the gifts of the Holy Spirit, which he himself gives, which do not suffer their lovers to be rolled in the mire after the manner of swine, but render them clear and transparent in the likeness of waters, having nothing dark. ⟦s5⟧ Whence they are compared both to little rivers and to clean waters, not to troubled pools, because they see God, and are filled with the light of the divine [1155D] graces. ⟦s6⟧ Which graces, because they are given by grace alone, it is added: *Which are washed with milk.* ⟦s7⟧ Because, as the mother by natural love alone supplies milk to her sons, so God by grace alone gives the gifts of graces to his sons. ⟦s8⟧ Which settle beside the most full streams of waters: because the more clearly they see concerning God, the more they strive after the knowing of higher things.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Cheeks.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, the modesty and mercy of the Saviour, which shone forth in him in a singular way. ⟦s3⟧ *As garden beds.* ⟦s4⟧ Even as garden beds are set in order by men who look for the sweetness of the odour and the grace of the sight, so the Mediator, appearing in the flesh, delighted those present by the modesty and sweetness of his doctrine, and drew those who were absent: *set by the perfumers;* that is, by [1156A] the apostles and the prophets, who with one accord described in the sacred pages not only his discourses but also his manners, in the hidden mystery of his incarnation to come, out of modesty he wept for Lazarus, was dumb as a lamb, and the like. ⟦s5⟧ *His lips are lilies dropping myrrh.* ⟦s6⟧ The words of his doctrine are *lilies* which promise the brightness of the heavenly kingdom, grievous to them that suffer, *the first myrrh,* because they preach the contempt of pleasures.
