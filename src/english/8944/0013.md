## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Who shall give me.* ⟦s2⟧ Judaea is brought in, now inspired and taught, and she searches out God with the highest desire. ⟦s3⟧ *Without.* ⟦s4⟧ The beloved was within, while *in the beginning was the Word:* without, while *the Word was made flesh [n: (Joan. I)]*. ⟦s5⟧ *That I may kiss thee,* etc. ⟦s6⟧ That is, that I may see face to face, and speak mouth to mouth.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *There thou shalt teach me.* ⟦s2⟧ Thou wilt increase grace in [1164C] me, and I, not ungrateful, will increase my service.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *His left hand.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, the mystery of the incarnation, and the gifts which he gives in the present, shall be under her head: because, sustained by them in the present, she rests against worldly disturbances, and in a manner sleeps with him; *and his right hand:* that is, the good things to come, that is, the full vision of himself, *shall embrace me,* that is, shall so surround me on every side that no whirlwind of misery can come between.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *I adjure you.* ⟦s2⟧ The Bridegroom, hearing that that last Church wishes both to sleep in the present and one day to rest; and seeing that many are born of the Church who disturb others; admonishes them to desist from their disturbings. ⟦s3⟧ *Till she please.* ⟦s4⟧ Until, the services of the divine ministry being fulfilled, she herself, by the necessities of human frailty [1164D] admonished, consents to return to the common care.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Who is this?* ⟦s2⟧ While Judaea burns with so great a love of the Bridegroom now acknowledged, the Gentile, wondering and rejoicing with her, says: *Who is this? ⟦s3⟧ Under the tree.* ⟦s4⟧ To the wondering Gentiles he answers, he who is our peace, who made both one: and he admonishes her to remember the grace by which she herself was snatched away, and to rejoice together at the salvation of others. ⟦s5⟧ *I raised thee up.* ⟦s6⟧ Which was the effect of great kindness, because at that tree thy mother was inwardly blinded, from the time she said: *His blood be upon us, and upon our children [n: (Matth. XXVII)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Set me.* ⟦s2⟧ We set Christ *as a seal upon the heart,* when we so learn the things which he said as though they were truly the words of the divine wisdom. ⟦s3⟧ We are wont to carry about some sign [1165A] bound upon the finger or the arm, whereby we may be put in mind of that thing whereof we wish to have the more frequent remembrance. ⟦s4⟧ We set him, as a sign, upon the arm, when we so strive to hear and to follow the things which he did, as though they were examples of true virtue. ⟦s5⟧ *For love is strong as death.* ⟦s6⟧ After the corruption of the mother, he fitly subjoins concerning love, that it may be shown to have been given to the Church not out of merits. ⟦s7⟧ *As death.* ⟦s8⟧ As death separates the soul from the body, so does the love of God [separate it] from the love of temporal things. ⟦s9⟧ *Hard as hell,* etc. ⟦s10⟧ Because by no temptations of the enemy could it be called back from the care of our salvation: as hell is softened by no torments of the wretched, nor is it changed from the severity of its sentence. ⟦s11⟧ The harsher the comparison, the keener is the affection of the Lord who is jealous for us commended. ⟦s12⟧ *The lamps thereof.* ⟦s13⟧ That [1165B] is, the hearts of the faithful, in which love is contained as in a vessel, are *fire:* because inwardly they glow with the love of God and of their neighbour. ⟦s14⟧ They are also *a flame,* because outwardly they shine with the works of justice. ⟦s15⟧ These lamps are not put out at the coming of the Bridegroom.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Many waters,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because the house which is founded upon the rock of charity cannot fall when the floods rush upon it. ⟦s3⟧ *The floods.* ⟦s4⟧ Of temptations, which either visibly or invisibly tempt the hearts of the faithful. ⟦s5⟧ *If a man shall give.* ⟦s6⟧ Because where the love of eternal things abounds, the possession of things that pass away soon grows worthless, it is rightly subjoined, *If a man shall give.* ⟦s7⟧ The apostles, and the other saints, having let all things go, seemed to themselves to have lost nothing, provided they might have the true goods, whence Paul: *I count all losses as dung, that I may gain Christ* [1165C] [n: (Philip. III)]. [var: Vulg. Phlp 3:8 *propter quem omnia detrimentum feci, et arbitror ut stercora, ut Christum lucrifaciam*]

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Our sister,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ O you ministers and fellow soldiers of mine, this Church is new and tender for the retaining of a new love. ⟦s3⟧ *Our sister is little.* ⟦s4⟧ The Church, before she grows through the increase of virtues, cannot offer the breasts of preaching to weak hearers; she is called grown up when, joined to the word of God, filled with the Holy Spirit, she is defiled through the ministry of preaching in the conceiving of sons, whom by exhorting she travails with, and by converting she brings forth. ⟦s5⟧ *What shall we do to our sister,* etc. ⟦s6⟧ As if he should say openly: Small indeed in number is the Church of the Gentiles, and she does not yet suffice to undergo the ministry of the word. ⟦s7⟧ What then seems to thee, O Synagogue, to be done concerning the care of her, our sister that is, at that time when [1165D] I shall have begun to speak to her through my apostles and the successors of the apostles? ⟦s8⟧ Whether, as to one still little, we should commit to her small secrets of the heavenly mysteries, or should now render her greater by increase, so that, advancing well, she may be able to be capable of more perfect virtues? ⟦s9⟧ And to her, keeping silence, and rather listening for what he himself may will, he straightway discloses what it is fitting should be done, subjoining thus:

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *If she be a wall,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ If any one shall be found who can defend others from the incursions of them that err, by bravely resisting the arms of wickedness, whether fortified by the talent of nature, or steeped in philosophic knowledge: let us form him by the divine Scriptures. ⟦s3⟧ *If a door.* ⟦s4⟧ If there be any not so far instructed for repelling [1166A] the sophisms of heretics, but yet able, by the simplicity of faith, to open the right way by preaching to those who wish to enter the courts of the heavenly country, and by instructing little ones: let us set before him the unfading examples of the men of old, whereby he may the better fulfil his office.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *I am a wall.* ⟦s2⟧ Now he speaks generally of the whole Church begun from the foundation of the apostles, because from that time there were breasts in the Church, even unto the last times. ⟦s3⟧ *I am a wall.* ⟦s4⟧ The Church, hearing these counsels or promises of her Redeemer, now one does not wait for another, but each answers for himself with a devout voice. ⟦s5⟧ *Since which.* ⟦s6⟧ All these things I could not have of myself, but since he bestowed on me the gift of his peace, through the word of his reconciliation. ⟦s7⟧ *As one [1166B] finding peace.* ⟦s8⟧ Well do I say, finding peace, because in that peace which I have spoken of the vineyard was established for *the peaceable* Christ: which of old was foretold by the law and the prophets. ⟦s9⟧ Which peace contains not one nation of the Jews only, but has many peoples gathered out of the whole world, not by any merits of men, but by that peace alone which he came to make between God and man: and since that vineyard is the Lord's care, and since it is profitable to men that they be made partakers of it, it is rightly subjoined:

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *He delivered it to keepers.* ⟦s2⟧ There follows the voice of that peaceable one himself, which shows how great care he has for this vineyard, and what he keeps for ever for those who leave their own things, and what special reward he appoints for its teachers.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *A thousand are thine, O peaceable one.* ⟦s2⟧ A perfect price [1166C] is whatsoever it has. ⟦s3⟧ By *a thousand,* perfection; by *silver,* everything worldly is understood: which indeed is sometimes small in thee, but great by devotion. ⟦s4⟧ *A thousand,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ The same perfection which is signified by *a thousand* is noted also by *a hundred*, because either number is perfect, and it is the same with *two hundred,* as if he had said two thousand. ⟦s6⟧ *A thousand,* etc. ⟦s7⟧ The same thing is *pieces of silver* and *peaceable ones:* because money paid out for God reconciles peace between God and man.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Thou that dwellest in the gardens.* ⟦s2⟧ The Church, or any faithful soul, dwells in gardens; she who is now filled with the greenness of hope and of good works. ⟦s3⟧ For dry is the hope of this world, because all things that are loved here wither with haste: *She therefore that dwells in the gardens,* it behoves that, for the Bridegroom, she *make her voice to be heard [1166D],* that is, that she send forth the good things of preaching in so far as he whom she desires is delighted; because *the friends hearken,* namely all the elect, who, that they may live again unto the heavenly country, desire to hear the words of life. ⟦s4⟧ *Thou that dwellest.* ⟦s5⟧ And since with me a reward is kept for each one according to his merits, and a twofold recompense for the teachers: therefore, while the time of meriting is at hand, while the wedding day is deferred, let each one, as much as he is able, persist in the tilling of this vineyard. ⟦s6⟧ *The friends.* ⟦s7⟧ That is, the angels, whom I have given thee as helpers; and the spirits of the just, who are already with God, and pray for their brethren. ⟦s8⟧ *Make me hear.* ⟦s9⟧ I require of thee that thou dwell, one that will depart for no tumult; and afterwards I also require that thou make me hear thy voice.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1167A]VERS. 14.-- *Flee, my beloved.* ⟦s2⟧ The Church, assenting, answers: As long as thou wilt, be absent: defer thy presence; I with long-suffering hope will bear or do whatsoever thou shalt command. ⟦s3⟧ *Flee, my beloved.* ⟦s4⟧ She speaks not by way of wishing, for who ought to flee him whom he loves? but mindful of her own condition, she consents to his will. ⟦s5⟧ *Flee, my beloved.* ⟦s6⟧ As if: Thou indeed, who from the flesh hast been made comprehensible, from thy divinity surpass the understanding of our sense, and in thyself remain incomprehensible to us. ⟦s7⟧ This the Church cries to the Lord [1168A] in a prophetic spirit, after she has described the death and resurrection of the Lord and his ascension. ⟦s8⟧ He is said to flee us, when that which we wish to remember does not come to mind: when we do not hold in memory that which we wish. ⟦s9⟧ *Be thou like.* ⟦s10⟧ So flee, that most frequently, through the grace of compunction, thou mayest appear to the hearts of the faithful; who are *the mountains of spices,* because, all covetings being despised and their vices purged away, they are held by the love of heavenly things, and are fragrant with the odour of virtues, and are the good odour of Christ.
