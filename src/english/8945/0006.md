## CHAPTER X.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *And he wrote in. [n: (AUG. quaest. 14 in Deut.)]* In Exodus Moses, etc., *as far as* but he himself helps the choice of a good will.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *In Mosera,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In the book of Numbers he is written to have died upon [0462B] mount Hor, but perhaps the place is of two names. ⟦s3⟧ For Beroth of the children of Jacan is a place in the desert, as is Mosera, where Aaron died, as is read in the books of places. ⟦s4⟧ And it is shown unto this day at the tenth milestone from the city of Petra, upon the top of the mountain

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *The tribe of Levi.* ⟦s2⟧ Unless by this were signified the whole royal priesthood which pertains to the New Testament, a man who was not of that tribe would not dare to say: *The Lord is my portion [n: (Psal. LXXII)]*; and elsewhere: *The Lord is the portion of my inheritance,* etc. [n: (Ibid., XV.)]

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *The heaven of heaven,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Some say that there are three heavens: the airy, the ethereal, and the starry. ⟦s3⟧ Not a few say seven: the first airy, the second ethereal, the third olympian, the fourth fiery, the fifth the firmament, [0462C] the sixth watery, the seventh of the angels.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Circumcise,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It signifies spiritual circumcision, that is, the keeping of the mind and true abstinence. ⟦s3⟧ As if to say: when you have extinguished the wantonness of the flesh, cut away also the superfluities of the mind. ⟦s4⟧ All the senses are to be circumcised. ⟦s5⟧ Whence Moses complains that he is uncircumcised in his lips; and Stephen to the Jews: *Uncircumcised,* he says, *in heart and ears,* etc. [n: (Act. VII)]. ⟦s6⟧ Truly therefore he is circumcised who *stoppeth his ears that he may not hear blood, and shutteth his eyes that he may not see evil;* who does not offend with his tongue, whose heart is not weighed down with surfeiting and drunkenness, who washeth his hands among the innocent, and keepeth back his feet from every evil way; who chastiseth his body and bringeth it [0462D] into subjection, and with all watchfulness keepeth his heart. ⟦s7⟧ We need a twofold circumcision also, lest out of these things we seek vainglory, lest we be clean without and unclean within, and undergo the penalties of a feigned holiness, like the Sichemites, who imitated the circumcision of the patriarchs, not for the love of God, but for the cause of wantonness: therefore they gained nothing of reward, but on the third day perished within the ruins of their own city. ⟦s8⟧ For such men, at the time of the resurrection, when the virtues in which they trusted have been emptied out, die for ever.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Thy fathers went down into Egypt.* ⟦s2⟧ There is a going down into Egypt: *Easy is the descent of Avernus.* ⟦s3⟧ There is a going up into Jerusalem, because narrow is the way that leadeth to life, and virtue makes for the steep

## CHAPTER XI.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0463A]

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Out of which thou camest.* ⟦s2⟧ In mind, with Moses for leader, that is, with the law teaching us to despise earthly pleasures and to enter the land of promise with Jesus for leader.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of gardens.* ⟦s2⟧ Because all carnal men desire to multiply for themselves present happiness and an abundance of temporal things.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *From the beginning of the year.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, from the beginning of a good will unto the perfection of good work he directs the course of life. ⟦s3⟧ *For it is God who worketh in us to will and to accomplish, according to good will [n: (Philip. II)]*.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *The early,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because in the former time he bestowed upon his elect the understanding of the law; he granted the latter rain, because in the last time he laid open to us the mystery of his incarnation.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0463B] *And the latter,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because it is the last hour when his preaching reaches us, who says: *The lifting up of my hands, an evening sacrifice [n: (Psal. CXL)]*.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Thou shalt write them upon the door posts. [n: (AUG., quaest. 17.)]* Is this according to the letter, or is it a hyperbolical commendation, as many things are said?

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Mount Garizim.* ⟦s2⟧ Garizim is interpreted Division or Stranger; Hebal is interpreted Old Gulf. ⟦s3⟧ For they who separate themselves from vices, and judge themselves strangers and pilgrims in this world, merit also the eternal blessing. ⟦s4⟧ But they who cast themselves headlong into the gulf of the old life, serving carnal desires, shall lie under the eternal curse

## CHAPTER XII.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0463C]

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Tithes and firstfruits,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 18.)] It is to be asked how he commands that the tithings of all fruits and the firstlings of all cattle be not eaten save in the city where the temple was, whereas in the law he had commanded them to be given to the Levites.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Beware lest thou offer.* ⟦s2⟧ He cuts off the occasion of sacrificing to idols. ⟦s3⟧ For he therefore commands that victims be offered in the temple and at the altar of the Lord, that where religion is whole, there a fitting worship may be shown. ⟦s4⟧ But mystically he intimates that no gift is acceptable to God outside the catholic Church; for this is the place which God chose, that he might put his name [0463D] there, and dwell in it.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Without eating.* ⟦s2⟧ He forbids murder. ⟦s3⟧ The blood is put for the soul, because the seat of the soul, that is, of human life, is in the blood.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *In thy towns.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, thou shalt not assign to thy own merits the things thou hast received by the divine bounty, not to thy own strength, but to God, by whose gift thou hast had this [n: (Gal. VI)].

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *This only beware.* ⟦s2⟧ According to the letter the apostles commanded this: and in the blood both murder and the hatred of brethren are forbidden. ⟦s3⟧ For he that hateth his brother is a murderer [n: (I Joan. III)].

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For their blood.* ⟦s2⟧ Because there is something vital in the blood, and by it chiefly life is lived in this flesh, [0464A] which is diffused through all the veins: this he calls the soul, that is, the life of the body, which is ended by death. ⟦s3⟧ But mystically it is intimated that, when carnal men depart from their former manner of life and join themselves in the unity of faith and of good works, we should not turn their errors to our own use, but should pour them out, that is, should reckon them, brought forth by confession, to earthly frailty, and cast them away as unworthy of spiritual men.

## CHAPTER XIII.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *If there rise up.* ⟦s2⟧ He would have it understood that the things which are spoken by diviners not according to God, even if they come to pass, are not so to be received that the things foretold by them should come about; nor does he show that the things which so happen are beyond his own power. ⟦s3⟧ But he subjoins why he permits it, [0464B] namely, for the knowing how much they that serve them love God — not for him who knows all things.
