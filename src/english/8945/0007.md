## CHAPTER XIV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *You shall not cut yourselves.* ⟦s2⟧ Unto this day many nations cut themselves at the funerals of the dead. ⟦s3⟧ Whence: *Be not you sorrowful, even as the rest, who have not hope.* ⟦s4⟧ But to do these things belongs to them who despair of the resurrection.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Neither shall you make. ⟦s2⟧ The hairs of your head are numbered [n: (Matth. X)]*, that is, the thoughts, which ought not to be taken away from the comeliness of the head and offered to the devil, as certain men offer the bodily hairs to the devil.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Eat not.* ⟦s2⟧ The animals are unclean, [0464C] but what they express by signifying are the manners, namely, of men, and their acts, and their wills.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *The camelopard.* ⟦s2⟧ Which, sprinkled with white spots, is like a camel in the head, like a horse in the neck, like a buffalo in the feet, and for the rest a leopard: it is born in Ethiopia.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Every beast that into two.* ⟦s2⟧ The Church receives and incorporates into her own members those who divide the hoof, who know how to have the discernment of good and evil, and who chew the cud, that is, who meditate in the law day and night.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *That into two parts.* ⟦s2⟧ He, namely, who, taking up the two testaments, establishes himself with a firm step of justice and innocence

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *The camel.* ⟦s2⟧ The Jew, who strains out the gnat and swallows the camel, who chews the letter, but has not [0464D] a cloven hoof, because he does not divide the letter from the spirit: these are the scribes and Pharisees.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The hare.* ⟦s2⟧ By the *choerogyllus* and the hare he signifies the multitude of the Jews, for each animal is weak and feeble.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Because they chew the cud.* ⟦s2⟧ The Jews chew the words of the law, but they do not divide the hoof, because they do not believe in the Father and the Son, nor do they receive the two testaments. ⟦s3⟧ But certain heretics divide the hoof, yet do not chew the doctrine of truth in their mouth [n: (Levit. XI)].

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *These shall you eat of all that move in the waters.* ⟦s2⟧ By these are understood the Gentiles, who are born again in the waters of baptism.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Which have little fins.* ⟦s2⟧ Meditating the law of the sublime [0465A] and heavenly life; because ignorance of the divine law had long been among the Gentiles, he adds little fins and scales: for as fishes are scaled and finned, so to those whose ignorance is but temporal there is quickly added the knowledge of the sublime and of the heavenly life.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *And the griffin.* ⟦s2⟧ Which is a four-footed beast, like an eagle in head and wings, like a lion in the rest of its body, and it abounds in the Hyperborean mountains, most hostile to horses and to men.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *The ostrich.* ⟦s2⟧ Which is not lifted up from the earth, but is entangled in secular business.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The night owl.* ⟦s2⟧ Which sees sharply in the night, and not in the day. ⟦s3⟧ Like those who glory in the knowledge of the law, and do not take in the light of the Gospel.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The gull.* ⟦s2⟧ Which flies as a bird and swims as a water creature: he, namely, who [0465B] venerates both circumcision and baptism. ⟦s3⟧ To such Paul says: *If you be circumcised, Christ shall profit you nothing [n: (Gal. V)]*.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The hawk,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ They who seem meek, but ally themselves with the mighty and the greedy.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *The swan and the ibis.* ⟦s2⟧ These are said to be of a long neck, and to draw their food from the deep of the earth: but we ought to seek the bread which came down from heaven, and not to be solicitous what we shall eat [n: (Joan. VI Matth. VI)]

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *The night raven.* ⟦s2⟧ Which creeps and works by night, signifying those who are given over to the works of night, which are fornication, uncleanness, the worship of idols, and the like.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *The hoopoe.* ⟦s2⟧ Which loves mourning. ⟦s3⟧ But the sorrow [0465C] of the world worketh death: *But we ought to rejoice in the Lord, and in all things to give thanks [n: (II Cor. VII; I Thes. V)]*.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The bat,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Which flies about the earth, and walks with its wings. ⟦s3⟧ But our conversation ought to be in heaven [n: (Phil. III)].

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *But whatsoever.* ⟦s2⟧ Mystically it is enjoined upon the ecclesiastical man that he keep himself in holiness, and shun all uncleanness of sin, and suffer the stranger, that is, the Gentile, to remain in his own filth.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To the stranger that is within thy gates.* ⟦s2⟧ As if to say: leave unclean things to pagans and heretics. ⟦s3⟧ Hence in the Gospel the demons are permitted to go into the herd of swine [n: (Matth. VIII)], and it is said: *Let the dead bury their own dead* [0465D] [n: (Luc. IX)].

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In milk,* by middling disciplines, but by more austere ones, that by bitter things they might restore the sweet things which they lost.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *The tithe.* ⟦s2⟧ As if to say: the good things which thou dost, do according to the precepts of the divine law, and set them apart for the Lord, that thou mayest do all things unto his praise.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *The firstborn.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, the beginnings of good work, which are to be tithed, that is, to be consecrated to the Lord.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *To carry.* ⟦s2⟧ As if to say: they who are far from the perfection of the saints, and can neither ascend the summit of the virtues nor be free for contemplation — what they can, let them do humbly and devoutly, and let them make themselves friends of the mammon of iniquity, and treasures in [0466A] heaven, that they may be received into the eternal tabernacles, where there is feasting and rejoicing with friends, that is, with the saints and elect of God.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *And thou shalt buy with the same money,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Spiritually it is enjoined that we should always press upon good works, that after this life we may return to our own house, that is, to the heavenly fatherland.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Another tithe.* ⟦s2⟧ Besides that one, namely, which thou eatest with thine own and with the Levites, in the place which the Lord shall choose.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *And the Levite shall come.* ⟦s2⟧ He plainly shows that this tithe is not to be shared with him who offers it, but only with those to whom he commanded it to be paid out, among whom he set the Levite chiefly.
