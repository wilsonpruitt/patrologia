## CHAPTER XVI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Observe the month of the new corn.* ⟦s2⟧ Because in it the handfuls of ears, that is, the firstfruits, were offered to the Lord by the priest.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The first month, namely of the Hebrews, which among them is called nisan, among the Greeks also ξάνθικος, among the Latins April, in which the paschal lamb was slain.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He brought thee out.* ⟦s2⟧ Mystically. ⟦s3⟧ Let us too, delivered from the Egypt of this world and clothed with the new man, walk in newness [0467D] of life, consecrating the firstfruits of good works, and celebrating the phase, that is, a good passing over, that, Egypt being left behind, we may come to the land of promise.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And of oxen, in the place. [n: (AUG., quaest. 24.)]* He adds oxen, although he had given command concerning the sheep only, which he bade be taken from the sheep and the kids or the goats — or because of Christ, who according to the flesh was of the just and of sinners. ⟦s2⟧ For he does not say, of the sheep or of the goats, but, of the sheep and of the goats, although properly a sheep is not understood to be of the goats, lest the Jews should say that a goat was to be understood. ⟦s3⟧ Why then are oxen added? is it because of the other sacrifices, which are to be immolated on the days of the unleavened bread?

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0468A]VERS. 3.-- *Leavened bread.* ⟦s2⟧ Corrupt doctrine, or the old manner of life; whence: *Let us feast in the unleavened bread, not in the old leaven,* etc. [n: (I Cor. V)].

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Seven.* ⟦s2⟧ Through the whole time of the present life. ⟦s3⟧ As they ate unleavened bread for seven days, so let us live purely and simply through the seven days of this life, in which daily the lamb is slain and the pasch is celebrated, if, being renewed, we retain nothing of the old corruption. ⟦s4⟧ For what else is leaven than the corruption of nature? ⟦s5⟧ For what is leavened departs from its natural sweetness, and is corrupted with an adulterine rancidness.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *It shall not appear. ⟦s2⟧ Let us feast not in the old leaven, nor in the leaven of malice and wickedness,* etc. [n: (I Cor. V)]; as if to say: leave the old man, and put on the new.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0468B] *There shall not remain.* ⟦s2⟧ In this life all the sacraments of the Lord's incarnation are to be searched out, and all his precepts are to be fulfilled [n: (Ephes. IV)], before the morning, that is, the end of the world, comes.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Thou shalt not be able.* ⟦s2⟧ In one house the paschal lamb is eaten, and in one place the phase is immolated, that is, in the catholic Church: outside her the pasch is not rightly celebrated. ⟦s3⟧ He forbids the mysteries of Christ to be celebrated in the conventicles of heretics.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *In the evening at the sun's.* ⟦s2⟧ In the last age of the world, when the true Sun went down in the passion and loosed the Egyptian captivity, when Christ our pasch was immolated.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Thou shalt go into the tabernacles.* ⟦s2⟧ The service of the Lord being accomplished, in the morning of the resurrection we shall pass over into the eternal [0468C] tabernacles, that we may rest from all labour, rejoicing over the retribution received.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *On the seventh day.* ⟦s2⟧ By the eternal rest, which the number seven signifies: because of the sabbath, where there is no labour, no reckoning of working.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Seven weeks.* ⟦s2⟧ More fully in Leviticus. ⟦s3⟧ *Thou shalt number therefore from the morrow after the sabbath seven full weeks, unto the morrow of the searching out of the seventh week.*

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (AUG., quaest. 25.)] Seven weeks,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ If this Pentecost was commanded to be observed by the whole people, is it to be believed that all were bidden to put the sickle into the harvest on one day? ⟦s3⟧ But if each man observes that fiftieth day for himself, numbering from that day on which he puts in the sickle, it is not one for the whole people. ⟦s4⟧ But that one [0468D] is one which is reckoned from the immolation of the pasch unto the day of the giving of the law on Sina.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ We too celebrate Pentecost, that is, the coming of the Holy Spirit, who is the finger of God, by whom the law was written on the tables.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *The solemnity.* ⟦s2⟧ He who uses the present world as a tabernacle can keep festival and rejoice in it, because of the expectation of what is to come: *Because if the earthly house of this our habitation be dissolved, he shall have a house not made with hands in heaven [n: (I Cor. V)]*.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *When.* ⟦s2⟧ He signifies that, the perfection of all good things being consummated and many virtues gathered together, there is to be rejoicing and exulting with friends. ⟦s3⟧ When each one who here has been bountiful [0469A] in giving shall exult over the retribution received, then truly shall the solemnity of tabernacles be kept — not of changeable ones, that is, but of eternal ones; whence: *That they may receive you into eternal tabernacles [n: (Luc. XVI)]*.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *The male.* ⟦s2⟧ Not the female; for nothing soft, frail, or feeble ought to be in our manner of life: God requires of us all things strong [n: (Exod. XXIII, XXXIV; Eccli. XXXV)]. ⟦s3⟧ But if, in the faith of the Trinity, of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, with regard for the eternal reward, we sweat at good works in the time of the present life, we set a male in the sight of the Lord.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In all.* ⟦s2⟧ That they who come from every side may at once, at the entrance, find men prepared, from whom they may receive just judgments.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0469B]VERS. 19.-- *The eyes of the wise.* ⟦s2⟧ Among men, not with God; to whom it is said: *Woe to you that are wise in your own eyes [n: (Isa. V)]*.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Justly. [n: (GREG., lib. X Moral., cap. 19.)]* He unjustly pursues what is just who is stirred to the defence of justice not by zeal of virtue, but by love of a temporal reward, and does not fear to sell the justice he puts forward. ⟦s2⟧ Justly therefore to pursue the just is, in the asserting of justice, to seek justice itself: *There are ways which seem to men just,* etc. [n: (Prov. XIV)]. ⟦s3⟧ And: *Woe to you who justify yourselves before men [n: (Luc. XVI)]*.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Thou shalt not plant.* ⟦s2⟧ He cuts off the occasion of idolatry. ⟦s3⟧ For the nations were wont to immolate, and to adore statues in their shrines.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0469C] *A grove.* [n: (ISID.)] A wood, leafy unfruitful trees, etc., *as far as* nor does it permit the feigning of falsehood or of error to be joined to catholic truth.

## CHAPTER XVII.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And when there shall be found.* ⟦s2⟧ The law above all detests error and idolatry: for it is the greatest wickedness to pay to a creature the honour of the Creator; which the Gentiles do, or bad catholics, who, although they do not withdraw from the Church, yet are found rather to serve the devil through divers vices than to war for God through good works. ⟦s3⟧ These he bids to be overwhelmed with stones, whom an irrevocable sentence has foreseen to be condemned.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *In the mouth of two or three witnesses.* ⟦s2⟧ By the attestation, namely, of the law, of the prophets, and of the Gospel, according to [0469D] that: *The word which he hath spoken, the same shall judge him in the last day [n: (Joan. XII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ In the title of the cross too three tongues bear witness that Christ is the king of the Jews: the Hebrew, the Greek, and the Latin [n: (Joan. XIX)].

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let no man be put to death.* ⟦s2⟧ So great is the concord of the Old and the New Testament, that neither is without the other.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ In human judgment the authority of Holy Scripture is first to be sought.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 81.-- *If difficult.* ⟦s2⟧ He instructs the priests of the Church of God, that they may conduct ecclesiastical judgments reverently according to the power given them by God, and may decree justly, not at their own pleasure, but according to the decree of the law, lest by accepting a person they change the sentence.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0470A]VERS. 11.-- *According to.* ⟦s2⟧ Note, it is not said to thee that thou shouldst obey, unless they have taught according to the law; whence: *Upon the chair of Moses have sitten the scribes and the Pharisees [n: (Matth. XXIII)]*.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *At the command of the priest.* ⟦s2⟧ Of Christ, who is a priest for ever according to the order of Melchisedech, who put vicars in his own stead, to whom he says: *He that heareth you heareth me; and he that despiseth you despiseth me,* etc. [n: (Luc. X)]. ⟦s3⟧ By right therefore he undergoes the sentence of damnation who contemns the power of the Godhead.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *When thou shalt have entered into the land,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 26.)] It can be asked: why did the people displease God when it desired a king, seeing that here it is found to have been permitted? ⟦s2⟧ But it is to be understood that deservedly it was not according to the will of God, because he did not command this to be done, but permitted it to them that desired it. ⟦s3⟧ Nevertheless he commanded [0470B] that no stranger should be made king, but a brother out of that same people, a native, not one of another race. ⟦s4⟧ But as to what he says: *Thou shalt not be able:* it is to be understood, thou oughtest not.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I will set over.* ⟦s2⟧ The inhabitants of the land set a king over themselves against the sentence of God.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *He shall not have,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 27.)] It is manifest that Solomon transgressed this precept, etc., *as far as* it may come to men of another race.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Nor of silver. ⟦s2⟧ Lay not up to yourselves treasures on earth [n: (Matth. VI)]*.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *And he shall read.* ⟦s2⟧ Note with how great assiduity priests ought to read, since kings read assiduously. ⟦s3⟧ Reading itself is light and life; whence: *The words which I speak to you are spirit and life [n: (Joan. VI)]*.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Pride.* ⟦s2⟧ As if to say: let him know that he is a brother. [0470C] We all have one Father, God, to whom we say: *Our Father, who art in heaven,* etc. [n: (Luc. XI)].

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Pride.* ⟦s2⟧ It is a blessing to reign over Israel, that is, by reigning to make Israel, that is, men seeing God.
