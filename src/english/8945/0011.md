## CHAPTER XXI.

VERS. 1.-- *When there shall be found,* etc. A carcass, a body [0473B] without a soul, and a soul without God, who is its life. *In the land which the Lord thy God will give thee,* that is, in the Church. *Guilty of the slaughter,* the author of sedition; *The elders,* those greater in life and in wisdom. *The judges,* that is, the priests, whose office it is to judge concerning individuals, that is, to discern. *They shall measure from the place of the carcass,* that is, with the utmost discretion they shall investigate the author of the sedition. *They shall take a heifer of the herd,* that is, the flesh of Christ, which he took from the fathers. *Which hath not drawn the yoke,* because *he did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth. Nor hath it cut the earth with the ploughshare [n: (I Petr. II)]*, that is, it admitted no stain of sedition.

*And the guilty of the slaughter shall be unknown.* The devil, who is invisible and is therefore said to be unknown; for it is known that he put it into the heart of Judas to betray him [n: (Joan. XIII)].

[0473C] *The elders,* etc. The apostles and the teachers who by the attestation of Scripture have perceived that no nation was so ready for slaughter as the people of the Jews, who shed the blood both of the prophets and of the Lord.

VERS. 4.-- *And they shall lead her to a rough valley,* etc. Namely to a despised and most shameful death; whence: *Let us condemn him to a most shameful death. Which hath never been ploughed [n: (Sap. II)]*, because the death of Christ was without fault. Nor *hath it received seed [n: (Matth. XIII)]*, that is, the cockle of sin, which the enemy man oversowed. We can take the rough valley as the Gentile world, which was rough; stony, because it adored stones; in which the neck of the heifer was struck off, because for the salvation of the Gentiles the death of Christ was celebrated. The neck is the strength of the flesh, which he laid down in death. *They shall wash their hands over the heifer,* [0473D] that is, they shall purge their works through confidence in the passion of Christ.

VERS. 6.-- *And they shall wash their hands,* etc. That is, they shall show their works to be clean in the passion of Christ. For although the sacraments of that same passion are repeated daily, yet from all the fraud of the Jews they have hearts and works most clean.

VERS. 8.-- *Be merciful.* According to their office they bless the people, that vengeance be not rendered for the crime, but salvation for the redemption.

VERS. 10.-- *If he shall have gone forth to battle.* [n: (ISID.)] Let the Jews say, how do they keep these things? what is the reason that the woman is shaved and her nails pared? what if she be found without hair and without nails?

[0474A]VERS. 11.-- *A beautiful woman.* As Paul, who took from Menander: *Evil communications corrupt good manners [n: (I Cor. XV)]*. And from Aratus: *In him we live, and move, and are: for we are also his offspring [n: (Act. XVII)]*.

Others think that this woman comely in appearance signifies the reasonable discipline found among the Gentiles: for from her must be cut away every uncleanness of superstition, that she may be taken up unto the study of truth. For the disciplines of the Gentiles have nothing clean, because there is no discipline among them to which somewhat of uncleanness or of superstition is not mingled.

VERS. 13.-- *One month,* that is, thirty days, that after the faith of the Trinity and the work of the law she may deserve to be joined to the Church; for thrice ten make thirty.

[0474B] *And afterwards.* When neither in the head, that is, in the senses, nor in the hands, that is, in the works, will there be anything superfluous or unclean.

VERS. 15.-- *If a man have two wives,* etc. Christ joined to himself two wives, namely the people of the Jews and of the Gentiles, under the name of wedlock. The synagogue is hateful, ever contentious, stiff-necked, uncircumcised in heart; the Gentile Church is beloved. But he cannot prefer the son of the beloved to the son of the hateful, that is, prefer the Church of the Gentiles and her sons to the apostles and the other first men, who are the sons of the synagogue. He acknowledges the firstborn of the hateful, he gives him a double portion, because to the first sons of the synagogue he gave the law and the prophets, and the knowledge of his name, and to them first he preached the Gospel. For he is the firstborn of his children.

[0474C] *One beloved.* Animality, which seems sweet and delightful: it desires all voluptuous things and afflicts with no sorrow.

*The other hateful.* Rationality, which does not indulge the body, nor serve pleasure, but renounces the hidden things of dishonesty.

VERS. 16.-- *The firstborn.* The apostles and apostolic men, who hold the place of the firstborn, whose authority the Church follows.

VERS. 17.-- *But the son.* This is to be done to the letter, that no one follow the motion of his own mind, or love, or hatred, but nature.

*All things double.* The apostles received all things double, who were endowed with doctrine and with working.

To the other he will give that he may live, not that he may abound; as Abraham set the son of the handmaid [0474D] over the nations: upon the son of Sara he heaped not only temporal but spiritual things [n: (Gen. XII)].

VERS. 18.-- *If he beget. Obedience is better than victims, and to hearken rather than to offer the fat of rams; for it is as the sin of divining to rebel,* etc. [n: (I Reg. XV.)] Moses therefore bids the disobedient and vicious son to be overwhelmed with stones: the Gospel too rebukes such men with hard reproofs, as with stones: *Ye offspring of vipers, who shall teach you to flee from the wrath to come [n: (Luc. III)]*?

VERS. 22.-- *Hanged up.* His torment, with the people looking on; whence: *Them that sin reprove before all, that the rest may have fear [n: (II Tim. V)]*.

[0475A]VERS. 23.-- *It shall not remain.* That is, he ought not to be left in the same condemnation, but to be called back by the exhortation of teachers, so that reconciliation be denied to none while he lives.

*It shall not remain.* Penance is not to be deferred from day to day, but to be done at once, that through unfruitful [cj: *infructuosam*; read *fructuosam*, "fruitful"] penance offences may be hidden from the eyes of God; whence: *Blessed are they whose sins are covered [n: (Psal. XXXI)]*.

*Cursed of God. [n: (AUG., lib. XIV contra Faustum.)]* Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us [n: (Gal. III)]. [nt: (For Adam fell in the tree of transgression: therefore he merited a curse for himself and for his posterity, of which he says: Cursed is every one that hangeth on a tree.)] For he underwent a cursed, that is, an execrable and most shameful death.

[0475B] *[n: (ID., ubi supra, cap. 3, 4.)] He that hangeth is cursed of God,* etc. Certain heretics transfer this sentence to the calumny of the passion of Christ, etc., *as far as* well therefore is that called cursed which God hates: this hung upon the tree.

*Thou shalt defile.* With the tears of penance we ought to wash away the stench of sins, lest it breed scandal for others and torment for ourselves.
