## CHAPTER XXIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *He shall not enter.* ⟦s2⟧ All who live softly, [0477B] and do not accomplish a manly work, cannot remain in the congregation of the saints, nor are they worthy of entrance into the kingdom of heaven, which suffereth violence, and the violent bear it away.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Mamzer.* ⟦s2⟧ Degenerate in sense or in morals; or one whom the depravity of heretics has made degenerate in the receiving of the sacraments; or whom the rite of heathenism or of Judaism has stained. ⟦s3⟧ He shall be received into the Church when with entire faith he shall have kept the decalogue of the law according to the institution of the Gospel, and, strengthened by the Holy Spirit, shall have renounced carnal converse; whence: *If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments [n: (Matth. XIX)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And elsewhere: *I am the door: by me if any man enter in, he shall be saved [n: (Joan. X)]*.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *The Ammonite and the Moabite. [n: (AUG., quaest. 33.)]* Another edition, etc., *as far as* they imputed it to the women, [0477C] whom they chose rather to preserve.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The Moabite.* ⟦s2⟧ It signifies that heretics are ever to be driven from the Church, as if to say, *A man that is a heretic, after the first and second correction, avoid.*

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Because they would not meet us with,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Hebrews hand down that these nations, being near of kin to them, met those going forth out of Egypt with bread only, though they knew that we laboured in the desert most of all with thirst, and therefore they were made subject to this curse.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Mystically they designate heretics, who to those fleeing out of the Egypt of this world unto the true land of promise offer polluted bread, not sweetened with the water of wisdom, and therefore they are estranged from the Church.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And he turned.* ⟦s2⟧ Because often persecution is the cause of a greater prosperity. ⟦s3⟧ *For to them that love God all things [0477D] work together unto good [n: (Rom. VIII)]*.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Thou shalt not abhor the Idumaean.* ⟦s2⟧ The Idumaean, the bloody or earthly one, signifies the flesh (whose matter is earthly); the Egyptian, that is, the dark one, the sickly body. ⟦s3⟧ We ought not to abhor our flesh, but to cherish it and to furnish it with what is necessary, yet not to make provision for it in its desires: *He is thy brother,* he says, because we are of flesh and soul. ⟦s4⟧ Let us not abhor the Egyptian, that is, the body, in whose land we were sojourners; for the soul, which has a heavenly origin, dwells in the body as a sojourner; whence the Apostle: *While we are in the body, we are absent from the Lord [n: (II Cor. V)]*. ⟦s5⟧ *They that shall be born of them,* etc., that is, little by little they shall become spiritual.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0478A]By the Idumaean and the Egyptian is signified the present life; and although in renouncing it we shun the Egyptian land, that is, our former converse and the concupiscence of the world, yet while we are subject to the necessities of this world we do not altogether extinguish the Egyptian nation: but, in a manner set apart, we are content with daily food and clothing; in the third generation they shall enter into the Church, because a man of the world can be joined to the saints of God when he shall have believed perfectly in the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, or when he shall have been adorned with hope, faith and charity.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *When.* ⟦s2⟧ He instructs the spiritual soldiers of Christ, to whom it is said elsewhere: *Every one that striveth for the mastery refraineth himself from all things [n: (I Cor. IX)]*.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *If. [n: (AUG., lib. de bono conjugali, cap. 20.)]* Many things [0478B] the law sets in sacraments and in shadows of things to come, etc., *as far as* not because a dead body is sin, but it signifies the sin of a soul deserted by justice.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Mystically *[n: (GREG., lib. IX Moral., cap. 40.)] If there be among you a man who, defiled by a nocturnal,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ A nocturnal dream is a hidden temptation, etc., *as far as* and let it be repaired unto the merits of the faithful, which are to be presumed.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Thou shalt have.* ⟦s2⟧ For God loves bodily cleanness: therefore he commanded that nothing foul should remain in the camp.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Mystically. ⟦s2⟧ *[n: (GREG., lib. XXXI Mor., cap. 32.)] Thou shalt have a place,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Certain superfluities of thoughts burst forth from the womb of our mind, weighed down by the burden of a corruptible nature, as it were the heaviness of the belly. ⟦s4⟧ But we ought to carry a paddle under our girdle, so that, being always girt for the reproving of ourselves, [0478C] we may have about us the sharp goad of compunction, which may dig into the earth of our mind with the pain of penance, and may hide what bursts forth foully from us.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *In the girdle.* ⟦s2⟧ For with a girdle the reins are girt; therefore by the girdle is designated the contrition of an evil will; whence Job: *He looseth the belt of kings, and girdeth their loins with a cord [n: (Job. II)]*.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thou shalt cover it with earth.* ⟦s2⟧ Considering our earthly frailty, we ought to cover our filth.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Thou shalt not deliver up the servant [n: (AUG., quaest. 36.)]* The LXX: *Thou shalt not deliver up the boy to his master, who was set beside thee by his master.* ⟦s2⟧ Not that the master set him there, etc., *as far as* he explains this, saying: *He shall dwell among you in every place that shall please him [n: (I Reg. XXVII)]*.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0478D]VERS. 37.-- *There shall be no harlot. [n: (AUG., quaest. 37.)]* The LXX: *There shall be no harlot,* etc., *as far as* whence we treated there what might seem to us.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thou shalt not offer the hire of a strumpet. [n: (AUG. quaest. 38.)]* The LXX: *Thou shalt not offer,* etc., *as far as* it had to be said that it is an abomination to the Lord.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The price of a dog,* of a ravening one. ⟦s2⟧ The Most High approves not the gifts of the unjust. ⟦s3⟧ *He that offereth sacrifice of the substance of the poor is as one that sacrificeth the son in the presence of his father [n: (Eccli. XXXIV)]*.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Thou shalt not lend to thy brother upon usury.* ⟦s2⟧ He removes usury and avarice, and bids charity be spent.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And money, which is forbidden to be given for usury, because [0479A] it is a vice of covetousness to wish to exact what thou knowest thou hast not lent. ⟦s2⟧ This the Lord had, and thence delivered it to be laid out upon the poor, and invited us to the liberality of largess. ⟦s3⟧ There is another which we ought to give for usury; whence: *Oughtest thou not therefore to have committed my money to the bankers, that at my coming I might exact my own with usury? [n: (Matth. XXV)]*.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *But to the stranger.* ⟦s2⟧ To the unbeliever or the unjust, to whom we give money for usury when for the word of preaching we exact faith, penance and good working.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Thou shalt not delay to pay.* ⟦s2⟧ Whence Solomon: *It is ruin to a man to deliberate or to delay after vows [n: (Proverb. X)]*.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Going into thy neighbour's vineyard.* ⟦s2⟧ In the church [0479B] of another bishop one may correct or confirm certain persons; it is not lawful for him to rule the whole people, or to handle great affairs.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Without.* ⟦s2⟧ It is not lawful to carry this grace outside the church, because in one house the flesh of the lamb is commanded to be eaten.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *If thou go in.* ⟦s2⟧ There are certain places of Scripture which historically imbue with faith, certain which morally instruct in holy converse; certain which show the mysteries to be venerated not according to history but according to allegory. ⟦s3⟧ Warily therefore must one go into the standing corn of one's neighbour, that by discerning thou mayest pluck food, and mayest not, by that which must be hurried, incur the judgment of the reaped harvest.
