## CHAPTER XXIV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *If he shall take. [n: (RAB. in Deut., tom. II.)]* [0479C] A man took a wife, etc., *as far as* she remained abominable, as unworthy and defiled with error; whence: *To you it behoved us first to speak the word of God, but because,* etc. [n: (Act. XIII.)]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., lib. I de sermone Domini in monte.)] There were in the law many causes of putting away wives, etc., *as far as* that they who have wives be as though they had none [n: (I Cor. VII)].

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He shall write.* ⟦s2⟧ For the hardness of your heart Moses wrote this precept, choosing rather to allow a separation than that a murder be committed.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Lest he sin.* ⟦s2⟧ He pollutes his land who defiles his body with carnal concupiscences. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore it is necessary that the ambition of the present life be tamed the sooner, lest it prevail to have dominion. ⟦s4⟧ And when an earthly action has once [0479D] been repelled, it is not to be sought again: let not him look back who had put his hand to the plough.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *When he shall have taken.* ⟦s2⟧ As if to say, whosoever binds himself with carnal desires, let him acknowledge that he is not fit for the rule of the Church, nor for the spiritual warfare: because *no man being a soldier to God entangleth himself with secular business, that he may please him to whom he hath approved himself [n: (II Tim. II)]*; whence: *Impose hands hastily upon no man.*

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID.)] He takes away the upper millstone who says to one weeping for his sin: Thou shalt have no pardon for what thou hast committed. ⟦s2⟧ He takes away the lower who, soothing the sin, says: Do evil as much as thou wilt, God is merciful and ready to pardon; both millstones are usefully had, if hope be with amending fear, and fear with hope.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0480A] *[n: (GREG., lib. XXXIII Moral., cap. 16.)] Thou shalt not take the lower stone for a pledge,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Sometimes we say "to take" for "to offer," etc., *as far as* and he fears in vain who does not trust.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Thou shalt take away. [n: (AUG., quaest. 39.)]* Scripture says this continually, etc., *as far as* which, expounding, he says: *He that stole, let him now steal no more [n: (Ephes. IV)]*.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *The plague of leprosy.* ⟦s2⟧ Note that this infirmity befalls men on account of sin, as it did Ozias, who usurped the priesthood.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *When thou shalt demand back. [n: (AUG., quaest. 41.)]* It belongs to the work of mercy that the taker of a pledge should not enter into the house, etc., *as far as* having nowhere to sleep.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Is it not. ⟦s2⟧ He that announceth the Gospel, let him live of the Gospel [n: (I Cor. IX)]*. ⟦s3⟧ *For the labourer is worthy of his hire [0480B] [n: (Luc. X)]*.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *They shall not be put to death. [n: (AUG., quaest. 42)]*. ⟦s2⟧ The law also says, etc., *as far as* he did not wish three or four to be understood.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Thou shalt not pervert. [n: (AUG., quaest. 43)]*. ⟦s2⟧ The LXX: *Thou shalt not turn aside the judgment of the stranger and of the fatherless and of the widow. ⟦s3⟧ Thou shalt not take the widow's garment in pledge.* ⟦s4⟧ Why does he not say, etc., *as far as* continence is praised in them, desolation is not commended.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the widow.* ⟦s2⟧ The widow is the soul formerly joined to the devil, from whom, when she is converted, the garment of faith is not to be taken away.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Thou shalt not return.* ⟦s2⟧ By envying the Gentiles, who are strangers to the testaments of God.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *If thou hast gathered the fruits.* ⟦s2⟧ Even though the proud and the wicked gather, he does mercy who leaves this behind with the [0480C] mind that the needy may have it. ⟦s3⟧ Moreover, when these things are enjoined upon the people; they who are not in need are admonished not to seek these things. ⟦s4⟧ But if they have sought them, they are to be judged as robbers of the poor.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thy vineyard.* ⟦s2⟧ The vineyard from which is gathered the wine that maketh glad the heart of man [n: (Psal. CIII)].

## CHAPTER XXV.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *If there be a cause. [n: (AUG., quaest. 45)]*. ⟦s2⟧ The LXX: *But if there be a strife between men,* etc., *as far as* so also of the sins which seem to be committed not against God but against man, some are worthy of death, some of stripes.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Forty.* ⟦s2⟧ The number forty is completed by ten drawn four times. ⟦s3⟧ But we, according to the body, consist of four [0480D] elements: the precepts of the decalogue are taken by the number ten. ⟦s4⟧ He bids therefore that the judgment or the vengeance, in which the sin of the flesh is punished, be ordered according to the precepts, and he wills all the precepts to be understood in the decalogue.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID.)] In the number forty this time is figured, etc., *as far as* there we are found without sin.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *The mouth of the ox that treadeth out.* [n: (ISID.)] In the ox is expressed the life of those that work, of whom it is said: *For the labourer is worthy of his hire [n: (Luc. X)]*. ⟦s2⟧ Scripture wills therefore that carnal things be supplied to those from whom we receive spiritual things, who, being occupied in teaching, cannot provide themselves with the things needful.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *When. [n: (AUG., quaest. 46.)]* The LXX *If [0481A] brothers have dwelt together before,* etc., *as far as* or adoption made another father whom Joseph could have.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Shall have died.* ⟦s2⟧ He suffered and ascended into heaven while the synagogue still remained barren.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But he shall take,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He who is strong to rule, in whose face, when he is unwilling, the woman spits. ⟦s3⟧ For he who cares not to profit others by spiritual gifts — the Church, upbraiding him too for his goods, casts as it were spittle in his face.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *The shoe.* ⟦s2⟧ Of which it is said: *Shod feet in the preparation of the Gospel of peace [n: (Ezech. VI)]*. ⟦s3⟧ If we bear the care of our neighbour as we bear our own, we fortify both feet with a shoe; but he who, thinking of his own advantage, neglects that of his neighbours, as it were loses with disgrace the shoe of one foot.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0481B] *From the foot,* that is, he shall show him deprived of the reward of preaching. ⟦s2⟧ For the feet of teachers are shod, because, fortified by the examples of the fathers who went before, they have imitated their steps of preaching. ⟦s3⟧ He is shod on both feet who profits himself and others.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And one against the other.* ⟦s2⟧ The Catholic and the heretic, whose contention is perpetual, because their intention is unlike: for the one defends falsehood, the other truth; and it behoves us to resist error, lest the fool seem wise to himself.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And she shall put forth.* ⟦s2⟧ He shall have wished by false ignominy or by an abominable action to stain the converse of the Catholic or to defame his teaching.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The privy parts.* ⟦s2⟧ The weaker members of the Church, whom, namely, they will wish to deceive by flattering or to deride by insulting.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0481C]VERS. 13.-- *Thou shalt not have in thy bag.* ⟦s2⟧ In Leviticus the same is forbidden. ⟦s3⟧ Solomon also says: *A great weight and a small, and double measures, are unclean before God [n: (Prov. XX)]*.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID.)] *Thou shalt not have in thy bag.* ⟦s2⟧ Spiritually also we must take care that we have not diverse weights in the heart, that is, that, softening the rule of strictness for ourselves, we overwhelm those to whom we preach the word of God with stricter precepts, as with heavier weights. ⟦s3⟧ And when we do this, we weigh out the reward and the fruit of the Lord's precepts with a diverse weight; whence: *A double weight is an abomination to the Lord [n: (Prov. XX)]*.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Remember what.* ⟦s2⟧ The Hebrews hand down that [0481D] Amalec slew the lepers and those with an issue of seed, and others set outside the camp, and these they say are the hindmost of the Lord's army. ⟦s3⟧ Amalec is "licking blood." ⟦s4⟧ These are the demons who withstand us as we go forth by baptism out of the spiritual Egypt, and who strive to slay, by suggesting sins, those who are hindmost in virtues and wearied in the journey of this life. ⟦s5⟧ These, when the Lord shall have given the power, we ought utterly to overthrow, and in every way to cast out and destroy their suggestions, lest, if we have kept back any remnants, as Saul did, we be overthrown with them as disobedient.
