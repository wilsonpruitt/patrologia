## CHAPTER XXVIII.

VERS. 1.-- *But if thou shalt hear.* Just as he foretells curses and punishments to the disobedient, so he promises blessings and rewards to the obedient.

VERS. 4.-- *The fruit of thy womb.* He marks the diverse orders of subjects. For some furnish bodily helps to the teachers; others can labour at what has been enjoined upon them; others, content with simplicity, are eager to please God through innocence [0484B] of life: every one has his own gift from God.

VERS. 5.-- *Blessed are thy remnants.* The reward of a good intention, even if the will has no effect, because *peace to men of good will,* etc. [n: (Luc. II.)]

VERS. 6.-- *Coming in.* He comes in through the door who, through the catholic faith, ponders and handles within according to the will of God: he goes out when he makes the same manifest before men to the praise of God.

VERS. 7.-- *In thy sight.* That thou mayest rejoice: *The just shall rejoice when he shall see the revenge [n: (Psal. LVII)]*.

VERS. 8.-- *And he shall bless.* Granting increase and happy success to good works.

*The heaven.* The prophets and apostles, of whom it is said, *The heavens shew forth the glory of God:* whom the Lord opens [0484C] when through them he grants an abundance of doctrine, that the hearts of carnal men, watered by this shower, may put forth the buds of good works.

*Thou shalt lend.* That is, so great will thy abundance be that thou wilt be able to give money to the nations, but they not so great. Or, ecclesiastical doctrine is understood.

*Thou shalt lend to nations.* The Church lends to many nations when she spends the divine graces upon the peoples. Nor does she take interest, because she seeks not a present recompense, but a future one. For she excels all, and is the head of the world, not the tail. She is always above, and not beneath; because although she suffers the pressures and persecutions of the wicked in the world, yet she is stronger than her adversaries, and by faith and virtue is pre-eminent over all. Whence, *the Lord shall set thee at the head,* etc.

[0484D]VERS. 14.-- *Thou shalt not turn aside [sic: aectinaveris]. [n: (AUG., quaest., 48.)]* LXX: *Thou shalt not pass beyond all the words,* etc., *as far as* nor are they to be worshipped on account of these things.

VERS. 15.-- *But if thou wilt not hear.* Just as he promised a blessing to those who keep the commandments, so he pronounces a curse upon those who neglect them.

VERS. 22.-- *May the Lord strike thee. [n: (AUG. in Enchirid., cap. 98.)]* Who would be so impiously senseless as to say that God cannot turn the evil wills of men — which he will, when he will, where he will — unto good? But when he does it, he does it through mercy: when he does not do it, he does not do it through judgment, *who hath mercy on whom he will, and whom he will he hardeneth.*

*And with corrupted air.* A life polluted by carnal uncleanness. [0485A] [d: A carnal life polluted by carnal uncleanness.] Carnal life is expressed by air, because the human body is quickened by the breath of the air.

VERS. 23.-- *Let the heaven be.* He compares the elements by which fruits are nourished to the hardest metals, because the sins of men change the order of things, and those things which were established for the comfort of the human race, whence: *The whole world fought for him against the senseless.*

The heaven is of brass to the transgressors of the law, when they merit no clemency from the holy teachers, but the harshest discipline, since they themselves have not a heart of flesh and a soft mind, but a stony and an iron one. Just as to a hard people the law was given on stone, that from the gift the receiver might be figured.

VERS. 24.-- *May the Lord give dust for the rain of thy land:* [0485B] as if to say, just as dust and ashes bear no fruit, so let the doctrine poured into thee not stir up the buds of virtues, but foretell an everlasting destruction. For the dust signifies the barrenness of good works, the ashes the burning; because the chaff of sins shall be burned with unquenchable fire.

VERS. 25.-- *And be dispersed.* Just as the Jews, taken captive, were dispersed throughout the breadth of the world because they gathered themselves against God, so the generation of the wicked, dispersed by unclean spirits, is troubled through kinds of punishments, whence: *Thy enemies shall perish, and all shall be scattered that work iniquity [n: (Psal. XCI)]*.

VERS. 27.-- *May the Lord strike thee with the ulcer of Egypt.* Ulcers are painful and festering malice. Blisters, inflated pride. Scab and itch, burning anger and the [0485C] madness of fury. These are inflicted in the present as a fitting punishment for sins: after this life (which is signified in the hinder parts) they shall bear the stench of their crimes in eternal torments.

*May the Lord strike thee.* Heretics and schismatics, wrapped in the blindness of error while the Gospel grows bright in the whole world, exercising their madness, contradict the catholic faith. Therefore they endure the calumny of the Church and are crushed by the truth.

Senseless and blind they were, when, seeing the miracles of Christ, they said that these were done in Beelzebub, and denied that Christ was God [n: (Matth. VII)], nor did they see the truth, that is, understand it.

VERS. 29.-- *And thou shalt grope.* Darkness came when Jesus was crucified, until the ninth hour, just as [0485D] there was darkness in the hearts of the Jews.

*Mayest thou not direct:* neither did they believe when Christ preached, nor were they willing to be directed through penance.

VERS. 30.-- *Mayest thou take a wife, and another,* etc. By the wife is taken the people joined to heretics, with whom the devil commits fornication while it is seduced into error. The beasts are snatched from the heretics when their subjects are freed from them: when they themselves are deprived of life and of doctrine. The substance too, which the Jews prepared for themselves singularly by teaching and by writing, the Church turns to her own use.

VERS. 33.-- *The fruit of thy land.* The whole usefulness of the ceremonies has been carried over to the Church. She enjoys abundance, the Jews perish of want, whence through [0486A] Isaiah it is said: *My servants shall eat, and you shall be hungry [n: (Isai. LXV)]*.

VERS. 35.-- *In the knees and in the calves.* Let him take away strength, namely, and [sic: *statibilitatem*]. Or let him be struck in the knees, whose last works are condemned.

*From the sole of the foot.* Just as from the beginning of a depraved will unto the completion of the deed thou hast exercised malice, whence *from the sole of the foot unto the top of the head there is no soundness in him [n: (Isai. I)]*. This was fulfilled in the last captivity.

VERS. 36.-- *And he shall lead thee.* Heretics, or bad catholics, who despise the precepts of God, are taken captive by demons and by divers vices. As the servant, so also the master; and the king as the people, the teacher as the disciple.

[0486B] *And the king.* Shalmaneser king of the Assyrians led Hosea king of Samaria into captivity with the ten tribes: Nebuchadnezzar took captive Joachin, and Zedekiah king of Judah, together with the people.

VERS. 38.-- *Seed,* etc. We too must take heed that we lose not the bread which strengthens the heart of man, and the wine which gladdens it, and the oil which makes the face cheerful.

VERS. 43.-- *The stranger.* The casting off of the Jews and the choosing of the Gentiles is openly signified, whence: *The kingdom of God shall be taken away from you, and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruits thereof,* and elsewhere: *So shall the last be first, and the first last.*

*[n: (RAB. in Deut., tom. II.)] The stranger that dwelleth with thee,* etc. The creator of all through the mystery of his incarnation, etc., *as far as* and shall have esteemed the blood of the testament [0486C] polluted.

VERS. 66.-- *And thy life shall be.* Nothing is worse among the curses which befell the Jews by the desert of their pride, than to see their own life, that is, the Son of God, hanging, and not to believe in him. But when curses are spoken out of prophecy: they are not from an evil wish, but are foretold. And it could sound thus to the Jews: Thou shalt see thy life hanging, and thou shalt not believe thy life — that is, amid the threats and the treacheries of enemies thou shalt see thy life hanging in uncertainty.

## CHAPTER XXIX.

VERS. 1.-- *These are the words,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 49 in Deut.)] LXX: *These are the words of the testament,* etc., *as far as* to Abraham concerning circumcision, or to Noah.

[0486D]VERS. 3.-- *The great temptations,* etc. *[n: (AUG., quaest. 50.)] And the Lord God hath not given you a heart to know,* etc., *as far as* since the judgments of God, however hidden, are nevertheless just.

VERS. 6.-- *You have not eaten bread,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Hence it appears that the Israelites, etc., *as far as* not the beginning of war, but the beginning of wine.

VERS. 18.-- *Lest perhaps there be. [n: (AUG., quaest. 52.)]* LXX: *Is there any among you,* etc., *as far as* he had not coveted another's wife for himself.

VERS. 19, 20.-- *The drunken the thirsty, and the Lord,* etc. That is, lest anyone full of malice and of idolatry seduce him who thirsts to learn and to know the truth, as the heretics do.

## CHAPTER XXX.

[0487A]

VERS. 11.-- *This commandment, which I,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 54.)] LXX: *For this commandment,* etc., *as far as* without which, if they be done outwardly with the hands and be not done in the heart, the precepts of God are not fulfilled.

VERS. 14.-- *The word is nigh thee,* etc. That is, the commandments of God are measured and tempered to human strength. This can be taken of the incarnation of Christ, who, having assumed humanity, was seen upon earth and conversed among men.
