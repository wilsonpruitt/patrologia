## CHAPTER XXXIII. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Smite their backs.* ⟦s2⟧ As if to say: they seemed to themselves to shed the blood of the martyrs unpunished, but behind their back, as it were, an unforeseen punishment was being prepared, that is, eternal damnation, into which they shall fall and shall not be able to rise. ⟦s3⟧ Whence: *A rod for the back of the imprudent [n: (Prov. XXVI)]*.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *And to Benjamin.* ⟦s2⟧ He seems to allude to the history, because Jacob, patriarch and prophet, filled [0497A] with the Spirit, loved Benjamin uniquely, and by God's dispensation the place devoted to the divine worship is decreed to be kept by his tribe; whence he adds: *As in a bridal chamber all the day,* etc.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Most beloved.* ⟦s2⟧ Christ is the most beloved of God the Father, who says of himself: *This is my beloved Son [n: (Matth. XVII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ He dwells confidently at the right hand of the Father, to whom it is said: *Sit thou at my right hand [n: (Psal. CIX)]*.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Otherwise: Benjamin, called *son of the right hand* instead of son of sorrow, expresses Paul, made an apostle and a vessel of election out of a persecutor: for he himself was of this tribe; and it was fitting that, as the Lord's forerunner was foretold by a prophetic oracle, so the teacher of the gentiles, who was to profit the whole world, should be proclaimed among the great mysteries of the Church. ⟦s2⟧ In whose mind Christ [0497B] dwelt with confidence, when amid innumerable perils he preached him steadfastly before gentiles and kings. ⟦s3⟧ Of whose soul it is said:

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As in a bridal chamber he shall abide all the day.* ⟦s2⟧ Either enjoying with wondrous delight a marital wedlock, and making it fruitful with the offspring of the virtues, and forsaking the secret place of that most peaceful breast through the unquiet of no vice; or, sitting in it as in a bridal chamber, like a comely bridegroom, he joins to his embraces, through his preaching, the virgin minds of believers. ⟦s3⟧ Whence: *After her shall virgins be brought to the king [n: (Psal. XLIII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ He himself also says: *For I have espoused you to one husband, that I may present you a chaste virgin to Christ [n: (II Cor. XI)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The Divinity also dwells confidently in the humanity of Christ. ⟦s2⟧ *He shall abide in a bridal chamber,* signifies that [0497C] in the womb of the Virgin the Church was joined to the heavenly bridegroom, she who rests confidently in the strength of Christ's power and in his works.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And he shall rest between his shoulders.* ⟦s2⟧ Burdens are carried upon the shoulders, and therefore in them sturdy patience is designated. ⟦s3⟧ And the more gladly did Christ rest in him, the harder were the labours he bore for his sake: the more closely was he embraced, the less was he torn from that embrace by any terror. ⟦s4⟧ Whence he says: *Who shall separate us from the charity of Christ? [n: (Rom. VIII.)]*

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *To Joseph also he said.* ⟦s2⟧ Joseph is interpreted *increased.* ⟦s3⟧ This is Christ, who, cast off by his brethren the Jews, was made prince in the Egypt of this world, and freed the human race from the want of famine by the dispensation of the evangelical [0497D] wheat: who is truly the Saviour of the world.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the blessing of the Lord be his land.* ⟦s2⟧ To the letter, it signifies the fertility which the two tribes out of Joseph, that is, Ephraim and Manasses, had in abundance of crops, of fruits, and of pasture for their flocks. ⟦s3⟧ This is Basan, a most fertile region, whence it is interpreted *fatness.*

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ By the mildness of the sky, and by dew, the fruits, and the abundance of springs and of rivers, are delighted with man, which things are signified in the deep.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thou hast blessed, O Lord, thy land,* namely the Church, which is filled divinely, not by its own power, but by the blessing of the virtues. ⟦s2⟧ Whence: *Blessed be God, [0498A] who hath blessed us with every spiritual blessing in heavenly things in Christ [n: (Ephes. I)]*.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the heavens.* ⟦s2⟧ The apostles and evangelists are called heaven because of the unity of faith and of doctrine, of whom it is said elsewhere in the plural: *The heavens declare the glory,* etc. [n: (Psal. XVIII.)] For in them, shining with life and contemplation, God dwells as in heaven, thundering terror, raining down consolation, and flashing with miracles.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the dew.* ⟦s2⟧ Of heavenly preaching, which is of wondrous subtlety and grace, by which hearts are watered against the heat of temptation, and that they may always be green with virtues; whence: *God give thee of the dew of heaven,* etc. [n: (Gen. XXVII.)]

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the deep,* namely that it may become the fatter, bedewed from the spring that rises up, like the paradise of God. ⟦s2⟧ Whence: *A spring rose [0498B] out of the earth, watering the whole surface of the earth [n: (Gen. II)]*. ⟦s3⟧ From the sea his understanding abounded, and his thought from the great deep. ⟦s4⟧ For in vain does the word of the teacher water from without, unless the grace of the Creator waters from within. ⟦s5⟧ Whence he subjoins: *Of the fruits of the hills,* etc.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Of the sun.* ⟦s2⟧ Of Christ, who is the sun of justice, who says: *The just shall shine as the sun in the kingdom of their Father [n: (Matth. XIII)]*, because we shall be like to him.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And of the moon.* ⟦s2⟧ Of the Church, which receives these fruits from the sun, that is, from Christ: let her therefore always look upon the sun with full devotion, lest at any time, turned away, she lose the light.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Of the fruits of the everlasting hills.* ⟦s2⟧ The everlasting hills are the faithful of the Old Testament, humble sons founded with unmoved firmness upon the faith of the prophets and patriarchs [n: (unde: Mons domus Domini elevabitur super colles (Isa. II) )], with whose fruits the land of Joseph [0498C] is blessed, when the Church bears fruit in faith by the doctrine of Christ's prophecy and by the example of virtue. ⟦s3⟧ And it is to be noted that above, in the fruits and the dew of heaven, evangelical doctrine is signified, here, in the top of the ancient mountains and the fruits of the everlasting hills, the legal and prophetic; and by both the land of Joseph is enriched, because holy Church is formed by the pages of both Testaments and by the examples of the new Fathers and the old; whence: *All fruits, my beloved,* that is, *the new and the old, I have kept for thee [n: (Cant. VII)]*, and: *Every scribe instructed bringeth forth out of his treasure new things and old [n: (Matth. XIII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ The Manichaeans therefore and the Jews (because the former do not receive the Old, the latter the New Testament) cannot inherit in this rich land.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0498D]VERS. 16.-- *And of the fruits.* ⟦s2⟧ The Church, fruitful in the whole world with the fruit of justice and with the largess of the charisms, has received of the fulness of her Joseph; whence there follows:

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The blessing of him who appeared.* ⟦s2⟧ The whole Joseph, that is, head and body, is full of blessing, but the fulness of the blessing is chiefly in the head; whence: *Like the ointment on the head, which ran down upon the beard of Aaron,* etc. [n: (Psal. XXXII.)] He, pre-eminent over his brethren, that is, over the faithful, by the privilege of grace, shone forth with the sevenfold Spirit as a true Samson, a Nazarite from the womb of the Virgin, that is, consecrated to God, as it were with his seven locks untouched.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Upon the head of Joseph,* of Christ, who is the head of the Church, in whom all nations are blessed, of whose [0499A] passion and resurrection it is subjoined: *As of the firstborn of a bull,* etc.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As. [n: (AUG., quaest. 56.)]* The firstborn of a bull is his beauty. ⟦s2⟧ It is not to be read in such a way that he is called the firstborn of a bull: but, since he is the firstborn, his beauty is the beauty of a bull, because of the horns of the cross.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Christ is the fatted calf, whom the Father sacrificed for the returning Son as the firstborn out of the herd of the fathers, who on the third day was raised up and appeared in glory, who is the firstborn of the dead and the prince of the kings of the earth, and who after the glory of the resurrection adds horns out of the power of the passion.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Horns.* ⟦s2⟧ He compares Joseph to the horns of a rhinoceros, signifying that Ephraim would have the chief place among the ten tribes, over which Jeroboam [0499B] of the tribe of Ephraim reigned in the time of Roboam the son of Solomon.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The horns of a rhinoceros.* ⟦s2⟧ It signifies the horns of the cross, of which Habacuc says: *There is his strength hidden [n: (Habac. III)]*. ⟦s3⟧ For through the weakness of the flesh he is crowned, like a ram, with the thorns of the mockers, and by these horns he stuck fast; but by the incomparable strength of the passion he stood forth a unicorn, the conqueror of death, and of him who had the empire of death, whence: *That which is the weak thing of God is stronger than men [n: (I Cor. I)]*

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He shall winnow.* ⟦s2⟧ By the preaching of the cross, separating the chaff from the wheat: for the word of the cross is foolishness to them that perish.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The nations. ⟦s2⟧ Ask of me, and I will give thee the nations,* etc. [n: (Psal. II.)] For of the inheritance of the true Joseph (which is [0499C] the glorious land) all these things are said; and of its tillers, gathered out of both peoples, there follows:

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *These are the multitudes of Ephraim.* ⟦s2⟧ These two tribes out of Joseph are blessed not in themselves, but in their parent. ⟦s3⟧ They signify the sons of both peoples, who are blessed in Christ, according to that word: *In thy seed shall all nations be blessed [n: (Gen. XXII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ These the patriarch Jacob, blessing Ephraim and Manasses with his hands crossed in the manner of a cross, and preferring the younger to the firstborn, signified were to be blessed in the faith of Christ's cross, marking in the younger the people faithful from among the gentiles, lesser in time within the grace of Christ, but greater in faith: to whom the grace of the people of Israel passed over, of whose inestimable multitude it is said: *These are the multitudes,* etc., whence: *Many are the children of the desolate, more than of her [0499D] that hath a husband [n: (Isa. LIV)]*.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Manasses, deprived of the honour of the firstborn and yet blessed, is the Jewish people, which for the greater part of itself was reprobated for unbelief, yet in the remainder obtained the blessing, whence: *Blindness in part happeneth in Israel,* etc. [n: (Rom. II.)]

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *And to Zabulon.* ⟦s2⟧ Zabulon and Issachar received their passion in Galilee, where Christ chiefly taught, whence he chose the apostles. ⟦s3⟧ Through them therefore the apostles are designated, and to them gladness is promised in their going out, because going out from their own borders they subjected the people of the gentiles to Christ, and in their tabernacles for the conversion of the Jews, from whom they themselves also were chosen. ⟦s4⟧ For both peoples therefore gladness is promised them, present and to come: [0500A] because in the present they rejoiced over their conversion, and in the heavenly places they were rewarded with eternal gladness.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In the tabernacles,* of the Jews, whence: *May God enlarge Japheth, and may he dwell in the tents of Sem [n: (Gen. IX)]*: because in the churches of the Jews there dwells the breadth of the lesser people through the unity of faith.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *To the mountain.* ⟦s2⟧ Sion, namely, or Jerusalem, or contemplation and the height of the virtues.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They shall call.* ⟦s2⟧ Teaching that justice is only in the faith of Christ. ⟦s3⟧ *For the end of the law is Christ, unto justice to everyone that believeth [n: (Rom. X)]*. ⟦s4⟧ On the mountain they sacrifice the victims of justice, while they teach that all who come to Christ (in whom the Church is built) are justified not by the works of the law but by the faith of Christ, according to that word: *But the just [0500B] man liveth by faith [n: (Habac. I)]*. ⟦s5⟧ True Christians therefore are the victims of justice, which are sacrificed by the apostles, while they are taught to mortify earthly desires, that spiritual things may be made alive; whence: *If by the Spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you shall live [n: (Rom. VIII)]*.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *There they shall sacrifice.* ⟦s2⟧ There is no place of true sacrifice outside the catholic Church, which is set upon this mountain.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Who suck the flood.* ⟦s2⟧ These tribes gave themselves to trafficking, bringing back merchandise from regions across the sea, and hidden treasures, silver, namely, gold or gems, *they shall suck as milk,* that is, they shall receive them easily.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Who suck the flood of the sea.* ⟦s2⟧ It signifies the calling of the gentiles. ⟦s3⟧ For the sea signifies the whole race of men; [0500C] whence: *The kingdom of heaven is like to a net cast into the sea, and gathering together of all kinds of fishes [n: (Matth. XIII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ By the sea, the world; by the net, doctrine; by every kind of fishes, every sex, age and condition is understood. ⟦s5⟧ The apostles therefore sucked the flood of the sea as milk, because they drew the throngs of the peoples flowing together to the doctrine of the Gospel into the increase of Christ's body.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Hidden. ⟦s2⟧ The Lord knoweth who are his [n: (I Tim. II)]*. ⟦s3⟧ *For many are called, few,* etc. [n: (Matth. XX.)]

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The sands are the multitude of the unbelieving; the treasures, the precious souls of the elect. ⟦s2⟧ So therefore did they suck, that of the hearers of the Gospel some were carried over as vessels of honour into the treasures of the king, while others remained empty, like barren and [0500D] useless sands.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *And to Gad he said.* ⟦s2⟧ In the blessing of Gad he seems to foretell the might of the strong men, or the judgeship, who were of that tribe, such as Jephte, and many others.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In breadth.* ⟦s2⟧ Because all the fulness of blessing rested upon Christ, whence he blessed his own.
