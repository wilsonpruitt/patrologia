## CHAPTER XXXIII. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Gad.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, girded, signifies Christ, who, being about to fight against spiritual wickednesses, appeared in the weakness of the flesh girded with the power of the Godhead, according to that word: *The Lord is clothed with strength and hath girded himself [n: (Psal. CXII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ And elsewhere: *The Lord strong and mighty in battle,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ He is blessed in breadth, because not among the Jews alone, but among all [0501A] nations he received a most ample kingdom. ⟦s5⟧ Whence the Prophecy says:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *From the rising of the sun unto the going down thereof, the name of the Lord is worthy of praise.* ⟦s2⟧ But because he came to this glory through the humility of the passion, he subjoins concerning his death:

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He rested as a lion. ⟦s2⟧ I have power to lay down my life [n: (Joan. X)]*. ⟦s3⟧ The power of death, not the necessity, is expressed; whence: *No man taketh it away from me, but I lay it down. [n: (Ibid.)]*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He roared as a lion.* ⟦s2⟧ For whatsoever he said, he powerfully fulfilled.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And he took the arm and the head.* ⟦s2⟧ When, rising from the dead, he subjected to himself the strength of his enemies. ⟦s3⟧ He took also the head, that is, he bent the loftiness of worldly empire to the receiving of the faith; whence: [0501B] *And all kings shall adore him; all nations shall serve him [n: (Psal. LXXI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ There follows concerning the new state of his kingdom.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *And he saw his principality, that in his own portion.* ⟦s2⟧ When kingdoms and nations had been subjected to him, his power appeared, not by the terror of arms, but by the strength of doctrine.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Laid up.* ⟦s2⟧ Hidden, while he writes his laws in the hearts of the faithful; whence he says: *You have the unction from the Holy One, and you have no need that any man teach you; his unction teacheth you of all things [n: (Joan. II)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Or, *Laid up,* because in the law and the prophets he lay hidden in figures, and taught while concealed the things which he declared openly in the Gospel; whence: *Beginning from Moses and all the prophets, he expounded to them [n: (Luc. XXIV)]*.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0501C] *Who was with the princes of the people.* ⟦s2⟧ A helper, whence: *The Lord working withal and confirming the word with signs that followed [n: (Marc. XVI)]*, because he says: *Without me you can do nothing [n: (Joan. XV)]*.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He did justices.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, justifying those who believe in him, and at length he will judge according to their merits with Israel, that is, with his people chosen for the vision of God.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Justices.* ⟦s2⟧ The justifications of believers. ⟦s3⟧ For he himself justifies the ungodly out of faith. ⟦s4⟧ For neither shall any flesh be justified by the works of the law.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Dan also.* ⟦s2⟧ Others think that by Dan is signified Antichrist, whom they reckon will be born of this tribe: he is the whelp of the lion, that is, of the devil, who shall flow plentifully from Basan, because he is full of confusion; [0501D] for Basan is interpreted confusion, or fatness, or the locust.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Dan also means judgment, Basan ignominy. ⟦s2⟧ But Dan signifies Christ, who says: *For judgment I am come into this world [n: (Joan. IX)]*. ⟦s3⟧ The whelp of a lion, because of his strength, and because he deigned to become a little one; but taking upon himself our weakness, he did not lose his own power, *for that which is the weak thing of God is stronger than men [n: (I Cor. I)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Of him it is said: *The lion of the tribe of Juda hath conquered,* etc. [n: (Apoc. V.)] The ignominy of the cross indeed he bore from the Jews, who thought that his name would be blotted out by death: but he who through the weakness of that flesh was a whelp in the passion, appeared through the power of the Godhead an unconquered lion in the resurrection; whence the Apostle: *And although he was crucified through weakness, [0502A] yet he liveth by the power of God [n: (II Cor. XIII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ He flowed plentifully, therefore, as it were out of Basan, when, coming through the ignominy of death to the glory of immortality, he watered the whole world with the streams of the Gospel; whence Isaias: *The earth is filled with the knowledge of the Lord, as the covering waters of the sea [n: (Isa. II)]*.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Dan is a lion's whelp.* ⟦s2⟧ This he says because of Samson, who was of the tribe of Dan, and strong as a lion, whose possession reached as far as Basan. ⟦s3⟧ But Antichrist shall flow plentifully from Basan, because he shall be sated with the fatness of luxury and of all the vices.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He shall possess the sea and the south.* ⟦s2⟧ Because his possession reaches from the southern quarter westward as far as the Tyrrhenian sea.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He shall possess the sea and the south.* ⟦s2⟧ For through the apostles [0502B] the Church is won for Christ out of Jews and gentiles. ⟦s3⟧ For by the western sea (which is here understood) the Jews are figured, who serve the shadow and the copy, and have not the light of knowledge and of truth; and because for them the sun of justice has set, they have remained in perpetual darkness. ⟦s4⟧ This west the apostles possessed, when out of them they subjected to Christ very many enlightened by the grace of faith, that they who once were darkness might be light in the Lord. ⟦s5⟧ By the south is understood the Church gathered out of the gentiles, which, the veil of the law being taken away, enjoys the light of the Gospel, and lying down with the bridegroom at noon, contemplates with unveiled face the glory of the Lord.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *To Aser also he said: Blessed be Aser among his sons, let him be pleasing to his brethren.* ⟦s2⟧ Aser, blessed, that is, Christ, the hope of our beatitude, whose firstfruits he showed [0502C] to us in rising from the dead. ⟦s3⟧ He is blessed among his sons, because believers, born again through his grace, have filled the whole world with his likeness; to whom it is said: *Believe in the light, sons, that you may be of the light [n: (Joan. XII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ They bring forth the fruit of light in all goodness and justice and truth. ⟦s5⟧ And this was done because he was pleasing with true charity to his brethren, that is, to the apostles, whom he chose out of Judea, so that, the figures being despised, they might rejoice in the truth of the Gospel, and because *they were accounted worthy to suffer reproach for the name of Jesus.* ⟦s6⟧ And whence they had so great an abundance of charity, he adds, *let him dip his foot in oil,* etc.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And let him dip his foot in oil.* ⟦s2⟧ Christ our head, ascending into heaven, anointed most abundantly with the oil of the Holy Spirit the apostles — his feet, who were to go about the world preaching, while still standing on the earth [0502D] — that they might fail through no labour, but be strong with the brightness of unfading joy. ⟦s3⟧ He furnished also the incorruptible defence of a shoe, of which it is subjoined:

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Iron and brass cal. [n: (GREG., lib. XXXIV Moral., cap. 5.)]* This concerning the Church, etc., *as far as* with these shoes the true pasch is to be celebrated.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *As the days of thy youth.* ⟦s2⟧ It signifies that this tribe abounded in prosperity and delights until the old age of the captivity.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As.* ⟦s2⟧ To the Church is promised a raw and green old age: that as in the beginning she strongly overcame all assaults, so at the end that is at hand she may overcome the fury of Antichrist, whence: *Behold I am with you all days [0503A] even to the consummation of the world [n: (Matth. XXVIII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ And elsewhere: *My old age in plentiful mercy (Psalmus XCI)*.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *There is no God.* ⟦s2⟧ Marvelling at the things said above, in praise of Christ, who did all these things, he breaks out full of gladness.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Who rideth upon the heaven.* ⟦s2⟧ By his own power he ascended, and daily draws the Church to himself. ⟦s3⟧ *Draw me after thee, run in the odour of thy ointments [n: (Cant. I)]*.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The clouds.* ⟦s2⟧ The apostles, who rain with words, flash with miracles: whom, ascending into heaven, he sent to preach; whence: *The clouds gave forth a voice [n: (Psal. LXXVI)]*, which run to and fro with none to delay them, working signs and wonders not by their own power but by Christ's, and terrifying the hearts of mortals as with lightnings, whence: [0503B] *I have fought a good fight, I have finished my course [n: (II Tim. IV)]*.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *His dwelling place is above,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The body raised from the dead and placed at the right hand of the Father, of which it is said: Destroy this temple [n: (Joan. II)].

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The arms.* ⟦s2⟧ The power, namely, of the passion, which defends all, and which he received on the cross with arms outstretched.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Underneath the arms.* ⟦s2⟧ These arms, namely, defend those who fight against the true Amalec: nor are they weighed down with weariness, as Moses's were, but lift up with unwearied strength, until they obtain the true victory. ⟦s3⟧ Let us venerate therefore in the highest the firstfruits of our resurrection, in the lowest the sacrament of our redemption, that we ourselves also, the adversary being overthrown, may obtain the glory of immortality; concerning which it is promised to the true Israel: *He shall cast out [0503C] the enemy from before thy face.*

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And alone.* ⟦s2⟧ Living, namely, after his own manner and by his own laws, whence Balaam: *The people shall dwell alone, and shall not be reckoned among the nations [n: (Num. XXIII)]*.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *The eye of Jacob.* ⟦s2⟧ He who here sweated to supplant the vices, there shall unceasingly contemplate, with the lightest glance of the mind, the glory of Christ, who for our sake took the earth of true flesh: so that he by whose body and blood he was here fed through the sacrament of wheat and wine, may there be filled full with the perpetual vision of his divinity, whence: *I shall be satisfied when thy glory shall appear [n: (Psal. XVI)]*. ⟦s3⟧ And elsewhere: *I will manifest myself to him [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Then Jacob, turned into perfect Israel, shall see God face to face, with a knowledge of God so ample, so subtle, so delightful, filled as it were [0503D] with the lightest dew, that the knowledge of the preachers by which we are instructed in the present may seem to grow dark; whence: *Or knowledge shall be destroyed [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And elsewhere: *Dark water in the clouds of the air, before the brightness in his sight [n: (Psal. XVII)]*; and when it shall have been come to, all this doctrine shall cease, *and they shall all be taught of God [n: (Joan. VI)]*; whence he rightly adds: *Blessed art thou, Israel,* etc

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the heavens shall be darkened,* as though the dew were poured out so abundantly for the making fruitful of the earth, that the sight of men should grow dark. ⟦s2⟧ In which it is signified that the Jews, since they loved earthly wealth beyond measure, were darkened, and could not know Christ; whence: *If we let him alone so, all will believe in him,* etc.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0504A]VERS. 29.-- *Who is like to thee, O people?* ⟦s2⟧ As if to say: therefore blessed, therefore none is like to thee, because thou hopest for salvation not from thy own strength, but from the grace of Christ, who saveth his people from their sins.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the sword of glory.* ⟦s2⟧ In the world to come striking the adversaries with irrevocable severity, when he shall avenge the blood of his servants. ⟦s3⟧ The sword is now hidden here in the lowliness of those who suffer; it shall be revealed in the glory of those who rise again, and therefore it is called the *sword of glory* of Israel. ⟦s4⟧ Christ therefore as a shield protects the one who fights, and as a sword of glory shall avenge the one who triumphs. ⟦s5⟧ To him, as to the head, the glory of the body is referred at the end of the prophecy, when it is said:

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They shall deny.* ⟦s2⟧ They shall abominate and hate, like Balac, who asked Balaam to drive back the Israelites by cursing them.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0504B] *Thou shalt tread.* ⟦s2⟧ By subjecting to the Church those who abominate and persecute her.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is to be noted that there are only eleven blessings. ⟦s2⟧ For Simeon is not found among these blessings, in whom Judas is excluded from the apostles, only eleven remaining. ⟦s3⟧ Thence also by eleven encampments the sons of Israel came from Horeb to the place in which they received Deuteronomy.
