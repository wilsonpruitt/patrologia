## CHAPTER XXXIV

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *He went up therefore.* ⟦s2⟧ Moses as well as the other fathers of the Old Testament, lifted up in spirit to the height of contemplation, were able to foresee the grace of the New Testament, which is given in Christ, both by the conferring of the virtues and by the receiving of the heavenly kingdom: [0504C] but they could not await it in the flesh, and died in faith and hope, and were led down to the lower regions, where they awaited the coming of the Redeemer, who by the blood of his redemption should free them from thence.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And he showed him.* ⟦s2⟧ Moses saw the land of promise, and did not enter it, because the law foreshadowed in words and figures the coming of Christ and the doctrine of the Gospel, but led no man to perfection.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *All the land.* ⟦s2⟧ A part of all the land, so much as he could see from the highest mountain. ⟦s3⟧ Or he showed him all the land in spirit.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And he died there,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ God spares none of the elect, and avenges even the least sins, even in his friends; whence: *Moses and Aaron his priests,* and he added: *Thou wast propitious to them, and [0504D] taking vengeance on all their inventions.* ⟦s3⟧ The law, namely, being ended, which served the Lord in its own time, the city was destroyed and the altar, nowhere sacrifices, nowhere libations, nowhere priest or pontiff.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *In a valley.* ⟦s2⟧ On the mountain he died, in a valley he was buried, because the law was glorious in its own time: but compared with the Gospel it seems hidden and lowly; whence: *I gave them precepts that were not good.*

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And no man hath known.* ⟦s2⟧ The Jews assert that this was done lest the Jews, ever prone to idolatry, should worship a dead man as God; but according to allegory it signifies that the time and manner of the ending of the law are hidden in the foreknowledge of God alone, and that neither the depth of the wisdom of God, nor the secret of his counsel, lies open to anyone, [0505A] because his judgments are incomprehensible and his ways unsearchable.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Moses was a hundred and twenty years old.* ⟦s2⟧ If from one, in the natural order, as far as fifteen, thou shalt join together the sums of the single numbers — one, that is, and two and three, and so onward — they make a hundred and twenty. ⟦s3⟧ And fifteen is made up of seven and eight: seven is referred to the sabbath, eight to circumcision, because on the eighth day the boy was circumcised. ⟦s4⟧ But if thou look to the New Testament, thou hast the seven gifts of the Holy Spirit, and in the eighth the resurrection of Christ; the hundred and twenty therefore signify those who even in the Old Testament strive to fulfil the precepts of the Law and of the Gospel.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Moses was a hundred and twenty years old.* ⟦s2⟧ This signifies that he, as well as the other fathers of the Old Testament, after the perfection of the commandments of God, which is in the two [0505B] precepts of charity, are through the grace of Christ and the redemption of his blood made fit for eternal rest and for the life to come; whose *eye was not dimmed,* the gaze, namely, of holy intention was not darkened with any cloud, nor were the *teeth* of malice *moved,* because their order of discretion was not disturbed by the tempest of the vices, a hundred and twenty are the years of the lawgiver, by which number the height of Solomon's temple was confirmed. ⟦s3⟧ The primitive Church also, after the passion and the resurrection and the Lord's ascension, received the grace of the Holy Spirit in this number of men. ⟦s4⟧ For fifteen, which is made up of seven and eight, signifies the life to come, which is now carried on in the sabbath of souls and shall be perfected in the resurrection of bodies. ⟦s5⟧ Fifteen also, brought into a triangle, that is, reckoned together with its own parts, [0505C] makes a hundred and twenty. ⟦s6⟧ Fittingly therefore is the beatitude of the elect in the life to come signified by the hundred and the twenty, and the third upper room of the house of God is completed, because after the labours of the faithful, after the rest of souls, the full felicity of the Church shall be fulfilled in the glory of the resurrection.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0506A]VERS. 8.-- *And they wept.* ⟦s2⟧ Because death reigned even in those who had not sinned, from Adam unto Moses: therefore tears follow him as he goes down to the lower regions.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (HIERON., epist. ad Paulam.)] It is no wonder if to Moses and Aaron, etc., *as far as* but to be within, that is, to minister to the will of the Lord.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they were completed.* ⟦s2⟧ From this the custom seems to have arisen, that for the faithful departed the offices of piety are discharged for thirty days.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *He laid upon him.* ⟦s2⟧ In his Scriptures he bore witness to him, saying: *God shall raise up a prophet,* etc. [n: (Deut. XVIII)], whence: *If you did believe Moses, you would perhaps believe me also [n: (Joan. V)]*.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And there arose not,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This (as is reported) Esdras added of his own, who by a divine understanding restored the library burnt by the Chaldeans, and found the letters which the Jews now use, whence he was also called *a swift scribe* [0506B].

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And there arose not a prophet.* ⟦s2⟧ Because Moses was more excellent than all the prophets of the Old Testament: whence the Jews arrogantly say: *We are the disciples of Moses [n: (Joan. IX)]*. ⟦s3⟧ But after the Lord of the prophets came incarnate into the world, all that former dignity and shadow of things to come ceased: because *the law and the figure was given by Moses: grace and truth by Jesus Christ, who is the end of the law unto justice to everyone that believeth [n: (Hebr. X; Joan. I; Rom. X)]*.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And he buried him in the valley.* ⟦s2⟧ The Lord, namely. ⟦s3⟧ And by this it is signified that, the hindrance of nature having been removed through Christ, the clean soul, once separated, ascends to God: and the body which is buried remains in the earth until the judgment. ⟦s4⟧ Ecclesiastes XII: Until the dust [0506C] return into the dust whence it was, and the spirit return to God who gave it. ⟦s5⟧ Which return may he grant to be prosperous for us, who liveth and reigneth for ever and ever. ⟦s6⟧ *Amen.*
