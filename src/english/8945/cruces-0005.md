⚠ **No plate read exists for this work** (`data/plate-reads.json` records nothing

## 0005

- **0461A — *damnant*, and it is the one marker fired in this stint.** Migne prints
  *Sic in unaquaque anima agitur, ut maxima lucra conferant, aliquando minima
  damnant.* The sentence has no subject for *conferant* and the indicative
  *damnant* breaks out of the *ut* clause, so it does not construe. Gregory's sense
  (the source of the whole preceding paragraph, note `[n: (RAB., GREG.)]`) is that
  **the smallest losses sometimes confer the greatest gains** — i.e. *minima damna*
  (nom. pl.) as the subject of *conferant*. Rendered as printed and glossed
  additively under Pattern 18: `[cj: *damnant*; read *damna*, "the smallest
  losses"]`. Nothing was removed; the printed reading keeps its English.
- **0459D–0460A — Cassian's vice-names kept in italic Latin**: *gastrimargia*,
  *philargyria*, *acedia*, *cenodoxia*. These are the technical Greek terms of the
  eight-logismoi scheme, on the same footing as the register's *precaria* /
  *emphyteusis* rule; the plain-Latin members of the same list (*ira*, *tristitia*,
  *superbia*, *fornicatio*) are translated. Fixed for the whole work: do not vary.
  Note that *pigritia* (under *tristitia*) is rendered "laziness" and *acedia* is
  left in Latin, so the two are not collapsed.
- **0460B — the lemma and its gloss spell the name differently.** The lemma prints
  *Haetheum et Gergezaeum*, the Jerome extract immediately after prints *Hethaeum*.
  Both spellings are preserved ("the Haethite" / "the Hethite"): the variation is on
  the plate and is exactly the kind of datum a normalized English would destroy.
- **0461A — *Ἀπὸ τοῦ ἀναθήναι*.** Reproduced verbatim and untranslated (the
  following *id est, a sursum ponendo* is Migne's own gloss and is translated). The
  Greek infinitive as printed is not a form of ἀνατίθημι that a grammar will own
  (ἀνατιθέναι expected); whether this is Migne's type or Corpus Corporum's is not
  decidable from our files, so nothing is marked.
- **0460A — *Cappadocesve partem Hevaeorum*** has no verb of its own; *deleverunt*
  carries over from the preceding clause. Rendered with the elision preserved ("or
  the Cappadocians a part of the Hevites"), no supply.

## 0006

- **0462B, 0462D (end of CAPUT X), 0463B (end of CAPUT XI)** — three glosses end
  with **no terminal stop** on the plate (*…in montis vertice* · *…tendit in ardua
  virtus* · *…maledictioni subjacebunt*). Pattern 8: none was supplied, so the
  English stops unpunctuated too. This is not an omission.
- **0463D — double negation carried.** *meritis tuis non assignabis … non viribus
  tuis, sed Deo*: both negatives stand in the English ("thou shalt not assign to thy
  own merits … not to thy own strength, but to God"), though English would idiomatically
  drop the second.

## 0007

- **0465D — *In lacte,* mediocribus disciplinis, sed austerioribus.** The *sed* has
  no negative to answer: "by middling disciplines, but by more austere ones" is
  self-contradictory as printed. The expected shape is *non mediocribus … sed
  austerioribus*. **No negative was supplied** (Pattern 7a) and no marker fired —
  the printed words are all real words in their slots, and the missing particle is
  as likely our digitization's loss as Migne's; a plate read would settle it.
- **0464B–0466A — the *choerogyllus*** (*Per choerogyllum et leporem*) is kept in
  italic Latin: the animal has no settled English name and the gloss turns on its
  pairing with the hare, not on its identification.
- **0464C — *sed quae significando exprimunt, mores, scilicet hominum***: the
  relative has no expressed antecedent; rendered as the loose apposition it is
  ("but what they express by signifying are the manners, namely, of men").

## 0008

- **0467B — the verse labels run out of order**: *VERS. 19* (*De primogenitis*) and

## 0009

- **0469D–0470A — *VERS. 81.-- Si difficile.*** The lemma is Deut. 17:8, so the
  label should read **VERS. 8**; *81* is not a verse of this chapter, and the label
  that follows it is *VERS. 11*. Carried as printed. No `[sic:]` — this is a
  structural label rather than prose, our Latin is not the plate, and the gate of
  rule 8a stands; a plate read would decide whether the extra digit is Migne's.
- **0468C — *ad alteram diem explorationis hebdomadae septimae*** (Lev. 23:16).
  The received text reads *expletionis*, "of the completion" — which is what the
  passage needs, since the fifty days are being counted to their end, not to a
  searching-out. Rendered as printed ("of the searching out"). **`[var:]` withheld
  pending a plate read** (rule 8a); it is owed here.
- **0468D — *Quia si terrena domus hujus habitationis dissolvatur, habebit domum
  non manufactam in coelis*, cited `[n: (I Cor. V)]`.** Two divergences at once:
  (a) the citation is 2 Cor. 5:1, not 1 Cor. 5 — printed reference kept verbatim
  per the `refDisplay` rule, no correction in the text; (b) the received text has
  *habemus* ("we have"), Migne prints *habebit* ("he shall have"), which the gloss's
  own third-person frame (*Qui … utitur … potest*) actually supports. Rendered as
  printed. **`[var:]` withheld pending a plate read.**
- **0470A — *Supra cathedram Moysi sederunt scribae et pharisaei*** (Matt. 23:2)
  for the received *Super cathedram*. Rendered as printed ("Upon the chair"); the
  divergence is not material to the gloss, and is logged only so the absence of a
  marker is not read as agreement.
- **0470B — *intelligendum est, non debebis.*** Rendered "it is to be understood,
  thou oughtest not" — the point of Augustine's remark is that the future
  indicative *non poteris* has deontic force, so the English keeps the bare future
  of the lemma and the "ought" of the gloss distinct.
- **0468A–0468B — three separate glosses cite 1 Cor. 5**; each `[n: (I Cor. V)]` is
  reproduced at its own position, and the two quotations of *Epulemur* differ from
  one another as printed (*in azymis, non in fermento veteri* at VERS. 3; *non in
  fermento veteri, neque in fermento malitiae et nequitiae* at VERS. 4). Neither was
  conformed to the other or to the Vulgate — 7a″.
