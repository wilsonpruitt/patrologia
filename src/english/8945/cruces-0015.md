## 0482A — *cartalo* — no marker

Migne prints *ponamus in cartalo conversationis nostrae*; the Vulgate at Deut. 26:2
has *cartallo*. Single-*l* *cartalum* is an attested medieval spelling of
*cartallum*, "basket," so this is Pattern 9, not a defect: rendered normally as
"basket," no italics, no marker.

⚑ Incidentally noted at the same read: **the plate prints a comma at
*de Aegypto, sic nos*** which our TEI does not carry. Recorded as a transcription
loss, not a defect of Migne's; the English carries the comma, since Pattern 8
follows the plate.

## 0482C — *hoc legis implesse Tobiam* — no marker, rendered as printed

Migne prints, verbatim (plate-verified):

> VERS. 12. — *Quando compleveris:* hoc legis implesse Tobiam, sed qui longe erant
> a templo tertio anno collectas trium annorum decimas dabant pauperibus…

An accusative-and-infinitive (*implesse Tobiam*) with **no governing verb** — almost
certainly *legimus* has dropped out ("we read that Tobias fulfilled this of the law"),
and *hoc legis* is "this [precept] of the law." The English renders the printed words
as closely as they allow and leaves the clause hanging as the plate leaves it.

**Declined for Pattern 18** deliberately: the defect is a missing main verb, not a
false assertion, and 18a keeps the `[cj:]` bar to negations, correlatives, and
readings that make the page assert what the author is arguing against. Nothing here
asserts anything false; it simply fails to construe.

## 0483D — *trangressione* — `[sic: *trangressione*]`

keeps its verb (*permanere*), carries no negation and no conjunction inside the
marker, so nothing has to be supplied beside it.

## 0484D–0485A — the doubled clause — `[d: …]`

**Plate-verified.** Col. 484 ends *Et aere corrupto. Vita polluta ex carnali im-* and
col. 485 opens *munditia. Carnalis vita polluta ex carnali immunditia. Carnalis vita
per aerem exprimitur, quia aeris spiramine humanum corpus vivificatur.* The doubling
is Migne's, not our transcription's.

**The two copies differ, so Pattern 11a governs and the marked copy is argued, not
positional.** The intrusive copy is the **second** (*Carnalis vita polluta ex carnali
immunditia.*): its tail repeats the phrase immediately before it — the eye-skip's
source — and its head, *Carnalis vita*, is a false start on the sentence that then
actually runs, *Carnalis vita per aerem exprimitur*. Delete it and the passage reads
*Vita polluta ex carnali immunditia. Carnalis vita per aerem exprimitur, quia…*,
which is exactly the argument: corrupted air glosses a polluted carnal life, and
carnal life is figured by air because breath quickens the body. Here position and
argument agree — the second copy is both the later one and the intrusion — but the
reasoning is recorded because 11a requires it whenever the copies are not identical.

## 0488C — *In fidelibus* — `[sic: *In fidelibus*]`, sense supplied beside it

**Plate-verified**: Migne's own type reads *In fidelibus autem dicitur: Vos ex patre
diabolo estis.* This is a word-division defect of the split kind (Pattern 10),
*Infidelibus* set as two words, and it is the dangerous shape 7a⁗-b names: the
quarantine is a whole lexeme, and the lexeme carries the polarity.

Conjecture: *Infidelibus*, certain. The gloss is built as a contrast — *Dedit eis
potestatem filios Dei fieri, his qui credunt in nomine ejus* (John 1:12, of
believers), answered by John 8:44, *Vos ex patre diabolo estis*, which is addressed
to **unbelievers**. Read as printed, "among the faithful it is said: you are of your
father the devil" reverses the contrast the sentence exists to draw.

The whole broken run is carried per Pattern 10 (*In* stays inside it), and because
the run carried the polarity, **7a⁗ requires the force to stay outside the
quarantine**: the English reads "But to the unbelieving [sic: *In fidelibus*] it is
said." The marker says the type is Migne's; this note says what it was meant to be.

## 0490D–0491A — *terna flamma* — `[cj: *terna*; read *aeterna*, "eternal"]`

**Plate-verified on both sides of the break, and the break is clean:** col. 490 ends
*id est carnem, propter opera libidinis,* — at a comma, with no hyphen and no lost
syllable — and col. 491 opens *terna flamma comburet;*. So *terna* is what Migne
prints, and nothing was lost by the digitization.

*terna* is a real word (nom. fem. sg. of *ternus*, "threefold") and it occupies its
slot, agreeing with *flamma* — so Pattern 12 has nothing to wrap, and the faithful
English is "a threefold flame shall burn." That is the founding Pattern 18 shape
(11081 @0030B, *munde* for *mundo*): the page asserts something the author is not
arguing. His subject two sentences earlier is *aeternis suppliciis* and *ardebit
usque ad inferos*; a threefold flame appears nowhere in the passage and answers
nothing in it. The gloss is additive — Migne's word keeps its English and the
conjecture stands beside it.

⚠ Not Pattern 14: the divergence sits in the glossator's own prose, not inside the
quoted scripture. The quotation (*comedent terram et nascentia ejus*) closes before
*id est carnem*.

## 0491C — the Isaiah quotation — `[var: …]`

**Plate-verified**: Migne prints *Audite verbum Domini, principes Sodomorum Gomorrhae

Cause not speculated on, per Pattern 14: compression by the glossator quoting from

research question.

## ⛔ 0492B — *pre dexteram* — OUR defect, not Migne's. No marker fired.

**This is the finding of the stint that changes a file, and it goes the other way
from all the rest.** Our Latin twin (`src/latin/8945/0019.md`) reads:

> *Tollam in coelum manum meam, et jurabo **pre** dexteram meam…*

*pre* is not a Latin word and the obvious move was `[sic: *pre*]`. **The plate refutes
it.** Gallica f246, right column, band B, reads plainly:

> *Tollam in cœlum manum meam, et jurabo **per** dexteram meam, et dicam: Vivo ego in
> æternum.*

So **Migne prints *per*, and *pre* is Corpus Corporum's transcription error.** The
gloss on the same column confirms it independently — Migne's own next sentence
re-quotes the phrase as *Jurare per dexteram, id est per Filium*.

Consequences, and they are the point of rule 8a:
1. **The English renders *per*** — "and I will swear by my right hand" — because
   Pattern 7 renders what Migne's plate prints, and the plate prints *per*.
2. **No `[sic:]` was fired.** It would have accused Migne's type of a defect that is
   ours. This is the class 8a exists to prevent and it turned up on the first work
   where the check was actually run against a second witness.
3. ⬜ **Owed at merge: a TEI patch.** `data/tei-patches/8945.json` should correct
   *pre* → *per* with this provenance, after which 0019 needs re-chunking. It was not
   done inside this stint because four sibling stints were writing chunks of the same
   work concurrently and a re-chunk rewrites them all. **Until the patch lands, the
   Latin column on the reading page shows *pre* beside an English that says "by" —
   which is a visible inconsistency, and this note is the record of why.**
