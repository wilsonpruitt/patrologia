## 1. @0493B — the empty parentheses after the Hebrew names → `[ed:]`

Migne prints *Sinai ( ) interpretatur amphora mea…, Seir ( ) pilosus…, Pharan ( ) ferocitas eorum…*
Three parentheses, all empty in our source. The type inside them has been dropped in the
digitization: the same empty parenthesis appears in this work at chunk 0001, *Qui Hebraice*
**Choreb** *( )*, where what stands inside can only be the Hebrew form of the name. Rendered
faithfully the English shows a hole that reads as *our* bug — the 11055 case exactly — so **one
`[ed:]` is placed at the first occurrence and covers all three**, supplying nothing and naming
what recovery requires (Pattern 13, and 13a clause 3: the loss is not recoverable from our source).

⚠ **Merge note for the work-level cruces:** this class is not confined to my chunks. Chunk 0001
carries it too, and whatever that stint did with it and this must agree at merge time. If the
other stint rendered it silently, one of the two has to move.

## 2. @0496C — *pli* for *pii* → `[sic: *pli*]`, the stint's only carried type

*et quasi holocaustum ab ara **pli** cordis flamma devotionis in coelum subvolat.* The sense is
past doubt (*ab ara pii cordis*, "from the altar of a devout heart"), and *pli* is not a word,
so there is nothing to render: carried untranslated in italics under Patterns 7 and 10, and
marked under Pattern 12 so the defect is not read as ours. **7a⁗ check:** the marked run is a
single attributive adjective — the clause keeps its subject (*flamma devotionis*), its verb
(*subvolat*), and carries no negation, interrogative or conjunction inside the marker.
⛔ Plate read owed before this ships (§ head).

## 3. @0497C — *requiscet*: rendered, deliberately NOT marked

Lemma *Et inter humeros illius **requiscet**.* — for *requiescet*, and the gloss that follows
turns on the verb (*Tanto autem libentius Christus in illo **requievit***). Loss of *e* in hiatus
is an ordinary medieval orthographic reduction, i.e. Pattern 9's "attested spelling", not type
unique to this plate; and a `[sic:]` here would swallow the lemma's only verb, the shape 7a⁗ was
written against. **Rendered "he shall rest".** If a plate read later shows the sort itself is
broken, the marker can be added without touching the English.

## 4. @0497D — *homine delectantur*: no nominative in the sentence

*Temperie coeli, et rore fructus, et fontium et fluminum abundantia, homine delectantur, quae in
abysso significantur.* Every candidate subject is ablative and *homine* is singular ablative, so
the sentence has no nominative to govern *delectantur*. Rendered as closely as the printed words
allow ("…are delighted with man"). **Conjecture: *homines delectantur*** — "men are delighted by
the mildness of the sky, by dew, by fruits, and by the abundance of springs and rivers" — which is
what the surrounding fertility gloss is plainly saying. Declined for `[cj:]`: the English is
incoherent rather than false, and the word that went wrong is neither a negation nor a correlative
(Pattern 18a's scope, ruled 2026-08-18).

## 5. @0499D — *passionem* where the sense wants *possessionem*

*Zabulon et Issachar in Galilaea **passionem** acceperunt, ubi Christus maxime docuit.* The clause
is about tribal territory (Galilee is where their lot fell, and therefore where Christ taught and
chose the apostles), so *possessionem* is near-certain. **Rendered "received their passion in
Galilee"** — Pattern 7, no silent repair. Declined for `[cj:]` on the same ground as §4: a
substitution that leaves the sentence odd, not a false assertion of the class Pattern 18 admits.

## 6. Punctuation carried against the English grain (Pattern 8)

- **@0494D, Isa. 1:19** — Migne prints *Si volueritis et audieritis, me bona terrae comedetis*,
  with the comma falling **before** *me*, where the Vulgate has *audieritis me, bona terrae
  comedetis*. Rendered with the pronoun left where the plate puts it ("…and shall hearken, me you
  shall eat the good things of the land"). The comma is not deleted and the pronoun is not moved.
- **@0496C** — *pro vitulis pergentes mugiebant sed accepto, itinere gressus non deflectebant*:
  the comma falls **inside** the ablative absolute. English syntax has no position for a mark
  there; rendered "but, the journey once taken up, they did not turn their steps aside", i.e. the
  mark is kept but re-sited by one word. Logged rather than silently smoothed.
- **@0505B** — *quia non est perturbatus tempestate vitiorum, ordo discretionis eorum centum et
  viginti sunt anni legislatoris quo numero…* is one run-on with a single comma and no full stop.
  Kept as a run-on; no stop or semicolon supplied.
- **@0503D** — *Beatus es, Israel,* **etc** ends the paragraph with no point after *etc*. The
  English ends the same way.
- **@0494B** — the plate prints **VERS.--3.--** (dashes before the numeral, unlike every other
  verse label in the work). Reproduced as printed.

## 7. Scripture as Migne prints it — divergences logged, none marked `[var:]`

Every lemma in this stint was read word by word against the printed Latin before the English was
written (7a″). The divergences found are all of the kind that a plate read could reassign to the

## 9. Two marker-class judgments, stated so a later reader can overturn them

- **@0498B — `[n: (unde: Mons domus Domini elevabitur super colles (Isa. II) )]` stays `[n:]`,
  untranslated.** It is longer than a locator and it quotes a verse, but its content is a
  proof-text with its own citation, i.e. apparatus, not editorial *prose* making a statement
  (Pattern 15's test is content, and this is a citation carrying its quotation). It is the only
  note in the stint where the call was close.
- **@0502D — *Ferrum et aes cal.* is carried untranslated, unmarked.** The lemma cue truncates
  mid-word (*calceamentum*), and a truncation is not a defect: it is Migne abbreviating a lemma he
  expects the reader to complete — the gloss immediately below supplies it (*Incorruptibile quoque
  praebuit **calceamenti** munimentum*). Carried as printed in its italic run, on the L4 principle;
  no `[sic:]`, because nothing is broken.
- **@0503A — the inline *(Psalmus XCI)*** sits inside the italic quotation as running text, not as
  an `[n:]` note. Kept verbatim and in place; it is not promoted to a note marker, which would
  change the note count against the twin.

## 10. Negation audit (Pattern 7a)

Every `non · nec · neque · nisi · nullus · nihil · nunquam · nusquam` in the four chunks was
checked against its English, in both directions. Count of negative particles in the Latin body and
of their English exponents agree at every site; nothing was dropped and nothing supplied. Three
places where the printed negation makes the English harsher than a smooth reading would like, and
all three stand:

- **@0493B** *prohibere novit, **non** juvare: **nec** spiritu dilectionis liberat* — "it knows how
  to forbid, not to help: nor does it set free by the spirit of love".
- **@0504D** *nusquam sacrificia, nusquam libamina, nusquam sacerdos vel pontifex* — all three
  *nusquam* carried ("nowhere… nowhere… nowhere"), not compressed into one English negative.
- **@0505A** *nec profunditatem sapientiae Dei, vel secretum consilii alicui patere* — the *nec*
  is carried onto both members ("neither the depth… nor the secret… lies open to anyone").

Also carried: *Dedi eis praecepta **non** bona* (@0504D, Ezech. 20:25), *in eos qui **non**
peccaverunt* (@0506A), *oculus **non** caligavit* / *nec dentes moti* (@0505B), *Non est ita
legendum* (@0499A), *quia **non** de tua virtute* (@0504A), *Neque enim ex operibus legis
justificabitur omnis caro* (@0501C), and *non necesse habetis ut aliquis doceat vos* (@0501B).
