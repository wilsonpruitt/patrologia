## CHAPTER II.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *I said therefore.* [n: (HIER.)] [var: Vulg. Eccl. 2:1 *Dixi ego in corde meo*, "I said in my heart"] After that in the multitude of wisdom, etc., *as far as* then it will not be vanity, but truth.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Laughter I counted error.* [n: (ID.)] Where we say, etc., *as far as* they promise themselves glad and prosperous things.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *I thought in my heart.* [n: (ID.)] Drunkenness, etc., *as far as* what is to be shunned.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *I planted vineyards.* ⟦s2⟧ In the house of a rich man, etc., *as far as* without which the other trees will be dried up.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *And I made me ponds,* etc., *as far as* and awaits the late rain from heaven.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1119B]VERS. 7.-- *I got me servants:* those serving through fear, and those desiring spiritual things, and not fearing.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ We call those homeborn who are indeed of the Church, and go before the aforesaid menservants and maidservants; nor yet are they free.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The homeborn are said to be the sons of servants, who were born in the house of their lord.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Herds also and great flocks of sheep.* ⟦s2⟧ These are they who, without reason and without knowledge of the Scriptures, do indeed labour in the Church, but do not yet come to the man, that is, to the image of God.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (HIER.)] *And indeed the possession of a herd.* ⟦s2⟧ Note that, etc., *as far as* or homeborn.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *The substance of kings.* [n: (ID.)] The teachings of the philosophers, etc., *as far as* and he reproves the prudence of the prudent [1119C].

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I made me singing men.* ⟦s2⟧ A singer, etc., *as far as, Singing men and singing women,* that either sex might delight me.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The delights of the sons of men.* ⟦s2⟧ Wisdom, namely, which, like a paradise, has various fruits and manifold delights. ⟦s3⟧ Of which the Psalmist: *Delight in the Lord, and he will give thee the requests of thy heart [n: (Psal. XXXVI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And, *Thou shalt make them drink of the torrent of thy pleasure [n: (Psal. XXXV)]*.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Who were before me in Jerusalem.* ⟦s2⟧ According to the letter: It is not great praise. ⟦s3⟧ For before Solomon there reigned Saul, in whose time the kingdom began, and David, under whom the kingdom was small enough.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It continued.* ⟦s2⟧ Others, *it stood,* that is, wisdom remained with him while he was placed in the flesh, not increasing by moments, as in us, but standing in fulness.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1119D]VERS. 10.-- *And all things which.* ⟦s2⟧ But by how much these things grew with zeal, by so much was wisdom lacking, because it hurried [me] through all desires, and I thought this to be the fruit of my labours, if I should serve lust; but at last, returning into myself, I saw that all things are vain.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Under the sun.* [n: (HIER.)] Christ. ⟦s2⟧ In the sun he hath set his tabernacle. ⟦s3⟧ He therefore who has not yet come to the brightness of the sun and to its order and constancy, in him Christ does not dwell.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *And I saw that so far.* ⟦s2⟧ When I was thinking this, that the wise man is light, the fool darkness: the eyes of the one in heaven, of the other on earth, there suddenly stole upon me [the thought], why they perish by a common death, are pressed down by a common misery, and the like.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1120A]VERS. 18.-- *Again I detested.* [n: (HIER.)] By spiritual labour the saints meditate day and night in the law of the Lord, etc., *as far as* for it is not wisdom to gather earthly riches.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Being about to have.* ⟦s2⟧ This befell Solomon himself, who had a son Roboam not like himself. ⟦s3⟧ From which we understand that a son is not even worthy of his father's inheritance, if he be foolish.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *For when another.* [n: (ID.)] Above, concerning the uncertain heir, etc., *as far as* from reason.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Is it not better.* [n: (ID.)] After I had gone through all things, etc., *as far as* as much as suffices?

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *To the good man.* [n: (ID.)] To him that corrects his own manners, etc., *as far as* and to be composed by the presumption of the spirit.

## CHAPTER III.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1120B]

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *All things have their time.* [n: (HIER.)] Above he taught the uncertain and fluctuating state of the human condition, etc., *as far as* they are contained neither by place nor by time.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *A time to plant,* etc. [n: (ID.)] It is doubtful to none, etc., *as far as* because perfect charity casteth out fear.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *A time to destroy,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ We cannot build up good things, unless we have first destroyed the evil ones, as it was said to Jeremias by God, that he should first root up and dig under and destroy, and afterwards build and plant [n: (Jerem. I)].

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *A time to mourn,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ We must mourn now, that in the time to come we may dance that dance [1120C] which David [danced] before the ark of the testament [n: (II Reg. VI)]. ⟦s3⟧ Displeasing to the daughter of Saul, he pleased God the more.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *A time to scatter stones,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] I wonder, etc., *as far as* and calls to penance.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A time to embrace.* ⟦s2⟧ A time to embrace wisdom, and to cleave more strictly to contemplation; and a time to be made far from the embrace, namely of wisdom, that we may serve the care of the time, and those things without which we cannot live.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *A time to keep silence, and a time.* [n: (HIER.)] We teach in the churches what we do not know. ⟦s2⟧ We do not learn any of the arts without a teacher, this alone is so cheap and so easy that it does not need a master. ⟦s3⟧ Nothing seems to us to be right, save what we learn, so that after much silence we may be made masters out of disciples.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1120D] *A time to speak.* ⟦s2⟧ The discipline of Pythagoras is to keep silence for five years, and afterwards, being learned, to speak.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *A time of love.* ⟦s2⟧ A time of loving, after God, wives, children, kinsfolk, and a time of hating them, in martyrdom. ⟦s3⟧ Or, *Because now we see through a glass in a dark manner [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*, etc. ⟦s4⟧ There will be a time of loving the presence of God, and a time of hating, when, beholding face to face and advancing unto better things, we shall begin to hate and to despise what we have loved.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A time of war.* ⟦s2⟧ Now, for those not doing penance; a time of peace in the time to come, when all Israel shall be saved; *A time of war,* be not secure, take the armour of God.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1121A]VERS. 10.-- *I have seen the affliction which he gave,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] It is said that God therefore, etc., *as far as* he made them to grow, to remain and to be changed even to the end of the age.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *And I have known.* [n: (HIERON.)] Therefore man was set as a husbandman in the world, etc., *as far as* hence:

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Every man that eateth and drinketh,* etc., For many now are the tribulations of the just, but in the time to come there shall be no labour in Jacob.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I have learned,* etc. [n: (ID.)] There is nothing new in the world, the course of the sun, the changes of the moon, the dryness of the earth and of the trees, or their greenness, were founded together with the world; and these things God therefore tempered by a fixed reckoning, that we might understand the Providence of God in them, and might fear before the face of God. ⟦s2⟧ But the countenance of the Lord is upon them that do evil things.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1121B]VERS. 16.-- *I saw under the sun.* [n: (ID.)] When in this world I sought judgment and truth, etc., *as far as, For all things shall be required in their own time.*

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *I said in my heart.* [n: (ID.)] As if, etc., *as far as* and that the earth is filled with cattle and herds.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Who knoweth.* [n: (ID.)] He does not say, etc., *as far as* and be a certain asserter of a thing as though doubtful.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Downward.* ⟦s2⟧ To hell. ⟦s3⟧ For it can come to pass that they are quickly changed, and often those who seemed learned are led down to hell; and simple countrymen are crowned.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *And I have found.* ⟦s2⟧ Troubled by the former error, etc., *as far as* which are to be in the world afterwards.

## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *I turned myself to other things.* ⟦s2⟧ This place [1121C] David in Psalm LXXII, and Jeremias in his own volume, pursue more fully.

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And I praised.* [n: (HIER.)] Others understand this place thus, etc., *as far as* and that they are blessed so long as they enjoy the angelic choir.

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3. ⟦s2⟧ *And happier.* ⟦s3⟧ Because he neither saw these evils, nor experienced them: nor according to the error of those who think that souls exist before they become men. ⟦s4⟧ It is therefore better not to be at all than to live ill: whence, *It were better for him if that man had not been born,* etc. [n: (Matth. XIV)]. [var: Vulg. Matt. 26:24 *bonum erat ei, si natus non fuisset homo ille*, "it were good for him if that man had not been born"]

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *It is better.* [n: (ID.)] After the anxieties and the miseries, etc., *as far as* we are not able to receive the heat of eternal life.

⟦p48⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Better is a child.* [n: (ID.)], *as far as [1121D] the end of the chapter.*
