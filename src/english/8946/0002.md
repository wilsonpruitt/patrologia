## CHAPTER V.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Be not rash, God.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he hears and sees all things, and reproves thy folly, although he himself is in heaven, and thou on earth.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2. ⟦s2⟧ *Many cares.* ⟦s3⟧ As it were, just as he who thinks upon many things frequently dreams of those very things, so he falls back into folly who disputes of God beyond his measure. ⟦s4⟧ Or, because we now see through a glass and in a dark manner, as it were through sleep, we reckon that we hold what we do not have, and yet we have nothing.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *When thou hast vowed a vow.* [n: (HIER.)] [var: Vulg. Eccl. 5:3 *Si quid vovisti Deo, ne moreris reddere*, "if thou hast vowed any thing to God, defer not to pay it"] It is better not to promise, etc., *as far as, he shall be beaten with many stripes.*

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1122A]VERS. 4.-- *And much better.* ⟦s2⟧ As the Jews who said: *All things whatsoever God hath commanded us, we will do [n: (Exod. XXIV, XXXII)]*: and they adored the calf. [var: Vulg. Exod. 24:3 *Omnia verba Domini, quae locutus est, faciemus*, "all the words of the Lord, which he hath spoken, we will do"]

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Give not thy mouth.* ⟦s2⟧ As if, say not that thou sinnest out of the infirmity of thy flesh, as though turning back the fault upon its author.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Before the angel.* ⟦s2⟧ Who stands by every man, and announces all things to God: think not that all things lie hidden from God.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Where there are many dreams.* ⟦s2⟧ As if, *Give not thy mouth,* etc., because this life is as it were dreams, and many things seem true which are false; so also is thy excuse.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *If thou shalt see the oppressions.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: *Say not before the angel, There is no Providence:* which is against those who think that all things are done by chance, and deny the providence of God. ⟦s3⟧ *If* nevertheless *thou shalt see the oppressions of the needy,* [1122B] etc.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Of all the earth,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ God is patient and a repayer, who says, *When I shall take a time, I will judge justices.*

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Sweet is the sleep.* [n: (HIER.)] The covetous rich man, etc., *as far as* for they shall see God.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *There is also another infirmity.* ⟦s2⟧ We can [understand] these things of the philosophers, etc., *as far as* since it is written: *I will destroy the wisdom of the wise,* etc.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *This therefore hath seemed good to me,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He says that it is better, etc., *as far as* which the grace of God inspires in helping good works.

## CHAPTER VI.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1122C]VERS. 1.-- *There is also another evil.* ⟦s2⟧ He describes, in another manner also, the covetous rich man, who, although he has all good things, none the less torments himself with excessive sparingness, not knowing for whom he gathers.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *A man to whom God hath given riches.* [n: (HIER.)] This can be taken of the people of Israel, etc., *as far as* we shall all hasten to judgment.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Burial.* [n: (ID.)] So covetous that he does not even think of burial, or, often, being slain for his riches, he is cast forth unburied: or, because he leaves himself no memory, though he abounds in all good things.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *All the labour:* that is, what a man, that is a rational being, has learned by labouring, he has in his mouth, because he teaches others.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *His soul shall not be filled.* [n: (ID.)] Because he always [1122D] desires to learn what he may teach: and this he has more than the fool, because with the blessed poor man he tends toward Christ, who is life and who says: *Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven.*

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *He that is to be.* [n: (ID.)] Of all men who are to be surrounded with a body, God already knows the name; that a man may not be able to answer back against his maker, why he was made thus, or thus. ⟦s2⟧ For by how much the more we shall have inquired, by so much the more is our vanity shown, and our words superfluous; and free will is not taken away by the foreknowledge of God, but causes go before, why each thing was made.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *There are words.* ⟦s2⟧ As if, the Son of God indeed is to be man, but do thou not inquire further [1123A] of what kind, in what manner and of what sort, but believe his generation.

## CHAPTER VII.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *A good name is better.* [n: (HIER.)] The memory of good works, which delights the hearts of hearers, as ointments delight the nostrils: Whence, *The memory of the just is in praise, the name of the wicked shall rot.* [var: Vulg. Prov. 10:7 *Memoria justi cum laudibus, et nomen impiorum putrescet*, "the memory of the just is with praises"]

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the day.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, It is better by dying to escape the torments of a wretched life, and the tribulations, and the labours, than to be born unto these things and to endure them.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Anger is better,* against one's own sin or another's, because thou correctest, not because thou desirest vengeance [n: (Prov. XXVII)].

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *It is better to be reproved by a wise man.* ⟦s2⟧ Better are the wounds of a friend than the deceitful kisses of an enemy. [1123B] *The words of a flatterer, more senseless, unto thorns,* that is, stirring the cares of the world, which lead to the burnings of Gehenna: the perfect wise man needs no subtlety, is troubled by no false accusation. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore it is to be understood of the wise man set in progress, who is sometimes troubled at the iniquity of a judgment, when God does not punish at once.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Better is the end.* ⟦s2⟧ Perfection. ⟦s3+4⟧ *He that shall have persevered shall be saved.* [var: Vulg. Matt. 24:13 *qui autem perseveraverit usque in finem, hic salvus erit*, "but he that shall persevere unto the end, he shall be saved"] Better is justice consummated than begun, and the epilogues of a speech than its openings; here the contest, there the victory.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Than the arrogant.* ⟦s2⟧ Contrary to this arrogant man is he who in the Gospel is read of as poor in spirit [n: (Matth. V)].

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *What thinkest thou?* ⟦s2⟧ Otherwise: Thou oughtest so to live that the present days may always be better to thee than the past. ⟦s3⟧ For it is always the time of working, lest, when [1123C] thou hast begun to decrease, it be said: *You did run well, who hath hindered you not to obey the truth? [n: (Gal. V.)]*

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Consider the works of God.* ⟦s2+3⟧ How great, how wonderful; one he has chosen by gratuitous mercy, another he despises by a just judgment, whence, *Jacob I have loved, Esau I have hated [n: (Mal. I)]*. [var: Vulg. Mal. 1:2-3 *et dilexi Jacob, Esau autem odio habui*, "and I have loved Jacob, but have hated Esau"] And, *All the ways of the Lord are mercy and truth.*

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 15.-- *The evil day* [n: (HIER.)] of judgment, which seems evil to the evil, whence, *That day is a day of wrath,* etc. [var: Vulg. Soph. 1:15 *Dies irae dies illa*, "a day of wrath is that day"] Or, good things and evil, that is prosperous and adverse, according as they shall have justly befallen, bear thou manfully. ⟦s3⟧ For all things [come] justly, though hiddenly, and this is *that he may not find,* etc.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *And be not. ⟦s2⟧ The wicked shall not live out half their days,* as if, Do not, being impenitent, provoke God, that he may inflict punishment even upon one yet living, as he did to Dathan [1123D] and Abiron. [var: Vulg. Ps. 54:24 *Viri sanguinum et dolosi non dimidiabunt dies suos*, "bloody and deceitful men shall not live out half their days"]

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *He neglecteth nothing.* ⟦s2⟧ He does good to all without respect of persons. ⟦s3⟧ Or, he provides in prosperous things and in adverse.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Ten princes.* [n: (HIER.)] The angels, who are set over our city, that is the Church, as guardians, that is as helpers. ⟦s2⟧ And they are called ten, because the number ten is perfect.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *But also to all.* ⟦s2⟧ He teaches the wise man not to attend to the tongues of slanderers, lest, when he himself does the same, he should wish himself to be pardoned and others to be reproved. ⟦s3⟧ For if he shall have done this, he will always be curious and angry, because he will not even be able to restrain the tongues of his servants.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Of hunters,* of demons, who hunt [1124A] our souls, whose nets are women, and whose dragnets are heretics.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Behold this.* ⟦s2⟧ This I have found, etc., *as far as* the woman is readier for a fall.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *That I might find the reason.* ⟦s2⟧ Whatever thing or discernment there is between men and their manners: who is perfectly good save Christ alone?

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *A man.* ⟦s2⟧ Christ, who is the head of all good men, which is figured by the number of a thousand, which is a perfect number.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *Only this.* [n: (HIER.)] Lest he should seem to condemn the common nature of all men, and to accuse God its author, he watchfully added, *Only,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: Man indeed was made good, but being left to free will, [1124B] he falls of his own accord and mingles himself with vain and superfluous cares.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Who is such as the wise man,* etc. [n: (ID.)] He said above that a good man is scarcely found, though men were made good by God. ⟦s2⟧ Now, as one giving thanks, he enumerates what good things [God] gave them, wisdom, reason, to know hidden things.

## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *I observe the mouth of the king.* [n: (HIER.)] He seems indeed to command according to the Apostle, etc., *as far as* his will is to be obeyed.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Neither can any man say to him,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The commandments of God are not to be discussed, why he commanded them, but to be done.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *It is not in man's dominion.* [n: (ID.)] [var: Vulg. Eccl. 8:8 *Non est in hominis potestate*, "it is not in man's power"] As if, [1124C] thou oughtest not to take it ill, if thou canst not know things to come, if thou art often oppressed by more powerful wicked men, because all things are quickly ended, and death snatches all things equally.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *I saw the wicked buried,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Not even in death does it repent them, of whom, by how much the accusation is rarer and more difficult, by so much the greater does the liberty of sinning seem to them to be.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *There are just men to whom evils happen,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As to the sons of Aaron sacrificing, according to the Hebrew, and Manasses, after so great evils, being brought back from captivity into the kingdom, lived happily.
