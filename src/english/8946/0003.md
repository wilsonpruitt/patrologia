## CHAPTER IX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *There are just men,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *Better is a dog.* [n: (HIER.)] The living dog, the poor just man, the dead lion, the powerful [1124D] wicked man, who carries away nothing with him out of the world.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *But the dead.* [n: (ID.)] Certain men assert that they increase even after death, etc., *as far as* rational creatures, and to merit.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *They shall be.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, they shall avail thee after death.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *War.* ⟦s2⟧ Of the victors, or also the imitators of war, according to that: *Without me you can do nothing.*

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Riches.* ⟦s2⟧ Of good works.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *As fishes shall be taken.* ⟦s2⟧ The heretics also have their own dragnet cast, and snares wherewith they may take the simple.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *A poor man.* ⟦s2⟧ Christ, of whom the Psalm: [1125A] *I am poor and sorrowful [n: (Psal. LXVIII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ And in whom all the treasures of wisdom and of knowledge are hidden [n: (Col. II)].

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *By his own wisdom.* ⟦s2⟧ Not by power.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *The words of the wise.* [n: (HIER.)] Who seek not favour and applause, but the salvation of their hearers: nor do they cry out that they may be heard, but they desire that they may be heard favourably.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Among fools.* ⟦s2⟧ Fools are they who seek not to profit, but to acquire praise for themselves.

## CHAPTER X.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Place.* ⟦s2⟧ Hold the station of faith, lest thou be overcome: and do not accomplish it, if thou hast already thought evil.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He that diggeth.* ⟦s2⟧ He opened a pit, and dug it out: and he fell [1125B] into the ditch which he made.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *If he bite.* [n: (HIER.)] As if, just as a serpent, biting secretly, pours in venom, so also does he who detracts. ⟦s2⟧ Or, the serpent, that is the devil, bites in silence, when he who has sinned, at the devil's persuasion, is unwilling to confess.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 19.-- *In laughter they make bread and wine, that they who drink may feast.* [var: Vulg. Eccl. 10:19 *In risum faciunt panem et vinum ut epulentur viventes*, "that they who live may feast"] They preach, more that they may allure than that they may profit.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *All things obey money,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ And the priests taught for hire.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *In thy thought,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He simply admonishes that we should not, either through anger or through levity, speak evil of our superiors, because often the things which we have said are reported to them, even when we were not afraid, even if in the bedchamber.

## CHAPTER XI.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1125C]

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Send bread.* [To those] fit for fruit-bearing.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Give portions seven.* [n: (HIER.)] The Old Testament, whose seventh day, namely the sabbath, is especially honoured; *and also eight,* which is the New; for without the one the other avails not.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *If the tree fall to the south, or to the north,* [n: (ID.)] as if he should say: The place which thou shalt have prepared for thyself here, thou shalt have in the time to come.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *He that observeth the wind soweth not.* [n: (ID.)] As if he should say: Bestow thyself upon the Almighty, or bodily comfort, or the word of God (lest perchance thou pass by him who is worthy to receive); and at every time. ⟦s2⟧ *He that observeth the wind.* ⟦s3⟧ In the midst of the tempest one must [1125D] sow, because we know not what is the will of the spirit, and this is, *How,* etc.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *In the morning sow thy seed,* etc. [n: (ID.)] [1126A] At every time, namely from morning unto evening, and conversely: or in boyhood and in old age. ⟦s2⟧ *For he that perseveres shall be saved.* [var: Vulg. Matt. 24:13 *qui autem perseveraverit usque in finem, hic salvus erit*, "but he that shall persevere unto the end, he shall be saved"]

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Rejoice therefore, O young man,* etc. [n: (ID.)] From here unto the end of the book, etc., *as far as* they may know that they are to be judged.

## CHAPTER XII.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ From here unto the end of the chapter it is read in three ways, namely of judgment, of old age, of captivity, and certain glosses serve sometimes two senses, but sometimes they are found according to all the senses.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Before the sun and the light, and the stars and the moon be darkened,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] The sun shall be turned into darkness, etc., *as far as* the clouds and the rains cease for him.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1126B]VERS. 4.-- *And they shall shut the doors in the street,* lest they see the sports of the young: for all such things are unbearable to them.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they shall rise up.* ⟦s2⟧ Because they cannot sleep, the blood growing cold, the moisture dried up by which sleep is nourished.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *The caper tree shall be destroyed.* [n: (HIER.)] The caper avails for the reins, etc., *as far as* but in adversity they desist from good.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Before the cord be broken.* ⟦s2⟧ He returns to the things above, where he says: *Remember thy Creator,* etc., and with a like ending he concludes the sentence.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And return.* ⟦s2⟧ Before thou return into Babylonia whence thou didst go forth in the loins of Abraham, [1126C] and begin to be worn away in Mesopotamia, whence thou didst set out.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Vanity of vanities.* ⟦s2⟧ After the description of human frailty and of death, he beautifully concludes what he had proposed: *Vanity of vanities.* ⟦s3⟧ As if, be not wanton, seek not delights, since thou knowest that thou passest away by a swift end and hastenest to judgment.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *The words of the wise.* [n: (HIER.)] Lest he should seem rashly to forge new precepts after the law given through Moses, God himself forewarning, he says that these are the words of the wise.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *The end of speaking.* ⟦s2⟧ Because, as a public speaker, he had brought forward the diverse opinions of diverse men: by this ending he reconciles them all, namely *Fear God.* ⟦s3⟧ This alone, the Hebrews say, was held in authority, [1126D] because here he shows his own intention, although in many things he seems to exhort to pleasures.
