## CHAPTER ONE.

[1183C]

VERS. 1.-- *All wisdom,* etc. [n: (RAB. in Eccli., tom. III.)] He begins from the eternal wisdom of God, which is Christ, who is always with the Father before the ages, according to that word: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God [n: (Joan. I)]*. *Is from the Lord God.* Because Christ, the fountain of life, the true light, is born of the Father, *and all things were made by him, and without him was made nothing [n: (Ibid.)]*. Whoever lacks this wisdom walks in darkness, and knows not where he goes. *For the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God; and the prudence of the flesh is death [n: (Sap. XI)]*.

VERS. 2.-- *The sand of the sea, and the drops of rain.* [n: (RAB. ubi supra )]. He compares bodily things with spiritual, so that from the most difficult a thing altogether impossible may be estimated. [1183D] For if these things, which were established in a fixed number and measure and weight, no one can count, how much less shall he be able to search out the wisdom of God, which is without beginning and without end, unutterable, inestimable?

VERS. 4.-- *She was created before all things.* Created according to his humanity in the counsel of God the Father, that is, he was predestined to be incarnate for the salvation of the human race. And it is to be noted that, on account of the unity of the person, Christ is sometimes said to be begotten, sometimes created.

VERS. 5.-- *The fountain of wisdom.* The origin of all wisdom proceeds from the Word of God, which, God from God, abides always with the Father in the heavens; but having entered the world through the dispensation of his humanity, it gave the commandments of salvation, whence: *Thou hast the words of eternal life [n: (Joan. VI)]*.

VERS. 6.-- *The root of wisdom.* He shows that no one is able [1184C] to penetrate the depth of God, or the [sic: *magnituditudinem*] of his goodness and mercy. Wherefore, for the liberation and enlightenment of the human race, he deigned to become man; whence: *Who shall declare his generation [n: (Isa. LIII)]* ?

VERS. 9.-- *He himself created her.* That is, he dispensed the coming of the Only-begotten through the Word coeternal with himself together with the Holy Spirit: how through the Virgin's childbearing he should come for the salvation of the world, and should repair his creatures, and should call man back to the knowledge and love of himself, giving him the gift of the Holy Spirit, that by his gift he should be loosed for ever.

VERS. 11.-- *The fear of the Lord is glory,* etc. *Blessed is the man that feareth the Lord: he desireth exceedingly in his commandments. Glory and riches are in his house [n: (Psal. III)]*, etc. [var: Vulg. *volet nimis*, "he shall delight exceedingly," for *cupit nimis*] *The fear [1184D] of the Lord.* There are two kinds of fear. Of the one it is said, *Fear* [is not in charity] *of the soul in charity, [n: (Joan. IV)]*, etc. They who have this fear cease from sinning for dread of hell, and amend their life: this fear brings charity in, but it is also cast out. For he who fears in this way does not yet love nor desire good things, but guards against evil ones; yet nevertheless he corrects himself, and begins to desire good things. Of the other it is written, *The fear of the Lord is holy, it endureth for ever and ever [n: (Psal. XVIII)]*. [var: Vulg. *permanens*, "enduring"] For every man fears lest he lose the good things, and the presence of God, which he desires to enjoy for ever; of which it is said:

VERS. 12.-- *The fear of the Lord shall delight the heart.* This bestows inward sweetness, and in the time to come eternal life.

VERS. 14.-- *Love,* [n: (RAB.)] The true love of God [1185A] is wisdom, etc., *as far as* it shall itself be the reward in the time to come; whence: *Thou shalt fill me with joy with thy countenance, delights at thy right hand [n: (Psal. XV)]*, etc.

VERS. 17.-- *The fear of the Lord.* Without the fear of God, and the keeping of his commandments, there is no religion; whence: *If any man think himself to be religious, not bridling his tongue, but deceiving his own heart, this man's religion is vain [n: (Jac. I)]*.

VERS. 19.-- *To him that feareth the Lord.* That is, to him who by the fear of God keeps the rule of justice in faith and in teaching and in work.

VERS. 23.-- *And he saw.* [n: (RAB.)] All things are in his sight; for each one he keeps a worthy recompense.

VERS. 25.-- *The branches.* Through wisdom man has [1185B] the divine knowledge of avoiding evil, and the understanding of doing good; for it works both in the hearer unto the end; whence: *He that hearkeneth to me shall not be confounded, and they that work by me shall not sin. They that explain me shall have life everlasting [n: (Eccli. XXIV)]*. *The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord. A good understanding to all that do it [n: (Psal. CX)]*.

VERS. 26.-- *In the treasures.* In the meditation of the law both are declared: namely, how evils are to be avoided, and how spiritual wisdom is to be learned and good works to be practised, in which things is true religion. *The understanding of wisdom.* Which forbids sins and carnal desires; whence: *A pestilent man loveth not him that correcteth himself, nor doth he go to the wise [n: (Prov. XV)]*. [var: Vulg. *corripit*, "him that rebuketh him"]

VERS. 27.-- *Fear,* etc. Because through penance [1185C] it cleanses from past sins, or forbids those that are to be committed. For he who does not correct himself through the fear of God falls into the abyss of sin.

VERS. 30.-- *Good sense.* Whence in the Proverbs, *The lips of the wise preserve them [n: (Prov. XIV)]*. And elsewhere: *The crafty man doeth all things with counsel: but he that is a fool hath laid open his folly [n: (Ibid. XXXI)]*. [var: Vulg. *aperiet*, "shall lay open"] It belongs, therefore, to a wise man to hold his words, and to carry out all things with moderation. *They shall declare his sense.* With an even mind, rendering not evil for evil, but good for good.

VERS. 33.-- *Son, coveting wisdom.* How thou mayest come to wisdom, wisdom herself shows, because without the keeping of justice no one comes to the summit of wisdom; whence James: *Who is a wise man,* saith he, *and endued with knowledge among you? let him show [1185D] good conversation in the meekness of wisdom [n: (Jac. III)]*. [var: Vulg. *ostendat ex bona conversatione operationem suam*, "let him show, by a good conversation, his work"]

VERS. 34.-- *For wisdom and discipline.* (RAB). As has often been said, *The fear of the Lord is the keeping of wisdom and discipline [n: (Prov. XIII)]*, to which belong right faith and temperance of manners, because without faith it is impossible to please God, and the life of good men is always tranquil; whence in Matthew: *Learn of me, because I am meek and humble of heart, and you shall find rest for your souls [n: (Matth. XI)]*. For he who truly fears God cannot be without faith and good works, which fill a man with virtues and make him acceptable to God. Whence: *To the man that is good in his sight God hath given wisdom and knowledge and joy. But to the sinner [1186A] he hath given affliction and superfluous care [n: (Eccle. II)]*.

VERS. 36.-- *With a double heart.* A man double of mind is inconstant in all his ways: he, namely, who wishes to rejoice with the world and to reign with Christ.

VERS. 37.-- *A hypocrite..* Bearing one thing in his heart, and showing another in his work. Whence: *Beware of false prophets, who come to you in the clothing of sheep [n: (Matth. VII)]*.

VERS. 38.-- *Attend unto them,* etc. It is manifest that he who follows heretics purchases damnation for his own soul, because in the judgment the hidden things of hearts shall be revealed, and it shall be rendered to every man according to his works; *for God will destroy all that speak a lie [n: (Psal. LXI)]*. Who abhorreth the bloody [1186B] and deceitful man; whence: Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees, which is hypocrisy. And elsewhere: *The lord of that servant shall come, and shall divide him, and shall appoint his portion with the hypocrites: there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth [n: (Matth. XXIV)]*.
