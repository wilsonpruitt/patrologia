## CHAPTER VI.

[1190D]VERS. 1.-- *Be ye not made,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He shows the force of true charity, etc., *as far as* his own strength shall be dashed to pieces.

VERS. 4.-- *Wicked.* Upon a good will there follows the fruit of good work, and upon a malicious mind no prosperity at all. For if charity be lacking, in vain dost thou spend labour upon virtue; whence: *He that offendeth in one point is become guilty of all [n: (Jac. II)]*. And, *from him that hath not, even that which he hath shall be taken away from him [n: (Luc. XIX)]*.

VERS. 6.-- *Let many be at peace with thee,* etc. Peace is to be kept with all, so far as our frailty allows, but not counsel with many, because it is written: *They are called merciful men, but who shall find a faithful man? [n: (Prov. XX.)]*

[1191A]VERS. 7.-- *If thou wouldst get a friend,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Setting forth false friends under various kinds, he comes at last to the true friend: because many profess themselves friends in prosperity, and appear enemies in adversity. Whence: *If he shall humble himself against thee,* etc.

VERS. 13.-- *Take heed.* [n: (RAB.)] That is, have discernment, etc., *as far as: Now I beseech you, brethren, that you mark them who make dissensions and offences [n: (Rom. XVI)]*.

VERS. 14.-- *A strong friend.* [var: Vulg. *Amicus fidelis protectio fortis*, "a faithful friend is a strong defence"] A friend is a sharer of love, to whom thou shouldst so join thy mind that out of two there be made one; to whom thou shouldst entrust thyself as to thyself; from whom thou shouldst fear nothing, and of whom thou shouldst ask nothing dishonourable. For friendship is not a thing of tribute, but full of comeliness; whence: *You are my friends, if you do the things that I command [1191B] you [n: (Joan. XV)]*, etc. The Lord has given us a pattern of friendship for us to follow: that we do the friend's will, that we open our secrets to him, and he to us; whence: *But I have called you friends, because all things whatsoever I have heard of my Father, I have made known to you [n: (Ibid.)]*.

VERS. 16.-- *A faithful friend.* According to God, namely. He treats with his friend of the things necessary for the medicine of eternal life and of immortality: whence Cicero says that friendship cannot be except among good men, and that friendship ought to be free, and inclined toward every happiness.

VERS. 17.-- *He that feareth the Lord.* [var: Vulg. *Qui timet Deum*] [n: (RAB.)] The full and perfect grace of friendship, etc., *as far as* that thou mayest believe thyself about to depart from this world every day.

VERS. 27.-- *With all thy mind.* [n: (RAB.)] With the heart, not [1191C] with the body, must one approach wisdom, etc., *as far as* the perception of the truth; whence: *Now this is eternal life, that they may know thee, the only true God [n: (Joan. XVII.)]*, etc. [var: Vulg. *solum Deum verum*, "the only God, true"]

VERS. 33.-- *Son, if thou wilt attend.* Wisdom admonishes us to meditate assiduously upon the divine law. First she exhorts us to learn; afterward, to apply the soul to this; then, to incline; and lastly, to bring diligence to the hearing. For thus does wisdom promise that a man shall be wise. For it is necessary that he who desires the knowledge of true wisdom, which is Christ, should first humbly subject himself to catholic teachers, and all the affections of his soul to their discipline. Thereafter, turning the intention of his heart away from every crooked desire, let him diligently bring it to the hearing of the word of God, and let him beware [1191D] that he spend his meditation of the law not for the wages of earthly things, nor of human praise, but for the love of that good itself, so that he may receive the fruit of true wisdom.

VERS. 35.-- *In the multitude.* As if to say: Be not wise in thine own eyes. Trust not thine own wit; the arrogance of pride has above all supplanted heretics.

VERS. 38.-- *Have thy thought upon the precepts.* [n: (RAB.)] Deservedly is it given to him, etc., *as far as* and he assiduously meditates the law and fulfils it in work.

## CHAPTER VII.

*Thou shalt not reap them sevenfold.* [n: (RAB.)] Perfect vengeance, etc., *as far as He taketh seven other spirits more wicked than himself [n: (Luc. XI)]*.

[1192A]VERS. 4.-- *Nor of the king a chair of honour.* He cannot exercise the work of just governance who has neglected the sentence of true godliness; whence: *The kings of the Gentiles lord it over them; but you not so: but he that is the greater among you, let him become as the lesser [n: (Luc. XXII)]*.

VERS. 5.-- *For he is the searcher.* No presumption is more destructive than to be proud of one's own justice or knowledge; whence: *There is not a man upon earth that doeth good, and sinneth not [n: (Eccl. VII)]*. [var: Vulg. *Non est enim homo justus in terra*] And elsewhere: *Hast thou seen a man to seem wise to himself, and the fool hath more hope than he [n: (Prov. XXVI)]*. [var: Vulg. *magis illo spem habebit insipiens*]

VERS. 6.-- *Seek not to be made a judge, unless thou be strong enough.* Every man ought to weigh his own powers, and to take up the care of others in proportion to the measure of his strength: lest, while he delights in a place of glory, he become to his subjects the author of ruin; and [1192B] he who is weighed down by the burden of his own faults should wish to be made a judge of other men's.

VERS. 7.-- *Sin not against the multitude.* He who is strong in reason, and prevails by the power of free choice, let him not sin through the insolence of his subjects, but let him steadfastly resist another's wickedness: nor let him think that he sins with impunity if he acquiesces in the will of the many; whence: *And not only they that do them, but they that consent to him that doeth them [n: (Rom. I.)]*, etc.

VERS. 13.-- *Love not a lie.* [var: Vulg. *arare*, "to plough a lie"] Every lie is evil; and to bring scandal upon our neighbours is destructive. Much more, therefore, to contrive fraud against a friend, and to lay a charge upon him: since a lying witness shall perish; whence in the Proverbs: *As he is guilty that shooteth arrows unto death, so is he that hurteth his friend deceitfully* [1192C] [n: (Prov. XX)].

VERS. 15.-- *Be not full of words.* As if to say: Do not teach thine own opinion; hear the wisdom of the elders. For it is safer to hear than to speak; whence: *Let every man be swift to hear, but slow to speak, and slow to anger [n: (Jac. I)]*.

*Hate not laborious works.* To sinning man knowledge was given, that by the labour of his hands he should seek his food; whence: *In the sweat of thy face shalt thou eat bread [n: (Gen. III)]*, etc.

VERS. 19.-- *For the vengeance of the flesh,* etc. Because the flesh of sinners, which flows away in luxury, shall be tormented with the fire of hell and with worms, where the worm dieth not, and the fire is not quenched: by the *worm* the rottenness of hell is signified, by the *fire* the burning. Or the *worm* is the late [1192D] repentance for crimes, which in the torments does not cease to gnaw the conscience of sinners, so that the fire is the punishment raging without, and the worm the grief accusing within.

VERS. 20.-- *Do not transgress against thy friend for money.* [n: (RAB.)] Many have perished for avarice, etc., *as far as: he that hateth bribes shall live [n: (Prov. I)]*.

VERS. 21.-- *For the grace of her modesty.* The eye hath not seen, nor the ear heard, nor hath it entered into the heart of man, what things God hath prepared for them that love him. By this woman may be understood wisdom, which elsewhere is called the precious pearl, which the spiritual merchant, when he has found it, sells all and buys it. Whence: *Live thy life with the woman whom thou hast loved;* as if to say, follow wisdom [1193A] and the knowledge of the Scriptures, and join her to thyself as a wife.

VERS. 22.-- *Hurt not the servant.* It is openly shown in what manner servants are to be nourished: with the affection, namely, of sons, by the right of nature; and to be disciplined, because of the instability of the mind; whence in what follows, *Fodder and a rod and a burden for an ass; bread and correction and work for a servant.*

VERS. 23.-- *Neither forsake him in his need.* Oppressed by the violence of servitude. *For whether servants or free, we are all one in Christ [n: (I Cor. XII)]*.

VERS. 26.-- *Daughters,* etc. As he teaches that good servants are to be held with the affection of sons, so also that handmaids are to be held as daughters, but with discipline: whence, *Give thy son his way, and he shall make thee afraid: play with him, and he shall grieve thee. Smile not upon him, lest thou be sorry, and at the last [1193B] thy teeth be set on edge [n: (Eccli. XXX)]*.

VERS. 28.-- *A wife.* He commends conjugal chastity, removing every detestable union. But mystically he admonishes that we, holding firmly the catholic faith in true religion, which we obtained first after the receiving of baptism, cast off the perfidy of heretics.

VERS. 32.-- *Love,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The fear of God and love, etc., *as far as* because charity covereth a multitude of sins.

VERS. 35.-- *The gift of the arms.* Above, for those things which are offered to the ministers of God, he promised the purging of sins: but now, for alms, pardon and the grace of blessing, because as water quenches fire, so alms quenches sins.

[1193C]VERS 37.-- *And to the dead.* To alms belongs the burial of the dead; much more, therefore, the consolation of the living: whence Paul teaches to rejoice with them that rejoice, to weep with them that weep. The proof of love is the showing of the work: whence, *He that hath the substance of this world, and shall see his brother in need, and shall shut up his bowels from him, how doth the charity of God abide in him? [n: (I Joan. III.)]*
