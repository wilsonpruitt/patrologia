## CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 1.-- *Strive not with a powerful man.* [n: (RAB.)] With a proud man. Whence, *Answer not a fool according to his folly, lest thou be made like him [n: (Prov. XXVI)]*: because among the proud there are always quarrels, but the servant of the Lord must not quarrel.

[1193D]VERS. 2.-- *With a rich man.* Who pants with the heats of avarice. For thence arise envyings, contentions, and the like, *for the desire of money is the root of all evils [n: (I Tim. VI)]*.

VERS. 13.-- *Kindle not the coals.* [n: (ID.)] Whence the Apostle: *If a man be overtaken in any fault, you who are spiritual, instruct such a one in the spirit of meekness [n: (Gal. VI)]*.

VERS. 15.-- *Lend not.* [n: (RAB.)] Whence: Drink water out of thy own cistern, and have the streams of thy own well to thyself alone.

VERS. 17.-- *Judge not against a judge.* As if to say, be subject to those set over thee and to judges, who judge according to God.

## CHAPTER IX.

[1194A]

*Be not jealous over the wife,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He teaches chastity, etc., *as far as* she does not consent to heretical depravity.

VERS. 3.-- *A woman that hath many desires.* The Synagogue of the heretics, which flits about through various errors.

VERS. 4.-- *Be not continual with a dancing woman.* The throng of heretics, which leaps apart through various errors, so that whomever it has seen to be a lover of its eloquence it may draw to itself like a harlot; whence: *She entangled him with many words, and drew him with the blandishments of her lips [n: (Prov. VII)]*, etc. [var: Vulg. *protraxit illum*, "drew him away"]

VERS. 5.-- *Gaze not upon a maiden.* Turn away thine eyes, that they may not see vanity: let it not please thee to see what it is not lawful to covet. But mystically we ought not to love the beauty of heretical eloquence, nor to attend to feigned [1194B] virtues.

VERS. 7.-- *In the streets of the city,* etc. The same thing under another figure. For the streets of the city are the conventicles of the heretics: which separate themselves from that city of which it is said: *Glorious things are said of thee, O city of God [n: (Psal. LXXXVI)]*.

VERS. 14.-- *Forsake not a friend.* Greater is the confidence on the side of a thing known than of one unknown. We ought also not to turn aside from the doctrine of the holy Fathers and hear new teachers.

VERS. 15.-- *New wine,* etc. A new teacher ought to be earnest that he may firmly grasp sound doctrine and sound practice, and afterward be of profit to himself and to others.

VERS. 25.-- *Full of tongue.* The heretic, who is always contentious, and therefore hateful to God and to men.

## CHAPTER X.

[1194C]

VERS. 1.-- *A wise judge,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The government is made upon his shoulder. Whence: *Woe to the land whose king is a child [n: (Isa. IX)]*, that is, the devil, who always loves novelties, and whose princes eat in the morning, that is, before the day of death, saying: *Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we shall die [n: (Isa. XXII)]*.

VERS. 5.-- *Upon the face of the scribe.* [n: (ID.)] My tongue is the pen of a scribe that writeth swiftly, because he sends in his own stead to preach.

VERS. 8.-- *A kingdom from one nation.* [n: (RAB.)] This nearly all, etc., *as far as: the kingdom of God shall be taken away from you, and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruit thereof [n: (Matth. XXI)]*.

[1194D]VERS. 9.-- *For the covetous man.* After the change of the kingdom he treats of avarice, for the sake of which the force of governance is chiefly neglected: *for the desire of money is the root of all evils [n: (I Tim. VI)]*. And pride accompanies avarice; whence, *Charge the rich not to be high-minded [n: (Ibid.)]*. [var: Vulg. *non sublime sapere*]

VERS. 12.-- *And a king is today.* Power and riches cannot prolong life. A limit is set which cannot be passed over. The pleasantness of the flesh shall be turned into rottenness and stenches, and at last into worms. But mystically, the man who lives in sins and does not come to his senses shall die an eternal death; and he shall not escape the devil, the old serpent, and his beasts, that is, the demons.

VERS. 14.-- *The beginning of pride.* For there is no greater [1195A] apostasy than for a creature to withdraw from the Creator, which is deservedly ascribed to pride. For it is pride if a creature wishes to be made equal to the Creator. This was to the devil and to man the beginning of perdition: whence, *For pride is the beginning of all sin.* Although elsewhere it is said: *The desire of money is the root of all evils [n: (I Tim. VI)]*. There is no covetousness without pride, for no man is proud unless he is covetous, and conversely. Of pride are born heresies, schisms, detractions, envy, wordiness, boasting, contentions and the like, which proceed from covetousness. But covetousness makes men gluttonous, intemperate, drunken, fornicators and the like, who cannot be without pride. Let us therefore beware of covetousness and pride, not two evils, but one from which all draw their beginning.

[1195B]VERS. 19.-- *The lands.* [n: (RAB.)] When Jerusalem by the Romans, etc., *as far as* their posterity was blotted out, and the glory of the martyrs was exalted; whence: *The Lord keepeth all them that love him, but all the wicked he will destroy [n: (Psal. CXLIV)]*.

VERS. 22.-- *It was not created.* [n: (ID.)] Vices were not created, etc., *as far as* wrath arises, which the constancy of virtue overcomes.

VERS. 23.-- *The seed of men shall be honoured.* The holy teachers are held in honour among good men, especially those who fear the Lord: whence, *Let the priests that rule well be esteemed worthy of double honour [n: (I Tim. V)]*, etc.

VERS. 25.-- *The glory of the rich,* etc. Truly it is glory to fear God. He who fears him keeps justice in all things: whence, *Do not with respect of persons hold the faith [1195C] of Jesus [n: (Jacob. II)]*.

VERS. 27.-- *Great is the judge.* Although earthly power be great, it cannot excel him that feareth the Lord: for to them that fear God nothing is wanting, *but the rich have wanted and have suffered hunger [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]*, etc.

VERS. 28.-- *To a wise servant.* This often comes to pass according to the letter, that one ignoble by birth is preferred to the noble by the knowledge and the faith of nobility: whence, *A wise servant shall rule over foolish sons [n: (Prov. XVII)]*. But mystically the servant of the Church, who devoutly keeps the Christian religion, is set before the Synagogue; whence, *The elder shall serve the younger.* And, *Jacob I have loved, but Esau I have hated [n: (Gen. XXV)]*.

VERS. 30.-- *Better is he.* As if he should say: It is better that thou be earnest to labour always in good work, whereby [1195D] the reward of justice may be rendered thee, than that thou consume thy days in vanity, and be in want of that bread which came down from heaven. He who in the fear of God and in the observance of religion shall establish his glory: whence, *He that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord [n: (II Cor. X)]*.

## CHAPTER XI.

VERS. 1, 2.-- *The wisdom of the humble shall exalt his head.* There follows: *Praise not a man,* etc. As if to say: Judge not the quality of the mind according to the appearance of the body: whence, *Look not on his countenance nor on the height of his stature, because I have rejected him, nor do I judge according to the look of man [n: (I Reg. XVI)]*. For man seeth the things that are open. But the Lord beholdeth the heart. [var: Vulg. *parent*, "the things that appear"] In worldly honours [1196A] one must not be proud, because the world passeth away, and the concupiscence thereof.

VERS. 5.-- *Many tyrants.* [n: (RAB.)] It comes about by divine dispensation, etc., *as far as* he inherited the blessing of the patriarchs.

VERS. 8.-- *Before thou hear.* He who answers before he hears, that is, who desires to be a teacher before he learns; or to judge the causes of others before he discerns them: shows himself to be a fool.

VERS. 10.-- *Son, meddle not with many matters,* etc. As if he should say: Be not curious about superfluous things, and consent not to the depravity of sinners. For not only they who do evil, but they who consent to them that do it, are worthy of death. *Son, meddle not with many matters,* etc. Many are the acts of the heretics, who do not keep the unity of the faith and the path of the truth, [1196B] and spend their whole labour in composing books. They are copious in the art of rhetoric and dialectic: nor are they free from the beloved, because their meditation is in guile. Whence Ecclesiasticus: *Son, make not many books, of which there is no end.* But all the books of divine Scripture (because they agree) are one book: whence, *In the head of the book it is written of me [n: (Psal. XXXVI)]*, etc. *Son, meddle not with many* earthly gains, or with any businesses whatsoever, because a mind intent upon many things has less sense for each. When the care of governance has been undertaken, every man is found unequal to the several matters, while he is divided among many: nor does he consider his own losses, while he handles those of others.

VERS. 11.-- *There is a man that laboureth.* He designates Christian works, in which the poor in spirit abound in all [1196C] good things, and they are in want who labour in acquiring riches: whence, *The rich have wanted, and have suffered hunger,* etc.

VERS. 18.-- *There is one that is enriched,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He layeth up treasure, and knoweth not, etc., *as far as* but they leave to others who discern truth and falsehood.

VERS. 30.-- *Before death,* etc. [n: (ID.)] It is foolish to praise, etc., *as far as* who come in the clothing of sheep, but inwardly are ravening wolves.

VERS. 32.-- *For as they belch forth,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] A likeness of the more sagacious animals, etc., *as far as* they may cast them headlong into the cage of error and the snare of deception.
