## CHAPTER XII.

VERS. 3.-- *For there is not,* etc. It is a most grievous sin to be unwilling to repent, nor to redeem sins by alms [1196D], but to add sins to sins: whence, *Upon three crimes of Damascus, and upon four I will not convert it (Amos, I)*. A doubtful and deceitful teaching, which they expend in the exposition of Holy Scripture.

VERS. 5.-- *Give to the good,* etc. The like is read in Tobias: *Set thy bread and thy wine upon the burial of a just man, and eat and drink not thereof with sinners [n: (Tob. IV)]*. That is, for the reason that they are sinners, do not share with them; as they who nourish players while the poor of Christ hunger. But he who gives to a needy sinner, not because he is a sinner but because he is a man, nourishes not a sinner but a just man: because he loves not the fault, but the nature. If he understands a sinner and an ungodly heretic [1197A] while they are in error, there is to be no communicating with them: whence, *Give not that which is holy to dogs [n: (Matth. VII)]*.

VERS. 8.-- *A friend shall not be known in good things.* A friend and an enemy are more easily proved in adversity, because there are many friends of the table who do not remain in the day of necessity. He therefore was a true friend who said: *Who is weak, and I am not weak? [n: (II Cor. XI.)]*

VERS. 10.-- *Trust not an enemy.* Historically he treats of false friends, from whom we ought to beware. But mystically he marks the heretics, who are not truly friends, whose teaching is full of the rust of error and of wickedness: whose speech creeps like a cancer, although they feign themselves faithful and catholic. Let us not therefore [sic: *obsentiamus*] to them, nor grant them the power which [1197B] the *right hand* signifies: lest they cast us down from our place, that is, from the state of right faith. For they strive maliciously to overturn our chair, that is, our teaching.

*Who shall heal.* The heretic who bears the office of a teacher, and says that he is a physician of souls: *For if the salt shall have become insipid, it is good for nothing more, but to be cast out [n: (Matth. V)]*. [var: Vulg. *Quod si sal evanuerit, in quo salietur?* "if the salt lose its savour, wherewith shall it be salted?"]

VERS. 15.-- *In his lips.* By these sentences he marks those who speak in the heart and lie in wait to slay the innocent: who, when they have deceived anyone, move the head, clap the hand, and rejoice when they have done evil.

## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- *He that toucheth pitch shall be defiled.* [n: (RAB.)] [1197C] By a similitude he shows, etc., *as far as* lest they should wish to imitate the evils which they frequently look upon.

VERS. 2.-- *He shall take a burden upon himself.* [n: (ID.)] These things instruct us morally, that we should follow humble things and flee proud ones: *Because God resisteth the proud, but to the humble he giveth grace [n: (Jacob. IV)]*. But mystically he teaches, etc., *as far as* because they will show themselves worthy of eternal confusion, when they shall be tormented for ever.

VERS. 11.-- *Be not,* etc. He does not abhor the virtue of humility: but teaches that a firm authority is to be held against the assailants of the truth, whom we must resist strongly.

VERS. 12.-- *Summoned.* We must act modestly with the powerful of this world, who frequently deride the simplicity of the innocent: to whom nevertheless we ought to render the due [1197D] honor, but not against God, or against his commandments. Whence, *Honor all men, love the brotherhood, fear God, honor the king [n: (I Petr. II)]*.

VERS. 16.-- *Beware of thyself, and take heed.* As if: nothing is safe among the lovers of the world, who follow changeable things and are changed along with them. Whence: *The fool is changed as the moon [n: (Eccle. XXVII)]*. In these, heretics and false Christians can be understood, who are not to be believed, because they are inconstant in all their ways.

VERS. 18.-- *All thy life.* [n: (RAB.)] The rest being refuted, etc., *as far as* because the Lord is nigh to all that call upon him in truth.

VERS. 19.-- *Every beast.* He looks back to the things above, [1198A] that thou shouldst, namely, decline the society of the rich and of the powerful, and shouldst join thyself to him who is thy consort in zeal and in religion. For this is a firm friendship, which a likeness of virtues joins together.

*If he shall have fellowship.* The rapacity of the wolf agrees not with the innocence of the lamb, nor the rage of the greedy man with the modesty of the simple. For there is no communion of cleanness with uncleanness: *For as the dog returneth to the vomit, so he that repeateth his folly [n: (Prov. VI)]*. There is therefore no parity of the proud rich man with the humility of the poor of Christ.

VERS. 25.-- *The rich man moved.* What he rebukes concerning the rich man and the poor man, he pursues: because there are many who extol the deeds and the sayings of the rich man: few who understand the teaching and the virtue of the humble. The arrogant find [1198B] preachers, but the humble find despisers. Whence: *Riches add many friends, but from the poor man even they whom he had are separated [n: (Prov. XIX)]*.

VERS. 31.-- *The heart of a man changeth.* In the judgment of the countenance the change of the mind is detected: whence, *A glad heart maketh a cheerful countenance; in the sorrow of the mind the spirit is cast down.*

VERS. 32.-- *The trace of a good heart.* Because many are feigned and counterfeit: therefore it is with difficulty found in whom the heart and the face, that is, thought and work and teaching, agree in good: but when thou shalt find this, give glory to God, because this is his gift.

## CHAPTER XIV.

VERS. 1.-- *Blessed is the man,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] By sinning. Hence [1198C] James saith: *He that offendeth not in word, is perfect [n: (Jacob. III)]*. In that word, namely, the offense of which human frailty can avoid.

VERS. 3.-- *To a covetous and niggardly man.* He describes the plague of avarice, which torments the covetous man lest he obtain the fruit of his own labor, *Who layeth up treasure, and knoweth not for whom he shall gather these things [n: (Psal. XXXVIII)]*.

VERS. 5.-- *He that is evil to himself.* These things describe according to the letter the mind of the covetous and of the envious, who rejoice not in their own goods. But allegorically they signify the philosophers and heretics who without the faith of Christ gather riches of depraved dogma, which profit them nothing, nay rather confer eternal punishment.

VERS. 7.-- *And if he do well.* [n: (ID.)] He had said that nothing is [1198D] more wicked, etc., *as far as* but now he repeats the same.

VERS. 9.-- *The insatiable eye.* Because without the fruit of good things he consumes his life, and delivers his barren soul to the fires of Gehenna to be tormented.

VERS. 10.-- *An evil eye toward evil things.* The eye of the heretics is always bent upon evils, and it merits not the heavenly bread, that is, the understanding of divine wisdom, and at the table of Scripture it withers with want.

VERS. 11.-- *Son, if thou have.* He exhorts that, the luxury of the world being despised, we should offer to God the oblations of good works: because the space of life in which we can work is short: for after death, which hastens on, there is no time for working, but for receiving the wage: Whence, *Work ye while it is day: for the night cometh, when it is no longer lawful to work.* [var: Vulg. Joan. 9:4 *Me oportet operari opera ejus qui misit me, donec dies est: venit nox, quando nemo potest operari*, "I must work the works of him that sent me, whilst it is day: the night cometh, when no man can work"]

[1199A]VERS. 18.-- *All flesh.* As hay in the earth or a leaf on a tree is born, grows, becomes green, dries up and is cast down, so our flesh is born of the offspring of parents, grows in infancy and in boyhood, flowers in adolescence, ripens in youth, withers in old age, is cast down in death.

VERS. 22.-- *Blessed is the man,* etc. *Blessed are the undefiled in the way, who walk in the law of the Lord. Blessed are they that search his testimonies. [n: (Psal. CXVIII.)]*

VERS. 25.-- *He shall set up his little dwelling.* That is, performing the office of a teacher, he shall join to sound teaching the works of justice, that he may instruct his hearers by word and by example.
