## CHAPTER XVIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *He that liveth for ever.* [n: (RAB.)] The substance of things was created all at once, etc., *as far as* to know or to declare the greatness of his power: whence, *No one knoweth the Son but the Father, neither doth any one know the Father but the Son, and he to whom it shall please the Son to reveal him [n: (Matth. XI)]*.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *There is no diminishing.* [n: (ID.)] Nothing is to be diminished from the holy Scriptures, nothing to be added: whence, *If any man shall add to these things, God shall add unto him the plagues written in this book [n: (Apoc. XXII)]*, etc.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *A hundred years.* [n: (ID.)] He compares the fewness of our days to a drop of the sea, and to a pebble [1202A] of the sand: because in comparison with eternal happiness the brevity is exceeding great, and the misery greatest: whence, *Man born of a woman, living for a short time, is filled with many miseries [n: (Job. XIV)]*.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *The mercy of man.* ⟦s2⟧ Few there are who have mercy on their neighbor: whereas the mercy of God overflows through the whole world, who maketh his sun to rise upon the good and the bad.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *He that receiveth teaching.* ⟦s2⟧ He exhorts those who receive the teaching of the Gospel, that they show mercy to their neighbors: and that they hasten to forestall the day of judgment by works of mercy; whence: *Blessed are the merciful, for they shall obtain mercy [n: (Matth. V)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Of an evil word.* ⟦s2⟧ A gracious tongue [1202B] maketh a man well-favored; whence: *He that moderateth his words is learned and prudent [n: (Prov. XVII)]*.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Shall not the heat.* ⟦s2⟧ The dew defends the standing corn from the heat of the sun: and a good word defends the giver from the wickedness of him that is angry; whence: *A hard word stirreth up strifes, a gentle one mitigateth them when stirred up [n: (Prov. XV)]*. [var: Vulg. *sermo durus suscitat furorem* (Prov. 15:1) and *qui patiens est mitigat suscitatas* (Prov. 15:18)]

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *The fool shall upbraid bitterly.* [n: (RAB.)] In the mouth of the fool are quarrels, etc., *as far as* they defile with a lying word.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Prepare justice.* ⟦s2⟧ That thou mayest see what in thee pleases or displeases God, and mayest correct thy faults.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Before judgment.* [n: (RAB.)] These things admonish us to be always ready, nor to flatter ourselves with a foolish security: but to labor in the service of God even to the end of life.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1202C]VERS. 23.-- *Before prayer [sic: orationm].* ⟦s2⟧ When thou shalt stand to pray, forgive if thou have anything against anyone.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Remember.* ⟦s2⟧ Whence: *The Lord is just, and hath loved justice: his countenance hath beheld equity [n: (Psal. X.)]*.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Of poverty.* ⟦s2⟧ Of that which is to come after this life, that thou mayest make friends who may receive thee into everlasting dwellings.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *All these things.* ⟦s2⟧ Spring, summer, autumn, winter, day and night, all of them never rest; and yet all things compared to eternity pass swiftly away: and all things are shut up before the eyes of the Lord with a fixed end. ⟦s3⟧ But he himself is always the same, and his years shall not fail.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *A wise man.* ⟦s2⟧ Blessed is the man who is always fearful, that he may beware of his own sins and may fulfil the precepts [1202D] of the Lord: Because *the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom. ⟦s3⟧ A good understanding to all that do it [n: (Psal. CX.)]*.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *To him that findeth.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, when he shall have found her, and shall have received the knowledge of her, she herself shall give the confession of right preaching, and the zeal of good working.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Men of sense.* ⟦s2⟧ As it were: the crafty acknowledge wisdom, and men of sense show forth prudence in words by their work.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Be not mediocre.* ⟦s2⟧ He provokes us to the works of mercy. ⟦s3⟧ For if we shall be slothful, not only shall temporal solaces be wanting to us, but rather the future goods of our soul: whereas a temporal alms shall be repaid with an eternal wage.

## CHAPTER XIX.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1203A]

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *A drunken workman.* [n: (RAB.)] He looks back to the things above where he says, *Be not delighted in crowds,* by whom, namely, the vice of drunkenness is thought to be a light thing, though it is the loss of good things and the multiplication of crimes. ⟦s2⟧ *Little things.* ⟦s3⟧ An idle word, laughter, revellings, drinkings, and the like.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And women.* [n: (RAB.)] Fornication, by which, the truth of the faith being forsaken, they are joined to demons.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *He joineth to harlots.* ⟦s2⟧ He shows them to be blameworthy; because, true wisdom being left behind, they are made fools, and join themselves to harlots.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He that believeth,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The levity of the mind is described, which is infirm and changeable: whence, *The fool is changed as the moon. ⟦s3⟧ He that believeth.* ⟦s4⟧ Of charity the Apostle says, *She believeth all things, [1203B] hopeth all things, endureth all things [n: (I Cor. XII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ But there it is the perfection of a good will, which is always ready for every good work. ⟦s6⟧ Here, however, the levity of the mind is described, which has not the state of firmness.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Repeat not a hard and wicked word.* [n: (RAB.)] That is, after weeping, etc., *as far as* repeat not the deed to a most wicked man. [var: Vulg. *verbum nequam et durum*, "a wicked and hard word"]

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *To friend and to enemy.* ⟦s2⟧ He exhorts that we have caution and discretion: lest to friend and to enemy, that is, to catholic and to heretic, we lay open our secrets: because the enemy, although he flatter, attempts to deceive.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Hast thou heard,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ A foolish word does not move the fool and the wise man equally. ⟦s3+4⟧ For the fool disturbs the sense, so that he may express a groan of grief through an abominable act: but whatever shall have happened disturbs not the blow. [1203C] But he must beware who trusts in the firmness of his heart, lest he retain the memory of a hard word.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Rebuke a friend.* ⟦s2⟧ We ought to rebuke offending friends with all patience, because they sin sometimes through ignorance, sometimes through negligence, sometimes through disobedience and contempt, if perchance, being rebuked with discretion, they may do penance, and may have diligence and caution in the future: whence, *Instruct such a one in the spirit of meekness [n: (Gal. VI)]*.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *There is that slippeth with his own tongue.* ⟦s2⟧ There is a great difference between him who sins through simplicity or ignorance, and him who sins through malice. ⟦s3⟧ For it can happen that a man offend in a word which nevertheless [1203D] he does not think to be evil; but he sins more who is full of guile and of wickedness.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *He is lessened in wisdom.* ⟦s2⟧ In the understanding of the mysteries, yet not in the keeping of God's commandments. ⟦s3⟧ Whence there follows:

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Better is,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He, namely, who in the fear of God fulfils what he knows: whence, *For he that knoweth the will of his lord and doth it not, shall be beaten with few stripes [n: (Luc. XII)]* [var: Vulg. *vapulabit multis*, "shall be beaten with many stripes"]: for mercy is granted in small measure.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *There is a sure skilfulness,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He begins to give the difference between the saints and the hypocrites. [n: (RAB.)] So the heretics and the hypocrites show the appearance of virtues, but have not the truth of it: and when they shall have found a place for sinning, they show what they had in the heart.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1204A]VERS. 26.-- *By the look.* [n: (ID.)] By outward tokens the affection of the mind is known, whence: *A good tree is not that which bringeth forth evil fruits [n: (Luc. VI)]*, etc. ⟦s2⟧ *And out of the abundance of the heart the mouth speaketh [n: (Matth. XII)]*.
