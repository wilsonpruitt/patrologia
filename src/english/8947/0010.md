## CHAPTER XXIV

VERS. 2, 3.-- *And in the midst of the people,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Because in the showing forth of miracles he stirred up the people to the praise of God. *Of his power,* etc. Of the angels, of the religious.

VERS. 5.-- *I from the mouth.* Because the Word is consubstantial, coeternal. Because I am not a creature. Yet he is called the firstborn of every creature, not, that is, in time, but in honour [n: (Exod. IV)].

[1208C]VERS. 6.-- *I made in the heavens.* [n: (RAB.)] In the heavens light arises, by a cloud flesh is covered, because as by its own brightness it surpasses the darkness of a cloud, so the spiritual creature surpasses the bodily in dignity.

VERS. 7.-- *My throne.* [n: (ID.)] The human body weighed down by no weight of sin, but raised on high by the power of God, in which the might of the Godhead exercised its judgments.

VERS. 12.-- *He created me,* etc. According to the humanity conceived by a singular purity of the Holy Spirit, brought forth of the Virgin.

VERS. 13.-- *In Jacob.* By the incarnation of the Saviour it was granted to the Jews in a special manner to have the use of him, and he came especially to the Jews. Whence by *Jacob* the Jews are figured; [1208D] but by *Israel*, the Gentiles.

VERS. 14.-- *From the beginning.* The mystery of the incarnation, foreknown and predestined before the ages, remains unto the age of the age. And he disposed the dwellings of the Church, that is, the rite of the true religion.

VERS. 15.-- *And so in Sion.* [n: (RAB.)] Sion is the Church, which is also Jerusalem, which is built from the first just man unto the last, and in which wisdom daily bears fruit.

VERS. 17.-- *In Lebanon.* [n: (RAB.)] *Lebanon* is a mountain of Phoenicia, etc., *as far as* expressing the constancy and the action of those who adorn the Church with the loftier virtues.

VERS. 18.-- *As a palm tree.* A tree long-lasting in its leaves, and with sweet fruits; it is called *palma* because it is the adornment of a victorious [1209A] hand: or because it spreads out its branch after the manner of a human palm. This the Greeks call φοινίκον, because the phoenix, a bird, lives a long time. To this the Church is likened, which, despising to be bowed down to earthly things, raises itself up to, heavenly things: that when the contest is finished it may receive the heavenly palm, and, like long-lasting leaves, keeps perpetually the words of right confession.

VERS. 19.-- *As an olive tree.* In Greek ἐλαἴον, that is, the fruit: for the tree is called ἐλαία, the juice oil. And it is a tree notable for peace: by it the Church is signified, which lights up the fields of the whole world with comeliness, and sanctifies them by a special grace through the anointing of chrism. *As a plane tree.* The plane tree (*platanus*) is so called from its height, or because it is spreading and ample. For the Greeks call what is ample πλατός. He expressed, therefore, the name and the form of the tree [1209B] in saying: I am spread abroad as a plane tree in the streets. [var: Vulg. *quasi platanus exaltata sum juxta aquam in plateis*, "exalted... beside the water"] And it is most tender, and soft in its leaves, and like to vines, and it signifies Christ, who through his own members is spread abroad in the world, mild and meek.

VERS. 20.-- *As cinnamon.* Cinnamon and balsam are aromatic trees. Cinnamon is so called because its bark is round and slender after the manner of a reed (*canna*). It is produced in India and Ethiopia, in a short shrub of only two cubits, of a blackish or ashen colour, of the very slenderest twigs. For what is thick is despised; but the slenderer is the dearer: and when it is broken, it sends out a visible breath after the manner of a cloud or of dust.

VERS. 21.-- *Balsam.* [n: (RAB.)] Pliny says *aspaltum*; it is born in the same course of heaven, etc., [1209C] *as far as* mindful of the Lord's passion they are ready to suffer for the Lord. *As myrrh.* Myrrh is in Arabia a tree five cubits high, like to a thorn, which the Greeks call ἄκανθον, whose drop is green and bitter, whence also it received its name: all which things agree with the mortification of the flesh, which is felt bitter for a time, but is willingly undertaken in hope of the ever-green fatherland. And it is likened to a thorn, because to save the mind it afflicts the body. The drop flowing of its own accord is more precious; that drawn out by a wound of the bark is baser, because it is of greater power to mortify a sound body (upon which an abundance of things smiles) than a sick one, or one afflicted with poverty.

*As storax.* [n: (ID.)] Storax, galbanum, etc., *as far as* it is called storax, because it is a drop of the tree flowing forth and congealed, for the Greeks call a drop στακτήμ. [1209D] *And galbanum.* Galbanum [n: (ID.)] etc., *as far as* after the likeness of ammoniac, and least of all woody. *Ungula.* *Ungula* is that which in Exodus is called *onycha*. For in Greek it is ὀνύξ, in Latin *ungula*, because it is likened to a human nail. *A drop.* [n: (ID.)] The drop which is also called ammoniac, etc., *as far as* by how much virtue is joined to virtue, by so much the more sincerely is the incense presented. *Libanus.* Libanus, as they say, is a tree [n: (ID.)] etc., *as far as* when the trees are cut a tear flows, because the hearts of the humble, pricked with compunction, send forth pure prayer to God. *Libanus* in Hebrew signifies whiteness: in Greek, θοῦς. Whence in the Canticles, where we read *unto the hill of frankincense [n: (Cant. IV.)]*, certain codices have *unto the hill of Libanus. Balsam unmingled.* [n: (ID.)] The balsam tree is in Judaea, etc., [1210A] *as far as* and it poured forth the sacraments of salvation. Whence, *And my odour as unmingled balsam.*

VERS. 22.-- *The turpentine tree,* etc. [n: (ID.)] It is a tree, etc., *as far as* and they are without spot before the throne of God.

VERS. 29.-- *They that eat me,* etc. [n: (ID.)] We eat wisdom, etc., *as far as: Taste and see, for the Lord is sweet [n: (Psal. XXXIII)]*, etc.

VERS. 34.-- *He set David.* [n: (ID.)] The four rivers of paradise, etc., *as far as* who sendeth forth discipline as light, because his precepts bestow eternal life, whence: *Teach ye all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit; teaching to keep all things whatsoever I have commanded you [n: (Matth. XXVII)]*.

VERS. 35.-- *As Phison.* [n: (ID.)] Phison, which is also [1210B] the Ganges, etc., *as far as* and it is said to overflow, like the Nile, upon the lands of the East. *As the Tigris,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The Tigris, a river of Mesopotamia, etc., *as far as* because it is borne swiftly as the tiger, a beast.

VERS. 36.-- *As the Euphrates.* [n: (ID.)] The Euphrates, a river of Mesopotamia, etc., *as far as* and the land which is encompassed by them is called Mesopotamia. *As the Jordan.* The Jordan, a river of Judaea, is named from two springs: *Jor,* namely, and *Dan,* from which, joined together, it is gathered. It rises under Mount Lebanon, and divides Judaea and Arabia, and near Jericho flows into the Dead Sea.

VERS. 37.-- *As Geon.* Geon here encircles Ethiopia, so called because it waters the land of Egypt. For *Ge* in Greek signifies earth: which among the Egyptians is called Nile, on account of the mud which it draws, which makes fruitfulness, [1210C] whence also it is called Nile.

VERS. 38.-- *He shall not search out.* [n: (RAB.)] Christ is the author of wisdom, etc., *as far as: No one knoweth the Son but the Father, nor doth any know the Father, but the Son [n: (Matth. XI)]*, etc.

VERS. 39.-- *For from the sea.* The sea signifies that the depth of wisdom is inestimable and incomprehensible: since no one can penetrate it save one and only God, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, who truly knows himself, and his own counsel, and gives to his creature to know so much as is expedient.

VERS. 41.-- *As a path.* [n: (ID.)] Because God of God, light of light. Figuratively the Father is called the origin of the Godhead: because of the Father the Son is begotten and the Holy Spirit proceeds, [1210D] who is signified by water: whence, *Rivers of living water shall flow out of his belly [n: (Job. VII)]*. *The water of saving wisdom hath given him to drink [n: (Eccli. XV)]*. *I as the river Dorix.* [var: Vulg. *ego quasi fluvii dioryx*, "as a channel of a river"] Wisdom is likened to a most rapid river, etc., *as far as* whence the third tongue of the Greeks is called Doric.

VERS. 46.-- *Yet doctrine.* [n: (RAB.)] The writer of this work signifies that the book is mystical, and useful for reading. For historically it teaches morality, and allegorically the mystery of faith.

VERS. 47.-- *See ye,* etc. The ecclesiastical teacher profits not himself alone, but others also, by writing and by teaching: whence, *They that are learned shall shine as the brightness of the firmament, and they that instruct many [sic: erudiunmultos] unto justice, [1211A] as stars unto perpetual eternities [n: (Dan. XII)]*.
