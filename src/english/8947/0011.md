## CHAPTER XXV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Man and woman.* [n: (RAB.)] Whence Paul, *Husbands, love your wives, as Christ also the Church [n: (Eph. V)]*. ⟦s2⟧ We can take the spirit in the man, the soul in the woman, and if these consent together in the fear and love of God, whatsoever they shall ask shall be done for them, whence: *If two of you shall consent, it shall be done to them [n: (Matth. XVIII)]*.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Three kinds.* [n: (ID.)] These things can be transferred to the devil, etc., *as far as* because he would not be subject to his Maker.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *What in youth.* ⟦s2⟧ He who in youth does not learn wisdom, in old age has not the fruit of it. ⟦s3⟧ Whence, *The slack hand hath wrought poverty, [1211B] but the hand of the strong prepareth riches [n: (Prov. X)]*. ⟦s4⟧ *And he that meditateth in the law of the Lord shall give fruit in his season [n: (Psal. I)]*.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *How comely.* [n: (ID.)] The hoary head is comely, etc., *as far as* it is not lawful for them to be ignorant of the knowledge and the wisdom of the law.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Nine things unsuspected,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *With the heart we believe unto justice, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation [n: (Rom. X)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Nine, therefore, are the things unsuspected, that is, foreign to all suspicion of falsehood, which right faith brings forth for a man.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Wisdom.* ⟦s2⟧ Which is to beware of evil. ⟦s3+4⟧ Whence, *And behold the fear of the Lord, that itself is wisdom, and to depart from evil, understanding [n: (Job. XXVIII)]*. [var: Vulg. *ipsa est sapientia … intelligentia*; Migne prints masc. *ipse* and acc. *intelligentiam*] Hence it is that one is added over and above to the kinds of the virtues.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1211C]VERS. 16.-- *The fear of God.* [n: (RAB.)] Because as the number nine is imperfect, etc., *as far as* but lest the presence of God forsake thee, which thou dost embrace, and desirest to enjoy for ever.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Every wound.* ⟦s2⟧ There is no greater grace than the true gladness of the mind, nor a greater wound than the sadness of a deceitful heart in idolatry or in heresy, because in its wickedness is all malice.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *And every wound.* ⟦s2⟧ He sees the wound of the heart who understands the injury of his own depravity, but he does not see it who does not correct it by penance.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *There is no head,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The wickedness of heretics compared, etc., *as far as* therefore its dwelling together is hurtful to all.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *Every malice is short.* ⟦s2⟧ Small is every [1211D] malice in comparison with idolatry and with heretical depravity, because they sin not through the frailty of the flesh, but through the pride of the mind.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *If a woman have the pre-eminence.* ⟦s2⟧ Historically he shows how hurtful is the pre-eminence of a woman, etc., *as far as* she will be contrary to the man, that is, to the catholic teacher, by thinking perversely and by teaching depraved things.

## CHAPTER XXVI.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Of a good woman.* [n: (RAB.)] As before he blamed the wicked woman, etc., *as far as* and in the time to come she shall rejoice in the receiving of rewards.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *But of the rich man.* ⟦s2⟧ Rich or poor, coming with a devout mind to the grace of Christ, shall enjoy perpetually the delight [1212A] of the vision of God, because God is no respecter of persons.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Three things.* ⟦s2⟧ By these likenesses he calls those who are separated from the unity of the true religion, that is, Jews, pagans, heretics, and false Christians.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *The accusation of a city.* ⟦s2⟧ The folly of the foolish. ⟦s3⟧ For as brute animals do not know how to discern between a friend and an enemy, and between good and evil; so the foolish are ignorant of the distance between catholic doctrine and heretical, but obey true and false teachers alike. ⟦s4+5⟧ Against whom it is said elsewhere, *Immediately she followeth him as an ox to the victim, and as a wanton lamb, and knoweth not that he is dragged to the victim [n: (Prov. VII)]*. [var: Vulg. *quasi bos ductus ad victimam … et ignorans quod ad vincula stultus trahatur*, "as an ox led to the victim … not knowing that the fool is drawn to the bonds"] But the craftiness of heretics deceives the unwary not with open face, but by guile.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1212B]VERS. 11.-- *A drunken woman.* ⟦s2⟧ He describes the habit of a harlot, who by the movement of her body and the movement of her eyes displays her lust. ⟦s3⟧ But mystically it signifies the heretic, who in pride of understanding and in eloquence of words boasts of his fornication, that he may the more easily draw to himself the lovers of novelties, itching in their ears, who turn away their hearing from the truth and are turned unto fables.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *The fornication of a woman.* [n: (RAB.)] By these three things he shows the curiosity of the human mind, etc., *as far as* through the pride of life, when he added, *You shall be as gods [n: (Gen. III)]*.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *She shall fatten his bones.* ⟦s2⟧ With charity, namely, whence: *He that keepeth my commandments, he it is that loveth me, and he shall be loved of my Father, and I will love him [n: (Joan. XIV)]*.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1212C]VERS. 18.-- *A woman,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because the wisdom of God fills the souls of the elect, and grants them a guard of the mouth and an efficacy of work, nor does it permit them to turn aside from itself, because everyone who is born of God sinneth not, by violating, that is, faith or charity: *Because the seed of God,* that is the word by which he was born again, *he hath in himself.*

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *Golden pillars.* ⟦s2⟧ Teachers shining with wisdom are set upon silver bases, when in Holy Scripture they are made notable by their preaching.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *In two things.* [n: (RAB.)] Wisdom marks, etc., *as far as* or, having forsaken the path of truth, he follows the by-ways of error.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *He is hardly stripped.* [n: (RAB.)] The worst act, not an honest thing, is condemned. ⟦s2⟧ That trader is to be abominated who through immoderate ambition loads his wares [1212D] more with perjury than with price.

## CHAPTER XXVII.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Because of poverty,* etc., [n: (RAB.)] As in the midst. ⟦s2⟧ Here by comparison, etc., *as far as* and in the time to come eternal punishment remains.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *As in the shaking of a sieve.* [n: (ID.)] As a sieve, etc., *as far as: Satan hath desired you, that he might sift you as wheat.*

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *As the dressing of a tree.* [n: (ID.)] Every single tree, etc., *as far as* the tilling of a tree therefore makes manifest in the fruit the quality of it.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Before speech.* ⟦s2⟧ Because speech indicates the thought. ⟦s3⟧ As if he should say: Let no one praise him whom [1213A] he has not known by manifest tokens to be worthy of praise. ⟦s4⟧ But speech can be taken for a good work, so that we praise no one before the proving of good works. ⟦s5⟧ Whence: *Praise not any man before death.* [var: Vulg. Eccli. 11:30 *Ante mortem ne laudes hominem quemquam*]

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *In the midst.* [n: (RAB.)] He teaches temperance and caution in speaking, etc., *as far as: But he that shall do and teach, he shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven [n: (Matth. V)]*.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Speech,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because as their action is contrary to the good: so also their speech is prone to the saying of evil things, burdensome to the hearing.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *He that discloseth.* ⟦s2⟧ He shows wherein friendship is most of all wounded, namely in the evil of betrayal, which loses faith, namely, nor keeps the equity of friendship, and therefore breaks up charity.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1213B]VERS. 22.-- *As a roe.* ⟦s2⟧ The *caprea*, a wild goat, is so called as it were *capiens ardua*, taking the steep places. ⟦s3⟧ And roes are called *ibices* or *avices*, for this reason, that after the fashion of birds they keep to the high and lofty places: and they dwell in the heights, so that from their loftiness they are scarcely open to human sight. ⟦s4⟧ These, if at any time they have perceived beforehand the hostility of wild beasts or of men, casting themselves down from the high peaks, catch themselves unhurt by their own horns in the branches of the trees.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *But to disclose,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He shows the inconstancy of the betrayer's manners, etc., *as far as: A man that is an apostate, an unprofitable man, walketh with a perverse mouth, he winketh with the eyes, he presseth with the foot,* etc. [n: (Prov. VI)].

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *To him that deviseth the worst counsel.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he does not foresee that his day will come. ⟦s3⟧ For the [1213C] heretic or schismatic reckons that his own sect is better than the catholic truth: whence he mocks himself.

## CHAPTER XXVIII.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Remember.* [n: (RAB.)] He who fears God restrains wrath or fury, and does not forget the testament of God, who says: *Forgive, and it shall be forgiven you [n: (Luc. IX)]*.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Contention.* ⟦s2⟧ An indiscreet action: whence, *Be not over-much just, neither be more wise than is necessary, lest thou be stupefied.* ⟦s3⟧ Immoderate discipline therefore stirs up the heat of fury, and forward speech the shedding of blood.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *The third tongue.* ⟦s2⟧ Heretical doctrine, which agrees neither with the Old nor with the New Testament, and [1213D] troubles the hearts of the hearers, and passing from nation to nation, stirs up sedition.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *But the wound.* ⟦s2⟧ Worse is the destruction of the soul, which heretics slay, than any torment whatever of the body.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Blessed is he that is covered.* ⟦s2⟧ Blessed is he who does not consent to heretics, and whose heart their eloquence does not overturn, because as he is a stranger to their malice, so he is separated from their punishment.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Hedge in thy ears.* ⟦s2⟧ After he had rooted out the evil of the tongue, he turns himself to the instruction of the hearer.
