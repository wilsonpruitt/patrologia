## CHAPTER XXIX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Until they receive.* [n: (RAB.)] A sickness of this kind the fear of God better cures. ⟦s2⟧ For he who [1214A] dreads God as though always present intends to defraud no one.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *To many,* etc. [n: (ID.)] He marks those who wish to injure no one, but through fear of things that will perish are unwilling to do good. ⟦s2⟧ Against whom it is said: *Lay not up to yourselves treasures on earth [n: (Matth. VI)]*.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *For the commandment's sake.* ⟦s2⟧ Mercy is to be shown to all who are in need, nor is the charity of one's neighbour to be lost for covetousness.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *And hide not.* ⟦s2⟧ For he who shall have seen his brother to be in need, and shall have shut up his bowels from him, how doth the charity of God abide in him?

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16, 17, 18.-- *Above the shield of the mighty,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Alms avail more against the darts of the enemy than [1214B] earthly arms: because these bring forth a doubtful, those a most certain victory, that death may be swallowed up in victory.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *He that shall lose,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, he who does not keep the unity of faith and the sincerity of the law, but is entangled in divers errors.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *The grace of the surety,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The teacher, namely, who promises eternal life for faith and good works. ⟦s3⟧ This sentence rebukes Pelagius, who sets free will before grace, whereas the elect are set free by grace alone.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Recover thy neighbour.* ⟦s2⟧ He counsels that according to thy strength thou shouldst afford comfort to thy neighbour, and so provide for thyself, lest thou fall into a snare.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *The beginning of life,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He teaches frailty, and hospitality, and honesty, etc., [1214C] *as far as* and the Lord made coats of skins for our first parents after sin.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Better is the fare of the poor man,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ To the letter. ⟦s3⟧ It is better to take a slender fare beneath a mean hut in safety, than to seek splendid banquets with weariness in uncertain places. [n: (ID.)] Allegorically. ⟦s4⟧ Better is the fare, etc., *as far as* where there is labour and superfluous care.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *The least thing.* [n: (ID.)] Let what thou hast suffice thee, etc., *as far as* a good name is better than many delights.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Pass over.* ⟦s2⟧ As if: Thou who takest delight in another's lodging, return to thine own, and of thine own labour feed thyself and others. ⟦s3⟧ Seek not for thyself superfluous food from another's house. ⟦s4⟧ The receiving of thy neighbours sufficeth thee.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1214D]VERS. 35.-- *A reproach.* [n: (RAB.)] But mystically the ecclesiastical man is admonished, etc., *as far as* but that in the judgment to come he may bear the everlasting reproach of confusion.

## CHAPTER XXX.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *He that loveth his son.* [n: (RAB.)] He admonishes fathers, both carnal and spiritual, to instruct their sons diligently, that they may live honourably, and not run about through the teachings of others by wantonness.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *An untamed horse.* ⟦s2⟧ An untamed horse goes headlong; an undisciplined son rushes into sin. ⟦s3⟧ He teaches therefore spiritual fathers to instruct and correct their sons; lest they slacken the reins to them immoderately, [1215A] lest they fall into the foulness of vices, and themselves bear everlasting disgrace.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Better is a poor man.* ⟦s2⟧ Sweet is the sleep of him that works, whether he eat much or little. ⟦s3⟧ For the fullness of the rich man does not suffer him to sleep.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *The health of the soul.* ⟦s2⟧ The true health of the soul is justice, to which gold and silver cannot be compared, because riches shall not profit in the day of vengeance. ⟦s3⟧ But justice shall deliver the soul from death.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Good things hidden.* [n: (RAB.)] He speaks against those who hide the talent of the word; whose knowledge is unprofitable for virtues, like banquets and treasure for the dead.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *As an eunuch.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not serve for the embrace of a virgin, so is the adoration of an idol, unfitting [1215B] and unprofitable to those who adore.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *The joyfulness of the heart.* ⟦s2⟧ Because no virtue stands without it. ⟦s3⟧ *But the greater of these is charity [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *They shorten the days,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *Bloody men shall not live out half their days [n: (Psal. LIV)]*. [n: (ID.)] That is, the prosperity which they promise to themselves they cannot have for ever.

## CHAPTER XXXI.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *The wood of stumbling.* [n: (RAB.)] As Adam in the forbidden wood, etc., *as far as* which shines with the splendour of eloquence, and seduces the curious.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *At a table.* [n: (ID.)] To the letter, continence, etc., *as far as* but let us hold the way of the holy [01215C] teachers.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Say not,* etc. [n: (ID.)] As if he should say: Do nothing out of presumption, nor put thy hand to writing before thou hast learned.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Use,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if: Love sobriety, act temperately, and nothing to excess, lest thou fall into the scandal of confusion. ⟦s3⟧ It befits the Christian therefore before all things to love humility, to be wary in the reading of Scripture, to hold right faith, to keep measure in knowledge, and the rule of charity, that he may be approved before God and men.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *How sufficient.* ⟦s2⟧ A slender fare is profitable both to the soul and to the body, but a superfluous one on the contrary. ⟦s3⟧ *A little wine.* ⟦s4⟧ Spiritual wine also, [1215D] taken moderately, is profitable both to the body and to the soul: for immoderately it makes a man rave, and disjoins him from God, whence anxious care, and the greatest disturbance, and perpetual torment.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Sound sleep.* ⟦s2⟧ Sleep is finer on a small fare. ⟦s3⟧ But mystically he who is sober in the spiritual wine rests in the contemplation of truth until the morning of the resurrection.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *And vomit,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Physicians say that vomiting purges the stomach: it lessens the bile. ⟦s3⟧ But mystically he who goes beyond measure and is weighed down with carnal sins, let him vomit out by the confession of sin the foulness of it, and by the chastisement of the body return to health.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *In all thy works.* ⟦s2⟧ He admonishes that we lose not [1216A] time, that we work well, that keeping the commandments of God we may find rest for our souls.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Bountiful.* ⟦s2⟧ To the letter: he invites to almsgiving: because *he that soweth in blessings shall also reap of blessings [n: (II Cor. IX)]*. ⟦s3⟧ But mystically he who faithfully dispenses the bread of the word, and does not hide the testimony of truth, shall be blessed both in the present and in the time to come.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *For many,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] That is, drunkenness has overthrown many, which lessens the understanding, stirs up wrath, inflames lust, reveals the secrets of the mind.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *Fire.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, as fire softens the hardness of iron and makes it glow; so wine, inflaming the hearts of the proud, drives them to crime.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *Wine is an equal life.* ⟦s2⟧ Wine soberly drunk [1216B] does not take away the life of equity; but brings in sobriety and the other ornaments of the virtues. ⟦s3⟧ *Wine.* ⟦s4⟧ It is plain that provocation, wrath, and many ruins are born of drunkenness, and that bitterness of soul and the wounds of crimes are multiplied.

## CHAPTER XXXII.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *A ruler.* [n: (RAB.)] He who is set over others in dignity ought not to be proud: *He that is the greater among you, let him become as the lesser,* etc. [n: (Matth. X)].

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *A little gem of a carbuncle.* [n: (RAB.)] It holds the chief place among burning gems, etc., *as far as* and he venerates the mysteries of the Lord's passion with a worthy sacrament.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Before the hail.* [n: (ID.)] The flash of the lightning shall go before the weight of the hail; so the brightness of grace [1216C] before modesty.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *For him that riseth, the hour.* [n: (ID.)] He admonishes that we be not sad, etc., *as far as* who by grace made us, and filled us with all good [works].

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *A man of counsel.* ⟦s2⟧ By how much anyone is the more prudent, by so much the more he uses the counsel of his neighbours, that he may add to himself understanding.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Son, without counsel.* ⟦s2⟧ These things can be taken of the catholic and of the heretic. ⟦s3⟧ For the catholic, fearing God, does all things by the counsel of the teachers and by the witness of the Scriptures. ⟦s4⟧ But the heretic, trusting in his own wisdom, presses on also with his own inventions: but when his folly shall have appeared, he shall be convicted by divine sentences through the holy preachers
