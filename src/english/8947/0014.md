## CHAPTER XXXVII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Every friend.* [n: (RAB.)] Above he set forth more diligently the true friend and the false, saying: *And he will not remain in the day of tribulation.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *But a companion.* ⟦s2⟧ A true friend loves with his whole soul, and goes forward in deed. ⟦s3⟧ Whence, *Let us not love in word nor in tongue, but in deed and in truth [n: (I Joan. III)]*.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *O presumption.* ⟦s2⟧ He detests hypocrisy, [1219D] when an evil man feigns himself to be good, *covering over the dry malice,* in which, namely, there is no fatness of charity or of mercy.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *For the belly's sake.* ⟦s2⟧ For gluttony, not for friendship. ⟦s3⟧ Heretics can be understood among false friends, who do all things rightly, as though they were counsellors of souls, when they are betrayers. ⟦s4⟧ Whence, *Beware of false prophets who [n: (Matth. VII)]*, etc. ⟦s5⟧ *With a father-in-law.* ⟦s6⟧ A father-in-law, who is a kinsman on the woman's side, shows a womanish and changeable mind; and likewise jealousy, which belongs chiefly to souls.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Every counsellor.* ⟦s2⟧ He still teaches that all caution is to be had in taking to oneself friends and counsellors. ⟦s3⟧ For many feign themselves friends and counsellors, [1220A] and stretch out snares. ⟦s4⟧ Whence Jeremiah, *Let every man keep himself from his neighbour [n: (Jer. IX)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Attend ye not.* [var: Vulg. *Non attendas*, "attend thou not," singular] [n: (RAB.)] He has enumerated the divers kinds of those who give counsel, among whom he has noted many persons who are at variance with the truth. ⟦s2⟧ Whence he has enjoined that counsel is not to be taken with such, whose counsel is fraught with peril. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore the counsellor's prudence, faith, will, and religion must be looked to beforehand.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *The soul of a holy man.* ⟦s2⟧ The diversity of counsellors having been shown, he adds how far the knowledge of the saints, poured in by the Holy Spirit, excels human wisdom.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *A skilful man.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he had spoken before concerning the tongue, he continues concerning good and evil teachers.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *The life of a man.* [n: (ID.)] The present life of men [1220B] is not in uncertainty with him. ⟦s2⟧ Whence, *The days of man are short, the number of the months is with thee [n: (Job. XIV)]*.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *Be not greedy.* ⟦s2⟧ According to the history, he praises frugality, he blames surfeiting, which brings the devourer into infirmity. ⟦s3⟧ But mystically he admonishes that we be temperate in temporal things. ⟦s4⟧ Whence, *Thou hast found honey, eat what is sufficient for thee [n: (Prov. XXV)]*, etc. ⟦s5⟧ And elsewhere: *Not to be more wise than it behoveth [n: (Rom. XII)]*.

## CHAPTER XXXVIII.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Honour the physician.* [n: (RAB.)] He would have us be discerning, etc., *as far as* both, therefore, are to be honoured, but the spiritual are to be preferred.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Was it not from the wood.* ⟦s2⟧ He touches an ancient history, [1220C] because in the desert of Sur the children of Israel found bitter water, but God showed Moses a wood, which, being cast in, made the water sweet; where, according to the history, it is shown that waters are made sweet by the medicine of the wood But mystically the bitter water signifies the law, to which, if the confession of the cross and the sacrament be joined, it is made fit for drinking.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Curing in these.* [n: (ID.)] Against divers infirmities physicians set divers things, namely meats and drinks, plasters, and ointments: that the medicines may be suited to the diseases. ⟦s2⟧ In like manner do the spiritual physicians.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Turn away.* ⟦s2⟧ Like to this is that: *Decline from evil, and do good. ⟦s3⟧ And from every,* etc., and not the body only, but that thou mayest be *innocent in hands and clean of heart.*

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1220D]VERS. 11.-- *Give sweetness.* ⟦s2⟧ The sacrifice of bread and wine, offered in memory of the Lord's passion, chiefly heals the wounds of souls, and is exceedingly necessary not to the living only, but to the dead also.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *He shall fall into the hands of the physician.* [n: (ID.)] Of sickness and of trouble, etc., *as far as* and they instantly beseech the Lord that he would direct their works, and grant refreshment to the sick.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Son,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because he has discoursed of infirmities and of the cures of physicians, he adds concerning the funerals of the dead.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Remember the last things.* [n: (ID.)] An admonition of good fathers, that their sons may prepare for themselves the viaticum of good works.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1221A]VERS. 22.-- *For neither is there any returning.* ⟦s2⟧ As though he should say: Therefore be thou cautious in all things, lest perchance at the death of a neighbour thou grieve more than is meet.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *In rest.* ⟦s2⟧ As though: Keep continually the memory of him that is to die, and trust, and take comfort in hope, lest in the last moment it fail.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *He that holdeth the plough.* ⟦s2⟧ He signifies the holy preachers, who at all times, by teaching and by exhorting, instruct their hearers, that they may draw forth the virtue of the inner man, and reform the human soul to the image of God. ⟦s3⟧ Whence, *As a wise architect I have laid the foundation [n: (I Cor. III)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And elsewhere: *My little children, of whom I travail in birth again, until Christ be formed in you [n: (Gal. IV)]*. ⟦s5⟧ As many as are the kinds of virtues, so many workings and lessons do they imprint by their teaching [1221B] upon the minds of the elect.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *So every craftsman,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The order of preachers, who through divers offices perform their ministry, temper their doctrine according to their hearers: who form the frail and the weak by word and by example, that they may make vessels unto honour.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He shall give his heart.* ⟦s2⟧ When by the traces of good works he shows to those subject to him that the rolling conversation of the present life is unchangeable.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *In his arm.* [n: (RAB.)] Because by many examples, etc., *as far as* that in the furnace of the heart he may make firm the working of the disciples by the fire of charity.

## CHAPTER XXXIX

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *If he shall continue.* [n: (RAB.)] Of Christ, whose [1221C] members they are: from their own they have all things: who is the virtue and the wisdom of the Father.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Hearken unto me.* [n: (ID.)] Understand my doctrine, ye minds of the elect begotten through baptism, and bring forth the fruit of martyrdom in the course of this mortal life.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *And with harps.* [n: (ID.)] Whence: *Give praise to the Lord on the harp [n: (Psal. XXXII)]*. ⟦s2⟧ The harp is the hollowness in the lowest part of the wooden belly, etc., *as far as* which, after the form of the psaltery, come down to us from above.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *In his word.* [n: (ID.)] When he gathered the waters together, etc., *as far as* or the water of persecution is restrained by the dispensation of God.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *The works of all flesh.* ⟦s2⟧ The presence of God goes before all things, and whatsoever is done in time is present to him without [1221D] time. ⟦s3⟧ *Therefore there is nothing new, nor anything changeable in his sight [n: (Act. I)]*.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *It is not to say,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He forbids us to be curious, and to search out the things which it is not fitting to know. ⟦s3⟧ For all things shall be made manifest in their own time, when the Lord shall enlighten the hidden things of darkness, and shall make manifest the counsels of hearts.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *A blessing,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As, after the outpouring of the flood, the waters returned, that the dry land might appear and bring forth; so, after the extinction of the ungodly, the power of bearing fruit is given to the elect.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *The beginning of that which is necessary.* ⟦s2⟧ Before the flood men were nourished with the natural food of the fruit of the earth, much less were flesh-meats granted them.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1222A]VERS. 35.-- *Fire, hail.* [n: (RAB.)] All things obeyed man before sin; but after sin the world of lands fights against him. ⟦s2⟧ Thence famine and death, and a thousand perils of death, the beasts also rage against him, and the sword of heavenly vengeance.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *In the commandments.* ⟦s2⟧ Because, according to the sentence from above, in the fitting time a worthy vengeance shall lay waste sinners.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *Therefore from the beginning, I was confirmed.* ⟦s2⟧ He looks back to the things above, where he says: *It is not to say,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ For he sees the incomprehensible judgments of God, and his unsearchable ways, and dares not thence to speak or to write anything rashly: but disputing temperately of these things, he left not the cause, but the manner, manifest to the divine counsel.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *It is not to say.* ⟦s2⟧ He gives profitable counsel, [1222B] that we discuss not his works incautiously, but praise and bless the Creator in all things.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 41.-- *And now in all his own time.* [var: Vulg. *Et nunc in omni corde et ore collaudate*, "and now with all the heart and mouth praise ye"] As though he should say: Say not that former times were better than they now are. ⟦s3⟧ Virtues make the days good, vices evil.
