## CHAPTER XL.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Thoughts.* [n: (RAB.)] He enumerates the evils which man endures for his sins.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *A little.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the flesh, dead and without feeling, seems to rest for a time, the soul already feels pain, and after the resurrection it shall live with its own body in eternal punishments.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *He is troubled.* [n: (ID.)] Therefore shall the wicked be tormented in punishment, etc., *as far as* of the noble, namely, [1222C] and of the ignoble.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And for them was the flood made.* ⟦s2⟧ To sinning man a manifold punishment has been appointed, that, being corrected, he may repent, and thereafter beware; but because he neglects it, and goes forward in his punishments, God brings the flood upon the world, that he might destroy the man whom he created. ⟦s3⟧ But in comparison with the punishment to come, that which man suffers in the present is nothing.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And all the waters.* [n: (ID.)] The works, namely, of sinners do not obtain the fruit of the heavenly reward, but the destruction of punishments.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *The substances of the unjust.* ⟦s2⟧ They begin to sprout, but they waver in temptation, and suddenly wither at the first heat of persecution.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *The grandchildren of the ungodly are not many.* ⟦s2⟧ Often [1222D] we see the offspring of the ungodly quickly fail, chiefly when the sons imitate the iniquity of the fathers.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *The life of him that is sufficient for himself.* ⟦s2⟧ Sweet is the sleep of him that labours, whether he eat little or much. ⟦s3⟧ *Thou shalt find a treasure.* ⟦s4⟧ For he truly proves himself to be a Christian who believes and does the commandments of Christ.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Wine and music rejoice the heart.* ⟦s1⟧ These naturally soothe and gladden the soul, but the love of wisdom and a tongue that teaches sweetly strengthens the understanding much more.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *The eye shall desire grace and beauty.* ⟦s2⟧ The adornment, namely, of the temple and the diversity of its worship, which the former people had under the law, the preaching of the Gospel, sown throughout the world, surpasses by the greenness of good works

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1223A]VERS. 26.-- *Riches and powers.* [n: (RAB.)] Riches given in the Lord, etc., *as far as* both these and all the resources of the world.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *A man that looketh.* ⟦s2⟧ For it is foolish to be idle, and to seek a reward from another's labour. ⟦s3⟧ For every one shall receive according to his own works.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *In the mouth of the imprudent want shall be trodden down,* [var: Vulg. *condulcabitur*, "shall be sweetened"] etc. ⟦s2⟧ He whom it pleases to lie idle in torpor, and not to profit his neighbours by teaching, shall at length burn with the pains of hell and with too late repentance.

## CHAPTER XLI.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *O death,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] To him who loves the prosperity of the world and riches, bodily death is bitter, because he neglects the joys of the life to come.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1223B]VERS. 5.-- *This judgment.* [n: (ID.)] It is common to all flesh, nor can any man escape it, even though he live a hundred or a thousand years or more; whence: *What man is he that shall live, and shall not see death? [n: (Psal. LXXXVIII)]*.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *The children of abominations.* ⟦s2⟧ Abominable are the children of sinners, who, namely, have imitated their ungodly fathers.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Of an ungodly father.* ⟦s2⟧ He who nourishes his sons ill, or ruins them by his example, is guilty of their destruction. ⟦s3⟧ Or, he who begets evil works out of a depraved will, shall be damned for them

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Unto destruction.* ⟦s2⟧ He answers to the things above, in which he discoursed of temporal death: because, as man, created out of the earth, returns for original sin into [1223C] the earth, though he is to rise again in incorruption; so the ungodly, accursed for their sins, shall go with body and soul into everlasting destruction.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Have a care of a good name,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That thou live well and religiously. ⟦s3⟧ For thus is a good name gotten.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Better is the man that hideth his folly,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He that shall hide up corn shall be cursed among the peoples, but a blessing upon the head of them that sell.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Nevertheless return ye in those things which proceed.* [var: Vulg. *reveremini*, "revere ye"] Because he foretold that a good name and a good and profitable life are [good], and reproved those who hide knowledge, he persuades that they return to the study of teaching, because it is not good to be irreverent and disobedient [1223D] to one's own teacher.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 20.-- *It is not good to observe every irreverence.* [var: Vulg. *reverentiam*, "reverence"] According to the letter, he instructs us that we beware of vices, and give our labour to virtues, and that we choose not the worse, despising the better, whereupon follows everlasting confusion.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *Turn not away thy face from thy neighbour,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] When the angels shall gather out of the Church all scandals, etc., *as far as* and has mingled itself with the fornications of the heretics.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Look not upon another man's wife,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Carnal concupiscence, which ought to lie under the spirit: this we ought not to search into, nor to be made subject to its suggestions.

## CHAPTER XLII.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1224A]

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Repeat not the word,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He forbids to utter good and evil together out of the secret of the heart; whence: Doth a fountain send forth out of the same hole sweet and bitter water?

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Of the equality of the balance,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Everywhere equity is to be followed, etc., *as far as* let us also govern those subject to us with the discretion of discipline.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the equality of the balance.* ⟦s2⟧ How greatly hypocrisy and guile displease the Lord is shown by these words: *A deceitful balance is an abomination before God.* ⟦s3⟧ He who weighs the cause of the poor man otherwise than that of the powerful; or who thinks his own errors lighter than those of others; or they who lay upon men's shoulders burdens not to be borne; or he who does good things in public and evil in secret, is abominable [1224B] to the Lord.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And of a very wicked servant.* ⟦s2⟧ He signifies that the servant who is not corrected by words is to be restrained by stripes. ⟦s3⟧ The servant of vices also is to be corrected by severe discipline.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Where there are many hands,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He admonishes that where there are divers wills gaping after spiritual gain, dispensing must be done with caution. ⟦s3⟧ For some seek the word with a will to tempt, others with a will to learn, but not all things are fitting for all.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Of discipline.* ⟦s2⟧ He who reproves each one with fitting discipline shows himself learned and an approved teacher in the sight of the saints. ⟦s3⟧ Nor is youthful age to be despised in a teacher whom life and discretion commend.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1224C]VERS. 9.-- *The daughter of the father.* [n: (RAB.)] Mystically the daughter of the father, etc., *as far as* or she may transgress the law of God, being made barren in all good things.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The daughter of the father.* ⟦s2⟧ He teaches, to the letter, that parents bear the care of their offspring, and nourish them with discipline, and that they loose not the reins to wantonness and pleasure.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Over a wanton daughter,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Who requires a diligent keeping. ⟦s3⟧ For if she is left to her own will, she confounds her teacher and makes him blush in the multitude of the people. ⟦s4⟧ Whence, she ministers to thee perilously whose countenance thou often regardest. ⟦s5⟧ For unchaste eyes know not how to consider the beauty of the soul, but that of bodies.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *For better is the iniquity of a man,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The woman signifies either the sex, etc., *as far as* [1224D] because she does things worthy of confusion.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *I will remember therefore the works,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Thus far he has discoursed of divers matters, etc., *as far as* and afterward he describes the strong deeds of the fathers.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *For the Lord hath known,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because all the ages of the world are present to him, with whom nothing is past, nothing to come; he alone reveals mysteries, who knows the hearts of each, who beholds all thoughts and words.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *The great works of wisdom.* ⟦s2⟧ When, namely, he revealed to the prophets and to the apostles the sacrament of his counsel, hidden from ages and generations, to the faithful. ⟦s3⟧ For he himself is before all and after all, to whom nothing is added, nothing diminished.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1225A]VERS. 23.-- *And as a spark.* ⟦s2⟧ We consider the works of God as it were a spark, because we perceive but a small knowledge thereof.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *All things are double,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] God has so ordered all things, that the things which seem contrary to one another may agree, as the elements or the four seasons.
