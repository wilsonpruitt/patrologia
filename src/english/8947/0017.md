## CHAPTER XLVIII.

VERS. 1.-- *And Elias arose.* [n: (RAB.)] Who burned up with heavenly fire the two captains of fifty sent by king Ozias [n: (IV Reg. I)].

VERS. 2.-- *Who brought in.* [n: (ID.)] Mystically, the famine of three years figured the hunger of the word of God and of the faith of the Trinity, etc., *as far as* that afterward he might come who, neither [1228B] translated nor carried up, should pierce the ethereal heaven by his own power.

VERS. 13.-- *Elias indeed was in a whirlwind,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Since the disciple is not above his master, etc., *as far as* for he who touches the death of Christ with firm faith is partaker of his resurrection.

*Fulfilled in Eliseus.* Who prayed on the day: *Let thy spirit be double in me,* and obtained it. Whence he was unconquerable in patience and in virtue, who both in life and in death wrought many signs.

VERS. 17.-- *There was left a very small people.* Because the house of David, and the tribe of Juda, and Benjamin, did not utterly cast off the worship of God, but some of them were idolaters, as Joram and Achaz: others clave to God, as Ezechias and Josias.

[1228C]VERS. 19.-- *Ezechias fortified his city,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] That Ezechias stopped up the fountain, etc., *as far as* the city, its enemies destroyed, is fully shown in Paralipomenon to have been delivered.

## CHAPTER XLIX.

VERS. 1. *The memory of Josias.* [n: (RAB.)] The memory of king Josias, etc., *as far as* he himself by the zeal of God cleanses the land of Juda and Jerusalem from all abominations, who says: *The zeal of thy house hath eaten me up.*

VERS. 5.-- *Except David, and Ezechias, and Josias,* etc. These three, in comparison with the others, are not said to have sinned. For what they sinned, they blotted out by worthy penance, and by devotion and piety they pleased God.

[1228D]VERS. 8.-- *They burned the chosen city of holiness,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Certain men understand this place in the person of Christ, etc., *as far as* but these being destroyed, he built the Church of God.

VERS. 10.-- *Ezechiel who saw,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Mystically, Ezechiel, etc., *as far as* and to have foretold remedies to the penitent.

VERS. 11.-- *In the shower.* He signifies that he preached that a rain of vengeance was to come upon sinners, and promised the consolation of mercy to those walking by the way of the commandments of God.

VERS. 13.-- *For he also,* etc. Because he signifies Christ, who is the image of God: that whosoever shall believe in God, he may be sealed as with a ring.

[1229A]VERS. 14.-- *And Jesus the son of Josedec.* Because through the grace of Jesus Christ, who is our king and priest, after the captivity of the devil's oppression, the Church is built again.

VERS. 15.-- *Nehemias.* [n: (RAB.)] *God the comforter,* or *comforted by the Lord,* etc., *as far as* who builds the city of God, that is, the Church, and comforts the mourning.

VERS. 16.-- *No man,* etc. Enoch, *dedication.* This signifies Christ, who built and consecrated the Church with his own blood; who came into the world without sin, and is to be received with glory into heaven.

VERS. 17.-- *As Joseph.* Joseph, like Jacob his father, desired the land of promise, and detested Egypt; instructing us that we should desire the land [1229B] of the living, that we may rest in peace.

VERS. 18.-- *And his bones were visited.* [n: (RAB.)] He signifies that Joseph, etc., *as far as* and they buried him in Sichem, which is Neapolis, a city of the Samaritans.

VERS. 19.-- *Seth and Sem,* etc. Seth the son of Adam, and Sem the firstborn of Noe, were preferred, as well in faith as in manners, before the men of their own time; in whom Christ is signified, who is God and man.

## CHAPTER L.

VERS. 1.-- *Simon the son of Onias.* [n: (RAB.)] He came to men in his own time, etc., *as far as* since Jesus describes so expressly the life and the manners of Simon.

VERS. 3.-- *The wells of waters.* The depths of the Scriptures, which lie hid under figures in the divine books, [1229C] but overflow from the mouth of the preachers, as it were a sea, to the satisfying of very many.

VERS. 5.-- *And he enlarged the entrance of the house of the court.* [var: Vulg. *ingressum domus et atrii*, "the entrance of the house and of the court"] Because in the rude, whom the court signifies, and in the perfect, who are signified by the house, the merit of the masters increases.

VERS. 11.-- *In his receiving the robe,* etc. The order of preachers, in receiving the robe of glory, and in clothing itself with the consummation of the virtues, shows the judgment of its own dignity, and the adornment of faith, and the vesture of good working. For these things ought to appear in priests, that they may be able worthily to teach.

VERS. 13.-- *And in receiving the portions from the hand of the priest.* [var: Vulg. *de manu sacerdotum*, "from the hand of the priests," plural] He receives the portions from the hand of the priest, who exacts from his ministers the act of good working.

VERS. 15.-- *And performing the consummation at the altar.* The consummated oblation is given to the most high King, when the body of Christ is offered to God.

VERS. 17.-- *He poured out at the foundation.* In commemoration of Christ, who is the foundation of the Church, the odour of this oblation is most sweet to God almighty.

VERS. 18.-- *Then the sons of Aaron cried out.* The priests of Christ set forth their preaching through a consummated passion. Well are the trumpets called beaten out, because preachers grow the more, the more they are struck with the hammers of tribulations.

VERS. 19.-- *Then all the people,* etc. When the ecclesiastical office is performed by the ministers of the altar in [1230A] memory of Christ, the people, stirred up to the praise of God, prostrates itself upon its face, that is, shows the humility of its heart. *To give prayers to the Almighty.* Because whatsoever is done for the love of God is before God a prayer and an odour of sweetness; because the whole Church is busied that it may persevere in prayer until the end of the world, when the body of Christ shall be honoured in incorruption, and the members shall be joined to their own head.

VERS. 21.-- *And the people besought,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Because whatsoever is offered to God in the unity of faith is received with him; whatsoever is outside it, is reckoned to impiety. A great sound is made in the Church, when to the priests praying and blessing all the people answers: Amen: which sound pertains as well to the cry of the voice as to the devotion of the heart.

[1230B]VERS. 22.-- *Then coming down,* etc. The assembly of the saints rejoicing. *He lifted up.* When it gives thanks for the perfection of the labour in the salvation of the whole people, then it multiplies praises, repeats prayer, because it never ceases from praises.

VERS. 23.-- *To show the power of God.* To preach, namely, his mercy, who gives us joy of heart, and peace in eternal times, that all the men of Israel, and the lovers of the eternal King, may preach the power and the goodness of God in the deliverance of his people.

VERS. 27.-- *Two nations,* etc. A twofold ruin, namely, heathenism, which is polluted with idolatry and with vain conversation.

VERS. 29.-- *The doctrine of wisdom,* etc. He commends his own work, [1230C] showing how profitable it is to him that reads, that meditates, and that works.

## CHAPTER LI.

VERS. 1.-- *The prayer of Jesus the son of Sirach.* It is better referred to the whole Church than to one person, because in it many kinds of tribulations and divers consolations are described.

*I will give glory to thee, O Lord the king.* [n: (RAB.)] Jesus Christ is the helper and protector of his spouse, who delivered himself up for her, that he might free her from the perdition of original sin.

VERS. 5.-- *The gates of tribulations.* These are the mouths of princes and of tyrants, whence come forth threats and deceits, which compel the servants of God to deny the faith.

[1230D]VERS. 6.-- *From the pressure of the flame.* This befell the three children bodily in the furnace of fire; but all the saints are delivered spiritually from the heat of persecution.

VERS. 9.-- *And my life was drawing near to hell.* Because the life of the flesh, daily failing, tends toward death. For he calls death *hell*, because the death of the flesh is the vengeance of the first sin, as hell is the perpetual punishment of sinning souls.

VERS. 10.-- *They compassed me about on every side,* etc. The Church, seeing herself to be compassed about by enemies, and human consolation to be wanting, seeks help from God, from whom is every good, to whom she ascribes it that she is delivered from her enemies.

[1231A]VERS. 18.-- *When I was yet younger.* [n: (RAB.)] In youth, or in boyhood, etc., *as far as* how from infancy he sought wisdom.

*Before I wandered,* etc. He signifies that before the error of boyhood or of youth he desired wisdom, and asked it of the Lord, and persevered even to the last.

VERS. 19.-- *As an early grape.* He compares the comeliness of wisdom to an early grape, that he may show that delight is with her. For a grape is called early because it ripens quickly.

VERS. 24.-- *I purposed,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] First he says that he entered into counsel that he might do it, etc., *as far [1232A] as* for being thus confirmed, he shall come to the fruit of wisdom.

VERS. 29.-- *My belly.* Moved to repentance through the knowledge of wisdom. *Therefore.* To come to the perfection of wisdom is not of human study, but of a divine gift.

VERS. 30.-- *The Lord hath given me,* etc. A good reward is a learned tongue, which made [him] instruct and correct the brethren.

VERS. 37.-- *Let the soul rejoice.* Great is the joy of the heart, if it remembers the mercy of the judge on high.
