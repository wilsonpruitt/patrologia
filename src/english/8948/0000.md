## THE PROLOGUE OF BLESSED JEROME ON THE BOOK OF ESTHER.

[n: ( Vide inter opera B. Hieronymi, tom. VIII.)]

## CHAPTER ONE.

[0739C]

[n: (RAB., expos. in lib. Esther, tom. III.)] The book of Esther, which the Hebrews number among the hagiographa, contains the mysteries of Christ and of the Church, etc., *as far as* who reigned after Darius his uncle, surnamed Nochus, forty years.

VERS. 2.-- « Susan the city, » etc. Susan is a chief city in Persia, which historians say that the brother of Memnon founded, and that it took its name from the river Susus. There is the royal house of Cyrus, adorned with white and variegated stone, with golden columns, and with panelled ceilings and with gems: containing a likeness of the heaven marked out with glittering stars, and many incredible things. There Assuerus celebrated a banquet most great in riches and abundant in delights. For the power of the sacred discourse sometimes so [0739D] narrates the things that are past that it sets forth the things to come: so approves the doer that it contradicts him in his ministry: so condemns the deeds that it urges these same to be done mystically.

VERS. 3.-- « In the third year therefore. » In the time, that is to say, of this age he [Christ] laid open the mystery of his incarnation, and most abundantly ministered the spiritual feasts of preaching, and of his own body and blood. The first time was before the law, the second under the law, the third under grace.

« A great banquet [sic: *convivum*] for all the princes. » -- « The kingdom of heaven is likened to a man that is a king, who made [0740C] a marriage for his son [n: (Matth. XXII)]. » And elsewhere: « A certain man made a great supper: and called many [n: (Luc. XIV)]. » The history of this banquet shows the pomp of riches and the luxury of the king: but allegorically it signifies the spiritual delights of Christ, which he dispenses to each one. For Christ is that most wealthy king who, entreated by the prayers of his wife, that is, of the Church, delivers the Jews, that is, his own confessors, out of the hand of their enemies, and justly condemns those same [enemies]. For neither is it necessary that, if the good things of certain men signify Christ, their evil things also should suit him, that is to say, Christ. For Moses signified Christ in many things; but not in this, that he doubted at the waters of contradiction. But neither did Aaron in the making of the calf. Nor Solomon in the filth of lust. So Assuerus in that judgment, [0740D] as in the bountiful banquet, signifies Christ, just as Isaias in Cyrus the king of the Persians signifies Christ, of whom he afterwards subjoins: « I girded thee, and thou hast not known me [n: (Isa. XLV)], » etc. For if unjust kings in their evil deeds signify the devil; why do not just kings in their good deeds show forth Christ? Nabuchodonosor commanded the peoples, when the sound of the symphonies and of the musicians was heard, to fall down and adore the statue. And the devil by worldly sweetness bends the human race away from rectitude of mind to the following of avarice, which is the service of idols.

VERS. 5.-- « The days of the banquet. » The first of the great ones, or of the primitive [0741A] faithful, were refreshed, according to that: « I was not sent but to the sheep that are lost of the house of Israel [n: (Matth. XV)]. » In the sacrament of circumcision, of the Lord's resurrection, and of the New Testament and of the true circumcision: and there are eight beatitudes, to which that banquet leads, which is held only in the days; but the wicked feasted in the nights.

VERS. 6.-- « And there hung. » Fine linen signifies the mortification of the flesh. Purple, the blood of martyrdom. Marble columns, the steadfastness of the teachers. Well therefore is it said that hangings of divers colours were suspended by cords of fine linen and of purple through rings of ivory upon marble columns; because the comeliness of the Church ought to shine forth in the teachers, in the meditation of wisdom and in the [0741B] ascent of the virtues, through the mortification of the flesh and through chastity together with the dignity of martyrdom; and by their word and example to come to the knowledge of others: that being instructed and strengthened by these, they may deserve to enter the hall of paradise.

« And they were supported by marble columns. » That is, by the teachers, of whom it is said: « I have established the pillars thereof [n: (Psal. LXXIV)]. » From elsewhere [cj: *Ex*; read *Et*, "And elsewhere"]: « He made the columns of silver, the resting place of gold [n: (Cant. III)]. »

« Upon the pavement. » The emerald is so called from its exceeding greenness. Parian, a kind of most white marble. For by the greenness of faith and the whiteness of chastity is built the foundation of humility. « For blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven. [n: (Matth. V)]. » And: « He that humbleth himself [0741C] shall be exalted [n: (Luc. X)]. And elsewhere: « Learn of me, because I am meek [n: (Matth. XI)], » etc.

VERS. 7.-- « Wine. » According to that: « The charity of God is poured forth in our hearts [n: (Rom. V)], » etc. To one is given the word of knowledge, to another of wisdom, to another faith, to another the grace of healings.

« Should compel the unwilling to drink, » etc. No one is compelled to receive a spiritual gift: God wills sons, not slaves: he wills the will, not necessity, according to that: « He that can take it, let him take it [n: (Matth. XIX)]. » Likewise. « If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments [n: (Ibid.)]. » And: « If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all things that thou hast [n: (Ibid.)], » etc. [var: Vulg. Matt. 19:21 *vade, vende quae habes* — the verse has neither the conjunction nor "all things"]

VERS. 8.--Every one what he would, » etc. According to the swiftness and the capacity and the profit [0741D] of each. For so is preaching to be tempered, that it may become useful in all, may harm none, and may pass among the vices of all as a two-edged sword. So let it take away, cutting off on account of pride, that it may not increase timidity. So laying upon the idle and the sluggish the solicitude of work, that it may not increase importunate activity in the restless and the curious, and so of the rest.

VERS. 9.-- « In the palace, where king Assuerus, » etc. Jerusalem, where the temple is and the holy of holies, or in the meditation of holy Scripture; in which the Godhead grants the knowledge of its own power.

VERS. 11.-- « That he might show, » etc. « When the fulness of time came, God sent the Son [n: (Gal. IV)], » etc. For then the Son of God, being incarnate, manifested with more abundant grace the mysteries of the law [0742A] with which he had before fed the faithful.

VERS. 12.--Who refused. » Despising not only the envoys, but the king's command also; whence in the Gospel the elder son, being asked by his father to the banquet, would not go in: and they who were called to the supper, being taken up with other matters, would not come, and according to the indignation of the master of the house they were rejected and others appointed in their place.

VERS. 16.-- « And Mamuchan answered, in the hearing of the king and of the princes: The queen hath injured not only the king, » etc. He, reckoned last in the order of the seven wise men, promulgates the sentence, and signifies Paul the apostle, who says: « I am the least of the apostles, who am not worthy to be called an apostle [0742B] [n: (I Cor. XV)]. » And to the Jews contradicting and filled with zeal he says: « Because you judge yourselves unworthy of eternal life, behold we turn to the Gentiles [n: (Act. XIII)]. »
