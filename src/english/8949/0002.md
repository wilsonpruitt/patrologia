## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

*To the midwives. [n: (ORIG., ibid.)]* He strives also to corrupt the midwives. *Of whom one was called Sephora,* [0186D] that is, *sparrow; the other Phua,* namely, *ruddy* or *modest;* through these he seeks that the males be slain and the females kept alive: but the midwives feared God, and did not do as he had commanded them. The midwives have taught us, before our time, to hold a certain form of rational instruction. For midwives are physicians, and they cherish both males and females at their birth. Instruction therefore is the common rational knowledge, and it reaches almost every understanding, it instructs all and cherishes all. If anyone in it be of manly spirit, one who would seek heavenly things and follow after things divine, he would come, as one healed and made strong, the readier through these instructions to the understanding of things divine. For one of them is, as it were, a *sparrow,* which teaches loftier things and calls upward on the wings of rational teaching; [0187A] *the other, ruddy* or *modest,* is moral, it composes manners, teaches modesty and honourableness. To me, however (since Scripture says of them that *they feared God, and did not act according to the precept of the king of Egypt* ), they seem to preserve the figure of both Testaments, and Sephora, who is interpreted *sparrow,* to suit the law, which is spiritual; but Phua, who is *ruddy* or *modest,* to indicate the Gospels, which are red with the blood of Christ and glow throughout the whole world with the gore of the passion. By these, therefore, the souls that are born in the Church are tended as by midwives, because from the Scriptures all the medicines of instruction are supplied. Yet Pharaoh attempts through these to slay the males, when he suggests heresies and depraved doctrines to those who are studious in the divine Scriptures. But the foundation of God stands [0187B] unmoved. For the midwives fear God, that is, they teach the fear of God, which is the beginning of wisdom. Thus what follows is the more fittingly adapted, that because the midwives feared God, they made themselves houses. Which according to the letter has no consequence: for a house is not made for the reason that God is feared. But if thou see how the Scriptures of the New and the Old Testament, teaching the fear of God, make houses for the Church, and fill the whole world with houses of prayer, it will seem reasonably written. Thus therefore these midwives, because they fear God, that is, teach the fear of God, do not do the precept of the king of Egypt, but keep the males alive; nor yet is it said that they obeyed by keeping the females alive. But I say confidently, they do not keep the females alive. For neither are vices nor [0187C] luxury taught in the Church, nor are sins nourished, which is what Pharaoh wishes when he bids the females be kept alive; but virtue alone is cultivated and nourished. But let us refer these things to each single person. If thou fearest God, thou wilt not do the precept of the king of Egypt: for he commands that thou live in delights and covet things present; thou, if thou fearest God and dost exhibit the office of a midwife, if thou desirest to profit thine own soul, keepest the males alive, that is, thou dost heal and cherish the inner man who is in thee, and through good acts and understandings dost gain eternal life.

VERS. 16-21.-- *If it be a male,* etc. [n: (ISID.)] *Allegorically.* Pharaoh bids the males be killed, the females live: so the devil, lest the strength of faith prevail, the virtues being slain, wills the vices to live, and strives to kill the strong and manly senses of the mind by which we savour of things divine, that is, [0187D] reason, prudence, constancy, innocence, and faith; and that which is womanish, weak, frail and prone to vices he desires to survive, that is, ambition, violence, lust, wrathfulness, and things like these, which are the figure of women.

VERS. 19.-- *Who answered,* etc. [n: (AUG. in Gen., tom. III.)] It is asked whether such lies are approved by divine authority, etc., *as far as* but those whose conversation is already in heaven, I do not think ought to shape their measure of speech concerning truth and falsehood by the example of the midwives.

[n: (GREG., Moral., lib. XXXVIII, cap. 2, 3.)] A lie is above all things to be guarded against, etc., *as far as* for by sparing [0188A] they endeavoured to cover the life of the infants, by lying, their own.

[n: (AUG., in lib. de Locutionibus in Exod., tom. III.)] And because the midwives feared God, etc., *as far as* so that what is consequently set forth concerning the wages to be fixed by Laban may seem to pertain to the making of that very house.

VERS. 22.-- *Pharaoh therefore commanded,* etc. [n: (ORIG., homil. 2.)] The Egyptians, whom Pharaoh here commanded to keep only the females alive, hated the males. For they hated the virtues, they nourished the vices. Now also the Egyptians lie in wait, if a male be born to the Hebrews, that they may slay him at once, unless they beware, keep watch, and hide him. Scripture relates that someone of the tribe of Levi begot a male, and saw that the infant was goodly [0188B] and hid him. Let us not, therefore, do our justice before men, but with the door shut let us pray to our Father in secret, and let not the left hand know what the right hand does, lest it be snatched away by the Egyptians, be set upon, be drowned in the waves. If I give alms because it is the work of God, I beget a male; but if I seek praise from men, and do not hide it, it is seized by the Egyptians, it is plunged into the river, and with so much labour and so much zeal I have begotten a male for the Egyptians.

*Whatsoever is of the female [n: (HIERON., in Ecclesiasten., c. 3)]*. Wherever in the Scriptures a woman is read of, and the frailer sex, let us transfer it to the understanding of matter. Whence Pharaoh too does not wish males to be born, but females, who are near to matter; and, on the contrary, none of the saints is related, save very rarely, to have begotten daughters. And [0188C] Salphaat alone, who died in his sins, begot all daughters: Jacob is the father of one daughter, and on her account is brought into peril.
