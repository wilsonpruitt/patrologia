## CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1-5.-- *He went out,* etc. [n: (RAB. in Exod., tom. II.)] Hence Josephus says: But Amram, fearing lest, being seized, he should fall under the king's anger, etc., *as far as* or to lead out, as Moses was led out of the river.

*Alleg.* The parents of Moses, whose faith and works the natural law adorned [n: (ID., ibid.)], *as far as* she herself also is taught to blush to worship idols.

*A man of the house,* etc. [n: (DIOD.)] Not without design is the father of Moses passed over: for Christ also, whose type Moses bore, is without a father.

[0188D]VERS. 2.-- *And seeing,* etc. [n: (CHRYSOST., hom. 54 in Act. apost.)]. It is wonderful that not through those things which seem to be of safety, but plainly through their contraries, all things are done for us. Pharaoh ordered the boys to be cast into the river; unless the boys had been cast in, Moses would not have been saved: when he was being kept safe, he was not in honour; when he was exposed, then he was made to be in honour. A Jew threatened him, saying: *Wilt thou kill me?* and this profited him, that he might philosophize in solitude and live in safety.

*[n: (ORIG., hom. 2.)] Allegorically.* Pharaoh's daughter is the Church gathered from the Gentiles, to whom it is said by the prophet: *Forget thy people, and thy father's house, for the king hath desired thy beauty [n: (Psal. XLIV)]*. [var: Vulg. *Et concupiscet rex decorem tuum*, "And the king shall desire thy comeliness"] [0189A] This, therefore, is she who went out of her father's house and came to the waters, that she might be washed from the sins which she had contracted in her father's house: and at once she took on bowels of mercy, and had pity on the infant; and she, coming to the marsh, found Moses lying exposed by his own, and caused him to be nursed. He is nursed among his own, there he passes his infancy; being made stronger, he is brought to her and is adopted as a son. Moses signifies the law. The Church, therefore, coming to the waters of baptism, received the law shut up within the tibin and smeared with bitumen. Now the tibin is a kind of covering woven out of papyrus, and formed of rods or of the bark of trees. The law therefore lay shut up within coverings of this sort, smeared with pitch and bitumen, that is, with the base and foul understandings of the Jews; it lay soiled and forgotten until the Church should come from the Gentiles and take it up [0189B] out of the miry and marshy places into the halls of wisdom and the royal roofs. Yet it passes its infancy among its own: for among those who do not understand it spiritually it is little, and an infant, and has the food of sucklings; when it comes to the Church, Moses is stronger and more robust; for the veil of the letter being removed, perfect food is found in the reading of him. The wages of the nursing she receives from Pharaoh's daughter, with whom the law is born and nursed. But what is it that the Synagogue receives from the Church? I think it is that which Moses says: *I will lead you into emulation by that which is not a nation: by a foolish nation I will stir you up to wrath.* [var: Vulg. *Ego ad aemulationem vos adducam in non gentem : in gentem insipientem, in iram vos mittam*, "I will bring you to emulation… I will send you into wrath"] The Synagogue therefore receives this wage from the Church, that it should no longer worship idols: for seeing the Gentiles so turned to God that they no longer know idols, it blushes any longer [0189C] to worship idols. We also, if we have had Pharaoh for a father, if he begot us in evil works, when we shall have come to the waters, let us take up the law of God: let not the letters be foul to us as an obscure covering; let us eat those things of it which are small and of milk; let us take up those which are perfect, and let us store them within the royal roofs of the heart. We have a great and strong Moses: let us think nothing small of him, nothing base, but all magnificent, all excellent, all elegant: for all is great that is spiritual and of a lofty understanding.

VERS. 6-8.-- *And seeing in it a little one,* etc. [n: (THEOD.)] *Mystically.* This above all could betray him to her, for the Egyptians had not yet admitted such a mystery, although in a later time they observed the law of circumcision [0189D] in imitation of the Hebrews. Whence Jeremias: *I will consider all that cut round the foreskin, the Egyptians and the sons of Edom. [n: (Jer. IX)]* [var: Vulg. *visitabo super omnem qui circumcisum habet praeputium, super Aegyptum, et super Juda, et super Edom*, "I will visit upon everyone that hath the foreskin circumcised, upon Egypt, and upon Juda, and upon Edom"]

[n: (ISID.)] Moses, that is, Christ, is found by the faithful at the river of the laver and the water of baptism, etc., *as far as* and receiving him with religious observance they guard him.

VERS. 9-11.-- *And when he was grown she delivered him,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Josephus relates that Pharaoh's daughter brought Moses, when grown, to her father, who set his own crown upon him, which Moses at once threw down, because he saw a graven image sculptured there. Whence the priest who was present wished to kill him, saying that Egypt was to be overthrown by him, as he had [0190A] received from the oracle of the demons. But Pharaoh's daughter delivered him. And when he was of full age, he was sent by Pharaoh against the Ethiopians. And since he knew that he would journey through deserts full of serpents, he took ibises, that is, Egyptian storks, and, having measured out his camp, brought them forward, that they might devour the serpents and put them to flight: and thus the army passed the night in safety. When therefore he had come to the city which he was to storm, the queen of the Ethiopians fell in love with him, and according to the agreement delivered the city to him and married him: hence it is that Aaron and Mary quarrelled against Moses on account of his Ethiopian wife.

VERS. 12.-- *And when he had looked about this way and that.* [n: (Aug. Quaest. in Exod. )] Concerning this deed of Moses we have disputed sufficiently in that work which we wrote concerning the life of the patriarchs [0190B] against Faustus: was the natural disposition in him praiseworthy, by which he committed this sin, as rich land is praised, before useful seeds are sown, by a certain wildness of useless herbs: or is the deed itself to be justified? Which does not seem so, because he as yet bore no lawful power, neither received from God nor ordained by human society: and yet, as Stephen says in the Acts of the Apostles, he thought his brethren understood that by him God would give them salvation; so that by this testimony Moses may seem to have been able to dare this, having been already divinely admonished (which Scripture in that place passes over in silence).

[n: (ISID.)] *Allegorically.* Moses, that is, Christ, slew the Egyptian, that is, the devil, who is injurious to us in this pilgrimage, by defending us, and in the sand [0190C] buried him slain. For he lies hidden in those who have no stable foundation. But he builds the Church upon a rock, and likens those who hear his word and do it to a prudent man, who built his house upon the firm rock. But others he likens to a fool, who builds upon the sand.

VERS. 13.-- *And going out the next day,* etc. In this Moses bore the figure of Christ, who as the corner stone strove to make peace in himself between the discord of the two peoples. He who did the injury and despised the reconciler is the Jewish people, which rejected the preaching of Christ reconciling, and persisted an enemy of the believing people. Whence John IX: *Be thou his disciple, we are the disciples of Moses.*

VERS. 14-21.-- *Moses feared,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] If thus [0190D] this word has been divulged. Two things are to be noted in this expression: namely, that the sentence hangs and is so left; and then, that he put the word for the deed.

VERS. 22.-- *Who bore,* etc. *Allegorically.* Christ joined to himself the Church from the Gentiles, which by the gift of the sevenfold Spirit is one in the catholic faith. Whence: *One is my dove;* which bears a son who is called *a stranger,* and another who is *God's help:* for the saints are strangers upon the earth, but they never lack the help of God.

VERS. 23-25.-- *But after much time,* etc After Moses had taken a wife in the land of Madian, the king of Egypt died: because when Christ converted the nations throughout the world [0191A] to the faith, the principality of the devil was destroyed.

*Groaning.* Unless the king of Egypt die in thee, thou dost not know thyself to be oppressed by the servitude of Egypt, and by the works of clay and brick; nor dost thou groan in heart, nor cry out to the Lord that he may free thee.
