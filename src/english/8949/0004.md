## CHAPTER III.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2, 3.-- *And there appeared to him.* [n: (ISID.)] *Allegorically.* ⟦s2⟧ There was a flame in the bramble, and it was not burnt up. ⟦s3⟧ The bramble, the thorns of the sins of the Jews: the flame in the bramble, the word of God, that is, the law given to that people. ⟦s4⟧ But the flame does not burn up the bramble, because the law that was given does not consume their sins. ⟦s5⟧ Others understand by the flaming bramble the Church, which is set aflame by persecutions, and which, [0191B] when the Lord speaks to her, is not burnt up. ⟦s6⟧ That the Lord appeared to Moses in the bramble shows that he appears to the faithful in the Church, and nowhere else. ⟦s7⟧ The Hebrews say that God appeared to Moses in a bramble for this reason, that the Jews might not be able to carve themselves an idol out of it. ⟦s8⟧ For God always cuts away the occasion of idolatry.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (GREG. lib. XXII Moral., cap. 20.)] Moses, mingled with the Egyptians, was as it were awake to the world, and therefore did not hear the voice of the Lord: but when the Egyptian had been slain, after he fled into the desert, while he dwelt there forty years, he fell asleep to earthly tumults, and therefore he perceived the divine voice. ⟦s2⟧ For when the mind is lulled from outward things, the word of God penetrates more truly. ⟦s3⟧ For the throng of earthly thoughts, clamouring about it, closes the ear of the heart. ⟦s4⟧ For a man does not perfectly suffice for both things [0191C] when he is divided, because if he opens his hearing outwardly, he grows deaf within.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *And the Lord seeing,* etc. [n: (GREG., praef. in Moral., c. 2.)] The angel who is described as having appeared to Moses is recorded now as the Lord, now as an angel: an angel, because by speaking outwardly he serves; the Lord, because presiding inwardly he bestows the efficacy of speaking. ⟦s2⟧ For when he who speaks is governed from within, he is recorded both as an angel through his service, and as the Lord through his inspiration.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He called him out of the midst,* etc. [n: (GREG. lib. XXVIII Moral., c. 5.)] Often God speaks through angels to earthly and to heavenly substances at once: as here, when he joined the fire and the bramble, and united one thing above and another below. ⟦s2⟧ Which is then only done, when something [0191D] great is signified by the conjunction. ⟦s3⟧ For in addressing Moses through the kindled bramble, he showed that he would become a teacher of the people, one who should both receive the flame of the law and not shun the thorn of sin: so that from that people should come forth one who by the fire of the Godhead should take the sins of our flesh as it were the thorn of the bramble, and should preserve the substance of his humanity unconsumed in that very flame of the Godhead.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5-7.-- *Loose thy shoe,* etc. [n: (THEOD.)] For two reasons he bids him loose his shoes: first, that he may render him more and more religious and reverent, so that he may receive his commands with trembling; then also he is taught by what reason the priests should minister in the tabernacle of God: for they discharged the priestly office with bare feet.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0192A] [n: (ISID.)] Because, namely, no one can worthily stand, or see God, unless he lay aside all things earthly and mortal. ⟦s2⟧ Otherwise, it was the custom of the ancients that if a bridegroom repudiated his bride, in sign of the repudiation he should be unshod. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore Moses is bidden to be unshod, and not to approach shod, as a bridegroom, to the Church, which is signified in the bramble; for this is to be reserved for Christ, who is the true bridegroom. ⟦s4⟧ Of whom John says: « Whose shoe's latchet I am not worthy to loose [n: (Joan. I)].

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *I am the God,* etc. [n: (HILAR. lib. IV de Trinitate.)] We must not speak of God otherwise than as he himself has spoken of himself to our understanding. ⟦s2⟧ It is an angel of God who appeared in the fire out of the bramble: and out of the bramble in the fire God speaks; thou hast the dispensation [0192B] in the angel, because in the angel it is an office, not a nature; thou hast God in the name of nature, because the angel of God is God.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *I have seen the affliction,* etc. [n: (GREG., Moral. )] For minds scattered abroad it is hard for each to return to himself: because evil ways hold those once taken so delightfully, in proportion as what has pleased is not lawful. ⟦s2⟧ Because, when the gift of discipline does not stand in the way, no penalty of retribution is foreseen to terrify. ⟦s3⟧ But with the eyes of the heart closed the soul is cast headlong, and penetrates temporal evils the more securely, the more harshly it despairs of eternal goods. ⟦s4⟧ But this wickedness of the reprobate separates the life of the elect, as grains from chaff, and while it afflicts, it sets free: because while it presses violently, it compels men to hasten to things above; which in the Israelite [0192C] people, when Moses called and Pharaoh raged, is signified. ⟦s5⟧ For Moses was sent to call, when Pharaoh pressed with hard works, so that the one by calling might draw, the other by raging might drive, that the people, basely fixed in servitude, might be moved either by being called or by being driven; this is done daily while, the heavenly rewards being preached, the reprobate are permitted to rage against the elect: so that those whom Egypt, that is, the present life, has oppressed by flattering, it may help by pressing; so that while God calls, torment may drive.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8-13.-- *And I will bring out of the land,* etc. [n: (GREG., homil. 32.)] To a rude people something had to be promised concerning the present life, that it might be able to be more sturdily strengthened in the future. ⟦s2⟧ For if it did not receive small things, it would not believe great ones. ⟦s3⟧ God therefore by bestowing earthly things persuades to heavenly things also, [0192D] so that receiving what it saw, it might hope for what it did not see.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Into a good and spacious land, in a land which flows with milk,* [var: Vulg. *in terram quae fluit lacte et melle*, accusative *in terram*, "into a land which floweth with milk and honey"] etc. [n: (Ibid.)] Present prosperity is sometimes given that it may call to a better life, sometimes that it may more fully condemn for ever. ⟦s2⟧ Hence to the Israelites the land of Chanaan is promised, that they may at some time be called to hope for things eternal: because a rude people would not believe promises, unless it received something near at hand from the promiser. ⟦s3⟧ Not therefore by hope alone is it drawn to things, but sometimes by things to hope. ⟦s4+5⟧ Whence Psalm CIV: *And he gave them the regions of the nations, and they possessed the labours of the peoples, that they might keep his justifications and seek after the law.*

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0193A]VERS. 14-16.-- *He who is hath sent me to you,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Moses is sent to punish Pharaoh and at last to slay him: the law was sent to destroy the devil.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Jerome, writing to Marcella concerning the ten names of God, puts in the sixth place the name *ehieh* ( אהיה ), which is read in Exodus: *He who is hath sent me to you.* ⟦s2⟧ For God alone, who has no beginning, holds the name of true essence: which was spoken by an angel to Moses when he asked his name, because in comparison with him who truly is, because he is unchangeable, the things that are changeable are as though they were not; for what is said to have been, is not: and what is said will be, is not yet. ⟦s3⟧ But God only is, who knows not to have been, or to be about to be. ⟦s4⟧ And the Father alone with the Son and the Holy Spirit [0193B] truly is. ⟦s5⟧ Compared with whose essence, our being is not; whence also we say: God lives, because the divine essence lives by a life which death does not possess.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17-20.-- *To a land flowing with milk and honey,* etc. [n: (AUG., Quaest. 4 in Exod.)] Ought we to take this spiritually, because according to propriety this was not the land which was given to the Hebrews? ⟦s2⟧ Or is it a manner of speech by which the richness and sweetness of the land is praised?

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *You shall not go out,* etc. [n: (AUG., ibid.)] That the Lord commanded the Hebrews to take from the Egyptians vessels of silver and of gold, and raiment, and added, *and you shall make spoil of them,* [var: Vulg. *et spoliabitis Aegyptum*, "and you shall spoil Egypt"] the judgment of this command cannot be unjust. ⟦s2⟧ For it is a command of God, concerning which there was no judging, but obeying. ⟦s3⟧ For he himself [0193C] knows how justly he commanded; it belongs to the servant to do obediently what he commanded.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Vessels of silver.* ⟦s2⟧ We ask silver and gold from the Egyptians, whereof to fashion a tabernacle for God, when we read the poets and the philosophers, that we may expound the divine Scripture more wisely and more eloquently.
