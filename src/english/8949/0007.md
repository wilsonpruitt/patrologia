## CHAPTER V.

VERS. 1.-- . . . . . *Let my people go. [n: (ORIG., hom. 3 in Exod.)]* Moses does not will that the people, being set in Egypt, should serve God, but that it should go out into the desert, and there serve him. For while a man remains in the dark deeds of the world, and is engaged in the obscurity of business, he cannot serve God. *For ye cannot serve God and mammon [n: (Matth. VI)]*. We must go out therefore [0198A] from Egypt: the world must be left behind, not in place but in mind, if we wish to serve the Lord. Whence John says: *Little children, love not the world, nor the things which are in the world [n: (I Joan. II)]*.

VERS. 2.-- *Who is the Lord?* He expresses the pride of the devil, who, scorning to be made subject to God, says: *I will be like the Most High [n: (Isa. XIV)]*; and as though doubting of the Lord's nativity, says:

*If thou be the Son of God, say that these stones be made bread [n: (Matth. IV)]*. But afterwards, as though under constraint, the demons cried out: *Why art thou come to torment us before the time? We know that thou art the Son of God [n: (Matth. VIII)]*. After torments they know what before they had refused to know. Pharao also, when he had been scourged, asks that supplication be made for him to the Lord. *Who is the lord, that I should hear his voice?* etc. Thou seest [0198B] what raw pride does; you will see a little later how much it profits under scourges; how much better a man is made when he has been beaten; he who now says, I know not the Lord, when he has been beaten will say: Pray to the Lord for me. Let no one therefore be so untaught in the divine discipline as to think the divine scourges a ruin and a penal destruction. Behold, even Pharao, most hardened, profits when he is beaten: for before the stripes he knows not the Lord, and when beaten he asks that supplication be made for him; and profiting under punishments, he knows how to acknowledge why he is punished. *I know not,* he says, *the Lord.* In the Gospel, being beaten, he amends this utterance, for the demons cried out: *We know thee who thou art: thou art the Son of the living God.* After the scourges he will let Israel go also, and will even urge it to depart. *For there is no fellowship of light with darkness, no part of the faithful with the unbeliever [n: (II Cor. VI)]*.

[0198C]VERS. 3.-- *The God of the Hebrews hath called us,* etc. [n: (AUG. in Exod. )] It is asked how it is said to the people, etc., *as far as* that he may obtain a just judgment.

[n: (ORIG.)] *Morally.* We set out on a three days' journey from Egypt, if we so keep ourselves from every defilement of soul and of body and of spirit, that our whole spirit, and soul, and body may be preserved unto the day of Christ.

[n: (ORIG.)] We set out on a three days' journey from Egypt, if, taking away rational, moral and natural wisdom from worldly things, we turn them to the divine ordinances. We set out on a three days' journey from Egypt, if, purifying in ourselves things said, done or thought (for these are the three by which men can sin), [0198D] we are made clean of heart, that we may be able to see God. But the prince of Egypt, when he sees himself the more vehemently pressed to let the people of God go, desires in the second place to obtain that they go not farther off, that they may not set out for the whole three days, saying: *Go not far away.* He does not will the people of God to be far from him. For he wills that, if not in deed, at least in speech they should sin; if not in speech, at least in thought. He does not will that they should set out from him for the whole three days. He wills to possess in some of us one day, in others two, in others the whole three days. Blessed are they who withdraw from him the whole three days.

*A journey of three days into the wilderness, and let us sacrifice to God,* etc. [n: (ISID.)] We walk a journey of three days going out of Egypt, that we may come to the place where [0199A] we ought to offer sacrifice to the Lord. This way is Christ, who says in John XIV: *I am the way, the truth and the life.* For whosoever shall have confessed Christ with the mouth, and believed with the heart that God raised him up on the third day, shall be saved, coming to the place in which a sacrifice of praise may be offered.

[n: (STRAB.)] *A journey of three days,* etc. At the third encampment, after they had crossed the Red Sea, they came to Ethan, which place is also called Mara, because of the bitter waters. Or of three days, that is, of three months, because in the third month after they went out of Egypt they came to Sina, where they sacrificed and received the law, where the tabernacle also was set up.

[n: (ORIG., hom. 3 in Exod.)] Now also Moses, whom we have with us (for we have Moses and [0199B] the prophets), that is, the law of God, wills to lead thee out of Egypt, if thou wilt hear it; it wills to set thee far from Pharao, to rescue thee from the work of clay and of straw: if only thou wilt hear, and understand spiritually; it does not will thee to remain in the deeds of the flesh and of darkness, but to go out to the desert, namely to a place empty of the disturbances and waves of the world, and to the quiet of silence. For the words of wisdom are learned in silence and quiet. In this place thou shalt be able to offer to the Lord the law of God, and to acknowledge the power of the divine voice. Therefore Moses desires to cast thee out of the waves of business and the noise of peoples, and out of Egypt, that is, out of the darkness of ignorance, that thou mayest hear the law of God and receive the light of knowledge.

VERS. 4-8.-- *Why, Moses and Aaron, do ye stir up,* [0199C] etc. [n: (ORIG., ibid.)] While the people is with him, and works clay or brick, while it is occupied with straw, he does not think it to be perverted, but to walk rightly. But if it should say, I wish to go a three days' journey, and to serve the Lord, he says the people is perverted through Moses and Aaron. Today also, if Moses and Aaron, that is, the prophetic and the priestly word, should stir up a soul to the service of God, to go out of the world, to renounce all that it possesses, to attend to the law and the word of God: straightway thou wilt hear the friends and confederates of Pharao saying: See how men are seduced, and youths are perverted, that they may not labour, may not serve as soldiers, may not do anything that profits; leaving necessary things behind, they follow after trifles and idleness. What is it to serve God? They are unwilling to labour and seek occasions of idleness: these were then [0199D] the words of Pharao, these his friends speak now also, and not only with words, but they persecute with stripes as well. He commands the scribes of the Hebrews to be scourged, straw not to be given, work to be exacted; these things the fathers suffered, and now the people of God suffers them. They who have wholly given themselves over to the prince of this world prosper; all things, as they think, fall out happily: but to the servants of God even a small and lowly sustenance does not come. For these are the straws which were given by Pharao; whence certain men, being wearied, say to Pharao: Why dost thou afflict thy people? For being overcome by stripes they fall from the faith, and confess themselves to be the people of Pharao, for not all who are of Israel, these are Israelites.

VERS. 9-13.-- *Let them be oppressed with works and let them fulfil [0200A] them,* etc. *Mystically.* From the time when Moses began to speak to Pharao the people of God is the more afflicted: from the time when the word of God comes into the soul, the devil rises up more sharply, and sends in greater vices. For before the word of God came, which should reprove vices, they endured in peace; but when it came, a great disturbance arises.

VERS. 14-20.-- *And they were scourged who,* etc. The children of Israel were more grievously worn down after they had determined to go out of Egypt; because they who wish to renounce the world and evil works are assailed by the devil with fiercer temptations.

VERS. 21.-- *Let the Lord see,* etc. [n: (GREG. l. XXIX, Moral., cap. 14.)] When the divine light illumines the human heart, *as far as* he grieves that he as it were stinks in the eyes of Pharao. [0200B]

*Because to stink,* etc. *[n: (ORIG. Hom. 3.)] Allegorically.* They speak truth, although they know it not, like Caiphas, who said: *It is expedient that one should die for the people [n: (Joan. XVIII)]*; he knew not what he was saying. For as the Apostle says: *We are the good odour of Christ, to some unto life, to others unto death,* so the prophetic word is a sweet odour to them that believe; but to the doubting and unbelieving, and to those who confess themselves the people of Pharao, it is made an execrable odour. Moses himself also says to the Lord: From the time when I spoke with Pharao, thou hast afflicted thy people; because before the word of God is heard there is no tribulation or temptation: but when the trumpet of preaching shall have shown the signal of war, the battle of tribulations arises. From the time therefore when Moses and Aaron began to speak to Pharao, [0200C] the people of God is afflicted. From the time when the word of God has been uttered into thy soul, a contest is stirred up within thee of the virtues against the vices, which before endured within thee in peace. But when the word of God has begun to make the distinction between one thing and another, a great disturbance arises, and without treaty a war is born: because injustice does not agree with justice, nor drunkenness with sobriety, nor lying with truth. Therefore let us not be troubled if we seem an execrable odour to Pharao. For the execration of vice is called virtue. But as Moses stood before Pharao, let us also stand; let us not be bent, *having our loins girt about with truth, and our feet shod with the preparation of the Gospel of peace [n: (Ephes. VI)]*. And we stand confidently, we beseech the Lord that he set our feet upon a firm rock, lest our feet be moved. But let us stand, that is, [0200D] let us withstand, according to that of I Peter V: *Whom resist ye, strong in faith: that Satan may quickly be crushed under our feet.* For by how much the more steadfastly and bravely we stand, by so much the more weakly Pharao. But if we shall have begun to be weak or doubting, he will become stronger. For when Moses lifted up his hands, Amalec was overcome; when he let them down as though weary, he grew strong. Let us therefore in the power of the cross of Christ lift up our arms, and raise in prayer holy hands, that we may deserve the help of God. Hence James says: *Resist the devil and he will fly from you [n: (Jac. III)]*. Let us act therefore that he may not only fly, but be crushed under our feet, extinguished in the depth of the abyss.

But if we withdraw from the Egypt of vices, [0201A] we escape the waves of the world as it were by a solid path, through Jesus Christ.

VERS. 22, 23.-- *Lord, why hast thou afflicted this people?* [n: (AUG.)] These are not words of contumacy or of indignation, but of inquiry and of prayer, as appears from those things which the Lord answered. For he did not reprove his unbelief, but disclosed what he was about to do.
