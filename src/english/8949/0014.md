## CHAPTER XII.

[0217D]VERS. 2-4.-- *This month,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] This is April, which among the Hebrews is called Nisam, among the Macedonians Ξανθικός; and it is called *Aprilis* as it were *aperilis*, because then the earth is opened for the bringing forth of shoots, herbs and flowers: or *Aprilis* from Venus, as it were *Aphroditis*; for Venus in Greek is called *Aphrodite*. The Hebrews hold this month as the first, because the fifteenth of its Kalends was the first day of the world: the fourteenth, the second day; the thirteenth, the third; the twelfth, the fourth, on which the sun and the moon were made, and then was the first equinox; for the sun in the east and the moon in the west divided the sphere of the world equally.

[n: (STRAB.)] This is Nisan, according to the Hebrews, in which the world was made, whose full moon is always to be looked for after the vernal [0218A] equinox: in which the people of Israel is freed from the servitude of Egypt, and the human race is snatched by the blood of Christ from the power of the devil. On the tenth day of the month the lamb is taken, on the fourteenth it is sacrificed: because in the law the passion of Christ is figured, and, the grace of the Gospel flashing forth, it is fulfilled; for the number ten, because of the decalogue, signifies the law, but the number four the four gospels.

VERS. 5, 6.-- *And it shall be,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 42 in Exod., tom. III.)] That manner of speech may disturb a man, etc., *as far as* what was figured by that precept.

*According to which rite,* etc. *Allegorically.* Because Christ took flesh of the just and of sinners, that no one might despair of pardon. Or because Christ was a kid to the Jews, who said: *This man is a sinner (Joan. [0218B] IX)*, but to us a lamb without spot. Or because he appeared *in the likeness of sinful flesh* and *died for sin, whereas he had no sin [n: (I Petr. II)]*.

[n: (STRAB.)] On the tenth day of April he came to Jerusalem, and on the following day he was received by the crowds with branches of palms and with divine praises. Through the three remaining days he was watched by the Jews until the fourteenth day, that is, until the fifth day of the week, on which he supped with the apostles, and afterwards was betrayed.

VERS. 7.-- *And they shall take of the blood thereof,* etc. [n: (GREG., homil. 22 in Evang., tom. II.)] The blood is set on when it is drunk not only with the mouth of the body, but also with the mouth of the heart, etc., *as far as* because we bear the cross of his passion upon the forehead.

VERS. 8.-- *And they shall eat the flesh,* etc. [n: (GREG., ibid.)] [0218C] By night we eat the lamb, etc., *as far as* for he offers praise out of leaven who prepares for God a sacrifice out of rapine.

*With wild lettuces,* etc. [n: (GREG., ibid.)] Wild lettuces are exceeding bitter, etc., *as far as* that he may know that all things are dispensed through the power of the Holy Spirit.

VERS. 9-11.-- *The head with the feet,* etc. *[n: (GREG., ibid.)] The head of Christ is God [n: (I Cor. XI)]*. To devour therefore the head of the lamb, etc., *as far as* let no one be sluggish on the journey of this life, lest he lose his place in the fatherland.

[n: (GREG. NYSS., de Vita Mosis. )] By those wrappings of things he admonishes us that through these riddles we should be willing to consider and to understand that this our life is a certain passing over. For after we have entered upon it, we are always driven by a certain necessity toward the going out, [0218D] for which he wills us to be prepared with hands and feet and the remaining equipment for the journey. Lest therefore our feet be hurt by thorns on this steep journey of living, we ought to travel not with bare feet, but fortified with harder shoes. But the thorns, which, fixed in the feet, not only hold back and retard but also kill, are sins, from which the hardness of the shoes defends: namely a continent, spare and hard life, which weakens and breaks the points of the thorns, by which, from a small and slender beginning, sins so make their way onward to the things before them, that they slay utterly. To the leading of this life an ankle-length and flowing garment, that is, a voluptuous and vicious manner of living, which lies open most widely, is a hindrance: wherefore it is to be drawn in [0219A] with a certain girdle and constrained, lest it flow about hither and thither. But the girdle is modesty and a careful manner of living, which admonishes us to use things for necessity, not for pleasure. The staff with which we drive off wild beasts, and by which we are sometimes supported, is hope. But the food set before us is only from the fire, since faith, which is signified by that food, ought to be most burning, possessing nothing moist or slippery.

*Phase.* In Hebrew it is *Pesach* פסח , in Greek Πάσχα, that is, *a passing over;* because it was first celebrated by the children of Israel as they went out of Egypt: we also must pass over from things transitory to things eternal, from things earthly to things heavenly.

VERS. 12, 13.-- *In all the gods,* etc. The Hebrews report that all the idols of Egypt were broken in pieces on that night, [0219B] and the temples also, either by an earthquake or by an inundation of the river Nile. But allegorically, as we go out of Egypt, the idols of errors fall down, and all the cultivation of perverse doctrines is shaken.

VERS. 14-17.-- *And you shall have this day,* etc. *[n: (AUG., in Exod. quaest. 43, tom. III.)] And you shall keep this day in your generations as lawful, everlasting* or *eternal;* which in Greek is called αἰὼνιον, is not to be taken thus, etc., *as far as* at every time of the present life simplicity is to be kept, malice to be shunned.

VERS. 18-20.-- *In the first month, the fourteenth day,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] *Mystically.* The evening of the fourteenth day of the first month, on which the Pasch is celebrated and the immolation of the lamb is accomplished, signifies the putting off of the old man and the beginning of the new, which is begun in baptism, [0219C] that figures the death of Christ. Whence: *As many of us as are baptized in Christ Jesus, are baptized into his death,* etc. [n: (Gal. III)]. From the evening therefore of the fourteenth day of the first month until the evening of the twenty-first, a whole week namely, we are commanded [jubentur] to eat unleavened bread, because from the receiving of baptism until the end of life we ought to live simply and without guile. Whence Peter: *Laying aside all malice and all guile and dissimulation, as newborn babes,* etc. [n: (I Petr. II)].

VERS. 21.-- *The ancients.* Note that he communicates the commandments of God first not to boys, not to young men, but to the elders, because in the aged is wisdom, and in length of time prudence.

VERS. 22.-- *And a bunch of hyssop,* etc. [n: (STRAB.)] [0219D] Hyssop is a lowly herb, growing upon rocks, purging the breast and the lung: and it signifies true humility, of which Christ afforded us the example in himself. We dip therefore the bunch of hyssop in the blood, when, mindful of the humility of Christ, we study to imitate him.

*Dip it in the blood,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 46 in Exod.)] It is asked what blood he calls that which is beside the door, since he would have understood the blood of that lamb by whose immolation the Pasch is made. Or does he command that it be done in that manner consequently, although he has been silent of this, that the same lamb be slain beside the door? Or, which is the more credible, he therefore said *of the blood which is beside the door,* because he who is to anoint is about to set [0220A] beside the door the very vessel in which he received the blood, that he may have it at hand while he dips.

VERS. 23-29.-- *And he will not suffer the destroyer,* etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Whether the smiter was a good angel or an evil one, it is not necessary to inquire: for whoever he was, he obeyed the command of the Lord. Yet it is to be known that evil angels are wont to work such plagues, by the permission of God.

The tenth precept is: *Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's goods,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ex 20:17 *Non concupisces domum proximi tui … nec omnia quae illius sunt*, "Thou shalt not covet thy neighbour's house … nor any thing that is his"] Contrary to this is the tenth plague, the death of the firstborn. By another precept theft is forbidden, by which rapine also is condemned: for it is worse to snatch away by violence than by stealth. He therefore who wishes to take away openly or secretly has his own precept: but to covet a neighbour's goods, even if thou seek it by a just succession, [0220B] is not lawful. For those who wish to possess other men's goods as it were justly, seek also to be heirs to them when they die. When thou praisest a thing left behind, as though the man possessed it by right, God condemns him who desires it unjustly. For thou who seekest to be made heir to another dost not wish that he should have heirs, among whom nothing is dearer than the firstborn. By right therefore art thou punished in thine own firstborn, thou who, by coveting other men's goods, seekest out as it were a shadow of right, and losest the firstborn of the heart, which are the better things. The firstborn indeed is faith. For no one does good things in whom faith has not gone before. All good works are spiritual sons, but among these faith is the firstborn. Whosoever therefore thou art that secretly covetest another's goods, thou losest faith within. For thou servest not by charity but by fraud, as one loving him whom thou desirest to die, that thou mayest succeed him.

[0220C]VERS. 30-33.-- *For there was not a house,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 44 in Exod.)] Since only the firstborn were dying, how was there no house in which there was not one dead? Or had this also been divinely provided, that in all the houses there should be firstborn, in whom the Egyptians should be struck, even those who dwelt in the land of Gessen? For here they were of men, or of animals, not of the land; that is, men and animals that were firstborn were dying, by a hidden and angelic stroke: not anything had been done in the earth or in the heaven, as the frog, or the locust, or the darkness. For since the land of Gessen had been free from such plagues, the benefit reached the Egyptians who dwelt in it with the Hebrews: but by this plague all were struck.

VERS. 34.-- *The meal sprinkled,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] [0220D] *Mystically.* Without leaven the children of Israel set out from Egypt; that we, going out of this world, may cast away the leaven of malice and of wickedness, and feast in the unleavened bread of sincerity and of truth.

VERS. 35, 36.-- *And they asked,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 45.)] This had already been done before the deaths of the firstborn. But now it is repeated by way of recapitulation, for it was narrated when it was done. For how could it now come about that these things should be lent amid so great a mourning: unless perhaps one should say that by this plague also those Egyptians were not struck who dwelt with the Hebrews.

*Vessels of gold,* etc. These signify the doctrines which are learned out of the very custom of the nations by no unprofitable study; or precious souls out of the nations, as [0221A] vessels of gold and of silver; with garments, that is, with bodies: which join themselves to the people of God, that once for all they may be freed out of this world as out of Egypt.

VERS. 37-45.-- *And they set forward,* etc. [n: (ORIG. hom. 5 in Exod.)]. He who prepares to set out from Egypt, who desires to leave behind the dark deeds of the world and the darkness of errors, must first set out from Ramesse, which is interpreted *the commotion of the moth.* If thou wilt come to this, that God may be thy leader, and that the rock may follow which bestows spiritual meat and drink, go out from Ramesse, lest thou lay up treasure where the moth destroys and thieves dig through, etc.

*If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all that thou hast, and give to the poor, and thou shalt have treasure in heaven, and come, follow me [n: (Matth. VI)]*: this is to set out from Ramesse, and to follow Christ.
