## CHAPTER XII. (cont.)

[0221B] *From Ramesse to Socoth,* etc. [n: (ORIG. ibid.)] Let us see what is the place of encampment to which they come. From Ramesse they came into Socoth, which is interpreted *tabernacles.* When therefore, leaving Egypt, thou hast shaken off the moths of every corruption and hast cast away the incitements of the vices, thou shalt dwell in tabernacles, by which, namely, we would not be unclothed but clothed upon. And the dwelling in tabernacles shows that he who hastens to God is unencumbered. But not even in this must one stand still: the camp must be moved from Socoth also, and one must hasten into Othon, which is interpreted *signs to them,* because here, namely, thou shalt hear that God goes before them by day in a pillar of cloud, and by night in a pillar of fire; not this in Ramesse, nor in Socoth, which are the second camp. The third are those in which the divine signs are wrought: this [0221C] was the three days to which Moses and Aaron were hastening, and Pharao contradicted them, saying: *Go no further.* He was unwilling that the children of Israel should reach the place of the signs, that they might be able to enjoy the mysteries of the third day. Whence Osee VI: *God will raise us up after two days, and on the third day we shall rise again, and we shall live in his sight.* [var: Vulg. Os 6:3 *Vivificabit nos post duos dies; in die tertia suscitabit nos, et vivemus in conspectu ejus*, "He will quicken us after two days: on the third day he will raise us up"] The first day is for us the passion of Christ; the second, that on which he descended into hell; the third, that on which he rose again: therefore on the third day he went before them by day in a pillar of cloud; but by night, in a pillar of fire. *It is necessary therefore that they who are baptized in Christ should be baptized in his death, and be buried together with him, and on the third day rise again, whom, namely, he hath raised up with himself and hath made to sit in the heavenly places [n: (Rom. VI)]*.

*About six hundred thousand,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 47.)] It is asked [0221D] whether the Hebrews came to so great a number through those years in Egypt, etc., *as far as* that is, after the death of Joseph they were oppressed with hard servitude.

VERS. 46-51.-- *Nor of him,* etc. The bone of Christ was not broken on the cross, because, seeing him already dead, they did not break his legs: or the strength of the Godhead in the flesh is not corrupted by blasphemy, because he is not mere man, but true God.

## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 2-8.-- *Sanctify unto me every firstborn,* etc. Because I slew all the firstborn of the Egyptians in your deliverance.

VERS. 9-12.-- *And it shall be as a sign,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 48 in Exod.)]. What is it that he says when he gives commandment concerning the pasch, [0222A] etc., *as far as* otherwise grace would now be no longer grace.

VERS. 13-15.-- *But every firstborn of man,* etc. [n: (CYRILL.)] He commands the firstborn of men to be redeemed, that he may forbid manslaying: he who created all things that they might be incorruptible.

[n: (GREG., lib. XXVII Moral., cap. 1.)] The ass signifies uncleanness, the sheep innocence. Therefore to exchange the firstling of an ass for a sheep is to turn the beginnings of an unclean life into the simplicity of innocence, so that after the sinner has done those things which the Lord rejected as unclean, he may now by his doing bring forth those things which he may lay upon the sacrifice of God. For we are all born in sin, and conceived of the delight of the flesh, and have contracted original guilt to ourselves. Whence also by our own will we are entangled [0222B] in sins. But he too who after the malice of the flesh is turned to the innocence of the heart exchanges the firstling of an ass for a sheep, that is, the malice of his former life. For he who ceases to be such as he was before, and, having laid hold of cleanness of the flesh, keeps innocence, has exchanged the firstling of the ass for a sheep in the offering of God.

VERS. 16.-- *And as a thing hung up.* The Jews, understanding this ill, used to bind little patches or phylacteries about their heads, in which they had the ten words written before their eyes.

VERS. 17.-- *When therefore he had sent them out,* etc. [n: (GREG. lib. XXIV Moral. cap. 13)]. Often the converted, in the very entrance of their conversion, receive tranquillity of the flesh, gifts of prophecy, the teachings of doctrine, miracles of signs or the grace of healings. Afterward they are wearied by the hard [0222C] provings of temptations, from which they believed themselves to be free: because if in the beginning the bitterness of temptations were to beat upon them, they would easily return to the vices from which they have not far withdrawn. From those therefore who go out of Egypt wars near at hand are taken away: because to those who leave the world a certain tranquillity is first shown, lest, troubled in that very tenderness and beginning, they return to what they had left: first therefore they feel the sweetness of security, first they are nourished by the quiet of peace; but after the sweetness has been known they bear the contests of temptations the more tolerably, the more deeply they have known in God the things they love.

VERS. 18-20.-- *God led them not,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 49)]. Here it is shown that all things ought to be done which can rightly be done by counsel, for the avoiding of those things which [0222D] are adverse, even when God is most openly a helper [n: (Epist. ad Dam., tom. I)].

*And the children went up armed,* etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Aquila, who translates word for word, not, as they suppose, from contentiousness, but from diligence, where the Seventy set, *but in the fifth generation the children of Israel went up out of the land of Egypt,* rendered, *and the children of Israel went up armed out of the land of Egypt,* although, in place of what we have called *armed,* it may, according to the ambiguity of the Greek speech, be understood *equipped* or *furnished,* on account of the household stuff wherewith they despoiled the Egyptians. But where the Seventy set *in the fifth generation,* they who have diligently inspected the books of the Hebrews report that not "fifth" but "five" is written, so that the sense may be: but five went up, [0223A] the children of Israel, out of the land of Egypt; and because this seemed to be less understood, "generation" was added. But Aquila is said to have translated properly both in the rest and chiefly in this place, as all the benches of the synagogues agree.

*[n: (AUG., quaest. 53.)] But in the fifth progeny,* etc. Whether he means to reckon a progeny within a hundred years, etc., *as far as* Naason begot Salmon.

VERS. 21-22.-- *And the Lord went before them,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] When the mystery of the third day has been received by thee, God will begin to lead thee forth and to show thee the way of salvation.

[n: (GREG.)] In the fire is terror, in the cloud the gentle soothing of a vision. The day is the life of the just man; the night, of the sinner. Whence Ephes. V: *You were once darkness, but now [0223B] light in the Lord,* etc. [var: Vulg. Eph 5:8 *Eratis enim aliquando tenebrae: nunc autem lux in Domino*, "For you were heretofore darkness"] By day the pillar was shown by the cloud, and by night by the fire; because almighty God will appear both gentle to the just and terrible to the wicked. Coming to judgment, he soothes these by the mildness of his lenity, and terrifies those by the strictness of his justice. Otherwise: the pillar of cloud went before the people in the desert, and shone with the splendour of fire not by day, but by night: because Christ, affording leadership to his followers by the example of his own conversation, shone with no light to those who trusted in their own justice; but upon those who acknowledged the darkness of their sins he shed the fire of his love. Christ is the cloud and the pillar, because he is upright, and firm, and upholds our infirmity; because he shines by night and does not shine by day, *that they who see not may see; and they who see may become blind [n: (Joan. IX)]*. The sacrament of Christ also [0223C] is manifest as it were by day in the flesh as it were in a cloud: but in the judgment as it were in the terror of night; because then there will be great tribulation, and it will shine as fire to the just, and burn the unjust.
