## CHAPTER XIV.

VERS. 2.-- *Phihahiroth, which is between Magdalum,* etc. [n: (ORIG., hom. 5 in Exod.)] Let us see what way is enjoined to be chosen. Turning back from Othon, he says, make your journey between Epauleum and Magdalum, which is over against Beelsephon, etc. Epauleum is interpreted *a winding ascent,* Magdalum *a tower,* Beelsephon *the ascent of a watchtower,* or *having a watchtower.* He believed perhaps that God would show a level and easy way, but it is an ascent, and a winding one. For the way by which one goes toward the virtues is not downhill, but the climb is narrow and difficult. [0223D] Whence Matthew VII: *For the way which leadeth to life is strait and narrow.* [var: Vulg. Mt 7:14 *Quam angusta porta, et arcta via est, quae ducit ad vitam*, "How narrow is the gate, and strait is the way that leadeth to life"] The journey therefore by which one goes forward is *a winding ascent,* and *the ascent of a watchtower* or *having a watchtower.* The ascent belongs to deeds, the watchtower to faith; it is shown therefore that both in deeds and in faith there is much difficulty, much labour: for many temptations meet us, many stumbling-blocks, when we would do the things that are God's. Then in faith thou wilt find many winding things, very many questions, the objections of heretics, the contradictions of unbelievers: this journey therefore must be made by those who follow the Lord. But there is also a tower on this road, of which it is said in Luke XIV: *Which of you, wishing to build a tower, doth not first sit down and reckon the charges, whether he have wherewith to finish it?* [var: Vulg. Lc 14:28 *Quis enim ex vobis volens turrim aedificare, non prius sedens computat sumptus, qui necessarii sunt, si habeat ad perficiendum*, "which are necessary, whether he have wherewith to finish it"] This then is [0224A] the tower, high and lofty by these virtues; and Pharao, seeing it, says: These men are astray. With Pharao, he who follows God is said to go astray, because the way of wisdom is winding, having many difficulties and turnings. If thou confess one Father invisible, and his one only-begotten Jesus Christ, and one Holy Spirit, this seems winding, difficult and incredible among unbelievers. If thou say that the God of majesty was crucified and is the Son of man who came down from heaven, these things seem winding and difficult; and he who hears (unless he hear it with faith) says that these men are astray. But be thou fixed in this faith, knowing that God has shown thee this way of faith, who says: Taking up the camp from Othon, place it between Epauleum and Magdalum.

[0224B] *He had been.* [n: (ORIG.)] Fleeing Egypt thou comest to the ascent of works and of faith, and to the building of the tower: thou comest to the sea, and the waves of temptations meet thee. Whence II Tim. III: *For they who will to live godly in Christ suffer persecution,* [var: Vulg. 2Tim 3:12 *Et omnes, qui pie volunt vivere in Christo Jesu, persecutionem patientur*, "And all who will to live godly in Christ Jesus shall suffer persecution"] because our life upon earth is a temptation. But if thou follow Moses, that is, the law of God, Egypt pursues thee. But see what shall come to pass: *There lifted himself up,* he says, *the angel of the Lord, who went before the camp of Israel, and he went away behind them. He lifted up also the pillar of cloud from before their face, and it stood behind them, and entered in between the camp of the Egyptians and Israel.* [var: Vulg. Ex 14:19-20 *Tollensque se angelus Dei, qui praecedebat castra Israel, abiit post eos: et cum eo pariter columna nubis, priora dimittens, post tergum stetit, inter castra Aegyptiorum et castra Israel*, "the angel of God … and with him together the pillar of cloud, leaving the front, stood behind"] This pillar of cloud is made a wall to the people of God: on the Egyptians it lays darkness; the pillar of fire is not carried over to the Egyptians, that they may see the light: but the pillar of cloud, that they may remain in darkness, because they loved darkness, not [0224C] the light. And thou therefore, if thou withdraw from the Egyptians, if thou flee from the power of the demons, see how great helps are prepared for thee, provided thou remain strong in faith: let not the horsemen and chariots of the Egyptians terrify thee, nor do thou cry out against the law of God, that is, against Moses, saying: *As though there were no sepulchres in Egypt, so hast thou led us forth.* [var: Vulg. Ex 14:11 *Forsitan non erant sepulchra in Aegypto, ideo tulisti nos ut moreremur in solitudine*, "Perhaps there were no graves in Egypt, therefore thou hast taken us away"]

VERS. 11-12.-- *Perhaps there were no sepulchres,* etc? [n: (ORIG., homil. 5 in Exod.)] These are the words of a mind failing under temptation: blessed is he who so receives temptations that no thought of doubt creeps upon him. To Peter also it is said in Matthew XIII: *O thou of little faith, why didst thou doubt?* These are the words of temptation and of frailty. Otherwise it is false, for it is better to die in the desert than to serve the Egyptians. For he who dies in [0224D] the desert, by this very thing, that he is separated from the Egyptians, that is, from the rulers of darkness and from the power of Satan, has had some profit, even if he could not attain to the whole. For it is better for one seeking the perfect life to die on the road than not even to set out. Whence the opinion of those seems to be false who, while they set forth the way of virtue as arduous and perilous, judge that it is not even to be begun. For it is better to die on the road than to be slain among the Egyptians and overwhelmed by false and bitter waves.

VERS. 13-14.-- *For the Egyptians whom you now,* etc. *[n: (AUG. quaest. 5 in Exod.)] For as you have seen the Egyptians today, you shall not again see them any more unto eternal time.* [var: Vulg. Ex 14:13 *Aegyptios enim, quos nunc videtis, nequaquam ultra videbitis usque in sempiternum*, "the Egyptians whom you now see, you shall see no more for ever"] But how are these words [sic: *ververba*] to be taken, [0225A] since the Israelites afterwards saw Egyptians? Or is it because those who then saw them did not see them further, since both they died who were pursuing, and these all, each on the day of his own death? For their descendants saw the descendants of those. Or is it to be understood, you shall not see them as today, that is, not as today pursuing and enemies, so that there may be no question even about the eternal time which he has here set down; because although both shall see one another at the time of the resurrection, yet not so as today.

VERS. 15-17.-- *Why criest thou to me?* etc. [n: (GREG.)] It is not our words that make voices before the ears of God, but our desires. For if we ask eternal life with the mouth and do not desire it with the heart, in crying out we are silent: but if we desire with the heart, though we are silent with the mouth, in keeping silence we cry. [0225B] Whence in the desert, when the people made a din with their voices, and Moses was silent from the noise of words, being silent he is heard, and it is said to him: *Why criest thou to me?* For in desire the secret cry does not reach human ears, but fills the divine.

[n: (ORIG., ut supra. )] No voice of Moses is heard, and the Lord says: *Why criest thou to me?* I would gladly know how the saints cry to God without a voice. The Apostle to the Galatians IV says: *For God will give the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying: Abba, Father.* [var: Vulg. Gal 4:6 *Quoniam autem estis filii, misit Deus Spiritum Filii sui in corda vestra, clamantem: Abba, Pater*, "And because you are sons, God hath sent the Spirit of his Son into your hearts"] And he adds: *the Spirit himself intercedeth for us with unspeakable groanings.* [var: Vulg. Rom 8:26 *sed ipse Spiritus postulat pro nobis gemitibus inenarrabilibus*, "the Spirit himself asketh for us"] And again in Romans VIII: *And he that searcheth the hearts knoweth what the spirit desireth.* So then, at the asking of the Holy Spirit, the cry of the saints is heard through silence. Moses is commanded to strike the sea with his rod, [0225C] that it may give way to the people of God as they enter, and that the waters which were feared, being made a wall on the right hand and on the left, may not only know no ruin, but may even afford a defence. The liquid takes on solidity, and the floor of the sea dries into dust: understand the goodness of the Creator; if thou obey his will, if thou follow his law, he compels the elements to serve thee against their nature. I have heard it handed down by our elders that for the several tribes there were made several divisions of the waters, and a way of its own for each. And this is shown from that which is said in Psalm CXXXV: *Who divided the Red Sea into divisions,* etc. And elsewhere, in Psalm LXVII: *There is Benjamin the lesser in a stupor. The princes of Juda, their leaders; the princes of Zabulon and the princes of Nephthali:* [var: Vulg. Ps 67:28 *Ibi Benjamin adolescentulus, in mentis excessu; principes Juda, duces eorum; principes Zabulon, principes Nephthali*, "There is Benjamin a youth, in ecstasy of mind"] an entrance seems to be enumerated proper to each tribe. The Apostle [0225D] says that a baptism was accomplished in Moses in the cloud and in the sea, so that thou who art baptized in Christ, in water and in the Spirit, mayest know that the Egyptians pursue thee and would call thee back to servitude, the rulers, namely, of the world, and the spiritual wickednesses which thou didst serve before. But thou goest down into the water, and comest out unharmed and risest up new, washed from the filth of thy sins, made ready to sing a new song. The Egyptians as they pursue are drowned in the deep, and if they seem to beseech Jesus not to drown them again in the deep.

[n: (ID.)] Otherwise, if thou flee Egypt, that is, the darkness of ignorance; if thou follow Moses, that is, the law of God, and the sea meet thee, that is, the waves of gainsayers, do thou also strike the struggling waves with the rod, that is, with the word [0226A] of the law and with the vigilance of the Scriptures; open thyself a way by disputation through thy adversaries, and the waves will give place to the victor; and while they who before gainsaid stand amazed and fixed with astonishment, thou wilt cut a straight path of faith by the lawful lines of disputation, and thou wilt so far advance in the word of doctrine that thy hearers, whom thou hast instructed in the words of the law, will now rise up against the Egyptians like waves of the sea, and will not only assail but even overcome and extinguish them. For he extinguishes Egypt who does not the works of darkness, who lives not carnally, but spiritually; who either drives out filthy thoughts from his heart, or does not receive them at all, according to that of Ephesians VI: Taking the shield of faith, that you may be able to extinguish all the fiery darts of the wicked one. [var: Vulg. Eph 6:16 *in omnibus sumentes scutum fidei, in quo possitis omnia tela nequissimi ignea extinguere*, "in all things taking the shield of faith, wherewith you may be able to extinguish all the fiery darts of the most wicked one"] In this way therefore we can even today see the Egyptians dead, and Pharao himself, [0226B] if we live with so great faith that God crush Satan under our feet speedily through Jesus Christ.

VERS. 19-20.-- *And the angel of God lifting himself up,* etc. [n: (STRAB.)] The angel, who is interpreted *messenger,* signifies the teachers, who announce to us the precepts of life, and with the cloud, that is, the knowledge of the Scriptures, go before the camp of Israel, that is, the Church, because with the knowledge of the Scriptures they preside. When the Egyptians come upon them he goes back behind, because in the time of persecution they set themselves in front on behalf of their subjects. But the cloud was dark to the Egyptians, bright to the Hebrews.

VERS. 21-31.-- *All the night,* etc. Because through the whole time of this life, which the night signifies, the grace of the Holy Spirit makes the waters of baptism passable to the faithful, [0226C] that they may pass over from spiritual Egypt into the land of promise: but it chokes the army of the enemy.

[n: (ISID.)] The Red Sea signifies the baptism consecrated by the blood of Christ; the enemies following at the back die together with their king, because past sins are blotted out in baptism, and the devil is choked: the Egyptians press on as far as the sea, and sins as far as baptism. After the passage of the Red Sea the people sing, the enemies being drowned; and the faithful, coming up from the laver, their sins extinguished, chant a hymn, saying: *Let us sing to the Lord, for gloriously,* etc.
