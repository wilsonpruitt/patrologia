## CHAPTER XV.

VERS. 1.-- *Then sang Moses,* etc. [n: (ORIG., homil. 6 n Exod.)] We read many canticles in the Scriptures, [0226D] but this is the first of them all, which, when the Egyptians and Pharao were drowned, is sung after the victory. For it is the custom of the saints, when the adversary is overcome (since they know that the victory was wrought not by their own strength but by the grace of God), to offer to God a hymn of glorifying. Yet as they sing they take timbrels in their hands, as is related of Mary and Aaron. And thou therefore, if thou hast crossed the Red Sea, if thou hast seen the Egyptians drowned and Pharao cast headlong into the deep, canst thankfully sing a hymn to God, and say: *Let us sing to the Lord gloriously,* etc. And thou wilt say this more worthily if thou have a timbrel in thy hand, that is, if thou have crucified thy flesh with its vices and concupiscences.

*For gloriously,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] It is not enough, *he is glorified;* [0227A] for it is one thing to be glorified, another to be gloriously glorified. For when Christ took flesh for us, he was glorified, who came to seek what had perished, yet not gloriously, because he had no comeliness nor beauty; he was glorified when he came to the cross and endured death; whence John XVII: *Father, the hour is come, glorify thy Son,* etc. [var: Vulg. Jo 17:1 *Pater, venit hora: clarifica Filium tuum*, "Father, the hour is come, clarify thy Son"] The passion of the cross therefore was also a glory, wherein he was glorified, yet not gloriously, but humbly; whence Philip. II: *He humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.* And Wisdom II: *Let us condemn him to a most shameful death.* In these things therefore he was glorified concerning himself humbly, not gloriously; whence Luke XXIV: *Because it behoved Christ to suffer, and so to enter into his glory.* [var: Vulg. Lc 24:26 *Nonne haec oportuit pati Christum, et ita intrare in gloriam suam?*, "Ought not Christ to have suffered these things, and so to enter into his glory?"] When he shall come in the glory of the Father, and of the angels, [0227B] to judge the earth, and shall slay the true Pharao, that is, the devil, with the spirit of his mouth, when he shall shine again in the majesty of the Father: then shall he be gloriously glorified, when all shall honour the Son as they honour the Father.

*He is magnified, the horse and the rider he hath cast into the sea,* etc. In the signs of Egypt. But he was gloriously magnified, because he drowned Pharao and his army. Or he was magnified in working miracles before the passion, in preaching, in dying on the cross: but he was gloriously magnified when he rose again a conqueror, the devil conquered, hell despoiled.

*The horse,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] The men who pursue us are horses; and all who are born in the flesh are figuratively horses, and have their riders. There are those whom [0227C] the Lord mounts, and they go about the whole earth; of whom it is said in Habacuc III: *Thy cavalry is salvation.* [var: Vulg. Hab 3:8 *Qui ascendes super equos tuos, et quadrigae tuae salvatio*, "and thy chariots are salvation"] Others the devil and his angels mount. Judas was a horse, and while he had the Lord for his rider he was of the cavalry of salvation: for, sent with the other apostles, he gave health to the sick and soundness to the languishing; but when he laid himself down under the devil, who entered into him after the morsel, being made the horse of Satan and led by his reins he rode against the Lord. All persecutors therefore are neighing horses, but they have evil angels as riders by whom they are driven, and therefore they are fierce. If thou see thy persecutor raging beyond measure, know that he is being pricked by his rider, and therefore he is savage and truculent.

VERS. 2.-- *This is my God,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] He therefore [0227D] shall be both my God and the God of my father. Our Father, who made us and begat us, is Christ, who said in John XVI: *I go to my Father,* etc. [var: Vulg. Jo 16:28 *vado ad Patrem*, without *meum*] If therefore I acknowledge that he is my God, I will glorify him; if also that he is my father's, I will exalt him: for it is a higher understanding, how Christ, that he may bind together and unite the truth of the one God, calls him his God whom by nature he knows as Father.

VERS. 3.-- *The Lord is as a man of war,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] Another reading: *The Lord shall destroy wars.* Amos IX: *The Lord is his name.* [var: Vulg. Am 9:6 *Dominus nomen ejus*, "The Lord is his name"; the wording printed is that of Jdt 16:3] He destroys not only visible battles, but those also which are ours against *principalities, and powers, and the rulers of these darknesses.--The Lord* indeed *is his name,* and there is no [0228A] creature of which he is not Lord. The chariots of Pharao and his army he cast into the sea: the chosen riders, the *ternistratores,* he drowned in the Red Sea. [var: Vulg. Ex 15:4 *currus Pharaonis et exercitum ejus projecit in mare: electi principes ejus submersi sunt in mari Rubro*, "his chosen princes are drowned in the Red Sea"] Pharao, as being more powerful in malice and holding the kingdom of wickedness, drives chariots: it is not enough for him to mount one horse, but he drives many at once, and compels them with a twisted lash. Those whom thou seest fouler in luxury, more savage in cruelty, more loathsome in avarice, know that they are of the chariots of Pharao, which he yokes to his car: on these he is borne and flies over the open fields of crimes, with loosened reins. There are other chosen riders, chosen, that is, unto malice.

[n: (ORIG.)] They are called *ternistratores* for this reason, that there is a threefold way of sinning for men. For they sin in deed, in word and in thought. Therefore they are called *ternistratores* [0228B] one by one, because they possess these three ways of sinning in us, and are ever on the watch and work by ambushes, that either the one may draw an evil word out of the wretched man, or the other wring out an iniquitous work, or the third snatch away a most wicked thought. Finally, where the seed of the word of God falls and perishes, a threefold place is described: by the wayside, among thorns, in stony ground. Contrariwise the good earth brings forth a threefold fruit, sixtyfold, hundredfold, thirtyfold [n: (Matth. XIII)]. For there is also a threefold way of doing well, since something good is done either by work, or by thought, or by word; whence the Apostle says in I Cor. III: *He who buildeth upon the foundation silver, gold, precious stones,* [var: Vulg. 1Cor 3:12 *Si quis autem superaedificat super fundamentum hoc, aurum, argentum, lapides pretiosos*, "gold, silver, precious stones"] indicating a threefold way of good things; and calling the way of evil things wood, hay, stubble. These *ternistratores* therefore are wicked angels, [0228C] who keep watch in these ways, that they may drive anyone into sin; whom the Lord will plunge into the Red Sea, and will deliver over to fiery waves in the day of judgment, and cover with a sea of punishments.

VERS. 5, 6.-- *They went down into the deep like a stone.* [n: (ORIG.)] Because, namely, such men were not stones out of which the children of Abraham might be raised up, but such as loved the deep and liquid element, that is, the bitter and flowing pleasure of things present. Whence it is said of them: *They were sunk like lead in most mighty water.* [var: Vulg. Ex 15:10 *submersi sunt quasi plumbum in aquis vehementibus*, "they were drowned like lead in the mighty waters"] Sinners are heavy, and iniquity is read to sit upon a talent of lead: hence the wicked are drowned in the deep like lead, but the saints walk upon the waters, because they are light, [0228D] and are not weighed down by the burden of sin. Christ walked upon the waters, who truly did no sin: Peter also walked, but doubted a little; for he had somewhat of lead, on account of which it is said to him in Matthew XIV: *O thou of little faith, why didst thou doubt?* All therefore are saved by fire, that if perchance anything of the kind of lead has been admitted, the fire may boil it away and dissolve it, so that all may be made the best gold, since the gold of that land is the best which the saints are to possess; and as the furnace tries gold, so tribulation tries just men. All therefore must come to the melting-pot. For God sits and melts, and purges the sons of Juda. If any man bring many good works and little of iniquity, that little is dissolved and purged as by fire, and the whole remains pure gold. [0229A] If any man bring more lead, more is burned away, that it may be the more boiled down; so that even if little, yet something of purified gold may remain; but he who comes wholly leaden is plunged *like lead in most mighty water.*

VERS. 7-10.-- *Thou hast sent forth thy wrath,* etc. [n: (AUG. quaest. 55 in Exod.)] Another reading: *Thou hast sent forth thy spirit and the sea covered them,* etc. Behold, now for the fifth time the Spirit of God is mentioned, etc., *as far as* Scripture only commanding, without the help of grace.

VERS. 11.-- *Who is like to thee,* etc. [n: (ORIG., ut supra. )] He does not compare God to the images of the nations, nor to the demons who falsely claim to themselves the name of gods, but he calls those gods who are so named by grace and by participation of God, according to that of Psalm LXXXI: [0229B] *I have said: You are gods.* And in the same place: *God hath stood in the assembly of gods.* But these, although they are capable of God, and seem to be endowed with this name by grace, yet none is like to him in power or in nature; and as to what is said, *we shall be like to him,* this likeness is referred not to nature but to grace, as a picture is like to him whose image is expressed in it as to grace, but far unlike as to substance. For that is the appearance of flesh, and the comeliness of a living body; this is a dye of colours, and wax laid upon boards that lack sense. None therefore among gods is like to the Lord, invisible, namely, incorporeal, unchangeable, without beginning and without end. None is the creator of all things save the Father, creating all things through the Son, and sanctifying all things in the Holy Spirit.

[0229C]VERS. 12, 13.-- *Thou hast stretched forth,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] That moves me which he says: *Thou hast stretched forth thy right hand and the earth swallowed them,* [var: Vulg. Ex 15:12 *Extendisti manum tuam, et devoravit eos terra*, "Thou hast stretched forth thy hand"] as though the cause of their being swallowed by the earth was that he stretched forth his right hand. If thou consider how the Lord, lifted up on the cross, all the day stretched out his hand to a people not believing but contradicting, and how death for iniquity crushed the unbelieving people that cried out: *Crucify, crucify him [n: (Joan. XIX)]*, thou wilt find how *he stretched forth his right hand, and the earth swallowed them.* Yet one must not altogether despair. For if he who has been swallowed come to himself, he can be vomited forth like Jonas. All of us the earth once swallowed and held in the inmost parts of hell: therefore Christ came down not only to the lands, but also to the lower parts of the earth: and there he found us swallowed up, and [0229D] sitting in the shadow of death; and leading us out thence, not only to the earth, lest we be swallowed again, but he prepares also the kingdom of heaven.

[n: (ORIG.)] The earth swallows up the wicked even today, who always think of earth, do earthly things, speak of earth, wrangle, desire earth, place their hope in it, look not up to heaven, think not of things to come, fear not the judgment of God, nor desire his promises; when thou seest such a one, say ye that the earth has swallowed him: and if thou see any man given over to lust and to the pleasures of the body, in whom the mind avails nothing but desire possesses the whole, say thou that the earth has swallowed him.
