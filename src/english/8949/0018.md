## CHAPTER XV. (cont.)

VERS. 14, 15.-- *They went up,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] The nations heard [0230A] and were angry, sorrows took hold of the inhabitants of Philisthiim, etc. [var: Vulg. Ex 15:14 *Ascenderunt populi, et irati sunt: dolores obtinuerunt habitatores Philisthiim*, "The peoples went up, and were angry: sorrows laid hold of the inhabitants of Philisthiim"] As to the history, it is certain that none of these nations was present at the wonders: how then were the Philisthiim, and the Moabites, and Edom, and the remaining nations which he enumerates, terrified with terror or angered? But, spiritually, thou wilt find that the Philisthiim, that is, *falling by the cup,* and Edom, namely, *the earthly one,* tremble: and their princes run to and fro and quake, bound fast with sorrows, when they see their kingdoms, which are in hell, penetrated by him who descended into the lower parts of the earth, that he might snatch away those who were possessed by death. Hence they fear and tremble, because they have felt the greatness of his arm; hence also the inhabitants of Chanaan pined away, that is, *the changeable* and *the moveable,* when they saw their kingdoms shaken, and [0230B] the strong one bound. Let fear then come upon them, and trembling: the demons tremble and fear the cross of Christ, in which they were triumphed over, and their principalities and power were stripped from them. Fear therefore and trembling shall fall upon them, when they shall see in us the sign of the cross faithfully fixed, and the greatness of the arm which the Lord spread out upon the cross, as Isaias LXV says: *All the day I have spread forth my hands to a people that believeth not,* etc. [var: Vulg. Is 65:2 *Expandi manus meas tota die ad populum incredulum*, "I have spread forth my hands all the day to an unbelieving people"; the wording printed is that of Rom 10:21] They will not fear thee otherwise, nor will the trembling of thee rush upon them, unless they see in thee the cross of Christ, so that thou mayest say: *But God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ [n: (Gal. VI)]*.

VERS. 15, 16.-- *Let them become unmoveable as a stone,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] To be made as a stone is not to be a stone by nature. For that would not be made which was not. This [0230C] we have said on account of those who say that Pharao or the Egyptians were of an evil nature, and were not brought into this from the freedom of choice; and who accuse God the Creator as cruel, as one who turns men into stones: he did not say, Let them become as stones, and then keep silence, but he appointed a time and a measure of the domination, namely, *until thy people pass over.* The former people, which was before us, was made as a hard and unbelieving stone, not that the nature of a stone should remain, but until the people pass over which the Lord has purchased. Whence Rom. XI: *For blindness in part hath happened in Israel, until the fulness of the Gentiles should come in, and then Israel should be saved;* [var: Vulg. Rom 11:25-26 *quia caecitas ex parte contigit in Israel, donec plenitudo gentium intraret, et sic omnis Israel salvus fieret*, "and so all Israel should be saved"] which by unbelief was made as a stone, whence Matthew III: *For God is able of these stones to raise up children to Abraham.* But [0230D] since he is the creator of all things, how is he said to have purchased what there is no doubt is his own? For it is said in the canticle of Deuteronomy: *Is not this the same Lord thy God, who made and created thee, and purchased thee? [n: (Deut. XXXII.)]* [var: Vulg. Dt 32:6 *numquid non ipse est pater tuus, qui possedit te, et fecit, et creavit te?*, "Is not he thy father, that hath possessed thee, and made thee, and created thee?"] For each man seems to acquire that which was not his own. Whence the heretics say of the Saviour that those whom he purchased were not his: for at a price given he bought men whom the Creator had made, and they say it is certain that everyone buys what is not his own. For the Apostle says in I Cor. VII: *You are bought with a price.* But hear the Prophet: *For your sins you are sold, and for your iniquities have I put away your mother [n: (Isa. L)]*. [var: Vulg. Is 50:1 *Ecce in iniquitatibus vestris venditi estis, et in sceleribus vestris dimisi matrem vestram*, "Behold, for your iniquities you are sold, and for your wickednesses have I put away your mother"] We are therefore all creatures of God, but each is sold by his own sins, [0231A] and departs from the Creator. We are God's, therefore, according to that we were created by him: but made servants of the devil, according to that we are sold by our own sins. But Christ coming redeemed us who served the devil, to whom we had sold ourselves by sinning; and thus he seems both to have received back his own, whom he created, and to have purchased strangers, who by sinning had acquired a strange master. But he is rightly said to have redeemed us, who gave his own blood for our price. But what did the devil give, that he should buy us? Manslaying is the devil's money, for he is a murderer from the beginning: thou hast done a manslaying, thou hast received the devil's money. The same is to be said of adultery, sacrilege, theft, false witness and the like: for with this money he buys and makes his own servants all who have received anything, however much, of this [0231B] revenue of his.

VERS. 17.-- *Thou shalt bring them in and plant them,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] The Lord will not have us planted in Egypt, nor in low and lowly places, but in the mountain of his inheritance. Of this planting the Prophet says in Psalm LXXIX: *Thou hast brought a vineyard out of Egypt, thou hast cast out the nations and planted it: thou wast the guide of the way in its sight, and thou didst plant its roots, and it filled the earth. Its shadow covered the mountains, and its branches the cedars of God.* [var: Vulg. Ps 79:10 *plantasti radices ejus, et implevit terram*, "thou didst plant its roots, and it filled the earth"; Migne prints *replevit*] Dost thou mark how God plants, and where? He plants not in valleys, but on mountains, in high and lofty places. For those whom he leads out of Egypt, that is, out of the world unto faith, he will not have set again in lowly places, but that their conversation be lofty, and that they dwell on mountains and on the heights, not creep along [0231C] the ground. He will not have his vineyard bear its fruits any longer cast down in a low place, but that the shoots be led upward, be set on high, become layered vines: and layered not upon lowly trees, but upon lofty and most high cedars of God, namely the prophets and apostles: to whom if we be joined, and the vine which God brought out of Egypt, and our shoots be spread through their branches, and being entwined with them we be made layers, bound one to another by the bonds of charity, we shall bring forth very much fruit. *For every tree that maketh not good fruit shall be cut down and cast into the fire [n: (Matth. III)]*.

*Thy most firm dwelling place,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] In the prepared habitation which thou hast prepared, O Lord. See the goodness of the merciful Lord: he will not bring thee to labour, he will not that thou thyself make thee a dwelling place; [0231D] he will now bring thee into a habitation already prepared. Whence John IV: *Others have laboured, and you have entered into their labours.*

*Thy sanctuary,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] *Thy holy place, O Lord,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ex 15:17 *sanctuarium tuum, Domine*, "thy sanctuary, O Lord"] The tabernacle of God, or the temple, is called a holy place, because it sanctifies those who approach. In Hebrews IX this is said to be made not by the hand of men, but by the hand of God: for thy sake therefore God both plants and builds; he is made a husbandman and a builder, that nothing be wanting to thee. Whence the Apostle in I Cor. III: *You are God's husbandry, God's building.* What then is this holy place prepared not by the hand of men, but of God. Prov. IX: *Wisdom hath built herself a house,* that is, the flesh of Christ, not by the hand of man, that is, not [0232A] by human work: the temple of the flesh is built in the Virgin, but the stone cut without hands grew, and became a great mountain [n: (Dan. II)]: this is the holy place of the flesh assumed, and cut both without hands and without the work of men out of the mountain of human nature and the substance of flesh.

VERS. 18.-- *The Lord shall reign,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] *O Lord, who reignest unto the age and unto the age and yet further.* [var: Vulg. Ex 15:18 *Dominus regnabit in aeternum et ultra*, "The Lord shall reign for ever and beyond"] As often as it is said *unto the age,* a length of time indeed is signified, but that there is some end; and if it is said unto another age, something longer is signified, but still an end is set: and as often as ages of ages are named, perhaps some term is signified which, though unknown to us, is yet appointed by God. But that he has here added *and yet further,* leaves no sense of any term [0232B] or of an end: for whatsoever thou hast thought, in which thou supposest the end to stand, the prophetic word always says to thee, *and yet further;* as if it should say to thee, Thou thinkest the Lord will reign unto the age of the age, and yet further thou thinkest unto the ages of ages: and yet further, and whatsoever thou shalt have said of the spaces of his kingdom, the prophet always says to thee, *and yet further.*

VERS. 19.-- *But the children of Israel,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] And thou, if *thou art a son of Israel, thou canst walk on dry ground in the midst of the sea.* If thou shalt be in the midst of a depraved and perverse nation, going as the light of the sun in the midst of sinners, the moisture of sin shall not soak thee, and as thou passest through this world no wave of lust shall bespatter thee, no heat of covetousness shall lash thee. He who is Egypt, and follows Pharao, is drowned in the waves of vices. [0232C] But he who follows Christ, and walks as he walked, to him the water becomes a wall on the right hand and on the left. He himself goes forward by the middle way, over the dry ground he turns aside neither to the right hand nor to the left, until he come out to liberty, and sing a hymn of victory to the Lord, saying: *Let us sing to the Lord,* etc.

VERS. 20, 21.-- *A timbrel.* [n: (STRAB.)] According to Jerome it is a kind of trumpet having at the top a reed or a pipe; through which it renders a sounding voice. The choir, according to the same, is a simple skin with two hemlock pipes; through the one it is blown into, through the other it renders a sound: by both therefore is signified the narrow understanding of the old law. For how brief and how small it is, is shown from this, that it is carried by the hand of a woman. According to Cassiodorus a timbrel is a skin stretched [0232D] between two pieces of wood: by which the maceration of the flesh is signified. But a choir is a multitude singing together, and it is called *chorus* as it were the song of those of one age. Because therefore in a choir all the voices sound equally, by it charity is signified, whereby we are all one in Christ. Whosoever therefore renders praises to God in jubilation, let him do this in the choir, that is, in charity, and in the timbrel, that is, in the mortification of the flesh. Whence Psalm CL: *Praise ye him with timbrel and choir,* etc.

VERS. 22.-- *And Moses took Israel,* etc. The baptized are led through the world as through a desert after the passage of the Red Sea, not yet having the promised country, but hoping and awaiting it through patience, [0233A] while they are wearied by laborious and perilous temptation. But lest they turn back in heart into Egypt, neither there does Christ forsake them: for that pillar too does not withdraw.
