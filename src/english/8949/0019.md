## CHAPTER XV. (cont.)

VERS. 23, 24.-- *And they came,* etc. [n: (ORIG., hom. 6 in Exod.)] After the passage of the Red Sea, after the choirs and the timbrels, after the triumphal hymns, one comes to Mara, that is, to *water* that is *bitter,* which the people could not drink; one comes to the peril of thirst. But Moses cried to the Lord, and he showed him a wood, by which the water was made sweet, and there God set for him justices and judgment; where, namely, there was bitterness, where there was thirst, and, what is heavier, in an abundance of waters, there was no other place more worthy and more fitting. Besides, as to what he says, *God showed him a wood,* and he cast it into the water, and it was made sweet, as though God could not [0233B] make the water sweet without a wood, or as though Moses did not know the wood. But if the law be taken according to the letter, it is bitter, and is itself Mara. For what is so bitter as that a child should receive the wound of circumcision on the eighth day, and that tender infancy should suffer the hardness of the iron? For the cup of the law is bitter to the people of God, not the people that was baptized in Moses in the cloud and in the sea, but that which in spirit and in water cannot drink and taste it, nor bear the bitterness of the victims, nor the observance of the sabbath. But if God show a wood to be cast into this bitterness, the water of the law becomes sweet and drinkable. Prov. III: *Wisdom is a tree of life to all that embrace her.* [var: Vulg. Pr 3:18 *Lignum vitae est his qui apprehenderint eam*, "She is a tree of life to them that lay hold on her"] If therefore the wood of the wisdom of Christ shall have been cast into the law, showing how circumcision is to be understood, [0233C] and the sabbath, and the law of leprosy and the like, the bitterness of the law is turned into the sweetness of spiritual understanding, so that the people can drink which has left the idols and taken refuge with God. If it hear the law teaching about sacrifices, it at once shrinks back and cannot drink. Into this bitterness therefore, that is, into the letter of the law, God set justices and testimonies, as it were, namely, into a certain little vessel he shuts up [condidit] the treasures of his wisdom and knowledge. Whence: *We have this treasure in earthen vessels [n: (II Cor. III)]*. That this water therefore may be drunk, God shows a wood to be cast into it, that he who drinks may not die, may not feel the bitterness. If therefore any man drink without the wood of life, that is, without the mystery of the cross, without the faith of Christ, without spiritual understanding, he shall die of the bitterness. Whence: *The letter killeth,* [0233D] that is, the water of Mara, if it be drunk unchanged and not turned into sweetness.

VERS. 25-27.-- *Who showed him,* etc. [n: (AUG., Quaest. 57 in Exod.)] There was a kind of wood that had this power: or could God have done it with any wood whatever, who was doing such great wonders? Yet what is said seems to signify, *he showed him,* as though it were already such; unless perhaps it was a place where no wood at all was to be found, so that this very thing was of the divine help, that the Lord showed him a wood where there was none, and by the wood made the waters sweet, prefiguring the glory and the grace of the cross. But even in such a wood who is to be praised save the Creator and the Shower of it.

*There he appointed for him precepts. [n: (ORIG., hom. 7 in Exod.)]* [0234A] Why did he add: After *God set for him justices and judgments, there he tempted him, saying: If thou wilt hear the voice of the Lord thy God.* [var: Vulg. Ex 15:25 *ibi constituit ei praecepta, atque judicia, et ibi tentavit eum*, "there he appointed for him precepts and judgments, and there he tempted him"] He seems to expound why he gave justifications and judgments, namely that he might tempt them whether they would hear the voice of the Lord God, and keep his commandments. For as to the former people, what good or perfect thing could be commanded to them who murmured and gainsaid, who a little after, forgetting the wonders of God, turn to idols? Precepts therefore are given in which they may be tempted; whence: *I gave you precepts and justifications that are not good, in which they shall not live [n: (Ezech. XX)]*. [var: Vulg. Ez 20:25 *Ergo et ego dedi eis praecepta non bona, et judicia in quibus non vivent*, "Therefore I also gave them precepts that were not good, and judgments in which they shall not live"] For being tempted in the precepts they are not found faithful, and therefore it was found that what was unto life was unto death for them. For the same thing, if it be kept, begets life; if it be not kept, death. To those therefore who keep them not [0234B] the commandments are called not good, and such as they shall not live in. But with the wood of the cross mingled in, being turned into sweetness and spiritually understood, the commandments of life have also been kept. *All the languor which I laid upon Egypt I will not bring upon thee.* He seems to say that if a man keep the commandments he shall suffer no sickness, that is, neither fevers, nor other bodily pains. But Job, altogether most just and full of all piety, is filled from the head to the feet with the worst ulcer. But the just do not have those infirmities which the Egyptians have. Egypt is the world: to love the world therefore and the things that are in the world is the Egyptian languor; to observe months, seasons, to cling to the courses of the stars, to serve the flesh, to give labour to luxury and to be at leisure for delights, is the Egyptian languor.

[0234C] *And they came,* etc. [n: (ORIG., ibid.)] The people is not led into Elim before, where there were twelve fountains of waters, in which there was nothing of bitterness, but very great pleasantness from the thickness of the palm trees: but first it was led to the salt and bitter waters, which being sweetened by the wood shown by the Lord, one comes to the fountains. If we follow the history alone, it does not much edify us to know to what place they came first, to what second. But first the people is led to the letter of the law, from which, while it remains in its bitterness, it cannot withdraw. When therefore it has been sweetened by the wood of life, and spiritually understood, it passes from the Old Testament to the New, and to the twelve apostolic fountains, where are found seventy trees of palms. For not the twelve apostles alone [0234D] preached the faith of Christ, but seventy others also, through whom the world knew the palms of Christ's victory. It is not enough therefore for the people of God to drink the water of Mara, although sweetened by the wood of life; the Old Testament alone does not suffice for drinking, for one must come to the New, of which one drinks without scruple and without difficulty. The Jews also are even now at Mara, that is, at the bitter waters: for God has not yet shown them the wood by which they grow sweet, because *unless they shall have believed, they shall not understand [n: (Isa. VII)]*. [var: Vulg. Is 7:9 *Si non credideritis, non permanebitis*, "If you will not believe, you shall not continue"]
