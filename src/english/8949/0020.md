## CHAPTER XVI.

VERS. 2.-- *And all the congregation of Israel murmured,* etc. [n: (ORIG., homil. 7 in Exod.)] O ungrateful [0235A] people! it desires Egypt, it that saw the Egyptians destroyed; and the flesh of Egypt, it that sees the flesh of the Egyptians given both to the fishes and to the birds! It murmurs against Moses, nay rather against God; but the first time pardon is granted, and the second, and perhaps the third: but if they do not cease, but persist, hear what follows, it is related in the book of Numbers [n: (Num. XXI)], and the same Apostle rehearses it, saying [n: (I Cor. X)]: *Neither do you murmur, as some of them murmured, and perished by the serpents. These things were written on account of us, upon whom the ends of the world are come.* [var: Vulg. 1Cor 10:10 has *et perierunt ab exterminatore*, "and perished by the destroyer"; the serpents belong to v. 9, and v. 11 opens *scripta sunt autem ad correptionem nostram*] But if we do not cease to murmur and complain against God, we shall incur a like peril of offence, or a like example of it. For when we lay the blame upon the inclemency of the heavens, the unfruitfulness of the crops, the rarity of showers, and upon the prosperity of some and the unprosperity of others, [0235B] we murmur against God. But these things are pardoned at first, and are severely avenged upon those who persist, because serpents shall be sent upon them, that is, they shall be handed over to unclean and poisonous spirits, which by hidden bites feed upon the inner chambers of the heart.

[n: (ORIG.)] For the correction of those who read, the sin of the people is set down: that it murmured and was ungrateful for the divine benefits, when it had received the heavenly manna. In the second month, on the fifteenth day of the month, which is not written without reason. Recall the laws of the pasch which were spoken [n: (Num. IX)], and thou wilt find there that this is the time which is appointed for the keeping of a second pasch by those who have been unclean in soul, or taken up with business abroad. Those who had not been unclean in soul, or were not journeying far off, [0235C] kept the pasch on the fourteenth day of the first month; but those far off on pilgrimage, and the unclean, keep the second pasch at this time, in which the manna came down from heaven. On the day on which the first pasch is kept, the manna does not come down, but on that on which the second is kept: the first pasch is that of the Jewish people; the second is ours. For we were unclean in soul, we who *worshipped idols; and we were pilgrims, and far off, strangers and pilgrims from the testaments of God,* etc. [n: (Eph. II.)] But manna is given from heaven, not on the day on which the first pasch is kept, but on that on which the second is: *For the bread which came down from heaven [n: (Joan. VI)]* did not come to those who were celebrating the first solemnity, but to us, who have received the second. *For Christ our pasch is sacrificed, who as true man came down as bread from heaven [n: (I Cor. V.)]* Let us see [0235D] meanwhile what is shown to have been done on this day: On the fourteenth day of the second month the people murmured, etc.

VERS. 3.-- *We sat over the fleshpots,* etc. They had flocks of sheep and herds of oxen, of which they could eat; but they burned with desire for the fish and the fowl in which Egypt abounds.

VERS. 4.-- *Behold I will rain,* etc. [n: (ORIG., homil. 8 in Exod.)] I should wish first to have a discourse upon this scripture with the Jews, to whom the oracles of God were entrusted, as to what seems to them of that which it says: *For six days together you shall gather, but on the sixth day double.* It appears therefore that that day is named the sixth which is set before the sabbath; but the sabbath is the seventh day. [0236A] I ask therefore on what day the manna began to be given? and I wish to compare our Lord's day with the sabbath of the Jews. For from Scripture it appears that on the Lord's day the manna is first given, gathered for six days together; on the seventh, which is the sabbath, there is a ceasing. Since therefore on the Lord's day God rained manna, and not on the sabbath, let the Jews understand that even then our Lord's day was preferred to their sabbath; and it was signified that on that day the grace of God did not come down from heaven to them, and that the heavenly bread, which is the word of God, did not reach them; whence Osee III: *For many days the children of Israel shall sit without king, without prince, without victim, without sacrifice, without priest.* [var: Vulg. Os 3:4 names instead *sine altari, et sine ephod, et sine theraphim*, "without altar, and without ephod, and without theraphim," and has no victim and no priest] But on our Lord's day the Lord always rains manna, that is, the heavenly words which are joyful to us. Those unhappy men sigh even [0236B] after manna as their fathers did, and do not deserve to receive it; for they cannot eat what is small like coriander seed, and white like hoar frost. For they perceive nothing in the word of God that is small, subtle and spiritual, but all of it fat and gross. *For the heart of this people is grown gross.* The interpretation also of this name sounds thus. For manna is interpreted *What is this?* The very force of the name invites to learning, so that when thou hearest the law of God recited, thou mayest always inquire and say to the teachers, *What is this?* Thou therefore, if thou wilt eat manna, and receive the word of God, know that it is small and very subtle, like coriander seed: for it has somewhat of the herb whereby it can nourish and refresh the weak. *For he that is weak eateth herbs [n: (Rom. XIV)]*. For it has also somewhat of sharpness, and therefore is as hoar frost; [0236C] but of whiteness and sweetness very much. What is whiter, more splendid, than divine learning? what sweeter, more delightful, than the words of God, which are above honey and the honeycomb?

*Loaves.* The manna, namely: it is a manner of speech by which bread is put for food; and Scripture loves rather to say *loaves* in the plural than *bread* in the singular.

*That I may prove.* This proving is a testing, not a seducing to sin; nor are they proved for this reason, that God did not know: but he shows them to themselves, that they may become the more humble in asking help and in acknowledging the grace of God.

VERS. 5.-- *But on the sixth day,* etc. [n: (ORIG., hom. 7.)] On the sixth day let double be gathered into store, as much as may suffice also for the sabbath. The sixth day is this [0236D] life in which we now are, whence in six days God made this world; on this day therefore we ought to lay up as much as will suffice in the world to come. For if thou acquire here anything of good work, if thou lay up to thy credit anything of justice, of mercy and of piety, this will be food for thee in the world to come. For he who acquires here ten talents [n: (Luc. XIX)], there shall receive ten cities, and *what a man shall sow, that also shall he reap [n: (Gal. VI)]*. The rich of this world lay up those things which are corrupted in this world, nay together with this world: but goods laid up remain until the morrow. And it is written. *They who were unfaithful kept of the manna, and worms boiled forth out of it and it putrefied;* but what was laid up for the day of the sabbath remained whole. And thou [0237A] therefore, if for the sake of the present life and the love of the world thou lay up treasure, straightway worms boil forth, of which it is said in Isaias LXVI: *Their worm dieth not;* [var: Vulg. Is 66:24 has the future *non morietur*, "shall not die"; the present is the form of Mc 9:43-47] these are begotten by avarice and the blind greed of riches in those who have money, and who, seeing their brethren in need, shut up their bowels from them. Whence the Apostle [n: (I Tim. VI)] charges *the rich not to be highminded, nor to trust in the uncertainty of riches,* etc.; nor do worms come to be in us from any other source than from the manna, that is, the word of God. Whence John XV: *If I had not come and spoken to them,* etc. After the word of God has been received, therefore, if anyone sins, that very word is made a worm, which ever digs into his conscience and gnaws the secret places of his breast.

VERS. 6.--[n: (ORIG.)] *In the evening you shall know,* etc. *Toward evening [0237B] you shall know that I am the Lord; and in the morning you shall see the majesty of the Lord.* Let the Jews answer me how the Lord is known toward evening, and his majesty seen in the morning? Where was he acknowledged toward evening, and his majesty seen in the morning? But this is not understood save in the Gospel [n: (Matth. XXVIII)], in which on the evening of the sabbath, which *dawneth on the first day of the week, Mary Magdalen came and the other Mary to the sepulchre, and they found the stone rolled back from the monument, and an earthquake made,* and the monuments opened, and the centurion and the guards of the sepulchre saying: *Indeed this was the Son of God.* The Lord was acknowledged in this evening, because he himself was the Lord: he was acknowledged in the power of the resurrection, and in the morning his glory was seen. When the other women had come on the first day of the week very early in the morning, they found angels [0237C] sitting at the sepulchre with great brightness, and saying: *He is not here, but is risen from the dead. Come and see the place,* etc. [n: (Luc. XXIV.)] His majesty therefore was seen in the morning, because the resurrection also was announced by angels.

VERS. 7-11.-- *But what are we,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 59 in Exod., tom. III.)] They wished that they availed not so much as God, etc., *as far as* he showed the Holy Spirit to be God by adding, Acts V: *Thou hast not lied to men, but to God.*

VERS. 12-13.-- *In the evening you shall eat flesh* [n: (ORIG., ut surpra. )] Morally we can understand that for each one of us there is a morning and a beginning of the day, the time in which we are first enlightened and kindled to the light of faith. In this time we cannot eat [0237D] the flesh of the word, that is, we are not yet capable of perfect doctrine; but after long exercises, after very much progress, when we are already near to evening, that is, to the faith of perfection, then we are made capable of more solid food. Let us hasten therefore to receive the manna, which yields in the mouth what savour each one wills. Whence Matthew IX: *Be it done to thee according to thy faith.* [var: Vulg. Mt 9:29 has the plural *secundum fidem vestram fiat vobis*, "according to your faith be it done unto you"] And thou therefore, if thou receive with all faith and devotion the word of God which is preached in the Church, be it done to thee whatsoever thou desirest. If thou art in tribulation, it consoles thee, saying: *A contrite and humbled heart God spurneth not [n: (Psal. L)]* [var: Vulg. Ps 50:19 has *non despicies*, "thou wilt not despise," in the second person addressed to God]; if thou rejoicest for the hope to come, it heaps up joys for thee, saying: *Be glad in the Lord, and rejoice, ye just [n: (Psal. XXXI)]*; if thou art wrathful, it says: *Cease from anger, and leave [0238A] indignation [n: (Psal. XXXVI)]* [var: Vulg. Ps 36:8 has *derelinque furorem*, "leave wrath"]; if thou art in griefs, it heals thee, saying: *The Lord healeth all thy diseases [n: (Matth. IV)]*; if thou art consumed by poverty, it says in consolation: *The Lord lifteth the needy from the earth, and raiseth up the poor out of the dunghill [n: (Psal. CXII)]* [var: Vulg. Ps 112:7 has the participles *suscitans a terra inopem, et de stercore erigens pauperem*, "raising up the needy from the earth, and lifting the poor out of the dunghill"]. Yet if anyone receives it unfaithfully, and does not eat it, but hides it, worms boil forth out of it. Nor be thou troubled if the word of God is brought to this, that it is thought to be made a worm; for he himself says: *I am a worm, and no man [n: (Psal. XXI)]*. For as he himself is the one who becomes to some a ruin, but to others a resurrection [n: (Luc. II)]; so also is he the one who in the manna is made to the faithful the sweetness of honey, to the unfaithful a worm. For he is the word of God which convicts the minds of the wicked and pierces the conscience with the darts of correction. For he is the one who is made fire to those to whom he opens the Scriptures [0238B], who say: *Was not our heart burning within us?* etc. [n: (Luc. XXIV.)] To others he is fire, in whom he burns up the thorns of the evil earth, that is, consumes evil thoughts in the heart; and therefore for sinners neither does the convicting worm ever die, nor is the burning fire quenched: but for the just and the faithful he remains sweet and pleasant. Whence it is said in psalm XXXIII: *Taste and see,* etc.
