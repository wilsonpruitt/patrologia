## CHAPTER XVI. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] Another reading: *In the evening you shall eat flesh, and in the morning you shall be filled with loaves.* ⟦s2⟧ Let the Jews say what consequence there is, that in the evening they should eat flesh without loaves, or in the morning loaves without meat? what of the divine gift, what of the tempering of heavenly grace, is shown in this? ⟦s3⟧ Is this that wherein they can acknowledge God, who eat flesh in the evening without loaves, [0238C] and do they say that the majesty of God appears, if again loaves are eaten without flesh? let them keep such things to themselves, for those who, acquiescing in you, think that God is acknowledged in quails; but we, for whom at the end, toward the evening of the world, the Word was made flesh, say that God is acknowledged in that flesh only which he took from the Virgin: for this flesh of the Word of God no one ate either in the morning or at noon, but toward evening: for the coming of the Lord in the flesh was made toward evening, as John also says: *Children, it is the last hour [n: (I Joan. II)]* [var: Vulg. 1Jo 2:18 has *Filioli*, "Little children"] .-- *In the morning,* he says, *you shall be filled with loaves.* ⟦s4⟧ To us therefore the bread is the Word of God: *He is the living bread which came down from heaven, and giveth life to the world [n: (Joan. VI)]*. ⟦s5⟧ But as to its saying that this bread is given in the morning, whereas he was incarnate toward evening, it is so to be understood [0238D] that the Lord came toward the evening of the world and near its end; but by his coming, since he is the sun of justice, he restored a new morning to believers; a new light, namely, of knowledge, in which they are filled with loaves who have received his precepts. ⟦s6⟧ Nor wonder that the word is called both the flesh of God, and bread, and is called milk and herbs, and is named diversely according to the measure of believers and the capacity of those who take it. ⟦s7⟧ Or, after the morning made by the resurrection, he filled believers with loaves, because he granted the books of the law and the prophets, before unknown, for the instruction of the Church, and he himself is made bread in the Gospel, while the other books of the law and the prophets or of the histories may be called many loaves, with which believers from among the Gentiles are filled. ⟦s8⟧ Whence: *They shall go up into the mountain, and [0239A] shall drink wine, they shall be anointed with ointment.* ⟦s9⟧ Fittingly therefore do we receive flesh toward evening and are filled with loaves in the morning: because neither could we eat flesh in the morning, for the time was not yet; nor at noon, for scarcely do the angels eat flesh at noon, and to that order perhaps the noonday time is granted.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And in the morning.* ⟦s2⟧ On the ninth day, when Christ, the sun of justice, by his coming restored to believers the things that were lost.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID.)] By the food of birds is figured the preaching divinely sent, which runs through sounding words, as winged things through the air, on which they are fed by faith who set out for the heavenly fatherland. ⟦s2⟧ Or the utterances of the old law, which fed the carnal people as with flesh through words divinely sent, as through flying things. ⟦s3⟧ Whence also it is given in the evening, because [0239B] all things granted to them carnally were to have an end. ⟦s4⟧ But in the manifest light of faith the manna is given, which is Christ, namely the *living bread which came down from heaven [n: (Joan. VI)]*, who is rained through the evangelical clouds upon the whole world: not now upon the murmuring and tempted Synagogue, but upon the believing and hoping Church.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *In the likeness of hoar frost upon the earth.* ⟦s2⟧ The hoar frost signifies Christ: for the manna was first given on the Lord's day, and Christ rose again on the Lord's day.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Pounded.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, most clean. ⟦s3⟧ For as wheat, or millet, or some other such thing is bruised in a little mortar with a pestle and stripped of its husks, so the body of Christ was stripped of mortality through the tribulation of the passion and the pestle of the cross. ⟦s4⟧ Or the small manna is the subtlety of heavenly [0239C] doctrine, which is pounded with the pestle that the literal sense may be stripped away.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Man hu. [n: (GREG., lib. XXVII Moral., cap. 7.)]* What is designated by the manna given from above to the children of Israel, save the wonder of the heavenly sweetness? ⟦s2⟧ For sweet is the food which is received from on high; it is called *man hu*, which is interpreted *What is this?* which we say when we behold what we wonder at not knowing. ⟦s3⟧ The soul therefore perceives the heavenly manna, when, lifted up by the voice of compunction, it is astonished at the new appearance of the eternal refreshment, so that, filled with the divine sweetness, it may rightly answer *What is this?* because while it is held suspended away from the lowest thought, it beholds those things which are from on high and wonders at them as at things unwonted. ⟦s4⟧ And when by this voice the deafness of our torpor is broken, the practice of the old life is [0239D] changed, so that the soul, breathed upon by the heavenly Spirit, both desires in the highest things what it had despised, and despises in the lowest things what it had desired.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ VERS. 16, 17.-- *According to the number of your souls,* etc. [n: (AUG., locutio 78.)] Often Scripture calls men souls, the whole from a part: but not readily fleshes for men, but flesh; as in psalm LV: *I will not fear what flesh can do;* which it says more openly in the same psalm, *what man can do to me.* [var: Vulg. Ps 55:5 has *non timebo quid faciat mihi caro*, "what flesh can do to me," keeping the *mihi* which the second quotation retains] But it would not say, according to the number of your fleshes, as it said, *according to the number of your souls.*

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18, 19.-- *According to what they were able to eat,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because in the present life everyone ought to learn as much as [0240A] he can, lest he leave anything unexamined, lest he begin to learn when he cannot.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *But some of them left of it until the morning,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The beginning of the other life, in which it will not be permitted to give leisure to doctrine, but the manna will putrefy, and swarm with worms, that is, it will beget for them eternal punishment.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *And when the sun grew hot, it melted.* ⟦s2⟧ Because then all understanding of Scripture will lie open, since *now we see through a glass in a dark manner, but then we shall see face to face [n: (I Cor. XIII)]*.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Double food.* ⟦s2⟧ The things necessary, namely, for soul and for body. ⟦s3⟧ For in the seventh, which is understood as the age of the dead, there will, namely, be no place for working.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23-32.-- *The rest of the sabbath,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ By hearing the word of God and doing it, or by living well and showing mercy [0240B] to others. ⟦s3⟧ What was laid up for the sabbath was not corrupted, because good things done for the rest to come remain whole in the life to come. ⟦s4⟧ Otherwise worms boiled forth and what was kept putrefied: for those who lay up treasure for the sake of the present life and the love of the world, for them boils forth the worm that shall not die.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33, 34.-- *And lay it up before,* etc. [n: (AUG. quaest. 61 in Exod.)] It can be asked where Aaron was to lay it up before the Lord, when there was neither any tabernacle, nor had the ark of the testament yet been set up. ⟦s2⟧ Or was it perhaps of the future, and did he therefore say *lay up,* that it might be understood that it could then be laid up before the Lord, when the ark was to be? ⟦s3⟧ Or rather was it said 'before the Lord' of that which is done by the very devotion of him who offers, in whatsoever place [0240C] it were placed? ⟦s4⟧ For where is God not? ⟦s5⟧ But what he adds: *And Aaron laid it up before the tabernacle to be kept,* rather asserts the former sense. ⟦s6⟧ For in this manner Scripture spoke by prolepsis of what was done afterwards, when the tabernacle of the testimony began to be.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35, 36.-- *And the children of Israel ate manna forty years,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *(AUG., ibid., quaest. 62.* ⟦s3⟧ Another reading: *And the children of Israel did eat manna forty years,* etc., *as far as* for God knew what they desired and from what kind of flesh he might satisfy them.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They ate manna forty years.* ⟦s2⟧ The body of Christ, namely the Word of God, which we now perceive in a dark manner, but then face to face [n: (I Cor. 13)].

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Until they came into a habitable land,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 63 in Exod., tom. III.)] Another reading: *Until [0240D] they came into the region of Phoenice.* ⟦s2⟧ By repetition he sets forth what land he said was inhabited, etc., *as far as* for antiquity changed the names of many lands, places, rivers and cities for certain causes.
