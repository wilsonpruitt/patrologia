## CHAPTER XVII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1, 2.-- *All the multitude of the children of Israel therefore setting forward,* etc. [n: (ORIG., hom. 11 in Exod.)] All who wish to live piously in Christ suffer persecution, and are assailed by enemies: and he who walks the road of this life ought always to be ready and to stand in the camp. ⟦s2⟧ Whence all the synagogue of the children of Israel set forward from the desert of Sin according to their camps by the word of God. ⟦s3⟧ There is indeed one Synagogue of the Lord. but it is divided into four camps, which are described in the book of Numbers as placed [0241A] round about the tabernacle of the Lord. ⟦s4⟧ Do thou therefore, armed and serving in the camp of the Lord, not entangle thyself in secular business, and keep watch always in the camp of the Lord, that it may be said of thee that thou hast set forward by the word of the Lord from the desert of Sin, and comest into Raphidim. ⟦s5⟧ Now Sin is interpreted *temptation*; Raphidim, *soundness of judgment:* he who comes well out of temptation comes to soundness of judgment. ⟦s6⟧ For in the day of judgment he will be sound. ⟦s7⟧ Whence Apocalypse II: *To him that overcometh, I will give of the tree of life, which is in the paradise of my God.* ⟦s8⟧ He therefore comes to soundness of judgment who orders his words well in judgment.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Sin.* ⟦s2⟧ Here Sin is taken specially for one station out of those five which were from the Red Sea as far as mount Sina, which Josephus describes by name. ⟦s3⟧ For in general that whole desert is called Sin, [0241B] as also Raphidim is.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They encamped in Raphidim. [n: (HIERON. de Loc. Hebr., tom. III.)]* It is a place in the desert beside mount Horeb. ⟦s2⟧ And it is to be known that the whole wilderness as far as mount Sinai is called Sin, and that from the whole province the place of a single station also took its name, as Moab is the name both of a city and of a province. ⟦s3⟧ In this solitude there are five stations, Zuph, and the desert of Sin, Alus, Depheca, Raphidim. ⟦s4⟧ Whence it is well said that *all the multitude of the children of Israel set forward from the desert of Sin by their stations according to the word of the Lord, and they came into Raphidim.* ⟦s5⟧ Whence it is plain that many stations are denoted by the name of one region.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3, 4.-- *The people therefore thirsted there,* etc. [n: (ORIG., ubi supra )]. ⟦s2⟧ Another reading: *The people,* he says, *thirsted for water, [0241C] and murmured against Moses.* ⟦s3⟧ It was enough to say that *he thirsted,* what need was there to add *water?* ⟦s4⟧ The addition is not superfluous. ⟦s5⟧ There are therefore diverse thirsts, and each man has his own. ⟦s6⟧ They who are good hunger and thirst after justice [n: (Matth. V)]. ⟦s7⟧ Others too say: *My soul hath thirsted after thee, O God [n: (Psal. XLI)]* [var: Vulg. Ps 41:3 has *sitivit anima mea ad Deum fortem vivum*, "my soul hath thirsted after the strong living God"]. ⟦s8⟧ But they who are sinners suffer not the thirst of water, not the hunger of bread, but of hearing the word of God. ⟦s9⟧ The people therefore thirsted for water, which ought to have thirsted for justice. ⟦s10⟧ But God, the instructor of the simple, the master of the unwise, corrects faults and amends errors, saying to Moses that he take the rod, and strike the rock, and bring forth waters. ⟦s11⟧ For he wills them now to drink of the rock and to make progress, and to come to the inner mysteries. ⟦s12⟧ For they murmured against Moses, and therefore the Lord commands that he show them the rock [0241D] whereof they may drink. ⟦s13⟧ For if there is anyone who, reading Moses, murmurs against him, and the law according to the letter displeases him, because in many things it seems not to hold together, Moses shows him the rock, which is Christ, that from it he may drink and repair his thirst. ⟦s14+15⟧ But this rock does not give waters unless it is struck, and when struck it brings forth fountains: for Christ, being struck and slain upon the cross, brought forth the fountains of the New Testament. ⟦s16+17⟧ Whence Zacharias VIII: *I will strike the shepherd, and the sheep of the flock shall be scattered.* [var: Vulg. Zach 13:7 has the imperative *Percute pastorem, et dispergentur oves*, "Strike the shepherd, and the sheep shall be scattered"; the words printed here are those of Mt 26:31] For unless he had been struck, and blood and water had gone forth from his side, we should all suffer thirst for the word of God. ⟦s18⟧ Whence: *And they drank of the spiritual rock that followed them: and the rock was Christ [n: (I Cor. X)]*.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID.)] The people, thirsting for water, murmured against [0242A] Moses: therefore God commands that he show them the rock whereof they may drink. ⟦s2⟧ But if anyone, reading Moses, murmurs against him, and the letter of the law displeases him, Moses shows him the rock, which is Christ, and leads him to it, that from it he may drink and repair his thirst.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Go before the people,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Not Moses alone leads the people to the waters, but also the elders of the people. ⟦s3⟧ For not the law alone announces Christ, but also the prophets and the patriarchs and all the elders by birth.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the rod,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 64 in Exod.)] It is read that Aaron, not Moses, struck the river with the rod. ⟦s2⟧ For Moses divided the sea with that same rod, not the river. ⟦s3⟧ What then does this mean: *Take the rod wherewith thou didst strike the river?* ⟦s4⟧ Or did he perhaps call the sea a river? and is an example of this manner of speech to be sought? ⟦s5⟧ Or was what Aaron [0242B] did rather attributed to Moses, because through Moses God commanded the things which Aaron did, and in the one was the authority, in the other the ministry? ⟦s6⟧ Whence: *He shall be to thee for the people, but thou to him in those things which are unto God.*

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6, 7.-- *And thou shalt strike the rock.* ⟦s2⟧ Christ, who was struck upon the cross by the Jews: to whom the rod came near, that is, the wood of the passion, that water might remain for the faithful.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *But Amalec came,* etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Amalec was the son of Ismael, from whom are the Amalecites, who are also Ismaelites: these are the Saracens. ⟦s2⟧ Now it is interpreted *drinking* or *licking up blood,* and it signifies the devil, who ever assails the saints going out of Egypt and making toward the land of promise, and desires to drink the blood of souls.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0242C] [n: (ORIG., hom. II in Exod.)] The war made with the Amalecites is described. ⟦s2⟧ Before the people ate bread from heaven, and drank water from the rock, it is not said to have fought; but it is said to it: *The Lord will fight for you, and you shall hold your peace.* ⟦s3⟧ There is therefore a time when the Lord fights for us, and does not permit us to be tempted above what we can bear, nor suffer those unequal in strength to come to a stout encounter. ⟦s4⟧ Job also, being already perfect, fulfilled the contest of his temptation; and thou therefore, when thou hast begun to eat manna, the heavenly bread of the word of God, and to drink water from the rock, and when thou hast drawn near to the inner things of spiritual doctrine, look for the fight, prepare thyself for war.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9, 10.-- *And Moses said to Josue,* etc. [n: (ORIG., ibid.)] Another reading: *Moses said to Jesus: [0242D] Choose out men for thee,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Up to this point mention was never made of the blessed name of Jesus. ⟦s3⟧ Here first Moses called Jesus, and said to him: *Choose men for thine own self.* ⟦s4⟧ Moses calls Jesus, the law invokes Christ, that he may choose for himself mighty men from the people: Moses could not choose, but Jesus alone, who says in John XV: *You have not chosen me, but I have chosen you [n: (Matth. XII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ He is the leader of the elect and the prince of the mighty. ⟦s6⟧ He contends with Amalec. ⟦s7⟧ For he himself enters into the house of the strong man, and binds the strong man, and plunders his vessels.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I will stand on the top of the hill.* [n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] Moses, he says, went up not to the top of the mountain, but to the top of the hill. ⟦s2⟧ For it was kept for him to go up then to the top of the mountain when Jesus was to go up, [0243A] and with him Moses and Elias, and there to be transfigured in glory. ⟦s3⟧ Now therefore, as not yet glorified by the transfiguration of Jesus, he does not go up to the top of the mountain, but to the top of the hill. ⟦s4⟧ And when Moses lifted up his hands, Israel prevailed. ⟦s5+6⟧ Moses lifts up his hands, he does not stretch them out; Jesus, who embraced the whole world, being lifted up upon the cross, says: *I have stretched out my hands to a people that believeth not [n: (Isa. LXV)]*. [var: Vulg. Is 65:2 has *expandi manus meas tota die ad populum incredulum*, "I have spread forth my hands all the day to an unbelieving people"; Rom 10:21, whose wording is nearest, has *expandi manus meas*] To lift up the hands, this is to raise one's acts to God, that a man may not have his acts cast down and lying on the ground, but pleasing to God and raised toward heaven. ⟦s7⟧ He therefore lifts up his hands who lays up treasure in heaven. ⟦s8⟧ For where the treasure is, there is the eye, there the hand. ⟦s9⟧ He lifts up his hands who says: *The lifting up of my hands an evening sacrifice [n: (Psal. CXL)]*. ⟦s10+11⟧ If therefore our acts be lifted up, Amalec is overcome: whence Isaias [0243B] XXXV: *Lift up the hands which hang down and the feeble knees.* [var: Vulg. Is 35:3 has *confortate manus dissolutas, et genua debilia roborate*, "strengthen ye the feeble hands, and confirm the weak knees"; the wording printed here is nearest Hbr 12:12] If the people keeps the law, Moses lifts up his hands, and the adversary is overcome. ⟦s12⟧ If it does not keep the law, Amalec grows strong. ⟦s13⟧ Since therefore for us the fight is against the princes and powers, the rulers of the darkness of this world, if thou wilt conquer, lift up thy hands, that is, thy acts; let thy conversation be not on earth, but in heaven. ⟦s14⟧ Do thou too lift up thy hands to heaven unto God, fulfil the commandment of the Apostle: *Pray without ceasing [n: (I Thess. V)]*; and then shall come to pass what is written, that as the calf licks up the green grass in the fields, so shall this people lick up the people that is upon the earth; wherein it is brought in that the people of God fought not so much with hand and with arms as with voice and tongue, and laid its enemies low by prayer. ⟦s15⟧ Lift up thy acts therefore, be instant in prayer: this is the Christian's [0243C] fight, which overcomes the enemy. ⟦s16⟧ And I think that by this figure two peoples are signified, of whom one, from the Gentiles, lifts up the hands of Moses, and raises on high the things which he wrote and the understanding of them, that so it may conquer; but the other, that is, the Jewish people, does not lift up the hands of Moses from the earth, nor understands anything high or lofty in him, and so is overcome by its adversaries.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Having the rod of God,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 65.)] Now it is called the rod of God, which first was called Aaron's, afterwards is called Moses'. ⟦s2⟧ As the spirit of Elias is spoken of, which is the Spirit of God, of which Elias is said to be partaker; and the justice of God, which is ours. ⟦s3⟧ Whence: not knowing the justice of God, and wishing to establish their own [n: (Rom. X)], that is, [0243D] as though prepared by themselves for themselves.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And when Moses lifted up his hands,* etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Because when the law is understood mystically, and the passion of Christ is spiritually weighed, the devil is overcome and put to flight. ⟦s2⟧ But when the hands are let down, that is, when the precepts of the law are less zealously practised, the enemy follows after as victor.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12, 13.-- *Taking therefore a stone,* etc. [n: (GREG., homil. 33 in Evang.)] Moses sat upon a stone, etc., *as far as* those whom she justly condemns, he himself mercifully sets free.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *For I will blot out,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] In this he signifies that almighty God, through the law (which Moses signifies, and which pronounces concerning Christ), blots out [0244A] the memory of the spiritual enemies. ⟦s2+3⟧ Whence psalm XIX: *Their memory hath perished with a noise.*

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15, 16.-- *And Moses built an altar,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ All who conquer the devil ought to build in their hearts altars of faith, on which they may offer spiritual victims.
