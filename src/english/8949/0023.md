## CHAPTER XVIII.

VERS. 1-4.-- *And when Jethro had heard,* etc. That Moses, when he was about to deliver the children of Israel, sent away his wife and sons, whom afterwards he received back in the desert, signifies that preachers, when they are sent to the work of preaching, ought to unburden themselves of temporal solicitude: but when afterwards they are taken up to the episcopate or to some other dignity, then they ought to bear solicitude for those subject to them [0244B] both in spiritual and in temporal things.

VERS. 5-11.-- *Jethro therefore came,* etc. [n: (ORIG., hom. 11 in Exod.)] Another reading: *After these things Moses came to the mount of God, and Jethro went out of the camp to meet him as he came toward him, nor did he lead him to the mount of God, but to his own tent.* For the priest of Madian could not go up into the mount of God, nor yet go down into Egypt, nor could the wife of Moses; but now she comes to him with her sons. For no one could go down into Egypt, and undergo the contests of the Egyptians, save an approved athlete, and one who, striving in the contest, refrains himself from all things. Moses, because a great and mighty athlete, went down into Egypt to the contests and the exercises of the virtues; Abraham too and Jacob, who [0244C] by his very name is an athlete, when he had gone down into Egypt, was made as the stars of heaven in multitude. To others it turns out contrariwise to have gone down into Egypt. I know that Jeroboam, fleeing from Solomon, went down into Egypt, and rent and corrupted the people of God; because going down into Egypt he received from king Susachim as wife Tethimene, the sister of his wife.

VERS. 12-15.-- *Jethro therefore offered,* etc. *[n: (AUG. in Locutionibus, locut. 85.)] And Jethro the kinsman of Moses took holocausts,* etc. He put *took* for *offered*: or is it perhaps a matter of the sense, not of the manner of speech, so that he is understood to have offered what he is understood to have taken; although Moses is not read to have sacrificed, nor Aaron, nor any one of the Hebrews who were led out of the land of Egypt: yet nevertheless it was said above that Moses set up an altar. But Jethro [0244D] we read to have been a priest of Madian, that is, of the nation of Madian. But it is a wonder if at his coming Moses began to sacrifice, and not rather he himself who was a priest.

*And they came,* etc. [n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] Another reading: *There came,* he says, *the elders, Aaron, and all Israel, to eat bread with the kinsman of Moses in the sight of God.* Not all eat bread in the sight of God, but they who are elders, who are the older men, that is, who are perfect, proved in their merits, who whether they eat or drink, do it to the glory of God. All therefore that the saints do, they do in the sight of God: the sinner flees from the sight of God. Adam, after he had sinned, fled from the face of the Lord, and being questioned answered: [0245A] *I heard thy voice, O Lord, and I hid myself, because I was naked [n: (Gen. III)]*. [var: Vulg. Gn 3:10 has *vocem tuam audivi in paradiso: et timui, eo quod nudus essem, et abscondi me*, "I heard thy voice in paradise; and I was afraid, because I was naked, and I hid myself"] Cain went out from the face of the Lord, because he was unworthy of the sight of the Lord. Therefore they who receive a fuller knowledge of God, if they do evil, do it before God. Whence psalm I: *To thee only have I sinned,* etc. He who does evil before God straightway repents, and says, *I have sinned.* He who departs from the sight of God knows not how to be converted and to purge his sin by repenting. This therefore is the difference between doing evil before God, and departing from the sight of God.

[n: (ORIG.)] Not in vain did Jethro come to Moses, nor in vain did he eat bread with the elders of the people in the sight of God; he gives Moses a counsel approved and useful, that he should choose men, and appoint as princes of the people such as worship God, are able, and hate pride. For such [0245B] they ought to be as are not proud, and hate pride; that is, that not only should they themselves be without vice, but should hate the vices in evil men: I do not say the men, but the vices.

*Before God,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 66.)] It is asked: where, seeing that there was neither a tabernacle, nor the ark of the testament, which were instituted afterwards? nor can we take this as said of the future, as it was said of the manna, which was placed in a vessel, a golden one, namely. Therefore we ought to take that as done before God which was done in honour of the Lord.

VERS. 16-18.-- *I will show the precepts,* etc. [n: (ID., quaest. 67.)] It can be asked how Moses here said, etc., *as far as* he could not judge justly.

VERS. 19, 20.-- *But hear my words,* etc. *(ID., [0245C] quaest. 68.)* The first question is why God should have his own servant, etc., *as far as* whereby it becomes the fuller, the more freely it is extended into things heavenly and eternal.

*Be thou to the people,* etc. [n: (ID., quaest. 69.)] But as to what he adds, etc., *as far as* but whence this can be proved I know not.

VERS. 21-23.-- *But provide,* etc. Unto the keeping of humility he is instructed by a stranger, lest he who was learning so many secrets of God from God himself should be lifted up, and should think that he had no need of human counsel. It was also prefigured that the teaching of the Gentiles would surpass the knowledge and the pride of the Jews; and that the things which were lacking in the law, believers from among the Gentiles would suggest for their fulfilling, and would bring a spiritual understanding to the law of God. Moses also knew that the law would hear this people, and [0245D] would do whatsoever it should say, and that a lessening of the law would also come about at the suggestion of the Gospel.

*And appoint,* etc. [n: (ORIG., hom. 11.)] Thou shalt ordain them, he says, tribunes and centurions, and they shall point out [cj: *indicabunt*; read *judicabunt*, "shall judge"] the people at every hour, but nevertheless what shall be of greater weight they shall refer to thee. Let the princes of the people and the elders of the commons hear, who ought at every hour to judge the people, always and without ceasing to sit in judgment, to settle suits, and to reconcile those at variance, to call back the discordant into favour. Let each one learn from the holy Scriptures his own office. Moses, he says, that he may be in those things which are unto God, and may set forth the word of God to the peoples. But the other princes, whom he called tribunes, because they are set over a tribe, or centurions, or captains of fifty, [0246A] who are set over the lesser judgments, deciding those matters which pertain to individuals. And this figure I think is to be kept also in the world to come. Whence Matthew XIX: *When the Son of man shall sit upon the throne of his majesty, you also shall sit upon twelve seats,* etc. Thou seest therefore that not only does the Lord judge, to whom the Father has given all judgment [n: (Joan. V)], but he has appointed princes to judge of lesser causes; but the matter that shall be of greater weight, let them refer to him. Therefore the Lord says of one man, Matthew V: *That he shall be in danger of the council;* but of another, Matthew XII: *He shall be in danger of hell fire.* But also of every idle word they are said to render an account. And therefore he did not say that we shall render an account to God, as concerning perjury. *But thou shalt render thy oaths to the Lord.* Matthew XII: *The queen* also *of the south [0246B] shall rise in judgment with the men of this generation, and shall condemn it.* [var: Vulg. Mt 12:42 has *cum generatione ista*, "with this generation"; *cum viris generationis hujus*, with its men, is the wording of Lc 11:31, which closes *et condemnabit illos*, "shall condemn them"] Behold another kind of judgment, Matthew XIII: *He that hath ears to hear, let him hear:* for all these things are a shadow of things heavenly and an image of things to come.

[n: (IDEM.)] As it is written in Ecclesiastes I: *The eye shall not be satisfied with seeing, nor the ear with hearing,* [var: Vulg. Ecl 1:8 has *non saturatur oculus visu, nec auris auditu impletur*, "the eye is not filled with seeing, neither is the ear filled with hearing"; the gloss takes up *satiari* from the form printed here] neither can we be satisfied in considering the things which are written, wherewith it may build us up, in how many ways it may instruct us. For when I behold that Moses, a prophet full of God, to whom God spoke face to face [n: (Exod. XXXIII)], received counsel from Jethro his kinsman, a priest of Madian, I am struck dumb with exceeding wonder. For Moses heard the voice of his kinsman, and did whatsoever he said to him. He did not say, Since God speaks to me what I ought to do, how shall I receive counsel from a man, and from a Gentile man, [0246C] a stranger to the people of God? and he listens not to who it is that speaks, but to what is said. We therefore, if we find anything wisely said by the Gentiles, let us not, together with the author's name, despise the sayings also; nor, because we hold the law of God, does it become us to swell with pride and to despise the words of the prudent; but proving all things, let us hold fast that which is good. But they who today are set over peoples, I do not say if certain things have already been revealed to them by God, but if they have any merit in the knowledge of the law, disdain the counsel of an inferior, even of a priest, much less of a layman, much less of a Gentile: but Moses, who was meek above all men [n: (Num. XII)], received [sic: *accepti*] the counsel of an inferior, that he might give to princes a pattern of humility, and might mark out the image of a mystery to come. He knew that one day it would come to pass that Gentiles would bring forth good [0246D] counsel, bringing to Moses a good and spiritual understanding for the law, and that the law would hear them, doing all things that they should say. For the law cannot do as the Jews say, because the law is weakened in the flesh, that is, in the letter, and can do nothing according to the letter. For the law brought nothing to perfection [n: (Hebr. VII)]. But according to this counsel which we bring to the law, all things can be done spiritually, and sacrifices offered which now cannot be offered carnally; the law of leprosy can be kept spiritually. As therefore we understand, perceive and give counsel, the law does all things: but according to the letter not all things, but few.

VERS. 24.-- *Moses did all things which he had suggested,* [0247A] etc. A man familiar with God, most wise, not blushing to be taught by a lesser man and a Gentile, gave a pattern to teachers, that they should not blush to be instructed by any whomsoever.

VERS. 26, 27.-- *But whatsoever was of greater weight,* etc. [n: (AUG., loc. 88.)] Others: *But the weighty matter they referred upon Moses:* for so the Greek speech has it, which the Latins have translated, *to Moses.* By that manner of speech indeed the very care itself is figured, whereby it was said *upon Moses,* as though a burden laid upon him is signified. And *matter* moreover *weighty* was said for a weighty question.
