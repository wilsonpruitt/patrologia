## CHAPTER XXII.

VERS. 1.-- *If any man steal,* etc. [n: (STRAB.)] According to the letter, five oxen are rendered for one ox, and four sheep for one sheep: because an ox has five uses, a sheep four. For an ox is sacrificed, ploughs, feeds, gives milk, and furnishes its hide for various uses; but a sheep is sacrificed, and feeds, and yields milk and wool. Allegorically, however, by the ox is understood any Jew, and if any Jew sell him, he will lie under the penalty of the law, which is contained in the five books of Moses. By the sheep, the Christian, who imitates Christ, [0261A] of whom it is said: *He was led as a sheep to the slaughter [n: (Isa. LIII)]*. If any man sell this one, let him render four sheep, that is, let him do penance according to the ordinance of the four Gospels.

VERS. 2-5.-- *If a thief be found breaking open a house, or undermining it,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 84 in Exod.)] If a thief be found breaking in, and being struck shall die, etc., *as far as* for now he is more than a thief.

VERS. 6-13.-- *If a fire break out*, etc. [n: (RAB.)] We kindle a fire when we prepare correction for our subjects, that we may consume the thorns of vices. But it must be seen to, lest, while we pursue the vices, we injure the virtues, and harm before their ripeness those who had begun to bring forth the fruit of good works. He, therefore, who shall have done this, let him bear worthy penance, and what he has injured through slothfulness [0261B] let him diligently restore.

*The ears of corn.* Tender hearers not yet coming to the ripeness of fruit: whose vices he who sharply and indiscreetly rebukes, casts fire as it were into the ears of corn, and does not suffer them to come to ripeness.

VERS. 14-16.-- *He who from his neighbour,* etc. We ask a loan of our neighbour when for an eternal recompense we take up the care of the Lord's flock. But if we always set before the eyes of the heart the fear of God, as of one exacting money, we are as it were always in his presence. Whence II Cor. V: *Knowing the fear of the Lord, we persuade men.* And elsewhere, II Cor. XII: *As before God, in Christ we speak.* The death, therefore, or the maiming of the beast is not imputed to the shepherd, if he has borne its care strenuously, and as though God were present; otherwise he is compelled to render the loss. [0261C] Whence Matth. V: *He shall not go out thence, till he repay the last farthing.*

VERS. 17.-- *If the father of the virgin,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The sense of the sentence is historical, out of which the discourse is born. If, therefore, the historical sense builds up the reign of charity and destroys that of cupidity, there is no need for it to be expounded allegorically. Yet the reader ought to honour the sentence with the dowry of right faith, and to leave it untouched by his own exposition.

VERS. 18-20.-- *Thou shalt not suffer wizards to live.* Those who work by the sleights of the magic art and by diabolical feignings, understand as heretics, who are to be excommunicated from the fellowship of the faithful who truly live, until the evil-doing of error die in them

VERS. 21.-- *Thou shalt not grieve the stranger.* [n: (RAB.)] He commends the love of one's neighbour through the compassion of mercy, [0261D] lest, moved by the goad of fury or of avarice, we neglect the right of nature. But what we wish men to do to us, the same let us do to them, and what we do not wish to be done, let us not do.

VERS. 22-24.-- *You shall not hurt the widow and the orphan.* [n: (RAB.)] By the widow, holy Church, which in the day of judgment awaits Christ, whom now she has not present; by the orphan, the Christian people, to whom the world their father, and Babylon their mother, is dead, may be taken: whom he forbids to be grieved by persecution or by worldly pride, lest perchance such harrying come to the spiritual ruin of those who vex them in vain with hatreds and reproaches.

VERS. 25.-- *If money on loan,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ex 22:25 *Si pecuniam mutuam dederis*, "If thou lend money"; Migne's nominative *pecunia* does not construe with the accusative *mutuam*] [n: (RAB.)] He warns the preachers [0262A] of the Gospel not to seek superfluity, but having food and clothing, to be content with these, according to that of Matth. X: *Freely you have received, freely give.*

VERS. 26, 27.-- *If a pledge,* etc. [n: (AUG., loc. 99.)] He put the genus for the species: for it was so said as if he had given a pledge of any garment, whereas Scripture testifies from what follows that it speaks specially of him who has no garment but that one wherewith he may be covered by night.

*[n: (GREG., lib. XVI Moral., cap. 2.)] A pledge,* the confession of sin. For our neighbour is made our debtor, etc., *as far as* let him feel that what he has done amiss is presently remitted by us.

VERS. 28.-- *Thou shalt not speak ill of the gods,* etc. *(AUG., quaest, [0262B] 86 in Exod.)* It is asked whom he called gods, etc., *as far as* or to be honoured with the services of latria.

VERS. 29-31.-- *Thy tithes,* etc. [n: (ISID. in Gen., tom. V.)] By the firstfruits of the crops or of the firstborn are shown the first beginnings of good works, etc., *as far as* and the effect of perfection is granted.
