## CHAPTER XXIII.

VERS. 3.-- *Neither shalt thou have pity on a poor man in judgment.* [n: (AUG.)] That is, lest against justice thou favour the poor man for mercy's sake. Mercy is good, but it ought not to be against judgment. Now Scripture calls judgment that which is just. But lest anyone, on account of this sentence, should think that God forbids mercy, there fittingly follows:

[0262C]VERS. 4-6.-- *If thou meet thy enemy's ox,* etc. As if to say: show mercy even toward thine enemies, when the power is free from judging; for when thou leadest back the straying ox of thine enemy and restorest it, thou art not sitting as judge between any parties.

VERS. 7-9.-- *Thou shalt fly lying.* [n: (RAB.)] The Lord loves truth and judgment, and to the performing of these he exhorts his people, because he is no respecter of persons.

VERS. 10-12.-- *Six years thou shalt sow thy ground.* [n: (AUG.)] It is asked what the poor gather, if in the seventh year the land is thus spared, etc., *as far as* six years, when those things are gathered which are sown.

[n: (RAB.)] In the number six is the perfection of works, etc., *as far as he that soweth in blessings, in blessings shall also [0262D] reap.* [var: Vulg. 2Cor 9:6 *de benedictionibus et metet*, "shall reap also of blessings"; Migne repeats the preposition of the first member]

VERS. 13.-- *And by the name of strange gods,* etc. He permits swearing by his own name, not because it is good to swear, but because he knew that men are prone to swearing; he does not will that they swear by the names of outward gods, lest by frequent oaths they be led into the worship of them. So too God does not approve victims, yet he had rather that sacrifice be made to himself than to idols.

VERS. 14-18.-- *Three times in every year,* etc. Here, that is, God requires the faith of the holy Trinity: for at the Pasch the Son is immolated, at Pentecost the Holy Spirit is given, at the Scenopegia the power of the Father's majesty is declared.

*Three times in every year,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] This [0263A] is: at every season of the present life, and in the confession of the holy Trinity, through faith and hope and charity, you shall render the services of good works to God your creator. For we celebrate the Pasch in the month of new things, when, delivered by the blood of the unspotted Lamb from Egyptian servitude, we pass through spiritual waters out of the old man into the new, not in the oldness of the letter but in the newness of the spirit, whereby we come into the land of promise through the grace of Christ. But we celebrate the solemnity of the month of firstfruits when we consecrate to the Lord the firstfruits of our works, of our wills and of our words; the solemnity also at the going out of the year, when, the crops being gathered in, that is, the fruits of the virtues, we strive to come to the end of life and to the entrance of the heavenly kingdom; in tabernacles [0263B] commemorating for seven days, because through all the time of the present life we acknowledge that we are pilgrims, and seek rest not here but in the time to come, diligently taking care that we appear not empty in the sight of God.

*The solemnity also,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] When the crops, that is, are ripe, when the fruits of the whole year have been gathered: namely in September, which the Hebrews call by the Chaldee word thisri, תשרי, and the Macedonians ὑπερβεραῖον. At the beginning of it the Jews, keeping holiday, sound the trumpet more than in the other months, and offer more things; in which month is the day of expiations, and the day of the Scenopegia on the fifteenth day, which is the solemnity of Tabernacles.

VERS. 19.-- *Thou shalt not boil a kid,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 90 in Exod.)] How it is to be understood according to the propriety of the words, I know not whether it can be found out, [0263C] etc., *as far as* but afterwards it was not lambs in milk that suffered, but rams in the flock.

[n: (STRAB.)] *Morally.* The kid, that is, the sinner, is not to be boiled in his mother's milk, that is, dissolved by blandishments, but sharply rebuked and refuted.

VERS. 20, 21.-- *Behold I will send an angel,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Josue signifies the leader of the people, who is therefore called an angel because he is sent to announce the will of the Lord; for ἄγγελος in Greek is called *a messenger*.

VERS. 22.-- *I will be an enemy,* etc. [n: (GREG.)] The root of all evil is pride, of which it is said in Eccli. X: *Pride the beginning of all sin.* Now its offspring are the seven principal vices: vainglory, envy, [0263D] anger, sadness, avarice, gluttony of the belly, lust. And because our Redeemer grieved that we were held captive by these seven vices of pride, he came full of the Spirit of sevenfold grace to the spiritual battle of our deliverance.

VERS. 23, 24.-- *And an angel shall go before thee.* [n: (RAB.)] Josue led the people of Israel into the land of promise, casting out seven nations and destroying their idols and their altars. Mystically the true Jesus exterminates these nations with their images, when he drives out the seven principal vices and every idol of the enemy from our hearts, that we may securely possess the land of our own nature.

*Whom I will destroy.* [n: (CHRYSOST.)] Every vice has its own dwelling in our heart, [0264A] and, claiming that dwelling for itself, it exterminates Israel in the recess of our soul, that is, the contemplation of the highest things, and does not cease to oppose; for virtues cannot abide together with vices. But when the vices have been overcome by the Israelites, that is, by the virtues, the place which the spirit of concupiscence or of fornication possessed, chastity shall obtain; the place which fury had seized, patience shall claim; the place which sadness working death had occupied, saving gladness full of joy shall possess; the place which sloth laid waste, fortitude shall cultivate; the place which pride trod down, humility shall make honourable. And so, the several vices being expelled, their places, that is, the affections, shall be possessed by the contrary virtues; and these are named the sons of Israel, that is, of the soul that sees God: who, when they have driven out all the passions of the heart, are to be believed not so much to have seized [0264B] other men's possessions as to have recovered their own; because, according to the ancient tradition, the sons of Sem obtained by lot, in the division of the world, the land of the Chananaeans, which the Chananaeans possessed by violence: whence by the just, judgment of God they were driven out. The will of the Lord also assigned the possession of our heart not to vices but to virtues, which after the transgression of Adam, when the vices grew insolent like Chananaeans, were driven from their proper region. But when by the grace of God they are restored by our diligence and by our labour, they do not so much occupy another's land as receive back their own.

VERS. 25-27.-- *I will bless thy loaves,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Another reading: *I will bless thy bread and thy wine,* etc. Although these promises can be understood spiritually also, etc., *as far as* which the ungodly shall not receive.

[0264C]VERS. 28.-- *Sending out hornets. [n: (AUG., quaest. 93.)]* I will send wasps, etc. This God promises, etc., *as far as* if anything is said by Christ.

Another reading. *I will send wasps before thee, and it shall cast out the Amorrhites,* etc. He turns himself from the plural to the singular number. And *it shall cast out the Amorrhites* is understood of the wasp, namely as of the locust, as of the frog: not that it is one, but by the singular the plural is understood.

VERS. 29-33.-- *I will not cast them out from the face,* etc. [n: (STRAB.)] These nations signify the vices, which are extinguished in the saints neither all together nor at one time, lest the monsters of the earth be multiplied, that is, lest they be lifted up into pride and vainglory and things of this kind. For when the saints fight against the greater vices and conquer, they leave [0264D] the lesser, and these are permitted to have dominion, lest through elation they fall more grievously. Whence to Paul was given a sting of the flesh for the keeping of humility [n: (I Cor. XXII)].
