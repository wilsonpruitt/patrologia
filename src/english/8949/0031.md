## CHAPTER XXIV.

VERS. 1.-- *And to Moses he said: Come up to the Lord, thou and Aaron,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 85 in Exod., tom. III.)] Up to this place the justifications of Scripture are understood, etc., *as far as* but the Latins say that those are justifications which the Greeks call δικαιώματα.

*Thou and Aaron,* etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Moses and Aaron and Nadab and Abiu. By these four are signified evangelical men, that is, those who follow the evangelical precepts. [0265A] But by the seventy (which number consists of the number seven taken ten times, or of the number ten multiplied seven times) are signified those who, helped by the grace of the Holy Spirit, fulfilling the ten of the law, stretch toward the contemplation of the Creator. Or, according to the letter, those of whom it is said in Num. XI: *Take unto thee seventy men,* etc., among whom were Heldad and Meldad. [var: Vulg. Nm 11:16 *Congrega mihi septuaginta viros de senibus Israel*, "Gather unto me seventy men of the ancients of Israel"]

VERS. 2.-- *And Moses alone shall come up to the Lord.* [n: (RAB.)] First it is commanded that Moses and Aaron, Nadab and Abiu, and the seventy elders should come up to the Lord. And at once it is subjoined that Moses alone should come up to the Lord, and that the rest should not draw near, but adore afar off. Where we are morally instructed to weigh our own strength, and not to reach the citadel of contemplation beyond our power, but so far as the divine clemency [0265B] calls, and the rule of Scripture invites.

[n: (GREG.)] Moses, that he might receive the sublime precepts of God, climbed on high; and that he might penetrate inward things, he is hidden from outward tumults: because holy men, who are compelled by necessity to serve outward offices, always flee back studiously to the secret places of the heart, and there ascend the summit of their inmost thought, and receive the law as it were upon a mountain, while, the tumults of temporal actions being set aside, they search out upon the height of their contemplation the sentence of the supernal will.

VERS. 3-5.-- *We will do [n: (AUG., quaest. 95.)]* It is to be noted that again the people, etc., *as far as* they laid hold of justice.

VERS. 6-9.-- *He took therefore,* etc. *[ID., quaest. 98.]* [0265C] It is to be noted that Moses now sacrificed for the first time, etc., *as far as* for that the decalogue was written on tables of stone appears afterwards.

*Bowls.* A *cratera*, or *crater*, is so called ἀπὸ τοῦ κρατεῖν, which is *to hold,* in that it is able to hold much wine.

VERS. 10, 11.-- *Under his feet.* God is not contained by place, nor does he set the members of a body in a posture, as it belongs to our body to sit, and to lie, to stand, and the like, which are of the body only. But God is a spirit. Therefore that he shows himself under a bodily appearance, or by signs bodily expressed, is not the appearing of his substance, which is what he himself is; rather the assumption of visible forms lies subject to all his power.

VERS. 12.-- *Come up to me,* etc. *(BED., lib. I de [0265D] Tabernac., cap. 11, tom. II.)* Being about to speak of the figure of the tabernacle, etc., *as far as* that the Church might be designated as about to speak throughout the world with the tongues of all nations.

*And I will give thee.* [n: (ID.)] Like to this is that: *Teaching them to observe all things whatsoever I have commanded you [n: (Matth. XXVIII)]*. Therefore neither Moses, nor the apostles, nor anyone whatever, ought to teach other things than those which the Lord taught and prescribed by the decree of his counsel to be observed, in which he willed that we should have eternal life; and just as nothing of these ought to be passed over, so it is needful to commit to the hearers all things which he commanded.

VERS. 13.-- *Moses and Josue rose up. [n: (AUG., quaest. 103 in Exod.)]* Josue, not mentioned among those four, suddenly appears with Moses, etc., *as far [0266A] as* that at the time now recorded he was not yet called Jesus, but when it was written.

[n: (BED.)] Josue signifies Christ in name and in action; who is called the minister of Moses, because Christ *came not to destroy the law, but to fulfil [n: (Matth. V)]*. He follows the footsteps of Moses in all things, because in all that Moses wrote he is typically designated, and, as an inseparable companion, is found if he be rightly sought. Whence John V: *If you did believe Moses, you would perhaps believe me also.*

[n: (BED., de Tabernac., lib. I, cap. 1, tom. II.)] Aaron ( אהרן ) is interpreted *the mountain of strength,* Hur ( חור ) *fire* or *light,* etc., *as far as* and the mind burns toward the love of the Creator.

VERS. 15.-- *And when Moses was gone up, [n: (BEDA., ibid.)]* As the mountain the height of perfection, etc., *as far [0266B] as* he protects with heavenly blessing.

VERS. 16.-- *And the glory of the Lord dwelt. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Not the loftiness only, but the very name of the mountain, etc., *as far as* on the seventh we are bidden to rest.

*Covering it with a cloud. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* For six days he is covered with the cloud and with the glory of the Lord, etc., *as far as* that he might come through to the loftier gifts of the divine vision and converse.

VERS. 17.-- *And the appearance was,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] The appearance of the glory of the Lord appeared as a burning fire, etc., *as far as* although they are not able to follow them fully.

VERS. 18.-- *And Moses entering in,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] Moses, having entered the midst of the cloud, went up into the mountain, etc., *as far as* that by the observance of them they may come to eternal salvation.
