## CHAPTER XXVI.

VERS. 1.-- *And the tabernacle shall so be made,* etc. [n: (BED., lib. II de Tabernac., cap. 3, tom. II.)] [var: Vulg. Ex 26:1 *Tabernaculum vero ita facies*, "the tabernacle thou shalt make thus"; Migne's impersonal passive drops the command addressed to Moses] The tabernacle [0270C] which Moses made in the wilderness, and the temple which Solomon made in Jerusalem, etc., *as far as* and they may be joined by the bond of divine and of proper love.

[n: (ID., ubi supra. )] The figure of both sanctuaries can be distinguished thus, etc., *as far as* he depicts with a wonderful variety of figures.

*Of twisted fine linen, and of hyacinth and purple, and scarlet twice dyed, diversified. [n: (ID., ubi supra, cap. 2.)]* In these four colours of the curtains, etc., *as far as* the purple, of a bloody colour and dyed with the blood of shellfish, figures hearts devoted to God and ready to die for God.

VERS. 2.-- *The length of one curtain shall have twenty-eight cubits, the breadth four,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] [0270D] If from one up to seven thou number all the numbers in order, by a full order, *as far as* that through faith and good works it may tend toward rest.

*Of one measure,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] Although the curtains differed by a varying embroidery, yet they were all tempered to one measure of length and breadth, because although the elect have differing gifts, yet there is one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all.

[n: (ID., ubi supra. )] Josephus relates, etc., *as far as* the tabernacle is adorned as it were by the one connecting of ten curtains.

VERS. 4.-- *Loops of hyacinth in the sides,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] By loops of hyacinth the curtains are coupled to one another, [0271A] etc., *as far as* and they trust that they shall abide in it for ever.

[n: (BEDA, ibid.)] It was commanded also that fringes should be made at the corners of the cloaks, etc., *as far as* being gathered from the four winds, we shall be joined in an indivisible fellowship.

VERS. 5.-- *Fifty each,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] In the fiftieth year, etc., *as far as* and in the receiving of which there is truly rest and joy, it is rightly figured.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] The curtains have fifty loops, because it is brought about by the gift of the Holy Spirit that the elect are coupled to one another in the fellowship of peace, which is the bond of perfection: and the hope also and the memory of that fellowship and peace to come, which is had in this life, makes those who are separated by times or by places to be of one accord in the same piety.

[0271B] [n: (ID., ibid.)] The curtains have loops on either part, etc., *as far as* when the just are leagued together by a harmonious quality of virtues.

*Thou shalt make also fifty rings. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* This is more fully explained when it is said, etc., *as far as* nay rather, that out of all the elect the Church of Christ may be perfected.

VERS. 7.-- *Thou shalt make also tent-cloths,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] This is found thus in what follows: *He made also eleven tent-cloths of goats' hair,* etc. The tent-cloths, with which the tabernacle is covered, etc., *as far as* while like tent-cloths they bear the storms without.

The tent-cloths of haircloth signify the coarse minds of men, which by the hidden judgment of God are preferred, however harshly, because they fear not to serve temporal cares, that they may [0271C] bear the winds and rains of temptations.

*Thou shalt make also tent-cloths,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] Haircloth belongs to penitents, etc., *as far as* yet how great is the perfection of their hearts is declared when it is said: *The length of one tent-cloth,* etc.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] The tent-cloths of haircloth, or of goats' hair, etc., *as far as* nor do they receive the apostolic grace.

[n: (AUG., quaest. 108 in Exod.)] There are eleven hairy veils, etc., *as far as and whose sins are covered.*

*For a covering,* namely lest the inner things, tender and bright, be troubled by any adversity whatever; for otherwise neither does the fine linen shine, nor the scarlet glitter, nor is the hyacinth adorned with its blue colour: those therefore who advance in the bosom of the Church [0271D] ought not to despise their superiors, if they see them taken up with outward things.

VERS. 8.-- *The length of one tent-cloth. [n: (BEDA, lib. II de Tabernac., cap. 3, tom. II.)]* The length has the number ten, etc., *as far as* who nevertheless, according to the measure of human perfection, are exalted among men.

*The measure shall be equal. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Because there is one faith in which the whole Church is saved, etc., *as far as* where the solid firmness of a wall was wanting.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] Consider also the measure of the curtains, etc., *as far as* So much for the curtains.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] The tent-cloth has thirty cubits of length, etc., *as far as* in that they are leaders of the flocks that follow their lords.

[0272A]VERS. 9.-- *The sixth tent-cloth in the front.* Mystically, however, in the front of the roof the sixth tent-cloth is doubled, when all who enter the holy Church are so initiated by faith and by the sacrament of the Lord's passion that they understand that they must always live by the imitation of it as well. For as it were at the entrance the sixth tent-cloth is doubled, that we may both obtain the sacraments of the Lord's passion and be instructed by his examples. Of the sacraments Peter says: *Who hath regenerated us unto a lively hope, by the resurrection of Jesus Christ from the dead.* And he says also of the imitation of the passion: *Christ therefore having suffered in the flesh, be you also armed with the same thought.*

VERS. 10.-- *Thou shalt make also fifty loops in the edge of one tent-cloth. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)]* As we have expounded these things above concerning the curtains, etc., *as far as* so much the more does he seek the help of his neighbour, [0272B] whereby he may be strengthened.

VERS. 11.-- *Of brass, wherewith the loops may be joined, that of all there may be made one covering,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Now the buckles are rightly of brass, made, that is, of a sounding metal, because the humble conscience of the just has a great voice with God. Whence the poor man of David, when he was in anguish and was pouring out his prayer not into the ears of men but in the sight of God: *O Lord,* (he says) *hear my prayer, and let my cry come to thee.*

VERS. 12.-- *But that which shall remain over,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] That these things may be more fully understood, etc., *as far as* ten again of descent along the boards of the eastern wall.

VERS. 13.-- *And a cubit.* Because the tent-cloths passed beyond the curtains by one cubit [0272C] on the southern side of the house, and by another on the northern, and therefore reached to the ground, since these had thirty cubits of length and those two less.

VERS. 14.-- *Thou shalt make also a covering.* [n: (STRAB.)] The first covering was that of the curtains of fine linen, purple and scarlet; the second that of the eleven tent-cloths; the third of the reddened skins of rams; the last was of hyacinth-coloured skins. Now the curtains so covered that they came down at the sides, yet did not reach the ground; but the tent-cloths covered both roof and sides, and did reach the ground. But the reddened or violet skins covered the roof only, nor did they slope down to the other parts.

*Of skins,* etc. [n: (BED., ibid., cap. 4.)] The rams are [0272D] the doctors, the leaders of the Lord's flock. Whence: *Bring to the Lord, O ye children of God, bring,* etc. As if to say: O angels, appointed to this office, bring to the Lord into the heavens the imitators of the apostles. Hence also the sixth station of the people that went out of Egypt is named helin, that is, rams, in which there were twelve fountains and seventy palm trees, that so by name and by kind it might hold the figure of the apostles.

*Reddened. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The skins of rams are reddened for covering the roof of the tabernacle, etc., *as far as* lest they fail amid oppressions, they fortify

*Of violet skins. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The hyacinth is of a heavenly colour, etc., *as far as* that is, imitating the life of heaven.

VERS. 15.-- *Of setim wood. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* These boards [0273A] signify the apostles and their successors, through whose discourse the Church was enlarged, etc., *as far as* nor was the Hebrew name translated, because this wood is scarcely found among other peoples.

VERS. 16.-- *Every one shall have ten cubits in length, in breadth a cubit and a half. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)]* The length of the boards is the height, which had ten cubits, etc., *as far as: and I will show him my salvation [n: (Psal. XC)]*.

VERS. 17.-- *In the sides. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* It is mortised in the sides of the boards, etc., *as far as* we receive in the bosom of love, and believe that we are received by them.

VERS. 18.-- *Of which twenty shall be on the south side. [n: (ID., ubi supra, cap. 6.)]* How great the length of the tabernacle was, etc., *as far as* lest anyone think that virtues [0273B] without work suffice him.

VERS. 19.-- *For which thou shalt cast forty sockets of silver,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] The bases by which the boards were held up are the words and the books of the law and of the prophets, by which the apostles and evangelists proved that the things they willed to write and to preach were true and divine. Whence: *Then was fulfilled that which was spoken by the prophets.* And elsewhere: *Now all this was done, that the scriptures of the prophets might be fulfilled [n: (Matth. XXI)]*. [var: Vulg. these words stand at Mt 26:56; the address Migne prints points to Mt 21:4, which reads *ut adimpleretur quod dictum est per prophetam dicentem*] And Peter says: *We have the more firm prophetical word [n: (II Petr. I)]*.

[n: (BED., GREG.)] By the silver bases of the boards the order of the prophets is signified: who, when they first spoke of the Lord's incarnation, rose as it were as bases from the foundation, and sustained the weights of the fabric set above them, [0273C] and they sustain the boards, that is, the apostolic preaching spread abroad in the world, as firm and molten bases: because the life of the apostles is instructed by their preaching, and is made solid by their authority. Whence two bases joined together are set under each board: because while the prophets agree in their words concerning the incarnation of Christ, they build up the preachers that follow, so that, in the point where they do not differ among themselves, they may fix those others the more strongly. They are commanded to be cast of silver. For the brightness of silver is preserved by use, and without use turns to blackness; but the sayings of the prophets, before the coming of Christ, were not in the use of spiritual understanding, since they could not be beheld through their obscurity, and therefore they remained as it were black: but after Christ, coming, wiped them before our eyes with the hand of his incarnation, [0273D] whatever of light lay hidden in them shone forth, and he gave the understanding of the preceding fathers into use, because he expounded the words by the things.

[n: (BEDA.)] Rightly are two bases set under each board, that the consent of the prophetic attestation in all the things which the apostles said may be shown. Or they are put beneath, two by two, at the two corners, that, the corners being well propped, the whole board may be able to stand upright: because the beginning of all apostolic and evangelical discourse is prefigured in the prophetic books, and the end of the whole life of the apostles and of their successors, from the beginning of faith unto the end of the present life, is inserted in the mystical boards of the old testament.

VERS. 20.-- *On the second side also.* The southern side, which looks toward the south wind, signifies the ancient people of God, [0274A] which, long ago receiving the light of the knowledge of the law, glowed with the love of God. The second side, which looks toward the North, signifies the Gentiles, who until the time of the incarnation were torpid in the darkness and cold of unbelief; of whose calling it is said: *I will say to the North: Give up; and to the South: Keep not back [n: (Isa. XLIII)]*. As if to say: I will say to the people of the Gentiles, long benumbed without faith: Let there come of thy sons to the faith of my confession and of my love. Yea and to the people of Israel, which already enjoys the light of my knowledge: Keep not back the Gentiles from being received into the lot of election.

*Twenty boards.* [n: (BEDA.)] Now the board on either side was of one number, and of one measure, and of one making: because one and the same faith, hope and charity is preached to either people through the apostles, and to the promised land [0274B] of the heavenly kingdom either people is called. Of both they receive a general precept: *Going into the whole world, preach the Gospel to every creature [n: (Marc. XVI)]*, that is, to uncircumcision and to circumcision. And without any difference it is subjoined: *He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved.*

VERS. 21.-- *Two sockets.* A base is a foundation, from the Greek βασεία, that is, I found. By the bases the prophets are signified, upon whose authority the apostles and all doctors lean. And they are called two because of the concord of the prophets, who all thought one thing.
