## CHAPTER XXVIII.

VERS. 1.-- *Take also unto thee,* etc. [n: (BEDA, lib. III de Tabernac., cap. 1, tom. II.)] How the tabernacle, etc., *as far as* and all the masters of the Church.

[n: (ID.)] *Take also,* etc. The making of the tabernacle being described, [0278C] etc., *as far as* they are consumed by the fire of the vengeance from above.

VERS. 2.-- *And thou shalt make a holy vesture. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The holy vestments of Aaron, etc., *as far as* and a dispenser of the word of God.

VERS. 4.-- *And these. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* How each of these was made, etc., *as far as* they ought to be excellent and glorious to the sight of the inward judge.

[n: (GREG. Past., lib. II, cap. 3, tom. III.)] The priest is bidden on either shoulder, etc., *as far as* as in the diverse fashion of the superhumeral the twisted fine linen shines white.

VERS. 6.-- *And they shall make the superhumeral. [n: (BED., lib. III de Tabernac., cap. 4.)]* Because on the shoulders we bear burdens, etc., *as far as* when through abstinence the flesh [0278D] is wearied.

*And scarlet twice dyed, and twisted fine linen, of embroidered work. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The scarlet shines with the appearance of fire, etc., *as far as* showing the scarlet in his own habit.

VERS. 7.-- *It shall have two edges joined at the top on both sides, that they may be closed into one. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Of this it is said in what follows, etc., *as far as* than that we seek the rewards of the invisible life.

VERS. 8.-- *The very texture also and all the variety of the work shall be of gold and hyacinth and purple, and scarlet twice dyed, and twisted fine linen. And thou shalt take. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Not gold in one part, etc., *as far as* the priest ought to be bright.

VERS. 9.-- *Two stones,* etc. That the priest may imitate the faith [0279A] and life of the patriarchs, and be mindful of the twelve tribes in his sacrifices and prayers, and that the people, beholding, may not stray from the merits of the patriarchs.

VERS. 11.-- *Set in gold,* etc. [n: (BED., ibid.)] Fitly are the stones bidden to be set in gold and to be encompassed about, etc., *as far as* they may be able to slip away from the keeping of the mind.

VERS. 12.-- *And Aaron shall bear their names before the Lord upon both shoulders for a memorial.* Because the priest, diligently weighing at every hour the life of the saints that went before, ought in adversity and in prosperity to be fortified with the ornament of the virtues, so that, walking by the arms of justice on the right hand and on the left, he may be bent aside on no side of the lowest delight.

[n: (BED. ibid.)] For three causes the names of Aaron, etc., [0279B] *as far as* or may set forth the examples of the fathers which they are to follow.

VERS. 13, 14.-- *Thou shalt make also hooks of gold, and two little chains of purest gold, linked one to another, which thou shalt put into the hooks. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* These hooks were in the topmost corners, etc., *as far as* they are to be knit together by charity and by good intention.

[n: (GREG., Past., part. II, cap. 2, tom. III.)] It is commanded that upon Aaron's breast the rational of judgment be fastened with binding fillets, etc., *as far as* subjects are not ruled without great fear.

VERS. 15.-- *Thou shalt make also the rational of judgment with work,* etc. [n: (BED., ubi supra, cap. 5.)] As in the superhumeral the perfection of works, etc., *as far as* they may study earnestly to think what befits them.

[0279C]VERS. 16.-- *Double. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The rational was made, etc., *as far as* that he may lay hold of the prize of the supernal vocation of God.

VERS. 17.-- *And thou shalt set in it. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* This setting of divers gems in the rational, etc., *as far as* that they too may deserve to be made members of the highest priest.

[n: (ID.)] We can in the varied comeliness of the stones, etc., *as far as* it profits him nothing.

VERS. 19.-- *The onyx.* [n: (ID.)] Rightly moreover the two onyx stones, etc., *as far as* he himself may advance to better things.

VERS. 22.-- *Thou shalt make in the rational,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The order of the work, etc., *as far as* and they would be moved apart one from another.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] The superhumeral the consummation of good [0279D] works, etc., *as far as* if he does not, hastening, hold it in with the hooks of good action.

[n: (ID.)] That the superhumeral was drawn tight with a belt, etc., *as far as* through the working of the virtues.

VERS. 28.-- *A hyacinth fillet,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] The hyacinth fillets by which both habits of the priest are coupled are the bindings of heavenly desire, which, knit into the golden rings, draw the superhumeral together with the rational, when, the light of the everlasting fatherland being known, we sigh after that glory, and, that we may deserve to enter in, are earnest to serve in the tabernacle of the present Church with concordant faith, and life, and work, and profession.

VERS. 29.-- *And Aaron shall bear the names of the children of Israel in the rational of judgment upon,* etc. The names of the children [0280A] of Israel Aaron bears upon his breast as a memorial before the Lord for ever, when the prelate never intermits the care of his subjects, but strengthens their life by consoling, rebuking, exhorting, and is busy to commend it to the Lord to be guarded with frequent prayers.

[n: (GREG., lib. XXVIII Moral., cap. 9.)] The priest, when he enters the tabernacle, is bidden to bear on his breast twelve stones graven with the names of the children of Israel: this is our Redeemer, who, offering himself a sacrifice for us, while he had strong preachers at the beginning, bore twelve stones under the head, in the first part of his body.

It behoves the priest to bear the memory of the fathers on his breast, chiefly when he ministers at the altar, that in the ministry which is [0280B] the more sacred he may the more skilfully adorn himself with his whole study, with all the industry of mind and of body, looking upon the examples of the saints, that as their earnest imitator he may present himself fitted to the divine gaze.

VERS. 30.-- *And thou shalt put in the rational of judgment,* etc. [n: (BED., ubi supra. )] That the priest, namely, may remember, etc., *as far as* in that which he dispenses in God's stead.

*And thou shalt put in the rational,* etc. [n: (AUG., Quaest. 117 in Exod.)] In what thing or metal the doctrine and the truth are set upon the rational, etc., *as far as* set upon the λογίον.

VERS. 31.-- *Thou shalt make also a tunic,* etc. [n: (BED., ubi supra. )] The priest is clothed with a tunic reaching to the feet, wholly of hyacinth, etc., *as far as* it is rightly added:

VERS. 32.-- *In the midst whereof above there shall be a head-opening [0280C] and a woven border round about it: as it is wont to be made,* etc. The hyacinth tunic has a most firm head-opening, and a border woven out of itself, lest it be easily rent, when the beginning of a good work, propped by the strong root of the fear of the Lord, is proved to be fortified also against all the snares of the ancient enemy. Such a border clothes and adorns the priest's neck round about, when it affords to the ruler confidence in preaching heavenly things: this, that he has not only lived rightly in the course of his life, but has also begun his beginning from rectitude, like Samuel, Jeremias, John, who from their very infancy, filled with spiritual grace, were also set apart in the order of teachers. For because the voice is in the neck, by the neck is expressed the practice of right speaking, *as it is wont to be done in the outermost parts of garments:* as [0280D] by the garments the works, so by the outermost parts of the garments the consummation of the works is figured. Or, the outermost parts of the garments are our last anxieties, in which the faithful, when they are compelled to end their life, strive the more intently to purge themselves of every stain of evils, taking heed with fear and trembling lest, being brought before the strict judge, they be cast out into the outer darkness for the filthy habit of vices, but may appear clothed, as the elect of God, with bowels of mercy, benignity, modesty, patience and charity, which is the bond of perfection [n: (Coloss. III)]. The hyacinth tunic, therefore, has a border woven in the head-opening, as it is wont to be made in the outermost parts of garments: when an excellent teacher begins the work of the virtues from so great a perfection as another scarcely at any time arrives at by [0281A] long labour; and when with so great fear of service he walks at every hour with his God, as another, about to die and to enter upon the last judgment, scarcely suffices to have. But because the whole perfection of the priest consists in works and in the doctrine of truth, according to that: *Jesus began to do and to teach [n: (Act. I)]*, rightly is it subjoined: *And beneath at the feet of the same tunic [n: (Exod. XXVIII)]*.

*Thou shalt make also a tunic,* etc. [n: (BEDA, ut supra. )] The tunic is called of the superhumeral, etc., *as far as* that from the head even to the feet it ought to be covered by the grace of the virtues.

VERS. 33.-- *And beneath,* etc. [n: (GREG., lib. I Regist., c. 29.)] When the ruler prepares himself to speak, etc., *as far as* you may hold the unity of the faith with wary observance.

[n: (BEDA, ut supra. )] Beneath at the feet of the tunic there are made, [0281B] etc., *as far as* they disclose by the distinct sound of speech.

[n: (ID.)] We can understand in the twelvefold number of the gems of the rational, etc., *as far as* Of the same year David also says: *Thou shalt bless the crown of the year of thy goodness [n: (Psal. LXIV)]*.

[n: (ID.)] Or as was said above, etc., *as far as* in the splendour of divers virtues.

VERS. 35.-- *That it may be heard,* etc. [n: (GREG., ubi supra. )] The priest going in or coming back, etc., *as far as* the very works of the priest may cry out.

[n: (BED., ubi supra. )] But if we will attend to the words of Josephus, etc., *as far as* it might be preached to the parts of the foursquare world.

VERS. 36.-- *Thou shalt make also a plate,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid., c. 7.)] The golden plate on the forehead of the high priest, etc., *as far as* that [0281C] we may confess that she has risen again.

*Holy to the Lord,* etc. [n: (ID.)] That which is bidden to be graven on the plate, etc., *as far as* which he repaired by his passion.

[n: (ID.)] We bear on the forehead the Holy of the Lord graven in gold, etc., *as far as* who ought to show to all an example of virtue.

VERS. 37.-- *And thou shalt tie it.* The plate which contains the Holy of the Lord is tied with a hyacinth fillet to the tiara of the high priest, when by the hope of heavenly rewards, which the hyacinth signifies, we are strengthened in faith, and keep the more closely undefiled the mysteries of our redemption, or the image and likeness of our maker and redeemer, the more we have known that there is no other way of salvation. Of the tiara it shall be said in its own place. [0281D]

VERS. 38.-- *And Aaron shall bear.* The priest bears, namely, the iniquities in the gifts and offerings which the children of Israel have offered and sanctified to the Lord: when for the fruits of worthy penance, that is, for alms and the other works of justice which the penitent do, he absolves them from the guilt of their crimes. These things indeed the divine ordinance figured in the habit of the high priest as to be so performed, and taught manifestly in words. But, alas for the grief! certain men receive offerings, nay rather exact them, and do not carry away nor chastise the iniquities of the people, when for this alone ought they to take carnal things, because they minister spiritual ones. But because he can labour effectually for his subjects, and preach, and beseech the Lord, whose [0282A] mind is always furnished with the memory of the divine name, it is fitly subjoined: And the plate shall be always on his forehead, etc.

*And the plate shall be,* etc. [n: (BED., ubi supra, cap. 8.)] If the plate written with the name of God shall have been always on Aaron's forehead, etc., *as far as* they hasten to earn.

VERS. 39.-- *And thou shalt gird the tunic. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Why the linen one is called drawn tight, etc., *as far as* when it is said: *And thou shalt make a tiara of fine linen,* etc.

[n: (BED., ibid.)] In what manner this was made, etc., *as far as* it may render it harsh and hard to the hearers.

*And the tiara,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] The tiara, which is called cidaris and mitre, etc., *as far as* he might accomplish by the fear of God.

[n: (ID.)] Of the making of the belt, etc., *as far as* and the other garments, when it is said:

[0282B]VERS. 40.-- *But for the sons of Aaron thou shalt prepare linen tunics,* etc. [n: (Ibid.)] But it must be said of the belt of the high priest, etc., *as far as* and faith the girdle of his reins.

*Thou shalt prepare tunics,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] The priests have linen tunics, etc., *as far as* and the smell may be worthily kept for God.
