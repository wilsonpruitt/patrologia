## CHAPTER XXX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Thou shalt make also an altar,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *(BEDA, ubi [0283C] snpra, cap. 11.)* ⟦s3⟧ The commands of the consecration being fulfilled, etc., *as far as* they draw near even in the body.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *An altar. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Fitly also the altar of incense, by as much as it excelled in the brightness of its metal, by so much was it less in size: for the holier, the fewer.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of setim wood. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Fitly of setim wood, like to the white thorn and incorruptible, etc., *as far as* it quickens with the varied grace of gifts.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Having a cubit. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Of one cubit is the length of the altar and the breadth, etc., *as far as* he may spend the office of piety.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Foursquare. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Rightly the altar of incense, etc., *as far as* everlasting gladness shall be theirs.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Horns. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The horns signify the eminence of faith and [0283D] of virtue, etc., *as far as* having the appearance of piety, they deny the power thereof.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *And thou shalt overlay it,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] Fitly moreover it with brass, etc., *as far as* with how great sweetness of God they are refreshed in the hiding of his face.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Both the gridiron,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] Well the gridiron of the altar, etc., *as far as* it is rightly subjoined:

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thou shalt make also to it a crown,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] The altar of incense has a crown round about: in those who are lesser in merits, and dare not openly protest that the reward of the good fight and of servitude and of faith is laid up for them: yet they do all things with the intention of pleasing God well, but with the hope of receiving the reward.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *And two rings,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] There can [0284A] be taken in these rings the four books of the Gospel, etc., *as far as* the things that follow show: *And thou shalt set the altar,* etc.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And he shall burn incense,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid., cap. 12.)] Incense, or thymiama, etc., *as far as* let us render thanks.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] *And he shall burn incense,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In the morning, the sun being risen, all things clearly, etc., *as far as* in a dark place.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In the morning when he shall dress. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* In the morning Aaron the incense, etc., *as far as* and they delight to have leisure for prayers.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *You shall not offer. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* In what follows, etc., *as far as* they water their couch every night.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] We can interpret these altars, etc., [0284B] *as far as* [sic: *uque ad*] it can be taken.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And he shall pray,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] Because Christ our priest, etc., *as far as* he rightly subjoins:

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And he shall appease,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Aaron appeases upon the altar of incense, etc., *as far as* Isaac and Israel, etc.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And the Lord spoke,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The altar of incense being described, there remains the description of the brazen laver, in which the priests about to enter the tabernacle should wash their hands and feet; but one command is set before it.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *When thou shalt take. [n: (BEDA, ubi supra, cap. 13.)]* Of this precept David was forgetful, etc., *as far as* his own riches.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Half a sicle. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* That is, ten obols, etc., *as far as* it can by no [0284C] disturbance of the unbelieving be corrupted.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *He that is counted in the number,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] The number twenty, etc., *as far as* awaits in heaven.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *The rich man shall not add,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] Because whether a man be great in merits and perfect, or little, and set in the advancement of the virtues, upon all alike is laid the law of the decalogue, by which they should love God and their neighbour

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *And the money received,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] This is done when whatsoever good we do, etc., *as far as* it shall be smitten with the stroke of strict vengeance.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *And the Lord spoke,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid., cap. 14.)] There can in this laver or basin, etc., *as far as* which watched at the entrance of the tabernacle.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *With its base. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The base on [0284D] which the basin was set, etc., *as far as:* that you present your bodies, etc. [n: (Rom. XII)].

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To be washed,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Christ also was washed with the water of this laver, before he entered in to offer at the altar, and burned the incense of his sacred body for our salvation on the altar of the cross: for out of love for us he shed tears, as at the raising of Lazarus.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Lest perhaps they die. [n: (BED., ibid.)]* For the death to be feared is the eternal one, etc., *as far as: if* he eats and drinks, etc. [n: (I Cor. XI.)]

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *Take to thee* [n: (STRAB.)] σμύρνα in Greek, in Latin is called myrrh, which is a tree in Arabia of five cubits, whose twigs the Arabians burn up, that they may sprout and bear fruit the better. ⟦s2⟧ From the reek of which [0285A] they would contract incurable diseases, did they not meet it with the smoke of storax. ⟦s3⟧ The gum of this tree is properly called stacte, and by misuse also myrrh.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of cane.* ⟦s2⟧ It is the shoot of the cinnamon, and it grows in India or Ethiopia, of two cubits only. ⟦s3⟧ And it is called cinnamon, because after the manner of a reed it has slender and folded canes, of an ashen or black colour; which, being broken, give back a visible breath, and the slender indeed is precious; but the thick, cheap and despised. ⟦s4⟧ The cane is an aromatic kind near Libanus. ⟦s5⟧ Of myrrh, of cinnamon, of cane, of cassia. ⟦s6⟧ These four kinds signify the four principal virtues, by which with the oil of unction, that is, with the grace of mercy, the Church of God is to be dedicated and sanctified.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *And they shall be,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *(AUG., quaest 135 in [0285B] Exod.)* When, namely, they shall have been anointed. ⟦s3⟧ What then shall now be the difference between those inner things which are covered by the veil?

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He that shall touch.* ⟦s2⟧ In like manner it was said above of the altar of sacrifices, which is so to be understood that it was lawful for the people to touch the tabernacle, when they offered victims, or any gifts whatsoever, and by touching they were sanctified, on account of that oil with which all things were anointed: not as the priests, who also, that they might discharge an eternal priesthood, were anointed from it.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *Onycha.* ⟦s2⟧ Onycha is said to be a very small shellfish of sweet smell, of the size of a human nail: whence also it is called onycha in Greek, because ὄνυξ, onyx, in Greek is called a nail.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Galbanum.* ⟦s2⟧ Galbanum is said to be the juice of a fennel-plant, and the best grows in Syria: it is gristly, [0285C] and less woody, and by its smell serpents are put to flight.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And frankincense.* ⟦s2⟧ Thus in Greek, libanum in Latin it is called, from Libanus a mountain of Arabia: for there is another Libanus, of Phoenicia, in which are the cedars.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Frankincense* the best, namely that which is called masculine, because after the manner of a human testicle it is round, whose tree grows in Arabia, in a withe tangled and twisted according to the quality of the air.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Mystically* moreover these four kinds signify the kinds of the holy virtues.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *And thou shalt make incense,* etc. [n: (GREG., lib. I Moral., cap. 39, tom. I.)] We make incense compounded of spices, etc., *as far as* it does not scatter from itself an odour more subtle.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID.)] Incense, which is compounded of four most odorous kinds ground to the greatest fineness, that is, of stacte, onycha, galbanum, and frankincense, signifies the prayers of the faithful; although these four may see the four elements to signify: so that frankincense, which is bright, may be compared to air, stacte to the waters, galbanum and onyx to earth and to fire, so that by these there may be an incense pleasing to God of all things which are in heaven and beneath heaven, and in the earth, and in the waters

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *With work,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 136.)] We ought not to think that an ointment is made, but incense or frankincense, which is set upon that altar of incense where it was not lawful to sacrifice, and it was within the holy of holies.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *Such,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] We, namely, who ought not to use prayers and divine praises in the flatteries [0286A] of men. ⟦s2⟧ For God alone is so to be served, whence above it is forbidden that the flesh of man be anointed from it.
