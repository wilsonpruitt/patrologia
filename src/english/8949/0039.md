## CHAPTER XXXI.

VERS. 2.-- *Beseleel,* etc., who is interpreted *in the shadow of God.* As if he should say, *The Lord is my light and my salvation, whom [n: (Psal. LXII)]*. [var: Vulg. Ps 26:1 *Dominus illuminatio mea et salus mea: quem timebo?*, the very verse Migne's own next citation names as Psal. XXVI; his *Psal. LXII* points elsewhere] From him David is said to have drawn his origin, and the name of embroiderer, because Beseleel built the tabernacle with embroidered work.

VERS. 3.-- *And I have filled [n: (AUG., quaest. 128.)]* Whether those works are to be attributed to the gift of the Holy Spirit, which seem to pertain to handicraft? or whether this too was said significatively, so that they pertain to the divine Spirit of wisdom and of understanding and of knowledge, which are signified by these [0286B] things? Then also, whereas here he is said to be filled with the Spirit, that man with the divine Spirit of wisdom and of knowledge, the Holy Spirit is not yet read of.

VERS. VI.-- *Oliab,* who is interpreted *my protection,* as though he should say by his name, *The Lord is the protector of my life; of whom shall I be afraid [n: (Psal. XXVI)]*? And well do they build the tabernacle who so constantly trust in the Lord.

[n: (STRAB.)] It is to be noted that in the construction of the tabernacle the mystery of the Trinity is shown, as in the baptism and in the transfiguration. For in the baptism the Father sounded forth in the voice: and the Son was baptized; in the appearance of a dove the Spirit appeared. Ooliab also, who is interpreted *my protection, father,* the person of the Father: Beseleel also, who signifies *in the shadow of God,* the person [0286C] of the Holy Spirit: of whom it is said. *And the power of the Most High shall overshadow thee.* For by the power of the Most High the Holy Spirit is understood. Whence the Lord says: *I know that power has gone out from me [n: (Luc. VIII)]*. But the bidding, that is, the word by which the tabernacle was bidden to be made, is the Son, through whom the Father works all things in the power of the Holy Spirit.

VERS. 13.-- *See,* etc. In vain do the Jews glory in the legal observance. For it is openly said that it is a sign of that which is to come, namely of rest, and of blessedness; or of the rest of the saints in the present, because they rest from the tumult of worldly works and of vices.

VERS. 18.-- *Two tablets [n: (AUG., quaest. 140.)]* Whereas God spoke many things, two tablets only of stone are given to Moses, which are called the tablets of the testimony; [0286D] because all the other things which God commanded are understood to hang upon those ten precepts which were written on the two tablets, if they be diligently sought out and well understood; as those ten themselves upon two, namely the love of God and of the neighbour: on which, namely, *the whole law hangs and the prophets [n: (Matth. XXII)]*. [var: Vulg. Mt 22:40 *In his duobus mandatis universa lex pendet, et prophetae*, "On these two commandments dependeth the whole law and the prophets"; Migne has *tota* for *universa*]

## CHAPTER XXXII.

VERS. 1.-- *Make us,* etc. Gods in the plural, although one calf only was made, after the likeness, namely, of the ox Apis, whom they worshipped in Egypt; but he who cultivates idolatry subjects himself to all vices, and serves as many gods and demons as vices.

VERS. 2.-- *And Aaron said to,* etc. *(AUG., quaest. 141 [0287A] in Exod.)* Aaron is understood to command hard things, that in this way he might call them back from that purpose. Yet that very hard thing was done, that there might be gold for the making of the idol: which I thought was to be noted on account of those who are grieved if they are bidden to have any such thing divinely done for eternal life, or to bear it with an even mind.

VERS. 6.-- *And the people sat down to eat,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ex 32:6 *sedit populus manducare*; Migne prints *comedere*] Eating and drinking drove them to play, and play to idolatry: because if the fault of vanity is not warily restrained, the unwary mind is devoured by iniquity. Whence: *He that despises little things, falls little by little.* [var: Vulg. Sir 19:1 *qui spernit modica paulatim decidet*, "he that despiseth small things shall fall by little and little", a future; Migne has the present *decidit*, and reverses the order of the first two words] For if we neglect to care for small things, being insensibly seduced, we boldly perpetrate even greater ones.

VERS. 8.-- *These are thy gods. [n: (AUG., quaest. 142.)]* [0287B] The Lord, showing to Moses what the people had done concerning the calf and concerning the idol which they had made out of their own gold, says that they had said: These are thy gods, O Israel, who brought thee out of the land of Egypt. Which it is not read that they said; but God showed that this was their mind, for they bore in their heart the sense of these words, which could not lie hid from God.

VERS. 10.-- *Let me alone,* etc. [n: (GREG., lib. IX Moral., cap. 11.)] What is it to say to a servant, etc., *as far as* he could not temper the anger of God for himself.

[n: (ID., lib. XX Moral., cap. 6.)] It can build up the reader according to the history, etc., *as far as* nor mercy of discipline.

VERS. 13.-- *Remember Abraham, Isaac,* etc. He brings in the fathers, who are loved, on behalf of the sons, who offend, [0287C] and do not deserve to be heard; because the good things of the holy fathers often come to the aid of the sons.

VERS. 14.-- *And the Lord was appeased that he would not do the evil.* [n: (AUG.)] By malice he wished punishment to be understood here, as is that: The malice of their going out was esteemed. According to this both good and evil are said to be from God, not according to the malice by which men are evil. For God is not evil: but he brings evils upon the evil, because he is just.

VERS. 19.-- *And being very angry. [n: (ID., quaest. 144.)]* Moses, being angry, seems to have broken the tablets of the testimony written with the finger of God. Yet by a great mystery the repetition of the New Testament was figured, because the old was to be abolished, and the New to be established. It is indeed to be noted with what great supplication he laboured to God for the people, [0287D] who was so severe in taking vengeance upon them.

VERS. 20.-- *And catching the calf which they had made. [n: (ISID. in Exod., tom. V.)]* The body of the devil is signified in the calf, etc., *as far as* he was swallowed up.

*And he gave it to the children of Israel to drink.* The Hebrews hand down that, when the children of Israel drank the water infected with the dust of the calf, those who had committed idolatry bore the dust of the gold upon their beards, by which sign also the guilty or those free of the crime appeared, and the guilty were slain.

VERS. 24.-- *And they gave me,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 145.)] He spoke compendiously, etc., *as far as* he does not refute.

VERS. 25.-- *For Aaron had stripped him unto the ignominy [0288A] of filth and among his enemies,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ex 32:25 *spoliaverat enim eum Aaron propter ignominiam sordis, et inter hostes nudum constituerat*, "for Aaron had stripped him by reason of the ignominy of filth, and had set him naked among his enemies"; Migne has *in ignominiam*] It is to be noted that what the people did is attributed to Aaron himself, because he consented to the doing of what they had ill demanded. For it is rather said, Aaron stripped them, because he yielded to them, than that they stripped themselves, who demanded so great an evil. With the ornaments out of which the idol had been fashioned, either because he had shown their evil will which lay hid before, or because he had lost the help and protection of God.

VERS. 27.-- *Let every man put his sword,* etc. [n: (GREG., Past. p. III, c. 26.)] The sword, etc., *as far as* Whence it is subjoined:

*Let every man kill his brother,* etc. He killed those whom he found to be punished by the sword of rebuke, and he does not spare those whom he loves by kinship of the flesh. If therefore he is called of God who is stirred [0288B] by the zeal of divine love to smite vices, assuredly he denies himself to be of God who, so far as suffices, refuses to rebuke the life of the carnal.

VERS. 28.-- *And they did,* etc. [n: (AUG. in Locutionibus, locut. 124.)] He put the plural for the singular, etc., *as far as* and these manners of speech are interposed by fixed rules.

VERS. 31.-- *Or forgive them,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 147 in Exod.)] He says this indeed securely, etc., *as far as* that he might pour out those words to God on their behalf.

VERS. 35.-- *Which Aaron made. [n: (AUG. quaest. 148.)]* It is asked: since Aaron was said above to have stripped the people, etc., *as far as* that which the Apostle says, crying out: *For how unsearchable are his judgments:* etc. [n: (Rom. XI)]. [var: Vulg. Rom 11:33 *quam incomprehensibilia sunt judicia ejus*, "how incomprehensible are his judgments," and without *enim*; Migne has *inscrutabilia*] [var: Vulg. Ex 32:35 *quem fecerat Aaron*, a pluperfect; Migne has the perfect *fecit*]
