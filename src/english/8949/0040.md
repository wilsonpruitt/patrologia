## CHAPTER XXXIII.

[0288C]

VERS. 1.-- *And he spoke,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 149 in Exod.)] God being angered, etc., *as far as* he terrified them with his voice.

*Go,* etc. [n: (ID., quaest. 150.)] Straightway, as though to Moses himself, etc., *as far as* from the face of the Lord.

VERS. 2.-- *I will send a forerunner. [n: (AUG., quaest. 150.)]* God is with the just by visiting, by showing mercy, and by strengthening; but with the impious by avenging and by punishing. And the sense is this: With these I cannot be for thy sake, so as to avenge and to punish; nor for their sake, so as to visit and to strengthen: I will at least send my angel.

VERS. 4.-- *The people hearing.* [n: (RAB.)] He says that the people heard a most evil [0288D] word, that is, a most cruel and most sorrowful one. For what greater benefit can be bestowed upon anyone than to have God's grace present, and to acknowledge his Maker to be favourable to him; or what greater peril is there than to endure the wrath of his Author? He has nothing of good who shall lack the grace and the presence of his Creator.

VERS. 7.-- *Taking the tabernacle,* etc. This is to be taken by way of prolepsis, if it is taken of that tabernacle which was shown to Moses in the mount, and made, the Lord enjoining it, by Beseleel and Ooliab. Or else it was another tabernacle, to which they were already assembling for judgment.

[0289A]VERS. 8.-- *And when he went out,* etc. [n: (GREG., hom. 13 in Ezech.)] The Hebrew people, freed from Egyptian servitude, etc., *as far as* they endeavour to go forward.

*And they looked,* etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Moses holds the figure of the law. The Jewish people therefore looked upon the back of Moses, because it willed to understand nothing mystical, nothing spiritual in the law, but only the surface of the letter. Therefore his face was hidden, that is, the true and pure knowledge. When he had entered the tabernacle, the pillar of cloud came down and stood at the door. Another rendering says that when Moses wished to enter the tabernacle, the cloud set itself before him and hindered him. Here, however, it is said that when he had entered, the cloud spoke with him before the door. It is to be known, therefore, that he was entering [0289B] some part, namely the court, and then, before he entered into the tabernacle, the cloud set itself against him: in this place, therefore, it signifies those who are less perfect in knowledge and in work, and are not strong enough to penetrate the inmost things of the Scriptures. But elsewhere, when he entered into the midst of the cloud, he held the figure of the perfect, namely of those who search out the secret things of the mystical senses.

*And the Lord spoke.* Scripture speaks according to the opinion of the people, who thought that Moses spoke mouth to mouth with God, whereas he was speaking and appearing to him through a subject creature, that is, through an angel and a cloud. For his substance neither of men nor of angels (as it is) could anyone see, whence: *But to the King of ages, immortal, invisible,* etc. And elsewhere: *He dwelleth in light inaccessible,* etc. *(I Tim. [0289C] I, VI)*.

*[n: (GREG., in I Reg., cap. 10.)] The face of God* is the knowledge of him. Now God is known through a glass, and is known through himself. Through a glass here, through himself in heaven. But the glass is that light which God inhabits; and he himself is that light which he himself is.

*If therefore I have found grace,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 151.)] By these words Moses shows, etc., *as far as* that they may keep themselves clean and inviolate, and may go forward.

VERS. 17.-- *And thee thyself I have known,* etc. *[n: (AUG., quaest. 153.)] Because I know thee above all men,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ex 33:17 reads *ex nomine*, "by name"; Migne prints Augustine's *prae omnibus*, "above all," and the question that follows is put only because of it] Does God know some things more and some things less? etc., *as far as* but it is to be sought more diligently in the earlier [0289D] parts of Scripture whether it be truly so.

VERS. 19.-- *I will show. [n: (AUG., quaest. 154.)]* When Moses had said to the Lord, etc., *as far as* the Lord Christ is called upon among all nations.

*And I will call. [n: (AUG., quaest. 154.)] I will call,* however, he said, not "I shall be called," putting an active verb for a passive, by an unwonted manner of speech, in which perhaps he willed to signify that he himself does this, that is, that it comes about by his grace that the Lord is called upon among all nations.

*And I will have mercy,* etc. [n: (AUG., ubi supra. )] That which he added, etc., *as far as* thus God declared that he would show mercy.

[n: (AUG., lib. de Praedestinatione et Gratia, cap. 10.)] [0290A]That men are called by God with no merits going before, etc., *as far as* if he who called should forsake them.

*Thou shalt not be able,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 154.)] After the commendation of his mercy, etc., *as far as* one must die to this life.

VERS. 20.-- *For not,* etc. [n: (GREG., lib. XVIII Moral., cap. 37, tom. I.)] John also says: etc., *as far as* because satiety is ever kindled out of desire.

VERS. 21.-- *Behold,* he says, *there is a place with me, and thou shalt stand upon,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 154.)] Again, when Scripture interposes the article and says, etc., *as far as* the faithful people stood upon the rock.

*[n: (GREG., lib. XXV Moral., cap. 16.)] There is a place with me,* etc. The place is the Church. The rock is the Lord; Moses, [0290B] the Jewish people, which did not believe the Lord preaching upon earth. It therefore stood upon the rock, beholding the back parts of the Lord as he passed by, because, that is, after the passion and ascension of the Lord, being led within the Church, it deserved to receive Christ by faith, and of him whose presence it did not see, it came to know the hinder parts.

*[n: (GREG. ibid.)] There is a place with me,* etc. The Church, namely, etc., *as far as* Of which solidity it is said: *Upon this rock I will build the Church.*

VERS. 22.-- *And when my glory shall pass,* etc. [n: (EUCHERIUS, lib. de Form. spirit.)] But God is said also to pass over, when out of the hearts of certain men, in which he was before believed by faith, afterwards, faithlessness creeping in or some offence whatever, he withdraws from them and passes over to others: as from the Jews to the Gentiles, [0290C] from heretics to Catholics, and from any irreligious persons whatever to the religious.

*And I will protect* [n: (AUG., ubi supra. )] *and I will cover with my hand over thee,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ex 33:22 *protegam dextera mea, donec transeam*, "I will protect with my right hand, till I pass by"; Migne prints Augustine's wording *tegam manu mea super te*], *as far as* then he weaves in what the Lord says afterwards: *Hew thee two tables of stone,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ex 34:1 *Praecide … tibi duas tabulas lapideas*, "Cut thee two tables of stone"; Migne prints *Excide*, "hew out"]
