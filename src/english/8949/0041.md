## CHAPTER XXXIV.

VERS. 7.-- *Who renderest the iniquity of the fathers to the children and to the grandchildren unto [sic: iu] the third and fourth,* etc., [n: (GREG., lib. XV Moral., cap. 22.)] Since it is written, etc., *as far as* who saw that they were following after evil.

VERS. 14.-- *The Lord is jealous. [n: (AUG., quaest. 158 in Exod.)]* For the Lord God is jealous, etc., *as far as* and he destroys every one that goeth a-fornicating from him.

VERS. 20.-- *The firstling of the ass,* etc. [n: (ISID.)] By [0290D] the ass uncleanness is designated, by the sheep innocence. To exchange, therefore, the firstlings of the ass for a sheep is to turn the first beginnings of an unclean life to the simplicity of innocence, so that, after the sinner has done those things which the Lord rejects as unclean, he may now by his doing bring forth such things as he may lay clean upon the sacrifice of God.

VERS. 21.-- *On the seventh day. [n: (AUG., quaest. 160.)]* When he was giving precept concerning the Sabbath, etc., *as far as* that at every season there must be a resting on the sabbath.

VERS. 24.-- *No man shall lie in wait [n: (AUG., quaest. 161.)]* That which he said, etc., *as far as* where God was to have his tabernacle or temple.

VERS. 25.-- *Thou shalt not immolate,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 162 et 163.)] What is it that he says: Thou shalt not slay upon [0291A] leaven the blood of my immolated things? [var: Vulg. Ex 34:25 *Non immolabis super fermento sanguinem hostiae meae*, "Thou shalt not offer the blood of my victim upon leaven"; Migne's *immolatorum meorum*, "my immolated things," is the very wording the question that follows is built upon] Or does he here call those things his immolated things which are slain at the pasch, and give precept that there be no leaven in the house at that time, when the days of unleavened bread are come?

VERS. 26.-- *Thou shalt not boil a kid in milk.* [n: (AUG., ubi supra. )] Thou shalt not boil a lamb in the milk of its mother, etc., *as far as* but it is a historical narrative.

[n: (ISID.)] Moses fasted forty days, and Elias, and the Lord himself. For it is enjoined upon us out of the law, out of the prophets, and out of the Gospel. Which testimony it has from these. Whence upon the mount the Saviour shone forth in the midst between the two persons, that we may bridle our greediness away from all the allurements of the world as by the fast of temperance, so long as the perfection of the Decalogue is preached through the four parts of that same world, that is, to the whole globe, [0291B] so that ten multiplied four times may signify the number of forty.

VERS. 28.-- *And he wrote upon the tables,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 166.)] It was said of Moses that he himself wrote, etc., *as far as* but by the grace of God.

VERS. 29.-- *And when he came down. [n: (ISID. in Exod., tom. V.)]* Moses went up again into the mount, etc., *as far as* whose image was in the tables.

[n: (ISID. ibid.)] When Moses came down with the tables, etc., *as far as* whence: *Open thou mine eyes, and I will consider the wondrous things of thy law [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*.

VERS. 30.-- *But Aaron seeing,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ex 34:30 *Videntes autem Aaron et filii Israel cornutam Moysi faciem*, "But Aaron and the children of Israel seeing the horned face of Moses"; Migne prints a singular participle, and the gloss beneath it has them see *Moysen*, Moses himself, not his horned face — which is the reading Origen's homily is built upon] [n: (ORIG., hom. 12 in Exod.)] It is written that Aaron and all the children of Israel saw Moses, etc., *as far as* for the things which he speaks have glory, but it is covered and hidden, [0291C] and all his glory is within.

[n: (ORIG. ibid.)] Consider this also, that in the law the countenance of Moses is glorified, although it be covered with a veil, etc., *as far as* that we, freed from the servitude of the world, may seek after the liberty of knowledge.

VERS. 34.-- *And then he spoke to the children of Israel [sic: Isael] all things which had been to him,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 172 in Exod.)] After Moses came down from the mount, etc., *as far as* that they might receive this name.

## CHAPTER XXXV.

VERS. 5.-- *Set apart. [n: (ORIG., hom. 13 in Exod.)]* Another reading: Take from your own selves a redemption for the Lord, etc., *as far as* and thy silver, that is, thy word, tried.

[0291D] [n: (ID.)] You know, who are wont to be present at the divine mysteries, etc., *as far as* all these things which are in us do them.

*Every one.* [n: (ID.)] Another reading: Each one of you, he says, etc., *as far as* so, if the master at his coming find anything of thine in his tabernacle, he claims thee for himself, and calls thee his own.

*Brass.* [n: (ID.)] It seems to be put for strength, etc., *as far as* Let all things therefore be offered to God: sense, speech, and voice.

VERS. 6.-- *Hyacinth.* [n: (ID.)] Let us see the rest: hyacinth, etc., *as far as* and you shall offer firstfruits to the Lord.

*And scarlet twice dyed. [n: (ID. homil. 13 in Exod.)]* Let us see why the scarlet is doubled, etc., *as far as* that [0292A] with the light of knowledge he may mingle a little fire of severity.

[n: (ID.)] If any man worthily understand the going out of the Hebrews from Egypt, etc., *as far as* and it designates nobler garments: *Put you on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh in its delights [n: (Rom. XIII)]*. [var: Vulg. Rom 13:14 *induamini … in desideriis*, "put ye on … in its desires"; Migne prints *Induite vos* and *in deliciis*, "in its delights"]

[n: (GREG., ubi supra. )] In the construction of the tabernacle, etc., *as far as* they gave forth a sound as it were without light.

[n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] After these things the men received from their wives, etc., *as far as* whence: *All men take not this word, but they to whom it is given [n: (Matth. XIX)]*.

VERS. 6.-- *And goats' hair. [n: (ORIG. hom. 19.)]* Goats' hair is offered, etc., *as far as* he who offers to the Lord the skins of rams.

VERS. 21.-- *Whatsoever for the service,* etc. (GREG., *ubi [0292B] supra.* ) In the adorning of the tabernacle the men together with the women offer gifts, etc., *as far as* they are brought forth according as he himself shall have given.

VERS. 27.-- *But the princes offered stones,* etc. [n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] The princes offered their gifts, etc., *as far as* that they may deserve to be set over the peoples.

[n: (ORIG. hom. 9 in Exod.)] The princes offer also oil that shall serve for the lamps and for the chrism, etc., *as far as* when the Lord shall have set thee in a green place, upon the water of refreshment.

VERS. 30.-- *Beseleel. [n: (RAB. in Exod., tom. II.)]* He is called a shadow, etc., *as far as* they strengthen their brethren by exhorting them in justice and judgment.

## CHAPTER XXXVI.

[0292C]VERS. 1.-- *Beseleel therefore made,* etc. [n: (RAB., in Exod. )] It is to be noted that not only from the royal tribe, etc., *as far as* he willed them to belong to the unity of his own body.

VERS. 2.-- *And when he had called them,* etc. [n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] And after the people had offered, Moses, he says, called, etc., *as far as* and oughtest thou to fit it in its own place and bring it forth?

*And they who willingly,* etc. *[n: (AUG., quaest. 170 in Exod.)] And all who of their own will would go to the works, that they might finish them,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ex 36:2 *et qui sponte sua obtulerant se ad faciendum opus*, "and who had of their own accord offered themselves to do the work"; Migne sets the Vulgate lemma and Augustine's *vellent ire ad opera* side by side] Moses made mention of certain men, etc., *as far as* but liberally and of his own accord devoted.

VERS. 4.-- *Whereupon the craftsmen,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 171)]. Note that the wise workers of the works were holy also in their manners: yet they could, if they had willed, have carried off much; but modesty forbade, or religion made them afraid.

## CHAPTER XXXVII.

[0292D]

VERS. 1.-- *And Beseleel made the ark. [n: (ISID. in Exod., tom. V.)]* In the ark of the testament there was a golden urn, etc., *as far as* flourishing always with the greenness of immortal memory.

VERS. 2.-- *A golden crown,* etc. Because in that the Church is spread abroad through the four parts of the world, she is preached as girt about with the four books of the holy Gospel, which nevertheless is enclosed within the compass of one crown, that is, within the firmness of the same faith, that is, its unity.

VERS. 4.-- *The bars also. [n: (ISID., ibid.)]* Because strong and persevering teachers are to be sought out as timbers that cannot rot, who, ever cleaving to the instruction of the sacred volumes, [0293A] may proclaim the unity of the Church, and, as it were with rings let in, may carry the ark; and they are commanded to be overlaid with gold also, that while they sound in the ears of others with their word, they themselves may also shine with the splendour of their life.

VERS. 6.-- *He made also the propitiatory,* etc. [n: (ISID., ibid.)] The propitiatory above the ark of the testament, etc., *as far as* because Christ is the head of the Church.

VERS. 17.-- *And he made the candlestick,* etc. Which signifies the Holy Spirit, who with sevenfold grace enlightens the Church that stands in the unity of faith; or Christ bearing the seven churches, in which the splendour of the sevenfold Spirit shines forth.

VERS. 23.-- *With the snuffers.* Which in Isaias are called tongs: namely the two Testaments, by which sins are purged, and which are joined together within themselves by the union of the Holy [0293B] Spirit.

## CHAPTER XXXVIII.

VERS. 8.-- *He made also a brazen laver,* etc. [n: (GREG., hom. 17 in Evang.)] Moses sets a brazen laver, etc., *as far as* that we may strive to go forward by those things by which we know that they whom we venerate went forward.

VERS. 20.-- *The pins also.* [n: (STRAB.)] The pins, according to the letter, were fixed at the top of the pillars, to which the cords were bound: which cords raised the curtains from the ground, and, being made fast above by certain little loops but below bound to the aforesaid pins, stretched out the curtains, that they might not draw into a wrinkle.

But mystically the cords signify Holy Scripture, of which Solomon says: *A threefold cord is hardly broken* [0293C] [n: (Eccle. IV)]: because Holy Scripture, consisting of history, morality and allegory, can be broken asunder by no doctrines of heretics. The pins, made after the manner of a tongue, signify the tongues of the teachers who expound Scripture. They raise the curtains also from the ground and stretch them out: because they teach their subjects to follow and to imitate heavenly things, and to draw no wrinkle of evil work or of deceit [n: (Ephes. V)].
