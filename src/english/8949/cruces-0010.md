## 1. ⭐ THE HEADLINE FINDING — Rabanus's Decalogue is not quoted from Exodus 20

Rabanus's running series of precepts (*secundum … tertium … quartum … quintum … sextum …
septimum … octavum … nonum … decimum praeceptum*) is the spine of this whole range: each
commandment is matched to its answering plague. **Migne's wording for seven of the ten does not
agree with the Decalogue as Exodus 20 prints it**, and two of them agree instead with a
different book. Collated against `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt`:

| col | Migne prints | Ex 20 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0205D | *Non accipies nomen Dei tui in vanum* | 20:7 *Non assumes nomen **Domini** Dei tui in vanum* | `[var:]` — verb changed, *Domini* dropped |
| 0207A | *Memento diem sabbati sanctificare* | 20:8 *Memento **ut** diem sabbati **sanctifices*** | `[var:]` — infinitive for *ut* + subjunctive |
| 0207D | *Honora patrem tuum et matrem tuam* | 20:12, exact | agrees; no marker |
| 0209D | *Non mœchaberis* (plate) | 20:14 *Non moechaberis*, exact | agrees; see §3 |
| 0210B | *Non occides* | 20:13, exact | agrees; no marker |
| 0211A | *Non furaberis* | 20:15 *Non furtum facies* | `[var:]` — and see below |
| 0214C | *Falsum testimonium non dices* | 20:16 *Non loqueris contra proximum tuum falsum testimonium* | `[var:]` |
| 0215D | *Non concupisces uxorem proximi tui* | 20:17 *Non concupisces **domum** proximi tui, nec desiderabis uxorem ejus* | `[var:]` — and see below |
| 0220A | *Non concupisces rem proximi tui* | 20:17 (as above) | `[var:]` |

⭐ **Two of these are the brief's own warned case — a ✓ against the wrong address.** The
pre-scan marked *Non furaberis* `✓ Rom 13:9` and *Non concupisces uxorem proximi tui* `✓ Dt 5:21`.
Both ticks are real and both are the finding: Rabanus is enumerating **Exodus's** Decalogue, and
Migne's wording at those two places is **Romans 13:9's** and **Deuteronomy 5:21's** respectively.
`Non furtum facies` (Ex 20:15) and `furtumque non facies` (Dt 5:19) are the two forms the Decalogue
actually carries; *Non furaberis* is neither, and it is Paul's.

**Every one of these was read at the plate** (f103 c1, f104 c1/c2, f105 c1, f106 c1, f107 c2,
f108 c1, f110 c2) and is Migne's setting, not a transcription artifact. The class looks like a
catechetical short form of the Decalogue circulating in Rabanus's source rather than a series of
compositor's slips: the divergences are consistent in kind (abbreviation, and NT phrasing), and
the two that agree with Exodus agree with it exactly.

## 2. `[var:]` markers standing outside the Decalogue class

**0207A — Isaiah 66:2, and the gloss rests on the word Migne prints.** Migne:
*Super quem requiescet Spiritus meus nisi super humilem et **quietum**?* The Clementine has
nothing of the sort — *ad quem autem respiciam, nisi ad pauperculum, et contritum spiritu, et
trementem sermones meos*: a different verb and different objects. The exposition immediately
following turns on *quietum*: *Inquieti ergo a Spiritu sancto resiliunt … nec admittunt quietem
sabbati spiritualis.* Conform the lemma to the Vulgate and the argument loses its keyword.
Plate-confirmed on f104 c1 (col 207, band A). This is 7a″'s founding shape exactly.

**0210D — Romans 9:20, and Migne prints the verse twice, differently.** He sets
*O homo, **tu qui es** qui respondeas Deo (Rom. IX)*, which is not a question and has no main
predicate; **eleven lines below, at 0211A, he sets the same verse correctly as
*O homo, **quis es** qui respondeas Deo.*** Both readings were read at the plate on f105 c2 (band D)
and f106 c1 (band A) — two separate columns, two separate crops. Rendered literally the page reads
"O man, thou who art, who repliest against God", which loses Paul's rebuke; the `[var:]` carries the
received interrogative beside it. `[sic:]` does not reach this (*qui* is a real word) and Pattern 18
`[cj:]` is explicitly excluded inside a quoted scripture, so Pattern 14 is the right home.
⚠ Under 7a⁗ question 3, a swallowed interrogative converts a question into a claim: the English
therefore does **not** quietly restore the question, and the note is what discloses it.

**0214C/D — Galatians 5:15, and again the gloss follows Migne.** Migne prints
*Si mordetis et **consumitis**, videte ne ab invicem consumamini (Gal. v)* where the Vulgate has
*comeditis*. Two lines above, Rabanus's own gloss reads *Falsus enim testis nocet mordendo et
**consumit** mentiendo* — the exposition's verb is Migne's, not the Vulgate's. Plate-confirmed on
f107 c2 (col 214, bands C–D).

**0217A — Isaiah 7:15, whose second clause is wholly different.** Migne:
*Butyrum et mel manducabit; **priusquam faciat aut proferat maligna, eliget bonum** (Isa. VII)*.
The Clementine: *Butyrum et mel comedet, **ut sciat reprobare malum, et eligere bonum***. Origen's
own continuation picks up Migne's word — *quoniam **priusquam** sciat puer bonum aut malum* — so
again the argument stands on the printed reading. Plate-confirmed on f109 c1 (col 217, band A).

## 3. ⛔ CANDIDATES WITHDRAWN AT THE PLATE — three, and each would have been a false claim

These are recorded so nobody re-opens them from the Latin alone. All three look like defects in
our TEI twin and are not.

**(a) 0209D `maechaberis` — the plate prints the œ ligature.** Our twin reads
*Non **mae**chaberis*, which against Ex 20:14's *moechaberis* is a live divergence and, on the
ae/oe axis, a live Pattern 9 argument. **The plate (f105 c1, band D, native-resolution crop) reads
`Non mœchaberis`** — Migne's own ligature, which our transcription has read as `ae`. So there is no
divergence from the Vulgate at all: Migne agrees with Ex 20:14 exactly. The English renders the
plate ("Thou shalt not commit adultery"), **no `[var:]` and no `[sic:]` fires**, and the TEI is
filed for patching. ⚑ The same page prints *quaedam **mœ**chia* at 0215D, where our twin correctly
has `oe` — so the misreading is local to one sort, not systematic.

**(b) 0207A `circum venit` — a word broken at a line end, with no hyphen.** Our twin sets
*et tanta calliditate **circum venit**, ut deceptus nec videat…* — a split run that satisfies
Pattern 10 exactly and would have taken a `[sic: *circum venit*]`. The plate (f104 c1, band A)
breaks **circum | venit** across a line end with **no hyphen**: it is one word, *circumvenit*.
This is the class CLAUDE.md names as "ours and it reads as Migne's", and the 8957 `Per eat`/*Pereat*
precedent is the same shape. **Marker withdrawn**; rendered "encompasses".

**(c) 0214D `[n: (ORIG.)] hom. 4 in Exod.)` — the unbalanced parenthesis is MIGNE'S.** Our twin
prints an italic run containing a closed `(ORIG.)` followed by `hom. 4 in Exod.)` with a second
closing parenthesis and no opener — which reads unmistakably as a Corpus Corporum markup failure,
and I had it drafted as a TEI patch. **The plate (f107 c2, band D) sets it exactly so:
`(ORIG.) *hom. 4 in Exod.*)`.** Migne's own defective punctuation. Pattern 8 governs: preserve,
never repair. **No patch, no marker**; the citation rides untranslated as printed.

## 4. Candidates examined and DECLINED (with the reason, so they are not re-raised)

- **0208B `Digitus Dei hic est`, word order.** Migne inverts *est hic* here alone; the same clause
  prints *Digitus Dei est hic* five other times in my range (0207B, 0207C, 0212A, 0212D, 0217A) and
  Ex 8:19 has *est hic*. **Plate-confirmed as Migne's** (f104 c2, band B). Both readings are sound
  Latin and the sense is identical; **English has no exponent for Latin word order**, so rendering
  the two differently would invent a distinction Migne does not make. Both render "This is the finger
  of God" and the divergence is recorded here. Same logic as Pattern 9's gender rule.
- **0210D `cui vult miseretur` for Rom 9:18 `cujus vult`.** Plate-confirmed. *Misereor* takes the
  dative in late Latin as readily as the genitive; the referent and the sense are unchanged, and no
  agency is mis-bound. No marker.
- **0212C `Ignem veni mittere in terra` for Lc 12:49 `in terram`.** Plate-confirmed (f106 c2, band C).
  A single letter, and the ablative for the accusative of goal; English "in the earth" carries the
  printed case. Below the Pattern 14 bar (no sense change), and a single-letter call is exactly the
  class the 9004 measurement warns against. Crux only.
- **0212C, the doubled Luke reference.** Migne prints `de quo dicit Dominus Luc. XII :` in the
  running text **and** `(Luc. XII)` as a parenthetical after the quotation. Plate-confirmed. This is
  his ordinary citing habit (attribution in prose plus locator note), not dittography — Pattern 11
  concerns a repeated *run of text*. No `[d:]`.
- **0207C `Jamnes` for *Jannes*, and `mente corrupti` for 2 Tim 3:8 `corrupti mente`.** Both
  plate-confirmed (f104 c1, band C). *Jamnes* is an attested form of the name; the inversion changes
  nothing and nothing in the gloss depends on it. Also *Sicut* opens the quotation where the Vulgate
  has *Quemadmodum autem*, which is the ordinary trimming of a quotation introduced by *Unde*.
- **0217D `Nisam` for *Nisan*.** Plate-confirmed (f109 c1, band D), with `Hic est **Nisan**` printed
  eight lines below in the next gloss. Tempting, and declined: it is a **proper name**, so there is no
  lexicon against which it fails, and the 8950 `Bethehem`/`Bethlehem` precedent is directly on point —
  crux only, no `[sic:]`, no patch.
- **0205D `qui loquitur mendacium, de suo loquitur` (Jo 8:44 `cum … ex propriis`).** Migne's *de suo*
  is what makes Rabanus's antithesis work (*de Deo loquitur … de suo loquitur*); it is an adaptation,
  carries no citation, and the sense is identical. Declined.
- **0206A `Fecerunt autem malefici` (Ex 8:7 `Fecerunt autem et malefici`).** One connective, in an
  abridged lemma closing with *etc.* Plate-confirmed. Declined.
- **0215A `Moses` for *Moyses* in the lemma of Ex 10:13.** Migne spells the name both ways throughout
  the range (*Moyses* at 0210A, *Moses* at 0211C and here). His own variance; no marker.
- **0218D `poenitus` for *penitus*.** An oe/e spelling, Pattern 9. No marker.
- **Origen's and Augustine's scripture is quoted from their own Old Latin throughout** (0210C
  *Et induravit Dominus cor Pharaonis regis Aegypti et servorum ejus*; 0212C–D the whole sequence
  *Orate pro me Dominum*, *Euntes immolate*, *Peccavi ante Dominum Deum vestrum*, *Ite et servite*,
  *Surgite, exite de populo meo*; 0213A Gen 31:42 *nunc me inanem dimisisses* for *forsitan modo
  nudum me*; 0215B Ex 10:12; 0219D Ex 12:22 *ex sanguine qui est juxta ostium*; 0219B Augustine's
  *aeternum vel aeternalem*). **Marking each would put a `[var:]` on every patristic quotation in the
  book.** The class is recorded here once; no markers fired. Where a father's citation is instead the
  thing his argument turns on, it is treated above (0219B, §5).

## 5. Migne's mis-bound references — his, and therefore untouched

`[n: …]` contents are Migne's citations and ride verbatim on both sides, so none of these takes a
marker. They are recorded because a reader will otherwise think the fault is ours.

- **0219C — `[n: (Gal. III)]` on a quotation of Romans 6:3.** Migne prints
  *Quicunque baptizati sumus in Christo Jesu, in mortem ipsius baptizati sumus, etc. (Gal. III).*
  That is Rom 6:3; Gal 3:27 reads *Quicumque enim in Christo baptizati estis, Christum induistis*.
  Plate-confirmed (f110 c1, band C). ⚑ Migne's *in mortem* (accusative) against the Clementine's
  *in morte* is a divergence that runs **toward** the Greek εἰς τὸν θάνατον, so it degrades nothing
  and takes no `[var:]`.
- **0221A — `[n: (Matth. VI)]` on a quotation of Matthew 19:21.** Plate-confirmed (f111 c1, band A).
  The cause is visible on the page: two lines above, the gloss alludes to Mt 6:19–20
  (*ne thesaurizes ubi tinea exterminat, et fures effodiunt*), and the Mt 6 locator has migrated onto
  the Mt 19 quotation that follows. ⚑ The quotation also reads *vade et vende **omnia** quae habes*
  where Mt 19:21 has *vade, vende quae habes* — the *omnia* is Luke 18:22's. Conflation, not a
  sense-damaging divergence; recorded, no marker.
- **0218B — `[n: (I Petr. II)]` on *pro peccato mortuus est, cum peccatum non haberet*.** Nearer
  1 Pet 3:18 with 2 Cor 5:21 than 1 Pet 2:22. Migne's citation; recorded only.

## 6. Renderings decided, and why

- ⭐ **`ingravare` = "make heavy", `indurare` = "harden", kept apart throughout.** This is not a
  stylistic choice: at 0209B Augustine's whole point is the difference between *Ingravatum est cor
  Pharaonis* and *ingravavit Pharao cor suum*, and at 0210C–0213B Origen builds an eight-plague
  argument on *induratum esse* versus *indurasse Dominum*, closing at 0213B where Augustine glosses
  *Ego enim **induravi** cor ejus* with *dicitur, **ingravavi** cor ejus*. Collapsing both into
  "hardened" — which is what Douay does at Ex 8:32 — would make three separate expositions
  unintelligible. Applied uniformly across 0010–0014.
- **`sciniphes` → "sciniphs"** (the received English of the Douay at Ex 8:16), not italic Latin:
  an English exponent exists, so Pattern 7's carry-through does not apply.
- **`cynomyia` kept in italic Latin**, with "dog-fly" beside it where Migne himself glosses it
  (*id est, canina musca*). The word is the Septuagint's and the gloss at 0208A is explicitly about
  the Seventy's choice of it, so an English-only rendering would erase what is being discussed.
- **`calamita`, `rubeta` rendered "the calamite", "the bramble-frog".** Migne's own etymology
  (*eo quod in vepribus agat*) is preserved by the English form; the plate sets both roman.
- **0210B `Neque enim nihil.`** Rendered "For neither is it nothing." — both negations carried, per
  7a, though the idiom is a litotes.
- **0216D `Nec non locustarum ab ea subducit exempla`.** Rendered "Nor does it fail to draw off…" —
  the doubled negative carried rather than flattened into "and also".
- **Pattern 17 applied throughout:** *thou/thee/thy* for every singular address (0207A–B
  *Esto ergo mansuetus… vacare non vis*; 0209D *si pecus es*; 0210B *Si posses videre*; 0211A
  *Dum furaris*; 0220B *Cum laudas… puniris*), *you* only where the Latin is plural (0212D
  *Peccavi ante Dominum Deum vestrum et in vos*).
- **0219C anchor placement.** Our twin sets `[0219C]` inside *Christi mortem [0219C] figurante*.
  English word order cannot hold the anchor between those two words; it is placed at the nearest
  construction boundary ("…begun in baptism, [0219C] that figures the death of Christ"), in order and
  in the same sentence.
- **0215C `*Posuit* enim Deus *tenebras latibulum suum*`.** Two italic runs with roman *enim Deus*
  between them, which English word order cannot reproduce; rendered as two adjacent italic runs after
  "For God". Both runs preserved, in order.
- **0219D — a supplied question mark removed.** A first draft closed Augustine's third alternative
  (*An, quod est credibilius, ideo dixit…*) with a question mark, matching the parallel *An praecipit
  … occidatur?* above it. **The plate ends it with a full stop** (f110 c2, band A), so it stands as a
  statement opening with "Or" — Pattern 8, never supply. Question-mark counts are now exact against
  the twin in all five chunks (2/2, 4/4, 0/0, 1/1, 3/3).

## 8. ⛔ TWO ITEMS FOR MERGE-TIME RECONCILIATION — they cross stint boundaries

Neither of these is mine to settle alone, and both are invisible to `verify-english`.

### 8a. *praeceptum* is being rendered two ways inside one enumerated series

Rabanus's Decalogue series **begins in chunk 0009 and ends in chunk 0014**, so it is split across
two stints, and the two halves do not agree:

| chunk | stint | *praeceptum* rendered |
|---|---|---|
| 0000 | peer | **precept** (1) |
| 0002 | peer | **commandment** (3) |
| 0009 | peer | **commandment** (3) — *Primum praeceptum de colendo uno Deo* |
| 0010–0014 | this stint | **precept** (14) |

As it stands the reader meets "the **first commandment** is concerning the worship of one God"
at 0205B and then, three lines later on the facing column, "the **second precept**" — the same
series, the same word, two Englishes. That is drift of exactly the kind the merge step exists to
catch (11551's *stylus*, 11063's sense labels, 10804's *amica mea*).

**Recommendation, and the reason is not taste:** render *praeceptum* = **precept** and
*mandatum* = **commandment**, because Migne's Latin uses both and one sentence in my range needs
them apart — 0206C *legem significat, quae mundum decem plagis **mandatorum** corripit* stands
eleven lines from 0205D *Secundum **praeceptum***. Collapsing both into "commandment", which is
what 0002 and 0009 do, loses a distinction the plate makes. ⚠ **This is a recommendation to the
merge, not a unilateral call**, and I have not edited a peer's chunk. *praeceptum* occurs in
**22 of the 44 chunks**, most of them still untranslated, so whichever way it is settled should
be settled once and carried forward.

### 8b. The first precept diverges too, and it is in a peer's chunk

The Decalogue divergence class in §1 above is **incomplete as it stands**, because its first member
falls just outside this stint's range. At **0205B** (chunk 0009) Migne prints
*Primum praeceptum de colendo uno Deo: **Non erunt tibi dii alii praeter me***, where Ex 20:3 reads
*Non habebis deos alienos coram me* — a different verb and a different phrase, the same shape as the
seven divergences in my table. **Chunk 0009 fires no `[var:]` there**, which is entirely reasonable
from inside that stint: the pattern is only visible once several precepts stand together, and the
0009 stint had exactly one of them.

**No action taken here** — 0205B is a peer's column and, on the ratchet discipline, firing a marker
on a column I read only for its band-C/D content would be the wrong kind of tidying. It is recorded
so that the merge can decide whether the class should be marked uniformly across the two chunks or
left to the crux, and so that the class is not later reported as a fresh discovery.
