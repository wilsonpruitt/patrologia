## §3 · The 7a″ collation — ranked by whether the gloss is BUILT on the divergence

### Tier 1 — the gloss expounds a word its own lemma does not print

**0243A · Isa 65:2 (Rom 10:21).** Migne prints *Extendi manus ad populum non credentem* and cites
*Isa. LXV*. The Clementine's Is 65:2 reads *Expandi manus meas tota die ad populum incredulum*, and
Rom 10:21, whose second half the printed words match exactly, reads *Tota die expandi manus meas ad
populum non credentem*. **Neither witness prints *extendi*, and the whole gloss turns on that verb:**
three words earlier Origen writes *Moyses elevat manus, **non extendit**; Jesus … dicit: Extendi
manus*. The contrast lifting-vs-stretching is the exposition, and it rests on a verb the received
text does not have. `[var:]` fired. ⚑ The 0015–0019 stint reports the same *Isa. LXV* address
carrying Rom 10:21's wording at 0230B; **this is the same site glossed a second time, with a third
verb.**

**0246B · Eccl 1:8.** Migne prints *Non satiabitur oculus visu, nec auris auditu*; the Clementine
reads *Non saturatur oculus visu, nec auris auditu impletur*. The very next clause of the gloss is
*nec nos **satiari** possumus considerando quae scripta sunt* — **the argument's verb is taken from
the printed form**, and a rendering conformed to *saturatur* would leave the gloss echoing nothing.
`[var:]` fired.

**0248B–C · Ecl 9:8, 2Cor 7:1, Act 11:9 — three in one paragraph, all in the same direction.**
Origen is arguing that no one comes to the word of God but *lotis vestibus*, in clean garments, and
Migne's three proof-texts have all been pulled toward *mundus*:
- *In omni tempore sint vestimenta tua **munda*** against Ecl 9:8 *candida*, "white";
- *Purificatus es corpore, **mundatus** ab omni inquinamento* against 2Cor 7:1 *mundemus nos*, a
  first-person exhortation turned into a second-person statement of fact;
- *Quae ergo Deus **mundavit**, tu **immunda** ne feceris* against Act 11:9 *tu ne commune dixeris*,
  "do not call common" (Act 10:15 has *Quod Deus purificavit*).
Three `[var:]`. Taken singly each is modest; taken together they are the argument, and the third is
the strongest, because *immunda ne feceris* replaces a verb of **speech** with a verb of **making**
in a passage about washing.

**0239D · Ps 55:5.** Migne prints *Non timebo quid faciat caro*; the Clementine has *quid faciat
**mihi** caro*. Augustine's locution then quotes the SAME psalm two clauses later as *quid faciat
**mihi** homo* — **so the *mihi* Migne drops in the first quotation is retained in the second, on
the same line, in a gloss whose whole point is the pairing of the two.** `[var:]`.

### Tier 2 — Migne's address names one place and his printed words are another's

**0241D · Zach 13:7 / Mt 26:31.** Migne prints *Unde **Zach. VIII**: Percutiam pastorem, et
dispergentur oves gregis.* Zach 13:7 reads *percute pastorem, et dispergentur oves* — an imperative,
and no *gregis*. The printed words are **Mt 26:31 verbatim**. The gloss depends on the divergence in
its own way: *percussus enim Christus, et in cruce mactatus, Novi Testamenti fontes produxit* needs
the first-person future ("I will strike"), which is the evangelist's form, not the prophet's.
`[var:]` naming the Zacharias reading. (The chapter number is Migne's own and rides as printed.)

**0246A–B · Mt 12:42 / Lc 11:31.** Migne prints *Matth. XII: Regina quoque austri surget in judicio
**cum viris generationis hujus**, et condemnabit **eam***. Mt 12:42 reads *cum generatione ista … et
condemnabit eam*; *cum viris generationis hujus* is **Luke's** (Lc 11:31), which then closes
*condemnabit **illos***. So the printed sentence is a conflation that belongs to neither address
whole. `[var:]`. This is the class the 0015–0019 stint names, and it is live twice more in my range
in a form that takes no marker (§7).

**0249A · Lc 8:10 / Is 6:9 — and it is a NEGATION.** Migne prints *ut videntes non videant, et
audientes **nos** intelligant*. Lc 8:10 reads *et audientes **non** intelligant*; the Clementine's
Is 6:9, which is the address Migne gives, reads something else again (*Audite audientes, et nolite
intelligere*). The printed *nos* is a real word, so Pattern 12 has nothing to wrap and the English
renders it as printed: "and hearing they might understand us." **Negation fidelity cuts the other
way here** — the Latin prints no *non* at that point, so the English carries none, and the `[var:]`
is what tells the reader the received text has one. ⛔ See §6: **the two copies of PL 113 differ at
this word**, and that is why no `[sic:]` was fired.

### Tier 3 — real divergences, sense affected, gloss not built on them

- **0235A · 1Cor 10:9–11.** *Neque murmuraveritis, sicut quidam eorum murmuraverunt, et a serpentibus
  perierunt. Scripta sunt haec propter nos, in quos fines saeculorum devenerunt.* v. 10 ends *et
  perierunt ab exterminatore*; *a serpentibus perierunt* is v. 9, about tempting, not murmuring; and
  v. 11 opens *scripta sunt autem ad correptionem nostram*. A three-verse conflation quoted as one.
  It matters because the gloss immediately threatens *mittentur in eos serpentes*.
- **0236A · Os 3:4.** *sine rege, sine principe, **sine hostia**, sine sacrificio, **sine sacerdote***
  against *et sine altari, et sine ephod, et sine theraphim*. Migne's list has a victim and a priest
  the prophet's has not, and drops three items the prophet's has.
- **0237A · Is 66:24.** *Vermis eorum non **moritur*** under the address *Isa. LXVI*, where Isaiah has
  the future *non morietur*; the present is the form of Mc 9:43–47. ⚑ Worth noting that the same
  commentary then prints *vermis qui **non morietur*** at 0240B — Migne carries both tenses.
- **0237D · Ps 50:19.** *Cor contritum et humiliatum Deus non **spernit*** against *Deus, non
  **despicies***: a third-person statement where the psalm addresses God in the second person.
- **0237D · Mt 9:29.** *Fiat tibi secundum fidem tuam* against the plural *Secundum fidem vestram,
  fiat vobis*. The gloss applies it to the single reader (*fiat tibi quodcunque desideras*), so the
  singular is doing work, but the singular is also the form of Mt 8:13 and 15:28 and may be memory.
- **0238A · Ps 36:8.** *derelinque **indignationem*** against *derelinque **furorem***.
- **0238A · Ps 112:7.** *Dominus **allevat** de terra inopem, et de stercore **erigit** pauperem*
  against the participles *Suscitans … erigens*, and with a subject supplied that the psalm has not.
- **0238C · 1Jo 2:18.** ***Pueri**, novissima hora est* against *Filioli*.
- **0241C · Ps 41:3.** *Sitivit anima mea **ad te, Deus*** against *ad Deum fortem, vivum*.
- **0243B · Is 35:3.** *Remissas manus et genua debilia erigite* under the address *Isa. XXXV*, where
  Isaiah reads *Confortate manus dissolutas, et genua debilia roborate*. The printed words are nearest
  **Hbr 12:12** (*remissas manus, et soluta genua erigite*), and even there *debilia* is Isaiah's.
- **0245A · Gn 3:10.** *Audivi, Domine, vocem tuam, et abscondi me, quia eram nudus* against *Vocem
  tuam audivi in paradiso, et timui, eo quod nudus essem, et abscondi me* — the fear and the paradise
  both gone, and a vocative *Domine* supplied.

## §4 · The two markers that are claims about Migne

**0246C · `[sic: *accepti*]` — and it is a `[sic:]`, not a `[cj:]`.** The plate prints
*sed Moyses, qui erat mansuetus super omnes homines (Num. XII), acce-|pti consilium inferioris, ut
principibus daret formam humilitatis*. **Both witnesses agree**: Gallica sets `pti` at the head of
the next line, unmistakable at 10×, and the archive layer prints `accepti`. *accepti* is a real
Latin form (gen. sg. / nom. pl. of *acceptus*) — but it has **no grammatical slot here**: strip it
and the sentence from *sed Moyses* to *daret* has no finite verb at all. That is Pattern 12's first
test for a real word, so the type is carried and, under the 2026-09-05 amendment, the sense is
rendered beside it: `received [sic: *accepti*] the counsel of an inferior`. Conjecture: *accepit*.
⚑ **This is only the second `[sic:]` in the book** (the 0015–0019 stint has 0224D `ververba`), and
it is fired against a page on which no other reading was doubtful and neither copy is worn.

**0245D · `[cj: *indicabunt*; read *judicabunt*, "shall judge"]`.** The plate prints *Ordinabis,
inquit, eos tribunos et centuriones, et **indicabunt** populum omni hora*; at 4× the word opens with
a clean `in` and no descender, and the archive layer prints *indicabunt* too — **so it is Migne's,
not ours.** It is a real word that renders in place ("they shall point out the people"), so
Pattern 12 has nothing to wrap. But the gloss two lines below is *qui debent omni hora populum
**judicare**, semper et sine intermissione sedere in judicio*, and Ex 18:22 LXX is *κρινοῦσιν*. The
printed word therefore makes the sentence say what Origen is arguing against, which is Pattern 18's
bar; the gloss is additive and Migne's word keeps its English.

## §6 · Declined, and the reasons

- **0236A `panis coelestis. qui`** and **0238A `abscondit. vermes`** — Gallica shows a tailless
  point where our twin has a comma, on two consecutive leaves. The archive prints a comma at both.
  Two witnesses split; our twin sides with the archive; **no patch, and no `[sic:]` either way.** The
  finding that matters is that **f118 and f119 under-ink comma tails**, so every other call made on
  those two leaves was re-weighed.
- **0249A `nos intelligant` — the two copies of PL 113 are two printings here.** Gallica prints
  *non videant … **nos** intelligant*; the archive, **rendered as an image at 500 dpi**, prints
  *non **vident** … **non** intelligant*. Two words differ on one line, **both impressions clean**,
  and our twin agrees with Gallica at both. There is no one to accuse: no `[sic:]`, no patch. The
  English renders the reading our twin and Gallica share and a `[var:]` carries the received text.
  ⛔ This is the PL 114 "two printings, not two photographs" class appearing in PL 113, and it should
  be weighed before any future letter-level call in this tome is settled from one copy.
- **0243B `non tam manum et armis`** — plate-confirmed. An accusative where the sense wants an
  ablative (*manu*), beside a correctly ablative *armis*. Declined: a case slip that renders in place
  ("not so much with hand and with arms") and asserts nothing false. Pattern 18's ruling explicitly
  keeps mood, voice, number and person anomalies out unless a negation or correlative is at stake.
- **0239B `murmuranti et tentati Synagogae`** — plate-confirmed. *tentati* fails to agree with the
  dative *Synagogae*. Same class, same decline; English has no exponent for it.
- **0247C `ac per hoc in ista umbra futuri secundum agni immolati diem festum;`** — plate-confirmed,
  a clause with no verb, closing on a semicolon. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow and
  not repaired. Nothing is missing from our twin; the ellipsis is Migne's.
- **0240C `*(AUG., ibid., quaest. 62.*`** — the plate genuinely opens this attribution and never
  closes it. Same class as the book's one unclosed guillemet at 0192A, which the lemma brief forbids
  us to repair. Reproduced verbatim, untranslated; no marker, no patch.
- **0244A `exonustare`** and **0241C `sites`** (plural of *sitis*) — plate-confirmed, both attested
  medieval forms. Pattern 9: translated normally, no marker.
- **Migne's own citation slips, all plate-confirmed, all left to ride as printed under the
  `refDisplay` principle:** *psal. I* at 0245A for Ps 50:6 (*Tibi soli peccavi*); *psal. XIX* at
  0244A for Ps 9:7; *Matth. XII* at 0246A for Mt 5:22 (*Reus erit gehennae ignis*); *(Matth. XII)*
  at 0242D appended to a quotation the text itself introduces as *Joan. XV*; *Isa. VI* at 0249A for
  what is Lc 8:10's wording; *(Matth. IV)* at 0238A for what is nearest Ps 102:3. **A wrong address
  beside right words is not a `[var:]`** — Pattern 14 is about the quoted wording. Where both the
  address and the wording are in play the marker fired (0241D, 0246A–B, 0249A).
- **0238B–0239A `Ascendent in montem, et bibent vinum, unguento ungentur.`** — plate-confirmed, and
  not traced to any Clementine verse. Recorded unidentified rather than forced onto Amos 6:6.
- **Band-letter arithmetic and repeated column numbers** — not reported (runbook false positive 1).
  Every chunk boundary in the range was checked at the plate for text broken mid-word or
  mid-construction and **all five are clean**: 0019/0020 falls on a verse address at 0234D,
  0024/0025 falls immediately before the CAPUT XX head at 0249C.

## §7 · Two things about this range worth carrying forward

**The fount's 3 reads as a 5 on the Gallica copy, and the archive copy is the corrective.** On f122
the plate's `VERS. 12, 13` renders as `12, 15` while its genuine `VERS. 12-15` renders identically.
Any numeral call in this tome needs the second witness, and the archive's OCR *text layer* — not
just its image — distinguishes them.

**Where the archive's OCR layer and its IMAGE disagree, render the image.** At 0249A the OCR layer
and the rendered page happened to agree, but the OCR layer had already manufactured a separate error
on the same line elsewhere in this range (it drops *malas* from *malas cogitationes* at 0238B, which
both our twin and Gallica carry). The layer is nearly free and worth grepping every time; it is
never the last word.
