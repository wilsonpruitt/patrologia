Called in Hebrew בראשית , BERESIT, that is In the beginning; in Greek ΓΕΝΕΣΙΣ, that is Generation.

## CHAPTER ONE.

[0067B]

VERS. 1.-- « In the beginning he created, » etc. He does not say: In the beginning God says, Let heaven and earth be made. But he said: « Let there be light, and light was made, » because under the name of heaven and earth there was to be comprehended universally whatsoever God made, and thereafter it was to be set forth part by part how he made it. Whence there follows: « God said, Let it be made, » that is, by his Word he made it. Or because, when formless matter, spiritual or corporeal, was first coming to be, it was not to be said: « God said, Let it be made; » for it was unfitting that « let it be made » should be said by God, since imperfection does not imitate the form of the Word, save when, by the conversion of its own kind to the Creator, the creature is made perfect; so that when it is said: « God said, Let it be made, » we may understand that he calls back to himself the imperfection of the creature. [0067C] When therefore it is said: « In the beginning God made heaven and earth, » [var: Vulg. Gen. 1:1 *creavit*, "created"] there is a commemoration of the Son, because he is the beginning; but when it is said: « God said, Let it be made, » it is commemorated that he is the Word. By "beginning" he notes the origin of the creature that exists from him; by "the Word," the perfecting of the creature called back from him to himself, that it may be formed by imitating the unchangeable form of the Word. For the Word of God has not a formless life, to whom to be is the same as to live; nay rather, to live the same as to live blessedly. But the spiritual creature [0068B] has a formless life, to whom to be is not the same as to live; nor to live the same as to live blessedly: because, turned away from the wisdom of God, it lives foolishly and wretchedly.

« In the beginning, » etc. [n: (BEDA, Hexaem. lib. I, tom. II, col. 13 ª.)] Scripture, in intimating the creation of the world, shows by its first word the eternity and the omnipotence of God: for him whom it declares to have created the world in the beginning of times, it signifies to have existed eternally before times. And him whom it relates to have created heaven and earth at the outset of the founding, it declares to be omnipotent by so great a swiftness of working, for whom to have willed is to do. Well therefore is it said: « In the beginning he created, » etc. Both, that is, at once, although both are not uttered at once by a man. Whence the prophet shows the earth to have been made in the beginning, saying, [0068C] psal. CI: « In the beginning thou, O Lord, didst found the earth, » etc.

« In the beginning, » etc. Heaven, not the visible firmament, but the empyrean, that is, the fiery or intellectual heaven, which is so called not from burning but from splendour, and which was straightway filled with angels. Whence in Job XXXVIII: « Where wast thou when the morning stars praised me, » etc. And note that three elements are here commemorated. By the name of heaven we understand the air. By the name of earth, the earth itself and the fire that lies hid in it. Of the fourth, that is, of water, mention is made in what follows.

[0069A] [n: (ALCUIN. in Gen. tom. I.)] « In the beginning. » In the Son, by whose being made man it lay open who were the heavenly and who the earthly. Heaven, the spiritual creature, from its beginning perfect and blessed. Earth, corporeal matter, as yet imperfect. Heaven, the formless matter of spiritual life, such as it can exist in itself, not turned to the Creator in whom it is formed. Earth, the corporeal, without any quality that appears in formed matter.

[n: (BEDA in Gen., tom. II, col. 191.)] Scripture says: « Who madest the world of formless matter [n: (Sap. XI)]. » [var: Vulg. Sap. 11:18 *quae creavit orbem terrarum ex materia invisa*, "which created the world out of matter unseen"] Matter was made out of nothing. The species of the world, out of formless matter. Accordingly God made two things before all time, the angelic creature and formless matter. For although Solomon says: « Who lives for [0069B] « ever created all things together [n: (Eccli. XVIII)], yet according to origin formless matter goes before formed species. And so God made the world, and the angels, and the soul out of nothing: man and the remaining creatures out of something.

[n: (Ibid., col. 192.)] There are seven heavens, whose names are these, air, ether, olympus, the space or fiery region, the firmament, the heaven of the angels, and that of the Trinity. Jerome, however, says that the heaven of the Trinity is the first, that of the angels the second, the firmament the third.

*Mystically.* « In the beginning God created heaven. » Those, namely, who have borne the image of the heavenly one. « And the earth, » that is, those who afterwards, by growing proud, bearing the earth, that is, the image of the earthly man, made themselves deformed. « But the earth was void, » because [0069C] it had laid aside its good form. « And empty, » of the fruit of good work. « And there were darknesses, » the privation, namely, of the true light; « upon the face of the deep, » the hearts, namely, of the proud.

VERS. 2.-- « But the earth was void, » etc. [n: (BEDA, Hexaem. tom. II.)] He shows what heaven, or what kind of heaven, was made in the beginning together with the earth. For he subjoins that of the earth which he did not wish to be understood of heaven. For this higher heaven, which is set apart from the world's revolving, as soon as it was created was filled with holy angels: whom the Lord testifies to have been founded in the beginning together with heaven and earth, saying, Job XXXVIII: « Where wast thou when the morning stars praised me, and all the sons of God rejoiced? » He calls the same angels both morning stars and sons of God. For of the heaven in [0069D] which the luminaries are set, how or when it was made, he tells afterwards.

[n: (STRAB.)] « Void and empty. » Useless, namely, and unfruitful, and unordered. For all the elements were mingled and confused, and this whole space of air was full of waters; not such as they now are, but they were like thin clouds, such as those above the heavens still are.

[n: (BEDA ubi supra.)] « There were darknesses, » etc. Those are not to be heard who say by way of reproach that God created darkness before light: because he made no darkness in the water or in the air; but by a distinct order of providence he first created the waters together with the heaven, and the earth, and adorned these with the grace of light when he willed. And it is to be noted that with the heaven two elements of the world were created, [0070A] into which two others were inserted, water, namely, and earth, in which are fire and air. And the waters covered the whole surface of the earth with so great a depth that they reached even to those places where until now they partly rest above the firmament. But the earth itself and the water are called formless matter, because all the things which we see took their beginning either from these or out of nothing: and before they came into the light, there was not whence they might have form.

« And the Spirit of the Lord was borne over the waters. » [var: Vulg. Gen. 1:2 *Spiritus Dei*, "the Spirit of God"] [n: (AUG., lib. I de Gen. ad lit., c. 5, tom. III.)] Either he called the whole corporeal matter water, that he might intimate whence were made and formed all the things which we see formed on the earth out of a moist nature through divers species; or the spiritual life, as it were floating, before the [0070B] form of its conversion. The Spirit of God was borne above, because that which he had begun to be formed and perfected lay beneath the good will of the Creator. It is also said, « The Spirit of the Lord was borne over the waters. » Lest, by doing works, God should be thought to love through the necessity of want rather than through the abundance of beneficence. For fittingly is something first intimated to have been begun, over which he might be borne: not in place, but by a power surpassing all things.

« And the Spirit of the Lord was borne, » etc. [n: (HIERON. in Gen. t. III, col. 939.)] In the Hebrew it has *merahephet,* that is, it brooded, or cherished, after the manner of a bird warming eggs with the heat of a living thing. We understand therefore that this is not said of the spirit of the world, as many suppose; but of the [0070C] Holy Spirit, who is himself the quickener of all things: and if the quickener, also the founder; if the founder, also God. Psal. CIII: « Send forth, he says, thy Spirit, and they shall be created. » [var: Vulg. Ps. 103:30 *Emittes*, "Thou shalt send forth"]

« And the Spirit, » etc. In whom, subsisting, we might rest, quickened by his breath and washed by the wave of baptism. « And the Spirit of the Lord was borne over the waters, » etc. As God, namely, and Creator, he presided over the floating and confused matter, that he might distinguish it when he willed. The whole Trinity therefore is understood to have worked here, God, that is, the Father; the beginning, the Son; the Spirit of God, the Holy Spirit.

« And the Spirit, » etc. [n: (ISID., in Gen.? )] The hearts, namely, that were tossing to and fro, which had lost the quiet of the mind, [0070D] because the Spirit did not rest upon them, but was borne above them: as one able to call back their nature from confusion into something better. Then throughout the whole text of this Scripture we see certain six laborious ages, though distinguished as it were by neighbouring bounds, so that in the seventh rest may be hoped for; and we see that they have a likeness to the six days in which those things were made which Scripture records God to have made. For the first beginning of the human race, in which it began to enjoy this light, is compared to the first day, on which God made light. This age is as it were the infancy of the whole world, which we think of, by the proportion of its greatness, as one man. For man, when he is first born into the light, passes his infancy, the first age, which extends from Adam to Noah in ten generations: the flood is as it were the evening of this [0071A] day, because our infancy is blotted out by the flood of forgetfulness.

VERS. 3.-- « And God said: Let there be light. » [n: (AUG., de Gen. ad litt., lib. I, c. 17, tom. III.)] The light made on the first day is understood as spiritual or corporeal, etc., *as far as* the beginning of a morning to come. [n: (BEDA, Hexaem. )] But if corporeal light was made on the first day, fittingly is the adorning of the world begun from light, by which the rest, which were to be created, might be seen. But if it be asked: where was it made, since the deep covered the whole height of the earth? it is plain that it was made in those parts which the daily light of the sun now illumines. Nor is it a wonder that light can shine in waters, since they are even oftentimes lit up by the working of sailors, who, plunged in the deep, having let oil out of the mouth, light up the waters for themselves: which waters in the beginning were far rarer [0071B] than they now are, because they were not yet gathered into one place.

« And God said: Let there be light. » [n: (AUG. ibid., c. 2.)] Not temporally: for if temporally, then changeably also; if changeably, then also through a subject creature, and light is not the first creature. But perhaps, etc., *as far as* that it may be and that it may abide.

« Let there be light. » [n: (AUG. ibid., c. 4, 5, 9.)] On that condition, namely, by which all things subsist timelessly in the wisdom of God before they subsist in themselves. « And light was made, » that is, the angelic and heavenly substance, in itself temporally; as it was in wisdom, so far as regards his unchangeableness, eternally. Or there is noted the formlessness, namely, and the imperfection of this creature before it was formed in the love of its Founder: for it is formed when [0071C] it is turned to the unchangeable light of the Word.

« Let there be light. » [n: (AUG. ibid., c. 10, 11, 16.)] If light was made corporeally, etc., *as far as* for there were not yet animals to which this vicissitude might be exhibited.

VERS. 4.-- « And God saw the light, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] God made light and darkness, whence Dan. III. « Bless the Lord, O light and darkness, » when every creature is invited to bless.

[n: (ISID., in Gen., c. 2.)] Each one has in his works and in an upright life six distinct days, after which he may hope for rest: on the first day, the light of faith, since he first believes in invisible things, for which faith the Lord deigned to appear.
