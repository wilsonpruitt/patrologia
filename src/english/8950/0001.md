## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

« And the evening was made. » [n: (AUG., de Gen. contra Manich., l. I, c. 4; BEDA, in Hexaem.)] The light going down [0071D] little by little, and after the space of the divine length passing under to the lower parts, the evening was made, as it is now wont to come about by the accustomed circuit of the sun. And the morning was made when the same light returned over the earth and began another day, and one day of twenty-four hours was completed. But it is to be noted that the night in that three days' space was wholly dark, which after the stars were created shone with some light: it was fitting also that the days should begin from light and stretch toward the morning of the following day, so that the works of God might be signified to have been begun from light and completed unto light.

VERS. 5.--. . . . « And the evening was made, » etc. [n: (GREG., l. VIII Moral., c. 6, tom. I.)] In no wise in this life is sin so forsaken by the exercise of justice [0072A] that one may abide in it unshaken: because, if uprightness drives out guilt, guilt sits at the doors of our thought and knocks, that it may be opened to it. Whence Moses says: « Light was made; » and a little after: « The evening was made. » For the Creator, foreknowing man's guilt, expressed then in time what is now busied in the mind. For the shadow of temptation follows the light of uprightness. But because the light of the elect is not put out by temptation, not night but evening is recorded to have been made, because temptation hides the light of justice, it does not destroy it.

[n: (AUG., de Gen. ad litt., l. IV, c. 22, 23, tom. III, col. 311, 312.)] It is to be noted that the other creatures, etc., *as far as* that the one, namely, may belong to the day, this to the evening.

[0072B]VERS. 6.--. . . « Let there be a firmament, » etc. [n: (GREG., l. XXVII Moral., c. 24.)] The angelic powers which stood fast in the love of God received this in retribution, that they should abide in the contemplation of their Founder in everlasting happiness, and should subsist eternally in that wherein they were founded. Whence in Moses the heaven is said to have been made, and the same is afterwards called the firmament, because the angelic nature was first founded subtle in the higher places, and afterwards, that it might never be able to fall, was more wonderfully confirmed.

« Let there be a firmament, » etc. [n: (AUG., l. II de Gen. ad litt., c. 2, col. 264.)] It is asked whether that heaven which exceeds the spaces of the air is now made, etc., *as far as* because its interval divides between certain vapours of the waters and the waters which are on the earth.

[0072C] « Let there be a firmament, » etc. [n: (AUG., ibid., c. 4.)] It is to be noted that the firmament is not so called on account of standing still, etc., *as far as* but in whatever manner they are there, that they are there we do not doubt.

« Let there be a firmament in the midst. » [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem., t. II.)] Of the heaven, in which the stars are fixed, etc., *as far as* But of what sort they are, and for what they are useful, he knows who founded them.

VERS. 7.-- « And God made the firmament, » etc. [n: (AUG., l. II de Gen. ad litt., c. 8, col. 269.)] It is asked why, when he had said: « Let there be a firmament, and it was so done, » he afterwards adds: « And God made the firmament, » etc., *as far as* saying: « And God said, Let it be made, » etc.

[n: (Ibid., c. 1, 3, 5.)] Above the air is pure fire, etc., *as far as* The nearness of the supercelestial waters therefore makes it [0072D] cold.

[n: (Ibid.)] But it is to be noted that on the first day, when light was made, it was said only: « Let there be light, » and this in the word, and « light was made, » in the work, and straightway the created light clave to the uncreated light, as nearer to it and more capacious than every creature. On the second day there is a certain delay, and a multiplying of words, « let it be made, » and « God made, » and « it was so done » : that we may understand these natures to be prone to fail, and less apt to obey.

[n: (HIERON., l. I contra Jovin., t. II, col. 211.)] It is also to be noted that the works of this second day are not said, according to the Hebrews, to be good, although they are good like the rest: which comes about on account of the binary number, the chief of alternation, [0073A] which first departs from unity: and it is set as a figure of bigamy and of many reprehensible things; whence the things which enter into the ark two by two are unclean, an odd number clean. Or they are not said to be good for this reason, that in this work the separation of the water is not made perfect, which is made in the next, where he says: « Let the waters be gathered together, » as will be said there.

[n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 9.)] It is wont to be asked of what shape the heaven is: but the Holy Spirit, although our authors knew, did not will to teach through them save what profits salvation. It is also asked: does the heaven stand still, or is it moved? If it is moved, they say, how is it a firmament? If it stands still, how do the stars fixed in it go round? But the firmament can be so called not on account of standing still, but on account of firmness, or the untransgressible boundary [0073B] of the waters. But if it stands still, nothing hinders the stars from being moved and going round. On the second day there was made as it were the firmament of discipline, which distinguishes between carnal and spiritual things, as between the lower and the higher waters.

[n: (ISID., in Gen. c. 2, tom. V, col. 213.)] On the second day he set the firmament, that is, the holy Scriptures in the Church. Of which it is said, Isa. XXXIV: « The heaven shall be folded together like a book. » By this firmament he divides the higher waters, that is, the people of the angels, for whom there is no need that they should hear the word of God in reading. But he set that firmament above the infirmity of the lower peoples, that looking up at it they may know how it distinguishes between carnal and spiritual things, as it were between the higher and the lower waters.

[0073C] « And he divided the waters which were, » etc. [n: (AMBR., l. II Hexaem., c. 3, tom. I, col. 148.)] When thou hearest these things, why dost thou marvel if above the firmament a wave could be hung? etc., *as far as* Jordan also, its stream turned back, returns into its own spring.

VERS. 8.--. . . . « And the evening was made, » etc. [n: (ISID., ubi supra.)] The morning of the second day begins from Noah, etc., *as far as* because neither is boyhood apt for generation.

VERS. 9.-- « And God said, » etc. [n: (AUG., lib. II de Gen. ad litt., c. 11, tom. III.)] On this day it seems to have been done, etc., *as far as* Whence the water is gathered together, the earth appears; for water is slippery and flowing, earth stable and fixed.

« Let the waters be gathered together? » [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem. )] The waters which had filled all things between heaven and earth are gathered [0073D] into one place, that the light, which in the two days past had lit up the waters with a clear light, might shine brighter in the pure air; and that the earth might appear, which lay hidden; and that what was slimy with waters might become dry and apt for shoots. If it be asked where the waters were gathered together which had covered all parts of the earth up to the heaven, it could come about that the earth, subsiding, afforded hollow parts by which it might receive the floating waters: it can also be believed that the first waters were rarer, which covered the lands like a cloud, but that by the gathering they were thickened.

VERS. 10.-- « And God called the dry land Earth. » [n: (BEDA, ibid.)] Before, for the distinguishing of the waters, he called the whole more solid part of the world earth, saying: « In the beginning God created heaven and earth. » After [0074A] the world now begins to be formed, and the face of the earth appears, the waters withdrawing, for the distinguishing of the part which was still pressed down by the waters, the portion which it was permitted to be dry received the name of earth, in that it is trodden by the feet. « And the gatherings together of the waters he called Seas. » For so among the Hebrews all gatherings of waters are called, whether salt or sweet. And because, by the continuity of all the waters which are on the earth, he said that they were gathered into one place, he now calls them in the plural the gatherings of waters, on account of their manifold bays. Although it is agreed that there are many seas, yet he says the waters were gathered into one place, because all are joined to the great sea. If any lakes seem to be shut up in themselves, yet by hidden passages they are rolled back into the sea: the diggers of wells [0074B] also prove this, because all the ground is filled with waters through invisible veins, whose beginning is from the sea.

VERS. 11.-- « And he said: Let the earth bring forth, » etc. [n: (AUG., l. II de Gen. ad litt., c. 12.)] The governance of the Ordainer is to be marked: although the creature of herbs and of trees is distinct from the kind of the lands and of the waters, so that they cannot be numbered among the elements: yet because, fixed by their roots, they are bound to the earth, he willed them to belong to the same day.

[n: (BEDA, ubi supra.)] It is plain from these words that the world was perfected and adorned in the springtime, in which green herbs are wont to appear, and trees laden with fruits. And at the same time it is to be noted that the first shoots of herbs or kinds of trees came forth not from seed, but from the earth: because at one command of the Founder there appeared [0074C] the dry land trimmed with herbs and clothed with groves, which of themselves brought forth the fruits and seeds of their own kind. For it behoved that the form of things should come forth perfect at God's first command: as man, for whose sake are all things, was fashioned in a youthful age.

[n: (AUG., ibid. l. III, c. 18.)] It is also asked concerning thorns, and thistles, and certain unfruitful trees, why or when they were created? since God said: « Let the earth bring forth the green herb, » etc. But by the name of fruit a certain usefulness is signified: and there are many usefulnesses, manifest or hidden, of all the things which the earth nourishes by their roots. We can, however, answer without qualification that after sin the earth brought forth thorns and thistles for man's labour: not that they were born elsewhere before, and afterwards in the fields which man tilled: but before and afterwards in the same [0074D] places, yet before not for man, but afterwards. Whence « it shall bring forth to thee, » that is, that there should begin to be born for thee unto labour the things which before were born only for the food of other animals.

« Making seed. » [n: (BEDA, ibid.)] All things were created perfect, and man was perfect in age, and the trees with leaves and fruit: whence this is called a « tree bearing fruit. »

VERS. 12.-- « And the earth brought forth the herb, » etc. [n: (GREG., l. XIX Moral., c. 12; l. VI, c. 16, tom. I.)] What history testifies was truly so done, that it might signify something else to be done. For the earth is the Church, which refreshes us with the food of the word, and guards us with the shade of its patronage: by speaking it feeds, by helping it protects; [0075A] so that it may bring forth not only the herb of refreshment, but also the tree of protection.-- « And the tree. » The tree brought forth seed according to its own kind, when our mind gathers from the consideration of itself what it should do toward another, and bears the shoot of upright work. Hence a certain wise man says: « What thou wouldest not have done to thee, do not thou to another [n: (Tob. IV)]. » And elsewhere: « Whatsoever you would that men should do to you, do you also the same to them [n: (Matth. VII)]. » As if to say, look upon one another as your own species, and know from your own selves what it behoves you to show to others.
