## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

Seed. » [n: (STRAB., RAB.)] "Sementis" is of crops and of trees; "semen," of men and of animals. "Seminium" or "sementum," the beginning of anything whatever. On the third day, on which a man prepares his mind for the sowing of the [0075B] fruits of good works: the taint being separated, and the waves of carnal temptations, he has as it were dry land, temptations being set apart, so that he can say: « I serve the law of God with the mind, » etc. [n: (Rom. VII)].

VERS. 13.-- « And the evening and the morning were made. » [n: (ISID. in Gen., tom. V, col. 207.)] The morning of the third day begins from Abraham, etc., *as far as* The evening in the sins of the people, by which they were transgressing the divine commandments, until the wickedness of Saul.

[n: (ISID., ibid.)] On the third day he gathered together the lower and salt waters, that is, the unbelievers, shaking themselves with the waves of concupiscences and of temptations: and he set apart from them the dry land, that is, the faithful thirsting for the fountain of faith. But the unbelievers he restrained, lest with the waves of their iniquity they should trouble the dry land, that is, the soul thirsting for God, [0075C] but that it might be free for it to bring forth the fruits of good works, and to love its neighbour, so that it may have in itself seed according to its own likeness; that is, that out of its own weakness it may have compassion on another in need, and may produce a strong fruit-bearing tree, that is, a beneficent one, by rescuing the oppressed and affording him the shade of its protection.

VERS. 14.-- « But God said: Let there be luminaries, » etc. [n: (AUG., de Gen. ad litt., lib. II, c. 13, 14.)] Because to the constituting of the visible world two days are assigned, namely to the highest and the lowest part of the world: to the visible and movable parts of the world, which are created within the world, the three remaining ones were allotted, etc., *as far as* unto the inchoation, or principate, of the night, which comes about when she is full.

And let them divide the day. » [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem.)] By that [0075D] division, namely, that the sun should light the day, the moon the night. For this was added, when the stars were made, to the increase of the first light, that the night also might come forth luminous, irradiated by the splendour of the moon or of the stars, which hitherto had known darkness only. And although the night seems dark because the air which is nearest to the lands is darkened, yet the higher spaces of the air are always bright with the brightness of the stars.

[n: (AUG., lib. II de Gen. ad litt., c. 18.)] It is wont to be asked whether these luminaries are bodies only, or have ruling spirits, and are breathed upon by them vitally, as flesh is animated through the souls of animals. But concerning so obscure a matter nothing is rashly to be believed.

« And let them be for signs, » etc. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)] Because before [0076A] the stars were made, the order of the times was marked by no tokens, neither the midday hour nor any other. The luminaries therefore are « for signs, » etc.; not that from the founding of these the times, or the days, or the years began; but because by their risings, or their passings, the order of times, of days and of years is marked.

VERS. 15.-- « That they may shine, » etc. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)] The luminaries always shine in the firmament, but at fitting times they illumine the earth. For sometimes the cloudy air stands in the way: the sun also, having risen, hinders the moon and the stars with its greater light from illumining the earth. Whence it is so called, because, the moon and the other stars being dulled, it alone shines upon the lands by day.

VERS. 16.-- « God made two luminaries. » [n: (BEDA, ibid.)] We can take them to be great luminaries, not so much [0076B] by comparison with others as in themselves; as the heaven is called great, and the sea great. For the sun is called great, because it fills the globe of the earth with its heat: as does the moon with its light, which, in whatever part of the heaven they may be, illumine the whole earth, and are seen by all. A great argument of their greatness is that to all men the same orb of them appears: for if it were smaller to those placed afar and greater to those near, it would be an indication of littleness.

« The greater luminary, » etc. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)] The sun, namely, not only in the form of its body, but in the greatness of its light, because it lights up the lesser luminary and the stars. It is greater also in heat, because it warms the world, whereas in the days past before its creation it had had no heat. But that the moon is discerned to be of equal and of one greatness [0076C] with the sun, they say comes of this, that it goes far more distant from the lands and higher than the moon, and therefore its greatness cannot be discerned by us. For all things set farther off appear smaller.

« And the lesser luminary, » etc. And although it sometimes happens that the moon and the greater stars are seen by day, yet it is agreed that they bring the solace of their light not to the day, but to the night.

[n: (AUG., de Gen. ad litt. lib. II, c. 15, tom. III.)] For then, at the beginning of the night, the moon is seen, when she is full, at other times not: and by day she begins to be seen before her fulness, and in the progress of the night.

VERS. 17, 18-- . . . . « That they might shine, » etc. These things can be understood of the great luminaries and of the stars; so that what is said, « that they might rule the day, » should belong specially to the sun, [0076D] what is subjoined, « and the night, » to the moon and the stars; but what is added, « that they might divide the light and the darkness, » should suit all alike; which, wheresoever they go, carry light about them; whence, when they are absent, they leave all things dark. If it be asked what the daylight could have been like before the creation of the stars, we answer, such as we see when the rising of the sun is at hand, but it does not yet appear: when, namely, the day shines with the dulled rays of the stars, but the risen sun by no means blazes forth.

[n: (ISID., in Gen., tom. V, col. 207.)] On the fourth day the luminaries glittered in the firmament of heaven, etc., *as far as* because after good works there comes the illumination by which the species of the supernal truth is seen.

[0077A] [n: (AUG., de Gen. contra Manich., lib. I, c. 24, tom. III, col. 194.)] On the fourth day, on which in that firmament of discipline the mind works and distinguishes spiritual understandings, it sees what is the unchangeable truth, which shines like the sun in the soul; and how the soul is made partaker of it, and bestows order and beauty upon the body, as the moon lighting the night. And how all the stars, that is, spiritual understandings, shine in the obscurity of this life as it were in the night.

VERS. 19.-- « And the evening and the morning were made, » etc. [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem., tom. II, col. 13.)] In this evening the people of God in Egypt offered the lamb, etc., *as far as* he might grant hope and faith.

« And the evening was made, » etc. [n: (ISID., ubi supra.)] [0077B] The morning of the fourth day, the kingdom of David. This age is like to youth, which reigns among all the ages, and is the firm firmament of all the ages: whence it is compared to the fourth day, on which the stars were made in the firmament of heaven. The splendour of the sun indeed figures the excellence of the kingdom, the splendour of the moon shows the people obeying the king, as it were the Synagogue. But the stars are its princes, and all things founded on the stability of the kingdom, as it were in the firmament.

VERS. 20.-- « God also said, » etc. [n: (BEDA, ubi supra.)] After the face of heaven had been adorned on the fourth day with luminaries, the lower parts of the world, namely water and air, are adorned on the fifth day with their own living things.

« God also said: Let the waters bring forth the creeping thing, » etc. *(AUG., lib. II de Gen. ad litt., ex c. 1, 2, 3, 4, [0077C] 5 et 6, tom. III.)* It behoved, in the creating of the inhabitants of the lower part, etc., *as far as* Which is not to be understood save of such as have been turned into the nature of the waters by the quality of a thicker air.

« Of a living soul. » [n: (AUG., ibid., c. 8.)] That is, the things which are in living souls, as it is said: "The ignoble things of men," that is, those who among men are ignoble. Which some think was said on account of their slowness of sense, or because fishes are of no memory; but it is most certain that they have memory.

[n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 3, 4.)] Concerning the conversion of the elements there is a question. Some say that all things can be turned into all things, etc., *as far as* for that we feel the waters and the winds by touch; hence it is that the solid earth is mingled with all the elements, but in these thicker ones it is felt [0077D] more.

« Under the firmament » , etc. Even though there lies between an immense space, yet the birds which fly above the earth fly under the starry heaven: just as men who are on the earth are said to be under heaven. Another translation: « Beside the firmament of heaven, » because the spaces of this air which bears up the birds are neighbours to the ether.

[n: (AUG., de Gen. ad litt., l, III, c. 9, 10, tom. III, col. 284.)] It is to be noted that certain philosophers have so distributed the animals to each element, etc., *as far as* yet they have been permitted to hold only the misty places of the air, which is to them as it were a prison until the time of judgment.

VERS. 21.-- « And God created the great whales, » etc. [0078A] [n: (THEOD. q. 18 in Gen.)] Boys need now bugbears, now straps also and rods: by the former we frighten them, by the latter we instruct them: Since therefore God perceived that we too are prone to sloth, he founded the beasts, as it were certain straps and bugbears, that by frightening us with them he might draw us to himself, and that we might implore his help. But as those who are perfect despise bugbears of this sort, and straps; so those who have been brought up in virtue do not dread the onsets of beasts. Indeed the beasts stood by Adam before he had sinned, professing their obedience. And again the lion, the leopard, and all the fiercest of the beasts followed Noah himself as he entered the ark, after the manner of sheep. And the lions were present with Daniel, who, though otherwise famished, yet did not dare to approach him, because [0078B] they beheld in him the characters that bore before them the divine image. Likewise the viper, which had fastened its teeth in the hand of the Apostle, when it had found nothing in him tender or soft to sin, straightway leapt back and cast itself into the fire, exacting after a fashion punishment of itself, because it had attacked a body in no wise suited to it. But we for this cause dread the beasts, because we neglect virtue. Moreover the beasts are not useless to us for other things either: for out of them physicians compound not a few medicines for driving away diseases: let no one therefore, considering the beasts, complain of the Creator, but let him search out their usefulness.

« And every soul, » etc. In distinction from man, who was to be made to the image of God, and, if he kept the commandments, would remain in perpetual unchangeableness. [0078C] But the other animals were so made that some should yield to others for nourishment, or should fail through age.

VERS. 22.-- « And he blessed them, » etc. [n: (AUG., lib. de Gen. imp., c. 15, tom. III.)] He willed the blessing to avail unto fruitfulness, which appears in the succession of offspring, so that by that blessing by which they were created weak and mortal, they might guard their own kind by being born.
