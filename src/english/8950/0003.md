## CHAPTER ONE. (cont.)

[n: (CHRYSOST., hom. 7 in Gen., tom. IV, col. 66.)] The blessing itself, therefore, is to be increased and multiplied in a great number and multitude. For because living creatures were animate, and willed to be perpetually, therefore he subjoined: « Increase. » For that word preserves them even unto the present day. « Increase and multiply, » etc. [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem.)] It belongs to both [0078D] kinds of living things made out of the waters, that is, to fishes and to birds. For as all fishes cannot live save in the waters, so very many birds; which, if they sometimes rest on the lands and make their young there, yet live not from the land but from the sea, and use the sea more willingly than the land.

*Mystically. [n: (ISID. et AUG., ubi supra.)]* On the fifth day there were made in the waters the creeping things of living souls, that is, men renewed unto life through the sacrament of baptism. There were made flying things, that is, souls flying to the things above. On the fifth day the mind that has advanced begins to work in the actions of a turbulent world, as it were in the waters of the sea, for the profit of brotherly fellowship, and out of the corporeal acknowledgments which belong to the sea, that is, [0079A] to this life, to bring forth the creeping things of living souls, that is, works which profit living souls; and great whales, that is, the strongest acknowledgments, by which the waves of the world are broken and despised; and the flying things of heaven, that is, voices preaching heavenly things.

VERS. 23.-- « And the evening and the morning were made, etc. [n: (AUG., de Gen. contra Manich., lib. II, c. 23, tom. III.)] Mystically, the morning is the passing over into Babylon, when by that captivity the people was lightly settled in a foreign leisure, etc., *as far as* because they were so blinded that they could not even know Christ.

VERS. 24.-- « God also said: Let the « earth bring forth, » etc. [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem., tom. III, col. 13.)] After the heaven was filled with stars and the air with flying things, which on account of its nearness is called heaven, etc., [0079B] *as far as* under the name of four-footed beasts all things are comprehended except the beasts and the creeping things, whether tamed, or wild and of the fields.

« Let the earth bring forth, » etc. [n: (AUG., lib. III in Gen., c. 11, tom. III.)] It followed that he should now adorn the earth with its own animals. By saying three times, according to its own kind, etc., *as far as* And under the name of cattle and of beasts every irrational animal is wont to be comprehended. « In its own kind. » [n: (AUG., ibid. c. 12,)] These things are perhaps said according to kind, because they so arose that others are born of them, and preserve by succession the form of their origin. But of man this was not said, because one was being made, of whom the woman also was made. For there were not made many kinds of men, as of herbs and of trees; so that it should be said, according to its own kind, as [0079C] if it were said kind by kind, that among things alike, and belonging to one origin of seed, they might be distinguished from the rest.

[n: (AUG., ibid. c. 14.)] Concerning certain minute animals there is a question, etc., *as far as* except that they were fore-sown in them potentially and materially.

[n: (AUG., ibid. c. 15, 16.)] Concerning venomous and destructive creatures it is asked, etc., *as far as* all which, rightly considered, are praised.

[n: (AUG., ibid., c. 17.)] This also moves us, etc., *as far as* but let them not doubt to prepare the numbers of a godly fortitude for all things.

[n: (THEOD., quaest. 18 in Gen.)] Let us not despise the beasts, as being altogether unharmed by them, as though they were of no strength. Sometimes God permits two or [0079D] three out of many thousands either to be stung by scorpions or bitten by serpents; that we, fearing lest we should suffer anything like, may call upon God the Creator for help.

[n: (AUG. in Gen., l. III, c. 13.)] It is asked why there was not added here what was said of the animals of the waters: « And God blessed them, saying: Increase and multiply, » etc. Perhaps because what had been said of the first creature of a living soul, he left to be understood of the second, especially because in the works of this day he was about to say many other things. But herbs and trees, because they have not the affection of generating, he perhaps judged unworthy of this blessing. Which was of necessity repeated in man, lest anyone should think [0080A] that there is sin in the office of begetting sons, as in fornication without wedlock.

VERS. 25.-- « And God made the beasts of the earth, » etc. [n: (BEDA in Hexaem.)] It is a change of order. Nor need we care in our own speech which of the creatures we name first in order, since God created all things together.

[n: (ISID., in Gen., tom. V, col. 207.)] On the sixth day the earth brings forth the living soul, when our flesh, abstaining from dead works, brings forth living shoots of the virtues, according to its own kind, that is, by imitating the life of the saints. Whence I Cor. IV: « Be imitators of me, » etc. We live according to our own kind when we imitate the saints as our neighbours. The earth brings forth beasts, that is, men growing proud in ferocity. [0080B] And cattle, that is, those living simply. And serpents, namely the crafty, and those who discern good from evil, and who as it were by creeping search out earthly things, through which they may understand the heavenly.

VERS. 26.-- « Let us make man. » [n: (AUG., ibid., c. 19.)] There is intimated the plurality of the persons of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit, and straightway the unity of the deity, when it is said: « And God made man to the image of God, » not the Father to the image of the Son only. For it would not truly be said, to our image; but it was so said: « God made man to the image of God, » as if it were said « to his own image. » But when it is said « to the image of God, » whereas it was said above « to our image, » it is signified that the plurality of persons does not bring it about that [0080C] we should believe in many gods, but that we should receive the Father, and the Son, and the Holy Spirit as one God.

« Let us make man. » [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem.)] It appears why it was said that the rest should be made, etc., *as far as* but that he might show that he had received back true flesh after death.

« And let him rule, » etc. [n: (AUG., lib. III de Gen. c. 20.)] It is also to be noted that, when he had said « to our image, » he immediately subjoined: « And let him rule over the fishes of the sea and the fowls of the air. » That we may understand, namely, that man was made to the image of God in this, wherein he excels the irrational creatures. And that is reason, or mind, or understanding, or if it be more conveniently called by another name, that, namely, wherein he cleaves to the truth that is to be contemplated.

[0080D]VERS. 27.-- « And God created man to the image, » etc. [n: (BEDA, in Gen., c. 1.)] He who, not by imitating any of the saints whatever, but by gazing upon truth itself, works justice, so that he may understand truth itself and follow it: he has received power over all things, because everyone made spiritual, and like to God, judges all things, and is himself judged by no man.

« And God created, » etc. [n: (AUG., ubi supra, cap. 20, 21, 22.)] It is to be noted that, as in the creating of light it is said: « Let there be light, » and straightway there follows, « and light was made; » so when it is said: « Let us make man, » there is brought in, « and God made man to the image of God: » because that nature is intellectual, as that light was: and this is for it to be made, namely [0081A] to acknowledge the Word through which it is made. etc., *as far as* but fore-sown materially.

« Male and female he created them, » etc. [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem. )] Afterwards it is set forth more fully both whence and in what manner men were made, etc., *as far as* although virginity be preferred.

[n: (BEDA, ISID., ut supra. )] After these things « God made man to his own image and likeness, » a perfect man, namely, etc., *as far as* whence Paul: « We have sown unto you spiritual things, it is a great matter if we reap your carnal things [n: (I Cor. IX)]. » [var: Vulg. 1 Cor. 9:11 *Si nos vobis spiritualia seminavimus*, a conditional, "If we have sown unto you spiritual things", closing as a question]

VERS. 28.-- . . . . . « And rule over the fishes, » etc. What he had said before of them as it were by counselling, he now says commanding, so that man may have, namely, dominion over all the creatures in the lower part of the world. Which he follows out [0081B] one by one.

« And rule over the fishes of the sea. » [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem. )] It is asked with what usefulness man received dominion among the other living things, etc., *as far as* and that the gaping of beasts and the venom of serpents gave way.

VERS. 29.-- « And God said: Behold, I have given, » etc. [n: (BED., ibid.)] It is plain that before the sin of man the earth brought forth nothing harmful, no poisonous herb, no barren tree. For all the herbs and trees were given to men, and to the birds, and to the living things of the earth for food. Whence it is plain that then animals did not live by the eating of animals, but fed harmoniously on herbs and fruits.

VERS. 30.-- . . . . . « And it was so done. » [n: (AUG., de Gen. ad lit. lib. III, c. 13.)] It is to be noted that where power is given to man of eating herbs and fruits, there is subjoined, [0081C] « and it was so done: » wherein it is signified that, when God said this, man knew these things to be granted to him for food, not that he ate them at once. For if we refer to all the aforesaid things what he says, « and it was so done, » it will follow that we say they were already multiplied on the sixth day, which was done after many years.

VERS. 31.-- « And God saw, » etc. [n: (AUG., ibid.)] It is asked: since the rest are severally called good, why is man, made to the image of God, said to be this along with the rest? But perhaps God foreknew that he would sin, and would not remain in the perfection of the image. For he who is good severally, is more so with all, but it is not convertible. It was therefore cautiously provided that there should be said what would be true in the present, and would signify the foreknowledge of the future. [0081D] But God ordered all things so that, if any severally should by transgressing be deformed, yet always with them the whole should be beautiful.

« And the evening and the morning were made, » etc. [n: (ISID., in Gen., tom. V, col. 207.)] The morning of the sixth day is the preaching of the Gospel through Christ, etc., *as far as* which has no evening.

*Morally. [n: (AUG., de Gen. contra Manich., lib. I in fine.)]* On the sixth day the earth brings forth the living soul, that is, man, out of the stability of his mind, where he has spiritual fruits, that is, good thoughts; he governs all the motions of his soul, so that there may be in him a living soul, that is, one serving reason and justice, not earthliness and sin. So man is made to the image and likeness [0082A] of God, male and female; that is, understanding and action: by whose coupling a spiritual offspring may fill the earth, that is, may subject the flesh, and the other things which have already been said in the perfection of man. For in these days the evening is the very perfecting of the several works, and the morning the beginning of those that follow.
