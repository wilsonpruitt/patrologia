## CHAPTER II.

VERS. 1.-- « Therefore the heavens and the earth were finished, » etc. [n: (ALCUIN., in Gen. tom. I, col. 517.)] In no way can it be told how God made heaven and earth. But this exposition, by the order of the days, sets forth as it were a history of things done, yet above all it attends to the foretelling of things to come, etc., *as far as* which the Lord showed to lie hidden to good purpose.

[0082B] « Therefore the heavens were finished, and the earth, and all the furniture of them. » *[n: (AUG., de Gen. contra Manich., lib. I, c. ult.)] Allegorically.* After those works of, as it were, six days, works very good, let man hope for perpetual rest, and let him understand what it is, » and God rested on the seventh day from his works: » because he himself also works these good things in us, who commands that we should work. And he is rightly said to rest: because after all these works he will furnish us with rest, in the way that the master of a household builds a house, when he commands his servants to do it; and afterwards to rest from his works, when, the building finished, he bids them rest.

[n: (AUG., ibid.)] Each one of us has in his works and in an upright life these six days, as it were, marked off, after which he ought to hope for quiet: on the first day [0082C] the light of faith, when first he believes things visible, for which faith the Lord vouchsafed to appear visibly. On the second day there was made as it were a firmament of discipline, which distinguishes between carnal and spiritual works, as the firmament between the lower waters and the higher. On the third day, on which he prepares and raises up his mind to bring forth the fruits of good works, the stain and the waves of carnal temptations being set apart, he has his mind dry, as it were, from the disturbances of the sea, so that he can now say: « With the mind I serve the law of God [n: (Rom. VII)], » etc.

VERS. 2, 3.-- « And God finished, » etc. [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem. )] Another version: « God completed on the sixth day the works which he made, » which brings no question with it, because the things that were done in it are manifest, etc., [0082D] *as far as* for Solomon did some work when he dedicated the temple.

[n: (HIER., Quaest. Hebr. in Gen., tom. III, col. 935.)] In the Hebrew it stands « on the seventh day. » We shall therefore press the Jews hard, who glory in the leisure of the sabbath, because already then, in the beginning, the sabbath was loosed, while God works on the sabbath, completing his works on it.

« And God finished, » etc. [n: (AUG., lib. IV de Gen. ad litt., c. 4, tom. III.)] It is asked whether, etc., *as far as* according to him they would not be made perfect.

« And God rested, etc. [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem. )] Not as though wearied by working, but he rested from the whole work, because he ceased to make a new creature. For to rest is said to be to cease, as in Apocalypse IV: [0083A] « They had no rest, saying: Holy, holy, holy, » that is, they did not cease to say it.

[n: (BEDA, ibid.)] More deeply too he will be understood to have rested from his whole work, etc., *as far as* but he gave rest in himself to rational creatures.

« And God rested, » etc. [n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 8.)] God did not labour in working, who made by a word alone, saying: « Let it be made. » Nor does a man labour by saying, « Let it be made. » But perhaps he is said to have laboured in thinking what should be made. From which care, things being finished, he is said to have rested. But to be wise in this fashion is to be witless. That God rested, therefore, is that he furnished rest in himself to the rational creature; that is, that we may be borne by desire thither, where we may rest, that is, may seek nothing further. For as he is said to make what we make by his working in us, [0083B] and to know when we know, so to rest, when by his gift we rest.

[n: (Ibid., c. 11.)] It is no wonder if, foretelling in this manner the day on which Christ was to rest in the sepulchre, he said: « On the seventh day he rested, » being thereafter about to work the orders of the ages.

[n: (Ibid., c. 12.)] But it can be understood that God rested from making the kinds of creature, etc., *as far as* and does not cease to govern.

[n: (Ibid., c. 14.)] But God was neither wearied by creating nor refreshed by ceasing: rather through his Scripture he exhorts us to the desire of rest, by saying that he sanctified the day on which he rested. For he is not read to have sanctified the other days of his work, as though with him rest avails more than working.

[0083C] [n: (Ibid., c. 15.)] It is also a vice of the soul so to delight in its own works, etc., *as far as* by that rest from which he never withdrew.

[n: (Ibid.)] But if he were said to have rested from things to be made, etc., *as far as* but by him we are blessed.

[n: (Ibid.)] But in himself God has always rested: yet in the days in which the completing of things is recounted, he rested on the seventh only, which follows the perfection of things; for he rests from things perfected, who has no need of things perfected that he may be more blessed.

[n: (Ibid., c. 17.)] His works we see to be good: but his rest we shall see after our good works. To signify which, he commanded one day of the sabbath to be observed: which in the time of grace has been taken away, in [0083D] which he keeps a perpetual sabbath who works all good things in hope of what is to come, and does not glory in his good works as though he had not received them.

[n: (Ibid., c. 18.)] With him there is to his rest neither morning nor evening, because it is neither opened by a beginning nor closed by an end. But in works perfected it has a morning, and not an evening; because the perfected creature begins to be turned to the quiet of the Creator, but that quiet has no end of its perfection, and so the rest of God is begun not for God himself, but for the perfection of things, and it has a morning in that which is perfected by him: but in its own kind it is ended as by an evening, which in God has no evening, because nothing will be more perfect than perfection itself.

[0084A] [n: (Ibid.)] But in those days, etc., *as far as* which are recorded as created before the luminaries.

[n: (Ibid., c. 20.)] It is asked how it is said on the seventh, etc., *as far as* except that which was created first and called day.

[n: (Ibid., c. 21.)] But how could the light go round, etc., *as far as* and the rest.

[n: (Ibid.)] But it is easier that we confess ourselves ignorant, etc., *as far as* and it will be false that he completed all his works on the sixth day.

[n: (Ibid., c. 22.)] But since bodily light, etc., *as far as* and that by which the creature is known in itself is rightly called evening.

[n: (Ibid., c. 25.)] But because the angels so know the creature in itself, etc., *as far as* Therefore on the seventh day [0084B] no evening came on.

[n: (Ibid., c. 28.)] Nor let anyone think, etc., *as far as* and more truly morning?

[n: (Ibid., c. 35.)] That first day, therefore, etc., *as far as* and for this it deserved to be sanctified.

*[n: (BEDA, in Hexaem., tom. II, col. 13.)] Allegorically.* Whence it is said in Exod. XX: « Remember that thou keep holy the sabbath day. » But this blessing and sanctification signifies a greater blessing and sanctification, etc., *as far as* Therefore this day is not written to have an evening.

VERS. 4.-- « These are the generations. » [n: (RAB.)] He touches those who say that the world always was without a beginning, or who think that it was made by God, but out of matter which he did not make, but which is coeternal with him.

[0084C] « In the day that he made, » etc. He puts the day for the whole time in which the primordial creature was formed, etc., *as far as* without rain and human labour.

« These are the generations of the heaven, » etc. [n: (ISID., in Gen., t. V.)] There are numbered six days in which the whole of the age from beginning to end, etc., *as far as* if he had not sinned.

« Before it should spring up in the earth. » That is, before he sinned, that is, before he made himself subject to earthly desires. Whence there follows: « For God had not rained upon the earth, » that is, he had not yet, by prophetic or evangelical clouds, the shower of the word being sent forth, made the soul to live, and « there was no man to till the earth, » because after sin man began to labour on the earth, [0084D] he had need of those clouds, whence the shrub, that is the soul, grew green. The fountain of life watered it, that is, the flood of truth, speaking in his understanding, so that it needed no rain from the clouds before he sinned. This was the state of man before sin.

[n: (AUG., l. V, de Genes. ad lit., c. 1, tom. III.)] Another version has: « This is the book of the creation of heaven and earth, » etc., *as far as* it nourishes the great with truth, the little ones with affability.

[n: (AUG., ibid., l. VI, c. 10.)] In the first founding of the world, etc., *as far as* seed from the herb.

VERS. 5.-- « And every shrub, » etc. [n: (AUG., ibid., l. V, c. 4.)] The earth is understood to have brought forth herb and tree causally, that is, to have received the power of bringing forth. For in it, as in roots, had been made [0085A] the things that were to be through the times to come. He made them, therefore, before they were upon the earth, according to the formability of matter — the things that were to be formed by his word — preceding the forming not in time but in origin.

« For the Lord had not rained, » etc. [n: (AUG., ibid., c. 5.)] As though God did not then make in the way he now makes, etc., *as far as* nor could the creature be moved.

VERS. 6.-- « But a fountain, » etc. [n: (RAB.)] By the watering of this fountain, of whatever kind it was, the earth came on already clothed with herbs and trees. One fountain is said to have gone up, for some unity within the bounds of the earth; or the singular is put for the plural.

« But a fountain, » etc. [n: (BEDA, in Gen., c. 2.)] Here he seems to intimate what things come to be according to the intervals of times, etc., *as far as* afterwards, through these things that are known, [0085B] to be known in whatever measure.

VERS. 7.-- « He formed, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] *Mystically.* A broader narrative concerning man is unfolded figuratively, etc., *as far as* that he neither despise what he is, nor claim what he is not.

« The Lord God therefore formed man, » etc. [n: (AUG., de Gen. lib. III, cap. 20 et 25.)] First out of the slime of the earth was formed an animal body, etc., *as far as* for it has the necessity of dying.

[n: (Ibid. c. 27.)] The first robe is either justice, from which he fell: or, if it signifies bodily immortality, this he lost, since by sinning he could not attain to it. So then Adam had an animal body, not only before paradise, but in paradise as well (although in the inner man he was spiritual), which he lost by sinning, [0085C] and deserved the death of the body — he who by not sinning would have deserved the change into a spiritual body.

« He formed, » etc. [n: (AUG., l. VI de Gen. ad litt., c. I, tom. III.)] It must be seen whether it be a recapitulation, so that it is told how man was made, whom we read to have been made on the sixth day. Or whether then, when he made all things together, he made man also among them, so that with the accession of time he should be made in this manner too, in which he leads his life in this visible form? just as the hay, made before it sprang up, with time coming on and with the watering of the fountain, sprang up, so as to be upon the earth.
