## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., ibid. c. 2, 3.)] Let us look first according to recapitulation, etc., *as far as* but then invisibly, potentially and causally, in the manner in which things to come, not things done, come to be.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0085D] « He formed, » etc. [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem. )] The making of man, etc., *as far as* he created that in which he should live.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., de Gen. ad litt., l. VI, c. 13 et 17.)] It is asked whether God made man suddenly in the age of manhood, etc., *as far as* and therefore it was of necessity to come.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And he breathed into his face the breath of life, and man was made into a living soul. » [n: (AUG., ibid., l. VII, c. 3, 5, 7.)] Without doubt, « he blew, or he breathed upon, » is what must be said, etc., *as far as* a punishment if he should despise it?

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (Ibid., c. 26.)] It must be said, etc., *as far as* it is not lawful to doubt.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (Ibid., c. 27.)] It is commonly asked whether, if it be unwilling to be embodied, it is compelled, etc., *as far as* he made them.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0086A] [n: (Ibid., l. VI, c. 7.)] But perhaps to the soul made on the sixth day, where the image of God shines back, etc., *as far as* which is to be preferred in matters of this kind.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (IRENAEUS.)] The habit of life which makes a man animal is one thing; the life-giving Spirit, which works a spiritual man, is another. ⟦s2⟧ Whence Isaias: Who gave breath to the peoples upon the earth, and spirit to them that tread upon it; so that God gave breath indeed to every people that is upon the earth, but spirit only to those who tread down earthly desires: so that the breath is for a time, but the spirit eternal, inasmuch as it is properly the gift of God.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- « And he had planted, » etc. [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem., tom. III.)] From that beginning he planted paradise, etc., *as far as* and because he was about to speak specially of the tree of life and of the knowledge [0086B] of good and evil.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The Lord planted a paradise in Eden toward the East, » etc. [n: (AUG., de Gen. ad litt., lib. VIII, c. 1.)] There are three general opinions concerning paradise. ⟦s2⟧ One is of those who will have it understood only bodily, another of those who will have it only spiritually, but a third of those who take paradise in both ways. ⟦s3⟧ I confess that the third pleases me: so that the man made of slime, which is the human body, is understood to be placed in a bodily paradise. ⟦s4⟧ Adam, although he signifies something else, because he is the figure of him that was to come, is taken in his own nature as a man.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (Ibid.)] « And the Lord planted a paradise in Eden. » That is, in delights. ⟦s2⟧ Then he recapitulates this, to show how God planted paradise. ⟦s3⟧ « And God sent forth out of the earth every tree, » etc. ⟦s4⟧ For already [0086C] then the earth had brought forth every tree, namely on the third day. ⟦s5⟧ But since out of these kinds the trees were established in paradise, he brought forth openly, and in its own time, what the earth had already brought forth causally on the third day.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (STRAB.)] Certain codices have: « Eden toward the Rising. » From which we can conjecture that paradise is set in the East. ⟦s2⟧ But wherever it be, we know that it is earthly, and, with the Ocean set between and mountains standing over against it, most remote from our world, set on a height, reaching as far as the lunar circle; whence the waters of the flood by no means came thither.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « In which he placed him. » [n: (AUG., ubi supra, cap. 5.)] If anyone thinks that souls stripped of the body are contained in bodily places, etc., *as far as* by one cake of which God freed a man from hunger for forty days.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0086D]VERS. 9.-- « And the Lord brought forth the tree of life also. » [n: (AUG., Ibid., c. 4.)] Of wisdom it is said: « It is a tree of life to them that lay hold on her. » But there is an eternal Jerusalem in the heavens, etc., *as far as* which narrated things done in their own times.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The tree of life also, » etc. [n: (BEDA, in Hexaem., tom. III, col. 13.)] In the one there was for man a sign of the obedience which he owed: in the other a sacrament of eternal life, which by obeying he would merit. ⟦s2⟧ It was called the tree of life, because it received from God that whoever ate of it, his body should be strengthened with a stable soundness, nor should it slip by any infirmity or age into the worse or into decline.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (STRAB.)] *Historically.* ⟦s2⟧ The tree of life had naturally this [0087A] power, that whoever ate of its fruit should be clothed with perpetual solidity, to be wearied by no infirmity, or anxiety, or weariness of old age, or feebleness.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And the tree of the knowledge of good and evil. » [n: (AUG. ubi supra, c. 6)] It was bodily like the other trees, and not harmful as food, etc., *as far as* which followed the transgression.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The tree of the knowledge of good and evil. » [n: (AUG., lib. II de Gen. contra Manich., c. 10.)] The mean of the soul and its ordered integrity is signified, in that it was planted in the midst of paradise, and called of the knowledge of good and evil: because the soul, which ought to be stretched out toward God, if, God being forsaken, it be turned to itself, and will enjoy its own power without God, then, punishment following, learns by experience what [0087B] lies between the good which it forsook and the evil into which it fell: and this will be for it to have tasted of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10, 11, 12, 13, 14.-- « And a river went out. » [n: (BED., ubi supra.)] To water paradise, etc., *as far as* and, emerging a little after, to hold their accustomed course.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 7.)] Another version: « A fountain went out of Eden, which watereth paradise, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ These rivers to the nations, etc., *as far as* namely trees fair and fruitful.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID., AUG.)] *Mystically.* ⟦s2⟧ The river going out of paradise is the affluence of eternal gladness, etc., *as far as* by bearing adversities.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (GREG.)] *Morally.* ⟦s2⟧ By four rivers going out of paradise [0087C] the earth is watered. ⟦s3⟧ Prudence, temperance, fortitude, justice hold together the solid building of our reason: because by these four virtues the whole structure of good work rises up. ⟦s4⟧ Four rivers water paradise, because while the heart is flooded with these four virtues, it is tempered from every heat of carnal desires.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Evilath. » A region of India which after the flood was possessed by Evila, the son of Jectan, the son of Heber, the patriarch of the Hebrews. ⟦s2⟧ Evilath is interpreted *bringing to birth,* because one who speaks the truth is more in travail than he brings forth. ⟦s3⟧ Pliny says that the regions of India abound in veins of gold beyond all others.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Bdellium. » According to Pliny it is an aromatic tree of the size of an olive, whose gum is clear, [0087D] bitter to the taste, of a good smell, but more fragrant with an infusion of wine.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Onyx. » It is a precious stone, so called because it has a whiteness mixed in it after the likeness of a human nail, because a nail is called in Greek ὄνυξ. ⟦s2⟧ The ancient version has « carbuncle and prase. » The carbuncle is of a fiery colour, and is said to light up the darkness of night. ⟦s3⟧ The prase is green, whence it took its name in Greek from the leek, which is called πράσον.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- « The Lord God therefore took, » etc. [n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 8.)] He has already said that man was placed in paradise. ⟦s2⟧ Now he recapitulates to what end he was placed there, « that he might dress it, » etc. ⟦s3⟧ For it is not credible that God condemned him to labour before sin, [0088A] etc., *as far as* whence Eccli. ⟦s4⟧ X: « for the beginning of man's pride is to fall away from God. »

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The Lord God therefore took the man, » etc. [n: (GREG., lib. XIX Moral., cap. 17, tom. I.)] It must be weighed that good things cannot profit, if the evils that creep in are not guarded against, etc., *as far as* but by growing proud under them they sink beneath them.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And the Lord God took, » etc. [n: (HIERON. Quaest. Hebr. in Gen., tom. III.)] For « pleasure » the Hebrew has « Eden. » The Seventy themselves, therefore, have rendered it now « Eden, » now « pleasure. » But Symmachus, who « had before translated it a flowering paradise, here put « pleasantness, » or « delights. »

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And he put him, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] *Mystically.* ⟦s2⟧ From that which is added, « And that he should keep it, it is hinted what kind of work [0088B] it was: because in the tranquillity of the blessed life, where death is not, all work is to keep what thou holdest. ⟦s3⟧ For a precept was given, that they should take of every tree of paradise, but not of the tree of the knowledge of good and evil: that is, that they should not so enjoy it as to violate the integrity of their nature by usurping what was forbidden.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- « And he enjoineth him, » etc. [n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 13.)] He was forbidden from the tree, etc., *as far as* what lies between the good of obedience and the evil of disobedience.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 17.)] It is asked whether to the man alone, or to the woman also, etc., *as far as* « let them ask their husbands at home. »

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (PROCOP. in Gen. )] Some say: If he willed to clothe us with immortality, what need was there of a precept, on account of [0088C] which we were to sin and then to feel the severe stings of his judgment? ⟦s2⟧ I answer: Since he is kind and gentle, and willed to lay claim to the human race for an incorruptible and immortal condition: lest they should set that benefit down to their own or proper power, he acted in this manner, he willed that they should render him thanks, and for them he also appointed the present life as a wrestling-school and, as it were, a schooling for the life to come. ⟦s3⟧ Then, bringing in death on account of sin, he willed that we should acknowledge our infirmity, and that thereafter we ourselves should the more desire the gift to come.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And he commanded him, saying: Of every, etc. [n: (AUG. de Gen. ad litt., l. VIII, 28, tom. III.)] If it be asked how he spoke these things, it cannot properly be comprehended by us. ⟦s2⟧ For it is certain that he speaks either through his own substance, [0088D] etc., *as far as* but through a creature subject to himself.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ It is asked: how could they speak, or understand one speaking, who had not learned by growing up among speakers, or by teaching? ⟦s2⟧ But God had made them such as could speak, and could learn from others if there were any.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 14.)] Of Christ it is said, Isa. VII: « Before the child knows good or evil, he shall despise wickedness, that he may choose the good. » How does he either despise or choose what he does not know? etc., *as far as* what he ought not to have lost.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- « But of the tree, » etc. [n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 15.)] The tree from this, etc., *as far as* that is, that they were not about to lose what they loved.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (SEVERIANUS.)] There were three differences of trees in [0089A] paradise. ⟦s2⟧ One indeed had been given him that he might live, another that he might live well, the third that he might live always.
