## CHAPTER II. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « For in whatsoever day, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ He does not say, If thou eat, thou shalt be mortal, but « thou shalt die the death. » For man died in his soul when he sinned, because God withdrew from him, who is the life of the soul: upon which followed the death of the body, the soul departing from him, which is the life of the body; and this befell Adam when he ended the present life. ⟦s3⟧ It can be understood thus, that when they sinned, they were at once punished with that death of which it is said in Rom. seven: « Unhappy man that I am! who shall deliver me from the body of this death? » and in the same, chapter eight: « The body is dead because of sin; » he does not say mortal, but « dead, » although it is mortal, because it is to die. ⟦s4⟧ It is not to be believed that before sin those bodies were so, [0089B] but animal, not yet spiritual, yet not dead — bodies, that is, which must needs die, which came about on the day of the transgression.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Thou shalt die the death. » A twofold death is signified: that of the soul, the Lord departing, who is its life; or that of the body, which he received at the end of his life. ⟦s2⟧ But at once he incurred the necessity of dying.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- « The Lord also said, » etc. [n: (AUG., l. IX de Gen., c. 8.)] Those who hold that it was not lawful to have intercourse in paradise for the begetting of children, etc., *as far as* soul from soul, or in whatever other way the soul comes to be.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- « He brought them, » etc. [n: (AUG., l. IX de Gen. ad litt., c. 14, tom. III.)] Not as hunters do, or fowlers; nor was there a voice of command made out of a cloud, such as rational souls are wont to understand; for this [0089C] beasts or birds do not naturally understand, etc., *as far as* they are understood to have been created of earth.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (HIERON.)] It seems that the first language of the human race was Hebrew, because the names which we read in Genesis until the division of tongues are agreed to be of that language.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- « And he called, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] *Mystically.* ⟦s2⟧ Signifying the Gentiles who should be saved in the Church through Christ, and should receive the name of Christ, which before they did not have; whence, Isa. LXV: « I will call my servants by another name. »

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « But for Adam there was not found, » etc. [n: (AUG., lib. IX de Gen. ad litt., cap. 15 et seq.)] It must be seen how the forming of the woman was made, which is mystically called a building, etc., *as far as* that we may understand that through that ecstasy Adam said these things by God's gift.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0089D] « But for Adam there was not found a helper. » No faithful just man can be made equal to Christ. ⟦s2+3⟧ Psalm XLIV: « For he is beautiful in form above the sons of men. » For no one could deliver the human race but himself. ⟦s4⟧ Whence Apocalypse V: « No one was found worthy to open the book and to loose the seals thereof, » etc.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « But for Adam there was not found, » etc. [n: (AUG., de Gen. contra Manich., l. II, c. 13.)] The woman was made for the help of the man, etc., *as far as* and to subjugate to the dominion of reason, grace cooperating.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- « The Lord therefore cast, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ *[n: (ISID. ex Aug., ut supra, c. 13, 14.)] Mystically.* ⟦s3⟧ These things cannot be seen with bodily eyes. ⟦s4⟧ But the further a man withdraws from things visible to the secrets of understanding, as it were by falling asleep, [0090A] the better and more sincerely he sees, etc., *as far as* and to be referred to the Church.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., lib. X de Gen. ad litt., c. I.)] Those who think that the soul is from a soul, etc., *as far as* which had been said of the man.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., ibid., c. 19, 20.)] If it be asked whence Christ received his soul, I would rather hear men better and more learned; etc., *as far as* to whom what answer might be made has not yet occurred to me.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The Lord therefore cast a deep sleep, » etc. [n: (GREG., Moral., l. XXX, tom. II.)] We do not attain the summit of contemplation, if we do not cease from the oppression of outward care: nor do we look upon our own selves, so as to know that there is in us one thing rational which rules, another animal which is ruled, unless, betaking ourselves to the secret of silence, [0090B] we are lulled asleep from outward disturbance. ⟦s2⟧ Which Adam well figured in his sleeping, from whose side the woman presently came forth. ⟦s3⟧ For he who is caught up to the understanding of inward things closes his eyes to visible things, and then understands that there is in himself one thing which ought to rule, as a man; another which should be ruled, as a woman.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22, 24.-- « And he built. . . . ⟦s2⟧ Wherefore a man shall leave, » etc. [n: (ISID., in Gen., t. V.)] What was fulfilled in Adam according to the history, etc., *as far as* For the serpent prefigures the poisons of heretics.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- « And they were both naked. » [n: (AUG.)] If anything is said in the words of God or of any prophet whatever which seems absurd to the letter and therefore to have been said figuratively, yet it is not to be doubted that it was said for the sake of its signification. ⟦s2⟧ But the bodies of the two human beings [0090C] in paradise were naked, and they were not ashamed, because they had felt no law in their members warring against the law of the mind, which followed upon disobedience. ⟦s3⟧ They thought nothing was to be veiled, because they had felt nothing was to be restrained.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., de Civ. Dei, l. XIV, c. 17, tom. VII, col. 425.)] Deservedly is there shame chiefly of lust, etc., *as far as* and it rendered them confounded.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (RUPERT.)] That they were naked was not a matter of ignominy, but of honour: that they were not ashamed of their nakedness was not a matter of folly, but of security. ⟦s2⟧ For what? did it receive this from its maker, that the workmanship of God is shameful and to be blushed at? ⟦s3⟧ Or is it nature, and not rather conscience, that makes confusion of this kind? ⟦s4⟧ It is indeed as it were turned into nature, [0090D] this torment of confusion: nevertheless its origin sprouted not from the primeval condition, but from a later fault. ⟦s5⟧ For we blush at our nakedness for this reason, that we are conscious to ourselves how great an infirmity, nay how great a rebellion of our own flesh against us, we carry about. ⟦s6⟧ But then the conscience of the heart was sound, since no concupiscence of the eyes solicited it; seeing that the concupiscence of the flesh is the punishment of sin, by which the pride of the mind that went before is punished. ⟦s7⟧ God, says the Scripture, made man upright. ⟦s8⟧ But the uprightness of man lies in this, that the spirit should preside over and command the flesh, as the superior over the inferior: for the rational spirit of man was set midway between God and his own flesh, that it might obey God [0091A] and command the flesh. ⟦s9⟧ And because that former man disturbed and broke this order, by casting away the precept of God: the spirit, which was superior, was made subject to the lowest flesh, so that by a just punishment he should lie beneath the inferior, who scorned rightly to be beneath the superior. ⟦s10⟧ Hence that confusion of the face, breaking out from the secrets of conscience to public eyes, and driving a penal ignominy to go beneath the wretched solaces of coverings. ⟦s11⟧ This infirmity, I say, or rebellion of the flesh, was not yet: rightly therefore was each of them naked and they were not ashamed, because they had nothing at which to blush. ⟦s12⟧ That security of nakedness being lost, we groan, in that we are despoiled of glory and honour and wrapped about with rags. ⟦s13⟧ For so clothed we truly walk naked, even if — which is impossible — we should be clothed as the lilies [0091B] of the field are clothed. ⟦s14⟧ For not even Solomon in all his glory was clothed as one of these. ⟦s15⟧ If, I say, we could be clothed as these are clothed, even so are we despoiled and naked. ⟦s16⟧ Whence we look for the Saviour, our Lord Jesus Christ, who will reform the body of our lowliness, made like to the body of his brightness. ⟦s17⟧ For when the just shall shine as the sun in the kingdom of their Father, shall they be covered with tunics and cloaks? ⟦s18⟧ By no means, but to the glory of their maker, whatever vessels of mercy they shall have been, they will lie open to one another with mutual sights of glory, shining without, and joyful within; whole in body, and glad in mind; blessed, I say, in body and soul alike.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And they were both naked, » etc. [n: (ISID., in Gen., ex Aug. de Gen. contra Manich., lib. II, c. 15.)] That [0091C] they were naked and were ashamed, etc., *as far as* because in consent the conscience is held guilty.
