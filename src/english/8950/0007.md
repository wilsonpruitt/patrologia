## CHAPTER III.

VERS. 1-4.-- « But the serpent also was more subtle than all the living creatures, » etc. [n: (AUG., lib. II de Gen. ad litt. c. 2, 3, tom. III.)] Others, « most prudent, » said by transference. Certain codices have « most wise, » not in the proper sense, as the wisdom of God, or of the angels, or of the rational soul, is wont to be taken in a good sense, etc., *as far as* but the power is from God.

« But the serpent, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] So the devil spoke through the serpent, which knew nothing, as he does through the possessed or the frenzied: for whomsoever he seizes, he takes away understanding and reason. But the good Spirit, whomsoever he has filled with grace, makes them seeing and understanding, [0091D] whence the spirit of the prophets is subject to the prophets.

« Who said to the woman. » [n: (AUG., lib. II de Gen. ad litt., c. 30, et ex capp. 7, 8, 6, 10, passim.)] The transgression of the woman is inexcusable, because she was mindful of God's precept. Yet forgetfulness, etc., *as far as these* c. 10: He foresees that there will be good men and evil, and creates them: furnishing himself to the good for their enjoyment, bestowing many things even upon the evil.

« Why hath he commanded, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Therefore the crafty enemy asks this, and calls to mind God's command, lest, when she had sinned, she should have an excuse as though she had forgotten it.

« Why hath he commanded, » etc. [n: (GREG. Moral., l. XXIII, tom. II.)] The enemy of the human race does the same daily: he labours to tear the words [0092A] of God out of the hearts of men, and to make his threats light, and to bring them to believe what he falsely promises, for he invites them to temporal things, that he may make light of the eternal punishments which God threatens. For, pledging the glory of the present life, he says: « Taste, and you shall be as gods, » and touch temporal concupiscence, and appear exalted in this world; and when he tries to remove the fear of the divine sentence, he says as it were: « Why hath God commanded you? »

[n: (AUG., lib. II de Gen. ad litt., c. 4, tom. III.)] It is asked why God permitted man to be tempted, etc., *as far as* or earthly, as is the soul of man.

[n: (Ibid., c. 5.)] Nor is it to be thought that man would have been cast down, unless there had gone before in him a certain elation to be repressed, so that through the humbling of sin he might know that he had falsely [0092B] presumed concerning himself, and that a nature once made is not well with itself, if it has withdrawn from its maker. For it is commended what a good God is, when it is well for no one to withdraw from him.

[n: (Ibid., c. 18.)] This question concerning the blessed life, etc., *as far as* less than in the life of the holy angels.

[n: (Ibid., c. 16.)] It can be thought not without cause that the devil fell from the beginning of time, etc., *as far as* to whom God did not give foreknowledge concerning themselves?

[n: (Ibid., c. 14.)] But how can they be blessed, to whom their own blessedness is uncertain? etc., *as far as* but on what ground they assert it does not occur to me.

[n: (Ibid., c. 21.)] But it must be thought not without cause that from the very beginning of the creature, etc., *as far as* foreseeing [0092C] how great things he would do out of him in his own goodness.

[n: (Ibid., c. 22.)] « But the beginning of the thing formed » is said, etc., *as far as* as evil men.

[n: (Ibid., c. 23.)] Whereas, then, it is thought that the devil never stood in the truth, etc., *as far as* more things agree with his body.

[n: (GREG., hom. 16, in Evang., tom. II.)] The devil tempted the first parent in three ways, etc., *as far as* by delight we are overcome, by consent we are bound.

VERS. 5.--. . . . . « You shall be as gods, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] *Mystically.* He frames his words with artful guile, so that, if he cannot overthrow them by contempt of obedience, at least in this, that he has corrupted faith and persuaded to idolatry, he may boast himself the victor: but if he has seduced them by contempt of obedience, he may stand [0092D] victor in both.

[n: (AUG., de Gen. contra Manich., l. II, c. 15, tom. III.)] The woman, being questioned, answered, etc., *as far as* for, with none ruling him, God alone is blessed through his own power.

VERS. 6, 7.-- « She saw therefore, » etc. [n: (ISID. ex Aug. ubi supra. )] How did they see, if their eyes were closed? But it was said so that we may understand that those eyes were opened, after they had eaten of the fruit, with which they saw themselves naked and were displeased: that is, the eyes of craftiness, to which simplicity is displeasing. For when a man has fallen from the inmost light of truth, there is nothing whereby pride would please, save by fraudulent pretences. For hence is born hypocrisy, in which those seem very [0093A] shrewd who have been able to deceive whom they would.

« She saw therefore, » etc. [n: (GREG., lib. XXI Moral., cap. 2.)] Lest we be turned about in slippery thought, we ought not to gaze upon what it is not lawful to desire, etc., *as far as* the discipline of the outward senses also must be kept.

« And she gave to her husband, who did eat. And the eyes of them both were opened, » etc. [n: (ISID. ex Aug. ubi supra, c. 16, 17.)] Of whom it was said: « Then they saw that they were naked, » [var: Vulg. Gn 3:7 *cumque cognovissent se esse nudos*, "and when they had known that they were naked"] with perverse eyes, namely, to which nakedness, that is simplicity, seemed a thing to blush at. etc., *as far as* « My soul is troubled within myself. » [n: (Psal. XLI.)]

« Who did eat, » etc. [n: (AUG., de Gen., lib. XI, c. 3, tom. III, col. 429.)] Namely to lust [0093B] after one another, etc., *as far as* which would convict the sinner by the deed, and teach the reader by the writing.

« They sewed together fig leaves. » [n: (ALCUIN., in Gen. t. I.)] Because they had lost the glory of simple chastity, they fled for refuge to the twofold itch of lust; whence John I: « When thou wast under the fig tree I saw thee, and I came down, » [var: Vulg. Jo 1:48 *cum esses sub ficu, vidi te*, with no *et descendi*] supply « to deliver thee. »

VERS. 8.-- « And when they had heard, » etc. [n: (AUG., l. II de Gen. ad litt., c. 33.)] For at that hour it was fitting that such as they should be visited, because they had fallen away from the light of truth, etc., *as far as* he may be believed to have appeared to the bodily senses by a local and temporal motion.

« He hid himself, » etc. [n: (AUG., ibid.)] When God turns away his face within and man is troubled, things like madness come about, through excessive fear and shame, with a hidden [0093C] instinct also not resting: so that unknowing they did things which should signify something to those who came after, for whose sake these things are written.

« At the afternoon air. » Whence at the ninth hour Christ gave up the spirit [n: (Matth. XXVII)]. He was crucified at the sixth hour, that man might be restored at the same hour at which he was cast out of paradise.

« In the midst of the tree of paradise. » He hides himself who, turned away from the precept of the Creator, lives by the will of his own error and choice.

VERS. 9.-- « And the Lord God called Adam. » [n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 34.)] By rebuking, that is, not by being ignorant, etc., *as far as* but now we are not reporting things signified, but defending things done.

VERS. 10-11.-- « And he said: I heard thy voice, » etc. [0093D] *[n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 34.)] Historically.* It is probable that a human form was wont to appear to the first men through a fitting creature, etc., *as far as* and there followed that of which they were ashamed.

« Thy voice, » etc. *[n: (ISID., in Gen. tom. V.)] Mystically.* He answers that, having heard his voice, he had hidden himself, etc., *as far as* and not to obey the precept of God.

« And God called Adam, » etc. [n: (GREG.)] Because he saw him fallen into fault, now under sin, as though hidden from the eyes of truth, because he does not approve the darkness of his error, he is as it were ignorant where the sinner is, and calls him and seeks him, saying: « Adam, where art thou? » [var: Vulg. Gn 3:9 *et dixit ei: Ubi es?*, without the vocative *Adam*] While he calls, he signifies that he is calling him back to penance; while he seeks him, [0094A] he intimates that he knows not sinners justly condemned.

VERS. 12.-- « And Adam said: The woman whom thou gavest, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] He does not say, I have sinned: for pride has the deformity of confusion, not the humility of confession. But it was written for this, and the questions were put for this, that they might be written truly and profitably, so that we may know with how great a disease of pride they labour who today try to refer evil to the Creator, and, if they do any good, to attribute it to themselves.

« The woman whom thou gavest, » etc. As though she had been given for this, that she herself should not obey the man, and both of them God.

« The woman whom thou gavest, » etc. [n: (GREG., lib. IV Mor., c. 27, tom. I, col. 661.)] In four ways is sin committed in the heart, etc., *as far as* at the last it is nourished by the seduction of false hope, or by the obstinacy of wretched despair [0094B].

VERS. 13.-- « And the Lord God said to the woman, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] Neither does she confess the sin, but refers it to another — in an unequal sex, with equal haughtiness. Yet David, born of these, says unto the end of the world: « I said: Lord, be merciful to me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against thee [n: (Psal. XL)]. » This ought to have been said by those two: but God does not yet regard the necks of sinners: there remained the labours and sorrows of death, and the wearing away of the world, and the grace of God, which comes to help in due time, teaching the afflicted not to presume of themselves.

« The serpent deceived, » etc. As though anyone's persuasion should prevail over God's precept.

« The serpent deceived me, » etc. [n: (AUG., ibid., lib. II, c. 30.)] How by these words, etc., *as far as* since with that [0094C] food he did not see her to be dead.

[n: (AUG., ibid., c. 28.)] It is to be noted that the devil was not permitted to tempt the woman, etc., *as far as* but reason shows that on this account he envied man.

[n: (ALC.)] The sin of the angel was left unspoken, that of man laid open, because the one was incurable, the other was cured.

« She saw therefore, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] She is plainly shown to be inexcusable, since it was open to her, under such deliberation, to mark the wiles of the enemy.

VERS. 14.-- « And the Lord God said, » etc. [n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 36.)] This whole sentence is figurative. Where nevertheless it must be believed that all things were said. The serpent is not asked why he did it, because it was not he that did it by his own will and nature, but the devil out of him, and through him, and in him: who already for his impiety and pride had been destined [0094D] for eternal fire. To him, therefore, is referred what is said to the serpent, and it is shown what he will be to the human race.

*Mystically.* « And the Lord God said to the serpent, » etc. [n: (AUG., de Gen. cont. Manich., lib. II, c. 17.)] The serpent, unquestioned, is the first to receive punishment, etc., *as far as* that he may shut out the very beginning of the evil suggestion.

« Upon thy breast, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] « Belly » the Seventy added, but in the Hebrew there is « breast [nt: In the Hebrew it is *gahon*, which signifies *belly* and *breast*.] » only, that he might lay open the craft and cunning of his thoughts, that is, that his every step is of wickedness and of fraud.

[0095A] « With breast and belly thou shalt creep. » [n: (GREG., lib. XXI Mor., c. 2.)] The evil of lechery is committed either in thought or in deed. For when the enemy is driven out from the accomplishment of the deed, he labours to defile the secret places of thought. He creeps with the belly, when, slippery, through the human members made subject to him, he practises lechery in deed. He crawls with the breast, when he defiles in thought. But because it is through thought that one comes to deeds, he is described as creeping first with the breast and afterwards with the belly.
