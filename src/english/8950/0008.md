## CHAPTER III. (cont.)

VERS. 15.-- « I will put enmities between thee and the woman . . . She shall crush thy head, » etc. [n: (GREG. l. I Mor., c. 38.)] To crush the serpent's head is to root out the beginnings of his suggestion from the entrance of the heart by the hand of watchful consideration; for when he is caught at the very outset, he contrives to strike the heel, because the man whom he does not strike in the first temptation [0095B] he aims to deceive at the end. But if the heart is corrupted in the temptation, the middle and the end of the action that follows are possessed by the enemy, because he sees the whole tree bearing fruit for him, the tree which he has vitiated at the root with the tooth of his venom.

[n: (HIERON.)] « She shall keep thy heel, and thou shalt crush her heel. » [var: Vulg. Gn 3:15 *ipsa conteret caput tuum, et tu insidiaberis calcaneo ejus*, "she shall crush thy head, and thou shalt lie in wait for her heel"] It stands better in the Hebrew: « She shall crush thy head, and thou shalt crush her heel, » because both our steps are hampered by the serpent, and the Lord shall crush Satan under her feet.

VERS. 16.-- « To the woman also, » etc. [n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 37.)] These things are better understood figuratively and prophetically, etc., *as far as* nature will be depraved, and [0095C] guilt increased.

« I will multiply thy sorrows, » etc. [n: (AUG., de Gen. contra Manich., l. II, c. 19.)] It is manifest that the woman's pains were multiplied, etc., *as far as* but first there was a struggling with grief against an evil habit.

VERS. 17-18.-- . . . « Cursed is the earth, » etc. [n: (AUG. ibid., c. 38.)] That these are the labors of the human race upon earth no one is ignorant, and that they would not be, if the happiness which was in paradise were held fast, stands beyond doubt.

[n: (STRAB.)] The earth is cursed, not Adam, so that all men for whose sake these things were written might be terrified, lest, doing the like, they should be struck with a like punishment. But Cain himself, because he added fratricide to the first transgression, is cursed when it is said: « Thou shalt be cursed upon the earth, » [0095D] etc.

[n: (AUG.)] He cursed the earth, not the waters, because man ate of the fruit of the earth against the prohibition, and did not drink of the waters, and in the waters the sin contracted from the fruit of the earth was to be washed away. Land animals too have more of the curse than water creatures, because they live more from the cursed earth. Hence Christ after the resurrection ate of fish, not of a land animal.

« Cursed is the earth in thy work. » [n: (GREG., Moral. lib. IV, c. 5, 6.)] God curses the sinning man, etc., *as far as* and the flame burned up the two captains of fifty.

« In labors, » etc. [n: (RAB.)] Thorns were there before, but not so as to bring labor upon man: for him they were, after the sin, [0096A] for labor and affliction, as were serpents and whatever things are harmful, harmless before. And it is to be noted that on Adam an outward penalty is laid: « Cursed is the earth in thy work; » but on the woman an inward one, when it is said: « I will multiply thy sorrows, » etc., because, having been seduced, she seduced her husband.

VERS. 19.-- « Dust thou art, » etc. [n: (GREG., ubi supra.)] Man was so constituted, etc., *as far as* old age passes over into death.

VERS. 20.-- « And Adam called the name of his wife Eve, » etc. [n: (AUG., ubi supra. )] These were the words of the first man, since she is the mother of all the living, as though he were rendering the reason of the name imposed by himself, namely why he had called her Eve.

*Mystically. [n: (AUG., de Gen. contra Manich., l. II, c. 21.)]* [0096B] After the sin and the judge's sentence Adam called his wife Eve, etc., *as far as* it is expressed in the skins which are stripped from dead bodies.

VERS. 21.-- « He made also, » etc. [n: (AUG., de Gen. lib. XI, c. 39.)] This too was done for the sake of signification, and in deeds it is asked what was done and what it signifies, and in words what was said and what it signifies.

[n: (STRAB.)] They themselves made girdles for themselves, that they might hide their sin: but God made tunics of skin, with which he clothed their whole body, because he justly condemned them both in body and in soul. But it is a foolish question to ask how, or by whose working, the skins were drawn off from the animals: for he who founded all things out of nothing made these [0096C] how and in what manner he willed.

VERS. 22.-- « And he said: Behold Adam is as one of us, » etc. [n: (AUG., ibid., c. 41.)] The plural number is used on account of the Trinity, etc., *as far as* as in the Church men are wont to be removed from the visible sacraments by the Church's discipline.

[n: (AUG., ubi supra, c. 42.)] It is a wonder if Adam was spiritual in mind, etc., *as far as* which thought the false to be true.

« As one of us. » [n: (AUG., de Gen. contra Manich., lib. II, c. 21.)] It can be understood in two ways, « as one of us, » that is, God, etc., *as far as* until by God's mercy he should live again who had been dead.

[n: (RUPERT.)] Lest he eat of the tree of life, and, this being done, Adam, made such and so wretched, live for ever. For what [0096D] if, having been made such, he should live for ever? assuredly nothing but eternal evil. For being already made wretched, if he be also eternal, what will he have but eternal wretchedness? Let us spare him, lest in this way it be ill with him « as one of us; » lest, as each of us is eternal (for the Father is eternal, the Son eternal, the Holy Spirit eternal), so he too be eternal, and for this the wicked eye of the devil laugh that he is truthful, who promised him the likeness of God. For it is indeed an evil for him to be temporal, but it would be worst of all to be eternal. Exceedingly evil and false is this likeness of God. Far better is it for him to be wholly unlike God. How? Namely thus, that, because he is wretched, he be also temporal; this is to be wholly unlike God. [0097A] For God is both eternal and happy, and his is an eternal happiness, a happy eternity. Of these the devil lost the one, that is, happiness, but eternity he did not lose; and his is an eternal unhappiness, an unhappy eternity. Let us spare the man, I say; and because he has lost happiness, let us cut off eternity also from the unhappy one, that in neither may he be « as one of us; » ours is an eternal happiness, a happy eternity; let his be a temporal misery, or a miserable temporality; that eternity may then be restored to him to his profit, when happiness has been recovered. In great wrath, therefore, the Lord remembered great mercy, in this very thing, that he did not grant the tree of life to wretched man: him indeed whom he willed to be so unlike himself that he should not be in both respects like the devil, that is, that man should not live, equally with the devil, [0097B] until the last judgment, and, equally with him, pass over without temporal death to eternal burning.

VERS. 23.-- « And he sent him forth, » etc. [n: (AUG., de Gen. contra Manich., l. II, c. 22.)] Into a place suited to him; as very often a bad man, when he has begun to live among the good, if he has been unwilling to be changed for the better, is driven from the congregation of the good by the weight of his own depraved habit.

VERS. 24.-- « And he cast out Adam. » [n: (HIERON. Quaest. Hebr., tom. III.)] LXX: « And he cast him out, and made him dwell over against the paradise of pleasure, and he set the cherubim, and the flaming sword, which is turned about to guard the way of the tree of life. » There is another sense in the Hebrew; for it says, « and the Lord cast out, » namely Adam, « and [0097C] made the cherubim and the flaming sword to dwell before the paradise of pleasure. » Not that he made Adam dwell over against the paradise of pleasure, but that, when Adam had been cast out, he placed the cherubim and the flaming sword before the doors of paradise, that no one might be able to enter.

« And he placed, » etc. [n: (AUG., ut supra. )] This is to be believed to have been done through the heavenly powers even in the visible paradise, so that through angelic ministry there was there a certain fiery guard, yet not in vain, but because it signifies something of the spiritual paradise.

« He placed, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] That is, through the ministry of angels he established a fiery guard. The flaming sword is the tribulations, griefs and labors of this life, by which we are exercised; or the sentence of just condemnation which was given to man, who was so condemned [0097D] that he could be redeemed by Christ's passion. Whence the sword is called turning, because it can be removed. For it was removed for Enoch and Elias, and daily it is removed for the faithful who pass from this life to the blessedness above. Nor does he say a flame and a sword, but « a flaming sword: » for they who return to paradise must burn away with the fire of the Holy Spirit all the concupiscences of this life, and cut off with the sword of the word of God all things harmful.

« And he placed, » etc. [n: (ISID., in Gen., tom. V.)] The cherubim, fullness of knowledge: this is charity, which is the fullness of the divine law. The flaming sword, temporal punishments, which are turning, since times are revolving. To the tree of life, therefore, there is no return [0098A] except through the cherubim, that is, the fullness of knowledge, that is, charity: and through the turning sword, that is, temporal endurance.
