## CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 1.-- « But Adam, » etc. [n: (AUG., lib. IX de Gen. ad litt., c. 4)]. In paradise virginity, outside it marriage, etc., *as far as* but afterwards begotten in exile.

« She conceived and bore, » etc. Cain and Abel, begotten of one mother, are a figure of all men who are propagated into this life from the root of sin; and some are to love the earthly city and its death-bearing delights, and, so far as in them lies, to possess them by ambition; these Cain signifies, who is interpreted *possession.* Others are to seek the city to come, and, mourning over the miseries of this habitation, [0098B] to pass over with all their desire to the glory to come — these Abel signifies, who is interpreted *mourning,* and is the later: because that which is spiritual is not first, but that which is animal. Hence Cain is called « a husbandman, » that is, one bent upon earthly works; but Abel « a shepherd of sheep, » that is, one loving simplicity and innocence.

*Allegorically.* [n: (ISID., in Gen. )] The two sons of Adam express two peoples, namely the Jewish, the elder by birth, etc., *as far as* as though reason did not permit the depraved motion to come to effect, but strove to subdue it.

VERS. 2.-- « And Abel was, » etc. In whom are signified those who mourn over their earthly dwelling. For Abel is interpreted *mourning,* or *vapor,* because he was a mourning to his parents, or, like a vapor, quickly vanished away.

[0098C]VERS. 3, 4.-- « And it came to pass, » etc. « Abel also offered, » etc. Because the just man in all that he does strives to please God through faith and charity (from which the other virtues arise, and without which they profit nothing), which is signified in the fat that was offered; whence psal. LXII: « As with marrow and fatness let my soul be filled. » Without charity the soul is lean and languid, and in nothing will it please God.

« And the Lord had respect to Abel, » etc. [n: (HIER., Quaest. Hebr. in Gen., tom. III.)] Whence could Cain have known this, unless Theodotion's rendering were true: « And the Lord set fire upon Abel and upon his sacrifice, upon Cain and his sacrifice he did not set fire? » Now we read that fire was wont to come from heaven to devour the sacrifice, as at the dedication of the temple [0098D] under Solomon, and when Elias built an altar on Mount Carmel.

[n: (ISID., ut supra. )] If thou offer rightly, and do not rightly divide, thou hast sinned: because, even though the Jews formerly offered those things rightly, in this they are guilty, that they did not distinguish the New Testament from the Old.

VERS. 5, 6, 7.-- . . . . « In the doors. » [n: (ALC.)] That is, sin will accompany thee going in and going out, nor will the Lord keep thy coming in and thy going out.

VERS. 8.-- « And Cain said, » etc. [n: (ISID., Quaest. in Gen., tom. V.)] He does not acquiesce in the Lord when he says, Be still, etc., *as far as* Christ therefore, the head of the younger people, is slain by the elder-born.

VERS. 9.-- « And the Lord said to Cain. » *(ISID., ubi [0099A] supra.)* The Lord questions Cain, not as one ignorant that he may learn, but as a judge that he may punish. To this day the Jews, when we question them out of the Scriptures concerning Christ, answer that they do not know what we say. Cain's lying ignorance is the Jews' false denial: but they would be Christ's keepers, if they believed with the heart and confessed with the mouth.

VERS. 10.-- « The voice of the blood, » etc. Great is the power of justice, which demands vengeance for the blood of the innocent.

VERS. 11.-- « Now therefore thou shalt be cursed, » etc. [n: (RAB.)] It is to be noted that in Adam's sin the earth is cursed. But Cain is cursed, because, knowing the condemnation of the first transgression, he added fratricide.

VERS. 11.-- « Now therefore thou shalt be cursed, » etc. By the place are signified the things which are in the place; as though thou shalt be cursed [0099B] by those who are upon the earth, who opened their mouth in the confession of sins, to receive the blood which was shed for the remission of them from the hand of the persecutor, who would not be under grace, but under the law, so that he should be cursed by the Church: which understands and shows him to be cursed: for they who are of the works of the law are under a curse.

VERS. 12.-- « When thou hast tilled, » etc. The reference is made to the word, not to the sense, because "earth" is taken here in one way, above in another: there for the Church, here for the earthly working which the Jew still practises; that is, carnal circumcision, the passover, the sabbath, and the like, which have a hidden power of understanding the grace of God, but do not give fruit to the Jews, who are unwilling to pass over to Christ. By "earth" the flesh of Christ can be understood, in [0099C] which the Jews by crucifying wrought our salvation. « For he died for our sins, and rose again for our justification: » [var: Vulg. Rom 4:25 *Qui traditus est*, "who was delivered up"] and because they denied him, they were not justified.

« A vagabond and a fugitive, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] Scattered among the nations, a fugitive from Jerusalem. LXX: « groaning and trembling. » Groaning, from the grief of the kingdom he has lost; trembling, at the multitude of the Christian people, lest thou be slain. « My iniquity is greater, » etc. He exaggerates his fault, but confesses it proudly, and does not beg pardon.

VERS. 13.-- « My iniquity is greater. » [n: (ISID.)] Adding sins to sins he despairs, and does not believe that he can obtain pardon, which is blasphemy against the Holy Spirit, which is not forgiven in this world nor in the world to come: because he thinks that God either will not forgive, [0099D] or cannot, as though either he could not do all things, or begrudged salvation. He does not count it a grievous thing that God is angry with him, but fears that he may be found and killed.

VERS. 14.-- « Every one therefore that shall find me, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Because from the trembling of the body and the agitation of a frenzied mind, whosoever shall find me will know me to be guilty of death.

VERS. 15.-- « It shall not be so. » That is, thou shalt not die quickly, as thou wishest, for a remedy of thy torment, but he shall be punished impersonally: that is, punishment shall be made of thee sevenfold, because thou shalt live in torment until the seventh generation, in which thou shalt be slain by Lamech; therefore every one who shall kill — supply the word — shall free thee from that torment of an evil conscience. This sense [0100A] appears more evidently. According to the LXX. « Every one that killeth Cain shall pay seven vengeances, » that is, whosoever shall kill thee in the seventh generation shall release thee from torment by death, thou who art to be reserved in life until that time, only for punishment, which punishment would be quickly ended if thou wert quickly slain. So he who should punish the Jews with visible death would take away from them the seven vengeances by which they are bound because of the guilt of Christ slain, because he would lighten the miseries and dispersions which they suffer.

« Every one that killeth Cain, » etc. LXX: « Shall pay seven vengeances. » Aquila, « sevenfold; » Symmachus, « sevenfold; » Theodotion, « by a week » — so they render it, wherein it is signified that he shall be punished long and completely, lest any one dare [0100B] to repeat a homicide.

« He that killeth Cain, » etc. [n: (HIERON., epist. ad Damas. )] If any one kill thee « he shall be punished sevenfold, » that is, he shall be punished with the heaviest vengeance: a man ought not to have killed him for whom God reserved life, either for his own punishment, or for the correction of others.

« But every one that killeth Cain. » [n: (ISID., ubi supra. )] It is to be read by ellipsis, etc., *as far as* and that is fulfilled: « Scatter them in thy strength [n: (Psal. LIII)]. »

[n: (HIER., ubi supra. )] That Aquila put « sevenfold, » etc., *as far as* and might deserve to be absolved.

« And the Lord set a mark upon Cain, » etc. [n: (ISID., Quaest. in Gen., tom. V.)] Though all the nations subdued by the Romans passed over into their rites, the Jewish nation, whether under Gentiles or under Christian kings, [0100C] did not leave the sign of circumcision and of carnal observance, etc., *as far as* where paradise was planted.

« He dwelt in the land at the eastern quarter of Eden. » [n: (HIER., ubi supra. )] LXX: « He dwelt in the land Ναΐδ Naid, in the Hebrew it is called ( *nod* ), that is, unstable and wavering, and of uncertain seat. It is not the land Naid, as our common folk think, but the Lord's sentence is fulfilled, while he strays to and fro a fugitive.

VERS. 17.-- « And Cain knew his wife. » [n: (ISID., ibid.)] Figuratively the offspring of the impious, seeking a foundation in the very origin of the world, built in this life a city which he called Henoch, which is interpreted *dedication,* because in their first beginnings they dedicate, while in this life which is before them they plant the root of the heart, [0100D] that here they may flourish to their wish, and wither away from the glory to come. But Abraham dwelt in little cottages, because the saints know themselves to be guests and pilgrims, awaiting the city that has a foundation, whose builder is God.

« And he built a city, » etc. [n: (AUG., lib. XV de Civitate, cap. 8, tom. VII.)] It is asked with what helpers he built it, if there were not more men than Scripture says. But it is to be known that there were then far more, because by living long they were able to beget more. Nor is it a wonder, since the children of Israel are read to have been so multiplied in four hundred years that the army could not be numbered.

VERS. 18.-- « Moreover Enoch, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Not [0101A] from all these are sure mysteries carved out, nor would they be found, unless first certain roots of the history were laid down; nor wouldst thou gather fruit from the branches of the trees, unless thou hadst first planted the trunks.

VERS. 19.-- « Who took two wives, » etc. [n: (ISID., ut supra. )] It is to be noted that the generations from Adam through Cain are terminated in the eleventh, in which transgression is signified. For Lamech is the seventh, to whom three sons and one daughter are added, that the eleventh may be filled out, by which sin is signified: for the generation is closed also by a woman, who was the beginning of sin, through which is the pleasure of the flesh, which resists the spirit. Whence she is called Noema, that is, *pleasure.* In this offspring Lamech first, against custom, against nature, committed adultery through bigamy.

[0101B]VERS. 20.-- « And Ada bore Jabel, » etc. [n: (HIER. in quaest. Hebr. )] Of bigamy is born Jabel dwelling in tents, which, being changeable, figure the instability of those who are carried about with every wind of doctrine. There is born also Tubal, who was the inventor of music, etc., *as far as* like Jabel, Tubal and Tubalcain.

VERS. 21.-- « And his brother's name was Jubal. » [n: (RAB.)] Josephus writes that he wrote music on two columns, the one of stone, the other of brick, of which the one would not be dissolved by the flood, the other which would not be dissolved by fire: which two judgments Adam had foretold were to come.

VERS. 22.-- . . . « Tubalcain. [n: (RAB.)] Who according to Josephus practised the arts of war, and fittingly taught the smith's craft; he also invented certain things which [0101C] belong to the concupiscence of the eyes.
