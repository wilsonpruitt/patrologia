## CHAPTER IV. (cont.)

VERS. 23.-- « And Lamech said, » etc. [n: (RAB.)] The Hebrews say that Lamech by long living fell into a darkening of the eyes, and had a young man as his leader and the director of his way. Practising the hunt, therefore, he aimed an arrow where the young man pointed, and by chance killed Cain lurking among the bushes; and this is what he says: « I have slain a man to my own wounding, » that is, by the wound which I inflicted I killed not a beast but a man; whence also, kindled with fury, he killed the young man.

VERS. 23.-- . . . « I have slain a man, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] That is, Cain: « to my own wounding, » because for him I shall be wounded, that is, slain; « and a young man to my own bruising, » because for him too I shall be condemned.

VERS. 24.-- « Sevenfold vengeance shall be given, » etc. [0101D] [n: (ALC.)] *Allegorically.* The sin of Lamech (who is interpreted *humbled* ) signifies the casting down of the human race, which fell when Adam transgressed, and could not rise, neither by the law, or by circumcision, nor by its own justice, until, after seventy-seven generations according to Luke, Christ, coming into the world, blotted out sin by the shedding of his blood; whence Matth. XVIII:

« I say not to thee seven times, but until seventy times seven times, » that is, that the Jew is to be received after seventy-seven vengeances, if he shall come to himself again and seek Christ's indulgence. The number seven signifies universality: by which, the eleventh being multiplied, there comes the seventy-seventh, in which transgression of every kind is signified; [0102A] which nevertheless in the Gospel is commanded to be forgiven to one who repents.

[n: (HIERON., epist. ad Damas. )] They say that from Adam to Christ there are seventy-seven generations according to Luke. As therefore in the seventh generation Cain's sin was loosed: « For God will not judge twice for the same thing, » and he who has once received in his life will not suffer in death the same torments which he suffered in his life: so also Lamech, that is, the sin of the whole world, shall be loosed by Christ's coming. A certain Hebrew told me that seventy-seven souls came forth from Lamech, who all perished in the flood.

VERS. 25.-- « He knew also again, » etc. Through Seth, who is interpreted *resurrection,* is signified the life of Christ from the dead: which is signified in the mother's words: [0102B] « The Lord hath given me another seed, » [var: Vulg. Gn 4:25 reads *Deus*, "God"] as though the Church said: I who bore mourning for the dead rejoice over him raised again.

VERS. 26.-- « Whom he called Enos, » etc. Enos is interpreted *man:* who « began to call upon the name of the Lord, » because through the death of Christ, which Abel signified, and through the resurrection, which Seth signifies, man becomes one confessing the Lord as founder, and as restorer of the life that was lost.

[n: (HIERON.)] « Whom he called Enos. » Enos is interpreted *man,* who « began to call upon the name of the Lord: » as Adam is interpreted *man,* so also Enos according to the Hebrew truth is *man,* or *husband;* and beautifully, because he had this name, it is said that then was the beginning of calling upon the name of the Lord: although very many of the Hebrews [0102C] think that then for the first time idols were fashioned in the name of the Lord and in his likeness.

## CHAPTER V.

VERS. 1, 2.-- « This is the book of the generation of Adam. » He was made to the likeness of God. Others, to the likeness of Adam. It is as it were a conclusion from what has gone before, with a recapitulation. « In the day that God created man. » For in the day he was created, because he was set in the brightness of divine contemplation. « To the likeness of God. » Because he gave him reason, by which, discerning good and evil, he might love with understanding, and not hand over the things loved to forgetfulness, and in this retain the likeness of God. But after by disobedience [0102D] he had blotted out this likeness, being made earthly he begot earthly children. Whence, such as the earthly man is, such also are the earthly.

[n: (CHRYSOST., hom. 21 in Gen., tom. IV.)] Consider how he used the same words which he used also at the beginning, that he might teach us that he does not deign to remember those generations after this, as having been made reprobate; but from Seth, who has now been born to them, he begins the genealogy, that from this thou mayest learn how great account God takes of the human race, and how he turns away from those whose mind is bloody.

VERS. 3-23.-- « And Adam lived, » etc. [n: (HIERON., in Quaest. Hebr. )]. It is to be known that up to the flood, where in our codices someone is said to have begotten at « two hundred » and the years that run beyond, in the Hebrew [0103A] it has « a hundred years » and the rest which follow.

« And he begot, » etc. As though he too bore the image of the earthly man, he who was put in the place of the just Abel, and by the interpretation of his name signifies *Christ.* But all bear the image of the earthly man before that of the heavenly, although many before the coming of Christ had through faith the same grace by which Christians are saved.

[n: (STRAB.)] They report that after Abel's death Adam vowed that he would not know his wife further nor beget a son. But at God's bidding he broke the vow, that the Son of God might be born from him, and man be redeemed; for he ought not to be born of the stock of Cain. Whence before Seth he is not read to have begotten any one, but after him it is said, because « he begot sons and daughters. » This reckoning of the genealogies [0103B] spreads as it were the branches of the histories, from which in their own places the fruits of the mysteries are gathered. And it is to be noted that in the generation of Seth no woman is set down by name, as in the generation of Cain: for it belongs to the just to do all things manfully, to usurp nothing womanish, nothing frail. This generation also proceeds by the tenth up to Noe, in whom the fulfilling of the commandments is signified, as by the eleventh in the generation of Cain transgression is signified. In the generation of the unjust Enoch is set first, who is interpreted *dedication,* because the reprobate fix the root of their hope in the present life, and wither away from the love of the unfading inheritance. Of whom it is said, John V: « He saw the fool with a firm root, and cursed his beauty forthwith. » [var: Vulg. Job 5:3, where the words stand in the first person — *Ego vidi … et maledixi*, "I have seen … and I cursed"] In the generation of the just Enoch is set seventh from Adam, because these [0103C] do not seek a city that abides here, but stretch out their expectation to the end of time, which is terminated by a sevenfold revolution, when the eighth eternity shall have succeeded. Whence this Enoch, who is set seventh, and was translated because he pleased God, signifies the seventh rest, to which after this life he who perseveringly cleaves to God is transferred without delay. The interpretation also of the names of this generation signifies the same: Adam is interpreted *man* or *red earth;* Seth, *placing*; or *resurrection;* Enos, *man;* Cainan, *lamentation;* Malalehel, *praising God;* Jared, *strengthened;* Enoch, *dedication* Mathusalam, *sending forth of death;* Lamech, *humbled;* Noe, *rest.* Man therefore, proceeding from the earth, rising again from sins, will be a man calling upon God and weeping for his former sins: and so [0103D] praising God, strengthened by the Spirit of God, he is dedicated unto life eternal. And, a conqueror of death, made subject to God, he possesses everlasting rest, which Noe signifies, who is interpreted *rest,* and is numbered in the tenth place, because through the fulfilling of the commandments the kingdom of heaven is possessed.

VERS. 24.-- « And he walked with God, » etc. [n: (ISID., in Gen.)] Enoch, the seventh from Adam, pleasing God, etc., *as far as* for three times four make it.

[n: (ALCUIN.)] Enoch is preserved from death for so long a time, in which it is shown that man would always have lived if he had not sinned.

VERS. 25-30.-- « Mathusala also lived, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] It is asked how Mathusala, according to the [0104A] reckoning of the years, could have lived after the flood, since all perished except those who entered into the ark. But this question the faultiness of the codices has bred: for not only in the Hebrew is it found otherwise, but also in the LXX rendering, in a few codices Mathusala is found to have died seven years before the flood. That according to the LXX the years of Mathusala are reckoned beyond the flood, comes of this, that he signifies Christ, whose life feels no age, so that in the greater ones too he might seem not to have felt the flood.

VERS. 31.-- « And all the days were, » etc. [n: (HIERON., in Quaest. Hebr., tom. III.)] « And all the days of Mathusala which he lived were nine hundred sixty-nine years, and he died. » According to a careful computation he is reported to have lived [0104B] fourteen years after the flood, etc., *as far as* in that year, namely, in which the flood began to be.

« But Noe, when he was five hundred years old, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)]

Noe is interpreted *rest,* because under him all the works behind were at rest through the flood.
