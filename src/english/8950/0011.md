## CHAPTER VI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- « And when men had begun to be multiplied upon the earth. » [n: (HIER., in Quaest. Hebr. )] The Hebrew word ( *Elohim* ) is of both numbers: for it signifies *God* and *gods:* therefore Aquila dared to say « sons of gods, » understanding holy gods or angels, whom Symmachus follows, saying: « The sons of the mighty seeing the daughters of men. »

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0104C]VERS. 2.-- « The sons of God seeing, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] By the sons of God are understood the religious sons of Seth, who, overcome by concupiscence, took wives from the daughters of men, that is, from the stock of Cain, and begot mighty men, immense, namely, in body, proud in strength, disordered in manners, who are called giants. ⟦s2⟧ It is not incredible that men of this kind were procreated by men, not by angels or by certain demons who are shameless toward women, since after the flood too the bodies not only of men but also of women existed of incredible size.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3, 4.-- « And God said: My spirit shall not remain, » etc. [n: (HIER., ibid.)] Hebr. ( *loiadon* ). ⟦s2⟧ « My spirit shall not judge men for ever, since they are flesh. » That is, because [0104D] the condition of men is frail, etc., *as far as* but of those whom he was about to destroy by the flood.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And his days shall be, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Before the flood, namely, for the doing of penance. ⟦s2⟧ But because they persevered in malice before the appointed term, they were destroyed in the hundredth year.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 5.-- « And the Lord seeing, » etc. [var: Vulg. Gn 6:5 reads *Deus*, "God"] Repentance does not fall upon God, nor grief of heart, whose sentence concerning all things is as fixed as his foreknowledge is certain: but Scripture uses accustomed words, fitting itself to our littleness, that from things known we may come to know things unknown. ⟦s3⟧ For God's anger is not a perturbation of mind, but the judgment which is inflicted upon the sinner: and the thought of his heart is the unchangeable reason of things that are to be changed.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0105A]VERS. 6-8.-- « It repented him that, » etc. [n: (GREG., Moral. lib. V)]. ⟦s2⟧ Very often in sacred discourse the wise draw the counsel of God from the wise men of the world. ⟦s3⟧ So now, for man's profit, the very founder of men takes upon himself the voice of human passion, though it is certain that he who beholds all things before they have come to pass has done nothing of which he should repent and change his mind. ⟦s4⟧ As therefore it is no wonder if spiritual men very often use the words of carnal men, so neither is it to be wondered at if the creator himself of all things, that he may draw the flesh to his own understanding, keeps or takes up in himself the speech of the flesh.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9-12.-- « Noe a just man. » Here by his acts he signifies Christ, who says Matth. XI: « Learn of me, because I am meek and humble of heart. » He alone is found just, to whom because of his justice seven men [0105B] are given. ⟦s2⟧ Christ too is just and perfect, to whom seven Churches, enlightened by the sevenfold Spirit, are granted together into one Church. ⟦s3⟧ Noe is delivered through water and wood, and his household with him; so the household of Christ through baptism and the cross.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « In his generations. » As though not of consummate justice, but according to his own generation.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- « I will destroy them with, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ The doctors hand down that the greenness and fruitfulness of the earth was far inferior after the flood; and that therefore the eating of flesh was granted to men, whereas before they lived only on the fruits of the earth.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- « Make thee an ark of timber. » Namely of timber strong and indissoluble and well compacted; which another translation calls « square, » so that it should be dissolved neither by the force of the winds [0105C] nor by the inundation.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Little rooms, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] LXX: « Thou shalt make in the ark things of two chambers and of three chambers: of two chambers in the lower parts, of three chambers in the upper. » They were therefore divided into five dwellings: the first, that is the lowest, was the dung-place, into which the dung flowed down, lest those who were in the ark should be harmed by the stench; the second, the storeroom; the third, of the fierce animals and of serpents; the fourth, of the tame; the fifth, that is the highest, of men and of birds. ⟦s2⟧ The door, where the two-chambered and the three-chambered parts were joined, that is, between the storeroom and the dwelling of the beasts. [n: (AUG.)] In the first dwelling, in the lower parts, the ark was of one chamber, in the second, above the lower dwelling, of two chambers. ⟦s3⟧ But in the third, above the second, of three chambers. ⟦s4⟧ Others say that in the lower parts [0105D] there were two little rooms, and that so the ark was of two chambers. ⟦s5⟧ Of these the lower received the dung, the second the food, which was called the storeroom; in the upper parts it was of three chambers, where men and animals were contained with a fitting distinction.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID. in Gen., tom. V.)] Noe built the ark of imperishable timbers, etc., *as far as* prophesied in the fifth, evangelized in the sixth.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15. ⟦s2⟧ « Of three hundred cubits, » etc. [n: (ISID., ibid.)] In the three hundred cubits the wood of the cross can be shown, etc., *as far as* because there is one God from whom are all things, and one faith, and one baptism.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (GREG. hom. XVI in Ezech.)] The ark which was commanded to be made three hundred cubits in length, fifty [0106A] in breadth, thirty in height, etc., *as far as* and through whom all advance who have known themselves to be sinners.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16-18.-- « In a cubit thou shalt finish the top of it. » So the Church, gathered into unity, is lifted up and made perfect; whence Matth. XI: « He that gathereth not with me, scattereth. »

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The door of the ark, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ For no one enters into the Church except through the sacrament of the remission of sins, which flowed forth from the opened side.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Upper chambers and third stories, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ LXX: « Things of two chambers and of three chambers. » For out of all nations the Church gathers a multitude either two-parted, from the circumcision and the uncircumcision; or three-parted, from the three sons of Noe, by whose offspring the world is filled. ⟦s3⟧ But the lower parts [0106B] of the ark are divided, because in this earthly life there is a diversity of nations: but at the top all are consummated into one, because Christ is all things and in all.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ALCUIN.)] Fittingly the ark was brought to a point at the top, as with a certain narrow roof, that it might scatter the threats of the rains, and, standing below in the waters four-square with stability, could be neither tilted nor sunk by the driving of the winds, nor by the onset of the waves, nor by the restlessness of the animals that were within.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (GREG. in Moral.)] The ark broad in the lower parts, narrow in the upper, etc., *as far as* great heaps of chaff, which are burned up with fire.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (GREG.)] But morally, he who turns himself away from the love of the world builds in his heart the ark of salvation, having in [0106C] himself length, that is, the faith of the Trinity, and length of life and of immortality; breadth in charity, by which he can do good even to enemies; height in hope, by which he may raise himself to heavenly things and refer the sum of his acts to the One.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19-20.-- « And of all, » etc. [n: (AUG. l. XV de Civit. Dei, c. ult., t. VII.)] It is asked concerning the tiniest creatures, so in the Church's sacraments the good and the bad are engaged.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21, 22.-- « Take thou therefore, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is wont to be asked concerning the animals which are thought to feed only on flesh, whether they were there beyond the number without transgression of the commandment, which the necessity of feeding the others had compelled to be shut in; or, what is rather to be believed, whether besides flesh there could have been foods which would suit [0106D] all. ⟦s3⟧ For many animals, whose food is flesh, feed on fruits and apples, especially on the fig and on chestnuts. ⟦s4⟧ A just and wise man, therefore, divinely admonished, was able to find out and lay up the nourishment which suited each, besides flesh. ⟦s5⟧ And hunger compels all to feed, and there is nothing which God cannot make sweet and wholesome, who would even grant by divine power that they should live without food.
