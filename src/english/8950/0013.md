## CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 1-3.-- « And God remembered « Noah, » etc. « He brought a spirit over the earth, » etc. [n: (ALCUIN., quaest. in Gen.)] It can be understood of that spirit of which it was said, « The Spirit of God was borne over [0108D] the waters: » then it was borne, that, the waters being gathered into their own *[Al., unum]* place, the earth might appear: now it is brought, that, the waters of the flood being taken away, it might lay bare the face of the earth. By the spirit a wind can be understood, according to that of Psalm CVI: « And the spirit of the storm stood, » by whose blasts, namely, the water would draw back.

VERS. 4.-- « And the ark rested in the seventh month, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] It signifies the sevenfold rest, and, because the perfect rest there, the number of the aforesaid squaring is repeated also. For on the twenty-seventh day of the second month the sacrament is committed, and again on the twenty-seventh day of the seventh month it is confirmed, when the ark rested: for what is promised in hope is exhibited in fact.

[0109A] [n: (AUG.)] It is asked whether these things were done after the hundred and fifty days, or whether by recapitulation all those things are recalled which began to be done after the forty days of rain, so that this alone pertains to the « hundred and fifty days », that the water was raised as far as the mountains themselves, either from the fountains of the deep, the rain having now ceased, or because it remained at that height with no wind drying it. But the rest of the things that have been said were not all done after the hundred and fifty days, but recalled: things which began to be done from the end of the forty days.

VERS. 5, 6.-- « For in the tenth month. » It is to be noted that in the tenth month the tops of the mountains appeared, which the waters of the flood had overtopped by fifteen cubits, because the eminence of the saints understood, through the fulfilling of the precepts, the sacraments of eternal rest and the glory [0109B] of the resurrection (which the wisdom of the proud knew not); whence Psalm CXVIII: « By thy commandments I have understood. » And elsewhere, Luke X: « Thou hast hid these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones. »

VERS. 7.-- « Who went forth, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] That the raven did not return after forty days, either cut off by the waters or enticed by a floating carcase, figures those made most foul by the uncleanness of covetousness: and therefore that those too intent upon the things which are outside in the world are either rebaptized, or are seduced and held fast by those whom baptism outside the ark, that is, outside the Church, has slain.

[n: (AUG.)] It is asked: did the raven die, or could it in some way live? For if there was earth where it might rest, the dove would likewise have found rest. [0109C] Whence it is conjectured that the raven could settle on a carcase, which the dove by nature shuns.

VERS. 8.-- « He sent forth also. » [n: (ISID.)] The dove sent forth, and returned without having found rest, signifies that through the New Testament rest is not promised to the saints in this world: for it was sent forth after forty days, by which number the life that is passed in the world is designated.

[n: (STRAB.)] The ark contains a raven and a dove, and the Church good men and bad. The raven sent out does not return, because the reprobate, clinging to the enticements of the world, perish. The dove returned, bearing a branch of olive with green leaves in her mouth, because the saints, rejoicing in simplicity, bear the gift of peace in their mouth, saying: [n: (Psal. XIX)] « With them that hated peace I was peaceable: [0109D] when I spoke to them, » etc.

VERS. 9.-- « She returned. » [n: (AUG.)] It is a question: how did the dove not find where to settle, when the tops of the mountains had already been laid bare, according to the order of the narrative? This can be resolved by recapitulation, so that things narrated later are understood to have been done earlier, or rather, they had not yet been dried.

VERS. 10.-- « Having waited, » etc. After other seven days, the dove being let go on account of the sevenfold spiritual working, brought back a fruitful shoot of olive, signifying that many baptized outside the Church, if the richness of charity be not wanting, are at a later time brought back, in the mouth of the dove, as in the kiss of peace, to the fellowship of unity.

[0110A]VERS. 11.-- « Bearing a branch. » [n: (ALC.)] The dove bears a branch of olive to Noah after the flood, the most foul bird having been driven out, because the dove of the Holy Spirit, coming to Christ after his baptism, announces peace with the branch of refreshment and of light.

VERS. 12.-- « And he waited nevertheless other seven days, » etc. [n: (ISID. in Gen.)] The dove let go after another seven days, and not returning, signifies the end of the world, when the rest of the saints shall be, not in the sacrament of hope, in which the Church is now joined together, so long as that is drunk which flowed from the side of Christ, but in the perfection of salvation, when the kingdom shall be delivered up to God and the Father, so that in that clear contemplation of truth we may have need of no mysteries.

VERS. 13.-- « In the six hundred and first year, » etc. [0110B] [n: (Ibid.)] Of Noah's life, that is, six hundred years being completed, the roof of the ark is opened, etc., *as far as* one being added, on account of the bond of unity.

VERS. 14.-- « In the second month. » *[n: (STRAB.)] Historically.* That is, in May, Noah completed a year in the ark, and on the very day on which he had entered, he went out. For he entered on the twenty-seventh day of the second month; does it say that he went out on the seventh and twentieth day of the same month. But it must be known that, if eleven be added to the present day, such as the moon is today, such will it be on that same day a year hence. When therefore Noah entered, it was the seventeenth moon, or the seventeenth day of the second month. Therefore after a year, eleven being added, it was the twenty-seventh day and the seventeenth moon.

« In the second month, » etc. *Mystically.* As was said above, [0110C] on the seventeenth day of the six hundredth year the earth was soaked with the waters of the flood, but on the twenty-seventh day of the second month it was dried, when it was commanded that they should go out of the ark and receive a compact, that they should no more perish by the waters of a flood. In which it can be understood that by ten and seven (as above) the state of those beginning can be signified, when vices are renounced; by twenty-seven, the advancement of the perfect in the consummation of the virtues. For if thou add ten to ten and seven, they become twenty-seven. For if to the ten precepts by which sins are renounced thou add the evangelical perfection, by which it is commanded not only not to be angry, but even to love one's enemies, and the like, thou hast come to that age of perfection in which thou needest not even the aforesaid sacraments; and going out [0110D] of the ark, that is, passing over from hope to sight, thou shalt find the rest promised in the sacraments.

VERS. 15-19.-- « Go out of the ark, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] They go out joined who entered separated, because « now the flesh lusteth against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh. »[var: Vulg. Gal 5:17 reads *Caro enim concupiscit adversus spiritum, spiritus autem adversus carnem*, with *enim* and *autem* for Migne's *nunc* and *et*.] But in the resurrection of the just the body will cleave to the spirit in perfect peace, with no want of mutability or concupiscence resisting.

VERS. 20.-- « And taking, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] Clean and unclean animals entered into the ark, but after the going out none are offered to God but the clean. But in that he says: « I will not strike every living soul as I have done, »[var: Vulg. Gn 8:21 reads *non igitur ultra percutiam*, "I will therefore no more strike"; Migne's lemma lacks *ultra*.] and added those things which his goodness grants of its bounty to the unworthy, perhaps the indulgence of the New Testament [0111A] is figured, so that the vengeance that is past belongs to the severity of the law, and this to the goodness of grace.

VERS. 21, 22.-- « For the sense, » etc. [n: (GREG., Moral. libr. XXXVIII.)] Let no one ascribe it to himself if he has overcome his thoughts: because the evil of corruption, which each one has taken up from the rising of carnal desires, he plies as his age advances; and unless the hand of the divine strength repress it the sooner, guilt devours every good of nature into the depth: « Neither he that planteth is anything, nor he that watereth, but God that giveth the increase [n: (I Cor. III)]. »
