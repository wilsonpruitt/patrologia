## CHAPTER IX.

VERS. 1.-- « And God blessed Noah and his sons; » etc. « Increase and multiply. » Man is set over [0111B] the rest of the animals for their terror, as a solace for the vengeance that is past, and lest a few men be overwhelmed by the more numerous beasts, and that they may know that they have dominion over the irrational, but not over the rational.

VERS. 2, 3.-- « And your terror, » etc. [n: (GREG. libr. II Moral. c. 11.)] In powerful men the virtue of humility is great, etc., *as far as* by which they seek not their own glory, but the profit of their subjects.

VERS. 4-8.-- « Flesh with blood. » [n: (ISID.)] All animals are given them for food, because in the calling of the Gentiles to the faith there is no difference, which is figured in the dish of Peter. They are forbidden to eat blood, lest the former life be held as it were stifled within the conscience, but let it be poured out through confession. The eating of flesh seems to have been granted after the flood, on account of [0111C] the barrenness of the earth and the frailty of man.

VERS. 9-12.-- « Behold, I will establish my covenant, » etc. God set a testament between himself and men and every living soul, that he might not destroy it by a flood: the bow, namely, which appears in the clouds, which never shines save from the sun. For those perish not in the flood, separated from the Church, who in the prophets and in all the holy Scriptures, as in the clouds of God, acknowledge the glory of Christ and seek not their own. But lest the worshippers of this sun swell up, let them know that Christ is signified by the sun just as by the lion, by the lamb, by the stone: for the sake of likeness, not by any substance of property.

VERS. 13-17.-- « I will set my bow, » etc. [n: (ALC. in Gen.)] God gave the bow for a sign of security, [0111D] lest fearful men should dread to be destroyed by another flood, often beholding the overflowing of the rains. This is set in the heaven, that it may be seen by all, and that in whatever tribulation we may lift up to him the eyes of our heart who dwells in the heavens. But because the bow is not save from the rays of the sun and a moist cloud, it seems that before the flood there had been no rains. The earth, however, could be made fruitful from dew and from the overflowing of the springs.

[n: (STRAB.)] The bow has two colours, blue and fiery, which express two judgments: one of water, which is past; the other of fire, which is believed to be to come at the end of the world: whence the blue colour is on the outside, but the fiery within.

[0112A]VERS. 18-20.-- « These therefore were the sons of Noah, » etc. [n: (HIER. in Q. Hebr.)] Often the Seventy, not being able to turn the letter *heth* into Greek, because it sounds a double aspiration, added the Greek χ *chi*, teaching that in words of this kind an aspiration is to be made. Whence they rendered *Cham* for that which is *Ham,* from whom Egypt is to this day called Ham in the language of the Egyptians.

VERS. 21.-- « And drinking wine, » etc. Christ also was made drunk, when he suffered; laid bare, when he was crucified. « In his own tabernacle, » that is, among those of his own household by blood, then was the mortality of his flesh laid bare. « To the Jews a scandal, to the Gentiles foolishness, but to them that are called, both Jews and Gentiles, » as it were Sem and Japheth, « the power of God and the wisdom of God: because that which is foolish of God is wiser than men. »

[0112B] [n: (ALCUIN.)] After the flood Noah was made drunk, because perhaps he did not know that one could be made drunk with wine. For neither are men read to have used wine before the flood. Therefore it is said of Noah especially that he planted a vineyard. The uncovering of the thigh follows drunkenness, as lust follows satiety.

VERS. 22.-- « And when he had seen, » etc. [n: (PROCOP. in Gen.)] From these circumstances the sin of Cham is enlarged. He holds his parent in contempt; he blabs out how basely his father had lain; nor does he tell it to one of his brothers, but to both: if more had been at hand, he would have uncovered the deed to all. The deed seemed a thing of laughter to him to whom it least ought; that deed was worthy of shame and of caution, lest the infamy of it should spread abroad to the rest: nor does he announce it to those who were of the household, but to outsiders.

[0112C] [n: (ISID.)] The Jews, seeing the death of Christ, mocked. But Sem and Japheth, two peoples believing out of the uncircumcision and out of the circumcision, turned away and covered him, etc., *as far as* « as it were running together into the peace of the corner. »

VERS. 23.-- « But Sem indeed, » etc. [n: (GREG. in Moral.)] We are said to turn away from that which we reprove. What then is it that the sons, with cloaks thrown over their backs, covered their father's shameful parts, coming turned away, unless that to good subjects the evils of their superiors so displease that they hide them, once known, from others: they bear the covering turned away, because, judging the deed and revering their master, they will not see what they cover.

VERS. 24, 25.-- « Cursed be Chanaan the boy, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] God does not pass over sins unavenged, but so [0112D] begins judgment in our correction that it rests in the damnation of the reprobate. For judgment begins at the house of God. What then is the end of those who have not believed, who feel no temporal scourges? Because they shall perish for ever; whose wickedness was figured in the sin of Cham. To whom it is said: « Cursed be Chanaan the boy, » etc. The father sinning, the son received the sentence, because the wickedness of the reprobate here prospers long, but perishes hereafter.

VERS. 26.-- « Blessed be the Lord God of Sem. » Though he be the God of all nations, yet even among those nations he is called, as by a proper name, the God of Israel — which is from the blessing of Japheth, because in the people of the Gentiles the Church has taken possession of the world, which was foretold [0113A] when it was said: « God enlarge Japheth. »

« Chanaan. » [n: (STRAB.)] From whom the Chananites, who by this curse are prophesied to be driven out of the place of their habitation.

VERS. 27-29.-- « God enlarge Japheth, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] From Sem the Hebrews, from Japheth the Gentiles. Because therefore the multitude of believers is broad, from breadth, which is called Japheth, breadth is named. But that which he says: « And let him dwell in the tabernacles of Sem, » is prophesied of us, who are occupied in the learning and knowledge of the Scriptures, Israel being cast out.

« God enlarge Japheth, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] Sem, the elder by birth, is himself the Jewish people, from whom the patriarchs and the prophets and the apostles, etc., *as far as* that is, their work.
