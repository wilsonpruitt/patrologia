## CHAPTER XI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0114C]VERS. 1.-- « And the earth was of one lip, » etc. [n: (AUG., in Gen.)] It was said above that the sons of Noah, or of his sons, were distributed through the lands according to their tongues. ⟦s2⟧ But this, which was earlier, he afterwards records by recapitulation. ⟦s3⟧ Yet he makes obscurity in this, that he so weaves it together by that manner of speech as though the narration of these things followed upon the things which were done afterwards.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- « And when they set out. » [n: (CHRYSOST., in Gen.)] See how the human race cannot subsist within its own bounds, but, coveting more, always seeks greater things. ⟦s2⟧ And this is what destroys the human race, that it will not acknowledge the measure of its own nature, but always desires greater things. ⟦s3⟧ Since, « they saw, he says, a plain in the land of [0114D] Sennaar, »[var: Vulg. Gn 11:2 reads *invenerunt campum in terra Senaar*, "they found a plain in the land of Senaar."] migrating, and, the former place being left, they dwelt there.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- « Come. » They feared a flood again; therefore, Nemrod being the author of it, they wished to set up for themselves a tower in which they should not fear a flood. ⟦s2⟧ A foolish and impious boldness, whence followed vengeance and the division of tongues.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID. in Gen.)] The tower, the pride of the world, or the dogmas of the heretics, etc., *as far as* the unity of confession and of faith.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- « Come therefore, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ He is understood to have said this to the angels; or according to that: « Let us make man to our image and likeness. » For it is subjoined in the singular number: « and he divided [0115A] them, » etc., as there: « God made man to his own image and likeness. »[var: Vulg. Gn 1:27 reads *Et creavit Deus hominem ad imaginem suam*, "And God created man to his own image," without *et similitudinem*.]

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (GREG. Moral. lib. II, c. 5.)] « Come, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is said to those who cleave to God, « Come, » because never to decrease from the divine contemplation but always to increase, and never to withdraw in heart, is, by a steadfast motion, always to be coming. ⟦s3⟧ The angels ascend by beholding the Creator, they descend by pressing down, with the sifting of strictness, the creature that unlawfully lifts itself up. ⟦s4⟧ To say therefore, « Let us go down and confound their tongue, » is to show within himself what is rightly done, and, by the force of the inward vision, to breathe with hidden motions into their minds the judgments that are to be displayed.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8, 9.-- « He divided them. » [n: (ALC.)] In this division of tongues God made nothing new, but divided the [0115B] modes of speaking and the forms of speech among the various nations. ⟦s2⟧ Whence we find the same syllables and letters of the same force joined otherwise in different tongues, and often the same nouns or verbs signifying something else; whence, where it is said in Psalm II: « With a rod of iron, » in the Greek there stands ἐν ραβδῳ σιδηρᾶ, *in raudo sidera.* ⟦s3⟧ Therefore in Latin *sidera* does not signify *ferrea*, as it does in the Greek, but *astra.*

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG. lib. XVI de Civit. c. 4.)] Rightly is the evil affection punished, even that upon which no effect follows. ⟦s2⟧ But of what kind was the manner of the punishment? ⟦s3⟧ For since the dominion of him who commands lies in the tongue, pride was condemned there, so that he who commanded should not be understood by man, the man who would not understand so as to obey God who commanded.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10-25.-- « Sem was a hundred years old. » [0115C] [n: (BEDA, Hexaem., lib. III, t. II, col. 131.)] A great question arises for us according to the letter, how Sem is said to be a hundred years old two years after the flood, when above he is asserted to have been born in the five hundredth year of Noah, and the flood is read to have come in the six hundredth year of the same Noah, etc., *as far as* « because God took him. »

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26-30.-- « Thare begot Abram and Nachor. » [n: (AUG. quaest. 25 in Gen.)] Not at the same time, but in their own times, from the point at which he began to beget. ⟦s2⟧ Nor is the order of birth to be regarded in these, but the signification of the dignity to come, in which Abram excels.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31-32.-- « In Ur of the Chaldees. » [n: (HIERON.)] In the Hebrew *in Ur Chesdim* ( )[ed: Migne prints the Hebrew word inside these parentheses; our digitized source has lost it and leaves them empty.], that is, *in the fire of the Chaldees.* ⟦s2⟧ Here the Hebrews tell the tale that Abram was cast into [0115D] the fire because he would not adore the fire which the Chaldees worship, and, delivered by God's help, escaped out of the fire of idolatry. ⟦s3⟧ Whence it is said to him: « I am he that brought thee out of Ur of the Chaldees. »[var: Vulg. Gn 15:7 reads *Ego Dominus qui eduxi te de Ur Chaldaeorum*, "I am the Lord who brought thee out of Ur of the Chaldees."]

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Aram the father of Melcha. »[var: Vulg. Gn 11:29 reads *Aran patris Melchae*; Migne prints the name *Aram* here and again in the gloss below.] [n: (Ibid.)] The same is the father of both. ⟦s2⟧ Aram, son of Thare, brother of Abraham and of Nachor, begot two daughters, Melcha and Sarai, surnamed Jescha. ⟦s3⟧ Nachor married Melcha, and Abram Sarai. ⟦s4⟧ For marriages between uncles and nieces had not yet been forbidden, which in the beginning were celebrated even between brothers and sisters.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Thare therefore took Abram, » etc., *as far as,* the days of Thare were two hundred and five years, and he died in Haran. » [n: (AUG.)] It is said first, « that [0116A] when Thare was seventy years old he begot Abram, and afterwards remained with all his own in Charan, and died. » And it was said to Abraham that he should go out of Charan, and he went out when he was seventy-five years old. ⟦s2⟧ But by recapitulation it is shown that the Lord had given the command while his father was living, etc., *as far as* from whom the Jews were named, on account of the royal tribe.

## CHAPTER XII.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- « Go out of the land, » etc. [n: (AUG. ubi supra.)] The narrative of Stephen on this matter is to be considered, etc., *as far as* thus therefore Abram was settled in that land, when he lived there until the birth of Jacob.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (STRAB.)] It is to be noted that he had already gone out of his own land, but had then gone out in body, not [0116B] in mind and will; for he had perhaps a mind of returning, when it was said to him: « Go out, » etc.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID.)] Christ left the land and the kindred of the Jews, and was magnified among the peoples of the Gentiles. ⟦s2⟧ We too must go out of our land, that is, out of earthly means and riches, and out of our kindred, that is, the converse of the vices, which, cleaving to us from our birth, are joined to us as it were by the affinities of blood; and « out of the house of our father, » that is, out of the memory of the world, that renouncing it we may be enlarged with the people of God, and, when the time shall come, be brought into the land of the heavenly promise.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2-3.--And I will make thee into a great nation, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] Two things are promised to Abraham, namely, that his seed shall possess the land of Chanaan, when it is said: [0116C] « And come into the land which I shall show thee. » But the other far more excellent, not of carnal but of spiritual seed, whereof he is to be the father, not only of the Jewish nation, but of all the Gentiles that follow his faith; which is thus promised: « And in thee shall all the nations of the earth be blessed. »[var: Vulg. Gn 12:3 reads *atque in te benedicentur universae cognationes terrae*, "and in thee shall all the kindreds of the earth be blessed."]

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- « Seventy-five years old. » [n: (HIERON. in Q. Hebr.)] If Thare, the father of Abraham, in the Chaldean region, etc., *as far as* and tarried there a longer while.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8-12.-- « Bethel. » [n: (RAB.)] It is interpreted *the house of God,* which afterwards was called Bethaven, that is, *the house of an idol.*

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Hai. » *Mystically.* ⟦s2⟧ Hai is interpreted *eye* or *fountain*. ⟦s3⟧ The patriarch therefore, making for the land of promise, pitched his tent between Bethel [0116D] and Hai, because the saints, regenerated by the font of baptism, circumspect in the course of this life, hasten to the tabernacle of God, that is, to the heavenly city.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13-14.-- « Say therefore, I beseech thee, » etc. [n: (AUG. lib. XVI de Civ., c. 19.)] It is asked why the patriarch was willing to lie, etc., *as far as* both his own life and his wife's chastity.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15-20.-- « The woman was taken up. » [n: (RAB.)] Sarai is excused, who is carried into the house of Pharao unwilling, not only in this, that neither body nor soul is defiled save by the consent of the mind, but also in this, that it was done with her husband agreeing and permitting.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG. Quaest. in Gen.)] With Pharao she could [0117A] be so long untouched, because in Esther it is read that the maidens were not brought in to the kings at once, but were kept for one year, and for six months were anointed with oil of myrrh, and for another six months were among divers perfumes. ⟦s2⟧ She could therefore through these delays of time remain untouched. ⟦s3⟧ And meanwhile the Egyptians used Abram well, until Pharao, scourged by God and knowing that she was his wife, gave her back untouched.
