## CHAPTER XIII

VERS. 1, 2.-- « Abram therefore went up, » etc. [n: (HIERON. in Q. Hebr.)] Delivered out of Egypt, he is rightly read to have gone up, but how could he be exceeding rich? But in the Hebrew verity there stands *exceeding heavy:* for he was weighed down by the burden of Egypt. And [0117B] although riches of gold and of silver may seem to be riches, yet if they are Egyptian they are heavy to a holy man; nor, as it is read in the Seventy, « He went away whence he had come into the desert as far as Bethel, » but, according to the Hebrew, « He went away on his journey by the south as far as Bethel. » For he set out from Egypt, not that he might seek the desert, which he had left behind in Egypt, but that by the south, which is contrary to the north, he might come to the house of God, where his tabernacle was between Bethel and Hai.

VERS. 3-5.-- « In Bethel, » etc. Now Bethel is a little city at the twelfth milestone from Helia, on the right hand as men go to Neapolis: which formerly was called Luza, that is, Almond, in the tribe of Benjamin: but Hai is in the tribe of Juda, set apart for the priests; Bethel, *the house of God;* Hai, *eye* or *fountain:* rightly therefore, pitching his tabernacle [0117C] between Bethel and Hai, he built an altar to the Lord, because the saints, withdrawing from the prince of the world and from the pleasures of the age, washed in the holy font of baptism, leading the course of the present life considerately, and offering to God upon the altar of faith the sacrifice of praise and of good work, tend happily to the house of God, that is, to the heavenly Jerusalem.

VERS. 6-9.-- « For their substance was much, » etc. « Let there not be, I pray thee, » etc. Let us also set nothing before concord and charity, but through the right hand of prosperity, or the left hand of adversity, let us persevere in peace and in love. The quarrel between the shepherds of Lot and the shepherds of Abraham signifies the controversy between catholic pastors and heretical subverters. Whence Lot also signifies the heresiarchs, who is interpreted *declining;* [0117D] and about the Jordan, that is, *a going down,* he chose a region for himself. His shepherds, the heretics and schismatics, who seek out fair and sloping places, and go not up into the mountain with Moses to the Lord, holding peace and unity; and they receive the choice of going whither they will; and fleeing grace, they commit themselves to free will. But the shepherds of Abraham signify catholic teachers, who flee from heretics even in place, according to that to Titus III: « A man that is a heretic, after the first and the second correction, avoid: »[var: Vulg. Tit 3:10 reads *post unam et secundam correptionem*, "after one and a second rebuke."] and this among the nations of the Chananite and the Pherezite, that is, in the land of promise, because between catholics and heretics there is wont to be contention whose is the kingdom of heaven.

[0118A] « For we are brethren. » In three ways are men called brethren in holy Scripture: by nature, as Jacob and Esau; by kindred, as Abram and Lot; by nation, as all the Jews. Whence, if thy brother be impoverished and sell himself to thee, etc.

VERS. 10-12.-- « As a paradise, » etc. Hence we learn that there is an earthly paradise, to which the earthly watering is compared. For this region was flat and of open country, and watered by the flowing Jordan, as Egypt by the Nile.

VERS. 13.-- « Before the Lord. » To whom the punishing of the crime is committed: for the crime was manifest to men also, but not to be punished.

VERS. 14.-- « Lift up thine eyes, » etc. As if to say, look through the four regions of the world; the possession of Palestine [0118B] is promised to him and to his seed. That in certain codices *mare* is put for *occidente*, it comes about because Palestine has the sea on the west.

VERS. 15-17.-- « All the land, » etc. Not this only was promised. For it was not said, I will give thee as much of the land as thou seest; but, I will give thee the land which thou seest. For since land further off was being given on every side, this especially was given which was seen. Whence he added also: « Arise and walk through the land, » etc., namely, that by walking through it he might come to that which, standing in one place, he could not see. But that land is signified which Israel, the seed of Abraham according to the flesh, not according to faith, had received: which was to be as the sand of the sea, because it cannot be numbered.

[0118C] « I will give to thee. » To Christ is promised the inheritance of all nations, who spread his Church through the whole world, to whom the Father says: « Ask of me, and I will give thee the nations for thine inheritance [n: (Psal. II)]. » And in Psalm LXXI: « And he shall rule from sea to sea. »

VERS. 18.-- « In Hebron. » It is a city in the tribe of Juda, which is also called Cariatharbe, that is, *the city of four;* for CHARIAT in Hebrew is [ed: two Hebrew words stand here on Migne's plate, one before CHARIAT and one before *arbe*; our digitized source has lost both, which has run his two italic phrases together into one.] *a city, arbe four.* For there is laid Adam the greatest, and Abram, Isaac and Jacob.
