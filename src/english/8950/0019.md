## CHAPTER XV.

VERS. 1.-- « These things therefore being done, » etc., « Fear not. » When the multitude of vices has been triumphed over, the hope [0121A] of every faithful man is strengthened by the Lord, that it may not waver, but may look for its reward.

VERS. 2.-- « And Abram said. [n: (HIERON. in Q. Hebr.)] « I go without children, » [var: Vulg. Gn 15:2 *ego vadam absque liberis*, "I shall go without children"] etc. *as far as* from him, they say, Damascus was both named and founded.

VERS. 5-8.-- « Look up to heaven, » etc. Above he said: « Thy seed shall be as the sand of the sea, » because in that people some were carnal and earthly, who are compared to sand, since they are dry and unfruitful of good works; others were as shining stars.

[n: (RAB.)] A twofold signification of the promised seed is given. When the stars are shown, it is said to him: « So will I make thy seed, » that is, the Christian nation, whose father thou art to be in faith, namely to glitter [0121B] with the light of the resurrection. But when the sand is shown, it is said: « So shall thy seed be, » etc., that is, the nation of the Jews shall be numerous, but dry and unfruitful.

VERS. 9-10.-- « Take me, said he, » etc. [n: (ISID. in Gen., tom. V.)] The heifer of three years, the Jewish people set under the yoke of the law, etc., *as far as* when by fire the good shall be separated from the evil.

« A heifer of three years, and a she-goat of three years, » etc. Because from Adam to Noah, and thence to Abram, and then to David, the people grew up.

VERS. 11.-- « And the birds came down, » etc. *[n: (GREG., lib. XVI Moral., c. 20.)] Tropologically.* In the very sacrifice of prayer importunate thoughts thrust themselves in, desiring to snatch it away or to violate it: but with the hand of holy discretion we ought to guard what we offer without, [0121C] lest malignant spirits and perverse thoughts snatch it away.

VERS. 12.-- « Dread rushed in, » [var: Vulg. Gn 15:12 *sopor*, "a deep sleep"] etc. [n: (AUG. in Quaest. Hebr.)] This question is to be handled on account of those who contend that these disturbances do not fall upon the mind of the wise man, etc., *as far as* a disturbance not to be named.

VERS. 13-15.-- « Know thou beforehand, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] The people of Israel is prophesied as about to serve in Egypt: not that it served the Egyptians four hundred years, but because this number was completed in that affliction, since it is reckoned from the time at which these things are promised to Abraham.

« Know thou beforehand, » etc. [n: (AUG. de Civ. lib. XVI, c. 24.)] It is not to be so understood, as though the people of God [0121D] had been four hundred years in that most harsh servitude, etc., *as far as* the people of God was afflicted in Egypt with hard servitude.

VERS. 16-21.-- « But in the generation, » etc. [n: (HIERON. epist. ad Damas.)] But Moses says elsewhere, etc., *as far as* and they arrive at Solomon.

## CHAPTER XVI.

VERS. 1.-- « Now Sarai the wife of Abram, » etc. [n: (RAB.)] Abram is no adulterer if, while his wife lives, he is joined to the handmaid, because the Gospel's law of one wife had not yet been promulgated: and he had heard that his stock was to be multiplied, but by what woman he knew not, since he knew Sara to be barren. There was in the intercourse no lustful pleasure, but a devout love of having offspring: [0122A] his wife too willed this and compelled it, she who could not have sons; and therefore she desired to receive from another one whom she might adopt in the place of her own.

VERS. 2-11.-- « Go in to the handmaid, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Note diligently that the procreation of sons is in the Hebrew called *a building up*, etc., *as far as* that, brought back as it were by an angel, she may return into favour and subject herself.

[n: (RAB.)] These two women are the two Testaments: Agar, the Old, which in the Synagogue nourished the Jewish people, liable to servitude; Sara, the New, which begot the Christian people into the liberty of faith. The sons of the handmaid were multiplied, so that they could not be numbered for their multitude, who above are compared to sand. That people therefore, a rival by its own fierceness, and contrary to all good things, and most of all when, with envy goading it, it persecutes the Christian people [0122B] throughout the whole world, and scattered far and wide, is burdensome to all by wandering and by trafficking.

VERS. 12.-- « He shall be a wild man. » Or *a rustic,* according to others. [n: (HIERON.)] In the Hebrew there stands *phara* which is interpreted *a wild ass.* It signifies that his seed shall dwell in the desert, that is, the Saracens, wandering and of uncertain abodes, who, raiding all the nations to whom the desert is joined on the flank, are assailed by all.

VERS. 13.-- « And Agar called. » Rightly did she so call his name, because he freely takes counsel for the wretched and comforts the lowly. Whence: « Thou considerest labour and sorrow, and to thee is the poor man left, » etc. « Verily here have I seen the hinder parts of him that seeth me. » Of like sort is [0122C] that saying: « Thou shalt see my hinder parts, for my face shall not appear to thee. » [var: Vulg. Ex 33:23 *faciem autem meam videre non poteris*, "but my face thou canst not see"] « For man shall not see my face and live [n: (Exod. XXXIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Ex 33:20 *non enim videbit me homo et vivet*, "for man shall not see me and live"] For that manifestation of the wisdom of God no man can see and live. It is that very form for which he sighs who loves God with his whole heart, his whole soul, his whole mind: for the contemplating of which, by loving his neighbour also as himself, so far as he can, he builds up. But his hinder parts, that is, his incarnation, which he took upon him in the last times, we now contemplate by longing, through faith and love, and in the time to come we shall enjoy them in eternal contemplation.

VERS. 14-16.-- « Therefore she called that well, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] To this day the well of Agar is shown in the desert of Arabia, between Cades and Barad, [0122D] which signifies the Scripture of the old law and of the new, which our Creator, the life of the living, the hope of the dying, gave to mortals out of regard of his loving compassion, that being taught and corrected from their vices they may deserve to be for ever worthy of the vision of God.
