## CHAPTER XVIII.

VERS. 1.-- « And the Lord appeared to him in the vale of Mambre. » [n: (ISID. in Gen. )] Abraham bears a threefold figure: the first, of the Saviour, who leaving his kindred came into the world; the second, of the Father, because he offered up his only Son; the third (which appears in this place), of the saints who received the coming of Christ with joy [0125D]. The tabernacle of Abraham signifies Jerusalem, where in their season the prophets and the apostles dwelt, and Christ at his coming was received by them that believed, and by them that believed not was hanged upon the tree.

VERS. 2, 3.-- « Three men. » Three men announce the coming of Christ, whom two angels accompany, Moses according to some, and Elias; the one a lawgiver, who in the law showed the coming of the Lord: the other, who is to come at the end, to announce the second coming of Christ and to preach his Gospel. Whence, when the Lord was transfigured on the mountain, they are seen with him. « He saw three, and adored one, » showing namely the Saviour, whose coming he was awaiting. Whence: « Abraham saw my day and was [0126A] glad [n: (Joan. VIII)]. » [var: Vulg. Jo 8:56 *Abraham pater vester exsultavit ut videret diem meum : vidit, et gavisus est*, "Abraham your father rejoiced that he might see my day: he saw it, and was glad"] For he foresaw the mystery of the sacrament to come. Whence he washed their feet, that he might show the purification of the laver that was to be at the end of the world; for the feet signify the last things.

« Whom when he had seen. » *[n: (GREG., lib. IX Moral., c. 51.)] Morally.* Abraham ran to meet three angels, because the man and master of the house, that is, the spiritual understanding, ought in the knowledge of the Trinity to pass beyond the barriers of the flesh, and as it were to go out at the door of the lowest dwelling. Let the care of the flesh, which Sara signifies, not appear abroad for ostentation, but behind the man's back, that is, under the discretion of the spirit, intent upon necessary things alone, let it not know how to uncover itself shamelessly, but to govern itself modestly.

[n: (Ibid., lib. XXVIII, c. 4)]. Abraham could not have seen the angels, received them into his lodging and set food before them, [0126B] unless they had taken a body out of air: for they would not appear, they would not take food, unless they had a solid body of a heavenly element. Nor is it strange that now they are called angels, now the Lord, because they are called angels who minister without, and the Lord is shown, who commands them within.

VERS. 4, 5.-- « But I will fetch. » He returns to the offices of hospitality, which can be shown even to angels, when they take a bodily form out of the elements: which Abraham understanding, adored the Godhead in unity, and ministered to the angelic dispensation in visible form.

VERS. 6.-- « Three sata. » These things are said in the type of the Church, which out of the three parts of the world is gathered into the unity of faith, and as bread without leaven [0126C] is baked by the fire of the Holy Ghost.

[n: (STRAB.)] A satum is a kind of measure. Three sata signify the three sons of Noah, out of whom the whole race of men was sown abroad. Therefore the Church is signified, or the evangelical doctrine that is to be sown throughout the whole world, and to be multiplied in the three parts of the world, Asia, Africa and Europe.

« Three sata, » of which Sara makes the loaves baked under the ashes, signify the three sons of Noah, out of whom the whole race of men was born: who, believing in the divine Trinity, were sprinkled with the water of baptism through Sara, that is, the Church, and were brought together into the one bread of the body of Christ. These are the three sata of meal which the woman in the Gospel leavened: they are unleavened loaves, because without the leaven of malice, and the anguish [ *Al.,* rancore] [0126D] of wickedness, and the fervour of perverse doctrine, the unity of believers ought to be.

« Baked under the ashes. » They are baked under the ashes, that being baked by the vapour of the Holy Ghost through repentance for their sins, they may be made food acceptable to God.

VERS. 7.-- « A calf most tender. » The tender and fatted calf is the body of Christ, which for the salvation of the world was offered up upon the tree of the cross. This is the calf that is killed for the prodigal son.

VERS. 8.-- « Butter and milk » he set before them with the flesh of the calf, because milk signifies the old law; whence I Cor. III: « Milk I gave you to drink, not meat, » etc. For he had delivered to them the commandment of the law, as milk pressed out of the breasts of the two tables, that is [0127A] the testament of faith, because they could not yet take the solid meat of the evangelical doctrine. The most rich and fat butter is the evangelical testimony, which like oil is given to the faithful for a sign. He set the calf before them with milk and butter, because neither can the body of the Lord be without the milk of the law, nor this without the Gospel. And they sat under the tree in sign of the Lord's passion, whereof they are the preachers.

VERS. 9.-- « Behold she is in the tabernacle, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Abraham is without with the angels, Sara is in the tabernacle: because it belongs to the strong and the learned to preach openly, to instruct by examples: to the weak and the unwise, to learn and to be subject.

« VERS. 10.--And she shall have a son, » etc. God promises a son to the barren Sara, saying: About this time [0127B] I will come; he signifies not the time, but the quality of his coming, since through the son promised again the faithful people was to be born. Whence Isa. LIV and Gal. IV: « Rejoice, thou barren, that travailest not, » etc.

« Which when she had heard, etc. [n: (AUG. Quaest in Gen. )] Sara is reproved because she laughed, and not Abraham, although he had laughed in like manner; because the laughter of the one was of wonder, that of the other of doubt, which he could discern who knows the heart. Sara denies that she laughed, whereas she cannot be hidden from God, but perhaps she thought that they were men. But Abraham understood God, yet in rendering the offices of humanity, which are necessary only to weak flesh; it is strange: unless he first judged them men, but men in whom he understood God to speak, certain signs of the divine majesty appearing: as Scripture often testifies that God [0127C] appeared in men, but afterward they knew that they had been angels.

VERS. 11.-- « And they were, » etc. The age of the elderly is less than that of the old, even though the old be called elderly. Whence, according to the physicians, an elderly man cannot get children of an elderly woman, even though the courses of women still flow. According to this Abraham wonders at the power of God concerning the promise of a son, and we can take it that the Apostle set down this miracle. For the body was not altogether dead for generation, if the woman had been of youthful age: but of one of advanced age he could not beget: for of Cethura he afterward begot, because she was young. For the physicians hand down that an old man cannot beget of a woman of more advanced age (even though the courses of women befall her), [0127D] but of a young one he can. Again a woman of advanced age, even though the courses of women still befall her, cannot bear by an elderly man, but by a young one she can. It was therefore a miracle, because both were old and of advanced age, and the courses of women had ceased for Sara.

VERS. 12-14.-- « Shall I give myself to pleasure? » etc. [n: (GREG. lib. IX Moral., c. 51.)] The care of the flesh, etc., *as far as* what else does our mind bring forth than joy?

VERS. 15.-- « I did not laugh, etc. [n: (AMBR.)] Or she therefore denied that she had laughed, because she was ignorant; she therefore laughed, because she prophesied.

VERS. 27-19.-- « Can I hide from Abraham what « I am about to do. » Well does the Lord commit his secrets to Abraham, [0128A] whom he knew to be a faithful executor of his will and of his secrets; of whom he himself was to be born in the flesh, « in whom are all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge hidden [n: (II Cor. II)]. » He promises also to Abraham not only rewards, but the justice of the obedience of his sons, and that the promises should be fulfilled concerning them. And again Isaac is promised, « about to be a great nation, » and that « in him shall all the nations of the earth be blessed. » [var: Vulg. Gn 18:18 *benedicendae sint in illo omnes nationes terrae*, "all the nations of the earth shall be blessed in him"] By which words two things are promised him: the Jewish nation according to the flesh, and all the Gentiles through faith.

VERS. 20.-- « The cry of Sodom and Gomorrha, » etc. [n: (AUG. Q. in Gen. )] If we take these words as those not of one doubting, but of one angry and threatening, there is no question. For after the manner of men God speaks [0128B] to men, and his anger is without disturbance, because he is unchangeable. But often in threatening we say thus: Let us see whether I do not do it to thee, and whether I shall not be able to do it to thee; or, let me know, that is, let me try whether I cannot. But disturbance does not fall upon God. It is the custom of human speech, to which God fits himself, that he may suit himself to human weakness.

[n: (GREG. lib. V Moral., cap. 7.)] But sin with a voice is a fault in action. But sin with a cry is a fault with liberty.

VERS. 21-23.-- « I will go down and see, » etc. [n: (GREG., Moral., c. 33.)] Let us not believe men's evil deeds before we prove them. And behold, through angels he came down to know their evils, and finding them wrapped in so great a crime [0128C], he as it were passed over his patience, and forestalled them with the fire of judgment before the day of judgment: who judges with tranquillity, that the greater crimes may be slowly believed, and, when truly known, more swiftly punished.

[n: (GREG., lib. I Reg., c. 178, tom. I.)] Whither should God go down, that he might know what things had been done? Or what should he not know, who is everywhere? But that he might give to our ignorance an example of discretion, that we ought not to believe grievous evils on hearing them, he says of himself that he goes down to know, of whom it is plain to all that even without going down he would know all things.

[n: (HILAR., lib. IX, de Trin.)] We have God not knowing that which nevertheless he is not ignorant of. For since he knows the sins to be exceeding great, he goes down again that he may see whether they are consummated; and if they are not yet consummated [0128D], that he may know: we understand that he does not then know them because he was ignorant before; but that he then knows, because it is the time for acting. Therefore for God not to know is not a diminution of ignorance, but the fulness of time.

VERS. 24-26.-- « If there be fifty just men, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] The number fifty, from which he descends down to ten, signifies penance. Whence also the fiftieth psalm is penitential. If therefore they had been converted, they would have been saved. But when God sees the offenders unwilling to be converted to penance, straightway he restrains the heat of immoderate luxury with the fire of hell. He said that up to ten just men Sodom should not perish, because if in any man the name of Christ be found through the keeping of the ten commandments, he shall not [0129A] perish. For the figure of the number ten signifies also the cross of Christ.

VERS. 27.-- « I will speak to the Lord, » etc. *[n: (GREG. lib. XVIII Moral., c. 33.)] Mystically.* The more the saints look into the inward things of the Godhead, the more do they see themselves to be nothing. Nowhere is Abraham read to have professed himself ashes and dust, save when he deserved to have speech with God. And he said: « I will speak to my Lord, whereas I am dust, » etc. For perhaps he would have believed himself to be something, if he had not felt the true essence which is above him. Whence David also cries out: « Remember, Lord, that I am dust [n: (Psal. X)], » etc.
