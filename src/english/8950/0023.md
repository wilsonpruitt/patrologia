## CHAPTER XIX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- « And there came two angels, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is to be noted that three angels appear to Abraham in an odd number, because God rejoices in an odd number. ⟦s3⟧ To Sodom they came in an even number, that is, two, which number is the first that departs from unity, and is the chief of otherness, figuring as it were a womanish softness. ⟦s4⟧ They came in the « evening, » because the sun of justice had already set for the Sodomites. ⟦s5⟧ « Who when he had seen, » etc. ⟦s6⟧ While Lot runs to meet them [0130A] and adores, he seems to understand that they are angels; while he invites them to the refreshing of the body, he seems to think that they are men. ⟦s7⟧ So of these and of those who appeared to Abraham the same is to be understood. ⟦s8⟧ And from this place of Scripture and from many others the virtue of hospitality is commended. ⟦s9⟧ Whence Paul, Heb. ⟦s10⟧ XIII: « Forget not hospitality: for by this some have pleased God, having received angels to their lodging. » [var: Vulg. Hbr 13:2 *per hanc enim latuerunt quidam, angelis hospitio receptis*, "for by this some have entertained angels unawares"]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: ( Glossa Graeca. )] It is to be noted that where the benefits of God are set forth, God is present; but where punishments are executed, angels are sent; that we may know that to do good is proper to God, delightful and pleasing to God, but that to take vengeance is unwelcome to God.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- « Wash. » Which is wont to be done for guests, that the affections, soiled with earthly dust, and with sloth, and [0130B] with faint-heartedness, as with dusty filth, may be cleansed. ⟦s2⟧ Whence in monasteries a prayer is celebrated for those who come from the road.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3-7.-- « He pressed them. » [n: (ALC.)] The angels as though compelled enter the house of Lot, that his charity, being tried and approved, might be rewarded, and that it might be shown how great a good hospitality is. ⟦s2⟧ They entered a hospitable house, and set the host free; the fire, entering the houses that were shut to guests, destroyed the sinners.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8, 9.-- « I have two daughters. » [n: (AUG., Q. in Gen.)] It is deservedly asked: is a balancing of outrages, or of any sins whatever, to be admitted, that we may do evils lest worse be done: or is it to be attributed to Lot's disturbance, and not to his counsel? and doubtless [0130C] this balancing is admitted most perilously. ⟦s2⟧ But if it was a disturbance of mind, it is in no wise to be imitated.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- « And they brought in Lot to them. » [n: (GREG., l. VI Moral., cap. 16.)] While the wicked oppose him, Lot is brought back within the house and fortified: because every just man, bearing the snares of the depraved, returns to his mind, and remains undismayed. ⟦s2⟧ The Sodomites cannot find the door in the house of Lot: because the corrupters of minds find no entrance of reproof against the life of the just man. ⟦s3⟧ Struck with blindness they go round the house as it were, because in their envy they scrutinize his deeds and words. ⟦s4⟧ But because on every side, out of the just man's life, a strong and praiseworthy action meets them, they wander and grope at the wall.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11-13.-- « And they struck them with blindness. » *(AUG. [0130D] ubi supra.)* ⟦s2⟧ In Greek ἀορασία, that is, *avidentia,* if it may be so called, which causes that a thing be not seen, not all things, but that which is not needful. ⟦s3⟧ For how would they fail in seeking the door, if they had been struck with blindness so that they saw nothing? for troubled by their own calamity they would have sought nothing further. ⟦s4⟧ With this ἀορασία were they struck who sought Eliseus, and who, walking with Christ after the resurrection, did not know him.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- « He went out, » etc. [n: (HIER., in Q. Hebr.)] In what follows the daughters of Lot are read to have been virgins; and he himself said to the Sodomites: « Behold I have two daughters who have not known man, » [var: Vulg. Gn 19:8 *Habeo duas filias, quae necdum cognoverunt virum*, "I have two daughters who have not yet known man"] etc. ⟦s2⟧ But now he is said to have had sons-in-law. ⟦s3⟧ But some think that those who had husbands perished in Sodom, and that those who were virgins [0131A] went out with their father. ⟦s4⟧ But the Hebrew verity has. ⟦s5⟧ « Lot went out, and spoke to the spouses who were to receive his daughters: » therefore his daughters, being virgins, had not yet been joined in marriage.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15, 16.-- « And when it was morning, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ *[n: (ISID.)] Mystically.* ⟦s3⟧ Lot, the brother of Abraham, just and hospitable in Sodom, signifies the body of Christ, which in all the saints groans among the wicked, to whose deeds it does not consent, and from whose commingling it shall be set free at the end of the world, when they are condemned to the punishment of eternal fire

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17-19.-- « In the mountain. » [n: (STRAB.)] *Morally.* ⟦s2⟧ The mountain signifies the height of the virtues, to which the angel exhorts, according to that of Marci XIX. ⟦s3⟧ « If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all that thou hast, » [var: Vulg. Mt 19:21 *vade, vende quae habes, et da pauperibus*, "go, sell what thou hast, and give to the poor"] etc. ⟦s4⟧ But he who shall see that he cannot climb up, it is better [0131B] that he remain in Segor, that is, in the little one, content with a lay and common life, than that he seize upon the mountain of virtue and return to secular things. ⟦s5⟧ Virginity is a high mountain, to which the angel exhorts: if any man sees that he cannot climb up, let him remain in Segor, that is, in lawful wedlock, because it is better to use a middling good than to be hurled down the precipices of lust. ⟦s6⟧ Whence I Cor. VII: « Let every man have a wife because of fornication. » « Neither can I in the mountain: » that is, being disturbed he did not believe the Lord, whom he recognized in the angels, as being disturbed he prostituted his daughters.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- « There is a city, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] *Allegorically.* ⟦s2⟧ Sodom is interpreted *blindness,* and expresses worldly desires: the height of the mountains, the contemplation of the perfect. ⟦s3⟧ But because many flee the enticements of the world, and yet [0131C] being set in action cannot reach the summit of contemplation, Lot went out from Sodom, but did not come to the mountains. ⟦s4⟧ Whence Lot says: « There is this city near, » etc. ⟦s5⟧ It is called « Near, » and yet it is safe for salvation, because the active life is neither wholly severed from the cares of the world, nor a stranger to the joy of eternal salvation.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « To which I can, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ Those who are not able to ascend to the contemplative life flee to the active life, in which they are saved.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (GREG., Past. III, 28.)] Morally.* ⟦s2⟧ To flee burning Sodom, etc., *as far as* whence it is said to Lot:

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21-23.-- « Behold also in this I have heard thy prayers, » because when prayer is poured out to God, such a life of wedlock is not condemned; whence I Cor. VII: « Do not [0131D] defraud one another, » etc.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- « And the Lord rained upon Sodom, » etc. [n: (GREG.)] Brimstone, the stench of the flesh, fire, the burning of carnal desire; when the Lord had determined to punish the crimes of the flesh, by the quality of the vengeance the stain of the crime was made known. ⟦s2⟧ For brimstone stinks, fire burns; they therefore who had burned unto perverse desires with the stench of the flesh, perished justly by brimstone and fire, that from a just punishment they might learn what they had done by unjust desire.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (STRAB.)] It is to be noted that the water of the flood destroyed the first sinners, who in like manner had offended God by the concupiscence of the flesh. ⟦s2⟧ But the Sodomites perished by a heavier punishment, namely by a flood of fire and brimstone. ⟦s3⟧ For although those exceeded the measure, they sinned as it were naturally [0132A]; but these against nature: therefore they were condemned by the example of the heaviest punishment.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- « All the inhabitants. » [n: (ALC.)] The infants were burned with their parents in Sodom, lest the most impious deed of the Sodomites could be turned aside, if any trace of their stock remained. ⟦s2⟧ And it was provided for them, lest living long they should follow the examples of their fathers and be punished more grievously. ⟦s3⟧ The parents also are guilty as much for themselves as for them. ⟦s4⟧ For the death of the sons is the crime of the parents. ⟦s5⟧ Therefore they shall be their accusers. ⟦s6⟧ It is also some kind of benefit, for him who is not glorious, not to be guilty; for it profits him who cannot be a king not to be poor.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26-28.-- « And his wife looking back. » [n: (ISID.)] *Mystically.* ⟦s2⟧ The wife figures the kind of those who, being called by grace [0132B], look back, and strive to return to the things they had left; of whom it is said, Luc. IX: « No man putting his hand on the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God. » Therefore it is forbidden them to look back. ⟦s3⟧ Wherein it is shown that there is no returning to the old life for those who have been regenerated through grace, if they desire to escape the last burning; the wife looking back was turned into salt, being made namely an example, and a seasoning whereby others may be salted. ⟦s4⟧ Whence Christ says, Luc. XVII: « Remember the wife of Lot, » that he might season us as it were with salt, that we should not be negligent, but should beware prudently.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- « For when he overthrew, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ Scripture intimates that God delivered Lot rather by the merits of Abraham, that we may understand that he was called just in a certain manner, [0132C] chiefly because he worshipped the one and true God, and by comparison with the Sodomites, among whom living he could not be bent to the like crime. ⟦s3⟧ « He remembered Abraham. » It is plain that Lot was delivered by the merits of Abraham, whom Scripture calls just. ⟦s4⟧ And it is to be known that he was just, not as Abraham was, but by comparison with the Sodomites.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- « And he went up, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ So the married sometimes ascend from the lowliness of the active life to the height of the contemplative.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And Lot went up from Segor, » etc. [n: (HIER. in Q. Hebr., tom. III.)] It is asked why, when at first he preferred Segor to flight into the mountain, etc., *as far as* he ought not to have doubted.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- « And the elder said to the younger, » etc. [0132D] Which is said in excuse of the daughters, because they thought that the human race had failed, and therefore lay with their father. ⟦s2⟧ But that which follows does not excuse the father, « and he knew not that they had slept with him; » for the Hebrews mark it above with an obelus, as superfluous, as though it were incredible, because the nature of things does not admit that anyone should have intercourse unknowing.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (STRAB.)] Lot is inexcusable, first because he did not believe the angel; then because he was made drunk, and sin was the cause of sin.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (HIERON.)] So depraved suggestion and delight, like incestuous women, lie in wait for the continent, that, having taken occasion, they may steep with the wine of deceit those who are torpid, and cast them down from the state of uprightness as though unwitting.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0133A]VERS. 32.-- « Come, let us make him drunk, » etc. [n: (ALC.)] The two daughters of Lot had learned that the consummation of the world was to be by fire: but, as girls not perfect, neither did they know that, when the Sodomites had been laid waste by fire, much space remained entire; and they supposed that some such thing had been done as they had heard of in the times of Noah, and that they were left surviving with their father for the repairing of the human race. ⟦s2⟧ And although the lying with their father was a great crime, a heavier impiety seemed if, keeping their chastity, they should destroy the hope of posterity. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore they take counsel, and soften their father's sadness or sternness with wine, and each on her several night receives conception from him without his knowledge, and they do not repeat it.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33-35.-- « They gave, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] Lot in this place signifies the law, because certain men begotten of her, [0133B] and set under her, by understanding ill do in a manner make themselves drunk; and by using her not lawfully, they bring forth the works of unbelief. ⟦s2⟧ For the law is good, if a man use it lawfully.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- « They conceived therefore, » etc. [n: (HIER.)] Moab ( ) is interpreted *from the father,* and the whole name has its etymology. ⟦s2⟧ But Ammon, of whom as it were the reason of the name is rendered, *son of my kindred,* or, as it is better in the Hebrew, *benammi* ( ) that is *son of my people,* is so derived that in part it is the sense of the name, in part the word itself. ⟦s3⟧ For Ammon, from whom the Ammonites, is called *my people.*

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37, 38. ⟦s2⟧ « She called his name Moab, » etc. [n: (THEOD.)] This one seems the more shameless, who wished to transmit her incest even to posterity. ⟦s3⟧ For Moab [0133C] signifies *from the father*: the other the more modest, who gave her son the name Benammi, that is *son of my people.*
