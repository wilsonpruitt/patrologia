## CHAPTER XXI.

VERS. 1-3.-- « And God visited Sara, » [var: Vulg. *Dominus*, "the Lord"] etc. The figure of circumcision is manifold. For it is [0134D] according to the Apostle a seal of the justice of faith, to Abraham and to his seed, and a judgment for chastising those who should belong to this seed and faith; and there arises from this seed a prophecy of the Saviour, who should cleanse us in the present life from all sin through baptism, and in the time to come, after the resurrection, free us from all corruption of death. And the number eight befits the glory of the resurrection: for the Lord rose on the eighth day, that is, after the seventh, the sabbath; and we, after the six ages of this world and the seventh, the sabbath, that is, the rest of souls which meanwhile is passed in the other life, shall rise again as at an eighth season, stripped of all the carnal corruptions and vices of concupiscence (in which lust chiefly reigns).

[0135A]VERS. 4.-- « He circumcised » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Although circumcision was enjoined for many causes, it holds forth in a special way the figure of Christ, who through baptism strips us of the old man, and in the time to come, after the resurrection, will circumcise away all mortality and corruption. And in that Isaac was circumcised on the eighth day, the eighth age of the world is designated, when the resurrection of the saints shall be generally celebrated.

VERS. 8.-- « The child grew therefore, » etc. He grows who, disposing ascents in his heart, comes to the solid food of doctrine. Whence, grow, and thou shalt eat me.

« He grew, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] Not on the day of his son's birth, nor on the day of his circumcision, but on the day of his weaning did he make a feast: because great ought to be the joy [0135B] of the spiritual age, when a man has been made such as may be fed with solid food, like to those to whom it is said I Cor. III: « I gave you milk to drink, not meat. »

VERS. 9.-- « And when Sara had seen. » etc. [n: (HIER.)] This is expounded in two ways: either that he made sport for idols, according to that of Exod. XXXII: « The people sat down to eat and to drink, and they rose up to play. » Or that against Isaac, as standing in the place of the elder in age, he claimed the birthright for himself; which Sara hearing did not bear.

VERS. 10.-- « Cast out this bondwoman, » etc. [n: (BED., c. 21; ISID., c. 17, in Gen.)] It is asked: why did Sara before wish to receive a son by the bondwoman, or why does she now bid him be cast out [0135C] with his mother? Which she does not do inflamed with jealousy, but compelled by the mystery of prophecy. For Agar, according to the Apostle, brought forth the carnal people unto bondage; but Sara the spiritual and free people, with which freedom Christ has set us free. It was signified therefore by this mystery that the former people, begotten in bondage, does not remain in the house of Sara, that is, of the Church, for ever, nor is heir or partner of Christians, nor possesses the kingdom of glory together with the noble son, that is, the faithful people.

VERS. 11, 12.-- « He took it hard, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] It is asked why Abraham was made sorrowful, since this was a prophecy which he rather than Sara ought to have known: but Sara said from revelation what had first been revealed to her. He however was moved [0135D] by a father's affection, to whom the Lord afterwards revealed it. Or perhaps both of them at first knew not what it was, and it was prophesied through Sara who knew it not, when she was moved with a woman's mind on account of the bondwoman's pride.

VERS. 13.-- « For thy seed. » [n: (AUG.)] It is to be noted that Ismael also was called by God the seed of Abraham, whereas the Apostle says Rom. IX: « Not the children of the flesh, but the children of the promise shall be counted in the seed. » But this belongs properly to Isaac, who is not the son of the flesh, but of the promise, and is spiritual seed from the promise, not from the flesh.

VERS. 14.-- « Taking bread. » etc. [n: (ALC. EX BEDA, c. 21.)] When therefore Abraham cast out Agar, taking bread, he laid it upon her shoulders for this cause, etc. *as far as* [0136A] because certain of the people are to seek refuge under the shadow of the wood of the cross.

« He took bread and a bottle of water. » [var: Vulg. *tollens*, "taking" — the participle Migne's own lemma prints above] [n: (AUG. Q. in Gen. tom. III.)] It is asked: how did he lay upon her shoulders a boy so great? For he who, before Isaac was born, had been circumcised at thirteen years old, when Abraham was ninety-nine, and Isaac was born when his father was a hundred; and Ismael was playing with Isaac when Sara was moved, doubtless with a boy somewhat grown, who had already been weaned: assuredly he was more than sixteen years old when he was driven out with his mother. But granting that we take what is said of his playing with the little one as said by recapitulation, before Isaac was weaned; even so a boy of more than thirteen years could not be laid upon his mother's shoulders; [0136B] but it must be supplied, he gave over her boy, etc.

« He laid it on her shoulder. » [n: (HIER. in Q. Hebr.)] When Isaac was born, Ismael was thirteen years old, and after his weaning he is driven out with his mother. Among the Hebrews the opinion varies, some asserting the time of his weaning to have been appointed at the fifth year, others at the twelfth. We therefore, that we may choose the shorter age, reckon Ismael to have been cast out with his mother after eighteen years, and that it did not befit one already a youth to have sat upon his mother's neck. But that idiom of the Hebrew tongue is true, that every son is called an infant and a little one in comparison with his parents. At Rome too, even to this day, all sons are called infants.

VERS. 15-21.-- « She cast the boy. » etc. [n: (HIER.)] [0136C] That which follows: « And she cast the boy under one fir tree, and going away sat over against him afar off, as it were a bowshot. » [var: Vulg. *abjecit puerum subter unam arborum* … *seditque e regione procul quantum potest arcus jacere*, "under one of the trees" … "as far as a bow can shoot"] For she said: I shall not see the death of my little one, and she sat over against him; and at once it is joined. And the boy cried out and wept; and the Lord heard the voice of the boy from heaven, where he was, and the angel said to Agar: Let nothing move. For in the Hebrew this is written first: « I shall not see the death of my boy. » So it is read consequently, that Agar sat over against the boy, and lifted up her voice, and wept, and God heard the voice of the little one. For while the mother wept and awaited the death of her son, God heard the boy, whom he had promised should be for a great nation. For the mother bewails not her own death, but her son's.

[n: (AUG. Q. in Gen., tom. III.)] It is asked how [0136D] the mother, etc., *as far as* as those walking, of whatever age, often do.

VERS. 17.-- « And he heard. » [n: (BED. et ISID. ubi supra.)] The boy cries out weeping, and God heard him; and, an angel showing it, she beholds the fountain, and it is said for those who are converted to Christ, and who, weeping for their sins, are heard, and with the eyes of the heart opened see the fountain of water, that is, Christ, who says Joan. VII « He that thirsteth, let him come to me and drink. » [var: Vulg. *Si quis sitit*, "If any man thirst"] The angel signifies Elias, through whom the people is to believe. Whence Malach. IV: « Behold I will send you Elias, who shall turn the hearts of the fathers unto the children. » [var: Vulg. Mal. 4:5-6, two verses joined: *Eliam prophetam, antequam veniat dies Domini* … *Et convertet cor patrum ad filios*]

« The angel of the Lord. » [var: Vulg. *angelus Dei*, "the angel of God"] [n: (ISID.)] In that Scripture pronounces him who speaks to Agar first an angel, afterwards the Lord, [0137A] he is to be believed to have been the Son of God, who spoke through the law and the prophets, who through obedience to the Father's will is called an angel, but according to nature is God.

VERS. 22.-- « At the same time. » [n: (AUG. ubi supra.)] It can be asked when Abraham made a covenant with Abimelech, etc., *as far as* which she knew not.

VERS. 23-30.-- « Swear therefore, » etc. Often they who are without, drawn on by the converse of the faithful, desire to be joined to them and to be formed to better things by their examples, that they may take counsel both for themselves and for their neighbours' salvation.

VERS. 31, 32.-- « Therefore it was called, » etc. There is a twofold cause why it was called in Hebrew *Bersabee:* either because Abimelech received seven ewe-lambs from the hand [0137B] of Abraham. For seven are called *sabee,* or because they swore there, for *sabaa* signifies *an oath.* But if we read this cause above this name, it is said by prolepsis; as Bethel and Galgala, which, before they had these names, were called otherwise. It is to be noted from what goes before and from the present passage that Isaac was not born at the oak of Mambre ( *Alon* in Hebrew, in Latin *an oak* ) as is had in the Hebrew, but in Gerara, where Bersabee is a town even to this day, which province not long ago was called *Salutaris* from the division of the governors of Palestine. Whence Abraham dwelt in the land of the Palestinians many days.

VERS. 33, 34.-- « But Abraham, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] Abraham (as Stephen says) had not received an inheritance [0137C] there, nor the space of a foot. But the inheritance is to be understood which God was about to give freely, not bought with a price. And the space about the well is understood in reference to that space of purchase for which the seven ewe-lambs had been given, when Abimelech and Abraham swore to one another.
