## CHAPTER XXII.

VERS. 1.-- « Which things after they were done, » etc. [n: (AUG., Quaest. in Gen., tom. III.)] James says that God tempts no man; but in ordinary usage we say "to tempt" for that which is "to prove". James however speaks of that temptation by which every man is entangled in sin [ *Al.,* peccator]. Whence I Thess. I: « Lest perhaps he that tempteth should have tempted you. » For elsewhere it is written Deut. XIII: « [0137D] Your God tempteth you, that he may know whether you love him, that is, that he may make you to know. » [var: Vulg. *quia tentat vos Dominus Deus vester, ut palam fiat utrum diligatis eum an non*, "the Lord your God, … that it may appear openly whether you love him or not"] For a man knows not the powers of his own love, unless he learns it by experience.

VERS. 2.-- « Take thy son, » etc. [n: (ALC. in Gen., tom. I.)] Abraham was not bidden to kill his son at once, but to lead him for three days to the sacrifice, that by the length of the time the temptation might be increased. For through three days, with cares increasing, a father's bowels are tormented, and for a long space the father gazes upon his son, takes food with him, so many nights the boy hangs in his father's embrace, lies in his lap, and at every moment the grief of killing his son is heaped up in a father's love.

« Whom thou lovest, » etc. [n: (BEDA in Gen., c. 22.)] By the reminder of love and the recalling of the name the temptation [0138A] is heaped up, and a father's affection is stirred at the memory of the promise, because it was said: « In Isaac shall thy seed be called, » as though, if he were killed, the whole hope of the promise would be frustrated.

« Go into the land. » [n: (HIER. Q. Hebr., tom. III.)] The Hebrews say that this mountain is that in which the temple was afterwards built, on the threshing-floor of Orna the Jebusite. Whence II, Par. III: « They began to build the temple in the second month, on the second day of the month, in mount Moria, » [var: Vulg. *Et coepit Salomon aedificare domum Domini … Coepit autem aedificare mense secundo, anno quarto regni sui*, "Solomon began to build the house of the Lord … in the second month, in the fourth year of his reign"] which is for that cause interpreted *illuminating* and *shining,* because there is *debir* ( ) that is, the oracle of God, and the Spirit who teaches men the truth and inspires prophecies.

VERS. 3.-- « Leading his ass, » etc. [n: (ISID. in Gen., c. 18.)] The ass signifies the senseless folly of the Jews, which bore all the sacraments and [0138B] knew them not.

VERS. 4.-- « And on the third day. » [n: (HIER. ubi supra.)] It is to be noted that from Gerara as far as mount Moria, that is, the seat of the temple, is a journey of three days: and Abraham is said to have arrived there on the third day. Wrongly therefore do they think that Abraham dwelt at that time by the oak of Mambre, since to mount Moria there is scarce a full day's journey. The three days in which they came to the place of the sacrifice signify three ages, before the law, from Abraham to Moses; under the law, from Moses to John; thence to the Lord; and whatever remains, the third day is that of grace, in which third age the sacrifice of Christ was completed.

VERS. 5-8.-- « Stay here, » etc. [n: (ISID. ubi supra.)] Hear the Apostle: « Blindness in part happeneth [0138C] in Israel, » that is, « with the ass, » that « the fulness of the Gentiles might come in, » that is, « after we have adored. » When the sacrifice of the cross shall have been preached among the Gentiles, that is, « when the fulness of the Gentiles shall have come in, we will return to you, » that is, « and so all Israel shall be saved. » The two servants left behind, and not brought to the place of the sacrifice, are the Jews who, living slavishly and savouring carnally, understood not the humility and passion of Christ. Two indeed, because they were to be divided into two parts, which came to pass when Solomon sinned, when the people was divided in respect of the kingdom, not by the error of impiety, to whom it is said Jer. III: « Israel the adversary, and Juda the prevaricator. » [var: Vulg. *aversatrix Israel, comparatione praevaricatricis Judae*, "the backslider Israel, in comparison of the treacherous Juda"]

« I and the boy, » etc. [n: (ALC.)] With undoubting mind he willed to slay his son. He is to be praised in the constancy [0138D] of offering and in the faith of raising up. For he knew most certainly that God cannot deceive, and that, though the boy were killed, yet the promise of God would remain safe. Whence the Apostle Hebr. XI: « By faith Abraham hesitated not, when he offered up his only-begotten, in whom he had received the promise, believing that God is able to raise up even from the dead. » [var: Vulg. Hbr 11:17,19 *Fide obtulit Abraham Isaac, cum tentaretur*, "By faith Abraham offered Isaac, when he was tried"; the opening words are not in Hebrews]

« Abraham said. » [n: (ISID. ubi supra.)] Abraham, leading his only son to be sacrificed, signifies God the Father. Abraham received a son in old age, but God grows not old, yet the promise concerning Christ had in a manner grown old when he was born. It began from Adam, when it was said: « They shall be two in one flesh » [n: (Gen. II)]; and it was completed in the sixth age of the world. [0139A] Sara's old age in the people of God, that is, in the multitude of the prophets, signifies this same thing, because in the end of the times Christ was born of the people of holy souls. Her barrenness signifies that in this world we are saved by hope, and that in Christ, as in Isaac, we are all born, which birth of the Church [ *Al.,* Ecclesia] in the end of the times not nature but grace brought forth. Abraham therefore signifies God the Father, Isaac Christ. For as Abraham offered his only and beloved son as a victim to God, so God the Father delivered up his only-begotten Son for us. And as Isaac carried the wood upon which he was to be laid, so Christ the cross on which he was to be fastened.

VERS. 9-11.-- « And when he had bound, » etc. [n: (ISID. ubi supra.)] Isaac with his feet bound is laid upon the altar, [0139B] and Christ is fastened to the cross. But that which was figured by Isaac was transferred to the ram, because Christ is a sheep. For he is a son, because born; a ram, because sacrificed. The ram sticks fast in the briars, the cross has horns. For if two pieces of wood be joined together, the shape of a cross is produced. Whence Habac. III: « Horns in his hands. » The ram therefore sticking fast by the horns is Christ crucified. But briars are thorns: wicked thorns, which hanged the Lord. For among the thorns of sinners he was hanged. Whence Jer.: « With the thorns of their sins this people hath encompassed me [n: (Jer. XXXVIII.)] » Others understand this ram bound in the briars to be Christ crowned with thorns before his immolation.

VERS. 12.-- « Thou hast not spared, » etc. [n: (AUG. ubi supra.)] Did Abraham then not spare his son [0139C] on account of the angel? But in the angel the Lord was figured, who is God and the angel of great counsel. For God was in the angel, and spoke in his person. This will appear the more in what follows, when it is said: « And the angel of the Lord called Abraham a second time from heaven, saying: By my own self have I sworn, saith the Lord. » For it is not easily found that Christ calls the Father Lord, especially before the incarnation. For according to the flesh this is congruously said, according to which it is said in the second psalm: « The Lord said to me, » etc. But that which is said in the same place, c. 9: « The Lord said to my Lord, » etc., is referred to the prophet who speaks; as that: « The Lord rained from the Lord, » that is, our Lord, namely the Son, from our Lord, namely the Father.

[0139D]VERS. 13.-- « The ram. » *[n: (ALC.)] Historically.* The ram which was sacrificed for Isaac, we have not imagined [it], but it is true. Therefore it is rather thought that an angel brought it from elsewhere, than that the Lord created it there out of the earth after the work of the six days.

« The ram among the briars, » etc. [n: (HIER. in Quaest. Hebr.)] Aquila put *a briar* or *a thorn-brake,* etc., *as far as* which is so said in Hebrew.

VERS. 14-19.-- « And he called the name, » etc. [n: (HIER. ubi supra.)] For that which is had here, etc., *as far as* namely through the incarnation.

VERS. 20-24.-- « Melcha. » [n: (HIER. ubi supra.)] Of Melcha the wife of Nachor, etc., *as far as* and in that very name it is read in Isaias.
