## CHAPTER XXIV.

[0140C]

VERS. 1, 2.-- « And Abraham was old . . . . . And he said to his elder servant: Put thy hand, » etc. [n: (GREG.)] He bids him put his hand under his thigh, and swear by the God of heaven, because through that member his flesh was to come down who should be Abraham's son by his humanity, and divine by his divinity; as though he said: Touch my Son, and swear by my God. Yet he bids him put his hand not upon the thigh but under the thigh, because thence was to come down one who should be man, yet above men; whence it was not fitting that he should put his hand upon the thigh, because no flesh is above that flesh.

VERS. 3-8.-- « That thou take not. » [n: (GREG.)] *Allegorically.* A wife of the daughters of the Chanaanites is forbidden to Isaac, [0140D] because no souls of the reprobate are joined to Christ. A wife is bidden to be taken from his kindred, because the Church of the elect alone was to be wedded to Christ, whom the Only-begotten himself, by predestination and foreknowledge, did not already hold as a stranger. The servant who is sent to bring back the wife is the order of the prophets and apostles, and of all teachers; who, while they perform the word of preaching, become the providers, as it were, of every soul that is to be joined to the only-begotten Son.

VERS. 9.-- « He put therefore, » etc. [n: (HIER. in Quaest. Hebr.)] The Hebrews hand down that in his sanctification, etc., *as far as* and that they should bear bravely the weight of tribulation, which the camels signify.

VERS. 10.-- « Of all his goods, » etc. [n: (GREG.)] [0141A] Because they who speak of the Lord show in themselves the riches of the virtues, that they may invite men so much the more quickly to follow God, as they show forth in themselves to their hearers the things they tell of.

VERS. 11-13.-- « By the well. » [n: (GREG.)] He stood by the fountain, and from a sentence fixed beforehand set forth which one was to be chosen, because holy preachers consider the flowing words. Whence they gather from what hearer, or from what hearers, they may take the confidence of certainty.

VERS. 14-16.-- « The maid therefore, » etc. [n: (AUG., Q. in Gen. )] It is to be asked what unlawful auguries differ from this petition, etc., *as far as* whereby it is forbidden to tempt God.

« Let down thy pitcher. » Humble thy worldly eloquence to draw up the prophetic word, that [0141B] I may bend thy will to my persuasion.

VERS. 17-21.-- « And the servant ran to meet her, and said: Give me a little, » etc. [n: (GREG.)] *Allegorically.* Every preacher thirsts for the soul of his hearer, therefore the servant asks, Rebecca gives the drink: because the Church of the elect satisfies the desire of preachers out of the strength of her faith. For she who confesses the God whom she hears, offers to the preacher the water of refreshment and cooling. And it is to be noted that she set down the pitcher, bending it from her shoulder, because that confession is pleasing which proceeds from a good work. Or she supplied water, because in that which she believed she did not remain empty. For at once she preached what she had heard, and by speaking brought forth many preachers out of herself. For the water in the pitcher is the knowledge of preaching in measure. For she studies not to be more wise than it behoveth to be wise. [0141C] The pitcher on the arm is the measure of preaching in work. And she gives drink not only to all his companions, but to the camels also, because the word of life is preached not only to the wise, but also to the foolish. Rom. I: « To the wise and to the unwise I am a debtor. » Or water is given even to the beasts, when it is ordered how care of the flesh is to be had, that it be not spent upon pleasure, and be not denied to necessity; whence Rom. XIII: « Make not provision for the flesh in its desires; » and Ephes. V: « For no man ever hated his own flesh. »

VERS. 22-24.-- « The man brought forth, » etc. [n: (HIER.)] *Beka* ( ), which is here put for a didrachma, is half an ounce: but *Schekel* ( ), which in Latin, though corruptly, is called *siclus*, is an ounce. [n: (GREG.)] Because the preacher [0141D] adorns the hearing of the Church by obedience, and its hands by work. The earrings are of two sicles, the bracelets of ten, because the first virtue of obedience lies in charity, which is the love of God and of the neighbour; and good work is perfected by the completion of the decalogue, so that when good things have begun to be done, no evil things may be done.

VERS. 25-28.-- « Straw also. » Because the Church, receiving spiritual things, renders earthly stipends to preachers; whence I Cor. IX: « If we have sown unto you spiritual things, it is a great thing if we reap your carnal things.

« A large room. » From the former people the Church showed that she had known the law of nature, and she received the preaching in the ample bosom of charity: for the teacher's spacious [0142A] dwelling is the breadth of charity in the hearer's heart; whence in the sixth of the second to the Corinthians: « Be not straitened in us, but in your own bowels; » [var: Vulg. *angustiamini autem in visceribus vestris*, "but you are straitened in your own bowels"] as if to say, enlarge the room of your mind for receiving doctrine, straiten it for thinking upon carnal things.

VERS. 29-32.-- « He had, » etc. Because carnal men joined to the faithful, while they behold spiritual gifts, hang in wonder, and if not so far as works, yet so far as the receiving of the faith they admit the preaching: for while they consider that good men shine with miracles, they refuse not the things they hear of eternity, although, not following the Church in their morals, they remain in carnal working.

« And he had. » Because there are carnal men joined to the faithful, by whose familiarity they sometimes come forth to hear [0142B] the word, and seeing the gifts of God bestowed on the faithful they marvel, and receive the word in the heart, which they are not at pains to fulfil in good morals.

VERS. 33-36.-- « I will not eat. » Because there are those who are ready to retain teachers out of temporal stipends: but these are unwilling to receive them, unless they first obtain eternal things. For if they do not find fruit in souls, they scorn to take stipends for bodies; they wash not the feet, because they relieve the desires of labour with no consolation.

VERS. 37-41.-- « And he made me swear, » etc. [n: (AUG. ubi supra. )] The meaning is the same, the words not all, or the same, or so spoken; which is to be said on account of those who calumniate the evangelists because in some words they do not altogether agree, although the matter and the meaning be the same; [0142C] for one man wrote this book, who could have set down the things he said above, when Abraham gave the charge, or the rest, had he judged them to belong to the matter: since the truth of a narration does not require anything except that there be truth of things and of meanings, whereby the will (for the making known of which words are made) may sufficiently appear.

VERS. 42-52.-- « I came therefore to-day, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] In the Hebrew it is written, etc., *as far as* let the Jews therefore show *alma* set down anywhere where it sounds a young girl and not a virgin.

VERS. 53-58.-- « And bringing forth, » etc. [n: (GREG. ubi supra. )] After the servant obtained the cause of the marriage, etc., *as far as* « He gave them to Rebecca. » Rebecca is not adorned with the gold of Bathuel, because the ornaments of a barbarian and unskilled man are not worthy. From the house of Abraham she receives ornaments, [0142D] because patience is adorned from the house of wisdom; from Abraham's servant she receives earrings, that is, golden words in the ears; and bracelets, that is, golden works in the hands, and other ornaments; by which are figured the diverse kinds of the virtues.

VERS. 59-60.-- « And her nurse. » It was seemly that a virgin setting out to her wedding without her parents should be cherished with the solace of a nurse.

VERS. 61.-- « Rebecca therefore, » etc. [n: (GREG.)] Because the Church has with her souls of lesser merit, who follow her in their morals: but they are not advanced to the couch of contemplation. She had also certain servants in her company, because with the prophets there were men who lived well, even though they had not the spirit of prophecy: [0143A] and with the apostles and teachers, men who held to their life, and yet did not preach.

« Who in haste, » etc. The servant returns in haste to his master, because preachers, when they obtain the life of their hearers, present it to God, of whose gift they have it, not to themselves.

VERS. 62.-- « At that time Isaac, » etc. [n: (GREG. ubi supra. )] The Living and Seeing One is the Lord, etc., *as far as* he bore now a crown of thorns, now a cross.

« He dwelt, » etc. [n: (PATERIUS in Gen. ex dictis GREG.)] Isaac, when Rebecca came, was dwelling in the south country, because Christ, when the Church came to him, abode in the minds of those whom not the cold of the body but the fervour of charity held: such as were Anna, Simeon, and some of the people of the Jews.

[0143B]VERS. 63.-- « And he was gone forth, » etc. [n: (HIER.)] By the south country he signifies Gerara, whence he had been brought to be sacrificed. He went forth, that he might exercise himself in the field. In the Hebrew it is read: « Isaac went forth to speak into the field, the evening being now come on; » where it is signified that the Lord prayed alone upon the mountain, and that Isaac, who was a type of the Lord, went forth from the house at the ninth hour, or before sunset, to prayer as a just man, and offered spiritual victims to God.

[n: (GREG. ubi supra. )] The field is the world, into which Christ went forth, because he vouchsafed to appear visibly.

Whence Habac. III: « Thou wentest forth for the salvation of thy people. » Isaac went out into the field to meditate, because Christ, holding forth the form of humility, showed the exercise of long-suffering, [0143C] of passion, and examples of patience, as trained young men are wont to meditate in the use of arms. The meditation of arms is the frequenting of the passion. For he who bore stripes, spittings, the lance and the cross, suffered his passion to be frequented in himself even unto death. We call the passions arms, because through them we are freed from our adversaries; whence Luc. XXI: « In your patience you shall possess your souls. »

« The day being now far spent, etc. Because he took upon him the exercises of the passions near the end of the world; whence psal. CXL: The lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice. »

VERS. 64.-- « She came down from the camel, » etc. [n: (GREG., lib. I, Moral., c. 14.)] Rebecca is brought to Isaac upon a camel, etc., *as far as* upon the crooked backs of camels they carry riches.

[0143D]VERS. 65, 66.-- « Who is that man, » etc. [n: (GREG. apud Paterium. )] Rebecca, having seen Isaac, learns who he is by asking the servant: because the Church, by the sayings of the prophets and apostles, understands what she ought to believe concerning Christ. And she straightway covered herself with a cloak, because the more she penetrates the mysteries of Christ, the more she is confounded at her past life. Whence she strives to cover her infirmity with a cloak; and she who before was carried freely upon the camel, coming down is covered with shame, to whom it is said as it were more shamefacedly: What fruit therefore hadst thou then in those things of which thou art now ashamed?

« But she, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Another edition, « teristrum, » which even now is a kind of Arabian garment wherewith the women of that province are veiled.

[0144A]VERS. 67.-- « Who brought her in, » etc. [n: (GREG. ut supra. )] Isaac brought her into his mother's tent, and took her to wife, because in the place of the Synagogue, of which Christ was born, he loves the Church: that she who by kindred, that is, by predestination, had been nearest, may by love be joined as his wife.
