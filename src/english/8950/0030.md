## CHAPTER XXV.

VERS. 1, 2.-- « But Abraham took another wife, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] *Allegorically.* Sara being dead, Abraham took Cetura. Not on account of incontinence, since he was already of great age: nor on account of the procuring of children, since seed as the stars of heaven had been promised him from Isaac. But as Agar and Ismael figured the carnal men of the Old Testament, so Cetura and her sons the heretics, who [0144C] deem themselves to belong to the New Testament. But both were concubines, Sara alone the wife; whence Cant. VI: « One is my dove, » etc.

*Allegorically.* After the assumption of the Church many souls deceived by heretical depravity clave to Christ, and these brought forth not catholics but schismatics, who divided the unity of the Church: whom the sons of Cetura signify, who are numbered below.

[n: (HIER., in Q. Hebr. )] Cetura, in Hebrew, *joined,* etc., *as far as* for the more a man is wearied in the flesh, the more robust is he in spirit and the fitter for the embraces of wisdom.

VERS. 3, 4.-- « The sons of Dadan were Assurim, and Lathusim, and Laomim. But of Madian was born Epha and Opher and Henoc and Abida. » [n: (HIER.)] They think that Assurim is to be rendered *merchants,* etc., *as far as* [0144D] and that the regions of India were occupied by very many of the sons of Abraham by Cetura.

VERS. 5-7.-- « And Abraham gave, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] Because gifts are given to the sons of the concubines, that is, of carnal men; but heretics, or Jews, do not attain to the promised kingdom, because they follow carnal gains; Isaac alone is the heir, because not the children of the flesh are counted in the seed, but of the promise.

VERS. 8-11.-- « And decaying he died in a good old age, and of advanced years, and full of days, and was gathered to his people. And they buried him, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] it is wrongly added in the LXX: « Abraham decaying died, » because it does not befit Abraham to decay or to be diminished. That also [0145A] which we have set down « in a good old age an old man, » and which is put more fully in the Greek codices, « full of days; » which, though it seem to expound the sense, in that he died full of light and of the works of day, yet makes the more for the anagogy if it be simply set down « full. »

[n: ( Glossa Graeca. )] Behold, Moses knew the life of the world to come, for no man can be added to those who are not.

VERS. 12.-- « These are the generations, » etc. These peoples are reckoned before the sons of Isaac, who, although they be of the seed of Abraham, are yet not reckoned in the seed, because that which is animal is first, then that which is spiritual. Whence after the reckoning of the carnal, the genealogy of Isaac is set down, of whom the promise was made, and the succession of the elect to be propagated.

[0145B]VERS. 13-15.-- « And these are the names of his sons in their titles and generations. The firstborn of Ismael Nabajoth, then Cedar, and Abel, » [var: Vulg. *Adbeel*] etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Twelve sons are born to Ismael, the firstborn Nabajoth, from whom all the region from the Euphrates to the Red Sea is called Nabathea, which is a part of Arabia. For their families, towns, villages and tribes are celebrated by the naming of these. From one of these, Cedar in the desert, and Duma another region, and Thema to the south, and Chedma, otherwise Chethma, to the eastern quarter, is named. As to what we read at the end of the chapter according to the LXX, « he dwelt over against the face of all his brethren, » it is truer which we have set down, « he fell asleep before all his brethren, » that is, he died in the hands of his sons, his children [0145C] yet surviving. For sons are called brethren; whence Jacob: « Let it be set before my brethren and thy brethren, and let them judge between us. » [var: Vulg. Gn 31:37 *pone hic … et judicent inter me et te*, "put it here … and let them judge between me and thee"] For it is not to be believed that Jacob, apart from his own children, had any brethren with him.

VERS. 16, 17.-- « Twelve princes of their tribes. And the years of the life of Ismael were, » etc. The number twelve, as it is consecrated in the apostles and in apostolic men, so is it execrable in the reprobate against the faith of the Trinity and the Gospel spread abroad through the four quarters of the world.

VERS. 18, 19.-- « And he dwelt from Hevila as far as Sur, which looketh towards Egypt, » etc. The region which Phison went about was so called from Hevila, the grandson of Noe, and it is a wilderness over against the face of Egypt. Sur also [0145D] is a wilderness between Cades and Barad, stretching the desert as far as the Red Sea and the borders of Egypt.

VERS. 20.-- « From Mesopotamia the sister of Laban. » A barren region, which is so called in Greek because it lies in the middle between two rivers, the Tigris and the Euphrates. For μέσος is said *middle,* ποταμὸς *a river*; hence hippopotamus, that is, a river horse.

VERS. 21, 22.-- « And he besought . . . but the little ones struggled in her womb, » etc. [n: (HIER.)] For the stirring or the collision the LXX put « they played » or « they kicked back: » Aquila, « the sons fought in her womb. » Symmachus, « they were borne upon the surface after the likeness of a ship. » [n: (GREG., Dial. I, 8.)] The things which the saints [0146A] effect by praying, etc., *as far as* for he in whom God had predestined to multiply the seed of Abraham obtained by prayer that he might have sons.

« Who heard her. » [var: Vulg. *eum*, "him" — Isaac, who is the one that prayed] [n: (CHRYSOST.)] Think not that he obtained forthwith and at once what he desired: for he continued twenty years praying and beseeching God. For as he taught us how many years he had when he took Rebecca (namely forty), so he assigned to us of how many years Isaac was when sons were born to him. « He was sixty years old, he says, when the little ones were born. »

« And she went, » etc., [n: (AUG., Quaest. in Gen., tom. III.)] It is asked: whither did she go, since there were as yet no prophets or priests of the tabernacle or of the temple of the Lord? Perhaps to the place where Abraham set up an altar. [0146B] But how answers were given there, Scripture is silent; was it through some priest? And it is a wonder, if there was one, that he was not named, and that no mention of priests was made. Perhaps there, when they had laid their desires before God in prayer, they slept, that they might be admonished through a dream: or Melchisedech was yet living, whose excellence was so great that it was doubted whether he were a man or an angel. Or perhaps even at that time there were men of God, in whom God could be consulted. Whichever of these, or perhaps somewhat else, it was. Rebecca however according to the Scripture consulted the Lord, and deserved his answer.

VERS. 23.-- « Who answering said: Two nations, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] By historical propriety the answer is found fulfilled, when for Israel's sake, that is, Jacob's, [0146C] the younger son overcame the Idumeans, that is, the nation which Esau propagated, and made them tributaries through David; which they were for a long time, until king Joram [n: (IV Reg. VIII)]. For then the Idumeans rebelled, and shook the yoke from their neck, as Isaac had prophesied when he blessed the younger before the elder, and said this same thing to the elder, when he afterwards blessed him.

VERS. 24-26.-- « The time was now come, » etc. [n: (HIER.)] Where we have put « hairy », in the Hebrew there stands *seyr* ( ); whence Esau, as we read elsewhere, was called *seyr,* that is, hairy.

« Forthwith the other, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] That this was done figuratively is known even to the Jews who believe not: how the people of the Church overcame the people of the Synagogue, and the people of the Jews, the greater in time, served [0146D] the lesser people of the Christians.

[n: (BED. in hoc cap., tom. II.)] In individual men also this may be said. « Two nations and two peoples » are within us, namely of the vices and of the virtues; the one lesser, the other greater: for the evil are more than the good, and the vices more than the virtues. Yet the people overcomes the people, « and the elder shall serve the younger, » namely the flesh the spirit, and the vices the virtues. Esau goes before, red, and all hairy as a skin: then comes forth Jacob holding his brother's heel; for the former people, polluted with the blood of the prophets and of Christ, was encompassed with the squalor of sin and of wickedness: whose heel the younger held, because the lesser people overcame the greater.

VERS. 27, 28.-- « Esau became, » etc. [n: (GREG.)] [0147A] It signifies the life of those who, poured out upon outward pleasures, follow the flesh. He is also called a husbandman, because lovers of the world cultivate outward things the more, the more they leave their own inward things untilled.

« Simple. » [n: (GREG.)] Because simple men shun to be scattered abroad by outward cares, and abide in thought and in the dwelling of conscience. For he dwells in tents who binds himself within the secret places of the mind, lest, while he gapes outwards after many things, he depart from himself, estranged in thought.

VERS. 29, 30.-- « And Jacob boiled, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] *Red* or *tawny:* in Hebrew it is called *Edom*: and from this, that Esau sold his birthright for red food, he received the name of the tawny, that is, of *Edom*.

VERS. 31, 32.-- « To whom Jacob said, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] [0147B] That Esau sold his birthright to his younger brother for food, and was afterwards deprived of the paternal blessing promised him, signifies the Jewish people: who in Exodus is called the firstborn son of God, and for the gains of the world not only lost the honour of his primacy, but also deserved not to obtain the prepared reward of the heavenly kingdom. Whence by way of reproach it is said Matth. XII: « The kingdom of God shall be taken away from you and shall be given to the Gentiles, » [var: Vulg. Mt 21:43 *dabitur genti facienti fructus ejus*, "shall be given to a nation yielding the fruits thereof"] etc. But the birthright was a priestly garment, clothed in which the elder-born offered victims to God with their father's blessing, as pontiffs: of which gift, through desire of earthly love, the Jews were bereft, together with the glory of the kingdom to come.

VERS. 33, 34.-- « Esau swore, » etc. [n: (GREG., lib. XXX, Moral., c. 13.)] But it is to be known that the vice of gluttony tempts us in five [0147C] ways, etc., *as far as* and not the things which the lust of eating suggests.
