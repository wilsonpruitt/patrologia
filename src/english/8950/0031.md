## CHAPTER XXVI.

VERS. 1-7.-- « Now a famine having arisen upon the land, » etc. [n: (AUG., Quaest. in Gen., tom. III.)] It is asked when this was done, that Isaac went into Gerara, etc., *as far as* that we may understand that temporal things can neither be given, nor ought to prosper, save from the one God.

VERS. 8-11.-- « And when they had passed through, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] From the time of Christ until the times of Constantine the faith was preached not in the assembly of the peoples, but as it were through hiding-places, and the Christians were gathered together in secret: at length the princes of the world, being now Christians, seeing through the miracles of Christ his majesty tempered [0147D] to human infirmity, that they might be joined together and be « two in one flesh, » permitted the sacrament of Christ and the Church to be preached now openly, and grieved that it had long lain hidden.

« He saw him sporting, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] What it means, in the sacrament of Christ and the Church, etc., *as far as* « that he might dwell in us. »

VERS. 12, 13.-- « Now Isaac sowed in that land, » [var: Vulg. Gen. 26:12 *Sevit*, "planted"] etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Although Isaac sowed in a foreign land, I do not think that so great a fruitfulness of barley was his. Whence I judge it better what is had in the Hebrew, and what Aquila translated: « He found in that year a hundredfold reckoned. » But Scripture, keeping silence as to the kind of crops which yielded a hundredfold, seems [0148A] to have shown in him the multiplication of all virtues.

VERS. 14-16.-- « For these things envying, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] Isaac, Christ, who first wills to dig wells, etc., *as far as* « we shall go. »

« The wells they also stopped up, » etc. [n: (GREG.)] *Morally.* Often, when we are intent upon the divine words, we endure the more grievously the snares of demons; which sprinkle upon our mind the dust of earthly thoughts, that they may darken the eyes of our purpose from the light of the inward vision. For we dig wells when we penetrate the deep things of Scripture, which the Allophyli secretly fill up: because they thrust in earthly thoughts and take away the water of divine knowledge, whence in psalm CXVIII: « Depart from me, O malignant ones, and I will search the commandments of the Lord. »

[0148B]VERS. 17.-- « And he departing, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] And Isaac went thence, and came into the valley of Gerara, and dwelt there. In place of *valle, torrentem* they have in the Hebrew. For never, after he was made great, could Isaac dwell in a valley, but in a torrent. Of which it is written in psalm CIX: « He shall drink of the torrent in the way. » Of which Elias drank in the time of famine; but because he was not perfect as Christ, the torrent dried up for him. But Christ also was delivered up in the torrent, dedicating our regeneration and the sacrament of baptism.

VERS. 18, 19.-- « He dug other wells, » etc. [n: (ISID. ex Greg.)] Isaac dug wells among a foreign nation: wherein we are admonished that in this pilgrimage we penetrate the deep things of our thoughts, and that, until the water of true understanding return, the hand of inquiry grow not sluggish in [0148C] draining out the earthly things of the heart. The Allophyli, lying in wait, fill up the wells, because unclean spirits, when they behold us digging earnestly, thrust the vexations of temptations into our thoughts.

VERS. 20, 21.-- « Against the shepherds, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Aquila and Symmachus translated « adverse and contrary, » but in the Hebrew there is « Satana; » whence it is plain that Satan is interpreted *contrary*.

VERS. 22-25.-- « Breadth. » In the Hebrew *Rehobot* ( ) [ed: Migne prints a Hebrew word within these parentheses; our digitized source has lost it and left them empty. Recovery requires the plate.], *breadths.* Whence that is proved which was said above in chapter X: « He himself built Ninive » and *rabooth,* that is, the streets thereof.

« Now he hath enlarged, » etc. Isaac was enlarged, and filled the whole earth with the knowledge of the Trinity: and in the whole [0148D] world he set the breadth of the Church, being nevertheless first known in Judea, and named in Israel: afterwards « their sound went forth into all the earth [n: (Psal. XVIII)], » that is, the sound of the apostles, who throughout the universal world dug wells, and by water showed [it] to all, baptizing all nations in the name of the Father, and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost [n: (Matth. XVIII)]. » The wells of Abraham, which he opened, he called by the same names as his father: for Moses and the prophets are called among us by their own names, nor are the names of these wells changed.

VERS. 26-31.-- « To which place, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Abimelech has not always peace with Isaac, etc., *as far as* and there are many other things in which the philosophers disagree with us, or agree.

[0149A]In place of « Ochozath, » *pronubo,* there is in the Hebrew « the company of his friends, » so that it may signify both the man and the crowd of friends which had come with the king: among whom was Phicol, the prince of his army.

« Abimelech, and Ochozath, » etc. [n: (ISID. in Gen., tom. V.)] Two came with Abimelech: Ochozath, his son-in-law, etc., *as far as* he is a debtor to the wise and to the unwise.

VERS. 32-35.-- « But behold, they came. » [n: (HIER.)] I know not how in the LXX « and the servants of Isaac came, telling him of the well which they had dug, and they said to him: We have not found water. And he called the name of it, Oath. » For what etymology there is, that it should for this cause be called Oath, etc., *as far as* it does not agree [0149B] that Isaac should not find water.
