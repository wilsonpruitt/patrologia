## CHAPTER XXVII.

VERS. 1, 2.-- « Now Isaac grew old, » etc. *[n: (HIPP. mart., ex Hieron., epist. ad Damasum, tom. I.)] Mystically.* Isaac bears the image of God the Father, Rebecca of the Holy Ghost, Esau of the former people and of the devil, Jacob of the Church and of Christ. The old age of Isaac signifies the consummation of the world; his darkening eyes, that faith has perished from the world, and that the light of religion has been neglected. Because he is called the elder son, it is the receiving of the law of the Jews. Because the father loved his meats and his hunting: they are men saved from error, whom every just man hunts by his teaching. The word of God, the blessing of the promise, and the hope of the kingdom to come, in which with Christ [0149C] they are to reign, and to celebrate the true sabbath. Rebecca, full of the Holy Ghost, knowing what she had heard before she bore, that « the elder shall serve the younger: » she bears the form of the Holy Ghost, who, what she knew was to be in Christ, beforehand meditated in Jacob, speaks to the younger son. « Go to the flock and bring me two of the best kids; » prefiguring the fleshly coming of the Saviour, in which he should set free those who were held liable to sins. For kids everywhere signify sinners. Two are commanded to be brought, that the taking up of the two peoples might be signified. That is, of a tender and human, that is, of a teachable and innocent soul. The robe of Esau is the faith, that is, the Scriptures of the Hebrews which were first given to them, with which the Gentile people was afterwards clothed; the skins put about [0149D] his arms, the sins of both peoples, which Christ, in the stretching out of his hands, fastened with himself to the cross: for he in his own body bore not his own, but others' sins.

VERS. 2.-- « And he called Esau. » [n: (ISID. in Gen., tom. V.)] Esau a hunter, and a husbandman, but Jacob, a simple man, dwelt in tents. Esau signifies the Hebrew people, Jacob the Gentile people. But Isaac [signifies] God, who made both peoples sons to himself, the one by the law, the other by faith. Esau was a husbandman, because the Jew served God for earthly things, to whom it was not said that he should be poor in spirit, meek, peaceable, merciful, suffering persecution, that he might have a plentiful reward in the heavens; but: « If [0150A] you keep my commandments, I will give you rains in their seasons, » etc., which pertain to the delights of the flesh. He was also a hunter, because by the pouring out of the blood of rams and of calves, and of goats, he appeased God. « Jacob, a simple man, dwelt in tents, » because the elect of the Gentiles studied to please God by faith and by will. By the tents we understand the divers Churches throughout the world, or the divers orders

VERS. 3-5.-- « Take thy arms, » etc. Isaac loved Esau, because he fed upon his hunting. Rebecca loved Jacob, Isaac [signifies] God; but Rebecca figures the foreknowledge of God, because God loved the people of the Jews, in that he was continually appeased by their oblations. But foreknowing that the people of the Gentiles would be far better, by the grace of his foreknowledge [0150B] he loved them more abundantly. While Esau tarried at the hunt, that he might set meats from the wild beasts before his father and be blessed, the mother brought Jacob to his father to receive the blessing: because the Jews still tarry in the things of the law, and come late to the faith of Christ, the mother, grace, offered the Gentiles, eagerly taking up the faith, to receive the blessing owed to the Jews. But in what manner they earned the gift of the blessing, the works of Jacob bear witness, which he did in order to receive the blessing.

VERS. 6-13. « She said to her son Jacob: I have heard thy father speaking, » etc. *Allegorically.* By the two kids we may understand the confession of sins of the Gentile people and its penance. The best kids, because the confession of sins and penance is very acceptable to God. [0150C] Whence Luke XV: « There shall be joy in heaven upon one sinner doing penance, » etc. *Historically.* By the one sinner he understands the people of the Gentiles, by the ninety-nine just, the Jews, who seemed to themselves just in the observance of the law. Whence Luke XVI: « For what cause do you justify yourselves before men, » [var: Vulg. Luc. 16:15 *Vos estis qui justificatis vos coram hominibus*, "You are they who justify yourselves before men" — a statement, not a question] etc.; who are set down in an imperfect number, because the law brought no one to perfection.

VERS. 14.-- « She prepared meats, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] *Mystically.* By the delightful meats which are prepared for Isaac from the kids, we understand the works of penance, etc., *as far as* it often brings past sins before the eyes of the mind.

VERS. 15-19.-- « And with the garments of Esau, » etc. [n: (HIER.)] In this place the Hebrews hand down that the firstborn discharged [0150D] the office of priests, and had a priestly vestment, clothed wherein they offered victims, before Aaron was chosen unto the priesthood.

VERS. 20-24.-- « How, said he, couldst thou find it so quickly, my son? » [n: (HIPPOLYT.)] Isaac asks of Jacob why he had come so quickly, marvelling, that is, at the swift faith of believers. Delightful meats are offered, because a victim pleasing to God is the salvation of sinners. Because after the eating the blessing follows, and he enjoys the odour thereof, he openly declares the power of the resurrection and of the kingdom. « Behold the smell of my son as the smell of a full field. » This is the blessing. With the odour of the name of Christ, as a field, the world is filled. The blessing is of the dew of heaven, that is, the rain of the divine word; and of the fatness of the earth, [0151A] that is, the gathering together of peoples; of the multitude of corn and wine, this is the multitude which he gathers from the sacrament of his body and blood. Peoples serve him, that is, the converted Gentiles. The tribes of the peoples adore him, believers converted through circumcision. He is the Lord of his brethren, that is, of the Jews. The sons of his mother adore him, because according to the flesh he was born of her: he that shall curse him, shall be cursed; and he that shall bless him, shall be blessed. Christ is blessed from the mouth of a people ignorant of the Father, that is, is truly spoken of, but by the Jews another is thought to be blessed, who is looked for by them that err.

VERS. 25, 26.-- « Which when he had eaten, being offered him, » etc. *Mystically.* After Isaac had taken food, Jacob offered him wine: so by the food the works of the active life [0151B] are signified; by the wine, which estranges the mind, the contemplative is understood, which makes the minds of the elect estranged from the love of the world. After the food and the drink. Jacob is blessed, because they receive the heavenly blessing from God who study to appease him unto the end by good works.

VERS. 27.-- « Blessing him, he said, » etc. [n: (GREG., hom. 6 in Ezech.)] The one is sent to hunt, etc., *as far as* which the elder, going forth abroad, left within.

[n: (GREG., ibid.)] Isaac, with darkened eyes, blessed his son Jacob not knowing him; the things that were to come he foresaw, and who stood present he knew not. For it is certain that the Jews, who had fully learned the law, knew the coming of Christ. Whence in Matthew II, Herod inquires of [0151C] the chief priests where Christ should be born; and they answered him forthwith, « In Bethlehem. » Therefore they had known him before, whom, at the time of the passion, while they despised him, they knew not; whose earlier knowledge, and later ignorance, is signified by the darkening of Isaac; who, while he blessed Jacob, foresaw what should befall him in time to come, but knew not that he stood present. So the people of the Jews received the mysteries of prophecy, but held their eyes blind in contemplation, because they saw not him present, of whom they foresaw many things in time to come. For they were not able to discern him set before them, the power of whose coming they had long before announced.

[n: (GREG., hom. 10 in Ezech., tom. II.)] Isaac knows not the son whom he blessed, etc., *as far as* and because through some it rises up into contemplation, through some it grows fat in [0151D] the works of the active life. There follows:

VERS. 28, 29.-- « God give thee of the dew of heaven, » etc. Dew falls from above, and finely, and we receive of the dew of heaven as often as, by the infusion of inward contemplation, we see something finely of the things above. But when we do good works through the body, we are enriched from the fatness of the earth.

« Abundance of corn, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] This is the multitude which he gathers from the sacrament of his body and blood.

VERS. 30-32.-- « Esau came, » etc. Late does Esau return to his father, because the former people shall be converted at the end. Whence in psalm LVIII: « They shall return at evening, and shall suffer hunger like dogs, and shall go round about [0152A] the city. » And elsewhere: « They opened their mouths, as one that eateth in secret. »

VERS. 33-35. « Isaac was affrighted. » When the elder son sought again the promised blessing, Isaac was affrighted, and knew that he had blessed the one for the other; nor is he indignant, the sacrament being known, but he confirmed the blessing, saying: « And I have blessed him, and he is blessed. » [var: Vulg. Gen. 27:33 *benedixique ei, et erit benedictus*, "and I have blessed him, and he shall be blessed"] This is the first blessing of Isaac, given to the lesser people of the Christians. But neither is the elder son altogether despised, because, when the fulness of the Gentiles shall have come in, then all Israel shall be saved. Of which second blessing there is a prophecy. « In the fatness of the earth, and in the dew of heaven from above shall be thy blessing. « In the fatness [sic: *pinguedidine*] of the earth, » that is, in the fruitfulness of things, and in the power of the kingdom, which was in that people; « and in the dew of heaven, » [0152B] the eloquences of God. For to them were entrusted the eloquences of God, and the testaments of the law. « Thou shalt live by the sword: » because that people, given over to blood, wrought slaughter upon Christ and the prophets, « And thou shalt serve thy younger brother, » [var: Vulg. Gen. 27:40 *et fratri tuo servies*, without *minori*] that is, the Christian people. « And the time shall come, when thou shalt shake off and loose his yoke from thy neck; » that is, when, converted through the knowledge of faith and the grace of Christ, thou shalt have laid down the burden of the law; when then thou shalt no longer serve the younger, but through faith shalt be called brother.

« Isaac was affrighted, » etc. So that a certain alienation of mind should follow, which is called ecstasy, which is wont to come to pass in the revelations of great things. Whence it is to be understood that Isaac was spiritually admonished to confirm the blessing upon Jacob, with whom he ought rather [0152C] to have been angry, because he deceived his father. In Adam also an ecstasy went forth, before he said: « They shall be two in one flesh [n: (Gen. II)], » which is « a great sacrament in Christ and in the Church. »

« And I have blessed him, » etc. [n: (ALC.)] If the will of a just man is good, how is it that Isaac blessed not Esau whom he wished, but Jacob whom he wished not? The will of a just man, as far as pertains to conscience, is good: but God alone judges of things to come. Isaac after the manner of men thought that the elder son was to be blessed: but God, who is the knower of hidden things, showed the younger worthy of the blessing, that he might show the blessing to be not of man, but of God: therefore in Numbers it is said to Moses and Aaron, the priests: « Put my name upon the children of Israel. I the Lord [0152D] will bless them [n: (Num. VI)]. » [var: Vulg. Num. 6:27 *Invocabuntque nomen meum super filios Israel, et ego benedicam eis*, "And they shall invoke my name upon the children of Israel, and I will bless them"] It belongs to the priest to bless, to God to grant the effect. But when Isaac understood through the spirit of prophecy that the blessing was destined to the younger, he said, « I have blessed him, and he shall be blessed. »

VERS. 36-39.-- « Rightly is his name called Jacob: for he hath supplanted me, » etc. [n: (HIER.)] Jacob is interpreted *a supplanter*. Therefore from this, that he had before deceived his brother, he alluded to the name, who was for this cause called Jacob before, because at his birth he took hold of his brother's heel.

VERS. 40.-- « And thou shalt serve thy brother, » etc. Because the Idumeans were tributaries to Israel, who is called Jacob.
