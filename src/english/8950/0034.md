## CHAPTER XXIX.

[0155C]

VERS. 1-10.-- « Jacob therefore setting out, » etc. « He saw a well in the field, » etc. The water of the well is the knowledge of the Old Testament: which the stone closed, because the letter of the law hid in it the spiritual sense; but when Christ came the stone was removed, when through the preaching of the New Testament the shadow of the law was shut out and the truth of the Gospel laid open.

*Allegorically.* By the well baptism, by the field the world is expressed: by the three flocks lying beside the well are signified those who pertain to the receiving of the faith of the Trinity, in humility seeking the grace of baptism.

« The stone. » The hardness of unbelief, which being cast away, the baptism of life is received. But the shepherds are the prelates [0155D] of the Church, who, the stone being removed, desire through Jacob to water the flocks: because, when Christ takes away unbelief from the hearts of those whom he has predestined to the sacrament of baptism, they are washed with the wave of purification.

VERS. 11-16.-- « He kissed her. » [n: (AUG.)] It was the custom, chiefly in that simplicity of the ancients, that kinsfolk should kiss kinsfolk, and it is done today in many places. But it may be asked: how did she receive a kiss from one unknown, if Jacob afterwards declared the kinship? It is therefore to be understood: either that he, who knew the kinship, confidently rushed to a kiss, or that Scripture afterwards narrated it by recapitulation, since Jacob first declared who he was.

VERS. 16-19.-- « The name of the elder was Lia, » etc. Victorinus [0156A] the martyr interpreted Rachel and Lia into the likeness of the Church and the Synagogue. He judges that Lia, the elder by birth, signifies the Synagogue, because she first bore the people of God: who is said to be blear-eyed, because the law given through Moses was wrought together and sealed. Rachel, the younger and the fair, first barren, afterwards fruitful, signifies the Church, which is later in time, but holy in body and in spirit. Her eyes are comely, because they deserved to see the Gospel. But she was long barren while the Synagogue brought forth a people. Jacob served for Rachel, and Lia is put under him in her stead: because Christ, that he might take the Church to himself, first joined the Synagogue to himself.

VERS. 20-35.-- « Jacob therefore served seven years for Rachel. » The servitude of Jacob of seven years for two wives signifies the time of the present life, [0156B] which is rolled round in seven days; in which the Lord took the form of a servant, obedient to the Father unto death. Jacob served, because « the Son of man came not to be ministered unto, but to minister [n: (Matth. XX)]: » He fed sheep, and Christ said [n: (Joan. X)]: « I am the good shepherd. » He took to himself for a reward the party-coloured flock: Christ gathered to himself the variety of divers nations. He set three rods, the barks being stripped off, in the troughs of the waters, that by the beholding of them the sheep might be multiplied: and our Lord set before the faithful people, in the water of baptism, the names of the three persons, of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost, that he who shall have looked upon this with a full heart may be made a sheep of God.

« And they seemed to him but a few days, » etc. It is to be asked: how was this said, since even a short time [0156C] seems long to them that love? It was said therefore because of the labour of the servitude, which love made easy.

[n: (AUG., lib. XXII contra Faustum.)] Four wives are cast up against Jacob as a huge crime. But since it was then the custom, it was not a crime; now however it is a crime, because it is not the custom, etc., *as far as* save the burning of carnal concupiscence.

[n: (ISID., in Gen., tom. V.)] There are therefore two free wives of Jacob, for both are daughters of the remission of sins, etc., *as far as* and he saw in the beginning the Word with God, and wills to bring forth, and cannot, because « who shall declare his generation? » [n: (Act. VIII.)]

[n: (ISID.)] Two free wives of Jacob, because two lives are preached to us in the body of Jesus Christ, etc., *as far as* But this cannot be in the land of the dying, which [0156D] is signified by these words.

VERS. 26.-- « It is not the custom in our place, » etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] She is called the elder who is the earlier in time, etc., *as far as* to one thing therefore we must tend, but for this many things must be borne.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] Accordingly the life which suits the study of contemplation, etc., *as far as* whence below in XXX: « Rachel was jealous of her sister. »

[n: (ID., ibid.)] Accordingly, because a pure understanding of that divine substance, etc., *as far as* not because a divine power.

## CHAPTER XXX.

VERS. 14-31.-- « And Ruben going out, » etc. [n: (AUG., lib. XXII contra Faustum.)] Why Scripture did not keep silence [0157A] about the thing done, etc., *as far as* and whatsoever is piously and wisely said of this may be wholesomely, or in part, received without the phantasm of carnal thought.

« And Ruben going out, etc. » [n: (AUG.)] *Historically.* But I know that some suppose of this kind, that, taken as food, it brings forth fruitfulness in barren women, and that Rachel therefore insisted that she should receive it, which I do not think, nor would she thus have conceived then. But now, since, after two other births of Lia from that night, the Lord has given her offspring, there is nothing wherefore we should suspect of the mandrake any such thing as we have experienced in no woman. For when I had myself seen these apples, and rejoiced that, because of this place of the sacred reading, they had fallen to me (for it is a rare thing), I searched out their nature as far as I could, [0157B] not by any knowledge further removed from the common sense, which teaches the powers of roots and the virtues of herbs, but so far as sight, smell and taste report, and I found the thing fair, sweet-smelling, but insipid in savour. Why then the woman desired it so greatly I know not, unless perhaps for the rarity of the fruit and the pleasantness of the odour.

VERS. 32-43.-- « Go round all thy flocks, » etc. [n: (HIER., in Quaest. Hebr.)] The sense is much confused among the Seventy, etc., *as far as* the end is near.

[n: (ALC.)] Why Jacob is not accused of fraud in this place, this without doubt he did as a prophet, nor is it to be believed that he did it without spiritual revelation.
