## CHAPTER XXXI.

[0157C]

VERS. 1-11.-- « But after he had heard, » etc. . . . . « He changed the wages, » etc. [n: (HIERON. in Quaest. Hebr.)] In place of what we have set down, « he changed my wages ten times, » the Seventy have set « ten years: » whereas the Hebrew word *monim* sounds a number rather than years; and from what follows this sense is proved, that at every bringing forth he changed the condition upon Laban. If he saw a party-coloured beast born, he would say: I will that in time to come party-coloured ones be born to me; then Jacob did not put the rods in the channels, that the young might be born of one colour: which being done, Laban would say that those cattle ought to fall to his own share; thus up to ten times the condition was changed: and whatsoever Laban had proposed should be born to himself, [0157D] was turned into the contrary colour. But now in six years ten times of bringing forth would seem incredible; but read Virgil, in whom it is said: « Twice pregnant are the flocks. » But the nature of Italian sheep and of those of Mesopotamia is said to be the same.

VERS. 12.-- « For I have seen all things, » etc. From this we learn that Jacob did it by no evil guile, but by divine disposition, or because all things were being done in a figure. Whence he deserved to be strengthened by an angelic vision.

VERS. 13-31.-- « Now therefore arise. » [n: (GREG., Moral. XXX, 16.)] After a long servitude the Lord commanded Jacob to return into his own country: then, his father-in-law not knowing it, he hastened away with his wives and his company. But Laban followed after him in mount [0158A] Galaad, seeking the idols with fury, and found them not. But Laban in this place bears the type of the devil. For he is interpreted *a whitening.* But the devil, though he be dark, « transformeth himself into an angel of light. » Him Jacob served, that is, the Jewish people on the part of the reprobate, of whom Christ was born according to the flesh.

VERS. 32-39.--. . . . « He knew not that Rachel had stolen the idols. » [n: (HIERON.)] Where we read « idols, » in the Hebrew *theraphim* is written; which Aquila interprets « figures, » or « images ». But I say this for this cause, that we may know what *theraphim* sounds in the book of Judges.

« Over against mount Galaad, » etc. Not that at that time it was called mount Galaad, but by anticipation [0158B] it is called by that name, by which it was afterwards named.

« Who, taking his brethren, pursued, » etc. [n: (GREG.)] *Historically.* By Laban the world can be expressed: which follows Jacob with fury, because it strives to oppress the elect, who are the members of Christ. Jacob took away the daughter of the world or of the devil, when Christ joined to himself the Church out of the Gentiles, to whom it is said through the prophet in psalm XLIV: « Forget thy people, » etc. In the idols avarice is signified, which is the service of idols. Laban, coming to Jacob, found not the idol, because, the riches of the world being shown to our Redeemer, the devil found no traces of concupiscence. But what Jacob had not, Rachel by sitting covered, who is interpreted *a sheep,* and signifies [0158C] the Church. But to sit is to seek the humility of penance. Whence in psalm CXXVI: « Rise up after you have sat. » Rachel by sitting covers the idols, because the Church, following Christ, has covered over the vice of earthly concupiscence by the humility of penance. Whence in psalm XIII: « Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered. » We therefore Rachel signifies, who by sitting press down the idols, if by doing penance we condemn the faults of avarice; which uncleanness of avarice hinders not those that run manfully, but those that walk effeminately are loosed through the blandishments of the world, which is signified by these words: « It hath befallen me according to the custom of women. » For as it were she made known that she suffered the things of a woman.

[0158D] « That I might kiss my sons and daughters. » After the manner of Scripture he calls the sons and daughters of Jacob his own, which calls kinsmen sons or brethren; whence in what follows: « And Jacob said to his brethren: Bring stones. » In the Gospel also the sons of Mary, the maternal aunt of the Lord, are called his brethren, in Matthew XII.

« I feared lest thou wouldst violently take away thy daughters, but as to thy accusing me of theft, » etc. As if to say, therefore I did not make it known to thee, lest by the bitterness of persecution thou shouldst overcome the weak souls, newly snatched from thee.

VERS. 40-45.-- « Day and night I was parched with heat, and with frost. Sleep fled from mine eyes. » The effect of heat and of cold is one.

VERS. 46-52.-- « Bring stones, » etc. (HIERON. [0159A] *de Loc. Hebr.* ) A heap, in the Hebrew tongue, etc., *as far as* he had changed by the speech in which he dwelt.

[n: (ISID.)] *Mystically.* Among the faithful, both Jews and Gentiles, the witness is a stone standing out into the likeness of Christ; and the heap of stones, which is the multitude of believers.

VERS. 53, 54.-- « Jacob swore, » etc. That is, by the fear wherewith he feared God, whom he commended above, saying, « And the fear, » etc.

VERS. 55.-- « But Laban in the night, etc. » That Laban and Jacob went over the river can signify two peoples coming to the Church through baptism. But Jacob going further forward, Laban returned, because, while the sons of light pass on in the advancement of virtues after baptism, the reprobate, from the dignity they have received, [0159B] return after Satan into apostasy.

## CHAPTER XXXII.

VERS. 2-6.-- « These are the camps of God. » Where *castra* is set, in the Hebrew is *mahanaim* ; that we may know, if at any time the interpreted word is set in another place, what place it signifies. And beautifully, being about to go to his enemy brother, he is received by the choirs of the angels accompanying him.

VERS. 7-9.-- « Jacob feared, » etc. [n: (AUG., Quaest. in Gen. )] It can be asked how he had faith in the promises of God, in that he said this. But it could come to pass that Esau should overthrow his camps, and that then, after that affliction, he should be present, and should fulfil what he promised. And we are admonished by this example that, although we believe in God, [0159C] we should nevertheless do the things that are to be done for the safeguard of our salvation. The words also of Jacob are to be considered, when he says: « God of my father Abraham, » etc., because in these words both human infirmity and the faith of piety appear.

VERS. 10.-- « I passed over the Jordan, » etc. Through baptism the wall from the Gentiles was joined to that which is from the Jews: yet to the primitive Church the fear of the Jews is not lacking, which Esau signifies.

VERS. 11-19.-- « Deliver me from the hand, » etc. So he prays that he may temper hope by fear, and comfort fear by hope, signifying that we should take counsel by reason for our life and for our household, and nevertheless call upon the help of God.

VERS. 20-27.-- « I will appease him, » etc. These things are interposed [0159D] by the writer. For it belongs not to a prudent man to uncover his art, and so to make himself suspected by those with whom he deals.

*[n: (GREG. hom. 14, in Evang., tom. II.)] Mystically.* When Jacob returns to his own parents, etc., *as far as* that with inward sweetness we may lay hold of God.

« And behold a man, » etc. [n: (ISID. in Gen., tom. V.)] In this chiefly the image of the Lord's sacrament is figured, etc., *as far as* that it may no more be able to beget.

VERS. 28, 29.-- « By no means, he says, shall thy name be called Jacob, but, » etc. [n: (HIERON. in Q. Hebr. )] Josephus in the first book of the Antiquities, etc., *as far as* we are led rather by the authority of Scripture, and of the angel, or of God, [0160A] who himself called him Israel, than by that of any secular eloquence.

VERS. 30-32.-- « And Jacob called, » etc. That also which follows, « and he blessed him, and Jacob called the name of that place Phanuel, saying, » etc. In the Hebrew it is called « Phanuel, » that we may know it to be the same place which in the other volumes of Scripture, according to the Hebrews, is read *Phanuel.*

[n: (GREG., lib. IV Moral., cap. 6.)] It is asked: since Truth says: « No man shall see my face; » And: « No man hath seen God at any time, » how does Jacob testify: « I saw the Lord face to face? » [var: Vulg. Gen. 32:30 *Vidi Deum facie ad faciem*, "I have seen God face to face"] For human minds, the inward eye being purged, etc., *as far as* whence I Cor. XIII: « Then shall I know even as I am known. »
