## CHAPTER XXXIII.

[0160B]

VERS. 1-9.-- « And Jacob lifting up his eyes. . . . and he divided the children of Lia and of Rachel, » etc. Aquila, *he divided them in half,* that he might make one company of the handmaids with their little ones, and another of the free women with their sons; and might send the handmaids first, the free women second: he himself running out before them all to adore his brother.

VERS. 10-13.-- « For so I have seen, » etc. [n: (AUG., Q. in Gen. tom. III.)] Whether they are the words of a fearful and troubled mind, etc., *as far as* those who are wont to hear and understand Greek.

VERS. 14.-- « And I will follow, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] It is asked whether he promised his brother a lie. For this, as Scripture relates, he did not do; but he went on by that [0160C] road which led to his own people. Or did he perhaps promise with a truthful mind, but afterwards, on further thought, choose otherwise?

« Socoth. » [n: (HIERON.)] Where we have *tabernacles,* in the Hebrew is read *Socoth* [ed: Migne prints a Hebrew word here, and the full stop that closes the sentence; our transcription of the Latin has lost both.] There is moreover to this day a city beyond the Jordan of this name, in the region of Scythopolis, of which we have written in the book of Places.

VERS. 18-20. « And he passed over into Salem, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] A question arises how the city Sichen is called Salem, since Jerusalem, in which Melchisedech reigned, was formerly called Salem. Either then both cities are of one name, which we find of many places of Judaea, so that the same is the name of a city and of a place in one tribe and another. Or we shall say that this Salem, which [0160D] is now named instead of Sichem, is interpreted *finished* and *perfect:* and that one which was afterwards called Jerusalem, *peaceful.* This word sounds each of the two, the accent being a little turned aside. The Hebrews hand down that the thigh of the limping Jacob was healed there: and that therefore the city obtained the name of *the healed* and *the perfected.*

## CHAPTER XXXIV.

VERS. 1.-- « And Dina went out, » etc. [n: (GREG. in Pastor., c. 30.)] Dina goes out to see the women of a foreign country, when the mind, neglecting its own pursuits and busying itself with the doings of others, strays outside its proper order. Her Sichem, the prince of the land, oppresses, because the devil corrupts her [0161A] whom he has found among outward cares. And his soul was knit to her, because he looks upon her as united to himself through iniquity. And because the mind, coming to itself from its fault, is stricken and strives to weep for what it has committed: the corrupter calls up before its eyes an empty hope and security, that he may take away the profit of its sorrow. Whence it is added: « And flattering he soothed her that was sad. » [var: Vulg. Gen. 34:3 *tristemque delinivit blanditiis*, "and he soothed her, being sad, with flatteries"] For now he speaks of the deeds of others as heavier; now that what has been done is nothing; now that God is merciful, and he promises time for penance: so that, while the mind is led on deceived by these things, penance may be put off, that then it may receive no good things, it whom evils now make sad: and that it may then be the more fully swallowed up in punishments, it which now rejoices in delights.

VERS. 2-32.-- « And when Sichem had seen her. . . . [0161B] and slept with her, » etc. [n: (AUG. Quaest. in Gen., tom. III.)] And he set his mind upon the soul of Dina the daughter of Jacob, and loved the virgin, and spoke according to the mind of the virgin. How is she called a virgin, if he had already slept with her? unless perhaps "virgin" is a name of age according to the Hebrew manner of speech, or rather, by recapitulation, that is recorded afterwards which was done before. For first he could set his mind upon her soul, and love the virgin, and speak according to the mind of the virgin, and then sleep with her.

« Peaceable. » Where the Seventy have rendered *peaceable,* Aquila has translated *finished, and perfect* for which is read in the Hebrew: *Selemim.* From which it appears that to be true which we said above of Salem.

[0161C] « They consented, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] A little before, Jacob, speaking with Esau, signifies that his sons are infants, etc., *as far as* but his sons are named in this deed as though they were the chief men and the authors of it.

« Having taken up their swords, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] So ought the pastors of the Church to avenge the sin of the flesh or of the soul with the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God: ready to avenge all disobedience.

« We are few, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] He says this because very many wars could arise, not because he had far fewer than would suffice for the storming of the city.

« Ought they to have abused our sister as a harlot, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] The zeal of the sons of Jacob for the avenging of their sister morally admonishes the pastors of the faithful to have care of the souls committed to them, [0161D] lest they be violently overcome by a bodily offence, or by spiritual fornication. « And let them be ready to avenge all disobedience, » and let them strike the ravisher with the sword of excommunication, lest he escape unpunished.
