## CHAPTER XXXV.

VERS. 4-5.-- « They gave him therefore, » etc. [n: (HIER. Q. in Gen., tom. III.)] Plainly it is by this order and by these advances that we must go up into Bethel, that is, into the house of God, which is the Church of Christ. The first thing is to renounce our former errors, and to profess the faith of the one true God, which is to put away strange gods: then to be baptized, which is to be cleansed; and thereafter to walk in newness [0162A] of life, which is to change our garments. And the earrings signify the trappings of false doctrine, glittering in speech, but empty of the sense of truth.

« And the earrings, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] It is asked: of what sort were the earrings, which were ornaments not pertaining to idolatry? but it must be understood that they were phylacteries of strange gods.

VERS. 6-9.-- « Jacob therefore came to Suzam, » [var: Vulg. Gen. 35:6 *Luzam*] etc. [n: (AUG.)] It is to be noted that three names of this city are recorded, etc., *as far as* for many causes names are added or changed.

[n: (HIERON.)] Behold, it is most manifestly proved that Bethel was formerly called, not Ulam, as was said above, but Luzan, that is, *an almond.*

VERS. 10.-- « Thou shalt no more be called, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] [0162B] It is asked: since it was once said, « Thou shalt no more be called Jacob, » etc., why is he afterwards read to have been called Jacob? But this name belongs to that promise in which God is seen as he was not seen before by the fathers, where the old name shall not be: because nothing of oldness shall remain here in the body itself, and the vision of God shall be the highest reward.

[n: (HIERON.)] « Thou shalt no more be called Jacob, » etc. For not yet is the name laid upon him by the angel, but it is foretold that it is to be laid upon him by God. What therefore is promised there as to come, is here taught to be fulfilled.

VERS. 11-13.-- « Increase and multiply. » *Morally.* This promise belongs to the spiritual seed of Jacob, as was already said before of Abraham. [0162C] For all who supplant vices, and put off the old man with his deeds, and bend the eyes of the mind upon God, rightly belong to Jacob, whose faith and deeds they imitate. « And kings shall come forth from his loins, » [var: Vulg. Gen. 35:11 *reges de lumbis tuis egredientur*, "from thy loins"] that is, the saints who according to the will of God truly rule themselves and their subjects: of whose spiritual seed, that is, by the imitation of faith, they are made fruitful in good works.

VERS. 14, 15.-- « But he set up a stone pillar in the place where he had spoken, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] That was done again in this place which had been done before; or that is recorded here which had been done before. But whichever of these it be, Jacob poured his offering upon the stone, not to the stone. Not therefore as idolaters are wont to set altars before a stone, and [0162D] to pour offering to the stone as to God.

VERS. 16, 17.-- « Ephrata. » [n: (HIERON.)] Ephrata and Bethlehem are the name of one city, under a like interpretation. For it is interpreted *fruitful* and *the house of bread,* because of the bread which came down from heaven.

VERS. 18-20.-- « And when she was going forth, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] What does it mean that Rachel, when she was in labour, called the same child Bennoni, *the son of my sorrow?* What but that Paul was to come of that tribe, who should afflict the sons of the Church in the time of his persecution? Otherwise by Benjamin the earthly Jerusalem is figured, which is in the tribe of Benjamin: whose people afflict their mother with grievous sorrow by shedding the blood of the prophets, and by crying out for the death [0163A] of Christ: « His blood be upon us, and upon our children [n: (Matth. XXVII)].

« And it came to pass, when she was giving up her soul. » [var: Vulg. Gen. 35:18 *Egrediente autem anima prae dolore*, "and her soul departing for pain"] [n: (HIERON.)] Since indeed she was dying, « she called his name, the son of my sorrow: but his father called his name Benjamin. » [var: Vulg. Gen. 35:18 *vocavit nomen filii sui Benoni, id est, Filius doloris mei : pater vero appellavit eum Benjamin*] In the Hebrew a likeness of the name sounds, *for son of my sorrow,* which name the dying mother laid upon him, is said *Bennoni. But son of the right hand,* that is, of strength, which Jacob changed it into, is said *Benjamin.* Whence they err who think that Benjamin is interpreted *son of days*: for the right hand is called *iamin,* and ends in *n;* but a day is called *iamim,* and is terminated in *m.*

VERS. 21.-- « Going forth thence, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] The Hebrews will have this place to be, etc., *as far as* or [0163B] what is truer, that by a certain prophecy the mystery to come was even then being shown.

VERS. 22-26.-- « Ruben went, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] *Allegorically.* This crime would not be written, were not the coming perversity of the people declared: although in him there was an outrage, in the Scriptures there is a prophecy of things to come, because by Ruben the firstborn people of Israel is figured: who defiled the couch of the concubine, that is, stained the law of the Old Testament by transgressing. And that the concubine signified the Old Testament, Paul shows, saying, Gal. IV: « Abraham had two sons, one of a bondwoman, and the other of a free woman, » [var: Vulg. Gal. 4:22 *unum de ancilla, et unum de libera*, "one of a bondwoman, and one of a free woman"] etc.

« And there were, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] It is asked how this is true, etc., *as far as* than that it be taken by synecdoche [0163C].

VERS. 27-29.-- « The city Arbee. » [n: (HIERON.)] For *Arbee* the Seventy have *a plain,* although Hebron is on a mountain. But the same city was anciently called Mambre, from the friend of Abraham.

« The city Arbee, » etc. Of the angels or of the holy souls, to whom he was gathered, no anxiety of temptations remaining, nor peril of sins.

## CHAPTER XXXVI.

VERS. 6-19.-- « He went into another country. » (STRAB) It may be asked: why is Esau said to have gone away into Seir after his father's death, and to have withdrawn from his brother, when already, when Jacob came, he is said to have dwelt in Seir? [0163D] But it could be that, while Jacob was fleeing into Mesopotamia, Esau, from grief at the blessing snatched away, withdrew from his parent, and dwelt in Seir the twenty years in which Jacob was away. Reconciled to him on his return, and having come back again from Seir, he dwelt with his brother so long as their father lived: and when he was dead, he withdrew from his brother, because they were rich, and could not dwell together.

VERS. 20, 21.-- « These are the sons of Seir, » etc. [n: (HIERON. in Q. Hebr. tom. III.)] After he had enumerated the sons of Esau, he goes further back to those who before Esau were chief men in the land of Edom, of the race of the Horrites, who are interpreted *free men.* In Deuteronomy it is written more plainly how the sons of Esau came, and, the Horrites being slain, inherited their land.

[0164A] « The sons of Seir, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] That is, the *free men* who, as is read in Paralipomenon, first dwelt in mount Seir; and when they were driven out. The Idumeans dwelt in their stead.

VERS. 22, 23.-- « And there was a sister, » etc. [n: (HIER.)] This is Thamna, of whom it was said above: And Thamna was the concubine of Eliphaz, the firstborn of Esau, etc. Therefore he makes mention of the Horrites, because the firstborn of the sons of Esau took a concubine of their daughters. And because Theman is named, and Cenez, and Amalec, and the rest, let us know that afterwards names were laid upon the regions and nations of the Idumeans from these.

VERS. 24-28.-- « This is Ana. » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Some say simply that he found hot waters in the desert, which we call thermae.

[0164B] « This is Ana, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Various things are disputed among the Hebrews concerning this chapter, etc., *as far as* that mules might be born against nature.

VERS. 29, 30.-- « These are the dukes. » He speaks of those going before, who are of Seir; not of these whom he begins to number, who are of Esau.

VERS. 31-43.-- « And the kings. » It is to be known that he does not record all the kings who were before the children of Ismael had kings, of whom the first was Saul, but only all those who reigned before Moses died. « And the kings. » He records according to the time in which the writer was living, but when Seir, who begot these men, dwelt there, Esau not yet having come into that land, it was called from him the land of Edom.

[n: (AUG., Q. in Gen., t. III.)] It is no wonder that to those numbering [0164C] from Abraham through Esau the father of the nation of Edom, etc., *as far as* for in that order where more are numbered, they died sooner than here where fewer are.

« Husan. » [n: (HIER.)] Whom some suspect to be Job, as is added at the end of his own volume; but the Hebrews affirm that he was begotten of the stock of Nachor, as has already been said above.
