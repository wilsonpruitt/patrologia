## CHAPTER XXXVII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- « Joseph, when he was sixteen years old, » etc., [n: (AUG. in Q. super Genesin. )] *Historically.* ⟦s2⟧ How can the death of Isaac, etc., *as far as* yet in Isaac's lifetime Joseph began to be seventeen years old.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And he was with the sons of Bala, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ Joseph, one of [0164D] the twelve sons of Jacob, beloved of his father beyond the rest, signified Christ, whom, born according to the flesh, God the Father preferred to the rest of the brethren begotten of the stock of Abraham. ⟦s3⟧ Whence Jacob loved him, « because he had begotten him in his old age. » For when the world was growing old, the Son of God was born of the Virgin, as it were a son of old age.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And he was with, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ In the primitive Church he did not cast off those who clave to him through the preaching of false apostles, whom the handmaids of Jacob signify, whom also he would not forbid, when they wrought miracles in his name.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- « And he made him a coat of divers colours. » [n: (HIER.)] For *a parti-coloured tunic* Aquila has rendered [0165A] ἀστραγάλον, that is, *reaching to the ankles,* Symmachus *sleeved,* either because it came down as far as the ankles and was marked out with a wonderful variety by the craftsman's hands, or because it had sleeves: for the ancients used rather colobia, that is, garments without sleeves.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (GREG., hom. XXV in Evang.)] Because Joseph is described as having persevered just among his brethren to the end of his life, he alone is said to have had a coat reaching to the ankles. ⟦s2⟧ What is a coat reaching to the ankles, but a completed action? ⟦s3⟧ For as it were a coat stretched down covered the ankle of the body, when a good action rules us before the eyes of God to the bounds of life. ⟦s4⟧ Whence Moses commanded the tail of the victim to be offered, that we may complete with a persevering end the good work which we begin.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4-9.-- « And his brethren seeing, » [0165B] etc. ⟦s2⟧ The brethren of Christ according to the flesh are the Jews, of whose kindred he took flesh. ⟦s3⟧ But because they saw that the grace of virtues was wrought in him, which bore witness to the Father's especial love, they envied him, and could not speak anything peaceably to him, and again and again attempted snares, and blasphemously said that the Master of the household cast out demons by Beelzebub the prince of demons.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID.)] This was spiritually fulfilled in Christ, etc., *as far as* in the figure of the stars.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10, 11.-- « Shall I, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ It may be asked according to the letter when he was adored by his father, who is designated by the sun; and by his mother, who is designated by the moon. ⟦s3⟧ For neither is Jacob read to have adored him when he went down into Egypt: and his mother was already dead: [0165C] but let us have recourse to the allegory, which is fulfilled in Christ.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12-22.-- « And when his brethren, » etc., « in feeding the flocks, » *as far as* « Bring me word, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] Jacob sends his son to take care of his brethren; and God the Father his only-begotten Son, to visit the human race sick with sins. ⟦s2⟧ Whence Galatians, chapter four: « God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh. » [var: Vulg. Rom. 8:3 *Deus Filium suum mittens in similitudinem carnis peccati*; the chapter cited, Gal. 4:4, has *Misit Deus Filium suum, factum ex muliere*] Joseph was sent to see whether all things were prosperous with the sheep: and Christ says, in Matthew the fifteenth: « I came not but to the sheep that had perished of the house of Israel. » [var: Vulg. Matt. 15:24 *Non sum missus nisi ad oves, quae perierunt domus Israel*, "I was not sent but to the sheep that are perished"]

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « In Dothaim. » *A falling away.* ⟦s2⟧ For they were in a great falling away, who were thinking of fratricide. ⟦s3⟧ « Ruben. » Foreseeing this, the prophetic word, which [0165D] Ruben signifies (who is interpreted *the vision of the son* ), deterred them by prophetic threatenings, that they might keep themselves innocent of the blood of Christ, their brother according to the flesh.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23, 24.-- « They stripped him, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] *Mystically.* ⟦s2⟧ The Jews too stripped Christ by death of his bodily coat, and that of divers colours, that is, adorned with the variety of all the virtues. ⟦s3⟧ And they sprinkled the coat with the blood of a kid: because, accusing him by false testimonies, they brought him into the odium of sin. ⟦s4⟧ Joseph is sent into the cistern, that is, into the pit: and Christ, stripped of human flesh, went down to hell.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25-27.-- « They saw travellers, Ismaelites. » [n: (AUG.)] It is asked why Scripture [names them] Ismaelites, etc., [0166A] *as far as* who say that Agar and Cetura were one and the same?

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28-33.-- « Drawing him out. » [n: (ISID.)] *Allegorically.* ⟦s2⟧ Joseph, lifted out of the cistern, is sold to Ismaelites that had been sold: and Christ, returned from hell, is bought by all nations in the traffic of faith. ⟦s3⟧ The one is sold off for thirty pieces of silver by the counsel of Judas, and this one is sold for the same number by the counsel of Judas Iscariot.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « They sold. » [n: (HIERON.)] The Hebrew verity has « pieces of silver. » for neither ought the Lord to have been sold for a baser metal than Joseph.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- « And having rent his garments, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] Jacob, bewailing the losses of his posterity, mourned as a father for a son that was lost, and as a prophet for the destruction of the Jews [n: (Matth. XXVI)]. ⟦s2⟧ He rent his garment: [0166B] which in the Lord's passion the chief of the priests did [n: (Luc. XXIII)]. ⟦s3⟧ But the veil of the temple also was rent, that he might prophesy his people stripped bare, and show the kingdom divided.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35, 36.-- « I will go down to my son. » etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is asked: is this to be understood of the wicked only, or do the good also go down into hell? ⟦s3⟧ If the wicked only, how does this man say that he wishes to go down mourning to his son? ⟦s4⟧ For it is not credible that he is in the pains of hell. ⟦s5⟧ Or are they the words of a troubled and grieving man, magnifying his own evils here also? or is it credible that near hell there were certain hidden places, and less penal, before the Lord's passion, to which the saints also went down? because none could enter paradise, until Christ should take away the flaming [0166C] sword.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (HIERON.)] But the Madianites sold Joseph in Egypt to Putiphar the eunuch, Pharaoh's chief cook. ⟦s2⟧ For the most part Scripture puts ἀρχιμαγείρους, that is, chiefs of the cooks, for masters of an army; for μάγειρος in Greek is *a cook,* and there is said μαγειρειύεν, which is *to kill:* because cooks kill the beasts that are to be cooked. ⟦s3⟧ Joseph therefore was sold to the chief of the butchers, not to Petefre, as it is written in the Latin, but to Putiphar the eunuch. ⟦s4⟧ Whereupon it is asked: how is he afterwards said to have a wife? ⟦s5⟧ The Hebrews hand down that Joseph was bought by this man for a foul service because of his exceeding beauty, and that afterwards, his manhood being withered by his master, he was chosen according to the custom of the hierophants, that is, of those who speak sacred things, into the pontificate of Heliopolis: [0166D] and that Aseneth was this man's daughter, whom Joseph afterwards took to wife.
