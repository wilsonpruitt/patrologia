## CHAPTER XXXVIII.

VERS. 1, 2.-- « At the same time, » etc. [n: (AUG., Q. in Gen.)] It is asked: how could these things have come to pass? For if, after Joseph came into Egypt, etc., *as far as* that he fell into love of her whom he took to wife, Joseph not yet being sold.

« Judas, » etc. He, like the other patriarchs, signifies Christ, who went down from the baptism [sic: *ab baptismum*] of John. For Hiras is interpreted *seeing God:* Odollamite, *testimony in water,* because John, seeing Christ coming to the baptism, said: « Behold [0167A] the Lamb of God, » etc. [n: (Joan. I.)] To this testimony the Synagogue clave to Christ, which Sue, the daughter of the Chanaanite, signifies, because to her was to be given the land *of humble profession,* which is the interpretation of Chanaan. This Synagogue therefore first clave to Christ, when Peter and Andrew, and the rest of John's disciples, were made disciples of Christ. In what order these things were fulfilled the history following signifies, relating how Judas went in to Thamar, who signifies the same as Sue.

VERS. 3, 4.-- « And she conceived, » etc. The Synagogue bore princes, who were set over her as husbands: of whom some were harmful, whom Her signifies, who is interpreted *made of skin* (for a skin signifies death), because the first parents, clothed with skins, were made over to death; others useless, whom Onan signifies, [0167B] who is interpreted *their sorrow,* namely to those whom they profit not.

VERS. 5-12.-- « She bore a third also, » etc. [n: (ISID. in Gen., tom. V.)] The third son, who is not joined to Thamar, etc., *as far as* and therefore as an Odollamite he bore testimony in water.

« Judas, » etc. From Juda came forth the royal tribe, which long ruled the Jewish people, which Thamar signifies, who is interpreted *bitterness,* because she showed herself bitter to Christ in his passion.

[n: (ISID.)] The Jewish people, to whom kings from the tribe of Juda, etc., *as far as* yet God slew them both, because he takes away the kingdom from such men.

VERS. 13.-- « To shear the sheep. » [n: (STRAB.)] *Allegorically.* The Lord came to shear the sheep, that is, [0167C] to unburden them of their sins, of which it is said: « Thy teeth as flocks of the shorn. » Thamar changes her habit: for she is also interpreted *changing*; she changes both habit and name, that of the Synagogue there may be a Church. But the name of bitterness remains: not that in which she ministered gall to the Lord, but that in which Peter wept bitterly. For Judas too is called *Confession*, let bitterness therefore be mingled with Confession, that true penance may be prefigured. This penance is made fruitful, the Church being established among all nations. For it behoved Christ to suffer, and to rise again, and that penance and remission of sins should be preached in his name among all nations. The habit of a harlot too is the confession of sins: for Thamar signifies the Church called out of the Gentiles; sitting in this habit at the gate of Enaiim [0167D], which is interpreted *a fountain:* because she was running like a hart to the fountains of waters, to come to the seed of Abraham. She is made fruitful, he not knowing it, according to that of Psalm XVII. « The people which I knew not hath served me. » She received in secret the ring, the necklace, and the staff, because she is sealed by vocation, adorned by justification, exalted by glorification. « And whom he predestinated, them he also called; and whom he called, them he also justified [n: (Rom. VIII)]; » but in secret, where the conception of holy fruitfulness is made.

VERS. 14-19.-- « Who, having laid aside the garments of her widowhood, » etc. It is to be noted also that in the times of the patriarchs there were fixed garments of widows, and not such as those of married women.

[0168A] « She sat in the crossway. » [n: (HIERON.)] « And she sat at the gate of Enaiim, which is in the way to Thamna. » The Hebrew word Enaiim is rendered *eyes.* It is not therefore the name of a place, and the sense is, « She sat in the crossway, » or at the meeting of the roads, where a traveller ought to look the more carefully where he is going.

VERS. 20-24.-- « And Judas sent. » The promised kid is sent as to a harlot, that is, the reproach of sin, by the Odollamite, as by one rebuking, « brood of vipers, » etc. But the reproach of sin did not find her whom the bitterness of confession had changed. In this deed of Juda it is not the sincerity of justice that is discerned, but the faithfulness of his promise. For if he had kept justice by keeping chastity, he would not have gone in, as he supposed, to a harlot; but what he promised, [0168B] he faithfully sent by the shepherd.

VERS. 25.-- « And when she was led forth, etc. » [n: (ISID.)] But afterwards, by the public tokens of the ring, the necklace and the staff, she overcame the Jews who judged rashly, whom Judas already signified; who to this day also say that this is not the people of Christ, nor the seed of Abraham, but, when the proofs of our vocation, justification and glorification are brought forward, they are confounded, and will confess that we rather than they have been justified. For the Church, which is accused by the Jews as an adulteress against the law, declares that she has the pledges: but she shows the staff, the sign of the passion, and the necklace, the lawful things of the law, and the ring, the pledge of immortality.

VERS. 26.--Who, when they were acknowledged, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] « And Judas acknowledged, etc. In the Hebrew it is found: « she is justified from me; » not that she was just, but that [0168C] in comparison with him she had done less ill, not in wandering baseness, but in seeking children.

VERS. 27, 28.-- « And when she was in labour, » etc. That the first, who is called Zara, put forth his hand, and the midwife bound the scarlet, and, he himself drawing his hand back, the later, who is called Phares, stretched out his hand, and in being born went before him, signified that Israel would stretch out his hand in the work of the law, and would draw it back defiled with the blood of the prophets and of Christ himself: but that the people of the Gentiles would afterwards break forth, so that « the first should be last, and the last first. »

VERS. 29, 30.-- « Why is the wall divided, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] For *a wall* Aquila and Symmachus have translated *a division,* which in Hebrew is said *Phares.* From this therefore, that he divided the membrane of the afterbirth, he took the name *of division* [0168D]. Whence also the Pharisees, who had separated themselves from the people as though they were just, were called Pharisees, that is, *divided.*

Zara is interpreted *the rising.* Because he appeared first, or because very many just men were born of him, as is said in Paralipomenon.

## CHAPTER XXXIX.

VERS. 1, 6.-- « Joseph therefore was brought into Egypt, » etc. [n: (AUG. in Gen.)] Scripture returns to the order from which it had departed, that it may relate those things which were done above. « And he was, » etc. [n: (GREG., lib. VI Moral., c. 11 seq.)] Many, puffed up with human wisdom, while they look upon the divine judgments as contrary to their own desires, etc., *as far as* and lightens his burdens, which he bears willingly.

[0169A]VERS. 7-11.-- « And after many days his mistress cast, » etc. [n: (GREG. lib. XXX Moral., cap. 19.)] He who is lifted up by the successes of the world, etc., *as far as* for because pleasure is born of prosperity, let it be struck down by the consideration of the same.

« His mistress cast her eyes, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] This is a figure of the Synagogue, etc., *as far as* for whence he had gone down as though to be punished, thence he set others free.

VERS. 12-23.-- « Who, leaving the cloak in her hand, » etc. So do the allurements of the flesh tempt us, and shake the garment of flesh: but the chaste man had rather break every bond than subject himself to baseness.

Who leaving, » etc. [n: (GREG.)] *Allegorically.* Because, while the Synagogue, believing Christ a mere man, wished to bind him in an adulterous embrace, he cast the covering [0169B] of the letter before her eyes, and offered himself to be seen by the Gentiles, that the power of his godhead might be known. Whence to this day, while Moses is read, a veil is upon the eyes of their heart, because the adulteress kept the cloak, and lost naked him whom she was wrongfully holding.
