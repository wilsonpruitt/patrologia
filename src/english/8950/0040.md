## CHAPTER XL.

VERS. 1-8.-- « These things being thus done, it happened that two eunuchs sinned, » etc. [n: (ISID. in Gen., tom. V.)] Joseph found two eunuchs in the prison, etc., *as far as* he is hanged upon the wood of transgression [sic: *trangressionis*].

« The butler of the king of Egypt, and the baker, against their lord, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Where we have put « the chief of the vintners » in the Hebrew is found *masqueh* [ed: Migne prints a Hebrew word here; our transcription of the Latin has lost it, leaving the comma that follows standing free.], that [0169C] word which we read long since in the name of Abraham's servant, whom we may in the common way call *a butler,* and to this day among the barbarians it is of the greatest dignity to have handed the cup to the king.

VERS. 9-15.-- « I saw before me a vine, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] « And behold a vine in my sight, and on the vine three stems, and it budding forth three stems. » The Hebrew tongue signifies three shoots, three branches, or layers, which are called by them *Sarigim* [ed: Migne prints a Hebrew word here; our transcription of the Latin has lost it, leaving the full stop that follows standing free.].

VERS. 16-19.-- « Three baskets. » [n: (AUG. Quaest. in Gen.)] Where in the Latin copies is written « three baskets of meal, » etc., *as far as* and of every kind of baker's work, namely in the same uppermost basket.

VERS. 20-23.-- « The birthday of Pharaoh. » Pharaoh [0169D] and Herod kept the day of their birth, because they thought that from the hour of their birth they had been kings.

## CHAPTER XLI.

VERS. 1.-- « After two years Pharaoh saw a dream. He thought that he stood, » etc. [n: (AUG. Q. in Gen., tom. III.)] As the servant of Abraham said, etc., *as far as* where earthly animals dwell.

VERS. 2-8.-- « Out of which came up seven oxen, » etc. [n: (HIERON. Q. Hebr.)] Behold, out of the river came up seven kine fair to look upon, etc., *as far as* they joined the Greek χ.

VERS. 9-16.-- « Then at length the chief of the butlers remembering, » etc. Because the Gentile people at last [0170A] by the setting forth of things past brought itself into the hope of things to come.

VERS. 17-32.-- « I thought that I stood, » etc. The seven years, which are shown by the seven full ears, or by the seven fat kine, signify the spiritual gifts by which the plenty of faith overflows with a bountiful loving-kindness. But the seven barren and lean ones, the famine of truth and of justice in the last time.

VERS. 33.-- « Now therefore, » etc. We see this fulfilled in the time of grace: because, apostolic doctrine abounding and being ministered by the sevenfold Spirit, bishops and teachers were ordained by divine disposition through the several cities, who out of the abundance of doctrine divinely bestowed upon them wrote the holy books, wherewith, when famine of the word abounds and teachers fail, we may relieve our need [0170B].

VERS. 34-37.-- « And the fifth part of the fruits, » etc. It is to be noted that the Egyptians gather the fifth part of the corn into the granaries of the king: because the sons of darkness, given over to the five senses, care not for heavenly things: but the true Israelites give tithes, because they labour to restore the heavenly country, of which a tenth part fell.

[n: (ISID.)] Joseph gathered through seven years all the abundance of the corn: that is, storing the grain of faith in the granaries of the saints, through the seven gifts, as it were through seven years, so that when the seven years of want shall have begun, that is, when the iniquity of the seven capital crimes shall have come under Antichrist, when there shall be a famine of faith and of salvation, then the saints may have a plentiful [0170C] harvest of justice, lest their faith fail through want of the word.

VERS. 38-40.-- « Who is full of the Spirit of God, » etc. [n: (AUG. Q. in Gen.)] For the third time, unless I am mistaken, the Holy Spirit is intimated to us in this book. First where it was said above [n: (cap. I)]: « The Spirit of God moved over the waters, » secondly where God says above [n: (cap. VI)]: « My Spirit shall not remain in these men, » [var: Vulg. Gen. 6:3 *Non permanebit spiritus meus in homine in aeternum, quia caro est*, "in man for ever, for he is flesh"] etc. Thirdly when Pharaoh says that the Spirit of God is in Joseph: yet we do not yet read of the Holy Spirit.

VERS. 41.-- « And Pharaoh said again, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] *Allegorically.* Joseph, who bore the type of Christ, earned a chariot, and a herald made proclamation before him, and Pharaoh set him over the whole land of Egypt: and our Lord, after he was sold away [0170D] by Juda, as Joseph by his brethren, and rose from the prison of hell, ascended the chariot of the heavenly kingdom, of which it is said in psalm LXVII: « The chariot of God is manifold by ten thousands; » and he received from the Father the power of preaching and of judging. Whence Phil. II: « He gave him a name which is above every name, » [var: Vulg. Phil. 2:9 *donavit illi nomen, quod est super omne nomen*, "he bestowed on him a name"] etc. He received also a ring, that is, the pontificate of faith, wherewith the souls of believers are sealed with the sign of salvation, and upon our foreheads and hearts the image of the eternal king is stamped by the sign of the cross. He is clothed with a robe of fine linen, that is, with holy flesh more shining than fine linen, and with the robe of immortality. He received also a golden collar, that is, a good understanding. A herald goes before him, that is, John the Baptist, who says in Matth. III: « Prepare the way [0171A] of the Lord. » He shall have another herald also, because he shall come at the trumpet of the angel.

VERS. 41, 42.-- « Behold, I have set thee over the whole land of Egypt, » etc. Joseph, brought out of the prison, is set over Egypt; and Christ rising again received « a name which is above every name, that in the name of Jesus every knee should bow [n: (Philipp. II)]. »

VERS. 43, 44.-- « A herald proclaiming, » etc. [n: (HIER. in Quaest. Hebr. )] « and a herald cried before him, and he set him over all the land of Egypt. » For which Aquila has translated: « And he cried in his sight, To the bending of the knee. » Symmachus, etc., *as far as* that in respect of the prudence of all he was a father, but in respect of age a most tender youth [0171B] and a boy.

VERS. 45-49.-- « And he called, » etc. [n: (HIER., ibid. )] And Pharaoh called the name of Joseph, etc., *as far as* that that suspicion of the Hebrews concerning him, of which we spoke before, may be proved true.

« And he gave him a wife, » etc. [n: (AUG., ubi supra. )] It is wont to be asked of which Putiphar, otherwise Petefre, as very many have it, and corruptly, as was said above, etc., *as far as* it is not perilous to faith, nor contrary to the truth of the Scriptures.

VERS. 50-58.-- « And there were born to Joseph two sons, » etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Observe, because of the question which is to be put afterwards concerning the sons of Joseph, that before the time of the famine, in which Jacob entered into Egypt, Joseph had only two sons, Manasses and [0171C] Ephraim.

VERS. 56, 57.-- « And Joseph opened all the granaries, » etc. *Allegorically.* Joseph saves Egypt from the want of corn, and Christ frees the world from the famine of the word. Christ opened his granaries into the whole earth, and by the dispensing of his corn subdued all things. Had the brethren not sold Joseph, Egypt had failed: had the Jews not crucified Christ, the world had perished. Joseph is interpreted *increase* or *enlargement:* but in that Joseph none had the enlargement save Egypt alone; in ours, the whole world. He dispensed wheat; ours the word of God. « Their sound hath gone forth into all the earth [n: (Psal. XVIII)]. »
