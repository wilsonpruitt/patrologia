## CHAPTER XLVII.

VERS. 9, 10.-- « The days of my pilgrimage, » etc. [n: (AUG. Q. in Gen., tom. III.)] What is it that Jacob says [0175B] to Pharao, etc., *as far as* they do not abide in the house for ever.

VERS. 11.-- « But Joseph to his father and his brethren . . . Ramesse. » It must be asked whether the land of Ramesses is itself Gessen: for this they had sought, and Pharao had commanded that it be given.

[n: (ISID.)] *Allegorically.* Joseph handed over to his father and his brethren the best land, Gessen, providing them with victuals, because famine had oppressed the land: so also the Lord, to his kindred, that is, to the patriarchs and prophets, of whom he is according to the flesh, or to all the saints, of whom he says in the Gospel, Matth. II: « These are my brethren, who do the will of my Father, »[var: Vulg. Mt 12:50 *Quicumque enim fecerit voluntatem Patris mei, qui in caelis est, ipse meus frater, et soror, et mater est*, "Whosoever shall do the will of my Father, who is in heaven, he is my brother, and sister, and mother."] to these he gives the best land, of which it is said, psal. XXVI: « I believe to see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living. »

[0175C]VERS. 12-15.-- « Providing victuals, to every one, » etc. Joseph measured out wheat to his own father, and yet his father did not adore him, neither when he saw him nor when he received the wheat: how then do we think the dream fulfilled, unless it contain the prophecy of a greater thing.

VERS. 16-19.-- « Bring your cattle, and I will give you, » etc. It is asked, since Joseph was gathering the corn whereby men might live, whence the cattle were living, when so great a famine had grown strong, and the brethren of Joseph said to Pharao: « There is no pasture for the cattle of thy servants, »[var: Vulg. Gn 47:4 *non est herba gregibus servorum tuorum*, "there is no grass for the flocks of thy servants."] etc. If therefore in that famine the pastures had failed in the land of Chanaan, why had they not failed in Egypt, since the same famine was everywhere? Or could it be, as is reported by those who know the places, that in the many marshes of Egypt pastures might well not be wanting, even [0175D] when there was a famine of the corn which is wont to come forth by the inundation of the Nile: those marshes are said to bring forth pasture more fruitfully when the water of the Nile rises less.

VERS. 20, 21.-- « He bought therefore, » etc. Joseph is not accused of injustice or of iniquity, since his fidelity is even commended from this, that he accepted the favour of no person, but weighed out with an even balance according to the buyer's price.

But mystically it signifies that all who are in the spiritual Egypt and court her riches are slaves. For no Egyptian is free, who is carnal, and sold under sin: for Pharao made them subject; whence perhaps it is said, Exod. XX: « I am the Lord thy God, [0176A] who brought thee out of Egypt, out of the house of bondage. »[var: Vulg. Ex 20:2 *qui eduxi te de terra Aegypti*, "who brought thee out of the land of Egypt."] Whence Rom. VI: « Let not sin reign in your mortal body. » etc. And in the same place: « You are the servants of him whom you have obeyed, whether of sin unto death, or of obedience unto justice. »[var: Vulg. Rom 6:16 *servi estis ejus, cui obeditis, sive peccati ad mortem*, "you are the servants of him whom you obey, whether of sin unto death."] But the Egyptians were brought by violence into slavery, the Hebrews indeed by violence into service. The Egyptians, however, sold their own selves, and it is no fault of the steward where what is deserved is repaid according to merits. This Paul also did, when he delivers to Satan that man who was unworthy of the number or the fellowship of the saints [n: (I Cor. V)]. Let no one therefore say that Paul acted too harshly, who cast the man out of the Church, that being expelled he might learn not to blaspheme: for the will of man is iniquitous, when, loving earthly things, it despises the heavenly, and for covetousness subjects itself to the devil. But God's [0176B] permission is just, when he leaves to his own choice, that he may fall, the man who, despising better things, has chosen worse. « He bought therefore, » etc. Allegorically the purchase of Joseph, who bought the land of Egypt with corn, signifies that Christ redeems the world by doctrine and by the price of his blood. And as to what he says: « Take seed, and sow the fields that you may have corn, » etc., the seed is the word of God, the fields are the bodies which, ploughed up by the evangelical share and sown with spiritual seed, bring forth the fruit of the virtues. The fifth part, which was given to the king, is the tribute of the five senses, which is to be paid to Christ the king. The four remaining are left to the possessors for seed and for food, because the use of the active life is granted to every one, that he may sow the works of the virtues and reap the fruit of justice unto life eternal, which is bestowed on us by Christ. Whence [0176C] it is said to Joseph: « Our salvation is in thy hand, » because the salvation of the world is in the power of Christ.

VERS. 22-28.-- « Except the land of the priests, » etc. This signifies that the land of the Church, in which are the true priests, is free from worldly tribute, which is fed unceasingly by the divine word, and shall suffer no loss of the spiritual bread.

VERS. 29, 30.-- « And when he saw that the day of his death drew near, » etc. [n: (AUG., Quaest. in Gen., tom. III.)] He binds his son by that oath, etc., *as far as* who appeared in baptism about the thirtieth year.

VERS. 31.-- « He swearing. » [n: (AUG., ubi supra. )] That which the Latin codices have, « He adored upon the head of his rod, » some have more correctly: « He adored [0176D] upon the head of his own rod, » or « on the head of his own rod, » or « on the top, *or* upon the top of his own rod. » And therefore it is rightly asked what it is that has been said, etc., *as far as* where the figure of a thing to come was prefigured.

[n: (HIERON.)] « And Israel adored toward the top of his rod. » In this place also certain men vainly pretend that Jacob adored the top of Joseph's sceptre: namely, that honouring his son he adored his power, whereas in the Hebrew it is read far otherwise: « And Israel adored at the head of the bed; » because after his son had sworn to him, being secure of his request, he adored God, turned toward the head of the bed. For a holy man, and one given over to God, weighed down by old age, kept his little bed [0177A] so placed that the very posture of one lying down was, without any difficulty, ready for prayer.
