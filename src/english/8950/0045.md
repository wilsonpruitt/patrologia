## CHAPTER XLIX.

[n: (ALBIN.)] In the blessings of Jacob the history is to be held and the allegory searched out: the history concerning the division of the land of promise, the allegory concerning Christ and the Church, which is to be revealed in the last times; yet first the foundations of the history must be laid.

VERS. 3, 4.-- « Ruben my firstborn, » etc. [n: (HIERON., Quaest. in Gen.)] « Ruben my firstborn [0178B], thou art my strength, » and the beginning of my sons,[var: Vulg. Gn 49:3 *et principium doloris mei*, "and the beginning of my sorrow."] etc., *as far as* thou hast lost the order of the firstborn.

VERS. 5.-- « Simeon and Levi, » etc. [n: (HIERON., ubi supra. )] LXX: « Simeon and Levi brethren have accomplished the iniquity of their own devising, » etc., *as far as* « let not my soul go into their counsel. »

VERS. 8.-- « Juda, they shall praise thee, » etc. [n: (HIERON., ubi supra. )] Because Juda is interpreted *confession* or *praise*, etc., *as far as* in other words the Church is shown according to the same sense, of which it is said, Matth. V: « A city cannot be hid, set upon a mountain. »

VERS. 9.-- « The whelp of a lion is Juda: to the prey, [0178C] my son, thou art gone up: resting thou hast couched as a lion, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] Or, the lion's whelp rested in sleep, because not of necessity but of power. Whence: « I have power to lay down my life, and no man taketh it from me, but I lay it down [n: (Joan. X)]. »[var: Vulg. Jo 10:18 *Nemo tollit eam a me : sed ego pono eam a meipso, et potestatem habeo ponendi eam*, "No man taketh it away from me: but I lay it down of myself, and I have power to lay it down."] But as to what he adds, « and as a lion's whelp, »[var: Vulg. Gn 49:9 *et quasi leaena*, "and as a lioness."] for thence he died whence he was born. Naturalists say that the whelp of a lion, when it has been born, sleeps three days and three nights. Then the place of its lair, shaken by the roaring of its father, rouses the sleeper: Christ also, lying three nights in the lair of the sepulchre, fulfilled the sleep of death. Well therefore did Christ rest as a lion, who overcame not only the bitterness of death but its dominion also, and as a lion's whelp, because on the third day he rose again. Whence it is added.

[0178D] « Who shall rouse him up? » etc. None, understand, save he himself who says, Joan. II: « Loose this temple, and in three days' space I will raise it again. »

VERS. 10.-- « The sceptre shall not be taken away from Juda, » etc. For so long were there kings of the seed of Juda among the Jews, until Christ should be born of the Virgin. For the first foreign king in the nation of the Jews was Herod, when Christ was already born. He therefore who denies that Christ has come, let him show a king of the tribe of Juda in the nation of the Jews.

VERS. 11.-- « Tying his colt to the vineyard, » etc. The Gentiles whom he joined to himself. Whence Ezech. XVIII: « As I live, saith the Lord, unless I put on these men as a garment. »[var: Vulg. Is 49:18, whose words these render, reads *Vivo ego, dicit Dominus, quia omnibus his velut ornamento vestieris*, "as I live, saith the Lord, thou shalt be clothed with all these as with an ornament"; Ez 18, the chapter Migne cites, has *si erit ultra vobis parabola haec in proverbium in Israel*.] For Christ cleansed us in the blood [0179A] of the grape when he hung like a cluster on the wood of the cross, and out of his side there went forth water for washing, and blood for redemption.

VERS. 12.-- « Fairer the eyes, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] Because their doctrine surpasses the austerity of the old law, and the evangelical precepts are clearer than the commandments of the law.

« With milk, » that is, the doctrine of the law, which fed the carnal or little people with milk: but « whiter, » the doctors of the Church, who eat and distribute the strong and solid food of the word. Whence Hebr. V: « Strong meat is for the perfect, » who are well called « whiter than milk, » because they are white, pure and free from every stain.

VERS. 13.-- « Zabulon on the shore, » etc. That he may show a refuge [0179B] to them that believe and a haven of faith to them that are in peril. Made firm against all the ends of the world, she awaits the shipwreck of the Jews and the storms of the heretics: who are carried about with every wind of doctrine, thence also were some of the apostles taken: there the Lord often taught. And he reaches as far as Sidon, that is, he comes as far as the Gentiles. Whence Isa. IX: « The land of Zabulon, and the land of Nephthali, the way of the sea beyond the Jordan, » etc.[var: Vulg. Is 9:1 *Primo tempore alleviata est terra Zabulon et terra Nephthali : et novissimo aggravata est via maris trans Jordanem Galilaeae gentium*, "at the first time the land of Zabulon and the land of Nephthali was lightened: and at the last the way of the sea beyond the Jordan of the Galilee of the Gentiles was heavily loaded"; the wording Migne prints is that of Mt 4:15.] Sidon, *hunting:* and out of those places were taken the hunters, of whom it is said Jer. XVI: « I will send many hunters, and they shall hunt you in every mountain. »[var: Vulg. Jr 16:16 *mittam eis multos venatores, et venabuntur eos de omni monte*, "I will send them many hunters, and they shall hunt them out of every mountain" — *eos*, "them," not *vos*, "you."]

VERS. 14, 15.-- « Issachar. » [n: (HIERON. Quaest. Hebr. )] He desired the good, resting in the midst of the clergy, etc., *as far as* and therefore let all the tribes serve him, [0179C] as bearing gifts to a master.

« Couching between the borders, » etc. To couch is to rest with the end of the world awaited, and to seek nothing of worldly things, but to long for the last things. The strong ass saw rest, and the best land: when the simple Gentile world raised itself up to the strength of the work, because it tends toward the country of life.

VERS. 16-18.-- « Dan shall judge his people, » etc. [n: (ISID. in Gen., tom. V.)] Others think that by these words Antichrist is foretold as to come of the tribe of Dan, etc., *as far as* and after the benefits of a feigned sweetness he plies the horns of power.

« Dan shall judge his people as one of the sceptres of Israel. Let Dan be a serpent in the way, a basilisk in [0179D] the path, biting the hoofs of the horse, and let his rider fall backward. I will look for thy Saviour, O Lord. »[var: Vulg. Gn 49:16-18 *sicut et alia tribus in Israel … cerastes in semita … ut cadat ascensor ejus retro. Salutare tuum expectabo, Domine*, "as another tribe in Israel … a horned serpent in the path … that his rider may fall backward. I will look for thy salvation, O Lord."] [n: (HIERON., ubi supra. )] Samson, the judge of Israel, was of the tribe of Dan, etc., *as far as* and he himself shall be the expectation of the nations.

VERS. 19, 20.-- « Gad. » [n: (HIERON.)] A robber shall rob before him,[var: Vulg. Gn 49:19 *Gad, accinctus praeliabatur ante eum*, "Gad, being girded, fought before him."] etc., *as far as* and, his enemies overcome, he fought bravely.

« Gad girded shall fight before him. » [n: (ISID.)] Christ shall fight before and behind against Antichrist: before, by the hidden coming of his humility; after, by the manifested glory of his majesty. Whence Moses, Deut. XXXIII: « Blessed in breadth is Gad, he hath rested as a lion: and he took the arm and the crown, and he saw his principality. » For Christ rested in the sepulchre, and humbled the crown [0180A] and pride of the lofty, and to him was given the principality, and honour, and a kingdom.

VERS. 21.-- « Nephthali. » [n: (HIERON.)] *Allegorically.* A shoot let loose, giving beauty to the nation,[var: Vulg. Gn 49:21 *Nephthali, cervus emissus, et dans eloquia pulchritudinis*, "Nephthali, a hart let loose, and giving words of beauty."] etc., *as far as* because there most of all he taught, as is written in the Gospel too.

« Nephthali a hart let loose, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] For the apostles and preachers, leaping forth swiftly like harts, overleap the hindrances of the world, and meditating on lofty things, give words of beauty, that is, they preach the doctrine of the Saviour.

VERS. 22-26.-- « Joseph a growing son, » etc. [n: (HIERON., ubi supra. )] « A son growing above a fountain, »[var: Vulg. Gn 49:22 *filius accrescens et decorus aspectu*, "a growing son and comely to behold."] etc., *as far as* ruling also over the ten tribes of Israel.

[0180B]VERS. 27. « Benjamin a ravening wolf, » etc. [n: (HIERON., ibid. )] « In the morning he shall eat the prey, and at evening he shall divide the food. »[var: Vulg. Gn 49:27 *mane comedat praedam, et vespere dividet spolia*, "in the morning let him eat the prey, and in the evening he shall divide the spoil."] Although the prophecy is most manifestly of Paul, in that in his youth he persecuted the Church, and in his old age was a preacher of the Gospel, etc., *as far as* concerning the priests who, serving the altar, should live by the altar [n: (I Cor. IX)].

« Benjamin a ravening wolf, » etc. [n: (ISID.)] A certain one of the doctors referred this to Jerusalem, etc., *as far as* then shall he divide the food, understanding that the letter is to be divided from the spirit, because « the letter killeth, but the spirit quickeneth [n: (II Cor. III)]. »

VERS. 28-31.-- « All these in the tribes, » etc. It is asked: why did Jacob appoint all whom he begot of free women and of handmaids [0180C] sons and heirs with equal honour? because, as Christ bestows heavenly rewards with like honour and glory upon all who are joined to his body through faith. « For there is no distinction there whether one be Jew or Greek, Barbarian or Scythian, bond or free: because through all things and in all things » is Christ,[var: Vulg. Col 3:11 *ubi non est gentilis et Judaeus, circumcisio et praeputium, Barbarus et Scytha, servus et liber : sed omnia, et in omnibus Christus*, "where there is neither Gentile nor Jew, circumcision nor uncircumcision, Barbarian nor Scythian, bond nor free: but Christ is all, and in all."] who put on the form of a servant, and served both for the free man and for the servant, that he might bestow an equal gift upon all. For whosoever earns the faith of the Lord is darkened by no stains of carnal birth. Whence Joel. II: « I will pour forth of my spirit upon all flesh. »[var: Vulg. Joel 2:28 *effundam spiritum meum super omnem carnem*, "I will pour out my spirit upon all flesh"; the wording Migne prints is that of Act 2:17.]

VERS. 32.-- « And being gathered, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] It must be asked: of what people is this said? For concerning the fathers there is no question. For it is known that he was gathered to Abraham and Isaac, but a people is a multitude. [0180D] Those, however, being so few, could not be called a people. It is to be understood therefore that this is said of the angels also, to whose fellowship the saints, passing out of the world, are gathered. Whence Hebr. XII: « You are come to mount Sion, and to the heavenly Jerusalem, and to the company of angels, »[var: Vulg. Hbr 12:22 *accessistis ad Sion montem, et civitatem Dei viventis, Jerusalem caelestem, et multorum millium angelorum frequentiam*, "you are come to mount Sion, and to the city of the living God, the heavenly Jerusalem, and to the company of many thousands of angels."] etc.
