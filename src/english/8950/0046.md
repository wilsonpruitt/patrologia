## CHAPTER L.

VERS. 1-3.-- « Which when Joseph saw, he fell . . . . forty days passed. » [n: (STRAB.)] Which number befits penance and humility. Whence in the law and in the Gospel it was observed by the prophets and by the Lord himself.

[n: (AUG.)] The forty days of burial which the Scripture records perhaps signify the penance whereby sins are buried. For not in vain were forty days [0181A] of fastings appointed, on which Moses, and Elias, and the Lord himself fasted, and the Church calls the chief observance of fastings Lent. Whence also concerning the Ninivites it is written in Jonas: « Yet forty days, and Ninive shall be destroyed [n: (Jon. III)], that through so many days, fitted, that is, to the humbling of the penitent, they may be understood to have wept for their own sins in fastings, and to have obtained mercy. Nor does this number befit the mourning of penitents only. For forty days after the resurrection the Lord was with his disciples, going in and going out, eating and drinking, and those were days of gladness. Nor are the seventy Interpreters to be believed to have erred, who said: « Three days, and Ninive shall be destroyed: » for, endowed with a greater authority than the office of interpreters is, by the prophetic spirit (by which also they are affirmed to have agreed with one mouth in their interpretations, which was a great miracle) they set down [0181B] « three days, » although they were not ignorant that « forty days » was read in the Hebrew; that in the glorification of Christ sins might be understood to be dissolved and abolished, « because he was delivered up for our offences, and rose again for our justification. » But the glorification of the Lord is known in his resurrection and ascension. Whence here, although he gave one and the same Spirit: first after he rose again, secondly after he ascended; and because after three days he rose again, but after forty days ascended: the one of these, which was done later, the Hebrews signify by the number of forty days; but the other, of the three days, which [0181C] belongs to the same matter, the Seventy record, not by the servitude of interpretation, but by the authority of prophecy. Let us not therefore say that one of these is false, and let us not wrangle for some interpreters against others; since they who interpret out of the Hebrew prove that what they interpret is written; and the authority of the Seventy, which is commended by so great a miracle divinely wrought, is confirmed by so great an antiquity in the churches.

« Seventy days. » [n: (STRAB.)] The heathen bewailed their dead nine days. Whence also the ninth day was called the novendial, when they were buried. The worshippers of God celebrated the obsequies for seven days. Whence Eccli. XXII: « The mourning for the dead is seven days: but the mourning of a fool is all the time of his life. »[var: Vulg. Sir 22:13 *Luctus mortui septem dies : fatui autem et impii omnes dies vitae illorum*, "the mourning for the dead is seven days: but for the fool and the ungodly, all the days of their life."] For the number seven signifies rest. Whence the seventh day is called the sabbath, [0181D] that is, *rest*. They celebrate therefore the seventh day of the dead, wishing that they may rest with the Lord from the labours of the present life. Hence therefore it is said: « Egypt wept for him seventy days, » which is the sevenfold number multiplied by ten. For seven times ten are seventy. It may also be said that, as the third day of a dead man is kept by the faithful, that the soul, which is of a threefold nature, may rest; so the seventh day is celebrated, that the soul, which sinned at the enticement of the flesh, may be reconciled to the body. In the number seven also there are three and [0182A] four; the number three belongs to the soul, which is irascible, and concupiscible, and rational; the number four to the body, which consists of four elements.

VERS. 4-21.-- « And when the time of the mourning was expired, » etc. [n: (AUG.)] As to what Joseph enjoined upon the great men of Egypt, that they should say to Pharao in his name: « My father made me swear, saying: In the monument which I made for myself in the land of Chanaan, there thou shalt bury me; »[var: Vulg. Gn 50:5 *eo quod pater meus adjuraverit me dicens : En morior : in sepulchro meo, quod fodi mihi in terra Chanaan, sepelies me*, "for that my father made me swear, saying: Behold I die: thou shalt bury me in my sepulchre which I dug for myself in the land of Chanaan" — *fodi*, "dug," which is the very word the gloss below reasons from, where Migne's lemma prints *feci*, "made."] it is asked how this may be true, since these words of his father, when he gave charge concerning his burial, are not read. But the words are to be referred to the sense, as other things above were likewise repeated. For words must serve a will that is to be uttered and brought to knowledge. But that Jacob dug himself a sepulchre is nowhere read above, yet assuredly, unless he had dug it, it would not now be said.

[0182B]VERS. 10.-- « And they came to the threshing-floor of Atad, » etc. This place is beyond the Jordan, and is distant from Hebron seventy thousand paces. They passed by, therefore, the place in which they were to bury him, and afterwards, the Jordan being crossed, returned to the place of the double cave. Whence it may be asked why they did this. If any one says that they feared wars, as the children of Israel were led about through the desert for forty years, until, the Jordan being passed, they came to the land of promise. But this was done for the sake of a greater signification. For in the place which is called the threshing-floor of Arad, afterwards the multitude of the children of Israel, murmuring against the Lord, was consumed by divine fire. With so great a mourning, therefore, not so much the death of Jacob as the coming ruin of the people was bewailed.

VERS. 22, 23.-- « And he lived a hundred and ten years. » [0182C] [n: (AUG., in Gen., tom. III.)] Since the Scripture says that Joseph, while he lived, saw these sons of his sons or grandsons of his sons, how does it join them to the sixty-five souls with whom it says that Jacob entered Egypt, etc., *as far as* which was propagated while he lived, through whom it came about that he entered in.

VERS. 24.-- « Carry away my bones, » etc. [n: (STRAB.)] Joseph, like his father, desired with his whole mind the land of promise, and detested the sojourn in Egypt. He instructs us each morally also, that so long as we are in this world we should always desire the land of promise, that is, of the living, and should wish to rest there: which is well done, if we imitate in our manners the number of Joseph's years. For he died when a hundred and ten years of his life were completed: let us also study [0182D] to come, through the keeping of the decalogue, to the eternal blessedness which the number one hundred signifies.

VERS. 25.-- « Embalmed with spices. » [n: (STRAB.)] Happy is the soul which, seasoned with the spices of the virtues while it dwells in the little coffer of the body, is kept by daily profiting for the everlasting life. For if it shall have been guarded in faith, hope and charity, it will deserve to come, by the grace of Christ, to the sight of the divine contemplation, whose adoption is perfect gladness; whence psal. XV: « Thou shalt fill me with joy with thy countenance. »
