## CHAPTER II.

[0693D]VERS. 1.-- *These are,* etc. Of Judea, that is, not of Babylonia. For to her belonged also those brought forth in Babylonia of the stock of the captives. For with their whole soul they sighed after Judea and Jerusalem, whose figure their leader Zorobabel bears, who by his very name shows himself born in Babylon, but by his intention and his work a citizen of Jerusalem. In a higher sense the children of the Church are children of the heavenly country, not only they who are already imbued with the sacraments of the Church, but also they who are without, that is, straying for a time among the ungodly, foreordained to life by divine election before the ages, and to be consecrated by the ministries of divine grace.

*And they returned into Jerusalem. [n: (BEDA in Esdram, tom. II.)]* For when, delivered from the power of Satan, etc., [0694A] *as far as* but the vision of peace and confession is begun in the present and perfected in the time to come.

*Every man into his city. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Therefore Scripture watchfully distinguished from what generation of the captives they returned, loosed, into their own country, etc., *as far as* for which he may reward them with a sure recompense.

VERS. 2.-- *The number,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] By the people of Israel he means the tribe of Juda and of Benjamin, etc., *as far as* is to be understood together with the priests and the Levites who had had their lot among them.

VERS. 58.-- *The Nathinaei, the sons,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] The Nathinaei, who are described in order after the priests, the Levites, the singers and the porters, were then the very men who are now called subdeacons in the Church, obeying the offices of the Levites and receiving the oblations in the temple [0694B] from the people.

*All the Nathinaei. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* In this place too, according to the letter, the grace of God is shown, by which even in the Old Testament Gentiles were received unto salvation, since, when the catalogue of those who were known truly to belong to the people of God had been set out, there were joined to them men of whom it was not known whether they were of Israel or of the proselytes: and their number too is set out among the children of Israel: for although, by the long separation of their fathers from the temple or from the people of God, they had not been able to show in what way they belonged to him: yet, having gone up out of the captivity when general leave was given, they were received as companions; for the rest of the Israelites rejoiced to have them, for the love of the common faith and religion, as brethren and acquaintances, although in the origin of the flesh they knew them [0694C] less.

But allegorically, among the penitents who, delivered from the captivity of the vices, go up to build the house of the Lord either in themselves or in others, there are some who, bound by very many and graver sins, seem estranged from God and from every act of piety and of chastity, so that nothing at all of the goodness and religion which they had received from their teachers seems to have remained in them, nor can they show whether they were begotten of Israel, that is, of holy Church, because by sinning such men have been made as though they had never belonged to the Holy Spirit, yet by repenting they are corrected and converted, so that their number is written in the heavens among the true Israelites in whom there is no guile.

VERS. 61.-- *And of the children of the priests. [n: (BED., ibid.)]* [0694D] The children of the transmigration act toward the priests with the same caution as toward the people. For they took care that it should be plainly apparent who truly belonged to the people of Israel or to the priestly race, and who were either suspect or begotten of a certain stock of proselytes, that is, of strangers. The suspected priests therefore they removed from the office of the altar, until their origin should more certainly become clear, but they kept them nonetheless in the fellowship of those passing over, in a peace of one mind with themselves.

But mystically the sons of the priests going up out of the Babylonian captivity seek the writing of their genealogy, and not finding it are cast out of the priesthood, when the ministers of the altar fall into such enormities or abominable doctrines that, even though by repenting they return to the salvation [0695A] of the soul, they are yet not worthy to be restored to the sacred grade. For though among the faithful they look for eternal life, yet they do not find, among the perfect priests, the writing of their grade, which they cannot claim again.

*All the multitude as one man,* etc. [n: (BED., ibid.)] [var: Vulg. Esr 2:64 *Omnis multitudo quasi unus*, without *vir*] These signify those who by the advance of an amended life busy themselves to overcome the vices and to climb the summit of the virtues, yet are not yet sufficient of themselves to provide the way of a regular life, but are restrained and directed by the industry of those who have gone before them in Christ.

*And among them. [n: (BED., ibid.)]* Mystically, in the temple or the people of God there are singers, etc., *as far as* and as it were by the sweetness of their voice they help those who build the temple.

VERS. 66.-- *Their mules. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* A mule is begotten of [0695B] an ass and a mare, a *mala* of a horse and a she-ass. Among the men who had gone up out of the captivity, the beasts also by which they were helped are described. And their number, like that of the men, is set down, because there are many in the Church either slower of understanding or even carnal, who nevertheless obey their spiritual masters devoutly, and bow the back of the mind to bear the burdens of brotherly need, and, snatched with the rest of the elect out of the confusion of the devil's captivity, press toward the walls of the city above, whose number also is kept whole in the memory of God; whence: *My imperfect being have thy eyes seen [n: (Psal. CXXXVIII)]*. And elsewhere: *Men and beasts thou wilt save for the Lord [n: (Ibid. XXXV)]*.

VERS. 68.-- *They offered freely into the house of God for,* [0695C] etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] It is a token of great devotion, etc., *as far as* by how much they observe that these use not even lawful things.

VERS. 69.-- *According to their strength.* For this is the measure of perfection, that each labour for the Lord according to his power, and confirm the state of the Church first in himself, then in his neighbours. They who live in this fashion are rightly called princes of the fathers, because by living more highly they take the lead, in perfection of life and of teaching, over those who in the Church are called fathers through their zeal of solicitude.

*Solids of gold. [n: (BED., ibid.)]* Gold and silver and priestly vestments, etc., *as far as* For by how much the more grievously they remember that they have erred, by so much the more ardently do they persist in good works.

VERS. 70.-- *The priests therefore dwelt, and the [0695D] Levites.* A Levite taken captive by the Chaldeans receives back his city, when a deacon, by the Lord's gift, makes whole again the virtue of perfection which, seduced by the devil, he had lost. But one born in Babylon enters his city by coming into Judea, when, regenerated in Christ and well instructed in the Church, he keeps according to rule the diaconate once received. For as the former was set free by the grace of Christ as it were from the Chaldean servitude, so this latter, by the performance of the good works in which the predecessors of the same grade lived, they ascend as it were to the walls of their own virtue or city.

*And all Israel.* [n: (BEDA ubi supra. )] By all Israel he means not the ten tribes only, which were once called Israel in distinction from the two which [0696A] were called Juda, etc., *as far as* concerning whom the Jews promise themselves that, when their Christ reigns, they shall return into Judea with the rest of the men of their nation, and shall reign in the whole world.

Allegorically the priests, the Levites, the singers, the porters and the Nathinaei, and all Israel dwell in their own cities, when the ministers of the altar and the teachers and the Christian people serve God faithfully in their own grades, whether they who, that same grade having at some time been defiled or lost by vices, have been recalled through penance, or they who, lately coming out of the kingdom of the devil to the Church, have gained for themselves a good grade. For the Levites or the children of Israel entered their own cities, not only they who had lost them when taken captive, but also they who, born of captives, had learned that these belonged to them by paternal succession. [0696B] But whosoever were so taken captive that, never returning, they left their lands to others to be possessed, they signify those who so depart from the Church by sinning that they never come to themselves again, and leave to others the rewards promised to themselves; whence: *Be mindful whence thou art fallen, and do penance, and do the first works: but if not, I come to thee, and will move thy candlestick,* etc. [n: (Apoc. II)]; and elsewhere: *Hold fast that which thou hast, that no man take thy crown [n: (Ibid. III)]*.
