## CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 1.-- *Now the enemies of Juda and Benjamin heard,* etc. [n: (BED. in Esdr. )] By the enemies of Juda and Benjamin he means the Samaritans, etc., *as far as* who at the right hand of the judge are about [0700C] to receive blessing and the eternal kingdom. They say therefore:

VERS. 2.-- *Let us build for you. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* [var: Vulg. Esr 4:2 *Aedificemus vobiscum*, "let us build with you"] When heretics aspire that the authority of preaching be granted them among catholics, etc., *as far as* in the person of an open enemy he assailed them most fiercely.

*It is not for us.* [var: Vulg. Esr 4:3 *Non est vobis et nobis*, "it is not for you and for us"] It is not for heretics to build the Church from which they are strangers, but only for those who, cleaving to Christ the true king and priest, have deserved to be called princes of the fathers, and for the pious care which they have toward those who desire to see Christ.

VERS. 3.-- *And it came to pass. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* As often as heretics, not only in cities but also in provinces, by teaching perversely or even by raging, hindered the doctrine of the true confession, etc., *as far as* there might be abundance of wise architects for the rebuilding, after the captivity, [0700D] of that same house.

VERS. 4.-- *Of the people of Juda.* By the people of Juda, that is, the trusting and glorifying people, he means him who seeks to build with whole mind for the Lord, that is, to turn the minds and the mouths of all whom he can to the doing of his will and to the seeking of his glory.

VERS. 5.-- *They hired. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The distinction of the words is to be noted, etc., *as far as* when they gain the protections of earthly princes against the Church, and how much this harms the faith became known, clearer than the light, in the time of the Arian perfidy.

*But in the reign of Assuerus. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* This Artaxerxes, etc., *as far as* but he permitted him, as one of lesser power, to handle and to decide these things.

[0701A] [n: (BEDA., ibid.)] Artaxerxes, who is persuaded and, being persuaded, commanded that Jerusalem should not be built, etc., *as far as* the enemies who held the neighbouring places being again overcome, they took possession of them.

VERS. 14.-- *The injuries.* Because they cannot endure those who strive to assail the kingdom of the devil, they fear that wars be stirred up in the Church, lest their heresies and deceits be warred down together with the doctrine of the Gentiles, under the leadership of him who saith: *I came not to send peace, but the sword [n: (Matth. XVI)]*. And again: *I came to send fire upon the earth [n: (Luc. XII)]*, etc., the sword, that is, of the word of God with which he may lay his adversaries low, the fire of charity with which, kindling the hearts of his own, he may burn up all the arms and shields of the contrary sect.

*Because the city.* [n: (BEDA ubi supra. )] The enemies themselves again confess concerning the city of the Lord, etc., *as far as* that is, [0701B] they refer the beginning and the perfection of honours to God from whom they had received them, by living well and by giving thanks.

VERS. 16-- *Beyond the river.* The Euphrates, that is, within which Syria lies, which signifies baptism, because it is one of the four rivers of paradise which water the whole world, as the four evangelists, inspired from one fountain of life, that is, from Christ, preach with harmonious voice the laver of salvation to all nations, or because Euphrates is interpreted *fruitful*: which agrees with the sacrament by which the world, washed and sanctified, brings forth to God the fruit of souls, thirtyfold, sixtyfold and hundredfold.

VERS. 17.-- *The king sent word to Reum Beelteem.* [n: (BEDA ubi supra. )] The king of the Persians asks, etc., *as far as* who persecute the Church and forbid it to be built.

[0701C] *The inhabitants of Samaria.* The enemies of the men of Jerusalem dwell in Samaria, which is interpreted a keeping, not that they keep the precepts of faith and of truth, who obstinately fight against the vision of true peace, but because they boast that the keeping of the virtues abides with them, while they fight by heresy against the walls of peace.

VERS. 23.-- *And so the copy. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)]* The order of the history is plain, etc., *as far as* and the king had only forbidden the city to be built.

## CHAPTER V.

*They prophesied. [n: (BEDA, lib. II in Esdram, tom. II.)]* In their books it is written more fully, etc., *as far as* Zorobabel also and Jesus, and the people who was with [0701D] them, are proved to have been of no less faith, who hearkened rather to the prophets commanding than to the command of him who forbade.

VERS. 2.-- *Zorobabel. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Born in Babylon, etc., *as far as* Every man too, when in calling upon God he seeks nothing other than him, can call him Salathiel.

*And Josue. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Josue or Jesus is interpreted *Saviour.* Josedec, the Lord just. He indeed, like the son of Nun, signifies Christ; for each of them brought the people into the land of promise, the one from the long sojourning of the desert, the other from the longer servitude of the captivity. So the true Jesus, rescuing his elect from all evils, leads them into the promised joys of the kingdom above, [0702A] he who is the son of Josedec, that is, of the Lord just, of whom it is said: *The Lord who is just will cut the necks of sinners,* etc. [n: (Psal. XII.)]

VERS. 3.-- *In the same. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The letter of the text is plain, that, strengthened by the words of the prophets, the nobles of the Jews could not be hindered from the holy work by the assaults of the enemies, from which, while the prophets were as yet silent, they had ceased through fear of the enemies, as is now done in the Church, when they who, held back by the snares of malignant spirits or of men, were sometimes slower to good work, being suddenly seized by the words of the teachers or of the divine Scriptures, can be overcome by no engines of temptations, nor called back from the purpose of their intention.

VERS. 4.-- *To which we answered. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* It seems [0702B] that he who wrote this was present, since Esdras is said to have written it, who is found not to have been then at Jerusalem, but to have come thither long after, in the reign of Artaxerxes. Either therefore he was truly there then, and, the temple being made, returned to Babylonia, that he might bring back more of the children of Israel into Jerusalem, or he joins himself to the builders, as though what is done toward the brethren, or what they themselves do, were his own, as the Apostle saith: *We who live, who are left, at the coming of the Lord, shall not go before,* etc. [n: (I Thes. IV)].

VERS. 6.-- *The copy of the letter. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* This letter is very unlike that one, etc., *as far as* and at length, learning that this took its origin from the God of heaven and earth, who alone truly is God, they share in his sacrifices and rejoice to help the building.

[0702C]VERS. 8.-- *With unpolished stone. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* New, that is, which they found unpolished, but by polishing fitted to the building, etc., *as far as* the other, given over to idolatry, had by no industry of spiritual architects, by no worship of piety, put off the deformity of a wild and earthly mind.

*And timber is laid in the walls, and the work,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] The timbers which are laid for the adornment or the fortification of the temple designate the same life of the saints in the adornment of the Church as the stones do: of which it is said: *All the trees of the woods shall rejoice,* etc.
