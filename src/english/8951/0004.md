## CHAPTER VI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Then Darius. [n: (BEDA in Esdram, lib. II, tom. II.)]* Josephus thus set down the name of the place in which these letters of Cyrus [0702D] were found; and there was found in Egbathanis, a city built in the region of Media, a book. ⟦s2⟧ But another translation has it thus: In Egbathana, in a most secure building of the region of the Medes: whence it seems that the name Medena signifies nothing other than Media.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *That they lay the foundations. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* It is not for us to expound, etc., *as far as* or of the porch of the house of the Lord which was before the face of the temple. ⟦s2⟧ Of which Scripture, when the palace of Solomon was being built, thus records: *He made a court,* etc. [n: (III Reg. VII)].

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Let them be restored. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Darius confirms by his own authority the letters that had been completed, restraining all the adversaries of the Jews, and commands the temple to be built in its own place, etc.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0703A]VERS. 6.-- *Now therefore.* ⟦s2⟧ Artaxerxes, who forbids the house of God and the city to be built, signifies those who, by stirring up persecutions, gainsay the building of holy Church, amid which persecutions the Church flourished most of all by the victory of the martyrs. ⟦s3⟧ But Darius signifies the pious devotion of those kings who, having recognized the godliness of the Christian faith, took care not to resist it but to help it by their own decrees, of whom many, the persecutions of their predecessors being forbidden, willed that they themselves with their subject peoples should be consecrated by the sacraments of the same faith: to whom that agrees well which Darius adds: *But by me it is decreed that,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ For who could set forth how much the Church has been helped and enriched by the liberality of kings?

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *That out of the king's chest. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)]* Out of the king's chest the charges are supplied for the work of God, etc., *as far as* what [0703B] Cyril, what John, what the rest of the saints said: he proved that he had been taught by the elders of the Jews, that is, of those who confess and praise God.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Calves and lambs. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* These animals, which are clean, etc., *as far as* whatever wholesome thing he has learned from the men of the Church, he spends it all upon the divine worship.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *According to the rite.* ⟦s2⟧ Because then only will the vows of those who offer be accepted, when they are offered according to the rite of catholic peace; but those which are mingled with heathen superstition or with heresy are not good.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And let them pray.* ⟦s2⟧ According to that of the Apostle: *I beseech therefore first of all that supplications and prayers be made, petitions and givings of thanks for all [0703C] men, for kings, and for all that are in high station [n: (I Tim. II)]*.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *By me therefore.* ⟦s2⟧ With a most grievous penalty he willed to punish him who should change the decree, that is, with the loss of all that he had and of his own soul, which also, fastened to a beam, he should lose by a long torment.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But mystically our works are likened to timbers, the good to fruit-bearing trees, but the depraved to willows and to such as are worthy of the fire, and a beam is taken from the house of him who gainsays the holocausts of the Lord, and is set up, and he himself is fastened upon it, when the works of those who fight against the peace of the Church become manifest to all, how useless and perverse they are: and they themselves are proved to have gained in them not the joy of life but the snare of death. ⟦s2⟧ The house also of such men is published, that is, [0703D] proscribed to the public treasury: when their bodies, with which they remained in this life, are delivered over in the resurrection into the power of cruel exactors, of unclean spirits, to be punished with everlasting torment.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *And God.* ⟦s2⟧ He desires his own decree to be confirmed by the divine power, and he himself, as a man endowed with a temporal kingdom, does by public law all that he can for the peace of the house of God. ⟦s3⟧ Which is now also done in the Church, when earthly powers converted to the faith set forth public edicts for the state of the Church, and take that she should always have quiet peace, the Lord helping and warring down her enemies.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *According to the prophecy.* ⟦s2⟧ For they had foretold that if they persisted in building the temple, presently, by the [0704A] Lord's gift, they would both complete the work itself and abound in all good things.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (BEDA ubi supra. )] Let no one, because Aggeus says that the foundations of the temple were laid under king Darius, etc., *as far as* sometimes separately under the name of treasuries, or of side-chambers, or of porches, or of courts; whence: *You that stand in the house of the Lord, in the courts of the house of our God [n: (Psal. CXXXIII)]*.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And Artaxerxes.* ⟦s2⟧ It is asked how the house is said to have been built at the bidding of Artaxerxes, when it is at once subjoined that it was completed and dedicated while Darius still reigned, unless perhaps it is to be believed that Artaxerxes also, having sent gold and silver, commanded: where, if anything were less than complete in the building or in the adornment of the temple or of the vessels, it should be finished. ⟦s3⟧ For when Esdras was hastening thither, [0704B] it is written that the same king with his princes or counsellors sent very much gold and silver and vessels to the temple.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *The month Adar. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)]* On the third day of which the house was completed, *as far as* in which all the more eminent parts were completed.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID., ibid.)] But the temple, although it signifies the elect soul because of the Spirit of Christ dwelling in it, etc., *as far as* from the upper we penetrate also into the third, when we heap up the beatitude of souls with the receiving of immortal bodies also.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *And they made.* ⟦s2⟧ Rightly do the children of the transmigration rejoice, etc., *as far as* because all the orders of the Church rejoice over sinners reconciled through penance.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0704C]VERS. 17.-- *And they offered.* ⟦s2⟧ They offer victims at the dedication, when they give thanks to God for the conversion of the erring, when many, beholding their devotion, gird themselves to greater works of the virtues, lest they be slower in working than those than whom they were more innocent in sinning less. ⟦s3⟧ Many also who went before in the faith emulate the more fervent zeal of the new-comers.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The he-goats of the goats.* ⟦s2⟧ He-goats are offered in this life for sin, when the elect beseech God that they may be delivered from sins: they are offered in the time to come, when those who have been delivered give thanks, and therefore *they shall sing the mercies of the Lord for ever [n: (Psal. LXXXVIII)]*; [var: Vulg. Ps 88:2 *Misericordias Domini in aeternum cantabo*, "I will sing"] because they will always remember that they were wretched and were set free by his gift.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For sin.* ⟦s2⟧ They offer also for the sin of all Israel, [0704D] because it behoves that we so favour the good of those who are with us; so make their goods our own by imitating them, that we may also entreat the Lord for the state of the whole Church. ⟦s3⟧ Whence in the Lord's prayer no man asks anything specially for himself, but for all who have the same Father in heaven. ⟦s4⟧ Again, the temple being built, the dedication follows, when the full sum of the elect, completed at the end of the world, shall come to the grace of the heavenly rewards, in which victims are offered: of which the same house, that is, the Church, rebuilt after the captivity of mortal misery through the glory of immortality, says: *Thou hast broken my bonds, O Lord, to thee will I sacrifice the sacrifice of praise [n: (Psal. CXV)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And because they are offered not only for the gifts of the virtues, but also for the worldly filth of the vices: [0705A] rightly after the calves, the rams and the lambs are the he-goats offered for the sin of all Israel: for the grace out of which he confers the benefits of the virtues takes away crimes also. ⟦s6⟧ Nor has Pelagius a place in the house of God, who persuaded himself and his own that by the liberty of the will they could either be freed from evils or be confirmed in good things.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *And they appointed.* ⟦s2⟧ Order demanded that, the house of God being built, the priests and the Levites who were to minister in it should presently be ordained. ⟦s3⟧ Which is to be observed by those who magnificently build monasteries, that they set in them teachers who may exhort the people to the works of God, and not serve their own pleasures there.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The priests.* [n: (BEDA ubi supra. )] That he says the priests in their orders, etc., *as far as* if we take care to attain to the rewards of the Lord together with them.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0705B]VERS. 19.-- *And the children made.* ⟦s2⟧ Therefore the celebration of the pasch is specially recorded, the house of the Lord having been built and dedicated, since it was doubtful to none that religious men then celebrated the pasch in its season, so that it might be mystically intimated that this is the summit of perfection, when, all the allurements of the world being past, we meditate with the whole intention of the mind always upon the entrance of the other life. ⟦s3⟧ For the pasch is interpreted a passing over, because the children of Israel in it passed through the immolation of the lamb from the Egyptian servitude to liberty, or because the Lamb himself, that is, Christ, immolated for us, passed out of this world to the Father, which we imitate when we pass from the lowest pleasures to the seeking of heavenly things, and we truly accomplish it when, loosed from the prison-house of the flesh, we enter the heavenly kingdom.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0705C] [n: (BEDA, ibid.)] Why the pasch is related after the building of the house, etc., *as far as* by how much the longer it possessed the ground of the heart empty of virtue.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *And they kept the solemnity. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The celebration of the pasch can in this place be typically referred to the time of our resurrection, etc., *as far as* whose sevenfold grace the Prophet commends to us.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Seven days.* ⟦s2⟧ Because through the whole time of the world life is to be led in sincerity and in truth, nay rather in all the victims and holocausts of good things: because Christ in the paschal season tasted death in passing, but overcame it by the eternal power of the resurrection.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In joy.* ⟦s2⟧ For this is the greatest joy of the just, now and in the time to come, that the work of justice or of the Church is perfect, the Gentiles also being converted who once resisted [0705D] and to the helping of her state, to the confirming of the peace of the Christian religion throughout the world.
