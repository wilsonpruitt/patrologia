## CHAPTER VII.

VERS. 1.-- *But after these things. [n: (Beda in Esdram, tom. II.)]* Thus far the return of the people from Babylon into Jerusalem, etc., *as far as* they might have whereby, being themselves admonished, they might learn inwardly to be restored, whence follows:

*After these things. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)]* This Artaxerxes, under whom Esdras went up from Babylonia to Jerusalem, Josephus thinks to be Xerxes the son of Darius, who reigned after him: but the books of the chronicles, the successor of that same Xerxes. Now Darius, under whom the temple was built, reigned six and thirty years, after whom Xerxes twenty years, after whom Archabanus seven months, [0706A] which the chronographers set down for a year: after him Artaxerxes for forty years.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] Esdras is called a swift scribe in the law of Moses, etc., *as far as* but the former letters remained among the Samaritans, in which they were wont to write those five books of Moses which alone they had received out of holy Scripture.

VERS. 7.-- *And they went up. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* It is to be noted that at the head of this book it is written that at the permission of Cyrus Zorobabel and Josue went up from Babylon, etc., *as far as* a perpetual one I give to those that come to the heavenly country.

VERS. 9.-- *Because in the first. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* On the first day of the month he began to go up from Babylon, etc., *as far as* as after four months of shining action, through which we journey on the way, [0706B] we pass the fifth month of perpetual reward in the light of the heavenly country.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] In the first month Esdras began to go up from Babylon, etc., *as far as* we may come to the promises above.

*According to the hand of God.* Strengthened, that is, by grace and by the divine protection, whereby he might prosperously accomplish what he had begun. So also Christ, because God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself, the hand of God also was upon his humanity, whereby, exalted in the passion, he both ascended to the walls of the city above and laid open the way to his faithful.

VERS. 10.-- *And Esdras prepared.* Great is the virtue of a man, to prepare his heart for obedience to the divine will, and to be able to say: *My heart is ready, O God, my heart is ready [n: (Psal. LVI)]*.

[n: (BEDA, ibid.)] Christ also prepared his heart, that [0706C] he might search out the law of the Lord, because he foresaw divinely that he was to take to himself such a man as should not only be without sin, but should also be full of grace and truth: who, with no law of sin fighting against him, should keep the law of God without any contradiction of mind or of flesh; whence: *In the head of the book it is written of me,* etc. [n: (Psal. XXXIX.)] Again, the Lord searched out the law of God, because, the traditions of the Pharisees being cast away, he taught men to understand holy Scripture mystically, and showed that the decrees of the Gospel which he brought were more perfect and more acceptable to God than those which he had sent before through Moses. Whence: *You have heard that it was said to them of old: Thou shalt love thy friend, and thou hast thy enemy in hatred. But I say to you: Love your enemies,* etc. [var: Vulg. Matt. 5:43 *Audistis quia dictum est: Diliges proximum tuum, et odio habebis inimicum tuum*, "thy neighbour" and "thou shalt hold in hatred"; the Vulgate carries no *antiquis*]

VERS. 12.-- *Artaxerxes the king.* Artaxerxes, who with devout [0706D] mind reverenced the temple and the priests of the Lord and served him, signifies Christian princes, as Darius does. Nor is it wonderful if the successors of Cyrus, who caused the temple and the city to be built, who loved and helped his servants and his law, figure Christian kings; since the Lord himself said through the prophet that Cyrus signified his Son: and honoured him with his name, saying: *Thus saith the Lord to my anointed Cyrus [n: (Isa. XLV)]*.

VERS. 13.-- *By me a decree.* Because the report of the divine power had come to the king, through which he had renewed the law that had been burnt by the Chaldeans (in the same proper words as before, though in another character of letters).

VERS. 14.-- *That to whomsoever.* He grants to all leave of going, [0707A] he compels none to go. Christian princes, compelling none, lest the will of faith be uncertain, permit all to whom it shall seem good to worship Christ in their kingdom.

*And of the seven counsellors.* In the book of Esther it is read that it was the custom of the kings of the Persians to use the counsel of seven in all things to be done or to be decided. The faithful use seven counsellors when in all that they do they follow the precepts of divine Scripture. Of which it is said: *The words of the Lord are chaste words, silver tried by the fire, proved of the earth, purified seven times [n: (Psal. XI)]*, that is, perfected by the holy enlightening of the sevenfold Spirit.

*Thou art sent.* Esdras is sent from the face of the king and of his seven counsellors, that he may visit India [cj: *Indiam*; read *Judaeam*, "Judea"] and Jerusalem, [0707B] and the princes of the world converted to the faith and strengthened by the persuasions of the Scriptures desire that Christ should come to save the Church and to gather it out of the Gentiles by daily help, which is what the name of Esdras signifies, saying: *O Lord God of hosts, turn again, look down from heaven,* etc. [n: (Psal. LXXIX.)]

*Which is in thy hand.* It is wonderful that a word is found in the letter of Artaxerxes which the prophets are wont to use: when he says that the law of God is in the hand of his servant, for it is written: The word was made in the hand of Aggeus the prophet. And the Lord did what he spoke in the hand of his servant Elias; and the Lord testified in Israel and in Juda by the hands of all the prophets: because, namely, the prophets preached the things that are God's no less by working than by speaking.

[0707C]Christ had the law in his hand: because he once appointed it through Moses as he willed; and now, changing that same law through himself, he transferred it to more perfect things.

VERS. 15.-- *And that thou carry.* The faith and the wisdom of the king and of his counsellors is to be noted, who understood that their gifts were rather to be offered through him who had the law of God in his hand, that is, fulfilled it in work.

*Whose tabernacle is in Jerusalem.* [n: (BEDA ubi supra. )] Faithfully and learnedly he says that he has his tabernacle in Jerusalem, etc., *as far as* and of him John says: *He is the propitiation for our sins [n: (I Joan. II)]*.

VERS. 16.-- *And all the silver.* Of this money the faithful in the Church desire that their good works should shine: that through this they may have a father with the saints: and that others may profit by their [0707D] examples. For as it were out of our silver and gold are sacrifices, libations and victims procured for offering to the Lord: when, the brightness of our works being seen, our neighbours are converted to the duty of piety: whereby they themselves also, by living well, are consecrated to the Lord; but there are certain most illustrious virtues of the saints: which cannot be shown to all as examples of work: but only recounted for the glorifying of the grace of God, such as that which Daniel and Jeremias prophesied as boys: that John, not yet born: that Cornelius, not yet baptized, received the Holy Spirit with his household, and innumerable miracles after the manner of silver in the house of the Lord. Nor out of this silver or gold alone can the oblations which are set upon the altar [0708A] be bought, because, when we hear such things, we ought indeed to marvel at them as divine: but we are not able to imitate them as being possible.

VERS. 19.-- *The vessels also.* The true Esdras delivered into the Jerusalem above, in the sight of the Father, the vessels of mercy which are handed over to him by men. Of which it is said: *How shall any one be able to enter into the house of the strong, and rifle his vessels, unless he first bind the strong? [n: (Marc. III.)]* [var: Vulg. Marc. 3:27 *Nemo potest vasa fortis ingressus in domum diripere, nisi prius fortem alliget*, "no man can enter into the house of the strong and rob his goods, unless he first bind the strong"] For the king of Babylon, that is, the devil, was strong: but bound and fettered by the Lord, he lost the vessels which he unjustly possessed, that is, the hearts of the chosen: nor could he gainsay him who took them away and led them back to the city above whose own they were.

VERS. 21.-- *And by me.* Christian princes command their subjects [0708B] that whatsoever Christ our high priest shall ask, they give it without delay, drawing back in nothing at all: let them give gold in the confession of the true faith, corn in the showing forth of good working; wine in the fervour of love; oil in the cheerfulness of mercy: and all these things are commanded to be given, under the number of a hundred, to the great priest and scribe of the law of the God of heaven, that is, to Christ, who, bringing down to us the heavenly commandments from the Father, promised heavenly rewards in the house of the Father to those who obey. For the number of a hundred, which in the reckoning of the fingers passes from the left hand to the right: designates those joys which are on the right hand of the index (that is, eternal life). But the talent is handed down as of threefold measure. The least, of fifty pounds the middle, of sixty-two pounds the greatest, of a hundred and twenty pounds. [0708C] The corus thirty bushels: the batos, which is also the ephi, the tenth part of the corus, that is, three bushels.

VERS. 22.-- *But salt. [n: (BEDA ubi supra.)]* Salt signifies wisdom, etc., *as far as* while they overcome by means of adversaries.

VERS. 24.-- *To you also.* By this privilege it is shown that the rest of the people of the children of Israel, coming to their country, paid the tributes of the king, which the king is known to have done with a discerning foresight, that they who were occupied above others in the divine service should be free from his own service, and that of those who possessed nothing of their own in the land but lived of the tithes of the people, no man should exact tribute. By all these things he is proved not only to have loved, but also to have learned excellently, what the worship of the divine service required.

[0708D]VERS. 25.-- *But thou. [n: (BED., ibid.)]* The king repeats what he had said above, etc., *as far as* to whom kings and princes have often sent letters for the state of the faithful.

VERS. 28.-- *And I being strengthened.* So each one is made fit by his own teaching for gathering others: drunken through the grace of God, he is strengthened in mind against all the things that hinder the work of the saints.
