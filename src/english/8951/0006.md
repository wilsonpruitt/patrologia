## CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 1.-- *These then are the princes,* etc. [n: (BEDA, in Esdram.)] He diligently enumerates the princes who went up with him from Babylon, etc., *as far as* that is, out of the life of those whom thou hast taught thou shalt be more glorious in the kingdom of God.

[0709A]VERS. 15.-- *And I gathered together. [n: (BEDA., ibid.)]* I do not remember to have read this name of a place elsewhere, etc., *as far as* he provided himself an abundance of ministers of the house of God, through whom, coming to Jerusalem, he might accomplish what things are necessary for the uses of the temple.

VERS. 17.-- *And I sent them to Eddo. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The Caspian Sea, according to Orosius, etc., *as far as* these being joined to him, Esdras is found to have had in his company the sum of nearly a thousand seven hundred men.

VERS. 21.-- *And I proclaimed there a fast. [n: (BEDA, homil. 8.)]* An example of fasting and of praying is given us, etc., *as far as* and so let him pray, and let him not doubt that what he asks will fall out prosperously.

VERS. 25.-- *And I weighed out to them the silver, and the gold, and the consecrated vessels. [n: (ID. in Esdram, tom. II.)]* By the silver, [0709B] and the gold, and the vessels which are sent from Babylon to Jerusalem, etc., *as far as* and through their successors it is to be built even to the end.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] There is a difference between these vessels which Esdras with the priests offers at Jerusalem, and those which Zorobabel and Jesus are related to have offered: because those were carried over from the temple of the Lord into Babylon, and were carried over into Jerusalem: these were made in Babylonia, but sent to Jerusalem for devotion's sake by the king or by the princes of the Persians, or even by the people of Israel which was tarrying in those parts. Those vessels therefore signify those who, after the knowledge and the sacraments of faith received, after works of virtue begun, are deceived by the devil and snatched away to their own confusion, but are called back by the grace of Christ to salvation: and these signify those who, [0709C] born with the sin of the first transgression, cleansed through the laver of regeneration by the ministry of the priests, are joined to the children of the Church.

VERS. 28.-- *You are holy of the Lord.* It behoves the teachers of the Church never to forget their holiness, because they have been consecrated by the Lord through the Holy Spirit unto the day of redemption, for the receiving whereof let them instruct their hearers also, that they who have already been offered to the Lord through the rudiments of faith may be the more confirmed also by the examples and the admonitions of those who have gone before them in the faith, and may be made worthy of the entrance of the city above.

VERS. 29.-- *Watch ye and keep.* Priests must watch, lest any of the souls entrusted to them, that is, of the vessels of the Lord, should perish: but that they be brought with their number whole to the buildings of holy virtue.

[0709D] *Until you weigh.* Show forth, that is, by instructing and by teaching, those whom the disposition above has committed to you, such that they may be found irreprehensible and fit for the treasure of the heavenly court, that is, for the seats of eternal peace and light, and this not by the judgment of any persons whatsoever who can be deceived, but of the apostles and of those who shall judge with the Lord. For these deservedly are princes of the priests and of the Levites and leaders of the families of Israel, that is, they are understood as men or souls seeing God, of whom it is said: *Thou shalt make them princes over all the earth [n: (Psal. XLIV)]*; and elsewhere: *Her husband is honourable in the gates.*

*We set forward. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)]* All things are full of mysteries, etc., *as far as* helped whereby we may the more effectually carry over the souls of the faithful to the [0710A] fellowship of the elect and to the citadel of the life of perfection, as holy vessels to the temple of the Lord.

VERS. 32.-- *And we abode. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Three days are spent in Jerusalem, etc., *as far as* as though after the joy of a three days' abiding in Jerusalem they are the more honoured on account of the gifts and the precious vessels which they offered.

VERS. 34.-- *And it was written down. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* This is done when teachers earnestly examine the life of their subjects, etc., *as far as* that he may be given a worthy retribution in heaven.

VERS. 35.-- *But they also who had come. [n: (ID.)]* Great devotion and religion is shown, when, coming to the temple, they first of all offer holocausts, not only for themselves, but also for all Israel, that is, [0710B] for those who had already returned home, and for those who were still in exile.

But mystically those have truly and perfectly escaped by repenting the captivity of the devil, by which they were held in sinning, who subject themselves with fixed intention to the divine service, who kindle themselves, wholly drawn away from the lowest things, with the flame of heavenly desire. For this is to offer holocausts, that is, wholly burnt sacrifices, to the Lord, to think or to do nothing in all things but his will.

*They offered holocausts to God,* etc. Not only for themselves. It is a token of a perfect mind when a man immolates for all Israel, that is, for the general welfare of the faithful, and as one mindful of unity and of brotherhood beseeches the mercy above.

VERS. 36.-- *And they lifted up the people.* [n: (AUG.)] By adorning them [0710C] with the various gifts which the king and his counsellors and his princes had sent, and by delivering also his ministers and priests from all straits.

Mystically. The people and the temple of God hold one and the same figure of the Church, which Esdras and the children of the transmigration lift up when the sacred vessels are taken away from Babylon, when preachers, gathering to her, by the Lord's help, new peoples of believers, show her honourable to all and terrible. Again, when those whom they train by examples in good conversation, or whom by words they promote or advance even to the receiving of the heavenly rewards: they lift up the people into the house of God, because they make great joy both for those abiding in the country above and for the elect still journeying on earth.

[n: (BEDA in Esdram, lib. II, tom. II.)] But this [0710D] transgression, plainly described in the prophet Malachy, etc., *as far as* they refer the cause to the prince, that is, to the archbishop, by whose authority the crime may be expiated.

But mystically foreign wives signify heresies and the superstitious sects of the philosophers, which, when they are incautiously admitted into the Church, defile by error the holy seed of catholic truth and of pure action. All sins also with which the heathen are polluted, while Christians are not ashamed to imitate them, as it were through foreign wives degenerate from the seed of the word of God by which they had been begotten, according to that: *Of his own will hath he begotten us by the word,* etc. [n: (Jac. I)], and as it were beget a profane offspring of the daughters of strangers, while, following the allurements [0711A] of the erring, they bring forth their perverse acts to the knowledge of all.

*He is not separated. [n: (BEDA., ibid.)]* It is to be noted that, Israel having been led away into captivity, etc., *as far as* the princes who ought to have corrected confess that they were the first to err.

## CHAPTER IX.

VERS. 3.-- *I rent my mantle. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* By garments works are wont to be designated, etc., *as far as* He sat mourning, that he might teach that pardon is to be earned by the lamentations of penance.

VERS. 4.-- *They came together.* Note how much the examples of teachers avail. Nothing is written of Esdras having spoken, but only that, the crime being heard, he wept, and drew the crowd of the faithful to himself not by crying out but by weeping.

[0711B] *They came together.* A great change of things, by the perfidy of the princes, many having followed luxury, are brought back, the great prince mourning and intimating what sinners ought to do: there come together to him all who fear the word of God, by which he threatens that transgressors are to be punished.

*The mantle being rent. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)]* Typically, that with the garment rent, etc., *as far as* he vouchsafed mercifully to transfer to himself even those things which truly befitted their frailty.

VERS. 5.-- *I bowed my knees,* etc. He prepared himself by compunction of heart and by affliction of body, that he might be made worthy of the hearing of the mercy above: and he breaks forth into these words of prayer. And he bows his knees, spreads out his hands, and pours forth prayers at the time of the evening sacrifice, [0711C] knowing that what is done in the spirit of humility and in a contrite mind is more acceptable than what is offered with the flesh and blood of cattle.
