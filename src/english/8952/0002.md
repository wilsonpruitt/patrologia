## CHAPTER III.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (RAB., in lib. Reg.)] Whence in those days there was no manifest vision. ⟦s2⟧ For on account of the sins of the people and of the prelates the Lord did not appear by visions or by angelic answers, as he appeared to the fathers.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *The lamp.* [n: (ID.)] Which burned in the tabernacle before the light of day. ⟦s2⟧ For under the light of day it was quenched, but it shone until the morning; or this signifies that Heli should remain in blindness until he were utterly quenched together with his offspring, that is, should die.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But mystically the blindness of Heli signifies the blindness of the Jews, who acknowledged not the Saviour present, [0545D] nor the brightness of his miracles, nor his teaching, until the priesthood together with the people and the temple was overthrown.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *They shall tingle.* [n: (RAB.)] It is the threatening of God upon Heli and upon his house, because, namely, for the sin of the sons of Heli the house of the Lord was deprived of the priesthood, whence the ears of them that hear shall tingle with a vehement astonishment.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *These things.* ⟦s2⟧ It was a proverb among the Hebrews. ⟦s3⟧ And the sense is: If thou shalt hide from me the evil which the Lord hath spoken to thee concerning me, let it come upon thy head.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *And he hid not. [n: (RAB., in lib. Reg.)]* Because nothing of those things which the Lord spoke, etc., *as far as* he did this for a sure reason of their profit.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0546A]VERS. 20.-- *From Dan.* [n: (ID.)] By the boundaries of Judaea he comprehends the whole body of the people. ⟦s2⟧ Dan is a small village at the fourth milestone from Paneas, which even to this day is so called: the boundary of the province of Judaea toward the North, from which the Jordan, bursting forth, has taken its name from the place. ⟦s3⟧ For the Hebrews call a river or a stream Jor. ⟦s4⟧ Dan is interpreted *judgment* or judging.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Bersabee.* ⟦s2⟧ In the tribe of Juda or of Simeon there is a great village at the twentieth milestone from Hebron, inclining toward the South: in which a garrison of the Romans is placed: from which the foolish [cj: *insipientes*; read *incipientes*, "beginning"] boundaries of Judaea are stretched as far as Dan. Nor ought anyone to wonder if he find cities of Juda in the tribe of Simeon or of Benjamin. ⟦s3⟧ For the tribe of Juda, strong and warlike, often overcoming its adversaries, held the chief place among all the tribes, [0546B] and therefore the lots of the other tribes are reckoned within its measuring line, and besides, the Scripture makes manifest that Simeon dwelt in the midst of the tribe of Juda.

## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And it came to pass. ⟦s2⟧ Israel,* a man seeing God, or strong with God, these are the believers. ⟦s3⟧ The Philistines, falling by the cup, namely the demons, who, made drunk with the cup of pride, themselves also fell, and hasten to pervert men. ⟦s4⟧ Against whom Israel, that is the faithful, that they may not yield but may overcome, encamp beside the stone of help, that is the law of Christ, where the stone, that is Christ, is found, of whom: *The stone which the builders rejected.* ⟦s5⟧ But while the faithful meditate in the law of the Lord, that they may find God their helper, [0546C] *the Philistines came into Aphec,* that is, into a new fury, then, because the unclean spirits behold the faithful pressing yet more upon the divine laws, they therefore devise sharper and newer contests, whence the unclean spirits easily lay low, as it were four thousand, those trusting in their own strength and glorying in human wisdom and by this despising the grace of the Gospel.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Beside the stone.* ⟦s2⟧ The stone of help which afterward Samuel set up for a sign, when first the Lord helped him.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Aphec.* [n: (RAB.)] It is a place beside the village Esron of the city of Israel, where Saul fought in the lot of Aser, and it is interpreted a new fury, or one that lays hold; for they who deserved the anger of the Lord were laid hold of by their enemies.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0546D]VERS. 4.-- *And they took.* ⟦s2⟧ The children of Israel took the ark, where it was captured. ⟦s3⟧ And the faithful from among the Jews carried over the two testaments to the nations.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Sitting upon.* ⟦s2⟧ For there was a table above the ark which the Cherubim covered with their wings, where the Lord appeared through an angel and spoke. ⟦s3⟧ The Cherubim, the fulness of knowledge, which is found in Christ and in the two testaments.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *With a shout.* ⟦s2⟧ A great shout, the voice of the preachers strengthened by miracles, brought forth out of the two testaments.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And it resounded.* ⟦s2⟧ At the voice of the preaching the wise of the world, and the demons also, groaned, believing themselves to be overcome.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0547A]VERS. 8.-- *Out of the hand.* ⟦s2⟧ The Jews worship one God, but the nations speak after their own manner.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And the ark.* ⟦s2⟧ The ark taken by the aliens signifies that the testament was to pass over to the nations.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *And when. [n: (RAB.)]* These things Josephus so relates, etc., *as far as* they received them anew out of the house of Eleazar.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *He fell.* ⟦s2⟧ And when Heli falls from the seat, the chair of the high priest remains empty, and the glory of the priests is quenched.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Dead.* ⟦s2⟧ Heli dies, his sons also die, because the high priesthood fails together with the old priesthood.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *The wife.* ⟦s2⟧ The wife of the priest did not perish before she brought forth a man child; nor did the synagogue wholly perish before the primitive church which was of her [0547B] believed.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *In that very.* ⟦s2⟧ By an evident sign it is figured that, when the carnal priesthood of the Jews was quenched, the carnal synagogue which cleaved to it carnally is quenched.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Fear not.* ⟦s2⟧ The believers console the synagogue, but despairing she by no means takes heed, nor does she account the new offspring unto glory, but unto ignominy. ⟦s3⟧ Whence there follows:

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *And she called.* ⟦s2⟧ Cabhod, glory; Icabhod, the glory is fallen. ⟦s3⟧ For the glory was carried over when the ark was taken, whence: *The kingdom of God shall be taken away from you, and shall be given to a nation yielding the fruits thereof.*

## CHAPTER V.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0547C]VERS. 1.-- [n: (RAB., ubi supra.)] Which is also Asdor, one of the five cities of the Palestines, decreed indeed to the tribe of Juda, but not retained, because it could not drive out the natives. ⟦s2⟧ And it is interpreted *the fire of the father,* or a burning. ⟦s3⟧ And rightly is that place so called where the idol Dagon was, because the coming of the ark into Azotus was the burning of the devil, the father of all enemies or of all the wicked. ⟦s4⟧ Dagon, who is interpreted *the fish of sadness,* signifies the devil who in the sea of this world devours sinners, who in Job also is named Leviathan and Beemoth [n: (Job. XL)].

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Azotus.* ⟦s2⟧ It is the place in which Samuel set the stone [n: (I Reg. VII)], as is shown in what follows.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *The head.* ⟦s2⟧ The pride of the devil, which is the beginning of all sin [n: (Eccle. X)].

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0547D] *Upon.* [n: (RAB.)] It signifies the appointed end, in which idolatry should cease. ⟦s2⟧ For the threshold is the end of a journey.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *The stump.* ⟦s2⟧ They found the back only, all the members being broken, which expresses the flight of idolatry. ⟦s3⟧ For he who flees gives his back to him that puts him to flight, whence: *For thou shalt make them turn their back [n: (Psal. XX)]*.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And he smote.* ⟦s2⟧ They who take up the testament of God and love the hinder things of this life shall justly be tormented by those same things in the hinder parts, which they ought to esteem as dung. ⟦s3⟧ For they who take up the testament of God, and looking back to the hinder things, strip not off from themselves the old vanity, are like to those who set the ark of the testament beside their idols. ⟦s4⟧ And the old things indeed [0548A] fall even when they are unwilling, because *all flesh is grass.* ⟦s5⟧ But the ark remains for ever, that is, the sure testament of the kingdom of heaven, where is the word of God for ever.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Let it be carried about.* ⟦s2⟧ That if, whithersoever it go, the smiting follow it, we may know the plague to be from God, which by the will of God was done through their counsel, that the plague might be common, since the fault was common.
