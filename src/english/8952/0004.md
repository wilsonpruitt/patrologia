## CHAPTER IX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0551A]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *By name Saul. [n: (RAB., in lib. Reg., tom. II.)]* Saul, who, God being offended, was made king over Israel, etc., *as far as* and from being peaceable he was made turbulent.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] Because Saul, seeking his father's she-asses, came to the prophet by whom he was anointed, it signifies that the Jews, following the folly of carnal sense, seek out the luxury of the world through devious errors; for the ass is a brute and lustful animal. ⟦s2⟧ And coming to the prophet, that is, to Moses, they hear that the she-asses have been found, that is, that the goods of the earth are owed to them, by whom also the oil of anointing and the government of the kingdom is promised, in which for a time they might snatch their brethren from the hand of their enemies.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0551B] [n: (ID.)] According to the justice of the present life.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] This pertains rather to Christ, of whom it is said: *Behold my chosen servant whom I have chosen [n: (Isa. XLIV)]*. ⟦s2⟧ And elsewhere: *Who is like to God among the sons of God [n: (Psal. LXXXVI)]* ?

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Staters.* ⟦s2⟧ The stater is a sicle which has twenty oboli, that is, ten denarii.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *That we may give to the man.* ⟦s2⟧ Although they had determined to give, we do not read that Samuel received it, yet if he had received it, it would not be reckoned as the reward of divination, but as the wage of a prophet.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Formerly in Israel.* ⟦s2⟧ As Bede says, they are not the words of the historian, but of Esdras.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A seer.* ⟦s2⟧ He is called a prophet, because through the Spirit he foreknows things to come.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *The hill,* etc., *going forth to draw [0551C] water.* ⟦s2⟧ A hill is the winding ascent of a mountain.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *And thou shalt anoint him ruler.* ⟦s2⟧ Saul anointed signifies Christ, who himself also was called Christ; whence, *Why wast thou not afraid to put forth thy hand against the Christ of the Lord [n: (II Reg. I)]*?

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *All things which.* [n: (RAB.)] The Hebrews hand down that it was in the heart of Saul that he was to be king, when he had seen in a vision that he was set on the top of a palm tree, which is the sign of one about to reign.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Taking therefore.* ⟦s2⟧ That Samuel led Saul into the high place and gave him refreshment signifies the prophets calling forth the people of the Hebrews by their teaching to higher things, that, refreshed with spiritual knowledge, he may stand on the summit of the virtues, and not slip back into [0551D] vices.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A dining-room.* ⟦s2⟧ A supper-room, so called from the three couches of them that recline.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Of set purpose,* that is, of Providence, it is given to be understood that he had already eaten, though not fully, because, the Lord revealing that Saul would come, the banquet was deferred.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *And when they were going down.* ⟦s2⟧ They went down from the high place, that in some more secret spot he might speak concerning the kingdom and anoint him.

## CHAPTER X.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And he took.* ⟦s2⟧ A vial is a four-cornered earthen vessel, having a hole in its side, by which the frailty of the kingdom is designated.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0552A]VERS. 6.-- *And thou shalt prophesy with them.* ⟦s2⟧ The Jews hand down that he prophesied concerning the world to come, concerning Gog and Magog, concerning the rewards of the just and the punishments of the wicked.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *When therefore.* ⟦s2⟧ As if to say, by these signs thou shalt be able to know that God has willed thee to be king. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore in all things act royally, because the Lord is with thee.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Seven days,* there is understood, in the consecration of thy kingdom, as in the consecration of the priests victims were offered for seven days.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *And they came.* ⟦s2⟧ It signifies that to the Jews all things which Moses and the prophets had foretold came to pass. ⟦s3⟧ But they themselves, holding the books of the law and of the prophets, seem to divine, when they are known to pronounce the mysteries to come concerning Christ and the Church that are written therein.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0552B]VERS. 12.-- *And who.* ⟦s2⟧ As if to say, who is greater than he in dignity and wisdom? ⟦s3⟧ He can therefore move among the prophets as a father and a master. ⟦s4⟧ *And who is their father* that is, of the prophets, there is understood, save God.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Mystically, the elders of Israel, Samuel being rejected, asked a king for themselves, and the Scribes with the Pharisees, Christ the true priest being cast off, cried out: *We have no king but Caesar [n: (Joan. XIX)]*, whence *Samuel* is interpreted: his name is God; Saul, asked for, or a petition, who was demanded by the people, is understood to be Barabbas. ⟦s2⟧ Cis, hard or vomiting; Abiel, my father is God, for the Jews, hard and vomiting, while confessing God the Father, deny the Son; but he who denies the Son denies also the Father who sent him [n: (I Joan. II)]. ⟦s3⟧ Saul, seeking his father's she-asses, was taken up into the kingdom. ⟦s4⟧ The Saviour, sent by the [0552C] Father to the sheep that had perished of the house of Israel, was appointed king over the whole people. ⟦s5⟧ For there are as many she-asses as there are sheep. ⟦s6⟧ Saul received the shoulder, and Christ the principality [n: (Matth. XV)]. ⟦s7⟧ Saul was anointed by Samuel, and the Only-begotten was anointed by the Father with a spiritual unction. ⟦s8⟧ Saul rose high above the number, [cj: *numero*; read *humero*, "from the shoulder"] because our head above us is Christ [n: (I Cor. XI)].

## CHAPTER XI.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.--[n: (RAB.)] Jabes, sense: Galaad, the heap of testimony. ⟦s2⟧ Naas, that is, the serpent, namely the devil, goes up against the sense instructed by the testimony of Scripture.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And he answered.* ⟦s2⟧ The war of Naas signifies the war of the heretics against the Church. ⟦s3⟧ Naas is interpreted a serpent, Ammon, pressing hard or straitening, [0552D] or the people of sorrow. ⟦s4⟧ Jabes, dried up; Galaad, the heap of testimony; Naas therefore signifies the old serpent, who is the prince of the Ammonites, that is, of the heretics. ⟦s5⟧ For this is the people of sorrow, which has not the joy of the Holy Spirit, which makes a covenant with the people of the Church, that it may pluck out their right eyes, that is, the sight of the sound orthodox faith. ⟦s6⟧ It leaves the left eye, namely that it may perceive only what is depraved. ⟦s7⟧ But the mind of the faithful, dried up from the filth of vices, heaps up the heap of testimony, that is, the knowledge of holy Scripture, wherewith it may manfully resist the enemy.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *In Bezech. [n: (RAB., in lib., Reg., tom. II.)]* The city, namely, of Adonibezec, etc., *as far as* the Church may spread abroad throughout the whole world.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0553A]VERS. 11.-- *They were scattered.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the congregation of the depraved is not joined by the unity of charity and of faith, it is separated one from another by dissension; whence: *Thy enemies, O Lord, shall perish and shall be scattered [n: (Psal. XCI)]*.

## CHAPTER XII.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *The wheat harvest.* [n: (RAB.)] The gathering, namely, of the Gentile people unto the faith.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Voices.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, thunders and rains. ⟦s3⟧ It is not the custom of Judaea to rain in the time of summer.

## CHAPTER XIII.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *A son of one year,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] By anticipation he speaks of Isboseth the son of Saul, etc., *as far as* and he had not judged the people for the whole time of his life as the others did.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0553B]VERS. 2.-- *Gabaa.* [n: (ID.)] It is the city of Phinees the son of Eleazar, etc., *as far as* the men remaining with Saul, catholics firmly cleaving to Christ.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *They hid themselves in caves.* [n: (ID.)] In hidden places, namely, etc., *as far as* they joined themselves with their own in the battle.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Thou hast done foolishly, nor.* [n: (ID.)] It seems to some that Saul sacrificed blamelessly, etc., *as far as* and therefore he is deservedly blamed and convicted of folly.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But if thou hadst not done so. [n: (EUCH. in lib. Reg. ex Aug.)]* It is not so to be taken as though the Lord had prepared Saul to reign for ever, and had been unwilling to keep this for him when he sinned: for he knew that he would sin. ⟦s2+3⟧ But he had prepared his kingdom, in which there should be a figure of the eternal kingdom, whence he added: *And now thy kingdom shall not [0553C] stand to thee.* [var: Vulg. 1Rg 13:14 *sed nequaquam regnum tuum ultra consurget*, "but thy kingdom shall no more rise up"] That therefore has stood and shall stand which was signified in him; but it shall not stand to this man, because he was not to reign for ever; nor his offspring; that at least through his descendants, one succeeding another, that might seem to be fulfilled which was said, for ever.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *But by no means.* ⟦s2⟧ The reprobation of Saul, the taking away of the kingdom of the Jews. ⟦s3⟧ David surviving him, Christ about to succeed to the people of the Jews.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He hath sought for himself.* ⟦s2⟧ Not as though not knowing where he is, but he speaks through a man after the manner of men, because even by so speaking he seeks us. ⟦s3⟧ For not only to God the Father, but also to the Only-begotten himself, who came to seek that which had perished, we were already not such that we should be chosen in him before the constitution of the world. ⟦s4⟧ He will seek therefore for himself, he said: he shall have his own, whence in the Latin tongue this [0553D] verb takes a preposition, and is said *acquirit*; which is plain enough. ⟦s5⟧ Even without the preposition *quaerere* is understood as *acquirere*, from which gains are called *quaestus*.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A man according to the heart.* ⟦s1⟧ It signifies David, or the mediator himself of the New Testament, who was figured in the chrism wherewith David was anointed, and his offspring. [n: (RAB.)] The devil also through the pagans, etc., *as far as* but we seek that which is to come.
