## CHAPTER ONE.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0563A]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *There appeared a man coming from the camp of Saul,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The Hebrews say that this man was the son of Doeg. ⟦s2⟧ For the Amalecite and the Idumaean are one, because the firstborn son of Esau was Eliphan, and the son of this man was Amalec. ⟦s3⟧ But let him have the diadem and the bracelet entrusted to him by his father.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Drawing near,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Everyone who contrives evil against his neighbour, or rejoices at his destruction, deserves from the judge on high the penalty of a just retribution. ⟦s3⟧ *For he that diggeth a pit falleth into it [n: (Prov. XXVI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And, *The mouth that belieth killeth the soul [n: (Sap. I)]*: and this man was lying that he himself had killed Saul.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *The sons.* ⟦s2⟧ The sons of Juda, sons of confession, [0563B] whom the true David wills to be fortified with the bow of Scripture, lest through ignorance they fall down in the sight of the demons.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The bow. [n: (RAB. in lib. Reg.)]* Because the Philisthiim are archers, by which art they had killed Saul, he wills them to learn that same art of war, or he commanded that the kings of Juda should teach fortitude, that is, that they should be strong and intent in the fear of the Lord, lest they perish as Saul did.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In the book. [n: (RAB., ubi supra.)] It is read,* or, *it is written.* ⟦s2⟧ They assert that this book is nowhere to be found, like the book of the wars of the Lord, etc., *as far as* how Saul, drawing back from the fear of the Lord, perished through obedience [cj: *obedientiam*; read *inobedientiam*, "disobedience"].

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Consider, Israel.* [n: (ID.)] Thou who hast remained, for what [0563C] fault they were slain, and beware lest thou too perish in their wickedness.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Geth.* ⟦s2⟧ A winepress, that is, Jerusalem, which killeth the prophets [n: (Matth. XXIII)], to whom the death of the Lord and of the apostles was a matter of mockery, and for this cause the true David commanded that his passion should not be announced to them.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *The mountains of Gelboe,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Others, high places, because, that is, O Saul, thy disobedience was accounted as idolatry in the high places, since it is the wickedness of idolatry to be unwilling to acquiesce. ⟦s2⟧ The mountains of Gelboe were most rich before the curse, and they say that even until now they lie under the curse, and they testify that it never rains there.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ David, king and prophet, who did not render evils [0563D] to his persecutors, when Saul and Jonathas had fallen in battle, in his lamentation curses the mountains of Gelboe: *Ye mountains of Gelboe, neither dew,* etc., *as far as* and the divine power fulfilled the sentence of him who cursed. ⟦s2⟧ And so for the spectacle of the king's slaying the elements paid the penalty.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (RAB.)] What have the mountains done amiss? etc., *as far [0564A] as* they who had deserved, iniquity requiring it, to receive the death of their king among themselves.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *From the blood of the slain.* ⟦s2⟧ Blood and fat furnish strength to the body, so those men gave fortitude to their subjects, by whom Saul and Jonathas were slain as though not by rustics. ⟦s3⟧ Or thus, Saul was reprobated by God, and slain basely by the uncircumcised, as though he had not been anointed with oil, because of the blood of the slain, that is, of the seventy-five priests who burned fat to the Lord, whom Saul commanded to be slain though they were harmless.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The arrow.* ⟦s2⟧ It signifies that Jonathan and Saul were warlike and most valiant, and it praises in them the comeliness of form and the constancy of mind.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (RAB.)] Allegorically. ⟦s2⟧ The holy prophet David figuratively [0564B] bewails Saul and Jonathan, etc., *as far as* lest, being again shamefully overcome, they give joy to their enemies.

## CHAPTER II.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Therefore after.* [n: (RAB.)] The going up of David with his two wives into Hebron signifies the calling together of the two peoples into the Catholic Church. ⟦s2⟧ For Hebron is interpreted *wedlock.* ⟦s3⟧ But the Church alone is the bride of the heavenly king. ⟦s4⟧ Thither David brought each man with his household, because the faithful are called one by one into that fellowship through the preaching of the Gospel, where there is no distinction of Jew and Greek.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *And they came.* [n: (ID.)] Because, while the proud and the despisers spurn the grace of God, the elect confess by the report of preaching and by zeal for the virtues that Christ [0564C] is king among his own people, to whom is given all power in heaven and on earth.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And he said.* [n: (ID.)] He greatly pleases our Redeemer who consigns to oblivion, by condemning them, the works of the wicked who are dead in their sins, and does not imitate the things that displease the Lord, but remembers to do the things he has commanded.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Isboseth.* [n: (ID.)] Who is interpreted *son of confusion,* by the help of Abner (who is interpreted *the father's lamp* ) reigned over all Israel, and the house of Juda alone followed David, because, while only those who believe and truly confess follow Christ, in the rest the error of confusion reigned, at the instigation of the devil, who *transformeth himself into an angel of light [n: (II Cor. XI)]*.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0564D] *He led about.* ⟦s2⟧ In the Hebrew it is said: *He brought him over into Mahanaiim,* which is interpreted *camps,* that is, he brought him over beyond the Jordan, and set him up as king in the camps.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And it was. [n: (RAB. in lib. Reg., tom. II.)]* He said well, that David reigned seven years and six months in Hebron, etc., *as far as* but it was founded [0565A] seven years before Thanis, a city of Egypt.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *He went out.* [n: (RAB.)] Josephus expounds this passage thus, etc., *as far as* this man was pursuing Abner, turning aside to neither part.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Now Asael was a most swift runner, like one of the roes.* [n: (ID. ex Greg.)] Asael signifies those whom a vehement fury seizes and drives headlong, etc., *as far as* they die as it were without the sword.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *Knowest thou not that despair is dangerous?* ⟦s2⟧ In the Hebrew it stands thus: *Knowest thou not that the end will be bitter, if thou strivest to destroy the people?*

## CHAPTER III.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *There was therefore. [n: (RAB. in lib. Reg.)]* The house [0565B] of David is the heavenly Jerusalem, etc., *as far as* and at last, according to its own deserts, it shall be sunk into the depth of the abyss.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And there were born.* ⟦s2⟧ The six sons who were born to David in Hebron signify the perfection of the saints, who from the spiritual wedlock of Christ and the Church spring up daily through the grace of the Holy Spirit.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Of Egla.* [n: (RAB.)] This is Michol, who alone is called a wife, because David obtained her as his first wife in his youth. ⟦s2⟧ She is said to have died in childbirth, whence in what follows it is written: *Therefore to Michol the daughter of Saul was no son born, unto the day of her death,* because she died in that very childbirth.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *A word.* [n: (ID.)] Abner exhorts all Israel, etc., *as far as* and everywhere they persecuted the faith of Christ [0565C].

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *An issue of seed.* ⟦s2⟧ According to the letter he seems to imprecate a flowing forth of lust, the plague of leprosy, effeminacy, to lie under the sword of the enemy, to beg.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Holding a spindle.* [n: (AUG.)] By the circuit of words are marked those who strive to defend their own deceit.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Falling. ⟦s2+3⟧ He that striketh with the sword shall perish by the sword [n: (Matth. XXVI)]*. [var: Vulg. Matt. 26:52 *omnes enim, qui acceperint gladium, gladio peribunt*, "for all that take the sword shall perish by the sword"] Heretics pierce through with the sword of the mouth, having first been pierced through with the sword of impiety.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *Delicate.* ⟦s2⟧ The ancients called things consecrated to the gods delicata, which we now call dedicata.

## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And two men. (RAB. in lib. Reg., [0565D] tom. II.)* ⟦s2⟧ These two men were princes in the army of Isboseth, and when Abner was dead, as the Hebrews hand down, they took counsel with Miphiboseth that they should kill Isboseth and set him up as king. ⟦s3⟧ But Miphiboseth betrayed the counsel; therefore it is thus brought in: *Now Jonathan had a son weak in his feet.* ⟦s4⟧ But they, terrified with fear, fled into Gethaim, and were there until that time in which, returning thence, they entered the house of Isboseth.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the portress.* [n: (ID.)] The portress cleanses the wheat; etc., *as far as* lest the enemies penetrate her through the opening of a neglected thought.
