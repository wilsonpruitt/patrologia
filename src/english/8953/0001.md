## CHAPTER V.

VERS. 1.-- *And they came.* [n: (RAB.)] So all the nations [0566A] come together to Christ, and as though caressing him they say: *We are thy bone and thy flesh:* when they love and venerate in him the incarnation which he took from our nature in the womb of the Virgin.

VERS. 3.-- *The ancients.* [n: (ID.)] The prophets and the apostles run together to Christ with equal faith and devotion.

*And they anointed.* [n: (ID.)] David is said to have been anointed twice, and Christ took the sovereignty first among the Jews, afterwards among the Gentiles.

VERS. 4.-- *And forty.* [n: (ID.)] It is asked: why are not forty years and six months reckoned in the sum? etc., *as far as* which consists of four tens, and signifies the perfection of times and of things.

VERS. 5.-- *In Hierusalem.* The Hebrews hand down that Sem the son of Noe, whom they call Melchisedech, founded [0566B] Salem. This city the Jebusaeans afterwards held, from whom it obtained the name Jebus. Thence Hierusalem, as it were Jebus Salem, afterwards by Solomon it was called Jerosolyma, as it were Jerosalomonia, which afterwards was called Aelia by Aelius Adrianus.

VERS. 6.-- *The blind and the lame.* Who are enough against thee. This they said, trusting in their walls. Or thus: thou shalt not enter unless thou take us away, whom, unarmed, thou reckonest as blind and lame, and thinkest to triumph over.

VERS. 7.-- *And he took.* [n: (RAB.)] As Josephus says, etc., *as far as* which is rightly called the citadel of Sion, that is, of contemplation, so that of it may rightly be said: *His place is made in peace,* etc. [n: (Psal. LXXV)].

David took the citadel of Sion, when first the blind and [0566C] the lame that hated the soul of David had been taken away. So the Lord acquired the primacy in the Church of the Gentiles, when his Scribes and Pharisees had been reprobated, who, blind and lame, hated the soul of Christ, that is, endeavoured to take away his life.

VERS. 8.-- *Of the housetops.* That is, of the rulers. He calls conduits of water, made of lead or of a rule, pipes, through which the water went over the wall throughout the whole city.

*Pipes.* They figure false knowledge, or the teachings of heretics, which the Lord destroys through Joab, that is, through the preachers. Jebusaeus is one treading down, this is the Gentile world, which before the faith trod down the commandments of God. Likewise Jebusaeus is one trodden down, because the devil is trodden down by the Lord, and is put under Joab to be trodden

VERS. 9.-- *And he built.* From the place so called, understand [0566D] the wall: and within, understand another wall, that is, he fortified the city with a double wall.

VERS. 11.-- *He sent also.* Hiram, living loftily, this is the Gentile world, lofty in Christ; he sends messengers to David, that is, teachers to Christ, and through them cedar timbers, that is, men uncorrupt and strong; and craftsmen, that is, men by whose skill minds are made uncorrupt, and, like stones, squared with the four virtues, so is the house of David built, that is, the Church of Christ, and it is known that God has confirmed our David king over Israel, that is, over those who see God.

[n: (RAB.)] The churches of heretics, which glory that they abide under the title of the Christian name. But because for the sake [0567A] of carnal gains they follow Christ, they are called not wives but concubines. But now, if kings have many wives or concubines, it is a sin: because the figures have passed away, for the sake of which this was at one time venial.

VERS. 13.-- *And there were born.* If by David Christ is signified, not undeservedly are Christians understood by his sons, who are of the Jews and of the nations, to whom the interpretations of the names agree: Samua, boldness; Sobab, turning back; Jabahar, he chose; Elisua, the salvation of my God; Nepheg, applying to the mouth; Japhia, he enlightens or shows; Elisama, my God hearing; Elioda, my God knows; Eliphalet, my God saving.

VERS. 20.-- *Baal Pharasim.* It is interpreted *the enemies' [0567B] division.* The place can be said to be so called because the Philisthiim left their idols in the place where they were divided.

VERS. 23.-- *Of the pear trees.* In the Hebrew *of the weepers,* that is, of the idols, where, that is, there were idols in which they trusted, which are called weepers, because they are worthy of weeping, and lead their own worshippers to weeping and misery.

*On the top.* [n: (RAB.)] The pear tree is named from the Greek πῦρ, which is fire. The pear trees are the saints: the top is the loftiness of their life; in which, when the Holy Spirit breathed, the sound of preaching thundered. Which is of one walking, that is, the gift of him coming in the flesh, on the top of the pear trees, that is, above the height of the saints, while this sound is heard by our David, the intelligible Philisthaeans [0567C] are overcome.

## CHAPTER VI.

VERS. 1.-- *He gathered together.* [n: (RAB., ex Euch.)] David, Christ, etc., *as far as* they who, coming down from Judaea, taught the brethren: *Unless you be circumcised after the manner of Moses, you cannot be saved [n: (Act. XV)]*.

VERS. 2.-- *Of him that sitteth.* Above the ark was a tablet which two cherubim covered with their wings, between whom the Lord appeared and spoke.

[n: (RAB., ex Greg.)] In the history of David the king and prophet, by which he is said to have brought up the ark of God, humility is shown approved, pride condemned, and rashness avenged. For David, who did not blush to dance humbly before the ark, soon deserved to receive the promise of the Son of God who should be born of [0567D] his stock, and his wife, who despised his humility, paid the penalty of perpetual barrenness. The priest also, who rashly touched the ark, purged the guilt of his own daring by an untimely death. Wherein it is to be considered how greatly he offends who approaches the body of the Lord guilty, if that man is punished with death who handled the ark, the figure of the Lord's body, with less veneration than he ought.

[n: (GREG., l. V Moral., cap. 10.)] Often, because they cannot be understood, etc., *as far as* they should not judge the deeds or words of better men as though they were weak.

VERS. 7.-- *Who died.* [n: (RAB., ex Euch.)] When the priest was dead he did not dare to turn aside the ark, etc., *as far as* the Gethaean can be called the water-people, who says, [0568A] *But God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of the Lord [n: (Gal. VI)]*.

VERS. 11.-- *Three months.* [n: (ID.)] By which, that is, faith, hope and charity are signified, etc., *as far as* and they bear the leadership of the sheep, crowning them in the blood of martyrdom.

*On instruments.* That is, bound to the shoulder, while they are touched by the hands of him that carries them. There is another kind of instrument which is made with water.

VERS. 14.-- *With a linen ephod, and David,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Not with a pontifical but with a linen garment, for humility's sake, etc., *as far as* he triumphed amid scourges.

VERS. 16.-- *And when he had entered the ark.* [var: Vulg. 2 Reg. 6:16 *Cumque intrasset arca Domini in civitatem David*, "and when the ark of the Lord had entered"] [n: (ID.)] While all were exulting and sounding hymns at the entrance of the heavenly citadel, etc., *as far as* because they who receive the word of God [0568B] only as far as the ear, without the offspring of good work, await the day of everlasting death.

VERS. 20.-- *And Michol the daughter of Saul went out to meet David,* etc. [n: (GREG., lib. XXVII Moral., cap. 27.)] It is worth beholding how great gifts of virtues David had received, etc., *as far as* yet within himself he pressed down the neck of his heart with the strong heel of discretion, saying: *And I will be humble in my own eyes.*

*Glorious.* When David was transferring the ark into Jerusalem, he was mocked by Michol. And when Christ was transferring the New Testament into his Church, he was a laughing-stock to the Jews on the cross, he appeared naked, while, hiding his power from them, he showed the infirmity of the flesh as it were a linen ephod. The handmaids of the servants bear the person of the saints, among whom by the triumph of the cross [0568C] he was made more glorious: and while Michol derides him, he appears more glorious to the handmaids, she who in the figure of the Synagogue remained barren.

*And was naked.* Naked, not altogether, but of his royal garments: and because he did not blush to humble himself before the Lord, soon he deserved to receive the promise of the Son of God who should be born of his stock.
