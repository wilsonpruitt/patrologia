## CHAPTER VII.

VERS. 3.-- *All that is in thy heart, go and do. [n: (RAB. in lib. Reg., tom. III.)]* It is plain that the spirit of prophecy does not always irradiate the minds of the prophets. *For the Spirit breatheth where he will [n: (Joan. III)]*. Hence Eliseus says: *The Lord hath hid it from me, and hath not told me [n: (IV Reg. IV)]*. Which God disposes out of the dispensation [0568D] of his loving-kindness: because, while he gives and withdraws the spirit of prophecy, he lifts up the minds of those who prophesy on high, and keeps them in humility, so that, receiving the spirit, they may find what they are of God, and again, not having the spirit of prophecy, they may know what they are of themselves.

[n: (GREG., lib. VII Moral., cap. 17.)] A man ought to be clean in himself from vices, etc., *as far as* he must needs blush to instruct the minds of his neighbours.

VERS. 5.-- *Shalt thou.* [n: (RAB.)] Another translation, etc., *as far as* he shall build me a house.

VERS. 10.-- *And I will appoint a place.* He describes the time of Solomon, in which the children of Israel had peace on every side, and were not afflicted as in the time of the Judges.

[0569A]VERS. 16.-- *And faithful. [n: (RAB., ex Aug. de Civit., lib. XVII, cap. 8.)]* This is truly said of Christ, etc., *as far as* the place therefore of so peaceful and secure a dwelling is eternal, where the true Israel shall be, that is, he that seeth God.

*And he sat before.* To sit before the Lord is to confess his benefits in humility.

*The law of Adam.* [n: (RAB.)] That is, of man, that he may serve thee in simplicity of heart and in purity, and that thou mayest do to him according to thy mercy, as thou hast done to me.

VERS. 23.-- *What is there.* In another land besides the elect land, for whose sake the Son of God came into the world as into Egypt, that by the commerce of his blood he might redeem to himself an acceptable people, a follower of good [0569B] works. But by the God of that same people we understand the Lordly man, whom he took from Egypt, that is, from the world, through the womb of the Virgin.

*Out of Egypt.* The nation and its God, or the nation and its God, the nation Israel, and its God Moses, of whom it is said: *I have appointed thee the God of Pharao [n: (Exod. VII)]*.

VERS. 27.-- *Thy servant hath found.* Nothing is more fleeting than the heart, which is found when it is restrained by discretion.

## CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 1.-- *The bridle.* There were five cities of the Philisthines which made Israel tributary. These David took to himself and made them tributary, as it is read: *David smote the Philisthiim, and humbled them, and took Geth and her daughters out of their hand,* etc. *(I Paral. [0569C] XVIII.)*

*The bridle.* Christ, triumphing over the powers of the air, broke the bridle of error which they had laid upon the human race.

VERS. 2.-- *Making equal.* For men living on the earth were not humbled to such a degree that, when the line was stretched over them, they appeared equal to the earth: but they were so vile and despised that in nothing were they worth more than the earth which has no men. But he put the line for the lot; because it is the custom to measure with a line, according to that word: *and by lot he divided to them the land with the line of distribution [n: (Psal. LXXVII)]*. And it signifies that he divided the regions of the Moabites to whom he would as freely as an owner divides his own fields.

[n: (RAB.)] And it signifies that he was able to kill [0569D] whom he would, and to spare whom he would. But that he made Moab, which is interpreted *from the father,* and Syria of Damascus and the other regions tributary, signifies that Christ holds all the nations under his dominion, and presses them, and exacts the tribute of good works. For Damascus, a noble city of Phoenicia, is called by the same name by which also the son of Maseth the handmaid of Abraham is called, and it is interpreted *the cup of blood.* But Syria, *lofty* or *moist;* for she who before, moist with lust, thirsted insatiably for human blood, now, cleansed by the baptism of Christ, lofty by the increase of virtues, desires the cup of his blood.

VERS. 11.-- *The king sanctified.* So Christ sanctifies to the Lord all whom he calls out of the nations, making some as it were gold, [0570A] that is, spiritual in understanding, others as it were silver, that is, shining with the lustre of eloquence; others as it were brass, sounding in preaching, that they may faithfully serve his tabernacle.

VERS. 13.-- *When he had slain.* [n: (HIERON.)] That in certain codices twelve thousand are said to have been slain has grown in by the fault of the scribes. For in truth David slew eighteen thousand in the valley of the Saltpits. But Joab slew twelve thousand, as is written in the title of the fifty-eighth psalm. And it is signified that Christ, by the strictness of his examination, extinguishes in those who think perversely of him the folly of immoderate labour.

VERS. 16.-- *Recorder.* He held the king's place, by whose judgment the laws were written. The scribe wrote down word by word from the king's mouth.

[0570B]VERS. 17.-- *Priests.* A priest is as it were a sacred leader. But the sons of David held the leadership in the tribe of Juda.

[n: (RAB.)] They sat by the king guarding his body, and they are interpreted *slaying* and *making alive,* since according to their judgment the worthy were reserved for life, and others were led to death.

## CHAPTER IX.

VERS. 1.-- *And David said,* etc. [n: (RAB. in lib. Reg., tom. III.)] Behold, David has not forgotten the oath and the covenant which he made with Jonathas, etc., *as far as* because they come into the admiration of the people who, lately despised in the world, appear glorious in the Church.

## CHAPTER X.

[0570C]VERS. 1, 2.-- *And it came to pass after this, that the king of the children of Ammon died,* etc., *I will show,* etc. [n: (HIERON.)] When David fled from the face of Achis the king of Geth, he came to Naas the king of Ammon, who showed mercy to him. And so from Naas he came to the cave of Odollam, where his father and mother came to him, and all his father's house: thence he came to Moab, and left with him his father and mother and all his household. But as to what Achis the king of Geth said: *Shall this man enter my house? David departed thence and fled into the cave of Odollam [n: (I Reg. XXI)]*, it is to be understood of the house of Naas. If it be asked why he returned to Achis, whom he had fled from before, it is to be understood that the man to whom he returned was the son of him whom he fled. Whence in what follows, concerning that Achis with [0570D] whom David went down into battle, he is said to have been the son of Maoch. For he took this patronymic not from his father but from his mother, who was called Maacha.

VERS. 4.-- *Wherefore Hanon took the servants of David, and shaved off the beard,* [var: Vulg. 2 Reg. 10:4 *rasitque dimidiam partem barbae eorum*, "and shaved off half of their beard"] etc. [n: (RAB., ex Euch.)] These things signify the war of the devil against the Church. For Hanon, that is, their sorrow, signifies the devil, who of the Ammonites, that is, of the malignant spirits, that is, the ruler of a people of mourning and set always in straitness, who desires to press down or to straiten men. Hanon therefore shaves off the half of the beard of David's messengers, when the devil, by corrupting them, stains the speech or the manner of life of certain preachers. He cuts away the tunics as far as the groin, when [0571A] he reveals in the eyes of men the base deeds which he persuades to. For these it is needful that they sit in Jericho until the beard grow, lest they be the reproach of others and the anathema of all, that is, until the increases of the virtues be born in them, and they be held worthy to be presented to their king. But our David does not suffer his soldiers to be unavenged, but, when the army is gathered, he avenges the wrong of his own, and not only does he confound his adversaries through the victory of his saints, but he also delivers them at the last judgment to everlasting fires.
