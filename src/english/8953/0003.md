## CHAPTER XI.

VERS. 1.-- *And it came to pass, at the return of the year, at the time when,* etc. [n: (RAB., ex Hieron.)] That is, in the spring, when, the harshness of the cold being driven away, fodder is found for the beasts of burden, etc., *as far as* but they were overcome.

[0571B]VERS. 2.-- *While these things.* [n: (RAB.)] David sinned grievously, etc., *as far as* who healed so grievous a wound of sin by the confession of humility.

*Bethsabee.* [n: (ISID.)] The well of fullness, or the seventh well. But the Church is called the well of living water, and to this well the name of the sevenfold number is joined in signification of the Holy Spirit, by reason of Pentecost, on which day the Holy Spirit was sent from heaven. To forty-nine, that is, to seven times seven, one is added, since unity is commended. Whence: *Careful to keep the unity of the spirit in the bond of peace [n: (Eph. IV)]*. And so by the spiritual gift, that is, by the sevenfold gift, the Church has been made the well of fullness, because there has been made in her *a fountain of water springing up into life everlasting, whereof he that shall drink shall not thirst for ever [n: (Joan. IV)]*.

[0571C] [n: (GREG.)] Weighed down as we still are by the burden of corruptible flesh, we are not able so to live that no delight of guilt can beat upon us. But it is one thing to be touched unwillingly, and another to consent. But holy men keep themselves with a circumspection so much the more watchful, as they disdain to be beaten upon by sinister motions even in passing, so that, if perchance the heart should covet anything unlawful, the eye, restrained by the mastery of discipline, would refuse to see: for as temptation is often drawn in through the eyes, so sometimes what has been conceived within compels the eye without to serve it. For very often some thing or other is looked upon with an innocent mind, but in the very looking the soul is confounded by the sword of concupiscence. For David did not look upon the wife of Urias of set purpose because he had coveted her, but [0571D] rather he coveted because he looked incautiously. But by the just examination of retribution, he who uses the outward eye negligently is blinded in the inward eye. But often concupiscence already lords it within, and the soul, enticed, exacts after the manner of a tyranny that the bodily senses be servants to its own uses, and compels the eyes to serve its own pleasures, and opens the windows of light unto the darkness of blindness. Whence holy men, when they feel themselves beaten upon with sinister delight, restrain by the mastery of discipline those very lights through which the appearance of a form enters into the mind, lest a seductive seeing should be a servant to a depraved thought: and if this is ever neglected to be subtly guarded, the uncleanness of thought passes over into action.

[0572A] [n: (GREG.)] Often some thing or other is a virtue in the history, a fault in the signification; just as sometimes a fault in the deed is a virtue in the writing of prophecy. For who would not detest that David, walking on the roof, coveted Bethsabee the wife of Urias, whom nevertheless, when he returned from the battle, he admonishes to go home and to wash his feet? And he straightway answered, saying: The ark of God is under skins, and shall I take my rest in my own house? Whom David received at his own table, and gave him the banquet through which he was to die. David therefore holds the figure of him of whom it is written: *He hath set his tabernacle in the sun [n: (Psal. XVIII)]*. Who bids Bethsabee be brought forth to him, that is, to join to himself by a spiritual understanding the law of the letter that was joined to a carnal people. For Bethsabee is called *a well of seven*, because through the kinship of the law, by the pouring in [0572B] of a special grace, perfect wisdom is ministered to us. But Urias signifies the Jewish people, who is interpreted *my light of God:* for the Jewish people, which is lifted up by the knowledge of the law it has received, glories as it were in the light of God. But David took away this man's wife and joined her to himself, because the Redeemer, appearing strong of hand, while he made known that the law speaks spiritually of himself, showed her to be a stranger to the Jewish people by that whereby they were held according to the letter, and joined her to himself, because he declared himself to be preached through her. Yet he admonishes Urias to go home and to wash his feet, because, being incarnate, he commands the Jews to return to their conscience, and to wipe away with weeping the filth of their works; that they may understand the commandments of the law spiritually, and, after so great a hardness of precepts, finding the fountain of baptism, may run to the water [0572C] after their labour. But Urias, who remembers that the ark of the Lord is under skins, answers that he cannot enter his own house. Which is to say: I see the commandments of God in carnal sacrifices, and I do not seek to return to my conscience through spiritual understanding. For he says as it were that the ark of God is under skins, because he understands the precepts of God not otherwise than by performing the ministry of a carnal sacrifice. Yet David calls this man, who is unwilling to go home, to his table, because, while the Jews scorn to return to their conscience, Christ nevertheless preaches spiritual things, saying: *If you did believe Moses, you would perhaps believe me also, for he wrote of me [n: (Joan. V)]*. For the Jew holds the law, which speaks of his divinity, whom he disdains to believe. Whence Urias also is sent to Joab with letters by which he was to be slain, [0572D] because the Jew carries the law by whose conviction he is to die. For while, holding fast the commandments of the law, he strives to fulfil them, he himself carries down the judgment whereby he is condemned. What therefore is more wicked than David by this deed? What more clean than Urias? But as to the mystery: what more holy than David? What more faithless than Urias? Because he, through the fault of his life, marks the innocence of prophecy; and this man, through the innocence of his life, expresses fault in prophecy. For the power of the sacred eloquence so narrates what is past that it expresses what is to come: so approves the thing in the deed that it contradicts it in the mystery. So it condemns things done, that it urges these same things to be done mystically.

VERS. 15-- *Set ye Urias,* etc. [n: (GREG., lib. XXXIII Moral., cap. 10.)] To this end are the sins of David and of Peter [0573A] set down in Scripture, that the ruin of the greater may be the caution of the lesser, etc., *as far as* by a secret order of dispensation therefore, whence the iniquity of the devil is permitted to rage, thence the goodness of God is accomplished, because thence it obeys the biddings of supernal grace, whence it works the wrath of its own will.

## CHAPTER XII.

VERS. 13.-- *And Nathan said to David,* etc. [n: (RAB. ex Euch.)] Yet all the things which were foretold to him for his sin he afterwards endured. God blots out the offence, but does not leave it unavenged. For either a man, repenting, punishes it in himself, or God, taking vengeance for these things, strikes the man. The sin therefore is not spared, because it is not remitted without vengeance. For so David deserved to hear after his confession, *The Lord hath taken away thy sin,* [0573B] and yet, afflicted afterwards with many torments, in escaping he paid off the guilt of the fault which he had committed. So we are absolved by the wave of salvation from the fault of the first parent, but even when absolved we still die carnally, because God cuts away our offences, either through ourselves or through himself, even when he remits them. For he takes pains to wipe away from his elect by temporal affliction the stains of iniquities, which he does not will to see in them for ever.

VERS. 27.-- *And Joab sent messengers,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] This victory of David, which Joab began, etc., *as far as* and in the Gospel it is said, *that many shall come from the East and from the West, and shall sit down with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven [n: (Matth. VIII)]*.

*The city of waters,* etc. Because of the abundance of water which was there, or, as some say, of peoples.

[0573C]VERS. 30.-- *And he took,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] In Paralipomenon it is thus read: *David took the crown of Melchon from his head, and found in it a talent weight of gold, and most precious gems. And he made himself a diadem thereof [n: (I Par. XX)]*. For Melchon is not a proper name, but is interpreted *their king.* Melchon, as the Hebrews will have it, is an idol of the Ammonites, whose diadem of gold and gems David is said to have melted down and purified according to the law, and to have made himself a diadem.
