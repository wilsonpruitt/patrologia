## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 6.-- *Little broths.* It is what is fit for supping up.

VERS. 14.-- *He forced her.* [n: (RAB.)] The incest of Amnon, the elder son of David, upon his sister Thamar, and the parricide [0573D] of Absalon upon his brother Amnon, warns us that we should always act warily, lest vices lord it over us; and lest the prince of sin (who promises a false peace to those in peril) find us unready and butcher us unawares. For Absalon is interpreted *father of peace* or *the father's peace,* Amnon *giving,* Thamar *bitterness.* For he who gives his members to lust, and serves iniquity unto iniquity, falls into the bitterness of sin, although the enemy shows himself as it were a father of peace, and promises prosperous things for such deeds. For it is needful that we return quickly to penance, lest perchance the devil conspire through the malignant spirits unto our slaying, and hand us over to everlasting death.

VERS. 15.-- *And he hated her,* etc. Let us too [0574A] hold in hatred the crime committed, more than we had loved it before the committing, and for this cause we shall the more quickly obtain pardon, if only we do not offend anew in another vice, as Amnon did: who, because after the incest he did not avoid drunkenness, for this cause did not escape death.

VERS. 37.-- *Tholomai.* He was the father of Maacha the mother of Absalom, whom the Hebrews say David took in battle, and, her hair and her nails being cut off according to the law, joined to himself as a wife, and begot of her Thamar and Absalom.

VERS. 39.-- *And he ceased.* In the Hebrew, *the king ceased to go out after Absalom.* For it is related that David wished to go out after Absalom, that he might call him back, but thinking that as often as he saw him he would bring back to memory the death of Amnon, he ceased to go out.

## CHAPTER XIV.

[0574B]

VERS. 6.-- *And thy handmaid,* etc. [n: (HIERON.)] The Hebrews say that this woman truly had two sons, and that they strove for the inheritance, and that the one was slain by the other; yet the woman set herself in the person of David: and the sons in the person of Amnon and Absalom: but the kindred which rises up against the son, in the person of the other sons of David.

VERS. 9.-- *Upon me, my lord.* [n: (ID.)] As though she said: because the one has risen up against the other, let the iniquity be upon me, if indeed it must be; but let there be none upon thee, because thou art without fault, and as I am without fault if one son of mine has been killed by the other, so also art thou, if Absalom killed Amnon who was worthy of death.

## CHAPTER XV.

[0574C]VERS. 1.-- *Therefore after.* For two years he remained in Hierusalem and did not see the king, in the third he was brought in before the king, in the fourth he made himself chariots and horsemen: and when that was done he drove out his father from the kingdom. Nor was he cast out of the kingdom for the seduction and the slaying of the priests, but for adultery and murder, Nathan foretelling all.

VERS. 7.-- *But after forty years. [n: (HIERON., quaest. in Reg., tom. III.)]* The fortieth year was passing since Saul overthrew the city Nobe, etc., *as far as* Isaac and Jacob.

VERS. 24.-- *And they set down the ark,* etc. That God might be consulted through Abiathar the priest, whither David should go.

VERS. 25.-- *Sadoc.* The just man bears the person [0574D] of the saints, who at the time of the Lord's passion departed indeed in body, but were not separated in love.

VERS. 32.-- *Chusai.* The friend of David. Chusai is interpreted *hastening,* who, returning to Jerusalem, scattered the counsel of Achitophel, signifies the hidden disciples of the Lord from among the Jews, Nicodemus, Joseph and the like.

## CHAPTER XVI.

VERS. 9, 10.-- *What is it to me?* Abisai alone had said: *I will go and cut off.* And David answered Abisai and Joab: *What is it to me?* Do you wish to kill this man as you killed Abner?

*Let him alone. [n: (GREG. lib. XXX Moral., cap. 9.)]* He who, [0575A] pressed by the insults of words, cannot or is not able to keep the virtue of patience, etc., *as far as* by whose intervention, God being judge, a heavier punishment is turned aside.

VERS. 13.-- *But Semei.* [n: (RAB.)] This is Nabath the father of Jeroboam according to the Hebrews, who is called the son of Jemini, and in zeal for the house of Saul curses David. But the name of his grandfather was Jemini. This same Semei came of the tribe of Ephraim the son of Joseph, whence he says to David: *I came the first this day of all the tribe of Joseph.* [var: Vulg. 2 Reg. 19:20 *de omni domo Joseph*, "of all the house of Joseph"] But Semei, as is read above, was of the kindred of Saul. And Saul was of Benjamin. In Esdras Semei is read to be the son of Cis, because he was called both Cis and Gera, his father is here read to be the son of Jemini, that is, of Benjamin; but he is said to have come the first of the tribe of Joseph, because he dwelt in the tribe [0575B] of Joseph.

VERS. 15.-- *Achitophel.* [n: (RAB.)] He is interpreted *the ruin of a brother.* Judas, working the ruin of Christ, gave counsel to the Jews against Christ, each of the two perished by hanging.

## CHAPTER XVII.

VERS. 10.-- *And the most valiant.* [n: (HIERON.)] In the Hebrew: *The most valiant himself, whose heart is as a lion, shall be loosed with fear,* understand "not", that most valiant one is understood to be David. Whence: *For he knoweth.*

[n: (ISID. in lib. I Reg.)] According to the allegory it behoves us to consider, etc., *as far as* from whose face Christ fled when he forsook her and passed over to the Gentiles.

VERS. 14.-- *Absalom.* Others take Absalom to be Judas the traitor, whom Christ bore with as though he were ignorant of his [0575C] thoughts, and shared the banquet with him, in which he committed to the disciples the figure of his body and blood. But in the very betrayal he received a kiss from him, therefore Absalom is called *the peace of peace,* because the father had a peace which that man had not.

*Achitophel.* [n: (RAB.)] Who, departing from David, etc., *as far as* just as Achitophel perished by hanging in his own house, who in another's house lay in wait for the life of David. *For he that diggeth a pit falleth into it. And the wicked shall be taken in their own snares [n: (Prov. XI; XXV)]*.

*Chusai.* [n: (ID.)] The Arachite, the old friend of David, etc., *as far as* that they may be converted by the occasion of benefits.

[n: (ID.)] But that Jonathan, who is interpreted *the dove's house,* and Achimaas his brother, appointed to search out the deed of Absalom and to report it to David, [0575D] fleeing from Absalom, etc., *as far as* and men, being protected, are freed from the persecution of the enemy.

## CHAPTER XVIII.

VERS. 9.-- *And it happened that Absalom, sitting on a mule, met the servants of David.* [n: (RAB.)] Absalom, fleeing on a mule, was hanged in an oak by the hair of his head, etc., *as far as* they pass over into the dispersion of the nations, and they themselves lost the sovereignty and the people alike.

## CHAPTER XIX.

VERS. 22.-- *What is it to me.* David feared that, if Semei were killed, the rest who had in like manner offended the king would be unwilling to make him king over themselves.

VERS. 24.-- *Unwashed.* In the Hebrew, *unstained.* For Miphiboseth had made himself wooden feet according to the Hebrew, [0576A] which he used in place of his natural ones: for he was lame.

VERS. 29.-- *Thou and Siba.* [n: (HIERON., quaest. Hebr. in Reg. )] In the Hebrew: *I said, Thou and Siba divide the field.* Which David, unmindful of the covenant and the oath, etc., *as far as* he uttered words, turning them aside, against the Most High.

VERS. 37.-- *And there is.* But it is in the Hebrew: *Behold thy servant* is read in Hebrew *Chime han Chanaan,* Chanaan is interpreted *sighing;* as long as he was with his father he was called sighing: but after he passed over to the teaching of David, he was called not Chanaan but Chamaan, that is, faithful, etc., *as far as: Ephraim and Manasses shall be to me as Ruben and Simeon.* And so the great Moses and Josue his successor [0576B] assigned to them a double lot.

## CHAPTER XX.

VERS. 9.-- *And he took hold,* etc. To hold the chin with the right hand is as it were to flatter out of kindness. But he sends his left hand to the sword, who secretly strikes out of malice.

VERS. 19.-- *Am not I.* [n: (RAB.)] This is the word of the law, in which it is commanded that the children of Israel, when they have entered the land of Chanaan, should first offer peace, and should make tributary those who receive the peace; but should destroy those who do not receive it: therefore the wise woman is said to have cried out *Am not I.* As though: Why dost thou destroy this city before thou offer peace according to the law? why dost thou not keep for the Israelites the law which of old was kept for strangers.

VERS. 22.-- *Who cast forth the severed head.* [n: (ID.)] The return of the people [0576C] to David after the killing of Absalon, etc., *as far as* that is, it returned to Christ the Son of God
