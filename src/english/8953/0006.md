## CHAPTER XXIII.

VERS. 1.-- *These however are.* [n: (HIERON.)] They are the last, because after the psalter and the other measures he is said to have composed this measure, in which he says: *David the son of Isai said: The man said, for whom a ladder was set up unto the Christ [0577B] of the God of Jacob.* [var: Vulg. 2 Reg. 23:1 *cui constitutum est de christo Dei Jacob*, "to whom it was appointed concerning the Christ of the God of Jacob"]

[n: (RAB.)] Because they signify the last praise of confession, which, the enemy being overcome and the victory of death accomplished, the saints shall sing for ever.

*Excellent.* Because he prophesied most brilliantly of the Christ of God and of his incarnation and the redemption of the human race.

VERS. 3.-- *In the fear.* He who exercises dominion over his subjects in the fear of God brings forth works of light illumined by the sun of justice, and nothing dark remains in them, but, watered by the shower of heavenly grace, living in the flesh he brings forth the shoots of the virtues as fruit.

VERS. 5.-- *Nor is there.* [n: (RAB.)] He who meditates in the law of the Lord day and night becomes like a tree which, planted beside the waters, gives its fruit in its own [0577C] time, and whatsoever he shall do shall prosper.

VERS. 7.-- *And if any man.* He, that is, who, communicating in the sins of others because of the hardness of his heart, is pressed down by the weight of iniquities, and is pierced through with the darts of sins, and his works, worthy of eternal fires, shall be brought to nothing.

VERS. 8.-- *These are the names.* [n: (ISID.)] A catalogue of valiant men is woven in the figure of the saints, who, although they advance in the loftiness of the virtues, yet do not reach unto the excellence of the divine Trinity. For this is what is written: Unto the first three he came not. For who among the clouds shall be equal to the Lord among the sons of God?

*Of the valiant.* Of these valiant men Josephus says: The first of all was Eusebius the son of Achimaea, who, frequently breaking into the battle-line [0577D] of the enemy, did not cease until he had killed nine hundred. After him was Eleazar the son of Dodo, who had been with the king in Sarpha. He alone stood fast when the Israelites feared the army of the Philisthines and were fleeing, and killed many, so that with the blood of the slain his sword clave to his right hand. But the Israelites, seeing the Palestines turned to flight, came down from the mountains and pursued them, and a famous victory came to the Israelites. The third was Semaeias the son of Heli, who, in the place of the jawbone, when the Hebrews were fleeing, alone bore the armies of the Palestines, laying some low and turning others to flight.

*He himself.* [n: (RAB.)] The rod from the root of Jesse brought forth a flower without nuptial work, who, from one man bearing the type of the nations, [0578A] by one onset, that is, by the command of a word, drove out a legion of demons.

*The most tender little worm of the wood.* [n: (ID.)] The warlike and modest valour of the man is signified, etc., *as far as* the fortitude in the slaying of eight hundred.

VERS. 11.-- *Semma the son.* Hearing.

*Age of,* etc. Meditating or speaking.

*Arari.* Mountainous, who bears the person of Christ or of his preachers.

VERS. 12.-- *He stood.* [n: (RAB.)] Christ, standing with the Holy Spirit in the field of the Church, protects the weak as barley, or rather he defends with the sword of the mouth the modesty of chastity, as though gleaners, against the incontinent, against luxury.

VERS. 13.-- *Three had gone down.* The three valiant men named [0578B] above, and Jesboam the son of Achamoni, whom Josephus names Eusebius the son of Achimea. And Eleazar the son of Ahohi, whom he says to be the son of Dodo, and Semma the son of Age, whom he calls Semeias the son of Heli. But the Hebrews think that those three were Abisai the son of Sarvia, and Sobochai the Usathite, and Jonathan the son of Sammaa the brother of David, who were mentioned above in the battles of the Philisthines. With which opinion that which follows seems to agree: *Abisai also the brother of Joab the son of Sarvia,* etc. For hence it appears that Abisai was chief and named among the three that follow.

[n: (GREG.)] It is to be considered that he who has committed unlawful things should abstain even from certain lawful things, that thereby he may make satisfaction to his Creator, that he who has committed forbidden things should cut off from himself even things allowed, and should reprehend himself in [0578C] the least things, whom he remembers to have sinned in the greatest. The law of the Old Testament forbids another man's wife to be coveted: but it does not penally forbid that brave things be commanded to soldiers by a king, or that he desire water. But David coveted another man's wife and took her away, and worthy stripes followed upon his fault, and the evil which he committed he corrected by the laments of penance. And when long afterwards he sat over against the squadrons of the enemy, he desired water out of their cistern; and his chosen soldiers, breaking in among the troops of the enemy, brought back unhurt the water which he had desired. But the man, taught by scourges, at once reprehended himself for having desired water at the peril of his soldiers, and pouring it out to the Lord he offered it in libation. For the water poured out was turned into a sacrifice of the Lord, because he slew the fault of concupiscence by [0578D] the penance of his own self-reproof. He therefore who did not fear to covet another man's wife, afterwards was terrified even because he had coveted water.

VERS. 16.-- *But he offered it in libation.* He gave his soldiers an example of fortitude and constancy, overcoming nature so that being thirsty he did not drink, that the army might learn to endure thirst.

But mystically he did not thirst for the water, but he foresaw Christ who was to be born in Bethlehem of a virgin: for whose laver and blood he thirsted. Therefore he did not drink the water, but poured it out. For he thirsted for the eternal fountain which is not sought at the peril of others, but washes away the perils of others.

VERS. 19.-- *The first three.* They will have them understood to be wisdom, humility and fortitude, for David [0579A] sat most wise: he himself was the little worm of the wood, that is, humble, and he killed eight hundred as one valiant; unto these three virtues none of David's valiant men attained.

VERS. 20.-- *And Bana.* Who was master *of Capseel,* that is, of the congregation of God in Cerethi and Pheleti, who are interpreted *killing* and *making alive. He himself slew* those whom Paralipomenon names the two Ariel of Moab. These men therefore were most valiant in the kingdom of Moab, whence they are also called lions. For Ariel is interpreted *the lion of God.*

*In the midst.* Josephus here says more openly what it was, namely, a cistern exceeding deep, which in the time of winter was levelled up with a heap of snow. Into this a lion fell unknowing, and, shut in, cried out with a great roaring. But Banaias, who came with the others to the sight, leapt down into the cistern [0579B] and killed the lion. Banaias, who is interpreted *the Lord is builder,* signifies Christ, who builds the Church. Whence Paul: *You are God's building.* He, in the time of snow and cold, that is, when the grace of Christ shone forth upon the world, and the cold of charity chilled men, killed the lion, that is, the devil. But the Hebrew understands this lion to be Moab, who was slain in the midst of the cistern, that is, in the house of the Lord, where he held the horns of the altar. But the altar is called a cistern, because as the water of a cistern washes and cleanses, so the sanctuary of the Lord purges sins. In the days of snow, because he expiated sin by death. Whence: *Thou shalt wash me,* which was fulfilled in the midst of the cistern; *and I shall be made whiter than snow,* which was fulfilled in that he says *in the days of snow.*

[0579C]VERS. 21.-- *An Egyptian man.* That is, an imitator of the Egyptian who blasphemed God, whom Moses killed at the Lord's bidding, for that man blasphemed God, this man a prophet and a king.

*Worthy.* For he was looked upon as one who, if he went out of Jerusalem, would be killed.

*A spear.* The law of God, on which if he had meditated, remaining in Jerusalem, he would not have perished, but because he despised the king's command, Banaias came down to him with a rod, that is, in the uprightness of justice, and wrested away the spear which he was not holding rightly.

VERS. 23.-- *Who were.* It had moved no one that in the sum not thirty but thirty-seven are read. For thirty and seven are read in this manner. Seven, that is, these valiant men: David, who is called Adeodatus, [0579D] Abisai, Sobochai, Jonathan, Eleazar, Semmaa the son of Agge of Arari, and Banaias, behold, seven. And the thirty are these, Asael, Eleanam and the rest.

VERS. 39.-- *Urias.* He is put last for that which follows: *And the fury of the Lord added,* etc. For vengeance had already been taken upon David and upon his house, but not upon the people, who either did not withstand David or consented.
