## CHAPTER ONE.

[0581A]

VERS. 1.-- *And king David had grown old. [n: (HIERON., epist. ad Nepotianum, tom. I.)]* Does it not seem to thee, if thou follow the killing letter, to be a fiction, or the sports of the Atellan farces? A cold old man is wrapped about with garments, and save by the embrace of a young girl he grows not warm. Bethsabee was yet living, etc., *as far as* and Rahab the harlot, in a figure of the Church, hung out the little net containing the mystery of blood, that when Jericho perished her house might be saved, whence it is said of the saints: *These are they that came of the heat of the house of Rechab [n: (I Par. II)]*. And elsewhere: *I am come to send fire upon the earth [n: (Luc. XII)]*, etc., which fire, kindled in the heart of the disciples, constrained them to say: *Was not our heart burning [n: (Ibid., XXIV)]*?

*And when he was covered,* etc. David suffered cold exceedingly, [0581B] either because he was born of aged parents, or because he had poured out much blood in battle, or rather because, from the time when he saw the angel striking the people, he wasted away with vehement dread, whence he remained in the discomfort of cold even unto death.

VERS. 5.-- *But Adonias.* He signifies the Jewish people, who seems to be the elder son, because he received the law first, and who, despising the Gentile people, which was called later through the grace of God, thought to reign alone with the Lord. For Adonias is interpreted *ruler lord*. But the people of the Church is set in the kingdom in his stead, together with the true Solomon, that is, with our peaceable one, whose body it is, by divine ordinance, and from that kingdom the elder son, that is, the former people, is cast headlong by the desert of his perfidy.

## CHAPTER II.

[0581C]

VERS. 5.-- *Thou also.* David gave charge to Solomon concerning the just requital of those who had been a solace to him, or had unjustly harmed him, whom he himself had patiently borne. And the prophecy indicates what evil or what good must come to pass in time to come, which the true Solomon will fulfil. Yet meanwhile the patience of God often grants that the good be afflicted and the reprobate enjoy delights.

VERS. 19.-- *Bethsabee therefore came to the king.* [n: (RAB.)] Adonias, by the intervention of Bethsabee, etc., *as far as* nor the law which the Jews suppose to be their helper with God.

VERS. 36.-- *And thou shalt not go out,* etc. The true Solomon commands us who dwell in Jerusalem never to [0581D] go out. But if they who were before subject to us have fled from us, let us not on that account go out to follow the footsteps of the fugitives, lest, while we wish to save them that flee, we ourselves perish, but rather let the dead bury their dead, and let us, while it is lawful, pluck out and cut off from us the eye, the hand, the foot that scandalizeth.

## CHAPTER III.

[0582A]

VERS. 4.-- *Solomon therefore went.* In Gabaon was the greatest high place, where was the tabernacle which Moses made, and the brazen altar, upon which Solomon, offering a thousand victims, deserved a divine oracle.

VERS. 14.-- *But if thou wilt walk in my ways,* etc. Without the keeping of God's commandments no man can attain long days, that is, blessedness.

[n: (GREG., lib. VIII Moral., cap. 17.)] After the Lord lights up the darkness of our error with the light of his own knowledge, etc., *as far as* that we may be reformed unto the substance of the incorruption that is promised.

[n: (HIER., epist. 131, tom. I.)] Some think that this is to be understood of the Synagogue and the Church, etc., *as far as* it is also to be noted what the Church saith [0582B] against the Synagogue.

VERS. 17.-- *I and the woman.* Because after the resurrection of the Saviour one Church was gathered out of both peoples. And elegantly he added: *I brought forth beside her in the chamber.* For the Church of the Gentiles, which had not the law and the prophets, brought forth in the house of the synagogue, and went not out of the chamber, but entered in. Whence: *The king brought me into his chamber [n: (Cant. II)]* [var: Vulg. *in cellaria sua*, "into his storerooms" (Cant. 1:3)]. And: *I will bring thee into the house of my mother, and into the chamber of her that conceived me [n: (Ibid., III)]*.

[n: (RAB.)] If thou considerest Pilate washing his hands and saying: *I am clean from the blood of this just man [n: (Matth. XXVII)]* [var: Vulg. *Innocens*, "innocent" (Matt. 27:24)]: and the centurion confessing: *Truly this was the Son of God [n: (Marc. XV)]*, and those who before the passion desired through Philip to see the Lord, [0582C] thou doubtest not that the Church brought forth first; and afterward that the people of the Jews was born, for whom the Lord prayed: *Father, forgive them,* etc., and in one day three thousand believed, and another five thousand.

[n: (GREG.)] By the nursing mothers the order of teachers, etc., *as far as* they themselves receive their sons whole and living, when in the last judgment they obtain a perfect recompense out of their life.

VERS. 19.-- *Dead.* [n: (RAB.)] For while she follows the observance of the law and couples the grace of the Gospel to the law, she is covered over with the error of darkness.

VERS. 20.-- *She took the son.* The living one, that is: not that she might possess him, but that she might kill him. For she did this not out of love of the son, but out of hatred of her rival.

VERS. 21.-- *Whom beholding more diligently,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] [0582D] It were long to show how through Paul and other churchmen the Church understood that he was not her son who was feared in the law, and in the light knew him whom in the darkness she did not see. The two women, of whom the one burned with love, while in the other dissimulation crept in, figure the Church, and the Synagogue or heretical [0583A] depravity, of whom each both slays her own by nourishing them wickedly, and by alluring persuades those of another until she destroy them. Between the two women the Lord, while by the spirit of his mouth he decides what is just, restores to each what is owed.

VERS. 24.-- *He said therefore.* [n: (RAB.)] Feigning ignorance, he measures human affections according to the dispensation of the flesh, as also there: *Where have you laid him [n: (Joan. XX)]*? And elsewhere: *Who hath touched me [n: (Luc. VIII)]*?

VERS. 25.-- *Divide.* [n: (ID.)] The Lord tries nature by nature, and wills, according to the will of them both, to divide the living son into law and grace: not that he approves it, but he says that he wills this in order to convict the calumny of the Synagogue.

VERS. 26.-- *But she said.* [n: (ID.)] The Church, who [0583B] knows him to be her own, willingly yields him to her rival, that he may live at least with her, lest, divided between law and grace, he be smitten with the sword of Christ, whence: *Behold, I Paul say to you, that if you observe the law, Christ shall profit you nothing [n: (Galat. V)]* [var: Vulg. *si circumcidamini*, "if you be circumcised" (Gal. 5:2)].

## CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 22.-- *The food of Solomon.* [n: (RAB.)] The refreshment of Christ, who is fed with right faith and with good works, which are daily offered to him by the Catholic Church.

VERS. 23.-- *Twenty oxen.* [n: (ID.)] The preachers of the New Testament, who have as it were a doubled Decalogue in themselves, when they preach the New and the Old Testament equally, being full of the love of God and of their neighbour.

*Besides the venison.* By the hunting of divers animals [0583C] are meant those who out of divers nations are daily taken by the nets of the Gospel through spiritual hunters, and are brought in for the food of the Saviour.

VERS. 26.-- *Forty,* etc. By forty are designated the seasons of the ancient law, which Moses fasted forty days that he might receive, and the people that was instructed in it was forty years in the desert.

*And twelve.* This number signifies the present time, in which the apostolic doctrine of the New Testament nourishes the people.

VERS. 28.-- *Barley,* etc. That nothing be wanting in the house of the king, the order of preachers labours by writing and by speaking, that there may be abundance upon the table of the Lord, whence the faithful shall be nourished.

[0583D]VERS. 32.-- *And his canticles were.* It is agreed that neither the canticles nor the disputations of Solomon are extant today.

*Five thousand.* [var: Vulg. *quinque et mille*, "a thousand and five" (III Reg. 4:32)] Because of the five senses, whereof he who governs them well in divers virtues is blessed, and through them sings as it were five thousand canticles to the Lord.

VERS. 33.-- *Hyssop.* [n: (RAB.)] Hyssop, a lowly herb and cleaving to the rock, etc., *as far as* for then he bowed down the cedar, that is, the arrogance of the world, even to the humility of hyssop, that is, even to the foolishness of the cross.

*And he treated.* [n: (ID.)] *For all things are naked and open to Christ [n: (Hebr. IV)]* [var: Vulg. *oculis ejus*, "to his eyes" (Heb. 4:13)]. And he disputes when he sets before us the reason of each several thing in his Scriptures, [0584A] and makes manifest the sacraments which were hidden from ages and generations, that we may consider the blessedness of the angels, the usefulness of men, the cunning of the demons, and, reproving what is evil, may follow after what is good.
