## CHAPTER VII.

[0593B]

*But the house of the woman vi. [n: (BED., lib. de templo Salom., cap. 17.)]* Of the Church, that is, of the present Church, for which Christ her husband rose again when he had tasted death, etc., *as far as* For brass is very lasting and full of sound.

VERS. 14.-- *He made all the work.* Because the holy teachers, while they faithfully persevere in the ministry of the word, work the work of God, because by speaking outwardly they open the way of truth to those whom he himself, by enlightening them within, has foreordained unto eternal life. Whence: *I have planted, Apollo watered, but the Lord gave the increase [n: (II Cor. III)]*.

VERS. 15.-- *And he fashioned. [n: (BED. ubi supra, cap. 18.)]* The pillar before the doors of the temple is eighteen cubits high, etc., *as far as* that in any part we may turn aside from the straight way by which we walk toward the promised [0593C] country.

*Ten,* etc. For three times six make eighteen: the three, that is, pertain to faith because of the Trinity; the six to work, because in six days the world was made, the three are multiplied by six, when the just man lives by faith and heaps up the knowledge of pious faith by the performance of good action.

*And a line. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)]* The rule of the apostolic institution encompasses both pillars, when the teacher sent to preach to Jews or to Gentiles takes care to do and to teach those things only which the Church received and learned through the apostles. For he who shall have willed to teach, to live or to preach otherwise, and to despise the apostolic decrees or to establish new ones at his own pleasure, is not a pillar in the temple of God: because while he scorns to follow [0593D] the apostolic institutions, he agrees not with the line of twelve cubits, either by the thinness of sloth or by the thickness of pride.

VERS. 16.-- *Two,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] The heads of the pillars, etc., *as far as* the grace of evangelical perfection is implanted.

VERS. 17.-- *Seven apiece,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] This is thus written in Paralipomenon, etc., *as far as* because the fathers of both testaments received through the grace of the one sevenfold Spirit that they should be elect.

VERS. 18.-- *And two.* [n: (ID.)] The pomegranates seem to have been made round about the capitals from the lower part, and the nettings to rise out of the pomegranates themselves: whereby the capitals were covered in some part, it is signified that virtues [0594A] are born of virtues, and that the saints *walk from virtue unto virtue [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)]*.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] There were two rows of nettings round about the capital, etc., *as far as* and in those things which we understand it lies widely open.

*That they might cover. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Since it has been said of the nettings, etc., *as far as* it is fitly subjoined: *And the capitals that were upon the tops of the pillars,* etc.

*Of pomegranates.* Which with one rind without enclose many grains within, etc., *as far as* but the grace of faith, of hope and of love, and of the other goods of the mind, which is within, is not discerned.

VERS. 19.-- *As it were,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] By the lilies is designated the brightness of the country above, and the pleasantness of paradise fragrant with the flowers of immortality, etc., *as far [0594B] as* because not except through the Gospel did that longed-for voice sound to the world, *Do penance,* etc. [n: (Matth. III)]. *In the porch.* And it is to be noted in this sentence of Paralipomenon that the same porch of the temple is also called the vestibule of the temple. Whence: *Between the vestibule and the altar they wept;* that is, between the porch and the altar.

VERS. 20.-- *And again [n: (BED. ubi supra.)]* Whose making, that is, designates the loftiness of the everlasting kingdom, etc., *as far as* but because that fellowship is granted to the faithful of both peoples, it is rightly subjoined: *Of pomegranates. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* In the pomegranate he signifies the whole Church, etc., *as far as* and they gather the elect of both peoples into the one citadel of blessedness.

[0594C] *The pillar. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The right pillar signifies the teachers of the primitive Church, etc., *as far as* nothing of diligence at least for the understanding of the errors of those over whom they are set.

*He made also. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* This sea was made for a figure of baptism, etc., *as far as* when he foretold that this was to be given to them that labour in the vineyard.

VERS. 23.-- *From brim to brim.* Because from the first baptized in the name of Jesus Christ even to the last who at the end of the world shall believe and be baptized, the whole choir of the faithful ought to enter upon the same way of truth, and to hope for a common crown of justice from the Lord.

*Of five cubits.* Because whatsoever we offend in by sight, hearing, taste, smell and touch, the grace of God releases to us [0594D] through the washing of the life-giving font. But the remission of past sins does not suffice, unless a man thereafter be zealous for good works; otherwise the devil who had gone out of the man, if he shall see him to be empty of good works, returns the more manifoldly, and makes the last things of that man worse than the first; whence it is subjoined:

*And a little cord of thirty cubits encompassed it.* The discipline of the heavenly precepts, by which we are bound back from pleasures; whence: *A threefold cord is hardly broken [n: (Eccl. IV)]*, because the observance of the commandments, which is made firm in the hearts of the elect by faith, by love, and by hope of the recompense above, can be loosed by no obstacle. The little cord goes about the sea, when we are zealous to fortify with pious works the sacrament of baptism which we have received. This [0595A] is of thirty cubits, for five times six make thirty. And by the senary number, in which the Lord made man when he was not, and remade him when he had perished, our good working is figured. And six are multiplied by five, that they may come to thirty, when all the senses of the body are subjected to the divine commands.

Otherwise: three times ten make thirty, and the human race after the flood, from the offspring of the three sons of Noe, filled the breadth of the whole world. For Sem obtained Asia, Cham Africa, and the offspring of Japheth Europe and the islands of the sea. And because the mystery of baptism, together with the performance of works and the hope of heavenly things, was to be ministered to all nations, a little cord of thirty cubits encompassed the sea in which baptism was figured. But here too it must be said that the Lord, when he was thirty [0595B] years old, came to baptism, and because by his own baptism, which he received in his thirtieth year, he consecrated our baptism, rightly did a cord of thirty cubits go about the sea which figured our baptism, that it might be signified that by the gift of him who underwent baptism without sin, baptism is dedicated to us who believe in him unto the remission of sins.

*And a carving.* Since it has been said before that a little cord of thirty cubits went round the sea, and it is now added that this carving, set beneath the brim, went about by ten cubits, it is plain that the vessel was spread out and widened after the manner of a phial, which from the thirty cubits of circuit that it had at the brim was drawn in to ten. Now a storied carving is one which imitates [0595C] certain histories of things. Whence by the storied carvings with which the sea is surrounded are marked the examples of former times, which are to be beheld by us, that we may see by what works the saints from the beginning pleased God, with what obstinacy the wicked persisted in their crimes, with how great unhappiness the reprobate perished. How at the beginning of the world's birth Cain was condemned for the malice of envy, Abel crowned by the merit of justice, Lamech cursed for adultery and murder, Enoch brought back to paradise for the grace of piety, Cham after the flood detested for impiety, Sem and Japheth endowed with perpetual blessing for the obedience of reverence, Abraham made heir of the divine promise by the merit of faith, and the rest of the multitude of the nations left behind for unbelief. At the coming also in the flesh of [0595D] the Lord, Judaea was cast off for her perfidy, the Gentile world brought back by grace unto salvation, and things of this kind, which, diligently considered in both testaments, profit the studious. Therefore perhaps there are two rows of storied carvings in the brazen sea, that they who have been steeped in the font of baptism may hearken to the histories of both testaments: therefore they were of ten cubits round about, that whomsoever in those same histories they shall have looked upon as given up to obeying the heavenly commands, and held suspended with their whole intent toward the rewards above, they may strive to imitate.

VERS. 25.-- *Twelve. [n: (BED. ubi supra)]*. The Apostle, the evangelists, nay rather all the ministers of the word; whence: *Thou shalt not muzzle the mouth of the ox that treadeth out the corn,* etc. [n: (I Cor. IX)]. These bear the sea set upon them, when the apostles [0596A] and their successors fulfil with prompt devotion the office of the Gospel enjoined upon them.

*Of which three.* Because in all the parts of the four-square world they preach the faith of the Trinity. Hence also twelve apostles, that is, four times three, were chosen, that, evangelizing the faith and confession of the Trinity through the four quarters of the world, they might baptize all nations in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit. Whose words, deeds and sufferings, and those of their successors, we can easily see and know in the present by reading. But what glory of recompense awaits them in time to come we cannot yet see.

VERS. 26.-- *The thickness. [n: (BED. ubi supra.)]* He who receives the laver of baptism unto salvation and life ought to have faith and hope and charity, without which no man [0596B] can work rightly; whence it is subjoined, *The thickness.* Because the receiving of baptism is fortified by the strength of faith, of hope and of charity, nor is it shown to be profitable to them that receive it otherwise, unless the firm certainty of these virtues confirm the mind and the bodies of the receivers.

*Of a lily. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The lily, with the grace of its odour accompanying it, a white colour without, etc., *as far as* whence it is subjoined:

*It held two thousand baths, and three thousand,* etc. [n: (ID., ibid.)] The bath is a measure of the Hebrews, etc., *as far as* he makes them partakers of the same kingdom.

VERS. 27.-- [n: (ID., ibid.)] A thousand signifies perfection, because it makes the denary squared into a solid. For ten times ten make a hundred, which figure is already four-square, but as yet flat. But that it may [0596C] rise into height and be made solid, multiply a hundred by ten, and there are made a thousand. By which number the stable and as it were four-square conscience of the just is marked. For whithersoever you set down a square, it will stand, so the mind of the elect is bent by no temptation from the state of rectitude.

*And he made. [n: (ID., ibid., cap. 20.)]* In many ways and in many manners the same sacraments of our salvation are figured, etc., *as far as* he made them glow with divine love.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] That ten bases were made for the carrying of the lavers, etc., *as far as* but calling upon God the Father by one and the same name, because of the gift of the one Spirit.

*Of four cubits in length. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The length and breadth of the bases was of four cubits each, because the preachers, whether they bear outwardly the adversities of the world and [0596D] the length of exile and of present labours, or enlarge the heart in the love of God and of their neighbour with inward exultation, are always zealous for the virtues, prudently, that is, discerning between good and evil, bravely enduring adversities, temperately restraining the heart from the appetite of pleasures, and holding justice in their working.

VERS. 28.-- *And the work itself of the bases.* The boards of which the bases were made were four-square, and in them were round shapes, which are called little crowns or plaited work, in the midst of which were carvings of lions, etc.

VERS. 29.-- *Between the little crowns also and the plaited work, lions and oxen and cherubim. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* The surface of the bases was not plain on any [0597A] part, etc., *as far as* whence here also, after the carvings of the cherubim, it is well subjoined:

*And beneath, lions.* Because the teachers, both in the severity of strictness whereby they judge sinners, and in the meekness of gentleness whereby they remit to the penitent, fear the judgment of God, lest by binding and loosing unjustly they be justly bound by him whose judgment cannot err.

VERS. 30.-- *And four wheels. [n: (BEDA, ibid.)]* The four books of the Gospels, etc., *as far as* they lift them up from the lowest things and, as when wheels are put on, the axles raise the base higher from the ground.
