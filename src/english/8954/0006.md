## CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 6.-- *Into the holy place. [n: (BED., lib. de Templo Salom.)]* The outer house of the temple designates the pilgrim Church, the holy of holies the felicity of the heavenly country above. The ark brought into the holy of holies, the assumed humanity of Christ led within the veil of the heavenly court.

*Under the wings of the cherubim.* [n: (ID.)] Moses made two golden cherubim, which he set upon the propitiatory that was over the ark. Solomon added two greater ones, under whose wings he is now said to have placed the ark, with the propitiatory and the two former cherubim.

[0600A]VERS. 8.-- *And when the staves stood out.* [n: (ID.)] This more manifestly, etc., *as far as* if the staves, projecting beyond the door, did not give place to the doors that were to be drawn forward for shutting.

*Who also were there.* In Paralipomenon it is said: *The ark hath therefore been there until this present day:* which was added by the historian, signifying that it remained there down to the times of his own age, which cannot be understood of Esdras, because the temple had already been burnt, but of Nathan or some one of the prophets, by whom all the deeds of their own times are believed to have been written.

VERS. 9.-- *In the ark.* [n: (RAB.)] There was in the ark a golden urn having manna, etc., *as far as* because the citizens of the country above alone there fully contemplate the glory of the Redeemer.

[0600B]VERS. 10.-- *A cloud filled the house of the Lord, and they could not.* [n: (ID.)] That is, it filled the synagogue, because the darkness of unbelief filled their minds, and the priests could not minister because of the cloud, because, while they despise to search out the mystical senses covered with the veil of the letter, and the sacraments of Christ's nativity, they lost the ministry due to their faith through the cloud of error, so that, their deserts exacting it, they do not acknowledge the worship of belief, they to whom the Lord in the cloud of his own teaching narrated even open things concerning himself. But because the darkness of unbelief had filled the minds of the hearers, an intervening cloud hid, as it were, the ray of the sun that was sent forth.

VERS. 55.-- *He stood.* In Paralipomenon there is related [0600C] what is not said here: *Solomon had made a brazen base, and set it in the midst of the basilica, standing upon which he blessed all the Church of Israel.* [var: Vulg. 2 Par. 6:13 *stetitque super eam, et deinceps flexis genibus contra universam multitudinem Israel*, "and he stood upon it, and then, kneeling down before the whole multitude of Israel"]

*And he blessed.* [n: (RAB.)] The temple of the Lord, etc., *as far as* and by works of virtue they profit their neighbours.

VERS. 64.-- *In that day. [n: (RAB. in lib. Reg., tom. III.)]* What is it that Solomon built the middle of the court, offering there, etc., *as far as* acceptable sacrifices are daily offered spiritually to God.

*The altar.* That which Moses made was set before the temple over against its doors, and because it was narrow, and was not able to contain, he sanctified the middle of the court under the open sky, where he set that greatest altar which he had made of twenty cubits.

VERS. 65.-- *A festival. [n: (RAB. ubi supra.)]* In [0600D] the Words of the Days, etc., *as far as* which Josephus also attests.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] The solemnity which Solomon and all Israel made, etc., *as far as* which the Father hath conferred upon Christians in Christ.

*From the entrance of Emath. [n: (RAB., ibid.)]* By the entrance of Emath he designates the northern region of Judaea, by the river or torrent of Egypt the southern. But this river is not the Nile, but another stream which flows into the sea beside Rivocorula. And Emath is a city of Syria. It is now called Epiphania, from Antiochus Epiphanes.

VERS. 66.-- *Rejoicing.* Chanting hymns with delight, so that in that gladness they returned to their own homes without labour.

## CHAPTER IX.

[0601A]

VERS. 11.-- *Furnishing to the king. [n: (RAB. ubi supra.)]* Much gold and silver, etc., *as far as* because they would not bring forth the bud with fulness of fruits.

VERS. 17.-- *He built.* [n: (RAB.)] The cities which he is said to have built, etc., *as far as* that they may stand forth unconquerable to spiritual enemies.

[n: (RAB.)] The cities which Solomon built were of the remnants of the Amorrhites, which the children of Israel could not destroy, as is read in Jesus son of Nave [n: (Jos. IX)]: these the king of Egypt afterwards overthrew and gave as a dowry to his daughter, the wife of Solomon, and therefore Solomon rebuilt them.

VERS. 18.-- *And Palmira.* An uninhabitable region, for [0601B] that nowhere was water found; but in that place where the city was built, springs abound, and the palm, which in Greek are called Palmira. It is itself Emath, or Epiphania or Antioch.

VERS. 20.-- *The whole people. [n: (RAB. ubi supra.)]* He who had not been of the children of Israel, our peaceable one made tributaries, together with those who are not in the number of the sons; but he uses them in a servile condition for his own service. Such men, although in many things they are opposed, yet often serve the uses of the Church, when in the present time they furnish comforts out of their own goods.

*Of the children of Israel. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Those who are sons not of the bondwoman but of the free, etc., *as far as* and may lead them into the paths of justice.

VERS. 23.-- *Fifty.* [n: (ID.)] On the fiftieth [0601C] day after the Pasch both the law was given on Mount Sina, and the Paraclete Spirit came upon the disciples. What therefore does this number of overseers signify, unless that they who by the grace of the Holy Ghost deserve to have knowledge of the law of the Lord, they themselves are able to rule well both themselves and others?

VERS. 25.-- *He offered also.* [n: (ID.)] Our peaceable one offers, etc., *as far as* and asks those things which are pleasing to God.

VERS. 26.-- *A fleet.* [n: (ID.)] The fleet is the Church, etc., *as far as* history, allegory, tropology, anagoge in the two Testaments.

VERS. 28--. *In Ophir.* [n: (ID.)] Ophir is the name of a province in India. It is named from Ophir, one of the posterity of Heber, and it is also called the golden land, because it has [0601D] golden mountains, which are inhabited by lions and most savage beasts. To which none dares to approach otherwise, save such as, standing in a ship near the shore, take into their own ship the earth which they have found dug out by the claws of the lions, so that if the beasts perceive them, they may easily be received back into the sea.
