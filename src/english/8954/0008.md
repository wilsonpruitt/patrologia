## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- *Behold a man of God.* Whose name is here kept silent, in [0604B] Paralipomenon he is called or named Gaddo. He is that Gad who admonished David to go into the land of Juda. [n: (RAB.)] A prophet sent by God, etc., *as far as* pardon might be granted to the penitent.

VERS. 11.-- *A prophet.* This false prophet was chief of the priests, and was greatly honoured by the king as one divine; but fearing lest through the word of the man of God the king should withdraw from the worship of idols, and should kill him as a malefactor, he devised how he might deceive the man of God, acting cunningly enough, that while he transgressed the precept of God he might incur his anger, as afterwards he did, when he believed a man rather than God. And through this the things which he had foretold might be shown to be false, and so they might be able to persuade the king, as he did. But the king, hearing the event, thought void the things which the man of God had said, and [0604C] did not turn aside from his evil way.

*All the works,* etc. [n: (GREG.)] In the deeds of the prophet marvellous things go before, wretched things follow after: in which matter what do we gather? unless that, having gloried within himself because of the great works, he was straightway shaken from his inward solidity, and thence a fault crept upon him in his work, whence glory had crept upon him in his heart, so that, deceived, he might learn from a false prophet that what had happened before was in no wise of his own strength.

VERS. 21.-- *Thus saith the Lord.* [n: (ID.)] And rightly did he receive the sentence of death by the mouth of him by whose leading back he strayed from the precepts of life, that from thence he might truly take his punishment, whence he had negligently admitted his fault.

VERS. 24.-- *And the lion stood,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Here it is shown, [0604D] that the sin of disobedience was loosed in death itself, because this same lion, which presumed to kill him living, dared not touch him slain. For he that had the power of killing received not licence to eat of the corpse of the slain, because he whose fault had been punished in life was now just by death.

VERS. 31.-- *Bury me,* etc. The evil prophet, knowing that the things which the man of God had foretold would come to pass, commanded that he should be buried beside the man of God, lest at any time his bones should be dug up to be burnt, but should be preserved, through the holy prophet, as the same book testifies below to have been fulfilled.

## CHAPTER XIV.

[0605A]

VERS. 25.-- *Sesac came up,* etc. There followed the king of Egypt a thousand and two hundred chariots, sixty thousand horsemen, and forty thousand footmen, of whom Libya and the Ethiopians had very many. And so, invading the region of the Hebrews, he held the strongest cities without a battle, etc.

## CHAPTER XV.

VERS. 6.-- *Yet there was war between Abia and Jeroboam all,* etc. [var: Vulg. III Reg. 15:6 has *inter Roboam et Jeroboam omni tempore vitae ejus*, "between Roboam and Jeroboam all the time of his life"] Jeroboam, having gathered a great army, pitched his camp against Abia the son of Roboam, who succeeded his father in two tribes. Which when Abia had heard, he was astonished; but being strengthened, with a chosen army he met Jeroboam in the place which is called [0605B] the Mount of the Amorrhites. And when he had made a speech to the army of Jeroboam, at last he said: Wherein are you confident of victory? Is it in golden calves and altars, which are the tokens of your impiety and not of religion? Or hath the multitude afforded you hope? But there is no strength there, where (though with many thousands) though one fights unjustly. For only in justice and piety does the hope of victory consist, which is assuredly with us, keeping the laws from the beginning and worshipping the true God, etc. But God dissolved the boldness of Jeroboam's army; and made the army of Abia the higher. For, so great a slaughter as is related to have been committed in no war, neither of the Greeks nor of the Barbarians, they obtained a marvellous victory, God conferring it. For five hundred thousand of the enemy were laid low [0605C] in the battle, etc.

## CHAPTER XVI.

VERS. 1.-- *And the word was made,* etc. Mention of this prophet is made in no other place, but he is brought in suddenly like the others, whence Abdias is said to have fed a hundred prophets in caves. And Elias saith: *They have slain thy prophets.* And the sons of the prophets are read to have met Eliseus, of whom no other mention is made, no special naming.

VERS. 3.-- *Behold. [n: (RAB. in lib. Reg., tom. III.)]* The Lord cuts down the posterity of this man or of any wicked man whatsoever, when he avenges his sins after the end of his life. He cuts down also the posterity of his house, when he condemns the imitator to eternal torments. Whosoever therefore perseveres in depraved works even to the end of his life, his posterity [0605D] shall be cut down.

VERS. 4.-- *He that shall die.* [n: (ID.)] Of Baasa, that is, of the body of the devil most full of confusion, etc., *as far as* they hurry away with them to eternal destruction.

VERS. 6.-- *In Thersa reign.* [n: (RAB.)] Six years he spent in Thersa, but the rest in the city which is called Semeron, but by the Greeks Samaria. He named it Semeron from Semer its former lord, and it is the metropolis of the kingdom, and afterwards the whole region is called Samaria from him, which is now called Sebaste, where the bones of blessed John the Baptist rest.

VERS. 34.-- *Hiel built.* [n: (RAB.)] Hiel, *living to God;* Bethel, *the house of God* is interpreted, he restored the walls of Jericho, destroyed by [0606A] Josue and laid under anathema. When any one of those who in the Church had taken up the habit of religion returns to the doing of the crimes which the Lord Jesus had forgiven him on the day of his baptism, and by living luxuriously seeks again the pomps of the devil which he himself had anathematized, and when he prefers the doctrines of errors or the fables of the Gentiles to the ecclesiastical truth wherewith he was imbued, going forth as it were from Bethel, he raises up again the ruins of Jericho. He loses both the foundations of faith from which he had begun good buildings, and the enclosures of good action by which he ought to have been perfected.

*In Abiram.* [n: (ID.)] When the founder of Jericho was laying the foundations, his firstborn, Abiram by name, died. When he was fortifying the gates, he lost Segub his youngest son, according to the imprecation [0606B] of Josue.

## CHAPTER XVII.

VERS. 1.-- *And he said.* [n: (RAB.)] Elias is interpreted *My God the Lord,* or *My God,* or *Strong God,* and signifies Christ. Thesbites is interpreted *taking captive* or *converting*: and of the Lord it is written: *Turn,* O Lord, *our captivity.* As with Melchisedech, so let the origin of Elias be kept silent, since his name is brought in suddenly, and this in prefiguration of the Saviour.

*In whose sight.* [n: (ID.)] The just man stands in the sight of the Lord now, and shall stand also in time to come, now, that he may bring back the power of his Creator to his mind, and so live faithfully, in time to come, that he may truly receive the crown of life.

[0606C]VERS. 3.-- *Hide thyself.* [n: (RAB.)] Our Elias was hidden etc., *as far as* and the obedience which was shown to the Lord by the faithful of the Jews.

*By the torrent of Carith.* The torrent Cyson, which is also called Carith, is interpreted *knowledge* or *cutting off* or *division,* agrees with the Jewish people.

VERS. 10.-- *A woman.* [n: (ISID.)] The widow whom the Lord feeds not with the bread of wheat but of the word is she who, her husband being dead, may marry whom she will, that is, having shaken off the yoke of servitude, takes upon her the sweet yoke of Christ, and the light burden.

[n: (RAB.)] This widow signifies the Church of the Gentiles, etc., *as far as* it is said not to have failed.

[n: (ID.)] The widow with whom Elias lodged, and whose meal and oil he blessed, is the synagogue of the Jews, [0606D] which, widowed by the death of Moses, was nourishing a little son, that is, the carnal people of the Jews. He, at the coming in of Elias, that is, of our Saviour, was sick of the fever of unbelief, because he believed not in him, but despised him. Whence the mother Synagogue complains of the coming of the Saviour, as though he himself were the cause of the slaying of the people, saying: *What have I to do with thee,* etc. Hence it is that Caiphas saith: *It is expedient for you that one die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not [n: (Joan. XI)]*.

[n: (ID.)] As the prophet was not exasperated by the words of the widow, but rather had compassion, so our Redeemer is not exasperated by the malice of the Jews, but having compassion on his people took him from his mother's bosom, when he took him [0607A] from the carnal observance of the law, and laid him upon his own bed, that is, showed him that he had life in his death, and stretched himself three times, while he instilled the faith of the Trinity.

VERS. 19.-- *And Elias took the child.* [n: (RAB.)] Elias took the boy and restored him to his mother, when our Lord, saving the believing people, showed signs and miracles to the unbelieving, that they might know that he truly lives who believes in his name, and so it shall come to pass that the Jewish people, overcome by the truth, shall cry out to Christ - *Now in this I know that thou art a man of God.*
