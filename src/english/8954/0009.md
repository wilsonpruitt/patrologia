## CHAPTER XVIII.

VERS. 3.-- *And Abdias*. Abdias, prince of the army of Israel, whose name is interpreted the servant of the Lord, [0607B] prefigured those who of the princes of the Jews believed, and were secret disciples, as Nicodemus and Joseph.

VERS. 30.-- *He repaired the altar.* [n: (RAB.)] And our Redeemer, contending against the prince of the world and his satellites and overcoming them, purging the altar of the Lord which had been destroyed, that is, the hearts of the faithful, from the stain of iniquity, dedicates an altar to God, which is recorded to have been built of twelve stones, because out of those who follow the prophetic and apostolic faith or doctrine a most acceptable altar is built to God, in which the sacrifice of praise is offered for an odour of sweetness.

VERS. 32.-- *And he made a trench.* [n: (ID.)] Namely, out of a contrite and humbled heart, etc., *as far as* we may sing rejoicing for ever: *The Lord he is God.*

[0607C]VERS. 40.-- *Take the prophets of Baal.* [n: (RAB.)] So our Redeemer, when the day of judgment comes, will send his angels and they shall gather all scandals out of his kingdom and shall cast them into the pool of fire, where they shall be tormented according to their hardness and impenitent heart.

VERS. 41.-- *Of a great rain.* [n: (ISID.)] When Christ the Son of God was born among men and took the tenderness of our flesh, after he had tasted death, and as conqueror ascended from the world to the heavens, he sent from on high to the earth the shower of divine grace through the sevenfold Spirit, which should cleanse us from sin, and make us bring forth spiritual fruits.

[n: (RAB.)] In the virtues of Elias, who was strong by the power of God, in his infirmities he acknowledged what he was able of himself. [0607D] There he showed what he had received, here he guarded what he had received. In miracles he was displayed, in infirmities he was preserved.

## CHAPTER XIX.

VERS. 3.-- *Elias therefore was afraid, and rising up he went away,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Holy men, the spirit lifting them up, are caught away to the highest things: but so long as they are in this life, lest they be proud, they are held down by temptations. Hence it is that Elias, when he had gone forward with so many virtues, afterwards fled from Jezabel, a queen indeed, yet a mere woman: and he who raised the dead, foresaw things to come, did other glorious things also, struck with fear, flees death from the hand of a woman, seeks death from the hand of God, and receives it not.

[0608A]VERS. 5.-- *And behold an angel of the Lord touched him.* [n: (RAB.)] The angel who fed Elias signifies the angel of great counsel; by whose work we subsist both in bodily and in spiritual nature.

VERS. 6.-- *And behold at his head,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Elias twice fed expresses the infirmity of our nature, for whom a single feeding does not suffice, but a double, that we may be able to ascend to things above. For as the body cannot subsist without bodily food, so neither can the soul live without the word of God.

[n: (ID.)] So we also, when we shake off from us the sleep of sloth, must needs, being strengthened by divine comfort, hasten with all our might, by the step of good works, through all the time of the present life, to ascend into the mountain of God and into his holy place, that there we may find [0608B] eternal rest.

VERS. 11.-- *And a wind.* [n: (ID.)] A wind before the Lord overthrows mountains and breaks rocks in pieces, etc., *as far as* to begin to go forth to the knowledge of the truth.

*Not in the wind.* That is, think not that the Lord comes with the wind, but after the wind of commotion, that is, the whirlwind and the rush of the wind, fire shall pass by, then a whistling of a thin air, after this the Lord shall pass by, that he may speak with thee.

VERS. 14.-- *I only,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] What was hard for so great a prophet to know, that servants had remained to God in this world? But he who, being humble, had known even the hidden things of God, being lifted up knew not even the open things. Whence it is certain that he enlightens himself with a ray of humility who subtly weighs the good things of others: because while [0608C] he beholds outwardly, and in others, the things which he himself has done, he presses down that swelling of pride which strives to break forth within out of singularity. Hence it is that by the voice of God it is said to Elias, who reckoned himself alone. *I have left me seven thousand men,* [var: Vulg. III Reg. 19:18 *Et derelinquam mihi in Israel septem millia virorum*, "And I will leave me seven thousand men in Israel"] that while he knew that not he alone had remained, he might bow down the glory of elation which was rising in him out of singularity.

[n: (RAB.)] Not otherwise, save that he foretold that he should be king. Eliseus not otherwise than by casting his mantle upon him. Those two kings neither he himself in person, nor Eliseus his disciple, anointed, but a certain one of the prophets was sent to anoint Jehu.

VERS. 17.-- *Whosoever.* [n: (ID.)] The guilty, divine justice makes some to be punished by kings with the sword, others it pierces through by prophets and priests with the sword of the tongue.

[0608D]VERS. 19.-- *He departed.* When our Redeemer, coming down from heaven, by the divine judgment acquired a people still gaping after earthly works, in whom he wrought salvation, when he converted him to the faith. For Elias is interpreted the Lord God, Saphat judging, Eliseus the salvation of my God. Upon whom the prophet cast his mantle, when the Lord clothed the people with the catholic faith. Whence the Apostle: *You who are baptized in Christ, have put on Christ [n: (Gal. III)]*. Leaving the oxen he ran after Elias: because the choir of the elect, having heard, *unless a man renounce all things, he cannot be my disciple [n: (Luc. XIV)]*, straightway ceased to gape after earthly gains, to serve secular desires, and so preached the word of life [0609A] to others, for this is to kiss father and mother, to will to correct by speech whomsoever one can, whether of the Jews or of the Gentiles.

[n: (RAB.)] The Christian people also, slaying carnal desires, turns its whole army into the work of evangelizing, whence it may be able to furnish sufficient food to its helpers.

VERS. 21.-- *He followed.* [n: (ID.)] He follows our Elias and ministers to him who, following his footsteps, that is, keeping his commandments, busies himself to render worthily ministry and honour.
