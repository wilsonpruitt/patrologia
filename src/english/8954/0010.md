## CHAPTER XX.

[n: (RAB.)] Benadad signifies the devil, who draws together divers armies of malignant spirits for the overthrowing [0609B] of the people of God, but is overcome by the servants of the princes of Israel, that is, by the good hearers of teachers, because what they hear with their ears they fulfil in deeds. The ancient enemy is turned to flight, and all his army is thrown into confusion by the soldiers of Christ, who are well said to be two hundred thousand and thirty-two, because they who perfectly hold the knowledge of both Testaments, and keep the faith of the Trinity with a twofold charity, these are approved to be fit for the warfare of the highest king.

VERS. 10.-- *These things may the gods do to me.* Samaria, by the death [cj: *morte*; read *more*, "after the manner"] of cities, had ground within almost level with the walls themselves, by which support it might withstand outside the strokes of the ram, and the height of the walls had far overtopped the surface of the ground. The proud king therefore said, terrifying the besieged, that he had so great a multitude that, if each [0609C] one of the soldiers had brought one stone or stake or turf to build a mound against the city, a mound would rise up which would seem to be level with the surface that was within the walls, so that fighting on equal ground they might cast darts and firebrands against the city.

*Let not the girded man glory.* [n: (RAB.)] He is girded who is encompassed with a girdle, ungirded who has laid the girdle aside. The king of Israel therefore said to the king of Syria, glorying as though he had already taken Samaria which he had begun to besiege: *Let not the girded man glory equally as the ungirded.* As if he should say: Glory not as a conqueror, thou who, still set in the line of battle, knowest not whom victory will follow: since he who believed that he should conquer, was conquered.

VERS. 20.-- *And he fled.* [n: (RAB.)] That is, the devil, the prince of the wicked, whose eyes are lofty. [0609D] For Syria the lofty, trusting in the horse of its pride, with horsemen, namely, all proud ones, because he himself is the head over all the children of pride, being overcome by the army of Christ is turned to flight, and the king of Israel strikes the horses and chariots, etc., because the King of kings overwhelms the spiritual wickednesses by blotting out the sins of the human race.

[n: (ID.)] The devil, although he is often overcome by the saints, yet [0610A] again renews the battle against them, and while he is overcome in one way, straightway he strives to conquer in another

[n: (ID.)] The malignant spirits, if they are overcome in spiritual things, prepare war in bodily things, busying themselves to cast souls headlong from the things above to the lowest, that they may the more easily conquer. If they see them desire heavenly things, they thrust earthly things upon them to be loved. If, prosperity being granted, thanks shall be rendered to God, they strive that they may be broken by adversities. But as the Syrians, so the demons, where they trusted to overcome, there were overcome.

VERS. 38.-- *He went therefore.* Micheas used a parable because of the words which he was about to say to the king, that he might show that this pertained rather to him than to himself.

VERS. 39.-- *Thy life shall be for his life.* It must be provided for by the servants of God that they flatter not themselves after the example of Achab with a pernicious [0610B] security, so long as the enemy lives, lest, while they reckon themselves as it were conquerors, they be the more quickly dashed down through the fraud of the devil promising peace: because as then the prophet declared to the king that vengeance would be at hand for his inconsiderate pity, so now the prophetic word declares that, if we make a covenant with the devil, eternal punishment hangs over us thereby.

VERS. 43.-- *And in a rage he came into Samaria.* Achab, being angry, commanded the prophet to be kept shut up, and confounded by the words of Micheas returned home.

## CHAPTER XXI.

*After the words,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] *Naboth.* He is interpreted conspicuous, etc., *as far as* but flowed away through vain desires.

## CHAPTER XXII.

[0610C]

VERS. 17.-- *I saw all Israel dispersed,* etc. And by these things he shows that his depravity was the cause of the slaughter. For if it had had a good and pious shepherd, it would have overcome the enemies by force. Then he shows also the manner of obtaining salvation. If the Lord is, saith he to them, for God, let every one return to his own place in peace. If you believe in God, and wish to know from him what is to be done, dismiss the army.

VERS. 19.-- *I saw the Lord,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The throne of the Lord, the angelic powers, over whose minds God presiding disposes all things below. The army of heaven, the multitude of ministering angels: the right hand part of the angels the elect, the left hand part the reprobate. For God is shut in neither by the right hand nor by the left: who [0610D] is so within all things that he is also without. But how are the evil of the army of heaven? Because although driven from the ethereal heaven, they yet still tarry in the airy heaven; whence Paul: *Against the spirits of wickedness in the high places [n: (Ephes. VI)]*. The whole army stands by God, because both the will of the elect spirits serves [cj: *deseruit*; read *deservit*, "serves"] the divine power, and the sense of the reprobate, serving its own malice, obeys the judgment of the Almighty.
