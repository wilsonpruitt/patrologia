## CHAPTER VI.

VERS. 5.-- *And it happened.* [n: (RAB.)] When the presence of Christ, working through the body, was cutting down the impious Jews like unfruitful trees; whence: *Behold the axe is laid to the root of the tree.* [var: Vulg. Matt. 3:10 and Luke 3:9 *Jam enim securis ad radicem arborum posita est*, "For now the axe is laid to the root of the trees"] From them, the passion intervening, the body itself does service [cj: *deservit*; read *deseruit*, "forsook"]: it went down into the deep, in that, laid in the sepulchre, it rose again as to the haft, the spirit returning.

[n: (ID.)] Great care must be taken lest the understanding grow numb in idleness, [0614C] lest in the exercise of work it vanish away through the vice of elation.

[n: (GREG.)] The iron in the haft is the understanding in the heart: by this the woods are cut down, when they that act perversely are rebuked. And while this is done loosely, while the fall of vainglory is not shunned; the iron is lost in the water, because out of a dissolute work the understanding is made foolish, which is given for this end, that it may be restored before the eyes of the giver out of good action, whence there follows: *This very thing.* But the elect, if ever they offend, return swiftly to the heart, and pursue their fault with tears, whence: *Alas, alas,* etc. They who weep look carefully not only at the evils they have committed, but also at the good things they ought to have rendered out of the gift received, and this is: *Alas, alas, alas, and this I had borrowed;* as if he should say: That I lost through the looseness [0614D] of negligence which I received of the Redeemer's grace, that I might render it again through good works: but God never forsakes the mind which truly acknowledges itself in its sins. Whence Elisaeus sent the wood down, and brought the iron to the surface, because the Redeemer humbles the heart of the sinner, and restores the understanding which he had lost. Whence in another translation it is said: *That he broke the wood and cast it in, and so took up the iron.* To break the wood is to bruise the heart from elation, to cast it to the depths, to humble it by the thought of its own infirmity. Straightway the iron returned, because the understanding runs back to the use of its former exercise.

VERS. 14.-- *And when they were come.* [n: (ID.)] He who, trusting in the arms of wickedness, prepares ambushes for the servants of Christ, [0615A] blinded by the night of perfidy, wages war against the Church.

VERS. 15.-- *Alas, alas,* etc. [n: (GREG.)] Here he marks those who through faint-heartedness fear bodily punishments.

VERS. 16.-- *Fear ye not.* [var: Vulg. 4 Kings 6:16 *Noli timere*, in the singular] Fear ye not them who kill the body, and after that have no more what they may do to you.

*There are more.* [n: (ID.)] I can ask my Father, and he will give me more than twelve legions of angels.

*Lord, open.* [n: (ID.)] It is very needful that our Elisaeus open the eyes of the heart to consider, that all who trust in him shall not be confounded.

VERS. 18.-- *Strike.* [n: (ID.)] It is good that the eye [0615B] which scandalizes be plucked out, that the gaze of pride and of malice may be stopped up, and the eye of sound faith and of simplicity be opened.

[n: (RAB.)]. After Elisaeus had led the enemies into Samaria, he did not suffer them to be slain, but preparing refreshment for them, he sent them away in peace. And Saul, long kicking against the goad, the Lord first blinded, and then enriched with spiritual knowledge. And he commanded us thus: *Love your enemies, and do good to them that hate you [n: (Luc. VI)]*.

VERS. 24.-- *He gathered together.* [n: (RAB.)] Benadad and his army are the devil and the wicked, namely pagans, Jews, and heretics, whom he stirs up to wage war against the Church. By such is the people of God afflicted, which is set in Samaria, that is, in the keeping of the law: and there comes a famine, [0615C] when it is not permitted to the teachers to preach the word of God. But when Elisaeus reveals it, that is, when the Redeemer makes it known through the Gospel, the salvation which is far from sinners is known to be near to them that fear God. For the Lord says: *To-morrow a bushel,* etc. A bushel of fine flour is the perfect measure of the divine wisdom which is in the New Testament. Two bushels of barley are the knowledge of the law and of the prophets, which are bought for one stater, that is, for the catholic faith, in the gate of Samaria, that is, in the apostolic preaching, through which entrance is made into the Church. For when the whirlwind of persecution which is made to-day has ceased, the Lord will give to-morrow, that is, in the time to come, tranquillity, that the preaching may be perfectly accomplished.

VERS. 25.-- *And there was a famine,* etc. [n: (AUG., serm. 211, de temp.)] While the wicked nation departs from the worship of God, [0615D] it is straitened with a dreadful famine, and troubled with a just necessity. Why, O most wretched one, dost thou suffer famine? Why art thou torn with so great want? The penalty of famine grows daily, because daily the guilt too grows. The scourges persist, because in the people the offences too persist. Henceforth shun sins, restrain thy offences, turn to God, leave the idol: then thou shalt be able to overcome thine enemies by the divine help. Nor shall any one be able to hurt thee further, if, wickedness being condemned, the divine worship shall have sprouted in thee. Ah, the wickedness! The sinful people feeds on dreadful corpses and on the dung of doves, nor does it seek the worship of God. Famine and necessity demand defiled flesh and dreadful food; and still hardness worships the thing it has fashioned. Natural affection is turned into food, the offspring received is changed [0616A] into fodder. The murder of kin is done before men come to the deadly banquet. Innocent blood is poured out, that the raging hunger of the parents may be fattened.

## CHAPTER VII.

VERS. 3.-- *Four,* etc. [n: (RAB. in lib. Reg., tom. III.)] They are lepers, etc., *as far as* that they may preach the truth of faith in the four parts of the world.

VERS. 17.-- *Now.* The captain holds the type of the Scribes and Pharisees, to whom the Truth says: *Woe to you, Scribes and Pharisees, hypocrites, who shut the kingdom of heaven before men: for you yourselves enter not in, nor do you suffer them that are going in to enter [n: (Matth. XXIII)]*. Whom the crowd trod down in the gate, because he hindered the step of those entering unto faith: whence, deservedly trodden down, that is, condemned by the scrutiny of the just judge, he shall be sent [0616B] into everlasting darkness.

## CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 7.-- *Benedab, king of Syria,* etc. When the king of the Syrians had come into Damascus, and had learned that the Divinity had sent fear and flight upon him and upon his army, and that it had not been brought about by an invasion of enemies, being exceedingly afflicted in that he had God so angered against him, he fell into a sickness.

## CHAPTER IX.

VERS. 1.-- *Go. [n: (RAB. in lib. Reg., tom. III.)]* It marks out the sovereignty of the Gentiles, which the Lord appointed that it should destroy with vengeance alike the sacrilegious city, which slew the prophets and the Lord of the prophets, and persecuted his apostles, and should overthrow the priesthood which it had emptily held after Christ, [0616C] and should cast down the temple, and moreover should hurl the impious Synagogue, which always thirsted for the blood of the saints, from the summit of the kingdom, and should slay its rulers.

VERS. 29.-- *And he reigned.* [n: (RAB.)] It is said of Ochozias, etc., *as far as* and he reigned one year.

## CHAPTER X.

VERS. 15.-- *Is thy heart,* etc. Hence therefore it is plain that even among the tribes there were certain men endowed with piety, for whose sake the Lord bore care of the others also.
