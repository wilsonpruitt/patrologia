## CHAPTER THIRTEEN.

VERS. 14.-- *And Elisaeus.* [n: (RAB.)] Our Redeemer bids the rulers of the Church clothe themselves with spiritual arms, and fight against the ancient enemy. [0619D] The bow is the sacred word, which consists of both Testaments as of horn and string. For by the Old Testament the horn is figured, because of its hardness; but by the New, which was made when the Lord was incarnate, the string is figured: and while the string is drawn, the horn is bent, because when the New Testament is examined, the Old is bent from its own stiffness to spiritual understanding. The arrows are the words which the teachers put forth. We therefore, when we weigh the sayings of Holy Scripture, bend the bow; when we give the words of doctrine, we send forth the arrows.

VERS. 16.-- *He put over.* [n: (ID.)] By his own examples the Lord directs and strengthens the actions of the teachers.

VERS. 17.-- *Open the window.* [n: (ID.)] With the light of knowledge [0620A] he exhorts his own to be first enlightened, and so to send forth the darts of words.

*The arrow.* [n: (ID.)] Holy preaching is the most certain slaying of spiritual enemies, if it is carried on perseveringly, whence the teacher ought not, for covetousness, to neglect the care of souls, but rather through piety to press forward to eternal rest, which Aphec signifies: for it is interpreted: "he will hold" or "he will lay hold," whence the Apostle, having enumerated the vices, added: *But thou, man of God, fly these things, and pursue justice and piety, and lay hold on eternal life [n: (I Tim. VI)]*.

VERS. 18.-- *Take. And when he had.* [n: (ID.)] It is enjoined upon the teachers with the dart of preaching the earth, etc., *as far as Faith without works is dead.*

[n: (RAB.)] The resurrection of the corpse, etc., *as far as* and [0620B] to have place there rather than here.

## CHAPTER FOURTEEN.

VERS. 1.-- *In the second year. And he did. [n: (RAB., in lib. Reg., tom. III.)]* Amasias did what was good in the sight of the Lord, but not with a perfect heart: for he did good works which please God, but not with a perfect heart, because he displayed them under a feigned appearance before men, and so before God they were not accepted.

VERS. 7.-- *In the valley.* [n: (ID.)] It was at the Valley of the Saltpits where they made salt. Either by the mud of the brine being cut away and dried and burned; or by the salt waters of the wells being boiled, and brought by cooking as far as the firmness of salt; or by any other method whatsoever by which salt is wont to be made.

*The rock.* [n: (ID.)] Petra is a noble city of Arabia in the land of Edom, which in the book of Numbers is called Recem, [0620C] and is so named by the Syrians. But Josephus says it is not a city, but a great rock set in Arabia.

*Jectehel.* [n: (RAB.)] The name which Amasias the victor imposed is interpreted "the assembly of God" or "the help of God," he acting faithfully that it might seem to be of present memory, in that he had taken it either as the assembly of the people of God, or with God helping.

VERS. 25.-- *He restored the borders of Israel,* etc. He says of Jeroboam: *He restored the borders of Israel, from the entrance of Emath unto the sea of the wilderness.* Emath, which is now called Epiphania, was the northern border of Israel. But the sea of the wilderness is the Dead Sea, which stretches in breadth for five hundred and eighty stadia as far as Zoara of Arabia, and in length a hundred and fifty, as far as the neighbourhood of the Sodomites. [0620D] Therefore Jeroboam, encamping against the Syrians, laid waste all their province (as Jonas had prophesied).

*Which is in,* etc. Hepher was a tower not far from the temple, of enormous bulk and height: whence it is called Hepher, that is, of darkness, or cloudy: in that it lifts its head as far as the clouds, which in Zacharias is called the cloudy tower.

## CHAPTER FIFTEEN.

VERS. 1.-- *In the twentieth year Azarias. [n: (RAB., in lib. Reg., tom. III.)]* He is Ozias, etc., *as far as* above it is called the gate of Ephraim.

VERS. 35.-- *He built.* [n: (RAB.)] Joatham built the high gate of the house of the Lord, which in [0621A] the Acts of the Apostles is called Beautiful, and by the Hebrews is called the Gate of Joatham.

## CHAPTER SIXTEEN

VERS. 3.-- *He consecrated.* [n: (RAB.)] Beenon of the son of Ennon. Thophet, or Thaphet, is in the valley of Beennon beside Acheldemach, a place once most delightful, where they were wont to sacrifice to demons. In Hebrew Gehennon, that is, the valley of Ennon; whence in the Gospel it is called gehenna, because as they perished there in body and in soul who worshipped idols, so the sinners who pass over to hell shall be punished with eternal damnation.

VERS. 18.-- *Musach also.* [n: (RAB.)] It is a building set in the vestibule of the temple, where the kings, when they went to the temple on the sabbath, used to cast in alms; and [0621B] so the Musach of the sabbath is the treasury of the kings, as the corban is of the priests. *Musach also,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He did not for this reason turn it into the temple of the Lord, that it might serve there for the worship of God, but that it might be profaned together with the temple, being eager to please the king of the Assyrians rather than God, so that when he came thither he might, on seeing it, covet it and carry it off, as with many other things, Achaz granting them to him. that he might find favour with him.

## CHAPTER SEVENTEEN.

VERS. 27.-- *You have brought.* [n: (RAB.)] The Assyrian king, that is, the devil with his army, daily afflicts the people of the Church by besieging and laying it waste, when, plucking them out of their own seats for the sins they have committed, he carries them away into a strange land. Whence: *Upon the rivers of Babylon there we sat and wept, when we remembered [0621C] Sion.*

VERS. 29.-- *And every one.* [n: (ID.)] In the book of Places it is read that Benoth and Nergel were cities which they built in Judaea who had passed over from Babylonia. Asima also they built as a town who had come to it from Emath. Nebahan and Tharthac are cities which the Hevites founded in Judaea.

[n: (ID.)] It seems that, according to the sequence of the discourse, the names of the idols also, which these nations had formerly served, can here be understood, because when it had been said: *And every nation made its own god,* there is subjoined, as if to complete the sentence: *For the men of Babylon made Sochoth Benoth;* that is, tabernacles: Benoth, which is the name of an idol. In what follows [0621D] it is shown that Adramalech and Anamelech were idols of the city of Sepharvaim, so it seems to follow that Nergel also was of the Cutheans, Asima of the Emathites, and that Nebahath and Tharthach were idols of the Hevites.

VERS. 33.-- *And when.* [n: (RAB. ex Euch.)] Heretics have certain sacraments in common with holy Church, *as far as* and we understand of the grandsons.
