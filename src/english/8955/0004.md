## CHAPTER XVIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In the third year. ⟦s2⟧ Ezechias.* [n: (RAB.)] "Laying hold of the Lord," or "the strength of the Lord." ⟦s3⟧ For our Redeemer, the Lord strong, the Lord mighty in battle, not only crushed the idols of the nations, and scattered the worship of idolatry, that they might have the knowledge of the one God, [0622A] and be worthily made over to his worship: nay, he crushed the very letter of the Mosaic law, which that people of the law kept sounding forth by continual reading and held to be a great thing, and taught them to understand in it the spiritual sense.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *And he broke.* ⟦s2⟧ Ezechias broke the serpent, because he hoped in the Lord God, not in the brazen serpent.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He called it Nohestan,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Which is interpreted "their brass," that him whom they worshipped for a deity, they might acknowledge, at his words, to be metal, not God.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Rabsaces.* [n: (RAB.)] Because he speaks in Hebrew: they think him a son of Isaias, who was a traitor along with Sobna; or a Samaritan, and that he therefore knew the Hebrew tongue. ⟦s2⟧ Who, as it were a kind of contrary strength, imitated [0622B] the prophets by his arrogance. ⟦s3⟧ The prophets say: Thus saith the Lord. ⟦s4⟧ This man said: *Thus saith the great king, the king of the Assyrians.*

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Eliacim.* [n: (RAB.)] Of this Eliacim Isaias said: *I will call my servant Eliacim [n: (Isa. XXII)]*, who was high priest after Sobna: whom the Hebrews relate to have betrayed Jerusalem, being terrified by the threats of Rabsaces, and that, the citadel being excepted, as also the temple, the Assyrians took the whole city; and some assert that this same Sobna went out with Eliacim to Rabsaces.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Dost thou trust.* ⟦s2⟧ No history relates that Ezechias asked the help of Pharao.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *In the Lord.* [n: (RAB.)] It is true that they had confidence in God, but it is false that Ezechias took away the high places of God and the altars: for this he did not [0622C] against God, but for God, that, the old error being destroyed, he might bid God be adored in the temple of Jerusalem.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *I will give you.* [n: (ID.)] Rabsaces, that he might show the fewness of the besieged, promises two thousand horses, for which Ezechias would have no riders: this came not from the weakness of the people, as though they knew not how to ride, but because God commanded that the king of Israel should not multiply to himself horses and wives.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *The Lord said.* [n: (ID.)] To that which he had said, If you shall say, We trust in God, he craftily answers that he had come not by his own will, but by God's, who says: *Come up.* ⟦s2⟧ And the argument is: since I have taken many cities, and a part of Jerusalem remains untouched, it is manifest that I have come by the will of God.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0622D]VERS. 26.-- *We beseech thee.* [n: (ID.)] The sense is, etc., *as far as* which had a likeness to the land of Judaea, as well in situation as in fruits.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Let not Ezechias seduce you,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The accusation of Rabsaces is a testimony to Ezechias, namely that, when the cities had been taken, he trusted in the Lord: and he strengthened the people that they might hope in the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ Whence Rabsaces wishes to destroy what the other had built up, and says to the people: *Let not Ezechias seduce you, and let him not give you confidence in the Lord your God.*

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (RAB.)] He shows that the Samaritans served the gods of these cities or nations, etc., *as far as* as our translator renders it.

## CHAPTER XIX.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0623A]

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (RAB., in lib. Reg., tom. III.)] Wonderful is the king's humility and prudence, etc., *as far as* and that the enemy should die in his own land.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *And when he had heard.* [n: (ID.)] God willing it, Rabsaces abandoned the siege of Jerusalem, and found his own lord, Lachis being either taken or forsaken, assaulting Lobna. ⟦s2⟧ Sennacherib, going to meet the king of the Ethiopians, sent letters to Ezechias, that those whom he had not taken by force he might terrify by speech. ⟦s3⟧ Herodotus relates that Sennacherib fought against the Egyptians and besieged Pelusium, and that, the mounds being now raised and the city about to be taken, Taracha king of the Ethiopians came to their aid.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (RAB.)] Before, Ezechias, terrified with the dread of the Lord, [0623B] dared not adore in the temple, nor pour out free prayers there, now, Isaias saying: *Fear not because of the words which thou hast heard [n: (Isa. XLI)]*, he boldly entreats the Lord.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *The virgin daughter.* [n: (RAB.)] Because, while all the nations adore idols, she alone keeps the chastity of the religion of the one God, and she, lest she should stir him to greater blasphemy, answered him not when he was present: after he had departed she moved her head to entreat God, or at least to deride his arrogance, being sure of vengeance. ⟦s2⟧ Nor did he himself blaspheme the Lord by himself, but through his servants, that the arrogance might be the greater.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *The high places.* [n: (ID.)] The height of the mountains and the ridges of Libanus, the lofty cedars and fir trees. ⟦s2⟧ Or it is to be taken by metaphor of all the nations and [0623C] of their princes. ⟦s3⟧ Or of Jerusalem, which is Libanus, the cedars and fir trees, the powerful and the nobles.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *And I have dried up.* [n: (ID.)] Either through the multitude of his army he dried up all the streams, so that he was compelled to dig wells for himself. ⟦s2⟧ Or all the people, whom the waters signify, is laid waste by his own army.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Hast thou not heard.* [n: (ID.)] These things in the person of the Lord, etc., *as far as* which may tame thy fierceness, and lead thee back among the Assyrians.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *This shall be a sign.* [n: (ID.)] Of those things which I foretell, etc., *as far as* nay, by the zeal wherewith he was zealous for his people against the impious.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Eat.* ⟦s2⟧ Isaias thus: *Eat this year the things that spring of themselves, and in the second year feed upon fruits. ⟦s3⟧ But in the third year sow, plant vineyards and eat [0623D] the fruits of them, and he shall send forth that which shall have been saved in the house of Juda; and what is left over shall take root downward, and shall bear fruit, because out of Jerusalem shall go forth remnants, and salvation from mount Sion. ⟦s4⟧ The zeal of the Lord of hosts shall do this [n: (Isa. XXXVII)]*. ⟦s5⟧ All things having been laid waste by the army, *eat what could again sprout,* or *what could escape from among the hoofs of the horses. ⟦s6⟧ In the second year, what springs of itself without labour.* ⟦s7⟧ Or this was said by way of pretence, because they thought that they would perish of famine.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *And for David my servant.* [n: (RAB.)] For the hope of things to come he shakes off present fear. ⟦s2⟧ And he says that they are preserved not by their own merit, but by the mercy of God, nay also by the memory of David their father: in [0624A] which they are admonished both of their own negligence and of his faith and justice, because God so far loves justice that he protects posterity by the virtue of their forefathers.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *It came to pass therefore.* [n: (RAB.)] So many are cut down by one angel in one night, and without wounds cruel death runs through them. ⟦s2⟧ Concerning which it is read in the Paralipomenon: *And the Lord sent an angel, who struck every strong man and warrior, and the prince of the army of the Assyrians.* ⟦s3⟧ And he returned with ignominy into his own land. ⟦s4⟧ And he was preserved, that he might be made a witness of the majesty of him whom a little before he had despised.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *And when he was adoring in the temple.* [n: (RAB.)] As though he were bringing back a victory over his enemies, the despiser of the true God is slaughtered in the shrine of a false deity: nor does he perish by the angelic [0624B] sword, which was the common lot, but by the murder of his sons.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] Pharao also is preserved through the ten plagues of Egypt, that he may perish last.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Into the land of the Armenians.* ⟦s2⟧ Ararath is a region in level Armenia, through which the Araxes flows, of incredible fertility, at the roots of Mount Taurus. ⟦s3⟧ Therefore Noe's ark also was borne not to the mountains of Armenia generally, but to the loftiest mountains of Taurus which overhang the plains of Ararath.
