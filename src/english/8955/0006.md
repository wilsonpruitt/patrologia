## CHAPTER XXIII.

[0626C]

VERS. 10.-- *He defiled. [n: (RAB. in lib. Reg., tom. III.)]* Josias, scattering there upon Thopheth either the bones of the dead (as he is read to have done in the places following) or any other unclean things whatsoever, so that the place might appear to all who looked upon it fit for abomination rather than for delight.

*In the valley.* [n: (ID.)] The valley of Ennom is beside the wall of Jerusalem toward the east, etc., *as far as* because Thopheth is called breadth.

VERS. 11.-- *He took away.* [n: (RAB.)] The Jews had made themselves over to the superstitions of the Gentiles, who among other things are wont to paint or to make the image of the sun as a beardless boy, because through the ages no old age befalls it, [0626D] born daily with a new rising. To which they assign a chariot and horses, because Elias was caught up to heaven in a fiery chariot and with fiery horses, which among other things they see painted upon the wall, and they are deceived by the nearness of the name: for in Greek the sun is called Elios.

VERS. 13.-- *The high places.* [n: (RAB.)] Scripture speaks of places set upon leafy hills, in which they sacrificed either to demons; or, to the Lord, being enticed by the pleasantness of the places, against the prohibition, the altar which was in the temple being left, they flocked thither about to offer: whence more often in this book it is said of the less perfect kings: But nevertheless he took not away the high places.

*Which he had built.* [n: (ID.)] Here it is shown that Solomon never [0627A] perfectly repented of the crime of idolatry he had committed: for if he had truly repented, he would have laboured before all things that the idols which he had built should be taken out of the city: and that he should not leave, to the scandal of the foolish, as though done wisely, those erroneous things which he himself, though he was most wise, had done.

*And Chamos.* [n: (RAB.)] Elsewhere it is read that Solomon built a shrine of Chamos upon the mountain which is over against Jerusalem. But it is not contrary; because that mountain was in so great nearness to the city that it seemed to belong to it, and to defile it with the filth which was heaped up upon it.

*To the offence of Moab.* [n: (ID.)] The idols are called an offence, because either God is offended by them, or they bring offence and ruin to their own worshippers.

VERS. 16.-- *He took the bones,* etc. Josias commanded that the bones of the dead [0627B] should be burned upon the altar, because their sacrifices were profane, and through the error of idolatry they not only offered cattle to demons, but delivered even themselves into the power of demons: because by the just judgment of God they were to be scattered together with the altar which they had made for the idols. Mystically, they who, made over to the service of the devil, end a sacrilegious life in time, shall be consumed with eternal torments, God rendering according to merits

VERS. 21.-- *Keep the phase.* [n: (RAB.)] Josias holds the type of the Saviour, etc., *as far as: and to you that fear shall arise the sun of justice.*

*For there was no phase kept like to it,* etc. Because, the figures being taken away in the day of judgment, the saints shall pass over from death to life, and so shall rejoice in the sight of their creator, [0627C] *because the God of gods shall be seen in Sion.*

[n: (RAB.)] That Josias, the idols being cast out of the land, together with the pythons, the soothsayers, and all uncleannesses, is read to have celebrated the phase of the Lord, teaches morally that we should first cleanse the land of the heart from vices, that we may be able to serve God, and so celebrate a pasch pleasing to the Lord: *Not indeed with the leaven of malice and wickedness, but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth [n: (I Cor. V)]*.

[n: (ID.)] Josias was wounded in the field of Mageddo, whereof also he straightway died. And the people mourned him much, and Jeremias most of all. For then he is said to have lamented thus: How doth the city sit solitary! Of this mourning it is said: And there shall be a mourning in Jerusalem, like the mourning of Adremon, which is the name of a king who then reigned in Charchamis, to whose aid Josias had come [0627D] against Nechao king of Egypt.

[n: (ID.)] The slaying of Josias by Pharao Nechao, done in Mageddo, signifies the persecution of the ancient enemy against the preachers, whom he strives through envy to deliver over to death, and endeavours to take away out of the land of the Church: that so he may the more easily scatter the whole people. Pharao is scattering, Nechao a striking, Mageddo of temptation. For the devil, being permitted to rise up against the saints, here strives with all his strength, that by tempting he may strike and scatter the flock of the Lord, and may first take away those by whose comfort they should have been helped to fight against the enemy.

VERS. 30.-- *Joachaz the son of Josias.* [n: (ID.)] By another name he is called Sellum, but because he was most perverse, [0628A] he reigned a short time, for Nechao bound him, in that the people had made him king against Nechao's will, and led him into Egypt.

[n: (ID.)] Joachaz held back signifies evil teachers, whom the people of the land, that is, those given over to earthly cupidity, choose as prelates consenting to their own vices, whom Pharao bound in Rebla, when the devil, enervating them through a multitude of flatterers, deceives the hearts of prelates, and so plunges them, bound with the chains of sins, into the dark abyss of a spiritual Egypt.

VERS. 35.-- *But the silver and the gold he gave,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The malignant one demands of the carnal people the tribute of his own service, that both in understanding and in speech they may be ready in all things for his obedience. Joachim exacts the tribute at the king's command, because the devil, through masters given over to him, exacts of every one [0628B] to pay the tribute of sin according to his strength, and so in those wickedly set over others he daily prepares destruction for the subject.

## CHAPTER XXIV.

[n: (RAB.)] When any one whatsoever has once made himself over to vice, etc., *as far as* and let him implore help from God.

[n: (RAB.)] Josephus relates that he was slain by Nabuchodonosor, and cast out before the walls of Jerusalem and unburied, of whom it had been foretold: *He shall be buried with the burial of an ass [n: (Jer. XXII)]*.

*And Joachim reigned,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The name Joachim is written otherwise in the designation of the father, who is also Eliacim: otherwise when the son is signified, who is also Jechonias, for when it marks the father it is written with *k* and *m*: but when the son, [0628C] it is written with *ch* and *n*.

VERS. 8.-- *Jerusalem. [n: (BEDA in lib. Reg., quaest. 30, tom. II.)]* Jerusalem and the land of Israel, etc., *as far as* that is, spiritual knowledge is turned to the works of sinners.

VERS. 14.-- *[n: (ID., ibid.)] The craftsman and the goldsmith, and nothing was left, except the poor of the people of the land. [n: (RAB. ex Bed., ibid.)]* What above the Philistines, reigning over the people of Israel, are related to have done, when it is said: *There was no smith to be found in all the land of Israel, for the Philistines had taken care lest perhaps the Hebrews should make a sword or a spear to fight back withal [n: (I Reg. XIII)]*, so now the Chaldeans busy themselves that there may remain in it no craftsman and goldsmith who might set in order or repair the ruined walls of the city

[0628D] [n: (RAB.)] They are evil rulers who abuse the divine gift and bounty, and falsely usurp to themselves the name of justice. For Matthathia or Matthanias is interpreted a gift or a bounty: Sedecias, the just Lord. He reigned eleven years, because he was a transgressor of the law, which is signified by ten, the number. The number nine, which has less than ten, signifies the imperfection of the law, as eleven signifies transgression. Rightly therefore did Nabuchodonosor in the ninth year of the reign of Sedecias besiege the city in the tenth month, and on the tenth day of the month: because, when evil shepherds neglect to fulfil in work and in teaching the commandments of the decalogue, which they hold by knowledge, it must needs be that the ancient enemy with his army surround with a siege the people committed to them, and shut them in with a fortification [0629A] of error and of vices built up, walling round the city: and so famine prevails in the city, the famine, that is, of the word of God, when the bread of doctrine is not dealt out to the people of the land.
