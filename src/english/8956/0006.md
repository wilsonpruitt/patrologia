## CHAPTER VII.

VERS. 1.-- *And it came to pass in the days of Achaz,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] The things that are written up to this place were seen under Ozias and Joathan his son; those that follow, under Achaz, a most impious king, who, being forsaken by God, is overcome by his enemies.

*Achaz.* Who shut the doors of the temple and adored the Baalim, consecrated his son to an idol, took away the brazen altar, and set an altar of idols in the temple. Rightly therefore was he forsaken by the Lord: *And his enemies rose up against him [n: (II Par. XXVIII)]*.

*He went up,* a second time. For first the Assyrians of the tribe of [1245B] Juda, etc., *as far as* Herod and Pilate were made friends in the passion of Christ.

VERS. 3.-- *He was forsaken.* By his own brother Rabsaces, who had gone over to the Assyrians, as the Hebrews hand down.

*Jasub thy son.* [n: (ID.)] Who is interpreted *a remaining companion and one that turns back,* who was the people of Juda that was to be delivered out of the hands of the two kings.

*In the way.* There Rabsaces, afterward at the command of Sennacherib, blasphemed the people of God.

VERS. 4.-- *Of two tails.* Two tails, etc., *as far as* and he was led with the ten tribes into the mountains of the Medes and the Persians.

VERS. 6.-- *Tabeel.* [n: (ID.)] Tabeel, the good God, as if he should say: There let us set the idol of our God; or, to the letter, he so calls the Son of man.

[1245C]VERS. 8.-- *Yet sixty,* etc. As if he should say: In the meanwhile they shall be content with their own borders, and Rasin shall rule over Damascus as over the metropolis of Syria, and Samaria shall be the head of the cities of Ephraim, and in it in the meanwhile the son of Romelia shall reign.

*Sixty-five years.* From the twenty-sixth year of the reign of Ozias, when he was struck with leprosy, from that time Joathan his son reigned seven and twenty years, his father being yet alive. After his death, sixteen years; after whom Achaz other sixteen; afterward, Ezechias reigning, six years. Ephraim, that is, the ten tribes led into captivity, ceased to be a people.

VERS. 11.-- *Ask thee a sign.* [n: (ID.)] That he who did not believe the prophet might be terrified by the authority of the Lord; [1245D] as if he should say: It seems hard to thee that most powerful kingdoms should be brought to an end in a short time, and that thou shouldst be delivered, and thy people; but ask thee a sign.

*Ask thee a sign.* Moses received a sign from the earth, etc., *as far as* it looks to both the death of the Lord and his ascension.

*I will not ask.* Not humbly, but proudly. For many are read to have asked signs of the Lord and to have received them; but Achaz answered thus, as having authority: *Thou shalt not tempt the Lord thy God.* Or, he feared that in the sign God should be glorified and the idols made cheap.

VERS. 14.-- *Behold a virgin shall conceive.* [n: (ID.)] In Hebrew *alma;* in Latin also *alma* is called holy, etc., *as far [1246A] as* that it arose by violence is plain even to fools.

*Emmanuel.* [n: (ID.)] Some say that Isaias had two sons, etc., *as far as* for the sake of which the Word was made flesh.

VERS. 15.-- *To reprobate the evil.* [n: (ID.)] As if he should say: Set in the swaddling-bands of infancy he shall have the discernment of good and evil. By these words we perceive that the infancy of the Saviour did not diminish the divine wisdom in him, although it be said: *But the child advanced in age and wisdom,* [var: Vulg. Lc 2:52 *Et Jesus proficiebat sapientia, et aetate, et gratia*, "And Jesus advanced in wisdom, and age, and grace"] that the truth of the human body might be proved: for infancy is no prejudice to the divine wisdom.

VERS. 16.-- *He shall be forsaken,* etc. [n: (ID.)] as if he should say: The land of Syria and of Samaria shall be laid waste from the invocation of his name, and the house of Juda shall be delivered from the two kings.

[1246B]VERS. 18.-- *The fly.* [n: (ID.)] That is, the Egyptians, an unwarlike people and foul with idolatry. We read that Josias was slain by the Egyptians and the people of Israel subjugated; and not long after in time Nabuchodonosor with an innumerable army took Jerusalem, burned the temple, and set Assyrian inhabitants in Judaea.

VERS. 20.-- *A razor.* [n: (ID.)] The king of the Assyrians (who in Jeremias is called the dove of God), in him as in a most sharp razor, and in those who are beyond the river, namely the Assyrians, the Lord shall shave in Judaea the head and the hairs of the feet, namely all the strength of kings and of princes and of their subjects.

VERS. 22.-- *He shall eat butter.* He shall be delighted in [1246C] the passion of Christ or in his own. For butter is made out of many mixings.

VERS. 25.-- *And all the hills that shall be raked with the hoe.* [n: (ID.)] The plains being left for fear of enemies, they shall flee to the mountain places, tilling the rough parts of the mountains with their own hand; they shall sustain a wretched life. The rest shall be for pastures.

## CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 1.-- *And the Lord said to me: Take thee.* [n: (HIER.)] First the prophet foretells to Achaz the things to come, etc., *as far as* whence again the virginal birth is described.

*Quickly the spoils.* [n: (ID.)] This is the name of the child, as if he should say: Suffer not the devil to reign any longer; and send not angels, send not Prophets, but come down thyself.

[1246D]VERS. 2.-- *Urias,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Urias, the light of the Lord; Zacharias, the memory of the Lord; Barachias, the blessing of the Lord: by these witnesses the nativity of the Lord is proved. Whence, to two witnesses going to Emmaus, beginning from Moses and the prophets, he discoursed of the things which had been said of himself.

VERS. 3.-- *To the prophetess.* [n: (ID.)] To the Holy Spirit, from whom is all prophecy, which in Greek is of the neuter gender, *pneuma;* in Hebrew of the feminine, *Ruach;* in Latin it is of the masculine gender, and therefore it is allotted by different positions, for it is of none. Or to Mary, whom no one doubts to have been a prophetess.

VERS. 4.-- *The child.* [n: (ID.)] Isaias therefore is commanded, etc., *as far as* he should save the house of David by invocation alone.

[1247A] *Because the people hath cast away the waters of Siole.* [n: (HIER.)] In which the blind man washed himself, etc., *as far as* he attempted to possess Juda, his own land. [var: Vulg. Is 8:6 *populus iste aquas Siloe*, "this people the waters of Siloe"]

VERS. 7.-- *The waters of the river.* [n: (ID.)] The army of Sennacherib, whose power, seizing the land of Samaria, is compared to the flooding of a river.

VERS. 9.-- *Gather yourselves together, O ye people.* [n: (ID.)] As if: Against Emmanuel you can do nothing, etc., *as far as* because perversity cannot overcome what is right.

VERS. 12.-- *Say ye not.* [n: (ID.)] As if he should say: Fear not the conspiracy of the two kings against Juda: a conspiracy is the consent of many unto evil.

*Fear ye not, neither be afraid.* Thou and the peoples that are with thee, because they seem to be feared; because the beginning of wisdom [1247B] is the fear of the Lord.

VERS. 14.-- *For a stone.* [n: (ID.)] Which the builders rejected, etc., *as far as* joining sins to sins and bound with the snares of their offences.

*To the two houses.* Or in the two families, etc., *as far as* through their own traditions they scattered and defiled the precepts of the law.

VERS. 16.-- *Seal the law.* As if he should say: Open it, Lord, to my disciples, etc., *as far as* we can also understand doctrines.

VERS. 18.-- *My children, born of God.* The other Prophets and their disciples. Some read this of the two sons of Isaias, etc., *as far as* who were for a portent of worldly wisdom and of the pride of the Jews. [var: Vulg. Is 8:18 *Ecce ego et pueri mei quos dedit mihi Dominus*, "Behold I and my children whom the Lord hath given me"]

VERS. 20.-- *Thou shalt not be to them.* [n: (ID.)] That is, if this people [1247C] will not seek the truth from the Lord, it shall not have Christ, namely the light of truth, which it neglects. [var: Vulg. Is 8:20 *non erit eis matutina lux*, "there shall not be for them the morning light"]

VERS. 21.-- *To his king.* To the devil and to the traditions of the fathers, that is, to ancient errors.
