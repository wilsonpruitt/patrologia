## CHAPTER XI.

VERS. 1.-- *And there shall come forth a rod.* [n: (HIER.)] Up to the beginning of the vision of Babylon the prophecy is of Christ, which we expound by parts, lest we confound the memory of the reader.

*Shall come forth.* The Jews interpret the rod and the flower to be Christ himself, etc., *as far as* and thou art my firstborn Son, who reignest for ever.

VERS. 2.-- *And shall rest.* [n: (ID.)] Because in him dwells all the fulness of the Godhead.

*The Spirit.* As the word of the Lord is called light, life and resurrection, etc., [1250C] *as far as* these are the seven eyes upon one stone, according to Zacharias.

VERS. 3.-- *And the spirit shall fill him.* [n: (ID.)] Spiritually, for the sake of the little ones who have need of fear, which perfect charity casts out. For he that fears has punishment, and is not perfect in charity.

*Not according to.* He shall not accept the person, because God is not an accepter of persons, but in every nation he that fears God is acceptable to him.

*He shall reprove.* [n: (ID.)] The Pharisees, and them that reproved the apostles [sic: *apotolos*], when, walking on the sabbath through the corn, they plucked the ears with their hands and rubbed them and ate.

VERS. 4.-- *The rod of his mouth.* [n: (ID.)] By the rod the sentence of judgment can be understood. For in the beginning of his preaching [1250D] he said: *Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven shall draw near;* [var: Vulg. Mt 3:2 *appropinquavit*, "hath drawn near"] and he subjoined: *Every tree that doth not bring forth good fruits shall be rooted up and cast into the fire.* [var: Vulg. Mt 3:10 *Omnis ergo arbor quae non facit fructum bonum, excidetur*, "Every tree therefore that doth not bring forth good fruit shall be cut down"]

VERS. 5.-- *He shall slay the wicked one.* For if Michael shall slay him (as some say), yet God himself shall do this in Michael.

*Faith.* [n: (HIER.)] The faithful, by working well, shall cleave to him who was made wisdom, justice and sanctification, who says: *I am light and life.*

VERS. 6.-- *The wolf with the lamb.* [n: (ID.)] Paul with Peter, whence: *Benjamin a ravening wolf.* And: *Feed my sheep. The wolf shall dwell with the lamb.* It was not the lamb and the kid that dwelt with the wolf and the leopard, but the wolf and [1251A] the leopard imitate the innocence of the lambs and the kids, namely Christ.

*The wolf shall dwell.* [n: (HIER.)] Our judaizing Jews, etc., *as far as* nothing evil, save baseness.

[n: (ID.)] The calf and the sheep, the humble and the innocent, etc., *as far as* let the Church furnish this in the world.

VERS. 7.-- *The lion as the ox,* etc. [n: (ID.)] For the princes of this world, and other simple folk, are content with the surface of the history; for they do not understand the wheat and the marrow, that is, the inner sense.

VERS. 8.-- *The infant.* Christ, or the apostles, children in malice, perfect in understanding: who cast out demons from possessed bodies.

*Of the basilisk.* The basilisk, the king of all serpents, etc., *as far as* from possessed bodies, namely.

[1251B] *The hand.* That is, the divine power: he shall deliver up captive the asp or the basilisk thereupon seized, that is, the devil.

VERS. 9.-- *For it is filled.* After his manner, at the end the prophetic words are laid open, lest they be taken to the letter.

VERS. 10.-- *Who standeth for a sign,* whence: *Behold this child is set for the ruin and for the resurrection of many in Israel, and for a sign which shall be contradicted [n: (Luc. II)]*. The Seventy: Who shall rise up to be a prince, etc., *as far as* the same signifies a sepulchre. For a sleeping and a rest, with another word but the same sense, we have rendered *sepulchre.*

VERS. 11.-- *The remnant.* [n: (ID.)] The Apostle: *The remnant shall be saved.* For the histories relate that the apostles, [1251C] spread abroad through the whole world, preached the Gospel: and indeed they penetrated the Persians and the Indians, so that Ethiopia gave her hand to God.

VERS. 12.-- *A sign.* Of the cross, in which is the victory, that all may know in what the devil was overcome.

*From the four quarters.* [n: (ID.)] This seems to have been fulfilled at Pentecost, etc., *as far as* that the calling of the whole world might be shown.

VERS. 13.-- *And it shall be taken away,* etc. [n: (ID.)] When Isaias foretold this, between the two and the ten tribes there was an implacable discord; but they that believed were made peaceable in the faith of Christ and the unity of charity.

VERS. 14.-- *The precept of the hand,* etc. What they command, they do; and they teach both by word and by example.

VERS. 15.-- *The tongue of the sea.* [n: (ID.)] Which praised idols. [1251D] The sea, that is, the people of Egypt (which above all others worships idols), that it may praise and fear God, and condemn and cast away idols.

*And he shall lift up.* As Idumaea, and Moab, and the children of Ammon shall give their hands to the apostles, so also the Egyptians.

*And he shall strike.* That is, he shall lessen it by setting seven princes for one, etc., *as far as* that they might pass over dry-shod.

*That they may pass through it,* etc. Under a metaphor the Nile is said to be divided and cut into parts, etc., *as far as* the Lord preparing the way by the Gospel.

## CHAPTER XII.

VERS. 1.-- *I will give thanks to thee.* [n: (HIER.)] As, the Red Sea being passed, [1252A] they said: Let us sing to the Lord, etc. So, the kingdom of the world being destroyed, the Church says: *I will give thanks to thee.*

VERS. 3.-- *Out of the fountains of the Saviour.* [n: (ID.)] Whence: *Bless ye the Lord from the fountains of Israel,* etc., *as far as* that the prophecy may agree with the Gospel.

## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- *The burden of Babylon.* [n: (HIER.)] Babylon, the metropolis of the Chaldeans, etc., *as far as* who says: *All these will I give thee, if falling down thou wilt adore me.*

VERS. 7.-- *They shall be dissolved,* etc. Because no work worthy of the justice of God shall be found.

VERS. 8.-- *Burnt.* As it were coals, by excessive grief, or in very fact. For many were burnt. *Burnt with fire.* With the fire which they kindled for themselves, because they cannot say: [1252B] *The light of thy countenance, O Lord, is signed upon us [n: (Psal. IV)]*, and: *But we, beholding with open face the glory of the Lord [n: (II Cor. III)]*. [var: Vulg. 2 Cor 3:18 *Nos vero omnes, revelata facie gloriam Domini speculantes*, "But we all, with open face beholding the glory of the Lord"]

VERS. 10.-- *The sun is darkened.* These things can be referred to the day of judgment, when all the lights shall be seen to be darkened in comparison of the brightness of Christ.

VERS. 11.-- *And I will visit the evils of the world.* I will take vengeance that I may correct, etc., *as far as: But my mercy shall not be taken away from them.*

*And to rest.* As if he should say: This I will do, that he who is proud may be humbled.

VERS. 12.-- *A man shall be more precious than gold,* etc. [n: (ID.)] All rare things are precious, that is, a man of Babylon shall be found more rarely than gold. Likewise the word of the Lord is called precious, that is, they were rarely found. In the day of [1252C] judgment also the Lord shall visit the evils of the world, and shall make the arrogance of the demons to be still, and few elect shall be found because of the persecution of Antichrist.

VERS. 13.-- *I will trouble the heaven,* etc. Not that they are animate, but because of the indignation of the Lord, who looks upon the earth and makes it tremble. Perhaps, the captivity being at hand, the heaven was moved by thundering and the earth by shaking. It can be read of the day of judgment.

VERS. 14.-- *The fleeing doe.* The doe is the devil, or perverse doctrine, whose masters are signified in the doe, which in Greek is called δορκὰς.

VERS. 17.-- *Behold I will stir up,* etc. We read in Genesis X that Madai also, the son of Noe, was the author of the Medes: who is interpreted measure, or from a powerful or a strong one. Therefore against the Babylonians, who are confounded in mind, [1252D] there are stirred up by the powerful and strong God fortitudes for punishing, which shall render to every one according to his works.

VERS. 19.-- *And Babylon shall be that city,* etc. [n: (ID.)] It was not afterward rebuilt, etc., *as far as* to be devoured by wolves and dogs.

VERS. 21.-- *Beasts,* etc. By all these beasts that shun the light we understand the fortitudes of demons or of angels, to whom they are delivered up for punishment.

*Ostriches.* Of which there has been treatment enough in Job: which are not raised higher from the earth, although they seem to have wings.

*Hairy ones.* Wild shaggy men, who are also incubi, or satyrs, or kinds of demons.

[1253A]VERS. 22.-- *Owls in the houses.* Of the bigness of a raven, but sprinkled with spots, which with their beak set in the marsh make a horrid screeching.

*Sirens.* They are serpents crested and winged, like other sea-fishes, in the shape of a woman; or portents of the devil, which by sweet singing deceive men, the shipwreck of this world, as they pass by with ears not closed.
