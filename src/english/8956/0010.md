## CHAPTER XV.

VERS. 1.-- *The burden of Moab.* This is the vision of Isaiah had concerning the burden, that is, concerning the destruction of Moab. Moab, the son of Lot, whose offspring and province was called Moab: in it is Ar, which is now Ariopolis the metropolis.

*The burden of Moab.* [n: (HIER.)] There is a circumcision carnal and spiritual, etc., *as far as* Lot too, when Sodom was perishing, came into Segor, and the sun rose.

[1256A] *Ar,* that is, adversary, etc., *as far as* afterward they were laid waste by the Chaldeans.

VERS. 2.-- *The house is gone up.* To the high places, in which it had been proud, that it might not sacrifice, but that it might bewail where it had sinned.

*Dibon.* [n: (ID.)] Their flowing: A lie passes and flows away, and never remains in the same state; but the word of the Lord is compact and stable. Whence manna is seen as it were ice: it does not flow, but stands firm.

*Upon Nabo.* [n: (ID.)] Because the sense of all this prophecy also is one, etc., *as far as* when their soul shall have howled to itself.

VERS. 3.-- *Every howling.* Another reading: Every one howls, Moab namely, and so it shall go down into weeping. [1256B] As if: It goes up to the high places, that it may weep before the idols. But when it found no help there, it went down to weeping into the land or into its own house.

VERS. 4.-- *Hesebon shall cry out.* From the beginning of the kingdom, which is in Hesebon, as far as Jasa the city, where it ends, everywhere clamor, everywhere mourning.

*The ready men of Moab shall howl.* Away from their wives, brave warriors. Hence Abraham is said to have chosen the home-born servants of his house ready, that is, without wives.

VERS. 5.-- *My heart to Moab.* That is, that I may provoke them to repentance, etc., *as far as* which is interpreted *the lion of God.*

*Unto Segor.* [n: (ID.)] Segor is one of the five cities of the Sodomites, etc., *as far as* if after the deliverance she had not sinned.

[1256C]VERS. 6.-- *Nemrim.* Nemrim is a town upon the sea, etc., *as far as* and therefore the grass shall wither.

VERS. 7.-- *And their visitation.* Visitation in evil: *I will visit their iniquities with a rod [n: (Psal. LXXXVIII)]*; visitation in good: *The Orient from on high hath visited us [n: (Luc. I)]*.

*To the torrent of the willows.* Thus far concerning the captivity of the Moabites by the Assyrians, now concerning that which was by the Chaldeans.

VERS. 8.-- *Its howling.* As if: therefore shall Moab howl, because where there was riot before, and delight by reason of the watered places and the pleasantness of the springs, there now are streams of blood.

VERS. 9.-- *The water of Dibon.* Dibon, silence; Dibon, weeping; and yet it is one city.

## CHAPTER XVI.

[1256D]

VERS.--1. *Send forth the lamb.* [n: (HIER.)] For this reason the prophets are scarcely understood, etc., *as far as* who may bear rule in the whole world.

*The rock of the desert.* [n: (ID.)] Ruth, namely, because according to the precept of the Lord, etc., *as far as* for as Christ came for either people, so he willed to be born of either.

*To the mount of the daughter of Sion.* Jerusalem, or rather the Church, which is in contemplation as it were the daughter of watching. In Jerusalem also he chiefly preached, wrought miracles, suffered, died, and rose again, and redeemed the human race.

VERS. 2.-- *So shall they be.* So shall they pass over into captivity [1257A] by Arnon: which is the boundary of the Moabites and of the Amorrhites.

[n: (HIER.)] *Arnon* (which is interpreted illumination) of those who, their errors forsaken, pass over to the knowledge of the truth.

VERS. 3.-- *Take counsel.* Do nothing without counsel, and be not carried about with every wind of doctrine; follow the angel of great counsel.

*Gather counsel.* [var: Vulg. Is 16:3 *coge concilium*, "call the assembly together"] Out of the straying, namely, and the erring, by making a Church.

*Set as night.* Thy tabernacle, namely, in which thou didst think to rest, etc., *as far as* that it may receive and hide with itself the fleeing people of God.

VERS. 4.-- *My fugitives.* Who have fled from me, who have gone out from the Church, who have followed their own sense, [1257B] not the doctrine of the Holy Spirit.

*For the dust is at an end.* He passes suddenly to the slaying of Sennacherib, who is dust and wretched, because his glory is as dust which the wind casts forth from the face of the earth, and he passed wretchedly from this light, slain by his own sons in the temple of his god.

VERS. 5.-- *And he shall prepare.* [var: Vulg. Is 16:5 *Et praeparabitur*, passive, "and a throne shall be prepared"] The Jews expound this of Ezechias, who, after the death of Sennacherib, reigned in peace. But they are better referred to Christ, and some things are referred to the first, some to the second advent.

VERS. 6.-- *He shall sit upon it.* Resting and judging, upon the hearts of the good.

*We have heard the pride of Moab.* After the manner of Scripture, because it had announced glad things, lest it should make them negligent, it terrifies [1257C] with a threatening.

*And the indignation.* [n: (ID.)] Against God he set his mouth in heaven, disparaging the prophets. Adding that word of the Gospel: *All who came to thee before me were thieves and robbers.* [var: Vulg. Jo. 10:8 *Omnes quotquot venerunt, fures sunt, et latrones*, "All, as many as came, are thieves and robbers"] He calls Moses and David murderers, Josue a man of blood.

VERS. 7.-- *He shall howl.* One to another, that is, all the diversities of heretics and of philosophers shall bellow against one another in torments.

*Upon the walls.* Not built of squared stone, as they are the temple of God, in which the stones are so polished that neither hammer was heard, nor axe.

VERS. 8.-- *The suburbs of Hesebon.* All their thoughts (which is what Hesebon signifies) do not belong to the city of God; of which: *The stream of the river maketh joyful the [1257D] city of God [n: (Psal. XLV)]*; but they are suburbs, that they may be believed to belong to the city of the Lord.

*Sabama.* Lifting up height, etc., *as far as* the lords of the nations, therefore, the apostles and apostolic men, cut off the shoots of the vine of Sabama.

*They wandered.* Ranging over the whole land of Moab.

*In the desert.* That at last they might not have those whom they should slay.

*The branches.* He pursues the metaphor of the vine.

*They passed over the sea.* [n: (ID.)] That they might see the wonders of the Lord in the deep of temptations, delivered out of them.

VERS. 9.-- *The vine:* The vine of Moab, from the neighbourhood of the place, is such as Sodom.

*Sabama.* He who lifts himself up against the sentence of God.

[1258A]VERS. 10.-- *From Carmel.* Carmel is a mountain between Phoenicia and Palestine overhanging Ptolemais, in which Elias prayed. And it is often put for any fertility whatever; whence here also: *Gladness and rejoicing shall be taken away from Carmel [n: (III Reg. XVIII)]*, that is, the fertility in which the Moabites rejoiced.

*He shall rejoice.* Because heretics boast that they have the knowledge of the spiritual circumcision.

*The voice.* Lest the poisons of the dragon be pressed out thence, and the drinkers be slain.

VERS. 11.-- *My belly.* The prophet, who as a harp, composed by the musician's art, and conceiving from the fear of God, begets many children, that every string may render its own sound.

*And the bowels.* As a harp does not [1258B] send forth a composed sound, etc., *as far as* and James, to whom one is lacking, to him all are lacking.

VERS. 12.-- *Her holy things.* To the holy thing, namely, of the Church, which she shall endeavour to make her own, she shall not prevail: for we do not at once perfectly take hold of virtue.

*She shall not prevail.* In them, namely, they shall not find the help in which she hoped, and so it appeared, because she worshipped in vain.

VERS. 13.-- *This word.* In the preface, etc., *as far as.* For what had begun for thee, here is completed.

VERS. 14.-- *In three years:* as if he should say: As few clusters remain, etc., *as far as* but, his error forsaken, converted, for three years only he is left small and slight and inglorious.

*Of a hireling.* [n: (ID.)] False doctrine does all things for the sake of gain, [1258C] etc., *as far as* it is a lighter sin to sin openly than to feign sanctity.
