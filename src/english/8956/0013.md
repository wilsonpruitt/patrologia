## CHAPTER XXI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1262C]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *The burden of the desert of the sea.* ⟦s2⟧ Of Babylon, called a sea for the multitude of its people.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It cometh from the desert.* ⟦s2⟧ In which the Lord was tempted, in which the Israelites were struck by serpents.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *He that is unbelieving.* ⟦s2⟧ A general sentence, as though it were the fault of individuals that they are overwhelmed in the storm.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Go up, Aelam.* ⟦s2⟧ The LXX: The Aelamites and the legates of the Persians. ⟦s3⟧ Aelamites, despisers. ⟦s4⟧ *Go up,* as if he should say: O Persians and Medes, fear not, mount your horses, besiege Babylon. ⟦s5⟧ *Go up.* ⟦s6⟧ Trusting in the strength of God, he speaks boldly against his adversaries.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *All the mourning.* ⟦s2⟧ Whereby it made thee and other nations to mourn; [1262D] or, that none may now mourn, pressed by the power of Babylon; or, that it may be afflicted with so great evils that it dare not mourn against thee.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Prepare the table.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: O Persians and Medes, take food, etc., *as far as* wherein ye may quench the fiery darts of the devil.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Behold,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ What may fall upon him after the table; or, behold, O Balthasar, what he has written on the wall: *Mane, Tethel, Phares.* [var: Vulg. Dn 5:25 *Mane, Thecel, Phares*]

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *A watchman.* ⟦s2⟧ Those who watch are called prophets.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *A rider.* ⟦s2⟧ Cyrus the prince was ruling over the Medes and the Persians, etc., *as far as* the letter, namely, and the spirit, refer to the two Testaments.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1263A]VERS. 8.-- *A lion.* ⟦s2⟧ The same is a lion for the strength of his mind, who is a watchman by the prerogative of contemplation.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Upon the watchtower of the Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, set by day in contemplation, ready by days and by nights to hear and to speak the things which he shall have commanded.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Standing all the nights.* ⟦s2⟧ That I may keep myself from all sin, that I may be worthy to hear the word of the Lord.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Behold this man cometh.* ⟦s2⟧ What follows, he signifies that he has seen, namely that the Saviour comes, sitting upon a two-horse chariot, and joining two animals, that is, an ass and a camel, to one chariot.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It is fallen, it is fallen.* ⟦s2⟧ When Cyrus and Darius were laying Babylon waste, the idols also were broken in pieces, the temple of Bel being overthrown and burned with fire.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *My threshing.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: O people, [1263B] who art to be stored up in my barns, whom I have threshed with divers straits, that I might beat out the chaff and gather the pure wheat into the barns, the things which I have heard from the Lord, from the God of Israel, etc.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *The burden of Duma.* ⟦s2⟧ The LXX: Of Idumaea, etc., *as far as* the burden of Duma crieth to me.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He crieth to me.* [n: (HIER.)] God, namely, my keeper, etc., *as far as* he speaks thereafter to the nations: *If ye seek me,* seek *more earnestly [n: (Joan. IX)]*. [var: Vulg. Is 21:12 *si quaeritis, quaerite*, "if you seek, seek"]

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *The morning cometh,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if: to my people I will give the light of gladness, etc., *as far as* by working, and so I will hear you.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *The burden in Arabia.* ⟦s2⟧ The Arabs are the Saracens, etc., *as far as* thence God addresses the fleeing Jews thus:

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1263C] *In the thicket at evening.* ⟦s2⟧ O ye Jews, who shall flee the Babylonian siege in the thicket, that is, ye shall sleep in the woods of the Saracens, fleeing the Chaldeans in the paths of Dodanim, which is interpreted *among the near of kin* or *the kindred,* that is, in the way which leads to your kinsmen the Saracens. [var: Vulg. Is 21:13 *in semitis Dedanim*]

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Evening.* [n: (ID.)] Which is the beginning of night. ⟦s2⟧ He who begins to sin is in the evening; he who has come to the height is in the middle of the night. ⟦s3⟧ In the middle of the night the firstborn of Egypt are slain. ⟦s4⟧ Peter denies; when the light is now near, he repents.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In the paths.* [n: (ID.)] Because evening is the beginning of evils, etc., *as far as* when he rebelled against his father.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Meeting him that is thirsty, bring.* ⟦s2⟧ This can be said against those who have given themselves over to idleness and sloth and are content with their own salvation, and reach out no hand to the penitent and the converted.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1263D] *Bring water to him that is thirsty.* ⟦s2⟧ To your brethren, wearied with the burning of the sun and with thirst, bring water, lest they perish in the wilderness.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Yet in one,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: Because ye have not had mercy upon them, ye shall soon endure the misery of captivity. ⟦s3⟧ A year after the captivity of the Jews and the overthrow of Jerusalem, these also were laid waste.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As in the year of a hireling:* [n: (ID.)] which is: As a hireling his year, etc., *as far as* and shall have eaten the heavenly bread.

## CHAPTER XXII.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *The burden of the valley.* ⟦s2⟧ Jerusalem, once the mistress of the nations, etc., *as far as* who have fallen from the lofty sense of the Scriptures to the lowest things.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1264A] *The valley of vision.* ⟦s2⟧ The LXX: Sion, that is, of the watchtower, namely of the Church.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *What aileth thee also.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: Since philosophy and worldly wisdom, disputing of lofty things, despises the simplicity of the Church, why dost thou also follow after high things?

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Full.* ⟦s2⟧ The doctrine of the heretics is full of much talking and of clamor.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thy slain.* [n: (HIER.)] Of the valley, namely of Sion, by their own will, that is, they went over to the heretics. ⟦s2⟧ In comparison with those who have been conquered after a battle, they are more unhappily wounded who give themselves up of their own accord: as in martyrdom, he who denies after torments is more lightly punished than he who denies Christ without compulsion.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *All the princes,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As is read in Jeremias, [1264B] Sedechias by night through a hole in the wall, etc., *as far as* and they were led into Babylon.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They fled.* ⟦s2⟧ In flight of one mind, whose prince is the fleeing dragon, the crooked serpent.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And of weepings.* [n: (ID.)] The LXX: Of error. ⟦s2⟧ Not that weeping and error are from the Lord, but from the occasion of the Scriptures which he gave them to read there arises an occasion of error, and they can say: *Why hast thou made us to err from thy way? [n: (Isa. LXIII.)]* [var: Vulg. Is 63:17 *Quare errare nos fecisti, Domine, de viis tuis*, "Why hast thou made us to err, O Lord, from thy ways?"]

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Elam.* ⟦s2⟧ A city in the province of Persia, in which are the best archers: *Elam took the quiver.* ⟦s3⟧ Elam, their ascent, that is, the pride of the heretics, took the quiver, *that he may shoot in the dark at the upright of heart.* [var: Vulg. Ps 10:3 *ut sagittent*, plural, "that they may shoot"]

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The wall.* ⟦s2⟧ He stripped bare the bulwarks of the Church with his shield, that is, by his assault.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1264C]VERS. 8.-- *The covering.* ⟦s2⟧ The temple, which before was covered and shut against the nations and the polluted.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The armoury.* ⟦s2⟧ Beside the temple was a house which David built for the storing of arms, about which were many trees; whence it is called the house of the forest. ⟦s3⟧ It too was not of stone, but of wood: for from the moisture of the mortar and of the stones (as they say) iron rusts.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *And you gathered together.* ⟦s2⟧ The land of promise, although most rich, etc., *as far as* the lower, which could be taken by the enemy, the upper, which could not.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *And a pool.* [n: (ID.)] Not a well which has living and eternal water, but broken pools which cannot hold water.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Between two walls.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, both the New and the Old [1264D] Testament: they made for themselves new waters, which they think are more inward and contain greater mysteries than the Old Testament.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *And the Lord God of hosts shall call.* ⟦s2⟧ Daily the Lord provokes the heretics to repentance, etc., *as far as* but, making light of it and despairing, they said: *Let us eat and drink, for to-morrow we shall die [n: (Jerem. XXXIV)]*.

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Ye shall die.* [n: (ID.)] To your vices, or ye shall go down with them to the lower regions, etc., *as far as* the things which are here said do not belong to the captivity made in the time of Ezechias.

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Sobna.* [n: (HIER.)] Turn thee now, etc., *as far as* whence the Lord threatens him with these evils

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1265A]VERS. 16.-- *What dost thou here?* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: Why dost thou wish to build thee a house? etc., *as far as* than which it is to be called a sepulchre and a memorial.

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Crowning he will crown thee.* ⟦s2⟧ Thou, namely, who once hadst the crown of the pontiff, and the sanctification in a plate of gold (in which the name of God was written), shalt be crowned with tribulation and anguish.

⟦p48⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *With tribulation.* ⟦s2⟧ In place of the golden plate in which the name of the Lord was written, which the priest bore on his forehead once in the year.

⟦p49⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *As a ball.* ⟦s2⟧ As a sphere cannot stand in a sloping and spacious place, so thy people shall be scattered to the ends of the world.

⟦p50⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *My servant Eliacim.* ⟦s2⟧ By Sobna the proud, etc., *as far as the son of Helcias,* that is, of the father [1265B] of the Lord.

⟦p51⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *The key.* ⟦s2⟧ By the key which he bears upon his shoulder, the sceptre of the kingdom and the sign of victory, that is, *the cross we can* understand, which Christ himself bore upon his own shoulder.

⟦p52⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The house of David.* ⟦s2⟧ He calls the house of David the house of the Lord, in which was the golden key.

⟦p53⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And he shall open.* ⟦s2⟧ Whence: *He that openeth and no man shutteth, shutteth and no man openeth.* ⟦s3⟧ What he unlocked in his passion cannot be shut; what he shut in the ceremonies, no man shall open.

⟦p54⟧ ⟦s1+s2+s3⟧ VERS. 24.-- *And I will hang upon.* [var: Vulg. Is 22:24 *Et suspendent super eum*, "And they shall hang upon him"] Whence in the Gospel: *All the people hung upon him.* [var: Vulg. Lc 19:48 *omnis populus suspensus erat, audiens illum*, "all the people was in suspense, hearing him"] To-day also they hang upon the word of his wisdom.

⟦p55⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *The peg shall be taken away.* ⟦s2⟧ The things which are said, etc., [1265C] *as far as* the Jews shall be converted to the faith.
