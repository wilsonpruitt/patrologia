## CHAPTER XXIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *The burden of Tyre.* ⟦s2⟧ Tyre, the most noble city of the Chananites, etc., *as far as* this misery Isaias foretells: *The burden of Tyre,* etc.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Howl ye.* ⟦s2⟧ In the Greek more openly: Howl, ye ships of Carthage: ye, namely, who were wont to come to Tyre for the sake of trading. ⟦s3⟧ Carthage was a colony to the Tyrians, built and tilled by them.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Ships of the sea.* ⟦s2⟧ In Hebrew Tharsis, that is, the exploring of joy or contemplation, because the contemplation of joy and of gladness shall be turned into tears.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Trading.* [n: (HIER.)] Traders, etc., *as far as* and they pass over from other vices to others.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The seed of the Nile, the harvest of the river, the fruits,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is so construed: [1265D] the seed of the river Nile was her harvest and her fruits, namely Tyre's. ⟦s3⟧ The land of the Chananites, in which Tyre is, is unfruitful: Egypt, which the Nile waters, is fruitful in fruits, which Tyre used to exchange with the Egyptians.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *The sea.* ⟦s2⟧ Of the world, namely, in which are small living things with the great; the dragon (whom the Lord formed to make sport of him), seeing those taken by the hunters of Christ who had been taken by the demons, says to the strength contrary to the hunters (which Sidon signifies): Blush, O Sidon.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I have not been in labour.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: It has profited nothing to have been in labour, to have brought forth and nourished up young men, because Tyre was taken without defence.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *When it shall have been heard in Egypt,* etc. [1266A] This is understood both of all the vices and of the heresies, because when one is taken another is greatly afraid, and in the confusion of the one the other feels that it is to be taken.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Pass over, howl, ye that dwell.* ⟦s2⟧ As has been said above, when they could not resist Nabuchodonosor, those hastened who were able to go on board the ships: the rest remained in the city.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Is not this your city, which gloried from the days of old in its antiquity,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if he should say: This I therefore command, because your city Tyre, its old error laid aside, shall pass over to the truth.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Her princes, her traffickers, the nobles of the earth,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Among other traders, or even in kingdoms.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *That he might bring down to disgrace,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Lord is not the author of disgrace, but he shows what [1266B] is disgraceful, according to that word: *He shall defile him with defilement,* not that the priest is the author of the defilement, but that he shows him defiled whom many thought clean.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *The river.* ⟦s2⟧ Of the Babylonians. ⟦s3⟧ *Upon the rivers of Babylon there we sat and wept, while we remembered Sion [n: (Psal. CXXXVI)]*.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *No girdle any more for thee.* ⟦s2⟧ The strength of resisting, for with a girdle we bind up the loins: in the loins we are said to have strength.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *He hath troubled the kingdoms.* ⟦s2⟧ Namely of the Egyptians and of the Moabites, and of the others round about. ⟦s3⟧ For when Tyre had been taken and overthrown, the other kingdoms were troubled with fear. ⟦s4⟧ The kingdom of Egypt also was given to Nabuchodonosor in place of Tyre overthrown.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1266C]VERS. 12.-- *The daughter of Sidon.* [n: (ID.)] In the land of Chanaan are Tyre and Sidon; whence: The Chananite woman went out of the borders of Tyre and Sidon, meeting the Saviour [n: (Matth. XV)].

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Pass over.* ⟦s2⟧ Often we see certain men pass over from their own purpose to another, from soldiering to trading: from lawsuits to arms they change their industry, that they may change their ill fortune. ⟦s3⟧ But, by God's dispensation, all things fall out contrary, that, constrained by miseries, they may trust not in themselves, but in the Lord the Creator.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *There was no such people.* ⟦s2⟧ Until the death of Nabuchodonosor the Chaldeans and the Assyrians were the mightiest above all; afterward the people of the Greeks and of the Romans was mightier.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They dug through.* ⟦s2⟧ When Nabuchodonosor invaded Tyre [1266D] with a fleet, he overthrew not only the wall, but the houses also; and what was the trafficking of the cities became for seventy years the drying of nets.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Ships of the sea,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In Hebrew Tharsis, etc., *as far as* the churchman destroys and subjects it to himself.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Seventy years.* [n: (HIER.)] Tyre, once straitened, etc., *as far as* who then may not have hope of salvation, if he shall have sung well, if he shall have well composed the strings, that is, the virtues?

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] The seventh and the seventieth, which is completed either by single days or by seven decades, signifies perfect penance, so that, the time of penance being fulfilled, Tyre is by right restored to her ancient state.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1267A] *The song of a harlot.* ⟦s2⟧ Who, forsaken by her lovers, is wont to compose songs for her consolation, and to call to mind things past; so also Tyre, returned.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Take thee a harp.* [var: Vulg. Is 23:16 *Sume citharam*, without *tibi*] The things which follow concerning this burden look to the return of Tyre.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ *Sing well, often,* etc. [var: Vulg. Is 23:16 *frequenta canticum*, "repeat the song"] He admonishes her always to sing the song of her miseries, lest she suffer the same again. ⟦s3⟧ For they say that this is the nature of music: if it find a man glad, it makes him gladder; if sad, it renders him sadder.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *And her trafficking shall be, and her hire.* ⟦s2⟧ As Jerome says on Jeremias, etc., *as far as* they sacrificed their hire to the Lord.
