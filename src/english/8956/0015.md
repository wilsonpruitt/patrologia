## CHAPTER XXIV.

VERS. 1.-- *Behold the Lord shall make naked,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] [1267B] After the particular rebuke of the several nations, etc., *as far as* he passes over from the Egyptians and many others to the general burden of the last judgment.

VERS. 2.-- *It shall be.* [n: (ID.)] All alike shall either be rewarded for their good deeds or damned for their evil ones. There shall be no difference between the noble and the base-born, the priest and the layman, the servant and the lord, etc. For all shall stand on equal terms before the tribunal of Christ; nor shall there be any respect of persons with God.

*So the priest.* A good people with a good priest shall receive the crown of life; the wicked together shall bear the torments of hell without respect of person.

*The earth.* [n: (ID.)] Earthly works shall be brought to nothing, so that, the image of the earthly man being abolished, the image of the heavenly may remain. For flesh and blood shall [1267C] not possess the kingdom of God, because this mortal shall put on immortality.

VERS. 6.-- *A curse shall devour the earth.* As in the beginning God blessed every creature, so in the end he curses those who are earthly; and not sojourners, but dwellers in the earth: those also who sin in it, forgetful of their condition, raging against one another. And few shall remain, who have the image of the heavenly.

VERS. 7.-- *The vintage hath mourned, the vine hath been made weak.* [n: (ID.)] At the consummation, the remembrance of the world's past delights shall be matter of torment; whence the rich man clothed in purple, who had received good things in his lifetime, lifting up his eyes out of hell, beholds Lazarus at rest [n: (Luc. XIX)]; whence: *Woe to you that are filled, for you shall hunger [n: (Luc. VI)]*.

*All have groaned who shall rejoice,* [var: Vulg. Is 24:7 *qui laetabantur corde*, "who rejoiced in heart"] etc. This testimony [1267D] we press upon those who at banquets riot not only in gluttony and drunkenness, but in the ears, so that through all the senses the strength of the soul is softened.

VERS. 10.-- *The city of vanity is broken down.* The world, in which all things are vain, vanity of vanities and all is vanity.

VERS. 11.-- *There shall be a crying over wine.* By wine, error; for drunken men go astray. By the streets (which in Greek are named from breadth) we understand the spacious and broad way which leads to death. Over these, then, who have gone astray in the broad and spacious way of this world, there shall be a cry of grief in the day of judgment.

VERS. 13.-- *In the midst of the earth.* Jerusalem, which is (as they say) the navel of the earth. And many suppose that the Lord, being about to judge upon Mount Sion, [1268A] from which he ascended, shall sit in the air, shall come in a cloud.

*As if a few olives,* etc. Few being left, when persecution shall have devoured the earth, etc., *as far as* the clusters also being gathered, scarcely a few remain on the empty vines.

VERS. 14.-- *And they shall praise: when the Lord shall have been glorified.* [n: (ID.)] They shall praise who shall be able to remain and to flee the oppression of Antichrist, or the burning of the hell that hangs over them.

*They shall neigh.* By the likeness of horses, showing forth the greatness of their gladness.

VERS. 16.-- *From the ends of the earth we have heard praises, the glory of the just one.* In Hebrew, *the wings of the earth.* Now the wings or the ends of the earth are the prophets and the apostles, who fly from earthly things to heavenly, and bound the manner of living by certain ends of precepts. The saints therefore say: [1268B] *As* from the prophets and the apostles *we have heard, so* now do we *see* it fulfilled.

*My secret to myself.* [n: (ID.)] He can neither fully declare the rewards of the good, nor the punishments of the wicked, which he foresees in the spirit; and thence he grieves, saying: *Woe is me.*

VERS. 17.-- *Fear and the pit.* These things may be referred both to the times of the martyrs, etc., *as far as* so also shall it be in the time of Antichrist.

VERS. 18.-- *They are opened,* etc. That God may see the iniquities of Antichrist and judge them. For while he does not punish sins, he makes as though he did not see them.

*The foundations of the earth.* The princes of the world, earthly works.

VERS. 19.-- *With breaking shall it be broken.* Not that the earth is reduced to dust, or to nothing, but because all earthly things shall pass away. And as a drunken man knows not [1268C] what he does, so all who dwell in the earth shall be drunk with the greatness of their evils and shall be astonished at all things.

VERS. 20.-- *A tent of one night.* [n: (ID.)] Which is carried off by the traveller, so that no trace remains, so shall the figure of this world pass away [n: (I Cor. VII)], and the earth shall be deserted so that there be no dwelling of men in it any more.

VERS. 21.-- *And it shall be,* etc. As if he should say: The windows of heaven are opened, so that, the Lord looking upon the sins of the earth, every figure of earthly works may pass away and fall, and not rise again in its former stature. The Lord therefore shall visit in that day upon the host of heaven, that he may judge not only earthly things, but heavenly.

*Upon the host.* [n: (ID.)] *My sword is inebriated in heaven. The stars are not clean in his sight. And in his angels he found depravity [n: (Isa. XXXIV, Job. XV, IV)]*. [var: Vulg. Job 15:15 *caeli non sunt mundi*, "the heavens are not clean"]

[1268D] *The kings of the earth.* [n: (HIER.)] *The rulers of darkness, against the spirits of wickedness in the high places [n: (Ephes. VI)]*; whence in Daniel: *The prince of the kingdom of the Persians went out to meet me, and the prince of the kingdom of the Medes [n: (Dan. X)]* [var: Vulg. Dan. 10 has neither this sentence nor any *prince of the kingdom of the Medes*; 10:13 sets *princeps regni Persarum* and 10:20 *princeps Graecorum*]

VERS. 22.-- *And they shall be gathered together.* [n: (ID.)] Origen says that, after this sentence: *Go, ye cursed, into everlasting fire [n: (Matth. XXV)]*, after many days, that is after a thousand years, they are to be visited and set free. But the Church does not receive this. For it has been said: *The wicked shall go into everlasting punishment, but the just into life everlasting [n: (Matth. XIII)]*. [var: Vulg. Matt. 25:46 *Et ibunt hi*, "and these shall go"] Now what is everlasting lacks all end, and as the joy is everlasting, so also is the punishment.

*One bundle into the pit.* [var: Vulg. Is 24:22 *unius fascis*, "of one bundle"] Whence: *Bind them in bundles to burn.*

[1269A] *And after many.* It is to be noted that he does not add: by the Lord or by the angels, etc., *as far as* which has been left to the Lord's judgment.

VERS. 23.-- *And it shall blush.* Whence the creature groaneth and travaileth, etc., *as far as* such as was Abraham, dead in a good old age.

## CHAPTER XXV.

VERS. 1.-- *Amen.* [n: (HIER.)] A Hebrew word, which the Lord often uses in the Gospel: *Amen, amen,* that is, truly, truly, *I say to you.*

VERS. 2.-- *A city.* Overthrown by Nabuchodonosor, it was restored by Nehemias and by Jesus the high priest. Overthrown by the Romans, it was restored by Aelius Hadrian; but not in the same place, nor to its former state.

*And for ever.* [n: (ID.)] That the fable of the thousand years' reign [1269B] and of the golden and jewelled Jerusalem may rest.

VERS. 10.-- *And Moab shall be threshed,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Although he was speaking generally of the consummation of the world, etc., *as far as* by Moab therefore we rightly understand the devil, always the adversary of the sons of God.

*Straw in a wain.* Straw is threshed by iron-shod wains, that the beasts may the more easily both feed upon it and chew the cud.
