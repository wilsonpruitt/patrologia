## CHAPTER XXXI

VERS. 1.-- *Woe to them that go down into Egypt.* [n: (ID.)] He has treated of the glory of the good and of the punishment of the reprobate; now he returns to the remnant which was left [1275A] in the land of Juda, when Sedecias and the others had been led into Babylon. But first he warned them not to go into Egypt: now, because they have gone, he threatens evil.

*They have not trusted in the Holy One of Israel.* They sought not with the heart, but with words; for they said to Jeremias: *Pray for us to the Lord, what we ought to do?* and they refused to obey. To which is like: *They have not called upon God.* Which was said of wicked priests.

VERS. 2.-- *But he that is wise.* Whence Jeremias: *Cursed be the man that trusteth in man,* etc., *as far as* the place where the Lord was crucified is specially called *Sodom and Egypt [n: (Apoc. XI)]*.

VERS. 4.-- *For thus saith.* [n: (HIER.)] This of those who, led into captivity with Sedecias, after seventy years, by the Lord's mercy, returned, when the Israelites and the Egyptians had been slain in [1275B] Egypt, in whose help they trusted.

VERS. 5.-- *So shall the Lord protect.* Thus did the Lord against the Samaritans, who attempted to hinder the building of the temple. And in the time of the Machabees he routed many by a few.

VERS. 6.-- *Turn ye again.* In prophecy he keeps neither the order of time nor of history, etc., *as far as* so with the whole affection of the mind they might return.

VERS. 9.-- *Fire is in Sion.* Sion, the Church, in which is a fire and a furnace to devour sinners, that is, to consume wood, hay and stubble.

## CHAPTER XXXII.

VERS. 1.-- *Behold, in justice shall he reign.* [n: (HIER.)] The prophecy concerning those who had gone down into Egypt being finished, he begins [1275C] concerning the coming of Christ and of his apostles.

VERS. 2.-- *And a man shall be as.* [n: (ID.)] As if he should say: He shall be as safe in tribulations as one who, fleeing the wind and the whirlwind, hides himself in a safe place; and who finds the purest springs in the desert; who avoids the heat of the sun by an overhanging rock.

VERS. 9.-- *Women.* [n: (ID.)] He addresses the matrons who are to be taken captive by the Romans, that they may do penance; or, by the women he marks the Synagogues, or the cities of Judaea. *Rise up, ye rich women:* After the calling of the nations, over whom Christ has ruled in justice, the discourse is directed to the rich women.

VERS. 10.-- *After days and a year.* Whence: *Behold, now is the acceptable time, behold, now is the day of salvation [n: (II Cor. VI)]*; and elsewhere: *The acceptable year of the Lord [n: (Isa. LXI)]*.

[1275D] *After days.* That is, the preaching of Christ. For those who were impenitent, although not straightway led captive, yet were straightway troubled, because they refused to receive the preaching of the Lord.

VERS. 12.-- *Mourn upon the breasts. Daughters of Jerusalem, weep not over me, but weep for yourselves, and for your children.* [var: Vulg. Is 32:12 continues *super regione desiderabili, super vinea fertili*; the words Migne sets here are Luke 23:28]

VERS. 14.-- *Of the wild asses.* [n: (ID.)] Of any wild beasts and birds whatsoever, which dwelt there for a long time.

VERS. 15.-- *Until there be poured out.* Let the Holy Spirit, at the preaching of Elias and Enoch, come down upon us, as In Pentecost upon the apostles. And the prophet joins himself to them by suffering with them.

[1276A] *The desert.* The solitude, namely, of the nations shall be turned into the riches of Israel, and Israel shall be reputed among the nations.

VERS. 17.-- *And the service of justice.* That we may pray in silence, not in the streets, not crying out, not in much speaking.

VERS. 18.-- *In tabernacles.* [n: (ID.)] Already he dwells confidently in the mansions of heaven, whence: *In my Father's house there are many mansions [n: (Joan. XIV)]*; or, in the Churches which hope trustingly in the Lord.

VERS. 19.-- *Hail,* as if he should say: When the Christian people shall have sat in the beauty of peace, then the wrath of God shall rage against the wood.

VERS. 20.-- *Blessed are you that sow.* [n: (ID.)] In the exposition of Ecclesiastes. That master is held worthy of blessedness who sows upon the watered breast of his hearers, [1276B] the people being gathered together both from the Jews and from the nations.

## CHAPTER XXXIII.

VERS. 1.-- *Woe to thee that spoilest.* [n: (ID.)] Against Sennacherib: these things may also be referred to any plunderer and to the devil himself.

VERS. 2.-- *O Lord, have mercy on us.* [n: (ID.)] In the person of the people besieged by Sennacherib, or rather, of the apostles and of all the faithful.

*In the morning.* Those who were left of the army of Sennacherib; they saw their companions dead and were turned to flight.

VERS. 3.-- *Of the angel.* Of Michael, namely, who is the prince of the Jews, or of Gabriel, the strength and leader of battles.

VERS. 4.-- *The bruchus.* From the locust the bruchus is born, etc., *as far as* the dead also beget bruchi.

[1276C]VERS. 6.-- *In thy times.* O Ezechias; or, in his times, according to others, namely of the people set free.

*His.* [n: (HIER.)] Of salvation, namely: for without fear neither wisdom nor knowledge profits anything, whence: *Thou shalt have many good things if thou fear God [n: (Tob. IV)]*; [var: Vulg. Tob. 4:23 *multa bona habebimus si timuerimus Deum*, first person plural, "we shall have many good things if we fear God"] and: *The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom [n: (Psal. CX)]*.

VERS. 8.-- *The covenant.* Which the Lord made with Abraham, namely: *I will multiply thy seed as the stars,* etc. [n: (Gen. XXII)]; [var: Vulg. Gen. 22:17 *sicut stellas caeli*, accusative, "as the stars of heaven"] and elsewhere: *Thou shalt dwell in this place from henceforth and for ever [n: (Zach. XI)]*.

VERS. 9.-- *Libanus.* The temple: *Basan,* the country of Og, which Moses gave beyond the Jordan to the two tribes and a half: this also most fruitful. Carmel, a mountain of the land of promise, most fertile. By these things therefore we ought to understand that the Jews have lost all fruitfulness.

[1276D]VERS. 14.-- *Which of you shall be able.* He recounts the mighty works of God and calls God a fire.

VERS. 17.-- *The land.* Of the living. *Blessed are the meek, for they shall possess the land,* or, being placed in heaven, they despise earthly things.

VERS. 20.-- *A tabernacle that.* [n: (ID.)] The tabernacle which the Jews had through the desert, etc., *as far as* but the Church is founded immovable upon the firm rock.

VERS. 22.-- *For the Lord.* [n: (ID.)] *He shall judge the world in justice.* [var: Vulg. Ps 95:13 *judicabit orbem terrae in aequitate*, "shall judge the world in equity"]

VERS. 23.-- *Thy tacklings are loosed,* etc. He returns to the earthly Jerusalem, and speaks of her under the figure of a ship: the things above were said of the city of our solemnity [1277A] which the Lord surrounds with his rivers.

## CHAPTER XXXIV.

VERS. 4.-- *All the host of the heavens.* [n: (HIER.)] The sun, and the moon, and the stars shall seem to be darkened, when the true light, which is Christ, grows bright. The evil spirits also, their power lost, shall be shut up in hell.

*And they shall be folded together as a book.* Not the inner one, namely, etc., *as far as* for nothing shall afterwards be written in them.

VERS. 6.-- *In Bosra.* [n: (ID.)] In the city of the world fortified against God. For Bosra means fortified, or from God; for the world is made firm by the will of the Lord; whence: *He hath founded it upon the seas:* and: *I have established the pillars thereof.* Bosra and Edom the masters of the Hebrews will have to be understood as Rome, and will have all things to be said of her which are contained in the following chapter.

[1277B]VERS. 8.-- *For it is the day of the vengeance of the Lord, the year of retribution.* [var: Vulg. Is 34:8 *annus retributionum judicii Sion*, "the year of the recompenses of the judgment of Sion"] After the general vengeance upon the world, he returns to Jerusalem, to whom he was speaking first, and describes her destruction in plain speech, which was wrought by the Romans.

VERS. 11.-- *The ibis.* A water-bird hostile to serpents: Josephus says that these birds were carried by Moses into the war against the Ethiopians; because serpents abound in that land through which he was to go.

VERS. 12.-- *Her nobles.* For the apostles had already passed over to the nations: other faithful, by the admonition of the Holy Spirit, when the captivity was at hand, passed over the Jordan into the kingdom of Agrippa, who had peace with the Romans.

## CHAPTER XXXV.

[1277C]VERS. 1.-- *Shall rejoice.* The captivity of the Jews having been set forth, he treats of the conversion of the nations.

VERS. 2.-- *Saron.* Of the people of the Jews; whence: *Saron shall be as a desert [n: (Isa. XXXII)]*. [var: Vulg. Is 33:9 *factus est Saron sicut desertum*, past tense, "Saron is become as a desert"]

VERS. 7.-- *The greenness of the reed.* The fulfilling of Scripture, which is signified by the reed; and the help of the intention, which by the rush: for the reed and the rush spring up and grow green beside the waters.

## CHAPTER XXXVI.

VERS. 1.-- *And it came to pass.* [n: (HIER.)] The books of Kings and of Paralipomenon agree, etc., *as far as* he besieged Jerusalem.

VERS. 3.-- *Sobna.* [n: (ID.)] Sobna the scribe, another man, not the proud one, who, as the Hebrews hand down, terrified by the threatening of Rabsaces, delivered up the lower part of the city, [1277D] the citadel and the temple remaining.

VERS. 4.-- *Thus saith.* As perhaps he had heard from his father, thus saith the Lord: and that his words might have greater weight, he marks the arrogance: Thus saith the great king, as it were the contrary power.

VERS. 6.-- *Behold thou trustest.* [n: (ID.)] He lies plainly. For nowhere is it read that Ezechias put his hope in Egypt.

VERS. 8.-- *Neither wilt thou be able.* Not from weakness, but from the keeping of the precepts of the Lord, who through Moses commanded concerning the king of Israel, saying: *Thou shalt not multiply to thyself horses, and chariots, nor many wives.* [var: Vulg. Deut. 17:16–17 *non multiplicabit sibi equos… Non habebit uxores plurimas*, third person, and with no mention of chariots]

VERS. 16.-- *Make a blessing with me,* etc. Praise me, bless me. Or as another [1278A] edition has it: Do what is useful for you, that you may receive me and peace may remain with you.
