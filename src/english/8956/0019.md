## CHAPTER XXXVII.

VERS. 1.-- *And it came to pass, when king Ezechias had heard it, he rent his garments.* [n: (ID.)] They rend their garments, because they had heard Rabsaces blaspheming. The king rent them, believing that this had come about from his own sins and the people's.

VERS. 2.-- *Isaias the son of Amos.* In the book of Kings it is read, who sent to Isaias the prophet. But here, because Isaias is weaving the history about himself, he names himself not the prophet, but the son of the prophet.

VERS. 4.-- *For the remnant.* For the ten tribes had already been led away, the cities of Juda and Benjamin laid waste: the lower part of the city also delivered into the hands of the enemy by the wicked and proud pontiff Sobna. *Lift up thine eyes,* etc.

[1278B]VERS. 7.-- *Behold I will give him a spirit,* etc. As if he should say: I will not tell all the things that I shall do to the Assyrian, lest I seem to boast of my own power, but this one thing: *Behold I will give.*

VERS. 8.-- *And he found.* That Sennacherib king of the Assyrians fought against the Egyptians, etc., *as far as* the writers of Chaldean history.

VERS. 15.-- *He prayed.* [n: (HIER.)] Now more secure of deliverance. For before he went up, but he is not read to have prayed, because for his own sins he feared that these things would come to pass.

VERS. 18.-- *For truly, O Lord:* it is proved by many histories that the kings of the Persians came into Greece, and overthrew and spoiled the temples.

VERS. 21.-- *And he sent.* The history of the book of Kings and of Paralipomenon is mingled with the prophecy, because there is [1278C] a prophecy concerning the deliverance of the city, concerning the destruction of the Assyrian, concerning the turning back of the sun, and concerning the years added to Ezechias.

VERS. 22.-- *The daughter of Jerusalem.* She who before had not answered, is now sure of the vengeance, secure of the punishment.

VERS. 25.-- *And I have dried up.* So great was the army that they dried up the streams, and therefore made wells by the scarcity of water.

VERS. 29.-- *Pride.* [n: (ID.)] I will bear it no longer, that thou mayest know that thou couldst not do these things by thy own strength, but by my will. For the wicked nations and the unfruitful trees deserved to be cut down by thee, as by an axe.

VERS. 30.-- *But to thee.* [n: (ID.)] All these things the Prophet speaks to Ezechias by messengers, what Sennacherib said, what the Lord answered him: now he turns his words to him himself, lest he doubt of the things to come which he [1278D] foretold. For in this above all were the prophets believed, because they testified not only of things far off to come, but of things to be fulfilled close at hand.

*And in the year.* That is, when the harvest was at hand, the Assyrians rushed in, and laid waste the harvests, and the vineyards, and the olive groves: and because of the length of the siege, what was found from the grains they had for food in the first year and the second; for since in the year of the siege they could not sow, they took what they found from the fruits and the crops.

VERS. 33.-- *Therefore.* [n: (ID.)] He returns to his purpose, and after the hope of things to come he shakes off the present fear. For the gladness over those things which he was promising as future will not be so great as the care over the things at hand

VERS. 35.-- *For my own sake.* He signifies that they are preserved not by [1279A] their own merit, but by God's clemency, nay rather by the memory of David their father. Wherein they are admonished of their own negligence, and of his faith and justice, which God so loves that he protects the posterity of the saints for the merit of the saints.

VERS. 37.-- *And he returned.* Therefore he was spared, that he might know the power of God and check the blaspheming mouths, and might proclaim the majesty of him whom before he had despised.

VERS. 38.-- *His sons.* Envying (according to the tradition of the Jews) Asaraddon, etc., *as far as* spared by his sons to be slain in the temple of his own god.

*Ararat.* [n: (HIER.)] A level region of Armenia, through which flows the Araxes, of incredible fruitfulness, at the foot of Mount Taurus, which stretches as far as there. But the ark of Noe was not carried to the mountains of Armenia generally, [1279B] which is called Ararat, but to the highest mountains of Taurus, which overhang the plains of Ararat.

## CHAPTER XXXVIII.

VERS. 1.-- *In those days he was sick.* [n: (HIER.)] Because he did not render worthy thanks to God for the victory, but vanished away in pride, as the book of Kings and of Paralipomenon discloses; whence: Whom God loves, he chastises, lest he be lifted up into pride after incredible triumphs.

*Thou shalt die.* To which is like: *Ninive shall be overthrown:* and many things foretold under a threat, and not fulfilled; God not changing his sentence, but provoking us to the knowledge of himself. For the Lord is patient over wickedness.

VERS. 2.-- *To the wall.* [n: (ID.)] Of the temple, namely, beside which Solomon built his palace.

VERS. 3.-- *I beseech thee, O Lord.* Being about to go to the Lord, [1279C] he recounts his works. Happy the conscience which in the time of evils can remember good things.

*I beseech thee, O Lord.* Hearing that he was to die, he does not ask for many years, but leaves it to God's judgment what he may be pleased to grant: he knew that Solomon had pleased God, because he had not asked for a greater number of years of life.

*And what is good.* He destroyed the idols of his father, he opened the doors of the house of the Lord which the other had shut, he broke to pieces the brazen serpent which the Jews worshipped in Gabaon, and scattered it to the wind.

*And he wept.* He wept because he was dying without children, etc., *as far as* and that we read of very many raised up after death.

VERS. 4.-- *And the word of the Lord came.* In a reversed order the history is here related, which in the volume of Kings [1279D] is read in better sequence.

VERS. 5.-- *Behold I will add to thy days.* According to what was seen, etc., *as far as* and concerning the years added to Ezechias.

VERS. 7.-- *And this shall be a sign to thee from the Lord, that the Lord hath done this word which he hath spoken.* [var: Vulg. Is 38:7 *faciet*, future, "that the Lord will do"] [n: (ID.)] Of the present time, namely, and of the future, that as the sun turns back to its own beginning, so the life of Ezechias may return to the years that have run out. To us also, living in the seventh and the eighth age, may the spaces of life be prolonged after the resurrection of Christ.

VERS. 10.-- *I said.* [n: (ID.)] When God had given Ezechias the victory without toil, etc., *as far as* sinners die in the midst of their days.

*In the midst of my days.* Whence: Wicked and deceitful men shall not [1280A] live out half their days, because they do not fulfil the works of the virtues, nor amend their faults by doing penance.

*To the gates of hell. I sought.* Where the house appointed for every living man was set before the coming of Christ.

VERS. 11.-- *Of quiet.* Some copies have: My generation is at rest, which Jerome also once translated, deceived by the ambiguity of the word, and afterwards corrected.

VERS. 15.-- *He shall answer me.* [n: (ID.)] What I have done amiss I shall amend, be thou my surety and protector: *For it is not of him that willeth, nor of him that runneth, but of God that sheweth mercy [n: (Rom. IX)]*.

*When he himself.* These things justly to me, as if he should say: nothing remains but patiently to endure and to entreat him who is strong, who did what he willed.

[1280B] *I will think over.* What is past does not help him that endures; whence the saying of Epicurus is foolish, who asserts that present evils are lessened by the remembrance of past goods.

*In the bitterness of the soul,* as if he should say: and these too in bitterness, because a present tribulation feels nothing of past good, and human happiness is always sprinkled with some adversities.

VERS. 18.-- *For hell shall not confess.* The damned without end neither do penance in hell, nor render praises to God.

VERS. 19.-- *The living, the living,* etc. Not hell nor death [sic: *velmors*].

*The father to the children.* Whence: ask thy father, and he will declare, etc.

VERS. 21.-- *Upon his wound.* The Hebrews say [1280C] that Sehin (שחין) sounds an ulcer, not a wound, etc., *as far as* when God shall have made it.

## CHAPTER XXXIX.

VERS. 1.-- *At that time.* [n: (HIER.)] It has been said above that Asaraddon succeeded Sennacherib, etc., *as far as* and, his manly strength being lost, he is reduced to a womanish softness.

VERS. 2.-- *And in all his power.* [n: (ID.)] In the treasures of the house of the Lord, in which he chiefly offended. For this was not of his power. Yet already he had taken the plates of gold from those doors of the temple.

*In his treasures.* He is silent about the treasures of the Lord, fearing to be blamed, because, namely, he showed them whatsoever he had in his power, that is, the furniture of the temple; [1280D] whence:

VERS. 7.-- *And they shall be eunuchs.* [n: (ID.)] The Hebrews hand down that this was fulfilled in Daniel and his companions, who, when Joachim the son of Josue had been taken, were taken with their people and led into Babylon and made eunuchs, or emasculated; yet it is not to be thought that they were eunuchs in body, but in mind, like *those who have made themselves eunuchs for the kingdom of heaven [n: (Matth. XIX)]*.

VERS. 8.-- *Let there be peace only.* He ought to have confessed his sin with David, and turned away the wrath of God from his posterity, or to have suffered with Moses, who says: *Forgive them this trespass, or blot me out of the book which thou hast written [n: (Exod. XXXII)]*. Because he did not do this, therefore he does not answer him; but, turning to the people, he says: Be comforted, etc.
