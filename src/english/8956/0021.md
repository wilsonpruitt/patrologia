## CHAPTER XLI.

VERS. 1.-- *Let the islands be silent.* [n: (HIER.)] Because the Gentiles are in the salt sea, namely those who are beaten by the salt and bitter waves of the world.

VERS. 2.-- *Who has raised up from the east.* [n: (ID.)] Certain of the Hebrews refer this to Cyrus king of the Persians, etc., *as far as* who from the beginning knew these things.

VERS. 3.-- *A path under his feet.* He shall not feel the labor or the weariness of that life: *He rejoiced as a giant to run the way.*

VERS. 5.-- *The islands saw.* [n: (ID.)] That is, the nations round [1283B] about, etc., *as far as* and he strengthened him with the nails of the commandments, that he might abide in the fear of the Lord.

VERS. 8.-- *And thou, Israel, my servant.* [n: (ID.)] The Lord speaks lovingly to the apostles, etc., *as far as* afterward, being chosen, we are joined into the friendship of God.

VERS. 9.-- *And from afar.* Already the Jews are scattered even to the ends of the earth, and thence unto the end many are called.

VERS. 14.-- *Worm of Jacob.* [n: (ID.)] The apostles are called worms, as imitators of him who says: *I am a worm and no man [n: (Psal. XXI)]*. And as a worm penetrates the earth, so does their preaching the hearts of those who hear.

*You that are dead of Israel.* Like him who says: *I die daily for your glory, brethren [n: (I Cor. XV)]* [var: Vulg. 1 Cor. 15:31 *per vestram gloriam*, "by your glory"]; and elsewhere: *And I live, now not I; but Christ lives [1283C] in me [n: (Gal. II)]*.

VERS. 15.-- *I have made thee as a threshing wain.* The churchman has the teeth of preaching, etc., *as far as* that those ill gathered together may be well divided.

VERS. 19.-- *I will give in the wilderness the cedar.* [n: (ID.)] The varieties of the trees signify the diversity of spiritual grace, lest even one string be wanting from the harp of the Lord, and any virtue of the graces seem to be lacking to the Church: and the loftiness of these, as it hastens to heavenly things, the olive tree enlightens, the fuel of light, the rest of him who labors or is wearied.

*The thorn, and the myrtle, and the olive tree.* The Seventy: *Sethim,* which is a tree like the white thorn, etc., *as far as* and therefore it signifies the comforters of holy Church.

VERS. 22.-- *Declare ye.* [n: (ID.)] He signifies that [1283D] the idols have the power neither of hearing nor of answering, etc., *as far as* it is plain that the idols lost their voice after the coming of Christ.

VERS. 23.-- *If you can.* Neither can the demons, save by the permission of God, in that they attend upon the idols; for they could destroy neither the swine nor holy Job's beasts without the permission of God. Paul too delivered certain men to Satan.

VERS. 25.-- *I have raised up.* [n: (ID.)] He still speaks against the idols and against their worshippers, etc., *as far as* that they may believe in him who came from the rising of the sun.

VERS. 26.-- *Who has declared from the beginning?* as if he should say: There was none among the Gentiles who declared the coming of the Son. But if there is one who has declared it, we shall say [1284A] that he is just; at the coming of the Son, the demons, who in the idols announced things to come, had fallen dumb.

*Hearing your words.* Because the brute hearts of the Gentiles understood their own error, and forsook the false oracles.

VERS. 27.-- *The first shall say to Sion.* They signify the Church; behold, sons have stood by, namely thine, whom I have given to thee by my faith, namely the apostles, by whose preaching sons have been born to thee.

VERS. 29.-- *Behold, all are unjust.* Whatever we have said of the idols or of the Gentiles, let us refer to the heresiarchs and to the doctrines of depravity; because, while Christ announces peace to the Church and shows the truth, they themselves follow errors, and are compared to the wind and to the whirlwind.

## CHAPTER XLII.

[1284B]VERS. 1.-- *Behold my servant.* [n: (HIER.)] It is to be noted that the apostles are called Jacob, Israel, and the seed of Abraham, to whom afterward, as to servants and men, it is said: *Fear not, worm of Jacob,* etc. But Christ is called not the seed of Jacob or of Israel, but the servant.

*I have given my Spirit.* In the conception; whence: *The Holy Spirit shall come upon thee,* etc. [n: (Phil. II)]. And it shone forth in the baptism, when the Spirit in the likeness of a dove rested upon him [n: (Luc. I)].

VERS. 3.-- *Smoking flax.* The priesthood now almost extinguished; and of flax is the priestly vestment made.

*He shall bring forth judgment.* He shall judge, namely, not fearing the Scribes and the Pharisees.

VERS. 4.-- *He shall not be sad.* [n: (ID.)] This Matthew [1284C] did not set down; or else the words between the one *judgment* and the other were taken away by a copyist's error. And the sense is: he shall preserve at all times an evenness of countenance; which the philosophers falsely boast of Socrates, namely that he was neither sadder nor gladder than his wont.

*Judgment in the earth.* Whence: *For judgment I came into this world, that they who do not see may see,* etc. [n: (Joan. IX)].

VERS. 6.-- *Justice.* It was just that he who was despised by the Jews should pass over through the apostles to the Gentiles.

VERS. 7.-- *The bound.* With the cords of sins: in the Gospel, the Lord loosed him whom Satan had bound.

VERS. 8.-- *To another.* The Son and the Holy Spirit is not another than the Father in the Godhead, and therefore has the same glory [1284D] and the same praise.

[1284D]VERS. 9.-- *I will make known to you.* That you may know that I am the Lord, and there is none other beside me: and that you who behold the former things fulfilled may believe the things to come which I promise.

VERS. 10.-- *A new thing.* In the newness of the Spirit, not in the oldness of the letter; and not only in the Old Testament, but also in the New.

*You that go down into the sea.* [n: (ID.)]. First fishers of fishes, afterward of men; whence, according to the letter: they crossed many seas.

VERS. 11.-- *Cedar.* The son of Ismael: from him the Saracens, who are better called Agarenes: but by the name of the free woman they have veiled the shame of the handmaid. For Ismael was the son of the handmaid Agar, and from her the Saracens. For he begot twelve princes. By his name too the province itself is called.

[1285A] *From the top of the mountains.* [n: (HIER.)] *Go up upon a high mountain. From the top of the mountain.* [var: Vulg. Is. 42:11 *de vertice montium*, plural, as in the lemma just quoted] From the height of virtue and of contemplation, that they may teach by example also the things they preach.

VERS. 13.-- *He shall cry out.* Whence: *They have provoked me with that which was no God [n: (Deut. XXXII)]*; and I will provoke them with a foolish nation.

VERS. 14.-- *As a woman in labor.* The birth of my counsel I will pour forth into the light as a woman her infant; so will I bring forth the grief I have dissembled.

VERS. 16.-- *In the paths.* [n: (ID.)] In the knowledge of the prophets, who are the paths to Christ, when they foretell his nativity, his resurrection and his ascension.

VERS. 17.-- *Let them be confounded. When the fulness of the Gentiles shall have come in.* Then Israel also, being converted, shall repent of its [1285B] error, who before served idols.

VERS. 22.-- *A snare to the young men.* He returns to the preaching of the Lord, rebuking the Scribes and the Pharisees: for these were snares to the simple, that they might not be converted to the Lord.

VERS. 23.-- *Who is there.* The prophet exhorts them, that at least some may hear and understand the cause of their captivity and beware.

VERS. 25.-- *Upon him.* That what he had before passed over in silence he might now no longer pass over in silence, and might rebuke not with words but with torments.

*And he knew it not.* As the histories relate, neither did the Gentiles understand that this was done in vengeance for the Son, nor did the Jews, blinded by their own malice.
