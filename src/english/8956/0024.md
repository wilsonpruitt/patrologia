## CHAPTER XLVII.

VERS. 1.-- *Come down, sit,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] Because we are all creatures of God; nor, as the heretics hold, is the nature of Babylon damnable; or else because of the luxury and adornment of that most powerful city, which, already old and near to its setting, boasts itself a maiden and a girl.

VERS. 2.-- *Meal,* or rather, offer intercourse to the Medes and Persians, who are most ardent in this. According to this the Hebrews say that the Philistines, when they had torn out Samson's eyes, compelled him to grind, that is, to have intercourse with their women, that from him they might get men of great and unconquerable stature.

VERS. 3.-- *And he shall not resist,* as Moses did to God when he willed to blot out the sons of Israel. But it signifies the angel [1290C] set over Babylon, who says with the others: *We have healed Babylon, and she is not healed.*

VERS. 5.-- *Sit.* The speech is referred to Babylon, and he addresses her as one once a queen, now a handmaid. Sit, lowly and vile. Be silent, as one sorrowful, considering thy misery.

VERS. 6.-- *I was angry.* He renders the reason why he so afflicted the Chaldeans, lest he should seem to have done it without cause, who works nothing without cause; and that the Chaldeans might know that they did not overcome the people of God by their own strength.

VERS. 9.-- *Suddenly.* By the unforeseen inrush of Cyrus and Darius. For she did not fear that she could be overcome by the Medes and Persians (who in comparison with her were of no strength, and were themselves subject).

VERS. 11.-- *Thou shalt not know the rising.* Unlooked for the calamity shall come, [1290D] foretold neither by the magi nor by the soothsayers; for they did not fear a despised and subject people.

VERS. 13.-- *The augurs of heaven.* The astrologers, who judge that all things are ruled by the stars. As if he should say: As though they did not provide all things for thee? These are commonly called mathematicians, who think that worldly affairs are ruled by the course of the stars; whence: *From the east came magi saying: We have seen his star in the east [n: (Matth. II)]*. Or else, stirred up by the oracle of Balaam, who said: *A star shall rise out of Jacob [n: (Num. XXIV)]*.

VERS. 14.-- *They are become as stubble,* etc. The Seventy: Having coals of fire, thou shalt sit upon them, these shall be to thee for a help. As if: the Gazareni and the magi lead thee by error into pride. But the burning [1291A] of Babylon he calls to repentance through punishments.

## CHAPTER XLVIII.

VERS. 1.-- *Hear these things.* [n: (HIER.)] It is the custom that when the carnal Israel is treated of, Jacob or Israel should be named simply and without an addition of name, as in this place, where he addresses those who are called Jacob or Israel only in name.

*And out of the waters of Juda.* He makes mention of Juda, because when the ten tribes had been led captive, Juda and Benjamin and the Levites remained in the land of promise, and had a king of Juda.

VERS. 2.-- *For of the holy city.* [n: (ID.)] As if he should say: You boast that you are the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and that you have the privilege of the Lord of hosts, whereas you take to yourselves, empty as they are for you, the appellations of Jacob, of Israel, and of the holy city, of God [1291B] almighty.

VERS. 3.-- *The former things from that time.* I foretold that the Babylonians were to be overcome, and suddenly I shall do what I threatened: so that when the things foretold shall have come, thou mayest know that they befell neither by chance nor by the nod of thy gods.

*The former wonders,* namely those which I did in the midst of them from the time they went out of Egypt, and when the captivity by Nabuchodonosor was at hand: but now, before they are taken captive, he foretells their deliverance and the devastation of the Chaldeans; whence *suddenly*, namely, the things I foretell as coming, I have wrought.

VERS. 4.-- *An iron sinew.* Hence Stephen: *stiffnecked and uncircumcised in hearts [n: (Act. XVII)]* [var: Vulg. Act. 7:51 *incircumcisis cordibus et auribus*, ablative, "and ears"].

*Thy forehead brazen:* whence Ezechiel: *For all the house of Israel is of a hardened forehead [n: (Ezech. III)]*, etc. And [1291C] Jeremias: *Thou hast had the forehead of a harlot woman, thou wouldst not blush [n: (Jer. III)]*.

VERS. 6.-- *And they are kept.* As if he should say: I was about to announce very many things, but because thou dost not remember what I say, what I was about to say I keep in reserve.

VERS. 9.-- *For my name's sake.* Namely, that it be not profaned: *I will put far off; I have done it:* [var: Vulg. Is. 48:9 *longe faciam furorem meum*, "I will put my fury far off"] my fury, that is, that I should not destroy thee, because thou hast adored the calf: *and with my praise* (that thou mightest praise me, namely): *I will bridle thee* with my law.

VERS. 10.-- *In the furnace of poverty.* [n: (ID.)] By purging thee through poverty, giving thy riches to the enemies; or in Egypt, where it is shown that very many are tempted by riches and by poverty.

VERS. 12.-- *Hear me, Jacob.* [n: (HIER.)] He is still speaking to the same men, [1291D] etc., *as far as* but he calls them the called.

[1291D]VERS. 14.-- *Assemble yourselves, all of you.* Why then does earth and ashes glory, and one ignorant of his own frailty?

*He has announced these things.* That Cyrus should destroy Babylon, or that my Son was to be born of a virgin.

*The Lord has loved him.* These things belong properly to Christ. Yet they can be referred to Cyrus and to Darius, as far as that place: *His way is made straight.*

*His own:* because he shall take it. Or, the Son, in redeeming the world, who also cast out the Chaldeans (that is, the demons); whence, *the prince of this world shall be cast forth.*

VERS. 15.-- *I have called him.* Cyrus, or the Son, saying through Gabriel: *And thou shalt call his name Jesus.*

VERS. 16.-- *He has sent me.* [n: (ID.)] He distinguishes the whole Trinity briefly, fully and plainly.

[1292A]VERS. 17.-- *I am the Lord.* Who promise to Israel the things to come. He renders the reason why he had afflicted them before.

VERS. 21.-- *They did not thirst.* [n: (ID.)] Of those coming from Egypt, according to the letter, etc., *as far as* filled with the water of wisdom.

*He clove the rock.* The rock was Christ. The divine word is cloven into parts, so that what we cannot take in whole, we may take by parts.

*And the waters flowed out. One of the soldiers opened his side with a lance, and immediately there came forth the blood of our redemption, and the water of baptism,* [var: Vulg. Jo. 19:34 *exivit sanguis et aqua*, without *nostrae redemptionis* and *baptismi*] dedicating to us baptism and martyrdom.
