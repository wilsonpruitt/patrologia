## CHAPTER LXII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *For Sion's sake.* [n: (HIER.)] The prophet in his own person, exceedingly gladdened by all those things [1305C] which the incarnate Son confers upon the world. ⟦s2⟧ And this is it: *For Sion's sake I will not hold my peace,* however much he forbid him to speak, however much he threaten death.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let a lamp be kindled.* ⟦s2+3⟧ *Lampas* in Greek, *flamma* in Latin; whence: *I am come to cast fire upon the earth, and what will I but that it burn?* [var: Vulg. Lc 12:49 *nisi ut accendatur*, "but that it be kindled"; the lemma's own verb] Set alight by this, the apostles spoke with divers tongues.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *A new name.* [n: (ID.)] It is the custom of the Hebrews to impose new names from new things, etc., *as far as* likewise first Simon, afterwards Peter.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *A crown of glory.* ⟦s2⟧ Of the faithful coming from all the parts of the world, as it were a crown.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Thou shalt not be called.* ⟦s2⟧ He addresses the Church gathered out of either people, as if: thou shalt not be content with this end of appellations.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1305D]VERS. 5.-- *And he shall rejoice,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *For this cause shall a man leave father and mother [n: (Gen. II; Matth. XIX)]*. ⟦s3⟧ *This is a great sacrament in Christ and in the Church [n: (Ephes. V)]* [var: Vulg. Eph 5:32 *Sacramentum hoc magnum est, ego autem dico in Christo et in Ecclesia*, "but I speak in Christ and in the Church"].

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Upon thy walls.* [n: (ID.)] The apostles, the preachers, who fortify the Church, whose guardians are the angels. ⟦s2⟧ Whence: *All are ministering spirits, sent for their sakes who receive the inheritance of salvation [n: (Hebr. I)]* [var: Vulg. Hbr 1:14 *Nonne omnes sunt administratorii spiritus, in ministerium missi propter eos, qui haereditatem capient salutis?*, a question, with *in ministerium* and the future *capient*]. ⟦s3⟧ Or the walls of the Churches are the disciples of the apostles, whose guardians are the apostles themselves.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Till he establish Jerusalem.* ⟦s2⟧ Which is, either that she herself, spread far and wide, be praised everywhere, or that she everywhere praise her Creator always.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *He hath sworn* by the Son, namely, who is the power of God [1306A] and the wisdom of God. ⟦s2⟧ *In his right hand.* ⟦s3⟧ In the Son. ⟦s4⟧ Whence: *Thou hast made all things in wisdom [n: (Psal. CIII)]*, who is both the arm and the strength of the Father: Whence, *All things were made by him [n: (Joan. I)]*. ⟦s5⟧ *If I will give thy wheat.* ⟦s6⟧ The wheat of the Jews and their wine I have often given to their enemies, as is read in the book of Judges [n: (Judic. VI)]: that in the time of harvest the Madianites used to rush in and gather their crops. ⟦s7⟧ And when they were led away captive, Samaritans were put in their place to eat their harvests. ⟦s8⟧ But in this place he understands the food of the Church, the virtues and good works, the body and blood of Christ. ⟦s9⟧ That is, the praises and the works which thou hadst spent upon idols, the demons used to eat. ⟦s10⟧ But after the coming of the Son the Lord was delighted in them. ⟦s11⟧ The wheat, therefore, and the wine, the food (namely of the Jews), signified the body and blood of Christ. [1306B] Whence, *My flesh is meat indeed, and my blood is drink indeed [n: (Joan. VI)]*. ⟦s12⟧ This is not taken today by heretics, pagans, Jews, the enemies of the Church.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Through the gates.* ⟦s2⟧ The ministers of the Church, who bring others in. ⟦s3⟧ Or the virtues, which lead into heaven, as it were from virtue to virtue. ⟦s4⟧ *Make the way plain.* ⟦s5⟧ By taking away scandals; by remitting sins in baptism. ⟦s6⟧ *Choose ye.* ⟦s7⟧ Or, cast ye forth, according to the Septuagint, which is: take away the hardness of unbelief from the hearts of men; or, the reprobate and the hard, from the fellowship of the faithful.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Say ye to the daughter of Sion.* ⟦s2⟧ Namely to the primitive Church, which was first out of the Jews: whence, *The sons of my mother have fought against me [n: (Cant. I)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Or according to that: *But as many as received him [n: (Joan. I)]*, etc.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they shall call him,* saying: You are the [1306C] holy people, you are the redeemed of the Lord God [var: Vulg. Is 62:12 *Et vocabunt eos, Populus sanctus, redempti a Domino*, "they shall call them"]. ⟦s2⟧ For so certain codices have it.

## CHAPTER LXIII.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Who is this.* ⟦s2⟧ Certain angels, not fully knowing the mystery of the incarnation, of the passion, of the resurrection, seeing the Lord ascend to the heavens with a multitude of angels and of holy men by his own power, not by the help of angels, marvel at the mystery of the incarnation, and of the passion, and of the resurrection, and of the ascension: and how he opens the kingdom of heaven to himself and to others; whence also they say to the angels accompanying the Lord: *Who is this?* ⟦s3⟧ And David likewise: *Who is this King of glory? [n: (Psal. XXIII)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Augustus, however, seems to say upon Genesis [cj: *Augustus*; read *Augustinus*, "Augustine"] that all the angels from their very creation knew at once, in the contemplation [1306D] of their Creator, whatsoever he himself was about to do. ⟦s5⟧ But Jerome plainly declares that certain angels, until the mystery of the incarnation should be accomplished, did not know it to the full.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of Edom.* ⟦s2⟧ Edom [n: (ex HIER.)] from the eating of red lentils, etc., *as far as* or, from hell despoiled, victor with his faithful.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Beautiful in his robe.* ⟦s2⟧ *Formosum*, warm. ⟦s3⟧ Whence *formosus* as it were *calidus*, because from its colour the face is made ruddy and rosy, and thence comely.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Why then.* [n: (ID.)] As though the angels should say: We have learned, etc., *as far as* clemency befits thee more than cruelty.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Alone.* ⟦s2⟧ Samson alone, his locks cut off, [1307A] is exposed to the Philistines; neither angel nor archangel nor any heavenly spirit. ⟦s3⟧ Finally, of the human race no one, whether Jew or Gentile, is a helper: alone he fights, alone he overcomes. ⟦s4⟧ *He is sprinkled.* [n: (HIER.)] My blood by their rancour, etc., *as far as* whose blood is often sprinkled upon the garments of the victor.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And there was not,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Whence: *All my friends have forsaken me, and my acquaintance are gone from me as strangers [n: (Job. XIX)]* [var: Vulg. Job 19:13 *Fratres meos longe fecit a me, et noti mei quasi alieni recesserunt a me*; the first half answers rather to Job 19:14 *Dereliquerunt me propinqui mei*, "my kinsmen have forsaken me"]. ⟦s3⟧ *And it saved.* ⟦s4⟧ Jerome, to Augustine and Alipius, shunning the impiety of Sabellius: we distinguish three persons expressed under their property, not saying that the Father is himself to himself, not the Son himself to himself, not the Holy Spirit himself to himself: but that the person of the Father is one, of the Son another, of the Holy Spirit another. ⟦s5⟧ For we confess not names only, [1307B] but the properties of names.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Of the mercies.* [n: (HIER.)] The prophet in the person of the Jewish people repenting and acknowledging the mercy of the Lord.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *He was not afflicted.* [n: (ID.)] The voice which is, etc., *as far as there is no helper but Michael your prince, who stands for the sons of my people [n: (Dan. X)]* [var: Vulg. Dn 10:21 *nemo est adjutor meus in omnibus his, nisi Michael princeps vester*; the closing clause is Dn 12:1 *qui stat pro filiis populi tui*, "thy people," and is not cited].

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And an angel.* [n: (ID.)] The Septuagint. ⟦s2⟧ Not a legate nor an angel himself, but the Lord saved them.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *The spirit of his holy one.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, of Moses: whence, *They exasperated Moses in the camp, and Aaron the holy one of the Lord. [n: (Psal. CV.)]* [var: Vulg. Ps 105:16 *Et irritaverunt Moysen in castris; Aaron, sanctum Domini*, "provoked"] Or *of his holy one*, of Christ, in whom they would not believe. ⟦s3⟧ For the Son of God who redeemed us, he it was who delivered them from Egypt and the Red Sea. ⟦s4⟧ *The Holy Spirit,* or, *of his holy one.* ⟦s5⟧ Certain codices [1307C] have, namely, the Holy Spirit, whom he grieves who grieves a spiritual man: whence, *He that toucheth you, is as one that toucheth the apple of my eye. [n: (Zach. II.)]* [var: Vulg. Zach 2:8 *qui enim tetigerit vos, tangit pupillam oculi mei*, without *quasi*: he touches it, rather than is as one who touches it] Or, the angel sent for a guard, whom they often offended by doing ill.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Of Moses.* ⟦s2⟧ Because there is no other who prays for them. ⟦s3⟧ *Where is he,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ The prophet, seeing the casting-off of his people, their misery and their affliction now impending because of the denial of the Son of God, cries out in compassion, saying: *Where is he?* as if he should say: The clemency of the Almighty. ⟦s5⟧ *Where is he that put?* ⟦s6⟧ Of Moses, namely, because at Moses' prayer the Lord took of his spirit and gave it to seventy men.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Who divided the waters before them.* ⟦s2⟧ Or it is said. ⟦s3⟧ When Moses had struck the sea, and had divided it into twelve ways, and while the rest hesitated, the tribe of Juda entered in, [1307D] and for this obtained the dignity of the kingdom.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *The spirit of the Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ The angel, namely; whence: *And my angel shall go before thee [n: (Exod. XIV)]* [var: Vulg. Ex 23:20 *Ecce ego mittam angelum meum, qui praecedat te*, and Ex 32:34 *angelus meus praecedet te*; Exodus XIV as cited reads *angelus Dei, qui praecedebat castra Israel*]. ⟦s3⟧ For *spirit* is the name of a nature, *angel* of an office.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Look down from heaven and behold.* ⟦s2⟧ The Lord is everywhere whole and always, whence: *I fill heaven and earth [n: (Jer. XXIII)]* [var: Vulg. Jr 23:24 *Numquid non caelum et terram ego impleo?*, a question]. ⟦s3⟧ Yet he is said to have his seat in heaven, according to that: *Heaven is my seat [n: (Isa. LXVI)]* [var: Vulg. Is 66:1 *Caelum sedes mea*; *Coelum mihi sedes* is the wording of Act 7:49, and the verse is quoted in its Isaian form at 1312A], because, namely, in the heavenly angels and in just men (whose manner of life, that is, is in the heavens) his will is chiefly done. ⟦s4⟧ *Where is thy zeal,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ As if he should say: Because we have sinned, and thou hast begun to hate us, thy zeal has withdrawn: and when it withdrew strength withdrew, and the affection of a father's bowels is overcome.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1308A]VERS. 16.-- *Abraham hath not known us.* [n: (HIER.)] As if he should say: Because we have offended thee, etc., *as far as* let him not forget his sons, lest through them the name of God be blasphemed among the Gentiles.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Why hast thou made us to err.* ⟦s2⟧ Paul, writing to the Corinthians, that is, to the people of a single city, on account of the diversity of those dwelling there (of saints, namely, and of sinners), now praises, now reproves: so the present chapter, which is woven out of the prayer of the people beseeching God, now praises the Lord, now accuses, according to the second rule of Ticonius, which is concerning the body of the Lord.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *As in the beginning.* [n: (HIER.)] Before thou didst call us, etc., *as far as* they shall not be called the people of God, Christians namely from Christ.
