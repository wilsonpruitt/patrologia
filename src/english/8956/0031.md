## CHAPTER LXIV.

[1308B]

VERS. 1.-- *O that thou wouldst rend.* [n: (HIER.)] In his own person, etc., *as far as* likewise David: *He bowed the heavens, and came down [n: (Psal. XVII)]*.

VERS. 2.-- *The waters should burn. [n: (SYM.)] The waters of the sea should burn with fire.* Which is, that the peoples of the world should be inflamed with the fire of charity. Whence: *I am come to cast fire upon the earth, and what will I but that it burn? [n: (Luc. XII.)]* [var: Vulg. Lc 12:49 *nisi ut accendatur*, "but that it be kindled"] and so *thy name should be made known,* which is Christ Jesus, *to thy enemies,* namely to the Jews.

VERS. 3.-- *We shall not bear it.* We Jews, but we shall blaspheme, saying: *By Beelzebub the prince of devils he casteth out devils [n: (Matth. XII)]* [var: Vulg. Mt 12:24 *Hic non ejicit daemones nisi in Beelzebub principe daemoniorum*, a denial: "he casteth not out devils but by Beelzebub"]. For the prophet says this in the person of unbelievers. *Thou camest down.* As though, his prayers having been heard, he says: *Thou camest down,* etc. [1308C] *The mountains.* Their power lost: *Now shall the prince of this world be cast out [n: (Joan. XII)]*. And to the Jews it is said: *The kingdom of God shall be taken away from you [n: (Matth. XXI)]*, namely Holy Scripture and all glory and all dignity.

VERS. 4.-- *From the beginning of the world.* Hence Paul, writing to the Corinthians, takes his testimony, not word for word, but expressing the sense. *The eye.* Because it is not a body or a colour. What the Apostle sets down, *it hath not ascended into the heart of man*, because it is greater, and the heart ought to be directed thither. For he prepared his very self, the knowledge of himself, the knowledge of the holy Trinity; whence: *This is eternal life, that they may know thee the true God, and Jesus Christ whom thou hast sent [n: (Joan. XVII)]* [var: Vulg. Jo 17:3 *ut cognoscant te, solum Deum verum*, "thee, the only true God"]. Him no one can see; whence that: *No man hath ever seen God [n: (Joan. I)]*.

VERS. 5.-- *Behold thou,* etc. In the person of the unbelieving people, [1308D] who, wishing to pass over to the truth of the Gospel, put forward with the mouth a penance which he had not in the heart.

VERS. 6.-- *Of a menstruous woman,* etc. *Mene* in Hebrew, *luna* in Latin; whence *menstruatae*, women who each several month are wont to endure a flux of blood. And then men ought to abstain from them. For then are conceived those condemned in their members, the blind, the lame, the leprous and the like: so that, because the parents were not ashamed to be mingled in the bedchamber, their sins may lie open to all, and be openly reproved in their little ones. *Wind.* A motion of the air, as there, *He commanded the winds and the sea [n: (Matth. VIII)]*. Or the Holy Spirit: *Of a vehement Spirit coming (Act I)*. For here *Spirit* is put for wind, with the signification of the Holy Spirit. Or, the temptation of demons and of evil [1309A] men, as that: *The floods came, and the winds blew [n: (Matth. VII)]*: and this is as it were a wind.

VERS. 8.-- *And now, O Lord.* As if he should say: If we consider our own merits, we must despair; if thy clemency, who scourgest every son whom thou receivest, we dare to pour out prayers, for thou art our father. *The works of thy hands.* As if he should say: Look upon thy work, not upon our merits. The right confession of the mystery of the Trinity is the ignorance of knowledge.

VERS. 9.-- *Be not angry, O Lord.* This is, we do not shun wrath, nor do we further seek that patience through which we treasure up to ourselves wrath in the day of vengeance: but we beseech that thou be not angry with us enough, that is, that thou remember not our iniquity in the time of vengeance.

VERS. 10.-- *Sion is a desert.* Sion and Jerusalem in [1309B] this place signify the same thing, the temple, namely, and the city, where once was the watchtower of the virtues and the vision of peace. But after the people of the Jews denied their king, and added that: *We have no king but Caesar [n: (Joan. XIX)]*, Jerusalem was deserted by God: whence, *Your house shall be left to you desolate [n: (Luc. XIII)]*; and that, *Arise, let us go hence [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. And in the temple voices were heard (as the ecclesiastical history says): Let us pass over from these seats.

VERS. 11.-- *The house.* Allegorically. The house of God is the Church, and every faithful soul, which is sometimes corrupted by the fires of luxury and of avarice.
