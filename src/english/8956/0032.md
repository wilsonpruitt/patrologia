## CHAPTER LXV.

VERS. 1.-- *They have sought,* etc. [n: (HIER.)] To this the Son answers, [1309C] etc., *as far as* and *The children that are strangers have lied to me [n: (Psal. XVII)]*.

VERS. 2.-- *I have spread forth my hands.* [n: (ID.)] Praying on the cross for them, etc., *as far as* yet they themselves were always unbelieving.

VERS. 3.-- *A people that to anger.* [n: (ID.)] Allegorically. Every heretic solicits God to anger [cj: *procat*; read *provocat*, "provokes"], etc., *as far as* and to build four-square, and to strengthen with lines the dust cut upon the brick.

VERS. 4.-- *In the shrines.* *Delubra* are so called from *delendo*, blotting out, because there the soul was thought to be washed away. Or, because near by were the pools where both the priest and the victim were washed. Allegorically. [n: (HIER.)] Heretics in the memorials of their masters, etc., *as far as* so that whoever has not been like to them they detest as one lost.

VERS. 5.-- *In my fury.* Fury, forgetfulness, anger, repentance, we ought so to take in God as we take hands, feet, eyes, ears, and the other members which he is said to have: not that he yields to these perturbations who extinguishes them in us, but through our own words; we understand God's disposition toward us.

VERS. 6.-- *I will not be silent.* He who said above: *I have been silent, I have always held my peace, I have been patient,* [var: Vulg. Is 42:14 *Tacui semper, silui, patiens fui*, "I have always been silent, I have held my peace"; the adverb goes with the first verb] now says: *I will not be silent,* that is, that I yield no longer, nor will I bear their iniquities.

VERS. 7.-- *The iniquities of the fathers,* etc. So that, namely, you may be punished not only for your own sins, but also for the iniquities of the fathers whom you have imitated. For otherwise the son does not bear the iniquity of the father. *Who have sacrificed [1310A] upon the mountains.* When in Bethel and in Dan they adored the golden calves. The heretic also, contemning the simplicity of the Church, sacrifices upon the mountains, and upbraids God while he contemns his precepts.

VERS. 8.-- *As.* [n: (HIER.)] As if he should say: For the sake of the wicked I will not destroy the good: *But as,* etc. For he had said that Israel should perish.

VERS. 9.-- *Out of Jacob a seed.* The seed of Jacob and of Juda, the apostles, possessing the mountains, that is, others, namely believers eminent in the virtues: whence, *Unless the Lord of Sabaoth had left us a seed, we had been as Sodom [n: (Rom. IX)]*. *My holy mountains.* [var: Vulg. Is 65:9 *possidentem montes meos*, "my mountains," without *sanctos*] The Septuagint: *My mountain,* namely Christ, who is the mountain of mountains. For the mountains are the apostles, who out of the Jews possessed Christ, in whom they are founded. *And they shall inherit.* [1310B] [n: (HIER.)] Whence: *The seed of Abraham his servant: the sons of Jacob, his elect.* Whosoever are still seed, etc., *as far as you have not received the spirit of bondage again in fear, but you have received the spirit of the adoption of the sons of God [n: (Rom. IX)]*.

VERS. 10.-- *The plains.* [n: (ID.)] Others, the woodlands; whence: *The Lord shall discover the thickets of the woods [n: (Psal. XVIII)]* [var: Vulg. Ps 28:9 *Vox Domini praeparantis cervos: et revelabit condensa*, without *silvarum*, "of the woods," and with the voice of the Lord for subject], that robbers may cease to lurk there, and that beasts and venomous creatures may pass over into the Church of God. *Into folds of flocks.* Another translation has Saronas, which is a region between Joppe and Lydda and Jamnia, fruitful and full of pasture, but infested by robbers: whence it is interpreted *the valley pouring out blood. And the valley of Achor.* Where Achor the son of Charmi, having stolen of the spoils of Jericho, perished with all his house. And because he troubled the people and there [1310C] perished, the place is called the valley of Achor, that is, of trouble or of tumult. In which is signified the Gentile world, as also by Saronas, which is interpreted *the valley shedding blood,* which, full of the shedding of blood, remained in the tumult and in the trouble of vices. But the valley of Achor is turned into folds of flocks or sheepfolds, and into a couching-place of herds, that is, into churches, and into the rest of preachers and of other faithful, who are designated by the herds and the flocks of sheep. *Of herds.* Of the preachers, who arm and defend the Church. For *armentum* is so called from *armando*, arming.

VERS. 11.-- *And you that have forsaken,* etc. Allegorically. All who desert the Church, and forget the holy mountain of God, delivering themselves over to spirits of error or to the unclean doctrines of demons, prepare [1310D] a table for fortune, and believing that nothing pertains to God, but that all things are governed either by the course of the stars or by the changeableness of fortune, these shall be delivered to eternal punishments, so that none of them can escape.

*The table of fortune.* [n: (HIER.)] A goddess of the Egyptians, for whom at the end of the year they used to prepare a table heaped with all manner of food, and with cups mixed with mead: either as though she had furnished a happy year, or because she was about to furnish a happy one to come.

VERS. 12.-- *I called.* Saying through the prophets: *Be converted to me and you shall be saved [n: (Isa. XLV)]*. And through myself: *Do penance [n: (Matth. III)]*. And elsewhere: *Come to me, all you that labour and are burdened [n: (Ibid. XI)]*.

VERS. 13.-- *Behold my servants.* [n: (HIER.)] As if he should say: [1311A] Not only shall you fall by the sword, etc., *as far as* and, *I shall be satisfied when thy glory shall appear.*

VERS. 15.-- *And you shall leave your name.* So that some, affirming, may say: If I do otherwise, may it befall me as it befell the Jews slain by the sword of the Romans. Or thus: I swear by God, in whom the Jews once believed. Or, because God was their oath, which Christians now swear when compelled by necessity, namely *The Lord liveth.*

*And he shall slay thee.* That you may not have even him who says, *I am the way, the truth and the life [n: (Joan. XIV.)]* Nor may you be called circumcision, but concision.

VERS. 16.-- *Because to oblivion.* [n: (HIER.)] That they may not remember the idols, etc., *as far as* Perhaps in the time to come also the memory of their former manner of life shall be altogether blotted out.

[1311B]VERS. 18.-- *For behold.* Out of angels, namely, and out of men, one Church; and they shall exult and rejoice, that I who created may exult and rejoice in them. *A rejoicing.* Of the whole earth, that she who of old was the joy of the Only-begotten may be, made new, a gladness to all nations. *A joy.* Which we can understand either after the resurrection, or after baptism in the first resurrection. This refers to the general resurrection in which all shall rise again in perfect age. And this is it: *There shall not be,* etc.

VERS. 20.-- *A child of a hundred years,* etc. He, namely, who although he lived long, yet lived childishly, serving the vices: and Gregory expounds this thus. *A child of a hundred years. And a sinner of a hundred years.* The child's life is indeed drawn out long, that he may be corrected from childish deeds: but if he is not restrained from the perpetration of sin even by length [1311C] of time, this very length of life, which he received through the mercy of God, grows for him unto a heap of malediction.

VERS. 21.-- *Houses.* [n: (HIER.)] The virtues, for which they shall rest in the heavenly places, etc., *as far as* For many saints build houses, but do not dwell in them.

VERS. 22.-- *For according to.* [n: (ID.)] As Christ etc., *as far as It is a tree of life to them that have laid hold on her,* namely on wisdom, which is Christ.

VERS. 23.-- *The seed of the blessed.* The apostles, and other teachers in preaching, or in other works, as the Jews, because they shall receive an eternal reward, *for they shall come with joyfulness carrying their sheaves [n: (Psal. CXXV)]*.

*Grandchildren.* Whom their sons have begotten in the faith, [1311D] as Mark the disciple of Peter, and Luke the son of Paul.

VERS. 24.-- *Before they call.* In trouble, in grief of mind, but in joy and gladness: whence, *For what is our joy or crown of glory, are not you [n: (II Thes. II)]*? [var: Vulg. 1Thes 2:19 *Quae est enim nostra spes aut gaudium, aut corona gloriae? nonne vos*, with *spes*, "hope," and cited there to the first epistle] etc.

VERS. 25.-- *The wolf.* The wolf is Saul, that is, the persecutor, of whom, *Benjamin a ravenous wolf [n: (Gen. XLIX)]*. *The lamb.* Those, namely, who in white garments follow the lamb whithersoever he goes: of whom it is said to Peter, *Feed my lambs [n: (Joan. XXI)]*. *And the lion and the ox shall eat.* A man, namely, eloquent and most powerful in the world, the eloquence of philosophy being despised, shall eat straw, that is, the usefulness and the surface of the history, with the oxen, that is, with the preachers of the Church. These things the lions eat [1312A] until by many labours one comes to the depth of the mystery, as Augustine and Cyprian and the rest. *Neither shall they kill.* Their fierceness laid aside, either in the present or in the time to come, and this is it: *In all the mountain,* etc. This the Jews take carnally, saying that in the time of their own Christ there shall be for them so great peace and security that wolves and lambs, lions and oxen, serpents and men shall dwell together, and shall eat, nor shall they harm those who dwell in the temple and on the holy mountain. From which it is gathered that all the rest shall be killed. But that this is false is shown by these words: *Thus saith the Lord: Heaven is my seat, and the earth my footstool,* etc.; yet this too the Jews understand carnally.
