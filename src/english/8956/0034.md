## CHAPTER LXVI. (cont.)

*Carcasses,* because they shall fall into eternal damnation, according to which demons also can be called carcasses, according to which men also are so named. Whence: *An enemy hath done this [n: (Matth. XIII)]*. *Their worm.* Material, or of the conscience; whence: *As the moth eats the garment, and the worm gnaws the wood, and the fire devours the timber, so grief and sadness torment the heart of a man [n: (Prov. XXV)]* [var: Vulg. Pr 25:20 *Sicut tinea vestimento, et vermis ligno, ita tristitia viri nocet cordi*, with no verbs of eating and no clause about fire]. *Unto satiety.* Because, namely, the good shall see them separated from themselves by the just judgment of God, their power lost by which they raged against the good, and the vengeance of God upon them so great that they demand no greater. They who now say, *How long, O Lord, dost thou not revenge our [1316C] blood [n: (Apoc. VI)]*? [var: Vulg. Apc 6:10 *Usquequo Domine (sanctus et verus), non judicas, et non vindicas sanguinem nostrum de iis qui habitant in terra?*] They shall be satisfied also with their own beatitude, giving thanks when they have seen the unspeakable punishment of the wicked.
