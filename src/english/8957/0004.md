## CHAPTER III.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0759B]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- « After these things, etc. » He makes them attentive, as it were to hidden things which were covered in closed vessels, but which are uncovered when their mouth is opened, that we may be refreshed by their inmost fragrance. ⟦s2⟧ Thus far a threefold exposition; from here on, according as the several passages require.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (GREG.)] « He cursed his day. » Behold, this is not according to the surface of the history, because this is both evil and impossible. ⟦s2⟧ For the day on which he was born no longer was, and it is idle to curse a thing that does not exist, but pernicious if it did exist; nor can a past day now be turned into darkness, nor can the other things that follow suit it, nor would the untimely birth (which he says afterwards) enjoy rest: therefore here nothing is according to the letter.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0759C] « After these things. » That is, after all the aforesaid things which he suffered, his friends also being silent. ⟦s2⟧ Since therefore he patiently gave thanks in all things and taught well, he is not now, with none instigating him, to be believed to break out into cursing from impatience; but he says this calmly, because amid evils he praised God. ⟦s3⟧ « He cursed. » [n: (Ibid.)] A curse is spoken of in two ways, etc., *as far as* and applies the remedy.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- « Let the day perish on which I was born. » Not created, etc., *as far as* but brought in the darkness of sin.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Let the day perish. » The day is the prosperity of the world, which ends in night, because it leads on to tribulation; or the day is the delight of sin, the night the blindness of the mind. ⟦s2⟧ Man is spoken of in three ways: by nature, by fault, and [0759D] by weakness. ⟦s3⟧ Man therefore is born in the day, conceived in the night: because he is not carried off to the delight of sin unless he is first weakened through the darkness of the mind. ⟦s4⟧ But let the day perish, that is, let the delight of sin be destroyed by the vigour of justice.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And the night. » [n: (Greg. in Job l. IV.)] That is, let penitence extinguish what the blinded mind perpetrates through consent, while it does not warily see through the blandishments of delight, lest the fault which begins to flatter should drag it to destruction.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- « That day, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] That is, let it be seen, at the outset of delight, to what end of perdition it hurries a man, and let it be tormented by penitence. ⟦s2⟧ But if it has been so punished. ⟦s3⟧ « Let it not require. » In judgment, that he may punish; [0760A] and he will not illumine. ⟦s4⟧ He illumines with light who reproves; but he is as it were covered who is not recalled into the memory of the judge. ⟦s5⟧ Whence: « Whose sins are covered [n: (Psal. XXXI)], » nor then let them be shown in the sight of all.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- « Let it not be illumined, etc., let them darken it. » That is, the day of delight, lest it be seen by him who sees all things. ⟦s2⟧ « Darkness. » [n: (GREG.)] That is, the laments of penitence, or the hidden judgments of God, from which we are absolved by prevenient grace, which we know not how to merit. ⟦s3⟧ « And the shadow of death. » That is, the death of Christ according to the flesh, etc., *as far as* in what mercy has succoured him, let man punish it. ⟦s3⟧ Whence follows: « Let gloom seize upon it. » That is, the confusion of the mind, of which it is said: « For there is a confusion that brings glory. [n: (Eccli. IV.)] » « And let it be wrapped about. » On every side. ⟦s4⟧ « With bitterness. » [0760B] Of penitence. ⟦s4⟧ But if the day, that is, the delight of sin, is so punished, what of the night, that is, the consent to fault?

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- « Let a dark whirlwind possess that night. » It is as it were a whirlwind of a storm stirred up, etc., *as far as* for the blindness of the damnation in which we were born. ⟦s2⟧ « Let it not be counted in the day of the year. » The year is the grace from above, etc., *as far as* but is forever without share in that fatherland. ⟦s3⟧ Whence he adds:

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- « Let that night be solitary. » Because it is separated from that throng, or loses the man whom it had made its companion; and when the elect have been redeemed by grace, he alone with his own body is handed over to Gehenna. ⟦s2⟧ « Nor worthy of praise. » Man, when laid low, worshipped idols, in which he praised demons as the darkness of night; [0760C] but now that the idols have been rejected, the night is worthy of praise.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- « Let them curse it. » [n: (Ibid.)] In the old translation, etc., *as far as* and at the end, being loosed, may openly wage wars. ⟦s2⟧ « Leviathan. » That is, their addition, that is, of men, in whom after his suggestion he does not cease to add evils. ⟦s3⟧ Or a reproach, because when he promised to add divinity to Adam, he took away even what he had. ⟦s4⟧ He is called by the prophet a twisting serpent, who softly, as by the coaxing endings of words, pours in his snares. ⟦s5⟧ And a bar, because being hard through malice he strikes unto death. ⟦s6⟧ Once roused he will act openly, but meanwhile in secret.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- « With gloom » Of that night. ⟦s2⟧ « Let the stars be darkened. » That is, hypocrites, who shine by their [0760D] works before the eyes of men. ⟦s3⟧ These are they which are drawn by the tail of the dragon, that is, by the last persecution of Antichrist; these are darkened, because the malice of the enemy so prevails over them that they are wrapped in open evils, such outwardly as they are within. ⟦s4⟧ « Let it look for light. » This according to his members, etc., *as far as* which by prophesying waited for Christ, but did not know him when he came. ⟦s5⟧ « Nor the rising of the dawn as it springs up. » [n: (Ibid.)] The rising of the dawn is the new birth of the resurrection, in which the saints shall rise with their flesh to see the eternal light. ⟦s6⟧ But however much the elect shine here, they cannot penetrate what that glory of that new birth will be. ⟦s6⟧ This night did not close but opened the doors of the womb, because, when man was conceived, it unbarred the desires of concupiscence unto sin. [0761A] And when these doors, that is, the desires of carnal concupiscence, have been unbarred, we are dragged on to innumerable evils of corruption. ⟦s7⟧ Whence, weighed down, we groan; because justice requires this, that what we did willingly we should endure unwillingly.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- « Because he did not close the doors of the womb, etc. » This of the head himself, etc., *as far as* as we say that robbers give life to their captives, if they do not take it away.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- « Why did I not die in the womb. » It is not to be believed, etc., *as far as* lest suggestion should draw him to delight. ⟦s2⟧ « And having come forth from the womb. » [n: (GREG.)] Because sin conceived outwardly, etc., *as far as* while they bear the strife of their own flesh against the spirit.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13, 14.-- « For now. » [n: (Ibid.)] By "now," [0761B] which belongs to present time, he designates the rest that stands always in the present, that is, eternal rest. ⟦s2⟧ « I should rest with kings. » That is, with the angels, in that they rule the things subject to them. ⟦s3⟧ Who are also consuls, because by announcing to us the will of God they take counsel for us. ⟦s4⟧ Or by kings he means holy preachers, who rule themselves; and consuls of the earth, because they counsel earthly men well. ⟦s5⟧ He says therefore that if he had not sinned, he would have ascended thither even unredeemed, whither the apostles and holy preachers after the redemption arrive with great labour, who are not deferred as the ancients were, but soon after death enter heaven. ⟦s6⟧ « Who build, » Every earthly man is pressed in his heart by dense tumults of thoughts, and is trampled by the throng of desires as by crowds. ⟦s7⟧ But to build solitudes is to drive out from the secret place of the heart the tumults of earthly desires, [0761C] and to pant after the love of inmost rest, so that it may be said: One thing I have asked of the Lord, and this will I seek after, etc.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- « With princes who possess gold. » The princes are the rulers of the Church, etc., *as far as* but he who has foreseen things to come already calls to mind things past.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- « Or as an untimely birth. » [n: (Ibid.)] Because that which is an untimely birth comes forth before its time, and being extinguished is straightway hidden away. ⟦s2⟧ These are the saints, with whom he considers that he could have rested, who from the beginning of the world were before the time of the redemption, and yet mortified themselves to the world. ⟦s3⟧ The first time, before the law, which brought forth fathers dead a second time, was as it were the womb of an untimely birth; and they were as it were dead from the womb, [0761D] who without the tables of the law feared God by nature, and believing in the Redeemer to come slew their pleasures. ⟦s4⟧ « Hidden. » Because, few being recorded by Moses, etc., *as far as* whom the spirit of prophecy irradiated, as Job himself. [n: (Ibid.)] Now he opens the rest of this light, and shows in plainer words what is daily done in it concerning the manner of life of the wicked.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- « There the wicked. » That is, in the light of Christ, which the ancients awaited, the Gentiles cease from the throng of the desires of the world, and with tranquil mind already foretaste eternal rest. ⟦s2⟧ And this is: « And there they rested. » In the world the strong are said to be strong in vigour, not wearied. ⟦s3⟧ But he who is strengthened in the love of God healthfully [0762A] fails from his own strength; and the more strongly he seeks eternal things, the more wearied he is, and dies to earthly things. ⟦s4⟧ Whence: « My soul longeth and fainteth for the courts of the Lord [n: (Psal. XXXVIII)]. » When by "there" he means the light, he shows the place of the elect in which they are contained.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- « And they who once were bound. » The just, though at rest from desires, yet while in the body are bound by the trouble of their own corruption. ⟦s2⟧ For the body weighs down the soul, and the flesh strives against the spirit. ⟦s3⟧ There are also bodily chains, hunger, thirst, and the like, which here cannot be loosed; but from them all they shall be loosed in the liberty of the glory of the sons of God. ⟦s4⟧ See the degrees: the wicked, being converted, cease; the well-exercised rest in the inmost bosom; those absolved from the chains of corruption come to liberty. ⟦s5⟧ But what they did here meanwhile, he adds: « Who have not heard, [0762B] etc. ⟦s6⟧ « The exactor is the devil, who once bestowed on man the coin of deception and daily demands the debt of death; his voice is temptation. ⟦s7⟧ He hears who is tempted and resists; he hearkens who consents. ⟦s8⟧ The same concerning inward peace, because what the mind greatly loves it repeats the oftener.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.--Small and great are there. » [n: (Ibid.)] Because here there is a distinction of works, there it will be of dignities. ⟦s2⟧ Whence: « In my Father's house there are many mansions. [n: (Joan. XIV.)] » « And the servant. » He who sins is the servant of sin, because here he never becomes free while he fears the judge; but there he will be free, where there will be no doubt about pardon. ⟦s3⟧ There will indeed be there a memory of fault, not such as defiles the mind, but such as, without injury to blessedness, binds one more closely to blessedness and gladness, as when healed [0762C] we remember our pains without pain; whence we give the more thanks to the physician.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- « Why is light given to the wretched? » [n: (GREG. in lib. Job lib. V.)] Having contemplated that rest, where, made free by the Lord, he looks down upon the prosperities of the world, which draw men back from it. ⟦s2⟧ « Why is light given to the wretched, » that is, prosperity in the wretchedness of our pilgrimage: because, although it does not utterly crush the just man, yet it divides the mind between the love of God and the management of itself. ⟦s2⟧ Whence he adds: « And life to those who, etc. » There is bitterness to every just man, etc., *as far as* or to be set over others in rule.
