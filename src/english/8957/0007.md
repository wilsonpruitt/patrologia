## CHAPTER IV. (cont.)

« Qui autem reliqui fuerint. » That is, the despised of the world, whom the age has left as unworthy: as the Egyptian boy, that is, the lover of the age, black with sins, is often left despised by the age, that he may not run with it, but, broken by adversities, may lie torpid. But him David, that is, Christ, finds, and turns to his own love, and feeds with the food of the word, and makes the guide of his journey, that is, his preacher: by which guide he destroys them that feast together, that is, the gladness of the world, which scorned to have him for a companion.

« Auferentur ex eis. » Because God has chosen them for himself.

« Morientur, et non in sapientia. » Having set the lot of the just between, he returns to the destruction of them that understand not. [0767C] The reprobate despise the just, because through their own death they seek things invisible, or through death they seek the invisible life. But they themselves, who flee alike from death and from wisdom, do indeed utterly forsake wisdom, being unwilling to die for the truth, and yet they do not escape death.
