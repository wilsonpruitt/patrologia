## CHAPTER V.

VERS. 1.-- « Voca ergo. » [n: (Ibid.)] Since he had truly brought this forth against the wicked, though he had secretly set Job in their number, being puffed up with the pride of wisdom he adds an open mockery against Job: « Voca ergo, » that is, if in affliction thou criest out, God does not answer, that is, he does not give effect, because in tranquillity thou didst despise him; and « ad aliquem sanctorum, » that is, [0767D] thou hast not the saints for helpers in affliction, because thou hadst them not for companions in mirth.

[n: (Ibid.)] After the mockery he adds a sentence, etc., *as far as* these things trouble the wise, but anger through vice butchers the foolish.

« Et parvulum occidit. » That is, while he envies him whom he thinks the greater, etc., *as far as* or by having a part it straitens the quantity for us.

[n: (GREG. lib. VI Moral., cap. V.)] Because he is made solid with all his desires in earthly love, whence Cain first built a city on the earth, a stranger to heaven he lives here more happily.

VERS. 3.-- « Et maledixi pulchritudini ejus. » [n: (IDEM.)] Because, whereas from the seen prosperity of the evil the feet of the weak totter, the strong, seeing what punishment [0768A] follows upon their glory, straightway reprove their prosperity. The weak man is changed with the quality of things: while he sees prosperous things, he praises; while they are changed, he judges them to be nothing: but the firm man, together with that very glory, considers the punishment that follows, and straightway condemns it. But because they that thrive in the world draw many with them, he adds:

VERS. 4.-- « Longe fient filii ejus. » That is, his followers.

« In porta. » [n: (Ibid.)] The gate of the kingdom is the day of judgment, in which the elect enter into glory, before which the sons of this world are lifted up, but then they shall be crushed.

VERS. 5.-- « Et non erit qui eruat. » [n: (Ibid.)] Because the truth snatches away there those whom here it presses down through discipline.

[0768B] « Cujus messem. » Every man has a gift of understanding, whereby he understands right things and speaks them: but because he neither loves nor does the same, he fasts from the harvest which he has; but he who hungers for justice, hearing right things from him, refreshes himself, whence: « Quae dicunt, facite [n: (Matth. XXIII)]. »

« Et ipsum rapiet armatus. » [n: (Ibid.)] The devil is overcome as unarmed when, suggesting open evils, he tries to take away all good things alike: but he conquers armed, when he passes by some good things untouched and secretly corrupts others.

« Et bibent. » [n: (IDEM.)] The fool often has riches, etc., *as far as* and the diligent man is hindered by slowness? He answers:

VERS. 6.-- « Nihil in terra fit sine causa. » Because thereby the one is more justly punished for negligence, the other crowned for diligence. But because we are schooled to the understanding of right things [0768C] not only by diligence, but also by persecutions, it is added: « Et de humo non egredietur. »

« Et de humo non egredietur. » [n: (Ibid.)] The ground, that is, the elements of the world, are turned into the punishment of them that offend; whence it is said: « Pugnabit pro eo, » that is, for the Lord, the world of the earth against the senseless; but yet sorrow shall not come forth out of them, because punishment is not born of those things which strike, but of those men who by sinning have wrung out the strokes: but the more the outward punishment chastens, the more is the mind lifted to things above; whence he adds:

VERS. 7.-- « Homo ad laborem nascitur. » Because it is impossible in this pilgrimage to be without labor: but in that the flesh is afflicted, the mind is lifted to the seeking of high things. « Vel homo, » that is, the carnal man, feels the evils [0768D] of the world, because he greedily desires its good things. But he whose mind passes beyond these, lifted like a bird to the heights on the wing of hope, feels no evil, and rejoices even in scourges; but because no man avails for these things by his own strength, he adds:

VERS. 8.-- « Quamobrem ego te. » That is, I ask him through whom I know that these things are given.

VERS. 9.-- « Mirabilia absque numero. » Because the greatness of the divine works can neither be examined by quality nor numbered by quantity.

VERS. 10.-- « Qui dat pluviam. » [n: (Ibid.)] These words are mystical, because Job's friends had been schooled by his fellowship. « Dat pluviam terrae: » when he pours the grace of preaching into the dry hearts of the Gentiles.

« Irrigat autem aquis universa. » When he fills a man [0769A] with the Spirit unto fruitfulness. Man is called all things, because he has something in common with all things. Or all things, that is, rich and poor, and all the differences of men, because a recompense follows a good work.

VERS. 11.-- « Qui ponit humiles in sublime. » [n: (GREG.)] That they who are now despised may judge with the Lord.

« Moerentes. » Because the more they are now troubled by adversities, and chasten themselves by lamentations, so much the loftier soundness shall they have even in this life. These things may be understood of the elect, where the humble transcend with lofty mind all temporal things, and the afflicted are raised up in soundness, not in madness, as they who rejoice when they have done evil. But these are glad out of the certainty of eternal salvation.

[0769B]VERS. 12.-- « Qui dissipat cogitationes. » [n: (IDEM.)] When the reprobate think evil things, God sometimes, lest they prevail against the good, takes away their strength, that they may lack the effect of the work, and be guilty in conscience before God.

VERS. 13.-- « Qui apprehendit sapientes. » [n: (Ibid.)] Because human deeds serve the counsels of God fittingly even then when they resist: as in the selling of Joseph, that he might not be adored; in the demanding of the foreskins by Saul, that David might be destroyed; in the death of the Lord, that all might not believe in him. God grants some things being favorable, permits others being angry: which things he then turns to the use of his own counsel, so that the things which fight against his counsel serve his counsel; and what is done without his will is not contrary to his will, whence: « Omnia quaecunque voluit, etc. »

[0769C]VERS. 14.-- « Per diem incurrent. » Because in the very presence of the truth they were blinded by the error of unbelief, seeing the miracles of Christ and doubting of his godhead.

« Quasi in nocte. » By groping we seek the things we do not see with the eyes: the Jews had seen the miracles, and still as it were groping they were seeking: « Si tu es Christus, dic nobis palam [n: (Joan. X)]. » But Christ could not long be held back by their hands.

« Per diem incurrent tenebras. » [n: (Ibid.)] The mind of the envious, when it is afflicted at another's good, is darkened by the ray of the sun.

« Et quasi in nocte sic palpabunt in meridie. » The day of a good work shines outwardly in one's neighbor: they grope as in the night, because, blind with spite, they busy themselves to find [0769D] something to reprove. Behold Lot, that is, the just man, is shut in and fortified within the house while evil men set themselves against him, that is, he returns to his mind and remains within; the blind men of Sodom go about, that is, the envious search out his deeds and words, and find no door, that is, no approach for reproof, but grope the wall, because everywhere the praiseworthy action of the just man meets them.

« Palpabunt. » But the poor in spirit, that is, he who is not lifted up in his own eyes, is saved from the sword of the mouth, that is, from wicked persuasion.

VERS. 15.-- « Porro salvum faciet egenum a gladio oris. » The sword of the mouth is the accusation of the Jews. The hand of the violent is the heathen world which crucified him: but these Christ overcame by rising again. In which needy one, that is, in the humble people of the faithful, the hope of the life to come is made solid. [0770A] But iniquity, which now opens wide its mouth in reproach of Christ, shall close it through punishment, or being converted ceases from injuries. The same things generally of the depraved.

« De manu violenti pauperem. » That is, from the adverse power, so that he be neither drawn by the blandishments of persuasions nor broken by the pain of torments. But the poor man is not drawn away, because his hope, fixed in things eternal, is made solid against these things. And when the poor man has come to the fruit of that hope, the proud man is struck dumb and draws in his mouth: because torments bind the tongue which was speaking against the just by detraction. But that the elect may escape torments and attain to rewards, he must here be purged by scourges, whence he adds:

VERS. 17.-- « Increpationem ergo Domini ne reprobes. » [n: (Ibid.)] He rejects it who complains that he suffers unjustly. [0770B] Eliphaz therefore, thinking that Job was smitten for purgation, not for the proving of his strength, because Job speaks freely, seeking the causes of his smiting, thinks that he rejects the rebuke of the Lord. So heretics turn to evil what is rightly done by the Church.

VERS. 18.-- « Quia ipse vulnerat. » [n: (IDEM.)] In two ways God wounds those whom he brings back to salvation. Sometimes he strikes in the body that he may heal the mind; sometimes without outward scourges he strikes the hardness of the mind with desire of himself, and so heals it, which lay secure and dead in the world. But while the mind pants after God and despises all worldly things, all things are straightway turned for it into temptation, and afflict it; but while it is worn down by a passing labor, it is delivered from the everlasting one; [0770C] and therefore he adds:

VERS. 19.-- « In sex tribulationibus. » [n: (Ibid.)] By the number six, which a seventh follows, the working and the course of the present life is signified: because on the sixth day God, finishing all things, made man, and on the seventh he rested, which has no evening, that is, no end; in the seventh tribulation therefore he delivers, lest in the seventh evil touch him: because here, chastening as a father, he wears down, but in the day of judgment he hides from the stroke.

VERS. 20.-- « In fame. » [n: (Ibid.)] He reckons up the evils of the present life and the helps of God. The famine of the mind is the silence of the word of God: which being withdrawn, the temptation of the flesh grows strong, which is war. But God even in famine snatches the soul from death, because he refreshes it with his word. « Et in bello. » Against the temptations of the flesh he makes men strong. But [0770D] such a one sometimes fears detractions, whence he adds:

VERS. 21.-- « A flagello linguae. » That is, from the upbraiding of reproach, for which, being mocked, he ceases from good; whence David: « Et a verbo aspero. » From this the holy man is hidden, because, while he seeks not praise here, neither does he feel reproaches; but because after these things a man fears some torment of bodies, there follows: « Et non timebis calamitatem. » That is, of persecution: but because such a one prepares security for himself in the terror of the judgment to come, he adds:

VERS. 22.-- « In vastitate et fame. » The wasting is [sic: *es*] the gehenna which is without, etc., *as far as* that in the moment of death he may not shudder at its onset, there is added: « Et [0771A] bestias terrae. » Which at the time of death are let loose to snatch away souls, but he does not fear them raging who has not followed them fawning: and the beginning of the recompense is, at death, security of mind. But when a man lives well, heed must be taken lest any one, despising the rest, glory in his singularity, but let him consider the good of fellowship, whence he adds:
