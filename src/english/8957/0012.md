## CHAPTER VIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- « And answering. » [n: (GREG.)] It must be known first of all that, as Eliphaz noted the most holy Job chiefly for pride, though he touched briefly upon hypocrisy also, so this man too intends to note him chiefly for hypocrisy; but before he does this, [0778C] he sets something first, from which it is weighed how burdensome the words of Job himself had been to him, words namely of edification: as though therefore he could no longer endure a man speaking well, he broke out indignantly into these words, saying:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- « How long wilt thou speak such things. » As if to say: why dost thou speak useless things? and a manifold spirit . . . . .

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Still thou speakest such things. » [n: (Ibid.)] Because to a wicked man the words of edification are heavy, so that he cannot bear them, as it were; and because he does not wish to be corrected, he accuses things well said.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And a manifold spirit. » [n: (Ibid.)] While he attributes manifoldness to his speech, he reproves the poverty of his understanding. ⟦s2⟧ And note that there are four kinds of speaking. [0778D] For some are ample in mouth and in sense, who are to be praised; others straitened in both, who are to be pitied; others ample in sense, but unable to speak out, who are to be helped; others poor in sense, but torrential in tongue, who are to be despised: and this last kind this man twists into a charge against Job.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- « Doth God. » [n: (GREG.)] These things Job had neither denied nor been ignorant of, etc., *as far as* as though they had perished justly, they strike others, whence he adds:

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4, 5.-- « Even if thy sons have sinned, etc. ⟦s2⟧ If at daybreak: » as though they themselves were in the light, and the Church were in the night: which they summon, that it may rise through the knowledge of God, and through the prayer of penitence wash away things past, « if clean » in thought, « upright » in work.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0779A]VERS. 6.-- « He will awake. » He who now sleeps from giving help to thee who art erring.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And he will render peaceable. » [n: (GREG.)] That is, he will restore the tranquillity of the present life, which alone they reckon to be God's reward.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The dwelling of the just man. » [n: (Ibid.)] They say it is the counsel of the mind that is made peaceable, because if they draw men to their own side, they fall silent from contention. ⟦s2⟧ They promise also the riches of understanding by saying.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- « So much. » But because their words are not easily believed, they bend the sentences of the fathers into an argument for their own error, whence it is added:

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « That thy former things. » Because they promise both that what was lost be recovered, and rewards of the present life greater still.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- « For ask the generations. » They advise that it be not seen, [0779B] but investigated, because they do not wish that to be looked upon which lies plainly open to all.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- « Of yesterday: » sometimes they teach morally that present things are to be inferred from things past, namely from their passing, that these are nothing; whence he adds, « of yesterday. » Because, if the former generation is called to mind, this life is known to pass swiftly as a shadow.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- « They shall teach. » They praise the fathers along with us, that thereby he may assail us the more.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « From the heart. » [n: (Ibid.)] As if to say: thou hast simplicity of spirit in the mouth, not in the heart: they the contrary. ⟦s2⟧ Often the wicked, not attending to themselves, tear the good; and either they bring forth good things which they have learned not by seeing but by hearing, or they thrust upon others the evils which they themselves do; and thus by their deeds they excuse themselves, by their words they judge. ⟦s3⟧ So Baldath, [0779C] a feigner of justice, says wonderful things against hypocrites, and presumes to teach the just man about the things which would suit the depraved. ⟦s4⟧ Senseless is he who, while the gardens thirst, pours water into the river.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- « Can it live. » [n: (Ibid.)] He compares the hypocrite to a bulrush or a sedge, etc., *as far as* but let him show it by others' crimes.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Before all herbs. » [n: (Ibid.)] The just also are herbs according to the flesh, because all flesh is grass [n: (Isa. XLVIII)]; and they wither, because their works fail together with the life of the flesh. ⟦s2⟧ But those of the hypocrites wither before: because they forsake good things as soon as they have obtained the things for the sake of which they feigned. ⟦s3⟧ These are « the grass of the housetops, which withered before it was plucked up. »

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- « So are the ways of all. » Behold to what he compares [0779D] the bulrush or the sedge: the hypocrite's hope is glory, and worldly things of this kind, which perish; eternal things abide.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- « His folly shall not please him. » [n: (Ibid.)] Folly is to sell a great thing at a cheap price, that is, to do laborious good works for favour: in this he is now delighted, but in punishment it will displease him, and then he will know that all the things which have passed were nothing, whence:

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And his confidence is like the spider's web. » The spider's web is woven with care, and is scattered by a breath of wind: so whatever the hypocrite sweats out, the breeze of favour carries off.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- « He shall lean upon his house. » The house of the mind is whatever thing the soul inhabits by love: the house of the hypocrite therefore is [0780A] favour. ⟦s2⟧ Or, the house is confidence in one's own holiness, which will stand before men, but not before God.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- « He seemeth moist. » The bulrush, the hypocrite, seems moist in the night, that is, he appears to men as one filled with the grace of holiness, but before the sun, in the day of judgment, he will wither.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And in his rising his shoot. » Every herb is first brought forth from the earth, etc., *as far as* the hypocrite, even when he acts rightly, wishes to be seen by many.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- « Upon a heap of stones. » [n: (Ibid.)] The roots are thoughts; the stones, because "living" is not added, are men both good and bad; the hypocrite therefore, who lingers among the stones, multiplies his thoughts in seeking the admiration of men: for through all that he does he seeks praises with hidden thought, having openly [0780B] that which his enemies may plunder. ⟦s2⟧ As Hezekiah, who after many benefits done to him showed all his goods to the messengers of the king of Babylon: whence he presently hears: « All shall be carried off into Babylon [n: (II Reg. XX)]; » [var: Vulg. (IV Reg. XX, 17) *auferentur omnia*, "all things shall be carried away"] so too, while praise is sought, virtue and beauty are handed over into the hand of the enemy; whence Baldad says:

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And he shall abide among the stones. » Because he stands there, where he makes firm the intention of his mind: and though he be full of good things, if God shall have swallowed him up, that is, taken him away by death, from his place, that is, from the favours of this life; he will say: I know thee not, as it is said to the foolish virgins [n: (Matth. XXV)]: and rightly, because it is not enough for him unless he has made others also like himself, and this is what he says: for this is the joy of his way.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « For this is the joy, etc. [n: (Ibid.)] That he may be punished also for their [0780C] guilt, but:

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- « God will not cast away the simple man. » Whose simplicity may now be called folly by the craftiness of the double-minded.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Nor will he reach out his hand. » [n: (Ibid.)] So as to raise them from the depth in which they seek glory. ⟦s2⟧ The malignant are hypocrites, who do not even do good things well. ⟦s3⟧ Or, because outwardly they show themselves to their neighbour pure and simple, while inwardly they hide duplicity; against whom Moses says: « Thou shalt not put on a garment woven of wool and linen together. » [n: (Deut. XXII.)]

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- « Until it be filled. » Not that afterward, which is certain: but not before, which may seem doubtful to men: as if to say, he neither forsakes the simple before the judgment, nor ceases to strike the wicked.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Let thy mouth be filled with laughter, » that is, the mind with the joy [0780D] « of eternal security, whence:

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And thy lips with rejoicing. » Jubilation is conceived in the heart with such great gladness as speech may not unfold, and yet the mouth sounds it.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- « They that hate. » [n: (Ibid.)] Confusion shall clothe the enemies of the just in the judgment, because then, the judge being seen, all their evils are turned before the eyes of the mind, and the guilt that presses them down clothes them on every side. ⟦s2⟧ And they come to this for the reason that here they loved transitory things alone, whence he adds:

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And the tabernacle, » that is, the building of eternal felicity, which they multiply though it will fall upon them, that here they may defend themselves from necessities [sic: *necessatibus*] as from heat and rain: in which the mind dwells, and which the good wayfarer despises.
