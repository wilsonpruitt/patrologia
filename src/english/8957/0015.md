## CHAPTER X.

VERS. 1.-- « Taedet animam meam vitae meae. » [n: ( Ibid. )] But when this life grows cheap through love of the heavenly, he who was defending his faults now accuses them. And this is:

« Dimittam eloquium » which I was using in my own defense.

[0785B] « Adversum me, » because by confessing I accuse myself, not as one who still blushes to confess, because he does not forsake his evils with so great a desire; but because sometimes a man confessing openly does not groan, but says with gladness what ought to be lamented, he adds:

« Loquar in amaritudine, » that bitterness may punish what the tongue accuses. Out of penitence security is born, and more confidently he asks how it is dealt with toward him. And this is:

VERS. 2.-- « Dicam Deo: Noli me condemnare, » because my punishment quenches thy torments.

« Indica mihi cur me ita judices, » that is, thou scourgest me, whether unto security or for what fault in particular; because even if he is just, he yet has some evils: and therefore he asks that God show himself to him, so that what God punishes in him, [0785C] he too may punish by weeping. Therefore I seek the cause; for I know that it is not unjustly.

« Cur me ita judices. » In two ways he judges here. Either he sends a punishment, etc., *as far as* whom also by striking he keeps in innocence. He says therefore:

VERS. 3.-- « Nunquid bonum. » This question demands a denial, etc., *as far as* because he considers the weak man.

« Opus manuum. » As if to say: thou dost not impiously destroy him whom thou hast made freely.

« Impiorum, » that is, of the malignant spirits, who, since they cannot return to life, seek companions unto death; whose counsel it was that they should break by adversities Job who was upright in prosperity; but God [0785D] does not suffer a man to be tempted above his strength.

VERS. 4.-- « Nunquid oculi carnei. » The eyes of the flesh know nothing but certain deeds done in time, because they too are closed together with time, and human sight follows after whatever work there is, it does not run before it.

VERS. 5.-- « Dies. » The days too and the years of man differ from the eternal: to whom nothing is past, nothing is to come, but he beholds all things present.

VERS. 6.-- « Ut quaeras iniquitatem meam. » And so that thou mayest know that I have done nothing impiously, as though he were asking humbly: why dost thou prove by scourges in time him whom thou hast known with thee perfectly and before all time?

VERS. 7.-- « Cum sit nemo » [n: ( Ibid. )] As if: what remains to thee but to spare him whom no one can withstand? For by how much none can hold back thy vengeance, by so much the more [0786A] easily ought mercy to spare; and that he may be appeased, since we cannot offer our own work, let us offer him his own work.

VERS. 8.-- « Manus tuae. » As if: weigh it mercifully, lest what thou hast made perish.

« Fecerunt. » According to the soul.

« Totum in circuitu. » According to the body, etc., *as far as* when of the rest he said only: *fiat.*

« Et sic repente. » As if: him whom thou settest before others with such dignity, why dost thou despise with such vileness? But together with this dignity, which is through the spirit? there is infirmity through the flesh, which may move a merciful judge, whence he says:

VERS. 9.-- « Memento, quaeso, » etc. Whence the fault is venial, whereas it is unforgivable in an angel, who was strong. It is clay, when water floods the earth: [0786B] so is man, when the soul waters the flesh, whose death he plainly adds:

« Et in pulverem, » etc. As if: see the matter and the punishment, and spare the fault; and because he had set forth the quality of man as created, he adds the order of propagation, whence he says:

VERS. 10.-- « Nonne sicut lac, » etc. Man was made as clay, but propagated as milk is milked out by seed, that he may be curdled as cheese into flesh. He is clothed with flesh and skin, made solid with bones and sinews. After the creation of the body, which is the lesser thing, there is added the wondrous breathing-in of quickening.

« Nonne sicut lac, » while the mind is drawn by the Spirit of God and by its oldness as milk is milked out, because it is formed in a kind of beginning; as cheese it is curdled, [0786C] when, growing rich, it is pressed together by thoughts, so that it no longer flows away in desires, but is made solid in one love. But against these first rudiments the flesh murmurs out of its old habit.

VERS. 11.-- « Pelle et carnalibus. » That is, the mind is walled about by the siege of carnal motions; but he who outwardly loosens it for wars, inwardly strengthens it through the bones of the virtues, that it may be humble and strong. And God rightly bestows the uprightness of living rightly, because he spares past offenses.

VERS. 12.-- « Vitam et misericordiam. » [n: ( Ibid. )] But these good things he has given in vain, unless he himself keep them, whence he adds:

« Et visitatio tua. » The same things of the inward man, [0786D] etc., *as far as* he considers the gifts of God more subtly.

« Vitam et misericordiam. » [n: ( Ibid. )] Life is given together with evil minds, etc., *as far as* after he had said of himself the gifts of mercy generally, he adds:

VERS. 13.-- « Tamen scio, quia universorum memineris » which is said: why do I tremble concerning myself, who know that all the nations are to be gathered.

« Licet haec celes in corde. » [n: ( Ibid. )] Because as yet thou dost not show it in open speech. Those who are certain of pardon are wont to be touched by the memory of their former wickedness, and to be beaten upon, unwilling, by unlawful thought, whence he adds:

VERS. 14.-- « Si peccavi. » God spares for a time, etc., *as far as* but let them who are beaten fix their hope more strongly upon the help of God alone.

[0787A]VERS. 16.--Propter superbiam. » [n: (GREG.)] While the lioness greedily seeks food for her whelps, etc., *as far as* but if we grow numb from holy desire, he sets forth the examples of those who watch well.

VERS. 17.-- « Multiplicas iram tuam adversum me, » that is, thou showest it manifold, because even if he scourges the good, what shall it be for the impious? Thus, the manifold anger being foreseen, man returns to himself, and punishes his depraved deeds.

VERS. 19.-- « Fuissem quasi non essem, de utero. » [n: ( Ibid. )] The same as what was said there: « sicut abortivum absconditum, » etc., or, they who, being conceived, did not see the light.

VERS. 20.-- « Nunquid non paucitas dierum meorum. » [n: ( Ibid. )] Let not the delight of the present life take hold of me, because it passes quickly.

[0787B] « Dimitte ergo me . . . dolorem meum. » [n: ( Ibid. )] That is, temper the scourges, etc., *as far as* which the foresight of hell chiefly does, whence he says:

VERS. 21.-- « Antequam vadam. » Hell is called earth, because the captives are held there.

« Antequam vadam. » Not that he who laments goes, but that he goes who does not lament: as if I should say to a debtor, pay before I constrain thee.

« Non revertar. » [n: ( Ibid. )] Because mercy does not deliver the damned any further.

VERS. 22.-- « Miseriae et tenebrarum. » Because grief torments without those whom the blindness of the mind darkens within; or, there no one returns any more to the light, as in this life: where, though there be misery, it is permitted to return to the light by conversion.

[0787C] « Umbra mortis. » Death is separation from God; the shadow is its darkness; because the penal fire has burning and not light, so that they may be punished in both who sinned in both.

« Nullus ordo. » [n: ( Ibid. )] Not that God does not ordain, *as far as* the quality of things is not kept. Whence he also adds:

« Sempiternus horror. » Here is the fear of evil to come, etc., *as far as* lest they should come to the torments whom he who saw Lazarus saw in punishment.
