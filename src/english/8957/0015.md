## CHAPTER X.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- « My soul is weary of my life. » [n: ( Ibid. )] But when this life grows cheap through love of the heavenly, he who was defending his faults now accuses them. ⟦s2⟧ And this is:

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « I will let go my speech » which I was using in my own defense.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0785B] « Against me, » because by confessing I accuse myself, not as one who still blushes to confess, because he does not forsake his evils with so great a desire; but because sometimes a man confessing openly does not groan, but says with gladness what ought to be lamented, he adds:

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « I will speak in bitterness, » that bitterness may punish what the tongue accuses. ⟦s2⟧ Out of penitence security is born, and more confidently he asks how it is dealt with toward him. ⟦s3⟧ And this is:

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- « I will say to God: Do not condemn me, » because my punishment quenches thy torments.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Show me why thou judgest me thus, » that is, thou scourgest me, whether unto security or for what fault in particular; because even if he is just, he yet has some evils: and therefore he asks that God show himself to him, so that what God punishes in him, [0785C] he too may punish by weeping. ⟦s2⟧ Therefore I seek the cause; for I know that it is not unjustly.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Why thou judgest me thus. » In two ways he judges here. ⟦s2⟧ Either he sends a punishment, etc., *as far as* whom also by striking he keeps in innocence. ⟦s3⟧ He says therefore:

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- « Is it good. » This question demands a denial, etc., *as far as* because he considers the weak man.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The work of the hands. » As if to say: thou dost not impiously destroy him whom thou hast made freely.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Of the impious, » that is, of the malignant spirits, who, since they cannot return to life, seek companions unto death; whose counsel it was that they should break by adversities Job who was upright in prosperity; but God [0785D] does not suffer a man to be tempted above his strength.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- « Are the eyes of flesh. » The eyes of the flesh know nothing but certain deeds done in time, because they too are closed together with time, and human sight follows after whatever work there is, it does not run before it.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- « Days. » The days too and the years of man differ from the eternal: to whom nothing is past, nothing is to come, but he beholds all things present.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- « That thou mayest seek my iniquity. » And so that thou mayest know that I have done nothing impiously, as though he were asking humbly: why dost thou prove by scourges in time him whom thou hast known with thee perfectly and before all time?

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- « Whereas there is no one » [n: ( Ibid. )] As if: what remains to thee but to spare him whom no one can withstand? ⟦s2⟧ For by how much none can hold back thy vengeance, by so much the more [0786A] easily ought mercy to spare; and that he may be appeased, since we cannot offer our own work, let us offer him his own work.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- « Thy hands. » As if: weigh it mercifully, lest what thou hast made perish.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « They have made. » According to the soul.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Wholly round about. » According to the body, etc., *as far as* when of the rest he said only: *fiat.*

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And so suddenly. » As if: him whom thou settest before others with such dignity, why dost thou despise with such vileness? ⟦s2⟧ But together with this dignity, which is through the spirit? there is infirmity through the flesh, which may move a merciful judge, whence he says:

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- « Remember, I beseech thee, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ Whence the fault is venial, whereas it is unforgivable in an angel, who was strong. ⟦s3⟧ It is clay, when water floods the earth: [0786B] so is man, when the soul waters the flesh, whose death he plainly adds:

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And into dust, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ As if: see the matter and the punishment, and spare the fault; and because he had set forth the quality of man as created, he adds the order of propagation, whence he says:

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- « Was it not as milk, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ Man was made as clay, but propagated as milk is milked out by seed, that he may be curdled as cheese into flesh. ⟦s3⟧ He is clothed with flesh and skin, made solid with bones and sinews. ⟦s4⟧ After the creation of the body, which is the lesser thing, there is added the wondrous breathing-in of quickening.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Was it not as milk, » while the mind is drawn by the Spirit of God and by its oldness as milk is milked out, because it is formed in a kind of beginning; as cheese it is curdled, [0786C] when, growing rich, it is pressed together by thoughts, so that it no longer flows away in desires, but is made solid in one love. ⟦s2⟧ But against these first rudiments the flesh murmurs out of its old habit.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- « With skin and carnal things. » That is, the mind is walled about by the siege of carnal motions; but he who outwardly loosens it for wars, inwardly strengthens it through the bones of the virtues, that it may be humble and strong. ⟦s2⟧ And God rightly bestows the uprightness of living rightly, because he spares past offenses.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- « Life and mercy. » [n: ( Ibid. )] But these good things he has given in vain, unless he himself keep them, whence he adds:

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And thy visitation. » The same things of the inward man, [0786D] etc., *as far as* he considers the gifts of God more subtly.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Life and mercy. » [n: ( Ibid. )] Life is given together with evil minds, etc., *as far as* after he had said of himself the gifts of mercy generally, he adds:

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- « Yet I know that thou art mindful of all » which is said: why do I tremble concerning myself, who know that all the nations are to be gathered.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Though thou hide these things in the heart. » [n: ( Ibid. )] Because as yet thou dost not show it in open speech. ⟦s2⟧ Those who are certain of pardon are wont to be touched by the memory of their former wickedness, and to be beaten upon, unwilling, by unlawful thought, whence he adds:

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- « If I have sinned. » God spares for a time, etc., *as far as* but let them who are beaten fix their hope more strongly upon the help of God alone.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0787A]VERS. 16.--Because of pride. » [n: (GREG.)] While the lioness greedily seeks food for her whelps, etc., *as far as* but if we grow numb from holy desire, he sets forth the examples of those who watch well.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- « Thou multipliest thy wrath against me, » that is, thou showest it manifold, because even if he scourges the good, what shall it be for the impious? ⟦s2⟧ Thus, the manifold anger being foreseen, man returns to himself, and punishes his depraved deeds.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- « I should have been as though I were not, from the womb. » [n: ( Ibid. )] The same as what was said there: « as a hidden untimely birth, » etc., or, they who, being conceived, did not see the light.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- « Shall not the fewness of my days. » [n: ( Ibid. )] Let not the delight of the present life take hold of me, because it passes quickly.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0787B] « Let me go therefore . . . my sorrow. » [n: ( Ibid. )] That is, temper the scourges, etc., *as far as* which the foresight of hell chiefly does, whence he says:

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- « Before I go. » Hell is called earth, because the captives are held there.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Before I go. » Not that he who laments goes, but that he goes who does not lament: as if I should say to a debtor, pay before I constrain thee.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « I shall not return. » [n: ( Ibid. )] Because mercy does not deliver the damned any further.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- « Of misery and of darkness. » Because grief torments without those whom the blindness of the mind darkens within; or, there no one returns any more to the light, as in this life: where, though there be misery, it is permitted to return to the light by conversion.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0787C] « The shadow of death. » Death is separation from God; the shadow is its darkness; because the penal fire has burning and not light, so that they may be punished in both who sinned in both.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « No order. » [n: ( Ibid. )] Not that God does not ordain, *as far as* the quality of things is not kept. ⟦s2⟧ Whence he also adds:

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Everlasting horror. » Here is the fear of evil to come, etc., *as far as* lest they should come to the torments whom he who saw Lazarus saw in punishment.
