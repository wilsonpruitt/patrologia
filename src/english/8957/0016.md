## CHAPTER XI.

VERS. 2.-- « Nunquid qui multa. » [n: (Greg. in Job. lib. X.)] He begins from insults, etc., *as far as* who think nothing right except what they themselves perceive.

« Aut vir verbosus. » A true saying, because it is read, [0787D] silence is the cultivation of justice, but not said fittingly to the occasion. He who cannot bear right things, careless of being corrected, girds himself for an answer.

VERS. 3.-- « Tibi soli tacebunt homines, » etc. [n: ( Ibid. )] He whom conscience gnaws thinks all right things a mockery of himself, and reckons what is said generally to be said of him. Whence he is kindled also to speak against them, that so at least he may govern his shame as though he were not guilty, if he accuses others; whence, lying, he adds:

VERS. 4.-- « Dixisti enim: Purus est sermo meus. » Job had not said it; but it is a solace to wrongdoing if the life of him who rebukes is defiled, even falsely.

VERS. 5.-- « Atque utinam Deus loqueretur tecum, » etc. [n: ( Ibid. )] The perverse wish good things in word only, [0788A] that they may seem kindly, and that the things which are in the present may be shown to be evil, whence this man too says:

« Utinam Deus loqueretur. » As if he should say, thou now speakest to thyself, while thou art carnally wise: thou art empty of the spirit of truth; as though I had compassion rather on thy ignorance than on thy punishment:

[n: ( Ibid. )] « Labia, » that is, judgments, through which, being laid open, etc., *as far as* he acknowledges to God: and if he acknowledges his sins, he patiently bears the scourges. And this is:

VERS. 6.-- « Et intelligeres quod multa minora. » Unjustly he charges the just man with iniquity, but this insult proceeds from zeal for God, and is venial. Not knowing also the merits of the just man, out of ignorance he adds a mockery:

VERS. 7.-- « Forsitan vestigia Dei comprehendes. » [n: ( Ibid. )] The kindness of his visitation, etc., *as far [0788B] as* because his essence shall be fully seen by none.

VERS. 8.-- « Excelsior. » Because in the circumscription of his own spirit he transcends all things.

« Profundior inferno. » Because by transcending he bears upward.

VERS. 9.-- « Longior terra. » Because he exceeds the measure of the creature by the perpetuity of eternity

« Latior mari. » Because he so possesses the times, etc., *as far as* or knowest thou not how to reprove thine own self in temptations?

VERS. 10.-- « Si subverterit. » [n: ( Ibid. )] He overturns heaven, etc., *as far as* that either, resisting, she may be exercised, or, being seduced, she may be broken, whence rightly he says:

« Quis contradicet ei? vel, quis ei dicere potest cur ita facias? » Because none can withstand the judgment of God, [0788C] or pierce into it by inquiry. Why does he overturn or straiten?

VERS. 11.-- « Ipse enim novit. » [n: ( Ibid. )] While the mind is lifted up by elation, etc., *as far as* whither vanity together with iniquity drags it, namely to swelling, he adds:

VERS. 12.-- « Vir vanus in superbiam. » [n: ( Ibid. )] The vain man is made bold out of his fault, etc., *as far as* through unbridled liberty he runs boldly astray through his desires.

VERS. 13.-- « Tu autem firmasti. » [n: ( Ibid. )] That the heart is established is not in this place, etc., *as far as* he subjoins to him, as though teaching: « et eris stabilis, » etc.

VERS. 14.-- « Si iniquitates. » [n: ( Ibid. )] Iniquity in the hand, etc., *as far as* if the conscience of his own guilt accuses him.

[0788D]VERS. 15.-- « Et eris stabilis. » Because by so much the less does he tremble at the judgment, as he stands the more solidly in good acts.

VERS. 16.-- « Miseriae quoque. » [n: ( Ibid. )] The soul feels the evils of the present life the more harshly, the more it neglects to weigh the good that follows. But if it fix the eye of the heart upon the things that abide, it beholds that whatever tends to an end is nothing.

« Et quasi aquarum. » Because the miseries that pass do not shake by force, but pour in a touch of sorrow, while without he grieves for the stroke, within he grows cold from temptation, against which the divine light is kindled the more, whence it is added.

[0789A]VERS. 17.-- « Et quasi meridianus. » The noonday brightness at evening is the renewal of virtue in temptation.

« Et cum te consumptum. » [n: (GREG.)] When the just man has begun to sink through outward evils also, then his brightness begins: on the contrary, the light of the wicked is in the day of this life, their darkness at evening. « Et cum te consumptum. » Because, falling without, he is renewed within, and the harder the things he bears, the more surely he hopes for the rewards, and this is:

VERS. 18.-- « Et habebis fiduciam. » Those who are buried sleep securely, etc., *as far as* he sleeps secure within himself, hidden from the acts of the world.

VERS. 19.-- « Requiesces et non erit. » For he who is fixed in the desire of eternity, since there is nothing in [0789B] the world that he seeks after, there is nothing about the world that he dreads.

« Et deprecabuntur. » The depraved, when they preach right things, promise to others as a great thing that which they themselves seek after; but lest by long promising what they are should lie open, they return the more quickly to words of uprightness, whence it is added:

VERS. 20.-- « Oculi autem impiorum. » By the name of eyes, etc., *as far as* he does not find where he may be able to hide himself.

« Et spes illorum abominatio, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] Because the spirit perishes forever from that very source from which the flesh lives sweetly for a time.
