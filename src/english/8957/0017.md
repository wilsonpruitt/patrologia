## CHAPTER XII.

VERS. 1.-- « Respondens autem Job. » [n: (GREG.)] [0789C] This Sophar said truly, etc., *as far as* whence Job adds:

VERS. 2.-- « Ergo vos estis soli homines. » He who thinks that he goes before all men in reason exults that he alone is a man. « Ergo vos estis soli homines. » For he who reckons that he alone is wise, what else does he think but that this same wisdom will die with him?

VERS. 3.-- « Et mihi est cor sicut. » That he may correct their pride, he denies that he is their inferior; and lest he pass the bound of his own humility, he keeps silence that he is their superior. See with what discretion, in which he shows what they ought to feel concerning themselves.

« Quis enim haec quae nostis ignorat? » As if he should say: since the things you say are known to all, why do you swell singularly over [0789D] the knowledge of what has been said?

VERS. 4.-- « Qui deridetur. » [n: (Ibid.)] Because human derision makes him near to God whom innocence of life keeps a stranger to human depravities.

VERS. 5.-- « Lampas contempta. » [n: (Ibid.)] By the name of the rich he designates the elation of the proud; but the simplicity of the just man is called both a lamp and despised: a lamp, because it shines within; despised, because among the minds of carnal men it is reckoned abject.

« Parata ad tempus statutum. » The appointed time of the despised lamp is the predestined day of the last judgment, in which every just man who is now looked down upon is shown with how great power he shines.

VERS. 6.-- « Abundant tabernacula praedonum. » [n: (Ibid.)] He shows that the riches of the present life are nothing, etc., [0790A] *as far as* and yet the good things they have they received from God.

« Nimirum interroga jumenta, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] Beasts of burden are those who are duller in perception. The fowls of heaven are those who are wise in lofty things.

VERS. 8.-- « Pisces. » [n: (Ibid.)] The curious are understood. And the sense is: if thou shouldst inquire of these, they confess God the creator of all things. It can also be understood according to the letter.

VERS. 10.-- « In cujus manu anima. » In this place, by the soul of every living thing, the very life of the body is signified.

« Spiritus universae carnis. » [n: (Ibid.)] That is, man's effect of spiritual understanding, which things are in the power of God. It can also be that by the soul of every living thing the life of beasts of burden is signified.

VERS. 11.-- « Nonne auris verba dijudicat. » From [0790B] these bodily things, etc., *as far as* and what you know savors to me to the marrow through inmost love.

VERS. 13.-- « Apud ipsum est sapientia, » etc. [n: (GREG. in Job, l. XXI.)] For with God in the beginning the Word was God, who is both the power of God and the wisdom of God; he has counsel, because he disposes his own things; understanding, because he knows ours.

VERS. 14.-- « Si destruxerit, nemo est qui aedificet. » The Almighty destroys the human race when he abandons it, he builds it when he fills it: and unless this be done, a man is admonished outwardly by a preacher in vain.

« Si incluserit hominem. » Every man through that, etc., *as far as* and not to soften the reprobate heart through grace.

VERS. 15.-- « Si continuerit aquas, omnia siccabuntur. » [0790C] Because, if the knowledge of preachers is withdrawn, etc., *as far as* he referred the sevenfold grace

« Si emiserit eas, subvertent. » For if the grace of the Holy Spirit is poured in, etc., *as far as* straightway the earth is overturned: because the hardness of the sinful mind is changed out of the obstinacy of its own immobility.

VERS. 16.-- « Apud ipsum est fortitudo. » In the former words in which he says, etc., *as far as* he shall judge for the elect in the kingdom in what manner the wisdom of the Father is.

« Novit et decipientem et eum qui decipitur. » [n: (Ibid.)] He knows the deceiver, etc., *as far as* is sometimes wont to be made the beginning of vengeance.

VERS. 17.-- « Adducit consiliarios in stultum finem. » By counsellors he means preachers, who furnish their hearers with the counsel of life. And these, when they preach for the sake of temporal things, [0790D] are led away into a foolish end: because they tend thither by labor whence they ought to flee by rectitude of mind.

« Et judices. » They who are set over the examining of the morals of others are rightly called judges; but when they by no means carefully discuss them, nor know how to correct them, they are led into stupor.

VERS. 18.-- « Balteum regum dissolvit. » For in those who seemed to rule their own members well, he destroys the girdle of chastity because of the fault of elation. And because in the loins is the delight of the flesh, he girds their loins with the rope of sin and of delight.

VERS. 19.-- « Ducit sacerdotes inglorios. » [n: (Ibid.)] Nay, they shall not find glory before the judge, which [0791A] they do not now seek out in the morals of their subjects by zeal for preaching.

« Et optimates supplantat. » [n: (GREG.)] Because, when by just judgment he abandons the mind of those who rule, they neglect heavenly things, and fall here into their own pleasures.

VERS. 20.-- « Commutans labium veracium. » Because often those who were truthful in preaching heavenly things, while they love temporal things, rush headlong into the loving of earthly things, and the lip of the truthful is changed. Or it can be understood of the Jews, who, being truthful before, announced that Christ would come, and they themselves denied this same one when present.

« Et doctrinam. » [n: (Ibid.)] The doctrine of the elders has been taken away, because the Jews by no means followed in believing the things which they remembered that their fathers had foretold.

VERS. 21.-- « Effundit despectionem super principes. » [0791B] The Jews, abiding in the commandments of the law, seemed to have rule through faith: the Gentiles, pressed down in the deep, lay hidden through unbelief; but when Judaea denied the mystery of the Lord's incarnation, the Gentile world believed. Thus:

VERS. 22.-- « Revelat profunda de tenebris; » when the mystical senses of the allegories are recognized in the hidden words of the prophets.

« Et producit in lucem umbram mortis. » [n: (Ibid.)] He says the shadow of death, etc., *as far as* the death of the flesh is least of all feared.

VERS. 23.-- « Qui multiplicat gentes. » [n: (Ibid.)] Because he both extends the nations by the fruitfulness of offspring, and yet leaves them in their own unbelief, and those whom he had left in the fall of unbelief he at some time led back [0791C] to the faith, and when they are thus restored there comes to pass what follows, « qui immutat cor, » etc.

VERS. 24.-- « Qui immutat cor principum. » [n: (Ibid.)] The heart of the princes of the earth has been changed, etc., *as far as* the way could not lie open against the author of all things.

VERS. 25.-- « Palpabunt quasi in tenebris. » [n: (Ibid.)] For as it were in darkness he gropes, etc., *as far as* « Nonne hic est filius fabri? »
