## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- « Ecce omnia. » [n: (GREG.)] For all these things to the blessed man, etc., *as far as* he saw some things to be in works, others in words; there follows:

« Haec vidit oculus meus, et audivit auris mea. » But because words have no profit if they [0791D] lack understanding, there follows:

« Et intellexi singula. » For when something is shown, or heard, if understanding is not granted, it is not prophecy at all; and because he is not lifted up over that understanding, he shows it when he says:

VERS. 2.-- « Secundum scientiam veram. » [var: Vulg. Job 13:2 *vestram*, "according to your knowledge"] By these words he made known of how great humility he was, who denies that he is inferior to them whose life he had far transcended by living holily: and he affirms that he knows according to their knowledge, he who by knowing heavenly things passed beyond their earthly thoughts through the spirit of prophecy.

VERS. 3.-- « Sed tamen ad Omnipotentem loquar. » [n: (Ibid.)] We speak with the Almighty while we beseech his mercy [0792A]. We dispute with him while, joining ourselves to his justice, we discuss our own deeds with subtle searching. Or he disputes with God who, obeying him here, will afterward judge the peoples with him, who even in the meantime does not cease to judge concerning the wicked.

VERS. 4.-- « Prius vos ostendam, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] So the Church says of heretics, « prius vos ostendam fabricatores; » for as a building is framed out of stones, so also a lie is framed out of words.

VERS. 5.-- « Atque utinam taceretis. » [n: (Ibid.)] Seeing that they wish to appear, etc., *as far as* by which they may seduce weak minds.

VERS. 6.-- « Audite ergo correctionem meam. » [n: (Ibid.)] Rightly therefore he brought in correction first and judgment after, because unless the swelling of the fool be first pressed down by correction, [0792B] the judgment of the just man is by no means known through understanding.

VERS. 9.-- « Decipietur ut homo vestris fraudulentiis. » Heretics practise fraud upon God, who build up things that do not please him for whom they speak, and offend him while they strive to defend him.

VERS. 10.-- « Ipse vos arguet quoniam. » There are those who feel the truth of God in the heart, etc., *as far as* by how much in the secret of thought they know the right judgments of God.

VERS. 11.-- « Statim. » [n: (Ibid.)] By a human word the very strictness of his rectitude is called the motion of God: which shall trouble by vengeance the reprobate, who here refuse to be troubled by penitence.

« Et terror ejus irruet. » Because for the reprobate fear does not beget [0792C] rest, but punishment begets fear, whence the prophet: « Sola vexatio intellectum dabit auditui [n: (Isa. XXVIII)]: » because when they are punished for the commandments of God despised, then they understand what they have heard.

VERS. 12.-- « Memoria vestra. » However much a man for the sake of memory, etc., *as far as* and the elated flesh wastes away in rottenness.

VERS. 13.-- « Tacete paulisper ut loquar. » He shows that they have spoken with the sense of the flesh: whom he therefore restrains to silence, that he may say the things which his mind shall have suggested to him. As if he should say: I speak not carnally, but spiritually, because through the sense of the spirit I hear what I utter through the ministry of the body.

VERS. 14.-- « Quare lacero carnes meas dentibus. » Teeth in this place signify the inward [0792D] senses, etc., *as far as* if I cannot profit those who look upon me, that is, you.

« Dentibus meis. » The teeth are the inward senses, which search out particulars, etc., *as far as* lest it be able to chew spiritual things.

« Et animam meam porto in manibus. » [n: (Ibid.)] To carry the soul in the hands is to show the intention of the heart in operation, which is done for the building up of one's neighbor. And the sense is: Why do I either judge myself strictly before men, or show what I seek in my work, if I profit my neighbors neither by judging evil things nor by showing good ones?

VERS. 15.-- « Etiamsi occiderit me, in ipso sperabo, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] He is truly patient who both [0793A] is worn down by adversities, and yet is not bent from the uprightness of hope.

« Verumtamen vias meas. » For thus is the Lord the Saviour then found, if now, for the fear of the Lord, your sin is reproved by your own selves.

VERS. 16.-- « Non enim veniet in conspectu ejus omnis hypocrita. » Now indeed he shall come before the tribunal of the judge, etc., *as far as* he refuses to come into the sight of the Lord.

VERS. 17.-- « Audite sermonem meum. » By this, that he names riddles, he shows that he has figured speech, whence in the voice of the faithful people he adds:

VERS. 18.-- « Si fuero judicatus, scio quod justus, » which does not disagree from his person, etc., *as far as* he is not ignorant that he has been made just out of a gift.

[0793B]VERS. 19.-- « Quis est qui judicetur mecum. » Because in outward acts he has nothing for which he may be reproved, he freely seeks an accuser. But because the hearts even of the just reprove themselves for foolish thought, it is added:

« Quare tacens consumor? » As if he should say: As I have lived so as to fear no accuser without, would that I had so lived as not to fear an accusing conscience within me. For he is consumed keeping silence who finds within himself that whereby he is burned.

VERS. 20.-- « Duo tantum ne facias. » And the face of God he here calls his animadversion: in which, while he beholds sins, he punishes them; from which no one is hidden, unless the two things he asks be removed, concerning which there follows:

VERS. 21.-- « Manum tuam longe fac a me. » By [0793C] the hand, which he asks to be put far off, etc., *as far as* but he serves through dread.

VERS. 22.-- « Voca. » [n: (GREG.)] He seeks the presence of Christ, that through it he may both hear what he does not know, and be heard in the things which he knows. Or, God calls by choosing, man answers by obeying; man speaks by desiring, God answers by appearing. But panting thus, he examines himself more subtly.

« Voca me et ego respondebo tibi. » To call is God's part, to look upon us by loving and choosing. To answer is ours, to obey his love with good works.

Aut certe loquar. » We speak when we ask for his face by desire: he answers those who speak when he appears to us who love him.

VERS. 23. « Quantas habeo iniquitates, » etc. It is the labor [0793D] of the just, that they may find their own selves, and, finding them, may lead them on to better things by weeping and correcting. Iniquity seems graver than sin, wickedness than offense.

VERS. 24.-- « Cur faciem tuam abscondis? » [n: (Ibid.)] Man had the contemplation of God in paradise, but through fault he ran into darkness. And, roused out of punishment, he returns to love, and says:

« Et arbitraris. » Because if thou didst look upon me as a friend, thou wouldst not deprive me of the light of thy vision.

VERS. 25.-- « Contra folium, » etc. Man is called a leaf, etc., *as far as* whom thou knowest to be thus infirm?

VERS. 26.-- « Scribis. » Because what we speak passes away, [0794A] what we write remains. God is said not to speak but to write bitternesses, when his scourges long endure upon us. For once he fixed the sentence concerning the mortality of the flesh, which shall not be able to be changed until the last.

« Et consumere. » It is plain that the sins of the young and of the old are grave, if the just so fear that which they committed in a weak age.

VERS. 27.-- « Posuisti in nervo pedem meum. » God has set the foot of man in the fetter, because he has bound his depravity by the strong sentence of his strictness.

« Et observasti omnes semitas meas. » A path is narrower than a way; but since we take ways to be actions, we rightly take paths to be the thoughts of actions.

« Et vestigia pedum meorum considerasti. » He called the intentions [0794B] of works footprints; or, depraved works, which, while we do them, we set forth as a bad example to the brethren who behold this, and, as with a foot bent outside the way, we leave crooked footprints to those who follow.

VERS. 28. « Qui quasi putredo. » As a garment is eaten by the moth sprung from itself, so man has in himself the rottenness whereby he is consumed. Or he is tempted out of the corruption of his own flesh, as a garment is consumed by the moth sprung from it.

« Quantas habeo iniquitates. » [n: (Ibid.)] As it were, man through the blindness of his exile does not know himself, which befalls him because he hides himself from the face of God, for which, already admonished by the punishment itself, he grieves. « Contra folium. » Man was a tree in his creation, but dried up in temptation a leaf, in his casting down stubble.

[0794C] « Consumere me vis, » because he finds that he sinned in his youth, etc., *as far as* as though they had then been without discretion.
