## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- « Behold all things. » [n: (GREG.)] For all these things to the blessed man, etc., *as far as* he saw some things to be in works, others in words; there follows:

« These things my eye hath seen, and my ear hath heard. » But because words have no profit if they [0791D] lack understanding, there follows:

« And I have understood every one. » For when something is shown, or heard, if understanding is not granted, it is not prophecy at all; and because he is not lifted up over that understanding, he shows it when he says:

VERS. 2.-- « According to true knowledge. » [var: Vulg. Job 13:2 *vestram*, "according to your knowledge"] By these words he made known of how great humility he was, who denies that he is inferior to them whose life he had far transcended by living holily: and he affirms that he knows according to their knowledge, he who by knowing heavenly things passed beyond their earthly thoughts through the spirit of prophecy.

VERS. 3.-- « But yet I will speak to the Almighty. » [n: (Ibid.)] We speak with the Almighty while we beseech his mercy [0792A]. We dispute with him while, joining ourselves to his justice, we discuss our own deeds with subtle searching. Or he disputes with God who, obeying him here, will afterward judge the peoples with him, who even in the meantime does not cease to judge concerning the wicked.

VERS. 4.-- « First I will show you, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] So the Church says of heretics, « first I will show you to be framers; » for as a building is framed out of stones, so also a lie is framed out of words.

VERS. 5.-- « And would that you kept silence. » [n: (Ibid.)] Seeing that they wish to appear, etc., *as far as* by which they may seduce weak minds.

VERS. 6.-- « Hear therefore my correction. » [n: (Ibid.)] Rightly therefore he brought in correction first and judgment after, because unless the swelling of the fool be first pressed down by correction, [0792B] the judgment of the just man is by no means known through understanding.

VERS. 9.-- « Shall he be deceived as a man by your deceits. » Heretics practise fraud upon God, who build up things that do not please him for whom they speak, and offend him while they strive to defend him.

VERS. 10.-- « He himself shall reprove you, because. » There are those who feel the truth of God in the heart, etc., *as far as* by how much in the secret of thought they know the right judgments of God.

VERS. 11.-- « Straightway. » [n: (Ibid.)] By a human word the very strictness of his rectitude is called the motion of God: which shall trouble by vengeance the reprobate, who here refuse to be troubled by penitence.

« And his terror shall rush in. » Because for the reprobate fear does not beget [0792C] rest, but punishment begets fear, whence the prophet: « Vexation alone shall give understanding to the hearing [n: (Isa. XXVIII)]: » because when they are punished for the commandments of God despised, then they understand what they have heard.

VERS. 12.-- « Your memory. » However much a man for the sake of memory, etc., *as far as* and the elated flesh wastes away in rottenness.

VERS. 13.-- « Be silent a little while, that I may speak. » He shows that they have spoken with the sense of the flesh: whom he therefore restrains to silence, that he may say the things which his mind shall have suggested to him. As if he should say: I speak not carnally, but spiritually, because through the sense of the spirit I hear what I utter through the ministry of the body.

VERS. 14.-- « Why do I tear my flesh with my teeth. » Teeth in this place signify the inward [0792D] senses, etc., *as far as* if I cannot profit those who look upon me, that is, you.

« With my teeth. » The teeth are the inward senses, which search out particulars, etc., *as far as* lest it be able to chew spiritual things.

« And I carry my soul in my hands. » [n: (Ibid.)] To carry the soul in the hands is to show the intention of the heart in operation, which is done for the building up of one's neighbor. And the sense is: Why do I either judge myself strictly before men, or show what I seek in my work, if I profit my neighbors neither by judging evil things nor by showing good ones?

VERS. 15.-- « Although he should kill me, I will hope in him, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] He is truly patient who both [0793A] is worn down by adversities, and yet is not bent from the uprightness of hope.

« Yet my ways. » For thus is the Lord the Saviour then found, if now, for the fear of the Lord, your sin is reproved by your own selves.

VERS. 16.-- « For no hypocrite shall come into his sight. » Now indeed he shall come before the tribunal of the judge, etc., *as far as* he refuses to come into the sight of the Lord.

VERS. 17.-- « Hear my speech. » By this, that he names riddles, he shows that he has figured speech, whence in the voice of the faithful people he adds:

VERS. 18.-- « If I shall be judged, I know that I am just, » which does not disagree from his person, etc., *as far as* he is not ignorant that he has been made just out of a gift.

[0793B]VERS. 19.-- « Who is he that would be judged with me. » Because in outward acts he has nothing for which he may be reproved, he freely seeks an accuser. But because the hearts even of the just reprove themselves for foolish thought, it is added:

« Why am I consumed keeping silence? » As if he should say: As I have lived so as to fear no accuser without, would that I had so lived as not to fear an accusing conscience within me. For he is consumed keeping silence who finds within himself that whereby he is burned.

VERS. 20.-- « Only do thou not two things. » And the face of God he here calls his animadversion: in which, while he beholds sins, he punishes them; from which no one is hidden, unless the two things he asks be removed, concerning which there follows:

VERS. 21.-- « Put thy hand far from me. » By [0793C] the hand, which he asks to be put far off, etc., *as far as* but he serves through dread.

VERS. 22.-- « Call. » [n: (GREG.)] He seeks the presence of Christ, that through it he may both hear what he does not know, and be heard in the things which he knows. Or, God calls by choosing, man answers by obeying; man speaks by desiring, God answers by appearing. But panting thus, he examines himself more subtly.

« Call me and I will answer thee. » To call is God's part, to look upon us by loving and choosing. To answer is ours, to obey his love with good works.

Or else I will speak. » We speak when we ask for his face by desire: he answers those who speak when he appears to us who love him.

VERS. 23. « How many are my iniquities, » etc. It is the labor [0793D] of the just, that they may find their own selves, and, finding them, may lead them on to better things by weeping and correcting. Iniquity seems graver than sin, wickedness than offense.

VERS. 24.-- « Why hidest thou thy face? » [n: (Ibid.)] Man had the contemplation of God in paradise, but through fault he ran into darkness. And, roused out of punishment, he returns to love, and says:

« And thou thinkest. » Because if thou didst look upon me as a friend, thou wouldst not deprive me of the light of thy vision.

VERS. 25.-- « Against a leaf, » etc. Man is called a leaf, etc., *as far as* whom thou knowest to be thus infirm?

VERS. 26.-- « Thou writest. » Because what we speak passes away, [0794A] what we write remains. God is said not to speak but to write bitternesses, when his scourges long endure upon us. For once he fixed the sentence concerning the mortality of the flesh, which shall not be able to be changed until the last.

« And to consume. » It is plain that the sins of the young and of the old are grave, if the just so fear that which they committed in a weak age.

VERS. 27.-- « Thou hast put my foot in the fetter. » God has set the foot of man in the fetter, because he has bound his depravity by the strong sentence of his strictness.

« And thou hast observed all my paths. » A path is narrower than a way; but since we take ways to be actions, we rightly take paths to be the thoughts of actions.

« And thou hast considered the steps of my feet. » He called the intentions [0794B] of works footprints; or, depraved works, which, while we do them, we set forth as a bad example to the brethren who behold this, and, as with a foot bent outside the way, we leave crooked footprints to those who follow.

VERS. 28. « Who as rottenness. » As a garment is eaten by the moth sprung from itself, so man has in himself the rottenness whereby he is consumed. Or he is tempted out of the corruption of his own flesh, as a garment is consumed by the moth sprung from it.

« How many are my iniquities. » [n: (Ibid.)] As it were, man through the blindness of his exile does not know himself, which befalls him because he hides himself from the face of God, for which, already admonished by the punishment itself, he grieves. « Against a leaf. » Man was a tree in his creation, but dried up in temptation a leaf, in his casting down stubble.

[0794C] « Thou wishest to consume me, » because he finds that he sinned in his youth, etc., *as far as* as though they had then been without discretion.
