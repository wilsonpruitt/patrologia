## CHAPTER XIV.

VERS. 1.-- « Homo natus de muliere. » [n: (GREG.)] Here by the name of woman infirmity is designated, and the sense is: What has he of strength in himself, who is born of infirmity, who is both straitened unto life, and enlarged unto misery.

VERS. 2.-- « Qui quasi flos egreditur. » Because he shines in the flesh:

« Conteritur, » because he is brought back into dust and rottenness.

« Et fugit velut umbra. » He compared man not to one merely fleeing, but to a shadow. For he who has not the warmth of the heart in the love of God, [0794D] and yet does not hold the life which he loves, flees like a shadow.

« Et nunquam in eodem statu, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] For he always fails from that very point whence he believes himself to advance into a span of life.

VERS. 3.-- « Et dignum ducis super hujuscemodi, » etc. For God to open his eyes is to exercise judgments, and to see whom he strikes

VERS. 4.-- « Quis potest facere, » etc. For he who alone is clean of himself, etc., *as far as* he says that Christ alone was not born of unclean seed.

VERS. 5.-- « Breves dies hominis, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)], because with God even the things that slip away stand fast: he declares that the number of the months is with him. Or, in days the brevity of time, in months [0795A] the multiplied spaces of days are designated: to man therefore the days are brief; but because our life is stretched out afterward, the number of our months is recorded to be with God.

« Constituisti terminos ejus. » [n: (GREG. in Job. l. XII.)] For none of the things that come to pass for men in this world come to pass without the hidden judgment of God.

VERS. 6.-- « Recede paululum ab eo. » Withdraw, that is, now take away the scourges of the present life, and show the good things of eternal rest

« Donec optata veniat, » when he receives eternal rest as the recompense of his labor

VERS. 7.-- « Lignum habet spem. » etc. He hints that the human race is as it were despised, and how far insensible things themselves seem to go before it. Or according to [0795B] the mystical sense: the tree is preferred to man, for here every carnal man is the man; the tree is taken as the just man, who, when in death he is afflicted with suffering, is recovered in the greenness of eternal life.

« Et rami ejus pullulant, » because out of the suffering of the just man the faithful receive the greenness of spiritual life.

VERS. 8.-- « Si senuerit in terra radix. » [n: (Ibid.)] The root of the just man is holy preaching, because from it he arises and in it he subsists: which among the hearts of earthly men is believed to be spent of all its strength: « Et in pulvere truncus emoritur, » because among the hands of persecutors his body is deprived of life.

VERS. 9.-- « Ad odorem aquae germinabit, » etc. Because through the breathing of the Spirit, in the hearts of the elect, by the example of his own work, he makes the seed of virtue.

[0795C] « Et faciet comam, » etc. For a cut trunk to make foliage, etc., *as far as* he made foliage, because the apostles who had withered grew green unto the faith as before.

VERS. 10.-- « Homo vero cum mortuus fuerit. » [n: (Ibid.)] But by that very loss of justice man dies, he is stripped of the garment of innocence granted in paradise, he is consumed, the destruction of the flesh following: « ubi ergo est, » he who is not in his love, where « verum esse » is?

VERS. 11.-- « Quomodo si recedant aquae de mari. » The sea is the mind of man, etc., *as far as* we are now filled with the sweetness of prosperity.

VERS. 12.-- « Donec atteratur coelum non evigilabit. » Because, unless the end of this world shall have come, the human race shall not awake from sleep.

VERS. 13.-- « Quis mihi hoc. » Before the coming [0795D] of the Mediator, knowing that he was to descend to hell, he asks there for the protection of his Creator.

« Constituas mihi tempus in quo recorderis mei. » He asks for the coming of the Redeemer to the lower regions.

VERS. 14.-- « Putasne mortuus homo rursum. » Just men are wont, etc., *as far as* that afterward he may strengthen the more.

« Cunctis diebus. » He therefore who awaits his own change with so great desire, shows how certain he is concerning the resurrection.

VERS. 15.-- « Vocabis me et ego respondebo. » Then do we truly answer God who calls, when at the command of the highest incorruption we shall rise incorruptible.

[0796A] « Operi manuum tuarum porriges dexteram, » as if he should say: For this reason shall the corruptible creature be able to persist unto incorruption, because it shall be raised by the hand of thy power; or by the right hand the Son is meant, who was sent incarnate to raise up man.

VERS. 16.-- « Tu quidem gressus, » etc. He numbers the steps, and spares the sins: he who subtly considers our deeds, and yet here mercifully relaxes them to the penitent.

« Dinumerasti, sed parce. » He numbers, while he turns us to weep for the single things we have done; he spares, because he does not punish the things we have punished, whence he still adds:

VERS. 17.-- « Signasti quasi in sacculo, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] In secret, etc., *as far as* he heals because here through scourges he utterly relaxes.

[0796B]VERS. 18.-- « Mons cadens defluit. » [n: (Ibid.)] There are two kinds of temptations, etc., *as far as* or long and slow, as thou permittest Solomon to be consumed.

« Saxum, » that is, the mind of the just man is carried,

« De loco, » that is, from justice to fault by too great an impulse.

VERS. 19.-- « Lapides, » that is, strength, « aquae » constant blandishments.

« Terra, » which sanctifies.

VERS. 20.-- « Roborasti eum paululum, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] Man has been strengthened a little, because here he has received the powers of living for a short time; but in this brevity, wherein he has been strengthened, he gathers whence he may find in perpetuity; either that he may always rejoice, or that he may not escape torments.

[0796C] « Immutabis faciem ejus, et emittes. » The face of man, etc., *as far as* unwilling, he is compelled to pass over to things eternal.

VERS. 21.-- « Sive nobiles fuerint filii. » For just as those who are still living are ignorant in what place the souls of the dead are held: so the dead, carnally, do not know how the life of those set in the flesh is ordered after them.

VERS. 22.-- « Attamen caro ejus dum vivit dolebit. » For even those who live spiritually here suffer the tribulation of the flesh.

« Et anima illius super. » For it is fitting that a man should always find sorrows in himself, who, having left the Creator, sought joy in himself.
