## CHAPTER XV.

[0796D]VERS. 2.-- « Shall a wise man answer? » [n: (GREG.)] All things that are perceived in the saints displease the wicked as though uttered into the wind, and they reckon the words of the good to be not sayings of reason but goads of fury.

« And he will fill with burning. » [n: (Ibid.)] They who also strive to cloak, under a defence of the Lord, the things which they perceive themselves to be saying contumeliously; whence he adds:

VERS. 3.-- « Thou reprovest him with words. » [n: (Ibid.)] And they think that no one has the fear of the Lord save him whom they have drawn to the folly of their own profession; whence he adds:

VERS. 4.-- « As much as is in thee, » etc., it is said, that is, thou hast taken away. As if he were saying: Presuming upon thy own justice, thou disdainest to entreat the grace of thy Creator. When [0797A] heretics find no true evils against the good, they invent things to reprove, that they may seem just.

VERS. 5.-- « For iniquity hath taught. » [n: (GREG.)] Iniquity teaches the mouth when out of an evil life there is conceived something worse to be said.

« Thou imitatest the tongue of blasphemers » [n: (Ibid.)] Heretics, as though venerating God, deny the lowly things, that is the flesh and the death, and say that the Church, confessing these, blasphemes: and if any adversity befall her, they say it comes to pass for this injury; whence he says:

VERS. 6.-- « It shall condemn thee, » etc. But sometimes they wish to crush as though by reasoning; whence:

VERS. 7.-- « Art thou the first man. » But this defence of God is taken up by them for this reason, etc., *as far as* weigh that thou art ignorant of the counsel.

[0797B]VERS. 9.-- « What knowest thou. » [n: (Ibid.)] These words openly show out of what elation of mind proceeds whatever sounds as though for the Lord's defence.

VERS. 10.-- « Both the aged and the ancient. » Heretics, because they cannot establish their own doctrines by reason, strive to defend them among the foolish by the authority of the ancients: and they say that the doctors of the Church were their masters.

VERS. 11, 12.-- « Is it a great thing? » There follows: « What doth thy heart lift thee up. » Often the mind of the just is suspended to the contemplating of higher things, so that outwardly their face seems to have been struck with amazement. But the reprobate think that this force of contemplation comes about through hypocrisy; for what is in themselves they suppose to be in others.

VERS. 13.-- « Why doth thy spirit swell against God? » [0797C] For the most part the just, afflicted by certain necessities, are compelled to confess their own works, which blessed Job had done, and which is thought to be elation; but in the ears of God those things are not proud which are brought forth with a humble heart; and as it is a grave fault to arrogate to oneself what one is not, so for the most part there is no fault if one humbly says the good that is.

« Sermons of this kind. » [n: (Ibid.)] Of thy good things; yet Job did not in these swell against God, but humbly said the things he had truly done; often the just and the unjust have the same words, and whence those please, these give offence, as the Pharisee who justifies himself in work [n: (Luc. XVIII)], and Ezechias who justifies himself in thought [n: (IV Reg. XX)].

VERS. 14.-- « What is man? » By this very thing, that he is called *homo*, one earthly and weak is expressed. For he has his name from *humus*, the ground.

[0797D] « And that one born should appear just, » etc. For how should he appear just who is born of her who stood forth as the pourer-out of injustice? For the woman first poured out injustice to the man in paradise.

VERS. 15.-- « And the heavens are not clean. » By this name of the heavens he repeated what he had signified by the appellation of the saints, who before the strict judgment of God cannot themselves be clean unto perfection.

VERS. 16.-- « How much more abominable. » [n: (Ibid.)] Any perverse man is abominable because of the uncleanness of his stain.

« Unprofitable, » because of the justice of a perfect life; who is said « to drink iniquity, » because what is drunk has no delay in the swallowing. Fault therefore, because it is [0798A] perpetrated by an unjust man without any drawing back, iniquity is drunk as it were water.

VERS. 17.-- « I will show thee. » Because he teaches with a lesser authority who speaks the things he has heard than he who speaks the things he had seen, in order that Eliphaz might arrogate to himself a stronger authority, he says:

« What I have seen I will tell thee. » It is proper to the arrogant, etc., *as far as* which he fortifies by the authority of the Fathers.

VERS. 18.-- « The wise confess. » Behold the audacity of heretics, etc., *as far as* that a stranger, that is the devil, has passed through their hearts. Behold the sentences.

VERS. 20.-- « All the days. » Even the elect are wont on certain days to be proud, but before they end their life they change their hearts from elation; the impious man, however, is proud on all days, and so ends his life [0798B] that he in no wise withdraws from elation.

« And the number of the years of his tyranny. » Properly a tyrant, etc., *as far as* whose life is held under uncertainty?

VERS. 21.-- « The sound of terror always. [n: (Ibid.)] A depraved mind is always in labours, as a good mind is always tranquil: because it either contrives evils which it may bring in, or fears lest these be brought in upon it by others.

VERS. 22.-- « He believeth not that he can return from darkness unto: » because while he believes himself struck on every side by snares, with health despaired of he always grows toward wickedness.

VERS. 23.-- « When he shall move himself, » etc. Bread here is the wage of the present life, etc., *as far as* avarice despises those victuals.

[0798C]VERS. 24.-- « He shall terrify, » etc. In everything that the wicked man does, etc., *as far as* if perchance that should fail him which he may set against it out of deceit.

VERS. 25.-- « He hath stretched out, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] To stretch out hands against God, etc., *as far as* is now done by many one by one.

VERS. 26.-- « He hath run, » that is, in an evil work he had no obstacle from adversity.

« And he is armed with a fat neck. » A fat neck is opulent pride propped up by abounding things as though by much flesh.

VERS. 27.-- « It hath covered his face. » Fatness covers the face, because the desired abundance of earthly things presses down the eyes of the mind.

« And from his sides. » [n: (Ibid.)] The sides of the rich [0798D] are those who cling to them; the fat hangs down from his sides, because whosoever clings to a powerful and wicked man, he too swells from that man's power as though from fatness.

VERS. 28.--He hath dwelt in cities. » Because a city is so named from the converse of those who live together, etc., *as far as* they show beyond doubt that they have fallen from the building of life.

VERS. 29.-- « He shall not be enriched, » because the thought of the proud man shall not be inhabited by the grace of its own Author.

« Nor shall he continue. » [n: (Ibid.)] As if he should say: This which he seems to have passes away outwardly, and that which could not pass away he has not inwardly.

« Nor shall he send into the earth, » because, as at a light [0799A] breath, so at the motion of the hidden sentence his life is plucked up; or, « into the earth he shall not send, » etc., because he never plants the thought of his heart unto the desire of eternal life.

VERS. 30.-- « He shall not depart from darkness. » For because he does not seek the light of justice, he does not depart from darkness.

« His branches, » etc. Because they too who are joined to him burn with earthly desires, so that they bear not the fruit of good work, since they pant through wickedness after the seeking of the lowest things.

« And he shall be taken away by a breath, » because he shall receive the sentence of striking in that wherein he restrains not the breath of his mouth by the fear of God.

VERS. 31.-- « He shall not believe. » Because alms [0799B] to redeem him, etc., *as far as* because he redeems not the soul which he restrains not from vices.

VERS. 32.-- « Before his days. » [n: (GREG.)] Because those long in mind, etc., *as far as* death meets the undisciplined.

VERS. 33.-- « His hands » are dried up from every good working.

« He shall be hurt as, » etc. If a flourishing vine, etc., *as far as* makes them strangers from fruit in holy zeal; whence he adds:

« And as an olive casting off. » The olive, when it is in flower, etc., *as far as* so that they cannot discern with what intention they do anything.

Or « in the first flower. » A weak man ought not to rule or to do lower things, because in earthly acts he grows cold. [0799C] Or, when he sees that the things he does give pleasure, he works those same good things more ardently, and as it were glows in holy zeal, whence he perishes by the heat.

VERS. 34.-- « For the congregation of the hypocrite, » etc. Hypocrites gather good works, but that gathering of theirs is barren, because through what they do they do not seek to receive fruit in the eternal retribution.

« And fire shall devour their tabernacles, » etc. He calls tabernacles the bodies in which their souls dwell: which here the desire of praise, but afterwards the fire of gehenna, shall burn up.

« Tabernacles. » Thought is a tabernacle in which the mind dwells, which the heat of avarice lays waste. Or, [0799D] he calls the heart a tabernacle, which the burning of praise consumes. Or, the body is the tabernacle of the soul, and they who here burn in mind shall burn in flesh with the fires of gehenna.

VERS. 35.-- « He hath conceived sorrow. » When he thinks perverse things, he is in travail with iniquity; when he has begun to accomplish it, he has conceived. The mind also of the hypocrite is never free from evil, because it envies others the things which it desires, from whose despising it may be the more renowned; whence also it prepares deceits, that it alone may take the esteem.

« His womb prepareth deceits, » that is, the mind in which thoughts are generated conceives the greater malice against neighbours the more it seeks alone before all to be seen more innocent: this he says, because he thought Job stricken for hypocrisy.
