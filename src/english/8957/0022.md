## CHAPTER XVII.

VERS. 1.-- « My spirit shall be made thin. » [n: (GREG.)] The spirit of the elect is made thin by fear of the judge, and if they find in themselves any carnal thoughts, they consume them by the burning of penance. But the spirit of the wicked grows thick through the swelling of elation. « And only the grave [0803B] remaineth for me. » He considers himself almost dead, because he in no wise is ignorant that he is to die whence there follows:

VERS. 2.-- « I have not sinned. » He did not sin so far as to deserve these scourges, because he was struck not for the correcting of a fault but for the increasing of grace. Or, the Head says this, who without fault took upon himself the penalty of fault, that is, the bitterness of the passion.

VERS. 3.-- « Set me beside thee. » Through the ascension; afterwards, because Judaea was stirred against the disciples, he adds: « The hand of whomsoever. » Because now they act in vain against him who sits in heaven.

VERS. 4.-- « Their heart far off. » For if they knew the keeping of discipline, they would in no wise despise the precepts of the Redeemer. « Therefore not, » etc. For they who study not [0803C] to keep their life by discipline lie always in the depths through their desires, nor do they raise their mind to the hope of the heavenly country.

VERS. 5.-- « He promiseth a prey, » etc. He turns the sentence to the head of the wicked, namely the devil, whose « companions » are the apostate angels, who fell with him. « Sons » are perverse men, who out of his depraved persuasion are begotten in the working of malice. « He promiseth therefore a prey to his companions, » because he promises to the malignant spirits the souls of the depraved, to be seized at the end.

« The eyes of his sons shall fail. » [n: (Ibid.)] Because the intentions of men, etc., *as far as* what they may grieve over, they suffer without end.

VERS. 6.-- « He hath set me. » Everyone who is struck by a scourge, etc., *as far as* and he wishes for his [0803D] adversary that same penalty which he beholds to have befallen a just man.

VERS. 7.-- « It hath grown dim from indignation. » The eye grows dim from indignation, when those very men who in the Church have the light of truth behold the perverse prevail against the innocent: because they are troubled by wonder at the hidden judgment. But when the just grow dim unto indignation, the weak for the most part fall away even to unbelief, whence there follows: « And my members as it were into nothing. » By the name of members the tenderness of the weak is expressed, who, while they see the perverse flourish and the just tormented, repent of having begun good things, and so withdraw the sooner to the doing of evils, as though the good which they had begun had harmed their life.

VERS. 8.-- « The just shall be astonished at this. » This is [0804A] what he had said, « my eye hath grown dim toward indignation. » [var: Vulg. Job 17:7 *ab indignatione*, "from indignation"] « And the innocent shall be raised up against the hypocrite. » The perfect innocent man is raised up against the hypocrite, when he despises him together with his flower, and by preaching the things that are right declares that he is to be despised by the rest.

VERS. 9.-- « And the just shall hold his way. » Because, the hypocrite having been considered, while he beholds him obtain out of a depraved will the things that are of the world, he himself is bound the more strongly to the love of heavenly things. Knowing that eternal rewards shall not be wanting to good desires: since temporal goods are not denied even to depraved and double hearts. « And he that hath clean hands shall add strength. » Because seeing the earthly glory of the perverse, etc., *as far as* which cannot be [0804B] common to him with the reprobate.

VERS. 10.-- « Therefore all you. » After he has brought in the outward advancements of the evil, but the inward ones of the good, he exhorts the elect that they be converted, by forsaking evils, and come, by doing good things.

VERS. 11.-- « And not in you. » Desiring their conversion he longs that he may find no wise man among them, as if he should say: Learn to be fools among yourselves, that you may be able to be truly wise in God.

« My days have passed. » « Days, » prosperities: « Thoughts tormenting the heart, » he calls the care of earthly happiness: which, the more heavily they press the elect, the more subtly does the judge think that accounts of them are to be exacted these thoughts are scattered together with that same earthly happiness.

[0804C]VERS. 12.-- « They have turned night into day. » Scattered thoughts turn « night into day, » because to the just it is sometimes more pleasing to suffer evils out of adversity than to be wearied out of prosperity by the care of an earthly stewardship.

« And again after darkness. » [n: (Ibid.)] Light is hoped for after darkness, etc., *as far as* and would place the souls of the just in a perpetual seat already.

VERS. 13.-- « If I wait. » The former saints were able to endure adversities, etc., *as far as* to have prepared for themselves a rest in hell.

VERS. 14.-- « To rottenness I have said. » He calls rottenness his father, etc., *as far as* that we may be wearied in mind by restless thoughts as it were by certain worms.

[0804D]VERS. 15.-- « Where is then now my expectation. » The expectation of the just is Christ; whom they knew would come at some time, but they sought that he should come sooner: whence here is added, now: « And who considereth my patience? » God considers the patience of the just; but he is said as it were not to consider, because he appears less quickly than the prayer of desire would have, and defers the grace of his dispensation unto longer times.

VERS. 16.-- « Into the deepest hell shall all my things descend. » Before the coming of the mediator Job was to descend into hell, etc., *as far as* the retribution was still awaited to be received in the rest of hell alone. « Thinkest thou that at least for himself there will be rest for me? » [var: Vulg. Job 17:16 *ibi*, "there"] It is to be weighed which of us is secure [0805A] concerning rest: if even he trembles about it, whose praises of virtue the very judge who strikes him proclaims.
