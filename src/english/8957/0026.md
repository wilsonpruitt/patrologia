## CHAPTER XX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Therefore my thoughts. » [n: (GREG. in Job., l. XV.)] As if he should say: because I consider the terror of the last judgment, therefore I am confounded in fear by the tumults of my thoughts. [0811C] « And my mind is hurried into diverse things. » When now he weighs with anxious dread the evils which he neglected to do, now the blameworthy things in which he stands, now the right things which he sees to be still wanting to him. ⟦s2⟧ These men, out of Job's company, knew indeed how to live rightly, but not knowing how to weigh God's judgments subtly, they believe that the just cannot be stricken here. ⟦s3⟧ And therefore they suspected him to be wicked, and slip down into rebuke: to which nevertheless they descend under a certain reverence. ⟦s4⟧ Whence this man adds.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- « The doctrine wherewith, etc. » [n: (Ibid.)] These things having been moderately set down first, etc., *as far as* and this I find, that « the praise of the wicked, etc. »

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- « This I know from the beginning. » For if we lead the eyes of the mind from the very rising of the human race as far as this present time, [0811D] we see how brief was everything that could be brought to an end.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- « And the joy of the hypocrite. » The joy of the hypocrite is after the likeness of a point. ⟦s2⟧ For as the stylus, in making a point, is lifted while it is set down, so the hypocrite, while he touches the praise of the present life, loses it.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « After the likeness of a point, » because the gladness passes away, the punishment abides: it appears for a moment, it disappears for ever.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- « If he shall have gone up, » that is, even though he feigns that he leads a heavenly life, even though he makes a show of his understanding as like to the true preachers, yet like a dunghill he shall be destroyed in the end, because his mind shall be damned for the stench of its own vices.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0812A]VERS. 7.-- « And they that had seen him shall say. » [n: (Ibid.)] They who saw this man lifted up will say, when he is extinguished, Where is he? because he appears neither in this world where he had been lifted up, nor in the rest of eternity, of which he was thought to be.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- « As a dream that flieth away. » The life of the hypocrite, etc., *as far as* because sleeping he was seeing the favours of men.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- « The eye that had seen him. » The place of the hypocrite is the heart of flatterers: for there he rests, where he finds favours. ⟦s2⟧ The eye therefore that had seen him shall not see him, because in death he is hidden from his lovers, who had been wont to look upon him with admiration.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Neither shall he behold him any more. » Because the tongues of flatterers [0812B] do not follow him unto judgment with their favours.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- « His children shall be worn away, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because those who are born in hypocrisy from the imitation of him, while they do not hold the solidity of truth, fail in the neediness of the heart.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- « His bones shall be filled. » The youth of the hypocrite is the beginning of depravity, etc., *as far as* to be emptied of all solidity and to perish.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- « For when it shall have been sweet. » [n: (Ibid.)] In the mouth of the heart iniquity is sweet, which he covers under the covering of bland speech. ⟦s2⟧ For the evil would be on the tongue, and not under the tongue, if the hypocrite in speaking laid open his malice.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « He shall spare it and not. » He spares the evil which he loves, who does not pursue it by repenting; and he does not forsake it, [0812C] but conceals it in his throat, because he so keeps it in thought that he never brings it forth in the voice.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (BEDA.)] That is, he strokes, cherishes and nourishes his own evil, and delighting himself in it, never repents so as to depart from it.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- « His bread in his belly. » For the most part the hypocrite is eager to be instructed by the bread of sacred eloquence, not that he may live by it, but that he may appear learned; but while he understands crookedly the word of doctrine which he seeks ill, he slips also into heresies: it befalls him that, as of the gall of asps, so he dies of the bread. ⟦s2⟧ But because he in no wise keeps God's words in work, even if he understands them rightly, he loses them in the present life, so that he does not know what he wished to do.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- « Riches. » Those of the sacred law, which he devoured by reading, [0812D] he shall vomit up by forgetting: which God by a just judgment plucks out of his memory.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The head of asps. » [n: (Ibid.)] The asp is a small serpent, etc., *as far as* as the viper kills by its tongue.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- « Let him not see the streams. » [n: (Ibid.)] The streams of the river are the gifts of the Holy Spirit, etc., *as far as* it signifies the mystery of Christ's incarnation.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- « He shall pay for all the things that he did. » For he shall pay out in torment those unlawful desires which he kept here, nor shall he be consumed in death; but that he may be tormented without end, he is compelled to live without end, so that he whose life was here dead in fault, there his death may live in punishment. ⟦s2⟧ Let him say therefore, « he shall pay for all the things that he did, » and yet he shall not be consumed, because he is tormented and not extinguished: he dies [0813A] and lives, he fails and subsists always, and is without end.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- « Because breaking in pieces. » He breaks and strips bare the house of the poor man, because him whom he crushes by power he does not blush to despoil by avarice

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- « Neither was his belly satisfied. » For the belly of the wicked man is avarice, and in it is gathered whatever is swallowed down by perverse desire. ⟦s2⟧ But it is clear that avarice is not extinguished by the things desired, but increased; for after the manner of fire, when it has received logs to consume, it grows, and whence the flame seems for a moment to be pressed down, thence a little after it is seen to spread abroad.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And when he shall have had what he had coveted. » It is of greater [0813B] wrath when that is granted which is ill desired, because from thence a sudden vengeance follows, because he obtained this also which he coveted while God was angry.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- « There remained nothing of his city. » [var: Vulg. *cibo*, "food," where Migne prints *civitate*; the gloss below reads "food"] When the hypocrite is stricken, nothing remains of his food, because when he is led to punishments he is estranged from all the things he had possessed.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Therefore nothing, etc. » Because, withdrawn from this life, he goes naked to the judge.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- « When he shall be filled, he shall be straitened. » [n: (GREG.)] When he has heaped up much he is straitened; for he is made anxious how he may guard what he has acquired.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « He shall burn, and every sorrow. » On this side and that he fears those who lie in wait, etc., *as far as* by so much the more easily is he hurried away to [0813C] torment. ⟦s2⟧ There follows:

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- « Would that his belly may be filled, etc., let him rain down war upon him. » For God to rain down war, etc., *as far as* he scorned to foresee.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- « He shall flee from weapons of iron. » [n: (Ibid.)] The covetous man sometimes filches what belongs to another, etc., *as far as* he sets himself before the strokes of the eternal judgment. ⟦s2⟧ But meanwhile,

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- « The sword drawn out, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ The wicked man, while he contrives crooked things in thought, is as a sword in the sheath. ⟦s3⟧ It goes out of the sheath when it is laid open by the iniquity of an evil work; drawn out, however, by the seducer, but going out by his own will,

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « They shall go and come, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ Malignant spirits are to good minds, etc., *as far as* elation over continence [0813D] is put in its place, etc.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- « All darkness is hidden. » For he who does not fulfil all things at once in effect holds silently in his mind all the things that may hurt.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « It shall devour him, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ The fire of Gehenna, which is not kindled by human effort, but was created by God from the origin of the world.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « He shall be afflicted, being left. » The tabernacle of the wicked man is the flesh, because he dwells in it gladly, and with it his spirit shall be tormented. ⟦s2⟧ The good do not place their joy in the flesh, but in heaven. ⟦s3⟧ Left by the flesh from which he is drawn away, which he loved here and would not leave: then, because of the torments, he will wish to leave it, but shall not be able.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0814A]VERS. 27.-- « They shall reveal; » [n: (Ibid.)] because here he spared neither the good nor the bad, in the fearful examination the life of the just and of sinners alike shall accuse him.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- « It shall be laid open. » When every evil that was being born in his conscience is shown forth, at the coming of the judge.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « He shall be dragged away in: » because in the time of vengeance, hurried away to punishments, he is separated from the face of the eternal judge.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- « This is the portion of a wicked man. » For when the justice of God exacts punishment of the reprobate for their perverse acts, it renders them evils even to the retribution of their words.
