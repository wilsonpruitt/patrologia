## CHAPTER XXI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0814B]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- « Is it against. » Blessed Job, if while pleasing God he displeased men, would have no causes of sadness; but because he believed that he had displeased God through the present scourges, being made doubtful about the life he had passed before, he now called his mind to sadness: for if, the scourges being absent, he were disputing against a man about the merits of his own life, he ought in no wise to have been made sad.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Is my disputation against man. » As if: not yet a hypocrite, if I were dealing with men concerning my life, I should not fear: and if I displeased them, I should not care; but because it is with God, I am rightly saddened. ⟦s2⟧ « But do you attend to me, » as I do God, yet I do not fear you.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- « Attend ye, » that is, consider what I have done, and wonder at the things which I suffer.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0814C] « And lay your finger upon your mouth, » that is, add to your speech the virtue of discretion.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- « And I, when I shall have remembered. » [n: (Ibid.)] Because he had not forgotten his own acts, etc., *as far as* he subjoins against his words:

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- « Why then do the wicked live. » They are raised up with honours, they are strengthened with possessions, because the divine patience bears with them.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And comforted with riches, » etc., so that they are allowed to subsist long in them; but because there are those to whom to have these things is a punishment, since they have no heirs to whom they may leave them. ⟦s2⟧ He adds:

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- « Their seed continueth. » For the increase of their felicity, heirs are given along with the patrimony.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0814D] « Before them. » Lest any necessity should withdraw, at least from their eyes, the things in which their mind exults; but lest, these being barren, the stock should be extinguished in them, he adds:

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « A crowd of kinsmen and of grandchildren in their sight. » Behold, they are said to have life, honours, riches, sons, grandsons: but if domestic care burned within, this felicity would not be glad, whence he adds:

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- « Their houses are secure, and peaceable, and there is not. » For what would the aforesaid felicity be, if it were not glad? ⟦s2⟧ If things are prosperous within, in the fields also.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Their ox hath conceived. » For the masters of flocks the first felicity is, etc., *as far as* whence the fruitfulness of the household is subjoined:

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0815A]VERS. 11-- « They go forth like flocks. » That, as greater things have been granted them to have, so many may spring up to be guarded.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And their little ones exult in play. » But lest we should think that play a mean thing, he says:

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- « They hold the timbrel and the harp. » As it were: while the masters swell with honours and possessions, those subject to them rejoice in sportive doings.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- « They spend their days in good things. » Because all the length of the present life is known to be a point, since it is ended by an end. ⟦s2⟧ For whatever could pass away was sudden.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And in a moment they go down to hell. » But the end is worst; behold whither he was tending, because the prosperity of the present life is no witness of innocence, since both they who have used [0815B] it perish, and they who are scourged are set free: which is against Job's friends.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- « Who have said to God: Depart from us. » [n: (GREG.)] They say to God, Depart from us, who refuse to afford him an approach to themselves, and assail him with crooked deeds.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The knowledge of thy ways. » The way of God is peace, humility, patience: and they who despise these say, We will not have the knowledge of thy ways.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- « Who is the Almighty? » etc. ⟦s2⟧ « For the fool hath said in his heart: There is no God. »

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And what doth it profit us if we pray? » The wicked, who seek not God but outward gifts, when they see that these same are lacking to those who serve, themselves [0815C] scorn to serve.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- « But yet, because they are not. » He has his goods in his hand who, by despising things temporal, presses them under the dominion of the mind. ⟦s2⟧ But whosoever loves them too much subjects himself to them rather than them to himself.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Their counsel is far. » The counsel of the wicked is to seek earthly glory, to neglect the eternal. ⟦s2⟧ But because in this life they do not prosper continually, there follows:

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- « How often shall the lamp of the wicked be put out. » Often the wicked man reckons the life of his sons to be his lamp; but when the son who is too much loved is taken away, the lamp of the wicked man, which was seen, is put out; and so it is also with wealth and honours.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ And a flood shall come upon them. » A flood comes upon the wicked [0815D] when they suffer the waves of sorrows from some [sic: *exaliqua*] adversity.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And he shall divide sorrows, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ He who keeps eternal sorrows for the wicked man also pierces his mind here sometimes with a temporal sorrow; for because he strikes both here and there, he divides the sorrows of his fury upon the wicked man, that he may be crushed with a double crushing.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- « They shall be as chaff. » Because by the blast of God's anger they are suddenly lifted up and carried away to the fire:

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And as the ashes which, » because the whirlwind, snatching them, carries them off to eternal punishments.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- « God shall keep for his children. » Because he who imitates the iniquity of a parent is bound also by his offence.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0816A] « And when he shall have repaid. » For the wicked man does not know the evils he has done, save when he has begun to be punished.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- « His eyes shall see. » Because he had so hardened in iniquity that he did not fear to be struck even in the very things he loved, provided that he might fulfil what he had crookedly thought: concerning which hardening it is subjoined:

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- « For what is it to. » Not that in hell he does not think about his kindred, etc., *as far as* lest anyone presume to discuss it, there follows:

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- « Shall any man teach God knowledge? » [var: Vulg. Job 21:22 sets *Deus* in the nominative, "Shall God teach any man knowledge"] that is, he does nothing unjustly with us, who has justly judged the angelic nature also, which is subtler than we; whence nothing remains to complain of.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- « This man dieth. » Behold, he sets down those things in which one may discuss the secret of the Almighty, by asking [0816B] why he permits these things so to be. ⟦s2⟧ The surface of the letter is plain enough of itself. ⟦s3⟧ But as to what he says:

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- « His bowels are full of fat. » For fat comes of abundant food, etc., *as far as* behold, the rich man rejoices emptily, the poor man is afflicted more emptily.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- « And yet they shall sleep together in the dust. » [n: (Ibid.)] What care is there about an unlike life, prosperous or adverse, when the end in the dust is alike? ⟦s2⟧ That life is to be thought upon, where by the resurrection an unlike end is reached, where the happy man passes to punishments, and the afflicted to rest.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And yet together in the dust. » That is, in earthly desires they shall close the eyes of the mind:

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And worms, » which are of the flesh, that is, fleshly cares [0816C] press them: which was not so in Job, who neither swelled over the things he had, nor was anxious over the things he lost. ⟦s2⟧ But they think this, wherefore he subjoins:

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- « Surely I know your thoughts. » For him whom they saw stricken, etc., *as far as* nor do they pass over perfectly to things eternal, whence he adds:

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- « Ask any one of the way. » [var: Vulg. *viatoribus*, "wayfarers," for *via*; the gloss expounds the wayfarer] He is called a wayfarer who considers the present life to be for him a way and not a country, who scorns to fix his heart in love of the passing world, but seeks things eternal.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- « Who shall reprove his ways before him. » While he was speaking of the body of all the wicked, etc., *as far as* neither Elias nor Enoch by their own powers, but by God's.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- « He himself unto the graves. » This in life, etc., *as far as* because he shall be received only in the hearts of the depraved.

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0816D] « And in the heap. » Because in the congregation of sinners he puts forth the snares of his cunning; and since the wicked are many, they are rightly named a heap.

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- « He was sweet. » Cocytus is called the mourning of the sick, etc., *as far as* signifying that there is there an unjust mourning.

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And after him every. » [n: (Ibid.)] Here is understood the man who savours of human things; he draws every man after him, because all whom he finds carnal he snatches under the yoke of his dominion: who even now, before he appears, draws innumerable men, though not all, because many are drawn back from carnality.

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- « How then do ye comfort me, etc. » [0817A] Since, if the wicked man is permitted to prosper in this life, which has been shown concerning Antichrist, it is necessary that the elect should be held under the bridle of the scourge.

⟦p48⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Since your answer, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ For they could not comfort him, in that by their own words they went against the truth. ⟦s3⟧ For when they called him a hypocrite, since by lying they were committing a fault, they were increasing the punishment of the afflicted just man; because the fault of another's deceit tortures him who loves the truth.
