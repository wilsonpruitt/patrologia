## CHAPTER XXIII.

VERS. 1.-- « And Job answering said: Now also in bitterness. » [n: (GREG.)] The mind of the just man is always in bitterness, etc., *as far as* who seek to seduce by sweet speeches.

VERS. 2.-- « And the hand of my stroke. » Now the hand of the stroke is the strength of the smiting; but because out of adversities the just thirst the more for the face of their author, there follows:

VERS. 3.-- « Who will grant me. » The elect, when they see anything whatever done against their wish, run back to the hidden judgments of God, that in them they may see that what seems to run without in disorder [0819B] is not disposed within in disorder. And this is what he adds:

« That I might find him. » As it is: that God now defers, so that he may be the more desired.

« And come even to his throne. » The throne is the angels, etc., *as far as* while they see that God disposes better things, whence:

« I will set judgment before God. » To set judgment before God, etc., *as far as* to consider how terrible the judge, now hidden and silent, will afterwards appear.

VERS. 4.-- « I will fill my mouth. » Because, while he contemplates the judge's examination against himself, he pursues himself bitterly with the invective of penance.

VERS. 5.--That I may know the words which, » etc. For when [0819C] we pursue our faults by repenting, we find what the judge could say to us about them in his examination: which he does not know, because he neglects his own evils.

VERS. 6.-- « I would not that he contend with much strength. » For who could be found just in the examination, if God should sift the life of men according to the majesty of his strength: for however just a man be, if he is strictly judged, he is pressed by the mass of that greatness, whence he adds:

« Nor of greatness. » As if he should say: If he contended with me according to his strength, he would press me with his own mass. And note that, while he shuns the strength, he desires the weakness of the incarnation, whence he adds:

VERS. 7.-- « Let him set forth equity. » The mediator of God and of men, etc., *as far as* as victor I may bring forth the judgment of [0819D] my absolution.

VERS. 8.-- « If to the east. » [n: (Ibid.)] As if he should say: the incarnate Son, etc., *as far as* But not even in all these does the soul fully know itself, whence:

VERS. 10.-- « But he indeed knoweth the way. » That is, I am not able to see him who sees me.

« And he hath tried. » Not being lifted up does he compare himself to gold, but he humbly considers his own things, which is allowed to every just man. And this is: « And try thou me as gold. » [var: Vulg. *probavit me quasi aurum*, "he hath tried me as gold," in the indicative] For by the burning of tribulation both vices are taken away and merits increased.

VERS. 11.-- « His steps he hath followed. » Blessed Job in his future working, etc., *as far as* he runs back as to an example set before him.

[0820A]VERS. 12.-- « And in my bosom I have hidden. » We hide the words of his mouth in the bosom of the heart, since we hear his commandments not in passing, but as to be fulfilled in work; and if the words come forth in work, they nevertheless lie hidden in the heart. If the mind is not lifted up within, but if praise is sought without, then the word of God is not hidden in the bosom.

VERS. 13.-- « For he alone is. » Principally and unchangeably. For he alone is through himself, who does all things, in which he himself is to be feared, not the things which we suffer. Concerning whose unchangeableness he adds:

« And no man can turn away. » Because judgments once fixed can in no wise be changed.

« And his soul. » That very power by which he disposes all things is called his soul.

[0820B]VERS. 14.-- « When he shall have fulfilled. » He fulfils his will in me, because he afflicts me with many strokes. But « many like things are at hand with him, » because if he purposes to strike, there is still found where the wound may grow.

VERS. 15.-- « And therefore at his face. » He is shaken with the dread of rectitude, when he beholds himself not to be fit for the accounts of one rendering them, if he be judged strictly.

VERS. 16.-- « God hath softened. » He ascribes therefore the power of his own strength not to himself, but to its author.

« He hath troubled me. » Never are the hearts of the just secure, but troubled with the fear of the judge; which hearts nevertheless are comforted by the gifts they have received, whence: « For I have not perished. » The scourges of the just either purge past sins or turn away future ones; which is not so in Job, but his virtue is increased.

[0820C]VERS. 17.-- « For not. » He, set in the scourge, perishes from the health of the body because of the impending darkness, who is struck for the things done in the past, that he may be hidden from future punishments.
