## CHAPTER XXVII.

VERS. 1.-- « He added also, » etc. [n: (GREG. in Job., l. XVIII.)] The Church suffers bitter things from her enemies in two ways: either by words, where her wisdom is exercised; or by swords, where her patience is proved. Now he speaks of that persecution wherein she is assailed by false assertions.

VERS. 2.-- « God liveth. » There are those who, suffering adversities, [0825D] do not believe that God is: some judge that God is, but that he by no means cares for human affairs. This man, set in the figure of the Church in affliction, that he might confess that God is, says: « God liveth. » And that he might declare that he cares for human affairs, he adds: « Who hath taken away my judgment, » etc.

VERS. 4.-- « My lips shall not speak. » [n: (Ibid.)] That formerly iniquity, etc., *as far as* to meditate is of studious depravity.

VERS. 5.-- « Far be it from me. » Because Solomon says: « He that justifieth the wicked, and he that condemneth the just, both are abominable before God. » And this is: « Far be it from me. » For he would depart from innocence if he judged good things of those evil men.

[0826A]VERS. 6.-- « My justification. » [n: (Ibid.)] He would forsake the justification he had begun, if he turned aside into the praise of sinners. He well stands off from others' faults who first keeps himself from his own: as it were, for this cause I fear to sin concerning them, because my heart does not reprove me.

« For neither doth my heart reprove me. » As though he should say, etc., *as far as* yet he strongly resisted the thought.

VERS. 7.-- « As the wicked, my enemy. [n: (Ibid.)] The wicked man is called unfaithful, etc., *as far as* he who agrees not with me in work.

VERS. 8.-- « For what is the hope of hypocrisy. » The hypocrite, who in Latin is called a dissembler, is a greedy plunderer, who, while acting wickedly he desires to be venerated for sanctity, snatches the praise of another's life.

VERS. 9.-- « Will God hear? » Because he finds no [0826B] remedy in his crying at the judgment who now loses the fitting time of crying. Concerning whose depravity it is subjoined:

VERS. 10.-- « Or will he be able to delight in the Almighty. » He who is overcome by the love of earthly things is in no wise delighted in God:

« And to call upon God in every. » The hypocrite calls upon God only then, when the tribulation of earthly things straitens him.

VERS. 11.-- « I will teach you by the hands. » The Son is called the hand of God, etc., *as far as* it is a sin to him.

VERS. 14.-- « If they shall have been multiplied. » [n: (Ibid.)] They who are begotten by the preaching of heretics are struck by the sentence of the judgment to come.

« And the grandsons. » The grandsons of heretics are, etc., [0826C] *as far as* he leaves some obstinate in malice. There follows:

VERS. 15.-- « They that shall be left. » Left, that is to say, out of that people of heretics, they shall be buried in destruction, because while they do not return to the light of truth, they are pressed down by earthly understanding.

« And his widows shall not weep. » By widows he means the subject peoples left destitute by the death of the heretic: who, while the preacher of error is snatched away to eternal torments, often return to the grace of true knowledge.

VERS. 16.-- « If he shall have heaped together. » [n: (Ibid.)] Silver, that is, the eloquence of heretics, etc., *as far as* they weave together the testimonies of Holy Scripture.

VERS. 17-- « He shall indeed prepare. » Because a man full of right faith gathers the testimonies of Scripture which the heretic brings forward, [0826D] and thence convicts the obstinacy of error, as David slew Goliath with his own sword.

« And the innocent shall divide the silver. » For the innocent to divide the silver, etc., *as far as* because they mingle false things with true.

VERS. 18.-- « He hath built his house as a moth. » [n: (Ibid.)] The moth builds itself a house by corrupting: so too the heretic makes the place of his perfidy nowhere but in the minds which he corrupts.

« And as a keeper. » The shelter of the keeper, etc., *as far as* the discourse is raised up, when it is subjoined.

VERS. 19.-- « When the rich man shall have slept. » The rich man sleeps and opens his eyes, because when he dies in the flesh his soul is compelled to see what it scorned to look to beforehand.

[0827A]VERS. 20.-- « It shall lay hold on him. » For, want driving him, the burning rich man among those below was compelled even to the begging of the least things, who here, tenacity driving him, came even to the denying of the least things.

« By night shall it oppress him. » By night he calls the hidden time of a sudden departure; by the name of tempest he designates the whirlwind of judgment.

VERS. 21.-- « The wind shall take him away. » The burning wind is called the malignant spirit, who drives him whom he has now kindled with the fire of perverse concupiscence afterwards to the flames of hell.

« And as a whirlwind shall snatch away. » The place of the perverse is the delight of temporal life.

VERS. 22.-- « And he shall send upon him. » As often as God corrects the sinner by striking, he sends forth the scourge [0827B] to this end, that he may spare. But when by striking he shuts up his life as it remains in sin, he sends forth the scourge, but by no means spares.

« Out of his hand. » Because by the hand operation is understood, he flees out of the hand of the stricken man who, while he beholds the destruction of the depraved, forsakes the ways of depravity, whence there follows:

VERS. 23.-- « He shall press over him. » To clasp the hands is to confirm the works of life in rectitude, by beholding in another's punishment what he should fear.
