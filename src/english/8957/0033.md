## CHAPTER XXVIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- « Silver hath the beginnings of its own veins. » [n: (GREG.)] After the punishments of the proud and powerful, etc., *as far as* that they be made solid by no authority of the sacred books.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0827C] « And there is a place for gold. » In this place the heretics [sic: *haeritici*], etc., *as far as* he shall first have burned here in the workshop of charity.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- « Iron is taken out of the earth. » Heretics are wont to glory of justice, and they accuse the Catholics of having been wicked. ⟦s2⟧ Against whom it is said, by way of humble confession: « Iron is taken out of the earth. » Because the stronger men are separated from the earthly action which they formerly held, and become the champions of the Church.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And the stone loosed. » Because a hard heart, etc., *as far as* and he may become sonorous through preaching.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- « He hath set a time for the darkness. » The measure of the wicked man, that is to say, whereby they may cease to be wicked.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And of all things. » Because he beholds the end of the elect, etc., *as far as* that it should blind itself with the darkness of hardened perfidy.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0827D] « The stone also of darkness. » The people of the Jews, hard through perfidy, etc., *as far as* he expressed his image in himself.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- « The torrent shall divide from the people. » [n: (Ibid.)] But by the name of torrent, etc., *as far as* by the name of torrent the watering of holy preaching may be understood.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Those whom he hath forgotten, » that is, the Jews, etc., *as far as* because they would not afford a way to the heart for the words of life.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- « The land out of which arose. » Because Judaea both had before the law which should refresh, and afterwards, the miracles of the Redeemer having been seen, the envy which should burn up.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- « The place of the sapphire, its stones. » [n: (Ibid.)] [0828A] Sapphires are of an airy colour. ⟦s2⟧ And he says this, because never were there wanting out of Judaea holy souls that should lead a heavenly life:

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And its clods. » By clods he means the gatherings of the several orders, etc., *as far as* But why she lost her good things, there follows:

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- « It knew not the path. » In this place by the name of bird he is signified, etc., *as far as* he would not consider.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Nor hath the eye beheld. » Our Redeemer, of the vulture, etc., *as far as* concerning whom it is subjoined:

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- « The sons of the merchants have not trodden it. » Merchants are they who carry on spiritual business, etc., *as far as* the sons of merchants, pastors and doctors.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0828B] « Nor hath the lioness passed through it. » The Church is called a lioness, because she slays those living ill in vices by the mouth of holy preaching, whence it is said to Peter: *Slay and eat.* ⟦s2⟧ And so the Church passed into Judaea, but did not pass through: because out of her she snatched a few to the faith, but yet she did not utterly extinguish the unfaithful people from perfidy. ⟦s3⟧ But, repulsed by the Jews, what did she do?

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- « He hath stretched forth his hand to the flint. » Because he sent the arm of his preaching to the hardness of the nations.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « He hath overthrown the mountains from the roots. » The roots are the inmost thoughts of the proud: the mountains were overturned from the roots, because the powers of the world fell from their inmost thought to the worship of God.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- « In the rocks he hath cut out streams, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] [0828C] that is, in the hard hearts of the nations he opened rivers of preaching.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And his eye hath seen every precious thing. » God saw the precious thing, when he looked upon the human soul, abject in itself, with the illumination of the grace of his wisdom.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- « The depths also. » The Lord searches out the depths of the rivers, etc., *as far as* and by no merit of ours does the wisdom of God come to us.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- « Neither is it found in the land. » By the land he means the human soul. ⟦s2⟧ This wisdom cannot be found in the land of those who live pleasantly, because whosoever is still fed with the pleasures of this life is separated from the understanding of eternal wisdom.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14. ⟦s2⟧ « The deep saith. » By the deep he means the hearts of men, etc., *as far as* which he calls foolish [0828D] before God, by their wicked works.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The sea speaketh: Not, » the minds of the restless, etc., *as far as* but she should come through her own self. ⟦s2⟧ There follows:

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- « Fine gold shall not be given for it. » By fine gold the holy angels are named, etc., *as far as* but not without the contagion of fault.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Neither shall silver be weighed out in exchange for it. » Because the life of the just who preach, or of those who write the words of God, with however great a light of sanctity it may be strong, does not by its own presence impart to us the coming of the heavenly wisdom.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- « It shall not be compared with the dyed colours of India. » By India this world is understood, etc., *as far as* and a garment without dye.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0829A] « Nor to the sardonyx stone. » The sardonyx holds the likeness of red earth, the sapphire an airy appearance: by the sardonyx therefore are understood men, or the fathers of the Old Testament, who follow after certain earthly things; by the sapphire, the preachers of the New Testament, who seek heavenly things alone, or the angels.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- « Gold shall not be made equal to it, or. » [n: (GREG.)] After it was said, etc., *as far as* because they are a creature.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Nor shall vessels be exchanged for it. » A lofty vessel of gold, etc., *as far as* but he believes him the Lord of the prophets.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- « Neither shall they be remembered in comparison. » For what are men compared with God? ⟦s2⟧ Nothing.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « But wisdom is drawn. » Because, since it is invisible, etc., *as far as* « And thou knowest not whence it cometh, or whither [0829B] it goeth. »

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- « The topaz shall not be made equal to it. » For that it shines again with every colour, etc., *as far as* which he shows when he adds:

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Nor with the cleanest dyes. » The cleanest dyes are those who humbly keep the grace of the virtues that comes upon them. ⟦s2⟧ For they would not be dyed, if they had held holiness naturally.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- « Whence then cometh wisdom? » Because it is born of the invisible Father, etc., *as far as* « and where is the place of understanding? »

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- « It is hidden. » Because so long as men live here, God cannot be seen through the appearance of his own nature; but through images he was seen by Moses, Jacob, and others.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « It lieth hid also from the birds of heaven. » *(GREG. in Job, [0829C] l. ⟦s2⟧ XIX.)* ⟦s3⟧ Because, being set in this corruptible flesh, etc., *as far as* in their sayings and consolations they breathe again from the weariness of this life.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- « Destruction and death have said, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] Because to see God is the same as to have him: therefore the wicked do not see this wisdom, because, repulsed through pride, they were by no means able to have it. ⟦s2⟧ But to have heard its fame is to have known its power from its might.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- « God understandeth the way. » As though he should say, etc., *as far as* she was to fill the hearts of the elect in all the parts of the world. ⟦s2⟧ There follows:

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- « For he beholdeth the ends of the world. » For God to look upon is to reform lost things unto grace.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- « Who made a weight for the winds. » By the swiftness [0829D] and loftiness of the winds, etc., *as far as* to temper glory with infirmity mingled in.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And he weighed the waters, » etc. [n: (Ibid.)] By waters he means minds by the Holy Spirit, etc., *as far as* but he fled the woman, stricken with fear.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- « When he was setting a law for the rains. » The rain is the sayings of those who preach, etc., *as far as* which keeps in work that which it has sounded forth.

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- « Then he saw it, and declared it. » He saw this incarnate wisdom, that is, he made it to be seen, and to be declared, and made it to be prepared by preachers and to be searched out by hearers. ⟦s2⟧ For he prepares it for himself, whosoever has taken care, by living well, to have it propitious in the day of judgment.

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0830A] « He saw it, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ He saw, because it is a form, etc., *as far as* what man should know of it in himself, whence it is subjoined:

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- « And he said to man, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because we cannot penetrate the nature of wisdom, what it is in itself: from God's condescension we have heard what it is in us. ⟦s3⟧ For it is certain that he has it, of whom it is not uncertain that he fears God.

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Behold the fear of the Lord, that itself, » etc., as though he should say If thou fearest God, it is agreed that thou art full of the wisdom which among men is called the fear of the Lord. ⟦s2⟧ But because he truly understands the force of divine fear who keeps himself from all depraved works, he adds: « And to depart from evil is understanding. »
