## CHAPTER XXX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- « But now me the younger. » [n: (GREG.)] Those younger in time deride the Church, when they who have gone out from her despise the words of her doctrine.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Whose fathers I did not deign, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ The flock of the Church, therefore, etc., *as far as* and she scorns to number them among true fathers.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- « With whom was the strength of hands. » The strength of hands is greatness, etc., *as far as* but concerning one's neighbour [0833B] to think better things than concerning oneself.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- « By want. » For by immoderate sports, the more they crave the knowledge of the supernal science, the more they lose it.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And barren with hunger. » Because they desire to know, whence they may be learned and talkative, not composed in their ways; whence they scarcely take hold of outward things, who suppose that they have penetrated hidden ones.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Who gnawed in the wilderness, etc. ⟦s2⟧ Heretics who from the universal, etc., *as far as* But they lick, who take away by the blandishment of the tongue.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Squalid with calamity. » They are declared squalid with calamity and misery, because they are despised both by the ruin of their ways and by the depravity of their senses. ⟦s2⟧ Calamity is as it were a sickness: misery is the neglect of it, whence it becomes [0833C] graver. ⟦s3⟧ No one would be squalid, if in his own necessities he did not add the misery of pleasure.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- « And they chewed herbs, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ To chew herbs, etc., *as far as* but not to hold a right intention in the same works.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (Ibid.)] For what by herbs, etc., *as far as* since they serve avarice alone, they are filled with the root of the juniper, and this is.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And the root of the junipers. » Junipers have, in place of leaves, thorns as it were, etc., *as far as* who for the most part follow after in their words the outward gains alone.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- « Who snatching these things from the valleys. » They snatch those things from the valleys, etc., *as far as* to the carnal things which press within.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- « They dwell in the deserts of the torrents. » [0833D] [n: (Ibid.)] The finders-out of perverse things, etc., *as far as* are the earthly things which the evil love.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And in the caverns of the earth. » [n: (Ibid.)] For heretics, by clandestine, etc., *as far as* he descends even unto evils.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Or upon the gravel. » We call gravel the most compacted stones, which the river water drags along: heretics dwell upon the gravel, because they drag those minds of men which are made solid by no weight of stability.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- « Who among such things glad. » By the name of *brambles,* etc., *as far as* whence it is also openly subjoined:

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And to be under brambles, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ They rejoice indeed over temporal things, etc., *as far as* that it should not please them to cease from the yoke of [0834A] worldly servitude, even if it were permitted.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- « The sons of the foolish and of the ignoble. » Their sons who were, etc., *as far as* who have been made exiles from the land of the living.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- « Now into their song, » etc. ⟦s2⟧ The time of the Church is expressed, in which she is publicly derided by the reprobate.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And I am made a proverb to them. » He will be made a proverb to the depraved, because when they behold the good dying by torments, they take from them a likeness of cursing, saying: So may it come to thee as to that man who was butchered.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- « They abhor me, and flee far from me. » The wicked flee far from the Church, etc., *as far as* he knows how to ascribe his own evils to the justice of the judge. ⟦s2⟧ There follows:

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- « For he hath opened his quiver. » [0834B] By the quiver the hidden judgment of God is signified. ⟦s2⟧ When therefore the Lord sees sins, etc., *as far as* but in the rebuke the strength of counsel is opened.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And he hath put a bridle into my mouth. » For holy doctors, while they see the hearts of those who resist forsaken by God, afflicted and groaning fall silent, lest they speak to the unworthy, who would also become worse. ⟦s2⟧ He spares the wicked by keeping silence, to whom nevertheless he commends his own life through patience.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- « At the right hand of the Orient calamities. » [n: (Ibid.)] Our Redeemer is called the Orient, etc., *as far as* because, when persecution breaks out, the just suffer the assaults of the depraved.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « They have overthrown my feet. » By the feet of the Church her outermost members are signified, which, while they are subservient to [0834C] earthly works, can be deceived the more swiftly, the less they understand lofty things.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And they have oppressed with paths as with floods. » The path of the adversaries is compared to floods, because the life of the depraved, troublesome with insolent unquietness, glides in like a tempest to overwhelm (so to speak) the ship of the heart.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- « They have scattered my ways. » Let blessed Job say this of malignant spirits; let the Church say it of her persecutors, who scatter her ways, while in the minds of certain weak ones they disturb the way of truth by a squalid persuasion.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « They have lain in wait for me and have prevailed. » Those lying in wait prevail, when those whom they cannot openly [0834D] draw to evil, they pervert by feigning good things.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And there was none who brought help. » For the most part the just, caught in tribulation, reckon that the hand of the helper is delayed, when the savagery of their persecutors straitens them a little longer; and the remedies of the deliverer are indeed swift, but they seem slow to grief.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- « As with a broken wall. » The Redeemer is a wall to us, etc., *as far as* which indeed comes to pass, because power is given by God to enemies.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And the door being opened they rushed in upon me. » When with the perverse in this life, etc., *as far as* they strive to destroy the defences of the faith.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And they are rolled down unto my miseries. » The reprobate are rolled down through their own miseries even to ours, when [0835A] they draw also certain who are joined to us into those evils in which they themselves are entangled.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- « I am brought to nothing. » In the estimation of the evil, etc., *as far as* and they are to be taken according to the history, which is plain.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Thou hast taken away my desire as a wind. » The faithful people declares that it suffers, because it grieves that those whom it loves suffer. ⟦s2⟧ The wind therefore takes away desire, when any transitory thing destroys the appetite for eternity.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « It passed away as a cloud. » A cloud stands out on high, but the blast drives it to its course: salvation therefore passed away as a cloud, because the glory of the perverse, the higher it is, is not fixed. ⟦s2⟧ When the desires and the acts of the weak are ended, it returns at the voice of the elect.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- « But now in my very self. » In [0835B] our own selves now the soul, etc., *as far as* whence it soon returns to those who are falling.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- « By night my bone is pierced with sorrows. » He so speaks his own things, that they suit also those who fall, etc., *as far as* as though they already had a certain opening of fear.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And they that devour me. » [n: (GREG.)] Because malignant spirits, who consume the carnal out of the Church, know not how to rest from temptation so much the more, as they are weighed down by no burden of flesh.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- « In the multitude. » What else is the garment of Job, etc., *as far as* who clung to him by the bond of love.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And as with the collar of a tunic. » The collar of the tunic surrounds the neck, etc., *as far as* they strive to extinguish the voice [0835C] of preaching.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- « I am compared to mud. » In the judgment of the reprobate, the Church of the elect is compared to mud, because, trodden down for a time, she is despised; she is likened to embers and to ashes, because they reckon that she has come to those evils only which they see exhibited in her bodily.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- « I cry to thee. » The Church in the time of her persecution, etc., *as far as* and those who are deferred that they may profit, fear lest they fail.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- « Thou art changed to me into one that is cruel. » In God neither cruelty nor mutability comes, etc., *as far as* But nothing worthy can be said of him.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And in the hardness of thy hand. » The hand of God is then said to be contrary to our will, when it pursues by striking that which displeases him in us.

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0835D]VERS. 22.-- « Thou hast lifted me up, and as it were. » Because the glory of the present life is discerned as it were on high, etc., *as far as* their elevation forthwith falls.

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- « I know that thou wilt deliver me to death. » For no one comes hither, etc., *as far as* therefore it is aptly subjoined:

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- « Nevertheless not to their consuming. » The Lord his hand, etc., *as far as* who, standing outwardly, lay prostrate from the state of the mind.

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- « I wept once. » Compassion is more than a gift, etc., *as far as* he joins to them his own weeping in continual prayer.

⟦p48⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (Ibid.)] In the time of the last persecution of the Church, she recalls to memory the good things which she did in the time of peace. [0836A] Desiring which eternal joys, but pressed with evils while they are deferred, she adds:

⟦p49⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- « I awaited good things, and evils came to me. » The faithful people awaits good things, but receives evil.

⟦p50⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « I looked for light, and darkness broke forth. » It awaits the light, and runs into darkness, because for the grace of remuneration it hopes now to share in the joys of the angels, and yet, deferred here for a longer time, it endures the hands of its persecutors, as Tobias.

⟦p51⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- « My inward parts have boiled up without any rest. » That the inward parts of holy Church boil up, etc., *as far as* and this is, « his inward parts have boiled up. »

⟦p52⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « The days of affliction have prevented me. » For the Church [0836B] of the elect knows that in the last persecution she will suffer many evils. ⟦s2⟧ But the days of her affliction go before her, because she bears among her the life of the wicked heavily even in the time of peace, who already show in their depraved ways what is to come.

⟦p53⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- « I went mourning. » The holy man raised up, etc., *as far as* he comes by a rough way to his country.

⟦p54⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « Rising up without fury. » Often the tumults of seditious men provoke the minds of their own superiors: who, if they are good, have a cry against the clamour of the insolent tumult, and have not fury, since those whom they mercifully bear with, they cease not to teach.

⟦p55⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- « I was the brother of dragons. » By the name of dragons [0836C] the malicious, etc., *as far as* but the feather of virtue lifts him not at all from the earth.

⟦p56⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- « My skin is blackened. » By the name of skin the weak of the Church, etc., *as far as* who are in her are tormented by the zeal of faith.

⟦p57⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- « The harp is turned to mourning. » As if he should say In the time of my peace, etc., *as far as* because vices are to be extinguished, not the flesh.
