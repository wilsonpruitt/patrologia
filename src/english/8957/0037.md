## CHAPTER XXXII.

VERS. 1.-- « But the three sent. » [n: (GREG.)] At length the full things of blessed Job, etc., *as far as* and by keeping themselves in oldness they harm by their depraved discourses the watch [0840B] of the ruler.

VERS. 2.-- « And Eliu was angry and indignant. » Eliu, that is, My God, etc., *as far as* although he is severed from their life by the depravity of elation.

« But he was angry against Job. » For the followers of vain glory, etc., *as far as* they press those set within by the quality of their speaking, that is, by the vice of elation.

VERS. 6.-- « And Eliu the son answering. » [n: (GREG. in Job., l. XXIII.)] Because these things lack the solidity of gravity, etc., *as far as* yet he vehemently exercises the practice of it.

[n: (Ibid.)] It is no small condemnation out of a good thing, etc., *as far as* that in his ruin they might learn what they should fear concerning elation.

VERS. 11.-- « For I awaited your words, [0840C] I heard your prudence. » When he says « I awaited, etc., » he declares himself to the words, etc., *as far as* in whose words they reprove perverse doctrines.

VERS. 12.-- « But, as I see, there is none who can convict Job. » Often heretics through this that they are, etc., *as far as* whose voice Eliu meets, saying:

VERS. 13.-- « Lest perhaps you say. » As if to say, in vain you say, etc., *as far as* and he keeps eternal rewards for him that arrives.

VERS. 14.-- « He hath spoken nothing to me. » [n: (Ibid.)] Eliu indeed had heard blessed Job publicly, etc., *as far as* when they judge themselves wiser than even those who reprove them. But because he says:

« And I will not answer according to your words. » Well to blessed Job not, etc., *as far as* but concerning [0840D] themselves they suspect things worthy of more than is needful.

VERS. 15.-- « They were afraid and answered no more. » Well are the words of the friends of Job reported by Eliu to have been afraid, because sometimes the defenders of the Church arrogantly, although they keep not the order of speaking, nevertheless disturb their adversaries by the strength of their sayings, etc. *Vide Greg. loc. cit. usque ad fin. cap.*
