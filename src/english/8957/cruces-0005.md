## Load-bearing vocabulary, fixed for this stint

- `*usque ad*` (the abridgment formula) → **as far as**, in italics, matching 8950/8955.
- `« … »` reproduced 1:1 with the Latin twin, spacing included; the `VERS. n.-- ` address kept
  as printed. This is a **guillemet book**: no lemma is italicized.
- `flagellum/flagella` → **scourge(s)** · `percussio` → **stroke** · `verbera` → **stripes** ·
  `increpatio` → **rebuke** (and `increpationem … ne reprobes` → "reject not the chastening",
  Migne's own noun kept as "chastening" only inside the lemma; the gloss's `increpationem`
  renders "rebuke", following the Latin's own two uses).
- `sospitas` → **soundness** · `compunctio` → **compunction** · `praedicatio` → **preaching** ·
  `electi` → **the elect** · `reprobi` → **the reprobate** · `insensati` → **the senseless**.
- `dissimulare` → **dissemble** throughout (lemma and gloss), because the gloss at 0763C turns
  on the word (`potestas … propter tumorem dissimulanda`).
- **Pattern 17:** Job is addressed in the grammatical singular throughout ch. V, so *thou/thy/
  thee* (`contempsisti`, `habes`, `ne reprobes`, `non timebis`, `scies`, `pertracta`).

## Vulgate divergences in the lemmata (7a″ walk, all plate-confirmed)

Collated against `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt`, book chapter:verse cited.

1. **0762C** `Qui exspectant mortem` ‖ Clem Job 3:21 `qui expectant mortem`. Orthographic only
   (*exspectant* is the plate's; f381). No marker, Pattern 9.
2. **0763A** `Et quasi inundantes aquae` ‖ Clem Job 3:24 `et tamquam inundantes aquae`.
   A real lexical divergence (*quasi* for *tamquam*), plate-confirmed f382; sense unchanged, so
   no `[var:]`. Recorded because the gloss expounds the words as printed.
3. **0763D** `Ecce docuisti plurimos` ‖ Clem Job 4:3 `Ecce docuisti multos`. Plate-confirmed
   f382. Rendered "very many"; **the English must not read "many"** — the gloss argues from

4. **0764A** `Nunc autem venit, etc., quasi non servasti recta quae dicebas` ‖ Clem Job 4:5

   paraphrase carried inside Migne's guillemets, not scripture; rendered as printed, with its
   `non`.
5. **0766A** `Vacate et videte: quoniam ego sum Deus` [n: (Psal. XLV)] ‖ Clem Ps 45:11 — agrees.
6. **0766B** `Levitae radant omnes pilos carnis suae` [n: (Levit. XIV)]. **The wording is
   Num 8:7** (`et radant omnes pilos carnis suae`), not Lev 14:9 (`radet … totius corporis
   pilos`). Migne's own citation is reproduced verbatim in the `[n:]` and is **not** corrected —
   this book leans on `(Ibid.)` chains and a corrected antecedent would mis-resolve them.
7. **0766C** `Imago coram oculis me` ‖ Clem Job 4:16 `imago coram oculis meis`. **Plate-confirmed
   at native resolution (f383, clean type, no broken sort).** The printed *me* has no
   grammatical slot after *coram oculis*; rendered as literally as the words allow ("An image
   before the eyes me") with `[var: Vulg. Job 4:16 *meis*, "an image before mine eyes"]`.

8. **0766C** `Nunquid homo comparatione Dei justificabitur?` ‖ Clem Job 4:17 `Numquid homo, Dei

9. **0766C** `Nemo venit ad Patrem, nisi per me` (Jn 14:6) is printed **unmarked** — no

10. **0768B** `Quae dicunt, facite` [n: (Matth. XXIII)] ‖ Clem Mt 23:3 `Omnia ergo quaecumque

11. **0768C** `Et de humo non egredietur` ‖ Clem Job 5:6 `et de humo non oritur dolor`.

12. **0768C** `Pugnabit pro eo` — Wisd. 5:21 (`et pugnabit pro illo orbis terrarum contra

## Defects and near-defects

- **0764A `vel bel bona`** — ⛔ **NOT Migne's.** The plate (f382, native crop) sets
  `dicentes eos vel prava dicere, vel bona quae dicunt non tenere`, with no `bel`. The intrusive
  non-word is Corpus Corporum's. The English renders the plate and fires **no marker**; the
  divergence is filed in `data/briefs/8957-PENDING-TEI-PATCHES-0005.md`.
- **0770D `Vastitas es gehenna`** — the plate (f385, native crop at 1360px) sets **`Vastitas e s`**
  at the end of the line, `gehenna` beginning the next: a spaced/broken setting where *est* is
  required. The line break falls after *s*, so the break is not the explanation for the space
  between *e* and *s*. *es* is a real word with no grammatical slot in the clause (Pattern 12
  test 1), so the marker stands and the sense is rendered beside it:
  "The wasting is [sic: *es*] the gehenna which is without". The marker wraps the **twin's**
  form because `verify-english` checks it as a substring of the twin; when the spacing patch
  lands it should become `[sic: *e s*]` (Pattern 10, the whole run).
- **0771A `ecclesiae moribus et linguas distantes`** — plate-confirmed `linguas` (f386, native
  crop). Accusative beside the coordinate ablative *moribus*, both governed by *distantes*.
  **No marker**: English has no exponent for the case, "differing in customs and tongues" is the
  same rendering either way, and marking it would put a defect claim on a construction that is
  merely non-classical (Pattern 9's boundary). Recorded so a later sweep does not churn it.
- **0763B `dum qui apud Deum decernatur, nesciunt`** — the plate reads *qui* (f382), where *quid*
  would construe more naturally. Rendered literally, "while they know not who is adjudged before
  God." Pattern 7; no marker (the type is sound and the clause construes).
- **0766D** the paragraph closes `… cum ejus judicium de sua afflictione redarguit` with **no
  terminal stop** on the plate (f383). Pattern 8: nothing supplied; the English stops where the
  plate stops.
