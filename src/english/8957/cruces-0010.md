## Markers fired (4 sites), each on a column read at the plate this session

- **0777A `[sic: *substiti*]`** — *et qui hic exspectatus ad poenitentiam substiti, jam non
  subsistat*. Plate-confirmed (f389, mid-line, no line or column break anywhere near it, so the
  hyphen hypothesis is excluded). A first-person perfect stranded among third-person finite verbs:
  a real word with **no grammatical slot**, which is Pattern 12's test (1), the *Pecte* / *ama*
  class of 11632. Conjecture: *substitit*, "he stood firm / held out". ⚠ Per **7a⁗ question 1**
  the marked run was the relative clause's only verb, so the sense stands in plain words beside
  the marker ("stood firm [sic: …], let him now not stand firm") rather than leaving a hole.
- **0778A `[cj: *in corruptionem*; read *incorruptionem*, "incorruption"]`** — *tunc peccatum
  plene tolletur, cum in corruptionem homo mutabitur*. The type is **not broken**: *in
  corruptionem* is sound Latin, so `[sic:]` has nothing to wrap. But the faithful English then
  says sin is fully taken away when man is changed **into corruption**, which is the opposite of
  what the sentence is arguing (the resurrection is the subject two clauses later: *Desiderat
  ergo Redemptorem vel resurrectionem*). Pattern 18 exactly, and the additive gloss keeps Migne's
  word on the page. Plate-confirmed f389.
- **0780D `[sic: *necessatibus*]`** — *ut se hic a necessatibus quasi ab aestu et imbre
  defendant*. Confirmed at **native resolution** (f390 crop 1000,2680,880,180): a non-word for
  *necessitatibus*, mid-line, not at a break. 7a⁗: the marked run is the object of *a*, so "from
  necessities" stands beside the marker.
- **Five `[var: …]`**, all Pattern 14 (a legible plate whose scripture does not agree with the

## Cruces proper

- **0773C — a negation that contradicts its own sentence, and it is Migne's.** *Cum enim dolor vel
  tentatio **non** percutit, elatio mentis succisa cadit.* Read at native resolution (f387 crop
  100,1480,780,220): the *non* is unmistakably printed. Gregory's argument, and this glossator's,
  is that pride falls **when** sorrow strikes; with the *non* the page says the opposite. Carried
  into the English exactly as printed, per Pattern 7a. No marker: the type is not broken and the
  word is a negation, so a `[cj:]` was weighed and **declined** — 18a's admitted class is a
  *dropped or intruded* correlative where the answering member is printed, and here nothing
  answers it. The contradiction is the evidence; it is not ours to remove.
- **0777C — unconstruable, and printed exactly as our TEI has it.** *quod est, suspendium elegit,
  hoc est, desperavit, de eodem intulit, parce: quod est, mortem ossa: hoc est, non ultra vivant
  hoc tertio.* *mortem ossa* juxtaposes an accusative and a plural with no verb between them, and
  *hoc tertio* has no antecedent. Rendered as closely as the printed words allow ("which is, death
  the bones: that is, let them not live further, this the third time") and left there. Both
  negations carried. No conjecture is defensible: the passage is corrupt past construing and
  9604 @1347D's rule applies — do not quietly pick one.
- **0777A — `(GERG.)` for `(GREG.)`, and it is Migne's.** Plate-confirmed f389. Reproduced
  verbatim inside the `[n: …]` marker, untranslated, like every other note. **No `[sic:]` stacked
  on it**: the misprint stands identically in the facing Latin column, which is Pattern 9's own
  argument for normalised section labels, and a marker inside a note's verbatim contents would
  blur whose observation it is.
- **0781B — `Quis creat` for `Qui creat`, declined.** Plate-confirmed f391. A real word; the
  English ("Who creates, that the things created may accord with him, ordains…") reads as a
  question that is not one, but it **asserts nothing false**, and 18a keeps mood/person anomalies
  that merely fail to construe outside the pattern. Crux only. This is also the lemma the brief
  names as opening a guillemet that never closes; both are Migne's, on one line.
- **0782B — `Quantus ergo se.`** Job 9:14 has *quantus ergo sum ego*. Rendered « How great
  therefore himself. » The same address prints again at 0782C as « Quantus ergo. » without the
  *se*, so Migne's own two settings disagree; declined for a marker because an abridged lemma is
  a fragment by design and the sense is carried by the gloss beneath it.
- **0783C — `usque ad quam Christus venerit`.** The `*usque ad*` resumption lands mid-clause on a
  bare *quam*, whose comparative or antecedent is inside the abridged matter. Rendered "than
  Christ came" and left mid-clause; nothing supplied, per the 8950 precedent for a formula that
  resumes on an apposition.
- **0783B — `nostram duplam carnis et animae destruat`.** *duplam* is feminine with no noun;
  Gregory's *duplam mortem* is the obvious supply and is **not** supplied. Rendered "our double of
  flesh and of soul".
- **0785A — a question mark on a statement, kept.** *sed beneficiis Dei nemo serviliter timens
  respondet?* Plate-confirmed f393. Pattern 8: the mark rides as printed, and the clause is
  already direct, so 8b's colon recasting does not apply. The *nemo* is carried.
- **0785A — a run-on Migne does not punctuate.** *quae prius dulcis erat etiam moerens eam
  tolerat*: two clauses fused with no mark between them. Rendered with a single comma for
  readability and no terminal mark supplied anywhere; the fusion stays visible.
- **0776A — `lacessit` where the sentence wants a passive.** *Qui de gravitate operis lacessit,
  nisi spe finis relevaretur* — an active indicative against a following subjunctive apodosis.
  Plate-confirmed f388. Rendered "who chafes at the heaviness of the work", which is what the
  active can bear; declined for a marker (18a, a voice anomaly that merely fails to construe).
- **0775C — an elided verb.** *qui prius de suis, recte judicant de dictis alienis*: the first
  clause has no verb of its own. The English repeats "judge", which the printed *judicant*
  recovers; recorded because it is a supply, however small.
- **0773B — `Jam non irascar tibi, et zelus meus recessit a te`** is quoted without guillemets and
  without a citation; it is Ez 16:42 recast (*requiescet indignatio mea in te et auferetur zelus
  meus a te*). Not marked: it is adduced as *dicit* to a sinning people, not as a proof-text, and
  Migne prints no quotation marks for it.
