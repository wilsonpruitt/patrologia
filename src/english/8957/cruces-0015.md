## Load-bearing vocabulary, fixed for this stint

Following the 0005–0009 stint's conventions except where noted; nothing here diverges from them.

- `*usque ad*` → **as far as**, in italics. Where the abridgment formula is broken by a column
  anchor (0788A, `*usque [0788B] ad*`) the anchor stays inside the italic run in the English too:
  `*as far [0788B] as*`.
- **This is a guillemet book and the words inside `« »` are TRANSLATED**, following 8950 Genesis
  and the majority of this work's shipped chunks (0013 among them). The marks themselves are
  reproduced 1:1 with the Latin twin, spacing included — translating a lemma is a substitution
  *inside* the existing marks, never a change to them. This covers second lemmata re-quoted
  mid-gloss (« and thou shalt be steadfast, », « who changeth the heart, », « where then is he, »,
  « true being ») and the Gloss's own guilleted paraphrase at 0788B (« Who shall contradict him?
  or, who can say to him why thou doest thus? »), whose second half is not scripture at all.
  The `VERS. n.-- ` address is kept exactly as printed, including 0793C `VERS. 23. «` and 0794B
  `VERS. 28. «`, which the plate sets without the `--`.
  ⚠ **An earlier draft of these five chunks left the lemmata untranslated.** That was wrong for
  this corpus and has been repaired; the guillemet counts (41/42 · 31/31 · 36/36 · 43/44 · 42/42)
  are unchanged by the repair, which is the check that proves no mark moved.
- **Truncated lemmata are rendered as the fragments they are, never completed** (Pattern 8's
  "never supply", at phrase scale). Where Migne cuts before the governing verb the English cuts
  with him: « Doth he who many things. » (0787C) · « A vain man unto pride. » (0788C) ·
  « And when thee consumed. » (0789A, twice) · « He himself shall reprove you, because. » (0792B) ·
  « Thinkest thou that a dead man again. » (0795D). These read abruptly because the plate is
  abrupt; the full verse is one click away in the Latin column.
- `flagellum/flagella` → **scourge(s)** · `percussio` → **stroke** · `elatio` → **elation** ·
  `tumor` → **swelling** · `reprobi` → **the reprobate** · `electi` → **the elect** ·
  `praedicatio` → **preaching** · `praedicator` → **preacher** · `solidatur/solidetur` →
  **is made solid** (carried from the peer stint, where the same verb runs through ch. V).
- `gentilitas` → **the Gentile world** · `gentiles` → **the Gentiles** (0791B, where the two stand
  in one sentence against *Judaea*).
- `districtio` → **strictness** · `animadversio` → **animadversion** (0793B, kept because the
  gloss is defining Migne's own word).
- `viriditas` → **greenness** (0795B, where the tree/just-man figure turns on it) · `coma` →
  **foliage**.
- **Pattern 17:** God is addressed in the grammatical singular throughout Job's speeches
  (*judices*, *quaeras*, *scisti*, *fecisti*, *parce*, *abscondis*, *privares*, *permittis*), so
  **thou/thee/thy**; Job's three friends are addressed in the plural (*vos estis*, *nostis*,
  *dicitis*, *taceretis*, *vestris fraudulentiis*), so **you/your**. The switch is visible in the
  English exactly where the Latin makes it.

## Vulgate divergences in the lemmata (7a″ walk) — the deliverable

Collated against `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt`, book chapter:verse cited. **Every entry
below is plate-confirmed**; the marks in the brief ordered the work but decided none of it.

### Material, and marked

1. **0791D** `« Secundum scientiam veram. »` ‖ Clem Job 13:2 `Secundum scientiam vestram et ego
   novi`. **Plate-confirmed at native resolution (f396 left column, clean type, no broken sort):
   Migne prints *veram*.** The divergence is material and the exposition proves it: the gloss two
   lines later reads *et secundum eorum scientiam se nosse confirmat* — "according to **their**
   knowledge" — which is *vestram*, not *veram*. So the page's own argument rests on a word the
   lemma no longer carries. **Rendered as printed — « According to true knowledge. », never "your
   knowledge" —** and marked
   `[var: Vulg. Job 13:2 *vestram*, "according to your knowledge"]`. Pattern 18a routes a
   divergence inside quoted scripture to Pattern 14, never to `[cj:]`. **The only marker fired in
   this stint.**

### Real divergences, recorded, no marker

2. **0786C** `« Pelle et carnalibus. »` ‖ Clem Job 10:11 `Pelle et carnibus vestisti me`.
   **Plate-confirmed at native resolution (f393 right column).** *carnalibus* for *carnibus* is a
   real lexical divergence, and unlike (1) it is **internally consistent**: the gloss expounds it
   at once as *carnalium motuum obsidione mens vallatur*. The sense is not turned against the
   author, so no `[var:]` — but **the English must not read "flesh"**: the lemma is rendered
   « With skin and carnal things. », which is what Migne printed, and the gloss's "carnal motions"
   then follows from it on the page.
3. **0788A** `« Et intelligeres quod multa minora. »` ‖ Clem Job 11:6 `quod multo minora exigaris`.
   Plate-confirmed f394. *multa* for *multo*, one letter and both sound Latin; the gloss is
   abridged past the point where it would decide. No marker.
4. **0788C** `« Si iniquitates. »` ‖ Clem Job 11:14 `Si iniquitatem quae est in manu tua abstuleris`.
   Plate-confirmed f394. Migne's lemma is **plural** where the Clementine is singular — and the
   gloss's own first words are singular, *Iniquitas in manu* — but the gloss is cut by `usque ad`
   immediately after, so nothing in the printed matter turns on the number. Recorded, no marker.
   (This is the brief's one ⚑ MATCHES-ONLY-ELSEWHERE line; the match at Jr 14:7 / Ps 129:3 is an
   accident of the plural form and not the source of the quotation.)
5. **0792A** `« Prius vos ostendam, »` and, re-quoted in the gloss,
   `« prius vos ostendam fabricatores; »` ‖ Clem Job 13:4 `prius vos ostendens fabricatores
   mendacii`. **Plate-confirmed f396, both occurrences.** *ostendam* (first person future) for
   *ostendens* (participle); Migne also stops short of *mendacii*, which the gloss then supplies
   in its own voice (*ita etiam mendacium sermonibus fabricatur*). Rendered as printed; no marker,
   because the clause construes and asserts nothing the Gloss is arguing against.
6. **0792A** `« Audite ergo correctionem meam. »` ‖ Clem Job 13:6 `Audite ergo correptionem meam`.
   **Plate-confirmed f396 at native resolution**, and Migne's text is internally consistent —
   *correctionem* prints twice more in the gloss immediately below (*prius correctionem post
   judicium intulit*, *nisi per correctionem*). A real variant reading, not a slip; rendered
   "correction" throughout. No `[var:]`.
7. **0795B** `« Et in pulvere truncus emoritur, »` ‖ Clem Job 14:8 `et in pulvere emortuus fuerit
   truncus illius`. Plate-confirmed f398. Different verb form and word order, same sense.
8. **0795C** `« ubi ergo est, »` ‖ Clem Job 14:10 `ubi, quaeso, est?`. Plate-confirmed f398.
   *ergo* for *quaeso*; rendered « where then is he, », never "where, I beseech thee". The gloss
   weaves the lemma into its own sentence, and the guilleted `« verum esse »` beside it
   (« true being ») is **the Gloss's own phrase, not scripture** — it does not stand in Job 14 at
   all (the brief's ✓ points at Dt 17:4, a different book).
9. **0796C** `« Attamen caro ejus dum vivit dolebit. »` ‖ Clem Job 14:22 `dum vivet`.
   Plate-confirmed f398. Present for future; the gloss uses the present (*qui spiritualiter
   vivunt*). No marker.
10. **0787A** `qui concepti non viderunt lumen` (unmarked, outside the guillemets) ‖ Clem Job 3:16
    `vel qui concepti non viderunt lucem`. Plate-confirmed f394. *lumen* for *lucem*; no marker,
    and nothing supplied — the phrase is not inside a lemma, so it is carried unmarked
    (Pattern 6: we do not add marks Migne does not print).
11. **0791C** `« Nonne hic est filius fabri? »` (closing an `usque ad` abridgment, unattributed)
    ‖ Clem Mt 13:55 `Nonne hic est fabri filius?`. Plate-confirmed f396. Word order only.
12. **0792C** `« Sola vexatio intellectum dabit auditui [n: (Isa. XXVIII)]: »` ‖ Clem Is 28:19
    `et tantummodo sola vexatio intellectum dabit auditui` — agrees, abridged at the head.

## Defects and near-defects — one candidate, withdrawn

- **0785C `negationem`** (in `Interrogatio haec negationem exigit`) — on the Gallica copy the sort
  after `negatio` is worn or over-inked and reads ambiguously at native resolution, with clean
  type on either side of it. **No marker, and none is possible:** our TEI carries the correct
  `negationem`, and a `[sic:]` must wrap type that appears verbatim in the Latin twin. The
  reading is also the only one the clause admits. Recorded as an inking artifact of this
  photograph, not a claim about Migne's type. **The archive.org second witness was not opened,
  because no outcome there could change either the Latin or the English.**
- **0790A `Id est hominis effectus intelligentiae spiritualis quae in Dei potestate sunt`** —
  plate-confirmed f395. Plural *quae … sunt* against singular *effectus*. **No marker:** English
  has no exponent for the disagreement ("which things are in the power of God" is the same
  rendering either way), and Pattern 9's boundary keeps a bare number/gender mismatch out of the
  apparatus. Rendered literally. Recorded so a later sweep does not churn it.
- **0791A `qui ante veraces Christum venturum nuntiaverunt, se praesentem hunc negaverunt`** —
  plate-confirmed f396. The `se` has no comfortable slot; the clause wants *sed* or a bare
  *praesentem hunc negaverunt*. **No marker** (the type is sound and *se* is a real word occupying
  a possible reflexive slot); rendered as literally as the printed words allow, "and they
  themselves denied this same one when present". Pattern 7. The conjecture *sed* is recorded here
  and deliberately not in the text.
- **0788B `Quia modum creaturae perennitate aeternitatis excedit`**, **0794D `et quem feriat
  videre`**, **0795A `et bona aeternae quietis ostende`** and **`pro recompensatione sui laboris
  accipit`**, **0790C `septiformem gratiam retulit`** — all five sentences end with **no terminal
  stop on the plate** (f394, f397, f398, f395). Pattern 8: nothing supplied; the English stops
  where the plate stops.
- **0786A `Sed cum hac dignitate, quae est per spiritum? est infirmitas per carnem`** — the
  mid-sentence `?` is **Migne's own** (plate f393). Preserved, not moved, not deleted. Pattern 8;
  this is not the 8b indirect-question case, because the mark is not being refused by English
  grammar — it simply stands where he set it.
- **0786D `usque ad postquam, de se dixit dona misericordiae generaliter, subdit:`** — the
  abridgment tail is elliptical where it resumes, as `usque ad` tails in this book routinely are.
  Rendered literally ("after he had said of himself the gifts of mercy generally, he adds:"),
  no marker, no supplied word.

## Considered and rejected

- **A `[d:]` at 0786B / 0789C / 0794B.** Three lemmata print twice in close succession —
  `« Nonne sicut lac, »` (0786B, twice), `« Ergo vos estis soli homines. »` (0789C, twice),
  `« Quantas habeo iniquitates »` and `« Contra folium »` (0793C/0793D announced, then re-quoted
  at 0794B). **None is a dittography.** In each case the plate (f393, f395, f397) sets the second
  occurrence as a fresh lemma opening a fresh gloss — the Gloss's ordinary habit, and the same
  finding the 0005–0009 stint recorded at 0768C. No `[d:]`.
- **A `[sic:]` or `[cj:]` on either unopened guillemet** (0787A, 0793C). Plate-confirmed as
  Migne's; the standing class. Nothing supplied, nothing marked.
- **A `[var:]` on `carnalibus` (0786C), `correctionem` (0792A) or `ostendam` (0792A).** All three
  are real divergences and all three are recorded above, but in each the printed lemma is
  internally consistent with the gloss built on it, so the reader is not being told something the
  author is arguing against. Pattern 14's bar is a divergence "in a way that changes the sense",
  not any divergence at all. `veram` at 0791D is the one in this range that clears it, and it
  clears it because the gloss contradicts the lemma on the page.
- **A `[cj:]` anywhere in this range.** No site meets Pattern 18's bar: no printed real word here
  makes the English assert something the Gloss is refuting, and no negation or correlative is
  lost or intruded. Every `non`, `nec`, `nisi`, `nihil`, `nemo`, `nunquam` and `nequaquam` the
  Latin prints is carried into the English, and none is added — including at 0792C, where
  *reprobis non timor requiem, sed poena timorem parit* keeps its negative and the sentence keeps
  its shape.
