## CHAPTER XIV

VERS. 2.-- *Dividing all things by lot,* etc. [n: (ISID.)] The nations being blotted out, Josue divides by lot the land of promise among the peoples: Christ also cast out from before the face of his faithful the nations, that is, the spirits of the errors of the Gentiles, and divided the land by lot, bestowing upon each one his proper gifts.

VERS. 3.-- *For to two tribes.* [n: (ID.)] Moses beyond the Jordan to two tribes only, *as far as* the perfection therefore and the sum of all good things is in the Holy Ghost.

*Except the Levites.* [n: (ID.)] The Levites do not receive an inheritance of land, etc., *as far as* that they may be able to say: *The Lord [0516C] is the portion of the inheritance,* etc. [n: (Psal. XV.)]

VERS. 4.-- *Neither did they receive.* [n: (ID.)] Dwellings for the Levites, etc., *as far as* who live by the dispensing of all things.

VERS. 6.-- *And he spoke. [n: (ADAM., hom. 18.)]* Caleb first received an inheritance from Jesus, *as far as* for he himself taught and opened all things; he himself revealed to Paul that the law was spiritual.

*Thou knowest.* [n: (ID.)] This is what the Lord said, etc., *as far as* because he wrote all things wisely.

VERS. 11.-- *So strong.* For the holy man is alike strong, both in things present and in things past; both in things new and in things old; in the Gospels and in the law. This therefore is what he says, that he is strong under Jesus as he was then with Moses, because in the mysteries of both Testaments a watchful heart [0516D] is strong.

VERS. 12.-- *Give therefore.* The holy man seeks nothing lowly, nothing abject, but a high mountain, in which are great and fortified cities, because the wise man seizes fortified cities, and destroys the fortifications in which the wicked trust.

*In which. [n: (ADAM., hom. XVIII.)]* Cities and walls are the doctrines of the wicked, etc., *as far as* as he was made the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, in Christ Jesus.

VERS. 15.-- *And the land ceased,* etc. [n: (ADAM., homil. 19 in Jos.)] This is not written in the distribution of Moses, but in the distribution of Jesus. If therefore thou wilt be made worthy to obtain an inheritance from Jesus, it behoves [0517A] thee first to finish all wars and to stand in peace, that of the land of thy flesh it may be said: *because the land hath ceased from wars.*

## CHAPTER XV.

VERS. 1.-- *The lot therefore of the children of Juda,* etc. Whereas it is the custom of Holy Scripture to name the four quarters of the world, which they call axes or regions, in the description of the borders of the tribe of Juda only two are named. *And the borders, he says, of the children of the tribe of Juda were, according to their peoples, from the borders of Idumaea, from the desert of Sin toward the west.* [var: Vulg. Jos 15:1 *a termino Edom, desertum Sin, contra meridiem*, "from the border of Edom, the desert of Sin, toward the south"] And when he had described the borders from the west, he says, *and from the east the salt sea,* but the north and the south he passed over in silence. The borders therefore of Judaea are next to the borders of Idumaea, which is interpreted earthly: for after earthly things there follow straightway the borders [0517B] of Judaea, but from the *desert, he says, of Sin,* that is, of temptations. After temptations therefore follow the borders of the inheritance of Juda. The East and the West are named as the neighbouring places which we spoke of above, that is, the confines. The valley of Egypt also is marked as neighbouring the western parts, which is from the desert toward the west. But from the east the borders are girded by the salt sea. After these things, from the hills, he says, which stretch toward the west, and thence one goes up from the west as far as Cadesbarne, and so it is stretched out from the west to Cades. These are their borders from Africa, and thence it went on as far as the valley of Egypt. See how in the parts of the west he names Africus, Egypt and the sunset, that every man may know that these must be passed over by him, and so at last that he comes to the royal tribe, from which our Lord arose. But from the borders [0517C] of Idumaea is the desert of Sin; therefore it is necessary that we pass through the wilderness of temptations, that we may be able to come into the inheritance of the children of Juda. It behoves us also to go up the ascent of the hills of Accabin, which is interpreted scorpions, because scorpions must be passed by us and trodden upon, of which he writes: *Behold, I have given you power to tread upon serpents and scorpions,* etc. [n: (Luc. X.)] He therefore who wishes to enter into the inheritance of the tribe of Juda, let him go up these ascents, let him tread upon and escape these scorpions. Perhaps it was to Ezechiel, as he made this journey, that the Lord was saying, *Son of man, thou dwellest in the midst of scorpions [n: (Ezech. II)]*. And the borders, he says, are as far as Cadesbarne. Cades is interpreted holy or sanctification. The borders therefore of Juda reach as far as sanctification. It is said also that from the eastern parts [0517D] of Juda is the salt sea, and after these things in its portion is named the fountain of the sun, and the city of the sun, and in the same tribe there is also the city of letters. It behoves therefore every one who wishes to enter into the portion of the children of Juda to pass first the salt sea, that is, to overcome the waves and the whirlwinds of this world, and to escape all the things of this world which, for their uncertainty and slipperiness, are compared to the waves of the sea, that he may be able to come to the land of Judaea, and to the fountain of the Sun of justice, which is *a fountain of water springing up into life everlasting,* and afterwards to find the city of the Sun of justice. There is also in Egypt a city of the sun, but that one has received the name of this sun which the heavenly Father bids to rise upon the good and the bad [n: (Matth. V)]. But that which is in Judaea is made [the city] of the saints only, because [0518A] it is the city of God, and that fountain aforesaid, being made a river, makes glad the city of God [n: (Psal. XLV)]. Thou also, if thou be fortified on every side, and encompassed with the wall of continence, strengthened with the towers of patience and magnanimity, shalt be made the city of God. But if to this thou shalt have added for thyself the light of the knowledge of God, and the Sun of justice shall have shone upon thee, thou shalt be made the city of the sun, or shalt inherit by lot the city of the sun. But if thou shalt study in the law of God day and night, and the book of the law shall not have departed from thy hand, thine inheritance shall be made the city of letters.

[n: (ADAM., hom. 20 in Jos.)] Not a little profit is conferred upon the soul, etc., *as far as* but when one day and a second and a third have gone by, in its own time, through hidden passages, the power of the food or of the drink, carried back to the sight, little by little cleanses the vision.

[0518B]VERS. 13.-- *But Caleb. [n: (ADAM., hom. 20.)]* The beginning of the distribution of Jesus, etc., *as far as* Note that Caleb is called the son of Jephone, and Othoniel the son of Cenez, who is Caleb the younger, that is, his kinsman.

VERS. 18.-- *Sitting on an ass. To whom. [n: (GREG., lib. III Dialog., c. 34.)]* Axa sits upon an ass, etc., *as far as* that the upper watered ground should be set first, and the lower afterwards.

VERS. 63.-- *But the children of Juda could not destroy the Jebusite the inhabitant of Jerusalem. [n: (ADAM., hom. 11 in Jos.)]* We understand spiritually that the Jebusite dwelt with the Jews in Jerusalem unto the present day according to the parable of the Gospel, etc., *as far as* these the children of Juda could not cast out of Jerusalem unto the present day.

[0518C] *The Jebusite,* that is, a treading down, namely those who make a scandal in the Church, and who, having received the knowledge of the mysteries, tread down by assailing them the pearls of the word of God.

*[n: (ORIG. ubi supra.)] Jerusalem* is interpreted the vision of peace, etc., *as far as* but they cannot all be cast out at once.

*Unto the present day,* that is, the everlastingness of the age, after the manner of Scripture. But this cannot stand according to the letter: for how does the Jebusite dwell with the children of Juda in Jerusalem so long as this age lasts, when not even the children of Juda dwell there for ever?

## CHAPTER XVI.

VERS. 1.-- *There fell,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 19 in Jos.)] [0518D] It follows. VERS. 10.-- *And the Chanaanite dwelt.* And the Chanaanite dwelt in Ephrem, etc., *as far as* Whence it appears to have been inserted by the LXX, who knew that it had been done.

[n: (GREG., lib. IV Moral., c. 22.)] The Chanaanite, that is, the gentile people etc., *as far as* that of itself it does not subdue greater things.

[n: (ADAM., hom. 21 in Jos.)] Ephrem is interpreted *fruitfulness,* etc., *as far as* He subjected the Chanaanites to the children of Ephrem.

## CHAPTER XVII.

VERS. 12.-- *Neither could they. [n: (ADAM., hom. 22 in Jos.)]* The Chanaanites are said a third time to be among the children of Ephrem, [0519A] etc., *as far as* the Chanaanite is said to be destroyed by us.

[n: (ADAM., nom. 22.)] That also is to be passed over, etc., *as far as* he transfers it as a dowry.

VERS. 14.-- *And they spoke. [n: (ORIG., hom. 22.)]* After these things it must be seen, etc., *as far as* that the natural vigour of inborn virtue may shine forth.

*We shall not be able to go up to the mountains. [n: (ADAM., ibid.)]* The mountain is not enough for us, etc., *as far as* they make Chanaanites out of Israelites.
