## CHAPTER XI.

[n: (AUG., quaest. 49 in Jud.)] Jephte is interpreted *opening,* etc., *as far as* because they do not keep the precepts of the law and do not show faith to him as to a husband.

VERS. 3.-- *He dwelt in the land of Tob. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* In a land, namely, good, or rather best. For what in Greek is ἀγάθον, this in Latin is called *optimum.* And this is what Tob is interpreted, where the resurrection seems to be understood. For what land is more the best than the earthly body clothed with the excellence of immortality and of incorruption.

[0529C] [n: (ID., ibid.)] Before the passion he ate with publicans and sinners, etc., *as far as* that they may be justified and may learn his ways.

*In those days. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* Those who had cast off Christ, etc., *as far as* that by old age the last times may be signified.

VERS. 8.-- *And the princes said. [n: (AUG., ibid.)]* Some such thing was figured in Joseph, etc., *as far as* of which kingdom of his there shall be no end.

*And that thou fight.* [n: (AUG.)] That Jephte is sought as leader against the children of Ammon, at whose overthrow those might be set free who desired to make war under him as leader: because Ammon is interpreted 'son of my people', or 'people of mourning'; either those enemies are signified who out of that same nation remained in unbelief; or all who are predestined to hell: *where there shall be [0529D] weeping and gnashing of teeth,* as to a people of mourning. Although the devil and his angels are fittingly understood as the people of mourning: either because they procure eternal misery for those whom they deceive, or because they themselves are appointed to eternal misery.

VERS. 9.-- *I will be your prince. [n: (ID., ibid.)]* That is, a king whom that nation did not yet have in the time of the judges, etc., *as far as* but Jephte signified him who is the true king.

[n: (ID.)] A king, namely, as it is written in the title of the cross, etc., *as far as* like those who are contained in the books of Kings.

*Are not the things which he possesseth,* etc. Are not whatsoever things Chamos hath given thee to inherit? [var: Vulg. Jdc. 11:24 *nonne ea quae possidet Chamos deus tuus, tibi jure debentur?*, "are not the things which Chamos thy god possesseth due to thee by right?"]

[0530A]VERS. 29.-- *And going about Galaad. [n: (AUG., quaest. 49 in Jud.)]* Galaad is interpreted *casting away,* etc., *as far as* which he signifies by saying also *from the watch-tower of Galaad of passing over and passing over to the children of Ammon.* [var: Vulg. Jdc. 11:29 *Maspha quoque Galaad, et inde transiens ad filios Ammon*, "Maspha also of Galaad, and passing thence to the children of Ammon"]

VERS. 30.-- *If thou wilt deliver the children of Ammon. [n: (AUG., ibid.)]* Whomsoever Jephte may have thought of, he does not seem to have thought of his daughter; for he would not have said: *alas for me! my daughter, thou hast hindered me,* etc. [var: Vulg. Jdc. 11:35 *Heu me, filia mea! decepisti me, et ipsa decepta es*, "Alas me, my daughter! thou hast deceived me, and thou thyself art deceived"] For he shows [sic: *In icat*] himself to have been hindered, so that he might not fulfil that which he had thought of. But whom could he think of, who did not have sons? Or perhaps he thought of his wife, and God willed not that this should come to pass, and that he should not leave this unpunished; lest anyone afterwards should dare it: and that by a great providence, out of that which happened, a great mystery of the Church might be figured. From both, therefore, was the prophecy fitted? namely, what he thought of in vowing, [0530B] and what befell him unwilling. For if he thought of his wife, the wife of Christ is the Church. But because the wife of this Jephte could not be a virgin, in that his daughter rather met him, the rashness of the vow did not remain unavenged, and the virginity of the Church was figured, to whom it is said: *Daughter, thy faith hath made thee whole, go in peace [n: (Luc. VII)]*. [var: Vulg. Lc. 7:50 *Fides tua te salvam fecit: vade in pace*, "Thy faith hath made thee safe: go in peace" — *Filia* stands at Lc. 8:48, not at Lc. 7:50]

[n: (AUG., quaest. 49.)] Concerning the daughter of Jephte there is wont to be a great question, etc., *as far as* so that all the things which were done afterwards may seem to be understood as works of the Spirit of the Lord who was made upon him.

[n: (ID.)] But among praiseworthy men, etc., *as far as* that it may be altogether avoided, but only that it may be deferred, he labours.

[n: (ISID., in Jud., tom. V.)] Jephte signifies Christ, because departing from the face of his brethren, that is, of the Jews, he received the princedom among the Gentiles, because [0530C] he fulfilled all the mysteries of human salvation as one under oath: and he offered his own flesh, as it were a daughter, for the salvation of Israel. And he swore to the Father and vowed according to that word: *As he swore to the Lord, he vowed a vow to the God of Jacob,* namely, that he would fulfil the mystery of religion in the passion of his own flesh for the salvation of the human race. These things therefore were so done through Christ that they seem to have been completed for the religion of the oath.

VERS. 37-39.-- *And I will bewail,* etc. *And he did to her,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] A chaste virgin, that is, the Church, etc., *as far as* on account of whom the two testaments also are so called.

*From thence the custom grew. [n: (AUG., quaest. 49.)]* But as to what was done by ordinance in Israel, that from days to days they came together to lament the daughter of Jephte, four days in the year, I do not think that it signifies anything after the completed [0530D] holocaust, which shall be in eternal life, but the past times of the Church, in which the mourners were blessed. By the four days its universality was figured: because of the four parts of the world through which it is spread far and wide. But as to the proper sense of the history, I do not think that the Israelites decreed this, except because they understood that in her the judgment of God had been shown rather for the striking of the father, lest anyone thereafter should vow such a sacrifice. For why was a lamentation decreed, if that vow is a vow of gladness?

## CHAPTER XII.

VERS. 7.-- *So Jephte judged,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] As those two months, on account of the seventy days, signify the senary number of the six ages, so here [0531A] the sevenfold multiplied six times figures the same thing, so far as it pertains to the six ages of the world; for six times seven are forty-two, nor was it in vain that Jephte himself judged the people six years. [cj: *septuaginta*; read *sexaginta*, "sixty"]

## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 2.-- *But thou shalt conceive,* etc. [n: (ISID., in Jud.)] Samson has certain things as a type of Christ, etc., *as far as* and it was said of Christ also by the prophet: *There shall arise to you the sun of justice, and health in his wings.*

VERS. 3.-- *Nor shalt thou eat any unclean thing. [n: (AUG., quaest. 50.)]* The unclean thing is the dissolution of discipline which had begun to be in Israel, and had already made them fall away to the eating of those things which God had forbidden among the animals: for already they were passing over even to the worship of idols.

[n: (AUG., quaest. 52 in Jud.)] As to what the woman says was told her by the angel, etc., *as far as* the Nazarites observed on certain days, [0531B] making their vows and paying their vow.

VERS. 19.-- *And so Manue took. [n: (AUG., quaest. 53.)]* As to what Scripture says, that Manue was ignorant that it was an angel of the Lord, etc., *as far as* that he should be delivered to his enemies for forty years.

VERS. 20.-- *And when they had seen it. [n: (GREG., lib. V Moral., cap. 23.)]* What is it that at the vision of the angel the man is timid, the woman bold, unless that when heavenly things are shown to us, the spirit indeed shakes itself with dread, but yet hope presumes. For from that source hope raises itself to the daring of greater things, from which the spirit is troubled that sees first the things which are above, because when the mind, thus lifted up, beholds the loftier things of the heavenly secrets, all the solidity of human strength trembles.

[0531C] [n: (AUG., quaest. 54 in Jud.)] What is it that, after the angel was made manifest to Manue and to his wife, etc., *as far as* that he would not receive a sacrifice, but would himself be the sacrifice to come.

## CHAPTER XIV.

VERS. 5.-- *And so Samson went down,* etc. [n: (ISID., in Jud. tom. V, cap. 8.)] When Samson was making his way to the strangers for the sake of seeking a wife, he slew a lion: and Christ, about to call the Church from among the Gentiles, overcame the devil, he who says: *Rejoice, for I have overcome the world [n: (Joan. XVI)]*. [var: Vulg. Jo. 16:33 *sed confidite, ego vici mundum*, "but have confidence, I have overcome the world"] Out of the mouth of the slain lion a honeycomb is drawn, because, as we behold, the kings of the earthly kingdom, who before raged against Christ, now, their savagery destroyed, preach the sweetness of the Gospel, and afford it defences.

## CHAPTER XV.

VERS. 8.-- *And he smote them,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 55 in Jud.)] LXX: Samson smote the strangers [0532A] leg upon thigh, that is, so wonderfully that in their amazement they set the leg of the one foot upon the thigh of the other.

VERS. 12.-- *Swear ye. [n: (AUG., quaest. 56 in Jud.)]* What is it that Samson says to the men of Juda, etc., *as far as* unless they be learned by hearing or by reading, so neither are the modes of speech.

VERS. 16.-- *With the jawbone. [n: (GREG., lib. XIII Moral., cap. 6.)]* The jawbone of the ass signifies the preachers. For our Redeemer, holding in the hand of his power the simplicity and the patience of those who preach, slew the carnal from their vices, and, the jawbone being cast upon the ground, afterwards poured forth waters: because the bodies of the preachers, delivered over to death, showed forth great miracles to the peoples.
