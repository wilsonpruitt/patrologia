## THE PROLOGUE OF JEROME ON THE BOOK OF JUDITH.

[n: ( Vide inter opera beati Hieronymi, tom. VIII. )]

## PREFACE.

[0731D]

[n: (RAB. expos. in lib. Judith, tom. III.)] It is asked at what time and under what kings the history of Judith took place, etc., *as far as* unless perhaps it be said that Cambyses, who is said to have assailed the neighbouring nations and to have overcome Egypt, since the kingdom of the Assyrians and of the Persians was one, overcame some king named Arphaxat resisting him in Media, and subdued [sic: *subju averit*] him to his own empire.

## CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- « Arphaxat. » [n: (RAB., ubi supra. )] Mystically, by Arphaxat the proud are expressed, whose endeavour and labour, which is carried on through the vastness of swelling and of elation, falls easily into the power of Nabuchodonosor, that is, of the devil: for he himself is king over all the sons [0732D] of pride: « Which he named. » Which is the metropolis of the province of Media, which Deiocus king of the Medes founded, but Arphaxat wondrously enlarged, even as Daniel the prophet, according to Josephus, under king Darius set in that same city of Media a mausoleum exceedingly famous, built with wondrous art, which, on whatever day it is beheld, is thought to have been built on that same day, because its beauty is thus fresh and its material solid. There unto this day the kings of the Persians and of the Medes are buried, and he to whom this charge shall be committed is a Jewish priest.

« In glory. » These in chariots and these in horses, etc. But God cast the chariot of Pharao and his army into the sea: therefore he that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord.

VERS. 6.-- « In the great plain. » [n: (RAB., ubi supra. )] By the names of places, etc., *as far as* and at length they yielded the victories to the Chaldeans or the Assyrians.

[0733A] « Then was it exalted. » When the devil subjects the multitude of the lost to his own will, his heart is lifted up, ascribing that to his own power, not to the divine permission: and he burns the more hotly for the destruction of very many lost ones, the more he sees himself prevailing over certain men through depraved suggestion, whence follows.

« And he sent to all. » [n: (RAB., ibid.)] The devil sends legates through the several provinces, etc., *as far as* whence: « All with one mind contradicted, etc. »

VERS. 12.-- « Then Nabuchodonosor being indignant. » The devil, blinded by his own pride, arrogates to himself the empire of the whole world, striving that, having drawn men away from the worship of piety, he may make them partakers of his own impiety: who, exulting over the power of his kingdom, saith: « I will ascend into heaven, and I will set my seat in the north, and I will be like [0733B] [sic: *milis*] the Most High. » [var: Vulg. Isa. 14:13–14 *In caelum conscendam, super astra Dei exaltabo solium meum… sedebo in monte testamenti, in lateribus aquilonis*, "I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God… in the sides of the north"] And in the Gospel: « All these things will I give thee, if falling down thou wilt adore me. »

## CHAPTER II.

VERS. 7.-- « Then Holofernes. [n: (RAB., in lib. Judith, tom. III.)] That Holofernes signifieth the princes of the nations who persecuted the Church, etc., *as far as* that all should adore the dragon, who gave such power to the beast.

VERS. 12.-- « And when he had passed through. » [n: (ID., ibid.)] By the several provinces, and by the names of the places which are contained in the history, the distinctions of persons and of degrees and of dignities are designated, out of which the devil claims for himself some part, nor does he dread the difficulty of the battle, but strives to work a great [0733C] ruin of a great power.

VERS. 13.-- « And he plundered all. » Taking away the profit of knowledge, and the other things by which they ought to have served God: and turning them to the use of his own service: he [the devil] slays them that resist with the edge of the sword, because those whom he cannot bend to consent, he slays bodily.

VERS. 17.-- « He went down into the plains. » Damascus is interpreted a draught of blood, in which are expressed the princes of the nations, who thirst for the blood of the faithful: chiefly in the time of harvest, that is, in the consummation of the age, when the fury of Antichrist rages through the breadth of the world.

## CHAPTER III.

VERS. 1.-- « Then they sent [sic: *mise unt*], » etc. The mighty of this world and the lovers of pleasures, who study to reconcile to themselves the prince of the world [0733D], that they may escape the peril of death and the loss of pleasure; of whom it is said: « He who willeth to be a friend of this world is made an enemy of God. » [var: Vulg. Jas 4:4 *quicumque ergo voluerit amicus esse saeculi hujus*, "whosoever therefore will be a friend of this world"] For in these the care [sic: *solli itudo*] of this world, and the deceitfulness [sic: *fallaci*] of riches choketh the word, and it maketh no fruit, for these, according to the names of these provinces, are lifted up with pride, they follow vanity: they enter the earth by two ways, and they shall be joined to the company of them that mourn, or that pay the penalties of their own luxury, in hell: where they shall pass from the snow waters to excessive heat, « and their worm shall not die, and the fire shall not be quenched. »

## CHAPTER IV.

VERS. 13.-- *Be ye mindful.* By the examples of the saints [0734A] [Eliachim] strengthens them, whence Job [n: (Cap. XII)]: *Thou renewest witnesses against me,* and Paul to the Hebrews, praising the power of faith, brought in copious examples of the fathers, who by faith conquered kingdoms, wrought justice.

## CHAPTER V.

VERS. 5.-- « Then Achior the leader, » etc. As it were a prince of heretics, anyone [sic: *qui ibet*] given over to idolatry, who has learned of the divine works and miracles which he often tells to others, although he has not perfectly learned the catholic faith, and has not been born again by the baptism of Christ. Or by Achior heretics are designated, who, although they hold not in all things the way of truth, yet in their teaching preach many true things which agree with our faith. These fight against the catholic Church, but, overcome by reason, they do not conceal the truth at all, for heretics [0734B] mingle good things with bad; because if they always spoke evil things, they could not lie hid; as he who offers a cup of poison touches the rim of the chalice with honey, that what is sweet may first be perceived, lest what is deadly be feared. Sometimes, however, being corrected [sic: *corre ti*] they are saved, as Achior, seeing the victory of Judith, being joined to the people of God, circumcised the flesh of his foreskin: the lepers of the Gospel also express heretics, who, while they insert crooked things among right ones, as it were sprinkle a sound colour with spots.

## CHAPTER VI.

VERS. 10.-- « Moreover the children of Israel coming down from Bethulia came. » The children: the teachers of the Church loose him that is bound to the tree, when they teach their catechumens [0734C] to dread neither the rage of persecutors nor death. They loose him as it were bound to the tree, when they pluck the fearful mind from the dread of the cross, and instruct it unto suffering, and this is better done if (by the example of Ozias and Carmin, who, comforting Achior, poured out devout prayers with all the people to the Lord) the masters of the Church with the rest of the faithful commend their hearers to the Lord with devout prayers, that by his gift that may be had which human weakness does not deserve.

VERS. 19.-- « And he made him a great supper. » He makes it when the fast is fulfilled, who refreshes with evangelical doctrine and with the banquets of the virtues a soul long languishing and lacking the bread of the word of the Lord. To this banquet all the priests are called, that by their exhortations and examples the neophytes may be strengthened for receiving [0734D] or for keeping the faith.

## CHAPTER VII

VERS. 7.-- « Yet there were fountains not far from the walls. » As in the book of Kings the Philistines take away the ironsmiths, lest they should make for the Hebrews either a spear or a sword. And all Israel went down to the Philistines, to sharpen the ploughshare and the mattock and the axe and the hoe. For this above all the devil is eager, that he may take away the streams of doctrine and the arms of the virtues, and so may swiftly accomplish his wickedness unto the destruction of the servants of God: hence the princes of the nations and Julian the apostate forbid Christians not only divine but also human philosophy.

VERS. 12.-- « Then being gathered together unto Ozias. » Carnal men [0735A] who say « Lord, Lord, but their heart is far from me [n: (Matth. VII, XV)]. » For there are in the net of the Lord both good and bad fishes, even to the shore of the judgement to come, who, bearing heavily the discomforts of the present life, had rather use present delights than that heavenly goods be reserved for them in the time to come, who afflict their masters with importunate complaints, and force them to consent to the luxury of the world, whence follows:

VERS. 23.-- « And these five days, » etc. Five days, the five senses of the body, by which the present life is led, as it were a truce of five days the sluggish teacher demands, who without discrimination promises his hearers a bodily vice [cj: *vitium*; read *vitae*, "the bodily solace of life"] for a solace, as though the magnificence of the highest Giver were in his own power, whereas rather the time of bestowing, and the manner, consists in the power of the giver rather than of the receiver [0735B]: for if the solace of the present life is denied, they forsake their subjects, that, yielding to persecutions, they may avoid bodily punishment. This compact our Judith, that is, the Church, rejects and despises.
