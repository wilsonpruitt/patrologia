## CHAPTER ONE.

VERS. 1.-- *He called,* etc. [n: (ISICH. in Levit. )] By a conjunction he joins what follows to what precedes; the beginning, namely, of this book with the end of the preceding: for the work of one and the same day is contained in both. The book of Numbers too, because it has nothing intervening between itself and Leviticus, obtains a like proem; that is: *And the Lord spoke to Moses in the desert of Sinai,* etc. [n: (Num. I)]. Nothing, therefore, did the people do after the history of Leviticus and before the history of Numbers: for it had not changed its place, because it had not yet [0299A] dedicated the tabernacle, nor had much time gone by. For in the first month of the second year, at the beginning of that same month, they began to act. But in Exodus and Genesis, because much intervening time had flowed by, that of the death of Joseph and of the going forth of the sons of Israel, a conjunction does not hold the opening, but a beginning suited to their dispensation is dispensed: *These are the names of the sons of Israel, who went into Egypt.*

[n: (ISICH.)] According to the Seventy, *He called back.* Just as, according to them, God *called back* Beseleel by name, when he commanded the tabernacle to be made according to the figure which was shown on the mount: so now too he has commanded a certain image of the intelligible sacrifices to be described; for this calling back intimates [0299B] a certain dispensation of things or of words looking to the things above.

*[n: (ORIG. hom. 1 in Levit.)] And he called,* etc. In the beginning, etc., *as far as* those who bring clean inward ears to the hearing of these things.

[n: (ISICH. in Levit. )] That God does not love the sacrifices of the Jews, etc., *as far as* and openly this calling intimates the dispensation of words or of things looking to the things above.

[n: (ORIG. homil. 1 Exod.)] The man whom he first set to offer to the Lord, etc., *as far as* the sacrifice too is twofold, which saves both earthly things and heavenly.

[n: (ID., ibid.)] The man who, made to the image and likeness of God, lives reasonably, etc., *as far as* and his neck is as a turtledove's.

[0299C] [n: (ISICH. in Levit. )] He designates the simple man, etc., *as far as* salvation is granted even to beasts of burden.

[n: (ID.)] God requires sacrifice that it may profit us, not himself, etc., *as far as* which at the last may speak testimony for us or against us.

VERS. 3.-- *Of the herd,* etc. [n: (ISID.)] The first sacrifice is the calf, that is, Christ, *of the herd,* that is, begotten of the stock of the patriarchs, who with the plough of his cross subdued the earth of our flesh, and enriched the seed of the Holy Spirit with the fruit of the virtues.

[n: (ORIG., ut supra. )] The proud calf, our flesh, etc., *as far as* but the oil of charity and of peace and of the other virtues they had not.

*To appease,* etc. But according to the Seventy he says this to be acceptable to the offerer, namely, if indeed it be to us [0299D] truly acceptable, and our heart, judging rightly and cleaving to no passion, shall have approved the gift as worthy to be received: then we can pray in the sight of the Lord, that is, obtain remission. Whence before him we shall persuade our hearts, because if our heart reprove us, we know that God is greater than our heart, and knoweth all things, etc.

VERS. 4.-- *And he shall put,* etc. What we call a victim is in Greek called καρπώμα, in which the intelligible holocaust is signified. For he fructifies spiritually who by a spiritual manner of life is made a holocaust: for which cause he who offers puts his hand upon the head of the victim, which signifies the beginning of the aforesaid manner of life, of which he who offers is himself to himself the cause of the promise [0300A]. For he offers of his own accord, because it is a gift, and every gift is voluntary. For if he shall have transgressed [sic: *trangressus*] his promise, he accuses himself, when he shall have paid the penalties. And so he will be acceptable, and profiting toward his own expiation, when by the placing of the hand he shall have made it manifest that he offered of his own accord, not of necessity.

VERS. 5.-- *And he shall immolate,* etc. [n: (ISICH. in Levit. )] For not only our own conscience and manner of life ought we to offer to God, etc., *as far as* as Christ is called the door, so is the apostolic doctrine which he bestows.

*The sons of Aaron.* [n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] Of the race of Aaron were Annas and Caiaphas, and the others who pronounced Christ to be guilty of death, and poured out his blood [0300B] at the base of the altar: for there was he slain, where the altar was and its base, whence: *It cannot be that a prophet perish outside Jerusalem.*

VERS. 6.-- *And being drawn off,* etc. [n: (ORIG., ibid.)] The priest draws off the skin, etc., *as far as* he joined earthly things to heavenly and human things to divine.

(ID) He commands the entrails with the feet to be washed with water, in signification of baptism. He washes the entrails who purges his conscience; he washes the feet who receives the consummation of the sacrament. *For he who is clean needeth not save to wash his feet, but is clean wholly [n: (Joan. XV)]*: [var: Vulg. Jn 13:10 has *Qui lotus est*, "he that is washed," where Migne prints *Qui enim mundus est*] nor can anyone have part with Jesus, unless he wash his feet.

[n: (ISICH. in Levit. )] The skin is drawn off when we are despoiled of riches and of whatsoever things are worldly, etc., *as far [0300C] as* imitating Christ so far as we can.

[n: (GREG. lib. I Moral., cap. 40.)] We draw off the skin of the victim, etc., *as far as* let him lay nothing slippery upon the altar of his prayer.

VERS. 10.-- *But if,* etc. [n: (ISICH. in Levit. )] With a few things changed he says the same, etc., *as far as* whence: The son shall not bear the iniquity of the father, etc.

*A yearling,* etc. [n: (ISID.)] The lamb offered is Christ the innocent crucified. Whence: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who taketh away the sins of the world.* [var: Vulg. Jn 1:29 has the singular *peccatum*, "the sin of the world"] Well is a kid offered, because through the death of Christ the devil, the author of sin, is slain.

[n: (ISICH.)] This he did not command concerning oxen, etc., *as far as* therefore it is called a holocaust even without the working [0300D] of justice.

VERS. 11.-- *At the side,* that is, the altar looks toward the Gentile world, because the passion of Christ was celebrated for the Gentiles. But we offer the sacrifice at the northern part of the altar, while we mindfully believe the passion of Christ to have been wrought for the Gentiles.

VERS. 13.-- *He shall burn all things.* He commands the offerer to place all these things upon the altar, that we may fit our whole man to the spiritual scriptures which have been spoken concerning the intelligible altar of the Lord's body, and may study to live to the imitation of him.

*And the odor.* When our sacrifice becomes a holocaust, and we offer all things upon the intelligible altar, as imitators of Christ conforming the manner [0301A] of our life to him, then is there offered to the Lord a most sweet odor.

VERS. 14.-- *But if.* [n: (ISID.)] The turtledove, the flesh of Christ, according to that saying of Solomon: *Fair are thy cheeks as a turtledove's:* the dove signifies the Holy Spirit. Whence: *Upon whom thou shalt see the Spirit descending as a dove, this is my Son.* [var: Vulg. Jn 1:33 continues *et manentem super eum, hic est qui baptizat in Spiritu Sancto*, "and remaining upon him, this is he that baptizeth in the Holy Spirit"] The turtledove, therefore, the dove, is the flesh of Christ joined to the Spirit: these three sacrifices man offers, that is, Christ, namely of oxen, of cattle, and of birds.

(ISICH. *in Levit.* Wonderful is the subtlety of the words, etc., *as far as,* they contemplate and savor heavenly things, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God.
