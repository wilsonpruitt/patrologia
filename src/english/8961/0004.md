## CHAPTER IV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1-2.-- *And he spoke* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *The soul that shall have sinned,* etc. [n: (ORIG., hom. 2 in Levit.)] Rightly does he say "the soul," etc., *as far as* and the spirit is a leader unto virtue, if he be willing to follow it.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISICH.)] *When a soul shall have sinned,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The lawgiver omitted nothing necessary, etc., *as far as* whence, *He who knew not sin was made sin for us.* [var: Vulg. 2 Cor 5:21 reads *eum qui non noverat peccatum pro nobis peccatum fecit*, "him who knew no sin he made to be sin for us," where Migne's verb is passive]

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *If the priest,* etc. (ORIG. ⟦s2⟧ It is to be observed that in the sin of the high priest the lawgiver does not add [0306A] that he sinned unknowing or unwilling: for neither can ignorance fall upon him who has been advanced that he might teach the rest.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] *If the priest,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Terror and mercy together, etc., *as far as* who is gnawed by the consciousness of his infirmity.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (Id.)] *If the priest,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The high priest, the sense of piety and of religion, etc., *as far as* is repaired by the death of Christ.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *A calf without blemish to the Lord,* etc. [n: (ID.)] In the second place we find a calf offered by the high priest for a holocaust, etc. ⟦s2⟧ *as far as* in which Christ suffered.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *He shall also take of the blood,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He bore the blood of the calf into the tabernacle, that is, into heaven: when after the resurrection, ascending the heavens, [0306B] he stood before the countenance of the Father, as it were offering the blood of the passion, making intercession for us.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Seven times:* for the remission, namely, of sins, and under the ministration of spiritual gifts. ⟦s2+3⟧ But the blood signifies the passion, according to that saying: *I am clean from the blood of this just man.* [var: Vulg. Mt 27:24 reads *Innocens ego sum*, "I am innocent"] And elsewhere: *His blood be upon us,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Concerning this sprinkling Peter says: *Unto obedience and the sprinkling of the blood of Christ,* etc.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The veil,* the humanity of Christ. ⟦s2⟧ For as the veil was between the holy of holies and the outer tabernacle, so the flesh of Christ is between us, who are the outer tabernacle, and the divinity, which is signified by the holy of holies. ⟦s3⟧ Against the veil of the testimony, therefore, the priest sprinkled the blood of the calf, because Christ [0306C] made his own flesh bloody with the blood of the passion.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Against the veil,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ By many names does he call the flesh of the Lord, because its operation is manifold. ⟦s3⟧ Whence the Lord himself named himself, now a sacrifice [n: (Luc. XXI)], now a shepherd, again a vine, then the bread of life and the grain of wheat, by transferred words. [n: (Joan. X, XV, VI, XII)] So Moses too called his flesh a veil. ⟦s4⟧ Whence Paul: *He hath consecrated for us a way through the veil, that is, his flesh [n: (Heb. X)]*. ⟦s5⟧ The veil of the sanctuary too is named from this, because in it the flesh of the holy of holies is figured.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Upon the horns.* ⟦s2⟧ This altar signifies the seat of God, that is, the Seraphim; whence Isaiah: *There flew to me one of the Seraphim, and in his hand a coal, which he took with the tongs from the altar [n: (Isa. VI)]*. ⟦s3⟧ The incense, according to [0306D] the Seventy, the compositions of all the virtues. ⟦s4⟧ For where does it befit the virtues to be composed, if not in the seat of God? for from heaven they descend to us. ⟦s5⟧ Whence James says: *But the wisdom that is from above [n: (Jac. III)]*. ⟦s6⟧ But four virtues, wisdom, justice, fortitude, temperance, because they contain the kinds of the other virtues, are named horns: these God anoints with his own blood, strengthening them, namely with a greater and more perfect strength, that they may be able to prevail against the adverse powers and forces.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the altar,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Of the angelic powers. ⟦s3⟧ For if men render to God a pleasing odor of good works, how much more the holy angels, remaining in the purity of their nature? ⟦s4⟧ But if they did not commit the sin which was to be expiated by the blood of Christ, [0307A] yet through his blood they were made at peace with us, and their number was repaired.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Unto the base.* ⟦s2⟧ The base, the lower part of the altar, signifies weak men. ⟦s3⟧ For by the blood of Christ not only was the number of the angels restored, but man also redeemed: therefore Christ is called the redemption of the angels too [n: (Col. I)].

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the altar of holocaust.* ⟦s2⟧ Of the body of Christ. ⟦s3⟧ For he himself is the priest, he himself the sacrifice, he himself also the altar. ⟦s4⟧ For as he works many things, so fittingly is he called by many names. ⟦s5⟧ Whence Paul: *We have an altar whereof they have no power to eat who serve the tabernacle [n: (Heb. XIII)]*: that is, the body of Christ, of which it is not lawful for the Jews to eat; this altar is at the entrance [0307B] of the tabernacle, that is, of heaven, because through it we have entrance into heaven.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the fat.* ⟦s2⟧ Mystically, the delights and desires of the flesh; all of which, however, are not to be received into Christ, such as the desire of the gullet and of coition. ⟦s3⟧ But those which he had, he had from us, as thirst and hunger, and things of this kind. ⟦s4⟧ And these he offered to the Father, to whose glory he transferred them, and set them forth for us as an example, that we may refer our desires unto God.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *The two little kidneys and the caul that is upon them near the flanks.* [n: (ISICH.)] Which things are done also in the sacrifice of the peace offerings, or of salvation. ⟦s2⟧ And he divides the sacrifice into two, that it may be permitted to all to imitate the sacrifice of Christ, both to those, namely, who are of a lower manner of life, [0307C] to whom the sacrifice of the peace offerings is suited, and to those who follow after higher things, for whom it suffices if they imitate what follows.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *But the skin and all the flesh with the head and the feet and the rest.* ⟦s2+3⟧ This he commands, that thou divide not Christ, as certain men divide him, saying: He who suffered was of Mary, he who was of God did not suffer; *But to us there is one God Jesus Christ, through whom are all things [n: (I Cor. VIII)]*. [var: Vulg. 1 Cor 8:6 reads *nobis tamen unus Deus Pater ex quo omnia … et unus Dominus Iesus Christus per quem omnia*, distinguishing the Father from the Lord Jesus Christ] For the flesh in the Lord is the whole humanity, according to what is said: *The Word was made flesh [n: (Joan. I)]*. ⟦s4⟧ Whence he says: *His flesh with the head and the feet,* or the extremities according to others, that by these words we may believe him who suffered for us to be the first and the last: for he is before all things, and in him all things consist. ⟦s5⟧ *With the entrails and the dung,* [0307D] the Seventy: with the belly and the excrement. ⟦s6⟧ But the belly signifies the mind; let us therefore in no wise doubt that he who suffered for us had a human mind, which certain of the heretics deny. ⟦s7⟧ The dung signifies the sin of the human race, which the crucified took upon himself. ⟦s8⟧ Dung, though it be unclean, does not pollute him who carries it; nor is it of our nature, although it be within us: so our sins, which the Lord bore, were foreign to him, nor did they pollute him.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *With the entrails and the dung,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] Perhaps by way of comparison, etc., *as far as* the lawgiver figures his mystery more fully in what follows.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *But if all the multitude.* [n: (ISICH.)] The same thing he commands in the sacrifice of the multitude and of the priest, etc., *as far [0308A] as* for he prayed for his very crucifiers: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do.* [var: Vulg. Lk 23:34 reads *Pater, dimitte illis, non enim sciunt quid faciunt*]

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *He shall carry the calf outside the camp* [n: (ID.)] The conclusion of the law suffices us for making plain the present matters, for nothing is here commanded to be changed for the sin of the multitude. ⟦s2⟧ It is manifest that he understands the sin of the people and of the priest to be the same, and therefore in both he ordained the same sacrifice.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *If a prince shall have sinned and done.* [n: (ID.)] As in the voluntary sacrifices, etc., *as far as* for the sabbath was given for rest from evils.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ORIG.)] This prince can be called the force of reason which is within us, which if it sin, so that we do something foolish, let us fear that sentence: *Ye are the salt of the earth. ⟦s2⟧ But if the salt shall have become insipid, it is worth nothing [0308B] but that it be cast forth and trodden underfoot by men. [n: (Matth. V.)]* [var: Vulg. Mt 5:13 reads *quod si sal evanuerit in quo sallietur ad nihilum valet ultra nisi ut mittatur foras*, "if the salt lose its savour, wherewith shall it be salted?"]

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *A he-goat of the goats.* ⟦s2⟧ Not a she-goat, that is, an easy or middling penance, but a hard and rough one. ⟦s3⟧ For by as much as a man excels in understanding, by so much, if he sins, does he undergo a greater punishment for a greater fault.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *He shall put his hand,* etc., manifesting his sin through himself, and offering his own confession. ⟦s2⟧ But he himself, according to the Seventy, does not slay it, but others do; because the help of others is necessary to him, that his penance may be received, nor is the priest commanded to slay it, for he says:

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And when he shall have immolated,* etc., nor did he add who ought to immolate, for it is lawful in the sacrifice of penance [0308C] to use the helps of another: whence James: *Pray one for another, that ye may be saved.*

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Where it is wont to be slain,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because there please God alike a worthy penance for sins and the strong works of the just.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *The horns of the altar,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Upon the cross are offered the firstfruits of the blood which is offered for sin, because the work of the cross and its effect is the perfect remission of our sins.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And pouring out the rest.* ⟦s2⟧ Attributing, namely, all penance to the evangelical preaching, whose intention is that he who repents may correct himself and be saved. ⟦s3⟧ Whence in the beginning of the Gospel the Lord says providently: *Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven draweth near.* [0308D] [n: (Matth. III.)] [var: Vulg. Mt 3:2 reads the perfect *adpropinquavit*, "hath drawn near"]

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *But if he shall have sinned,* etc. [n: (ORIG. hom. 2. in Levit.)] Doing one thing out of all the commandments of the Lord, etc., *as far as* when he acknowledges it, to immolate a sacrifice of praise.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the people,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] For the distinguishing of him, etc. ⟦s2⟧ *as far as* he holds it necessary for our sake.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *But if of the cattle.* [n: (ISICH.)] To the oblation of the she-goat which those who are of the people offer, etc., *as far as* he commands like things concerning the she-goat and the sheep.
