## CHAPTER VI

VERS. 1, 2.-- *He spoke,* etc. *The soul that shall have sinned,* [0311C] etc. [n: (ISICH.)] It is the same sin which is committed against holy things, etc., *as far as* it is like to robbery.

*[n: (ORIG. hom. 4 in Levit.)] The soul that shall have sinned,* etc. I think that our soul, etc., *as far as: For no man putteth new wine into old bottles.*

*The thing lost,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] LXX: *perdition.* The heretics labour to find perdition in the Scriptures, whence they may build up their error: but perhaps, hearing the words of the Church, they come to their senses, and understand that what they had found is perdition, and restore it, that is, they let it go back to its own author.

VERS. 5.-- *And the fifth part besides,* etc. The tongue, namely, in which is one of the five senses: this is offered, that for the rest he may not sin, because through it each one of the things aforesaid was committed.

[0311D] [n: (ORIG.)] LXX: not *the fifth part,* but *fifths,* as twice five, etc., *as far as* we may refer them to the one God.

VERS. 8.-- *And the Lord spoke to Moses, saying.* [n: (ISICH.)] Here he speaks of the continual sacrifice, etc., *as far as* and let him frame his whole life unto perfection.

VERS. 10.-- *Of linen,* not of wool: he wills the tunic and the drawers to be of linen, not of wool [n: (Exod. XXVIII)]. For a linen garment is fitting and soft, and clinging to the body; but a woollen one is rough, because justice and chastity are fitting to the soul and cleave much to it. A linen garment also can easily be had, so also the virtues, if anyone shall wish to attain them.

[0312A] *And he shall take up the ashes,* etc. The ash is the consummation of a good work. Aaron shall carry the ashes outside the camp: they who after this life present their good works to God the Father through Christ the great high priest. And there they are reduced to embers, while he stands before the face of God for us. For the camp is the present life, in which we always fight against spiritual wickednesses [n: (Heb. IX)]. They are placed beside the altar, while the consummation of good works is laid up in the ark of the heart. For the altar in this place signifies the heart. *Our glory is this, the testimony of our conscience,* etc. [n: ( Eph. ult. )]

VERS. 11.-- *And being clothed with others.* [n: (ORIG.)] It is to be observed that the priest uses certain garments in the mystery of the sacrifices, and others when he goes forth to the people. Whence: *We speak wisdom among the perfect* [0312B] [n: (II Cor. I)]. And elsewhere, clothed in a far lower robe: *Nothing did I judge,* he says, *myself to know among you, but Jesus Christ, and him crucified [n: (II Cor. I)]*.

VERS. 12.-- *Fire on. [n: (AUG. quaest. 12 in Levit.)]* He does not will the fire to be wholly extinguished, but that, when the holocaust shall have burned until morning, the remains being taken away, it be again renewed, whereby the other things which are laid on may burn.

*Which the priest shall feed. Allegorically.* In the morning Christ was judged, in the evening crucified. Fire therefore is laid under morning and evening: because the charity of God is nourished in us, while we call to mind Christ judged in the morning and crucified in the evening.

[n: (GREG. lib. XXV Moral., cap. 7.)] Our heart is the altar of God, etc., *as far as* that the more God is seen, the more he may be loved.

[0312C] *And the holocaust being laid on above he shall burn it,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 13 in Levit.)] It is to be seen whether no day was passed over, etc., *as far as* which is a wonder, if he were silent and did not admonish that such things be offered in the evening.

VERS. 14.-- *This is.* The lawgiver seems to repeat what he has said, renewing the memory by the repetition of the commandment, and showing the usefulness and the necessity of the law. Yet he inserts something new, in which he hints that the commandment is necessary and inexcusable. As therefore he repeated the sacrifices concerning the holocausts, mingling assiduity with them, as is that: *Let the fire on the altar always burn and never fail:* [var: Vulg. Lv 6:12–13 has the futures *ardebit* … *numquam deficiet*, "shall always burn … shall never fail"] so in the sacrifice of fine flour, that is of theology, he adds that it must be eaten only in a holy place, and by a male.

*And before.* According as Christ shall have revealed, [0312D] who is the true altar. In these things therefore let us establish theology which Christ incarnate delivered to us, that we may think of the Father in him, and of him in the Father, and that the Spirit is of the Son and proceeds from the Father.

VERS. 16.-- *The remainder,* etc., that we may not wholly cease from theology, because the sum of knowledge is conversant with the world to come.

*In the place.* The Church, which is the entrance and the vestibule of heaven; in it one must speak of the Godhead, whence in Psalm LXVII: *In the churches bless ye God,* each man is a household church to himself, while he ceases from earthly business: here he can speak to himself of God and exercise theology. Such are they to whom a part of the sacrifice is given, namely, to meditate and to speak of God.

[0313A] VERS. 17.-- *As for sin.* He hints that this sacrifice is like to that which is both for sin and for offence: for discourse concerning God is had chiefly unto the expiation and the cleansing of the soul, when to oil and frankincense, that is, to almsgiving and prayer, the sacrifice of the word of God, that is, of theology, is joined.

VERS. 18.-- *The males only.* It belongs not to all to dispute of God, but to those who have nothing womanish, nothing flowing, in their thoughts or their works. *It is lawful and everlasting.* For we ought not to presume to dispute of God, while through worldly imaginings we turn aside from the dignity of males.

*Everyone that shall touch.* [n: (ISICH.)] This cannot stand according to the letter: for shall a murderer or a profane man be sanctified if he shall have touched? for none is excepted. But [0313B] Christ, in whose figure these things went before, is immolated: he who shall have touched his flesh, that is, who with a faithful heart shall have placed the whole hope of salvation in his passion and resurrection, shall straightway be sanctified.

[n: (ID.)] *Everyone who,* etc. Every commemoration of God bestows sanctification. Whence: *But it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer.* But when he says that the one touching is sanctified, he plainly shows that in disputing of God, that is, in theologizing, it is enough for us only to touch: for he who wishes to be warmed, if he shall have put his hand into the fire, shall suffer burning instead of warming.

*This is the oblation. [n: (AUG., quaest. 14 in Levit.)]* Other are the sacrifices which he commemorated in Exodus, by which through seven days the priests are sanctified, that they may discharge [0313C] the priesthood. Another is that which he commemorates, which the high priest offers when he is appointed, that is, when he is anointed: whence he adds: *In the day in which he shall anoint him,* not "them," although he commands the second priests also to be anointed; this sacrifice is everlasting by succession, namely of priests. Or because that which it signifies is everlasting.

*This is,* etc. Aaron is the mountain of strength, that is, Christ; his sons are all the faithful; the day of anointing, the time of the incarnation, in which he was anointed with the oil of gladness above his fellows.

*An ephi.* The humanity of Christ. An ephi indeed, holding three measures, signifies the Trinity, if however the ephi is the flesh of the Lord, so that the eminence of the divinity may be shown, and the inseparability of the Lord's flesh with it; none the less [0313D] the sacrifice is perpetual. For by one oblation he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified: whence also *he is able to save for ever.*

*In the sacrifice.* [n: (ORIG.)] The law of Moses is the sacrifice of the priest, etc., *as far as* in which there is nothing slack, nothing wanton [n: (Matth. XVI)].

*In the morning.* In the morning the passion of the Lord began, from when in the hall of Caiphas he was smitten with blows, and it was drawn out even to the evening; for since the darkness interposed, from the sixth hour to the ninth hour it is divided into morning and evening.

VERS. 21.-- *Which in the frying-pan. [n: (GREG. hom. 12 in Ezech.)]* No such sacrifice is to God, etc., *as far as* it is reckoned a holocaust to God.

VERS. 22.-- *The priest.* LXX: *The anointed priest for [0314A] him, of his sons, shall do these things.* For Christ is *for* Aaron, not in his place, because he transferred the priesthood into himself, and took it away from Aaron. *Of his sons,* because he descended from Jacob through Mary and from the same tribe.

*Who to the father.* One Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things [n: (Rom. XI)]. For the Lord of glory was crucified, and it is the passion of the incarnate Word, which was in the flesh of Christ: *Who wholly emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, and obedient to the father even unto the death of the cross.* [var: Vulg. Phil 2:7–8 has neither *totum*, "wholly," nor *patri*, "to the father," and reads *factus obediens usque ad mortem, mortem autem crucis*]

VERS. 23.-- *For every.* Whatsoever things are in the sacrifice of the peace offerings, these also he bids to be done herein.

*Nor shall any one.* What is eaten is broken small: he breaks small the sacrifice of Christ who says that he died for sins, whereas he did not commit sin.

[0314B] [n: (ORIG.)] *Nor shall any one eat.* The sacrifice in this place is to be taken as the Word himself, and the doctrine, of which none eats, that is, disputes or handles it over again; but it is a holocaust. For whatsoever he said, whatsoever he appointed, endures by an eternal consecration.

*The priest who,* etc. [n: (AUG. quaest. 18 in Levit.)] Not that he lays on, etc., *as far as* this too he now briefly commemorates.

[n: (ORIG. homil. 5 in Levit.)] Certain of the victims are of God alone, etc., *as far as* better do we reserve these things for the fire.

VERS. 27.-- *Whatsoever shall touch.* Although the oblation be another's, that is, the penitent's, it shall be sanctified even if he do not repent. Likewise if a little one shall have been baptized, [0314C] who has not yet sinned of himself, he is cleansed from the original

*If of the blood,* that is, if the substance of the flesh shall have been stained with sins. Whence: *A garment mingled with blood shall be for burning, and food for the fire [n: (Isa. IX)]* of hell, namely: it shall be washed in a holy place, that is, he shall have hope in baptism, which not only washes away things past, but instructs him that he may be able to repent of things to come.

VERS. 28.-- *But the earthen vessel,* etc. The infirm: namely, the feeble, who after penance is performed slips back to sins; he must be shattered by these, by the terror of judgment.

VERS. 29.-- *Every male,* etc. The priest offering and eating the whole sacrifice is Christ, who offers our penance and eats the salvation of sinners. But the flesh of the sacrifice, because it pertains [0314D] to penance, every male among the priests eats, the strong, namely, and the vigorous, to whom it is said: *Do manfully, and let thy heart be strengthened, and wait thou for the Lord [n: (Psal. XXVI)]*.

VERS. 30.-- *It shall not be eaten,* etc. Because it pertains to Christ, who by his own authority and by the proper power of his divinity grants remission. For his passion, through which remission is procured for us, was not that of a mere man, but of God incarnate. Therefore it is not eaten by men, but is consumed by fire, because to give remission belongs to God alone, who is signified by fire, *for our God is a consuming fire [n: (Deut. IV)]*.
