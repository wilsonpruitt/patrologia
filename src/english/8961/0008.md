## CHAPTER VIII.

VERS. 2.-- *Two rams.* The passion of Christ is signified, who is the leader of the people. And rightly two rams are immolated, [0318C] because Christ twice committed to us the mysteries of his passion: first when he ate the mystical lamb at the supper, secondly when he established the same mysteries in the consecration of bread and wine.

VERS. 3.-- *And thou shalt gather together.* Moses called together the whole synagogue, although the Lord had chosen and commanded, yet the synagogue is called together: for in the ordaining of a priest the presence of the people is required, that all may know and be certain that he who is more excellent out of all the people, more learned, more holy and more eminent in virtue, he is chosen to the priesthood. He washed him therefore and clothed him. With what garment? with a tunic, and he girded him with a girdle. And again: *He clothed him with an ankle-length tunic [n: (Eccli. XLV)]*, or, as we read elsewhere, *with an inner one [n: (II Reg. XV)]*. But I see the high priest clothed with two tunics [0318D]. Yet Jesus forbids the apostles to be clothed with two tunics, whereas Moses and Jesus, that is the law and the Gospel, ought to agree [n: (Matth. X)]. Others say that Jesus's commanding them not to have two tunics is not contrary to the law, but more perfect than the law: as also when the law forbids homicide [n: (Exod. XX)], but Jesus cuts away anger [n: (Matth. V)]; and when the law forbids adultery, but Jesus cuts off concupiscence. I do not shut up the pontifical sacraments within the narrowness of this understanding: something further seems to me to be shown by that form. That high priest, whom Moses was then ordaining, knew that circumcision was spiritual: yet he kept also the circumcision of the flesh, because he could not be uncircumcised; he had [sic: *habeba*] [0319A] therefore two tunics, one of the carnal mystery, another of the spiritual understanding. For he could not be the high priest of those who then were, unless he offered carnal sacrifices, although he knew that they ought to be spiritual. Fittingly therefore he had two tunics. But the apostles, who were to say: *If you be circumcised, Christ shall profit you nothing [n: (Gal. V)]*, and who were utterly to reject the literal observance, are rightly forbidden to have two tunics; one suffices them, and this the inner one, but that which is outside, the tunic of the law, they will not have, because Jesus permits them to have one, and that the inner one.

VERS. 6.-- *And when he had washed.* [n: (HIERON., ubi supra. )] Even then the cleansing of the world, etc., *as far as* that what we conceive in mind, we may carry out in deed.

[n: (ORIG., hom. 6 in Levit.)] Let Moses wash thee, etc., [0319B] *as far as* in what order a high priest is appointed.

VERS. 7.-- *He clothed.* [n: (HIERON., ibid. )] Let us learn first the common vestments of the priests, etc., *as far as* because a work of this kind cannot be made on the loom.

[n: (ID.)] The second is the *poderis* of linen, that is, the ankle-length garment of a double linen cloth, which Josephus calls the byssine; this clings to the body, so close and with sleeves so tight that there is no wrinkle in the garment, and it comes down to the legs. Such linen garments soldiers are wont to have, which they call shirts, fitted to the limbs and drawn tight, that they may be unencumbered for all things. This tunic the priests use, having the beauty of the clothed and the swiftness of the naked, and it is called the linen one.

The third is the girdle, most like to the skin which a serpent puts off in summer, as a long pouch woven into a round [0319C] shape, under a covering of scarlet, purple, hyacinth and fine linen: for the sake of comeliness and of strength, so marked with embroidery that you would think the various flowers and gems were not woven in by the craftsman's hand, but added. By this belt the linen tunic is drawn tight between the navel and the breast, which belt, having the breadth of four fingers and coming down for the greater part to the legs, when there is need of dispatch for the sacrifices is thrown back over the left shoulder.

[n: (HIERON.)] The fourth is round, etc., *as far as* about to die at once.

[n: (ID.)] The sixth in Hebrew, etc., *as far as* he may bear on his shoulders.

[n: (ID.)] The seventh is a little one, etc., *as far as* and it would be thought by those who saw it to be one weaving.

[0319D] [n: (ID.)] The eighth is the golden plate, etc., *as far as* and let the name of God crown and protect the whole beauty of the high priest.

But the pomegranates and the little bells set in the lower parts show forth lightnings and thunders, or the earth and the water and all the elements sounding together among themselves, and so interwoven with one another that all are in each.

[n: (HIERON.)] The Hebrews the four colours, etc., *as far as* his providence penetrates all things.

[n: (ORIG., hom. 6 in Levit.)] In Exodus, where command is given concerning the priestly vestments, etc., *as far as* lest the holy be given to dogs and pearls be set before swine [n: (Marc. VII)].

*And bringing in,* etc. LXX: *He put on him the tunic and girded [0320A] him with the girdle and clothed him with the epomis.* He follows out the incarnation of Christ. For he took up the whole man, that is, body and soul; the *tunic* he called earthly, because it is of the earth; the epomis heavenly, because ὑπωρίς is the *poderis.* And *of hyacinth,* which is the heavenly colour: for he took up both, and saved both.

[n: (HIERON.)] On the hyacinthine tunic, pomegranates and little bells: because so great ought the knowledge of the high priest to be, that his steps and his motions and all things be vocal, and that he sound forth by his bearing the truth which he has conceived in mind; whatsoever he does, whatsoever he speaks, let it be doctrine. Without little bells, without divers colours, gems and flowers of the virtues, he neither enters into the Church, nor is he a high priest.

[0320B] *The shoulder-piece,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] Moses laid upon the high priest the ephod, which is the adornment of the shoulders from the drawing round of the vesture: but the shoulders are the tokens of labour and of work. He wills therefore that the high priest be adorned in works: for knowledge alone does not suffice, *but he that shall do and teach, he shall be called great [n: (I Tim. III; Matth. V)]*.

*The ephod.* [n: (HIER.)] The ephod and the two stones of emerald or of onyx, which cover the shoulders from above, are interpreted the two hemispheres, of which one is above the earth, the other beneath the earth; or the sun and the moon which glow red from above.

[n: (ID.)] The two stones on the ephod are Christ and the Church, containing the names of the twelve apostles who were sent to preach, or the letter and the spirit [0320C] in which are all the mysteries of the law; on the right the spirit, on the left the letter [n: (II Cor. III)]. Through the letters we come to the words, through the words to the sense. A beautiful order, signifying the sacraments by the very garb: on the shoulders works, on the breast reason, whence also the priests eat the little breast.

VERS. 8.-- *Which binding fast.* [n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] He girded him according to the making of the shoulder-piece, etc., *as far as* because according to his works he uses the girdle of virtue.

*He fitted to the rational,* etc. [n: (HIERON.)] The rational, set in the midst, points to the earth, which, after the manner of a point, though it have all things in itself, is walled about by all the elements.

[n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] He laid upon him the logion, λογίον, etc., *as far as* for nothing after these things is added to the head [0320D] of the high priest.

[n: (HIERON., ubi supra. )] The rational is twofold, open and hidden, etc., *as far as* and let doctrine and truth follow upon these which go before.

[n: (ID.)] Twelve stones, etc., *as far as* showing knowledge to us.

*He put the plate.* [n: (ID.)] The learning of all things profits nothing, unless it be crowned by the knowledge of God, which is always his with the Father: whence he set the plate above the tiara.

[n: (ID.)] All things are imperfect unless a charioteer be sought for the chariot, and unless he himself who founded them, standing over the creatures, rule them. What was once shown in the plate is now shown in the sign of the cross: more precious is the blood of the Gospel [0321A] than the gold of the law; then, according to Ezekiel, the sign thau was fixed on the forehead of them that mourned [n: (Ezech. VIII)]: now, bearing the cross, we say: *The light of thy countenance is signed upon us, O Lord,* etc. [n: (Psal. IV)]

The plate of gold on the forehead of the high priest, and the name of God inscribed, signify that all things which are upon the earth are governed by the will of God. For it was just that the priest of God, bearing on his vestments the figure of all creatures, should declare that all things stand in need of the mercy of God, and that, when he sacrificed, the universal creation should be expiated: where he should pray by voice and by bearing not for his children, his parents, and his kindred, but for every creature.

VERS. 10.-- *He took,* etc. He himself is the Son of God, who anoints and who is anointed: anointing by the word of the divinity, according to [0321B] which he has all things in common with the Father and the Holy Spirit. By the Father he is anointed according to that: *God, thy God, hath anointed thee with the oil of gladness,* etc. [n: (Psal. XLIV)]. The Holy Spirit anointed him according to Isaiah: *The Spirit of the Lord is upon me, because he hath anointed me [n: (Isa. LXI)]*. But the things which the Father and the Holy Spirit do, these same the Son also does. He anoints therefore according to the divinity, he is anointed according to the humanity. Whence also Daniel sees him offered, and does not see the one offering, because he offers himself. Moses therefore anointed and offers, bearing the figure of Christ.

VERS. 11.-- *They sanctify.* [n: (ORIG.)] It signifies the creature above enriched by our sanctification: that is, through the Lord's flesh taken from us. Whence: *that it might be made known to the princes and powers through [0321C] the church,* etc. [var: Vulg. Eph 3:10 has *ut innotescat principatibus et potestatibus in caelestibus*, "that the manifold wisdom of God may be made known to the principalities and powers in heavenly places"]

VERS. 12.-- *Pouring,* etc. The head of Christ is the divinity. For as the head is not divided from the feet, so neither is the divinity of Christ from the humanity after the anointing [n: (I Cor. XI)]. Whence he says: *God, thy God, hath anointed thee,* etc. [n: (Psal. LIV.)] For thus God is anointed and sanctified.

VERS. 13.-- *The sons,* etc. [n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] Mark the difference of the lesser priests to the greater, etc., *as far as* even if he shall have received the name of the first.

*And he clothed,* etc. Like tunics and girdles or belts and tiaras or mitres are put upon the sons of Aaron, for they put on him, are girded with him, bear him on the head; for the loins are girded with truth, which is Christ, and they are clothed with the breastplate of justice: and Christ is justice, as also the helmet of salvation, for he himself [0321D] is the salvation of God.

VERS. 14.-- *He offered,* etc. [n: (ISICH.)] What the power of this law is, etc., *as far as* but of necessity the sons of Aaron, as sharing with him in the sacrifice, lay on their hands.

[n: (AUG., quaest. 23 in Levit.)] When he was recounting the sacrifices for sins, etc., *as far as* and the blood poured out at the base of the altar of the sacrifices.

*He offered,* etc. [n: (ISICH.)] The law, the priest figuring Christ in many things, etc., *as far as* he commanded for sheep.

*He offered,* etc. The handing down of the mysteries, and the mystical pasch, which Christ celebrated with his disciples [n: (Matth. XXVI)], is signified by the *ram: the second* he is named, [0322A] because after the lamb immolated at the supper he offered himself; whence Aaron laid his hands upon the head of the ram together with his sons, because Christ celebrated the common supper with his disciples; but Moses in a special manner offered this sacrifice, because more than the others he expressed the figure of Christ, who chiefly made his own sacrifice.

VERS. 24.-- *He touched the tip of the ear,* etc. If by Aaron we understand Christ, the tip of his ear was touched with blood, because he obeyed the Father even unto death. If the priests of the Church, their ears are to be touched, and the thumbs of their hands and feet, that they may consecrate to the Lord the eminence of their actions and of their steps. *He touched the tip,* etc. What the effect of this sacrifice is, the anointing of the ears and of the hands and feet [0322B] shows: for it is celebrated on account of our obedience and our good actions and our manner of life, that, lacking the disobedience of Adam, we may attain to the obedience of the Only-begotten.

*His hands,* etc. That we may be strengthened in good action. For the things that are anointed are strengthened, the things that are not anointed are destroyed. For where the anointing of the immolated blood was not, Egypt was struck. But first the priest, and afterwards his sons, were anointed with the blood, because Christ first received the blood at the supper, then gave it to the disciples.
