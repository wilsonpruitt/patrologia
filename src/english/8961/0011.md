## CHAPTER X.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0325D]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And Nadab having taken. [n: (AUG. quaest. 31 in Levit., tom. III.)]* Fire going out from the Lord, the sons of Aaron died, etc., *as far as* they will suffer torments.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And having taken,* etc. [n: (ISID., quaest. in Levit., tom. V.)] They offer strange fire, etc., *as far as* lest we burn with vices.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISICH.)] Here those who teach strange things, etc., *as far as* whereby they were able to earn life.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., ubi supra. )] Another reading, etc., *as far as* through whom he gave us the Scripture.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *And Moses having called Misael and Elisaphan.* מישאל Misael, *the touching of God.* אלצפן Elisaphan, [0326A] or Elzaphan, *the illumination of God,* or *the mirror of God.* ⟦s2⟧ To these it is with right commanded that they take up Nadab and Abiu outside the camp. ⟦s3⟧ For these signify the saints, who, being illumined by God, touch him with the hands of faith, and contemplate God with perfect faith. ⟦s4⟧ Whence they are well called the sons of Oziel, which signifies *the Gospel:* whence he is said to have been Aaron's cousin on the father's side, because great is the kinship of the Gospel and of the Law. ⟦s5⟧ For the Gospel is the exposition of the Law: and the sons of the Gospel thrust heretics, truly dead, from the camp of the Church. ⟦s6⟧ Or Misadai and Elisaphan: the first, *the touching of God,* that is Misadai; Elisaphan is interpreted *the watchtower of my God.* ⟦s7⟧ Whosoever knows how to touch God, and to behold, namely, the flesh and the divinity of Christ, as Thomas who, handling the flesh with his hand, said: *My God and my Lord [n: (Joan. XX)]*, [var: Vulg. Jo 20:28 *Dominus meus, et Deus meus*, the order reversed] is able to take away the fire [0326B] of strange doctrine from the face of the saints, that is, of the priests. ⟦s8⟧ The tunics of the priests: the manner of life and the life by which they are clothed with the habit of the priesthood. ⟦s9⟧ They are cast out therefore, together with the priesthood, from the fellowship of the priests and also of the people. ⟦s10⟧ Whence he adds, *outside the tabernacle,* that is, the church of the people: the sons of Aaron's uncle do these things.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Aaron,* that is David, through whom the prophecies are understood, which make Christ manifest, and in a manner bring him forth to those who know him not. ⟦s2⟧ But the brother of prophecy is the Gospel; for the things which were done in the Gospels were foretold by the prophets. ⟦s3⟧ The sons therefore of the brother of Aaron's father, that is, of the Gospel, cast out such men. ⟦s4⟧ Whence: *If any man come to you, and bring not this doctrine, receive him not into the house [n: (II Joan.)]*.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Heads,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ According to the letter. ⟦s3⟧ Because they had lately [0326C] been consecrated to God, he commands that they lay not aside the holy vestments, but stand ready to serve God.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But allegorically, that they make not a division of persons in Christ. ⟦s2⟧ For the head of Christ is God, but Christ consists of divinity and humanity; for the tiara of the head, that is of the divinity, is the humanity, by which the divinity was covered as with a cap; this is not to be taken away, nor is a division of persons in Christ to be preached, but one person of two substances.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Your heads,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 32 in Lev.)] That is, lest they lay aside the mitres, etc., *as far as* whence he adds:

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Let your brethren and all the house of Israel bewail the burning.* [n: (ISICH.)] We are not ignorant what the mitres signify, [0326D] etc., *as far as* of which: *His unction teacheth you of all things.*

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Wine, and every thing that may make drunk.* [n: (ID.)] Until now the Church keeps this law, etc., *as far as* that is, to have breathed spiritually.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Wine, and every thing that,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ They are bidden to abstain from those things which cast down or overturn the state of the mind, when they enter the tabernacle. ⟦s3⟧ But let us never be made drunk, but let us always be sober in mind and in body.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *When you shall enter. [n: (AUG., quaest. 33)]*. ⟦s2⟧ When therefore it was lawful for them to drink, etc., *as far as* that this may be the lawful office of the priests for ever in their generations.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0327A]VERS. 10.-- *That you may have knowledge to discern.* [n: (AUG.)] This too is set down ambiguously, etc., *as far as* or is it rather to be referred to both?

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Take the sacrifice,* etc. [n: (ISICH.)] That the little breast is confidence, etc., *as far as* but the things that belong to the peace and welfare offerings are of the whole people.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Take ye,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The hidden things, that is, and the deep sacraments of Scripture, which the common sort does not grasp, are to be kept and eaten by the perfect; *beside the altar,* that is, beside the faith and the authority of the Lord's passion.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *The little breast,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *[n: (AUG., quaest. 34.)] The little breast.* ⟦s3⟧ Although several names have been given to the several things, etc., *as far as* it was laid upon the altar from the little breast.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0327B]VERS. 16.-- *Among these.* [n: (ISICH.)] Because every sin is blotted out through penance, and they who committed the aforesaid sin of another doctrine and of blasphemy were not saved, the lawgiver sets forth the cause of their death.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Among these the he-goat,* that is, because the sons of Aaron did not eat that which was for sin in the holy place, that is, they did not cause the penance for the sin committed to be performed in the church. ⟦s2⟧ To whom this is commanded, whom Christ appointed as his vicars in the Church. ⟦s3⟧ For he himself offered the blood of his own oblation, which was offered for our blood, into the holy place, that is, into heaven, in the sight of the Father. ⟦s4⟧ But this sacrifice [sic: *sacricium*] ought to be eaten in the holy place, that is, in the church, unto the propitiation of sins, whence he adds: *As it hath been commanded [0327C] to me,* that is, as I have commanded. ⟦s5⟧ For Moses bears the person of Christ. ⟦s6⟧ He finds it burnt up, because they had sinned against the Holy Spirit: by the intelligible fire of the Holy Spirit the oblation for penance was taken away. ⟦s7⟧ *For to them that sin wilfully there is not left a sacrifice for sin [n: (Heb. X)]*. ⟦s8⟧ For as true penance earns pardon, so feigned penance provokes God: *because the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee the feigned [n: (Sap. I)]*. ⟦s9⟧ But this the holy Fathers have called the unremissible sin, and the blasphemy against the Holy Spirit: to persevere in crimes unto the end of life, to despair of one's own salvation, to distrust the power and the mercy of the Redeemer.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ADAMANT.)] Not to sin belongs to God alone, etc., *as far as* save what is true and sincere.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0327D] [n: (AUG., quaest. 36 in Levit.)] Indeed, since six beasts are commanded to be offered for the people, etc., *as far as* all which had been for sin.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Aaron answered,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In the person of the Church, whose figure he bears, as also that of Christ, Aaron seems to say this. ⟦s3⟧ As it were, because after the coming of the Saviour they were appointed to minister, and to offer for penance, and to set forth the prophetic life. ⟦s4⟧ Otherwise: The teachers perished, and in them these things befell the Church, so that it mourned the death of the priests and the masters, whence it could not eat to-day for sin, nor pray: but it was displeasing to the Lord.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] *Aaron answered,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ If to-day they offered the things which are for their sin, etc., *as far as* But [0328A] why this was done, Aaron answered, and it pleased Moses.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To-day.* ⟦s2⟧ As if he said: if they had sinned this before the remission of the Saviour, perhaps it would be pardoned, since knowledge was not yet made manifest to the world: whence, *If I had not come, and spoken to them, they would not have sin; but now they have no excuse for their sin [n: (Joan. XV)]*.
