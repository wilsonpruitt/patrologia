## CHAPTER XI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *The Lord spoke.* [n: (ISICH.)] Whatsoever was made by God is clean by the very authority of its institution. ⟦s2⟧ In the animals, therefore, are depicted the manners of men, and their acts, and their wills, from which they become clean or unclean.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0328B] [n: (ID.)] The Lord said to Noe: *Every thing that moveth and liveth shall be meat for you [n: (Gen. IX)]*, etc. ⟦s2⟧ In Genesis also it was said: *God saw all the things that he had made, and they were very good [n: (Gen. I)]*. ⟦s3⟧ But here certain things are forbidden as evil, and are decreed unclean. ⟦s4⟧ Yet these things are not contrary to one another: for the Lord both restrains for a little the gluttony of the Jews, and the spiritual exposition of the precepts instructs what manner of men they ought to be. ⟦s5⟧ For the Law is the nourishment of the rational soul when it is understood in a reasonable way. ⟦s6⟧ And as in the passages above he figured those who offer by the oblations, so now by the things that are eaten he expresses those who eat.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Every thing that hath,* etc. [n: (ISID.)] Among fishes whatsoever hath not fins and scales [0328C] is abominable: rough things are held for clean. ⟦s2⟧ For bristly and shaggy, firm and grave manners of men are approved. ⟦s3⟧ They who are without these are called unclean, to whom smooth and slippery, weak and effeminate manners are attached. ⟦s4⟧ He joins fins also and scales; for whatsoever fishes are finned are also scaly. ⟦s5⟧ If to any their ignorance can be dissolved, to these belongs also lofty knowledge and a heavenly life: but they who are not such, with these not even to take food.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Morally.* [n: (GREG.)] Fishes which have fins and scales are wont to give leaps above the waters. ⟦s2+3⟧ In finned fishes, therefore, are figured the elect souls, which alone pass over into the body of the heavenly Church; which, upheld by the fins of the virtues, leap through heavenly desire to contemplation, although they slip back into themselves through the frailty of the flesh. [0328D] Those alone, then, pass over into the body of the elect as chosen food, who, while they serve the lowest things, ascend to the things above by the leaps of the mind, lest they always lie hid in the depths of cares, where they have no care of the highest love, as of the free air. ⟦s4⟧ They therefore who are occupied with temporal things dispose their outward affairs well then, when they anxiously flee back to the inward ones, and love not the din of disturbance, but rest within themselves in the bosom of tranquillity.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Whatsoever fins,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ There are also fishes having a shell for a skin, whose scale cannot be taken away, because they neither lay aside the scales of ignorance at the word of God, nor receive the knife of the spirit: and although they be found in the sea of baptism, or [0329A] in the rivers of penance, they are nevertheless abominable among those things which move and live in the waters, because by living ill they corrupt the regeneration and the life which they received in baptism.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Flesh.* [n: (RAB. in Levit. )] When he forbids the swine, etc., *as far as* seeking the darkness of night.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *These are they which of the birds,* etc. [n: (ISICH.)] He adds the things which belong to the life of contemplatives, etc., *as far as little women laden with sins.*

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *The vulture after its kind.* [n: (ID.)] Which rejoices in wars. ⟦s2⟧ Whence it follows an army, awaiting the carcasses of the dead: for he who gives his labour to contemplation ought not to be fed on another's grief.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *The ostrich.* ⟦s2⟧ The ostrich is a bird, but it cannot fly, and is always about the ground, like [0329B] certain men who serve God and entangle themselves in secular businesses [n: (Job XXXIX; II Tim. II)].

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The owl.* ⟦s2⟧ They say that this sees keenly by night, and little by day. ⟦s3⟧ Such are they who glory in the contemplation of the Law, and are not able to receive the light of the Gospel. ⟦s4⟧ *The night-raven.* ⟦s5⟧ It seizes prey only by night. ⟦s6⟧ Such are the nations that gape after the works of night, which are fornication, uncleanness, immodesty [n: (Gal. V)].

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The gull.* ⟦s2⟧ The gull is an animal dwelling as much in water as on land: for it both flies and swims. ⟦s3⟧ To this are likened they who venerate both circumcision and baptism.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The hawk.* ⟦s2⟧ The hawk is among the birds of prey, but it grows tame, and works its rapine together with men. ⟦s3⟧ Him do they imitate who seem gentle, but strive to seize together with the powerful and the covetous and the ravening.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0329C] *The didapper and the ibis.* ⟦s2⟧ These are of long neck, and draw their food out of the depth of the earth or of the water (as it is said), and are not to be imitated. ⟦s3⟧ For he who gives himself to contemplation ought to stretch towards the higher things, and not to be solicitous about the lower.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *The hoopoe.* ⟦s2⟧ This is mournful and loving of grief. ⟦s3⟧ But the sorrow of the world worketh death. ⟦s4⟧ But he who loves God ought to rejoice, to pray without ceasing, in all things to give thanks [n: (I Thess. V)].

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The bat.* ⟦s2⟧ Which flies about the ground, uses its wings for feet: which is foreign to those who contemplate, lest their contemplation be occupied in earthly things.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Every one of the winged things that goeth upon [0329D] four feet,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ They who seem to be like the contemplatives, but cling like creeping things to earthly concupiscences, even if they walk upon four feet, that is, if they feign the evangelical preaching, which calls to penance; because they do not truly embrace the social life, serving pleasures, they are abominable.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *But whatsoever walketh,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because, although they are in the flesh, they walk not according to the flesh [n: (II Cor. X)], but leap away from filthy actions, and mingle with earthly men, that they may instruct and teach: as the Lord with sinners and publicans [n: (Matth. IX)]. ⟦s3⟧ We ought to have legs loftier than our feet, lest we halt on either foot, as [0330A] the Jews in the law and the prophets; but let us have straight legs, like the Cherubim, of whom it is said: *Their legs were straight, and their feet winged [n: (Ezech. I)]*. [var: Vulg. Ez 1:7 *et pedes eorum, pedes recti, et planta pedis eorum quasi planta pedis vituli*; no received verse reads *crura eorum recta*]

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *The bruchus.* ⟦s2⟧ Which leaps, but less than the attacus, or the locust, which leap more. ⟦s3⟧ Such are they who associate with sinners, for association, not for consent or imitation.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The ophiomachus.* ⟦s2⟧ Which fights with serpents; but for us the fight is with the ancient serpent and his angels [n: (Apoc. XII)].

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *That he bear.* ⟦s2⟧ Like one who calls evil good, and bitter sweet [n: (Isa. V)]: for by praising the evil from which he ought to have restrained, he makes it his own and bears it.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *Every beast that.* [n: (ISICH.)] The law passes over no sin; whatsoever things are forbidden in the Gospel [0330B] are expressed by the riddles of the Law, *that in the mouth of two witnesses every word may stand [n: (Matth. XVIII)]*, [var: Vulg. Mt 18:16 *duorum, vel trium testium*, "of two or three witnesses"] and that in both laws thou mayest praise God, and, as it forbids, mayest flee evil.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *That walketh upon hands,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He who transgresses the evangelical manner of life, but walks upon his hands, laying on hands, that is, for the sake of filthy lucre [n: (I Tim. IV)], is rightly compared to all beasts, so that thou mayest call him either an adulterer, or covetous, a murderer and the like: for by a wicked imposition of hands he communicates with them all.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Whosoever shall carry carcasses of this kind.* ⟦s2⟧ By speaking false testimonies, by calling the worthy unworthy, and the clean unclean.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *The weasel.* ⟦s2⟧ Voracity. ⟦s3⟧ For the mouse for the [0330C] voracity of its belly shuns no dangers.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The crocodile.* ⟦s2⟧ Which, of hard skin and malicious, is fed on mud.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *The chameleon.* ⟦s2⟧ It shows pity, and out of weakness feigns itself tame, though it is cruel.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The mole.* ⟦s2⟧ A blind animal begotten of the earth. ⟦s3⟧ For it is created, as they say, of earth rained upon and muddy, but first corrupted.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *He that toucheth,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ If they were unclean by nature, he would be unclean who touched them even living. ⟦s3⟧ The carrion of such are their actions, that is, guile, theft, duplicity, and things of this kind, in which is the death of the soul.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *And upon what,* etc. [n: (ISICH.)] The lawgiver uses a wonderful division, etc., *as far as* as well [0330D] a wooden vessel as a garment.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *All food,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ By food and drink he understands good works, therefore he said: *The food which you shall eat, and the liquid that is drunk,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ Because both the food and the drink of evil actions is forbidden, lest anyone partake of them. ⟦s4⟧ If therefore any carrion whatever fall upon good actions, which are in every vessel, that is, in every man, the action will necessarily be unclean. ⟦s5⟧ For if upon a good action there fall pride, guile, hypocrisy, or any carrion whatever, that is, anything bringing death to him that partakes, this man also will be unclean: for an evil intention falling upon a good action defiles it.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *If water be poured upon it. [n: (AUG., quaest. 37 in Levit.)]* Concerning the vessel, that is, which [0331A] was made unclean by unclean carrion, if perchance it held water.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Water.* ⟦s2⟧ Of baptism: for the discourse is about the baptized; but of the unbelieving he makes no mention. ⟦s3⟧ Whence Paul: *I have written to you not to keep company with fornicators [n: (I Cor. III)]*, not indeed with the fornicators of this world, etc.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *Or ovens.* ⟦s2⟧ By this the masters are designated, in whom the foods of sacred doctrine are cooked by the working and the fire of the Holy Spirit. ⟦s3⟧ Nor is it a wonder: for Moab is called a cauldron: *Moab is the pot of my hope [n: (Rom. II)]*.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Pots with feet,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Because they who teach not to steal, steal; and they who preach not to commit adultery, commit adultery: yet the doctrine is clean. ⟦s3⟧ Whence fountains and the rest.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0331B]VERS. 37.-- *If it fall.* [n: (ISICH.)] He leaves nothing unexamined, etc., *as far as* if any carrion whatever fall upon them, they become unclean.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *He that toucheth its carcass shall be unclean.* [n: (ISICH.)] We ought to abominate the life of the wicked, to imitate that of the just. ⟦s2⟧ For he who touches the life of the evil shall be unclean, until he has done penance.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *He that eateth.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, has defended it as an advocate, and by helping has transferred it upon himself: let him wash his garments, that is, his wicked actions, until he has done penance.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 41.-- *Every thing that creepeth upon the earth.* ⟦s2⟧ This the lawgiver set forth above, but he repeats it, admonishing the hearer to the frequent meditation of the law.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 47.-- *And that you may know what to eat.* ⟦s2⟧ These foods [0331C] do not cleanse or pollute us, but we ourselves: for we quicken the things that are eaten, and as every food is good [n: (I Petr. II)], and nothing is to be rejected which is received with thanksgiving; so the things that are eaten, that is, which nourish the soul, namely good actions, we quicken, if we use them well: if ill, we mortify them. ⟦s3⟧ For he who makes an alms out of rapine mortifies it, and takes away its power; and they who abstain from foods, but disparage their brethren, mortify their abstinence.
