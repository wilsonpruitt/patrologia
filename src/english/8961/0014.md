## CHAPTER XIII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And the Lord spoke.* [n: (ISID.)] God speaks once, and two things are heard: Moses hears the Spirit, [0332D] as the loftier; Aaron the letter, as the lower, who announces the letter to the people. ⟦s2⟧ Whence *he shall speak to the people, and shall be thy mouth, but thou shalt be in those things which pertain to God [n: (Exod. IV)]*. [var: Vulg. Ex 4:16 *ipse loquetur pro te ad populum … tu autem eris ei in his quae ad Deum pertinent*, "he shall speak for thee" and "thou shalt be to him"]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *A man.* [n: (ID.)] Sometimes it is taken in an excellent sense, of him who keeps the image of God and the dignity of his nature. ⟦s2⟧ Sometimes of a sinner, in whom that dignity is deformed, as here.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In whose flesh,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He bears leprosy in flesh and skin who strives to persuade to carnal or outward things, as the Corinthians, who thought that the resurrection would be in the pleasure of the flesh: and that to those who remain in the faith, although they live carnally, sins are not imputed.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Or as it were shining.* ⟦s2⟧ One leprosy is exceedingly, or moderately, [0333A] dark, the passion not yet boiling up, which needs much deliberation. ⟦s3⟧ Another is exceedingly clear and manifest, whose recognition is open.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The plague of leprosy.* [n: (ISID.)] Leprosy is false doctrine; the lepers are heretics, who, not having the unity of the true faith, profess various errors of doctrine, mixing true things with false, and by their spreading defile the Church, as leprosy defiles bodies.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ He bears leprosy in the body who disparages the Church, as the Carpocratians, who deny the resurrection of the flesh, and the Novatians, who condemn marriages, and take away penance from those who sin.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Or to any one of his sons,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Of kinsmen according to the spirit, from whom he has the priesthood, in whom there is a perfect and full prophecy, that they may be able to discern [0333B] subtle things and the hidden motions of the soul.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *The hairs.* ⟦s2⟧ Thoughts, by which the soul is adorned and the mind is covered, and by which they are begotten, as the head begets the hairs of the head, by which it is adorned and covered. ⟦s3+4⟧ Whence *the hairs of your head are all numbered [n: (Matth. X)]*. [var: Vulg. Mt 10:30 *Vestri autem capilli capitis omnes numerati sunt*; this word order is that of Lc 12:7] Hence the Nazarites do not shave the head [n: (Num. VI)], because they have no superfluous thoughts; but to lay aside holy ones is a loss to a holy man.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Lower than the flesh,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Lower than himself: whence rightly he does not merit pardon. ⟦s3⟧ For to be overcome by another and a greater is bearable; but not to work the good commanded by God (for the Lord divides as he wills), so that a man becomes worse than himself, this is unbearable.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *He shall shut him up.* ⟦s2⟧ He is shut up a second time, that he may be diligently inspected, and, a double trial being gone through, [0333C] a strict judgment may be made. ⟦s3⟧ For the sin was manifest to the priest, but he did not know that the mind consented to the passion, but conjectured it: therefore he inspected again. ⟦s4⟧ Hence the Lord, after the denial, asks a third time how Peter's mind stands [n: (Joan. XI)], that we may not judge quickly, but may inquire often. ⟦s5⟧ He is shut up for seven days. ⟦s6⟧ For the number seven marks the fullness of the present life, which is passed in seven days; and when it is fulfilled, sin and servitude shall cease. ⟦s7⟧ Whence in the divine Scriptures the seventh is named rest.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *He shall cleanse him, because it is a scab.* ⟦s2⟧ So Paul removed from the tabernacle of the saints him who had committed fornication at Corinth (because the passion was manifest), and delivered him to Satan; and when he had inspected him a second time, and had seen him repent, he commanded him to be received [n: (I Cor. V)].

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0333D]VERS. 10.-- *And when the colour shall be white in the skin.* ⟦s2⟧ A manifest transgression of the letter of the law, which is proper to the Jews, as in sacrifices and purgations and things of this kind. ⟦s3⟧ For there is also one common to the nations, concerning adultery, rapine, avarice, and the like.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *But if it shall flower forth.* [n: (ISICH.)] He shows the Jewish people to be leprous in part, as being in part a transgressor of the law: whence the priest casts him out as unclean. ⟦s2⟧ But the Gentile, leprous in his whole body, who transgresses the whole law, he does not cast out; but judges him clean, because he was not subject to the yoke of the law.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (HIERON.)] *But if it shall flower forth,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In this leprosy, as it were flowering and white, he designates diverse crimes. ⟦s3⟧ Because [0334A] when the pleasure of worldly life is esteemed as white and flowering, then the grievous contagion of vices rages in the body. ⟦s4⟧ For either the spots of avarice or those of lust break out. ⟦s5+6⟧ But the crime of avarice can easily be cut away, whence: *Give alms, and all things shall be clean unto you.* [var: Vulg. Lc 11:41 *et ecce omnia munda sunt vobis*, "and behold, all things are clean unto you"] Lust also is cleansed by fastings and abstinence. ⟦s7⟧ But to all of them a remedy is applied through the priest. ⟦s8⟧ For one is cleansed by the oblations of sacrifices; another there is which is cleansed more easily; another which is cleansed after being placed outside the camp; another which is not cleansed at all.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *Living flesh,* etc. [n: (ISICH.)] The Jewish commandment, which is said to live so far as regards the commandment, or because, well understood, it brings life.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *For the flesh,* etc. [n: (ISID.)] He bears leprosy in living flesh [0334B] who judges anything false concerning the soul, which is the life of the flesh. ⟦s2⟧ As the Luciferians, who say that the soul is propagated from the substance of the flesh; and the Arabici, who think that the soul dies together with the body.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *A scar,* etc. [n: (ID.)] They bear leprosy in the scar of a healed wound who, after the acknowledgment of the faith and the medicine of God which they receive in Christ, again show some token of error or of the perfidy of the old doctrine.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *White.* ⟦s2⟧ Because it comes from the wound of the transgression of the law; for the law is white and not warmed by the sun of justice [n: (Act. XV)].

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Somewhat reddish.* ⟦s2⟧ Because it comes from an evangelical prevarication, as to eat what is offered to idols, or blood, or what is strangled; and both are done against Christ, whose are the Law and the Gospel.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0334C]VERS. 20.-- *It shall defile.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the sin is in a settled habit, and the evil consuming of the soul has settled in through the ulcer. ⟦s3⟧ Whence he called it a leprosy flowering forth out of the wound.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The flesh and the skin which the fire had burned,* etc. [n: (ISICH.)] He seems to repeat the things said above, but partly he says the same, partly he adds; for he sets down another passion, but the same indication. ⟦s2⟧ For he treated of the ulcer which is begotten by some sickness of neglected flesh, and grows. ⟦s3⟧ The ulcer, then, of our flesh is pleasure, drunkenness, gluttony and things of this kind, which come forth when the body runs riot and itches within itself. ⟦s4⟧ But now he treats of a greater plague, that is, of the burning of fire. ⟦s5⟧ Of which it is said: *The fire shall consume the adversaries [n: (Isa. LXXIV)]*. [var: Vulg. Is 26:11 *ignis adversarios tuos devoret*, Heb 10:27 *ignis aemulatio, quae consumptura est adversarios*; no verse reads as printed, and Isaiah has sixty-six chapters]

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Because the plague,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In the scar of a burning the Manichees have leprosy, who in the torment of abstinence [0334D] burn up their own bodies, and through unbelief beget thence not cleanness, but leprosy.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *A man or a woman.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, who errs concerning the divinity, as the heretics, who not only deny the Father to be the maker of the world, but even affirm him to be the author of evils, as Manichaeus.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Or the beard,* etc. [n: (ISID.)] They bear leprosy in the beard who think perversely of the apostles and disciples of Christ, and feign that they preached false things. ⟦s2⟧ For the beard is the ornament of a man, and the apostles are the teachers of the body of Christ.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISICH.)] We find that the lawgiver set down six kinds of leprosy, etc., *as far as* because they defend the perfidy of their error with open depravity.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] He sets down diverse colours of leprosy, etc., *as far [0335A] as* it signifies a lying tongue and weakness.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *He shall shut him up seven days, and on the day,* etc., not so much for punishment as for the sake of a strict trial; for the time is to be observed in all cases, more strictly in those whose tongue runs unbridled about the divinity.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *If on the seventh day.* ⟦s2⟧ Because the word in which one offends, if he who has sinned once denies it, ought not to be adjudged. ⟦s3⟧ For who judges a man guilty by that which he has confessed with his tongue, when he denies that he uttered it with his will?

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And his garments being washed he shall be clean,* etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Some things committed before baptism, etc., *as far as* of which it is said: *He that sinneth against the Holy Spirit, it shall not be forgiven him,* etc. [var: Vulg. Mt 12:32 *qui autem dixerit contra Spiritum sanctum, non remittetur ei*, "he that shall speak against"]

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *Black hairs,* that is, if he has mourned his impiety, and has done true penance. ⟦s2⟧ For they that mourn [0335B] and do penance are disfigured with a black habit.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *A man or a woman,* etc. [n: (ISICH.)] In the prevarication of the divine doctrine, etc., *as far as: whereby they may not live.*

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *He is bald and clean.* ⟦s2⟧ Simple, not to the foolish; for there is no one to whom at least a few hairs do not remain.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 44.-- *Whosoever therefore is spotted,* etc. [n: (ISID.)] Heretics are cast out of the Church until they be purged. ⟦s2⟧ With their tunics unsewn, that is, their secrets made manifest, they are commanded to sit. ⟦s3⟧ With the head bared, that their nakedness may be seen by all. ⟦s4⟧ With the mouth closed, that they may not speak impious things any further.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 46.-- *He shall dwell outside the camp.* ⟦s2⟧ So John shuts out the intelligible leper from the intelligible dwelling: [0335C] *He that bringeth not this doctrine, receive him not into the house, and say not God speed to him [n: (II Joan. 10)]*. [var: Vulg. 2Jo 10 *Si quis venit ad vos, et hanc doctrinam non affert, nolite recipere eum in domum, nec ave ei dixeritis*]

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISICH.)] These things are ridiculous when interpreted according to the letter, etc., *as far as* lest it be said to him, *Thou that preachest that men should not steal, stealest; but to the sinner God hath said: Why dost thou declare my justices? [n: (Rom. II.)]*

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 47.-- *A woollen garment,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Woollen, precious, and warming the mind, etc., *as far as* namely, and good works.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 48-- *In the warp,* etc. [n: (HIERON.)] The warp is the soul: which, when it is twisted by the spindle of temptation into the fineness of sons, is bound together by the solidity of a firmer virtue. ⟦s2⟧ The soul, then, stands, yet as erect; the sense of the body lies beneath as the woof; but some sins are [0335D] of the body, others of the soul.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISICH.)] They who attend to the letter alone, etc., *as far as* but also of that which is wont to come about concerning the virtues.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] Leprosy, in a linen garment, etc., *as far as* in the manner of life of this life.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A skin or whatsoever,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The temptation of tribulation, etc., *as far as* nor let him rashly receive it as clean.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 49.-- *If white,* etc. [n: (ID.)] LXX: *Greening and growing yellow,* that is, pride, which flowers for a little: whence, *As the herbs of the field they shall quickly fall [n: (Psal. XXXVI)]*, or vainglory. ⟦s2⟧ For Christ wills the virtues to be without vain glory.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 56.-- *He shall break it off.* ⟦s2⟧ Through penance [0336A] he shall cut off the passion; pride is broken off when humility is brought near; vain glory, when flattery and vain praise are taken away.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 57.-- *A flying leprosy. [n: (GREG., hom. X in Ezech.)]* Sometimes we recognise that we have done evil, and we avoid it, etc., *as far as* through immoderate continence he passes over to the vice of wrath.
