## CHAPTER XIX. (cont.)

VERS. 28.-- *Stigmata.* The lawgiver takes care also for our comeliness, that we may not misuse the flesh even in those things which disfigure the body.

VERS. 29.-- *Prostitute not thy daughter.* The carnal one, or the soul. Whence Psal. XXI: *Deliver, O God, my soul from the sword, and my only one from the hand of the dog.* These, [0352D] by withdrawing from God, commit fornication with the demons.

VERS. 30.-- *Keep my sabbaths.* From the one sabbath every lawful rest is called a sabbath. So from one general abstinence the lawgiver is at pains to run through the particulars, commanding us to abstain from every evil. Whence he adds: *And fear my sanctuary.* For as they who approach worthily sanctify it, so they who presume wickedly, and approach unclean, so far as in them lies, pollute it, as Nadab and Abiu. Whence the Apostle: *Among you are many weak and feeble,* etc. [n: (I Cor. XL.)] Thence it is added: *I am the Lord,* holy, namely, and also dwelling in the holy ones and avenging the wrong done to the holy ones

VERS. 31.-- *To the magicians.* LXX: *Ventriloquists, that is,* [0353A] pythons, or false prophets, who out of the belly of their heart bring forth certain fables and stinking doctrines.

*Neither to the soothsayers,* etc. LXX: *Poisoners,* who, namely, invoke the names of the demons, and sometimes heal the body, that they may kill the soul.

VERS. 32.-- *Before the hoary head,* etc. *In old men is wisdom, and in length of time prudence [n: (Job XII)]*. [var: Vulg. Job 12:12 *In antiquis est sapientia*, "In the ancients is wisdom"] Hence Paul says: *Let the priests be accounted worthy of double honour, especially they who labour in the word and doctrine [n: (I Tim. V)]*.

VERS. 33.-- *If a stranger dwell,* etc. The land given us by God is the cultivation of the virtues, namely, and good doctrine. If therefore any man will be converted to our faith, and be enrolled a citizen of our land, let us not afflict him, nor lay upon him a heavy yoke, as the Pharisees did, [0353B] of whom it is said: *They bind heavy and insupportable burdens, but with their own finger they will not move them [n: (Matth. XXIII)]*. Let him be to us as one home-born, who, namely, has been nourished in the faith, and has grown in piety. Hence Peter says to those who wished to circumcise the believers of the Gentiles: *Why tempt ye God, to put a yoke upon the necks of the disciples? [n: (Act. XV.)]*

VERS. 35.-- *Do not,* etc. In the foregoing he commands that justice be kept in judgment, here in weights and measures, and that we do not misuse to injustice the boundary-stones of justice: for these are the boundary-stones of sellers and buyers, as the laws are of judges; that men may beware of every injustice against a neighbour. For what to the rich man are the possessions of fields and the treasures of money, that to the poor man is a measure of wine and of wheat, and the like.

[0353C] [n: (ISICH.)] *Do not,* etc. In the judgment of the mind we ought not to act unjustly, nor to corrupt the measures of natural justice, judging our own sins in one way and our neighbour's in another. *For with what judgment we shall have judged, it shall be judged also of us; and with what measure we shall have measured, it shall be measured again to us [n: (Matth. VII)]*. [var: Vulg. Mt 7:2 *judicaveritis ... judicabimini ... mensi fueritis, remetietur vobis*, in the second person: "with what judgment you judge, you shall be judged"]

VERS. 36.-- *A just balance,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Let us have divine balances also, and just divine measures; that is, let us keep the laws of divine Scripture holily and justly: bringing in nothing of our own, taking away nothing of theirs, lest this be said to us: *Why do you transgress the commandment of God for your traditions? [n: (Matth. XV)]*.
