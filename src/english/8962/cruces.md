### Divergences found, weighed, and NOT marked — with the reason

- **0721A, Neh 8:1** — Migne *Congregatusque omnis populus*; Vulg. *Congregatusque **est** omnis

- **0722C, Neh 8:9** — Migne *Dixit autem Nehemias, **et** ipse est **Athersata***; Vulg. *Dixit

## §3 · `[sic: …]` — CARRIED TYPE (2)

Both are the same shape — **a real word with its final syllable gone** — and both are
rendered AND marked, per 7a⁗: the marker records the plate, it does not stand in for the

### 0721D · *peregrim* for *peregrini*

*meminisse nos oportet, quia* **incolae sumus in terra, et peregrim sicut patres nostri**.
Rendered "and **sojourners** [sic: peregrim] **as our fathers were**". The marker sits
**inside** the already-italic scripture span and takes no second italic marker, per Pattern
10; the verifier's substring test still binds it to the Latin twin.

### 0721C · *Christ* for *Christi*

*quia qui* **Christ** *sunt, carnem suam cum vitiis et concupiscentiis crucifigunt* (Gal
5:24). Rendered "they who are **Christ's** [sic: *Christ*] crucify their flesh…". Without
the rendering the clause would have lost its predicate entirely — the Tobit failure exactly.

⚠ **These two are flagged together as a candidate DIGITIZATION defect, not certainly
Migne's.** Two truncations of a final *-i*, seven lines apart, in a book otherwise free of
broken type, is the profile of a transcription fault rather than a compositor's. Ruth's
*significaRuth* (0534A) was exactly this class and proved to be Corpus Corporum's line-join,
patched upstream. **Col. 721 was not among the pages read.** If a plate read shows *peregrini*
and *Christi*, these are `data/tei-patches/8962.json` fixes and both `[sic:]` markers come
out. Marked as the plate we have, disclosed as suspect.

## §4 · MIGNE'S OWN, RENDERED LITERALLY

### 0724D–0725A · *Piscis bonus … Piscis autem malus* — ⭐ **RESOLVED AT THE PLATE. THE HOLE WAS OURS, NOT MIGNE'S, AND NO MARKER IS OWED.**

**This was logged as the work's one unconstruable passage, rendered literally, and both a
`[cj:]` and an `[ed:]` were declined on the ground that "the defect is a hole between words,
not a wrong word." The blind reader flagged the same site independently. The plate has now
said why.** Our text read:

> *Piscis bonus, est pia fides, quam qui a Domino **malus**, infirma cogitatio quae se curis
> hujus mundi ultra modum immergit…*

**p. 367 prints three lines, and Corpus Corporum skipped the middle one entire:**

>     (1) ibid.) Piscis bonus, est pia fides, quam qui à Domino
>     (2) petit serpentem infidelitatis non accipit. Piscis au-
>     (3) tem malus, infirma cogitatio quæ se curis hujus

The transcription resumed at `malus` and **also dropped the orphan turnover `tem`**, so eight
words vanished and the two halves fused into one clause that could not be parsed. `Piscis
au-|tem` is the join: the eye went from the first line's end to the third line's, and the
hyphenated word at the break is what let the seam close invisibly.

**Patched in `data/tei-patches/8962.json`, re-chunked, and re-rendered here from the restored
Latin.** The sentence now turns on Luke 11:11 — *quam qui a Domino **petit serpentem
infidelitatis non accipit*** — the good fish asked of the Lord against the serpent given in
its place, which is exactly the antithesis the following *Piscis **autem** malus* requires and
that our text had lost along with the *autem*.

⛔ **Nothing is marked, and that is the correct outcome, not an omission.** `[sic:]`, `[cj:]`
and `[ed:]` all say something about the text a reader is looking at; here the words are simply
**there**, and Migne's page was never defective. The only trace this episode leaves is this
entry.

⚑ **Register — and it is the whole lesson of the site: the ONLY detector for a dropped line
is that the passage will not construe.** No count, parity, anchor, band or ratio check can see
one, because everything that survives the skip is internally consistent: the note count was
right, the anchors were right, the italic spans were right, and the word ratio sat mid-band.
**A silent repair here would have destroyed the only evidence there was.** Rendering the
wreck literally, declining to conjecture over it, and naming it as the first thing a plate
reader should be sent to is what recovered the text — the three moves are one move.

⚑ **One word is supplied in the new English and it is named here**: *Piscis **autem** malus,
infirma cogitatio…* has no copula, because Migne's own *Piscis bonus, **est** pia fides* two
clauses earlier supplies it by ellipsis. English cannot omit it, so "But a bad fish **is** a
weak thought" carries it. Nothing else was added; the *quam … quaerunt vendere* relative is
left running exactly as long as it runs in the Latin.

⚠ **Second patch on the same page, six lines below.** The plate prints **`coangustatis`**
where our text had `coangustati`. The form is dative/ablative plural and can only agree with
**`nobis`**, not with the nominative `Tyrii` — so the gloss is on the buyers, not the sellers.
My first English read "which the Tyrians, **that is, the straitened,** seek to sell to us on
the sabbath", attaching it to the Tyrians; it now reads "which the Tyrians seek to sell **to
us, that is, to the straitened,** on the sabbath". **This is not cosmetic**: it is *we* who are
narrowed by worldly cares in Bede's figure, and the old English said the Tyrians were.

### 0714D · *quae comparatio terrenorum quasi dextera ad sinistram sunt* — `[cj: *comparatio*; read *comparatione*]`

The nominative *comparatio* stands in a clause that needs the ablative of comparison. Taken
literally the hundred **is** "the comparison of earthly things", where the argument makes it
the heavenly goods **compared with** earthly ones — the English asserts what he does not.
The gloss is additive: the printed word keeps its English and the conjecture stands beside it.

### 0716D · *benedicitur turris Ophel* — `[cj: *benedicitur*; read *bene dicitur*, "is well named"]`

*Denique ubi in Michaea scriptum est:* **Et tu turris gregis nebulosa***, benedicitur turris
Ophel.* The whole paragraph is an etymology: Ophel = *tenebrarum vel nubili*, and Micheas's
*nebulosa* is adduced to show the name fits. **Nothing in it blesses anything.** Read as the
run-together *bene dicitur* the sentence is exactly the etymological pay-off the paragraph
has been building. Rendered "the tower of Ophel is blessed", conjecture beside it.
⚠ Deliberately **not** marked `[sic:]`: *benedicitur* is a real and common word, so Pattern
12 has nothing to wrap and Pattern 10 does not reach it. This is precisely the 11081
*munde*/*mundo* shape that Pattern 18 was made for.

### 0721C · *aliisque exsultationis laudent nomen Domini* — `[cj: *aliisque*; read *labiisque*]`

*sicut adipe et pinguedine repleantur, aliisque exsultationis laudent nomen Domini* — the
allusion is Psal. 62:6, *labiis exsultationis laudabit os meum*, and *exsultationis* is left
governing nothing at all by *aliis*. Rendered "and with others of exultation may praise the
name of the Lord", conjecture beside it. **The rival considered and rejected:** *alii …
exsultationis* as a partitive ("and others of exultation") — rejected because it leaves
*exsultationis* a genitive with no head noun either way, and because the psalm verse is
already the paragraph's source for *adipe et pinguedine* (Psal. 62:6 in full).

### Declined — read clean, or read and let alone

- **0715A** *Aedificavit quoque agens industrie omnem murum* — Migne prints no commas round
  *agens industrie* where the Clementine does. Punctuation only, inside a quotation; the
  English uses commas because the participial phrase cannot sit unmarked in English prose.
  Recorded, not marked.
- **0717B** *ab altitudine contemplationis, **quae** … **suspendunt*** — a plural relative and
  plural verb against a singular *contemplationis*. Rendered "which suspend"; no emendation
  offered. Number, like gender (Pattern 9), is Migne's and is legible on the facing column.
- **0720B** *nec unquam **desinit** qui … **ambiant*** — singular verb, plural relative. Rendered
  "nor do they ever cease who…", i.e. normalized to English plural, **because English cannot
  print a subject-verb mismatch without reading as our typo**. Logged so the mismatch is not
  lost: it is on the plate side and visible there.
- **0726A** *populus … cum opera virtutum … **operantur*** — same class, same treatment.
- **0714C** *filius Joachim, **qui** post patrem suum Jesum filium Josedec … pontificatum tenuit* —
  the *qui* may be Eliasib or Joachim. English "who" keeps the ambiguity exactly as Latin
  does; nothing needed deciding, and nothing was decided.
- **0717C** *curas rerum temporalium, Domino scilicet animae subjugatas* — two datives in a row.
  Rendered **"subjected, namely, to the Lord of the soul"**, taking *Domino … animae* as one
  phrase. **The rival — "to the Lord [and] to the soul" — LOSES because it requires supplying
  a coordinating conjunction the plate does not print**, which is the exact defect Ruth's
  0533D produced and both blind readers caught.
- **0722C** *erat enim **dionymos*** — a Greek loan, "of two names". Rendered "for he was of two
  names" rather than carried in italics: it is a real word used correctly, so Pattern 12 does
  not apply, and italicizing it would add an italic span the Latin does not have.
- **0715A, 0715A** *Verba dierum* — Migne's Hebrew-style title for Paralipomenon. Rendered
  literally, **"the Words of the Days"**, not silently converted to "Chronicles" or
  "Paralipomenon" (which he uses himself at 0716D, and which is rendered as he prints it there).
- **Column-band arithmetic** — 0725B is followed by 0726A with 0725C–D absent, because **Tobit
  (8970) begins at 0725B and the two works interleave by band.** Runbook false positive 1;
  no `[ed:]`, no patch, no plate check.
- **0722A** *(BEDA, ubi supra, [0722B] cap. 28.)* — a siglum set in **italics in the body**
  rather than as an `[n:]` note, with a column anchor inside it. Reproduced exactly: italic
  span, contents unanglicized, anchor in place. It is the only one in the book.
- **0724C** *etc. usque ad* at VERS. 23 lacks the comma its 60 siblings carry (*etc.* then
  *usque ad*). Reproduced bare.

## §7 · THE FOUR TESTS, RUN ON MY OWN FILES

1. **Marker-strip.** Every `[sic:]`, `[cj:]`, `[var:]`, `[n:]` was stripped and the residue
   read for stranded articles, prepositions and doubled marks. **Both `[sic:]` sentences read
   whole without their brackets** ("and sojourners as our fathers were"; "they who are
   Christ's crucify their flesh"), and all three `[cj:]` sentences carry Migne's own word in
   plain English before the gloss. No clause lost its object.
2. **Negation hosts.** All 37 printed negatives (*non* ×25, *nec* ×2, *neque* ×1,
   *necdum* ×1, *ne* ×2, *nunquam* ×1, *nil* ×1, *nullo* ×1, *sine* ×2, *noluerunt* ×1) were listed, each was
   assigned to the **single Latin word it attaches to**, and the English was checked for the
   negative **on that word**. Four fired 2a's trigger condition (a negative near a quantifier
   or correlative) and all four were proved from structure, not from what reads well:
   *non parvo tempore* (0714C, on *parvo*), *non solum media pars* (0718C, on *media pars*),
   *nec solum apostoli, sed et prophetae* (0719C, on *apostoli*), *non solum qui …
   aedificarunt, sed etiam infirmiores* (0724B, on the relative clause). The two *non … sed*
   phrases where English word order puts "not" before the verb (0723D *non suo sensu … sed*;
   0720B *non … de neophytis … sed de illis*) are the shape 8960 @0740B settled as correct.
3. **Punctuation by column band.** The anchors partition both twins identically, so `?`, `:`,
   `;`, `«`, `»` and `!` were counted **band by band**, not by total. **The first run returned
   twenty mismatched bands** — five deletions of a mark Migne prints (0713D *lugebat:*, 0714B
   *pervagatur;*, 0718C *aedificant:*, 0719B *patet:*, 0720A *suscipiant;*) and fifteen marks
   supplied where he prints a comma. **All twenty were repaired**; the band comparison now
   returns zero mismatches on every mark in every chunk. ⛔ Totals would have hidden most of
   this: chunk 0002's colons came out 4 against 5 while its semicolons came out 2 against 0.
4. **Anchor positions (2b).** Each of the 52 column anchors was checked against **the word it
   stands beside in the Latin**, not merely for presence. One was moved (0718D, from after the
   sentence's full stop to before it, matching *ab Ecclesia [0718D] repellunt*). The rest
   stand at the same word boundary in both columns.
5. **The fourth test.** Each paragraph was read back against the Latin asking only *does it
   assert what he asserts*. Three repairs came out of it and nothing else. **0713A**
   *Hierosolymorum moenia* had been read as "the walls of the men of Jerusalem", over-reading
   a plain neuter plural place-name. **0724D** had been given a plural *qui* ("they who from
   the Lord") where the text prints the singular, which made a broken sentence read slightly
   smoother than it was — the exact tell §5 of the brief names, and the tightening is what
   kept the wreck visible until the plate could be read. **0725A** *coangustatis* hung the
   gloss on the wrong noun (§4), found on the re-render after the patch.
   ⚑ **And the test's limit is worth recording beside its successes: it could tell that 0724D
   did not assert anything, but nothing in it — or in any other check here — could say that
   eight words were missing rather than mangled.** Only the plate could.

6. **Italic-span parity.** With our English-only markers (`[var:]`, `[cj:]`, `[sic:]`)
   stripped, the two columns carry **199 italic spans each — 19 / 50 / 38 / 40 / 52 — in the
   same order and the same places.** ⚠ The two `[sic:]` markers are deliberately set
   differently and it is not an inconsistency: 0721C's sits in roman prose and takes italics
   (`[sic: *Christ*]`, Pattern 12's own form), while 0721D's sits **inside** an already-italic
   scripture span and takes none (`[sic: peregrim]`), because nested italics are not
   expressible — the rule Pattern 10 states for exactly this case.
