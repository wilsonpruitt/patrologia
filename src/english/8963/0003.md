## CHAPTER III.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Of the priests that were anointed,* etc. [n: (RAB. in Num., tom. II.)] The four sons of Aaron, etc., *as far as* they are consumed by the fire of divine vengeance.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Bring the tribe. [n: (AUG., quaest. 4 in Num.)] Take the tribe of Levi,* etc., *as far as* in the other camps round about of the children of Israel.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0386B]VERS. 10.-- *The stranger.* [n: (ID.)] *The stranger that shall touch it shall die.* ⟦s2⟧ It must be asked how he said it, etc., *as far as* for from hence he was speaking.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *I have taken the Levites. [n: (ID., quaest. 6.)]* What is it that the Levites, etc., *as far as* nor are the descendants of the Levites or of their cattle reckoned for these.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *[n: (ORIG. hom. 3 in Num.)] I have taken the Levites from the midst of the children of Israel for every firstborn,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The Levites are taken for the firstborn, etc., *as far as* so that it is more to be taken for a firstborn than to be born a firstborn.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ LXX: *From the midst of the children of Israel,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Levi is the third among the sons of Israel; from what midst therefore are the Levites taken? ⟦s3⟧ I find the Sunamite answering the king of Israel confidently: *In the midst of my people I dwell* [0386C] [n: (IV Reg. IV)]. ⟦s4+5⟧ And John saying of Christ: *There standeth one in the midst of you, whom you know not.* [var: Vulg. *medius autem vestrum stetit*, "hath stood"; the gloss below turns upon the present tense] I think therefore that he who has never turned aside to the right hand or to the left can be said to stand in the midst, *who did no sin, neither was guile found in his mouth [n: (Isa. LIII)]*. ⟦s6⟧ And because he always stands, he is said to stand in the midst. ⟦s7⟧ But he who is his imitator, like the Sunamite, is indeed not said to stand, because sometimes he leans either to the right or to the left, *for no one is clean from filth, not even if his life were of one day [n: (Job XV)]*: yet he is said to dwell in the midst of the people. ⟦s8⟧ The Levites therefore are taken from the midst of the children of Israel. ⟦s9⟧ For the Levites are they who did not know their own right hand and left, but, following Moses, that is, the law of God, did not spare father or mother. ⟦s10⟧ And thou therefore, if, when temptation comes and [0386D] the wrath of sin, thou neither lean to the right hand nor to the left, nor transgress the law of God, but stand in the midst fixed and stable, nor bend thy knees to sin, nor follow the head of a beast, that is, the image of folly: thou shalt be taken from the midst of the children of Israel, and shalt be placed in the number of the firstborn.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He openeth.* ⟦s2⟧ Not everyone who has opened the womb is worthy of the primacy, whence: *The wicked are alienated from the womb, they have gone astray from the belly, they have spoken false things [n: (Psal. LVII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ For how could anyone go astray from the way of God as soon as he came forth from his mother's womb? ⟦s4⟧ Or how could a boy but lately brought forth speak false things? ⟦s5⟧ It is necessary therefore to seek such a belly and womb as this may fit: *The wicked are alienated from the womb,* etc. ⟦s6⟧ And that will be the [0387A] womb which every firstborn opens who is sanctified to the Lord. ⟦s7⟧ The Lord opened the womb of Lia, which was closed, and she bore the patriarchs; and likewise Rachel's, that she too might bear, whose sight was keen and her aspect comely [n: (Gen. XXIX, XXX)]. ⟦s8⟧ But in many places of Scripture thou wilt find wombs opened, of which, if thou consider each according to its place, thou wilt find how sinners go astray from the womb, and others opening the womb are sanctified in the order of the firstborn.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *From which.* [n: (ORIG.)] Because the firstborn of Israel were not sanctified before, etc., *as far as* and might place him in the order of the firstborn.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Number the sons of Levi.* [n: (ID.)] Let us review more attentively, etc., *as far as* and with perpetual watches they celebrate the divine ministries.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0387B]VERS. 17.-- *Levi by their names.* ⟦s2⟧ Levi, added or taken up. ⟦s3⟧ This is the order of the doctors, which, chosen out of the world by the grace of God for the divine service, is united to the companies of the angels. ⟦s4⟧ Whence: *I have chosen you out of the world [n: (Joan. XV)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And elsewhere: *But they are as the angels of God in heaven [n: (Matth. XXII)]*. [var: Vulg. *erunt sicut angeli Dei in caelo*, "shall be as the angels"]

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Gerson and Caath and Merari.* ⟦s2⟧ The three sons of Levi signify the preachers of the holy Trinity, notable in confession, wonderful in faith, hope and charity. ⟦s3⟧ Nor does the interpretation of their names in any way conflict with this exposition, but greatly agrees with it. ⟦s4⟧ For Gerson is interpreted a stranger, Caath patience, or grinding teeth. ⟦s5⟧ Merari bitter things, or bitterness; for the minister of God knows himself a stranger and a pilgrim in the world, and [0387C] is patient. ⟦s6⟧ He grinds the word of God with the tooth of discretion, according to the capacities of his hearers; and showing the world bitter to himself, he calls others back from pleasures. ⟦s7⟧ These are numbered from one month old, because they ought to consecrate the beginning of life to God, lest, promoted after many crimes, they cause the name of God to be blasphemed, whence: *A bishop must be blameless,* etc. [n: (I Tim. III.)]

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *These shall pitch behind the tabernacle.* ⟦s2⟧ A fitting order of the sons of Levi is shown, namely where those who are first in order should keep the east side, Moses and Aaron with their sons having the custody of the sanctuary in the midst of the children of Israel. ⟦s3⟧ Let the Caathites, from whom Moses and Aaron sprang, hold the south side, under the care of Eleazar the priest, who is the elder among the sons of Aaron. ⟦s4⟧ After these the Gersonites to the western [0387D] side, under the care of Eliasaph. ⟦s5⟧ Last the Merarites to the northern side, under the guardianship of Ithamar the younger son of Aaron. ⟦s6⟧ But in these it is morally expressed that the ministers of the church ought diligently to keep their own order, and not to seek greater things than their proper place demands.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *On the west, under the prince Eliasaph the son of Lael.* ⟦s2⟧ Where the day is shut up, the stars set. ⟦s3⟧ In these it is signified that the ministers of the tabernacle ought to flee concupiscence, to mortify carnal desires by God's help, to await the last day and the end of labour, so that, dead to the world and living to God, they may be able to say that: *But God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ [n: (Gal. VI)]*.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0388A]VERS. 29.-- *To the south.* ⟦s2⟧ This signifies that, after the light of knowledge has been received, the preacher ought with discretion to bestow the word of patience and of consolation upon his neighbours. ⟦s3⟧ Under Eleazar, that is, by the help of God, according to that: *Unless the Lord build the house, they have laboured in vain that build it.*

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *On the side.* ⟦s2⟧ The last, the Merarites under Ithamar (who is interpreted bitter, or where the palm is), obtained by lot the north side, where an icy dread endures. ⟦s3⟧ In which the ministers of the Church are warned not to grow torpid through security; because although they have already entered the Church by the door of faith, have received the light of knowledge, have mortified the desires of the flesh, yet they have not yet come to perfect sweetness, that is, to eternal blessedness. ⟦s4⟧ Something bitter remains; [0388B] one must contend with him who directs his throne to the north, whose hardness it is impossible to soften; whence: *His heart shall be hardened as a stone,* etc. [n: (Isa. XIV; Job XXI)]. ⟦s5⟧ But when he shall be overcome, the palm being obtained, one must exult and rejoice.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *To the east side.* ⟦s2⟧ The east side signifies the rising of faith, where Moses, who is interpreted taken up out of the water, and Aaron, a mountain of strength, encamp with their sons. ⟦s3⟧ For they who preside in the Church, to whom the keys of the heavenly kingdom have been committed [n: (Matth. VI)], ought to know the order of entrance, and to preach it to others. ⟦s4⟧ Because everyone must enter into the light of faith through the gate of confession to the water of baptism, that, taken up out of the water, he may be a son of adoption; and, begetting by the strength of constancy sons of good [0388C] work, may diligently guard the entrance of faith by which he entered the Church, lest, idle and useless, he be cast away.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *Twenty-two thousand.* ⟦s2⟧ Fitly are the Levites entitled by the number of twenty-two. ⟦s3⟧ For by this number the Hebrew letters are comprehended, and, according to some, the books of the Old Testament. ⟦s4⟧ God's ministers are warned by this prescription to live and to teach according to the law of God, and in no way to go beyond it.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ORIG., hom. 4 in Num.)] Many are the differences of numbers, etc. ⟦s2⟧ *as far as* the mystery also of human generation is expounded.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 47.-- *Five sicles.* ⟦s2⟧ Mystically, whoever wishes to consecrate to God the beginning of his conversation and of his will ought diligently to guard the five senses of the body, [0388D] lest through wantonness he slip away into error or unclean desires. ⟦s3⟧ But according to the sicle of the sanctuary, which is 20 oboli, that is, according to the doctrine of the decalogue, which consists in the two precepts of charity, let him refer all his works to the house of the high priest and of his sons.
