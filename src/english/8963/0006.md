## CHAPTER VII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *It came to pass moreover,* etc., *the tabernacle. [n: (RAB. in Num., tom. II.)]* The Church, or the perfect, in whom the spices of the virtues and of prayers are burned, or the humanity of Christ: in which he presented to the Father the victim of our redemption. ⟦s2⟧ For he himself is priest, he himself victim and altar; because, holding death in his power, he took it upon himself by his own will. ⟦s3⟧ Whence: *No man taketh [0394A] my life from me, but I lay it down, and I take it up again [n: (Joan. X)]* [var: Vulg. *Nemo tollit eam a me: sed ego pono eam a meipso, et potestatem habeo ponendi eam, et potestatem habeo iterum sumendi eam*, "No man taketh it from me, but I lay it down of myself"].

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *The princes of Israel and the heads,* etc. [n: (RAB. in Num.)] The patriarchs, the prophets and the apostles, etc., *as far as it shall be done for them by my Father.*

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Six wagons.* [n: (RAB.)] The wagons which the children of Israel offered to the Levites for the carriage of the tabernacle signify temporal comforts, which devout peoples repay to their preachers, so that without care and hindrance they may have leisure for the divine service. ⟦s2⟧ And so that they also who announce the Gospel may live by the Gospel: *For if they sow spiritual things, it is no great matter if they reap temporal things [n: (I Cor. IX)]* [var: Vulg. *Si nos vobis spiritualia seminavimus, magnum est si nos carnalia vestra metamus?*, "If we have sown unto you spiritual things, is it a great matter if we reap your carnal things?"].

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The wagons rightly adorned with faith and with the affection of mercy, that they may be acceptable to God, are twelve oxen: for they who [0394B] labour in the service of the saints under the yoke of apostolic doctrine do all things honestly and in order. ⟦s2⟧ Or by oxen we understand teachers, who work worthily in the field of the Lord. ⟦s3⟧ Of whom it is said: *Thou shalt not muzzle the mouth of the ox that treadeth out the corn [n: (Deut. XXV; I Tim. V)]*. ⟦s4⟧ For they who by living and by preaching keep the apostolic rule are to be honoured with double honour.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *One ox,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Because the unity of faith, etc., *as far as* that they ought to hasten to the tabernacle of God with due obedience.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 9.-- *But to the sons of Caath.* [n: (RAB.)] It is written above, etc., *as far as* nor do they themselves who seem to have something of exercise and of learning.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *The princes offered for the dedication of the altar on the day whereon.* ⟦s2⟧ Each one according to his power ought to offer faith, [0394C] doctrine, and good work to the profit and honour of the Church.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *It was anointed, his oblation before the altar.* ⟦s2⟧ At the time of the incarnation, when after the resurrection and the ascension the disciples received the outpouring of the Holy Spirit [n: (Act. II)].

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Let the princes offer gifts on each several day.* ⟦s2⟧ The preachers are the leaders of the right way, who, although they offer the same doctrine of faith and the same examples of good work, yet distribute their oblations according to the diversity of place and of time.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *A silver dish.* ⟦s2⟧ These have a narrow mouth, and signify the narrowness of the letter of the law and the obscurity of the prophets.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ The various vessels made for offering libations are the various [0394D] distinctions of the word according to the various capacity of the hearers. ⟦s2⟧ For the wise are to be taught in one way, the unwise in another; the rich in one way, the poor in another; the whole in one way, the sick in another; in one way the rude people of the Jews under the shadow of the law, in another the Christian, beholding in the truth of the Gospel the mysteries of the law laid open, who is to be nourished unto a perfect man.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *A silver bowl having seventy sicles,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Spiritual knowledge is in the law and in the Gospel, which by the ministry of spiritual grace was written and set forth for the salvation of men, hence the sacrifice is duly made, because nothing is so acceptable to God as the meditation of the law with the execution of the work.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (RAB., in Num., tom. II.)] The bowl, first made of glass, [0395A] etc., *as far as* in the New Testament it was more manifestly given.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *A little mortar.* [n: (RAB.)] The heart of the elect, full of divine wisdom, containing the spices of the virtues, whence it is said: *Because the multitude of believers had one heart and one soul [n: (Act. IV)]*.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of ten sicles,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Because with the fulfilling of the ten commandments they send forth the odour of doctrine and of prayer, that they may deserve to receive the penny of eternal life.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *An ox of the herd,* etc. [n: (ID.)] These, various persons, etc., *as far as* we might strive always to reform our life into better things.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ He offers an ox who spends useful labour upon his neighbours in the Church of God; a ram, who directs himself and others in the way [0395B] of justice; a lamb of a year old, who is simple and innocent. ⟦s2⟧ All these in holocaust, for the love of God alone and the desire of eternal life. ⟦s3⟧ He offers a he-goat for sin who does not cease to repent and to pray for his sins. ⟦s4⟧ In the sacrifice of peace offerings he offers two oxen, who with good works fulfils the two precepts of charity, namely, the love of God, by keeping his commandments; and of his neighbour, by taking thought for his necessities, on which the whole law hangs, and the prophets. ⟦s5⟧ And he offers five rams, five he-goats, five lambs of a year old, because he strongly leads the five senses of the body in the way of truth, and restrains them from sins, and sets forth a pattern of simplicity.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 85.-- *Two thousand,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] In the silver vessels, etc., *as far as* thither one does not come.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0395C]VERS. 86.-- *Twelve golden mortars.* [n: (ID.)] Of which each severally has ten sicles, and all together a hundred and twenty sicles of gold, signify that the hearts of the elect keep the knowledge of the law in all things according to the apostolic doctrine. ⟦s2⟧ For twelve times ten make a hundred and twenty, at which number the primitive Church in Jerusalem received the Paraclete Spirit [n: (Act. II)]. ⟦s3⟧ Whence it is added:

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In weight,* etc. [n: (ID.)] For the sanctuary is the Church, whose rule of faith and of understanding we follow, believing that all things which are written in the law and the prophets are wholesome for us. ⟦s2⟧ But whatever is held and understood or observed outside the unity of the Church begets the damnation of the soul.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 87.-- *Oxen,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Oxen, rams and [0395D] lambs, comprehended in the number of twelve, signify those who, whether in the office of teacher, or in the headship of some honour, or living simply, strive to do all things according to the apostolic and prophetic doctrine.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He-goats,* etc. [n: (ID.)] These are they who strive to accomplish penance according to the rule of the apostolic faith.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 88.-- *Oxen,* etc. [n: (ID.)] In these is signified spiritual knowledge with the perfection of good works, because the course of the day is accomplished in twenty-four hours: they signify that the light of knowledge and of faith is necessary to every Christian.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Sixty.* ⟦s2⟧ Sixty, that is, six times ten, shows the perfection of the commandments. ⟦s3⟧ For it is necessary [0396A] that prelates, subjects, and penitents do all things according to the doctrine of the Church, so that their oblation may hold the rite and order of peace offerings, that they may deserve the grace of God, and not his offence.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 89.-- *And when,* etc. [n: (GREG., lib. XXXIII Moral., cap. 21, tom. II.)] What is it that Moses, etc., *as far as* and how kindly he descends to the lowest, and mightily runs back to the highest.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *From the propitiatory.* ⟦s2⟧ Because through the grace of the divine propitiation God opened to us, after the fault of transgression, the way of truth.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Between the two cherubim.* ⟦s2⟧ Because through an angelic vision, not in his own substance, God appeared to Moses; whence: *The law ordained by angels in the hand of a mediator [n: (Gal. III)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Or because through both Testaments with a harmonious [0396B] voice he instructs us unto the faith of the truth; or because through the Son, who appeared in the flesh in the midst of the two Testaments, the Father spoke to the human race.
