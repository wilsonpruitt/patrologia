## CHAPTER XXI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *When he had heard this.* ⟦s2⟧ The Canaanite heard that Israel had come into the place of the spies, where the people had once given offense; he joined battle, and [0415A] led Israel away captive. ⟦s3⟧ And again in the same place there is fighting: by reason of a vow the victor is vanquished, and the vanquished prevail. ⟦s4⟧ In which it is understood that when enemies have set upon us, destitute of God's help, and have taken us captive, we should not despair: for it can come about that we conquer where we were conquered.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Anathema. [n: (AUG., quaest. 40 in Num.)]* Another reading, etc., *as far as* it is the same as "I devote to destruction, I abhor."

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *They set forward,* etc. [n: (HIERON., epist. ad Fabiolam. )] The thirty-fifth station, etc., *as far as* but with the mouth confession unto salvation [n: (Rom. X)].

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Make a serpent [n: (ISID. in Num., tom. V.)]* Those bitten by the serpents were healed when the brazen serpent was lifted up and looked upon, etc., *as far as* he lives through his Divinity as though of brass.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0415B]VERS. 10.-- *And the children of Israel setting forward encamped,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The thirty-seventh station is in Oboth, which is rendered "magicians" or "pythons." ⟦s3⟧ Because after the image of God, which is shown forth in the reason of the heart, and after the confession of faith, which is uttered with the mouth, there rise up serpents and maleficent arts; but let us, keeping our heart with all watchfulness, stop our ears, that we may not hear the voices of those who enchant and the songs of the sirens.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Whence going forth.* [n: (HIERON., ubi supra. )] Into Jeabarim, etc., *as far as* but this belongs properly to those who look upon the rising of the Sun of justice.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *They came to the torrent Zareth,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The thirty-ninth station was in Dibongad, etc., *as far as* but let us fear the wilderness set before us.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0415C]VERS. 14.-- *In the book of the wars. [n: (AUG., quaest. 42.)]* He did not say in what book it is written, etc., *as far as* a war of the Lord should be written in any book of theirs.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *From that place,* etc. [n: (ORIG., hom. 12 in Num.)] What is it that the Lord so greatly commands, etc., *as far as* because the substance and the nature of the Trinity is one.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID., ibid.)] Those wells too can be understood, etc., *as far as* and he removed the earth which the Philistines had thrown in.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ But it is to be noted that, while Abraham was living, the Philistines did not dare to fill up the wells or to throw earth into them; but when he was dead they grew strong and lay in wait for his wells, yet they are repaired by Isaac. ⟦s2⟧ Abraham's servant too found Rebecca at the wells, who is interpreted Patience: she is made the wife of Isaac, but at [0415D] the wells [n: (Gen. XXIV, XXIX)]; likewise Jacob, when he came to Mesopotamia having his father's command not to take a wife of another race, found Rachel at the wells; and Moses found Sephora [n: (Exod. II)]. ⟦s3⟧ If therefore thou hast understood what and of what sort the wives of the saints are, if thou wilt take Patience to wife, or Wisdom and the other virtues of the soul, and say what was said of wisdom: *Her have I sought to take to me for a wife [n: (Sap. VIII)]*, be assiduous about these wells, and there thou wilt find a spouse of this kind, because it is certain that the virtues dwell at the streams of the living word. ⟦s4⟧ There are therefore many wells within the soul, and very many others in the several sayings and senses of the Scriptures: yet there is here a more eminent one *which they digged,* not just any men, but [0416A] *the princes,* and of these the loftier sort, whom he called kings. ⟦s5⟧ Therefore hymns are sung to God at this well, and it is written that this is the well concerning which the Lord spoke to Moses: *Gather the people together, and I will give them water out of the well.*

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Gather the people together, and I will give.* ⟦s2⟧ The law calls thee to the well, that is, to Christ, the Son of God, subsisting indeed in his own proper substance, yet named together with the Father and the Spirit from one fount of Deity. ⟦s3⟧ To this well, then, that is, to the faith of Christ, the law calls thee. ⟦s4⟧ Whence he himself says that *Moses wrote of me [n: (Joan. IV)]*. ⟦s5⟧ And he calls us to drink the waters, and to sing him a song, that is, *that we may believe with the heart unto justice and confess with the mouth unto salvation [n: (Rom. X)]*.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Let the well ascend.* [n: (ORIG.)] Begin for him the [0416B] well, that is, set the well as the beginning of all things, because he himself is the beginning and the firstborn of every creature [n: (Apoc. III)].

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ Or thus, so that the words are directed from the person of Moses to the people: Give a beginning to your heart, that it may begin to understand what the well is, from which spiritual waters are to be drawn and the people of believers refreshed. ⟦s2⟧ Begin therefore for him, that is, for Israel, this well, so that if anyone sees the mystical sense from the heart, he may be able to draw God out of the depths. ⟦s3⟧ To this well Moses, that is, the law, gathers men, to which someone may seem to come, but unless he be gathered through Moses he is not acceptable to God. ⟦s4⟧ Marcion seems to himself to have come, and Basilides, and Valentinus: but because they did not come through Moses, nor did they receive the law and the prophets, they cannot praise [0416C] God from the fountains of Israel. ⟦s5⟧ Such men therefore do not come to the well, *which the princes digged [n: (Gen. XIV)]* and the kings hammered out. ⟦s6⟧ But there is a salt valley in which are wells of bitumen: and every heresy and every sin is in a valley, and in a salt valley. ⟦s7⟧ For sin does not ascend upward, but always goes down to the lowest and lower places. ⟦s8⟧ Every heretical thought therefore, and every act of sin, is set in a valley, salt and bitter. ⟦s9⟧ For what sweetness, what pleasantness can sin have? ⟦s10⟧ But if thou comest to a heretical opinion and to the bitterness of sin, thou hast come to the wells of bitumen, which is food and nourishment for fire If thou tastest water from these wells, if thou receivest the heretical sense and the bitterness of sin, thou wilt prepare within thyself fuel for the fire and the burnings of [0416D] gehenna. ⟦s11⟧ To such it is said: *Walk in the light of your fire,* etc. [n: (Isa. L.)]

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *The princes digged it and the kings hammered it out.* ⟦s2⟧ The princes therefore digged, the kings hammered out — which is said from hammering out, not from cutting away. ⟦s3⟧ For that is said to be hammered out which is cut or shaped in stone. ⟦s4⟧ The princes therefore, as the lesser, digged, that is, sank a certain depth as it were into the softness of the earth: but those whom he calls kings, as the stronger, penetrate not only the depth of the earth but the hardness of the rock, that they may reach the deeper waters and search out the very veins of the abyss, knowing that the judgments of God are a great deep. ⟦s5⟧ These are the apostles, one of whom says: *But God hath revealed to us by his Spirit; for the Spirit searcheth all things, yea, the deep things of God* [0417A] [n: (I Cor. II)]. ⟦s6⟧ Those therefore who through the Holy Spirit can search out the deep things and penetrate the profound mysteries of the well are said to be kings, who hammered out the well in the rock, because they penetrated the hard and difficult secrets of knowledge. ⟦s7⟧ The princes therefore digged this well, and the kings of the nations hammered it out, that is, the Apostles, who gathered the nations to the obedience of faith, and laid open to all the knowledge of Christ, in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge. ⟦s8⟧ For they fulfilled the commandment of God, *teaching all nations, and baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Ghost [n: (Matth. XXVIII)]*. ⟦s9⟧ And by this they hammered out, that is, laid open, the knowledge of the well. ⟦s10⟧ These things too which we have in our hands are a well to us, and all the Scripture of the law and of the prophets: the evangelical also and [0417B] the apostolic together are one well, which none can dig or hammer out but kings and princes, those, namely, who can take away the earth and remove the surface of the letter, and bring forth from the lower rock, where Christ is, the spiritual senses as living water. ⟦s11⟧ They are called kings because they have driven out the reign of sin and have set the reign of justice in their own members. ⟦s12⟧ For it becomes him to teach others who has first done what he teaches; whence: *He that shall do and shall so teach men, he shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven [n: (Matth. V)]*; and to be great in the kingdom is to be a king.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Princes.* ⟦s2⟧ The same men can be called both princes and kings. ⟦s3⟧ Or let us understand by princes the prophets: for they placed the sense and the prophecy concerning Christ buried deep in the profundity of the letter, and therefore it is said of them: [0417C] *Unless you hear it in secret, your soul shall weep.* ⟦s4⟧ And elsewhere: *How great is the multitude of thy sweetness, O Lord, which thou hast hidden for them that fear thee [n: (Psal. XXX)]*. ⟦s5⟧ The kings are the apostles, who search out the deep things of God and rule the Churches, whence Christ also is called King of kings.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the leaders prepared it.* [n: (ORIG.)] *They hammered out,* he says, etc., *as far as* and searching out the secrets of the word of God.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And they set forward.* [n: (ORIG.)] Setting forward from the well, etc., *as far as* that our affection and our faith may be proved.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ He who drinks of this well profits thereby, that he may have gifts to offer to God, of which it is said in the law: *My gifts, my givings. [n: (Levit. II)]*. ⟦s2⟧ Of those things therefore which God has given, offering is made to God; and he has given the knowledge of himself, and man offers him his faith and his affection.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0417D]When we have offered the things that are of ourselves, we obtain the things that are of God. ⟦s2⟧ For when we have offered our faith and our affection, then he himself bestows the diverse gifts of the Holy Spirit, of which it is said: *But all things are of God [n: (Rom. XI)]*.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *In Bamoth.* ⟦s2⟧ Bamoth is the coming of death, by which, namely, we die together with him, that we may live together with him, and mortify our members upon the earth. ⟦s3⟧ *For we are buried together with him by baptism unto death [n: (Rom. VI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ For he who keeps the order of the way of salvation ought to go through these things one by one, and after many things to come to this place.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ORIG.)] *And from Bamoth,* he says, etc., *as far as* nay rather, by advances is the soul's journey to heaven made ready.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0418A]VERS. 21.-- *And he sent.* [n: (ID.)] Israel sends legates to the king of the Amorrhites, etc., *as far as* he gathers to himself cisterns broken or crushed.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To Sehon the king.* ⟦s2⟧ Sehon, who is interpreted a barren tree, or one lifted up, is the king of the Amorrhites, who are interpreted those who bring into bitterness, or those who speak. ⟦s3⟧ This is the devil, lifted up and barren, of whom it is said: *The prince of this world cometh, and in me he hath not anything [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. ⟦s4⟧ And elsewhere: *Behold, the prince of this world shall be sent forth [n: (Joan. XII)]*, not because he created the world, but because there are sinners in the world, and he himself is the prince of sin, according to what is said: *For the whole world is set in wickedness [n: (Joan. V)]*, that is, he himself is the prince of the world.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *The king's highway.* ⟦s2⟧ Which says: *I am the way, [0418B] the truth and the life [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. ⟦s3⟧ Of whom it is said: *Give to the king thy judgment, O God [n: (Psal. LXXI)]*. ⟦s4⟧ In this we must walk, and turn aside nowhere, neither into the field nor into the vineyard, that is, neither to diabolical works nor to diabolical thoughts, if we would pass peaceably through the world. ⟦s5⟧ But this the more provokes the prince of the world, that we say we will not remain with him, nor tarry, nor touch anything of his: hence he grows angry and stirs up persecutions and perils, and threatens torments. ⟦s6⟧ Hence he says: *Sehon gathered together his people,* etc.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *The army:* namely the princes and judges of the world and all the ministers of wickedness, who always assail the people of God.
