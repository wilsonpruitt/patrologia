## CHAPTER XXII.

[0419B]

VERS. 2.-- *And seeing. [n: (AUG., quaest. 46.)]* What Scripture says, etc., yet they were not one nation, but two neighboring ones.

VERS. 5.-- *He sent therefore messengers to Balaam the son of Beor. [n: (ORIG., Hom. 13.)]* War threatens thee, king Balac, etc., *as far as* the king therefore sends to him, saying: *Come now, and curse me this people,* etc.

VERS. 6.-- *If by any means I may be able.* He seems not to trust wholly in Balaam, being terrified by the report of the mighty works which had been done among the people of God.

*For I know that he is blessed.* [n: (ORIG.)] I do not believe that he knew that whomsoever Balaam had blessed would be blessed, but that he said these things for flattery's sake, that by [0419C] extolling his art he might make him readier for himself. For the magic art knows not how to bless, nor does it know how to bless demons, and Isaac, and Jacob, and all the saints: of the impious none has known how to bless.

VERS. 7.-- *The price of divination.* Others: *Divining instruments among the arts,* which human curiosity has put together. There are certain things which Scripture has named divining instruments. But Gentile custom calls them either tripods or curtains, or by words of this kind, which, as though consecrated to this end, are wont to be moved and handled by them. But divine Scripture in the prophets names the Ephod in the vernacular speech, which they hand down to be the garment of those who prophesy [n: (I Reg. XXX)]. Yet in divine Scripture prophecy is one thing, divination another. For he says: *There shall be no auguring in Jacob, neither divination in Israel.* [var: Vulg. *Non est augurium in Jacob, nec divinatio in Israel*, "There is no augury in Jacob, nor divination in Israel"] [0419D] Divination therefore is utterly renounced, for it is accomplished by the work and ministry of demons. Therefore Balaam, having received his divining instruments, when the demons were wont to come to him, sees them put to flight and God present: whom for that reason he says he consults, because he does not see those who were accustomed to appear to him. God came therefore to Balaam, not because he is worthy, but that those might be put to flight who had been wont to be present for cursing and for doing harm: for hereby God was providing for his people.

VERS. 8.-- *God came,* etc. It is not said whether this was done in sleep, although it is certain that it was done by night. For afterward Scripture says: *Balaam rising in the morning,* etc.

Let no man glory if God speaks to him. For he knows [0420A] how he must speak with such men, since he speaks even with the reprobate: and he who speaks through angels, himself speaks.

*[n: (ORIG., Hom. 13.)] God came, and said to him,* etc. It can be objected: Granted that Balaam calls upon demons, etc., *as far as* and for a proof of great faith they venerated a little child as though a great king.

VERS. 16.-- *Thus saith Balac the son of Sephor,* etc. Balac is a shutting out or a devouring: in which is signified some power of this world set against us, which desires to shut out and devour the spiritual Israel, and uses no ministers but the chief priests and scribes and Pharisees. These it invites, to these it promises a reward; but they, like Balaam, feign in all things to refer themselves to the Lord, and to act with zeal for God. For they say: *Search [0420B] the Scriptures and see that a prophet riseth not out of Galilee.* And: *We have a law, and according to the law he ought to die, because he made himself the Son of God [n: (Joan. VII, XIX)]*.

VERS. 18.-- *If Balac would give me,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 48.)] That Balam said, when more honorable messengers had been sent to him a second time, etc., *as far as* whence *They have followed the way of Balaam the son of Beor, who loved the wages of iniquity.* [var: Vulg. *viam Balaam ex Bosor, qui mercedem iniquitatis amavit*]

VERS. 20.-- *God came therefore.* Balaam is troublesome to God, and all but extorts leave for himself to go and curse the children of Israel, and to call upon demons — he to whom God had already come.

[n: (ORIG., Hom. 13.)] It is difficult in the exposition of the history, etc., *as far as* because with God neither the works of the wicked nor those of the good are idle.

*If they have come to call thee.* [n: (RAB.)] Balaam was a diviner, etc., *as far [0420C] as* not so much in heart and understanding as in mouth and speech.

[n: (ORIG.)] If thou shouldst see a power set against thee, etc., *as far as* namely those of the Gentiles who believe.

VERS. 22.-- *And the angel of the Lord stood.* He, namely, who was present with the children of Israel, of whom it is written: *My angel shall go with thee [n: (Exod. XXXII)]*.

VERS. 23.-- *The ass seeing the angel standing.* The magician sees demons, the ass an angel, not because she was worthy to see, any more than to speak, but that Balaam might be confuted; whence a dumb beast reproves the prophet's madness.

[n: (GREG., Past., part. III, adm. 13.)] Balaam presses on to reach his purpose, etc., *as far as* because, going on to curse, he changed his voice, not his mind.

[0420D]VERS. 25.-- *To the wall.* That is, to the fence of that vineyard in which the angel was not, since he was on the other side, in the furrows of the vineyards, according to the Septuagint.

VERS. 26.-- *To the place.* No longer in the furrows of the vineyards, but between the fences themselves, that is, he stood in the way, so that she could go neither to the right hand nor to the left.

VERS. 27.-- *She fell down under,* etc. Because, being beaten, she did not go back, nor turn aside to one side, because she was not frightened from the other side, but the angel was in the middle of the way, in a narrow place.

VERS. 28.-- *And he opened.* That through her Balaam might be reproved, and confuted by the voices of a dumb beast, who seemed to be a diviner and wise.

*And she spoke.* (ISID). The ass on which Balaam sits sees the angel [0421A] and speaks; that is, the brute Gentile world, which of old Balaam — that is, the seducer of idolatry — laid low with what error he would, as a brute animal resisting with no reason; but she saw the angel whom the man could not see, and she saw, and reported, and spoke; that we might know that at the coming of the great angel that Gentile people, its hardness and the nature of its stupidity being changed, would speak with tongues loosed unto God.

[n: (ORIG. hom. 14)]. The ass on which Balaam was sitting, etc., *as far as* but the greater of these is charity, which alone never falls.

VERS. 29.-- *He answered.* Doubtless he was carried along so full of covetousness that he was not even terrified by the miracle of so great a portent, and answered as though speaking to a man; whereas God had not turned the soul of the ass into a rational [0421B] nature, but had made that which pleased him to sound forth from her, to restrain that man's frenzy; perhaps prefiguring this: *That God hath chosen the foolish things of the world, that he might confound the wise,* for the spiritual and true Israel.

VERS. 38.-- *Behold I am here: shall I be able to speak any other thing than what God,* etc. [n: (ORIG., hom. 15.)] Balaam is set down in the Scriptures now as blameworthy, now as praiseworthy, etc., *as far as* Balaam, enlightened by that Lucifer of whom it is said: *And how did Lucifer fall, who rose in the morning? [n: (Isa. XIV.)]* [var: Vulg. *Quomodo cecidisti de caelo, Lucifer, qui mane oriebaris*, "How art thou fallen from heaven, O Lucifer, who didst rise in the morning"]
