## CHAPTER XXIV.

VERS. 1.-- *And when,* etc. [n: (ORIG.)] Since none [0423D] of the demons, etc., *as far as* and that each one in his own order will obtain the glory of the resurrection.

*He went not.* That is, he will not consider the will of God by foolish observations, as he was wont to do in many animals and beasts: but he will acknowledge that God has no care for oxen, nor for sheep, nor for birds and the other animals [n: (I Cor. IX; Rom. XV)], but he will understand that the things written about them were written for the sake of men.

VERS. 2.-- *The spirit rushing upon him. [n: (AUG., quaest. 51 in Num.)] And the spirit of God came upon him,* that is, upon Balaam, etc., *as far as* and many like things.

VERS. 3.-- *Balaam said. [n: (ORIG., hom. 17.)] Balaam the son of Beor said,* etc., *as far as* and therefore he says that his eyes were [0424A] uncovered, because he was able to see what he saw.

*Whose is stopped,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Because in sleep, with his bodily eyes closed, he saw these things by the ministry of an angel. But he has the eyes of the mind partly open, partly closed, who acknowledges the mysteries to come but does not correct his error. So the wicked do one thing in knowledge, another in their manners: they show one thing with the mouth, another in deed.

VERS. 5.-- *How beautiful. [n: (ORIG., hom. 17.)]* Another reading, etc., *as far as* as shading groves, as paradises above the rivers, etc.

VERS. 6.-- *As woody valleys.* [n: (ORIG.)] Others, etc., *as far as* and in their teaching they take delight as men walking through the shady places of the groves.

*As watered gardens beside the rivers.* [n: (ID.)] Another reading, etc., [0424B] *as far as* he is deservedly said to dwell in the tabernacle.

*Cedars.* Of God, namely, which take up the branches of the vine brought over out of Egypt: in which rests that fruit whose shadow covered the mountains.

[n: (ORIG.)] Another reading, etc., *as far as* and that he may destroy death, the last enemy.

VERS. 8.-- *He brought him out of.* God brought him out of Egypt, that is, after the death of Herod, whence: *Out of Egypt have I called my Son [n: (Osee. XI)]*. Which seems to be taken from this place and inserted into the Gospel, or else from the prophet Osee.

After the death of Herod, or out of the world, the Father brought him out to himself, that he might make a way for those who were to ascend from this world to God.

*Whose strength is like,* etc. For casting down all the kingdoms of the demons, [0424C] he founded his own one kingdom in glory as a true unicorn: for the horn signifies a kingdom.

*They shall devour the nations,* etc. [n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] *He shall eat the nations of his enemies, and shall suck the marrow of their fatness,* etc. The nations, namely, etc., *as far as* who is intelligible and holy, and is called both one and manifold and subtle.

*And they shall pierce with arrows.* The Seventy: *And with their darts they shall shoot the enemy.* For Christ by his words overcame the devil [n: (Matth. IV)], and overcomes and breaks all who contradict him: for everyone who sins is his enemy while he sins; but if, pierced by the words of God and acknowledging his sins, he be pricked to the heart and turned to repentance, he is said to be pierced by his darts.

[0424D]VERS. 9.-- *Lying down he hath slept as a lion,* etc. If thou hast understood how much rest the way of wisdom has, how much grace, and how much sweetness, do not dissemble, do not neglect it, set out upon the way, and do not shudder at the solitude of the desert; for to him who dwells in tabernacles of this kind the heavenly manna comes, and thou shalt eat the bread of angels [n: (Exod. XVI)]. Only begin quickly, and angels will come into thy company, whom the cedars signify.

*Whom none shall dare to rouse up;* the Seventy: *Who shall rouse him up?* For now he is said to be raised up by the Father; now he himself says that he will raise up the temple of his body [n: (Joan. II)], and therefore the feeling of one inquiring, as it were, is signified.

*He that blesseth.* Those who bless, bless Christ, [0425A] being taken up into the communion of the Father's blessing. But those who curse are accursed. The Jews who cursed Christ are lamentably accursed. For what could befall those who curse wisdom and truth and life, save that they should lie exiled from all these? For all these things Christ is [n: (Joan. 9)].

*He that curseth thee shall be reckoned as accursed: And Balac being angry against.* [n: (ORIG.)] I think that not only does he curse Christ who utters a cursing word against him, but also he who under the name of a Christian acts wickedly and lives basely, and by dishonorable words or deeds causes his name to be blasphemed. Just as not only does he bless God who blesses him with words alone, but also he who by his acts, his life and his manners causes the name of God to be blessed.

[0425B]VERS. 11.-- *The Lord hath deprived thee of the honor appointed for thee.* The Lord shows that Balac too understood that Balaam was no longer being deceived by the ministry of demons, but was being carried over to better things by the power of God.

VERS. 13.-- *I shall not be able to go beyond the word of the Lord my God, to utter either good.* Balaam, knowing that the answers were not brought to him by his accustomed ministers, but from him who has power over all, is rightly seen to have protested that he could not go beyond the word of the Lord, nor bring forth anything of good or of evil out of his own mouth. For he who speaks cannot be bought over by sacrifices or by gifts, with whom there is no change; and therefore the priest cannot be changed for a price where God is not moved by gifts. Yet Balam, about to depart, begins [0425C] to prophesy again. And he says: *I give thee counsel,* etc.

VERS. 14.-- *I will give thee counsel [n: (ORIG., hom. 18.)] Come, I give thee counsel,* etc., *as far as* as one who truly sees and has truly heard.

VERS. 15.-- *Balaam said.* [n: (ID.)] *Balaam the son of Beor,* etc., *as far as* which were granted for our profit.

*[n: (GREG., lib. XV Moral., cap. 22.)] The man hath said, whose eye is stopped.* The wicked man does not know the evils he does, except when he has begun to be punished for them. Balaam furnished counsel against the Israelites, but afterward in his punishment he saw what he had first committed by his fault. But the elect, because they ought not to sin, see beforehand: their eyes are open before [0425D] the fall. The unjust man, however, opens his eyes after the fall, because after the fault, already in punishment, he perceives that he ought to have avoided the evil which he did.

(VERS. 16.-- *Who falling hath his eyes opened.* Often the evil man marvels at the eternal judgments, and when he hears the preaching of the heavenly country he loves it, and is astonished at the works of the divine ordering. Lying down, therefore, he considers the miracles, because he weighs the power of God by knowing it, but does not love it by living.

VERS. 17.-- *I shall see him, but not now.* Another reading: *I will show to him, and not now. I will pronounce blessed, and he draweth not nigh.* These things are referred to those whose person he bears. Because the doctors of the law and the scribes show that Christ was promised in the law and the prophets, but not now; that is, when he came, but when the fulness of the Gentiles [0426A] shall have come in, then they will show, then they will pronounce blessed, now they blaspheme. But the time in which these things shall be is far off, and at the end of the world. Therefore he says: *I will show to him,* that is, to the people which shall then be saved, *but not now.*

*Not now,* while I speak these things: Because *when the fulness of time came, God sent his Son [n: (Gal. IV)]*.

*A star shall rise out of Jacob.* [n: (ORIG.)] Concerning this star, etc., *as far as* that both may appear to have been plainly prophesied.

*And he shall strike the leaders, nailing the principalities and powers to the cross [n: (Col. II)]*. Otherwise he cannot save the Moabites, unless he had first laid waste the leaders of impiety.

*And he shall waste all.* [n: (ORIG.)] *And he shall spoil all the children of Seth.* This is Seth the son of Adam, etc., *as far as* he called back from death to life.

[0426B] *And Idumea shall be his possession.* [n: (ID.)] *And Edom shall be an inheritance to him, Esau his enemy,* etc. Edom is the same as Esau, etc., *as far as* through obedience he will stand forth a partaker in spirit of the inheritance to come.

VERS. 18.-- *But Israel shall do valiantly.* [n: (Id.)] *And Israel hath acted with strength.* Because, namely, then Edom, etc., *as far as* who can destroy both soul and body in hell.

*And that he may destroy the remnants of the city.* [n: (ID.)] He will destroy the man delivered out of the city, etc., *as far as* with whom if we die together, we shall also live together [n: (II Tim. II)].

VERS. 20.-- *The beginning.* This cannot be referred to the historical Amalec: for he was not the first in antiquity; but to the spiritual Amalec, who by turning peoples away from God and by making Gentiles out of the worshippers of God is [0426C] named Amalec. And he is called the beginning of the Gentiles, because he gave the beginning whereby men became Gentiles, changing the glory of the incorruptible God into the likeness of the image of corruptible man: for as in the people of God Christ is the beginning, so in the people which turns away from God and becomes Gentile the beginning is Amalec.

[n: (ORIG.)] Amalec is the beginning of the Gentiles, etc., *as far as* and let every sect which turns the people away from God be set forth.

VERS. 21.-- *He saw also,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Another reading, etc., *as far as* and let them learn not to blaspheme.

He does not seem to name the Cinite as one greatly blameworthy, to whom Saul said: *Depart from the midst of Amalec, and I will not strike thee, because thou hast shown mercy to Israel,* etc. [n: (I Reg. XV.)] His dwelling place is strong, if he sets his nest in [0426D] the rock, which is Christ.

[n: (RAB.)] The Cinite, etc., *as far as* and they pass beyond by the flight of the heart everything that passes away.

VERS. 22.-- *Chosen.* The chosen, of the number of the wise, the searchers out of truth, live with everlasting life; whence: *they that explain me shall have life everlasting [n: (Eccli. XXIV)]*.

*Assur.* The devil, namely, to whom heretics are handed over to be set straight and corrected.

VERS. 23.-- *A parable.* He is often said to speak in parables, lest we should take anything in these things not as a parable, but according to the letter.

*Who.* So happy is he who may see these things, perceive them, understand them, and believe that God has so done them.

*When these things.* When, namely, *a star shall rise out of Jacob, a man shall spring up out of Israel, and shall destroy Amalec, and the seed [0427A] shall perish.* That is, when Christ shall come and shall destroy the worship of idols, and shall subdue the power of the demons.

VERS. 24.-- *They shall come.* He seems to signify that about the time of the Lord's incarnation the Romans should come, about to overcome the Assyrians, the Hebrews and all the men of the East, and at last to lose their kingdom, according to the statue of Daniel [n: (Dan., II)]; or that at the coming of Christ at the end of the times, which the western part signifies, all the kingdoms of the world should be overcome by the sword of the spirit, that is, by the word of God, and that in the time of Antichrist the preaching of the Gospel should cease.

[n: (ORIG.)] Another reading, etc., *as far as* to him over all the power of the enemy.

*The Hebrews.* A Hebrew is one who passes over from Egypt to [0427B] the land of promise, from darkness to light, from death to life: but he cannot obtain it without a great struggle; he afflicts therefore and is afflicted, he strikes down his adversary and is struck down. The Assyrians therefore afflict the Hebrews, that is, the people of God, just as they are afflicted by them.

*They also shall perish.* [n: (ID.)] *And they likewise shall perish.* Not together with the Hebrews, namely, but likewise, etc., *as far as* whom the deceit of the devil led down into death.

VERS. 25.-- *And he rose up. [n: (ID., hom. 20.)]* Because Balaam, constrained by the power of God, etc., *as far as* but king Balac at once obeyed the counsel.
