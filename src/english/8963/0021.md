## CHAPTER XXX.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *If any man.* ⟦s2⟧ Over the soul which comes unto the perfect man no one has dominion in vows, but she has her own liberty in them.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ORIG., hom. 24 in Num.)] The law of vows is laid down, etc., [0432C] *as far as* that thou do only that which pertains to the divine worship.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] Of the souls which are in the Church of God, etc., *as far as* nor need we be left by the Father under tutors and procurators.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He shall have bound himself by an oath,* etc. [n: (AUG., quaest. 56 in Num.)] This does not belong to every, etc., *as far as* but by his vow he himself makes it not lawful for himself.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *A woman if,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ That is, if it be a female, and a soul as yet tender, over which a husband or a father has dominion in vows: the fault is not always in her, but sometimes it returns upon the husbands or the parents.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And an oath;* LXX: *And the definitions which she has defined against her own soul.* ⟦s2⟧ Not that they harm their own soul by such vows: but "against the soul" is said [0432D] against the animal delight, as there: *You shall afflict your souls.*

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG., quaest. 57)] With reason is it asked in this place also concerning the vow of virginity: for women, even virgins, are wont to be so called in Scripture; and the Apostle seems to speak of the father, when he says: *Let him keep his virgin, and let him give his virgin,* etc. [n: (I Cor. VII)] [var: Vulg. 1 Cor 7:37-38 *servare virginem suam* … *qui matrimonio jungit virginem suam*, "to keep his virgin" … "he that giveth his virgin in marriage"]. ⟦s2⟧ In this way, *nuptum*, where some have understood his virgin, that is, his own virginity: yet they show this by no like locution of the Scriptures, since it is most unusual.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Neither shall she be held liable.* ⟦s2⟧ LXX: *The Lord shall cleanse her, because her father refused.* ⟦s3⟧ That is, he shall hold her clean, and shall so judge, as it is said: *The priest shall cleanse him, and by cleansing thou shalt not cleanse the guilty (Levit. [0433A] XIII)*. ⟦s4⟧ That is, thou shalt not call him clean who is unclean.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *If she have a husband.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, if she be grown up and marriageable, to conceive the seed of the word of God and to take in the secrets of spiritual teaching; whence: *But I will that you all present a chaste virgin to one husband, Christ [n: (II Cor. XI)]* [var: Vulg. 2 Cor 11:2 *Despondi enim vos uni viro, virginem castam exhibere Christo*, "For I have espoused you to one husband"].

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG. quaest. 59)] Because the Lord commanded, etc., *as far as* since God himself commanded this, and willed this.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *A wife in the house.* [n: (AUG.)] Of her he spoke first, who vowed in the house of her father, etc., *as far as* yet so, that that motion consent to nothing but reason.

## CHAPTER XXXI

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0433B]VERS. 2.-- *Revenge first.* [n: (ORIG.)] Scandals had befallen the children of Israel by the shuffling of the Madianites, etc., *as far as* where deceit unto sinning is laid under one that walks rightly.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And so thou shalt be gathered. ⟦s2⟧ At the last he says: Thou shalt be added to thy people,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ This is against the Samaritans, who deny the resurrection of the dead, nor receive the faith of the world to come. ⟦s4⟧ For no man is added to those who are not: it is certain therefore that there is some people to which he says that Moses is to be added.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *A thousand men of every,* etc. [n: (RAB. in Num., tom. II.)] Mark the magnificence of the divine power, etc., *as far as* for armed with such weapons we shall put to flight the camps of the devil.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *And their kings,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ *(ORIG., hom. 25 [0433C] in Num.)* The kings of the Madianites are killed, etc., *as far as* he was made unto us of God justice, and peace, and redemption.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Balaam also.* ⟦s2⟧ It is plain that by the counsel of Balaam the women of the Madianites were suborned who had deceived Israel; whence he is here killed as the author of the crime.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ORIG.)] It was said above that Balaam returned *unto his own place.* ⟦s2⟧ But perhaps he did return, although Scripture has not said so: or perhaps he returned *unto his own place* from that place where he was offering sacrifices, namely unto that place where he had his lodging as a stranger; for it is not said unto his own house, or unto his own country. ⟦s3⟧ But of Balac it was said, *unto himself.* ⟦s4⟧ That is, where he dwelt as a lord.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Why the women.* [n: (RAB.)] Mystically, returning from the war he keeps a woman for spoil [0433D], etc., *as far as,* that we may desire the things which are pleasing to God, and study to accomplish them.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *At the suggestion.* ⟦s2⟧ Here it is shown more evidently that the women were suborned at the suggestion of Balaam himself.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ISID.)] Balaam did not doubt, a store of women having been offered, that the Israelites would fall by the ruin of fornication, because he knew that the concupiscible parts of their souls were corrupted: so the demons make trial of every man, delivering snares to those affections of the soul in which they have perceived us to be sick.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 21.-- *Eleazar.* ⟦s2⟧ Note that Eleazar speaks not to all the people, but to the men of valour, [0434A] who were returning from the battle: but they are men of valour who go forth to war, who strive in the contest, and abstain from all things; but the others are not men of valour.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *Gold.* ⟦s2⟧ It is the time of war in this world against spiritual wickednesses and carnal concupiscences. ⟦s3⟧ The choir of angels beholds us, and the loving expectation of the heavenly virtues hangs upon us, when or how we shall return from the battle, who brings back more gold or silver, who looks curiously upon precious stones. ⟦s4⟧ They inquire also who carry brass, who iron, who lead, or a vessel of wood, or of earthenware, or anything of this kind necessary for the uses of a great house. ⟦s5⟧ According therefore to those things which each one shall have carried back, the merit of his dwelling shall be assigned to him. ⟦s6⟧ Yet all [0434B] these things are tried, some by fire, some by water the fire shall try of what sort each man's work is.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And you shall wash,* etc. [n: (ORIG., ubi supra. )] Thou seest how everyone who goes out of the battle of this life needs cleansing, etc., *as far as* that he may enter clean into the city of God.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Who fought. [n: (ORIG., hom. 26 in Num.)]* There are certain men in the people of God, etc., *as far as* by so much is the number of five hundred more excellent than fifty.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ORIG.)] That there are differences of advancement and of merits in the people of the faithful, etc., *as far as* But the little chains signify the connections of the word and of doctrine.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 50.-- *That thou mayest pray.* [n: (ORIG.)] To make God propitious for us, etc., *as far as* for whom alone it is fitting [0434C] that he be made propitious.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 51.-- *And they received.* ⟦s2⟧ Another reading: *And Moses and Eleazar the priest took the gold from all the tribunes and centurions, and brought it into the tabernacle of the testimony, a memorial of the children of Israel before the Lord.* ⟦s3⟧ Note that the things which are said are referred not to visible sight, but to the memory of the mind. ⟦s4⟧ For blessed is he who remembers that he has done some good work before the Lord, and has offered gifts well-pleasing to God, namely the virtues of the mind and the ornaments of piety.

## CHAPTER XXXII.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And the sons of Ruben,* etc. [n: (ORIG., hom. 26 in Num.)] All the things which are said are to be weighed not only from the discourse [0434D] which is spoken, but from the person of the speaker, etc., *as far as* but through Moses they receive an inheritance beyond the Jordan.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *The land which the Lord struck. [n: (GREG., lib. XXVII Moral., cap. 10.)]* There are many in the Church who despise to be little ones, etc., *as far as* while they are blinded by things transitory.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Very many cattle.* [n: (ORIG.)] *There are cattle and much flock,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The former people, etc., *as far as* who is the land of promise to enter.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *If the sons of Gad and the sons of Ruben shall pass over,* etc. [n: (ORIG., hom. 26.)] Another reading, etc., *as far as* but that which is above, and free, and is the mother of us all.
