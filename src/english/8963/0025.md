## CHAPTER XXXV.

VERS. 11.-- *Determine what cities. [n: (AUG., quaest. 64 in Num.)]* What is it that he says, etc., *as far as* if it shall have been made manifest in judgment that he killed unwillingly.

[n: (RAB. in Num. )] If perchance, for conscience of sins, the cities of the land of promise, etc., *as far as* because Sichem was bought from strangers for a hundred lambs.

VERS. 16.-- *Guilty,* that is, liable to eternal death; whence, *If anyone shall scandalize one of these little ones that [0445D] believe in me,* etc. [n: (Matth. XVIII)]. And elsewhere: *Everyone that hateth his brother is a murderer, and hath not eternal life [n: (I Joan. III)]*. Such a one, according to Exodus, is commanded to be torn away from the altar [n: (Exod. XXI)], because, being unworthy of the sacraments of the Lord, he is removed from the participation of the sacred altar, [0446A] and is not called back to pardon save through worthy penance.

VERS. 28.-- *But after he shall be dead. [n: (GREG., homil. 6 in Ezech.)]* After the death of the high priest the manslayer is commanded to return into his own land, because the human race by sinning brought death upon itself, after the death of the true priest, that is, of Christ, received the absolution of its guilt.

VERS. 26.-- *Defile not the land of your habitation.* He defiles the land of his habitation who does bloody and wicked works, by scandalizing his neighbours and slaying them spiritually. And unless the desires of the flesh be slain through the confession of sins and worthy penance, the land of his body will in no way be cleansed, nor will the Creator of all dwell in his soul, [0446B] where he will see the squalor of a filthy manner of life bearing rule.

## CHAPTER XXXVI.

VERS. 1.-- *And there came also the princes,* etc. [n: (RAB. in Num., tom. II.)] Pious thoughts ought to provide, etc., *as far as* lest, if they be carried on with a perverse intention, they be invaded by unclean spirits; whence: *Take heed that you do not your justice before men [n: (Matth. VI)]*.

VERS. 6.-- *Let them marry.* The Apostle says the like: *Let her marry whom she will, only in the Lord [n: (I Cor. VII)]*, that is, a catholic husband, lest, namely, the possession of good works and of right faith be lost, if she marry a Gentile or a Jew.

But tropologically it is lawful for the woman, that is, for the pious intention, [0446C] to join herself through a good action to whatever labour she will, yet so that she keep piety and religion in all things, lest she suffer the loss of her work, for which she hopes for the possession of the eternal inheritance, if she join herself to a stranger, that is, to an earthly thing.

[n: (RAB.)] This is done when anyone out of the number of believing Jews joins himself to a believing teacher from among the Gentiles, that by his teaching he may beget sons, that is, works of piety. Under the name of an uncle also the elect from among the Gentiles may be called, because they are brothers of the holy preachers who were established in Judaea. There ought to be no schism in spiritual kinship, where there is one faith in profession. And as there is one shepherd, there ought to be one sheepfold: *For we are all brethren [n: (Joan. X; Matth. XXIII; II Cor. XI)]*.

[0446D]He who studies to have humility, and is well pleased in his own infirmities; keeps a solemnity for himself, and renders acceptable gifts to God, and stands forth as king of his own manner of life, and is moved by the advance of the virtues, walking from virtue to virtue.
