## CHAPTER FOUR.

VERS. 1.-- *The sons of Juda.* How Phares and Hesron and the three that follow are said to be the sons of Juda, when [0638B] elsewhere we read Phares and Zaram to be the sons of Juda, and Hesron the son of Phares; but the Hebrews assert that, the names being changed, the same sons of Juda, whom Scripture elsewhere names by other names, are here set down. For they say that Phares, who is interpreted *division,* has here his own proper name. But that Hesron is Zaram, though Hesron is interpreted *a court,* Zaram *a rising.* That Charmi also is Heber, for Charmi is interpreted *my vineyard,* Her *watching.* And Ur they say to be Onam, though Ur is interpreted *the left hand,* Onam *a murmuring,* and Sobal to be Sela, the least of the sons of Juda whom Sue bore to him. And Sobal is interpreted *an ear of corn,* or *vain oldness.* Sela *sent, or his shadow.*

VERS. 3.-- *Asalelphuni.* [n: (RAB.)] They suspect her [0638C] to be the mother of Samson, begotten of the stock of Juda, whose husband was Manue of the tribe of Dan, who is also read in the book of Judges to be of Sara.

VERS. 4.-- *Gedor and Ezer.* [n: (ID.)] They are places possessed by the sons of Ur; but it is a wonder that Ur is here called the father of Bethlehem, when above Salma was called his father, unless perhaps he here calls a forefather a father, because Hur was the father of Salma who was the father of Bethlehem.

VERS. 9.-- *And Jabes was.* [n: (ID.)] The order seems confused, because, the sons of Halaa and Naara being numbered, he suddenly brings in the kindred of Arahel the son of Aram, and thereupon narrates of Jabes; but the Hebrews say that Assur of Halaa begot Etam, and he Cos. And Cos begot Soboba, and Soboba Jabes, who was a teacher of the scribes. Between Cos and Jabes are inserted the kindreds [0638D] of Aharehel (or Aharahel, otherwise Aral), because he is Obab the son of Aran, that is, of Jetro the kinsman of Moses. And because Jabes taught them; they are inserted in his progeny as though begotten of his forefathers.

*And the mother*, the Synagogue of whom Christ was born, who bore him in sorrow, because she grieved at his coming through unbelief, whence the Jews, envying, said: *If we let him alone so, all will believe in him [n: (Joan. XI)]*.

VERS. 10.-- *He called upon.* This befits Christ, whom the Father sanctified and sent into the world; of whom it is said: *Behold the Lamb of God, who taketh away the sins of the world,* [var: Vulg. Jo. 1:29 *peccatum mundi*, singular] etc. [n: (Joan. I)]. Him God blessed with a multiplication of sons, whom through the water of baptism the most holy [0639A] Church brings forth [n: (I Cor. I)]. He enlarged his borders, because he gave him the inheritance of all nations, and extended his possession unto the ends of the earth. With whom was the hand of the Lord, because the power of the Godhead wrought mighty works and miracles through him. He also is the power of God and wisdom, through whom the Father founded all things that are in the heavens and on earth, whom the malice of the Jews could not hurt, who said of himself: *The prince of this world cometh, and in me he hath not anything.* Through him also his members are blessed, that is, the Church, to whom it is said by Isaias: *Enlarge the place of thy tent,* etc. [n: (Isa. LIV)]. With him is the hand of the Lord, because in her the Son of God works, who is the arm and the right hand of the Father. *And the gates of hell shall not prevail against him,* [var: Vulg. Mt. 16:18 *adversus eam*, "against her," of the Church] nor shall the devil have dominion over him, because *power [0639B] is given to him over all the strength of the enemy*

VERS. 11.-- *And Caleb the brother.* [n: (RAB.)] We read above that Caleb was the son of Hesron, whose brothers were Ram and Jerameel. The Hebrews say therefore that Ram, the brother of Caleb, is here called Sua. And Ram is interpreted *lofty,* Sua *lowly,* which befits the Jewish people, because it was lofty in the worship of the one God and in the power of the kingdom; but because it would not receive the Son of God, it was made lowly and vile, so that the first become the last.

VERS. 12.--[n: (ID.)] *Recha.* Some think that David is called by the name of Recha. For Recha is tender. Whence David says of himself: *I am tender and an anointed king [n: (II Reg. III)]*. [var: Vulg. 2 Rg. 3:39 *Ego autem adhuc delicatus, et unctus rex*] He also is said to be as it were a tender [0639C] little worm of the wood. And therefore they are called the men of Recha, because they were noted here for the sake of setting forth the genealogy of David.

VERS. 13.-- *And Saraia begot.* Of Saraia it is said that Joab came forth, who according to the interpretation of the name is called the father of the valley of craftsmen, because of his sons were the architects who built the house of the Lord. And the craftsmen who dwell in the valley may be called teachers of the law, whom *knowledge* doth not *puff up, but charity edifieth [n: (I Cor. VIII)]*, [var: Vulg. 1 Cor. 8:1 *Scientia inflat, caritas vero aedificat*, with no negative] whom modesty and humility exalt, as Gamaliel and Paul his disciple [n: (Act. V, XXII)].

VERS. 17.-- *Ezra.* [n: (RAB-HIERON.)]. The Hebrews say that Ezra, who is interpreted *a helper,* is Amram the father of Moses and Aaron; that Jether, that is, *help,* [0639D] is Aaron; and that Mered, *who rebels,* is Moses. And that Epher or Opher, that is, *dust,* was Heldad. And Jalon and Meldath, who were two of the seventy elders whom Moses received from God for the help of ruling the people: for they hand down that Moses, after the law was received, persuaded his father in the wilderness to put away his mother, because she was his own friend, for she was the daughter of Levi, after which Amram took another, of whom he had Heldath and Meldath.

*And he begot Maria.* [n: (ID.)] They would have Sammai to be understood as Moses, etc., *as far as* because he governed the people in the wilderness, and by his doctrine governs them daily.

VERS. 18.-- *Of the daughter of Pharao.* [n: (ID.)] He calls the mother of Moses the daughter of Pharao, who also according to the propriety [0640A] of the name is called the daughter of the Lord, because, namely, she had a good will in the nourishing of the boy, and she is said to have been received by Amared, that is, by Moses, because, her idols being left, she was converted to the worship of God.

VERS. 21.-- *Her the father of Lecha.* [n: (ID.)] He returns to the progeny of Phares of which David sprang, that he might end the progeny of Juda, of which the royal tribe arose in David. For they hand down that he was Elimelech the husband of Noemi, in whose time, because of the transgressors of the law, the sun stood still, that on seeing the miracle they might be converted to the Lord; and because they despised this, the famine grew strong, and he who seemed the first in the tribe of Juda, driven by want, was cast out of his country with his wife and his sons, and died in his sojourning together with his sons.

VERS. 22.-- *Secure and Burning.* [n: (ID.)] That Mahalon [0640B] and Chelion, who are here called Secure and Burning, were in Moab, because they took Moabite wives, whence in the Hebrew they are called not princes, but husbands, as the teachers of that tongue hand down.

*Who returned into,* etc. Noemi and Ruth, who returned into Bethlehem when the plenty of bread was heard of.

VERS. 23.-- *Potters dwelling.* By the potters who labour in plantations and hedges we understand teachers, who form and compose the vessels of the Lord, for to fashion we call to compose, whence we call the composers of clay potters. These plant and dress the vineyard of the Lord with doctrine, and by exhorting and praying they fortify it. These abide with the king in their works, because, remaining in the faith of Christ, whatsoever they say, whatsoever they do, they do to the glory of God.

[0640C]VERS. 28.-- *They dwelt in,* etc. The progeny of Ruth, which is said to have dwelt in those very hedges and plantations, when this plantation came to the royal dignity and to the defence of the people.

*And they abode there.* In Genesis six are written, but here Sober is passed over: because there is no progeny from him. But certain names are changed for their own reasons. For in Genesis Gemuel, that is, the mother of God; here Hamuel, that is, reading with God. A certain one is called in Genesis Echa, but here Jarib, that is, contending, because from him came forth Zambri, who, fornicating with a Madianite woman, was slain by Phinees the priest. Jacim in Genesis, that is, preparation; but here he is named Zara, that is, a rising.

*And all the kindred not,* etc. Because Simeon had not [0640D] so great an offspring as Juda; whence in Josue it is said: Simeon by his kindreds received an inheritance in the midst of the children of Juda.

*These cities,* etc. Because Sicelech and Bersabee, which belonged to Juda and were seized by Simeon, returned by David into the former inheritance of Juda.

VERS. 38.-- *These are they that were named.* The stock of Simeon and their acts being described, it is mystically signified that the holy teachers, that is, the princes of the Church, providing pastures for the flocks of Christ, set out into Gador or Gader, which is interpreted *his hedge.* This is the Church, which is the defence of the faithful: there are pastures as far as the East of the valley, that is, the incarnation of the Word, *who emptied himself, taking the form of a servant* [0641A] [n: (Philip. I.)] In him are found pastures. For he himself says: *By me if any man enter in, he shall go in and go out, and shall find pastures [n: (Joan. X)]*, [var: Vulg. Jo. 10:9 *salvabitur*, "he shall be saved," which Migne's text does not print] namely the virtues. *In him a land most wide and quiet,* that is, the copious grace of the Holy Spirit; in which they of the stock of Cham had dwelt before, which signifies that the multitude of the nations left their idols and flowed together to the worship of the one God. These therefore came, whom we described above by name, in the days of Ezechias king of Juda, that is, at the coming of Christ. For Christ himself is the power of God, whom Ezechias, namely the strength of the Lord, signifies.

*They smote the tents:* he smites by faith the tents of the wicked and the dwellers among the wicked, when, denying all impiety and worldly desires, he is washed by the baptism of Christ from the filth of sins, [0641B] afterwards studying to live soberly, justly and piously in this world, that what is written may come to pass: *And it shall be in the place where it was before said: You are not my people: there they shall be called the sons of God [n: (Osee. I)]*.

VERS. 42.-- *Five hundred men.* The Fathers of the New Testament, who went into mount Seir and smote the remnants of the Amalecites, because, trampling down the pride of this world, they press down the deformity of the vices; these are said to be four, because, truly instructed in the evangelical doctrine, they fitly wage spiritual war against the demons; and, the remnants of the Amalecites being smitten, they dwelt there, because, the demons being driven out, they set virtues in the place of the vices, the possession of which makes a man blessed unto this day, which can always be said unto the end of the world, because the Church [0641C] remains unto the end of the world, in which daily the ancient enemy is triumphed over.
