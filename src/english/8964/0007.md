## CHAPTER XI.

VERS. 3.-- *The elders.* [n: (RAB.)] The prophets and the apostles run together to Christ with equal devotion, whence: *The princes of the people came together in one with the God of Abraham* [0647B] [n: (Psal. II)] [var: Vulg. Ps 2:2 *principes convenerunt in unum adversus Dominum*, "against the Lord"].

VERS. 5.-- *And they said.* Setting the blind and the lame and the lepers upon the walls in contempt of the king, and saying that the blind and the lame had kept him out.

Mystically, David casting the Jebusite out of Jerusalem signifies Christ driving the demons from the hearts of the faithful, and setting his own dwelling in the same place. For Jebusaeus is interpreted *trodden down,* Jerusalem *the vision of peace.* Whence also he says to his disciples: *Behold, I have given you power to tread upon serpents,* etc. [n: (Luc. X)]. Well therefore is it written in Kings, that when the blind and the lame who hated his soul had been cast out, David took the citadel of Sion, and called it his own city, because, while Christ drives the malign spirits and the throng of vices out of the soul, enlightening it with wisdom, it is deservedly [0647C] called the citadel of Sion, that is, of contemplation, and the vision of peace, of which it is rightly said in Psalm LXXV: *God is known in Judaea, and his name is great in Israel. And his place is made in peace.*

VERS. 8.-- *And Joab built the rest of the city.* For by the diligence of the soldier he fortified the city. Josephus, however, says that David fortified the upper city, and by joining the citadel to it made one body, and surrounded the whole with a wall. And that he set Jacob over the work of the walls, drove out the Jebusites and called it Jerosolyma. For Jero in Hebrew is a fortification. Christ too first preached the Gospel in Judaea, And strengthened the church with the fortification of his faith. Joab, who built the rest of the city, signifies the apostles and apostolic men who after [0647D] the ascension of the Lord converted very many of the gentiles; whence: *We are God's helpers, God's fields.* [var: Vulg. 1 Cor 3:9 *Dei agricultura estis*, "ye are God's husbandry"]

VERS. 11.-- *Jesbaam.* [n: (HIERON.)] Josephus names him Eusebius, and they say he was the son of Achimeas, who, frequently breaking into the enemy's line, ceased not until he had slain eight hundred. Others understand this to be David, because Jesbaan is interpreted *sitting in the people.* But Ahamoni, most wise, whence in Kings it is written: *Sitting in the chair, most wise [n: (II Reg. XXIII)]*. But there he is compared to a little worm of wood, because wisdom was diminished in him because of the sin which he had committed against Urias. Whence there he slew eight hundred at one onset, but here three hundred, for on account of the sin his fame was made less.

VERS. 12.-- [0648A] *The Ahohite.* Ahohi, as the Hebrews hand down, was the brother of Isai the father of David, whose son was Eleazar, who was the mightiest among the three, whom Josephus says to be Eusebius the son of Achimeas, and Eleazar the son of Addi, and Semeia the son of Heli

[n: (GREG. hom. 34. in Evang.)] It is to be pondered with the utmost care, etc., *as far as* rigid against himself, he abstained even from things lawful.

VERS. 21.-- *Nevertheless unto the three first.* Here it appears that Abisai was chief among the three that followed. But he did not attain to the three first, Jesbaam, Eleazar and Semmaa.

But mystically it is signified that no one of the saints, though he be eminent in virtues, attains to equality with the power of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. Since, [0648B] *who among the clouds shall be equal to the Lord?* etc. [n: (Psal. LXXXVIII.)]

VERS. 22.-- *Ariel Moab,* that is, lions, as it is said in Kings, namely, most valiant men in the kingdom of Moab, like lions.

*He slew a lion.* The Hebrew judges this lion to be Joab, who was slain in the house of the Lord holding the horns of the altar. For the altar is called a cistern, because as the water of a cistern cleanses, so the sanctuary of God expiates the sins of men.

[n: (RAB.)] Whom the book of Kings, etc., *as far as* and he wrested away the spear which he was not rightly holding.

*In the time of snow.* Whereby can be signified the grace of Christ, which shone forth to the world, and the coldness of charity, whereby men grow cold. But Banaias, who is interpreted *builder [0648C] Lord*, signifies Christ, of whom it is said: *The Lord that buildeth up Jerusalem,* etc. [n: (Psal., CXLVI.)] He wrought many works, who says: *My Father worketh until now, and I work [n: (Joan. V)]*. He is of Cabseel, that is, of the congregation of the Lord, because to him belongs the whole company of the saints — he who, coming down from heaven and made man, slaying by his own death the lion, that is, the devil, who possessed the world grown numb with the cold of its unbelief, plunged him into hell. He, overcoming by the rod of the cross the Egyptian, that is, Antichrist, blackest with sins, that is, abusing the five senses of the body unto crimes, condemned him, guilty by his own wickedness, with perpetual power. In the days of snow, because by death he expiated sin. Whence: *Thou shalt wash me, and I shall be made whiter than snow* [0648D] [n: (Psal. L)].

VERS. 24.-- *Among the three.* Abisai and Sobochai and Jonathan, to whom he was equal in strength, who was over the thirty, as the Hebrew affirms.

## CHAPTER XII.

VERS. 12.-- *These also came.* Thus far he has enumerated the mighty men of David who helped him that he might reign over Israel.

*Who were most valiant,* etc. According to the letter it signifies that they were nimble and strenuous in every kind of warfare. But mystically it describes the soldiers of Christ as not sluggish but zealous, in prosperity and in adversity, on the right hand and on the left.

[0649A]VERS. 8.-- *Swift.* [n: (RAB.)] He says it for keenness of sense or for agility of body. But mystically, they who profess the warfare of Christ ought not to be little ones in sense, but to provide good things, not only before God, but also before all men

VERS. 14.-- *The last,* etc. Here they signify the teachers of the Jews and of the gentiles, of whom Paul, of the tribe of Benjamin, says: *Peter, James and John, who seemed to be pillars, gave me the right hands, and of the fellowship of Barnabas,* etc. [n: (Gal. XI.)] [var: Vulg. Gal 2:9 *Jacobus, et Cephas, et Joannes … dextras dederunt mihi*, "James, and Cephas, and John"] These, coming to Christ, were tried in all things. Whence [n: (Matth. XVI)]: *But whom do you say that I am?* Peter answered for all: *Thou art Christ, the Son of the living God.* To whom the Lord: *Thou art Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church.* And elsewhere: *Receive ye the Holy [0649B] Spirit, whose sins you shall forgive, they are forgiven them [n: (Joan. XX)]*.

VERS. 15.-- *They passed over the Jordan.* The Jordan, in which Christ was baptized, signifies baptism: this the teachers cross in the first month, *when it is wont to overflow above,* because, when the Lord appeared in the flesh and consecrated the acceptable year, after he had consecrated baptism by his own baptism, they spread it abroad through the whole world by preaching and by baptizing.

*And they put to flight all that dwelt* [n: (RAB.)]. Because by the preaching of the Gospel they put to flight the Jews, whom the Eastern quarter signifies on account of the rising of the knowledge of the law, or the gentiles, in whom natural prudence goes down, while they ascribe the worship of God to idols. Whence: *Thou hast given me my enemies downward, and hast destroyed them that hated me* [0649C] [n: (Psal. XVII)] [var: Vulg. Ps 17:41 *dorsum*, "the back"].

VERS. 19.-- *Moreover of Manasses.* These, namely, the seven princes who came from Manasses, signify the first teachers of the Church coming from Judaea, who, enlightened by the sevenfold Spirit, help our David against the robbers, that is, the heretics or the malign spirits, who cease not to snatch from the Church and to lead captive whom they can; whence: *But thanks be to God, who always maketh us to triumph in Christ Jesus [n: (II Cor. I)]*.

VERS. 22.-- *The army of God.* Which, namely, went out of Egypt, that is, six hundred thousand, so the Hebrews expound it. But mystically, in the army of our David the number of the faithful grows daily, until they be completed who are predestined to life eternal.

[0649D]VERS. 23.-- *That they might transfer.* Because the holy teachers greatly desire that the worship of the old law be transferred into the spiritual understanding of the Gospel, and that all things be made new.

VERS. 27.-- *Joiada also, a prince of the stock of Aaron,* etc. [var: Vulg. 1 Par 12:27 *Jojoda*] The Hebrews hand down that this was Phinees the son of Eleazar, in comparison with whom Sadoc is called a boy, as being less in age and in merit.

VERS. 32.-- *Of the sons also of Issachar, men,* etc. They hand down that the teachers of the law were in the tribe of Issachar, who taught the several times of the ceremonies to be observed, and prescribed what Israel ought to do. These signify the teachers of the Church, to whose precepts their subjects give obedience.

[0650A]VERS. 35.-- *Twenty,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The apostles, namely, and apostolic men come together to our David and are spiritually joined to him, and others as well from the Jews as from the gentiles, striving with one heart and with equal desire that David may be made king, and that they themselves may be the kingdom of Christ.

VERS. 39.-- *Three days.* At all times, namely, holding the faith of the Trinity, and receiving what the first teachers, that is, the apostles, had prepared as nourishment out of Holy Scripture, and giving thanks for the bestowal of all good things.
