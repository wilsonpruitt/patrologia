## CHAPTER XIII.

VERS. 1.-- *And he took counsel.* With the apostles, the evangelists, who carried over the faith of the incarnation from the Jews to the salvation of the gentiles. For this is to carry over to himself the ark of the Lord from the hill of Cariathiarim [0650B] (which is in Juda, and is interpreted *the city of woods* ): namely, to bring the faith of the Lord from the eminence of the Scriptures set among the Jews, where the shady darkness of figures abounds; to the understanding of the gentile people, where the Church is founded.

VERS. 3.-- *For not.* [n: (RAB.)] Because the Old Testament is interpreted, that people could not open the spiritual sense which lay hidden, as it has now been revealed to the apostles and prophets, namely, that the gentiles are co-heirs and fellow partakers of the promise of the Lord.

VERS. 5.-- *From Sihor.* Sihor is a river of Egypt, which, as they report, is derived from the Nile, and is interpreted *a new and turbid firmament,* whence: *What now wilt thou in the way of Egypt, that thou mayest drink turbid water (Jerem. [0650C] VII)*? *Cariathiarim.* A city of Damascus, which in Amos is Emathabal, that is, Emath the great, which is now called Epiphania. But by Sihor and Emath he comprehends the whole land of promise. By Sihor, a brook, namely, or a torrent, he signifies the southern part of Egypt. By Emath the northern. *Cariatharim,* which elsewhere is Cariathabaal, a city of forests, one of the cities of the Gabaonites in the tribe of Juda, at the ninth milestone from Helia as one goes to Diospolis, of which was Urias the prophet, whom Joachim slew in Jerusalem, according to Jeremias. The son also of Sobal is called Cariathiarim, as is shown above

VERS. 6.-- *Every man.* The nobler elect. But the number of these men is comprised in Kings, where it is read: *And David gathered together all the chosen men [0650D] out of Israel, thirty thousand [n: (II Reg. VI)]*.

VERS. 9.-- *And when they had come,* etc. [n: (RAB. ex Hieron.)] They came to the threshing-floor of Chidon, etc., *as far as* as though he should say: *God forbid that I should glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ [n: (Gal. VI)]*.

VERS. 11.-- *Because the Lord had divided Oza.* [n: (HIERON.)] The Hebrews hand down that Abinadab the priest had commanded his sons Oza and Haio to carry the ark upon their shoulders according to the precept of the law. But because they did not carry it, nor gave it to the Caathites to be carried, the Lord struck Oza, and divided him.

*The division of Oza.* It is handed down that his arm and shoulder had withered, where the ark of the Lord was to have been carried, and that in the place of it a certain division was made.

[0651A]VERS. 17.-- *In the house of Obededom.* [n: (HIERON.)] Because he too was a Levite. But when the cause for which Oza was struck was known, David commanded the ark to be carried on the Levites' own shoulders, as appears in what follows. It is to be noted how greatly he offends who receives the body of the Lord unworthily, if a devout priest perished who laid hold of the ark, the figure of the Lord's body, with less veneration than he owed.

Allegorically, however, David signifies Christ, the ark the Church. which Christ wills to bring into his own city, whence: *I was not sent but to the sheep that are lost of the house of Israel [n: (Matth. XV.)]* And elsewhere: *Blindness in part has happened in Israel,* etc. [n: (Rom. XI)].

## CHAPTER XIV.

[0651B]VERS. 3.-- *David also took,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The many wives of David signify the many nations which are joined to Christ by faith. The concubines, the churches of the heretics, which glory in the title of the Christian name. But because they follow Christ for the sake of carnal gain, they are called not wives, but concubines. Now, if kings have many wives, it is a crime, because the figures have passed away.

VERS. 8.-- *And hearing.* When the grace of the Gospel had been noised abroad and the kingdom of Christ everywhere preached, the nations rose up on every side, contradicting the Gospel of peace.

VERS. 15.-- *In the top of the pear trees.* [n: (RAB.)] But in the Hebrew, *of the weeping ones,* that is, of the idols, he bids them come, etc., *as far as* whom the Holy Spirit directs by forewarning.

[0651C]VERS. 16.-- *From Gabaon as far as Gazera.* Christ strikes those who stand upon the hill of pride, and crushes the hedge of hardness, dividing their obstinacy that he alone may bear rule in the world, whence there follows: *And the name of David was spread abroad in all regions, and the Lord,* etc.

## CHAPTER XV.

VERS. 2.-- *Then David said: It is unlawful,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Christ wills not that the unworthy discharge the ecclesiastical office, but the learned in knowledge and the approved in honesty of manners bear the ark of God, that is, the Church, on the shoulders of pious labour, and exult, whence: *Do the work of an evangelist, fulfil thy ministry [n: (II Tim. IV)]*.

VERS. 3.-- *And he gathered together all Israel.* Not [0651D] only David, but also all the prophets in the Church of Christ, by words and by deeds, gave place to the incarnation, that he might the sooner be received when he came, having been prophesied of beforehand.

VERS. 12.-- *Be sanctified with your brethren.* The ministers of the New Testament ought to be clean in life and in doctrine, to whom it is said: *Be ye holy, for I am holy [n: (Levit. XIX)]*. Who ought, by the words of right faith and by the example of their work, to commend to the faithful the incarnation of Christ and the redemption of the human race, etc.

VERS. 16.-- *With nables, to wit,* etc. Nablum in Hebrew, Psalterium in Greek, laudatorium in Latin. The psaltery has a swollen belly in its upper parts, so that the threads of the strings, bound fast and skilfully struck, [0652A] may render a most sweet melody. To this the cithara seems contrary, which holds in its lowest part what the psaltery holds in its head. The psaltery with ten strings is the Church with the Decalogue of the law. Opposed to this is the cithara, which is composed of twenty-four strings after the manner of the letter delta (as the most skilled hand down), and is roused by the fingers into diverse modes. This signifies the Church, which with the doctrines of the twenty-four elders has a threefold form after the manner of the letter delta, and signifies the faith of the holy Trinity. But by the hands of the apostles and evangelists it is figuratively roused into the diverse measures of the Old and the New Testament, one way in the letter, another way in the sense.

*With lyres.* The lyre is so called, ἀπὸ τοῦ λύριν, that is, from the variety of voices, because it makes diverse sounds. This signifies the Church [0652B], which in the variety of diverse disciplines, historically, allegorically, tropologically, and by anagogy, utters a sweet sound, yet departs not from the simplicity of the symphony. But cymbals struck one against another make a sound, whence: *Praise him on well-sounding cymbals,* etc. [n: (Psal. CL)]. The well-sounding cymbals are our lips, which, struck one against another, render the sweet harmony of the human voice.

VERS. 21.-- *They sang for the octave,* etc. [n: (HIERON.)] They signified, namely, in the day of judgment the coming of the Lord, in which, the week of the world being ended, he will come to judgment. Whence the sixth psalm, which is sung with the trembling of judgment, is marked beforehand with such a title. And this number contains great mysteries. Eight souls entered into the ark of Noe, and, the world perishing, [0652C] were saved. David was the eighth son of Jesse, whom the Lord chose. On the eighth day circumcision purifies. On the eighth day, that is, the Lord's day, Christ rose again.

VERS. 24.-- *And Sebenias and Josaphat,* etc. These seven priests sounding with trumpets before the ark, holy preachers, are full of the sevenfold Spirit of grace, who preach the Gospel in the Church of Christ, and preach the mystery of the incarnation to their hearers.

*They sounded.* The trumpet is made one way for gathering the people, another way for leading them forth, another for victory, another for pursuing enemies, another for the shutting up of cities, etc. But the customary trumpet, among those skilled in such matters, is formed in this way, that it be blown at the narrow head through three brazen pipes, as through four brazen voice-conduits which through a brazen base produce fourfold voices, [0652D] so that they may put forth a most vehement bellowing: which signify the Gospel, divinely inspired by the threefold confession of the three persons, at the narrow head, that is, in the nativity of Christ, through four brazen voice-conduits, that is, through the four evangelists; and on a brazen, that is, a stable foundation of faith and of work, to send forth into the world a most vehement voice, as it were the voice of a gathering trumpet.

VERS. 27.-- *But David.* Christ makes manifest the example of his incarnation and of his passion, before hidden from the Jews; for wood [cj: *lignum*; read *linum*, "flax"] sprung from the earth, coming by manifold labour to the whiteness of a garment, shows the truth of human flesh triumphing amid the scourges, and this is: *David was in an ephod.* [var: Vulg. 1 Par 15:27 *David autem etiam indutus erat ephod lineo*, "and David also was clothed with a linen ephod"]

[0653A] [n: (GREG., lib. XXVII Moral., cap. 27)]. It is pleasing to behold how great the gifts of the virtues, etc., *as far as* such as I show myself outwardly, despising myself, such do I regard myself inwardly.

VERS. 29.-- *Michol the daughter of Saul.* [n: (RAB.)] While all were exulting and resounding with hymns at the entrance of the ark, etc., *as far as* she despises David thus humbled: because, when at the last the Jews believe, certain men follow Christ in profession and Antichrist in work: to whom it agrees that Michol, who for the figuring of the instability of carnal men is interpreted *all water,* is called not the wife of David, but the daughter of Saul. For they who serve Christ only in outward show are not crowned with him, but are damned with the persecutors whom they have imitated.
