## CHAPTER XVI.

[0653B]VERS. 1.-- *They brought therefore. [n: (RAB. in Paral., tom. III.)]* The ark is set in the midst of the tabernacle which David had pitched for it, that is, the faith of the Church is preached, goes forward, and is engrafted in the hearts of the faithful, whom God foreordained to life eternal.

VERS. 2.-- *He blessed the people.* Christ commends also the devotion of the Church, to the Father, he who also at the right hand of the Father intercedes for us, and as the true David blesses, and feeds them with the nourishments of the saving mystery.

VERS. 3.-- *And he divided.* The faithful receive these gifts when, being many, one bread and one body in Christ, chastising the wantonness of their flesh and subjecting it to servitude, they seethe it with the fire of the Holy Spirit, and make the fruits of good works, most fat with the oil of mercy, [0653C] to boil with compassion for their neighbour. But on the contrary the daughter of Saul, having entered the king's chamber in vain, does not bear fruit, because they who receive the word of God only as far as the ear die without the offspring of good work.

*Fried with oil.* Flesh most clean, namely, from the salt of sin, roasted for the cause of human salvation in the frying-pan of the cross, full of the oil of charity. *For God so loved the world, as to give the only-begotten Son,* who suffered out of charity.

VERS. 7.-- *In that day.* Because Christ established the order of the teachers on the summit of ecclesiastical dignity. For Asaph is interpreted *gathering.* Asaph therefore and his brethren are the apostles and apostolic men appointed for the gathering of the faithful, of whom it is said: *Thou shalt appoint them princes over all the earth.* [0653D] But the manner of praise which David himself composed, the writer of the history has inserted, saying: *Give praise to the Lord,* etc.

VERS. 8.-- *Give praise,* etc. Alleluia is set as a title before this psalm, and according to Jerome it is of the neuter gender. *Allelu,* praise ye: *ia* signifies the invisible God, which is one of the ten names of God, as Jerome hands down in his epistle to Marcella.

VERS. 36.-- *And let all say.* [n: (RAB.)] The gentile, namely, who was as yet unbelieving when this man sang, who nevertheless was led by the power of prophecy to say, out of love for all men, Amen amen, that is, so be it, so be it, as today Amen is answered to those who pray, that the devout intent of the priests and the sound [0654A] devotion of the people may agree together in prayers, according to that: *Amen, amen, I say to you, that if two of you shall consent upon earth, concerning every thing whatsoever they shall ask, it shall be done to them,* etc. [n: (Matth. XVIII.)] [var: Vulg. Mt 18:19 *Iterum dico vobis*, "Again I say to you"]

VERS. 39.-- *In the high place.* Some say that the tabernacle of God, after the children of Israel had entered into the land of Chanaan, was first in Sylo, then in Nobe, afterwards in Gabaon, and at last in Sion. Others, that there was a place in Jerusalem called Gabaon, and that David set the tabernacle and the ark there. For Gabaon is interpreted *a hill.* And they judge that the place was called a high place for the dignity of the divine worship. But in the book of Places we read that Gabaon is the metropolis of the Hevites in the tribe of Benjamin, and there is now shown a village of the same name at the fourth milestone from Bethlehem toward the west, beside [0654B] Rhama and Rhamon, where Solomon, when victims had been offered, merited the divine oracle. And it was set apart for the Levites.

*That he might bless.* [n: (HIERON.)] That he might have leisure for the begetting of sons. Others take the blessing by antiphrasis; and the house, as Michol the daughter of Saul, whom David rebuked, because she despised him dancing before the ark of the Lord.

## CHAPTER XVII.

VERS. 2.-- *And Nathan said.* [n: (RAB., ubi supra. )] It is plain that the spirit of prophecy does not always irradiate the minds of the prophets; whence Eliseus: *Let her alone, for her mind is in bitterness, and the Lord hath hid it from me,* etc. [var: Vulg. 4 Rg 4:27 *Dimitte illam : anima enim ejus in amaritudine est*, "Let her alone: for her soul is in bitterness"] Which happens by divine dispensation, that they who receive the Spirit may find what was of God, and they who lose it, [0654C] what is of themselves.

VERS. 4.-- *Thou shalt not build.* Because thou art a man of blood. For he ought to be clean in himself who wills to correct the things of others, that he may flee earthly desires, and see the more clearly what is to be fled, the more truly he himself turns aside from these by knowledge and by life. A hand that holds mud would not be able to wipe away the filth cast upon it. And this, according to the sequence of the ancient translation, is well said to David, labouring at outward wars, by way of signification and by the divine voice: *Thou shalt not build me a temple, because thou art a man of blood.* He builds the temple of the Lord who gives himself to the correcting of his neighbours' minds. We are the temple of God, who are established unto true life out of his indwelling, whence: *For the temple of God is holy, which you are [n: (I Cor. III)]*. A man of blood [0654D] builds [cj: *aedificat*; read *non aedificat*, "does not build"] the temple of God. For he who still lies among carnal things must needs blush to instruct the minds of his neighbours.

VERS. 9.-- *Nor the children.* [n: (RAB.)] Foreign enemies at intervals of time, as of old first peace, then war. Yet there are found there longer times of peace than Solomon had. For under Aod there were eighty years of peace. Far be it, therefore, that these things be understood of Solomon.

VERS. 11.-- *And when thou shalt have fulfilled.* But Solomon began to reign while his father reigned, which befell none of those kings, so that it appears that he is not the one whom that prophecy foreshows. But Christ, the true peaceable one, truly reigned after the death of David.

[0655A]VERS. 12.-- *He shall build* a house, that is, the Church: the house of David, because of the temple of God made of men, not of stones, where the people may dwell for ever with God and in God, and God with the people. Therefore, since according to the Septuagint it was said by Nathan: And the Lord will declare to thee that thou shalt build him a house, afterwards it is said by David: *Because thou, God of Israel, hast revealed to the ear of thy servant, saying: I will build thee a house [n: (II Reg. VII)]*. For this house we also build by living well, and God by helping, whence: *Unless the Lord build the house,* etc. [n: (Psal. CXXVI.)]

*And his throne.* It can be taken hyperbolically of the son of Isai. But indeed Christ, born of the seed of David, has been exalted above all principality and power and might, to whom the Father *gave a name which is above every [0655B] name, that in the name of Jesus every knee should bow,* etc. [n: (Philip. II.)]
