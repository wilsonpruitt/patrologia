## CHAPTER XVIII

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *And the daughters.* [n: (RAB.)] Lesser cities belonging to the mother city, etc., *as far as* and the flesh is compelled to serve the spirit.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Adarezer.* ⟦s2⟧ Who is interpreted seemly help; king of Soba, which is interpreted burnt or turning, signifies the vices of the gentile world kindled by natural fervour: against which Christ fights and conquers *with the sword of the spirit, which is the word of God [n: (Ephes. VI)]*.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *And he hamstrung.* ⟦s2⟧ Christ hamstrings the proud height of the world's human wisdom that fights against him; yet what he finds convenient and fitted [0655C] for preaching, he reserves to himself.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *In Damascus.* ⟦s2⟧ Damascus, a noble city of Phoenicia, is interpreted *a cup of blood.* ⟦s3⟧ Syria, *lofty* or *moist.* ⟦s4⟧ For the gentile world, formerly flowing with lust and proud in spirit, thirsted for human blood: now cleansed by the baptism of Christ, exalted by the advance of virtues, it desires the cup of his blood.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Quivers.* ⟦s2⟧ The teachings of the gentiles in which the liberal disciplines are contained, which Christ took away from the gentile world and turned to the service of the Church: for they avail for the overthrow of error and the confirmation of truth.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Solomon.* ⟦s2⟧ Because therefore our king is peaceable, he turned the oldness of speech, out of the lowliness of the overthrown [0655D] gentile world, to the cleansing grace of baptism and to the firmament of faith, through the preaching of holy teaching, unto the use of the Church. ⟦s3⟧ For Tebath is interpreted *a little one cometh,* and Chun *beauty.* ⟦s4⟧ For he who acknowledges himself a little one is made fair and comely in the beauty of virtues.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *In the valley of the Saltpits.* ⟦s2⟧ In Kings it is thus written: *David made himself a name when he returned after Syria was taken, in the valley of the Saltpits, eighteen thousand being slain [n: (II Reg. VIII)]*, because, namely, he raised for himself a triumphal arch: he also made himself a name, because, when Syria of Damascus and Syna Sobal had been conquered, he is said to have raised the other.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Ten and eight thousand.* ⟦s2⟧ But in certain codices it is found that David slew not twenty and eight thousand in the valley of the Saltpits [0656A], which came about by the fault of the scribes. ⟦s3⟧ But Joab laid low twelve thousand, as is written in the title of the fifty-seventh psalm. ⟦s4⟧ But here Abisai is reported to have slain eighteen, yet for the sake of the royal dignity the victory has been ascribed to David. ⟦s5⟧ And because Christ, by the strictness of his examination, extinguishes in those who think perversely of him the folly of immoderate savour, whence: *He that turneth his own tongue, falleth into evil [n: (Prov. XVII)]*. [var: Vulg. Prov. 17:20 *qui vertit linguam incidet in malum*, without *suam* and in the future, "shall fall into evil"]

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Achimelech.* ⟦s2⟧ That is, my brother a king: who in Kings is Abimelech, that is, my father a king, instructs the priests of the Church, that they who by the order of dignity hold a father's place, may through humility be reckoned brothers, and may not lord it over the clergy.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Phelethi.* [n: (HIERON.)] Admirable ones, or givers of life, [0656B] whom some say were the seventy judges, after the manner of those whom Moses, at the Lord's bidding, chose in the desert. ⟦s2⟧ Others say that the Cerethites were appointed to this, that they should punish the guilty, but the Phelethites that they should defend the innocent.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Moreover.* ⟦s2⟧ The sons of David are the faithful of Christ, whom holy Church bears to him, who are *a chosen generation, a kingly priesthood [n: (I Petr. II)]*.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The first at the hand.* ⟦s2⟧ Because by good works they merit for themselves the kingdom of heaven, so much the nearer to God as they are the holier.

## CHAPTER XIX.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Ammon.* [n: (RAB.)] Who is interpreted, etc., *as far as* whose *countenance is upon them that do evil* [0656C] [n: (Psal. XXXIII.)] [var: Vulg. Ps. 33:17 *Vultus autem Domini super facientes mala*, "But the countenance of the Lord is upon them that do evil"]

## CHAPTER XX.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *He gathered together.* [n: (RAB.)] Joab began the war, David finished it. ⟦s2+3⟧ For Christ wages war when the order of the preachers of faith opposes the mighty of the world, but the end of the contest and the triumph is referred to Christ, *who worketh in us to will and to accomplish [n: (Philip. II)]*. [var: Vulg. Phil. 2:13 *qui operatur in vobis et velle, et perficere*, "who worketh in you both to will and to accomplish"] Who fitted to himself the crown which he took from the devil, namely the faithful, who surround him as it were a crown, whence: *Thou shalt bless the crown of the year of thy goodness [n: (Deut. VII)]*.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *A crown,* etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Because by melting it down he purified it according to the law. ⟦s2⟧ But the Hebrews hand down (because it was unlawful for a Jewish man to take anything of gold or silver from idols, since the law [0656D] forbids idolatry) that Ethai the Hethite (who had come to David from the Philistines) snatched the crown from the head of Melchon, so that it might be lawful for a Jew to take from a man's hand what it was not lawful to take from an idol's head.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *War.* [n: (RAB.)] It is to be noted that in Paralipomenon three wars of David are chiefly read of, etc., *as far as* whence: *In God we shall do mightily,* etc. [n: (Psal. LIX)].

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Another war.* [n: (ID.)] In the book of Kings thus: *The third was in Gob against the Philistines, in which Adeodatus the son of Saltus, an embroiderer, a Bethlehemite, slew Goliath the Gethite,* etc. [n: (II Reg. XXI)]. ⟦s2⟧ Gob, etc., *as far as* but the Hebrews judge Araphan the mother of the giants to be Orpha the daughter-in-law of Noemi.

## CHAPTER XXI.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0657A]

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *And there was found,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] In the book of Kings thus. ⟦s2+3⟧ That there were found of Israel eighty thousand valiant men and of Juda fifty thousand fighting men. [var: Vulg. 2 Reg. 24:9 *octingenta millia virorum fortium … quingenta millia pugnatorum*, "eight hundred thousand … five hundred thousand"] But the Hebrews assert that this number was found by Joab, but that the writer would not show more than is written in Samuel and in the book of Kings, in which only those who survived the slaughter are contained.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *And he struck.* ⟦s2⟧ He speaks of the death which first came by reason of sin: this raged among the people, because often for the evil of the flock the life of the shepherd offends. ⟦s3⟧ David, lifted up with swelling, sinned by numbering the people, and the people received the punishment, because according to the merits of the peoples the hearts of the rulers are disposed. ⟦s4⟧ But the just [0657B] judge, by the chastisement of those very men, corrected the vice of the sinner, on whose account he had sinned. ⟦s5⟧ But because, growing proud by his own will, he was not free from the fault, he received the vengeance of the fault; for so that which struck the people bodily laid the ruler low with grief of heart.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I have sinned exceedingly,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In the book of Kings thus, etc., *as far as* bound to do this, David offended God.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *And he said.* ⟦s2⟧ If he should choose famine, he would seem to act against others: because, having much corn, he would not suffer want along with the rest. ⟦s3⟧ If he should choose to be conquered by his enemies for three months, having about him the most valiant men, he would fear nothing, though the army lay under slaughter. ⟦s4⟧ He chose therefore a suffering common to himself and to the rest, in which the fear should be equal for all.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0657C]VERS. 14.-- *Seventy thousand men.* [n: (RAB.)] For the heads of the people and the elders were numbered. ⟦s2⟧ But the Hebrews affirm that so many thousands were slain out of the whole people as the thousands by which the number written in Paralipomenon exceeds the number which is in Kings. ⟦s3⟧ For they say that in Paralipomenon both those who had remained and those who were slain were numbered: but in Kings only the living.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *And that he should build an altar to the Lord God in the floor of Ornam the Jebusite.* [n: (RAB.)] Well, when David wished to appease the Lord, is he bidden to build an altar in the ark of Areuna or Ornam, because God is not appeased by man unless in the ark of the heart, through the light of right faith and of true love, he set up for him an altar of humility, in which he may offer [0657D] the sacrifice of praise.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *David therefore gave to Ornam for the place six hundred sicles of gold of most just weight, and built there an altar to the Lord,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ In Kings thus, etc., *as far as* which afterward Solomon built, and its courts.
