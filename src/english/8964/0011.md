## CHAPTER XXII.

VERS. 2.-- *That they should be gathered together.* [n: (RAB.)] Out of the Church Christ chose the craftsmen of his house, who should hew and polish the rude manners of men, that in the spiritual building they might come together to one another through the diverse joinings of faith and of charity. Whence: *For this cause have I hewed by the prophets, and I have slain them by the words of my mouth [n: (Osee VI)]*

[0658A]VERS. 3.-- *Iron.* He ordered the firmness of faith by sacred teachings, that they who enter into the Church might be pierced through with the goads of fear, and so be knit to one another through the faith of love and of fellowship. Whence: *Pierce thou my flesh with thy fear [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*. And elsewhere: *Jerusalem, which is built as a city, whose participation thereof,* etc. [n: (Ibid. CXXII)].

*Of brass.* The sounding lessons and spiritual songs which the holy Fathers prepared for the instruction of the Church for Christ the true peaceable one.

VERS. 4.-- *Timber also.* Men renowned for virtues, whom they trained up for an example or inserted in their writings.

*The Sidonians and Tyrians.* Because through the preachers, who were chosen out of the gentiles, such men came to the knowledge of the Church.

[0658B]VERS. 5.-- *He is a little child and delicate.* In the time of the Old Testament the knowledge of the incarnation of Christ was obscure: small also was the number of the faithful, who are the members of Christ.

*He prepared the charges.* Moses prepared the charges of the house of the Lord, saying: *O Israel, what doth the Lord thy God ask of thee? but that thou fear the Lord thy God, that thou walk in his ways,* etc., and other like things. [var: Vulg. Deut. 10:12 *Et nunc Israel, quid Dominus Deus tuus petit a te, nisi ut timeas Dominum Deum tuum, et ambules in viis ejus*, "And now, Israel … and that thou walk in his ways"]

VERS. 8.-- *Much.* It signifies that the Jews, because of the carnal observance of the law and their bloody works, could not prepare a spiritual building for the Lord; or because they had hands polluted with the blood of Christ and of the prophets, whence: *Jerusalem, Jerusalem, thou that killest the prophets, and stonest them that are sent to thee* [0658C] [n: (Matth. XXIII)].

VERS. 14.-- *A hundred thousand talents of gold, and a thousand thousand talents of silver.* [n: (RAB.)] The Fathers of the Old Testament inserted in their writings depth of meaning and comeliness of speech.

*Of talents.* The talent surpasses the other weights: and it signifies both the greatness and the perfection of the thing.

VERS. 15.-- *Workmen, stonecutters, and masons,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The preachers, who are called stonecutters, masons, and workers in wood, and by many other names, because according to the quality of the hearers the teaching of the preachers is shaped. For the wise are to be taught in one way, the unwise in another, the proud in one way, the humble in another.

VERS. 17.-- *David also commanded all the princes [0658D] of Israel that they should help Solomon,* etc. It signifies that the law and the prophets teach with firm authority, where they who bear rule in the Church by holiness and virtue may help the true Solomon in his building, by teaching, that is, and by working; whence: *Adore ye the Lord in his holy court [n: (Psal. XVIII)]*.

*A sanctuary to the Lord God, that the ark of the covenant of the Lord may be brought in, and the vessels consecrated to the Lord into the house which is built,* etc.-- *The soul of the just is the seat of wisdom [n: (Sap. VII)]*. In this house the ark rests, that is, the faith of the incarnation of Christ.

## CHAPTER XXIII.

VERS. 1.-- *David therefore,* etc. After the completion [0659A] of the Old Testament there followed the incarnation of the Saviour, of whose kingdom there shall be no end, whence: *When the fulness of time came, God sent his Son [n: (Gal. IV)]*.

*And they were numbered.* They who discharge the ministry of the Lord ought to keep the faith of the holy Trinity and the fulfilling of the commandments. Whence: *There were found thirty-eight thousand men.* For whosoever with sound faith and right work serves God unto the end, shall come both to the grace of the resurrection and to the eternal beatitude which are signified in the octonary.

*Twenty-four.* Because the true ministers of light ought to be instant, and not to turn aside from the way of justice. For in twenty-four hours the whole world is lighted through its circuit, and the shadow of darkness is driven away. Whence the seventy-two disciples sent to preach, because [0659B] the faith of the holy Trinity with the works of light suffices unto justice: for thrice twenty and four times three make seventy-two, by which number the diversity of tongues and the series of the books of either Testament is comprehended.

*Of the overseers.* Because no one ought to desire the dignity of this order, save he who is zealous for perfection and for teaching. For by the senary number the world was made, and man was created on the sixth day, on which he was also redeemed.

[n: (RAB.)] Because the law and the prophets, etc., *as far as* and let him patiently bear all things, bitter and angry at his own sins.

VERS. 25.-- *For David said: The Lord God hath given rest,* etc. [n: (ID.)] While the children of Israel were in the desert, etc., *as far as* but the Gospel stable and [0659C] fixed.

VERS. 28.-- *And they shall be under the hand of the sons of Aaron, for the service of the house,* etc. That the lesser may learn to obey the greater, and the greater, helped by the lesser, may be able the more worthily to fulfil their office: as is done in the Church today.

*In the chambers and in the place of purification.* Bedchambers or cells in which the Levites and priests dwell. These also, the ministers of the true temple, ought diligently to watch the entrance, to let in the worthy, but to drive back the unworthy.

VERS. 29.-- *For the seething fine flour, and for the parching,* etc. The ears, namely, of the firstfruits, which were parched and whose grains were eaten, which they commonly call grancas; in certain codices it is read: *For the seething fine flour.* In others *for the seething,* only: but [0659D] in the Hebrew it is read, *for the roasting.* [var: Vulg. 1 Par. 23:29 *et ad similae sacrificium … et ad torrendum*, "and for the sacrifice of fine flour … and for the roasting"]

## CHAPTER XXIV.

VERS. 2.-- *And executed the priestly office.* [n: (RAB.)] It is possible through Eleazar the elder son of Aaron, etc., *as far as* because the children of the desolate are many more than of her that hath a husband.

VERS. 4.-- *By families sixteen.* The octonary is doubled. In the law carnal circumcision is celebrated on the eighth day, but in the Gospel the circumcision of hearts in the present, and of bodies in the future.

VERS. 6.-- *And he described.* [n: (RAB.)] The order of preachers, etc., *as far as* and is deservedly reckoned by the name of justice.

[0660A]VERS. 7.-- *Now the lot came out.* If any think that God is to be consulted by lots in necessity after the example of the apostles, let them see that the apostles did this when the number of the brethren had been gathered, and had first poured forth prayers to God.

*The first Joiarib, the second,* etc. David distributed the Levitical tribe and the priestly order into twenty-four lots, showing that the Church fully has the priestly and the royal dignity and perfectly keeps the teaching both of the law and of the Gospel, or the perfection of work, which is commended in the senary, and the brightness of evangelical preaching, which is figured in the quaternary. For four times six make twenty-four.

## CHAPTER XXV.

VERS. 1.-- *David therefore and the chief officers of the army set apart for the ministry,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Because the true [0660B] David our king and Lord, etc., *as far as* and overleaps all covetousness of earthly things, he worthily pays his praises

VERS. 7.-- *All the teachers.* These are they who today expound the canons of the divine books with an evangelical understanding, for four times seventy-two make two hundred and eighty. [cj: *ducenta octoginta*; read *ducenta octoginta octo*, "two hundred and eighty-eight" — four times seventy-two, and the reading of 1 Par. 25:7] But that the books of the Old and the New Testament are seventy-two is not to be doubted. Whence whosoever discharges a divine office, being intent upon the four principal virtues, let him apply himself to evangelical teaching, and according to its rule let him aim to shape whatsoever he shall have learned or taught.
