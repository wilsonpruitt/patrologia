## CHAPTER XXVI.

VERS. 2.-- *Of Meselemia.* [n: (RAB.)] Who is interpreted [0660C] *the Lord rendering,* and signifies the order of the apostles and of the first teachers, to whom the eastern gate, that is, the primitive Church, is entrusted, to whom the promises of the ancient Fathers were rendered.

*Of Zacharias.* Who is interpreted the memory of the Lord, and signifies the teachers of the gentiles, because they were ordained to preach by the first preachers. Whence *while the apostles were praying and fasting, the Holy Ghost said: Separate me both Paul and Barnabas [n: (Act. III)]*. [var: Vulg. Act. 13:2 *Ministrantibus autem illis Domino, et jejunantibus, dixit illis Spiritus Sanctus : Segregate mihi Saulum et Barnabam*, "And as they were ministering to the Lord and fasting, the Holy Ghost said to them: Separate me Saul and Barnabas"]

VERS. 4.-- *Obededom.* Red: they who were begotten in servitude, and, drenched with the redness of the blood of Christ, held their guilt in the open; rightly are they brought back to their former grade through the condescension of the masters, whence Paul: *I made myself the servant of all, that I might gain all [n: (I Cor. IX)]*. [var: Vulg. 1 Cor. 9:19 *omnium me servum feci, ut plures lucrifacerem*, "that I might gain the more"]

[0660D]VERS. 15.-- *To the south.* [n: (RAB.)] The southern gate signifies the Jewish people, enlightened by the law and the prophets, near to the receiving of the warmth of faith and of the light of wisdom.

VERS. 16.-- *To the west, near the gate,* etc. By the western region the end of the world is signified, in which shall be the persecution of Antichrist: and *iniquity shall abound, and the charity of many shall grow cold [n: (Matth. XXIV)]*. [var: Vulg. Matth. 24:12 *Et quoniam abundavit iniquitas, refrigescet caritas multorum*, "And because iniquity hath abounded, the charity of many shall grow cold" — the first verb perfect, not future] The doorkeepers of this gate are the teachers of that time, who strengthen the Church by teaching and by exhorting: whence Sephim is interpreted humble or of the plain, and Hosa divining. For the Church shall be humbled in the persecution, and in the field of wantonness shall be dissolute. But raised up in the action of the perfect, which is foresignified by divine [0661A] words, whence: *The words are shut up until the appointed time [n: (Dan. XII)]*.

VERS. 17.-- *Six Levites, and to the north four a day,* etc. The senary signifies the good work of the ministries of God, the quaternary the evangelical teaching and the fulness of the principal virtues.

*Two and two.* He says that the doorkeepers are two and two in the little cells and in the way, because whether in the cells of their heart they bear their own care, or in the way expound outwardly the word of God, they should altogether keep the two precepts of charity and the rule of either Testament.

VERS. 24.-- *The sons of Moses,* etc. Who were chiefly honoured by David according to Josephus, and had been set over the treasures, signifies the teachers from among the Jews, to whom the adornment of the Church, that is, the beauty [0661B] of the soul, is entrusted.

VERS. 25.-- *His brethren also, Eliezer.* He shows that the gifts of the princes are the treasures of the saints, for the things which they offered to the enemies, they gave into the furnishing of the temple. For whosoever, labouring well, acquires anything of virtue or of good work, let him ascribe the good to God, not to himself. Whence: *Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to thy name give glory [n: (Psal. CXIII)]*.

VERS. 29.-- *And over the Isaarites was Chonenias,* etc. These are they who in the Church humbly and obediently receive with devout mind the things which they hear from their masters, and they who cannot with the tongue, show the teaching of the virtues by doing.

VERS. 30.-- *To Israel beyond the Jordan westward,* etc. The Israelites who beyond the Jordan [0661C] received a possession signify those who, content with a middle state, do not seek the citadel of virtues by passing over the turbulence of this world, but yet do not neglect to exercise the commandments of God in work. But because no one does these things save by the gift of God, he is said to be set over them, Asabias, that is *the Lord doing,* so that they may say with the prophet: *All our works thou hast wrought in us [n: (Psal. XXVI)]*. [var: Vulg. Isa. 26:12 *omnia enim opera nostra operatus es nobis*, "for all our works thou hast wrought for us" — and the words are Isaias', not the psalm's]

VERS. 31.-- *And the prince of the Hebronites was Jeria, according to their families and kindreds.* In Hebrun David began to reign, which signifies those who, having lived well, make a beginning, and prepare a kingdom for David in his own life. Over the Hebronites Jerias, fearing the Lord, is prince, because *the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom [n: (Psal. CX)]*. But Hebron is interpreted a marriage: *for he that [0661D] feareth, advanceth that he may love, and cast fear out of doors [n: (I Joan. IV)]*.

## CHAPTER XXVII.

VERS. 24.-- *It was not put in the records.* [n: (RAB.)] Because, wrath supervening, the number was disturbed, nor were they distributed into the ministries according to it.

VERS. 25.-- *Over the treasures,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The treasure of Christ is knowledge, the wisdom of virtue, out of these he honours those who serve him. Over these is set Azmoth, who is interpreted strong in death, for the teaching of a man is discerned through patience; whence: *Better is the patient man than the valiant [n: (Prov. XVI)]*. Azmoth also the son of Adiel, who is interpreted my father God. For whatsoever of virtues the saints have, they ascribe to God.

[0662A] *And of these treasures.* For he is deservedly set over the treasures of the true Solomon in the cities, villages, and towers, who by faith and by work sets forth the dignity of the name of Jonathan and of his father Ozias. For Jonathan is the house of God, and Ozias the fortitude of the Lord. For he who by the gift of God receives the light of knowledge, and bravely keeps in work the rightness of faith, not undeservedly receives the care of the churches of God.

VERS. 26.-- *Ezri the son of Chelub.* Ezri, helped by the Lord; Semeias, hearing the Lord; Zabdias, fruit vehement or abounding. For he who hears the commandments of the Lord, helped by the aid of his mercy, gathers an abundant fruit of good work unto life eternal.

VERS. 28.-- *The oliveyards and the figyards, which were in the plains,* etc. In these are olives and figs, and they signify [0662B] the assemblies of the saints, who have the oil of mercy in the breast, and the sweetness of meekness in mortality. Which virtues are in the plains, which are in the open and at hand for all, and seek for their hearers not the accepting of persons, but the rightness of good will.

*Balanam the Gederite.* Balanam, who is interpreted having grace, is set over the oliveyards and the figyards. For out of the grace of God is given the abundance of good works. But Joas, who is interpreted hoping or the strength of the Lord, keeps the storehouses of oil, because no one comes to the reward save he who through strength of mind stretches by the confidence of hope toward the heavenly kingdom.

VERS. 29.-- *Saphat.* Who is interpreted *I have judged,* the son of Abdi, that is, of the father of grace, signifies the apostles [0662C] set over the other teachers by the choice of the Lord, to whom it is said: *You also shall sit upon twelve seats, judging the twelve tribes of Israel [n: (Matth. XIX)]*.

VERS. 30.-- *And over the camels Ubil the Ismaelite, and over the asses Jadias.* These animals are misshapen and unclean, and they signify the gentiles distorted by sin and unclean with lust, but coming into the kingdom of our David they have as masters Ubil and Jadias, who, despising philosophy and the empty deceit of the world, chose the simplicity of the Gospel, as humble and despised, whence: *If any man among you seem to be wise, let him become a fool [n: (I Cor. III)]*.

VERS. 32.-- *Jonathan.* [n: (RAB.)] When Jonathan was, etc., *as far as* whence: *Upon whom shall my spirit rest, but upon the humble and quiet, and him that trembleth at [0662D] my words?* [var: Vulg. Isa. 66:2 *ad quem autem respiciam, nisi ad pauperculum, et contritum spiritu, et trementem sermones meos?*, "but to whom shall I have respect, but to the poor little one, and the contrite of spirit, and him that trembleth at my words?"]

*Achitophel [n: (II Reg. XVI)]*. Who afterwards, depraved by covetousness, treated with Absalon of the slaying of David, and is interpreted my brother falling, or rushing in, or treating. This is Judas Scarioth, who among the apostles was familiar with the Lord, but afterwards, corrupted by avarice, by treating with the Jews of his death, earned the ruin of everlasting death, who also, according to the book of Kings, hanged himself with a halter. By Achitophel also the Jews can be signified, who by the prerogative of the Fathers were once most dear, and by the converse of God most renowned: but afterwards, making a counsel of death against Christ, were destroyed by the Romans in their own house, as Achitophel was, that is, in Jerusalem.

[0663A]VERS. 33.-- *Chusai.* That is, an Ethiopian and faithful to David, betokens in all things the gentiles, of whom it is said: *A people which I knew not hath served me, at the hearing of the ear it hath obeyed me [n: (Psal. XVII)]*.

VERS. 34.-- *After Achitophel was Joiada.* Judas prevaricating, the twelfth place did not remain empty: but there succeeded Matthias, knowing the grace of God, humble and faithful: who kept humbly the grace which he received by the gift of God. For Joiada is interpreted knowing or acknowledging; Banaias, the builder of the Lord. For him whom the grace of God chose unto an apostle, the acknowledging of sound faith made firm in that very dignity. Abiathar, who for a time is among the counsellors of David, but in the election turned aside from the Lord with Joab against Solomon, signifies those who, corrupted by covetousness, do not keep the grade of their dignity, [0663B] which they received from the Lord. For these, if they will not have remained in the prison of penance according to the fear of Achitophel, are to be punished for ever by the true Solomon.

*Joab.* That is, enemy, betokens those from whose person it is said: *I was directed unto all thy commandments, I have hated every way of iniquity [n: (Psal. CXVIII)]*, to whom it is said elsewhere: *Be ye perfect, as your heavenly Father is perfect [n: (Matth. V)]*. For Joab is interpreted the same father.
