## §B · THIS RANGE'S CRUCES

### B1 · 0634B · *Fulvida vestimenta ejus ex Rosor* — an unfixable base verse and an unattested name. NO MARKER.

*Appellatur et alia Bosor civitas Esau in montibus Idumeae, de qua dicitur: **Fulvida vestimenta ejus
ex Rosor*** — and then Migne's sentence simply stops, with **no terminal point** (Pattern 8: it stops in
the English too).

Two things are wrong here and neither earns a marker.

1. ***Rosor* is a name that occurs nowhere** — not in the Clementine, not in this work, and Migne's own
   sentence has printed ***Bosor*** twice in the twelve words before it. An initial *R* for *B* is the
   obvious reading. ⛔ **But an unfamiliar proper name is a real word for the purposes of the 2026-08-23
   addendum, and a real word is Migne's until a plate says otherwise.** Rendered as printed ("from
   Rosor"), logged here, **no `[sic:]`** — a `[sic:]` is a public claim that his plate is defective, and
   seven such claims were withdrawn in one batch for exactly this.
2. **The quotation matches no Clementine verse.** The nearest is Is 63:1–2 (*tinctis vestibus de Bosra …
   Quare ergo rubrum est indumentum tuum*), and *fulvida* answers the *fulvus* of Migne's own Edom
   etymology at 0633C. **`[var:]` declined**: a marker naming a verse I cannot establish would be a
   public claim resting on a guess (8965 §4's *Oraculum in abditis* precedent).

quotations: *exsiccans* · *auriga* · *ascendens* · *divisio* · *atrium* · *ortus* · *vinea mea* ·
*vigilans* · *sinistra* · *murmuratio* · *spica* · *vana vetustas* · *missus vel umbra ejus* ·
*excelsus* · *humilis* · *auxiliator* · *auxilium* · *qui rebellans* · *pulverem* · *sepes ejus* ·
*redemptus Domini* · *carceratus* · *frutex Domini* · *Saba Arabia* · *scientia* (this last a word lifted

liturgical-composition boundary of style rule L3, applied to the other genre that shadows scripture

⚑ **And the etymologies are where the names have to be right.** Three glosses in this range are built on
the *letters* of the name, and the English keeps them for that reason: *Phaleg* = division (0632C),
*Alian* = cast out against Genesis's *Aluham* = exalted (0633C–D), *Hamram* = reddened against Genesis's

gloss expounds** — which is what §C-a, §C-b and §C-c are about, and why they were worth a marker.

### B5 · 0632C · *Sem, Ar.* — an ABBREVIATION, reproduced, not expanded

Clem. 1 Par 1:24 reads *Sem, **Arphaxad**, Sale*. Migne prints *Ar.* with its own full stop inside the
italic span, which is the shape of an abbreviation rather than of a clipped lemma. **Reproduced exactly**
(Ruth §1.2's expansion licence covers a *clipped* lemma, not an abbreviated word), and the gloss below
turns on the count of generations, not on the name. Logged so a later reader can tell a decision from an
oversight.

### B6 · 0641D · *Sed cum violas,* etc. — a truncation, rendered as the phrase it truncates

Clem. 5:1 reads *sed cum **violasset** thorum patris sui*. *violas* is a real Latin word ("thou
violatest", or "violets") that cannot stand in a *cum*-clause of this shape, and Migne's own `etc.` marks
the span as clipped. Rendered "*But when he violated,* etc." — the verb the truncation stands for,
stopping where Migne stops. **No `[var:]`**: this is Migne's clip, not a divergent reading of the verse.
⚠ It is also a left-margin candidate (0641D, foot of column). Raised, not asserted.

### B9 · Places the Latin will not construe. RENDERED AS PRINTED, NO MARKER.

This corpus has no marker meaning "Migne prints a word that will not construe" (Ruth @0534A
*societate fieri*; 8965 §2b), and inventing one for a single site is worse than saying so plainly.
**Each of these is rendered as closely as the printed words allow, and nothing is emended.**

- **0631C · *qui ab Amano et Tauro omnia maritima usque ad oceanum **possidere** Britannicum.*** An
  infinitive where a finite verb is wanted; it can only be read as an accusative-and-infinitive still
  governed by *videbimus* twelve words back, with *qui* standing for its subject. Rendered "who from
  Amanus and Taurus possess all the seaboard as far as the British ocean" — **English has no exponent
  for a stranded infinitive in this position**, so the crux is where the difficulty is recorded.
- **0631D · *Sic homines a Dei timore revocabat, ut spem in propria virtute **poneret**.*** A singular
  verb where the sense wants the plural *homines*. **Rendered singular** ("that he might set his hope in
  his own strength"), the subject supplied as **Nemrod**, who is the subject of *revocabat* in the same
  sentence and of every finite verb around it. ⚑ The rival — that *poneret* is a loose singular for the
  men — is **rejected because nothing but the sense supports it**, and the sense is precisely what
  Pattern 7 forbids using to override the morphology.
- **0638A · *id est sanctarum animarum quibus **Christo**, quem Cyrus … significavit … annumeravit.***
  Two datives (*quibus*, *Christo*) with a verb that wants a nominative subject. Rendered with Christ as
  the subject of *annumeravit*, which is the only reading that yields a sentence; the dative *Christo* is
  left without an English exponent, because English marks no case on a name. Logged, not marked.
- **0640B · *Mahalon et Chelion, qui hic Securus et Incendens dicuntur, **fuisse** in Moab, quia …***
  An accusative-and-infinitive with **no governing verb anywhere in the paragraph**. The English gaps it
  in the same place ("That Mahalon and Chelion … were in Moab"), and no verb was supplied.
- **0643B · *quae in Aegyptum, fame cogente, a patre venalis ducta a Manasse: qui horreis praeerat …,
  in conjugium misericorditer suscepta est.*** An anacoluthon straddling Migne's colon. **The colon is
  preserved and the break is preserved**; nothing was joined up.

### B10 · Real words that read oddly, weighed and left standing

Per the addendum: a real, well-formed word is Migne's until a plate says otherwise, refuted five of five.
None of these fires a marker.

- **0633D · *rubricatus, sordidus vel **tumulentus***.** Jerome's onomasticon glosses *Hamran* as
  *rubricatus sive **temulentus*** ("drunken"), and *temulentus* is very likely the word. But
  *tumulentus* is a well-formed formation on *tumeo*, so it is **rendered on its own letters** as
  "swollen", and the conjecture stays here where a reader can weigh it. **No `[cj:]`**: Pattern 18 needs
  the faithful English to mislead, and "reddened, foul or swollen" of a man named "the red one" does not.
- **0639D · *eo quod ipsius **amica** esset, fuit enim filia Levi*.** *amica* ("friend") is a real word
  and the sentence's reason (*fuit enim filia Levi*) wants a kinship term. **`[cj:]` DELIBERATELY NOT
  FIRED, and the reason is that the two rivals cancel:** *amita* ("paternal aunt") is the obvious repair
  from the Jewish tradition, but the **Vulgate itself** reads *patruelem suam* at Ex 6:20 — a cousin, not
  an aunt. A `[cj:]` naming one of them would tell a reader the matter is settled when the plate's own
  comparand points the other way. Rendered "because she was his own friend".
- **0642C · *cantorum David qui organis musicis laudes **Domine** canebant*.** A vocative where the
  dative *Domino* belongs. **English has no exponent** for the difference in this position (Pattern 9's
  shape), so it is rendered "praises to the Lord" and logged, not marked.
- **0632B · *quae vario eventu subversa bellorum*** — *bellorum* separated from *eventu* across the
  participle. Word order, not a defect; rendered "overthrown by the varying issue of wars".
- **0634B** — Migne says Job's wife bore him ***septem** filios* and then names **six**: *Eliphaz, Armoe,
  Diasath, Philasia, Dipharceluth, Thelon*. **Both reproduced.** The count is his and the list is his,
  and the discrepancy is exactly the kind of thing this edition exists to leave visible.
- **0641B · *hi **quatuor** dicuntur*** under the lemma ***Viri quingenti*** ("five hundred men").
  Migne's, reproduced; the *quatuor* is his allegory of the four evangelists and does not answer the
  lemma's number.
- **0633A · *sed **usque in praesens**, quia procul a nobis sunt*** — an adverbial phrase with no verb of
  its own, rendered as printed ("but down to the present").
- Read clean and rejected as candidates, with reasons: *Syriae Cheles* (0631A, 0631D) for *Syria Coele*,
  a standard medieval form, printed twice identically · *questionibus* for *quaestionibus* (0637A) and
  *coelestis/coelis* throughout, orthography, Pattern 9 · *Requietatio* beside *requietio* (0636A), a
  live doublet in this very paragraph · *ononymos* (0634C), the Greek ὁμώνυμος, rendered "of the same
  name with it" · *Castium* (0632A) for Jerome's *Casluim*, an unfamiliar name and therefore a real word
  (brief) · *Hala filii Beor* (0634A) where Clem. 1:43 has *Bale filius Beor*, a name in Migne's prose
  and not a lemma · *Aluham* (0633C) where Gn 36:23 reads *Alvan* — likewise Migne's prose, and it is
  the very form his etymology expounds.

### B11 · 0633C · Pattern **8b** applied once, and it is the batch's only added mark

*Quaeritur, cum Thamna in Genesi legatur … quomodo inter filios numeretur, cum in sequentibus legatur
ipsa Thamna soror Lotam filii Seir, qui et Edom**?*** — Migne closes an **indirect** question with a
question mark, and English cannot end that construction with one. Recast into direct form behind a colon
per 8b: "**It is asked: since Thamna is read in Genesis … how is she numbered among the sons, when …?**"
⚑ **This is the one place in the batch where the English carries a mark the Latin band does not**: the
colon-count in band 0633B is 1 Latin against 2 English, and it is this and only this. Recorded because
test 3 otherwise returns zero mismatched bands, and a reader running that test must be able to tell a
ruled recast from a defect.

⚠ The other two *Quaeritur* sites in the range close on a **semicolon**, not a question mark (0637A
*cur haec Egla sola uxor David dicatur;* · 0637B *cur non in summa quadraginta anni … numerentur;*), so
8b does **not** apply to them and no colon was introduced. 0642B's *Unde quaeritur: quomodo … ?* is
already direct behind Migne's own colon and needed no recasting.

### B12 · 0639A–B · Migne's MASCULINE pronouns for the Church, reproduced

*Per eum quoque benedicuntur membra ejus, id est, Ecclesia … **Cum eo** est manus Domini, quia **in ipsa**
operatur Filius Dei … *Et portae inferi non praevalebunt adversus **eum**,* nec diabolus dominabitur
**ei***. Within four lines Migne refers to the Church by a feminine (*in ipsa*) and three masculines
(*eo*, *eum*, *ei*), and the Vulgate's own *adversus eam* is the masculine's comparand (§C-g).
**All four are rendered with the gender Migne prints** — "With him is the hand of the Lord, because in
her the Son of God works … against him … over him." ⚑ Rendering the masculines as "it" would have been
smoother and would have hidden the one thing worth seeing: **the gloss has slid from the Church back to
Christ mid-sentence and Migne never marks the turn.** Both construals are live and neither is adopted
silently; this is where the second one is recorded.

### B13 · 0641D · *praebuisse Israel auxilium Ituraeos, Naphaeos et Nodab* — who helped whom

Rendered "**that the Ituraeans, the Naphaeans and Nodab, the sons of Ismael, furnished help to Israel**",
taking *Israel* as the dative it most naturally is and the three names as the accusative subject of
*praebuisse*. ⚑ **The rival was weighed and lost**: 1 Par 5:19–20 has those peoples **fought against**
Israel, so a reader who knows the chapter will expect the opposite — but the reversal would require
reading *Israel* as the subject and *auxilium* against its own verb, and **a construal that exists only
because it agrees with the history is the thing this edition is for refusing.** Migne's sentence says
what it says. Recorded here so the difficulty is visible.

## §C · THE TEN `[var:]`, AND WHY EACH EARNED A PUBLIC CLAIM

Every one rests on `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt`, cited by book chapter:verse. None rests on
recollection, and **in none of them was Migne's wording replaced** — the marker stands *beside* the
faithful rendering, which is all Pattern 14 permits.

⚑ **The first four are the genealogy class the brief predicted, and they share a shape that makes them
more than orthography: in each, Migne's own gloss expounds a form his own lemma does not print.** That is
not a spelling variant; it is a lemma and its exposition disagreeing on the page.

**a · 0632C · 1 Par 1:19.** Migne's lemma ***Phaley***; Clem. ***Phaleg***. His next four words are
*Phaleg enim divisio* — **the etymology is built on the *g***, and *Phaley* explains nothing.

**b · 0633C · 1 Par 1:40.** Migne's lemma ***Aliam***; Clem. ***Alian***. Two lines below: *In
Paralipomenis vero **Alian**, id est ejectus* — **Migne cites the Clementine's form correctly against his
own lemma**, and the whole gloss (exalted in Genesis, cast out in Paralipomenon) turns on it.

**c · 0633D · 1 Par 1:41.** Migne's lemma ***Hamran***; Clem. ***Hamram***. His next four words: *In
Paralipomenis **Hamram**, id est rubricatus*. The same shape as (b), one verse later.

**d · 0635A · 1 Par 1:51.** Migne's lemma ***Dux Aliha***; Clem. ***dux Alva***, and Gn 36:40 likewise
*dux Alva*. Here the disagreement runs the **other** way: Migne's gloss *defends* his lemma
(*Alva in Genesi legitur … in Paralipomenis **Aliha***) against a Clementine that reads *Alva* in both
places. **Marking it is what lets a reader see that his Paralipomenon is not ours.**

**e · 0636A · 1 Par 2:52.** Migne ***Qui dividebat** dimidium requietionum*; Clem. *qui ***videbat***
dimidium requietionum*. ⭐ **This is the batch's most consequential divergence and the marker is what
makes the gloss legible**: the exposition beneath it uses *videre* four times and *dividere* not once
(*ita dimidium requietionum **vidisse*** · *id est historiam **viderunt*** · *Vel dimidium requiei
**videt*** · *Tunc … perfectionem requietionum **videbit***). Migne's lemma and Migne's gloss are
reading two different verbs.

**f · 0638D · Jo 1:29.** Migne *Ecce agnus Dei qui tollit ***peccata*** mundi*; Clem. *Ecce agnus Dei,
**ecce qui** tollit ***peccatum*** mundi*. Plural for singular. The liturgical form has displaced the
Vulgate's, and **number is the class 8965 fired on twice** (*videritis*, *Tangebant*). The dropped
*ecce* rides in §D as a clip.

**g · 0639A · Mt 16:18.** Migne *portae inferi non praevalebunt adversus ***eum****; Clem. *adversus
***eam***, of the Church. **English has an exponent for this and the divergence is a change of
referent**, so the marker is needed to keep "against him" from reading as our slip. See B12.

**h · 0639B · 2 Rg 3:39.** Migne *Ego **sum tener** et unctus rex*; Clem. *Ego **autem adhuc delicatus**,
et unctus rex*. ⭐ **The 7a″ trap in its purest form:** the gloss exists to explain the name *Recha* as
*tener*, quotes this verse to prove it, and says four words later *quasi vermiculus ligni **tener***.
**The Vulgate's word is *delicatus*, and the argument's keyword is not in the Vulgate at all.**

**i · 0639C · 1 Cor 8:1.** Migne *quos **scientia* non *inflat, sed charitas aedificat***; Clem.
*Scientia inflat, caritas **vero** aedificat* — **with no negative anywhere in the verse.** Migne (or his
glossator) has negated an apostolic sentence to bend it into a relative clause praising humble teachers.
⛔ **The negative is rendered, on its printed host *inflat*, exactly as the plate has it**, and the
marker records that the Vulgate does not carry it. Stated, not explained: Pattern 14 forbids speculating
about **why** two witnesses differ. (The *charitas*/*caritas* orthography and the *sed*/*vero* particle
are §D and are not what fired the marker.)

**j · 0641A · Jo 10:9.** Migne *Per me si quis introierit, **ingredietur** et egredietur, et pascua
inveniet*; Clem. *Per me si quis introierit, ***salvabitur : et*** ingredietur*. A whole clause dropped
from the **middle** of the verse, and "he shall be saved" is not a small one to lose.

⚑ **And the boundary, because it decided one of these and refused another:** an omission from the middle
of a quotation fires where the dropped words are a **predication of their own** (Jo 10:9's *salvabitur*)
and does not fire where they are a **descriptive phrase inside a clause that survives intact**
(Ps 77:9's *intendentes et mittentes arcum*, §D). Both were weighed; the second lost because nothing in
either the verse's assertion or the gloss's argument changes without it.

## §E · THE TESTS, RUN, AND WHAT THEY RETURNED

**Test 1 (a `[sic:]` must not swallow the word it marks).** **Vacuously clean: zero `[sic:]`, zero
`[d:]`, zero `[ed:]` and zero `[cj:]` in this range.** Run anyway on the ten `[var:]`, which are the only
markers standing inside a sentence: each was deleted and the host sentence read back, and all ten leave a
complete clause with its subject, verb and **object** intact (the fourth question — *does the clause still
have the thing it acts on?* — asked and answered). All five files were then stripped of every marker
mechanically and swept for a stranded article, preposition or copula sitting on punctuation: **no hits**
beyond the *id est* → "that is," formula and Migne's own clipped lemma *Habitaverunt in,*.

**Test 2 + 2a (name the host of every printed negative).** All 33 printed negation tokens in the five
Latin chunks were extracted mechanically, each matched to the single Latin word it attaches to, and then
found on that word in the English. **No defect found.** The trigger population 2a names — a negative on a
verb with a quantifier, correlative or predicate adjacent — was walked one by one:

- **0631D *ut **non Deo** suam felicitatem, sed propriae virtuti ascriberent*** — the host is *Deo*, not
  *ascriberent*, and the `sed propriae virtuti` correlative proves it: "to ascribe their happiness **not
  to God, but to their own strength**."
- **0635D *quod Sesan **non habuerit filios**, sed filias*** — the host is *filios*, proved by *sed
  filias*: "had **not sons, but daughters**."
- **0640B *in Hebraeo **non principes**, sed mariti dicuntur*** — host *principes*, proved by *sed*.
- **0639C *quos scientia **non inflat**, sed charitas aedificat*** — host *inflat*, proved by the verb
  pair the *sed* pivots on; this is the §C-i site and the negative is Migne's own addition to the verse.
- **0642A ***non quod** eum transtulerit in Assyrios, **sed quod** Teglathphalnasar … cepit*** — host is
  the whole *quod* clause, proved by the answering *sed quod*.
- **0640D *quia Simeon **non** [0640D] habuit **tantam** sobolem **quantam** Judas*** — ⭐ the sharpest
  2a case in the batch: a negative standing before a verb with a *tantam … quantam* correlative one word
  away. **The host is the quantity, not the verb** — the claim is not that Simeon had no offspring but
  that he had not *so great* an offspring — and the English scopes it that way ("Simeon had not so great
  an offspring as Juda"), proved by the correlative and not by what reads well.

"the tenth [0630D] from Adam" · *parit [0639A] Ecclesia* → "the most holy [0639A] Church" · ***data**

*Ecclesiae [0641D] populum* → "the Church's [0641D] people" · *Simeon non [0640D] habuit* → "Simeon had
not [0640D] so great".

**Test 3 (punctuation by column band, never by total).** Colons, semicolons, question marks, exclamation
marks and guillemets were compared **band by band** across all 54 anchor-partitioned bands in the five
chunks. **Sixteen mismatches were found and repaired, and they ran overwhelmingly one way: fourteen were
semicolons or colons this translator had SUPPLIED**, at 0631C, 0632A (×2), 0632D, 0633B (×2), 0634B,
0634D (×2), 0635A, 0637D, 0639D, 0640B and 0643C (×2). **Two were marks of Migne's that had been
DELETED** and are now restored: the semicolon at 0633A (*decem generationes posuit**;** ita a Sem*) and
the colon at 0640D (*unde in Josue dicitur**:** Simeon per cognationes suas accepisse*), where the
English had run the clause on. **Final state: zero mismatched bands except the single ruled Pattern 8b
colon at 0633B (§B11).** Question marks: 2 Latin, 2 English, in the same two bands. Guillemets: zero on
both sides. Em-dashes in the English: **zero**.

⚠ **No comma→semicolon sweep was performed and none was contemplated.** Migne prints 42 semicolons of his
own in these five chunks, so this book is nowhere near Tobit's zero-semicolon exception, and the traffic
ran the other way: all but two of the repairs **removed** one of ours.

**Test 4 (does it ASSERT what he asserts?).** Every paragraph was read back against the Latin with the
counts already known clean, asking only that question. It is what produced §B9 (five places rendered
without a governing verb rather than given one), §B12 (the masculine pronouns for the Church left
standing), §B13 (the Ituraeans helping Israel against what the chapter elsewhere says), §B10's *septem
filios* against six names and *hi quatuor* under *Viri quingenti*, and §C-e — the *dividebat*/*videbat*
divergence, which no count-based check could see, because **nothing is missing from the lemma; it simply
reads a different verb from the four *videre*s that expound it.**

⚑ Two habits, both applied: a sentence that reads smoothly where the Latin is rough was treated as a
suspect (which is how 0638A's stacked datives and 0640B's governorless infinitive were caught), and the
four abridgment tails were **parsed as strictly as ordinary prose** — the formula removes words, it does
not suspend grammar.
