## §M · THE MERGE — three stints, two things reconciled, one boundary ruled

Three translators, none able to see the others when it mattered. The merge is a check, not a
concatenation, and it was made **against the English files themselves rather than the three
vocabulary tables** — a table records what a translator believed it did.

⭐ **The second and third stints read their predecessors' cruces off disk and inherited from them
deliberately**, which is how this is supposed to work: *doctores* → teachers (15 occurrences, zero
"doctors"), *interpretatur* → "is interpreted", *charitas*/*dilectio* held apart, *verus pacificus*
→ "the true peaceable one" (which recurs at 0654D and gave the seam a real cross-check), proper
names form for form, *as far as* for the abridgment formula. **Three extensions were declared
rather than smuggled**: *septenarius* → "the septenary number", *signat* → **betokens** (a fourth
verb kept off *significat*), *gentilitas* → "the gentile world". And 8965's substantival
number-names, imported at the head of the run, **finally bite in the last stint** — the senary, the
quaternary, the octonary.

### ⛔ The one drift that was real: the divergence marker had two forms

The opening stint wrote **`[var: Vulg. *Alian*]`** — the Clementine's word and nothing else. The
later two wrote **`[var: Vulg. 1 Par. 9:11 *Mosollam* …]`**, with book, chapter and verse. Ten
markers against twenty-four.

⚑ **The third stint noticed and deliberately did NOT harmonize it, flagging it for the merge
instead. That was the right call and is worth stating as practice:** a translator that silently
conforms to a peer's format leaves no record that the two ever differed, and the next reader cannot
tell an inherited convention from a coincidence.

**Ruled to the house form, and all ten normalized here**, each reference resolved against
`sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt` rather than assumed: Phaleg → 1 Par. 1:19 · Alian → 1:40 ·
Hamram → 1:41 · dux Alva → 1:51 · videbat → 2:52 · peccatum mundi → Jo. 1:29 · adversus eam →
Mt. 16:18 · adhuc delicatus → 2 Rg. 3:39 · Scientia inflat → 1 Cor. 8:1 · salvabitur → Jo. 10:9.
**All 34 markers in the work now carry an explicit reference.** The reason the bare form will not
do is the reason `[var:]` exists at all: the marker is a public claim about another text, and a
claim a reader cannot look up is not checkable.

### ⚖ THE BOUNDARY OF THE MIRROR RULE — ruled here, and reversible if Wilson disagrees

The third stint capitalized two words the plate sets lower-case — *faciente **domino*** (0661C,
in the etymology of *Asabias*) and *In **regum*** (0657D) — and **flagged the departure rather than
hiding it**, asking whether the mirror should be absolute.

**Ruled: it should not, and the plate itself is the argument.** In the very chunk containing
*In regum ita*, Migne also prints ***In Regum ita*** — both casings of the same phrase, a few lines
apart. **His case is not carrying a distinction there, so mirroring it would transcribe an accident
as though it were a signal.**

So the rule has a stated scope: **mirror the plate where the case is SEMANTIC IN ENGLISH** —
*Ecclesia*/*ecclesia* is the founding case, and it qualifies because "the Church" and "the church"
are two different claims a reader can tell apart. **Do not mirror where English has no lower-case
option for the referent** — the divine name, a book title. Rendering *faciente domino* as "the lord
doing" would not be fidelity; it would be a misspelling wearing fidelity's clothes.

⚑ *Ecclesia* itself was mirrored word for word throughout all three stints, and the lower-case
sites were verified at the source, not taken on trust — including 0011's *ecclesiarum* → "the
churches of God".

## §T · WHAT THE FOURTH TEST FOUND, AND WHY IT IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS HERE

Every containment check passed on this book from the start. **Three conformations were caught only
by reading the finished English back against the Latin asking whether it asserts what he asserts** —
and all three were the familiar English Bible arriving over the plate:

**Absalom → Absalon · Judas Iscariot → Judas Scarioth · In Hebron → In Hebrun.**

⛔ **Nothing mechanical sees these.** The name is present, spelled like a name, in the right place,
and it reads better than the truth. They are the exact defect this edition exists to catch, and a
genealogy is where they are easiest and least visible — which is why the first stint was warned
about them and the third still found three.

The same test produced the reading repairs no count would show: *fidei* taken as an accusative
object (0656B), *sibi* as "to him" (0657D), and a Douay-supplied object in Osee 6:5.

## §V · THE DIVERGENCES THAT CARRY AN ARGUMENT

Thirty-four is a high count, and the reason is that this book **reasons from its lemmata**. Four are
worth naming because in each the divergence is not incidental to the gloss but load-bearing:

- ⭐⭐ **0657A — an arithmetic argument with a wrong operand.** The gloss quotes 2 Reg. 24:9 at
  **80,000 and 50,000** where the Clementine reads **800,000 and 500,000**, and then at 0657C
  reasons *from the difference between the two books' numbers*. The marker is what lets a reader see
  that the argument is being run on figures the received text does not have.
- ⭐⭐ **0636A — *qui **dividebat** dimidium requietionum*** for the Clementine's *videbat*
  (1 Par. 2:52). **The gloss underneath uses *videre* four times and *dividere* not once.**
- ⭐ **0639C — Migne negates an apostolic sentence.** He prints *scientia **non** inflat*; 1 Cor. 8:1
  has no negative at all. The negative was rendered **on its printed host** and the divergence
  marked — never silently repaired, which is exactly 7a.
- ⭐ **0659C–D — the gloss does textual criticism on its own lemma** and lands on *ad torrendum*,
  which is the Clementine's reading. *torrentem* and *torrendum* are held apart as "the parching"
  and "the roasting"; collapsing them would have erased the argument being made.

## §B · THIS RANGE'S CRUCES

### B1 · 0634B · *Fulvida vestimenta ejus ex Rosor* — an unfixable base verse and an unattested name. NO MARKER.

*Appellatur et alia Bosor civitas Esau in montibus Idumeae, de qua dicitur: **Fulvida vestimenta ejus
ex Rosor*** — and then Migne's sentence simply stops, with **no terminal point** (Pattern 8: it stops in
the English too).

Two things are wrong here and neither earns a marker.

1. ***Rosor* is a name that occurs nowhere** — not in the Clementine, not in this work, and Migne's own
   sentence has printed ***Bosor*** twice in the twelve words before it. An initial *R* for *B* is the
   obvious reading. ⛔ **But an unfamiliar proper name is a real word for the purposes of the 2026-08-23
   addendum, and a real word is Migne's until a plate says otherwise.** Rendered as printed ("from
   Rosor"), logged here, **no `[sic:]`** — a `[sic:]` is a public claim that his plate is defective, and
   seven such claims were withdrawn in one batch for exactly this.
2. **The quotation matches no Clementine verse.** The nearest is Is 63:1–2 (*tinctis vestibus de Bosra …
   Quare ergo rubrum est indumentum tuum*), and *fulvida* answers the *fulvus* of Migne's own Edom
   etymology at 0633C. **`[var:]` declined**: a marker naming a verse I cannot establish would be a
   public claim resting on a guess (8965 §4's *Oraculum in abditis* precedent).

### B3 · 0642C · *Joel, qui et **Vas seni*** — a split run in a NAME. Carried as printed, no marker.

the Hebrew, which is not our business). Migne prints it **as two words**, in prose, and this is exactly

who is also Vas seni" — because a name's letters are what the reader needs, and joining them would be our

### B9 · Places the Latin will not construe. RENDERED AS PRINTED, NO MARKER.

This corpus has no marker meaning "Migne prints a word that will not construe" (Ruth @0534A
*societate fieri*; 8965 §2b), and inventing one for a single site is worse than saying so plainly.
**Each of these is rendered as closely as the printed words allow, and nothing is emended.**

- **0631C · *qui ab Amano et Tauro omnia maritima usque ad oceanum **possidere** Britannicum.*** An
  infinitive where a finite verb is wanted; it can only be read as an accusative-and-infinitive still
  governed by *videbimus* twelve words back, with *qui* standing for its subject. Rendered "who from
  Amanus and Taurus possess all the seaboard as far as the British ocean" — **English has no exponent
  for a stranded infinitive in this position**, so the crux is where the difficulty is recorded.
- **0631D · *Sic homines a Dei timore revocabat, ut spem in propria virtute **poneret**.*** A singular
  verb where the sense wants the plural *homines*. **Rendered singular** ("that he might set his hope in
  his own strength"), the subject supplied as **Nemrod**, who is the subject of *revocabat* in the same
  sentence and of every finite verb around it. ⚑ The rival — that *poneret* is a loose singular for the
  men — is **rejected because nothing but the sense supports it**, and the sense is precisely what
  Pattern 7 forbids using to override the morphology.
- **0638A · *id est sanctarum animarum quibus **Christo**, quem Cyrus … significavit … annumeravit.***
  Two datives (*quibus*, *Christo*) with a verb that wants a nominative subject. Rendered with Christ as
  the subject of *annumeravit*, which is the only reading that yields a sentence; the dative *Christo* is
  left without an English exponent, because English marks no case on a name. Logged, not marked.
- **0640B · *Mahalon et Chelion, qui hic Securus et Incendens dicuntur, **fuisse** in Moab, quia …***
  An accusative-and-infinitive with **no governing verb anywhere in the paragraph**. The English gaps it
  in the same place ("That Mahalon and Chelion … were in Moab"), and no verb was supplied.
- **0643B · *quae in Aegyptum, fame cogente, a patre venalis ducta a Manasse: qui horreis praeerat …,
  in conjugium misericorditer suscepta est.*** An anacoluthon straddling Migne's colon. **The colon is
  preserved and the break is preserved**; nothing was joined up.

### B10 · Real words that read oddly, weighed and left standing

Per the addendum: a real, well-formed word is Migne's until a plate says otherwise, refuted five of five.
None of these fires a marker.

- **0633D · *rubricatus, sordidus vel **tumulentus***.** Jerome's onomasticon glosses *Hamran* as

  *tumulentus* is a well-formed formation on *tumeo*, so it is **rendered on its own letters** as
  "swollen", and the conjecture stays here where a reader can weigh it. **No `[cj:]`**: Pattern 18 needs
  the faithful English to mislead, and "reddened, foul or swollen" of a man named "the red one" does not.
- **0639D · *eo quod ipsius **amica** esset, fuit enim filia Levi*.** *amica* ("friend") is a real word
  and the sentence's reason (*fuit enim filia Levi*) wants a kinship term. **`[cj:]` DELIBERATELY NOT
  FIRED, and the reason is that the two rivals cancel:** *amita* ("paternal aunt") is the obvious repair
  from the Jewish tradition, but the **Vulgate itself** reads *patruelem suam* at Ex 6:20 — a cousin, not

  comparand points the other way. Rendered "because she was his own friend".
- **0642C · *cantorum David qui organis musicis laudes **Domine** canebant*.** A vocative where the
  dative *Domino* belongs. **English has no exponent** for the difference in this position (Pattern 9's

- **0632B · *quae vario eventu subversa bellorum*** — *bellorum* separated from *eventu* across the

- **0634B** — Migne says Job's wife bore him ***septem** filios* and then names **six**: *Eliphaz, Armoe,

- **0641B · *hi **quatuor** dicuntur*** under the lemma ***Viri quingenti*** ("five hundred men").
  Migne's, reproduced; the *quatuor* is his allegory of the four evangelists and does not answer the

- **0633A · *sed **usque in praesens**, quia procul a nobis sunt*** — an adverbial phrase with no verb of

- Read clean and rejected as candidates, with reasons: *Syriae Cheles* (0631A, 0631D) for *Syria Coele*,

### B11 · 0633C · Pattern **8b** applied once, and it is the batch's only added mark

### B12 · 0639A–B · Migne's MASCULINE pronouns for the Church, reproduced

## §C · THE TEN `[var:]`, AND WHY EACH EARNED A PUBLIC CLAIM

Every one rests on `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt`, cited by book chapter:verse. None rests on
recollection, and **in none of them was Migne's wording replaced** — the marker stands *beside* the
faithful rendering, which is all Pattern 14 permits.

⚑ **The first four are the genealogy class the brief predicted, and they share a shape that makes them
more than orthography: in each, Migne's own gloss expounds a form his own lemma does not print.** That is
not a spelling variant; it is a lemma and its exposition disagreeing on the page.

**a · 0632C · 1 Par 1:19.** Migne's lemma ***Phaley***; Clem. ***Phaleg***. His next four words are
*Phaleg enim divisio* — **the etymology is built on the *g***, and *Phaley* explains nothing.

**b · 0633C · 1 Par 1:40.** Migne's lemma ***Aliam***; Clem. ***Alian***. Two lines below: *In
Paralipomenis vero **Alian**, id est ejectus* — **Migne cites the Clementine's form correctly against his
own lemma**, and the whole gloss (exalted in Genesis, cast out in Paralipomenon) turns on it.

**c · 0633D · 1 Par 1:41.** Migne's lemma ***Hamran***; Clem. ***Hamram***. His next four words: *In
Paralipomenis **Hamram**, id est rubricatus*. The same shape as (b), one verse later.

**d · 0635A · 1 Par 1:51.** Migne's lemma ***Dux Aliha***; Clem. ***dux Alva***, and Gn 36:40 likewise
*dux Alva*. Here the disagreement runs the **other** way: Migne's gloss *defends* his lemma
(*Alva in Genesi legitur … in Paralipomenis **Aliha***) against a Clementine that reads *Alva* in both
places. **Marking it is what lets a reader see that his Paralipomenon is not ours.**

**e · 0636A · 1 Par 2:52.** Migne ***Qui dividebat** dimidium requietionum*; Clem. *qui ***videbat***
dimidium requietionum*. ⭐ **This is the batch's most consequential divergence and the marker is what
makes the gloss legible**: the exposition beneath it uses *videre* four times and *dividere* not once
(*ita dimidium requietionum **vidisse*** · *id est historiam **viderunt*** · *Vel dimidium requiei
**videt*** · *Tunc … perfectionem requietionum **videbit***). Migne's lemma and Migne's gloss are
reading two different verbs.

**f · 0638D · Jo 1:29.** Migne *Ecce agnus Dei qui tollit ***peccata*** mundi*; Clem. *Ecce agnus Dei,
**ecce qui** tollit ***peccatum*** mundi*. Plural for singular. The liturgical form has displaced the
Vulgate's, and **number is the class 8965 fired on twice** (*videritis*, *Tangebant*). The dropped
*ecce* rides in §D as a clip.

**g · 0639A · Mt 16:18.** Migne *portae inferi non praevalebunt adversus ***eum****; Clem. *adversus
***eam***, of the Church. **English has an exponent for this and the divergence is a change of
referent**, so the marker is needed to keep "against him" from reading as our slip. See B12.

**h · 0639B · 2 Rg 3:39.** Migne *Ego **sum tener** et unctus rex*; Clem. *Ego **autem adhuc delicatus**,
et unctus rex*. ⭐ **The 7a″ trap in its purest form:** the gloss exists to explain the name *Recha* as
*tener*, quotes this verse to prove it, and says four words later *quasi vermiculus ligni **tener***.
**The Vulgate's word is *delicatus*, and the argument's keyword is not in the Vulgate at all.**

**i · 0639C · 1 Cor 8:1.** Migne *quos **scientia* non *inflat, sed charitas aedificat***; Clem.
*Scientia inflat, caritas **vero** aedificat* — **with no negative anywhere in the verse.** Migne (or his
glossator) has negated an apostolic sentence to bend it into a relative clause praising humble teachers.
⛔ **The negative is rendered, on its printed host *inflat*, exactly as the plate has it**, and the
marker records that the Vulgate does not carry it. Stated, not explained: Pattern 14 forbids speculating

## §2 · THE ELEVEN `[var:]`, AND WHY EACH EARNED A PUBLIC CLAIM

Every one rests on `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt`, cited by book chapter:verse. None
rests on recollection, and in none of them was Migne's wording replaced — the marker stands
**beside** the faithful rendering.

**a · 0644A · 1 Par 9:11.** Migne *Helciae filii **Mosoliam***; Clem. *filius Helciae, filii
**Mosollam***. A proper-name form. Marked because this range is a name-list book and a name is
a fact, not orthography — the launch brief asks for exactly this class, and the Latin column
prints the form beside it so a reader can see which witness has which.

**b · 0644C · 1 Par 9:28.** Migne *super vasa **minister***; Clem. *super vasa **ministerii***.
Not a spelling: Migne's reading puts a **singular predicate noun** against the printed plural
*erant*, and the English is deliberately left to show it ("there were also a minister over the
vessels"). ⚑ *minister* is a real, well-formed Latin word, so under the addendum-A rule it is
**Migne's** and takes no `[sic:]`; the divergence is the whole of what is asserted.

**c · 0645A · 1 Par 9:35.** Migne ***Jahiel***; Clem. ***Jehiel***. **The gloss is built on the
letters** — *Jahiel, qui in Regum, Ahiel … Jahiel disertus Dei* — so a form conformed to the
Vulgate would make the exposition explain a word that is not on the page. This is 7a″ in its
name-list form.

**d · 0646D–0647A · 1 Par 10:12.** Migne *Quercum ***quaerat*** in Jabes*; Clem. *subter
quercum, ***quae erat*** in Jabes*. Rendered as printed ("Let him seek the oak in Jabes"), which
is a jussive in the middle of a narrative lemma. ⚑ **The obvious conjecture is that *quae erat*
has been fused into *quaerat*** — the Pattern 10 fused direction, which no mechanical test sees.
**No `[sic:]` was fired**, because the fused direction is exactly the class our digitization has
owned 19 times out of 19 in the sibling book, and a `[sic:]` would blame Migne's plate for a
defect that is probably ours. The `[var:]` asserts only what the Clementine reads, which is
checkable. **Plate candidate — see §7.**

**e · 0647B · Ps 2:2.** Migne, under his own note *(Psal. II)*, prints ***Principes populi
convenerunt in unum cum Deo Abraham***. Clem. Ps 2:2 reads *principes convenerunt in unum
**adversus Dominum***. The marker gives Ps 2:2, the verse Migne cites. ⚑ Recorded and **not**
argued in the marker: the closing words are those of **Ps 46:10** (*Principes populorum
congregati sunt cum Deo Abraham*), so the printed line is a weave of two psalms. Pattern 14
forbids speculating why two witnesses differ, so the marker states the reading Migne's own
citation points at and the crux carries the rest. **It matters to the gloss**: the sentence is
adduced to show prophets and apostles running together **to Christ**, which *cum Deo Abraham*
gives and *adversus Dominum* destroys.

**f · 0647D · 1 Cor 3:9.** Migne *Dei adjutores sumus, **Dei agri***; Clem. *Dei enim sumus
adjutores : **Dei agricultura estis***. A different predicate — "God's fields" for "God's
husbandry" — in a sentence adduced for the apostles' work of cultivation.

**g · 0649A · Gal 2:9.** Migne ***Petrus,** Jacobus et Joannes … dextras **mihi dederunt***;
Clem. ***Jacobus, et Cephas, et** Joannes … dextras **dederunt mihi***. **A different name and a
different order of names** in a proof-text whose whole function here is to identify who the
pillars were. ⚑ Migne's note reads *(Gal. XI.)* — Galatians has six chapters. Notes pass through
verbatim (sacred-marker rule) and the reference was **not** corrected; recorded here so the
indexer's unparsed bucket has an explanation waiting.

**h · 0649C · Ps 17:41.** Migne *Inimicos meos dedisti mihi ***deorsum***; Clem. *dedisti mihi
***dorsum***. One letter, two different assertions — "downward" against "their back". Both are
real words, so this is Pattern 14 (the divergence sits inside a quoted scripture) and **not**
Pattern 18, per 18a's explicit rule about where the divergence sits.

**i · 0649D · 1 Par 12:27.** Migne ***Joiada***; Clem. ***Jojoda***. Name form, same reasoning
as **a**. The gloss identifies him with Phinees, so the reader needs to see which spelling the
identification was made on.

**j · 0653A · 1 Par 15:27.** Migne *David erat **in Ephod***; Clem. *David autem etiam
**indutus erat ephod lineo***. The marker is fired for the **dropped *lineo***, not for the
construction: the gloss immediately before it expounds a fibre *de terra ortum* coming
*ad candorem vestis* — **the linen is the word the exposition needs**, and it is the word
Migne's lemma does not print. Same shape as 8965 §3d.

**k · 0654A · Mt 18:19.** Migne ***Amen, amen, dico vobis** quia si duo ex vobis consenserint*;
Clem. ***Iterum dico vobis**, quia si duo ex vobis consenserint*. ⭐ **The gloss is built on the
divergence**: the whole paragraph is about the gentile saying *Amen amen, id est fiat, fiat*, and
Migne's lemma is adduced *secundum illud* — a proof-text chosen for an opening that the received
text does not have. Also *ex omni re* for Clem. *de omni re*, not separately marked.

**l · 0654B · 4 Rg 4:27.** Migne *Dimitte **eam**, quia ***animus*** ejus in amaritudine est*;
Clem. *Dimitte **illam** : ***anima*** enim ejus in amaritudine est*. Fired for *animus* against
*anima*: the gloss's subject is that **the prophet's mind** is not always irradiated
(*prophetarum mentes*), and *animus* is the word that meets it.

## §3 · THIS RANGE'S CRUCES — decisions, and the reasons a later reader cannot reconstruct

### 3a · 0645B · *Abinadab … Abinadab … Abinadab* — three times, two etymologies. RENDERED AS PRINTED, NO MARKER.

> *Abinadab vocatur et interpretatur [0645B] Abinadab **pater meus votum,** Abinadab **populus
> meus votum.***

Two different etymologies are attached to one printed name. The standard Hieronymian pair is
***Abinadab*** = *pater meus spontaneus/votum* and ***Aminadab*** = *populus meus
spontaneus/votum*, so the second occurrence is very likely *Aminadab*.
⛔ **Not marked, and not emended.** *Abinadab* is a real, well-formed proper name in its own
grammatical slot; addendum A refutes the corpus-frequency argument for real words 5 times out
of 5, and Pattern 12 reserves `[sic:]` for type that cannot render in place. It renders in
place. The conjecture lives here, where the rule says it lives.
⚑ **Not dittography either.** Pattern 11 wants a doubled *run*; this is a three-term list in
which the second and third terms take different predicates, so marking either copy would
quarantine a term the sentence needs. 11a's test (which copy is the intrusion?) has no answer
here, because neither copy duplicates a neighbouring phrase.

### 3b · 0647C · *constituisse **Jacob*** where the sentence is about Joab. RENDERED "Jacob", NO MARKER.

Josephus's report: *Super opus autem murorum constituisse **Jacob**, expulisse Jebusaeos et
Jerosolymam vocasse.* The man set over the walls is **Joab**, named twice in the same paragraph
(*Joab autem reliqua urbis exstruxit* opens it, *Joab, qui reliqua urbis exstruxit* closes it).
⛔ **A familiar English Bible spelling must not arrive over the plate, and neither may a
familiar name from four lines away.** *Jacob* is a real, well-formed name occupying its slot;
it is Migne's until a plate says otherwise. Rendered "Jacob", logged, no marker. **Plate
candidate — §7.**
⚑ It was weighed against Pattern 18 and **declined**: 18's bar is a sentence that says something
*the author is arguing against*, and a wrong name in a report of Josephus is a wrong fact, not a
reversed argument. 18a is explicit that mood/person/number anomalies that merely fail to
construe stay declined, and a name substitution is no stronger.

### 3c · 0648A · *Verumtamen usque ad tres primos.* — the clip was NOT completed with a negative.

Migne's lemma stops where 11:21 continues *non pervenerat*. Ruth's convention 3 renders a
fragment as the phrase it stands for, and the gloss's own next sentence supplies the negative
(*Sed ad tres primos **non** pervenit*), so "Nevertheless he had **not** attained to the three
first" was available and reads better.
⛔ **Declined, and the reason is 7a: no negation appears in the English that the Latin does not
print.** Ruth's worked case (*Non te.* → "Let no man see thee") expands a fragment **whose
negative is itself printed**; here the negative is precisely the word Migne stops before.
Rendered *"Nevertheless unto the three first."* — a readable clip, with the negative arriving
one sentence later exactly where Migne prints it. **The rival reading is named here so that a
blind reader meets it**, and it lost on the rule, not on taste.

### 3d · 0649A · *quando inundare consuevit super,* — the clip stops on a preposition.

12:15 continues *super ripas suas*. Rendered *"when it is wont to overflow above,"* — the
comma is Migne's and is carried, and "its banks" was **not** supplied. Same reasoning as 3c:
convention 3 licenses rendering a fragment as its phrase, it does not license importing the
Vulgate's next two words into an italic span that stops before them. The Latin twin keeps the
clip findable.

### 3e · 0648C · the column anchor falls INSIDE an etymology, and the English splits there too.

Migne prints *qui **aedificator** [0648C] **Dominus** interpretatur*. English word order would
put "the Lord the builder", which moves the anchor off its word. Rendered ***builder* [0648C]
*Lord*** — an apposition that reads as one, with the anchor standing beside the same two words
it stands between in the Latin. Splitting mid-construction to match Migne is the 8995 precedent
and is deliberate, not an oversight.
⚑ **One other anchor is not word-adjacent in the same way and is recorded:** 0652B, where Migne
has *Haec Ecclesiam [0652B] significat* and the English reads "This signifies the Church [0652B],
which…". The anchor sits against *Ecclesiam*/"the Church" in both; English cannot put it before
"signifies" without inverting the clause. All 46 anchors in this range were checked **against the
word they stand beside**, not merely for presence and order (test 2b).

### 3f · 0650C · *Cariathiarim* and *Cariatharim* — both printed forms kept.

Migne spells the city two ways in one paragraph, and glosses each separately. Neither was
normalized to the other or to the Clementine's *Cariathiarim*, because the second gloss
(*quae alibi Cariathabaal*) is doing different work from the first.
⚑ Recorded and **not** marked: the same paragraph makes Cariathiarim *civitas Damasci … quae in
Amos Emathabal*, which is a confusion of Cariathiarim with Emath (Amos 6:2). It is Migne's, it
is renderable, and it is rendered.

### 3k · 0646C · *Videntes indocti … ignorantes …* — two construals, and which one the English took.

> *Videntes indocti verae humilitatis medicamina lavacrumque baptismi, ignorantes in Judaea
> magistros suos certamine spirituali victos…*

Either participle can govern the accusatives. **Taken:** *ignorantes* governs the medicines and
the laver, *videntes* governs the defeat of the masters — "The unlearned, ignorant of the
medicines of true humility and of the laver of baptism, seeing in Judaea their own masters
overcome…". **Rejected:** *videntes* governs the medicines and *ignorantes* the defeat, which
would make them unaware of a ruin that the same sentence then says troubled them
(*majorum ruina turbati*). ⚑ Both construe; the second is self-defeating, and that is the whole
of the reason. Named because a blind reader will stop here.

## §4 · DIVERGENCES CHECKED AND DELIBERATELY LEFT UNMARKED

Recorded because Pattern 14 fires on divergence that **changes the sense**, not on every
difference, and because a decline that is not written down is indistinguishable from a lemma
nobody read.

- **A dropped or added particle, with nothing turning on it:** Ps 75:2–3 with an added *et*
  (0647C) · 2 Rg 6:1 without *rursum* (0650D) · Gal 6:14 without *autem* (0650C) · Lv 19:2
  *quoniam* for *quia* (0651C) · 1 Cor 3:17 without *enim* (0654C) · Phlp 2:9 without *illi*
  (0655A) · Jo 3:16 without *suum* (0653C) · Mt 18:19 *ex omni re* for *de omni re* (0654A,
  where the marker is fired for the opening instead).
- **Word order only:** 2 Tim 4:5 *Fac opus* for *opus fac* (0651C) · Jr 2:18 *Quid nunc tibi
  vis* for *Et nunc quid tibi vis* (0650B).
- **A preposition or orthography:** Lc 10:19 *super serpentes* for *supra serpentes* (0647B) ·
  Ps 150:5 *bene sonantibus* split for *benesonantibus* (0652B) · the *coelum*/*caelum* and
  *quae*/*quæ* classes throughout.
- **Number, with nothing built on it:** Mt 21:43 *fructum ejus* for *fructus ejus* (0646D) ·
  1 Par 13:9 *Cum autem pervenissent* for *pervenisset* (0650C). ⚑ Both were weighed against
  8965 §3f, where a number divergence **was** marked because the gloss expounded the plural.
  Neither gloss here touches the number: 0646D abridges into prose about the nations, and
  0650C is abridged away by *usque ad* in the next four words.
- **A dropped title in a vocative:** 2 Rg 7:27 without *Domine exercituum* (0655A). Weighed

## §8 · THE TWO `[cj:]`, AND WHY EACH CLEARS PATTERN 18'S BAR

Both open on a word Migne **did** print, in italics, so the verbatim-in-the-Latin-twin guard is
satisfied and no emendation of our own invention can be smuggled in.

**a · 0653A · *lignum* → `for wood [cj: *lignum*; read *linum*, "flax"] sprung from the earth`.**

> *lignum namque de terra ortum multiplici labore ad candorem vestis perveniens, veritatem
> humanae carnis inter flagella triumphantis ostendit, et hoc est: David erat in Ephod.*

*lignum* is a perfectly good Latin word, so Pattern 7 renders it and Pattern 12 has nothing to
wrap — the founding shape of Pattern 18. **The English then asserts that wood, sprung from the
earth, is brought by manifold labour to the whiteness of a garment**, which is false and is not
what the passage argues. Everything in the sentence is flax: *de terra ortum* (the plant),
*multiplici labore* (retting, hackling, spinning, bleaching), *ad candorem vestis* (the white
linen), and the lemma it explains is **the ephod**, which 15:27 makes *stola byssina* and
*ephod lineo*. The commonplace behind it is Bede's, on the linen of the ephod.
⚑ **The rival was weighed:** leave it unglossed and put the conjecture in the crux only. That is
precisely the 11081 @0030B failure Pattern 18 was written to close — *munde* for *mundo*, a real
word, a crux nobody opens, and a page asserting the wrong thing. It lost on that precedent.
⚠ If the plate reads *linum*, the defect is our digitization's and the `[cj:]` should become a
silent correction plus a crux line. **The marker is honest either way** — it claims only that the
word printed in our Latin column should be read *linum* — but the plate read in §7 would settle
which.

**b · 0654D · *aedificat* → `A man of blood [0654D] builds [cj: *aedificat*; read *non aedificat*, "does not build"] the temple of God.`**

> *Templum Domini aedificat, qui corrigendis proximorum mentibus vacat. … **Vir sanguinum
> templum Dei aedificat.** Qui enim adhuc carnalibus incubat, necesse est ut instruere proximorum
> mentes erubescat.*

The paragraph's whole argument is that a man must be clean in himself before he corrects others
(*Debet enim in se mundus esse, qui aliena corrigere vult*), that a muddy hand cannot wipe away
filth, and that David is refused the temple **because** he is a man of blood
(*Non tu aedificabis mihi templum, quia vir sanguinum es*). The sentence as printed reverses
that, and **the very next sentence explains why it must be a denial** — *for he who still lies
among carnal things must needs blush to instruct the minds of his neighbours*. 7a's own tell:
the next clause denies what the English just asserted, and the contradicting neighbour is on the
same plate.
⚑ **This is 18a's admitted class.** The bar as 18a restates it is whether the English asserts
something false, and the ✅ list there includes a **dropped word** glossed on the word Migne did
print (7561 @0648, *solum* for *non solum*). A lost negation is named in 18a as the defect class
the whole 7a⁗ apparatus exists for, and it has nowhere else to live where there is no `[sic:]`.
⚑ **The rivals were weighed and are named, because naming a rival is not adjudicating between
them.** (i) *Vir sanguinum* as a rhetorical question — declined, Migne prints a full stop and
Pattern 8 forbids supplying a mark. (ii) *aedificat* read as a concessive ("even a man of blood
builds") — declined, there is no concessive particle and the next sentence's *enim* explains a
denial, not a concession. (iii) Leave it, log it — declined on 18a's ruling that incoherence is
not a defence where the word that went wrong is a negation.

## §2 · THE TWELVE `[var:]`, AND WHY EACH EARNED A PUBLIC CLAIM

**The gate applied throughout, stated so it can be checked:** Pattern 14's own test — a divergence
*"in a way that changes the sense."* A **word substituted, added or dropped inside the italic span
such that the span asserts something else** fires. A pure clip, a dropped or added particle
(*autem*, *quoque*, *et*, *At*, *Propterea*), a word-order change, a case adapted to the gloss's
own syntax, a proper-name spelling, and an orthographic variant (*caritas*/*charitas*,
*coelo*/*caelo*, i/j) **do not fire** and are all in §4. The witness prefix is `Vulg.`, matching
what 8965's shipped chunks print, so the two books read alike where they will stand side by side.
**Eleven are argued a–k below; the twelfth, on 2 Reg. 24:9 at 0657A, is argued at §3a because the
reasoning about it is inseparable from the gloss's arithmetic.**

### a. 0656A — Prov. 17:20, a tense and an added possessive
Migne: *Qui vertit linguam **suam**, **incidit** in malum.* Clem. Prov. 17:20: *qui vertit linguam
**incidet** in malum.* Future → present, and *suam* added. The gloss is about Christ extinguishing
folly **now**, in his examining, so the tense is doing work.

### b. 0656C — Ps. 33:17, the Lord dropped out of the quoted span
Migne prints, inside the italics, *vultus **est** super facientes mala*, with *cujus* standing
outside them. Clem. Ps. 33:17: ***Vultus autem Domini** super facientes mala.* The subject of the
verse — *Domini* — is not in the span at all; an *est* stands where it was, and the span has been
re-hinged on a relative antecedent supplied by the gloss. A reader collating the span against the
psalm meets a different sentence.

### c. 0656B — Phil. 2:13, *nobis* for *vobis*
Migne: *qui operatur in **nobis** velle et perficere.* Clem.: *qui operatur in **vobis** et velle,
et perficere.* First person for second. The gloss applies it to *us*, the faithful who are the
crown, so the divergence is load-bearing and not a slip a reader should have to guess at.

### d. 0658B — Deut. 10:12, *O Israel* for *Et nunc Israel*, and *ut* for *et*
Migne: *O Israel quid Dominus Deus tuus petit a te? nisi ut timeas … **ut** ambules in viis ejus.*
Clem.: *Et nunc Israel, quid Dominus Deus tuus petit a te, nisi ut timeas … **et** ambules.* The
vocative replaces the temporal frame, and the second clause is subordinated to the first instead
of coordinate with it. ⚑ Migne's own `?` mid-quotation is preserved exactly (Pattern 8); it falls
on a direct question and needs no 8b recasting.

### e. 0659C–D — 1 Par. 23:29, and it is the most interesting lemma in the range
Migne: *Ad ferventem similam, et ad torrentem.* Clem. 23:29: *et ad **similae sacrificium** …
et ad **torrendum**.* Two substitutions in one short lemma. ⭐ **The gloss then does textual
criticism on its own lemma**: some codices have *Ad ferventem similam*, others *ad ferventem* only,
*sed in Hebraeo habetur, ad torrendum* — and *ad torrendum* is exactly what the Clementine prints.
So Migne's page preserves the variant **and** the correction.
⛔ **The rendering is where this could have been lost.** *torrentem* (participle) and *torrendum*
(gerund) are one root, and English has no clean exponent for the difference; rendering both "for
the roasting" would have erased the very distinction the gloss is arguing. They are held apart as
**"for the parching"** (*ad torrentem*, the lemma) and **"for the roasting"** (*ad torrendum*, the
Hebrew reading), and *ad ferventem similam* / *ad ferventem* as **"for the seething fine flour"** /
**"for the seething."** The four are distinct English phrases because they are four distinct Latin
readings.

### f. 0660C — Act. 13:2, and **Paulum for Saulum**
Migne: *orantibus apostolis et jejunantibus, ait Spiritus sanctus: Segregate mihi et **Paulum** et
Barnabam.* Clem. Act. 13:2: *Ministrantibus autem illis Domino, et jejunantibus, dixit illis
Spiritus Sanctus : Segregate mihi **Saulum** et Barnabam.* Four divergences, of which the name is
decisive: the Spirit separates **Saul**, who is not yet Paul. *orantibus apostolis* for
*ministrantibus illis Domino* is the second material one — Migne's span names the apostles and
makes the act prayer rather than ministry, which is precisely what the gloss needs (*a primis
praedicatoribus ad praedicandum ordinati sunt*).

### g. 0660C — 1 Cor. 9:19, *omnes* for *plures*
Migne: *ut **omnes** lucrifacerem.* Clem.: *ut **plures** lucrifacerem.* "All" for "the more" —
and the gloss's whole point is the universality of the condescension (*Omnium servum me feci*), so
the substitution is the one that makes the proof-text prove it.

### h. 0660D — Matth. 24:12, a perfect turned future
Migne: ***abundabit** iniquitas, et refrigescet charitas multorum.* Clem. Matth. 24:12: *Et quoniam
**abundavit** iniquitas, refrigescet caritas multorum.* The gloss is expounding the western gate as
*finis mundi … persecutio Antichristi*, and Migne's future puts the whole verse in the time to
come where the Clementine has a perfect protasis. (*charitas* for *caritas* is orthography and is
in §4.)

### i. 0661C — Isa. 26:12, *in nobis* for *nobis*
Migne: *Omnia opera nostra operatus es **in** nobis.* Clem. Isa. 26:12: *omnia enim opera nostra
operatus es nobis.* "Wrought **in** us" for "wrought **for** us" — and the gloss has just said
*nemo haec agit nisi dono Dei*, so the indwelling reading is the one it wants. ⚑ Migne's note
reads *(Psal. XXVI)*; the words are Isaias'. Notes ride verbatim, so the marker names the true
book and §6a carries the citation.

### j. 0662C–D — Isa. 66:2, an Old-Latin-shaped quotation
Migne: *Super quem requiescet Spiritus meus, nisi super humilem et quietum et trementem sermones
meos?* Clem. Isa. 66:2: *ad quem autem **respiciam**, nisi ad **pauperculum, et contritum
spiritu**, et trementem sermones meos?* Three substitutions: the Spirit resting for the Lord
looking, *humilem* for *pauperculum*, *quietum* for *contritum spiritu*. Only the last four words
are common ground. **This one has no `[n:]` at all** — it is the resumption tail of an *usque ad* —
so without the marker a reader has nothing to collate against.

### k. 0664D — Job 21:13, *qui sibi videntur* added
Migne: ***Qui sibi videntur** in bonis **ducere** dies suos, et in puncto ad inferna descendunt.*
Clem. Job 21:13: *Ducunt in bonis dies suos, et in puncto ad inferna descendunt.* Job's flat
indicative becomes a self-deception. Standing as it does at the very end of the book, beside
Ps. 54:24 on the bloody and deceitful, the added clause changes who is being described.

## §3 · THIS RANGE'S CRUCES — the numbers, the names, and the words that will not construe

### ⭐ a. 0656A — *In Regum libro ita* gives **80,000 and 50,000** where Kings gives 800,000 and 500,000, in a gloss that is doing arithmetic between the two books
Migne, at 1 Par. 21:5: *In Regum libro ita. Quod inventa sunt de Israel **octoginta millia**
virorum fortium et de Juda **quinquaginta millia** pugnatorum.* Clem. 2 Reg. 24:9: ***octingenta
millia** virorum fortium … **quingenta millia** pugnatorum.* Both of Migne's are real, well-formed
numerals, so the non-word rule does not touch them; both are an order of magnitude short of the
text they are explicitly quoting (*In Regum libro ita*). **This earned a `[var:]`** — not because
the numerals look wrong, but because the passage presents itself as the text of Kings, and because
the gloss at 0657C then reasons *from the difference between the two books' numbers* (*tot millia
interfecta … quot millibus numerus in Paralipomenon scriptus, numerum qui in Regum est excedit*).
An arithmetic argument with a wrong operand is exactly the class the launch brief calls the most
valuable kind, and a reader who cannot see the operand cannot see the argument.

### ⭐ b. 0656A — *non viginti et octo millia*, and 0656A *decem et octo* with no *millia*
Three numerals in one short gloss, all real words, all rendered as printed and none marked:
1. *In quibusdam autem codicibus invenitur David **non viginti et octo millia** in valle Salinarum
   occidisse, quod vitio scriptorum factum est.* The lemma four lines above is *Decem.*, clipping
   18:12's *decem et octo millia*, and the gloss is about which number the codices carry.
   **The `non` is placed on the numeral, not on the infinitive** — it stands immediately before
   *viginti et octo millia* and the whole sentence is a dispute about the quantity, so the English
   reads "slew not twenty and eight thousand". (Test 2a's trigger fired here: a negative with a
   quantifier adjacent to a plausible verb. The structure, not the readability, decided it.)
2. *Joab vero duodecim millia prostravit, sicut in titulo **quinquagesimi septimi** psalmi
   scribitur.* The twelve thousand in a psalm title is **Ps. 59:2** (*in valle Salinarum duodecim
   millia*), not Ps. 57. Rendered "the fifty-seventh psalm" as printed.
   ⛔ **A `[cj:]` was weighed here and DECLINED, and the rival is named because naming a rival is
   not adjudicating between them.** The rival was `[cj: read *quinquagesimi noni*]`. It lost
   because, unlike the 288 at 0660B, **nothing inside the printed sentence contradicts the printed
   ordinal** — the datum the gloss actually reasons from (*duodecim millia*) is reported
   correctly, and only the pointer is off. A `[cj:]` is for a real word whose faithful English
   makes the author assert what he did not; here he asserts the twelve thousand, and he is right.
3. *Hic autem **decem et octo** Abisai interfecisse perhibetur* — the numeral stands with no
   *millia*. Rendered "eighteen", bare, as printed. It is the same phrase as the lemma *Decem.*
   with its noun elided, which in an abridged recension is unremarkable and is not a defect.

### ⭐ c. 0660B — *ducenta octoginta* against *quater septuaginta duo*: the one `[cj:]`
*quater enim septuaginta duo, **ducenta octoginta** faciunt.* Four times seventy-two is 288, and
the lemma's own verse (1 Par. 25:7) reads *cuncti doctores, **ducenti octoginta octo***. Migne's
numeral is a real, well-formed form, so Pattern 12 has nothing to wrap and Pattern 7 renders it —
**and the English then asserts an arithmetic the author did not** (the very case Pattern 18 was
made for, 11081 @0030B). So: *ducenta octoginta* keeps its English, "two hundred and eighty",
and `[cj: read *ducenta octoginta octo*]` stands **beside** it, additive. The rival treatment,
rendering "two hundred and eighty-eight" and logging a crux, is the silent repair Pattern 7a
forbids and was never available.
⚠ Whether the lost *octo* is Migne's compositor or our digitization **is not asserted**: a plate
read would settle it, and the `[cj:]` claims only that the sum requires it.

### d. 0658D — *Anima justi sedes est sapientiae* is cited *Sap. VII* and is nowhere in the Clementine
Searched as a phrase and by its parts across all 73 books: neither *Anima justi* nor *sedes est
sapientiae* occurs. It is a medieval adage that travels under Wisdom's name. **No `[var:]` was
fired**, and the reason is exact: Pattern 14 sets our reading against a *received reading*, and
here there is none to name. The crux is the right home, and this is it.

### e. 0657C–D — *in **arca** Areuna vel Ornam*, where the lemma prints *in **area** Ornam*
The lemma is 21:18's *in **area** Ornam Jebusaei* — the threshing-floor. The gloss twice writes
*arca*: *altare jubetur in **arca** Areuna vel Ornam construere*, and then *nisi in **arca** cordis
… constituat illi altare humilitatis*. **Both rendered "ark", as printed, and no marker fired.**
*arca* is a real, well-formed word, and the addendum-A rule is that a real word that reads oddly is
Migne's until a plate says otherwise — refuted five times out of five when a corpus argument was
pressed against one. It is also not obviously an error: the second *arca cordis* is a settled
figure, and the gloss may be trading the floor for the ark deliberately, which is why "floor" was
not quietly restored in either place. A plate read of col. 0657C–D would settle it and would be
worth having.

### f. 0661C, 0657D — *faciente **domino*** and *In **regum** ita*: lowercase where Migne elsewhere capitalizes
Migne prints a lowercase *d* in the Asabias etymology and a lowercase *r* in *In regum ita* at
0657D, against *Domino* and *In Regum libro* everywhere else in the range. **Both are capitalized
in the English** — "the Lord doing", "In Kings thus" — and this is a deliberate departure from the
mirroring rule, stated so it is checkable. 8965 §M's mirroring rule was made for
*Ecclesia*/*ecclesia*, where the case carries a **semantic** distinction (the Church against a
church). *dominus* and *regum* carry none: lowercasing them in English produces a page that reads
as our typo and tells a reader nothing about the plate, which the facing Latin column already shows
him. The two sites are named here so that a later sweep can reverse the decision if Wilson wants
the mirror to be absolute.

### g. 0662A — *Jonathas enim **domum** Dei*: an accusative in a predicate slot
*Jonathas enim domum Dei, et Ozias fortitudo Domini.* The second half prints the nominative
*fortitudo*; the first prints the accusative *domum* where the same construction wants *domus*.
**English has no exponent for the case of a predicate noun** — "Jonathan is the house of God" is
the only English there is — so this falls under Pattern 9 and takes no marker and no emendation.
Logged, and marked so a later sweep does not churn it.

### h. 0663B — *Interpretatur enim Joab idem pater*
Real words, and it will not construe: *idem pater* stands as the interpretation of Joab, whose
etymology the same paragraph has just given as *inimicus*. Rendered literally, "For Joab is
interpreted the same father", and no conjecture is offered in the text. A plausible one
(*Idithun pater*, or *idem* for *Idida*) would be guesswork on a page where nothing else is broken.

### i. 0662D — *cupiditate **de pravatus***: the range's only split run
*Qui postea cupiditate **de pravatus** cum Absalon de nece David tractavit.* A word split across a
space — the **non-word class**, which the addendum records as **our digitization's error 19 times
out of 19 in the sibling book** and every time in the batch that measured it. **Rendered in plain
English, "depraved by covetousness", and NO marker fired**, exactly as 8965 handled its seven split
runs: a `[sic:]` here would be a public claim that Migne's compositor split the word, and seven
false `[sic:]` were withdrawn from a previous batch for making precisely that claim. Raised as a
plate candidate in §7, never patched.

### j. 0661B — *quae enim hostibus offerebant, in supellectilem templi donabant*
"For the things which they offered **to** the enemies, they gave into the furnishing of the
temple." The dative *hostibus* with *offerebant* gives a sentence in which spoils are offered to
the enemy and then given to the temple; the argument wants the reverse direction (*donaria
principum thesauros esse sanctorum*). Rendered as printed. **No marker**: *hostibus* is a real
word in a grammatical slot, and translating *offerebant* as "took from" would be Pattern 7a's
mechanism 1, the opposite word chosen to rescue a sentence.

## §8 · ONE PLACE THE PRINTED CASE WOULD NOT CARRY THE CONSTRUCTION — 0660A, and the rival is named

*ostendens **Ecclesiae** sacerdotalem et regiam dignitatem plene habere et legis, et Evangelii
doctrinam perfecte servare.* The two infinitives want an accusative subject and Migne prints the
**dative or genitive** *Ecclesiae*. Two readings:

- **dative** — "showing **to the Church** that [she] fully has…", with the accusative subject
  elided;
- **genitive with *dignitatem*** — "showing the priestly and royal dignity **of the Church** to be
  fully had…", which requires a passive the Latin does not print.

**The dative reading was taken**, and the English reads "showing that the Church fully has the
priestly and the royal dignity and perfectly keeps the teaching both of the law and of the Gospel."
The genitive lost because it can only be got into English by turning two active infinitives passive,
which is a larger intervention than supplying the elided accusative subject that a dative reading
already implies — and because **both readings assert the same thing**, so nothing a reader needs is
at stake. No marker: the words are all real, all in place, and the difficulty is a construction, for
which this apparatus has no marker and should not invent one (the Ruth 0534A precedent).

   incoherent where the Latin is not** — the shape the fourth test exists to catch. Repaired with an
   em-dash and "he who", the least invasive way to rebind it.
3. **0643B — an ablative agent moved off its verb, so a sentence about a marriage stopped naming who
   married her.** *a patre venalis ducta **a Manasse**: qui horreis praeerat cibariis **patri** datis,
   in conjugium … **suscepta est*** — *ducta* already has its agent in *a patre*, so *a Manasse*
   governs *suscepta est*. The English gave *ducta* two agents and left *suscepta est* agentless.
   Second defect in the same sentence: *cibariis **patri** datis* is the girl's father, rendered "his
   father", pointing the reader at Manasse's. Both repaired.
4. **0634B — an adjective rebound to make an impossible agreement.** *septem filios et tres filias
   **praedictas** uxor genuit*: *praedictas* is accusative feminine plural and **cannot** modify
   *uxor*, a nominative singular. The English moved it to the noun that made the sentence read
   smoothly ("the aforesaid wife") and dropped it from the noun that carries it. Repaired to "the
   aforesaid three daughters". Low severity in sense, but a repair with no marker is the class that
   leaves no trace.

### Migne's own defects — checked, and all carried literally

Reported by the reader as *carried literally*, correctly refusing to call them undocumented, since it
could not see this file. All are argued above. Among them: the **intrusive** *non* at 0656A (where
carrying it preserves a self-contradiction that is Jerome's point about scribal corruption); the
**dropped** negation at 0654D, carried affirmative **with** its `[cj:]`; the 4×72 arithmetic at 0660B;
*arca* for *area* at 0657C–D, where the gloss's own re-use (*in arca cordis*) shows the reading is
consistent in Migne rather than a one-off slip; and the 80,000/50,000 gloss at 0657A, carried with
its `[var:]` and with the direction of *excedit* preserved at 0657C.

### ⬜ TWO THINGS LEFT FOR WILSON, deliberately not acted on

- **The lemma-expansion practice is inconsistent inside this work, and the inconsistency is real even
  though none of the three expansions is a defect.** *Achar qui.* → "Achar who troubled Israel",
  *Her pater.* → "Her the father of Lecha", *Iniit autem.* → "And he took counsel" are all
  **fragments**, which convention 3 licenses. But *Mesraim autem genuit.* (0632A) is the identical
  shape — transitive verb, object elided, Vulgate continuation available — and stands as "And Mesraim
  begot." So do *Filii autem.*, *Jectan autem.*, *Sem, Ar.* ⚑ The standing ruling is to **expand the
  stump rather than contract its twin**, which would mean expanding these; that is a change to a
  shipping work's lemmata and is Wilson's call, not mine. ⚠ Note the cardinals here are all correct
  and bare (*Viginti* → "Twenty", *Septuaginta millia virorum* → "Seventy thousand men") — this work
  did **not** repeat the sister book's numeral confusion.
- **0646C — a "reads better than its Latin" site the reader flagged and declined to assert.**
  *Videntes indocti … medicamina lavacrumque baptismi, **ignorantes** in Judaea magistros suos …
  victos*: the English swaps the two participles' objects. The reader's reason for not calling it a
  defect is good — the alternative parse is the only one compatible with *majorum ruina turbati* six
  words later, since men "not knowing" cannot be "troubled by the ruin" — while **Migne's comma
  slightly favours the other binding.** Genuinely undecided; left as printed.
