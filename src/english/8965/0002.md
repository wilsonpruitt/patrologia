## CHAPTER III. (cont.)

VERS. 17.-- *The same pillars also.* Therefore were two pillars made, and so disposed, that they may teach that the entrance of the heavenly kingdom is, in prosperous things and in adverse, to be [0670C] held before our eyes. But it is to be noted that the porch of the temple is also called the vestibule, and that what we read, between the vestibule and the altar, ought to be understood, between the porch and the altar. The second pillar signifies the preachers who were sent to the Gentiles to preach. Or the right pillar signifies those who prophesied that the Lord would come in the flesh. But the second, those who bear witness that he has already come and has redeemed the world by his own blood. Both pillars are reckoned under a like name, when the one is called firmness and the other in strength, so that one fortitude, of faith and of work, may be shown to be in all teachers, and the teachers of our own time may be silently marked, who wish to be seen and to be called pillars of the house of God, since in themselves, of a firm faith for despising the pomps of the world, for desiring the invisible good things, they [0670D] have nothing of strength for correcting, nor of diligence for understanding, the errors of those over whom they are set.
