## CHAPTER IV. (cont.)

VERS. 19.-- *The golden altar.* The hearts of the perfect, shining with the light of inward charity and of chastity, whose loftiness the place of the altar also signifies. For it stood [0674D] before the door of the holy of holies, as we read in the making of the tabernacle, in which not the blood of victims nor libations, but only incense was burned, the upper part whereof, ascending, covered the ark, and filled the oracle with the odour of sweetness. In which are figured the saints, who, while, the desires of temporal things being neglected, they seek with their whole intent the things of heaven, are as it were set within beside the oracle: nor far removed from the veil by which the temple and the holy of holies are parted; because in body only do they dwell on earth, but in their whole mind they are in heaven. And from this altar the smoke of the incense ascends within the holy of holies where the ark is laid up; that the prayers of the saints, kindled by the flame of charity, may come to heaven, [0675A] where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God. For on this altar not the blood of victims but only incense is burned, because such men have not works of flesh and of blood which they might slay to the Lord upon the altar of the heart, but they offer the vows of tears and of prayers for the desire of the kingdom of heaven.

VERS. 21.-- *And the tongs,* etc. The tongs for the use of the altar were made of brass. But these also, by which the lamps were cleansed, signify the interpretation of the spiritual sense through the offices of the teachers. For carrying over the very words of the sacred utterance from the history into allegory, from the shadow into the truth, they multiply as it were the light of knowledge in the Church, being set upon the mystic candlestick.

VERS. 22.-- *And he graved the doors.* These are the teachers and [0675B] the priests, who by instructing, by baptizing, by communicating the mysteries of the body and blood of the Lord, open to us the first thresholds of the Church.

*And so all was completed.* When the true peaceable one on the last day glorifies all the elect with the immortality of the resurrection. But as long as the state of this world is carried on, Solomon does indeed the work of the house of the Lord, but does not complete it: because the Lord inspires and helps the hearts of the elect that they may work good things, yet to none does he grant to be without sin, which he reserves for the faithful in the time to come. But he completes the whole work of his temple and renders it fit for the dedication, when he leads the elect, carried over out of this life, to the everlasting kingdom: whence in seven years it is built, and in the eighth, being finished, it is dedicated. For in seven days all time is rolled round, and the eighth is [0675C] the day of judgment and of resurrection, to which agrees that which follows.

## CHAPTER V.

VERS. 1.-- *Solomon therefore brought in all the things,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] This is done when Christ, etc., *as far as* but many mansions in her for the receiving of all whom, fearing and loving him, *the Lord blesseth, the little with the greater.* [var: Vulg. Psal. 113:21 *Benedixit omnibus qui timent Dominum, pusillis cum majoribus*, "He hath blessed all that fear the Lord"]

VERS. 9.-- *And of the bars.* [n: (ID.)] It is to be noted that, etc., *as far as* by so much the less do they see the inward joys.

VERS. 10.-- *And there was nothing else.* He shows generally that in the floor [cj: *area*; read *arca*, "the ark"] there was nothing contrary to the law of the Lord comprised in the two tables; but that those things only were laid up there which according to the commandments of the law were typically made for the worship [0675D] of the one God and for the commending of our faith, that the shadow of things to come might in all things afford testimony to the truth to come, and, when that appeared in the world, might receive its end. And there was in the ark, according to Paul, a golden urn holding manna, because in the man Christ dwells all the fulness of the Godhead bodily. There was also the virgin [cj: *virgo*; read *virga*, "rod"] of Aaron, which, having been cut down, had flowered anew: because all power of judging is with him, whose judgment in the lowliness of the passion seemed to have been taken away. There was also the tables of the testament, because in him are all the treasures of wisdom and of knowledge hidden. There clung to it also the bars by which it was carried, because the teachers who once laboured in the word of Christ now rejoice together in the present vision of the glory [0676A] of Christ. Whence: *I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ [n: (Phil. I)]* [var: Vulg. Phil. 1:23 *desiderium habens dissolvi*, "having a desire to be dissolved"].

VERS. 12.-- *Clothed in fine linen.* By the stoles of fine linen is designated the white chastity of bodily cleanness: with this are clothed all who stand by at the service of God, and who sound out his praises with mouth, with manners, and with act. For the cymbals signify the confession of the mouth, the psalteries mortality, the harps the mortification of the flesh, and the strenuousness of good action, being furnished with twenty-four strings, that is, with prophetic and apostolic doctrines.

*The priests.* Or rather the teachers, expressed by the hundred and the twenty, because the lawgiver completed with this number the sum of his years, and on the day of Pentecost the believers received in the same number the grace of the Paraclete Spirit.

[0676B]VERS. 13.-- *Therefore when all.* [n: (RAB.)] All the priests and Levites, etc., *as far as* they lost the ministry of the true priesthood.

VERS. 14.-- *For he had filled.* Because the mysteries which lay hid under the veil were opened through the coming of Christ to them that believed and worthily received. But to the rest, unwilling to come to the faith, the former blindness remained. Whence: *To you it is given to know the mystery of the kingdom of God,* etc. [n: (Matth. XIII.)]

## CHAPTER VI.

VERS. 1.-- *In the darkness.* Whence Job: *A cloud is his hiding place [n: (Job. XXII)]*. And the Psalmist: *Darkness under his feet: and he made darkness his hiding place [n: (Psal. XVII)]*. The hiding place of the Lord is Holy Scripture: which under [0676C] parables and figures bears the heavenly sacraments. Whence: *I will confess to thee, O Lord, father of heaven and earth, for that thou hast hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and hast revealed them to little ones [n: (Matth. XI)]* [var: Vulg. Matth. 11:25 *Confiteor tibi, Pater, Domine caeli et terrae, quia abscondisti*, present "I confess", and "Father, Lord of heaven and earth"].

## CHAPTER VII.

VERS. 5.-- *He dedicated.* [n: (RAB.)] Christ cleansed it with his own blood, and sanctified it with spiritual grace, and multiplied it with the diverse gifts of the virtues. And the children of Israel dedicate it, because every faithful man, according to the gift bestowed on him by God, turns and advances his neighbours by word and by example to better and more perfect things.

VERS. 7.-- *He sanctified.* Because Christ perfects in the Church those things which in the law could not be perfected because of its weakness. For she is the court of the house [0676D] of the Lord, through whom the entrance lies open into the heavenly Jerusalem: because therefore holocausts and sacrifices could not be offered on the altar of the Old Testament; because all things were done there figuratively, our king and priest raised up a new altar of faith in the Church, upon which they daily offer fat holocausts and sacrifices acceptable to God. Whence *The sacrifice of praise shall glorify me, and there is the way,* etc.

VERS. 8.-- *He made therefore.* [n: (RAB.)] I do not think that this is to be understood otherwise, etc., *as far as* in the rest of souls to come, in the perceiving of the truth itself in reality.

VERS. 10.-- *Therefore on the day,* etc. That is, after the eighth day of the solemnity of the scenopegia: as is contained in Kings. And it signifies that after the end of that life, and after [0677A] the sabbath-rest of holy souls, wherein before the judgment they rest; in the eighth age, that is, on the day of the resurrection, the saints shall receive immortal bodies, and are to be placed in the heavenly mansions according to the qualities of their merits, where they may ever rejoice, praising God in all the good things which the Father has conferred in Christ upon the Christian people.
