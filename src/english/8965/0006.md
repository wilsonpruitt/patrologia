## CHAPTER NINE.

[0678B]VERS. 1.-- *The queen also. [n: (RAB. in Paral., tom. III.)]* The queen of the south, who was stirred up from the uttermost ends and came to hear the wisdom of Solomon, prefigured the Church of the gentiles that was to come to Christ: who, clothed about with variety in a gilded vesture, forgetting her people and the house of her father, was of a barbarian nation, not in mind, because she desired to become a fellow-citizen of the saints, whence: *The queen of the south shall rise up unto judgment with this generation [n: (Matth. XII)]*.

VERS. 3.-- *Which after she had seen,* etc. These things agree with the Church, who, when the miracles of Christ had been heard, was stirred up to seek him. But after she came to him through baptism and faith, having considered the testimonies of Scripture, she acknowledged the presence of the Godhead, and what she had heard of him before seemed little to her in [0678C] comparison with the grace received. Whence also, marvelling, she breaks forth into his praise, saying: *Blessed are thy men,* etc.

VERS. 12.-- *Of the woods.* Christ sets precious woods, that is, holy teachers, whose greenness of faith never withers, nor do the leaves of their words fall away, but they render the fruit of good works in their seasons: he sets them for the fortification of the house of the Lord and of the royal house, that is, for the strengthening of the Church, and that, fenced about on every side by their doctrines and examples, she may not feel ruin. These are said to have been fashioned as harps and lyres for praising the Lord, because all things whatsoever they shall do shall prosper, and they proclaim the praise of the Lord in prosperity and in adversity with heart and mouth and work, and do all things unto the glory of God, and all the day and the night they cease not to praise the name of the Lord.

[0678D]VERS. 13.-- *Six hundred.* The senary multiplied by the senary makes sixty. The centenary also, drawn out six times, yields a hundred and six. The denary signifies the perfection of the commandments, the centenary the recompense of eternal blessedness. For the king desires our perfection, that he may have those to whom he may render a perfect wage, whence: *Be ye perfect as also your heavenly Father is perfect. If thou wilt enter into life, keep the commandments [n: (Matth. V, XIX)]*. And after a little. *If thou wilt be perfect, go and sell all things that thou hast,* etc., and *every one that shall leave house,* etc., *shall receive a hundredfold and shall possess life everlasting.*

*And all the kings of Arabia.* Divers nations and traders and all the kings of Arabia [0679A] and the satraps of the lands offer to Solomon gold and silver: because every nation and every person, striving as they are able, offers to our peaceable one the gift of right faith, purity of understanding, the fulness of holy confession, the splendour of eloquence, whence: *The kings of Tharsis and the islands shall offer a gift,* etc. *And all kings shall adore him, all nations shall serve him [n: (Psal. LXXI)]*.

VERS. 15.-- *He made therefore.* Because the fathers of both Testaments, perfect in teaching and in working, whatsoever they taught by word they proved by work.

*Golden spears.* This is the armour of God, wherewith we resist against the wiles of the devil: this the holy preachers have, who guard the house of God, and keep watch at its door, lest the ancient enemy should enter in, whence: *The bed of Solomon sixty [0679B] valiant men encompass [n: (Prov. III)]*.

VERS. 16.-- *Three hundred also.* It signifies perfect faith in the confession of the holy Trinity. Fitly do the guardians of the house of God bear golden spears and shields: because the preachers, who are the guardians of the Church, shining with the brightness of wisdom, pierce their enemies through with the javelins of words, and with the shield of faith quench the most wicked fiery darts.

*Six little arms also.* [var: Vulg. 2Par 9:18 *Sex quoque gradus … et brachiola duo altrinsecus*, "six steps … and two little arms on either side"] In the senary is the perfection of good works. For in six days God made the world, and the senary is the first perfect number. And by good and perfect works there is an ascent to heaven.

*A golden footstool.* The saints, shining with the splendour of virtues, in whom God dwells. There may also be noted [0679C] by the footstool those who are weak in the body of Christ, as penitents, or those who shall be converted last, namely toward the end of the world, who nevertheless are golden, baked and proved by the fire of the tribulations of that time.

*Standing beside the little arms: but also other twelve little lions,* etc. Because the holy Fathers, whatsoever of good they did in the Gospel, ascribed it not to themselves but to God, whence: *Not to us, O Lord, not to us, but to thy name give glory [n: (Psal. CXIII)]*.

*The little arms,* etc., in Kings thus: *And two hands on this side and on that holding the seat [n: (III Reg. X)]*. These are the consolations of divine grace, which carry the Church forward to the heavenly kingdom. Or this is designated by the binary, [0679D] because in both Testaments this above all is commended, that nothing of good can be perfected except by divine help. Whence: *Without me you can do nothing [n: (Joan. V)]*.

VERS. 19.-- *Six steps.* Because by teaching and by example they strive, lest the labour of the unwise should waver in working, but should stoutly keep uprightness in the advance of the virtues.

*There was not such a throne,* etc. Because of the Church it is written: *Strength and beauty are her clothing,* etc. [n: (III Reg. X.)] And: *Many daughters have gathered together riches: thou hast surpassed them all [n: (Prov. XXXI)]*.

VERS. 20.-- *All things also.* The souls of the saints, which serve the will of God, shining with the splendour of wisdom and of charity, contain the drink of living water, [0680A] *which becomes in them a fountain of water springing up into life everlasting [n: (Joan. IV)]*.

*Silver.* The word of God is not in speech, but in power, as in the preaching of the Gospel; not in the persuasive words of human wisdom, but in the showing of the spirit and of power.

VERS. 21.-- *For the ships of the king went to Tharsis,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Christ sends into the world the apostles, the teachers of the Gospel, whom he chose from among the Jews, together with the preachers chosen from among the gentiles, of a threefold confession but of one faith, that in them that believe they may bring to him the gold of wisdom, the silver of eloquence, and the ivory of chastity. But the ape is a clever animal, and given to imitating man, and can signify the philosophers, who sometimes change the meanings of true wisdom, [0680B] and expound the acts of the moral life in their disciplines. The peacock, which displays its praised feathers, signifies those who for the praises of men make a show of the appearances of divers virtues; but being instructed by the teaching of the preachers they learn to do good with a view to God alone, and are fitted for the delights of the eternal king.

VERS. 25.-- *Forty.* The quadragenary signifies the times of the ancient law. For Moses, being about to receive the law, fasted forty days, and the people instructed by him went about the desert for forty years. But the senary signifies the present time, in which the apostolic teaching nourishes the people of the New Testament. Rightly therefore are the horses of Solomon placed in the stables numbered by the quadragenary, because through the hardness of the old law the insolence of the former [0680C] people was restrained. But the duodenary signifies the present time, in which the apostolic authority nourishes the people of the gentiles with the teaching of the New Testament.

*In Jerusalem.* In the vision of peace. The distance of places shows the distinction of affections. For they who are in the cities of the chariots signify those who are instructed in the divers disciplines of the church by spiritual masters. But others, already instructed, being fitted for the use of the heavenly king, are worthy to have his presence. Whence: *The chariot of God with ten thousands,* etc. [n: (Psal. LXXVI.)] And elsewhere: *Ascending upon thy horses, and thy cavalry is health [n: (Rabac. III)]*. [var: Vulg. Hab 3:8 *et quadrigae tuae salvatio*, "and thy chariots salvation"]

*Of the Philistines.* Of them that fall doubly. For Philitshiim is interpreted a double ruin. This comes to pass when, through the remission of sins, the grace of power overflows from its beginning in the [0680D] Jordan of baptism even from the conversion of all nations to the faith of Christ, so that where there was a flowing away of carnal desires, there the seed of virtues may be established; and where the idolatry and the shameful converse of the peoples was a double ruin, there through the confession of right faith and the worship of piety the life of the faithful may rise to the heavenly kingdom. For his power reaches even to the borders of Egypt, that is, to the darkened minds of unbelievers, which he fills with the light of faith; whence: *Before him the Ethiopians shall fall down,* etc.

VERS. 27.-- *And so great an abundance.* The true Solomon affords in the Church an abundance of silver as of stones, when he turns the senseless hearts of the gentiles, which stones they worshipped in place of God, to the understanding of the true God, to the meditation of the law, [0681A] and to the preaching of the Gospel. Of whom it is said: *They that make them become like unto them,* etc. [n: (Psal. CXIII)]. And elsewhere: *God is able of these stones to raise up children to Abraham [n: (Matth. III)]*. He affords also a multitude of cedars as of sycamores, because the barrenness of the gentiles, who would not raise themselves on high, he advanced to the fruitfulness of good works and of virtues.

VERS. 27.-- *And of cedars.* The cedar is by nature incorruptible, pleasant in odour, bright in appearance, which, being kindled, by its brightness puts serpents to flight and destroys them.

*But the rest.* Which he did after he transgressed the law of God, when foreign wives drew him even to idolatry: many indeed, like Solomon, begin well and end ill, and no man ought to be called blessed before his death and his last funeral rites.
