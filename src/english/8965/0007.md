## CHAPTER TEN.

[0681B]

VERS. 8.-- *He forsook the counsel of the old men,* etc. [n: (HIERON.)] The Hebrews say that these were Banaias the son of Joiada and Jahihel the son of Achamoni, who is also called Chilla: whose counsel being forsaken, Roboam began to treat with young men, that is, with Aduram, who in what follows is said to have been stoned, and with such as he. But we often call a man young not in age, but in mind. For Roboam was young not in age, but in understanding: who began to reign in the forty-first year of his age.

VERS. 13.-- *And the king answered,* etc. He signifies here the evil rulers in the Church, who, given over to earthly desires, neglect the care of their subjects, and rejoice in the multitude of those who are obsequious to them. And Roboam is interpreted the breadth of the people. These are they who enter the broad [0681C] way which leads to death, not the narrow, which leads to life; and, forsaking the counsel of the fathers whose words and deeds tend to the height of perfection, they choose to obey the counsel of young men, who burden them with praises and flatteries, who are blind and leaders of the blind, etc., whence: *Woe to thee, O land, whose king is a child,* etc. [n: (Eccl. X.)]

## CHAPTER ELEVEN.

VERS. 21.-- *He loved,* etc. Now he had eighteen wives joined to him by law, and thirty concubines. And there were born to him eighteen sons, and sixty daughters.

## CHAPTER TWELVE.

VERS. 2.-- *And in the fifth year of the reign of Roboam he went up.* [n: (RAB.)] Evil rulers through their negligence provoke [0681D] the prince of darkness into the Church, who carries off the treasures of the house of the Lord and the treasures of the king, when he withdraws the knowledge of the Scriptures and the works of the virtues from the incautious and the slothful.

VERS. 9.-- *Sesac the king.* The fine linen of haircloth, or the joy of haircloth. Fine linen indeed signifies subtlety: haircloth, which is made of the hairs of goats, sins. In the fine linen of haircloth, therefore, is the craftiness of diabolical fraud in the persuasion to sin. Sesac carries off the riches of Jerusalem, when the devil, who craftily deceives and rejoices in the multitude of sinners, despoils churchmen of spiritual knowledge and of the work of virtue: and those who seemed comely in the Church, being despoiled, he plunges with himself into the abyss of perdition.

*The shields.* Solomon made golden shields, and delivered them to the keepers [0682A] of his house, when Christ committed spiritual knowledge through the two Testaments to the teachers of the Church for the protection of the people. This the ancient enemy through carelessness draws back again. For when they are intent upon earthly things, and care less for the heavenly, he leaves them stripped of their former office and of knowledge, as men despoiled of their riches in their own house. But such men, when they see themselves deprived of spiritual understanding, seek by eloquence an abundance of words, the sonorousness whereof they set against their enemies on behalf of their subjects as though it were the firmness of brazen shields. But by how much brass is baser than gold, by so much is eloquence inferior to the fruit of true wisdom. Whence: *Where there are very many words, there is frequently want [n: (Prov. XIV)]*.

VERS. 15.-- *Of Semeias.* Who above reproved Roboam and the sons [0682B] of Juda for their sins.

*Addo.* Who is also Abdo, this is he who was sent by the Lord into Samaria to reprove Jeroboam for the altar which he had made, who was slain on the way, because, seduced by a false prophet against the commandment of the Lord, he ate there.

## CHAPTER THIRTEEN.

VERS. 4.-- *Semeron.* [n: (RAB. ex Hieron.)] It is a city which Jesus took, the king of it being slain, which they say is now called Sebaste, a town of Palestine, where the relics of John the Baptist were laid. But we read in Kings that Amri king of Israel bought the mountain Semeron from a certain Semer, and built a city on the top of it, which from the name of its lord, namely Semer, he called Semeron.

[0682C]VERS. 19.-- *Bethel.* [n: (RAB.)] A city at the next milestone from Elia, on the right hand as men go to Neopolis, which formerly was called Luza, that is Amigdalon, and it fell to the lot of Benjamin beside Bethan and Hai, which Jesus stormed, the king of it being slain.

*Ephron.* It is a city in the tribe of Juda. Whence also a very large village to the north is to this day called Ephrea, at the twentieth milestone from Elia.

VERS. 20.-- *In the days.* Not of Jeroboam, in whose twentieth year, according to the book of Kings, Asa the son of Abia reigned over Juda and Jerusalem, but of Abia, who, Bethel being received, kept the idols for Israel unto a stumbling-block, as the Hebrews assert.

## CHAPTER FOURTEEN.

VERS. 2.-- *He went out,* etc. When Azarias had reigned ten [0682D] years, Zareas king of Ethiopia moved his camp against him with an exceeding great army, leading ninety thousand foot indeed, and ten thousand horse, and three hundred chariots. The camp being pitched against him, when Asa had seen the multitude of the Ethiopians, he begged victory of God with a great cry, and that he himself might destroy so many thousands of men. For he said that trust was to be placed in no other than in him who was able to show even a few stronger than many, and to make the weak prevail over the strongest.

## CHAPTER FIFTEEN.

VERS. 15.-- *And Azarias,* etc. Azarias the prophet, meeting them, commanded that they should stay the step of their journey a little from the way, and then he began to say to them [0683A] that that victory had been granted them by God, for that they had kept themselves just and holy, and had done all things by the will of God. And he said that they, remaining in his commandments, would prevail over their enemies, and would have by divine power happiness and prosperity; but forsaking religion, would come into the contrary, and that a time would come in which no true prophet would be found among that people, nor priest, for which cause both the cities were to be overthrown, and the nation to be scattered over all the earth, and that they would have a miserable and needy life.

## CHAPTER SIXTEEN.

VERS. 14.-- *With spices,* etc.--This is the vain boasting of fame, which philosophers or poets bestow after the death of their kings, or even false Christians upon their dying [0683B] masters, and these things they cast into the fire: because all those things are shown to be liable to the fires of hell, which they have here brought forth without profit and without the edification of the brethren.

*They burnt over him,* etc. Note that for kings, as the Hebrews hand down, burnings of precious garments together with spices were made for reverence' sake. Whence elsewhere: *According to the burnings of thy forefathers shall they do to thee.* For it was the custom among the ancients that the precious ornaments of kings should be burned upon a pyre after their death in place of funeral rites, but without the bodies.

## CHAPTER SEVENTEEN.

VERS. 3.-- *He walked in the ways of David,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] He shows that he was an executor of the divine commandments, as David was, before he sinned against the wife [0683C] of Urias, the satisfaction for which sin gained him an everlasting memory.

VERS. 5.-- *All Juda gave gifts to Josaphat.* Here he signifies the faithful of the gentiles, who, forsaking the error of their fathers, come together to Christ, washed by baptism and anointed with chrism, receiving from Christ the Christian name and royal dignity. Whence: *You are a chosen generation, a kingly priesthood,* etc. [n: (I Petr. II.)] For in the time of Josaphat, the dowry of the Lord, or he who is a dowry, or he who is endowed, and because the Church of the gentiles, purchased by the blood of Christ, given by the pledge of the Holy Spirit, merited the fellowship of the heavenly Bridegroom and the society of the kingdom. To whom infinite riches have been made when divers nations believe, and in the acquisition of divers virtues.

[0683D]VERS. 10.-- *Therefore there was a fear,* etc. For the virtues which are wrought in the Church, the boldness of the gentiles is taken away, and they are stirred up to bring to Christ the gifts of faith and the tribute of good work, and of pious confession, and the flocks of simplicity.

## CHAPTER EIGHTEEN.

VERS. 2.-- *And he went down,* etc. These are the heresiarchs who lead the crowd of the lost through the by-ways of errors, and plunge them into the precipice of death. Josaphat was joined to Achab by affinity, and went down to him into Samaria to his help against his enemies: and he signifies those who from among catholics incline themselves to the friendship of heretics: who, although they depart not from the path of right faith, nevertheless do not shun the fellowship and communion [0684A] of them that err. Against whom it is said: *A man that is a heretic, after the first and second correction, avoid [n: (Tit. III)]*. Whence Josaphat is reproved by Jehu the seer, the son of Anani, that prophet namely who had rebuked Asa, because he had given help to the impious, and was joined in friendship with those who hate the Lord.

[n: (GREG., lib. II Moral., cap. 15, tom. I.)] The throne of the Lord, the angelic powers, etc., *as far as* and by so much the more hardly would he be found unto punishment, by how much the more securely he was living in guilt.

## CHAPTER NINETEEN.

VERS. 10.-- *Every cause that shall come to you,* etc. [n: (AUG., lib. IV de Civ., cap. 3.)] Justice being removed, what are kingdoms but great robberies? because even robberies themselves, what are they but little kingdoms?
