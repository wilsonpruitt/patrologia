## CHAPTER XX.

[0684B]VERS. 1.-- *Of the Ammonites.* He signifies the Idumaeans, but the writer of the history chose rather to note what was being done by the name of their associates, than to publish abroad the impiety of the shameless.

VERS. 2.-- *From these places,* etc. He signifies those places which are beyond the Sea of Salt, which is otherwise named the Asphaltite lake or the Dead Sea.

*Engaddi.* It is called so to this day, a very large village of the Jews beside the Dead Sea, whence comes balsam: which Solomon calls the vineyards of Engaddi.

VERS. 16.-- *For they shall go up by the ascent named Sis,* etc. [n: (RAB. in Paral., ex Hieron.)] Which is also Jeruel according to the Hebrews. For they say that Sis, etc., *as far as* a terror was struck into the strangers on every side.

Mystically, the Moabites, the Ammonites, and the Idumaeans signify the pagans, [0684C] the Jews, and the heretics, who stir up war against the faithful without cause, and strive to take away the Christian religion. But Josaphat, that is, the people of Christ, attacks them not with bodily arms, but with spiritual, seeking the help from above with the inmost devotion of the heart.

VERS. 25.-- *Josaphat therefore came,* etc. The holy teachers with the crowd of the faithful gather from the spoils of their enemies whatsoever in Physics, Ethics and Logic they have believed to be profitable in reading and in writing and in teaching, and turn it, through faith in the holy Trinity, to the use of the whole Church, so that what those men possessed unjustly and unprofitably, the faithful, possessing it profitably, may possess unto the salvation of souls.

VERS. 26.-- *The valley of blessing.* The humility of the Church, [0684D] where praises are worthily rendered to God. And he who, instructed in the teaching of the four Gospels, comes through the faith of baptism to the unity of the Church, praising there duly and conversing worthily with God, shall enter into the vision of perpetual peace with everlasting praise and gladness.

## CHAPTER XXI.

VERS. 2.-- *All these, the sons of Josaphat,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Imitators of his works, while their brother Joram was ever slipping into worse things, whence Joram himself is read to have slain them together with the princes of Israel.

VERS. 14.-- *Behold the Lord will strike thee,* etc. By the Philistines and the Arabians who laid waste his land, and plundered the substance and the sons and likewise the wives of Joram, who is interpreted, Who is high: who from [0685A] a religious father turned aside to perfidy, and made the sons of Juda to commit fornication: he signifies those who, imbued with faith and regenerated by baptism, forsake the truth of faith, following the error of heretics, and perverting others with themselves. Whence Elias, that is, the prophetic word, refutes them: because all the authority of the divine books reproves such men, and deservedly rebukes them for their perversity with the gravest protestations.

VERS. 20.-- *And he reigned eight years,* etc. Concerning Joram, that he reigned eight years and lived forty; but that his son Ochozias, when he had begun to reign, was forty-two years old, and if this is so, he begot a son two years before his father himself was born. But the Hebrews say of Joram, who is described to have lived forty years and to have reigned eight years, [0685B] that he lived forty years and reigned twenty and eight. For the eight years which are attributed to him in the kingdom are those in which he reigned before he slew his brethren, while he still lived innocently. But the remaining twenty years, in which he reigned afterwards, are for this reason not numbered, because he lived in sickness and tribulation: and therefore they are attributed to his son, who is reported according to the book of Kings to be not more than twenty-two years old, lest the years remaining over from the sum of the numbers should make an error in the reckoning of the times.

## CHAPTER XXII.

VERS. 6.-- *Azarias the son of Joram,* etc. [var: Vulg. 2Par 22:6 *Ochozias filius Joram*, "Ochozias the son of Joram"] [n: (RAB., ex Hieron.)] Who above is *Ochozias,* that is, laying hold of the Lord: but Azarias, the help of the Lord. And therefore [0685C] according to the Hebrews his name is changed for the better: because according to the commandment of the Lord he visited the sick man.

VERS. 7.-- *Against Jehu,* etc. Jehu signifies the sovereignty of the gentiles, which Christ destined against the sacrilegious city, which slew the prophets and the Lord of the prophets, and persecuted the apostles; that he might punish it with a worthy vengeance, and destroy the shrine of the priests after the coming of Christ, and overthrow the temple: and cast down the impious synagogue, thirsting for their blood, from the height of the kingdom as another Jezabel, and slay its own rulers.

VERS. 10.-- *For indeed Athalia,* etc. Athalia, who endeavoured to extinguish the seed of David and to blot out the royal stock, signifies the impiety of the Synagogue, which through the wickedness of its mind lies in wait for the seed of Christ, raging with implacable [0685D] hatred, which once seemed to reign when, being a thing of time, she observed the ceremonies of the law. Athalia is interpreted the temporal one of the Lord, but by the strenuousness of Josabeth (who is interpreted the fulness of the Lord, that is, of the Church in which are the true delights) Joas is preserved, who is interpreted the memory of the Lord, that is, Christ, in whom is the memory of the name of the Lord: lest he should be slain in the saints through the cruelty of her that raged, but should be nourished in the house of Joiada the high priest, who is interpreted the beloved of the Lord, that is, Christ, of whom the Father saith: *This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased [n: (Matth. XVII)]*. Whose house is the Church, where, abiding in the faith of the elect, he daily makes increase of his body, that in the time of judgment he may lift up the sceptre of the kingdom and his power against those who were pressing him down.

## CHAPTER XXIII.

[0686A]

VERS. 13.-- *Upon the step.* [n: (RAB.)] The pillar, namely, and the base, which Solomon had prepared and set in the midst of the basilica, having five cubits of length, and five of breadth, and three in height.

VERS. 16.-- *And Joiada made a covenant.* Joiada is interpreted knowing the Lord, or loving him: because he taught the sons of Israel to know the Lord. He made a covenant therefore between the Lord and the people and the king, that they should obey the Lord, namely, and his law. He made one also between the king and the people, that the people should deal faithfully toward the king, and the king should rule the people justly according to the teaching of the priests and Levites, whose office it was to make known the commandments of the Lord.

## CHAPTER XXIV.

[0686B]VERS. 4.-- *After which it pleased Joas,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] King Joas reproves the priests, etc., *as far as* lest through the negligence of teachers the multitude of hearers should perish.

VERS. 7.-- *And her sons,* etc. Not that she then had sons, because, Ochozias being dead, in that he had had no son, Athalia endeavoured to slay all the royal seed, but he calls her sons the priests of the idols, who had despoiled the temple, and had adorned their idols out of its ornaments, for often imitators are called sons.

VERS. 12.-- *But they hired,* etc. This the apostles do, when through the disciples subject to them they scattered the seeds of the divine word throughout the world, that they might set over their hearers workmen fit for the will of God: whereof some fashion timbers, when they prepare themselves and those obedient to them [0686C] as fruitful timbers in the house of God. Others made the roofs sound, when they built up again those things which had been broken by heresy or by schisms. Others hew stones, when they stoutly rebuke the hard of heart and the unbelieving. Others work the metals of iron and of brass, when they demand from each one the strength of faith and the confession of catholic truth, that the house of God may be filled, according to that word. *To every one of us is given grace according to the measure of the giving of Christ [n: (Ephes. IV)]*.

VERS. 14.-- *The remaining part of the money,* etc. That seems contrary which is read in the book of Kings: *But there were not made of the same money the water-pots of the temple and the flesh-hooks [n: (IV Reg. XII)]*. But it is not said here that of that same money which was offered by the people in the temple of the Lord for the repairing of the house of God there were made [0686D] the sacred vessels for the ministry of the temple and for holocausts, but of the remaining part of the money, that is, of that which had been contributed by other donations. For it could come to pass that moneys offered at divers times should furnish the material for divers workmanships, just as one teaching is that by which the untaught are imbued unto faith, and another that by which the perfect are instructed unto fulness.

VERS. 18.-- *And there was wrath.* [n: (RAB., ex Hieron.)] The Hebrews hand down that the princes of Juda adored the king as God, and that he willingly received it: whence he offended the Lord exceedingly, and suddenly experienced the wrath which he deserved. For this fault also Herod is read in the Acts of the apostles to have been struck by an angel.

VERS. 25.-- *Of Zacharias the son of Joiada,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] The Lord in the Gospel, making mention of Zacharias slain between the temple and [0687A] the altar, etc., *as far as* which they call Siloe, pointing to the red rocks they think them to be polluted with the blood of Zacharias.

VERS. 26.-- *And they lay in wait,* etc. The Hebrews assert that the Ammonites and the Moabites lay in wait for king Joas because of the unjust slaying of Zacharias, to aggravate the malice of the Israelites, who would not avenge the priest of God, whom the sons of strangers avenged.
