## CHAPTER XXV.

VERS. 11.-- *Into the valley of Salt,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Where salt was made, either from the hay of the brackish ground, which in many places is cut and dried and burned, or from the salt waters of wells heated and brought by boiling to the firmness of salt. Or otherwise, where Joab also is read to have slain twelve thousand of the Idumaeans. But for the valley of Salt [0687B] the old edition set Gemel, as though it were the name of the region.

VERS. 16.-- *I know, saith he, that the Lord hath thought,* etc. Who wills that no man perish, but condemns by a just judgment him who perseveres in sin. Therefore Amasias perished for this cause, that being admonished by the prophet to do penance, he obeyed not, but remained so unto the end.

VERS. 18.-- *The thistle which is in Libanus,* etc. He hints at this likeness: that Amasias, sprung of a most noble birth, coming of the stock of David, on account of the foulness of idolatry passing his life so and made vile, considers not his own measure, but lifts himself up beyond measure with the arrogance of pride.

VERS. 21.-- *Bethsames.* [n: (RAB.)] In Kings Bethsames is called a town of Juda: because it belongs to the kingdom of Juda, that is, to the two tribes over which the house of [0687C] David ruled; but according to the situation of the places it belongs to the tribe of Benjamin.

VERS. 24.-- *And with Obededom,* etc. Because he took away not only the vessels which he found in the house of God, but also those which the doorkeepers and the guards of the temple watched over. But in Kings it is read that David chose from the sons of Obededom seventy-two to minister in the house of God, and for the guard of the temple.

VERS. 27.-- *Who after he departed.* From the time that he heard not the counsel of the prophet, nor would be converted to the Lord, a conspiracy was prepared and deferred, until it should come to its consummation.

## CHAPTER XXVI.

VERS. 3.-- *Ozias when he had begun to reign.* Who is also [0687D] Azarias. Ozias is interpreted the strength of the Lord. Azarias, the help of the Lord, because as long as he did rightly, aided by divine help he waged very many wars happily, and triumphed over his enemies. But before he attempted unlawful things through pride, being struck with leprosy he darkened the dignity of his name and of his honour, shut out from the people until the day of his death.

VERS. 9.-- *Upon the gate of the valley.* [n: (RAB.)] This is the gate of Ephraim, etc., *as far as* but concerning this gate and the other gates of Jerusalem we have spoken abundantly in Esdras, following Bede.

VERS. 16.-- *But when he was strengthened,* etc. Ozias was at the first just, and did what was right in the sight of the Lord: whence he both conquered his enemies, and had many prophets in his reign. Hence, as long as Zacharias the priest, surnamed the understanding, lived, he pleased God, serving in the temple with [0688A] all reverence. But after Zacharias died, wishing to offer the gifts by himself, he invaded the dignity of the priesthood not so much piously as boldly, nor did he obey the Levites and the priests who cried out against him: whence he was struck with leprosy in the forehead, which the priest covered with a plate of gold, which in Ezechiel the Lord commands to be signed with the impress of the letter Thau.

*And having entered the temple of the Lord,* because, clothed in the priestly stole, at the approach of a solemn festival he entered into the temple to the golden altar, about to offer incense, according to Josephus. Whence before the city, in the place which is called Eroge, the middle part of the mountain toward the west was rent, and going forward by its own rolling for four stadia it stood still against the eastern mountain, so that it closed the ways and overwhelmed the royal cities. The Hebrews will have it [0688B] that this happened in the twenty-fifth year of Ozias, whose remaining years are twenty-seven.

Ozias reigned fifty-two years, at which time Amulcus among the Latins and Agamestor among the Athenians were ruling, and when he was dead Isaias saw the vision, as he himself testifies, namely at that time in which Romulus the founder of the Roman empire was born.

VERS. 23.-- *In the field of the royal sepulchres.* Not in the royal burial-places, not in the city of David. For heretics and schismatics are unworthy of the society and communion of the holy Church, nor is their memory held among catholics: because, stained with errors and squalid with vices, they are separated from their fellowship.

## CHAPTER XXVII.

VERS. 1.-- *Joatham was five and twenty years old,* [0688C] etc. At the beginning and at the end he numbers the years of Joatham, because as he began with justice, so he persevered unto the end. Whence the times of his life are proclaimed as worthy of praise.

VERS. 3.-- *He himself built the gate,* etc. Which in the Acts of the apostles is called Beautiful, and which according to the Hebrews and by them is called even to this day the gate of Joatham (because he himself built it).

*Ophel.* It was a tower not far from the temple, of wonderful height. Whence also it received the name Ophel, that is, of darkness or of cloud. But where it is written in Micheas: *And the tower of the flock, cloudy, of the daughter of Sion [n: (Mich. IV, 8)]*: in the Hebrew it has, *the tower Ophel.* The tower Ophel signifies the eminence of Scripture, which, founded upon history, buries the head of its spiritual sense among the clouds, while it hides the greatness of knowledge [0688D] from the eyes of men. Whence: *He made darkness his covert [n: (Psal. XVII)]*. Joatham, whose life is praised, signifies Christ, of whom it is said: *Great is the Lord and exceedingly to be praised [n: (Ibid., XLVII)]*, etc. For Joatham is interpreted consummated or perfect, and the Apostle says of Christ: *And being consummated, he became to all that obey him the cause of eternal salvation,* etc. [n: (Hebr. V)]. He himself built the high gate of the house of the Lord, and wrought the mystery of his incarnation with wonderful power, who says of himself: *I am the door. By me if any man enter in, he shall be saved [n: (Joan. X)]*. This gate was called high, of which the Psalmist says: *The Lord is high above all nations [n: (Psal. CXII)]*, etc.

## CHAPTER XXVIII.

[0689A]

VERS. 3.-- *In the valley of Benennom.* [n: (RAB.)] This is the valley of Benennom, etc., *as far as* for all of them that commit adultery, their hearts are as an oven.

## CHAPTER XXIX.

VERS. 3.-- *In that same year,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] Not otherwise would he imitate David his father, who from infancy, being given to God, magnified the worship of God in the beginning of his reign.

But morally he admonishes the rulers of the Church, that they be not slothful in the office committed to them, but do all things diligently.

Mystically Ezechias, who not only performed the work of God strenuously by himself or by his own men, but rather stirred up others by messengers to a like zeal, signifies Christ, [0689B] which the interpretation of his name also testifies. For Ezechias is laying hold of the Lord, or the strength of the Lord. But Christ is the power of God and the wisdom of God, who washed away by his blood all the transgressions of the fathers which had been under the law, and plainly restored the worship of piety in the Church. He himself sent legates to call together the nations unto the faith. First the prophets, then the apostles, and their successors: he appointed the pasch to be celebrated in the second month; because, the shadow of the law ceasing, he taught that the true pasch was to be kept, of which Paul says: *For Christ our pasch is sacrificed [n: (I Cor. V)]*.

VERS. 6.-- *Our fathers have sinned,* etc. [n: (HIERON., quaest. in Paral., tom. III.)] This is written fully, etc., *as far as* and they adored toward the rising of the sun.

## CHAPTER XXX.

[0689C]VERS. 1.-- *He sent also,* etc. [n: (HIERON.)] When he had publicly sent messengers to all Israel, he sent secretly to Manasses and Ephraim, to those whom he knew could be converted by letters. For they were the prouder because of the royal dignity which was in the house of Ephraim.

## CHAPTER XXXI.

VERS. 1.-- *They broke the images,* etc. After the coming of the Saviour, not only in Jerusalem and in Juda, but in all the nations of the world idolatry was destroyed, and the pride of the devil fell. And as the number of the faithful increases, the worship of demons decreases, until at the end of the world the last death be overwhelmed, and the Lord alone shall reign in that day.

## CHAPTER XXXII.

[0689D]VERS. 4.-- *They stopped up all the fountains.* Against pagans and heretics who fight, diligent caution must be used, and the mysteries must be veiled, whence: *Give not that which is holy to dogs, neither cast pearls before swine.*

VERS. 5.-- *He built.* [n: (RAB. in Paral. )] Christ built the Church which is the city of God, etc., *as far as* and he appointed teachers, the leaders of spiritual wars.

## CHAPTER XXXIII.

VERS. 12.-- *And he did penance exceedingly before the God of his fathers.* [n: (RAB.)] The penance of Manasses being heard, no man ought to despair of the mercy of God, but rather to presume of pardon through penance.

## CHAPTER XXXIV.

[0690A]

VERS. 5.-- *The bones moreover.* [n: (RAB.)] Therefore he burned the priests upon the altars of the idols, that he might show that the sacrifices of those men were deadly, who not only offered victims of cattle to demons, but delivered up even themselves to idolatry. Here he signifies all those who, made over to the service of the devil, have ended a sacrilegious life in time, as men to be consumed with eternal torments according to the desert of each.

VERS. 22.-- *To Olda the prophetess,* etc. [n: (HIERON.)] Olda was the wife of Sellum the uncle of the father of Jeremias, and the father of Ananeel. She dwelt in the second. It was a place of Jerusalem outside the wall of the city and the outer wall, which was called the second: of which mention is made in the prophet Sophonias.
