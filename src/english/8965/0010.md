## CHAPTER XXXV.

[0690B]

VERS. 1.-- *And Josias made in Jerusalem,* etc. [n: (RAB.)] We are admonished to purge first the ground of our heart from all vices, and so to celebrate the pasch to the Lord, *not with the old leaven, nor with the leaven of malice and wickedness: but with the unleavened bread of sincerity and truth [n: (I Cor. V)]*. But mystically Josias, that is, the strength of the Lord, signifies Christ, of whom it is read: *The Lord strong and mighty: the Lord mighty in battle [n: (Psal. XXIII)]*. Josias is also interpreted the salvation of the Lord, and *Christ saves his people from their sins [n: (Matth. I)]*. He it is who with the zeal of God cleanses the land of Juda and Jerusalem from all uncleannesses, who said through the prophet: *The zeal of thy house hath eaten [0690C] me [n: (Psal. LXVIII)]*. Of whom elsewhere: *Whose fan is in his hand, and he will thoroughly cleanse his floor [n: (Matth. III)]*. He celebrates the true phase in the eighteenth year of his reign: because in the eighth age, the general resurrection being granted, he leads his saints with the perfection of good works to the penny of eternal blessedness, all scandals having first been cast out of his kingdom.

VERS. 3.-- *Put the ark in the sanctuary of the temple, which Solomon the son of David king of Israel built.* The Hebrews hand down that the ark was carried out of the temple in the time of Achaz, when idols had been put by him in the temple, and that it was brought over into the house of Sellum the husband of Olda, the uncle of Jeremias. For it could not be together with the idols.

[0690D]VERS. 18.-- *There was no Phase like to this,* etc. Because, the figures being taken away in the day of judgment, the saints shall pass from death to life, from corruption to glory, and in the sight of their Creator in the heavenly Jerusalem they shall rejoice for ever: because the God of gods shall be seen in Sion, because he hath made his mercy wonderful in the fortified city.

VERS. 21.-- *To which God commanded me to go in haste.* Because from the words of Jeremias and of the other prophets he was able to know the will of the Lord, by which judgment was to be passed upon a sinful people. Whence Rabsaces also said of Sennacherib king of the Assyrians: *The Lord commanded me to go up to the land [n: (Psal. LXXXIII)]*, because the prophets had foretold that he would go up, which was by no means hidden from him

VERS. 22.-- *In the field of Mageddo.* The slaying of Josias, [0691A] who was slain by Pharao Nechao in Mageddo, signifies the persecution of the ancient enemy against the preachers, whom he strives to deliver to death and to take away from the Church, that he may the more easily invade and scatter the whole people. For Pharao is interpreted scattering, Nechao striking, Mageddo of temptation. For the devil is permitted to rise up against the saints: and he contends with all his strength for this, that by tempting and striking the flock of God he may first take them away by scattering them, by whose comfort others fight against the enemy.

## CHAPTER XXXVI.

VERS. 4.-- *And he appointed.* Nechao appointed Joakim king, and laid a fine upon the land of a hundred talents of silver: because the devil demanded the weight of service from the people of the lost, [0691B] that both in understanding and in speech they might be ready for his obedience.

*And he turned.* It is to be noted that of the same Joakim the son of Josias it is thus written in Kings: *Joakim slept with his fathers, and Joachin his son reigned in his stead [n: (IV Reg. XXIV)]*. Josephus however says: When the king of Babylon had entered the city, he by no means kept faith, but slew the strongest young men and those comely in beauty together with king Joakim, whom he commanded to be cast forth unburied before the wall. But his son Joachin he appointed king of the whole province. And those who were set in dignities, to the number of three thousand, he led into Babylon, among whom was Ezechiel, still a boy. This end therefore had Joakim the king, who lived ten and thirty years, and reigned [0691C] eleven years. But the successor of the kingdom of Joakim, a king by name Ononimus, reigned three months and ten days. And it is to be known that when once a man has made himself over to any vice, he will be liable to many masters. Whence Joakim, whom Pharao before compelled to pay tribute to himself, him Nabuchodonosor king of Babylon compels to serve three years. Nabuchodonosor signifies the same one that Pharao does; the prince, namely, of confusion and the ruler of these darknesses. And when the devil has compelled anyone to pay him the tribute of sin, he brings him wholly [0692A] into his own dominion, and exacts full service, by persuasion, namely, by delight, by consent, and by thought too, by speech, and by work.

[n: (HIERON., quaest. in Paral., tom. III.)] It is to be known that the name of this Joachin, etc., *as far as* being corrupted by the examples of their superiors they go with them into the prey of the devil.

VERS. 10.-- *But the king.* Sedecias signifies those who undertake the rule among the people of God: for Sedecias is interpreted the just one of the Lord. For they receive the seat of justice for the exercising of the works of justice, they receive the dignity of honour and of office, chiefly because they deserve to be called sons of Josias, that is, of the salvation of the Lord, through the grace of baptism and the anointing of chrism: but degenerating they slip back into the foulness of vices, and turn themselves [0692B] as subjects to impiety, and keep not the faith which they had promised to God, nor observe the covenants of peace with men. Such men the divine law daily, and the miracles of the prophets and of the apostles, admonish to withdraw from their sins and to return to the Lord through penance: but once hardened, they despise the words of salvation, whence God, being angered, sends against them the king of the Chaldeans, that is, the devil with his satellites, that is, the spiritual wickednesses, who, sparing no age, yielding to no dignity, carry over into Babylon, that is, into confusion, whomsoever they take; and they burn the house of the Lord, because they compel the faithful, who are the temple of God, to blaspheme. They destroy the walls of Jerusalem, when they cast down the watches of the shepherds, they burn the towers, when they burn up the prelates through unlawful desires [0692C]: they demolish everything precious, when they reduce to nothing those who applaud themselves upon the prerogative of virtue and of knowledge.

The end of the book of Paralipomenon is like to the beginning of the book of Esdras the priest: whence what we have expounded there, there is no need to repeat here. Concerning Cyrus also, how he signifies Christ, and what things are said of him, and what things are referred to him, we have expounded in Isaias.

[n: (RAB. ex Beda.)] Solomon built the temple of the Lord in Jerusalem in seven years, etc., *as far as* namely, and of the city of God from which they had been cast out, they receive.
