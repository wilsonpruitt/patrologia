## §M · THE MERGE — what was reconciled between the two stints, and what needed nothing

Two translators cannot see each other's work, and they diverge on terminology without knowing it
(11551 shipped one half calling a column's members *stylus*/*epistylium* and the other
"epistyle"/"shaft", which left the author's own etymological argument explaining nothing). So the
merge is a check, not a concatenation. **Both stints stated their vocabulary before it could be
compared, and the comparison was then made against the English files themselves, not against the
tables** — a table records what a translator believed it did.

**Checked across the seam, and consistent without intervention:**

- **The number-names.** *senarius · centenarius · denarius · binarius · quadragenarius ·
  duodenarius* → **the senary · the centenary · the denary · the binary · the quadragenary · the
  duodenary**, never flattened to "six" or "a hundred", because in CAPUT IX–X the number-name *is*
  the argument. All 12 occurrences fall inside chunk 0006; the first stint's range contains none,
  so there is no seam here to fail at.
- **`Ecclesia` / `ecclesia`.** Capitalization **mirrors the plate, word for word.** The second
  stint stated the rule; the first had followed it silently — its single lowercase "the church"
  (0003, in the *atrium sacerdotum* gloss) stands opposite the single lowercase *ecclesia* its
  Latin twin prints, against three capitalized forms in the same chunk. Verified at the site, not
  taken on trust.
- ***doctores* → teachers, never "doctors"**, in both stints and across nine of the eleven chunks
  (18 occurrences). *praedicatores* stays **preachers**, held apart.
- ***pascha* → pasch**, in both (0001, 0009, 0010). No "Passover" anywhere in the work — which
  matters here, because 0010 is where Migne prints *phase* and *Phase* beside it and the second
  stint kept those **form for form**. Flattening either word to "Passover" would have destroyed a
  distinction the plate itself makes.
- **The abridgment formula**, rendered *as far as* throughout, every occurrence inside its own
  italic span answering an italic *usque ad* in the Latin. Both stints decided `usque` **per
  occurrence** rather than by rule, which is what the brief requires: **50 tokens work-wide**, of
  which **10 sit inside another word** (*utriusque*, *cujusque*) and the remainder split 28 formula
  / 13 ordinary prepositional uses. The trap at 0680D — where *usque ad* is not the formula but
  9:26's own words — was caught.
- **Pattern 17.** thou = singular, you = plural — 21 second-person singular forms in the English
  (*thou* 7, *thee* 4, *thy* 10), each named in its stint's table with the Latin that fixes the
  number.
- **Proper names kept form for form as Migne prints them**, in both — *Philitshiim*, *Neopolis*,
  *Eroge*, *Amulcus*, *Ononimus*, *Zareas*, *Benennom*, and *Jesus* where Migne writes it for
  Josue. None normalized toward a familiar English Bible spelling.

**Held apart deliberately, and stated here because a later sweep would otherwise churn it:**
*charitas* is **charity** and *dilectio* is **love**, throughout. The pair is not decorative — the
`[var:]` at 0668C exists because Migne's Rom. 13:10 lemma prints *charitas* where the Clementine
prints *dilectio*, and an English that used one word for both would make that marker unreadable.
The same applies to *typice* / *figuraliter* (**typically** / **figuratively**), *scientia* /
*sapientia* (**knowledge** / **wisdom**), and *significat* / *figurabat* / *designatur*.

**Nothing was rewritten in either stint's English at the merge.** No drift was found that required
it, and a merge that silently edits a translator's chunk destroys the only record of what that
translator actually read.

**One thing the merge deliberately does NOT do: reconcile the two lemma collations into one list.**
They cover disjoint column ranges, each was checked against `clementine-flat.txt` independently, and
each names the lemmata it found to **agree** as well as those that diverge — roughly 200 spans
between them. Flattening them would lose which reader checked what, which is exactly what a later
blind read needs to know.

### ⭐ ONE CANDIDATE WAS SETTLED AT THE PLATE, AND IT WENT MIGNE'S WAY — `(Rabac. III)` @ 0680C

Not raised by either translator, because it is inside an `[n:]` note and notes pass through
verbatim: the citation index caught it instead, as the work's single **unparsed** reference.
*Rabac.* is no book of the Bible, and an R for an H in *Habac.* is exactly the shape of an OCR
fault — the non-word class that has been ours 19 times out of 19.

**It is Migne's.** Read on the second witness (Gallica `bpt6k5505319w`, **f340 = cols 679/680**,
band C, one HTTP request, map `f = (column + 1) / 2` confirmed against the printed corner numbers
before the read): the plate prints *sanitas (Rabac.* **III***)* in that form. So the non-word rule
has met its first counter-instance from inside a citation, and the shape of the exception is worth
naming — **the rule was measured on words of the TEXT, and an abbreviated book-name in a note is a
different class of thing**, short enough that a single wrong sort produces a plausible-looking
abbreviation rather than visible nonsense.

**What was done:** the printed form stays in the text verbatim, as the sacred-marker rule requires,
and the *index* resolves to `Hab.3.8` through `data/citation-corrections.json`, which keeps
`refDisplay` verbatim and the printed key auditable. The work's unparsed count went 1 → 0. No
`Ibid.` depends on the reference.

⚑ **The same crop independently confirmed two of the second stint's calls**, which is why it is
recorded here and not merely in the corrections file: Migne really does print *equitatus tuus
sanitas* against the Clementine's *et quadrigae tuae salvatio* (so the `[var:]` at 0680C stands on
the plate, not only on the file), and he really does print *usque **ab** conversionem*, which that
stint declined to touch with a `[cj:]`. **Both declines were right, and one page settled both.**

## §2 · THIS RANGE'S CRUCES

### 2a · *Centum.* (0665C) and *Ducenti.* (0677C) are CLIPS and are expanded; *Posteriora.* is not

Both are one-word lemmata and **both are clips whose own gloss proves the missing word.**
*Centum.* (1:17 *equum centum quinquaginta*) is glossed *Qui sunt **ter quinquageni*** — thrice
fifty, which is 150, not 100. *Ducenti.* (8:10 *ducenti quinquaginta*) is glossed by taking the
*centenarius* and then the *quinquagenarius* in turn — 2×100 + 50. **Rendered "A hundred and
fifty." and "Two hundred and fifty."**, per Ruth §1.2: the fragment is rendered as the phrase it
stands for. Rendered as bare "A hundred" and "Two hundred" each gloss would state a falsehood
about its own lemma.

⚑ **And the control's class, per the brief.** *Posteriora.* (0671C) and *Sacerdotes.* (0676A) are
**complete** one-word lemmata with nothing elided, and are rendered as they stand. Contrasting
them with *Centum.* would manufacture an inconsistency that is not there.

### 2b · 0665B · *doctorum testamentum praedicatione* — will not construe. RENDERED AS PRINTED, NO MARKER.

*Quia doctorum **testamentum** praedicatione ad cognoscendam fidei veritatem per praedicatores in
usum veri Salomonis homines adducuntur.* The clause is passive (*adducuntur*), so the
nominative/accusative *testamentum* has nothing to govern it and nothing to be governed by;
*testament**i***, with *praedicatione*, is the construction the words want, and the same
paragraph's *per utrumque testamentum* two lines below shows the phrase is live in his mind.
**The conjecture is deliberately NOT adopted**: nothing here is broken type (Pattern 12), nothing
is lost from the digitization (Pattern 13), and this corpus has no marker meaning "Migne prints a
word that will not construe" — the Ruth 0534A precedent (*societate fieri*) is exactly this and
took none. Rendered "Because by preaching, the testament of the teachers, men are brought…",
which leaves the stranded word visible where the plate leaves it.

### 2e · 0674D–0675D · TWO `[cj:]`, and why each is Pattern 18 and not a corpus-frequency patch

Both are **real, well-formed Latin words**, so Pattern 7 renders them and Pattern 12 has nothing
to wrap; the addendum's rule (a real word is Migne's until a plate says otherwise) is obeyed —
**the printed word keeps its English and the conjecture stands beside it, additively.**

1. **0675C · *in area nihil fuit contrarium legi Domini*** → `in the floor [cj: *area*; read
   *arca*, "the ark"]`. The lemma is 5:10 *Nihilque aliud erat **in arca***, and Migne's own next
   sentence opens *Erat autem **in arca** secundum Paulum urna aurea*. The rival reading (that
   *area* is meant, the threshing-floor of 3:1) **loses** because the whole paragraph inventories
   what was inside the ark — urn, rod, tables, bars — and a threshing-floor holds none of them.
2. **0675D · *Erat et virgo Aaron, quae excisa denuo floruerat*** → `the virgin [cj: *virgo*;
   read *virga*, "rod"]`. *virga Aaron quae fronduerat* is Heb 9:4, which is the very list Migne
   is walking (*secundum Paulum*), and *excisa … floruerat* is said of a cut rod, not of a
   person. The rival (*virgo* as a Marian figure) **loses** because nothing in the sentence or
   its neighbours makes the turn to Mary, and the clause's three companions are all objects in
   the ark.

⛔ Neither was allowed to replace Migne's word in the running text; **the reader meets "the
virgin" and "the floor" on the page**, with the conjecture beside them, which is what Pattern 18
requires and what a `[sic:]` would have destroyed.

### 2f · 0674D · *Erat et tabulae testamenti* — singular verb, plural subject. NO MARKER.

### 2g · 0666A · *Significat quod gentes … transmittit* — plural subject, singular verb; and TWO construals

Rendered **"It signifies that the Gentiles, the report of the Gospel having been heard, sends
diverse persons through faith unto baptism"**, disagreement carried, for the reason in 2f.
**The rival construal was weighed and lost:** *audita fama Evangelii* could be read as a

and that reading leaves *gentes* and *diversas personas* stacked as two unexplained accusatives.
**A construal that exists only because it tidies the grammar is the thing this edition is for
refusing.**

### 2h · 0667C · *latitudo … significant* — the same class, in the *sicut … ita* frame

### 2i · 0672B · *In utrique parte templi … ut utraque populo* — the two forms are exchanged

### 2l · 0678A · *et eum sapientiae et consilio divina ascribentes tribuunt* — DECLINED, with the reason

abridgment removes words, it does not suspend grammar — so its oddity is reported, not excused.

## §3 · THE TWELVE `[var:]`, AND WHY EACH EARNED A PUBLIC CLAIM

Every one rests on `sources/vulgate/clementine-flat.txt`, cited by book chapter:verse. None
rests on recollection, and in none of them was Migne's wording replaced — the marker stands
*beside* the faithful rendering.

**a · 0665A · 1 Par 21:29.** Migne *eadem tempestate **erant** in **excelsis** Gabaon*; Clem.
*ea tempestate **erat** in **excelso** Gabaon*. Three changes at once, two of them number. It
earns the marker because the gloss introduces it as *Supra enim scriptum est* — a claim about
what stands written.

**b · 0665C · Jo 19:30.** Migne *emisit spiritum*; Clem. *tradidit spiritum*. **The word Migne
prints is Matthew's** (Mt 27:50 *emisit spiritum*) under a note reading *Joan. XIX*. A different
content verb in a proof-text adduced for the sixth day's completion.

**c · 0666A · Jo 1:33.** Migne ***videritis***, plural; Clem. ***videris***, singular. Under
Pattern 17 the English must move with it, and does: "Upon whom **you** shall see", not the
familiar "thou shalt see". ⚑ This is the 7a″ trap in its purest form — the remembered English is
singular, and nothing but the printed letters decides it.

**d · 0667B · 3 Rg 6:18.** Migne drops ***intrinsecus***, and the gloss it introduces is about
walls *clothed* on their inner face with cedar and gold — so the dropped word is the one the
exposition needs.

**e · 0668C · Rom 13:10.** Migne *Plenitudo **autem** legis est ***charitas***; Clem. *Plenitudo
**ergo** legis est ***dilectio***. Fired for *charitas*, not for *autem*: this range holds
*charitas* and *dilectio* apart everywhere (see §0), and the verse is adduced to prove that the
angels' twofold love **is** charity.

**f · 0668D · 2 Par 3:12.** Migne ***Tangebant** parietem*, plural; Clem. ***tangebat***,
singular. **The gloss is built on the plural** — *Duos aeque parietes alis suis **tangunt***,
*Extendunt … alas*, all the way down the paragraph. Marking it is what lets a reader see that
the exposition and the lemma agree with each other and not with the Vulgate.

**g · 0669A · Heb 1:14.** Migne *Omnes **enim** sunt … qui haereditatem **capiunt** salutis*;
Clem. ***Nonne** omnes sunt … qui haereditatem **capient** salutis**?*** Migne turns a rhetorical
question into a flat assertion and the future into a present. ⚑ **No question mark was supplied**
— Pattern 8 forbids it, and the whole point of the marker is that his sentence is not a question.

**h · 0673B · Heb 13:7.** Migne *quorum intuentes exitum conversationis **intueamini et** fidem*;
Clem. *… **imitamini** fidem*. "Behold ye also their faith" for "imitate their faith", in a gloss
whose next clause is *ut … et ipsi magni efficiantur* — becoming, not merely beholding.

**i · 0675C · Ps 113:21.** Migne *benedicit Dominus pusillis cum majoribus*; Clem. *Benedixit
omnibus qui timent Dominum, pusillis cum majoribus*. Present for perfect, and *Dominus* promoted
to subject because the glossator has taken *omnes qui timent* up into his own sentence
(*omnes quos timentes se et diligentes*). The tense is what earns the marker.

**j · 0676A · Phlp 1:23.** Migne ***Cupio** dissolvi et esse cum Christo*; Clem. ***desiderium
habens** dissolvi, et esse cum Christo*. A different verb, and the form every patristic citation
of this verse uses. Stated, not explained: Pattern 14 forbids speculating about **why** two
witnesses differ.

**k · 0676C · Mt 11:25.** Migne ***Confitebor** tibi, **Domine, pater** coeli et terrae,
**quoniam** abscondisti*; Clem. ***Confiteor** tibi, **Pater, Domine** caeli et terrae, **quia**
abscondisti*. Future for present, and the two vocatives exchanged.

**l · 0677C · Ps 83:8.** Migne *sicut palma florens **ascendit** de virtute in virtutem, **ut
videat** Deum deorum in Sion*; Clem. ***ibunt** de virtute in virtutem: **videbitur** Deus deorum
in Sion*. Active for passive, singular for plural, purpose for statement — three changes that
together make the Church the subject, which is exactly what the gloss needs her to be.

## §5 · THE TESTS, RUN, AND WHAT THEY RETURNED

**Test 1 (a `[sic:]` must not swallow the word it marks).** **Vacuously clean: zero `[sic:]`
fired in this range.** Run anyway on the two `[cj:]`, which are the only markers sitting inside a
clause: stripped of the bracket, "There was also the virgin of Aaron" and "that in the floor
there was nothing contrary to the law" both read complete, with subject, verb and object intact
(the fourth question — *does the clause still have the thing it acts on?* — asked and answered).
All six files were then stripped of every marker mechanically and swept for a stranded article or
preposition sitting on punctuation: **no hits** beyond the whitespace the stripping itself leaves.

**Test 2 + 2a (name the host of every printed negative).** All 53 printed negation tokens in the six
Latin chunks were extracted mechanically and each was matched to the single Latin word it
attaches to, then found on that word in the English. **One defect, found by this test and
repaired:** at **0675A** *Non enim in hoc altari **sanguis hostiarum** sed thymiama tantum
incenditur* had been rendered "For **not on this altar** is the blood of victims but only incense
burned" — the negative floated off its host onto the prepositional phrase, and the English read
well, which is what made it invisible. **The `sed thymiama tantum` correlative proves the host is
*sanguis hostiarum***, not *in hoc altari* and not the verb. Now: "For on this altar **not the
blood of victims but only incense** is burned." ⚑ Exactly the class 2a predicts, with a
grammatically plausible rival adjacent to the real host.

The trigger population 2a names (a negative on a verb with a quantifier or correlative nearby)
was walked one by one: *Nec enim **semper** … arctari* (0674A) and *non **semper** vasa … tenebantur*
(0674A) both take the negative on *semper* and are proved by their *sed* / *ubi* pivots ·
*non **tantum** in regione Jordanis, sed* and *non **solum** in Judaea, sed* (0674B) both take it on
the phrase, proved by *sed* · *non ad tectum templi, sed usque ad coenaculum* (0666D), phrase,
proved by *sed* · *non in oculto agant, sed exemplum … praeferunt* (0668A), phrase, proved by
*sed* · *nulli **tamen** absque peccato esse donat* (0675B), on *nulli* · *tanto **minus** interna
gaudia vident* (0675C), a comparative left uninverted ("by so much the less") · *nihil habeant
roboris … vel industriae* (0670D), on the two genitives, not on *habeant* — see 2s below.

**Test 2b (each anchor against the WORD it stands beside).** All 55 column anchors in the range
were checked against their Latin neighbour word, not merely for presence and order. Five sit
mid-construction and were split to match: *habendum* → "to be [0670C] held" · *dissolvi* → "be
[0671C] dissolved" · *texit eam* → "[0668B] he covered it" (inside the italic lemma, as Migne
sets it) · *est* in *id [0668C] est* · *construunt* → "they [0666B] build up". Four sit with no
space in the Latin (*[0665A]VERS. 10*, *[0667B]Notandum*, *[0671D]VERS. 5*, *[0676B]VERS. 13*,
*[0678A]VERS. 14*) and are reproduced without a space.

**Test 3 (punctuation by column band, never by total).** Colons, semicolons, question marks and
exclamation marks were compared band by band across all 55 anchor-partitioned bands in the six
chunks. **Two mismatches were found and repaired** — a supplied semicolon at **0666A** (Migne has
a comma before *de quo dicitur*) and a supplied semicolon at **0677D** (Migne has a comma after
*Holocaustum totum incensum dicitur*). Final state: **zero mismatched bands**. The single
question mark (0667C, *nisi aliquid mysticum ostendat?*) is in its own band in both twins, and
the lower-case *sed* Migne prints after it is preserved.

**Test 4 (does it ASSERT what he asserts?).** Every paragraph was read back against the Latin
with the counts already known clean. It returned **2j** (three sentences that stop without a
verb or a stop, none of them closed), **2k** (a colon introducing nothing), **2l** (the
resumption tail that will not construe — caught precisely by refusing to let "the abridgment made
it odd" excuse it), **2b** (*testamentum*), and the two `[cj:]` sites of 2e. None of these is
visible to any count-based check: every one of them is present, in order, at parity.

**The `usque ad` count, since the brief named it.** The Latin of these six chunks carries **28
tokens containing `usque`**. **18 are the abridgment formula**, each in its own italic span, and
all 18 are `*as far as*` in the English with no variation. **Five are ordinary** and were decided
one at a time from what stands on either side: *usquequo aedificaretur templum* (0665A, "until")
· *sed usque ad coenaculum inferius pervenerunt* (0666D, "but as far as") · *deinde usque ad
supremum templi tectum* (0666D, "then as far as") · *ad quam usque Jesus persecutus est* (0677B,
"as far as which") · *a fluvio Cophene usque ad regionem Indiae* (0678A, "as far as the region of

*utriusque populi* (0669A), *cujusque justi* (0670B), *utriusque Testamenti* (0671A), *cujusque
electi* (0677C), *cujusque temporis* (0678A). ⚑ **No lemma's own words were swallowed by the
formula reading** — the two that could have been, 0666D's twin *usque ad* pair, sit in Migne's

### 2s · 0670D · the *nihil* with three genitives — how the host was fixed

### How a bare lemma was decided — the rule applied, since the brief warns against manufacturing an inconsistency

Two classes, and they are different (brief, "name the control's class"):

- **A lemma that yields a readable English phrase naming the thing glossed is rendered as it stands** —

**E1a · 0684A · *reperetur* — a non-word with TWO candidate reconstructions, and the choice is recorded**

`et tanto durius **reperetur** ad poenam, quanto securius viveret in culpa` (the tail of the Gregory
abridgment). *reperetur* is a form of no Latin verb; measured against the whole corpus of `src/latin/`,
**it occurs exactly once — here** — while *reperiretur*'s family runs to hundreds of well-formed tokens
and *reservare* to twenty-six. Two reconstructions were weighed:

1. ***reperiretur*** — "would be found". Two letters dropped (*reper**ir**etur*), and the imperfect
   subjunctive matches *viveret* in the answering clause.
2. ***reservaretur*** — "would be reserved". This is Gregory's own idiom (*ad poenam reservare*), and
   idiomatically the stronger reading.

**Reading 1 was taken and rendered "would be found unto punishment."** Why reading 2 lost: a non-word is
a *transcription* accident, and the criterion for a transcription accident is orthographic distance, not
idiom — 1 is two dropped letters, 2 is five substituted ones. **A plate read settles it in one look**, and
if the plate has *reservaretur* the English needs one word changed. ⚑ **Plate candidate — 0684A.**

### E2 · 0678D · the arithmetic does not work, and both words are REAL. Rendered as printed.

*Senarius per senarium multiplicatus, sexaginta facit. Centenarius quoque sexies ductus **centum et sex**
reddit.* Six by six is thirty-six, not sixty; a hundred taken six times is six hundred, not a hundred and
six. The lemma above is *Sexcenta.* (9:13, *sexcenta sexaginta sex talenta*), so the passage is deriving
**600 + 60 + 6**, and the two conjectures that yield it are visible: *per **denarium*** in the first
sentence (6 × 10 = 60) — **and Migne's very next sentence is *Denarius mandatorum significat
perfectionem***, a word with nothing else in the paragraph to attach to — and ***sexcenta* reddit** in the
second. **Neither is adopted and no marker fires:** every word printed is a real, well-formed Latin word,
which the 2026-08-23 addendum refuted five times out of five as a ground for patching. Rendered exactly:
"The senary multiplied by the senary makes sixty. The centenary also, drawn out six times, yields a
hundred and six." The conjectures are here **by decision, not by oversight**.

### E3 · 0680B–C · one sentence printed twice, with a different first word. NO `[d:]`, and why.

*Senarius vero praesens tempus significat, in quo apostolica doctrina Novi Testamenti populum nutrit* …
[two sentences later] … *Duodenarius vero praesens tempus significat, in quo apostolica auctoritas populum
gentium nutrit Novi Testamenti doctrina.* The verse is 9:25, whose second number is **twelve**
(*curruum equitumque duodecim millia*), so *Duodenarius* is the word the argument needs; and the first
copy **interrupts** the quadragenary exposition, which resumes immediately after it with *Bene ergo per
quadragenarium numerantur equi Salomonis*.

**Pattern 11a was weighed and NOT applied.** Its case (11613 @1230D) is an eye-skip: the same clause
re-set, adjacent, one copy ending in a word copied from the line before. Here the two copies are **two
sentences apart, and their tails differ in wording as well as in the first word**, so they are not one run
set twice, and a `[d:]` would be a claim about compositor behaviour I cannot support from the file. ⛔ And
the thing 11a exists to prevent is not at risk: nothing in either copy reverses the argument, and
*senarius* as a figure of perfection is used **in this very chunk** (*In senario bonorum operum
perfectio*). Both are rendered as printed. ⚑ **Plate candidate — 0680B**, on the narrow question whether
Migne prints *Senarius* there.

### E4 · 0687D · *priusquam* where the sense wants *postquam*. Rendered as printed, NO `[cj:]`.

*Sed **priusquam** per superbiam illicita tentavit, lepra percussus nominis et honoris offuscavit
dignitatem.* Ozias was struck with leprosy **after** the presumption, not before, and Migne's own gloss on
26:16 four lines later narrates it in that order (*Sed postquam Zacharias obiit … unde lepra in fronte
percussus est*). **Declined**: *priusquam* is a real, well-formed word occupying its slot, the sentence
construes as printed if the clause is read as bounding the preceding *quandiu recte egit*, and Pattern
18's bar is a negation or a correlative, which this is not. Rendered "But before he attempted unlawful
things through pride, being struck with leprosy he darkened the dignity of his name and of his honour."

### E5 · 0680C–D · *usque **ab** conversionem* — a preposition that cannot govern its case. NO `[cj:]`.

*ab inchoatione sui in Jordane baptismatis **usque ab conversionem** omnium gentium ad fidem Christi
redundat gratia potestatis.* The frame is *ab inchoatione … usque ad*, "from the beginning … even to";
*ab* takes no accusative, so *ab conversionem* is not Latin, and the English cannot show the difference
between the two prepositions except by rendering the one printed. **Rendered "even from the conversion of

contradiction is Migne's and is what the edition exists to show. **`[cj:]` weighed and declined**, on the
nearest control there is: **11535 @1360C, *ab* for *ad subdiaconatum*, direction reversed, declined and
closed by the 18a census.** The conjecture is *ad*; it lives here.

### E6 · 0681A · *quos lapides pro Deo coluerunt* — the relative's antecedent, named

## §Z · THE BLIND POLARITY READ (step 4a), AND WHAT IT REPAIRED

Run 2026-08-25 by a reader **fenced from this file**, per the runbook: given the cruces first, every
agent in the 2026-07-28 sweep returned "all already logged" and found nothing new, while blind
readers on the same works found four live defects. All 11 chunks read against their Latin twins.

⭐⭐ **EVERY PRINTED NEGATION IN THE WORK WAS CORRECT AND CORRECTLY CARRIED. Not one polarity
defect.** That is now the fifth consecutive book of this series where the name of the class has
proved to be something else. **The yield was the REFERENT**, and it is worth stating as the finding
rather than as a list: what English cannot leave implicit and Latin can.

**Repaired, seven sites:**

1. **0672B — the grace of confirmation had become the grace of baptism.** *cum post ablutionem
   baptismi gratia implentur spirituali*: the genitive *baptismi* belongs to *ablutionem*, and
   *gratia … spirituali* is the ablative with *implentur*. Our English lifted the genitive off its
   noun and fused the ablative into it — "filled with the grace of baptism that is spiritual".
   ⛔ **It collapsed the exact distinction the paragraph exists to draw**, which its own next
   sentences spell out: *Lavatur … aqua baptismi* against *per impositionem manus episcopi, accipit
   donum Spiritus sancti*. Now "after the washing of baptism they are filled with spiritual grace."
2. **0686B — a supplied pronoun made Athalia a man**, in a sentence that names her in its own
   middle clause: "Not that **he** then had sons … **Athalia** endeavoured … he calls **his** sons".
   *molita est* is feminine, and the lemma's *ejus* is hers (2 Par 24:7). The lemma itself carried
   it too, "*And his sons*". Now *And her sons* / she / her.
3. **0689D — a participle bound to the wrong noun.** *Contra paganos et haereticos pugnantes* became
   "Against pagans and heretics **who fight**", making the caution owed to a subset of heretics and
   evaporating the reason for veiling the mysteries — which is why the proof-text is *Nolite sanctum
   dare canibus*. Now "For those fighting against pagans and heretics".
4. **0692A–B — agency inverted.** *se subditos convertunt ad impietatem* read as "turn themselves as
   subjects", which **removes the subjects from a sentence about rulers corrupting them**. Migne's
   own Hieronymus excerpt six lines later is the control: *praepositorum depravati exemplis cedunt
   cum ipsis in praedam diaboli*. Rendered "turn those subject to them to impiety" **with a
   `[cj: *se*; read *sibi*]`** — the printed word construes, so the reading is shown, not smuggled.
5. **0688A — the wrong man pleased God.** *placuit Deo* sat as a bare "he" immediately after
   "Zacharias the priest … lived", and its subject is **Ozias** (2 Par 26:5 is the control: Ozias
   sought the Lord *in diebus Zachariae*). Named.
6–7. **Two numeral lemmata contracted to what Migne prints** — see §Z2.

### §Z2 · THE LEMMA-EXPANSION FINDING — RAISED, ACTED ON, AND THEN WITHDRAWN

⛔⛔ **THE CORRECTION STANDS FIRST, because a false contraction shipped for part of this session and
the reasoning that produced it was inverted.** The reader flagged five lemmata printing longer in
English than in the Latin twin, each addition exactly the Vulgate continuation. Two of them —
*Centum.* and *Ducenti.* — were contracted to "A hundred." and "Two hundred." **That was wrong, and
both have been reverted.**

**What the plate did and did not settle.** Gallica `bpt6k5505319w` (map `f = (column + 1) / 2`,
calibrated against the printed corner numbers) shows **f333 a bare *Centum.* and f339 a bare
*Ducenti.***. That answered the reader's honest caveat — the words are **not** dropped from our
Latin, our Latin is right — and it answered nothing else. **Whether a bare lemma should be expanded
in English is a question about convention 3, and no plate can decide it.**

⛔ **And convention 3 decides it the other way.** Both lemmata are **clips of two-word numerals**:
2 Par 1:17 reads *equum **centum quinquaginta*** and 2 Par 8:10 *ducenti quinquaginta*. A lemma whose
governed word is missing is a **fragment**, and the Ruth pilot's convention 3 renders a fragment as
the phrase it stands for, not as a word-for-word stump. The settled ruling on a work that treats the
same bare lemma two ways is explicit: **resolve it by EXPANDING the stump, not by contracting its
twin.**

⛔⛔ **The gloss argument was backwards, and it is the part worth remembering.** The contraction was
justified on the ground that Migne's gloss supplies the figure itself — *Qui sunt ter quinquageni*
under *Centum.*, *Centenarius … Quinquagenarius vero* under *Ducenti.* — so that an expanded English
"pre-empts the gloss's own arithmetic." **It does the opposite.** *Ter quinquageni* is **thrice
fifty**, which is 150, not 100: the gloss is not withholding the figure, it is **confirming** it, and
it is the proof that Migne's *Centum.* means *centum quinquaginta*. Contracting the English left the
lemma saying **one hundred** on a page whose gloss immediately says **one hundred and fifty**.

**So all five were licensed expansions and none was a defect.** *Carduus qui,* etc. (carrying Migne's
own *etc.*), *Obturaverunt cunctos.*, *Ad quam Deus.* — the second stint's deliberate, stated
applications of convention 3 — and the two numerals with them.

⚑ **What survives as a real observation is smaller and duller: the two stints of this work state
different rules for a bare cardinal.** The second stint ruled that an English cardinal stands as a
substantive so nothing is elided (*Sexcenta.* "Six hundred.", *Quadraginta.* "Forty."), which is
right **for a complete numeral** and does not reach a clipped one. Migne's *Sexcenta.* is the whole
of 2 Par 1:17's six hundred; his *Centum.* is half of its hundred and fifty. **Same-looking lemma,
different class** — which is the brief's own warning that a control must be the same class as the
thing it controls. No further change was made on either account.

⚑ **The general lesson, and the reason this is written out rather than quietly reverted: a blind
reader's finding can be right about the FACT and wrong about the RULE.** The five English lemmata
really did print words the Latin twin does not — that observation was correct and worth having. What
it could not supply is whether the convention licenses them, and acting on the fact without checking
the rule turned three sound renderings into candidates and two into errors.
