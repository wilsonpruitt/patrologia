## CHAPTER XV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *A mild answer breaketh wrath.* ⟦s2⟧ He who humbly receives the words of one rebuking him, the guilt which he has borne already draws near to pardon. ⟦s3⟧ He who kicks back at one reproving him provokes against himself the wrath of the judge.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Belcheth out folly.* ⟦s2⟧ By perverting the Scripture, by setting his own opinions before authority; like the foolish philosophers, as Porphyry and Julian, who against [1098A] the teachers of the Church poured forth the streams of their folly.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *The fool laugheth at the discipline of his father.* ⟦s2⟧ Every sinner is a fool who, given over to earthly acts, foresees nothing to come: he laughs at discipline while he disparages the divine correction.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *The heart of fools is unlike.* ⟦s2⟧ Either unlike to itself, by divers thoughts, and it never remains the same. ⟦s3⟧ Against which it is said of Anna: *And her countenance was no more changed into divers looks.*

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Evil doctrine. ⟦s2+3⟧ He that shall break one of these least commandments, and shall so teach men, is the least in the kingdom of heaven.* [var: Vulg. Matth. 5:19 *Qui ergo solverit unum de mandatis istis minimis, et docuerit sic homines, minimus vocabitur in regno caelorum*, "he shall be called the least in the kingdom of heaven"] Yet to such men, if they have been corrected, the way back to pardon lies open: but if they receive not reproofs, they shall utterly die.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1098B]VERS. 15.-- *All the days.* ⟦s2⟧ All the time of the people of God, etc., *as far as* they are delighted as with a continual banquet.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *It is better to be called to herbs.* ⟦s2⟧ For it is far more profitable to keep the innocence of a simple life with charity, etc., *as far as, depart from me, ye workers of iniquity.*

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *Of the slothful,* whom it irks to work justice, etc., *as far as* but because this does not hinder good men, he adds:

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The way of the just is without stumbling-block.* ⟦s2⟧ For the just, whatever adversity they have found in their manner of life, do not stumble, because by the leap of eternal hope and of inward contemplation they leap over adversities.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *A man rejoiceth.* ⟦s2⟧ It is human to rejoice in [1098C] a sentence as though prudently uttered. ⟦s3⟧ But the truly wise man diligently inquires not only what he speaks, but also the fitness of the place, and of the time, and of the person to whom he speaks.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *From the lowest hell,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The first hell is the blindness of the sinning soul, and of the soul lacking the knowledge of the true light, of which: *They go down alive into hell [n: (Psal. LIV)]* [var: Vulg. Psal. 54:16 *descendant in infernum viventes*, "let them go down alive into hell," a wish, not a statement].

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Of the widow the Church,* namely of his own spouse, for whose life he vouchsafed to die.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *Shall be established:* in the judgment, when he shall render worthy rewards not only to deeds, but also to all words.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *The light of the eyes rejoiceth the soul.* ⟦s2⟧ It rightly delights the human mind, etc., *as far as* for so [1098D] the work glows the more.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The light of the eyes.* ⟦s2⟧ Another translation: *The eye seeing good things delighteth good hearts,* namely the good things of the Lord in the land of the living, which, because it beholds them in the eye of the mind, it is deservedly delighted in heart, although it be afflicted outwardly.

## CHAPTER XVI.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 1.-- *It is of man to prepare the mind.* [var: Vulg. Prov. 16:1 *Hominis est animam praeparare, et Domini gubernare linguam*, "to prepare the soul, and of the Lord to govern the tongue"] How then is it of man to prepare the mind, and of God to govern the tongue? but he signifies the time of persecution, of which it is said, *Take no thought how, or what you shall speak: for it is given in that hour, what you shall speak [n: (Matth. X)]* [var: Vulg. Matth. 10:19 *aut quid loquamini: dabitur enim vobis in illa hora, quid loquamini*, "or what you shall speak: for it shall be given you in that hour"].

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *All the ways.* ⟦s2⟧ So the actions of all good men, [1099A] namely, and what evil men think in secret, he discerns with a sure balance, whence he subjoins.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Reveal to the Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ We reveal our works when, in all the things that we do, we call back into memory him whom we know that nothing escapes, and beg his help: and then he directs not only our works, but our thoughts.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *All things for his own sake,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ There was no cause of working for the Lord save his will alone, namely that he might show his goodness to the rational creature, which he would make blessed for ever: but him who of his own accord forsook the good of his condition, that is, the devil, with his followers, he condemned with just severity.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *It is an abomination.* ⟦s2⟧ Whosoever attributes to himself [1099B] the good which he does, even if he seems to work no evil with his hands, loses the innocence of the heart, wherein he has set himself before the giver of good things: and therefore the Lord abominates such a man, whom he sees to be ungrateful to him in his benefits.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *When they shall have pleased the Lord.* ⟦s2⟧ So great is the holiness of the divine religion, etc., *as far as* but they were even converted to the truth of the faith.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Better is a little with justice,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is better in a simple manner of life to serve God devoutly than to abound in many virtues and to despise the deeds of one's neighbours.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Divination is in the lips of the king.* ⟦s2⟧ What other king does not err in judgment, etc., *as far as* who should fill the mouths of his prophets.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1099C] *Weight and balance.* ⟦s2⟧ The stones of the just are strong, etc., *as far as* he distributes the measure of faith and of the graces.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *The will of kings,* etc., that is, of the just. ⟦s2+3⟧ *Many kings and prophets have desired to see the things which you have seen, and have not seen them.* [var: Vulg. Luc. 10:24 *multi prophetae et reges voluerunt videre quae vos videtis, et non viderunt*, "many prophets and kings … the things that you see"] For these both resist vices and are thronged about with the service of the virtues, as with attendants. ⟦s4⟧ For the kings of the earth often detest just lips, as Herod did John.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *The messengers of death.* ⟦s2⟧ The angels of Satan, heretics, etc., *as far as* whom it has befallen to have given offence by speaking foolishly.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *He shall pacify.* [n: (HIER., de morte Fabiolae. )] David purged homicide and adultery by a hunger of seven days.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *In the cheerfulness of the king's countenance is life.* ⟦s2⟧ Whosoever deserve to see the cheerful countenance of Christ, to them [1099D] it shall be given to live with him for ever. ⟦s3⟧ Otherwise also *all flesh shall see the salvation of God:* but then the just shall see him appeased, the reprobate angered.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And his clemency is as the latter rain.* ⟦s2⟧ The latter rain in Judea has been wont even to this day to come when the harvests are already ripe, etc., *as far as* but the watering of the Lord above does it.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *A fountain of life.* ⟦s2⟧ Because that preacher truly opens to his hearers the ways of life, etc., *as far as* they despise with a foolish heart the good things which they say.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Well-ordered words are a honeycomb,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Many through eloquence, etc., *as far as* which proceed from the inward sweetness of the soul.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *The soul of him that laboureth laboureth for himself,* etc. [1100A] It is plain according to the letter, etc., *as far as* because he must needs do the good things which he says.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 27.-- *The unwise man diggeth evil.* [var: Vulg. Prov. 16:27 *Vir impius fodit malum*, "the wicked man diggeth evil"] This can be understood especially of the heretic, etc., *as far as* whence James: *The tongue inflameth the wheel of our nativity, being set on fire by hell [n: (Jacob. III)]*.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *An unjust man allureth his friend,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ This can be taken of the heretic and of every favourer of evil men: *for the sinner is praised in the desires of his soul, and the unjust man is blessed.*

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *He that with astonished eyes deviseth,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is more grievous to accomplish evil than to do it, etc., *as far as* that he may not see what retribution of his evils follows.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *The patient man is better than the valiant,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It is a lesser [1100B] victory to storm cities, etc., *as far as* and lays it low by the humility of patience.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Lots are cast into the lap.* ⟦s2⟧ As lots are cast into the lap, etc., *as far as* as it were out of the lap of the divine examination.

## CHAPTER XVII.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Better is a morsel.* ⟦s2⟧ It is better to do small good things with charity, without the knowledge of preaching (for this is to be a dry morsel) than to shine the more with virtues, with an admixture of discord.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *The crown of old men,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The old men, the patriarchs, the prophets, who are celebrated with divine praise by the sons of their sons, that is, by the successors of the apostles: [1100C] and it is the glory of the preachers of the New Testament, who have deserved to be the sons of the ancient fathers.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *A most acceptable gem.* ⟦s2⟧ He who faithfully awaits the rewards to come, etc., *as far as* prudently understanding, *That to them that love God all things work together unto good [n: (Rom. VIII)]*.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *It is better to meet a bear,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ It was easier for the holy teachers to meet the fury of heathendom, etc., *as far as,* and this is often more easily done than to call a heretic back to the right faith, or to bring back to good work a catholic acting perversely.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *He that letteth out water.* ⟦s2⟧ To let out water, etc., *as far as* whence on the contrary: *He that imposeth silence on a fool appeaseth wrath.*

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *What doth it profit.* ⟦s2⟧ What does it profit the unbelieving people [1100D] of the Jews, etc., *as far as* since he has neglected to have the wisdom of godly action?

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *At all times.* ⟦s2⟧ He who truly loves God keeps his love at all times; nor in the straitness of suffering does he forsake him whom he confessed in the tranquillity of peace.

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *A foolish man will clap his hands.* ⟦s2⟧ He is a fool who, when he takes up his brother's soul to be ruled, glories in his own acts, and does not rather bestow upon him with humble compassion whatever help of salvation he can.

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A joyful mind.* ⟦s2⟧ He who rejoices in the inward consolation of the Holy Spirit, etc., *as far as* has lost the glory of love.

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1101A]VERS. 24.-- *In the face of the prudent.* ⟦s2⟧ In the face of Christ shone the wisdom of the Godhead, etc., *as far as* they search it out with their whole intent.

⟦p48⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *It is not good to do harm to the just.* ⟦s2⟧ And it can be taken both of the judge and prince of the ages himself, who for our salvation willed to be struck, and of all the rulers of the Church, who have either been stripped of their goods by the wicked, or been consumed by death itself.
