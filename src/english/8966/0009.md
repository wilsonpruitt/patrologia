## CHAPTER XXI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *As the divisions.* ⟦s2⟧ The heart of all men, not only of the king, is in the hand of the Lord, because *in his hand are all the ends of the earth.* ⟦s3⟧ But by the king he calls every holy man, who knows how to conquer in himself the wars of the vices and to root out the shoots of malice. ⟦s4⟧ For as the Lord fills with the divisions of the waters the bounds of the lands and of the air together, and covers also with waters the higher parts of the heavens, *So the heart of the king whithersoever he will* he inclines, because as he bestows the divisions of graces according to his own will, [1104C] both on angels and on men; so he renders the hearts of the saints worthy of whatsoever gifts he will. ⟦s5⟧ And let the Pelagian be silent, because without the grace of God there is no place for salvation.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *The perverse way.* ⟦s2⟧ Justly before the Lord, etc., *as far as* he rightly carries out what he took from his origin by nature.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *He that stoppeth his ear.* ⟦s2⟧ Generally, etc., *as far as* nor worthy of the hearing of the divine mercy.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Terror to them that work iniquity.* ⟦s2⟧ Because they know that it is forbidden by God; although they are carnally delighted by the perpetrating of their own wantonness, yet they do not lack the inward terror of the mind, because they do not doubt that they shall suffer for ever for the things which they did ill in time.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1104D]VERS. 16.-- *In the assembly of giants.* ⟦s2⟧ Of demons, etc., *as far as* the judge himself bearing witness, *Depart from me, ye cursed, into everlasting fire, which was prepared for the devil and his angels [n: (Matth. XXV)]*.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *The wicked is given for the just.* ⟦s2⟧ The just man and the upright are the same, etc., *as far as* when they might much more easily have done so, they had no care of wisdom.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 20.-- *A desirable treasure.* ⟦s2⟧ The reprobate not only do not attain the virtues, etc., *as far as* whence, *Fear ye not them that kill the body,* etc. [n: (Matth. X.)]

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *The city of the strong.* ⟦s2⟧ Namely the world, etc., *as far as* ecclesiastical discipline refutes.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1105A]VERS. 28.-- *A lying witness.* ⟦s2⟧ He who testifies that he serves God, etc., *as far as* through the justice of the judge he receives the palm after the victory.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *The wicked man.* ⟦s2⟧ Judas, etc., *as far as* which by denying he transgressed.

## CHAPTER XXII.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *It is better.* ⟦s2⟧ For if a man should gain the whole world, he would deservedly despise it all, that his name might be written in the heavens, and his memory be fixed eternal among the saints and the angels.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *A good name.* ⟦s2⟧ Which is praised by the testimony of the faithful, not of the unskilled crowd, whence: *If I yet pleased men, I should not be the servant of Christ!*

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Good favour.* ⟦s2⟧ Because a man is praised by the good for good works: nay rather, for the gifts of merits conferred upon him is glorified [1105B] the Father who is in the heavens.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *The rich and the poor.* ⟦s2⟧ Neither honour the rich man for his riches, nor despise the poor man for his want: because they are a divine work, and made to the image and to the work of God.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *The prudent man saw the evil.* ⟦s2⟧ Many of the princes believed, etc., *as far as* and they obtain them in acts held in common.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *The end of modesty.* ⟦s2⟧ Because doubtless the perfection of the virtues is, etc., *as far as* lest perchance he deserve to be subdued with punishments.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Riches and glory.* ⟦s2⟧ The end of modesty is the fear of the Lord, glory and life, because the perfection of the virtues in this life is that we should chastely fear God; the end of the virtues in the world to come is that we should receive the riches of the promised inheritance, the glory of the heavenly kingdom, [1105C] and life everlasting.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *It is a proverb.* ⟦s2⟧ The jar will long keep the smell wherewith, when new, it was once steeped. ⟦s3⟧ And Greek history relates that Alexander the Great could not be free either of the manners and gait of Leonides his tutor, or of the vices wherewith he had been infected as a little boy.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *He that soweth iniquity.* ⟦s2⟧ It is plain, because he who teaches iniquity either by word or by example shall suffer vengeance. ⟦s3⟧ But because there follows:

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And the rod of his wrath.* ⟦s2⟧ He seems to speak of that iniquity, etc., *as far as* whence: *For all that take the sword shall perish by the sword [n: (Matth. XXVI)]*.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *The slothful will say,* unto good works, etc., *as far as* or, as it did other just men, may consume him with torments.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1105D]VERS. 16.-- *He that oppresseth by calumny.* ⟦s2⟧ It is plain, because the rapacious lose both what they take away from the poor and what they seemed to possess justly, when in the judgment they receive punishments for the things which they have done.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *My son.* ⟦s2⟧ Thus far the title of the parables of Solomon which was set above, etc., *as far as* and he charges him to apply his heart to the understanding of the things which the wise speak.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Be not a friend.* ⟦s2⟧ But generally, etc., *as far as* they begin to imitate.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To an angry man, neither walk thou with a man.* ⟦s2⟧ To anyone, chiefly to a Jew, who raged with a mad mind against Christ and his disciples, from whose friendship they are kept back who take care to be saved: lest by their [1106A] neighbourhood they also should perish, which it has been proved could happen by the case of Judas.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *Be not with them that fasten hands.* ⟦s2⟧ Be not with them, etc., *as far as* wherewith thou didst seem to be clothed?

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *Pass not over.* ⟦s2⟧ Do not thou pass over the bounds of the catholic faith, which the teachers established from the beginning.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Hast thou seen a man swift in his work? before.* ⟦s2⟧ Whomsoever thou shalt have seen swift in his work, etc., *as far as* he took heed that their error was to be set down.

## CHAPTER XXIII.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *When thou shalt sit.* ⟦s2⟧ It is to be read allegorically, etc., *as far as* whence he well subjoins:

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Desire not,* that is, desire not [1106B] to listen to the words of him who deceives his hearers by the sweetness of a lie.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Set a measure.* ⟦s2⟧ Of which above: *Pass not over the ancient bounds which the fathers have set,* namely the bounds of the faith and of the truth given by the fathers.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Eat not,* that is, speak not of the Scriptures with a heretic, who envies the salvation of men, choosing rather to deceive than to be corrected; for as the soothsayer and the guesser of dreams judges of the things which he does not know; so the heretic interprets as he pleases the things which he does not understand in the Scriptures?

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Eat,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Learn, says he, secure, the things which I say, and do the things which I teach; whereas in the things which he teaches he himself has no [1106C] sure faith, knowing that he has invented what he teaches.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *The meats which thou hadst eaten.* ⟦s2⟧ The perverse meanings which thou hadst learned from heretics must needs be either forsaken by thee, who art to be corrected by penance, or else thou must bear punishments for them after death, and lose the words of confession wherewith thou hadst thought that they, as they preached, were humbly to be favoured.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *In the ears. ⟦s2+3⟧ Give not that which is holy to dogs, neither cast thou pearls before swine.* [var: Vulg. Matth. 7:6 *Nolite dare sanctum canibus: neque mittatis margaritas vestras ante porcos*, "neither cast ye your pearls" — plural, and *vestras*] As he admonished thee not to listen to the doctrines of heretics: so now, that thou shouldst not, seduced by the love of vain glory, thrust the secrets of truth everywhere upon unclean men.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Touch not,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ He calls the Lord the kinsman of little ones and of orphans, etc., *as far as* he who by disquieting them has hurt their good manner of life [1106D] does not escape the judgment of the Lord.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Let not thy heart envy sinners.* ⟦s2⟧ If all the day, etc., *as far as* whence in the Apocalypse: *Be thou faithful unto death, and I will give thee the crown of life [n: (Apoc. II)]*.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 20.-- *Be not in the feasts of sinners.* [var: Vulg. Prov. 23:20 *Noli esse in conviviis potatorum*, "in the feasts of great drinkers"] To bring flesh together for eating is, in the converse of disparagement, to speak by turns the vices of one's neighbours: of whose punishment it is presently subjoined: *Because they give themselves to drinking,* etc.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *They that give their shares shall be consumed.* ⟦s2⟧ A share, a contribution, etc., *as far as* the languor of their own detraction has seized.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *Who hath woe, whose.* ⟦s2⟧ He asks by disputing, etc., *as far as* whereof he says to the Jews: *You are of your father the devil [n: (Joan. VIII)]*.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1107A] *Who hath quarrels? who hath ditches? ⟦s2⟧ Quarrels,* because he keeps not concord, etc., *as far as* and very often he falls into the ditch, of which: *For a harlot is a deep ditch,* etc.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Who hath wounds without cause?* because many, having drunk too much, have received wounds through swelling, which they suffered from no cause of the sword.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Who hath the undermining of the eyes?* [var: Vulg. Prov. 23:29 *cui suffusio oculorum?*, "who hath redness of eyes?"] because the immoderate draught of wine brings in the blindness both of the outward and of the inward sight.

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *Is it not to them.* ⟦s2⟧ He does not forbid the drinking of wine for necessity, but the tarrying over wine beyond the time and the profit, and the striving at cups to be drained, whence Isaias: *Woe to you that are mighty to drink wine, and stout men at mingling drunkenness [n: (Isa. V)]*.

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 33.-- *Thy eyes shall behold.* ⟦s2⟧ It is almost a natural [1107B] vice, after wine, to covet a woman in the heart, foulness of words also going with it.

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 34.-- *And thou shalt be as one sleeping.* ⟦s2⟧ In the midst of the sea they sleep, etc., *as far as* he loses the zeal of carefulness.

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 35.-- *They have beaten me.* ⟦s2⟧ It is the voice of one struck and sleeping, etc., *as far as* that he may always be made drunk with pleasures.
