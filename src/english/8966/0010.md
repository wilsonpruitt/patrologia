## CHAPTER XXIV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Emulate not evil men.* ⟦s2⟧ Desire not to imitate those whom thou seest entangled in the vices aforesaid; but take care to bestow thy labour upon wisdom, whence he subjoins: *By wisdom shall a house be built.*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *A wise man.* ⟦s2⟧ Not every strong man is wise, [1107C] but the contrary: because although he be weak in body, if wisdom be present, he is altogether strong, and overcomes the contests of the adversary, that is, of the devil.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Wisdom is too high for a fool.* ⟦s2⟧ The fool cannot ascend unto wisdom, because he lies in the lowest places of the vices; because although in the present he thinks that he knows somewhat, in the gate, that is, in the going forth of this life, he shall find that he was nothing.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *He that deviseth to do evil.* ⟦s2⟧ He discloses whom he calls a fool: not one slow of wit, but him who sins even by thinking.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *If thou despair in the day of distress.* ⟦s2⟧ Nothing is more execrable than despair: and he that has had it loses constancy both in the common labours of this life and, what is worse, in the contest of the faith.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1107D]VERS. 11.-- *Deliver them that are led to death.* ⟦s2⟧ Deliver them that are deceived by heretics, etc., *as far as* if those about to perish of cold, refresh thou with food, with raiment.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Eat, my son.* ⟦s2⟧ He compares the doctrine of wisdom to honey and to the honeycomb, etc., *as far as* and it is perceived with delay.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Lie not in wait.* ⟦s2⟧ Seek not occasion, etc., *as far as* whence he subjoins:

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *For the just shall fall seven times.* ⟦s2⟧ The just man is said also to fall, etc., *as far as* they so consent to sin that they repent not.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *When thy enemy shall fall.* ⟦s2⟧ Above he forbade snares against the just man, etc., *as far as* lest, when thou art fallen into sin, he, coming to himself again, be healed.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1108A]VERS. 21.-- *Fear the Lord, my son.* ⟦s2⟧ By the Lord and the king the Father and the Son can be understood, who are to be worshipped with the same honour of divinity: *For he that honoureth not the Son, honoureth not the Father, who sent him,* etc. [n: (Joan. III)].

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *With backbiters.* ⟦s2⟧ With those, namely, etc., *as far as* and of the hearers that listen to them.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *These things also to the wise.* ⟦s2⟧ When some reprobate is snatched from the world by a sudden destruction, etc., *as far as* or that through fear there be never a departing from the way of truth.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *He shall kiss the lips.* ⟦s2⟧ A kiss is a sign of peace and of love, etc., *as far as* converted unto better things.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *Prepare thy work without.* ⟦s2⟧ When the work is prepared, to till the field [1108B] diligently, etc., *as far as* within, the whole fabric of the virtues be destroyed, the hunger of good increasing.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *I passed by the field of the slothful man,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ To pass through the field and the vineyard of the slothful and foolish man, etc., *as far as* or every negligent man loses it by the persuasion of the devil.

## CHAPTER XXV.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *These also are parables.* ⟦s2⟧ From here he begins to speak in a new order, etc., *as far as* namely such as seem apt for figures.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *The glory of God.* ⟦s2⟧ The glory of the Lord, etc., *as far as* whence he calls himself rather the son of man than the Son of God.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *To search out.* ⟦s2⟧ The speech, etc., *as far as* for straightway he heard: *Blessed art thou, Simon Barjona, because flesh,* etc.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1108C]VERS. 3.-- *The heaven above and the earth beneath.* ⟦s2⟧ As the height of heaven and the depth of the earth cannot be known by men, so the knowledge of the apostles and of the prophets, who knew the secret things of the Divinity by the revealing of the Spirit, transcends our frailty.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Take away impiety.* ⟦s2⟧ Take away impiety, etc., *as far as* lest, being rigid through arrogance, they incur the hatred of the people.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Appear not glorious.* ⟦s2⟧ As was set forth above, etc., *as far as* whence: *Be you humbled under the mighty hand of God, that he may exalt you in the time of visitation [n: (I Petr. III)]*.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Golden apples.* ⟦s2⟧ The sacred words, etc., *as far as* he lays open, the veil of the letter being removed.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *A golden earring shining with pearls.* [var: Vulg. Prov. 25:12 *Inauris aurea, et margaritum fulgens*, "a golden earring, and a shining pearl" — two ornaments, where Migne's genitive makes one] [1108D] Rightly the humble hearer, etc., *as far as* and he annexes the more pleasing brightness of a burning gem.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *As the cold of snow in the day of harvest.* ⟦s2⟧ It seems unseasonable to snow in the time of harvest, etc., *as far as* he takes his rest by acting prudently.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *A boastful man.* ⟦s2⟧ The same seems to be, *Neither flatter any man with thy lips.* ⟦s3⟧ But that is the same which the Apostle says: *Having an appearance of godliness, but denying the truth thereof [n: (II Tim. III)]*. [var: Vulg. 2 Tim. 3:5 *habentes speciem quidem pietatis, virtutem autem ejus abnegantes*, "the power thereof"]

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Thou hast found honey, eat what is enough for thee.* ⟦s2⟧ It can also be understood thus, etc., *as far as* or thou come to him so often that he be wearied.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1+2⟧ VERS. 19.-- *A rotten God and a foot.* [var: Vulg. Prov. 25:19 *Dens putridus, et pes lassus*, "a rotten tooth, and a weary foot"] Jeremias, etc., *as far as* in the day of judgment he shall lie open naked.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1109A]VERS. 20.-- *Vinegar upon nitre, he that singeth.* ⟦s2⟧ Nitre is so called from the province of Nitria, etc., *as far as* but renders the water fit for washing.

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *For thou shalt heap coals,* that is, the ardour of charity. ⟦s2⟧ For wisdom would not teach that thou shouldst do good things to an enemy for the sake of evil. ⟦s3⟧ For it often comes to pass that an enemy, overcome by frequent benefits, lays aside the rigour of hatred, and applies the warmth of charity to his mind.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *The north wind.* ⟦s2⟧ If with a cheerful countenance thou hearest one that backbites, thou givest him fuel for backbiting. ⟦s3⟧ But if with a sad one, as a certain man says, he learns not gladly to say that which he has learned not gladly to hear.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *It is better to sit in a corner of the housetop, than with a contentious woman.* ⟦s2⟧ Doma is a high and secret place. ⟦s3⟧ For in the Acts of the Apostles [n: (Act. X)], where Peter to the upper places [1109B] ascendeth to pray, for the upper places doma is written in the Greek.

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Cold water.* ⟦s2⟧ Cold water therefore, etc., *as far as* or, the contest being finished, they lead them in to the palm of recompense.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *A troubled fountain.* ⟦s2⟧ It often happens that they who shone endowed with greater knowledge, at the last, wishing to know more than is granted to human frailty, fall into the pit of unwisdom, whence he adds:

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *As he that eateth much honey.* ⟦s2⟧ For the sweetness of honey, etc., *as far as* whence there follows:

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- *As a city that lieth open,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ For if it has not the wall of silence, etc., *as far as* it fights against itself by much speaking.

## CHAPTER XXVI.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1109C]

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *As snow in summer.* ⟦s2⟧ Snow in summer and rains in harvest, etc., *as far as* which the calamity of the Arian tempest proved.

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *For as a bird.* ⟦s2⟧ He compares words to birds, etc., *as far as* of which: *Whatsoever thou shalt bind upon earth shall be bound also in heaven [n: (Matth. XVI)]*.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Answer not . . . ⟦s2⟧ Answer a fool according to his folly.* ⟦s3⟧ They are not contrary; for each of them agrees according to the time and the diversity of persons; while the fool both despises, because he receives not wisdom: and foolish pride is cast down by its own folly, whence: *I am become unwise, you have compelled me [n: (II Cor. XII)]*.

⟦p41⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Lame of feet.* ⟦s2⟧ A wise man can send a fool unknowingly, nor yet the glory of his own wisdom does he [1109D] lose: because concerning one unknown he believes the good which he had heard. ⟦s3⟧ But he also who knowingly sends a heretic to preach is lame of feet: *And drinking iniquity;* because he has both lost the going forth of good work without, and makes drunk the inward parts of his sense with a draught of folly.

⟦p42⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *As if a thorn should grow.* ⟦s2⟧ A thorn grows in the hand of a drunken man, etc., *as far as* or he seeks the reproaching of others.

⟦p43⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *As a dog.* ⟦s2⟧ A dog, vomiting, casts off the food by which his breast was oppressed, etc., *as far as* which after confession, while they desire it, they take up again.

⟦p44⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Thou hast seen a man.* ⟦s2⟧ For judgment therefore came I into this world [n: (Joan. IX)], that they who see not may see, and they who see may become blind [n: (Eccl. X)].

⟦p45⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1110A]VERS. 13.-- *The slothful man saith.* ⟦s2⟧ Many, when they hear the words of exhortation, etc., *as far as* they cease not to lie in their own depravities.

⟦p46⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *The slothful man seemeth to himself wiser,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Seven men speaking sentences, etc., *as far as* and they showed that they had perfected many.

⟦p47⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *As he that taketh a dog by the ears.* ⟦s2⟧ Strive not with words, it is profitable to nothing but to the subverting of the hearers. ⟦s3⟧ Whosoever is simple in understanding, and, when two are wrangling between themselves, his ear being caught by a biting speech, quickly begins to bark like a dog and to beget contentions, but the wise man altogether avoids it.
