## PSALM II.

« A psalm. » The modulation that is made by the touching of ten strings is the good work that is done by the keeping of the ten commandments. « David. » [n: (CASS.)] Strong of hand, or fair of aspect, is Christ, who warred down the powers of the air, upon whom the angels also desire to look.

VERS. 1.-- « Why have the nations raged, etc. » [n: (CASS.)] This psalm is the second in order, the first in title; whence in the Acts of the Apostles it is called the first. First he rebukes the persecutors and threatens them, [0846B] and foretells as a prophet. « Vain things. » [n: (Ibid.)] To hold him in death; or, that the world should not go away after him.

VERS. 2.-- « Kings. » [n: (Ibid.)] The former, who slew the infants, is Herod, the latter, he who consented with Pilate in the death of Christ. Kings and princes. [n: (Ibid.)] He could also, according to Ambrose, speak in the plural of one man. For only king Herod and prince Pilate are shown by the voice of Peter, in the Acts of the Apostles, to have conspired against Christ.

VERS. 3.-- « Let us break asunder. » [n: (AMB.)] Or they came together, saying: Let us break asunder their reasonings, that is, of the Lord and of his Christ, or of Christ and of the disciples, by which they wish to bind us to themselves. [n: (AUG.)] And although it might be said otherwise, it fits more openly those who are unwilling that the yoke of Christ be laid upon them. « Bonds. » The promises of eternal life, [0846C] and the threatenings of hell, by which they wish to subjugate us to themselves. « Let us cast away. » Their reasonings being destroyed, let us shake off his dominion from us. [n: (Ibid.)] The yoke, that is, the law: but « he that dwelleth in the heavens. »

VERS. 4.-- « He shall laugh them to scorn. » [n: (Ibid.)] As when he rose again with the guards set: « He shall deride, » when they were scattered through the world. The derision of God is the force of the foreknowledge which God gives to his saints, that, seeing the name of Christ to be in all nations hereafter, they may understand that those men meditated vain things.

VERS. 5.-- « He shall speak to them. » [n: (Ibid.)] He opens it more plainly when he says, he shall trouble them. The anger of God is a motion that arises in a soul which knows the law of God, when it sees that law passed over by an evil man, through which motion many things are avenged: or, the anger of God is the darkening of the mind of the transgressors [0846D] of the law.

VERS. 6.-- « But I. » [n: (CASS.)] Secondly, he brings in his words concerning his almighty kingdom and his unspeakable generation, where we learn of his divinity and his humanity. « King. » [n: (ID.)] So also are the kings of the earth appointed by him. « Preaching upon Sion, » so also are the prophets, « The Son, » so also is Israel, « I have begotten thee. » This to Christ alone.

VERS. 7.-- « The Lord hath said. » The person of him that reigns. « My Son. » [n: (AUG.)] Therefore do I give thee the nations for an inheritance rather than to any obedient servant: because thou art Son by nature, and consubstantial by an ineffable begetting. « This day. » [n: (Ibid.)] Although it might be taken of the day on which he was born according to man, yet, since "this day" signifies [0847A] a presence, let it be taken of the everlasting generation of the wisdom of God.

VERS. 8.-- « Ask. » [n: (AUG.)] It pertains to the taking up of man: which was done for the salvation of men, and according to which he intercedes for us. « An inheritance. » [n: (CASS.)] Inheritance is from lord (herus), because in it he holds dominion with free power.

VERS. 9.-- « Thou shalt rule them. » [n: (AUG.)] Partly thou shalt rule, partly thou shalt break, according to that word: « Behold, this one here is set for the ruin and for the resurrection of many [n: (Luc. II)], » so that he who is just may be justified still: and he who is in filth shall be defiled still [n: (Apoc. XXII)]. » « A rod of iron. » [n: (CASS.)] The power of Christ is the rod of iron; the sinner is the potter's vessel, which is both of clay and easily broken.

VERS. 10.-- « And now, ye kings, understand. » Now, [0847B] that is, by the things that have been said. [n: (CAS.)] Thirdly, on account of these things laid open, he admonishes all to obey humbly, and persuades them from the useful, that they perish not, and from the honourable, because blessed are they that trust in him. In this, after Christ, he speaks as it were an Apostle.

[n: (AUG.)] Or, now that I am appointed king, be not sorrowful, ye kings of the earth; for it is expedient for you to be under him, from whom come understanding and learning, that you may not rule rashly, but may serve the Lord of all, and may exult in blessedness.

VERS. 12.-- « Lest at any time he be angry. » [n: (Ibid.)] The doubt is not according to the prophet, but according to those men themselves, who think upon the anger of God with doubting. « From the just way, » that is, being taken away from Christ, or from good working.

[0847C]VERS. 13.-- « When his wrath shall be kindled. » [n: (CASS.)] Now he does not burn when he chastises, as a father, but in the time to come he will burn, because there will be nothing of patience in the time to come. « In a short time his anger. » Because he examines not each of these things separately, but all of them together.

## PSALM III.

VERS. 1.-- « A psalm, » [n: (AUG.)] concerning the passion and resurrection of Christ: the son is Judas, from whom he fled, when, at his departing, he withdrew with the rest into the mountain; or rather when the wisdom of God forsook his mind, so that he was wholly the devil's, which forsaking is called a flight because of its swiftness. Whence: « That which thou dost, do quickly [n: (Joan. XIII)]. » To him Christ showed peace, as David also did to his wicked son, whence he is called the peace of the father: but Judas, like Absalom, perished by hanging. He sets forth two opposed [0847D] parts. The voice of Christ, as it were wondering, when he came to save: « Lord, why, etc. »

VERS. 2.-- « Lord, why. » [n: (CASS.)] The first psalm of those which treat briefly of the passion and the resurrection, against those men, and chiefly the pagans, who do not think that the glory of majesty can descend to lowly things.

VERS. 3.-- « Many say, etc. » [n: (AUG.)] Or this each man says who is assailed by vices and desires.

« They say to my soul: There is no salvation, etc. » By the heaping up of vices there creeps in a despair of health, as though the vices were insulting the soul; or also by devils and his angels, through suggestions.

[0848A]VERS. 4.-- « Thou art my upholder. » [n: (CASS.)] In Christ. The head, that is, Christ, or the mind which serves the law of God. « Thou hast struck all. » It is rightly said in the predestination concerning the devil and his angels. « Upon the people. » This is each one of us. [n: (ID.)] Or the Church and its head speaks this, set amid the storms of persecutions throughout the whole world. Many say, desiring to extinguish the Christian name, « There is no salvation. » Otherwise they would not hope to destroy the Church that was sprouting far and wide. « Upholder. » Because it was taken up in Christ and exalted with him. The head is he himself who first ascended into heaven.

VERS. 5.-- « And he hath heard me from the mount, etc. » That is, by Christ the prayer of the Church is heard, or its righteousness.

VERS. 6.-- « I have slept: » according to that word: [0848B] « Rise from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten thee [n: (Ephes. V)]. » It is no wonder that the Saviour says these things, when he did far greater things for the salvation of souls at that time when he was absent from the body, which things are recounted in the catholic epistle under the name of Saint Peter: « When he preached, having gone forth, to those spirits who were in prison, who had at one time been unbelieving [n: (I Petr. III)]. » [var: Vulg. 1 Pt 3:19 *spiritibus veniens praedicavit*, "coming, he preached to the spirits"; Migne prints *profectas*, an unconstruable feminine accusative for what the sense requires as *profectus*, "having gone forth."]

VERS. 7.-- « I will not fear thousands, » who try to extinguish the name of Christ.-- « Rise up. » O Christ our head, in whose resurrection is salvation.

VERS. 8.-- « For thou hast struck, etc. » Now that the Church reigns, the enemies have been struck with confusion. « The teeth of sinners, » that is, their cursing words; or the princes become teeth unto destruction. Therefore believe, O men, [0848C] that « salvation is the Lord's, » and, O Lord, « let thy blessing be upon thy people. With my voice. » These things are mine by desert, because « with my voice. » He who is good, and asks good things, cries with his own voice; with another's voice, that is, with one not according with his own life. « I have slept. » The end of the matter, whence he sets forth that there is nothing to fear. « I, » with weight; as though: they rage, but I would not sleep, unless I willed it. Or, I slept by consenting; I was made drowsy, by doing evil; I rose up, by forsaking sins. « For. » [n: (AUG.)] He does not weigh back, nor besides does he make safe because he has struck: but from thence the teeth were broken, because he struck; that is, the cursing words were brought to nothing; or, the teeth are those who cut off certain men from the number of the saints, and incorporate them among the evil.
