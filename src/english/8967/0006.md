## PSALM VI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0851D]VERS. 1.-- « The octave » is the coming of the Lord, in which the sinner fears to be rebuked, and therefore repents. [n: (AUG.)] Some call the octave the coming of the Lord, etc., *as far as* whence the Church, fearing, here prays.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- « O Lord, not in thy fury. » [n: (BASIL.)] It is a deprecatory confession, which avails with God. ⟦s2⟧ First there is an exordium, in which he wins goodwill, by the power of the judge, by his own weakness, by the custom of sparing: because he wills to be entreated not by the dead, but by the living. ⟦s3⟧ « Rebuke. » [n: (AUG.)] To be rebuked is graver than to be accused, whence damnation is feared. ⟦s4⟧ To be chastised is milder, that is, to be amended or instructed. ⟦s5⟧ They are rebuked in the judgment, who are without a foundation. ⟦s6+7⟧ They are amended, that is, purged, who have built upon that foundation wood, hay, stubble. [0852A] Here therefore he says: « Heal, » lest there be work there, whence he adds: « Have mercy. »

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- « Weak » [n: (AUG.)] The soul is made weak by guilt, from which do thou heal her, because the bones, that is, the mind, is troubled at his presence, and the soul, that is, sensuality: therefore how long dost thou put it off, as if he said: What remains but that thou shouldst heal? ⟦s2⟧ « How long. » [n: (AUG.)] He hints that he has long wrestled with vices. ⟦s3⟧ But God's delay is not cruelty, but is that he may convince the soul into what evils she has flung herself. ⟦s4⟧ For she does not yet pray so perfectly that it may be said to her: « While thou art yet speaking I will say, behold I am here [n: (Isa. LXV)]. » [var: Vulg. Is 65:24 *adhuc illis loquentibus, ego audiam*, "while they are yet speaking, I will hear"] That out of the difficulty of the healing she may thereafter be the more on her guard, and that it may be shown what punishment there is for the unconverted, if there is so great a difficulty for the converted, whence: « If the just man shall scarcely be saved, where shall the ungodly appear? » [n: (I Petr. IV.)]

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0852B]VERS. 5.-- « Turn thee. » [n: (CASS.)] Thou who wast turned away, or, from vengeance. ⟦s2⟧ « Deliver, » from imminent death. ⟦s3⟧ Or, deliver the soul bound by sins. ⟦s4⟧ « Save me, etc., » by giving virtues.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- « For there is not. » [n: (CASS.)] Therefore I hasten to be released here, because after death and in hell confession is not received. ⟦s2⟧ « In hell. [n: (AUG.)] The rich man confessed who saw Lazarus, although out of the deep, and by comparison with him he was compelled to confess his own evils, even so far as to ask that his brothers also be forewarned by these: but after the judgment, in the deeper darkness, they shall see no light of God, to whom they might confess. ⟦s3⟧ Or, he calls death the sin in which he is unmindful of God who despises his commandments.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- « I have laboured. » [n: (CASS.)] He narrates the hardships [0852C] by which penitents are turned away [sic: *poenitentes avertitur*]; he groans while he sees what he has done, and what punishment is to come. ⟦s2⟧ « The bed. » The conscience, which to some is rest, to some torment. ⟦s3⟧ The couch, sensuality; I will make fertile what was dry. ⟦s4⟧ Or, the bed is the delight of the body, in which a man is dissolved. ⟦s5⟧ The couch, the heap of sins as of garments, which being loosed, by the shower of tears, a new harvest of virtues may grow.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- « From wrath. » [n: (AUG.)] From anger, either God's, which is in the judgment, etc., *as far as* lest any despair because of the grievousness of his sins, he adds: « Depart, etc. » [n: (CASS.)] A chastisement, that he may be separated from those in whose company he sinned, and may press on in the commandments of God. ⟦s2⟧ « Enemies, » [n: (AUG.)] that is, vices, or evil men, who are truly the enemies of those who turn themselves to God, from whom [0852D] every good man is separated, not in place, but in mind. ⟦s3⟧ But because so great a labour of penitence is not vain, signifying that he has been heard, he adds: « Depart. »

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9, 10.-- « The Lord. » Thrice the Lord, because he shows that the Trinity was present to his prayers: he began with tears, being heard he exults. ⟦s2⟧ « The Lord hath heard. » [n: (CAS.)] He gives an example that those who pray faithfully are heard.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- « Let them be ashamed and be troubled. » [n: (CASS.)] As in conclusion, now free from sins, exulting, he prays for his enemies, that they may be converted, that, like himself, they may have pardon. [n: (AUG.)] « Let them be ashamed. » Not here (where rather they mock those who leave all things, and make the weak ashamed), but in the judgment.
