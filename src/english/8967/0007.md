## PSALM VII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0853A]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- « Unto the end, a psalm. [n: (AUG.)] Chusi, who went over to Absalom's party, etc., *as far as* for it was not to be hidden from those for whom it was done. [n: (AUG.)] This psalm can be understood in the person of Christ; if any things are said humbly, let them be referred to our weakness, which he bore. ⟦s2⟧ First he asks to be delivered out of all tribulations.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- « O Lord my God. » [n: (CASS.)] This is the first of those psalms in which by the acts of David the future mysteries of the Lord are signified. ⟦s2⟧ The mystery of the incarnation was a silence to the Jews; as David from his son, so Christ endured persecution from the people whom he had nourished. ⟦s3⟧ « Deliver. » [n: (AUG.)] As it were, when [0853B] every war of the vices has been overcome, now indeed nothing remains but the envious devil.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- « Lest he seize, etc. » « Who may redeem, etc. » [n: (CASS.)] Proper words: it belongs to the Lord to deliver, to the lion to seize. ⟦s2⟧ The order, first he sets the persecutors, then the lion. ⟦s3⟧ For when a man has been cleansed from all spiritual wickednesses he is delivered from the devil.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4, 5.-- « O Lord, if I have done this. » [n: (Ibid.)] Literally, of David and Absalom. ⟦s2⟧ « If I have done this, » that is, if this evil has befallen me through a past fault. ⟦s3⟧ Or, if there be now iniquity in my hands, that is, in my works. ⟦s4⟧ Or, « if I have rendered evils to them that repaid me, etc., » (which is called equity among men), « deservedly » (even though it may not seem so to men) « let me fall away from my enemies empty, » from the fruit of meekness.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0853C] [n: (AUG.)] « If I have done this, » or, the universal sin, which he named without a name, which is pride, which is the root of all evils. ⟦s2⟧ Or, that which follows, « if there is iniquity, » which is taken of every sin. ⟦s3⟧ « If I have rendered. » [n: (Ibid.)] That is, if I have not imitated thee in a just silence, that is, in the patience which thou didst work for me. ⟦s4⟧ « Let me deservedly fall away from my enemies. » [n: (Ibid.)] He does not imprecate this on himself by an oath, but says what befalls those who avenge themselves: he prophesies therefore, he does not swear by an execration; which is the gravest kind of oath. ⟦s5⟧ Made empty by a vain and proud gladness, in that he could as it were not be overcome: Solomon, « Better is he that overcometh anger than he that taketh a city » [n: (Prov. XVI)]. [var: Vulg. Prv 16:32 *Melior est patiens viro forti, et qui dominatur animo suo expugnatore urbium*, "Better is the patient man than the valiant, and he that ruleth his spirit than he that taketh cities"]

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0853D]VERS. 6.-- « Let him tread down, etc. » [n: (Ibid.)] The soul of the sinner, of which, « Earth thou art, and into earth thou shalt return [n: (Gen. III)], » [var: Vulg. Gn 3:19 *pulvis es et in pulverem reverteris*, "dust thou art, and into dust thou shalt return"] is the food of the serpent, to whom it was said in the malediction, ( « Earth shalt thou eat, » ) that is, of the devil, because it is earth, that is, earthly. ⟦s2⟧ And therefore he here says: « Let him tread down my life in the earth, that is, let him incorporate it into himself when it has been brought down to earth, and let it there be despicable. ⟦s3⟧ « My glory. » [n: (Ibid.)] Which ought to be in the conscience, where God sees; and solid in the Lord, as: « He that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord [n: (I Cor. I)]. » « Let him bring down into the dust, » that is, into pride and boasting, namely that I should wish to glory among men. ⟦s4⟧ « Let him bring down into the dust. ⟦s5⟧ The dust which the wind casteth forth. » [n: (Ibid.)] The boasting of the proud. ⟦s6⟧ He wishes therefore that [0854A] his glory be solid within, where God sees: for it is dust, if he glories among men; behold how greatly pride is to be shunned.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7, 8.-- « Arise, O Lord. » He asks that the glory of Christ's resurrection may come to his aid. ⟦s2⟧ « In thy anger. » For this perfect man does not here pray against men, but against the devil, that he may lose his possession, namely sinners and the ungodly, who are his possession. ⟦s3⟧ And because the taking away of a man, because it is a punishment to the devil, is called the anger of God against him. ⟦s4⟧ Or, he prays here for Christ's resurrection, as in the preceding for his incarnation: as « O Lord my God, etc. » « In the borders. » The world, or hell, are the borders of the devil.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ « And arise, O Lord. » The devil is proud, because [0854B] he is overcome by iniquity, he adds: Or, « Arise, » that is, be made known in the whole world, by rising again. ⟦s2⟧ « In the precept, » that thou mayest make the precept of humility to be fulfilled: and this follows thereupon, because the Synagogue, out of divers peoples, or specially of the Jews, « shall compass thee about, » by loving, or by persecuting: « For this, » that is, for her that loves, « return, » that is, make them know thy omnipotence, thou who didst descend that they might see thee; or « for this, » that is, for her that persecutes, in whom thou findest no place, « return on high, » that is, withdraw thyself from those who persecute thee. ⟦s3⟧ « Synagogue. » [n: (Ibid.)] The congregation of the people, etc., *as far as* the Lord judges, of the judgment of him that returned, that is, of Christ. ⟦s4⟧ « Innocence. » [n: (Ibid.)] Because I have not rendered to them that repaid me. ⟦s5⟧ « Upon me. » [n: (Ibid.)] Justice and innocence [0854C] are upon me, because not from me, but from God.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- « Searching the hearts. » [n: (Ibid.)] What each one thinks. ⟦s2⟧ « The reins. » What delights each one; because the end of care and of thought is delight, to which one strives to attain. ⟦s3⟧ Men can see our works in words and in deeds: but with what mind they are done, and whither they desire to attain, God alone sees; who, when he sees the heart to be in heaven, and not to be delighted in the flesh, but in the Lord, that is, when the thoughts are good, directs also their ends, the just man, and not only directs, but helps the just man.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- « A just help. » [n: (Ibid.)] There are two offices of medicine, namely to heal disease and to keep health. ⟦s2⟧ According to the first he said in another psalm: « I am weak, heal me, O Lord. » According to [0854D] the other this perfect man here says, « my just help is from the Lord. » For both that medicine and this make men whole: but that one carries a man over from sickness to health, this preserves him in health itself. ⟦s3⟧ That one is a remedy for escaping disease; this affords a remedy lest he fall back into disease. ⟦s4⟧ By that one we are healed when sick, by this we are kept when whole.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- « God the judge. » [n: (CASS.)] Here he terrifies by the fear of the judgment to come, and promises hope to those who return. ⟦s2⟧ « Is he angry. » [n: (AUG.)] Another reading: « Doth he bring anger every day. » In the Greek more significantly, « he bringeth anger: » because the anger with which he punishes is not in himself, but in the angels of anger, through whom he punishes: who are delighted with human punishment not for the sake of the justice in which they do not rejoice, but for the sake of [0855A] malice: whom he does not gather every day for vengeance, but by patience invites to repentance.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- « His sword. » [n: (AUG.)] Another reading: He hath made bright his javelin. [n: (CASS.)] « His sword, » he will exercise manifest vengeance, nor is this said unequally, because he hath bent his bow, » he threatens, lest his patience be thought remiss; « he hath prepared, » that he may be thought about presently to send the arrow. ⟦s2⟧ « His bow. » [n: (Ibid.)] From a like case. ⟦s3⟧ For as he who has bent a bow threatens, so through sacred Scripture he threatens the wicked, lest the patience of God be thought remiss; and « he hath prepared, » who may be thought about presently to send an arrow: as one prepares a bow, that he may send an arrow. ⟦s4⟧ « He hath bent his bow, » [n: (AUG.)] by tempering the Old Testament [0855B] through the New Testament: « He hath prepared, » by explanation. [n: (Ibid.)] Or, « he hath prepared. » God prepares and disposes, etc., *as far as* but for those burning with zeal, arrows.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- « Behold. » As it were, these things God hath prepared in the Scriptures. [n: (Ibid.)] Whence it seems to a man to be the cause of damnation. ⟦s2⟧ But « behold » that it is not from God, but from man, because « he hath opened a pit, etc. ⟦s3⟧ He hath conceived sorrow, » [n: (Ibid.)] that is, by the appetite of temporal things. ⟦s4⟧ « He hath brought forth, » he has effected, sent out: and in the iniquity sent out there is a pit, that is, a downfall for others. ⟦s5⟧ Whence he adds:

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- « He hath opened a pit. » And so he is comparable to one opening a pit: for he opens by conceiving [0855C] fraud: he digs out by travailing, by pressing on the work of fraud; he falls in by bringing forth fraud, namely, by perpetrating it.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- « His sorrow shall be turned, etc. » The head, the soul, the crown, the reason: over which sins have dominion, and remain over: and they shall come down upon it when they shall thrust it into punishments.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- « I will confess to the Lord. » [n: (CASS.)] The conclusion, as it were gathering all things into a sum, he says that he confesses cheerfully.
