## PSALM VIII.

VERS. 1.-- « Unto the end, for the winepresses, etc. » For the winepresses, that is, for the Churches, etc., *as far as* souls flow out into rest.

[0855D]VERS. 2.-- « O Lord, our Lord, how admirable is thy name! etc. » [n: (CASS.)] First the praises of God, his majesty, and his exalted working are described. « In the whole. » [n: (Ibid.)] Let not the Jews therefore, or any others, claim for themselves what belongs to all, which is the intent. « Above the heavens. » [n: (AUG.)] Literally, or the Scriptures: because it exceeds the utterances of all the Scriptures; or, « above the heavens. » Continuing with what follows, that is, into the Scriptures, which he bends down to babes, on account of the enemies of the cross: who are destroyed, when the Scriptures are seen, that is, understood, to be led down as far as babes: and when through them they are nourished unto strength, and are raised to the sublimity of understanding.

VERS. 3.-- « Out of the mouth of infants. » [n: (Ibid.)] Of the unwise, to whom the Apostle gave milk, whom those signified [0856A] who praised the Lord, in whom the Lord uses this testimony. Out of these he hath perfected praise, because if only the perfect were in the Church, no counsel would be taken for the human race; as now, when even the least, not yet capable of the knowledge of spiritual things, are nourished by faith in the history of things temporal. « Enemies » are all who speak things contrary to the dispensation made through the Crucified. « Defenders » the heretics seem of the faith, the philosophers of wisdom, although yet the former impugn the faith, and the latter true wisdom. « Sucklings, » [n: (CASS.)] not in age, but as Peter has it, « as newborn babes [n: (I Petr. II)], » who cannot yet take food, but milk; and it is: thou art proclaimed not only by the perfect, but also by the unwise. Or, he calls infants and sucklings the apostles, who were simple and unlearned men; whence: « The foolish things of the world [0856B] « God hath chosen, that he may confound the strong [n: (I Cor. I)]. » [var: Vulg. 1Cor 1:27 *quae stulta sunt mundi elegit Deus, ut confundat sapientes*, "the foolish things of the world God hath chosen, that he may confound the wise"; *fortia* answers to a second clause, *infirma mundi elegit Deus*] The praise is more perfect. Or, out of the mouth of infants and sucklings, that is, out of the words of the Hebrew children thou hast perfected praise, which seemed to be done childishly, on account of the enemies, that it might not seem to be human wisdom, but divinely given. « Thine enemies. » The pagans. « The enemy. » The Jew, who is an enemy of God, while he defends the Father alone: because « he that honoureth not the Son, honoureth not « the Father, » or an enemy in his will.

VERS. 4.-- « For I shall see. » Before the Jew, in spirit. « Thy heavens, » the apostles, I shall see present in their descendants; this well suits those of the first age. « The works of thy fingers. » Because they are perfected by the cooperation of the Trinity, which is signified here in the three fingers, as there: « Who weigheth the bulk of the earth with three fingers. » [n: (Isa. XL.)] And through them, [0856C] « The moon and the stars which thou hast founded. » Not they.

VERS. 5.-- « What is man. » [n: (Ibid.)] Secondly, he says that the nature of man, once corrupted, is exalted in Christ above all things. For before he had shown the omnipotence of the Godhead. By man, sinners; by the son of man, the spiritual. [n: (Ibid.)] Man, any vile person, of whom God is mindful in showing mercy, the Son of man, Christ as man: since through him thou visitest him, when the Word was made flesh, in which the physician came to the sick. « Or the son. » [n: (AUG.)] The disjunction hints that there is a distance between man and the son of man. Every son of man is man, but not conversely, as Adam. Every man therefore, who bears the image of the earthly: the Son of man, who is heavenly, and deservedly a new man, [0856D] son of man: because the new is made out of the old by a certain change. [n: (AUG.)] Why, leaving the higher things, does he speak of these lowest ones? But the sheep, etc., *as far as* who by the seed of the word of God make the hearts of men to bear fruit.

VERS. 8.-- « Moreover the cattle also. » He notes therefore that the wicked also are subject to Christ, which seems less: for these carnal men dwell in the breadth of the plain, that is, in the amplitude of pleasures, not in the mountains of virtues. « The cattle of the field. » [n: (Ibid.)] Who live in the flesh, where they climb nothing steep: but the broad way, to which they cling, is that which leads to destruction: whence also Abel is slain in the field.

VERS. 9.-- « The birds, » the proud: of whom, « They have set their mouth against heaven [n: (Psal. LXXII)]. » Whose head says: « I will ascend into heaven, and I will exalt [0857A] my throne, and I will be like the Most High [n: (Isa. XIV)]. « The fishes of the sea that walk through the paths of the sea, » the curious. These three, the pleasure of the flesh, pride, curiosity, include all vices, which are the concupiscence of the flesh, the ambition of the world, that is, pride, the concupiscence of the eyes, since through the eyes curiosity chiefly prevails.
