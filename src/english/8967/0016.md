## PSALM XXI.

VERS. 2.-- *God, my God.* [n: (CASS.)] A complaint of being forsaken is set before, and prayer also, as in all the psalms of the passion. [n: (AUG., CASS.)] *God, my God:* he doubles it out of affection, or *God* of all through [0873C] the power of creation, *mine* properly through worship. [n: (AUG.)] When he had said this on the cross, he shewed that this psalm was written of himself. *Look upon me.* [n: (CASS.)] It is not in the Hebrew, but was added by the seventy interpreters. *Why hast thou forsaken me?* [n: (AUG.)] This is a wonder, since God also was with him; but it was for us, who in him are his body. *Far from my salvation,* that is, from that of my old man, because *salvation is far from sinners,* and these are the words not of justice, but of sins. Or, far from salvation are the words of my sins, which are to be taken away.

VERS. 3.-- *I shall cry.* [n: (AUG.)] He speaks of us, of his own body, as when perchance he says: *Let this chalice pass from me,* that is, he is thought to have feared to die; but he said these things for his own weak ones who fear to die, and so this also is the voice [0873D] of the members: *I shall cry and thou wilt not hear. By day, and thou wilt not hear, and by night, and not unto folly.* [var: Vulg. Ps 21:3 cries "by day" once only, without the doubled "and thou wilt not hear," and closes with *mihi*] [n: (CASS.)] Because there are no shadows in him, and yet he is not heard unto bodily salvation.

VERS. 4.-- *Thou dwellest in the holy place.* [n: (AUG.)] And so it is a wonder, because thou hast delivered the fathers; but I am a worm.

[n: (CASS.)] Thou dwellest in the holy place, because God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself. *The praise of Israel,* [n: (AUG.)] that is, of him that sees thee, not of him who wished to hide himself from thy sight.

VERS. 5.-- *In thee have our fathers hoped: they have hoped.* [n: (CASS.)] It is a great beauty, while he repeats *they have hoped.*

[0874A]VERS. 7.-- *But I am,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Thus far Christ in the person of the old man; here now he speaks properly in his own person. *But I am.* Now properly Christ: I was born without seed, that I might be beyond men in man, in whom I was made a reproach, that at least so the pride of man might imitate the humble. *A worm and no man, a reproach.* [n: (CASS.)] So vilely trodden down, etc., *as far as* a worm, born without coupling, mortal, humble and vile, creeping in silence.

VERS. 9.-- *He hoped in the Lord,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Saying: *Hope thou in the Lord,* etc. The irony of the Jews; behold, as it were a historical narration, not a prophecy, lest there be any excuse, lest there be any ambiguity.

VERS. 10.-- *From the breasts.* Not from the time when I began to be suckled, etc., *as far as* but thou hast been my God. *From the womb.*

VERS. 11.-- *From the womb.* [n: (AUG.)] Not only of a mother, which is the law of being born for all; but from the darkness of the Synagogue, in which he is unbelieving, [0874B] not yet born into the light of Christ, who trusts in those carnal observances of the sabbath and of circumcision.

VERS. 12.-- *Depart not from me,* thou who hast already begun.

*Thou hast drawn me out:* not a man; lest the virgin's childbearing be doubted, it was done by the power of God. *My hope from the breasts:* true humanity and great perfection; for from the breasts he does what one scarcely come of age can do.

*In thee was I cast,* not into the sins of man. Others are cast into Adam, whence they are guilty. *From my mother's womb, [0874C] my God.* [n: (CASS.)] Neither was the conception without God, nor in sin, as man's is; whence the Angel: *The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee.*

VERS. 13.-- *They have surrounded.* [n: (ID.)] The passion is described, which prayer concludes; to convince the Jews he relates things to come as though they were past.

VERS. 14.-- *They have opened.* [n: (ID.)] As wild beasts wishing to swallow him down, their mouth, not unlocked unto wisdom. *As a lion.* [n: (AUG.)] As a lion seizing they surrounded him, as one roaring they opened their mouth.

VERS. 15.-- *As water.* When water is poured out, etc., *as far as* whence, leaving him, they fled. [n: (CASS.)] As water is the sacrament of the washing away of sins, etc., *as far as* and it was made solid. *Like wax.* Well does he compare the mysteries of the law [0874D] to wax, etc., *as far as* for they have surrounded. *In the midst of my belly.* [n: (AUG.)] The belly signifies the weak in the Church, or the memory of the Church.

VERS. 16.-- *My strength is dried up like a potsherd.* [n: (CASS.)] Not as hay, but as a potsherd, which by fire is made the firmer. *My tongue.* [n: (AUG.)] They kept my precepts with them, through whom I was to speak: and through them I was brought down into the kindred of those who were the dust of death.

VERS. 17.-- *They have dug.* [n: (ID.)] The gospel narration. Although these things are so to the letter, the digging of the hands hints that they did not believe his works; of the feet, that they mangled the Apostles. [n: (CASS.)]: they have dug. [0875A] Dug earth bears fruit, so Christ wounded gave the fruit of life.

VERS. 18.-- *They have numbered all my bones.* [n: (CASS.)] The punishment of the cross is properly described. Thus they numbered the bones, that is, the Apostles; lest they should grow into a greater number, they sought to extinguish them. *They have looked upon me.* [n: (ID.)] With diligence and with delight, whereas the eyes are wont to be turned away from a cruel thing. Behold how obstinate they are: nothing was done by chance, nor was their heart softened by so many miracles.

VERS. 19.-- *They parted my garments among them,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] His own sacraments could be divided by heresies, but not the coat from above, that is, given by God; this is charity, or unity, which no man can divide. [n: (CASS.)] But perhaps it came to certain men even by the gift of God, [0875B] etc., *as far as* as the olive branch brought by the dove to the ark. [n: (AUG.)] Or, through the one soul the head and the body, which being one rejects diversities.

VERS. 22.-- *From the unicorns, my lowness,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The Jews, seeking earthly things only: or, boasting themselves singularly of the worship of God, or being singularly proud, the son also having been slain after the servants.

VERS. 24.-- *Ye that fear the Lord, praise him,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Human fear brings forth not praise, but blame.

VERS. 25.-- *Let all the seed of Israel fear him, because he hath not despised,* etc. Whom he means by the seed of Jacob he opens, adding: *Of the poor man.* [n: (AUG.)] Not of him who cries out with the words of sins out of a vain life, but [0875C] of him who is not swollen with the pomps of the world.

VERS. 26.-- *With thee is my praise in a great church,* etc. [n: (ID.)] He places his praise with God, who teaches that there be no presuming in man.

VERS. 27.-- *They shall praise the Lord that seek him: they shall live.* [n: (ID.)] The poor are rich in God: therefore poor, because they praise God that they may seek him, and he himself is the riches of the poor. *Their hearts shall remember for ever and ever, and shall be converted to the Lord.* [n: (ID.)] Because he is the food of the heart.

VERS. 30.-- *They have eaten and have adored.* [n: (ID.)] Let not the dispenser forbid to eat, but let him warn the exactor to fear. *In his sight shall they fall.* [n: (CASS.)] The Church receives both, but he himself sees and discerns.

[0875D]VERS. 32.-- *It shall be shewn,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Or to the angels, who offer the prayers of men to God, not for instruction, but for ministry.

[n: (CASS.)] Note that the psalms of the passion end in the great hope of Christians, that it may be known that salvation is from the passion.
