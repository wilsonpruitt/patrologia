## PSALM XXI.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *God, my God.* [n: (CASS.)] A complaint of being forsaken is set before, and prayer also, as in all the psalms of the passion. [n: (AUG., CASS.)] *God, my God:* he doubles it out of affection, or *God* of all through [0873C] the power of creation, *mine* properly through worship. [n: (AUG.)] When he had said this on the cross, he shewed that this psalm was written of himself. ⟦s2⟧ *Look upon me.* [n: (CASS.)] It is not in the Hebrew, but was added by the seventy interpreters. ⟦s3⟧ *Why hast thou forsaken me?* [n: (AUG.)] This is a wonder, since God also was with him; but it was for us, who in him are his body. ⟦s4⟧ *Far from my salvation,* that is, from that of my old man, because *salvation is far from sinners,* and these are the words not of justice, but of sins. ⟦s5⟧ Or, far from salvation are the words of my sins, which are to be taken away.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *I shall cry.* [n: (AUG.)] He speaks of us, of his own body, as when perchance he says: *Let this chalice pass from me,* that is, he is thought to have feared to die; but he said these things for his own weak ones who fear to die, and so this also is the voice [0873D] of the members: *I shall cry and thou wilt not hear. ⟦s2⟧ By day, and thou wilt not hear, and by night, and not unto folly.* [var: Vulg. Ps 21:3 cries "by day" once only, without the doubled "and thou wilt not hear," and closes with *mihi*] [n: (CASS.)] Because there are no shadows in him, and yet he is not heard unto bodily salvation.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Thou dwellest in the holy place.* [n: (AUG.)] And so it is a wonder, because thou hast delivered the fathers; but I am a worm.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (CASS.)] Thou dwellest in the holy place, because God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself. ⟦s2⟧ *The praise of Israel,* [n: (AUG.)] that is, of him that sees thee, not of him who wished to hide himself from thy sight.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *In thee have our fathers hoped: they have hoped.* [n: (CASS.)] It is a great beauty, while he repeats *they have hoped.*

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0874A]VERS. 7.-- *But I am,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Thus far Christ in the person of the old man; here now he speaks properly in his own person. ⟦s2⟧ *But I am.* ⟦s3⟧ Now properly Christ: I was born without seed, that I might be beyond men in man, in whom I was made a reproach, that at least so the pride of man might imitate the humble. ⟦s4⟧ *A worm and no man, a reproach.* [n: (CASS.)] So vilely trodden down, etc., *as far as* a worm, born without coupling, mortal, humble and vile, creeping in silence.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *He hoped in the Lord,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Saying: *Hope thou in the Lord,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The irony of the Jews; behold, as it were a historical narration, not a prophecy, lest there be any excuse, lest there be any ambiguity.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *From the breasts.* ⟦s2⟧ Not from the time when I began to be suckled, etc., *as far as* but thou hast been my God. ⟦s3⟧ *From the womb.*

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *From the womb.* [n: (AUG.)] Not only of a mother, which is the law of being born for all; but from the darkness of the Synagogue, in which he is unbelieving, [0874B] not yet born into the light of Christ, who trusts in those carnal observances of the sabbath and of circumcision.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Depart not from me,* thou who hast already begun.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Thou hast drawn me out:* not a man; lest the virgin's childbearing be doubted, it was done by the power of God. ⟦s2⟧ *My hope from the breasts:* true humanity and great perfection; for from the breasts he does what one scarcely come of age can do.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In thee was I cast,* not into the sins of man. ⟦s2⟧ Others are cast into Adam, whence they are guilty. ⟦s3⟧ *From my mother's womb, [0874C] my God.* [n: (CASS.)] Neither was the conception without God, nor in sin, as man's is; whence the Angel: *The Holy Ghost shall come upon thee.*

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *They have surrounded.* [n: (ID.)] The passion is described, which prayer concludes; to convince the Jews he relates things to come as though they were past.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *They have opened.* [n: (ID.)] As wild beasts wishing to swallow him down, their mouth, not unlocked unto wisdom. ⟦s2⟧ *As a lion.* [n: (AUG.)] As a lion seizing they surrounded him, as one roaring they opened their mouth.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *As water.* ⟦s2⟧ When water is poured out, etc., *as far as* whence, leaving him, they fled. [n: (CASS.)] As water is the sacrament of the washing away of sins, etc., *as far as* and it was made solid. ⟦s3⟧ *Like wax.* ⟦s4⟧ Well does he compare the mysteries of the law [0874D] to wax, etc., *as far as* for they have surrounded. ⟦s5⟧ *In the midst of my belly.* [n: (AUG.)] The belly signifies the weak in the Church, or the memory of the Church.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *My strength is dried up like a potsherd.* [n: (CASS.)] Not as hay, but as a potsherd, which by fire is made the firmer. ⟦s2⟧ *My tongue.* [n: (AUG.)] They kept my precepts with them, through whom I was to speak: and through them I was brought down into the kindred of those who were the dust of death.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *They have dug.* [n: (ID.)] The gospel narration. ⟦s2⟧ Although these things are so to the letter, the digging of the hands hints that they did not believe his works; of the feet, that they mangled the Apostles. [n: (CASS.)]: they have dug. [0875A] Dug earth bears fruit, so Christ wounded gave the fruit of life.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *They have numbered all my bones.* [n: (CASS.)] The punishment of the cross is properly described. ⟦s2⟧ Thus they numbered the bones, that is, the Apostles; lest they should grow into a greater number, they sought to extinguish them. ⟦s3⟧ *They have looked upon me.* [n: (ID.)] With diligence and with delight, whereas the eyes are wont to be turned away from a cruel thing. ⟦s4⟧ Behold how obstinate they are: nothing was done by chance, nor was their heart softened by so many miracles.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 19.-- *They parted my garments among them,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] His own sacraments could be divided by heresies, but not the coat from above, that is, given by God; this is charity, or unity, which no man can divide. [n: (CASS.)] But perhaps it came to certain men even by the gift of God, [0875B] etc., *as far as* as the olive branch brought by the dove to the ark. [n: (AUG.)] Or, through the one soul the head and the body, which being one rejects diversities.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 22.-- *From the unicorns, my lowness,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The Jews, seeking earthly things only: or, boasting themselves singularly of the worship of God, or being singularly proud, the son also having been slain after the servants.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *Ye that fear the Lord, praise him,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Human fear brings forth not praise, but blame.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *Let all the seed of Israel fear him, because he hath not despised,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ Whom he means by the seed of Jacob he opens, adding: *Of the poor man.* [n: (AUG.)] Not of him who cries out with the words of sins out of a vain life, but [0875C] of him who is not swollen with the pomps of the world.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *With thee is my praise in a great church,* etc. [n: (ID.)] He places his praise with God, who teaches that there be no presuming in man.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *They shall praise the Lord that seek him: they shall live.* [n: (ID.)] The poor are rich in God: therefore poor, because they praise God that they may seek him, and he himself is the riches of the poor. ⟦s2⟧ *Their hearts shall remember for ever and ever, and shall be converted to the Lord.* [n: (ID.)] Because he is the food of the heart.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *They have eaten and have adored.* [n: (ID.)] Let not the dispenser forbid to eat, but let him warn the exactor to fear. ⟦s2⟧ *In his sight shall they fall.* [n: (CASS.)] The Church receives both, but he himself sees and discerns.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0875D]VERS. 32.-- *It shall be shewn,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Or to the angels, who offer the prayers of men to God, not for instruction, but for ministry.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (CASS.)] Note that the psalms of the passion end in the great hope of Christians, that it may be known that salvation is from the passion.
