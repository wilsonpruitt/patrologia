## PSALM XXXI.

*The understanding of David.* [var: Vulg. Ps 31:1 heads the psalm with the dative, "to David himself, understanding"] [n: (AUG.)] A psalm concerning the grace by which we are Christians, as the Apostle says [n: (Rom. IV)]. This psalm therefore begins with pardon, treating against the vice of men who do not know their sins or else defend them, and who boast of their merits. [n: (CASS.)] Against the vice of men, then, [0887A] this psalm is set, etc., *as far as* he strengthens those who stand in it.

VERS. 1.-- *Blessed are they whose iniquities are forgiven,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Not those in whom he finds no sin, but those to whom he forgives it. *Iniquities.* He means the tinder of sin, namely concupiscence, etc., *as far as* but that sin may be no more. [n: (HIERON.)] Iniquities, before faith and baptism: sin, after faith and baptism.

VERS. 2.-- *Blessed is the man.* [n: (HIERON.)] To whom God forgives, for not of himself. He covers it, that it be not revealed in the judgment; and so it will not be imputed unto punishment. There is no guile in him that accuses himself: he holds in the heart what he confesses here with the mouth.

*Blessed:* because formerly, not confessing to God, I came into sin, while I cried out, if I did any good.

VERS. 3.-- *I was silent.* [n: (AUG.)] He who will not confess [0887B] his sins labours without cause in the defence of sins: and since in this he also boasts of his merits, his strength fails the more. [n: (CASS.)] *Blessed is he to whom he hath not imputed.* To some, who have grievous sins, God imputes them; to others he does not impute them, through mercy, even though they have some — namely to those who do not proclaim themselves just when they are evil, but acknowledging themselves make satisfaction. This I now profess, but formerly: *Because I was silent,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Or, *I was silent,* as to sin with my voice, *while I cried out all the day:* while the penalty of sin, or an inward blinding, showed me a sinner, or while my evil life cried out, though I was silent, whether I had done any good.

*While I cried out all the day,* etc. Although a man be not conscious to himself of anything, yet he is not just: for who understands his offences? Whence: *There is a just man that perisheth in his own justice.* And the Apostle: *I am conscious to myself of nothing,* etc.

[0887C]VERS. 4.-- *The thorn is fastened in.* [n: (CASS.)] *The thorn,* which lifts up the whole body, that is, pride. [n: (AUG.)] Likewise *misery,* etc., *as far as: in the hands of a drunken man.*

VERS. 5.-- *My sin.* Diapsalma. Confession, whence pardon, as above the growing old from governance. *I have made known.* [n: (CASS.)] For *I said* before, etc., *as far as* and God hears.

VERS. 6.-- *For this shall he pray.* [n: (ID.)] Diapsalma. Commending repentance, he says it is common to all the saints; which sundry sects reject, against whom he flees for refuge to God. [n: (AUG.)] *For this.* Therefore every saint prays, because thou hast forgiven, otherwise he would not be a saint. *In the time,* etc. It is the time of grace, the law, pressing and convicting the guilty, compelled them to cry out, grace heals. *But yet in the flood,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Although the saint pray, [0887D] etc., *as far as* the saint will pray, saying: *Thou art a refuge. To him.* That is, to God, for he changes the person, as often. They do not draw near who say that fate makes them sin, or fortune, which rules all things, or the nation of darkness, which rebels against God.

VERS. 8.-- *I will give thee understanding.* [n: (ID.)] Diapsalma. It is answered to the penitent: *I will give thee understanding,* etc. Behold a psalm of understanding, lest the suppliant be thought neglected, with God for witness. *Thou, deliver.* [n: (CASS.)] Thou who hast promised, *I will give thee understanding.* Or Christ answers the penitent: A glad end, God gives understanding: whence he exhorts others to the same.

VERS. 9.-- *Become ye not as the horse and the mule,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Having received understanding, this penitent turns himself [0888A] to the proud who defend their sins: therefore you, whom I invite, etc. *The horse,* the untamed Gentile; *the mule,* the Jew slow to believe. [n: (CASS.)] *The horse,* without discernment of its riders; *the mule,* takes any burdens whatever, etc., *as far as* that is, boasting of one's own strength. *Their jaws.* Or, *the jaws* with which they chew, that is, give victuals more sparingly, that being compelled by necessity they may return. [n: (AUG.)] Or, thou wilt break them here, and in the world to come there will be many scourges. Or: *Become ye not as the horse,* because there are many scourges here and in the world to come. *Who come not near to thee.* [n: (CASS.)] For there are those who, if they sin through the weakness of the flesh, do not withdraw from devout prayers.

VERS. 10.-- *Many are the scourges of the sinner,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] After the bridle, scourges are added for taming, lest he be of those unpunished ones to whom a roving licence [0888B] of their own way is now given: so this man above said that he was tamed.
