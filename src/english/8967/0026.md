## PSALM XXXV.

VERS. 1.-- *Unto the end, a word.* [var: Vulg. Ps 35:1 *In finem. Servo Domini ipsi David.*, "to the servant of the Lord, to David himself"] [n: (CASS.)] He treats here of the error of certain men, etc., *as far as* which nevertheless hangs over all.

There is no fear before the eyes: because there is guile, that is, because [0895B] he obeys the flesh and not God, though reason suggests otherwise, namely that God sees even if no other does: or, that the unjust man himself, what he sees, knows from God, so that it may be found by that same one that his own iniquity is to be held in hatred with God and with all men.

[n: (ID.)] First, he asserts that evils are from man.

VERS. 2.-- *The unjust hath said,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] There are two kinds of sinners: because either through the weakness of the flesh they cannot follow the commands of the law; or they are blasphemers who sin freely, as though God cared not for this; whence it is: *He hath said,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] A description of the wicked man. *There is no fear of God.* [n: (CAS.)] Because guile was unwilling to study the law, by which it might be excused through ignorance.

VERS. 3.-- *For he hath done deceitfully.* [n: (AUG.)] Many search out their iniquities, etc., *as far as* nor are they excused for [0895C] ignorance.

VERS. 4.-- *The words.* (ID). He shows the iniquity, etc., *as far as* that he might act well.

VERS. 5.-- *On his bed.* That is, in the heart, in which evil works please them; and this is, *he hath set himself in every way,* with zeal and with lingering: he found indeed, and knew them to be evil, but nevertheless he did not hate them. *He was unwilling.* [n: (AUG.)] Not that he could not; he acts more perniciously against himself who does not will what he can. For there are certain things which men cannot understand even if they will, as: *In the beginning was the Word,* and yet, that they might act well, they can understand. *On his own bed.* [n: (ID.)] That is, in the heart, where the Lord commanded us to pray with the door shut, etc., *as far as* wisdom, namely of the flesh, which is an enemy to God.

VERS. 6.-- *O Lord, in heaven.* [n: (Cass.)] Good things are from God [0895D] through mercy. As if he should say: Evils are from man, but goods from mercy. O Lord, in those who are *heaven,* that is, who know hidden things, is thy mercy: and that they themselves are *clouds,* is from thee. [n: (AUG.)] Or *in heaven,* that is, mercy, etc., *as far as* but announces by sending forth his truth.

*Even to the clouds.* That is, the preachers, through whom God thunders in announcing, flashes in miracles, rains in teachings. Who are also mountains, whom the sun first clothes with light, which afterward, from them and yet not by them, descends to the lowest of the valleys: for they are ministers of teaching, but God enlightens.

VERS. 7.-- *Thy judgments.* [n: (AUG.)] That thou hast mercy on these, and blindest those; they are utterly searchable to none: [0896A] yet thou savest both, these bodily only, those spiritually as well, wherein is great mercy.

VERS. 8.-- *Men,* etc. He shows that temporal things also are from God.

VERS. 9.-- *They shall be inebriated.* [n: (ID.)] The thing cannot be expressed, but by this likeness he intimates that, being full, they lose their mind, so that they no longer recognize their own fathers, sons, and other friends. Or, then they are drunk in reality, who now hope. *With the torrent.* As if: There shall be a rush of grace and mercy to inebriate them. [n: (ID.)] *With the torrent.* Not that the beatitude should be passing, but plentiful. Or, thy will breaks all obstacles in man. Or, the wisdom of Christ is a torrent, because it is sudden and swift.

VERS. 10.-- *For with thee is the fountain of life.* [n: (CAS.)] *With thee,* O Christ, *is the fountain,* that is, the beginning of every good; [0896B] and *in thy light,* because through his preaching we see the light of the Father and of the Holy Spirit, and therefore *hold forth,* that in this world one may go more safely.
