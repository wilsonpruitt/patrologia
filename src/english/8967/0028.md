## PSALM XXXVII.

VERS. 1.-- *A psalm of David, in the remembrance of the sabbath.* [n: (AUG., CASS.)] That we may always have in memory the sin which we are utterly unable to flee, so that we may at least restrain ourselves from it, he here brings in the torment of penitence in many ways, so that he seems to set forth Job himself as an example to recruits, that they may embrace torments, less indeed than their deserts, by which we are freed from [0898B] eternal punishment. [n: (AUG.)] The sabbath is the rest of eternity, which he who is conscious of sin dreads to lose, and he who is grievously afflicted desires to have.

VERS. 2.-- *Lord, rebuke me not in thy indignation.* First, he seeks mercy through punishments summarily. *Rebuke me.* [n: (CASS.)] That the punishment may be from mercy unto correction, not from anger unto vengeance.

VERS. 3.-- *The arrows.* [n: (AUG.)] The vengeances which thou wast threatening to Adam, saying: *In whatsoever day ye shall eat, ye shall die the death.* [var: Vulg. Gn 2:17 *in quocumque enim die comederis ex eo, morte morieris*, "in what day soever thou shalt eat of it, thou shalt die the death," singular where Migne prints the plural] Because he ate, they are fixed in us also; and not for an hour, but *thou hast confirmed* them, that it may be even to the end of the world. [n: (AUG., CASS.)] *For the arrows.* He numbers the things he suffers, etc., *as far as* that which can lightly be hurt.

VERS. 4.-- *There is no health,* etc. [n: (AUG., CASS.)] From [0898C] the pressing of the vengeance put forth upon Adam. Or, he is terrified by the fear of the wrath to come, so that there is no health in his flesh: after the manner of a modest man, who is already afflicted by the scourges to come. Or, from *the face of wrath,* because I have foreseen the face of wrath, that is, what thou threatenest, that I may not see the wrath. *There is no peace.* A grievous sorrow reaches to the inward parts from the face of sins, because the holy man is afflicted with sins beyond the punishments of the body, thinking that he deserves heavier things. *There is no health.* [n: (CASS.)] Here he seeks mercy from the power of the judge, and from the weakness of the person. *There is no peace in the bones.* He takes the bones for the flesh. And he expounds what he had said, adding another thing: namely, from *the face of sins.*

VERS. 5.-- *They are gone over.* [n: (AUG.)] Or, they have lifted up, that is, because iniquity lifted up the head of the proud man against God, God weighed him down, as with a bundle, and pressed him [0898D] that he should not now be raised up.

VERS. 6.-- *They are putrefied.* [n: (CASS.)] A narration, in which the miseries are enumerated. *My scars.* [n: (AUG.)] Or *my bruises;* another reading, that is, my sins. He is not sound who has scars, which have putrefied and stink: that, turned away from them, we may adore the sabbath, and groan: *Running with the bridegroom after the odor of the ointments.*

VERS. 7.-- *I am become miserable.* [n: (ID.)] It having been said in what manner [sic: *qualiliter*] the iniquities are gone over, he says how they are made heavy.

VERS. 8.-- *For my loins.* [n: (CASS., AUG.)] Not only do I sin by my own weakness, but also the devil, who afflicts the body and wearies the soul with empty imaginings; and through this there is again no health, as also [0899A] above through the arrows. [n: (AUG.)] *The loins,* or, the soul is filled, etc., *as far as* when we shall see the truth as it is.

VERS. 9.-- *I roared from the groaning.* [n: (CASS., AUG.)] The *groaning* is of the heart, the *roaring* of the voice: from a likeness of the beasts, to which he compares himself: that a stronger groaning may be shown: the virtue of patience, which breaks forth into a groan, not into words.

VERS. 10.-- *Before thee is all.* [n: (CASS.)] Such is it as deserves to be before thee, because he asks for sins. [n: (AUG.)] and therefore *it is not hidden,* but the groaning is heard, because it is godly, for the liberation of the soul: others God cares not for. *My desire.* [n: (ID.)] It is prayer, nor does he ever cease to pray who ceases not to love: charity is a cry.

[0899B]VERS. 11.-- *My heart is troubled.* [n: (CASS.)] He expounds the sorrows of the mind, because the virtue of patience has left him. *Troubled,* because the mind has succumbed to its own suffering. *The light,* that is, reason lately disturbed, which he has not with him because of the huge troubles.

VERS. 12.-- *My friends and my neighbors.* [n: (ID.)] And in these two comforts human nature rejoices, but to this man they are a heaping up of suffering. [n: (AUG.)] *My friends.* Thus far he has confessed the things he suffers from himself within, etc., *as far as* and these things also befit anyone living among men.

VERS. 14.-- *As one deaf they heard not* [var: Vulg. Ps 37:14 *audiebam*, "I heard not"]*, and as one dumb not opening his mouth.* [n: (CASS.)] Not opening it to the iniquity of those who counselled him, etc., *as far as* against [0899C] the sorrows of the body he roared.

VERS. 16.-- *For in thee, O Lord, have I hoped,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Because amid so many evils he hoped in the Lord, he affirms that he is to be heard in the recalling of his amendment. *I have hoped.* A saving medicine in evils, to hope, not to fail. *In thee, O Lord,* who changest sad things into glad.

VERS. 17.-- *For I said: Lest at any time my enemies should rejoice over me.* [n: (AUG.)] Likewise the head concerning the weak feet, though it be in heaven. It does not forsake that which it has on earth. *While my feet are moved.* [n: (CASS.)] If they are at any time moved by human weakness, the impious laugh at that whereof the godly grieve.

VERS. 18.-- *I am ready.* [n: (ID.)] Knowing that I have offended. [0899D] *Sorrow,* because he has offended: the impious man defends himself; the just man is angry against himself.

VERS. 19.-- *For my iniquity.* [n: (ID.)] The two modes of perfect penitence, confession and amendment, through weeping, alms, and the like.

VERS. 20.-- *They live.* [n: (AUG.)] They enjoy the world, being established in worldly things, and daily they become more.

VERS. 22.-- *Forsake me not.* [n: (ID.)] Absolved from things past, professing God with thanksgiving to be the author of salvation, he joyfully cries out that he may not now be left. [n: (CASS.)] Because the offense is heavier after pardon. [n: (AUG.)] An exultant conclusion, that there may be a sure hope for those who imitate.

*Depart not from me.* How then is it said above: *Why hast thou forsaken me?* It is the voice of the old man who was crucified [0900A] with him, but here he speaks in his own person.
