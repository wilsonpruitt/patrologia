## PSALM XXXVIII.

VERS. 1.-- *Unto the end.* [n: (AUG.)] Because here it is concerning eternal blessedness, for which it is delightful to leap across, even if it be hard to the flesh. And thence he teaches to pass over from the world to God. But the leaper always draws upon himself, from his good repute, the envy of the wicked and their slander: whence he resolves to keep silence among the wicked, lest he say anything amiss. *Idithun.* [n: (ID.)] Leaping over them, that is, those who remain in the world. [n: (CASS.)] First, concerning the restraint of the tongue among blasphemers and slanderers: whence he also desires to know the end, where these things are not.

VERS. 2.-- *I said: I will take heed to my ways,* etc. [n: (AUG., CASS.)] This psalm admonishes to restrain, among blasphemers, [0900B] the wicked, and slanderers, the incontinence of the tongue, contentions, and brawls: for this silence is very useful. [n: (CASS.)] I said: I deliberated in my heart: where wise men deliberate before they speak. *Ways* [n: (HIER.)] That is, actions and words. That I may not offend by foolish or idle things.

*In my tongue.* [n: (AUG.)] Not without cause is the tongue in the word: because it easily slips. *I have set to my mouth.* After I passed over earthly delights, etc., *as far as* teaching us to beware not to imitate this. [n: (CASS.)] *I have set* even to the point that I was dumb, etc., *as far as* who seeks an occasion of contention.

VERS. 3.-- *From good things, and my sorrow,* etc. [n: (ID.)] To be announced, etc., *as far as* that is, of those who leap over, I have spoken. [n: (AUG.)] Which is, unwillingly I kept silence from the wicked, [0900C] etc., *as far as* and if the acts of one leaping over are anything.

VERS. 4.-- *My heart grew hot within me.* [n: (AUG.)] If the slothful man shall be condemned, etc., *as far as* he becomes a greedy steward.

VERS 5.-- *I have spoken.* [n: (ID.)] Not to a hearer who is to be instructed, but to God who hears, by whom I am heard within. *And the number of my days.* [n: (HIER.)] The number of the days which now are, is not truly for him who leaps over; but to him who clings it seems to be.

VERS. 6.-- *Behold, measurable.* [n: (CASS.)] As it were, the end being known, and the days which are, he attends to those things which he has left, and compares them one with another. *And the substance,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] He who sees that this substance is nothing, etc., *as far as* which shall be before thee.

[0900D]VERS. 7.-- *But yet.* [n: (CASS.)] Seeing the coming of Christ, he returns to the weakness which he has among the virtues. [n: (AUG.)] Great are the things we have said and believe; but nevertheless that is not eternal, but all is vanity. And, desiring the eternal in place of mutability, he reproaches human life, to which he is subject, in what he says. Lest anyone think the saints strangers to temptations. Or, though the days be measurable, though there be substance, it is nothing: yet all is vanity in the rational creature, which according to divers men craves the whole creation. *But yet.* Although I gape after eternal things, etc., *as far as* whose conversation is in heaven. *Every man.* [n: (AUG.)] If he is evil, he is vain in worldly things; if he is good, since he wills to be with Christ, his life is vain.

[0901A]-- *But yet.* [n: (AUG.)] Diapsalmus. How all are under vanity and chance; and thence he sets over against it that he waits for the Lord, and asks to be delivered. *He passeth in an image.* [n: (ID.)] Although he thus pursues vanity, etc., *as far as* because with misery and toil he craves earthly things. *He layeth up treasure.* [n: (ID.)] Out of the other vices the Prophet chose avarice, to show what he intends: for it is itself the root of all evils.

VERS. 8.-- *Expectation.* [n: (ID.)] Of the first coming, or of the second, etc., *as far as* not in our purses.

VERS. 9.-- *Deliver me from all my iniquities.* [n: (CASS.)] Not only from these, lest he roll back into those things which I leapt over, but from all altogether. For man is imperfect: because he has not received what he desires; perfect, who knows this very thing that is wanting to him.

[0901B]VERS. 10.-- *I was dumb.* [n: (ID.)] Held in reproach, etc., *as far as* and it is necessary, because I have failed.

*Remove thy scourges from me.* [n: (AUG.)] Because *thou hast made* me, do not destroy me; only yield that I may be brought forth; not that I may be broken small

VERS. 11.-- *With rebukes for iniquity.* With which thou hast rebuked the human race: which nevertheless thou hast rebuked out of our own desert, on account of our iniquities, which rebukes are unto correction; for otherwise iniquity would remain uncorrected. *Thou hast corrected man.* [n: (AUG.)] There is a formless sorrow which does not amend: a formed one, which by its instructions supplies a form. And such a sorrow is a precept, that is, it holds the place and the image of a precept.

VERS. 12.-- *And thou hast made the soul to waste away like a spider.* [n: (ID.)] Dried up from the love of the virtues and of knowledge. [0901C] As a spider weaves an empty web in the gathering of temporal things. Or, while it draws forth iniquity from itself like a spider, it dies by the voiding of its bowels. [n: (CASS.)] Or, the spider has a slender body, and stands not on the ground but on high, and weaves its webs by the voiding of its bowels: so the soul of one converted and afflicted forsakes earthly things, and, wasted away by the fear of God, accomplishes subtle works. *But yet.* [n: (AUG., CASS.)] Although he be rebuked, although they waste away, yet he is confounded by human weakness in various things, save he who contemplates God with a pure mind.

VERS. 14.-- *Hear my prayer.* [n: (CASS.)] Diapsalmus. A journey. He asks for himself the evils of the world to be at hand, and his sins to be forgiven, that he may go forth with a prosperous end. [n: (ID.)] But because I do not ask these things, etc., *as far as* mercy to be moved.

[0901D] *Be not silent, for I am a stranger.* [n: (AUG.)] Already with thee, but still a lodger: because I am to pass hence to eternal things, where I shall not be a lodger. *A stranger with thee.* [n: (CASS.)] Not received in the city of the devil, but in thine, translated from Babylon to Jerusalem. *As all my fathers.* Lest anyone be thought excepted from this condition. *A stranger.* I forsake my own kingdom for thine. *A pilgrim.* Not yet choosing a seat for myself here.

*Forgive me.* [n: (ID.)] Here he asks that it be forgiven him, so that being sure he may come to the judgment.
