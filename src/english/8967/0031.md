## PSALM XL

VERS. 1.-- *Unto the end.* [n: (HIER.)] *An understanding for the sons of Core,* that although poor and suffering, etc., *as far as* the Apostle saying: *But to me,* etc.--[n: (AUG.)] It is as it were [0903D] the voice of one man, of the head and of the body.

VERS. 2.-- *Blessed.* He is mocked who reverences a dead poor man; but *blessed.* [n: (AUG.)] The Prophet celebrates him that understands concerning the needy and the poor. [n: (HIER.)] First the Prophet from himself, afterward he will bring in Christ. *Who understandeth.* [n: (AUG.)] Note *who understandeth,* that it may be given even to those who do not ask: he who gives to one asking does well; he who understands one that is silent is without doubt blessed.

VERS. 3.-- *May the Lord preserve.* [n: (ID.)] Well does the Prophet wish it for him that understands concerning the needy and the poor, which is a sure promise. *And may he quicken.* [n: (ID.)] In the life to come, or, here through justification.

VERS. 4.-- *The Lord bring help.* [n: (CASS.)] Do not think that the blessed man has rest here: he mitigates his sorrows, and prays [0904A] that the infirmity of man be not overcome by hard things; as it were, these temporal goods are a bed of rest: as it were, he makes them full of sorrows, that hope be not had in them. *The bed.* [n: (AUG.)] For *the whole* indeed *couch,* that is, worldly happiness, God providently turns into straits. [n: (CASS.)] Here he himself, trembling, asks for mercy: because he who knew that he had sinned feared to be handed over to these things by right: but lest the servants of God, afflicted by these things, should seem deserted by God, since God himself bore such things; whence he says: *My enemies have spoken.* Or, *a bed of sorrow.* [n: (AUG.)] The flesh, in which is sin, etc., *as far as* when Adam was weakened by transgressing. *Help* [n: (AUG., HIER.)] In the infirmity of the flesh, which weighs down the soul. *The couch.* [n: (AUG.)] There is some earthly thing which the sick man, etc., *as far as* for the house of eternal habitation.

[0904B]VERS. 6.-- *My enemies.* [n: (CASS.)] The Lord concerning his passion, which was after the manner of the needy one. [n: (AUG.)] *My enemies.* These things are said specially of Christ, but they can be said also of his members: and today tribulation is made, that the name of Christ may be extinguished: but through his own death and that of his own the Church has grown the more.

VERS. 7.-- *And if he came in.* [n: (CASS.)] Note that the wicked are to be borne with.

[n: (ID.)] Not only open enemies, but also those who feigned themselves friends.

VERS. 8.-- *He went forth abroad.* From the bosom of truth; an evil entrance, an evil going out. *Unto the selfsame.* [n: (AUG.)] That is, with one counsel they all whispered against me. *They whispered.* [n: (CASS.)] They do not dare to uncover their counsel, because in the crime all are *partners:* whereas few [0904C] are wont to be the *authors* of crimes.

VERS. 9.-- *Shall he.* [n: (AUG.)] He who has the power of laying down his soul, has he not also the power of taking it up again?

VERS. 10.-- *I have hoped.* [n: (AUG.)] In the members, because other brethren hoped somewhat in him, because he seemed such as the rest. So too he says that he hungers in the poor.

VERS. 11.-- *But thou, O Lord.* [n: (AUG.)] Of the resurrection, which is the glory of the needy one, for the confirming of the hope of the faithful. *And I will requite them.* [n: (AUG.)]. For because they are scattered through the world, it is for admonition, not yet for damnation.

VERS. 13.-- *But me.* [n: (ID.)] He affirms what he prayed for.

VERS. 14.-- *Blessed.* [n: (CASS.)] A concluding praise, the passion and resurrection having been set forth. *The God of Israel.* [0904D] [n: (AUG.)] The Jews are our book-carriers, who carry the codices for us: we are Israel. Otherwise the pagans would have thought the things said of Christ and the Church to be feigned, but they are overcome by the testimony of their enemies. *From the age,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] From which all things began to be administered, unto the age to come, where all things are eternal.

*Let it be done.* [n: (HIER.)] The doubling shows that this is to be done by all. In place of *let it be done,* in the Hebrew is written *amen, amen,* which Aquila translated *truly, truly,* or *faithfully.* And here ends the first book of the Psalter according to Jerome.
