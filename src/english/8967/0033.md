## PSALM XLIII.

[0907B]

VERS. 1.-- *Unto understanding.* [n: (AUG.)] Let them not so seek these things to be done for themselves, etc., *as far as* and let not these things be loved. [n: (CASS.)] He distinguishes the benefits of God, which were given to beginners in the world, to the perfect in heaven.

That it may be the more wonderful that present things are left behind, he recalls things past.

VERS. 2.-- *God, with our ears.* [n: (AUG., CASS.)] First he says: *God,* which one is wont to do when glad or heavily afflicted.

*We have heard.* That there may be greater faith in the matter, lest it be thought doubtful.

*Fathers.* Not young men, that they may be seen as our true ones, who would tell sure things to their own.

[0907C] *The work which,* etc. [n: (Ibid.)] And although he always works, yet he is said to work when he shows some sign of newness.

VERS. 4.-- *But thy right hand.* [n: (AUG.)] That is, the good success which thou gavest them. *And thy arm,* etc. [n: (Ibid.)] That is, strength; and *illumination,* that is, wise counsel: all which thou hast done out of thy good will.

VERS. 5.-- *Thou art thyself my king and my God.* [n: (AUG.)] The times are changed; the author is not changed.

VERS. 7.-- *For I will not hope in my bow.* [n: (CASS.)] The lesser strength of man, because it avails less in war.

*A sword.* The greater strength, which in war is believed to avail more. [n: (AUG.)] Or, *my sword shall not save me,* but thou, because in this world also thou hast saved us, lest they should drag us [0907D] after them, and thou hast made them ashamed: and therefore then *we shall be praised.*

VERS. 8.-- *Thou hast saved,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Note also that things said in the past are to be taken for things to come.

VERS. 10.-- *But now thou hast cast us off.* [n: (CASS.)] Diapsalmus. He enumerates the evils which the good did here, yet not having forgotten God. [n: (CASS.)] He exaggerates the evils, that he may obtain the good will of the judge. *Thou hast confounded,* while here they are held in reproach by the wicked. [n: (AUG.)] These things are truly to come, etc., *as far as* this is a fast not of abstinence, but of luxury.

VERS. 11.-- *Thou hast turned away.* [n: (CASS.)] For those who fail. Or, to be turned away is to flee the enemy; whence: *If they shall have persecuted you in one city, flee into another.* [var: Vulg. Mt 10:23 reads "Cum autem persequentur vos in civitate ista, fugite in aliam": "and when they shall persecute you in this city, flee into another"]

[0908A] *They plundered.* This happens to those put to flight, that they fall as prey, who cannot resist their enemies.

VERS. 12.-- *Thou hast given us like sheep for food.* [n: (CASS.)] Those persisting in faith. Here diverse martyrs, that is, diverse kinds of martyrdom: some food for beasts, others delivered to the Gentiles into servitude; whence he says: *Like sheep for food,* where the slaughter of certain ones is noted.

VERS. 13.-- *Thou hast sold thy people without a price.* [n: (AUG.)] While they are given to foreigners and no one gains: which is done for proving or for correcting; or no one is gained for them, or if any, yet few.

VERS. 14.-- *A reproach to our neighbours.* [n: (CASS.)] A grievous affliction is heaped on by neighbours, because that is not uttered in passing which one inflicts from among those standing round. *A derision to those who are round about.* [n: (CASS.)] Derision is a confused voice of gladness, [0908B] announcing insult with immoderate hilarity. Contempt is abject vileness.

VERS. 15.-- *Into the likeness of the Gentiles. [n: (Ibid.)]* [var: Vulg. Ps 43:15 has the dative, "in similitudinem gentibus": "for a likeness to the Gentiles"] In the likeness of Christ they are set beside the Gentiles, when they afflicted them with equal punishments.

VERS. 17.-- *Of him that reproacheth. [n: (Ibid.)]* To reproach is to curse to the face; to speak against is to detract from one absent, and to bite deceitfully.

VERS. 18.-- *They have come upon us. [n: (Ibid.)]* Because they were not in us through fault.

VERS. 19.-- *It hath not gone back. [n: (Ibid.)]* So that it should go backward, although sometimes it descends from the contemplation of the deity. *Thou hast turned aside.* [n: (AUG.)] Our paths were in the pleasures and prosperities of the world: but thou hast taken them away from their own way, and hast shown the narrow way which leads to [0908C] life. [n: (HIER.)] Or *and thou hast turned aside,* that is, thou hast made our works to hang upon thy law. Or, *thou hast not turned aside* (another reading), that is, thou hast not permitted our paths to turn aside and go astray. *Paths,* that is, works; *from thy way,* that is, from the law; therefore because *thou hast humbled us.*

VERS. 20.-- *The shadow of death.* [n: (AUG.)] Or, this mortality is the shadow of death: but true death is damnation with the devil.

VERS. 21.-- *If we have forgotten the name of God. [n: (Ibid.)]* This is the understanding of the sons of Core, who on account of all these evils do not forget God, knowing that God will require these things.

VERS. 22.-- *He hath known. [n: (Ibid.)]* God is said to harm [sic: *Nocere*] both what he knows and what he makes thee know. *He hath known,* because he will require on account of thee, that thou mayest find thyself, and give [0908D] thanks to him who made thee, as was done in Peter, who proved what he could do. *For thy sake we are put to death.* [n: (CASS.)] Not for our crimes, but that thy faith may be increased among the Gentiles.

VERS. 23.-- *Arise. [n: (Ibid.)]* He prays for help, that he may succour those afflicted here at the time of the resurrection. [n: (AUG.)] Thus far not forgetting; and that we may persevere: *Arise, for on thy account we are put to death.* [n: (AUG., HIER.)] And although they know that Christ does not sleep, and that he has risen for himself, yet they pray him to rise through faith, not for himself, but for the Gentiles, who think him dead. For if they too believed that he had risen, believing in him they would not persecute, but they too would believe. [n: (AUG.)] Or *arise,* strengthen.

[0909A] *Thou sleepest.* Thou seemest to neglect. Wherefore, even if we know not, thou knowest why: either for fault, or for benefit. But if for fault, would that it were absent; if for benefit, would that it were present. Some thou dost repel, because thou withdrawest thy face, that is, thy knowledge, from them: thou sleepest to certain ones who groan over evils.

VERS. 24.-- *Why dost thou forget?* that is, thou givest not the spirit of fortitude, without which we are helpless. *And of tribulation.* [n: (CASS.)] Because thou dost not mitigate it, etc., *as far as* who easily yield to unlawful motions, and cleave to earthly things.

VERS. 26.-- *Arise, O Lord.* Here *arise* is set without sleeping, which pertains to the resurrection, in which is the hope of all the faithful; in which human captivity is destroyed; through which we are helped [0909B] and set free. *In the earth our belly.* [n: (AUG.)] He who fixes his knees on the earth has whereby he may be more humbled: but he who is more humbled, so that his belly also cleaves to the earth, has nothing further whereby he may be more humbled. He expresses therefore an exceeding humiliation, so that, since it has come to the highest point, the mercy of God may now be present. Or he laments those who are the belly, that is, the carnal, and *they have cleaved,* that is, consented to the wicked.
