## PSALM XLV.

VERS. 1.-- *Unto the end.* [n: (CASS.)] A psalm of David, of some faithful man of Judea, that the testimony may be the more certain.

*For the hidden things.* For the manifestation of the hidden things, that God is man, that salvation is promised to both peoples, that blindness has befallen Israel, that the Apostles passed over to the Gentiles, that peace is on earth; whence: *They shall turn their swords into ploughshares and their spears into sickles* [0911D] [n: (Isa. II)].

*For the sons of Core.* [n: (CASS.)] The sons of the cross. *Of the hidden things,* that is, he speaks of the coming of Christ.

VERS. 2.-- *Our God.* [n: (CASS., AUG.)] In the coming of the Lord all hope is shown to be had, even in the passing over of the Apostles to the Gentiles. *They have found us exceedingly.* [n: (AUG.)] For we were fleeing them. Or, *they have found us* exposed through Adam. *Exceedingly,* by so much the more powerful is he who delivers.

VERS. 3.-- *The earth shall be troubled, and the mountains shall be removed,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Judea, as it were dry land: without idolatry, hemmed about by the bitterness of the Gentiles. Or, *the sea,* [n: (AUG.)] the world.

*The heart of the sea.* The savagery of the Gentiles

[0912A]VERS. 4.-- *They have sounded, and are troubled,* etc. Those who contradict, the Gentiles: or the Apostles by preaching.

*And their waters were troubled.* The reference is made to the sense, not to the word: that is, the waters of the sea, namely the peoples, or their doctrines, which have been made foolish, the doctrine of the Saviour flashing forth; whence: *They were swallowed up,* etc. And this is the rod of Moses, which devoured the rods of the Magi.

VERS. 5.-- *The stream of the river.* [n: (CASS.)] Diapsalmus. Because the Church is founded in Christ, to whom the nations and kingdoms are made subject. [n: (AUG.)] As if he should say: Thus the sea rages; but amid these waves and noises they fear not, who have fled to that refuge.

VERS. 6.-- *He shall help her.* [n: (CASS.)] Or, *with his countenance,* that is, by the showing forth of his help, when by helping he [0912B] declares himself. *In the morning.* In the very beginning of a good work; and this, *at daybreak,* that is, through the illumination of the Holy Spirit, who is light as it were of day.

VERS. 7.-- *He gave.* As it were a great reward. *His voice.* Preaching, at which the earthly man trembled. He shall tremble for fear of the judge.

VERS. 8.-- *The Lord of hosts. [n: (Ibid.)]* Briefly he defines what Christ is. *With us.* As it were: He who sent the angels, now he himself has come, and is with us, because with us as our upholder; therefore: *Come,* as it were I invite you now secure. *Ours.* Not save of the sons of the cross. *The God of Jacob.* Who gave us as great things as he gave Jacob: for he is not the God of him alone.

VERS. 9.-- *Come and see. [n: (Ibid.)]* Diapsalmus. The mighty works of the incarnation. *He hath set.* As it were signs, for the calling of [0912C] the people. *Prodigies.* As concerning the birth from the Virgin, and the star, and the rest. *Making wars to cease.* [n: (AUG.)] Because through his coming the idolatry and superstition of the whole world perished, or, at that time for his presence the world was at peace.

VERS. 11.-- *Be still. [n: (Ibid.)]* If the arms are burnt up, *be still,* not having wherewith you may fight.

## PSALM XLVI.

VERS. 2.-- *All ye Gentiles.* [n: (AUG.)] As if he should say: they insult, the boys, saying: *Bald head, bald head,* you, all the Gentiles to whom grace has passed over: *Clap your hands,* etc. The Gentiles to whom grace has passed over, and whom in the former psalm he called to faith, he invites to praise.

VERS. 3.-- *High.* [n: (HIER.)] Powerful in what he wills. [0912D] *Terrible.* [n: (REMIG.)] Because he is to judge in power.

VERS. 4.-- *He hath subdued the peoples unto us.* [n: (CASS.)] This befits faithful men, to whom all outsiders are made subject, or, he has subjected to himself those who are to be associated with us. *The peoples,* the Jews. *Unto us. [n: (Ibid.)]* Namely to the universal Church; this it befits all the faithful to say, to whom outsiders are made subject. *Under the feet. [n: (Ibid.)]* By *feet,* understand preachers, to whom the peoples are made subject. [n: (AUG.)] *Under the feet.* So is the Church exalted, that it has those not yet believing under its feet.

VERS. 5.-- *He hath chosen for us,* etc. Or in the person of the Apostles, he chose to give us his inheritance, of many peoples, that we may cultivate it, striking their hearts with the sword of preaching, and destroying their malice. [0913A] *The inheritance.* [n: (AUG.)] The heavenly inheritance is the beauty of the Christian people, in which he shall be comely, shining as the sun, who now is abject. *The beauty of Jacob which,* etc. That is, those like to Jacob or signed by Jacob, who give earthly things for heavenly, as Jacob did, which was the grace of God, because *he loved him, Esau being reprobate.*

VERS. 6.-- *He is ascended.* [n: (CASS.)] Diapsalma, the ascension and the kingdom of God is described, whence he urgently admonishes to sing psalms. *In jubilation.* [n: (AUG.)] The joy of the wondering Apostles, etc., *as far as* what can neither be kept silent nor be uttered.

VERS. 7.-- *Sing ye psalms.* [n: (CASS.)] Praise by works: very salutary is that which is so often repeated, because it is God who creates, and a king governing the things created.

VERS. 8.-- *Of all the earth. [n: (Ibid.)]* Not as Jupiter of Crete, [0913B] Mars of Thrace. *Wisely.* He teaches how one ought to sing psalms. Of old they sang psalms without understanding, while they worshipped stones. [n: (AUG.)] No one does wisely what he does not understand.

VERS. 9.-- *He shall reign. [n: (Ibid.)]* Now is this prophecy fulfilled: *God sitteth upon the holy seat. The holy seat.* [n: (AUG.)] Thrones, Virtues, and the saints.

VERS. 10.-- *The princes of the peoples.* [n: (CASS.)] The first-fruits of all the Gentiles came together by believing in Christ, because, the Jews being cast out, the Gentiles succeed into the sons of Abraham, having the promise, because the Jews were lifted up in pride. [n: (HIER., CASS.)] Or *the strong gods,* namely the Apostles, whom neither death nor life shall separate from the charity of Christ. *Lifted up,* by miracles and doctrine.
