## PSALM LXI.

[0932C]VERS. 1.-- *Unto the end, for Idithun.* [n: (AUG.)] *Leaping over,* seeing so many things that meet him, he submits himself to God, who is higher than he; and to those beneath him he answers as from on high, by rebuking. *Leaping over,* he answers the envious, who wish to deceive.

VERS. 2.-- *Shall not. [n: (Ibid.)]* That is, *let not the foot of pride come to me. I shall not be moved any more,* that is, *and let not the hand of the sinner move me. Subject to God* always, desiring nothing besides him. It shall be indeed, because *from him is my salvation,* from the Father the Son, the author of salvation, because he is God also, lest he be thought less.

VERS. 4.-- *How long do ye rush in. [n: (Ibid.)]* Or, according to another reading: *How long do ye lay on,* and the sense is the same. For the wicked lay *upon a man,* that is, upon the Church, the burdens of sufferings: so that unto the end [0932D] every faithful man may fill up in his own body what is wanting of the sufferings of Christ. *All ye together.* [n: (CASS.)] Or, according to another reading: *All* of you, as though ye all kill that man, so far as concerns the man, not *all* one man, but the Church; but because God is my upholder I shall not be moved [cj: *quis*; read *quia*, "because"]. [n: (AUG.)] *How long,* from the blood of Abel, etc., *as far as* not, namely, that which is *the most high founder. Upon a man.* [n: (CASS.)] Power has been given you over the body, not over the soul. *Ye rush in.* Not lightly, but ye kill the innocent; ye think that he is without a defender, and that he easily falls, like a leaning wall, which would fall even if it were not pushed. *And as a fence,* which is a construction of stones without the binding of mortar; and lest by its very newness thou [0933A] shouldst think it firm, he adds *pushed,* [var: Vulg. Ps 61:4 *depulsae*, "thrust down"] or, ye must not rush in, because ye kill your own selves, all of you, even those who consent.

VERS. 5.-- *The price.* [n: (CASS.)] Christ, that he should not be worshipped, or, to take away blessedness. *They have thought.* [n: (AUG.)] Note the diverse kinds of enemies, etc., *as far as* of her the prophet says: *Glorious things are said of thee, O city. I ran in thirst.* For although he always drinks them, yet he always thirsts beyond. *With their mouth.* Because they are not able by open evils, they attempt by soft ones.

VERS. 6.-- *But yet to God,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Diapsalma. Again he exhorts himself, that is, his own soul, to be made subject, and admonishes the rest. *My soul is subject.* [var: Vulg. Ps 61:6 *Verumtamen Deo subjecta esto, anima mea*, an imperative, "be thou subject to God, O my soul"] This petition fixes strength in our mind.

VERS. 8.-- *In God is my salvation. [n: (Ibid.)]* Then [0933B] safe and glorious in him: but now what? He himself is the God of my help.

VERS. 9.-- *Hope ye. [n: (Ibid.)]* His own faith being published abroad, he admonishes all who labour under superstitions to hope in the Lord. *Pour out.* [n: (AUG.)] Through tears the heart is poured out, etc., *as far as:* Whence, *Prick the eye, and it produces a tear; prick the heart, and it produces knowledge [n: (Eccl. XXII)]*. [var: Vulg. Sir 22:24 *Pungens oculum deducit lacrimas, et qui pungit cor profert sensum*, "he who pricks the eye draws out tears, and he who pricks the heart brings forth understanding"] *Hearts,* etc., evil thoughts.

VERS. 10.-- *But yet vain. [n: (HIER.)]* Or, join it thus, God is our helper; and although he so works with us, yet the sons of Adam are vain and lying in the balance, perverting the Scriptures, by which they may prove false things. [n: (CASS.)] Diapsalmus. He reproaches those who go astray, and admonishes them to trust in God, not in things that perish, which is what the psalm intends. *Lying in the balances.* Who with unjust weights [0933C] deceive buyers, or wish to seem just, though they are deceitful. *In the balances.* In the scale of justice, whereby they deceive the more easily. *Unto the same thing,* whereby they may be believed fair.

VERS. 11.-- *Hope ye not in.* [n: (AUG.)] As if he should say: Neither hope ye, nor desire ye, the things which, when gathered, are snatched away by others. *Do not,* thirsting he turns himself to them. *Do not. Rapines.* The prey which thou wilt seize is in a trap, thou holdest and art held; thou holdest, another's goods that is, and art held by the devil. O ruinous gains! thou findest money, thou losest justice. *Let them abound. Set not your heart.* [n: (CASS.)] He does not say: Do not possess, for he does not condemn riches, whereby we purchase heaven; but the heart set upon them, which does not spend but hoards.

VERS. 12.-- *He hath spoken once.* [n: (AUG.)] *Set not your heart upon them.* Why? Because the *power* of judging belongs to the Son of God, [0933D] etc., *as far as* nor let mercy be despaired of on account of that power. *Power belongeth to God:* there is no power to any man save from God who is just, although his equity lie hidden from thee.

## PSALM LXII.

*A psalm of David.* [n: (CASS.)] He speaks of the Church, which in the drought of the world thirsts, and seeks after mercy, until she lay hold of things eternal, teaching men to converse well in the midst of a depraved nation. [n: (AUG.)] A psalm in the person of the head and of the members; his voice is ours, and ours is his.

VERS. 2.-- *O God, my God, to thee from the light,* etc. [n: (Ibid.)] How she fares in the desert, namely the bride, she desires to see God, whom she also praises, and she wishes to do this [0934A] the more. *God. [n: (Ibid.)]* Common, *my God* in a somewhat more particular way; see the affection of devout entreaty through this doubling. *From the light.* Or at daybreak, when Christ rose again, that the Church may praise him then, when by the example of his own resurrection he quickened her to rise. *I watch,* even while I sleep, not loving the things that are of the world. [n: (CASS.)] And I thirst in mind and in body. *From the light.* [n: (AUG.)] For thou wouldst not watch; unless the light arose, which should rouse thee. *My soul hath thirsted for thee:* and here the flesh for God, while it seeks from him the things needful here.

VERS. 3.-- *Desert. [n: (Ibid.)]* Where none of the saints dwells. *Trackless,* because there is no way by which thou mayest go out thence. *Waterless,* because neither, while thou tarriest where thou art, is there water there. But God here made a way, Christ, and sent the water [0934B] of his Word, filling the preachers with the Holy Ghost: *That there might be made in them a fountain of water springing up into life everlasting [n: (Joan. IV)]*. [var: Vulg. Jo 4:14 *fiet in eo fons aquae salientis in vitam aeternam*, "shall become in him a fountain", indicative and singular] *So in the sanctuary have I appeared to thee, that I might see,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] He to whom God does not appear, let him be in holy desire, etc., *as far as* For the sun by its own light makes us to see, and so also does God.

VERS. 4.-- *Above lives. [n: (Ibid.)]* Of human life, of which there are many, as that of the soldier, of the husbandman and of the rest: better is what thou givest to the corrected than what the perverse choose. *My lips.* After the benefits of God: he promises praise. *Shall praise,* toward my neighbour, for whom also I am solicitous, because unless thy mercy went before me, I could not praise thee, and as with the lips, so let them bless in life. Or thus, *Because above lives.*

VERS. 5.-- *Thus will I bless. [n: (Ibid.)]* As thou hast granted, through [0934C] mercy, that I should praise thee, *so will I bless,* that I may ascribe to mercy the life in which I praise. *In thy name I will lift up my hands. [n: (Ibid.)]* He lifts up his hands who, persevering in good works, with hands stretched out in the figure of the cross, adores God [d: unde]; whence the Apostle: Lifting up pure hands in prayer, without anger and contention.

VERS. 6.-- *With fatness. [n: (Ibid.)]* In the inward parts. Fatness is in the flesh, that is, wisdom, which is in secret, and the strength of the spirit.

*I will lift up my hands. [n: (Ibid.)]* In Exodus. Moses, while he lifted up his hands to the Lord, etc., *as far as* he works for earthly things, he is overcome by Amalec.

VERS. 7.-- *If I have remembered.* [n: (CASS.)] He sets forth the good things and the evil of either party, etc., *as far as* save he who works in [0934D] Christ.

[n: (CASS.)] *In the mornings,* that is, in the light of Christ, as above, *from the light I watch unto thee,* that the son of light, that is, of Christ, may watch unto the author of light, unto God.

VERS. 8.-- *A helper,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] In the perils of Idumaea, etc., [sic: *atc.*] *as far as* because *it hath upheld me* from perils, *thy right hand,* that is, propitiation.

VERS. 10.-- *But they have sought my soul in vain.* But the king. In these two verses he compares the good things and the evil of the different parties.

*Into the lower parts of the earth.* [n: (CASS.)] Because they were not carried up to the heavens. Evil thoughts are swallowed up by the earth, because, born of the earth, they sink into the depths of the earth, as into mire.

VERS. 11.-- *They shall be the portions of foxes,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] [0935A] The beginning of the world, etc., *as far as* while by a perverse will they join themselves to the deceitful.

VERS. 12.-- *In God.* [n: (CASS.)] The Father, whence: *I am in the Father, and the Father in me. They swear,* not by him, lest he should command an oath, but in him, by promising him obedience.
