## PSALM LXVIII. (cont.)

[0949D]VERS. 24.-- *Eyes.* [n: (AUG.)] The teachers who go before. *Let them be darkened,* that is, let them lose the light of knowledge. *And their back,* etc. That is, the lesser ones. For when they who go before lose the light of knowledge, those who follow are bowed down to bear the burdens of sins and to seek after earthly things; or, *the back,* the mind of the same. Always this concerns those who do not repent.

VERS. 25.-- *Pour out,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] That it may be abundant after the manner of a river.

*Let it take hold.* That it may not be permitted to escape.

VERS. 26.-- *Let their habitation become.* [n: (AUG.)] This now in the open, etc., *as far as* in which they grew proud against God. *In their tabernacles.* [n: (CAS.)] The temple and the royal palace. To this he applies *to dwell within,* which is done by the affection of a devout [0950A] mind; to houses, *to dwell,* which is done by bodily abiding.

VERS. 27.-- *Thou hast smitten.* [n: (CAS.)] Not by his own fault: thou hast smitten him by the passion, unto the salvation of the Gentiles; this they fulfilled with a cruel purpose.

*And upon the sorrow.* Because it was for their perdition.

*They have added.* To inflict death. Or, upon the sorrow of my wounds, that is, of those which the lance and the nails had fixed in me, they added the guards of the sepulchre.

VERS. 28.-- *Add thou iniquity upon iniquity,* etc.) [n: (AUG.)] Their iniquity was that they should kill a man, and upon this God added that they should kill his Son, so that, as the servants sent before, so also the heir himself.

[0950B]VERS. 29.-- *Let them be blotted out,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Because they think themselves written down, etc., *as far as* and that they shall stand at the right hand. *Out of the book of the living,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] The knowledge of God, which predestined and life, those whom he foreknew to be made conformable to the image of his Son. Yet it is not so to be taken, as though God writes anyone in this book whom he afterwards blots out: but according to the hope of those who think themselves written down.

VERS. 30.-- *I am poor,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] Here he sets forth the dispensation of the incarnation and the holiness of his purpose, saying: What is the end of the passion? *Sorrowful.* [n: (AUG.)] This according to the head: he sorrows for all: whence: *He himself hath borne our sorrows [n: (Isa. LIII)]*, which no other did. Therefore he says:

VERS. 32.-- *And it shall please.* [n: (CAS.)] The praise of a pure heart [0950C] pleases more than the slaying of cattle. *A young calf.* [n: (AUG.)] For sins a young calf was offered, which signified the newness that was to follow; which has its defence in its horns, the guard of its feet in its hoofs (by which it may tread down the rough things of the world); *bringing forth,* because the faithful man is daily increased by the power of God. [n: (AUG.)] The calf stirs up the earth with its hoofs; so he who does not contradict, but nevertheless has a base earthly relish, is to be stirred up with hoofs: and upon this calf my praise will be pleasing among the angels, where neither will there be an adversary to be winnowed, nor a sluggard to be stirred up out of the earth.

VERS. 33.-- *Let them see.* [n: (CAS.)] That the offering is now in the mind, not in things, where the poor man is made equal to the rich. And, O ye poor, do this nevertheless with your gaze upon God: *seek ye the Lord.* [var: Vulg. Ps 68:33 *quaerite Deum*, "seek ye God"] Or, that you may see, seek God, [0950D] the food of the mind. *The bound.* [n: (AUG.)] He who seeks beyond what is needful loads on fetters; but sufficient for the day is its own evil. *The bound.* [n: (ID.)] Bound by mortality, wherewith the Lord, being offended, put fetters on his servants: but from thence he hears them crying out.

VERS. 35.-- *Heaven, earth, sea.* [n: (CAS.)] According to the letter, that is, the heavenly things, the earthly, and the swimming things: these praise him, while from them the creator is praised. [n: (ID.)] It is the riches of a poor man to see created things, and thence to praise the creator. *Let them praise him,* etc. [n: (ID.)] What before he urged all together, now he urges one by one; or, let the heavenly things, the earthly, the swimming things praise him. And, that he may perfectly include all things, he adds: *All things that are in them:* [var: Vulg. Ps 68:35 *et omnia reptilia in eis*, "and all the creeping things in them"] thus every creature is invited to praise. [n: (HIER., CAS.)] As if he should say: *Let every [0951A] knee bow, of those in heaven, on earth, and under the earth [n: (Phil. II)]*. *And all the creeping things in them.* [n: (CAS.)] All monstrous kinds, sticking fast in earthly sins.

VERS. 36.-- *For God will save Sion,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] In the hope of the faithful people the building of the Church is brought to its end: whence every creature is urged to praise.

VERS. 37.-- *And they that love his name,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] Why go through them one by one? all who love thy name shall dwell there. [n: (AUG.)] These are the seed of the servants. For this verse expounds the preceding one.

## PSALM LXIX.

VERS. 1.-- *In remembrance.* [n: (CAS.)] Memory in two ways, etc., *as far as* and they take up the hope of the resurrection. [n: (AUG.)] The voice of those in tribulation for Christ, etc., [0951B] *as far as* first asking that they be helped, and that the wicked be put to shame.

VERS. 2.-- *O God, unto my help.* He urges that we glory in God, not in ourselves. [n: (CAS.)] Here every petition is an affirmation, whence tribulations are not to be feared. This little verse is said beforehand at the beginning of all the hours, because, when we are wont to pray, the devil strives most of all to tempt us, so that dying flies may spoil the sweetness of the ointment.

VERS. 3.-- *Let them be confounded.* [n: (CAS.)] To be confounded is to be troubled over one's perversity. *Let them be ashamed,* lest punishment, an offspring from justice, follow them. *That seek,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] The times in the Church are diverse: first there was the onset of persecutors, whence, *that seek;* now there remains the ill will of those who plot, whence, *that will.* [n: (ID.)]

[0951C]VERS. 4.-- *Let them be turned backward.* [n: (ID.)] There are two kinds of persecutors, etc., *as far as* he comes forth whole who is caught by neither.

VERS. 5.-- *Let all rejoice and be glad in thee.* [n: (CAS.)] He urges the good to be glad, whom, being needy, God helps and delivers. *Always.* Because he ought not ever to cease from his praise. *Let God be magnified.* [n: (CAS.)] [var: Vulg. Ps 69:5 *Magnificetur Dominus*, "Let the Lord be magnified"] Said after our manner, where one grows by praises; for it is not God, but we, who by praising make progress.

VERS. 6.-- *But I am needy.* [n: (CAS.)] What I exhort, I do, etc., *as far as* where he is rich, he ever goes about, desires and receives. *Delay not.* [n: (ID.)] That seems slow which is sought with great desire. [n: (AUG.)] Lest what thou art to come should seem to me too slow — which comes about through patience; and he who lacks it, to him it seems slow, and [0951D] he is turned aside from it: like Lot's wife, who became a pillar of salt, that she might season thee: for she is an example to thee, that thou look not back.
