## PSALM LXXI.

VERS. 1.-- *Unto Solomon,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] The true peaceable one, etc., *as far as* he who is here commended according to [0954A] each nature. [n: (CAS.)] The fourth psalm concerning the two natures of Christ admonishes that we should believe Christ, true God and true man, to be king, and it is a prophecy about Christ.

VERS. 2.-- *O God, judgment.* [n: (CAS.)] Concerning the judgment, in wishing that it be given to the Son. *Thy justice,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Note that repetitions of the same thing, or in other words, greatly commend the divine utterances, which are chiefly in the psalms: and in that kind of speech by which the affection of the mind is to be moved.

VERS. 3.-- *Mountains.* [n: (AUG.)] The Prophets, the Apostles, who by their height come near to heaven; *peace* to be announced to the faithful people, Christ. *And the hills.* Let them receive *justice,* that is, Christ, or *the mountains peace for the people:* and *hills* he says of both sorts of preachers, those going before and [0954B] those following. Or *let the mountains receive,* that is, the greater ones.

*Peace.* The word of reconciliation, namely the word of the Gospel, to this end, that they may announce it to the people.

*And the hills.* That is, the lesser ones; justice, that is, obedience, that they may obey the teaching of truth.

By saying *thine* and *thy own,* he shows that all things which are the Father's are the Son's also, and conversely.

VERS. 4.-- *He shall judge the poor of the people.* [n: (CAS.)] He treats the same matter concerning the same judgment, by affirming it. After the wish, by the power of prophecy, he says what things are to be furnished at the Lord's coming. [n: (AUG.)] Or according to another reading: *Justice shall judge the poor man.* But those codices which have [it] in the letter are the more approved. *The poor of the people.* That is, those who are poor among the people, in which the good and the bad are [0954C] mingled together. *He shall humble the calumniator.* For while he helps by his grace, etc., *as far as* he who strives to drag the innocent into guilt. [n: (HIERON., AUG.)] Or, *he shall humble,* and, the devil being conquered, *there shall remain with the sun* a brightness; that is, the Son with the Father, sitting at the Father's right hand for ever, that is *before the moon,* unto generation and generation.

VERS. 7.-- *He shall come down like rain,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] Behold who and of what sort is that king who is to judge. [n: (AUG.)] He alludes to the deed of Gedeon, etc., *as far as* and afterwards the Gentile world was rained upon, the fleece of the Jews being dry. [n: (CAS.)] Christ shall come down, etc., *as far as* who then, after such open likenesses, doubts of the Virgin's childbearing? *Into the fleece, and like drops.* [n: (AUG.)] *The fleece,* Judaea, which was to be despoiled of the authority of doctrine, as a sheep [0954D] of its fleece: or, because it withheld the rain of doctrine, which it was unwilling to preach to the Gentiles.

VERS. 8.-- *And he shall rule from sea to sea.* [n: (CAS.)] He shows what the breadth of the kingdom is. *From the river.* [n: (AUG.)] From the Jordan, where the voice was:. *This is my beloved Son.* Thence therefore his teaching began: thence the authority of the master is spread as far as the ends of the earth, the Gospel of the kingdom being preached. [n: (CAS.)] Thence the rule of baptism flowed out through the whole world. In few words he teaches great sacraments.

VERS. 10.-- *The kings of Tharsis and the islands shall offer presents.* [n: (CAS.)] The obstinate are called enemies, etc., *as far as* or, it can be taken historically of the kings who came to adore Christ.

[0955A]VERS. 12.-- *For he shall deliver the poor man from the mighty,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] He treats of his works, etc., *as far as* in the participation of justice.

VERS. 14.-- *From usuries.* [n: (AUG.)] *Usuries,* sins, etc., *as far as* by helping through grace to the doing of justice. Or, *from usuries,* concerning what is to come, that is, eternal punishments; and *iniquity,* that is, sin itself, for which the punishment was owed. *Name.* The Christian name. *Before him.* Because in his kingdom they shall live before him.

VERS. 15.-- *And he shall live, and to him shall be given of the gold,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] Concerning the honour paid to the king. *Of Arabia.* [n: (AUG.)] By Arabia, the Gentiles; by gold, wisdom, which excels among the teachings. *Of him always.* [n: (ID.)] By multiplying, or by obtaining, or concerning his institution, because he himself taught us to pray.

[0955B]VERS. 16.-- *A firmament.* [n: (AUG.)] It belongs to the Prophets, because what they foretold he fulfilled. *On the tops of the mountains.* [n: (ID.)] The authors of the Scriptures, the Prophets and Apostles; whom he strengthens, because all things foretold by them have been fulfilled in him.

*In the earth.* Because for the sake of those who are on the earth all things were written. Whence he himself also came into the earth, that he might fulfil them.

*Above Libanus.* [n: (AUG.)] As Libanus brings forth tall cedars, so Christ brings forth saints to heaven. *Like hay.* [n: (CAS.)] It is not age that is compared to the old man, but greenness to the saints. The good, departing from this city of the present Church, shall flourish in that blessedness. [n: (AUG.)] In Genesis under the name of *hay* grain also is understood, etc., *as far as* and whatever [0955C] is held for great in this world shall be compared to the hay of the earth [sic: *corparabitur*].

VERS. 17.-- *Let his name be blessed,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] He admonishes all to speak praises to him, because all shall be blessed in him. *Before the sun.* [n: (ID.)] That is, before every creature, lest thou think the Son of God temporal. *All nations.* [n: (AUG.)] Lest thou take it of the twelve tribes, he adds *all nations.*

VERS. 18.-- *Blessed be the Lord God of Israel,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] He praises from his own part. Note the exclamatory blessing, with admonition and prophecy. *So be it, so be it,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] It is a magnificent thing, that he concludes thus: it belongs to one who wishes, to say it once; to one who desires exceedingly, to repeat it. As if he said: What thou hast commanded, Lord, so let it be done, let it be done, until what began from the river may reach as far as the ends of the earth. [n: (HIER.)] It is to be known that the seventy-[0955D]first psalm is the end of the second book: whence also at the end it has: *So be it, so be it,* for which in the Hebrew it is said: *Amen, amen.*

VERS. 20.-- *The praises have failed.* [n: (ID.)] This also is to be observed the more diligently, that that saying: *The hymns of David the son of Jesse have failed,* [var: Vulg. Ps 71:20 *laudes*, "praises"] belongs to the end of the seventy-first psalm. But that which follows: *A psalm of Asaph,* is the beginning of the next one.

A hymn has three things: the praise of God with a song: for unless it be sung joyfully, it is not a hymn: and he does not sing who does not love. If there be praise, and it is not of God, it is not a hymn: and if it be of God, and not with a song, it is not a hymn. Beyond his custom he adds: *the son of Jesse;* for David, a man, a certain son of Jesse, reigned in [0956A] Israel in the time of the Old Testament, in which the New was, but hidden: just as Christ also was in David and in Abraham, but hidden, as the fruit is in the root, yet does not appear. Now few of the Prophets knew both Christ and the New Testament, namely in secret, and they declared that it was to be revealed. In that time, therefore, for that kingdom in which the man, the son of Jesse, reigned, for carnal things of this sort a carnal people praised God. But that kingdom and the rest of this sort failing, the praise fails, not of the Son of God, but of *the son of Jesse.* And these things failing which God withdrew, that those things might be sought which lay hidden in them, *Asaph* speaks, that is, the Synagogue, but a faithful one, which piously worshipped God, although for the sake of earthly things, which it sought from God, not from demons, as the Gentiles did. But observing [0956B] blasphemers to abound in these things, for the sake of which it served, it began to waver, not knowing what lay hidden in these figures, as though there were not anything better which God might give: but admonished by these things withdrawn from it to seek those things which it does not have in common with the wicked, chastising itself with wonder, it says: *How good is Israel!* But to whom? *To the upright in heart.* [var: Vulg. Ps 72:1 *his qui recto sunt corde*, "to them that are of a right heart"] First therefore, describing the wickedness of the wicked, it says that it was jealous of their prosperity; yet corrected from that, and being corrected it corrects others.

[n: (CAS.)] Here it is treated, that *the praises have failed:* because God now does not give pleasant things, as to boys who are to be enticed to serve; and this the Synagogue says, which now bears witness against itself against that which it did before, that it may be the more credible.
