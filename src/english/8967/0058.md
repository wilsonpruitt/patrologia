## PSALM LXXVI.

VERS. 1.-- *Unto the end, for Idithum.* [n: (AUG.)] The congregation speaks, passing over, that it may arrive at the end, who is Christ. It teaches therefore to trample upon the desires and enticements of the world, to set nothing before itself, save him from whom [0965A] all things are. [n: (CAS.)] Transcending all things, it seeks God and finds him.

VERS. 2.-- *I cried to the Lord with my voice.* [n: (AUG.)] With the intention of devotion toward the Lord, seeking nothing else from him, because he is Lord, and because he is God. *My voice unto him.* [var: Vulg. Ps 76:2 *voce mea ad Deum*, "with my voice unto God"] The voice is for this, for the sake of which it is uttered; this he adds, lest he be thought to cry to God for anything else than for himself, above whom there is nothing. [n: (CAS.)] He does not say what he asks, because there is no need that it be said to him who knows what is expedient. *He hath attended to me.* [n: (CAS.)] *For God to attend* is to give the whole of what is asked, and more than is asked.

VERS. 3.-- *In the day of my trouble I sought.* [n: (AUG.)] How didst thou seek? etc., *as far as* he who does not do justice before men, that he may be seen. *He refused to be comforted.* [n: (ID.)] He expounds the tribulation, etc., *as far as* [0965B] he who in all men either finds or fears a scandal, is secure in none.

VERS. 4.-- *And I was exercised.* [n: (AUG.)] Or, *I chattered,* that is, I exulted, speaking as a chatterer, who, for gladness, neither would nor could be silent: and again in chattering the spirit failed, which before had wasted away with weariness of evils.

VERS. 5.-- *They prevented the watches.* [n: (CAS.)] Diapsalma. God being found, who alone is good, he deliberates: will he bring him back? [n: (AUG.)] They prevented the watches of others. *My eyes,* like that man who rises before the time to his business. Or, *my eyes,* my affections. *They prevented,* the diligence of others. Or, by *watches,* he signifies the night offices which we make, and which we usually so call among the praises of God, and therefore: *Troubled.* [n: (CAS.)] [0965C] Because I could not obtain what I seek. *I spoke not.* I did not murmur against God. [sic: *Dum*] Or, *I was troubled,* because while by night he searches out the secrets of the mind, he sees the human race rushing into sins: and he is tormented with a loving grief for others, who has already passed beyond vices. *He is silent,* because by night he has not the solace of human conversation, at which time he thinks more warily; therefore he subjoins: *I thought upon the days,* etc. *My eyes.* Otherwise *my enemies prevented the watches.* [n: (AUG.)] That is, they watched over me, etc., *as far as* yet he does not cease, he wishes to transcend this also, whence he subjoins:

VERS. 6.-- *I thought upon the days of old,* etc. Therefore troubled: because *I thought upon the ancient days of Adam,* in which man fell. But over against *the ancient days, the years* he sets *eternal:* therefore *troubled,* because man desires these, and the eternal [0965D] he neglects. *I thought upon the days of old, and I had in my mind the eternal years.* [n: (CAS.)] What befell Adam for his disobedience? From him I have known this defect of man to be. And the eternal years, which by persisting he would have had, I had in my mind, which the foolish do not. *And moreover I meditated in the night,* that is, in this defect. *With my heart,* that is, with reason, whether in any way I might return to the eternal years. He speaks silently with reason, who is quietly exercised in it.

VERS. 7.-- *And I was exercised.* In that very meditation: *I swept my spirit;* I purged the mind. Others [read] *I winnowed my spirit,* thus dealing with myself:

VERS. 8.-- *Will he cast off for ever,* the human race? *that he may be more favourable.* As one who has already been [0966A] favourable by his grace in the Old Testament: will he not add that he may be more so in the New through the coming of the Son? this he thinks, who treats of God's goodness. *I was exercised and I swept.* [n: (AUG.)] Or, *I chattered,* not now outwardly, etc., *as far as* where he finds this, that *God will not cast off for ever. That he may be more favourable,* namely that he himself, who was angry by casting off, by bringing in evils, may be gentler by delivering, by calling back.

VERS. 10.-- *Or will God forget to shew mercy.* [n: (AUG.)] In another manner, there is no mercy unless God bestow it: and he himself will be without it. The stream runs, the fountain is dried up.

VERS. 11.-- *And I said: Now have I begun.* [n: (CASS.)] What he found by deliberating, namely that Christ changes men, and redeems them. [n: (ID.)] *And I said.* To this meditation, [0966B] etc., *as far as* when, passing beyond myself also, I came to God, here there is no danger. *I have begun.* [n: (AUG.)] Something, where I am secure, where enemies do not prevent me, now the Most High has begun to change me. *The change of the right hand,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] From wrath to mercy, in that of servants there are made sons. [n: (ID.)] Of this change, etc., *as far as* these are the things that soothe a holy man.

VERS. 13.-- *In thy inventions.* [n: (AUG.)] That is, in the precepts which thou hast invented, or by which thou art found. [n: (CAS.)] Or *in thy observances,* that is, in the precepts which are to be observed, I shall be exercised: because it is not enough to read the Scriptures, but it behoves to show the fruit of works: instructed by these he breaks forth into praises. [n: (AUG.)] *In thy inventions* or *thy affections,* not in mine; or there is affection in earthly things: so in the works and in the meditation [0966C] of God there is a certain inward affection, when the world is beheld, and in creatures the creator is sought, and is found nowhere displeasing, but everywhere pleasing.

VERS. 14.-- *O God, in the holy place.* [n: (AUG.)] By the things aforesaid I am certain that I shall come, and with him for guide. [n: (ID.)] *O God, in the holy place,* that is, in Christ, the way of returning to thee. And since this is so, *who is a great God?* etc. *Thy way.* [n: (ID.)] Of worshipping thee and of coming to thee, whence he joyfully prefers the power of God to the images, which were falsely worshipped.

VERS. 15.-- *Wonders.* [n: (AUG.)] The deaf, the blind, the feeble, the dead, the paralytic were healed. These things were done in bodies. In the soul certain men are sober, who a little before were drunkards, unbelievers, adorers of images. *To the peoples thy power.* [n: (ID.)] *To the unfaithful, that* they may know [0966D] Christ only in body, *to the faithful,* in reality and in Godhead; he subjoins: What thou hast bestowed, *thou hast redeemed.*

VERS. 16.-- *The sons of Joseph.* [var: Vulg. Ps 76:16 *filios Jacob et Joseph*] [n: (AUG.)] That is, the Gentile people. Or, by *Joseph* Christ is understood: because, as Joseph, sold into Egypt, reigned, so Christ reigned among the Gentiles, having first been humbled in the martyrs. [n: (ID.)] *Joseph,* who came to the Gentiles, is interpreted *increase:* and he signifies the Gentiles, from whom the people of God has been increased. Or, *Joseph,* Christ sold by the Jews, and driven to the Gentiles, *his sons,* whom he chose out of the Gentiles.

VERS. 17.-- *They saw.* [n: (CAS.)] Diapsalma. He expounds what things Christ works in the world. [n: (AUG.)] As if he should say: Since thou hast made thy power known, either through him [0967A] *they saw thee, O God the Father,* or, *thee, O Christ,* who art the power and the arm, etc.

VERS. 18.-- *A multitude.* [n: (CAS.)] Not a few, but *a multitude. The noise,* in the groaning for faults, in the giving of thanks: for the divers vows of the peoples sound as it were a broken sea. *The noise of waters.* [n: (AUG.)] Whence are these noises of waters, this troubling of the deeps? because *the clouds have uttered a voice,* of which: *I will command my clouds that they rain no rain upon it.* [var: Vulg. Is 5:6 *et nubibus mandabo ne pluant super eam imbrem*]

VERS. 19.-- *The voice of thunder.* [n: (CAS.)] The voice of thunder is rolled about, so that a wrinkled and winding sound is perceived. An allegory: *in the wheel* of the whole world. *A wheel.* [n: (HIER., AUG.)] It is that which he expounds as the circle of the earth, which the clouds went about, thundering with precepts, flashing with miracles. *The earth trembled.* [n: (CAS.)] They who dwell in the earth. That very [0967B] earth is called a sea by a likeness, because human life is bitter, subject to tempests, where the greater fish devours the lesser.

VERS. 20.-- *In the sea.* [n: (AUG.)] To the letter, when Christ walked upon the waters. Or, in the hearts of men, whom he subjects to himself. Or, by *the land,* the Jews; by *the sea,* the Gentiles are understood. *And thy footsteps shall not be known.* A silent rebuke of the Jews; as if he should say: Although he came openly, and showed himself by miracles, he was not known by the unbelieving Jews whom he led forth in the hand of Moses and Aaron, that is, by the working of the miracles which they did. *Thy footsteps,* etc. He touched some, that is, the Jews, who say, Christ has not yet come; they do not see him walking upon the sea.

VERS. 21.-- *As sheep.* Because they are not truly sheep, but [0967C] goats. *Of Moses and Aaron.* [n: (CAS.)] Moses is interpreted [sic: *intrepretatur*] *taken up from the river;* Aaron, *a mountain of strength:* by these names the powers which they wrought are expressed. Moses among other things made a way in the sea, signifying that the people is delivered through baptism. Aaron held the image of the Church, which is compared to a most strong mountain.
