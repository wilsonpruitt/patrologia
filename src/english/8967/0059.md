## PSALM LXXVII.

VERS. 1.-- *Understanding.* [n: (AUG.)] Because these things narrated, etc., *as far as* the things that seem open are parables and riddles. *Attend.* [n: (CAS.)] First the Lord himself speaks, that reverence may grow. *My people,* who obey me. The mouth of God is Asaph, who spoke these things below: *How great things we have heard from the beginning.* Of the Old Testament: or, the faithful Synagogue reproaches the wicked.

[0967D]VERS. 2.-- *In parables.* [n: (AUG.)] Parables are likenesses of things. *Propositions.* Which in Greek are *problemata,* questions having something to be resolved.

VERS. 3.-- *How great things.* [n: (CAS.)] He tells summarily what God did for the Jews: yet for men ungrateful to him, and thus, if not to them, it profits the sons nevertheless, lest they become as their fathers. [n: (AUG.)] First God spoke in a man, now the man himself: *How great things we have heard.* That foretold word God spoke from himself, lest his speech be despised.

VERS. 5.-- *And he raised up,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] This testimony. *The beginning,* whence above: *I will utter propositions from the beginning.* The beginning is the Old Testament, the end the New. There is fear, which is the beginning: but here is Christ, *the end of the law unto righteousness to every one that believeth [n: (Rom. X)]*, who gives the Spirit, [0968A] whence: *Charity, which drives out fear [n: (I Joan. IV)]* [var: Vulg. 1 Jo 4:18 *perfecta caritas foras mittit timorem*, "perfect charity casteth out fear"]. The old law is called the testimony of this New Testament, because it has testimony from the law and the Prophets: whence that was called the tabernacle of testimony which prefigured so many mystical things: *Testimony* therefore the Old Testament is called in so far as it proves and testifies, law in so far as it commands. [n: (CASS., REM.)] *He raised up* the testimony as though dead in Adam, that is, the law; or, while it is written down it is raised up, having been lulled asleep before by ignorance. *A testimony in Jacob,* this perhaps is that Jacob, having wrestled with the angel, went halt when his thigh was touched: which signifies that the Jews partly stand fast in faith, partly depart from salvation: and so what befell Jacob was a testimony of this thing. Behold a parable. *To their sons.* And these same sons are another generation, namely the people of grace.

[0968B]VERS. 6.-- *Another generation.* Through the generations the commandments of God were handed on, lest any think them given to one only.

VERS. 7.-- *That they may set.* This is the profit of the fathers' preaching. *Let them seek out.* [n: (AUG.)] Those who were said above to have known, now *let them seek out,* that hoping in God they may be helped by him to fulfil it.

VERS. 8.-- *Froward.* [n: (CAS.)] Which does not receive the truth, and is therefore twisted, *provoking* beyond all bitternesses, so that they were armed against the Saviour. Whence by Ezechiel the Lord says: *Go and speak to the provoking house [n: (Ezech. II)]*. *It set not its heart aright.* [n: (ID.)] He has a twisted heart who does not correct what he has ill understood, and does not perform what nature teaches: *His spirit was not trusted with God [n: (Joan. I)]*, that is, and if reason persuaded him well, he yielded to it. [0968C] *It was not trusted.* [n: (AUG.)] When the spirit of man works together with the Spirit of God working, that is done which God commands: and this is not save by believing in him who justifies the ungodly, which faith the froward and embittering generation has not.

[n: (ID.)] He speaks much more expressly, etc., *as far as* but that generation, although it believed God, yet not in God.

VERS. 9.-- *Shooting the bow.* [n: (AUG.)] He speaks figuratively, etc., *as far as* David to his brethren, so the Christian people to the people of the Jews. Or, *The sons of Ephrem.* [n: (ID.)] *Bending and shooting the bow,* that is, shooting something with a bow. Or, *bending the bow,* and shooting arrows, that is, promising to keep the law, saying openly: *All things whatsoever the Lord shall say to us, we will do [0968D] and we will hear [n: (Exod. XXIV)]* [var: Vulg. Ex 24:7 *Omnia quae locutus est Dominus, faciemus, et erimus obedientes*, "all things that the Lord hath spoken we will do, and we will be obedient"]. *They were turned* and failed *in the day of battle,* that is, of temptation; whence Osee the prophet says: *Ephraim is become [sic: facti] a dove [sic: *fuctus*] without understanding, not having a heart [n: (Ose. VII)]* [var: Vulg. Os 7:11 *factus est Ephraim quasi columba seducta non habens cor*, "as a dove that is decoyed"], because not hearing, but temptation proves the promise of obedience.

*Of his benefits.* [var: Vulg. Ps 77:11 *benefactorum*, "his good deeds"] [n: (EUTHYM.)] Of the benefits which he conferred upon the people of Israel, as, that he led them through the Red Sea unwetted. *Of his wonders* however he says, because he had turned the rivers of the Egyptians into blood. But as to his saying that God showed these things to them, he does not say it as though they themselves had seen them with their own eyes, but because they received them so from their elders.

VERS. 12.-- *Before their fathers,* etc. [n: (CAS.)] He begins now to tell what he did for them from the leading out of Egypt: [0969A] and yet they did not cease from murmuring and from temptation. [n: (AUG.)] *Before the fathers.* Who the fathers are, etc., *as far as* the wood of the cross coming to it as the rod of Moses. *In the land of Egypt.* [n: (ID.)] That he may take away all doubt, he names both the province and the place of the deeds. So Christ, set upon the earth, knew before the fathers, and taught the humble commandment: *Learn of me, for I am meek and humble of heart. [sic: *Discit*] [n: (Joan. VII.)]*

VERS. 14.-- *In a cloud.* [n: (HIER. et CASS.)] This they judged to be future. *The day* is Christ, yet he is hidden from the faithless by the *cloud* of the flesh; and as for them the *cloud* against the heat of the sun, so for us Christ through the incarnation gives refreshment against the heat of vices. *All the night,* all the time of life. *In the light of fire.* The fire of the Holy [0969B] Spirit keeps Christians in this world by the brightness of his name.

VERS. 15.-- *The rock.* [n: (CASS.)] *In the wilderness.* Before, the water of the sea was as a rock, now the rock is a fountain. *In the wilderness,* where the gift must needs be the more welcome. *He gave them drink.* As cattle, not as men, because they gave no thanks.

VERS. 16.-- *As.* [n: (CASS.)] The water which comes forth from Christ fails by no diminishing, but becomes a fountain of water springing up unto life everlasting.

VERS. 17.-- *To sin yet more.* [n: (CASS.)] They added this, that amid so many benefits they did not believe: and that this might be understood as the greatest sin, when the Lord had said in the Gospel: *The Spirit shall convince the world of sin,* he added by way of exposition: *Of sin indeed, because they believe not in me [n: (Joann. IV, XVI)]* [var: Vulg. Jo 16:9 *quia non crediderunt in me*, "because they have not believed in me"]. As therefore he there calls sin *not to believe,* so here by *to sin* he means, not to believe. [0969C] *Unto wrath.* [n: (ID.)] Not believing that God could do hard things for them, which is most grievous in the sight of God; so we too, washed by baptism, provoke God to wrath. *In the waterless place.* [n: (AUG.)] Either in another wilderness according to the letter, or rather in themselves, because although the skins were full of water from the rock, their minds nevertheless were dry.

VERS. 19.-- *A table.* [n: (CASS.)] Mystically, the refreshment of wisdom, or the body of Christ.

VERS. 21.-- *Therefore he heard.* [n: (CASS.)] In that, vengeance being deferred, he gave first what they were asking, lest he should seem not to be able. *He deferred.* [n: (AUG.)] The punishment; giving first what it was asking, though by way of tempting, lest he should seem not to be able: and after he had given, fire, that is wrath, went up upon Israel. And this is what he said before: *A fire was kindled in Jacob.*

[0969D]VERS. 23.-- *And he commanded.* Both were set down briefly, namely that he heard, and that wrath went up; here he begins to tell it openly. And first he follows out how he heard, afterwards in the following part how he took vengeance. *And the gates of heaven.* [n: (CASS.)] The gates are the preachers themselves, by whom entry is made into heaven; or the Scriptures. *He opened.* Because he gave his Spirit to his own, that they might understand the Scriptures.

VERS. 24.-- *Manna to eat.* [n: (AUG.)] It signifies the bread which came down from heaven, which is the food of angels: which, that man might eat it, *the Word was made flesh [n: (Joan. I)]*, who is rained upon the whole world through the evangelical clouds, and, their hearts being opened as heavenly gates, is preached to the believing Church, [0970A] not to the tempting Synagogue. [n: (CASS.)] *Manna, what is this?* because this food is sought after with wonder, and is known to be the body of Christ. *The bread of heaven.* [n: (ID.)] No other than Christ, by whom the heavenly ones, that is the angels, are refreshed in the contemplation of him; and this bread, that is the Word, on which great food the angels are fed, made milk through the flesh, man eats.

VERS. 26.-- *The south-west wind.* Because the quails are there.

VERS. 27.-- *And he rained upon them,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] So also a little faith, etc., *as far as* whence: *If I have told you earthly things, and you believe me not, how, if I tell you heavenly things?* etc. [n: (Joan. III.)] [var: Vulg. Jo 3:12 *Si terrena dixi vobis, et non creditis : quomodo, si dixero vobis caelestia, credetis*, "how, if I shall speak to you heavenly things, will you believe"]

*As dust.* [n: (CASS.)] *Dust* here signifies the subtlety of understanding, which climbs to the things above. *Sand,* the countless abundance of prudence seasoned [0970B] with the savour of salt. *Feathered fowls.* Heavenly desires, on which the soul is fed and grows fat: these we can receive within the enclosures of the Church, whence he adds:

VERS. 28.-- *And they fell in the midst of their camp.* [n: (AUG.)] Signifying by this that the winds come of their own accord to men, and carry to them the words divinely sent, that they may hear them in their own seats, and about their tents: because their sound went out into all the earth.

VERS. 29.-- *And they did eat.* They were convicted, who had thought this impossible to God.

VERS. 30.-- *As yet their meats,* etc., *and the wrath of God,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Of the punishments, and of their feigned returnings, through fear of penalties: and yet the sentence of God is tempered by mercy. *Their meats.* [n: (ID.)] The manna, the quails. *He hindered the chosen of Israel.* [n: (AUG.)] That [0970C] is, he hindered them from profiting those whom they desired to counsel with a father's affection. Or rather, he fettered them, that the chosen might bear troubles together with them for an example, not of righteousness only, but of patience also. *He hindered.* [n: (ID.)] By their murmuring, as Moses, who said: *Can we bring forth water for you out of the rock? [n: (Num. XX.)]*

VERS. 32.-- *In all these things they sinned still, and they believed not in his wondrous works.* Corrected after all these things, the sins are the graver, which ever increase.

VERS. 33.-- *And they failed,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Whereas they might, by believing, have had their days in truth without failing. *Their days in vanity and their years.* [n: (CASS.)] From the likeness of him who dies at his rising: he was born in vain. *With haste.* [n: (AUG.)] And the life which seems the longer [0970D] passes quickly.

VERS. 34.-- *They sought him.* [n: (CASS. et AUG.)] Not those slain, but those left. Yet he speaks thus because they are one people, as of one body. *Early they came to him,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Diligently, or before the light; whence: *It is vain for you to rise before the light.* The godly man prays in the light, the ungodly before the light. *They sought.* They returned, they came. [n: (AUG.)] All these things for the sake of gaining temporal goods and of avoiding evils: and so not God, but those things were worshipped.

VERS. 37.-- *Neither were they counted faithful in the covenant,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] He who truly believes does works: whence faith is so called because the things said are done; whence he says: *Nor faithful in his precepts.*
