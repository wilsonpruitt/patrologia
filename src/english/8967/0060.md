## PSALM LXXVII. (cont.)

[0971A]VERS. 38.-- *But he is merciful, and will be propitious.* [n: (AUG.)] Here the wicked promise themselves impunity, but there is mercy even for the wicked, yet before the judgment, because he makes the sun to rise upon the good and the evil. So he spared that nation also, that he might not root it out utterly, but it abides in the succession of those that come after, as Cain when he had received the sign, that none should slay him. *Their sins,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Of the people, which is the same in the succession of the sons; as also afterwards *he took them away out of Egypt, and brought them into the mountain of his sanctification [n: (Gen. IV)]*, whereas not them, save in the succession. So here too, when it is said: *he will be propitious to their sins,* we understand that also to be fulfilled which is said by the Apostle: *The remnant shall be saved [n: (Rom. IX)]*, which shall believe in Christ, and this he foresaw here, when he adds: *And he scattered them not. And he abounded that [0971B] he might turn away,* [var: Vulg. Ps 77:38 *et non disperdet eos*, "and he will not destroy them"] etc., because: *He kindled not all his wrath,* etc. That is, he did not punish them as much as they deserved: because he forgave even this, that they slew the Son.

VERS. 39.-- *A spirit that goeth and returneth not,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ps 77:39 *spiritus vadens et non rediens*, "that goeth and cometh not again"] Therefore he calls them back by grace, because by themselves they cannot return: whence in the Proverbs it is said of the way of iniquity: *All that walk in it shall not return [n: (Prov. II)]* [var: Vulg. Pr 2:19 *Omnes qui ingrediuntur ad eam non revertentur*, "all that go in unto her shall not return"], namely by themselves, but by grace they shall be called back. This is the wandering sheep, which was brought back to the flock upon the shoulders of the loving shepherd.

VERS. 40.-- *How often.* [n: (CASS.)] Being about to tell the plagues of Egypt, wrought for their sake, by comparing their evils with the benefits of God, he says that they forgot, and that they tempted God. [n: (AUG.)] Because they are flesh, or a spirit that goes, how often then? as though to say, they provoked him many times.

[0971C]VERS. 41.-- *And they turned back,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] He repeats their unbelief, that he may call to mind what plagues God brought upon the Egyptians for their sake, whence they ought to have been not ungrateful.

VERS. 42.-- *From the hand of tribulation.* [var: Vulg. Ps 77:42 *de manu tribulantis*, "from the hand of him that afflicted them"] [n: (CASS.)] Tribulation is wont to be kept in memory, but not by them. Beautifully he sets the hand of God against the hand of Pharao, saying: *His hand and from the hand of [sic: tribu];* which differ greatly. [n: (AUG. et CASS.)] Mystical interpretations from this place.

VERS. 44, 45.-- *And he turned,* etc. *He sent upon them wrath,* etc. Here he begins to set forth the plagues wrought in Egypt for the deliverance of the Jews, and he sets down ten plagues, as in Exodus ten. Yet certain things are set down here which are not in Exodus, which we cannot interpret save figuratively, where also the things that were done are called parables, [0971D] as he is often wont not to follow the order of the history, but to add other things, or to change certain things for the sake of the signification. *Water* therefore *into blood* signifies to think carnally of the causes of things. *The dog-fly.* [var: Vulg. Ps 77:45 *coenomyiam*, the common fly] They are dog-like manners. *The frog.* Talkative vanity.

VERS. 46, 47.-- *Blight.* Which others interpret as rust, harms in secret, and is likened to a vice that hardly appears, such as to trust much in oneself. For a noxious breath works this in the fruits unseen: so in manners does hidden pride, when he thinks himself to be something who is nothing. *The locust.* Malice hurting with the mouth, namely by faithless testimony.

*Hail.* It is iniquity carrying off the goods of another; whence thefts, robberies, plunderings. But the waster himself is more wasted [0972A] by his own vice. *Frost.* The vice by which charity toward one's neighbour freezes in the darkness of folly as with the cold of night.

VERS. 48.-- *Fire.* Which in Exodus is not taken save with the hail in the lightnings, that it might hurt the fruits: which nevertheless is not read to have burned the possessions, signifies the monstrousness of anger, by which murder also is done. By the death of the cattle the loss of chastity is signified. Concupiscence, from which offspring arises, is common to us with the beasts: to have this tamed and ordered is the virtue of chastity. *The death of the firstborn.* It is the loss of the righteousness by which each man is sociable to the human race. The Egyptians were smitten with ten plagues against the ten commandments written on the tables. And here are ten plagues, though they be not set down in that order, but in place of three which [0972B] are in Exodus, that is the sciniphs, the boils, the darkness, which are not here. Three others are here which are not there, rust, frost, and fire. For such things are frequently varied for a fitting understanding. *Signs.* [n: (CASS.)] They are, upon hard hearts, as it were the traces of a stamp impressed. *Wonders,* which betokened certain things to come. *Thanis,* [var: Vulg. Ps 77:12 and 43 *Taneos*] a city, the humble commandment, which is necessary for us here, that in the time to come we may be raised up. *And he gave to the blight.* These things are not read in Exodus, therefore let them be expounded only by a mystical interpretation. *The locust.* [n: (AUG.)] Devouring one another, it signifies detractors, who out of envy gnaw the fruits of others. *Their mulberry trees with frost.* [n: (AUG.)] The mulberry is of a warm nature, by which charity is understood, which is killed by the frost. *To the hail* [n: (ID.)] The things the hail could not hurt, that is the soft ones, Exodus afterwards says were eaten [0972C] by the locusts. *He delivered up.* [n: (CASS.)] By the death of the cattle the fall of fools is shown.

[n: (ID.)] *To fire. Fire* is covetousness, which lays waste the possession, that is, the state of the mind.

VERS. 49.-- *The wrath of his indignation,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] The order of vengeance: first God is angry at their sins, and when they are not pricked in heart, then he abandons the malice of man in his own indignation, wrath and tribulation, that they may be afflicted by their own adversities who do not obey God, as it is said there: *Therefore he delivered them up unto a reprobate sense [n: (Rom. I)]*. The indignation of man belongs to swollen pride; wrath, to unspeakable audacity; tribulation, to confounded despair: then through the devil they are hurled headlong into unlawful assaults, stripped of the defence of God. *By evil angels.* [n: (AUG.)] By the judgment of God [0972D] these things were done, etc., *as far as* by evil angels therefore God punishes both good and evil men, but the good with bodily punishments only, the evil with punishments both bodily and spiritual.

VERS. 50.-- *Of his wrath.* [n: (AUG.)] There was a path of the wrath of God, by which with hidden equity he might punish the Egyptians; and for it God made a way, that he might bring them forth as out of hidden places, through evil angels, into open crimes, and so punish them openly.

VERS. 51.-- *The firstborn in Egypt.* [n: (AUG.)] The firstborn are the highest commandments, as: Thou shalt love the Lord thy God with thy whole heart; and when these are not kept we are bereaved of the offspring of reason.

VERS. 52.-- *And he took away his people as sheep, [0973A] and he led them like a flock in the desert.* [n: (AUG.)] Mystically. This is done within us also, where, being taught, we are carried over in mind out of the power of darkness into the kingdom of God, sheep in spiritual pastures. [n: (CASS.)] *And he took away.* In that he led those brought out of bondage into the land of promise; and again they offend.

VERS. 54.-- *And he brought them into the mountain of his sanctification.* [n: (AUG.)] Not the same men whom he took away, but those of the same name and nation; yet it signifies that many were converted to the Church, which he would rather have understood; she is the mountain which Christ purchased. *And he cast out the nations from before them,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Or the malignant spirits. All which things Christ cast out from before the faithful; *and he divided them the land by lot.* By this it is signified that one and the same Spirit works all things in us, dividing [0973B] to every one according as he will.

VERS. 55.-- *In their tents.* [n: (CASS.)] In which sins reigned, he made virtues to reign.

VERS. 57.-- *Even as their fathers were turned,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Throughout he speaks as though of the same men: yet now of those in the land of promise, whose fathers they were who embittered him in the desert. *A crooked bow,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The perverse intention of the mind, as against God, who had said: *Thou shalt not have strange gods [n: (Exod. XX)]* [var: Vulg. Ex 20:3 *Non habebis deos alienos coram me*, "thou shalt not have strange gods before me"], which he opens more fully. *Provoked unto wrath,* or, *into a crooked bow,* which is unbent with difficulty, and they themselves easily went back. Or, *a crooked bow,* which does not shoot from afar, but turns its darts upon itself, that is, the malice of the deceitful.

VERS. 58.-- *To jealousy.* Or they were jealous; [0973C] another reading, when they give the honour of God to another.

VERS. 59.-- *God heard.* [n: (CASS.)] Of the vengeance in the land of promise, which was turned back also upon the very men through whom he punished.

VERS. 60.-- *And he put away the tabernacle.* [n: (AUG.)] At that time of Eli the priest, when they were overcome by the Allophyli, and the ark of the Lord was taken, and they themselves laid waste with a great slaughter. For it is read in the book of Kings that there fell then of the people thirty thousand footmen. *He put away the tabernacle of Silo.* [n: (BEDA.)] Which was in Silo, etc., *as far as* but because they did not attend to it, nor made a tabernacle for God: therefore the Lord deservedly put it away. *He dwelt among men.* [n: (AUG.)] Since therefore there was nothing worthy in which he might dwell, he put away that which he had appointed not for his own sake, but for theirs. [0973D] [n: (CASS.)] So he forsakes our bodies, which the Holy Spirit inhabits while they are well treated.

VERS. 61.-- *And he delivered up.* [n: (CASS.)] For if the good too are delivered into captivity along with sinners, it is a discipline; they are not captives in mind. Or, delivered to the enemy, but free and joined to God. *Their strength.* [n: (AUG.)] He diminished the liberty of choice, and took away the virtues in the wicked, which is not so in the good, although at times they are taken captive bodily along with the wicked.

VERS. 65.-- *And he was awakened.* [n: (CASS. et AUG.)] He ascribes the manners of men to the Lord, for the sake of explaining the matter.

*As one asleep.* This is said in the person of the mad, who think thus, when he does not come to the aid of those in peril, that he is drunk with wine, when he does not come to their aid so [0974A] quickly. And in truth he does sleep for those who so madly think, and is a stranger, both to the negligent and to the unbelieving, who rouse him by no voice of praise or of work. But he is roused to vengeance by evil deeds, as here by the sins of those who set the ark among the idols. *As a mighty man,* because men are then quick to wrath, and mighty for feats of strength, when after a debauch they rise from sleep. [n: (AUG.)] *And he was awakened.* Christ, drunk with the wine of the passion, sleeping in the sepulchre, was awakened from death, as a mighty man in the resurrection. [n: (ID.)] *As one asleep.* He seemed to sleep when he gave his people into the hands of those whom he hates: where it is said to them: *Where is thy God?*

VERS. 66.-- *And he smote iniquity.* [var: Vulg. Ps 77:66 *percussit inimicos suos in posteriora*, "he smote his enemies on the hinder parts"] [n: (AUG.)] He shall be tormented with this punishment, etc., *as far as: reproach* indeed [0974B] *everlasting he gave to them.*

VERS. 67.-- *And he put away.* [n: (CASS.)] Of king David, and of the seat of the kingdom. [n: (AUG.)] He did not say, etc., *as far as* thus he proceeds from the beginning of the election of the Hebrew nation down to Christ.

VERS. 69.-- *As,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Unicorns are they whose firm hope is raised up unto that one thing, of which it is said: *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek after. His sanctuary.* It is the holy people, a royal priesthood.

VERS. 70.-- *David his servant.* [n: (AUG.)] Who of Juda, etc., *as far as: the teeth* of the Church are they through whom she speaks, or through whom she passes the rest into her own body as though by eating. *A flock of shorn sheep* it is called, because it laid down the burdens of the world as fleeces, and laid at the feet of the Apostles the prices of the goods it had sold; [0974C] *coming up from the washing* of baptism, it brings forth twins, that is, the works of the twofold charity; whence: *there was not a barren one among them;* from these Christ was carried over, but he feeds among the Gentiles the flocks which are Jacob and Israel.
