## PSALM LXXVII. (cont.)

VERS. 71.-- *Jacob. Israel his inheritance.* [n: (AUG.)] He repeats it: Unless it be said that now, according to this present state, Jacob is a *servant:* when he shall see face to face, an *inheritance.* Whence also he received the name: for Israel is so called from the contemplation of God. He carried him over to this end, that he might feed him, and so he did.

VERS. 72.-- *And he fed them,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] It seems perhaps, etc., *as far as* and he taught this which he did.

## PSAL. LXXVIII.

VERS. 1.-- *A psalm of Asaph.* [n: (CASS.)] The second psalm of lamentation. He prays Christ to come to the help of the nation which for its hardness is to be grievously [0974D] afflicted. We are admonished to rejoice at the good things of our neighbours, and to be afflicted at their calamities.

*O God, the nations are come.* [n: (ID.)] The first thing is the complaint, and there are numbered how great things Jerusalem bore in the times of Antiochus, when it was not destroyed, and by these the persecution of the Church is signified. [n: (AUG.)] *They are come.* He does not speak as to one that knows not, etc., *as far as* but now they are plainly fulfilled.

*Into thy inheritance.* [n: (ID.)] Of the people of Israel; the inheritance of God are the elect, but not the blinded; therefore these things can be said of the times after the ascension of Christ; but also before Christ had come, for then too there was an inheritance, where the Prophets were, and the saints led away into Babylon; or most of all under Antiochus. [n: (CASS.)] But if [0975A] any man, etc., *as far as* whose circuit is through the whole world, where the persecution also is.

VERS. 2.-- *The dead bodies.* [n: (CASS.)] Carcases, as though they raged not only against the living. To bury belongs to piety, to cast forth belongs to cruelty. *They have poured out.* [n: (ID.)] He heightens it, that he may move the judge.

VERS. 4.-- *We are become.* [n: (CASS.)] The glory of Jerusalem, wonderful in the whole world, is turned into *a reproach to our neighbours,* which is heavier than if it were to men far off, because these are always at hand. Or, the neighbours are the affections, the kinsfolk being now willing to believe, so that the martyrs say this.

VERS. 5.-- *How long, O Lord?* [n: (CASS.)] A prayer on his own behalf against enemies and insulting neighbours; as though they rage thus: But, O Lord, how long? [n: (AUG.)] He asks that he be not angry unto the end, that is, that [0975B] that laying waste may not continue, but that he temper his correction. By the name of *wrath* is understood the vengeance upon iniquity, by the name of *jealousy,* the exacting of chastity, that the soul may not commit fornication away from God. Note that this befalls men for their sins, though they be just, as the three children and the Prophets in the captivity bear witness. *As fire.* [n: (ID.)] Burn so long, until the gold of the virtues be perfectly purged, and the wood, that is the vices, be turned to ashes.

VERS. 7.-- *They have devoured.* [n: (AUG.)] By delivering them to the teeth of wild beasts. *They have laid waste,* by the overthrow of the city, which was stripped of its own citizens, or because they slew those put in the place of the citizens who had been slain; Jacob for the man, the man, that is, for the nation which offended. The patriarch is pleasing, that the memory of him may soften the sins of the people. *Jacob.* [n: (AUG.)] This is the inheritance spoken of above. *The place.* [n: (CASS.)] The city, where the temple was, to which they were gathered for the feasts.

[0975C]VERS. 8.-- *Of our iniquities.* [n: (BEDA.)] That is, those which we have contracted from our fathers, to whom, that is to say, correction is no longer owed, but damnation, unless the compassion of thy grace be at hand. For it is written: *The soul that shall sin, the same shall die.* And, *I will visit the sins of the fathers upon the children, unto the third and fourth generation [n: (Ezech. XVIII.)] Of old.* [var: Vulg. Ex 20:5 *visitans iniquitatem patrum in filios, in tertiam et quartam generationem*, "visiting the iniquity of the fathers upon the children"] (CASS) He does not speak of past ones only, since in this time also certain Jews were following idols; but of the like of the ancient ones, in which they sinned through countless years. *Let them prevent us.* [n: (ID.)] Before the judgment which every man fears, because unless indulgence go before in the judgment, the guilty man is not acquitted. *Exceedingly.* Lest any man presume upon his deeds.

VERS. 9.-- *Help us.* [n: (CASS.)] In the war for the defence of the laws, because they trust not in their own powers, but in the powers of God. [0975D] *For the glory.* Not for our merit; that, being delivered, we may render honour to thy name. *For the glory of thy name.* Let it be ascribed to the goodness of the holy name, not to us, since it cannot be had by our own deeds. *Help us, O God.* By this he is neither ungrateful to grace, nor does he take away free choice. For he who is helped does something also through himself. *Deliver us.* [n: (AUG.)] He delivers, that is, he rescues from evils, since he both helps us to do righteousness, and is propitious to the sins without which there is no living here.

VERS. 10.-- *Lest perhaps.* [n: (AUG.)] That word customary with the faithless: Where is he? Either he is not, or he cannot; an insult not to be borne. *Let it be made known.* Either here iniquity is destroyed in those converted, which he had wished for before, or, at the end [0976A] they perish, which he says here not by wishing it, but by prophesying. [n: (AUG.)] Because the just man is not delighted at the punishment of his enemy, since he does not hate him, but at the divine justice, since he loves God; the unjust man does the contrary.

VERS. 11.-- *Of them that are in fetters.* [n: (AUG.)] By the corruptibility of the flesh, which weighs down the soul, and through which the persecutor sometimes prevails: these fetters none feel save those who groan being burdened; from these the Apostle desired to be loosed, and to be with Christ. Or, the fetters are the commandments of God, by which the saints are bound together, which, patiently borne, are turned into ornaments, whence: *Put thy foot into her fetters [n: (Eccli. VI)]*. [n: (CASS.)] *Of them that are in fetters,* he speaks according to the letter of those whom the Gentiles bound: who pray with groaning that the Church may not fail in her brethren, and so [0976B] the punishment consumes them. *According to the greatness.* [n: (AUG.)] For a thing so great followed among Christians as the enemies would never have believed. *The children,* because they were kindled by the example, not terrified. *Possess the children of them that have been put to death.* [n: (ID.)] This the holy martyrs always prayed, that their blood might not be unfruitful to those that came after, that the crop might grow from that whence it was thought that it would perish. [n: (CASS.)] A godly prayer, that he cast not away the children of those who were punished for him. *Possess,* that they be stained by no error.

VERS. 12.-- *And render.* [n: (CASS.)] Although he renders it bodily, yet it is the salvation of their souls. *Sevenfold,* it signifies the perfection of the heavenly gift, when we are filled with the Holy Spirit. *Into their bosom.* In the secret place of the mind, where conversion takes place. *Their reproach,* let them render praises for injuries: a glorious vindication, when God is praised by the mouth [0976C] of his enemies. Or *sevenfold.* By the sevenfold retribution he signifies the perfection of the punishment, as also of good things, when it is said that he shall receive sevenfold in this world: which is set down for all good things, as having nothing, and possessing all things; because in this number fulness is signified. Or *sevenfold,* for what is without end. *Their reproach,* [n: (AUG.)] Render this, that is, for that reproach give them up to a reprobate sense. For man, when he is given up to a reprobate sense, that is, in his inward sense, receives the desert of the punishments to come.

VERS. 13.-- *But we.* [n: (CASS.)] A conclusion of exultation, not of conquerors, but of sufferers. And a sweet conclusion of the lamentation. [n: (AUG.)] *But we.* These are the remnant gathered by Matathias. Or, generally, it is to be taken of every kind of the godly and of true Christians, [0976D] so that the Christian people also is mingled there; this is the flock for which he prayed, that he might not be angry any longer. [n: (ID.)] Or, *in the generation,* that is, in the world to come we shall declare it, not for the instruction of others, but for the honour that is due.
