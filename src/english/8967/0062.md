## PSALM LXXIX.

VERS. 1.-- *Understanding, for them that shall be changed.* [var: Vulg. Ps 79:1 *In finem, pro iis qui commutabuntur*, "Unto the end, for them that shall be changed"] [n: (AUG.)] For the better, etc., *as far as* a testimony of faithlessness. *Asaph.* Being enlightened [sic: *Illuminatur*] he treats of the coming of Christ and of his vineyard, a good testimony concerning the head himself and his body, the king and the people, the shepherd and the flock.

VERS. 2.-- *Thou that rulest,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Here Asaph, enlightened, speaks of the coming of Christ, who converts [0977A] man, and makes firm the vineyard brought out of Egypt: this is the testimony for them that are changed. [n: (CASS.)] Above he was fearful, who was praying for the sins of the Synagogue: now he presumes, and to those changed for the better he gives a testimony. This psalm is the testimony of the Synagogue concerning the head himself, and his body. [n: (ID.)] A prayer for the first coming, through which the reparation of the human race is made. [n: (ID.)] Being finished, he makes an end. O God, O Son. *Thou that rulest,* spiritually, *Israel,* who see thee, *Give ear,* that is, pour the light of goodness upon us, that we may be bright from thee, who of ourselves are dark. *Joseph.* [n: (ID.)] Thou leadest the faithful people into the folds of God, as sheep to the pastures, of whom: *And other sheep I have that are not of this fold, and them I must bring, that there may be one fold and one shepherd [n: (Joan. X)]*. Each has its own proper word: Israel *is ruled,* [0977B] Joseph *is led,* that is, the devout people. *Be made manifest.* [n: (ID.)] He asks that he who was in the secret of majesty may appear before the Jews through the incarnation, as it had been promised, working in them these powers which are signified by these names, Ephraim, Benjamin, and Manasse. [n: (AUG.)] *Be made manifest before.* Known to the angels, be made known to men.

VERS. 3.-- *Before Ephraim.* [n: (AUG.)] That is, in the nation of the Jews, where these tribes are, namely before fruitfulness, and before the son of the right hand, that we also may be the fulness of knowledge through charity, which is God; and before Manasse, that is, the forgotten one, that thou mayest now come into his mind, according to that saying: *All the ends of the earth shall remember, and shall be converted.* [n: (ID.)] *Stir up.* He repeats, etc., *as far as* crucified through weakness, rise again through power. [0977C] [n: (CASS.)] *Stir up thy power* of miracles, etc., *as far as* whence: *God is faithful, who suffereth you not to be tempted above that which you are able,* etc. [n: (I Cor. X)].

VERS. 4.-- *Thy face.* [n: (AUG.)] This face was veiled to Asaph in the time of the weakness of Christ, and then Asaph was sick, not being able to look upon him veiled with a cloud: of which it is said: *God ascended upon a light cloud,* [var: Vulg. Is 19:1 *Ecce Dominus ascendet super nubem levem*, "behold, the Lord will ascend upon a light cloud"] and he shall enter, or shall go into, Egypt, that is, flesh free from sin; but he enlightened it when he rose again and ascended, and so we are saved.

VERS. 5.-- *How long wilt thou be angry?* [n: (AUG.)] Wrath passes from the servant, that he may not scourge, as is plain, but that he may not condemn: for wrath endures even toward those converted, but as a wrath that corrects. *For he scourgeth every son whom he receiveth [n: (Isa. IX, Heb. XII)]*.

VERS. 6.-- *Thou wilt feed us* [n: (AUG.)] Here he sets forth the sufferings [0977D] which the saints are to suffer at the time of the coming. As though he said: Although I long for the coming of Christ, yet at that time we are to suffer many things, because *he fed,* etc. *In measure,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] According to thy strength, that thou mayest be instructed, not crushed; whence: *God is faithful, who suffereth you not to be tempted above,* etc. [n: (I Cor. X)]. Or, according to the measure of faults.

VERS. 7.-- *Thou hast made us a contradiction.* [n: (CASS.)] While our neighbours, who are of us, are against us. Our enemies mock us, from all of whom, that we may be saved, *O God of hosts,* etc.

VERS. 8.-- *O God of hosts.* [n: (AUG.)] It is the way of one who desires, to repeat often what he prays for with such prayers.

VERS. 9, 10.-- *A vineyard.* [n: (CASS.)] Of the vineyard, how it was [0978A] begun, and perfected. [n: (AUG. et CASS.)] *A vineyard out of Egypt,* as if to say, we shall be saved, but thou wilt destroy the Jews, for whom thou didst so much: and that not for a light fault, but because they did not know the coming of Christ. Or, we shall be saved because thou hast so foreshown it. *A vineyard out of Egypt.* But why wilt thou destroy? this is of the unbelieving. *Out of Egypt.* As he cast out those men from Egypt, so he cast the Church out of the darkness of vices: he *plants* the Gentiles in virtues, and *leads* them through this desert of the world. *The roots,* he makes the martyrs and others firm, and fills the world. [n: (CASS.)] *A vineyard out of Egypt,* through mystical figures, etc., *as far as* which has been so spread abroad through the world. *A guide,* while he leads her by a spiritual journey. *The roots,* the prophets, the Apostles, the martyrs and others, from whom, stretching out her *branches,* she takes and covers the world. *A guide of the way.* [n: (AUG.)] Or another [0978B] reading, etc., *as far as* and it is not another, but the same vineyard: thence the Apostles, thence also the other just men, although some branches were broken off.

VERS. 11, 12-- *It covered* [n: (CASS.)] The height and the breadth of the vineyard are here set forth. The vineyard is from above, which overshadows even the loftier mountains with life and doctrine, and makes them grow great. Vines that climb trees are called *bushes,* by which the lesser ones in the Church are understood. *Cedars,* not the proud, but those that are *God's,* are the martyrs, who are high in the Church, and these too the Church protects in her bosom. *The shoots,* the sons of regeneration, who flowed from the river Jordan, because there the rule of baptism began. *The mountains.* [n: (AUG.)] The prophets, etc., *as far as* likewise *they cover the cedars of God,* that is, the doctors. *The cedars of God.* [n: (ID.)] Because there are cedars [0978C] to be overthrown, namely the proud, who are the cedars of the world, but not of God.

VERS. 13.-- *Why.* He wonders why the defence of the Lord was withdrawn from such a one as this. [n: (AUG.)] *Why.* Because she was proud against the planter, and slew the servants sent to her, and at last the son and heir, the fence was destroyed, and the nations found her destroyed, and blotted her out. This Asaph first bewails, but not without hope: for he speaks now of those who direct their heart.

VERS. 14.-- *The boar.* [n: (AUG. et CASS.)] Vespasian, strong and savage, and hostile to the Jews, who among other creatures hold this beast unclean. *A singular wild beast.* Titus, who consumed the remnant with a terrible devouring, who is called *singular,* that is, proud. Or the boar and the *wild beast* are taken for the same thing, namely the Roman army. [0978D] And see by what sins man labours and perishes. *Singular.* [n: (AUG.)] Every proud man says: *I am, I am, and there is none other. The boar.* [n: (CASS.)] The devil, strong and fierce, *out of the wood,* because his thoughts are wild and trackless. *But thou, O Lord God of hosts.* Or continue it thus: So the vineyard was laid waste, but that the remnant may be saved, *O God of hosts, turn again,* and at the end of the last age serenely *behold,* he prays this remedy for those changed for the better.

VERS. 16.-- *And perfect thou that which thy right hand hath planted.* [n: (AUG.)] Set up no other, but perfect this one. For in this is the seed of Abraham: there is the root that bears the wild olive. Perfect it, *because blindness in part has happened in Israel, that the fulness of the Gentiles might come in, and so all [0979A] Israel should be saved [n: (Rom. XII)]* [var: Vulg. Rom 11:25 *donec plenitudo gentium intraret*, "until the fulness of the Gentiles should come in"]. *Upon the son.* [n: (CASS.)] Founded upon and [cj: *et*; read *te*, "thee"], etc., *as far as* but of the fire of good love it is said: *I am come to send fire on the earth, and what will I, but that it burn? [n: (Luc. XII.)]* [var: Vulg. Lc 12:49 *nisi ut accendatur*, "but that it be kindled"]

VERS. 18.-- *Let thy hand be.* [n: (CASS.)] A great sacrament, and a gift to God, because the salvation of Israel could be doubted so long, until Christ came: but then the promise was fulfilled, nor does the Church, joined to her spouse, depart from him any more. He says therefore: *Let thy hand be,* he prays for the working of the holy incarnation.

VERS. 19.-- *We depart not.* [n: (CASS.)] Any more, when *thou shalt quicken* the son of man: this is the profit that comes from him.

VERS. 20.-- *O Lord God of hosts, convert us, show thy face, and we shall be saved.* [n: (CASS.)] Great is the sweetness of this little verse, in which he sets forth the rule of the whole of religion. [0979B] For first God *converts* us, and then he shows us *before his face,* that is, he looks upon us with the light of his goodness: after which he at last *saves* us. This verse therefore the prophet repeats, because through it each man briefly implores what is profitable.
