## PSALM LXXX.

VERS. 1.-- *For the winepresses.* [n: (AUG.)] In which there are three things, etc., *as far as* a psalm is sung for Asaph, that is, for the Lord's congregation. *Asaph,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Because according to the history he speaks to the Jews, spiritually to Christians. [n: (AUG.)] First he says what is to be done by the elect, who are the oil, the wine, the birds.

VERS. 2.-- *He rejoiceth in God.* [var: Vulg. Ps 80:2 *Exsultate Deo adjutori nostro*, the plural imperative, "rejoice ye in God our helper"] Asaph, having set before himself the image of a certain solemnity, [0979C] stirs up the elect to the festival.

VERS. 3.-- *Take a psalm.* [n: (AUG.)] The words of God, that human acts may come together into one fellowship: for this is the peace of Jerusalem, this is the reckoning of what is given and what is received; whence the Apostle: *If we have sown unto you spiritual things, is it a great matter if we reap your carnal things [n: (I Cor. IX)]*.

VERS. 4.-- *Blow the trumpet.* [n: (AUG.)] Thus far, up to the division, these things are to be taken either according to the letter, simply of the Jews, or allegorically of Christians. [n: (AUG.)] *Blow the trumpet,* and declare, etc., *as far as* whence Isaias: *Cry, cease not, lift up thy voice like a trumpet,* etc. *On the new moon with the trumpet.* [n: (CASS.)] At the beginning of the month the Jews were bidden to sound the trumpet, that is, at the beginning of the new moon, which is also called the feast of trumpets, which they celebrated [0979D] in memory of that thing, that the Lord showed Abraham the ram sticking among the briers, which he offered up in the place of Isaac.

VERS. 5.-- *For it is a commandment in Israel; and a judgment,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Therefore the trumpet must be sounded, because the law was given to the Jews through Moses, and because he is to be their judge.

VERS. 6.-- *In Joseph.* [n: (AUG.)] The Gentiles are Joseph, etc., *as far as* where he ought to have had a heart.

VERS. 7.-- *From the burdens.* [n: (AUG.)] By what burdens: *hands in the basket,* that is, by servile work. Sins are servile works, according to that saying: *Whosoever committeth sin is the servant of sin [n: (Joan. VIII)]*. *They served in the basket. [n: (ID.)]* In the uncleannesses of sins, in earthly and miry works.

[0980A]VERS. 8.-- *In tribulation,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] So the Prophet: the Lord turns aside, saying, *In tribulation,* the benefits of God are reckoned to them, that the offence may be judged the heavier. *I heard thee in the secret place.* [n: (AUG.)] That with the heart thou mayest believe unto justice. *I proved thee,* that with the mouth confession may be made unto salvation. *Of the storm,* etc. Not in the storm of the sea, but of the heart: where he that is heard must be proved, in the water of contradiction: for he that already walks the way of God has men withstanding him in many ways. This is the water of contradiction, which the ancients felt, but now it is well dried up: for then the waters of the peoples were troubled, and resisted fiercely. Then to Samson, that strong man, that is, to Christ, going to take a wife from among the strangers, that is, the Church from among the Gentiles, there met a roaring lion, that is, the people of the Gentiles, but he tore him in pieces as a kid [0980B] of the goats: and because that roaring people was nothing but a sickly sinner. But when that fierceness had been slain in him, he no longer roars so; nay, in that very people we now find laws in favour of the Church, as it were a honeycomb in the mouth of the lion. *The water of contradiction.* Christ was so even in himself, as it was said of him when he was born: *This one shall be unto the ruin, and the resurrection of many, for a sign which shall be contradicted [n: (Luc. II)]* [var: Vulg. Lc 2:34 *Ecce positus est hic in ruinam et in resurrectionem multorum in Israel*, "behold, this child is set for the ruin and for the resurrection of many in Israel"], as the Jews contradicted the title of the cross.

VERS. 9.-- *Hear, O my people.* [n: (CASS.)] Diapsalma. Thus far of the oil of the winepresses, now of the lees, which is said by way of warning; here the reprobate are reproved. *I will testify unto thee.* The Lord himself makes himself the witness of the judgment of truth, both here and in the world to come, that none may doubt. Then he adds what he would have him understand: Israel, if thou wilt hearken to me; and by calling him Israel he signifies one familiar to himself, [0980C] and invites him to his commands. *In thee,* in thy heart, where truth, not falsehood, ought to be. *New,* lest the Word of the Father be thought to be under time. *A strange god,* one that is such in name, not in essence. *Strange:* It is well-nigh an equal crime to think God new, and to serve idols.

VERS. 10.-- *There shall be no new god in thee, neither,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Some make themselves new gods, either idols, or a Christ unequal to the Father, and feign other such things in the heart: and so in all of them earthly thought is the temple of idols; the opinion is various, the vanity one. Therefore Samson bound together the tails of the foxes, that he might set fire to the harvests of the Philistines. Samson is interpreted *their sun,* namely of those to whom he himself shines, not of all: of whom the wicked shall say: *We have erred from the way of truth, and the sun of justice hath not shined upon us [n: (Sap. V)]* [var: Vulg. Sap 5:6 *justitiae lumen non luxit nobis, et sol intelligentiae non est ortus nobis*, "the light of justice hath not shined unto us, and the sun of understanding hath not risen upon us"]. And elsewhere: *He is the sun [0980D] of justice, and health is in his wings [n: (Malac. IV)]*. The foxes are the treacherous, and most of all the heretics, lurking in winding caverns, deceiving, stinking with their smell: who, although they seem to disagree with one another, yet agree in evil, that is, in their hinder parts, and are held by a like vanity, and burn the harvests, not of our own, because God knows who are his, but of the strangers, which are vessels of dishonour.

VERS. 11.-- *Who brought thee out.* God is pointed out by his own deeds.

VERS. 13.-- *And he let them go.* And although I filled the mouths of the Prophets, yet they were not heard by the lees, whence a fitting vengeance: because *and I let them go,* etc.

VERS. 15.-- *Perhaps for nothing.* [n: (CASS.)] If we shall resist God, [0981A] he himself does not contradict our enemies, nor does he lay the hand of defence upon them.

VERS. 16.-- *The enemies of the Lord,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] These things likewise the Prophet. *They have lied.* Not through men performing what was promised: this the pagans do not, because they promised nothing.

VERS. 17.-- *With the fat of corn,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] According to the letter, either of the manna, or of the body of Christ, or with a spiritual understanding.
