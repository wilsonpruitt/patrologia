## PSALM LXXXII.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *A canticle of a psalm.* [n: (CASS.)] Because in things active divine contemplation is to be held. ⟦s2⟧ But he deters the wicked from the persecution of the people of God: while the good he admonishes unto patience. ⟦s3⟧ A psalm on the coming of Christ to judgment, where the good, who here suffer, shall be crowned: but the wicked, who persecute them, shall be tormented.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *O God, who is like?* [n: (CASS.)] The fear of the judge to come being set forth, he describes the malice of the enemies. [n: (AUG.)] *O God, who is like?* ⟦s2⟧ Though a man, as others; though [0982D] judged, thou art reckoned with the wicked: who is like, when thou shalt judge in brightness? therefore then be not silent, thou who at the first wast silent, and didst restrain thy power, that thou mightest be held and slain. ⟦s3⟧ *O God, who is like?* ⟦s4⟧ This is not referred to the divinity, to which he thought that no man was to be compared. ⟦s5⟧ Therefore he says not: *Who is like to thee?* which would certainly be referred to the divinity; but he says, *Who shall be like to thee?* referring this to the form of a servant, in which he shall then be unlike the rest of men, when he shall appear as judge in glory; or this can be referred to the divinity according to what he says: *O God, who is like to thee?* in the power of the Godhead: for Christ, whom the wicked saw without, this one sees within, namely as almighty.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0983A]VERS. 3.-- *For behold,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] He designates the last times of Antichrist, when the things which are now kept down by fear shall break out into free utterance: which utterance, because it was irrational, is called rather a noise than a speech. ⟦s2⟧ *They that hate.* [n: (CASS.)] Many, but one head, Antichrist: which is lifted up, that he may be called God.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Upon thy people.* ⟦s2⟧ They have lifted up their own head, but they have not brought down thy people.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] *Upon thy people.* ⟦s2⟧ He says this derisively: for they cannot hurt the people of God, for whom God is, whence the Apostle: *If God be for us, who is against us? [n: (Rom. VIII.)]*

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *They have said, Come.* [n: (AUG.)] Behold the counsel, in which *they have devised vain things.* ⟦s2⟧ This is that noise, [0983B] which is not speech, but rattles emptily. ⟦s3⟧ *From the nation.* [n: (CASS.)] In the singular, because all Christians are one nation, since they are born from one font of baptism; or all the good, whom that Antichrist calls one nation: behold the persecution of Antichrist, who intends to take away utterly the Christian name as an evil thing.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *They have ordained a testament.* [n: (AUG.)] For a testament, in the Scriptures, is not that alone which is not valid unless the testator be dead, but every compact also, and agreement which is valid between the living, is called a testament, as Laban and Jacob made a testament. ⟦s2⟧ So also the wicked have made as it were a compact among themselves, that with one mind they should persecute the good.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *The tabernacles of the Idumeans.* ⟦s2⟧ He has set forth [0983C] with what zeal they have dealt maliciously: then he shows now who they are that deal maliciously, saying: *The tabernacles of the Idumeans,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ *Of the Idumeans, and the Ismaelites.* [n: (CASS.)] By these names he declares the enemies of Christ, who in the times of Antichrist are likened to so many evil men: because as these against Israel, so those shall then war against the Christians. ⟦s4⟧ *The Moabites* [n: (AUG., HIER.)] Whence the Moabites, whom Lot, being drunk, begot of his elder daughter. ⟦s5⟧ *The Agarens.* ⟦s6⟧ From Agar, who are also interpreted *proselytes.*

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *Gebal.* [n: (AUG., HIER.)] A vain valley. ⟦s2⟧ For *Ge* is valley [sic: *vallit*], *Bal,* vain. ⟦s3⟧ *Ammon.* ⟦s4⟧ Whence the Ammonites; Lot, being drunk, begot him of his younger daughter.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Amalec.* ⟦s2⟧ The son of Eliphaz the firstborn of Esau, from whom the Amalecites.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *Assur.* [n: (AUG., HIER.)] Literally Nabuchodonosor, [0983D] who was king of the Assyrians. ⟦s2⟧ *The Idumeans.* ⟦s3⟧ Earthly or bloody. ⟦s4⟧ *The Ismaelites.* ⟦s5⟧ Obeying themselves, not God, because they follow their own depraved thoughts. ⟦s6⟧ *Moab,* from the father, which is in evil. ⟦s7⟧ Good indeed was the father, whom the daughter used unlawfully, as the law also is good, if a man use it lawfully, not incestuously. ⟦s8⟧ *The Agarens.* ⟦s9⟧ Proselytes, that is, newcomers, who are not citizens, but creep in with an alien mind, and, an occasion of hurting being found, show themselves. ⟦s10⟧ *Gebal.* ⟦s11⟧ A vain valley, that is, deceitfully humble. ⟦s12⟧ *Ammon.* ⟦s13⟧ A troubled people, or a people of grief. ⟦s14⟧ *Amalec.* ⟦s15⟧ A people licking the earth. ⟦s16⟧ *Strangers.* ⟦s17⟧ Although by a Latin name they declare themselves aliens, and for this cause enemies, yet in Hebrew they are called *Philisthiim,* that is, falling by drink, as men drunken with worldly luxury. ⟦s18⟧ *Tyre.* ⟦s19⟧ In Hebrew *sor,* tribulation or anguish, according to that [0984A] saying. ⟦s20+21⟧ *Tribulation or anguish upon every soul of man that worketh evil. [n: (Rom. II.)] Assur.* [var: Vulg. Rom 2:9 *Tribulatio et angustia*, "and", not "or"] Depressing, or lifted up. ⟦s22⟧ *Lot.* ⟦s23⟧ Declining: the apostate angels, sons of declension, because by declining from the truth they departed into the retinue of the devil, whence in the Apocalypse: The dragon fell, and drew with him the third part of the stars, of whom the Apostle: *Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against principalities and powers [n: (Ephes. VI)]*. [var: Vulg. Eph 6:12 *adversus principes et potestates*, "princes"]

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Madian.* ⟦s2⟧ Declining judgment. ⟦s3⟧ *Sisara,* the shutting out of joy. ⟦s4⟧ *Jabin,* wise, of whom: *Where is the wise? where is the scribe? ⟦s5⟧ Cison,* where they were overcome, is interpreted hardness. ⟦s6⟧ *Endor,* where they perished, the fountain of generation. ⟦s7⟧ And carnal generation is understood, not those regenerated by the water of baptism. ⟦s8⟧ *Oreb,* dryness, *Zeb,* a wolf, *Zebee,* the victim [0984B] of a wolf, not of God. ⟦s9⟧ *Salmana,* the shadow of commotion. ⟦s10⟧ All these things suit the wicked, whom the people of God overcame in good. ⟦s11⟧ *Do to them as to Madian and Sisara, as to Jabin at the brook.* [n: (CASS.)] Of the vengeance upon the wicked; as all those were overcome in figure by the people of God, so he prays that these may be overcome in truth. ⟦s12⟧ *As Madian.* [n: (AUG.)] Namely those who decline judgment, not thinking themselves to be judged: and who shut out the judgment to come; and all these perished in Cison, and in the fountain of Endor.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *Make their princes as,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ These things to all in common. ⟦s3⟧ But specially, *make their princes:* this he wishes for the soldiers of Antichrist, who are not to be converted.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Let us possess by inheritance the sanctuary of God.* [n: (AUG.)] God's: they invade it as their own.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0984C]VERS. 14.-- *As a wheel, and as stubble before the face of the wind.* [n: (CASS.)] By this likeness he foretells their many calamities.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Of the wind.* ⟦s2⟧ The lightness of sinners is compared to the wind, because it flits hither and thither.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Fire and a flame burning the mountains.* [var: Vulg. Ps 82:15 *Sicut ignis qui comburit silvam, et sicut flamma comburens montes*, "as fire which burneth a wood"] [n: (AUG.)] They are fitly said to be in the judgment, when both the dense woods of sinners and the mountains of pride shall be burned.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Fill their faces.* [n: (CASS.)] The punishments of the contumacious being prophesied, he prays for those who shall believe, who have not praisers of their crimes, but blamers, whom reproach often corrects. [n: (AUG.)] It is better to fall that one may repent, than to be proud under the hope of religion: whence the Prophet says: *And thou, daughter of Sion, shalt come even unto Babylon, [0984D] and there shalt thou be delivered [n: (Mich. IV)]*.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Fill their faces.* ⟦s2⟧ This good and desirable thing would not be prophesied for them, unless among those enemies there were such as this shall be granted to before the judgment: because even now the head heals and washes the same body, but in part, not universally as at the end: and yet from thence some shall be converted, for whom he here prays.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *Let them blush and be troubled,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Here of the wicked, who shall not be converted, *Let them blush,* in the time to come for the evils which they have done. ⟦s2⟧ *And be troubled,* for the punishments which they shall suffer. ⟦s3⟧ And this *for ever and ever,* that is, unto eternity. ⟦s4⟧ *And be troubled.* ⟦s5⟧ Again he returns to those who are confounded in the present that they may not be confounded unto eternity; as if he should say: Let those be troubled unto eternity: but those seeking [0985A] the name of the Lord now, let them both be confounded for their sins wholesomely, and perish, in so far as they are wicked. ⟦s6⟧ And so let them know; or, let the wicked then be confounded, in soul and in body, and perish in soul beyond repair. ⟦s7⟧ And now let them know what they now will not. [n: (CASS.)] It is to be noted that Asaph has been brought in through the eleven preceding psalms, that the hardness of the Jews might be softened by the frequent voice of the Synagogue itself, or that they might believe the very name to which they seemed devoted. ⟦s8⟧ But in the aforesaid psalms he treated of the incarnation [sic: *psalmi*] of the Lord, and of the faith of the Gentiles, and of the judgment, by which the transgression of the Jews is refuted. ⟦s9⟧ Whence they are fitly eleven: for the number eleven is the number of transgression, because it both passes the ten, and does not reach the twelve, whence there were also eleven curtains of hair in the tabernacle [0985B] of the Lord, and the Apostles were in the number eleven, the most unhappy Judas being cast out at the time of the passion, when they failed from the faith, whence: *Thou hast appointed darkness, and it is night [n: (Psal. CIII)]*.
