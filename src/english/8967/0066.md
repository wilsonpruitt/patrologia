## PSALM LXXXIII.

VERS. 1.-- *Unto the end.* [n: (AUG.)] Nothing is here said of visible winepresses, whence it is plain that they are spiritual. The grape upon the vine, the olive hanging upon the tree, enjoy the free air, and neither is that wine, nor is this oil, before the pressing: so those whom God foreknew before the ages to be made conformable to the image of his Son, before they come to the service of God, enjoy in the world a delightful liberty. But when they come to the service of God, it is said to them: *Son, when thou comest to the service of God, stand in justice and in fear, and prepare thy soul for temptation* [0985C] [n: (Eccle. II)]. Behold the winepress: they are crushed, they are stripped in the coverings of carnal desires, as in husks; they are stripped of the old man, and they flow down into the storehouse of God: therefore the churches of the present time are called winepresses. But who among these are the sons of Core, that is, the bald, namely of Christ? For bald Eliseus, mocked by the boys, was a figure of Christ, who was crucified on Calvary. Therefore is the Christian pressed here, that he may seek the rest which is not here, and God becomes for him a refuge. For he who leans upon earthly things leans upon a staff of reed. *For the winepresses.* [n: (AUG.)] In the winepress the fruit of the grape or of the olive flows out: so in pressures the merits of the saints appear, which lay hidden in peace. [n: (CASS.)] The second psalm on charity. First, of the inestimable desire of the eternal house.

[0985D]VERS. 2.-- *How lovely.* Behold how great the warfare. *For the sparrow,* how secure.

VERS. 4.-- *For the sparrow,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] To two he renders two: *the heart* as *the sparrow; the flesh* as *the turtle,* to which he gives also *young ones:* with the heart we think upon God, as the sparrow flies to the house; with the flesh we do works. [n: (CASS.)] As the sparrow forsakes the woods, and rejoices when a house is found, where it fears no snares: so the soul rejoices, while it knows that a house is prepared for it in heaven. The spirit, which is as a sparrow flying on high, reaching towards God himself. *A nest, etc.* [n: (AUG.)] *A nest* for a time, *a house* true unto eternity. By the nest therefore he means faith, and the conformity of the sacraments of the Church, which nest the turtle seeks, that she may lay up her young. For she does not cast them anywhere, [0986A] but in the nest of the Church she does good works wholesomely, for which she is saved: wherein she differs from others who do works, but not in the nest of faith. For the pagans also do works, they clothe the naked, etc., and heretics do many things: but not in the nest of the Church, and therefore they are trodden down, they are not preserved. This flesh is the woman, by whom the sparrow is seduced, but she shall be saved by the bearing of works, if in the nest, that is, if she shall have persevered in faith. But God supplies that whereof the nest may be made, for he clothed himself with the hay of the flesh that he might come to thee. *Thy altars, O Lord.* [n: (CASS.)] Where souls feast at the heavenly banquet, where the soul may rejoice as in a house. [n: (AUG.)] And the flesh lays up its works, whence: Blessed are they who, etc., where he shows to what beatitude the warfare leads.

VERS. 5.-- *Blessed are they that dwell in thy house, O Lord; [0986B] for ever and ever they shall praise thee.* [n: (AUG.)] This the good have already, namely, that they dwell in thy house. There follows: *For ever and ever they shall praise thee.* This is the whole business of the saints there: there is no necessity there, which is the mother of all human actions, for whom do they do anything there, where none are in need; they have therefore and they act, whence they are blessed. But every blessed one is called blessed by having or by acting.

VERS. 6.-- *Blessed is the man whose help is from thee: he hath disposed ascensions in his heart.* [n: (CASS.)] By what help one may come to the house.

VERS. 7.-- *In the vale of tears.* [n: (CASS.)] The vale of tears is the humility of one repenting. [n: (AUG.)] But whence the weeping: because he afflicted us with the law, through which we knew that sin fights against the spirit, and we groan, [0986C] namely how we may come to the place which he has appointed, by his power and help assuredly. *In the place which he hath appointed. For he shall give a blessing.* Which cannot be set forth, which eye hath not seen, nor ear heard, that is, the kingdom to come, which God has disposed and ordained, that it may be come to by degrees.

VERS. 9.-- *O Lord God of hosts.* [n: (CASS.)] He prays and humbles himself for the obtaining of this help. *God of Jacob.* [n: (AUG.)] Whom thou madest Israel by appearing to him, so make me also Israel.

VERS. 10.-- *Look upon the face,* etc. For he himself speaks in us, whence the Apostle: *Do you seek a proof of him that speaketh in me, Christ!* And he is received in us, as he says, *He that receiveth you, receiveth me. Look.* The Father always looks upon Christ. [n: (CASS.)] But make [0986D] him to be looked upon by the nations through the presence of the incarnation: or, thus by the face we become known to others. [n: (AUG.)] *Look* therefore *upon the face,* that is, make Christ known to all, that we may be able to go from virtue to virtue

VERS. 11.-- *One day.* Unchangeable, namely without rising and setting, to which yesterday does not yield, which tomorrow does not press upon. *Above thousands,* above the infinite present days, which have an end. For which cause *I have chosen,* though I might reign, *to be abject* in the opinion of men, *in the house,* in the Church, *Behold how sweet it is, to be pressed by the burden of the winepress.*

VERS. 12.-- *Because mercy.* [n: (CASS.)] Therefore I had rather be in the house of God than elsewhere, because in it he gives mercy, by which he justifies, after the truth of the promised [0987A] reward. But to whom? to those who walk in innocence.

VERS. 13.-- *O Lord of hosts, blessed is the man that hopeth,* etc. Thou who dost such great things, which cannot be told.
