## PSALM LXXXVI.

VERS. 1.-- *A psalm.* [n: (CASS.)] Because it treats of the Church according to power, where it does works. *Of a canticle.* That from the worldly Jerusalem thou mayest be lifted up to the understanding that is from above, namely from action to contemplation. The Prophet preaches the heavenly Jerusalem to the sons of Core, which he commends [0989B] by its foundations, and its gates, and its fame, that their affection may be increased. *The foundations thereof.* A psalm on the heavenly Jerusalem: the Prophet, full of the spirit, turning many things over with himself concerning this city, of which he had as yet said nothing, breaks out into this: *The foundations thereof,* etc. The first praise is from the foundations; let the solidity of the whole city be noted. *Thereof,* is not referred to anything set down before: but to the meditation of the Prophet's heart, because he had never been silent about it in his heart, even if in his mouth.

VERS. 3.-- *Things said.* [n: (AUG.)] These are those things which the Lord foretold of it by the prophets.

VERS. 4.-- *I will be mindful.* [n: (ID.)] Diapsalma. The Lord, concerning the same Jerusalem. *I will be mindful of Rahab.* He shows what those glorious things are; as if he should say: These are they: in that city, says the Prophet in the person of God, [0989C] *I will be mindful of Rahab and of Babylon,* which do not belong to the people of the Jews. *Glorious things of thee,* where the Jew is not alone, but all the Gentiles. *Rahab.* She let down the scarlet through the window, that is, the Church of the Gentiles has upon its forehead the sign of the blood of Christ.

Whence: *Publicans and harlots shall go before you into the kingdom of heaven. Rahab,* [var: Vulg. Mt 21:31 *in regnum Dei*, "into the kingdom of God"] breadth or violence, the Gentiles, who wander broadly and violently through vices. To Babylon belong all the wicked, as to Jerusalem all the saints. *Behold the strangers.* I will be mindful. Why hast thou said this? *Behold the strangers,* that is, the Gentiles: and *Tyre,* the anguish of vices, or of penance.

VERS. 5.-- *Shall Sion.* According to another reading. *Mother Sion shall say, a man.*

[n: (AUG., CASS.)] As if he should say: Whence shall strangers come to [0989D] Jerusalem? Hear whence. There is a certain man, that is, Christ, who says *Mother Sion,* that is, the Synagogue is my mother according to the flesh; and through him they come; who is that man? He is the one who *was born in her* and founded her; he himself says, *mother,* to that city.

[n: (CASS.)] Or, he commends Sion spiritually, etc., *as far as* of what princes?

[n: (AUG.)] Of these, (according to another reading) *who were made,* namely princes, *in her,* that is, who were made princes; before they were in her, they were not princes, but the outcast things of the world: *God chose* indeed *the weak things of the world, that he might confound the strong.*

VERS. 7.-- *As of those rejoicing.* [n: (CASS.)] Diapsalma. He concludes in few words, touching the beatitude to come. [0990A] [n: (AUG.)] *As of those rejoicing,* all things being compared, he concludes: *As of those rejoicing. As,* because such shall the joy be as we do not know here, and therefore since it could not be expressed, he says, as he could, *as of those rejoicing.*

## PSALM LXXXVII.

VERS. 1.-- *Mahalath.* [n: (AUG.)] A choir, that the choir may answer the singer in harmony, etc., *as far as* for a certain man of the East is said to have been.

VERS. 2.-- *O Lord God.* [n: (CASS.)] He admonishes us to imitate the death of Christ. First he prays for help, reporting the contempt he has from his enemies: whence, praying in the form of a servant to the Father, he says: *O Lord.*

VERS. 3.-- *Let it enter in the sight.* [n: (ID.)] The power of pure prayer, which as a certain person enters to God [0990B] and performs its charge: whither the flesh cannot come.

VERS. 4.-- *For it is filled.* [n: (AUG.)] These affections of human infirmity, etc., *as far as* but tokens of human infirmity.

VERS. 5.-- *Counted.* Because I have borne not only grievous things, but reproachful ones also.

VERS. 6.-- *As the slain sleeping.* [n: (CASS.)] Because he also was wounded with a lance, and shut in a sepulchre, but he differs by that which follows, of whom thou art not yet mindful.

*In the sepulchre.* That is, lulled asleep in carnal pleasure, whence God does not rouse them.

*And they are cast off from thy hand.* [n: (AUG.)] For this is not referred to the sleeping, among whom are the just, [0990C] of whom although God is not mindful *as yet,* [var: Vulg. Ps 87:6 *quorum non es memor amplius*, "no more"; the gloss's *ut resurgant* rests on "as yet"] that they may rise again: yet of these it is said: *The souls of the just are in the hand of God [n: (Sap. III)]*. Or, *they have put me* by the truth of the thing, *In the lower pit,* that is, in the waters of hell, namely in darkness and in the shadow of death.

VERS. 7.-- *In the lower pit.* [n: (CASS.)] Or *of hell:* because by so abject a death he was taken away into the darkness of oblivion, whom no one shall seek. And *the shadow of death,* the place of the wicked, in which they are punished.

VERS. 8.-- *It is confirmed upon me.* [n: (ID.)] They thought that he had perished by the anger of God, and that God had brought all his indignations upon him, when he suffered him to be crucified.

VERS. 9.-- *Thou hast put far away.* [n: (ID.)] What his friends did in the passion.

[0990D] *Acquaintances.* Not those whom he knew: because then he knew all as God, but those to whom he was known.

VERS. 10.-- *All the day.* [n: (AUG.)] By *hands stretched out* is understood the continuance of good works, from which he never ceased. For on the cross that stretching out was not for even one whole day, nor can the whole be taken for the part when he adds *whole;* but because these works did not profit all, he adds.

VERS. 11.-- *Is it for the dead.* He means the dead in heart, for whom indeed the miracles of Christ are wrought, but they do not profit them: for wonders were wrought for those dead in body, when certain ones came to life again. And when the Lord pierced hell, and thence ascended a conqueror of death, a great miracle was wrought for the dead. *Is it [0991A] for the dead.* [n: (CASS.)] He shows to whom their own works do not profit. *Or shall physicians raise up,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] In the Hebrew not physicians, etc., *as far as: No man can come to me, unless the Father draw him.*

VERS. 12.-- *In the sepulchre.* Whence in the Gospel of such men: *You are like to whited sepulchres, which outwardly appear to men beautiful: but within are full of dead men's bones,* as if to say, so also you by hypocrisy and iniquity. *Mercy and truth.* [n: (AUG.)] Scripture loves to join mercy and truth: especially in the psalms.

VERS. 13.-- *In darkness.* The dead laid in the sepulchre, and the darkness, and the land of forgetfulness, are the same.

VERS. 14.-- *I, O Lord, have cried to thee.* [n: (CASS.)] The things which he endures in his own; behold the choir answering the precentor.

[0991B] [n: (ID.)] *And I as it were,* with those, with whom I am one.

[n: (AUG.)] *And I.* To these dead their works do not profit, etc., *as far as Why, O Lord, dost thou cast off.*

VERS. 15.-- *Why.* [n: (CASS.)] Behold how much he desires. *Why, O Lord.* He sets forth that the cause is to be sought: which is, that by the delay of good, and by adversities multiplied, by which the prayer is as it were repulsed, it may be kindled more ardently.

VERS. 16.-- *Lifted up.* [n: (ID.)] First with a swollen heart, afterwards, *Humbled.* Through confession. *Troubled,* that is, confounded for his sins, by condemning them [sic: *damnado*].

VERS. 17.-- *They have passed.* [n: (CASS.)] They did not happen gently.

[n: (AUG.)] Because it was not of the faithful, but of the unbeliever that it was said [0991C]: *The anger of God abideth on him [n: (Joan. III)]*. For he scourges the sons whom he receives, whence: *Whom I love, I rebuke and chastise [n: (Apoc. III)]*. And elsewhere: *I will strike and I will heal [sic: *Percutium*]: I will kill and I will make to live [n: (Deut. XXXII)]*. [var: Vulg. Dt 32:39 *ego occidam, et ego vivere faciam : percutiam, et ego sanabo*, the two members in the reverse order] [n: (ID.)] *They have passed and troubled.* Because one thing we already suffer from condemnation, and another we fear from eternal vengeance. *Thy terrors.* [n: (CASS.)] Which I suffer: because it pleased thee, not by my desert. [n: (AUG.)] The terrors of the judgment to come, which all flesh fears.

*They have troubled me.* According to the infirmity of the flesh. [n: (AUG.)] *They have troubled me,* sometimes, terrors: they so trouble the mind of one thinking, with evils hanging round about, that they seem like waters to flow about, and at once to surround on every side, whence they both seem to be friends, and desert after their manner, whence the Apostle: *All have forsaken me* [0991D] [n: (II Tim. IV)].

VERS. 18.-- *They have surrounded me.* [n: (CASS.)] Sins shut him in on every side with a copious flood.

*All the day.* [n: (AUG.)] Because at no time are evils wanting to the Church until the world be ended. But all these things are done, that the prayer of the Church may come before God until that salvation come, where there shall be nothing of evils.

VERS. 19.-- *Friend* [n: (CASS.)] Even those who are joined by a pious and faithful love.

*Neighbour and acquaintances,* etc. Kindred. Often have human offices been withdrawn from the just through fear.
