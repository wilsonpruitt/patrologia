## PSALM LXXXVIII. (cont.)

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 48.-- *Remember what my substance is.* [n: (ID.)] David says this, set in the flesh among the Jews, in hope in Christ. ⟦s2⟧ What *is my substance?* ⟦s3⟧ And if the Jews fail, *my substance* does not fail, the root does not wholly perish, whence Mary shall come: and of her shall come the seed to whom the promise was made. ⟦s4⟧ For of that people came the Virgin Mary, of the Virgin Mary the flesh of Christ, and that flesh is not a sinner, but the cleanser of sinners. ⟦s5⟧ *For hast thou in vain.* ⟦s6⟧ All the sons of men have gone into vanity, yet not *in vain didst thou establish* them: whence then dost thou cleanse them from vanity? through *the seed,* in which is *my substance;* concerning which he adds: *Who is the man?*

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0996A]VERS. 49.-- *Who is the man?* [sic: *hemo*] [n: (ID.)] This befits none, save Christ: for even if he finds those rising again, and they shall not see death any more: yet they themselves do not *deliver their own souls out of the lower hand,* [sic: *iuferiori*] but he does who *can lay down his soul, and take it again, from whom no one shall take it away.* [var: Vulg. Ps 88:49 has the singular throughout, "he shall deliver his soul out of the hand of hell" — the gloss's whole argument rests on the plural it prints] [n: (CASS.)] Or thus, no one shall live, or shall deliver his soul: and since this is so, *where are the mercies?* ⟦s2⟧ He now asks that the promises be fulfilled which he made to David.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 50.-- *Where are the mercies?* [n: (ID.)] Diapsalma. ⟦s2⟧ Sixthly, in the members also the promises are fulfilled, not temporally, but spiritually. ⟦s3⟧ And he speaks in the person of the Church.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 51.-- *Be mindful, O Lord.* [n: (AUG.)] That thou mayest repay; he seems to have forgotten, while he defers. ⟦s2⟧ *Of the reproach of the servants.* [n: (CASS.)] Brought upon the servants by many nations. [0996B] [n: (AUG.)] Which I (the Church) *have held,* that is, have borne. ⟦s3⟧ *In the bosom,* in the secret of the mind, because openly she did not dare to speak.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 53.-- *Blessed.* [n: (CASS.)] Against the curses of the nations. [n: (AUG.)] Blessed. ⟦s2⟧ An exclamation out of the brightness of Christ that has been seen.

## PSALM LXXXIX.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *A prayer of Moses.* [n: (AUG.)] When the old life and the new, etc., *as far as* and he sees wonders out of the law of God.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (CASS., REM.)] This psalm treats of the failing of the human race, which shall be taken away through Christ.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *O Lord, a refuge.* [n: (CASS.)] First, he sets forth an eternal refuge for man in time.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Before the mountains.* ⟦s2⟧ But lest he should seem [0996C] not to have been, before he was a refuge to us, he adds *before. ⟦s3⟧ The mountains, the earth.* [n: (AUG.)] Behold, every rational creature is distinguished [sic: *Elcce*] by this difference, that by the name *of mountains* is meant the loftiness of the angels; and by the name *of the earth* the lowliness of men: and therefore not unfittingly did he so distinguish the words, that he should say that *the mountains,* that is, the angels, *were made,* and that *the earth,* that is, men, *was formed. ⟦s4⟧ They should be made, or it should be formed.* ⟦s5⟧ To be formed, or fashioned, is wont to be said of bodies: to be made, out of nothing. ⟦s6⟧ *From the world.* ⟦s7⟧ He would more fitly say, etc., *as far as* but here *the world* is put for the eternal. ⟦s8⟧ *From the world.* ⟦s9⟧ Behold what eternity is a refuge to us, etc., *as far as: Turn not away, for a thousand years. ⟦s10+11⟧ Thou art.* Excellently does he say it, because he is unchangeable. ⟦s12⟧ He does not say thou shalt be, or thou wast, since the unchangeable essence of God has not known the past, or the future; whence it is said: *I am [0996D] who am.* ⟦s13⟧ And, *He who is hath sent me to you?* ⟦s14⟧ And: *But thou art the selfsame, and thy years shall not fail.* [sic: *aani*]

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Turn not away.* [n: (CASS.)] Secondly, he sets out the infirmity of man, for which he asks that help be brought. ⟦s2⟧ *Unto lowliness.* ⟦s3⟧ That is, earthly concupiscence, etc., *as far as* who taught us to seek the things of heaven.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Yesterday's day.* [n: (AUG.)] He does not say, at least as tomorrow's, etc., *as far as* in anxiety, and trembling, as nothing [sic: *qnasi*].

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *In the morning as grass.* [n: (AUG., CASS.)] Let it be changed from the quality of the night.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In the evening let it fall.* ⟦s2⟧ Cut down, or broken, at the last. [n: (CASS.)] *In the morning as grass.* ⟦s3⟧ I have said that the years are held, etc., *as far as* the youth which flowers and passes away.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [0997A] *In the evening.* ⟦s2⟧ Old age, when the vigour of a man grows cold. ⟦s3⟧ *Or, in the morning as grass.* ⟦s4⟧ A fitting comparison, as if to say, as grass is changed from the quality which it has in the night, to a worse one in the morning when the sun is risen: so is a man changed for the worse.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *We have failed in thy wrath, and in thy fury we have been troubled.* [n: (AUG.)] Made mortal and liable to suffering, which nevertheless we fear to end by death, whence follows: *And in thy fury we have been troubled. ⟦s2⟧ The world.* ⟦s3⟧ Our depraved life. ⟦s4⟧ Or *the world,* those who come after. ⟦s5⟧ *In the enlightening of the countenance.* [n: (CASS.)] Those things are covered which have pardon: those are *enlightened* which are punished.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 9.-- *For all our days.* [n: (AUG.)] Our knowledge has failed, darkened in its reason by sin, or, the time of life has been shortened, and has almost *failed,* [0997B] because the days of our life have been brought down to fewness. ⟦s2⟧ *Our years as.* [n: (CASS., AUG.)] The misery and shortness of this life is expressed here by the likeness of *the spider,* which weaves empty webs, that it may catch flies by guile: whence as a spider, that is, we laboured among corruptible things, we wove empty things. [n: (CASS.)] Life has been shortened through the wrath of God, because they sinned in the presumption of a longer life. ⟦s3⟧ And we ourselves *have failed in thy wrath.* [n: (AUG.)] That is, our essence has failed through hardships. ⟦s4⟧ *The years . . . shall meditate,* they shall not work, because they pass without profit. ⟦s5⟧ Or, *they shall meditate* passively, that is, our years shall be reckoned *as a spider,* that is, as empty and useless.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Seventy.* [n: (ID.)] The appetite for earthly things which are done in seven days: if in virtues, eighty, that is, eternity is sought. ⟦s2⟧ *Years.* ⟦s3⟧ Or *the years,* [0997C] another reading. ⟦s4⟧ By which space of time they are called long-lived, who even up to *eighty* years are seen to have some strength. ⟦s5⟧ *Eighty.* ⟦s6⟧ Because even short of eighty years some are infirm, etc., *as far as* awaiting the redemption of the body. ⟦s7⟧ *Meekness.* ⟦s8⟧ It is clemency, if it be God's: a taming, if it be ours. ⟦s9⟧ For he himself, being meek through clemency, by taming us makes us meek, and so we shall be corrected.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *Who I have known.* [var: Vulg. Ps 89:11 has the third person, "who hath known"] [n: (ID.)] The power of a man's anger is to kill the body, and afterwards to do nothing. ⟦s2⟧ God both punishes here, and afterwards sends into hell: and by few is his greater anger understood.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *So make known thy right hand.* [n: (CASS.)] Thirdly, he prays and affirms deliverance. [n: (AUG.)] *Thy right hand.* ⟦s2⟧ He so *made known* Christ, etc., *as far as* [0997D] that he may set him at the right hand.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And them that are learned in heart in wisdom. ⟦s2⟧ Or, fettered in heart.* ⟦s3⟧ Which is the same thing, because they who are taught in wisdom thrust the foot of the mind into the fetters of God, that they may not stray from his way. ⟦s4⟧ These God so made known in the New Testament, that for faith they should spurn all things which are held great, in the Old, and should seek in eternal death: attending to this he speaks, on behalf of those who suffer many things from the world for the sake of eternal things.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *Turn thee.* ⟦s2⟧ As though his face were turned away from those in tribulation. ⟦s3⟧ *How long.* ⟦s4⟧ Elsewhere, a little. [n: (CASS.)] Not wholly, which profits the more, if some tribulation always admonishes us.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 14.-- *In the morning we have rejoiced in thy mercy, [0998A] and we have been delighted.* [sic: *delectatio*] [n: (AUG., CASS.)] Now it is night, in which we hunger: but then we shall be filled with the countenance, whence: *I shall be satisfied when thy glory shall appear.* ⟦s2⟧ And elsewhere: *In the morning I will stand before thee, and will see.* ⟦s3⟧ But now instead of it *we have been glad,* even in the evil days in which we say: *Look upon thy servants.*

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *Look upon thy servants.* ⟦s2⟧ What God does in himself, the Prophet prays may be done for his people: he prays, namely, that he may spare the sinning Jews, if not for their merits, at least for the sake of the works which he has wrought in them.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *And direct their children.* ⟦s2⟧ As if to say, even if they themselves have been scattered through iniquities [sic: *iniqui-tales*], at least the children, at the last, may be corrected by faith, who of themselves are depraved and unconvertible.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *The brightness of the Lord our God upon us, [0998B] and work ye.* [var: Vulg. Ps 89:17 reads "and direct thou the work of our hands upon us"] [n: (HIER., CASS.)] Let the image of God, restoring, be imprinted on the mind. ⟦s2⟧ Or the brightness of God is upon us, while we are adorned by the impress of the cross, and bear the standard of triumph on the forehead. [n: (CASS.)] For the image is threefold, etc., *as far as* that I may remember thee, understand thee, love thee.
