## PSALM XCI.

VERS. 1.-- *A psalm of a canticle.* [n: (AUG.)] Because of the weariness of minds, etc., *as far as* seeking the glory of God, not thine own.

VERS. 2.-- *It is good to confess.* [n: (CASS.)] First, he treats how the good man, being untroubled, keeps the sabbath of the mind, perceiving that all things are done well by God, which the foolish man does not know. [n: (AUG.)] The good things of the present, given to us by God, are a consolation: when they are lacking, it is justice. For God both consoles us, and amends us: in both, therefore, let us be [1000D] thankful to him, saying: *It is good to confess,* etc.

VERS. 4.-- *Upon the ten-stringed instrument.* [n: (ID.)] He sings a psalm who does good with cheerfulness. He with whom it is done in sadness does not himself do it: and he carries the psaltery, he does not sing upon it. *Upon the ten-stringed instrument.* If thou only speakest well, it is a canticle without the harp; if thou workest, and speakest not, it is a harp without the canticle; and therefore both speak well and do well.

VERS. 5.-- *For thou hast delighted.* [n: (ID.)] Whence the Apostle: *We are his own moulding,* [var: Vulg. Eph 2:10 reads "for we are his workmanship" — the word the gloss picks up two lines later in *In factura tua*] etc., *as far as: How great are thy works. In thy handiwork.* [n: (CASS., HIER.)] Both in the beauty of the fabric of the world itself, and in its obedience, and in its ordering, and in its causes, and in its effects, and in the works of the hands which I use, or which I marvel at.

[1001A]VERS. 6.-- *How great are thy works.* [n: (CASS., HIER.)] In substance, and in form, and in force, and in the rest. *Thy thoughts are exceeding deep,* by which thou hast made and dost govern such things.

VERS. 7.-- *The senseless man,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Thou hast delighted me in thy works, but *the senseless man shall not know,* and the fool, who, if he knows, does not beware for himself, *shall not understand these things,* which he has said, or which he says afterwards. The highest kind of knowledge is to know that God bestows such great things as human sense is not able to attain to.

*The senseless man* is he who is empty of heavenly wisdom, and full of human craftiness; who does not know the works of God, because he thinks that all things are done by chance; *the fool* is a brute, who does not know even worldly things.

VERS. 8.-- *When they have sprung up.* [var: Vulg. Ps 91:8 has the future perfect, "when they shall have sprung up"] [n: (CASS.)] He asserts that the wicked shall [1001B] perish, etc., *as far as* the Jews thought that thou shouldst die by the common lot, that is to say.

VERS. 9.-- *But thou art the Most High.* [n: (AUG.)] Thou waitest from above out of eternity, etc., *as far as* not long-suffering, from the word eternal.

VERS. 11.-- *And shall be exalted.* [n: (ID.)] Even though they themselves shall perish, etc., *as far as* all heresies shall perish.

VERS. 12.-- *And hath looked down.* [n: (ID.)] And therefore even now he has looked down, and it shall hear from the truth within. *And from them that rise up.* [var: Vulg. Ps 91:12 reads "and among them that rise up"] [n: (CASS.)] Or, it shall hear at the last those that rise up against me, the malignant: when they shall rebuke themselves, saying, *What hath pride profited us, and riches? [n: (Sap. V.)] My ear.* [n: (AUG.)] Of him that stands at the right hand: it shall hear in the world to come this, namely, *Go, ye cursed, into everlasting fire.* [var: Vulg. Mt 25:41 reads "Depart from me, ye cursed"] From which evil hearing the just man shall not fear. Behold, this is [1001C] for these men; *what of the just? [n: (Matth. XXV.)]*

VERS. 13.-- *The just man as the palm tree.* They as hay. The just man as the palm tree, for its height, and because its root is rough, its foliage fair at the top and at the end, and it bears fruit late; so also does labour make the just man rough here, whom in the heights the beauty of immortality shall crown, and, as it were late, after long labours he shall receive the wage of his works. *The just man as the palm tree.* [n: (CASS.)] The third part. That the just flourish, so that by this promise they may devoutly rejoice. *The cedar.* [n: (AUG.)] The palm and the cedar, these trees do not wither in the summer, as hay does.

VERS. 14.-- *In the courts of the house.* [n: (CASS.)] *The court* is the beginning of the dwelling; *the house* is the inner part. *In the courts* therefore *they shall flourish,* that is, in the very beginning of the resurrection, when they shall hear: *Come, ye blessed of my Father [n: (Matth. XXV)]*.

VERS. 15.-- *They shall still be multiplied.* [n: (ID.)] The Church [1001D] shall the more begin to be most fruitful, when it shall have been brought to the end of the world: for then by the sufferings of the elect the number of the blessed shall quickly be filled up. And *old age* is here taken otherwise than above.

VERS. 16.-- *That they may declare.* [n: (ID.)] Here, or, when they shall receive the reward they shall declare that he is just, who fulfils to his own the things promised. *And there is no iniquity in him.* [n: (AUG.)] As that he should permit those to suffer here for his sake, whom he does not adorn there. *And he is upright,* who rewards, not unjust so as to defraud of the reward, but kind so as to heap it up.

## PSALM XCII.

*Unto the day,* etc. [var: Vulg. Ps 92:1 reads "on the day before the sabbath" — as the gloss itself has it in its own next words] [n: (AUG.)] On the day before the sabbath, etc., *as far as* which the sixth day signifies.

VERS. 1.-- *The Lord hath reigned,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] The praise [1002A] of Christ is sung here, etc., *as far as* from the praise of the house, there, *Thy house. Clothed with beauty.* [n: (AUG.)] Christ is beautiful in these things, etc., *as far as* the world which shall not be moved. *He hath put on beauty.* [n: (CASS.)] That is, comeliness, etc., *as far as* from the incarnation, when he taught men.

*He hath girded himself about.* He does not say, he girded, or he girded up, or he girded on, but he girded about. For we are girded when we are about to work, we are girded on when we are about to fight, we are girded up when we are about to go, we are girded about when we are about to minister. [n: (AUG.)] The girding therefore signifies works, etc., *as far as* because *better is he that overcometh anger than he that taketh a city. He hath girded himself about.* [n: (CASS.)] Ascending with the company of angels, or by strenuousness in working.

*For he hath established.* [n: (AUG.)] He has put on beauty and strength, that he might found the earth, whence he adds: *For he hath established.*

VERS. 2.-- *From then.* [n: (ID.)] Or from that time; it marks the time of grace, [1002B] and perhaps he understands this from the day before the sabbath, that is, in the sixth age of this world, in which the Lord came: but lest thou think that he then began, when he came born, he adds, *thou art from everlasting.*

VERS. 3.-- *The floods have lifted up their voice.* [n: (AUG.)] Because the disciples, hearing, etc., *as far as* of the world, which began to be angry, or to persecute. *The floods have lifted up their waves,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] This is wanting in others, etc., *as far as* he who does all things well, he adds: *The Lord is wonderful on high.*

VERS. 5.-- *Testimonies.* [n: (AUG.)] Because *the lifting up of the sea is wonderful,* [var: Vulg. Ps 92:4 has the plural, "wonderful are the surges of the sea"] etc., *as far as* and crowns out of the foretelling. [n: (CASS.)] *Testimonies.* Or thus, etc., *as far as* it contains, though in brief words.
