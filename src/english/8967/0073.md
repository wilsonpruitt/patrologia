## PSALM XCIII.

*A psalm of David* [var: Vulg. Ps 93:1 *Psalmus ipsi David, quarta sabbati.*, "a psalm for David himself, on the fourth day of the week"] [n: (AUG.)] To the weak, who also murmur [1002C] in heart, etc., *as far as* the whole teaching patience. *On the fourth day of the week,* on which day God made the luminaries, etc., *as far as* whence he admonishes to patience.

VERS. 1.-- *The Lord is the God of revenges.* Be not hasty to punish another, if he has hurt thee; even as thou wouldst not be willing to be punished at the asking of another whom thou hast hurt: and if he sins against thee, sin not worse by blaspheming God, nor wish for vengeance; which, if it comes, will perhaps find thee first.

*He hath acted freely.* [n: (AUG.)] In the time of his weakness, as when he taught in the temple, as one having power, because neither his truth nor his power can be withstood. And because it was so then, now *Be thou exalted, because, how long shall sinners.*

VERS. 4.-- *They shall utter.* [n: (CASS.)] He sets forth their sins, etc., *as far as* it goes on by way of exaggeration. *They shall speak, all that work,* etc. *They shall utter,* they [1002D] answer others, [n: (AUG.)] that is, they have what they may answer, etc., *as far as* the wicked speak, saying that God does not care for these things.

VERS. 6.-- *The widow and the stranger.* [n: (CASS.)] These God protects, etc., *as far as* without the devil for a father and concupiscence for a mother.

VERS. 7.-- *Neither shall he understand.* [n: (AUG.)] As it were, he will not attend to these things, he neglects these things, nor does he attend that he may see, God does not care for me, what I may do?

VERS. 8.-- *Understand ye.* [n: (CASS.)] The second part; against blasphemers, etc., *as far as* that he opens to his own friends his counsel, which is unknown to the wicked. *Ye foolish.* Who openly blaspheme, saying that God does not see. Or thus: You *unwise,* that is, ignorant, understand, you who by now, by living well, [1003A] are *in the people,* that is, in the number of the good. *And you foolish,* that is, improvident, and if you know, *at length be wise.*

VERS. 10.-- *Who correcteth the nations.* [n: (AUG.)] Daily, for the original sin, will he not rebuke for the things said above? Or, *Who instructeth.* [var: Vulg. Ps 93:10 *qui docet hominem scientiam*, "who teacheth man knowledge"] For the sake of which instruction God sent his Son, etc., *as far as* whence the Apostle: *We shall all stand before the judgment seat of Christ, that every one may receive, [n: (Rom. XIV)]*, etc. [var: Vulg. Rm 14:10 *omnes enim stabimus ante tribunal Christi*, with no such clause following; "that every one may render", *ut referat unusquisque*, stands at 2Cor 5:10]

VERS. 11.-- *The Lord knoweth.* [n: (ID.)] But thou knowest not the thoughts of God, since they are just; but when friends have been made, God brings forth his counsel, why he spares the wicked. *For they are vain.* The thoughts of such men are vain, in that they themselves of themselves cannot know it, but the Lord knows them to be vain.

VERS. 12.-- *Blessed is the man.* [n: (CASS.)] These are the vain, etc., [1003B] *as far as* all shall know God, *the heavens shall be folded up as a book. And out of thy law,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] From the law learn patience, *until there be dug,* it is not thine to know how, and why it is dug for him.

VERS. 13.-- *Until,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] The happiness of the wicked is a *pit;* the toil of the good is the father's scourge; happiness is a *pit,* because when God spares by impunity, a man is made lifted up, and when he thinks himself high, he falls. *Let a pit be dug.* Behold the counsel of God: therefore he spares the wicked, that *a pit may be dug for them.* It is still being dug, and thou already wishest to bury him: thou art too hasty. *A pit for the sinner,* etc. Not for every one, but for the proud, etc., *as far as* give thanks to him from whom thou hast it, and despise not another.

VERS. 16.-- *Who shall rise up.* [n: (CASS.)] The third part, where he says that there is a tossing for the just, and the wave of the world, [1003C] that is, evils through the wicked, so that being pricked they may cry to God, and that nothing else may please them, and then deliverance becomes the more welcome. [n: (AUG.)] Who *shall rise up,* as if he should say, etc., *as far as* but whence shall I conquer? *Unless because the Lord. He will stand with me,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Patient in the battle line; but because he conquers who bears the violence; he loses who strikes.

VERS. 17.-- *Unless because the Lord.* [n: (CASS.)] By vain persuasions he could have been deceived, unless the Lord had delivered him.

VERS. 18.-- *He helped me.* Even as the Lord delivered Peter walking upon the water, that he might not be drowned, who said to the Lord: *Lord, if it be thou, bid me come to thee [n: (Matth. XXVI)]*.

[n: (AUG.)] That Christ by his own power, etc., *as far as* whence he subjoins, *thy comforts have gladdened my soul.*

[1003D]VERS. 19.-- *In the heart.* [n: (ID.)] He signifies wholesome thoughts, namely, that while he thought upon the eternal recompenses, he might despise temporal evils.

VERS. 20.-- *Doth there cleave.* [n: (ID.)] No wicked man sits with thee; and since these have nothing with thee, to what end then are they made? that thou mayest prick by means of them, and that they who are pricked may cry to thee, and at the last that these may perish; which things are shown in these three following verses. *The seat of iniquity.* [n: (CASS.)] It is the possession of the world, with love of it, which does not cleave to God, who commands his own to flee these things. *Who framest.* [n: (AUG.)] From this I understand that the wicked man does not cleave to thee, etc., *as far as* and that pain is a precept; but the other is unformed.

*Pain,* that is, a precept out of pain or toil, [1004A] etc., *as far as* and then the wave rises, in which he is stirred up.

VERS. 22.-- *And the Lord became my refuge, and my God the helper of my hope.* [n: (ID.)] Wherefore? because if thou wert not in peril, thou wouldst not seek a refuge: thou art pricked with pain, that thou mayest seek a remedy; the hope of the world departs and the hope of God comes.

## PSALM XCIV.

*Praise.* [n: (CAS.)] Devotion. *Song* signifies gladness.

VERS. 1.-- *Come, let us rejoice.* [n: (THEOD.)] King Josias was exceedingly pious, etc., *as far as* but the psalm is framed in the person of Josias, and of the priests of God. [n: (CASS.)] Concerning the time of grace, in which there is an entering into rest, the prophet, [1004B] describing the praises of God, first invites the Jews, whom he foresaw to be able to resist Christ.

VERS. 2.-- *His face,* etc. [n: (AUG., CASS.)] The sight of the judge to come, lest there be anything then in us to be sifted. *In confession.* [n: (AUG.)] Praising him, accusing ourselves, etc., *as far as* of which praise the psalm treats. *In psalms let us make a joyful noise.* [cj: *psalmi*; read *psalmis*, "in psalms"] What we say in psalms, or do, let us feel in jubilation.

VERS. 3.-- *Above all gods.* Or rather he calls men gods, over whom he is king, not over demons.

VERS. 4.-- *For in his hand,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] After the greatness of the Lord foretold, he comes to his clemency.

VERS. 5.-- *The sea.* [n: (AUG.)] The world, whose waves God confines within a bound, not suffering us to be tempted above [1004C] that which we are able [n: (I Cor. X)]; which waves also make increase for us, God working it, nor do the scandals hurt thee, which receive their measure from God.

VERS. 6.-- *Come, let us adore.* [n: (CASS.)] First he called the untaught to exultation: now, the glory of God being shown, he calls to penance, which is the harder. *Let us weep.* [n: (AUG.)] If perchance you are solicitous concerning the sins which have made you far off, weep: a tear extinguishes the flame of sin which burns in the conscience. *Who made us.* Behold great confidence: for of thyself thou canst fail, thyself thou canst not remake, he remakes thee who made thee.

VERS. 7.-- *And we are the people,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Elegantly is the order of the words changed: He did not say the sheep of the pasture and the people of his hand, that thou mayest understand those very ones to be sheep who are the people, sheep of his hand, not those which from afar, because [1004D] they are, and sheep which we did not make, but he himself by his own grace made us sheep.

VERS. 8.-- *To-day if you shall hear the voice,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] But we are the people and the sheep. But to all these a condition is set forth, *if you shall hear his voice,* which voice he subjoins, *To-day. Harden not.* [n: (ID.)] Now Christ himself admonishes, lest they be hardened, lest it befall them as it did the fathers.

VERS. 9.-- *As in the provocation.* [n: (ID.)] The majesty of Christ speaking, etc., *as far as* the fault of the parents is recalled, that the vengeance may be feared.

VERS. 10.-- *Forty years.* [n: (AUG.)] By forty years he signifies all the ages, etc., *as far as* as he himself says: *I am with you unto the consummation of the world.*
