## PSALM XCV.

[1005A]

*A psalm of David.* [var: Vulg. Ps 95:1 *Canticum ipsi David, quando domus aedificabatur post captivitatem.*, "a canticle for David himself, when the house was building after the captivity"] [n: (CASS.)] In the Old Testament it is read, etc., *as far as* this psalm does not treat of that actual one.

VERS. 1.-- *Sing ye to the Lord.* [n: (ID.)] He forewarned the Jews in the preceding psalm that, their hardness being laid aside, they should believe Christ; but because it was to abide, he now admonishes all the Gentiles. The second psalm concerning both comings. First he speaks to the prelates, that they themselves should sing and declare. *A new canticle.* [n: (AUG.)] Cupidity sings the *old,* etc., *as far as* whosoever shall have entered in, is without.

VERS. 2.-- *To the Lord, and bless.* [n: (HIER., CASS.)] Thrice to the Lord, for the distinguishing of the trinity of persons; because the praise of Christ is the honor of the Trinity. And there is [1005B] the figure epimone, that is, a frequent repetition of the same sentiment, which heightens it unto praise or unto blame.

VERS. 3.-- *Declare ye.* He speaks to the prelates, declare, because thus grows the building of the house. *From day to day.* [n: (CASS.)] The Old and the New Testament are days, which grow bright by the eternal sun, the one foretells, the other shows the thing done. [n: (HIER.)] He declares therefore from day to day, etc., *as far as* by which the feet of our affections are fenced from the thorns of the vices.

VERS. 4.-- *Great is the Lord.* [n: (AUG.)] Made little, because you are little, understand him great, and you shall be great in him.

VERS. 5.-- *He made the heavens.* [n: (ID.)] The apostles and the angels, whence if he made the heavens and the angels, then the apostles also, who command the demons.

[1005C]VERS. 6.-- *In the sight.* For such men, namely those confessing and just, behold him themselves, and are beheld by him. *Holiness and magnificence.* [n: (AUG.)] As if he should say: He who wishes to be magnificent and mighty, as the angels are, let him first be holy, which comes to pass from him who sanctifies: for of thyself thou canst not be holy. When therefore he sanctifies, a man is made holy, and so magnificent.

VERS. 7.-- *Bring to the Lord, ye kindreds.* [n: (CASS.)] The second part, where he speaks to the subjects, etc., *as far as* that none be excepted, native or stranger. *Glory.* [n: (ID.)] Because a man converses well, in whom God is glorified. *Honor.* When from good things thanks are rendered, whence, *There was not found who would give honor to God, but this stranger. Into the courts.* [n: (ID.)] The *courts,* the prophets or the apostles, by whom there is a going to the Lord; from the [1005D] courts, that is, the prophets or the apostles, there is a coming into the court of the Catholic Church, where the majesty is adored. [var: Vulg. Lc 17:18 *Non est inventus qui rediret, et daret gloriam Deo, nisi hic alienigena.*, "who returned and gave glory to God"] [n: (AUG.)] Or, Enter ye into the court, that is, into the doctrine of the prophets and the apostles.

VERS. 9.-- *Adore ye the Lord in his court,* etc. That is, in the Catholic Church, or by the *courts,* prophecy; by the *court,* the Gospel. *Let it be moved.* All being admonished, he desires that they be moved. As if he should say, enter in and adore, you that are good. *But the earth,* etc. *The whole earth.* [n: (CASS.)] Not some part of it; therefore, lest anyone say: *Lo, here is Christ, lo there,* he subjoins, *For he hath corrected the world of the earth,* etc.

VERS. 10.-- *Say ye among the nations.* [n: (ID.)] That he reigned and corrected and judged. *He hath reigned.* Another reading: [1006A] *He hath reigned from the wood [n: (Matth. XIII)]*. [n: (AUG.)] Of the cross, by which man passes over the *sea,* and becomes a *world* which is not moved.

*The peoples in equity,* etc. Others add, *And the nations in thy wrath.*

[n: (CASS.)] The peoples therefore, that is, the good, he shall judge in equity, but the nations, that is, the wicked, in his wrath.

VERS. 12.-- *Then shall they rejoice.* [n: (CASS., HIERON.)] *Then,* when he shall correct, etc., *as far as* because he is come, that is, he shall come to judge the earth. *Because he is come.* [n: (AUG.)] First he came in the Church, in the clouds, that is, in the apostles, and filled the whole world; whence: *From henceforth you shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds [n: (Matth. XXVI)]*: he who believes that coming shall rejoice in the last coming.

VERS. 13.-- *He shall judge the world.* [n: (CASS.)] He generally includes every [1006B] man, to be judged according to the equity of God. But how he is judged he sets forth by saying, *the peoples in truth,* because in the separation there shall be two peoples, to whom he shall give the sentence of truth, so that some go into life, others into punishments. [n: (AUG.)] By *the world* he means all the sons of Adam, etc., *as far as* the south.

## PSALM XCVI.

*A psalm for David himself.* [var: Vulg. Ps 96:1 *Huic David, quando terra ejus restituta est.*, "to this David, when his land was restored to him" — the clause this gloss expounds] According to the letter he touches here upon this, that when Absalom, the wicked son of David, was dead, who while he lived persecuted his father, the land was restored to David, and he reigned in peace, of which it is not here treated, but rather of the thing signified. [n: (AUG.)] David signifies Christ, whom the wicked sons, that is, the Jews, crucified, and abjured the kingdom to him. But *the land was restored to him,* when Judaea, which had crucified him, believed: [1006C] and the apostles, who had wavered, were confirmed: but other sheep also were led in from among the Gentiles.

Or, *the land was restored,* when his flesh rose again, after which resurrection all these things which are here sung were done.

[n: (AUG.)] Foreseeing these things in the spirit, the prophets rejoiced, etc., *as far as* for to the palate of faith the honey of the divine promise tastes sweet.

[n: (CASS.)] The fourth psalm concerning the first coming, by which the world was set free. He describes first the powers of the Lord, shows that all things are restored to him, and reproves the idolaters.

VERS. 1.-- *The Lord hath reigned.* [n: (CASS.)] The prophet, seeing the superstitions of the world abound before the resurrection of Christ, sets against them: *The Lord hath reigned.* [n: (AUG.)] [1006D] Who then is another to be worshipped? as it were, none; thereupon:

*Let the earth rejoice.* What remains to the idols? As it were, nothing.

*The islands.* The Churches of the whole world. *Many.* Not all, because of the assemblies of the heretics.

[n: (CASS.)] Thereupon he tells what manner of Lord he is in the first coming.

VERS. 2.-- *Clouds and darkness.* To the wicked, who are blind, he is cloudy and dark, to whom he shines with no brightness; but to the good, *Justice and judgment.* They who are corrected are his seat. Before his coming *fire shall go before,* because the wicked, kindled with wrath at the preaching of the prophets, took counsel of their slaying, and were inflamed unto indignation round about, because they were many, whereas the preachers were few.

[1007A]VERS. 3.-- *Fire shall go before him,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] As it were, they who are corrected are the seat of God, etc., *as far as* if they are wicked, unto consuming; if good, unto repairing.

VERS. 4.-- *His lightnings have shone forth.* [n: (ID.)] The clouds grew cheap in their human form, but a wondrous brightness appeared in their words or deeds, and thereupon the earth was moved.

VERS. 5.-- *They flowed down.* [n: (ID.)] Even if the pagan does not believe just now, yet he is overcome.

VERS. 7.-- *Let them be confounded.* This has now been done. [n: (AUG.)] For idolatry now ceases. And let them blush who adore stones, because they are dead.

VERS. 8.-- *Adore ye.* [n: (ID.)] The adoration of the angels therefore ceases, but they adore. The evil angels themselves wish to be adored, the good adore, and do not suffer themselves to be adored. Or at least by this example let idolatry cease.

[1007B] *He hath heard and was glad.* [n: (CASS.)] Secondly, after the restoration of the land, an exultation of every kind, etc., *as far as* as the brethren rejoiced over Cornelius, whom Peter baptized. *And they rejoiced.* [n: (AUG.)] The apostles heard, who were brethren in Judaea, that the Gentiles had received the word of God, and they were glad and magnified God.

VERS. 9.-- *The earth . . . the gods.* [n: (ID.)] By *the earth* and *the gods* understand sinners and just men. Or, he signifies that he rules over things earthly and things heavenly.

VERS. 10.-- *The Lord keepeth.* [n: (AUG.)] If, when thou hast begun to hate the malignant, persecutions follow after, fear not, because the Lord keeps the souls of his saints, for the taking away of whom is the last kind of persecution.
