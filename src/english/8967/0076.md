## PSALM C.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1010A]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *A psalm for David himself.* [n: (CASS.)] This psalm contains the form of perfect sanctity, as the hundredth number is a perfect one.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *For David himself.* ⟦s2⟧ That the whole of what is here taught be applied to the virtues of Christ, nothing to an earthly king. ⟦s3⟧ Here we are taught to seek good things and to refuse evil, which is the perfect converse of life.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Mercy and judgment I will sing.* [n: (CASS.)] Here Christ sings, and we in him. ⟦s2⟧ First he sets forth concerning mercy and judgment. ⟦s3⟧ *Mercy and judgment I will sing.* ⟦s4⟧ These two are joined in a mutual fellowship: in them he briefly includes all the works of God. ⟦s5⟧ And here is set before us the cause why he so bears himself, as he afterwards subjoins. [n: (AUG.)] Let no one presume upon mercy unto impunity, [1010B] because there is judgment for him; let no one who has been changed for the better fear judgment, because mercy goes before. ⟦s6⟧ These are always together in him: but through the times they are distinguished according to their effects, mercy now, judgment in the time to come.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *I will sing and I will understand.* [n: (ID.)] This the singing grants, that I may understand with the mind what lies not open to the eyes, that is, that I may believe that the Lord will come, whom this man so greatly desires, who will come in the unspotted way. [n: (CASS.)] Because he passed through the world without sin; or, I will sing and I will understand, being in the unspotted way, when, etc. ⟦s2⟧ Or, *I will sing,* I will work cheerfully, and through this I shall understand. ⟦s3⟧ *When wilt thou come to me.* ⟦s4⟧ In the second coming, or in the death of each one. ⟦s5⟧ Or, *when wilt thou come to me,* through the flesh in the first coming, because [1010C] in the unspotted way. ⟦s6⟧ *I walked about.* [n: (CASS.)] Secondly, he sets forth what the unspotted way is. [n: (AUG.)] *I walked about.* ⟦s7⟧ What is the unspotted way? ⟦s8⟧ It begins from innocence, and in it is consummated; and there is no need of many words: Be thou innocent, and thou hast fulfilled justice. ⟦s9⟧ But in two ways a man does harm, namely if he makes another wretched, and if he abandons the wretched. ⟦s10⟧ He is innocent who harms neither himself nor another. [n: (CASS.)] *I walked about.* ⟦s11⟧ As if he should say: I sought not transitory things, but I ran the way of the world, being in the midst of thy house. ⟦s12⟧ Innocence has this power, that it makes a man dwell in the midst of the house of God, as an honorable example to others.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *I did not set before me.* [n: (CASS.)] He sets forth innocence by parts. ⟦s2⟧ First, because he has nothing with the wicked, he says: *I did not set before me,* down to, *mine [1010D] eyes.* ⟦s3⟧ After which, that he has a part with the just, and that he drives the wiles of the devil out of his heart, there, *in the mornings.* [sic: *mututinis*] [n: (AUG.)] *I did not set before me.* ⟦s4⟧ As it were: *I walked about in innocence:* in this indeed (the whole Christ says it,) namely that *I did not set before me,* that is, I did not love an unjust thing.

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *It did not cleave.* [n: (CASS.)] He sets forth the transgressions. ⟦s2⟧ *A perverse heart,* he who transgresses in heart; the man turning aside, the heretic who dissents from the Catholics: and him who strays from the right paths I did not acknowledge, because even if they are sealed with the sacraments along with us, yet they are not true men.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *The proud.* [n: (AUG.)] And Jesus feasted, etc., *as far as* whence: *Blessed are they that hunger and thirst after justice [n: (Matth. VIII)]*.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 6.-- *Mine eyes.* ⟦s2⟧ Here he pursues the other part of innocence, namely, that he has a part with the just. ⟦s3⟧ As if he should say: With such men thou didst not eat, whence then wast thou fed? ⟦s4⟧ *Of the earth.* [n: (AUG.)] Those living on the earth, where there are so many enticements, whence a greater crown. ⟦s5⟧ Or [n: (CASS.)] those who are throughout the whole world. ⟦s6⟧ *That they may sit with me.* [n: (AUG.)] That they may abide with me in the truth of faith, or share with me in the eternal kingdom, or judge with me.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *He shall not dwell in the midst of my house.* [n: (CASS.)] That is, of the Church. ⟦s2⟧ Even if he is for a while borne with for the sake of correction, he does not stand out by merits, he can do nothing; he is not grain in my people, but chaff. ⟦s3⟧ *He shall not dwell,* this befits priests most of all, that they dwell not with the depraved.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *In the morning.* [n: (CASS.)] The third part, where he shows [1011B] what the end of the wicked is. ⟦s2⟧ And here Christ speaks. ⟦s3⟧ *In the morning.* ⟦s4⟧ After the wicked have been shunned, the just man, returned to himself, adds that he drove the suggestions of the demons out of his heart *in the morning,* that is, when at the beginning of the temptation they begin to appear, as it were in the twilight. ⟦s5⟧ *I put to death.* ⟦s6⟧ By prayer, whereby they ought to be cast out, lest they grow. ⟦s7⟧ *Sinners.* ⟦s8⟧ The demons, who make all flesh to sin. ⟦s9⟧ *The city of the Lord.* ⟦s10⟧ The soul of the just man, whence the devil is scattered and removed. ⟦s11⟧ *All that work iniquity.* ⟦s12⟧ That is, the insinuations or the injections of the devil.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] He has now recounted that none clave to him but the good [cj: *et*; read *ei*, "to him"], etc., *as far as* this befits the just man also, who foresees this to be to come, and therefore so bears himself.
