## PSALM CIII.

VERS. 1.-- *A psalm of David.* [n: (CASS.)] Likewise God [cj: *Deus*; read *Deum*, "to bless God"] admonishes men to bless, first through heaven and earth and their adornment. [n: (AUG.)] Although indeed all things here may be devoutly taken according to the letter, etc., *as far as* in all things the spiritual sense is to be sought. *Bless the Lord, [1015D] O my soul,* etc., *magnified.* [sic: *mugnificatus*] In knowledge, because thou art always great by nature, who thinkest [cj: *putes*; read *potes*, "thou canst"] not to grow or be lessened, because thou art perfect; *Confession,* that is, thou hast on every side creatures which confess thee, and which are comely and full of light. Or, comeliness is in the essence of God, and light on every side. [n: (CASS.)] Or it signifies the incarnation, through which the confession and the glory of the heavenly majesty became known, and men confessed him, who are light by knowledge. [n: (AUG.)] Thou hast put on confession, that is, the Church, etc., *as far as* and a garment without spot or wrinkle.

VERS. 2.-- *Stretching out the heaven thus.* [n: (CASS.)] What things confess him? Heaven, earth, sea, with all their adornment; of each of these in what follows. [n: (AUG.)] *Stretching out [1016A] the heaven like.* According to the letter, the heaven, as it were a roof, etc., *as far as* afterwards at the end, like a book, Scripture shall be folded up.

VERS. 3.-- *Who coverest.* [n: (ID.)] According to the letter they are waters, etc., *as far as* whose upper parts are the two precepts of charity. Or thou who *coverest with waters,* that is, with the spiritual sense, the surface of Scripture; or by spiritual gifts thou fortifiest their charity, that nothing may be able to separate them. *Upon the wings of the winds,* etc. Wind is a movement of the air, etc., *as far as* but the charity of Christ surpasses the charity of all.

VERS. 4.-- *A burning fire.* [n: (CASS.)] That is, the seraphim, who are the higher order, thou makest thy ministers, whence in Isaiah: *There flew to me one of the seraphim,* who are the higher order.

VERS. 5.-- *Who hast founded the earth upon its own [1016B] stability.* [n: (ID.)] Therefore the Holy Spirit in this psalm speaks things difficult according to the letter, that we may seek spiritual things.

VERS. 7.-- *At the voice of thunder.* [n: (ID.)] By terrible winds the waters shall be dried up; the wicked shall fear at the threatening of the judgment.

VERS. 8.-- *They ascend.* [n: (AUG.)] The wicked, as mountains, etc., *as far as* that the Church may be free to preach. [n: (CASS.)] God permits the proud, etc., *as far as* that they may no longer take possession of the world.

VERS. 10.-- *In the valleys.* [n: (AUG.)] From the humble, made subject to grace, even if they are mountains by spiritual height, flow the fountains of the Holy Spirit, who rests upon the humble. *Between the midst.* [n: (ID.)] That is, between the midst, etc., *as far as* and such a mountain as this is not to be heard.

[1016C]VERS. 11.-- *All shall drink.* [n: (ID.)] All were in the dish let down thrice to Peter, etc., *as far as* that the Church may be gathered. *Wild asses.* [n: (ID.)] Great untamed asses of the woods, etc., *as far as* or the proud Jews shall put off drinking until the last time.

VERS. 12.-- *Over them the birds of the air.* [n: (ID.)] Not all birds abide, etc., *as far as* others on the left hand are condemned.

VERS. 15.-- [sic: *VERS. 15.*] *Of the fruit of thy works.* [n: (ID.)] Let no man therefore glory in his own works, but *he that glorieth, let him glory in the Lord,* for by the grace of God the earth is filled. Thus therefore grace is given freely. If it is rendered to the works of men, it is called a wage.

VERS. 14.-- *For the service of men.* [n: (ID.)] Whence *whereas I was free from all, I made myself the servant of all,* etc., [1016D] *as far as* to whom he gave an example of taking necessary things from those subject to them. *In oil. To every man is given the Spirit unto manifestation:* [var: Vulg. 1 Cor. 12:7 *Unicuique autem datur manifestatio Spiritus ad utilitatem*, "to every man the manifestation of the Spirit is given unto profit"] grace, therefore, and a certain brightness, etc., *as far as* that the face may be made glad with oil. *And bread.* Bread, therefore, and wine are the same thing. For this bread and wine is justice, is truth, is Christ: and both the one and the other gladdens and strengthens the heart.

VERS. 16.-- *Which he hath planted.* [n: (ID.)] Why does he leave the brightness of the world to those planted by himself? Because there is the coot, a bird which dwells in the waters; these are they who abide in the grace of baptism. Or, the heron is a most rapacious bird, and such as these Christ does not reject.

VERS. 17.-- *There the sparrows.* [n: (ID.)] Small flying things, etc., *as far as* that they may build their nests in the cedars of Libanus. *The heron's* [1017A] *house.* [n: (AUG.)] *Is the leader of them,* that is, of the sparrows, or of the cedars, which nevertheless cannot be said in Latin, because cedars are of the feminine gender; and this in no way holds according to the letter, for how is the house, that is, the nest of the coot, which is a bird of the sea or of the marsh, the leader of sparrows? But by this he hints that spiritual things are to be sought. And if the sparrows build their nests among the rich of this world, etc., *as far as* but they shall not perish, because the rock is their leader. [n: (CASS.)] Or the coot is a bird which dwells in the waters; these are they who abide in the grace of baptism, whose house is baptism. And it is as if he should say: Who are the sparrows? Those who are baptized. And this is it, *the coot's house,* that is, baptism, which is the house of the regenerate, *is the leader of them,* that is, of the sparrows, that is, of the Christians.

[n: (HIER., CASS.)] The heron: a most rapacious bird, etc., [1017B] *as far as* because he himself, being converted, converted many others to the faith. *For the harts: a rock.* [n: (AUG.)] The harts are great spiritual men, etc., *as far as* this is that *from which* (as was said above) *the birds of the air give their voices.*

VERS. 19.-- *He hath made the moon.* [n: (CASS.)] After earthly things he passes over to the heavenly, which nevertheless act for the earth, that is, serve it. After so many kinds of Christians, which he signified by the animals, and after the aforesaid ordering, ascending he adds concerning Christ and the Church, namely how the Church here in time waxes and wanes, as though she made the settings and risings of the true sun.

VERS. 20.-- *Thou hast appointed,* etc. *And night was made* in them, that is, the dark obstinacy of the mind. *And these shall pass through.* [n: (AUG.)] Not, as above, the converted, the Gentiles, but the demons, who at the time of the passion found the hearts [1017C] of the wavering apostles; but when Christ rose again, because they cannot prevail in all, they abide *in the dens,* that is, in the hearts of the obstinate: so, when Christ sets in some man, evils grow strong; when he rises again, they flee. *The setting.* The sun so sets that it may rise: shall he then that sleeps, that is, Christ, not go on to rise again? He set because he willed it, no man takes away his life; when he set, darkness came upon the apostles, their hope failed.

VERS. 21.-- *The young lions,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] There are also princes of the demons, as the whelps too are contemptible demons, which in the darkness seek to seduce souls; the greater lion always *goeth about seeking whom he may devour [n: (I Pet. V)]*, and they are fed with the errors of men.

VERS. 22.-- *The sun is risen,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] After [1017D] he has said what the setting of the true sun did, he adds concerning the rising.

VERS. 24.-- *With possession.* [n: (AUG.)] Elsewhere, *creature.* [n: (ID.)] Through Christ the Father, etc., *as far as* yet the wood was given, upon which we might sail across.

VERS. 25.-- *This great sea,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] The second part, where he says: How he leads the Church across the sea of this world to the shore of the homeland. *Creeping things,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Evil spirits, whose snares creep.

VERS. 26.-- *This dragon which thou hast formed.* [n: (ID.)] Besides these animals, etc., *as far as* he was made greater in malice. *For mocking.* [n: (ID.)] It is said in Job *(Job XL)* that Satan is mocked by the angels of God, who are set over the powers of the air, that they may not hurt us.

VERS. 27.-- *Meat.* [n: (ID.)] The dragon's food is earth, [1018A] because it was said to him: *Thou shalt eat earth all the days of my life [n: (Gen. III)]*. [var: Vulg. Gen. 3:14 *cunctis diebus vitae tuae*, "all the days of thy life"] Because they that love earthly things are the devil's food. But the food of the just man is the word of God, whence, *Not in bread alone doth man live, but in every word that proceedeth from the mouth of God [n: (Deut. VIII)]*.

VERS. 28.-- *Thy hand, all things shall be filled with good.* [n: (CASS.)] Or the hand is the power which abundantly fills all things.

VERS. 29.-- *But when thou turnest away.* [n: (AUG.)] Many, being filled with good, have ascribed it to themselves; but God, willing to prove to them that they have it not from themselves but from God, so that with the good they may have humility, sometimes troubles them, turns away his face, and they fall into temptation.

VERS. 30.-- *Send forth thy spirit.* [n: (CASS.)] The dust is called theirs, because from themselves they have it that they are [1018B] dust. But the Spirit is the Lord's: behold the medicine for those returned into dust.

VERS. 32.-- *To tremble.* [n: (AUG.)] Better is the trembling of humility than the confidence of pride. Whence the Apostle: *With trembling work out your salvation [n: (Phil. II)]*. *The mountains.* [n: (ID.)] Those who were proud, boasting that all things came from themselves. Even as it happened on mount Sina.

VERS. 33.-- *I will sing.* [n: (CASS.)] The third part, where he promises that he will sing always. As if he should say: These foretold things the Lord has done, and these being beheld. *In my life.* [n: (AUG.)] Present and to come; of hope and of sight. Now hope is our life, afterwards it shall be eternal. The life of mortal life is the hope of immortal life. *As long as I am.* [n: (ID.)] Because in him I am without end, I will sing psalms as long as I am, lest any other act of that life be supposed than to sing psalms: [1018C] because he who is praised cannot come to be wearisome, it does not become wearisome to praise him; but he is ever loved, ever praised.

VERS. 34.-- *Let it be pleasant.* Another reading: *Let my disputation be sweet to him.* [n: (AUG.)] For the disputation of man with God is the confession of sins. Whence Isaiah [n: (Isa. I)]. *Take away the wickednesses from your souls, say ye to do well, judge for the fatherless, and come and let us dispute, saith the Lord.* [var: Vulg. Isa. 1:16-18 *auferte malum cogitationum vestrarum ab oculis meis … discite benefacere … et venite, et arguite me, dicit Dominus*, "learn to do well … and come and reprove me"] Thy disputation, therefore, is the sacrifice of thy humility, which is sweet to God.

VERS. 35.-- *Bless the Lord, O my soul.* [n: (ID.)] He repeats what stood at the beginning of the psalm: *Bless the Lord, O my soul,* hinting by this that the end of good men is like the beginning.
