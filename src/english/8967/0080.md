## PSALM CIV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1018D]

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Alleluia.* [n: (CASS.)] Here for the first time *alleluia,* nor was it set down before this by anyone; and no tongue has presumed to change it. ⟦s2⟧ *Alleluia,* praise ye. ⟦s3⟧ *Ia* is the name of the invisible God, which belongs to him alone. ⟦s4⟧ Through twenty psalms, which have this title, the great works of God are told, and great is the exultation in them. [n: (CASS.)] Here the prophet commends the good among that people of Israel, who together with other believers are the true seed of Abraham, the children of the promise.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Give glory to the Lord.* [n: (AUG.)] The first psalm to which alleluia is prefixed, etc., *as far as* he subjoins what temporal things the mercy of God gave them.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *All things:* [n: (CASS.)] all things; there is nothing that shame should [1019A] keep silent. ⟦s2⟧ Or, all the things which they shall tell are wonderful, for no one can relate them all.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Be ye praised in the name,* that is, rejoice that you are called Christians from Christ.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] Be ye praised: he does not forbid being praised, if they are good; if they are not, then not save in the Lord, who makes men good. ⟦s2⟧ Be ye praised in the name. ⟦s3⟧ Which elsewhere is, *In the Lord shall my soul be praised:* what he adds there, *let the meek hear and rejoice:* here too in a manner, *let the heart rejoice,* as if he should say,

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *The face,* etc. [n: (ID.)] That is, the presence, etc., *as far as* the wicked man can fear the presence which the good man loves. [n: (HIER.)] From the martyr Hippolytus, etc., *as far as* there about to take his Church from among the Gentiles.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *Remember, and the rest.* [n: (AUG.)] Seek [1019B] him, etc., *as far as* if it is a great thing to seek what God is, at least behold the things which he has made. ⟦s2⟧ *The wonders,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Little by little he ascends from visible wonders to judgments: thence to the everlasting testament, where there is both precept and promise.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *In all the earth.* [n: (ID.)] Think not that the sons of Abraham are in one nation according to the flesh, because in all the earth are Jacob and Israel, to whom he makes known his judgments, but not to another nation.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *For ever,* [n: (ID.)] that is, unto eternity, whence afterwards, unto an everlasting testament. ⟦s2⟧ Which testament, if it be taken as the Old on account of the land of Chanaan, of which it follows. ⟦s3⟧ How is it everlasting, since that earthly inheritance cannot be everlasting? ⟦s4⟧ *Of the testament.* ⟦s5⟧ For that is called a testament which [1019C] is promised, and the teaching in which the promise is declared, and for the keeping of which it is rendered. [n: (ID.)] The promise is what we ought to receive: the commandment, what we ought to do. ⟦s6⟧ This is faith, that the just man live by faith, and to this faith an inheritance [sic: *haere ditas*] is promised. ⟦s7⟧ *Abraham, Isaac and Jacob.* ⟦s8⟧ Behold three patriarchs, whose God he calls himself in a special manner: *I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, the God of Jacob.* ⟦s9⟧ Behold a threefold authority.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *Unto an everlasting testament,* [n: (ID.)] that is, that from the very word fulfilled, etc., *as far as* and the son abides in the house for ever.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *The land of Chanaan,* [n: (CASS.)] that is, that which is signified by it: for he gave this land also as a figure of the eternal inheritance [n: (I Joan. IV)]. [n: (AUG.)] Fittingly the eternal inheritance is called the land of Chanaan, [1019D] because from it servants are driven off and sons received.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *When they were in number.* [n: (CASS.)] What supports of temporal life he bestowed upon the fathers and upon their sons, as well in flesh as in faith, not that they should cleave to these things, but should refer them to the eternal good, which is, that being drawn by them they should seek out the justifications and the law of God.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *From a kingdom.* [n: (AUG.)] Not that the kingdom of Israel then was, but that it was to be.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Touch ye not.* [n: (ID.)] These words, although they are not in the history, God nevertheless spoke them either secretly in the hearts of men, or by an angel. ⟦s2⟧ *The anointed,* [n: (ID.)] because even then they were Christians, though secretly, whence *Abraham rejoiced that he might see my day; he saw it and [1020A] was glad [n: (Joan. VIII)]*. ⟦s3⟧ For no one, either before or after, was reconciled to God apart from this faith, *for there is one mediator, Christ [n: (I Tim. II)]*.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 16.-- *And he called.* [n: (AUG.)] The fourth part, where he begins to narrate, etc., *as far as* he commanded that to be which already was in his disposition. ⟦s2⟧ *So he called, and all the staff of bread was broken [n: (Gen. XXXVII)]*.

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *A man.* [n: (ID.)] Joseph, sold by his brothers, was afterwards exalted in Egypt for his justice. ⟦s2⟧ *Joseph.* [n: (ID.)] If Joseph was both humbled and exalted, etc., *as far as* but *seek ye first the kingdom of God, and all things shall be added.*

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18.-- *In fetters.* [n: (ID.)] We do not read of Joseph's fetters in Genesis, but what was there passed over is to be believed, since the Spirit says it in the psalm. [1020B] [n: (CASS.)] And although he found favour with the keeper of the prison, yet he was first in anguish. ⟦s2⟧ There follows. ⟦s3⟧ *The iron pierced,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ As if, besides the evils of the body, *the iron,* that is, the tribulation of hard necessity, which was in his burning and anxious soul, *pierced through,* etc. ⟦s5⟧ Or according to another reading, *his soul passed through the iron,* that is, the tribulation of hard necessity. ⟦s6⟧ The same sense as the former.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 23.-- *In the land of Cham,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] Cham the father of Chanaan, from whom the Egyptians draw their origin.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 24.-- *And he increased his people,* etc. [n: (ID.)] The fifth part. ⟦s2⟧ Behold, he sets forth briefly what was done in Egypt, which afterwards he relates more fully. ⟦s3⟧ He tells also how he strengthened his people over their enemies.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1020C] *And he strengthened him.* ⟦s2⟧ By the signs of Moses, namely; for before this the male offspring of the children of Israel were being killed, and they themselves were worn down in the making of bricks. ⟦s3⟧ But afterwards, by a mighty hand and by the signs and portents of the Lord, they were made men to be feared and honoured.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 25.-- *He turned.* [n: (ID.)] He begins to relate how this was done. ⟦s2⟧ He turned: not that God is the author of evil, but he uses evils well; while he increased the prosperity of Israel, they were turned to envy, because they were already evil. ⟦s3⟧ *That they should hate.* ⟦s4⟧ Envy is the hatred of another's prosperity.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 26.-- *He sent.* ⟦s2⟧ He who could do all things by himself, that he might teach with what moderation he had done these things. ⟦s3⟧ *Whom he chose, him.* [n: (ID.)] It would have been enough to say, whom he chose: but nothing [1020D] is to be sought in this, that he added, *him,* but it is the custom of the divine manner of speech, for the sake of explanation.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 27.-- *The words of his signs.* [n: (ID.)] By which it was preached by them, etc., *as far as* he wrought therefore through them words not of voices, but of signs or of wonders.

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 28.-- [n: (ID.)] Or: *They provoked his words,* which is plain. ⟦s2⟧ Or, Moses and Aaron did not provoke his words, because they bore patiently with those men, namely the Egyptians, even the hardest of them, on account of the commandment of the Lord, until all things which the Lord had appointed should be done upon them. ⟦s3⟧ *Darkness,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] They suffered it among the plagues. ⟦s4⟧ But here he says that their hearts were *darkened,* who despised the precepts of God. ⟦s5⟧ Blindness is a fitting [1021A] beginning of the plagues, without which they would not have incurred the rest.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 29.-- *He turned their waters.* [n: (AUG.)] The second plague, which in Exodus is the first, which signified that he would turn the waters of the Red Sea also, and that unto death for them.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 30.-- *The earth brought forth.* [n: (AUG.)] He turned their land into frogs, because they were so many that this is fitly said by way of hyperbole.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 31.-- *He spoke, and there came the dog-fly,* etc. [n: (ID.)] For in his word they were without time, before they came to be in time through that same word: or through angels, or through Moses he in a manner spoke, that it should be done then, when it was to be done.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 32.-- *He made their rains,* etc. [n: (ID.)] It is like to that: *and their land gave forth frogs.*

⟦p30⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1021B]VERS. 34.-- *The locust and the bruchus.* [n: (ID.)] The locust and the bruchus are one plague, because the one is the parent, the other the offspring.

⟦p31⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 36.-- *And he struck.*[n: (Id. )] Although there are ten plagues, etc., *as far as* who could not despise the wage of their labours, though a temporal one.

⟦p32⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 37.-- *And he brought them out,* etc. ⟦s2⟧ The sixth part, according to Cassiodorus and Augustine. ⟦s3⟧ Above, the justice of God told what evils it brought upon the enemies of the Israelites: now he subjoins what temporal goods the mercy of God gave them, that being helped here they may seek the law of God. [n: (AUG.)] *And he brought out*: God said to Abraham, etc., *as far as* then there follows the second benefit, *And [sic: *Ft*] there was no one weak, and the rest.*

⟦p33⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 38.-- *Because the fear of them fell upon [1021C] them.* ⟦s2⟧ They feared Israel, lest he should consume what was left of them.

⟦p34⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] Here was fulfilled what he said above, and he strengthened him over his enemy.

⟦p35⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 39.-- *He spread a cloud for their protection.* ⟦s2⟧ And this is the second part, according to Augustine, where he subjoins what temporal goods the mercy of God gave them, the evils having been told above which the justice of God brought upon their enemies.

⟦p36⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (AUG.)] Now he begins the benefits which were done for them as they journeyed in the desert.

⟦p37⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 40.-- *They asked,* [n: (AUG.)] the good, for the support of the weak. ⟦s2⟧ *The quail: and he filled them with the bread of heaven:* which is also flesh, because here he speaks not of the embittering of the wicked, but of the faith of the elect: they themselves are to be understood [1021D] to have asked, whereby the murmurs of the embittered might be held down.

⟦p38⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 42.-- *For he remembered his holy word.* [n: (ID.)] By all these benefits God commends in Abraham the merit of faith, whence he adds: *for he remembered.*

⟦p39⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 44.-- *And he gave them.* [n: (AUG.)] Not that they should worship him for the sake of these things, but that they should refer them to the eternal good, that is, *that they may keep his justifications. ⟦s2⟧ They possessed,* etc., not that these things are to be held for the highest good, but that they may be free for those things whereby the highest and eternal good may be acquired, not that in them they should melt away in luxury or grow torpid.

⟦p40⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 45.-- *The justifications,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] They are where [1022A] there is a plain and absolute precept, as, *Thou shalt love God and thy neighbour,* which are to be kept. ⟦s2⟧ The law is of those things which are to be kept not bodily but spiritually: and therefore it is to be sought out, because it is a shadow of things to come. ⟦s3⟧ *The law.* ⟦s4⟧ As concerning its circumcision and the other matters, which are to be diligently investigated, because they are spoken mystically.
