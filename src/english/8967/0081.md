## PSALM CV.

VERS. 1.-- *Alleluia. Alleluia.* Some say that one *alleluia* belongs to the end of the preceding psalm, and the other to the following. For Jerome hands down that this rule is to be held. To whose assertion Cassiodorus also agrees. But the greater number assign it, wherever alleluia stands, to the psalm that follows: and sometimes once, sometimes twice; as the Lord in the Gospel now said *amen,* now *amen amen,* for the greater certainty [1022B] of the matter. With these Augustine agrees, especially since no codices of the Greeks have alleluia at the end of a psalm.

*Give glory to the Lord.* [n: (AUG.)] This psalm is joined to the preceding, etc., *as far as* he admonishes therefore to a twofold confession, of praise and of sins.

[n: (CASS.)] The Hebrew, having left the perfidy of his fathers and being turned to the mercy of God, persuades others to be turned to the same mercy, praying that they may be joined to the well-pleasing people, which was to be gathered at the coming of Christ.

VERS. 2.-- *Who shall declare.* [n: (CASS.)] He that asks for mercy is filled with the consideration of the divine works, and marvelling says: *Who shall declare?*

VERS. 3.-- *Blessed are they that keep.* [n: (AUG.)] Or because [1022C] there follows, etc., *as far as* judgment and justice, because *Blessed are they that,* etc.

VERS. 5.-- *To see,* etc. [n: (AUG.)], that we may see thee in that goodness which thou bestowest upon the elect, that is, by thy grace let us see, lest we remain blind. *That thou mayest be praised:* it is strange that in very many codices, etc., *as far as* because he is not praised save on account of thee.

VERS. 6.-- *We have sinned.* The second part, where he enumerates the sins of the fathers which they committed in Egypt, and yet says that they were delivered by God.

*Iniquity.* [n: (CASS.)] A third time he says the same, that the confession may appear pure; a man is loosed the more quickly, the more keenly he condemns himself.

VERS. 7.-- *They understood not.* [n: (AUG.)] That is, what thou didst will to give them through those things, that is, not a temporal but [1022D] an eternal good; which is awaited through patience, and therefore they, being impatient, murmured, and wished to be happy in temporal things. [n: (CASS.)] There are two things which commend benefits, understanding and memory, and he reproves both of these in them. And first the understanding, when he says: *they understood not the wonders. They provoked, going up.* [n: (AUG.)] Fearing God and despairing, etc., *as far as* whence it deserved to obtain among the others the privilege or the princedom of the kingdom.

VERS. 9.-- *As in the desert.* [n: (ID.)] Because where there were depths, it was made by dryness like a desert where there are no waters.

VERS. 10.-- *He redeemed them from the hand.* [n: (ID.)] At what price? As if he should say, at none; but it was done as a figure of baptism, [1023A] in which we are redeemed from the hand of the devil by the blood of Christ; whence also he said *the Red Sea,* because by its colour blood was figured.

VERS. 12.-- *And they believed in.* [n: (AUG.)] Behold the hard hearts of the Jews, which believe not before but after miracles: it had been better to believe him who promised.

VERS. 13.-- *They quickly did, forgetting.* The third part, where he sets forth the sins which they committed after the going out: nor yet did the mercy of God fail them, he being appeased through Moses. Or rebuke, by the justice of God, taking vengeance upon certain of them through Moses. [n: (CASS.)] *They quickly did.* With the utmost speed they were changed, etc., *as far as* for God put off giving, that he might prove them.

VERS. 14.-- *They lusted a lust in the desert.* [n: (AUG.)] Such a manner of speech, as *they praised,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] [1023B] The excess of the desire is shown by the doubling of the word.

VERS. 15.-- *Fulness.* This is not that fulness of which it is said. *Blessed are they that hunger and thirst after justice, for they shall be filled [n: (Matth. V)]*.

*Into their souls.* [n: (AUG.)] The animal nature, which is sustained by food: whence also in the Gospel: *Is not the soul more than the meat?* Or, souls, that the will, which had asked in despair, might be overcome by abundance; after fulness there follows sedition, whence he adds.

VERS. 16.-- *And they provoked.* [n: (CASS.)] Canvassing for honour by envy, Moses in the camp, and Aaron the holy one of the Lord: this provoking was for the priesthood which Aaron had, and his sons.

VERS. 17.-- *The earth was opened.* [n: (ID.)] The quality of the punishment inflicted declares the praise of God, who has avenged his own; they are devoured by the earth [1023C] who savoured of earthly things.

VERS. 18.-- *In the synagogue.* [n: (ID.)] For Core and two hundred and fifty with him, wishing to have the priesthood of Aaron, took censers, to burn incense, and therefore were consumed by divine fire. *Sinners.* [n: (AUG.)] He does not say sinning, which belongs to a good man too: but *sinners,* men exceedingly wicked and laden with heavy burdens of sins.

VERS. 19.-- *And they made.* [n: (CASS.)] Not enduring the absence of Moses, while he himself tarried on the top of mount Sinai to receive the law, they made a calf. *Horeb.* It is interpreted *Calvary,* where the crucifixion took place, etc., *as far as* this the impatience of the fickle did, as all evils are born of it.

VERS. 20.-- *In the likeness.* [n: (AUG.)] He does not say into [1023D] a likeness, but in a likeness. And it is such a manner of speech as above, *and they believed in his words.*

VERS. 21.-- *They forgot.* [n: (CASS.)] Great forgetfulness indeed in the memory, if not the deliverance.

*Terrible things.* Because they afflicted their adversaries, and showed to these men whom they ought to fear.

VERS. 23.-- *Had stood in the breach.* [n: (AUG.)] Saying: *If thou forgive them not, blot me out of thy book.* How much avails the intercession of the saints? Moses, secure concerning the justice of God, by which he could not blot him out, obtained mercy for those who would justly have perished.

*That he might turn away his wrath.* Concerning the water of contradiction and the immolation of the sons, whereat God was the more vehemently angered, and yet afterwards had mercy. *And for nothing.* The fourth [1024A] part, where he likewise enumerates yet other sins, and says that God was appeased through Phinees.

*They believed not his word.* [n: (AUG.)] They despised that which was signified by her, etc., *as far as* they did not await his counsel.

*In his tents.* [n: (CASS.)] They murmured, because this befalls not them that labour but the idle, whence the greater odium of the crime grows.

VERS. 26.-- *And he lifted up his hand over them,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] This justice of God is set first, that the mercy which follows may be the more glorified, which mercy is shown in what follows, where God is said to be appeased by the intervention of Phinees.

VERS. 28.-- *And they were initiated to Beelphegor.* [n: (ID.)] Or, they were consecrated, as though worshipping only for a time: [1024B] a graver excess and a more vehement wrath: they devoted their sons to the demons. [n: (AUG.)] *And they were initiated,* before he should say, etc., *as far as* he delivered him whose destruction was to be the greater.

VERS. 30.-- *And Phinees stood up.* [n: (CASS.)] With a firm mind, etc., *as far as* because he who acts well, prays.

VERS. 31.-- *And it was reputed,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] God, who knew with how great charity for the people that was done, reputed this to his priest unto justice, not only so long as there is a generation, but even unto eternity.

VERS. 32.-- *And they provoked,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] The fifth part, where he treats of the water of contradiction and of the immolation of the sons, whereat God was the more vehemently angered, and yet afterwards had mercy. As if: they so provoked him in their own devices, as was said above; now [1024C] again they have provoked him.

VERS. 33.-- *And he made a distinction with his lips,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] This miracle, etc., *as far as* he did not hold the confidence he ought to have held. [n: (AUG.)] *And he made a distinction.* As if: God could not do this, etc., *as far as* yet he was not estranged from the kingdom of the grace of God.

VERS. 35.-- *The nations.* Those seven peoples which possessed the land of promise.

VERS. 36.-- *And it became to them a stumbling-block.* [n: (CASS.)] The land of promise, given as a reward, was turned to ruin for those who transgressed, because by conformity with the nations they became idolaters.

VERS. 37.-- *And they sacrificed.* [n: (AUG.)] Although that history does not relate these things, yet this prophet cannot lie. [n: (CASS.)] If not in the first, then in the following [1024D] age was done what David relates.

VERS. 38.-- *And slain.* [n: (AUG.)] A figurative manner of speech, the container for the contained, that is, men; for they themselves were slaying their own souls by sacrificing their sons, and they were defiled in their works. [var: Vulg. Ps. 105:38 *Et infecta est terra in sanguinibus*, "and the land was defiled with blood"]

VERS. 39.-- *In their own devices.* [n: (ID.)] Not that they had invented them first, but because they imitated others. Which is plainer in the Greek, both in these places and above; whence others also render it, pursuits, or affections, or pleasures.

VERS. 40.-- *He abhorred.* [n: (CASS.)] That is, he turned away from them: and to be turned away from is the utmost of all evils, whence ruin follows, *and he delivered.*

VERS. 41.-- *And he delivered them,* etc. [n: (ID.)] Behold, they who gloried in idols lie under the reproach of servitude, [1025A] because they serve their enemies, which is a hard thing. *They that hated.* It is grievous to serve one who has been offended, who is appeased by no compliance.

VERS. 43.-- *Often he delivered,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] A twofold mercy; for above he set out first the benefits of God, and yet their iniquity does not cease; he brought in vengeance, and again he has mercy on the afflicted.

*In his own counsel.* [n: (AUG.)] The counsel of man is evil to the man himself, who seeks the things that are his own, not the things that are God's.

VERS. 45.-- *Of the testament.* [n: (CASS.)] The cause that Israel did not perish for its debt is the New Testament, wherein is the coming of the Lord, who was promised to Abraham. *It repented him.* [n: (AUG.)] Because he changed what seemed about to destroy them. [n: (CASS.)] Then he repents, when he sees us repent, and he is such to us as we are to him. [n: (AUG.)] God does nothing as by a sudden [1025B] counsel, but what he foreknew from eternity. But he is said to change that which, according to the causes of things, would seem to follow, when other causes intervene on the contrary part. But both the causes, and what should come to pass, he unchangeably foresaw.

VERS. 46.-- *Unto mercies.* [n: (CASS.)] Because of the diverse gifts: prophets, apostles, martyrs and confessors were of the Jews, whom he gave unto mercies: and this *in the sight of all, they had taken them,* [var: Vulg. Ps. 105:46 *in conspectu omnium qui ceperant eos*, "in the sight of all that had taken them captive"] that is, of the Gentiles, that the Gentiles might marvel at them, who before had despised them. Or, made free before the demons, under whom they had been captives.

VERS. 47.-- *Save us.* [n: (CASS.)] The sixth part. Now filled with the abundance of the praise of God, he prays for that which he saw was to come, that the Church may be gathered out of all nations, [1025C] and may sing eternal praises. [n: (AUG.)] *Save us,* as if: Thou hast given mercies in the sight of all, etc., *as far as* there follows. *And gather us from among the nations.*

VERS. 48.-- *Blessed be the Lord.* [n: (CASS.)] This is the praise which he desires that they, being gathered, should sing; this the Church now sings, glad for so great a good. *From everlasting unto,* etc., that is, from eternity unto eternity, because he shall be praised without end: this is the third consummation of the body of Christ in immortality, which was prayed at the beginning of the psalm: *Remember us,* O Lord, *in the good pleasures of thy people,* to see, to rejoice, that thou mayest be praised with thine inheritance.
