## PSALM CVI.

*Alleluia, alleluia.* [n: (AUG.)] This psalm commends [1025D] the mercy of God, etc., *as far as* the things which the Apostle set down, although they signify the same.

VERS. 1.-- *Give praise to the Lord.* [n: (ID.)] This psalm teaches what we are through ourselves, etc., *as far as* he first admonishes the redeemed to give praise. *Give praise.*

[n: (CASS., AUG.)] Of which there are seven divisions, etc., *as far as* in the seventh he treats of heretics, there, *And they were made few.*

VERS. 3.-- *And from the sea.* [n: (CASS.)] For the south he puts the sea, understanding thereby the ocean of that quarter; and this can be taken for any quarter of the world whatever.

VERS. 4.-- *They wandered.* [n: (ID.)] The second part, where is the first temptation, etc., *as far as* fourthly, confession for having been heard, that is, praise.

[1026A]VERS. 5.-- *Hungry.* [n: (AUG.)] He signifies the curious of the world, who sought out the truth by divers opinions, and found it not. *Their soul,* etc. God commends himself, because it is expedient for us; we fail that we may ask, and may love him who comes to our aid.

VERS. 6.-- *And he led.* [n: (AUG.)] As it were, he rescued them from ignorance, showing the way, Christ. Behold, he freed them from error; but in what manner he freed them from the hunger and thirst wherewith they burned, he will say afterwards; but he sets confession first.

VERS. 8.-- *Wonderful things.* [n: (HIER.)] As it were, in a wonderful manner of redemption were the mercies given.

VERS. 9.-- *Him that is hungry.* [n: (CASS.)] Him that desires to know, or him that desires not to know, but fails through too great fasting, as is wont to befall one worn out [1026B] by too great hunger or by infirmity, so that he desires not to eat, but fails through too great fasting.

*Sitting.* [n: (AUG.)] The third part, where the second temptation is set down, etc., *as far as* let us also consider that we can do this.

VERS. 11.-- *Because they exasperated.* [n: (ID.)] This is another thing, attempting yet again, etc., *as far as* whence he was wroth with them.

*The counsel of the Most High.* That they who were proud might be humbled in straits, which was a wholesome counsel, that the disease of the world might be cured by a contrary remedy.

VERS. 12.-- *Nor was there any who.* [n: (ID.)] For when God withdraws his aid, thou canst labour against vices, but not conquer; and when thou art pressed by thine own depraved habit, the heart will be humbled, that thou mayest learn to cry out, whence he subjoins: *And they cried out.*

[1026C]VERS. 13.-- *And they cried out.* [n: (CASS.)] From a like cause the same sentence as above.

VERS. 16.-- *Brazen gates.* [n: (ID.)] The brazen gates are the vicious customs of men, which suffer not those shut in to go out.

*Bars.* Unclean spirits, who bolt the gates. He puts brazen and iron for strength, but the power of God breaks them.

VERS. 18.-- *All meat.* [n: (AUG.)] The fourth part, where the third temptation is set down, which is weariness. They who above were languishing with hunger are in like manner endangered by loathing; lest therefore thou think men secure through satiety, since they die of loathing also. And this is *all meat,* etc.

[n: (AUG.)] After he has come from ignorance to truth, from error to the way, from the hunger of wisdom to the word [1026D] of faith, and after he has overcome the difficulties of evils, so as to live without complaint, there remains weariness, so that sometimes neither to read nor to pray delights him; he has escaped unskilfulness and concupiscence, but he dies of weariness.

VERS. 23.-- *They that go down the sea.* The fifth part, etc., *as far as* if the whole ship is in peril.

VERS. 24, 25.-- *They have seen the works of the Lord.* [n: (AUG.)] Or *they saw,* etc., *as far as* he expounds their storms, subjoining, *he spoke, and there stood. In the deep. He spoke, and there stood,* subjoining, *the spirit of the storm.* [n: (ID.)] The deep is the heart of men, whence the winds break forth, and there arise tempests of seditions and dissensions.

VERS. 27.-- *As a drunken man.* [n: (ID.)] They stagger, because their prosperity or their adversity is greater than their strength. [1027A] *And all their wisdom was swallowed up.* They appear restlessly wise when they speak, when they read; but when the tempest comes, all human counsels fail; what is to be done is not seen. It remains therefore to cry out, that God may make their heart firm, lest it be tossed about by temptations.

VERS. 32.-- *In the Church.* [n: (AUG.)] Behold whence he has spoken all these things, and where all that is foretold is done.

VERS. 33.-- *He hath turned rivers.* [n: (CASS.)] The sixth part, thus far: how he makes even the evils of enemies to profit. [n: (AUG.)] *He hath turned rivers.* Consequently the Church is commended, etc., *as far as* whence also the benefits of God toward the Church are openly shown.

VERS. 34.-- *In saltness.* [n: (CASS.)] Salt moisture, where it prevails, takes away fruitfulness.

[1027B] *From the malice of them that dwell.* [n: (AUG.)] Because of the malice of those who inhabited that land, that is, the tabernacle of the Lord, with deceitful minds.

VERS. 37.-- *And they sowed.* [n: (CASS.)] They sow fields and plant vineyards who purify their bodies by a heavenly institution, that they may bear the fruits of good works.

VERS. 38.-- *Exceedingly:* [n: (CASS.)] for "greatly," or because many are called, but few chosen.

VERS. 39.-- *And few.* [n: (AUG.)] The seventh part, where somewhat is said also of heretics, etc., *as far as* they were not of the fewness of the elect. *Afflicted.* [n: (CASS.)] Either those who persevere under heretics, or heretics by the snares of the devil. Sorrow and tribulation to those who forsake the author of salvation.

[1027C]VERS. 40.-- *It was poured out.* [n: (AUG.)] Or, contempt was poured out, etc., *as far as* and what has he done even concerning those that were led astray? Behold.

VERS. 41.-- *He helped the poor man.* God could have suffered them always within, but we should not have profited by them. But when they are separated, and disquiet us with their questions, there is born in us a zeal of inquiry and an example of fear, so that, *He that standeth, let him take heed lest he fall.* And what is done concerning those led astray, in the poor man that is helped? *And he set as sheep.* Thou didst think him one poor man; but this poor man is of a great household, of many churches: yet there is also one Church, one sheep. So great are these mysteries, long hidden, and therefore sweetly found.

VERS. 42.-- *The upright shall see and shall rejoice.* [n: (AUG.)] [1027D] Behold whither the Psalmist tends: to this, namely, that he may show that the good are exalted through the mercy of God, as the wicked are crushed through pride.

VERS. 43.-- *Who is wise, and will keep these things?* [n: (ID.)] The conclusion, as if he should say: since the Lord helps the poor man, therefore: *Who is wise? The mercies.* That he delivers from error, etc., *as far as* and the unjust shall stop his mouth.

## PSALM CVII.

VERS. 1.-- *A canticle of a psalm.* [n: (AUG.)] From the last parts of the two, etc., *as far as* that the words of the prophets might be fulfilled. [n: (CASS.)] Here are many of the same things as above, etc., *as far as* and they are well joined together, which in no wise disagree. [n: (ID.)] In the first there is treated the flight [1028A] of David, etc., *as far as Canticle* belongs to divine things, *Psalm* to actual works.

VERS. 2.-- *Prepared.* [n: (CASS.)] The sixth psalm concerning the two natures in Christ. *My heart is ready.* First Christ gives thanks, because, taking upon him the passion, he rose again into glory. Secondly, what salvation has been wrought for the whole earth, there, *That they may be delivered.*

VERS. 7.-- *That they may be delivered.* [n: (ID.)] The second part: what salvation has been wrought for the whole earth over which is the glory; here also the lowliness of the humanity and the power of the majesty are shown.

*Save.* That they may be delivered in the time to come, here save them, lest they fall. Or, he asks that he himself be saved in the resurrection, that the faithful may be delivered, who by such a sign perceived his Godhead. As it were: that I may sing and give praise, *Save [1028B] me* in the resurrection by thy right hand, that is, by the Word, who is the arm of the Father, that *so thy beloved may be delivered.* The order of the words is inverted. For he says: Save me and hear me, and save me.

VERS. 8.-- *I will rejoice.* [n: (AUG.)] Or, I shall be exalted: above, I shall be glad, but here, I shall be exalted, which agrees well enough, because gladness comes of exaltation. And here Christ speaks in the person of the Church.

VERS. 9.-- *And Ephraim.* [n: (ID.)] Ephraim is mine, that is, mine is the fruit-bearing, which is the taking up of my head. This fruit-bearing is from this, that the head took me up, or I the head. [n: (CASS.)] Or Ephraim, that is, fruit-bearing, is the taking up of my head, because when my head took up the crown of thorns, it bore fruit beyond all others: for Christ gained the whole [1028C] world.

VERS. 11.-- *Who bringeth me into the strong city.* [n: (ID.)] As man, Christ asks, that he may answer; he denies that this can be done by man. The city. Hell, which none could open. The Idumeans are the Gentiles, to whom he became known after the resurrection: these things could be done by the Godhead alone.

VERS. 12.-- *Thou hast cast us off, and thou wilt not go forth.* [n: (ID.)] He says "us" in the plural, because here Christ suffers together with his members; above, speaking in the singular, "he will bring me." Seek the expositions of all these things in the ninth psalm.
