## PSALM CXIV.

⟦p1⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Alleluia.* ⟦s2⟧ This title is often repeated, because the praise of God is both the dignity of works and the price of labours, that is, it is both the merit and the reward. [n: (AUG.)] The Prophet, delivered from the pit of sins and set upon the rock, [1036D] first gives thanks because he was heard, and says that he has called upon him against all perils.

⟦p2⟧ ⟦s1⟧ This psalm is the consolation of the faithful against the sorrows of the world.

⟦p3⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *I have loved.* [n: (ID.)] This the sheep says which had strayed, the son returned, the pilgrim of this world.

⟦p4⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (CASS.)] *I have loved.* ⟦s2⟧ Not for the temporal goods given to me, as the unbelievers do, but *because,* etc. ⟦s3+4⟧ God is loved even by unbelievers because he gives good things; whence: *I will praise thee when thou shalt do well to him [n: (Psal. XLVIII)]*. [var: Vulg. Ps 48:19 *confitebitur tibi*, "he will praise thee," in the third person, which is what the gloss's argument requires] He is loved perfectly, him [cj: *eum*; read *cum*, "when"] the mind is not moved by adverse things, but is kindled by hope: which manner he afterwards expounds, *I have loved.* ⟦s5⟧ Not because he made me a king, or gave me prosperity, but because he hears my prayer in straits.

⟦p5⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *And in my days.* [n: (AUG.)] Namely of my misery, which days I made for myself by sinning, which are full of labour and sorrow, that is, of the old rottenness.

⟦p6⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I will call upon.* ⟦s2⟧ Which is not without faith; whence the Apostle: *How shall they call on him in whom they have not believed? [n: (Rom. X.)]* When therefore he says, *they shall call upon,* note that the lover is not idle. ⟦s3⟧ In which, *They have compassed me,* etc. ⟦s4⟧ Or, in the days of salvation, that is, in the time of fulness; whence: *Behold, now is the acceptable time,* etc. [n: (II Cor. VI)].

⟦p7⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 3.-- *Sorrows.* [n: (CASS.)] Sins, whence come the sorrow and the death of the soul; or eternal sorrows, and this shall be in hell. ⟦s2⟧ *The perils of hell.* [n: (AUG.)] For, steeped in carnal pleasure, I did not perceive the evils that compassed me about.

⟦p8⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 4.-- *Trouble and sorrow.* [n: (ID.)] As it were: those things first compassed me about, but afterwards I found trouble [1037B] and sorrow, that is, outward affliction, to be profitable.

⟦p9⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 5.-- *O Lord, deliver.* [n: (CASS.)] Secondly he cries again, that being delivered he may come to rest. ⟦s2⟧ *The Lord is merciful.* [n: (AUG.)] Because he first inclined his ear to me and called me.

⟦p10⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Just.* ⟦s2⟧ Because he scourges again. ⟦s3⟧ *He hath mercy.* ⟦s4⟧ Because he receives unto blessedness. ⟦s5⟧ Nor ought that to be so bitter which he scourges, as that is sweet which he receives, because the Lord keeps the little ones, whom, when they are grown, he seeks for heirs.

⟦p11⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 7.-- *Turn thee.* [n: (ID.)] Not by thy merits or by thy strength, but because the Lord hath done well to thee.

⟦p12⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 8.-- *From death, the eyes.* [n: (ID.)] Death is unbelief; whence: *Let the dead bury their dead;* and again: *The dead shall not praise thee, O Lord [n: (Psal. CIII)]*.

⟦p13⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1037C]VERS. 9.-- *I will please.* [n: (ID.)] He said, etc., *as far as* and this in the land of the living, who live eternally.

## PSALM CXV.

⟦p14⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Alleluia.* ⟦s2⟧ This psalm is fitly entitled with the voice of praise, because in this psalm are the words of the martyrs, who were strong in confession and obtained the rewards of God.

⟦p15⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 10.-- *I have believed,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] First the benefits of God are recalled; and while they doubt what worthy thing they might render for them, the chalice of the passion presents itself, which is given by God. ⟦s2⟧ Secondly they call themselves servants, and sons of the handmaid, that is, of the Church, there: *O Lord, for I,* etc. ⟦s3⟧ It is a true and fitting order, first to believe, afterwards to speak: lest one not yet believing should dare to speak, but that confession should follow faith. ⟦s4⟧ But lest confession be ascribed to the strength of man, he says [1037D] that this is proper to man, that he is a liar: which he would not easily know, unless he had been carried by an excess of mind to the things above. ⟦s5⟧ For it is a great thing if a man marks that he is unwise. ⟦s6⟧ And since man has nothing good of his own, what is there to be rendered again? ⟦s7⟧ *I have believed.* [n: (AUG.)] He says not: *I have believed and I have spoken,* because he did not only believe the other things which faith requires, but together with them he believed also both what reward he ought to hope for by speaking, and what punishment he ought to fear by keeping silence; whence the love of speaking is put in from the reward, and the necessity is laid on from the punishment. ⟦s8⟧ *For which cause.* ⟦s9⟧ For he believes not perfectly who speaks not what he believes; that other servant is slothful, or fearful, but this one is faithful: what he received, he lays out and gains withal, and therefore enter thou [cj: *intra*; read *intrat*, "he enters"] into the joy of his Lord, who says: *Well done, [1038A] good and faithful servant, enter into the joy of thy Lord [n: (Matth. XXV)]*.

⟦p16⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 11.-- *In my excess.* ⟦s2⟧ Or, in my ecstasy. [n: (AUG.)] And ecstasy is here spoken of, etc., *as far as* that *every man is a liar.*

⟦p17⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 12.-- *What shall I render to the Lord.* [n: (ID.)]? ⟦s2⟧ This man, affrighted, looks back upon his own infirmity, and sees that he must not presume of himself. ⟦s3⟧ For of himself man is a liar, as Peter also, when he presumed, was found a liar: but it is the power of God, if he does not believe. ⟦s4⟧ Seeing this wrought in himself by the grace of God, he subjoins, *which he hath rendered to me.* ⟦s5⟧ He says not, for all the things which he hath bestowed. ⟦s6⟧ What goods of man, therefore, went before, that of all the gifts of God it might be called not a bestowing, but a rendering again? ⟦s7⟧ What had gone before but sins? ⟦s8⟧ God therefore renders to men [1038B] good things for evil, having suffered for them, whereas men render evil things for good.

⟦p18⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 13.-- *The chalice of salvation.* [n: (ID.)] The passion, from a likeness, etc., *as far as* which is said afterwards.

⟦p19⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (CASS.)] *The chalice,* because it is drunk under measure. ⟦s2⟧ Whence, *God is faithful, who suffereth you not to be tempted above that which ye are able [n: (I Cor. X)]*. ⟦s3⟧ *Of salvation,* because it is propitious unto eternal salvation.

⟦p20⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 15.-- *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints.* [n: (AUG.)] He who suffered first grants that his own sufferings be imitated; and therefore: *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints.* ⟦s2⟧ He bought it with the blood which he shed for them, that they might not doubt to die for him. ⟦s3⟧ Bought therefore at this price, he confesses his own condition: *O Lord, for I,* etc.

⟦p21⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [1038C]VERS. 16.-- *O Lord, for,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Secondly they call themselves servants, and sons of the Church, because there is no place of true sacrifice outside the Catholic Church, lest any of the heretics should think his martyrdom to be pleasing to God. ⟦s2⟧ *O Lord.* ⟦s3⟧ He wonders that he has received such things as the Lord received for the redemption of the world. ⟦s4⟧ And to increase the wonder he repeats: *I am thy servant. ⟦s5⟧ The son of the handmaid.* [n: (AUG.)] Of the heavenly Jerusalem, free from sins, the handmaid of justice. ⟦s6⟧ But they who are outside the Church are not sons of the handmaid, even though they call themselves servants (because they have the name of Christ) and martyrs, as the heretics do. ⟦s7⟧ *The son of the handmaid,* Not a schismatic. ⟦s8⟧ Or, as a handmaid brings forth not for herself, but for the Lord; so I too in my death shall bring forth some for thee, as also in my life.

⟦p22⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 17.-- *Thou hast broken my bonds.* [n: (CASS.)] By the power [1038D] of martyrdom the bonds of sins are broken, because all things are washed away by the blood. ⟦s2⟧ This verse is believed by some to be of such power that a man's sins are forgiven, if at the end of his life it be said with a threefold confession.

⟦p23⟧ ⟦s1⟧ [n: (ID.)] Who *precious. ⟦s2⟧ O Lord, to thee will I sacrifice;* speaking to God, he affirms what he had said above.

⟦p24⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *I will sacrifice.* [n: (AUG., CASS.)] And because thou hast broken them, to thee will I sacrifice, as one that is thankful, *The sacrifice of praise.* ⟦s2⟧ So that:

⟦p25⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 18, 19.-- *I will pay [sic: *reddum*] my vows.* ⟦s2⟧ Not everywhere, but *in the courts,* that is, in the Catholic Church, not in that of the heretics. ⟦s3⟧ *Of the house of the Lord.* ⟦s4⟧ He shows which it is.

⟦p26⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *In the sight of all the people.* [n: (CASS.)] He ought to be praised publicly who was willing to suffer for all, that the more may be edified. ⟦s2⟧ *I will pay my vows.* ⟦s3⟧ Myself, who am the image [1039A] of God, which must be rendered to God, etc., *as far as* and here he names Jerusalem more openly. [n: (AUG.)] But lest [sic: *ue*] any think that by Jerusalem the Jews alone are signified, who glory in this name, let him hear the following psalm [cj: *Psalmus*; read *Psalmum*, "the following psalm"]: *Praise the Lord, all ye nations.* ⟦s4⟧ These are the courts of the Lord, for that is the people.

## PSALM CXVI.

⟦p27⟧ ⟦s1⟧ *Alleluia.* [n: (CASS.)] A short psalm, the first of all that is an atom, that is, without division: for by reason of its brevity not to be received [cj: *recipi*; read *recipit*, "it admits"] division, but it surpasses all others by the compendiousness of its utterance, and it is as it were the point of the psalms, out of which the divers kinds are formed. ⟦s2⟧ A wondrous variation against weariness, to speak wholesome things, now in few words, now at large.

⟦p28⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 1.-- *Praise the Lord, all ye nations.* [n: (CASS.)] That none out of the whole world be passed over. [n: (AUG.)] And [1039B] these are those courts of the house of the Lord, and that whole people, whereof he treated in the preceding psalm. ⟦s2⟧ Not the Jews alone (who attempt to arrogate it to themselves) do I say, praise ye him. ⟦s3⟧ And praise ye together. [n: (CASS.)] that the praise of all may be brought into one, which belongs to the Catholic Church.

⟦p29⟧ ⟦s1⟧ VERS. 2.-- *Confirmed.* [n: (CASS.)] Because what he promised through the prophets he fulfilled by the coming of Christ; and he made his mercy firm, that it may never be moved, because he who gave it keeps it. ⟦s2⟧ *It remaineth for ever.* [n: (AUG.)] Whether in those things which he promised to the godly, or in those which he threatens to the ungodly.
