## PSALM CXVII.

*Alleluia.* [n: (CASS.)] The fifth psalm concerning the first coming, adorned with many figures, has verses of one ending, some of one beginning, some intercalary, and ornaments of this kind. The faithful people, loosed from sin [1039C], etc., *as far as Blessed is he that.*

VERS. 2.-- *Let him now say.* [n: (ID.)] As I admonish you, so do you admonish one another; or, *let him say,* that is, let him confess.

*Because for ever.* Behold four verses of one ending.

VERS. 5.-- *Out of tribulation.* [n: (ID.)] He sets an example from himself, that he may invite others to mercy.

VERS. 6.-- *The Lord is my helper.* [n: (ID.)] The same beginning in two.

VERS. 7.-- *I shall look down.* Elsewhere, *I shall see.* [n: (ID.)] Here, that I may become safer and more wary. *My enemies.* Those to be damned.

VERS. 8.-- *It is good.* [n: (ID.)] Secondly, he says that trust is to be placed in God alone, through whom he escapes the enmities of the Gentiles, and comes to the true life.

VERS. 9.-- *In princes.* [n: (AUG.)] In the angels, etc., [1039D] *as far as* he who makes them good according to their measure.

VERS. 10.-- *All nations.* [n: (CASS.)] Behold whence he delivers from hidden or from open tribulation: either by not suffering one to fall, or by raising up from the fall [cj: *revelando*; read *relevando*, "by raising up"].

VERS. 11.-- *They that compassed me about,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] As it were rendering the reason whence he is able to overcome so many things, he tells what he first suffered in the head.

VERS. 12.-- *They surrounded me,* etc. [n: (ID.)] What was done by men who knew not, the Holy Spirit foretells with mystical subtlety. Judah not knowing, they made God, by his passion, the concern of all, and sweeter to us: as bees, while they think to lay up for themselves, make honey for others. *And they were enkindled.* Or this is better understood of the Church. For after the passion of the Lord the Jews also were enkindled against the Church of the Gentiles, like fire in [1040A] the thorns of sins. For these, that is, sins, they burned up in the faithful people by the fire of persecution; and wishing to harm it, they profited it. *Because I was avenged.* While, being converted, malice being extinguished in them, they were joined to the Christian people; or, when, being judged in the end, they shall feel the judgment of truth without mercy.

VERS. 13.-- *Being thrust at,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] As when good men are given over to punishments, that they may fall from the faith, and the Lord upheld. [n: (AUG.)] Because he to whom the Lord is strength and praise falls as little as the Lord falls: but those fall who wish to be strength and praise to themselves.

*I was thrust at and overthrown,* etc. After he has shown that he delivers by not suffering one to fall, he shows also that he delivers by raising up from the fall.

VERS. 14.-- *He is become my salvation.* [n: (ID.)] [1040B] He is not made what he was not to himself; but he is salvation to them converted, which he was not to them turned away.

VERS. 17.-- *I shall not die.* [n: (ID.)] The Church says it, since the persecutor thinks that by a slaughter of funerals he can blot out the Christian name, whereas he rather increases it.

VERS. 18.-- *Chastising.* [n: (CASS.)] But why he bore so many things, he shows: *Chastising,* whatever they do is the chastisement of the Lord. Let them claim nothing to themselves; he is not given over to death whose vices are purged. [n: (AUG.)] Often a father amends his sons by worthless servants, when for those he prepares punishment, for these an inheritance. And what the inheritance is may be known from this: *Open ye the gates.* What within? *Having entered I will confess.*

VERS. 19.-- *Open ye.* [n: (CASS.)] In the third place he desires that the gates of Jerusalem be opened to him, where he speaks also of the corner [1040C] stone. Open, O prophets, who bear the keys of the kingdom of heaven. *The gates.* [n: (CASS., HIER.)] They who have entered through these gates of doctrine praise truly; outside the Catholic Church there is no praise of God. [n: (CASS.)] All these gates lead to one, which is Christ, through whom the just enter into heaven; whence: *I am the door, through which if any man enter in, he shall be safe [n: (Joan. X)]* [var: Vulg. *per me si quis introierit, salvabitur*, "by me, he shall be saved"]. And elsewhere: *No man cometh to the Father, but by me [n: (Joan. XIV)]*. [n: (AUG.)] Behold, he has made mention of both worlds: and because this gate has appeared as Christ, *I will confess to thee,* giving thanks with gladness, *because thou hast heard* at the coming of the Son; for the just of former times were asking for the coming. *The gates of justice.* That is, the gates of death, etc., *as far as* that is, through it unto life.

[1040D]VERS. 22.-- *They that build.* [n: (ID.)] The Jews alone, worshipping God, were building, the rest being destroyed through idolatry; but they did not finish, because they despised Christ.

VERS. 23.-- *This is done by the Lord.* Or, *This is done by the Lord for him,* according to another reading. [n: (ID.)] That is, unto the honour of the Father.

VERS. 24.-- *This is the day.* This is the jubilee year, figuratively observed under the law, which is called *remitting,* or *beginning,* because then bondmen were set free, possessions were restored to their owners: and we on this day have been redeemed from the servitude of sin, to whom an eternal inheritance has been restored. [n: (CASS.)] *This is the day.* Although he made all days, this one singularly, on which the Lord came, on which it is fitting to rejoice, because the devil lost his right and man lived again; and because this is so,

[1041A]VERS. 25.-- *O Lord, save,* etc. Behold the desire of a great petition. *Save,* from the ruin of the world. *Give prosperity,* that these sorrows may be turned into eternal joys.

VERS. 26.-- *Blessed,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] Fourthly he urges that the courts of God be frequented, rejoicing in the coming of Christ. [n: (AUG.)] Cursed therefore is he that comes in his own name, etc., *as far as* he who shall be cursed. [n: (CASS.)] *Blessed.* Praise for the coming; and, O ye other Christians, we the elders, as it were priests, *have blessed you,* which profits much, because *we have blessed you out of the house of the Lord.*

*Out of the house.* [n: (ID.)] That is, the Catholic Church, etc., *as far as* because glad is the confession that follows benefits.

VERS. 27.-- *Appoint a day.* [n: (AUG.)] The great, who go beyond, who with the mind touch the Word, God with God, etc., *as far as* that is, that he may attain unto eternal joys.

[1041B] *A solemn day.* In the old law [n: (Deut. X.)] the Pasch, Pentecost, the feast of tabernacles, three chief solemnities, in which it was commanded that no man should appear empty in the sight of the Lord. He is empty who has not Christ in himself, who is the foundation, which no man is able to change. Hence it is that on those three solemnities the faithful people offers a coin by an offering of the hand, signifying the inward offering. For on a coin there is the name and image of the king. Let each one therefore offer a coin spiritually, that he may have in himself the *name* of the king, that is, of Christ, being called a Christian from Christ; and may show forth to God the *image,* that is, the soul, marked with the light of his countenance. *The horn of the altar.* [n: (CASS.)] Or by the *altar* is understood the sacrament of the body and blood, etc., *as far as he shall not have life everlasting.*

[1041C]Or, the *altar* is understood to be the mind of man; whence in the law it was commanded [n: (Exod. XXVII)] that an altar be made four-cornered and hollow: four-cornered, because the human mind ought to be fortified with four virtues, prudence, justice, fortitude, temperance; and the same ought to be hollow to receive the word of God and the fire of love. For swelling drives back what is poured in; whence in Leviticus it is commanded [n: (Levit. I)] that the little bladder of the throat and the feathers be cast away, when a turtle-dove or a pigeon is offered. The bladder signifies the puffing up of pride; the feathers, empty searchings into lofty things; cast these away therefore, that thou mayest be a hollow altar.

VERS. 28.-- *Thou art my God.* And that we may speak more openly, this is the sense of these words: Let not this feast day upon earth, on which the lamb was slain, suffice: [1041D] but let it be appointed in frequent celebrations, until with minds exalted we come to his inward Godhead, where we shall sing praises thus: *Thou art my God,* etc. And so, as he began with praise, so he ends with it.

VERS. 29.-- *Give praise to the Lord.* From the beginning which we forsook, unto the end to which we return, there is nothing more wholesome, nothing that may delight more, than the praise of God
