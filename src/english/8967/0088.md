## PSALM CXIX.

VERS. 1.-- *A canticle of degrees.* [n: (CASS.)] The beatitude [1042A] which the foregoing psalm sang in a united variety, etc., *as far as* by these degrees therefore one ascends and descends with the unstumbling foot of the mind. [n: (AUG.)] *A canticle of degrees.* The name of degrees is ambiguous among us, etc., *as far as* and the height of his own Godhead. The first degree, to go out from the evils of the world, and to renounce all vanity. [n: (CASS.)] The beginning of advancement is to leave carnal vices and to make supplication to the Lord; whence in the old law, when a Gentile bondman was bought, his nails were pared and his hair was shorn [n: (Deut. XII)]. Because vanity must be put off on every side, that a man may be made over to the service of God. Here, from the renunciation of the world, one comes by certain degrees of merits to that perfect and eternal charity which is set at the summit.

*Unto the Lord when I was in trouble.* [n: (CASS.)] First, beginning [1042B] to ascend, because he finds many besetting him, he asks to be delivered from their tongues: secondly, he complains of the mingling of the wicked, *Woe is me.*

VERS. 2.-- *O Lord, deliver,* etc. When a man begins to ascend, that is, when he purposes to advance, the world being despised, cleaving to God alone, he begins to suffer the tongues of adversaries, that is, of detractors, and (what is graver) of those who, as though counselling him, turn him away from salvation: he who does not suffer these does not yet endeavour to advance. Being about to ascend, therefore, he prays first against them: *O Lord, deliver my soul from wicked lips.*

VERS. 3.-- *What shall be given to thee,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] After the manner of one thinking overmuch he hesitates, and seeks a remedy. *What shall be given to thee, or what shall be added?* He has set down two words, *to be given and to be added;* arrows are given for arms, coals are [1042C] added as it were for consolation.

VERS. 4.-- *Arrows.* [n: (AUG.)] The heart is shot through with the words of God, etc., *as far as* that there may be a clean place for the building of God.

VERS. 5.-- *Woe is me.* [n: (CASS.)] Secondly he complains of his sojourning and of the mingling of the wicked. Having received the arrows and the coals, being made wheat, he is not yet in the barn, but on the threshing-floor where much chaff is trodden; seeing therefore many evils which he had not seen before, he cries: *Woe is me.* To no man do the wicked appear, unless he has been made good; *For when the blade was sprung up, and had brought forth fruit, the cockle appeared. Prolonged.* Though the time be short, to desire it seems exceedingly long. *Cedar.* [n: (ID.)] The son of Ismael, from whom the Saracens are descended, who would more truly be called Agarenes from Agar: but from Sara, the worthier and the free woman, they have claimed a name for themselves, and both of these are read to have dwelt in [1042D] tents. The tents of Ismael are rightly called Cedar: Cedar belongs to Ismael. [n: (AUG.)] Isaac therefore, etc., *as far as* if now the evil cannot be separated from the good, they must be borne with.

## PSALM CXX.

VERS. 1.-- *A canticle of degrees.* [n: (CASS.)] The second degree, to seek the divine defence, against which nothing is shown to be able to prevail. [n: (AUG.)] For he upon whom, as he goes out of the world, dangers press, ought to demand the divine help, etc., *as far as* Such is this man, who lifts his eyes to God. [n: (CASS.)] First he lifts his eyes and prays: secondly, he affirms what he prayed for, showing the power of the help obtained.

*I have lifted up mine eyes.* [n: (ID.)] He who cried out as one bowed down, [1043A] now taking breath, lifts his eyes to the holy intercessors.

VERS. 3.-- *Neither let him slumber.* [n: (AUG.)] If faith sleeps in thee, Christ sleeps in thee.

[n: (CASS.)] This is said after human custom; as when a weary watchman slumbers, he suffers theft.

VERS. 4.--[n: (AUG.)] *Behold he shall not slumber.* Christ slumbers, etc., *as far as* but lest thou still suppose this of some man, he subjoins, *The Lord keepeth.*

*He that keepeth Israel.* [n: (AUG.)] He who cannot see the face, etc., *as far as* and for this man Christ does not sleep.

VERS. 5.-- *The Lord keepeth.* [n: (CASS.)] The second part, where he affirms what he had prayed. And as if one should ask, Who is that keeper? he subjoins, *The Lord keepeth,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] It is not without cause, etc., *as far as* the love of God, namely, and of the neighbour.

[1043B]VERS. 6.-- *By day the sun shall not burn thee, nor the moon by night,* etc. [n: (ID.)] God is the sun, etc., *as far as* or *the moon,* that is, the Church, will not suffer thee to be burned in the night of tribulation. [n: (HIER.)] Or *by day* prosperity is understood, etc., *as far as* so now he keeps us both in prosperity and in adversity.

VERS. 7.-- *May the Lord keep thy coming in.* [n: (CASS.)] He concludes all: to good beginnings may he join good endings. [n: (AUG.)] *May the Lord keep thy coming in.* He keeps those who enter, etc., *as far as* now unto eternity nothing shall hurt.

## PSALM CXXI.

VERS. 1.-- *A canticle of degrees.* [n: (CASS.)] The third degree. A great joy: to be conversant in the Church with a pure mind, and worthily to serve God as a soldier in this pilgrimage: whence we sigh for the heavenly Jerusalem, to [1043C] which we are lifted by the two wings of love.

*I rejoiced at these things,* etc. First he rejoices that he has been admonished to go to Jerusalem, where the saints, secure, shall judge with the Lord. Secondly, being about to ascend, he wishes for an abundance of peace: *Pray ye for the things that are for the peace,* etc.

VERS. 4.-- *A testimony to Israel.* [n: (AUG.)] That is, in whom it is known that they are truly Israel. For when the grains are with the chaff, the whole is thought to be chaff; but when they shall be separated, then shall there be a testimony to Israel.

VERS. 5.-- *Seats.* [n: (ID.)] That is, the just, in whom God sits, etc., *as far as* let all therefore who are to be questioned beware for themselves.

VERS. 6.-- *Pray ye.* [n: (CASS.)] The second part, where he speaks to the judges, wishing them the abundance of peace which he proclaimed out of love of the brethren. *To them that love.* [1043D] [n: (AUG.)] He who loves in another the good which he himself cannot have, it is reckoned to him as though he had it; whence: If charity has not what to give, let it give a cup of water; and as much is reckoned to him as to Zacchaeus his many gifts [n: (Matth. X)]. The means are unequal, but the charity is not unequal.

VERS. 7.-- *Let peace be made.* [n: (ID.)] Charity is a virtue strong as death, etc., *as far as* he subjoins: *Because of my brethren.*

*In the towers.* [n: (ID.)] That is, in thy high places. The judges are few, but many shall pertain to each one of them, whom they shall receive into everlasting tabernacles, and then shall there be *abundance in the towers;* but God is their whole abundance, both of those who receive and of those who are received.

VERS. 9.-- *Because of the house of the Lord God.* Or thou mayest so read it [1044A] that this verse is the exposition of the preceding. As it were, *I spoke peace of thee, for neighbours, brethren,* namely: *I have sought good things for thee, because of the house of the Lord our God.* The sense of these words is not changed. *For thee.* O thou, heavenly Jerusalem, because thou art the house of God, and so in this, that I love thee, I love God.

## PSALM CXXII.

*A canticle of degrees.* [n: (CASS.)] The fourth degree, that amid whatsoever straits one must steadfastly presume upon the Lord, until he take pity and hear. He who above lifted his eyes to the mountains, now lifts them to God.

VERS. 1.-- *To thee have I lifted up.* [n: (ID.)] First the voice of the troubled looking to God, not to other patronages; secondly, he prays for mercy against those who insult him. [n: (AUG.)] *To thee have I lifted up.* Whither should he lift them, save whither he tended in ascending? [1044B] this man does not regard himself, as the proud man does who pleases himself: for he who is displeasing to himself, such a one pleases God; and he to whom God is pleasing is himself displeasing to himself. *I have lifted up.* The ascent therefore is in the affection, etc., *as far as* but he who is a pilgrim and desires his country.

VERS. 2.-- *Behold as the eyes,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] The servant lifts his eyes to the hands of his master, when he is in want or when he is beaten, because both are in the master's power. *As the eyes of the handmaid.* [n: (AUG.)] *The servant* is the people; *the Lord,* Christ; *the handmaid,* the Church; the mistress, the power of God and the wisdom of God. And though we are now sons and friends by grace, yet we are servants by creation, because every creature serves God. [n: (CASS.)] He gives a similitude from the manservant and the handmaid, the master and the mistress, lest the feminine and weaker sex suppose itself excepted.

[1044C]VERS. 3.-- *Have mercy.* [n: (ID.)] The second part, where he prays for mercy against those who insult him. The servant who is beaten, having lifted his eyes to God, breaks forth into the needful cry: *Have mercy.*

*We are filled.* As when it is said: What dost thou believe, madman; dost thou see what thou believest? Has anyone returned from the dead, and told thee what is done there? Behold, what I love, I see and enjoy.
