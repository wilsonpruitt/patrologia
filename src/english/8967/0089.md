## PSALM CXXIII.

VERS. 1.-- *If it had not been that.* [n: (CASS.)] First the holy martyrs, mindful of how great things they have escaped, ascribe them to mercy alone; secondly, they give thanks that they have been delivered.

VERS. 3.-- *Perhaps they had swallowed us up alive.* They live who know that an idol is nothing. They are dead who think it to be something. Therefore the dead are swallowed up, who [1044D] think that the evil which they do is a thing to be done; but the living are they who indeed know that what they do is evil, but being overcome by persecution pass over into the fellowship of the ungodly, and prefer the sweetness of this life to God, as certain men did who for fear of death sacrificed to idols. [n: (AUG.)] Mark also that Peter, that is, the Church, swallows up many, but not alive. To whom it is said: *Slay and eat. [n: (Act. X.)]* [var: Vulg. *occide*, "kill"] For he eats none, that is, sets none among his members, save one slain, that is, one renouncing the devil. He therefore is slain as to evil, but quickened as to good, who is eaten by Peter. The contrary befalls him whom the devil swallows down.

VERS. 4.-- *Water.* [n: (AUG.)] The sinful people (and it is from the likeness of the Egyptians): which water flows as a torrent [1045A] with violence, but quickly passes; whence he subjoins: *a torrent. --Perhaps* the Latins set down, as best they can, for the Greek ἄρα in the place of doubting, which is more intelligibly said: *thinkest thou, has our soul passed through?* that is, the greatness of the peril scarcely suffers this to be believed.

VERS. 5.-- *Water insupportable.* [n: (AUG.)] Without substance, as are vices, and sins, and the sweetness of this life, etc., *as far as* and might bring back to the father the prodigal son worn out by long need.

VERS. 6.-- *Blessed be the Lord.* [n: (CASS.)] The second part, where the saints give thanks that they have been delivered and that the snare has been broken.

VERS. 7.-- *Our soul.* [n: (AUG.)] Or, as when someone makes a great noise, etc., *as far as* which is as it were a snare. *Of them that hunt.* [n: (ID.)] The hunters are [1045B] the persecutors; the bait in the trap, the sweetness of this life. *The snare is broken.* [n: (CASS.)] The martyrs being slain, there remains no sweetness of life whereby they might be taken again: but they themselves were not broken along with it, nay rather delivered. *Broken.* [n: (ID.)] As if he should say: because it was of iron; not torn apart, like tow: so great was the fortitude of the passion, so great the power of him that delivered.

## PSALM CXXIV.

VERS. 1.-- *A canticle.* [n: (CASS.)] The sixth degree: the confidence of the most faithful Christian, which he has in the Lord, is compared to the most solid mountains, that he may not be jealous of those who are happy in this world. *They that trust.* [n: (AUG.)] This psalm teaches, etc., *as far as* who are these? what manner of Jerusalem is that? he briefly describes it.

VERS. 2.-- *The mountains round about,* that is, the angels, the prophets, [1045C] the apostles are its wall: whence Isaias: *And there shall be in it a wall and a bulwark [n: (Isa. XXVI)]*; but lest thou remain among the mountains, he adds, and the Lord round about. *Let there not then be hope in the mountains,* but in the top of the mountains: whence Isaias: *In the last days there shall be a mountain on the top [n: (Isa. XXI)]*, etc. *They that trust.* [n: (CASS.)] First he affirms, etc., *as far as* because they are both many and are one. *Mount Sion.* [n: (HIER.)] Christ, on whom the Church is founded: that mountain, according to the letter, shall be shaken at the end of the world.

VERS. 3.-- *The rod of sinners.* [n: (AUG.)] For they bear rule sometimes for the discipline of the good, but this shall not be always, as the prophet here plainly says: and therefore the just do not fail, because they know the time to come, in which Christ, appearing in his brightness, shall gather [1045D] before him all nations, and shall divide them as a shepherd divides the kids from the sheep, and shall set the sheep on the right hand, the kids on the left. *The lot.* [n: (CASS.)] That is, the just chosen by lot, or who are the lot and the inheritance of God.

VERS. 4.-- *Do good, O Lord.* [n: (ID.)] The second part, where, the people being confirmed, he prays for what he knows shall come to pass, that prosperity may come to the good, vengeance to the wicked.

VERS. 5.-- *But such as turn aside.* [n: (ID.)] By a like sentence also do they perish who turn aside from the commands of the prince, and they who strive to will evil works; those failing, together with those who compel them to fail. *Peace upon Israel.* [n: (AUG.)] By one name is called the paternal inheritance, which is Christ. For he is our peace, who has made both one, who shall give us peace above peace.

## PSALM CXXV.

[1046A]

VERS. 1.-- *A canticle of degrees.* [n: (CASS.)] The seventh degree, in a figure of the Old Testament, prophesies through Christ who is to come the return from captivity, as it were from Babylon: whence now consolation, and a sowing that more may be reaped. [n: (AUG.)] Those redeemed by the blood of Christ and turned back from captivity sing these things, while they are pilgrims, groaning by the way. Sion is Jerusalem, eternal in the angels, captive in men. *When the Lord turned.* [n: (CASS.)] First the greatness of the deed is shown, with the greatness of the joy: secondly he prays for the turning from captivity still to come, there, *Turn again, O Lord. When the Lord turned.* [n: (AUG.)] Or, when he shall have turned, etc., *as far as turn again, O Lord, our captivity. As.* [n: (ID.)] It is not always set for a likeness, but [1046B] for a property, as here; whence it is also said: He did as a just man, which he truly is; so these are made *as men comforted,* that is, they rejoiced, as receiving consolation in the resurrection of Christ, who went before that we might follow.

VERS. 2.-- *Our mouth.* [n: (CASS.)] The secret of the heart, where joys first are, which afterwards break forth by the office of the tongue. [n: (AUG.)] *Our mouth.* Of the heart: thence proceeds the evil and the good which cleanses or defiles us; whence not: *That which proceedeth out of the mouth, defileth [n: (Marc. VII)]* [var: Vulg. Mc 7:15 *quae de homine procedunt, illa communicant hominem*; Migne's wording is that of Mt 15:11]. Not of the mouth of the flesh, but of the heart. For thou art not unclean if thou namest thefts and other evils: but while thou thinkest of them, even if thou keep silence. *Among the Gentiles.* [n: (ID.)] Not the Gentiles, because not all believe. *He hath magnified,* because they see them work miracles also; a firm testimony from an adversary. [1046C] [n: (CASS.)] And he has truly magnified, lest thou doubt because of the words of the Gentiles.

VERS. 4.-- *Turn again, O Lord.* [n: (ID.)] The second part. He prays for the turning which is to come, in which there shall be a sowing in hope of joy. [n: (ID.)] The redemption of the coming of the Lord having been foretold, they pray again that their own sins be forgiven. In the general remission they rejoice, and they pray pardon for themselves. *Turn again, O Lord, our captivity.* [n: (ID.)] There was none according to the letter in the days of David, but under the devil the world was captive, etc., *as far as* it is not therefore to be given over, because *he that soweth in tears.* [n: (THEOD.)] He calls Jerusalem the south, etc., *as far as* that they may be constrained, and make their way after the manner of a torrent.

VERS. 5.-- *In tears.* [n: (AUG.)] Spiritual sowing is always in tears, whence: *Blessed are they that mourn.* But they [1046D] who sow in the flesh, sow with gladness: but afterwards weeping: because *mourning taketh hold of the end of joy.* [n: (ID.)] Zacchaeus, *Behold the half,* etc., *as far as: He that shall give a cup of cold water shall not lose his reward [n: (Matth. X)]*.

VERS. 6.-- *Going.* [n: (CASS.)] By the advancement of a better life. *Casting,* sending on before them into that world. *Their own,* not of rapine, but of their own labour; *coming,* to the judgment. This is common to all, but to joy none come but the good. *And they wept.* [n: (AUG.)] Because they were wretched, etc., *as far as* he wishes that many be sick.
