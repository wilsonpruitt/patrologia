## PSALM CXXVI.

VERS. 1.-- *A canticle of degrees of Solomon.* [n: (CASS.)] The eighth degree, etc., *as far as* and in himself, as the corner stone, he built up the Church of two walls, of the Jews and [1047A] of the Gentiles. [n: (AUG.)] Here is added *of Solomon,* because it was he who built the temple, in a figure of Christ the peacemaker, who built the Church of two walls. Lest therefore thou shouldst understand that Solomon, he says, *Unless the Lord build.* In him is hope to be placed, but in no man: because even that Solomon fell who built the temple. [n: (CASS.)] First, he admonishes how they that ascend ought to bear themselves, namely that they learn that the building and the keeping are from none but God. And exaltation in the world to come is only after humiliation. Secondly, he tells what the inheritance is, and how it is gathered, there, *Behold the inheritance. Unless the Lord.* [n: (ID.)] As if he should say [sic: *dieeret*]: now Jesus has come, he has brought back from captivity: now the city Jerusalem is being built; but let them that ascend learn that the building and the keeping are from none but God, and exaltation [1047B] only after humiliation in the world to come; for although they are glad of the grace of the New Testament, yet in so great a gift no man ought to presume. *The house.* [n: (AUG.)] To this every man ascends who advances: from this every man falls who fails; by loving God thou ascendest, by loving the world thou fallest. For these are the songs of lovers, and of men burning with a certain holy desire. *They have laboured.* [n: (ID.)] All labour in this building who preach the word of God in the Church. *The city.* [n: (CASS.)] He passes from the house to the city, that presumption be made neither in individuals nor in a small gathering. And although house and city, according to the metaphor which is drawn from different things, are diverse: yet the Church is one, which both signify.

[1047C]VERS. 2.-- *Before the light.* [n: (AUG.)] If ye rise before the light rises; the light is Christ, after whom we must rise, not before; as he does who sets himself before him: wishing to be lofty here, where he was humble: as also the sons of Zebedee: but the resurrection, that is, the exaltation, follows the sitting, that is, the humiliation, as our light Christ rose lofty after death. *Ye shall have sat.* Sitting sometimes signifies the honour of judging, but here humiliation. *Ye that eat the bread of sorrow.* [n: (AUG.)] He eats the bread of sorrow who groans in this pilgrimage, that is, in the vale of weeping refreshes himself out of his affliction, saying: *My tears have been made my bread day and night [n: (Psal. XLI)]* [var: Vulg. *Fuerunt mihi lacrimae meae panes*, "my tears have been my bread"]. *Sleep.* [n: (CASS.)] Temporal death, in which is a secure rest.

VERS. 3.-- *Behold the inheritance.* [n: (ID.)] Secondly, what the [1047D] inheritance is and how it is gathered. As if he should say, the beloved shall rise after sleep. *The fruit of the womb.* [n: (AUG.)] The womb of the Church, etc., *as far as* because he acquired all men through the obedience of his passion, *whence, ask of me, and I will give thee the Gentiles for thy inheritance [n: (Psal. II)]*.

VERS. 4.-- *As arrows.* [n: (ID.)] But whence so great an inheritance was made: because the Lord is mighty: when he shot forth the arrows, the apostles, mightily from the bow of holy Scripture, those that were shot forth reached unto the ends of the earth; and such are the *children of them that were shot forth,* as were they that were shot forth. *The children of them that were shot forth.* [n: (ID.)] The children of the apostles; to whom it was said, *Shake off the dust from your feet [n: (Matth. XVIII)]*. But they so appear, etc., *as far as* who were sent by the hand of God unto the ends of the earth.

## PSALM CXXVII.

[1048A]

VERS. 1.-- *A canticle of degrees.* [n: (CASS.)] The ninth degree is, that the peacemaker be chastely feared, who alone exalts, keeps, and builds. First he enumerates the good things of him that fears; secondly he wishes the good things of him that fears. *Blessed are all they that fear the Lord.* It is to be known that there are four fears, etc., *as far as* of which the Prophet here says: *Blessed are all they that fear the Lord.*

VERS. 2.-- *The labours of the hands.* [n: (ID.)] And note that he began from the plural, saying: *Blessed:* but because all are one in Christ, in the singular there follows, *The labours of thy hands.* [n: (AUG.)] He says the labours of the fruits, when he ought to say: The fruit of the labours; but because they are taken up with joy even in the labour, for the sake of hope, they themselves also give delight, and are even now food; but more delightful [1048B] shall their fruit be, when the saints, coming [cj: *veniens*; read *venient*, "shall come"], *carrying their sheaves with joyfulness.*

VERS. 3.-- *Thy wife.* [n: (CASS.)] Although this might be taken according to the letter, etc., *as far as* it pertains to the New Testament. [n: (AUG.)] Or by *wife* the Church is understood, and the speech is directed to Christ, when *thy* and *thine* are said; nor yet is the voice or the person changed, because of the unity of the head and the body. For Christ is one man, both head and body, and in the head each one of the faithful has this.

VERS. 4.-- *He shall be blessed.* [n: (CASS.)] Where blessing is, all good things flow together unto the joy of sweetness.

*He shall be blessed.* [n: (ID.)] The second part, where out of piety he asks that they that fear be blessed; here he wishes good things for him that fears, which, namely, are not common to the wicked.

[1048C]VERS. 5.--Or, *The Lord.* [n: (AUG.)] Of the Jews, Out of Sion, etc., *as far as* which they cannot so do.

## PSALM CXXVIII.

VERS. 1.-- *A canticle of degrees.* [n: (CASS.)] The tenth degree, that he who fears chastely may bear all things for his sake, which is now expedient for the Church among the wicked.

*Often have they fought against me.* [n: (AUG.)] The wicked, although they hear the word of God, etc., *as far as* that they bear so many wicked men. [n: (CASS.)] Showing first what things he has borne, he comforts the weak. Secondly he treats of the reprobation of the persecutors, there, *The Lord is just. Often have they fought against me.* As it were: O sons, do not marvel at these things, etc., *as far as* because the Church grows in adversities.

VERS. 2.-- *For indeed they could not against me.* [n: (AUG.)] [1048D] Namely, prevail, so as to draw me into consent unto sin.

VERS. 3.-- *Upon the back.* [n: (CASS.)] What the wicked cannot do publicly to all, they attempt to urge secretly upon single persons, so that they who would be seen coming from the front may not be felt from behind. *They have lengthened out iniquity.* Elsewhere, *they have put their injustice far from me.* [n: (AUG.)] Who, although the wicked are with the good in the world, even if they be also in the Church: yet lest any suppose iniquity to stand beside justice, although I bear them, nevertheless their iniquity is far from me; let no one therefore fear the wicked mingled with him, who are far off.

*The Lord is just.* [n: (CASS.)] The second part, where he treats of the reprobation of the persecutors.

VERS. 4.-- *They that hate Sion.* [n: (ID.)] Whom not only open persecutors hate, but also they who enter [1049A] feignedly. For they who will not keep the word of God hate the Church.

*Let them be as grass upon the housetops.* [n: (CASS.)] The wicked are compared to grass upon the housetops, etc., *as far as* but in neither is there fruit for the wicked.

[n: (GREG.)] The bulrush is said to wither before all herbs, etc., *as far as* it loses, as it were, the appearance of greenness, etc.

VERS. 6.-- *Which before it be plucked up.* [n: (AUG.)] They are not yet finished in the judgment of God, and already they have not the sap of greenness.

VERS. 8.-- *And they have not said.* [n: (CASS.)] It was a custom among the Hebrews that a wayfarer should bless those at work: whence it is here said conversely, that to those who work in vain no blessing is given.

[1049B] *And they have not said.* Whom the angels shall not gather, to whom the apostles have not preached. And this is what he says:

[n: (AUG.)] *And they have not said,* etc. Chiefly among the Jews it was the custom to say to any whosoever were at work: *The blessing of the Lord be upon you. We have blessed you in the name,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] They bless the good, not in their own name, but *in the name of the Lord:* from whom are all things, not from them.
