## PSALM CXXIX.

VERS. 1.-- *A canticle of degrees.* [n: (ID.)] The eleventh degree, etc., *as far as* a glad conclusion is set down with the certainty of pardon, there, *Let Israel hope in the Lord.*

*Out of the depths.* [n: (AUG.)] He who feels himself in the deep, etc., *as far as* as the Scripture says: *The sinner, when he shall have come [1049C] into the depth of vices, contemneth.* [var: Vulg. Prov. 18:3 *impius, cum in profundum venerit peccatorum, contemnit*, "the ungodly … the depth of sins"]

VERS. 3.-- *Who shall abide it.* [n: (ID.)] Because well nigh all human life is barked at round about by sins, and every conscience is accused by its own thoughts. That heart is not chaste which presumes upon its own justice. Let it therefore presume upon the mercy of God, *For with him there is propitiation,* whence he subjoins:

VERS. 4.-- *For with thee there is propitiation.* As it were: In what hope dost thou cry? Because there is mercy in thee, wherefore he speaks thus: Or join it thus.

[n: (AUG.)] No man can abide it, etc., *as far as* one hope therefore there is: *For with thee there is propitiation,* etc., *For thy law's sake,* [n: (ID.)] I have waited for the *law* of mercy, etc., *as far as* and thou hast given this law in the very necessity.

[1049D]VERS. 6.-- *The morning.* [n: (ID.)] Let him hope in the morning watch who hopes for nothing else from God than immortality. *From the morning watch,* etc., *as far as* thence in the law the head and the tail of the victim were offered to the Lord. *Let Israel hope in the Lord.* [n: (CASS.)] The second part, where is the glad conclusion: Secure concerning himself, he preaches that they should hope always in the Lord. As it were, as his soul has hoped, so let Israel hope.

VERS. 8.-- *And he shall redeem Israel from all his iniquities.* [n: (ID.)] Here he has now said what that one shall do, whom he called merciful and plentiful.

## PSALM CXXX.

VERS. 1.-- *A canticle of degrees.* [n: (CASS.)] The twelfth degree is humility, which he here [1050A] shows that he has within and without. To humility therefore he exhorts.

*Lord, it is not.* [n: (ID.)] First, setting himself forth as humble, he brings in punishment for the man that is not humble; secondly, by his own example he exhorts to hope. Showing therefore that he is humble within and without, he says. *Neither have I walked.* [n: (AUG.)] I was not proud, I would not, as it were, walk in wonderful things, etc., *as far as: The greater thou art, humble thyself in all things.* [var: Vulg. Sir. 3:20 *Quanto magnus es*, "howsoever great thou art"]

VERS. 2.-- *If I was not humbly minded.* [n: (ID.)] Nourished in milk, which is the Word made flesh, that so I might grow up to the bread of angels, to the Word which is with God, but not.

*I have exalted,* as the heretics do. Who, if they have in any way begun to grasp something, or have seemed to grasp it, thereupon grow proud; and while they are still tender upon their mother, and are carried in her hands that they may grow, they who were carried in the womb [1050B] that they might be born: they are parted from the milk before the time, whence they are quenched.

*So the reward.* [n: (ID.)] He binds himself with a curse, etc., *as far as* so shall God reveal that wherein he is otherwise minded, *because he resisteth the proud, and giveth grace to the humble.*

*As one is weaned.* For as five ages are recounted, etc., *as far as* because God was man; but not changed into man.

VERS. 3.-- *Let Israel hope.* [n: (CASS.)] The second part, where he concludes, admonishing the rest by his own example.
