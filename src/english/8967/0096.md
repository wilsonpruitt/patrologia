## PSALM CXXXVIII.

VERS. 1.-- *Unto the end, a psalm of David.* [n: (CASS.)] A psalm full of the depth of mysteries, etc., *as far as* veiled in the prophets, manifest in the Gospel. [n: (AUG.)] The whole Christ speaks, as one entire man, etc., *as far as* as two in one flesh. *Lord, thou hast proved me.* [n: (CASS.)] First, concerning the knowledge of God, etc., *as far as* fourthly, how the wicked do him no hurt, there, *If thou wilt kill, O God,* etc. *Lord, thou hast proved.* [n: (AUG.)] Now restored in Jerusalem, etc., *as far as* this is the person of the whole of human nature returning.

[1059B]VERS. 3.-- *Thou hast understood.* [n: (CASS.)] The foreknowledge of the Godhead is here shown, etc., *as far as* because the manhood assumed cannot be made equal to the divine substance. *From afar off.* [n: (AUG.)] While I am still on pilgrimage, before I come to the fatherland, as the father ran to meet the younger son returning, because he understood his thoughts from afar off, because he had said within himself, worn out with want [sic: *egestare*]: *I will arise and go to my father.*

*My path,* namely the evil one, by which I went, forsaking my father.

*And my line.* (Otherwise) my boundary, how far I came: far indeed had I gone, but thou wast there. He speaks as a fugitive caught, the vengeance of God following after him.

VERS. 4.-- *And all my ways:* before I went in them; and thou didst permit me to go in labour, that I might return if [1059C] I would not labour: this I confess. *For there is no guile in my tongue:* [var: Vulg. Ps 138:4 *quia non est sermo in lingua mea*, "for there is no speech in my tongue"] now justified in thy grace.

VERS. 5.-- My *last things,* when I fed the swine, *The old things,* when I asked for my portion of the substance: this according to the parable. But mystically, *The old things,* when through the ancient sin we fell: *The last things,* the penalty, when we came into this mortality, which is the last, if we are willing to return; otherwise the eternal remains. *Thou hast formed me,* unto labours, unto which we are all born. *Thou hast laid.* (Otherwise) Thou hast made heavy thy hand, the avenging hand, weighing down the proud man wholesomely, that it may raise up the humble man, in the hand made heavy.

VERS. 6.-- *Wonderful,* and incomprehensible to me. *Thy knowledge is become.* It is not easy for me to contemplate thee, whom in my pride I forsook, as it was before, when I was with thee, when I said, give me my portion of the substance. [1059D] *From me,* that is, from my sin.

*It is strengthened,* (otherwise) it has prevailed. *And I shall not be able* to attain to it of myself. [n: (AUG.)] In Exodus it is read that the Lord, etc., *as far as* I am not hidden from thee, whose boundary thou seest.

VERS. 7.-- *Whither shall I go from thy spirit?* [n: (CASS.)] The second part, where he now determines that he must not flee from him, but to him, from him angered to him appeased, which shall be through him with whom the world is full: *For the Spirit of the Lord filleth all things. Whither shall I flee from thy face?* [var: Vulg. Wisd. 1:7 *Spiritus Domini replevit orbem terrarum*, "the Spirit of the Lord hath filled the whole world"] he turns himself this way and that, as though seeking a place of flight.

VERS. 8.-- *If I shall ascend into heaven, thou art there,* etc. [n: (AUG.)] If I shall lift myself up, I find one to repress me; if I shall be proud of my own justice, *Thou art there,* whose justice it truly is. *If I shall descend,* if by sinning I shall come, *Into hell,* [1060A] or into the deep, and unwilling to confess: I shall say, who sees me? *Thou art present,* to avenge. He found a counsel, but thus shall I be able to flee from him angered.

VERS. 9.-- *If I shall take my wings,* the two wings of charity, which I had lost.

*Wings.* [n: (CASS.)] Which are of my own power, that is, the efficacy of the Godhead, whereby he raised the body out of the sepulchre. These he took *at daybreak,* before the light, that is, at the time of the resurrection: and *he dwelt in the uttermost parts of the sea,* that is, above the widest end of the world. *And if I shall dwell in the uttermost parts.* [n: (AUG.)] In mind, at the end of the world, that I may not love the world, but its end, in which there may be rest for me. But he himself also leads thither, whence he subjoins:

VERS. 10.-- *For even there shall thy hand.* But considering the length of the way, he subjoins:

[1060B]VERS. 11.-- *And I said, perhaps darkness shall tread me down,* etc. The iniquity of the world abounds, whereby the wings of charity can grow cold. And because in the night I despaired of crossing the sea, the night was made a light to me: There came a woman, the wisdom of God, who had lost the groat wherein is the image of the king, that is, man: she lighted from herself a lamp, which is of clay, that is, flesh, and the night being lit up she found the groat.

*And I said.* [n: (AUG.)] I deliberated.

*Perhaps thee.* By darkness he means that lower part of the rational creature, pressing upon him as an adversary, namely wicked men or the flesh, whence he fears: And it is, *Darkness,* that is, the lower rational creature holding the left-hand way.

*They have trodden me down.* [n: (ID.)] So I feared, but *the night [1060C] was made a light, in my delights,* [var: Vulg. Ps 138:11 *et nox illuminatio mea in deliciis meis*, "and night shall be my light in my pleasures"] that is, to those who, being before night, shall be enlightened in delights. *Because thee.* Sinners.

VERS. 12.-- *They shall not be darkened,* etc., *and the night,* by the sinners that dwell there. *As the day,* that is, the saints out of the Jews. *As his darkness, so also his light,* because in so far as he acknowledges, in so far he pardons. But whence is this to me? *because thou hast possessed my reins. Because darkness.* [n: (AUG.)] He who confesses not his sins darkens his own darkness; but if he confesses, God enlightens. *As his darkness.* Of our night, that is, the miseries of human life, etc., *as far as* whom the light does not lift up.

VERS. 13.-- *Because thou hast possessed my reins.* [n: (ID.)] Whence is this difference in thee? because the inward possessor [1060D] holds not only the heart, that is, the thoughts, but the reins also, that is, the delights: that the light of night may not delight, but the light of his own wisdom.

VERS. 14.-- *I will confess to thee.* [n: (CASS.)] The third part, where he tells how great is the exultation and the multiplication for him that flies back to God, God enlightening him and possessing his reins. *My soul shall know exceedingly.* [var: Vulg. Ps 138:14 *anima mea cognoscit nimis*, "my soul knoweth right well," in the present tense] [n: (AUG.)] Now my soul knows greatly: before, wonderful was *thy knowledge from me, and I could not attain unto it;* but now I can, because thou hast enlightened the night, hast possessed the reins, hast received me out of the womb, that is, because thy grace came to me and enlightened me.

VERS. 15.-- *My bone is not hidden from thee, which thou hast made in secret.* [n: (ID.)] Os from osse, not from ore, etc., *as far as* whence the Apostle: *As sorrowful, yet always [1061A] rejoicing.* [n: (BASIL.)] Again he speaks of the foreknowledge of God, and either says that all things are his secrets, or that other thing which he says, namely the workmanship and the fashioning. *My substance in the lower parts of the earth.* [n: (AUG.)] In the flesh, and yet the bone yields not to the evils of this lower place, etc., *as far as* let them not therefore despair.

VERS. 16.-- *Thy eyes did see my imperfect being, and in thy book all,* etc. [n: (ID.)] He who has hitherto spoken in the person of the body, here now speaks in the person of the head. *Days shall be formed.* (Otherwise) *They shall wander through the day.* The day here abiding was still Christ, etc., *as far as* because the apostles were made leaders and shepherds of the Church. As it were, deservedly I strengthened them, etc., *as far as* in the promise it was said to Abraham: *I will multiply thy seed as the stars of heaven and as the sand of the sea.*

[1061B]VERS. 18.-- *I am still with thee,* etc. As before death, which the Jews thought they had taken away. Or, *still,* that is, in death itself, which is not seen of a dead man. Or, though the resurrection be a sign of the Godhead, yet *I am still* hidden from the many, *with thee,* that is, as thou art.

[n: (AUG.)] *I am still with thee.* Not yet with them by knowledge, because they do not yet acknowledge him, because they did not acknowledge Christ soon after the resurrection. Or, through all this time I am with thee in secret at the right hand of the Father, before he be revealed in that brightness wherein he is to come.

VERS. 19.-- *If thou wilt kill, O God, the sinners,* etc. [n: (CASS.)] The fourth part, how he is not hurt by the wicked. [n: (AUG.)] *If thou wilt kill.* He subjoins what he suffers meanwhile, [1061C] etc., *as far as* in the judgment he will say openly: *Amen I say to you, I know you not, depart from me.* [var: Vulg. Mt 7:23 *numquam novi vos : discedite a me*, "I never knew you: depart from me"; the opening words are those of Mt 25:12 *Amen dico vobis, nescio vos*]

VERS. 21.-- *Have not they that hate thee, O Lord.* As it were, *If thou wilt kill, Have I not hated them that hate thee?* Them will I also slay.

[n: (AUG.)] As it were, heretics not understanding, etc., *as far as* how then am I defiled by being mingled with them?

*Prove me, O Lord.* [n: (ID.)] Hitherto concerning heretics and others following them, separated from the Church; now he treats also of the wicked, or of feigned men joined to himself, whom the Church suffers, and groans over. As it were, heretics separate themselves and others, etc., *as far as* but thou, O God, *Prove me and know.*
